## Three-line Summary

Double-blinded RCT (n=40 mandibular molar extraction sites) comparing eggshell-derived nanohydroxyapatite (EnHA)+PRF vs demineralized freeze-dried bone allograft (DFDBA)+PRF as a barrier membrane in socket preservation.

Both groups improved significantly over time (pain, wound healing, bone height/density, lamina dura), but no statistically significant between-group difference was found at 1 or 4 months; EnHA+PRF trended numerically better on wound healing, bone height, and bone density.

Clinical implication: EnHA is a plausible lower-cost/biowaste-derived alternative to DFDBA when paired with PRF as a barrier membrane, but this page is built from an abstract-only source (PMC full text unavailable), so exact statistics could not be verified.

## Summary

This double-blinded RCT randomized 40 mandibular molar extraction sockets 1:1 into two socket-preservation modalities: eggshell-derived nanohydroxyapatite (EnHA) graft with PRF used as a barrier membrane (Group I), versus demineralized freeze-dried bone allograft (DFDBA) with PRF as a barrier membrane (Group II). Patients were followed clinically (pain at day 1/2, wound healing at day 7/14) and radiographically (lamina dura, bone height, bone density on IOPA at baseline, post-grafting, 1 month, and 4 months). Both groups showed significant within-group improvement over time across all endpoints. No statistically significant between-group difference emerged clinically or radiographically, though EnHA+PRF trended numerically favorable for wound healing, bone height gain, and bone density. The authors concluded EnHA+PRF "seems to offer significantly better results" — a framing that should be read cautiously given the reported lack of between-group significance (see Limitations). Because only the structured abstract was retrievable (PMC full text was empty), exact p-values and methodological details (randomization/blinding procedure, precise numeric outcomes) are not independently verifiable from this source.

## Key Contributions

- First wiki-held head-to-head RCT of eggshell-derived nanohydroxyapatite (EnHA) — a biowaste-derived graft material — against a standard allograft (DFDBA) in extraction socket preservation.
- Uses PRF specifically as a **barrier membrane** over the graft (not mixed in as "sticky bone" or used alone), a distinct application from other PRF-socket-preservation papers already in the wiki.
- Double-blinded design for a graft-material comparison, which is uncommon given visibly different graft materials — strengthens confidence in clinical assessments where achievable.
- Tracks a broad panel of clinical (pain, wound healing) and radiographic (lamina dura, bone height, bone density) endpoints out to 4 months.

## Methodology

**Design**: Double-blinded, randomized controlled clinical trial; 40 mandibular molar extraction sites randomized 1:1.

- **Group I (EnHA+PRF)**: Eggshell-derived nanohydroxyapatite graft placed in the socket, covered with a PRF membrane acting as the barrier.
- **Group II (DFDBA+PRF)**: Demineralized freeze-dried bone allograft placed in the socket, covered with a PRF membrane acting as the barrier.

**Outcome measures**:
- *Clinical*: Pain (day 1, day 2 postoperative); wound healing (day 7, day 14).
- *Radiographic*: Intraoral periapical radiographs (IOPA) at baseline (pre-grafting), immediately post-grafting, 1 month, and 4 months — evaluating lamina dura changes, bone height, and bone density.

Full methodological detail (randomization method, allocation concealment, exact statistical tests) was not retrievable — only the structured PubMed abstract was available (PMC full text returned empty).

## Results

| Endpoint | Within-group change (both groups) | Between-group difference (EnHA+PRF vs DFDBA+PRF) |
|---|---|---|
| Pain (day 1–2) | Significant improvement over time | No significant difference |
| Wound healing (day 7–14) | Significant improvement over time | No significant difference (EnHA+PRF numerically favored) |
| Bone height (radiographic) | Significant increase over time | No significant difference (EnHA+PRF numerically favored) |
| Bone density (radiographic) | Significant increase over time | No significant difference (EnHA+PRF numerically favored) |
| Lamina dura changes | Significant change over time | No significant difference |

Both EnHA+PRF and DFDBA+PRF were effective socket-preservation modalities; neither was statistically superior to the other at any measured time point. The authors' summary conclusion favored EnHA+PRF descriptively, but this is not supported by a reported statistically significant between-group difference — treat the "EnHA is better" framing as a numerical trend, not a confirmed effect, pending full-text verification.
