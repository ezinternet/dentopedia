## Three-line Summary

Narrative literature review (PubMed/Scopus, 2000–2025, no meta-analysis) synthesizing biological principles and clinical outcomes of seven bone-substitute categories used in alveolar ridge augmentation for implant dentistry.

Autogenous bone remains the biological gold standard (osteogenic+osteoinductive+osteoconductive) but shows up to 55% volume loss within 6 months; a 1:1 xenograft:autograft mix reportedly achieves the highest new bone formation (65.8%), and the review proposes a defect-type-specific selection algorithm (horizontal → xenograft/allograft + resorbable membrane ± PRF; vertical → autograft + alloplast/xenograft; complex → 3D-printed scaffold).

As a non-systematic narrative review without a formal search strategy or quantitative synthesis, conclusions carry selection-bias risk and should be read as a clinical decision-aid framework rather than pooled-effect evidence.

## Summary

This 2026 narrative review from a Serbian military-medicine/dental group synthesizes the biological mechanisms and clinical performance of seven categories of bone substitutes used in alveolar ridge augmentation (ARA) for implant dentistry: autogenous bone, allografts, xenografts, alloplastic materials, demineralized tooth matrix (autologous dentin graft, ADG), platelet concentrates (PRP/PRF), and 3D-printed scaffolds. Drawing on PubMed/Scopus literature (2000–2025) without a formal systematic protocol, the review organizes each material by biological mechanism (osteogenic/osteoinductive/osteoconductive potential), evidence summary, clinical outcomes, and limitations/controversies, then translates the synthesis into four comparative tables culminating in a practical, defect-type-specific graft-selection algorithm intended for chairside decision-making.

## Key Contributions

- **Seven-material comparative synthesis**: Systematic (in structure, not search methodology) side-by-side treatment of autograft, allograft, xenograft, alloplast, demineralized tooth matrix, PRF/PRP, and 3D-printed scaffolds — each scored for osteogenic/osteoinductive/osteoconductive properties, resorption profile, advantages, limitations, and clinical indications (Table 1).
- **Defect-type decision algorithm**: Horizontal defects → xenograft or allograft + resorbable collagen membrane ± PRF; vertical defects → autograft + alloplast/xenograft + membrane; complex/irregular defects → 3D-printed scaffold ± growth factors (Table 4). A parallel procedure-based guidance table (Table 3) assigns Option A/B/C tiers with level-of-evidence ratings (e.g., GBR around implants: autogenous+DBBM 1:1 ± PRF [LoE II–III]; sinus lift: DBBM alone [LoE I–II]).
- **Composite-ratio evidence**: Highlights a pilot study (Kamat 2025) reporting the highest new bone formation (65.8%) with a 1:1 xenograft:autograft ratio + i-PRF, versus lower yields at 2:1 ratio and xenograft-alone — direct quantitative support for the "composite grafting" recommendation.
- **Explicit heterogeneity flagging on adjuncts**: Notes contradictory hyaluronic acid (HYA) evidence — some studies show benefit in socket preservation/early bone formation, others report limited effect or even increased crestal bone loss with HYA/PRF — modeling appropriate epistemic caution rather than overselling adjunct biologics.
- **Surgical/soft-tissue integration**: Frames graft-material selection within peri-implant gap management (≤2 mm may heal spontaneously; >2 mm typically grafted), flap design (periosteal-releasing "sausage technique," minimized vertical releasing incisions), and keratinized mucosa/soft-tissue thickness considerations — positioning biomaterial choice as one variable within the broader GBR/immediate-implant surgical protocol.

## Methodology

Narrative literature review. Databases: PubMed and Scopus, no publication-year restriction (bulk of cited literature spans 2000–2025). Search terms combined via Boolean AND/OR: "bone graft," "bone substitute," "guided bone regeneration," "alveolar ridge augmentation," "dental implants," "xenograft," "alloplast," "demineralized dentin," "platelet-rich fibrin." Inclusion criteria: human clinical studies, relevant preclinical animal studies, RCTs, and systematic reviews providing data on biological behavior and/or clinical performance of graft materials for ARA. A PRISMA-style flow diagram documents the study-selection process, but synthesis is explicitly qualitative/narrative — no quantitative meta-analysis was performed, due to substantial heterogeneity in biomaterial composition, surgical protocols, defect characteristics, and outcome-assessment methods across the included literature. Level-of-evidence (LoE) grading was applied post hoc to the practical clinical-guidance table (Table 3), based on the highest evidence tier available in the cited literature per procedure.

## Results

| Graft type | Key property | Resorption/timing benchmark | Primary indication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Autograft | Osteogenic+inductive+conductive; gold standard | Up to 55% volume loss @ 6 mo; ~7%/yr vertical | Small–large defects, block grafting |
| Allograft | Osteoinductive+conductive | Variable; slower integration than autograft | Moderate defects |
| Xenograft | Osteoconductive | Slow resorption; excellent volume maintenance | Ridge augmentation, sinus lift |
| Alloplast (β-TCP/HA) | Osteoconductive | Slow–moderate; ~25% residual particles | Small/contained defects |
| Demineralized tooth matrix (ADG) | Osteoinductive+conductive (autogenous) | Moderate; implant placement feasible 4–6 mo | Extraction sites |
| PRF/PRP | Growth-factor adjunct (non-structural) | Not applicable | Adjunct to combined regenerative procedures |
| 3D-printed scaffold | Osteoconductive ± osteoinductive | Variable | Complex/irregular defects |

- Post-extraction dimensional loss cited as baseline context: 29–63% horizontal, 11–22% vertical within 6 months.
- Composite grafting: 1:1 xenograft:autograft ratio produced the highest new bone formation (65.8%) versus 2:1 ratio (lower) and xenograft-only (lowest) in a cited pilot study.
- Titanium-PRF combined with nanocrystalline HA or demineralized bone matrix: significant bone fill and ridge-width gain reported within 14–16 weeks.
- Implant survival >90% frequently reported across augmented sites in the reviewed literature, though the authors caution that biomaterial/protocol/defect heterogeneity limits direct pooled comparison and that few long-term RCTs exist to confirm superiority of any single material.
- Practical clinical-guidance table (Table 3) example: for GBR around implants, Option A = autogenous+DBBM (1:1) ± PRF (LoE II–III); for sinus lift, Option A = DBBM alone (LoE I–II).
