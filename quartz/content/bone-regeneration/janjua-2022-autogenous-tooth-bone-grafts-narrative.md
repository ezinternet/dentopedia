## Three-line Summary

Narrative review synthesizing the biology, preparation method, clinical applications, and complications of autogenous tooth bone graft (AUTO-BG), a technique using a patient's own extracted teeth (ground dentin/enamel) as an autogenous bone-graft substitute.

AUTO-BG shows biocompatibility and physical/biochemical properties resembling autogenous cortical bone (BMPs, type I collagen, calcium phosphates), with reported outcomes including ~5 mm ridge-width gain, ~96% implant survival after sinus augmentation, ~87% bone gain in GBR, and an overall implant failure rate of ~2.3–3.9% across pooled case series.

Complication rates (wound dehiscence, infection ~9.1% in one series, hematoma ~3.64%, marginal bone loss ~2.5 mm/5yr) are comparable to other graft sources, but the tedious chairside preparation, need for a specialized dentin grinder, limited graft quantity per tooth, and absence of long-term RCT data currently restrict AUTO-BG to a niche option for smaller craniofacial defects.

## 세줄요약

환자 자신의 발치 치아(분쇄된 상아질/법랑질)를 자가골 이식재 대용으로 사용하는 자가치아골이식(Autogenous Tooth Bone Graft, AUTO-BG)의 생물학·제작법·임상 적응증·합병증을 종합한 narrative review.

AUTO-BG는 자가 피질골(autogenous cortical bone)과 유사한 생체적합성·물리화학적 특성(BMP, type I 콜라겐, 인산칼슘)을 가지며, 보고된 결과로는 치조제 폭 증가 약 5mm, 상악동거상술 후 임플란트 생존율 약 96%, 골유도재생술(GBR)에서 골획득률 약 87%, 여러 case series를 종합한 전체 임플란트 실패율 약 2.3~4.0%.

합병증(창상개열, 감염 최대 9.1%, 혈종 3.64%, 5년간 변연골소실 약 2.5mm)은 타 이식재와 유사한 수준이나, 번거로운 chairside 제작 과정(전용 dentin grinder 필요), 치아당 확보 가능한 골이식재 양의 제한, 장기 RCT 데이터 부재로 인해 소규모 두개안면 결손에 국한된 틈새 옵션으로 남아있음.

## Summary

This 2022 narrative review consolidates evidence for autogenous tooth bone graft (AUTO-BG / AUTO-BT) — a technique, first described in 2003, that repurposes a patient's own extracted teeth as an autogenous bone-graft source without requiring a separate donor-site surgery. Human dentin's compositional similarity to bone (55% inorganic / 45% organic, dominated by type I collagen and calcium phosphates, with embedded BMPs and growth factors in its demineralized matrix) underpins the material's osteogenic, osteoinductive, and osteoconductive properties. The review covers preparation (block vs. powder type, the Smart Dentin Grinder chairside protocol), eight clinical application domains (bone/ridge/sinus augmentation, periodontal defects, GBR, alveolar cleft grafting, socket preservation, restorative/endodontic uses), and a structured complications catalogue, concluding that AUTO-BG performs comparably to allograft/xenograft/alloplast sources but remains a niche option due to preparation burden and thin long-term evidence.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates the biochemical rationale: dentin shares near-identical composition with bone (type I collagen, DSPP, osteopontin, osteonectin, embedded BMPs/growth factors in the demineralized dentin matrix), explaining AUTO-BG's osteogenic/osteoinductive/osteoconductive triad.
- Documents the standardized chairside preparation protocol via Smart Dentin Grinder (Kometa Bio): grind → NaOH/ethanol cleanser (7–10 min) → PBS wash (3 min) → graft-ready, plus the alternative high-temperature (1200°C) sintered "tooth ash" method.
- Maps AUTO-BG across eight clinical domains with outcome benchmarks per domain (see Results table), grounding indications in specific reported figures rather than general claims.
- Provides a quantified, 8-category complications catalogue (dehiscence, infection, hematoma, screw exposure, resorption, loss of primary stability, osseointegration failure, block fracture) directly comparable to other graft sources' known complication profiles.

## Methodology

Narrative (non-systematic) review of PubMed, MEDLINE, EMBASE, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar (search terms: "Autogenous tooth," "Tooth graft," "Tooth bone graft," "bone graft," "autogenous bone graft"). No PRISMA flow diagram, no formal risk-of-bias appraisal, no meta-analysis. Evidence is a first-author synthesis pooling case series, small clinical trials, one systematic review (Gharpure et al., animal primary-stability data only), and histological/SEM/XRD physical-property studies — largely from a small number of overlapping South Korean research groups (Kim, Lee, Jeong, Jun, Schwarz). Confidence classified as `narrative-review`; treat quantitative figures as reported-in-source-study values, not pooled meta-analytic estimates.

## Results

| Application | Key benchmark |
|---|---|
| Ridge augmentation | ~5 mm width gain; ~0.1 mm/yr resorption; implants placed within 26 weeks |
| Sinus augmentation | ~5 mm bone-height gain; ~0.76 mm/yr post-loading loss; ~96% implant survival |
| GBR | ~87% net bone gain, membrane use made no significant difference |
| Socket preservation | ~15% horizontal bone loss; negligible vertical loss |
| Periodontal furcation/intrabony defects | 1–2 mm probing-depth reduction, 3–4 mm linear bone gain; statistically comparable to DFDBA + chorion membrane |
| Full graft-to-bone replacement | 12–15 months |
| Bone density vs. Bio-Oss | 981 HU (AUTO-BG) vs. 968 HU (Bio-Oss); ~60% new-bone-volume ratio |
| ISQ | mean 67.3 at placement → mean 75.5 at second-stage surgery |

Pooled complication rates: wound dehiscence (most common; 2/9 and 1/12 patients in two case series), infection up to 9.1% (worst-case series, n=51; others report negligible), hematoma 3.64%, marginal/crestal bone loss ~2.5 mm over 5 years (or 0.28±0.13 mm vertical / 0.15±0.08 mm horizontal at 4–6 months per a separate review), loss of primary stability 11.6% (animal studies only, no human data), implant failure 2.3–4.0% (pooled human case series: 165 implants/72 patients, ~6 failures). Authors conclude these rates show no statistically significant difference from other commonly used grafting sources.

Key limitation acknowledged by the authors: tedious/technique-sensitive preparation, need for a specialized dentin grinder, limited graft quantity obtainable per tooth (restricts use to smaller defects), and absence of long-term RCT data — currently limiting AUTO-BG to niche/limited-defect clinical use.
