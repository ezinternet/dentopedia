## Three-line Summary

Retrospective pilot cohort (n=11 patients, 16 autogenous onlay bone grafts from the mandibular ascending ramus, 22 subsequent implants) assessed with 3D CBCT volumetric analysis (automated image registration) at baseline, immediately post-graft, and 12 months post-augmentation.

Mean graft volume fell from 372.2 ± 179.4 mm3 (immediate post-op) to 230.2 ± 185.7 mm3 at 12 months — 43.7% ± 19.0% resorption (p<0.001); resorption was significantly greater in the maxilla (60.2% ± 16.4%) than the mandible (37.6% ± 16.7%, p=0.01); age had no significant effect (p=0.175); no implants were lost.

Small, single-center pilot without a control group or long-term implant-outcome tracking beyond ~510 days; the volumetric methodology (automated registration) is more precise than prior linear/caliper protocols but the magnitude of resorption (~44%) is markedly higher than some prior reports (e.g., Kloss et al. 12.5%), underscoring the wide reported variability for onlay-graft resorption.

## Summary

This retrospective pilot study from University Medical Center Freiburg used precise 3D CBCT volumetric analysis — anchored by an automated image-registration procedure rather than manual linear/caliper measurement — to track resorption of autogenous cortico-cancellous onlay bone grafts harvested from the mandibular ascending ramus (retromolar donor site) prior to dental implant placement. Eleven patients with 16 grafts (4 maxilla, 12 mandible) were scanned at baseline, immediately post-augmentation, and 12 months post-augmentation, with two blinded examiners independently delineating graft volume (inter-observer ICC 0.999). At 12 months, mean graft volume had shrunk from 372.2 ± 179.4 mm3 to 230.2 ± 185.7 mm3, a highly significant 43.7% ± 19.0% resorption. Maxillary sites resorbed significantly more (60.2%) than mandibular sites (37.6%, p=0.01); patient age was not a significant predictor. Despite this substantial volumetric loss, all 22 implants placed into the healed grafts survived through the observation period (~510 days), and the paper's discussion situates its ~44% figure against a highly inconsistent prior literature (12.5% to 51% resorption at comparable timepoints across different graft sites and measurement protocols), attributing part of that variability to weaker (non-registered, manual, or gray-value-based) measurement methods used in earlier studies.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes a precise, automated-registration-based 3D CBCT volumetric resorption benchmark (43.7% ± 19.0% at 12 months) for autogenous ascending-ramus/retromolar onlay bone grafts — a donor site and technique distinct from the iliac-crest and chin-graft literature more commonly cited for autograft resorption.
- Demonstrates a statistically significant jaw-site effect (maxilla resorbs ~1.6x more than mandible for the same graft/surgical protocol), a variable often not isolated in prior onlay-graft resorption studies.
- Very high inter-observer reliability (ICC 0.999) validates the automated image-registration measurement protocol as more reproducible than manual slice-by-slice or gray-value-assignment approaches used in earlier studies (Lee and Kim; Kloss et al.).
- Confirms implant survival (0 losses, 22 implants, ~510-day observation) despite the large volumetric resorption, suggesting the graft's functional/mechanical adequacy at the time of implant placement (~104 days post-augmentation) is not fully captured by later volumetric shrinkage alone.

## Methodology

Retrospective single-center cohort. Screened 220 patients treated with retromolar (ascending ramus) autogenous bone grafts; 11 patients (10F/1M, mean age 53.0y, range 20-69) met inclusion criteria (alveolar crest width <3 mm, height >7 mm; no smoking, malignancy, antiresorptive/radio/chemotherapy, pregnancy/nursing, or bone-metabolism disease) and had CBCT scans available at all three required time points (T0 pre-augmentation, T1 immediate post-augmentation, T2 12 months post-augmentation). Surgery: crestal incision, cortico-cancellous block harvested via piezotome/chisel from the ascending ramus, adapted with diamond burs, fixed with two titanium screws, tension-free closure; perioperative clindamycin, ibuprofen, and 0.1% CHX rinse. CBCT (iCat Cone Beam 3D, 0.3 mm voxel, 5 mA, 120 kV) at each time point was aligned via automated rigid image registration (restricted to mandible or maxilla per site); the T0 bony contour was delineated and used as the region-of-interest reference against which grafted-bone volume at T1/T2 was measured by manual slice-by-slice axial delineation and automated interpolation (MeVisLab). Two blinded examiners measured independently. Statistics: paired t-test (baseline vs 12-month volume), linear regression with robust variance estimators (jaw, age effects); STATA 16, alpha=0.05; no formal power calculation (all eligible patients used, achievable effect size 2.3 for n=11).

## Results

- 11 patients, 16 grafts (4 maxilla, 12 mandible), 22 implants placed after 104.4 ± 11.5 days healing; prosthetic loading at 209.6 ± 24.6 days post-augmentation; 0 implant losses over 510.4 ± 180.2 days observation.
- Inter-observer ICC = 0.999 (p<0.0001).
- Graft volume: 372.2 ± 179.4 mm3 (T1, immediate post-op) → 230.2 ± 185.7 mm3 (T2, 12 months) = 43.7% ± 19.0% resorption (p<0.001).
- Jaw effect: mandible 37.6% ± 16.7% (113.7 ± 34.7 mm3 loss) vs maxilla 60.2% ± 16.4% (217.7 ± 56.2 mm3 loss), p=0.01.
- Age effect: regression coefficient -1.73 (older = slightly less loss), not significant, p=0.175.
- Comparative context from prior literature (cited in Discussion): Sbordone et al. 35-51% (iliac crest) / 45% (chin onlay); Kloss et al. 12.5% ± 7.8% at 12 months (mandibular single-tooth defects); Lee and Kim 25.4% at 5.5 months (ascending-ramus onlay + particulate cortical bone + fibrin sealant + collagen membrane).
