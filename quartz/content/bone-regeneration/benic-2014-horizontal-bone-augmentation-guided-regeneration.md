## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Benic & Hämmerle, Periodontology 2000, 2014) synthesizing evidence on guided bone regeneration (GBR) for horizontal alveolar ridge augmentation, covering barrier membranes, bone substitutes, defect classification (Class 1–5), and surgical techniques for both simultaneous and staged implant placement.

Native collagen membranes + deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM) are the established standard for most GBR indications; autogenous bone blocks are preferred for large horizontal defects requiring volume stability; titanium-reinforced e-PTFE membranes are reserved for Class 3 dehiscence defects without bone wall support.

As a 2014 narrative review without systematic search protocol or meta-analysis, quantitative outcome pooling is absent; the defect classification (Class 1–5) and material selection hierarchy remain clinically influential foundations for subsequent evidence synthesis.

## 세줄요약

Benic & Hämmerle (Periodontology 2000, 2014) 서술적 고찰 — 수평 치조골 증대를 위한 유도골재생 (Guided Bone Regeneration, GBR)의 차폐막·이식재·결손 분류 (Class 1–5)·수술 술식 총정리; 동시 식립과 단계적 프로토콜 모두 포괄.

천연 콜라겐막 + 탈단백 우골 무기질 (Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral, DBBM)이 대부분 GBR 적응증의 표준; 대형 수평 결손은 자가골 블록 권장; 골벽 지지 없는 Class 3 결손은 티타늄 강화 팽창 폴리테트라플루오로에틸렌 (expanded Polytetrafluoroethylene, e-PTFE) 차폐막 적용.

## Summary

Benic & Hämmerle (University of Zurich) authored this comprehensive narrative review for the GBR-focused issue of Periodontology 2000 (Vol. 66, 2014). The paper synthesizes the scientific and clinical basis of GBR for horizontal ridge augmentation, covering three interdependent domains: (1) barrier membranes, (2) bone grafts and substitutes, and (3) clinical defect classification with technique-specific protocols. It also surveys emerging research directions including growth factors and new scaffold biomaterials.

**Biological principle**: GBR is founded on the tissue-guided healing concept of Nyman & Karring (early 1980s): cells that first populate a wound determine the tissue type that forms. Barrier membranes exclude faster-migrating non-osteogenic cells (fibroblasts, epithelial cells) and allow slower-migrating osteoprogenitor cells to dominate the space.

## Key Contributions

**Membrane classification:**
- *Nonresorbable*: e-PTFE (first generation; porous, prone to infection on exposure), d-PTFE, Ti-reinforced e-PTFE (space-maintaining for large/challenging defects), Ti mesh
- *Resorbable — natural*: Native collagen (standard for most GBR; spontaneous re-epithelialization on exposure; major drawback = collapse resistance); cross-linked collagen (prolonged degradation; contrasting data on inflammation and dehiscence rate)
- *Resorbable — synthetic*: PLGA (comparable to native collagen in small defects, inferior to e-PTFE in larger ones), PEG hydrogel (in situ polymerizing; comparable to native collagen in RCT for dehiscence repair)

**Bone graft taxonomy (four origins):**

| Category | Origin | Key examples |
|---|---|---|
| Autograft | Same patient | Chin, retromolar ramus (intraoral); calvarial, iliac (extraoral) |
| Allograft | Same species | Freeze-dried bone, demineralized freeze-dried bone, block allograft |
| Xenograft | Other species | DBBM (bovine, gold standard), porcine, equine collagenated bone |
| Alloplast | Synthetic | TCP, HA, biphasic CaP, calcium sulfate, bioactive glass, PLGA |

DBBM: gold standard for GBR of dehiscence/fenestration defects and simultaneous implant placement; in block form, histologically shows moderate new bone formation at periphery with stable volume; residual DBBM particles found unchanged at 11 years after sinus floor augmentation.

**Defect classification (Class 1–5) — material/technique routing:**

| Class | Morphology | Material of choice | Approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Alveolar crest defect (extraction socket) | Bone substitute + resorbable membrane | Simultaneous |
| 2 | Peri-implant dehiscence, bone walls support volume | Particulate graft + resorbable membrane | Simultaneous |
| 3 | Peri-implant dehiscence, no bone wall support | DBBM + Ti-reinforced e-PTFE | Simultaneous |
| 4 | Combined horizontal + vertical | Case-dependent (per Class 3 or Class 5 logic) | Staged or simultaneous |
| 5 | Vertical ridge deficiency | Autogenous bone block ± graft + resorbable membrane | Staged (4–6 months before implant) |

**Primary (staged) ridge augmentation protocol**: Autogenous bone block (chin preferred for volume; retromolar ramus preferred for lower neurosensory risk) + DBBM particulate coverage + resorbable collagen membrane; donor site filled with bone substitute; flap coronally advanced for tension-free closure; 4–6 months healing.

## Methodology

Narrative review; not a systematic review. No explicit search protocol or PICO registration. Coverage: animal experiments (rat, dog, monkey) through human RCTs, systematic reviews, and consensus statements through approximately 2013. ~242 references. Lead author (Benic) and senior author (Hämmerle) from the University of Zurich ITI-affiliated group, directly involved in GBR clinical research.

## Results

- **Native collagen membranes**: Well-documented with low complication rates in both animal and human studies; current standard for most GBR indications. On mucosal dehiscence, spontaneous epithelialization occurs without surgical intervention — significant clinical advantage over e-PTFE.
- **Ti-reinforced e-PTFE**: Documented for large lateral and vertical augmentations; premature exposure → porous surface colonized by oral microbes rapidly → infection → early removal → impaired regeneration.
- **Block allograft + collagen membrane for atrophic anterior maxilla**: ~83 implants placed in one RCT after 6 months; mean 33 ± 18% new bone, 26 ± 17% residual allograft; 98.8% implant survival at mean 48 months.
- **DBBM block for horizontal augmentation**: 11/12 patients sufficient for prosthetically optimal implant placement at 9–10 months; firm integration at re-entry but moderate histological new bone formation.
- **Submerged vs. transmucosal healing**: Multiple RCTs report equivalent outcomes for implant survival, marginal bone level, and soft-tissue parameters; submerged mode loses ~1 mm keratinized mucosa width.
- **PLGA vs. Ti-reinforced e-PTFE**: In one multicenter RCT (n = 40): 81% mean vertical defect fill (PLGA) vs. 96% (Ti-reinforced e-PTFE) at 6-month re-entry; e-PTFE maintained horizontal thickness and had fewer soft-tissue complications.
- **Growth factors (2014 evidence horizon)**: BMP-2 in 6 clinical studies showed positive effect on local augmentation, sinus elevation, and horizontal ridge augmentation; PDGF promising in case series; GDF-5, PTH under evaluation; all require future RCTs.
- **Cortical perforation of recipient bed**: Recommended to allow early vascularization and improve bone repair.
