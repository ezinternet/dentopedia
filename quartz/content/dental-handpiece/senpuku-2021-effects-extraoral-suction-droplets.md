## Three-line Summary

Field study (3 healthy volunteers, 2 private-clinic + 1 university-hospital sessions, July–October 2020) comparing bacterial ATP (RLU) and colony-forming-unit (CFU) contamination around the dental chair across three suction conditions during 10-min simulated ultrasonic scaling: A = no suction, B = oral suction only, C = oral + extraoral suction.

Oral suction (B) and oral+extraoral suction (C) significantly reduced RLU at the left-desk position and reduced colony counts behind the chair head vs. no suction (A); extraoral suction (C) uniquely and significantly outperformed oral suction alone (B) at the left-behind-chair-head position, and no significant colonies appeared on BHI (oral-bacteria) plates, implying most droplets/aerosols originated from the ultrasonic scaler waterline rather than saliva.

Protection from combined oral+extraoral suction was spatially limited to the left and posterior sides of the dental chair head for a right-handed operator — the position where a chairside dental assistant typically stands — suggesting extraoral suction's main added value is protecting the assistant, with only 3 volunteers/sessions and no ethics-committee submission limiting generalizability.

## 세줄요약

건강한 자원자 3명 대상 실제 진료실 현장 연구(2020년 7~10월, 개인 치과의원 2회+대학병원 1회) — 10분 모의 초음파 스케일링(Ultrasonic Scaling) 중 흡인 조건 3군(A=무흡인, B=구강내흡인만, C=구강내+구강외흡인)별로 치과 유니트 주변 세균 ATP(RLU)·집락형성단위(Colony-Forming Unit, CFU) 오염도를 비교했다.

구강내흡인(B)·구강내+구강외흡인(C) 모두 좌측 데스크 위치 RLU와 체어 뒤쪽 집락수를 무흡인(A) 대비 유의하게 감소시켰고, 구강외흡인(C)은 체어 좌후방 위치에서 구강내흡인 단독(B)보다도 유의하게 우수했다; BHI(구강세균용) 배지에서는 유의한 집락이 없어, 비말·에어로졸이 타액보다 초음파 스케일러 급수관 물에서 주로 기원했음을 시사한다.

구강내+구강외흡인의 방어 효과는 우측잡이 술자 기준 체어 좌측·후방으로 공간적으로 제한되었으며 이는 체어사이드 보조인력이 주로 서는 위치와 일치해 구강외흡인의 핵심 이득이 보조인력 보호에 있음을 시사하나, 자원자 3명·세션 3회에 그치고 윤리위원회 심의 절차를 별도로 거치지 않아 일반화에 한계가 있다.

## Summary

This field study assessed whether extraoral suction adds infection-control value beyond standard intraoral (oral) suction during dental scaling, using biological contamination endpoints rather than physical particle counts. Three healthy volunteers (31–52 years old) underwent 10-minute simulated ultrasonic scaling (no actual tooth/calculus contact) at two Tokyo private-clinic sessions and one university-hospital session (July–October 2020), under three suction conditions: no suction (A), oral suction only (B), and oral plus extraoral suction (C). Contamination was measured two ways — ATP bioluminescence (relative light units, RLU) via surface swabs at 14 chairside positions, and bacterial colony-forming units (CFU) on open BHI and R2A culture plates placed radially around the chair. Both B and C significantly reduced RLU and colony counts at several positions relative to A, and critically, C (extraoral + oral) significantly outperformed B (oral alone) at the position left-behind the dental chair head — the location a chairside assistant typically occupies. The near-absence of BHI (oral-bacteria) colonies alongside positive R2A (waterline-bacteria) colonies suggests the simulated-scaling droplets were dominated by ultrasonic scaler irrigant water rather than patient saliva. Clinically, the findings support adding extraoral suction to standard oral suction specifically to protect chairside personnel, while acknowledging the small sample and lack of a dedicated ethics submission.

## Key Contributions

- Provides biological (ATP/RLU + CFU), not just physical/particle-count, evidence that extraoral suction reduces droplet/aerosol contamination beyond oral suction alone.
- Shows the added benefit of extraoral suction is spatially localized (left-behind the dental chair head), not a uniform whole-room effect.
- Suggests ultrasonic scaler waterline water, not saliva, is the dominant contamination source in simulated scaling (BHI negative, R2A positive).
- Frames the chairside dental assistant, who stands to the operator's left, as the primary beneficiary of extraoral suction.

## Methodology

Field (non-manikin) study: real dentists/hygienists performed 10-min simulated ultrasonic scaling (no tooth/calculus contact) on 3 healthy volunteers across 3 sessions (AO1 Dental Clinic, Tokyo ×2; Nihon University Graduate School of Dentistry at Matsudo ×1), 30 July–8 October 2020. Three suction groups: A (no oral, no extraoral suction), B (oral suction only), C (oral + extraoral suction, ARTEO unit). Two contamination assays: (1) ATP bioluminescence via LuciPac pen swabs at 14 named chairside positions, read on a Lumitester luminometer as RLU (cleanliness threshold <500 RLU); (2) bacterial CFU on open BHI agar (oral-bacteria-favoring) and R2A agar (waterline/heterotrophic-bacteria-favoring) plates placed radially at 0.5–1 m around the chair, opened before and closed 10 min after scaling. One-way ANOVA/Kruskal–Wallis across groups with Bonferroni post hoc (SPSS 24, p<0.05); no formal ethics-committee submission for this study (referenced a related prior approval, project code 424).

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| RLU, left-desk position (No. 5) | Only position significantly exceeding the 500-RLU threshold in group A; significantly lower in B and C vs. A (ANOVA); C numerically < B, not significant |
| CFU (R2A), behind chair head (No. 1) | Significantly lower in B and C vs. A (post hoc) |
| CFU (R2A), left-behind chair head (No. 7) | Significantly lower in C vs. both A and B (post hoc) — extraoral suction's distinct added benefit |
| BHI (oral-bacteria) plates | No significant differences among groups — droplets/aerosols inferred to be waterline-water-dominated, not saliva-dominated |
| Spatial pattern | Combined oral+extraoral suction protection confined to left and posterior sides of the chair head (right-handed operator) — the chairside-assistant position |
