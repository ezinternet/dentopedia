## Three-line Summary

This scoping review examined the efficacy of single-tuft toothbrushes (STBs) in removing dental plaque from challenging oral surfaces. The review identified 403 articles across 7 databases, with 9 studies (8 RCTs, 1 in-vitro) meeting inclusion criteria, evaluating STB effectiveness on interproximal molars, lingual surfaces, and orthodontic appliance areas.

STBs demonstrated superior plaque removal in hard-to-reach areas compared to conventional toothbrushes alone, particularly on buccal/lingual interproximal surfaces of molars and around orthodontic brackets. When combined with conventional brushes, STBs showed significant improvement (p<0.05), though they did not consistently outperform floss or interdental brushes across all metrics. User satisfaction was high among orthodontic patients, but awareness remained low (5.6% usage rate).

STBs are effective adjuncts for managing difficult-to-access oral regions but cannot fully replace conventional toothbrushes or other interdental devices. Clinical application should focus on specific patient populations (orthodontic, implant, partial edentulism) with targeted education programs to address the awareness-accessibility gap.

## 세줄요약

이 주제범위 문헌고찰은 첨단칫솔 (Single-Tuft Toothbrush, STB)의 접근이 어려운 구강 부위 치면세균막 제거 효과를 평가했다. 7개 데이터베이스에서 403개 문헌을 검색하여 9개 연구(무작위대조시험 8개, 체외실험 1개)를 선정했으며, 대구치 인접면, 설측면, 교정장치 주변 부위에서 STB의 효과를 분석했다.

첨단칫솔은 대구치 협·설측 인접면과 교정 브라켓 주변에서 일반칫솔 단독 사용보다 우수한 치면세균막 제거 효과를 보였으며, 일반칫솔과 병행使用时 통계적으로 유의한 개선이 있었다(p<0.05). 그러나 치실이나 치간칫솔에 비해 모든 지표에서 일관되게 우월하지는 않았다. 교정환자에서 사용 만족도는 높았으나, 인지도 부족으로 사용률은 5.6%에 불과했다.

첨단칫솔은 접근이 어려운 부위 관리에 효과적인 보조 도구이나, 일반칫솔이나 타 보조용품을 완전히 대체할 수는 없다. 교정·임플란트·부분 무치악 환자 등 특정 집단에 초점을 맞추고, 인지도 및 접근성 개선을 위한 교육 프로그램이 필요하다.

## Summary

This scoping review systematically synthesized evidence on the plaque removal efficacy of single-tuft toothbrushes (STBs) across challenging oral surfaces. Following Arksey & O'Malley's framework and Peters et al. guidelines, the authors searched 7 databases (PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, KCI, DBpia, KMBASE, RISS) using keywords including "single-tuft toothbrush," "end-tuft toothbrush," and Korean terms, covering literature from 1976 to November 2024. Of 403 initially identified records, 9 studies met inclusion criteria: 8 randomized controlled trials (4 crossover designs with ≥3-day washout) and 1 in-vitro experiment on zirconia crowns with artificial plaque. Four studies used a 72-hour plaque accumulation window to capture early biofilm before host immune activation, based on the Plaque Free Area (PLA) concept. Primary outcomes included plaque indices (O'Leary, PHP, Quigley & Hein), gingival bleeding index (GBI), and probing pocket depth (PPD).

STBs demonstrated significantly superior plaque removal on maxillary buccal and mandibular lingual interproximal surfaces of molars compared to conventional brushes alone (Lee & Moon, 2011: p<0.05). In orthodontic patients, combined STB + conventional brush use significantly reduced plaque on both anterior and posterior teeth versus conventional brush monotherapy (Cunha et al., 2018: p<0.05). However, STBs did not consistently outperform floss or interdental brushes across all metrics; Im (2019) found no statistically significant difference in O'Leary or PHP indices, while Kim et al. (2021) reported STBs were more effective at line angles whereas interdental brushes excelled at interproximal contacts. Chlorhexidine 0.2% rinsing failed to prevent plaque accumulation at 72 hours (Hasegawa et al., 2017), contradicting earlier reports. GBI improvements were inconsistent across studies, attributed to insufficient 72-hour window to detect inflammation changes. User satisfaction was highest for STBs among interdental devices, yet usage rate was only 5.6% (4/75 participants) due to low awareness and limited accessibility. The authors concluded STBs are effective adjuncts for hard-to-reach areas but cannot fully replace conventional brushes or other tools; clinical application should target specific patient populations with enhanced education programs.

## Key Contributions

- First scoping review synthesizing single-tuft toothbrush (STB) plaque removal efficacy from 9 studies (8 RCTs, 1 in-vitro) spanning 1976–2024
- STBs demonstrate superior plaque removal on molar interproximal surfaces and around orthodontic brackets compared to conventional brushes alone (p<0.05)
- STB + conventional brush combination significantly outperforms conventional brush monotherapy in orthodontic patients
- No statistically significant difference found between STB, floss, and interdental brushes in O'Leary and PHP indices (p>0.05)
- User satisfaction with STB is highest among interdental devices, but usage rate is only 5.6% due to low awareness
- 72-hour intervention window is insufficient to detect gingival bleeding index (GBI) changes
- Identified need for standardized STB use protocols and safety evaluations (gingival trauma, dentin hypersensitivity, wear)

## Methodology

This scoping review followed the Arksey & O'Malley (2005) framework and Peters et al. (2015) guidelines to map the evidence on single-tuft toothbrush (STB) plaque removal efficacy. Two reviewers independently screened 403 records from 7 databases (PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, KCI, DBpia, KMBASE, National Assembly Library, RISS) using search terms including "single-tuft toothbrush," "end-tuft toothbrush," "unituft toothbrush," "sulcus brush," and Korean translations. Inclusion criteria required primary research with plaque removal experiments, without age or comparator restrictions, covering journal articles, theses, and conference proceedings up to November 2024. Exclusion criteria included no full text, manufacturer comparison studies, non-English/Korean publications, and non-primary research. After title/abstract screening and full-text review, 9 studies were selected: 8 RCTs (4 crossover with ≥3-day washout) and 1 in-vitro experiment on zirconia crowns with artificial plaque. Four studies used a 72-hour plaque accumulation window to capture early biofilm before host immune activation, grounded in the Plaque Free Area (PLA) concept from Waerhaug (1952) and Maliska et al. (2006). Plaque was visualized with erythrosine disclosing agent, distinguishing visible plaque index (VPI) from disclosed plaque index (DPI). Outcomes included plaque indices (O'Leary, PHP, Quigley & Hein), gingival bleeding index (GBI), probing pocket depth (PPD), and user satisfaction. Data were charted using a descriptive-analytical method with standardized extraction into Excel; Zotero managed references.
