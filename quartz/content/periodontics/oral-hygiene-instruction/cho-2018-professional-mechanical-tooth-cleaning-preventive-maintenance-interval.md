## Three-line Summary

A prospective interventional study in 33 healthy adults receiving weekly professional mechanical tooth cleaning (PMTC) for 4 visits followed by 3‑ and 6‑month maintenance visits. Plaque control score (O’Leary index) and gingival index (Löe & Silness) were measured at each visit.

At the 4th weekly visit, plaque control score improved from 38.98 to 57.57 (p<0.001) and gingival index decreased from 1.55 to 0.85 (p<0.001). At 3‑month follow‑up, scores worsened (plaque 46.33, GI 1.15; p<0.05), but both remained improved vs baseline. At 6‑month follow‑up, plaque score was 48.93 (p<0.05 vs baseline) and GI was 1.01 (p<0.05 vs 3‑month).

The optimal maintenance interval based on participant questionnaire responses (54.5% chose 1 month) and the finding that hygiene ability declined at 3 months suggests a 1‑month interval when proper self‑care skills are not yet established. Use of phase‑contrast microscopy and evaluation index scores showed motivational potential.

## 세줄요약

건강한 성인 33명을 대상으로 주간 전문가 치면세정술 (Professional Mechanical Tooth Cleaning, PMTC)을 4회 방문 동안 시행하고, 이후 3개월 및 6개월 유지관리 방문을 진행한 전향적 중재 연구. 각 방문에서 치면세균막지수 (O’Leary index)와 치은염지수 (Löe & Silness index)를 측정하였다.

4번째 주간 방문에서 치면세균막관리점수는 38.98에서 57.57로 향상되었고 (p<0.001), 치은염지수는 1.55에서 0.85로 감소하였다 (p<0.001). 3개월 추적관리 시 점수는 악화되었으나 (치면세균막 46.33, 치은염 1.15; p<0.05), 두 지표 모두 기준치보다는 개선된 상태를 유지하였다. 6개월 추적관리 시 치면세균막점수는 48.93 (기준 대비 p<0.05), 치은염지수는 1.01 (3개월 대비 p<0.05)이었다.

참가자 설문 응답 (54.5%가 1개월 선택)과 3개월 시점에서 위생 관리 능력이 저하된 결과를 바탕으로, 올바른 자가관리 능력이 확립되지 않은 상태에서의 적정 관리주기는 1개월로 제안되었다. 위상차현미경과 평가점수 산출이 동기부여에 효과적인 것으로 나타났다.

## Summary

This prospective study enrolled 33 healthy adults to evaluate the efficacy and optimal maintenance interval of a preventive program centered on professional mechanical tooth cleaning (PMTC). Participants received weekly PMTC and toothbrushing instruction over 4 visits, followed by 3‑month and 6‑month maintenance appointments. Plaque control score (O’Leary) and gingival index (Löe & Silness) were assessed at each visit. At the 4th weekly visit, plaque control score significantly improved from 38.98 to 57.57 (p<0.001) and gingival index decreased from 1.55 to 0.85 (p<0.001). However, at 3‑month follow‑up, both measures worsened (plaque: 46.33, GI: 1.15; p<0.05 vs Visit 4), though they remained better than baseline. At 6‑month follow‑up, plaque score was 48.93 (p<0.05 vs baseline) and GI was 1.01 (p<0.05 vs 3‑month). Participants with greater interest in dental health showed better plaque control. The use of phase‑contrast microscopy and regular feedback of evaluation scores were identified as motivational aids, though their effects were not statistically significant (p>0.05). Questionnaire responses indicated that 54.5% of participants recommended a 1‑month maintenance interval. The authors concluded that while the PMTC‑based preventive program is effective, a 1‑month interval may be more suitable when patients have not yet developed adequate self‑care skills, and that re‑education at 3‑month intervals is valuable.

## Key Contributions

- Four weekly PMTC sessions significantly improved plaque control and gingival health in healthy adults (p<0.001).
- At 3‑month follow‑up, oral hygiene ability declined, indicating that a 1‑month maintenance interval may be preferable until proper self‑care skills are established.
- Phase‑contrast microscopy and evaluation index feedback show potential as motivational tools, though not statistically significant.
- Participant interest in dental health correlates with better plaque control outcomes.
- 54.5% of participants selected 1 month as the ideal maintenance interval in post‑program questionnaires.
- Six‑month follow‑up showed partial recovery of gingival health versus 3‑month, supporting the importance of re‑education.

## Methodology

This was a prospective interventional study with repeated measures, conducted at a clinical research institute in a private dental clinic in Busan, South Korea. Healthy adults (n=33, mean age 40s, 84.9% college graduates) were recruited via the clinic website. Exclusion criteria included multiple untreated caries requiring endodontic treatment or advanced periodontitis with bone loss. All participants received baseline scaling and rotary brushing instruction. The intervention consisted of weekly PMTC using a low‑speed handpiece with EVA tip and rubber cup, plus toothbrushing education, from Visit 1 to Visit 4. Motivational aids included phase‑contrast microscopy at Visit 1 to visualize oral bacteria, and provision of evaluation index scores (via verbal feedback and text messages) at follow‑ups. Maintenance visits occurred at 3 and 6 months. Outcomes were plaque control score (O’Leary index, assessed on 4 surfaces of 6 selected teeth) and gingival index (Löe & Silness, assessed on 4 surfaces per tooth). Statistical analyses included paired t‑tests for within‑group changes, independent t‑tests for group comparisons, and one‑way ANOVA for motivation method effects, using PASW Statistics v.18.0.

Post‑program questionnaire indicated that 54.5% of subjects recommended a 1‑month maintenance interval, 39.4% recommended 3 months, and 6.1% recommended 6 months.
