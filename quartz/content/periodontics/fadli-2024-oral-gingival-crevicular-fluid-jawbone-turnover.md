## Three-line Summary

Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR, 37 observational studies, 10-year search August 2014–2024) mapping oral-fluid bone-turnover biomarkers across periodontitis, MRONJ, and osteoporosis in GCF and saliva.

GCF key markers are IL-1β, sclerostin, OPG, and IL-34 (mainly from periodontitis/osteoporosis studies); saliva key markers are OC, IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, OPG, and MMP-9 spanning all three diseases; no human GCF bone-turnover data exist for MRONJ.

GCF and saliva hold promise as non-invasive bone-turnover diagnostics, but all markers are systemic rather than oral-cavity-exclusive, limiting disease-specific diagnostic precision; MRONJ GCF studies in humans are an unmet gap.

GCF·타액은 비침습 골대사 진단 도구로서 가능성이 있으나, 모든 표지자가 전신성으로 질환 특이 진단 정밀도가 낮고, 인체 MRONJ GCF 연구는 미개척 분야로 남아 있다.

## Summary

Bone turnover in the jawbone underlies three distinct diseases — periodontitis, medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ, formerly BRONJ), and osteoporosis. Conventional bone-turnover markers are measured in blood/serum, but this scoping review asks whether **oral fluids** — gingival crevicular fluid (GCF, an inflammatory exudate from periodontal tissue) and saliva — can serve as non-invasive alternatives.

Following PRISMA-ScR and the JBI manual, the authors searched five databases plus grey literature (August 2014–August 2024), screened 14,965 + 34 citations with Rayyan and EndNote, and included **37 observational studies**. Risk of bias was appraised with RoBANS 2. The synthesis maps which markers appear in GCF versus saliva and which overlap across the three diseases.

The headline finding is convergence: **GCF's most significant markers are IL-1β, sclerostin, OPG, and IL-34** (chiefly from periodontitis and osteoporosis studies), while **saliva's are OC, IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, OPG, and MMP-9** (spanning all three diseases, including the only MRONJ signal). Because GCF is a component of saliva, many markers overlap and saliva is the more convenient medium. A key gap: **no human GCF bone-turnover biomarker study for MRONJ exists** — only an animal model. No biomarker is oral-cavity-exclusive; all are systemic, so oral fluid reflects overall bone-metabolic status but cannot yet make a precise, disease-specific diagnosis from a single marker.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates oral-fluid (saliva) and GCF bone-turnover biomarkers across periodontitis, MRONJ/BRONJ, and osteoporosis in one map — rather than the usual blood/serum sources.
- Defines the significant panels: GCF = IL-1β, sclerostin, OPG, IL-34; saliva = OC, IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, OPG, MMP-9.
- Documents extensive GCF–saliva overlap and argues saliva is the more practical sampling medium (GCF is part of saliva).
- Frames periodontitis as the shared inflammatory crossroads: IL-1β/IL-6/TNF-α are common to all three diseases, and periodontal inflammation is a risk factor for MRONJ.
- Surfaces the MRONJ GCF evidence gap (no human studies) and the general limitation that no oral-fluid marker is disease-specific.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Scoping review, JBI manual, reported per PRISMA-ScR; OSF-registered (20 Sept 2024).
- **Search**: Web of Science, EBSCOhost Dentistry & Oral Sciences Source, Cochrane Library, Scopus, PubMed + grey literature (Google Scholar); Aug 2014–Aug 2024.
- **Eligibility**: Bone-turnover biomarkers of osteoporosis, MRONJ, or periodontitis measured in oral fluid/GCF; English; full text; RCT, case-control, analytical observational (cohort), cross-sectional, or case series; ≥10 cases and ≥10 controls. Excluded systematic reviews, case reports, commentaries, editorials, letters.
- **Screening**: Rayyan + EndNote X20; two independent reviewers; consensus/third-reviewer arbitration.
- **Quality**: RoBANS 2 across 7 domains.

## Results

**Flow**: 14,965 (databases) + 34 (grey) citations → 5,030 + 26 after 10-year limit and de-duplication → 136 + 26 at eligibility → **37 included**. Study distribution: periodontitis > MRONJ/BRONJ > osteoporosis.

**GCF (18 studies)**
- Periodontitis: sclerostin ↑; TRAP/TRAcP-5b ↑ (osteoclast resorption); DPD ↑; interleukins/TNF-α/MMPs ↑ scaling with stage (healthy < gingivitis < periodontitis); IL-34 ↑; IL-1β + IL-37 ↑.
- MRONJ: **no human GCF bone-turnover studies**; only an animal model (IL-36α ↑ in inflamed periodontal tissue).
- Osteoporosis: OPG ↑ after 3–6 months bisphosphonate treatment (post-menopausal); IL-6 and RANKL ↑ when chronic periodontitis coexists with osteoporosis.

**Saliva (24 studies)**
- Periodontitis: CTX, OC, ON ↑ (correlate with PPD/BOP/bone loss); IL-1β, IL-6, MMP-8 ↑; RANKL/OPG ratio ↑; OSM, LIF, IL-11 ↑ (decline after periodontal treatment); MIP-1α, MMP-2, MMP-9 ↑.
- MRONJ: MMP-9, TNF-α, IL-6 ↑ (also periodontal-bone-loss drivers); periodontal inflammation posited as MRONJ risk factor.
- Osteoporosis: BAP, OC, NTX, CTX ↑; calcium, OC, ALP ↑ in male osteoporotic patients.

**Marker taxonomy (Table 3)**: formation (osteoblast) = OC, BAP, (P1NP*); regulators = RANKL, OPG, sclerostin; resorption (osteoclast) = CTX, NTX, TRAcP-5b, DPD, calcium, ICTP. P1NP, BSP, and OP (*) were absent from all included studies.

**Overlap & appraisal**: IL-1β is the most-studied marker in both GCF and saliva; OC and CTX are the most-studied salivary proteins. No biomarker is oral-cavity-exclusive — all are systemic, limiting oral-fluid-only diagnostic specificity, though they mirror overall health. Risk of bias was high for "confounders" and "blinding of assessors" across all studies.
