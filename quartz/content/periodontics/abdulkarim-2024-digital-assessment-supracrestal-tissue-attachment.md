## Three-line Summary

Retrospective in-vivo digital-superimposition study (DICOM+STL, 19 patients / 203 teeth) measuring supracrestal tissue attachment dimension (STAD) non-invasively by combining CBCT, intraoral scan, and clinical probing in periodontally healthy implant-planning patients.

Mean STAD was 2.05 mm (range 0.02–5.85 mm); mandibular teeth were larger than maxillary (p=0.001) and anterior/canine larger than posterior (p<0.001); STAD correlated inversely with buccal bone thickness (r=−0.419), gingival thickness (r=−0.283), and keratinized tissue width (r=−0.138).

A thin phenotype (thin bone + thin gingiva + narrow keratinized tissue) carries a larger STAD, meaning the biologic width that must be respected is patient- and tooth-specific — not a universal 2 mm.

## 세줄요약

후향적 in-vivo 디지털 중첩 연구(DICOM+STL, 19명/203치): CBCT·구강 스캔·임상 탐침을 병합해 초치조상부 조직부착 치수(Supracrestal Tissue Attachment Dimension, STAD)를 비침습적으로 측정.

평균 STAD 2.05 mm(범위 0.02–5.85 mm); 하악>상악(p=0.001), 전치·견치>구치(p<0.001); 협측 골두께(Buccal Bone Thickness, BBT, r=−0.419)·치은두께(Gingival Thickness, GT, r=−0.283)·각화치은폭(Keratinized Tissue Width, KTW, r=−0.138)과 역상관.

얇은 표현형(Thin Phenotype, 얇은 골·치은·좁은 각화치은 공동 발현)에서 STAD가 더 크므로, biologic width는 모든 치아에 2 mm 고정 적용이 아닌 환자·치아별 개별 평가가 필요함.

## Summary

This study measured supracrestal tissue attachment (STA, the renamed "biologic width") non-invasively by superimposing CBCT (DICOM) hard-tissue data on intraoral-scan (STL) soft-tissue surfaces, combined with clinical mid-facial probing, in 19 periodontally healthy implant-planning patients (203 teeth). The overall mean STA dimension (STAD) was 2.05 mm — closely matching Gargiulo's classic histologic 2.04 mm — but the range was very wide (0.02–5.85 mm), and STADs varied systematically by tooth position: larger in the mandible than the maxilla (mean difference 0.44 mm, p=0.001) and larger in anterior/canine than posterior teeth in both arches. STADs correlated inversely with buccal bone thickness (BBT, r=−0.419), gingival thickness (GT, r=−0.283), tooth width (r=−0.373), and keratinized tissue width (KTW, r=−0.138), and positively with the CEJ-to-bone-crest distance (r=0.574). BBT, GT, and KTW clustered together (BBT–GT r=0.679) into what the authors call a "phenotypic package": a thin phenotype (thin bone + thin gingiva + narrow keratinized tissue) carries a **larger** STA, whereas a thick phenotype carries a smaller one. The clinical message is that the attachment dimension a clinician must respect when placing a subgingival restorative margin or planning crown lengthening is patient- and tooth-specific, not a fixed 2 mm.

## Key Contributions

- First combination of CBCT + intraoral scan + clinical probing for **noninvasive in-vivo** STA measurement (prior data came from cadavers or invasive bone sounding).
- Quantitative demonstration that STA is **not universally 2.04 mm** — it varies by tooth position and phenotype (range 0.02–5.85 mm).
- The "phenotypic package": thin BBT + thin GT + narrow KTW co-occur and predict a **larger** STA; thick phenotype predicts a smaller STA.
- Practical corollary: mandibular teeth, anterior teeth, and thin-phenotype sites have the largest, most easily violated STAs.

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective observational digital-measurement study; IRB-approved (A.T. Still University).
- **Sample**: 19 periodontally healthy patients (14 F / 5 M; mean age 52.7 y), 203 teeth grouped MxA/MxC/MxP/MnA/MnC/MnP. Altered passive eruption, recession, restorations, wear, and periodontal disease excluded.
- **Technique**: CBCT (DICOM) + IOS (STL) best-fit superimposition (Blue Sky Plan); mid-facial probe depth (MFPD) and KTW measured clinically. **STAD = FGM–BC − MFPD**, where FGM–BC = FGM–CEJ + CEJ–BC.
- **Stats**: one-way ANOVA + Bonferroni post hoc; Pearson correlation + linear regression; SPSS v29; digital-examiner ICC 0.99.
- **Limitations**: small n, retrospective, uncalibrated clinical examiner, CBCT resolution loss on magnification, molars measured on mesial roots only, mid-facial sites only.

## Results

| Tooth position | Mean STAD (mm) | SD | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall (n=203) | **2.05** | 0.99 | 0.02–5.85 |
| Maxillary anterior (MxA) | 2.34 | 0.73 | 0.86–4.36 |
| Maxillary canine (MxC) | 2.12 | 1.04 | 0.82–4.73 |
| Maxillary posterior (MxP) | 1.34 | 0.85 | 0.02–3.80 |
| Mandibular anterior (MnA) | 2.56 | 0.72 | 1.44–4.19 |
| Mandibular canine (MnC) | 2.70 | 1.19 | 1.06–5.85 |
| Mandibular posterior (MnP) | 1.57 | 0.76 | 0.12–3.67 |

Key correlations with STAD: FGM–BC r=+0.830, CEJ–BC r=+0.574 (positive); MFPD r=−0.480, BBT r=−0.419, tooth width r=−0.373, GT r=−0.283, KTW r=−0.138 (negative). No association with age (p=0.272) or gender (p=0.558). Mandibular > maxillary STAD (p=0.001); anterior/canine > posterior in both arches (p<0.001). BBT, GT, KTW mutually correlated (BBT–GT r=0.679) and inversely with CEJ–BC.
