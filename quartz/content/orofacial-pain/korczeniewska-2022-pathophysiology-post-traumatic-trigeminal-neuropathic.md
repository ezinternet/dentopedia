## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of the peripheral pathophysiology of post-traumatic trigeminal neuropathic pain (PTNP), based primarily on animal (mostly rodent, largely infraorbital-nerve-injury) models, deliberately excluding central mechanisms.

PTNP arises in only ~3% of trigeminal nerve injuries (3.3% after facial fractures, <8% after implant therapy, 3–13% after endodontic treatment) via a peripheral cascade: inflammatory-mediator release → receptor/channel phosphorylation → chemokine signaling (CCL2, CXCL13, CXCL10) → peripheral sensitization → ectopic spontaneous activity in Aβ/Aδ/C fibers → ion channel alterations (Nav1.8/1.9, K+, T-type Ca2+) → blood-nerve-barrier disruption → satellite glial cell (SGC) activation/ganglionic sensitization → phenotypic Aβ-fiber switching (allodynia); oxytocin and specific microRNAs modulate this cascade.

Central mechanisms are explicitly excluded as "outside the scope" due to complexity, and most findings are extrapolated from spinal (not trigeminal-specific) nerve injury models — a major evidence-quality limitation the authors themselves flag.

## 세줄요약

외상후 삼차신경병증성 통증 (Post-Traumatic Trigeminal Neuropathic Pain, PTNP)의 말초 병태생리를 다룬 내러티브 리뷰 — 주로 설치류 안와하신경 만성 압박손상 (Infraorbital Nerve Chronic Constriction Injury, ION-CCI) 동물모델 기반, 중추 기전은 의도적으로 제외.

삼차신경 손상 중 약 3%만 PTNP로 진행(안면골절 후 3.3%, 임플란트 <8%, 근관치료 3–13%); 염증매개물질 방출→수용체/채널 인산화→케모카인 신호(CCL2, CXCL13, CXCL10)→말초감작 (Peripheral Sensitization)→Aβ/Aδ/C 섬유의 이소성 자발활동 (Ectopic Spontaneous Activity)→이온채널 변화(Nav1.8/1.9, K+, T-type Ca2+)→혈액-신경장벽 붕괴→위성신경교세포 (Satellite Glial Cell, SGC) 활성화/신경절감작→Aβ섬유 표현형전환(이질통, Allodynia)의 캐스케이드; 옥시토신(Oxytocin, OXT)·마이크로RNA가 이를 조절.

중추 기전은 저자들이 명시적으로 "범위 밖"이라 밝히며 제외했고, 대부분의 근거가 삼차신경 특이적이 아닌 척수신경 손상 모델에서 외삽된 것 — 저자 스스로 인정하는 핵심 근거 한계.

## Summary

Post-traumatic trigeminal neuropathic pain (PTNP) is defined by the ICOP (International Classification of Orofacial Pain) as unilateral or bilateral facial/oral pain following trauma to the trigeminal nerve(s), persisting or recurring for more than 3 months, with other signs/symptoms of trigeminal nerve dysfunction. It can follow major craniofacial trauma or — more commonly in dental practice — iatrogenic injury from implant placement, endodontic treatment, third molar extraction, or local anesthetic injection. Only a minority of trigeminal nerve injuries (~3%) progress to painful neuropathy; most resolve with no residual deficit or produce a non-painful neuropathy.

This review synthesizes the peripheral cascade underlying that minority progression, drawing mainly on rodent (largely infraorbital nerve chronic constriction injury, ION-CCI) models: injury triggers release of inflammatory mediators (bradykinin, PGE2, serotonin, ATP, H+) that phosphorylate sodium (Nav1.8/1.9) and reduce potassium channel currents, lowering activation thresholds (peripheral sensitization). Chemokines (CCL2, CXCL13, CXCL2, CXCL10) recruit macrophages and amplify nociceptor excitability. Sensitized axons/ganglion cell bodies generate ectopic spontaneous activity, with a pathological phenotypic switch in large-diameter Aβ fibers (normally touch/vibration) toward substance-P expression and nociceptive signaling — the proposed substrate for allodynia. Blood-nerve-barrier disruption (via Hedgehog/TLR4 signaling) permits immune cell infiltration and neuroinflammation; bacterial LPS priming can accelerate and worsen allodynia via TLR4 signaling, while (paradoxically) local P. gingivalis exposure showed an anti-nociceptive, IL-10-mediated effect in one rat model. Satellite glial cells (SGCs) surrounding the trigeminal ganglion (TG) proliferate (peaking ~4 days post-injury), upregulate GFAP, and drive ganglionic (intra-ganglion, cell-to-cell) sensitization via substance-P/NK1-receptor signaling and proinflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α). Oxytocin, administered intraganglionically, attenuates hypersensitivity via vasopressin-1A-receptor interaction, K+-channel recovery, and TRPV1/TRPV4 inhibition — an emerging analgesic mechanism. Multiple microRNAs (miR-146b-5p, miR-384, miR-155-5p, miR-132-3p, miR-186, miR-32-5p, miR-195, miR-21, miR-223-3p) regulate this cascade, some (miR-21) specifically through blood-nerve-barrier impairment independent of inflammation.

The review is explicit about its own boundary: central mechanisms, though co-dependent with peripheral ones, are excluded "due to their complexity" — this paper covers half of PTNP pathophysiology by design, and most cited mechanistic evidence is extrapolated from spinal (DRG) rather than trigeminal-specific nerve-injury models, a gap the authors call for future research to close.

## Key Contributions

- Organizes the peripheral pathophysiological cascade of PTNP specific to the trigeminal system: receptors/inflammatory soup → chemokines → peripheral sensitization → spontaneous activity → ion channel alterations → neurovascular/blood-nerve-barrier disruption → microbial priming → ganglionic (SGC) sensitization → phenotypic Aβ-fiber switching (allodynia).
- First narrative synthesis to organize oxytocin's peripheral analgesic mechanism (V1A-R interaction, K+ channel recovery, TRPV1/TRPV4 inhibition) and microRNA regulation specifically in the PTNP/trigeminal context.
- Reports dentally-relevant iatrogenic PTNP incidence: facial fracture 3.3%, implant therapy <8%, non-surgical endodontic treatment 3–13%, surgical endodontic therapy ~5%.
- Explicitly documents trigeminal-vs-spinal system differences (e.g., sympathetic fiber sprouting reported in DRG but not TG) as the rationale for why spinal-model extrapolation is imperfect and trigeminal-specific research is needed.

## Methodology

Narrative (non-systematic) literature review — no PRISMA protocol, no reported search strategy, no quantitative synthesis. Evidence is drawn predominantly from rodent (rat/mouse) infraorbital nerve chronic constriction injury (ION-CCI) and trigeminal ganglion (TG) models, supplemented by extrapolation from spinal/dorsal root ganglion (DRG) nerve-injury literature where trigeminal-specific data are lacking.

## Results

- **Epidemiology**: ~3% of trigeminal nerve injuries overall progress to PTNP; 3.3% after facial fractures; <8% after implant therapy; 3–13% after non-surgical endodontic treatment; ~5% after surgical endodontic therapy.
- **Clinical course**: onset typically 5th decade, female predominance; poor prognosis — only 10–20% report significant improvement, 30% some improvement, 50% worsen or unchanged at long-term follow-up.
- **Peripheral sensitization drivers**: TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-1beta, TRPV1, NGF, P2X/P2Y, neurokinin, CGRP, glutamatergic signaling; Nav1.8/Nav1.9 phosphorylation increases firing; reduced K+ (IA, IK) current increases spike frequency/duration in TG neurons.
- **Chemokine signaling**: CCL2 (via CCR2, recruits macrophages, boosts Nav1.8 activity), CXCL13/CXCR5 (ERK-mediated cytokine production, p38 MAPK), CXCL2 (mechanical allodynia), CXCL10/CXCR3 (ERK/AKT signaling) — all implicated in trigeminal neuropathic pain maintenance.
- **Ganglionic sensitization**: SGC proliferation peaks ~4 days post-injury around ATF-3+ neurons; GFAP upregulation, CD45/CD163 expression (macrophage/leukocyte markers); Connexin 43 and glutamate synthase silencing alters nociceptive responses — SGCs function as a "first relay point" amplifying afferent signals toward central sensitization.
- **Oxytocin**: intra-TG administration attenuates orofacial mechanical hypersensitivity via V1A-R interaction, K+ channel functional recovery, and TRPV1/TRPV4 inhibition; suppresses CGRP, IL-1B, TNFa upregulation in TG and spinal trigeminal nucleus caudalis.
- **MicroRNAs**: miR-146b-5p/-384/-155-5p/-132-3p elevated in trigeminal-neuralgia patient serum; miR-186 suppresses NLRP3 inflammasome signaling; miR-32-5p downregulation (Cav3.2 regulation) and miR-195 upregulation (Sonic Hedgehog/Patched1) promote neuropathic pain; miR-21 elevation drives blood-nerve-barrier leakage (claudin-1 loss, MMP9 rise) independent of inflammation; miR-223-3p alleviates pain via MKNK2/MAPK-ERK suppression.
- **Bacterial priming**: LPS/TLR4 signaling disrupts the blood-nerve barrier and accelerates/worsens allodynia (IL-1B, iNOS elevation) — pre-existing inflammation is a proposed risk factor for PTNP. Conversely, local P. gingivalis exposure showed an anti-nociceptive, IL-10-mediated effect in one rat pain model, a notable non-linear exception.

## Limitations

- **Central mechanisms explicitly excluded** — the authors state this review covers only peripheral events "due to their complexity"; PTNP pathophysiology is peripheral AND central, co-dependent, so this paper deliberately addresses only half the picture. Any clinical model built on this page alone should not be read as a complete PTNP mechanism.
- Nearly all cited mechanistic evidence derives from **rodent spinal/DRG models, not trigeminal-specific models**, with repeated caveats that the two systems differ (e.g., DRG-exclusive sympathetic fiber sprouting).
- Narrative (non-systematic) methodology — no search protocol, no quality appraisal, confidence graded accordingly (`narrative-review`).
- PTNP prevalence estimates are explicitly flagged by the authors as unreliable due to inconsistent diagnostic terms/criteria across studies.
