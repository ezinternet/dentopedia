## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 10 studies (1,350 patients, 1,623 implants) comparing immediate implant placement (IIP) outcomes between diabetic (well-/poorly-controlled) and healthy patients, follow-up ≥6 months.

Implant survival showed no significant difference regardless of glycemic control (RR=1.00 well-controlled, RR=0.96 poorly-controlled, both NS), but marginal bone loss and bleeding-on-probing were significantly higher in diabetics, worsening with poorer control (poorly-controlled MBL MD=0.39mm, BOP MD=0.24, both P<0.00001).

IIP is clinically feasible in diabetics with survival comparable to healthy individuals, but poor glycemic control carries a measurably higher risk of peri-implant bone loss and inflammation, so strict perioperative glycemic management is essential.

당뇨(양호·불량 혈당조절) 환자와 건강 대조군의 즉시식립(Immediate Implant Placement, IIP) 결과를 비교한 10편 체계적고찰+메타분석 (환자 1,350명, 임플란트 1,623개, 추적 ≥6개월).

임플란트 생존율은 혈당조절 수준과 무관하게 유의차 없음(양호 조절군 RR=1.00, 불량 조절군 RR=0.96, 모두 비유의)이나, 변연골소실(MBL)과 탐침출혈(BOP)은 당뇨군에서 유의하게 높고 혈당조절이 나쁠수록 악화(불량군 MBL MD=0.39mm, BOP MD=0.24, 모두 P<0.00001).

당뇨 환자에서도 IIP는 임상적으로 시행 가능하며 생존율은 건강군과 대등하지만, 혈당조절이 불량하면 골소실·염증 위험이 유의하게 높아 술 전후 엄격한 혈당관리가 필수적이다.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 10 studies (1,350 patients, 1,623 implants, follow-up ≥6 months) comparing immediate implant placement (IIP) outcomes between diabetic patients (stratified into well- and poorly-controlled) and systemically healthy controls. The authors evaluated four outcomes: implant survival rate (SR), marginal bone loss (MBL), probing depth (PD), and bleeding on probing (BOP). Implant survival was statistically comparable between diabetics and healthy patients regardless of glycemic control level, supporting the clinical feasibility of IIP even in diabetic patients. However, both MBL and BOP were significantly higher in diabetic patients, with the magnitude of the difference scaling with glycemic control quality — poorly-controlled diabetics showed roughly 5× the MBL difference of well-controlled diabetics versus healthy patients. The authors conclude IIP is feasible in diabetics, particularly with good glycemic control, but strict perioperative glucose management is essential to minimize peri-implant bone loss and inflammation risk.

## Key Contributions

- First meta-analysis specifically isolating IIP (rather than implants in general) as the intervention when comparing diabetic vs healthy outcomes.
- Stratifies diabetic patients by glycemic control quality (well- vs poorly-controlled), revealing a control-dependent gradient in MBL/PD/BOP that a pooled "diabetic vs healthy" analysis would obscure.
- Confirms implant survival is not the sensitive outcome for diabetic risk in IIP — peri-implant soft/hard tissue health markers (MBL, BOP) are.

## Methodology

- **Design**: SR+MA, PICO framework, PubMed/Embase/Web of Science/Cochrane/Google Scholar/CNKI searched Jan 2000–Mar 2025.
- **Included**: 10 studies (1,337 initially retrieved → 900 post-dedup → 84 full-text reviewed → 10 included), 23–655 patients per study (168–922 implants), follow-up 6 months to 15 years.
- **Comparison**: diabetic (well-/poorly-controlled) vs healthy patients, both receiving IIP.
- **Quality**: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale; pooling via RevMan 5.4, random-effects model when I²≥50%; subgroup analysis by glycemic control and follow-up duration; sensitivity analysis (leave-one-out); funnel plot for publication bias.

## Results

- **Survival rate**: well-controlled DM vs healthy RR=1.00 (95% CI 0.97–1.02, P=0.79); poorly-controlled DM vs healthy RR=0.96 (95% CI 0.88–1.06, P=0.47) — no significant difference in either group.
- **MBL**: well-controlled DM MD=0.08mm (95% CI 0.03–0.14, P=0.004); poorly-controlled DM MD=0.39mm (95% CI 0.25–0.53, P<0.00001) — both significant, worsening with poorer control.
- **PD**: well-controlled DM not significant (MD=0.17mm, P=0.09); poorly-controlled DM significant (MD=0.62mm, 95% CI 0.38–0.86, P<0.00001).
- **BOP**: well-controlled DM MD=0.12 (P<0.00001); poorly-controlled DM MD=0.24 (95% CI 0.23–0.24, P<0.00001).
- Notable between-study heterogeneity flagged by the authors as a limitation on the precision of these pooled estimates.
