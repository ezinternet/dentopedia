## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PROSPERO #49604; OVID MEDLINE/PubMed/EMBASE/Cochrane to May 2017; no meta-analysis — instrument heterogeneity) of 9 studies (1 RCT, remainder cohorts/case series; 1,102 references screened) on patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) comparing immediate vs early/conventional loading of immediately placed (Type 1) single-tooth implants in partially edentulous patients.

Only 3 of 9 studies supplied comparative data; across PROMs (VAS, satisfaction indices, OHRQoL) patient satisfaction was uniformly high with little difference between loading protocols, and qualitative evidence showed a positive OHRQoL impact from immediate placement and loading.

Heterogeneity prevented meta-analysis and any definitive protocol preference, but immediate loading is well-accepted from the patient's perspective and warrants clinical consideration; future immediate-loading RCTs should embed standardized PROM instruments (OHIP-14, validated VAS).

## Summary

This systematic review (PROSPERO #49604, registered Oct 2016) is the first to answer a focused PICO question on **patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs)** comparing immediate versus early/conventional loading for **immediately placed (Type 1) single-tooth implants** in partially edentulous patients — filling a gap in an IIP literature that had concentrated on clinician-measured endpoints (survival, marginal bone loss). From 1,102 screened references, 9 studies were included (1 RCT, the remainder prospective/retrospective cohorts and case series), but **only 3 provided head-to-head comparative data** and the PROM instruments were too heterogeneous (VAS, OHIP-14/OHRQoL scales, ad hoc satisfaction surveys) to pool, so **no meta-analysis was performed**. Across the available data, **patient satisfaction was uniformly high regardless of loading protocol**, differences between immediate and early/conventional loading were small, and qualitative evidence indicated a **positive impact of immediate placement and loading on OHRQoL**. The authors conclude that immediate loading is well-accepted from the patient's perspective but that the paucity and heterogeneity of comparative PROM data preclude an evidence-based protocol preference, recommending that future immediate-loading RCTs adopt standardized PROM instruments.

## Key Contributions

- **First SR** to specifically address a PROM-focused PICO question comparing immediate vs early/conventional loading for Type 1 (immediately placed) single-tooth implants.
- Synthesis of heterogeneous PROM instruments (VAS, OHIP-14/OHRQoL scales, satisfaction surveys) across studies that largely lacked head-to-head comparison.
- Evidence that **patient satisfaction is overall high regardless of loading protocol** in single-tooth edentulous spaces, with immediate placement/loading positively affecting OHRQoL.
- Identifies the **paucity of comparative PROM data** (only 3 of 9 studies) and instrument non-standardization as the limiting factor for an evidence-based protocol preference, and sets a methodological agenda (standardized PROMs in future RCTs).

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review (PROSPERO #49604, Oct 2016); no meta-analysis (instrument heterogeneity).
- **Databases**: OVID MEDLINE, PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews (search to May 2017).
- **Inclusion**: RCTs, prospective/retrospective cohorts, case series (n ≥ 5) on immediate implant placement with any loading protocol, reporting PROMs.
- **Population**: partially edentulous patients, single-tooth gap, immediately placed (Type 1) implants.
- **Outcomes**: PROMs — quality of life (OHIP-14, OHRQoL), visual analog scales (VAS), patient satisfaction surveys.
- **n**: 1,102 references screened → 9 studies included (3 with comparative data; 1 RCT).

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Studies included / screened | 9 of 1,102 (1 RCT, rest cohorts/case series) |
| Comparative (head-to-head) data | Only 3 of 9 studies |
| Meta-analysis | Not possible — heterogeneous PROM instruments |
| Patient satisfaction | Uniformly high across **both** loading protocols |
| Immediate vs early/conventional loading (PROMs) | Little difference (VAS, satisfaction indices) |
| OHRQoL | Qualitative positive impact from immediate placement + loading |
| Formal QoL synthesis | None — instrument heterogeneity precluded pooling |

Most included studies reported only descriptive statistics without formal effect sizes; the review therefore supports a **narrative** conclusion of high, protocol-independent patient satisfaction rather than a quantitative protocol ranking.
