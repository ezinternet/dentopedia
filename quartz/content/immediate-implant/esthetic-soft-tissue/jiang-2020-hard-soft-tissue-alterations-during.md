## Three-line Summary

Randomized controlled trial of 42 participants (40 completed) receiving immediately placed and provisionalized maxillary incisors with or without simultaneous connective tissue graft (CTG), comparing hard and soft tissue changes over 6 months using 3D CBCT superimposition.

The test group (with CTG) showed significantly less buccal tissue collapse in the zone 2–5 mm apical to the gingival margin; however, mid-facial gingival margin migration and radiographic new bone formation occurred similarly in both groups without significant differences.

CTG can partially compensate for facial tissue collapse in immediate implant + provisionalization, but does not improve maintenance of mid-facial gingival margin position during the 6-month healing phase — new bone formation occurs regardless of CTG.

## Summary

This randomized controlled trial enrolled 42 participants (40 completed) with single unsalvageable maxillary incisors in the esthetic zone, randomizing them to immediate implant placement plus immediate provisionalization with or without a simultaneous connective tissue graft (CTG). Using 3D CBCT superimposition at baseline and 6 months, the design isolates CTG as the sole variable within an otherwise identical immediate-placement-plus-provisionalization protocol. The CTG group showed significantly less buccal tissue collapse in the zone 2–5 mm apical to the gingival margin, but mid-facial gingival margin migration (apico-palatal direction) and radiographic new bone formation in the socket void were statistically indistinguishable between groups. The findings indicate CTG protects buccal contour/volume during early healing but does not by itself stabilize the mid-facial gingival margin position, and that socket new-bone fill proceeds on its own trajectory regardless of CTG use — a clinically relevant dissociation between volume preservation and margin-level maintenance that a 2D/linear-only assessment would likely have missed.

## Key Contributions

- Isolates the effect of simultaneous CTG within an otherwise-identical immediate implant + immediate provisionalization protocol using 3D CBCT volumetric superimposition, rather than the 2D/linear measures used in many earlier trials.
- Shows CTG significantly reduces buccal tissue collapse in the 2–5 mm apical zone relative to no-CTG.
- Shows CTG does **not** significantly affect mid-facial gingival margin migration — decoupling "volume/contour preservation" from "margin-level stability" as two distinct outcomes that don't necessarily move together.
- Demonstrates socket-void new bone formation proceeds similarly with or without CTG (except a consistent bucco-coronal triangular gap in both groups), i.e., CTG has no detectable radiographic hard-tissue effect.

## Methodology

Randomized controlled trial (parallel groups, ChiCTR-1900028494), single unsalvageable maxillary incisors in the esthetic zone. 42 participants randomized, 40 completed (2 lost to follow-up). Test: immediate implant placement + immediate provisionalization + simultaneous CTG. Control: immediate implant placement + immediate provisionalization, no CTG. Outcomes — mid-facial gingival margin migration, buccal tissue collapse (volumetric), and socket-void new bone formation — were assessed via digital impressions and CBCT, 3D-superimposed at baseline (pre-extraction) and 6 months.

## Results

| Outcome | Test (with CTG) | Control (no CTG) | Between-group difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buccal tissue collapse (2–5 mm apical to margin) | Significantly less collapse | Greater collapse | Significant, favors CTG |
| Mid-facial gingival margin migration (apico-palatal) | Present | Present | Not significant |
| Socket-void new bone formation | Present (except bucco-coronal triangle) | Present (except bucco-coronal triangle) | Not significant |

## Related Papers

- [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/seyssens-2021-immediate-implant-connective-tissue-graft-sr-ma]] — Seyssens' SR+MA (5 RCTs + 3 NRSs, search to Jan 2020) pools a ~0.4–0.7 mm midfacial-recession benefit for CTG; because that search cutoff predates this trial's June-2020 publication, this RCT sits outside its pooled dataset as independent evidence. It reinforces the SR+MA's pro-CTG direction on buccal tissue/contour, but adds a qualifier the pooled estimate doesn't isolate: here CTG's benefit was confined to buccal tissue collapse (volume/contour), not the mid-facial gingival margin position itself, which moved similarly with or without graft.
- [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/torra-moneny-2024-connective-tissue-grafts-immediate-implant-sr-ma]] — This later RCT-only SR+MA (5 RCTs, 2017–2024 search window — a window this trial falls inside) pools a favorable-but-non-significant buccal gingival level difference for CTG (MD 0.09 mm, p=0.05). The wide, underpowered confidence interval Torra-Moneny attributes to a "small, heterogeneous evidence base" is consistent with — and this trial's own null result on mid-facial gingival margin migration reinforces — that pooled non-significant finding.
- [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/de-angelis-2021-immediate-implant-provisionalization-soft-tissue-retrospective]] — Same core comparison (CTG vs. no added graft at immediately placed and provisionalized single anterior implants), extended with a third arm (xenogeneic collagen matrix) in a 1-year retrospective design. De Angelis found the no-graft group lost significantly more facial soft-tissue thickness **and** buccal marginal level than either grafted arm — a stronger margin-protective effect of grafting than this trial's null finding on gingival margin migration. Extends this trial's comparison with an additional graft material and longer observational follow-up, while sitting in tension with it on whether grafting protects the margin position specifically.
- [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/fettouh-2024-soft-tissue-volume-midfacial-gingival-immediate-implant]] — A closely comparable 3-arm RCT (CTG vs. xenograft bone vs. customized healing abutment alone, thin-labial-bone maxillary esthetic sites) found CTG produced the smallest midfacial gingival margin change of the three arms (−0.74 mm) — i.e., CTG *did* preserve margin position there. A different angle on the same question: different comparator arms (bone graft and CHA-alone rather than a plain no-graft control) and a thin-phenotype-selected population may explain why Fettouh found a margin-protective CTG effect that this trial's CTG-vs-nothing comparison did not.
- [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chan-2019-randomized-controlled-trial-compare-aesthetic]] — Different angle within the same overall protocol family: Chan isolates provisional-crown vs. healing-abutment as the variable (both arms without CTG) and likewise found no significant intergroup difference in mid-facial mucosal marginal level at 12 months. Read together, both single-center RCTs suggest the mid-facial margin position at immediately placed/provisionalized anterior implants resists a single added intervention (CTG here, provisional-crown design in Chan's) — whatever stabilizes it is likely multifactorial rather than driven by either variable alone.
