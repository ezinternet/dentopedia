## Three-line Summary
Systematic review (17 studies: 4 RCT, 6 prospective, 2 comparative prospective, 3 controlled cohort, 2 retrospective; 626 immediately placed and immediately restored [IIPR] implants in 609 patients) evaluating which clinical parameters (implant type, primary stability, gingival biotype, flap status, socket grafting, jump-space, loading protocol) influence hard/soft-tissue outcomes in the anterior maxilla.

65.6% of implants placed flapless; jump space averaged 1.38–2.25mm across studies reporting it; 405/626 sockets grafted; 419 implants reached ≥32 Ncm torque or ISQ≥60; overall success 97.96%, survival 98.25% at mean 31.2-month follow-up; mean crestal bone and interproximal mucosa change <1mm; midfacial mucosal change <0.95mm regardless of gingival biotype (thick in 60.5% of sites) except in one study.

Immediately placed and immediately restored implants in the anterior maxilla show consistently favorable hard/soft-tissue outcomes across a heterogeneous evidence base, with flapless surgery and grafting-free protocols feasible in appropriately selected cases, but strict patient selection was a common feature across all included trials.

## Summary
This systematic review synthesizes 17 studies (only 4 RCTs, the rest prospective/retrospective cohorts) covering 626 immediately placed and immediately restored (IIPR) implants in the anterior maxilla, focusing on which clinical parameters — rather than placement timing alone — drive hard- and soft-tissue outcomes. Two-thirds of implants (65.6%) were placed flapless, and the residual gap between implant and alveolar wall ("jump space") averaged 1.38–2.25mm where reported; about two-thirds of sockets were grafted. Overall success (97.96%) and survival (98.25%) were high at a mean 31.2-month follow-up, with crestal bone and mucosal-level changes generally under 1mm regardless of gingival biotype (thick in 60.5% of sites), except one study that found thin biotype associated with more recession. The authors conclude IIPR gives promising results in the anterior maxilla, and that flapless, graft-free protocols are options for well-selected cases — but strict patient selection was common to every included trial, limiting how far the findings generalize to unselected populations.

## Key Contributions
- Maps which specific clinical parameters (implant type, primary stability, biotype, flap status, grafting, jump space, loading) drive hard/soft-tissue outcomes in IIPR, rather than just comparing placement timing
- Quantifies flapless-surgery prevalence (65.6%) and jump-space dimensions (1.38–2.25mm) across the pooled evidence
- Reports high pooled success (97.96%) and survival (98.25%) specific to the IIPR protocol at mean 31.2-month follow-up

## Methodology
Systematic review, PubMed search January 2000–September 2015 plus hand search of selected journals, English-language abstracts only. Included: human clinical trials, ≥10 participants, reporting hard/soft-tissue outcomes. Study types: RCT, prospective, prospective comparative, retrospective. 17 studies (4 RCT, 6 prospective, 2 comparative prospective, 3 controlled cohort, 2 retrospective) with 626 IIPR implants in 609 patients.
