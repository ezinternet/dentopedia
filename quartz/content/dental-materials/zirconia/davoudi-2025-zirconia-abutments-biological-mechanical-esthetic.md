## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 14 AMSTAR-screened systematic reviews/meta-analyses (published through March 2023; 6,456 patients, 10,063 implants) comparing zirconia (Zr) vs. titanium (Ti) implant abutments across biological, esthetic, and mechanical/survival domains.

Zr abutments showed no difference or favorable results vs. Ti for marginal bone loss (4/10 reviews favored Zr, 6/10 no difference), soft tissue recession, and gingival discoloration; mechanical complication risk ratios were non-significant (0.87, 0.52) and survival rates statistically comparable (Zr 98.6–98.8% vs. Ti 98.62–99.4%) despite Ti's theoretically superior fracture resistance (1454 N vs. 443.6 N) and flexural strength (2000 MPa vs. 900–1200 MPa).

Clinically, Zr is a viable, esthetically favorable alternative to Ti without meaningful loss of mechanical reliability provided excessive occlusal forces/improper angulation are avoided; key limitation is substantial inter-review heterogeneity and inconsistent/underpowered pooling for objective esthetic indices (PES/WES/ICAI/CIS).

## 세줄요약

우산형 검토(Umbrella Review) — AMSTAR 체크리스트로 선별된 14편의 체계적 문헌고찰/메타분석(2023년 3월까지, 환자 6,456명·임플란트 10,063개) 대상, 지르코니아(Zirconia, Zr) vs. 티타늄(Titanium, Ti) 임플란트 지대주(abutment)의 생물학적·심미적·기계적/생존 지표 비교.

변연골소실(Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)은 10편 중 4편 Zr 우세·6편 무차이; 연조직 퇴축·치은 변색도 대체로 무차이 또는 Zr 우세; 기계적 합병증 위험비(Risk Ratio)는 비유의(0.87, 0.52), 생존율도 통계적으로 동등(Zr 98.6~98.8% vs. Ti 98.62~99.4%) — 이론적 파절저항(Ti 1454N vs. Zr 443.6N)·굴곡강도(Ti 2000MPa vs. Zr 900~1200MPa) 상 Ti 우위와 대비되는 임상 결과.

임상적으로 과도한 교합력·부적절한 경사만 피하면 Zr 지대주는 기계적 신뢰성 저하 없이 심미적으로 유리한 Ti의 대안이 될 수 있으나, 리뷰 간 이질성(heterogeneity)이 크고 심미지수(PES/WES/ICAI/CIS) 메타분석은 불충분·비일관적이라는 한계가 있다.

## Summary

This umbrella review pooled 14 systematic reviews/meta-analyses (AMSTAR score SR ≥14/32, MA ≥16/32; searched through March 2023 across PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, ProQuest, Cochrane) comparing zirconia (Zr) and titanium (Ti) implant abutments across biological, esthetic, and mechanical/survival outcomes, covering 6,456 patients and 10,063 implants. Biological indices (marginal bone loss, pocket probing depth, bleeding on probing, plaque accumulation, recession) were generally comparable between materials, with several reviews favoring Zr — attributed to Zr's smoother, more corrosion-resistant surface promoting better epithelial/fibroblast adhesion and lower bacterial colonization. Esthetic outcomes (gingival discoloration, PES/WES, ICAI, Copenhagen Index Score) trended favorable or neutral for Zr but could not be reliably meta-analyzed due to index heterogeneity. Mechanical complications (veneer failure, screw loosening, fracture) and implant/abutment survival were statistically equivalent between Zr and Ti, despite Ti's clear theoretical advantage in fracture resistance and flexural strength — a gap the authors attribute to adequate torque/angulation control (stock Zr ≤15–20°, CAD/CAM Zr ≤30°) mitigating Zr's lower intrinsic strength in practice.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates 14 underlying SR/MAs into a single evidence-consistency map (per-outcome tally of "Zr favored" / "no difference" / "Ti favored") — the clearest single-source comparison currently available in this wiki for Zr-vs-Ti abutment selection.
- Explicitly reconciles bench-level material properties (Ti's superior fracture resistance/flexural strength) with clinical-outcome equivalence, offering mechanistic explanations (torque control, angulation limits, occlusal force management) for why theoretical mechanical inferiority of Zr does not translate into worse survival.
- Identifies mucosal thickness (>2 mm) as the practical threshold beyond which Zr-vs-Ti abutment choice becomes esthetically inconsequential for visible discoloration, even though spectrophotometry can still detect a difference.

## Methodology

Umbrella review (SR/MA of SRs/MAs). Search: PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, ProQuest, Cochrane through 2023. Screening: 457 titles after dedup → 16 full-text-assessed → 14 retained after AMSTAR quality filtering (SR <14/32 or MA <16/32 excluded as high risk of bias); inter-rater kappa 0.90 (abstract) / 0.93 (full-text). Outcomes extracted: biological (MBL, PPD, BOP, plaque accumulation, recession, keratinized mucosa width/thickness), esthetic (PES, WES, Copenhagen Index Score, Implant Crown Aesthetic Index, papilla index, gingival discoloration), mechanical/survival (veneer failure, screw loosening, fracture, abutment/implant survival).

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Marginal bone loss | 10 reviews: 6 no difference, 4 favored Zr |
| Pocket probing depth | 7 reviews: majority no difference, 1 favored Zr, 1 favored Ti |
| Recession | 7 reviews: majority no difference, some favored Zr |
| BOP / plaque accumulation | 6 reviews: 2 favored Ti, rest no difference |
| Gingival discoloration | 8 reviews: 2 favored Zr, 4 no difference, 2 not meta-analyzed |
| Mechanical complication risk ratio | 0.87 and 0.52 (both non-significant); prevalence <7.9% |
| Survival rate | No significant difference (5 reviews); Zr 98.6–98.8% vs. Ti 98.62–99.4%; Totou et al. found Ti superior specifically for abutment fracture |
| Theoretical fracture resistance | Ti 1454 N vs. Zr 443.6 N |
| Theoretical flexural strength | Ti 2000 MPa vs. Zr 900–1200 MPa |
