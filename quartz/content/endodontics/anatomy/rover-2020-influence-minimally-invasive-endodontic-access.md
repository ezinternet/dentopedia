## Three-line Summary
In vitro comparative study of 40 extracted human mandibular incisors across four groups (traditional vs. minimally invasive access × TRUShape vs. MTwo instruments), evaluated by micro-CT before and after preparation and filling.
Minimally invasive access cavities produced significantly more voids in root canal fillings than traditional access (P < 0.05); no significant differences were found in preparation quality, hard-tissue debris, pulp chamber cleaning, or fracture resistance.
MI access preserves tooth structure but at the cost of filling homogeneity — clinicians should apply extra vigilance during the obturation phase when using contracted access designs.

## Summary
This in vitro study compared traditional and minimally invasive (MI) endodontic access cavity designs in 40 extracted human mandibular incisors, using two rotary NiTi systems (TRUShape and MTwo) across four experimental groups. Micro-CT imaging was performed at three time points — pre-selection, post-preparation, and post-filling — to quantify canal transportation, centring ratio, debris accumulation, filling voids, pulp chamber remnants, and fracture resistance. Minimally invasive access was the only variable that produced a statistically significant outcome: a higher density of voids within root canal fillings (P < 0.05), suggesting that reduced coronal access restricts condensation efficacy. All other measured parameters — instrumentation quality, hard-tissue debris, pulp chamber cleaning, and fracture resistance — showed no significant difference between access designs or instrument systems. The findings highlight a functional trade-off: MI cavities conserve dentin but may compromise filling quality, which has long-term implications for seal integrity and treatment success.

## Key Contributions
- **Filling void trade-off confirmed**: MI access cavities introduced significantly more root canal filling voids than traditional access, establishing a quantitative cost to the structural preservation benefit.
- **Preparation quality unaffected**: Access cavity design did not influence canal transportation, centring ratio, noninstrumented areas, or accumulated hard-tissue debris, supporting equivalence in shaping efficacy.
- **Fracture resistance neutral**: Despite the filling quality difference, MI access cavities did not reduce fracture resistance compared to traditional access, indicating that structural preservation alone does not confer fracture resistance benefit in the short term.

## Methodology
- **Design**: In vitro experimental comparative study (four parallel groups, n = 10 each)
- **Sample**: 40 intact extracted human mandibular incisors, matched by periapical radiograph
- **Groups**: Traditional/TRUShape (T/TRU), Traditional/MTwo (T/MT), Minimally Invasive/TRUShape (MI/TRU), Minimally Invasive/MTwo (MI/MT)
- **Imaging**: Micro-CT at pre-selection, post-preparation, and post-filling time points
- **Primary outcomes**: Voids in root canal fillings, canal transportation and centring ratio, noninstrumented canal areas, accumulated hard-tissue debris, filling remnants in pulp chamber, fracture resistance
- **Statistics**: Shapiro–Wilk normality, one-way ANOVA, Bonferroni post-hoc (α = 0.05)
