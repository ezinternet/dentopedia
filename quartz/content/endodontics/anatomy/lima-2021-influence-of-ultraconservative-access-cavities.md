## Three-line Summary

In vitro study of 40 extracted mandibular molars (n=10/group) comparing ultraconservative vs traditional access cavities paired with XP-endo Shaper or Reciproc instrumentation, evaluated by micro-CT and thermomechanical cycling followed by load-capacity testing.

Ultraconservative access cavities produced significantly more unprepared canal areas, greater pulp-chamber filling voids, and higher canal transportation with Reciproc — but not with XP-endo Shaper — while load-capacity was equivalent across all four groups.

Access cavity conservation trades shaping efficiency (especially with reciprocating systems) against dentin preservation; preserved fracture resistance despite geometric compromises suggests cavity design choice may not acutely compromise structural integrity.

## 세줄요약

발치된 하악 대구치 40개(n=10/그룹)에서 초보존적 접근와동 (Ultraconservative Access Cavity, UltraAC) 대 전통적 접근와동 (Traditional Access Cavity, TradAC)과 XP-endo Shaper 또는 Reciproc 기구 조합을 미세전산화단층촬영 (Micro-computed Tomography, microCT) 및 열기계적 순환 후 하중용량 검사로 평가한 체외연구.

초보존 접근에서 미성형 근관 면적·충전재료 잔존·Reciproc 사용 시 근관편이 (Canal Transportation) 유의 증가; XP-endo Shaper와의 조합에서는 근관편이 차이 없음; 모든 그룹에서 하중용량 (Load Capacity) 동일.

접근 보존성은 성형 효율(특히 왕복운동 기구)과 상아질 보존 간의 trade-off를 나타내며, 파절저항성 보존은 기하학적 타협이 즉각적 구조적 손상을 초래하지 않음을 시사한다.

## Summary

This in vitro study investigated how ultraconservative versus traditional access cavity preparation affects root canal shaping efficacy, filling quality, and fracture resistance in mandibular molars instrumented with two single-file NiTi systems (XP-endo Shaper and Reciproc). Forty extracted mandibular molars were allocated to four groups (n=10) based on access design × instrument, scanned by micro-CT before and after instrumentation and filling, then subjected to thermomechanical cycling and load-to-fracture testing. Ultraconservative access cavities resulted in significantly greater unprepared canal wall percentages and more voids and remnant filling material in the pulp chamber, regardless of instrument used. Canal transportation was higher with ultraconservative + Reciproc but not with ultraconservative + XP-endo Shaper, suggesting continuous-rotation adaptive instruments partially compensate for restricted access geometry. Critically, fracture load capacity did not differ among groups, indicating that the geometric penalties of ultraconservative cavity design do not immediately translate into reduced structural strength under laboratory conditions.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrated that ultraconservative access cavities significantly increase unprepared canal wall area and pulp-chamber filling voids compared to traditional access, quantified by micro-CT volumetrics
- Showed instrument-specific interaction: XP-endo Shaper (continuous rotation, variable taper) maintained canal transportation equivalence with both access types, while Reciproc (reciprocating) showed significantly greater transportation under ultraconservative access
- Confirmed load-capacity equivalence across all access-cavity × instrument combinations following thermomechanical cycling, despite geometric disadvantages of ultraconservative cavities
- Identified that UltraAC + XP-endo Shaper achieved the lowest dentin removal volume but accumulated the highest hard tissue debris (AHTD), illustrating a simultaneous conservation-contamination trade-off

## Methodology

- **Design**: In vitro experimental study with matched-pair micro-CT analysis and two-way ANOVA (randomized-blocks, α=0.05)
- **Sample**: 40 extracted, caries-free mandibular molars with no prior endodontic treatment; 4 groups, n=10/group
- **Groups**:
  - TradAC/RC — Traditional access + Reciproc
  - TradAC/XP — Traditional access + XP-endo Shaper
  - UltraAC/RC — Ultraconservative access + Reciproc
  - UltraAC/XP — Ultraconservative access + XP-endo Shaper
- **Imaging**: High-resolution micro-CT at baseline, post-instrumentation, and post-filling
- **Outcomes**: Unprepared canal area (%), dentin removed (mm³), accumulated hard tissue debris (AHTD, %), canal transportation in MB/ML/distal canals (µm), filling voids, remnant filling material in pulp chamber, load capacity (N) after thermomechanical cycling

## Results

| Outcome | TradAC/RC | TradAC/XP | UltraAC/RC | UltraAC/XP | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unprepared canal area | Lower | Lower | **Higher** | **Higher** | p<0.05 |
| Dentin removed | Intermediate | Intermediate | Intermediate | **Lowest** | p<0.05 |
| AHTD | Higher | Lower | Higher | **Highest** | p<0.05 |
| Canal transportation — MB | Intermediate | **Lowest** | **Highest** | Intermediate | p<0.05 |
| Canal transportation — ML/distal | Intermediate | **Lowest** | Intermediate | **Lowest** | p<0.05 |
| Voids + remnant filling material | Lower | Lower | **Higher** | **Higher** | p<0.05 |
| Load capacity after thermomechanical cycling | No difference among all groups | — | — | — | NS |

**Key patterns**:
- TradAC/XP achieved the lowest canal transportation across all canal systems
- UltraAC/XP conserved the most dentin but generated the most hard tissue debris
- UltraAC groups (both instruments) had significantly more pulp-chamber voids after obturation
- Fracture load capacity was statistically equivalent in all groups despite geometric differences
