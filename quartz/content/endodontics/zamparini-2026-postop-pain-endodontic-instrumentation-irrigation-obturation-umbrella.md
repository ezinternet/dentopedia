## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 25 systematic reviews with meta-analysis (9 irrigation, 8 instrumentation, 8 obturation; 368 records screened, 2016–2025) synthesizing post-operative pain (POP) evidence across the three main procedural phases of non-surgical root canal treatment, AMSTAR-2 appraised, PROSPERO-registered (CRD420261331928).

Lower NaOCl concentrations (1–3% vs ≥5%), irrigant activation techniques, and intracanal cryotherapy were repeatedly linked to reduced short-term (6–48h) POP; rotary instrumentation systems were more often but not consistently associated with lower POP than reciprocating/manual; no clinically consistent POP difference was found between bioceramic/calcium silicate-based and resin-based obturation sealers.

Findings should be read as tentative directional trends rather than firm recommendations — obturation-domain reviews showed very high primary-study overlap (corrected covered area 17.6%), and 4/25 SRs lacked protocol registration while 11/25 did not justify excluded studies.

## 세줄요약

근관치료 후 통증(Post-operative Pain, POP)에 관한 25편의 체계적 문헌고찰(systematic review, SR)+메타분석을 종합한 우산리뷰(umbrella review)로, 세정(irrigation, 9편)·기구조작(instrumentation, 8편)·충전(obturation, 8편)의 3개 시술 단계를 통합 분석했다(2016–2025년, 368편 스크리닝, AMSTAR-2 평가, PROSPERO 등록).

낮은 차아염소산나트륨(Sodium Hypochlorite, NaOCl) 농도(1–3% vs ≥5%), 세정 활성화(irrigation activation) 기법, 근관 내 냉동요법(intracanal cryotherapy)이 반복적으로 단기(6–48시간) POP 감소와 연관되었고, 회전형(rotary) 기구가 왕복형(reciprocating)·수동(manual) 기구보다 더 자주(그러나 일관되지는 않게) 낮은 POP와 연관되었으며, 바이오세라믹(bioceramic)/규산칼슘(calcium silicate) 실러와 레진(resin) 실러 간 임상적으로 유의한 POP 차이는 없었다.

충전 영역의 1차 연구 중복도(corrected covered area, CCA)가 17.6%(매우 높음)로 나타나 개별 SR 간 결론이 독립적 재확인이 아닐 수 있으며, 25편 중 4편은 프로토콜 미등록·11편은 제외 연구 근거 미제시 등 방법론적 한계로 인해 확정적 임상권고가 아닌 잠정적 경향으로 해석해야 한다.

## Summary

This umbrella review synthesizes 25 systematic reviews with meta-analysis (published 2016–2025) evaluating post-operative pain (POP) in relation to three core procedural phases of non-surgical root canal treatment: irrigation (9 SRs), instrumentation (8 SRs), and obturation (8 SRs). It is the first umbrella review to cover all three domains jointly rather than a single procedural focus (prior umbrella work in endodontics targeted hydraulic sealers only). Following PRISMA guidelines and PROSPERO registration (CRD420261331928), the authors screened 368 records down to 25 included SRs, appraised each with AMSTAR-2, and quantified primary-study overlap across SRs within each domain using the corrected covered area (CCA) statistic. The bottom line is cautious: several procedural choices show recurrent associations with reduced short-term POP, but methodological weaknesses (unregistered protocols, missing exclusion justifications, inconsistent risk-of-bias handling) and high-to-very-high overlap of primary studies across reviews (esp. in obturation) mean the findings are tentative trends, not firm clinical recommendations.

## Key Contributions

- First joint umbrella review of POP across irrigation + instrumentation + obturation (previous umbrella reviews in this space were narrower, e.g., hydraulic-sealer-only).
- Restricted inclusion to SRs with meta-analysis (not narrative SRs), improving quantitative comparability of the 25 included reviews.
- Formal overlap quantification (CCA) per procedural domain — a rarely applied caution layer in endodontic pain literature, revealing "high" overlap in irrigation (11.1%) and instrumentation (12.5%) and "very high" overlap in obturation (17.6%).
- AMSTAR-2 appraisal exposes systemic weaknesses across the 25 SRs: 4 unregistered, 11 without justified exclusion lists, inconsistent publication-bias assessment.

## Methodology

Umbrella review per PRISMA + Choi et al. 2023 umbrella-review recommendations, PROSPERO-registered. PIOS question: non-surgical endodontic patients; irrigation/instrumentation/obturation techniques; POP intensity/duration outcome; SRs with meta-analysis only. Searches of PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science plus manual journal search, English language, 2016–2025. Two independent reviewers screened 368 records → 25 SRs included after full-text assessment (26 excluded at that stage for reasons including laser interventions, pediatric populations, single- vs multiple-visit comparisons, primary teeth, pharmacological management, vital pulp therapy, intracanal medicaments). Standardized, piloted data-extraction form (first author, year, pain factor, sample size, indication, pain-measurement method, outcomes, evidence quality, limitations). Methodological quality of each SR appraised with AMSTAR-2 (not used for GRADE certainty of underlying trials). Qualitative narrative synthesis (no de novo primary-study meta-analysis); overlap of primary studies across SRs quantified per domain via citation matrix and corrected covered area (CCA), thresholds slight/moderate/high/very high at 0–5/6–10/11–15/>15%.

## Results

- **Irrigation (9 SRs, CCA 11.1%, high overlap)**: lower NaOCl concentration (1–3% vs ≥5%) associated with less POP (2 SRs); irrigant activation/agitation reduced POP at 6–48h vs syringe irrigation; NaOCl vs chlorhexidine showed no significant POP difference (1 SR); intracanal cryotherapy reduced POP at 6–24h in 4 SRs (fading by 48–72h/7d), with one SR specific to symptomatic apical periodontitis at 24h.
- **Instrumentation (8 SRs, CCA 12.5%, high overlap)**: apical patency vs non-patency showed possible POP reduction at 24/48h (2 SRs, not always significant); foraminal enlargement associated with higher early POP in necrotic teeth (1 SR); of 5 SRs comparing instrumentation systems, most favored rotary (especially multi-file) for lower POP vs reciprocating/manual, but 1 found no difference and 1 favored reciprocating kinematics.
- **Obturation (8 SRs, CCA 17.6%, very high overlap)**: no clinically consistent POP difference between bioceramic/calcium silicate-based and epoxy resin-based sealers, especially at 3–7 days; some SRs showed slightly lower 24h pain with bioceramic sealers (inconsistent); comparisons involving zinc oxide–eugenol and calcium hydroxide-based sealers were similarly null.
- **Quality**: across all 25 SRs, 4 lacked protocol registration, 11 lacked a justified list of excluded studies, publication bias was often unassessed, and funding reporting was inconsistent.
- Certainty-of-evidence judgments were taken as reported by each included SR and not reassessed with GRADE at the umbrella level — authors explicitly frame all associations as tentative directional patterns rather than stable clinical conclusions.
