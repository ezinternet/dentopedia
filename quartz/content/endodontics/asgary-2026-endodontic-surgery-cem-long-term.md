## Summary
This 13-year single-center retrospective cohort (STROBE-compliant) evaluated long-term outcomes of periapical surgery using calcium-enriched mixture (CEM) cement as root-end filling material. Of 197 teeth (169 patients) with persistent AP after failed orthograde therapy, the overall success rate was 94.9%. Failure-free survival was 96.4% at 1 year, 92.7% at 3 years, and 87.3% at 5 years. Multivariate Cox regression identified systemic disease (ASA ≥II) as the sole significant predictor of failure (HR=5.87, p=0.010): failure occurred in 14% of systemically compromised patients vs 2.6% of healthy patients. Tooth-related variables (lesion size, crown-root ratio, tooth type, post presence) and demographic variables showed no significant associations. CEM's superior performance was attributed to its hydroxyapatite formation and continuous calcium/phosphate release, promoting cementogenesis and osteoblastic differentiation.

## Key Contributions
- 94.9% long-term success with CEM — exceeds amalgam/IRM historical rates; comparable to MTA (91.6%)
- Systemic disease: only independent predictor (HR=5.87; 5.4× higher failure risk)
- Mechanism: hyperglycemia → AGE accumulation → ↓osteoblast activity; chronic inflammation → MMP/TIMP imbalance → delayed bone remodeling
- Lesion size, crown-root ratio, root-end preparation depth: not independently predictive
- Single endodontist, standardized protocol (root-end resection + ultrasonic prep + CEM fill)

## Methodology
Retrospective observational cohort (STROBE). Single center (Shahid Beheshti University, Tehran). Study period 2011–2024. Inclusion: persistent AP unresponsive to prior orthograde therapy, ≥6 months radiographic follow-up. Outcome: Rud/Molven classification (healed vs diseased = success vs failure). Kaplan-Meier survival curves; Cox proportional hazards (multivariate). Intra-observer reliability: κ=1.00, ICC=0.83.
