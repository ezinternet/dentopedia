## 한줄요약

치근단 육아종 (Periapical Granuloma, PG)이 염증성 치근낭 (Inflammatory Radicular Cyst, IRC)으로 이행하는 3단계 분자기전(말라세즈 상피세포잔사 (Epithelial Cell Rests of Malassez, ERM) 증식→상피내벽형성→낭종성장)을 정리하고, CBCT·초음파·MRI 등 비침습적 진단법의 정확도(31~100%, 연구별 편차 큼)를 조직병리(gold standard)와 비교한 서사적 문헌고찰.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Eur Endod J 2023) synthesizing the molecular biology of inflammatory radicular cyst (IRC) formation from periapical granuloma (PG) via proliferation of epithelial cell rests of Malassez (ERM), and appraising non-invasive diagnostic methods against histopathology.

IRC formation is a three-stage process (cytokine/growth-factor-driven ERM proliferation → epithelial lining formation → MMP/RANKL/VEGF-driven cyst growth), yet only ~20% of epithelium-containing PGs (~45% of all PGs) progress to IRC and the reason is unknown; CBCT accuracy for PG-vs-IRC differentiation is highly variable (31–83%) while ultrasound (63–100%) and MRI (70.5–100%) trend more consistent but remain study-dependent.

Histopathology via serial sectioning of an excisional biopsy remains the only reliable diagnostic standard for distinguishing PG from IRC — since both are stages of the same inflammatory continuum, no non-invasive imaging modality can yet substitute for it when the differential affects the surgical-vs-nonsurgical endodontic treatment decision.

## Summary

Rios Osorio et al. (2023) provide a comprehensive narrative review of the biological mechanisms underlying inflammatory radicular cyst (IRC) formation from periapical granuloma (PG). They frame IRC development as a three-stage process — epithelial proliferation (of ERM remnants in the periodontal ligament, driven by pro-inflammatory interleukins IL-1/IL-6/TNF-α and growth factors EGF/KGF/TGF-α/TGF-β), epithelial lining formation (three competing theories: nutritional deficiency, abscess, merging of epithelial strands — none fully validated), and cyst growth/expansion (MMP-mediated ECM degradation, RANKL/RANK/OPG-driven bone resorption, VEGF-driven fluid accumulation and hydrostatic pressure). A central unresolved question threads through the paper: although ~45% of all PGs contain epithelial cells, only ~20% of those progress to a true IRC — the trigger for this selective transformation remains unknown. The paper also classifies IRCs as true cysts (self-contained, generally requiring surgery) versus bay cysts (connected to the apical foramen, potentially resolvable with non-surgical root canal therapy), and closes with a detailed appraisal of non-invasive/minimally-invasive diagnostic methods (CBCT, ultrasound, MRI, cytokine/fluid aspiration) benchmarked against histopathology, concluding that histopathology (via serial sectioning of an excisional biopsy) remains the only reliable gold standard despite promising but inconsistent imaging accuracy.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates the current three-stage molecular model of PG→IRC transformation (ERM proliferation → epithelial lining → cyst growth).
- Comprehensive tables of growth factors (EGF, PDGF, TGF-α, TGF-β, KGF, VEGF) and interleukins (IL-1 through IL-33) implicated in IRC pathogenesis, with cellular sources and biological effects.
- Critically reviews three competing cyst-cavity-formation theories and notes none fully explains selective PG→IRC progression.
- Summarizes Simon's (1980) true-cyst/bay-cyst classification and its clinical treatment-selection relevance.
- Aggregates a 16-study comparative accuracy table of CBCT/ultrasound/MRI/fluid-aspiration vs histopathology for PG-vs-IRC differentiation.
- Clarifies histological diagnostic pitfalls: incisional/random-section biopsies can falsely suggest a cystic epithelial lining that serial-sectioned excisional biopsy would not confirm.

## Methodology

Narrative (non-systematic) literature review synthesizing histologic, immunohistochemical, and diagnostic-imaging studies on IRC etiopathogenesis, classification, histology, and differential diagnosis from PG. No PRISMA protocol, formal search strategy, or risk-of-bias appraisal is reported; the diagnostic-accuracy comparison (their Table 3, aggregating 16 published studies of CBCT/ultrasound/MRI/cytokine-aspiration vs histopathology) is a narrative compilation, not a meta-analysis.

## Results

**Epidemiology**: IRCs comprise ~54% of 317 biopsied apical lesions in one series; 46.6–68% of all maxillary cystic lesions are IRCs.

**Etiopathogenesis**: ~45% of all PGs contain epithelial cells; only ~20% of epithelium-containing chronic periapical lesions progress to IRC (mechanism unknown). Key drivers: IL-1/IL-6/TNF-α → ERM proliferation; NF-κB regulates IL-1/IL-6/TNF-α/MMP expression; KGF is a potent epithelial mitogen; MMP-13 shows the strongest association with PG→IRC transformation; RANKL/RANK/OPG and VEGF drive bone resorption and intracystic fluid accumulation respectively.

**Classification**: True cysts generally require surgical intervention; bay cysts (directly connected to the apical foramen) may resolve with non-surgical root canal therapy alone.

**Diagnostic accuracy vs histopathology** (selected from their Table 3):

| Method | Accuracy range (studies cited) |
|---|---|
| CBCT | 31.1–83% (AUC 0.44–0.76 across 7 studies) |
| Ultrasound ± Doppler | 62.5–100% across 8 studies |
| MRI | 70.5–100% (2 studies) |
| Cytokine/fluid aspiration | Qualitative only — high IL-1α, TNF-α, MCP-1, RANTES in IRC fluid |

Histopathology (serial-sectioned excisional biopsy) remains the diagnostic gold standard; incisional/random sectioning risks false-positive "epithelial lining" findings that can misclassify a PG as an IRC.
