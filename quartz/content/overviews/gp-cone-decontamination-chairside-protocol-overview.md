> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **바텀라인**: 거타퍼차(gutta-percha, GP) 콘은 열가소성이라 고압증기멸균(autoclave)·건열멸균이 불가능하다 — 개봉 후 취급·에어로졸·핀셋 접촉으로 오염되므로 근관충전(obturation) 직전 **화학적 진료실 소독(chairside decontamination)이 무조건 필요**하다. 표준 프로토콜은 **차아염소산나트륨(Sodium Hypochlorite, NaOCl) 1분 침적 + 헹굼**.
> - **실제 오염률은 낮지만 무시할 수 없다**: 사용 중 GP 콘 오염률은 표본 방식에 따라 **1.9%(MRSA/MSSA 표적 스크리닝, Teles) ~ 22.9%(전체 세균 배양, Bracciale)**. 낮아도 검출균에 병원성 메티실린내성 황색포도상구균(Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus, MRSA)이 포함돼 위험이 0은 아니다.
> - **오염은 브랜드가 아니라 취급·박스 체류시간이 좌우**: Dentsply·R&S 브랜드 차이 무의미, 대신 **덜 쓰이는 큰 사이즈(K30, D-size)**가 개봉 박스에 오래 남아 핀셋 접촉이 많아 오염 집중(Bracciale).
> - **1차 소독제 = NaOCl (gold standard)**: 고농도 5.25%가 대장균속 아닌 **엔테로코쿠스 페칼리스(Enterococcus faecalis)·황색포도상구균(S. aureus)을 1~5분 내 제거**(Aucinaite SR). 2.62%·5.25% 모두 1분에 *B. subtilis*·*C. albicans* 완전 제거(Raheja).
> - **핵심 부작용 — 시간 통제 필수**: NaOCl **>1분 침적은 GP 탄성 손실 + 염화물 결정(chloride crystal) 석출**을 유발. 원자간력현미경(Atomic Force Microscopy, AFM)상 침적 3·5분에 표면 조도(RMS)가 유의하게 악화(Raheja). → **1분 지키고, 세정액(detergent)/치오황산나트륨(sodium thiosulfate)으로 반드시 헹궈** 결정 제거.
> - **클로르헥시딘(Chlorhexidine, CHX)의 자리**: 세균에는 근거 부족·NaOCl보다 약하나, **칸디다(Candida) 등 진균에 유망**하고(교반 없이도 효과) GP 탄성을 해치지 않음. 진균 우려·탄성 보존이 중요할 때 대안(Aucinaite).
> - **나노입자(은 AgNP·키토산 ChNP)는 이점 없음**: *C. albicans*는 1분에 잡지만 *B. subtilis*는 **5분이나 걸리고** 표면 손상은 NaOCl과 비슷 → 진료실 효율에서 NaOCl 대체 불가(Raheja).
> - **소독 효율(회수율)**: NaOCl 프로토콜이 오염 콘의 **76.4%(Bracciale, 5.25% 1분+세정 5분) ~ 100%(Teles, 3% 60초) 소독**. 표적균·농도·헹굼 유무에 따라 편차.
> - **근거 등급**: 앵커는 체계적 문헌고찰(SR, in vitro 7편)이나 **포함 연구 전부 중등도~높은 비뚤림 위험**이고 메타분석 불가. 나머지 3편은 in-vitro. 임상결과(RCT) 근거는 0건 — 권고는 미생물학적·표면분석 근거에 기반.
> - **임상 SOP**: ① 사용 중 박스의 마스터·보조 콘 모두 소독 대상 ② **NaOCl(3~5.25%)에 1분** 침적 ③ 무균 증류수/치오황산나트륨 세정액으로 헹궈 염화물 결정 제거·건조 ④ 침적 1분 초과 금지(탄성 손실) ⑤ 진균 우려 시 CHX(±cetrimide) 고려 ⑥ 콘을 집는 핀셋과 근관에 넣는 핀셋 분리.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 4 papers (1 systematic review + 3 in-vitro studies): gutta-percha cones cannot be heat-sterilized (thermoplastic), so chairside chemical decontamination before obturation is mandatory; in-use cone contamination is low (1.9% MRSA/MSSA-targeted vs 22.9% all-bacteria culture) but includes MRSA, concentrating in thread/driver recesses of less-used oversized cones (brand is irrelevant; handling and box-residence time drive contamination).

All evidence converges on NaOCl (3–5.25%) 1-minute immersion as the practical gold standard: it eliminates Enterococcus faecalis and S. aureus within 1–5 minutes (Aucinaite SR), and 2.62–5.25% NaOCl completely removes B. subtilis and C. albicans in 1 minute (Raheja), achieving 76.4–100% sterility of contaminated cones; but immersion beyond 1 minute causes elasticity loss and chloride crystal precipitation (AFM surface deterioration at 3–5 min), so a mandatory rinse with detergent or sodium thiosulfate is required.

Chlorhexidine (CHX) is reserved for fungal concerns (effective against Candida without agitation; preserves GP elasticity), while silver and chitosan nanoparticles take 5 min for B. subtilis (vs 1 min for NaOCl) with similar surface damage — offering no clinical efficiency advantage; the entire evidence base is in vitro/SR with zero clinical-outcome RCTs, so recommendations are microbiology-based only.

## 세줄요약

4편(체계적 문헌고찰 1 + in vitro 3) 종합: 거타퍼차(gutta-percha, GP) 콘은 열가소성으로 열멸균 불가능해 근관충전(obturation) 직전 화학 소독이 필수; 사용 중 콘 오염률은 MRSA/MSSA 표적 검사 1.9%, 전체 세균 배양 22.9%로 낮지만 MRSA를 포함하며, 오염은 브랜드가 아닌 취급·박스 체류시간 의존적(덜 쓰이는 큰 사이즈가 집중).

근거 전체가 **차아염소산나트륨(Sodium Hypochlorite, NaOCl) 3–5.25% 1분 침적** 으로 수렴 — 엔테로코쿠스 페칼리스(Enterococcus faecalis)·황색포도상구균(S. aureus)을 1–5분 내 제거, 오염 콘 76.4–100% 소독 달성; 단 1분 초과 침적 시 GP 탄성 손실 + 염화물 결정 석출(원자간력현미경 표면 악화) → 무균증류수/치오황산나트륨으로 반드시 헹굼.

클로르헥시딘(Chlorhexidine, CHX)은 진균 우려 시 대안(GP 탄성 보존·교반 없이 효과), 은·키토산 나노입자는 B. subtilis에 5분 필요(NaOCl의 1분 대비 불리)해 진료실 효율에서 NaOCl 대체 불가; 이 모든 근거는 in vitro/SR으로 임상 결과(RCT) 연구는 0건이다.

## Summary

Gutta-percha (GP) cones remain the obturation gold standard, but their thermoplastic composition means they **cannot be autoclaved or hot-air sterilized**. Once a package is opened, cones are exposed to glove and tweezer handling, aerosols, and repeated instrument access, so **chairside chemical decontamination immediately before obturation is the only sterility safeguard** and a required link in the endodontic aseptic chain. Despite this, no single standardized protocol has been formally established.

This overview synthesizes four papers that together answer three practical questions: (1) *how contaminated are in-use cones really?*, (2) *which agent and exposure clears them best?*, and (3) *what is the cost of disinfection to the cone itself?* The evidence converges on a **short, high-concentration NaOCl immersion (≈1 min) with a mandatory post-rinse** as the best-supported chairside protocol, while chlorhexidine (CHX) and nanoparticle agents occupy narrower niches.

## The Clinical Problem — why decontaminate at all

Two same-group Portuguese studies quantified contamination on cones sampled **not from sealed boxes but from boxes already in clinical use** (open ~4–8 weeks, ~8 appointments/week), the realistic cross-infection scenario:

| Study | Sampling | Contamination | Notable organisms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bracciale 2020 (in-vitro) | 240 points, 4 brands, all-bacteria culture | **22.9%** | not speciated |
| Teles 2024 (in-vitro) | 315 points, 2 brands, MRSA/MSSA-targeted (PCR) | **1.9%** (6/315) | 1 MRSA + 5 MSSA |

The two rates differ mainly because of **what was screened** (any aerobic/anaerobic growth vs a targeted *S. aureus* selective medium), not a real contradiction. The shared message: contamination is **prevalence-low but non-negligible**, and can include a nosocomially important, methicillin-resistant pathogen. Bracciale further showed contamination is **unrelated to brand** and instead concentrated in **less-used, oversized cones** (K30 at 16.4%; all Dentsply D-size positive) that linger longer in open boxes and undergo more tweezer handling — implicating handling/residence time over box exposure per se.

## The Agent — NaOCl is the practical gold standard

- **Aucinaite 2025 (systematic review, 7 in vitro studies)** — higher-concentration NaOCl (5.25%/6%) eliminates *E. faecalis* and *S. aureus* within **1–5 min**; 5.25% NaOCl reduced *E. faecalis* ~8.5× more than 2% CHX at 1 min (p=0.003). This is the anchor conclusion.
- **Raheja 2025 (in-vitro)** — both **2.62% and 5.25% NaOCl cleared *B. subtilis* and *C. albicans* in 1 min**, whereas silver nanoparticles (AgNP, 250 µg/mL) and chitosan nanoparticles (ChNP, 625 µg/mL) needed **5 min** against *B. subtilis*. Nanoparticles offered **no efficiency advantage**.
- **Efficacy of the whole protocol (disinfection success on already-contaminated cones)**: Bracciale's 5.25% NaOCl (1 min) + detergent (5 min) rinse cleared **76.4%**; Teles' 3% NaOCl (60 s) cleared **100%** of its six positives. Concentration, contact time, target species, and rinse step all move this number.

## The Cost — disinfection damages the cone (time must be controlled)

The decisive practical caveat, consistent across the cluster:

- **Raheja's AFM data**: at **1 min**, NaOCl caused minimal surface change (RMS not significantly different from fresh control); at **3 and 5 min**, *all* agents significantly degraded surface topography. Damage is **progressive with immersion time**.
- **Teles/Aucinaite caveat**: NaOCl immersion **>1 min risks loss of GP elasticity and precipitation of chloride crystals** on the surface unless rinsed off.

→ The protocol must be **time-limited (~1 min) and followed by a rinse** (sterile water, or a detergent / sodium-thiosulfate solution that neutralizes residual NaOCl and strips chloride crystals). Longer is not better.

## Where CHX and nanoparticles fit

- **CHX**: weaker/limited evidence against bacteria and inferior to 5.25% NaOCl at short times, but **promising against *Candida*** (2% CHX was the only agent effective without agitation at 1 and 5 min in the SR) and it **does not deteriorate GP elasticity**. Reasonable where fungal contamination is a concern or GP preservation matters; CHX + cetrimide broadened bacterial efficacy.
- **AgNP / ChNP**: adequate against yeast at 1 min but slow (5 min) against a Gram-positive bacillus, with comparable time-dependent surface damage — **no chairside advantage over NaOCl**.

## Bottom-line Chairside SOP

1. **Always disinfect** master and accessory cones drawn from in-use boxes — contamination is low but non-zero and may include MRSA.
2. **Immerse in NaOCl (3–5.25%) for ~1 minute** — the best-supported agent and time.
3. **Rinse** in sterile distilled water or a detergent / sodium-thiosulfate solution to remove chloride crystals and residual NaOCl; **dry** on sterile gauze.
4. **Do not exceed ~1 min** immersion — longer degrades GP elasticity and surface.
5. **Consider CHX (± cetrimide)** when fungal contamination is suspected or elasticity preservation is prioritized.
6. **Handling hygiene**: separate tweezers for retrieving vs placing cones; minimize open-box residence of large/rarely-used sizes.

## Evidence Grade & Gaps

- Anchor is a **systematic review**, but all 7 included studies are **in vitro at moderate/high risk of bias**, and no meta-analysis was possible (species/concentration/time heterogeneity). The three supporting papers are also **in vitro**.
- **No clinical-outcome (RCT) evidence** links cone decontamination to reduced treatment failure — recommendations rest on microbiological and surface-analysis endpoints.
- **Open questions**: optimal concentration × time trade-off minimizing surface damage; standardized rinse/neutralization step; efficacy against *E. faecalis* biofilm (one SR study found neither 5.25% NaOCl nor 2% CHX fully eliminated it at 5 min); real-world impact of the <2–23% contamination on endodontic outcomes.
