## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251239499) of 11 systematic reviews (2016–2024) on complications of zygomatic implants (ZIs), comparing the original surgical technique (OST) vs zygomatic anatomy-guided approach (ZAGA/AGA); no meta-analysis was performed due to substantial study heterogeneity (AMSTAR quality tool used).

Sinusitis was the most common complication across all included reviews (range ~2.4–27.2% depending on technique and study), followed by soft-tissue infection (~2–7.5%) and paresthesia (~0.55–10.78%); implant survival was consistently high (e.g., Chrcanovic 2016: 95.21% cumulative survival at 12 years over 4556 ZIs; Brennard-Roper 2023: 96.2% at 6 years), and ZAGA used immediate loading far more often than OST (89.6% vs 22.3%, Kammerer 2023).

High ZI survival does not equate to a complication-free procedure — complication rates may be systematically underestimated due to inconsistent reporting, and the medico-legal discussion stresses that ZI should be reserved for cases with no viable alternative, with thorough informed consent and reliance on surgeon expertise, given the potential severity (e.g., orbital penetration, diplopia) of some adverse events.

## 세줄요약

관골 임플란트(Zygomatic Implant, ZI) 합병증에 관한 11편의 체계적 문헌고찰(2016–2024)을 종합한 umbrella review(PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO 등록)로, 원래 술식(Original Surgical Technique, OST)과 관골 해부학 유도 접근법(Zygomatic Anatomy-Guided Approach, ZAGA/AGA)을 비교했으며 연구 간 이질성으로 메타분석은 시행하지 않았다(AMSTAR 도구로 질 평가).

전체 연구에서 가장 흔한 합병증은 부비동염(sinusitis, 술식·연구에 따라 약 2.4–27.2%)이었고, 연조직감염(약 2–7.5%)과 이상감각(paresthesia, 약 0.55–10.78%)이 뒤를 이었으며, 임플란트 생존율은 전반적으로 높았다(Chrcanovic 2016: 12년 누적생존율 95.21%, ZI 4556개; Brennard-Roper 2023: 6년 96.2%); ZAGA는 OST보다 즉시부하(immediate loading)를 훨씬 더 많이 사용했다(89.6% vs 22.3%, Kammerer 2023).

높은 생존율이 곧 합병증 없는 시술을 의미하지는 않으며, 보고 불일치로 합병증 발생률이 과소평가되었을 가능성이 있고, 의료법적(medico-legal) 논의에서는 다른 대안이 없는 경우에만 ZI를 선택하고 충분한 사전동의(informed consent)와 술자의 숙련도가 핵심임을 강조한다(안와손상·복시 등 중증 합병증 가능성 때문).

## Summary

This umbrella review synthesizes 11 systematic reviews (2016–2024, PRISMA 2020/PROSPERO CRD420251239499) on complications of zygomatic implants (ZIs) used to rehabilitate severely atrophic edentulous maxillae, comparing Brånemark's original surgical technique (OST) against the zygomatic anatomy-guided approach (ZAGA/AGA). Because of substantial heterogeneity across the source reviews, no meta-analysis was performed; findings were synthesized narratively and quality-appraised with the AMSTAR tool. Implant survival was consistently high across techniques (e.g., 95.21% cumulative survival at 12 years across 4556 ZIs in Chrcanovic 2016; 90.3–100% OST vs 90.4–100% ZAGA in Kammerer 2023), but complications — most commonly sinusitis, followed by soft-tissue infection and paresthesia — were reported in every included review and may be systematically underestimated given inconsistent reporting standards. A dedicated section on medico-legal aspects argues that, given the procedure's invasiveness and the severity of rare but serious adverse events (orbital penetration, diplopia), ZI should be reserved for cases lacking viable alternatives, supported by thorough informed consent and performed only by experienced surgeons.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates complication-incidence ranges for sinusitis, soft-tissue infection, oroantral fistula, paresthesia, ocular/orbital injury, and prosthetic complications across 11 heterogeneous systematic reviews into one comparative reference.
- Directly contrasts OST vs ZAGA/AGA on sinusitis incidence (27.2% vs 3.7% in one comparative subset) and immediate-loading prevalence (22.3% vs 89.6%).
- Adds an explicit medico-legal framework (case selection, informed consent, surgeon-experience liability) largely absent from prior ZI outcome-focused reviews.

## Methodology

Umbrella review of systematic reviews (with/without meta-analysis), PRISMA 2020-compliant, PROSPERO-registered, using a PEO (Population–Exposure–Outcome) question. Searched MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, BioMed Central, Cochrane Library, and PROSPERO through 1 June 2025, English-language articles from the last 10 years only. 11 systematic reviews met inclusion criteria (each itself covering 11–101 primary studies). Quality assessed with the AMSTAR tool. No meta-analysis performed due to cross-review heterogeneity; data synthesized narratively in tabular form.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| 12-year cumulative survival (Chrcanovic 2016, n=4556 ZIs) | 95.21% |
| 6-year survival (Brennard-Roper 2023) | 96.2%; failure 0.7%/year |
| Survival range (Kammerer 2023) | OST 90.3–100%; ZAGA 90.4–100% |
| Sinusitis (most frequent complication, range across reviews) | ~2.4–27.2% (higher with OST/Brånemark technique) |
| Sinusitis, OST vs ZAGA (comparative subset) | 27.2% vs 3.7% (statistically significant) |
| Soft-tissue infection | ~2.0–7.5% |
| Oroantral fistula | 0.4–13% (technique-dependent) |
| Paresthesia | 0.55–10.78%; usually self-limiting (1 wk–3 mo) |
| Immediate loading prevalence, OST vs ZAGA | 22.3% vs 89.6% |
| Peri-implant recession at ZAGA abutment interface (pooled subset) | 52.9% (9/17 patients) |

**Clinical bottom line**: ZI placement (OST or ZAGA) for severely atrophic edentulous maxillae achieves consistently high implant survival, but complications — sinusitis chief among them — are common and may be under-reported; ZAGA appears associated with lower sinusitis risk and far higher immediate-loading use than OST. Given the invasiveness and potential severity of rare complications (orbital penetration), ZI candidacy should be limited to cases without viable alternatives, with rigorous informed consent and an experienced surgical team.
