## Three-line Summary
Case report of 2 patients with severely resorbed anterior maxillary alveolar ridges treated with ARi® basal cortical implants (4.0–4.1 × 9.0–10.0 mm) combined with BCP grafting and collagen membranes; one case additionally received a VIP flap.
Both implants achieved 40 N/cm² insertion torque at placement; at 2-year follow-up, peri-implant tissues were stable with satisfactory esthetic outcomes.
Basal cortical anchorage improves primary stability in severely resorbed sites but does not eliminate bone augmentation requirements in the anterior esthetic zone.

## Summary
This case report evaluates the clinical applicability of the ARi® Implant System (MegaGen), a basal cortical implant designed to achieve primary anchorage in dense basal cortical bone rather than compromised cancellous alveolar bone, in two patients with severely resorbed anterior maxillary ridges. The ARi® system incorporates the XPEED® nanostructured calcium-incorporating surface to enhance early osseointegration and the Magic Cuff® connection to support peri-implant soft tissue stability. In both cases, adjunctive BCP bone grafting and collagen membrane placement were required to restore ridge morphology; Case 1 also employed a vascularized interpositional periosteal (VIP) flap. Primary fixation torque of 40 N/cm² was achieved at both ARi® sites despite severely compromised bone volume, and at the 2-year follow-up, peri-implant tissues were radiographically and clinically stable with satisfactory esthetic outcomes. The authors conclude that while basal cortical anchorage provides a biomechanical advantage in severely resorbed sites, it does not eliminate the need for guided bone regeneration; papillary recession and extended transmucosal pathways remain clinical challenges. Conflict of interest is notable: lead authors are affiliated with MegaGen, the ARi® manufacturer.

## Key Contributions
- First published case documentation of ARi® Implant System for severely resorbed anterior maxillary ridges
- Demonstrates 40 N/cm² primary fixation torque achievable via basal cortical anchorage in severely compromised bone (horizontal ridge atrophy)
- Confirms that BCP + collagen membrane augmentation is still required even with basal cortical implants in the anterior esthetic zone
- VIP flap + BCP shown as an effective composite strategy for anterior soft tissue and ridge contouring
- XPEED® surface rationale presented: enhanced BIC, osteoblast proliferation, and higher removal torque in prior preclinical studies
- 2-year stable outcomes at both sites; longer-term multicenter RCT data still needed

## Methodology
| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Prospective case report |
| n | 2 patients, 3 implants (2 ARi® + 1 Blue Diamond control) |
| ARi® dimensions | 4.0 × 9.0 mm (Case 1, #22); 4.1 × 10.0 mm (Case 2, #11) |
| ARi® surface | XPEED® — nanostructured calcium-incorporating |
| Augmentation | BCP (Bone Matrix I) + collagen membrane (both cases); VIP flap (Case 1 only); Lyoplant collagen membrane used in Case 2 |
| Insertion torque | 40 N/cm² at placement (both ARi® implants) |
| Follow-up | 24 months; panoramic radiographs annually |
| Antibiotic | Amoxicillin 500 mg TID × 7 days (pre-op day 1 through post-op day 6) |

## Results
| Case | Patient | Site | Implant | Augmentation | 2-yr Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 58M, heavy smoker | #22 (early: ~3 mo post-extraction) | ARi® 4.0 × 9.0 mm | BCP + collagen membrane + VIP flap | Stable; papillary recession; laminate veneer added |
| 1 (control) | Same patient | #11 (healed socket) | Blue Diamond 4.1 × 10.0 mm | BCP + collagen membrane | Stable |
| 2 | 50M, non-smoker | #11 (early: ~5 mo post-extraction) | ARi® 4.1 × 10.0 mm | BCP + Lyoplant collagen membrane | Stable; extended transmucosal path noted |

Key biomechanical finding: the ARi® thread design engages basal cortical bone, theoretically distributing occlusal forces and reducing crestal bone stress. FEA support from cited literature, but direct FEA of ARi® was not performed in this report.

Residual esthetic challenges: (1) papillary recession at the lateral incisor in Case 1 requiring laminate veneer salvage; (2) apically placed implant in Case 2 creates an extended transmucosal pathway, increasing peri-implant hygiene difficulty.
