## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD420251032758) of systematic reviews examining bruxism as a risk factor for implant-supported prosthesis failure, screening 2395 records to identify 8 eligible systematic reviews (5 with meta-analysis), published January 1994–April 2025.

Quality varied widely across the 8 included SRs (2 with perfect AMSTAR scores, 1 with a perfect Glenny et al score); more recent SRs and meta-analyses consistently found bruxism to be an important risk factor, with one meta-analysis reporting an odds ratio of 4.68 for implant failure in bruxers vs non-bruxers.

Clinical implication: bruxism should be treated as a significant risk factor for implant-supported prosthesis failure warranting pre-treatment screening and management; key limitation is substantial heterogeneity across the underlying systematic reviews (populations, bruxism-diagnosis criteria, outcome definitions).

## Summary

This umbrella review (Barboza et al, LECOM SDM / National Institute of Dental Sciences Brazil, 2026) is the first umbrella-level synthesis to specifically assess the methodological quality and directionality of systematic reviews examining bruxism as a driver of implant-supported prosthesis failure. Searching PubMed/MEDLINE and Scopus (1994–2025) plus reference-list hand-searching yielded 2395 records, narrowed to 8 eligible systematic reviews (5 including meta-analysis). Quality appraisal via AMSTAR and the Glenny et al checklist revealed wide variance among the included SRs despite a converging directional conclusion: more recent SRs and meta-analyses consistently identified bruxism as an important implant-failure risk factor, and one meta-analysis reported an odds ratio of 4.68 for failure in bruxers versus non-bruxers. This upgrades a previously narrative-level clinical claim (see [[implants/survival/song-2021-implant-complications-bruxism-patients]]) to SR+MA-level evidence, though heterogeneity across the primary systematic reviews (bruxism-diagnosis methods, outcome definitions) remains a caveat.

## Key Contributions
- First umbrella review to isolate bruxism specifically as the exposure of interest for implant-supported prosthesis failure (vs prior umbrella reviews covering implant failure risk factors broadly).
- Dual quality appraisal (AMSTAR + Glenny et al) applied across 8 included SRs, showing methodological quality is uneven even where directional conclusions converge.
- Reports a headline pooled effect size (OR 4.68) from one of the 5 meta-analyses, giving a quantitative anchor for the bruxism-implant-failure association.
- PROSPERO-registered a priori (CRD420251032758).

## Methodology
- **Design**: Umbrella review (SR of SRs)
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus + manual reference-list search
- **Date range**: January 1994 – April 2025
- **n**: 2395 records screened → 50 full-text assessed → 8 SRs included (5 with meta-analysis)
- **Quality tools**: AMSTAR; Glenny et al checklist
- **Outcome**: implant-supported prosthesis failure/complication rate stratified by bruxism status
