## Three-line Summary

Controlled animal histologic study (양 하악, 5마리, 임플란트 40개, 6주) comparing very high insertion torque (삽입 토크, HT = mean 110 Ncm) versus low torque (LT = mean 10 Ncm) using split-mouth design with sacrifice at 1–6 weeks.

HT implants showed significantly greater bone apposition and removal torque than LT at every timepoint; critically, **no bone necrosis was observed** in the HT group despite 110 Ncm in dense cortical bone.

HT caused a significant transient drop in primary stability at 7 days (biomechanical remodeling dip), which ISQ/RFA failed to detect — demonstrating that RFA is insensitive to the early bone-implant interface remodeling dynamics.

## Summary

Trisi et al. (2011) performed the only histologic animal study to directly test whether very high insertion torque causes peri-implant bone necrosis (osseocompression necrosis). Forty tapered implants were placed in the mandibles of 5 sheep using an extraoral approach — HT group (mean 110 Ncm) on one side and LT group (mean 10 Ncm) on the contralateral side. Animals were sacrificed at 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6 weeks. Histomorphometry showed HT produced significantly greater bone apposition than LT at every healing timepoint. Removal torque was also consistently higher for HT. Critically, histologic examination found **no evidence of bone necrosis** (no osteocyte lacunar emptying, no avascular necrotic zones) in the HT group despite the extreme torque value of 110 Ncm. HT did cause a significant transient dip in primary stability at 7 days — the classic early biomechanical remodeling phenomenon — but RFA/ISQ was unable to detect this change. The authors conclude that high torque in dense cortical bone does not cause necrosis and actually enhances bone formation, supporting the mechanistic basis for high-torque immediate loading protocols.

## Key Contributions

- **Directly refutes the osseocompression necrosis hypothesis**: 110 Ncm in dense cortical bone produced no histologic evidence of bone necrosis
- Demonstrates that high IT promotes *more* bone apposition than low IT in the early healing weeks (1–6 weeks) — mechanistic support for high-torque immediate loading
- Identifies the **7-day primary stability dip** in HT implants as a histologic remodeling event, not pathologic — the bone briefly resorbs then reforms in higher quantity
- Exposes a critical RFA/ISQ limitation: **ISQ cannot detect the early stability dip or differentiate bone apposition levels** — cannot be used as a real-time bone quality monitor
- Provides the animal/histologic pillar of the "high IT is safe" argument (balanced against Marconcini 2018's clinical 3-year harm data)

## Methodology

**Design:** Controlled split-mouth animal experiment  
**Animal:** 5 hybrid sheep; mandible (extraoral approach); 40 tapered-screw-form implants  
**Groups (split-mouth, same jaw):**
- HT: mean 110 Ncm (test, dense cortical bone site)
- LT: mean 10 Ncm (control, contralateral side)

**Sacrifice:** 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 weeks (1 animal per timepoint, ~8 implants each)  
**Outcomes:**
- Removal torque (Ncm)
- RFA / ISQ (Osstell)
- Histology: bone necrosis markers (osteocyte lacunar emptying, avascular zones)
- Histomorphometry: bone apposition area, bone-implant contact (BIC%)

## Results

| Timepoint | HT bone apposition | LT bone apposition | HT removal torque | LT removal torque |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 week | Higher* | Lower | Higher* | Lower |
| 2 weeks | Higher* | Lower | Higher* | Lower |
| 3 weeks | Higher* | Lower | Higher* | Lower |
| 4 weeks | Higher* | Lower | Higher* | Lower |
| 6 weeks | Higher* | Lower | Higher* | Lower |

*Significantly higher (p values not available in abstract)

**Bone necrosis:** None observed in HT group — no osteocyte lacunar emptying, no avascular necrotic zones

**Primary stability (ISQ):**
- HT: significant **stability drop at day 7** (early remodeling dip)
- LT: significant stability **increase at week 4** (secondary stability onset)
- RFA could not detect these histologic/biomechanical dynamics
