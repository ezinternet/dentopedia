## Three-line Summary

Double-blind RCT, 68 implants in 21 patients (straight n=33, concave n=35), IPX 4×10 mm subcrestal hexagonal internal connection implants, one-abutment-one-time, 24-month follow-up in Spain.

Concave abutments showed significantly less MBL during the pre-loading healing phase (avg −0.18 vs −0.54 mm, p=0.002); mixed linear regression confirmed significantly less cumulative MBL in the concave group (effect sizes 1.948–2.105, p=0.006–0.026), and — critically — greater abutment height paradoxically mitigated bone loss in the concave group while worsening it in the straight group (interaction p<0.01).

Concave (slim) abutments offer a bone-protective advantage concentrated in the early pre-loading phase and provide a more favorable height–bone relationship; clinical magnitude is small (<0.5 mm over 2 years) and data beyond 24 months are lacking.

## 세줄요약

이중맹검 무작위대조시험(RCT), 임플란트 68개(21명), IPX 4×10 mm 치조골하 내부육각형 연결, 24개월 추적(스페인).

보철 전 8주 시점에서 오목형(Slim) 아부트먼트군의 평균 변연골소실(MBL)이 유의하게 적었고(−0.18 vs −0.54 mm, p=0.002), 혼합선형회귀 분석에서 오목형 군의 전 기간 MBL이 유의하게 적음 확인(p=0.006–0.026); 오목형 군에서 아부트먼트 높이 증가가 오히려 골소실을 감소시키는 역방향 교호작용 확인(p<0.01).

오목형 아부트먼트의 골 보호 효과는 보철 전 초기 치유기에 집중되며, 임상적 차이 크기는 작고(<0.5 mm) 2년 이후 장기 근거는 아직 없다.

## Summary

This double-blind RCT (Feb 2020–July 2021, Spain) compared straight vs. concave (Slim) abutments on marginal bone remodeling over 24 months. All implants were IPX hexagonal internal connection, 4 mm diameter × 10 mm length, placed subcrestally (~1 mm below alveolar crest) using the one-abutment-one-time protocol. The study extends the same group's previously published 6-month RCT (Pérez-Sayáns et al.).

The primary finding is that concave abutments appear to protect marginal bone primarily during the **surgical healing phase** (before prosthetic loading). This early advantage is captured in both simple t-test comparisons and — more robustly — in mixed linear regression models accounting for within-patient implant clustering. Post-loading, the two groups converge in simple comparisons, though the regression model still shows a sustained protective effect.

The most clinically novel finding is the **abutment height × shape interaction**: in straight abutments, taller height is associated with more bone loss (consistent with prior literature). In concave abutments, taller height is associated with *less* bone loss — a reversed effect suggesting concave geometry confers resilience to height-related biological width challenges, possibly through improved biological width accommodation or a platform-switching-like effect.

## Key Contributions

1. **24-month extension** of the 6-month Pérez-Sayáns 2023 dataset — longest follow-up RCT comparing full-length straight vs. concave abutment shape on MBL
2. **Quantification of height × shape interaction**: straight group → height worsens MBL; concave group → height mitigates MBL (p<0.01, mixed regression)
3. **Early protective effect confirmed**: concave advantage is already present at 8 weeks (pre-loading); interaction terms at T8wk and T6mo are significant; T24mo reaches borderline significance mesially (p=0.072) and full significance distally and on average (<0.01)
4. Use of GAMs (generalized additive models) to visualize non-parametric height effect in addition to parametric mixed models

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Balanced, randomized, double-blind, two parallel arms, no control group |
| N (enrolled) | 80 implants; final 68 (21 patients) after attrition |
| Implant | IPX hexagonal internal connection (Galimplant), 4×10 mm, subcrestal |
| Straight arm | n=33; straight aesthetic antirotational abutment, 2 or 3 mm height |
| Concave arm | n=35; Slim antirotational abutment (concave along full length), 2 or 3 mm height |
| Protocol | One-abutment-one-time; prosthesis at 8 weeks post-surgery |
| Prosthesis | Screw-retained metal-porcelain single crowns, 20 N torque |
| Follow-up | TB (baseline), T0 (8 wks), T6 (6 months), T24 (24 months) |
| Primary outcome | MBL/MBG by digital intraoral radiology; ISQ by RFA (Ostell) |
| Statistics | Mixed linear regression + GAMs; per-protocol analysis |
| Blinding | Patients + statistician blinded; radiographic evaluator not blinded |

## Results

### MBL by Phase

| Phase | Straight (avg ± SD) | Concave (avg ± SD) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline → T0 (surgical healing) | −0.54 ± 0.53 mm | −0.18 ± 0.37 mm | **0.002** |
| T0 → T6 (early loading) | −0.08 ± 0.47 mm | −0.01 ± 0.34 mm | 0.487 |
| T6 → T24 (late loading) | +0.38 ± 0.41 mm | +0.38 ± 0.68 mm | 0.972 |
| T0 → T24 (total post-load) | +0.30 ± 0.58 mm | +0.37 ± 0.72 mm | 0.689 |

Negative = bone loss; positive = bone gain (MBG).

### Mixed Linear Regression (adjusted for patient clustering)

- Concave group coefficient (all models): +1.948 to +2.105 (p=0.006–0.026) — concave = less MBL
- Abutment height (straight group): positive coefficient — taller → more MBL
- Concave × height interaction: −1.058 to −1.144 (p=0.001–0.008) — in concave, taller → less MBL

### Stability (ISQ at 24 months)
- Straight: 70.2 ± 8.7 | Concave: 71.0 ± 9.1 — no significant difference

### Adverse Events
- Implant failure: 2/68 (2.6%)
- Abutment fracture: 1/68 (0.8%)
- No mucositis, crown mobility, or ceramic chipping
