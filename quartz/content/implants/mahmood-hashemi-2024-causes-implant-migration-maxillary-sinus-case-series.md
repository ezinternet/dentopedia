## Three-line Summary

Retrospective case series (J Dent Shiraz 2024, Tehran University of Medical Sciences), 39 patients over 25 years (1996–2021) whose dental implants migrated into the maxillary sinus, all retrieved via the Caldwell-Luc approach under local anesthesia by a single surgeon.

Displacement occurred during placement in 22/39 (inadequate bone height 11, over-drilling 11, closed sinus lift 6, immediate placement 3) and postoperatively in the rest (healing-abutment stage 5, prosthetic stage 3 — both from failed osseointegration); no complications over 2–10-year follow-up.

Clinical implication: thin residual bone <5 mm should be sinus-lifted before placement to prevent migration, and Caldwell-Luc is a reliable low-complication retrieval technique — but displacement makes 31/39 patients reluctant to re-treat.

## Summary

A 25-year single-surgeon case series from Tehran that answers a practical question the technique papers skip: *at which step does an implant actually fall into the sinus, and does Caldwell-Luc reliably get it back out?* Across 39 referred patients, more than half of displacements (22/39) happened **during placement** — split between inadequate bone height, over-drilling/wide socket, crestal (closed) sinus lift, and immediate placement — while the remainder occurred **postoperatively** at the healing-abutment or prosthetic stage, all tied to failed osseointegration. Every case was retrieved with the **Caldwell-Luc** intraoral bony-window approach under local anesthesia, with no complications over 2–10 years. It is the large real-world Caldwell-Luc counterpart to the extended-window technique ([[implants/an-2017-treatment-dental-implant-displacement-maxillary-sinus]]) and the impact-quantification study ([[implants/lee-2024-characteristics-impacts-implant-displacement-maxillary-sinus]]).

## Key Contributions

1. **Stage-by-stage cause map** of implant migration into the sinus (intraoperative vs postoperative sub-causes).
2. **All-Caldwell-Luc** real-world evidence (n=39, 25 years) with uneventful 2–10-year follow-up.
3. Highlights the **patient-reluctance** cost — 31/39 declined re-treatment after displacement.

## Methodology

Retrospective case series; 39 patients (21F/18M, age 28–73) referred over 25 years for an implant displaced into the maxillary antrum. Panoramic + CBCT localization. Preop Co-amoxiclav 625 mg TID (48 h) + 0.2% chlorhexidine rinse. **Caldwell-Luc under local anesthesia**: carbide bur #5 osteotomy under saline irrigation → lateral-wall bony window → sinus-membrane incision → retrieval by suction or rongeur → 4-0 Vicryl closure. Postop antibiotics (1 week), nasal decongestant, analgesic; nose-blowing avoidance 2 weeks; CHX rinse twice daily ×7 days. Descriptive analysis only; single surgeon.

## Results

**Causes of migration (n=39):**

| Stage | Cause | M | F | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intraoperative | Inadequate bone height | 6 | 5 | 11 |
| Intraoperative | Over-drilling / wide socket | 4 | 7 | 11 |
| Intraoperative | Closed (crestal) sinus lift | 2 | 4 | 6 |
| Intraoperative | Immediate placement (fresh socket) | 2 | 1 | 3 |
| Postoperative | Healing-abutment placement (no osseointegration) | 3 | 2 | 5 |
| Postoperative | Prosthetic procedure (inadequate osseointegration) | 1 | 2 | 3 |

- Displacement **during placement = 22/39**; the dominant intraoperative mechanism is loss of primary stability (poor bone quality/quantity, over-preparation, sinus perforation).
- **31/39 patients (13M, 18F) reluctant** to undergo further implant placement afterward.
- **No complications** over 2–10-year follow-up.
- **Prevention threshold**: residual bone height <5 mm → perform a sinus lift before placement (echoing Sgaramella: bone <5 mm at 19/24 sites, sinus lift omitted in 15/21).
- Literature comparators: Manor (55 cases, Caldwell-Luc in 52; sinusitis + oroantral communication most common complications), Hamdoon (11; Caldwell-Luc least challenging), Mumtaz, Maciel.

**Why Caldwell-Luc (authors' argument):** gold standard for sinus entry regarding mucosal regeneration, better healing, less inflammation, feasibility under local anesthesia, and fewer complications than endoscopic surgery (which needs general anesthesia, costlier equipment, longer operating time).
