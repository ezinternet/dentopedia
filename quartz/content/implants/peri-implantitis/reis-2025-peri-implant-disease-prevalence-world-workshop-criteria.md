## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (20 studies of 1979 screened; MEDLINE, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science; mixed-effects model; JBI appraisal) estimating worldwide peri-implant disease prevalence using exclusively the 2017 World Workshop standardized criteria — the first SR+MA to apply this diagnostic standard.

Peri-implant mucositis: 63.0% patient-level (CI 57.6–68.2%) and 59.2% implant-level; peri-implantitis: 25.0% patient-level (CI 21.1–29.3%) and 18.0% implant-level; non-smoker implant-level estimates were 38.2% and 5.2% respectively — all substantially higher than or confirming earlier estimates, with significant continental variation.

Approximately two in three implant patients have peri-implant mucositis and one in four have peri-implantitis, constituting a major public health challenge requiring standardized prevention protocols.

## Summary

This 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis (Reis et al., J Dent) is the first to estimate worldwide peri-implant disease prevalence using exclusively the 2017 World Workshop criteria. Including 20 cross-sectional studies identified from 1979 records across MEDLINE, Scopus, Embase, and Web of Science, a mixed-effects meta-analysis showed peri-implant mucositis in 63.0% of patients and 59.2% of implants, and peri-implantitis in 25.0% of patients and 18.0% of implants. These estimates are substantially higher than the pre-2017 figure of 43% mucositis from Derks & Tomasi 2015, largely because the 2017 WW criteria (BOP + ≥3mm probing depth increase or bone loss) have broader mucositis inclusion. Continental subgroup analysis showed significant geographic variation in both diseases. Non-smoker rates were markedly lower (mucositis 38.2%, peri-implantitis 5.2% implant-level). Risk of bias was high in 50% of studies, indicating ongoing data quality issues. The authors conclude this constitutes a significant and growing public health challenge.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA restricted to studies using the 2017 World Workshop standardized diagnostic criteria
- 20 studies vs 11 in derks-2015 — larger and more homogeneous evidence base
- Mixed-effects model with patient-level and implant-level analysis
- Reveals mucositis prevalence (63%) is substantially higher than pre-standardization estimates (43%)
- Continental subgroup: significant geographic differences in peri-implant disease burden
- Non-smoker baseline: peri-implantitis 5.2% implant-level — key for risk stratification
- Directly addresses the definition-heterogeneity limitation derks-2015 identified as its primary weakness

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis (mixed-effects model)
- **Diagnostic inclusion criteria**: 2017 World Workshop Classification only (cross-sectional studies)
- **Databases**: MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science
- **Screening**: 1979 records identified → 20 studies included
- **Risk of bias**: JBI Critical Appraisal Tool for Studies Reporting Prevalence Data
- **Outcomes**: Patient-level and implant-level prevalence for peri-implant mucositis and peri-implantitis
- **Subgroups**: Continental, smoking status

## Results

| Disease | Level | Prevalence | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peri-implant mucositis | Patient | 63.0% | 57.6–68.2% |
| Peri-implant mucositis | Implant | 59.2% | 55.8–62.4% |
| Peri-implantitis | Patient | 25.0% | 21.1–29.3% |
| Peri-implantitis | Implant | 18.0% | 15.8–20.5% |
| Mucositis (non-smoker) | Implant | 38.2% | 33.4–43.2% |
| Peri-implantitis (non-smoker) | Implant | 5.2% | 3.6–7.5% |

Risk of bias: 50% high, 45% moderate, 5% low. Continental differences: significant for both diseases (p<.05).
