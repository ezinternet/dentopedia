## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (Shetty & Menaka 2023, JISP) of platelet concentrates (PRF/CGF/L-PRF) for peri-implantitis treatment, searching 5 databases (441 hits) down to 4 eligible studies (2 RCTs, 1 prospective trial, 1 case series; 130 implants), of which only 1 directly compared platelet concentrates against open flap debridement (OFD).

The single PC-vs-OFD RCT showed PRF membrane significantly improved bleeding on probing, probing depth, clinical attachment level, keratinized mucosa, and recession vs. OFD alone at 6 months; the other three studies (PC combined with graft materials against varied comparators) also showed clinical improvement, with the largest bleeding-on-probing reduction (24.32%) seen combining CGF with a xenograft.

High heterogeneity in study design, sample size, and PC preparation protocol prevented meta-analysis, and the authors explicitly label the evidence "preliminary" and "promising but not conclusive" — this should not be read as an established indication for platelet concentrates in peri-implantitis treatment.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰(Shetty & Menaka 2023, JISP)로 혈소판농축제제(Platelet Concentrates, PC — PRF/CGF/L-PRF)를 임플란트 주위염(Peri-implantitis) 치료에 사용한 근거를 5개 데이터베이스에서 검색(441편 중 최종 4편 — RCT 2, 전향적 시험 1, 증례군 1, 임플란트 130개)했으며, 이 중 개방판막소파술(Open Flap Debridement, OFD)과 직접비교한 연구는 1편뿐.

유일한 PC-vs-OFD RCT는 PRF막이 6개월 시점 탐침시출혈(Bleeding on Probing, BOP)·탐침깊이(Probing Depth, PD)·임상부착수준(Clinical Attachment Level, CAL)·각화점막(Keratinized Mucosa, KM)·치은퇴축에서 OFD 단독 대비 유의한 개선을 보였고, 나머지 3편(PC+골이식재 vs 다양한 대조군)도 개선을 보였으며 CGF+이종골(Xenograft) 병용군에서 BOP 감소가 24.32%로 최대.

연구설계·표본크기·PC 제조법의 높은 이질성으로 메타분석은 불가했고, 저자들은 근거를 "예비적(preliminary)"·"유망하나 결정적이지 않음(promising but not conclusive)"으로 명시 — 임플란트 주위염에서 PC를 확립된 적응증으로 간주해서는 안 됨.

## Summary

This systematic review (Shetty & Menaka 2023, *Journal of Indian Society of Periodontology*, PROSPERO CRD42020202363) asks whether platelet concentrates (PCs — PRP, PRF, L-PRF, CGF) improve clinical/radiographic outcomes when used to treat peri-implantitis, compared against open flap debridement (OFD) or conventional flap therapy.

Searching 5 databases plus handsearch (441 records, English, through Dec 2020) yielded only **4 eligible studies** meeting the ≥5-patient, ≥6-month-follow-up criteria: 2 RCTs (Hamzacebi 2015, Isler 2018), 1 prospective clinical trial (Guler 2017), and 1 case series (Martins 2019), totaling 130 treated implants. Critically, **only Hamzacebi 2015 directly answers the PICO question** (PRF membrane vs. OFD); the other three studies used PC combined with graft materials against different, non-OFD comparators (collagen membrane, xenograft combinations), so the review is really synthesizing a heterogeneous mix of "PC + something" interventions rather than a clean PC-vs-standard-care comparison.

Findings across the four studies were directionally positive — PRF/CGF/L-PRF, whether alone or combined with graft materials, improved BOP, PD, CAL, KM, and reduced mucosal recession at 6–12 months — but the authors are explicit that **high heterogeneity in study design, sample size, surgical technique, and PC centrifugation protocol precluded meta-analysis**, and that with only one true comparative RCT the evidence is "preliminary," "promising," but **not conclusive**.

## Key Contributions

- First SR focused **exclusively** on platelet concentrates for peri-implantitis treatment outcomes (distinct from prior general implant-therapy PC reviews).
- Clear PICO-driven synthesis of BOP, PD, MBL (primary) and CAL, MR, KM (secondary) outcomes across the 4 included studies, with formal Cochrane risk-of-bias grading.
- Transparently identifies the central evidentiary gap in this space: essentially only one comparative RCT (Hamzacebi 2015, PRF vs. OFD, n=38) exists as of the search date (through 2020).

## Methodology

- PRISMA-guided SR, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42020202363); no meta-analysis performed due to heterogeneity.
- 5 databases (PubMed, Google Scholar, ProQuest, Cochrane, Ovid MEDLINE) + handsearch; English language, through Dec 31, 2020.
- Inclusion: RCT/CCT/case series/case-control, ≥5 patients, ≥6-month follow-up, PI treatment combined with PC, human studies only.
- Two independent reviewers with third-reviewer arbitration; Cochrane risk-of-bias tool via RevMan 5.4.1.
- Planned analysis: weighted mean difference with 95% CI, I² heterogeneity statistic — meta-analysis abandoned given high heterogeneity and RCT scarcity.

## Results

| Study | Design | n (implants) | Follow-up | Intervention | Comparator | Key finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hamzacebi 2015 | RCT | 38 | 3, 6 mo | PRF membrane | OFD | Significant improvement in BOP, PD, CAL gain, KM gain, recession vs. OFD alone at 6 mo — **the only true PC-vs-OFD comparison in this review** |
| Guler 2017 | Prospective clinical trial | 35 | 6 mo | PTG + PRF membrane | Xenograft + collagen membrane + PRF membrane | Clinical improvement in both arms; no clean PC-absent control |
| Isler 2018 | RCT | 52 | 6, 12 mo | Bio-Oss + CGF | Bio-Oss + collagen membrane (Bio-Gide) | Largest BOP reduction (24.32% ± 11.22%) at 6 mo; significant MBL improvement with CGF to 12 mo |
| Martins 2019 | Case series | 5 | 6 mo | L-PRF + xenograft | None | Greatest PD reduction and significant CAL gain, but uncontrolled |

- Pooled mucosal recession across PRF-treated cohorts: 0.62 ± 0.49 mm (baseline) → 0.14 ± 0.28 mm (6 months), p<0.001 — less recession than the ~1 mm typically expected after peri-implant surgery.
- Risk-of-bias: studies graded high-to-moderate quality; randomization explicitly reported only by Isler 2018; participant/personnel blinding unclear in all 4 studies; outcome-assessor blinding present in only 1 study; attrition bias flagged in 2 studies.
- **No meta-analysis** — heterogeneity in design, sample size, surgical/decontamination technique, and PC centrifugation protocol was judged too high.
- Author conclusion (verbatim intent): PRF over conventional therapy was beneficial; PC combined with grafts also showed benefit; but "due to a low number of well-controlled RCTs without a standardized comparison group, a definitive conclusion is not possible" — the role of PC in peri-implantitis treatment is "still in its infancy."

**Clinical read**: This SR should be cited as hypothesis-generating/promising signal for PRF-family adjuncts in peri-implantitis surgical therapy — not as practice-changing evidence. The evidence base is thin (4 studies, 1 direct comparator trial, 130 implants) and heterogeneous; it does not support routine adoption of platelet concentrates as standard-of-care for peri-implantitis over established surgical/reconstructive approaches.
