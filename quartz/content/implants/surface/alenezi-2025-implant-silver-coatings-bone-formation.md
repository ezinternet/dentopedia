## Three-line Summary
Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA, INPLASY202590035) of 16 animal studies (Wistar rats, NZW rabbits, Sprague–Dawley rats, Labrador/beagle dogs, pigs, Merino sheep) comparing silver-coated vs uncoated titanium implants on bone-to-implant contact (BIC), bone area (BA), and bone volume/total volume (BV/TV).
Pooled BIC significantly favored Ag-coated implants (Z=2.01, p=0.04); pooled BA (Z=1.09, p=0.28) and BV/TV (Z=0.35, p=0.73) showed no significant difference; heterogeneity was very high across all three outcomes (I²=96–99%).
Ag coatings appear to improve interfacial bone contact without harming peri-implant bone volume in animal models — no included study reported a detrimental bone effect — but evidence is short-term (≤6 months) and heterogeneous, so dose-response and long-term/clinical translation remain open.

## Summary
This systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 16 animal studies to evaluate whether silver (Ag) antibacterial coatings on titanium implants alter bone formation, addressing a long-standing concern that Ag's dose-dependent cytotoxicity might impair osseointegration even as it suppresses peri-implant infection. Across a range of animal species and Ag-incorporation methods (nanotubular structures, plasma immersion ion implantation, electrodeposition, atomic layer deposition, wet chemical plating, magnetron sputtering, hybrid coatings), pooled bone-to-implant contact (BIC) significantly favored Ag-coated implants, while pooled bone area (BA) and bone volume/total volume (BV/TV) showed no significant difference from uncoated controls. No included study reported a detrimental bone effect from Ag. Heterogeneity was very high (I²=96–99%) for all three outcomes, and most studies followed animals for ≤6 months, so the findings support Ag's dual antibacterial/interfacial-bone-contact role in animal models without yet resolving long-term or dose-response questions relevant to clinical translation.

## Key Contributions
- Meta-analytic evidence that Ag-coated Ti implants significantly improve BIC (Z=2.01, p=0.04) without significantly changing BA or BV/TV — i.e., Ag's benefit appears localized to the bone-implant interface rather than overall peri-implant bone volume.
- No included study reported bone-formation harm from Ag, addressing a recurring cytotoxicity concern in the coating literature.
- Contextualizes Ag against Zn (favorable bactericidal effect + enhanced osteoblastic activity) and Cu (broad-spectrum antibacterial but greater cytotoxicity risk at elevated release) coatings.
- Proposes an immunomodulatory mechanism beyond direct antibacterial action: Ag-driven macrophage M1→M2 polarization and pro-angiogenic signaling supporting osteogenesis.

## Methodology
PRISMA-guided SR+MA (INPLASY202590035); PubMed/Web of Science/Scopus search (May 2025, ≤20-year window, English only); PICO = animal models / Ag-modified Ti implants / unmodified Ti implants / bone formation (BIC, BA, BV/TV). 16 animal studies met eligibility. Quality assessed via ARRIVE 2.0 (21 items); risk of bias via SYRCLE RoB tool. Weighted mean differences pooled via Review Manager 5.3.3, random-effects model used given significant heterogeneity.

## Results
| Outcome | Effect (Ag-coated vs. control) | Heterogeneity |
|---|---|---|
| BIC | Z=2.01, p=0.04 — significant, favors Ag | I²=96% (Tau²=190.60; Chi²=555.54, df=16, p<0.001) |
| BA | Z=1.09, p=0.28 — not significant | I²=99% (Tau²=394.55; Chi²=3583.07, df=22, p<0.001) |
| BV/TV | Z=0.35, p=0.73 — not significant | I²=98% (Tau²=112.23; Chi²=2432.44, df=23, p<0.001) |

ARRIVE 2.0 quality coefficients: 0.71–0.93 (8 studies Excellent, 8 Average, 0 Poor). SYRCLE RoB: allocation concealment and outcome-assessor blinding mostly unclear.
