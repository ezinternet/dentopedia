## Three-line Summary
Systematic review + meta-analysis (RevMan 5.4.1, random-effects) of 9 in-vivo animal studies (rat/dog/pig/rabbit, 421 screened → 9 included) comparing bioactive-surface-modified titanium dental implants (collagen, bioactive peptides, BMP-2/HA, rhBMP-2/PLGA) against unmodified titanium controls over a 30-day observation window.
Bioactive surface modification significantly improved both bone-to-implant contact (BIC: MD=7.29, 95% CI 2.94–11.65, I²=50%, Z=3.28, p=0.001) and implant longevity (MD=7.52, 95% CI 3.18–11.85, I²=47%, Z=3.40, p=0.0007) versus unmodified surfaces.
Collagen-based coatings consistently promoted early bone integration and BMP-2 combinations were most effective for osseointegration, but the evidence base is animal-only with a 30-day follow-up ceiling, so human long-term translation remains unvalidated.

## Summary
This systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 9 in-vivo animal studies (rat, dog, pig, rabbit; 473 records screened down to 9 via PRISMA/PECO) comparing bioactive-surface-modified titanium dental implants — collagen coatings, bioactive peptides, BMP-2 combinations, and rhBMP-2/PLGA — against unmodified titanium controls. Using RevMan 5.4.1 random-effects models, the review found bioactive surface modification significantly improved both bone-to-implant contact (BIC: MD=7.29, 95% CI 2.94–11.65, moderate I²=50%) and implant longevity within a 30-day window (MD=7.52, 95% CI 3.18–11.85, I²=47%). Collagen-based coatings consistently promoted early bone integration across independent research groups, and BMP-2 combined with hydroxyapatite (HA) activated osseointegration most effectively, though protein-delivery dosing and repeat-application challenges were noted versus gene-delivery alternatives. All evidence is animal-derived with a short (30-day) follow-up ceiling, so the authors explicitly call for human clinical validation.

## Key Contributions
- First quantitative pooled meta-analysis (not narrative-only) of bioactive vs unmodified titanium surfaces across both BIC and implant-longevity outcomes in a unified PECO framework
- Collagen-based coatings identified as consistently favorable for early bone integration (Bae 2018, Cho W 2021 — independent groups, same direction)
- BMP-2 + HA/HAP identified as most effective for activating osseointegration (Pang 2021), with a noted trade-off: protein-delivery dosing/repeat-application challenges vs gene-delivery (rhBMP-2/PLGA, Yoo 2015)
- Direct textual comparison against Lopez-Valverde et al.'s biomolecule-coating SR/MA: both converge on BMP-based coatings as most favorable, but this review's heterogeneity (I²=50%/47%) is far lower than Lopez-Valverde's I²=99%

## Methodology
PRISMA-guided SR+MA using a PECO (Population/Exposure/Comparator/Outcome) framework; 6 databases searched (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar); SYRCLE risk-of-bias tool (animal-study-adapted) for quality appraisal; RevMan 5.4.1 random-effects meta-analysis with forest plots (MD, 95% CI). 473 records → 421 after dedup → 375 sought full-text → 9 studies included, spanning rat tibia, dog mandible/jaw, pig parietal bone/forehead, and rabbit femur/tibiae over 2–84 weeks.

## Results
| Outcome | MD | 95% CI | I² | Z | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bone-to-Implant Contact (BIC) | 7.29 | 2.94–11.65 | 50% | 3.28 | 0.001 |
| Implant longevity (30-day) | 7.52 | 3.18–11.85 | 47% | 3.40 | 0.0007 |

Included agents: Type I Collagen/GA (Bae 2018, Cho W 2021), bioactive peptide (Barros 2009), PPL10BMP (Cardoso 2017 — no BIC benefit found for this specific agent), Type I Collagen/Apatite (Cecconi 2014), vitronectin-derived peptide (Cho 2019), biomimetic active peptide P-15 (Lutz 2010), BMP-2+HA (Pang 2021 — most favorable), rhBMP-2/PLGA (Yoo 2015).
