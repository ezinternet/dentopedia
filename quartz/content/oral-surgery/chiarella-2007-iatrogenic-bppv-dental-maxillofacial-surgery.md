## Three-line Summary

Narrative review + personal case series (ENT/otorhinolaryngology perspective, 2007) describing benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) judged to be iatrogenic, arising after dental and maxillo-facial surgical procedures.

No incidence figures are reported (abstract-only source); the cases are linked to surgery mainly by temporal relationship, within the accepted post-traumatic pathophysiologic model of BPPV (otoconia displacement).

Clinical implication: dental/maxillofacial surgeons and referring ENT specialists should recognize post-operative positional vertigo as a plausible surgical-trauma complication, but a definitive cause-effect link cannot be established from case-level association alone — full text was not retrieved, limiting extraction of procedure-specific detail.

## Summary
This 2007 narrative review and personal case series, published in an ENT journal (Acta Otorhinolaryngologica Italica), examines benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV, 양성돌발성두위현훈) arising as an iatrogenic complication of dental and maxillo-facial surgery. The authors situate their observations within the widely accepted post-traumatic pathophysiologic model of BPPV — mechanical trauma or vibration during surgery is thought to dislodge otoconia (이석) from the utricle into the semicircular canals, producing positional vertigo. They report several personal cases they attribute to iatrogenic origin based on the temporal relationship between the surgical procedure and symptom onset, while explicitly noting that this temporal association alone cannot establish a definitive cause-effect link. No quantitative incidence data are available from the abstract; only a structured abstract was retrieved (full text not obtained via PMC), so procedure-specific detail (which dental/maxillofacial interventions, latency, resolution) is not extractable here.

## Key Contributions
- Establishes dental and maxillo-facial surgery as a recognized (rare) surgical-trauma risk category for BPPV in the ENT literature — an outside-specialty confirmation of a mechanism dental clinicians mostly encounter only within sinus-lift complication reporting.
- Frames the mechanism explicitly: surgical vibration/positional trauma → otoconia displacement → BPPV, independent of any single dental procedure type.
- Models appropriate epistemic caution: acknowledges that temporal association between surgery and vertigo onset is not proof of causation — a useful caveat when counseling patients or interpreting isolated post-operative vertigo reports.

## Methodology
Narrative review combined with a personal case series (small, unquantified n in the available abstract) from an ENT/otorhinolaryngology practice. No systematic database search, no control group, no statistical analysis reported.
