## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42022326584, PRISMA 2020) of 19 systematic reviews / 10 meta-analyses with quantitative pain synthesis, network meta-analysis ranking 7 nonpharmacological complementary interventions after third molar surgery.

Ozone therapy was most effective for early pain (SMD −0.84) and PRF/derivatives most effective for late pain (SMD −1.03); LLLT was least effective for early pain (SMD −0.32); surgical drainage least effective for late pain.

Findings limited by unclear risk of bias in most included reviews and lack of a standardized application protocol for each complementary intervention — clinical adoption should await further high-quality RCTs.

## Summary

This umbrella review synthesizes evidence from meta-analyses on seven nonpharmacological complementary interventions used to manage pain after third molar (wisdom tooth) surgery: platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) and derivatives (L-PRF, A-PRF), low-level laser therapy (LLLT), Kinesio taping, cryotherapy, surgical drainage, ozone therapy, and hyaluronic acid. Using corrected covered area (CCA) to handle overlapping primary studies across reviews and a frequentist network meta-analysis (fixed-effect model), the authors ranked interventions separately for early (48-72h) and late (5-7 day) postoperative pain.

## Key Contributions

- First study to rank these interventions head-to-head via network meta-analysis rather than isolated pairwise comparisons.
- Distinguishes intervention effectiveness by pain timing (early vs. late), a distinction most primary reviews collapse.
- Demonstrates that PRF preparation protocol (A-PRF vs. L-PRF, driven by centrifugation speed/time) meaningfully changes which time window benefits most — A-PRF for early pain, L-PRF for late pain.

## Methodology

- PROSPERO-registered (CRD42022326584), PRISMA 2020-compliant.
- Included: systematic reviews of RCTs with quantitative meta-analysis of pain outcomes after third molar surgery, any complementary/nonpharmacological intervention vs. no-intervention/placebo control.
- Excluded: qualitative-only SRs, primary RCTs, observational studies, narrative/scoping reviews.
- Searches: MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase (to 20 June 2022), Google Scholar top-100, hand search — no language/date limits.
- ROBIS risk-of-bias tool for each included review; CCA to quantify RCT overlap between reviews (0-5% slight → >15% very high).
- Selection rule when overlap was very high: highest-ROBIS-quality review used for main analysis; most-recent/most-RCTs review used as tiebreaker; low-overlap reviews retained for sensitivity analysis.
- Effect sizes standardized to SMD + SE; early-pain (48h+72h) and late-pain (5-7d) estimates pooled separately; frequentist network meta-analysis (netmeta package, R, fixed-effect model) used for ranking.

## Results

- 659 records screened → 22 eligible meta-analyses → 19 retained after full-text review, spanning 7 intervention types.
- Quality: 12/19 reviews high risk of bias for eligibility criteria; 6/19 for study selection; 7/19 for data collection; 9/19 for synthesis (heterogeneity/sensitivity handling).
- Overlap (CCA): Kinesio taping 80%, PRF 27.1%, LLLT 24% — all very high; final quantitative synthesis drew on 10 meta-analyses (2018-2022, 132-1060 patients, 4-21 RCTs per review).

**Early pain (primary synthesis, high-quality studies):**
- Ozone therapy: SMD −0.84 (95% CI −1.09 to −0.59) — large effect, most effective.
- Kinesio taping, surgical drainage, PRF: moderate effect.
- LLLT: SMD −0.32 (95% CI −0.49 to −0.15) — minimal effect, least effective.

**Late pain (primary synthesis):**
- PRF application: SMD −1.03 (95% CI −1.56 to −0.50) — large effect, most effective.
- Ozone therapy: SMD −0.72 (95% CI −0.99 to −0.45) — upper-moderate effect.
- Surgical drainage: SMD −0.13 (95% CI −0.38 to 0.12) — smallest, not significant.

**Sensitivity analysis** (most recent, lowest-overlap reviews) confirmed PRF derivatives and ozone therapy as top performers for both time windows; LLLT remained weakest for early pain; Kinesio taping and surgical drainage showed effect only for early pain, consistent with their mechanism being most relevant during the first 72h of peak inflammation.

**Mechanistic notes:**
- Ozone: proposed antioxidant-pathway activation (Nrf2, HIF-1a), high hydrosolubility, blockade of oxidizing algogenic receptors — but evidence base limited by few RCTs and high heterogeneity; no standardized administration protocol exists.
- PRF: A-PRF (higher monocyte fraction → faster vascularization/cytokine release) outperforms L-PRF for early pain; L-PRF (denser, slower-dissolving) outperforms A-PRF for late pain via prolonged socket protection from bacterial contamination.
