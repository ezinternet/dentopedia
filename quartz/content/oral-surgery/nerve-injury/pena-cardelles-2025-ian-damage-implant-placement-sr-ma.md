## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA; 14 observational studies; meta-analytic subset ~1636 patients) evaluating neurosensory alteration (NA) incidence stratified by implant-apex-to-mandibular-canal distance.

NA incidence was 0% at ≥2 mm and 0% at 1–2 mm, but jumped to 68% at 0–1 mm and 53% when the implant intruded into the canal; intra-canal intrusion produced hypoesthesia in 42% and hyperesthesia/neuropathic pain in 46%.

A 1 mm apex-to-canal distance may be sufficient to avoid NA — updating the classic 2 mm rule — but the estimate rests on heterogeneous retrospective studies with very wide confidence intervals, so prospective RCTs are needed.

## Summary

Peña-Cardelles and colleagues (Harvard School of Dental Medicine) pooled 14 observational studies to answer one focused question: how does the distance between the implant apex and the mandibular canal affect the risk of neurosensory alteration (NA) of the inferior alveolar nerve (IAN)? The headline finding is a **sharp threshold effect**. When implants sat ≥2 mm or even 1–2 mm from the canal, pooled NA incidence was 0%. When implants came within 0–1 mm, incidence rose to 68%, and when they intruded into the canal, to 53%.

This is the basis for the paper's central clinical claim: the long-standing **2 mm safety margin (Misch & Crawford 1990; Bartling 1999) may be safely refined to 1 mm** in the era of CBCT and digital planning. Because both the 1–2 mm and ≥2 mm bands showed 0% NA, the authors argue 1 mm of intact bone between apex and canal is the practical safety floor.

Two cautions temper the message. First, the confidence intervals for the risky bands are very wide (0–1 mm: 0.09–1.28; intrusion: 0.13–0.82), reflecting few pooled studies per band — the point estimates are directional, not precise. Second, injury still occurred despite CBCT planning, via ~0.86 mm of canal intrusion, underscoring that a good virtual plan must be faithfully translated to placement (computer-assisted implant surgery, CAIS).

## Key Contributions

- **Distance-stratified NA incidence** — the first SR+MA to report NA by discrete apex-to-canal bands (0–1, 1–2, ≥2 mm, intrusion) rather than a single lumped figure.
- **A refined safety threshold** — the authors conclude a 1 mm apex-to-canal distance "might be safe," on a pooled 1636 patients (vs Bartling's original 94). Note the hedge is theirs: this is a claim advanced on imprecise pooled estimates, not an established replacement for the 2 mm rule.
- **NA-type resolution** — canal intrusion uniquely carries high hyperesthesia/neuropathic-pain risk (46%), whereas the 0–1 mm band without intrusion is dominated by hypoesthesia (58%).
- **Planning/technique message** — 76% of NA cases in one study relied on periapical/panoramic (not 3D) planning; even CBCT-planned cases can fail if placement deviates from plan → CAIS emphasis.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis, PRISMA. Databases: MEDLINE (PubMed), Web of Science, Scopus, up to 3 Mar 2023. English, last 10 years, human subjects. Inter-reviewer concordance 92.5% (Kappa).
- **Selection funnel**: 1254 records → 771 (10-yr) → 761 (English) → 667 (human) → 496 (de-duplicated) → 58 full text → **14 included**.
- **Grouping**: implant-apex-to-canal distance 0–1 / 1–2 / ≥2 mm, plus intra-canal intrusion. NA classified per IASP as hypoesthesia vs hyperesthesia/neuropathic pain.
- **Synthesis**: SPSS v28; fixed-/random-effects by heterogeneity; forest plots.
- **Risk of bias**: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale — 5 good, several medium, remainder low quality.

## Results

**NA incidence by implant-apex-to-canal distance (~1636 patients):**

| Distance | NA incidence (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| ≥ 2 mm | 0% (0.00–0.00) |
| 1–2 mm | 0% (0.01–0.02) |
| 0–1 mm | 68% (0.09–1.28) |
| Intrusion into canal | 53% (0.13–0.82) |

**NA type by distance:**

| Distance | Hypoesthesia (95% CI) | Hyperesthesia / neuropathic pain (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 2 mm | 0% (0–0) | 0% (0–0) |
| 1–2 mm | 0% (0–0) | 0% (0.01–0.02) |
| 0–1 mm | 58% (0.06–1.11) | 10% (0.1–0.3) |
| Canal intrusion | 42% (0.42–1.26) | 46% (0.16–1.08) |

> [!warning] Confidence intervals are reproduced verbatim from the published paper and are internally inconsistent.
> Several are not interpretable as stated: a 0% point estimate is paired with a CI of 0.01–0.02 (1–2 mm band), the 0–1 mm CI upper bound exceeds 1.0 (1.28), and the canal-intrusion hypoesthesia point estimate (42%) equals its own lower bound (0.42–1.26). Treat every point estimate here as **directional only**. The one number that carries real weight is the qualitative threshold effect (0% below 1 mm of separation vs. a large jump at 0–1 mm), not any specific percentage.

- Both the 0–1 mm and intrusion groups differed significantly from the 1–2 mm and ≥2 mm groups.
- IAN most commonly injured, then lingual nerve; numbness the most common symptom (91% in one series).
- Recovery: ~80% by 6 months, 91% by 1 year; one cohort 23/1065 disturbed at 1 month, all resolved by 13 months.
- Higher risk in women (one study: 3.29× vs men; older women >60 most affected).
- **Clinical bottom line**: keep ≥1 mm of bone between the implant apex and the canal roof; treat canal intrusion as a distinct, higher-risk event (adds a 46% hyperesthesia/neuropathic-pain hazard); plan with CBCT and translate the plan faithfully (CAIS).
