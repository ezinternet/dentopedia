## Three-line Summary

Prospective blinded three-arm feasibility trial at a Brazilian cardiology institute (InCor, University of São Paulo) in atrial fibrillation patients: 20 on rivaroxaban once daily, 18 on dabigatran/apixaban twice daily, 20 on warfarin as control — 58 patients, 84 extractions, DOACs never discontinued.

Extractions were timed to trough plasma concentration (14 h after last rivaroxaban dose; 8 h after last dabigatran/apixaban dose; warfarin arm at INR 2.0–3.0), and 24-hour bleeding was 0/20 (0%) rivaroxaban, 2/18 (11.1%) dabigatran/apixaban, 2/20 (10%) warfarin — no significant difference (P=.324) and zero major bleeding events.

Trough-timed scheduling makes uninterrupted DOAC extraction operationally concrete, but this is a small preliminary feasibility trial with no discontinuation comparator, so it cannot establish that timing itself confers the safety.

발치를 최저 혈중농도 (trough) 시점에 맞춰 시행 — 리바록사반 최종 복용 14시간 후, 다비가트란/아픽사반 8시간 후, 와파린군은 국제표준화비율 (International Normalized Ratio, INR) 2.0–3.0 — 24시간 출혈은 리바록사반 0/20(0%), 다비가트란/아픽사반 2/18(11.1%), 와파린 2/20(10%)로 군간 유의차 없었고(P=.324) 대량출혈은 0건이었다.

## Summary

Most of the anticoagulant-extraction literature answers a binary question — interrupt the direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) or not — and has largely converged on "do not interrupt." This Brazilian trial from the Heart Institute (InCor) of the University of São Paulo asks the next question down: given that therapy continues, *when in the dosing interval* should the extraction happen. The protocol keys the appointment to the drug's trough plasma concentration, exploiting the fact that DOACs, unlike vitamin K antagonists, swing substantially between peak and trough within a single day.

Fifty-eight anticoagulated atrial fibrillation patients underwent 84 extractions across three arms defined by pharmacokinetics: once-daily rivaroxaban extracted 14 hours after the last dose (n=20), twice-daily dabigatran or apixaban extracted 8 hours after the last dose (n=18), and a warfarin control arm extracted at a therapeutic INR of 2.0–3.0 (n=20). No DOAC was discontinued. Bleeding was assessed at a 24-hour return visit.

The rivaroxaban arm had no bleeding of any grade. The twice-daily DOAC arm had 2 minor events (11.1%) and the warfarin control 2 minor events (10%), a difference that was not statistically significant (P=.324). No major bleeding occurred anywhere in the cohort. The authors present this as preliminary evidence that a non-discontinuation protocol is feasible in routine practice — with emphasis on preliminary, since 4 events across three arms supports feasibility rather than equivalence.

## Key Contributions

- **Converts a principle into a schedule.** "Continue the DOAC" is a policy; "extract 14 hours after the rivaroxaban dose, 8 hours after the dabigatran or apixaban dose" is something a front desk can book. This is the operational layer our other anticoagulant holdings largely leave implicit.
- **Benchmarks against uninterrupted warfarin rather than against no anticoagulant.** Uninterrupted VKA extraction at therapeutic INR is the long-accepted standard of care, so it is the fair comparator for a proposed DOAC protocol.
- **Refuses to pool DOACs by class.** Once-daily and twice-daily agents have different trough intervals, so they are separated into distinct arms with distinct timing rules — a distinction most syntheses collapse.
- **Studies the population where interruption actually costs something**: atrial fibrillation patients in a cardiology institute, where the thromboembolic downside of a held dose is not hypothetical.

## Methodology

- **Design**: prospective blind clinical trial, feasibility framing. Randomization is not stated in the abstract; allocation appears to be by anticoagulant, which is why this page is graded `prospective` rather than `rct`.
- **Setting**: Department of Dentistry, Heart Institute (InCor), Clinics Hospital of the University of São Paulo, Brazil.
- **Population**: anticoagulated patients with atrial fibrillation requiring tooth extraction.
- **Arms and timing rule**:
  - Rivaroxaban, once daily — n=20 — extraction **14 h** after last dose.
  - Dabigatran or apixaban, twice daily — n=18 — extraction **8 h** after last dose.
  - Warfarin control — n=20 — extraction at **INR 2.0–3.0**.
- **Anticoagulant handling**: DOACs continued without interruption throughout.
- **Outcome**: bleeding assessed at a **24-hour** return visit, classified minor vs major.
- **Volume**: 58 patients, 84 extractions.
- **Not available from the abstract**: local hemostatic measures, surgical vs simple extraction breakdown, arch distribution, CHA2DS2-VASc/HAS-BLED scores, renal function, concomitant antiplatelet therapy, exclusion criteria, and which party was blinded.

## Results

| Arm | Timing rule | n | Minor bleeding | Major bleeding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rivaroxaban (once daily) | 14 h post-dose | 20 | 0 (0%) | 0 |
| Dabigatran / apixaban (twice daily) | 8 h post-dose | 18 | 2 (11.1%) | 0 |
| Warfarin (control) | INR 2.0–3.0 | 20 | 2 (10%) | 0 |

- **Between-group comparison**: not statistically significant, **P = .324**.
- **Major bleeding**: **0 events** across all 58 patients and 84 extractions.
- **Direction of effect**: uninterrupted DOAC extraction at trough produced bleeding rates at or numerically below uninterrupted warfarin at therapeutic INR. With only 4 events total, this supports feasibility and not equivalence.

**Limitations that bound the reading.** The trial is small and underpowered — P=.324 means no difference was detected, not that none exists. Critically, there is **no discontinuation arm and no peak-timed arm**, so the study cannot separate the effect of trough timing from the general safety of continuation that our other holdings already establish; a patient extracted at peak concentration was never observed. Allocation was by drug rather than randomized, making the warfarin arm a different-drug comparison rather than a true control. The 24-hour follow-up window would miss delayed bleeding, a recognized DOAC pattern. Because only the abstract was retrievable, the local hemostatic protocol — which the rest of our anticoagulant literature identifies as the decisive variable — is unknown here, which limits reproduction of the protocol as published.
