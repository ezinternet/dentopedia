## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional online questionnaire administered to general dentist (GD) members of the US National Dental Practice-Based Research Network, measuring self-reported pre- and post-procedure practice for patients taking vitamin K antagonists (VKA) and direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs).

47.6% (VKA) and 41.2% (DOAC) of surveyed GDs reported "always" consulting the patient's physician before an extraction; 27.1% (VKA) and 8.7% (DOAC) reported using laboratory tests to assess bleeding risk, with the International Normalized Ratio (INR) preferred by 68.4% of those testing VKA patients while most respondents were "unsure" which test applies to DOACs.

This measures practice variation and knowledge gaps rather than clinical efficacy: reported management diverges most sharply around DOACs, where respondents could not name an assessment test — DOAC management is where practitioner uncertainty concentrates.

## Summary

This is a survey of behaviour, not a study of outcomes. An online questionnaire was administered to general dentist (GD) members of the US National Dental Practice-Based Research Network to determine their self-reported pre-operative and post-operative practices when treating patients taking vitamin K antagonists (VKA) and direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs). Respondents were queried on four domains: consultation with the patient's medical professional before dental treatment, use of laboratory tests to assess bleeding risk, measures used to control bleeding, and self-rated comfort level delivering care. No patient-level outcomes — bleeding events, complications, healing — were measured, so nothing here speaks to whether any reported practice is safe or effective.

The reported picture is one of substantial variation. Fewer than half of surveyed GDs reported "always" consulting the patient's physician before an extraction (47.6% VKA, 41.2% DOAC), meaning the majority do not treat medical consultation as an invariant step. Laboratory testing was reported by a minority and dropped sharply for DOACs (27.1% VKA versus 8.7% DOAC). Among those who do test VKA patients, INR was the preferred test (68.4%); for DOAC patients, the modal response was that respondents were "unsure" which blood test to use. Reported hemostatic practice was more consistent, converging on cellulose-based agents (65.1%), collagen-based agents (60.6%), and epinephrine infiltration (60.6%).

Read against the efficacy evidence this wiki already holds, part of the reported practice pattern points toward over-caution rather than under-treatment. The systematic-review evidence in [[drug/anticoagulants/inchingolo-2024-anticoagulation-therapy-dental-practice-sr]] and [[drug/anticoagulants/dou-2025-bleeding-dentoalveolar-doac-vka-sr-ma]] does not establish routine pre-extraction INR testing or universal physician consultation as requirements for most simple extractions in stably anticoagulated patients — yet substantial fractions of respondents reported performing them as standing practice. The contrast is stated here as a contrast between measured practice and the wiki's held efficacy evidence; this survey itself makes no such claim and is not evidence for or against any management protocol.

## Key Contributions

- Supplies a practice-behaviour axis absent from the wiki's anticoagulant holdings, which are otherwise efficacy evidence: what community general dentists report doing, split explicitly by VKA versus DOAC.
- Localises the knowledge gap to DOACs — reported laboratory testing falls to 8.7% and the modal answer on which test to use is "unsure", whereas VKA practice converges on INR.
- Maps which local hemostatic agents are actually reached for in US community practice (cellulose, collagen, epinephrine infiltration).
- Provides a baseline against which the effect of guideline dissemination or continuing education on anticoagulant management could later be measured.

## Methodology

- **Design**: cross-sectional online questionnaire survey (`evidence_level: cross-sectional`).
- **Population**: general dentist members of the US National Dental Practice-Based Research Network. Respondent N, response rate, and respondent demographics are not stated in the retrieved abstract and could not be captured — full text was not available.
- **Comparison of interest**: reported practice for VKA-treated versus DOAC-treated patients (within-respondent, not between-groups of patients).
- **Domains queried**: (1) interaction with the patient's medical professional prior to dental treatment; (2) use of laboratory tests to assess bleeding risk, and which test; (3) measures to control bleeding; (4) comfort level delivering dental care.
- **Outcomes**: proportions of respondents reporting each practice. **No clinical or patient outcomes measured.**
- **Analysis**: descriptive proportions as reported in the abstract; no inferential modelling is described in the retrieved text.

## Results

All figures are the proportion of **surveyed general dentists reporting** the practice — they are not patient outcomes and not recommendations.

| Reported practice | VKA patients | DOAC patients |
|---|---|---|
| "Always" consult the patient's physician before an extraction | 47.6% | 41.2% |
| Use laboratory tests to assess bleeding risk before an extraction | 27.1% | 8.7% |
| Preferred laboratory test among those who test | INR — 68.4% | most respondents "unsure" which test to use |

| Most-used hemostatic agent (reported) | Proportion of respondents |
|---|---|
| Cellulose-based | 65.1% |
| Collagen-based | 60.6% |
| Epinephrine infiltration (vasoconstrictor) | 60.6% |

- Authors' stated conclusions: general dentists are uncertain about the best approach for patients taking DOACs; INR is not routinely used by most GDs to assess bleeding risk in VKA patients; commonly used hemostatic agents are cellulose-based, collagen-based, and vasoconstrictors.
- Comfort level with delivering care was queried but no numeric result appears in the retrieved abstract.
- **Limitations**: self-reported practice rather than observed behaviour (recall and social-desirability effects); US practice-based research network sample, so generalisability to other health systems including Korea is limited and network members may be more research-engaged than average practitioners; response rate and non-responder characteristics unavailable, so response/selection bias cannot be quantified; **no patient outcomes measured**, so the survey cannot support any safety or efficacy statement; ingested abstract-only, so denominators, subgroup breakdowns, questionnaire wording, and comfort-level results are unavailable.
