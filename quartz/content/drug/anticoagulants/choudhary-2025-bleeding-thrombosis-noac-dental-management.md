## Three-line Summary

Narrative evidence synthesis (Cureus, 2025) on perioperative dental management of patients taking novel oral anticoagulants (NOACs/DOACs); structured search of PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane, and Scopus to October 2025 gave 412 de-duplicated records → 148 full-text → 86 included, explicitly declared non-PRISMA with no meta-analysis.

Core position: continue NOAC therapy for low-to-moderate-risk dental procedures with local hemostatics (cited prospective extraction data: 6.9% clinically significant bleeding, all conservatively managed, zero thromboembolic events; local tranexamic acid 7% lower oral-bleeding risk; cited SR of 7 studies/931 patients found minor bleeding predominant), and for higher-risk surgery omit one dose for twice-daily agents or two for once-daily, timed to pharmacokinetic trough, without bridging.

Key limitation: a Cureus narrative review is the weakest evidence tier in this category — no pooled estimates, no risk-of-bias appraisal, empty reference brackets in the retrieved text that block tracing of its numbers, and no numeric CHA₂DS₂-VASc or HAS-BLED cut-offs; use it as a decision framework, not as evidence outranking the SR/MA pages here.

## Summary

This narrative evidence synthesis addresses a gap the authors identify in dentistry: procedure-specific guidance for patients on novel oral anticoagulants (NOACs, used here interchangeably with DOACs) is fragmented, so clinicians fall back on institutional protocols or expert opinion. Its distinguishing move is to treat the problem as a genuine two-sided balance — the thromboembolic cost of interrupting anticoagulation is weighed against procedural bleeding risk, rather than bleeding being optimized in isolation.

The synthesis organizes management around four levers: pharmacokinetics (half-life, renal clearance fraction, and dosing frequency determine whether and when to omit a dose), risk stratification (CHA₂DS₂-VASc for thrombotic propensity against a low/moderate/high procedural bleeding tier), local hemostasis (topical tranexamic acid, gelatin sponge, oxidized cellulose, primary closure), and minimally invasive technique (flapless implant placement, piezoelectric osteotomy, diode/erbium laser). Reversal agents — idarucizumab for dabigatran, andexanet alfa or prothrombin complex concentrate for factor Xa inhibitors — are positioned as emergency-only tools whose prophylactic use before elective dental procedures is unwarranted and potentially pro-thrombotic.

The conclusions align with the continue-therapy consensus already well supported in this wiki by systematic reviews and meta-analyses. What this paper adds is not stronger evidence but an integrated decision structure, including an explicit dose-omission rule keyed to dosing frequency and a renal-function checkpoint. Its evidentiary standing is modest: Cureus is a low-barrier journal, the design is narrative rather than systematic, and the retrieved full text carries empty reference brackets that prevent attributing its quantitative claims to specific primary studies.

## Key Contributions

- Gives the **thromboembolic arm equal weight** to the bleeding arm, framing dental NOAC management as a balance rather than a bleeding-minimization problem.
- Ties the interruption decision to **pharmacokinetics and renal function** rather than to a fixed interval: a dose-omission rule keyed to dosing frequency (one dose for twice-daily agents, two for once-daily) with appointments scheduled at trough.
- Cross-tabulates a **three-tier procedural bleeding risk** ladder (low: supragingival scaling, routine restoration, single extraction → moderate: multiple extractions, single implant → high: extensive flap surgery, multiple implants) against CHA₂DS₂-VASc thrombotic risk.
- Consolidates local hemostatics, minimally invasive technique, and reversal agents into a single framework, with reversal explicitly demoted to a last-resort role in dentistry.
- Explicitly rejects **bridging anticoagulation** for dental procedures on the grounds that it carries its own hemorrhagic burden.

## Methodology

- **Design**: narrative evidence synthesis. The authors state core PRISMA *principles* were followed (structured search, predefined eligibility, dual independent screening, transparent selection reporting) but that this is **not** a PRISMA-compliant systematic review.
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, Scopus — inception to **October 2025**; no language restriction, non-English articles with English abstracts screened.
- **Supplementary**: hand-searched reference lists; guidelines from the American Dental Association, European Society of Cardiology, and International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis; AAOMS and IADR conference abstracts; targeted (non-exhaustive) grey literature.
- **Inclusion**: RCTs, prospective/retrospective cohorts, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, expert consensus documents on perioperative dental management of NOAC patients; last-decade publications prioritized.
- **Exclusion**: case reports (unless illustrating rare critical complications), in vitro/animal-only studies, pharmacokinetic studies unrelated to dental procedures, articles without perioperative relevance, insufficient methodological detail.
- **Screening**: two independent reviewers on titles/abstracts, discrepancies resolved by consensus.
- **No quantitative synthesis**: no meta-analysis, no pooled effect estimates, no formal risk-of-bias or GRADE appraisal.

## Results

**Article flow**

- 412 records after de-duplication → 148 full-text assessments → **86 included**; 62 excluded for insufficient relevance, case-report-only evidence, or lack of NOAC-specific perioperative data.

**Bleeding figures cited from the included literature** (secondary citations, not new data)

- Prospective NOAC extraction study: clinically significant bleeding **<10% (6.9%)**, all managed conservatively — no transfusion, no hospitalization, and **no thromboembolic complications**.
- Prospective series of **49 patients**: bleeding episodes more frequent with rivaroxaban and apixaban than with the other agents.
- López-Galindo and Grau-Benítez systematic review — **7 studies, 931 patients**: minor bleeding (immediate or delayed) was the predominant postoperative complication in both NOAC and vitamin K antagonist patients after uncomplicated extraction; interruption judged unnecessary for simple extractions.
- Kwak et al. retrospective study, **120 NOAC patients**: most dental treatment performed safely without increased bleeding likelihood.
- Miclotte et al., **26 NOAC patients vs 26 controls**: skipping the morning dose avoided excessive intra- and postoperative bleeding.
- Curto et al.: edoxaban interruption generally unnecessary for minor oral surgery.
- Local tranexamic acid systematic review: **7% lower risk** of oral bleeding.
- RCTs comparing continued vs briefly interrupted regimens: **no meaningful difference in major bleeding**; minor seepage modestly increased with continued therapy.

**Timing and interval recommendations**

- Twice-daily agents (apixaban, dabigatran): omit **one** dose — holding the morning dose allows sufficient clearance by the following morning in normal renal function.
- Once-daily agents (rivaroxaban, edoxaban): omit **two** doses / allow a longer interval to reach trough; schedule in the **morning after skipping the prior evening dose**.
- High-risk extensive surgery: consider a **24–48 hour** preoperative pause plus postoperative tranexamic acid prophylaxis, with multidisciplinary planning.
- Postoperative **24–48 hour** follow-up to reduce rebleeding; **no bridging anticoagulation** at any tier.

**Risk-stratification thresholds**

- **CHA₂DS₂-VASc** anchors thrombotic risk in nonvalvular atrial fibrillation; "elevated" scores trigger brief dose omission at moderate procedural risk, but **no numeric cut-off is stated**.
- **HAS-BLED** is acknowledged as developed for spontaneous rather than procedural bleeding and described as a "useful, albeit imperfect" framework because its components (hypertension, renal dysfunction, prior hemorrhage) overlap with procedural risk factors; **no numeric threshold given**.
- Preoperative **creatinine clearance (CrCl)** assessment required; dabigatran contraindicated at CrCl < 30 mL/min; edoxaban reduced to 30 mg once daily at CrCl 15–50 mL/min or low body weight.

**Pharmacokinetic profile (half-life / renal clearance / bioavailability / reversal)**

| Agent | Target | Half-life | Renal clearance | Bioavailability | Reversal | Trial |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dabigatran | Thrombin (IIa) | 12–17 h | ~80% | ~6–7% (prodrug) | Idarucizumab | RE-LY |
| Rivaroxaban | Factor Xa | 5–13 h | ~33% | ~80–100% with food | Andexanet alfa / PCC | ROCKET-AF |
| Apixaban | Factor Xa | ~12 h | ~25% | ~50% | Andexanet alfa / PCC | ARISTOTLE |
| Edoxaban | Factor Xa | 10–14 h | ~50% | ~62% | Andexanet alfa / PCC | ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48 |

Apixaban has the least renal dependence and is flagged as most often feasible without interruption; dabigatran is the most renal-sensitive and requires the longest pause when CrCl is reduced.

**Reversal agents**

- Idarucizumab neutralizes dabigatran by active-site binding, restoring thrombin generation within minutes; dental-specific use remains **anecdotal**, reserved for uncontrolled postoperative oral bleeding refractory to local measures.
- Ciraparantag reverses apixaban and rivaroxaban but is limited by high cost, short duration of action, and rebound anticoagulation potential; prothrombin complex concentrate is an alternative when andexanet is unavailable, though dental evidence is sparse.
- **Routine reversal before elective dental procedures is unwarranted and potentially pro-thrombotic.**

## Limitations

- **Lowest evidence tier in this category.** Cureus is a low-barrier, author-pays journal and this is a *narrative* synthesis: no meta-analysis, no pooled estimates, no formal risk-of-bias or GRADE appraisal, no PRISMA flow diagram. Its recommendations should not be read as stronger than — or as superseding — the systematic reviews and meta-analyses already held here.
- **Citation integrity.** Reference brackets are empty in the retrieved PMC full text, so the quantitative claims above **cannot be traced to specific primary studies** from this article alone. Several figures (notably the 6.9% bleeding rate) coincide with values reported elsewhere in this category and should be verified against the primary source before clinical citation.
- **Author-conceded limitations**: most underlying evidence observational with few RCTs powered for dental endpoints; heterogeneity in procedural definitions, bleeding classification, and NOAC regimens; long-term thrombotic outcomes after brief interruption underreported; renal impairment, polypharmacy, and antiplatelet co-administration underrepresented in trials; procedure-specific bleeding risk not granularly quantified; no cost-effectiveness evaluation.
- **No operational thresholds** for CHA₂DS₂-VASc or HAS-BLED despite both being invoked as decision anchors — the "elevated score" trigger is left entirely to clinician judgment, limiting reproducibility of the proposed algorithm.
- Portions of the retrieved prose are garbled (e.g., a truncated sentence in the dabigatran pharmacology paragraph), suggesting limited copy-editing.
