## Three-line Summary

Case series (Medicine 2023, n=3 overweight women, ages 27–46, BMI 35.6, subcutaneous semaglutide 0.5 mg/week for 6–16 weeks) — first documented association between a GLP-1 receptor agonist and hyposalivation; Sjögren-negative, non-diabetic (HbA1c 5.9–6.0).

Mean modified Schirmer test 9 mL/3 min at baseline; onset approximately 4 weeks after starting semaglutide; proposed mechanism: body-water loss (natriuresis/diarrhea) and nasopharyngitis-driven mouth breathing.

Outcomes were favorable (drug discontinuation, pilocarpine 5 mg/day, or conservative management all restored acceptable flow); dental clinicians should query GLP-1 agonist use in patients presenting with unexplained xerostomia.

## Summary

This oral-medicine case series reports the **first documented association between semaglutide (a GLP-1 receptor agonist marketed as Ozempic/Wegovy) and hyposalivation**. Three overweight women (ages 27–46, mean BMI 35.6) using subcutaneous semaglutide 0.5 mg weekly for weight loss (6–16 weeks) presented with severe xerostomia, coated/dry tongue, and minimal frothy saliva. Objective unstimulated salivary flow by **modified Schirmer test averaged 9 mL at 3 minutes** (8–10). Alternative causes were systematically excluded — dehydration, radiation, diabetes (HbA1c 5.9–6.0), and Sjögren's syndrome (RF, anti-Ro, anti-La, ANA all normal; no eye dryness). The proposed mechanism links semaglutide's body-water-loss (natriuretic/diuretic) properties, diarrhea-related dehydration, and nasopharyngitis-driven mouth breathing. Outcomes were favorable: the patient who discontinued the drug normalized (30 mL/3 min), one continued on pilocarpine (25 mL), and one continued conservatively (27 mL). The authors recommend counseling patients starting semaglutide about hyposalivation risk and dental follow-up.

## Key Contributions

- **First report** of semaglutide/GLP-1-agonist–associated hyposalivation, a previously undocumented oral adverse effect.
- Objective, reversible, duration-related pattern documented with the modified Schirmer test.
- Practical **management options** (discontinue vs continue + pilocarpine vs conservative) and a call to counsel patients + arrange dental follow-up.

## Methodology

Retrospective clinical case series (n=3) in an oral-medicine clinic. Unstimulated salivary flow measured by **modified Schirmer test** (mL/3 min). Diagnosis by exclusion — dehydration, head-and-neck radiation, diabetes, and Sjögren's all ruled out via history, oral exam, HbA1c, and a normal Sjögren autoantibody panel.
