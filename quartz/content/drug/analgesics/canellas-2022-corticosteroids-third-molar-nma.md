## Three-line Summary

Frequentist network meta-analysis (61 RCTs, 3561 subjects; Embase/PubMed/Cochrane) comparing 5 corticosteroids — dexamethasone, betamethasone, methylprednisolone, prednisolone, triamcinolone — by drug, dose, and route for reducing inflammatory complications (edema, pain, trismus) after mandibular third molar surgery.

Dexamethasone 8mg submucosal injection and pterygomandibular injection significantly reduced edema vs placebo (−3.58 [−6.98; −0.17] and −3.56 [−6.30; −0.82] respectively); dexamethasone 8mg submucosal also significantly reduced pain on days 1 and 2 (−30.95 and −15.25 VAS units) — contradicting earlier binary CS-vs-no-CS syntheses that found no pain benefit; no serious adverse effects across all agents.

Dexamethasone 8mg (submucosal injection or oral tablets) is the preferred perioperative corticosteroid for third molar surgery — this NMA supersedes earlier binary (CS vs no-CS) pooling by ranking 5 agents head-to-head and identifying the optimal agent, dose, and route.

## Summary

This 2022 frequentist network meta-analysis (Canellas, Ritto, Tiwana; Br J Oral Maxillofac Surg) is the largest head-to-head comparison of corticosteroids for mandibular third molar surgery, including 61 RCTs and 3561 subjects. Unlike earlier binary meta-analyses (such as markiewicz-2008) that pooled any corticosteroid vs placebo without comparing specific agents, this NMA separately ranks dexamethasone, betamethasone, methylprednisolone, prednisolone, and triamcinolone across multiple doses and routes. Dexamethasone 8mg (submucosal injection and pterygomandibular injection) emerged as the clear top-ranking agent for edema reduction, and — crucially — submucosal dexamethasone 8mg also showed statistically significant pain reduction on postoperative days 1 and 2, directly contradicting markiewicz-2008's null pain finding. P-score ranking confirmed dexamethasone 8mg submucosal and oral tablets as the highest-probability best options. No serious adverse effects were detected across any corticosteroid type or route.

## Key Contributions

- Largest NMA to date for third molar surgery corticosteroids (61 RCTs, 3561 subjects — vs 12 RCTs in markiewicz-2008)
- First head-to-head agent comparison (5 drugs × multiple doses × multiple routes) — resolves the "which CS" question that binary syntheses left unanswered
- Identifies dexamethasone 8mg as the superior agent for edema reduction across two routes (submucosal injection and pterygomandibular injection)
- Demonstrates significant pain reduction for dexamethasone 8mg submucosal on days 1 and 2 — contradicts markiewicz-2008's no-pain-benefit finding for CS pooled
- No serious adverse effects across all agents reviewed — confirms the safety profile for all 5 corticosteroids studied

## Methodology

- **Design**: Frequentist network meta-analysis (NMA)
- **Databases**: Embase, PubMed, Cochrane Library (no language restrictions)
- **Inclusion**: RCTs; mandibular third molar surgery; corticosteroid interventions
- **Comparators**: Dexamethasone, betamethasone, methylprednisolone, prednisolone, triamcinolone (multiple doses and routes including submucosal, pterygomandibular, IM, oral)
- **Outcomes**: Oedema (mm), pain (VAS), trismus/mouth opening (mm)
- **Sample**: 61 studies, 3561 subjects
- **Ranking**: P-scores for best treatment probability across agent × dose × route combinations
- **Source**: Abstract only (BJOMS, Elsevier — no open-access PMC version available)
