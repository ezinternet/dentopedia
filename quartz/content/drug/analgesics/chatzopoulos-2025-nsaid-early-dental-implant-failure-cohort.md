## Three-line Summary

Retrospective EHR cohort of 12,943 patients receiving 49,997 implants (NSAID group n=3133 vs non-NSAID control n=9810), testing whether common NSAID use is associated with early implant failure (removal within six months).

After adjustment for age, gender, diabetes and osteoporosis, ibuprofen raised the odds of early failure at both implant level (OR 2.29, 95% CI 1.48–3.55) and patient level (OR 2.87, 95% CI 1.83–4.51); naproxen was significant at implant level (OR 2.65, 95% CI 1.22–5.75) but not at patient level (p=0.0689).

Signals caution in post-operative analgesic planning after implant placement, but this is an association from a retrospective design with confounding by indication — not proof of causation.

## Summary

The role of NSAIDs in osseointegration has long been debated, with conflicting findings from animal models and small human trials. This large-scale retrospective cohort used an electronic health record (EHR) database to test whether use of common NSAIDs is associated with early dental implant failure — defined as implant removal within six months of placement — in a real-world population of 12,943 patients who received 49,997 implants. NSAID users (n=3133) were compared with non-users (n=9810). Because the NSAID group was older and had more diabetes and osteoporosis at baseline, multivariable logistic regression adjusted for age, gender, diabetes, and osteoporosis, and analyses were run at both implant and patient levels. Ibuprofen was associated with roughly a doubling-to-tripling of the adjusted odds of early failure, consistent across levels; naproxen showed a significant association at implant level only. The authors conclude clinicians should consider these findings when formulating post-operative analgesic plans — while noting the design supports association, not causation.

## Key Contributions

- Largest human cohort to date on the NSAID → early implant failure question, addressing a gap left by animal models and small trials.
- Provides adjusted odds ratios for ibuprofen and naproxen at both implant and patient levels.
- Association persists after adjustment for age, gender, diabetes, and osteoporosis.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort from an EHR database.
- **Sample**: 12,943 patients, 49,997 implants; NSAID group n=3133 vs non-NSAID control n=9810.
- **Primary outcome**: Early implant failure = implant removal within six months of placement.
- **Statistics**: Multivariable logistic regression odds ratios adjusting for age, gender, diabetes, and osteoporosis; implant-level and patient-level analyses.
- **Baseline imbalance**: NSAID group older (62.8 vs 58.7 y, p<0.0001), more diabetes (22.0% vs 16.7%, p<0.0001) and osteoporosis (11.0% vs 7.0%, p<0.0001).

## Results

Adjusted odds ratios for early implant failure (NSAID vs non-NSAID):

| Drug | Implant-level OR (95% CI) | Patient-level OR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Ibuprofen | 2.29 (1.48–3.55), p<0.0001 | 2.87 (1.83–4.51), p<0.0001 |
| Naproxen | 2.65 (1.22–5.75), p=0.0137 | NS (p=0.0689) |

Ibuprofen was associated with a significantly increased risk of early failure at both levels; naproxen reached significance at implant level but not patient level. The effect held after adjustment for the measured confounders. The association is not evidence of causation — the retrospective design carries confounding by indication (NSAID users were older and had more diabetes and osteoporosis), and dose/timing/duration were not available in the abstract.
