## Three-line Summary

10-year retrospective chart review (2013–2022, n=192 hospitalized patients, 302 pus-culture isolates) at a Korean tertiary center, of causative bacteria and antibiotic sensitivity in odontogenic fascial-space abscess.

Viridans streptococcus dominated (51.7%, rising from 41.8% to 60.9% over the decade); its penicillin G sensitivity fell from 68.4% to 52.0%, while 3rd-generation cephalosporin sensitivity (cefotaxime 93.5%, ceftriaxone 95.5%) stayed high and stable.

Penicillin-only empirical therapy for odontogenic fascial-space abscess is increasingly questionable; deep-neck-space abscesses carry a distinct, more resistant microbiology (Klebsiella pneumoniae) warranting routine culture/sensitivity testing.

## Summary

This 10-year retrospective chart review (2013–2022) analyzed pus cultures and antibiotic sensitivity from 192 patients hospitalized for odontogenic fascial-space abscess at a Korean tertiary center, yielding 302 bacterial strains. Viridans streptococcus was by far the most common isolate (51.7%) and its share rose over the decade (41.8%→60.9%), alongside an overall shift toward Gram-positive aerobic dominance (56.8%→72.4%) and a decline in Gram-negative anaerobes (23.3%→12.8%) — a pattern the authors attribute to increasing pre-hospital broad-spectrum antibiotic use before tertiary referral. Critically, viridans streptococcus sensitivity to penicillin G fell significantly (68.4%→52.0%) while sensitivity to 3rd-generation cephalosporins (cefotaxime 93.5%, ceftriaxone 95.5%) remained high and stable throughout. Deep-neck-space abscesses showed a distinct microbiology (more Gram-negative aerobes, notably Klebsiella pneumoniae, which showed rising multidrug resistance but preserved piperacillin/tazobactam sensitivity 88.9%), supporting routine culture/sensitivity testing when infection spreads beyond primary/secondary spaces.

## Key Contributions

- 10-year, single-center, culture-confirmed microbiology and antibiotic-sensitivity trend data for hospitalized odontogenic fascial-space abscess — fills a real-world evidence gap that outcome-focused SRs of empirical antibiotic regimens do not cover.
- Documents a temporal decline in penicillin-class sensitivity of the dominant pathogen (viridans streptococcus) alongside maintained 3rd-generation cephalosporin sensitivity.
- Characterizes deep-neck-space-specific microbiology (higher Gram-negative aerobic burden, Klebsiella pneumoniae with rising resistance), supporting a differentiated empirical strategy by anatomic severity.

## Methodology

- Retrospective chart review, Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Dankook University Hospital, South Korea; IRB DKUDH IRB 2023-03-007.
- n=192 hospitalized patients (2013–2022) with odontogenic fascial-space abscess who underwent incision & drainage with pus culture + antibiotic sensitivity testing (swabbing method); excluded negative/contaminated (>3 species) samples.
- Split into 2013–2017 (n=79) vs 2018–2022 (n=113) cohorts for temporal comparison; fascial spaces classified as primary/secondary/deep-neck via enhanced CT.
- Statistics: chi-square (isolate/sensitivity ratio between cohorts), one-way ANOVA (isolate differences by fascial space); SPSS v28, p<0.05.

## Results

| Isolate | Frequency (overall) | Key antibiotic sensitivity |
|---|---|---|
| Viridans streptococcus | 51.7% (41.8%→60.9% over decade) | Penicillin G 60.4% (↓68.4%→52.0%); ampicillin 80.5%; cefotaxime 93.5%; ceftriaxone 95.5%; vancomycin 99.4% |
| Prevotella spp. | 16.9% (22.6%→11.5% over decade) | not separately reported |
| Klebsiella pneumoniae | 5.6% (↑ in deep-neck-space abscess) | Ampicillin 0%; amoxicillin/clavulanate 83.3% (↓ over decade); piperacillin/tazobactam 88.9%; cefotaxime/ceftazidime ~78–83% |
| S. anginosus group | 4.6% | not separately reported |
| Peptostreptococcus spp. | 4.6% | not separately reported |

- 302 strains total, mean 1.57 strains/patient; aerobic 73.8% / anaerobic 26.2%; Gram-positive 73.2% / Gram-negative 26.8%.
- Deep-neck-space abscesses: significantly lower Gram-positive aerobic %, significantly higher Gram-negative aerobic % (and Klebsiella pneumoniae) vs primary/secondary spaces.
- Bottom line stated by authors: penicillin should not be selected as first-line empirical antibiotic for odontogenic fascial-space abscess given declining sensitivity; 3rd-generation cephalosporins remain reliable; culture/sensitivity testing is particularly important for deep-neck-space involvement.
