## Three-line Summary

Companion SR+MA to the ADA 2019 CPG (Lockhart 2019): updated 2 prior Cochrane reviews for new RCTs (none found), ultimately including 3 RCTs and 8 observational reports; trial estimates for pain, swelling, and analgesic use at 7 days showed both benefit and harm at very low to low certainty across all target conditions (SIP, PN-SAP, PN-LAAA).

No statistically significant benefit from antibiotics as adjuncts to definitive coronal or drainage therapy (DCDT) or alone was demonstrated for patient-reported outcomes; pooled pain-intensity differences were consistently near zero (MD ≈ 0.09–0.39 on 0–3 VAS at 24–72 h) with wide CIs crossing both benefit and harm.

Additional population-level harms from antibiotic prescribing (antibiotic resistance, Clostridioides difficile infection, anaphylaxis) carry potentially large magnitude at very low to moderate certainty, providing the harm context that underpins the CPG's strong conditional non-prescribing recommendations.

## Summary

Tampi 2019 is the systematic review and meta-analysis that serves as the evidence base for the ADA 2019 Clinical Practice Guideline on antibiotic use in dental pain and swelling ([[drug/antibiotics/lockhart-2019-antibiotic-pulpal-periapical-pain-swelling]]). It updated two prior Cochrane systematic reviews and found no new RCTs, yielding a final evidence pool of 3 RCTs and 8 observational reports. The SR assessed benefits (pain, swelling, analgesic use) and harms (antibiotic resistance, Clostridioides difficile infection, anaphylaxis) using the GRADE framework.

Across all three target conditions — 증상성 비가역적 치수염 (Symptomatic Irreversible Pulpitis, SIP), 치수 괴사 + 증상성 근단 치주염 (Pulp Necrosis + Symptomatic Apical Periodontitis, PN-SAP), 치수 괴사 + 국소 급성 근단 농양 (Pulp Necrosis + Localized Acute Apical Abscess, PN-LAAA) — the pooled trial evidence showed no statistically significant benefit from antibiotics, with all effect estimates close to zero and confidence intervals spanning both benefit and harm at very low to low certainty. The population-level harm data provided moderate-certainty evidence for large magnitude harms, informing the strong conditional recommendations against antibiotic prescribing in the companion CPG.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the GRADE evidence profiles underlying the ADA 2019 antibiotic CPG — the SR that the CPG formally cites
- Pooled 3 small penicillin RCTs (N=29–43 per analysis) via meta-analysis; found no significant effect on pain intensity at 24h, 48h, or 72h
- Documented that no new RCTs had been conducted since the 2 prior Cochrane reviews (Aminoshariae 2016, Agnihotry 2019 updates)
- Quantified population-level harms: ~23,000 US deaths/year from antibiotic-resistant infections; 14% of community-associated CDI preceded by antibiotics; ~50 million dental antibiotic prescriptions/year in the US with 30–85% potentially inappropriate
- Established that all existing RCT evidence uses penicillin only — zero evidence for amoxicillin, clindamycin, or azithromycin in these conditions

## Methodology

**Design**: PRISMA-compliant systematic review with meta-analysis  
**Updated reviews**: Aminoshariae 2016 and Agnihotry 2019 (Cochrane SR updates)  
**Databases**: MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, CINAHL (searches through 2019)  
**Population**: Immunocompetent adults ≥18 years with SIP ± SAP, PN-SAP, or PN-LAAA; immunocompromised excluded  
**Evidence assessment**: GRADE approach; Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for RCTs; paired independent reviewer process

### Included Studies

| Study | Condition | N | Intervention | Comparator | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fouad 1996 | PN-LAAA | 31 | Penicillin 500 mg + DCDT | Placebo + DCDT | 3 days |
| Nagle 2000 | SIP (± SAP) | 40 | Penicillin 500 mg q6h × 7d + analgesics | Placebo + analgesics | 7 days |
| Henry 2001 | PN-SAP | 41 | Penicillin 500 mg q6h × 7d + pulpectomy | Placebo + pulpectomy | 7 days |

8 observational studies were included for population-level harm outcomes (CDI, antibiotic resistance, anaphylaxis).

## Results

### Benefit Outcomes — RCT Evidence

**SIP (antibiotics alone, no DCDT; Nagle 2000)**:
- Pain intensity at 7 days: no meaningful difference (very low certainty)
- Analgesic use (ibuprofen): MD −0.40 (95% CI −4.23 to 3.43), p=0.84
- Analgesic use (acetaminophen/codeine): MD 2.45 (95% CI −1.23 to 6.13), p=0.19

**PN-SAP/PN-LAAA (antibiotics as adjunct to DCDT; Fouad 1996 + Henry 2001 pooled)**:
- Pain at 24h: MD 0.09 (95% CI −0.37 to 0.55), p=0.69; I²=0%
- Pain at 48h: MD 0.39 (95% CI −0.13 to 0.91), p=0.14; I²=0%
- Pain at 72h: MD 0.12 (95% CI −0.32 to 0.56), p=0.59; I²=0%
- Intraoral swelling: similar null pattern; very low certainty

### GRADE Certainty Summary

| Outcome | Condition | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Pain (VAS) | SIP, no DCDT | Very low |
| Pain (VAS) | PN-SAP/LAAA + DCDT | Very low to low |
| Intraoral swelling | PN-SAP/LAAA + DCDT | Very low |
| Analgesic use | SIP | Very low |

### Population-Level Harm Outcomes

| Harm Domain | Key Finding | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Antibiotic-resistant infection mortality | ~23,000 deaths/year in US (CDC 2013) | Moderate |
| C. difficile infection | 14% of community-associated CDI preceded by antibiotic use | Low |
| Anaphylaxis (penicillin) | Serious/fatal reactions from dental prescriptions documented | Very low to low |
| Dental antibiotic prescribing volume | ~50 million/year; 30–85% potentially inappropriate | Moderate |

### Conclusions

For all 3 conditions, trial evidence showed both potential benefit and harm at very low to low certainty. No statistically significant benefit was demonstrated. Population-level harms are potentially large. The findings directly underpin the ADA CPG non-prescribing recommendations.

## Limitations

- Only 3 RCTs; very small sample sizes per pooled analysis
- All RCTs used penicillin — no evidence for amoxicillin, clindamycin, or azithromycin
- High risk of bias in all 3 RCTs (attrition bias, selective reporting)
- Heterogeneity in DCDT type performed across trials
- Harm outcomes extrapolated from observational sources not specific to pulpal dental prescribing
