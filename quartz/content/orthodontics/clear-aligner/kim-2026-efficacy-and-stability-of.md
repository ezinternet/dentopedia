## Three-line Summary

Prospective multicenter cohort, 48 growing patients (mean age 9.3±1.7y; CA group n=24, SME group n=24) with maxillary transverse deficiency, comparing clear-aligner (Invisalign) vs slow maxillary expander (SME) arch expansion at pretreatment, post-expansion, and post-retention (≥3 months).

Predefined non-inferiority analysis (margin 1.6 mm) found CA was not inferior to SME on the primary outcome (intermolar transverse expansion); CA showed significantly greater canine cusp-tip expansion at both timepoints, while age was a negative predictor of canine-region expansion, and CA (but not SME) showed significant buccal molar tipping relapse during retention (ΔMI −7.1±9.8°, P=0.030).

Both appliances produced clinically significant and largely stable transverse gains, supporting CA as a viable alternative to conventional expanders for growing patients with mild-to-moderate transverse deficiency — but the non-randomized (preference-based) allocation and molar-tipping relapse specific to CA temper how far this generalizes.

## Summary

This prospective multicenter cohort study (Asan Medical Center + a private clinic, Seoul, Korea) compared clear aligners (Invisalign®) to conventional slow maxillary expanders (SME, Hyrax/Haas-type) for correcting maxillary transverse deficiency (MTD) in 48 growing patients (mean age 9.3±1.7 years; CA n=24, SME n=24). Using a predefined non-inferiority framework (margin 1.6 mm), the study found that CA-based expansion was **not inferior** to SME on the primary outcome — intermolar transverse expansion — measured via digital model analysis (iTero® scans) at pretreatment (T0), post-expansion (T1), and post-retention (T2, ≥3 months). Both appliances produced clinically meaningful transverse gains at crown and gingival levels across canine, premolar/deciduous-molar, and first-molar regions, largely maintained through retention. A notable CA-specific finding was significantly greater canine cusp-tip expansion versus SME (regression β=0.898–1.483, P≤0.017), and age was a significant negative predictor of canine-region expansion. However, the CA group also showed significant buccal molar tipping relapse during retention (ΔMI −7.1±9.8°, P=0.030) that was not seen in the SME group, echoing prior evidence that aligner-driven expansion tends to be more dentoalveolar/tipping-based than skeletal/bodily. The study is limited by non-randomized (patient-preference) allocation and Invisalign-Korea funding.

## Key Contributions

- Rare **prospective, multicenter** design directly comparing CA vs conventional SME in growing/mixed-dentition patients — most prior comparative literature on this topic is retrospective.
- Applies a **predefined non-inferiority statistical framework** (1.6 mm margin) rather than simple superiority testing, raising the methodological bar for CA-expansion evidence in children.
- Provides rare **long-term retention data (T2, up to 25 months)** for CA-based expansion, addressing a previously identified evidence gap (most aligner studies report only immediate post-expansion outcomes).
- Identifies a novel **CA-specific advantage in the canine region** and **age as a negative predictor** of canine expansion — new covariate-level findings for clinical patient selection.
- Documents a **CA-specific buccal molar tipping relapse** during retention not seen with SME, adding nuance to the "non-inferiority" headline conclusion.

## Methodology

Prospective multicenter cohort (Sept 2022–Sept 2024), IRB-approved (Asan Medical Center 2022-1182; National Institute for Bioethics Policy P01-202305-01-045), reported per STROBE. 50 patients enrolled, 48 analyzed (1 lost to follow-up per group; CA n=24, 13F, mean age 9.8±1.4y; SME n=24, 11F, mean age 8.8±1.8y) with MTD, mixed/early permanent dentition, CVM stage ≤IV. Allocation was **non-randomized**, by patient/guardian preference. CA group used Invisalign® with ClinCheck®-planned staged expansion (~0.2 mm/stage) and optimized attachments, worn 20–22h/day. SME group used site-standard Hyrax (bonded) or Haas-type (banded) expanders, activated ¼ turn twice weekly, followed by 2–6 months passive retention. Outcomes measured via iTero® intraoral scans at T0 (pretreatment), T1 (post-expansion), T2 (post-retention, ≥3 months): crown- and gingival-level transverse widths (CL3–CL6, GL3–GL6), arch perimeter, molar buccolingual inclination (MI), and gingival-to-crown (G-C) ratio (bodily vs. tipping indicator). High measurement reliability (ICC >0.93–0.95). Statistics: paired/independent t-tests, multiple linear regression (Δ as DV; group, age, sex as covariates), sample size powered for non-inferiority (23/group), subgroup Mann-Whitney U for retention-duration effect.

## Results

- No significant intergroup difference in the primary outcome (intermolar/molar-region expansion) at either T0–T1 or T0–T2 → non-inferiority conclusion supported.
- CA showed significantly greater canine cusp-tip expansion than SME at both intervals (β=0.898, P=0.017 at T0–T1; β=1.483, P=0.015 at T0–T2); age was a significant negative predictor of this expansion (β=−0.383 to −0.549, P≤0.003).
- CA group alone showed significant buccal molar tipping relapse over the full observation period (ΔMI −7.1±9.8°, P=0.030); SME showed no significant change (ΔMI −1.0±10.9°, P=0.759); the between-group difference itself did not reach significance (P=0.177).
- Gingival-level intercanine width relapsed toward baseline in both groups (limited long-term stability at that specific site), while other transverse dimensions were well maintained through retention.
- Treatment/retention durations were similar between groups; retention-duration subgroup analysis (≤6mo vs >6mo) found no significant effect on stability outcomes, though subgroup sizes were small.

## Clinical Implications

For growing patients with mild-to-moderate MTD, clear aligners can be considered a viable, non-inferior alternative to conventional slow expanders for achieving transverse expansion — with the caveats that (1) expansion is more dentoalveolar/tipping-based in the molar region, raising a relapse risk that clinicians should monitor and potentially counter with overcorrection/root-torque attachments, and (2) the canine region may respond especially well to CA, particularly in younger patients. The non-randomized design means selection bias (patients/families choosing CA vs SME) cannot be excluded, and industry funding (Invisalign Korea) should be considered when weighing the strength of the non-inferiority conclusion.
