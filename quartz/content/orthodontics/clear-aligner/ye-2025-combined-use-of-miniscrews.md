## Three-line Summary

Case report, n=1 (11yr 3mo girl), Angle Class III malocclusion with severe unilateral posterior scissor bite treated with combined facemask + clear aligner (Anglebutton attachment) + posterior miniscrew anchorage/intrusion + occlusal pads on the healthy side.

Active treatment lasted 17 months and achieved maxillary protraction, correction of the scissor bite, Class I canine/molar relationships, and correction of the occlusal cant via intrusion of the extruded upper-left posterior segment.

Single case report — no comparator or long-term follow-up data reported in the retrieved text; illustrates feasibility of using clear aligners as dental anchorage during orthopedic (facemask) correction in a growing Class III patient.

## Summary

This case report describes the treatment of an 11-year-3-month-old girl with an Angle Class III malocclusion and severe unilateral posterior scissor bite, using a triple-combination protocol: facemask (FM) for maxillary protraction, clear aligner therapy (CAT) with AngelButton™ attachments (used both to bond facemask elastics and to create a bite-plane/occlusal effect), and miniscrew (temporary anchorage device, TAD) anchorage placed in the posterior region for absolute anchorage and intrusion of the extruded upper-left buccal segment. Occlusal pads were used on the unaffected (healthy) side. After a gingivectomy of the upper posterior segment and virtual aligner planning (iOrtho® software), active treatment over 17 months achieved maxillary protraction, correction of the scissor bite and occlusal cant, and Class I canine/molar relationships with an improved facial profile.

## Key Contributions

- Adds a rare unilateral scissor-bite + skeletal Class III presentation to the clear aligner case-report literature, demonstrating CAT combined with orthopedic maxillary protraction rather than purely dentoalveolar correction.
- Shows clear aligner attachments (AngelButton™) can function as an anchorage interface for facemask elastics, extending the described role of clear aligners beyond tooth movement alone.
- Demonstrates simultaneous use of posterior miniscrew anchorage for both anchorage reinforcement and intrusion, correcting an occlusal cant from a unilaterally extruded buccal segment.
- Reports a comparatively efficient active treatment time (17 months) for a complex three-dimensional skeletal/dental discrepancy in a growing patient.

## Methodology

Single case report (n=1). Patient: 11-year-3-month-old female with chief complaint of posterior occlusal difficulty and protruding lower incisors, habitually chewing only on the unaffected side. Diagnosis: Angle Class III malocclusion, severe unilateral posterior scissor bite, mild mandibular/chin asymmetry, mesoprosopic facial pattern, extruded upper-left buccal segment causing occlusal cant, incisor crowding. Treatment objectives: improve soft-tissue profile/esthetics, correct the scissor bite, eliminate the occlusal cant, achieve ideal overjet/overbite, resolve incisor crowding. Given the patient's growth potential and maxillary insufficiency, the clinicians opted to treat immediately (rather than delay to growth completion). Protocol sequence: gingivectomy of the upper posterior segment → intraoral scan and virtual aligner setup (iOrtho® v5.6, Angelalign Technology) → clear aligners with AngelButton™ attachments (bonding facemask elastics around the upper canines, plus an additional buccal attachment) → facemask for maxillary protraction → miniscrews placed in the posterior region for anchorage/intrusion → occlusal pads on the healthy side. Active treatment duration: 17 months.

## Results

Maxillary protraction with upper incisor proclination was achieved. The occlusal plane was corrected by intruding the upper-left posterior segment via miniscrew anchorage. The scissor bite was unlocked and corrected, attributed by the authors to the combination of occlusal-surface attachments and the aligner's bite-plane effect. Ideal intercuspation with Class I canine and molar relationships was achieved, along with an improved, more symmetrical facial profile and smile. No quantitative cephalometric measurements or long-term stability data were present in the retrieved (truncated ScienceDirect preview) text — this source should be treated as reporting qualitative/descriptive outcomes only until fuller text can be obtained.
