## Three-line Summary

Longitudinal clinical study (n=40, 20 fixed-appliance [FA] + 20 clear-aligner [CA] patients, mild-moderate crowding per Little's irregularity index) measuring unstimulated salivary TNF-α (ELISA) at baseline (T0) and 24 h (T1), 7 days (T2), and 21 days (T3) after appliance placement.

TNF-α rose and peaked at 24 h in both groups (FA 51.51 vs CA 43.29 pg/mL) then declined through day 21; FA levels were significantly higher than CA at T1, T2, and T3 (all p<0.05), with no significant difference at baseline.

Fixed appliances provoke a greater salivary inflammatory (bone-turnover) response than clear aligners in the initial treatment phase, consistent with the intermittent (aligner) vs continuous (fixed) force-delivery difference — small single-center sample without correlation to clinical pain or root-resorption outcomes.

## Summary

This longitudinal comparative study from Indian dental institutions (Journal of Orthodontic Science) measured salivary tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) — an early cytokine mediator of osteoclastic bone resorption during orthodontic tooth movement — in 40 patients with mild-moderate crowding, split evenly between fixed-appliance (FA) and clear-aligner (CA) treatment. Unstimulated saliva was sampled before appliance placement and at 24 hours, 7 days, and 21 days after, with TNF-α quantified by ELISA. Both groups showed the expected acute inflammatory pattern — a sharp TNF-α rise peaking at 24 hours, followed by decline — consistent with prior reports on cytokine response to orthodontic force. However, the FA group's TNF-α levels were significantly higher than the CA group's at every post-placement time point (24 h, 7 d, 21 d), despite no baseline difference, and CA levels returned close to baseline by day 21 (FA did not, per within-group tests). The authors attribute this gap to the fundamental biomechanical difference between appliance types: FAs deliver continuous force via the archwire, while aligners deliver intermittent force that likely allows periodontal tissue reorganization between activations, provoking a comparatively muted inflammatory response. This is the first study identified in the wiki to compare CA and FA at the cellular/biomarker level rather than only clinical/periodontal indices, offering a plausible biological mechanism for why aligners are consistently reported as gentler on periodontal tissue in clinical-outcome studies.

## Key Contributions

- Provides **biomarker-level (not just clinical-index) evidence** that fixed appliances provoke a stronger acute inflammatory/bone-turnover response than clear aligners.
- Establishes a **time-course pattern**: TNF-α peaks at 24 h in both appliance types, but only the CA group returns near baseline by day 21.
- Offers a **mechanistic explanation** (continuous vs. intermittent force delivery) that complements existing clinical-index CA-vs-FA comparisons.
- Uses a **practical, non-invasive salivary assay**, supporting feasibility of biomarker-based monitoring during early orthodontic treatment.

## Methodology

- **Design**: longitudinal, two parallel non-randomized groups; Group A (n=20, FA), Group B (n=20, CA).
- **Sample**: 40 patients (22 M, 18 F), mean age 22 ± 7 years; mild-moderate crowding (1–6 mm, Little's irregularity index); healthy periodontium (probing depth ≤3 mm), no recent anti-inflammatory/steroid/antibiotic use, no systemic disease.
- **Sampling**: unstimulated saliva via draining method at T0 (pre-placement), T1 (24 h), T2 (7 d), T3 (21 d); centrifuged, supernatant stored at −20 °C.
- **Assay**: TNF-α by ELISA (Weldon Biotech, India).
- **Statistics**: Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank test (intragroup across time), Mann-Whitney U test (intergroup FA vs CA at each time point); SPSS v20.0, α=0.05.

## Results

| Time point | FA (pg/mL ± SD) | CA (pg/mL ± SD) | FA vs CA |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 (baseline) | 43.32 ± 7.35 | 41.93 ± 6.51 | ns (p=0.65) |
| T1 (24 h) | 51.51 ± 7.38 | 43.29 ± 6.07 | p=0.0102* |
| T2 (7 d) | 49.79 ± 7.64 | 42.97 ± 5.96 | p=0.0500* |
| T3 (21 d) | 48.68 ± 7.66 | 41.97 ± 6.03 | p=0.0490* |

- Both groups peak at T1 (24 h) then decline; FA remains elevated relative to its own baseline through T3, while CA's T0-T3 difference was **not** statistically significant (near-return to baseline).
- Intergroup gap (FA higher than CA) reaches significance at every post-placement time point but not at baseline — ruling out a pre-existing group difference.

## Clinical Bottom Line

- Expect a measurable acute inflammatory response (salivary TNF-α) with **either** appliance type in the first 24 hours; this is a normal correlate of tooth-movement-induced bone remodeling, not a red flag.
- Clear aligners' comparatively lower and faster-resolving TNF-α response supports the **biological plausibility** of their reported periodontal-comfort advantages (lower pain, better periodontal indices) — useful for patient counseling on early-treatment expectations.
- Do not over-extrapolate: this is a **surrogate biomarker** study without correlation to actual pain scores, root resorption, or long-term periodontal outcomes — treat as mechanistic support for, not a replacement of, clinical-outcome evidence.
