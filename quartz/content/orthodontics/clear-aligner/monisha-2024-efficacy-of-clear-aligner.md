## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42021288179) of 6 studies (3 RCT, 3 non-RCT; n=449) comparing expedited clear aligner wear protocols (7-day, 10-day) vs. the conventional 14-day protocol for orthodontic tooth movement (OTM) efficacy in adults.

4/6 studies found no significant difference in OTM efficacy between schedules; 2 studies (including an RCT) found the 14-day protocol significantly more accurate for specific posterior movements (maxillary intrusion/distal tipping/buccal torque, mandibular intrusion/extrusion); GRADE certainty was very low for OTM efficacy, high for pain (no difference, 10-day vs 14-day).

Clinical implication: a one-size-fits-all shortened wear schedule is not well supported for complex posterior movements — authors recommend a hybrid protocol (shorter wear for simple movements, 14-day for movements shown to need it) pending higher-quality trials.

## Summary

This systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42021288179) asks a practical chairside question: does shortening clear aligner wear time per tray (7-day or 10-day activation) compromise tooth movement efficacy compared to the conventional 14-day protocol? From 9076 records, 6 studies (3 RCTs, 1 prospective cohort, 2 retrospective cohorts; total analyzed n=449, adults only, Invisalign) met inclusion criteria. Substantial methodological heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis, so findings are synthesized qualitatively. Four of six studies found no significant efficacy difference between expedited and conventional schedules, but two studies — including an RCT with low risk of bias (Al-Nadawi 2020) — found the 14-day protocol significantly more accurate for specific posterior tooth movements: maxillary posterior intrusion, distal crown tipping, and buccal torque, and mandibular posterior intrusion and extrusion. No anterior tooth movement showed a significant difference between schedules in any study. Pain perception (VAS, 1 RCT) showed no difference between 10-day and 14-day wear, with high certainty. Overall GRADE certainty for OTM efficacy was very low, reflecting risk of bias and inconsistent findings across studies with similar comparisons. The authors propose a "hybrid aligner-wear protocol" — shorter wear for simple movements, 14-day wear for movements shown to need more time — as a clinically pragmatic middle path rather than a uniform expedited schedule.

## Key Contributions

- First SR specifically on comparative efficacy of expedited (7-day/10-day) vs. conventional (14-day) clear aligner activation schedules.
- Applies Cochrane RoB 2.0 (RCTs) and Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (non-RCTs) plus GRADE certainty rating — a level of methodological rigor not previously applied to this specific question.
- Identifies which tooth movements are most sensitive to shortened wear time (posterior intrusion, distal tipping, torque) versus movements that appear robust to shortening (anterior movements generally).
- Synthesizes pain-perception evidence separately from efficacy evidence, showing these two clinical concerns dissociate (no pain penalty for 10-day wear, but possible efficacy penalty for complex posterior movements).
- Proposes an actionable "hybrid wear protocol" recommendation rather than a blanket "expedited is fine" or "expedited is unsafe" conclusion.

## Methodology

PRISMA-adherent SR, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42021288179). Searched MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, Google Scholar, DOAJ, Cochrane Library, OpenGrey, and ClinicalTrials.gov without language/date restriction (search to 18 Feb 2024). PICOS: adults (>18y) undergoing clear aligner therapy in both arches; intervention = activation schedule <14 days (any manufacturer); comparator = same appliance at 14-day schedule; primary outcome = OTM efficacy (occlusal indices or digital superimposition); secondary outcome = pain perception (VAS/questionnaire). RCT, prospective, and retrospective designs eligible; in vitro studies, case reports, and narrative reviews excluded. Two independent reviewers handled screening (Rayyan), extraction, RoB assessment, and GRADE rating, with disagreements resolved by discussion. Of 9076 initial records, 6 studies were included after full-text review of 34 candidates. Meta-analysis was planned contingent on homogeneity but was precluded by substantial methodological and outcome-reporting heterogeneity; synthesis is descriptive/qualitative.

## Results

**OTM efficacy (primary outcome):**
- No significant difference (P>0.05) in 4/6 studies (2 RCT: Zhao 2023, Clements 2003; 2 retrospective: Stephens 2022, Wei 2023).
- Significant difference favoring 14-day protocol in 2/6 studies:
  - Al-Nadawi 2020 (RCT, low RoB): 14-day > 7-day for maxillary posterior intrusion (0.3 vs 0.4mm), distal crown tip (2.8° vs 3.9°), buccal crown torque (2.8° vs 4.4°); 14-day > 7-day & 10-day for mandibular posterior intrusion (0.3 vs 0.4mm) and extrusion (0.2 vs 0.3mm). No difference for any anterior movements or for 10-day vs 14-day maxillary comparisons.
  - Castroflorio 2023 (prospective cohort, low RoB): 14-day protocol reduced "lack of correction" by 12% more than 7-day (P=0.009); 7-day vs 10-day not significant.
- GRADE certainty: **very low** for both 7-day-vs-14-day and 10-day-vs-14-day OTM efficacy comparisons (downgraded for risk of bias and very serious inconsistency — contradictory results between studies with comparable designs, e.g., Al-Nadawi vs. Zhao for 10-day vs 14-day).

**Pain perception (secondary outcome):**
- Zhao 2023 (RCT, low RoB): no significant difference in VAS scores between 10-day and 14-day groups at any timepoint; pain peaked on day 1 of stage 1 in both groups.
- GRADE certainty: **high**.

**Treatment completion:** Bollen 2003 found a non-significant trend toward higher completion with 14-day wear (37% vs 21% for 7-day, P<0.2), with substantial attrition in both arms (only 15/51 patients completed).

## Clinical Implications

- For **anterior tooth movements**, expedited 7-day or 10-day wear appears to perform comparably to 14-day wear across all studies examining anterior teeth — no evidence penalty identified for shortening wear time in simpler cases.
- For **posterior movements requiring intrusion, distal tipping, or torque** (maxillary and mandibular), the single low-RoB RCT directly testing this found the 14-day protocol significantly more accurate — suggesting these specific movements may need the longer force-application window.
- **Pain is not a barrier** to shortened wear (10-day vs 14-day: no difference, high-certainty evidence) — patient comfort does not favor either schedule.
- The proposed **hybrid protocol** (7-day wear for simple/anterior movements, 14-day wear for complex posterior movements) is a reasonable pragmatic compromise given current evidence, though it is a synthesis recommendation from the authors rather than a tested intervention itself.
- Overall evidence quality is **very low** — this should be treated as a preliminary signal, not a settled answer; potential harms of expedited wear (root resorption, periodontal compromise) remain unstudied and are an explicit evidence gap.
- Limitation for generalizability: all included studies used adult patients and Invisalign exclusively; applicability to adolescents or other aligner systems is unproven.
