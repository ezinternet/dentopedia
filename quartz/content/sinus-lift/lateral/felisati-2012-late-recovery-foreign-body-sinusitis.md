## Three-line Summary

Case report (n=1, 55-year-old male) with recurring, antibiotic-resistant ethmoidomaxillary sinusitis after right lateral-window maxillary sinus floor augmentation, caused by bone graft infection and displacement of xenograft material into the maxillary sinus.

Combined nasal endoscopy + oral surgery removal of the infected graft achieved apparent resolution, but sinusitis relapsed and CT confirmed residual graft fragments; a second, more precise endoscopic local treatment led to spontaneous expulsion of the fragments 6 hours later and prompt recovery.

Displaced/infected xenograft can cause a late, antibiotic-refractory sinusitis that surgical drainage alone may not fully clear on the first attempt — CT follow-up is warranted when symptoms persist, and successful re-augmentation is possible 6 months after resolution.

## Summary

A 55-year-old male was referred with recurring sinusitis following a right maxillary sinus floor augmentation (측방창 상악동거상술, lateral-window MSFA) performed for implant purposes. Workup revealed antibiotic-resistant ethmoidomaxillary sinusitis caused by two compounding factors: infection of the bone graft, and displacement of the previously placed xenograft material into the maxillary sinus, where it acted as a foreign-body nidus for persistent infection. This is a rare but clinically instructive late complication of lateral sinus lift — distinct from early postoperative infection, presenting as a chronic, treatment-resistant sinusitis months to years after the original augmentation.

Initial management combined nasal endoscopy with oral (intraoral) surgery to remove the infected graft and restore normal sinus drainage. The procedure appeared successful, but sinusitis relapsed despite 2 weeks of postoperative antibiotic therapy and local medications — underscoring that antibiotics alone, and even a single surgical removal attempt, may be insufficient once graft displacement/foreign-body infection is established. A CT scan confirmed the cause of relapse: residual grafting fragments still present in the maxillary sinus. Rather than immediately proceeding to a second full surgical procedure, the clinicians performed a more precise, targeted endoscopic local treatment; six hours later, the patient spontaneously expelled the remaining graft fragments and recovered promptly. The patient went on to have a second, successful maxillary sinus floor augmentation 6 months later, with no further complications reported.

## Key Contributions

- Illustrates a distinct late-complication phenotype of lateral-window sinus floor augmentation: combined bone graft infection **and** intra-sinus graft displacement, producing antibiotic-resistant chronic sinusitis rather than an early postoperative infection.
- Shows that initial combined endoscopic + surgical graft removal, even when apparently successful, does not guarantee resolution if residual foreign material remains — CT confirmation of complete graft clearance is important when symptoms persist post-treatment.
- Demonstrates a favorable escalation pathway: a second, more precise endoscopic local treatment (rather than repeat full surgery) triggered spontaneous expulsion of residual fragments within hours, followed by prompt recovery and successful re-augmentation at 6 months — supporting a staged, less-invasive-first approach to relapsed foreign-body sinusitis when feasible.

## Methodology

Single case report (n=1). A 55-year-old male with recurring sinusitis after right MSFA underwent diagnostic imaging that identified graft infection and xenograft displacement into the maxillary sinus. Treatment: (1) combined nasal endoscopy + oral surgery to remove infected graft and restore drainage; (2) 2 weeks of postoperative antibiotics and local medications; (3) on relapse, CT imaging confirmed residual graft fragments; (4) a second, more accurate endoscopic local medication was performed; (5) follow-up after spontaneous fragment expulsion; (6) repeat MSFA at 6 months.

## Results

- First combined endoscopic/surgical removal: apparent early success, but sinusitis relapsed.
- Relapse persisted through 2 weeks of antibiotic therapy plus local medications.
- CT confirmed persistent grafting fragments in the maxillary sinus as the cause of relapse.
- Second, targeted endoscopic local treatment → spontaneous expulsion of graft fragments 6 hours later → prompt clinical recovery.
- Successful repeat maxillary sinus floor augmentation performed 6 months after recovery.
