## Three-line Summary

In vitro 3×2 factorial push-out study (75 glass fiber posts from 60 decoronated premolars; post-surface conditioning: none vs 24% H2O2-silane vs Nd:YVO4 laser-silane; cement: self-adhesive dual-cure [SADCRC, RelyX Unicem] vs conventional dual-cure [CDCRC, All Bond 2 + Duo-Link]; 10,000 thermocycles).

Nd:YVO4 laser produced the highest surface roughness (Ra 1.32±0.22 µm) statistically equal to H2O2 (1.28±0.18 µm), both exceeding unconditioned control (0.43±0.11 µm, p<0.05); conditioned-group bond strengths were statistically comparable between laser and H2O2 at all canal thirds; CDCRC bond strength declined cervical→apical (highest 11.47 MPa cervical) while SADCRC rose cervical→apical (highest 11.31 MPa apical).

Nd:YVO4 laser is a viable chemical-agent-free alternative to hydrogen peroxide for glass fiber post conditioning; SADCRC performs satisfactorily at all canal thirds and is preferred apically, while CDCRC outperforms cervically when conditioning is used.

## Summary

This in vitro study from King Abdulaziz University (Jeddah, 2026) compared two surface-conditioning methods for glass fiber posts (GFPs) — 24% hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and Nd:YVO4 laser irradiation, both followed by silanization — against an unconditioned control, and crossed each conditioning group with two dual-cure resin cement classes: a conventional total-etch dual-cure cement (CDCRC, All Bond 2 + Duo-Link) and a self-adhesive dual-cure cement (SADCRC, RelyX Unicem). Sixty single-rooted premolars were endodontically treated, post-space prepared, and 75 conditioned/cemented glass fiber posts (White Post DC #2) were sectioned into cervical, middle, and apical thirds after thermocycling (10,000 cycles) for push-out bond strength (PBS) testing, plus surface roughness (Ra) measurement by profilometry and failure-mode analysis by stereomicroscopy.

The Nd:YVO4 laser produced the highest Ra (1.32±0.22 µm), statistically indistinguishable from H2O2 conditioning (1.28±0.18 µm) but significantly rougher than the unconditioned control (0.43±0.11 µm). Push-out bond strength followed the same pattern: laser- and H2O2-conditioned groups were statistically comparable to each other at every canal third, within each cement class, and both clearly outperformed the unconditioned control. The highest single bond strength was in the laser-conditioned CDCRC group's cervical third (11.26±1.17 MPa); the lowest was in the unconditioned CDCRC group's apical third (3.78±0.21 MPa). A striking directional contrast emerged along the canal: CDCRC bond strength declined from cervical to apical in all conditioning groups, while SADCRC bond strength increased from cervical to apical in all conditioning groups — attributed to differing dependence on acid-etch demineralization/tubule density (CDCRC) versus moisture tolerance and reduced-demineralization chemistry (SADCRC). The authors conclude Nd:YVO4 laser is a viable, likely safer, alternative to hydrogen peroxide for GFP surface conditioning, and that self-adhesive resin cement performs satisfactorily across all canal thirds.

## Key Contributions

- First study to directly compare Nd:YVO4 laser irradiation against hydrogen peroxide as a GFP surface conditioner, using a shared silanization step, across both a conventional and a self-adhesive dual-cure resin cement.
- Demonstrates the laser achieves equivalent roughness and bond strength to H2O2 at all three canal thirds — a chemical-agent-free alternative avoiding oxidative degradation/cytotoxicity concerns associated with hydrogen peroxide.
- Reveals opposite bond-strength gradients along the canal for the two cement classes: CDCRC weakens apically (etch/tubule-density dependent), SADCRC strengthens apically (moisture-tolerant, less etch-dependent) — a practically relevant finding for cement selection by canal region.
- All conditioned groups' bond strengths (7–11+ MPa) substantially exceed the ~5 MPa threshold the authors cite as necessary to withstand masticatory forces.

## Methodology

- **Sample**: 60 single-rooted premolars (straight canals, curvature ≤20°, no caries/cracks/resorption/prior RCT), decoronated to 15 mm, embedded in acrylic resin.
- **Endodontic prep**: ProTaper Universal rotary to F3, 2.5% NaOCl + 17% EDTA irrigation, single-cone gutta-percha/AH Plus obturation.
- **Post-space prep**: gutta-percha removed to 10 mm (Peeso reamers), ~4 mm apical gutta-percha retained (radiographically confirmed).
- **Conditioning groups** (81 White Post DC #2 glass fiber posts split n=25 initially; 75 posts used across cemented/tested groups):
  1. No conditioning (silane only, control)
  2. 24% H2O2 immersion (60 s) + Monobond Plus silane
  3. Nd:YVO4 laser (1064 nm, 8 ns pulse, 25 kHz, 5.3 MW/cm², 200 µm/150 µm indentation pattern, 33 s exposure) + silane
- **Ra measurement**: profilometer (Surfcorder SE700), 5 posts/group, 3 measurements averaged.
- **Cementation**:
  - CDCRC: 37% phosphoric acid etch → All Bond 2 adhesive → Duo-Link cement, light-cured.
  - SADCRC: RelyX Unicem, one-step self-adhesive, light-cured.
- **Aging**: 1 week 37°C water storage + 10,000 thermocycles (5–55°C).
- **Sectioning/testing**: transverse slices per third (cervical/middle/apical), push-out on universal testing machine (0.5 mm/min, third-specific plunger diameters), MPa.
- **Failure mode**: stereomicroscope, adhesive/cohesive/admixed classification.
- **Stats**: Shapiro-Wilk, one-way ANOVA + Tukey post hoc (SPSS v21.0).

## Results

**Surface roughness (Ra, µm)**: Control 0.43±0.11 < H2O2-silane 1.28±0.18 ≈ Laser-silane 1.32±0.22 (laser vs H2O2 not significant, p>0.05; both vs control p<0.05).

**Push-out bond strength (MPa) by canal third**:

| Group | Cervical | Middle | Apical |
|---|---|---|---|
| No conditioning + CDCRC | 6.21±0.91 | 5.09±0.13 | 3.78±0.21 (lowest overall) |
| No conditioning + SADCRC | 5.12±0.20 | 5.86±0.11 | 6.75±0.21 |
| H2O2-silane + CDCRC | 11.47±0.52 | 9.67±0.43 | 7.02±0.54 |
| H2O2-silane + SADCRC | 9.41±0.27 | 10.80±0.19 | 11.31±0.21 |
| Laser-silane + CDCRC | 11.26±1.17 | 9.52±0.54 | 7.32±0.43 |
| Laser-silane + SADCRC | 9.56±0.17 | 10.76±0.23 | 11.23±0.17 |

- Laser vs H2O2 conditioning: not significantly different at any canal third, within either cement class (p>0.05).
- CDCRC groups: bond strength declines cervical→apical (p<0.05) — attributed to acid-etch efficiency dropping apically (reduced dentinal tubule density/diameter, debris, limited light penetration).
- SADCRC groups: bond strength rises cervical→apical (p<0.05) — attributed to reduced tubule density favoring the cement's chemistry and higher moisture tolerance apically, while cervical bond strength is limited by the self-adhesive cement's weaker demineralization capacity relative to phosphoric-acid etching.
- Failure modes: admixed failures predominated for both conditioned groups combined with SADCRC (all thirds) and for the cervical third of conditioned+CDCRC groups; middle/apical CDCRC and all no-conditioning groups showed predominantly adhesive failures.
