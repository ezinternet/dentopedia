## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42022314972) of 5 RCTs (329 participants; 4 European + 1 Turkish, published 2017–2020) evaluating treatment approaches for tinnitus associated with DC/TMD-diagnosed TMD.

Pooled meta-analysis showed a significant positive treatment effect on tinnitus symptoms (OR 0.26 [0.19, 0.37]; RR 0.49 [0.40, 0.59]; RD −0.31 [−0.38, −0.24]), with a multidisciplinary non-invasive approach (manual therapy + exercise + education) producing the strongest individual-study results; baseline tinnitus severity, gender, quality of life, age, and tinnitus duration predicted treatment outcome.

Only 5 small RCTs were available, more than half carried risk-of-bias concerns, and treatment-modality heterogeneity was substantial (I² up to 78%), so the multidisciplinary-approach recommendation should be treated as the best current evidence rather than definitive.

메타분석에서 치료가 이명 증상에 유의한 긍정적 효과(오즈비 0.26 [0.19, 0.37]; 위험비 0.49 [0.40, 0.59]; 위험차 −0.31 [−0.38, −0.24])를 보였고, 개별 연구 중 다학제적 (multidisciplinary) 비침습 접근(도수치료+운동+교육 병용)이 가장 우수; 기저 이명 중증도 (Baseline Tinnitus Severity), 성별, 삶의 질, 연령, 이명 지속기간이 치료 결과 예측인자.

## Summary

This SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42022314972, PRISMA-compliant) evaluated randomised controlled trials of treatment approaches for patients with tinnitus associated with temporomandibular disorders (TMD), where TMD was diagnosed using the (Research) Diagnostic Criteria for TMD (DC/TMD or RDC/TMD). Searching Medline, Web of Science, and LILACS up to 14 March 2023 yielded 635 records; after screening, 5 RCTs (329 total participants, mean age 43.3 y) met inclusion. Three treatment paradigms were represented: manual therapy + exercise + education (2 RCTs, same underlying Spanish cohort — one on outcomes, one on predictors), orofacial physical therapy with a delayed-start design (2 RCTs, same underlying Belgian cohort — one on outcomes, one on predictors), and low-level laser therapy (LLLT, 1 RCT, Turkey). Pooled meta-analysis (OR, RR, RD) confirmed a statistically significant positive effect of treatment on tinnitus symptoms across all three effect measures, and the authors concluded that a multidisciplinary non-invasive approach is the most efficacious treatment for tinnitus in TMD patients. Baseline tinnitus severity, gender, quality of life, age, and tinnitus duration were identified as predictive factors of clinical outcome.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA to specifically evaluate RCT-level treatment outcomes (not just cross-sectional association) for tinnitus in patients diagnosed with TMD via DC/TMD/RDC-TMD criteria.
- Pools 5 heterogeneous RCTs across 3 treatment paradigms into a single meta-analysis using three complementary effect measures (OR, RR, RD), all pointing toward a significant positive treatment effect.
- Synthesizes predictive factors (tinnitus severity, gender, QoL, age, tinnitus duration) across the two predictor-focused RCTs into a clinically usable prognostic picture.
- Confirms via RoB 2 that only one of five included RCTs (Demirkol et al.) carries "some concerns" across all bias domains, giving a relatively favorable overall risk-of-bias profile despite the small evidence base.

## Methodology

**PICO**: Participants — patients with tinnitus and DC/TMD-diagnosed TMD; excluded were tinnitus of medical origin (infections, otosclerosis, Ménière's disease), other correlated syndromes, otologic/craniofacial cancers, prior maxillofacial surgery, animal studies, and incomplete records. Non-RCT designs (cross-sectional surveys, case reports/series, reviews/meta-analyses, opinion pieces, protocols) were excluded.

**Databases**: Medline, Web of Science, LILACS, no date/language/type restriction, searched to 14 March 2023, plus manual reference-list search of prior SRs. Two independent reviewers screened and extracted data (Excel sheet: authors, year, country, sample, gender, mean age, TMD diagnosis method, tinnitus diagnosis, intervention, control, outcomes/predictive factors); disagreements resolved by consensus/third reviewer.

**Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB 2 for randomised trials, two reviewers + third-reviewer arbitration.

**Statistics**: R 3.65; mean difference and SD computed on extracted data; meta-analysis on measurable outcomes using odds ratio, risk ratio, and risk difference with forest plots.

**The 5 included RCTs**:

| Study | Country | n | Intervention vs Control | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delgado de la Serna 2020 | Spain | 61 | Manual therapy + exercise + education (n=31) vs exercise + education (n=30) | Treatment outcomes |
| Plaza-Manzano 2021 | Spain | 56 | Same design as above (predictor analysis) | Predictive indicators |
| Van der Wal, Michiels 2020 | Belgium | 80 | Early-start (n=40) vs delayed-start (n=40) orofacial physical therapy | Treatment outcomes |
| Van der Wal, Van de Heyning 2020 | Belgium | 101 | Same design as above (predictor analysis) | Predictive indicators |
| Demirkol 2017 | Turkey | 31 | Nd:YAG laser (n=15) vs 810 nm diode laser (n=16), LLLT to external auditory meatus | Treatment outcomes |

Study flow: 635 records → 472 excluded pre-screening + 63 duplicates → 100 full texts assessed → 5 RCTs included (PRISMA 2020). 4 RCTs from Europe (2 Spain, 2 Belgium), 1 from Turkey; all published 2017–2020; 175 participants in intervention arms, 154 in control arms.

## Results

**Delgado de la Serna 2020** (manual therapy + exercise + education vs exercise + education alone): the manual-therapy arm showed significantly greater improvement in TMD pain (NPRS, F=10.639, p<.001, η²=0.153), tinnitus severity (VAS, F=17.878, p<.001, η²=0.233), THI (F=39.291, p<.001, η²=0.501), CF-PDI (F=18.096, p<.001, η²=0.395), depressive symptoms (BDI-II, F=14.234, p<.001, η²=0.194), mandibular range of motion (mouth opening F=17.683, p<.001; lateral excursions F=18.594, p<.001), and pressure pain thresholds at masseter (F=29.494, p<.001), temporalis (F=18.594, p<.001) and TMJ (F=15.448, p<.001). Health-related quality of life (SF-12) did not differ between groups (F=0.590, p=.622).

**Plaza-Manzano 2021** (predictors, same cohort): higher baseline tinnitus severity and tinnitus-related handicap predicted better 3- and 6-month outcomes (explaining 12–42% of variance across outcomes); higher baseline pressure-pain hypersensitivity over temporalis predicted poorer outcomes (10–41% of variance).

**Van der Wal, Michiels 2020** (early- vs delayed-start orofacial physical therapy, TFI 25–90 at baseline): 34% of patients achieved a clinically relevant TQ improvement (cutoff ≥8.72) immediately post-treatment, rising to 46% at follow-up; TFI clinically relevant improvement (cutoff ≥13) was 41% immediately and 61% at follow-up.

**Van der Wal, Van de Heyning 2020** (predictors, same cohort): a model using "duration of tinnitus" and "higher initial TQ somatic subscale score" correctly predicted TQ outcome in 68.5% of patients; a model using "age," "female gender," and "duration of tinnitus" predicted TFI outcome in 68.1%. Individual predictors: female gender OR 2.70, higher TQ somatic subscale score OR 1.52, painful TMJ palpation OR 2.46, better speech-in-noise test score OR 0.88.

**Demirkol 2017** (LLLT): both laser arms improved significantly from baseline to 1 month (Nd:YAG p=.001; diode p=.005); median percentage improvement in VAS tinnitus severity was 100% for the Nd:YAG group vs 30% for the 810 nm diode group.

**Pooled meta-analysis (all 5 RCTs)**:
- Odds ratio 0.26, 95% CI [0.19, 0.37]; heterogeneity χ²=6.24, df=4, p=.18, I²=36% (moderate); overall effect z=7.75, p<.00001.
- Risk ratio 0.49, 95% CI [0.40, 0.59]; heterogeneity χ²=5.12, df=4, p=.28, I²=22% (low); overall effect z=7.53, p<.00001.
- Risk difference −0.31, 95% CI [−0.38, −0.24]; heterogeneity χ²=18.34, df=4, p=.001, I²=78% (substantial); overall effect z=8.53, p<.00001.

**Risk of bias**: only Demirkol et al. showed "some concerns" across all RoB 2 domains; high risk of performance bias (blinding) in 2 RCTs; 80% of studies had low risk of bias from randomisation; only 40% had minimal risk of performance bias; 100% had complete outcome-data reporting.

**Conclusion**: a multidisciplinary non-invasive approach (exercise + education + manual therapy) is the most efficacious treatment identified for tinnitus in DC/TMD-diagnosed TMD patients. Baseline tinnitus severity, gender, quality of life, age, and tinnitus duration are predictive factors of clinical outcome.

**Limitations**: only 5 RCTs available for meta-analysis; risk-of-bias concerns in more than half of included studies; substantial heterogeneity in treatment modalities (I²=78% for the risk-difference estimate); BMI not assessed as a predictor despite plausible relevance; authors call for larger multicentric prospective RCTs with longer follow-up.
