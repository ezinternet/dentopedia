## Three-line Summary

Observational cross-sectional study (n=12 edentulous individuals) comparing four centric relation (CR) recording techniques — extraoral gothic arch (EOGA), intraoral gothic arch (IOGA), deglutition (D), and frontal manipulation with tongue elevation (FMTE) — each repeated 3× by the same operator, reproducibility assessed via tri-dimensional condylar-housing displacement on a semi-adjustable articulator.

Deglutition showed significantly greater anteroposterior displacement (right 3.78±0.69 mm, left 3.45±0.74 mm) than the other three techniques (EOGA 3.00±0.00/3.00±0.00, FMTE 2.81±0.52/2.82±0.79, IOGA 2.90±0.65/3.12±0.44 mm; p<0.001); IOGA (340.40±163.41 s) and EOGA (285.93±133.84 s) took substantially longer than D (86.00±34.33 s) and FMTE (101.33±36.72 s) (p<0.001).

Authors conclude graphic recordings show better reproducibility and accuracy, but the reported anteroposterior means place FMTE numerically lowest and EOGA/IOGA/FMTE close together — the clean statistical separation is deglutition being worst, not graphic tracing being clearly best; graphic tracing's real, unambiguous cost is chair time (~3× longer).

## Summary

According to PubMed, this observational cross-sectional study ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jopr.13635); PMID 36573906) compares four centric relation (CR) recording techniques in edentulous individuals: extraoral gothic arch (EOGA) tracing, intraoral gothic arch (IOGA) tracing, deglutition (D), and frontal manipulation with tongue elevation (FMTE). Twelve subjects each had all four techniques recorded three times by the same operator, with reproducibility quantified as the tri-dimensional displacement of the condylar housing relative to the condylar guide wall (glenoid cavity) on a semi-adjustable articulator, and time spent per technique timed in seconds. This spans the same static-versus-functional design space seen elsewhere in the CR-recording literature, but pits two functional/graphic techniques (EOGA, IOGA) against two guided/physiologic techniques (D, FMTE) rather than against a static interocclusal record.

## Key Contributions

- Fills a gap explicitly named by a prior systematic review with overlapping authorship ([[complete-denture/de-moraes-melo-neto-2021-centric-relation-condylar-reproducibility-sr]]): that SR found no edentulous-patient data and no head-to-head comparison of graphic (gothic arch) vs. physiologic techniques. This study is a direct clinical follow-up filling both gaps.
- Rare **four-way** comparison (EOGA, IOGA, D, FMTE) in the same edentulous cohort, same operator, each technique repeated 3×.
- Times each technique to the second — graphic tracing (EOGA/IOGA, 286–340 s) costs roughly **3× the chair time** of deglutition/FMTE (86–101 s), a practical cost largely absent from comparable literature.
- **Caveat on the headline conclusion**: the authors state "graphic recordings showed better reproducibility," but the reported anteroposterior means only cleanly separate deglutition (significantly worst) from the other three techniques — FMTE (2.81/2.82 mm) is numerically the *lowest* displacement of all four, and EOGA (3.00/3.00 mm), IOGA (2.90/3.12 mm) are close to it. The data support "graphic and FMTE both beat deglutition," not "graphic beats FMTE."
- **Data-quality flag**: EOGA is reported as exactly 3.00 ± 0.00 mm on both sides — zero standard deviation across 12 subjects × 3 repetitions is implausible for a continuous measurement and likely reflects a measurement-resolution or rounding artifact rather than genuinely zero variability.

## Methodology

- **Design**: observational cross-sectional study, single operator.
- **Sample**: 12 fully edentulous individuals.
- **Techniques** (all 4 performed on every subject): extraoral gothic arch (EOGA) tracing, intraoral gothic arch (IOGA) tracing, deglutition (D), frontal manipulation with tongue elevation (FMTE).
- **Repetition**: each technique repeated 3×, same time of day, 30-minute interval between techniques.
- **Reproducibility outcome**: tri-dimensional displacement of the condylar housing (mandibular condyle position) relative to the wall of the condylar guide (glenoid cavity) on a semi-adjustable articulator — anteroposterior, mediolateral, superior-inferior, per side.
- **Time outcome**: seconds from the start of each technique until wax occlusion fixation.
- **Statistics**: two-way ANOVA + Tukey for anteroposterior displacement (technique × side); one-way ANOVA + Tukey for time; Kruskal-Wallis for mediolateral/superior-inferior displacement across techniques; Mann-Whitney for right-vs-left side comparison. α<0.05.

## Results

**Anteroposterior (AP) displacement, mm (mean ± SD)** — technique factor significant, ANOVA α<0.001, F=11.396

| Technique | Right side | Left side |
|---|---|---|
| Deglutition (D) | **3.78 ± 0.69** | **3.45 ± 0.74** |
| Extraoral gothic arch (EOGA) | 3.00 ± 0.00 | 3.00 ± 0.00 |
| Intraoral gothic arch (IOGA) | 2.90 ± 0.65 | 3.12 ± 0.44 |
| Frontal manipulation + tongue elevation (FMTE) | 2.81 ± 0.52 | 2.82 ± 0.79 |

D differed significantly from the other three techniques; EOGA, IOGA, and FMTE were not clearly separated from one another in the reported summary statistics.

**Time spent, seconds (mean ± SD)** — technique factor significant, ANOVA α<0.001, F=21.118

| Technique | Time |
|---|---|
| Intraoral gothic arch (IOGA) | **340.40 ± 163.41** |
| Extraoral gothic arch (EOGA) | 285.93 ± 133.84 |
| Frontal manipulation + tongue elevation (FMTE) | 101.33 ± 36.72 |
| Deglutition (D) | 86.00 ± 34.33 |

Mediolateral and superior-inferior displacement were analyzed (Kruskal-Wallis across techniques, Mann-Whitney for side) but numeric values are not given in the abstract.

**Authors' conclusion**: "Graphic recordings showed better reproducibility and accuracy of the position of the centric relation" — see the Key Contributions caveat above on how far the reported AP numbers actually support this.
