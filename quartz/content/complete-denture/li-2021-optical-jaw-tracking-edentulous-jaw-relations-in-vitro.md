## Three-line Summary

In vitro accuracy study establishing a recording method for edentulous jaw relations using an optical jaw tracking system, validated against a positioning-cylinder control method on maxillary/mandibular edentulous arch models and complete dentures mounted on an articulator.

Four eccentric jaw relations were simulated — protrusion (A), left laterotrusion (B), right laterotrusion (C), small opening (D) — with 3D mandibular deviation of 131±39 μm, 133±44 μm, 120±51 μm, and 112±52 μm respectively; mean absolute displacement values were all under 200 μm.

Authors conclude bench accuracy is "acceptable for clinical demand," but this is a bench claim on rigid models with no soft tissue, muscle, or mucosal displacement — they themselves state that patient studies are still required to verify it clinically.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this in vitro accuracy study ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.11607/ijp.7126); PMID 33651032) from Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology establishes and quantitatively validates a recording method for edentulous jaw relations using an optical jaw tracking system. Maxillary and mandibular edentulous arch models and complete dentures were mounted on an articulator, and four eccentric jaw relations — protrusion, left and right laterotrusion, and small opening — were simulated with metal-foil wax records. A test group used the optical tracking system's 3D trajectory data to rehabilitate digital arch relations at each position; a control group used direct measurement of positioning-cylinder center points pasted on the model surfaces. With the maxilla as the common reference, the two groups' arch relations were registered against each other and 3D mandibular deviation was calculated. This is purely a bench validation of the tracking hardware's capture accuracy on rigid models — it does not involve patients, soft tissue, or muscle, and the authors say so explicitly in their own conclusion.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes and quantitatively validates a recording method for edentulous jaw relations using an optical jaw tracking system, benchmarked against a positioning-cylinder-based control method on the same articulator-mounted models.
- Reports sub-200 μm 3D mandibular deviation across four eccentric jaw relations (protrusion, left/right laterotrusion, small opening) — an order of magnitude below the 0.74-0.86 mm that [[complete-denture/singh-2026-nick-notch-intraoral-gothic-arch-jaw-relation]] treats as clinically perceptible, consistent with this being a rigid bench measurement rather than a mouth measurement.
- Direct methodological precursor to the same group's patient study [[complete-denture/li-2022-digital-edentulous-maxillomandibular-relationship-jaw-tracking]] (n=10 edentulous patients), which reports 0.76-1.35 mm whole-arch 3D deviation (95% CI, p<0.001) and concludes accuracy "needs to be further improved" — the bench-to-mouth gap is roughly **6-10×**, a concrete, quantified illustration of how far an in vitro accuracy claim travels once soft tissue, muscle activity, and mucosal displacement are introduced.
- Scopes the validation to eccentric/border-adjacent positions (protrusive and lateral excursions, small opening), not the centric relation record itself — this paper validates trajectory-capture accuracy of the tracking hardware, not centric relation determination.

## Methodology

- **Design**: in vitro accuracy study, articulator-mounted edentulous arch models and complete dentures.
- **Test group**: optical jaw tracking system recorded 3D trajectories of targets from intercuspation to four positions — protrusion (A), left laterotrusion (B), right laterotrusion (C), small opening (D) — aided by metal foil wax records; trajectories used to rehabilitate digital arch relations for A-D.
- **Control group**: six pairs of positioning cylinders pasted onto axial model surfaces; center points of the cylinder bottom surfaces in A-D measured directly to rehabilitate digital arch relations.
- **Registration**: maxilla used as the common reference area; test-group arch relations registered onto the control group; 3D deviations of the mandible calculated.
- **Outcome decomposition**: displacements analyzed in horizontal left/right, horizontal anterior/posterior, and vertical directions, in addition to overall 3D deviation.

## Results

**3D mandibular deviation by simulated jaw relation**

| Position | 3D deviation |
|---|---|
| Protrusion (A) | **131 ± 39 μm** |
| Left laterotrusion (B) | **133 ± 44 μm** |
| Right laterotrusion (C) | **120 ± 51 μm** |
| Small opening (D) | **112 ± 52 μm** |

- Mean absolute values of mandibular displacement (horizontal L/R, horizontal A/P, vertical) were all less than 200 μm.
- Authors' conclusion: "the accuracy of jaw relation records as measured on an articulator was acceptable for clinical demand." Immediately qualified by the authors themselves: "Further investigations among patients are required to clinically verify the results of this study."
- **Bench-to-mouth calibration**: this study's ~0.11-0.13 mm bench figures sit an order of magnitude below the 0.74-0.86 mm that [[complete-denture/singh-2026-nick-notch-intraoral-gothic-arch-jaw-relation]] treats as clinically perceptible — which is exactly why the bench number here is reassuring while, by the same yardstick, the same team's later patient-level number (0.76-1.35 mm in [[complete-denture/li-2022-digital-edentulous-maxillomandibular-relationship-jaw-tracking]]) is not.
