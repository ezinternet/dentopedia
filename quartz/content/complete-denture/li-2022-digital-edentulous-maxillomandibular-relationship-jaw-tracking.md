## Three-line Summary

Prospective clinical accuracy study (n=10 edentulous patients) at Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology testing an in-house jaw movement tracking system that records habitual opening-closing trajectory and mandibular rest position (MRP), then derives a digital maxillomandibular relationship from the highest-density trajectory intersections on a plane 2 mm above MRP.

Compared against conventionally fabricated complete dentures (intercuspal position) as control, the anterior reference point showed no significant displacement difference, but whole-arch 3D deviation was significant (95% CI 0.76-1.35 mm, p<0.001).

Authors conclude the method is established in principle but accuracy needs further improvement before clinical use — a proof-of-principle result, not a validated clinical technique.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this Journal of Prosthodontics (2022;31(8):663-672) prospective accuracy study ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jopr.13529); PMID 35510309) from the Center of Digital Dentistry, Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology, presents a direct digital method for determining and recording the edentulous maxillomandibular relationship using a custom-made jaw movement tracking system. The method covers **both axes** of the relationship in one workflow: the **vertical** relationship is fixed as a plane parallel to the ala-tragus and horizontal lines, constructed 2 mm above the measured mandibular rest position (MRP) — an operationalized freeway space/interocclusal rest space — and the **horizontal** relationship is derived from the point of highest density among the intersections of the tracked habitual opening-closing trajectory with that plane, interpreted as the position of highest movement repeatability. This dual coverage is exactly what makes the paper relevant to the centric-relation-plus-vertical-dimension (CR+VD) question in edentulous prosthodontics: most digital methods target only one axis. However, the result reads as cautionary rather than confirmatory. Against a control group (the patient's own new complete denture, teeth in intercuspal position), the anterior reference point showed no significant displacement difference — but the 3D deviation across the **entire mandibular arch** was significant, 95% CI 0.76 mm to 1.35 mm, p < 0.001. A single point agreeing while the whole arch does not is a classic signature of a **rotational/angular** discrepancy rather than a pure translational one. The authors themselves conclude only that a method "was established" and that "the accuracy needs to be further improved" — language that should not be read as validating a working clinical technique. A whole-arch deviation on the order of ~0.76-1.35 mm sits at or above magnitudes this wiki elsewhere treats as clinically perceptible (cf. [[complete-denture/singh-2026-nick-notch-intraoral-gothic-arch-jaw-relation]], where 0.74-0.86 mm extraoral Gothic-arch error is characterized the same way).

## Key Contributions

- Single-workflow determination of **both** the vertical relationship (rest-position-based, 2 mm above MRP) and the horizontal relationship (highest-density trajectory-plane intersection) from one tracked jaw-movement dataset — most competing digital approaches address only one axis.
- Registers multisource data (tracking trajectory, impression scans, facial-structure scans) through a custom recording tool — a proof-of-concept full digital chairside-to-record pipeline for edentulous patients, not a bench-only measurement.
- Reports accuracy against a clinical (not merely bench/in-vitro) reference, using whole-arch 3D deviation rather than a single linear distance — a more sensitive outcome measure than an anterior-point-only comparison.
- Clinical successor to the same group's own in-vitro bench validation ([[complete-denture/li-2021-optical-jaw-tracking-edentulous-jaw-relations-in-vitro]]), enabling a direct bench-to-patient accuracy comparison.

## Methodology

- **Design**: prospective clinical accuracy study, single institution (Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology).
- **Sample**: 10 edentulous patients.
- **Tracking**: in-house jaw movement tracking system recorded the trajectory of habitual opening-closing jaw movement and mandibular rest position (MRP).
- **Scanning**: 3D surface scanning of conventional maxillomandibular impressions and of facial structures with the patient in MRP.
- **Registration**: trajectory data + impression scans + facial scans registered together via a custom-made recording tool.
- **Vertical relationship**: a plane parallel to the ala-tragus and horizontal reference lines, constructed 2 mm above MRP; its vertical position set the vertical relationship.
- **Horizontal relationship**: intersections of the tracked trajectory passing through that plane were located and their density distributions analyzed; the coordinates of highest density (interpreted as highest movement repeatability) were used to construct the digital maxillomandibular relationship — the **test group**.
- **Control group**: the maxillomandibular relationship of the patient's new complete dentures, artificial teeth in intercuspal position, made by the conventional clinical workflow — i.e. the reference standard is a conventional denture's output, not an independently verified ground truth.
- **Outcomes**: displacement of an anterior reference point (referenced to centric relation position), compared test vs control with a Wilcoxon signed-ranks test; 3D deviation of the entire mandibular arch, evaluated with a one-sample t-test.

## Results

| Outcome | Finding | Statistic |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior reference point displacement (vs centric relation position) | Max displacement in horizontal anteroposterior direction for both groups; **no significant difference** test vs control | Wilcoxon signed-ranks |
| Whole mandibular arch 3D deviation (test vs control) | **Significant** deviation | 95% CI 0.76-1.35 mm, p < 0.001 (one-sample t-test) |

- A single anterior point matching control while the whole-arch deviation is significant suggests the discrepancy is predominantly **rotational/angular**, not a simple anteroposterior shift.
- **Bench-to-clinic contrast**: the group's own in-vitro predecessor ([[complete-denture/li-2021-optical-jaw-tracking-edentulous-jaw-relations-in-vitro]]) reported 3D deviations of 112-133 μm (0.11-0.13 mm) on bench models — roughly **6-10× smaller** than the 0.76-1.35 mm seen here in patients, a substantial accuracy drop plausibly from soft-tissue compliance, patient movement, and MRP/facial-scan registration error absent on a bench.
- Author conclusion (per abstract): the method "was established," but "the accuracy needs to be further improved" — a proof-of-principle outcome, not a clinically validated one.
- **Design limits to weigh alongside the numbers**: n=10, single centre; an in-house, non-commercial device (not independently reproducible); and a control group that is itself a conventional denture's intercuspal output rather than a verified true jaw relation, so agreement/disagreement with control does not by itself prove accuracy. The core method premise — that the highest-density point of *habitual* closure marks the desired reference position — is also a substantive assumption: habitual closure is not necessarily centric relation, particularly in long-term edentulous patients who have lost periodontal/proprioceptive feedback.
