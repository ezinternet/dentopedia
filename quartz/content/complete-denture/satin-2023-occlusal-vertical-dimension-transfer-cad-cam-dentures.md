## Three-line Summary

In-vitro simulation study comparing two OVD-transfer workflows for digital complete dentures — duplicate dentures (DD, n=15) versus occlusion rims (OR, n=15) — using mounted edentulous casts and STL superimposition against a control cast scan.

OR produced significantly larger overall deviation than DD at all three measured locations (0.72 ± 0.13 mm vs 0.45 ± 0.23 mm; F = 46.00, p < 0.0001); 98.9% of deviations were positive, meaning both workflows systematically increased OVD rather than scattering randomly.

The authors conclude DD is more accurate for OVD transfer than OR, but explicitly flag that the magnitude of difference (~0.27 mm) may not be clinically significant — a live, unresolved question rather than a settled one.

디지털(CAD-CAM) 완전틀니 제작 시 수직고경(Occlusal Vertical Dimension, OVD) 전달 정확도를 duplicate denture(중복의치, DD, n=15) 워크플로와 occlusion rim(교합제, OR, n=15) 워크플로로 비교한 in-vitro 모형 실험.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this in-vitro study ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jopr.13669); PMID 36815435) investigated which of two commonly used digital-denture workflows more accurately transfers an already-determined occlusal vertical dimension (OVD) from a fitted or provisional state into the final CAD-CAM denture: duplicating an existing denture (**DD workflow**) versus constructing new **occlusion rims (OR workflow)** from a fresh final impression. This is a distinct question from OVD *determination* (covered elsewhere in the wiki) — here OVD is assumed already correct, and the study asks how faithfully each digital workflow preserves it. Mounted edentulous casts (not human patients) simulated an edentulous patient; each workflow's output was scanned with fiduciary markers, virtually articulated, exported as STL, and superimposed in Geomagic Control X against a control STL of the articulated casts. Deviations at three locations were compared with two-way ANOVA. The DD workflow showed smaller deviation from the control than the OR workflow at every measured location, and this difference was highly significant. Critically, the direction of error was consistent — nearly all deviations increased OVD rather than decreasing it — indicating both workflows carry a systematic bias rather than random noise. The authors nonetheless stop short of calling the DD-vs-OR gap clinically significant, leaving that judgment open.

## Key Contributions

- Isolates OVD-**transfer** fidelity as distinct from OVD-**determination**, quantifying how much a digital denture workflow itself adds to (or preserves) an already-correct vertical dimension.
- Establishes that error is directional, not just in magnitude: 98.9% of deviations were positive, meaning both DD and OR workflows systematically **increase** OVD — a predictable, and therefore clinically compensable, bias rather than random scatter.
- Provides comparative benchmarks for digital OVD-transfer accuracy: DD 0.45 ± 0.23 mm vs OR 0.72 ± 0.13 mm overall deviation from control.
- Explicitly hedges the clinical significance of the ~0.27 mm gap between workflows rather than overclaiming DD's superiority as clinically meaningful.

## Methodology

- **Design**: In-vitro simulation study; mounted edentulous casts used to simulate a completely edentulous patient — **not** a clinical/patient study. n=15 per workflow refers to fabricated specimens, not patients.
- **Groups**:
  - **DD (duplicate denture) workflow** — complete dentures fabricated and relined (n = 15).
  - **OR (occlusion rim) workflow** — final impressions taken and occlusion rims constructed (n = 15).
- **Digitization**: Relined dentures and occlusion rims, each with fiduciary markers, were scanned and virtually articulated, then exported as STL files.
- **Control**: A scan of the articulated casts (simulated patient), with embedded fiduciary markers, exported as STL and used as the reference standard.
- **Measurement**: STL files for each workflow superimposed over the control in 3D inspection software (Geomagic Control X, 3D Systems, NC); deviations (mm) measured at three locations.
- **Statistics**: Two-way ANOVA (α = 0.05) comparing average 3D deviation among groups and locations.
- **Scope limitation**: DD requires an existing, well-fitting denture to duplicate — it is not an available option for a first-time edentulous patient with no prior denture.

## Results

| Measure | DD workflow (n=15) | OR workflow (n=15) | Statistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall mean deviation from control | **0.45 ± 0.23 mm** | 0.72 ± 0.13 mm | F = 46.00, p < 0.0001 |
| Deviation direction | — | 98.9% of measurements positive (increased OVD) | — |
| Difference across measurement locations | — | No significant difference between points | — |
| Location × method interaction | — | — | F = 0.02, p ≈ 0.98 (none) |

- OR deviation was significantly larger than DD at **all three** measurement locations, with no interaction between location and method — the DD-vs-OR gap held consistently across sites.
- Nearly all deviation (98.9% of measurements) was positive, i.e., both workflows tended to **increase** OVD rather than decrease it — a systematic, one-directional bias rather than random error.
- Authors' own conclusion: DD showed less OVD-transfer deviation than OR, "but the deviation was small and may not be clinically significant."
- **Clinical calibration note**: [[complete-denture/singh-2026-nick-notch-intraoral-gothic-arch-jaw-relation]] found horizontal-plane (extraoral Gothic arch) jaw-relation error of 0.74-0.86 mm to be potentially clinically perceptible. The OR-workflow vertical deviation here (0.72 mm) is numerically similar, but the two are not directly equivalent — vertical OVD error is partly absorbed by freeway space / interocclusal rest space in a way horizontal jaw-relation error is not, so a like-magnitude vertical error does not automatically carry the same clinical weight.
