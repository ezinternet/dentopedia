## Three-line Summary

Prospective within-subject experimental study (18 dentate adults, Angle Class I occlusion, 21 recruited/3 dropped out; each participant received 8 occlusal splints varying OVD increase method [articulator-based vs intraoral], amount [2 mm vs 5 mm], and virtual facebow use [yes/no]) evaluating whether a virtual-articulator workflow reproduces occlusal contacts accurately when the occlusal vertical dimension (OVD) is increased, using Group D (2 mm intraoral increase, no facebow) as the reference baseline.

The number of occlusal contacts was not significantly affected by OVD increase amount, method, or virtual facebow use — but spatial deviations of those same contacts were: no significant X (lateral) deviation, but significant Y (anteroposterior) and Z (superoinferior) deviations in several groups, most pronounced with a 5 mm intraoral increase (Z effect sizes moderate-to-large, d = 0.57–1.20); articulator-based 5 mm increase produced fewer deviations than intraoral, and the virtual facebow did not substantially reduce deviations in any comparison.

Contact count is an insensitive metric that can mask real spatial drift — the same number of contacts land in different places as OVD increases, particularly vertically at 5 mm — and because deviations are measured relative to Group D rather than a validated ground truth, the study quantifies inter-method disagreement, not absolute accuracy.

## 세줄요약

전향적 개체-내(within-subject) 실험 연구(치열 있는 성인 18명, Angle Class I, 21명 모집·3명 탈락; 참가자당 교합 스플린트 8개 — OVD 증가 방법[교합기 vs 구강내] × 증가량[2mm vs 5mm] × 가상 안궁이전(virtual facebow) 사용 여부[유/무] 조합) — Group D(구강내 2mm 증가, 가상 안궁이전 없음)를 기준선으로 가상 교합기(virtual articulator) 워크플로가 수직고경(OVD) 증가 시 교합접촉을 정확히 재현하는지 평가.

교합접촉 개수는 OVD 증가량·방법·가상 안궁이전 사용 여부에 유의한 영향을 받지 않았으나, 같은 접촉점의 공간적 편차는 유의했다 — X(측방) 방향 유의차 없음, Y(전후방)·Z(수직) 방향은 여러 군에서 유의한 편차(특히 구강내 5mm 증가에서 두드러짐, Z방향 효과크기 중간~큼 d=0.57–1.20); 교합기 기반 5mm 증가가 구강내 방식보다 편차가 적었고, 가상 안궁이전은 어느 비교에서도 편차를 유의하게 줄이지 못했다.

접촉 개수는 실제 공간적 이동을 놓칠 수 있는 둔감한 지표다 — OVD가 증가할수록(특히 구강내 5mm·수직 방향) 접촉점 개수는 그대로여도 위치는 이동한다. 모든 편차는 검증된 ground truth가 아니라 Group D 대비 상대값이므로, 이 연구는 절대 정확도가 아니라 방법 간 불일치(disagreement)를 정량화한 것이다.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this Turkish prospective experimental study ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.4047/jap.2025.17.6.339); PMID 41536754; PMCID PMC12798328) asked a question digital-workflow prosthodontics has not settled: when a patient's occlusal vertical dimension (OVD) is increased for splint or restorative therapy, does a fully digital pipeline — intraoral scanning, virtual facebow, and a virtual articulator — actually reproduce the occlusal contacts accurately, and does it matter whether the increase is performed on the articulator or intraorally, or whether a virtual facebow is used at all? Eighteen dentate Angle Class I adults (of 21 recruited, 3 dropped for reasons unrelated to the protocol) each received eight occlusal splints spanning a full factorial of OVD method (articulator-based vs. intraoral), amount (2 mm vs. 5 mm), and virtual facebow use (yes/no), with Group D — a 2 mm intraoral increase without a facebow — serving as the reference baseline for all comparisons.

The headline finding has a sharp edge that is easy to misread: the **number** of occlusal contacts was statistically unaffected by OVD amount, method, or facebow use, but the **position** of those contacts was not. No significant deviation appeared in the X (lateral) direction, but significant deviations occurred in Y (anteroposterior) and especially Z (superoinferior), and both grew with the amount of OVD increase — most pronounced at 5 mm intraorally, where Z-direction effect sizes reached moderate-to-large (d = 0.57–1.20). This is the paper's real message, and it is easy to state the finding backwards: "no significant effect on contact number" does **not** mean the virtual articulator reproduces occlusion faithfully at increased OVD — it means a naive contact-count check will not catch the drift that is actually happening. The directional pattern is mechanistically coherent: increasing OVD opens the mandible by rotating it about the condylar axis, so error should — and did — grow with the amount of opening and express itself vertically (Z) and anteroposteriorly (Y), while sparing the lateral (X) axis, which the bilateral, symmetric recording protocol was designed to keep stable.

Two further findings carry direct clinical weight. First, when the OVD increase was 5 mm, the **articulator-based** method produced fewer deviations than the **intraoral** method — the opposite of the common assumption that intraoral registration is inherently "more direct" and therefore more accurate; at larger openings, transferring the increase digitally on the articulator held up better than recording it in the mouth. Second — and this is the negative finding worth its own weight — the **virtual facebow did not substantially reduce deviations** in any comparison. The facebow's entire clinical rationale is to transfer the maxilla's spatial relationship to the hinge axis so that the arc of closure, and therefore the consequences of a VD change, can be reproduced individually rather than assumed; a workflow-relevant negative result here is exactly what a clinician deciding whether to invest in virtual-facebow hardware/software would want to know before doing so. The authors attribute this partly to the virtual facebow's reliance on the arbitrary Beyron hinge-axis point rather than a true kinematically recorded axis, and partly to the flat splint surfaces used (a software constraint) which may have understated the facebow's intended benefit on natural cusp–fossa geometry.

Two interpretive limits deserve equal emphasis. The title says "relative deviations" deliberately: every group was compared to Group D, a clinically reasoned but **unvalidated** baseline — no group in this study was shown to be objectively correct, so the results describe disagreement among digital methods, not absolute accuracy against a gold standard. And the population is dentate adults receiving occlusal splints for a reversible, controlled OVD increase (undergraduate dental students, Angle Class I, no TMD, no missing teeth) — this says nothing directly about edentulous complete-denture VD determination, a different clinical problem with a different anatomical starting point.

## Key Contributions

- **Isolates where digital OVD-increase workflows introduce error**, via a factorial design (method × amount × facebow) that lets each factor's contribution to deviation be read separately rather than confounded together.
- **Decouples contact count from contact position** — the paper's central, easy-to-misread finding: number of occlusal contacts was unaffected by any factor, but spatial position (Y, Z) was significantly affected. "Same number of contacts" is not "same occlusion."
- **Directional error pattern matches arc-of-closure mechanics**: no X (lateral) deviation, growing Y/Z deviation with OVD amount — internally coherent with mandibular rotation about the condylar axis during opening.
- **Negative finding on the virtual facebow**: it did not significantly reduce deviations in any comparison — a commercially and conceptually notable result given the facebow's rationale is to make VD-change consequences individually transferable.
- **Explicit relative-not-absolute framing**: no group is a validated ground truth; the study quantifies inter-method disagreement, an interpretive limit the authors state directly rather than let readers infer.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective, single-center, within-subject (each of 18 participants received all 8 splint conditions), Istanbul University Faculty of Dentistry, Dept. of Prosthodontics.
- **Sample**: G*Power sample-size calculation (Cohen's d = 0.8, 80% power, α = .05) indicated 16 needed; 21 recruited for dropout buffer; 3 dropped (2 scheduling, 1 gag reflex) → **18 completed** (14 F / 4 M, 18–35 y, dental students, Angle Class I, no missing teeth, no TMD).
- **Baseline/OVD-increase records**: Trios 3 IOS for MIP scan (MIP-0); OVD increased intraorally via calibrated tin foil (2.0 ± 0.05 mm or 5.0 ± 0.07 mm, Gerber Resilience Test technique) under the first-premolar buccal cusps bilaterally → scanned as MIP-2/MIP-5.
- **Virtual facebow**: AFT System One facial scan (Bellus3D camera + intraoral transfer fork stabilized with PVS putty), Beyron arbitrary hinge-axis points marked bilaterally on skin.
- **8 splint groups (A–H)**, full factorial of method (articulator A/B/E/F vs. intraoral C/D/G/H) × amount (2 mm A/B/C/D vs. 5 mm E/F/G/H) × facebow (yes A/C/E/G vs. no B/D/F/H); **Group D (intraoral 2 mm, no facebow) = reference baseline**. Designed in Exocad on an Artex CR semi-adjustable virtual articulator (dynamic occlusion disabled — static comparison only), 3D-printed (Formlabs Form 2, Dental LT Clear Resin).
- **Contact recording**: CR contacts marked with 40 µm blue Artifol, MIP with 40 µm red Artifol (contact points counted/photographed); scannable PVS bite material (Occlufast Rock) recorded after 5-minute deprogramming, scanned (TRIOS 3).
- **Analysis**: Geomagic Control X superimposition, Group D as global best-fit registration control; most prominent occlusal-contact landmark on teeth 4–7 bilaterally measured in X/Y/Z per subject, averaged across the 8 posterior teeth before statistics; single calibrated examiner.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 25.0; Friedman test (omnibus), Wilcoxon signed-rank post-hoc with Bonferroni correction (28 pairwise comparisons, adjusted α < .0018); Spearman's rho; Cohen's d effect sizes.

## Results

| Direction | Significant among groups? | Pattern | Effect sizes |
|---|---|---|---|
| X (lateral) | No (p > .05) | Group F smallest, Group H largest | Negligible (d = −0.10 to 0.05) |
| Y (anteroposterior) | Yes | Group C smallest; differs significantly from Group G (largest, shifted posteriorly) | Mostly small (d < 0.3); G and H moderate (d = 0.49, 0.45) |
| Z (superoinferior) | Yes | Group C smallest; Group H largest | C moderate (d = 0.77), F medium (d = 0.57), G and H large (d > 1.1) |
| Number of occlusal contacts (CR & MIP) | No (Friedman, p > .05) | Not affected by amount, method, or facebow; no correlation with deviation magnitude in any direction | — |

- **Virtual facebow effect**: not statistically significant in any comparison; Group G's facebow-present Z-deviation (0.466 mm) was not meaningfully lower than its non-facebow 5 mm intraoral counterpart Group H (0.438 mm).
- **Method comparison at 5 mm**: articulator-based increase produced fewer/smaller deviations than intraoral increase, particularly in Y and Z.
- **Clinical thresholds cited**: AAO-defined acceptable deviation < 0.5 mm; other literature cites < 1 mm as non-critical — observed deviations mostly fell within these, but the consistent moderate-to-large Z-direction effect sizes at 5 mm were flagged by the authors as the clinically relevant exception requiring monitoring.
