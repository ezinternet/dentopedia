## Three-line Summary

Prospective controlled clinical trial (n=40, 4 groups of 10; non-randomized preference allocation) measuring DIAGNOdent values around clear-aligner composite attachments at baseline, 1, 3, and 6 months; groups received 1450 ppm fluoride toothpaste alone (control), nightly CPP-ACP, intensive nano-hydroxyapatite (nHAp), or quarterly 5% NaF varnish.

All three remineralization protocols improved enamel demineralization significantly vs control (which worsened +4.35 units); nHAp gave the greatest reduction (−5.36), numerically superior to fluoride varnish (−4.45) and CPP-ACP (−4.02); overall group effect F=438.4, partial η²=0.974.

Active remineralization from the start of aligner therapy is warranted at attachment sites; nHAp ranks first in this pilot but the non-randomized design, n=10/group, and more intensive nHAp protocol limit generalizability; passive fluoride toothpaste alone is insufficient.

## Summary
BMC Oral Health 2025 controlled clinical trial (Istanbul). Clear-aligner attachments create plaque-retentive enamel-composite margins where white spot lesions (WSLs) form; anterior attachments are an independent WSL risk factor (OR=2.192). Forty patients on active aligner therapy were allocated (by preference) to control (fluoride toothpaste) or one of three add-on remineralization protocols, and demineralization around attachments was tracked quantitatively with DIAGNOdent at baseline, 1, 3, and 6 months.

## Key Contributions
- **First quantitative clinical read** on demineralization specifically at clear-aligner attachment margins — an understudied, high-risk site.
- **Passive monitoring fails**: the control group WORSENED +4.35 DIAGNOdent units (+42%) over 6 months despite 1450 ppm fluoride toothpaste → argues for active prevention from treatment start.
- **Head-to-head hierarchy**: nano-hydroxyapatite (nHAp) gave the greatest reduction, significantly beating both CPP-ACP and quarterly fluoride varnish; aligners double as delivery trays, prolonging contact time for daily home agents.

## Methodology
Prospective controlled clinical trial (exploratory pilot; non-randomized allocation by patient preference), STROBE-adapted, NCT07229105. 4 groups (n=10 each), all on 1450 ppm F toothpaste base: A control; B CPP-ACP (Tooth Mousse in aligner nightly); C nHAp (professional 5% gel at T0/T1/T2 + daily home 1% gel — intensive dual protocol); D 5% NaF varnish at baseline + 3 mo. Outcome: DIAGNOdent (4 margins/attachment, cut-offs 0–12/13–24/≥25), intra-examiner ICC 0.85, analyzed with a linear mixed-effects model adjusted for baseline attachment number.

## Results
| Group | Δ DIAGNOdent (T0→6 mo) [95% CI] |
|---|---|
| Control (F toothpaste) | **+4.35** [3.77–4.93] (worsened) |
| CPP-ACP | −4.02 [−4.47, −3.56] |
| **nHAp** | **−5.36** [−5.99, −4.72] (best) |
| Fluoride varnish | −4.45 [−4.78, −4.11] |

- Group effect F=438.4, p<0.001, partial η²=0.974; baseline attachment number not significant (p=0.470).
- nHAp > CPP-ACP (MD −1.34, p<0.001) and > fluoride (MD −0.91, p=0.019); CPP-ACP ≈ fluoride (p=0.375).
- CPP-ACP and nHAp declined monotonically; fluoride varnish was biphasic (reservoir depletion, then recovery after 3-mo re-application) → quarterly application may be too infrequent for attachment sites. No adverse events; home compliance >95%.
- **Caveat**: non-randomized preference allocation, n=10/group pilot without a priori power (post-hoc power >0.99 from the large effect size), single center/examiner, and Group C's protocol was more intensive than the others.
