## Three-line Summary

Retrospective audit of 50,000 intraoral PSP (photostimulable phosphor) digital radiographs taken over 2 years at a Riyadh university clinic (April 2018–April 2020), classified by two OMF radiologists (kappa 0.99).

7.1% of images required retakes for artifacts; periapical images accounted for 80.8% of retakes; operator errors dominated (70.4%: cone cut 39.5%), followed by plate errors (bite marks 40.3%) and scanning errors (delayed scanning 39.9%).

Targeted training for cone-cut prevention, prompt scanning (<10 min), and plate-integrity protocols are the highest-yield interventions to reduce avoidable retakes and radiation dose.

## Summary

This large retrospective audit fills a receptor-specific gap: while film-radiograph artifacts are well documented, the artifacts characteristic of **PSP (photostimulable phosphor) digital plates** were not. Across 50,000 intraoral radiographs, **7.1% needed retakes for artifacts, and periapical (PA) images accounted for 80.8% of them** — so PA is where intraoral artifacts concentrate. The authors sort artifacts into three causes: **operator errors** (70.4% of retakes — dominated by **cone cut**, 39.5%, then improper placement and projection geometry), **plate errors** (bite marks 40.3%, image size reduction, scratches, contamination), and **scanning errors** (delayed scanning 39.9% from ambient-light latent-image fading, white lines from scanner-slit dust, black lines from electromagnetic interference). Operator errors dominating points to a **training** lever; plate/scanning errors point to **handling and scanner maintenance**. The clinical frame is dose: each artifact-driven retake is avoidable extra radiation. Paired with the interpretive-artifact SR (Dioguardi 2025), this covers the *technical/acquisition* half of PA artifacts.

## Key Contributions

- Provides **prevalence and a 3-category taxonomy** (operator / plate / scanning) for intraoral PSP artifacts — the technical, acquisition-side artifacts.
- Quantifies that **periapical images bear 80.8%** of artifact-driven retakes.
- Identifies the **top offenders** (cone cut, bite marks, delayed scanning) and their fixes.
- Near-perfect interobserver reliability (kappa 0.99) for the classification.

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective descriptive audit; 50,000 intraoral PA+bitewing radiographs (Apr 2018–Apr 2020), PNU Riyadh.
- **Equipment**: Gendex Expert DC (7 mA/65 kVp); Soredex DIGORA Optime PSP + laser scanner; MIPACS viewer.
- **Assessment**: retakes auto-logged; two OMF radiologists classified all retakes; 1,000 re-read at 2 weeks (kappa). SPSS v23 frequencies.

## Results

| Level | Finding |
|---|---|
| Retake rate | 3,550 / 50,000 = **7.1%** (kappa 0.99) |
| By image type | **PA 80.8%** (2,869), bitewing 14.6%, blank 4.6% |
| Category split | operator 70.4% · plate 19.3% · scanning 10.3% |
| Top operator error | **cone cut 39.5%** (then placement 30.4%, geometry 22.4%) |
| Top plate error | **bite marks 40.3%** (then size reduction 25.4%, scratches 19.6%) |
| Top scanning error | **delayed scanning 39.9%** (then white lines 37.2%) |

Most common artifacts overall: **bite marks, image size reduction, scratches, delayed scanning**. Prevention: technician training (operator errors), gentle plate handling and integrity checks (plate errors), prompt scanning (<10 min ambient light) and scanner maintenance/shielding (scanning errors).
