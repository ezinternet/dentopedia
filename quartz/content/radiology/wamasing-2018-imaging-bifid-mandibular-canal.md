## Three-line Summary

Retrospective imaging study (n=154 patients / 249 evaluable hemi-mandibles) using high-resolution 3D-VIBE MRI through the neurovascular bundle (NVB) to classify bifid mandibular canal (BMC) subtypes and, secondarily, to assess nutrient canal (NC) incidence.

BMC was found in 6.4% of hemi-mandibles (Type 1 4.4%, Type 2 2.0%); NC (including the retromolar canal) had a much higher MRI-detected incidence of 58.4–97.6%, and BMCs were essentially invisible on panoramic radiography (0/11+5 cases) or CT (only 1 case detected).

Clinically, MRI substantially outperforms panoramic radiography/CT for BMC and NC detection relevant to mandibular anesthesia/surgery risk, but the retrospective single-center, lesion-referred (not healthy-population) sample limits generalizability of both incidence figures.

## Summary

This retrospective study used high-resolution MRI (3D-VIBE sequence) to directly visualize the inferior alveolar neurovascular bundle (NVB) and characterize the bifid mandibular canal (BMC) — an anatomic variant where the mandibular canal splits into two or more branches, with implications for failed nerve block and neurovascular injury risk during implant/surgical procedures. From 154 patients' archived MRI datasets (mandible-lesion referrals, 2015–2016), 249 hemi-mandibles were evaluable after excluding 59 for NVB morphology/artifact issues. BMC was classified into three subtypes and found in 6.4% of hemi-mandibles overall. As a secondary outcome, the same MRI dataset was scored for nutrient canal (NC) presence — a separate, smaller-caliber accessory canal system including the retromolar canal — which showed a much higher incidence (58.4–97.6%). The study also cross-checked whether panoramic radiography or CT could detect the MRI-confirmed BMC cases, finding they largely could not (0 detected by panoramic radiography, 1 of the MRI-positive set detected by CT), supporting MRI as the more sensitive modality for both variants.

## Key Contributions

- High-resolution 3D-VIBE MRI incidence figure for BMC (6.4%) with a 3-tier subtype classification (Type 0/1/2) based on direct NVB visualization rather than inferred bony-canal morphology.
- Direct cross-modality comparison on the same patients: MRI-confirmed BMCs were essentially undetectable by panoramic radiography or CT, arguing for MRI's superior sensitivity for this variant.
- Secondary NC incidence figure via MRI (58.4–97.6%), a data point using a fundamentally different imaging modality than the periapical-radiograph/CBCT methods more commonly used in the NC literature.

## Methodology

Retrospective analysis of 154 archived patient MRI datasets (3D-VIBE sequence, obtained for mandible lesion work-up, Jan 2015–Apr 2016) at a single institution. Right and left mandibles were scored independently, yielding 308 candidate hemi-mandibles; 59 were excluded for NVB morphology issues or imaging artifacts, leaving 249 evaluable hemi-mandibles. Each was classified as Type 0 (no BMC), Type 1 (one NVB splitting into two en route), or Type 2 (two fully separate NVBs). NC presence (including the retromolar canal) was scored on the same MRI data. For MRI-identified BMC cases, corresponding panoramic radiographs and/or CT scans (where available for that patient) were reviewed to determine whether the bifidity could also be identified by those conventional modalities.

## Results

- BMC overall: 6.4% of 249 hemi-mandibles (Types 1+2).
- Subtypes: Type 0 (no BMC) 233/249 (93.6%); Type 1 11/249 (4.4%); Type 2 5/249 (2.0%).
- NC (incl. retromolar canal), MRI-detected incidence: 58.4–97.6%.
- Cross-modality detection of MRI-confirmed BMC: 0 cases detected on panoramic radiography; 1 case detected on CT.
- Authors' conclusion: BMC incidence per MRI was 6.4%; because BMCs were not reliably verified by panoramic radiography or CT, MRI should be used to assess BMC.

**Limitations**: retrospective, single-center, lesion-referred (not screening/healthy-population) sample; ~19% of candidate hemi-mandibles excluded for morphology/artifact reasons; wide NC incidence range (58.4–97.6%) suggests non-standardized scoring rather than a tight point estimate; abstract-only source (PMC full-text body was empty for this article) limited verification of exact NC subgroup methodology.
