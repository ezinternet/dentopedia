## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Int J Mol Sci, 2023) synthesizing the oral microbiome's role in periodontal disease (PD) pathogenesis and its downstream links to systemic disease.

Frames PD as network-level dysbiosis (not single-pathogen) of a >700-species oral biofilm, and traces mechanistic links from oral dysbiosis to COPD/cystic fibrosis, COVID-19 severity, Alzheimer's disease (P. gingivalis/gingipains-tau, BBB permeability via SCFAs/LPS/amyloid), cardiovascular disease (TMAO, LPS-TLR4, foam cell formation), diabetes (SCFA-mediated insulin resistance), rheumatoid arthritis (P. gingivalis/A. actinomycetemcomitans-driven anti-CCP/citrullination), and pancreatic/colorectal cancer.

Narrative-review level evidence (no systematic search/quality appraisal) — useful as an orientation map of oral-systemic mechanisms and key organisms, but individual disease-association claims should be verified against the primary/SR literature cited within.

## 세줄요약

국제분자과학저널(Int J Mol Sci, 2023) 게재 서술적 리뷰로, 구강미생물총(oral microbiome)이 치주질환(PD) 발병기전에 미치는 역할과 전신질환과의 연관성을 종합했다.

PD를 단일 병원균이 아닌 700종 이상 구강 바이오필름의 네트워크 수준 미생물 불균형(dysbiosis)으로 규정하며, 만성폐쇄성폐질환(COPD)·낭포성섬유증, COVID-19 중증도, 알츠하이머병(P. gingivalis/gingipain-tau 경로, BBB 투과성), 심혈관질환(TMAO, LPS-TLR4 경로), 당뇨병(단쇄지방산 매개 인슐린저항성), 류마티스관절염(P. gingivalis/A. actinomycetemcomitans의 항-CCP항체·시트룰린화 유발), 췌장암·대장암까지 구강-전신 연결 기전을 정리했다.

서술적 리뷰 수준 근거(체계적 검색·질 평가 없음)로, 구강-전신 기전의 오리엔테이션 지도로는 유용하나 개별 질환-연관성 주장은 원문 인용 문헌으로 재확인이 필요하다.

## Summary

This narrative review (Kozak & Pawlik, Int J Mol Sci 2023) positions periodontal disease (PD) as the product of oral-microbiome dysbiosis rather than infection by any single pathogen, then extends that framework outward to systemic disease. Periodontitis affects 10-50% of the global population; genetic contribution to disease inheritance was estimated at OR 0.38 (twin studies) and OR 0.15 (family studies) in cited work. The oral cavity hosts >700 species (bacteria, fungi including Candida, viruses, archaea, protozoa), of which only ~57% of bacterial species are officially named. The review traces the shift from the historical "red complex" (P. gingivalis, T. forsythia, T. denticola) model toward a polymicrobial-synergy-and-dysbiosis (PSD) framework, then surveys probiotic/synbiotic evidence (Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium, S. salivarius) for periodontal disease, caries, Candida infection, and halitosis (present in ~90% of PD cases). The second half of the review maps oral-microbiome links to six systemic domains: respiratory disease (COPD via chronic airway inflammation; cystic fibrosis via Pseudomonas-Streptococcus interactions), COVID-19 (disease-associated species linked to symptom duration/severity), Alzheimer's disease (P. gingivalis gingipain-tau interaction, BBB permeability via SCFA/LPS/bacterial amyloid), cardiovascular disease (TMAO-driven atherogenesis, LPS-TLR4 inflammatory signaling), diabetes (SCFA-mediated beta-cell/insulin pathway, bidirectional periodontitis-diabetes exacerbation), rheumatoid arthritis (P. gingivalis and A. actinomycetemcomitans driving anti-CCP antibody formation and protein citrullination), and cancer (pancreatic and colorectal, via salivary microbiome shifts and gut-barrier dysfunction).

## Key Contributions

- Reinforces the network/dysbiosis model of periodontal pathogenesis (vs. single-pathogen "red complex" model), consistent with the PSD literature already in the wiki.
- Provides a compact cross-disease map connecting oral dysbiosis to six systemic disease domains in one place — useful as a quick-reference orientation before deep-diving into any single disease-association paper.
- Surveys probiotic/synbiotic evidence across four oral conditions (periodontal disease, caries, candidiasis, halitosis) in a single section.
- Names specific mechanistic actors per systemic disease (TMAO for CVD, SCFA/gingipain-tau for AD, anti-CCP/citrullination for RA) that can serve as search terms for deeper literature retrieval.

## Methodology

Narrative literature review; no PRISMA flow diagram, explicit search strategy, or study-quality/risk-of-bias appraisal reported in the retrieved full text. Five sections: Introduction (PD epidemiology/risk factors) -> Microbiome in oral health -> Oral microbiome in periodontal disease -> Oral microbiome in systemic diseases -> Conclusions. Confidence label: `narrative-review` (no meta-analytic pooling; treat individual disease-association claims as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmed, and verify against primary/SR sources before clinical use).

## Results

- Periodontitis prevalence 10-50% globally (severity-dependent); heritability OR 0.38 (95% CI 0.34-0.43, twin studies) / OR 0.15 (95% CI 0.06-0.24, family studies), per Nibali et al. as cited.
- ~90% of PD cases co-present with halitosis, driven by the same sulfur-compound-producing anaerobic species implicated in periodontal pathogenesis.
- Alzheimer's disease: P. gingivalis detected in AD-patient brain tissue; its gingipain proteases inhibit tau function (a hallmark AD mechanism), and mouse infection studies showed brain colonization with amyloid-plaque component growth (Dominy et al., as cited) — proposed as a rationale for gingipain inhibitors as an AD treatment avenue.
- Cardiovascular disease: microbiome-derived TMAO increases foam-cell formation and VCAM-1 expression (monocyte-endothelium adhesion); bacterial LPS binds TLR4 to drive TNF/IL-6 secretion, contributing to atherosclerotic plaque formation/instability.
- Diabetes: SCFAs modulate pancreatic beta-cell function/insulin production; periodontitis and diabetes were described as bidirectionally exacerbating, with anti-IL-17 antibody treatment proposed to alleviate the diabetic-microbiome pathogenicity axis (Xiao et al., as cited).
- Rheumatoid arthritis: P. gingivalis induces anti-CCP antibodies and Th17 cell formation; A. actinomycetemcomitans leukotoxin-A drives neutrophil hypercitrullination (Konig et al., as cited) — both organisms proposed as autoimmunity contributors in RA.
- Cancer: salivary microbiome shifts (altered genus abundance) reported in pancreatic cancer patients vs. healthy controls (Farrell et al., as cited); oral-microbiome-driven gut-barrier dysfunction proposed as a colorectal-cancer mechanism.
