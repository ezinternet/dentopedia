Treatment of Small Peri-Implant Buccal Dehiscence Defects With SCTG or GBR: 3-Year Results of a Pilot Study

Authors: Anina N. Zuercher, Fabienne Rusch, Franz J. Strauss, Ronald E. Jung, Hyun-Chang Lim, Daniel Thoma
Journal: Clinical Implant Dentistry and Related Research, 2026; 28(6):e70192
DOI: 10.1111/cid.70192
PMID: 42958619
PMCID: PMC13720216
Source: PMC full text

[Full text retrieved via PubMed MCP from PMC13720216]

ABSTRACT
Objective: To evaluate and compare subepithelial connective tissue grafts (SCTG) and guided bone regeneration (GBR) for the treatment of small peri-implant buccal bone dehiscences over a 3-year follow-up, in terms of profilometric contour change, peri-implant parameters and patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs).

Material and Methods: Patients presenting with small buccal bone dehiscences (≤ 3 mm) after single implant placement were included. Defect sites were randomly treated with either SCTG or GBR, while five patients without dehiscences served as a negative control group. All patients received an implant crown and were followed for 3 years. Profilometric measurements, marginal bone levels, peri-implant parameters, and PROMs were recorded at baseline, 1, and 3 years. Data were analyzed descriptively.

Results: A total of 16 patients were available for the 3-year follow-up (SCTG, n = 5; GBR, n = 6; negative control, n = 5). Both augmentation approaches maintained stable buccal soft-tissue contours. At 1 mm below the mucosal margin, the median buccal contour change was 0.19 mm (Q1: -0.28; Q3: 0.29) in the SCTG group and 0.11 mm (Q1: -0.20; Q3: 0.30) in the GBR group. At 3 mm below the mucosal margin, the corresponding median contour changes were 0.05 mm (Q1: -0.36; Q3: 0.31) for SCTG and -0.05 mm (Q1: -0.22; Q3: 0.07) for GBR. Peri-implant clinical parameters (probing depth, bleeding on probing, and plaque) remained stable, indicating maintained peri-implant health in both groups. Marginal bone levels were preserved throughout the observation period and oral health-related quality of life remained high and comparable between the SCTG and GBR groups.

Conclusion: Within the limitations of this pilot study, both SCTG and GBR were associated with stable buccal contour conditions and favorable peri-implant health over a 3-year follow-up period. These findings were observed under adequate self-performed plaque control and adherence to supportive care. Adequately powered RCTs are needed to confirm these findings.

1 Introduction
The long-term success of dental implants depends not only on predictable osseointegration but also on the maintenance of healthy and stable peri-implant tissues. Once integrated, the peri-implant mucosa forms a protective soft-tissue barrier that shields the underlying bone from microbial and mechanical challenges. The integrity of this mucosal barrier is therefore essential for long-term implant success and aesthetic stability.

In many clinical situations, particularly after tooth extraction, small buccal bone dehiscences (≤ 3 mm) are present at implant placement. These defects represent a frequent and clinically relevant challenge, as they may compromise buccal contour stability and soft-tissue support over time. A reduced buccal bone dimension is often associated with a thinner peri-implant mucosa, which may predispose the site to contour flattening, mucosal recession and increased susceptibility to marginal bone remodeling.

Among the available augmentation techniques, guided bone regeneration (GBR) remains the gold standard for managing peri-implant dehiscence-type defects. However, subepithelial connective-tissue grafts (SCTG) have increasingly been applied as a biologically driven alternative, particularly in cases with small buccal dehiscences where soft-tissue support is critical.

2 Materials and Methods
2.1 Study Design: 3-year follow-up of a previously published pilot RCT comparing SCTG and GBR for small buccal peri-implant dehiscence defects. Performed at Clinic of Reconstructive Dentistry, University of Zurich. Registered: DRKS00012733. Ethics: BASEC-ID 2017-01401. CONSORT guidelines followed.

2.2 Study Population: Patients ≥18 years, single-tooth implant placement in posterior maxilla or mandible, good general and periodontal health. Full-mouth plaque and bleeding scores <25%, probing depths <4 mm.

2.3 Clinical Procedures: Titanium implants (OsseoSpeed EV, Dentsply Sirona; diameter 3.6 mm premolar, 4.2 mm molar sites). Buccal bone dehiscence measured intraoperatively. Defects ≤3 mm randomized to SCTG or GBR. Healing period 3-6 months before prosthetic phase. Monolithic multilayered zirconia crowns, screw-retained.

2.4 Outcome Measures:
Primary: Buccal contour changes via STL superimposition (SMOP software) at 1 mm and 3 mm below mucosal margin.
Secondary: Peri-implant parameters (PD, BOP, PCR, KM width), BBT via CBCT, MBL via standardized periapical radiographs, OHIP-14 PROMs.

3 Results
3.1 Study Population: 16 patients at 3 years (SCTG n=5, GBR n=6, control n=5). One SCTG patient excluded (peri-implantitis treatment). One control lost to follow-up.

Baseline: Age mean SCTG 58.4 (SD 5.5), GBR 58.7 (SD 14.3), control 56.6 (SD 19.9). Defect depth: SCTG median 3.0 mm (IQR 1.8-3.0), GBR 2.8 mm (IQR 1.5-3.0).

3.2 Buccal Contour Changes (1 year to 3 year delta):
ROI-1 (1 mm below margin): SCTG median 0.19 mm (Q1: -0.28; Q3: 0.29), GBR 0.11 mm (Q1: -0.20; Q3: 0.30), control 0.27 mm (Q1: -0.03; Q3: 0.35)
ROI-3 (3 mm below margin): SCTG median 0.05 mm (Q1: -0.36; Q3: 0.31), GBR -0.05 mm (Q1: -0.22; Q3: 0.07), control 0.11 mm (Q1: -0.11; Q3: 0.18)

Tissue width change (TW, 1 year to 3 year):
TW-1: SCTG -0.32 mm (Q1: -0.62; Q3: 0.33), GBR -0.17 mm (Q1: -0.53; Q3: 0.15), control 0.10 mm
TW-3: SCTG -0.14 mm (Q1: -0.86; Q3: 0.19), GBR -0.03 mm (Q1: -0.37; Q3: 0.09), control -0.18 mm

3.3 Peri-implant Parameters at 3 years:
PD mean: SCTG 3.4 mm, GBR 3.1 mm, control 3.1 mm
BOP: SCTG 0.3 (SD 0.2), GBR 0.3 (SD 0.2), control 0.1 (SD 0.2)
PCR: SCTG 0.3, GBR 0.2, control 0.1
KM width mean: SCTG 3.0 mm, GBR 2.1 mm, control 3.0 mm

3.4 Buccal Bone Thickness at 3 years:
BBT-0 (implant shoulder): SCTG median 0.05 mm (Q1: 0.00; Q3: 2.06), GBR 0.55 mm (Q1: 0.00; Q3: 2.73), control 1.34 mm (Q1: 0.35; Q3: 2.32). ICC 0.96 (95% CI: 0.79-0.99).
BBT-1: SCTG 1.53 mm, GBR 0.82 mm, control 1.74 mm (medians)
BBT-3: SCTG 2.26 mm, GBR 0.98 mm, control 2.07 mm (medians)

3.5 Marginal Bone Loss at 3 years:
MBL median: SCTG 0.0 mm (Q1: -0.1; Q3: 0.2), GBR 0.3 mm (Q1: 0.0; Q3: 2.1), control 0.2 mm (Q1: -0.1; Q3: 0.9). ICC 0.99 (95% CI: 0.96-0.99).

3.6 PROMs:
OHIP-14 median: SCTG 0.0 (Q1: 0.0; Q3: 1.0), GBR 0.0 (Q1: 0.0; Q3: 3.0), control 3.0 (Q1: 0.0; Q3: 8.0)

4 Discussion
Key points: (1) Both SCTG and GBR maintained stable buccal contour over 3 years; most remodeling occurs in the first year. (2) Peri-implant clinical parameters remained stable in both groups. (3) Marginal bone levels and BBT preserved. (4) SCTG showed greater KM width (3.0 vs 2.1 mm). (5) EFP S3 Guideline: no current evidence that soft tissue augmentation alone reduces peri-implant disease incidence in thin tissue sites (<2 mm). (6) Adequately powered RCTs required.

Limitations: Small sample (pilot), descriptive analysis only, CBCT limitations for thin bone, carefully selected patients, non-randomized control, 3 years still mid-term.

5 Conclusions
Both SCTG and GBR associated with stable buccal contour and favorable peri-implant health at 3 years under adequate plaque control and maintenance care.
