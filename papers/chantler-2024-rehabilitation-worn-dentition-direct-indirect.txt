PMID: 39670301 | DOI: 10.1111/jerd.13384 | Source: PubMed (abstract only; no PMCID)
Chantler JGM, Pirc M, Strauss FJ, Rohr N, Thoma DS, Ioannidis A. Rehabilitation of the Worn Dentition With Direct and Indirect Minimally Invasive Concepts - A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. J Esthet Restor Dent 2024 (epub 2024-12-13); 37(3):690-701.
Article types: Journal Article, Meta-Analysis, Systematic Review. Keywords: dental erosion, direct restoration, indirect restoration, tooth wear, worn dentition.

ABSTRACT
Objective: To report and compare the survival of minimally invasive direct and indirect restorations of different materials for restoring the worn dentition.
Methods: Systematic search in six databases: MEDLINE, Web of Science, Scopus, CENTRAL, VHL, EMBASE. PICO: "In dentate patients with a worn dentition (P), does rehabilitating their lost tooth structure with indirect restorations (I) or direct (C) effect the survival and success of treatment (O)?" Study characteristics and restoration survival rates extracted. No language restrictions. Survival and prevalence estimates calculated with random-effect models.
Results: Search yielded 5009 entries; 14 publications selected for full-text analysis: 2 RCTs, 7 prospective, 5 retrospective studies, varying methodological quality. 52% direct and 48% indirect restorations, varying materials and follow-up. Survival of direct composite resin vs indirect manually processed composite resin: no significant difference (n = 2; OR 1.79 [95% CI 0.64-5.05]; p = 0.270). Prospective articles, pooled prevalence of surviving restorations: direct (n = 3) 99% [95% CI 0.97-1.00]; indirect (n = 5) 100% [95% CI 0.99-1.00]. Lithium disilicate recorded the least failures at 1.8%.
Conclusions: Although survival estimates of indirect manually processed and direct composite resins were not significantly different, indirect restorations - especially lithium disilicate ceramic - may offer better survival than direct resin restorations in worn dentition. Despite limited evidence, indirect ceramic restorations appear well-suited for long-term treatment of worn dentition, whilst direct composite resins are more suited as a medium-term solution.
