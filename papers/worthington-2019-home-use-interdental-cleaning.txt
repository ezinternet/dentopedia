=== ABSTRACT ===

Background
Dental caries (tooth decay) and periodontal diseases (gingivitis and periodontitis) affect the
majority of people worldwide, and treatment costs place a significant burden on health services.
Decay and gum disease can cause pain, eating and speaking difficulties, low self-esteem, and even
tooth loss and the need for surgery. As dental plaque is the primary cause, self-administered
daily mechanical disruption and removal of plaque is important for oral health. Toothbrushing can
remove supragingival plaque on the facial and lingual/palatal surfaces, but special devices (such
as floss, brushes, sticks, and irrigators) are often recommended to reach into the interdental area.

Objectives
To evaluate the effectiveness of interdental cleaning devices used at home, in addition to
toothbrushing, compared with toothbrushing alone, for preventing and controlling periodontal
diseases, caries, and plaque. A secondary objective was to compare different interdental cleaning
devices with each other.

Search methods
Cochrane Oral Health's Information Specialist searched: Cochrane Oral Health's Trials Register (to
16 January 2019), CENTRAL (the Cochrane Library, 2018, Issue 12), MEDLINE Ovid (1946 to 16 January
2019), Embase Ovid (1980 to 16 January 2019) and CINAHL EBSCO (1937 to 16 January 2019).
ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO ICTRP were searched for ongoing trials. No restrictions were placed on
language or date of publication.

Selection criteria
Randomised controlled trials (RCTs) that compared toothbrushing and a home-use interdental
cleaning device versus toothbrushing alone or with another device (minimum duration four weeks).

Data collection and analysis
At least two review authors independently screened searches, selected studies, extracted data,
assessed studies' risk of bias, and assessed evidence certainty as high, moderate, low or very low,
according to GRADE. Indices measured on interproximal surfaces were extracted where possible.
Random-effects meta-analyses used mean differences (MDs) or standardised mean differences (SMDs).

Main results
35 RCTs (3929 randomised adult participants) were included. Studies were at high risk of
performance bias (blinding of participants not possible). Only two studies were otherwise at low
risk of bias. Many participants had a low level of baseline gingival inflammation.

Devices evaluated plus toothbrushing versus toothbrushing alone: floss (15 trials), interdental
brushes (2 trials), wooden cleaning sticks (2 trials), rubber/elastomeric cleaning sticks (2
trials), oral irrigators (5 trials). Devices compared with floss: interdental brushes (9 trials),
wooden cleaning sticks (3 trials), rubber/elastomeric cleaning sticks (9 trials), oral irrigators
(2 trials). Rubber/elastomeric cleaning sticks were also compared with interdental brushes (3
trials).

No trials assessed interproximal caries, and most did not assess periodontitis. Gingivitis was
measured by indices (most commonly Löe-Silness, 0-3 scale) and by proportion of bleeding sites.
Plaque was measured by indices, most often Quigley-Hein (0-5).

Primary objective — comparisons against toothbrushing alone:
- Low-certainty evidence: flossing + toothbrushing may reduce gingivitis (GI) at 1 month (SMD
  -0.58, 95% CI -1.12 to -0.04; 8 trials, 585 participants), 3 or 6 months. Bleeding sites and
  plaque results were inconsistent (very low-certainty).
- Very low-certainty evidence: interdental brush + toothbrushing may reduce gingivitis (GI) at 1
  month (MD -0.53, 95% CI -0.83 to -0.23; 1 trial, 62 participants); no clear difference in
  bleeding sites (MD -0.05, 95% CI -0.13 to 0.03; 1 trial, 31 participants). Low-certainty evidence:
  interdental brushes may reduce plaque more than toothbrushing alone (SMD -1.07, 95% CI -1.51 to
  -0.63; 2 trials, 93 participants).
- Very low-certainty evidence: wooden cleaning sticks + toothbrushing may reduce bleeding sites at 3
  months (MD -0.25, 95% CI -0.37 to -0.13; 1 trial, 24 participants), but not plaque (MD -0.03, 95%
  CI -0.13 to 0.07).
- Very low-certainty evidence: rubber/elastomeric sticks + toothbrushing may reduce plaque at 1
  month (MD -0.22, 95% CI -0.41 to -0.03), not gingivitis (GI MD -0.01, 95% CI -0.19 to 0.21; 1
  trial, 12 participants; bleeding MD 0.07, 95% CI -0.15 to 0.01; 1 trial, 30 participants).
- Very low-certainty evidence: oral irrigators may reduce gingivitis (GI) at 1 month (SMD -0.48,
  95% CI -0.89 to -0.06; 4 trials, 380 participants), not at 3 or 6 months. Low-certainty evidence:
  oral irrigators did not reduce bleeding sites at 1 month (MD -0.00, 95% CI -0.07 to 0.06; 2
  trials, 126 participants) or 3 months, or plaque at 1, 3 or 6 months (SMD -0.16, 95% CI -0.41 to
  0.10; 3 trials, 235 participants at 1 month).

Secondary objective — comparisons between devices:
- Low-certainty evidence: interdental brushes may reduce gingivitis more than floss at 1 and 3
  months, no difference for periodontitis (probing pocket depth). Plaque evidence inconsistent.
- Low- to very low-certainty evidence: oral irrigation may reduce gingivitis at 1 month compared to
  flossing; very low-certainty — no difference between devices for plaque.
- Very low-certainty evidence: interdental brushes or flossing vs interdental cleaning sticks did
  not demonstrate superiority of either.

Adverse events
No severe events caused by devices; no evidence of differences between groups in minor effects
such as gingival irritation.

Authors' conclusions
Using floss or interdental brushes in addition to toothbrushing may reduce gingivitis or plaque, or
both, more than toothbrushing alone. Interdental brushes may be more effective than floss.
Available evidence for tooth cleaning sticks and oral irrigators is limited and inconsistent.
Outcomes were mostly measured in the short term and participants in most studies had a low level of
baseline gingival inflammation. Overall, the evidence was low to very low-certainty, and the effect
sizes observed may not be clinically important. Future trials should report participant periodontal
status according to the new periodontal diseases classification, and last long enough to measure
interproximal caries and periodontitis.
