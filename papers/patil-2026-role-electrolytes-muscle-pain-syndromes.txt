Source: PubMed PMID 41812583 / PMC12994056 / DOI 10.1016/j.identj.2026.109488 (condensed working copy)
Title: The Role of Electrolytes in Muscle Pain Syndromes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis With Implications for Temporomandibular Disorder
Authors: Patil S, Falkowski A, Venkataiah VS, Bhandi S, Licari FW, Patil AG. Int Dent J 2026;76(3):109488 (published 2026-03-10)

ABSTRACT: TMD = major cause of chronic orofacial pain; masticatory myalgia central. PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD420251120631. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, CENTRAL Jan 1995-Aug 2025. RCT/quasi-RCT of electrolyte supplementation (Mg, Na, Ca, K) for cramps or myalgia; RoB 2; random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird). 13 trials (Mg 10 RCTs). Pregnancy cramps (4 trials, N~364): Mg reduced cramp frequency vs placebo, pooled RR 1.35 (95% CI 1.05-1.74, P=.02). Nocturnal/persistent leg cramps in adults (4 trials, N~396): MD -0.42 cramps/week (95% CI -1.15 to 0.31, P=.26), no effect. IV Mg no benefit in older adults; perioperative trial reduced fasciculations and postoperative myalgia. Na-based solutions reduced cramp susceptibility in exercise and cirrhosis; Ca and K lacked supportive evidence. RoB low to moderate. Mg most plausible candidate for translation to TMD myalgia.

METHODS: Eligible: randomized/quasi-randomized human trials of oral/IV/topical electrolytes, effect isolable from other agents, >=1 outcome (cramp frequency, pain VAS/NRS, duration, responders). Excluded: dialysis renal disease, arrhythmia, preeclampsia/eclampsia, bowel-prep. English, human. Last search 2025-08-15. Two reviewers screened/extracted; third cross-checked. Meta-analysis when >=2 comparable studies; I2, Cochran Q; sensitivity excluding high-RoB; funnel/Egger if >=10. TMD extrapolation prespecified as hypothesis-generating.

RESULTS: 75 records after removing fibromyalgia articles; 34 full texts; 13 RCTs and mechanistic experimental studies included; n 9-184. 10 Mg trials, 3 multi-electrolyte/sodium beverage (ORS, electrolyte drink, pickle juice). No Ca or K monotherapy trial. Pregnancy (4 RCTs): Dahle (oral Mg reduced cramp distress), Nygaard (Mg citrate reduced frequency+intensity), Supakatisant (Mg bisglycinate reduced both), Araujo (Mg citrate = placebo). Nocturnal/chronic adults (4): Frusso and Roffe (Mg citrate not superior; Roffe non-significant trend), Roguin Maor (Mg oxide, older adults, no benefit), Barna (Mg oxide monohydrate reduced frequency and duration, improved sleep, 60 days). Garrison: IV MgSO4 in older adults with rest cramps, no effect. Ojo: high-dose IV MgSO4 pretreatment reduced suxamethonium fasciculations and postoperative myalgia. Earp: electrolyte drink (Na,K,Mg) raised stimulation threshold frequency, less cramp pain in healthy young adults. Lau: ORS during heat exercise reduced electrically induced cramp susceptibility vs spring water. Tapper: pickle juice reduced cramp severity in cirrhosis; no change in sleep or HRQoL.
Risk of bias: pregnancy trials mostly low; Frusso and Roffe some concerns; Barna and Supakatisant low; small crossover studies some concerns; overall moderate quality.
Meta-analysis: pregnancy RR ~1.35 (1.05-1.74, P=.02), ~364 women; nocturnal/chronic MD -0.42 episodes/week (-1.15 to 0.31, P=.26), nearly 400 participants. Garrison, Ojo and the 3 multi-electrolyte studies not pooled (heterogeneity).
TMD extrapolation: no RCT of electrolytes in TMD myofascial pain. Null adult-cramp pooled effect is a negative translational signal for systemic Mg in TMD myalgia (adults closer to TMD patients than pregnant women). A recent RCT (cited, not part of the review) of local MgSO4 injection into masseter reduced pain and improved function in TMD myalgia = proof of concept. Authors favor targeted local administration over systemic supplementation. Na/Ca/K low translational relevance.
LIMITATIONS: heterogeneity (population, Mg salt, duration), small samples, older trials with incomplete reporting, inferential extrapolation, sparse Na/Ca/K evidence.
Future: well-powered placebo-controlled Mg trials in TMD myofascial pain (systemic and local); outcomes pain, function, QoL.
