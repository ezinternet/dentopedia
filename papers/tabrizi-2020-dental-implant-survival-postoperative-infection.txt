Dental Implant Survival after Postoperative Infection
Tabrizi R, Zarchini R, Ozkan BT, Majdi S
Journal of Maxillofacial and Oral Surgery 2020;21(3):796-801
DOI: 10.1007/s12663-020-01460-6
PMCID: PMC9474760
PMID: 36274880

[ABSTRACT ONLY — full text not retrieved via PMC]

PURPOSE
Early postoperative infection can lead to dental implant failure. This study aimed to evaluate the frequency of failed and survived implants after acute postoperative infection and the related factors.

MATERIALS AND METHODS
Cross-sectional cohort study evaluating early infection after dental implant surgery.
Study variables: site of implant placement, age and gender, bone augmentation, postoperative antibiotic therapy, smoking, time of infection occurrence/diagnosis.
Outcome: failed vs survived implants (2 groups).
n = 980 patients total.

RESULTS
- 34 (3.46%) out of 980 patients developed postoperative infection following implant placement
- 25 males, 9 females
- 10 implants (29.4%) survived (group 1)
- 24 implants (70.6%) failed (group 2)

Significant differences between failed vs survived groups (p < 0.05) regarding:
- Number of smoker patients
- Fresh socket vs delayed implant placement
- Patients who received bone graft
- Mean time of infection diagnosis

Kaplan–Meier analysis:
- Without postoperative antibiotics: risk of failure increased by 1.1× (HR) when infection occurred at 4 days after surgery
- With postoperative antibiotics (smokers): risk of failure increased when infection occurred after 6 days
- With postoperative antibiotics (non-smokers): risk of failure increased when infection occurred after 9 days

CONCLUSION
Smoking, early infection, fresh socket placement, and placement of implants along with bone substitutes may increase the failure rate after acute infection in dental implant placement.
