Source: PubMed MCP (PMID 42458694, PMC13583377), DOI 10.1111/joor.70271. Condensed working copy.
Ferrillo M, Calafiore D, De Rosa A, Sedran A, Longo UG, Vecchio M, Ammendolia A, Scotti N, Giudice A, de Sire A. Efficacy of Myofascial Release Therapy and Related Soft-Tissue Techniques for Pain in Temporomandibular Disorders: A Systematic Review of Randomized Controlled Trials. J Oral Rehabil 2026;53(10):1972-1984.

ABSTRACT: SR of RCTs; PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science from inception to 31 Jan 2026; 857 articles -> 7 included. RDC/TMD or DC/TMD diagnosis. Rehabilitation period 10 days to 5 weeks; frequency 1-3 sessions/week; medium/long-term follow-up in 4 studies. RoB 2: 0 low, 6 some concerns, 1 high. GRADE: low certainty for pain reduction and mouth function; very low for QoL. Conclusion: MFR seemed to reduce pain and improve mouth function in myogenous TMD; clinically meaningful efficacy (surpassing 15-22 mm MCID) supported only by a subset of trials; MFR may be part of multidisciplinary approach.

METHODS: PROSPERO CRD420251161805 (registered 5 Oct 2025; not fully prospective - screening had begun). PRISMA 2020. Protocol amendments (population restricted to myogenous TMD, RCT only, intervention subgroups: direct MFR, instrument-assisted MFR, soft-tissue mobilization, multimodal incl. MFR; RoB 2). English only; exclusions: growing children, TMJ trauma, rheumatic/inflammatory disease, congenital/neoplastic, cross-over, animal. A priori MCID for pain (VAS/NRS): 15-22 mm on 100 mm (1.5-2.2 on 10-pt). Narrative synthesis; no meta-analysis (heterogeneous comparators, techniques, frequencies, follow-up). Kappa (RoB) 0.84. GRADE used.

RESULTS: 857 records, 421 screened after dedup, 58 full-text, 51 excluded, 7 included (2018-2025). n per study 34-72; total 359 (71 M, 288 F); mean ages 23-46 y. RDC/TMD (3 studies) or DC/TMD (4). Sessions 10-45 min.

TABLE 2 (one line per trial):
Brochado 2018 Braz Oral Res: n=51 (MT 16, PBM 18, MT+PBM 17); RDC/TMD; MT 21 min, 3x/week x4 wk; PBM 808 nm GaAlAs laser 12 sessions; VAS at 7,14,21,28,60,90 d. All groups reduced pain by D14 (MT 4.4->1.7; PBM 4.1->1.9; MT+PBM 5.2->2.4; all p<0.001); no between-group difference; stable to D90 (MT 0.9, PBM 1.6, MT+PBM 1.9). Mixed myogenous/arthrogenic population.
Leite 2020 J Manipulative Physiol Ther: n=34 women; RDC/TMD; diacutaneous fibrolysis (DF) 2x/week x4 wk (8 sessions, ~10 min) vs sham; VAS at 4 wk: DF 3.9 [IQR 0.7-8.2] vs 0.6 [0-2.3], p=0.0001 (as printed); between-group difference at T1 p=0.02 (text)/p<0.01 (table); mouth opening time-by-group p=0.017.
Serrano-Hernanz 2023 J Oral Rehabil: n=72 chronic myofascial TMD; DC/TMD; pressure release technique (trigger points, masticatory+cervical) 45 min 1x/week x5 wk vs sham; follow-up 3 months. Experimental VAS 7.2+-0.9 -> 4.9+-1.2 (T1) -> 4.4+-1.8 (T2), p<0.001; between-group p<0.001 at T1 and T2; sham reduction below the a priori 1.2 cm clinically significant decrease.
Romeo 2024 J Oral Rehabil: n=62 myogenic TMD; DC/TMD; musculoskeletal physiotherapy (incl. myofascial release) 10 sessions x45 min over 3 months + occlusal splint + education vs splint + education; between-group VAS rest p=0.04, VAS open p<0.01, VAS chew p=0.01, MMO p=0.04; clinically important improvement OR 3.06 (p=0.04) for VAS open and 6.89 (p<0.01) for VAS chewing.
Espejo-Antunez 2024 J Appl Oral Sci: n=46 women with myofascial pain; DC/TMD; soft tissue mobilization + release +/- dynamic cervical electrical stimulation (electro-massage) 2x/week x2 wk, ~25 min; significant time x group for pain (p<0.001), masseter/upper trapezius PPT, vertical mouth opening; large effect size favoring ES+MT; between-group difference -1.59 cm post and -1.90 cm at 4 wk.
Ferreira 2024 J Bodyw Mov Ther: n=60 myogenic TMD; DC/TMD; dry needling vs manual therapy (MFR cervical, suboccipital, TMJ) vs CBT; 30 min/wk x4 wk; follow-up 30 d; VAS decrease over time only in DN and MT groups (p<0.001); time x group at 30 d p<0.001. Rated high RoB (domain 4).
Gurudut 2025 Int J Ther Massage Bodywork: n=34 myofascial pain; RDC/TMD; soft tissue manipulation (massage + MFR of facial muscles) 5 sessions on alternate days vs chlorzoxazone 250 mg + aceclofenac 100 mg twice daily x7 days; both groups improved (p=0.001); NPRS between-group p=0.066 (n.s.); MMO, CGPQ v2.0, TMJ disability index differed between groups (p=0.001).
MCID benchmarking: Brochado - no significant between-group difference, within-group reductions did not clearly surpass MCID; Espejo-Antunez - exceeded; Romeo and Ferreira described as clinically meaningful.

DISCUSSION / LIMITATIONS: Effects of MFR cannot be attributed to a single technique (heterogeneous, multimodal). Only one trial compared MFR with oral analgesics: no difference. Two RCTs combined MFR with instrumental therapy (electrotherapy, PBM): improvement only with electrotherapy + MFR. QoL not consistently measured. Limitations: registration not fully prospective; few studies; >half at risk-of-bias concern; heterogeneity in modality/follow-up; no cost-effectiveness; no meta-analysis; Axis II not used as stratifier.
CONCLUSION: MFR seemed to reduce pain and improve mouth function; certainty low; clinically meaningful only in a subset; consider within multidisciplinary approach; more rigorous isolated-MFR trials needed.
