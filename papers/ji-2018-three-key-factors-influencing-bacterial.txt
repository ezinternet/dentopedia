Three key factors influencing the bacterial contamination of dental unit waterlines: a 6-year survey from 2012 to 2017

ABSTRACT
Background: Contaminated output water from dental unit waterlines (DUWLs) is a potential risk to both patients and dental personnel frequently exposed to this water or aerosols.
Aim: To evaluate the contamination level and prevalence of bacteria in the output water of DUWLs, and to identify key factors to provide technical support for formulating relevant policies.
Methods: A special sampling connector was developed for collecting dental handpiece output water and a measurement device to assess retraction of a dental chair unit (DCU). Output water from dental handpieces and air/water syringes were collected as representative of DUWLs. Water samples tested with reference to China's national standard.
Results: From 2012 to 2017, 318 DCUs randomly selected from 64 hospitals in Tianjin, China. Of these DCUs, 78.93% had NO disinfection to prevent DUWL contamination. 343 (56.14%) samples complied with the guidelines on DUWL output water. The highest bacterial concentration was 1.8 × 10⁵ CFU/mL. The three key influencing factors were:
  1. Daily or weekly disinfection of DUWLs.
  2. Water supply source being hospital self-made purified water or purchased purified bottled water.
  3. DCU with a valid anti-retraction valve.
Potential infectious agents isolated included Bacillus cereus, Burkholderia cepacia and Pseudomonas aeruginosa.
Conclusions: High rate of contamination in DUWLs, highlighting the need to develop national standards. Need to disinfect DUWLs periodically and use a cleaner source of water; more attention should be paid to the efficacy of DCU anti-retraction valves.
