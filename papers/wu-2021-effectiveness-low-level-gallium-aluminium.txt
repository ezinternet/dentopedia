Source: PubMed / PMC8718212 (condensed working copy). PMID 34967349. DOI 10.1097/MD.0000000000028015
Wu X, Zhu J, Zheng B, Liu J, Wu Z. Effectiveness of low-level gallium aluminium arsenide laser therapy for temporomandibular disorder with myofascial pain: A systemic review and meta-analysis. Medicine (Baltimore) 2021;100(52):e28015.

ABSTRACT
TMD causes masticatory muscle pain and mouth opening limitations. Benefits of low-energy lasers vary greatly with equipment and energy output. Systematic evaluation of low-level GaAlAs laser for TMD with myofascial pain. Searched PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov from inception to April 5, 2020, for RCTs comparing low-level laser with sham/placebo or no intervention in TMD with myofascial pain. Three reviewers independently screened, extracted and assessed quality (Cochrane ROB tool v5.1.0). Meta-analysis in RevMan 5.3 and Stata 15.1.
Results: 8 RCTs, 181 patients. VAS pain at end of treatment: WMD = -0.76, 95% CI -1.51 to 0.01, P = .046; 3-4 weeks after treatment: WMD = 1.24, 95% CI -0.04 to 2.51, P = .057 (no significant difference). Maximum mouth opening (MMO): end of treatment WMD = -0.03, 95% CI -4.13 to 4.06, P = .987; 3-4 weeks WMD = 1.22, 95% CI -2.94 to 5.39, P = .565.
Conclusion (abstract): insufficient evidence to indicate efficacy of low-level GaAlAs laser in improving TMD pain and maximal oral opening.

METHODS
Databases searched to April 5, 2020; RCTs only, English only. Inclusion: RCT; English; low-level GaAlAs laser as intervention; RDC/TMD axis 1. Exclusion: non-RCT; other laser types; fibromyalgia, injury, osteoarthritis or other diseases; animal, conference, review, case report; incomplete trials or no full text; incomplete data. Control could be other intervention, sham/placebo or no intervention. Primary outcome VAS pain; secondary outcome MMO. RevMan 5.3, Stata 15.1; I2 < 50% -> fixed-effects, else random-effects; sensitivity analysis; funnel plot. Two independent examiners (Zheng, Liu) screened/extracted, third (Zhu) resolved disagreements; Cochrane ROB (6 domains).

RESULTS
85 articles retrieved; 8 RCTs (2006-2020), 181 patients eligible. Sample per study 9 to 60; 25 (13.8%) of 181 male (two studies did not report sex). Sites: masseter, temporalis, medial/lateral pterygoid, joint capsule.
ROB: 5 of 8 adequate random sequence generation; 2 low-risk allocation concealment; 7 low-risk blinding.
Included studies (Table 1): Carrasco 2009 (3 energy-density arms 25/60/105 J/cm2, n=10 each; VAS); Ferreira 2013 (n=20 vs 20; VAS); de Godoy 2014 (n=5 vs 4; VAS, MMO); Khalighi 2016 (n=20 vs 20; VAS, MMO); De Carli 2013 (n=11 vs 10; VAS, MMO); Borges 2018 (n=11 vs 11; VAS, MMO); Cetiner 2006 (n=24 vs 15; VAS, MMO); Khiavi 2020 (n=5 vs 5; VAS, MMO).
Laser parameters (Table 2): all GaAlAs diode, continuous mode where reported; wavelength 780 (Carrasco, Ferreira, de Godoy), 810 (Khalighi), 808 (De Carli), 830 (Borges, Cetiner), 940 nm (Khiavi); energy density from 2.5 to 112.5 J/cm2 (heterogeneous); power 30-500 mW; sessions from 10 to 12, or weekly for 3 months (Ferreira), twice weekly for 4 wk (Carrasco).
VAS pain (all 8 studies): fixed-effects showed substantial heterogeneity (I2 = 89.3%); random-effects still I2 = 89%. Sensitivity analysis: Khalighi 2016 and Cetiner 2006 main heterogeneity sources; remaining 6 studies analysed with fixed-effects -> significant VAS difference at end of treatment, P = .046 (numerical estimate stated in abstract).
VAS at 3-4 weeks: six studies had follow-up; I2 = 89%; Ferreira 2013 and Cetiner 2006 main sources; remaining 4 studies fixed-effects: no significant difference (P = .057).
MMO at end of treatment: 5 studies measured; de Godoy and Borges excluded because of different measurement methods; remaining 3 I2 = 80.3%; Khalighi source of heterogeneity; remaining 2 studies no significant difference (P = .987, I2 = 0.0%). MMO at 3-4 weeks: 2 studies, no difference (P = .565, I2 = 0.0%).
Descriptive: Khalighi and others: pain decreased significantly after laser; Ferreira 2013: pain fell faster and to lower level (P <= .002) in laser group; Cetiner 2006: pain decreased (P < .001) but no between-group difference at 1 month. de Godoy: no MMO change in any arm (P > .1). Borges: only left-side opening improved (P < .05). Khalighi: MMO improved (P < .05).
Funnel plot symmetrical, taken as no publication bias.

DISCUSSION / LIMITATIONS
6 of 8 studies used sham controls, 2 used drug comparators (piroxicam, naproxen). Laser reduced pain at end of treatment but not maintained; no MMO benefit. Evidence described as moderate quality. ROB: allocation concealment most common problem (only 2 studies). Sham groups partly improved (probe pressure on trigger point). Drug comparators: Khalighi (laser better than drug at follow-up) vs De Carli (similar at end of treatment, drug more stable at follow-up). Inconsistent with Munguia 2018 (LLLT better than placebo at 3-4 weeks; different laser types and sham criteria). Clinical heterogeneity in energy density, power density, pulse/continuous, time, frequency, sessions. de Godoy: small n, split-mouth design. Khalighi rated low quality by ROB tool. Small total sample; 86% female.
CONCLUSION (body): GaAlAs laser superior to control in reducing pain at end of treatment, only moderate evidence; no advantage in stability or MMO; insufficient data and high-quality evidence for strong conclusions.
