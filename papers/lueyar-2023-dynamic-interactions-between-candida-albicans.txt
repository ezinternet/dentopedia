Dynamic interactions between Candida albicans and different streptococcal species in a multispecies oral biofilm

ABSTRACT
The oral cavity is colonized by a plethora of bacteria, fungi, and archaea, including streptococci of the
mitis group (MSG) and the yeast Candida albicans. This study investigates the role of streptococcal
species in oral biofilm development and cross-kingdom interactions between commensal MSG members and the
pathogen yeast C. albicans using a multispecies supragingival biofilm model. Nine different in vitro
biofilms were grown, quantified with culture analyses, and examined with confocal laser scanning
microscopy (CLSM). A four-species biofilm without any streptococcal species was used as a basic biofilm.
In each subsequent inoculum, one species of MSG was added and afterward combined with Streptococcus
mutans. The eight-species biofilm contained all eight strains. Culture analyses showed that presence of
S. mutans in a four-species biofilm with S. oralis or S. oralis subsp. tigurinus did not differ
significantly in C. albicans CFU counts compared to biofilms without S. mutans. However, compared to other
mitis species, Streptococcus gordonii combined with S. mutans resulted in the lowest CFUs of C. albicans.
CLSM showed that biofilms containing both S. mutans and one species of MSG seemed to induce the
filamentous form of C. albicans; but when several MSG species were combined with S. mutans, C. albicans
was again found in its yeast form.

METHODS (summary)
- In vitro multispecies "supragingival" biofilm model (Zürich model; Thurnheer/Guggenheim), grown on
  saliva-conditioned hydroxyapatite (HA) discs, anaerobic, 37 °C. Strains identified by Zürich OMZ numbers.
- Species panel (8 strains): Candida albicans, Streptococcus mutans, Streptococcus oralis, Streptococcus
  oralis subsp. tigurinus, Streptococcus gordonii, plus a non-streptococcal "basic" background community.
- Biofilm growth: first 16 h in mFUM + 0.3% glucose; then 48 h in mFUM + 0.15% glucose + 0.15% sucrose.
  Dip-washed 3×/day, media refreshed daily. Total run ≈ 64 h.
- Quantification: biofilm vortexed off HA, sonicated, serially diluted, spiral-plated. CBA plates = total
  CFU + MSG + S. mutans; BIGGY agar = C. albicans CFU. Colonies counted by stereomicroscope; MSG recognized
  by α-hemolysis (green), C. albicans strain non-hemolytic.
- FISH probes: MUT590-Cy5 (S. mutans), MIT447-FAM (mitis group), Caal1249-Cy3 (C. albicans); total DNA
  SYTO40. CLSM: Leica SP5, ×63 oil, Imaris 9.6 reconstruction.
- 3 independent experiments, triplicate biofilms; two-way ANOVA + Tukey; α = 0.05.

KEY RESULTS
- Adding more streptococcal species progressively reduced C. albicans CFU vs the basic biofilm.
- S. mutans + S. gordonii (biofilm 7) → significant reduction in C. albicans CFU vs biofilm with S. mutans
  alone (p < 0.01); eight-species biofilm → strongest reduction (p < 0.0001). S. gordonii + S. mutans gave
  the LOWEST C. albicans CFUs of all mitis-group combinations.
- S. oralis (biofilm 5) or S. oralis subsp. tigurinus (biofilm 6) added with S. mutans did NOT
  significantly change C. albicans CFU relative to biofilms without S. mutans.
- C. albicans presence generally did not significantly change total or MSG CFU counts.
- Morphology (CLSM): C. albicans grew as yeast in the absence of streptococci; S. mutans + a SINGLE MSG
  species induced filamentous (hyphal) forms; when SEVERAL MSG species co-existed with S. mutans,
  C. albicans reverted to yeast form (attributed to increased inter-streptococcal competition).
- Spatial: MSG near the salivary pellicle/HA base; C. albicans colonized the middle/upper biofilm.
- Candidal biomass (Imaris) showed no significant differences between compositions (large variance);
  authors caution that upon filamentation CFU no longer tracks candidal biomass.

DISCUSSION / MECHANISM POINTS
- Sucrose-rich conditions let S. mutans GtfB coat the C. albicans surface with α-glucans (EPS), creating
  binding sites that promote cross-kingdom cariogenic biofilm (Falsetta, Hwang, Koo work cited).
- Streptococcal H2O2 and saliva can induce C. albicans hyphae (oxidative stress); low pH favors yeast in
  deep layers. Some streptococcal metabolites/peptides inhibit hyphal formation.
- In vivo, C. albicans + mitis-group synergy increases neutrophil infiltration and soft-tissue lesion
  severity vs single-species C. albicans biofilm.
- First description of interactions including S. oralis subsp. tigurinus in an eight-species biofilm model.

LIMITATIONS
- In vitro model; fixed defined strains, not clinical saliva communities — proportions differ from mouth.
- CFU-based quantification loses meaning once C. albicans filaments; biomass measure underpowered (variance).
- Biofilm-matrix contribution not directly analyzed.
- Single C. albicans strain (SC5314-type lab strain); no clinical isolates.
