Title: An update on trismus: etiology, diagnosis and treatment

Trismus, commonly known as "lockjaw," is a clinical condition characterised by a persistent, involuntary restriction in the ability to open the mouth due to tonic contraction or spasm of the masticatory muscles. This narrative review proposes a novel etiological classification distinguishing neurological from non-neurological causes and introduces a stepwise diagnostic and management algorithm.

ETIOLOGICAL CLASSIFICATION:

Neurological — CNS Structural: TBI/DAI, brain tumours, stroke, MS, CNS infections (meningitis, encephalitis)
Neurological — CNS Non-structural: Parkinson's/PSP/CBD, status epilepticus, oromandibular dystonia, drug-induced (phenothiazines, metoclopramide, fluoxetine, tricyclics), Wilson's disease, uremic encephalopathy, hemimasticatory spasm, functional disorder, catatonia
Neurological — PNS Structural: trigeminal nerve lesions (V3 iatrogenic injury), tumours of masticator space
Neurological — PNS Non-structural: tetanus (tetanospasmin blocks GABA/glycine), heavy metal poisoning (Pb/Hg)
Non-neurological — Structural: TMJ disorders, tumours, trauma, orofacial infections (pericoronitis, periapical abscess, deep fascial space, peritonsillar abscess, osteomyelitis), mandibular/maxillary fractures, post-surgical oedema/hematoma, radiation therapy fibrosis, prolonged immobility, burns, autoimmune (RA, scleroderma, temporal arteritis), congenital malformations
Non-neurological — Non-structural: hypocalcaemia, hypomagnesaemia, malignant hyperthermia (succinylcholine/halothane masseter spasm), drug-induced (etomidate), muscle contractures, myofascial pain syndrome

DIAGNOSIS:
- Trismus: interincisal distance < 35 mm (normal 40–60 mm)
- Red flags: airway compromise, deep neck space infections, tetanus, CNS infections, intracranial lesions
- Clinical history, physical exam (interincisal distance, TMJ function, jaw ROM, cranial nerve assessment)
- Instrumental: MRI/CT, nerve conduction + trigeminal reflex, EMG, EEG, USS, FEES, jaw tracking

TREATMENT:
- NSAIDs + muscle relaxants (acute uncomplicated trismus)
- Discontinue offending drugs
- Correct metabolic disturbances
- Tetanus: immunoglobulin + supportive care
- Orofacial infections: antibiotics + surgical drainage
- BoNT-A: neurological trismus or non-neurological without structural restriction; inhibits ACh at NMJ
- Baclofen (GABA-B agonist): neurological spastic trismus (stroke, DAI, CP, MS); oral or intrathecal
- Physical therapy: passive stretching + jaw mobilisation exercises; multiple daily sessions
- Coronoidectomy: extra-articular trismus (temporalis fibrosis, post-radiation); requires postoperative PT
- Myotomy: muscular contracture; less predictable
- Free vascularized flap reconstruction: advanced fibrosis/post-oncologic cases

PROGNOSIS: Self-limiting in most cases, resolves within 2 weeks; post-RT fibrosis protracted course
