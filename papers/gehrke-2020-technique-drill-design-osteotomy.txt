Title: Effects of the technique and drill design used during the osteotomy on the thermal and histological stimulation
Authors: Sergio Alexandre Gehrke, Tiago Luis Eliers Treichel, Jaime Aramburú Júnior, Piedad N de Aza, Juan Carlos Prados-Frutos
Journal: Scientific Reports
Year: 2020
DOI: 10.1038/s41598-020-77762-z
PMID: 33244090
PMCID: PMC7691358
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7691358/

ABSTRACT
The research compared thermal alterations and bone healing responses using different drilling approaches for dental implant site preparation, examining how drill geometry and movement patterns influence outcomes. In vivo animal study using 12 female New Zealand rabbits with 48 total osteotomies (n=12 per group, 4 groups). Duration to evaluation: 30 days post-osteotomy.

STUDY DESIGN & GROUPS
Four experimental groups:
G1: Cylindrical drill + Continuous movement
G2: Cylindrical drill + Intermittent movement
G3: Conical drill + Continuous movement
G4: Conical drill + Intermittent movement

Drill specifications:
- Cylindrical sequence (G1, G2): 2.2 mm @ 800 rpm, 2.8 mm @ 600 rpm, 3.5 mm @ 500 rpm
- Conical sequence (G3, G4): All drills at 1500 rpm
- Final osteotomy length: 8 mm
- Implants: Cylindrical (8×4.1 mm) vs Conical (8×4 mm)

INTERMITTENT TECHNIQUE PROTOCOL
Intermittent movement involved inserting the drill sequence in a staged approach:
- Pass 1: 0→3 mm depth (drills marked at 3 mm)
- Pass 2: 0→5 mm depth (drills marked at 5 mm)
- Pass 3: 0→8 mm depth (full depth)
Drills were marked at 3 mm and 5 mm positions prior to surgery to standardize stopping points.

IRRIGATION
External saline at room temperature (23 ± 1°C), continuous throughout drilling.

TEMPERATURE MEASUREMENT
Type K thermocouple sensors inserted 1 mm from final osteotomy margin, 3 mm intrabone depth. Recorded: maximum temperature and time to return to baseline (33.5 ± 0.5°C).

TEMPERATURE RESULTS
Maximum temperatures:
G1 (cylindrical, continuous): 40.4 ± 1.38°C
G2 (cylindrical, intermittent): 37.8 ± 1.62°C
G3 (conical, continuous): 36.3 ± 0.90°C
G4 (conical, intermittent): 36.3 ± 0.80°C

Temperature variation (delta from baseline):
G1: 6.91 ± 1.4°C
G2: 4.30 ± 1.3°C
G3: 2.78 ± 0.6°C
G4: 2.77 ± 0.7°C

Statistical comparisons (temperature variation):
G1 vs G2: Δ2.608°C, p=0.0010, 95%CI [1.435–3.781]
G1 vs G3: Δ4.133°C, p<0.0001
G1 vs G4: Δ4.142°C, p<0.0001
G2 vs G3: Δ1.525°C, p=0.0015
G2 vs G4: Δ1.533°C, p=0.0283
G3 vs G4: Δ−0.008°C, p=0.9769 (NS)

Recovery time to baseline:
G1 & G2: ~20 seconds
G3 & G4: ~10 seconds

HISTOLOGICAL RESULTS (New Bone Formation at 30 days)
G1: 1.00 ± 0.3 mm²
G2: 1.48 ± 0.3 mm²
G3: 2.20 ± 0.4 mm²
G4: 2.43 ± 0.4 mm²

PMN cell counts:
G1: 62.4 ± 5.9 cells
G2: 50.7 ± 4.2 cells
G3: 44.4 ± 3.7 cells
G4: 42.4 ± 3.7 cells

CONCLUSIONS
1. Conical drills generate significantly less heat than cylindrical drills under both techniques.
2. For cylindrical drills, intermittent movement significantly reduces temperature (−2.61°C, p=0.001, ~37% reduction).
3. For conical drills, technique (continuous vs intermittent) shows no significant thermal difference (p=0.977).
4. Intermittent technique improves new bone formation and reduces PMN count vs continuous, regardless of drill type.
5. Clinical recommendation: Consider drill design when choosing technique; for cylindrical drills, intermittent movement is especially important.

LIMITATIONS
- Rabbit tibia: extreme cortical density alongside soft medullary bone, not representative of human jaw
- Type K thermocouple sensitivity limitations
- Variables not tested: drill speed variations, applied force, drilling duration specifics
- 30-day observation only
