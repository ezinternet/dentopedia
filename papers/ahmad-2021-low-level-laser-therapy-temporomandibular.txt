Source: PubMed / PMC8169142 (condensed working copy). DOI 10.25122/jml-2020-0169. PMID 34104237.
Ahmad SA, Hasan S, Saeed S, Khan A, Khan M. Low-level laser therapy in temporomandibular joint disorders: a systematic review. J Med Life 2021;14(2):148-164.

ABSTRACT: TMDs encompass ailments of the TMJ, masticatory muscles and allied structures. LLLT has evolved as an efficient non-invasive modality; previous SRs/MAs showed variable results. This SR evaluates efficacy of LLLT in TMD patients.

METHODS: PICO: population TMD; intervention LLLT; control placebo or other interventions (occlusal splints, analgesics, TENS, botulinum toxin); outcome efficacy. Search Jan 2000 - June 2020, PubMed and Science Direct only, plus hand-searching/cross-referencing. Inclusion: RCTs in humans with TMD; English; >=10 subjects. Exclusion: non-randomized or crossover studies; animal; non-English/pre-2000; <10 subjects; no outcome data; systemic disorders (RA, fibromyalgia) or non-TMD pain. Primary outcome: pain intensity (VAS). Secondary: mouth opening, lateral/protrusive movements, TMJ noises, masticatory efficiency, PPT, EMG, QoL, psychological/emotional aspects. Risk of bias via Robvis (NIHR). No meta-analysis.

RESULTS: 37 articles eligible (PRISMA flow). Robvis: authors state no potential publication bias; 33/37 (89.18%) overall low risk or some concerns, 4 high risk. 18 studies used RDC/TMD, 6 used VAS, 7 both. Lasers: GaAlAs 19 studies; GaAs 5; Nd:YAG, diode, red and infrared 2 each; InGaAlP and HeNe 1 each; combos of two laser types in 3 studies. Wavelength 632.8 nm to 1064 nm; dose 1.5 to 112.5 J/cm2 (5 studies not reported); power 1.76 mW to 500 mW (3 studies not reported). Sessions/week 1-7 (most 2/week); total sessions 4-20 (8 sessions in 8 studies, 12 in 7, 10 in 6). Application site: TMJ and/or muscles in 18, TMJ only 9, muscles only 8. Comparators: mostly placebo; 7 studies vs botulinum toxin A (Carli 2016), TENS (Chellappa 2020), ibuprofen (Marini 2010), needling (Uemoto 2013), occlusal splints (Oz 2010; Demirkol 2014), PDP (Cavalcanti 2016); 2 co-intervention designs (piroxicam - Carli 2012; oral motor exercises - Machado 2016).
Secondary outcomes: mouth opening 18 studies, lateral movement 13, protrusive 10, PPT 7, EMG 2, joint noises 2, psychological/emotional 2, masticatory efficiency 2, tinnitus 1, occlusal contacts 1.
Findings: 18 studies showed LLLT efficacious in reducing TMD pain; 12 showed similar efficacy to placebo/controls/other intervention; 4 showed varied effects on pain, mandibular motion, EMG, masticatory efficiency. Two studies: LLLT improved psychological/emotional aspects. Laser acupuncture suggested as alternative (Madani 2020, less chair time). Brochado 2018: PBM, manual therapy and combination all relieved pain; combination did not add benefit. Carli 2016: laser vs botulinum toxin A both reduced pain, laser faster; no significant MO change. Oz 2010: LLLT = occlusal splints. Altindis 2019: splint and LLLT both effective in MPS. Machado 2016: LLLT + oral motor exercises suggested more efficient.
Representative tables (selected rows): Ahrari 2014 n=20 810 nm 50 mW 3.4 J/cm2 LLLT>placebo; Emshoff 2008 n=52 HeNe 632.8 nm LLLT=placebo; Magri 2017 n=91 780 nm LLLT=placebo; Cunha 2008 n=40 830 nm LLLT=placebo; Godoy 2015 no significant differences; Kulekcioglu 2003 n=35 904 nm LLLT>placebo for MO/LM but = placebo for pain and sounds; Venezian 2010 LLLT>placebo pain, = placebo EMG; Uemoto 2013 LLLT>placebo only at 4 J/cm2.

DISCUSSION / LIMITATIONS: Only two databases searched (PubMed, Science Direct) due to limited access. Heterogeneity in dose, power, wavelength, number and frequency of applications, and patient samples. Contradictory literature attributed to dose variation (Bjordal). Authors advocate a further SR with meta-analysis with more databases. Authors note no documented adverse outcomes. Conclusion: LLLT appears efficient in diminishing TMD pain with variable effects on secondary outcomes; non-invasive, reversible, few adverse effects; may improve psychological/emotional aspects; promising regimen. Authors also state there is still no conclusive validation to substantiate or contradict LLLT for TMDs. Conflict of interest: none.
