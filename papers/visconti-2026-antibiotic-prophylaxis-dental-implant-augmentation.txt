Interventions for replacing missing teeth: antibiotic prophylaxis for dental implant placement with bone augmentation procedures

Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2026 (Protocol)
DOI: 10.1002/14651858.CD016385
PMID: 42695376 | PMC: PMC13543260

AUTHORS: Visconti RF et al. (RFV, ME, HM, CB)
NOTE: This is a PROTOCOL — no results available yet. Awaiting full review.

RATIONALE
Implant placement with concomitant bone augmentation (grafts, GBR, sinus lift) represents a higher-risk subgroup than standard implant placement. Early implant failures have been partly attributed to bacterial contamination; infections involving biomaterials (implants, bone substitutes, barrier membranes) are difficult to manage and may require implant removal. The role of antibiotic prophylaxis in this context is controversial — one RCT found no additional benefit, others found potential benefit.

This protocol complements the existing Cochrane review on antibiotics for implant placement WITHOUT bone augmentation.

OBJECTIVES
To evaluate the benefits and harms of systemic prophylactic antibiotics versus no antibiotics/placebo in people undergoing dental implant placement with concomitant bone augmentation procedures, and to assess whether effects differ according to dosage, duration, timing, and type of antibiotic.

POPULATION
Adults >18 years undergoing implant placement with:
- Bone grafting procedures (autogenous, xenograft, allograft, synthetic)
- Guided bone regeneration (barrier membranes ± grafts)
- Simultaneous sinus lift procedures

COMPARISONS
1. Prophylactic antibiotics vs no antibiotics/placebo
2. Different doses/durations of same antibiotic
3. Same antibiotic at different timing (pre- vs post-operative)
4. Different antibiotics

CRITICAL OUTCOMES (at longest follow-up, up to 1 year)
- Implant failure (mobility, removal for progressive MBL or infection)
- Prosthetic failure (prosthesis not placed, or failure secondary to implant failure)

IMPORTANT OUTCOMES (within first 3 months)
- Postoperative infections (suppuration, abscess, fistula, wound dehiscence)
- Adverse events (GI disturbances, allergic reactions, anaphylaxis)

STUDY INCLUSION
RCTs, parallel-group, minimum 3-month follow-up, ≥10 participants per arm.
Statistical unit: participant (not implant or prosthesis).

METHODOLOGY
- Databases: CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase + trial registries (no language/date restrictions)
- Risk of bias: Cochrane RoB 1 tool (7 domains)
- Synthesis: random-effects model, Mantel-Haenszel method; RR + 95% CI
- Certainty: GRADE approach
- Subgroup analyses for implant failures: augmentation type, horizontal vs vertical GBR, resorbable vs non-resorbable membrane

EQUITY CONSIDERATIONS
Antibiotic prophylaxis is generally inexpensive and widely accessible; inequity unlikely to be major concern.

STATUS: Protocol — currently awaiting RCT identification, data extraction, and synthesis.
