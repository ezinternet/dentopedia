J Oral Rehabil. 2021;48(6):738-744.

Rehabilitation of shortened dental arches: A fifteen-year randomised trial.
Walter MH, Marre B, Dreyhaupt J, Heydecke G, Rauch A, Mundt T, Hannak W, Kohal RJ, Kern M,
Nothdurft F, Hartmann S, Boning K, Boldt J, Stark H, Edelhoff D, Wostmann B, Wolfart S, Jahn F, Luthardt RG.
(German multicentre randomised trial; 14 dental schools/universities.)

BACKGROUND: Few long-term studies on treatments in the shortened dental arch (SDA) are available.
OBJECTIVE: Analyse long-term success of two different treatment concepts.
METHODS: Patients over 35 years of age with missing molars in one jaw and at least the canine and one
premolar present on both sides were eligible.
- Partial removable dental prosthesis (PRDP) group (N=81): molars and missing second premolars replaced
  by a precision-attachment retained prosthesis.
- SDA group (N=71): dental arch ended with the second premolar, which had to be present or replaced by
  a cantilever fixed dental prosthesis.
Follow-up examinations over 15 years.
RESULTS: A comprehensive outcome variable comprised four failure categories; Kaplan-Meier survival
(success) analyses conducted.
- Half of patients exhibited continuous preservation of per-protocol prosthetic status, totally
  unaffected by complications, for more than 10 years.
- Event-free success rates for moderate-or-worse failure implied loss of per-protocol prosthetic status;
  respective survival fell below 50% at 14.2 years (PRDP) and 14.3 years (SDA).
- In NONE of the analyses was a significant group difference found.
CONCLUSION: In patients with an SDA condition, changes in prosthetic status have to be expected. The
affected proportion increases almost linearly from shortly after treatment and comprises the majority
after 15 years. The influence of the examined treatments on success appears to be low.
