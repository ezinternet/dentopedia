Title: Prevalence and Risk of Dental Erosion Linked to Carbonated Drinks in Adolescents: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Authors: Juarez Lopez MLA, Gutierrez-Moreno MF, Castrejon-Delgado L, Sanchez-Rodriguez MA

ABSTRACT: PRISMA; PubMed, Scopus, WoS, LILACS, SciELO; observational studies; PEO framework. 24 studies; 21,541 adolescents; pooled prevalence 37.6% (95%CI 26.3-49.7). Carbonated beverage consumption vs erosion: OR 1.98 (95%CI 1.42-2.77; 7785 participants; I2=80%; P<0.0001).

METHODS: PROSPERO CRD42023398708. Search 2000 - 17 Dec 2023; English/Spanish. Case-control, cross-sectional, cohort. Exclusions: case reports, reviews, in vitro, insufficient data, severity-stratified. Outcome = presence/absence of erosive lesions. Carbonated beverages defined as sugar-containing effervescent drinks. Only studies on intake more than once daily or >7 times weekly entered the association meta-analysis (treated as equivalent). Risk of bias: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale; 24 studies with score >=5 included. Prevalence MA: MedCalc 23.0.2, Freeman-Tukey arcsine, DerSimonian-Laird random effects. OR MA: RevMan 5.4.1, Mantel-Haenszel, DL random effects. I2 heterogeneity; Egger test + funnel plot.

RESULTS: 131 records -> 101 after duplicates -> 24 full texts. 21 cross-sectional, 2 case-control, 1 cohort. Only 5 articles combinable for risk-factor MA. 21,541 participants; prevalence range 1.4%-80%; mean age 15.0 +/- 2.7. Indices: BEWE 8 studies, Lussi 3, Smith&Knight TWI 6, O'Sullivan 4, Carlsson 1, SEPRS 2. 19/24 (74%) reported significant association carbonated beverages-erosion; rest non-significant or combined acidic drinks.
Prevalence MA 37.6% (95%CI 26.3-49.7; forest plot caption says 26.6-49.7). OR 1.98 (1.42-2.77), 7785 participants, I2 80%. Sex-stratified analysis not possible.
Individual ORs (Table 2): Gonzalez-Aragon 2022 1.16 (1.03-1.31); Korkmaz 2020 cola 3.6 (2.04-6.51); Simangwa 2019 1.6 (1.3-2.1); Marro 2018 >1/day 2.13 (1.38-3.28); Skalsky 2018 1.65 (1.25-2.15); Provatenou 2016 2.11 (1.0-4.30); Shahbaz 2016 >8/wk 3.87 (1.43-10.49); Gonzalez 2016 >=4/wk 1.80 (1.03-3.07); Kirthiga 2015 no association (prev 1.4%); Al-Hadi 2013 drinking before bedtime 7.8 (3.94-15.42); Chrysanthakopoulos 2012 3.99 (1.37-11.59); El Aidi 2011 (cohort) 1.04 (1.01-1.07); Kumar 2011 2.8 (1.32-5.92); Bardolia 2010 >1/day 1.6 (1.1-2.3); Okunseri 2010 adjusted 1.24 (1.08-1.43); Wang 2010 >1/wk 1.29 (1.03-1.64); Waterhouse 2008 1.71 (1.12-2.62); Milosevic 2004 1.32 (1.08-1.62); Arnadottir 2003 (case-control, >800 ml/day) 1.7 (0.5-5.0) ns.
Risk of bias: NOS; 20 studies good quality, 4 fair (one 5 stars); comparability least fulfilled (11 studies). Egger 7.38 (95%CI -14.05 to 28.81; p=0.479); funnel plot asymmetric but no strong evidence of publication bias.

DISCUSSION/LIMITATIONS: modifiers: drinking at night, sipping slowly, retaining in mouth, post-exercise low saliva; milk protective (Gonzalez-Aragon); xerostomia. Carbonated drink pH typically 2.3-3.5 vs critical 5.5. Exposure definitions varied (frequency vs volume) -> only 25% of eligible studies in association MA; I2 80% -> interpret with caution; mostly cross-sectional, self-report (recall bias, overestimation), no temporality; funnel asymmetry. Future: longitudinal designs, standardised criteria, validated questionnaires.
CONCLUSION: erosive tooth wear affects 37% of adolescents; carbonated beverages >1/day consistent risk factor; health promotion should prioritise reducing intake. Funding: DGAPA-UNAM PAPIME PE 201525. No COI.
