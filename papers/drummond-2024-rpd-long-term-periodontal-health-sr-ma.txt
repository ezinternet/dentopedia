Long-term assessment of the periodontal health of removable partial denture wearers:
A systematic review and meta-analysis.
Drummond LB, Bezerra AP, Feldmann A, Gonçalves TMSV.

STATEMENT OF PROBLEM: The long-term effects of wearing removable partial dentures (RPDs) remain unclear.
PURPOSE: "Is the long-term use of RPDs deleterious to the remaining teeth?"
MATERIAL AND METHODS: PRISMA-guided. Six databases + non-peer-reviewed literature searched April 2024,
no language or follow-up restriction. Only clinical studies evaluating RPD long-term use included.
Risk of bias and certainty assessed (RoB 2.0; ROBINS-I; GRADE). Meta-analyses for survival rate and
periodontal health (alpha=.05).
RESULTS: 5577 records identified; 46 studies included, covering 4359 prostheses and 4072 participants
(mean age 60 +/- 5.2 years). Low to moderate risk of bias.
- 5-year survival cast-clasp RPD 95.1% (ER=0.951; 95% CI 0.900-0.977; P<.001)
- 5-year survival telescopic-crown-retained RPD 91.7% (ER=0.917; 95% CI 0.870-0.948; P<.001)
- No difference between them (P=.71)
- Abutment teeth higher risk of extraction after 5 years: OR=1.99 (95% CI 1.32-3.01; P=.001)
- Nonvital teeth higher risk of extraction: HR=2.961 (95% CI 2.023-4.335; P<.001)
- Tooth mobility unchanged (P=.98)
- Probing depth unchanged (P=.50)
- Gingival index increased: MD=0.477 (95% CI 0.12-0.83; P=.008)
CONCLUSIONS: High survival rate for both cast-clasp and telescopic-crown-retained RPDs, with few
periodontal changes to the remaining teeth.
