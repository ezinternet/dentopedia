Marschner F, Kanzow P, Wiegand A. Anamnestic risk factors for erosive tooth wear: Systematic review, mapping, and meta-analysis. J Dent 2024;144:104962. DOI 10.1016/j.jdent.2024.104962. PMID 38552999. Source: PubMed abstract (no PMC full text).

Objective: identify key risk factors for erosive tooth wear in permanent dentition.
Data: Observational studies reporting anamnestic risk factors for erosive tooth wear. Methodological quality/risk of bias assessed with the modified Newcastle-Ottawa scale for cross-sectional studies. Risk factors shown in a heatmap; random-effects meta-analyses of ORs where possible.
Sources: MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science and manual searches in February 2023. PROSPERO CRD4202339776.
Results: 87 publications reporting 71 studies included. 80 anamnestic risk factors in ten domains (socio-demographics, socio-economics, general health, oral diseases, medication, oral hygiene, food, beverages, dietary habits, leisure-related). Significant associations: male gender (p<0.001; OR=1.30, 95% CI 1.16-1.44), regurgitation (p=0.033; OR=2.27, 1.41-3.65), digestive disorders (p<0.001; OR=1.81, 1.48-2.21), acidic foods (p=0.033; OR=2.40, 1.44-4.00), seasoning sauces (p=0.003; OR=1.28, 1.13-1.44), nutritional supplements (p=0.019; OR=1.73, 1.28-2.35), carbonated drinks (p=0.019; OR=1.43, 1.17-1.75). Most included studies low risk of bias.
Conclusions: Future studies should use validated questionnaires. Clinicians should concentrate primarily on symptoms of GERD and dietary factors when screening patients at risk for erosive tooth wear.
