Evaluating Pain, Onset of Action, Duration, and Anesthetic Efficacy of Conventional and Buffered Lidocaine in Infiltration Anesthesia: A Comparative Clinical Study

Hamad SA, Al-Dawoody AD, Alraad AS, Ahmed OS
Journal of clinical and experimental dentistry. 2025 Oct 17;17(11):e1350-e1355.
DOI: 10.4317/jced.63093
PMID: 41459126  PMC: PMC12742648

ABSTRACT

Background: Buffered local anesthetics are proposed to alleviate injection pain, decrease onset time, and extend the duration of anesthesia. This research sought to evaluate the clinical effectiveness of buffered lidocaine compared to traditional lidocaine in patients having maxillary posterior teeth extraction due to chronic periapical lesions.

Methods: Double-blind, prospective, RCT. 100 adults (ASA I-II), 18-60 years, maxillary posterior teeth extraction. Group A: conventional 2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 adrenaline (pH ~4.2). Group B: buffered 2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine + 8.4% sodium bicarbonate (pH ~7.3-7.4). Supraperiosteal infiltration; 1.8mL per tooth. VAS for injection pain; onset to first numbness/tingling; duration to first analgesic request.

Results:
- VAS injection pain: Group B 2.8±0.7 vs Group A 4.2±0.9 (p<0.01)
- Onset: Group B 2.3min vs Group A 4.7min (p<0.01)
- Duration: Group B 45.8±7.6min vs Group A 36.4±8.2min (p<0.01)
- Reinjection: Group A 8% vs Group B 4% (p=0.678, NS)

Conclusion: Buffered lidocaine offers significantly better anesthetic efficacy: less injection pain, faster onset, extended duration. No difference in reinjection rate.

METHODS

Trial period: September 2024 to April 2025
Institution: Hospital dental clinic, Kurdistan region
Ethics: Kurdistan Board of Medical Specialties (No. 440, 29-08-2024)
n=100, 50 per group
Inclusion: 18-60y, ASA I-II, no local anesthetic allergy, maxillary posterior teeth with periapical lesions
Exclusion: pregnant/lactating, systemic infection, multiple extraction requirement, analgesics/sedatives

Buffering protocol:
- Add 0.18mL of 8.4% NaHCO3 to 1.8mL 2% lidocaine 1:100,000 epi (1:10 ratio)
- Prepared immediately before injection
- Final pH: ~7.4 (vs conventional pH 4.2)

Injection: 27-gauge needle, supraperiosteal, 1mL/min rate, single surgeon blinded to group
Assessment by second blinded investigator

RESULTS (DETAILED)

Both groups comparable at baseline (age, gender, baseline pain scores).

Injection pain (VAS):
- Group B (buffered): 2.8 ± 0.7
- Group A (conventional): 4.2 ± 0.9
- p<0.01

Onset of anesthesia:
- Group B: 2.3 minutes
- Group A: 4.7 minutes
- p<0.01 (Group B 51% faster)

Duration of anesthesia:
- Group B: 45.8 ± 7.6 minutes
- Group A: 36.4 ± 8.2 minutes
- p<0.01 (Group B ~26% longer)

Reinjection rate:
- Group A: 8%
- Group B: 4%
- p=0.678 (NS)

MECHANISM

Conventional lidocaine pH ~4.2 → irritates tissue, activates nociceptors → injection pain
Buffering with NaHCO3 raises pH to ~7.4 → closer to physiological pH →
- More uncharged base form → faster nerve membrane penetration
- Less tissue irritation → reduced injection pain
- Reduced vasodilation from acidic solution → epinephrine vasoconstriction more effective → longer duration

LIMITATIONS

- Focus on maxillary posterior only; mandible, implants not studied
- Fixed 1:10 NaHCO3 ratio
- VAS only; no objective biomarkers
