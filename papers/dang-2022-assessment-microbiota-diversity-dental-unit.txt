Assessment of microbiota diversity in dental unit waterline contamination
Dang Y, Zhang Q, Wang J, Wang Q, Han M, Niu Y, Li H, Li X. PeerJ. 2022 Jan 6;10:e12723.
DOI: 10.7717/peerj.12723 | PMID: 35036099 | PMCID: PMC8743008

ABSTRACT
Background: Dental unit waterlines (DUWLs) provide water for handpieces, air/water syringes, and mouth-rinse water outlets. DUWL contamination can negatively affect the operating environment and public health.
Methods: 350 5-mL dental water samples (from high-speed handpieces, air/water syringes, and mouth-rinse water outlets) from 60 dental chair units (DCUs) at a dental hospital determined bacterial concentrations by culture. 17 high-quality DNA from 60 250-mL air/water syringe water samples analyzed by 16S rDNA high-throughput sequencing.
Results: Median bacterial concentration 166 (31.5, 672.5) CFU/mL; range 0–3,816,000 CFU/mL. Only 42.6% of samples had bacterial concentrations below 100 CFU/mL. Kruskal–Wallis: three specialties had significantly different concentrations (H=27.441, P<0.01). Sequencing showed significant differences in community structure between periodontics and the other two specialties. 508 OTUs detected across all three specialties, with 160/182/176 OTUs unique to periodontics/endodontics/prosthodontics. LEfSe: Hydrocarboniphaga, Zoogloea, Aquabacterium, Hydrogenophaga enriched in periodontics; Acinetobacter, Geothrix, Desulfovibrio in prosthodontics; Alistipes, Clostridium XIVa, Serratia in endodontics. Seven potentially human-pathogenic genera (Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, Sphingomonas, Ochrobactrum, Rhizobium, Brevundimonas, Methylobacterium) >1% relative abundance detected.
Conclusions: Bacterial concentrations and microbial composition influenced by dental specialty; validated disinfection protocol needed.

INTRODUCTION
DUWLs provide water for handpieces, air/water syringes, and mouth-rinse water outlets. Iatrogenic infection caused by DUWL contamination has attracted attention. First reported DUWL-linked death: an 82-year-old woman died from Legionnaires' disease, with Legionella isolated from dental water and her bronchial aspirate. In 2015, nine children were infected after pulpotomy at a pediatric dentistry practice in Georgia, USA. Microorganisms in DUWLs, especially opportunistic pathogens, are potential risk factors for medically compromised/immunocompromised patients.
Bacterial concentrations far exceed safety standards: American CDC 500 CFU/mL, European Council 100 CFU/mL. Microorganisms come from supply water or patients' oral cavities via reverse suction (suck-back). They adhere to tube walls and colonize to form biofilms in complex networks of thin tubes due to long periods of water stagnation. Established biofilms release fragments containing bacterial aggregates and act as reservoirs of opportunistic pathogens, protecting them from antimicrobials.

METHODS
Water samples from 60 DCUs at a dental hospital: 19/21/20 DCUs in periodontics (PE-M), endodontics (EN-M), prosthodontics (PR-M). DUWLs supplied with municipal water meeting China's standard (heterotrophic plate count <100 CFU/mL at 37°C). Before sampling, outlets disinfected with alcohol; waterlines flushed 2 min before and 30 s after daily practice. 5-mL samples collected from high-speed handpieces, air/water syringes, and mouth-rinse outlets before and after daily practice. 250-mL air/water syringe samples collected from all 60 DCUs for DNA. Culture: BHI plate, 48 h at 37°C, 5% CO2; threshold 100 CFU/mL (China standard). 16S rRNA V3–V4 sequencing (Illumina MiSeq PE250), OTUs at 97% similarity.

Alpha-diversity: no significant differences among specialties (P>0.05). PCoA/ADONIS: significant community structure difference; periodontics far removed from endodontics+prosthodontics (which clustered together).

DISCUSSION
No significant difference before vs after daily practice: flushing reduces concentrations somewhat, but suck-back of saliva and blood keeps contamination a serious concern. Main biofilm factors: small lumen (0.5–2 mm), high surface-to-volume ratio (6:1), slow flow during work and stagnation outside work hours. Pseudomonas and Acinetobacter are human pathogens; Pseudomonas forms biofilm in plastic waterlines, grows in low-nutrient environments, resists antimicrobials. Acinetobacter causes nosocomial infections with drug resistance. Gram-negative endotoxins may cause allergic airway reactions and proinflammatory cytokine release during oral surgery.
Disinfectants (peracetic acid, hydrogen peroxide, sodium hypochlorite, chlorine dioxide, chlorhexidine, ozone, iodine, super-oxidized water) periodically or continuously; physical measures (filtration, flushing, reverse osmosis). Anti-retraction valves fitted distal to handpieces and air/water syringes prevent suck-back, but 51.72% of DCUs failed retraction evaluation from valve failure after months. Retraction volume should not exceed 40 μL. Specialty-driven differences likely reflect suck-back of oral microorganisms and hydrodynamic stress differences.

CONCLUSIONS
Bacterial concentrations 0–3,816,000 CFU/mL show DUWL contamination requires attention. Proteobacteria dominant; Pseudomonas and Acinetobacter human-pathogenic. Disinfection, scheduled maintenance, and water monitoring required. Periodontics had distinct community structure from endodontics/prosthodontics, likely due to suck-back interactions and hydrodynamic stress.
