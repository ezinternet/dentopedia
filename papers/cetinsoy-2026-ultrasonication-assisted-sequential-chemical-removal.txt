"Ultrasonication-Assisted Sequential Chemical Removal of Mature Biofilm on Retrieved Titanium Healing Abutments."
Clin Implant Dent Relat Res. 2026;28(2):e70131. (e-pub 2026 Apr)

== ABSTRACT (full) ==
Aim: Investigate effectiveness of ultrasonication (US) with different chemical solutions on decontamination of titanium healing abutments (HAs) and their surface characteristics.

Methods: 95 HAs (two distinct brands/designs) retrieved from patients after 4–6 weeks. Precleaned by immersion in enzymatic detergent (ED), randomized into 5 groups:
- Control: autoclave only.
- US in 40°C tap water + autoclave.
- US in 40°C 1% sodium hypochlorite (SH) + autoclave.
- US in 80°C citric + maleic + lactic acid (CA) solution + autoclave.
- US in 40°C ED + autoclave.
HAs stained + photographed (lateral + occlusal) before/after decontamination; stained areas calculated; SEM + EDX for surface morphology and elemental composition.

Results:
- Control (autoclave only): LOWEST debridement (mean 54.4%), highest residual contamination.
- SH: almost entirely removed debris — mean 99.7% (Brand1 99.6%, Brand2 99.9%), outperforming all groups (p<0.05).
- SEM/EDS: SH-decontaminated surface morphology + elemental composition similar to UNUSED HAs.

Conclusion:
- After precleaning by soaking in ED, 1% SH + US is preferred for decontaminating HAs — superior cleaning + minimal surface alteration regardless of HA macrogeometry.
- Where placing unused HAs is not feasible, this THREE-STEP protocol (ED soak → US + 1% SH → autoclave) may be an efficient, cost-effective alternative for AT LEAST ONE reuse cycle.

Study type: In vitro (mature biofilm, 2 brands). Newest in the set (2026).
