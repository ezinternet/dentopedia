Characteristics and impacts of dental implant displacement into the maxillary sinus
Lee C, Kim DY, Han JJ
Journal of Dental Sciences 2025;20(2):775-782 (e-pub 2024-10-22)
DOI: 10.1016/j.jds.2024.10.013  PMID: 40224111  PMCID: PMC11993010
Seoul National University, School of Dentistry / SNU Dental Hospital, Dept. of OMFS, Korea
Source: PubMed Central full text (JATS), retrieved via PubMed MCP.

=== ABSTRACT ===
Background/purpose: Dental implants displaced into the maxillary sinus are considered to require
surgical removal as the treatment of choice, but displaced implants affect the sinus in different
ways. This study evaluated characteristics and impacts of implant displacement into the maxillary
sinus.
Materials/methods: Fourteen sinuses with displaced implants were included. Demographic and
radiographic data were analyzed, focusing on the impact of demographics, timing of displacement,
duration of retention, and residual bone height on sinus opacification and sinus wall thickness.
Results: Implants displaced during/early after placement in 9 cases and after functional loading in
5. Higher sinus opacification (p=0.019) and increased wall thickness (p=0.198, ns) in displacement
after functional loading. Longer retention -> higher opacification/wall thickness (ns). Univariate
regression: timing of displacement significantly influenced opacification (p=0.013) and wall
thickness (p=0.013). In all cases, no evidence of site preparation or short implants.
Conclusion: Adequate pre-implant site preparation and timely intervention are essential to prevent
and manage implant displacement into the maxillary sinus.

=== INTRODUCTION ===
Posterior maxilla is type IV bone (thin cortex, low-density cancellous) -> poor quality, low primary
stability. Post-extraction resorption + sinus pneumatization -> insufficient quantity. Complications
in posterior-maxilla implants include impaired osseointegration, membrane perforation, oroantral
fistula, paranasal sinusitis, and displacement of graft/implant into the sinus. Reported displacement
cases rose ~17-fold: 13 cases (1990-2000) -> 222 cases (2011-2021). Symptoms: purulent nasal
discharge, obstruction, foul smell, facial swelling; rarely pansinusitis, intracranial infection, or
migration into nasal cavity/other sinuses. Surgical removal = gold standard, via three approaches:
Caldwell-Luc, lateral window, endoscopic endonasal. Most prior studies focused on surgical technique
and on pre-loading displacement; impact on the sinus and post-loading displacement understudied.

=== METHODS ===
Retrospective cohort (Helsinki, IRB SNU Dental Hospital ERI24010). Patients treated Oct 2020-Jan 2024
for implant migration into maxillary sinus. Inclusion: retrieval of displaced implant + pre/postop
radiographs (panoramic, Waters, CT). Recorded: demographics (age, sex, smoking, systemic disease),
pre-implant surgical history, site, timing of displacement, retention duration.
Surgery: modified ENDOSCOPIC-ASSISTED sinus surgery for retrieval +/- oroantral communication closure
under IV sedation/GA. 1.5-2 cm vestibular mucoperiosteal incision, flap; two vertical osteotomies on
anterolateral sinus wall to create a bony window sized for a four-hole microplate; microplate applied,
then superior+inferior horizontal osteotomies; microplate-attached bony window separated and stored in
warm saline; horizontal incision on sinus membrane (No.12 scalpel); implant located under endoscope;
removed with suction tip, sinus forceps, or curettes; inflamed mucosa selectively removed; warm
irrigation; bony window repositioned and fixed with microscrews; closure 4-0 Vicryl.
Radiographic: implant type (fixture only vs fixture+abutment) on panoramic; location + prior sinus
graft on CT; residual alveolar bone height (coronal CT). Sinus opacification score 0-4 (0 none,
1 mucosal thickening, 2 partial <70%, 3 subtotal 70-<100%, 4 total 100%). Sinus wall thickness
measured 8 mm above sinus floor at anterior+lateral wall midpoints (mean); >=3 mm indicates osteitis.
Postop healing on Waters at 6 months. Stats: SPSS 26; Mann-Whitney U, Fisher exact, Wilcoxon signed
rank; univariate linear regression; p<0.05.

=== RESULTS ===
15 underwent removal; 13 met criteria (2 excluded, no CT). One patient displaced twice (different
sites/times) -> 14 displacements. 8 male (61.5%), 5 female; mean age 64.6 +/- 9.9 (44-78). 10 smokers,
1 non-smoker, 2 unknown. Systemic: 5 none; 4 hypertension, 3 diabetes. Original sites: maxillary 2nd
molar 9 (64.3%), 1st molar 5 (35.7%). Timing: 9 during peri-implant surgery phase, 5 after functional
loading. Retention: 7 <3 months, 7 >3 months.
Radiographic: 12 implants within sinus, 2 across sinus floor. Residual alveolar bone height
4.7 +/- 1.9 mm (perioperative 3.9 +/- 1.6 vs post-loading 5.9 +/- 1.8, p=0.067).
- Sinus opacification: mean 2.4 +/- 1.5 preop; POST-LOADING 3.6 +/- 0.9 vs SURGERY 1.7 +/- 1.3
  (p=0.019, significant). Preop: complete opacification 5 (35.7%), subtotal 2 (14.3%), partial/none 7
  (50.0%). Postop Waters improved in ALL; 11 with postop CT dropped to 0.4 +/- 0.8 at 6 months
  (p=0.002).
- Sinus wall thickness: 1.6 +/- 0.8 mm (displaced) vs 1.1 +/- 0.2 mm (normal side), p=0.002. By
  timing: post-loading 2.3 +/- 1.1 vs perioperative 1.2 +/- 0.2 (p=0.198, ns). OSTEITIS (>=3 mm) in
  2/5 (40%) post-loading cases, 0 in perioperative.
- By retention: >3 months (opacification 3.0 +/- 1.2; wall 2.0 +/- 1.1) > <3 months (1.7 +/- 1.6;
  1.2 +/- 0.2) but ns (p=0.165; p=0.209).
Univariate regression -> preop sinus opacification: AGE (p=0.034, coef 0.088), DIABETES (p=0.025, coef
2.091), TIMING of displacement (p=0.013, coef 1.933). Sex/other systemic/retention/residual
bone/smoking not significant. Sinus wall thickness -> only TIMING significant (p=0.013, coef 1.094).

=== DISCUSSION / KEY POINTS ===
Displacement can irritate mucosa / obstruct ostiomeatal complex -> sinusitis, onset delayed months-
years; not all symptomatic. Literature sinusitis incidence 33-48%; many asymptomatic (Chiapasco 14/27
no foreign-body reaction/infection). Here 50% partial/no opacification preop. Post-loading displacement
= more severe opacification (possible pre-existing peri-implantitis odontogenic sinusitis worsening;
oral-pathogen-contaminated implants/abutments; residual cement). Chronic sinusitis increases normal
wall (0.1-0.9 mm) to ~2.6 mm (osteitis = neo-osteogenesis); osteitis linked to poorer ESS response
(worse prognosis). Post-loading cases had thicker walls (2 patients >3.0 mm = osteitis); perioperative
all <1.5 mm -> early surgical intervention advised for post-loading displacement.
Risk factors for displacement: insufficient bone volume, poor quality, low primary stability,
inadequate drilling, unfavorable occlusal load, osseointegration failure, surgical inexperience. Here
mean residual bone <5.0 mm yet NO site preparation / short-implant use in any of 14 cases. Consistent
with Galindo-Moreno (bone <6 mm in most; ~73.3% no grafting) and an SR (>half of 182 patients no
pre-implant surgery) -> inadequate site prep with insufficient bone raises displacement risk.
Limitations: small n; rare complication; no histopathology; osteitis extent/treatment consensus
lacking.

=== CONCLUSION ===
To prevent displacement, perform adequate bone grafting with a site-specific approach after thorough
preop analysis of residual alveolar bone. When displacement occurs, remove the implant promptly for a
more favorable surgical outcome (especially after functional loading).
