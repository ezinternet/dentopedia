Title: An Umbrella Review of the Association Between Periodontal Disease and Diabetes Mellitus
Authors: Heber Isac Arbildo-Vega, Fredy Hugo Cruzado-Oliva, Edward Demer Infantes-Ruíz, Franz Tito Coronel-Zubiate, Eric Giancarlo Becerra-Atoche, Wilfredo Terrones-Campos, Paul Martín Herrera-Plasencia, Oscar Alex Seminario-Trelles, Roberto Enrique Ortega-Gallegos
Journal: Healthcare (Basel) 2024;12(22):2311
DOI: 10.3390/healthcare12222311 | PMID: 39595508 | PMCID: PMC11593929

=====================================================================

ABSTRACT
Aim: To determine the clinical association between periodontal disease and diabetes mellitus through an umbrella review. Materials and Methods: A search for publications up to August 2023 was conducted using the following electronic databases: PubMed, Cochrane Database, Scopus, SciELO, Google Scholar, and OpenGrey. We included systematic reviews (SRs) with or without meta-analysis evaluating primary studies that investigated the association between periodontal disease and diabetes mellitus, and there were no time or language restrictions. Literature or narrative reviews, rapid reviews, intervention studies, observational studies, preclinical and basic research, abstracts, comments, case reports, protocols, personal opinions, letters, and posters were excluded. The AMSTAR-2 tool was used to determine the methodological quality of the included studies. Results: The preliminary search yielded a total of 577 articles, of which only 17 remained after discarding those that did not meet the selection criteria. Following their analysis, an association between periodontal disease and diabetes mellitus (type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus and gestational diabetes mellitus) was found. Conclusions: The findings and conclusions of this umbrella review indicate with high confidence that periodontal disease is associated with the onset of type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus and gestational diabetes.

=====================================================================

1. Introduction
Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a common metabolic disease resulting from a defect in either insulin secretion or insulin action, or a combination of both. This chronic disease is one of the most common in the world, and its prevalence is expected to increase by almost 50% in the coming years. It is estimated that by 2045, there will be around 700 million people living with diabetes, which will be one of the five main causes of death. Mortality in people with diabetes occurs due to complications, of which the most important include microvascular changes, neuropathy, retinopathy, nephropathy, delayed healing, and periodontitis.

Periodontitis is a disease characterized by chronic inflammation of the entire periodontium that can irreparably destroy the tissue surrounding the tooth, leading to gingival bleeding, increased tooth mobility, and tooth loss. It is estimated that approximately between 20 and 60% of the world's population suffers from periodontal disease.

DM and periodontal disease (PD) are associated, despite lacking a common pathophysiology. However, glucose alteration in these patients shows a positive association, which is evident in patients with poorly controlled diabetes having an 86% higher risk of developing periodontitis than non-diabetics or those with well-controlled diabetes. Furthermore, PD may be a risk factor for diabetes mellitus, suggesting a possible bidirectional association. The proposed link between the two diseases is based on characteristics of the immune response, neutrophil function, and cytokine biology.

The need for dentists to understand the associations and short- and long-term consequences of these diseases necessitates a deeper comprehension of the scientific basis using the highest quality evidence available for clinical decision-making. To date, no comprehensive synthesis or evaluation of all systematic reviews, including those from recent years, has been performed. The aim of this overview is to consolidate the available evidence and address: "What is our current understanding of the link between periodontal disease and diabetes mellitus?" In addition, the general confidence in the systematic reviews analyzing this issue is assessed.

2. Materials and Methods
2.1. Protocol and Registration — Executed per PRISMA-P and registered in PROSPERO (CRD42023464043). PRIO-harms provided the reporting basis. Methodology based on Pauletto et al. Ethical approval not required.
2.2. Eligibility — Included: systematic reviews (SRs) assessing primary research on the PD–DM relationship, with or without meta-analysis, no time/language constraints. Excluded: abstracts, remarks, case reports, protocols, personal opinions, letters, posters, rapid reviews, intervention studies, observational studies, preclinical/fundamental research, and literature/narrative reviews.
2.3. Information Sources — Electronic search across PubMed, Cochrane Database, SciELO, and Scopus completed 20 August 2023. Gray literature via OpenGrey and Google Scholar. Reference lists examined. Duplicates removed, exported to Zotero.
2.4. Selection — Rayyan software. Phase 1: two reviewers (F.C.O., E.I.) screened titles/abstracts. Phase 2: full-text reading by same two reviewers. Third reviewer (F.C.Z.) resolved disagreements.
2.5. Data Collection — Independent duplicate extraction by two reviewers (E.B., W.T.), cross-checked, disagreements resolved by third author (P.H.). Extracted: authors, year, study design, design of primary studies, number of studies (qualitative/quantitative), country, type of diabetes, results, conclusions, and use of meta-analysis, PRISMA, PROSPERO, GRADE.
2.6. Quality — AMSTAR-2 checklist, calibrated to Kappa 0.85, applied in duplicate by two reviewers (O.S., R.O.). 16 questions (yes/no/partial). Confidence classified high/moderate/low/critically-low per Shea et al.
2.7–2.8. Measurement/Results Summary — Meta-analysis findings considered as mean difference, standardized mean difference (SMD), relative risk (RR), odds ratio (OR). Findings categorized: number of teeth, clinical attachment level (CAL), prevalence, general association, plaque index, gingival index, probing depth (PD/PPD), bleeding on probing (BOP).

3. Results
3.1. Selection — After removing duplicates, 519 references remained of 572. Phase 1 selected 12 papers for full reading. After adding 2 from other umbrella reviews and 4 for various reasons, 18 SRs remained for synthesis. (Abstract states 17 after selection criteria.)
3.2. Characteristics — Included SRs published in English 2009–2023. Conducted in Portugal, Peru, Malaysia, China (2), Germany, Italy (3), Australia, Japan, Denmark, the Netherlands, Spain, Brazil (2), and the United States (2).
3.3. Methodological Quality — 13 SRs classified high confidence, 1 SR low confidence, 4 SRs critically low confidence (AMSTAR-2).
3.4. Overlapping — 296 primary studies identified within the SRs. 17.26% of primary studies included in more than one SR. 35 studies included twice, 8 three times, 7 four times.
3.6. General Association — 11 of the included SRs reported an association between PD and DM; 2 SRs reported no association. Meta-analysis in 6 SRs: relative risk ratio 1.26 (CI 1.12–1.41) to 1.86 (CI 1.25–2.77); odds ratio 1.69 (CI 0.68–4.21) to 2.59 (CI 2.12–3.15). Costa, León-Ríos, Nguyen, Graziani, Ismail, Borgnakke reported association descriptively; Tanaka reported no association.
3.7. Plaque Index — 3 SRs (meta-analysis) reported association: mean difference 0.20 (CI 0.18–0.23); SMD 0.54 (CI 0.20–0.87) to 0.71 (CI 0.19–1.22).
3.8. Gingival Index — 2 SRs (meta-analysis): SMD 0.46 (CI 0.08–0.84) to 0.63 (CI 0.39–0.87).
3.9. Clinical Attachment Level — 4 SRs reported association; 1 SR reported association with T2DM but not T1DM. Meta-analysis in all: mean difference 0.26 (CI 0.00–0.53) to 1.00 (CI 0.15–1.84); SMD 0.47 (CI 0.37–0.57) to 0.82 (CI 0.59–1.04).
3.10. Number of Teeth — 1 SR (meta-analysis): mean difference −2.14 (CI −2.87 to −1.40).
3.11. Prevalence — 3 SRs reported association. Meta-analysis in 2: odds ratio 0.19 (CI 0.08–0.37) to 1.85 (CI 1.61–2.11). Mauri-Obradors reported descriptively.
3.12. Probing Depth — 3 SRs reported association; 1 SR reported association with T2DM but not T1DM. Meta-analysis: mean difference 0.11 (CI −0.03 to 0.25) to 0.46 (CI 0.01–0.91); SMD 0.36 (CI 0.16–0.55) to 0.67 (CI 0.23–1.11).
3.13. Bleeding on Probing — 3 SRs reported association. Meta-analysis: mean difference 7.90 (CI 4.24–11.56); SMD 0.32 (CI 0.07–0.58) to 0.65 (CI 0.08–1.23).

4. Discussion
The association between PD and systemic diseases such as DM has drawn interest; numerous RCTs support an association. Three prior umbrella reviews reviewed the PD–DM relationship, but they also included studies analyzing whether periodontal therapy was effective in people with DM, so results must be interpreted with caution. This study identified 18 SRs on the association of PD with DM (T1DM, T2DM, GDM). SRs are the highest evidence level but must be evaluated cautiously for bias.

Limitations of included SRs: different study types, varying inclusion criteria, different population groups (children, adolescents, adults), different periodontal diagnostic criteria, and different diabetes types (T1DM, T2DM, GDM). More than 50% of included studies showed high confidence. AMSTAR-2 critical domains 2, 4, 7, 9, 13, 15 were problematic in some SRs (methods not registered, non-exhaustive search, no justification for excluded studies, unsatisfactory risk-of-bias technique, RoB not considered in interpretation, publication bias not reported). High overlap (studies included multiple times) may skew findings; well-conducted long-term RCTs from different groups are more valuable than new SRs.

4.1. Evidence Summary — Included SRs support a general PD–DM association, consistent with the EFP Consensus Report that periodontitis is associated with DM. Two studies reported no association: one focused only on the Japanese population (little evidence for that population); the other found the association supported by cross-sectional but not case–control studies. Patients with DM show higher probability of increases in plaque index, gingival index, missing teeth, prevalence, BOP, probing depth, and CAL. One study found PD associated with T2DM but not T1DM. Of included studies: 4 analyzed T1DM (1 high confidence); 3 analyzed GDM (all high confidence); 10 analyzed DM generally (8 high confidence).
4.2. Clinical Practice — Given the PD–DM association, accurate anamnesis and correct diagnosis are critical; least-invasive options such as scaling and root planing, lasers, and local antimicrobials are indicated.
4.3. Research — Immediate need for improved SR reporting quality; use quality-evaluation tools; use latest periodontal diagnostic definitions in primary studies.

5. Conclusions
There is strong evidence supporting the association between periodontal disease and various forms of diabetes mellitus — type 1, type 2, and gestational diabetes. The relationship is consistent across clinical parameters (plaque index, gingival index, CAL, probing depth, BOP), with diabetes patients showing higher likelihood of worsened periodontal conditions. Two studies did not find an association, highlighting the need for further research addressing differences in populations and methodologies.
