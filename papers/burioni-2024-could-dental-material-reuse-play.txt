Burioni R, Silvestrini L, D'Orto B, Tetè G, Nagni M, Polizzi E, Gherlone EF.
"Could Dental Material Reuse Play a Significant Role in Preservation of Raw Materials, Water, Energy, and Costs? Microbiological Analysis of New versus Reused Healing Abutments: An In Vitro Study."
Bioengineering (Basel). 2024;11(4):387.

== ABSTRACT ==
Aim: Compare reused+sterilized versus new healing abutments (HAs) to assess whether a decontamination and sterilization process removes residual proteins; whether reused HAs are as safe as new.
Materials/methods: Sept 2022–Oct 2023. HA screws divided into new vs previously-used-in-patients. Subjected to a decontamination+sterilization protocol; sterility evaluation + surface protein levels recorded.
Results: Significant difference in OD562 nm between new and reused HAs. Treated HAs still contaminated by residual proteins.
Conclusion: Although sterilization achieves total eradication of pathogens (from an infectious standpoint), surface proteins remain on reused healing abutments.

== KEY FULL-TEXT POINTS ==
Introduction:
- Manufacturers widely acknowledge HAs as essential DISPOSABLE (single-use) components.
- Reuse motivations: saving titanium, cost reduction, pollution reduction, WHO 2030 sustainability goals.
- Main reason for "disposable" designation: intrinsic susceptibility to contamination and thread wear → can harm the implant internal structure, screw engagement area, titanium composition.
- Analogy: stainless steel burs, titanium drills are reusable per manufacturer (ultrasonic bath + autoclave). So reusability is not unheard of in dentistry.
- Central concern: contamination post-use. Plaque biofilm + gingival epithelium + blood + saliva → free proteins/amino acids firmly attached to titanium, hard to remove. Residual debris disrupts abutment–implant alignment, introduces contaminants into peri-implant soft tissue → immune/inflammatory response from denatured proteins.
- Second concern: multiple sterilization cycles → surface alterations → weakened transmucosal barrier → bacterial infiltration → implant failure.

Materials/Methods:
- IRCCS San Raffaele Hospital, Milan. STROBE cohort guideline.
- HAs: Winsix, Biosafin (Cefla, Imola, Italy).
- One HA per patient, taken at implant reopening (bone healing 4–5 mo upper, 3 mo lower).
- Test group (TG) = 40 reused+sterilized; Control group (CG) = 35 new.
- Sterility: extraction under laminar flow, deposit in Tryptone Soya Broth, incubate 24 h at 37°C, turbidity = growth.
- Surface protein: Micro BCA assay, OD 562 nm spectrophotometry.
- Statistics: SPSS v20, Shapiro-Wilk, one-way ANOVA, independent t-test, Tukey adjustment, α=0.05.

Results:
- Sterility: NO turbidity in any sample (TG or CG). Protocol effective at removing infective agents / bacteria.
- Residual protein: reused HAs coated with surface proteins even after sterilization.
- Average residual protein in TG eluate = 23.88 µg/mL; total solution 500 µL → ~12 µg protein per screw.
- One-way ANOVA F(2,45)=7.34, p<0.01.
- Independent t-test new vs reused: t(30)=4.52, p<0.001.
- Mean surface protein: reused 18.76 µg/mL (SD 3.62) vs new 9.35 µg/mL (SD 2.15); mean difference 9.41 µg/mL (95% CI 4.28–14.54).

Discussion (cites prior literature):
- Concordant: a 120-HA study (autoclave alone vs +airflow polishing vs +NaOCl) → significant debris in first two, minimal staining with NaOCl → decontamination achievable with specific procedures.
- Bidra et al. SR: 6 studies; 3 said routine methods insufficient, 2 with additional regimen achieved complete removal, 1 said routine sufficient.
- Wadhwani et al.: 100 HAs from 8 clinics; even after sterilization, 99% contaminated with proteins/peptides → transmission concern.
- Stacchi et al.: 30 test HAs (mechanical+ultrasonic+autoclave) → 11 contaminated; all control HAs (automated cleaning + autoclave) contaminated.
- Sanchez-Garces: 55 used+sterilized HAs, no bacterial growth but significant debris (total organic carbon).
- Sennerby et al. (in vivo rat): used Ti screws → ↑ activated macrophages + fibrous encapsulation vs new.
- Cakan et al.: 60 used+sterilized HAs; contaminated screw grooves in 10.5%, debris-filled driver slots in 5.2%.

Conclusion:
- Infectiously: reuse could be considered safe (bacteria more resistant than viruses; all sterile).
- Protein: sterilization does NOT completely remove proteins; clinical significance of the new-vs-reused difference cannot be determined a priori.
- Call for further study on other sterilization procedures + biomechanical implications.
