Title: Single implants in dorsal areas - A systematic review.
Authors: Moy PK, Nishimura GH, Pozzi A, Danda AK
Year: 2016
Journal: European Journal of Oral Implantology, 9 Suppl 1:S163-72

ABSTRACT:

This study evaluated the efficacy of replacing single missing teeth in the posterior quadrants of the maxilla and/or mandible with an implant-supported dental prosthesis.

Three databases (MEDLINE/PubMed, Ovid MEDLINE, Cochrane CENTRAL) searched for publications from 1985 to 2014. 141 articles reviewed; 36 articles met inclusion criteria.

Results for immediate implant placement:
- Survival rate: 96.9%
- Success rate: 100%
- Mean bone loss: 0.85 mm

Results for delayed implant placement:
- Survival rate: 96.8%
- Success rate: 94.1%
- Mean bone loss: 0.55 mm

Results from studies comparing immediate versus delayed implant placement:
- Survival rates: 96.8% (immediate) vs 96.3% (delayed)
- Success rates: 85.8% (immediate) vs 93.3% (delayed)
- Mean bone loss: 0.57 ± 0.57 mm (immediate) vs 0.55 ± 0.37 mm (delayed)

Conclusions: Single molar implants provide a viable treatment option for replacing a single missing tooth in posterior quadrants of maxilla and mandible. No significant difference in survival rates between immediately placed vs delayed implant placement. Success rates slightly higher with delayed loading protocols than immediate loading protocols.

Scope: posterior quadrant single-tooth replacement; includes molar and premolar regions.
