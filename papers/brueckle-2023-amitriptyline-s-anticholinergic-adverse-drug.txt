Amitriptyline's anticholinergic adverse drug reactions - A systematic multiple-indication review and meta-analysis. PLoS One 2023;18(4):e0284168. Brueckle MS, Thomas ET, Seide SE, Pilz M, Gonzalez-Gonzalez AI, Dinh TS, Gerlach FM, Harder S, Glasziou PP, Muth C.

ABSTRACT: Half the US population uses drugs with anticholinergic properties. Amitriptyline is among the most frequently prescribed anticholinergic medicinal products, used for multiple indications. Objective: quantify (anticholinergic) ADRs in patients taking amitriptyline vs placebo in RCTs of adults. Databases searched to 09/2022; two reviewers selected RCTs with >=100 participants >=18 y comparing oral amitriptyline vs placebo for all indications. Twenty-three RCTs (5-300 mg/day) and 4217 patients (mean age 40.3 y) included. Most frequent anticholinergic ADRs: dry mouth, drowsiness, somnolence, sedation, fatigue. Random-effects MA: anticholinergic ADRs OR = 7.41 (95% CI 4.54 to 12.12) amitriptyline vs placebo. Non-anticholinergic ADRs as frequent as placebo. Meta-regression: not dose-dependent. Registration PROSPERO CRD42020111970.

METHODS (abridged): PRISMA; part of EVITA (German polypharmacy guideline update). MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO, PsycLIT, Psyndex, CENTRAL from inception to Sept 2022; citation analysis; grey literature; FDA/EMA/trial registries. Included randomized double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs of oral amitriptyline, any indication/dose/duration, >=100 adults, group-wise quantitative ADR data. RoB 2. Primary outcome: frequency of ADRs indicative of anticholinergic activity (ACH-ADRs) as binary outcome; also non-anticholinergic ADRs (NACH-ADRs) and general unspecific ADRs (G-ADRs). Odds ratio with 95% CI; risk difference (RD) and number needed to harm (NNH) for primary outcomes. Random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird). Meta-regression on dose; subgroups: sex, dose (50-99, 100-150, >150 mg), form, indication. Analysis only if >=5 studies; funnel plots if >=10.

RESULTS: 1,898 records screened, 471 full texts reviewed, 23 studies included. Published 1970-2018. Indications: depressive disorders (n=13), pain disorders (n=9), functional dyspepsia (n=1). Flexible dosing in 20/23; doses 5-300 mg/day; titration period 1-12 weeks, median 8. No study focused on older persons or multimorbidity; none reported adverse drug events. 4217 patients (67% female), mean age 40.34 y. RoB: 17 high, 4 medium, 2 low; 15 of the 17 high-RoB studies had attrition >=20%.
Primary - ACH-ADRs: 20 studies, 3,510 participants. Most frequently reported: dry mouth (9 studies), drowsiness (4), somnolence (2), sedation (2), fatigue (1), constitutional (1), unspecific (1). OR = 7.41 [4.54, 12.12], NNH = 2.89; RD = 0.35 [0.26, 0.46]; I2 = 83% (OR), 97% (RD). Stable in sensitivity analyses. Dose not a predictor (p = .40). Funnel plot no significant asymmetry (p = .27).
Primary - NACH-ADRs: 14 studies, 2442 participants. OR = 1.18 [0.74, 1.89], NNH = 34.27; RD = 0.03 [-0.02, 0.07]; I2 44%. Funnel plot asymmetry significant (p = .01).
Primary - G-ADRs: 21 studies, 3,437 participants. OR = 3.74 [2.69, 5.20], NNH = 6.23; RD = 0.16 [0.10, 0.22]; I2 49% (OR), 87% (RD).

Nine aggregated ADRs more frequent with amitriptyline, five inconclusive, none more frequent with placebo.

DISCUSSION (abridged): First SR comparing amitriptyline ADRs vs placebo across all indications. Odds of anticholinergic ADRs about seven times higher; general ADRs about four times. Headache favored amitriptyline, possibly due to migraine prophylaxis indication. No dose dependence found, but most studies used individual titration and did not report the dose at ADR occurrence. Leucht et al. (depression) reported OR 6.33 for anticholinergic ADRs, OR 13.50 for dry mouth, and OR 8.73 for genitourinary ADRs vs 4.78 here. Strength: placebo-controlled RCTs only. Limitations: >=100 participant criterion excluded 269 small studies; mean age ~40 limits generalizability to older people, who are more sensitive to anticholinergic effects; cognitive decline and falls not reported. Conclusion: about one in three patients (mean age ~40) on amitriptyline experience anticholinergic ADRs (RD 0.35, NNH 2.89); harm should be weighed against benefit and communicated to patients.
