The Causes of Dental Implant Migration into the Maxillary Sinus: A Case Series Study from 25 Years of Experience
Mahmood Hashemi H, Mohammadi S, Razmara F
Journal of Dentistry (Shiraz, Iran) 2024;25(1):86-90
DOI: 10.30476/dentjods.2023.95807.1898  PMID: 38544771  PMCID: PMC10963871
Tehran University of Medical Sciences, School of Dentistry, OMFS / Craniomaxillofacial Research Center, Iran
Source: PubMed Central full text (JATS), retrieved via PubMed MCP.

=== ABSTRACT ===
Dental implants to restore edentulous jaws are commonplace. In the maxilla, following tooth
extraction, alveolar bone height decreases, increasing the risk of implants migrating into the sinus.
Caldwell-Luc and endoscopic surgery are used to retrieve implants. This case series collected data
from 39 patients with implant displacement into the maxillary sinus over 25 years. All implants were
removed using the Caldwell-Luc technique. Migration happened following functional loading, during the
prosthetic procedure due to lack of osseointegration in 3 patients, and during implant placement into
a fresh socket in 3 patients. In the remaining cases, migration occurred preoperatively or
postoperatively prior to implant loading. Insufficient bone quantity sometimes causes migration; in
case of minimal bone height, a sinus lift before implant placement should be conducted. Retrieval via
the Caldwell-Luc approach proved a reliable technique.

=== INTRODUCTION ===
Posterior maxillary tooth loss -> edentulism, bone resorption. Implant placement is the treatment of
choice (safe, excellent prognosis). Panoramic + CBCT before insertion are imperative to assess
residual bone volume/landmarks. If ridge height is inadequate, sinus elevation (sinus lift) should be
performed. Failure to plan/assess bone may cause complications such as implant dislocation into the
sinus. Migration can induce sensory disorder, sinusitis, oroantral fistula; the dislocated implant
disturbs sinus anatomy and the mucociliary escalator. Migration can occur during placement or
postoperatively (during osseointegration, after load, or during prosthetic restoration). Contributing
factors: sinus pneumatization, extremely resorbed bone, excessive bite force, bruxism, absence of
primary stability, inexperienced surgeon, over-drilling, intense tapping, peri-implantitis bone loss,
and considerable pressure on non-integrated implant removal. Instant removal is generally suggested;
if delayed, control sinusitis first. Surgical approaches: Caldwell-Luc antrostomy (intraoral, through
a bony window in the lateral wall; better visualization, easier access, can be done under local
anesthesia) and endoscopic sinus surgery (transnasal via the ostium; general anesthesia, more
expensive equipment, longer operation).

=== METHODS / CASE PRESENTATION ===
Ethics: Tehran University of Medical Sciences IR.TUMS.DENTISTRY.REC.1401.036. Over 25 years
(1996-2021), 39 patients (21 female, 18 male) referred to a private clinic for implant displacement
into the maxillary antrum. Age range 28-73 (F), 30-70 (M). ALL removed via Caldwell-Luc under local
anesthesia by a single proficient surgeon.
Preop (48 h before): Co-amoxiclav 625 mg TID + 0.2% chlorhexidine (CHX) rinse. Local anesthesia;
osteotomy with dental carbide bur #5 under constant saline irrigation to create a bony window in the
lateral antral wall; incision in sinus membrane; implant retrieved by suction or rongeur; flap sutured
4-0 Vicryl. Postop: antibiotics (1 week), nasal decongestant spray, analgesic; avoid nose-blowing 2
weeks; 0.2% CHX rinse twice daily for 7 days.

=== RESULTS — CAUSES OF MIGRATION (n=39) ===
Displacement during implant placement (intraoperative) = 22 patients:
  - Inadequate bone height: 11 (6M, 5F)
  - Over-drilling / wide socket: 11 (4M, 7F)
  - Closed (crestal) sinus lift procedure: 6 (2M, 4F)
  - Immediate implantation into fresh extraction socket: 3 (2M, 1F)
Postoperative:
  - Placement of healing abutment (lack of osseointegration): 5 (3M, 2F)
  - Prosthetic procedures (inadequate osseointegration): 3 (1M, 2F)
[Table 1 grouping: Intraoperation = immediate implantation, inadequate bone height, closed sinus lift,
over-drilling; Post-operation = healing abutment placement, prosthetic procedures.]
Patient acceptance: 31 patients (13 men, 18 women) were RELUCTANT toward further implant placement
after displacement; the rest accepted resumption of reconstruction/implant placement.
Follow-up: no complications during 2-10 year post-surgery follow-up.

=== DISCUSSION ===
Prevention principles: dentists must recognize and manage this complication; accurate radiographic
assessment before retrieval to localize the implant. Migration can be intra- or postoperative
(before or after loading). Intraoperative displacement: low bone quality/quantity, inexpert surgeon,
over-preparation, heavy pressure during implantation, sinus perforation while drilling -> poor primary
stability (major reason at this stage); inadequate initial stability -> micromotion -> impaired
clotting/revascularization -> obstructed ossification. Postoperative before loading: loss of
osseointegration from bone infection, osteoporosis/osteopenia. After functional loading: erratic
masticatory force, destructive forces on surrounding bone, loading <3 weeks after placement.
Two management methods: Caldwell-Luc (intraoral anterolateral window) vs endoscopic (transnasal).
Caldwell-Luc is suggested for foreign-body retrieval, oroantral-fistula repair, failed endoscopic
surgery, mucous cell metaplasia. Authors used Caldwell-Luc for all cases. Comparators: Maciel (1 case,
insufficient bone height, Caldwell-Luc); Mumtaz (headache/nasal blockage 1 yr, Caldwell-Luc); Manor
(55 cases, sinus augmentation in 46 before placement, Caldwell-Luc in 52; sinusitis + oroantral
communication most common complications); Hamdoon (11 patients, Caldwell-Luc least challenging);
Sgaramella (21 cases/24 implants, displacement after op before load in 10, after loading in 1,
Caldwell-Luc in 8). Caldwell-Luc is the gold standard for sinus entry regarding mucosal regeneration,
better healing, less inflammation, local anesthesia, fewer complications vs endoscopy. Insufficient
bone height from pneumatization/alveolar loss is the leading cause; when bone height <5 mm, sinus lift
should be conducted before implant placement to prevent failure/migration (Sgaramella: <5 mm at 19/24
sites; sinus lift not done in 15/21). Most cases were male in several comparators (Mumtaz, Manor,
Hamdoon) though not Sgaramella. Displacement causes patient reluctance for re-treatment (here 31/39
reluctant) -> good pre-op bone evaluation prevents displacement and motivates completion of treatment.
Strength: no prior study across different implantation stages; extended follow-up.

=== CONCLUSION ===
A very thorough pre-operative bone assessment prevents such complications. With minimal bone height,
perform a sinus lift before placement to increase bone quantity/quality. Ensure primary stability at
insertion, and adequate osseointegration before impression-taking. Retrieval of a displaced implant
via the Caldwell-Luc approach proved a reliable technique with fewer complications.
