Immediate implant placement in a chronic periradicular resorptive lesion: A case report of timely restoration and successful outcomes

=== ABSTRACT ===

This case report presents the case of a 34-year-old male with a chronic periradicular abscess, fractured prosthesis, and significant mandibular bone resorption. The goal was to restore function and esthetics through immediate implant placement (IIP) despite infection and bone loss. Examination revealed Grade III mobility of teeth #19 and #20, a draining sinus, and extensive bone resorption confirmed by the cone-beam computed tomography. Using the Simple, Advanced, Complex classification, the case was classified into "complex" due to its pathology and poor bone quality. IIP was chosen after careful debridement and bone grafting with xenogeneic material. The implants were placed with primary stability, and a customized healing abutment was prepared for optimal soft-tissue healing. This case shows that IIP is effective in challenging conditions with infection and bone resorption, given careful planning, infection control, and grafting. Treatment was successful with long-term outcomes. Bone healing and implant integration were confirmed at follow-up, providing functional and esthetic restorations.

INTRODUCTION

Dental implantology has evolved significantly, offering predictable solutions for the rehabilitation of edentulous and partially edentulous patients. One major advancement is immediate implant placement (IIP) after tooth extraction. Traditionally debated, particularly for sites with active infection or significant bone loss, IIP is often delayed until infection resolution and bone healing. However, advances in surgical techniques, biomaterials, and implant design now enable immediate placement, even in compromised sites. The Simple, Advanced, Complex (SAC) classification system and cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) assessments allow better evaluation of the bone and surrounding tissues, providing a structured approach to treatment planning. Immediate implants are categorized as "Complex" when significant bone loss, infection, or other complicating factors are present. The key benefits of IIP include reduced treatment time, preservation of alveolar bone volume, and improved patient satisfaction. These must be weighed against risks, particularly implant failure in cases of infection or insufficient bone quality. A growing body of the literature explores the efficacy, indications, and outcomes of immediate implantation in compromised sites, including those with periradicular abscesses or extensive bone resorption. A recent study showed that IIP can be successful even with infection and bone loss, given proper infection control measures and patient selection. Xenogeneic bone grafts and systemic antibiotic therapy are crucial for procedure success, leading to favorable long-term outcomes. This case study aimed to discuss the rationale, benefits, and potential complications of IPP in compromised clinical scenarios, providing insights into factors influencing treatment success.

CASE REPORT

This case study adhered to the CAse REports guidelines. A 34-year-old male presented to the dental center with dull pain on chewing and persistent discharge in the mandibular left molar region. The patient was systemically healthy. Intraoral examination revealed fractured prosthesis #18, 19, and 20, along with a sinus draining into the buccal vestibule wrt #20. The initial probing depth was approximately 2-3 mm, while the width of the keratinized tissue was approximately 3 mm. CBCT revealed periapical radiolucency around teeth #19 and #20, indicative of a chronic periradicular abscess contributing to the Grade III mobility. The transverse section shows the extent of radiolucency (15 mm x 20 mm in size) toward the lingual aspect. The coronal section revealed a thinned lingual cortical plate (<0.5 mm) due to lesion around #19 and extensive buccal bone resorption around #20.

The patient was evaluated using the International Team of Implantology (ITI) assessment tool. The case was categorized as "COMPLEX," following the SAC classification system. The key determinants were the existing pathological condition and the quantity of bone available for initial stabilization. The bone quality was suboptimal despite adequate ridge width. The critical consideration was the implant placement schedule, as the patient had a 5-month window and sought implant-supported restoration. The patient was informed of all available treatment options but opted for immediate intervention owing to time constraints. After obtaining written informed consent, the decision to place an immediate implant was made based on the criteria #19 and #20.

Before surgery, the damaged prosthesis was removed. Gentle extraction was performed under local anesthesia (4% articaine with 1:100000 epinephrine) through inferior alveolar and buccal nerve blocks. The socket was debrided using Hufriedy CL85 Lucas DE surgical curette and CM10 Miller DE surgical curette. The granulation tissue was excised until a firm osseous surface was palpable with the curettes. Subsequently, the socket was irrigated with a saline solution until it appeared clinically clean. Two dental implants (5.0 mm x 13.5 mm and 6.5 mm x 13.5 mm) Megagen Anyridge were placed following the manufacturer's protocols. The rationale for selecting a wide diameter implant in this scenario is to secure primary stability from the adjacent bony walls, as the extensive defect precluded the presence of apical bone. After achieving primary stability (35 Ncm), the jumping distance (>1 mm) was filled with a xenogenic-derived bone graft (particle size: 250 microns). A customized healing abutment was prepared using a nanohybrid composite on temporary abutments and screwed onto both implants. Interrupted sutures were placed in the interdental region using 5-0 polyglactin 910 sutures. Steps for surgical procedure is depicted in Figures. The patient was instructed to perform a rinse with 0.2% chlorhexidine digluconate twice daily for the duration of 14 days, commencing 24 h postsurgery.

Owing to the extensive spread of the lesion, the patient was prescribed 1000 mg of augmentin (amoxicillin and clavulanic acid) twice daily and metronidazole 500 mg three times daily, along with the analgesic ibuprofen 400 mg three times daily for 5 days. To reduce postoperative discomfort, a single dose of intravenous dexamethasone 8 mg IM stat was administered after surgery. The sutures were removed 1 week postoperatively, and the patient reported no symptoms. Soft-tissue healing was adequate, with improvement observed during the 2-week follow-up. The patient was scheduled for monthly check-ups.

A CBCT scan 4 months postoperatively revealed exceptional healing around both implants, with a bone shield visible on the buccal and lingual aspects. The pathological lesion was fully healed, and significant bone formation was observed. An impression was created using the open-tray transfer technique. A fully monolithic zirconia restoration was fabricated, with the supragingival region glazed and the subgingival region ultra-polished, following a previously outlined protocol. This restoration was screwed onto the implants 4 months after placement. A ceramic crown was placed on tooth #18 to maintain proper interproximal contact and preserve the tissue architecture. CBCT scan 4 months postoperatively revealed exceptional healing around both implants. Immediate postoperative radiograph and the postrestorative radiograph wrt #19 and 20 show beginning of corticalization of the interim plant crestal bone.

The patient remained symptom-free with a fully functional prosthesis supported by implants at positions #19 and #20. A 2-year follow-up examination revealed healthy bone growth around the implant. The area around the implant platform exhibited cortical bone formation, indicating a healthy peri-implant environment. Further cortical bone development was observed near the implant-abutment junction at the 3-year follow-up, and the implants were completely functional.

DISCUSSION

IIP is widely accepted in dental implantology, offering reliable solutions for restoring edentulous areas, even with compromised bone or chronic infection. Historically, implant placement in infected sites faced hesitation due to osseointegration and implant failure concerns. Advancements in surgical techniques, biomaterials, and implant design have revolutionized immediate implantation, enabling clinicians to address complex conditions with favorable outcomes. The SAC system assesses the complexity of implant cases based on the clinical and radiographic findings. This case was classified into "complex" due to chronic infection, significant bone loss, and compromised tissue quality, emphasizing thorough preoperative planning. Detailed CBCT assessment is crucial for evaluating bone resorption and guiding the surgical approach. Cross-sectional imaging provided valuable information about lingual and buccal bone resorption and thinned cortical plates, guiding the surgical procedure. CBCT monitoring of bone healing is essential for evaluating implant placement success and confirming bone regeneration. Effective debridement of the infected socket is critical for reducing bacterial load and ensuring that the implant site is free from contaminants. The socket was meticulously cleaned using surgical curettes and irrigated with saline solution. Systemic antibiotics were administered for prophylaxis against potential postoperative infections. Antibiotic therapies such as amoxicillin and clavulanic acid are typically prescribed to manage infections and reduce the risk of peri-implantitis.

Infection-related bone loss can be managed with a combination of IIP and bone grafting. Xenografts have shown promising results in socket preservation. In this case, xenogenic bone grafts were used to fill the infection-created void and facilitate osseointegration. Studies report that xenografts combined with immediate implants offer effective support and encourage bone healing at compromised sites.

The patient experienced favorable outcomes despite infection and suboptimal bone quality, confirmed by radiographic findings showing significant bone formation around the implants after 4 months. The observed bone healing and cortical bone development around the implant platform over 2 years demonstrate the potential for immediate implant success in challenging scenarios. This is in agreement with studies showing that well-managed immediate implants can offer excellent long-term results even with infection present and that IIP is viable for restoring compromised areas, particularly when adequate primary stability is achieved. In this case, a torque of 35 Ncm secured the primary stability.

CBCT imaging 4 months postoperatively showed exceptional bone healing, with visible bone formation around the implants. This aligns with other studies indicating that with proper debridement, infection management, and bone grafting, immediate implants can achieve favorable healing outcomes. Further improvement was observed during the 2-year follow-up, with significant cortical bone formation around implants, validating the procedure's success. Studies have highlighted the long-term success of immediate implantation in infected sockets. A systematic review demonstrated a success rate comparable to noninfected sites, with a 96% survival rate over 5 years. Another study showed that immediate implants in infected sites with controlled infection and appropriate postoperative care had a 93% success rate.

The unique aspects of the reported case are as follows: Bone resorption occurs rapidly after tooth extraction, and implant placement into the extraction socket prevents vertical and horizontal bone loss, commonly seen at untreated sites. This is especially important in infected sockets, where bone preservation can significantly improve the esthetic and functional outcomes of implant restorations. We demonstrated the feasibility of IIP in complex conditions that are often contraindicated in traditional protocols. IIP preserves the alveolar ridge and surrounding soft tissues, which is crucial at sites of preexisting bone loss due to infection.

Combination of infection control and bone regeneration techniques: Using xenogeneic bone grafting to fill the gaps between implants, combined with immediate placement, is another unique aspect. Bone grafting in infected sites is typically avoided, but thorough debridement and grafting material help promote bone healing and implant stability.

Time-dependent treatment decision: The patient's need for rapid restoration within a 5-month window is unique. Urgency requires quick but effective evidence-based treatment decisions. Despite the complexity, the patient opted for immediate intervention, which was successfully performed within significant time constraints. This underscores the importance of patient-centered care and tailoring the treatment plan to meet specific needs and preferences. IIP reduces the need for a staged approach, shortening the total treatment time, which is beneficial for patients seeking rapid restoration and improved quality of life. This is especially relevant in patients with urgent functional needs or high esthetic concerns, such as the anterior maxilla. Immediate implants also offer psychological benefits, reduce the time patients are without a tooth, and eliminate the need for temporary restorations.

Successful long-term follow-up with full functional restoration: This case stands out for its successful long-term outcome, with full integration of implants and complete restoration of function. At the 2-year follow-up, cortical bone formation around the implant-abutment junction was evident, highlighting the predictability of immediate implantation, even with pathological conditions, when combined with proper treatment planning, surgical techniques, and postoperative care.

Use of custom immediate healing abutments: The creation of customized healing abutments using nanohybrid composite materials is noteworthy. In addition, using chairside customization allows for better adaptation to the individual patient's anatomy, improving tissue response and ensuring optimal healing conditions for implant integration.

The use of ultrapolished zirconia in the subgingival region promotes better epithelial adhesion, acting as an improved tissue barrier against microbial leakage into the connective tissue. This influences the overall tissue response and bone behavior around the implant platform. IIP allows for a significantly shorter treatment time than traditional approaches. Placing the implant immediately after tooth extraction prevents prolonged healing, leading to faster functional and esthetic restoration. The main advantage of IIP is alveolar ridge preservation. Placing the implant in the extraction socket minimizes bone resorption, which is particularly beneficial in cases of bone loss or resorption. This helps maintain anatomical ridge contours, providing a more predictable outcome for both the implant and the final restoration. IIP provides quicker resolution, reduces the need for temporary prostheses, and minimizes discomfort from edentulous periods. In this case, the patient opted for immediate intervention owing to time constraints, and the rapid restoration met his expectations. Immediate restoration of a missing tooth improves function and esthetics, particularly in the anterior region. Placing implants in teeth #19 and #20 was critical for restoring chewing function in a timely manner. Using xenogeneic bone grafting with IIP helped improve the bone volume and quality around the implant site, thus enhancing long-term success. One recent study evaluated the clinical outcomes (3-5 years) and found a success rate of 96% with an average mPES of 9.75 +/- 0.44.

Potential limitations of IIP in this case include the risk of implant failure due to infection or poor osseointegration, particularly at sites with existing infections (chronic periradicular abscess). Antibiotic therapy and thorough debridement were performed; however, active infection could cause complications, such as peri-implantitis or implant loss. While ridge width was adequate, suboptimal bone quality posed a challenge in achieving primary implant stability. The patient's bone quality could affect initial implant stability, increasing failure risk, or the need for additional interventions. A customized healing abutment was placed; however, immediate loading required high primary stability, which is difficult to achieve in infected or compromised bones. Insufficient primary stability can delay functional restoration. Despite careful surgical techniques, immediate implantation at compromised sites carries a higher risk of postoperative complications, including bleeding, swelling, and delayed healing. The patient did not report any symptoms during follow-up; however, complications were possible, especially extensive bone resorption and infection. The decision for immediate implantation was influenced by the patient's time constraints, necessitating an expedited treatment plan, which may limit the ability to fully manage potential complications such as infection control. Achieving optimal esthetic outcomes in cases of extensive bone loss, particularly in the anterior region, can be more challenging. This was not an issue in this case; however, esthetic results can be compromised if significant bone loss occurs or if the implant fails to integrate. IIP has gained acceptance for its potential to reduce treatment time and preserve bone volume.

CONCLUSION

IIP in infected sockets is viable, as scientific evidence supports its success in compromised scenarios, offering patients a functional, esthetic, and timely solution. Challenges remain in infection management; however, advancements in techniques and materials have made this approach safe and predictable. With proper patient selection and clinical management, immediate implantation in infected sockets improves bone preservation, treatment time, and patient satisfaction. IIP offers advantages in terms of treatment efficiency and patient satisfaction, as demonstrated in this case. However, risks include infections, compromised bone quality, and time constraints. This case report illustrates the successful treatment of a 34-year-old male with chronic periradicular abscesses and compromised mandibular molars following IIP. Assessment using the ITI tool and SAC classification system guided decision-making. The patient achieved excellent functional and esthetic outcomes with careful preoperative planning, meticulous surgical techniques, and postoperative care. Favorable bone healing and soft-tissue development observed during the long-term follow-up demonstrate the efficacy of immediate implants in challenging clinical scenarios.
