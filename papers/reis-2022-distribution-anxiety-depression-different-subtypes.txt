Source: PubMed PMID 35398904 (abstract only; no PMC full text). DOI 10.1111/joor.13331
Reis PHF, Laxe LAC, Lacerda-Santos R, Munchow EA. Distribution of anxiety and depression among different subtypes of temporomandibular disorder: A systematic review and meta-analysis. J Oral Rehabil 2022;49(7):754-767. Published 2022-04-22.

Background: The evidence on the relationship between anxiety and depression and patients with distinct subtypes of TMD is uncertain, so a thorough review study on the topic is still missing.
Objective: Investigate the distribution and severity of anxiety and depression in patients diagnosed with different subtypes of TMD.
Methods: Registered in PROSPERO (CRD42020150562), followed PRISMA 2020. Searched PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, SciELO (last search 12 March 2021) and reference lists. Eligibility: (i) TMD diagnosed with RDC/TMD or DC/TMD; (ii) anxiety and/or depression assessed with validated psychological instruments; (iii) allocation of patients into at least two distinct TMD subtypes with at least one group having myofascial pain (comparison group). Analyses in RevMan 5.3.5, random- or fixed-effects models (alpha 0.05). Quality of evidence assessed by a 10-item appraisal tool for prevalence studies and the Newcastle-Ottawa scale.
Results: 4086 records identified; 24 eligible; meta-analyses with 20 studies. Total 3678 subjects. Most studies found that patients with myofascial pain showed similar occurrence and severity of anxiety/depression compared with other TMD subtypes, although average prevalence seemed higher among diagnoses consisting of myofascial pain (muscular TMD). Despite moderate-to-high heterogeneity, anxiety and depression were more frequently distributed within patients with myofascial pain (p = .001). TMD patients without myofascial pain presented less severe anxiety and depression than patients with only myofascial pain (p <= .01). The type of psychological instrument seems to affect assessment of both states.
Conclusions: Patients with myofascial pain are more anxious and more depressed than patients with other TMD subtypes. Anxiety and depression are differently distributed within the TMD population; proper assessment of psychological state seems essential for adequate treatment and management of each subtype.
Keywords: RDC/TMD, TMD, TMJ, emotional state, myofascial pain syndromes, psychological distress.
