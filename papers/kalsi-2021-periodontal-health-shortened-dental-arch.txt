Evid Based Dent. 2021;22(3):112-113.  [Evidence-Based Dentistry commentary on an RCT]

Periodontal health in the shortened dental arch.
Kalsi HK, Edwards DC.

DESIGN: Multi-centre randomised controlled clinical trial with two prosthetic intervention arms,
conducted in 14 dental schools and universities in Germany from October 2000.
CASE SELECTION: Adults aged >=35 years with bilateral missing molars, both canines and at least one
premolar present on each side in one jaw. Randomly allocated to restoration of posterior teeth using a
removable partial denture (RPD), or a fixed prosthesis adhering to shortened dental arch (SDA)
principles (no replacement posterior to the second molar). Central randomisation, permuted blocks
stratified by age. Data collected at baseline (after pre-prosthetic treatment); follow-up at six
months, annually for five years, eight years and ten years.
DATA ANALYSIS: 215 participants randomised — RPD group n=109, SDA group n=106. Intention-to-treat (ITT)
and modified per-protocol analysis performed on RPD n=79 and SDA n=71. Per-protocol analysis on RPD
n=25 and SDA n=22. Significance two-sided p=0.05; CI 95%.
RESULTS: For ten-year vertical clinical attachment loss (CAL), ITT analysis showed differences between
RPD and SDA groups were statistically significant (p<=0.05) in both the study jaw (0.79 mm) and in all
teeth (0.69 mm), IN FAVOUR OF THE SDA GROUP. The SDA group also performed more favourably in both
bleeding on probing and plaque index parameters (statistically significant, positive regression
coefficients).
CONCLUSIONS: There is evidence of reduced impact on the periodontium from an SDA approach compared to
an RPD approach, ALTHOUGH THE DIFFERENCE IS UNLIKELY TO BE CLINICALLY SIGNIFICANT.
