Title: Evaluating the Clinical Success of Clear Aligners for Rotational Tooth Movements in Adult Patients: A Systematic Review
Authors: Giulia Benedetti, Nicolò Sicca, Gaia Lopponi, Claudia Dettori, Alessio Verdecchia, Enrico Spinas
Journal: Dentistry Journal (Basel)
Year: 2025
DOI: 10.3390/dj13100440
PMID: 41149087
PMCID: PMC12564582
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12564582/

DESIGN: Systematic review (PRISMA), 12 studies (1 RCT, 11 non-randomized)
Databases: PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane, LILACs, Ovid, ClinicalTrials.gov, ProQuest (through July 2025)
Population: Adults ≥18 years, full permanent dentition, clear aligner therapy only, 3D digital superimposition for accuracy measurement, pre-refinement outcomes only

OVERALL ROTATIONAL ACCURACY: 36–85%, average ~65%

ACCURACY BY TOOTH TYPE:
- Incisors: 35.1–90% (higher predictability; maxillary centrals > laterals)
- Canines: ~36–58% (poorest performance; maxillary canines ~50%; ~4.5° lack of correction)
- Premolars: 54–83% (~2.2–4.6° lack of correction)
- Molars: 74–85% (most reliable; first > second molars; ~0.4–2.3° error)

SECONDARY FINDINGS:
- Attachments (11/12 studies used them): inconsistent benefit; "true impact remains uncertain"
- IPR (only Kravitz isolated this): highest accuracy 43% with IPR but not statistically significant
- Staging: most used 2°/aligner; literature suggests <1.5°/aligner improves accuracy
- Wear schedule: predominantly 14 days
- Aligner material: Invisalign SmartTrack (multilayer polyurethane) most studied; superior force maintenance vs single-layer polymers

GRADE: RCT = moderate certainty; non-randomized = low certainty
Risk of bias: No studies at low overall risk (ROBINS-I); "some concerns" for RCT (RoB 2)

CONCLUSIONS:
"Limited yet improving predictability in rotational movements"
Attachments and IPR "may contribute but lack consistent validation"
"Well-designed RCTs with standardized protocols are required"

LIMITATIONS:
- Only 1 RCT, 11 non-randomized retrospective
- Heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis
- No standardized compliance assessment
- Adult patients only (no adolescents)
- High risk of bias across nearly all included studies
