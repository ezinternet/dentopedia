Source: PubMed / PMC10713775 (condensed working copy). DOI 10.1007/s00784-023-05317-3. Clin Oral Investig 2023;27(12):7275-7283.
Title: Platelet-rich fibrin ensures hemostasis after single-tooth removal under factor Xa inhibitors - a clinical prospective randomized split-mouth study. Kyyak S, Jari A, Heimes D, Heider J, Kammerer PW.

ABSTRACT: Prospective, double-blinded, randomized split-mouth study; local hemostatic effect of PRF inserted into the extraction socket vs hemostatic gelatine sponge (GS, "therapeutic gold standard") in patients on FXa inhibitors (apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban) without drug withdrawal. n=21 patients, 42 teeth. Bleeding assessed immediately, 30 min, 1 h, 1.5 h, 24 h, day 7. In 67% of cases mild oozing stopped 30-90 min after extraction via gauze pressure without delayed bleeding. No difference PRF vs GS and no significant difference among rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban (all p>0.15). Conclusion: PRF and GS are reliable hemostatic methods in post-extraction sockets of patients on FXa inhibitors. Clinical relevance: no need to discontinue FXa inhibitors for single-tooth removal.

METHODS: Dept of OMFS, University Medical Center Mainz, Germany; May-Dec 2022. Ethics: Landesaerztekammer Rheinland-Pfalz (2021-16116, 3 May 2022). Inclusion: any age/gender, monotherapy FXa inhibitor, no other antithrombotic, normal coagulation parameters, needing extraction of two teeth on contralateral sides of same jaw. Exclusion: hepatic dysfunction, hematologic disease, thrombocytopenia, immune/radio/chemotherapy, bisphosphonate/antiresorptive therapy, pregnancy, uncompensated diabetes, local anesthetic allergy, acute inflammation, cyst/tumor, teeth needing osteotomy or subperiosteal preparation, nerve canal contact, sinus communication. Patients took FXa inhibitor as usual. Articaine 4% + epinephrine 1:200,000. Extraction + socket curettage by investigator 1; investigator 2 (opened sealed randomization envelopes) inserted PRF plug in one socket (test) and GS (Hygitec) in the other (control); both closed with adaptive cross-mattress absorbable sutures (Vicryl 3/0). PRF: 10 ml venous blood per matrix, A-PRF tubes (Mectron), 1200 rpm, 177 g, 8 min; clot compressed with Xpression box. Post-op: cooling, paracetamol 500 mg 1-1-1 for 24 h. Bleeding assessed by blinded investigator 1 at 0, 30 min, 1 h, 1.5 h, 24 h, day 7. Classification: none / stopped after 30 min / 1 h / 1.5 h gauze tamponade; mild (oozing stopped by local pressure), moderate (needs intervention beyond pressure), severe. Sample size: 42 sites (21 test, 21 control), 21 patients. Paired t-test (two-sided), Kruskal-Wallis; p<=0.05; subgroup by FXa agent descriptive only.

RESULTS: Mean age 71 +/- 2.8 y (45-89); 11 F, 10 M; 3 smokers. Rivaroxaban 9, apixaban 7, edoxaban 5. Indications: arrhythmia/AF 9, DVT 10, PE 2. Only mild oozing (30-90 min) seen; no hemorrhage. Oozing events in 28 cases (of 42 sites): 24 stopped after 30 min gauze pressure; 2 after 1 h; 2 after 1.5 h. No difference test vs control (all p>0.05). No late bleeding after 1.5 h; none at suture removal day 7. Bleeding events by drug: rivaroxaban 18, apixaban 14, edoxaban 10 (p=0.15 t-test; p=0.45 KWT). Rivaroxaban and edoxaban stopped in all cases after 30 min; two apixaban patients needed 1.5 h. More events in posterior (side) than front teeth (16 vs 12; p=0.03 t-test; p=0.23 KWT) and mandible vs maxilla (18 vs 10; p=0.0008 t-test; p=0.02 KWT). 1.5-h oozing exclusively in posterior mandibular teeth of apixaban patients. Higher doses (rivaroxaban 20 mg, apixaban 5 mg) showed more oozing; edoxaban oozing:non-oozing ratio 4:1 (highest). Max creatinine 1.28 mg/dl; no correlation with creatinine (no stats, small subgroups). Two prolonged cases both >85 y (one creatinine 1.13, another smoker with creatinine 1.28). No correlation between time since last FXa intake and bleeding (p=0.1875).

DISCUSSION/LIMITATIONS: Simple extraction is low bleeding risk; German guidelines: do not stop apixaban/rivaroxaban/edoxaban for uncomplicated oral surgery. Little direct FXa evidence in oral surgery. No second control without hemostatic agent (historically higher bleeding in anticoagulated pts); the three FXa inhibitors pooled; small subgroups; no severe renal impairment included; few elderly and smokers; PRF collection adds minor morbidity and pre-op time. Mourao et al. used PRF without control group.
CONCLUSION: For extraction of non-adjacent teeth under FXa-inhibitor monotherapy without other bleeding disorders, no need to interrupt; PRF or hemostatic sponge plus adaptive sutures acceptable; max 1.5 h oozing expected, controlled by gauze pressure; can be done outpatient; early post-op control may not be needed; last-intake timing may not need to be addressed.
