Source: PubMed PMID 36389398 / PMC9651041, DOI 10.7717/peerj.13864. Né YGS et al. Treatment for dental erosion: a systematic review of in vitro studies. PeerJ 2022;10:e13864. (Condensed working copy. NOTE: PMC text dropped the italic words "in vitro" throughout, e.g. title reads "a systematic review ofstudies"; in vitro design inferred from artificial-saliva comparator, specimens of extracted human teeth, microhardness/profilometry outcomes.)

ABSTRACT
Dental erosion = chemical loss of mineralized dental tissue by nonbacterial acids. Aim: evaluate main treatments for dental erosion. PRISMA; OSF registration 10.17605/OSF.IO/XMFNZ. Six databases (PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane, Scopus, Lilacs) + grey literature (Google Scholar, OpenGrey). Risk of bias: Cochrane tool modified for in vitro studies; qualitative synthesis (no meta-analysis). 522 records; 4 studies included; 3 low RoB, 1 high RoB. Two studies: fluoride toothpaste; two: CPP-ACP on human teeth. CPP-ACP was the only product that promoted a significant increase in enamel microhardness and reduced tooth wear. Conclusion: no anti-erosion effect of different fluoride toothpastes (one of these studies high RoB); CPP-ACP showed anti-erosion efficacy applied before or after erosive wear.

METHODS
PICO: P eroded human permanent teeth; I any topical agent with active ingredient usable for erosion treatment; C artificial saliva group (no treatment); O change in enamel. Excluded: bovine teeth, technical papers, clinical cases, literature reviews, uncontrolled studies, letters, opinion; studies without artificial-saliva comparison group. Two independent reviewers; third for disagreement. RoB domains chosen as essential: allocation concealment, participant blinding, outcome-assessment blinding; high RoB = severe issues in two key domains.

RESULTS
522 records, 305 duplicates removed, 217 screened by title/abstract, 204 excluded, 13 full-text; excluded: 3 bovine, 1 dentine, 2 no intervention group, 3 primary teeth -> 4 included (Czech Republic, Brazil, Thailand, UK). Erosive challenge: citric acid (3 studies), Coca-Cola (1).
Table 1:
- Czech Republic: n=35 (General University Hospital Prague), third molars, microhardness. Sensodyne Pronamel; Elmex Erosion Protection; BioRepair Plus Sensitivity Control; SensiShield; Enamel Care; controls artificial saliva, Elmex Erosion mouth rinse. Enamel Care formed compact layer of deposits; Pronamel and BioRepair did not produce protective deposits; Elmex Erosion Protection and SensiShield showed high abrasivity and consequent low anti-erosion action.
- Brazil: n=150 (Univ. Sao Paulo), premolars, microhardness. Sensodyne Repair and Protect; Elmex Sensitive Professional; Sensodyne Rapid Relief; Blend-a-Med Pro Expert; Sensodyne Pronamel; Elmex Erosion Protection; Candida Protect Professional; Regenerate; controls artificial saliva, Colgate Caries Protection. All groups showed progressive surface loss. Pronamel and Elmex Erosion Protection lowest surface loss, not differing from controls and each other (p>0.05). Regenerate and Blend-a-Med Pro Expert highest surface loss.
- Thailand: n=40 (Chulalongkorn), central and lateral incisors, microhardness. CPP-ACP (Tooth Mousse) vs artificial saliva. Mean microhardness +13.27% CPP-ACP vs +2.53% artificial saliva. Two-way ANOVA: no significant interaction among CPP-ACP, artificial saliva and deionized water (p=0.548).
- UK: n=36 (Guy's Hospital), third molars, profilometry. Tooth Mousse (CPP-ACP); Tooth Mousse without CPP-ACP; artificial saliva. Tooth Mousse significantly reduced enamel wear (p<0.001).
Narrative: toothpaste effectiveness associated with tin, high phosphate and calcium, acid-resistant deposit formation, low abrasivity. Elmex Erosion adhered to enamel but scratched surface slightly (abrasive), Pronamel smoother. Among topical agents APF gel, Elmex Erosion, Sensodyne Pronamel showed best enamel-protection results (per text). Pastes with higher Ca/P concentration and smaller particle size more effective.
RoB: all studies had methodological problems in blinding of the test-machine operator, judged not to raise RoB given large samples; 3/4 low RoB; 1 high RoB due to lack of quantitative data.

DISCUSSION / LIMITATIONS
Fluoride products showed no significant reduction in enamel surface loss in included studies, but CPP-ACP had remineralizing effect. Few studies; heterogeneous substrate, erosion stages, evaluation times, erosive protocols. No negative control exposed only to water. Acquired pellicle cannot be reproduced in vitro. Results to be interpreted with caution; need standardized well-designed studies. Conclusion: CPP-ACP therapies may be beneficial; further studies needed for high-certainty evidence.
