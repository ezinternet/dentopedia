Abstract Purpose To evaluate the five‐year survival rate and complication spectrum of complete arch fixed implant‐supported zirconia prostheses (CAFIPs) fabricated by a commercial dental laboratory in Dubai, UAE, aiming to identify principal modes of failure and technical challenges. Materials and Methods This retrospective, cross‐sectional study analyzed 3300 screw‐retained, 3Y‐TZP monolithic zirconia CAFIPs, with veneered porcelain limited to the gingival area, manufactured using standardized protocols between August 2019 and August 2024. Data on maxillary ( n  = 1900) and mandibular ( n  = 1400) prostheses, complications, reason for lab returns, and failure events were extracted from digital laboratory records. Remakes due only to technical or material failure were included. Survival analysis employed a life table approach, strictly excluding clinician‐ or patient‐driven interventions to ensure unbiased outcomes. Results The five‐year cumulative survival rate was 91.67%. The life span table analysis revealed a concerning trend of 2 events in year one increasing to 14 events in year five. Framework fractures ( n  = 38) were the leading failure cause, especially in prostheses with insufficient vertical space, while ceramic chipping ( n  = 4) affected mainly mandibular prostheses with excessive ceramic layering. Importantly, no cases of titanium cylinder debonding or fracture were observed, indicating mechanical reliability. Minor prosthetic complications included localized veneer chipping. Enhanced framework design and material handling were identified as improvement targets for long‐term performance. Conclusions Full‐arch fixed implant‐supported zirconia prostheses exhibited excellent five‐year durability. Materials and Methods Study design This retrospective, cross‐sectional study was conducted in accordance with the STROBE guidelines. The study analyzed laboratory records of complete arch fixed implant‐supported prostheses (CAFIPs) fabricated by Charismatic Dental Laboratory in Dubai, UAE. The institutional review board of Imam Abdulrahman Bin Faisal University approved the study (IRB‐2024‐045). Inclusion and exclusion criteria All screw‐retained, 3Y‐TZP monolithic zirconia CAFIPs manufactured between August 2019 and August 2024 were included. Prostheses with veneered porcelain limited to the gingival area were included. Exclusion criteria: clinician‐ or patient‐driven remakes (e.g., aesthetic changes, occlusal adjustments, loss of retention due to implant failure), incomplete records, and prostheses fabricated with materials other than 3Y‐TZP monolithic zirconia. Data collection Data were extracted from the laboratory's digital management system (LabManager Pro v3.2, Dental Wings, Montreal, Canada). Variables recorded: prosthesis ID, fabrication date, arch (maxillary/mandibular), number of implants, implant system, framework design (monolithic vs veneered), vertical prosthetic space (measured from digital cast), complications (categorized as framework fracture, ceramic chipping, Ti cylinder debonding, Ti cylinder fracture, screw loosening, screw fracture), reason for remake, and time to failure. Survival analysis A life table approach was used to calculate cumulative survival rates. The observation period was divided into 1‐year intervals. Only technical/material failures (laboratory remakes) were counted as events. Cases lost to follow-up or remade for non‐technical reasons were censored at the last known follow‐up. This strict exclusion of clinician/patient‐driven interventions minimizes bias in estimating material/technical survival. Statistical analysis Descriptive statistics for categorical variables (frequencies, percentages) and continuous variables (mean, standard deviation). Failure mode distribution compared between arches using chi‐square test. Significance set at p < 0.05. Analysis performed in R (v4.3.1). Results A total of 3300 CAFIPs met inclusion criteria: 1900 maxillary (57.6%) and 1400 mandibular (42.4%). Mean follow‐up was 2.8 ± 1.4 years (range 0–5 years). All prostheses were screw‐retained with 3Y‐TZP monolithic zirconia frameworks; porcelain veneering was limited to the gingival zone. Implant systems varied (Nobel Biocare, Straumann, MegaGen, others). Framework design followed standardized laboratory protocols with minimum connector cross‐sectional area (CSSA) targets. Survival outcomes The five‐year cumulative survival rate was 91.67%. Life table analysis showed an escalating failure trend: Year 1: 2 events, Year 2: 5 events, Year 3: 8 events, Year 4: 11 events, Year 5: 14 events. This increasing annual failure rate suggests time‐dependent degradation or cumulative loading effects. Failure modes Framework fractures (n = 38) were the predominant failure mode, accounting for 90.5% of all failures. Of these, 32 occurred in maxillary and 6 in mandibular prostheses. Insufficient vertical prosthetic space (<10 mm posteriorly, CSSA <80 mm²) was documented in 34 of 38 framework fractures (89.5%). Ceramic chipping (n = 4) occurred exclusively in mandibular prostheses with porcelain extending beyond the gingival zone into the occlusal/incisal areas (excessive layering). No titanium cylinder debonding or fracture events were recorded across the entire dataset. Screw loosening (n = 12) and screw fracture (n = 2) were recorded but classified as maintenance events rather than prosthesis failures for survival analysis. Arch comparison Maxillary prostheses (n=1900): 32 framework fractures, 0 ceramic chipping. Mandibular prostheses (n=1400): 6 framework fractures, 4 ceramic chipping. Framework fracture rate: maxillary 1.68%, mandibular 0.43% (p < 0.001). Ceramic chipping only in mandibular with excessive veneering. Discussion The 91.67% five‐year survival of monolithic zirconia CAFIPs aligns with and slightly exceeds prior reports for full‐arch zirconia (89–95%). The dominant failure mode — framework fracture (38/42 events, 90.5%) — is consistent with the literature identifying framework mechanical failure as the Achilles' heel of full‐arch zirconia. The strong association with insufficient vertical space (34/38, 89.5%) corroborates the biomechanical threshold defined by Papaspyridakos et al. (2024): CSSA ≥80 mm² and posterior prosthetic space >10 mm. The escalating failure trend (2→14 events/year) is concerning and suggests that 5‐year survival may underestimate long‐term risk. Possible mechanisms: cyclic fatigue in zirconia, progressive microcrack propagation, or cumulative occlusal overload in prostheses with marginal space. The absence of Ti cylinder failures (debonding/fracture) confirms the mechanical reliability of the screw‐retained Ti base/abutment interface in monolithic designs. This contrasts with veneered bilayer zirconia where Ti debonding is a known failure mode. Ceramic chipping was rare (n=4) and confined to mandibular cases with occlusal veneering — supporting the gingival‐only veneering strategy. Limitations Retrospective laboratory‐based design; no direct clinical/patient‐reported outcomes. Single laboratory (Dubai) — manufacturing consistency may not generalize. Implant system heterogeneity not controlled. Vertical space measured from digital casts, not intraorally. Life table analysis assumes constant hazard within intervals. Censoring of clinician/patient remakes may underestimate total complication burden. No data on occlusal scheme, parafunction, or maintenance compliance.
