Effectiveness of HAPA-based oral hygiene instruction in patients over 65 years of age: a randomized controlled trial
Baumann L, Klusmann V, Bamberg C, Rüttermann S, Gerhardt-Szép S. BMC Oral Health 2025;25(1):1209. DOI 10.1186/s12903-025-06615-w. PMID 40684208. PMCID PMC12276706.
Source: PubMed Central full text via PubMed MCP get_full_text_article (retrieved 2026-09-30). Figures omitted; tables rendered as text.

ABSTRACT
Objective: to provide oral hygiene instructions tailored to behavioral stages based on the Health Action Process Approach (HAPA) and to assess their impact on oral health compared with standardized instructions in patients aged 65 and older.
Methods: 67 university clinic patients randomly assigned to intervention (n = 33) and control (n = 34); 62 patients, aged 65 to 87 years (M = 71.3, SD = 5.3), participated in both baseline and follow-up. GBI and PCR recorded at baseline and 12 weeks after instruction. In the intervention group a questionnaire surveyed oral hygiene behavior and motivation at the beginning of both visits.
Results: ANCOVA with baseline control revealed no difference in GBI between groups at 12 weeks. Both intervention, t(29) = 3.91, p < .001, d = 0.71, and control, t(31) = 3.67, p < .001, d = 0.65, improved in GBI. Only the intervention group showed significant improvement in PCR, t(29) = 2.29, p = .03, d = 0.42. Intervention group reported increased motivation and frequency of interdental care, d = 1.22.
Conclusion: first evidence on HAPA being applicable in dental prophylaxis among university clinic patients older than 65. General instruction was effective in reducing gingival bleeding; HAPA offered specific advantages in motivation, interdental care, and plaque.
Trial registration: DRKS00030040, registered 22.03.2023.

BACKGROUND (condensed)
EU share of individuals over 65 expected to reach 28.5% in 2050. Ageing brings polypharmacy, xerostomia, altered oral microflora, reduced buffer capacity, gingival recession exposing root cementum (root caries risk), reduced dexterity. Educating older patients on interdental cleaning such as interdental brushes is essential. HAPA (Schwarzer) distinguishes a motivational phase (risk perception, outcome expectancies, self-efficacy -> intention) and a volitional phase (action planning, coping planning, recovery self-efficacy); patients are Preintenders, Intenders or Actors; planning bridges the intention-behavior gap. Few dental HAPA studies existed, mostly in young patients/students or flossing planning in mixed-age samples; one Iranian study in >60-year-olds compared intervention providers, not HAPA vs standard care.

METHODS
Participants: aged 65 and older (EU definition); at least 10 remaining interproximal spaces; excluded if professional dental cleaning in prior 6 months or past treatment in the periodontal department; able to give informed consent; sufficient German. No cognitive tests; all physically able to attend independently. Setting: Carolinum Dental University-Institute, J.W. Goethe University, Frankfurt, Germany. Recruited Sept-Oct 2022; conducted Oct 2022-May 2023. Ethics vote 05.09.2022.
Sample size: based on Scheerman; SD approx 25% expected for primary outcome; 52 patients needed to detect a 20% difference at alpha 5%, power 80% (two-sided 2-sample t-test); with 20% drop-out, minimum 64.
Randomization/blinding: parallel-group, 1:1, simple randomization in R by a researcher uninvolved in instruction/assessment; sealed named envelopes opened by baseline investigators immediately before the session. Baseline instructors and participants not blinded. Follow-up clinical examiners (different, calibrated) blinded to allocation.
Statistics: ANOVA for baseline comparisons; MANOVA across HAPA stages; ANCOVA on 12-week follow-up controlling for baseline and age; chi-square for stage frequencies; repeated-measures ANOVA for HAPA measures within intervention group; MANCOVA for follow-up across stages; Bonferroni correction; SPSS 30. CONSORT 2025. No interim analyses.
Outcomes: Plaque Control Record (PCR; plaque disclosed, six surfaces per tooth, percentage); Gingival Bleeding Index (GBI; probing, bleeding after 10 s, six surfaces per tooth). GBI calibration: workshops with probing pressure 20-25 g; PCR calibration on test patients.
Intervention: sessions approx 65 min. Oral exam, GBI/PCR recorded by calibrated investigators. Control: standardized oral hygiene instruction with model and intraoral demonstrations, focus on interdental aids, individual fitting of interdental brushes; patients got adjusted interdental brushes and a record of ISO sizes. Intervention group: additionally a questionnaire (15 min) adapted from Schwarzer's flossing questionnaire with interdental brushes substituted; total score 0-69: 0-22 Preintender, 23-46 Intender, 47-69 Actor. Instruction content same as control but emphasis tailored to stage; each ended after 15 min with the patient writing a personal goal, given as a reminder with the brushes. Preintender: basics of oral hygiene, caries etiology and consequences, brush technique, importance of interdental brushes. Intender: knowledge gaps, concrete planning for behavior change, realistic short-term goals. Actor: implementation help, planning, maintenance (obstacles, how to counteract). All patients received professional teeth cleaning after instruction. Clinicians were trained, calibrated dentists.
Follow-up: 12 weeks; approx 30 min; questionnaire (intervention only) plus GBI/PCR by a different blinded calibrated examiner; then remotivation for all patients (intervention group referred to written goals). No adverse events.

RESULTS
67 recruited, 62 attended follow-up; drop-out 7.5% (three unreachable, one health reasons, one received OHI outside study). Mean interval 12.29 weeks (SD 1.21; range 10-19). 29 male, 33 female; mean age 71.3 (male) and 71.2 (female). Age range 65-87.
Table 1 baseline (n=62; intervention n=30; control n=32): age 70.53 +/- 4.29 vs 71.94 +/- 6.09 (p=.30); interproximal spaces 22.5 +/- 4.55 vs 22.47 +/- 5.28 (p=.98); GBI 9.5 +/- 5.76 vs 11.34 +/- 9.80 % (p=.37); PCR 62.27 +/- 17.05 vs 59.47 +/- 18.06 % (p=.53).
Table 2 baseline HAPA stages (n=30 intervention): Preintender n=3, Intender n=14, Actor n=13. Actors higher baseline interdental brush use (6.77 +/- 5.09 per week; Intenders 2.93 +/- 3.05; Preintenders 0), F(2,27)=5.04, p=.014.
Table 3 ANCOVA: GBI intervention 9.5 -> 5.70 +/- 4.13 (change -3.80 +/- 5.32, t(29)=3.91, p<.001, d=0.71); control 11.34 -> 5.56 +/- 3.93 (change -5.78 +/- 8.91, t(31)=3.67, p<.001, d=0.65); between-group F(1,57)=0.15, p=.70, eta2=0.003. PCR intervention 62.27 -> 57.00 +/- 15.63 (change -5.27 +/- 12.62, t(29)=2.29, p=.030, d=0.42); control 59.47 -> 60.69 +/- 15.33 (change +1.22 +/- 14.55, t(31)=-0.47, p=.319, d=-0.08); between-group F(1,57)=5.19, p=.026, eta2=0.083.
Table 4 stage change: Preintender 3 -> 0; Intender 14 -> 10 (text says 50% decrease; seven of 14 Intenders became Actors); Actor 13 -> 20 (+53.8%); chi2(2)=7.10, p=.019. The three Preintenders progressed to Intenders.
Table 5 repeated-measures ANOVA (intervention, n=30): interdental brush use per week 4.30 +/- 4.52 -> 9.93 +/- 4.79 (change 5.63 +/- 4.63, F(1,29)=44.46, p<.001, eta2=0.61); brushing frequency per week 12.8 -> 13.1 (p=.482); intention 2.82 -> 2.98 (p=.340); outcome expectancy 2.67 -> 3.14 (eta2=0.37, p<.001); risk perception 2.97 -> 3.24 (p=.168); action self-efficacy 2.73 -> 3.51 (eta2=0.43, p<.001); coping self-efficacy 2.97 -> 3.41 (p=.002, eta2=0.28); action planning 2.81 -> 3.34 (eta2=0.32, p<.001); coping planning 2.51 -> 2.85 (p=.021, eta2=0.17); behavioral control 2.51 -> 3.57 (eta2=0.58, p<.001). Bonferroni threshold 0.005.
Table 6 MANCOVA across stages at follow-up controlling for baseline: no significant differences in GBI, PCR, brushing frequency (all p>.16); interdental brush use tendency Actors 12.85 vs Preintenders 9.33, Intenders 7.36, F(2,23)=3.31, p=.055.

DISCUSSION (condensed)
Both groups improved in GBI (medium effects), consistent with Stenman et al.; PCR reduction significant only in the intervention group (57 vs 60.69 at follow-up); PCR is a dichotomous snapshot index and a 5% change may be meaningful in patients with already low PCR. Interdental brush use self-reported (recall bias); control group did not report brush use or brushing frequency. Baseline comparable to DMS 6 national data. Written commitments not separately assessed. Brushing frequency unchanged, consistent with Kakudate et al. (already high). Mean 22.5 interproximal spaces exceeded DMS 6 expectations, suggesting oral-hygiene-conscious university clinic sample.
Limitations: modest sample (64 planned); small subgroup sizes limiting stage analysis; 12-week follow-up only; self-reported questionnaire data; no blinding of participants/instructors (expectation bias); no socioeconomic or cognitive assessment; university clinic recruitment limits generalizability; examiner variability minimized by few calibrated practitioners; constant-force probes suggested.
Conclusions: HAPA-based intervention promoted interdental cleaning and improved oral health outcomes in older adults; proof of principle; larger, longer, objectively measured studies needed; simplified guidelines for routine practice (remotivation at 6-12 month check-ups).
