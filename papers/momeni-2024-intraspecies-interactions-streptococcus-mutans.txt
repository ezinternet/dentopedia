ABSTRACT

Early childhood dental caries (ECC) is the most common chronic disease among children,
especially among low socioeconomic populations. S. mutans is most frequently associated
with initiation of ECC. Although many studies report children with multiple S. mutans
strains (i.e., genotypes) have greater odds of developing ECC, studies investigating
intraspecies interactions in dental caries are lacking. This study investigates the impact
of intraspecies interactions on cariogenic and fitness traits of clinical S. mutans isolates
using in vitro (biofilm) and in vivo (Drosophila model) approaches. Association analysis
evaluated if presence of multiple S. mutans genotypes within the first year of colonization
was associated with caries. Initially, clinical S. mutans isolates from 10 children were
evaluated. S. mutans strains (G09 and G18, most prevalent) isolated from one child were used
for subsequent analysis. Biofilm analysis was performed for single and mixed cultures to
assess cariogenic traits, including biofilm biomass, intracellular polysaccharide, pH, and
glucan. Confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM) and time-lapse imaging were used to
evaluate spatial and temporal biofilm dynamics, respectively. A Drosophila model was used to
assess colonization. Results showed the mean biofilm pH was significantly lower in co-cultured
biofilms versus monoculture. Doubling of S. mutans biofilms was observed by CLSM and
colonization in Drosophila for co-cultured. Individual strains occupied specific domains in
co-culture and G09 contributed most to increased co-culture biofilm thickness and colonization
in Drosophila. Biofilm formation and acid production displayed distinct signatures in
time-lapsed experiments. This study illuminates that intraspecies interactions of S. mutans
significantly impacts biofilm acidity, architecture, and colonization.

IMPORTANCE: This study sheds light on the complex dynamics of a key contributor to early
childhood dental caries (ECC) by exploring intraspecies interactions of different S. mutans
strains and their impact on cariogenic traits. Utilizing clinical isolates from children with
ECC, the research highlights significant differences in biofilm architecture and acid production
in mixed versus single genotype cultures. The findings reveal that co-cultured S. mutans strains
exhibit increased cell density and acidity, with individual strains occupying distinct domains.
These insights, enhanced by use of time-lapsed confocal laser scanning microscopy and a
Drosophila model, offer a deeper understanding of ECC pathogenesis and potential avenues for
targeted interventions.

INTRODUCTION (key points)

- Dental caries affects >80% of the human population worldwide; most common chronic disease
  of children and adolescents (CDC). WHO: 60-80% of school children in industrialized countries.
- Early childhood caries (ECC): decayed/missing/filled tooth surfaces (DMFS) in children <6 y.
  Severe ECC (SECC) is highly prevalent in marginalized/low-SES groups.
- Cariogenic dominant microorganisms: mutans streptococci (MS), lactobacilli, bifidobacteria,
  yeasts. S. mutans is the most effective predictor of future caries incidence.
- S. mutans pathogenic features: (1) bind teeth via tenacious biofilms (dental plaque),
  (2) metabolize fermentable carbohydrates (esp. sucrose) to organic acids, (3) aciduricity
  (survive acidic environment). Intracellular iodophilic polysaccharide (IPS) indicates
  glycogen storage → continued acid production after carbohydrate depletion.
- Prior 8-year longitudinal epidemiological study (African-American, high-caries-risk,
  non-fluoridated population): children with higher S. mutans counts 5.6x more likely to
  develop caries; children with multiple S. mutans genotypes 4.5x more likely to have caries
  experience; younger children (<6 y) 2.9x more likely to have multiple genotypes.
- Gap: most research evaluates single or interspecies/polymicrobial biofilms; intraspecies
  interactions among multiple S. mutans genotypes are understudied. Prior intraspecies work
  focused on bacteriocins (mutacins) via stab-agar antagonism (some strains inhibit others).
- Hypothesis: presence of multiple S. mutans genotypes contributes significantly to cariogenic
  traits and may provide mutualistic benefit for S. mutans fitness.

RESULTS

1. Presence of multiple genotypes within the first year of detection is significantly
   associated with caries (n=78 children; Fisher's exact test).
   - At INITIAL detection: multiple genotypes NOT significantly associated with caries (p=0.327)
     [logical: 1-2 y lag from colonization to detectable caries].
   - WITHIN FIRST YEAR of detection: multiple genotypes SIGNIFICANTLY associated with ECC
     (p=0.019). Mean number of genotypes in multi-genotype children = 2.7 (range 2-6).
   - Most multi-genotype children acquired genotypes at 1-2.5 y of age.
   - Of 14 caries-free children, 12 (86%) did NOT maintain persistent S. mutans colonization
     over the 6-year study.
   TABLE 1 data:
     Initial detection — Caries: 1 genotype 44 (56.4%), >1 genotype 20 (25.6%); No caries:
       1 genotype 12 (15.4%), >1 genotype 2 (2.6%); p=0.327.
     Within first year — Caries: 1 genotype 23 (29.5%), >1 genotype 41 (52.6%); No caries:
       1 genotype 10 (12.8%), >1 genotype 4 (5.1%); p=0.019.
     Genotype count distribution (first year): 1→33, 2→28, 3→9, 4→4, 5→3, 6→1.

2. Biofilm pH significantly lower in co-cultured biofilms (in vitro static biofilms).
   - 9 of 10 children: co-culture biofilms trend lower pH than mean mono-culture (increased
     pHrodo fluorescence).
   - Overall population: co-cultures show significantly increased pHrodo fluorescence (lower pH)
     AND greater cell density (increased Syto9). First indication co-cultures may be thicker.
   - No overall significant differences in biofilm biomass or IPS for the population.
   - Child 5 (subject C-232, genotypes G09 + G18, the two most prevalent in this population)
     selected for further analysis. Co-culture consistently more acidic than mono-culture.
   - G18 mono-culture: significantly lower biomass than G09 and than UA159 control; but
     significantly higher IPS than UA159 (greater glycogen storage). G18 contributes most to
     acidic biofilm pH. G18 glucan significantly lower than G09/co-culture — acidic phenotype
     NOT explained by glucan differences.
   - Cell density (Syto9, 2D) consistently higher in co-culture than mono G18.

3. Co-culturing significantly increases S. mutans cell density and biofilm thickness (CLSM).
   - G09 tagged mCherry (red), G18 tagged GFP (green) for CLSM.
   - Co-culture total fluorescence (GFP+mCherry combined) almost DOUBLED vs mono-cultures,
     despite equal starting inoculum (50/50) — increased cell density in mix.
   - G09 showed significantly greater biofilm thickness in mix than its mono-culture.
   - Both mono-cultures had significantly reduced thickness vs UA159.
   - Mean volume/area: statistically significant doubling of S. mutans in mix vs mono/UA159.
   - Spatial architecture: mono G09 (red) = large aggregates with glucan matrix (blue) between;
     mono G18 = confluent "lawn" phenotype, minimal glucan. In co-culture, G09 forms
     larger/taller aggregates that collapse into "volcano-like" structures over negative space
     in the underlying glucan layer. Each strain occupies a SPECIFIC DOMAIN, no apparent overlap.
   - Caveat: mCherry tagging of G18 induced an aggregate phenotype artifact → use alternate
     fluorescent protein as control. mCherry did NOT cause G09 aggregation (GFP-G09 same).
   - Differential CFU plating (antibiotic-selective media): equal ~50/50 strain distribution
     pre- and post-biofilm; total inoculum comparable between mono/co-cultures.

4. Different clinical strains display distinguishable biofilm and acid-formation phenotypes
   (time-lapse imaging, 24-30 h).
   - Biofilm formation distinguishable as early as 3-4 h. G09 = dense microcolony-like
     mini-aggregates early; G18 = thinner, broader "fish net" networks of streptococcal chains.
   - Strains occupy distinct domains, no appreciable overlap.
   - Biofilm pH dynamics (pHrodo red, both strains GFP-tagged, 30 h): pHrodo fluorescence
     continually increased with TWO distinct phases (~10-11 h and ~24-26 h). GFP peaked at
     8-9 h, decreased (GFP pH-sensitivity / acid tolerance), then partially recovered and
     plateaued ~25-26 h. pHrodo peaked within 2 h after each GFP peak. Bi-phasic acid formation
     suggests an equilibration/acid-tolerance-response period.

5. Co-culture improves S. mutans colonization (Drosophila melanogaster capillary-feeding model).
   - Bacterial consumption (5% sucrose) comparable across strains; day > night consumption.
   - G09 colonization within Drosophila consistently GREATER when G18 also present (mutualistic).
   - On antibiotic-selective media, co-culture CFUs consistent with CLSM doubling; on plain THA
     (total count) CFUs equalled mean of mono-cultures → antibiotic-selective media needed for
     accurate differential recovery.

=====================================================================
DISCUSSION (key points)

- Presence of MORE S. mutans genotypes contributes to a significantly more acidic environment,
  enhancing cariogenicity (4-genotype children more acidic than 2-genotype).
- G18 contributes most to acidic biofilm pH; G09 contributes most to increased biofilm thickness.
  Co-culture is mutually beneficial for overall colonization (esp. G09) → greater cariogenic
  potential than either mono-culture.
- Increased cell density (Syto9 2D), CLSM doubling, and Drosophila colonization doubling all
  support that multiple S. mutans genotypes are mutually beneficial for biofilm formation and
  persistence. More robust/thicker biofilms improve colonization and persistence.
- Clinical strains differ markedly from the reference UA159 in cariogenic phenotype — biofilm
  experiments should include clinical strains, not just UA159.
- Greater G09 biofilm height + fuller G18 coverage increase surface area available for other
  organisms to attach → increased cariogenic potential of co-cultured S. mutans.
- 86% of caries-free children did not persistently colonize S. mutans (acquired and lost, or
  transient colonization) — candidate subjects for host immunology/microbiome studies.
- Summary: when multiple strains G09 and G18 are present, significant increases in colonization,
  surface-area coverage, cell density, and acid production. High prevalence of G09 and G18 +
  large percentage of children with multiple strains within first year of detection → having
  multiple genotypes (particularly G09 + G18) is a strong risk factor for early childhood caries.

MATERIALS AND METHODS (summary)

- Bacterial strains: S. mutans isolates from 78 African-American children (~1 y at baseline,
  followed to 6 y) from an 8-year longitudinal high-caries-risk, non-fluoridated population.
  >14,000 isolates → 34 representative genotypes by rep-PCR + whole genome sequencing.
  65.8% of children had ECC by age 4. Child C-232 (Child 5; G09 + G18) selected for depth study.
  UA159 = positive control reference strain.
- Mutant constructions: G09/G18 modified with fluorescent-protein + antibiotic-resistance
  plasmids (pVPT-GFP + Kan; mCherry + Ery). Kanamycin 1 mg/mL, erythromycin 10 µg/mL for
  S. mutans; kanamycin 50 µg/mL for E. coli.
- Static biofilm: THB + 1% sucrose, 96-well, 16 h, 5% CO2, 37°C. Biomass = crystal violet;
  IPS = iodine assay; pH = pHrodo red; glucan = cascade blue; cell density = Syto9. ImageJ
  quantification of fluorescence. 10-children screen = 1 biological replicate (trend only);
  C-232 = ≥3 biological × 3 technical replicates.
- CLSM: 8-well ibidi slides, overnight biofilm, Zeiss LSM 880/980, 40x water immersion,
  Imaris v10.0.1 analysis; height from orthogonal projections. 3 biological replicates.
- Time-lapse: Zeiss Cell Discoverer7 (CD7), 50x water immersion, hourly imaging 24-30 h,
  37°C/5% CO2, mCherry + GFP + oblique-illumination brightfield.
- Differential plating: antibiotic-selective THA, pre-plate (inoculum) vs post-plate (16 h
  biofilm, sonicated), CFU/mL enumeration.
- Drosophila colonization: capillary-feeding, 7 male Canton-S flies (1-3 d), antibiotic
  pre-treatment, twice-daily feeding of S. mutans in 5% sucrose for 3 days, 5 flies pulverized,
  serial dilution on antibiotic THA, CFU enumeration.
- Statistics: Fisher's exact test (genotype-caries association); Student's t-test or one-way
  ANOVA + Tukey post-hoc; significance p<0.05.
