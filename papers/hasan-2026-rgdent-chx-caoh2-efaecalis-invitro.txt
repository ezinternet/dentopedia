TITLE: Comparative antimicrobial efficacy of three intracanal medicaments in an in vitro Enterococcus faecalis root canal infection model
DOI: 10.1186/s12903-026-08268-9
PMCID: PMC13169846
PMID: 41917933

ABSTRACT:
Background: Enterococcus faecalis commonly persists in endodontic infections and shows high resistance to traditional intracanal medicaments like calcium hydroxide [Ca(OH)2]. Alternatives such as chlorhexidine (CHX) and RG-Dent (metronidazole, iodoform, dexamethasone) are promising, but comparative evidence, especially regarding optimal application duration, remains limited.
Methods: Sixty human single-rooted teeth were prepared, sterilized, and inoculated with E. faecalis for 21 days to establish an in vitro root canal infection model. Specimens were randomly assigned to three medicament groups (RG-Dent, 2% CHX gel, Ca(OH)2; n=20 each) with two subgroups by application duration (2 or 7 days; n=10 each). Bacterial loads (log10 CFU/mL) were measured pre- and post-treatment. Data were analyzed using two-way ANOVA, t-tests, Tukey's HSD (α=0.05), and effect size calculations.
Results: All medicaments significantly reduced bacterial loads (P<0.001). Both CHX (mean reduction 4.94±0.23) and RG-Dent (4.77±0.26) were significantly more effective than Ca(OH)2 (3.69±0.27) (P<0.001, d>4). CHX showed marginal superiority over RG-Dent (P=0.036, d=0.69). Application duration (2 vs. 7 days) had no significant effect (P=0.78) and there was no interaction between duration and medicament.
Conclusions: Both 2% CHX gel and RG-Dent were significantly more effective than Ca(OH)2 against mature E. faecalis in an in vitro root canal infection model. Prolonged application beyond 2 days did not enhance efficacy, supporting the use of shorter medicament protocols for efficient clinical disinfection.

FULL TEXT:

Introduction

Successful endodontic treatment depends on complete elimination of pathogenic microorganisms from the root canal system. Despite advances in instrumentation and irrigation, persistent infections remain a major cause of treatment failure, with success rates ranging from 70% to 95% depending on clinical context. The anatomical complexity of root canals, including isthmuses, lateral canals, and dentinal tubules, creates sanctuaries where bacteria can survive mechanical and chemical debridement. Among persistent pathogens, Enterococcus faecalis has emerged as the primary culprit, detected in 24-77% of failed treatments compared to less than 10% in primary infections. This gram-positive, facultative anaerobe possesses remarkable survival capabilities, including robust biofilm formation, deep dentinal tubule invasion (100-400 μm), alkaline pH tolerance through proton pump mechanisms, and prolonged survival under nutrient deprivation. These characteristics make E. faecalis particularly resistant to conventional intracanal medicaments and a critical target for therapeutic intervention.

Intracanal medicaments serve as adjunctive agents to eliminate residual microorganisms between appointments, prevent bacterial regrowth, and promote periapical healing. Calcium hydroxide has been the traditional gold standard due to its high alkalinity (pH ≈12.5) and broad-spectrum antimicrobial properties mediated by hydroxyl ion release. However, multiple studies have questioned its reliability against E. faecalis. Evans et al. demonstrated that E. faecalis maintains intracellular pH homeostasis even at pH 11-12 through active proton pumps, while Siqueira and Lopes highlighted that dentin buffering reduces the pH at the medicament-dentin interface, diminishing bactericidal activity. More recently, Teja et al. found that Ca(OH)2 alone was significantly less effective than its combinations with chlorhexidine, chitosan, or nanoparticles in eliminating E. faecalis from infected dentin (n=400 samples). Similarly, Çorak et al. reported that various intracanal medicaments outperformed Ca(OH)2 in simulated internal resorption cavities. These findings, combined with evidence of biofilm protection and limited penetration into deep tubules, have prompted investigation of alternative agents.

Chlorhexidine, a cationic bisbiguanide, has emerged as a potent alternative with superior efficacy against E. faecalis. Gomes et al. comprehensively reviewed CHX applications in endodontics, highlighting its broad-spectrum activity, substantivity (antimicrobial retention in dentin for up to 12 weeks), and ability to penetrate dentinal tubules. Krithikadatta et al. demonstrated that 2% CHX gel was more effective than Ca(OH)2 in disinfecting dentinal tubules infected with E. faecalis. The substantivity of CHX, documented by Rosenthal et al. and Komorowski et al., provides sustained antimicrobial activity beyond the initial application period. However, clinicians must consider potential interactions with sodium hypochlorite, which can reduce CHX efficacy and produce precipitates if not adequately flushed.

Antibiotic-based medicaments, particularly triple antibiotic paste (TAP) containing metronidazole, ciprofloxacin, and minocycline, have shown superior efficacy in laboratory studies. Parhizkar et al. reviewed TAP applications and noted clear superiority over Ca(OH)2 in reducing E. faecalis burden, though with concerns regarding tooth discoloration (especially with minocycline), cytotoxicity to stem cells, and antibiotic resistance development. Hoshino et al. first demonstrated the potent antibacterial activity of this combination against root canal bacteria. More recently, Rahmati et al. reported that double antibiotic paste (DAP) showed significantly better clinical outcomes than Ca(OH)2 in treating teeth with apical periodontitis. Nanoparticle-enhanced formulations, such as Ca(OH)2 combined with silver or chitosan nanoparticles, have also shown promise in improving antibacterial efficacy and dentinal tubule penetration, though heterogeneity in formulations and limited clinical data currently restrict broad recommendations.

RG-Dent (YG-Dent, Damascus, Syria) is a commercially available multi-component intracanal medicament paste containing three active ingredients: metronidazole (an antimicrobial agent targeting anaerobic bacteria through interference with DNA synthesis), iodoform (providing broad-spectrum antiseptic activity through gradual release of free iodine), and dexamethasone (a corticosteroid with anti-inflammatory properties). While this combination theoretically offers robust antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory benefits, its efficacy against E. faecalis has not been rigorously evaluated in peer-reviewed literature. The duration of medicament application represents another unresolved question. Traditional protocols recommend 7-14 days based on early studies by Sjögren et al., yet recent evidence suggests that maximal bactericidal activity may occur within the first 48 h. Ordinola-Zapata et al., in their comprehensive review of intracanal medicaments, noted that while 7-day application is commonly recommended, the evidence supporting this duration over shorter intervals remains limited. From a clinical perspective, shorter application times could enhance treatment efficiency, improve patient compliance, and reduce costs without compromising disinfection quality.

Given these gaps in knowledge, this study was designed with two primary objectives. First, to evaluate and compare the antimicrobial efficacy of three intracanal medicaments (Ca(OH)2, 2% CHX gel, and RG-Dent) against E. faecalis in an in vitro root canal infection model using a standardized 21-day in vitro model. Second, to assess whether extending the application duration from 2 to 7 days enhances antimicrobial effectiveness. We hypothesized that (1) no significant differences would exist among the three medicaments, and (2) extending application duration would not significantly increase bacterial reduction.

Materials and methods

Specimen preparation and root canal instrumentation: Sixty human mandibular premolar teeth with single roots and single, straight canals (Vertucci Type I) were selected from extracted teeth stored in 0.1% thymol solution at 4°C. Teeth with caries, cracks, previous endodontic treatment, or calcification were excluded. Crowns were sectioned at the cementoenamel junction, root lengths standardized to 16mm. Working length established by inserting a #15 K-file to visible apical foramen minus 1mm. Root canals prepared using ProTaper Universal NiTi rotary system (Dentsply Sirona) to final apical preparation size 30, 0.09 taper. Irrigated with 2mL 5.25% NaOCl between file changes, followed by 5mL 17% EDTA for 3 min, 5mL 5.25% NaOCl for 5 min with ultrasonic activation, and final 10mL sterile saline rinse. Apical foramen sealed with flowable composite resin; external root surfaces coated with red nail varnish. Specimens autoclaved at 121°C for 20 min; sterility verified by BHI broth incubation.

Bacterial contamination and intracanal infection model: Pure culture of E. faecalis (ATCC 29212) grown on BHI agar, adjusted to 0.5 McFarland standard (~1.5×10^8 CFU/mL). Each canal inoculated with 50µL suspension, incubated anaerobically (85% N2, 10% H2, 5% CO2) at 37°C for 21 days to establish long-term intracanal infection, with BHI broth replenished every 48h.

Experimental groups: After 21-day incubation, specimens randomly assigned to three main groups (n=20 each): Ca(OH)2 (Metapaste, Meta Biomed), 2% CHX gel (Clopexid, Biodinamic), RG-Dent paste (YG-Dent). Each subdivided into 2-day and 7-day application subgroups (n=10 each). Pre-treatment sampling (S1): saline fill, circumferential filing, three paper points to full working length. Suspensions serially diluted, plated on Mueller-Hinton agar, incubated 37°C for 24h, CFUs counted (logCFU/mL). Medicaments delivered via lentulo spiral filler; coronal access sealed with Cavit; incubated 37°C 100% humidity for 2 or 7 days. Post-treatment sampling (S2) same protocol. Primary outcome: logCFU reduction = logCFU(S1) - logCFU(S2). Technician performing CFU counting was blinded to group assignment.

Statistical analysis: R software v4.2.1, α=0.05, two-tailed. Sample size determined a priori for two-way ANOVA (3 medicaments × 2 durations), f=0.40, power=0.80, minimum 54 specimens; 60 teeth included. Shapiro-Wilk and Levene's tests for normality/homogeneity. One-way ANOVA + Tukey's post-hoc (or Kruskal-Wallis + Dunn's if assumptions violated) for medicament comparisons at each time point. Two-way ANOVA for Medication × Duration interaction. Paired t-tests/Wilcoxon for within-group pre/post comparisons. Effect sizes (partial η², Cohen's d) reported. 95% CI calculated for all mean differences.

Results

Baseline: Initial mean bacterial load across all 60 samples was 5.11±0.21 logCFU/mL, no significant differences among six subgroups at baseline (F(5,54)=0.82, P=0.54). Shapiro-Wilk confirmed normal distribution (all P>0.05); Levene's test showed homogeneous variances (F(5,54)=1.34, P=0.26).

All three medicaments produced statistically significant reductions in bacterial load from baseline to post-treatment at both time points. Paired t-test results (mean before → mean after, mean reduction, Cohen's d): RG-Dent 2-day: 5.11→0.32, reduction 4.79, d=19.7; RG-Dent 7-day: 5.09→0.34, reduction 4.75, d=18.7. CHX 2%: 2-day: 5.12→0.18, reduction 4.94, d=22.9; 7-day: 5.10→0.17, reduction 4.93, d=22.7. Ca(OH)2: 2-day: 5.13→1.47, reduction 3.66, d=15.0; 7-day: 5.11→1.40, reduction 3.71, d=14.1. All statistically significant (P<0.05).

Main effects: Two-way ANOVA revealed statistically significant and very large main effect for medicament type on logCFU reduction (F(2,54)=178.9, P<0.001, η²=0.869) — ~87% of variance explained by medicament choice. No significant main effect for application duration (F(1,54)=0.078, P=0.781, η²=0.001); no significant interaction effect (F(2,54)=0.026, P=0.974, η²=0.001) — effect of medicament did not depend on application time.

Pairwise comparisons (Tukey HSD): CHX vs. Ca(OH)2: mean difference 1.25 (95% CI 1.10-1.40), P<0.001, Cohen's d=5.08 (very large). RG-Dent vs. Ca(OH)2: mean difference 1.09 (95% CI 0.92-1.25), P<0.001, d=4.13 (very large). CHX vs. RG-Dent: mean difference 0.16 (95% CI 0.02-0.32), P=0.036, d=0.69 (medium). Post-hoc power analysis: >99% power to detect medicament-type effect; 56% power for CHX vs RG-Dent difference — larger sample needed to confirm this smaller difference with higher confidence.

Discussion

This in vitro study provides rigorous comparative evidence on antimicrobial efficacy of three intracanal medicaments against E. faecalis in structured intracanal microbial communities. Findings reject one of the two null hypotheses: significant differences exist among RG-Dent, 2% CHX gel, and Ca(OH)2, while extending application from 2 to 7 days provides no additional antimicrobial benefit. Both CHX and RG-Dent were profoundly more effective than Ca(OH)2, with very large effect sizes (Cohen's d>4). Lack of duration effect suggests maximal bactericidal activity is achieved within first 48h, challenging traditional 7-day protocols.

Superior efficacy of chlorhexidine: Results (mean reduction 4.94 logCFU) corroborate findings by Gomes et al. and Krithikadatta et al. reporting superior CHX efficacy vs Ca(OH)2. Mechanisms: membrane disruption via binding to negatively charged bacterial cell walls, protein precipitation at higher concentrations. CHX penetrates dentinal tubules to depths of 100-400μm. Very large effect size (d=5.08) supports Saatchi et al. systematic review conclusions that CHX (alone or combined with Ca(OH)2) is more effective than Ca(OH)2 alone.

Novel findings on RG-Dent efficacy: Potent antimicrobial activity (mean reduction 4.77 logCFU) is a novel finding — this multi-component paste had not been rigorously evaluated in peer-reviewed literature prior to this study. Likely attributable to synergistic effects: metronidazole interferes with DNA synthesis in anaerobic bacteria; iodoform provides sustained iodine release with broad-spectrum antiseptic activity (clinicians should be mindful of hypersensitivity/discoloration risk with iodoform, though likely lower with short 2-7 day application periods); dexamethasone provides anti-inflammatory properties (not captured in this in vitro model). Statistical comparability to CHX (P=0.036, d=0.69) positions RG-Dent as viable alternative, particularly for suspected polymicrobial anaerobic infections. RG-Dent does not contain minocycline (the primary cause of TAP-associated tooth discoloration), potentially mitigating this concern, though general concerns about antibiotic-based medicaments (cytotoxicity, resistance development) remain.

Limitations of calcium hydroxide against E. faecalis: Significant inferiority of Ca(OH)2 (mean reduction 3.69 logCFU) has important clinical implications. Despite widespread historical use, achieved substantially lower bacterial reduction than CHX or RG-Dent (P<0.001, d>4). Supports understanding that E. faecalis possesses robust defense mechanisms: proton pumps maintain intracellular pH homeostasis even at pH 11-12; dentin buffering reduces pH at medicament-dentin interface; biofilm extracellular polymeric substance matrix limits Ca(OH)2 penetration. Aligns with Teja et al. (n=400) and Çorak et al. findings. While Ca(OH)2 retains value for biological properties (hard tissue formation, anti-inflammatory effects), findings suggest it should not be relied upon as sole agent for disinfecting canals infected with E. faecalis. Clinicians may consider combining Ca(OH)2 with CHX or nanoparticles, or opt for more effective alternatives (CHX or RG-Dent).

Clinical implications of application duration: Most clinically significant finding is lack of difference between 2-day and 7-day application for any medicament (P=0.78, no interaction P=0.97) — maximal bactericidal activity against E. faecalis achieved within first 48h, challenging traditional 7-day-or-longer recommendation. Mechanisms: CHX and RG-Dent's primary antimicrobial effect occurs rapidly upon contact (CHX disrupts membranes within minutes to hours; antibiotic-based medicaments act within 24-48h); medicaments penetrating to reach bacteria in dentinal tubules do so relatively quickly with diminishing returns from additional time; remaining bacteria after initial elimination may be in areas inaccessible to medicaments. Clinically, chlorhexidine should not be introduced immediately after NaOCl without intermediate saline rinse (avoids insoluble precipitate formation).

Shorter inter-appointment medicament period may facilitate earlier obturation and reduce time temporary restoration remains in service, potentially lowering interim leakage-related complications — though clinical outcomes/cost-effectiveness were not directly assessed in this in vitro study. Superior CHX/RG-Dent performance supports their consideration as first-line intracanal medicaments for persistent E. faecalis infections, balanced against cost, availability, handling, discoloration risk, and allergy risk considerations.

Strengths: 21-day contamination protocol based on validated in vitro models reproduces long-term intracanal infection; standardized tooth preparation minimized specimen variability; three clinically relevant medicaments compared under identical conditions at two application times; adequate sample size (n=10/subgroup) for large effects with high power; blinded CFU counting.

Limitations: In vitro nature cannot fully replicate in vivo conditions (host immune response, tissue interactions, fluid dynamics); monospecies model doesn't reflect polymicrobial nature of endodontic infections; did not assess cytotoxicity to periapical tissues or effects on dentin structure/properties; RG-Dent's metronidazole content — effects on fetal organogenesis not fully known (relevant for pregnant patients, though human studies have not shown clear increase in congenital anomalies); only two time points evaluated; combination therapies not investigated; moderate statistical power (56%) for CHX vs RG-Dent difference suggests larger samples needed; CFU counting quantifies only viable culturable bacteria.

Conclusion

Within the limitations of this in vitro root canal infection model, both 2% chlorhexidine gel and RG-Dent paste demonstrated greater antimicrobial activity against E. faecalis than calcium hydroxide. Chlorhexidine and RG-Dent showed comparable performance, supporting their consideration as intracanal medicament options in persistent E. faecalis-associated infection scenarios. Extending intracanal medicament application from 2 to 7 days did not provide additional benefit under the conditions tested, suggesting shorter application periods may be sufficient to achieve maximal antimicrobial effect in this model. In contrast, calcium hydroxide showed lower antimicrobial performance, supporting the need to consider alternative or adjunctive strategies in challenging cases. Clinically, these findings may support more streamlined inter-appointment protocols; however, translation to practice requires confirmation in clinical studies.
