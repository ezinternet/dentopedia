Kohli D, Madhu N, Korczeniewska OA, Eliav T, Arany S. Association between medication-induced xerostomia and orofacial pain: a systematic review. Quintessence Int 2023;54(8):658-670.

ABSTRACT
Xerostomia (or oral dryness) is most commonly caused by medications that affect saliva secretion, and is often accompanied by symptoms of orofacial pain. Medication-induced xerostomia may or may not be associated with objectively demonstrable hyposalivation. The present study attempted to systematically identify an association between medication-induced xerostomia and orofacial pain.
Methods: A systematic search was conducted using WoS, PubMed, SCOPUS, and MEDLINE. Search terms: xerostomia OR "dry mouth" AND medication AND ("oral pain" OR "orofacial pain" OR "craniofacial pain" OR "burning mouth" OR "glossodynia") NOT Sjogren's NOT cancer. Inclusion criteria were medication-induced xerostomia and reported symptoms of orofacial pain. Four researchers performed the selection process and quality assessment and two researchers conducted data extraction.
Results: Seven studies with a total of 1,029 patients were included. Studies were conducted between 2009 and 2022 and consisted of cross-sectional studies, case-control studies, and one randomized crossover trial. All studies included male and female participants whose mean ages ranged from 43 to 100 years.
Conclusions: A positive association was found between medication-induced xerostomia and orofacial pain. No associations were found between salivary flow measurements (hyposalivation) and medication use. Future research should focus on saliva flow measurements, standardized assessment of medication-induced xerostomia, as well as the inclusion of accompanying orofacial pain diagnosis in the medical history.

KEY RESULTS EXCERPTS (from PMC full text)
- Hyposalivation defined as unstimulated salivation rate <0.3 ml/min. Prevalence of xerostomia in adults 14-46%; two studies reported medication-related xerostomia 27-30% vs 14-16% in non-medicated. Over 600 medications have anticholinergic effects. Orofacial pain affects 10-15% of adults.
- PRISMA; PICO: P patients taking xerostomia-causing medications; I medications; C xerostomia vs no xerostomia; O orofacial pain. Databases WoS, PubMed, SCOPUS, MEDLINE (Ovid). English only; studies exclusively on burning mouth syndrome excluded. Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for quality. Narrative synthesis; no meta-analysis.
- Seven studies, 1029 participants, 2009-2022; cross-sectional, case-control, and one randomized crossover trial; mean ages 43 to 100 years.
- Michalak 2022 (institutionalized seniors): oral complaints dryness 36%, lack of saliva 30%, pain 13.8%; higher mirror-sliding resistance (dryness) associated with higher oral pain VAS (p=0.018) and more medications (p=0.014).
- Van de Rijt 2020 (dementia residents): orofacial pain 48.8% by OPS-NVI, self-reported 37.8%; xerostomia a significant predictor of orofacial pain (OR 1.04, p<0.05); summated xerostomia inventory median 6 (IQR 5-10).
- Yuen 2009 (spinal cord injury): oral pain past 12 months 42%; ~60% xerostomia; oral pain positively associated with dry mouth.
- Barbe 2018 (all with medication-induced xerostomia): whole stimulated salivary rate 0.3 +/- 0.2 ml/min (Gum Hydral) and 0.4 +/- 0.2 ml/min (Biotene); mean oral pain score 0.8, dryness score 2.3-2.5, VAS dryness 70-80/100.
- Beker 2019 (centenarians): 18% xerostomia symptoms, 12% oral pain/discomfort; xerostomia associated with medication use (p=0.001; antidepressants, diuretics).
- da Silva 2011: 78% on medications; amitriptyline 35%, antihypertensives 16% (associated with more dry mouth, p=0.007); xerostomia inventory 45% dry mouth and 60% oral discomfort in orofacial pain vs 39% and 14% controls; salivary flow 0.125 vs 0.175 g/min (p=0.008); pain VAS mean 8.01.
- de Siqueira 2021: xerostomia inventory 7.14 vs 5.04 in craniofacial pain vs controls (p<0.001); 62.6% of craniofacial pain patients reported xerostomia; medication count weakly correlated with dry mucosa score; craniofacial pain patients took more medications (p<0.001); 46% took non-analgesic medications.
- Four of seven studies reported medications (antidepressants in all four).
LIMITATIONS: none of the studies included a control group without xerostomia; only one RCT; recall-based self-report; no dose/duration; xerogenic and non-xerogenic drugs combined; only two studies measured salivary flow; heterogeneous non-comparable scales; risk of bias could not be assessed (one RCT). No meta-analysis possible; conclusions to be relied on with prudence.
