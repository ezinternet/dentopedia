Title: Whitish patches on the buccal mucosa: Role of dermoscopy
Authors: Massara Baklouti, Emna Mnif, Khadija Sellami, Hamida Turki
Journal: Clinical Case Reports, 2023;11(2):e6946
PMID: 36846171
PMCID: PMC9944038
DOI: 10.1002/ccr3.6946

Abstract

Morsicatio is caused by chronic self-inflicted biting of the buccal mucosa that results in clinically whitish plaques. It is frequently confused with other dermatological mucosal disorders. To avoid needless invasive procedures, dermoscopy can help with differential diagnosis. Dermoscopy shows whitish and yellowish structureless areas and lines, small erosions, and some white scales. The lack of additional, more specific signs such as Wickham striae is crucial to guide the diagnosis. Dermoscopy can help to make the right diagnosis in front of whitish patches of the buccal mucosa, avoiding unnecessary invasive procedures and inappropriate treatment.

Full Text (Case Report)

A 46-year-old woman, with no medical history, presented with asymptomatic whitish plaques on her lips that had appeared 2 years earlier. She had received topical steroid treatment for a presumed diagnosis of oral lichen planus without improvement. On examination she had scaly, jagged, irregularly whitish patches with some thin erosions on the inner side of the lips. No other skin lesions or systemic symptoms were found.

Dermoscopy (Dermlite DL4 - 3Gen) revealed whitish and yellowish structureless areas and lines, small superficial erosions with dotted vessels, and some white scales without Wickham striae. The scales were loose and simple to remove.

The diagnosis of morsicatio labiorum was suspected and then validated by the patient herself, who admitted having a nervous temperament and intermittent self-mutilating behavior by biting her lips.

Discussion

Morsicatio is caused by chronic self-inflicted biting of the lips (labiorum) or buccal mucosa (buccarum), causing tissue alteration; in some cases linked to stress or mental disorder. Clinically it is characterized by gray-white desquamative mucosa patches and is frequently mistaken for other dermatological mucosal disorders — oral lichen planus was the main differential diagnosis in this case. Most patients are not even conscious of their biting habit, and misdiagnosis can lead to unnecessary invasive investigations.

Roughness, a desquamative peeling surface, bilaterality, and localization on movable mucosa help distinguish morsicatio from other whitish mucosal lesions. In some circumstances clinical doubt necessitates biopsy, but dermoscopy can aid diagnosis when lesions are easily accessible. Wickham striae are a pathognomonic dermoscopic sign of mucosal lichen planus; other distinguishing signs of lichen planus include dotted/linear/hairpin vessels, pigmented structures, and erosions with a white hyperkeratotic border — none of these were present in this case.

Dermoscopy can help avoid needless invasive procedures and inappropriate therapy when lesions are accessible. The dermoscope should be cleaned with 70% isopropyl alcohol or protected with transparent film before/after use. Simple patient counseling on avoiding lip biting is the mainstay of management; psychiatric evaluation may be needed in refractory cases. The authors state this is the first case reporting dermoscopic findings specifically in morsicatio labiorum.
