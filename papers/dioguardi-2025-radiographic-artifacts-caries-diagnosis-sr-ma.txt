Radiographic artifacts in the diagnosis of dental caries: systematic review with meta-analysis
Dioguardi M, Guerra C, Sovereto D, Martella A, Zhurakivska K, Tisci A, Esperouz F, Bizzoca ME, Sanesi L, Mastrangelo F, Lo Muzio L, Cirillo N, Ciavarella D, Ballini A.
Oral Radiology. 2025;42(2):281-296.
DOI: 10.1007/s11282-025-00879-2
PMID: 41331196  PMCID: PMC12988984
Source: PubMed Central full text (get_full_text_article)
Registration: PROSPERO CRD420251083823; PRISMA-compliant.

=== ABSTRACT ===
Radiographic caries diagnosis is complicated by radiographic OPTICAL effects — the Mach band effect and triangular-shaped radiolucencies (TSR) — which cause FALSE POSITIVES, especially in bitewing radiographs, risking overdiagnosis/overtreatment. Aim: clarify the real prevalence of these optical effects and quantify their influence on caries diagnostic errors.
SR+MA (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD420251083823). PubMed/Scopus/Cochrane + grey literature. Inclusion: clinical/in vitro/ex vivo studies on TSR and Mach band on intraoral radiographs (bitewing or periapical) reporting diagnostic performance/prevalence/misinterpretation. Risk of bias: AXIS, QUADAS-2. Random-effects meta-analyses.
Of 640 reports, 5 studies included. Overall prevalence of non-carious TSR on maxillary molars = 26.44% (270/1021). Meta-analysis: optical effects (TSR or Mach band) → false-positive caries/fracture diagnoses in ~13% of observations (60/464). High heterogeneity (I² > 90%); certainty low to very low.
Conclusion: optical effects (esp. TSR, Mach band) are highly prevalent in bitewing images and significantly increase false-positive caries diagnoses, especially in children. Interpret radiographs with caution; correlate with clinical exam.

=== THE THREE OPTICAL EFFECTS ===
1) CERVICAL BURNOUT: radiographic effect mimicking a carious lesion near the cementum-enamel junction (CEJ). X-ray beam passes tangentially across the curved proximal tooth surface → less dental structure → localized apparent radiolucency, typically between CEJ and alveolar crest where dentin is thinner. More pronounced in multirooted teeth when buccal/lingual roots don't overlap; shallow root bifurcation accentuates it.
2) MACH BAND EFFECT: optical ILLUSION — adjacent areas of differing density/brightness make the eye perceive exaggerated contrast at the interface. At the enamel-dentine junction (EDJ) on bitewing → apparent radiolucency suggesting caries. Positive Mach band = lighter band where radiopaque meets radiolucent; negative Mach band = darker band at the border. Masking the radiopaque enamel: if band disappears → false-positive optical effect; if persists → possible true outer-dentine lesion.
3) TRIANGULAR-SHAPED RADIOLUCENCIES (TSR): non-carious, predominantly on MESIAL surfaces of MAXILLARY molars (deciduous & permanent). Attributed to superimposition of the Carabelli cusp + maxillary-molar anatomy. Mach-band-related. Commonly mistaken for carious lesions.

=== METHODS ===
PRISMA + Cochrane Handbook. PROSPERO CRD420251083823. Databases: Cochrane, PubMed, Scopus + grey literature (Google Scholar, ScienceDirect, DANS). Search: "triangular-shaped radiolucencies" OR "Mach band". Two reviewers screen/extract; third adjudicates. Risk of bias: AXIS (primary/prevalence outcome), QUADAS-2 (secondary/diagnostic outcome). GRADE for certainty. Meta-analysis: OpenMeta[Analyst] v10, DerSimonian-Laird random effects. Publication bias: Egger's test + funnel plot.

Two outcomes:
- Primary (PICO): prevalence of non-carious TSR on deciduous/permanent molars.
- Secondary (PICO): proportion of false-positive diagnoses (TSR/Mach band misread as caries or fracture).

=== STUDY SELECTION ===
640 records → 589 after dedup → 9 full-text → 5 included (2 primary outcome, 3 secondary). 5 studies published 2001–2021.
Primary outcome studies (TSR prevalence, bitewing): Kühnisch 2008 (Germany, 113 children 11–12y, 445 surfaces), Khayam 2013 (Iran, 144 adults 18–24y, 576 molars/288 bitewings).
Secondary outcome studies (false positives): Nielsen 2001 (USA, 73 participants, Mach band as fracture, 26.0% FP), Movahhedian 2016 (Iran, in vitro, 94 observers×3 teeth, TSR as caries, 12.8% FP), Tabari 2021 (Iran, in vivo, 109 teeth pediatric, TSR as caries, 4.6% FP).

=== META-ANALYSIS RESULTS ===
PRIMARY — TSR prevalence on maxillary molars:
- Raw pooled: 270/1021 = 26.44%.
- Random-effects pooled proportion: 0.308 (95% CI 0.129–0.487), I² ≈ 98.1%, p<0.001.
- Subgroups: PERMANENT molars 0.199 (95% CI 0.036–0.363; 158/802; I²=97.5%); PRIMARY molars 0.477 (95% CI 0.225–0.728; 112/223; I²=92.1%).
- By tooth type: second primary molar >60% (60.3%), primary first molar 34.6%, permanent first molar 24.8–29.5%, permanent second molar 5.9%.
- Pooled by group: primary molars 51.1% vs permanent 27.4% — TSR much more prevalent in DECIDUOUS dentition (pediatric risk).

SECONDARY — false positives from optical effects:
- 60/464 = 12.93%; pooled 0.133 (95% CI 0.041–0.225), I²=89.5%, p=0.004.
- Range across studies: 4.6% (Tabari, TSR/in vivo) to 26.0% (Nielsen, Mach band as fracture/simulated).

Publication bias: Egger's test intercept −6.91, p=0.5677 → no significant asymmetry (but low power, few studies).
GRADE: primary outcome LOW; secondary outcome VERY LOW certainty (observational, high inconsistency I²>90%, indirectness, imprecision).

=== MITIGATION / CLINICAL ===
- MASKING TEST: opaque card over enamel margin — radiolucency disappears → Mach band (false positive); persists → true demineralization.
- Digital software / contrast adjustment (e.g., Qaramaleki et al.): analyzes grey-level variation within radiolucent area — varying grey → possible caries; uniform → Mach band illusion.
- Beam angle / centering, digital post-processing, and (future) AI-assisted tools.
- Clinical impact: ~13% false positives risk unnecessary restorative/endodontic/extraction treatment; over-two-thirds overestimation reported in one example; pediatric primary molars especially at risk. Radiography should support, not confound, clinical diagnosis.

=== LIMITATIONS ===
Only 5 studies; high heterogeneity (I²>90%); GRADE low–very low. Many studies used extracted teeth / lab / student observers, not real patients; no standardized TSR/Mach-band criteria; radiographic (not histological/clinical) reference in several. Need prospective, multicenter, standardized studies + observer training + AI.

=== CONCLUSION ===
Radiographic optical effects (TSR, Mach band, cervical burnout) have ~26% prevalence in bitewing radiographs (TSR mostly mesial maxillary molars), and cause ~13% false-positive caries/fracture diagnoses. Given low certainty, clinicians should combine radiographs with thorough clinical exam. TSR notably more prevalent in primary dentition (pediatric).
