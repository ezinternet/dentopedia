Title: Association between anticholinergic activity and xerostomia and/ or xerophthalmia in the elderly: systematic review
Prado-Mel E, Ciudad-Gutierrez P, Rodriguez-Ramallo H, Sanchez-Fidalgo S, Santos-Ramos B, Villalba-Moreno AM. BMC Pharmacol Toxicol 2022;23(1):94. Published 2022-12-21.

ABSTRACT
The aim: investigate the association between anticholinergic burden or anticholinergic drug use and xerostomia and/or xerophthalmia in elderly. Search in CINAHL, Embase and PubMed up to August 2022; people >=65 years; English/Spanish. 1211 articles identified, 10 selected: six cross-sectional studies, two cohorts, one case-control and one RCT (abstract). A total of 3535 patients (abstract). Most used scales: ADS and ARS. Four articles studied anticholinergic use vs xerostomia and/or xerophthalmia, positive relationship with xerostomia in all. Another 6 measured anticholinergic burden; four found positive relationship with xerostomia and/or xerophthalmia. Conclusion: clear relationship between anticholinergic drugs/burden and xerostomia; less conclusive for xerophthalmia.

Methods: Systematic review following CRD principles and PRISMA. Databases: PUBMED, CINAHL, EMBASE, until August 2022. Inclusion: observational or experimental studies relating anticholinergic activity (risk or burden), or failing that anticholinergic therapy, to xerostomia or xerophthalmia in people aged 65 or older in any clinical or social context. Excluded: letters, dissertations, clinical cases, case series, conference abstracts, languages other than Spanish or English. Two reviewers screened (first 50 references jointly); discrepancies resolved by two external reviewers. Confounders considered - Xerostomia: diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, Sjogren syndrome, polypharmacy, radiotherapy. Xerophthalmia: diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, Sjogren syndrome, polypharmacy, eye surgery. Quality: JBI checklist (cross-sectional), Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (cohort, case-control), Cochrane RoB 2 (randomized trials).

Results: Search of three databases resulted in 1211 studies, 11 duplicates removed. 1136 studies excluded on title/abstract, 64 pre-selected, 10 finally included: six descriptive studies, three analytical observational studies and one clinical trial. Collectively the 10 studies involved 3622 patients with an average age of over 75 years. Contexts: living alone, care homes, nursing homes and acute hospital wards; nearly all observational, high-income Western countries. Six described xerostomia and four reported both adverse effects. Seven articles gave prevalence data, xerostomia 10.6-59.2%. Only three measured xerophthalmia, prevalence 5.1-34%. Xerostomia assessed by self-administered questionnaires in five studies, clinical tests in three, one combined. Xerophthalmia: three questionnaires, one clinical test. Anticholinergic exposure ranged 3.9 to 100%. Only five studies report most prevalent drug class; antihistamines most prevalent in three of the five. Of five studies measuring burden, three used ARS, two ADS, one DBI and ACB in addition to ARS and ADS.
Four studies found a statistically significant association between anticholinergic use and prevalence of xerostomia. Only one study measured xerophthalmia vs anticholinergic use, found non-existent. Anticholinergic burden: Rudolph JL et al found positive association but with compound variable (peripheral effects: xerostomia, xerophthalmia, constipation). Positive association when comparing xerostomia prevalence in high burden vs lower or no burden. Xerostomia and xerophthalmia more prevalent in patients with significantly higher burden. No association when comparing any level of burden vs none (not stratified). The one clinical trial, reducing median ADS score by 2 units, did not show any difference in salivary secretion 4 weeks after intervention; similar salivary flow rates in both groups.
Confounding: all but one considered polypharmacy; three used Functional Comorbidity Index or Charlson Index for diabetes/RA; all used multivariable analysis adjusted to these covariates. None measured head and neck radiotherapy, eye surgery, or Sjogren syndrome. Quality: two cross-sectional studies methodologically adequate (>=7 of 8 JBI items). Rest of observational studies at risk of bias; main problems non-validated tools and lack of confounder control. Cohort studies NOS 6 and 5 stars; case-control NOS 6 (max 9). Clinical trial low risk of bias by RoB 2.

Discussion: very few papers assess these peripheral effects; heterogeneous methods. Xerostomia not necessarily hyposalivation (Villa et al). At least four different questionnaires, two unspecified; Xerostomia Inventory not used. ARS includes only 49 drugs, ADS 117; DBI considers dosage. Association close with high burden (ARS = 3), less conclusive at low/intermediate burden. Trial null result perhaps because deprescription impossible/limited. Xerophthalmia inconclusive due to few studies. Limitations: few studies, mostly observational, one clinical trial.
Conclusion: very few data on xerophthalmia; closer connection between high anticholinergic burden/use and xerostomia. Further studies with validated tools needed.
