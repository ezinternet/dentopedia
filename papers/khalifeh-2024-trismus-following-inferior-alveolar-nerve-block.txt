Title: Trismus Following Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block: A Case Report
Authors: Khalifeh B, Simonian K, Houmani M, El-Outa A, Khalil W
Journal: Cureus
Year: 2024
DOI: 10.7759/cureus.73394
PMID: 39659340

CASE: 59-year-old female, no significant medical history. Routine restorative treatment tooth 47; IANB administered twice (~3.4cc Persocaine® — 2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine). 12 hours later: right lower face oedema, mouth opening 18mm, lateral movement 9mm right / 3mm left, pain on TMJ + medial pterygoid palpation.

6 MECHANISMS OF IANB-RELATED TRISMUS:
1. Accidental medial pterygoid muscle penetration → muscle spasm
2. Intramuscular hematoma (inferior alveolar a/v injury → pterygomandibular space hematoma)
3. Needle-track infection (delayed onset 2–3 days)
4. Myotoxicity from local anesthetic
5. Barbed needle tip tearing muscle fibers on withdrawal
6. Large volume injection into restricted space → tissue expansion

MANAGEMENT TIMELINE:
Day 1–7: warm application + jaw exercises + Muscerol 2 (paracetamol+orphenadrine) + ibuprofen 400mg + aescin 20mg → Day 7: oedema resolved, NO improvement in mouth opening (18mm)
Day 8–12: IM Difen B12® (diclofenac + beclomethasone + cyanocobalamin) ×5 days + Augmentin® Day 10 → oedema resolved but opening worsened to 13mm (forced exercise)
Day 12–50: increased exercise frequency → progressive improvement: 15mm(D12), 17mm(D13), 19mm(D14), 28mm(D30), 42mm(D50) → full recovery maintained at 1-month follow-up

KEY POINTS:
- Normal mouth opening: 35–60 mm (women ≥35mm, men ≥40mm)
- Acute iatrogenic trismus: resolves typically 2–6 weeks (range 4–20 weeks)
- Chronic trismus risk: delayed management → scar tissue → permanent restriction
- Dexamethasone evidence: submucosal 4mg reduces post-third-molar oedema; submucosal route superior to IM for trismus per Kaewkumnert et al.
- Early, progressive jaw exercise is cornerstone of recovery
