Abstract Background/purpose: T raumatic loss of anterior teeth creates esthetic challenges
and often requires immediate rehabilitation. Although immediate implant placement and
restoration (IIPR) shortens treatment time, strict primary stability limits its use. A native-
crown restoration supported by fiber-reinforced composite (FRC) splints may broaden the in-
dications of immediate restoration, but its effectiveness remains unclear . This randomized
controlled trial aimed to evaluate the effects of the novel immediate restoration following
IIP on peri-implant bone remodeling in anterior esthetic zone trauma during osseointegration.
Materials and methods: T wenty participants undergoing IIP in the anterior maxilla were ran-
domized into two groups. In the experimental group, immediate restorations were performed
using patients’ native crowns stabilized with FRC splints, while the control group received con-
ventional delayed restorations. P eri-implant bone remodeling was quantitatively assessed
through cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) at baseline and 4 —5 months postopera-
tively . Secondary outcomes included gingival healing and implant stability quotient (ISQ).
Results: Both groups achieved 100% implant survival. Complete soft tissue closure occurred
within 4—5 months in all cases, with no significant intergroup differences. Similarly , no signif-
icant intergroup differences were found in peri-implant bone changes at the implant neck

Introduction
Dental trauma predominantly affects the maxillary anterior
region, commonly recognized as the esthetic zone.
1
In se-
vere and complex cases of dental trauma, tooth extraction
may represent the sole viable treatment option.
2
However ,
tooth loss, especially in the anterior esthetic zone, is
generally unacceptable due to the sudden occurrence and
profound impacts on quality of life.
3
These patients
consequently experience dual detrimental effects involving
both physical impairment and psychological distress.
4
Immediate implant placement (IIP) and restoration
following extraction of maxillary anterior teeth contribute
to restoring patient oral health and alleviating psychologi-
cal stress by rapidly rehabilitating both esthetics and
function in the anterior esthetic zone.
5
With the develop-
ment of implant technology and the theory of guided bone
regeneration (GBR), the indications for IIP have been
correspondingly broadened beyond previously strict
criteria.
6
Recent studies have demonstrated that successful
osseointegration may still be achieved even in the presence
of pulpal or periodontal inflammation.
7
However , immedi-
ate restoration following IIP requires strict adherence to
eligibility criteria, including optimal three-dimensional
implant positioning, adequate primary implant stability and
patient compliance, to ensure implant success.
8,9
As con-
ventional immediate restoration protocols rely on directly
loaded provisional crowns supported by temporary abut-
ments, adequate primary implant stability is a prerequisite.
When primary stability is insufficient during IIP , directly
loaded provisionalization becomes contraindicated, neces-
sitating either removable provisional prostheses or delayed
restoration protocols to minimize failure risks.
10
However ,
both approaches provide limited peri-implant soft tissue
support and are unable to effectively preserve natural
gingival contour or emergence profile during early healing.
This may compromise the clinical significance of immediate
implantation and the patients’ expectations to some
extent.
Inspired by post-traumatic splinting therapy for mobile
teeth, we developed a modified immediate restoration
strategy in which fiber-reinforced composite (FRC) splints
are used to bond the patient’ s native crown to the labial
surfaces of adjacent teeth following IIP .
11
Unlike conven-
tional immediately loaded provisional restorations, this
protocol provides immediate esthetic rehabilitation
without direct occlusal loading on the implant. Existing
evidence demonstrates that FRC splints exhibit exceptional
load-bearing capacity , maintaining structural integrity
under maximal occlusal forces generated during parafunc-
tional movements including bruxism.
12,13
Furthermore, use
of the patient’ s native crown may facilitate preservation of
the original cervical contour and peri-implant gingival ar-
chitecture, thereby supporting more favorable esthetic soft
tissue healing. Nevertheless, clinical evidence regarding
the safety and biological impact of this non-loaded imme-
diate restoration approach―particularly its influence on
peri-implant bone remodeling―remains limited.
Therefore, this randomized controlled clinical trial was
designed as a pilot exploratory study to evaluate the ef-
fects of this novel immediate restoration following IIP on
peri-implant bone remodeling in anterior esthetic zone
trauma during osseointegration.
Materials and methods
This study was approved by the institutional ethics com-
mittee (Approval No. SH9H-2022-T358-1). The trial was
conducted and monitored in accordance with the principles
of Good Clinical P ractice and followed the CONSOR T
reporting guidelines. It was prospectively registered at the
Chinese Clinical T rial R egistry (ChiCTR2300068934). All
participants provided written informed consent prior to
enrollment.
This parallel-group randomized controlled trial assessed
the safety and the impact on peri-implant bone remodeling
of a novel immediate restoration protocol using native
crowns combined with FRC splints (RTD, Saint-E

France) following IIP in the esthetic zone. The primary
outcome was labial bone plate change. Secondary out-
comes were implant survival rate and implant stability
quotient (ISQ). Labial bone plate thickness at implant sites
was assessed via cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT)
at baseline and 4—5 months postoperatively , with ISQ
measured at the corresponding postoperative interval.
P atients with traumatic injuries requiring single imme-
diate implant placement in the maxillary anterior teeth
were consecutively enrolled from the Department of Gen-
eral Dentistry . Inclusion criteria included patients aged 18
years or older who required extraction of traumatized
maxillary anterior teeth, had intact mesial and distal
adjacent teeth, a mouth opening of at least 30 mm, and
stable occlusion of the adjacent dentition. Exclusion
criteria were insufficient space for implant placement and

P articipant allocation employed simple randomization.
Randomization codes were generated using SPSS 21.0 (SPSS
Inc., Chicago, IL, USA), with the time variable serving as the

codes were sealed in opaque envelopes. P articipants
receiving odd-numbered codes were assigned to the
experimental group, while even-numbered codes were
assigned to the control group.

numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes based on enrollment
order . The experimental group received immediate restora-

closure after IIP with subsequent delayed restoration. Given
the distinct interventions, participant blinding was
