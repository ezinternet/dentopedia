ABSTRACT

Background

Temporomandibular disorders (TMD) are a set of musculoskeletal conditions involving the temporomandibular joint, masticatory muscles, and/or associated structures, characterized by symptoms as pain, joint stiffness with limited mouth opening, and joint sounds as crepitus. Rheumatic diseases (RD) are a heterogeneous group of conditions affecting the musculoskeletal system, including temporomandibular joint (TMJ). To date, there is a lack of systematic reviews that properly investigated the efficacy of conservative approaches in reducing pain in rheumatic patients affected by TMJ arthritis. Therefore, this systematic review aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of rehabilitative approaches in pain relief in rheumatic patients with TMJ arthritis.

Methods

PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science were searched from inception until February 25th, 2024, to identify studies including patients with diagnosis of rheumatic disease affecting the temporomandibular joint who underwent specific rehabilitative approaches to reduce pain intensity. The risk of bias of studies was assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Checklist.

Results

Out of 479 search results, 115 duplicates were removed, and 364 studies were considered as eligible for inclusion and screened for title and abstract. Out of these, we included 19 papers for full-text screening. Then, 5 papers were included in the synthesis by this systematic review. Four studies assessed patients affected by rheumatoid arthritis, one systemic sclerodermia, and one included patients affected by ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, Sjogren's syndrome, fibromyalgia, common variable immunodeficiency, and chronic polyarthritis. In the included studies, the interventions consisted of intraarticular TMJ injection of corticosteroids performed with or without anesthetics, or irrigation in three studies, dextrose subcutaneous TMJ perineural injection, and lower-level laser therapy (LLLT).

Conclusions

This systematic review showed that rehabilitative approaches (e.g., intra-articular injections and LLLT) might be effective in terms of pain relief in TMD RD-related. However, the heterogeneity of the rehabilitative approaches performed, and the low quality of the included studies do not allow to draw certain conclusions regarding the efficacy of these approaches. Further high-quality studies are mandatory to improve the robustness of the efficacy of the different rehabilitative techniques for pain relief in TMD patients affected by rheumatic diseases.

Introduction

Temporomandibular disorders (TMD) are a set of musculoskeletal conditions involving the temporomandibular joint, masticatory muscles, and/or associated structures []. TMD symptoms include joint sounds, pain, and restrictions in mandibular movements, and cause difficulty in performing basic daily activities as chewing, swallowing, and talking [].

Myofascial pain, disc displacement, arthralgia, and degenerative joint disease are possible TMD subtypes []. Degenerative joint diseases are characterized by deterioration and abrasion of TMJ soft and hard tissues, subchondral bone remodeling, and symptoms that include pain, joint stiffness with limited mouth opening, and joint sounds as crepitus []. Etiology of degenerative joint disease has been accepted as multifactorial, with several risk factors that include genetics, muscle or joints disturbance, anatomical factors, trauma, and systemic conditions as rheumatic diseases (RDs) [,].

RDs are a heterogeneous group of conditions involving the musculoskeletal system, including tendons, ligaments, muscles, bones, and connective tissues []. RDs affect health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and daily functioning and can cause high morbidity in the affected patient [].

The World Health Organization has reported rheumatic and musculoskeletal diseases among the main causes of disability [,]. Moreover, it has been estimated that RDs entail enormous financial costs, with Europe bearing almost 200 billion per year [].

Among RDs, rheumatoid arthritis (RA), systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), ankylosing spondylitis (AS), juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA), psoriatic arthritis (PA), osteoarthritis (OA), and systemic sclerosis (SSc) can cause inflammatory rheumatic arthritis, which represent rheumatic conditions solely confined to joints [,]. The TMJ could be involved in RDs, even in early stages of the disease [,].

Rheumatologists and dentists should evaluate patients with RDs for TMJ involvement and diagnosis should be performed through clinical assessment and radiological evaluation, in order to confirm the osseous changes in the affected TMJ, including osteophyte formation, flattening or deformity of the condylar head, cortical erosion and irregularities, joint space reduction, and subcortical cysts [].

Decreasing joint pain and improving joint function represent the main goals of rehabilitative approaches, which include behavioral therapy, physical therapy, exercises, occlusal splints, TMJ injections, laser therapy, transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS), and extracorporeal shockwave therapy [–].

To date, there is a lack of systematic reviews that properly investigated the efficacy of conservative and minimally invasive approaches in reducing pain in rheumatic patients affected by TMJ arthritis.

Therefore, the present systematic review aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of rehabilitative approaches in pain relief in rheumatic patients with TMJ arthritis.

Materials and methods

Eligibility criteria

This systematic review followed the focused question: 'Are the rehabilitative approaches effective in reducing pain in patients affected by rheumatic disease involving the TMJ?'. All studies were assessed for eligibility according to the following participants, intervention, comparison, outcomes, and study design (PICOs) model:

- Participants: patients with a diagnosis of rheumatic disease involving the temporomandibular joint;

- Intervention: rehabilitative approaches aimed at reducing pain;

- Comparison: placebo, sham treatments, or the approaches considered as interventions;

- Outcome: pain intensity at the TMJ, using visual analogue scale (VAS) or numerical rating scale (NRS).

- Study design: case series, cohort study, cross-sectional study, and randomized control trials (RCTs).

Only studies that evaluated and compared the effectiveness of rehabilitative approaches in pain relief in patients with TMJ RD using conservative approaches were included. Moreover, only studies providing data at the end of the intervention were included. Exclusion criteria were: (1) children or adolescents during growth; (2) history of TMJ trauma; (3) patients with congenital abnormality or neoplastic conditions in TMJ region; (4) cross-over study design; (5) studies written in a language different from English; (6) full-text unavailability (i.e., posters and conference abstracts); (7) studies involving animals.

This systematic review was conducted according to the guidance of Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines and the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions [,]. Systematic review protocol has been registered on the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) with number CRD42024517524.

Data extraction

Data were extracted by two independent reviewers using a data-extraction form developed for the review based on Microsoft Excel sheet. After a manual exclusion of the duplicates, a hierarchical method was used in the study evaluation following the PICOs model. In case of disagreement, the consensus was achieved through a third reviewer. Extracted data items included the number of original studies in the review, publication year, Nationality, study design, sample size and characteristics (e.g., age and population type), nature of the included interventions and control, outcomes measured (pain intensity), and main findings.

Quality assessment

The risk of bias was assessed by two reviewers using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Checklist. Any disagreement was discussed until a consensus was reached with a third reviewer. Moreover, we used the GRADE (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluations) approach [] to test quality of the evidence.

Main findings of the included studies

Alstergren et al. [] assessed the effects of GC (methylprednisolone 40 mg/ml) and lidocaine intraarticular injections in a cohort of 22 patients (29 TMJ) affected by rheumatic diseases. Patients were divided in specific or unspecific inflammatory joint disease and randomly evaluated at 2–3 weeks follow up or 4–6 weeks follow up. Authors found that GC injections reduced pain at rest and subjective symptoms (STR) at 2–3 weeks and 4–6 weeks follow up (VAS respectively reduced from 5.9 ± 2.8 to 3.3 ± 1.7 p-value < 0.001 and from 7.0 ± 1.0 to 5.1 ± 2.3 p-value < 0.05). Moreover, the pain during movement improved at 2–3 weeks follow up (from 4.1 ± 0.7 to 2.4 ± 1.7 p-value < 0.05), but the improvement was significant only in the group with specific inflammatory diagnosis (RA, AS, PA, SS and CVID). They analyzed Neuropeptide-Y-like Immunoreactivity (NPY-LI) in the joint fluid of the patients (comparing it with a control group of healthy subjects) and showed a significant reduction in patients affected by specific inflammatory joint disease at 2–3 weeks follow (p-value < 0.05).

Trieger et al. [] performed a preliminary study on 12 female patients with TMJ complaints affected by RA, showing that TMJ arthrocentesis followed by intraarticular GC injection (dexamethasone 8 mg/2 ml) was an effective treatment in reducing pain at 2,4 and 6 weeks follow up (mean decrease − 4.17 ± 2.52 p-value < 0.01). Results showed a better analgesic effect in both younger patients and patients with lower erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) at baseline.

Elshoura et al. [] analyzed 11 females affected by RA, who underwent from 4 to 6 subcutaneous perineural injection therapy of 5% dextrose and 8.4% sodium bicarbonate solution, around the auriculotemporal inflamed nerve and its site of constriction. At 4 weeks follow up VAS at rest was reduced from a median of 5 (0–8) to 3 (0–8) (p-value < 0.001), while VAS at max mouth opening was reduced from a median value of 7 (3–10) to 5 (1–10) (p-value < 0.001).

Benli et al. [] performed a single-blinded- RCT in order to evaluate the effect of LLLT in myogenic TMD patients affected by SLE. The study group was composed of 31 individuals, while 30 patients were treated with lower wavelength laser therapy (sham) and 30 controls with no treatment. They enrolled only patients with at least 5 of VAS and evaluated pain before the laser therapy (T0), immediately after the therapy (T1), and at 1-month follow-up (T2). Authors showed that at T1 a VAS was reduced in test and sham groups (median respectively of 5 (4–7) and 7(6–8) compared to a baseline of 8 (7–9) p-value < 0.001), whereas only in the study group results showed a significant reduction in chewing impairment (6283.7 ± 257.2 μm) at T1 and T2 compared with placebo (7076.9 ± 384.4 μm) and control (7224.3 ± 286 μm) (p-value < 0.05).

In the Kroese et al. study [], 35 subjects affected by RA with painful TMJ were treated with a single TMJ injection with GC (methylprednisolone 40 mg/ml) and lidocaine injection, with a follow up of a median of 3.1 weeks (T1) and 6.3 weeks (T2). A second injection was performed at T1 in 21 patients. The treatment reduced significantly pain at 3 weeks (median from 3 to 2, p-value < 0.01), however these benefits were not present at 6 weeks follow up. No side effects have been reported in each included study.

Discussion

RDs are systemic disorders that involve the musculoskeletal system and may impair many of the diarthrodial joints, and the TMJ is often involved early in the course of the disease [,].

TMJ degenerative diseases are frequently overlooked by both practitioners and patients, especially when systemic treatments are focused on other joints. However, TMJ impairment may lead to severe sequelae and disabilities that affect patient's quality of life, thus an early diagnosis and a timely and appropriate management are warranted. To date, several diagnostic methods was used to define the different causes TMJ, also in term of pathological and molecular mechanisms, using modern models of Artificial Intelligence [–].

A recent systematic review by Hysa et al. [] (including 56 papers) synthetized the prevalence of TMDs in patients affected by immune-mediated rheumatic diseases and evaluated the risk factors for their development. Among the several RDs affecting adult patients, the RA was reported as the disease that gathered most of the evidence on TMJ involvement. Due to the different measure of outcome assessment found in the literature, the authors reported a prevalence of TMJ impairment (in terms of pain and findings detectable at physical examination) ranging between 8% and 70%. Interestingly, the authors reported that TMJ symptoms seem to occur within 5 years from the RA onset, and the number of involved joints, global pain, and TNFα plasmatic concentration were identified as predictors. Several studies [–] reported that TMJ erosion detected by computerized tomography (CT) [] or conventional radiography [] is correlated with the RA disease activity and severity. To date, exercise, physical agent modalities, and intraarticular injection are recommended in several rheumatic diseases [,]. In this context, Maddali Bongi et al. evaluated the effects of two different 12-weeks rehabilitation protocols in a sample of patients affected by SSc and TMJ impairment. More in details, 22 subjects were randomly assigned to Group 1 (home exercises for TMJ, masticatory, mimic, and cervical spine muscles) and 25 subjects to Group 2 (home exercises and combined physiotherapeutic procedures performed by a physiotherapist). The authors showed that both rehabilitation protocols were effective in mouth opening, neck mobility, and pain, with higher improvements in Group 2, suggesting that the physiotherapist could personalize the exercises inducing better therapeutic effects.

Results from the present systematic review suggest that intra-articular injections of steroid represent a second line strategy in rheumatic diseases [], but their usefulness in terms of reducing paint at short and long term is still debated [,]. The paper by Vallon et al. compared the effects of intra-articular injections of steroid to non-steroid agents (sodiumhyaluronate or saline) into the TMJs of 21 RA patients []. The authors concluded that signs and symptoms were improved at the long-term follow-up (12 years). Specifically, two thirds of patients reported no TMJ tenderness or pain during function. Moreover, the long-term progression of joint destruction was low as no severe destructive or mutilating abnormality were found radiologically [].

At the light of our results, intraarticular injections of corticosteroid, with or without lidocaine, may represent an effective approach in reducing pain in patients affected by RD and TMD, at both short and long term [,,]. On other hand, positive effects of these treatment were supported by two low-quality studies. It well known that the systemic and articular manifestation of inflammatory process in RDs involve also the TMJ as condylar diarthrosis [,]. In addition, some inflammatory markers of rheumatic diseases have been linked to the progression of TMJ diseases [,]. For this reason, all the included studies demonstrated that the use of corticosteroids with high potency and structural characteristics, such as methylprednisolone and desametaxone, are effective in reducing pain. Moreover, only one study investigated the effectiveness of perineural injection of dextrose/sodium bicarbonate solution and of LLLT [,], making not possible recommendation on the use of these two techniques in management of TMD RD patients. On the other hand, at the light of results, both perineural injection and LLLT seem to be effective in pain management [,].

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first systematic review that analyze the effects of different conservative approaches in RD patients with TMD. A key advantage of our study is the assessment of papers quality, in terms both of risk of bias and strength of the evidence to give to the clinician a more scientific approach at TMD-RDs and the comprehensive search strategy on three different databases.

However, this systematic review has several limitations. Firstly, we would like to underline the low number of included studies that could negatively affect the clinical relevance of the study. However, this research adopted strict eligibility criteria and, at the same time, the investigated topic is not so present in the scientific literature. Secondly, the included studies evaluated different interventions. Thirdly, the low quality of the included studies and the risk of bias do not allow to draw a certain conclusion regarding the real efficacy of the analyzed treatment. Thus, a quantitative synthesis was not drawn due to the heterogeneity of the rehabilitative protocols and the heterogeneity of conditions, interventions, and outcomes made not possible to perform a meta-analysis.

However, this is the first systematic review in the scientific literature that summarized the evidence on the effectiveness of rehabilitative approaches in RD patients affected by TMJ arthritis.

Conclusion

Taken together, findings of this systematic review showed that rehabilitative approaches (e.g., intra-articular injections and LLLT) might be effective in reducing pain in RD patients affected by TMD, albeit the low quality of the included studies might affect these results. Indeed, further high-quality studies assessing larger samples are needed to better characterize the efficacy of rehabilitative approaches in these subjects.

In conclusion, conservative and rehabilitative treatments seemed not only to reduce pain, but also to improve mouth function, and quality of life, and could be taken into consideration for a multidisciplinary approach for the management of RD-related TMD patients.
