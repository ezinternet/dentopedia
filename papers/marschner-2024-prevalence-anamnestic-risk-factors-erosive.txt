Marschner F, Kanzow P, Wiegand A. Systematic review and meta-analysis on prevalence and anamnestic risk factors for erosive tooth wear in the primary dentition. Int J Paediatr Dent 2024;35(2):389-404. DOI 10.1111/ipd.13250. PMID 39056584. PMC11788517. (Source: PubMed / PMC full text; condensed working copy.)

ABSTRACT: SR+MA, PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42023397767. Searched PubMed, Embase, Scopus, WoS (Feb 2023) + gray literature. 26 sources / 23 observational (cross-sectional) studies, 16,777 participants, age 2-7 y. Prevalence 35.6% (95% CI 24.8-48.1). GERD OR 1.98 (1.37-2.87; p=.008 adj); acidic food OR 5.14 (3.56-7.42); acidic drinks OR 6.90 (4.64-10.25); holding drinks in mouth OR 1.82 (1.26-2.63; p=.035); regular snacking OR 1.58 (1.18-2.10; p=.041).

METHODS: PECO: children <=7 y primary dentition; cross-sectional observational studies (English/German). Exclusions: permanent dentition, insufficient data, case reports, reviews, prevalence-only studies, no anamnestic risk factors. Two independent reviewers. Random-effects throughout (high heterogeneity); fixed-effects sensitivity analysis; Bonferroni-Holm adjustment; funnel plot + Egger. Quality: modified Newcastle-Ottawa for cross-sectional (max 10 stars). GRADE for evidence.

RESULTS: 1934 records -> 64 full-text -> 42 excluded; +4 manual. 11 Asia, 7 Europe, 4 South America, 1 Oceania. Indices: O'Brien and modified 34.78%, TWI/modified 30.43%, BEWE 5 studies, O'Sullivan, SEPRS. Quality: most good; 11 sources high, 1 satisfactory. Prevalence meta-regression: continent (Oceania vs Europe est -4.536, p=.043; single study with 0% prevalence) and number of participants (est -0.002, p<.001; larger studies lower prevalence) significant; index, publication year, partial vs full-mouth not significant. No relevant publication bias for prevalence. 52 anamnestic risk factors documented; 9 meta-analyses, 29 risk factors as subgroups; gender (95.65% of studies) and age (69.57%) most often investigated; leisure factors not comparable.

Publication bias: only general-health domain had serious risk. Sensitivity (fixed vs random) confirmed random-effects results.
GRADE: prevalence and most domains very low certainty (inconsistency, imprecision, observational design); oral hygiene domain low; leisure domain no data.

DISCUSSION/LIMITATIONS: Prevalence 35.6% slightly lower than a recent MA of children <7 y (39.6%). Only dichotomous factors could be pooled; age, toothbrushing frequency not standardized, raw data not provided; socioeconomic and oral-hygiene domains underrepresented; few studies per subgroup (many n=1-2); high heterogeneity; acidic drinks estimate from a single study. Fruit protective association likely reflects inability to distinguish acidic vs non-acidic fruits; timing of acid intake may matter. Straw effect explained by avoiding direct tooth contact. Authors call for standardized questionnaires and raw data sharing. Funding: none; no COI.
