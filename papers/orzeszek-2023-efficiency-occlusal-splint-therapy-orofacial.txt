Source: PubMed / PMC10053140 (condensed working copy). Orzeszek S, Waliszewska-Prosol M, Ettlin D, Seweryn P, Straburzynski M, Martelletti P, Jenca A, Wieckiewicz M. Efficiency of occlusal splint therapy on orofacial muscle pain reduction: a systematic review. BMC Oral Health 2023;23(1):180. DOI 10.1186/s12903-023-02897-0. PMID 36978070.

ABSTRACT: Aim: examine RCTs on effectiveness of occlusal splints (OSs) in orofacial myalgia and myofascial pain (MP) vs no treatment or other interventions. PRISMA 2020. PubMed, CINAHL, Scopus; English; Jan 1 2010 - Jun 1 2022; last search Jun 4 2022. Cochrane RoB tool. 13 studies, 589 patients; all high risk of bias. Conclusion: insufficient evidence whether OS therapy offers advantage over other interventions or no treatment.

METHODS: Adults >=18 with RDC/TMD myofascial pain, DC/TMD myalgia/MP/tendonitis/myositis, or clear clinical orofacial muscle pain. Comparators: no treatment (education, counselling, self-exercise, self-massage), LED, acupuncture, low-level laser, device-supported sensorimotor training, Kinesio Taping, myofunctional therapy, physical therapy. Excluded: non-RCT, non-English, nonpainful TMD, prior TMD treatment, systemic/psychogenic/neurologic causes, recent facial/neck trauma, <20 participants. Two independent reviewers, third for disputes. Primary outcome: masticatory muscle pain reduction after OS therapy. Secondary: which co-therapy has the highest impact. Narrative synthesis; no meta-analysis (heterogeneous pain reporting/statistics).

RESULTS: 474 records -> 107 after duplicates -> 37 removed at screening -> 13 included. 589 participants: 276 OS, 108 conservative/counselling, 30 acupuncture, 30 laser, 23 sensorimotor training, 16 Kinesio Taping, 10 myofunctional, 37 manual therapy, 59 LED. One study used DC/TMD, rest RDC/TMD-type. Follow-up 4 weeks to 6 months. Splints: stabilization in 207 pts, reflex in 59, repositioning in 10. VAS for pain in all but one (CPI scale).
Risk of bias: all 13 high overall; allocation concealment unclear in all 13; randomization unclear in 8, adequate in 5; blinding of participants high in all; detection bias low in 6, high in 2, unclear in 5; attrition high in 1, unclear in 5; selective reporting in 1; other bias high in 3.
Individual studies:
- Grillo: acupuncture vs flat plane OS, 40 women, 4 weeks; VAS equally reduced in both (p<0.001); mouth opening increased.
- Barrero: acupuncture vs decompression splint, 20 pts, 5-week tx, assessed 30 days after; both reduced pain short-term; splint group not significant (p>0.05); acupuncture significant for all but masseter point (p=0.068).
- Oz: low-level laser (820 nm, 3 J/cm2, 300 mW, 10 sessions) vs OS 24 h/d for 3 months, 40 pts; both improved (p<0.01), no between-group difference.
- Manfredini: laser vs oral appliance vs counselling only; VAS at 3 wk, 3 and 6 months; at 3 wk VAS decreased only in laser group (p=0.018); at 6 months all groups improved (laser p=0.001, OA p=0.002, counselling p=0.048); all forms similar long-term.
- Giannakopoulos: sensorimotor training vs splint, 45 pts, 3 months; pain reduction 53% vs 40% (p<0.001 each), no between-group difference (p>0.05); training harder to use.
- Costa: LED +/- splint, six groups, 30 days; combined splint + LED differed from splint control for pain (p<0.05); two sessions/week lowered blood lactate.
- Felicio: orofacial myofunctional therapy vs OS vs untreated TMD control vs asymptomatic, 10 per group, 120 days; better results in myofunctional group than OS; headache frequency differed (p<0.05).
- Keskinruzgar: Kinesio Taping (n=16) vs splint (n=18) in sleep bruxism, 5 weeks; both reduced pain and improved opening; no difference.
- Grootel: physical therapy vs splint, 72 pts, 1-year follow-up; long-term success rate 51-60% both; Cohen's d 0.86 (physio) and 1.39 (splint); physio 10.4 weeks shorter, splint needed 7.1 fewer visits (p<0.001).
- Michelotti: education vs OS, 44 pts, 3 months; spontaneous muscle pain reduction better in education group (p=0.034; effect size 0.33); no difference in mouth opening (p=0.528), headache, chewing pain.
- Erbasar: NTI-tss + counselling vs counselling, 40 pts; no between-group difference in pain (p=0.922) or function (p=0.927).
- Conti: stabilization splint + counselling vs NTI + counselling vs counselling only, 51 pts; behavioural change effective; devices, esp. stabilization splint, gave earlier improvement; NTI no added benefit; short follow-up, small group.
- Ficnar: conservative therapy vs + laboratory stabilization splint vs + SOLUBrux prefabricated splint, 63 pts, 2.5 months; all reduced tender areas; mouth-opening gains with SS (p=0.041) and SB (p=0.004 at 2 weeks; p=0.021 final); faster in prefabricated SB group.

DISCUSSION / KEY FINDINGS: (1) OS similar to acupuncture, short-term only; (2) laser as effective as OS; (3) OS or laser may add short-term benefit, long-term similar to counselling/education; (4) sensorimotor training a cost-effective alternative; (5) LED + OS superior to individual treatments; (6) physical therapy similar to splint but faster; (7) counselling for behavioural change and self-care similar to splint, can be first choice. Earlier reviews mixed myalgia with TMJ disorders; this is the first restricted to painful muscle disorders.
LIMITATIONS: all RCTs English only and high risk of bias (no blinding); pain reported at different times/ways, different statistics preventing meta-analysis; baseline pain intensity not characterized; no long-term studies; no large groups with equal gender distribution.
CONCLUSION: Data insufficient for definitive conclusions on effectiveness of OSs for orofacial myalgia and MP vs no treatment or other intervention; long-term high-quality blinded large trials needed.
NOTE (internal inconsistency in source): Results text states inclusion window "January 1, 2010, and June 1, 2021" whereas Methods/Abstract state June 1, 2022.
