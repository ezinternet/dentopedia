The association between common NSAID use and early dental implant failure: A large-scale retrospective cohort study.
Chatzopoulos GS, Wolff LF. J Stomatol Oral Maxillofac Surg. 2025 Dec;127(3):102680.

The impact of NSAIDs on osseointegration remains controversial, with conflicting evidence from animal models and human clinical trials. Aim: investigate the association between common NSAIDs and risk of early dental implant failure in a large real-world cohort. Retrospective cohort using an EHR database of 12,943 patients receiving 49,997 implants. NSAID group (n=3133) vs non-NSAID control (n=9810). Primary outcome: early implant failure = implant removal within six months of placement. Multivariable logistic regression ORs, adjusting for age, gender, diabetes, osteoporosis. Analyses at implant and patient levels. NSAID group significantly older (62.8 vs 58.7 y, p<0.0001), higher diabetes (22.0% vs 16.7%, p<0.0001) and osteoporosis (11.0% vs 7.0%, p<0.0001). After adjustment, implant-level: Ibuprofen OR 2.29 (95% CI 1.48-3.55; p<0.0001), Naproxen OR 2.65 (95% CI 1.22-5.75; p=0.0137). Patient-level: Ibuprofen OR 2.87 (95% CI 1.83-4.51; p<0.0001), Naproxen p=0.0689 (NS). Conclusion: Ibuprofen and to a lesser extent Naproxen associated with significantly increased risk of early dental implant failure, independent of other risk factors. Clinicians should consider these findings when formulating post-operative analgesic plans.
