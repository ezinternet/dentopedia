Source: PubMed / PMC11467017 (PMID 39387914), DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s00784-024-05983-x
Katz MS, Ooms M, Heitzer M, Steiner T, Bock A, Peters F, Hölzle F, Modabber A. Platelet-rich fibrin as a hemostatic agent in dental extractions in patients taking anticoagulants or antiplatelet medication: a systematic review. Clin Oral Investig 2024;28(11):587. (condensed working copy)

ABSTRACT: Aim: evaluate whether PRF is effective in preventing postoperative bleeding after dental extractions in patients on anticoagulation or antiplatelet therapy vs stitches alone and different hemostatic agents. PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD42024562289. PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, SCOPUS. 789 studies identified, 11 met inclusion after full-text screening. Four studies: antiplatelet; seven: anticoagulants. All showed sufficient hemostasis with PRF; meta-analysis not possible (heterogeneity). Conclusion: PRF seems superior to stitches only and inferior to chitosan dressings for time of hemostasis; beneficial for faster wound healing and less postoperative pain.

METHODS: PICOS - P: patients on anticoagulant/antiplatelet undergoing extraction; I: PRF as hemostatic agent; C: other hemostatic agents or control sites; O: hemostasis, bleeding, postoperative pain; S: human clinical studies. Search up to 1 June 2024, PubMed/EMBASE/Cochrane/SCOPUS; German or English only; autologous PRF only (PRP/PRGF excluded); patients not on anticoagulants/AP excluded. Two independent screeners (MSK, AM), judge MO; Cohen kappa 0.822. RoB 2 for individual studies. No comprehensive statistical analysis due to heterogeneity.

RESULTS: 1782 records, 789 after duplicates removed; 13 to full text; 2 excluded (PRF hemostasis but patients not on anticoagulant/AP); 11 included. Design: 3 uncontrolled clinical studies (Sammartino 2011, de Almeida Barros Mourao 2018, Berton 2022), 1 non-randomized controlled (Harfoush 2016), 7 RCTs (Eldibany 2014, Sarkar 2019, Giudice 2019, Munawar 2020, Brancaccio 2021, Rajendra 2021, Kyyak 2023); two split-mouth (text); only one blinded. 20-300 patients per study, total 864 patients, at least 1148 teeth. Comparators: dry gauze, chitosan, hemostatic plugs, gelatin sponges, tranexamic acid, stitches-only control sites.
Table 1 rows: Sammartino 2011 single-arm n=50, 168 teeth, L-PRF no control | Eldibany 2014 RCT n=20, 20 teeth, PRF vs chitosan dressing | Harfoush 2016 controlled n=50, 50 teeth, PRF vs dry gauze | Mourao 2018 cohort n=25, 44 teeth, PRF no control (mean age 72.44) | Sarkar 2019 RCT n=60, 60 teeth, PRF vs chitosan | Giudice 2019 RCT split-mouth n=40, 160 teeth (4/pt): stitches control, hemostatic cellulose plug, L-PRF, A-PRF+ | Munawar 2020 RCT n=84, 84 teeth, PRF vs tranexamic acid | Brancaccio 2021 RCT split-mouth n=102, >=4 teeth/pt: stitches, cellulose plug, L-PRF, A-PRF+ | Rajendra 2021 RCT n=300, 300 teeth, PRF vs chitosan | Berton 2022 cohort n=112, 112 teeth (easy extraction <15 min), L-PRF no control (mean age 77.1) | Kyyak 2023 double-blind RCT split-mouth n=21, 42 teeth, A-PRF vs gelatin sponge (DOAC).
Medication: 4 studies antiplatelet, 4 VKA, 3 DOAC, one did not give anticoagulant information (as stated). PRF protocols: L-PRF (normal or prolonged centrifugation), A-PRF/A-PRF+, six studies unclassified.
All studies reported only mild-moderate bleeding, mostly solved by local compression. PRF superior to dry gauze (Harfoush), cellulose (Brancaccio/Giudice), stitches only (Giudice, Brancaccio). No significant difference PRF vs chitosan dressing (Eldibany), tranexamic acid (Munawar), gelatin (Kyyak) for postoperative bleeding episodes. Sarkar and Rajendra: hemostasis faster with chitosan than PRF (bleeding stabilized in 2-3 min in both). Risk of bias (RoB 2): 5 low, 6 moderate; concerns mostly lack of randomization and extent of outcome data.

DISCUSSION: Two former reviews: Filho 2021 (3 studies), Campana (6 studies, PRF and PRP mixed). Some studies prolonged centrifugation time for VKA or AP patients. Eldibany: chitosan patients had more alveolitis, delayed healing, greater pain; Sarkar: better healing and less pain with PRF than chitosan - PRF slightly inferior in time to bleeding control but superior in comfort and cost. Giudice, Munawar, Harfoush, Brancaccio: significantly less bleeding with A-PRF+ than stitches-only or dry gauze, or (as written in discussion) a tranexamic acid control group [inconsistent with Results stating no significant PRF vs TXA difference]. Risk of postoperative bleeding is low overall in AP monotherapy. VKA: Sammartino - two bleeding episodes in patients with INR 3.7 (mean INR 3.16), solved by compression; Eldibany - no delayed bleeding, mean INR 2.28; Harfoush - more bleeding >20 min without PRF, mean INR 2.4. INR is the most important parameter (Febbo: INR >=3 higher hemorrhage risk). DOAC: de Almeida - no bleeding; Kyyak and Berton mild oozing managed by compression; only Kyyak had a control (gelatin), no significant difference; effect in DOAC patients difficult to anticipate.
LIMITATIONS: no study analysed number/type of extractions, osteotomies, anterior/posterior teeth; heterogeneous PRF protocols; heterogeneity prevented statistical analysis; randomized studies with control group lacking.
CONCLUSION: PRF clot can support hemostasis after extraction in patients on AP or anticoagulants; superior to stitches only, inferior to chitosan dressings for time of hemostasis; RCTs comparing PRF with control group lacking; more research needed relating to extraction extent.
