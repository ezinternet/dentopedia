Title: Impact of Guided Endodontics on the Success of Endodontic Treatment: An Umbrella Review of Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses
Authors: Aakansha Puri, Dax Abraham, Alpa Gupta
Journal: Cureus. 2024 Sep 7;16(9):e68853.
PMID: 39381454
PMCID: PMC11459592
DOI: 10.7759/cureus.68853
Source: PubMed Central (full text), retrieved via PubMed MCP get_full_text_article.

ABSTRACT
Endodontic treatment, essential for preserving teeth affected by pulp or periapical diseases, often encounters challenges such as complex root canal anatomies and calcifications that can hinder success. Traditional techniques, although effective, are limited in addressing these complexities. Guided endodontics, which utilizes advanced imaging and navigation technologies, promises enhanced accuracy and precision, potentially improving treatment outcomes. Thus, this umbrella review aims to assess whether guided endodontics influences the outcome of endodontic treatment by synthesizing evidence from multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Comprehensive searches were conducted in PubMed, Scopus, EBSCOhost, and Cochrane databases to identify relevant studies. Additionally, grey literature was searched. The umbrella review protocol was developed and registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) (CRD42024564150). Inclusion criteria comprised randomized controlled trials (RCTs), randomized clinical trials, case series, and case reports. Exclusion criteria included reviews, non-English language articles, in vitro studies, ex vivo studies, randomized experimental (in vitro) studies, narrative reviews, and expert opinions. Studies involving patients undergoing guided endodontic treatment with dynamic and static navigation systems were included. The interventions aimed at improving the accuracy and precision of endodontic procedures, particularly in challenging cases such as locating calcified canals and performing microsurgery. The study involved three reviewers who independently appraised the systematic reviews for eligibility, data extraction, and review quality. The quality of the systematic reviews was evaluated using the "A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews 2" (AMSTAR2) assessment tool. The methodological quality of the systematic reviews was assessed. A pooled review of the studies showed that guided endodontics demonstrated more precise and better results than conventional endodontic procedures.

FULL TEXT

Introduction and background

Endodontic therapy is an essential treatment modality to preserve natural teeth affected by pulp pathology. Despite its success, challenges still prevail, such as pulp calcifications, periapical lesions, iatrogenic perforations, fractures, excessive tissue removal, inability to remove instrument fragments, and difficulty navigating heavily calcified canals. Although magnifying glasses, microscopes, and cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) can provide guidance, it can be challenging for operators to perform the therapy manually without errors.

Guided endodontics (static navigation (SN) and dynamic navigation (DN)) uses computer-aided navigation systems to aid in accurately locating and treating root canals. These technologies produce a visual guide that helps with precise endodontic procedures by combining real-time navigation with preoperative imaging, such as CBCT, thereby increasing the accuracy and predictability of endodontic procedures. Given the conflicting results of existing systematic reviews and meta-analyses on the effect of guided endodontics on endodontic procedures, the aim of this umbrella review is to evaluate the potential impact of guided endodontics on the outcome of endodontic therapy.

Review

Methods

The protocol of this umbrella review was registered in PROSPERO (CRD42024564150) and developed according to PRISMA guidelines.

Search Strategy
A comprehensive literature search was conducted in four databases - PubMed, Scopus, EBSCOhost, and Cochrane - to identify systematic reviews and meta-analyses published from 2016 up to July 2024. A manual search and a review of grey literature were also conducted. Search strategy: (((guided endodontics) OR (static navigation)) OR (dynamic navigation)) AND (systematic review). Two independent reviewers (Alpa Gupta and Aakansha Puri) screened titles/abstracts, then reviewed full text. Disagreements resolved by a third reviewer (Dax Abraham).

Eligibility Criteria
Included: systematic reviews and meta-analyses focused on guided endodontics; comprising RCTs, randomized clinical trials, case series, and case reports written in English.
Excluded: reviews, non-English articles, in vitro studies, ex vivo studies, randomized experimental (in vitro) studies, animal studies, narrative reviews, expert opinions, short communications, editorials.

Data Extraction
Extraction sheet included: first author, year, title, journal, number of databases searched, country of first author, search period, language, whether meta-analysis performed, number of included studies, study design, endodontic application, tooth involved, quality, outcome evaluation method, instrument of quality assessment. Success of endodontic treatment evaluated via: follow-up for tooth structure loss using CBCT analysis; angle deviation of bur tip in conventional vs guided endodontics (by CBCT); clinical/radiological evaluation of periapical lesion and its relationship to pain scale.

Methodological Quality Assessment
AMSTAR 2 (16-item checklist) used to assess quality; each item scored Yes/No/Partial Yes; reviews classified as high, moderate, low, or severely low quality based on overall score.

Results

Study Selection
112 studies identified initially. 87 duplicates + 2 ineligible (automation) excluded. 2 removed for other reasons. 11 assessed for eligibility; 5 excluded (2 pilot studies, 3 in vitro studies). Final: 6 systematic reviews included, of which 2 conducted meta-analyses.

Characteristics of Included Reviews (Table 1/2 summary)
1. Pena-Bengoa et al. (2023) - "Effectiveness of Guided Endodontics in Locating Calcified Root Canals: A Systematic Review" - Clinical Oral Investigations - PubMed/Scopus/EBSCOhost - Chile - search up to July 2022 - No meta-analysis - 45 studies included (21 clinical case reports, 11 case series, 12 ex vivo studies, 1 cohort study). AMSTAR 2 = 10.5/16 (moderate quality). Reported high success rate locating calcified canals; only 2 articles showed failures. Average drill tip deviation 0.46 mm, not operator-dependent but guide-design-dependent, making guided endodontics less suitable for complex anatomy. 9 case reports moderate risk of bias, 12 studies low risk of bias; 11 case series all low risk of bias (1 medium). Lack of adequate follow-up limits long-term success assessment; potential publication bias.

2. Zubizarreta-Macho et al. (January 2021) - "Efficacy of Computer-Aided Static Navigation Technique on the Accuracy of Endodontic Microsurgery: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis" - Journal of Clinical Medicine - Spain - search up to April 2020 - Meta-analysis: Yes, 7 articles. AMSTAR 2 = 14/16 (high quality). Random effects model, inverse variance method. Success rate for apex location using computer-aided SN = 96.8% (CI 93.0-100%), forecast range 91.4-100%. No heterogeneity (Q-test=6.15; p=0.407; I²=2.4%). SN was 27 times more effective than conventional endodontic microsurgery (odds ratio 27.7, 95% CI 11.3-68.1; z=7.23; p<0.0001; Q-test=0.80; p=0.671; I²=0%). Funnel plot asymmetry addressed via trim-and-fill.

3. Zubizarreta-Macho et al. (March 2021) - "Effect of Computer-Aided Navigation Techniques on the Accuracy of Endodontic Access Cavities: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis" - Biology (Basel) - Spain - search up to April 2020 - Meta-analysis: Yes, 13 studies. AMSTAR 2 = 14.5/16 (high quality). Random effects model, inverse variance technique. Overall root canal location success rate = 98.1% (CI 95.7-100%), no significant heterogeneity (Q=17.3; p=0.185; I²=25%). No significant difference between static (98.5%) and dynamic (94.5%) navigation (Q=0.57; p=0.451). In vitro success 96.2% (CI 92.4-100%) vs in vivo 100% (CI 97.3-100%). Sample sizes 2-60; 8 experimental trials, 4 case series, 1 clinical trial. CRIS scale (in vitro) and Jadad scale (clinical) used; the one clinical trial scored 0 on Jadad (poor quality), showing limitations in randomization/blinding. Concluded computer-aided navigation significantly enhances accuracy of endodontic access cavities; SN may be impractical with limited mouth opening/posterior teeth, resolved by DN's real-time adjustment.

4. Moreno-Rabie et al. (2020) - "Clinical Applications, Accuracy and Limitations of Guided Endodontics: A Systematic Review" - International Endodontic Journal - Leuven - search up to April 25, 2019 - No meta-analysis - 22 studies (15 case reports [11 guided access cavity, 4 guided endodontic surgery], 6 pre-clinical studies [in vitro/ex vivo], 1 observational study of 50 patients). AMSTAR 2 = 11.5/16 (moderate quality). Access cavities primarily anterior single-rooted teeth, mostly calcified canals; some dens invaginatus/evaginatus; remaining cases in maxillary/mandibular molars. Buchgreitz et al. (2019) observational study (50 patients): "acceptable" precision defined as some deviation but canal still locatable/instrumentable and apical lesion healed on follow-up. Funding sources not specified; bias addressed but not for all investigations.

5. Vasudevan et al. (2022) - "Dynamic Navigation in Guided Endodontics: A Systematic Review" - European Endodontic Journal - India - search up to July 2021 - No meta-analysis (heterogeneity too high: sample size, tooth type, outcome measures differed) - 14 studies (3 case reports, 11 in vitro studies). AMSTAR 2 = 12.5/16 (high quality). Case reports: low risk of bias, mean score 83.34%; 2 studies moderate risk of bias (mean 84.09%); 9 in vitro studies low risk of bias. DN systems used for access cavity preparation, pulp canal obliteration, endodontic retreatment, microsurgery. Accuracy: mean 3D lateral deviation 0.67 mm coronally, 0.9 mm apically; angular deviation 2.50°. Mean 2D horizontal deviation for endodontic access 0.9 mm; angular deviation 1.7°. DN reduced iatrogenic errors (misaligned access, gouging, unsuccessful canal location, perforation, incomplete root-end resection). Treatment times: 11.5 seconds (ultraconservative access) to under 45 minutes (endodontic microsurgery); chairside time significantly reduced vs freehand.

6. Poirazi et al. (2023) - "Post Removal Using Guided Endodontics: A Systematic Review of the Literature" - European Journal of Dental and Oral Health - Greece - Open Grey literature - search up to July 2023 - No meta-analysis - 9 studies (7 case reports, 2 in vitro). AMSTAR 2 = 10/16 (moderate quality). Heterogeneity and low evidence acknowledged; follow-up times not described; prognosis/patient perspective/intervention adherence/consent not met by included case studies. Success rate for fiber post removal without severe complications (fracture, root perforation) = 100% in reviewed case reports.

AMSTAR 2 Item-Level Scoring (Table 4) - all 6 studies scored across 16 AMSTAR 2 items; Items 11/12/15 (meta-analysis-specific) marked N/A for the 4 reviews without meta-analysis. Weak points across reviews: Item 1 (PICO components, partial for Pena-Bengoa), Item 2 (protocol pre-registration, partial for several), Item 7 (list of excluded studies + justification, partial for most), Item 10 (funding sources of included studies, "No" for Pena-Bengoa, Moreno-Rabie, Poirazi).

Discussion

Guided endodontics uses advanced navigation technology to improve precision; potential to enhance root canal therapy success (locating calcified canals, tooth structure preservation, overall procedure success), but efficacy data vary across studies with different methodologies/outcomes.

Outcomes assessed across the six reviews: Pena-Bengoa (success rate locating calcified canals); Zubizarreta-Macho Jan 2021 (SN accuracy in endodontic microsurgery); Zubizarreta-Macho Mar 2021 (computer-aided navigation accuracy in access cavities); Moreno-Rabie (clinical success rates/accuracy/limitations); Vasudevan (DN system effectiveness); Poirazi (efficacy/success of guided post-removal).

Pena-Bengoa (2023) details: 45 studies (mainly observational/ex vivo + 21 case reports + 11 case series). Joanna Briggs Institute critical appraisal tool used for case reports. 21/32 case report+series studies treated anterior teeth (2 dens invaginatus, 1 dentin dysplasia); remaining 6 treated maxillary/mandibular posterior teeth. Loureiro et al. (2020): guided endodontics significantly preserved dental tissue volume in maxillary molars vs conventional methods; small drill diameters more successful preserving tissue; ultrasonic tips with endodontic guides good for small teeth needing critical tissue preservation. Small CBCT voxel size (0.075-0.8 mm) gives clearer canal visualization, ideal for posterior teeth with high root/canal density. Planning time varies by printing type, software, printer; Connert et al. (2017): average planning time 9.4 minutes (range 7-12.8 min). Guided endodontics for calcified canals minimizes accident risk, tissue loss, working time, but evidence quality limited by lack of follow-up/population description.

Zubizarreta-Macho Jan 2021 details: real-time drilling guidance now possible via computer-aided DN with optical triangulation tracking (Mediavilla Guzman et al.). 7 studies; SN apex-location success 96.8% (CI 93.0-100%), forecast 91.4-100%; no heterogeneity (Q=6.15, p=0.407, I²=2.4%); 27x more effective than traditional procedures (Q=0.80, p=0.671, I²=0%); odds success ratio 27.7 (95% CI 11.3-68.1, z=7.23, p<0.0001).

Zubizarreta-Macho Mar 2021 details: SN requires CAD/CAM surgical templates via rapid prototyping; DN requires optical triangulation tracking with stereoscopic motion-tracking cameras for real-time guidance. Sample sizes 2-60; 8 experimental, 4 case series, 1 clinical trial. CRIS scale (in vitro) + Jadad scale (clinical) - randomization/blinding limitations found; the one clinical trial scored 0 on Jadad. Random effects meta-analysis: overall success 98.1% (CI 95.7-100%), no significant heterogeneity (Q=17.3, p=0.185, I²=25%). No significant difference static (98.5%) vs dynamic (94.5%). In vitro 96.2% (CI 92.4-100%) vs in vivo 100% (CI 97.3-100%). SN impractical with limited mouth opening/posterior-teeth restricted access; DN resolves via real-time adjustment/direct visualization.

Moreno-Rabie (2020) details: 22 articles (15 case reports [11 access cavity, 4 surgery], 6 pre-clinical, 1 observational). Access cavities primarily anterior single-rooted teeth, mostly calcified canals, some dens invaginatus/evaginatum; remainder in maxillary/mandibular molars. Buchgreitz et al. (2019) observational study of 50 patients: "acceptable" precision = some deviation but canal locatable/instrumentable, apical lesion healed at follow-up.

Vasudevan (2022) details: 3 case reports + 11 in vitro studies. Low risk of bias (case reports 83.34%, in vitro 84.09%). DN applications: access cavity prep, pulp canal obliteration, endodontic retreatment, microsurgery. No meta-analysis due to heterogeneity. Accuracy: mean 3D lateral deviation 0.67 mm coronal / 0.9 mm apical; angular deviation 2.50°. Mean 2D horizontal deviation for endodontic access 0.9 mm; angular deviation 1.7°. DN reduced iatrogenic errors (misaligned access, gouging, failed canal location, perforation, incomplete root-end resection). Treatment times: 11.5 sec (ultraconservative access) to <45 min (microsurgery); chairside time reduced vs freehand via multiple motion-tracking cameras on handpiece + patient.

Poirazi (2023) details: guided endodontics for fiber post removal; success rate 100% in reviewed case reports, no severe complications (fracture, root perforation).

Quality of Evidence and Methodological Considerations: Pena-Bengoa (moderate) - concerns: lack of clarity in conflicts of interest, insufficient study-selection detail. Moreno-Rabie (11.5/16, moderate) - limitations in risk-of-bias assessment, transparency in selection criteria. Zubizarreta-Macho (both, 14/16 and 14.5/16, high) - strong methodological rigor, comprehensive risk-of-bias assessment, thorough reporting.

Limitations and Future Directions: Variability in study quality (AMSTAR 2) suggests conclusions from some reviews should be interpreted cautiously. Reliance on advanced imaging/high-tech equipment may limit accessibility/generalizability in routine practice. Need for high-quality RCTs and long-term clinical studies to validate findings and standardize guided-endodontics protocols; cost-effectiveness analyses and user-friendly systems needed for wider adoption. Average drill tip deviation 0.46 mm may cause unwanted healthy-tissue wear. Zenhder et al. (2016) and Connert et al. (2017) also reported deviations (reproducibility issue). Nayak et al. (2018): guide design can cause deviations, making the technique less suitable for complex anatomies like C-shaped canals. Practical challenges: high costs, steep learning curve, limited accessibility.

Conclusions
Guided endodontics facilitates accuracy and predictability of endodontic procedures. This umbrella review demonstrates guided endodontics has notable advantages over conventional techniques: improved precision, reduced procedural errors, better clinical outcomes in locating calcified root canals and endodontic microsurgery. Limitations: varying quality of evidence among studies (especially case reports/series), drill tip deviation. Guided endodontics shows promise for complex endodontic cases, but further high-quality research needed to establish long-term effectiveness and routine clinical practice role.
