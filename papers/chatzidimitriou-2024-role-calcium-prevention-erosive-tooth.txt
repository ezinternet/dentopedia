The role of calcium in the prevention of erosive tooth wear: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

ABSTRACT (abstract only; no PMCID):
The loss of hard dental tissue due to recurrent acid challenges and mechanical stresses without bacterial involvement is known as erosive tooth wear (ETW). Many studies have concentrated on variables that may affect the ETW process and prevent its occurrence or reduce its advancement. However, to date, no previous systematic review has evaluated the role of calcium in preventing ETW. The purpose of the present systematic review was to review and critically appraise the scientific evidence regarding the role of calcium formulations in the prevention of ETW.
The review protocol was registered in PROSPERO (CRD42021229819). A literature search was conducted in electronic databases to identify in situ randomized controlled trials evaluating the prevention of ETW following the application of calcium formulations. Outcomes: mean enamel loss, surface microhardness, surface roughness, mean erosion/softening depth, mineral loss/precipitation and remineralization. Cochrane RoB 2.0 was used; meta-analysis used a random effects model.
The search retrieved 869 studies of which 21 were eligible. Risk of bias: 5 low, 12 unclear, 4 high. Addition of calcium in juice drinks led to reduced enamel loss, with blackcurrant juice presenting 2.6 times statistically significant less enamel loss compared to orange juice (p = 0.0001, I2 = 89%). No statistically significant difference in mean surface microhardness of eroded enamel between chewing gum with or without CPP-ACP (p = 0.31, I2 = 71%). Contradictory results regarding the effect of milk and CPP-ACP pastes on prevention of ETW.
Conclusion: Calcium formulations play an important role in ETW prevention, mainly through their addition to acidic drinks.
