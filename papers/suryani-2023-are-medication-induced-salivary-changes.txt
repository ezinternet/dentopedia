SOURCE: PubMed / PMC (PMID 37720502, PMCID PMC10501800), DOI 10.3389/fmed.2023.1164051
Suryani IR, Ahmadzai I, That MT, Shujaat S, Jacobs R. Are medication-induced salivary changes the culprit of osteonecrosis of the jaw? A systematic review. Front Med (Lausanne) 2023;10:1164051.

ABSTRACT
Purpose: assess the potential influence of medication-induced salivary changes on the development of MRONJ. Methods: PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane, Embase up to June 2023; modified Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS); meta-analysis not performed owing to heterogeneity of medications and outcomes. Results: 765 studies, 10 eligible. 272 MRONJ cases (35% women, 32% men, 32% no gender reported), mean age 66 years. Patients administered bisphosphonates, steroids, chemotherapy, thalidomide, interferon, and hormone therapy had a significantly higher association between decreased salivary flow and MRONJ occurrence. Bisphosphonates, denosumab, and other bone-modifying agents showed a significantly higher risk of developing MRONJ owing to changes in salivary microbiome profiles, cytokine profiles, interleukins, hypotaurine, and binding proteins. Conclusion: reduction in salivary flow and changes in salivary protein concentration were associated with MRONJ; limited evidence, interpret with caution. PROSPERO CRD42022327645.

METHODS (summary of full text)
Registered CRD42022327645; PRISMA (23 items). PICO: P cancer and osteoporosis; I medications that induce salivary changes; C no comparators; O MRONJ. Databases PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane, Embase, January 2013 to June 2023; no language restriction; grey literature ProQuest, OpenGrey, Google Scholar; manual cross-reference search. Exclusion: animal studies, case reports, systematic reviews, conference abstracts, letters, editorials, surveys; patients with radiotherapy history, metastasis to the jaws, or head and neck cancer surgery affecting salivary flow. Two independent reviewers (IA, MT), third expert (IS) for disagreement, Cohen's kappa for agreement. Data extracted: title, author, year, design, n, gender, age, underlying disease, agents, type of salivary change. RoB: modified NOS (selection max 4, comparability max 2, outcome max 3 stars; total 9).

RESULTS
Search yielded 765 articles; 10 studies eligible for qualitative synthesis; quantitative synthesis not possible owing to heterogeneity in assessment methodologies, agents, outcomes. 272 MRONJ cases (35% women, 32% men, 32% no gender), mean age 66 years (range 33-81). Predominant primary disease breast cancer (n=57), comorbidity hypertension (n=107). Most common salivary change xerostomia due to Sjogren's syndrome (SS n=35); main pharmacological contributors bisphosphonates (n=530), chemotherapy (n=11), corticosteroids (n=11).

Risk of bias: all studies rated good apart from one poor-quality rating (as printed); overall good (78%) and fair (22%) quality; outcome assessment had significant shortcomings.

DISCUSSION (key points)
Majority of studies assessed resting or stimulated whole saliva; others metabolic analysis and genotyping. Lack of standardization in collection, biomarkers, methods precluded direct comparison. Higher percentage of MRONJ patients were receiving chemotherapy at saliva collection vs non-MRONJ, which may partially explain differences. Decreased saliva production considered a risk factor for MRONJ, xerostomia mainly triggered by SS following bone-modifying drugs. In SS the prevalence of dental caries and early tooth loss is twice as high and infection risk 10 times more than general population. Polypharmacy significant predictor of xerostomia; highest prevalence of xerostomia and MRONJ in patients taking five or more drugs (71%); xerostomia and MRONJ nearly three times higher compared with patients taking no medication (case-control studies). Diabetes associated with decreased saliva production and possibly higher MRONJ risk. Drug holiday: Kim et al. proposed temporary cessation during extraction may reduce risk; another study found no evidence; ADA Council suggested lowering cumulative doses; no detailed guidelines. Bagan et al.: high salivary IL-6 after bisphosphonate therapy had 1.01 odds ratio of MRONJ, increasing with severity. Badros et al.: cytokine response (macrophage activation profile) main culprit. TNF-a and IL-1b promote inflammation and bone resorption. MMP8 and MMP9 overexpressed in MRONJ vs healthy. Hypotaurine elevated; may allow early detection (Yatsuoka). RBMS3 significantly associated with MRONJ. Further longitudinal, large-sample studies needed for sensitivity/specificity of biomarkers. Limitations: few studies, mostly small samples; heterogeneity in design, drugs, primary disease, outcome methods.

CONCLUSION
Reduction in salivary flow and changes in concentration of salivary proteins were associated with development of MRONJ; limited evidence, interpret with caution. Recommended: assess salivary specimens before and after development of MRONJ to validate biomarkers for early detection.
