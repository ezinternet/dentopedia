=== ABSTRACT (structured; as indexed in PubMed) ===

BACKGROUND

Insertion of dental implants causes bone deformation and induces residual bone compression
stress, which can lead to implant failure if the bone loss threshold is exceeded.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Five models of dental implants with different surface treatments (machined and acid etching),
thread profiles (triangular and trapezoidal), and body shapes (cylindrical and conical) were
inserted in synthetic bone blocks (polyurethane, D2-equivalent density). Insertion torque was
quantified using a digital torque driver, and bone deformation during insertion was measured using
strain gauge extensometry.

RESULTS

Bone deformation and insertion torque increased as insertion turns increased. Trapezoidal-thread
implants required higher insertion torque than triangular-thread implants. Conical-shaped implants
required higher torque than cylindrical-shaped implants. Machined-surface implant insertion
exceeded the synthetic bone's mechanical resistance, causing cracks. Conical implants performed
better than cylindrical implants. Trapezoidal-thread, conical-body implants induced torque and
compression that did not harm osseointegration. Machined implants had the worst performance,
producing cracks in the synthetic bone during insertion.

CONCLUSION

Excessive deformations in peri-implant bone led to bone necrosis and implant failure. Surgeons must
weigh surface treatment, thread profile, and body shape in relation to their effects on
osseointegration.
