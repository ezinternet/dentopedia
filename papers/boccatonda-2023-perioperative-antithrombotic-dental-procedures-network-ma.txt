Source: PubMed / PMC10093975, doi:10.3390/ijerph20075293 (condensed working copy)
Boccatonda A, Frisone A, Lorusso F, et al. Perioperative Management of Antithrombotic Therapy in Patients Who Undergo Dental Procedures: A Systematic Review of the Literature and Network Meta-Analysis. Int J Environ Res Public Health 2023;20(7). Pub 2023-03-28.

ABSTRACT: Dental surgery in patients on anticoagulants carries perioperative bleeding risk. Search: PubMed/Medline, EMBASE, Cochrane; RCTs only; no limits on period/follow-up. Results: 8 RCTs in qualitative synthesis. No thromboembolic complications in any study. Bleeding episodes mild, generally day 1. Conclusion: local haemostatic measures generally effective for bleeding control with no further pharmacological drug management or suspension.

METHODS: PROSPERO 399590; SRQR + PRISMA; search updated to 29 Oct 2022. Inclusion: RCTs, prospective and retrospective studies (title/abstract level); exclusion: SRs, editorials, non-English, case reports/series, in vitro. Two reviewers (F.L., A.S.). Data extracted: design, anticoagulant protocol, haemostatic agents, dental treatment, INR, complications, bleeding time-point, haemostasis time, thromboembolic events. RoB: OHAT tool in RevMan 5.5; 7 criteria; low risk = at least 5/7 positive. Network MA per Owen et al. method: (1) haemostatic agents: gauze compression, gauze/tranexamic acid, fibrin sponge; (2) anticoagulant management: full coumarins (full-CU), reduced coumarins (low-CU), 2-day suspension (2-DSu), LMWH (Hep).

RESULTS: 2051 records, 6 duplicates removed, 1963 excluded on title/abstract, 82 full-text; 74 excluded (36 off topic, 23 non-English, 3 case reports/series, 12 reviews); 8 included. All 8 RCT designs; one double-blind, one split-mouth. All evaluated coumarin VKA full therapy (DOACs excluded); one also reduced coumarins; two a 2-day suspension; one LMWH. Bleeding mostly immediate (<24 h); delayed bleeding in four studies. No significant complications/thromboembolic events. RoB: performance and detection bias with >75% unclear; Lourenco-Queiroz the only RCT scoring 7/7. Low-risk studies went into the network MA.
NMA anticoagulant dosage: 2-day coumarin suspension vs continuous full therapy: lower bleeding risk, p<0.05, OR 0.30 (95% CI 0.09-0.97). Low-dose vs continuous: p>0.05, OR 0.58 (0.21-1.62). LMWH vs continuous: p>0.05, OR 0.79 (0.28-2.21). Local haemostatic agents: gauze + tranexamic acid reported most effective for early bleeding (p<0.05); numeric effect sizes not given in retrieved text.

DISCUSSION (key points): Non-homogeneous protocols; limited power. First NMA on topic. Preop INR heterogeneous but all <3.0. Post-op bleeding mostly <day 1, controlled by local haemostasis/gauze/sutures; few needed tranexamic acid mouthwash. Puia et al.: bismuth subgallate plug, fibrin tissue adhesive or microfibrillar collagen, 11 cases/240 subjects without anticoagulant suspension. Calcium sulphate suggested for alveolar socket bleeding with INR>2.0. Background (cited literature, not new data): no need to stop antiplatelets; ASA discontinuation ~threefold severe CV events (MA of 50,279 subjects; OR 89.78 with coronary stents); DAPT discontinuation 5-10x MI risk with stents. Warfarin: continue if INR <=3.5 for minor procedures; bridging with heparin increases bleeding (Young et al. OR relevant bleeding 3.23, minor 1.52) without thromboembolic difference; Douketis 891 bridged vs 913 placebo. Hiroshi et al. cross-sectional: 496 continued vs 2321 discontinued, more bleeding with continuation. Another MA: 10.8% continued vs 8.3% discontinued. DOACs: fewer data; trough timing 12 or 24 h after last dose; Hanken: rivaroxaban 52 procedures 11.5% vs 0.7% bleeding in 285 controls; 12 pts 57 implants no bleeding when held 24 h; resume 6-8 h after if haemostasis. LMWH: 12 h after last dose; heparin 2-4 h.

CONCLUSIONS: A drug holiday of anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy for dental surgery seems to produce no increase of bleeding risk or severe complications. Withdrawal or heparin bridging related to greater risk of thrombotic events than bleeding (as stated by authors). Most bleeding mild and treatable locally. Stratify by procedure and patient; personalise for high-risk surgery or drug accumulation comorbidity. More DOAC data needed (renal insufficiency). Future anti-factor XIa drugs.
