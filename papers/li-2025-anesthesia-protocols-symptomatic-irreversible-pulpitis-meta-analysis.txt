Title: Evaluation of the effectiveness of local anesthesia approaches for symptomatic irreversible pulpitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Journal: Frontiers in Dental Medicine
Year: 2025
DOI: 10.3389/fdmed.2025.1679706
PMID: 41613590
PMCID: PMC12847389

Abstract

Objective
This meta-analysis assessed the efficacy of various anesthetic protocols for symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, comparing techniques and agents to identify the optimal anesthesia approach.

Methods
We conducted a comprehensive search of the Cochrane Library, PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and Embase databases up to July 10, 2025, identifying relevant studies based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. The primary outcome was the success rate of anesthesia. Data extraction and quality assessment were performed using a pre-designed form and the revised Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool. A fixed-effect model was used for meta-analysis when heterogeneity was low (I² ≤ 50%, P ≥ 0.1); otherwise, a random-effects model was adopted.

Results
Sixteen RCTs were included in the meta-analysis. Pooled analysis showed that modified anesthetic protocols for SIP were significantly more successful than conventional inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) using standard 2% lidocaine with epinephrine (OR = 3.34; 95% CI: 2.49–4.48). Studies conducted in Iran had the highest success rate (OR = 4.31; 95% CI: 3.59–5.17, P < 0.001). Inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) was more effective than buccal infiltration (OR = 4.03; 95% CI: 3.38–4.81, P < 0.001), and 4% articaine demonstrated the highest efficacy (OR = 4.18; 95% CI: 2.85–6.16, P < 0.001).

Conclusion
This meta-analysis assessed the efficacy of various anesthetic protocols for SIP, comparing techniques and agents to identify the optimal anesthesia approach. 4% articaine and IANB should be considered first-line options for managing SIP.

INTRODUCTION

Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP) is an acute, intensely painful inflammatory condition of the dental pulp and constitutes one of the principal emergencies encountered in daily dental practice. The predominant aetiologies—deep carious lesions or traumatic exposure—facilitate bacterial invasion, triggering an irreversible cycle of pulpal ischaemia, necrosis, and subsequent periapical pathosis. The global incidence of pulpitis is about 30%. Achieving effective local anesthesia is a key step in the treatment of SIP.

Two commonly used mandibular anesthesia techniques for the treatment of SIP are IANB and buccal infiltration (BI). Studies indicate that the failure rate of IANB is approximately 12%–65%. Major influencing factors include decreased local pH, activation and sensitization of nociceptive neurons, the action of inflammatory mediators, collateral innervation, and rapid drug tolerance.

In this meta-analysis, "conventional treatment" refers to standard IANB using 2% lidocaine with epinephrine (1:80,000–1:200,000), administered as a single-technique approach without supplementary injections or pharmacological modifications.

METHODS

Study selection: 247 articles were identified; 16 RCTs ultimately included.
Outcome: Primary outcome = success rate of anesthesia (adequate pain control during treatment, stable vital signs, no significant stress response)
Statistical: OR as effect size; random-effects model when I² > 50%; Egger test for publication bias.

INCLUDED STUDIES (16 RCTs)

1. Shapiro 2018 (US): Articaine vs Lidocaine BI; 36 vs 35; first/second molars
2. Shrimanikandan 2021 (India): Articaine dual vs Lidocaine; 30 vs 30; mandibular molar
3. Dianat 2019 (Iran): IANB+BI articaine vs lidocaine; 30 vs 30
4. Gao 2020 (China): 4% articaine vs 2% lidocaine vs 2% mepivacaine; 52 vs 52; mandibular posterior
5. Afkhami 2023 (Iran): Articaine volume comparison (BI); 15 vs 15; maxillary first molar
6. Mohammadreza 2023 (Iran): 4% articaine MBI vs 2% lidocaine IANB; 36 vs 36; mandibular molar
7. Eskandarinezhad 2023 (Iran): 3% prilocaine-felypressin vs 3% mepivacaine plain; 56 vs 56
8. Aggarwal 2024 (US): Intraligamentary vs intrapulpal 2% lidocaine; 47 vs 40
9. Aggarwal 2011 (India): Supplemental buccal articaine ± ketorolac/dexamethasone; 24 vs 26
10. Kreimer 2012 (US): Mannitol + lidocaine vs lidocaine alone; 55 vs 51
11. Aggarwal 2012 (India): 3.6 mL vs 1.8 mL lidocaine; 28 vs 27
12. Sampaio 2012 (Brazil): 0.5% bupivacaine vs 2% lidocaine; 35 vs 35
13. Ashraf 2013 (Iran): 4% articaine vs 2% lidocaine IANB+infiltration; 51 vs 51
14. Abazarpoor 2015 (Iran): Articaine 1.8 mL vs 3.6 mL; 40 vs 40
15. Afkhami 2021 (US): Double IANB vs IANB+BI articaine; 20 vs 20
16. Hassan 2023 (Pakistan): 4% articaine vs 2% lidocaine; 152 vs 152

RESULTS

Primary outcome (all 16 RCTs):
- Modified protocols vs conventional 2% lidocaine IANB: OR = 3.34 (95% CI: 2.49–4.48)
- Fixed effects model: OR = 3.79 (95% CI: 3.23–4.44)
- Heterogeneity: I² = 0% (< 50%), P = 0.52 → fixed-effect model appropriate

SUBGROUP ANALYSES

Geographic subgroups:
- Iran: OR = 4.31 (95% CI: 3.59–5.17, P < 0.001)
- United States: OR = 3.62 (95% CI: 2.91–4.27, P = 0.005)
- India: OR = 3.26 (95% CI: 1.70–6.28, P < 0.001)

Anesthesia technique:
- IANB vs buccal infiltration (BI): OR = 4.03 (95% CI: 3.38–4.81, P < 0.001) — IANB more effective

Anesthetic agent:
- 4% articaine: OR = 4.18 (95% CI: 2.85–6.16, P < 0.001) — GRADE: Moderate (imprecision, n=5 studies, wider CI)
- 2% lidocaine: OR = 3.94 (95% CI: 2.42–6.44, P < 0.001) — GRADE: Moderate (minor inconsistency)
- Both show significant improvement over conventional treatment; articaine slightly higher but overlapping CIs

GRADE ASSESSMENT

| Outcome | Quality Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Overall success (OR = 3.34) | High | 16 RCTs, low/mod ROB, I²=0%, no pub bias |
| Iran subgroup (OR = 4.31) | High | Multiple RCTs, low heterogeneity, narrow CI |
| IANB vs BI (OR = 4.03) | High | Direct comparisons, low ROB, low I² |
| 4% articaine (OR = 4.18) | Moderate | Imprecision (n=5 studies, wider CI) |
| 2% lidocaine (OR = 3.94) | Moderate | Minor inconsistency (Kung 2015 found no difference) |

Publication bias: Non-significant Egger test (P = 0.461 > 0.05) → no significant publication bias.

DISCUSSION KEY POINTS

1. 4% articaine highest success, but 2% lidocaine also significantly outperforms conventional approach (OR 3.94). When 3.6 mL articaine is used or combined with BI, it outperforms 2% lidocaine more consistently.

2. Under acute pulpitis, 4% articaine's 2.8× diffusion advantage is partially counteracted by the "bone barrier collapse—high clearance—low threshold blockage" triple effect, explaining apparent equivalence in some studies.

3. IANB (block near mandibular foramen, anesthetizes entire inferior alveolar nerve) > BI (diffusion through thick mandibular cortical bone, more limited).

4. The "conventional treatment" baseline (OR denominator) = IANB 2% lidocaine = failure rate ~12–65% in SIP.

CONCLUSION

Findings underscore the superior efficacy of 4% articaine and IANB for SIP. Both articaine and IANB should be considered first-line options. However, the 2% lidocaine IANB also significantly outperforms doing nothing/conventional approach. Operator technique, supplemental infiltrations, and drug combinations contribute substantially.
