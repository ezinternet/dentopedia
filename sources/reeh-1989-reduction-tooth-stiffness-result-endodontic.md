## Why Ingested
This is the foundational, most-cited biomechanical study behind the field's working assumption that restorative cavity design — not root canal treatment itself — drives cuspal weakening in endodontically treated teeth (ETT). Every later post-and-core survival/fracture paper in this wiki inherits this study's core distinction (endodontic access ≈ 5% stiffness loss vs. MOD cavity prep ≈ 63% loss); [[wiki/post-and-core/suksaphar-2017-survival-fracture-ett-crown-resin-sr]] builds directly on this premise when comparing full-coverage crowns against direct resin composite for posterior ETT with limited structural loss.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Journal of Endodontics 1989;15(11):512-516

- **Institution**: Not stated in the retrieved abstract (authors affiliated with University of Minnesota school of dentistry per later citing literature; not independently confirmed from this PDF)

## 2. Key Contributions
- First nondestructive occlusal-loading protocol to isolate the strength contribution of each individual endodontic and restorative step (access, instrumentation, obturation, cavity preparation) on the same tooth, using bonded strain gauges rather than destructive fracture testing.
- Established the widely-cited benchmark figures: endodontic procedures alone reduce relative cuspal stiffness by only ~5%, an occlusal cavity preparation by ~20%, and an MOD (mesio-occluso-distal) cavity preparation by ~63%.
- Demonstrated that loss of marginal ridge integrity, not pulp/canal treatment, is the dominant driver of cuspal stiffness loss — reframing "endodontically treated teeth are weak" as "teeth with MOD-type cavity preparations are weak."

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In-vitro nondestructive biomechanical experiment (bench study, not a clinical trial)
- **Databases**: N/A (primary experimental study, not a review)
- **n**: 42 extracted intact maxillary second bicuspids
- **Outcomes**: Relative cuspal stiffness (stress-strain response measured via encapsulated strain gauges bonded on enamel just above the cementoenamel junction, buccal and lingual), tracked sequentially through two procedure orderings: (1) unaltered → access → instrumentation → obturation → MOD prep; or (2) unaltered → occlusal prep → two-surface prep → MOD prep → access → instrumentation → obturation

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Endodontic procedures (access + instrumentation + obturation) alone: ~5% reduction in relative cuspal stiffness
- Occlusal cavity preparation alone: ~20% reduction
- MOD cavity preparation: ~63% average reduction in relative cuspal stiffness — the largest single loss observed
- The largest losses in stiffness tracked with loss of marginal ridge integrity, not with the endodontic steps
- Conclusion stated by the authors: endodontic procedures do not weaken teeth that retain intact marginal ridges

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **Abstract-only source**: this PDF is a paywalled publisher landing/abstract page (jendodon.com), not the full article — methodology detail beyond the abstract (statistical tests, full results tables, discussion) was not retrieved. If full text becomes available, re-ingest per INGEST.md Step 3 (re-read PDF, update wiki).
- Single tooth type (maxillary second bicuspid) and in-vitro nondestructive loading — external validity to other tooth types and to actual fracture (destructive) loading is not established by this study alone.
- n=42, single-center, 1989 — predates modern adhesive/bonded restorative materials that later literature (fiber posts, resin composite, endocrowns) uses to try to recover lost stiffness.

## 6. Related Work
- suksaphar-2017 (wiki/post-and-core/suksaphar-2017-survival-fracture-ett-crown-resin-sr): later systematic review comparing full-coverage crowns vs. resin composite for posterior ETT with limited structural loss — consistent with this paper's premise that preserving/restoring marginal ridge integrity is what matters, not the endodontic treatment itself.
- naumann-2017 (wiki/post-and-core/naumann-2017-ferrule-first-post-second-systematic-review): later systematic review establishing ferrule (residual coronal tooth structure) as the dominant survival factor over post presence — consistent with this paper's finding that structural loss, not endodontic treatment, drives weakening.

## 7. Glossary
- **Cuspal stiffness**: resistance of a tooth cusp to deflection under occlusal load; used here as a proxy measure of tooth strength.
- **MOD cavity preparation**: mesio-occluso-distal cavity preparation — removes both marginal ridges, the restorative pattern most associated with stiffness loss in this study.
- **Marginal ridge**: the raised enamel ridge at the mesial/distal edge of a posterior tooth's occlusal surface; its structural continuity is identified here as the key determinant of cuspal stiffness.
