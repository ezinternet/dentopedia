## Why Ingested
Adds a long-term (up to 10 years) implant survival cohort in a special-care population (registered physical, mental and multiple disabilities) to the survival cluster, where the host-risk pages cover systemic disease and habits but not disability or caregiver-dependent maintenance; it also provides a mechanical-complication profile (decementation, chipping) that complements the bruxism evidence → [[implants/survival/vidal-2025-bruxism-mechanical-complications-implant-srma]] and the long-term survival cohort [[implants/survival/song-2024-long-term-clinical-radiographic-outcomes]].

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-centre cohort (Dankook University Dental Hospital, Korea; 2013–2023) of 137 patients with registered disabilities who received 465 implants, followed 0.5–10 years.

Ten-year cumulative survival was 98.3% overall (98.1% physical, 99.1% mental, 95.7% multiple disabilities); 7 implants failed, 5 of them biological losses before prosthesis delivery, and the 343 restored implants had 99.7% functional survival.

Early healing is the main risk window and mechanical problems (decementation 9.33% of implants) outnumbered biological ones; limits are retrospective single-centre design, no between-group testing and 25% of implants still unrestored.

## 세줄요약

후향적 단일 기관 코호트 (한국 단국대학교 치과병원, 2013–2023)로 등록 장애 환자 137명에게 식립한 임플란트 465개를 0.5–10년 추적했다.

10년 누적 생존율은 전체 98.3% (신체장애 98.1%, 정신장애 99.1%, 중복장애 95.7%)였고 실패 7개 중 5개는 보철 장착 전 생물학적 실패였으며 보철이 장착된 343개의 기능 생존율은 99.7%였다.

초기 치유기가 주된 위험 시기이며 기계적 합병증 (크라운 탈락 임플란트의 9.33%)이 생물학적 합병증보다 많았다; 한계는 후향적 단일 기관 설계, 군간 검정 부재, 임플란트의 25%가 미수복 상태라는 점이다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Large cohort for this population: 137 patients, 465 implants, follow-up up to 10 years, with complications analysed in patients who completed definitive prostheses.
- Treats "multiple disabilities" as its own analytic category (authors state no previous cohort had done so).
- Separates overall survival (all placed implants, censoring unloaded ones) from functional survival of restored implants.
- Reports a detailed mechanical-complication profile and the role of temporary cement in a population where retrievability is prioritised.

## 3. Methodology
- Design: retrospective cohort; patients with a government-issued disability registration card (Ministry of Health and Welfare, Korea), implant treatment January 2013 – December 2023.
- Inclusion: disability card; radiographic and clinical records of surgery and/or prosthetic procedures; at least one documented follow-up after placement. Exclusion: insufficient implant information; no follow-up after placement or prosthesis delivery.
- Preparatory phase (restorative and periodontal treatment) assessed patient and caregiver motivation and ability to maintain hygiene; only those with adequate compliance proceeded to surgery.
- Treatment under general anaesthesia, intravenous sedation or chairside with behavioural management according to cooperation.
- Maintenance: prophylaxis at each stage; written and verbal caregiver instruction; recall at 2 weeks, 3 months, 6 months, then annually for cooperative patients; mandatory 1-month visit for uncooperative patients; examinations under GA or sedation every 1–3 years for severe behavioural challenges.
- Outcome definition: survival/failure per clinical criteria of Misch et al.; survival period = placement to documented failure or most recent follow-up.
- Statistics: Kaplan–Meier cumulative survival (SPSS 29.0); subgroup comparison by disability type; significance set at p < 0.05.

## 4. Key Results
- Sample: 137 patients (85 men, 52 women), 465 implants; the Methods give mean age 55.51 years (range 19–85) whereas the Results give 46.3 years (internal inconsistency in the source, both reported here). 35 patients (117 implants) had no definitive prosthesis.
- Disability type: physical 46 (33.5%), mental 69 (50.4%), multiple 22 (16.1%). Physical: 26 musculoskeletal, 10 neurological, 10 hearing/visual. Mental: 61 intellectual, 6 psychiatric, 2 autism.
- Distribution: 213 maxillary (67 anterior, 146 posterior) and 252 mandibular (31 anterior, 221 posterior) implants.
- Prostheses (343 implants analysed): screw-cement-retained 301 (76 single, 186 splinted, 39 bridge), cement-retained 25, overdenture 10, 7 undocumented; 189 (55.1%) temporary cement, 154 (44.9%) final cement. 117 implants unrestored and 5 failed before prosthesis delivery.
- Failures: 7 implants — 5 biological (failed osseointegration, infection or peri-implantitis, all before prosthesis delivery, at 1–5.5 months) and 2 mechanical (fixture fracture after a fall at 85.5 months; abutment screw fracture at the prosthetic stage).
- Cumulative survival at 10 years: 98.3% overall; 98.1% physical, 99.1% mental, 95.7% multiple disabilities (no formal between-group test). Functional survival of the 343 restored implants: 99.7%.
- Biological complications: 11 implants (peri-implantitis n=9, peri-implant gingivitis n=2, soft-tissue recession n=1); overall biological prevalence 5.54% (19/343) and mechanical 17.20% (59/343).
- Mechanical complications: crown decementation 32 implants (41 events; 9.33% of implants), porcelain chipping 12 implants (18 events), screw loosening 12 (12 events), screw fracture 3 (3 events); 5 decementation cases required prosthesis re-making.
- Decementation: 8 events after final cement and 33 after temporary cement; all recurrent decementation occurred in patients with intellectual disability. One patient with physical disability and severe nocturnal bruxism had six mechanical failures (four anterior and two posterior chipping events). Records identified 10 patients with bruxism and 2 with clenching.

## 5. Limitations
- Single-centre retrospective design; severity of cooperation and caregiver involvement not standardised across disability types (authors).
- Radiographic outcomes such as marginal bone level were not quantified (authors).
- 25% of implants had not reached the definitive prosthetic phase (authors); unloaded implants were treated as censored.
- No formal between-group testing by disability type; the multiple-disability estimate (95.7%) is descriptive only, with few events overall (7 failures).
- Bruxism and parafunction likely under-recorded because patients may be unable to self-report; Kaplan–Meier figures are crude, with no adjustment for confounders.
- Patients lost to follow-up after prosthesis delivery were excluded (selection effect), and only patients judged compliant in the preparatory phase were operated, limiting generalisability.

## 7. Clinical Relevance
- Risk of loss is concentrated before loading; protect early healing in patients with motor instability or limited cooperation.
- Decide on implant treatment after assessing patient and caregiver ability to maintain hygiene; give caregivers written and verbal home-care instruction.
- Prefer simple, retrievable prostheses (screw-cement-retained, temporary cement) and expect decementation as the commonest complication.
- Even when frequent recall is not possible, professional examination every few years (with sedation or GA if needed) was proposed by the authors.
