## Why Ingested

The wiki had the implant-side planning guide but nothing that puts root-canal treatment and implant replacement on the same outcome scale with an explicit critique of the studies that favour implants — this BDJ review supplies that counterweight and the Dental Practicality Index framework → [[implants/srikanth-2025-treatment-planning-single-tooth-implant]] and [[post-and-core/lane-2024-survival-success-rates-endodontically-treated]].

## Three-line Summary

Narrative clinical review (British Dental Journal 2025;238:779-789, King's College London / UCL Eastman) comparing endodontic retention of a compromised tooth against extraction and implant replacement, covering outcome definitions, the Dental Practicality Index, complications of both modalities, and the comparative literature.

Both modalities achieve high survival (primary root canal treatment success 85.2-92.6%, retreatment 77.2-83%; implant survival around 90.9% over 6.7-8.7 years in one pooled comparison), the comparative evidence is inconsistent and confounded, and the differences that do appear are small and late rather than large and early.

The decision should be driven by patient and tooth factors — restorability, periodontal support, strategic value, occlusion and parafunction — rather than by headline survival percentages, since parafunction alone raises implant mechanical/technical complication rates from 17% to 60%.

## Key Contributions
- States the outcome-definition problem plainly: endodontic success is defined biologically (apical periodontitis resolution) while implant success is defined mechanically (no mobility, <0.2 mm annual bone loss, no radiolucency), so cross-modality comparisons are only valid on *survival*.
- Introduces the Dental Practicality Index as the only validated holistic tool, weighting endodontic, periodontal, prosthodontic and contextual domains and mapping them to treat / refer / extract.
- Collates usable outcome ranges: primary root canal treatment success 85.2-92.6%, retreatment 77.2-83%; one pooled comparison (6.7-8.7 years) gives 86.02% for root canal treatment, 78.2% for retreatment, 63.4% for root-end surgery and 90.9% for implants.
- Critiques the pro-implant comparative studies directly — the Chatzopoulos analysis of >13,000 patients found 72.7% tooth survival at 77 months versus implants, but its Cox model omitted oral hygiene, periodontal disease, caries activity, bruxism and restorative status, and 58.8% of implant failures occurred in year one.
- Quantifies the parafunction penalty: mechanical and technical complication rates 60% with a parafunctional habit versus 17% without.
- Explains why endodontic-treated teeth fail — mostly biomechanically (unrestorable caries, cusp/crown fracture, vertical root fracture, periodontal disease) rather than biologically — and why implants fail — peri-implantitis risk concentrated in patients with poor plaque control, periodontitis history, smoking, submucosal cement and augmented bone.

## Methodology
Narrative review, no systematic search, no risk-of-bias tool, no meta-analysis. Structure: (1) why the save-versus-replace decision is difficult; (2) assessment indices including the Dental Practicality Index; (3) discrepancies in outcome definitions for both modalities (four tables); (4) outcomes and patient/clinical considerations for root-filled teeth; (5) outcomes and considerations for implants; (6) occlusal and biological complications; (7) comparative research; (8) conclusion.

## Key Results

**Outcome and survival figures cited**

| Modality | Figure | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Primary root canal treatment success | 85.2-92.6% | Systematic review data |
| Root canal retreatment success | 77.2-83% | Systematic review data |
| Root canal treatment survival | 86.02% | Pooled, 6.7-8.7 years observation |
| Retreatment survival | 78.2% | Same pooled comparison |
| Root-end surgery survival | 63.4% | Same pooled comparison (diluted by non-microsurgical studies) |
| Implant survival | 90.9% | Same pooled comparison |
| Root-filled tooth survival (large retrospective) | 72.7% at 77 months | Chatzopoulos et al., >13,000 patients |
| Implant failures in first year | 58.8% | Same study |

**Comparative findings.** At 72 months one study found no significant survival difference between modalities with high success in both. A systematic review of initial and secondary endodontic treatment versus implants found comparable survival for the first three years, after which root-filled tooth survival declined faster — but three of its included studies reported *lower* survival for implant-supported prostheses. Another review found no meaningful difference up to ten years, with single-tooth implants slightly superior and the gap widening after ten years. Root-filled teeth without cuspal coverage failed earlier than those with coverage.

**Complications.** Parafunction: 60% mechanical/technical complication rate versus 17% without. Peri-implant mucositis is plaque-driven and reversible; peri-implantitis adds progressive bone loss and is concentrated in poor plaque control, prior periodontitis, smoking, submucosal cement, ill-fitting restorations and implants in augmented bone (higher peri-implantitis than pristine bone, similar mucositis incidence).

## Limitations
Narrative review: no systematic search strategy, no formal quality appraisal, and the comparative section reports other authors' numbers without re-analysis. The authors themselves acknowledge that long-term prospective head-to-head data are lacking, that implant studies rarely count preparatory grafting in their outcome accounting, and that most comparative datasets are retrospective with incomplete confounder control. Practical guidance is largely expert opinion plus cited reviews rather than new synthesis.

## Glossary
- **Dental Practicality Index:** validated four-domain (endodontic, periodontal, prosthodontic, contextual) index scoring whether a compromised tooth should be treated, referred or extracted.
- **Survival vs success:** survival = element present at follow-up regardless of condition; success = present with defined healthy criteria met.
- **Cuspal coverage:** coronal restoration covering the cusps, needed to protect root-filled posterior teeth from fracture.
- **Parafunction:** bruxism/clenching beyond normal function.
- **Root-end surgery:** apicoectomy with retrograde obturation.
- **Peri-implant mucositis / peri-implantitis:** reversible soft-tissue inflammation vs inflammation with progressive supporting bone loss.
