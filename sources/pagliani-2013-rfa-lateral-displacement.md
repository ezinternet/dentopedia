## Why Ingested

The foundational in vitro paper establishing that 공명주파수분석 (Resonance Frequency Analysis, RFA) / 임플란트 안정성 지수 (Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ) measurements are a direct proxy for lateral micromobility (μm), with both traceable to bone density. This paper is routinely cited whenever RFA's physical meaning is discussed, and it provides the empirical substrate for the mechanism overview at [[overviews/rfa-isq-measurement-mechanism]] — specifically the claim that "ISQ is stiffness, stiffness is not wobble, but stiffness predicts wobble."

## Three-line Summary

(Line 1: in vitro bovine bone study, n=30 implant sites in 9 specimens, measuring RFA and direct lateral displacement under a standardized 25 N lateral force with simultaneous CBCT bone density quantification)
(Line 2: significant inverse correlation between RFA and lateral displacement in both perpendicular and parallel directions, P ≤ 0.001; RFA and displacement both correlated with bone density, P ≤ 0.001; flex constant (μm/N) calculated for each site)
(Line 3: demonstrates that RFA reflects implant micromobility, which is in turn determined by peri-implant bone density — grounding the ISQ number in a physically interpretable quantity)

## 1. Document Information

- **Journal**: Journal of Oral Rehabilitation 2013;40(3):221-227
- **DOI**: 10.1111/joor.12024
- **First published online**: 28 December 2012
- **Institution**: Authors affiliated with Gothenburg/Malmö institutions (Sennerby, Petersson) and Italian centers (Pagliani, Verrocchi, Volpe)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Directly demonstrates an inverse correlation (P ≤ 0.001) between RFA measurements and actual lateral displacement (μm) of dental implants under a standardized 25 N lateral force.
- Introduces the **flex constant (μm/N)** — lateral displacement normalized to applied force — as a derived mechanical stability metric.
- Shows that both RFA and lateral displacement measurements correlate significantly with bone density (CBCT-derived, P ≤ 0.001), closing the triad: bone density → micromobility → RFA.
- Demonstrates measurement in both perpendicular and parallel directions relative to the long axis of the bone specimens.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: In vitro experimental bench study
- **Specimens**: 9 fresh bovine bone blocks; 30 implant sites prepared in total
- **Bone density**: Quantified using cone beam computerized tomography (CBCT) and imaging software per site
- **Insertion**: Dental implants inserted with continuous insertion torque registration
- **RFA**: Measurements performed in both perpendicular and parallel directions relative to the long axis of the specimens
- **Fixation**: Bone blocks embedded in plaster and fixed in a specially designed rig for displacement measurement
- **Loading**: 25 N lateral force applied via an abutment, perpendicular and parallel to each implant
- **Outcomes**: Lateral displacement (μm); flex constant (μm/N); RFA values; bone density (HU)
- **Statistics**: Correlation analysis; P ≤ 0.001 significance threshold reported

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **RFA ↔ lateral displacement**: Significant inverse correlation in both perpendicular and parallel directions (P ≤ 0.001)
- **RFA ↔ flex constant**: Significant correlation in both directions (P ≤ 0.001)
- **RFA and displacement ↔ bone density**: Both measurement types correlated with CBCT bone density (P ≤ 0.001)
- The study supports the interpretation that ISQ/RFA measures the stiffness of the implant–bone system, which physically opposes lateral displacement

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- In vitro bovine bone model — bone geometry, remodeling dynamics, and soft-tissue effects absent
- Absence of soft-tissue interface (mucosa/gingiva) around the implant, which modifies clinical RFA readings
- Static 25 N lateral force is an experimental simplification; clinical occlusal forces are dynamic

## 6. Related Work

- Meredith 1996: Introduced RFA for implant stability quantification in the implant–tissue interface — foundational reference
- Trisi 2009/2011: Also linked insertion torque and bone density to implant micromotion (in vivo sheep model)
- Sennerby 2015: Extended this same flex-constant approach to two different implant macro-geometries in bovine bone, using 15 N lateral force
- Trisi 2010 (J Osteol Biomater): Compared ISQ to in vitro micromotion measurement with varying bone density and insertion torque

## 7. Glossary

- **RFA (Resonance Frequency Analysis, 공명주파수분석)**: Measures the resonance frequency of the implant–SmartPeg assembly driven by a magnetic pulse; output converted to ISQ
- **ISQ (Implant Stability Quotient, 임플란트 안정성 지수)**: Dimensionless 1–100 score derived from RFA frequency via a proprietary polynomial
- **Lateral displacement (측방 변위)**: Physical micro-displacement (μm) of implant apex under a defined lateral force; direct measure of implant micromobility
- **Flex constant (굴곡 상수, μm/N)**: Lateral displacement normalized to applied force; enables comparison across different force magnitudes
- **Micromobility (미세이동성)**: Sub-clinical implant motion at the bone interface; clinically not detectable by hand but measurable with instrumented rigs
- **CBCT (Cone Beam Computed Tomography, 콘빔전산화단층촬영)**: Volumetric imaging used here for bone density quantification per implant site
