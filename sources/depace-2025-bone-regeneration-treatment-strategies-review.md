## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (PubMed/MEDLINE search through 31 Dec 2024, English, human studies, 2019–2024, orthopedic/oncologic surgery literature explicitly excluding dental/oral-surgery topics) synthesizing current bone-substitute strategies across six categories: autografts, allografts, xenografts, naturally derived biomaterials, synthetic scaffolds, and composite materials.

Autologous bone remains the clinical gold standard (osteogenic + osteoinductive + osteoconductive, >10% failure/complication rate from donor-site morbidity); allografts (esp. demineralized bone matrix) and xenografts (bovine/equine, decellularized) are common alternatives, while synthetic bioceramics/bioactive glass/metals/polymers and composite materials (e.g., xenograft + resorbable polymer) are reviewed with dozens of named commercial products (Table 5) and clinical outcome data (e.g., 90% consolidation in joint fusions, 84% spinal fusion success with bioactive glass autograft extenders).

As a narrative (non-systematic, non-meta-analytic) review focused on orthopedic/oncologic applications rather than dental/oral surgery, findings should be read as broad materials-science context rather than dentistry-specific clinical guidance; scaffold vascularization, degradation-rate control, and manufacturing standardization remain unresolved challenges across all material classes.

## 세줄요약

Narrative review(PubMed/MEDLINE, 2024년 12월 31일까지 검색, 영문, 인간 대상, 2019–2024, 치과/구강외과 문헌 명시적 제외한 정형외과·종양외과 문헌)로, 골 대체재 전략을 자가골·동종골·이종골·천연유래 생체재료·합성 스캐폴드·복합재료 6개 범주로 종합.

자가골(autograft)은 여전히 임상 gold standard(골형성+골유도+골전도 특성, 공여부 이환으로 10% 이상 실패/합병증률)이며, 동종골(특히 탈회골기질 DBM)과 이종골(우골/마골, 탈세포화)이 대표적 대안이고, 합성 생체세라믹·생체활성유리·금속·고분자 및 복합재료(예: 이종골+흡수성 고분자)를 다수의 상용 제품명(Table 5)과 임상 결과(예: 관절유합 90% 골유합, 생체활성유리 자가골 보강 척추유합 성공률 84%)와 함께 정리.

체계적 검색전략·메타분석 없는 narrative review이며 치과가 아닌 정형외과/종양외과 초점이므로, 치과 임상 지침이 아닌 재료과학적 배경지식으로 해석해야 하며, 스캐폴드 혈관화·분해속도 조절·제조 표준화는 모든 재료군에서 여전히 미해결 과제.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Autograft failure/complication rate: >10%, driven by donor-site morbidity, secondary infection/fracture, pain.
- rhBMP-2 + autologous iliac crest bone + bone cells (bilateral alveolar cleft study, cited but oral-surgery-adjacent): improved regeneration, fewer additional surgeries.
- rhBMP-2 + autograft for non-union/bone defects (n=24): fusion rate 96% at 6 months, 100% at 1 year; no antibody development against BMP-2.
- Allograft immune sensitization: average 48% of patients remain immunologically sensitized post-allograft (Moraschini SR).
- Freeze-dried bone allograft for dorsal nasal augmentation (n=62): 85% success.
- Bioactive glass (BG) + local autograft in spinal fusion (n=396): 84% successful fusion; reduced infection risk.
- BG S53P4 for chronic osteomyelitis (n=50, post-debridement): 70.3% healed at 6 months, 83.3% at 12 months, without antibiotics.
- Joint fusion (foot/ankle) with bone grafts: consolidation rates up to 90%.
- Spinal fusion with autograft + substitute combination: 90% clinical success rate, reduced healing time.
- PCL/TCP 3D-printed cages for lumbar interbody fusion (n=22): bone fusion rate 95.2%.
- PMMA-BODBB (vs. BPO-catalyzed PMMA): highest osteointegration in rat cranial defect model among tested PMMA formulations.
- HAp pore filling 50–60% with cortical bone: bending strength increases to 40–60 MPa.
- Calcium sulfate: fastest-dissolving material (4–12 weeks), limited osteoconductive capacity, unsuitable for load-bearing sites.
- BMAC + ACL reconstruction (n=80): higher graft metabolic activity on MRI at 3 months, excellent knee function at 9 months in BMAC group.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative (non-systematic) review — no PRISMA protocol, no meta-analytic pooling; findings summarized qualitatively across heterogeneous study designs and outcome measures.
- Explicitly excludes dental/oral-surgery literature by search design, limiting direct dentistry applicability despite covering materials (xenograft, DBM, bioceramics, bioactive glass) used interchangeably in dental bone regeneration.
- Many cited studies for novel materials (e.g., PMMA-BODBB, TaC-PMMA, PHA-based scaffolds) are limited to animal models; authors explicitly call for clinical validation.
- Persistent unresolved challenges across all material classes: scaffold vascularization, degradation-rate mismatch with bone healing speed, and lack of manufacturing standardization causing batch-to-batch variability in mechanical/biological properties.
- Authors highlight AI/computational modeling and advanced 3D printing as promising but early-stage directions for implant customization and outcome prediction.

## 7. Glossary

- **DBM (Demineralized Bone Matrix)**: Allograft processed with acid to remove mineral content while retaining osteoinductive protein/growth-factor structure.
- **BCP (Biphasic Calcium Phosphate)**: Combination of hydroxyapatite (HAp) and tricalcium phosphate (TCP), tunable resorption/mechanical profile.
- **PMMA (Polymethyl Methacrylate)**: Thermoplastic bone-cement polymer; bioinert, used in vertebroplasty and as a filler, modified (e.g., PMMA-BODBB) to improve bioactivity.
- **PLGA (Polylactic-co-Glycolic Acid)**: Biodegradable copolymer of PLA and PGA; tunable degradation rate via acid ratio.
- **BMAC (Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate)**: Autologous concentrate rich in mesenchymal stem cells, growth factors, and cytokines used to augment grafts.
- **Stress shielding**: Bone-density loss around a rigid metallic implant due to mismatched elastic modulus reducing mechanical loading on native bone.
- **Alpha-Gal epitope**: Non-human mammalian cell-surface carbohydrate targeted by human immune rejection in xenografts; reduced via decellularization.
