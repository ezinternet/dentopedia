## Why Ingested

The wiki's antibiotic-prophylaxis pages ([[wiki/drug/antibiotics/wilson-2021-ie-prophylaxis-aha-scientific-statement]], [[wiki/drug/antibiotics/thornhill-2019-adverse-reactions-oral-antibiotics-dentists]], [[wiki/overviews/sinus-augmentation-perioperative-pharmacology-overview]]) all reflexively route the ~10% of patients carrying a penicillin-allergy label to clindamycin or a cephalosporin, but held nothing quantifying how weak the penicillin–cephalosporin cross-reactivity actually is or how large the delabeling opportunity is. This medical (allergy/immunology) narrative review supplies those numbers — dual-allergy frequency 0.7% overall / 3% with confirmed penicillin allergy, far below the old 8–10% dogma. **Dental relevance:** when a penicillin-allergy-labelled patient needs surgical or IE prophylaxis (extraction, implant, sinus lift), this is the evidence that cefazolin/cephalexin is almost always safe for an unverified, low-risk history, and that a pre-operative allergy evaluation can delabel most such patients.

## Three-line Summary

Single-author narrative review (Frontiers in Allergy 2022) synthesising the epidemiology of penicillin allergy and of perioperative anaphylaxis, their intersection, the pregnancy/Group B Streptococcus case, penicillin–cephalosporin cross-reactivity, pre-operative allergy evaluation, and post-event allergy referral.

Penicillin allergy is reported by ~10% of the population but most labelled patients are not truly allergic; perioperative anaphylaxis is rare (~1 in 6,531–10,000 procedures, 2% fatal); penicillin/cephalosporin dual allergy is 0.7% of meta-analysis patients (3% with a confirmed penicillin allergy), and multiple before/after studies show cefazolin can replace vancomycin/clindamycin in penicillin-allergy-labelled surgical patients with no signal of increased hypersensitivity.

The vast majority of penicillin-allergy-labelled patients can safely receive perioperative cephalosporins; pre-operative penicillin allergy evaluation / direct challenge delabels most low-risk patients; every patient who has had perioperative anaphylaxis should be referred to allergy/immunology because an agent is identifiable in only ~18% by history alone yet evaluation lets ~45/47 tolerate future anaesthesia.

## 세줄요약

단독 저자 내러티브 리뷰(Frontiers in Allergy 2022): 페니실린 알레르기 (Penicillin Allergy)와 수술기 아나필락시스 (Perioperative Anaphylaxis)의 역학, 그 교차점, 임신/B군 연쇄상구균 (Group B Streptococcus, GBS) 상황, 페니실린-세팔로스포린 (Cephalosporin) 교차반응, 수술 전 알레르기 평가, 사건 후 알레르기 의뢰를 종합.

페니실린 알레르기는 인구의 약 10%가 보고하나 라벨 보유자 대다수는 진짜 알레르기가 아님; 수술기 아나필락시스는 드묾(시술 6,531~10,000건당 1건, 2% 치명적); 페니실린/세팔로스포린 이중 알레르기는 메타분석 환자의 0.7%(확인된 페니실린 알레르기에서는 3%)이며, 여러 전후 비교 연구에서 페니실린 알레르기 라벨 환자에게 세파졸린 (Cefazolin)이 반코마이신 (Vancomycin)/클린다마이신 (Clindamycin)을 대체해도 과민반응 증가 신호 없음.

페니실린 알레르기 라벨 환자 대다수는 수술기 세팔로스포린을 안전하게 받을 수 있음; 수술 전 페니실린 알레르기 평가/직접 유발검사 (Direct Challenge)로 저위험 환자 대부분을 라벨 해제 (Delabeling); 수술기 아나필락시스를 겪은 모든 환자는 알레르기/면역내과로 의뢰해야 함 — 병력만으로는 원인 물질이 약 18%에서만 확인되나 평가를 거치면 47명 중 약 45명이 이후 마취를 견딤.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Frontiers in Allergy 2022;3:903161 (published 2022-06-09)
- **DOI**: 10.3389/falgy.2022.903161
- **PMID/PMCID**: 35769557 / PMC9234876
- **Type**: Narrative review, single author (Allison Ramsey, sole contributor)

## 2. Key Contributions
- Places penicillin allergy and perioperative anaphylaxis side by side, quantifying both and their overlap.
- Assembles the modern low-cross-reactivity evidence: dual allergy 0.7% overall, 3% with confirmed penicillin allergy — contradicts the historical 8–10% figure.
- Summarises institutional before/after interventions (Sexton, Grant, Macy) that increased perioperative cefazolin use in penicillin-allergy-labelled patients with no rise in hypersensitivity reactions.
- Reviews the pregnancy / Group B Streptococcus intersection, where penicillin G is the drug of choice and beta-lactams dominate 2nd/3rd-trimester and peripartum anaphylaxis.
- Makes the case for routine allergy/immunology referral after any perioperative anaphylaxis, and for pre-operative penicillin delabeling.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Narrative review (no systematic search protocol, no pooled analysis).
- **Scope**: Epidemiology of penicillin allergy; epidemiology of perioperative anaphylaxis; penicillin-attributable perioperative anaphylaxis; pregnancy; approach to the patient after perioperative anaphylaxis; penicillin/cephalosporin cross-reactivity; pre-operative penicillin allergy evaluation; future directions.
- **Evidence cited**: US National Inpatient Sample (Gonzalez-Estrada), UK/Ireland NAP6, Spanish and Mayo/MGH case series, Sousa-Pinto meta-analysis (6,147 patients, 77 studies), institutional QI interventions.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Penicillin allergy epidemiology (verbatim)**
- "Penicillin allergy is commonly reported, occurring in about 10% of the population"; Table 1: "10% of US general population". Female > Male predominance.
- Label harms: "lower cure rates, increased recurrence rates, increased risks for adverse effects from second line antibiotics, and increased mortality"; public-health: more MRSA / *C. difficile*, "longer and more costly hospital stays"; "the penicillin allergy label has been associated with an increased risk of surgical site infections".

**True-allergy rate (verbatim)**
- "a true cross reactivity figure is difficult to attain given that the majority of retrospectively studied patients with a reported penicillin allergy are not truly allergic"
- "the vast majority of patients with a penicillin allergy label can receive perioperative cephalosporins" (abstract).
- *(No single explicit "<5%" true-allergy percentage is stated in this text.)*

**Perioperative anaphylaxis epidemiology (verbatim)**
- "perioperative anaphylaxis is rare, with severe episodes estimated to occur in 1 in 10,000 surgical procedures … though this latter figure may be an underestimate". Table 1: "0.015 % of US surgical procedures".
- Gonzalez-Estrada, US National Inpatient Sample 2005–2014: "The incidence of perioperative anaphylaxis was 1 in 6,531 procedures, with 5% of cases near fatal and 2% fatal."
- UK/Ireland multicentre: "reported incidence of perioperative anaphylaxis of 1:1,300–1:13,000 may be underestimated, and found an incidence of 1:353 in their analysis".
- "An etiologic agent for perioperative anaphylaxis may not be identified in 28–50% of patients".
- Most common agents: "antibiotics, neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBA), sugammadex …, chlorhexidine, and latex"; beta-lactams lead in US + UK, NMBAs in mainland Europe/Australia/NZ.
- "Hepner et al. reported that the incidence of anaphylaxis to penicillin is 1–5 per 10,000 patients, with fatal anaphylaxis occurring in 1–2 per 100,000 treated patients."
- Cefazolin is "first line pre-operative antibiotic for most surgical procedures", "superior to second line antibiotics, such as clindamycin or vancomycin", and simultaneously "the most common cause of perioperative anaphylaxis in the United States".
- France: incidence of perioperative anaphylaxis to antibiotics rose "from 2% in the 1980s to 20% presently", penicillin and cefazolin leading.

**Penicillin / cephalosporin cross-reactivity (verbatim)**
- Sousa-Pinto meta-analysis: "included 6,147 patients in 77 studies (13 studies in surgical patients exclusively) identified only 44 patients with a cephalosporin allergy who had an index penicillin allergy, calculated as a dual allergy frequency in 0.7% of meta-analysis patients, but the frequency increased to 3% in patients who had a confirmed penicillin allergy."
- "the frequency of penicillin allergy in patients with reactions to cefazolin was 4.4% in the eight studies looking at this figure."
- "patients with an existing cephalosporin allergy have a higher risk of coexisting penicillin allergy … possibly … explained by the lower incidence of cephalosporin allergy in general as compared to the 10% incidence of reported penicillin allergy."
- Retrospective 734 surgical procedures / 690 penicillin-allergy-labelled patients on clindamycin, vancomycin or cefazolin: "no difference in hypersensitivity reactions regardless of the preoperative antibiotic used".
- Sexton et al. QI: cefazolin use "from 34% pre-initiative to >80% (in 756 patients)" — "no recorded hypersensitivity reactions".
- Grant et al. (Vancouver General Hospital): after policy allowing cefazolin despite penicillin allergy (except SCAR histories), "cefazolin use increased 18%, whereas vancomycin use decreased by over 11%, and clindamycin use decreased by 62%. There were no reports of anaphylaxis or severe allergic reactions".
- Macy et al.: removing the EHR penicillin/cephalosporin cross-reactivity alert produced "a significant increase in cephalosporin prescribing … without any concomitant increase in hypersensitivity reactions".
- Bottom line (verbatim): "this retrospective data is reassuring regarding the use of perioperative cephalosporins for most patients reporting an unverified penicillin allergy, but demonstrate a higher risk (3%) in patients with a verified penicillin allergy." "Consultation with an allergist can be considered in patients with a verified penicillin allergy or high-risk unverified penicillin allergy history."

**R1 side-chain concept** — *not discussed in this text.* The review attributes low cross-reactivity to epidemiology (most labels are not true allergy; cephalosporin allergy is rare) rather than to shared/dissimilar R1 side-chain structure. Flagged as a gap; the structural (R1 side-chain) explanation must come from another source.

**Pregnancy, penicillin and peripartum anaphylaxis (verbatim)**
- "Penicillin G is the preferred agent for Group B [Streptococcus] prophylaxis in pregnancy, and rare cases of anaphylaxis to penicillin-based antibiotics in pregnant women have occurred during labor and delivery."
- "Most of these patients presented with hypotension that resolved with appropriate treatment, though in one case, there was irreversible fetal neurological damage."
- "Hepner et al. highlight cases of anaphylaxis during labor since 2000, with 11 of 13 cases attributable to beta-lactams, five of which were penicillin-based antibiotics."
- SR of 12 articles on anaphylaxis in pregnancy: "occurred during Cesarean section in 49–74% of cases, with beta lactams as the most common cause in 58% of patients, with an allergy work-up only described in two of 12 studies"; beta-lactams became the most common cause in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters, 1st-trimester causes unchanged from non-pregnant (food, venom, drugs).
- Discussion (verbatim): "As with surgical prophylaxis, the benefit of using penicillin G for Group B [Streptococcus] prophylaxis outweighs the low risk of anaphylaxis."

**Management after perioperative anaphylaxis + allergy referral (verbatim)**
- "In most cases of perioperative anaphylaxis, patients have received multiple drugs within a short time frame, therefore, allergy/immunology follow up should be arranged for patients who have experienced perioperative anaphylaxis."
- "most patients will need to undergo testing and/or drug challenges in order to undergo future surgical procedures safely."
- MGH retrospective, 73 patients (21 beta-lactam reactors): "an etiologic agent was identifiable in 18% of patients undergoing allergy/immunology evaluation"; yet "the A/I evaluation allowed 45 of 47 patients to tolerate future anesthesia", the remaining two diagnosed with a mast cell disorder.
- Gurrieri et al. (Mayo): "23 of 38 patients with a history of perioperative anaphylaxis underwent future surgical procedures", IgE-mediated cases avoiding the culprit; 5 presumed non-IgE cases premedicated with prednisone + diphenhydramine tolerated all agents.
- Gonzalez-Estrada: "21 of 30 patients tolerated subsequent surgeries based on allergy evaluation recommendations".
- Spanish prospective clinic (473 patients skin-prick tested to 41 drugs regardless of exposure history): "seven of 17 patients with a history of anaphylaxis likely reacted to penicillin … good correlation with positive penicillin skin testing in cases where penicillin was suspected."
- "Optimally, health care systems would automatically refer patients who experience perioperative anaphylaxis to allergy/immunology for this purpose."

**Pre-operative penicillin allergy evaluation / delabeling (verbatim)**
- "There is neither evidence nor reliable tools to pre-screen patients without a penicillin or cephalosporin allergy prior to surgery … no evidence to justify widespread screening for mast cell disorders."
- Savic et al. (UK direct challenge, low-risk histories): "They de-labeled 55 of 56 patients prior to surgery, and of the patients in whom a penicillin-based antibiotic was indicated, 17/19 received the appropriate antibiotic."
- Canada multi-pronged pre-operative program: "194 Patients with a penicillin allergy label … penicillin skin testing followed by a challenge if indicated … four patients with positive skin testing, and 146 of 190 patients underwent oral challenge … the majority (77%) … received cefazolin, and only five patients received vancomycin"; patients not needing vancomycin saved a mean 22 min of OR time.
- UK survey: "only 40% of 4,798 responding anesthesiologists would administer penicillin to patients with a low risk reaction history … only 47% would administer penicillin in a patient previously de-labeled by an allergist" (erroneous concerns about policy change / single-dose oral challenge adequacy).
- "There is admittedly variation in the allergy/immunology literature regarding what constitutes a low risk penicillin allergy reaction history, with general agreement that remote, cutaneous-only reaction histories are low risk", disagreement over hives and "remote".

**Penicillin skin test NPV** — *no explicit negative-predictive-value figure is stated in this text.* The review reports "good correlation with positive penicillin skin testing in cases where penicillin was suspected" and delabeling success rates (Savic 55/56; Canada 4/194 skin-test positive) but does not quote an NPV number. Flagged as a gap.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Single-author narrative review — no systematic search, no risk-of-bias appraisal, selection of cited studies not reproducible.
- Cross-reactivity in *confirmed* penicillin allergy "has not been prospectively studied in large numbers"; the 0.7%/3% figures rest mostly on retrospective data with unverified labels.
- No structural (R1 side-chain) analysis; no explicit true-allergy rate or skin-test NPV.
- Author-identified future needs: risk-stratification and screening tools, genomic prediction, convenient pre-operative delabeling (telemedicine, challenges at pre-op visits), outcome data to justify removing the EHR cross-reactivity warning, uniform guidance on "low-risk" history.
- Not dental-specific — surgical/anaesthesia/obstetric context; dental application is inferential (see Why Ingested).

## 6. Related Work
- Wilson 2021 (AHA IE prophylaxis): supplies the penicillin-allergy alternative regimens (clindamycin 600 mg, cephalexin 2 g, cefazolin 1 g IM/IV) that this review argues are usually over-cautious.
- Thornhill 2019 (oral antibiotic ADR rates): the "amoxicillin allergy → clindamycin" reflex Thornhill flags as hazardous (clindamycin ~29× fatal ADR) is the same reflex this review says a cephalosporin should usually replace.
- Sousa-Pinto et al. meta-analysis (6,147 patients / 77 studies): the primary cross-reactivity evidence cited.
- Sexton, Grant, Macy: institutional QI interventions increasing perioperative cefazolin use in penicillin-allergy-labelled patients.

## 7. Glossary
- **Penicillin allergy label**: a documented report of penicillin allergy in the record, verified or (usually) not.
- **Perioperative anaphylaxis**: acute, systemic, life-threatening hypersensitivity reaction during the operative period; IgE- and non-IgE-mediated.
- **Delabeling**: formally removing an unverified penicillin-allergy label after history, skin testing and/or graded/direct oral challenge.
- **Direct (graded) challenge**: supervised administration of the drug (often a single oral dose, sometimes in steps) to a low-risk patient to confirm tolerance.
- **Dual allergy frequency**: proportion of patients allergic to both a penicillin and a cephalosporin.
- **Cefazolin**: first-generation cephalosporin, standard first-line surgical prophylaxis; distinct side chain from most penicillins.
- **Group B Streptococcus (GBS) prophylaxis**: intrapartum penicillin G to prevent neonatal GBS disease.
- **NMBA**: neuromuscular blocking agent; leading perioperative anaphylaxis trigger in mainland Europe/Australia/NZ.
- **SCAR**: severe cutaneous adverse reaction (e.g. SJS/TEN, DRESS) — an absolute contraindication to re-exposure and an exclusion from cefazolin-substitution policies.
- **NAP6**: 6th UK National Audit Project (Royal College of Anaesthetists), perioperative anaphylaxis.
