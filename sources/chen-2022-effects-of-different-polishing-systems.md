## Why Ingested

Serves the **post-adjustment finishing protocol** axis of the 2026-11-12 `zirconia-occlusal-adjustment` topic: after a diamond bur raises surface roughness (Ra), how many seconds of which three-step intraoral polishing system restore a clinically acceptable surface — and does that polishing heat drive a tetragonal→monoclinic phase transformation (상전이)? It is one of the few studies that pairs roughness measurement with XRD crystal-structure verification on the same specimens, extending [[dental-materials/zirconia/mohammadi-bassir-2017-grinding-overglazing-polishing-zirconia]].

## Three-line Summary

In vitro bench study (Applied Bionics and Biomechanics 2022, n = 48 zirconia discs 10×10×2 mm, ST zirconia "UPzir Solid Zirconia" sintered 7 h at 1500 °C) comparing a glazed/ground control group, an unpolished ground group (GR), and two three-step intraoral polishing systems (Youdent vs Toboom) at 20 s, 40 s and 60 s per step, with profilometry, SEM, and XRD.

Grinding without polishing produced the highest surface roughness (Ra); 20 s of polishing left surfaces significantly rougher than 40 s or 60 s in both systems, Youdent and Toboom at 20 s did not differ, but at 40 s and 60 s the Toboom groups were significantly smoother than the Youdent groups, and even 60 s remained rougher than the glazed surface.

XRD peaks after grinding and polishing matched the untreated baseline pattern, i.e. neither diamond-bur adjustment nor up to 60 s of polishing caused a monoclinic phase transformation — clinical takeaway: polishing already reaches clinically acceptable roughness by 20 s and the Toboom system at 60 s is best, but heat from polishing changes stress, not crystal structure.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Directly separates **duration** (20 / 40 / 60 s per polishing step) from **system brand** (Youdent vs Toboom) in a three-step intraoral polishing sequence after diamond-bur occlusal adjustment — the two variables clinicians can actually control at the chair.
- Quantifies that a diamond bur used for 20 s in water-cooled conditions produces the roughest surface of all groups (GR), confirming that occlusal adjustment without polishing leaves a wear-inducing surface.
- Shows a **ceiling effect in polishing time**: 20 s is already significantly rougher than 40 s and 60 s, while 40 s vs 60 s differences are not consistently significant — consistent with the cited literature that most polishing tools stop improving past ~40 s.
- Reports a **brand effect only at longer durations**: Youdent and Toboom were equivalent at 20 s, but Toboom was significantly smoother at 40 s and 60 s; SEM showed shallower scratches for the Toboom groups at matched durations.
- **Negative phase-transformation finding**: XRD diffractograms after grinding and after up to 60 s of polishing retained the same peak distribution as the untreated reference specimen, so polishing-generated heat did not trigger t→m transformation; the authors interpret any peak change as residual stress rather than structural change.
- Benchmarks results against clinical acceptance: glazing reduced roughness most, 60 s polishing remained rougher than glazed, but every polished group fell inside the clinically acceptable roughness window.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Specimen fabrication**: CAD/CAM design (3Shape, Denmark) of an ST zirconia disc, cut on a Cradle (China) machine tool; 48 specimens of 10 mm × 10 mm × 2 mm; sintered 7 h at 1500 °C (upfire C1, Upcera, China). One randomly chosen specimen was XRD-scanned to serve as the untreated reference (control) pattern.
- **Group allocation (n = 6 each)**:
  - `GL` — grinding-polishing group representing the finished restoration surface (polishing + grinding + glazing).
  - `GR` — grinding only: diamond bur, 20 s, water-cooled, parallel to the specimen surface, simulating clinical occlusal adjustment; no polishing.
  - Remaining 36 specimens → three-step polishing with either the Youdent (YOU) or Toboom (TOB) system at 20 s, 40 s or 60 s per step: `Y20`, `Y40`, `Y60`, `T20`, `T40`, `T60`.
- **Polishing protocol**: pressure-free polishing, direction parallel to the grinding direction, then ultrasonic cleaning in distilled water for 30 s and drying.
- **Roughness**: probe surface profilometer (DEKTAK-XT, Bruker, USA), GB/T 1031-2009 as reference; scan direction perpendicular to the grinding direction; 3 measurements per specimen, arithmetic mean used.
- **SEM**: one random specimen per group, platinum sputter-coated under vacuum, high-resolution field-emission SEM (SIGMA 500, ZEISS, Germany) at ×1000.
- **XRD**: one random specimen per group (Ultima TV, Rigaku, Japan) to detect surface crystal structure; patterns compared to the untreated reference to judge phase-transformation level.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 24.0, mean ± SD, one-way ANOVA with LSD post hoc, independent-samples t-test for two-group comparisons, chi-square for enumeration data; P < 0.05 significant.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Roughness ranking**: GR (ground, unpolished) had the highest roughness of all groups; glazing produced the smoothest surface; polished groups sat in between but within the clinically acceptable range.
- **Duration effect (within system)**: specimens polished for 20 s were significantly rougher than those polished for 40 s and 60 s with the same system; no significant roughness difference between 40 s and 60 s within a system.
- **System effect (at matched duration)**: Y20 vs T20 not significantly different; Y40 and Y60 significantly rougher than T40 and T60 → the Toboom system was superior once polishing time reached 40 s.
- **Best protocol**: Toboom at 60 s per step gave the lowest roughness, yet was still rougher than the glazed surface — attributed by the authors to the polishing system and operator technique.
- **Clinical threshold**: roughness reached the clinically acceptable window already at 20 s of polishing, so 20 s is the minimum useful duration while extra time yields diminishing returns.
- **SEM morphology**: GL smooth and regular with no obvious defects; GR rough with wide deep scratches and dense pits; scratching decreased progressively with polishing time in both systems; the Youdent surface stayed slightly rougher than Toboom at equal duration.
- **XRD**: after grinding and after each polishing protocol, peak positions and distribution were consistent with the untreated baseline — no tetragonal-to-monoclinic conversion detected; the authors conclude the polishing heat input expresses itself as surface stress, not as a phase change.
- **Reference values cited for context (not measured in this study)**: plaque attachment drops effectively when restoration Ra < 0.2 μm (Bollen et al.); enamel Ra is approximately 0.45–0.65 μm, so restorations at or below that range are clinically acceptable; clinical polishing pressure is usually ~2 N; t→m transformation is accompanied by a 3–4 % volume increase.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Flat specimens only**: polishing was performed on planar surfaces, which cannot reproduce the cusp/fossa morphology of an intraoral restoration or the access restrictions of the mouth.
- **Polishing pressure not controlled**: clinical polishing pressure is ~2 N, but no strict pressure standardization/measurement was performed.
- **No inter-system technical comparison**: abrasive type/grit, binder and bur geometry were not compared, so it is unknown *why* Toboom outperformed Youdent; the authors note zirconia-specific polishing tools and diamond-containing systems are reported to be more effective than silicon-carbide systems (Goo et al.).
- **No polishing-efficiency analysis of the grinding bur** itself (bur type/wear not an experimental variable).
- **Mechanical properties not tested**: roughness and crystal structure only — no flexural strength, fatigue or wear testing; the authors call for mechanical evaluation of monolithic zirconia after polishing regimens.
- **Single zirconia brand and grain size**: coarser-grain zirconia may roughen more through grain pull-out during finishing.
- **XRD is surface-averaged**: shallow transformed layers or stress-induced peak shifts may be under-detected relative to micro-Raman or grazing-incidence methods.

## 6. Related Work

- [[dental-materials/zirconia/mohammadi-bassir-2017-grinding-overglazing-polishing-zirconia]] — same adjustment→polishing sequence, but measures flexural strength and phase transformation; polishing after grinding partially restores surface and strength, overglazing alone does not.
- [[dental-materials/zirconia/tachibana-2021-polishing-zirconia-antagonist-wear]] — the clinical stake of this paper's Ra outcome: polished zirconia minimized antagonist wear across three contact conditions.
- [[dental-materials/zirconia/alfrisany-2019-occlusal-adjustments-zirconia-enamel-wear]] — counterpoint: after chewing simulation polished zirconia caused more steatite loss than glazed, i.e. Ra alone does not fully predict antagonist wear.
- [[dental-materials/zirconia/kosmac-1999-grinding-sandblasting-flexural-strength-zirconia]] — foundational grinding paper: grinding enlarges critical defects and lowers Weibull modulus while sandblasting raises strength via monoclinic transformation.
- [[dental-materials/zirconia/sarabi-2024-grinding-ltd-flexural-strength-zirconia]] — grinding/polishing raised strength and monoclinic content before aging, so "no phase change" is protocol-dependent (this study: ≤60 s, no aging).
- [[dental-materials/zirconia/janyavula-2013-wear-polished-glazed-zirconia-enamel]] — polished vs glazed zirconia and enamel wear, the clinical rationale for post-adjustment polishing.

## 7. Glossary

- **표면거칠기 (surface roughness, Ra)** — 산술평균 거칠기; 이 연구의 1차 결과변수, 단위 μm.
- **상전이 (phase transformation)** — 지르코니아의 정방정상 (tetragonal phase, t-ZrO₂) → 단사정상 (monoclinic phase, m-ZrO₂) 전환; 약 3–4 % 체적팽창 동반, 변태강화 (transformation toughening)의 근원이자 균열·잔류응력의 원인.
- **유약 (glazing)** — 소성 유리층으로 표면을 매끄럽게 하는 마무리; 이 연구에서 가장 낮은 Ra를 보인 대조 조건.
- **구강내 연마 (intraoral polishing)** — 회전 연마 기구·연마재를 이용한 의자 옆 마무리; 연삭 결함을 제거하고 대합치 마모를 줄이는 처치.
- **X선 회절 (X-ray diffraction, XRD)** — 표면 결정상 조성·피크 분포를 정량하는 분석법; 여기서는 상전이 판정에 사용.
- **주사전자현미경 (scanning electron microscope, SEM)** — ×1000 표면 형태 관찰; 연삭 흠 (scratch)과 함몰 (pit) 평가.
- **ST 지르코니아** — 3 mol% 이트리아로 정방정상 안정화한 (3Y-TZP 계열) 범용 치과 지르코니아.
