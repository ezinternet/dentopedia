## Why Ingested

Longest-follow-up (1–5 years) clinical survival data for bicortical engagement as an alternative to internal sinus lift with bone grafting in atrophic posterior maxilla. Specifically shows bicortical 6-mm implants achieved 100% survival and significant intrasinus bone gain, whereas 6-mm conventional unicortical implants failed at 48% — resolving the "short implant question" in this context. Extends the evidence base beyond the short-term stability data in [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/bhave-2023-unicortical-bicortical-anchorage-posterior-maxilla]].

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort, 53 patients / posterior maxilla; Group A (bicortical engagement, n=31) vs Group B (traditional unicortical, n=25); 1–5 years prosthetic loading.
Group A survival: 100%; Group B overall survival: 74.68% (6-mm unicortical subgroup only 51.85%); significant intrasinus bone gain on mesial/distal sides in Group A only.
Bicortical engagement 6-mm implants in atrophic posterior maxilla achieve 100% survival with intrasinus bone gain, dramatically outperforming unicortical 6-mm implants (51.85% survival).

## Document Information

- **Journal**: International Journal of Periodontics & Restorative Dentistry
- **DOI**: [10.11607/prd.3241](https://doi.org/10.11607/prd.3241)
- **Study type**: Retrospective cohort
- **n**: 53 patients (31 bicortical, 25 unicortical; 3 mixed)
- **Follow-up**: 1–5 years prosthetic loading

## Key Contributions

- Longest follow-up bicortical engagement clinical data (1–5 years)
- 100% survival for bicortical 6-mm vs 51.85% for unicortical 6-mm — clinically decisive difference
- Intrasinus bone gain documented (mesial and distal) in bicortical group only
- Marginal bone height difference significant only between 6-mm subgroups (A vs B)
- Supports bicortical engagement as equivalent to (or better than) internal sinus lift + graft for atrophic cases

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort, single center
- **Group A**: Bicortical dental implantation (n=31 patients)
- **Group B**: Traditional implantation without bicortical engagement (n=25 patients)
- **Outcomes**: Survival rates; marginal bone height (radiographic); intrasinus bone gain
- **Implant length**: 6 mm and 10 mm variants analyzed separately

## Key Results

| Group | Survival rate | 6-mm subgroup survival |
|---|---|---|
| A (bicortical) | **100%** | **Not specified separately (all 100%)** |
| B (unicortical) | 74.68% | **51.85%** |
| B (10-mm subgroup) | — | 93.33% |

- Marginal bone height: significant difference only between 6-mm subgroups (A vs B)
- Intrasinus bone gain: significant on mesial AND distal in Group A; not present in Group B

## Limitations

- Retrospective design; no randomization or allocation concealment
- Short abstract-only extraction; full methodology not accessible
- Mixed case in 3 patients (bilateral, one bicortical/one not) complicates group analysis
- 5-year maximum follow-up; no 10-year data

## Glossary

- **Bicortical engagement**: Implant tip deliberately engages sinus floor cortex providing dual-cortex anchorage
- **Intrasinus bone gain**: New bone formation within the sinus cavity around protruding implant tip
- **Marginal bone height**: Distance from implant shoulder to crestal bone level on radiograph
