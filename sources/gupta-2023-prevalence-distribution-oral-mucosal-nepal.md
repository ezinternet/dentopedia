정상범주 구강내 병소(Fordyce granules, linea alba 등) 전용 서브카테고리 [[oral-medicine/normal-variants]] 신설의 근거 논문. 16,572명 규모의 대표 cross-sectional 자료로 Fordyce's granules(8.84%), linea alba(연령별 최대 37.21%), frictional keratosis 등 normal variant의 인구 기반 유병률을 제공해 케이스 단위 보고([[oral-medicine/normal-variants/baklouti-2023-whitish-patches-buccal-mucosa-dermoscopy]])와 대비되는 역학적 기준선 역할.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study of 16,572 Nepalese dental OPD patients found 21.08% had oral mucosal lesions (OMLs) and 13.96% had normal mucosal variants (most commonly frictional keratosis, linea alba, and Fordyce's granules), with buccal mucosa the dominant site for both.

## 1. Document Information

- Journal: BioMed Research International, 2023;2023:9375084
- Publication date: 2023-10-18
- Funding: not stated (no external funding disclosed)
- Conflicts of interest: none declared

## 2. Key Contributions

- First Nepal-based epidemiological study reporting overall prevalence of both OMLs and normal variants (prior Nepali studies were histological/biopsy-based only, not population-representative).
- Provides age-, gender-, and site-stratified breakdown of Fordyce's granules, linea alba, and frictional keratosis as normal variants distinct from pathological OMLs.
- Large sample size (16,572) relative to most prior international prevalence studies (typically 500-5,000).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Cross-sectional retrospective record review, Dept. of Oral Medicine and Radiology, KIST Medical College and Teaching Hospital (Lalitpur, Nepal), data collection window Jan-Mar 2021 covering patient records Jan 2016-Dec 2019.
- Census sampling of all oral lesion cases meeting inclusion criteria (clinically/histologically diagnosed OML or normal variant with location data); incomplete records excluded, duplicate patients excluded via unique OPD number.
- Three separate data-entry proformas (age, gender, location) per WHO's guide to epidemiology and diagnosis of oral mucosal diseases/conditions.
- Statistics: descriptive frequency analysis, IBM SPSS v27.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- OML prevalence: 21.08% (3495/16572); normal mucosal variant prevalence: 13.96% (2314/16572); normal oral mucosa: 64.94%.
- Most common OML categories: tobacco-associated lesions 34.07%, tongue lesions 26.48%, OPMD 13.50%, ulcers 12.06%.
- Normal variants more common in males (56.08%) than females (43.91%); frictional keratosis most common in females (38.52%), linea alba most common in males (34.77%).
- Fordyce's granules prevalence 8.84% (most common among 11-20y age group, 31.76% of that group's normal variants); leukoedema prevalence 0.72%.
- Normal variants overwhelmingly located on buccal mucosa (80.10%), consistent with linea alba/frictional keratosis biomechanics.
- Cross-study comparison: Fordyce's granule prevalence highly variable across populations (1.2%-82.8%), underscoring population/examiner-dependent variability.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single tertiary center (Lalitpur region) — geography-specific, not nationally generalizable.
- Retrospective; no histopathological confirmation for most lesions (clinical diagnosis only).
- Authors recommend multicenter, countrywide follow-up study.

## 6. Related Work

- Contrasts with several higher-prevalence studies (Chiang et al. 92.83% OML overall prevalence, 82.8% Fordyce's granules) attributed to differing populations/exam protocols.
- Complements [[oral-medicine/normal-variants/nautiyal-2024-prevalence-keratosis-oral-cavity]] which drills down specifically into frictional/tobacco-pouch keratosis and morsicatio buccarum prevalence in a South Indian cohort.

## 7. Glossary

- **OML**: Oral Mucosal Lesion
- **OPMD**: Oral Potentially Malignant Disorder
- **Fordyce's granules**: ectopic sebaceous glands presenting as small yellow-white papules on oral mucosa/lip vermilion; a benign normal variant
- **Linea alba**: white horizontal line on buccal mucosa at the occlusal plane from chronic mild frictional keratinization, often linked to reduced buccal overjet
- **Leukoedema**: diffuse grayish-white, filmy mucosal opacification that disappears on stretching; benign normal variant
