## Why Ingested

Companion paper to [[jiang-2024-orofacial-pain-sleep-biobank]] (large-scale UK Biobank + Mendelian-randomization study on sleep and orofacial pain, same ingest batch): that paper supplies genetic-causal evidence for a sleep–TMD/orofacial-pain link, while this systematic review synthesizes the qualitative/clinical observational evidence (polysomnography, cohort, cross-sectional) for the same association. Also relevant to [[tmj/minakuchi-2022-sleep-bruxism-management-sr]], since this SR explicitly flags that the bruxism–TMD and OSA–TMD links remain inconsistent across studies — a nuance that qualifies bruxism-management recommendations.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review integrating 7 studies (2009–2024; polysomnographic, cross-sectional, prospective cohort, laboratory-experimental designs) on the association between sleep disorders and temporomandibular joint pain dysfunction syndrome in adults.

TMD patients consistently show worse sleep quality and higher pain sensitivity than controls; sleep apnea symptoms predicted increased TMD risk in a large cohort (OPPERA); degenerative TMJ changes were linked to stress and poor sleep; up to ~90% of TMD patients report poor sleep vs ~15% of non-TMD population; but bruxism–TMD and OSA–TMD associations were inconsistent across studies.

Clinically supports routine sleep screening (e.g., PSQI, STOP-Bang) and multidisciplinary (dental + sleep medicine + psychological) management of TMD; key limitation is the review's reliance on cross-sectional, mostly self-reported/small-sample primary studies with no meta-analysis, limiting causal inference.

## 세줄요약

2009~2024년 발표된 7편(수면다원검사·단면연구·전향적 코호트·실험연구)을 통합한 체계적 문헌고찰로, 성인의 수면장애와 측두하악관절 통증기능장애증후군(TMD) 간 연관성을 다룸.

TMD 환자는 대조군보다 일관되게 수면의 질이 나쁘고 통증 민감도가 높았으며, 대규모 코호트(OPPERA)에서 수면무호흡 증상이 TMD 발생 위험 증가의 유의한 예측인자였고, 퇴행성 TMJ 변화는 스트레스·불량한 수면과 연관됨(TMD 환자의 최대 약 90%가 수면불량 보고, 비TMD군은 약 15%); 다만 이갈이(bruxism)-TMD 및 폐쇄성수면무호흡(OSA)-TMD 연관성은 연구 간 비일관적.

임상적으로 TMD 치료 시 PSQI·STOP-Bang 등을 이용한 정기적 수면 선별검사와 치과·수면의학·심리치료를 아우르는 다학제적 접근을 지지하나, 메타분석 없이 대부분 단면적·자가보고·소규모 원저 연구에 기반해 인과관계 추론에는 한계가 있음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Synthesizes 7 studies (2009–2024) spanning polysomnographic, cross-sectional, prospective-cohort (OPPERA), and laboratory-experimental designs into a single narrative review of sleep disorders and TMD.
- Reports a consistent, reciprocal relationship between poor sleep quality and TMD pain/dysfunction across nearly all included studies, while explicitly flagging inconsistency in the bruxism–TMD and OSA–TMD subtopics.
- Frames a clinical mechanism pathway: sleep disturbance → descending pain-modulation dysregulation, central sensitization, elevated inflammation/cortisol → aggravated TMD pain; and conversely untreated OSA → hypoxia/systemic inflammation → worsened orofacial pain/TMD.
- Recommends routine sleep screening (PSQI, STOP-Bang) integrated into TMD workups and multidisciplinary (dental + sleep medicine + psychological/CBT-I) management.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Literature search across PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science using keyword combinations (specific search string not detailed in the retrieved text).
- Scope: English-language, adult-population, observational/comparative studies assessing sleep disorders (subjective questionnaires/sleep-quality indices or objective polysomnography) and TMD outcomes.
- Exclusions: case reports, reviews, animal studies, studies lacking clear sleep or TMD outcome measures.
- 7 studies met inclusion criteria (2009–2024); data extracted on study design, population, sleep-disorder type, and key findings; synthesized narratively in a single comparative summary table (no formal risk-of-bias tool or PRISMA flow diagram reported in the retrieved text; no meta-analysis/pooled effect size computed).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Summary of the 7 included studies (author, design, population, sleep measure, finding):

| Author (year) | Design | Population | Sleep measure | Key finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dubrovsky | Polysomnographic | Women with TMD | Sleep continuity, respiratory events | TMD patients had poorer sleep quality and altered respiratory parameters |
| Lee | Cross-sectional | Chronic TMD vs. healthy controls | Sleep quality questionnaires | TMD patients showed significantly poorer sleep quality |
| Sanders | Prospective cohort (OPPERA) | Community cohort | Sleep apnea symptoms | Sleep apnea strongly associated with increased TMD risk |
| Smith | Laboratory experimental | TMD patients | Sleep disorders and pain sensitivity | Sleep disorders linked with higher laboratory pain sensitivity |
| Yap | Cross-sectional | Degenerative TMJ patients | Sleep quality and emotional disturbance | Degenerative TMJ disease associated with disturbed sleep and emotional distress |
| Dias | Cross-sectional | Patients with bruxism and TMJ changes | Sleep bruxism, sleep quality | Bruxism associated with low sleep quality and TMJ degeneration |
| Tran | Comparative study | TMJ degeneration group vs. controls | Sleep quality | Poor sleep quality significantly associated with degenerative TMJ changes |

- Up to ~90% of TMD patients report poor sleep vs. ~15% of the general (non-TMD) population.
- A cited related SR found significant association between TMD symptoms and poor sleep at p < 0.05 in most included studies.
- Bruxism–TMD and OSA(sleep apnea)–TMD associations are heterogeneous/inconsistent across the literature, per this review and a broader cited SR; a cited SR on TMJ osteoarthritis specifically found insufficient evidence linking it to increased sleep disorders.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Predominantly cross-sectional primary studies; small sample sizes; heterogeneous diagnostic criteria for both TMD and sleep disorders across included studies.
- Most sleep assessments rely on self-report (questionnaires), introducing recall/memory bias; only some studies used objective polysomnography.
- No meta-analysis / pooled effect estimate; no formal risk-of-bias assessment or PRISMA methodology detailed in the retrieved text.
- Very few longitudinal or intervention studies in the corpus, limiting causal inference (reciprocal vs. directional relationship remains unresolved).
- Authors call for further studies to determine causality and to trial specific interventions (e.g., CPAP for OSA, CBT-I, nightguards) for their effect on TMD outcomes.

## 6. Related Work

- Cites a related systematic review on painful TMD and sleep quality (Dreweck et al., referenced in-text) that confirmed a robust association between painful TMD and poor sleep quality in adults.
- Cites a systematic review (Sánchez Romero et al., referenced in-text) reporting insufficient evidence linking TMJ osteoarthritis specifically with increased sleep disorders — a condition-specific exception to the general pattern.
- Positioned alongside [[jiang-2024-orofacial-pain-sleep-biobank]] (genetic/causal evidence, UK Biobank + Mendelian randomization) as complementary evidence tiers (observational/clinical vs. genetic-causal) for the same sleep–orofacial pain/TMD relationship.

## 7. Glossary

- **TMD (Temporomandibular Disorders)**: 측두하악관절장애 — cluster of musculoskeletal/neuromuscular conditions affecting the TMJ, masticatory muscles, and associated structures.
- **OSA (Obstructive Sleep Apnea)**: 폐쇄성 수면무호흡증 — repeated upper-airway obstruction during sleep causing hypoxia and sleep fragmentation.
- **Sleep bruxism**: 수면 중 이갈이/이악물기 — repetitive jaw-muscle activity during sleep, implicated in TMJ loading and degeneration.
- **PSQI (Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index)**: 피츠버그 수면의 질 지수 — validated self-report questionnaire for sleep quality screening.
- **STOP-Bang**: 수면무호흡 선별 설문도구 — screening questionnaire for obstructive sleep apnea risk.
- **Polysomnography (PSG)**: 수면다원검사 — objective, multi-channel physiological recording during sleep (EEG, respiratory events, etc.).
- **OPPERA (Orofacial Pain: Prospective Evaluation and Risk Assessment)**: 구강안면통증 전향적 평가·위험도 연구 — large US prospective cohort study on TMD risk factors.
- **CBT-I (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia)**: 불면증 인지행동치료.
- **Central sensitization**: 중추 감작 — amplified CNS pain processing contributing to chronic pain states.
