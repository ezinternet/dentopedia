## Why Ingested

Adds the elderly (older than 65 years) population to the implant survival cluster: a large retrospective series (902 implants, 346 patients, 2-17 years) concluding that age alone does not appear to affect survival. Complements the registry-style long-term retrospective of [[implants/survival/song-2024-long-term-clinical-radiographic-outcomes]] (same Korean research group) and the classic multi-factor risk analysis in [[implants/survival/moy-2005-dental-implant-failure-rates-risk]]. → [[implants/survival/park-2016-long-term-outcomes-of-dental]]

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective clinical and radiographic study of 902 implants in 346 patients aged 65-89 years, followed 2-17 years; survival and marginal bone level analysed in serial radiographs, with Cox regression for implant loss.

Survival was 95.39% (implant-based) and 99.98% (patient-based) with 29 failures; marginal bone loss 0.17 ± 0.71 mm (mean ± SD); failures were most numerous at age 65-69 years, and implant loss was significantly greater at 65-69 than 70-74 years (P < 0.05) and varied by implant system.

Implant therapy can be successfully provided to elderly patients and age alone does not seem to affect survival; the data are retrospective and only the abstract was available, so subgroup and covariate details are not verifiable.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. Large elderly-only implant cohort (older than 65 years) with long follow-up (up to 17 years).
2. Reports both implant-based (95.39%) and patient-based (99.98%) survival.
3. Cox regression with shared frailty used for implant loss, accounting for clustering of implants within patients.

## 3. Methodology

Retrospective analysis of implants placed in patients older than 65 years. Survival was recorded and analysed; marginal bone level changes were measured on serial radiographs; Cox regression with shared frailty analysis was performed for implant loss. (Further methods not available in the abstract.)

## 4. Key Results

- Survival rate: 95.39% (implant-based) and 99.98% (patient-based); 29 implant failures in total.
- Marginal bone loss: 0.17 ± 0.71 mm (mean ± SD).
- Failures were most numerous in patients aged 65-69 years.
- Cox regression with shared frailty: implant loss significantly greater in the 65-69 group than the 70-74 group (P < 0.05), and varied between specific implant systems.
- Authors' conclusion: implant therapy can be successfully provided to elderly patients; age alone does not seem to affect survival.

## 5. Limitations

- Retrospective design, as the authors acknowledge ("within the limitations of this retrospective study").
- Only the abstract was retrieved; covariates, definitions of failure, and the other age-group estimates are not verifiable.
- Variation between implant systems suggests system is a confounder of any age comparison.

## 7. Clinical Implications

Advanced age by itself need not be treated as a contraindication to implant therapy on this evidence; patient selection and implant system choice deserve attention.
