## Why Ingested
Long-term single-center ICG outcome dataset (n=173, up to 23 years) that directly underpins the ICG survival figures reported in [[wiki/bone-regeneration/mckenna-2022-autogenous-bone-graft-site-implant-survival]], which showed ICG survival declining at 24+ months (85.9%) versus intraoral grafts — Sethi's Kaplan-Meier data provides the granular trajectory (>92.3% at 200 months, 82% at 250 months) alongside unusually detailed donor-site morbidity reporting.

## Three-line Summary
Retrospective single-center analysis of 173 consecutive patients who received 190 anterior iliac crest onlay grafts for alveolar reconstruction (1992–2017), with 869 implants placed 3 months post-grafting and follow-up up to 274.8 months (mean 109 months).
Overall implant survival rate was 95% ± 2.7% (Kaplan-Meier); survival by deficiency class: IV 95% ± 9.7%, V 96% ± 7.4%, VI 98.7% ± 2.5%; 2/190 grafts (1%) failed before implant placement; seroma was the only donor-site complication (11/173 patients).
Iliac crest onlay grafts remain viable for large jaw defects where intraoral sources are insufficient, with acceptable long-term implant survival despite high loss to follow-up (31.8%).

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: International Journal of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery 2020;49:264–271
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijom.2019.07.001
- **Institution**: Centre for Implant and Reconstructive Dentistry, London, UK

## 2. Key Contributions
- Largest single-center ICG retrospective with up to 23-year follow-up (mean 109 months)
- Kaplan-Meier estimates: >92.3% survival at 200 months; 82% at 250 months (22 implants remaining)
- Donor-site morbidity was minimal: seroma in 11/173 patients; all returned to normal function
- Only 2/190 grafts (1%) failed before implant placement (both successfully retreated)
- Class VI deficiency showed highest survival (98.7%), Class IV lowest (95%)
- 31 patients had concurrent sinus augmentation; 8 had nerve lateralization

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Retrospective cohort, single center
- **Period**: 1992–2017 (25 years of consecutive patients)
- **n**: 173 patients, 190 onlay grafts (167 maxilla, 23 mandible), 869 implants
- **Inclusion**: Defects too large for intraoral bone alone; partially dentate and edentulous
- **Exclusion**: Radiotherapy or chemotherapy history
- **Donor site**: Anterior iliac crest (lateral aspect); harvested with surgical stents
- **Implant placement**: 3 months post-grafting; multiple systems (Osteo-Ti, Ankylos, Xive)
- **Outcome**: Kaplan-Meier implant survival; graft success; donor-site complications
- **Success criterion**: Smith and Zarb (≤1.5 mm bone loss year 1, ≤0.2 mm/year thereafter)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Overall implant survival**: 95% ± 2.7% (Kaplan-Meier)
- **Survival at 200 months**: >92.3% (95% CI)
- **Survival at 250 months**: 82% (22 implants under observation)
- **By deficiency class**: IV 95% ± 9.7% | V 96% ± 7.4% | VI 98.7% ± 2.5%
- **Implants lost**: 15/869 total (2 at exposure, 1 pre-exposure infection, 12 late in function)
- **Late failures**: peri-implantitis in 2 patients
- **Graft failures**: 2/190 (1%) — both retreated successfully
- **Soft tissue breakdown**: 13 grafts (11 healed with limited volume loss; 2 lost to infection)
- **Donor complications**: seroma 11/173; all discharged next day (except 5, day+1)
- **Lost to follow-up**: 55/173 patients (31.8%)
- **Mean observation**: 109 months (median 107.23 months)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- High loss to follow-up (31.8%); 19 monitored by referring dentists, 18 uncontactable >3 years
- Retrospective design; single center; no control group
- Multiple implant systems used across 25-year period (heterogeneous prosthetics)
- No pre- and post-graft bone volume measurements (no 3D volumetric assessment)
- Selection bias: patients requiring extraoral graft represent worst-case bone deficiency

## 7. Glossary
- **ICG**: Iliac Crest Graft (장골능 골이식) — autogenous bone from the anterior iliac crest for large jaw defects
- **Onlay graft**: (피개 골이식) — block bone graft placed on top of the residual ridge to augment deficient alveolar bone
- **Cawood-Howell classification**: (Cawood-Howell 분류) — six-class classification of jaw atrophy after tooth loss; Class IV–VI = severe deficiency requiring extraoral grafting
- **Seroma**: (혈청종) — accumulation of serous fluid at the surgical site; most common ICG donor-site complication
- **Smith-Zarb criterion**: (Smith-Zarb 기준) — implant success criterion: ≤1.5 mm bone loss in year 1, ≤0.2 mm/year thereafter
