## Why Ingested

The existing `orthodontics/clear-aligner/` collection (26 papers) is dominated by biomechanics, efficacy-vs-fixed, and adverse-effect systematic reviews — none directly assess what patients *want* and *expect* before starting clear aligner therapy (CAT). This prospective cohort study fills that patient-perspective gap with a structured, prospectively-collected pre-treatment questionnaire (n=82), complementing the treatment-outcome focus of [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/yassir-2022-cat-vs-fat-overview-systematic-reviews]] with data on patient psychology, motivation, and willingness-to-compromise that shapes informed consent and case selection conversations.

## One-line Summary

Prospective cohort study (n=82) using a 32-item VAS questionnaire found that patients seeking clear aligner therapy have very high, largely inflexible expectations regarding aesthetics, treatment predictability, and outcome durability, and are unwilling to compromise on outcome even for reduced cost or treatment time.

## 한줄요약

전향적 코호트 연구(n=82)로 32문항 VAS 설문을 사용해 분석한 결과, 투명교정장치(clear aligner therapy, CAT) 희망 환자들은 심미성·치료 예측가능성·결과 지속성에 대해 매우 높고 타협 불가능한 기대를 가지고 있었으며, 비용이나 치료기간 단축을 위해 치료 결과의 질을 양보할 의사는 거의 없었다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First part of a prospective cohort study (baseline / pre-treatment data) systematically assessing patients' needs, expectations, and concerns regarding CAT (Invisalign) using a prospectively distributed, structured questionnaire — as opposed to prior literature that relied on retrospective chart review, social media post analysis, or samples of laypeople/general dental patients not specifically seeking orthodontic treatment.
- Organizes 32 questionnaire statements into five factor-analysis-derived categories: (1) Limitations, (2) Second Opinion, (3) Efficiency and Predictability, (4) Willingness to Compromise, (5) Quality of Treatment.
- Links patient expectations to patient-specific factors: gender, age, initial malocclusion severity (PAR index), and OHRQoL (OHIP-14 simple count score).
- Demonstrates that patients seeking CAT hold high, rigid expectations on aesthetics, predictability, and outcome longevity, and are essentially unwilling to trade outcome quality for shorter treatment time or lower cost.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Prospective cohort study; this manuscript reports the pre-treatment ("prior to CAT initiation") baseline data as the first part of a larger prospective series (during- and post-treatment data to follow in later publications).
- **Setting/recruitment**: Patients scheduled for CAT (Invisalign "Comprehensive Package" or "Lite Package") at a private orthodontic practice in Vienna or the University Dental Clinic of Vienna, recruited September 2019–July 2022.
- **Exclusions**: Prior Invisalign "i7" or "Express Package" treatment, prior completed aligner treatment, age < 18 years, or planned combination therapy with fixed appliances.
- **Sample size**: Calculated at 66 based on OHIP standard-error targets (0.25); inflated to 84 to allow for ~20% dropout, distributed across 2 centers. Final analyzed sample: 82.
- **Instrument**: Non-validated 32-statement questionnaire developed by a group of experienced orthodontists, rated on a 50 mm visual analogue scale (VAS), converted to a 0–10 point scale. Agreement classified as "mild" (<3), "moderate" (3–7), "strong" (>7).
- **Factor structure** (5 categories, item counts, Cronbach's α): Limitations (n=9, α not explicitly stated separately in text but reported as 0.72 in Table 2), Second Opinion (n=5, α=0.78), Efficiency and Predictability (n=6, α=0.64), Willingness to Compromise (n=4, α=0.73), Quality of Treatment (n=8, α=0.38 — below the 0.7 acceptability cutoff).
- **Secondary instrument**: German OHIP-14 (Oral Health Impact Profile) to assess OHRQoL, 5-point Likert scale; OHIP simple count score computed per El Osta et al. method (max 14 points).
- **Other variables collected**: age, gender, educational degree, initial PAR (Peer Assessment Rating) index (malocclusion severity) from clinical records.
- **Statistics**: R version 4.4.3; descriptive statistics (means, SD); exploratory two-sample tests (no correction for multiple testing) comparing subgroups by gender, age (≤35 vs >35 years), PAR index (≤17 vs >17), and OHIP simple count (<1 vs ≥1). Factor analyses with multiple rotation types were attempted but deemed too heterogeneous for formal use; used only to roughly group items into the 5 categories.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Study population (n=82)**: 52.7% university clinic, 46.3% private practice; mean age 35 ± 12 years; 74.4% female; 48.8% higher educational qualification; mean PAR index 19 ± 10 (51.2% ≤17, 46.4% >17); mean OHIP score 5.9 ± 5.8 (43.9% simple count <1, 56.1% ≥1).

**Category 1 — Limitations**: Highest-rated concerns were avoiding visibility of orthodontic brackets (7.9 ± 2.8), pain during treatment (7.7 ± 2.7), and restricted eating/drinking (7.3 ± 2.8). Lower concerns: being perceived by others as wearing aligners (5.8 ± 3.2), attachment-related restrictions (4.1 ± 3.1), impaired oral hygiene (3.1 ± 2.9), and impact on self-esteem (2.3 ± 2.5, lowest-rated item).

**Category 2 — Second Opinion**: Strong agreement (7.2 ± 3.3) to seek a second opinion if told CAT were not possible. Moderate agreement to seek a second opinion if CAT required tooth extraction (6.7 ± 3.6), orthodontic brackets (6.1 ± 3.7), or interproximal enamel reduction (5.2 ± 3.6).

**Category 3 — Efficiency and Predictability**: Very high importance placed on preventing future dental/jaw problems (9.1 ± 1.5) and predictability of treatment success (8.6 ± 1.9); also high: time efficiency (7.7 ± 2.4), seeing a treatment-outcome simulation beforehand (7.5 ± 2.9), and cost-effectiveness (7.1 ± 2.7).

**Category 4 — Willingness to Compromise**: Uniformly low scores, indicating unwillingness to compromise: would not reduce treatment time (2.1 ± 2.3) or cost (2.4 ± 2.5) for a slightly inferior outcome; would not forgo elastics (2.6 ± 2.8) or attachments (2.4 ± 2.7) even if these limited tooth-movement possibilities.

**Category 5 — Quality of Treatment**: Very strong expectation that CAT would treat malocclusion at least as effectively as brackets (9.5 ± 0.9); strong expectation the result would last ≥5 years (8.7 ± 2.9) or a lifetime (8.8 ± 2.1); moderate agreement CAT would outperform brackets (5.8 ± 3.2) and that the dentist should be present at every appointment (7.0 ± 3.0); minimal expectation of effect on job opportunities (2.4 ± 2.5).

**Subgroup differences (exploratory, uncorrected for multiple testing)**:
- Female vs male: females rated predictability (8.9 ± 1.6 vs 7.7 ± 2.4, p=0.037) and time efficiency (8.1 ± 2.3 vs 6.8 ± 2.2, p=0.036) higher; males more likely to decline elastics (3.7 ± 2.9 vs 2.2 ± 2.7, p=0.034); females agreed more that CAT would outperform brackets (6.2 ± 3.4 vs 4.5 ± 2.6, p=0.022).
- Age >35 vs ≤35: older patients valued time efficiency more (8.5 ± 1.8 vs 7.1 ± 2.5, p=0.007) and were more likely to want to avoid attachments in the aesthetic zone (3.1 ± 3.0 vs 1.9 ± 2.2, p=0.041); younger patients more accepting of prolonged treatment time to achieve planned outcome (7.5 ± 2.3 vs 5.8 ± 3.5, p=0.015).
- PAR index >17 vs ≤17 (more severe malocclusion): greater worry about eating/drinking restrictions (8.5 ± 1.6 vs 6.6 ± 3.2, p=0.002) and pain (8.8 ± 1.6 vs 7.0 ± 3.0, p=0.001).
- OHIP simple count ≥1 vs <1: higher expectation of gaining confidence after treatment (7.2 ± 3.2 vs 5.5 ± 3.6, p=0.041); OHIP<1 group more likely to expect CAT to shorten treatment duration vs brackets (5.5 ± 3.1 vs 3.7 ± 3.0, p=0.016).

**Comparative context cited in Discussion**: prior social-media-based studies found 90% of patients initially expressed positive expectations toward CAT, yet 54.8% reported later dissatisfaction (aesthetics, discomfort, dietary restrictions, speech difficulties); 62% of patients in another prior survey rejected treatment with visible appliances; aesthetic motivation for CAT reported as high as 97% in prior literature vs ~70% for orthodontic treatment generally.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- The core 32-item questionnaire is **non-validated** — developed ad hoc by a group of experienced orthodontists and not previously used or psychometrically validated; not intended for scoring/index use.
- Category 5 (Quality of Treatment) Cronbach's α = 0.38, well below the 0.7 acceptability threshold, and Category 3 (Efficiency/Predictability) α = 0.64 also under threshold — internal consistency for these two categories is weak.
- Subgroup analyses are exploratory with **no correction for multiple testing**; findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating.
- Sample skewed female (74.4%) and adult (18–63 years, no adolescents) — findings may not generalize to adolescent CAT populations, though the authors note this demographic mirrors prior CAT literature (68–78% female, mean age 30–35).
- **No control group** of patients choosing fixed appliances — cannot directly compare expectation profiles between CAT and fixed-appliance patients.
- This is explicitly the **first part** of a larger prospective series; during-treatment and post-treatment expectation/experience data are planned as future publications from the same cohort.
- Single manufacturer/system studied (Invisalign only), single country (Austria) — generalizability to other aligner systems/healthcare contexts unconfirmed.

## 6. Related Work

- Cited prior work on CAT patient expectations via social media analysis reporting high initial positive expectations (90%) followed by substantial later dissatisfaction (54.8%).
- Cited retrospective study relying on expectations documented in dental records rather than structured prospective assessment.
- Cited studies surveying general dental patients/laypeople not specifically pursuing orthodontic treatment, and data collected >20 years ago — the gaps this study explicitly aims to close.
- References German-validated OHIP-14 and El Osta et al. simple-count scoring method for OHRQoL.
- References PAR (Peer Assessment Rating) index methodology for malocclusion severity scoring.

## 7. Glossary

- **CAT**: Clear Aligner Therapy — removable, sequential clear plastic orthodontic aligners (e.g., Invisalign).
- **OHRQoL**: Oral Health-Related Quality of Life.
- **OHIP-14**: Oral Health Impact Profile, 14-item validated questionnaire assessing OHRQoL; scored here via a simple-count method (0–14).
- **PAR index**: Peer Assessment Rating — standardized numerical measure of malocclusion severity, also used to evaluate orthodontic treatment outcomes.
- **VAS**: Visual Analogue Scale — here a 50 mm scale converted to a 0–10 point score for patient agreement ratings.
- **Cronbach's α**: Statistical measure of internal consistency/reliability for a set of questionnaire items; conventionally ≥0.7 considered acceptable.
