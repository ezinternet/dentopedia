## Why Ingested
This systematic review directly challenges the widely cited 150 µm "gold standard" micromotion limit by compiling all quantitative in vivo evidence. It reinforces [[implants/isq/brizuela-velasco-2015-insertion-torque-isq-micromobility]] and [[implants/isq/javed-2013-primary-stability-osseointegration-factors-influence]] by showing micromotion thresholds are context-dependent, not universal.

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review of 25 in vivo studies (1 human, 24 animal) examining the relationship between micromotion magnitude and osseointegration outcome, registered on PROSPERO (CRD42020196686).
Mean micromotion for osseointegrated implants was 112±176 µm vs 349±231 µm for non-osseointegrated (p<0.001), but ranges overlapped extensively (15–750 µm vs 30–750 µm) with no universal threshold identifiable.
Clinical implication: the 150 µm limit is misleading; implant factors (HA coating, thread geometry, pore shape) and environmental factors (loading frequency, rest periods, study duration ≥9 weeks) jointly determine tolerable micromotion.

## 세줄요약
25편의 생체 내 연구(인간 1, 동물 24)를 체계적으로 검토하여 마이크로모션 크기와 골유착 결과의 관계를 분석함 (PROSPERO CRD42020196686).
골유착된 임플란트의 평균 마이크로모션은 112±176 µm, 비골유착은 349±231 µm로 유의한 차이(p<0.001)이나, 범위가 15–750 µm vs 30–750 µm로 광범위하게 겹쳐 보편적 임계값은 없음.
임상적 함의: 150 µm '금지준'은 오해이며, HA 코팅·나사설계·공극형상(임플란트 요인)과 부하빈도·휴지기간·관찰기간≥9주(환경 요인)가 복합적으로 허용 마이크로모션을 결정함.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First comprehensive compilation of all quantitative in vivo micromotion-osseointegration data, refuting a universal 150 µm limit
- Identified overlapping micromotion ranges for success/failure (15–750 µm), demonstrating context-dependency
- Provided summary tables (Table 1, Table 2) mapping implant/environmental conditions to observed micromotion values for benchmarking new biomaterials

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review (PRISMA), PROSPERO registered (CRD42020196686)
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science (search up to 16 Nov 2020)
- **n**: 25 studies included (284 records → 72 full-text → 25 eligible)
- **Outcomes**: Micromotion (µm) for osseointegrated vs non-osseointegrated; %BIC; bone ingrowth; correlation with observation time

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Osseointegrated: 112±176 µm (n=28 samples); Non-osseointegrated: 349±231 µm (n=23); Mann-Whitney p<0.001
- Overlap: OI range 15–750 µm, Non-OI range 30–750 µm — no distinct threshold
- Applied micromotion: OI lower than Non-OI (p=0.001); Measured micromotion: OI lower than Non-OI (p=0.01)
- %BIC vs micromotion: positive correlation for OI group (Spearman ρ=0.41, p=0.02); no correlation for Non-OI (p=0.39) or full dataset (p=0.07)
- Observation time vs %BIC: positive correlation (Spearman ρ=0.40, p=0.01)
- Factors permitting higher micromotion with success: HA coating, larger threads in low-density bone, square pore cross-section, infrequent loading, rest period after initial loading, duration ≥9 weeks

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Majority of studies used "applied micromotion" as controlled condition (no mean/SD available), precluding formal meta-analysis per Borenstein et al.
- No standard interpretation of BIC across studies (mineralized bone contact vs total bone contact length)
- In vivo data coupled with FEA suggests interfacial stress-strain state, not micromotion per se, drives osseointegration — future work should integrate mechanobiology

## 6. Related Work
- Overgaard 1996 / Søballe 1992: HA coating tolerates 150 µm with rest period
- Goodman 1993: 750 µm once daily → success; twice daily → failure (loading frequency matters)
- Trisi 2015/2016: thread geometry (large vs small) and osseodensification alter micromotion/BIC relationship
- Engh 1992: human post-mortem hip stems <40 µm (OI) vs 150 µm (failed)

## 7. Glossary
- **Micromotion**: Temporary localized relative movement between implant surface and adjacent bone under functional loading (sub-millimetre)
- **Osseointegration**: Direct structural/functional connection between living bone and load-bearing implant surface
- **BIC (Bone-Implant Contact)**: Percentage of implant surface in contact with bone (definitions vary across studies)
- **Subsidence/Migration**: Permanent displacement of implant (distinct from reversible micromotion)
- **Interfacial stress–strain state**: Local mechanical environment at bone-implant interface driving tissue differentiation
