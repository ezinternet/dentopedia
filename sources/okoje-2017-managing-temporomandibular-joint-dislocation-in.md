## Why Ingested

전체 TMJ 탈구 (temporomandibular joint dislocation, TMJ dislocation) 문헌이 핀란드·유럽 코호트에 집중되어 있어, 이 논문의 가치는 **저자원(resource-limited) 환경의 삼차의료 (tertiary care) 맥락** 자체에 있음 — UCH Ibadan 응급실·치과센터 10년치 11예에서 히포크라테스 기법 (Hippocrates manoeuvre)이 실패·상승 (escalation) 경로까지 포함해 실제 처치 사슬 전체를 문서화함. [[tmj/stolbizer-2020-anterior-dislocation-of-the-temporomandibular]]는 급성 전방성 탈구의 단일 수동복원 (manual reduction) 기법만 다루고 만성·재발 (recurrence)·개방수술 (open reduction) 단계가 없는데, 이 논문은 그 상위 사슬을 채워 주며, 같은 코호트 규모 대조군으로는 [[tmj/tarhio-2023-causes-and-treatment-of-temporomandibular]](n=260, 핀란드)의 정량적 대비점을 준다. 또한 [[tmj/falletti-2026-update-trismus-etiology-diagnosis-treatment]]의 구강개긴 (trismus) 알고리즘이 다루지 않는 반대 축 — 입이 닫히지 않는 급성 잠김 — 의 원인 (aetiology) 분포를 아프리카 지역 코호트로 보강한다.

## Three-line Summary

Uncontrolled retrospective case series (n=11) from the emergency department and dental centre of University College Hospital Ibadan, Nigeria, over 10 years (2004–2013), describing the presentation, aetiology, management and outcome of TMJ dislocation; diagnosis rested on history, clinical examination and radiographic findings.

Mean age 44.4 ± 15.9 years (range 25–65, modal age 25; 7 female / 4 male); aetiology wide mouth opening 6 (54.6%), trauma 4 (36.4%), unknown 1 (11.0%); 7 acute, 2 recurrent, 2 chronic; 10 bilateral anterior and 1 unilateral right anterior, with no posterior, medial, lateral or superior types; 6/11 presented within 24 hours. Hippocratic bimanual reduction alone was applied in 5/11 (reported as 45%), with IMF + sedation in 2, Hippocratic manoeuvre under GA in 1 and spontaneous reduction in 2; one patient failed closed reduction by every route (Hippocratic awake, Hippocratic under GA, class III maxillomandibular traction wires for 5 days) and required open reduction under GA, and 7/11 (63.6%) were lost to follow-up before 2 weeks (range 1 day to 9 months).

Clinical take: conservative manual reduction is genuinely first-line for anterior dislocation even where follow-up and staffing are thin, but n=11, a single 45% figure for the core manoeuvre, no comparator and 63.6% attrition make this context-setting service data, not effectiveness evidence — and the follow-up attrition is itself the finding with the widest reach.

## 세줄요약

무대조 후향 환자 연쇄 (uncontrolled retrospective case series, n=11) — 2004–2013년 10년간 나이지리아 University College Hospital 응급실·치과센터에서 진료받은 악관절 탈구 (temporomandibular joint dislocation, TMJ dislocation) 환자의 발생 (presentation)·원인 (aetiology)·치료·결과를 병기 검토로 기술함. 진단은 병력·임상검사·방사선 소견에 근거함.

평균 연령 44.4 ± 15.9세(범위 25–65세, 최빈값 25세), 여성 7명·남성 4명. 원인은 과도한 입 벌림 (wide mouth opening) 6례(54.6%), 외상 (trauma) 4례(36.4%), 불명 1례(11.0%). 급성 (acute) 7례, 재발성 (recurrent) 2례, 만성 (chronic) 2례였고 전방성 양측성 (bilateral anterior) 10례·전방성 우측 편측성 1례로 후방·내측·외측·상방 탈구는 없었다. 6/11례가 24시간 이내 내원했다. 히포크라테스 기법 (Hippocratic manoeuvre) 단독 5/11례(45%), 악간고정술 (intermaxillary fixation, IMF) + 진정 2례, 전신마취 (general anaesthesia, GA) 하 기법 1례, 자연복원 (spontaneous reduction) 2례였으며, 1례는 의식 하 기법·GA 하 기법·Class III 악간철사 견인 5일 모두 실패 후 개방수술 (open reduction) 로 치료되었다. 7/11례(63.6%)가 2주 이내 추적에서 이탈했다(범위 1일–9개월).

임상적 시사: 전방성 탈구에서 보존적 수동복원 (manual reduction)이 추적 여건이 열악한 환경에서도 1차 선택지임을 뒷받침한다. 그러나 n=11, 핵심 기법의 단일 45% 수치, 대조군 부재, 63.6% 이탈률로 인해 효과 근거가 아니라 진료 환경 서술 데이터이며, 추적 이탈 자체가 가장 일반화 가능한 결론이다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Low-resource-setting (Nigerian tertiary) baseline for the full **escalation ladder** of anterior TMJ dislocation management in one series: bimanual manual reduction → sedation + IMF → reduction under GA → extra-oral technique → open reduction, plus eminectomy for recurrent disease.
- Documents a **Hippocratic-manoeuvre failure rescued by the extra-oral technique** (Case 7) — the only case in the series where a named alternative manual technique succeeded after repeated conventional attempts.
- Documents a **complete closed-reduction failure** (Case 8: Hippocratic awake, then under GA, then 5 days of class III maxillomandibular traction wires) requiring open reduction, with post-operative jaw opening limited and recovered with acrylic-screw jaw exercises over 6 weeks.
- Reports the **follow-up attrition of a TMJ dislocation population in a low-resource health system**: 7/11 (63.6%) lost before 2 weeks, and states that those followed beyond 2 weeks were precisely the recurrent and chronic cases.
- Attaches a **drug-induced mechanism** to the one idiopathic case — a patient on thioridazine (Mellenil) + diazepam with a background psychosocial disorder — and a **neurogenic/drug mechanism** to a 28-year epileptic on phenytoin + phenobarbitone whose seventh episode followed a fall during a fit.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: retrospective case review / uncontrolled retrospective case series (data from patient case files)
- **Setting**: Accidents and Emergency unit + Dental clinic, University College Hospital Ibadan; 2004–2013 (10 years)
- **n**: 11 patients (4 males, 7 females)
- **Variables retrieved**: demographics, aetiology, duration of dislocation, number of episodes, underlying illness, drug history, radiographic findings, treatment of previous and/or current episodes, outcome including complications
- **Diagnostic criteria**: history + clinical examination substantiated by radiographic findings
- **Analysis**: descriptive variables via Chi-square test; four tables (aetiology × clinical grouping, systemic disease, management, follow-up period)
- **Outcomes**: implicit — reduction success by method, need for escalation, recurrence after reduction, follow-up duration, complications
- **Case narratives**: 8 of the 11 patients are described individually (Cases 1–8); 3 cases are represented in the tables only
- **Comparator literature cited**: Lowery 2004 (37 dislocations / 7 yr, ~100,000 annual ED visits, USA), Sang 2010 (29 patients / 10 yr, Nairobi), Agbara 2014 (26 patients / 7 yr, Zaria Nigeria), Ugboko 2005 (96 Nigerian cases)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Demographics** — mean age 44.4 ± 15.9 years SD, range 25–65 years, modal age 25; 4 males, 7 females; 75% of males (3/4) presented below the mean age vs 28.6% of females (2/7).

**Aetiology × clinical grouping (Table 1)**

| Clinical grouping | Trauma | Wide mouth opening | Unknown | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acute | 4 (36.4) | 2 (18.2) | 1 (11.0) | 7 (63.6) |
| Recurrent | 0 (0.0) | 2 (18.2) | 0 (0.0) | 2 (18.2) |
| Chronic | 0 (0.0) | 2 (18.2) | 0 (0.0) | 2 (18.2) |
| **Total** | **4 (36.4)** | **6 (54.6)** | **1 (11.0)** | **11 (100.0)** |

**Systemic disease (Table 2)** — none 6 (54.6%), hypertension 2 (18.2%), psychiatric 1 (11.0%, thioridazine/Mellenil + diazepam), HIV infection 1 (11.0%, diagnosed but not yet started on ART), peptic ulcer disease 1 (11.0%).

**Anatomy** — 10 bilateral anterior, 1 unilateral right anterior; **no** superior, lateral, medial or posterior dislocation was seen.

**Management (Table 3)**

| Management | Trauma | Wide mouth opening | Unknown | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hippocrates's manoeuvre | 2 | 2 | 1 | 5 |
| Hippocrates + IMF + sedation | 0 | 2 | 0 | 2 |
| Hippocrates + GA | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| Spontaneous reduction | 1 | 1 | 0 | 2 |
| Unsuccessful | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| **Total** | **4** | **6** | **1** | **11** |

The Discussion states the Hippocratic manoeuvre "was successful in 45% of the cases owing to the acute nature of presentation" — i.e. 5/11, matching the manoeuvre-alone row. The Abstract breaks the same column differently: of the acute cases, 4 were managed with the Hippocratic manoeuvre, 1 with the manoeuvre under GA and 2 by spontaneous reduction; both recurrent cases were managed with the manoeuvre plus IMF; and "fifty percent of the chronic cases were successfully managed with the Hippocrates manoeuvre."

**Follow-up period (Table 4)**

| Follow-up group | Trauma | Wide mouth opening | Unknown | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 14 days | 2 | 5 | 0 | 7 |
| > 14 days | 2 | 1 | 1 | 4 |
| **Total** | **4** | **6** | **1** | **11** |

Follow-up ranged from 1 day to 9 months; the Discussion states that patients followed beyond 2 weeks "were those with recurrences or chronic dislocations."

**Presentation timing** — 6 of 11 presented within 24 hours of occurrence; the remainder were 2 recurrent and 2 chronic (>14 days) presentations.

**Case-level detail (Cases 1–8 of 11)**

- **Case 1** — 45 M shoemaker, 1 week after a fall from a moving bus with ~10 min loss of consciousness; prior tuberculosis 15 years earlier. Bilateral anterior dislocation; Hippocratic manoeuvre successful only after **4 attempts** + analgesia. Returned **2 days later with a recurrent dislocation** after yawning → Hippocratic manoeuvre modified with diazepam + barrel bandage; clear at 2 days.
- **Case 2** — 65 F trader, 1 year of right TMJ clicking on mastication, hypertensive on medication. Chronic recurrent **right** TMJ dislocation on opening radiograph. **Intra-articular triamcinolone 40 mg weekly × 6 weeks + physiotherapy**; symptoms improved, then did not return.
- **Case 3** — 25 F trader, 1 day after yawning, preauricular pain, no comorbidity. Anterior displacement beyond the articular eminence. Hippocratic manoeuvre + barrel bandage; full TMJ movement restored at 2 days.
- **Case 4** — 28 F student, 1 week after tooth brushing; known RVD awaiting HAART. Anterior open bite, posterior gagging of occlusion, oral thrush. Hippocratic manoeuvre under **diazepam** + paracetamol, successful at the **2nd attempt**.
- **Case 5** — 40 F designer, 1 day after a fall during an epileptic fit; **7th episode, first 13 years earlier**; epileptic 28 years on Epanutin (phenytoin) + phenobarbitone; enamel fracture of 11. Bilateral anterior; Hippocratic manoeuvre successful, no complication recorded.
- **Case 6** — 26 M student, ~1 day after yawning, **~10 previous episodes** all reduced with the Hippocratic manoeuvre; radiograph showed **short mandibular condyles**. Hippocratic reduction + MMF for 2 weeks → **immediate recurrence**; definitive **bilateral eminectomy** (articular eminences ground with round/acrylic burs under GA). Complete resolution of symptoms, restoration of TMJ function and resolution of associated facial nerve weakness at **5 months** post-operatively.
- **Case 7** — 60 F housewife, 3 hours after yawning, similar incident 4 months earlier, hypertensive on moduretic. Failed the Hippocratic manoeuvre, then reduced successfully with the **extra-oral technique** (steady pressure over the palpated coronoid process / anterior border of the ramus) + barrel bandage.
- **Case 8** — 48 F teacher, **5 days after an armed robbery** in which she was stifled to prevent shouting; prior attempts at a private clinic and a Lagos teaching hospital had failed. Hippocratic manoeuvre awake (several failures) → under GA (2 attempts, failed) → class III maxillomandibular wires for gradual traction with daily adjustment (no effect, abandoned after 5 days) → **open reduction under GA, successful**. Outcome was **limited mouth opening**, managed with jaw exercises using acrylic screws, with reviews to 6 weeks showing gradual restoration of jaw movement.

**Complications recorded** — immediate recurrence after IMF in Case 6; reduced mouth opening after open reduction in Case 8; recurrence within 2 days in Case 1. No case was reported as lost to acute failure of pain control or as having a bite/infection injury, though the Discussion notes the operator "has to take the risk of being bitten and possible disease transmission" as a general drawback of the Hippocratic manoeuvre.

**Comparative context quoted by the authors** — Lowery et al. 37 dislocations over 7 years in a US ED with ~100,000 annual visits; Sang et al. 29 patients over 10 years in Nairobi; Agbara et al. 26 patients over 7 years in Zaria, Nigeria. The paper attributes the small n to this low incidence. It also notes the mean age here (44.4 y) is "about a decade higher than previous studies", suggesting wear-and-tear and joint laxity as predominant predisposing factors, and that trauma accounted for 36.4% (road traffic crashes 18.2%) — "a percentage much smaller than expected" given that road traffic crashes dominate maxillofacial injury in Nigeria.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **n=11 over 10 years** — too small for the Chi-square test the Methods claim to have run; no test statistic, p-value or confidence interval appears anywhere in the paper, so the analysis is effectively descriptive despite the stated inferential framing.
- **Three cases are not narrated** — Cases 1–8 are described individually but the series is n=11, so 3 patients exist only as table cells; no per-case data exist for them.
- **"45%" is an ambiguous denominator** — the Discussion's 45% equals 5/11 (manoeuvre alone), but the Abstract reports a different decomposition of the same patients (4 acute + 1 GA + 2 spontaneous). A reader cannot reconstruct which of the 11 patients belong to which table cell.
- **63.6% lost to follow-up before 2 weeks** (7/11), with only 4 patients followed >14 days — recurrence and long-term joint function are therefore effectively unmeasured; the authors themselves call this "the sickness behaviour of the population".
- **No comparator arm** — success rates cannot be attributed to the Hippocratic manoeuvre versus extra-oral technique versus eminectomy.
- **Table 3 percentages are internally inconsistent** — the "Hippocrates's maneuver" row lists per-aetiology counts of 2, 2, 1 (labelled 18.2/18.2/11.0%) which sum to 47.4%, while the total is given as bare "5". Counts are reproduced here verbatim; percentages are not recomputed.
- **Selection and era bias** — the study period ended in 2013, so the reduction techniques described predate the current literature on sedation-free variants, botulinum toxin, and arthroscopic eminoplasty; the authors' own suggestion that it reflects "the understudied centre" is a candid statement of generalisability limits.

## 6. Related Work

- [[tmj/stolbizer-2020-anterior-dislocation-of-the-temporomandibular]] — the closest comparator: a 42-patient single-centre series of a *simplified non-traumatic* manual reduction technique achieving 100% repositioning without any anaesthesia. Read together, the two bracket the technique question — Okoje reports the conventional bimanual Hippocratic pathway with a 5/11 (45%) manoeuvre-alone success rate, one total closed-reduction failure and an open-reduction endpoint, whereas Stolbizer reports an anaesthesia-free variant at 100% in 42/42. Both are uncontrolled single-arm series from a single dental/ED service; neither can establish superiority.
- [[tmj/tarhio-2023-causes-and-treatment-of-temporomandibular]] — the direct quantitative contrast at n=260 (Helsinki, Finland, 14 years) vs n=11 (Ibadan, Nigeria, 10 years). Tarhio's cohort is 61.9% recurrent, 40.0% spontaneous, manual reduction attempted in 91.9%, eminectomy in only 1.9% (n=5); Okoje's is 18.2% recurrent, 54.6% wide-mouth-opening aetiology, 45% manoeuvre-alone success, and one open reduction. The most striking divergence is the rate of surgical end-point care, plausibly a function of surgical capacity rather than disease severity.
- [[tmj/prechel-2018-the-treatment-of-temporomandibular-joint]] — a clinical practice guideline on TMJ luxation. Read as the reference standard against which Okoje's pathway can be graded: the series provides real-world outcome data for exactly the conservative-first strategy the guideline recommends, but at a level of evidence the guideline would not accept as input.
- [[tmj/abrahamsson-2020-treatment-of-temporomandibular-joint-luxation]] — a systematic review of TMJ luxation treatment; the synthesis-level counterweight to this single-centre case series, and the appropriate evidence frame for the 45% figure.
- [[tmj/lin-2026-rapid-reduction-of-temporomandibular-joint]] — a contemporary acceleration-based reduction technique; its refractory-after-multiple-Hippocratic-attempts subgroup is the exact population Okoje's Case 7 (extra-oral rescue) and Case 8 (open reduction) illustrate in an African setting.
- [[tmj/falletti-2026-update-trismus-etiology-diagnosis-treatment]] — the trismus literature's mirror image: Okoje's series supplies the aetiology and management distribution of the *opposite* mechanical failure mode, the acutely locked-open jaw that cannot be closed.

## 7. Glossary

- **Open lock / 완전 탈구 (complete dislocation, luxation)**: complete displacement of the mandibular condyles from their articulating surface in the glenoid fossa, not self-reducible by the patient — hence hospital presentation. Distinguished from **subluxation (아탈구)**, a transient displacement without complete loss of articulating function that usually self-reduces.
- **Hippocrates manoeuvre (히포크라테스 기법)**: bimanual closed reduction — operator thumbs over the patient's molar teeth, pushing the dislocated jaw downward and backward onto the condyle. Applicable in the ED or on the dental chair; demanding in effort, usually modified with sedation, and carries an operator-bite risk.
- **Extra-oral technique (외부 기법)**: reduction by steady external pressure over the palpable coronoid process and anterior border of the ramus, exploiting their accessibility in anterior dislocation (Chen et al.); requires no intra-oral thumb placement and so avoids the bite risk.
- **IMF / MMF (악간고정술, intermaxillary / maxillomandibular fixation)**: post-reduction immobilisation to support the joint, used here for recurrent dislocations and for gradual traction (class III wires) in the failed closed-reduction case.
- **Eminectomy (유돌절제술)**: surgical removal/grounding-down of the articular eminence to remove the mechanical obstacle that lets the condyle lock anterior to it — first reported by Myrhaug (1951), modified by Blankestijn to avoid violating the intracapsular space, and described by the authors as presently the most popular surgical intervention for chronic dislocation.
- **Trismus (구강개긴)**: the opposite pole to dislocation — limitation of *opening*. Distinguishing the two is the first diagnostic step, since the management pathways diverge completely.
- **Recurrent vs chronic persistent (재발성 vs 만성 지속성)**: recurrent dislocation = multiple episodes provoked by everyday activity (yawning, laughing, talking); chronic persistent = an untreated acute dislocation that does not self-limit.
