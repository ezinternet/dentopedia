## Why Ingested

Adds a Korean single-centre retrospective cohort of narrow-diameter implants (NDI, ≤3.5 mm) with Cox-modelled systemic risk factors (smoking, osteoporosis) and a Ti-Zr vs CpTi comparison. Reinforces the Ti-Zr narrow-implant evidence in → [[implants/short-narrow/cao-2023-titanium-zirconium-narrow-diameter-single-crown]], and supplies clinical-cohort support for smoking as a failure predictor alongside → [[implants/survival/fan-2024-smoking-early-implant-failure-sr-ma]].

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort of 274 narrow-diameter implants (≤3.5 mm) placed 2013–2018 at the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Kyung Hee University Dental Hospital (Korea), with ≥1 year follow-up after final prosthesis; survival by Kaplan-Meier, risk factors by Cox model, marginal bone loss (MBL) on periapical radiographs.

The 3-year cumulative survival rate was 92.4% (19 failures: 12 early osseointegration failures, 7 peri-implantitis); smoking (OR=4.758) and osteoporosis (OR=3.420) were significant risk factors, Ti-Zr alloy had 94.6% survival, mean MBL was 0.33±0.32 mm vertical and 0.18±0.17 mm horizontal, and posterior NDIs (n=108) had 91.7% 3-year survival.

NDIs performed stably with no fixture or abutment fracture, but smokers and osteoporotic patients need cautious selection; retrospective, single-centre, non-randomized design with many dropouts calls for prospective confirmation, particularly for molar replacement.

## 세줄요약

경희대학교치과병원 구강악안면외과에서 2013–2018년에 식립한 좁은직경 임플란트 (Narrow-Diameter Implant, NDI, ≤3.5 mm) 274개의 후향적 코호트 연구; 최종 보철 후 ≥1년 추적, Kaplan-Meier 생존분석, Cox 모델, 치근단 방사선 사진 기반 변연골 소실 (Marginal Bone Loss, MBL) 평가.

3년 누적 임플란트 생존율 (survival rate) 92.4% (실패 19개: 초기 골유착 실패 12개, 임플란트주위염 7개); 흡연 (OR=4.758)과 골다공증 (OR=3.420)이 유의한 위험인자, 티타늄-지르코늄 (Ti-Zr) 합금 생존율 94.6%, MBL 수직 0.33±0.32 mm·수평 0.18±0.17 mm, 구치부 NDI(108개) 3년 생존율 91.7%.

픽스처·어버트먼트 파절은 없어 NDI는 안정적이었으나 흡연자·골다공증 환자는 신중한 선택이 필요하며, 후향적 단일기관 비무작위 설계와 다수의 탈락 때문에 특히 대구치 단일 수복은 전향적 연구로 확인해야 한다.

## 1. Document Information
- Journal: Maxillofacial Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery (2023); PMID 37526800; PMCID PMC10393933
- Setting: Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Kyung Hee University Dental Hospital (Korea); IRB KH-DT20014
- Study type: retrospective cohort (chart and radiograph review)

## 2. Key Contributions
- Reports 3-year cumulative survival of 274 NDIs (92.4%) with failure causes split into early osseointegration failure and peri-implantitis.
- Identifies smoking and osteoporosis as significant risk factors in a Cox model; other systemic diseases, implant characteristics, prosthesis type and bone grafting showed no significant difference.
- Describes mechanical complications and a posterior-region (premolar/molar) subgroup of 108 NDIs.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Patients receiving implants Jan 2013–Dec 2018, follow-up data through 2020; NDI defined as diameter ≤3.5 mm (categories 2 and 3 in the paper's classification; no <3.0 mm mini-implants).
- Inclusion: ≥1 year follow-up from final prosthesis; patients with insufficient chart/radiograph data or <1 year follow-up were excluded. The text gives the number of NDIs (274) but no patient count.
- Failure criterion: loss of the fixture or conditions contrary to the success criteria (clinical immobility; no peri-implant radiolucency/bone loss >1.5 mm; no persistent pain, paresthesia, discomfort or infection).
- MBL: periapical digital radiographs (ZeTTA PACS) at placement and 1 year after loading, four sites (mesial/distal vertical and horizontal); ICC >0.9.
- Statistics: Kaplan-Meier with log-rank, Cox proportional hazard model, t/Welch/Kruskal-Wallis for MBL, chi-square for complications; SPSS 25; significance P<0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Cohort: 65.3% female, mean age 54 years; 60.6% anterior; length 8.0–13.0 mm (10.0 mm in 78.5%); Ti-Zr alloy and mSLA surface most common.
- 3-year cumulative survival 92.4%; 19 failures: 12 initially dislodged (failed osseointegration, within ≤1 year), 7 peri-implantitis.
- Cox model: smokers OR=4.758, osteoporosis OR=3.420 (P<0.05); non-smokers had higher survival than smokers.
- Ti-Zr alloy survival 94.6%.
- MBL: vertical 0.33±0.32 mm, horizontal 0.18±0.17 mm; Ti-Zr about 0.1 mm less bone loss; smokers 0.72 mm and 0.38 mm (vertical, horizontal).
- Mechanical complications: screw loosening 5.5%, porcelain fracture 2.2%; no fixture or abutment fracture; more common with CpTi and stock abutments, and in posterior implants.
- Posterior NDIs: n=108 (38 splinted FDPs, 56 single FDPs, 14 FDPs with pontics); 3-year survival 91.7%; vertical MBL 0.32±0.29 mm, more in molar than premolar.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Retrospective, non-randomized, single-centre; the authors state bias cannot be controlled.
- Large number of dropouts limited interpretation of long-term follow-up (only ≥1 year required; 3-year survival is the reported horizon).
- Ti-Zr NDIs were preferentially placed in premolar sites, so routine NDI use for single molar replacement is considered premature by the authors.
- Material, region and surface were not allocated at random, so confounding is possible; the Cox estimates are labelled OR in the paper.
- Authors call for well-designed prospective studies.

## 7. Glossary
- NDI: narrow-diameter implant (≤3.5 mm); RDI: regular-diameter implant (>3.5 mm).
- Ti-Zr: titanium-zirconium alloy; CpTi: commercially pure titanium; mSLA: chemically modified sandblasted large-grit acid-etched surface.
- MBL: marginal bone loss; SVR: survival rate; FDP: fixed dental prosthesis.
