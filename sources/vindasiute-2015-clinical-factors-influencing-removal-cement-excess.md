## Why Ingested

[[wiki/prosthetic-materials/tooth-preparation/gehrke-2019-influence-margin-location-luting]] quantifies undetected cement excess in vitro as a function of abutment margin depth and luting material, and [[wiki/overviews/implant-prosthesis-retention-screw-vs-cement-overview]] names residual cement as the biological cost of the cement-retained branch — but neither holds patient-based data on which *clinical* factors actually predict incomplete cleanup. This 2015 study (65 patients, 65 implants) supplies that missing axis: implant location in the arch, implant diameter, and the undercut between restoration and tissue, with cement-covered area measured in pixels after retrieval. It extends the in-vitro margin-depth evidence rather than duplicating it.

## Three-line Summary

Clinical patient-based study of 65 patients receiving 65 single metal-ceramic restorations on implants; after cementation the restoration-abutment unit was unscrewed and cement remnant area quantified in pixels from photographs of all specimen quadrants, testing implant location, diameter and restoration-tissue undercut.

Cement-covered soft tissue / restoration area: molars 4% / 7%, premolars 3.8% / 7.3%, anteriors 3% / 3.4%; diameter 3.5 mm 3.3% / 7.4%, 4.0 mm 7.7% / 7.7%, 5.0 mm 3.9% / 2.1%; undercut 1 mm 3.5% / 5.4%, 2 mm 4% / 8.1%, 3 mm 4.8% / 8.4%.

Implant location was insignificant (p > 0.05) for both soft tissue and specimen, while diameter (soft tissue p = 0.026, specimen p = 0.600) and undercut (soft tissue p = 0.004, specimen p = 0.046) were related to remnant amount — minimize undercuts when cement retention is chosen; abstract-only (full text not retrieved).

## 세줄요약

임상 환자 기반 연구, 단일 금속-세라믹 (metal-ceramic) 보철물을 식립한 환자 65명·임플란트 65개; 접착 후 보철물-지대주 일체를 풀어 모든 사분면 사진의 픽셀 (pixel) 면적비로 시멘트 잔여물 (cement remnants)을 정량하고 임플란트 위치·직경 (diameter)·보철물-연조직 언더컷 (undercut)을 평가.

시멘트로 피복된 연조직/보철물 면적: 대구치 (molars) 4%/7%, 소구치 (premolars) 3.8%/7.3%, 전치 (anteriors) 3%/3.4%; 직경 3.5 mm 3.3%/7.4%, 4.0 mm 7.7%/7.7%, 5.0 mm 3.9%/2.1%; 언더컷 1 mm 3.5%/5.4%, 2 mm 4%/8.1%, 3 mm 4.8%/8.4%.

임플란트 위치는 두 표적 모두 유의하지 않았고 (p > 0.05), 직경 (연조직 p = 0.026, 보철물 p = 0.600)과 언더컷 (연조직 p = 0.004, 보철물 p = 0.046)은 잔여물과 관련 — 시멘트유지 (cement-retained) 보철을 택한다면 언더컷을 최소화해야 함; 초록만 확보 (abstract-only, full text not retrieved).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Patient-based quantification of undetected cement coverage stratified by three clinical factors: implant location (molar / premolar / anterior), implant diameter (3.5 / 4.0 / 5.0 mm), and restoration-tissue undercut (1 / 2 / 3 mm).
- Confirms that arch location is **not** a significant predictor of residual cement (p > 0.05 for soft tissue and specimen alike), while undercut is significant on both surfaces (p = 0.004 soft tissue, p = 0.046 specimen).
- Shows the diameter effect is outcome-dependent: significant for peri-implant soft tissue (p = 0.026) but not for the restoration specimen (p = 0.600) — the two area measures are not interchangeable.
- Translates into a chairside rule: if a cemented crown restoration is desired, undercuts should be reduced to a minimum for better removal of cement excess, irrespective of implant diameter and location.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Clinical, patient-based single-cohort study with ex-vivo specimen analysis. Cementation was performed in patients; the restoration-abutment unit was then unscrewed and the retrieved specimens were photographed and analysed. No randomization or comparative arm is described in the abstract (labelled `prospective` for the study-design field; the abstract does not itself use the word).
- **n**: 65 patients treated with single metal-ceramic restorations on 65 implants — 39 molars, 22 premolars, 4 anteriors; implant diameters 21 × 3.5 mm, 34 × 4.0 mm, 10 × 5.0 mm.
- **Undercut measurement**: undercut between restoration and tissue measured at each site; 1 mm in 118 sites, 2 mm in 96 sites, 3 mm in 46 sites.
- **Outcome**: ratio of total restoration area / peri-implant tissue area to the area of cement remnants, calculated in pixels from photographs of all quadrants of each specimen.
- **Statistics**: significance threshold set to 0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Cement-covered area, reported as **soft tissue % / restoration (specimen) %**:

| Factor | Level | Soft tissue covered | Restoration covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implant location | Molar (n = 39) | 4% | 7% |
| Implant location | Premolar (n = 22) | 3.8% | 7.3% |
| Implant location | Anterior (n = 4) | 3% | 3.4% |
| Implant diameter | 3.5 mm (n = 21) | 3.3% | 7.4% |
| Implant diameter | 4.0 mm (n = 34) | 7.7% | 7.7% |
| Implant diameter | 5.0 mm (n = 10) | 3.9% | 2.1% |
| Undercut | 1 mm (118 sites) | 3.5% | 5.4% |
| Undercut | 2 mm (96 sites) | 4% | 8.1% |
| Undercut | 3 mm (46 sites) | 4.8% | 8.4% |

Significance tests (α = 0.05):

| Factor | Soft tissue | Specimen (restoration) |
|---|---|---|
| Implant location | p > 0.05 (insignificant) | p > 0.05 (insignificant) |
| Implant diameter | p = 0.026 (significant) | p = 0.600 (insignificant) |
| Undercut | p = 0.004 (significant) | p = 0.046 (significant) |

Direction of effect: larger undercut categories tracked higher cement coverage on both surfaces (1 mm → 3 mm: soft tissue 3.5% → 4.8%; restoration 5.4% → 8.4%). Diameter showed no monotonic pattern (4.0 mm highest on soft tissue at 7.7%; 5.0 mm lowest on the specimen at 2.1%). Location differences were small and non-significant.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only**: PubMed abstract only; no PMC record and no PDF were obtained. Every number here is transcribed verbatim from the abstract; no full-text table, figure, or supplementary detail could be checked.
- **Ex-vivo specimen analysis**: the restoration-abutment unit was unscrewed after cementation, so remnants were measured on retrieved specimens at one time point — not in situ, not over time, and not with the biological response of the peri-implant sulcus.
- No peri-implantitis, bleeding, or marginal bone loss (MBL) outcome and no follow-up are reported in the abstract; cement area is a surrogate, not a clinical endpoint.
- No luting-cement type comparison, operator blinding, sample-size calculation, or undercut-measurement reliability data are reported in the abstract.
- Small and uneven subgroups (4 anteriors; 10 implants at 5.0 mm; 118 / 96 / 46 sites across undercut categories) make the percentage estimates unstable — especially the anterior 3% / 3.4% pair.
- Diameter is significant for soft tissue (p = 0.026) but not for the specimen (p = 0.600); the abstract does not explain the discrepancy, so neither surface should be treated as a proxy for the other.
- Future work: retrieve the full text to confirm the analysis set and cement protocol; test undercut reduction prospectively against patient-level peri-implant outcomes.

## 7. Glossary

- **Cement excess / cement remnant**: luting cement left undetected around a cement-retained restoration after cleanup; here quantified as the area fraction of soft tissue and restoration covered.
- **Undercut**: the gap between the restoration margin/contour and the adjacent peri-implant tissue, measured here at 1, 2 and 3 mm; the modifiable factor the authors target.
- **Implant diameter**: fixture width (3.5, 4.0 or 5.0 mm in this study), tested as a predictor of remnant area.
- **Pixel planimetry**: measurement of cement-covered vs total area on standardized quadrant photographs, expressed as a ratio in pixels.
- **Cement-retained restoration**: prosthesis luted onto the abutment (here a single metal-ceramic crown), as opposed to screw-retained; requires excess-cement removal.
