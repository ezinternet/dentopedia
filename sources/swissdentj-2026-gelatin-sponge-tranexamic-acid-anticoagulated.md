## Three-line Summary

Scoping review (Arksey & O'Malley framework, PRISMA-ScR reporting) of 16 studies on anticoagulated patients undergoing tooth extraction with combined gelatin sponge and tranexamic acid (TXA) local hemostasis; PubMed, Scopus, and ScienceDirect searched through April 2026.

Included studies were predominantly observational with few randomized trials; vitamin K antagonists (VKA) dominated while direct oral anticoagulants (DOAC) were underrepresented, and gelatin sponge + TXA combinations were classifiable only as simultaneous, sequential, or unspecified, with substantial heterogeneity in TXA formulation, protocol, and outcome definitions.

Combined gelatin sponge + TXA appears promising for achieving hemostasis without interrupting anticoagulant therapy, but the evidence base is too heterogeneous and too low in study design level to support a standardized protocol — this is a mapped research gap, not a pooled effect estimate.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First evidence map dedicated specifically to the **combination** of absorbable gelatin sponge and tranexamic acid (TXA), rather than to either agent alone, in anticoagulated extraction patients.
- Introduces an explicit taxonomy of how the two agents are combined in practice — **simultaneous, sequential, or unspecified** — exposing that a large share of the literature does not even report the sequencing.
- Documents the anticoagulant-class imbalance in the local-hemostasis literature: vitamin K antagonists (VKA) dominate, direct oral anticoagulants (DOAC) are underrepresented, so the combination protocol is least evidenced for the drug class most patients are now on.
- Names the concrete gaps blocking guideline-level recommendation: heterogeneous TXA formulations, non-harmonized protocols, and inconsistent bleeding-outcome definitions.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: scoping review following the **Arksey and O'Malley** framework, reported per **PRISMA-ScR** (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews).
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, ScienceDirect.
- **Search cutoff**: April 2026.
- **Included**: 16 studies of anticoagulated patients undergoing tooth extraction with local hemostatic interventions.
- **Synthesis**: descriptive/narrative — **no meta-analysis, no pooled effect estimate, no formal risk-of-bias grading reported in the abstract**.
- **Charted dimensions**: study design, anticoagulant class represented, TXA formulation, mode of combination with gelatin sponge, and reported outcomes.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Design ceiling**: a scoping review maps evidence; it does not pool or grade it. No pooled bleeding rate is available from this paper, so it cannot settle any comparative question against an SR+MA.
- **Evidence base is weak at its source**: predominantly observational studies with limited RCTs.
- **DOAC gap**: the underrepresentation of DOAC means the combination is least supported for the anticoagulant class now most prescribed.
- **Protocol heterogeneity**: TXA formulation, concentration, timing, and sequencing relative to the gelatin sponge vary or go unreported (`unspecified` category).
- **Outcome heterogeneity**: no shared definition of post-extraction bleeding across studies.
- **Authors' call**: harmonized intervention protocols and stronger comparative studies (RCTs with standardized outcome definitions) are needed before evidence-based clinical guidance is possible.
- **Ingest-side limitation**: this page is built from the abstract only; the 16 included studies are not individually identifiable here.

## 7. Glossary

- **Scoping review**: evidence-mapping design that charts the extent, range, and nature of literature on a topic; does not pool effects or grade certainty (contrast with systematic review with meta-analysis).
- **Arksey and O'Malley framework**: the canonical 5-stage methodological framework for scoping reviews.
- **PRISMA-ScR**: PRISMA extension for Scoping Reviews — the reporting checklist for this design.
- **Gelatin sponge (absorbable)**: e.g., Gelfoam/Spongostan — a passive/mechanical hemostatic scaffold that supports clot formation in the socket.
- **TXA (Tranexamic Acid)**: antifibrinolytic (lysine analogue) that blocks plasminogen activation, stabilizing the clot against the high fibrinolytic activity of saliva; used as mouthwash, soaked gauze, or sponge irrigation.
- **VKA (Vitamin K Antagonist)**: warfarin and similar; INR-monitored.
- **DOAC (Direct Oral Anticoagulant)**: dabigatran (thrombin/IIa), rivaroxaban/apixaban/edoxaban (factor Xa); not reliably monitored by INR.
- **Simultaneous vs sequential combination**: whether TXA and the gelatin sponge are applied together (e.g., TXA-soaked sponge) or in sequence (sponge placed, then TXA rinse/irrigation) — a distinction this review found frequently unreported.
