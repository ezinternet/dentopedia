## Why Ingested
User requested an evidence base for alveolar-bone blood-supply loss after periosteal elevation — this dog study is the core microvascular evidence, directly documenting what the elevated periosteal vascular plexus does after mucoperiosteal flap surgery (angiogenesis phase, then regression/reconstruction, coupled to bone resorption and formation). It supplies the healing-response half of the question that [[suture-wound-closure/iwanaga-2019-releasing-incisions-buccal-periosteum-adjacent]] covers from the vessel-injury-anatomy side, and mechanistically underlies the reduced late vascularity after flap surgery reported in [[implants/soft-tissue/kim-2009-blood-vessels-peri-implant-mucosa]].

## Three-line Summary
Animal experimental study (12 adult beagle dogs) tracking the periosteal vascular plexus after mucoperiosteal flap surgery at days 7, 14, 21, and 28, using three techniques: light microscopy of India ink-injected histological specimens, transmission electron microscopy of ultrathin sections, and scanning electron microscopy of acryl-plastic vascular corrosion casts.
Days 7–14: the interstitium of the elevated mucoperiosteal vascular plexus filled with sinusoidal new blood vessels — osteoclastic bone resorption around them at day 7, glomerulus-like vessels in bone resorption cavities plus activated osteoblasts at day 14 — with highly permeable endothelial fenestrations, putative vesiculo-vacuolar organelles (VVOs), and caveolae; days 21–28: the plexus was dissected through regression of endothelial cells and fibroblasts, reconstructed into a rough mesh structure, and the bone surface became smooth.
Periosteal elevation evokes a coupled angiogenesis–bone-remodeling sequence rather than simple permanent devascularization, with the vascular changes contributing to transport of substances involved in periodontal repair; abstract-only ingestion and the canine model limit direct clinical extrapolation.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Documents the in-vivo time course of microvascular change in the elevated mucoperiosteal vascular plexus after flap surgery — sinusoidal new-vessel filling (days 7–14), then regression and reconstruction into a rough mesh structure (days 21–28).
- Couples the vascular sequence to bone remodeling phases: osteoclastic resorption around day-7 new vessels, activated osteoblasts around day-14 vessels, and a smooth bone surface by days 21–28.
- Identifies ultrastructural correlates of vascular permeability during remodeling — endothelial fenestrations, putative vesiculo-vacuolar organelles (VVOs), and caveolae — supporting substance transport for periodontal repair.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: experimental animal study; no control group described in the abstract
- **n**: 12 adult beagle dogs
- **Intervention**: mucoperiosteal flap surgery (jaw/site not specified in the abstract)
- **Observation points**: days 7, 14, 21, and 28 after surgery
- **Techniques (3 parallel)**: (1) India ink intravascular injection → histological specimens → light microscope; (2) ultrathin sections → transmission electron microscope; (3) acryl plastic injection → vascular corrosion casts → scanning electron microscope
- **Outcomes**: morphology of the periosteal vascular plexus, new-vessel and endothelial ultrastructure, and associated bone resorption/formation

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Day 7**: interstitium of the elevated mucoperiosteal vascular plexus filled with sinusoidal new blood vessels; bone resorption by osteoclasts observed around these new vessels; many highly permeable fenestrations present in the vascular endothelium.
- **Day 14**: sinusoidal new vessels more markedly developed; some regions exhibited glomerulus-like morphology consistent with bone resorption cavities; activated osteoblasts present around the new vessels; highly permeable vesicles — considered possible vesiculo-vacuolar organelles (VVOs) and caveolae — noted in the vascular endothelium.
- **Days 21 and 28**: the mucoperiosteal vascular plexus was dissected through regression of endothelial cells and fibroblasts and reconstructed into a rough mesh structure; simultaneously the bone surface became smooth.
- **Quantitative note**: no counts, densities, or p-values are reported in the retrieved abstract — findings are qualitative/histomorphological.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Abstract-only ingestion — paywalled J Periodontol 2005 article, no PMC full text retrieved; all detail above comes from the structured PubMed abstract, so quantitative data in the full text (if any) is not captured here.
- Animal model (beagle dogs); extrapolation to human alveolar bone and flap protocols is limited.
- No non-elevated control site, no quantitative vascular metrics, and no linkage to flap tension or closure outcomes described in the abstract.
- Future work implied by the batch question: quantifying the magnitude and duration of the net vascular deficit of elevated periosteum relative to graft/wound healing outcomes.

## 7. Glossary
- **Mucoperiosteal flap**: full-thickness flap elevating mucosa and periosteum together off the bone.
- **Periosteal vascular plexus**: the vessel network within the periosteum, a major blood supply to the underlying cortical bone.
- **Angiogenesis**: formation of new blood vessels from pre-existing vasculature.
- **Sinusoidal new blood vessel**: dilated, sinus-like new vessel that fills the interstitium of the elevated plexus early after surgery.
- **Vascular corrosion cast**: a replica of the vessel lumen made by injecting acryl plastic and corroding away the surrounding tissue, examined under scanning electron microscopy.
- **Vesiculo-vacuolar organelles (VVOs)**: clustered vesiculo-vacuolar structures in vascular endothelium mediating transendothelial transport and permeability.
