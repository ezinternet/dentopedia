## Why Ingested

Surveillance sweep on bone-graft-material (allograft/xenograft/alloplast) surfaced this early systematic review of allogeneic block grafts in the atrophic maxilla. It supplies the maxilla-specific block-survival, horizontal-gain and histology data that sit underneath the larger all-site comparison in [[wiki/bone-regeneration/donkiewicz-2021-survival-rates-implants-autogenous-allogeneic]] (which it reinforces on implant survival) and adds allograft-block detail to the graft-class context of [[wiki/bone-regeneration/benic-2014-horizontal-bone-augmentation-guided-regeneration]].

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review with weighted-mean meta-analysis (random effects) of 15 prospective human studies (14 case series, 1 RCT; NOS 6.06 ± 1.04) on allogeneic bone block grafts for atrophic maxillary augmentation; 361 blocks followed 4–9 months.
9 blocks failed (2.4%; cumulative block survival 98%), all within 1–2 months, mainly from early membrane exposure; weighted horizontal gain was 4.79 mm (95% CI 4.51–5.08, 119 sites) and implant survival 96.9% (95% CI 92.8–98.7%, 228 implants, mean 23.9 months).
Allogeneic blocks look like a reliable alternative to autogenous blocks in the maxilla, but heterogeneous histology (necrotic bone persisting in some series), little vertical data and small samples mean the results need cautious interpretation.

## 세줄요약

전향적 인체 연구 15편(증례군 14편, 무작위대조시험 1편; 뉴캐슬-오타와 척도 6.06 ± 1.04)을 대상으로 한 가중평균 메타분석(체계적 문헌고찰, Systematic Review, SR)으로, 위축된 상악 증대에 사용한 동종골 블록 이식(Allogeneic Bone Block Graft) 361개를 4–9개월 추적했다.
블록 9개(2.4%)가 수술 후 1–2개월 내에 실패했고(누적 블록 생존율 98%) 주원인은 조기 차폐막 노출이었으며, 가중 수평 골증대량은 4.79 mm(95% 신뢰구간 4.51–5.08, 119부위), 임플란트 생존율은 96.9%(95% 신뢰구간 92.8–98.7%, 임플란트 228개, 평균 23.9개월)였다.
동종골 블록은 상악에서 자가골 블록의 믿을 만한 대안으로 보이나, 조직학적 이질성(일부 연구에서 괴사골 잔존), 부족한 수직 증대 자료, 작은 표본 때문에 해석에 주의가 필요하다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Pooled analysis of prospective human data on allogeneic block grafts restricted to the atrophic maxilla, reporting block survival, resorption/bone gain, implant survival and histology together.
- Quantifies block failure (9/361 = 2.4%) and shows failures cluster in the first 1–2 months, with early membrane exposure as the leading cause and fixation-screw loosening second.
- Gives a pooled horizontal gain (4.79 mm) and an implant survival estimate (96.9%) for implants placed in allogeneic block-augmented maxillae.
- Documents that allogeneic blocks behave differently from autogenous blocks early in healing (histology), while flagging that late remodeling fate remains unclear.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review with random-effects weighted-mean meta-analysis (Comprehensive Meta-analysis software); Kaplan-Meier estimator for block survival (SPSS 22.0); quality by Newcastle-Ottawa scale.
- **Databases**: MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane CENTRAL, Cochrane Oral Health Group Trials Register; English-language articles from January 2000 to December 2013, plus manual search of implant journals (2012–2013). Search by one reviewer; analysis by one blinded investigator.
- **Eligibility**: prospective human clinical trials of allograft bone blocks with survival rate outcome; excluded case reports/series with <10 subjects, SRs, animal studies, retrospective cohorts, unclear data. Mandible blocks excluded where site could be identified.
- **Selection flow**: 239 screened -> 109 by abstract -> 26 full texts -> 15 included (14 prospective case series, 1 RCT).
- **Outcomes**: block survival/failure and its timing/causes; resorption and horizontal/vertical gain; implant cumulative survival; histologic/histomorphometric findings.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Quality**: NOS mean 6.06 ± 1.04 (adequate, medium-high).
- **Block failure**: 361 blocks followed 4–9 months; 9 failed (2.4%) within 1–2 months; cumulative block survival 98%. Of the 9 failures, 5 corticocancellous and 4 cancellous; 7 had a membrane and 2 did not (too few for formal analysis of graft type or membrane effect).
- **Causes**: early membrane exposure the main reason (refs 16, 20, 27, 30); fixation-screw loosening second (ref 29). Odds of success rise from the third month.
- **Bone gain**: weighted horizontal gain 4.79 mm (95% CI 4.51–5.08) from 119 sites in 5 studies; vertical gain 2 ± 0.5 mm (two studies, limited data).
- **Resorption**: 10 ± 10% to 52 ± 25.97% at 6 months across studies; mean 21.70 ± 30.55%; high heterogeneity; small-sample observation that longer healing coincided with less bone gain.
- **Implant survival**: weighted mean 96.9% (95% CI 92.8–98.7%), 228 implants, mean follow-up 23.9 months.
- **Histology (6 studies; 2 with autogenous ramus-block controls)**: Acocella (9 months) newly formed bone 61.96 ± 11.77% surrounded by nonvital bone with empty osteocyte lacunae; Contar lamellar bone around Haversian canals with more empty lacunae centrally; Lumetti (6 months) mostly empty lacunae in allograft but viable osteocytes in new bone, minimal differences vs autograft apart from more connective tissue/inflammatory cells; Spin-Neto large necrotic segments, little osteoclastic activity and no direct contact between remodeled and grafted bone, whereas autograft showed small necrotic areas with abundant osteocytes.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Mostly non-randomized case series (14 of 15); only one RCT; search by a single reviewer; English-only.
- Small samples and high heterogeneity (resorption, histology); too few failures to analyze effects of graft type or membrane use; no clear conclusion on vertical augmentation.
- Block follow-up only 4–9 months; fate in late remodeling unclear; ideal implant timing after allogeneic block grafting undetermined.
- Authors call for a large RCT against intramembranous and endochondral autogenous blocks, comparison of allograft block types, the effect of buccal bone thickness, and the role of biologics such as BMPs.

## 6. Related Work
- Donkiewicz (2021): larger all-site SR of implant survival in autogenous vs allogeneic bone blocks (97.66% ALBB) — consistent with the 96.9% here.
- Benic & Hämmerle (2014): horizontal augmentation review that includes block allograft within its graft-class framework.
- Lumetti et al. and Spin-Neto et al. (cited within): histologic comparisons of allogeneic vs autogenous blocks with divergent findings.

## 7. Glossary
- **Allogeneic bone block**: cadaver-derived bone block (same species), used as an onlay graft instead of patient's own bone.
- **Corticocancellous vs cancellous block**: allograft blocks containing cortical plus cancellous bone vs cancellous bone only.
- **Newcastle-Ottawa scale (NOS)**: quality-assessment tool for nonrandomized studies.
- **Weighted mean (WM)**: pooled estimate weighting each study by size/precision; random-effects model accounts for between-study differences.
- **Empty osteocyte lacunae**: bone spaces lacking viable cells, an indicator of nonvital (necrotic) graft bone.
