## Why Ingested

Ingested as the pediatric evidence review underpinning the 2022 ADA clinical practice guideline on acute dental pain in children, arriving alongside the ADA guideline package (main guideline paper and chairside guides, ingested separately) — this SR supplies the effect estimates behind those recommendations. It extends the wiki's adult-anchored analgesia evidence base ([[wiki/drug/analgesics/moore-2015-single-dose-oral-analgesics-cochrane-overview]]) to children ≤ 12 years and independently confirms the ibuprofen + acetaminophen combination superiority seen in [[wiki/drug/analgesics/wells-2011-ibuprofen-acetaminophen-endodontic-necrosis-rct]].

## Three-line Summary

- First systematic review and meta-analysis (SRMA) of oral analgesics for acute dental pain in children ≤ 12 years: 6 RCTs (45–201 participants each, mean age 5.5–9.3 years), all post-extraction, informing the 2022 ADA pediatric guideline.
- Acetaminophen 15 mg/kg + ibuprofen 5 mg/kg reduced pain intensity importantly more than acetaminophen alone (MD, −0.75 points; moderate certainty); ibuprofen vs acetaminophen differences were trivial; acetaminophen-codeine gains were trivial; zero trials addressed irreversible pulpitis.
- Best available evidence supports ibuprofen ± acetaminophen first-line in children, but adverse-effect conclusions rest on very low certainty evidence, making pediatric safety data the urgent gap.

## 세줄요약

- 소아(≤ 12세) 발치 후 급성 치통의 경구 진통제에 관한 최초의 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis, SRMA): 무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) 6편(연구당 45–201명, 평균 연령 5.5–9.3세), 전부 발치 후 통증을 다루며 2022년 ADA(미국치과협회) 소아 지침의 근거가 됨.
- 아세트아미노펜(15 mg/kg) + 이부프로펜(5 mg/kg) 병합은 아세트아미노펜 단독보다 통증 강도를 중요하게 더 줄였고(평균차이 (Mean Difference, MD) −0.75점, moderate certainty), 이부프로펜 vs 아세트아미노펜 차이는 사소했으며 아세트아미노펜+코데인의 이득도 사소했고, 가역성 치수염 (irreversible pulpitis) 연구는 0편.
- 소아에서 이부프로펜 ± 아세트아미노펜 1차 선택을 뒷받침하나, 부작용 결론은 매우 낮은 확신도 (very low certainty)라 소아 안전성 연구가 시급한 한계.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First SR and meta-analysis of pharmacologic management of acute dental pain in children that assessed certainty of the evidence using GRADE.
- Direct comparative effect estimates for ibuprofen, acetaminophen, their fixed combination, and acetaminophen-codeine across pain intensity, pain relief, global efficacy rating, rescue analgesia use, and adverse effects in children ≤ 12 years.
- Documents a total evidence gap for irreversible pulpitis analgesia in children (zero eligible trials) despite pulpitis being a stated target condition.
- Provides the quantitative basis for the opioid-sparing recommendation in the 2022 ADA pediatric acute dental pain guideline (codeine/tramadol restricted by FDA in children ≤ 12 years since April 2017).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: SR and random-effects meta-analysis following PRISMA 2020; unregistered but followed preestablished methodology defined by the guideline panel questions (framed per National Academies opioid prescribing report).
- Eligibility: RCTs comparing any non-intravenous analgesic against another analgesic or placebo in patients ≤ 12 years undergoing simple or surgical tooth extraction or experiencing irreversible pulpitis; English-language peer-reviewed publications.
- Sources: MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, US Clinical Trials registry, inception through November 2020; 4,716 records screened, 858 full texts assessed, 6 RCTs included.
- Screening/data extraction: independent duplicate pairs in Covidence with third-reviewer adjudication; risk of bias per outcome using a modified Cochrane RoB 2.0 tool (4-level ratings across 5 domains).
- Synthesis: random-effects inverse-variance models in Review Manager 5.4; risk ratio for dichotomous outcomes (risk difference when events sparse), mean difference for continuous outcomes with scale harmonization; 95% CIs throughout.
- Certainty: GRADE per comparison and outcome, minimally contextualized with null effect thresholds (10% of baseline risk for dichotomous outcomes, 10% of scale range for continuous); absolute effects presented as natural frequencies per 100 patients; GRADEpro Summary of Findings tables.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Included trials: N randomized 45–201 per study; mean (SD) age 5.5 (1.9) to 9.3 (2.3) years; all trials involved simple extractions, half also surgical extractions; no study covered irreversible pulpitis.
- Pain intensity at 4 hours (1 [none]–4 [severe] scale): ibuprofen vs acetaminophen MD, 0.27 points (95% CI, −0.13 to 0.68; low certainty); ibuprofen vs placebo MD, −0.19 points (95% CI, −0.58 to 0.21; low certainty); acetaminophen vs placebo MD, −0.13 points (95% CI, −0.52 to 0.26; low certainty) — all trivial differences.
- Combination therapy: acetaminophen (15 mg/kg) + ibuprofen (5 mg/kg) probably reduces pain intensity 15 minutes after recovery from general anesthesia by an important amount vs acetaminophen alone (MD, −0.75 points; 95% CI, −1.22 to 0.27; moderate certainty) and trivially vs ibuprofen alone (MD, −0.01 points; 95% CI, −0.53 to 0.51; moderate certainty).
- Pain relief at 4 hours (1 [complete]–5 [none] scale): acetaminophen provides less relief than ibuprofen (MD, 0.2 points; moderate certainty) and more than placebo (MD, −0.30 points; moderate certainty), both trivial; ibuprofen vs placebo MD, −0.49 points, an important difference (low certainty).
- Global efficacy rating at 4 hours (1 [excellent]–5 [discontinued]): ibuprofen more efficacious than placebo by an important amount (MD, −0.97 points; moderate certainty); acetaminophen less efficacious than ibuprofen (MD, 0.53 points; moderate certainty).
- Rescue analgesia: acetaminophen may increase need at 4 hours vs ibuprofen (RR, 3.45; 95% CI, 0.80 to 14.92; low certainty) and decrease it at 7 hours (RR, 0.75; 95% CI, 0.19 to 2.93; low certainty); ibuprofen may decrease need vs placebo (RR, 0.39; 95% CI, 0.06 to 2.71; low certainty).
- Acetaminophen (240 mg) + codeine (24 mg): trivial advantages over ibuprofen (pain intensity MD, −0.29), acetaminophen alone (MD, −0.09), and placebo (MD, −0.16), all low certainty.
- Adverse effects: very low certainty across all comparisons — no reliable safety conclusions possible.
- Risk of bias: missing outcome data and selective reporting were the domains most often judged high/probably high risk; GRADE most frequently rated down for risk of bias and imprecision.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Zero trials on analgesics for irreversible pulpitis in children — authors explicitly cannot draw conclusions for this population despite it being a core target condition.
- Only 6 small RCTs (45–201 participants); pooled estimates impossible for several collected outcomes.
- Very low certainty evidence for all adverse-effects comparisons — the safety half of the benefit-harm equation rests essentially on no data.
- English-only, peer-reviewed-only inclusion may have missed unpublished/negative studies (authors judged impact unlikely to change conclusions).
- Wide CIs crossing both benefit and harm thresholds forced imprecision downratings (e.g., RR 3.45 for rescue analgesia at 4 h has CI spanning 0.80–14.92).
- Authors' call: methodologically rigorous RCTs examining analgesic effects — especially adverse effects — for post-extraction pain and irreversible pulpitis in children are urgently needed.
- Note: pooling was deliberately wide (across cointerventions and pre- vs postoperative administration); no important heterogeneity detected, including rectal vs oral acetaminophen administration.

## 7. Glossary

- Multimodal therapy: combining or staggering analgesics with different mechanisms (e.g., acetaminophen + NSAID) to optimize pain control while reducing doses of each agent.
- GRADE: Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation — framework rating evidence certainty (high/moderate/low/very low) per domain: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, publication bias, imprecision.
- Modified RoB 2.0: Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials adapted by this group, rating five bias domains at four levels (low / probably low / probably high / high).
- Rescue analgesia: additional analgesic taken when the assigned regimen fails to control pain; used here as a behavioral efficacy endpoint.
- Minimally contextualized threshold: pre-set effect-size threshold (here 10% of baseline risk or scale range) below which a statistically significant estimate is judged clinically trivial.
- Irreversible pulpitis: irreversibly inflamed dental pulp producing persistent pain; a stated target condition for which no eligible pediatric analgesic trial was found.
