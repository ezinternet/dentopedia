## Why Ingested

Class III camouflage is conventionally treated with fixed appliances or surgery; long-term (7-year) outcome data for clear-aligner-only camouflage of an adult skeletal Class III case are rare. This case complements [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/huang-2026-clear-aligner-mandibular-advancement-vs-functional-class-ii-sr-ma]] — both papers test clear aligners' ability to correct sagittal skeletal discrepancy non-surgically, but at opposite ends of the spectrum (Class II growing-patient functional-appliance substitute vs. Class III adult camouflage combined with restorative/implant work). Useful as a rare long-term stability anchor and as a multidisciplinary-treatment-planning exemplar for borderline-surgical Class III cases who decline orthognathic surgery.

## Three-line Summary

Case report, n=1, 28-year-old adult female with skeletal Class III malocclusion (Wits −4.92 mm, Holdaway angle 11°, full Class III molar relationship with a functional/positional component), treated non-surgically with clear aligners (Invisalign) integrated with restorative dentistry and implantology.

Dentoalveolar camouflage was achieved via bite opening (intrusion + retroclination of mandibular incisors, proclination of maxillary incisors: U1-SN 99.17°→109.74°, L1-MP 96.37°→88.08°), Class III elastics, and occlusal-surface attachment bite ramps; a discolored UL1 was ultimately extracted and replaced with an implant plus anterior esthetic restoration; SNB improved 79.58°→78.49° and soft-tissue profile improved (NA-Apo −5.31°→−2.51°).

Seven-year follow-up showed stable Class I occlusion, overjet/overbite, and molar intercuspation — supporting clear aligners as a viable non-surgical camouflage option in borderline (Holdaway >10.3°, Wits >−5.8 mm) skeletal Class III adults who decline orthognathic surgery, though as a single case report the generalizability and recurrence risk over even longer horizons remain unknown.

## 2. Key Contributions

- One of few published long-term (7-year) follow-ups documenting clear-aligner-only camouflage of an adult skeletal Class III malocclusion with a functional (mandible-retrudable) component.
- Demonstrates a multidisciplinary sequencing template: orthodontic dentoalveolar compensation → conservative monitoring of a compromised tooth → extraction/implant replacement → anterior esthetic restoration, all coordinated within a single aligner-based treatment arc.
- Reinforces published borderline surgical thresholds (Holdaway angle <10.3°, Wits <−5.8 mm per Eslami et al.) by presenting a case that fell just outside them (Holdaway 11°, Wits −4.92 mm) and was successfully camouflaged non-surgically.
- Uses occlusal-surface (molar) attachment thickness as a deliberate bite-ramp mechanism to open the bite and correct anterior crossbite, combined with Class III elastics to drive mandibular-plane clockwise rotation and occlusal-plane counterclockwise flattening.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: single case report, 28-year-old Chinese female, chief complaint of anterior malocclusion.
- Diagnosis: extraoral exam showed midface deficiency, concave profile, flattened smile curve, narrow buccal corridor; full Class III molar relationship; mandible could be retruded from centric occlusion to an edge-to-edge position (functional component); cephalometrics: Wits appraisal −4.92 mm, Holdaway angle 11°.
- Treatment-alternatives decision: combined orthodontic-orthognathic surgery was the theoretically indicated approach given the skeletal discrepancy, but the patient declined surgery. Because her Holdaway angle (11°) exceeded the surgical threshold (10.3°) and her Wits appraisal (−4.92 mm) was less severe than the surgical threshold (−5.8 mm) per Eslami et al., camouflage treatment was judged a viable alternative.
- Pre-treatment extraction: LL8 (lower left third molar) extracted before aligner therapy to facilitate the regional acceleratory phenomenon and more efficient distal molar movement.
- Appliance: Invisalign® Aligner (Align Technology Trading Co. Ltd., Shanghai, China), delivered in two phases — preliminary stage (maxillary 31 aligners, mandibular 31 aligners) and refinement stage (maxillary 38 aligners, mandibular 38 aligners).
- Mechanics: bite opening via intrusion and retroclination of mandibular incisors + proclination of maxillary incisors; occlusal-surface attachments on molars acting as bite ramps; Class III elastics to promote mandibular-plane clockwise rotation and occlusal-plane counterclockwise flattening.
- Restorative/implant component: a discolored UL1 (maxillary left central incisor) was initially managed with conservative observation, then extracted and replaced with an implant, with concurrent anterior esthetic restoration.
- Follow-up: 7 years post-treatment.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Occlusion: post-treatment bilateral super Class I molar relationship and Class I canine relationship achieved; upper and lower crowding corrected; ideal overjet and overbite established; anterior crossbite corrected.
- Dentoalveolar changes: maxillary incisor proclination, U1-SN 99.17° → 109.74°; mandibular incisor retroclination, L1-MP 96.37° → 88.08°; roots reported maintained within basal bone.
- Skeletal/soft-tissue changes: SNB 79.58° → 78.49° (mild reduction, consistent with camouflage rather than skeletal surgery); soft-tissue profile improved, NA-Apo angle −5.31° → −2.51°.
- Dental/implant outcome: discolored UL1 ultimately extracted and restored with an implant plus esthetic anterior restoration.
- Long-term stability: 7-year follow-up showed stable occlusal and cephalometric results, supporting durability of the camouflage approach in this case.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single case report (n=1) — no comparator group, no statistical inference possible; findings are hypothesis-generating only, not generalizable.
- Full body text was not accessible (paywalled abstract/snippet view only); some methodological and outcome detail (e.g., exact timing of UL1 extraction/implant relative to orthodontic phases, full cephalometric table, complication history) could not be extracted from the available preview.
- As with all Class III dentoalveolar camouflage, recurrence risk, gingival recession, and root resorption are recognized inherent risks of the compensatory approach (noted by the authors as general limitations of this treatment category); this case's absence of complications does not establish low risk broadly.
- No mention of whether periodontal or root-resorption surveillance (e.g., CBCT) was performed during or after treatment.

## 6. Related Work

- Eslami et al. — cited for the borderline surgical thresholds used to justify non-surgical camouflage (Holdaway angle <10.3°, Wits appraisal <−5.8 mm).
- Cozzani et al. (2013) — non-surgical, non-extractive treatment of severe Class III malocclusion with 24-year post-treatment follow-up (historical precedent for long-term camouflage stability, fixed-appliance era).

## 7. Glossary

- **Wits appraisal**: cephalometric measurement of the anteroposterior relationship between maxilla and mandible, projected onto the occlusal plane; more negative values indicate greater mandibular skeletal excess (Class III tendency).
- **Holdaway angle**: cephalometric soft-tissue angle used (among other uses) as a surgical-camouflage decision threshold in Class III cases.
- **Camouflage treatment**: orthodontic (dentoalveolar) compensation for an underlying skeletal discrepancy, used as an alternative to orthognathic surgery.
- **Class III elastics**: intermaxillary elastics that pull the maxillary arch forward/mandibular arch backward, used here to assist mandibular-plane and occlusal-plane rotation.
- **Regional acceleratory phenomenon (RAP)**: transient local increase in bone remodeling rate following surgical trauma (e.g., extraction), exploited here to facilitate faster molar distalization.
- **NA-Apo angle**: soft-tissue cephalometric angle describing facial convexity/profile.
