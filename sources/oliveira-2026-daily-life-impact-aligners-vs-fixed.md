## Why Ingested

Addresses PRO/QoL subtopic with DIDL instrument across treatment phases (initial vs final), capturing temporal dissatisfaction dynamics. Shows 0% dissatisfied at final CA phase — strong endpoint data for CA superiority. → [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/oliveira-2026-daily-life-impact-aligners-vs-fixed]]

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study of 112 Brazilian adults (4 groups by appliance × treatment phase) using the DIDL (Dental Impacts on Daily Living) questionnaire. Final clear aligner phase had zero dissatisfied patients vs 35.7% dissatisfied at initial fixed appliance phase. Adaptation over time was the primary driver of improving patient experience in both groups.

## 1. Document Information

- **Type**: Cross-sectional observational study
- **Journal**: Dental Press Journal of Orthodontics 30(5):e252562 (2026), DOI 10.1590/2177-6709.30.5.e252562.oar
- **PMID/PMCID**: 41538529 / PMC12788501
- **Sample**: n=112 adults (≥18y), 4 groups n=28 each: initial FA / final FA / initial CA / final CA; Fluminense Federal University clinic, Brazil

## 2. Key Findings

- Dissatisfaction rates (Fisher p=0.001): initial FA 35.7% > initial CA 14.3% > final FA 10.7% > final CA 0%
- Total DIDL score: no significant between-group differences (CA vs FA at either phase) — adaptation equalizes overall scores
- "General performance" domain: significant improvement initial vs final FA (p=0.025); CA already good early
- Comfort domain (final CA): 0 patients with breathing interference vs 16/28 FA; 2 CA vs 8 FA gingival bleeding
- Pain domain: no significant differences between any group
- Demographics: 67.8% women, mean age 36.5 years

## 3. Methodology

Cross-sectional; 4-group design (not longitudinal). DIDL instrument (36 items, 5 domains: appearance, pain, comfort, general performance/interpersonal, eating/chewing). Domain-weighted score -1 to +1 (<0=dissatisfied; 0–0.7=partial; >0.7=satisfied). Mann-Whitney + Fisher's exact test.

## 4. Clinical Implications

The initial placement of fixed appliances is the highest-dissatisfaction moment — targeted counseling and early-phase management (pain, hygiene, adjustment expectations) at this critical window can improve experience. Clear aligners show no such sharp initial dissatisfaction peak.

## 5. Limitations

Cross-sectional (4 independent groups, not the same patients followed over time); recall and selection bias; convenience sample; single institution; DIDL is comprehensive but less frequently used than OHIP-14, limiting cross-study comparison.
