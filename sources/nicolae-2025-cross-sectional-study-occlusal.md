## Summary

Cross-sectional study evaluating whether occlusal loading and periodontal status differ between teeth with deflective occlusal contacts (premature contacts in centric relation + protrusive/lateral interferences) and adjacent/homologous control teeth in 50 patients.

## Study Design

- **Design**: Cross-sectional, convenience sample
- **N**: 50 patients; 493 teeth with deflective contacts, 473 adjacent controls, 457 homologous controls
- **Ethics**: Carol Davila University Ethics Committee (PO-35-F-03, March 2025)
- **Institution**: Carol Davila University of Medicine and Pharmacy, Bucharest, Romania
- **Method**: OccluSense digital occlusal analyzer (Dr. Jean Bausch GmbH, 256 pressure levels, 100 Hz, 10 s, 60 µm sensor thickness); panoramic radiograph analysis; clinical periodontal measurements

## Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria

**Included**: Adults ≥18 y, primary and/or secondary occlusion disorders on digital exam, ≥10 pairs of occlusal units, panoramic within 6 months.

**Excluded**: Occlusal adjustments or periodontal treatment within 12 months; systemic conditions associated with periodontal disease (diabetes, hypertension, blood disorders, kidney disease, radiotherapy/chemotherapy history); pregnant women; non-cooperative.

## Definitions

- **Deflective occlusal contacts**: Nonfunctional occlusal contacts causing deviation in normal mandibular closure path; includes premature contacts (centric relation, just before maximal intercuspal position) and interferences (lateral/protrusive mandibular movements)
- **Premature contacts**: Present during centric path closure when mandible is deflected
- **Occlusal interferences**: Nonfunctional contacts during left/right lateral or protrusive movements

## Key Findings

### Occlusal Loading

Teeth with deflective contacts showed significantly higher maximum occlusal loading than controls:

| Group | Mean occlusal load |
|---|---|
| Deflective contact teeth | 208 |
| Adjacent control teeth | 72 |
| Homologous control teeth | 97 |

All comparisons significant (p < 0.05; Kruskal–Wallis, Friedman, Cochran's Q, chi-squared tests used).

### Periodontal Status

Deflective contact teeth showed more advanced periodontal damage:
- Deeper probing depths
- Greater gingival recession
- More alveolar bone loss
- Wider periodontal ligament space

### Contact Type Analysis

Deflective contacts in centric relation were more strongly linked to periodontal deterioration than contacts in protrusive or lateral mandibular movements, **despite similar occlusal force magnitudes**. This suggests direction/timing of contact matters beyond sheer force magnitude.

## Why Ingested

Provides cross-sectional clinical evidence for the [[overviews/occlusal-trauma-periodontitis-overview]] synthesis — adds a digital-occlusal-analysis dataset (OccluSense) quantifying force levels at deflective contacts and correlating with multi-parameter periodontal assessment (probing depth, recession, bone loss, PDL widening) in a controlled design comparing deflective vs adjacent vs homologous teeth. Supports the existing theme that occlusal overload is associated with periodontal damage while remaining a modifier-not-initiator framework topic.

## Limitations

- Small convenience sample (n=50), single center
- Cross-sectional design precludes causal inference
- Plaque/inflammation status not fully controlled
- OccluSense percentage values are relative to the session (not absolute N or MPa)

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study of 50 patients using OccluSense digital occlusal analysis to compare 493 teeth with deflective contacts vs adjacent/homologous controls; deflective-contact teeth carried substantially higher occlusal loading (mean 208 vs 72–97) and showed more advanced periodontal damage (deeper pockets, greater recession, bone loss, wider PDL space).

Centric-relation premature contacts associated with more periodontal damage than protrusive/lateral interferences despite similar force magnitudes, suggesting contact type and mandibular-path deviation matter independently of load.

Study adds digitally quantified force data to the occlusal-trauma/periodontitis literature but cannot establish causation; small convenience sample and cross-sectional design limit generalizability.

## 세줄요약

50명 환자에서 OccluSense 디지털 교합분석을 이용해 편위성 교합접촉치 493개를 인접치·대합치 대조군과 비교; 편위접촉치는 교합부하(mean 208 vs 72–97)가 유의하게 높고 치주 손상(탐침 깊이·치은 퇴축·골소실·치주인대 폭경 확장)도 더 진행되어 있었다.

정중위(CR) 조기접촉은 전방이동·측방이동 간섭보다 교합력 크기가 유사함에도 치주 손상이 더 강하게 연관되어, 힘의 크기보다 접촉 유형과 하악 편위 방향이 독립적으로 중요함을 시사한다.

단면 연구 설계상 인과관계 추론 불가; 소규모 편의표본(n=50) 단일기관 연구로 일반화에 한계가 있다.
