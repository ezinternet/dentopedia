## Why Ingested

The wiki holds NCCL evidence that is mostly short- to medium-term, including the 3-year Peumans RCT and the SR+MAs [[resin-bonding/assis-2023-acid-etching-universal-adhesive-nccl-sr-ma]] and [[resin-bonding/doshi-2023-universal-adhesive-nccl-strategy-sr-ma]]. This 14-year RCT, which the authors call the longest published NCCL follow-up, adds a long-term anchor for a mild 1-step self-etch adhesive against the 3-step etch-and-rinse gold standard → [[resin-bonding/peumans-2023-hema-free-1step-self-etch-nccl-14year-rct]]

## Three-line Summary

Monocenter RCT (lesion-level randomization) (KU Leuven; 52 patients, 267 NCCLs, patient recall 63% and restoration recall 71% at 14 years) comparing the HEMA-free 1-step self-etch adhesive G-Bond (GB) with the 3-step etch-and-rinse adhesive Optibond FL (OFL) under the microhybrid composite Gradia Direct; evaluated by two blinded examiners at 6 months and 1, 2, 3, 5, 9 and 14 years with Vanherle criteria and a 2-way GEE model.

At 14 years the overall clinical success rate was 58.9% (GB) vs 57.9% (OFL) and retention 80.6% vs 80.4%, with no significant difference (p > 0.05); 79 restorations failed (39 GB, 40 OFL), mainly from unacceptable marginal deterioration, then retention loss. GB showed more enamel-side marginal discoloration (OR 0.356, p = 0.006) and more increased sensitivity (18.9% vs 6.8%, p = 0.01), which the authors attribute to wear and recession rather than the adhesive.

Failure and retention loss rose sharply in the last 5 years (success 80.3%/79.5% at 9 years), driven by patient medical decline and recurring abrasion/erosion/abfraction, and multiple restorations per patient gave worse results than two per patient; limitations are single center, two operators, a 37% patient dropout, older non-FDI criteria and a single adhesive pair.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Longest published follow-up of NCCL composite restorations, per the authors (14 years).
- Shows that a mild HEMA-free 1-step self-etch adhesive performed equal to a 3-step etch-and-rinse gold standard over 14 years, although in-vitro bond-durability data predicted OFL would be superior.
- Documents the role of patient-level events (medical compromise, xerostomia, chemotherapy, recurrence of abrasion/erosion/abfraction) in late failure.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- 52 patients, 267 NCCLs needing a Class-V composite; all lesions per patient included (minimum 2 per patient); 133 GB and 134 OFL, assigned with a randomization table.
- Two operators (Master-after-Master trainees, 2-3 years of clinical experience); dentin roughened and enamel bevel 1-2 mm; contour strip in 213 teeth, retraction cord in 54.
- Two independent blinded examiners (not the operators); recalls at baseline, 6 months, 1, 2, 3, 5, 9 and 14 years.
- Key parameters: retention, marginal integrity, marginal discoloration, caries; success = none scored as failure (lost, severe marginal defect, deep marginal discoloration, caries). Vanherle et al criteria were used because the study predates FDI criteria.
- Statistics: logistic regression with generalized estimating equations (2-way GEE, compound symmetry) to account for clustering; Bonferroni correction for primary parameters; McNemar analysis and 100 random re-draws to compare one-pair-per-patient vs multiple restorations.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Recall at 14 years: 63% of patients (43 of 52), 71% of restorations (180; 95 GB, 95 OFL). Reasons for patient loss: 4 deceased, 4 health problems, 4 unreachable, 3 declined (COVID-19), 3 teeth extracted for other reasons. Failures at earlier recalls in non-recalled patients (11 teeth) were carried forward as failures.
- Overall success: GB 58.9%, OFL 57.9% (9-year: 80.3% vs 79.5%); failure odds ratio 1.008 (95% CI 0.536-1.896).
- Retention: GB 80.6%, OFL 80.4% (36 restorations lost, 18 each); OR 1.0025 (0.519-1.936), p > 0.999.
- Severe marginal defect: GB 14.5%, OFL 19.2%; deep marginal discoloration: GB 18.2%, OFL 13.2% (OR 0.776, p = 0.52); clinically acceptable marginal defect: 84.2% GB vs 76.7% OFL (p > 0.05).
- Caries at the margin: 4 teeth (2 GB, 2 OFL), 3 of them in one patient; OR 0.661 (0.201-2.174).
- Enamel-side marginal discoloration: significantly more with GB (OR 0.356, 95% CI 0.170-0.746, p = 0.006; incisal enamel side 72.7% vs 46.1%).
- Increased tooth sensitivity: GB 18.9% vs OFL 6.8% (OR 0.304, 0.122-0.759, p = 0.01); arose in the last 5 years and is attributed to gingival recession and recurring erosion/abrasion/abfraction.
- Secondary findings: more failures in the mandible than in the maxilla for both adhesives (p = 0.0058); OFL did better on sclerotic dentin than GB (p = 0.022, interaction p = 0.326); operator had no effect on retention (p = 0.94); GB lost more restorations in larger lesions (> 2.5 mm: 14 vs < 2.5 mm: 4; interaction p = 0.074).
- Multiple restorations per patient: with two per patient, retention was 86% (GB) vs 88% (OFL) and failure 65% vs 71%; with multiple per patient, retention was 80.6% vs 80.4%. Two patients with more than 10 restorations each had almost total failure after becoming medically compromised (chemotherapy/radiotherapy; xerostomia and reduced mobility).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Patient recall 63% (27 of 52 patients were 74 or older at the 14-year recall); failures from non-recalled patients were counted, which may lower success rates.
- Single center, two relatively inexperienced operators; in-house trials with more experienced operators and two restorations per patient reported higher 12-13-year results (retention 74-93%).
- Older Vanherle criteria rather than FDI criteria; microhybrid composite and one 1-step self-etch (a mild, pH about 2 adhesive) that are not current products; HEMA-free 1SEa results do not generalize to modern universal adhesives.
- Both groups are in the same patients, so the comparison is within patient but with multiple lesions per patient.

## 7. Glossary
- **1SEa**: 1-step self-etch adhesive (G-Bond, HEMA-free, pH about 2).
- **3E&Ra**: 3-step etch-and-rinse adhesive (Optibond FL), gold-standard control.
- **GEE**: generalized estimating equations, used for clustered lesions.
- **Vanherle criteria**: older clinical evaluation criteria used instead of FDI.
