## Why Ingested

Fills the niche of CA in complex malocclusions (severe crowding, extraction cases, large rotations/torque demands) with both occlusal outcomes (PAR) and OHIP-14 — directly addressing the gap that most PRO studies exclude complex cases. → [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/hamid-2026-complex-cases-aligners-ohip14]]

## Three-line Summary

Prospective comparative study (n=60; 30 CA, 30 FA) in complex malocclusions showing equivalent PAR reduction between modalities; fixed appliances had shorter treatment duration for extraction cases, but clear aligners showed significantly better OHIP-14 at 1 month (p<0.05) that converged by treatment completion. Complex cases with clear aligners require meticulous staging and high compliance.

## 1. Document Information

- **Type**: Prospective comparative cohort
- **Journal**: Bioinformation 22(3):1429-1432 (2026), DOI 10.6026/973206300221429
- **PMID/PMCID**: 42145383 / PMC13177106
- **Sample**: n=60 (30 CA, 30 FA), age 16–35y, complex malocclusion (≥2 of: severe crowding ≥6mm, extraction plan, large rotation ≥15° or torque ≥8°, deep/open bite, asymmetry); university orthodontic clinic

## 2. Key Findings

- PAR reduction: clinically significant and equivalent between CA and FA (p>0.05)
- Treatment duration: FA significantly shorter (p<0.05) — especially in extraction-heavy cases
- Clinical visits: fewer with CA (remote monitoring potential)
- Refinement stages: more with CA
- OHIP-14 at 1 month: CA significantly better (p<0.05)
- OHIP-14 at completion: no significant difference (converged)
- Extraction space closure and root control: FA advantage

## 3. Methodology

Prospective comparative; university clinic. Complex malocclusion defined by ≥2 of 5 criteria. CA group: attachments, elastics, and refinements permitted. FA group: preadjusted edgewise. Primary outcome PAR; secondary OHIP-14, duration, visits, refinements. Independent t-test or Mann-Whitney; chi-square; p<0.05.

## 4. Clinical Implications

Clear aligners are viable for carefully selected complex cases, but clinicians should set expectations: more refinement stages required, longer overall time for extraction cases, and high patient compliance is non-negotiable. The superior early OHIP-14 advantage may be a deciding factor for aesthetics-motivated complex patients.

## 5. Limitations

No randomization (allocation method unclear); relatively small n=60; extraction vs non-extraction not stratified in analysis; treatment duration specifics not fully reported; single institution; PAR examiner blinding not specified.

## 7. Glossary

- **PAR index**: Peer Assessment Rating — validated occlusal outcome index; reduction % indicates treatment success (>70% = great improvement)
- **Complex malocclusion**: multi-feature difficulty requiring advanced biomechanics (extraction, torque, deep/open bite)
- **Refinement stage**: additional aligner set ordered after initial series to complete treatment
