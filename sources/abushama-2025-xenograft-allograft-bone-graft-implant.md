## Why Ingested

기존 [[bone-regeneration/rao-2026-allogeneic-bone-plug-versus-dbbm-gbr-pilot-rct]]는 단일 파일럿 RCT로 동종골(allograft) plug와 DBBM(xenograft)이 GBR에서 통계적으로 동등하다고 보고했다. 본 논문(Abushama 2025)은 12개 연구(즉시식립·지연식립·ARP·상악동거상 전반)를 narrative synthesis로 종합해 같은 결론(양 재료 임상적으로 상호교환 가능)을 더 넓은 적응증 범위에서 재확인하므로, 단일 RCT 근거를 SR 수준으로 보강하기 위해 인제스트.

## Three-line Summary

PROSPERO-registered systematic review (PRISMA; RoB2/ROBINS-I) of 12 comparative studies (n=395 patients) evaluating xenograft (bovine/porcine, e.g. Bio-Oss) versus allograft (e.g. FFB, DFDBA) bone grafts across immediate implant (6 studies), delayed implant (2), alveolar ridge preservation (3), and sinus augmentation (1) contexts.

No consistent statistically significant differences emerged between xenograft and allograft on marginal bone level, implant survival/stability, gingival/plaque indices, or bone dimensional change; one study found allograft had higher newly formed bone (65% vs 45%, p<0.05) while another found the opposite pattern for residual graft particles, and meta-analysis was not feasible due to study heterogeneity.

Narrative-only synthesis (no pooled meta-analysis) across only 12 studies with substantial clinical/methodological heterogeneity and moderate-to-serious risk of bias (especially confounding in non-randomized studies) — findings support that both graft classes are broadly interchangeable but do not establish superiority in any specific clinical scenario.

## 세줄요약

PROSPERO 등록 체계적 문헌고찰(PRISMA; RoB2/ROBINS-I)로, 즉시식립(6편)·지연식립(2편)·치조제보존(ARP, 3편)·상악동거상(1편) 전반에 걸쳐 이종골(bovine/porcine, 예: Bio-Oss) vs 동종골(예: FFB, DFDBA)을 비교한 12개 비교연구(환자 395명)를 종합.

변연골수준, 임플란트 생존율/안정성, 치은/치태지수, 골 차원변화 등에서 이종골·동종골 간 일관된 유의차 없음; 한 연구는 동종골이 신생골형성 우세(65% vs 45%, p<0.05)를 보고했으나 다른 연구는 잔존 이식재 비율에서 반대 양상을 보였고, 이질성으로 인해 메타분석은 불가능했음.

메타분석 없는 narrative synthesis 수준(12편, 상당한 임상적·방법론적 이질성, 비무작위 연구의 교란요인 등 중등도-심각한 비뚤림 위험)으로 — 두 재료 모두 임상적으로 상호교환 가능함을 시사하나 특정 임상 상황에서의 우월성은 입증하지 못함.

- **Title**: Comparison of xenograft and allograft bone graft for oral and maxillofacial surgical preparation prior to dental implantation: A systematic review
- **Authors**: Abushama AA, Alim N, AlTuraiki AM, AlQahtani TT, Alotaibi NT, AlQahtani MM, AlQahtani NM
- **Journal**: F1000Research, 2025; 14:718
- **DOI**: 10.12688/f1000research.163924.1

- **Registration**: PROSPERO-registered SR; PRISMA-guided; no ethical approval required (secondary literature synthesis)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Directly frames the PICO question of xenograft vs allograft (not vs autograft, which dominates prior literature) for implant site preparation across the full range of pre-implant procedures: immediate implant, delayed implant, ARP, and sinus augmentation.
- Compiles clinical (gingival/plaque index, probing depth), radiographic (marginal bone level, CT-based bone volume/resorption), and histological/histomorphometric (newly formed bone %, residual graft %) outcome domains side-by-side across the 12 included studies.
- Applies dual risk-of-bias tools (RoB 2 for RCTs, ROBINS-I for non-randomized studies) and reports domain-level bias patterns (confounding as the dominant concern in observational studies).
- Explicit conclusion that clinicians may treat xenografts (e.g. Bio-Oss) and allografts (e.g. PerioGlas-class synthetic allografts, FFB, DFDBA) as materially interchangeable GBR biomaterials, shifting material choice to non-outcome considerations (cost, availability, patient preference regarding animal-derived tissue).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: PRISMA-guided systematic review, PROSPERO-registered; PICO — P: adult (≥18y) patients undergoing oral/maxillofacial implant site preparation; I: xenograft bone graft; C: allograft bone graft; O: clinical/radiographic/histologic success (marginal bone level, implant survival, newly formed bone %).
- **Search**: Google Scholar, MEDLINE/PubMed, Cochrane Database, searched to January 2024; English-language, human studies, publications from the prior ~8 years (since ~2016); combined MeSH + free-text terms with Boolean operators.
- **Eligibility**: adults ≥18y; CCTs, cohort, case-control, and comparative studies with full datasets; excluded animal/tissue-culture studies, chemotherapy/radiotherapy/cancer patients, heavy smokers (>10 cigarettes/day), and reviews restricted to autograft comparisons.
- **Screening**: two independent reviewers (Abushama, Alim), title/abstract then full-text screening; disagreements resolved by discussion.
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB 2 (revised) for RCTs; ROBINS-I for non-randomized studies (7 domains, worst-domain-drives-overall-rating approach).
- **Synthesis**: narrative synthesis only — meta-analysis was not attempted/possible due to variability among included studies.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Study flow**: 330 electronic + 20 manual hits → 350 after dedup removal of duplicates/triplicates → 165 full-text reviewed → 12 studies included (6 immediate implant, 2 delayed implant, 3 ARP, 1 sinus augmentation).
- **Study characteristics**: 395 total patients; mean age 40.46y; 9/12 (75%) university-based; graft distribution 58.1% bovine bone blocks (xenograft) vs 41.9% allograft bone blocks; sex reporting missing in 50% of studies (reported sex skewed female, 29.9% M / 70.1% F where reported).
- **Clinical indices**: gingival index at 1 year comparable between groups (allograft 0.86±0.31 vs xenograft 0.82±0.12 at full-mouth; 0.94±0.21 vs 0.80±0.26 at implant site); probing depth and plaque index declined similarly in both arms over 12 months with no statistically significant intergroup difference (P>0.05).
- **Marginal bone level / resorption**: pre-op crestal bone loss comparable (xenograft −1.85±1.26 mm vs allograft −1.75±1.51 mm, P=0.791); one RCT reported allograft resorption slightly higher than xenograft at 3 months (0.9±0.52 mm) with no significant between-group difference by 6–8 months (P>0.5); one implant-survival study (Xavier SP) found FFB 97.8% vs BBM 100% survival (P=0.352), but significantly different bone-volume resorption rates (FFB 31.2% vs BBM 12.22% median, P=0.009).
- **Histomorphometry**: mixed pattern — one study found allograft newly-formed-bone mean 65% (22–86%) vs xenograft 45% (13–67%), significantly higher for allograft (P<0.05), with correspondingly lower residual graft particles for allograft (27% vs 41%); another study found the opposite (new bone 25.6±10.1% allograft vs 35.3±16.8% xenograft; residual particles 33.9±9.4% allograft vs 22.2±13.4% xenograft) — underscoring the heterogeneity that precluded meta-analysis.
- **Implant stability**: no statistically significant difference between xenograft (188.6±22.5) and allograft (191.5±18.2) implant stability values in one study.
- **Ridge dimension change**: comparable horizontal/vertical shrinkage between groups across studies (e.g. horizontal change −1.4 mm allograft vs −2.6 mm xenograft in one study); one RCT found DBBM+collagen membrane produced significantly greater horizontal socket preservation than DFDBA+another membrane (+1.76 mm, P=0.0256) — a membrane/material combination effect rather than a pure graft-type effect.
- **Risk of bias**: RCTs mostly low-to-moderate risk on RoB 2 (one high-risk domain flagged); non-randomized studies showed more serious concerns on ROBINS-I, with confounding as the most consistently problematic domain.
- **Bottom line (authors' conclusion)**: xenografts (e.g. Bio-Oss) and synthetic/allogeneic alternatives (e.g. PerioGlas-class allografts) are "equally effective and reliable GBR biomaterials" for dental implant surgery preparation, regardless of augmentation procedure (ARP, sinus, immediate implant) — material choice can be guided by non-efficacy factors.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 12 studies met inclusion criteria, and meta-analysis was not possible due to clinical/methodological heterogeneity (different indications pooled: immediate implant, delayed implant, ARP, sinus augmentation, treated as one "xenograft vs allograft" question).
- Substantial risk-of-bias concerns, particularly confounding in the non-randomized studies (ROBINS-I), and one high-risk domain among the RCTs (RoB 2).
- Patient sex was unreported in 50% of studies and surgical site was unspecified in 41.7% of studies, precluding subgroup/site-specific conclusions.
- Small sample sizes within individual studies; the authors explicitly call for larger, more homogeneous, adequately powered studies, and highlight the anterior/esthetic zone (buccal wall dehiscence defects) as an under-studied but clinically important scenario for material choice.
- As a narrative-synthesis-only SR (no pooled effect estimates, no GRADE certainty rating reported), the strength of the "interchangeable" conclusion is lower than a quantitative meta-analysis would provide.

## 7. Glossary

- **Xenograft**: graft material derived from a non-human species (e.g. bovine — Bio-Oss; porcine — Gen-Os), processed to remove organic components while retaining the mineral (hydroxyapatite) scaffold.
- **Allograft**: graft material derived from human donor tissue (not the patient), e.g. freeze-dried bone allograft (FDBA), demineralized freeze-dried bone allograft (DFDBA), fresh-frozen bone (FFB), acellular dermal matrix (ADM).
- **BBM**: bovine bone mineral (a xenograft, e.g. Bio-Oss).
- **FFB**: fresh-frozen bone (an allograft).
- **DFDBA**: demineralized freeze-dried bone allograft.
- **Jumping gap / jumping distance**: the residual space between an immediately placed implant and the surrounding native bone wall, typically requiring graft material when >2 mm.
- **RoB 2 / ROBINS-I**: Cochrane risk-of-bias tools for randomized trials and non-randomized studies of interventions, respectively.
