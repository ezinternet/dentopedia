## Why Ingested

This RCT isolates connective tissue graft (CTG) as a variable within the immediate-implant-plus-immediate-provisionalization protocol, quantifying both its benefits (buccal tissue collapse reduction) and limitations (no effect on mid-facial gingival margin stability). The finding that CTG prevents facial collapse but not gingival recession adds a nuanced outcome to the immediate-implant esthetic pathway. Reinforces and refines [[wiki/immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chan-2019-randomized-controlled-trial-compare-aesthetic]] (both RCTs on immediate placement + provisionalization) and [[wiki/immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/de-angelis-2021-immediate-implant-provisionalization-soft-tissue-retrospective]] (CTG outcomes in similar context).

## Three-line Summary

Randomized controlled trial of 42 participants (40 completed) receiving immediately placed and provisionalized maxillary incisors with or without simultaneous connective tissue graft (CTG), comparing hard and soft tissue changes over 6 months using 3D CBCT superimposition.

The test group (with CTG) showed significantly less buccal tissue collapse in the zone 2–5 mm apical to the gingival margin; however, mid-facial gingival margin migration and radiographic new bone formation occurred similarly in both groups without significant differences.

CTG can partially compensate for facial tissue collapse in immediate implant + provisionalization, but does not improve maintenance of mid-facial gingival margin position during the 6-month healing phase—new bone formation occurs regardless of CTG.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Quantifies the isolated effect of simultaneous CTG within immediate implant + immediate provisionalization using 3D volumetric analysis (CBCT superimposition)
- Demonstrates CTG efficacy for buccal tissue collapse in the early healing zone (2–5 mm apical to margin) but neutral effect on gingival recession
- Highlights that radiographic new bone formation occurs reliably in both test and control groups, independent of CTG use
- Refines clinical expectations: CTG partially achieves esthetic stability (buccal contour) but not papillar/marginal stability in a 6-month horizon

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Randomized controlled trial (parallel groups)
- **Population**: Single unsalvageable maxillary incisors (esthetic zone)
- **Sample**: 42 participants randomized; 40 completed (2 lost to follow-up)
- **Test Intervention**: Immediate implant placement + immediate provisionalization + simultaneous connective tissue graft (CTG)
- **Control Intervention**: Immediate implant placement + immediate provisionalization without CTG
- **Primary Outcome Measures**:
  - Mid-facial gingival margin migration (apico-palatal direction; mm)
  - Soft tissue contour changes (buccal tissue collapse quantification)
  - Hard tissue remodeling (socket void new bone formation)
- **Imaging**: Digital impressions and cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) at baseline (before extraction) and 6-month follow-up
- **Analysis Method**: 3D superimposition of CBCT images (volumetric analysis)
- **Follow-up Duration**: 6 months

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Test Group (with CTG) | Control Group (without CTG) | P-value / Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buccal tissue collapse (2–5 mm apical) | Significantly less | Greater collapse | Significant difference |
| Mid-facial gingival margin migration | Apico-palatal direction | Apico-palatal direction | No significant difference |
| Socket void new bone formation | Present (except bucco-coronal triangle) | Present (except bucco-coronal triangle) | No significant difference |

### Key Findings:
- **Buccal tissue collapse reduction**: The test group (CTG) showed **significantly less** buccal tissue collapse in the area 2–5 mm apical to the gingival margin compared to controls.
- **Gingival margin recession**: In **both groups**, the mid-facial gingival margin migrated in an apico-palatal direction during 6 months—no protection from CTG.
- **New bone formation**: Radiographic evidence of new bone formation was observed in most areas of the socket void in both groups, except for a triangular space in the bucco-coronal region. No significant differences between groups.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **Follow-up duration**: 6 months is the early healing phase; longer follow-up needed to assess long-term gingival position stability and whether the tissue collapse differential persists.
- **Single-tooth esthetic zone only**: Results apply to maxillary anterior single incisors; generalizability to other sites (posterior, multi-tooth) not established.
- **CTG source/technique not detailed**: Specific CTG procurement (autograft vs allograft), thickness, and placement technique not fully specified in abstract.
- **Gingival recession despite CTG**: The lack of protective effect on mid-facial gingival margin position may indicate that CTG compensates for facial volume loss but cannot prevent resorption-driven marginal recession—different mechanisms.
- **Sample size and power**: 40 completions is moderate; no power analysis reported in abstract.
- **No data on papilla or WES**: Clinical esthetic scores (pink esthetic score, white esthetic score) not mentioned.

## 7. Glossary
- **Connective Tissue Graft (CTG)**: Subepithelial autograft harvested from palate and placed simultaneously with implant placement to augment soft tissue volume and contour.
- **Mid-facial gingival margin**: The edge of the free gingiva in the buccal/vestibular midline of the tooth or implant—critical esthetic landmark.
- **Apico-palatal migration**: Apical (downward/away from crown) and palatal (toward palate/inward) movement of the gingival margin, reflecting recession and resorption.
- **Buccal tissue collapse**: Loss of buccal soft tissue volume and contour in the facial-vestibular area, contributing to esthetic and functional deficits.
- **Socket void**: The space remaining in the alveolar socket after tooth extraction and before implant placement or healing; typically refilled by bone or graft.
- **3D Superimposition**: Digital overlay and registration of baseline and follow-up 3D images (e.g., CBCT) to quantify volumetric/dimensional changes at the tissue level.
