## Why Ingested

[[overviews/treatment-planning-decision-variability-overview]] turns on whether tooth-preserving alternatives actually perform well enough to make an extraction "unnecessary" — a claim that rested on intentional-replantation figures from [[endodontics/zhang-2025-intentional-replantation-periapical-periodontitis-prognosis-sr-ma]] with no autotransplantation counterpart in the wiki. This SR+MA supplies that missing pooled number (survival 95%, success 92%, 1,473 mature teeth) **and the caveat that keeps it honest**: it is a single-arm synthesis that never compares autotransplantation to implants, so it cannot rank the options it is being used to weigh.

Its secondary finding refines the endodontic timing question in a way no held paper covered — RCT timing does not move survival or success, but postoperative prophylactic RCT shows the lowest root resorption (11% vs 40%/40%, P=0.09). Scope is closed-apex only, which is why it does not transfer to [[endodontics/regenerative/alqedairi-2026-regenerative-endodontic-procedures-immature]], and it extends the PDL-viability logic of [[dental-trauma/debrier-2020-storage-avulsed-tooth-prior]] from avulsion storage to elective transplantation.

## Three-line Summary

PRISMA systematic review and meta-analysis (24 studies, 1967–2025, 11 countries, 1,473 autotransplanted teeth with complete root formation; no RCTs — 12 prospective and 12 retrospective cohorts) pooling survival, success, and root resorption rates overall and across three root canal treatment (RCT) timing strategies: postoperative prophylactic (15 studies, 879 teeth), intraoperative immediate (3 studies, 129 teeth), and clinical observation-based (7 studies, 434 teeth).

Overall pooled survival was 95% (95% CI 91–97%, I²=0%) and success was 92% (95% CI 80–97%, I²=68%); root resorption was 16% (95% CI 8–30%, I²=90%). RCT timing did not significantly affect survival or success (all pairwise P>0.05), but the postoperative prophylactic group had the lowest root resorption rate — 11% (95% CI 6–18%) — versus 40% in both the intraoperative immediate and clinical observation-based groups (prophylactic vs immediate, P=0.09, approaching significance).

This is a single-arm pooled estimate of autotransplantation outcomes — it does not compare autotransplantation against dental implants or any other treatment, and the RCT-timing conclusion is explicitly hedged by the authors as needing verification (non-randomized designs only, high heterogeneity). Scope is restricted to teeth with complete (closed-apex) root formation; immature/open-apex autotransplantation is a different population with different biology (pulp revascularization potential) and is outside this review.

## 세줄요약

PRISMA 체계적 문헌고찰·메타분석(SR+MA)로, 완전 치근형성(complete root formation) 자가치아이식(Autotransplantation) 치아 1,473개(24편, 1967–2025년, 11개국; RCT 없음 — 전향적 12편/후향적 12편)를 대상으로 근관치료(Root Canal Treatment, RCT) 시기 3가지 전략(술후 예방적, 발치 중 즉시, 임상관찰 기반)별 생존율·성공률·치근흡수율을 종합했다.

전체 생존율 95%(95% CI 91–97%, I²=0%), 성공률 92%(95% CI 80–97%, I²=68%), 치근흡수율 16%(95% CI 8–30%, I²=90%). RCT 시기는 생존율·성공률에 유의한 영향이 없었으나(모두 P>0.05), 술후 예방적 RCT군의 치근흡수율이 가장 낮았다 — 11%(95% CI 6–18%) vs 발치중 즉시·임상관찰군 모두 40%(예방적 vs 즉시 비교 P=0.09, 유의수준에 근접).

이 수치는 자가이식 단독의 단일군(single-arm) 풀링 추정치이며 — 임플란트나 다른 치료법과 직접 비교하지 않았고, RCT 시기에 대한 결론도 저자들이 명시적으로 "추가 검증 필요"라 단서를 단다(비무작위 연구설계만 포함, 높은 이질성). 적용 범위는 완전(폐쇄형 치근단) 치근형성 치아로 국한되며, 미성숙(개방 치근단) 자가이식은 생물학(치수 재혈관화 가능성)이 다른 별개 집단으로 이 리뷰의 범위 밖이다.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-08413-4); PMID 42032726; PMCID PMC13281487), this 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis (Li, Chen, Li, Ge; *BMC Oral Health* 26(1), PROSPERO CRD420251275391) is the first to systematically pool the relationship between root canal treatment (RCT) timing and prognosis for autotransplanted teeth with complete (mature, closed-apex) root formation.

**The number a treatment planner actually needs is the overall one.** Pooled across 24 non-randomized cohort studies (1,473 teeth), autotransplanted mature teeth showed 95% survival (95% CI 91–97%) and 92% success (95% CI 80–97%) — this review is the wiki's only pooled estimate of that figure. But it must be read with an explicit caveat the authors do not soften: this is a single-arm synthesis of autotransplantation outcomes alone. The review does **not** compare autotransplantation to dental implants, intentional replantation, or any other tooth-replacement/tooth-preservation option — there is no head-to-head or indirect comparative arm. The 95%/92% figures describe what happens to autotransplanted teeth, not how autotransplantation ranks against alternatives; "comparable to implants" is a claim made in the paper's *introduction* (citing prior literature, not this review's own data) and should not be read as this review's finding.

**The paper's actual research question is narrower: when should RCT be performed?** Three timing strategies were compared indirectly (most included studies did not randomize or directly compare timings within the same cohort): postoperative prophylactic RCT (initiated ~2 weeks post-transplant, obturated by 3–4 weeks; 15 studies/879 teeth), intraoperative immediate RCT (completed extraorally before replantation; 3 studies/129 teeth), and clinical observation-based RCT (treatment only on symptoms/radiographic signs; 7 studies/434 teeth). Timing made **no significant difference to survival or success** (all pairwise P>0.05 — prophylactic vs. immediate P=0.83; immediate vs. observation P=0.44; prophylactic vs. observation P=0.47 for survival). It **did** show a pattern for root resorption: prophylactic postoperative RCT had the lowest rate at 11% (95% CI 6–18%), versus 40% (95% CI 9–83%) in the immediate group and 40% (95% CI 5–89%) in the observation group — though only the prophylactic-vs-immediate comparison approached significance (P=0.09), and the immediate/observation subgroups were built from very few studies (3 and 2, respectively) with very wide, overlapping CIs. The authors' own framing is appropriately hedged: prophylactic RCT "may confer potential advantages" — suggestive, not established.

**Scope limit: complete root formation only.** This review explicitly excluded autotransplanted teeth with incomplete (open-apex) root formation — the classic candidate for pulp revascularization in adolescents, where regenerative endodontic procedures (REP) rather than routine RCT are the norm. Nothing in this review's prophylactic-RCT-reduces-resorption signal should be carried over to open-apex autotransplantation; that population has fundamentally different pulp/vascular biology (see [[endodontics/regenerative/alqedairi-2026-regenerative-endodontic-procedures-immature]]).

**Root resorption and follow-up duration.** The overall 16% root resorption figure (8–30% CI, from 16 studies/833 teeth) is not a small number for a failure mode that eventually costs the tooth, and it deserves its follow-up-duration context. A separate subgroup analysis stratified by follow-up length found: resorption 6% (95% CI 1–22%) at ≤3 years, 30% (95% CI 11–60%) at ≤5 years, and 16% (95% CI 12–22%, lower heterogeneity I²=46%) at >5 years — with the study authors reporting this subgroup's own "overall" resorption rate as 17% (95% CI 8–33%), a figure that does not exactly match the 16% headline number from the main root-resorption analysis; this reflects the different, partially overlapping sets of studies reporting follow-up duration versus reporting resorption at all, not a correction of one number by the other — reproduced here as the source presents it, not reconciled. Success rate showed a similar pattern by follow-up length: 99% (one study, ≤3y), 77% (≤5y), 80% (>5y). The practical reading: resorption risk does not simply accumulate monotonically with time in the pooled data (the ≤5-year bucket is paradoxically worse than the >5-year bucket), which the heterogeneity statistics (I²=89–92% in the shorter buckets) suggest is more a signal of inconsistent follow-up/reporting across studies than a true time-course of disease.

**Bottom line for the wiki.** Autotransplantation of a mature tooth has a wiki-documented ~95% survival / 92% success pooled outcome — a genuinely useful planning number, but not a number that has been tested against implants. Root canal timing is close to a non-issue for survival/success, but doing the RCT on a postoperative prophylactic schedule (rather than intraoperatively or by watchful waiting) plausibly — not definitively — lowers root resorption risk. None of this applies to immature/open-apex donor teeth.

## Key Contributions

- **First SR+MA specifically on RCT timing and autotransplantation prognosis** — prior reviews established autotransplantation survival/success generally; this is the first to isolate the timing-of-endodontic-intervention variable.
- **Quantifies the general planning number**: pooled survival 95% (91–97%), success 92% (80–97%) across 24 studies/1,473 teeth with complete root formation — consistent with prior estimates (Huang 2023: 98.0% 1-year / 90.5% 5-year survival; Yang 2025 third-molar SR+MA: 96% survival).
- **Resolves (tentatively) the timing-vs-outcome question**: no significant survival/success difference by RCT timing, but postoperative prophylactic RCT shows the lowest root resorption (11% vs. 40%/40%).
- **Explains the mechanism proposed for the resorption signal**: prophylactic timing (~2 weeks post-op) allows an initial window for periodontal ligament (PDL) healing before RCT intervention, avoiding both premature mechanical disturbance and delayed inflammatory root resorption from pulp necrosis; immediate intraoperative RCT prolongs extraoral/desiccation time, which may reduce PDL cell viability.
- **Documents a historical shift in clinical practice**: from balanced use of all three timing strategies (1967–1986) to prophylactic-RCT dominance (83% of studies, 1991–2018) to a recent (2021–2025) partial swing back toward observation-based RCT (42.9%), attributed to better preoperative imaging (CBCT), Doppler monitoring, and re-evaluation of mature-tooth pulp regenerative potential.
- **Flags its own evidentiary ceiling clearly**: zero RCTs among the 24 included studies; 16 of 24 at moderate risk of bias and 2 at high risk (NOS); high heterogeneity for success (I²=68%) and resorption (I²=90%) outcomes.

## Methodology

- **Design**: PRISMA-guided systematic review and meta-analysis; PROSPERO-registered (CRD420251275391).
- **PICO**: Population = patients undergoing tooth autotransplantation; Intervention/Comparison = different RCT timings in autotransplanted teeth with complete root formation (indirect/concurrent comparison); Outcome = survival rate (primary), success rate and root resorption rate (secondary).
- **Search**: Embase, PubMed, Science Direct, Web of Science, database inception to 2025-09-06; 1,082 records → 44 full-text reviewed → 24 included.
- **Inclusion**: primary human studies with inferable survival/success/resorption data, complete root formation, English language. Excluded: incomplete root formation, expert opinion/reviews, animal/in vitro studies, non-English.
- **Included studies**: 24, published 1967–2025, 11 countries; all non-controlled (no RCTs) — 12 prospective cohort, 12 retrospective cohort; sample sizes 17–319 patients (mean 65.3); 1,473 total autotransplanted teeth with complete root formation (15 studies/879 teeth postoperative-prophylactic RCT; 3 studies/129 teeth intraoperative-immediate RCT; 7 studies/434 teeth observation-based RCT).
- **Quality**: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS), max 8 points; 8 studies rated high quality (≥6 points), 16 at moderate/low quality; overall risk-of-bias profile: 16 studies moderate risk, 2 high risk.
- **Statistics**: R v4.5.1; logit-transformed pooled proportions, continuity correction for zero-event studies, random-effects model; subgroup analyses (exploratory only, not confirmatory) by RCT timing, tooth type, follow-up duration, and fixation method; forest plots with 95% CI; I² heterogeneity; α=0.05. Intergroup RCT-timing comparisons were indirect (inter-study), since almost no included study directly compared timings within the same cohort.
