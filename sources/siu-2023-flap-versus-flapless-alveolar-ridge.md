## Why Ingested
User requested evidence on whether periosteal elevation (flap raising) costs the alveolar bone its blood supply and compromises healing — this single-blinded human RCT is the direct clinical test of that hypothesis in alveolar ridge preservation (ARP), randomizing flapless vs flap ARP with 4-month clinical and histological re-entry outcomes. It carries the question posed in the dog model of [[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/araujo-2009-ridge-alterations-flap-vs-flapless]] into grafted human ARP and adds a prospective RCT data point beyond the search window of the flap-management meta-analysis [[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/lee-2018-flap-management-alveolar-ridge-preservation]].

## Three-line Summary
Single-blinded RCT (n=24, randomized to flapless vs flap alveolar ridge preservation) testing whether preserving the periosteal blood supply limits crestal ridge width/height loss; sockets in both groups grafted with demineralized bone matrix + mineralized particulate allograft under a barrier, re-entry at 4 months with histology.
Both groups lost significant ridge width (flapless 1.3±0.9 mm from 8.3±1.3 to 7.0±1.9 mm; flap 1.0±1.1 mm from 8.5±1.5 to 7.5±1.5 mm; each p<0.05) and midbuccal height (flapless 0.5±0.9 mm vs flap 0.9±1.3 mm; each p<0.05) with NO statistically significant between-group difference; flapless showed more vital mineralized tissue (44±10%, between-group p>0.05) and significantly thicker occlusal soft tissue at re-entry (p<0.05).
Periosteal blood-supply preservation does not translate into measurably better ridge dimensional outcomes in ARP — the flapless benefit appears in soft-tissue thickness and (non-significantly) histological tissue quality; abstract-only evidence, small n split between two groups, single-blinded design.

## 세줄요약
단일눈가림 무작위대조시험 (RCT, n=24): 치조제보존술 (Alveolar Ridge Preservation, ARP)에서 무절개 (Flapless) vs 절개 (Flap) 기법을 무작위 배정해, 골막 혈행 (Periosteal Blood Supply) 보존이 치조정 (crestal) 폭·높이 소실을 줄이는지 검증 — 양군 모두 탈회골기질 (Demineralized Bone Matrix, DBM) + 무기질 입자 동종이식골로 이식 후 차단막으로 피개, 4개월 재입술(re-entry) 시 조직학 검체 채취.
양군 모두 유의한 치조정 폭 소실 (무절개 1.3±0.9 mm, 8.3±1.3→7.0±1.9 mm; 절개 1.0±1.1 mm, 8.5±1.5→7.5±1.5 mm; 각 p<0.05)과 협측 수직 소실 (무절개 0.5±0.9 mm vs 절개 0.9±1.3 mm, 각 p<0.05)을 보였으나 군 간 차이는 통계적으로 유의하지 않았고, 무절개군은 생존 무기질화 조직 (Vital Mineralized Tissue)이 44±10%로 더 많았으나(군 간 p>0.05) 교합면 연조직 두께만 유의하게 두꺼웠다 (p<0.05).
골막 혈행 보존은 ARP의 치조골 크기(dimension) 유지로 이어지지 않음 — 무절개 ARP의 이점은 크기가 아닌 연조직 두께·조직학적 질에서 나타남; 초록(abstract)만 확보된 근거라는 점, n=24 소표본·단일눈가림 설계가 한계.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Directly tests the periosteal blood-supply-preservation hypothesis in ARP by randomizing flapless vs flap technique as the sole variable, with both clinical (dimensional) and histological endpoints at 4-month re-entry.
- Reports dimensional (crestal ridge width, midbuccal vertical change), histological (vital mineralized tissue %), and soft-tissue (occlusal thickness) outcomes for the same comparison in one trial.
- Shows that preserving the periosteal blood supply did NOT produce significantly better ridge dimensional outcomes — any flapless benefit appears in occlusal soft-tissue thickness and (non-significantly) in vital mineralized tissue content.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: single-blinded, randomized controlled trial; parallel allocation to flapless vs flap ARP
- **n**: 24 patients, randomly assigned between the two techniques (per-arm n not stated in the abstract)
- **Grafting protocol**: sockets grafted with demineralized bone matrix and mineralized particulate allograft, covered with a barrier in both groups — flap management was the only between-group variable
- **Re-entry**: performed at 4 months to obtain samples for histological analysis and for subsequent implant placement
- **Outcomes**: crestal ridge width change; midbuccal vertical change; % vital mineralized tissue (histology); occlusal soft-tissue thickness at re-entry

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Flapless ARP | Flap ARP | Between-group |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crestal ridge width, baseline | 8.3±1.3 mm | 8.5±1.5 mm | — |
| Crestal ridge width, 4 months | 7.0±1.9 mm | 7.5±1.5 mm | — |
| Crestal ridge width loss | 1.3±0.9 mm (p<0.05 within group) | 1.0±1.1 mm (p<0.05 within group) | NS |
| Midbuccal vertical change | 0.5±0.9 mm loss (p<0.05 within group) | 0.9±1.3 mm loss (p<0.05 within group) | NS |
| Vital mineralized tissue (histology) | 44±10% | value not stated in abstract | NS (p>0.05) |
| Occlusal soft-tissue thickness at 4 months | thicker | thinner | p<0.05 (flapless greater) |

- Both arms lost significant ridge width and midbuccal height over 4 months (each p<0.05 within group), but the between-group difference was not statistically significant for any dimensional outcome.
- Direction of the (non-significant) dimensional differences was mixed: flapless lost slightly more crestal width (1.3 vs 1.0 mm) but slightly less midbuccal vertical height (0.5 vs 0.9 mm).
- Flapless ARP revealed more vital mineralized tissue (44±10%) than the flap group, but this histological difference was not statistically significant (p>0.05).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **Abstract-only**: full text not retrieved (paywalled Journal of Periodontology 2023; no PMC record) — the flap group's vital mineralized tissue %, occlusal soft-tissue mm values, randomization/blinding mechanics, and per-arm n are not available from the abstract.
- **Small sample**: n=24 split between two groups (~12 per arm), limiting power to detect between-group differences; a true modest dimensional benefit of flaplessness cannot be excluded.
- **Single-blinded**: outcome assessment was blinded, but the surgeon could not be blinded to technique.
- **Single 4-month re-entry**: no earlier/later timepoints, so healing dynamics between 0 and 4 months are unresolved; conclusions on crestal ridge width, height, and % vital mineralized bone rest on one endpoint time.

## 6. Related Work
- **Lee et al. 2018** ([[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/lee-2018-flap-management-alveolar-ridge-preservation]]): SR+MA of 11 studies isolating flap management in ARP — bone width/height NS between flapped and flapless; this 2023 RCT, published after that review's search window, independently reproduces the null dimensional finding.
- **Araújo & Lindhe 2009** ([[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/araujo-2009-ridge-alterations-flap-vs-flapless]]): dog experiment showing no ridge-dimensional difference between flap and flapless extraction without grafting; this trial extends that question to grafted ARP in humans.
- **Kim et al. 2009** ([[implants/soft-tissue/kim-2009-blood-vessels-peri-implant-mucosa]]): canine histomorphometry showing flapless surgery preserves periosteal vascularity and yields denser peri-implant mucosal vessels — the vascular mechanism this trial's flapless arm relied on.
- **Chrcanovic et al. 2014** ([[implants/chrcanovic-2014-flapless-vs-conventional-implant-surgery-ma]]): implant-surgery analogue — flapless vs flapped implant placement showed no marginal-bone-loss difference.
- **Tarpara et al. 2025** ([[implants/tarpara-2025-flapless-flapped-clinical-outcomes-cohort]]): flapless implant placement cohort — early comfort and soft-tissue benefits without long-term crestal-bone difference, mirroring this trial's soft-tissue-not-bone pattern.

## 7. Glossary
- **Alveolar Ridge Preservation (ARP)**: grafting/sealing of an extraction socket at extraction time to limit post-extraction ridge resorption.
- **Periosteal Blood Supply**: the vascular network on the outer bone surface; elevated (stripped from bone) when a full-thickness flap is raised, theoretically compromising cortical perfusion.
- **Flapless technique**: socket access without raising a mucoperiosteal flap, leaving the periosteal attachment and blood supply intact.
- **Demineralized Bone Matrix (DBM)**: allograft bone matrix with mineral removed, retaining osteoinductive collagen proteins; here combined with mineralized particulate allograft.
- **Vital Mineralized Tissue**: histomorphometric fraction of newly formed living mineralized bone in the graft site at re-entry.
- **Midbuccal Vertical Change**: vertical height change of the buccal crest midpoint, the key vertical dimensional endpoint of ARP trials.
