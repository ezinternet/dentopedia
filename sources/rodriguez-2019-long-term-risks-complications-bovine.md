## Why Ingested

Ingested as part of a targeted PMC full-text sweep on graft-material displacement/scattering within the maxillary sinus — a known but under-reported complication of lateral-window sinus augmentation with non-resorbable bovine xenograft. This case series is the original/foundational report from this author group directly cites and extends the non-resorption findings of [[wiki/sinus-lift/lateral/mordenfeld-2010-msfa-dpbb-biopsies-11year]] (deproteinized bovine bone particles unchanged at 11 years) into a clinical complications framework — encapsulation, sinus/maxillary bone pathoses, oroantral communication, implant failure, foreign body reaction — and pairs with the companion follow-up paper [[wiki/sinus-lift/lateral/nowzari-2022-migration-bovine-derived-xenograft-particles]] from the same author group (JISP), which focuses specifically on the migration subset of these complications.

## Three-line Summary

Case series (n=5-6 patients, private-practice referral cohort, 2 to 13 years post bovine-xenograft bone augmentation — mostly maxillary sinus lift and socket grafting) documenting late complications of anorganic bovine bone (ABB) substitutes in dental implant surgery.

Reported complications: sinus and maxillary bone pathoses, displacement/scattering of graft particles, oroantral communication, implant failure, foreign body reaction with encapsulation, chronic inflammation, soft-tissue fenestration, and bovine-bone-associated cysts; symptoms resolved after surgical removal of the graft material in every case.

Bovine-derived graft material is not biodegradable in humans (particles persist for 10-20+ years per cited histologic studies) and its removal — sometimes technically demanding due to migrated/scattered particle configurations — is the definitive management once late complications appear.

## 세줄요약

증례군 연구 (개인 클리닉 의뢰 환자 5-6명, 소 유래 이종골이식재 (Bovine-derived Xenograft) 식립 후 2-13년 경과 — 대부분 상악동거상술 및 발치와 이식) — 치과 임플란트 수술에서 탈단백우골 (Anorganic Bovine Bone, ABB) 대체재의 후기 합병증을 기술.

보고된 합병증: 상악동·상악골 병리, 이식재 입자의 전위/산란, 구강상악동 누공 (Oroantral Communication), 임플란트 실패, 이물 반응 (Foreign Body Reaction)과 피막형성 (Encapsulation), 만성 염증, 연조직 천공 (Soft-tissue Fenestration), 소 유래 골 관련 낭종 — 모든 증례에서 이식재 외과적 제거 후 증상 소실.

소 유래 이식재는 인체 내에서 생분해되지 않으며(인용된 조직학 연구에서 10-20년 이상 잔존 확인), 후기 합병증 발생 시 유일한 근본 치료는 외과적 제거이며 이동/산란된 입자 형태에 따라 고난도 술기가 필요할 수 있음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- One of the few dental-literature reports directly addressing **long-term (2-13 year) clinical complications** of bovine-derived xenograft (anorganic bovine bone, ABB) rather than its efficacy — the authors explicitly note that "negative" outcome literature on ABB is scarce and likely underpublished.
- Documents a full spectrum of late complications in a single consecutive case series: sinus/maxillary bone pathoses, graft particle displacement/scattering, oroantral communication, implant failure, foreign body reaction with encapsulation, chronic soft-tissue inflammation, gingival/soft-tissue fenestration, and ABB-associated cyst formation (the latter not previously reported in dental literature per the authors).
- First report (per the authors) of a **cystic lesion directly associated with bovine bone particles** — histopathology showed hyalinized cyst wall, chronic mixed inflammatory infiltrate (lymphocytes, plasma cells, occasional macrophages), and necrotic debris with cholesterol clefts, with no malignancy or specific microorganism identified.
- Synthesizes prior histologic evidence (Mordenfeld, Ayna, Traini) that deproteinized bovine bone particles persist essentially unresorbed in humans for 10-20+ years, framing this non-biodegradability as the underlying mechanism for the observed late complications.
- Raises (but does not resolve) the theoretical long-term risk of prion-related disease transmission (bovine spongiform encephalopathy / variant Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease), noting that manufacturing heat-treatment protocols (300 degC for Bio-Oss, 1100 degC for PepGen P-15) have not been proven to inactivate BSE prions, and that residual growth-factor/collagen proteins detected in "anorganic" bovine bone products raise doubt about the consistency of deproteinization.
- Frames the discussion around the "Proteus Phenomenon" (Ioannidis and Trikalinos) — the tendency of a research field to alternate between overstated positive claims and later contradicting negative findings — as a caution against uncritical adoption of xenograft literature.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective case series / case reports, 5 patients (numbered Case 1-4 and "Patient 6"/Case 5 in the original text — apparent numbering gap in the published article, but 5 distinct cases are described).
- **Population**: Patients referred to a private practice specifically because of bone-augmentation complications (referral-biased sample, not representative of general ABB outcomes).
  - Case 1: 60F, left lateral incisor extraction-site xenograft, complication at ~2 years (mobile encapsulated mass, soft-tissue inflammation/edema/pain).
  - Case 2: 52F, full-arch maxillary rehabilitation with 9 implants after bone augmentation, complication at 5 years (facial edema, migrating bovine bone particles, peri-implantitis; implants ultimately removed).
  - Case 3: 59M (angina, hypercholesterolemia, aortic valve stenosis, periodontal disease; multiple cardiac medications), sinus bone grafting, complication at 7 years (graft displacement with sinus/maxillary bone pathology and an associated cyst — histopathology detailed).
  - Case 4: 87M (hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, osteoarthritis, asthma, periodontitis), sinus elevation, complication at 13 years (bovine-bone-associated left maxillary sinusitis, 80% sinus opacification on CT, ipsilateral rhinitis).
  - Case 5 ("Patient 6"): 31F, right lateral incisor site graft + implant, complication at 3 years (particle migration, soft-tissue fenestration, color/texture change of overlying soft tissue).
- **Diagnostic workup**: Clinical exam/palpation, periapical radiographs, CT (Case 4), and histopathologic examination (Case 3: fibrous/granulation tissue, mixed chronic inflammatory infiltrate, occasional macrophages, hyalinized cyst wall, necrotic debris with cholesterol clefts; no malignancy, no specific organism).
- **Intervention**: Surgical exposure/debridement and removal of the bovine bone particles/encapsulated mass in every case, followed by autogenous reconstruction where indicated (autogenous bone block grafting, autogenous connective tissue grafting) rather than re-grafting with xenograft.
- **Ethics**: Written informed consent and HIPAA documentation obtained for all patients; local ethical approval obtained; patient identity anonymized.
- Discussion section synthesizes prior published evidence (non-systematic, narrative) on ABB non-resorption (Mordenfeld 2010/2011, Ayna, Traini), foreign body reaction histology, osteoclast-mediated particle degradation and residual-protein detection in "anorganic" bone products, fungus-ball association with Bio-Oss (Scolozzi), acute sinusitis incidence after xenograft sinus lift (Chirila, 4.3%), and implant-associated cystic lesions (Sivolella, McCrea — nasopalatine duct cysts, distinct from the bovine-bone-associated cyst reported here).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Complication spectrum across the series**: sinus and maxillary bone pathoses, displacement/scattering of graft particles, oroantral communication, implant failure, foreign body reaction, encapsulation, chronic inflammation, soft-tissue fenestration, and bovine-bone-associated cyst.
- **Latency to complication**: ranged from **2 to 13 years** after the original augmentation procedure (Case 1: ~2y; Case 5: 3y; Case 2: 5y; Case 3: 7y; Case 4: 13y) — underscoring that these are late, not early-postoperative, complications.
- **Case 4 (13-year sinusitis)**: CT showed 80% opacification of the affected maxillary sinus with mucous membrane thickening and ipsilateral nasal fossa/middle meatus involvement; the contralateral ostiomeatal unit was clear; symptoms improved significantly after surgical debridement.
- **Case 3 (7-year cyst)**: histopathology showed a well-hyalinized cyst wall with chronic mixed inflammatory infiltrate and necrotic/cholesterol-cleft debris; no malignancy detected — described by the authors as the first reported bovine-bone-associated cystic lesion in dental literature.
- **Universal outcome**: in all cases, removal of the bovine bone graft material resolved or substantially improved the associated symptoms (pain, edema, inflammation, fenestration, sinusitis).
- **Cited non-resorption timeline** (from prior literature, synthesized in Discussion): DPBB particles show no significant size change at 10-11 years (Mordenfeld), residual particles detected at 14 years (Ayna) and at 20 years (Traini) — supporting the paper's core claim that bovine bone xenograft is effectively non-biodegradable in humans.
- **Cited comparator complication rates**: acute maxillary sinusitis after xenograft(+autogenous) sinus lift reported at 4.3% (Chirila); fungus ball in the maxillary sinus associated with Bio-Oss in all 13 reported cases (Scolozzi), against a general chronic-sinusitis fungus-ball prevalence of 28.5%.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Retrospective case series with a small, referral-biased sample (patients specifically referred for complications) — cannot estimate true incidence/prevalence of late ABB complications in the general implant/sinus-lift population.
- No control group of asymptomatic ABB-grafted patients for comparison; denominator (total ABB-grafted patients from which these cases arose) is unknown.
- Mixed graft indications (extraction-socket grafting, sinus lift, full-arch augmentation) and heterogeneous follow-up intervals limit generalizable time-to-complication estimates.
- Prion-transmission risk (BSE/vCJD) is raised as a theoretical concern based on manufacturing heat-treatment data, not demonstrated as an actual clinical transmission event in this series.
- The authors explicitly call for **long-term prospective clinical evaluation** of xenograft biological complications, given the current literature's near-total focus on short/medium-term efficacy outcomes rather than late safety signals.
- Numbering inconsistency in the original published case labels (Case 5 patient is labeled "Patient 6" in the source text) — preserved here as published; does not affect the clinical content.

## 7. Glossary

- **Anorganic bovine bone (ABB) / deproteinized bovine bone (DPBB)**: bovine-derived bone mineral processed to remove the organic (protein/collagen) component, marketed as a non-resorbable xenogeneic bone graft scaffold (e.g., Bio-Oss, PepGen P-15).
- **Foreign body reaction**: chronic inflammatory host response to a persistent, non-degradable implanted material, histologically characterized by multinucleated giant cells, lymphocytes, plasma cells, and histiocytes surrounding the material.
- **Encapsulation**: fibrous connective-tissue walling-off of a foreign material by the host, isolating it from surrounding tissue without incorporation/resorption.
- **Oroantral communication**: an abnormal connection between the oral cavity and the maxillary sinus, here occurring as a late complication of sinus-region grafting.
- **Fungus ball**: a non-invasive fungal mass within a paranasal sinus, associated in prior literature with anorganic bovine bone grafting of the maxillary sinus.
- **Bovine spongiform encephalopathy (BSE) / variant Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (vCJD)**: prion-mediated transmissible spongiform encephalopathy in cattle and its human analog, raised here as a theoretical long-latency risk of bovine-derived graft materials.
- **Proteus Phenomenon**: term (Ioannidis and Trikalinos) describing a research field's tendency to oscillate between extreme positive claims and later contradicting negative findings as more studies accumulate.
