## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Impact of oral rehabilitation on the quality of life of partially dentate elders in a randomised controlled clinical trial: 2 year follow-up
- **Authors**: Gerald McKenna, Patrick Finbarr Allen, Martina Hayes, Cristiane DaMata, Ciaran Moore, Michael Cronin
- **Journal**: PLoS ONE, 2018;13(10):e0203349
- **PMID**: 30307966 | **PMC**: PMC6181283 | **DOI**: 10.1371/journal.pone.0203349
- **Design**: Randomised controlled clinical trial, two-centre
- **Ethics**: Cork Teaching Hospitals Ethics Committee (ref: ECM 5 (9) 05/02/08)

## 2. Key Contributions

- First multi-centre RCT demonstrating SDA superiority over RPD for OHRQoL in partially dentate patients aged ≥65 at 2 years.
- Largest effect observed in older, more systemically unwell cohort (SFDH): SDA better by 7.9 OHIP-14 points at 24mo (p<0.0001).
- Demonstrates temporal divergence: RPD improvements diminish after 6 months; SDA benefits increase over time.
- Provides maintenance cost data: RPD patients returned >3× more frequently (p<0.001).
- Supports SDA as the preferred approach specifically for frail elderly — extends Witter's original SDA rationale to a frailty dimension.

## 3. Methodology

- **Patients**: 89 completed (65 RPD, 67 SDA enrolled; 44 RPD, 45 SDA at 24mo); age ≥65yr; Cork, Ireland
- **Centres**: CUDH (Cork University Dental Hospital) — standard dental hospital setting; SFDH (St Finbarr's Geriatric Day Hospital) — mobile dental unit, older/more systemically unwell patients
- **Randomisation**: Computer-generated, blocks of varying length, stratified by age and gender; allocation concealed; intention-to-treat analysis
- **RPD group**: Cobalt-chromium framework RPDs replacing all missing teeth
- **SDA group**: Restored to premolar occlusion of 10 occluding pairs using resin-bonded bridgework (RBB); posterior teeth distal to SDA left unopposed
- **Primary outcome**: OHIP-14 (lower = better OHRQoL)
- **Timepoints**: Baseline, 1, 6, 12, 24 months
- **Statistics**: Mixed model ANCOVA repeated measures; adjusted for age, gender, comorbidity (CIRS), social class, centre

| Outcome | RPD @24mo | SDA @24mo | Group difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| OHIP-14 summary (mean ± SD) | 7.4 ± 3.3 | 4.0 ± 2.3 | — |
| OHIP-14 improvement from baseline (12mo) | +5.7 (p<0.05) | +8.0 (p<0.001) | — |
| OHIP-14 improvement from baseline (24mo) | +4.2 (p<0.05) | +5.9 (p<0.05) | — |
| **CUDH adjusted difference @24mo** | — | — | **SDA better by 2.9 (p<0.0001)** |
| **SFDH adjusted difference @24mo** | — | — | **SDA better by 7.9 (p<0.0001)** |

OHIP-14 domains significantly favouring SDA at 24 months: physical pain, psychological discomfort, physical disability, psychological disability, functional limitation.

Maintenance burden: RPD patients returned >3× more frequently than SDA patients (p<0.001).

## 5. Limitations

- 2-year follow-up only (shorter than Walter 2021 15yr)
- Single operator, single dental lab — limits generalisability
- SFDH mobile dental unit environment may have disadvantaged RPD construction
- Resin-bonded bridgework (RBB) as SDA restoration — not all practices use RBB as primary approach
- No dietary/nutritional outcome reported in this paper (separate publications from same trial)
