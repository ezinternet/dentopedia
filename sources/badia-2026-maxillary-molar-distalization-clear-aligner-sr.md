## Why Ingested

Extends the distalization evidence base established by [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/bhate-2025-cat-maxillary-molar-distalization-class-ii-sr]] (11 studies, n=278) with a broader search (16 studies, 461 patients, up to April 2026) and adds critical analysis of auxiliary strategies, aligner-change intervals, and measurement timing as confounds — directly addressing the predictability gap flagged by [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/inchingolo-2023-clear-aligner-distalization-predictability-sr]].

## One-line Summary

Systematic review (16 studies, 461 patients, PROSPERO CRD42024573061) on maxillary molar distalization with clear aligners; predictability ranged 31.1–88.4%, meta-analysis not possible due to substantial heterogeneity.

## 한줄요약

## 2. Key Contributions

- Broadest available SR on clear aligner maxillary molar distalization (16 studies, 461 patients), superseding smaller prior SRs
- Conceptually separates *effectiveness* (mm achieved) from *predictability* (achieved/planned %) — two distinct outcomes often conflated in earlier literature
- Identifies timing of outcome assessment (end-of-distalization-phase vs. end-of-total-treatment) as a major source of inter-study heterogeneity
- Demonstrates inverse relationship between prescribed movement magnitude and predictability
- Provides GRADE certainty ratings (very low for both outcomes) — first such formal rating in this subfield

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Systematic review (PRISMA-compliant, PROSPERO registered)
- **Population**: Patients undergoing clear aligner therapy requiring maxillary molar distalization
- **Study types included**: RCTs, cohort studies (prospective/retrospective), case-control, cross-sectional, multicenter
- **Study types excluded**: Systematic/narrative reviews, case series/reports, in vitro
- **Included studies**: 16 (11 retrospective cohort, 5 prospective experimental)
- **Sample**: 461 patients (117 M, 295 F, 25 sex NR); age range 21.6–36 years mean
- **Aligner systems**: Predominantly Invisalign; two studies used Anglealign and Ordoline
- **Risk of bias**: NOS for retrospective (most 7–8/9); ROBINS-I for prospective (all low/moderate overall)
- **Evidence certainty**: GRADE — very low for both achieved distalization (mm) and predictability (%) due to risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, and imprecision
- **Meta-analysis**: Not performed — substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity in outcome definitions, reporting units, measurement timing, and statistical reporting
- **Kappa**: 0.88 inter-/intra-examiner reliability

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Primary Outcomes

- **Predictability of distalization**: 31.1% (Li et al.) to 88.4% (Simon et al.) — extremely wide range
  - Low end (≤40%): 4 of included studies
  - Mid range (~53–58%): Miao et al.
  - High end: Cui et al. (83.4–85.1%), Simon et al. (86.9–88.4%)
- **Achieved distalization (first molar)**: 0.84 mm to 4.86 mm
- **Achieved distalization (second molar)**: 0.86 mm to 2.98 mm
- **Average achievable movement**: approximately 2 mm

### Key Moderating Factors

- **Prescribed movement magnitude**: Inversely related to predictability — larger prescribed distalization → lower efficiency
- **Aligner change interval**:
  - 14-day intervals associated with higher predictability in multiple studies (Castroflorio: 12% reduction in lack-of-correction vs 7-day; Al-Nadawi: significantly enhanced precision; Linjawi: less aligner misalignment)
  - Only one study directly compared 7/10/14-day for maxillary molar distalization — insufficient evidence to definitively recommend 14 days
- **Auxiliaries**:
  - Class II elastics: reduce anterior proclination and maintain aligner contact; some studies show inadequate anchorage control without TADs
  - Vertical/rectangular attachments on buccal molar surfaces: counteract distal tipping
  - Microscrews/TADs: stronger anchorage than elastics alone; Liu et al. found no significant difference between elastics and microscrews (both groups: achieved significantly less than planned, efficiency 36.2–43.9%)
- **First vs second molar comparison**: 7 studies — no significant predictability difference; 4 studies — slightly higher predictability for second molars
- **Distalization protocols**: V-shaped, 50%, two-thirds sequential — no consensus on which is superior
- **Timing of outcome assessment**: End-of-distalization-phase studies give cleaner signal; end-of-total-treatment values confounded by subsequent mechanics

### Fixed vs Clear Aligner

- One study reported 17% greater effectiveness in distalizing first molars with clear aligners vs fixed; fixed appliances produced increased molar tipping and reciprocal mesial drift of second molars

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

**Author-identified limitations:**
- Substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity precluding meta-analysis
- Heterogeneous superimposition methods (craniofacial reference planes, palatal rugae, best-fit, mathematical models)
- Cephalometric-only studies included for effectiveness but not predictability — methodological limitation
- Poor compliance reporting: only verbal confirmation in most studies; compliance variability may explain much of the outcome variability
- Wide variation in distalization protocols without head-to-head comparisons

**Additional limitations:**
- All-but-two studies used Invisalign — limited generalizability to other aligner systems
- GRADE: very low certainty for both primary outcomes
- Five studies had <20 participants — low external validity
- Mostly retrospective design (11/16)

**Future research needs:**
- RCTs or prospective studies with standardized outcome definitions and measurement methods
- Separate reporting of planned and achieved movement for M6 and M7 with standard deviations
- Objective compliance monitoring
- Controlled comparison of distalization staging protocols

## 7. Glossary

- **Predictability**: Ratio of achieved to planned (prescribed) distal movement, expressed as % — distinct from effectiveness
- **Effectiveness**: Amount of distal movement actually achieved in mm — whether clinically useful movement was produced
- **Sequential distalization**: Protocol restricting simultaneous tooth separation to improve aligner contact — variants include V-shape, 50%, two-thirds
- **ROBINS-I**: Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies of Interventions assessment tool
- **NOS**: Newcastle–Ottawa Scale for cohort/case-control quality assessment (max 9 stars)
- **GRADE**: Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation — certainty-of-evidence framework
- **TADs**: Temporary Anchorage Devices (miniscrews/microscrews used as skeletal anchorage)
- **Anglealign / Ordoline**: Non-Invisalign clear aligner systems studied in 2 of the 16 included papers
