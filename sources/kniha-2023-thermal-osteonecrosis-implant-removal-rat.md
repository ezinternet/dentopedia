## Why Ingested

Fills a gap in the osteotomy-thermal literature by providing the first in vivo animal data on controlled thermoexplantation temperature thresholds: while [[implants/osteotomy-thermal/timon-2019-thermal-osteonecrosis-bone-drilling-orthopedic]] establishes the critical threshold at 50°C from a narrative review, this rat study verifies 50°C/1 min as the threshold for irreversible osteocyte death in a live model and evaluates ISQ and bone-level changes as measurable endpoints — directly preceding a planned pig study with osseointegrated implants.

## Three-line Summary

In vivo rat tibia study (48 Sprague–Dawley rats, 96 Modus 2.0 mini-screws, 12 groups at temperatures 2–4°C and 48–50°C × 1 min) comparing EDX mineralogy, TEM osteocyte morphology, ISQ, and radiographic bone level at day 0 and day 7 post-thermal treatment.

At 50°C/1 min, EDX showed significant increases in calcium (mean 25.31 wt%), phosphate (12.40 wt%), sodium, and sulfur (all p < 0.01 vs 48°C); TEM confirmed nearly complete osteocyte necrosis with empty lacunae; ISQ decreased non-significantly at 48–50°C; bone loss distances increased but did not reach significance over 7 days.

This preliminary animal study identifies 50°C/60 s as the candidate temperature–time threshold for controlled thermoexplantation, supporting feasibility of a subsequent in vivo pig study with osseointegrated implants; non-osseointegrated rat-tibia screws limit direct clinical translation.

- **Journal**: Head & Face Medicine 2023;19:4

- **Institution**: Department of Oral and Cranio-Maxillofacial Surgery, University Hospital RWTH Aachen, Germany

## 2. Key Contributions
- First in vivo rat data comparing multiple discrete warm (48–50°C) and cold (2–4°C) temperature thresholds for peri-implant osteonecrosis at 1 min, with matched EDX, TEM, ISQ, and radiographic endpoints
- EDX Ca/C and Ca/P ratios both increased with worsening temperature damage, validating these ratios as mineralization/damage markers in the osteotomy-thermal context
- 50°C/60 s confirmed as candidate thermoexplantation threshold: irreversible osteocyte death by TEM, significant EDX changes; lower temperatures (48°C, 49°C) produced partial damage, cold temperatures (2–4°C) also induced cellular injury but less than 50°C
- ISQ measured on mini-screws; confirms proof-of-concept that implant stability measurement is feasible in small animal models, though ISQ values are lower than human implant baselines due to smaller implant geometry

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In vivo controlled animal study (contralateral control design — test vs untreated tibia within each rat)
- **Animals**: 48 adult male Sprague–Dawley rats, 450 g, 4 months old
- **Implants**: Modus 2.0 3-mm cortical screws (Medartis AG); 96 implants total; NOT osseointegrated
- **Temperature groups**: 2°C, 3°C, 4°C (cold) and 48°C, 49°C, 50°C (warm), each n = 8 test + 8 control per temperature
- **Duration**: Thermal treatment 1 min; follow-up 7 days
- **Endpoints**: EDX (Ca, C, O, Na, P, S; 8 measurement points per sample), TEM (osteocyte morphology: vacuolization, shrinkage, detachment, necrosis), ISQ (Osstell; 4-direction mean), radiographic bone-level distance (ImageJ; standardized 90° dental X-ray CT)
- **Statistics**: Kruskal–Wallis + Dunn's multiple comparison; Kolmogorov–Smirnov normality; G*Power post hoc (power = 100% for EDX endpoint)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### EDX mineral composition at 50°C vs control (significant findings)
| Element | 50°C mean wt% | p vs 48°C | p vs cold groups |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calcium | 25.31 ± 4.78 | p < 0.01 | p < 0.01 (vs 2°C–4°C) |
| Phosphate | 12.40 ± 1.98 | p < 0.01 | — |
| Sodium | 0.69 ± 0.25 | — | p = 0.01 (vs 3°C) |
| Sulfur | 0.27 ± 0.17 | — | p < 0.01 (vs 3°C, 4°C) |
| Carbon | 48.70 ± 6.89 | p < 0.01 | — |

Ca/C ratio increased with temperature (reflecting calcification index); Ca/P ratio also increased.

### TEM findings
- All temperatures (cold and warm): vacuolization, shrinkage, detachment of osteocytes from bone matrix
- 50°C: nearly complete osteocyte necrosis — lacunae empty; irreversible cell death confirmed
- 2°C vs 50°C: both showed the most severe damage vs intermediate temperatures; hot > cold in severity
- 48°C, 49°C: partial injury, cells not uniformly necrotic

### ISQ and bone level
- ISQ (test vs control): decreased at 48–50°C in test group; inhomogeneous results at 3–4°C; no statistically significant differences (p > 0.05)
- Radiographic bone loss: increased distance (implant shoulder to bone contact) with higher temperatures; no statistically significant difference over 7-day follow-up (p > 0.05)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Non-osseointegrated implants (cortical screws, not dental implants) — cannot confirm thermal loosening of osseointegrated bodies; requires pig study with real osseointegrated implants
- Short 7-day follow-up — bone loss at 50°C may become significant at later time points; authors note CT/MRI more sensitive for early bone necrosis (plain film sensitivity ~41%)
- ISQ mini-screw adaptation: values structurally lower than human implant ISQ; comparison across studies limited
- Single examiner; no blinding described
- Cold temperature threshold for cryoexplantation still undefined — EDX/TEM changes at cold temperatures observed but without systematic dose-response conclusions

## 6. Related Work
- Timon & Keady 2019 ([[implants/osteotomy-thermal/timon-2019-thermal-osteonecrosis-bone-drilling-orthopedic]]): narrative review establishing 50°C as critical threshold for thermal osteonecrosis — the present study's in vivo data validates this
- Kniha et al. 2021 (Clin Oral Investig): preceding in vitro cadaver pilot by same group — 51°C/10 s and 5°C/30 s for matrix degeneration; TEM at 53°C showed decalcification/swollen mitochondria (reference [6, 8] in this paper)
- Eriksson & Albrektsson 1983 (J Prosthet Dent): classical vital-microscopy rabbit study on heat-induced bone injury (arterial/venous hyperemia, bone resorption after 3 weeks) — foundational reference [13]
- Trisi et al. 2014 (Implant Dent): in vivo histological study — 50–60°C heating of osseointegrated dental implants in dense bone; peri-implant pockets significantly larger vs control at 50°C/1 min (referenced as [17])
