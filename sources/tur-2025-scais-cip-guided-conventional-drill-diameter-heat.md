## Summary

Standardized in vitro study (Medical University of Vienna) comparing static computer-assisted implant surgery (S-CAIS) vs conventional implant preparation (CIP) in automated, intermittent protocol. Standardized bovine bone samples (BoneSim composite, type-2 bone density, 3mm cortical + 15mm cancellous), rectangular shape 56×18×10mm. CNC material testing apparatus (Lloyd LS1) for reproducible drilling. Stainless steel twist drills (Straumann PRO: 2.2, 2.8, 3.5mm). Multiple type-K thermocouple sensor arrays at 1mm and 2mm from osteotomy, depths 3/6/9/12/15mm. 240 total preparations: 10 repetitions per constellation.

Variables: 3 drill diameters × 2 depths (10, 12mm) × 2 irrigation modes (with/without external) × 2 techniques (S-CAIS vs CIP).

Key temperature findings (ΔT°C, 10mm drilling, without irrigation):
- 2.2mm: S-CAIS 13.06 vs CIP 13.62 (NS)
- 2.8mm: S-CAIS 16.70 vs CIP 16.54 (NS)
- 3.5mm: **S-CAIS 15.05 vs CIP 17.36** (p=0.0115 — CIP significantly higher)

12mm drilling, with external irrigation:
- 3.5mm: **S-CAIS 9.14 vs CIP 6.00** (p=0.0101 — S-CAIS significantly higher)

Overall pattern:
- Significant differences only with 3.5mm drills; smaller diameters (2.2, 2.8mm): S-CAIS and CIP statistically equivalent
- External irrigation dramatically reduces all temperatures regardless of technique
- Deeper drilling (12mm > 10mm) → higher temperatures invariably
- Maximum temperature depth shifts deeper with irrigation (cooling of superficial layers)

Critical: All temperatures exceeded 47°C threshold only WITHOUT irrigation; with external irrigation, all preparations safely below threshold.

Conclusion: S-CAIS is NOT globally hotter than CIP — it depends on drill diameter, depth, and irrigation. The primary concern is the 3.5mm large-diameter drill in S-CAIS at 12mm depth with external irrigation where guided geometry impairs cooling efficiency.

Limitations: in vitro only; room temperature conditions, no physiological vascularity; closed-guide only; only 10mm and 12mm depths tested.
