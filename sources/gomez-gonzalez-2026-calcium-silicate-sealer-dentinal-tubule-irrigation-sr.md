## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA 2020, OSF-registered protocol, 21 in-vitro studies, all rated low risk of bias by QUIN) synthesizing how final irrigation protocols and activation methods affect calcium silicate-based sealer (CSS) dentinal tubular penetration in extracted teeth.

NaOCl- and EDTA-based final irrigation protocols were the most frequently investigated and generally associated with more favorable penetration, and activation methods (PUI, laser, sonic, XP-Endo Finisher) generally outperformed conventional irrigation, but no single irrigant, chelating agent, or activation technique could be identified as superior due to substantial methodological heterogeneity across the 21 studies; penetration was consistently greater in coronal/middle thirds than apical third.

Dentinal tubular penetration is a laboratory surrogate outcome not shown to correlate with clinical sealability/success, so these findings should guide protocol emphasis on smear-layer removal and dentin conditioning rather than be extrapolated as proof of superiority of any one irrigation/activation regimen; meta-analysis was not possible.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰(PRISMA 2020, OSF 사전등록, in-vitro 연구 21편, QUIN 기준 전부 low risk of bias)로, 최종 세정 프로토콜과 활성화 방법이 발거치에서 칼슘실리케이트 실러 (Calcium Silicate-based Sealer, CSS)의 상아세관 침투 (Dentinal Tubular Penetration)에 미치는 영향을 종합했다.

NaOCl과 17% EDTA 기반 프로토콜이 가장 많이 연구됐고 대체로 더 나은 침투와 연관됐으며, 활성화 방법(PUI·레이저·소닉·XP-Endo Finisher)이 일반적으로 통상 세정보다 우수했지만, 21편 간 방법론적 이질성이 커서 어떤 세정제·킬레이팅제·활성화 기법도 우월하다고 단정할 수 없었다; 침투는 치관/중간부가 근단부보다 일관되게 우세했다.

상아세관 침투는 임상적 봉쇄성·성공과의 상관관계가 입증되지 않은 실험실 대리 지표(laboratory surrogate outcome)이므로, 이번 소견은 특정 세정·활성화 프로토콜의 우월성 근거가 아니라 도말층 제거와 상아질 표면 컨디셔닝 강조라는 방향성 정도로만 활용해야 한다; 메타분석은 수행하지 못했다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic synthesis of the effect of **final irrigation protocol** (as opposed to sealer choice) on **CSS dentinal tubular penetration**, covering 21 in-vitro studies.
- Maps the landscape of irrigants (NaOCl, 17% EDTA, CHX, citric acid, HEBP, QMix, maleic/glycolic acid, apple cider vinegar, chitosan nanoparticles) and activation methods (PUI, sonic, laser, XP-Endo Finisher, heat, lentulo, apical negative pressure) evaluated against CSS penetration outcomes.
- Confirms a recurrent anatomical trend: penetration is greater in coronal/middle thirds than the apical third, tentatively attributed to tubule density, canal diameter, and irrigant-exchange differences.
- Explicitly flags that dentinal tubular penetration is a laboratory surrogate not shown to correlate with clinical sealability — a methodological caution for the whole CSS-penetration literature.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Protocol**: PRISMA 2020; OSF-registered (10.17605/OSF.IO/5HTVN).
- **PICOS**: (P) extracted teeth; (I) root canal treatment with a specific final irrigation method + CSS filling; (C) alternative irrigation method; (O) CSS dentinal tubular penetration/adaptation; (S) in vitro study.
- **Search**: MEDLINE, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, SciELO; conducted October 2025; free-text search (no MeSH/Emtree) for cross-database consistency.
- **Screening**: Mendeley dedup → title/abstract screening → full-text assessment by 2 reviewers (J.G.-G., J.L.S.), disagreements resolved by a third reviewer (A.L.).
- **Data extraction**: 2 reviewers (J.G.-G., A.L.) into standardized tables — methodological variables (tooth type, sample size, instrumentation, final irrigation protocol, sealer, filling technique) and outcome variables (penetration assessment method/parameters, main results).
- **Quality assessment**: QUIN (Quality Assessment Tool for In Vitro Studies), 12-item checklist (2/1/0 per item); global score classified low risk (>70%), moderate (50–70%), high risk (<50%).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Study flow**: 82 potentially eligible → 12 duplicates removed → 52 excluded at first screening → 18 eligible on full-text → +3 from manual reference-list search → **21 studies included**.
- **Quality**: all 21 studies rated **low risk of bias** by global QUIN score (though domain-level weaknesses were still noted — incomplete reporting, unclear allocation, variable sample sizes, inconsistent outcome measures).
- **Irrigants**: NaOCl and 17% EDTA most frequently investigated, generally associated with more favorable CSS penetration; other agents (CHX, citric acid, HEBP, QMix, maleic/glycolic acid, apple cider vinegar, chitosan nanoparticles) less studied and inconclusive.
- **Activation**: conventional irrigation, PUI, laser-assisted activation, sonic activation, XP-Endo Finisher, and heat activation were compared; activation generally improved penetration over conventional irrigation alone, but which activation method performed best was inconsistent across studies (some favored laser, others PUI or XP-Endo Finisher).
- **Regional pattern**: penetration was generally greater in coronal/middle thirds than the apical third.
- **No meta-analysis possible** — outcome heterogeneity (max/mean penetration depth, penetration area/percentage, push-out bond strength, etc.) precluded quantitative pooling.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- All 21 studies are **in vitro only**; dentinal tubular penetration is a laboratory parameter, and prior studies cited found **no significant correlation** between penetration and sealability/interfacial adaptation — clinical extrapolation is not supported.
- All included studies used **single-rooted teeth**, limiting generalizability to multi-rooted/complex canal systems.
- Sample sizes varied considerably across studies.
- Marked heterogeneity in tooth type, instrumentation, irrigation sequence, activation method, sealer formulation, obturation technique, fluorescent dye (Rhodamine B leaching concerns with CSS noted), microscopy system, image-analysis software, and outcome parameters — reduces cross-study comparability.
- Free-text search strategy (no MeSH/Emtree) may have missed some indexed records, though intentionally broad/sensitivity-oriented.
- **Future work**: standardize instrumentation/irrigation/activation/drying protocols and outcome parameters; include multi-rooted/anatomically complex canals; adopt **PRILE 2021** reporting guidelines for in vitro endodontic research; clarify whether laboratory penetration differences translate to clinically meaningful outcomes.

## 7. Glossary

- **CSS** — Calcium Silicate-based Sealer (bioceramic root canal sealer).
- **CLSM** — Confocal Laser Scanning Microscopy, the most common method for assessing dentinal tubular penetration.
- **SEM / FM** — Scanning Electron Microscopy / Fluorescence Microscopy, alternative penetration-assessment methods.
- **PUI** — Passive Ultrasonic Irrigation.
- **QUIN** — Quality Assessment Tool for In Vitro Studies (12-item checklist used here for risk-of-bias rating).
- **PRILE 2021** — Preferred Reporting Items for Laboratory studies in Endodontology, a reporting-guideline recommended for future standardization.
- **POBS / PBS** — Push-Out Bond Strength, an alternate method of evaluating sealer penetration/adaptation.
- **Smear layer** — the debris layer created by instrumentation that final irrigation (esp. EDTA/citric acid) removes to expose dentinal tubules.
