## Why Ingested

악관절 탈구 (temporomandibular joint luxation, TMJ luxation) 문헌이 위키에 거의 존재하지 않아, 이 주제의 정량적 출발점으로 필요했음 — 기존 근거는 Abrahamsson 2020·de Almeida 2016 두 편의 시스템틱 리뷰(본 문헌이 직접 인용)뿐이었고, 표준 진료가 개별病例 보고서 (case report)에 의존하는 상태였음. 특히 신경계 질환 (neurological condition)이 재발 (recurrence) 위험을 높인다는 기전을 서술적 분류 차원 넘어 실제 코호트 수치로 정량화함 — [[tmj/falletti-2026-update-trismus-etiology-diagnosis-treatment]]의 신경계 비구조적 아관긴장 (Trismus) 분류를 탈구 영역으로 확장·확인함. 또한 재발 억제 부위로 성기게 사용된(3/260, 1.2%) 외항교근 (lateral pterygoid muscle) 보툴리눔독소A형 (Botulinum Toxin Type A, BoNT-A) 주사의 실행 공백을 드러내어, [[botulinum-toxin/bae-2025-ultrasonographic-assessment-lateral-pterygoid-muscle]]의 초음파 유도 주사술이 겨냥하는 실제 임상 수요를 명시함.

## Three-line Summary
Retrospective cohort study (n=260) from a tertiary oral and maxillofacial surgery emergency unit in Helsinki, Finland, covering 14 years (Jan 2007–Oct 2020), started from 800 ICD-coded records; the primary outcome was recurrent vs non-recurrent TMJ luxation, secondary outcomes were need for and type of surgical intervention.
Of all luxations 61.9% were recurrent, 40.0% spontaneous and 36.2% caused by yawning/vomiting/eating; 57.3% were bilateral, 94.2% could not close the mouth, manual reduction was attempted in 91.9% and needed no medication in 69.2%, and only 1.9% (n=5) underwent eminectomy — any general condition carried a 1.57-fold (95% CI 1.27–1.94, P<0.001) and a neurological condition a 1.34-fold (1.12–1.60, P=0.003) risk of recurrence.
Recurrent luxation is therefore the norm rather than the exception, and acute reduction is a temporising measure requiring neurological/geriatric collaboration; the main limitation is single-centre retrospective capture that excludes luxations reduced in private dental offices, so both recurrence and severity are likely underestimated.

## 세줄요약
핀란드 헬싱키 대학교병원 구강안면외과 응급실의 14년(2007-01~2020-10) 후향적 코호트 연구(n=260, ICD 검색 800건에서 수작업 재평가), 일차 결과는 재발성 여부, 이차 결과는 수술적 중재의 필요성·종류.
전체 탈구의 61.9%가 재발성, 40.0%가 자발성, 36.2%가 하품·구토·식사 유발, 57.3%가 양측성, 94.2%가 입을 다물 수 없었고 수동 복원 (manual reduction)은 91.9%에서 시행되어 69.2%는 무약물 성공, 수술(관절결절절제술, eminectomy)은 1.9%(5명)에 불과 — 전신 질환 존재 시 재발 위험 1.57배(95% CI 1.27–1.94, P<0.001), 신경계 질환 시 1.34배(1.12–1.60, P=0.003).
재발이 예외가 아니라 지배적 양상임을 보여 주며 급기 복원 (reduction)은 일시적 처치에 불과하므로 신경과·노인과 협업 관리가 필요함, 다만 단일센터 후향적 자료로 치과 진료소에서 복원된 탈구가 제외되어 재발률이 과소평가되었을 가능성.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **First large single-centre cohort on the aetiology of TMJ luxation (n=260 over 14 years)** — the paper's own framing: the prior literature on luxation treatment "is mainly based on case-reports and systematic studies are lacking", so the quantitative backbone for the field.
2. **Recurrent luxation is the majority presentation (61.9%), not the exception** — and it is the *primary outcome* by design, with recurrence defined as ≥2 dislocations in a lifetime.
3. **Quantifies the neurological–recurrence link**: neurological condition present → RR 1.34 (95% CI 1.12–1.60, P=0.003); any general condition → RR 1.57 (1.27–1.94, P<0.001), supporting a muscular-tonus/connective-tissue mechanism rather than a purely mechanical one.
4. **Establishes the real-world treatment distribution**: 91.9% manual reduction attempt, 69.2% successful with no medication, non-surgical aftercare dominant (jaw bandage 55.4%, mouth-opening restriction 55.0%, soft diet 46.5%, nightguard 7.3%), surgery in 1.9% — and BoNT-A injection into the masticatory muscles in only 1.2% (3/260).
5. **Reports the mortality signal**: 28.1% overall mortality within the study period; 68.5% of recurrent patients died (P=0.173), with mean time to death 884 days in recurrent vs 489 days in non-recurrent — a prognostic rather than therapeutic finding.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort / chart review (single tertiary centre, emergency-department cohort)
- **Period**: 1 January 2007 – 20 October 2020 (14 years)
- **Case identification**: Electronic patient management system search on 5 ICD codes — S03.4 (sprain and strain of jaw), S03.0 (dislocation of jaw), S03.5 (sprain/strain of joints and ligaments of other/unspecified head parts), K07.62 (recurrent TMJ dislocation), K07.69 (unspecified TMJ dysfunction). 800 records retrieved and manually re-evaluated; patients with any condition other than TMJ dislocation excluded → **260 confirmed cases**.
- **Primary outcome**: Type of luxation — recurrent vs non-recurrent. *Recurrent defined as occurrence at least twice in the lifetime.*
- **Secondary outcomes**: Need for, and type of, surgical intervention
- **Predictor variables**: age, sex, presence of neurological condition, mechanism of luxation
- **Mechanism categories (6)**: (1) spontaneous; (2) yawning, vomiting, or eating; (3) dentist visit or gastroscopy; (4) injury; (5) medical seizure; (6) habit or exercise
- **Other registered variables**: other general condition(s), regular medication, type of living conditions, referral unit, clinical parameters, radiographic interventions, need for manual reduction, aftercare instructions, mortality during the study period
- **Data analysis**: Descriptive statistics; chi-square test for between-variable significance; 2×2 tables for risk-ratio (RR) calculation
- **Ethics**: Approved by the internal review board of the Head and Neck Center of HUH; patient consent waived (retrospective)

### Additional figures quoted in the text

- Emergency-department frequency of TMJ luxation: **5.3 cases per year** (cited from Lowery 2004, not this cohort).
- Injury-caused luxation in the present study: **6.2%**, versus 20% (Agbara 2014) to 60% (Akinbami 2011) in previous series.
- Mean age in this cohort 51.4 years vs "around 40 years" in two previous series (Agbara 2014; Sang 2010).
- Mortality frequency in the recurrent group (n=50) was **2.2 times** that of the non-recurrent/control group (n=23), not statistically significant.
- **15 patients** had surgery planned but the procedure was cancelled for anaesthesiologic contraindications to general anaesthesia, and **five patients died before their planned surgery**.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Retrospective design** (self-declared weakness) — the authors state a prospective study would additionally have provided information on prosthetic treatment.
- **Single-clinic, single-period capture** — the authors note that some patients may have previously been treated for luxation at another clinic or hospital, so the number of recurrent patients "might be even higher".
- **Private-practice luxations excluded** — luxations treated in other units (e.g. dentists' offices) were not included, so the university-hospital sample likely over-represents more challenging cases relative to the general population.
- **Diagnostic practice is unstandardised** — imaging was used in only ~32% of cases and the authors explicitly raise the question of whether mandibular/condylar fracture differentials were sufficiently excluded; they conclude there is a need to standardise diagnostic measures and treatment patterns.
- **No good-quality treatment evidence exists** — the discussion cites de Almeida 2016 SR concluding there is no good-quality evidence on which treatments eliminate recurrent luxation long-term, with eminectomy treated empirically as the "gold standard".
- **BoNT-A uptake negligible (1.2%)** — the recurrence-prevention injection the literature most often proposes was given to 3 of 260 patients, so the cohort cannot support any efficacy inference about it.

## 7. Glossary

- **TMJ luxation (temporomandibular joint luxation / 탈구)**: displacement of the mandibular condyle anteriorly over the articular tuberculum (articular eminence) of the temporal bone, with simultaneous masticatory muscle spasm blocking relocation of the condyle behind the eminence. Clinically: pain, jaw deviation, inability to close the mouth, non-palpable condyle behind the eminence; bilateral cases show no deviation but a prognathic mandible.
- **Recurrent luxation (재발성 탈구)**: as defined in this study, TMJ luxation occurring **at least twice in a lifetime** — the study's primary outcome dichotomy.
- **Manual reduction (수동 복원)**: closed repositioning of the dislocated condyle, normally performed at the emergency stage without medication.
- **Eminectomy (관절결절절제술)**: surgical removal of the articular eminence to physically prevent re-dislocation; the only surgical procedure performed in this cohort (5 patients, 1.9%).
- **Preventive/non-surgical measures**: intermaxillary fixation, stabilization splint ("nightguard"), BoNT-A injection into the jaw muscles or the TMJ — the acute repositioning of a recurrent luxation is described as "only a temporary treatment" requiring these to follow.
- **Intermaxillary fixation (악간고정술)**: maxillomandibular wiring/immobilisation listed among non-surgical preventive options for recurrent luxation.
- **Risk ratio (RR, 위험비)**: from 2×2 tables here, the ratio of recurrence risk in exposed vs unexposed patients; RR > 1 with a 95% CI excluding 1 and P < 0.05 was treated as significant.
