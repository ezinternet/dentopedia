## One-line Summary

Nationwide cross-sectional survey (n=300 Turkish dentists) found median knowledge score 11/16, only 40% felt competent to manage emergencies, with critical gaps in anaphylaxis, hypertensive crisis, and epileptic seizure management.

## 한줄요약

전국 단면연구 (n=300, 터키 치과의사) — 중앙값 지식 점수 11/16, 응급 처치 역량을 느끼는 비율은 40%에 불과, 아나필락시스·고혈압 위기·뇌전증 발작에서 결정적 지식 격차 확인.

## 1. Document Information

- Journal: Healthcare (Basel), Vol. 14, No. 6, 2026
- Affiliation: Dicle University Faculty of Dentistry, Turkey
- Response rate: 66.6% (300/450 invited)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Large-scale nationwide survey bridging knowledge, attitude, AND clinical preparedness simultaneously
- Identifies specific emergency gaps: high-risk scenarios (anaphylaxis, hypertensive crisis, epilepsy) vs. common ones (syncope, bleeding)
- Quantifies the competence-knowledge dissociation: adequate theoretical knowledge ≠ clinical confidence
- Higher knowledge + available emergency equipment → significantly greater self-perceived competence
- Points to avoidance behavior (refusing high-risk patients) as a consequence of low preparedness

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional survey
- **Population**: 300 dentists practicing in Turkey (nationwide sample)
- **Instruments**: 16-scenario multiple-choice knowledge questionnaire + Likert-scale attitude/preparedness questionnaire
- **Analysis**: Chi-square tests with effect size; participants categorized by knowledge level
- **Recruitment**: 450 invited → 300 completed (66.6% response rate)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Overall knowledge**: Median score 11/16 (IQR 9–13)
- **High correct response**: Syncope, intraoral bleeding (common, frequently encountered)
- **Low correct response**: Hypertensive crisis, anaphylaxis, epileptic seizures (rare but high-risk)
- **Clinical competence**: Only 40% felt sufficiently competent to manage emergencies
- **Avoidance**: Avoidance of high-risk patients common among low-preparedness dentists
- **Equipment association**: Availability of emergency drugs/equipment significantly linked to greater confidence

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Cross-sectional design — no causal inference
- Self-reported attitudes and preparedness susceptible to social desirability bias
- Single country (Turkey); curriculum and training patterns differ globally
- Knowledge test is scenario-based but not validated with direct skill observation

## 7. Glossary

- **Preparedness**: Combined readiness — knowledge + equipment + confidence to act
- **Avoidance behavior**: Refusing to treat systemically complex patients due to insufficient emergency preparedness
- **Competence self-perception**: Subjective confidence in own ability to manage emergencies; dissociable from objective knowledge
