## Why Ingested

Technical note describing a novel rescue for profuse pulsatile hemorrhage from the posterior superior alveolar artery (PSAA) bone channel during lateral-window sinus floor elevation, after standard hemostatic measures failed. Extends the PSAA hemorrhage management overview, [[overviews/psaa-hemorrhage-management-lateral-sinus-lift-overview]].

## Three-line Summary

Single case report: during lateral-window sinus floor elevation under local anesthesia, profuse pulsatile hemorrhage from a relatively large PSAA branch at the inner sinus wall could not be controlled by vasoconstrictor-soaked gauze, absorbable hemostat, bone wax, or electrocautery.

After provisional occlusion with a sterile gutta percha point #100 achieved hemostasis, a titanium screw (Le Forte System, 1.4 mm × 6.0 mm) was inserted into the bone channel, stopping bleeding completely and immediately.

Implant was placed after screw removal at 5 months without complications; re-bleeding risk is considered low because the channel fills with new bone during healing.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Describes a reliable rescue technique for uncontrolled PSAA hemorrhage from a bone channel during lateral-window sinus floor elevation: physical occlusion with a titanium screw.
- Documents failure of routine hemostatic measures (vasoconstrictor-soaked gauze compression, absorbable hemostat, bone wax, electrocautery) for a relatively large arterial branch.
- Provides rationale that screw removal at ~5 months is safe because the bone channel fills with newly formed bone, making re-bleeding unlikely.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Single case report (technical note)
- **Setting**: Dental clinic, local anesthesia
- **Procedure**: Preoperative CT to evaluate sinus and locate intraosseous vessels; lateral approach sinus floor elevation with piezoelectric diamond insert OT5A (Mectron s.p.a.)
- **Hemostasis sequence**: vasoconstrictor (1:80,000)-soaked gauze placed between membrane and sinus wall → controlled membrane bleeding only; 10 min repeated compression of inner wall; absorbable hemostat [3]; bone wax [4]; electrocautery — all ineffective.
- **Rescue**: Distal bone edge of antrostomy removed for direct visualization; firm pressure over channel reduced but did not stop bleeding; sterile gutta percha point #100 provisionally inserted proximally into bone channel as deep as possible → complete hemostasis; replaced with titanium screw (Le Forte System, diameter 1.4 mm, length 6.0 mm; Jeil Medical Corp., Seoul, Korea).
- **Follow-up**: Artificial bone grafting (Bio-Oss®, Geistlich); screw removed at 5 months without complications; implant placed.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Hemorrhage: pulsatile, profuse, from a relatively large branch of the PSAA suspected at the inner aspect of the sinus wall, torn during membrane detachment.
- Routine methods (vasoconstrictor-soaked gauze, absorbable hemostat, bone wax, electrocautery) ineffective for wall bleeding.
- Provisional gutta percha point #100 → complete hemostasis.
- Titanium screw (1.4 mm × 6.0 mm) → complete and definite hemostasis immediately.
- Screw removal at 5 months: no complications; implant placed.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single case report; no controlled comparison or sample size.
- Technique requires direct visualization of the bleeding bone channel (distal bone edge removal).
- No claim about electrocautery efficacy in general — only that it was ineffective in this case.

## 7. Glossary

- **PSAA (Posterior Superior Alveolar Artery)**: artery supplying the lateral sinus wall, major feeder at risk during lateral-window sinus floor elevation.
- **Lateral window technique**: sinus floor elevation via an antrostomy in the lateral maxillary sinus wall.
- **Gutta percha point**: endodontic filling material used here as a provisional physical occluder.
- **Titanium screw (Le Forte System)**: 1.4 mm × 6.0 mm screw used to occlude the bone channel.
