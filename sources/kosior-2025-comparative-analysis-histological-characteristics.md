## Why Ingested

Same lead author (Kosior) as the existing [[wiki/implants/kosior-2025-histological-histomorphometric-implant-bed-preparation]] SR — that SR concluded classical rotary drilling can match alternative techniques "if rotation/cooling/load are optimal" but did not quantify which specific combination. This in vitro study directly tests that claim across 3 commercial drill systems × 3 speeds × 3 cooling conditions (54 osteotomies), providing the parameter-level evidence the SR's conclusion was pointing to; it also extends the drilling-heat cluster in [[wiki/overviews/dense-bone-osteotomy-heat-reimplantation-overview]].

## Three-line Summary

In vitro comparative histology study (J Clin Med 2025; 54 implant bed preparations in 4 swine ribs, 3 implant drill systems [Hiossen ET, Paltop, AnyRidge] × 3 speeds [800/1200/1500 rpm] × 3 cooling conditions [saline RT, saline 4°C, no cooling], DAPI/rhodamine fluorescence microscopy, 4-parameter 1-16 scoring, Kruskal-Wallis/Dunn's post hoc).

4°C saline cooling gave the highest bone-bed-quality score across all systems (best combos: Hiossen 1200 rpm/4°C = 15.3, Paltop 800 rpm/4°C = 15.2, AnyRidge 800 rpm/4°C = 13.7, out of 16); no cooling produced the lowest scores in every system, worsening further as speed increased (worst: Hiossen 1500 rpm/no cooling = 5.5), and each drill system had its own optimal speed (Hiossen best at 1200-1500 rpm, Paltop and AnyRidge best at 800 rpm).

Cooling temperature (4°C saline) was the single most influential variable on bone-bed quality across all three drill systems; the study used swine rib (not human) bone and did not control for operator technique or intrinsic drill-design differences, so in vivo human validation is needed before the specific speed/system recommendations are applied clinically.

## 세줄요약

체외(In Vitro) 비교 조직학 연구 (J Clin Med 2025; 돼지갈비뼈 4개, 임플란트 (Implant) 골와동 54개, 임플란트 드릴 시스템(Hiossen ET, Paltop, AnyRidge) × 속도(800/1200/1500 rpm) × 냉각조건(실온 식염수, 4°C 식염수, 무냉각), DAPI/로다민 형광현미경, 16점 만점 4항목 평가).

4°C 식염수 냉각이 모든 드릴 시스템에서 최고 골와동 품질 점수를 보였다(최적 조합: Hiossen 1200rpm/4°C=15.3점, Paltop 800rpm/4°C=15.2점, AnyRidge 800rpm/4°C=13.7점/16점); 무냉각은 모든 시스템에서 최저 점수였고 속도가 높을수록 더 악화되었으며(최악: Hiossen 1500rpm/무냉각=5.5점), 드릴 시스템마다 최적 속도가 달랐다(Hiossen은 1200-1500rpm, Paltop·AnyRidge는 800rpm에서 최적).

냉각 온도(4°C 식염수)가 세 드릴 시스템 모두에서 골와동 품질에 가장 큰 영향을 미치는 단일 변수였다; 돼지갈비뼈(인체 골 아님)를 사용했고 술자 숙련도나 드릴 고유 설계 차이를 통제하지 않아, 특정 속도·시스템 권고를 임상에 적용하기 전 인체 재현 연구가 필요하다.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Medicine 2025;14(7):2161
- **DOI**: 10.3390/jcm14072161
- **Institution**: Wroclaw Medical University; Wroclaw University of Environmental and Life Sciences, Poland

## 2. Key Contributions
- Direct head-to-head comparison of 3 commercial implant drill systems (Hiossen ET, Paltop, AnyRidge) under identical, systematically varied speed/cooling conditions on the same bone source.
- Introduces a standardized 4-parameter (surface smoothness, compressed tissue, carbonization, cracking/adjacent damage), 1-16 quantitative scoring system for qualitative histological grading of bone bed quality via fluorescence microscopy.
- Demonstrates that "optimal drilling protocol" is system-specific, not universal — each drill design has its own best-performing speed, but 4°C cooling is uniformly best across all systems.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In vitro comparative experimental study
- **Specimens**: 4 right-side swine ribs, 54 implant bed preparations (n=54), NeoSurge implant engine with 32:1 contra-angle handpiece, auto-reverse
- **Groups**: 3 implant systems × 3 speeds (800/1200/1500 rpm) × 3 cooling conditions (saline RT, saline 4°C, no cooling), n=6 per system-speed cell, n=2 per full combination
- **Outcomes**: Fluorescence microscopy (Nikon Eclipse 80i, DAPI + rhodamine staining, UV-2A filter, 40× magnification) scored 1-4 on each of 4 parameters (surface smoothness, compressed tissue presence, carbonization, cracking/adjacent tissue damage); single calibrated histologist rater; total score 0-16 classified Poor (0-7)/Medium (8-11)/Good (12-16)
- **Statistics**: Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn's post hoc comparisons (non-parametric; Jamovi 2.3, R 4.1)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Best overall combinations (total score/16): Hiossen 1200 rpm + 4°C saline = 15.3 (p=0.003 vs other Hiossen conditions); Paltop 800 rpm + 4°C saline = 15.2 (p=0.012); AnyRidge 800 rpm + room-temperature saline = best RT combo, AnyRidge 800 rpm + 4°C = 13.7 (p=0.021 cited in abstract for AnyRidge optimum).
- Across all systems, no cooling produced significantly more thermal damage, carbonization, and tissue fragmentation, especially at 1500 rpm (p<0.001); worst combination was Hiossen 1500 rpm + no cooling (score 5.5/16, Poor).
- Hiossen scored significantly higher than AnyRidge and Paltop on Surface Smoothness (Parameter 1) and higher than AnyRidge on Compressed Tissue Presence (Parameter 2).
- 800 rpm outperformed 1500 rpm across all 4 parameters and outperformed 1200 rpm on Parameters 1 and 4 — but this speed effect was confounded by the no-cooling subgroup (speed penalty was concentrated there).
- Note: the paper's own Abstract/Table 2 (Hiossen 1200/4°C best overall at 15.3) and its Section 5 Conclusions text (states Paltop was "most favorable" and Hiossen second) are internally inconsistent — this source page follows the Abstract + Table 2 numeric ranking as the more directly supported data.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Swine rib bone used as a human-bone surrogate; differs in cellular composition and mechanical/thermal response from human jawbone.
- Operator technique and drill-design/geometry differences (beyond speed/cooling) not controlled or isolated as variables.
- Single blinded-but-not-fully-independent-replicated histologist rater for qualitative scoring (calibration performed, but not multi-rater reliability).
- Authors explicitly call for in vivo human studies to validate clinical applicability of the specific speed/cooling combinations identified.

## 6. Related Work
- kosior-2025 (Histological/Histomorphometric SR, J Clin Med 14:4538): this in vitro study operationalizes/extends that SR's conclusion that optimized rotation/cooling/load lets classical drilling match alternative bed-preparation techniques.
- rugova-2024 (Thermal Evaluation of Bone Drilling): both find that cooling/irrigation and reduced speed lower thermal bone damage; Rugova used IR thermography (temperature), this paper uses fluorescence histology (tissue damage) — complementary outcome measures for the same mechanism.
- jung-2021 (Heat Development During Medical Drilling): narrative-review taxonomy (internal/external heat factors) that this paper's design (speed=internal, cooling=external) directly instantiates and quantifies.

## 7. Glossary
- **DAPI**: 4',6-diamidino-2-phenylindole — fluorescent nuclear stain used to visualize cell presence/viability in tissue sections.
- **Rhodamine**: fluorescent dye used alongside DAPI to differentiate non-mineralized tissue components and well-edge morphology.
- **Osteotomy**: the surgically prepared bone bed (drilled hole) into which a dental implant is placed.
- **Carbonization**: visible thermal charring of bone tissue lamellae, indicating heat damage exceeding the tissue's necrosis threshold.
- **Kruskal-Wallis test**: non-parametric statistical test for comparing three or more independent groups when data are not normally distributed (used here for ordinal quality scores).
