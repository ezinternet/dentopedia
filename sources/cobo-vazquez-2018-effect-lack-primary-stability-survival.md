## Why Ingested

위키의 low/absent primary stability 증거는 전부 immediate placement 맥락([[wiki/immediate-implant/primary-stability/jang-2025-flapless-immediate-implant-low-primary-stability]], [[wiki/immediate-implant/primary-stability/kim-2016-immediately-placed-implant-without-primary]])에 있었다. 이 코호트는 conventional(healed-site) placement에서 초기고정 부재가 생존에 미치는 영향을 정량화한 첫 연구로, "초기고정이 반드시 선결조건인가"라는 질문에 placement timing 축의 대조군을 추가한다.

## Three-line Summary
Cohort study (n=92 implants without primary stability, from 2,400 screened; conventional placement, ≥12-mo follow-up) classifying absent primary stability as B (light rotation with resistance), C (rotation without resistance), D (rotation + lateral oscillation).
Only 3 implants (3.26%) were lost — all in group C (7.89% of that group); no statistically significant effect of primary-stability grade (p=0.149), age, brand, or implant size on survival.
Clinical implication: poor/absent primary stability at conventional placement does not statistically predict implant loss, though group C (free rotation) showed a tendency toward greater early loss — a larger sample is needed.

## 세줄요약
코호트 연구 (초기고정 부재 92개 임플란트, 2,400개 스크리닝; 일반적(치유된 치조제) 식립, 12개월 이상 추적) — 부재 등급 B(저항감 있는 회전)·C(저항 없는 회전)·D(회전+측방동요)로 분류.
상실은 3개(3.26%)뿐이었고 모두 C군(해당군의 7.89%); 초기고정 등급(p=0.149)·나이·브랜드·크기는 생존에 통계적으로 유의한 영향 없음.
임상적 의미: 일반 식립에서 초기고정의 부재는 통계적으로 임플란트 상실을 예측하지 못하나, 완전 유리 회전(C)은 조기 상실 경향을 보임 — 더 큰 표본 연구 필요.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical and Experimental Dentistry 2018;10(1):e14-e19
- **DOI**: 10.4317/jced.54441
- **Institution**: Faculty of Dentistry, Complutense University of Madrid, Spain

## 2. Key Contributions
- First study to evaluate the lack of primary stability (during conventional loading) with implant survival as the outcome — prior work only analyzed stability presence/degree.
- Quantified that only 3/92 (3.26%) implants placed without primary stability failed, with all failures confined to the most compromised stable group (C: implant rotates without resistance).
- Provided a negative result — patient age, implant brand, and implant size did not influence survival among implants lacking primary stability.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Retrospective cohort (Master of Oral Surgery and Implantology, Complutense University of Madrid)
- **Sample**: 2,400 implants placed Sept 2011–July 2016 screened; final sample 92 implants placed without primary stability with ≥12-month follow-up
- **Inclusion**: surgical report documenting absence of primary stability; complete clinical/surgical data; ≥12-mo follow-up; manufacturer-recommended drilling sequence followed
- **Exclusion**: head/neck irradiation, uncontrolled diabetes, uncontrolled periodontal disease; immediate (post-extraction) placements excluded by design
- **Primary stability classification** (Rodrigo et al. criteria): B = light rotation with resistance; C = rotation without resistance; D = rotation + lateral oscillation
- **Outcomes**: implant survival (early loss); variables — age, sex, smoking, ASA, bone grafts, residual bone height, region, adjacency, implant length/diameter/design/manufacturer
- **Statistics**: Frequenties, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Fisher's exact / Chi-square, ANOVA, Student t (SPSS 22.0)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Stability grade distribution**: B 49 (53.26%), C 38 (41.30%), D 5 (5.44%)
- **Implant loss**: 3/92 (3.26%) overall; all three in group C → 3/38 (7.89% of group C); no losses in B or D
- **Group comparison**: no statistically significant difference in early-loss distribution across stability groups (p=0.149); corrected residual showed loss in C (7.9%) was statistically greater than expected (3.3%) in the contingency table
- **Age**: no difference between stability groups (p=0.88) or lost vs surviving (p=0.658)
- **Manufacturer**: no statistical relationship with loss (p=0.09) — lost implants were NOBEL (2 NobelReplace tapered groovy; 1 Brånemark MKIII straight groovy), sizes 4.3×10, 4.3×13, 3.3×15 mm
- **Cohort composition**: 59.8% female; 13% smokers; bone quality — type I 21.7%, II 22.8%, IV 55.4%; GBR used in 25%

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Small final sample (n=92) and single-institution protocol; selection of "implant without primary stability" and clinician tactile sensitivity are inherently subjective.
- Only conventional, manufacturer-sequence drilling used — results not generalizable to undersized/osteotome/expander-modified protocols.
- Authors call for larger samples to identify the influence of macrodesign and surface during osseointegration when primary stability was not achieved.

## 7. Glossary
- **Primary stability**: biomechanical engagement of an implant with bone at insertion; influenced by bone quantity/quality, implant geometry, surgical technique, insertion torque.
- **Secondary stability**: biological fixation from new bone formation around the implant after osseointegration.
- **RFA (Resonance Frequency Analysis)**: digital stability measurement (Osstell) based on resonance frequency of a transducer attached to the implant; outputs ISQ.
- **Periotest**: percussion-based digital stability device; authors note both RFA and Periotest have accuracy controversies.
- **Lekholm-Zarb (1985) classification**: bone quality (I–IV) and bone resorption (A–E) grading of the implant recipient site.
