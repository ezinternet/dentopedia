## Why Ingested

WHO 2018 권고("절개 봉합 즉시 항생제 예방투여 중단")를 근거로 종합한 최초의 umbrella review로, [[wiki/drug/antibiotics/mohammadpour-2025-antibiotic-prophylaxis-oral-surgery-sr]]가 제시한 "24시간 이후 연장 투여는 SSI(Surgical Site Infection) 감소 효과 없음" 결론을 일반외과 9개 메타분석(pooled RR 1.07)으로 독립적으로 재확인(reinforces)한다. 구강악안면외과 항생제 스튜어드십 논의에 일반외과 근거를 교차검증하는 자료로 채택.

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (systematic review of reviews) of 28 review articles (>457 primary studies) screened from 1156 records, synthesizing post-WHO-2018 evidence on discontinuing postoperative antibiotic prophylaxis (PAP) after surgical incision closure across general surgical settings.

Pooled meta-analysis of 9 reviews (80,483 patients) found no significant benefit to continuing PAP beyond 24 h post-surgery versus immediate discontinuation (RR 1.07, 95% CI 0.97–1.17, I²=25%, p=0.22); AMSTAR-2 rated review quality as critically low (16/28) to low (12/28), none high/moderate.

Supports WHO/CDC recommendation to stop PAP at incision closure; recommends de-implementation strategies (guideline refinement, multidisciplinary collaboration, resource availability, provider/patient education) to close the persistent practice-evidence gap, especially in LMICs.

## 세줄요약

우산 리뷰(review of reviews) — 1156편 중 스크리닝된 28개 리뷰(원저 457편 이상), WHO 2018 권고 이후 수술창 봉합 후 수술후 항생제 예방투여(Postoperative Antibiotic Prophylaxis, PAP) 중단에 관한 근거를 종합.

9개 메타분석 통합(환자 80,483명): 수술 후 24시간 초과 PAP 지속이 즉시 중단 대비 유의한 이득 없음(RR 1.07, 95% CI 0.97–1.17, I²=25%, p=0.22); AMSTAR-2 품질평가 결과 대부분 critically low(16/28)~low(12/28), high/moderate 없음.

WHO/CDC의 절개 봉합 시점 PAP 중단 권고를 뒷받침; 가이드라인 정비·다학제 협력·자원 확보·의료진/환자 교육 등 탈이행(de-implementation) 전략으로 실제 임상관행과 근거 간 지속되는 간극(특히 LMIC)을 좁힐 것을 권고.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. First umbrella review synthesizing PAP discontinuation evidence specifically following the WHO 2018 recommendations
2. Pooled risk ratio across 9 meta-analyses (80,483 patients) directly comparing PAP discontinuation (≤24 h) vs. continuation (>24 h)
3. Formal AMSTAR-2 methodological quality appraisal of all 28 included reviews — none rated high or moderate quality
4. Narrative synthesis of five de-implementation strategy themes for stopping unnecessary PAP: guideline refinement, multidisciplinary collaboration, resource availability, provider education/stewardship training, patient education
5. Identification of exceptions favoring continued PAP in specific contexts (cardiac/heart surgery review by Chesdachai 2022; non-best-practice-adherent studies in de Jonge 2020 sensitivity analysis)

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Umbrella review (systematic review of reviews) + meta-analysis of pooled review-level effect estimates
- **Registration/reporting**: PROSPERO CRD42024566124; protocol per PRISMA-P; reporting per PRIOR (Preferred Reporting Items for Overviews of Reviews)
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE and Scopus, search strategy built with PRESS guidance, MeSH terms
- **Search window**: Inception to 5 July 2024, English language, human subjects
- **Eligibility (PICO)**:
  - Population: humans undergoing surgery considered for antibiotic prophylaxis to prevent SSI
  - Intervention: immediate discontinuation of PAP at incision closure, or ≤24 h postoperative antibiotics
  - Comparator: continuation of PAP >24 h postoperatively
  - Outcome: incidence of SSI (meta-analyses); strategies/best practices for SSI prevention (narrative synthesis)
  - Design: systematic reviews, meta-analyses, scoping reviews, literature reviews published after 2018 (aligned with WHO guideline evidence base)
- **Screening**: Covidence software; two independent reviewers (R.B.M., G.M.B.), third-reviewer (M.V.M.) arbitration
- **Quality appraisal**: AMSTAR-2, 16 domains (7 critical), independently by two reviewers (G.G.N., B.J.N.), arbitrated by G.M.B.; overall quality categorized high/moderate/low/critically low
- **Statistics**: Risk ratio (RR) with fixed-effects model (Mantel-Haenszel), 95% CI, I² for heterogeneity (>50% = high); subgroup analysis by surgical site; R version 4.2.3; significance at 95% CI excluding 1 or p<0.05
- **Publication bias**: funnel plot of treatment effect vs. standard error across included reviews

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Screening yield**: 1156 articles screened → 28 review articles included (>457 primary studies)
- **Review composition**: 14/28 compared PAP discontinuation vs. continuation for SSI prevention; 13/28 synthesized best-practice strategies; 1 review (Cooper 2020) covered both
- **Pooled meta-analysis** (9 reviews, 80,483 patients): RR 1.07 (95% CI 0.97–1.17, I²=25%, p=0.22) — discontinuation within 24 h shows no statistically significant increase in SSI risk vs. continuation
- **Exceptions**: Chesdachai 2022 (heart surgery) favored continuation (RR 1.38, 95% CI 1.09–1.74); de Jonge 2020 including non-best-practice-adherent studies also favored continuation (RR 1.12, 95% CI 1.03–1.21, p=0.30); de Jonge 2020 subgroup — adherent to best practice RR 1.04 (95% CI 0.85–1.27, ns), non-adherent RR 0.79 (95% CI 0.67–0.94, favored continuation)
- **Quality**: 0/28 reviews rated high or moderate quality; 16/28 critically low; 12/28 low. Main deficiency: no justified list of excluded full-text studies (AMSTAR-2 critical domain 7); 14/28 did not pre-register protocol; only 1/28 addressed included-study funding sources
- **Strategy themes (narrative synthesis, 5)**: (1) reassessing/refining guidelines per surgical setting, (2) multidisciplinary collaboration (ID specialists, pharmacists, infection-control nurses), (3) resource availability (sterile equipment, antimicrobials, protocols), (4) provider education on SSI prevention/antibiotic stewardship, (5) patient education/shared decision-making

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No assessment of the impact of best-practice adherence on SSI prevention across reviews (only de Jonge 2020 conducted this subgroup analysis)
- Possible double-counting: a single primary study may be included in multiple reviews (inherent umbrella-review limitation)
- Overall review quality uniformly low/critically low by AMSTAR-2 — limits confidence in underlying primary-study pooling
- Authors call for future RCTs specifically testing de-implementation strategies, longitudinal antibiotic-resistance outcomes, cost-effectiveness analyses, and patient-centered adherence/satisfaction studies, especially in LMIC settings where non-evidence-based PAP persists

## 6. Related Work

- WHO (2018) global guidelines for prevention of surgical site infection — primary trigger recommendation this review synthesizes evidence around
- CDC guideline for prevention of SSI — concordant with WHO on perioperative-only antibiotic prophylaxis
- de Jonge et al. (2020) — key included review with best-practice-adherence subgroup/sensitivity analysis
- Chesdachai et al. (2022) — cardiac surgery review, notable exception favoring continued PAP
- Related dental/OMFS-domain synthesis in this wiki: [[wiki/drug/antibiotics/mohammadpour-2025-antibiotic-prophylaxis-oral-surgery-sr]] (independently reaches the same >24 h no-benefit conclusion for oral and maxillofacial surgery)

## 7. Glossary

- **PAP (Postoperative Antibiotic Prophylaxis)**: 수술 후 항생제 예방투여 — antibiotics continued after surgical incision closure to prevent SSI
- **SSI (Surgical Site Infection)**: 수술부위감염
- **Umbrella review**: 우산 리뷰 (review of reviews) — a systematic review that synthesizes findings from multiple existing systematic reviews/meta-analyses
- **AMSTAR-2**: A MeaSurement Tool to Assess systematic Reviews, version 2 — methodological quality appraisal instrument for systematic reviews
- **PRIOR**: Preferred Reporting Items for Overviews of Reviews — reporting guideline for umbrella reviews
- **De-implementation**: 탈이행 — the process of reducing or stopping low-value or unsupported clinical practices (here, unnecessary PAP continuation)
- **RR (Risk Ratio)**: 위험비 — ratio of SSI incidence in discontinuation group vs. continuation group
