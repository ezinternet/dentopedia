## Why Ingested

This SR directly extends [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/delgado-2025-thermoplastics-clear-aligners-review]], which provided the material-science conceptual framework (PETG vs TPU vs DPA polymer chemistry), by supplying the first comprehensive empirical systematic evidence across 95 studies quantifying how those material differences translate into measurable degradation: pooled ΔE staining values, force-decay curves, and morphological deterioration by polymer type and brand. It also reinforces [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/kobkiatkawin-2025-clear-aligner-color-stability-brand]]'s 4-brand in vitro finding that TPU/Invisalign exceeds the clinically perceptible ΔE threshold, now situating it within a broader 95-study synthesis.

## One-line Summary

Systematic review (95 studies, PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251110248) of clear aligner material aging across four property domains — chemical, physical, mechanical, morphological — finding material-dependent deterioration with pooled coffee ΔE = 70.9 (Invisalign consistently exceeding the clinically perceptible threshold) and force decay of 40–90% within 48 h, with TPU-based and directly printed aligners (DPAs) generally more susceptible than PETG.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **First comprehensive multi-domain material aging SR**: Prior SRs were either single-brand (Bakdach et al. — LD30 Invisalign only) or excluded chemical/morphological outcomes (Srinivasan et al.). Almotairy 2026 integrates all four property domains across 95 studies and multiple brands/materials.
2. **Pooled discoloration by beverage**: Weighted mean ΔE (7-day, 37 °C, comparable protocols): coffee = 70.9 >> tea = 18.4 > red wine = 11.5; Invisalign (TPU) consistently exceeds the clinically perceptible threshold (ΔE ≥ 3.3) across studies.
3. **Force decay quantified**: 40–90% of initial force lost within the first 48 h; rate and magnitude are material-dependent, directly informing aligner replacement intervals.
4. **TPU/DPA > PETG vulnerability**: TPU-based and directly printed aligners are consistently more susceptible to staining, surface morphological changes, and energy absorption reduction under cyclic loading; hardness/roughness/stiffness findings are inconsistent across studies.
5. **Trace elemental release documented**: Chemical composition is largely stable, but trace elemental release is brand-dependent (e.g., mercury detected in Eon brand in one study); warrants awareness for biocompatibility.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Systematic review; PRISMA 2020 reporting; no formal meta-analysis due to substantial methodological heterogeneity
- **PICOS**:
  - P: Plastics used to fabricate clear aligner appliances (multiple brands and material types)
  - I: Intraoral or simulated ageing (thermocycling, cyclic loading, liquid immersion, artificial saliva)
  - C: Different CA brands and material types
  - O: Chemical (composition changes), Physical (color/optical, water sorption, thermal), Mechanical (elastic modulus, tensile/flexural strength, hardness, stiffness, stress relaxation, force decay), Morphological (surface roughness, cracks, porosities)
  - S: In vivo, in vitro, and ex vivo studies
- **Exclusions**: Cytotoxicity, adverse reactions, microbial accumulation; case reports; review articles
- **Study selection**: 2471 records retrieved → 1075 after deduplication → 940 excluded at abstract screening → 40 excluded at full-text screening → 95 included
- **Risk of bias (RoB)**: QUIN for in vitro studies; JBI for cohort in vivo; Cochrane RoB 2 for RCTs; low 8 studies, moderate 62, high 25
- **Pooled estimates**: Where seven studies used comparable 7-day immersion protocols at 37 °C (coffee, tea, or red wine), a simple pooled weighted mean ΔE was calculated as Σ(n × ΔE) / Σn (no formal meta-analytic weighting; SDs not available for all)
- **Inter-reviewer agreement**: Cohen's kappa ≥ 0.81 (good-to-excellent)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Chemical Properties
- Overall, CA chemical composition remained largely stable during ageing across most studies
- Ester bond hydrolysis to carboxylic acid and alcohol groups observed after intraoral PETG use (ATR-FTIR; Arabpour 2025)
- Trace elemental release is brand-dependent: mercury detected in Eon aligner (EDX 17.31%; Alhendi 2022); nitrogen content highest in Invisalign (46.36%)
- Hot solutions (55 °C coffee/water) may increase FTIR peak intensity suggesting possible hydrolysis; cold and control solutions remained within clinical limits

### Physical Properties — Discolouration
- **Pooled weighted mean ΔE (7-day, 37 °C, comparable protocols):**
  - Coffee: ΔE = **70.9** (n from 7 comparable studies pooled)
  - Tea: ΔE = **18.4**
  - Red wine: ΔE = **11.5**
- Clinically perceptible threshold: ΔE ≥ 3.3; Invisalign (TPU) consistently exceeds this across staining studies
- PETG-based brands (Beforedent, Käse, MTM) show substantially lower ΔE, often within acceptable range
- Cleaning protocols (Invisalign foam, retainer brite, sonic cleaner) reduce but do not eliminate staining; whitening toothpaste yields lowest ΔE after intraoral use (3.21 vs 7.15 for Crystals in one RCT)

### Mechanical Properties — Force Decay
- **Force decay: 40–90% of initial force within 48 h** — range reflects material and study heterogeneity
- Stress relaxation studies: F22 Evoflex (TPU) showed highest stress retention at 15 days (39.2%); Duran (PETG) lowest (3.9%); general pattern of rapid decay in the first 0–8 h then plateau
- TPU aligners (Ghost Aligner, SmartTrack Invisalign): greater energy absorption decrease and more severe localized cracking under cyclic compression vs PETG (Barile 2024, 2025)
- PETG energy absorption stable under cyclic loading (~4 Nmm); TPU decreases significantly (7→2.6 Nmm)
- Directly printed aligners (DPAs): energy absorption decreases progressively; extremely poor stress relaxation retention (~10% vs PETG ~80%); some AM-PU types show gradual, less severe damage

### Mechanical Properties — Hardness, Stiffness, Modulus
- Findings inconsistent across studies; no clear directional consensus
- Both increases and decreases in hardness reported after thermocycling and beverage immersion
- Hot solutions (55 °C) had greatest impact on dimensional change; cold and control remained within clinical limits (<0.25 mm)
- Erkodur (PETG) > Duran (PETG) in hardness but Duran > Erkodur in elastic modulus; significance of post-thermocycling change was material-specific

### Morphological Properties
- Surface roughness increases after ageing in most studies, though magnitude and direction vary with material and ageing method
- TPU (especially Ghost Aligner) showed severe localized cracking under cyclic compression; PETG showed only minor damage; AM-PU minimal acoustic emission signals
- Microplastic (MP) release: all three tested brands released MPs; PETG (Essix Ace) produced MPs >30 μm; TPU (Ghost, Invisalign) produced MPs <30 μm with significantly smaller axis lengths (Barile 2025)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Substantial methodological heterogeneity**: precludes formal meta-analysis across all outcomes; pooled estimates only feasible for a small subset of comparable discolouration studies
- **High RoB in 26%** of studies (25/95); only 8 studies at low risk
- **Single-brand focus** in many included studies, limiting generalizability to non-Invisalign materials
- **Inconsistent outcomes**: hardness, roughness, stiffness findings are contradictory across studies; standardized testing protocols are absent
- **Clinical translation gap**: extent to which aging-induced material changes translate into clinically meaningful differences in tooth movement remains uncertain
- **Microplastic clinical significance**: MP release from all tested brands documented but health implications not yet established
- **No grey literature or clinical trial registry search**: possible publication bias toward studies showing significant degradation
- **Need for standardized protocols**: head-to-head brand comparisons under identical conditions are rare

## 7. Glossary

- **CA (Clear Aligner)**: Removable orthodontic appliance fabricated from thermoplastic polymer sheets or directly printed resins
- **DPA (Directly Printed Aligner)**: Aligners manufactured by additive manufacturing (3D printing) rather than thermoforming over a model
- **ΔE (Delta E / CIELAB colour difference)**: Numerical difference in CIELAB colour space; threshold ΔE ≥ 3.3 = clinically perceptible discolouration
- **Force decay / Stress relaxation**: Reduction of force delivered by the aligner over time due to viscoelastic polymer behaviour; critical determinant of whether clinically adequate force persists until the next replacement
- **PETG (Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol-modified)**: Most common polyester-based CA material; generally more colour-stable, harder, and more dimensionally stable than TPU under ageing
- **TPU (Thermoplastic Polyurethane)**: Polyurethane-based CA material (e.g., Invisalign SmartTrack); higher carbamate group polarity drives greater water/pigment absorption and staining susceptibility
- **ATR-FTIR**: Attenuated Total Reflectance Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy — used to identify polymer bond composition and detect chemical changes after ageing
- **RoB (Risk of Bias)**: QUIN for purely in vitro, JBI for cohort in vivo, Cochrane RoB 2 for RCTs
- **Pooled weighted mean ΔE**: Σ(n × ΔE) / Σn — simple sample-size-weighted mean across comparable studies; not a formal meta-analytic effect estimate
- **Microplastic (MP)**: Small plastic fragments released from aligner materials under cyclic loading; clinical health significance currently unknown
