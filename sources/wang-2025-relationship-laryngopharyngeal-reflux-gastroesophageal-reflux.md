## Why Ingested

The wiki's erosion pages state that reflux is an intrinsic acid source (see [[dental-erosion/kanzow-2016-etiology-pathogenesis-dental-erosion]], [[dental-erosion/west-2014-enamel-mineral-loss]]) but only as narrative-review assertion and no page held systematic evidence for it. This systematic review supplies that evidence for adults and underpins the "palatal wear suggests intrinsic origin, refer to medicine" advice in [[overviews/dental-erosion-etiology-prevention-synthesis]].

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (Dig Dis Sci 2025; 4 databases searched July 1999 to June 2024, 1382 records screened, 22 studies included) of GERD/laryngopharyngeal reflux (LPR) and dental erosion in adults.

Dental erosion was significantly more prevalent in adults with GERD or LPR than in healthy controls, and subjects with erosion had a higher prevalence of LPR or GERD (abstract reports no pooled effect size).

The association supports screening for reflux in patients with erosion and vice versa, but heterogeneous outcome measures, diagnostic methods and reflux definitions limit precision; this record is abstract-level only.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review, SR; Dig Dis Sci 2025) — 4개 데이터베이스 (1999.7~2024.6) 1382편 중 22편 포함, 성인의 위식도역류질환 (Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease, GERD) 및 후두인두역류 (Laryngopharyngeal Reflux, LPR)와 치아침식 (Dental Erosion)의 관계.

GERD 또는 LPR 성인에서 치아침식 유병률이 건강 대조군보다 유의하게 높았고, 반대로 치아침식 환자에서도 LPR/GERD 유병률이 더 높았음 (초록에 통합 효과크기 없음).

침식 환자의 역류 선별과 역류 환자의 치과 검진 근거가 되나, 결과 지표·진단법·질환 정의의 이질성이 크고 본 기록은 초록 수준이다.

## 1. Document Information

- Journal: Digestive Diseases and Sciences 2025;70(3):1078-1090 (e-pub 2025-01-18). PMID 39826062. Source: PubMed. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10620-024-08824-9)
- Authors' affiliations: otorhinolaryngology (Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital South Campus; Qilu Hospital, Shandong University) and Shanghai Xuhui District Dental Center.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Adult-population systematic review linking both GERD and LPR (not GERD alone) to dental erosion.
- Bidirectional finding: higher erosion prevalence in reflux patients, higher reflux prevalence in erosion patients.
- Calls for reflux to be defined by a universal guideline in future studies.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, Scopus; English-language literature July 1999 to June 2024; grey literature via OpenSIGLE and ICTRP; manual reference-list search.
- 1382 records identified; 22 studies met inclusion criteria. Adults only.
- Risk-of-bias tool, meta-analysis status and study designs of included studies are not stated in the abstract. The record is typed as a systematic review without meta-analysis (PubMed article type: Systematic Review).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Prevalence of dental erosion significantly higher in adults with GERD or LPR vs healthy populations.
- Subjects with dental erosion had greater prevalence of LPR or GERD.
- No numeric effect sizes, prevalence figures or heterogeneity statistics are given in the abstract.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Abstract-only record: per-study data, quality appraisal and effect sizes not retrieved.
- Authors flag heterogeneity in clinical outcome evaluation, diagnostic methods and disease definitions.
- Cross-sectional associations cannot establish direction of causation (reviewer inference; the abstract itself describes a bidirectional association).

## 7. Glossary

- GERD (Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease): reflux of gastric contents into the oesophagus.
- LPR (Laryngopharyngeal Reflux): reflux reaching the larynx/pharynx, often without typical heartburn.
- Dental erosion: acid-related, non-bacterial loss of tooth structure.
