## Why Ingested

Three-way randomized comparison — bicortical fixation vs indirect sinus elevation (greenstick fracture + graft) vs unicortical — measuring ISQ in 7–11 mm RBH posterior maxilla. Key finding: primary stability NOT statistically different among three techniques (p=0.13), but secondary stability converges to identical (~80 ISQ) by 3–6 months. Provides the RCT-level evidence that bicortical fixation can replace indirect sinus elevation without sacrificing stability, directly supporting [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/bhave-2023-unicortical-bicortical-anchorage-posterior-maxilla]] and [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/ng-2018-bicortical-engagement-atrophic-posterior-maxillae]].

## Three-line Summary

Randomized 3-arm trial, 38 patients / 45 implants, 7–11 mm RBH posterior maxilla; bicortical fixation (stopper-drill to engage both crests) vs indirect sinus elevation (greenstick + graft) vs unicortical fixation; ISQ measured immediately and at 3–6 months.
Primary ISQ: indirect sinus highest (75.9) > bicortical (71.4) > unicortical (69.6) — but NO statistically significant difference (p=0.13); secondary ISQ all converged to ~80 (p>0.999).
Primary stability is highest with indirect sinus elevation but not significantly different; secondary stability is identical; bicortical is simpler and more economical than indirect sinus elevation and allows longer implants than unicortical.

## Key Contributions

- Randomized 3-way comparison: bicortical vs ISE vs unicortical in 7–11 mm RBH
- Non-significant primary stability difference (p=0.13) — bicortical not inferior to ISE
- Identical secondary stability (ISQ ~80) across all three techniques at 3–6 months
- Primary ISQ significantly correlated with CBCT bone density (r=0.37)
- Bicortical uses stopper drills to engage both alveolar crest and sinus floor simultaneously — no osteotome required
- Recommends bicortical as the preferred technique: simpler, cheaper, allows longer implants

## Methodology

- **Design**: Randomized 3-arm trial
- **Inclusion**: 7–11 mm bone coronal to sinus floor (CBCT confirmed)
- **Bicortical (n=?)**: Stopper drills to bicortically engage alveolar crest AND sinus floor; implant: 4.7 mm D × 11.4 mm L mean
- **Unicortical (n=?)**: Stop 1 mm short of sinus floor; 4.3 mm D × 8.1 mm L mean
- **Indirect sinus elevation (n=?)**: Greenstick fracture + bone graft; 4.7 mm D × 11.8 mm L mean
- **ISQ**: Osstell, 6 measurements buccolingually, immediately post-placement and at stage-2 (3–6 months)
- **Statistics**: ANOVA + correlation

## Key Results

| Technique | Primary ISQ (mean ± SE) | Secondary ISQ |
|---|---|---|
| Indirect sinus elevation | 75.9 ± 2.3 | 80.0 ± 1.3 |
| Bicortical fixation | 71.4 ± 2.1 | 79.9 ± 1.2 |
| Unicortical fixation | 69.6 ± 2.1 | 80.0 ± 1.2 |

- Primary ISQ: p=0.13 (no significant difference)
- Secondary ISQ: p>0.999 (all identical)
- Primary ISQ ↔ CBCT bone density: r=0.37 (p significant)
- Implant dimensions differed significantly across groups (p=0.03 diameter, p<0.001 length)

## Limitations

- RBH restricted to 7–11 mm — results may not apply to more atrophic cases (<7 mm)
- Max 2 implants per patient; allocation by opposite sides (possible pairing effect)
- Abstract-only extraction; full methodology details unavailable
- Short-term follow-up to stage-2 surgery only; no long-term survival or MBL

## Glossary

- **Bicortical fixation**: Engaging both alveolar crest cortex AND sinus floor cortex with the implant; achieved here by stopper-drill technique
- **Indirect sinus elevation (ISE)**: Osteotome/greenstick technique elevating sinus floor with graft material
- **Unicortical**: Implant engages crestal cortex only; tip stops short of sinus floor
- **CBCT bone density**: Hounsfield-unit-based density from CBCT; r=0.37 with primary ISQ
