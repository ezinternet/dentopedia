## Why Ingested
치은상피세포 (Gingival Epithelial Cell)의 증식 및 창상 봉합에 대한 PRGF와 PRP의 직접 비교. [[periodontics/glim-2013-detrimental-dermal-wound-healing]]의 백혈구·성장인자 차이가 치유에 영향을 미친다는 주장을 in vitro 치은상피 모델로 검증한 첫 번째 논문.

## One-line Summary
In vitro study (n=3 donors) comparing PRGF and L-PRP on human gingival epithelial progenitor proliferation and wound closure: PRGF significantly enhanced proliferation on days 1–2, but both equally failed to improve wound closure vs. controls.

## 한줄요약
시험관 연구(기증자 n=3): PRGF가 치은상피 전구세포 증식을 1–2일차에 PRP보다 유의하게 촉진하지만(p<0.05), 두 군 모두 창상 봉합률은 대조군 대비 차이 없음.

## 1. Document Information
- Journal: European Journal of Dentistry, Vol. 19, No. 4, pp. 1055–1062, 2025
- Published online: January 20, 2025
- Institution: Matsumoto Dental University, Nagano, Japan
- Ethics: Matsumoto Dental University Ethics Committee (No. 0363); Declaration of Helsinki 2013

## 2. Key Contributions
- First in vitro study directly comparing PRGF vs. L-PRP effects on gingival epithelial cells (proliferation + wound closure + gene expression)
- PRGF leukocyte count median 0.3 × 10/mL vs. PRP 6.0 × 10/mL — confirmed meaningful leukocyte removal
- Established that early proliferative advantage of PRGF (days 1–2) does not translate to faster wound closure
- Showed TNF-α and integrin β4 mRNA are not significantly altered by either concentrate in this model
- EGF concentrations in PRGF (489 pg/mL) and PRP (279 pg/mL) are below the threshold (5,000–10,000 pg/mL) needed to upregulate integrin β4

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: In vitro, controlled; HGEPp (normal human gingival epithelium progenitors, pooled; CELLnTEC)
- Donors: 3 healthy male volunteers (ages 28, 30, 38); blood centrifuged at 580g × 8 min (Endoret BTI system)
- PRGF preparation: whole plasma column excluding leukocyte layer
- Controls: Positive (complete medium with BPE + growth factors) vs. Negative (BPE-free)
- Proliferation: WST-1 assay at days 1, 2, 3, 7, 14; n=1,000 cells/well (2,857 cells/cm²)
- Wound healing: cell-free culture insert (NUNC) model, 500 µm gap, measured at 8–24 h with ImageJ
- Gene expression: RT-PCR for TNF-α, integrin β4 (normalised to β-actin)
- Statistics: Kruskal–Wallis test (nonparametric)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Proliferation (WST-1):**
  - Day 1: PRGF > PRP (p=0.008); PRP < PC (p=0.009)
  - Day 2: PRGF > PRP (p=0.003); PRGF > NC (p=0.036); PRP < PC (p=0.012)
  - Days 3, 7, 14: no significant difference between PRGF and PRP
- **Wound closure rate:** No significant difference PRGF vs. PRP vs. respective controls at any time point (p>0.05)
- **Gene expression:** TNF-α and integrin β4 mRNA — no significant difference among all groups (p>0.05)
- **Leukocyte count:** PRGF 0.3 × 10/mL vs. PRP 6.0 × 10/mL (median); trend not significant (p=0.089)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Only 3 donors (Mongoloid males 20s–30s) vs. commercial cell line (Caucasoid females 20s) — mismatch in demographics and species of origin
- Growth factor and interleukin concentrations not quantified in this study's PRGF/PRP samples
- Single-donor wound healing assay (38-year-old male); no replication across donors
- Wound model uses scratch/insert gap; does not replicate in vivo gingival architecture

## 6. Related Work
- Glim 2013 — oral mucosal vs. dermal wound healing mechanisms
- Ohshima 2002 — EGF in saliva drives oral epithelial migration; discusses EGF threshold for integrin effects
- Dawes 2003 — physiological oral mucosal epithelial turnover rate (baseline context)

## 7. Glossary
- **PRGF (Plasma Rich in Growth Factors)**: platelet concentrate with near-complete leukocyte exclusion; Endoret/BTI system
- **L-PRP (Leukocyte-Platelet Rich Plasma)**: PRP retaining leukocyte-containing buffy coat fraction
- **HGEPp**: normal human gingival epithelium progenitors (pooled commercial cell line)
- **WST-1 assay**: colorimetric tetrazolium salt assay for viable cell count; absorbance 450/620 nm
- **integrin β4**: cell adhesion molecule contributing to epithelial migration and laminin binding
