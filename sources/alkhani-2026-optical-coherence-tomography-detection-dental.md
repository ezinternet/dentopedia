## Why Ingested

VRF/crack diagnosis in the wiki is currently anchored on radiographs, CBCT, and clinical adjuncts (transillumination, QLF, dye) as covered in [[cracked-tooth/kaur-2026-cracked-tooth-syndrome-umbrella-review]] and [[cracked-tooth/kindaro-2025-diagnostic-accuracy-detect-cracked-teeth]]; this scoping review adds a distinct, non-ionizing imaging modality (OCT) that has not been represented in the wiki, mapping its diagnostic accuracy (sensitivity 83%–98%, specificity 63%–100%) and — critically — its current technical barrier (1–3 mm penetration depth) that explains why OCT still cannot substitute CBCT as the reference standard for VRF diagnosis.

## Three-line Summary

Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR, 5 databases + grey literature, 10 in-vitro studies, 2008–2021, n=6–80 teeth per study) of optical coherence tomography (OCT) for detecting dental cracks and vertical root fractures (VRF) across TD-OCT, SD-OCT, and SS-OCT systems.

OCT showed sensitivity 83%–98% and specificity 63%–100%, outperforming visual inspection and transillumination in head-to-head comparisons; the two delivery approaches (catheter-based intracanal vs. non-invasive crown scanning) differ in penetration depth and procedural invasiveness.

OCT offers promising non-ionizing, high-resolution crack detection, but a 1–3 mm penetration depth limit and absence of intraoral/root-canal-compatible probe designs currently confine it to laboratory use — all 10 studies were in-vitro, and no in-vivo clinical data exist to support chairside implementation over CBCT.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First scoping review specifically synthesizing OCT's use for detecting dental cracks (including craze lines, fractured cusps, cracked tooth, split tooth) and vertical root fractures (VRF), across the full spectrum of crack severity per the AAE classification.
- Consolidates diagnostic performance metrics (sensitivity/specificity) across heterogeneous OCT systems (TD-OCT, SD-OCT, SS-OCT) and study designs into a single comparative table.
- Produces a structured technology comparison table (OCT vs. ultrasound vs. conventional radiographs vs. CBCT/micro-CT) mapping strengths, limitations, and current endodontic applications of each.
- Identifies the specific engineering bottleneck (probe size requiring ISO 50–90 K-file canal preparation, near-infrared wavelength limiting penetration to 1–3 mm) blocking OCT's transition from bench to chairside for VRF diagnosis.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Scoping review following the five-stage framework of Mak and Thomas, reported per PRISMA-ScR; protocol pre-registered on OSF.
- **Research question (PCC framework)**: Population = teeth with cracks or VRF; Concept = use of OCT imaging; Context = in vitro, ex vivo, or clinical studies; eligible study types = original studies, theses, or book chapters from the last 20 years.
- **Search strategy**: PubMed, Scopus, Embase, EBSCOhost, Google Scholar searched through January 2025, no language restriction; supplemented by ProQuest Dissertations and Theses Global, EBSCO Open Dissertations, university repositories, and manual reference-list/hand searches of specialty journals.
- **Screening**: Three independent reviewers screened titles/abstracts and then full texts; a fourth reviewer was available for disagreements (not needed — consensus reached by discussion).
- **Data extraction**: Authors, year, study design, sample size, hard-tissue type, diagnostic method, outcomes, conclusions, journal, conflicts of interest — recorded in Excel.
- **Bias handling**: No formal risk-of-bias tool applied (consistent with scoping-review methodology per Tricco et al.), but broad multi-database searching without language restriction and inclusion of grey literature aimed to reduce selection/publication bias.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Search yield**: 28,268 records identified across 5 databases (Google Scholar 27,650; PubMed 189; Embase 224; Scopus 120; EBSCO 85) plus 35 from manual/reference search. After deduplication (416) and irrelevant-citation removal (27,369), 518 remained for screening. Title/abstract screening excluded 482, leaving 36 for full-text review (2 inaccessible). Of 34 assessed, 24 excluded (5 reviews, 9 innovation-only/no device testing, 10 unrelated to endodontics).
- **Final included studies**: 10 in-vitro studies, published 2008–2021, sample sizes 6–80 teeth, covering VRF, enamel cracks, and apical root cracks.
- **Pooled diagnostic performance**: Sensitivity 83%–98%, specificity 63%–100% across studies that reported these metrics — described as outperforming other current diagnostic tools (visual inspection, transillumination, radiographs) in several head-to-head in-vitro comparisons.
- **Notable individual results**:
  - Shemesh et al.: catheter-based TD-OCT (M2-CV system) for VRF detection — sensitivity 0.93, specificity 0.95 (25 lower premolars), interobserver kappa 0.7–0.9.
  - Imai et al.: SS-OCT — sensitivity 0.90 (whole-thickness cracks)/0.95 (enamel surface cracks); specificity 0.63/0.75 respectively; interexaminer weighted kappa 0.611 (SS-OCT) vs. 0.178 (visual inspection).
  - de Oliveira et al.: apical root crack detection — SD-OCT sensitivity 0.917/specificity 0.875 vs. SS-OCT sensitivity 0.833/specificity 0.750 (difference not statistically significant).
  - Lee et al.: SS-OCT detected cracks in 65.1% of surfaces vs. visual inspection 47.4% and transillumination 52.3%.
  - Shimada et al.: SS-OCT sensitivity 0.98/specificity 0.75 for enamel demineralization; sensitivity 0.60/specificity 0.98 for dentin caries; interexaminer weighted kappa 0.61 (SS-OCT) vs. 0.18 (transillumination).
- **Penetration depth**: Typically 1–3 mm with near-infrared wavelengths <1000 nm; SS-OCT with longer wavelengths can exceed 3 mm but remains inadequate for full root visualization.
- **Delivery approach**: Two paradigms identified — (1) catheter-based OCT introduced into the root canal system (requires canal preparation to ISO 50–90 K-file; Shemesh et al., Birsch), and (2) non-invasive surface/crown scanning (majority of studies; Imai, Nakajima, Segarra, Shimada, Kim, Lee).
- **Comparative table (Table 4)**: OCT offers superior sensitivity/specificity for crack/VRF detection versus ultrasound, radiographs, and (implicitly) unaided clinical exam, but is currently outperformed by micro-CT for soft-tissue visualization and still requires excessive canal preparation relative to its promise as a minimally invasive tool.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **All 10 included studies were in vitro** — no clinical/human in-vivo trials were identified, raising concern about performance bias (lab conditions may not replicate clinical accuracy or feasibility).
- **Penetration depth limitation** (1–3 mm) is insufficient for imaging structures deep within the root, precluding reliable VRF detection beyond the coronal/cervical zone without intracanal probe insertion.
- **Device design barriers**: existing catheter-based OCT probes require canal preparation to large ISO sizes (50–90), and non-catheter systems are bulky/rigid, unsuited for posterior teeth or routine intraoral use.
- **No standardized OCT protocol**: heterogeneity in OCT system types (TD-OCT, SD-OCT, SS-OCT), imaging protocols, and outcome measures hinders direct cross-study comparison.
- **No formal risk-of-bias assessment** was applied (acceptable under scoping-review methodology but limits strength-of-evidence grading).
- **Future directions proposed by authors**: development of longer-wavelength (>1300 nm) OCT systems for deeper penetration; handheld/mobile and intraoral-scanner-integrated OCT devices; AI-assisted OCT image interpretation; well-designed randomized controlled clinical trials to validate real-world diagnostic accuracy, cost-effectiveness, and long-term prognostic impact versus CBCT (current gold standard).

## 6. Related Work

- Builds on OCT's established prior dental applications: caries detection (Shimada et al.), crown/veneer marginal fit (fixed prosthodontics), peri-implant tissue assessment, periodontal pocket measurement, and oral mucosal lesion detection — summarized in the review's Table 1.
- Explicitly contrasts OCT against CBCT (current reference standard for VRF, but high radiation dose and insufficient reliability with intracanal materials per von Arx et al., Fuss et al.), conventional radiographs (limited early-stage sensitivity), and ultrasound (poor hard-tissue visualization, larger probe size than OCT).
- Cites the 20-year technology-maturity gap between CBCT and OCT in endodontic adoption as the primary reason OCT cannot yet substitute CBCT clinically.

## 7. Glossary

- **OCT (Optical Coherence Tomography)**: Non-invasive imaging technique using low-coherence interferometry with near-infrared light to produce high-resolution, cross-sectional images of tissue microstructure.
- **TD-OCT / SD-OCT / SS-OCT**: Time-Domain, Spectral-Domain, and Swept-Source OCT — technical variants differing in image-acquisition method; SS-OCT offers longer wavelengths and deeper penetration than SD-OCT/TD-OCT.
- **VRF (Vertical Root Fracture)**: A crack extending between root and crown, typically leading to tooth loss; the most severe endodontic crack presentation.
- **PRISMA-ScR**: PRISMA Extension for Scoping Reviews — reporting framework used for this review.
- **PCC Framework**: Population, Concept, Context — framework for formulating scoping-review research questions.
- **CBCT (Cone Beam Computed Tomography)**: Current reference-standard 3D imaging modality for VRF diagnosis, but with radiation exposure and limited reliability around intracanal materials.
