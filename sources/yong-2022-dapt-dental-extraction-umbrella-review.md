## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PRISMA, PROSPERO-registered; PubMed/Ovid/Cochrane/Embase search) appraising existing systematic reviews on bleeding-related complications during/after dental extractions in patients on dual anti-platelet therapy (DAPT); 4 systematic reviews met inclusion criteria.

DAPT increases the risk of bleeding-related complications after dental extractions, but the difference may not be clinically significant since local haemostatic measures were adequate to control bleeding in the included reviews.

It may not be necessary to interrupt DAPT for dental extractions — local haemostatic agents are generally sufficient, whereas the complications of discontinuing DAPT (thromboembolic events) may be more severe or fatal; abstract-only retrieval (no PMC full text) limits access to per-review AMSTAR/GRADE detail.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Synthesizes existing **systematic reviews** (not primary studies) on DAPT and dental-extraction bleeding, sitting one evidence tier above single SRs.
- Registered prospectively on **PROSPERO** and conducted per **PRISMA** guidelines, strengthening methodological credibility.
- Frames the clinical question explicitly as a risk-tradeoff: bleeding-complication risk (manageable with local measures) vs thromboembolic risk from DAPT discontinuation (potentially severe/fatal).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design:** Umbrella review of systematic reviews, PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered.
- **Databases searched:** PubMed, Ovid, Cochrane, Embase.
- **Inclusion:** Systematic reviews assessing bleeding-related complications during/after dental extractions in patients on DAPT.
- **Included reviews:** 4 systematic reviews met inclusion criteria.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- DAPT patients show an **increased risk of bleeding-related complications** after dental extractions compared to patients not on DAPT, across the 4 included SRs.
- The magnitude of this increase **may not be clinically significant**, because local haemostatic measures (e.g., sutures, gelatin sponge, tranexamic acid mouthwash) were adequate to control both primary and secondary bleeding in the underlying reviews.
- **Bottom line:** it may not be necessary to interrupt DAPT before dental extraction; the risks of discontinuing DAPT (thromboembolic/cardiovascular events) may be more severe or fatal than the bleeding risk of continuing it.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only ingestion** — full text not retrieved (no PMC deposit); per-review AMSTAR-2/GRADE quality ratings, exact effect sizes, and the identities of the 4 included SRs are not available from the abstract alone.
- Umbrella-review conclusions are only as strong as the underlying SRs' quality and overlap; primary-study-level heterogeneity is not directly assessable from this abstract.
- No explicit handling of DAPT drug-combination heterogeneity (e.g., aspirin+clopidogrel vs aspirin+ticagrelor) is stated in the abstract.

## 7. Glossary

- **DAPT (Dual Anti-Platelet Therapy)** — combination antiplatelet regimen (typically aspirin + a P2Y12 inhibitor such as clopidogrel/ticagrelor), prescribed after cardiovascular/cerebrovascular ischemic events or stent placement.
- **Umbrella review** — a review that synthesizes multiple existing systematic reviews on the same topic, one tier above a standard SR/MA in the evidence hierarchy.
- **PROSPERO** — international prospective register of systematic review protocols.
- **Local haemostatic measures** — chairside interventions (sutures, gelatin sponge, oxidized cellulose, tranexamic acid) used to control post-extraction bleeding without altering systemic medication.
