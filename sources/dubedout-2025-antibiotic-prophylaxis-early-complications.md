## Why Ingested

Arrived in a root-folder drop (alongside ADA acute dental pain guideline materials, not ingested per user instruction) and lands squarely on the wiki's implant antibiotic prophylaxis shelf: it is a 2025 systematic review restricted to double-blind, placebo-controlled RCTs answering the same question family as [[wiki/drug/antibiotics/momand-2024-antibiotic-prophylaxis-early-implant-failure]] — but for early COMPLICATIONS as well as failure. It independently reaches the same non-recommendation conclusion from an essentially identical trial pool while adding secondary outcomes (surgical complications, patient-reported adverse events, pain) that momand-2024 did not synthesize.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42023427802; PRISMA; PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science to May 18, 2023) of 7 double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs with 1,846 participants testing preoperative antibiotic prophylaxis vs placebo for preventing early osseointegration complications and implant failure within 6 months in immunocompetent patients.

Six trials used amoxicillin (2 g in four trials, 3 g in one, and a 4-arm timing/regimen trial) and one used clindamycin 600 mg; no trial found a statistically significant difference in implant failure or postoperative complications (all p > 0.05; closest was Nolan et al.: 0/27 vs 5/28 failures, p = 0.0515), and RoB 2 judged 4 studies low risk and 3 with some concerns.

The findings do not support routine antibiotic prophylaxis in healthy patients undergoing implant surgery — aligned with AFSSAPS 2011, EAO 2015 and ITI recommendations — limited by a single-reviewer risk-of-bias assessment and a three-database search without grey literature.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Restricts the implant-prophylaxis evidence base to double-blind, placebo-controlled RCTs only (7 trials, 1,846 participants) — the design criterion that explains why broader reviews reached divergent conclusions.
- First synthesis in this question family to give equal weight to secondary outcomes: surgical complications (wound dehiscence, suppuration, fistula formation, abscesses), patient-reported adverse events (erythema, urticaria, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea), and postoperative pain (VAS).
- Explicitly frames the null result against antimicrobial resistance: 4.95 million deaths annually associated with resistant bacteria, 1.27 million directly attributable (Lancet 2022 Global Burden analysis, ref 3).
- Confirms guideline concordance: AFSSAPS 2011 (against prophylaxis in healthy patients), EAO Consensus 2015 (discouraged in systemically healthy individuals), ITI Consensus (antibiotics only after assessing overall medical risk).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: PRISMA-reported systematic review; protocol registered on PROSPERO (CRD42023427802). Narrative/tabular synthesis only — NO meta-analysis or pooled estimates performed.
- PICOS: (P) partially/totally edentulous immunocompetent patients without high-risk heart disease for infective endocarditis; (I) any preoperative antibiotic at any dose/duration; (C) placebo; (O) primary = early implant failure (lack of osseointegration) within first 6 months; secondary = surgical complications, patient-reported adverse events, pain; prosthetic complications excluded; (S) double-blind RCTs with maximum 6-month follow-up, English or French.
- Search: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science; MeSH + free terms; completed May 18, 2023; no handsearching or grey literature.
- Selection: Zotero for deduplication; Rayyan double-blind screening by two reviewers (JD, SC) at title/abstract and full-text stages; disagreements resolved by discussion (one article).
- Data extraction: two authors independently (JD, SC).
- Risk of bias: RoB 2 tool, assessed by a SINGLE reviewer (JD); no study excluded on risk of bias.
- PRISMA flow: 1,852 records → 1,047 after duplicate removal → 20 after title/abstract screening → 13 excluded at full text (3 no full text, 4 not clinical trials, 3 no placebo group, 1 follow-up > 6 months, 1 not double-blinded, 1 bacterial-flora outcome) → 7 included.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Included trials (all p-values verbatim from Table III):

- Esposito et al. 2008 (Italy, private practice, 11 centres, 4 mo): implant failure TG 2/158 vs CG 8/158, p value = 0,104
- Anitua et al. 2009 (Spain, private practice, 8 centres, 3 mo; single implants; PRGF co-treatment): TG 2/52 vs CG 2/53, p value = 0,362
- Esposito et al. 2010 (Italy, private practice, 10 centres, 4 mo): TG 5/252 vs CG 12/254, p value = 0,09
- Tan et al. 2014 (international multicentre, university, 7 centres, 2 mo; 4 arms): TG1 0/81, TG2 0/82, TG3 0/86 vs CG 1/80, p value = 0,47
- Nolan et al. 2014 (Ireland, university, 1 centre, 4 mo): TG 0/27 vs CG 5/28, p value = 0,0515 — all losses occurred during multiple-implant surgeries; all failures in procedures exceeding 1 h; ONLY significant outcome in the review: lower post-surgical pain in the antibiotic group at day 7 (p = 0.016)
- Momand et al. 2022 (Sweden, university/hospital, 7 centres, 6 mo): TG 6/238 vs CG 7/235, p value = 0,75
- Santamaría Arrieta et al. 2022 (Spain, university, 1 centre, 2 mo; clindamycin 600 mg): TG 2/31 vs CG 0/31, p value = 0,246

Aggregate findings:

- Implant failure: no statistically significant difference between antibiotic and placebo groups in ANY trial (all p > 0.05).
- Postoperative complications: no statistically significant difference in ANY trial (all p > 0.05).
- Pain (VAS, 3 trials): only Nolan et al. reported a significant reduction with antibiotic prophylaxis.
- Regimens: six trials amoxicillin (four × 2 g 1 h preop; one × 3 g 1 h preop; one 4-arm trial: 2 g preop vs 2 g immediately postop vs 2 g preop + 500 mg three times a day for 2–3 days vs placebo), one trial clindamycin 600 mg 1 h preop.
- Two trials included simultaneous bone grafting (Anitua; Momand).
- All studies rinsed with 0.2% chlorhexidine digluconate for 1 min before implant placement.
- Equal group sizes in all studies (antibiotic vs placebo).

Risk of bias (RoB 2):

- Low risk: 4 studies (Esposito 2008, Anitua 2009, Esposito 2010, Santamaría Arrieta 2022); some concerns: 3 studies (Tan 2014, Nolan 2014, Momand 2022).
- Highest-risk domains: D3 (bias due to missing outcome data) and D5 (bias in selection of the reported result); lowest: D1 (randomization process) and D4 (measurement of outcome) — low risk in key domains D1/D4 for all included studies, attributed to the double-blind placebo-controlled restriction.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only three electronic databases searched; no handsearching or grey literature — may have limited comprehensiveness.
- Risk of bias assessed by a single reviewer; a double-blind process would have enhanced reliability (authors' own stated limitation).
- Only 7 trials met inclusion; authors call for cautious interpretation.
- No meta-analysis/pooling — conclusions rest on vote-counting of individual trial p-values; small trials (e.g., Nolan n=55 analyzed, Santamaría Arrieta n=62) cannot exclude modest effects.
- Follow-up capped at 6 months — says nothing about later peri-implantitis or long-term survival.
- Future work implied by discussion: clarify optimal protocols (Romandini et al. found insufficient evidence for specific dosage), and whether multi-implant simultaneous placement / procedures > 1 h modify failure risk (Nolan signal).

## 7. Glossary

- Antibiotic prophylaxis: perioperative administration of antibiotics, typically before surgery, to prevent infection at the surgical site or distant locations — distinct from antibiotic therapy, which treats active infection with longer courses.
- Early implant failure: loss of the implant due to lack of osseointegration within the first 6 months after surgery (this review's primary-outcome definition: mobility or infection necessitating removal).
- Osseointegration: direct structural and functional connection between living bone and the implant surface.
- Immunocompetent: having a normally functioning immune system; this review's PICOS excluded immunodepressed patients to isolate healthy-population effect.
- Double-blind RCT: randomized controlled trial in which neither participant nor assessor knows the allocation (here: antibiotic vs identical placebo).
- RoB 2 (Risk of Bias 2): Cochrane tool for RCT bias across five domains (D1 randomization, D2 deviations from intended interventions, D3 missing outcome data, D4 outcome measurement, D5 selective reporting).
- VAS (Visual Analog Scale): patient-reported pain scale used in three of the included trials.
- Antimicrobial resistance (AMR): resistance of bacterial species to antimicrobial drugs; the stewardship rationale underpinning the review's non-recommendation.
