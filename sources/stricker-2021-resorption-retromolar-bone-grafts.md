## Why Ingested

Mined as a reference from [[wiki/bone-regeneration/bubalo-2026-bone-substitutes-alveolar-ridge-augmentation]], the narrative review that cites "up to 55% volume loss @ 6 months" and "~7%/yr vertical" resorption for autogenous onlay grafts as a headline claim without itself providing the primary CBCT-quantified data. This Stricker 2021 pilot study is that underlying quantification source: a 3D CBCT volumetric analysis (automated image registration, not manual linear calipers) of retromolar/ascending-ramus autogenous onlay grafts, reporting 43.7% ± 19.0% volume resorption at 12 months — directly grounding the autograft-resorption benchmark that Bubalo 2026 propagates.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective pilot cohort (n=11 patients, 16 autogenous onlay bone grafts from the mandibular ascending ramus, 22 subsequent implants) assessed with 3D CBCT volumetric analysis (automated image registration) at baseline, immediately post-graft, and 12 months post-augmentation.

Mean graft volume fell from 372.2 ± 179.4 mm3 (immediate post-op) to 230.2 ± 185.7 mm3 at 12 months — 43.7% ± 19.0% resorption (p<0.001); resorption was significantly greater in the maxilla (60.2% ± 16.4%) than the mandible (37.6% ± 16.7%, p=0.01); age had no significant effect (p=0.175); no implants were lost.

Small, single-center pilot without a control group or long-term implant-outcome tracking beyond ~510 days; the volumetric methodology (automated registration) is more precise than prior linear/caliper protocols but the magnitude of resorption (~44%) is markedly higher than some prior reports (e.g., Kloss et al. 12.5%), underscoring the wide reported variability for onlay-graft resorption.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Precise 3D volumetric CBCT resorption benchmark for autogenous ascending-ramus/retromolar onlay bone grafts, using an automated image-registration procedure (rather than manual linear/caliper measurement) to align baseline, immediate post-op, and 12-month follow-up scans within an identical region of interest.
- Very high inter-observer reliability (ICC 0.999) for the volumetric measurement protocol, addressing a known weakness of prior manual-delineation methods.
- Quantifies a significant jaw-site difference in resorption (maxilla > mandible) for the same graft-donor-site and recipient-site surgical protocol — a finding not universally reported in prior onlay-graft literature.
- Contextualizes wide inconsistency across the historical onlay-graft-resorption literature (12.5% to 51% at comparable timepoints across different studies/protocols), reinforcing that autograft resorption is highly protocol- and site-dependent rather than a single fixed value.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Retrospective analysis of patients ≥18 years who received autogenous cortico-cancellous onlay bone grafts harvested from the mandibular ascending ramus (retromolar donor site) using a piezotome and chisel, fixed with two titanium screws, prior to dental implant placement.
- Screened 220 patients treated with retromolar bone grafts; 11 patients (10 female, 1 male; mean age 53.0 years, range 20-69) met inclusion criteria (alveolar crest width <3 mm, vertical height >7 mm; no smoking, malignancy history, antiresorptive/radio/chemotherapy, pregnancy/nursing, or bone-metabolism-affecting disease) and had CBCT scans at all three required time points.
- CBCT scans: iCat Cone Beam 3D scanner, 0.3 mm voxel size, 5 mA, 120 kV, 8.9 s scan time; acquired pre-augmentation (T0), immediately post-augmentation (T1), and 12 months post-augmentation (T2).
- Automated image registration (rigid, restricted to mandible or maxilla per implant site) aligned T0 to T1/T2; bony contours of the T0 ROI were manually delineated slice-by-slice on axial images and volume calculated by automated interpolation (MeVisLab V.1). Two independent, blinded examiners performed all measurements.
- Statistics: paired t-test for baseline-vs-12-month volume difference; linear regression with robust variance estimators for jaw and age effects; STATA 16; significance threshold p<0.05. No formal sample-size calculation (all eligible patients with complete follow-up used; achievable effect size 2.3 for n=11).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 11 patients, 16 bone grafts (4 maxilla, 12 mandible), 22 dental implants placed after 104.4 ± 11.5 days healing; final prosthetic loading at 209.6 ± 24.6 days post-augmentation.
- No implants lost during the observation period (510.4 ± 180.2 days) and thereafter.
- Inter-observer ICC = 0.999 (p<0.0001) across all volume measurement time points.
- Mean augmented-site volume: 372.2 ± 179.4 mm3 at T1 (immediate post-op) → 230.2 ± 185.7 mm3 at T2 (12 months); resorption = 43.7% ± 19.0% (p<0.001, paired t-test).
- Jaw-site effect: mandible resorption 113.7 ± 34.7 mm3 (37.6% ± 16.7%) vs maxilla 217.7 ± 56.2 mm3 (60.2% ± 16.4%), significant difference (p=0.01).
- Age effect: regression coefficient -1.73 (older age associated with slightly less volume loss), not statistically significant (p=0.175).
- Comparative literature cited: Sbordone et al. reported 35-51% resorption for iliac crest transplants and 45% for chin onlay grafts; Kloss et al. reported only 12.5% ± 7.8% shrinkage at 12 months for mandibular single-tooth-defect grafts; Lee and Kim reported 25.4% resorption at 5.5 months for ascending-ramus onlay grafts covered with particulate cortical bone + fibrin sealant + resorbable collagen membrane.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small pilot sample (n=11 patients, 16 grafts); no control group or randomization; no formal sample-size calculation.
- Single-center, retrospective design with patients selected from a larger screened pool (220 → 11) based on availability of CBCT scans at all three required time points, introducing potential selection bias.
- Follow-up limited to 12 months post-augmentation (implants tracked to ~510 days observation); no longer-term volumetric or implant-survival data.
- Authors explicitly call for randomized controlled studies with larger samples to verify findings and to establish standard resorption values for clinical planning.
- Methodological point raised by authors: CBCT gray-scale values (GSV) cannot be reliably converted to Hounsfield units as in conventional CT, limiting quantitative tissue-density characterization even though volumetric boundary delineation via image registration remains valid.

## 7. Glossary

- **CBCT (Cone Beam Computed Tomography)**: Lower-radiation, higher-resolution alternative to conventional CT used for 3D dental/maxillofacial imaging.
- **Onlay bone graft**: Autogenous bone block fixed onto the surface of a resorbed alveolar ridge to augment volume prior to implant placement.
- **Ascending ramus / retromolar donor site**: Intraoral mandibular donor site for harvesting cortico-cancellous autogenous bone, an alternative to extraoral sites (iliac crest, chin/mandibular symphysis).
- **Automated image registration**: Computational alignment of sequential 3D scans (here, T0/T1/T2 CBCT) to a common coordinate system, enabling precise region-of-interest-matched volumetric comparison over time.
- **ICC (Intraclass Correlation Coefficient)**: Statistical measure of inter-observer measurement reliability/agreement.
- **GSV (Gray Scale Value)**: CBCT voxel intensity value; unlike CT Hounsfield units, GSV is not standardized across scanners/protocols and cannot reliably quantify bone density.
