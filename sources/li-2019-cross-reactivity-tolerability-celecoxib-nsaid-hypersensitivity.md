## Why Ingested

This is the single best quantitative answer to the recurring chairside question *"is celecoxib / a COX-2 inhibitor safe in a patient who reports an NSAID or aspirin allergy?"* — directly relevant to dental analgesic selection when non-selective NSAIDs are the default post-operative choice. It supplies the pooled challenge-study numbers (3.29% overall, 0.13% in respiratory reactors, 4.72% for meloxicam) that let a non-allergist risk-stratify, complementing the celecoxib safety data in [[drug/analgesics/beaudart-2025-celecoxib-safety-musculoskeletal-sr-ma]] (which covers gastrointestinal / cardiovascular / renal safety but not hypersensitivity cross-reactivity) and putting numbers on the "alternative agent" branch left qualitative in [[drug/analgesics/kotowska-rodziewicz-2023-nsaids-role-dentistry-review]].

## Three-line Summary

Systematic MEDLINE search (research letter) pooling 62 articles / 3218 single- or double-blind placebo-controlled oral challenges with selective COX-2 inhibitors (mostly celecoxib, rofecoxib) in adults with any NSAID hypersensitivity phenotype.

Overall challenge-reaction rate was 3.29% (106/3218), mostly urticaria/angioedema/pruritus with no severe reaction requiring emergency care or epinephrine; in the respiratory-reactor subgroup (AERD/AIA/isolated respiratory) only 1 of 753 challenges reacted (0.13%), whereas meloxicam cross-reacted in 4.72% of 847 challenges.

Celecoxib can be given without a supervised challenge to low-risk isolated-respiratory-reactors with well-controlled asthma; a history of urticaria/angioedema/anaphylaxis warrants supervised dosing or a 2-step graded oral challenge, allergy evaluation is advised before meloxicam, and first-dose caution is prudent in severe or poorly-controlled asthma.

## 세줄요약

체계적 MEDLINE 검색(연구 서한, Research Letter): 어떤 유형이든 비스테로이드성 항염증제 과민반응(NSAID Hypersensitivity)이 있는 성인에서 선택적 사이클로옥시게나제-2 억제제(Selective COX-2 Inhibitor, 주로 셀레콕십·로페콕십)로 시행된 단일/이중맹검 위약대조 경구 유발검사 3,218건(논문 62편)을 통합 분석.

전체 유발검사 반응률은 3.29%(106/3,218)로 대부분 두드러기·혈관부종·소양증이었고 응급진료나 에피네프린(epinephrine)이 필요한 중증 반응은 없었음; 호흡기 반응 아형(아스피린 악화 호흡기질환(AERD)·아스피린 유발 천식(AIA)·단독 호흡기 반응)에서는 753건 중 1건만 반응(0.13%), 반면 멜록시캄(meloxicam)은 847건 중 4.72%에서 교차반응.

잘 조절되는 천식을 가진 저위험 단독 호흡기 반응자에게는 셀레콕십을 감독 없이 투여 가능; 두드러기·혈관부종·아나필락시스(anaphylaxis) 병력이 있으면 감독하 투여 또는 2단계 점증 경구 유발검사가 필요하고, 멜록시캄 사용 전에는 알레르기 평가를 권고하며, 중증·조절불량 천식에서는 첫 투여 시 주의가 필요.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Cross-reactivity and tolerability of celecoxib in adult patients with NSAID hypersensitivity
- **Authors**: Lily Li, Tanya M. Laidlaw (Brigham and Women's Hospital AERD Center; Harvard Medical School, Boston, MA, USA)
- **Journal**: The Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology: In Practice, 2019; 7(8): 2891–2893.e4 (research letter — "To the Editor")
- **Publication date**: 2019-05-14
- **PMID**: 31100553 | **PMCID**: PMC7328117 | **DOI**: 10.1016/j.jaip.2019.04.042
- **Study type**: Systematic electronic literature search with pooled analysis of drug-challenge outcomes (PubMed indexes it as "Systematic Review"); published as a research letter, so methods are abbreviated and much detail is in the Online Repository.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Provides an updated pooled estimate of the selective COX-2 inhibitor challenge-reaction rate across **all** NSAID-hypersensitivity phenotypes: **3.29%** (106 reactions / 3218 challenges, 62 articles), with **no severe reactions** requiring emergency care or epinephrine.
- Separately analyzes the respiratory-reactor subgroup (AERD / AIA / isolated respiratory reactions) — a population whose COX-2 cross-reactivity was previously "unknown" beyond stable mild–moderate AERD — and finds a reaction rate of only **0.13%** (1 / 753).
- Quantifies that **meloxicam** (preferential, not selective, COX-2 inhibitor) cross-reacts more often — **4.72%** of 847 single-blind placebo-controlled challenges — supporting allergy evaluation before meloxicam in this population.
- Introduces a novel evidence-based **risk-stratification tool for non-allergy providers** that uses the allergy history to decide who can receive celecoxib without testing versus who needs supervised administration or formal allergy evaluation / graded challenge.
- Reconciles the reassuring pooled data with rare published case reports of celecoxib-induced bronchospasm in poorly-controlled / brittle asthma, yielding the practical caveat to delay first celecoxib administration in severe or poorly-controlled asthma.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Updated systematic electronic search of MEDLINE with a prespecified search strategy, identifying all **blinded, placebo-controlled** studies published **before November 2018**.
- **Search terms**: (celecoxib OR rofecoxib OR etoricoxib OR valdecoxib OR parecoxib) AND (hypersensitivity OR intolerance). No language restriction predefined.
- **Exclusions**: retrospective articles and case reports excluded from the pooled challenge analysis (case reports are discussed separately as narrative safety signals).
- **Supplementary searching**: manual searches of reference lists of included trials.
- **Analysis**: pooling of single- or double-blind placebo-controlled oral drug-provocation challenge outcomes; number of reactions / number of challenges = reaction rate, computed for the whole cohort, for the respiratory-reaction subgroup, and for meloxicam separately. (Detailed study tables are in the Online Repository.)
- **Comparators / agents**: celecoxib and rofecoxib were the two most commonly studied COX-2 inhibitors.
- **Classification framework**: NSAID hypersensitivity treated as 5 distinct phenotypes (see Glossary).
- **Context**: recommendations reflect standard practice at the Brigham and Women's Hospital AERD Center, a national referral center caring for >900 patients with AERD.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Population | Challenges | Reactions | Reaction rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any NSAID hypersensitivity phenotype (COX-2 inhibitors) | 3218 | 106 | **3.29%** |
| Respiratory reactors (AERD / AIA / isolated respiratory) | 753 | 1 | **0.13%** |
| Meloxicam (any NSAID hypersensitivity) | 847 | 40 | **4.72%** |

- **Reaction phenotype (overall)**: majority were urticaria and/or angioedema and pruritus; bronchospasm, headache, cough, and **1 episode of laryngeal edema** also reported. **No** study described a severe reaction requiring emergency medical care or epinephrine.
- **The single respiratory-subgroup reaction**: a patient with AIA confirmed by aspirin challenge developed transient urticaria at a 5 mg rofecoxib challenge dose but subsequently tolerated higher doses without symptoms. In the same AIA subjects, aspirin challenge raised urinary leukotriene E4 (LTE4) within 6 hours, whereas rofecoxib and placebo challenges did not change urinary LTE4.
- **Case reports of celecoxib-induced reactions in poorly-controlled asthma** (not in the pooled challenge set):
  - 30-year-old, poorly-controlled corticosteroid-dependent asthma + AERD: flushing, dyspnea, urticaria, FEV1 decrease 21% (open challenge).
  - 25-year-old, brittle asthma + AIA: dyspnea, FEV1 decrease 64%, required epinephrine reversal.
  - Poorly-controlled asthma + AERD: bronchospasm, FEV1 drop ~40% at a 15 mg dose (single-blind).
  - Poorly-controlled asthma: nasal symptoms, urticaria, dyspnea, FEV1 2.84 → 0.81 L on 50 mg celecoxib; after improved asthma control, repeat challenge to 200 mg 2 months later was entirely negative.
- **Risk-stratification recommendations**:
  - Low-risk **isolated respiratory** reaction history + well-controlled asthma → celecoxib **without** supervised challenge / further testing.
  - History of **urticaria / angioedema or anaphylaxis** → administration under supervision, or allergy evaluation and formal 2-step graded oral challenge (one-tenth to one-quarter dose, then full dose, with a 1-hour observation period after each).
  - **Before meloxicam** → allergy evaluation recommended.
  - **Severe or poorly-controlled asthma** → consider delaying first administration of celecoxib.
- Celecoxib is the only selective COX-2 inhibitor available in the United States.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Published as a research letter — abbreviated methods, no formal risk-of-bias assessment, PRISMA flow / heterogeneity statistics not reported in the main text (Online Repository holds the study tables).
- Pooled reaction rates are simple aggregates across heterogeneous challenge protocols (doses, blinding, phenotype mix); no meta-analytic weighting or confidence intervals in the letter.
- Search cutoff is November 2018.
- The 0.13% respiratory-subgroup rate rests on 753 challenges with only 1 reaction — reassuring but with wide uncertainty for rare severe events.
- Case-report signals (celecoxib bronchospasm) come from poorly-controlled / brittle asthma and cannot be rate-quantified; they drive the first-dose caution rather than a numeric risk.
- The risk-stratification tool is expert-derived from these data and standard practice at one referral center; it is not prospectively validated.

## 6. Related Work

- Prior literature had established that COX-2 inhibitors are safe in **stable mild–moderate** asthma with AERD and that oral challenge is often not required in that group; this study extends the evidence to the broader AERD / AIA / isolated-respiratory population.

## 7. Glossary

- **NSAID**: nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug.
- **COX-1 / COX-2**: cyclooxygenase isoenzymes. Most true NSAID hypersensitivity is driven by pharmacologic inhibition of constitutively expressed COX-1, not an immunologic (IgE / T-cell) mechanism — which is why selective COX-2 inhibitors are usually tolerated.
- **Selective COX-2 inhibitor (coxib)**: celecoxib, rofecoxib, etoricoxib, valdecoxib, parecoxib. Celecoxib is the only one currently marketed in the USA.
- **Preferential COX-2 inhibitor**: meloxicam — COX-2-selective at low dose but with COX-1 activity at higher dose, hence higher cross-reactivity than the coxibs.
- **NSAID hypersensitivity — 5 phenotypes**: (1) NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease = aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD) / aspirin-induced asthma (AIA); (2) NSAID-exacerbated urticaria/angioedema in patients **with** chronic idiopathic urticaria (CIU); (3) NSAID-exacerbated urticaria/angioedema in patients **without** underlying CIU; (4) urticaria/angioedema or anaphylaxis induced by a **single** NSAID; (5) delayed hypersensitivity reactions.
- **AERD / AIA**: aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease / aspirin-induced asthma.
- **CIU**: chronic idiopathic urticaria.
- **Drug provocation / challenge testing**: supervised graded administration of the drug — the primary diagnostic method, as no standardized in vitro test for NSAID hypersensitivity exists.
- **2-step graded oral challenge**: one-tenth to one-quarter dose, then full dose, each followed by 1 hour of observation.
- **LTE4**: leukotriene E4; urinary LTE4 rises after aspirin challenge in AIA but not after COX-2-selective challenge.
- **FEV1**: forced expiratory volume in 1 second — spirometric marker of bronchospasm.
