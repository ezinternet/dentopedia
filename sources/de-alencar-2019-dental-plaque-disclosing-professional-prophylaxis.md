## Why Ingested

The wiki already holds professional-prophylaxis evidence in adults ([[wiki/periodontics/non-surgical-instrumentation/lamont-2018-routine-scale-and-polish-periodontal-health]], [[wiki/periodontics/non-surgical-instrumentation/matthews-2024-dental-scaling-polishing-adults-rapid-review]]) but nothing on how the prophylaxis *procedure itself* should be executed in early childhood. This randomized crossover RCT supplies the first chairside technique question — whether staining plaque before professional prophylaxis improves removal quality and shortens chair time — and links it to the plaque-disclosure/education cluster ([[wiki/periodontics/oral-hygiene-instruction/choi-2017-dental-plaque-control-oral-hygiene-education-korea-sr]]).

## Three-line Summary

Randomized crossover clinical trial (n=25 children aged 18–36 months, ECC-prevention program, Bauru/São Paulo, Brazil) comparing professional dental prophylaxis with prior 1% malachite green plaque disclosing (GI) versus without disclosing (GII), after a 1-month washout.

Disclosing produced significantly better plaque removal on smooth surfaces (labial/buccal) and occlusal surfaces (Wilcoxon test; lingual/palatal p>0.05) and a significantly shorter prophylaxis duration (391.27 s ±142.2 vs 436.51 s ±128.4), with an inverse plaque-index–duration correlation present only in GI.

Staining before prophylaxis makes professional cleaning in infants more effective and less time-consuming, but access to lingual/palatal surfaces remains the limiting step, and the low baseline plaque index (1.49 smooth, 1.77 occlusal) limits generalizability to higher-caries-risk children.

## 세줄요약

무작위 교차 임상시험(18~36개월 아동 25명, 우식조기예방(ECC) 프로그램, Bauru/상파울루) — 전문가 치아 prophylaxis 전 1% 말라카이트 그린(malachite green) 치태 착색군(GI)과 미착색 대조군(GII)을 1개월 washout 후 비교.

착색군(GI)이 평활면(순협면) 및 교합면에서 치태 제거가 유의하게 우수했고(윌콕슨 검정; 설면/구개면은 p>0.05), prophylaxis 시간도 유의하게 짧았으며(391.27초 ±142.2 vs 436.51초 ±128.4), 치태지수–술식시간 역상관은 GI에서만 관찰됨.

유아 전문가 prophylaxis 전 치태 착색은 제거 효과를 높이고 술식 시간을 줄이지만, 설면/구개면 접근성이 여전히 한계이며 낮은 기저 치태지수(평활면 1.49, 교합면 1.77)로 고위험 아동에의 일반화는 제한적임.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study to test whether plaque disclosing before professional dental prophylaxis improves the quality of the procedure in early childhood.
- Shows statistically better plaque removal with prior disclosing on smooth (labial/buccal) and occlusal surfaces, although not on the lingual/palatal surfaces.
- Demonstrates a significantly shorter prophylaxis duration when plaque is disclosed first, despite the extra staining step (391.27 vs 436.51 s).
- In the disclosing group only, a longer procedure correlated with less residual plaque (time spent productively); no such relationship existed without disclosing.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Randomized crossover clinical trial; each child evaluated under both conditions after a 1-month washout.
- **n**: 25 children aged 18–36 months, enrolled in the ECC-prevention program of the Baby Clinic (convenience sample); inclusion = absence of caries lesions and at least 4 fully erupted teeth.
- **Groups**: GI = plaque disclosing before professional prophylaxis; GII = prophylaxis without disclosing. Allocation via opaque black envelope with cards #1/#2.
- **Staining**: 1% malachite green solution (propylene glycol vehicle) applied with cotton swabs after teeth were dried with gauze.
- **Indices**: Greene and Vermillion (smooth surfaces) and Mestrinho, Carvalho and Figueiredo (occlusal surfaces).
- **Procedure**: low-speed contra-angle handpiece + bristle brush + prophylactic paste, single operator; a separate blinded examiner (kept outside the room) scored plaque indices; a third dentist timed the procedure.
- **Calibration**: intraexaminer agreement κ = 87% (plaque indices evaluated in 10 children).
- **Outcomes**: residual plaque index by surface; prophylaxis duration (seconds); operator-rated difficulty (mild/major); child behavior (positive/negative).
- **Statistics**: Wilcoxon (plaque by surface), Mann–Whitney (behavior), Spearman (plaque vs duration/difficulty); significance level 5%.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Mean habitual dental plaque index at baseline: **1.49 (±0.86)** for smooth surfaces; **1.77 (±0.65)** for occlusal surfaces.
- Smooth surfaces (Wilcoxon): GI (with disclosing) showed statistically significant less residual plaque than GII for labial/buccal + lingual/palatal combined, and for labial/buccal alone; **lingual/palatal surfaces showed no significant difference (p>0.05)**.
- Occlusal surfaces (Table 1): statistically significant difference between GI and GII — disclosing improved removal (residual-score distribution 0/0/1 vs 1/0/1 across 126 occlusal surfaces).
- Prophylaxis duration (Table 2): **GI 391.27 s (±142.2)** vs **GII 436.51 s (±128.4)** — statistically faster with prior disclosing.
- Spearman correlations (Table 3, plaque index vs factor): duration — GI smooth −0.0801 (p=0.024), GI occlusal −0.186 (p=0.037); GII smooth −0.042 (p=0.235), GII occlusal −0.003 (p=0.965). Difficulty — GI smooth −0.0668 (p=0.0598), GI occlusal 0.186 (p=0.0376); GII smooth −0.05 (p=0.159), GII occlusal 0.311 (p=0.000).
- Longer duration was associated with less residual plaque only when disclosing was used (inverse correlation, GI) for both smooth and occlusal surfaces; in GII duration did not predict plaque quality (p>0.05).
- Difficulty correlated positively with residual occlusal plaque in both groups; no such association for smooth surfaces (p>0.05).
- Child behavior (crying, nervousness, fear) did not affect plaque indices in either group or surface (Mann–Whitney, p>0.05).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Low mean habitual plaque index (sample already enrolled in an ECC-prevention program); authors call for studies in children with higher biofilm accumulation / higher caries risk outside prevention programs.
- Small convenience sample (n=25), single center, single operator specialized in infant care — behavior effects may differ when performed by a general dentist.
- Literature comparison hampered by the near-absence of studies on disclosing solutions for infants and on disclosing as a prophylaxis aid.
- Lingual/palatal surfaces remained difficult to visualize/access; disclosing did not overcome this limitation.
- No true untreated control — comparison is disclosing vs no disclosing, not prophylaxis vs no prophylaxis.

## 7. Glossary

- **ECC (Early Childhood Caries)**: presence of one or more decayed, missing (because of caries), or filled primary-tooth surfaces in a child aged 71 months or younger.
- **Professional dental prophylaxis**: professional mechanical removal of dental plaque/biofilm from tooth surfaces with a low-speed handpiece, bristle brush, and prophylactic paste.
- **Plaque disclosing agent**: dye (here 1% malachite green) that selectively stains dental biofilm to make plaque visible.
- **Greene and Vermillion index**: plaque index for smooth tooth surfaces (OHI lineage).
- **Mestrinho, Carvalho and Figueiredo index**: plaque index for occlusal surfaces.
- **GI / GII**: GI = with disclosing before prophylaxis; GII = prophylaxis without disclosing.
- **κ (kappa)**: chance-corrected agreement statistic; here intraexaminer agreement = 87%.
