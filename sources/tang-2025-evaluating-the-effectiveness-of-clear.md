## Why Ingested

Curve of Spee (CoS) leveling is a challenge in clear aligner therapy, particularly in extraction cases. This retrospective study directly compares extraction vs. non-extraction patient outcomes and identifies specific factors (pre-treatment CoS, predicted CoS, Class II elastics, initial crowding) that impact post-treatment CoS. Extends [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/goh-2022-predictability-mandibular-curve-invisalign]] (which found <40% accuracy in non-extraction CAT) by specifically addressing the extraction-associated "roller coaster effect" discussed in [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/qian-2024-roller-coaster-effect]].

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study (n=60: 30 extraction, 30 non-extraction) comparing mandibular curve of Spee leveling with clear aligner therapy (Invisalign); 3D model measurements (Geomagic Studio).

Extraction group showed significantly greater discrepancy between predicted and actual CoS change (−1.38±0.74 mm vs. −0.84±0.58 mm, p<0.001), indicating reduced effectiveness compared to non-extraction group.

Post-treatment CoS positively correlated with pre-treatment CoS and predicted CoS, negatively with initial crowding; suggests extraction patients face inherent biomechanical limitations in CoS leveling that current attachment designs cannot fully overcome.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **First comparative analysis of CoS leveling in extraction vs. non-extraction CAT**: Prior literature (Goh 2022) established <40% accuracy in non-extraction cases; this study quantifies the additional challenge imposed by premolar extractions.
- **Quantification of the extraction penalty**: Discrepancy between predicted and actual CoS change was 1.64x greater in extraction group (−1.38±0.74 vs. −0.84±0.58 mm), a clinically meaningful difference.
- **Identification of modifiable factors**: Pre-treatment CoS, predicted CoS, Class II intermaxillary elastics, and initial crowding emerged as significant predictors; attachment type and bite ramps did not.
- **Clinical recommendations for attachment sequencing**: Proposes using G5/G7/horizontal beveled attachments initially for CoS leveling, then switching to G6 for space closure in extraction cases.
- **Evidence for biomechanical limitation in aligner stiffness**: Discussion links torque loss during space closure and the reduced anchorage from increased intercanine-to-second-premolar distance to CoS leveling failure.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

**Study Design**: Retrospective analysis of patients treated between January 2016–January 2022 under a single specialist orthodontist.

**Sample Collection**:
- 297 patients screened from the ClinCheck account; 60 enrolled (n=13 per group minimum required for power 0.8, type I error 0.05, type II error 0.20).
- **Inclusion criteria**: Age ≥18 y; completed first aligner series (≤3 remaining steps) + ≥1 refinement; treatment of all mandibular permanent teeth except 3rd molars; non-extraction OR bilateral mandibular first premolar extraction.
- **Exclusion**: Prior orthodontics/orthognathic surgery, craniofacial/dental abnormalities, severe periodontitis/periapical pathology, combined fixed-appliance/other modality.
- **Treatment protocol**: Aligner changes every 10 days; ≥20 h/day wear.

**CoS Measurement**:
- Three models evaluated: pre-treatment (baseline), predicted post-treatment (end of 1st aligner series), actual post-treatment (start of 1st refinement series).
- Models exported in STL format from ClinCheck software.
- Reference plane established using distobuccal cusp tips of bilateral mandibular 2nd molars + mesioincisal angle of mandibular central incisors (more occlusally positioned).
- CoS quantified as perpendicular distance from buccal cusp tip of mandibular 2nd premolar to reference plane (Geomagic Studio, 3D Systems).

**Baseline Measurements**:
- Dental characteristics: overbite, overjet, Angle classification (from pre-treatment models).
- Craniofacial characteristics: sagittal skeletal classification (ANB angle), vertical skeletal pattern (FH-MP angle) from cephalometry.

**Statistical Analysis**:
- Two researchers performed all measurements; 20% re-measured after 2 weeks for intra/inter-operator ICC (0.995–0.999 intra; 0.885–0.981 inter = excellent).
- Bilateral measurements pooled (n=120 per group for CoS analysis).
- Shapiro-Wilk normality test; independent t-tests for normal data, Mann-Whitney U for non-normal.
- Pearson/Eta correlation: pre-treatment CoS vs. potential factors.
- **Multiple linear regression** (extraction group only): post-treatment CoS ~ pre-treatment CoS + predicted CoS + initial crowding + gender + intermaxillary elastics + bite ramps + attachment types (L3, L5, L6, L7).
- SPSS 21.0; α=0.05.

### CoS Leveling Outcomes (Table 3)

| Measure | Non-extraction | Extraction | Difference | p |
|---------|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-treatment CoS (mm)** | 2.15±1.15 | 1.34±0.92 | 0.80±0.19 | <0.001* |
| Predicted CoS (mm) | 0.33±0.65 | -0.28±0.50 | 0.61±0.40 | <0.001* |
| **Post-treatment CoS (mm)** | 1.17±0.82 | 1.10±0.88 | 0.07±0.15 | 0.670 |
| Predicted change (mm) | -1.82±1.05 | -1.62±0.95 | -0.19±0.18 | 0.295 |
| **Actual change (mm)** | **-0.98±1.02** | **-0.24±0.99** | -0.74±0.18 | **<0.001*** |
| **Discrepancy predicted vs. actual (mm)** | **-0.84±0.58** | **-1.38±0.74** | 0.55±0.12 | **<0.001*** |

**Key Finding**: Both groups failed to achieve predicted improvements (actual change < predicted change). However, extraction group undercorrected significantly more—the "prediction gap" was 1.64× larger (−1.38 vs. −0.84 mm), demonstrating extraction's biomechanical penalty on CoS leveling.

**Clinical impact**: Even though predicted changes were similar (−1.82 vs. −1.62 mm, p=0.295), actual results diverged sharply (−0.98 vs. −0.24 mm, p<0.001). Post-treatment CoS did not differ significantly between groups (p=0.670), meaning extraction patients ended with the same CoS as non-extraction patients but lost more of the predicted improvement along the way.

### Predictors of Post-Treatment CoS (Table 4)

**Multivariate linear regression (extraction group, n=120 measurements):**

| Predictor | β (95% CI) | t | p |
|-----------|-----------|-------|-------|
| **Pre-treatment CoS** | 0.265 (0.041, 0.489) | 2.376 | **0.022*** |
| **Predicted CoS** | 0.749 (0.376, 1.121) | 4.045 | **<0.001*** |
| **Initial crowding** | -0.091 (-0.174, -0.009) | -2.227 | **0.031*** |
| **Class II elastics** | 0.668 (0.146, 1.190) | 2.576 | **0.013*** |
| Class III elastics | -0.330 (-1.865, 1.205) | -0.432 | 0.667 |
| Age | 0.027 (-0.004, 0.058) | 1.756 | 0.086 |
| Gender (Male) | 0.327 (-0.178, 0.831) | 1.304 | 0.199 |
| Bite ramps (Yes) | 0.429 (-0.640, 1.497) | 0.807 | 0.424 |
| L3 attachment (Rectangle) | -0.140 (-0.717, 0.437) | -0.489 | 0.627 |
| L5 attachment (Rectangle) | 0.253 (-0.385, 0.891) | 0.799 | 0.429 |
| L6 attachment (Rectangle) | -0.329 (-1.024, 0.366) | -0.952 | 0.346 |
| L7 attachment (Rectangle) | -0.335 (-0.983, 0.313) | -1.041 | 0.303 |

**Significant Predictors**:
1. **Pre-treatment CoS** (β=0.265): Deeper pre-treatment CoS → more difficult to level (1 mm deeper baseline → 0.27 mm less reduction).
2. **Predicted CoS** (β=0.749): Strongest predictor; software predictions are reasonably accurate for extraction cases (higher predicted = higher actual post-treatment).
3. **Initial crowding** (β=−0.091): More crowding → flatter final CoS (possibly because crowding limits anterior retraction, reducing torque loss).
4. **Class II elastics** (β=0.668): Use of Class II elastics → higher post-treatment CoS (i.e., less leveling achieved). Authors hypothesize this is because Class II elastics extrude molars and intrude 2nd premolars, counteracting CoS leveling goals.

**Non-Significant**:
- Attachment type (L3, L5, L6, L7): Optimized vs. rectangular made no difference.
- Bite ramps: No effect on CoS leveling (contradicts fixed-appliance literature; likely because aligners cover occlusal surface).
- Gender, age.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

**Acknowledged Limitations**:
1. **Retrospective, single-practitioner bias**: All patients treated by one specialist; selection bias unavoidable; generalizability to other practitioners/protocols unclear.
2. **Uncontrolled treatment variables**: Did not analyze sagittal/vertical skeletal patterns, duration of intermaxillary elastics use, or sequencing of tooth movements—all potentially influential.
3. **Attrition bias**: Patients with severe off-tracking who did not complete the 1st aligner series were excluded, potentially overestimating aligner effectiveness (especially in extraction group).
4. **CoS-only outcome**: Did not assess individual tooth movements (incisor intrusion, 2nd premolar extrusion, 2nd molar extrusion separately)—makes it difficult to pinpoint which tooth's movement is responsible for CoS leveling failure in extraction cases.
5. **Small Class III elastics sample**: Only 1 patient had Class III elastics (vs. 8 with Class II); any conclusion about Class III elastics is speculative.

**Future Research Needs**:
- Multisite prospective study to validate single-practitioner findings.
- Detailed 3D analysis of individual tooth movements (incisor torque loss, molar position changes) in extraction vs. non-extraction CAT.
- Biomechanical FEA comparing attachment designs in extraction cases.
- Investigation of timing (delayed vs. immediate 1st premolar extraction) on CoS leveling.
- Randomized trial of attachment sequencing (G5 initially → G6 for space closure).

## 6. Related Work

**Prior CAT + CoS Literature**:
- **Goh et al. (2022)**: Retrospective (n=?)—accuracy of CAT in leveling CoS in non-extraction patients <40%; this study found similar shortfall (−0.98 mm actual vs. −1.82 mm predicted = 54% accuracy in non-extraction group, 15% in extraction group).
- **Qian et al. (2024)**: The "roller coaster effect"—premolar extraction cases with CAT show posterior open bite and extrusion of incisors (relative anchorage loss). This paper identifies CoS leveling as the specific affected outcome.
- **Dai et al. (2021)**: Comparison of achieved vs. predicted crown movement in 4-premolar extraction CAT; found crown movement undercorrection.
- **Tang et al. (2023)**: Relative anchorage loss under reciprocal anchorage in CAT extraction cases.

**Biomechanical/FEA References**:
- **Upadhyay & Arqub (2022)**: Clear aligner stiffness insufficient for anterior intrusion and posterior extrusion (only 40% and 10% efficiency, respectively). Explains why CoS leveling (which requires these movements) is challenging.
- **Wang et al. (2022)**: Finite element analysis of Class II elastics with CAT—shows Class II elastics increase molar extrusion and 2nd premolar intrusion, which can worsen CoS.
- **Proffit et al. (2018, Contemporary Orthodontics)**: Beam model—increased beam length (from 1st premolar extraction) reduces stiffness exponentially, increases deflection range.

**Attachment Design**:
- **Align Technology (2013)**: G5 attachments for deep bite/CoS leveling.
- **Align Technology (2014)**: G6 attachments for 1st premolar extraction cases.
- **Kravitz et al. (2020)**: Mechanical considerations for deep-bite correction with aligners.

**CoS and Craniofacial Patterns** (contradictory literature):
- **Trouten et al. (1983)**: Negative CoS in hyperdivergent, pronounced in hypodivergent patients.
- **Orthlieb (1997)**: Flatter CoS in Class III vs. Class II.
- **Halimi et al. (2018)**: Regression analysis—no significant association between craniofacial patterns and CoS (supports this paper's finding).
- **Rozzi et al. (2017), Farella et al. (2002), Krüsi et al. (2022)**: Similar null findings for vertical/sagittal skeletal patterns ↔ CoS relationship.

**Bite Ramps**:
- **Husain et al. (2024)**: Precision bite ramps did not significantly improve overbite reduction in CAT; aligns with this study.
- **Blundell et al. (2022)**: SmartTrack + precision bite ramps vs. EX30 aligners for overbite control.

## 7. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Curve of Spee (CoS)** | Sagittal arch of mandibular dentition from incisor incisal edge to posterior buccal cusps; deepened in Class II and reduced in Class III; appropriate leveling is one of Andrews' six keys to normal occlusion. |
| **Clear aligner therapy (CAT)** | Transparent, removable thermoplastic appliances (Invisalign, etc.) for tooth movement; applied in serial steps. |
| **Invisalign** | Trademark CAT appliance by Align Technology; ClinCheck software plans treatment. |
| **ClinCheck** | Align Technology's proprietary software for designing aligner sequences and predicting tooth movement. |
| **Predicted vs. actual change** | ClinCheck software predicts final tooth positions after the 1st aligner series ("predicted"). Patient's actual dentition at start of refinement ("actual") often falls short of prediction. Discrepancy = predicted − actual. |
| **Torque loss** | Loss of anterior tooth inclination (increased lingual tilt of incisors) during space closure, caused by insufficient aligner stiffness for bodily anterior movement under retractive forces. |
| **Premolar extraction** | Removal of bilateral mandibular first premolars to reduce crowding and achieve Class I canine relationship; standard in many extraction cases. |
| **G5 attachment** | Invisalign attachment design optimized for leveling CoS by improving control of premolar extrusion and anterior intrusion. |
| **G6 attachment** | Invisalign attachment design engineered for 1st premolar extraction cases; facilitates canine bodily movement and posterior anchorage. |
| **Class II intermaxillary elastics** | Rubber bands connecting upper and lower arches to correct sagittal discrepancy; can extrude molars and intrude 2nd premolars. |
| **Beam model** | Engineering principle: increasing beam length proportionally decreases strength, exponentially decreases stiffness, and increases deflection range. Applied here: longer aligner span (after premolar extraction) reduces its ability to intrude anterior teeth. |
| **ICC (Intra-class correlation coefficient)** | Measure of measurement reliability; 0.885–0.981 inter-operator and 0.995–0.999 intra-operator indicate excellent agreement. |
| **Overbite** | Vertical overlap of upper and lower anterior teeth; excessive overbite correlates with deeper CoS. |
| **Overjet** | Horizontal overlap of upper and lower anterior teeth. |
| **Angle classification** | Molar relationship classification (Class I = ideal, Class II = distal molar step, Class III = mesial molar step). |
| **Sagittal skeletal classification** | ANB angle used to classify anteroposterior jaw relationship (Class I = 0–4°, Class II = >4°, Class III = <0°). |
| **Vertical skeletal pattern** | FH-MP angle used to classify divergence (hypodivergent, normodivergent, hyperdivergent). |
| **Geomagic Studio** | 3D analysis software used to measure CoS from STL models. |
