## Why Ingested

Narrows the same research group's anterior-tooth question in [[post-and-core/phengudom-2021-survival-fracture-endodontic-anterior]] to a specific and common presentation — cervical lesions with pulpal involvement — and adds the finding that additional structural loss (not restoration material per se) is what drives fracture risk up.

## Three-line Summary

This retrospective cohort study (n=175 ETT, 155 patients) compared fracture survival of endodontically treated anterior teeth with cervical lesions and pulpal involvement restored with resin composite (n=125) versus crowns (n=50) over a mean 32.9-month recall period.

No significant difference in survival against fracture was found between resin composite (85.6%) and crown (88%) groups (p=0.361). Additional tooth structure loss (class III/IV or contralateral class V) was a significant prognostic factor with 7.25-fold higher fracture risk (HR 7.25; 95% CI 1.68-31.30; p=0.008).

For single-surface cervical lesions with pulpal involvement, survival was 96.15% (resin composite) and 100% (crown), significantly higher than teeth with additional tooth loss (78.08% and 80.65%, respectively; p<0.05). Resin composite can be recommended for simple cervical lesions, but caution is needed for teeth with additional defects.

## 세줄요약

본 후향적 코호트 연구(ETT 175개, 환자 155명)는 치경부 병소와 치수 침범이 있는 근관치료 전치를 레진 복합레진(n=125) 또는 크라운(n=50)으로 수복했을 때 파절 생존율을 평균 32.9개월 추적관찰 하에 비교하였다.

레진 복합레진군(85.6%)과 크라운군(88%) 간 파절 생존율은 유의한 차이가 없었다(p=0.361). 추가 치질 소실(III급, IV급, 또는 반대면 V급 병소)은 유의한 예후 인자였으며, 파절 위험이 7.25배 증가하였다(HR 7.25; 95% CI 1.68-31.30; p=0.008).

단일면 치경부 병소의 경우 레진 복합레진(96.15%)과 크라운(100%) 생존율이 추가 치질 소실이 있는 경우(각각 78.08%, 80.65%)보다 유의하게 높았다(p<0.05). 단순 치경부 병소는 레진 복합레진 수복이 가능하나, 추가 결손이 있는 치아는 주의가 필요하다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First clinical study comparing resin composite versus crown restorations for endodontically treated anterior teeth with cervical lesions and pulpal involvement
- Demonstrated no significant difference in 33-month fracture survival between resin composite (85.6%) and crown (88%) restorations (p=0.361)
- Identified additional tooth structure loss as a significant prognostic factor (HR 7.25; 95% CI 1.68-31.30; p=0.008)
- Showed that single-surface cervical lesions with pulpal involvement have excellent survival with resin composite (96.15%) or crown (100%)
- Crown-root fracture was the most common failure mode in both groups (78% in resin composite, 83.3% in crown)
- Provided clinical evidence that resin composite can be recommended for simple cervical lesions without additional defects

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design:** Retrospective cohort study with mean 32.9 ± 15.8 months follow-up (range 12-60 months)
- **Setting:** Endodontic clinic, Faculty of Dentistry, Mahidol University, Bangkok, Thailand (2009-2022)
- **Sample:** 175 anterior ETT (n=125 resin composite, n=50 crown) from 155 patients (70 male, 85 female; mean age 62 ± 12.7 years)
- **Inclusion criteria:** ETT with carious/non-carious cervical lesions with pulpal involvement (labial/palatal), complete root formation, occlusal function, ≥12 months recall
- **Exclusion criteria:** Procedural errors, external resorption, preoperative cracks, orthodontic treatment, inadequate records
- **Outcomes:** Primary — tooth fracture incidence; Secondary — fracture pattern (crown, crown-root, root), restorability after fracture
- **Statistical analysis:** Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, Tarone-Ware test, non-proportional hazard model (full and predictive), Fisher's exact test; SPSS v.22 and STATA v.17
- **Sample size calculation:** Based on previous study (HR 2.07), 80% power, α=0.05; n=94 resin composite, n=47 crown

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Primary outcome — Overall survival:** 86.3% (151/175 teeth); resin composite 85.6% (107/125), crown 88% (44/50); no significant difference (p=0.361)
- **Fracture patterns:** Crown-root fracture — resin composite 78% (14/18), crown 83.3% (5/6); Crown fracture — resin composite 11.1% (2/18), crown 16.7% (1/6); Root fracture — resin composite 11.1% (2/18), crown 0%
- **Additional tooth loss as prognostic factor:** HR 7.25 (95% CI 1.68-31.30, p=0.008); 7.25-fold higher fracture risk
- **Single-surface class V survival:** Resin composite 96.15% (50/52), crown 100% (19/19)
- **Class V with additional tooth loss survival:** Resin composite 78.08% (57/73), crown 80.65% (25/31); significantly lower than single-surface (p<0.05)
- **5-year cumulative survival:** Resin composite — 91.51% at 24 months, 82.42% at 36 months, 80.41% at 60 months; Crown — 95.35% at 24/36 months, 66% at 54/60 months
- **Subgroup analysis (resin composite only):** Single-surface class V (96.15% survival) vs. Class V + class III/IV (83.33%, p<0.05) vs. Class V + contralateral class V (68%, p<0.05)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Retrospective design with inherent selection bias — restoration type determined by operator preference and remaining tooth structure
- Attrition bias (recall rate 71.14% overall, 66.49% resin composite, 86.21% crown)
- Subjective estimation of remaining tooth structure; digital scanning could provide more precise volumetric evaluation
- No post placement in resin composite group, which differs from some institutional protocols
- Relatively short mean follow-up (32.9 months) for long-term survival assessment
- Uneven group sizes (2:1 ratio) due to database distribution
- Single-center study limits generalizability
- Operators included undergraduates, postgraduates, and restorative dentists with varying experience

## 6. Related Work

- Phengudom et al. (2021) — Crown restorations decreased unrestorable fracture incidence in anterior ETT with cervical loss on ≥2 surfaces from ~1% to 18%
- Kaewchomphoo et al. (2022) — Premolar ETT with cervical exposure restored with resin composite showed similar fracture survival to crowns
- Machado et al. (2018) — Resin composite restorations sufficiently reinforce anterior ETT with cervical lesions
- Abduljawad et al. (2016) — Fiber posts enhance fracture resistance of anterior ETT with cervical cavities
- Naumann et al. (2018) — Remaining cervical tooth structure (ferrule) more important than post placement for resisting functional forces
- Jirathanyanatt et al. (2019) — Crown restorations improve survival of posterior ETT
- Ng et al. (2006) — Lateral functional force resistance decreases in ETT with cervical lesions on labial and palatal surfaces

## 7. Glossary

- **ETT (Endodontically Treated Teeth)**: 근관치료된 치아
- **NCCL (Non-Carious Cervical Lesion)**: 비우식성 치경부 병소 (치아 우식이 아닌 원인으로 발생한 치경부의 치질 소실)
- **Class V Cavity**: 치경부 우식 또는 비우식성 병소 (치경부에 위치한 5급 와동)
- **Crown-root fracture**: 치관-치근 파절 (치관부에서 치근까지 이어지는 파절)
- **Ferrule Effect**: 페룰 효과 (수복물이 치질을 1.5-2mm 감싸는 것으로 파절 저항성을 향상시키는 개념)
- **HR (Hazard Ratio)**: 위험비 (시간-사건 분석에서 위험률의 비)
- **Prefabricated fiber post**: 기성 섬유 포스트 (근관 내에 삽입하여 수복물의 유지와 강도를 높이는 기성품 포스트)
- **Intraradicular extension**: 근관내 연장 (수복물이 치경부 변연보다 1-2mm 하방으로 근관 내에 위치하는 것)
