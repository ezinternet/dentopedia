## Why Ingested

TMJ 통증에 대한 보존적 치료 (경피신경자극 TENS, 저출력레이저 LLLT, soft splint)를 12개월 추적으로 직접 비교하고 만성 경부통 (chronic cervical pain)과의 상관관계까지 검증한 RCT — 기존 [[wiki/tmj/salloum-2024-stabilization-splint-ultrasound-myofascial-pain-rct]]가 4주 단기 관찰인 것과 달리 1년 장기 추적 데이터를 제공하며, TMJ-경추 통증 간 상관을 정량적으로(Pearson r) 부정한 근거를 보강함.

## Three-line Summary

RCT, n=48 (12/group), chronic cervical + TMJ pain patients at SRM Dental College randomized to control, soft splint, TENS, or low-level laser (LLL), with occlusal equilibration in all groups except control, followed 12 months.

TMJ pain VAS at final follow-up was significantly lower with TENS (1.05±1.39) and LLL (0.87±0.89) vs. control (3.97±0.98) and soft splint (4.14±1.21) (Tukey HSD p<0.001 for those pairs); TENS vs. LLL and control vs. splint were not significantly different; Pearson correlation between TMJ and cervical pain reduction was weak (r range -0.452 to 0.336).

TENS and LLLT are equally effective and superior to soft splints for reducing TMJ pain over 1 year, but no meaningful correlation was found between TMJ pain relief and cervical pain relief — treating the jaw joint does not reliably resolve neck pain.

## 세줄요약

RCT, n=48 (군당 12명), SRM 치과대학에서 만성 경부통(chronic cervical pain) 및 TMJ 통증 동반 환자를 대상으로 대조군·soft splint·TENS·저출력레이저(LLL) 4군에 무작위 배정(대조군 제외 전 군 교합조정 병행), 12개월 추적.

최종 추적 시 TMJ 통증 VAS는 TENS(1.05±1.39)와 LLL(0.87±0.89)이 대조군(3.97±0.98)·splint(4.14±1.21) 대비 유의하게 낮음(Tukey HSD p<0.001); TENS vs LLL, 대조군 vs splint는 유의차 없음; TMJ-경부통 상관관계는 미약(Pearson r -0.452~0.336).

TENS와 LLLT는 동등하게 효과적이며 soft splint보다 TMJ 통증 감소에 우월하지만, TMJ 통증 개선이 경부통 개선과 유의한 상관을 보이지 않아 턱관절 치료만으로 경부통 해소를 기대하기 어려움.

## 1. Document Information

- **Full title**: Evaluation of the effect of different kinds of treatment modalities for temporomandibular joint pain and its relevance to chronic cervical pain: A randomized controlled trial
- **Journal**: Journal of Indian Prosthodontic Society (J Indian Prosthodont Soc)
- **Year**: 2024 (published online 2024-04-23)
- **DOI**: 10.4103/jips.jips_468_23
- **PMID**: 38650337 / **PMCID**: PMC11129811
- **Design**: Double-blinded, parallel-design, longitudinal prospective RCT
- **n**: 48 patients (12 per group), drawn from a 200-patient reference population already undergoing cervical physiotherapy
- **Duration**: 12-month follow-up (VAS recorded monthly; formal timepoints at baseline, ~3, 6, 9, 12 months)
- **Setting**: SRM Dental College, Ramapuram (Dept. of Prosthodontics) with patients sourced from SRM Medical Hospital Dept. of Orthopedics; IRB SRMDC/IRB/2020/MDS/No. 203; CTRI/2021/05/033809

## 2. Key Contributions

- One of few RCTs comparing TENS, LLLT, and soft splints head-to-head for TMJ pain with a full 12-month follow-up (most comparable trials, e.g., Salloum 2024, use 4-week to 5-month windows)
- Directly tests the cervical-spine/TMD comorbidity hypothesis with paired VAS + Pearson correlation, finding no meaningful correlation despite significant within-group cervical pain reduction
- Uses thermographic imaging (Fluke thermal camera) as an adjunct objective pain marker alongside VAS, showing visible temperature-pattern normalization post-treatment
- Confirms TENS and LLLT deliver statistically equivalent, and superior, TMJ pain relief vs. soft splints and no-adjunct cervical physiotherapy alone

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Screening**: 200 patients already in cervical physiotherapy screened via RDC/TMD (AXIS 2) Criterion 3 (pain) and Criterion 15g (occlusal interference); 40 met inclusion, inflated to 48 for expected 20% dropout
- **Inclusion**: Age 18-60, RDC/TMD-diagnosed TMD, both genders
- **Exclusion**: Head/face trauma, dental pain resolved by tooth treatment alone, systemic inflammatory bone disease (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis)
- **Randomization**: Simple randomization, 1:1:1:1 allocation, sealed opaque envelopes; participants and assessors blinded (double-blind)
- **Groups** (n=12 each), all except control also received occlusal equilibration (articulating-paper-guided enameloplasty via Lucia jig + semi-adjustable articulator):
  - **Group 1 (control)**: cervical physiotherapy only, monthly sessions for 1 year
  - **Group 2 (soft splint)**: occlusal equilibration + 2 mm vacuum-formed thermoplastic soft splint worn ~8 h/night
  - **Group 3 (TENS)**: occlusal equilibration + TENS (Vectrostim Plus), 30 min/session, 500 W, up to 80 Hz, 25 mA; positive electrode anterior/inferior to external auditory meatus, inert electrode on posterior neck (nuchal region)
  - **Group 4 (LLL)**: occlusal equilibration + GaAlAs diode laser (660 nm, 0.5 W continuous), 1 min/point at 3 sites (posterior joint, angle of jaw, intraoral posterior to maxillary tuberosity)
- **Outcome measures**: VAS (0-10) for TMJ pain and for cervical pain (numeric pain rating scale, McCaffery & Beebe 1994), recorded monthly over 12 months; thermographic imaging pre- and post-treatment
- **Statistics**: Repeated-measures ANOVA (within-group trend over time), one-way ANOVA (between-group at baseline and final follow-up), Tukey HSD post hoc, paired t-test (within-group before/after cervical pain), Pearson correlation (TMJ vs. cervical pain reduction); SPSS v22

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**TMJ pain VAS trajectory (repeated-measures ANOVA, within group, baseline→final)**:
| Group | Baseline | Final follow-up | F | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Control | 7.27±1.29 | 3.97±0.98 | 50.900 | 0.001 |
| 2 Soft splint | 7.53±0.70 | 4.14±1.21 | 53.801 | 0.001 |
| 3 TENS | 7.76±0.80 | 1.05±1.39 | 115.554 | 0.001 |
| 4 LLL | 7.61±0.61 | 0.87±0.89 | 423.146 | 0.001 |

**Between-group comparison at final follow-up (one-way ANOVA, F=29.798, p=0.001)**; Tukey HSD pairwise: Group1 vs Group3 mean diff 2.92 (p=0.001), Group1 vs Group4 3.10 (p=0.001), Group2 vs Group3 3.09 (p=0.001), Group2 vs Group4 3.27 (p=0.001); Group1 vs Group2 and Group3 vs Group4 both NS.

**Cervical pain**: all four groups improved significantly within-group (paired t-test, all p=0.001), mean reduction 0.92-1.67 points; between-group differences at final follow-up were NOT significant (one-way ANOVA F=2.025, p=0.124) — i.e., cervical pain improved similarly regardless of TMJ intervention.

**TMJ-cervical pain correlation (Pearson, post-treatment)**: Group1 r=-0.132, Group2 r=-0.02, Group3 r=0.336, Group4 r=-0.452 — no consistent or strong correlation across groups.

**Thermography**: qualitative color-shift (yellow→red, indicating decreased local heat/pain signal) most pronounced in TENS and LLL groups.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Pain diagnosis relies on subjective VAS/RDC-TMD criteria; confounders (stress, posture, parafunctional habits, sleep) not controlled
- Small sample (n=48, 12/group) limits power for subgroup and correlation analyses
- Single-center design (SRM Dental College) limits generalizability
- Thermography used as an industrial/healthcare Fluke camera adjunct only, not independently validated as a TMD outcome measure in this study
- Occlusal equilibration was co-administered with every active intervention except control, so isolated effect of splint/TENS/LLL vs. occlusal equilibration alone cannot be separated
- No blinding of patients to intervention type is explicitly stated for TENS/LLL (device-dependent — inherent limitation of physical-modality RCTs)

## 7. Glossary

- **TMD (Temporomandibular Disorder, 턱관절장애)**: Umbrella term for disorders of the TMJ and masticatory muscles
- **TENS (Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation, 경피신경전기자극)**: Electrical stimulation via skin electrodes for pain modulation
- **LLLT/LLL (Low-Level Laser Therapy, 저출력 레이저 치료)**: Low-power laser (here GaAlAs diode, 660 nm) applied to painful sites for photobiomodulation
- **RDC/TMD (Research Diagnostic Criteria for TMD)**: Standardized dual-axis diagnostic system (Axis I physical, Axis II psychosocial/pain) for TMD research
- **VAS (Visual Analog Scale, 시각적 상사 척도)**: 0-10 self-reported pain intensity scale
- **Occlusal equilibration (교합조정)**: Selective grinding/enameloplasty to eliminate occlusal prematurities/interferences
- **Trigeminocervical nucleus (삼차신경경부핵)**: CNS convergence point proposed to link cervical afferents with trigeminal (masticatory) motor pathways
