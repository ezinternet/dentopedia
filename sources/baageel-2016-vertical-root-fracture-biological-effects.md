## Why Ingested

Pre-2020 anchor for the VRF-imaging question: the wiki already holds the later pooled estimates ([[cracked-tooth/shokri-2024-is-cone-beam-computed-tomography]], [[cracked-tooth/habibzadeh-2023-diagnostic-efficacy-of-cone-beam]], [[cracked-tooth/de-2025-exploring-diagnostic-reliability-of-cbct]]) and the Korean imaging guideline ([[cracked-tooth/kim-2024-developing-evidence-based-clinical-imaging]]), but not this earlier narrative-synthesis SR (22 studies) that framed CBCT as "preferred" while deferring to the AAE/AAOMR joint statement. It documents how the evidence was read before pooled estimates existed and how it was later qualified.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without pooling (PubMed, Embase, Ovid, Google Scholar; searched December 2015, updated May 2016; modified QUADAS appraisal) of imaging accuracy for vertical root fracture (VRF), 22 studies included, plus a narrative overview of biological, psychological and clinical aspects of VRF.

Of the 22 studies, 12 favored CBCT over periapical radiographs, 5 found no difference, and 5 were non-comparative CBCT-parameter studies; the authors nonetheless call the evidence "overwhelming" that CBCT is the preferred detection method, while noting the included studies were highly heterogeneous.

CBCT should be used with caution, only after clinical tests and radiographs fail to give a definitive diagnosis (AAE/AAOMR 2015 joint statement), and before exploratory surgery; high-quality studies are still needed before CBCT can be called standard of care.

## 세줄요약

메타분석 없는 체계적 문헌고찰 (PubMed·Embase·Ovid·Google Scholar, 2015년 12월 검색·2016년 5월 갱신, 수정 QUADAS 평가) — 수직치근파절 (Vertical Root Fracture, VRF) 영상진단 정확도 22편 포함, 생물학적·심리적·임상적 측면의 서술적 개관 병행.

22편 중 12편은 콘빔 CT (Cone-Beam Computed Tomography, CBCT)가 치근단방사선사진 (Periapical Radiograph, PR)보다 우수, 5편은 차이 없음, 5편은 비비교 CBCT 파라미터 연구; 저자들은 포함 연구의 높은 이질성을 인정하면서도 CBCT가 선호되는 검출법이라는 근거가 "압도적"이라고 결론.

CBCT는 임상검사·방사선사진으로 확진이 안 될 때 (AAE/AAOMR 2015 공동성명) 신중히, 탐색적 수술 전에 사용해야 하며, 표준 진료로 인정하기엔 고품질 연구가 더 필요.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Narrative-synthesis systematic review of VRF imaging accuracy (22 studies; vote-counted, not pooled) as of May 2016.
- Table of earlier systematic reviews with discordant conclusions on CBCT for VRF (Chang, Corbella, Rose: no demonstrated superiority or insufficient evidence; Talwar, Long: better or high accuracy); the authors attribute the discordance to inclusion criteria and statistical choices.
- Biological/clinical overview: VRF mostly in teeth aged over 40 and endodontically treated; extraction is the most common treatment; psychological burden of tooth loss.
- Practical positioning of CBCT under the AAE/AAOMR 2015 joint statement (not routine; limited field of view; radiologist interpretation).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Systematic review with narrative synthesis; no meta-analysis, no PRISMA/PROSPERO registration reported in the text.
- **Databases**: PubMed, Embase, Ovid, Google Scholar; search December 2015, updated May 2016; keywords CBCT, VRF, diagnosis.
- **Inclusion**: CBCT as a diagnostic tool for VRF in permanent human teeth (in vitro or in vivo); in vivo VRFs verified by clinical/surgical exploration; all acquisition parameters (kVp, mA, FOV, voxel size) reported; sensitivity, specificity or ROC reported.
- **Exclusion**: unrelated studies, horizontal root fractures, fracture-management studies, CBCT for other manifestations, cracked teeth and craze lines.
- **n**: 22 studies included. Quality assessed with a modified QUADAS tool; the text does not give per-study risk-of-bias results (study tables are not in the retrieved PMC text).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 22 studies: 12 favored CBCT over PR, 5 reported no difference, 5 non-comparative (CBCT parameters/limitations; all described CBCT advantages).
- Included studies were highly heterogeneous (sample size, CBCT unit, PR type, testing parameters, in vitro vs ex vivo vs clinical, observer expertise).
- Prevalence of VRF after root canal treatment cited as 3.7-30.8%; mean time to VRF reported by one cited study 54 months.
- Cited primary-study findings: Takeshita et al. reported higher CBCT accuracy than PR with and without a metal post (values given in the text as 0.953 vs 0.753 and 0.778 vs 0.956; the text is ambiguous about which value belongs to which modality); a CBCT unit's make and model, voxel size, tooth condition, fracture position and examiner skill all affected detection; centre-scanned position was the only reliable one for post-restored teeth (Valizadeh 2015).
- Cost context (from Chang et al.): small-FOV CBCT at least CAD 125 vs CAD 20 for a periapical radiograph (Ontario).
- Conclusion: CBCT preferred for VRF detection but should follow failed clinical + radiographic tests, and precede exploratory surgery when surgery is the only alternative for confirmation.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Vote-counting of 22 heterogeneous, largely in vitro studies without pooling; the "overwhelming evidence" wording is stronger than the data presented (12 of 22 studies favoring CBCT, 5 null).
- Google Scholar as a primary database; no registered protocol; per-study appraisal not visible in the retrieved text.
- Study tables (included studies, earlier SRs) are not present in the PMC text extract, so per-study sensitivity/specificity could not be transcribed.
- Narrative sections on biological and psychological effects rest on a handful of cited studies, not a systematic search.
- Authors call for well-controlled, high-quality studies and guidelines on when CBCT is standard of care.

## 7. Glossary

- **VRF (vertical root fracture)**: fracture extending longitudinally along the root, from apex toward the crown.
- **CBCT**: cone-beam computed tomography; 3D imaging with submillimeter spatial resolution.
- **PR (periapical radiograph)**: 2D intraoral radiograph; fracture lines are visible mainly when the beam is parallel to the fracture plane.
- **QUADAS**: Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies (modified version used here).
- **AAE/AAOMR joint statement (2015)**: position that CBCT not be used routinely for endodontic diagnosis and screening, only when benefit outweighs risk in inconclusive cases.
