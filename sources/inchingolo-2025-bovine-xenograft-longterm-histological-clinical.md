## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251111685; 11 included studies out of 217 screened) evaluating long-term histological and clinical outcomes of bovine-derived xenografts (Bio-Oss/DBBM) in oral surgery — sinus augmentation, ridge augmentation, and alveolar ridge preservation.

GRADE certainty was moderate for short/mid-term implant survival and graft integration but low for long-term complications (graft migration, chronic inflammation, sinus pathology); ROBINS-I showed most of the 11 studies at moderate risk of bias, with only Mordenfeld (2017) rated low risk across all seven domains.

Bovine xenografts remain effective for short-to-mid-term ridge volume preservation and implant survival, but their limited resorbability raises real long-term concerns (chronic inflammation, particle migration, foreign body reaction, occasional need for surgical removal) — autogenous bone remains the gold standard, and clinicians should individualize xenograft use with transparent risk communication and informed consent.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251111685; 217편 스크리닝 중 11편 최종 포함) — 상악동거상술·치조제증대·발치와보존(ARP) 맥락에서 우골유래 이종골이식재(Bio-Oss/DBBM)의 장기 조직학적·임상적 결과를 평가.

GRADE 평가상 단기·중기 임플란트 생존·이식재 통합의 근거등급은 중등도(moderate)였으나, 장기 합병증(이식재 이동·만성염증·부비동 병리)은 낮음(low); ROBINS-I 평가에서 11편 중 대부분이 중등도 비뚤림 위험을 보였고 Mordenfeld(2017)만 7개 영역 전부 낮은 위험으로 평가됨.

우골유래 이종골이식재는 단기·중기 치조제 부피 유지·임플란트 생존에는 효과적이나 낮은 흡수율로 인해 만성염증·입자 이동·이물반응 등 장기 합병증 우려가 실재하며, 자가골이 여전히 표준(gold standard)이므로 술자는 투명한 위험 설명과 충분한 사전동의 하에 개별화된 사용 결정을 내려야 함.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review (per the authors) to specifically focus on **long-term safety and late-onset complications** of bovine-derived xenografts, rather than short-term volumetric/survival outcomes that dominate prior reviews.
- Applies formal **GRADE** certainty-of-evidence grading across four outcome domains (implant survival, histological remodeling/graft integration, long-term complications, comparisons with autograft/porcine graft) and **ROBINS-I** risk-of-bias assessment across the 11 included non-randomized studies.
- Synthesizes across three clinical contexts — maxillary sinus floor elevation, alveolar ridge preservation (ARP), and horizontal/vertical ridge augmentation — under a single risk-benefit framework for the same biomaterial class (DBBM).
- Highlights the "Proteus Phenomenon" (selective publication of positive findings) as a structural bias limiting the existing bovine-xenograft literature, and calls for standardized complication reporting and long-term histological validation in future trials.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review; electronic search of PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus for English-language studies 2005–2025.
- **PICO**: Population = dental implant patients requiring bone regeneration; Intervention = bovine-derived xenografts (e.g., Bio-Oss) for GBR/sinus augmentation; Comparison = autologous bone, porcine xenografts, synthetic substitutes, or platelet concentrates; Outcome = long-term clinical/histological outcomes (graft integration, complication rates, implant success).
- **Search yield**: 217 records identified (52 PubMed, 55 Web of Science, 110 Scopus) → 46 duplicates removed → 171 screened → 33 excluded on criteria + 9 not retrieved → **11 studies included**.
- **Inclusion criteria**: in vivo bovine-derived xenograft (e.g., Bio-Oss) use for GBR/sinus augmentation; open-access; English (or English abstract); adult populations.
- **Exclusion criteria**: meta-analyses, animal studies, in vitro research, review articles, off-topic studies, no free full text.
- Two authors (I.T. and G.D.V.) independently performed article extraction; disagreements resolved by discussion.
- **Quality appraisal**: GRADE (5 domains — risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias) for outcome-level certainty; ROBINS-I (7 domains) for individual non-randomized-study risk of bias (RCTs excluded from the ROBINS-I visual comparison for methodological consistency).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **11 included studies**: Barone (2005), Schmitt (2013), Mordenfeld (2017), Rodriguez (2019), Lai (2020), Hashemipoor (2020), Nowzari (2022), Couso-Queiruga (2023), Correia (2024), Huang (2025), Kim (2025).
- **GRADE certainty**: moderate for implant survival (RCTs consistently high survival, but heterogeneous follow-up); low-to-moderate for histological remodeling/graft integration; **low** for long-term complications (graft migration, chronic inflammation, sinus pathology — mainly case series/observational data); moderate for xenograft-vs-autograft/porcine-graft comparisons (similar short/mid-term outcomes, insufficient long-term data).
- **ROBINS-I**: most studies moderate risk of bias (confounding, missing data domains); Mordenfeld (2017) was the only study rated low risk across all 7 domains; Huang (2025) had a critical-risk domain (deviations from intended interventions, reflecting the novel hydraulic technique); Rodriguez (2019) and Nowzari (2022) — the two case series specifically on long-term xenograft complications — showed moderate risk across multiple domains, an inherent limitation of case-series/observational design.
- **Notable individual findings synthesized**: Huang (2025) hydraulic sinus-lift + A-PRF achieved 4.05 mm mean bone gain at 6 months with zero membrane perforations; Mordenfeld (2017) RCT found DBBM proportion (90:10 vs 60:40 with autograft) did not directly correlate with clinical outcome; Schmitt (2013) found autologous bone superior in osteoinductive potential vs Bio-Oss/Puros/BoneCeramic; Couso-Queiruga (2023) showed ARP mineralized tissue increasing from 13.5% (3 months) to 37% (9 months) with DBBM-C; Rodriguez (2019) and Nowzari (2022) documented complications (migration, chronic inflammation, oroantral communication, cyst formation) emerging 2–13 years post-grafting.
- **Conclusion**: bovine xenografts effectively preserve volume/support short-to-mid-term implant survival, but limited resorbability creates real long-term risk (chronic inflammation, foreign body reaction, particle migration, occasional need for secondary surgical removal); autogenous bone remains the gold standard where feasible; porcine xenografts and platelet-rich derivatives are promising lower-risk alternatives pending more long-term RCT data.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No meta-analysis** — narrative/GRADE synthesis only, due to heterogeneity in study design, follow-up duration, and outcome definitions across the 11 included studies.
- Most complication-focused evidence derives from **case series and observational studies** with small samples and inherent referral bias (e.g., Rodriguez 2019, Nowzari 2022), limiting incidence estimation and generalizability.
- Only free open-access, English-language studies were eligible — a source of potential selection/publication bias, compounded by the "Proteus Phenomenon" (favorable results overrepresented in the literature).
- Follow-up durations across included studies are heterogeneous and, for long-term complications specifically, the true incidence is likely underestimated/underreported in routine practice.
- Theoretical prion-transmission risk (BSE) from bovine xenografts, though currently unproven post-processing, cannot be excluded given multi-decade latency periods.
- Authors call for large-scale, multicenter RCTs with extended follow-up, standardized definitions of clinical success, systematic complication reporting, and robust histomorphometric validation, plus head-to-head comparative trials (bovine xenograft vs autograft vs porcine xenograft vs novel biomaterials).

## 7. Glossary

- **DBBM (Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral)** — anorganic bovine bone xenograft (e.g., Bio-Oss); processed to remove organic/protein content while retaining mineral scaffold architecture.
- **ABB (Anorganic Bovine Bone)** — synonym for DBBM used in some cited case-series literature.
- **GBR (Guided Bone Regeneration)** — surgical technique using barrier membranes ± graft material to direct new bone formation.
- **ARP (Alveolar Ridge Preservation)** — grafting technique to minimize post-extraction ridge dimensional loss.
- **GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation)** — framework for rating the certainty of evidence across outcomes (high/moderate/low/very low).
- **ROBINS-I (Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies of Interventions)** — 7-domain risk-of-bias tool for observational/non-randomized intervention studies.
- **A-PRF (Advanced Platelet-Rich Fibrin)** — leukocyte-rich platelet concentrate with denser fibrin structure than standard PRF, used as an adjunct/alternative to xenograft.
- **Proteus Phenomenon** — publication-bias pattern in which early positive findings on a topic are disproportionately represented in the literature.
