## Three-line Summary

Single case report (Hebrew University/Hadassah, Quintessence Int 50(7):576-582, 2019) demonstrating the use of Ossix Volumax — a thick (1.5-2.0 mm), resorbable, sugar-cross-linked porcine collagen scaffold with GLYMATRIX technology — as the sole augmentation material (no bone substitute) for lateral ridge defect correction at a posterior single-tooth implant site between natural teeth.

After 6 months of healing the scaffold had expanded, adapted to the buccal defect, and provided sufficient volume restoration for a prosthetically driven implant-supported crown; the final outcome at 2 years showed a favorable buccal contour and lifelike emergence profile with stable tissue.

The procedure was simpler than conventional GBR (no bone substitute required) and eliminated donor-site morbidity compared with connective tissue grafts, though the authors note it applies specifically to cases with sufficient bone for implant stability but crestal horizontal soft-tissue/bone deficiency.

## 세줄요약

단일 증례보고 (히브리대/하다사, Quintessence Int 2019) — 자연치 사이 구치 결손부의 협측 수평결손을 뼈 이식재 없이 Ossix Volumax(두꺼운 당가교 흡수성 돼지 콜라겐 스캐폴드) 단독으로 처리. 6개월 후 스캐폴드가 팽창·골화하여 치관측 협측 부피 회복 → 임플란트 보철 완성.

핵심 장점: 뼈 이식재 불필요, 공여부 이환율 없음(vs. CTG), 술식 간소화.

적용 조건: 임플란트 안정성은 충분하지만 치조정 수준에 수평(협측) 결손이 있는 경우 — 완전 골결손 시에는 별도 이식재 병용 필요.

## 2. Ossix Volumax Material Properties

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Manufacturer | Datum Dental (Israel) |
| Technology | GLYMATRIX — sugar cross-linking of collagen (same platform as Ossix Plus membrane) |
| Collagen source | Porcine tendons (approved, standardised process) |
| Thickness | 1.5–2.0 mm (dry); expands to ~2× when wetted |
| Surface | Signature herringbone pattern on both sides |
| Resorption | Slow, progressive; ossification begins after ~1 month; degrades over up to 6 months during mineralization |
| Barrier function | Maintains space + resists exposure degradation |
| Folding option | Can be folded/doubled for extra volume (up to ~4 mm) |

## 3. Clinical Protocol

**Pre-op**: CBCT showed adequate bone height for implant but horizontal buccolingual deficiency at crestal level (Seibert Class 3 soft tissue deficiency).

**Virtual planning**: MGuide software (MIS Implants), surgical template fabricated.

**Surgery sequence**:
1. Midcrestal incision, full-thickness buccal flap
2. Horizontal relaxing cuts for tension-free closure
3. Implant placed (immediate loading not done)
4. Ossix Volumax cut and adapted to buccal aspect of ridge + around healing abutment
5. Secured with internal resorbable mattress sutures (palatal → buccal flap → around healing abutment)
6. Primary closure with simple interrupted resorbable sutures
7. Provisional acrylic restoration modified (unloaded)

**Healing**: 6 months

**Outcome at 6 months**: Significant buccal tissue volume increase, confirmed osseointegration → impressions → zirconia abutment → 3 bilayered zirconia-porcelain crowns

**Outcome at 2 years**: Favorable lifelike emergence profile, stable buccal tissue contour

## 4. Key Clinical Insights

**When to use Ossix Volumax alone** (per authors):
- Sufficient bone for primary implant stability
- Horizontal crestal deficiency (buccal concavity, Seibert Class 3)
- Missing tooth between natural teeth (interpositional defect)
- Patient prefers to avoid donor-site harvest

**When bone substitute should be added**:
- Complete circumferential bone defect
- Large vertical component
- Type C socket (insufficient primary stability from residual bone)

**Mechanism of Ossix Volumax volume maintenance**:
1. Thick scaffold prevents collapse into defect space
2. Expansion when wet fills the buccal concavity
3. Gradual ossification replaces collagen with mineralized tissue
4. Resists early membrane exposure degradation (cross-linking)

## 5. Limitations

- Single case — no controls, no volumetric comparison
- 2-year follow-up only
- Not randomised vs. conventional GBR + bone substitute
- Specific indication (Seibert 3, bone sufficient for implant) — cannot extrapolate to larger defects
- Quintessence Int not OA; no PMC full text; ingested from PDF
