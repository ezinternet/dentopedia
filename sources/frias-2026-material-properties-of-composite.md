## Why Ingested

기존 위키의 CAT attachment 문헌은 생체역학·효과(어떤 tooth movement에 attachment가 도움이 되는가, [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/nucera-2022-composite-attachments-clear-aligners-sr]])와 trimline 설계([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/nakornnoi-2024-aligner-trimline-biomechanics-tooth-movement-sr]])에 집중되어 있었고, attachment를 구성하는 **composite resin 자체의 재료과학적 특성**(SBS, wear, roughness, microhardness, color stability, 재현정확도)을 다룬 페이지는 없었다. 이 SR(Frias 2026)은 그 재료과학 gap을 채우며, nanohybrid/high-viscosity resin vs flowable resin의 trade-off를 정리해 attachment 재료 선택 근거를 제공한다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA, PubMed/Scopus/Cochrane, 2015–2025) of 15 studies (11 in-vitro, 4 clinical) on composite resin material properties for clear aligner therapy (CAT) orthodontic attachments — evaluating shear bond strength (SBS), wear resistance, surface roughness, microhardness, color stability, and attachment-reproduction accuracy.

All tested composite resins showed clinically acceptable SBS (~6–14 MPa); nanohybrid/high-viscosity resins (e.g., Filtek P60, Tetric N-Ceram) showed better mechanical resistance, wear resistance, and dimensional stability, while flowable resins (e.g., GC Aligner Connect, Z350 XT Flow) showed easier handling/mold adaptation but higher surface roughness and discoloration susceptibility (coffee/red wine worst).

Clinicians should prioritize nanohybrid/high-viscosity resins for high-load posterior attachments and flowable resins with high filler content for esthetic anterior zones; key limitation is that most included clinical studies did not report sample size or follow-up duration, and SBS results reflect the full bonding protocol (adhesive + curing + resin) rather than the resin in isolation.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (PRISMA, PubMed/Scopus/Cochrane, 2015–2025): 투명교정(Clear Aligner Therapy, CAT) attachment용 컴포지트 레진(composite resin)의 재료 특성 15편(in-vitro 11편, 임상 4편) — 전단결합강도(Shear Bond Strength, SBS), 마모저항성, 표면조도, 미세경도(microhardness), 색안정성, attachment 재현정확도 평가.

모든 레진이 임상적으로 허용 가능한 SBS(약 6–14 MPa) 보임; 나노하이브리드/고점도 레진(Filtek P60, Tetric N-Ceram 등)이 기계적 저항성·마모저항성·치수안정성 우수, 유동성(flowable) 레진(GC Aligner Connect, Z350 XT Flow 등)은 취급성·몰드적합성 우수하나 표면조도·변색(커피·레드와인 최악) 취약.

임상적으로 후방부 고하중 attachment는 나노하이브리드/고점도 레진, 심미부(전치부)는 필러 함량 높은 유동성 레진 우선 고려; 핵심 한계는 임상연구 대부분이 표본크기·추적기간 미보고이며 SBS 결과는 레진 단독이 아닌 전체 접착 프로토콜(접착제+광중합+레진)을 반영한다는 점.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First SR in this wiki focused specifically on the **material science** of composite resins used for CAT attachments (as opposed to their biomechanical/clinical effect on tooth movement).
- Structured comparison table (Table 3) mapping best/worst-performing materials per outcome: SBS, wear resistance, surface roughness, microhardness, color stability, accuracy of reproduction, handling/adaptation.
- Clinical translation: posterior (high-load) → nanohybrid/high-viscosity; anterior (esthetic) → flowable with high filler load; standardized photopolymerization protocol (~1000 mW/cm² for ~20 s) recommended to reliably translate lab properties into clinical performance.
- Explicit methodological caveat: SBS outcomes confound resin composition with adhesive system/bonding protocol — cannot fully isolate resin's independent contribution.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Search**: PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane, September 2025; PICO framework; search string `(orthodontic attachment OR attachment) AND (composite resins OR composite) AND (clear aligner OR aligner OR Invisalign)`; period 2015–2025.
- **Study selection**: 299 records (215 PubMed, 54 Scopus, 30 Cochrane) → 49 duplicates removed → 250 screened → 233 excluded (SR, animal study, wrong design/outcome, off-topic) → 17 full-text → 2 excluded (incomplete registrations) → **15 included** (11 in-vitro, 4 clinical), published 2019–2025.
- Two independent reviewers (title/abstract screening), third-author arbitration, Cohen's Kappa reported; Rayyan platform used.
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB 2.0 (5 domains: D1 randomization, D2 deviations, D3 missing data, D4 outcome measurement, D5 selective reporting) for clinical trials; adapted criteria for in-vitro studies. Most studies low risk across all domains; a few "some concerns" (missing randomization detail, no blinding).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **SBS**: all resins in clinically acceptable range (~6–14 MPa up to 21±4 MPa for Tetric N-Ceram); nanohybrid/high-filler resins (Filtek P60: 83 wt% filler; Tetric N-Ceram: ~81 wt% filler) trended highest, but confounded by adhesive protocol variability across studies.
- **Wear resistance**: SonicFill and Filtek P60 (high filler, high viscosity) outperformed Z350XT/Z350XT Flow and Flow Tain (flowable); higher inorganic filler content correlated with lower volumetric wear.
- **Surface roughness/microhardness**: higher-viscosity materials smoother; flowable resins (e.g., Aligner Connect under high irradiance) rougher. Microhardness variable — no consistent superiority, filler-type/polymerization dependent.
- **Color stability**: nanohybrid/high-filler resins (Omnichroma, G-ænial Universal Injectable) most stable; flowable resins (Tetric PowerFlow) most prone to discoloration; coffee and red wine were the most aggressive staining agents in both in-vitro and clinical data.
- **Accuracy of attachment reproduction**: high-viscosity resins with PET-G molds gave better shape fidelity with less overflow; flowable resins more prone to excess material, especially under short irradiance (10 s).
- **Polymerization protocol**: irradiance near 1000 mW/cm² for adequate exposure time linked to degree-of-conversion 52–75% and reduced dimensional inaccuracy/adhesive failure risk.
- In-vitro vs in-vivo agreement: general trend agreement (nanohybrid better SBS/wear/shape fidelity in vitro and clinically), though clinical discoloration slightly higher than in-vitro predictions, and lab-observed microhardness/roughness differences tend to diminish somewhat in the oral environment.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 4 clinical studies, none reporting sample size or follow-up duration clearly — weakens strength of clinical conclusions and cross-study comparability.
- SBS is confounded by adhesive system, enamel conditioning, curing parameters, and testing methodology — cannot isolate the composite resin's independent contribution.
- Heterogeneous mold materials, photopolymerization protocols, and wear/accuracy testing methods across in-vitro studies.
- Most clinical studies assess only early-treatment performance — long-term degradation under continuous aligner friction unassessed.
- Authors call for standardized adhesive system/etching/curing reporting and longer-term clinical trials with defined sample sizes and follow-up.

## 7. Glossary

- **SBS (Shear Bond Strength)**: force per unit area required to debond the composite attachment from enamel; measured in MPa.
- **Nanohybrid composite resin**: resin with a mix of nano- and micro-scale filler particles, generally higher filler load (~72–83 wt%), associated with better mechanical properties.
- **Flowable composite resin**: lower-viscosity resin (higher TEGDMA/Bis-EMA diluent monomer content) offering easier handling/mold adaptation but typically lower filler content and mechanical resistance.
- **PET-G**: glycol-modified polyethylene terephthalate, the thermoformed template material used to shape attachments during direct bonding.
- **Degree of conversion**: proportion of monomer double bonds converted to polymer during light-curing; affects mechanical properties and residual monomer release.
- **RoB 2.0**: revised Cochrane Risk-of-Bias tool for randomized trials, used here to assess clinical study quality across 5 domains.
