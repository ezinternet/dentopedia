## Why Ingested

기존 [[wiki/endodontics/vpt/aapd-2025-vital-pulp-therapy-permanent-teeth-guideline]]가 증상성 비가역 치수염(symptomatic irreversible pulpitis)에서도 치수절단술(pulpotomy)을 선택적으로 고려할 수 있다고 명시했으나, 그 근거의 강건성(evidence robustness)은 불명확했다. 본 umbrella review(Mtalsi 2026)는 2018–2022년 사이 발표된 11편의 체계적 문헌고찰(SR)을 AMSTAR 2로 메타평가하여, 그 근거 대부분이 "critically low"~"low" 품질(moderate 1편)임을 드러내 신중한 해석을 요구한다. 재료 위계(Biodentine·MTA·CEM > CH)는 [[wiki/endodontics/vpt/fasoulas-2023-pulpotomy-direct-pulp-capping-sr-ma]]의 베이지안 네트워크 메타분석 결론과 일치해 이를 보강한다.

## Three-line Summary
Umbrella review (PRISMA/PROSPERO CRD42024500772, Cureus 2026) of 11 systematic reviews (2018–2022, from 332 initial hits) on pulpotomy for symptomatic irreversible pulpitis in mature permanent teeth, appraised with AMSTAR 2.

Pulpotomy success reached up to 100% overall (short-term 80–95%, often exceeding root canal treatment); calcium hydroxide had the lowest success (as low as 37%) while calcium silicate-based cements (Biodentine 80–100%, MTA 82–100% clinical, CEM 86–100%) performed best; long-term (>36 mo) success may decline (93.97%→71.3% at 60 mo in one included SR).

Methodological quality of the underlying SRs was mostly "critically low" to "low" (only 1/11 "moderate" per AMSTAR 2), so despite favorable numbers the evidence base calls for cautious interpretation and high-quality long-term RCTs.

## 세줄요약
Umbrella review(PRISMA/PROSPERO CRD42024500772, Cureus 2026): 332편 중 선별된 2018–2022년 체계적 문헌고찰(Systematic Review, SR) 11편을 AMSTAR 2로 메타평가, 성숙 영구치 증상성 비가역 치수염(symptomatic irreversible pulpitis)의 치수절단술(pulpotomy) 효과 분석.

전체 치수절단술 성공률은 최대 100%(단기 80–95%, 근관치료보다 우수한 경우 다수); 수산화칼슘(Calcium Hydroxide, CH)이 최저(최저 37%), 칼슘실리케이트계 시멘트(Biodentine 80–100%, MTA 임상 82–100%, CEM 86–100%)가 최고 성적; 장기(36개월 초과) 성공률은 감소 가능(포함 SR 1편에서 93.97%→60개월 71.3%).

기저 SR들의 방법론적 질은 대부분 "critically low"~"low"(AMSTAR 2 기준 moderate는 11편 중 1편뿐)로, 우수한 수치에도 불구하고 신중한 해석과 장기 고품질 RCT가 필요하다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First umbrella review synthesizing 11 SRs (2018–2022) specifically on pulpotomy for **mature** permanent teeth with **irreversible** pulpitis, appraised with AMSTAR 2.
- Consolidates material-specific success-rate ranges across the SR literature: CH (37–100%, generally lowest), MTA (44–100% depending on outcome type), CEM (78–100%), Biodentine (80–100%, highest and most consistent, lower discoloration risk), PRF (35.5–100%, experimental).
- Confirms short-term (≤12 mo) pulpotomy success (80–95%) often exceeds conventional RCT, with long-term outcomes broadly comparable, though one included SR (Cushley 2019) shows a decline from 93.97% (36 mo) to 71.3% (60 mo).
- Identifies non-material determinants of success: perioperative technique/hemostasis, periapical involvement, and — most emphasized — coronal seal quality (prosthetic crowns/amalgam > composite resin).
- Explicitly flags the evidence base as methodologically weak (10/11 SRs rated "critically low" or "low" on AMSTAR 2), tempering the otherwise favorable success-rate figures.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- PRISMA-guided umbrella review; PROSPERO-registered (CRD42024500772).
- 5-database search (PubMed, Google Scholar, Science Direct, CINAHL, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews), Oct–Dec 2022, English-language only, PICO framework (Population: children/general population with mature permanent teeth + caries + irreversible pulpitis; Intervention: pulpotomy; Comparison: pulpectomy; Outcome: healing/asymptomatic tooth).
- Dual independent screening + full-text review, third-reviewer arbitration for disagreements.
- Methodological quality of included SRs assessed with AMSTAR 2 (16-item tool) by two independent raters (kappa = 1.000).
- 332 initial hits → 15 duplicates removed → 304 excluded by title/abstract → 13 full-text reviewed → 2 excluded (primary/immature teeth) → **11 SRs included**.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Overall pulpotomy success up to 100%, varying by material and follow-up length.
- Material-specific clinical/radiographic success ranges (see source table): CH lowest (as low as 37%); MTA clinical 82–100% / radiographic 53.3–95%; CEM clinical 86–100% / radiographic 86–98%; Biodentine 80–100%.
- AMSTAR 2 quality distribution: 6 "critically low," 4 "low," 1 "moderate" (Cushley et al. 2019 — the highest-quality included SR).
- Short-term (≤12 mo) success 80–95%, exceeding RCT in several SRs; better postoperative pain control with pulpotomy reported in multiple SRs; long-term outcomes broadly comparable to RCT except a reported decline to 71.3% at 60 months in one SR.
- 4 of the 11 SRs directly compared pulpotomy vs. conventional root canal treatment, concluding comparable efficacy.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Underlying evidence base is predominantly "critically low"/"low" AMSTAR 2 quality — authors explicitly caution against over-interpreting the favorable success percentages.
- No long-term follow-up data beyond 5 years across any included SR.
- Substantial heterogeneity in population age, materials, and outcome-assessment criteria (clinical vs. radiographic vs. histological) across the 11 SRs.
- Pediatric-specific conclusions could not be drawn (only 3/11 SRs explicitly included pediatric cohorts; broad age range 6–79 years pooled across reviews).
- Authors call for multicenter, standardized-diagnostic-criteria RCTs with >5-year follow-up, especially in adolescents with mature permanent teeth.

## 6. Related Work
- Aligns with AAE (2021) and European Society of Endodontology (ESE, 2019) recommendations recognizing pulpotomy as viable under well-defined clinical conditions — see [[wiki/endodontics/vpt/aapd-2025-vital-pulp-therapy-permanent-teeth-guideline]] for the most recent (2025) consensus guideline extending this to permanent-tooth VPT broadly.
- Material hierarchy (Biodentine/MTA/CEM > CH) is corroborated by the Bayesian network meta-analysis in [[wiki/endodontics/vpt/fasoulas-2023-pulpotomy-direct-pulp-capping-sr-ma]].
- Complements single-study prospective data on MTA pulpotomy in mature adult teeth, e.g. [[wiki/endodontics/vpt/taha-2017-mta-pulpotomy-mature-permanent-carious]].

## 7. Glossary
- **Pulpotomy**: removal of the coronal (and, in partial pulpotomy, only a portion of the coronal) pulp tissue while preserving the remaining vital radicular pulp.
- **Irreversible pulpitis**: clinical diagnosis of pulp inflammation considered incapable of healing, classically an indication for root canal treatment; histologically often confined to coronal pulp only.
- **AMSTAR 2**: A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews, 16-item critical appraisal instrument for SR methodological quality (ratings: high/moderate/low/critically low).
- **MTA**: Mineral Trioxide Aggregate, a calcium silicate-based hydraulic cement used as a pulp capping/pulpotomy material.
- **CEM cement**: Calcium-Enriched Mixture cement, a bioceramic pulp capping material.
- **Biodentine**: a tricalcium silicate-based bioactive dentin substitute/pulp capping material (Septodont).
- **PRF**: Platelet-Rich Fibrin, an autologous biomaterial explored as a pulp capping scaffold.
- **DPSC-IP**: Dental Pulp Stem Cells - Inflammatory Pulp, a stem-cell population implicated in pulp regenerative potential even under inflammatory conditions.
