## Why Ingested

The wiki has bruxism + implant complications covered at the SR/overview level but lacks data specifically on **single crown** restorations (as opposed to full-arch or FPDs). This 278-patient Malmö University retrospective is the most detailed single-crown dataset showing bruxism is a significant Cox regression predictor for crown failure. Fills a prosthetics-type gap alongside [[implants/survival/chawki-2026-particularities-of-the-implant-supported]] and the concurrent vidal-2025 SR on bruxism + mechanical complications.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study (Malmö University, 2009–2019; 278 patients, 358 single crowns; mean follow-up 56.5 ± 29.7 months) investigating risk factors for implant-supported single crown failure and technical complications via Cox proportional hazard regression.

Twenty crowns failed (cumulative survival rate 93.5% at 5 years, 92.2% at 6–11 years); the most common failure causes were porcelain large fracture (n=6), crown repeatedly loose (n=6), and porcelain chipping (n=5); probable bruxism and male sex were the only two Cox-regression predictors significant in both univariate and multivariate models.

Ceramic chipping was more prevalent in bruxers vs non-bruxers (20/101 vs 22/257, p=0.014 log-rank); at least one technical complication occurred in 36/101 bruxers vs 52/257 non-bruxers (p=0.002), supporting bruxism as a key parafunctional risk for single-crown prosthetic morbidity.

## 세줄요약

말뫼대학교 회향 연구(2009–2019; 278명·358개 단일 크라운; 평균 추적 56.5개월) — Cox 비례위험 회귀로 임플란트 지지 단관 보철(Implant-Supported Single Crown)의 실패·기술 합병증 위험 인자 분석.

20개 크라운 실패(누적 생존율(Cumulative Survival Rate, CSR) 5년 93.5%·6–11년 92.2%); 주요 실패 원인 — 도재 대파절(n=6)·크라운 반복 이완(n=6)·도재 칩핑(n=5); 이갈이 가능(probable bruxism)과 남성이 단변량·다변량 Cox 모형 모두에서 유의한 예측 인자.

도재 칩핑은 이갈이군에서 비이갈이군보다 유의하게 많았고(20/101 vs 22/257, p=0.014 로그-순위), 기술 합병증 1건 이상 발생률도 이갈이군이 높아(36/101 vs 52/257, p=0.002), 단관 보철에서도 이갈이가 핵심 파라기능 위험 인자임을 확인.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Medicina (Kaunas) 2023;59(9):1603 (MDPI)
- **DOI**: 10.3390/medicina59091603 | **PMID**: 37763722 | **PMCID**: PMC10535933
- **Institution**: Faculty of Odontology, Malmö University, Malmö, Sweden
- **Type**: Retrospective cohort study (single center, treatment period 2009–2019)
- **Ethics**: Approved by Regional Ethical Committee, Lund, Sweden (Dnr 2018/856)

## 2. Study Population
- **Inclusion**: All patients receiving ≥1 implant-supported single crown at Malmö University Faculty of Odontology, March 2009 – December 2019; modern threaded cylindrical or conical implants only; any follow-up required (cases with zero follow-up after crown delivery excluded)
- **Final sample**: 278 patients, 358 implant-supported single crowns
- **Mean age ± SD**: 54.3 ± 12.6 years (range 25.3–91.7) at crown delivery
- **Mean follow-up ± SD**: 56.5 ± 29.7 months (range 0.2–146.2) after prosthesis installation
- **Crowns followed ≥5 years**: 167/358 (46.6%); ≥10 years: 6/358 (1.7%)
- **Implant distribution**: Straumann (n=177), Astra (n=121), Nobel Biocare (n=42), Biomet (n=15), other (Southern, Camlog, Ankylos; 1 each)
- **Mean implant-to-crown interval ± SD**: 6.8 ± 2.8 months (range 1.8–38.8)

## 3. Definitions
- **Implant failure**: Implant removal due to signs/symptoms of failed osseointegration (early or late) or implant fracture
- **Prosthesis failure**: Any condition leading to crown removal/replacement (implant failure per se not counted as crown failure)
- **Probable bruxism**: Self-report (questionnaire/anamnestic examination) PLUS clinical signs — masticatory muscle hypertrophy, tongue/lip indentations, linea alba, mechanical tooth wear, cracked teeth, repetitive prosthetic failures; both criteria required
- **Smoker**: ≥1 cigarette/day (everyday smoker definition)

## 4. Results — Implant Outcomes
- **Implant failures**: 7 implants failed (2 Straumann, 5 Astra); mean time to failure 76.5 ± 43.7 months (range 24.5–134.4)
- **Implant fracture**: 1 case (Astra Osseospeed 4.00 × 11.0 mm, region 46)

## 5. Results — Crown Survival
| Interval | Cumulative Survival Rate |
|---|---|
| 5 years | 93.5% |
| 6–11 years | 92.2% |

- **Total crown failures**: 20 crowns; mean time to failure 21.3 ± 23.5 months (range 0.3–82.1)
- **Failure reasons**:
  - Porcelain large fracture: n=6
  - Crown repeatedly loose: n=6
  - Porcelain chipping: n=5
  - Crown in infraposition: n=1
  - Prosthesis misfit: n=1
  - Crown overextended: n=1

## 6. Results — Cox Regression (Risk Factors for Crown Failure)
- **Univariate Cox significant predictors**: Male sex; probable bruxism
- **Multivariate Cox significant predictors**: Male sex; probable bruxism (both remained significant; multicollinearity not detected)
- Note: actual HR values and confidence intervals are reported in Tables 3–4 of the published paper; the PMC full text did not carry table content in the retrieved file

## 7. Results — Technical Complications
| Complication | Bruxers (101 crowns) | Non-bruxers (257 crowns) | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ceramic chipping | 20/101 | 22/257 | p=0.014 (Kaplan–Meier log-rank) |
| ≥1 technical complication (excl. screw-hole sealing loss) | 36/101 | 52/257 | p=0.002 (Pearson chi-squared) |

- **Most common complications overall**: Crown mobility; ceramic chipping
- **Notable finding**: Ceramic chipping observed even in monolithic zirconia crowns (attributed to low-temperature degradation and screw-access hole unsupported ceramic)
- **Bruxer count**: 101 crowns (male distribution: 37/145 crowns; female: 64/213 crowns; difference not significant, p=0.350)

## 8. Discussion Highlights
- Male sex risk interpreted via higher maximal bite force (not via bruxism co-distribution, which was non-significant between sexes)
- CSR 92.2% at 6–11 years is consistent with other 10-year reports: 91.7%, 95.2%, 95.9%
- Screw loosening most common probable mechanism for crown mobility; screw-retained design (94% of cases) enables retrievability
- Monolithic zirconia chipping: low-temperature degradation (tetragonal→monoclinic phase transition in humid environment) proposed; implant-restoration angulation may also contribute
- Limitations: retrospective design, treatment not standardized, multiple operators, incomplete records
