## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (bovine rib, n=80 osteotomy sites, 4 groups) comparing continuous vs intermittent drilling technique × two irrigation temperatures (23.7°C vs 6.0°C) on bone temperature during implant site preparation.

No significant temperature difference between continuous and intermittent drilling at the same irrigation temperature (W=86, p=0.45); cooled irrigation (6.0°C) significantly reduced temperature vs room-temperature irrigation (p=0.0001) regardless of technique; all groups remained below the 47°C necrosis threshold.

Irrigation temperature, not drilling technique (continuous vs intermittent), is the decisive thermal variable in this in vitro model.

## 세줄요약

In vitro 연구(소 늑골, n=80 골절제 부위, 4군): 연속 vs 간헐 드릴링 × 관주 온도(23.7°C vs 6.0°C) 2×2 비교.

연속 vs 간헐 기법 간 온도 차이 비유의(p=0.45); 냉각 관주(6.0°C)만 유의미한 온도 감소(p=0.0001); 전 군 47°C 미만 유지.

드릴링 기법(연속 vs 간헐)이 아닌 관주 온도가 결정인자.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Direct head-to-head comparison of continuous vs intermittent drilling in identical conditions — unusual design that isolates technique as the sole variable.
- Demonstrates no significant thermal benefit from intermittent (peck) drilling when compared to continuous drilling at the same irrigation temperature.
- Reinforces that chilled irrigation (6.0°C) is the dominant mitigating factor over drilling technique choice.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: In vitro experimental
- **Bone model**: Fresh bovine rib specimens (16 bone blocks, 5 sites per block)
- **N**: 80 implant sites, 13 mm osteotomy depth
- **Groups** (2×2):
  - A: Continuous drilling + 23.7°C saline
  - B: Intermittent drilling + 23.7°C saline
  - C: Continuous drilling + 6.0°C saline
  - D: Intermittent drilling + 6.0°C saline
- **Measurement**: Thermal probe (type not specified in abstract)
- **Statistics**: Wilcoxon signed-rank test (W statistic reported)
- **Note**: Specific peck interval (mm) not disclosed in abstract

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- A vs B (continuous vs intermittent, room-temp): W=86, p=0.45 — **NS**
- C vs D (continuous vs intermittent, cold): comparable (implying NS)
- B vs D (intermittent, room vs cold): W=45, p=0.0005 — **significant**
- C vs D (continuous cold vs intermittent cold): W=41, p=0.003 — **significant**
- All groups: bone temperature did not exceed 47°C

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- In vitro only; no irrigation perfusion from vital bone
- Peck interval not disclosed — cannot determine whether a different interval might show significance
- Abstract-level data only; statistical method (Wilcoxon) suggests non-normal distribution but sample details unclear
- Room-temperature saline at 23.7°C and cold at 6.0°C — intermediate temperatures not tested

## 6. Related Work

- Kapse 2022 ([[implants/kapse-2022-thermal-effect-operatory-room-temperature-surgical]]): same conclusion — irrigation temperature is the only significant variable in a 4-factor crossed design
- Gehrke 2020 ([[implants/gehrke-2020-technique-drill-design-osteotomy]]): contrasting finding — intermittent movement significantly reduces temperature for cylindrical drills (in vivo); the discrepancy may reflect bone model (bovine rib vs rabbit tibia) or drill geometry differences

## 7. Glossary

- **Continuous drilling technique**: Uninterrupted drill advance from surface to full osteotomy depth
- **Intermittent drilling technique**: Staged advance with drill withdrawal between passes (specific interval not disclosed)
- **W (Wilcoxon)**: Test statistic for the Wilcoxon signed-rank non-parametric test
