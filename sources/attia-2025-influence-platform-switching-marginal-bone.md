## Why Ingested

기존 platform switching(PS) 근거는 대부분 지연 식립(delayed placement) 기반이며, 즉시식립(Immediate Implant Placement, IIP)과 PS의 교차 지점은 wiki에 별도 근거가 없던 gap. 본 후향적 연구(6개월~23년 추적)는 그 gap을 직접 메우는 논문으로 인제스트. [[wiki/implants/mbl/juan-montesinos-2022-platform-switching-conventional-sr-ma]]의 PS 이점을 즉시식립 맥락에서 재검증.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective clinical study (37 patients with immediate dental implants, 21 platform switching (PS) vs 16 platform matching (PM), follow-up 6 months to 23 years, University Hospital Gießen) comparing marginal bone loss (MBL) and implant success.

Mesial MBL was significantly lower with PS (0.26mm vs 0.75mm, p=0.044); regression confirmed PM associated with significantly greater mesial bone loss (β=2.11, p=0.039); distal MBL and overall implant success (Buser/Albrektsson/Attia scores) showed no significant group difference.

Both configurations remained functional long-term (up to 23 years); PS may offer a mesial-bone-preservation advantage in immediate implants, but small sample size and an uneven smoking-status distribution (PS group only) limit certainty.

## 세줄요약

후향적 임상연구(즉시식립 임플란트 37명, 플랫폼 스위칭(PS) 21 vs 일반형(PM) 16, 추적 6개월~23년, 독일 기센대학병원) — 변연골소실(Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)·임플란트 성공률 비교.

근심측 MBL은 PS군에서 유의하게 낮음(0.26mm vs 0.75mm, p=0.044); 회귀분석에서도 PM이 근심측 골소실 증가와 유의하게 연관(β=2.11, p=0.039); 원심측 MBL·전체 성공률(Buser/Albrektsson/Attia 점수)은 군간 유의차 없음.

두 방식 모두 장기(최대 23년) 기능 유지; PS가 즉시식립에서 근심측 골보존 이점을 가질 수 있으나, 소표본·흡연자가 PS군에만 편중된 점이 확신도를 제한.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study (per authors) to specifically examine PS vs PM in the **immediately inserted** implant context, distinct from the majority of PS literature built on delayed placement.
- Very long observation window (6 months to 23 years) — captures both early remodeling and multi-decade functional stability.
- Introduces and applies the newly developed **Attia score** (18-point composite of clinical, radiographic, and patient-reported outcomes) alongside classic Buser/Albrektsson criteria, giving a more granular success measure than binary pass/fail.
- Multiple linear regression isolates the PS/PM effect on mesial total bone loss (TBL) after adjusting for confounders (age, sex, chronic illness, medication, smoking, jaw, implant diameter/length, service time).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective clinical study, University Hospital Gießen and Marburg (UKGM), Germany. Patients treated 2000–2023, follow-up exams June 2023–February 2024. Ethics approval 126/22.
- **Sample**: 37 patients with immediate implants after tooth extraction, under functional load ≥6 months. 21 PS (57%), 16 PM (43%). BEGO RI implant system used in 83.8% of cases.
- **Outcome — MBL**: measured on orthopantomograms (OPG) via SIDEXIS XS/4 Viewer software; postoperative OPG vs current OPG, mesial and distal sides.
- **Outcome — success**: Buser (1990) criteria; Albrektsson criteria (incl. <0.2mm/year bone loss after year 1); Attia score (0–18 points: mobility/fracture/malposition exclusion criteria, then up to 2 points each for pain-free/bone-loss/probing-depth/pus/bleeding, 1 point each for plaque/prosthetic-complications/mucosa, and 1 point each for foreign-body-sensation/paresthesia/aesthetic/masticatory/speech satisfaction).
- **Statistics**: SPSS v28 + Jamovi v2.3. Chi-squared, Fisher's exact, Mann-Whitney U, Spearman correlation for group comparisons; multiple linear regression (MLR) for total bone loss (TBL) predictors, adjusted for confounders.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Mesial current bone level**: PS 2.24 ± 0.74mm vs PM 3.00 ± 1.19mm (p=0.044) — significantly lower (better) in PS.
- **Mesial total bone loss**: PS 0.26 ± 1.12mm vs PM 0.75 ± 1.05mm (p=0.476, group comparison NS; but MLR below is significant).
- **MLR for mesial TBL** (R²=0.521): platform matching vs switching β=2.11 (95% CI 0.11–4.11, p=0.039) — PM independently associated with greater mesial bone loss after adjusting for service time and other confounders.
- **Distal MBL**: no significant difference (baseline p=0.940, current p=0.728, TBL p=0.774).
- **Success scores**: Buser — 97.3% success overall, no significant group difference (p=0.432). Albrektsson — 45.9% success overall, no significant difference (p=0.272). Attia score — PS 16.24 ± 1.04 vs PM 13.71 ± 6.59 (p=0.794, NS).
- **Confound**: smoking status differed significantly between groups — 42.9% smokers in PS group vs 0% in PM group (p=0.005); service time was significantly longer in PM (9.69 vs 3.18 years, p<0.001) since PM implants were, on average, older in this retrospective cohort.
- Patient satisfaction (chewing, speech, aesthetics, overall) high in both groups with no significant differences.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small sample (n=37; 21 PS vs 16 PM) limits statistical power, especially for distal-side and success-score comparisons.
- **Smoking confound**: all smokers were in the PS group (42.9% of PS patients) — despite smoking's known association with higher MBL/failure risk, PS still outperformed PM mesially, but this imbalance was not adjusted for as cleanly as service time.
- Wide, uneven follow-up range (6 months–23 years) mixes early remodeling with decades-long stability; PM group had substantially longer mean service time, complicating direct comparison.
- Authors explicitly call for prospective RCTs with larger samples, particularly in smokers/risk-factor patients, and for validating the novel Attia score's reproducibility in other populations.

## 7. Glossary

- **Platform Switching (PS)**: abutment diameter narrower than the implant platform, shifting the microgap inward.
- **Platform Matching (PM)**: abutment diameter equal to implant platform diameter (conventional configuration).
- **Total Bone Loss (TBL)**: current bone level minus baseline (postoperative) bone level, in mm.
- **Attia score**: an 18-point composite implant-success index combining clinical, radiographic, and patient-reported outcome measures, newly proposed by this research group.
- **Buser / Albrektsson criteria**: classic binary implant-success criteria based on mobility, radiolucency, pain/infection, and annual bone-loss thresholds.
