## Why Ingested

위키에 급성 악관절 탈구 (acute temporomandibular joint luxation, acute TMJ luxation) 복원을 **근거 등급으로 정리한 페이지가 없었고**, [[tmj/stolbizer-2020-anterior-dislocation-of-the-temporomandibular]]은 42예 단일군 기술 보고로 복원 기법 하나만 다룬다. 이 논문은 50년 간 출판된 무작위대조시험 (randomized controlled trial, RCT) 8편·338명을 systematic review로 묶어 "어떤 방법이 어떤 근거 수준을 갖는가"를 처음으로 정량화한다 — 급성 수동복원 (manual reduction)의 근거는 세 편(185명)뿐이라는 것과, 수술 기법 RCT가 **한 편도 없다**는 결론이 우리 위키의 근거 사각지대를 정확히 짚어준다. 또한 덱스트로스 증식치료 (dextrose prolotherapy)에 대해서는 위배 placebo 대조군 3편에서 유의한 우위를 보고한 기존 페이지 [[tmj/park-2024-dextrose-prolotherapy-tmd]]와 정면으로 다른 결론을 내놓으므로, 이 주제의 근거 기준점 (evidence-anchor)으로 필수 불가피하다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (SR) without meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials only, searching PubMed, Cochrane Library and Web of Science from database inception to an update on 26 March 2018: 113 unique abstracts screened → 9 full texts → 8 RCTs with 338 patients included, three studies (n=185) on acute manual reduction of luxation and five studies (n=153) on minimally invasive injection (autologous blood or dextrose prolotherapy) for recurrent luxation; **no RCT on any surgical technique was found**.

For acute reduction, first-try success was 87% (conventional intraoral) vs 67% (extraoral) vs 97% (wrist pivot) in n=90 (p<0.01 wrist pivot vs extraoral), and 86.2% vs 55.2% in 55 patients (p=0.009); a supine technique cut reduction time from 269±70 s to 173±17 s and pain (VAS) from 8.1±0.7 to 6.3±0.8. For recurrent luxation, autologous blood injection into the superior joint space **plus** pericapsular tissue with 4 weeks of intermaxillary fixation gave the largest interincisal reduction (11 mm vs 9 mm IMF alone vs 8 mm injection alone, p<0.0001) and **zero recurrences in 16 patients**, whereas injection alone recurred in 8/16; dextrose prolotherapy showed no significant advantage over placebo in any of three trials.

Clinical relevance: autologous blood injection plus intermaxillary fixation is the best-supported treatment for *recurrent* luxation, while acute episodes should first be managed by manual repositioning (수동복원) — but the entire evidence base for surgery (eminectomy and its variants) is case reports and non-randomised cohorts, with 6 of 8 trials at moderate risk of bias, follow-up never exceeding 12 months, and a 36-month recurrence-free benchmark (de Almeida et al.) that no included study met.

## 세줄요약

메타분석 없는 체계적 리뷰 (systematic review without meta-analysis, SR) — PubMed·Cochrane Library·Web of Science를 데이터베이스 inception부터 2018년 3월 26일까지 검색하여 동두인 평정으로 초록 113건 → 전문 9편을 거친 결과, 무작위대조시험 (randomized controlled trial, RCT) 8편·환자 338명만 포함(3편 185명 = 급성 수동복원, 5편 153명 = 재발성 탈구의 자가혈액/덱스트로스 주사). **수술 기법을 평가한 RCT는 한 편도 존재하지 않았다.**

급성 복원 1차 성공률은 구강내 기존법 87% · 외인법 67% · 손목축 회전법(wrist pivot) 97%(n=90, 외인법 대비 p<0.01), 별도 연구에서 86.2% 대 55.2%(p=0.009); 앙와위 기법은 소요시간 269±70초 → 173±17초, 통증(시각적 아날로그 척도, visual analog scale, VAS) 8.1±0.7 → 6.3±0.8로 개선. 재발성 탈구에서는 상관절강 (superior joint space, SJS) + 관절주변 조직 (pericapsular tissue, PT) 자가혈액 주사 (autologous blood injection, ABI)에 4주간 악간고정술 (intermaxillary fixation, IMF) 을 병행한 군이 절치간 거리 감소 11 mm(ABI 단독 8 mm, IMF 단독 9 mm, p<0.0001)와 **16명 전원 재발 0회**로 최고 성적을 보였고, ABI 단독군은 16명 중 8명이 재발함. 덱스트로스 증식치료 (dextrose prolotherapy) 는 위배군 대조 3편 모두에서 placebo 대비 유의한 우위를 보이지 못함.

임상적 함의: 재발성 탈구는 ABI + IMF가 현재 가장 과학적 근거가 충분한 치료이고 급성 에피소드는 수동복원 (manual reduction) 이 1차 선택이나, 관절융기절제술 (eminectomy) 을 포함한 수술 영역의 근거는 전부 사례보고·비무작군에 머물고 8편 중 6편이 중등도 위험편향, 추적기간은 12개월을 넘지 않으며 de Almeida 등의 재발소실 36개월 기준을 충족한 연구는 하나도 없음 — 수술 기법·장기 추적·환자 경험 평가가 필요하다. 덱스트로스 우위 없음은 [[tmj/park-2024-dextrose-prolotherapy-tmd]]의 무대조 후향 연구 결론과 대비됨.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **First evidence-tiering of the entire TMJ-luxation treatment literature**: of roughly 50 years of publications, only 8 RCTs met the criteria (338 patients) — and none of them evaluated a surgical technique, even though eminectomy is the commonly recommended operation for recurrent luxation.
- **Establishes the acute-reduction baseline**: manual repositioning is first choice, and the only comparative RCT data are three small technique-comparison trials (185 patients) with short-term outcomes only.
- **Names the single best-supported preventive regimen**: autologous blood injection into the superior joint space *and* pericapsular tissue combined with 4 weeks of intermaxillary fixation — 11 mm interincisal reduction and 0/16 recurrences at 1 year (Hegab 2013), the highest success rate in the review.
- **Kills the prolotherapy claim for luxation**: three of five injection trials had placebo arms, and dextrose was not superior to placebo for mouth opening, pain or luxation frequency in any consistent way (Mustafa 2018, Cömert Kiliç 2016, Refai 2011).
- **Documents the dose-escalation of repeat injections**: in Hegab 2013, 6/16 patients recurred after the first ABI, 2/16 after a second, and 0/16 after a third.
- **Documents the surgical-fallout pathway**: in Daif 2010, 6/15 superior-joint-space-only patients and 3/15 superior-space-plus-pericapsular patients recurred and were then treated surgically (patients refused further injections); those surgical outcomes were never reported.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Systematic literature review restricted to **randomized controlled trials** only; no meta-analysis (no pooling, no effect sizes).
- **Databases**: PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science — inception of each database to 31 October 2015, update search 26 March 2018.
- **Search string (PubMed, Table 1)**: ("Temporomandibular joint"[MeSH] OR "TMJ"[MeSH]) = 26,047 AND ("Luxation" 3,526 OR "Subluxation" 43,945 OR "Dislocation" 63,547 OR "Open lock" 397 OR "Hypermobility" 2,513 OR "JHS" 649 OR "Joint hypermobility syndrome" 2,220) = 73,406, then combined = 3,203, then RCT filter (718,112) = **93 hits**. Reference lists of included articles were hand-searched; grey literature excluded; authors not contacted.
- **Inclusion**: clinical RCTs in English, Swedish or German on patients diagnosed with TMJ luxation. **Exclusion**: non-RCT designs, dual publication, TMJ fracture studies, and studies with **fewer than 10 patients**.
- **Screening**: two authors (HA, BHH) independently read all titles/abstracts; disagreement resolved by discussion. 113 unique articles → 9 full texts → 1 excluded (Sato et al., did not meet criteria) → **8 articles included**.
- **Data extraction**: HA extracted, BHH reviewed — clinic setting, inclusion/exclusion criteria, n, age/sex, dropouts, treatment method, follow-up, results, authors' conclusions.
- **Quality assessment**: SBU (Swedish Agency for Health Technology Assessment) RCT tool, modified by combining both Cochrane Risk-of-Bias domains and adding a conflict-of-interest item under "other risks of bias". **6 of 8 articles = moderate risk of bias, 2 = low.**
- **Outcomes**: first-try successful reduction, reduction time, pain (VAS), maximal mouth opening (MMO) / interincisal distance, recurrence frequency, radiographic change.

### Background epidemiology quoted

- Two emergency departments with 100,000 combined annual visits reported an average of **5.3 TMJ luxation cases per year over 7 years**.
- TMJ luxation accounts for **3% of all reported dislocated joints in the body**; bilateral luxation with a straight-open mandible is most common.
- Women are more likely to luxate because the joint is generally more flexible; predictors include yawning, higher age and male sex in one retrospective Nigerian series.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No surgical evidence at all**: no RCT on eminectomy, Dautrey's zygomatic-arch down-fracture, miniplating, bone grafting, alloplastic eminence implants, lateral pterygoid myotomy/tendon scarification, temporalis tendon scarification or capsular plication. The authors note the ethical difficulty of a sham-surgery control (citing the arthroscopic-lavage knee RCT that found placebo surgery no worse).
- **Recruitment, not design, is the bottleneck**: recurrent luxation is too rare for single-centre RCTs — multicenter trials are needed; a 90-patient study and a 12-patient study (6 per arm) both passed the n≥10 filter.
- **Short follow-up**: from acute treatment with *no* follow-up to a maximum of 1 year. de Almeida et al. (2016) proposed a **36-month recurrence-free period** as the benchmark for a stable surgical result, and their own SR (cohort studies only, ≥3 years, no RCTs) concluded that no treatment could be shown to guarantee long-term elimination of recurrent luxation.
- **Methodological weaknesses across included studies**: absent placebo groups, small numbers, short follow-up, and "operator-dependent comparative studies rather than true RCTs" — the authors explicitly say several of the three reduction studies are not genuine RCTs.
- **Outcomes are narrow**: **no included study evaluated quality of life**; only three of eight measured pain, and pain intensity did not differ between placebo and treatment arms at follow-up.
- **Unevidenced options explicitly named**: jaw exercises (moderate effect claimed, evidence weak), and botulinum toxin into masseter and pterygoid muscles (no RCTs, no guideline consensus).
- **Requested next research**: well-designed RCTs of surgical and nonsurgical techniques with sufficient patient numbers, **long-term follow-up**, and explicit **patient-experience assessment**.
- **Heterogeneous success definitions** across the surgical literature (noted via Martins et al.) make comparisons difficult.

## 7. Glossary

- **TMJ luxation (dislocation)**: condyle translates anterior to the articular eminence and cannot return to the glenoid fossa without clinician assistance; the jaw is locked open/protruded, or deviated to the contralateral side in unilateral cases. Contrast **subluxation**, which catches briefly in the open position and reduces by patient self-maneuver.
- **Manual reduction (수동복원)**: manual repositioning of the condylar head back into the glenoid fossa — first-choice acute treatment. Variants compared here: conventional intraoral, extraoral, wrist pivot, and supine-position technique.
- **Intermaxillary fixation (IMF, 악간고정술)**: jaw immobilisation by wiring/splinting the teeth together; here 4 weeks, used as adjunct to reduce recurrence.
- **Autologous blood injection (ABI, 자가혈액 주사)**: injection of the patient's own blood into the joint — either the superior joint space (SJS) only, or SJS **plus** pericapsular tissues (PT); presumed to act by inducing fibrosis/scar that restricts condylar translation.
- **Prolotherapy (증식치료)**: injection of a sclerosing or proliferant solution (here 10%, 20% or 30% dextrose) to provoke a healing/fibrotic response in lax joint structures.
- **MMO (maximal mouth opening, 최대 개구량)**, measured as interincisal distance; reduction of excessive MMO is the primary efficacy outcome in the injection trials.
- **Eminectomy (관절융기절제술)**: surgical removal of the articular eminence to clear the path of the condylar head — the recommended operation for recurrent luxation, but with no RCT support.
