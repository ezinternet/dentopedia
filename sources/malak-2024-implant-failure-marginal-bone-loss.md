## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review with meta-analysis (8 systematic reviews with meta-analysis pooled; electronic literature search, no language restriction) comparing marginal bone loss (MBL) and implant failure between tilted and axial implants supporting full-arch or fixed partial dentures.

MBL was non-significant short-term (MD=0.00mm; 95% CI −0.01–0.02; P=.75) but significantly higher for tilted vs axial implants at 3-year (MD=0.08mm; 95% CI 0.05–0.11; P<.00001) and long-term follow-up (MD=0.18mm; 95% CI 0.15–0.20; P<.00001); implant failure risk did not differ (RR=1.02; 95% CI 0.85–1.23; P=.81).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Umbrella review (meta-analysis of meta-analyses) pooling 8 SR+MA/MA studies specifically on axial vs tilted implant MBL and failure — a higher evidence tier than any single SR+MA on this topic.
- Time-stratifies MBL comparison into short-term / 3-year / long-term follow-up, showing the tilted-vs-axial MBL gap widens with time (NS → significant → more significant).
- Confirms implant failure risk is statistically equivalent between tilted and axial configurations (RR=1.02, NS), reinforcing that tilting for anatomical convenience does not add failure risk.

## 7. Glossary

- **MBL (Marginal Bone Loss)**: radiographic loss of crestal/marginal bone height around an implant over time, measured in mm.
- **MD (Mean Difference)**: difference in mean outcome value between two groups (here, tilted minus axial MBL in mm).
- **RR (Relative Risk)**: ratio of failure probability in the tilted group vs the axial group.
- **Umbrella review**: a systematic review that synthesizes multiple existing systematic reviews/meta-analyses on the same topic, sitting at the top of the evidence hierarchy.
- **Tilted implant**: an implant placed at an angulation (typically 17–45°) relative to the occlusal plane, often used in the All-on-4 concept to avoid anatomical structures (e.g., maxillary sinus, inferior alveolar nerve) and increase anteroposterior spread.
