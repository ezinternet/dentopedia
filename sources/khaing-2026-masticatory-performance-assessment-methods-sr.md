## Why Ingested

The occlusion cluster holds plenty of papers that *use* masticatory performance (MP) as an outcome — [[removable-partial-denture/liang-2015-effects-removable-dental-prostheses-masticatory]] reports 28–39% comminution deficits and 28–83% mixing-ability deficits in shortened dental arches — but nothing that says **how MP is measured, against what criterion, and with what measurement error**. That is the missing lower layer: it decides whether a 28% deficit or a "masticatory efficiency improved" claim is interpretable at all. This PROSPERO-registered, COSMIN-appraised SR of 40 studies (2000–2025) is the anchor for that layer: it supplies the test-family taxonomy, the criterion-validity map, per-family levels of evidence, and the blunt verdict that **no standardized, clinically validated MP test exists**.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251165166; PubMed, Cochrane Library, EBSCOhost + Google Scholar, Jan 2000–Sep 2025) of 40 studies appraising the measurement properties of masticatory performance (MP) assessment methods in adults ≥18 y, using the COSMIN risk-of-bias checklist and Terwee criteria for property ratings and levels of evidence.

Mixing ability tests using colour-changeable or two-coloured chewing gum showed the most consistent evidence (reliability and validity sufficient (+), levels of evidence limited to strong; e.g. Kamiyama ICC 0.86–0.94, r = 0.979 vs colorimeter; Schimmel ViewGum ICC 0.95–0.97), while comminution tests were heterogeneous (sieving/optical scanning unknown to strong; fuchsine beads reliability unknown; gummy jelly limited to strong), and measurement error was under-reported across every family because MIC/SDC were rarely stated.

The authors conclude that no standardized, clinically validated MP test exists and recommend gum-based mixing ability tests as the most clinically reliable current option, with comminution tests retained for research pending standardization of test foods, particle analysis and error thresholds.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **A measurement-properties appraisal of MP methods, not another method inventory.** Prior reviews either described colour-change chewing gum without a validated appraisal framework (Tarkowska et al.) or applied one before digital/smartphone methods entered the field (Elgestad Stjernfeldt et al.). This review applies COSMIN RoB + Terwee criteria to 40 studies through September 2025, including smartphone colour analysis and automated particle analysis.
- **Three-family taxonomy with explicit sub-families**: mixing ability (colour-changeable gum, two-coloured gum, two-coloured wax), comminution (sieving/optical scanning, fuchsine beads, gummy jelly), and other (odour sensor, occlusal-support index).
- **Per-family levels of evidence** (strong / moderate / limited / conflicting / unknown) rather than a single global verdict — the review's answer to "which MP test should I use" is family-specific.
- **Criterion-validity map**: every family is tabulated against the reference standard it was validated against (colorimeter, glucose extraction, β-carotene automation, sieving X50, photographic surface area, visual scales), making cross-family comparison possible for the first time in one place.
- **Names measurement error as the systemic failure**: MIC (minimal important change) and SDC (smallest detectable change) are rarely reported, so longitudinal change and intervention effects cannot be interpreted even with reliable instruments.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: systematic review of measurement properties; PRISMA 2020 reporting; PROSPERO CRD420251165166; no meta-analysis.
- **Sources**: MEDLINE via PubMed, Cochrane Library, EBSCOhost (Dentistry and Oral Sciences Source); Google Scholar for grey/forward searching; plus manual reference-list checking. Search window 1 Jan 2000 – 30 Sep 2025; last search 20 Oct 2025.
- **Eligibility**: full-length English articles evaluating measurement properties of **objective** MP methods in adults ≥18 y (no age-subgroup restriction). Excluded: animal/in-vitro studies; MP measured only subjectively (interview/self-report questionnaires); craniofacial-anomaly, neuromuscular or other non-masticatory populations; studies using MP only as a clinical outcome without appraising the instrument; reviews, protocols, conference abstracts, editorials, letters, case reports/series ≤10 participants.
- **Selection**: two independent reviewers at title/abstract and full-text stages; disagreement by discussion, then a third or fourth reviewer.
- **Appraisal (two layers)**:
  1. **COSMIN RoB checklist** — only the domains relevant to performance-based instruments: reliability, validity, measurement error, responsiveness. Each domain carries 5–18 items rated very good / adequate / doubtful / inadequate, and the **"worst score counts"** principle sets the domain score.
  2. **Terwee et al. (2007) quality criteria** — each property rated **positive (+)** / **indeterminate (?)** / **negative (−)** from the reported statistics (e.g. reliability + if ICC/weighted kappa ≥0.70 or Pearson r ≥0.80; measurement error + if MIC > SDC or MIC outside the limits of agreement; criterion validity + if the gold standard is convincing and r ≥0.70).
- **Evidence synthesis**: Terwee levels — **strong** (consistent findings, multiple good-quality studies, or ≥1 excellent study), **moderate** (consistent, multiple fair-quality, or ≥1 good), **limited** (one fair-quality study), **conflicting**, **unknown** (only poor-quality studies). Studies of the same method were synthesised considering methodological quality, number of studies and consistency.
- **Extraction fields** (Table 3): study objective, participants, test food/material, method, measurement properties evaluated.

**Representative measurement-property statistics (Table 4, verbatim values)**:

- Colour-changeable gum — Hama (smartphone colour analysis): "ICC ≥ 0.97, r ≥ 0.92 vs colorimeter, Bland-Altman LoA ±6 ΔE"; measurement error rated inadequate (−); LoE moderate (reliability) / limited (validity) / limited (measurement error).
- Colour-changeable gum — Yashiro (10-grade visual colour scale vs colorimeter): inter-rater k = 0.94, agreement with colorimeter k = 0.91.
- Colour-changeable gum — Kamiyama: "ICCs = 0.86-0.94, strong correlation (r = 0.979) between colour scale and colorimeter"; LoE strong (reliability).
- Colour-changeable gum — Carneiro (acid-base reaction gum, image colour analysis): "k > 0.8 intra-/inter-examiner"; a* inversely and b* directly correlated with X₅.
- Two-coloured gum — Schimmel (ViewGum VoH vs 5-point visual scale): "ViewGum (VoH) – ICC 0.95-0.97, agreement with visual scale (r = −0.97)".
- Two-coloured gum — Fankhauser (smartphone vs scanner): VoH "highly correlated (r > −0.96); small mean difference (−0.044 to −0.006)".
- Two-coloured gum — Buser (smartphone app vs scanner VoH): VoH decreased with chewing cycles (P < .0001), "R² = 0.80-0.83" agreement.
- Two-coloured gum — Halazonetis: intra-/inter-rater reliability "LoA < 0.02"; SDHue vs chewing cycles R² = 0.94, P < .01; LoE strong.
- Two-coloured gum — Weijenberg (Diffpix, fully automated): ICC = 0.714; no significant correlation with wax cube test (rₛ = 0.429).
- Two-coloured gum — Endo (SCMI vs OCMR): k = 0.42–0.90; "reliable at ≥ 20 strokes".
- Two-coloured wax — Van der Bilt: visual vs digital mixing index r = 0.95 (P < .001); intra/inter-rater k = 0.55–0.78; 20 chews recommended.
- Two-coloured wax — Sugiura: MAI correlated with peanut comminution r = −0.56 to −0.70 (P < .001) but **not** with gummy-jelly glucose (ns).
- Sieving/optical — Sanchez-Ayala: intra-examiner ICC = 0.89–0.98, inter-examiner ICC > 0.93 (P < .001), random error <1 mm; LoE strong.
- Sieving/optical — Eberhard (optical scanning vs sieving): x₅₀ "highly correlated (r = 0.919-1.0) with sieving".
- Sieving/optical — Sugimoto: image-measured area vs sieve weight r = 0.83; thresholds 0.10 / 1.62 gave sensitivity 0.86, specificity 0.90.
- Sieving/optical — Van der Bilt: multiple sieving X₅₀ = 3.32 ± 0.76 mm, R² = 0.16 vs single sieving R² = 0.14.
- Fuchsine beads — Carneiro: AUC = 0.744 (sensitivity 73%, specificity 70%); Sanchez-Ayala: ICC = 0.65–0.76, mean difference −31.8% vs the silicone-cube reference (P < .001).
- Gummy jelly — Aiyar: glucose extraction vs colour-changing gum R² = 0.965; glucose vs chewing duration R² = 0.711.
- Gummy jelly — Sano: repeated 20 s glucose-extraction ICC = 0.924–0.950.
- Gummy jelly — Murakami: glucose vs visual scoring k = 0.86; vs photographic surface area (ISA) r = 0.70; ISA cut-off 1687 mm² matched the 100 mg/dL glucose threshold.
- Gummy jelly — Nokubi: 10-stage visual scale vs glucose rₛ = 0.91–0.98; ICC 0.946–0.947.
- Gummy jelly — Igarashi: visual scoring vs fully automated β-carotene system r = 0.86–0.87; inter-rater ICC = 0.93.
- Other — Goto (odour sensor OMX-SR): odour intensity vs gummy-jelly glucose r = 0.68; LoE unknown.
- Other — Ikebe (Eichner index): occlusal force and MP decreased with fewer occlusal contacts; B2/B3 premolar-contact loss critical; LoE limited.

**Authors' verdict**: gum-based mixing ability tests (colour-changeable and two-coloured) are "currently recommended as the most clinically reliable MP assessment methods"; comminution tests "retain conceptual value" but methodological variation limits reproducibility and they are "better suited for research settings or structured screening programs than for routine clinical assessment at present"; odour-based and occlusal-index approaches "lack sufficient evidence … to be recommended as standalone MP assessment tools". Overall: "no standardized clinical test with established diagnostic accuracy is currently available."

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Stated by the authors**: substantial heterogeneity in test foods, chewing instructions, protocols, outcome measures and analytical thresholds prevented quantitative synthesis; participant characteristics (age, dentition, occlusal support) varied widely; differing study design/reporting quality weakens the evidence grading; English-only full-length restriction may have excluded relevant studies.
- **Inferred from the text**: (a) **measurement error is the weakest link** — MIC/SDC were rarely reported, so the "limited/unknown" error grades reflect reporting absence as much as instrument performance; (b) **responsiveness is named as an in-scope COSMIN domain but never synthesised** — no per-family result section and no conclusion statement grades it for any family; (c) **internal inconsistency in the two-coloured wax section** — the body text says one wax study reported measurement error with "acceptable levels of error, indicating indeterminate precision", while the synthesis states measurement error "is generally unknown, as none of the studies reported the MIC, SDC"; (d) the family counts sum to 44 for 40 studies because of overlapping multi-material studies, which the text does not flag; (e) no meta-analysis, so effect-size pooling of reliability coefficients is absent (defensible given heterogeneity, but it also means no pooled ICC per family).
- **Future work called for by the authors**: standardized test foods and particle-analysis protocols; explicit reporting of MIC and SDC; rigorous validation of digital/smartphone and automated image-based methods against established references.

## 6. Related Work

- **liang-2015** (SR of RDP effects on MP in shortened dental arches) — the outcome-side counterpart: it reports MP deficits (28–39%) and post-prosthetic changes (2–32% comminution improvement) measured by exactly the comminution (X50) and colour-change mixing tests appraised here. Khaing supplies the metrological caveat that Liang's pooled comparisons cannot fully absorb.
- **Elgestad Stjernfeldt et al. 54** — the prior COSMIN-framework SR on MP measurement properties, predating smartphone/automated image analysis; this review explicitly positions itself as its updated successor.
- **Tarkowska et al. 53** — overview of colour-changeable chewing gum methods without a validated appraisal framework.
- **Ikebe et al. 52 / Eichner index** — the occlusal-support branch of MP assessment, retained here as "other methods" with limited evidence.
- **Goto et al. 51** — odour-sensor MP assessment, the newest "other" method, currently unknown-level evidence.

## 7. Glossary

- **Masticatory performance (MP)**: the ability to comminute or mix a test food during chewing — the construct this review appraises.
- **Mixing ability**: capacity to knead and blend a test material (colour-changeable gum, two-coloured gum, two-coloured wax); quantified as colour mixing.
- **Comminution**: reduction of test-food particle size, quantified by sieving (X₅₀ median particle size), optical scanning, dye release (fuchsine beads), glucose elution (gummy jelly) or photographic surface area.
- **COSMIN RoB checklist**: consensus-based tool for rating the methodological quality of studies on measurement properties; here restricted to reliability, validity, measurement error, responsiveness, using a "worst score counts" rule.
- **Terwee criteria**: 2007 quality criteria that convert reported statistics into +/?/− ratings per measurement property and then into levels of evidence (strong / moderate / limited / conflicting / unknown).
- **MIC vs SDC**: minimal important change (the smallest change a patient perceives as worthwhile) vs smallest detectable change (the smallest change distinguishable from measurement error). MIC > SDC is the criterion for a positive measurement-error rating — and the value pair the field most often fails to report.
- **VoH (variance of hue)**: ViewGum-derived index of colour mixing in a two-coloured gum; lower VoH = better mixing (decreases with chewing cycles).
- **SA1–SA5 scale**: 5-point subjective assessment scale for degree of gum colour mixing / bolus comminution.
- **X₅₀**: median particle size from sieving — the classical comminution outcome.
- **ISA (increase in surface area)**: photographic image-analysis outcome for chewed gummy jelly; cut-off 1687 mm² corresponds to the 100 mg/dL glucose-concentration threshold.
- **fuchsine bead test**: capsule of dye-containing beads; dye released on crushing is quantified by UV–Vis spectrophotometry.
- **Eichner index**: classification of posterior occlusal support (A/B/C groups) used as an occlusal-support proxy for masticatory function.
