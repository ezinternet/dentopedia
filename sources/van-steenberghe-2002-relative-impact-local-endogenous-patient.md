## Why Ingested

Cited by [[implants/survival/krisam-2019-factors-affecting-the-early-failure]] as one of the references (10–18) behind its background statement that early failures account for about 2–6% of implants placed. Ingested to make that benchmark traceable and to extend the evidence base of [[overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]].

## One-line Summary

Consecutive cohort of 399 patients (1,263 Brånemark implants) at the Leuven periodontology department, 1995–1997: success up to abutment connection was 97.8%, with heavy smoking, chemotherapy plus poor bone quality, radiotherapy, limited bone volume and breach of asepsis as the relevant factors (abstract only).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Classic machined-surface benchmark: about 2.2% early failure to abutment stage.
- Examines a wide panel of systemic conditions (hypertension, osteoporosis, thyroid, chemotherapy, diabetes, Crohn disease).
- Identifies breach of sterility (claustrophobic patients) as a local early-failure factor.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Consecutive cohort of all 399 patients treated 1995–1997 at the Department of Periodontology, University Hospital, Catholic University Leuven; 1,263 Brånemark-system implants. Data: endogenous factors (hypertension, osteoporosis, hypo/hyperthyroidism, chemotherapy, diabetes I/II, Crohn disease), smoking, radiotherapy, reason for tooth loss, bone quality/quantity, breach of sterility. Outcome: implant failure up to abutment connection. Statistical methods not given in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Success to abutment stage 97.8%. Heavy smoking and chemotherapy plus poor bone quality increased failure; radiotherapy, limited bone volume and claustrophobia-related asepsis breaches were the most relevant local factors (effect sizes not in abstract).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Abstract only. Machined Brånemark implants of the 1990s limit transfer to modern rough surfaces. Statistical approach and clustering handling unknown.
