## Why Ingested

Largest long-term clinical dataset for posterior maxillary bicortical anchorage: 88 patients, 124 implants, median 7-year follow-up (range 6 months–13 years). Fills the long-term survival gap not covered by Ng 2018 (1–5 years) or Wolff 2022 (2 years). Uniquely includes nasal floor as second bicortical engagement target (not only sinus floor), and reports immediate loading outcomes — a clinically common but previously data-sparse scenario. Extends [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/ng-2018-bicortical-engagement-atrophic-posterior-maxillae]] and [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/wolff-2022-two-year-followup-bicortical-unicortical-ise-posterior-maxilla]].

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study, 88 patients (124 posterior maxillary implants), bicortical anchorage (sinus or nasal floor engagement) + immediate function; median 7-year follow-up (range 6 months–13 years), October 1999–November 2008.
Cumulative implant survival rate 94.2% at median 7 years; 4 implants lost in 4 complete-edentulous patients; prosthetic survival 100%; marginal bone level 1.56 mm (SD 0.87) at 5 years; 30/88 patients were heavy bruxers with high mechanical complication rates.
Bicortical anchorage with immediate function is viable at 7 years; bruxism is the dominant mechanical risk factor; biological complications occurred in 17/88 patients (19%).

## Key Contributions

- Largest follow-up dataset for bicortical anchorage in posterior maxilla (median 7 years)
- 94.2% cumulative survival — within acceptable implant survival norms
- Includes nasal floor engagement as second bicortical target (not only sinus floor)
- Immediate function protocol validated at long-term follow-up
- Identifies bruxism as primary mechanical complication driver (30/88 patients)
- Prosthetic survival 100% despite 4 implant losses

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective clinical study; single center (Malo Clinic, Lisbon)
- **Inclusion**: October 1999 – November 2008; posterior maxillary edentulism (partial or complete)
- **Bicortical target**: Sinus floor cortex OR nasal floor cortex (crestal + floor engagement)
- **Implant systems**: Nobel Biocare — MkII (n=1), MkIII (n=6), MkIV (n=18), NobelSpeedy Groovy (n=99)
- **Loading**: Immediate function (provisional restoration within 24–72 hours)
- **Outcomes**: Survival, marginal bone level (MBL), biological and mechanical complications
- **11 patients dropped out** (12.5%) — 88 initially, 77 evaluated at final follow-up

## Key Results

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Implant survival (cumulative, median 7y) | **94.2%** |
| Implants lost | 4 (in 4 complete-edentulous patients) |
| Prosthetic survival | **100%** |
| Marginal bone level (5-year) | 1.56 mm (SD 0.87) |
| Biological complications | 17 implants in 17 patients (19%) |
| Mechanical complications | 49/88 patients (56%) |
| Heavy bruxers with mechanical complications | 30/49 patients |

## Limitations

- Retrospective design; no control group (no unicortical comparison)
- Mixed patient population (partial vs complete edentulism)
- Mixed implant systems across cohort
- Abstract-only extraction; breakdown by tooth position (premolar vs molar) unavailable
- High mechanical complication rate likely reflects immediate function + bruxism, not bicortical technique
- 11 patient dropout (12.5%)

## Glossary

- **Bicortical anchorage**: Implant engages both the alveolar crest cortex AND either the sinus floor or nasal floor cortex
- **Immediate function**: Provisional restoration placed within 24–72 hours of implant insertion
- **Mechanical complications**: Crown fracture, screw loosening, abutment fracture — not implant loss
- **Biological complications**: Peri-implantitis, soft tissue inflammation, fistula
