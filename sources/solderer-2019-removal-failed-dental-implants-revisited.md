## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Clin Exp Dent Res 2019, University of Zurich), 34 studies screened from 3,997, Q&A format on why/how failed dental implants are removed plus reinsertion outcomes.

Counter-torque ratchet technique (CTRT) is least invasive and should be first choice; trephine burs most common but sacrifice bone; 4 mm remaining osseointegration is the threshold for CTRT-alone (>200 Ncm removal torque → add trephine); reinsertion in previously failed sites survives 71–100%.

Clinical implication: pick the least-traumatic technique to preserve bone for reimplantation and let expected defect morphology guide the choice — but evidence is largely case reports (low certainty), and zirconia removal data are scarce.

## 세줄요약

Narrative review (Clin Exp Dent Res 2019, 취리히대), 3,997편 중 34편 선별, Q&A 형식으로 실패 임플란트를 왜/어떻게 제거하는지 + 재식립 결과를 정리.

역토크 래칫 기법(Counter-Torque Ratchet Technique, CTRT)이 가장 덜 침습적이라 1차 선택; trephine bur는 가장 흔하지만 골을 희생; 잔존 골유착 4 mm가 CTRT-단독의 임계값(제거토크 >200 Ncm면 trephine 병용); 이전 실패 부위 재식립 생존율 71–100%.

임상적 의미: 재식립을 위한 골 보존을 위해 가장 덜 침습적인 기법을 고르고 예상 결손 형태로 방법을 결정 — 단 근거는 대부분 증례보고(낮은 확실성)이고 지르코니아 제거 데이터는 희박.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. Structures implant explantation as five clinical questions (when/why, how, material influence, bone fate, reinsertion).
2. Ranks removal techniques by invasiveness with quantitative torque/bone data, positioning **CTRT as first choice** over the more common trephine bur.
3. First review to address **zirconia** implant removal — flags CTRT as likely contraindicated (low fracture toughness).
4. Consolidates **reinsertion survival** evidence (71–100%) and the sizing/surface strategy for the replacement implant.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative Q&A review. Two reviewers screened titles/abstracts (κ = 0.65); senior author adjudicated. Inclusion: RCTs, case series, retrospective studies, case reports, systematic and narrative reviews. Exclusion: non-osseointegrated implants, non-screw implants, orthodontic implants, animal studies. Evidence tabulated by question; no meta-analysis or formal risk-of-bias appraisal.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Removal techniques (ranked least→most bone-destructive):**

| Technique | Key parameters | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Extraction set (elevator/forceps) | mobile / minimal residual BIC | simplest |
| Counter-torque ratchet (CTRT) | counterclockwise torque; internal > external connection | least traumatic → **first choice**; 4 mm residual osseointegration = CTRT-alone threshold |
| Reverse screw technique (RST) | screw + counter-torque | for fractured/damaged implants |
| Piezosurgery | 24,000–29,500 Hz, saline cooling | less traumatic than trephine; spares nerves/vessels; pacemaker caution |
| Laser (Er,Cr:YSGG) | hydrokinetic, no thermal damage | ~½ bone removed vs trephine but >2× slower; hemostasis good |
| Trephine bur | 1,200–1,500 rpm, max water cooling | most common but sacrifices bone; risk mandible fracture/osteomyelitis; CAD/CAM guide reduces invasiveness |
| Electrosurgery (thermo-explantation) | monopolar 15 s → CTRT at 30 N after 1 wk | experimental; osteonecrosis risk (bone necrosis ≥47 °C >1 min) |

**Torque data (Anitua, BTI kit):** 78/91 implants removed by CTRT alone; 139/158 non-mobile implants removed at 146 Ncm without burs; trephine indicated at removal torque >200 Ncm. Removal torque lowest for plasma-sprayed, highest for acid-etched+sandblasted surfaces.

**Failure epidemiology:** overall failure 6.36% (642/10,096), 49% in first year; peri-implantitis is the leading late-failure cause (81.9–82.9%); implant fracture 0.4–1%; malpositioning ~10%.

**Zirconia:** BIC and removal torque similar to titanium, but fracture toughness far lower (4–18 vs 77 MPa·m^0.5) → CTRT likely to fracture the implant; thermal method also questionable (zirconia insulates).

**Reinsertion:** survival 88.7% (previously failed sites), 85% (twice-failed); 2nd attempt 71–100%, 3rd attempt 50–100%. Prefer larger-diameter, rough-surface replacement for primary stability; sinus-grafted sites 92–100%; immediate vs delayed reimplantation equivalent given adequate bone and no infection.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Evidence base is dominated by case reports and small series; no RCTs comparing techniques.
- No literature quantifies bone remodeling/resorption after removal of an osseointegrated failing implant.
- Zirconia and electrosurgery data are anecdotal.
- Failure/removal-success definitions are non-standardized; RCTs and consensus outcomes are warranted.

## 7. Glossary

- **CTRT** — Counter-Torque Ratchet Technique: counterclockwise torque applied via abutment/tool to unscrew an osseointegrated implant with minimal bone damage.
- **RST** — Reverse Screw Technique: a screw is driven counterclockwise into a fractured/damaged implant to grip it, then counter-torque removes it.
- **Thermo-explantation** — inducing controlled thermal osteonecrosis (osseodisintegration) at the bone–implant interface to loosen an implant for low-torque removal.
- **BIC** — Bone-to-Implant Contact.
