## Why Ingested
This in-vitro study quantifies how insertion torque (IT) and ISQ correlate across varying bone densities, cortical thicknesses, and drill sizes — directly extending the evidence base in [[implants/isq/baldi-2018-insertion-torque-isq-tapered-knife-edge]] and [[implants/isq/lee-2024-primary-implant-stability-isq-devices-invitro]] by isolating bone-quality variables that explain why IT alone is an unreliable surrogate for primary stability.

## Three-line Summary
In-vitro study using polyurethane foam bone blocks (#10–#50 densities) with 5 experimental groups (n=20/group) testing IT and ISQ correlations across bone density, drill diameter, cortical thickness, and cancellous density variations.
Insertion torque and ISQ correlated strongly only in homogeneous bone (r=0.82, P<0.001); correlations weakened or diverged when cortical thickness, cancellous density, or drill diameter varied independently.
Clinical implication: primary stability is multifactorial — insertion torque alone cannot predict ISQ; bone quality (cortical thickness, cancellous density) and surgical protocol (drill size) independently modulate the IT–ISQ relationship.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First systematic in-vitro isolation of bone-quality variables (cortical thickness, cancellous density, homogeneous density) and drill protocol variables (final drill diameter) on IT–ISQ correlation
- Demonstrated that IT–ISQ correlation is context-dependent: strong in homogeneous bone (r=0.82) but weak/absent when cortical or cancellous compartments are independently varied
- Provided quantitative benchmarks for IT and ISQ across 5 clinically relevant scenarios (bone density gradient, drill size, cortical thickness, cancellous density, combined cortical-cancellous with drill variation)

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In-vitro experimental study (bench top / laboratory study)
- **n**: 20 implants per subgroup × 5 groups × multiple sub-conditions = 400+ total measurements
- **Outcomes**: Peak insertion torque (Ncm), ISQ (Osstell Mentor, 1–100 scale)
- **Specimens**: Sawbones polyurethane foam blocks (#10, #20, #30, #40 homogeneous; #50 cortical; short fiber-filled epoxy sheets for cortical layers 0.5/1.0/1.5/2.0 mm)
- **Implants**: Osstem SS II fixtures, 11.5 mm length × 4.1 mm diameter
- **Drilling**: 800 rpm, copious irrigation, final drill diameters Ø2.7–Ø3.8 mm per group
- **Statistics**: One-way ANOVA, Pearson correlation coefficient, P<0.05 significance

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Group | Variable Tested | IT Trend | ISQ Trend | IT–ISQ Correlation |
|-------|----------------|----------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Homogeneous bone density (#10→#40) | ↑ with density | ↑ with density | r=0.82, P<0.001 (strong +) |
| 2 | Final drill diameter (Ø2.7→Ø3.6 mm, #20 bone) | ↓ with larger drill | No consistent trend | Weak / non-significant |
| 3 | Cortical thickness (0→2.0 mm, cortical #50, cancellous #20) | ↑ with thickness | ↑ with thickness | Positive, significant |
| 4 | Cancellous density (#10→#30, cortical 1.5 mm #50 fixed) | ↑ with density | Weak / inconsistent | Weak |
| 5 | Final drill diameter (Ø3.0→Ø3.8 mm, cortical 1.5 mm #50, cancellous #20) | ↓ with larger drill | Weak / inconsistent | Weak |

Key numerical benchmarks (mean ± SD, n=20):
- Group 1 (#40 bone): IT ≈ 45–50 Ncm, ISQ ≈ 75–80
- Group 3 (2.0 mm cortical): IT ≈ 45–50 Ncm, ISQ ≈ 75–80
- Group 2 (Ø2.7 mm drill): IT ≈ 50 Ncm, ISQ ≈ 65–70
- Group 5 (Ø3.8 mm drill): IT ≈ 25 Ncm, ISQ ≈ 60–65

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- In-vitro polyurethane foam model does not replicate living bone biology (vascularization, remodeling, viscoelasticity)
- Single implant system (Osstem SS II), single length/diameter — generalizability to other macrogeometries unknown
- No dynamic loading or healing simulation — only immediate primary stability measured
- Statistical analysis limited to Pearson correlation; multivariate modeling of interacting variables not performed
- Sample size per cell (n=20) adequate for correlation but underpowered for interaction effects

## 6. Related Work
- **baldi-2018-insertion-torque-isq-tapered-knife-edge**: Clinical study confirming IT–ISQ correlation in tapered knife-edge implants; this paper extends by showing correlation breaks down with bone-quality heterogeneity
- **lee-2024-primary-implant-stability-isq-devices-invitro**: Compares ISQ devices; this paper provides the bone-quality mechanistic context for why device agreement varies by site
- **immediate-implant/primary-stability** category papers: Clinical primary stability thresholds (IT ≥35 Ncm, ISQ ≥70) should be interpreted with bone-quality caveats demonstrated here

## 7. Glossary
- **Insertion Torque (IT)**: Peak rotational force (Ncm) required to seat an implant during placement
- **Implant Stability Quotient (ISQ)**: Resonance frequency analysis (RFA) derived unit (1–100) reflecting implant stability
- **Primary Stability**: Mechanical stability at implant placement, before biological osseointegration
- **Cortical Bone**: Dense outer bone layer; simulated here by short fiber-filled epoxy sheets (#50 density)
- **Cancellous Bone**: Porous inner bone; simulated here by polyurethane foam (#10–#30 density)
- **Osstell Mentor**: RFA device using magnetic peg transducer to measure ISQ
