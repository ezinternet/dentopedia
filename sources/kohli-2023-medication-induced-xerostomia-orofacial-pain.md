## Why Ingested

기존 [[oral-medicine/salivary-chemosensory/ramirez-martinez-acitores-2020-antihypertensive-xerostomia-salivary-flow-sr]]는 약물 계열과 구강건조·타액유량의 연결이 약하다고 보고했다. 이 SR은 결과 축을 "구강건조 → 구강안면통증"으로 옮겨, 약물 유발 구강건조가 통증 증상과 동반되는지를 묻고 [[oral-medicine/salivary-chemosensory/kim-2023-xerostomia-cellular-targets]]의 기전 층위에 임상 증상 층위를 더한다. 구강안면통증과 구강건조 사이 유일한 SR로서 위키의 공백을 메운다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA, no meta-analysis) of 7 studies (n=1,029; 6 cross-sectional/case-control designs plus 1 randomized crossover trial, 2009-2022) on medication-induced xerostomia and orofacial pain.

The authors report a positive association between medication-induced xerostomia and orofacial pain (e.g., xerostomia inventory 7.14 vs 5.04 in craniofacial pain vs controls, p<0.001; OR 1.04 in dementia residents), but no association between salivary flow measurements and medication use.

Evidence is low-level (one RCT, no xerostomia-free controls, recall-based self-report, no drug dose/duration data), so the conclusions should be used with prudence.

## 세줄요약

7편(n=1,029; 횡단·환자-대조 연구와 무작위 교차시험 1편, 2009-2022)을 다룬 체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review, SR)로, 약물 유발 구강건조증 (Xerostomia)과 구강안면통증 (Orofacial Pain)의 연관성을 봤다 (메타분석 없음).

약물 유발 구강건조증과 구강안면통증 사이에 양의 연관성이 보고됐다 (구강건조 설문 점수 7.14 vs 5.04, p<0.001; 치매 거주자 OR 1.04). 반면 타액 분비량 (Salivary Flow) 측정과 약물 사용 사이에는 연관성이 없었다.

근거 수준이 낮다: RCT 1편, 구강건조 없는 대조군 부재, 환자 회상 기반 자가보고, 약물 용량·기간 미보고. 결론은 신중히 해석해야 한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Self-described as the first review summarizing the association between xerostomia and orofacial pain in medicated patients.
- Finds a positive association between medication-induced xerostomia and orofacial pain; finds no association between salivary flow measures and medication use.
- Identifies gaps: standardized xerostomia assessment, saliva flow measurement, pain diagnosis in medical history, drug dose/duration.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- PRISMA-reported SR; PICO: medicated patients, xerostomia-causing drugs, xerostomia vs no xerostomia, orofacial pain outcome.
- Databases: WoS, PubMed, SCOPUS, MEDLINE (Ovid); English only; Sjogren's and cancer excluded by search; studies exclusively on burning mouth syndrome excluded.
- Two-phase screening by two reviewers (third and fourth reviewer for disputes); dual data extraction; Newcastle-Ottawa Scale planned; narrative synthesis.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 7 studies, 1,029 participants, mean ages 43 to 100 years; cross-sectional, case-control, and one randomized crossover trial.
- Michalak 2022: mirror-sliding dryness linked to higher oral pain VAS (p=0.018) and more medications (p=0.014).
- Van de Rijt 2020: orofacial pain 48.8% (OPS-NVI) in dementia; xerostomia predictor OR 1.04 (p<0.05).
- Yuen 2009 (spinal cord injury): oral pain 42%, xerostomia ~60%; pain positively associated with dry mouth.
- da Silva 2011: salivary flow 0.125 vs 0.175 g/min in orofacial pain vs controls (p=0.008); amitriptyline 35%, antihypertensives 16%.
- de Siqueira 2021: xerostomia inventory 7.14 vs 5.04 (p<0.001); 62.6% of craniofacial pain patients reported xerostomia.
- Barbe 2018: stimulated flow 0.3 +/- 0.2 and 0.4 +/- 0.2 ml/min; Beker 2019: xerostomia 18%, oral pain 12%, xerostomia linked to medication (p=0.001).
- Four of seven studies reported medications; antidepressants listed in all four.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No study had a control group without xerostomia; only one RCT; risk of bias could not be assessed.
- Recall-based self-report; heterogeneous, non-comparable xerostomia and pain scales; only two studies measured salivary flow.
- Drug dose and duration absent; xerogenic and non-xerogenic drugs pooled.
- Future: saliva flow measurement, standardized xerostomia assessment, recorded orofacial pain diagnosis.

## 7. Glossary

- Xerostomia: subjective feeling of oral dryness.
- Hyposalivation: objectively decreased salivation; unstimulated rate <0.3 ml/min.
- Orofacial pain: pain in the face and/or oral cavity (IASP definition).
- OPS-NVI: Orofacial Pain Scale for Non-Verbal Individuals.
- Anticholinergic drugs: block muscarinic receptors, reducing saliva secretion.
