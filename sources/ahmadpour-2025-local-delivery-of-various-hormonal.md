## Why Ingested

The "locally delivered drug **plus** bone graft to drive osteogenesis (골형성)" strategy had no page in this wiki — the two closest held pages each cover a single agent: a statin adjunct to guided bone regeneration (GBR, 유도골재생) in [[wiki/bone-regeneration/tale-2026-simvastatin-guided-bone-regeneration-sr]], and parathyroid hormone (PTH) supplementation for implant osseointegration (골유착) in [[wiki/implants/survival/kindaro-2026-parathyroid-hormone-implant-osseointegration-osteoporosis-sr]]. This SR is the only cross-hormone synthesis the wiki holds for the local-delivery-plus-graft frame (PTH · growth hormone (GH) · calcitonin), and it is the page that can calibrate how far the "local hormonal drug + bone graft" concept may be asserted — its answer is a preclinical vote count (22 animal/in vitro studies), which is exactly the boundary a reader needs stated in one place. It also names intelligent drug delivery as the live frontier that [[wiki/bone-regeneration/haugen-2026-bone-grafts-everything-you-need]] only lists as a future direction for graft materials.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA-guided PubMed search, 1970 – December 2023) of 22 studies — **all** of them animal in vivo (생체내) or in vitro (생체외) experiments — on local delivery of parathyroid hormone (PTH), growth hormone (GH) and calcitonin together with bone grafts (이식골) to improve osteogenesis; no human study was included.

Vote-count synthesis (proportion of studies with better outcomes than controls): **87.5%** of GH studies, **66.7%** of PTH studies and **60%** of calcitonin studies reported superior outcomes versus their control groups.

The authors conclude that locally administered hormonal drugs may positively affect osteogenesis in vivo and in vitro — most clearly PTH in surgical bone defect (골결손) healing and peri-implant (주위임플란트) response, and GH in peri-implant bone response — but because every included study is preclinical and the result is a study-level vote count rather than a pooled effect estimate, no clinical protocol is supported.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review, SR; PRISMA 지침, PubMed 1970년–2023년 12월 검색): 이식골 (Bone Graft) 과 병용한 국소전달 (Local Delivery) 호르몬 약제 — 부갑상선호르몬 (Parathyroid Hormone, PTH), 성장호르몬 (Growth Hormone, GH), 칼시토닌 (Calcitonin) — 의 골형성 (Osteogenesis) 효과를 다룬 22편의 연구. 포함된 연구는 **전부** 동물 생체내 (In Vivo) 또는 생체외 (In Vitro) 실험이며, 사람 연구는 없다.

투표 합산 (Vote Count, 대조군 대비 우월한 결과를 보고한 연구의 비율): 성장호르몬 (GH) 사용 연구의 **87.5%**, 부갑상선호르몬 (PTH) 사용 연구의 **66.7%**, 칼시토닌 (Calcitonin) 사용 연구의 **60%**가 대조군보다 양호한 결과를 보고.

저자들은 국소 투여 호르몬 약제가 생체내·생체외에서 골형성 (Osteogenesis) 에 긍정적으로 작용할 수 있다고 결론내리되 — 특히 수술적 골결손 (Bone Defect) 치유와 주위임플란트 (Peri-Implant) 반응에서는 PTH, 주위임플란트 골반응 (Peri-Implant Bone Response) 에서는 GH — 근거 22편이 전부 전임상 연구이고 결과가 통합 효과량 (Pooled Effect Estimate) 이 아닌 연구별 투표 합산이므로 임상 프로토콜을 뒷받침하지 않는다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **The only cross-hormone synthesis of the "local hormonal drug + bone graft" strategy held by this wiki.** The wiki holds one page for a statin adjunct to GBR ([[wiki/bone-regeneration/tale-2026-simvastatin-guided-bone-regeneration-sr]]) and one for PTH and implant osseointegration ([[wiki/implants/survival/kindaro-2026-parathyroid-hormone-implant-osseointegration-osteoporosis-sr]]); this page is the first that puts PTH, GH and calcitonin side by side against one another.
- **Keeps the co-administration frame visible.** The review's unit of analysis is not "a drug" but "a hormone delivered locally **along with** a bone graft to improve osteogenesis" — i.e. a graft-plus-pharmacologic-adjunct protocol, the same architecture as the statin/GBR page.
- **Provides a ranked direction-of-effect map for three hormones** (GH > PTH > calcitonin by proportion of studies favouring the hormone arm), which is the only cross-drug ordering this wiki can currently offer on this axis.
- **Names the two clinical target sites the authors privilege**: surgical bone defect healing and peri-implant bone response (PTH), and peri-implant bone response (GH).
- **Stands as an explicit evidence-vacuum marker.** The authors' own framing is hedged ("may positively affect", "may stimulate"), and every included study is animal or in vitro — the page therefore documents *where the field is*, not a therapy that can be applied.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design:** systematic review; the authors state they followed Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines.
- **Database and window:** PubMed only; **1970 to December 2023**.
- **Search:** described as a "structured search" designed to identify both **in vivo** and **in vitro** studies.
- **Screening:** articles were screened on **title and abstract**; **22 studies** were included after screening.
- **Eligibility content (as reported):** every included study was an animal in vivo study or an in vitro study, and all of them used **parathyroid hormone (PTH)**, **growth hormone (GH)** or **calcitonin**, delivered locally together with bone grafts, against a control group.
- **Synthesis:** narrative/vote-count reporting of the proportion of studies with better outcomes than controls, per hormone. **No meta-analysis is reported** — no pooled effect estimate, no confidence interval, no heterogeneity statistic (I²), no funnel plot.
- **Not reported in the abstract:** full search string, whether other databases or grey literature were searched, language restrictions, PRISMA flow counts (records retrieved, duplicates removed, full texts assessed, exclusion reasons), protocol registration (PROSPERO), risk-of-bias / quality appraisal tool (e.g. SYRCLE for animal studies), per-hormone denominators, animal species and models, drug doses, carriers or delivery vehicles, grafting materials, outcome definitions, and follow-up durations.
- **Institutions/affiliations** are given for all seven authors (Tabriz, Iran; see §1).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Hormone delivered locally with bone graft | Studies reporting better/superior outcomes vs control (vote count) | Where the authors locate the signal |
|---|---|---|
| Growth hormone (GH) | **87.5%** | peri-implant bone response |
| Parathyroid hormone (PTH) | **66.7%** | healing of surgical bone defects and peri-implant response |
| Calcitonin | **60%** | not further specified in the abstract |

- Total included studies: **22**, all animal in vivo or in vitro.
- Authors' conclusion (abstract, verbatim in substance): locally administered hormonal drugs "may positively affect osteogenesis in vivo and in vitro"; this is "especially true for PTH in the healing of surgical bone defects and peri-implant response and for GH in peri-implant bone response"; and they "may stimulate bone healing in surgical bone defects and enhance peri-implant bone response."
- **How to read the percentages.** These are study-level vote counts, not pooled effect estimates: the review counts how many studies in each hormone group beat their control, and does not pool magnitudes, variances or follow-up times. The abstract does not disclose the per-hormone denominators. The 87.5% figure is arithmetically consistent with 7 of 8 studies (and 66.7% with 2 of 3 or 4 of 6; 60% with 3 of 5) — this is an inference from the arithmetic, not a reported count, and the counts themselves are not stated.
- **No clinical outcomes.** No human participants, no implant survival, no ISQ (임플란트 안정성 지수, Implant Stability Quotient), no histomorphometric or radiographic figures, no safety or adverse-event data are reported in the abstract.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only ingestion.** Full text not retrieved (closed access; no OA copy via PMC, Unpaywall or OpenAlex). Search strategy, study list, per-study outcomes, risk-of-bias appraisal, dosages, carriers and follow-up are all unavailable here.
- **Vote counting instead of pooling.** The headline numbers (87.5% / 66.7% / 60%) are proportions of studies, not effect sizes. A study with a trivial and a study with a large effect count identically, so the percentages carry no magnitude information, no variance, and no pooling justification; heterogeneity across models, carriers and endpoints is not addressed at all.
- **Undisclosed denominators.** Per-hormone study counts are not given, so the precision of each percentage (and the number of studies that were null) cannot be judged.
- **No risk-of-bias reporting.** The abstract mentions PRISMA for the review process but no quality appraisal of the included animal/in vitro work (SYRCLE-type tool, or equivalent), so the vote counts are unweighted by study quality.
- **Preclinical-only.** Every included study was animal in vivo or in vitro. There is no human evidence, no clinical endpoint, and no safety data — the review's "may stimulate bone healing" statement is hypothesis-generating.
- **Single database.** Only PubMed is reported for a 1970–2023 window; Embase, Cochrane, Scopus and grey literature are not mentioned, so the recall of this review is unverifiable.
- **Indication-level vagueness.** "Surgical bone defects" and "peri-implant response" are named without model, defect size, graft type or dosing, so the review cannot yet tell a clinician whether the signal survives in a given protocol.
- **Future work implied by the gap:** randomized, blinded, adequately powered animal studies with standardised carriers and dosing; risk-of-bias assessment; quantitative pooling where outcome definitions allow; and — the missing step — human trials (e.g. hormone-loaded graft at defect or implant sites) with clinical and implant-level endpoints.

## 7. Glossary

- **Parathyroid hormone, PTH (부갑상선호르몬)** — anabolic hormone; the review's most-discussed agent for surgical bone defect healing and peri-implant response when delivered locally with a graft.
- **Growth hormone, GH (성장호르몬)** — the agent with the highest proportion of favourable studies (87.5%); the authors locate its signal in peri-implant bone response.
- **Calcitonin (칼시토닌)** — the third agent examined (60% favourable); osteoclast-inhibiting hormone, no indication-specific claim attached in the abstract.
- **Osteogenesis (골형성)** — new bone formation, the review's target outcome.
- **Local delivery (국소전달)** — placing the hormone at the graft/defect/implant site rather than systemically, so that local concentration is achieved with a small total dose.
- **Bone graft / bone graft substitute (이식골)** — the graft material with which the hormonal drug is co-administered; the review's protocol is always "hormone **plus** graft", never hormone alone.
- **Surgical bone defect (수술적 골결손)** — a surgically created defect in bone (e.g. a peri-implant dehiscence or a critical-size defect), healed with graft + local hormone.
- **Peri-implant response (주위임플란트 반응)** — bone formation and remodelling around an implant fixture; one of the two clinical target sites the review names.
- **In vivo / in vitro (생체내 / 생체외)** — the two study settings the review deliberately combined; no human (clinical) study was eligible or included.
- **Vote counting (투표 합산)** — synthesis by counting how many studies favoured the intervention, without pooling effect magnitudes; the review's only quantitative method as reported.
