## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Immediate Full-Arch Maxillary Rehabilitation Supported by Four Implants: A Retrospective Study with 15 to 20 Years of Follow-Up
- **Authors**: Miguel de Araújo Nobre, Armando Lopes, Ana Ferro, Carlos Moura Guedes, Ricardo Almeida, Mariana Nunes, Miguel Gouveia, Diogo Santos, Inês Vitor
- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Medicine, 2026;15(2), doi:10.3390/jcm15020446
- **PMID**: 41598384 | **PMC**: PMC12842169 (open access)
- **Institution**: MALO CLINIC, Lisbon, Portugal
- **Study design**: Retrospective observational cohort (STROBE guidelines)
- **Ethics**: Ethical Committee for Health, Authorization nº 002/2025

## 2. Key Contributions

- Longest published follow-up (up to 20 years) for immediate full-arch maxillary All-on-4 with the largest sample (n=740 patients, 2960 implants).
- Establishes implant CSurR 90.7% and CSucR 84.6% at 20 years for maxillary All-on-4 immediate loading.
- Documents stable long-term MBL trajectory: 1.07 mm @15yr → 1.46 mm @20yr.
- Identifies smoking (HR=1.93) and biological complications (HR=2.18) as independent risk indicators for implant failure via multivariate Cox regression.
- Demonstrates that mechanical complications act as a paradoxical MBL protector (OR=0.26) through the "pseudo-maintenance" mechanism of increased appointment frequency.
- Quantifies very long-term complication burden: mechanical 78.5%, biological 35.1% (patient level).

## 3. Methodology

- **Population**: 740 patients (440F, 300M; mean age 55.3yr, range 20–88yr); 2960 implants (NobelSpeedy Groovy n=2851, Mk IV n=92, Mk III n=17); treatment Nov 2002–Dec 2008
- **Inclusion**: edentulous maxilla or hopeless teeth; immediate function candidates
- **Exclusion**: active chemotherapy/radiotherapy; posterior healthy teeth with sufficient bone for partial restoration
- **Protocol**: 2 axial anterior implants + 2 distally tilted posterior implants (30–45° to occlusal plane); 30° angled MUAs on posteriors; provisional acrylic day of surgery; definitive prosthesis ≥6 months postoperative
- **Implant surfaces**: anodically oxidized (TiUnite)
- **Primary outcomes**: implant CSurR/CSucR (Buser et al. criteria), prosthesis success
- **Secondary outcomes**: MBL (periapical radiograph, parallel technique, image analysis software iRYS v13), mechanical/biological complications
- **Statistics**: Kaplan-Meier life tables, Cox proportional hazards, binary logistic regression; SPSS v26
- **Lost to follow-up**: 287/740 patients (38.8%); deceased 42, unreachable 246

## 4. Key Results

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Implant CSurR @15yr | 94.1% |
| **Implant CSurR @20yr** | **90.7%** |
| Implant CSucR @20yr | 84.6% |
| **Prosthesis success** | **98.1%** |
| MBL @15yr | 1.07 ± 1.38 mm |
| **MBL @20yr** | **1.46 ± 1.56 mm** |
| Mechanical complications (patient level) | 78.5% |
| Biological complications (patient level) | 35.1% |
| Biological complications (implant level) | 15.2% |

**Risk indicators for implant failure (Cox, multivariate)**:
- Smoking: HR = 1.93 (p<0.001)
- Biological complications: HR = 2.18 (p<0.001)

**Risk indicators for MBL > 3mm (logistic, multivariate)**:
- Smoking: OR = 3.60
- Biological complications: OR = 3.70
- Mechanical complications: OR = 0.26 (protective)

**Risk indicators for biological complications (logistic, multivariate)**:
- Age (younger at baseline = higher risk): OR = 0.48
- Smoking: OR = 1.78

**Sensitivity analysis**: best case (all LTF retained) 93.1%; worst case (all LTF failed) 59.4% at 20yr.

## 5. Limitations

- Single center (MALO CLINIC); retrospective design; reduces external validity
- 38.8% lost to follow-up; 26% and 7% ineligible radiographs for MBL at 15yr and 20yr respectively (underestimation possible)
- No control group (all patients treated with same protocol)
- Multivariate models lacked history of periodontitis and bacterial plaque — potential confounders
- Deceased patients (42) likely underestimated given average age 55yr + 20yr follow-up crossing COVID-19 pandemic

## 6. Related Work

- Previous 13-year publication from same group: 1072 patients, 99.2% prosthesis CSurR, 94.7% implant CSurR — current study uses a different cohort (n=740, treatment 2002–2008) and extends the horizon
- Compared to two-stage protocol studies with 20–25yr follow-up: implant survival 71.05–99.2% (range reflects varied inclusion criteria and sample sizes)
