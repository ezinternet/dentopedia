## Why Ingested

치과에서 마주치는 실신은 대부분 성인 혈관미주신경성 실신 (Vasovagal Syncope, VVS)이지만, 소아·청소년 환자에서는 기립성 빈맥 증후군 (Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome, POTS)·기립성 저혈압 (Orthostatic Hypotension, OH)·앉은자세 빈맥 증후군 (Sitting Tachycardia Syndrome, STS) 등 감별 진단군이 넓고 심장성 실신 (Cardiac Syncope, CS)이라는 치명적 감별이 숨어 있다. 본 2024 중국소아심장학회 지침은 이 감별을 수치 기준으로 정리해, 의자 옆에서 "그냥 미주신경성"이라 단정해도 되는 경우와 "치료하지 말고 의뢰"해야 하는 경우를 가르는 기준을 제공한다. 성인 시술 중 실신 대응을 다룬 [[drug/systemic-disease/malave-2022-vasovagal-reactions-during-interventional-pain]]의 소아·진단 쪽 보강 근거.

## Three-line Summary

Consensus guideline (Chinese Pediatric Cardiology Society et al., revised 2024, literature search to April 2024) covering the etiology classification, diagnostic procedures, postural tests, and individualized treatment of neurally mediated syncope (NMS) in children and adolescents. NMS accounts for 70%–80% of pediatric syncope (VVS and POTS ≈95% of NMS), cardiac syncope 2%–3%, and unexplained syncope about 20%; diagnosis rests on history plus active standing test, head-up tilt test (HUTT), or active sitting test with numeric hemodynamic cutoffs (e.g. POTS: HR rise ≥40 bpm or upright HR ≥130 bpm at 6–12 y / ≥125 bpm at 12–18 y). Treatment is a ladder — education and trigger avoidance, autonomic function exercise, water 30–50 mL/kg/day plus salt/oral rehydration salts for ≥2 months, then midodrine 1.25–2.5 mg/dose or metoprolol 0.5 mg/kg/day only for recurrent syncope with injury risk after non-pharmacological failure.

## 세줄요약

중국소아심장학회 등이 2024년 개정한 합의 지침(문헌 검색 2024년 4월까지)으로, 소아·청소년 미주신경성 실신 (Neurally Mediated Syncope, NMS)의 원인 분류·진단 절차·체위 검사·개별화 치료를 다룬다. 소아 실신의 70%–80%가 NMS(그중 혈관미주신경성 실신 (VVS)와 기립성 빈맥 증후군 (POTS)이 약 95%), 심장성 실신 (CS) 2%–3%, 원인불명 약 20%이며, 진단은 병력 + 능동 기립 검사·경두개 경사 검사 (Head-Up Tilt Test, HUTT)·앉은자세 검사와 수치 기준(POTS: 심박수 증가 ≥40 bpm 또는 기립 심박수 6–12세 ≥130 bpm / 12–18세 ≥125 bpm)으로 확정한다. 치료는 교육·유발인자 회피 → 자율신경 기능 훈련 → 수분 30–50 mL/kg/day + 염분/경구수분보충염 (Oral Rehydration Salts, ORS) 2개월 이상 → (재발·손상 위험·비약물 치료 실패 시에만) 미도드린 1.25–2.5 mg/회 또는 메토프롤롤 0.5 mg/kg/day 순의 사다리 구조다.

## 1. Document Information

- **Journal**: World Journal of Pediatrics 2024;20(10):983–1002
- **DOI**: 10.1007/s12519-024-00819-w
- **PMID / PMCID**: 39110332 / PMC11502568
- **Institution**: Chinese Pediatric Cardiology Society, Chinese Pediatric Society, Chinese Medical Association; Committee of Pediatric Syncope, College of Pediatricians, Chinese Medical Doctor Association (China)
- **Document type**: Revised clinical practice guideline (2018 Edition Guidelines → 2024 revision); each recommendation carries a class (I / IIa / IIb) and evidence level (A / B / C)
- **Scope**: Children and adolescents with syncope / orthostatic intolerance (not adults, not a dental document)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Consolidated etiologic classification of pediatric syncope into NMS (VVS vasoinhibitory/cardioinhibitory/mixed, POTS, OH, OHT, STS, SHT, situational syncope, carotid sinus syndrome), cardiac syncope, and unexplained syncope.
- Numeric diagnostic cutoffs for each orthostatic syndrome derived from active standing test / HUTT / active sitting test, including the newly introduced sitting tachycardia syndrome (STS) and sitting hypertension (SHT).
- Three-step diagnostic pathway (definite / suggestive / unexplained) that reserves cardiac work-up (Holter, ILR, exercise test, electrophysiology, imaging, genetic testing) for the suggestive group.
- Treatment ladder with explicit non-pharmacological first line (education, trigger avoidance, counter-pressure maneuvers, tilt training, dry-towel wiping, water/salt/ORS), and drug therapy restricted to recurrent syncope with injury risk and failed non-drug therapy.
- Biomarker tables (Table 4 for VVS, Table 5 for POTS) intended to individualize which non-pharmacological or drug option is likely to work.
- Explicit comorbidity emphasis: 30%–40% of children with VVS and/or POTS also have allergic disease; migraine, mental/sleep disorders, hyperventilation syndrome, chronic fatigue, joint hypermobility, and gastrointestinal disorders are common.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Expert consensus guideline revision — the expert groups collected clinical evidence, evaluated the preliminary recommendations of the 2018 edition, then held open-ended discussions to form recommendations.
- **Data sources**: PubMed, Cochrane, EMBASE, China Biomedical Database, Chinese Journal Full-text Database, searched to April 2024; search terms "syncope", "children", "adolescents", "diagnosis", "treatment".
- **Grading**: Classes of recommendation (I, IIa, IIb) and levels of evidence (A, B, C), applied per statement.
- **Diagnostic architecture (Fig. 1)**: Step 1 — history, physical examination, supine and upright BP, supine and upright ECG → triage into definite / suggestive / unexplained. Step 2 — targeted cardiac testing for the suggestive group. Step 3 — postural tests (active standing test, HUTT, active sitting test) for the unexplained group; re-evaluation with neurologist/psychiatrist input if still unresolved.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Etiology shares**: NMS 70%–80%; cardiac syncope 2%–3%; unexplained ~20%. Within NMS, VVS + POTS ≈95%. Incidence of syncope in children/adolescents 17.37%, higher in girls; 1%–2% of emergency department visits.

**Positive-response criteria**

| Test / syndrome | Criterion (verbatim values from guideline) |
|---|---|
| VVS (HUTT) | Syncope or presyncope plus any of: SBP ≤80 mmHg or DBP ≤50 mmHg or mean pressure decrease ≥25%; HR <75 bpm (4–6 y), <65 bpm (>6–8 y), <60 bpm (>8 y); sinus arrest or junctional escape; II/III degree AV block or cardiac arrest ≥3 s |
| Malignant VVS | Cardiac arrest >3 s during a syncope episode or the test |
| POTS | During upright phase of active standing test or first 10 min of HUTT: HR rise ≥40 bpm, or maximum upright HR ≥130 bpm (6–12 y) / ≥125 bpm (12–18 y); must exclude SBP fall >20 mmHg and/or DBP fall >10 mmHg. Test in the morning (circadian effect) |
| OH (classic) | Within first 3 min of standing/HUTT: SBP fall ≥20 mmHg and/or DBP fall ≥10 mmHg; initial OH = transient fall within 15 s of standing |
| OHT | Within first 3 min: SBP rise ≥20 mmHg and/or DBP rise ≥25 mmHg (6–12 y) or ≥20 mmHg (12–18 y); or upright BP ≥130/90 mmHg (6–12 y) / ≥140/90 mmHg (12–18 y) without obvious HR change |

**POTS risk factors (IIa; A/B)** — supine HR +10 bpm → 1.58× risk; water intake <800 mL/day → 3.88×; sleep <8 h/day → 5.91×. **OHT risk factors** — BMI >85th percentile 6.07×, BMI >95th percentile 7.48×, water <800 mL/day 4.03×; each extra hour of sleep −74.3%.

**Treatment (VVS)**
- Health education: trigger avoidance (prolonged standing, rapid position change, sudden stop after prolonged exercise, crowded/stuffy rooms, emotional stress including "painful stimuli or a medical operation"), presyncope recognition, physical counter-pressure maneuvers (knee bending, abdominal/limb contraction, hand clasp, elbow flexion, leg crossing, toe dorsiflexion), psychological support.
- Tilt training: standing 15 cm from a wall, from 5 min twice daily up to 30 min per session, supervised.
- Dry towel wiping: forearms and both legs, 5 min per part, twice daily.
- Water: up to 30–50 mL/kg/day (not more than 1500–1700 mL), targeting clear urine color. Salt/ORS for at least 2 months then re-evaluate. ORS preferred for VVS vasoinhibitory type; **not recommended** with hypertension, kidney disease, or heart failure.
- Drug therapy indication: recurrent syncope (>2 episodes per 6 months or >3 per year), with injury risk, poor response to non-pharmacological therapy.
  - Midodrine hydrochloride: 1.25–2.5 mg/time, once or twice daily; after 2–4 weeks up to 2.5 mg/time three times daily; monitor supine BP; contraindicated if baseline BP above the 95th percentile for age/sex or drug allergy.
  - Metoprolol: 0.5 mg/kg/day orally twice daily, up to no more than 2 mg/kg/day or maximum adult daily dose; contraindicated in significant sinus bradycardia, II/III degree AV block, bronchial asthma, drug allergy.
  - Fludrocortisone may reduce VVS events; sertraline experience limited (only when other drugs fail, monitor side effects).
- Pacemaker: considered for recurrent syncope with prolonged cardiac arrest (>4 s) or survivors of cardiopulmonary resuscitation, on pediatric cardiovascular specialist recommendation.

**POTS**: same autonomic exercise protocol; ≥8 h sleep/day; aerobic exercise 1–2 h/day at least 5 days/week, build-up ≥3 months; midodrine generally more effective than metoprolol or ORS, best effect at 3 months (extending to 6 months adds little); correct iron/vitamin deficiencies; treat comorbidities.

**OH**: non-pharmacological first (trigger avoidance, water/salt, counter-pressure maneuvers, elastic stockings), then midodrine or fludrocortisone. **OHT**: non-pharmacological only — no drug is recommended, but watch for primary hypertension in late adolescence/adulthood.

**Follow-up**: 1–3 months after diagnosis/treatment, then symptom-driven. Symptom control (not a negative HUTT) is the efficacy endpoint; POTS efficacy = orthostatic intolerance symptom score drop ≥2 points. 86% of POTS patients symptom-remitted 5 years after diagnosis.

**Situational syncope**: triggers listed include micturition, defecation, bathing, swallowing, cough, post-dinner, singing, **teeth brushing**, and hair grooming.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Consensus/expert-panel document, not a systematic review with meta-analysis; recommendation grades rest partly on expert discussion (many statements are IIa; B or I; C).
- Pediatric and Chinese-population scope — no adult data, and the diagnostic thresholds are age-banded (4–6, 6–12, 12–18 y), so they cannot be transplanted to adult dental patients.
- The document never discusses dental settings, chair position, local anesthetics with vasoconstrictors, or chairside rescue drugs; any dental framing on the wiki page is inference, not guideline content.
- HUTT and active sitting test require equipment and a protocoled environment; the dentist cannot perform them — they are referral endpoints.
- Biomarker tables (Tables 4–5) are prediction-oriented and largely single-center; the guideline itself does not validate them prospectively.
- Comorbidity burden (allergy, migraine, sleep, psychiatric, hypermobility) complicates both diagnosis and treatment and is handled only in general terms.

## 7. Glossary

- **NMS (Neurally Mediated Syncope)**: 실신을 일으키는 반사성 자율신경 조절 이상군; 소아 실신의 70%–80%.
- **VVS (Vasovagal Syncope)**: 혈관미주신경성 실신. 혈관억제형 (VVS-VI), 심억제형 (VVS-CI), 혼합형 (VVS-M)으로 세분.
- **POTS (Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome)**: 기립 시 심박수 이상 상승(≥40 bpm 또는 최대 ≥130/125 bpm) + 기립 불내성 증상.
- **OH / OHT (Orthostatic Hypotension / Hypertension)**: 기립 3분 내 수축기 ≥20 mmHg 하강(또는 이완기 ≥10) / 상승.
- **STS / SHT (Sitting Tachycardia Syndrome / Sitting Hypertension)**: 2024 개정에서 도입된, 앉은 자세 변화로 유발되는 빈맥/고혈압형.
- **HUTT (Head-Up Tilt Test)**: 경두개 경사 검사. NMS 확진 및 혈역학적 아형 결정.
- **Active standing test / Active sitting test**: 능동 기립 검사 / 앉은자세 검사.
- **ORS (Oral Rehydration Salts)**: 경구수분보충염. 고혈압·신장질환·심부전에서는 금기.
- **TLOC (Transient Loss of Consciousness)**: 일과성 의식소실. 실신 외 간질·대사·중독·심리적 가성실신 (PPS)도 포함하므로 감별 필요.
