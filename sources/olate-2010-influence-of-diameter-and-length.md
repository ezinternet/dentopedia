## One-line Summary

Retrospective study of 1,649 submerged implants in 650 patients: early survival 96.2%, with the highest early loss in short (6–9 mm) implants (9.9%) and an association of early loss with short length and anterior placement, but not with diameter or bone quality (abstract only).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Length-stratified early loss: short 9.9% vs medium 3.0% vs long 3.4%.
- No significant diameter or bone-quality effect on early loss.
- Unusually, anterior sites showed more early loss (4.3%) than posterior (2.8%).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Retrospective study of cylindrical acid-etched submerged implants from three manufacturers; 1,649 implants (807 maxillary, 821 mandibular as reported) in 650 patients, placed 1996–2004. Early failure assessed between placement and second-stage reopening. Length: short 6–9, medium 10–12, long 13–18 mm; diameter narrow/regular/wide. Statistics: stepwise and chi-squared analyses, univariate and multivariate logistic regression (SAS).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Early survival 96.2% (50 failures). Diameter: narrow 5.1%, regular 3.8%, wide 2.7%. Length: short 9.9%, medium 3.0%, long 3.4%. Anterior 4.3% (31) vs posterior 2.8% (19). Short implants (P=.0018) and anterior installation (P=.0013) associated with early loss; no association with bone quality or diameter.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Abstract only. Implant-level analysis without stated clustering control. Three implant systems and an 8-year span with changing indications; authors note learning-curve confounding.
