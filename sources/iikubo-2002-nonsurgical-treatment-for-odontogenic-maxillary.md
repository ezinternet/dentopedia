## Three-line Summary

Single-patient preliminary case report (25-year-old woman, left maxillary second molar with apical periodontitis and acute odontogenic maxillary sinusitis, OMS, refractory to 6 weeks of root-canal treatment plus antibiotics; Tohoku University Dental Hospital).

One session of saline irrigation (15 ml total, 3 x 5 ml, 23 G needle) through the root canal of the causal tooth, followed by 15 ml air, abolished the cheek pain, nasal obstruction and retrorhinorrhoea by 2 days; CT at 1 week showed near-disappearance of mucosal thickening and marked improvement of ostiomeatal unit obstruction, complete resolution at 6 months, and no recurrence or side effect at 12 months.

Transdental irrigation may open the ostiomeatal unit without tooth extraction or surgery in antibiotic- and endodontic-refractory OMS, but this is n=1 with concurrent antibiotics/endodontic therapy and no control, so efficacy cannot be separated from natural resolution.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Proposes irrigation of the maxillary sinus through the root canal of the causal tooth as a nonsurgical treatment for periapical-disease-induced OMS refractory to conservative treatment.
- Argues the method preserves the ostiomeatal unit (OMU), in contrast to functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS), which the authors call destructive to the OMU, and avoids the tooth loss of extraction.
- Describes a safety protocol: thorough canal cleaning and shaping first, apical length measured with an electronic apex locator, needle not protruded beyond the apex, air injected only after saline flowed without resistance.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: single-patient case report, preliminary; informed consent obtained for the irrigation.
- **Patient**: 25-year-old woman, non-contributory medical history; left upper second molar (crowned, root-filled about 5 years earlier) with percussion pain, periapical radiolucency and widened periodontal space; 3 days of worsening toothache, 1 day of spontaneous left cheek pain, temperature 37 degrees C, nasal obstruction, retrorhinorrhoea with fetid odor.
- **Imaging**: periapical, panoramic, posteroanterior and Waters films (left maxillary sinus opacified); axial CT showed near-homogeneous low-density filling with an air-fluid level and left anterior ethmoid involvement; coronal CT showed total obstruction of the left OMU.
- **Initial conservative therapy**: conventional root-canal treatment of the second molar, aminopenicillin chosen after antibiogram, clarithromycin (macrolide) plus pronase (anti-inflammatory enzyme), antipyretic analgesics.
- **Intervention**: syringe with 23 G irrigation needle inserted to the canal apex (length by Root ZX apex locator), 5 ml physiological saline x 3 (15 ml total) through the canal into the sinus without resistance, one session only; then 15 ml air injected into the sinus; cavity sealed with zinc oxide eugenol cement; medication resumed.
- **Follow-up**: clinical course, CT at 1 week and 6 months, 12 months symptom follow-up. After the irrigation the canal was obturated and macrolide plus pronase given preventively for 2 months.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- After initial endodontic therapy: slight fever, malaise and spontaneous pain resolved within 1 week, but oppressive cheek pain, nasal obstruction and retrorhinorrhoea persisted after 6 weeks; CT showed no significant interval change except improvement of anterior ethmoid opacification.
- During irrigation the patient felt fluid pass into the nasal cavity and nasopharynx with a fetid odor, then had serous odorless retrorhinorrhoea until the next day.
- All symptoms (cheek pain, nasal obstruction, retrorhinorrhoea) completely disappeared by 2 days after irrigation.
- CT at 1 week: previous maxillary mucosal thickening almost disappeared, OMU obstruction greatly improved, slight mucosal thickening remained on the sinus floor.
- CT at 6 months: maxillary mucosal thickening completely disappeared.
- No recurrence in the 12 months since irrigation; no side effect associated with saline irrigation.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Single case, no control; the symptom relief coincided with ongoing antibiotics (macrolide, aminopenicillin) and endodontic therapy, so the effect of irrigation alone cannot be isolated (inferred).
- Authors call it a preliminary report and the conclusion rests on one patient with 12 months of follow-up; no objective ostium patency or microbiology data are reported after irrigation.
- Feasibility depends on loss of any barrier at the apex through inflammatory bone and mucosa destruction (authors' reasoning), so it is not expected to apply to teeth without apical communication with the sinus (inferred).
- Authors identify debris or bacteria being pushed into the sinus and air embolism/emphysema from air injection as the main risks; neither occurred but no safety conclusion is possible from n=1.

## 7. Glossary
- **Odontogenic maxillary sinusitis (OMS)**: maxillary sinusitis caused by dental disease, mainly periapical inflammation of maxillary molars.
- **Ostiomeatal unit (OMU)**: the drainage and ventilation pathway of the maxillary sinus into the middle meatus (ostium, infundibulum, hiatus semilunaris).
- **FESS**: functional endoscopic sinus surgery, improving ventilation by removing tissue at the OMU.
- **Retrorhinorrhoea**: flow of nasal secretion backward into the nasopharynx (postnasal drip).
- **Pronase**: proteolytic enzyme anti-inflammatory agent used in Japan for sinusitis.
- **Root ZX**: electronic apex locator (Morita) used to measure canal length.
