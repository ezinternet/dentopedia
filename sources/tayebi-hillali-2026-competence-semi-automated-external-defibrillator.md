## Why Ingested

Simulation-based OSCE study documenting objectively measured AED competence deficits in final-year dental students, with a controlled two-phase intervention comparing conventional vs modified voice instructions — first paper to quantify the impact of instruction optimization on time-to-shock delivery in a dental education setting.

→ [[drug/medical-emergencies/tayebi-hillali-2026-competence-semi-automated-external-defibrillator]]

## Three-line Summary

Final-year dental students at the University of Santiago de Compostela showed critically deficient AED competence under conventional voice instructions (Phase 1, n=42): 8/10 checklist items were completed by <10% of students, and median time to shock delivery was 143 s — well above the accepted 90 s threshold. Modified 80-second instructions (Phase 2, n=39) yielded significant improvement in 7/10 items and reduced shock time to 116 s (−27 s, p<0.001), but post-shock CPR resumption remained poor in both phases (9.5% vs 7.6%, p=1.000). The authors conclude that optimized voice instructions improve task performance but cannot substitute for structured AED training integrated into the dental curriculum.

## 세줄요약

스페인 산티아고 데 콤포스텔라 대학교 치과 최종학년을 대상으로 한 시뮬레이션 기반 평가 (Simulation-Based Assessment)에서 기존 음성안내 조건(Phase 1, n=42)의 반자동 외부제세동기 (Semi-Automated External Defibrillator, AED) 실기 역량은 심각하게 부족했다: 10개 항목 중 8개에서 이행률 <10%, 제세동 (Defibrillation) 전달 중앙값 143초(허용 기준 90초 초과). 수정 음성안내(80초, Phase 2, n=39)는 7개 항목에서 유의한 개선과 쇼크 시간 116초(-27초, p<0.001)를 달성했으나, 쇼크 후 심폐소생술 (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation, CPR) 재개는 양 단계 모두 극히 저조(9.5% vs 7.6%)했다. 저자들은 최적화된 음성안내가 수행 능력을 향상시키나 정규 교육과정 내 구조화된 AED 훈련을 대체할 수 없다고 결론지었다.

## Document Information

- **Study design**: Two-phase simulation-based assessment (OSCE format), prospective cohort
- **Setting**: Faculty of Dentistry, University of Santiago de Compostela, Spain
- **Population**: Final-year undergraduate dental students
- **Phase 1**: 2023–2024 academic year, n=42 (33F/9M); conventional 22-s AED voice instructions
- **Phase 2**: 2024–2025 academic year, n=39 (31F/8M); modified 80-s instructions developed from Phase 1 error analysis
- **Equipment**: Philips HeartStartFRx training AED; Laerdal manikin with realistic impedances (wet T-shirt, metallic necklace)
- **Ethics**: Approved by Bioethics Committee, University of Santiago de Compostela

## Key Contributions

1. **Objective measurement of AED competence gap**: First study to use OSCE-style assessment (rather than surveys) to quantify actual AED performance deficits in dental students, finding 8/10 items completed by <10% under standard device instructions.
2. **Instruction optimization as intervention**: Demonstrated that extending and clarifying voice instructions (22 s → 80 s) significantly reduced shock delivery time by 27 s (p<0.001) across 7/10 checklist items.
3. **Irreducible post-shock gap**: Post-shock CPR resumption failed to improve with either instruction set (9.5% → 7.6%, p=1.000), identifying a gap that requires active training beyond better device instructions.
4. **European context**: Quantifies that ~0.003 cardiac arrests occur per dentist per year, but >90% could be reversed with defibrillation within 1 minute — highlighting the catastrophic cost of the 143 s baseline.
5. **Curriculum evidence**: >85% of Phase 1 students had never received university-led BLS/AED training; prior self-directed training (14.2%) did not confer competence — supporting mandatory curriculum integration.

## Methodology

**Assessment tool (10 items, 3 domains)**:

| Domain | Item |
|---|---|
| Preparation (4) | Turn on AED; Check battery; Follow verbal instructions; Connect electrodes to monitor |
| Electrode placement & shock (4) | Place electrodes on bare dry chest; Remove rescuers during analysis; Ensure no contact before shock; Deliver shock when indicated |
| Post-shock actions (2) | Immediately resume CPR; Reevaluate patient at 2 minutes |

**Competence criterion**: 100% of critical steps completed correctly.

**Phase 1**: Students received standard Philips HeartStartFRx voice prompts (22 s). All errors catalogued.

**Phase 2**: Modified 80-s instructions developed from Phase 1 error analysis. Same scenario, same equipment, new cohort.

**Statistical analysis**: Chi-square / Fisher's exact for proportions; Mann-Whitney U for time-to-shock (non-parametric, non-normal distribution).

## Key Results

| Item | Phase 1 (n=42) | Phase 2 (n=39) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power On | 4.7% | 100% | <0.001 |
| Check Battery | 0% | 61.5% | <0.001 |
| Follow Instructions | 4.7% | 97.4% | <0.001 |
| Electrode Connection to Monitor | 80.9% | 100% | 0.006 |
| Electrode Placement on Patient | 7.1% | 87.1% | <0.001 |
| Remove Rescuers During Analysis | 14.2% | 87.1% | <0.001 |
| No Contact Before Shock | 7.1% | 46.1% | <0.001 |
| Deliver Shock | 59.5% | 76.9% | 0.150 |
| **Resume CPR (post-shock)** | **9.5%** | **7.6%** | **1.000** |
| Reevaluation at 2 min | 9.5% | 0% | 0.117 |
| **Median time to shock (s)** | **143 (95%CI 129–156)** | **116 (95%CI 107–124)** | **<0.001** |

**Context figures**:
- Europe: 350,000 out-of-hospital cardiac arrests/year; survival <10%
- Dental practice incidence: ~0.003 cardiac arrest events per dentist per year
- Accepted threshold for shock delivery: ≤90 s (ILCOR); ideal <60 s
- ILCOR refresher recommendation: every 12–24 months, simulation/manikin-based

## Limitations

- Convenience sample from single institution; no sample size calculation
- Marked sex imbalance (~1 male per 5 students), limiting sex-stratified analysis
- Simulation duration may have limited valid assessment of 2-minute reevaluation item
- AED model-specific results (Philips HeartStartFRx); generalizability to other devices uncertain
- Different cohorts across phases preclude within-subject comparison
- No long-term retention assessment

## Related Work

- Breuer et al. — referenced for corroborating Phase 1 time data (time from opening defibrillator to rhythm analysis ~1 min)
- Multiple European surveys (Belgium, Poland, Italy, Greece): ~half of dentists had not received specific undergraduate BLS/AED training
- ILCOR guidelines: periodic simulation-based refresher every 12–24 months

## Glossary

- **AED / Semi-automated external defibrillator (반자동 외부제세동기)**: Device that analyzes cardiac rhythm and delivers shock upon user command; requires operator to press "shock" button (vs fully automated)
- **OSCE (Objective Structured Clinical Examination)**: Standardized competency assessment using simulated scenarios with predefined checklists
- **Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (병원 외 심정지)**: Cardiac arrest occurring outside hospital setting; survival <10% in Europe without rapid defibrillation
- **ILCOR**: International Liaison Committee on Resuscitation — sets global CPR/AED guidelines
