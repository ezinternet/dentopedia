## Why Ingested

First network meta-analysis (NMA) comparing 33 probiotic interventions as adjuncts to NSPT in periodontitis, pooling 1290 patients and identifying Lactobacillus reuteri as the top performer — provides a definitive evidence hierarchy for probiotic selection in clinical practice, extending [[periodontics/kwon-2021-current-concepts-management-periodontitis]] and the growing host-modulation adjunct evidence base in this category.

## Three-line Summary

Network meta-analysis of 33 RCTs (1290 patients) comparing probiotic strains as adjuncts to professional mechanical plaque removal (PMPR) in periodontitis; Lactobacillus-based interventions showed the most consistent benefits across PPD, CAL, and BOP outcomes.

Short-term PPD gains reached up to MD=1.48 mm (95% CI 1.24–1.72) with SRP+Lactobacillus combinations, and CAL gains up to 1.05 mm; long-term benefits were narrower, with Lactobacillus reuteri retaining significance at MD=0.80 mm PPD reduction (95% CI 0.30–1.29) beyond 3 months.

Evidence certainty remains very low (92% of comparisons) due to star-network topology relying on indirect evidence and high heterogeneity in probiotic strains/routes/durations — results must be interpreted cautiously but point toward L. reuteri as the best-supported adjunct probiotic.

## 세줄요약

33편 RCT(1290명)에 대한 네트워크 메타분석으로 비외과적 치주치료(PMPR) 보조제로서 다양한 프로바이오틱스 균주를 비교; Lactobacillus 기반 중재가 PPD·CAL·BOP 전반에서 가장 일관된 효과를 보였다.

단기 PPD 개선은 SRP+Lactobacillus 조합에서 최대 MD=1.48 mm(95% CI 1.24–1.72)에 달했고, CAL은 최대 1.05 mm 획득; 장기(>3개월)는 Lactobacillus reuteri만 PPD MD=0.80 mm(95% CI 0.30–1.29) 유의성 유지.

근거 확실성은 92%가 매우 낮음(간접 증거 의존 스타 네트워크, 높은 이질성) — 결과는 신중하게 해석해야 하나 L. reuteri가 가장 근거 있는 보조 프로바이오틱스임을 시사.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Probiotics in the non-surgical treatment of periodontitis: a systematic review and network meta-analysis
- **Authors**: Mendonça CD, Mata ADSPD, Azevedo LFR, Marques JF, Silveira JML, Marques DNDS
- **Journal**: BMC Oral Health. 2024;24(1):1224
- **PMID**: 39407177 | **PMCID**: PMC11481756 | **DOI**: 10.1186/s12903-024-05027-6
- **Protocol**: PROSPERO CRD42021250678
- **Study type**: Systematic review + network meta-analysis (frequentist weighted least squares)
- **Full text**: PMC open access

## 2. Key Contributions

- First NMA comparing diverse probiotic proposals for periodontitis — 33 RCTs vs prior reviews with ≤647 patients.
- Identifies Lactobacillus (especially L. reuteri) as top performer across PPD, CAL, and BOP in both short- and long-term.
- Demonstrates that probiotic therapy duration ≤1 month is sufficient — no additional benefit beyond 1 month for long-term PPD.
- Confirms that combining SRP with probiotics is safe — no adverse effects in any included study.

## 3. Methodology

- **Search**: MEDLINE, LILACS, Cochrane CENTRAL + grey literature to November 2023
- **Inclusion**: Adults ≥18 years, systemically healthy, untreated periodontitis, RCTs with ≥10 patients, ≥1 month follow-up
- **Exclusion**: Split-mouth designs, systemic disease (HIV/AIDS, diabetes), PST patients, children/elderly
- **Data synthesis**: NMA with frequentist random-effects model; results stratified by ≤3 months (short-term) and >3 months (long-term)
- **Consistency**: SIDE (local) + global incoherence tests — no inconsistency found (0%)
- **Confidence**: CINeMA framework

## 4. Key Results

**PPD (short-term ≤3 months)**
- 9 probiotic interventions significantly better than Splac: MD range 0.18–1.48 mm
- SRP+L. reuteri: MD=1.48 mm (95% CI 1.24–1.72, p<0.0001)

**PPD (long-term >3 months)**
- Only Lactobacillus retained significance: MD=0.80 mm (95% CI 0.30–1.29, p=0.0016)
- Sensitivity (≤1 month therapy): L. reuteri MD=1.16 mm (95% CI 1.06–1.25, p<0.0001), I²=0%

**CAL (short-term)**
- 16 interventions significant: MD range 0.16–1.05 mm
- SRP+Lactobacillus+multi: MD=1.05 mm (95% CI 1.03–1.07, p<0.0001)

**CAL (long-term)**
- 2 interventions significant: SRP+L. reuteri MD=0.43 mm (95% CI 0.24–0.62, p<0.0001)

**BOP (short-term)**: 4 combinations significant (MD 13.26–33.00%)
**BOP (long-term)**: 4 combinations significant (MD 5.02–23.31%)

**Ranking**: L. reuteri = best probiotic across all outcomes (P-score near 1.0 for PPD and BOP)

**Certainty**: Very low for 92% of comparisons due to indirect evidence, heterogeneity, risk of bias

## 5. Limitations

- Star-network topology with 9% direct evidence (PPD) and 8.92% (CAL) — heavy reliance on indirect comparisons
- High heterogeneity for some outcomes (PPD long-term, BOP short-term) despite sensitivity analysis
- No standardized probiotic administration route — L. reuteri dominance may reflect pharma funding bias
- Excluded split-mouth designs, potentially missing important evidence
- Very low certainty evidence overall

## 6. Related Work

- Prior SR by Donos et al. (2020): probiotics showed non-significant 0.38 mm PPD benefit
- Li et al. SR: MD=-0.60 mm PPD (similar to this study's findings)
- This NMA supersedes prior reviews by pool size (1290 vs 647) and comparative method

## 7. Glossary

- **NMA**: Network meta-analysis — allows simultaneous comparison of multiple interventions
- **P-score**: Surface under the cumulative ranking curve equivalent in frequentist NMA; higher = better ranked
- **PPD/PD**: Probing pocket depth
- **CAL**: Clinical attachment level gain
- **BOP**: Bleeding on probing
- **PMPR**: Professional mechanical plaque removal (= SRP/subgingival instrumentation)
- **Splac**: SRP + placebo (control arm)
- **CINeMA**: Confidence in Network Meta-Analysis framework (certainty grading)
