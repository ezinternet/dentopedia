## Why Ingested

위키는 교합접촉의 *기록 방법*(교합지·T-Scan·IOS)과 *시간에 따른 변화*([[occlusion/mao-2024-occlusal-changes-implant-supported-single-crowns]], [[occlusion/assoratgoon-2025-occlusal-contact-changes-implant-supported-prostheses]])는 다루지만, **합착(cementation) 그 자체가 교합접촉을 얼마나 바꾸는지**를 정량한 페이지는 없었다 — 즉 "합착 후 교합 재검사"라는 통상 권고의 정량적 근거가 비어 있었다. 본 in-vitro 연구(Wang 2024, n=30 크라운, 70 μm vs 120 μm 시멘트 스페이서)는 합착 후 접촉면적이 약 2.7배로 증가하고 접촉강도도 증가함을 p<0.001로 보고해, 교합접촉 양상을 설계 변수로 다루는 [[occlusion/packaeser-2025-core-material-occlusal-contact-fatigue-ceramic-crowns]]의 전제(설계된 접촉양상이 납품 시점까지 유지된다)를 정량적으로 한정(refines)한다.

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro bench study (Dentistry Journal 2024; Harvard School of Dental Medicine) — 30 identical zirconia crowns (Katana, 3Shape CAD-CAM) on 3D-printed casts of a prepared right maxillary first molar, mounted in maximum intercuspation, split into 70 μm (n=15) and 120 μm (n=15) cement-space groups.

Occlusal contact area more than doubled after cementation in both groups (70 μm: 2339 ± 1206 → 6281 ± 3310 μm²; 120 μm: 2071 ± 909 → 5545 ± 3491 μm²; both p<0.001), and contact intensity (grayscale) rose by 0–30 units in 74% of 70 μm samples and 57% of 120 μm samples.

Cementation reliably increases occlusal contact area and intensity and can shift contacts from point to area contact (occasionally from central fossa onto cusp inclines), so a thorough occlusal re-evaluation after cementation is mandatory — and the smaller cement space carries the higher risk of hyperocclusion.

## 세줄요약

In-vitro 벤치 연구(Dentistry Journal 2024; 하버드 치과대학) — 상악 우측 제1대구치 지대치를 3D 프린팅한 석고모형에 동일 설계 지르코니아 크라운(Katana, 3Shape CAD-CAM) 30개를 제작, 최대 교합 접촉(Maximum Intercuspation, MIP)으로 교합기에 장착하고 시멘트 스페이서(Cement Space) 70 μm군(n=15)과 120 μm군(n=15)으로 나눠 비교했다.

합착 후 교합접촉 면적이 두 군 모두 2배 이상 증가(70 μm: 2339 ± 1206 → 6281 ± 3310 μm²; 120 μm: 2071 ± 909 → 5545 ± 3491 μm²; 모두 p<0.001)했고, 접촉강도(그레이스케일)는 70 μm군 74%, 120 μm군 57%에서 0–30 단위 증가했다.

합착은 교합접촉 면적·강도를 일관되게 증가시키고 접촉을 점접촉에서 면접촉으로(때로는 중심와에서 교두 경사면으로) 이동시킬 수 있으므로 합착 후 교합 재평가가 필수이며, 시멘트 스페이서가 작을수록 과교합(Hyperocclusion) 위험이 크다.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **Quantifies the cementation step itself as an occlusal perturbation.** Pre-cementation interproximal and occlusal adjustments were made so each crown *maintained the existing occlusion* at MIP — so the pre→post difference isolates the effect of cementation, not of design or adjustment.
2. **Effect size on contact area**: both groups more than doubled contact area after cementation (see §4), all p<0.001, rejecting the null hypothesis of no difference.
3. **Cement space modulates the failure mode, not the direction.** Both spacers increased area; the *smaller* (70 μm) spacer produced the higher proportion of intensity (hyperocclusion) increases and more contact-area shift, while the *larger* (120 μm) spacer is framed as translating into a physical shift of the prescribed occlusion.
4. **Method**: PVS bite registration + calibrated DC light box + grayscale camera + ImageJ 1.54 to convert contact thickness into area (μm²) and intensity (grayscale) — a low-cost quantitative alternative to articulating paper or T-Scan.
5. **Clinical claim**: post-cementation occlusal evaluation is strongly recommended; post-cementation grinding damages the cemented zirconia surface (fragile crown, roughened surface), so proper planning and surface finishing matter.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Master model**: Columbia Dentoform maxillary right first molar prepared for an all-ceramic crown (horizontal preparation, chamfer finish line, Brasseler diamond burs); scanned with a desktop scanner (3Shape E3).
- **Cast fabrication**: digital impression used to 3D-print 30 identical sets of casts (SprintRay Pro S); each mounted in MIP on a semi-adjustable articulator (Artex CR, Amann Girrbach) with Type III stone, by a single operator.
- **Crown fabrication**: one design in 3Shape Dental System v2.102.1.0; 30 zirconia crowns (Katana, Kuraray Noritake) milled (Roland DWX-52D 5-axis); cement spacer applied to all intaglio surfaces including the marginal gap. **15 crowns at 70 μm, 15 at 120 μm**; each crown randomly assigned to a mounted cast.
- **Pre-cementation standardization**: interproximal and occlusal adjustments to confirm seat and maintain existing MIP occlusion; a thermoplastic insertion guide molded under 5 kg static load to reproduce insertion orientation.
- **Records**: PVS (Regisil 2X, Dentsply Sirona) triple-tray record under 5 kg static load before and after cementation; cementation with self-adhesive universal resin cement (RelyX Unicem, 3M) on the articulator under 5 kg load with the guide in place.
- **Measurement**: PVS records photographed on a calibrated DC light box (Schott) with a grayscale camera (CFW-1308M, Scion) → light-intensity map; contact area (μm²) selected from highest-intensity areas within the occlusal table; ImageJ v1.54; contact intensity read as peak grayscale value.
- **Statistics**: paired-sample t-tests within each group, α = 0.05. Changes in *position* of the occlusal table were not evaluated (pre/post scans could not be compared positionally).
- **n**: 30 crowns / 30 cast sets; 15 per group.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Contact surface area (Table 1, mean ± SD, μm²)**

| Group | Pre-cementation | Post-cementation | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| 70 μm (n=15) | 2339 ± 1206 | 6281 ± 3310 | <0.001 |
| 120 μm (n=15) | 2071 ± 909 | 5545 ± 3491 | <0.001 |

- Authors' framing: post-cementation mean contact area "more than doubled" vs pre-cementation in both groups (arithmetically ≈2.7× in each group).
- Both groups rejected the null hypothesis; cementation induced a coronal shift of the restoration, bringing more area into occlusion.

**Contact intensity change (Table 2 — % of samples per grayscale-increase bin)**

| Group | 0–10 | 10–20 | 20–30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 70 μm | 47% | 7% | 20% |
| 120 μm | 33% | 17% | 7% |

- Remaining samples showed no significant change in light intensity. Summed across bins: 74% of 70 μm samples and 57% of 120 μm samples showed some measurable intensity increase (arithmetic sum of the three reported bins).
- The 70 μm group had the higher proportion of samples with increased intensity → higher hyperocclusion risk with the smaller cement space.

**Pattern change (qualitative, reported in Discussion)**: many samples in both groups shifted from **point contact to area contact** after cementation; in extreme cases contacts migrated off the main functional area (central fossa) onto **cusp inclines** — a pattern that can destabilize occlusion and, if unadjusted, promote wear, microfractures, or restoration failure.

**Note on "number of contacts"**: the study quantified contact *area* and *intensity*, not contact counts — no per-crown contact count is reported in the text, so none is recorded here.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **In-vitro/typodont design**: no masticatory musculature or TMJ contribution; absolute values are not directly transferable to patients.
- **Insertion guide** method (thermoplastic guide, 5 kg static load) is less common clinically than simpler insertion (e.g., cotton roll), limiting generalizability.
- **Amplified variability**: the inherent cement space of the milled crowns was larger than the planned spacer difference and "overshadowed" it, inflating standard deviations in both groups (visible in the large post-cementation SDs).
- **No adjacent-tooth control**; occlusal table positional shift was not evaluated.
- Inter-group comparison was not the inferential target — statistics were paired within-group t-tests only, so the 70 vs 120 μm contrast is descriptive, not tested.
- Future work proposed: optimal cement-space setting for digital crown fabrication; patient-specific factors via electronic/optical jaw tracking.
- No discrepancy found between the ingest briefing and the paper's own numbers (all values transcribed from the PMC full text / Tables 1–2).

## 7. Glossary

- **Cement space / cement spacer (시멘트 스페이서)** — the designed internal gap (μm) between crown intaglio and abutment, accommodating cement; set in CAD software (recommended minimum ~50 μm for marginal adaptation).
- **MIP (Maximum Intercuspation, 최대 교합 접촉)** — the position of maximal occlusal contact; mounting/recording reference here.
- **Hyperocclusion (과교합)** — locally excessive contact intensity after insertion; risk of occlusal trauma if unadjusted.
- **Contact area (μm²)** — area of highest transmitted light through the PVS record within the occlusal table; proxy for contact surface.
- **Contact intensity (grayscale)** — peak grayscale value on the light-box scan; thinner PVS = more light = higher intensity = heavier contact.
- **Insertion guide** — thermoplastic guide molded on the occlusal surface under 5 kg load to reproduce the insertion path during cementation.
