## One-line Summary
Scoping review (10 studies, 2000–2020) of communication tools in esthetic dentistry: all studies found tools — verbal, visual (mock-up), or digital (2D/3D smile design, AR) — positively impacted patient satisfaction, treatment acceptance, or SDM quality.

## 한줄요약

## 2. Key Contributions
- First scoping review specifically on communication tools in esthetic dentistry and their impact on patient satisfaction
- All 10 included studies showed positive effect of communication tools on ≥1 outcome (satisfaction, acceptance, retention, SDM quality)
- Identified four tool categories: verbal, written, visual/mock-up, digital (2D/3D design, AR)
- Established that digital tools (smile simulation) improve patient understanding before treatment starts, reducing post-treatment dissatisfaction
- Identified gap: no standardized communication tools for SDM in dentistry exist, preventing cross-study comparison

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Design: Scoping review following PRISMA-ScR extension
- Databases: Medline, Embase, Cochrane, Web of Science (January 2000 – March 2020)
- Search: 6,678 records identified; 53 full-texts examined; 10 studies included
- Inclusion: clinical studies, human subjects, esthetic dentistry, patient communication, patient satisfaction, English, ≥10 participants
- Exclusion: case reports, non-esthetic dentistry, non-communication focus
- Data extraction: 13 categories per study (design, specialty, country, setting, patient demographics, tools used, satisfaction assessment method)
- Studies split by satisfaction assessment: structured questionnaire (Likert/yes-no) vs. semi-structured questionnaire (qualitative)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- All 10 included studies found communication tools positively impacted ≥1 outcome
- Outcomes improved: patient satisfaction, patient-dentist relationship, information retention, treatment acceptance, quality of care, treatment outcome
- Specialties covered: prosthodontics (3), orthodontics (3), orthognathic surgery (2), mixed (2)
- Digital tools (2D/3D smile simulation): improved pre-treatment expectation alignment and post-treatment satisfaction across all studies using them
- Mock-up (analog visual tool): similarly improved satisfaction and informed consent comprehension
- No study reported negative outcomes from enhanced communication

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Heterogeneous study designs prevented quantitative meta-analysis
- Short search window (2000–2020); rapidly evolving digital tools may not be captured
- Risk of bias not assessed (scoping review methodology)
- No standardized outcome measures across studies
- All studies had relatively small samples (n: 26–250)
- Future: develop validated, standardized SDM communication tools for dental fields

## 7. Glossary
- SDM: Shared Decision Making — collaborative process where clinician and patient jointly determine treatment
- PDA: Patient Decision Aid — tool supporting SDM (written, digital, video)
- AR: Augmented Reality — digital overlay of simulated outcomes on real images
- Mock-up: Analog wax-up or direct composite preview of proposed dental changes
- 2D/3D smile design: Digital simulation software projecting esthetic outcome on patient photos
