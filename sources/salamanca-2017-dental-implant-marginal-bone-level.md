## Why Ingested

이 논문은 다른 대부분의 platform switching(PS) 논문과 달리 결과를 "bone loss(골소실)"가 아니라 임플란트 숄더부터 골까지의 vertical/horizontal "gap(간극)"이 12개월에 걸쳐 줄어드는 "bone gain(골증가)/gap reduction(간극 감소)"으로 측정·서술한다 — 같은 방향(PS 우위)이지만 측정 관행이 다른 근거로서 인제스트. [[wiki/implants/mbl/juan-montesinos-2022-platform-switching-conventional-sr-ma]]가 종합한 PS의 변연골 보존 이점을 후향적 1년 코호트로 재확인.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective clinical study (51 patients, 60 Dentium implants — 30 platform-switched (PS) in 24 patients vs 30 platform-matched (PM) in 27 patients, Shuang-Ho Hospital, New Taipei, Taiwan, March 2010–January 2015, 12-month follow-up after final prosthetic restoration) measuring vertical and horizontal peri-implant marginal bone **gap** (distance from implant shoulder to the most apical/horizontal bone defect) on periapical radiographs, rather than the more typical "bone loss from baseline" framing.

Both groups showed gap **reduction** ("bone gain") over 12 months, but PS showed significantly more vertical gap reduction (0.93±1mm gain, p<0.05) than PM (0.23±0.58mm gain); horizontal gap gain was numerically greater in PS (0.50±0.56mm) than PM (0.22±0.53mm) but not statistically significant; a second reported metric — average vertical gap reduction from baseline to 12 months — was 0.92±1.11mm (PS) vs 0.29±0.85mm (PM), p<0.05, consistent with the first figure.

100% implant survival/success in both groups (Buser criteria + bone loss <1mm); authors conclude PS was more effective for peri-implant vertical (and, less conclusively, horizontal) marginal bone gap reduction at 1 year, consistent in direction with prior PS systematic reviews.

## 세줄요약

후향적 임상연구(51명, 임플란트 60개 — 플랫폼 스위칭(Platform Switching, PS) 30개/24명 vs 일반형(Platform Matching, PM) 30개/27명, 대만 신베이 슈앙호병원, 2010.3–2015.1, 최종 보철물 장착 후 12개월 추적) — 치근단 방사선사진으로 임플란트 숄더부터 골까지의 수직·수평 변연골 "간극(gap)"을 측정. 다른 PS 논문들의 "기저치 대비 골소실(bone loss)" 방식이 아니라 간극이 시간에 따라 줄어드는 것("골증가(bone gain)")으로 서술하는 점이 특이.

두 군 모두 12개월간 간극이 줄었으나("골증가"), PS의 수직 간극 감소(0.93±1mm 증가, p<0.05)가 PM(0.23±0.58mm)보다 유의하게 컸음; 수평 간극 증가는 PS(0.50±0.56mm)가 PM(0.22±0.53mm)보다 수치상 컸으나 유의하지 않음; 논문 내 별도로 보고된 "기저치→12개월 평균 수직 간극 감소"도 PS 0.92±1.11mm vs PM 0.29±0.85mm(p<0.05)로 동일 방향.

두 군 모두 성공률 100%(Buser 기준 + 골소실 <1mm 기준); 저자들은 1년 시점에서 PS가 수직(그리고 덜 확실하게 수평) 변연골 간극 감소에 더 효과적이었다고 결론지으며, 이는 기존 PS 체계적 문헌고찰들과 방향이 일치.

## 1. Document Information

- **Journal**: BioMed Research International, 2017, Article ID 7191534
- **DOI**: 10.1155/2017/7191534
- **PMID**: 29204445 / **PMCID**: PMC5674488
- **Institution**: Shuang-Ho Hospital, New Taipei, Taiwan; Taipei Medical University (IRB approval 201301034)
- **Study period**: March 2010 – January 2015; single trained surgeon performed all surgical and prosthetic procedures.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Measures peri-implant marginal bone change as a **vertical + horizontal "gap"** between the implant shoulder and the most apical/horizontal bone defect margin, tracked at 6 timepoints (baseline, immediately after loading, 1, 3, 6, 12 months after loading) — a finer-grained temporal resolution than most PS RCTs, which typically report only baseline and endpoint.
- Reports outcomes as **gap reduction ("bone gain")** rather than bone loss — i.e., the defect measured at implant placement partially fills in over the first year, and the paper quantifies how much more this happens with PS vs PM.
- Directly compares PS and PM using the *same* implant system/design (Dentium, internal conical connection) and the *same* surgeon, controlling for a common confound (differing implant systems) seen across the PS literature.
- 100% implant success rate (Buser criteria + bone loss <1mm) in both groups over the study period, contextualizing the MBL difference as clinically minor in absolute terms even though statistically significant for the vertical dimension.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective clinical study, Declaration of Helsinki (Edinburgh 2000) compliant, Taipei Medical University Joint IRB approval 201301034.
- **Sample**: 51 patients (30 men, 21 women), ages 28–80, 60 Dentium implants total. 30 PM implants in 27 patients (diameter 3.4–4.5mm, length 9.5–13mm); 30 PS implants in 24 patients (diameter 3.3–4.8mm, length 8–12mm), with a 0.6mm horizontal circular step between implant outer edge and narrower abutment. Max 3 implants/patient, one per quadrant, each replacing a single missing tooth.
- **Surgical protocol**: Early placement, implants inserted 6 weeks after tooth extraction; full-thickness mucoperiosteal flap; implants placed so the outer edge reached the marginal bone level (cover-screw apex level with bone crest). Second-stage surgery for healing abutments at 3 months. Impressions at 6 weeks post-abutment-connection surgery; permanent metal-ceramic crown delivered 2 weeks after impressions.
- **Inclusion/exclusion**: Good systemic health, nonsmoking or ≤10 cigarettes/day, FMPS ≤25%, FMBS ≤25%, PPD ≤3mm and PAL ≤2mm at adjacent teeth, KT ≥2mm, stable posterior occlusion, no parafunction; excluded active infection, inadequate bone volume, systemic disease, >10 cigarettes/day, betel nut/tobacco chewing, alcoholism, pregnancy/breastfeeding, untreated periodontal disease.
- **Radiographic measurement**: Standardized digital periapical radiographs (long-cone paralleling technique, Meijndert et al.), taken at baseline (before loading), immediately after loading, and 1/3/6/12 months after loading. Implant shoulder = reference point; vertical and horizontal bone gap measured mesially and distally with EZ-Dental professional imaging software (Asahi Co. Ltd.), calibrated against known implant length. Measured by two examiners independent of the surgeon.
- **Success criteria**: Buser et al. (1990) criteria (implant in situ, no persistent pain/discomfort/paresthesia, no suppurative infection, no mobility, no peri-implant radiolucency) combined with bone loss <1mm (mean of mesial and distal measurements).
- **Statistics**: Descriptive statistics (mean ± SD); Jarque-Bera normality test; independent- and paired-sample t-tests for between/within-group comparisons respectively; significance set at p<0.05, 95% CI. Unit of analysis = individual implant. Microsoft Excel Professional Plus 2016 used for analysis.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Metric | Platform Switching (PS) | Platform Matching (PM) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vertical gap, baseline | 2.09 ± 1.22 mm | 1.24 ± 0.86 mm | — |
| Vertical gap, 12 months | 1.17 ± 1.01 mm | 1.01 ± 0.58 mm | — |
| Horizontal gap, baseline | 1.55 ± 0.97 mm | 1.33 ± 0.82 mm | — |
| Horizontal gap, 12 months | 1.05 ± 0.56 mm | 1.11 ± 0.53 mm | — |
| Vertical gap gain (bone gain), baseline→12mo | 0.93 ± 1 mm | 0.23 ± 0.58 mm | <0.05 (between groups) |
| Horizontal gap gain (bone gain), baseline→12mo | 0.50 ± 0.56 mm | 0.22 ± 0.53 mm | NS |
| Average vertical gap reduction, baseline→12mo | 0.92 ± 1.11 mm | 0.29 ± 0.85 mm | <0.05 |
| Average horizontal gap reduction, baseline→12mo | 0.50 ± 0.87 mm | 0.22 ± 0.58 mm | NS |

- Within-PS-group: vertical gap reduction significant between baseline and 6 months, and between baseline and 12 months (all vertical measurements); horizontal gap reduction likewise significant baseline vs 6 months and baseline vs 12 months.
- Within-PM-group: vertical gap reduction significant only for distal measurements, baseline vs 12 months; horizontal gap showed no significant reduction at any timepoint.
- 100% implant success (Buser criteria + bone loss <1mm) and 100% survival in both groups over the 12-month observation.
- Overall: only the **vertical** gap-reduction difference between PS and PM reached statistical significance; the horizontal difference did not.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Retrospective design (no randomization), single-center, single-surgeon — internal validity strong (controls for surgeon skill) but limits generalizability.
- Short follow-up (12 months from loading) — cannot speak to long-term (multi-year) marginal bone stability.
- Soft-tissue thickness was not measured, despite the paper's own discussion noting soft tissue thickness (~3–4mm) is reported elsewhere to modulate the PS bone-preservation effect.
- Two implant diameter/length ranges differ slightly between PS (3.3–4.8mm / 8–12mm) and PM (3.4–4.5mm / 9.5–13mm) groups, though same implant system and design used.
- Authors explicitly call for longer follow-up studies and further investigation of soft-tissue thickness interaction with platform switching.

## 7. Glossary

- **Platform Switching (PS)**: Using an abutment narrower in diameter than the implant platform, shifting the implant-abutment microgap horizontally inward, away from the implant shoulder.
- **Platform Matching (PM)**: Abutment diameter equal to the implant platform diameter (conventional configuration).
- **Vertical/horizontal bone gap**: In this paper's convention, the distance from the implant shoulder to the most apical (vertical) or lateral (horizontal) point of the peri-implant marginal bone defect, measured on periapical radiographs — distinct from "bone loss from baseline," which most other PS papers report.
- **Bone gain / gap reduction**: This paper's term for the gap narrowing over the 12-month follow-up (i.e., bone filling in toward the implant shoulder), used instead of "bone loss" since the metric tracked is the defect size, not a loss from an initial flush baseline.
- **Buser criteria (1990)**: Implant success criteria — implant in situ, no persistent pain/discomfort/paresthesia, no suppurative peri-implant infection, no mobility, no peri-implant radiolucency.
