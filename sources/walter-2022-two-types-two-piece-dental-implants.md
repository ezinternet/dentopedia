## Why Ingested

Long-term (8-year) head-to-head RCT data on **two modern platform-switched two-piece systems** was a gap this wiki held only indirectly — [[implants/mbl/schwarz-2013-implant-abutment-connection-platform-switching]] set the 2013 expert consensus that long-term RCT data on non-matching implant–abutment junctions was scarce and called for exactly such trials, and [[implants/mbl/juan-montesinos-2022-platform-switching-conventional-sr-ma]] pooled short-term PS-vs-matching effects without ever separating one PS brand from another. This paper also lands as the 8-year tail of the Zurich long-term implant-design programme whose 15–17-year endpoint is already held in [[implants/pirc-2026-one-piece-two-piece-implants-15year-rct]] — and it directly answers the extrapolation caveat that page raised (whether modern platform-switched two-piece systems really behave better than the old Brånemark two-piece arm). The bone-vs-complication trade-off pattern reappears here with the same sign as in that 15–17-year trial, but between two brands rather than two designs.

## Three-line Summary
Single-center RCT 8-year follow-up (64 patients, 98 implants; University of Zurich, Switzerland) comparing two two-piece implant systems with non-matching (platform-switched) implant-abutment junctions supporting fixed restorations — S1 OsseoSpeed TX (Dentsply Sirona) vs S2 Straumann Bone Level SLActive (Straumann AG); 49 patients (42 S1 / 36 S2 implants) re-examined at mean 7.9 ± 0.4 years.
Survival was equivalent (S1 97.6% vs S2 97.2%, p=.930); the primary endpoint favored S1 (marginal bone level change from loading to 8 years: gain 0.21 mm vs loss 0.24 mm, intergroup p<.001, median difference 95% CI [0.27, 0.88] mm) while technical complications favored S2 (35.7% vs 16.7% of implants, p=.1413) and peri-implant mucositis favored S1 (21.4% vs 55.6%, p=.038) with peri-implantitis 0% vs 8.3%.
Both systems held bone within 0.3 mm of the implant shoulder over 8 years and all inter-system differences — including the statistically significant bone difference — were judged clinically negligible, so two-piece system choice should rest on handling and prosthetic preference rather than on survival or bone outcomes; the main weakness is the absence of a sample-size calculation (convenience sample) and a 23% dropout rate.

## 세줄요약
단일센터 무작위대조임상시험(Randomized Controlled Clinical Trial, RCT) 8년 추적(환자 64명, 임플란트 98개; 취리히대학교 스위스) — 고정성 보철(Fixed Restoration)을 지지하는 비매칭(non-matching) 임플란트-abutment 접합부를 가진 두 2피스(two-piece) 시스템 비교: S1 OsseoSpeed TX(Dentsply Sirona) vs S2 Straumann Bone Level SLActive(Straumann AG); 평균 7.9 ± 0.4년 시점(T8)에 환자 49명(이플란트 S1 42개, S2 36개) 재평가.
생존율(Implant Survival Rate, ISR)은 동등(S1 97.6% vs S2 97.2%, p=.930); 1차 평가지표인 변연골수준(Marginal Bone Level, MBL) 변화는 S1 우위(로딩 후 8년 간 골 증가 0.21 mm vs 골 소실 0.24 mm, 군간 p<.001, 중앙값 차이 95% CI [0.27, 0.88] mm), 기술적 합병증(Technical Complication)은 S2 우위(임플란트 35.7% vs 16.7%, p=.1413), 임플란트주위점막염(Peri-implant Mucositis)은 S1 우위(21.4% vs 55.6%, p=.038), 임플란트주위염(Peri-implantitis)은 0% vs 8.3%.
두 시스템 모두 8년 동안 임플란트 어깨(shoulder)로부터 0.3 mm 이내로 골을 유지했고, 골을 포함한 모든 군간 차이는 임상적으로 무시할 수준으로 판단되었음 — 2피스 시스템 선택의 근거는 생존율·골 지표가 아니라 조작성(handling)·보철적 선호도여야 함. 주요 한계는 표본수 계산(sample size calculation) 부재(편의표본(convenience sample))와 23% 이탈률(dropout rate).

## 2. Key Contributions

- First 8-year randomized head-to-head data on **two modern non-matching (platform-switched) two-piece implant systems**, the outcome combination for which the field had only short-term pooled estimates.
- Demonstrates that the **primary endpoint (marginal bone level) can differ significantly between two systems that are otherwise equivalent** — median gain 0.21 mm (S1) vs median loss 0.24 mm (S2), intergroup p<.001 — and yet the authors judge the difference clinically negligible (both < 0.3 mm from the shoulder).
- Quantifies the **bone-vs-technical-complication trade-off** between two contemporary systems: the better-bone system (S1) carried ~2× the technical complication burden (35.7% vs 16.7% of implants).
- Shows the trade-off is **not uniform across outcome classes**: peri-implant mucositis ran in the opposite direction to technical complications (21.4% S1 vs 55.6% S2, p=.038), so no single "better system" label applies.
- Low 8-year peri-implantitis incidence (0% S1, 8.3% S2) under an individually designed maintenance program, supporting the Derks/Tomasi-derived expectation that supportive care keeps peri-implant disease far below observational-study rates.
- Attributes the excess technical complications to **retention type (screw-retained) rather than to the implant system**, connection, or restoration material — a system-independent explanation consistent with the wider screw-vs-cement retention literature.

## 3. Methodology

- **Design**: Single-center randomized controlled clinical trial (computer-generated allocation), 8-year follow-up report. Not blinded (clinician-performed surgery/prosthetics).
- **Setting**: Clinic of Reconstructive Dentistry, University of Zurich; implants placed Feb–Dec 2009.
- **n**: 64 patients, 98 implants (67 maxillary, 31 mandibular). At T8 (mean 7.9 ± 0.4 years, mean age 64.3 ± 12.0) 49 patients re-examined — implant level 42 (S1) / 36 (S2); patient level 25 (S1) / 24 (S2) after one implant per patient was randomly preselected.
- **Arms**: S1 = OsseoSpeed TX 3.0–5.0 S / TX 4.5 (Astra Tech Implant System, Dentsply Sirona); S2 = Straumann Bone Level Implants 3.3 / 4.1 / 4.8 mm, SLActive surface (Straumann AG). **Both** use a non-matching, internally conical implant-abutment junction.
- **Protocol**: Shoulder generally placed at bone crest, but apico-coronal position deliberately **not** standardized (sink depth sometimes increased for prosthetic reasons); mucosa thickness left to surgeon; GBR with xenogeneic/synthetic graft + resorbable membrane if dehiscence or fenestration; restorations screw-retained or cemented per clinical situation (no fixed protocol); individual maintenance program from baseline.
- **Timepoints**: T_i (implant insertion), T_L (1–3 weeks after loading = baseline), T1, T3, T5, T8.
- **Outcomes**: Primary = patient-level marginal bone level (MBL) change T_L → T8. Secondary = implant and restoration survival; technical and biological complications; PD, BOP, PCR at 6 sites per implant (mesiobuccal, buccal, distobuccal, distolingual, lingual, mesiolingual) using UNC-15 probe; adjacent and contralateral tooth/implant sites also measured.
- **Radiography**: Standardized paralleling technique (Rinn holders, film holder + bite plate + 90° ring alignment) at every timepoint; MBL measured on ImageJ at 10–15× magnification, calibrated by the known inter-thread distance; distance-implant-bone (DIB) measured mesially and distally in 0.1-mm steps from the flat top of the implant shoulder to the bone crest; MBL change = difference between timepoints. Positive values = shoulder apical to bone crest.
- **Statistics**: Primary endpoint — Wilcoxon signed-rank within group, Wilcoxon rank-sum between groups with Bonferroni correction across three tests; nonparametric 95% CI for median change and Hodges–Lehmann estimate of between-group difference. Secondary endpoints — Wilcoxon rank-sum or chi-square (exact p if needed) at patient level (one implant per patient eliminates clustering); survival and complication rates at both levels. Supporting implant-level analyses by parametric mixed models (time × group with interaction, plus per-timepoint group models). **Multiplicity was controlled only for the primary endpoint.**
- **Definitions**: Peri-implant mucositis and peri-implantitis prevalence reported at T8 (the authors note these definitions are inconsistent across the published literature).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Survival (implant level, 8 years)

| Metric | S1 (OsseoSpeed TX) | S2 (Straumann BL SLActive) | Overall | Between-group p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Survival rate | 97.6% | 97.2% | 97.4% (2/98 lost) | .930 (mixed model) |
| Loss cause | technical complication → subsequent implant fracture | biological complication (peri-implantitis) | — | — |
| Patient-level survival | 100% | 100% | 100% | — |

Context: reported survival for the two systems in the literature spans 90.9–100% (S1) and 96.5–99.3% (S2) over 1–10 years; systematic reviews place 10-year survival at 93.8–97.5%.

### Marginal bone level — PRIMARY ENDPOINT (patient level, T_L → T8)

| Measure | S1 | S2 | Between-group |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median MBL change | **gain 0.21 mm** (Q1 −0.11; Q3 0.50) | **loss 0.24 mm** (Q1 −0.79; Q3 0.05) | **p < .001** |
| Within-group p | .007 | .001 | — |
| 95% CI for median change | [0.051, 0.384] | [−0.954, −0.107] | median difference (S1 − S2) **[0.27, 0.88]** |
| Shoulder-to-crest distance at T8 (median) | 0.00 mm (Q1 −0.32; Q3 0.23) | −0.31 mm (Q1 −0.79; Q3 −0.12) | p = .010, CI (S2 − S1) [−0.75, −0.11] |
| Implant-level mixed model | time × group interaction significant, p = .009 | | |

Reconstructive context for interpreting these numbers: mean MBL at implant placement was **−1.30 ± 1.00 mm (S1)** and **−1.26 ± 1.22 mm (S2)** — an intentionally increased sink depth — followed by physiological remodeling that brought bone close to the shoulder once final restorations were inserted. Mean MBL at 8 years was **0.04 ± 0.67 mm (S1)** and **−0.58 ± 0.78 mm (S2)**. Between loading and 8 years, within-system change was clinically negligible (< 0.3 mm) in both groups.

### Technical complications (entire 8-year observation period)

| Level | S1 | S2 | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implant level | 35.7% (15/42) | 16.7% (6/36) | .1413 (mixed model) |
| Patient level | 28% (7/25) | 12.5% (3/24) | .289 |

Complication profile: **screw loosening predominated**, followed by chipping and fracture of screws/abutments; these three accounted for **75% of all technical complications in S1**. The excess was *not* associated with restoration material (porcelain-fused-to-metal throughout) or restoration type (single crown, cantilever, multi-unit, splinted), but with **retention** — >90% of restorations were screw-retained, and the authors cite Sailer et al.'s pooled comparison (screw-retained 24.4% vs cemented 11.9%). This rate exceeds literature benchmarks of 5–15% over 5 years for single crowns (Jung et al.) and 4.6% at 5 years / 19.9% at 10 years for FDPs (Pjetursson et al.).

### Biological complications (T8)

| Outcome | S1 | S2 | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peri-implant mucositis, implant level | 21.4% (9/42) | 55.6% (20/36) | .038 |
| Peri-implant mucositis, patient level | 24% (6/25) | 50% (12/24) | .792 |
| Peri-implantitis, implant level | 0% (0) | 8.3% (3/36) | not applicable |
| Peri-implantitis, patient level | 0% | 12.5% (3/24) | .110 |

Consistent with systematic-review ranges (mucositis 19–65%, peri-implantitis 1–47%). PCR was low throughout — baseline 0.1 ± 0.1, and at FU-8 0.3 ± 0.3 (S1) vs 0.1 ± 0.2 (S2) — and the authors attribute the low peri-implantitis rate to the individually designed maintenance program plus consistently good oral hygiene.

> **Published-transcription note.** The published §3.6 sentence reads "24% (6 of 24 implants) in group S1 and 50.0% (12 of 25 implants) in group S2", which transposes the group denominators relative to §3.1 (patient level at T8 = 25 S1 / 24 S2). The percentages given in the Abstract (24% S1, 50% S2) only reconcile as 6/25 and 12/24, so the denominators are a publisher typo; the percentages are used here.

### Attribution of the primary result

The authors' four-part summary of the 8-year comparison: (i) high survival rates for both types of implant, (ii) stable marginal bone levels, (iii) higher rate of technical complications in S1, and (iv) higher rate of peri-implant mucositis in S2. Differences "appear to be clinically negligible".

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No sample size calculation.** A convenience sample was used because data on S2 did not exist when the trial began — the study is underpowered for complication endpoints, which is the most likely reason the 35.7% vs 16.7% technical-complication difference (and the 21.4% vs 55.6% mucositis difference at implant level) is nominally non-significant.
- **No multiplicity control beyond the primary endpoint** — the many secondary p-values (including several below 0.05) are unadjusted and should not be read as independent confirmations.
- **23% dropout by 8 years** (death, relocation abroad, refusal), leaving 49 of 64 patients; attrition was not analyzed against baseline characteristics.
- **Apico-coronal implant position was not standardized**, and initial sink depth differed from crest placement — which is why the change-from-T_L values and the absolute T8 values must not be conflated.
- **Very broad inclusion criteria** — no restriction on site (maxilla/mandible, anterior/posterior), GBR need, healing type, loading time, retention, or restoration type/material. The authors present this as a strength (general-practice representativeness) and simultaneously as the study's main drawback.
- **Brand confound.** The arms differ in manufacturer *and* surface *and* geometry; with only two system pairs, the "non-matching implant-abutment junction" variable cannot be isolated from brand-specific design.
- **Blinding absent** for surgical, prosthetic and radiographic assessment.
- **COI**: senior authors received research support and lecture fees from both Straumann AG and Dentsply Sirona.
- **Definitional heterogeneity** in mucositis/peri-implantitis case definitions across the literature limits cross-study comparison (the authors' own caveat).

## 6. Related Work

- **Pirc M, et al. 2026** (`implants/pirc-2026-one-piece-two-piece-implants-15year-rct`, PMID 42126189) — same Zurich group, 15–17-year follow-up of an RCT comparing **one-piece** Straumann SLA vs **two-piece** Brånemark TiUnite (MBL 0.08 vs 1.53 mm; technical complications 35.4% vs 5.8%). Different trial, same design question, and the same trade-off sign: the better-bone configuration carries ~6× the technical complications. That page explicitly warns the Brånemark two-piece result must not be extrapolated to modern internal-connection/platform-switched systems — this paper supplies exactly that missing evidence, showing the modern platform-switched two-piece arm (S2, Straumann BL) losing only −0.58 ± 0.78 mm at 8 years, an order of magnitude better than the legacy Brånemark arm.
- **Schwarz F, et al. 2013** (`implants/mbl/schwarz-2013-implant-abutment-connection-platform-switching`) — Camlog Foundation consensus that set the 2013 baseline: microgap position and connection type evidence was thin, and it called for long-term randomized trials. This paper is one of the trials that consensus was waiting for, and it confirms its central expectation (non-matching junctions keep marginal bone near the shoulder) while adding that inter-brand differences within that design are clinically negligible.
- **Juan Montesinos R, et al. 2022** (`implants/mbl/juan-montesinos-2022-platform-switching-conventional-sr-ma`) — SR+MA of platform switching vs **conventional/matching** platform (MD 0.255 mm less bone loss, p<0.05). Complementary rather than competing: that meta-analysis pools PS against non-PS, whereas both arms here are PS, so this paper cannot and does not test the PS-vs-PM effect size.
- **Messias AD, et al. 2019** (`implants/mbl/messias-2019-platform-switching-mbl-5year-rct`) — 5-year PS RCT (PS 0.23 mm lower MBL) of the same design family at a shorter horizon; the paper cites Messias et al. in its introduction as part of the evidence base that was still short of long-term data.
- **Gupta 2019** (`implants/mbl/gupta-2019-platform-switching-crestal-bone-loss-sr`) and **Salimi 2011** (`implants/mbl/salimi-2011-platform-switching-current-results`) — earlier platform-switching syntheses for context on the marginal-bone question.
- **Vélez J, et al. 2020** (`prosthetic-materials/abutment-screw/velez-2020-implant-connection-abutment-design-screw`) — in-vitro mechanical counterpart: Morse-taper internal conical connection gives the lowest implant-abutment misfit, with misfit decreasing as insertion torque rises. Supplies the biomechanical rationale (reduced microgap and micromovement) the authors cite for why non-matching junctions behave well.
- **Ebler S, et al. 2016** — 1-year report of this same trial (Clin Oral Implants Res 27(9):1169–1177, doi 10.1111/clr.12721, PMID 26459570). Not yet in this wiki; the 1- and 5-year timepoints of this trial remain an open ingest gap.

## 7. Glossary

- **Non-matching implant-abutment junction (platform switching, PS)**: implant-abutment interface in which the abutment sits on a shoulder of smaller diameter than the implant platform, shifting the junction inward and away from the crestal bone.
- **Marginal bone level (MBL) / distance implant-bone (DIB)**: radiographic distance from the flat top of the implant shoulder to the crestal bone crest, measured mesially and distally in 0.1-mm steps; positive values mean the shoulder lies apical to the crest.
- **Implant survival**: an implant still present and in function at follow-up, as opposed to implant success (survival **plus** acceptable bone/soft-tissue and prosthetic parameters).
- **Peri-implant mucositis**: reversible inflammatory soft-tissue condition around an implant with no supporting bone loss.
- **Peri-implantitis**: inflammatory condition with progressive supporting bone loss around an implant; the single most common late biological cause of implant loss.
- **Technical complication**: prosthesis-side event not requiring biological intervention — chiefly abutment screw loosening, screw/abutment fracture, and veneering ceramic chipping.
- **Hodges–Lehmann estimator**: nonparametric location estimate (median of pairwise differences) used here for the between-group confidence interval on the primary endpoint.
- **Dropout rate**: proportion of enrolled participants not re-examined at a given follow-up (23% here).
