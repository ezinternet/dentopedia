## Why Ingested

This UK CPD (Continuing Professional Development) review (British Dental Journal, 2019) translates the alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) evidence base into a practical why/when/how clinical protocol for general and restorative dentists. It is directly anchored on the [[wiki/bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/atieh-2015-alveolar-ridge-preservation-cochrane-review]] Cochrane review — its own Table 3 (xenograft +2.6 mm height / +1.97 mm width vs extraction alone) is a verbatim reproduction of that SR's pooled results — and it reproduces the socket-anatomy decision framework from [[wiki/bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/jung-2018-alveolar-ridge-preservation-esthetic-zone]] as its own Table 2. It fills a gap in the wiki's ARP cluster, which so far holds systematic-review-level evidence and narrative syntheses ([[wiki/bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/fok-2024-alveolar-socket-healing-evolving-knowledge]], [[wiki/bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/che-2024-alveolar-ridge-preservation-review]]) but no UK-primary-care-oriented, step-by-step surgical-protocol page (atraumatic extraction technique, packing, closure, suture timing, informed-consent talking points) aimed at the chairside ARP-vs-immediate-implant decision.

## Three-line Summary
Narrative CPD review (British Dental Journal 2019;227(4):264–274) by UK restorative-dentistry consultants, synthesizing ten systematic reviews published since 2009 on alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) case selection, materials, and technique.
ARP with xenograft or allograft preserves roughly 2 mm more alveolar bone height and width than unassisted extraction healing (Cochrane: +2.6 mm/+1.97 mm xenograft, +2.2 mm/+1.97 mm allograft), but 15–36% residual graft particles remain at a mean of 5.6 months and complete ridge preservation is never achieved.
No consensus exists on case selection, technique, or material choice; the authors propose a socket-type-based decision tree and a specific surgical protocol (no saline irrigation, atraumatic extraction, no routine antibiotics, sutures out at 7–10 days).

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: British Dental Journal 2019;227(4):264–274
- **DOI**: 10.1038/s41415-019-0647-2
- **Institution**: Eastman Dental Hospital, London (lead author); Croydon University & King's College Hospitals, Croydon, UK

## 2. Key Contributions
- Consolidates ten systematic reviews (2009–2018) on ARP into a single practical clinical reference, explicitly stating "the literature does not support the use of one technique, or material, as superior"
- Reproduces and applies Salama & Salama's socket-type classification (Type I/II/III) and Jung et al.'s (2018) treatment-modality decision framework (soft tissue / hard+soft tissue / hard tissue preservation) as a unified indications table
- Provides a concrete, citable surgical protocol including an evidence-based warning against socket saline irrigation (3.5× increased alveolar osteitis risk in a 75-patient split-mouth study) and against routine antibiotic prescription
- Publishes a decision-tree flowchart (Fig. 3) integrating immediate/early implant feasibility, aesthetic pontic-site considerations, and long-term implant planning into the ARP-or-not decision
- Summarizes graft material trade-offs (autograft/allograft/xenograft/alloplast, membrane types, growth factors) in tabular form (Tables 4–5), including a comparative summary from Jambhekar et al. 2015 across four material classes

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Narrative review / CPD clinical paper (not a systematic review — no stated search strategy, PRISMA flow, or quality appraisal of included studies)
- **Databases**: Not applicable — synthesizes findings from ten pre-existing systematic reviews (references 1, 3, 7–14) plus supporting primary literature (90 references total)
- **n**: Not a primary study; key quantitative anchor is the Cochrane review (Atieh et al. 2015) pooling 233 extraction sites in 184 healthy adults across 8 RCTs (7 high risk of bias, 1 unclear)
- **Outcomes**: Alveolar bone height/width change, residual graft particle percentage, vital bone percentage, implant survival/success/marginal bone levels, need for further augmentation at implant placement

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
| Comparison | Δ Bone height (mm) | Δ Bone width (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| ARP with xenograft vs extraction alone | 2.6* | 1.97* |
| ARP with allograft vs extraction alone | 2.2* | 1.97* |
| Xenograft vs allograft | No difference | No difference |

*Greater bone volume retained with graft material (from Atieh et al. 2015, Cochrane).

Additional benchmarks:
- Untreated bucco-lingual width reduction: ~50% by 12 months (two-thirds within the first 3 months); a separate SR reports mean 3.8 mm width / 1.24 mm height loss over 6 months
- Buccal wall ≤1 mm thickness at extraction → median 7.5 mm vertical loss at 8 weeks, vs 1.1 mm loss if buccal wall >1 mm
- Residual graft particles: 15–36% at mean 5.6 months post-ARP, varying by material (xenograft ~30%, encapsulated in connective tissue)
- DFDBA vs FDBA: DFDBA shows greater vital bone (38.45% vs 26.63%) and fewer residual particles (8.88% vs 25.42%) at 4–5 months
- Ideal graft particle size: 100–380 μm (smaller resorbed by macrophages; larger restrict vascularization)
- DBBM (deproteinized bovine bone mineral) resorption rate: ~10%/year; 26.4–35.1% vital bone at 9 months
- Saline socket irrigation associated with 3.5× increased alveolar osteitis incidence (split-mouth study, n=75, third-molar extractions)
- Mardas et al. 2015 SR conclusions (cited in full): ARP may decrease need for further augmentation; no evidence it increases implant-placement feasibility; implant survival/success/marginal bone comparable to unassisted healing; no evidence one ARP modality (GBR/socket filler/socket seal) is superior; most studies carry high/unclear risk of bias

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Narrative, not systematic — no documented search strategy or formal quality appraisal; conclusions rest on the authors' selection and interpretation of ten prior SRs plus supporting primary studies
- Underlying evidence base is itself weak: 7 of 8 studies in the Cochrane review were high risk of bias, one unclear
- No trials identified assessing clinical attachment levels, aesthetic outcomes, or prosthodontic outcomes specifically attributable to ARP
- Authors' proposed surgical protocol (e.g., 6-month delay preference, avoiding saline irrigation, no routine antibiotics) reflects clinical experience/expert opinion in several places, not exclusively trial evidence
- Growth factors, PRF, stem cells, enamel matrix derivatives, and bone marrow are flagged as requiring further evidence before validation

## 6. Related Work
- atieh-2015 (Cochrane review): primary quantitative source for this paper's own Table 3 (ARP vs extraction-alone bone dimension outcomes)
- jung-2018 (Periodontology 2000): source of the socket-anatomy/treatment-modality classification reproduced as this paper's Table 2
- fok-2024, che-2024: independent, later narrative reviews reaching the same "no consensus on material/technique" conclusion

## 7. Glossary
- **ARP (Alveolar Ridge Preservation)**: procedure to arrest/minimize alveolar ridge resorption after extraction, for future prosthodontic/implant treatment; synonymous with socket preservation, socket grafting, socket augmentation
- **DBBM (Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral)**: xenograft bone substitute (e.g., Bio-Oss); most commonly used ARP xenograft, resorbs ~10%/year
- **FDBA / DFDBA**: (Demineralized) Freeze-Dried Bone Allograft — mineralized (FDBA, osteoconductive) vs demineralized (DFDBA, potentially osteoinductive) human allograft bone
- **GBR (Guided Bone Regeneration)**: use of cell-occlusive barrier membranes to exclude epithelial/connective tissue from a bone defect, allowing osteogenic cells to regenerate lost bone
- **Bundle bone**: the lamina dura lining the periodontal ligament; always resorbs after extraction (0.2–0.5 mm thick, fully resorbed by 4 weeks) regardless of ARP or implant timing
- **Socket shield technique**: partial extraction therapy retaining a thin labial root fragment in situ while placing an implant palatal to it, aiming to preserve buccal bone contour as an ARP alternative
