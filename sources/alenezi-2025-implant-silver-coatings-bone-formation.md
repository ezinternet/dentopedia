## Why Ingested
Quantifies, via meta-analysis, whether silver (Ag) antibacterial implant coatings help or hurt bone formation — a dose/cytotoxicity concern flagged qualitatively but left unpooled by the broader dual-biofunctional-coating literature in [[implants/surface/zhang-2025-dual-biofunctional-implant-coatings-in-vivo-sr]] (found via `qmd search`), whose 16-study SR could not run a meta-analysis due to coating-type heterogeneity. Alenezi narrows to Ag specifically and pools BIC/BA/BV-TV across 16 animal studies.

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA, INPLASY202590035) of 16 animal studies (Wistar rats, NZW rabbits, Sprague–Dawley rats, Labrador/beagle dogs, pigs, Merino sheep) comparing silver-coated vs uncoated titanium implants on bone-to-implant contact (BIC), bone area (BA), and bone volume/total volume (BV/TV).
Pooled BIC significantly favored Ag-coated implants (Z=2.01, p=0.04); pooled BA (Z=1.09, p=0.28) and BV/TV (Z=0.35, p=0.73) showed no significant difference; heterogeneity was very high across all three outcomes (I²=96–99%).
Ag coatings appear to improve interfacial bone contact without harming peri-implant bone volume in animal models — no included study reported a detrimental bone effect — but evidence is short-term (≤6 months) and heterogeneous, so dose-response and long-term/clinical translation remain open.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First meta-analysis specifically isolating **silver** (vs. the broader antibacterial-coating literature) across BIC, BA, and BV/TV in animal models.
- Shows a **discrepancy between interfacial (BIC) and volumetric (BA, BV/TV) bone outcomes** — Ag's effect appears localized to the implant-bone interface rather than overall peri-implant bone remodeling.
- Surveys the range of Ag-incorporation methods (nanotubular structures, plasma immersion ion implantation, electrodeposition, atomic layer deposition, wet chemical plating, magnetron sputtering, hybrid coatings) and Ag forms (nanoparticles, ions, nanolayers, silver oxide).
- Discusses proposed mechanism beyond direct antibacterial action: Ag-driven M1→M2 macrophage polarization and pro-angiogenic signaling supporting osteogenesis.
- Contextualizes Ag against zinc (Zn) and copper (Cu) coatings: Zn shows favorable bactericidal effect + enhanced osteoblastic activity; Cu is broad-spectrum antibacterial but its elevated ion release carries more cytotoxicity concern than Ag.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis, PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-style registration INPLASY202590035.
- **PICO**: Population = animal models; Intervention = Ti implants modified/coated with Ag; Control = Ti implants without Ag modification; Outcome = bone formation (BIC/BA/BV-TV).
- **Databases**: PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus; search run May 2025; studies from the last 20 years; English only.
- **n**: 16 animal studies (qualitative + quantitative synthesis); pooled implant counts vary per outcome (BIC df=16, BA df=22, BV/TV df=23).
- **Outcomes**: BIC, BA, BV/TV (% around implant), extracted as means ± SD, pooled as weighted mean differences via Review Manager 5.3.3 (random-effects/inverse-variance when heterogeneity p<0.05).
- **Quality/RoB**: ARRIVE 2.0 (21 items, scored reported/unclear/not-reported) for methodological quality; SYRCLE RoB tool for risk of bias.
- **Exclusions**: clinical or in vitro studies excluded; studies not reporting BIC/BA/BV-TV or implant counts excluded.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
| Outcome | Effect (Ag-coated vs. control) | Heterogeneity |
|---|---|---|
| BIC | Z=2.01, p=0.04 — significantly favors Ag-coated | Tau²=190.60; Chi²=555.54, df=16 (p<0.001); I²=96% |
| BA | Z=1.09, p=0.28 — no significant difference | Tau²=394.55; Chi²=3583.07, df=22 (p<0.001); I²=99% |
| BV/TV | Z=0.35, p=0.73 — no significant difference | Tau²=112.23; Chi²=2432.44, df=23 (p<0.001); I²=98% |

- ARRIVE 2.0 quality coefficients ranged 0.71–0.93: 8 studies "Excellent," 8 "Average," 0 "Poor."
- SYRCLE RoB: allocation concealment and outcome-assessor blinding mostly "unclear" across studies; random-sequence-generation and selective-outcome-reporting domains showed lower concern.
- No included study reported a detrimental effect of Ag coating on bone formation.
- Most included studies had short-to-medium follow-up (≤6 months).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Very high statistical heterogeneity (I²=96–99%) across all three pooled outcomes, driven by differences in animal species, sample sizes, healing durations, Ag form/concentration, and coating technique.
- Risk of bias frequently unclear (allocation concealment, blinding) across included studies.
- Short-term follow-up predominates (≤6 months); long-term bone remodeling and implant survival are not captured.
- Dose–response relationship for Ag concentration/release kinetics vs. cytotoxicity not resolved by pooled analysis.
- Author calls for standardized long-term preclinical protocols in larger animal models, optimization of Ag concentration/particle size/release kinetics, and comparative clinical trials against other antibacterial coatings (Zn, Cu, chitosan).

## 7. Glossary
- **BIC (Bone-to-Implant Contact)**: percentage of implant surface in direct histological contact with bone.
- **BA (Bone Area)**: percentage of bone tissue within a defined region around the implant (areal bone density measure).
- **BV/TV (Bone Volume/Total Volume)**: volumetric fraction of mineralized bone within a region of interest, typically from micro-CT.
- **ARRIVE 2.0**: Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments guidelines — 21-item reporting-quality checklist for animal studies.
- **SYRCLE RoB**: Systematic Review Centre for Laboratory Animal Experimentation risk-of-bias tool, adapted from Cochrane RoB for animal studies.
- **PIII (Plasma Immersion Ion Implantation)**: a surface-modification technique embedding Ag ions into the titanium substrate via plasma exposure.
