## Why Ingested

기존 직접경구항응고제(Direct Oral Anticoagulant, DOAC) 발치 근거는 [[drug/anticoagulants/kim-2024-post-extraction-bleeding-direct-oral-anticoagulants]]처럼 "유지 vs 중단"을 후향적으로 비교한 것이 주류였는데, 이 논문은 **비항응고 건강 대조군**을 두고 술자·평가자 블라인딩과 전향적 수집으로 DOAC 환자의 절대 출혈 부담을 처음 정량화한다. 또한 [[drug/anticoagulants/izzetti-2024-doac-bleeding-management-tooth-extraction-prospective]]가 다루지 않은 **통증(VAS)과 연조직 치유** 결과를 같은 코호트에서 함께 측정해, [[drug/anticoagulants/dou-2025-bleeding-dentoalveolar-doac-vka-sr-ma]]의 통합 추정치를 약제별(rivaroxaban > edoxaban > apixaban > dabigatran)로 세분한다.

## Three-line Summary

Prospective single-blind case-control study (Bursa Uludağ University, Turkey, Jul 2022–Jul 2024; 599 screened, 430 enrolled) comparing 211 patients on uninterrupted DOACs (apixaban 69, dabigatran 29, edoxaban 55, rivaroxaban 58) with 219 non-anticoagulated healthy controls after single-tooth extraction, with the operating surgeon blinded to group at both surgery and day-7 assessment.

Bleeding grades were significantly worse in DOAC patients (grade 0 in 47.9% vs 73.1% of controls; grade 2–2 4.3% vs 0%; grade 3 8.1% vs 0%; χ²=48.40, p<0.001), with a clear per-agent gradient (rivaroxaban grade 3 13.8%, edoxaban 7.3%, apixaban 7.2%, dabigatran 0%; χ²=47.2, p<0.001), while pain was identical (VAS 4.01±2.01 vs 3.95±2.04, p=0.848) and only bone exposure differed among healing outcomes (4.7% vs 1.4%, p=0.039).

Uninterrupted DOAC extraction remains clinically manageable but is not bleeding-neutral — risk is agent-specific and warrants individualized planning rather than a uniform protocol; note the deliberately minimal hemostasis protocol (no sutures, no routine hemostatic agents) inflates absolute bleeding rates relative to normal practice.

## 세줄요약

터키 단일기관 전향적 단일맹검 환자-대조군 연구(2022.07–2024.07; 599명 선별, 430명 등록) — DOAC 지속 복용 환자 211명(아픽사반 69, 다비가트란 29, 에독사반 55, 리바록사반 58) vs 비항응고 건강 대조군 219명, 단일치 발치 후 비교. 술자가 수술 시점과 7일째 평가 시점 모두에서 군 배정을 모른 채 진행.

출혈 등급은 DOAC군에서 유의하게 나빴고(등급 0: 47.9% vs 대조군 73.1%; 등급 2–2: 4.3% vs 0%; 등급 3: 8.1% vs 0%; χ²=48.40, p<0.001), 약제별 기울기가 뚜렷했다(등급 3 — 리바록사반 13.8%, 에독사반 7.3%, 아픽사반 7.2%, 다비가트란 0%; χ²=47.2, p<0.001). 반면 통증은 동일했고(VAS 4.01±2.01 vs 3.95±2.04, p=0.848), 치유 지표 중에서는 골 노출만 차이가 났다(4.7% vs 1.4%, p=0.039).

DOAC 비중단 발치는 임상적으로 감당 가능하지만 "출혈 중립"은 아니다 — 위험은 약제별로 다르므로 획일적 프로토콜이 아니라 개별화된 계획이 필요하다. 단, 이 연구는 자연 지혈 경과를 관찰하려고 **봉합도 국소지혈제도 쓰지 않아** 절대 출혈률이 통상 진료보다 부풀려져 있다는 점을 감안해야 한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First prospective DOAC-extraction study to use a **non-anticoagulated healthy control arm** plus **blinding of both the operating surgeon and the day-7 outcome assessor**, isolating anticoagulation status from operator expectation bias.
- Quantifies bleeding on a **five-level ordinal scale that separates same-day prolonged hemostasis (grade 2−1) from delayed next-day oozing (grade 2−2)** — a distinction with different monitoring implications that binary bleed/no-bleed designs collapse.
- Adds two outcomes largely absent from the DOAC extraction literature: **postoperative pain (VAS)** and **soft-tissue healing** (erythema/edema, suppuration, bone exposure), showing these dissociate from bleeding.
- Provides a **per-agent bleeding gradient** across all four DOACs in one cohort (rivaroxaban worst, dabigatran mildest), supporting agent-specific rather than class-level risk assessment.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: prospective observational case-control, single-blind; STROBE checklist followed.
- **Setting/period**: Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Bursa Uludağ University, July 2022 – July 2024.
- **Enrollment**: 599 patients screened → 430 met eligibility (211 cases, 219 controls).
- **Cases**: adults ≥18 on apixaban, dabigatran, edoxaban, or rivaroxaban, requiring single-tooth extraction under local anesthesia.
- **Controls**: healthy adults on no anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy (none within the past 4 weeks).
- **Key exclusions (both arms)**: concomitant anticoagulant/antiplatelet agents, recent (<3 months) thromboembolic event, uncontrolled systemic disease, active infection at the site, pregnancy/breastfeeding, head-and-neck radiotherapy or bisphosphonate history, **any case requiring flap elevation or osteotomy**.
- **Surgical protocol**: one oral surgeon with ≥8 years' experience, blinded to group; 0.12% chlorhexidine rinse 30 s pre-op; 2% lidocaine with 1:80,000 epinephrine by infiltration; atraumatic extraction with periotomes and elevators; **no flaps, no osteotomy, no sutures**.
- **Hemostasis (deliberately minimal)**: sterile gauze pressure for 30 min, second compression cycle if bleeding persisted, ≥30 min observation. **Local hemostatic agents (oxidized cellulose, gelatin sponge) were intentionally withheld** in order to observe the natural course of hemostasis under anticoagulation. DOAC therapy was continued uninterrupted throughout.
- **Analgesia**: paracetamol 500–1000 mg as needed; NSAIDs excluded.
- **Blinding**: surgeon blinded to group at surgery and at the day-7 assessment; medication and systemic history recorded by a dental secretary and withheld from the surgeon/assessor. Patient blinding was not feasible.
- **Bleeding scale (day 7, structured interview + intraoral exam)**: grade 0 no bleeding; grade 1 excess clot, no treatment; grade 2−1 hemostasis requiring >30 min compression; grade 2−2 oozing on or after the next day, controlled by simple compression; grade 3 hemorrhage requiring treatment beyond compression (compression brace and/or electrocoagulation).
- **Pain**: 10 cm horizontal VAS (0 = no pain, 10 = worst imaginable), patient-marked **average** pain over the first postoperative week, measured in mm at the day-7 visit.
- **Healing**: binary present/absent for erythema (mucosal redness within 5 mm of the socket margin), edema, suppuration, bone exposure; intraoral photographs where applicable.
- **Power**: a priori G*Power 3.1.9.7 — w=0.2, α=0.05 two-tailed, n=350 → power >0.95 for chi-square; Cohen's d=0.4 → power ≈0.95 for two-tailed independent t-test.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 22.0; Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality testing; chi-square for bleeding grades, complications, and sex; Mann-Whitney U and Kruskal-Wallis H for pain; Welch t for age; α=0.05.

Bone exposure by agent (χ²=18.42, p<0.001): dabigatran 4/29 (13.8%), edoxaban 5/55 (9.1%), apixaban 1/69 (1.4%), rivaroxaban 0/58 (0.0%), control 3/219 (1.4%) — note this ordering is **inverse** to the bleeding gradient, which the author attributes speculatively to clot-stability and epithelialization effects rather than to bleeding volume.

Across five medication groups, only bone exposure varied significantly; sex (χ²=1.84, p=0.765), prior extraction (χ²=2.51, p=0.643), comorbidity (χ²=1.12, p=0.891), erythema/edema (χ²=2.49, p=0.645), and suppuration (χ²=6.23, p=0.183) did not.

**Author's conclusion**: extraction is clinically safe under uninterrupted DOAC therapy with meticulous technique and local hemostasis; bleeding severity varies by agent (rivaroxaban and edoxaban highest); outcomes support individualized risk-based perioperative planning over routine discontinuation.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Hemostasis protocol is not standard care**: sutures were never placed and oxidized cellulose/gelatin sponge were deliberately withheld to observe natural hemostasis. Absolute bleeding rates therefore represent a floor-of-intervention scenario and overstate what would be seen under routine local hemostatic management — a critical caveat when comparing these grade-3 rates (8.1%) to cohorts that suture and pack.
- Renal and hepatic function were not stratified despite their known effect on DOAC pharmacokinetics and bleeding risk; comorbidity was captured only as a binary present/absent.
- The DOAC arm was significantly older than controls (55.6 vs 51.7 y, p<0.0001) and this was not adjusted for; age is plausibly associated with both bleeding and healing.
- Seven-day follow-up may miss late-onset delayed bleeding and secondary infection.
- Tooth type and location were not standardized, and only single-tooth, non-surgical (no flap, no osteotomy) extractions were included — external validity to multiple or surgical extractions is unestablished.
- Single center, single operator; dabigatran subgroup small (n=29), limiting the "no severe bleeding with dabigatran" claim.
- Patient blinding was not feasible, so patient-reported outcomes (VAS pain, bleeding history at interview) remain open to reporting bias.
- Author recommends multicenter prospective studies with longer follow-up, comorbidity stratification, evaluation of local hemostatic agents in higher-risk DOAC users, and AI/machine-learning decision trees for perioperative risk.

## 6. Related Work

- Inokoshi et al. — elderly anticoagulated patients: post-extraction bleeding highest with rivaroxaban (32.4%), then apixaban (18.2%), warfarin (17.3%), edoxaban (5.7%); rivaroxaban significantly higher than edoxaban or dabigatran.
- Bensi et al. (systematic review and meta-analysis) — DOAC users may carry roughly threefold increased post-oral-surgery bleeding risk, with rivaroxaban highest among agents.
- Berton et al. (prospective) — 3 of 5 clinically significant DOAC bleeding events occurred in rivaroxaban users; no significant VAS pain difference between vitamin K antagonist and DOAC patients.
- Morimoto et al. — prolonged prothrombin time on rivaroxaban associated with higher bleeding risk.
- Hiroshi et al. — tooth-based bleeding incidence 1.65% (dabigatran) vs 3.41% (rivaroxaban), difference not statistically significant.

## 7. Glossary

- **DOAC (Direct Oral Anticoagulant)**: anticoagulant directly inhibiting a single coagulation factor — dabigatran (thrombin/factor IIa), apixaban/edoxaban/rivaroxaban (factor Xa). Predictable pharmacokinetics, fixed dosing, no routine monitoring.
- **VKA (Vitamin K Antagonist)**: e.g. warfarin; indirect multi-factor inhibition, INR-monitored; largely displaced by DOACs.
- **VAS (Visual Analog Scale)**: 10 cm horizontal line, 0 = no pain to 10 = worst imaginable pain; patient marks a point, score read in mm.
- **Bleeding grade 2−1 vs 2−2**: 2−1 = hemostasis achieved same day but requiring >30 min compression; 2−2 = oozing appearing on or after the following day, controlled by simple compression. The split separates immediate from delayed bleeding.
- **Bone exposure**: visible alveolar bone not covered by mucosa at the socket, here scored binary at day 7.
- **STROBE**: Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology — reporting checklist for observational designs.
- **Single-blind (this study)**: operator and outcome assessor blinded to group allocation; patients unblinded by necessity.
