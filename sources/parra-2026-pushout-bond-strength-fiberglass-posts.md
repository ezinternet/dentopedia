## Why Ingested

기존 위키의 fiber-post push-out 클러스터([[resin-bonding/dhawan-2026-bonding-total-self-etch-fiberpost]] — 접착시스템 종류축, [[resin-bonding/el-azzouni-2026-laser-hydrogen-peroxide-fiber-post-dual-cure]] — 포스트 표면처리축)는 시멘트/포스트 변수를 다뤘지만, 근관 세정 프로토콜(NaOCl 활성화 방식) 변수는 비어 있었다. 본 in-vitro 연구(Parra 2026)는 그 축을 직접 비교(mechanical vs ultrasonic NaOCl 활성화)하며, 자체 Discussion에서 TheraCem의 기질 무관 높은 결합강도를 [[resin/mahrous-2020-mdp-caf-self-adhesive-resin-cement-microshear]]를 인용해 뒷받침한다.

## Three-line Summary

In vitro experimental study — 14 extracted human premolars sectioned into 42 root-disc samples (21 per group), comparing push-out bond strength of prefabricated fiberglass posts cemented with TheraCem self-adhesive resin cement after intracanal NaOCl irrigant activation via mechanical rotary brush vs ultrasonic scaler.

Ultrasonic activation produced numerically higher mean push-out bond strength than mechanical activation (13.60±5.10 MPa vs 11.62±4.13 MPa), but the difference was not statistically significant (p=0.1745); both groups showed highest resistance in the apical third (ultrasonic apical 15.00±3.11 MPa, the highest value overall).

Clinically, the two NaOCl activation methods appear comparable for fiber-post bond strength within the limits of this small in-vitro study (n=14 teeth), though ultrasonic activation may be preferable when thorough apical-third canal cleaning is a clinical priority.

## 세줄요약

In vitro 실험연구 — 발거된 사람 소구치 14개를 42개 치근 디스크 시료(그룹당 21개)로 절단, 근관세정 NaOCl 활성화 방식(기계식 로터리 브러시 vs 초음파 스케일러) 후 TheraCem 자가접착 레진시멘트로 합착한 기성 파이버글라스 포스트의 푸시아웃 결합강도 (Push-out Bond Strength, PBS)를 비교.

초음파 활성화군의 평균 결합강도가 기계식군보다 수치상 높았으나(13.60±5.10 MPa vs 11.62±4.13 MPa), 통계적으로 유의하지 않음(p=0.1745); 양 군 모두 치근단부(apical third)에서 최고 결합강도(초음파 치근단 15.00±3.11 MPa, 전체 최고값).

임상적으로 본 소규모 in-vitro 연구(치아 n=14) 범위 내에서 두 NaOCl 활성화 방식의 결합강도는 유의한 차이가 없어 대등하게 간주할 수 있으며, 치근단부 세정이 특히 중요한 경우 초음파 활성화를 고려할 수 있다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Directly compares mechanical (rotary brush) vs ultrasonic NaOCl irrigant activation for its downstream effect on fiber-post cementation bond strength — a variable not previously isolated in the wiki's fiber-post push-out cluster.
- Confirms the apical-third bond-strength advantage reported elsewhere (thinner cement layer, greater post-to-dentin contact), with the single highest value (15.00 MPa) occurring in the ultrasonic-activated apical third.
- Provides a practical, low-cost NaOCl activation protocol comparison (chairside-feasible Versa Brush vs Biosonic ultrasonic scaler) using clinically representative TheraCem cementation.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: in vitro experimental
- **n**: 14 extracted human premolars → 42 root-disc samples (21 per group: mechanical vs ultrasonic NaOCl activation)
- **Protocol**: standardized endodontic prep (step-back technique, 5% NaOCl, gutta-percha/MTA Fillapex obturation), DT Light-Post IllusionX-RO fiberglass post, TheraCem self-adhesive cement, 30-day aging in 36°C distilled water, four transverse cuts per tooth (cervical/middle/apical thirds)
- **Outcomes**: maximum push-out bond strength (MPa) by activation method and root third (cervical/middle/apical); failure mode (adhesive/cohesive to dentin/post) via stereomicroscopy
- **Statistics**: two-factor ANOVA (zone × activation type), 95% CI, alpha 0.05; sample size pre-calculated by G*Power (effect size 1.78, power 0.8)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Overall: ultrasonic 13.60±5.10 MPa vs mechanical 11.62±4.13 MPa — **not statistically significant** (p=0.1745)
- Apical third: mechanical 12.10±5.25 MPa vs ultrasonic 15.00±3.11 MPa (highest value among all cuts in either group)
- Cervical third: mechanical 11.71±1.92 MPa vs ultrasonic 12.28±4.33 MPa
- Middle third: mechanical 11.03±4.98 MPa vs ultrasonic 13.51±7.35 MPa
- Failure mode: adhesive failure predominated in both groups, with some cohesive failures also observed

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Primary comparison (activation method) did not reach statistical significance — results should be read as "comparable," not as demonstrating ultrasonic superiority.
- Small sample (14 teeth, 7 per group before sectioning into 21 discs per group) limits power for subgroup (root-third) comparisons.
- Authors note a compressive-strength mismatch between the artificial support materials used and native bone, though they consider this non-critical given the study's experimental (bench) nature.
- In vitro push-out testing cannot fully replicate intraoral thermal/mechanical cycling or long-term clinical function; no clinical follow-up data.

## 6. Related Work
- Mahrous et al. 2020 (cited directly in Discussion; see [[resin/mahrous-2020-mdp-caf-self-adhesive-resin-cement-microshear]]) — reports TheraCem's substrate-independent high bond strength via 10-MDP micromechanical penetration, cited to explain this study's consistently favorable TheraCem bond values regardless of NaOCl activation method.
- Elfarraj et al. 2024 — hypochlorite antibacterial/cleaning effectiveness improves with sonic/ultrasonic activation, consistent with this study's numerically higher (non-significant) ultrasonic bond-strength trend.
- Van der Sluis et al. 2007 — links ultrasonic activation duration and NaOCl concentration to complete dentin smear-layer removal.
- Aveiro et al. 2020 — ultrasonic activation raises irrigant temperature, increasing collagen dissolution and dentin-tubule penetration, offered as a mechanism for the study's highest observed value (15 MPa, ultrasonic apical).
- Kahnamouei et al. — reported apical-third bond strength up to 16.72 MPa vs 4.38 MPa cervically, consistent with this study's apical-third advantage.
- Goracci et al. 2004 — methodological basis for preferring push-out over microtensile testing for post bond-strength measurement.
- García et al. 2019 and Moreno-Preciado et al. 2016 — prior reports of adhesive-failure predominance in fiber-post eviction/push-out testing, consistent with this study's failure-mode findings.

## 7. Glossary
- **Push-out bond strength (PBS)**: mechanical test measuring the force required to dislodge a cemented post from a root-dentin disc via axial loading, expressed in MPa or converted from N.
- **NaOCl activation (mechanical vs ultrasonic)**: supplementary agitation of sodium hypochlorite irrigant during root canal cleaning — mechanical via a rotary brush-type attachment (e.g., Versa Brush), ultrasonic via a piezoelectric scaler tip, both intended to improve irrigant penetration into anatomically inaccessible canal areas beyond passive irrigation.
- **Self-adhesive resin cement**: dual-cure luting cement (here, TheraCem) that bonds to dentin/post surfaces without a separate etch-and-rinse or primer step, via acidic functional monomers.
- **Fiberglass (glass fiber) post**: prefabricated post with elastic modulus similar to dentin, used to restore endodontically treated teeth with substantial coronal structure loss while distributing occlusal stress more favorably than rigid metal posts.
- **Thermocycling/aging**: simulated intraoral aging (here, 30 days in 36°C distilled water) applied before mechanical testing to approximate in-service degradation of the adhesive interface.
- **Root third (cervical/middle/apical)**: standard anatomical division of the root used to localize bond-strength measurements, since cement thickness and dentin tubule density vary along the canal length.
