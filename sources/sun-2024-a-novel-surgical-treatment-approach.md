## Three-line Summary

Single case report (BMC Oral Health 2024): a 34-year-old woman with a complete buccal-palatal vertical root fracture (VRF) of a root-filled, crowned maxillary right first premolar (#14) with a sinus tract, an 8-10 mm narrow palatal pocket and periapical plus palatal bone destruction on CBCT.

The tooth was extracted, the stained fracture was widened (~1 mm wide, 1.5-2 mm deep) with two trapezoidal retention forms, filled with adhesive resin, covered with iRoot BP Plus bioceramic cement (dual layer), and replanted with a 4-week fiber-band splint within about 15 min extraoral; at 24 months the tooth was asymptomatic with ~3 mm probing depth, normal mobility and radiographic/CBCT bone regeneration.

One tooth with one 24-month observation cannot show efficacy or compare against other VRF repairs; the authors themselves say clinical trials are needed, and the first-layer material (called adhesive resin but listed under a brand name) is described inconsistently in the text.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: BMC Oral Health 2024;24(1):489 (published 2024-04-24)
- **DOI**: 10.1186/s12903-024-04268-9
- **Institution**: School & Hospital of Stomatology, Wuhan University (State Key Laboratory of Oral & Maxillofacial Reconstruction and Regeneration), Wuhan, China

## 2. Key Contributions
- Proposes a "dual-layered" repair for a posterior-tooth VRF: adhesive resin in the widened fracture line plus two trapezoidal retention forms for fixation, then an iRoot BP Plus bioceramic cement layer over the resin and in the retrograde apical cavity for bioactivity, performed by intentional replantation.
- Rationale stated by the authors: resin bonds well but is poorly bioactive for periodontal healing; bioceramic is bioactive but bonds weakly; the combination targets both, and the authors note prior reported repairs were mostly of anterior teeth.
- Reports a single premolar with 24-month periodontal healing; explicitly asks for further clinical trials.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: case report (n = 1 tooth, 1 patient), 24-month review.
- **Diagnosis**: sinus tract near apex, 8-10 mm narrow isolated palatal pocket, vertical percussion sensitivity, radiograph and CBCT showing apical/palatal bone destruction and buccal-palatal fracture lines.
- **Procedure**: 2% articaine with epinephrine; gentle extraction; root stained with methylene blue and examined under a microscope; 3 mm apical resection with a high-speed handpiece; fracture widened to ~1 mm width and 1.5-2 mm depth; two trapezoidal retention forms (1 mm wide, 1.5-2 mm deep) on both sides of the fracture; 3 mm retrograde apical cavity by ultrasonic tip; fracture and retention forms filled with "self-etching light-curing adhesive resin (Ketac Molar Easymix; 3M ESPE)"; 0.5-1 mm of surface resin removed; resin surface and apical canal filled with iRoot BP Plus; replantation and elastic fixation with a fiber band for 4 weeks; total procedure within 15 min; saline coolant.
- **Outcomes**: symptoms, gingival mucosa, probing depth, mobility, radiograph and CBCT.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- 24-month review: asymptomatic, normal gingival mucosa, about 3 mm probing depth (versus 8-10 mm palatal pocket pre-operatively), normal mobility; radiograph and CBCT described as showing significant periodontal bone regeneration (periapical defect healed).
- No numeric bone-fill measurements, no intermediate follow-up and no resorption/ankylosis assessment are reported in the text.
- Discussion: the authors attribute the result partly to retention forms, bioactive iRoot BP Plus and a short extraoral time (<= 15 min here; they state > 30 min raises the chance of root resorption, citing prior work).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- n = 1, single operator/centre, single 24-month observation; no control or comparison with other VRF repairs; success cannot be generalised.
- The paper calls the first-layer material a "self-etching light-curing adhesive resin" while naming Ketac Molar Easymix (3M ESPE) as the product; the text does not reconcile this, so the actual first-layer material class is uncertain and is flagged here rather than corrected.
- The text says the tooth had been root-treated and crowned 2 years earlier but does not describe how the full crown was handled or what restoration was placed at replantation.
- Authors state further clinical trials are needed for long-term results.

## 6. Related Work
- Corbella 2025 (systematic review of surgical VRF techniques): low-quality case-series evidence only; no standard; case reports were excluded from that review.
- Salihu 2024 and Liao 2021: narrative reviews of the same repair literature (bonding + intentional replantation).
- Zhang 2025 (intentional replantation SR+MA, periapical periodontitis): general replantation prognosis context.

## 7. Glossary
- **Vertical root fracture (VRF)**: longitudinal fracture running along the root axis, often through the canal; extraction is the usual outcome.
- **Intentional replantation**: atraumatic extraction, extraoral treatment, and reinsertion of the same tooth.
- **iRoot BP Plus**: premixed calcium silicate-based bioceramic cement (root-repair material).
- **Retention form**: small mechanical groove cut to hold a filling material in place.
- **Dual-layered repair**: resin layer for fixation under a bioceramic layer for bioactivity.
