## Why Ingested

A practice-based network study (64 general practices, not a specialty clinic) that grounds the restoration-choice literature in [[post-and-core/fathi-2022-prosthetic-restoration-endodontically-treated-teeth-umbrella]] with real-world failure predictors — full-coverage restoration, proximal contact, periodontal health, and specialist-performed endodontics — that individual RCT/lab studies rarely capture together.

## Three-line Summary

This retrospective cohort study from the PEARL Network enrolled 1,298 patients across 64 general dental practices to evaluate the three- to five-year outcomes of definitive restorations placed after primary endodontic therapy, assessing factors associated with restorative success and failure.

Overall restorative failure rate was 13.9% (181/1,298; 95% CI, 12.1-15.8%), with 6.2% needing replacement, 4.4% replaced, and 3.4% presenting cracks or fractures. Multivariate analysis identified that molars, full-coverage restorations, presence of proximal contacts, good periodontal health, non-Hispanic ethnicity, specialist-performed endodontics, and older age were associated with restorative success.

These findings from private practice settings provide an evidence base to guide clinical decisions when planning restorations for endodontically treated teeth, highlighting the importance of full-coverage crowns and maintaining proximal contacts.

## 세줄요약

본 후향적 코호트 연구는 PEARL Network의 일반 치과 64개 진료소에서 근관치료 후 수복술을 받은 환자 1,298명을 등록하여, 3~5년 후 최종 수복물의 성공 및 실패 관련 인자를 평가하였다.

전체 수복 실패율은 13.9% (181/1,298; 95% CI, 12.1-15.8%)였으며, 수복물 교체 필요 6.2%, 수복물 교체 시행 4.4%, 치아 균열 또는 파절 3.4%로 나타났다. 다변량 분석 결과, 구치, 완전피개 수복물, 인접접촉점 존재, 건강한 치주 상태, 비히스패닉계, 전문의 시행 근관치료, 고령이 수복 성공과 연관되었다.

이 연구는 일반 치과 진료 환경에서 얻은 결과로, 근관치료 치아의 수복 계획 시 임상적 의사결정을 지원하는 근거를 제공하며, 완전피개 수복물과 인접접촉점 유지의 중요성을 강조한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Established a 13.9% restorative failure rate for endodontically treated teeth at 3-5 years in general practice (95% CI, 12.1-15.8%).
- Identified that full-coverage crowns are strongly associated with restorative success (OR 0.25, P < .01).
- Demonstrated that teeth without proximal contacts have significantly higher failure risk (OR for zero contacts vs two contacts = 3.70, P < .01).
- Found that specialist-performed endodontics (vs general practitioner) is associated with lower restorative failure (OR 0.63, P = .04).
- Showed that periodontally compromised teeth (attachment loss) have higher restorative failure risk (OR 2.10, P < .01).
- Identified premolars (OR 1.69, P = .01) and anterior teeth (OR 1.50, P = .10) as higher risk than molars.
- Reported that Hispanic/Latino ethnicity was associated with increased restorative failure (OR 1.81, P = .04).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: Retrospective cohort study conducted within the Practitioners Engaged in Applied Research and Learning (PEARL) Network, a practice-based research network (PBRN).
- Inclusion criteria: Patients ≤70 years who had undergone primary endodontic therapy and definitive restoration in a permanent tooth 3-5 years prior. The earliest-treated tooth in the period was selected as the index tooth. Exclusion criteria: incomplete apices, abutments for removable partial dentures, third molars, active orthodontic treatment.
- Sample: 1,298 eligible patients enrolled from 64 general practices. Median enrollment per practice = 11 patients (range: 1-100).
- Data collection: P-Is completed demographic forms; clinical data included pain on percussion, restoration status, proximal contacts, cracks/fractures, caries, periodontitis (clinical attachment level), and radiographs. Patients completed tooth sensitivity assessment and Oral Health Impact Profile.
- Outcome definition: Restorative failure was defined as tooth cracked/fractured, restoration replaced, or restoration judged to need replacement. Failure categories were mutually exclusive.
- Statistics: Multivariate logistic regression with backward elimination at P = .05. Factors significant at P = .10 in univariate analysis were included. Primary endpoint was restorative failure.
- Bias assessment: P-Is may have enrolled patients with favorable outcomes; retrospective design may introduce selection bias. Radiographs were evaluated by masked reviewers (periodontist and endodontist) using the periapical index (Orstavik et al., score ≥3).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Restorative failure rate (overall)** — 13.9% (181/1,298; 95% CI, 12.1-15.8%)
  - Restoration needs replacement: 6.2% (80/1,298)
  - Restoration replaced: 4.4% (57/1,298)
  - Tooth cracked or fractured: 3.4% (44/1,298)
- **Reasons for restoration replacement** (n=65) — Fracture led to replacement in 58% (38), secondary caries 30% (20), mobility 6% (4), other 6% (4).
- **Multivariate factors associated with restorative failure** (multivariate logistic regression):
  - Intracoronal vs full-coverage crown: OR 4.04 (95% CI, 2.79-5.86; P < .01)
  - Zero vs two proximal contacts: OR 3.70 (95% CI, 1.69-8.33; P < .01) [calculated from OR 0.27 inverse]
  - One vs two proximal contacts: OR 1.56 (95% CI, 0.75-3.23; P = .04) [calculated from OR 0.42 inverse]
  - Periodontal attachment loss: OR 2.10 (95% CI, 1.27-3.49; P < .01)
  - Premolar vs molar: OR 1.69 (95% CI, 1.10-2.60; P = .01)
  - Canine/incisor vs molar: OR 1.50 (95% CI, 0.93-2.42; P = .10)
  - Hispanic/Latino ethnicity: OR 1.81 (95% CI, 1.02-3.22; P = .04)
  - Specialist (vs general practitioner): OR 0.63 (95% CI, 0.41-0.99; P = .04)
  - Age (per year): OR 0.98 (95% CI, 0.97-1.00; P = .01)
- **Univariate findings** (not significant in multivariate):
  - Post placement: Metal posts vs no post OR 0.58 (95% CI, 0.41-0.82; P < .01); Fiber posts vs no post OR 0.91 (95% CI, 0.56-1.50; P = .72)
  - Hispanic/Latino vs non-Hispanic: OR 2.64 (95% CI, 1.61-4.31; P < .01)
  - Sex, race, initial pulpal diagnosis, periapical disease, number of visits, time to restoration: not significant (P > .05)
- **Post placement**: 724/1,298 teeth (55.8%) received posts: 80% metal (59% cemented preformed, 11% mechanical retention, 10% cast), 20% fiber (preformed fiber/composite).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Retrospective design may introduce selection bias; patients lost to follow-up could affect generalizability.
- Practices were predominantly suburban in the northeastern U.S., limiting generalizability to broader populations.
- P-Is may have selectively enrolled patients with favorable outcomes (though 19.1% endodontic failure rate mitigates this).
- Small number of Hispanic/Latino patients (89, 7%) may have led to site-effect bias.
- Post type analysis limited by small sample size for fiber posts (145 vs 579 metal posts).
- Confounding by indication: practitioners may choose less expensive intracoronal restorations for teeth with guarded prognosis.
- Findings on age and ethnicity may reflect unmeasured confounders.
- The study was conducted by volunteer practitioners, who may not represent the dental profession at large.

## 7. Glossary

- **PEARL Network (Practitioners Engaged in Applied Research and Learning)**: A dental practice-based research network (PBRN) established to conduct clinical studies in private practice settings.
- **Intracoronal restoration**: A restoration placed within the coronal portion of the tooth without cuspal coverage (e.g., amalgam, composite resin).
- **Full-coverage crown**: A restoration that covers the entire clinical crown, providing cuspal protection and a ferrule effect.
- **Ferrule**: A band of metal or ceramic that encircles the tooth structure at the crown margin, providing structural reinforcement and resistance to fracture.
- **Proximal contact**: The point of contact between adjacent teeth in the same arch.
- **Clinical attachment loss (CAL)**: A measure of periodontal destruction, defined as the distance from the cementoenamel junction to the base of the periodontal pocket.
- **Periapical index (PAI)**: A radiographic scoring system (0-5) for assessing apical periodontitis (Orstavik et al., 1986).
- **PEARL Network P-I (Practitioner-Investigator)**: A general dentist participating in the PEARL Network who enrolled patients and collected data for this study.
