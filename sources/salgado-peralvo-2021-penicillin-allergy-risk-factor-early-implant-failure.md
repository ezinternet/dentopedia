## Why Ingested

The wiki's implant-antibiotic evidence ([[wiki/drug/antibiotics/momand-2024-antibiotic-prophylaxis-early-implant-failure]], [[wiki/drug/antibiotics/torof-2023-antibiotic-dental-implant-procedures-sr-ma]]) is built almost entirely on amoxicillin-vs-placebo trials and routinely names clindamycin as the fallback for penicillin-allergic patients without examining whether that fallback carries its own failure risk. This SR isolates exactly that gap — five studies of implants placed under clindamycin cover in penicillin-allergic patients vs amoxicillin in non-allergic patients — and it pairs directly with [[wiki/drug/antibiotics/thornhill-2019-adverse-reactions-oral-antibiotics-dentists]]'s warning against the reflex "penicillin allergy → clindamycin" substitution.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (no meta-analysis; Medline + Web of Science, PRISMA, JBI critical appraisal) of 5 studies (4 observational cohorts, 1 retrospective case-control) comparing early dental implant failure in penicillin-allergic patients given clindamycin vs non-allergic patients given amoxicillin.

Penicillin-allergic patients on clindamycin showed markedly higher failure (e.g. 24.68% vs 8.03%, RR 3.84; 2.10% vs 0.80%, OR 3.10) and up to six-fold higher infection risk; immediate implants in these patients failed 5.7–10 times more often; 4 of 5 studies used self-reported penicillin allergy (SRPA) without confirmatory testing.

Penicillin allergy per se cannot be called a risk factor because the allergy was mostly unverified; the signal is best read as clindamycin being the likely culprit, so the authors advise allergy testing plus, in confirmed cases, avoiding clindamycin in favour of azithromycin 500 mg 1 h preoperatively.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰(메타분석 없음; Medline + Web of Science, PRISMA, JBI 비뚤림 평가) — 5편(관찰코호트 4·후향적 환자대조 1)으로 페니실린 알레르기 환자(클린다마이신 투여)와 비알레르기 환자(아목시실린 투여)의 조기 임플란트 실패율 비교.

페니실린 알레르기 + 클린다마이신군의 실패율이 뚜렷이 높았고(예: 24.68% vs 8.03%, RR 3.84; 2.10% vs 0.80%, OR 3.10), 감염 위험은 최대 6배, 즉시 임플란트는 5.7~10배 실패 — 단 5편 중 4편은 자가보고 페니실린 알레르기(Self-Reported Penicillin Allergy, SRPA)로 확진 검사가 없었다.

알레르기가 대부분 미확진이라 "페니실린 알레르기 자체"를 위험인자로 단정할 수 없으며, 신호의 실체는 클린다마이신일 가능성이 크다 — 저자들은 알레르기 확진 검사 후 확진 시 클린다마이신 대신 아지트로마이신 500 mg 술전 1시간 투여를 권고.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Antibiotics (Basel) 2021;10(10):1227
- **DOI**: 10.3390/antibiotics10101227
- **PMID / PMCID**: 34680808 / PMC8532851
- **Institution**: Complutense University of Madrid / University of Seville, Spain
- **Design**: Systematic review without meta-analysis (search updated to March 2021)

## 2. Key Contributions
- First systematic review to ask specifically whether penicillin-allergic implant patients — who are denied amoxicillin — carry a higher early-failure risk, rather than re-analysing amoxicillin-vs-placebo data.
- Separates two confounded explanations: penicillin allergy as an intrinsic (possibly HLA-linked) patient factor vs clindamycin as a suboptimal prophylactic agent; argues the evidence points at clindamycin.
- Proposes a concrete alternative protocol — azithromycin 500 mg 1 h preoperatively — with a pharmacokinetic/anti-inflammatory rationale (higher crevicular-fluid levels, lower G-CSF/IL-6/IL-8 during early healing).
- Assembles mechanistic support for clindamycin harm: high in-vitro resistance of peri-implantitis pathogens (~46.7%), MRSA selection, and dose-dependent suppression of osteoblast alkaline phosphatase / matrix calcification.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review (PRISMA), no meta-analysis performed.
- **Databases**: Medline/PubMed and Web of Science; Google Scholar and reference lists hand-searched; not time-restricted, updated to March 2021.
- **MeSH terms**: (penicillin allergy OR clindamycin OR erythromycin OR azithromycin OR metronidazole) AND (dental implant OR dental implant failure OR dental implant complications).
- **Screening**: two independent reviewers, third-reviewer consensus; corresponding authors contacted to clarify whether allergy was tested or self-reported.
- **Risk of bias**: Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Tool, 10 domains; low quality 0–5 domains, high quality 5–10 domains.
- **n**: 5 studies included (from 111 Medline + 202 WoS titles/abstracts). 4 observational cohort studies + 1 retrospective case-control. Allergy testing confirmed in only 1 study; the other 4 used self-reported penicillin allergy (SRPA).
- **PICO**: In penicillin-allergic patients not prescribed amoxicillin, is there an increased risk of dental implant failure vs non-allergic patients?

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Verbatim per-study findings:

- **Salomó-Coll et al. (2018)** — implant-level failure 8.03% (non-allergic) vs 24.68% (SRPA), "i.e., one in four implants failed" (p = 0.032), RR 3.84; clindamycin prescribed in 100% of SRPA patients. In SRPA, 21.05% of failures were late and 78.95% early (early failure = osseointegration failure 80% / uncontrolled infection 20%). Patient-level failure 5.17% (non-allergic) vs 18.86% (SRPA) (p = 0.046), RR 3.64.
- **French et al. (2015)** — "twice the risk" in confirmed penicillin-allergic patients on clindamycin vs amoxicillin, hazard ratio HR = 2.16; not significant (p = 0.11) due to few allergic patients and low overall failure (0.70%). Authors suggest avoiding immediate placement when penicillin cannot be given, placing implants on a delayed basis.
- **French et al. (2016)** — failure 0.80% (non-allergic; 53.80% early) vs 2.10% (SRPA; 80% early), significant (p = 0.002), OR = 3.10. Survival at 1/5/10 y: 99.50/98.90/98.40% (non-allergic) vs 98.10/97.30/97.30% (SRPA) (p < 0.002). Postoperative infection 0.60% vs 3.40% — "the risk in allergic patients who were prescribed clindamycin was six times higher" (p < 0.05). Immediate implants (n = 687; 12.30%): failure 1% in non-allergic (n = 630) vs 10.50% in SRPA — "10 times higher" (p < 0.001).
- **Wagenberg and Froum (2006)** — "5.70 times higher risk of immediate implant failure secondary to infection" in SRPA on clindamycin (8.52%) vs non-allergic on amoxicillin (2.95%), significant (p < 0.001), RR = 3.34.
- **Block et al. (2021)** — retrospective case-control, 224 patients with ≥1 implant failure; logistic regression: significant association between SRPA and failure within 1 year of placement (OR = 2.98), but not between years 1–4 or after 4 years. Preventive antibiotics not specified.

JBI quality: 4 of 5 studies high-quality (5–10 domains), 1 low-quality.

Discussion-level figures (supporting studies, not among the 5 included):
- Basma et al. (2021): post-op infection after socket grafting 10.7% (clindamycin) vs 2.7% (amoxicillin), OR 4.5; ridge augmentation 22.5% vs 4.2%, OR 6.9 — "risk of infection in these regenerative procedures after clindamycin administration is 5.5 times higher."
- Khoury et al. (2018): subantral graft infection in 0.48% of all patients — all SRPA (6% of SRPA patients).
- Immediate placement per se: early failure 5.1% vs 1.1% for delayed (6 months post-extraction) (p = 0.02).
- ~10–20% of patients report penicillin allergy; 80–99% are no longer allergic after testing; HLA-B*55:01 carriers have 33% higher relative risk of penicillin allergy.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Very few studies (5), no RCTs, substantial clinical heterogeneity → results must be interpreted with caution; no meta-analysis possible.
- 4 of 5 studies relied on self-reported (unconfirmed) penicillin allergy — allergy status and antibiotic choice are confounded, so the review cannot separate "allergy" from "clindamycin."
- Two included studies did not specify which (or whether) preventive antibiotics were given (Block 2021; and antibiotic type unstated in some bone-loss analyses).
- Authors call for RCTs comparing clindamycin vs amoxicillin in non-allergic patients, and clindamycin vs azithromycin in test-confirmed penicillin-allergic patients, plus study of allergy-associated polymorphisms and osseointegration.

## 6. Related Work
- Momand et al. (2024) — stricter SR+MA (blinded placebo-controlled RCTs) finds routine amoxicillin prophylaxis does not reduce early failure in healthy patients; this review extends the question to the allergic subgroup and the alternative drug.
- Torof et al. (2023) — SR+MA recommending preoperative amoxicillin 2 g with clindamycin 600 mg as the penicillin-allergy alternative; this review qualifies that fallback.
- Thornhill et al. (2019) — NHS/MHRA ADR data showing clindamycin has the highest fatal-reaction rate and warning against the reflex "penicillin allergy → clindamycin" substitution.
- Salgado-Peralvo et al. (2022) consensus on preventive antibiotic therapy in dental implant surgery — the authors' subsequent consensus statement developing this review's azithromycin recommendation.

## 7. Glossary
- **SRPA**: Self-Reported Penicillin Allergy — patient-declared allergy without confirmatory skin/challenge testing; ~80–99% are not truly allergic on testing.
- **Early implant failure**: loss of the implant before functional/prosthetic loading, due to failure of osseointegration (local/systemic factors); ~5% of all failures, more frequent in women and younger patients.
- **Late implant failure**: loss after osseointegration and loading, usually bacterial infection, parafunction, or mechanical/prosthetic factors.
- **JBI Critical Appraisal Tool**: Joanna Briggs Institute 10-domain instrument for methodological quality of prevalence/observational studies.
- **Clindamycin**: lincosamide antibiotic historically used as first-line prophylaxis in penicillin-allergic dental patients; associated here with higher implant failure/infection and, in other data, with C. difficile colitis and osteoblast toxicity.
- **Azithromycin**: macrolide; proposed alternative (500 mg 1 h preoperatively) with higher gingival/peri-implant crevicular-fluid concentrations and an anti-inflammatory profile during early healing.
