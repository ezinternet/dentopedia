## Why Ingested

심혈관질환(고혈압·고콜레스테롤혈증) 위험군 환자가 복용하는 심장 관련 약물(RAAS 억제제 ACEI/ARB, 스타틴)이 치주 상태에 미치는 영향을 다룬 SR+MA. 기존 [[oral-medicine/ramirez-martinez-acitores-2020-antihypertensive-xerostomia-salivary-flow-sr]]가 항고혈압제의 타액분비/구강건조 부작용을 다뤘다면, 본 논문은 같은 약물군이 치주조직 염증·치주염 위험에 미치는 직접적 영향을 정량화하여 "심장약 복용 환자의 치과 고려사항" 지식을 보강한다.

## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42024583905; PRISMA 2020; 6 studies qualitative, 4 meta-analyzed) evaluating how RAAS inhibitors (ACEI/ARB) and statins affect periodontal status in cardiovascular-risk patients (hypertension vs hypercholesterolemia).

ACEI/ARB effects on periodontal status in hypertensive patients were inconsistent, whereas systemic statin therapy significantly reduced bleeding-on-probing (−13.4%, p=0.007) and probing depth (−0.38mm, p<0.00001) in hypercholesterolemic patients.

The authors themselves judge the statistically significant PD reduction (−0.38mm) not clinically significant given the absence of a validated MCID; proposed RAAS-inhibitor harm mechanisms (ACEI kinin/Th1 polarization; ARB hyposalivation) remain at the level of isolated primary studies.

## 세줄요약

SR+MA(PROSPERO CRD42024583905; PRISMA 2020; 정성 6편·메타분석 4편) — 심혈관 위험군(고혈압 vs 고콜레스테롤혈증)에서 RAAS 억제제(ACEI/ARB)·스타틴이 치주 상태에 미치는 영향을 평가.

고혈압 환자에서 ACEI/ARB의 치주 영향은 비일관적이었던 반면, 전신 스타틴 요법은 고콜레스테롤혈증 환자의 탐침시출혈(BOP −13.4%, p=0.007)과 치주낭깊이(PD −0.38mm, p<0.00001)를 유의하게 감소시킴.

다만 검증된 최소임상중요차이(MCID)가 없어 저자들 스스로 통계적으로 유의한 PD −0.38mm를 임상적으로 유의하지 않다고 판단; RAAS 억제제 유해 기전(ACEI 키닌/Th1, ARB 타액저하)은 개별 일차연구 수준에 머묾.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First umbrella-adjacent synthesis to jointly evaluate two distinct cardiac-medication classes (RAAS inhibitors and statins) against periodontal status outcomes in disease-matched populations (hypertension vs hypercholesterolemia), rather than treating "antihypertensive" as one undifferentiated exposure.
- Quantifies statin effect on periodontal parameters in patients using statins as systemic hypercholesterolemia therapy (not as a post-periodontal-treatment adjunct, which is the more commonly studied scenario per Muniz et al. and Greethurst et al.).
- Surfaces mechanistic candidates for RAAS-inhibitor harm (ACEI-related kinin/Th1 polarization pathway; ARB-related hyposalivation) that had previously been reported only in isolated primary studies (Rodrigues et al., Chatzopoulos et al., Wang et al., Pająk-Łysek et al.).
- Explicitly flags the absence of a validated minimum clinically important difference (MCID) for probing depth/BOP reduction — the statistically significant PD finding (-0.38mm) is judged not clinically significant by the authors themselves.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review + meta-analysis of retrospective/cross-sectional/case-control clinical studies (not RCT-restricted).
- Databases: PubMed, ProQuest, EBSCO, SpringerLink.
- PICO: Population = hypertension or hypercholesterolemia patients; Intervention = ACEI, ARBs, or statins (systemic administration only); Comparison = other medicated/non-medicated comparator; Outcome = periodontal indices (BOP, plaque index, gingival index), CAL, probing depth (primary); periodontal disease diagnosis (secondary).
- Screening: Rayyan platform, 2 blinded reviewers, no disagreements reported.
- Risk of bias: ROBINS-I (non-randomized studies of interventions).
- Statistics: RevMan 5.4, mean-difference random-effects synthesis, 95% CI, forest plots.
- Yield: 6,669 records → 427 duplicates removed → 6,242 title-screened → 16 abstract-screened → 14 full-text assessed → 6 included qualitatively → 4 meta-analyzed (2 excluded: 1 no mean/SD, 1 no comparator).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**RAAS inhibitors (ACEI/ARB) — hypertension, qualitative only (2 studies, not pooled):**
- Rodrigues et al. (case-control, combination antihypertensive therapy, ACEI mean duration 7.5y vs non-ACEI 6.1y): all periodontal parameters (BOP, PI, CAL, PD) significantly worse in ACEI group, but PD/CAL differences (0.47mm/0.26mm) not clinically significant.
- Ustaoğlu et al. (cross-sectional, single-drug therapy, ~3y duration): no significant difference across ACEI/ARB/CCB except GI (ARB 1.38 vs CCB 1.78 — not clinically significant).
- Supporting external evidence (not part of this review's pooled data, cited in Discussion): Chatzopoulos et al. — ACEI use associated with 4.847× periodontitis risk; Wang et al. — 1.64× risk; Pająk-Łysek et al. — ARB associated with 3.32× periodontitis risk.

**Statins — hypercholesterolemia, meta-analyzed (4 studies):**
- Bleeding on probing (BOP) index: MD -13.4% (95% CI -23.08 to -3.72, p=0.007) — significant, statin < comparator.
- Probing depth (PD): MD -0.38mm (95% CI -0.53 to -0.23, p<0.00001) — statistically significant, but authors judge not clinically significant (no established MCID; a 1mm PD change is only clinically meaningful when it crosses the 3mm-healthy/4mm-pathologic threshold).
- Clinical attachment loss (CAL): MD -0.16mm (95% CI -0.40 to -0.07, p=0.17) — not significant.
- Plaque index / gingival index: not pooled (heterogeneous index definitions across studies).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small pooled sample (4 studies for meta-analysis; only 2 for the RAAS-inhibitor qualitative arm — effect "could not be determined").
- No study used the 2017 AAP/EFP periodontitis staging/grading classification; none reported marginal bone loss.
- Non-RCT designs (case-control, cross-sectional, retrospective cohort) throughout — confounding by combination therapy, treatment duration heterogeneity, and study-design heterogeneity limits causal inference for the RAAS-inhibitor arm.
- Statistically significant PD reduction (statins) explicitly flagged by authors as of uncertain clinical significance.
- Authors call for further research controlling treatment duration and restricting to single-drug therapy to isolate RAAS-inhibitor-class effects from confounding antihypertensive combinations.

## 6. Related Work

- Bertl et al. SR+MA — statins improve periodontitis outcomes via bone-promoting/anti-inflammatory/antibacterial mechanisms (cited as prior positive evidence base).
- Kwon et al. — contrary finding that systemic statin use increases periodontitis risk 1.32×.
- Muniz et al. SR+MA — statins as adjunct to non-surgical periodontal therapy (systemic/dentifrice/local routes) in periodontally-treated, not hypercholesterolemic, populations; local-statin meta-analysis showed larger PD/CAL effects (PD -1.93mm, CAL +1.82mm at 6mo) than the present study's systemic-statin findings.
- Tabrizi et al. SR+MA — systemic statin therapy reduces CRP, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1 (mechanistic support).
- [[oral-medicine/ramirez-martinez-acitores-2020-antihypertensive-xerostomia-salivary-flow-sr]] — antihypertensive-induced xerostomia/salivary flow reduction, a related but distinct oral adverse-effect pathway for the same drug classes.

## 7. Glossary

- **RAAS**: Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system.
- **ACEI**: Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor.
- **ARB**: Angiotensin II receptor blocker.
- **BOP**: Bleeding on probing (index).
- **PI**: Plaque index.
- **GI**: Gingival index.
- **CAL**: Clinical attachment loss.
- **PD**: Probing depth.
- **ROBINS-I**: Cochrane Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies of Interventions tool.
- **MCID**: Minimum clinically important difference.
