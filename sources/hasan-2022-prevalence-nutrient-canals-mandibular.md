## Why Ingested

Ingested as part of a topical sweep on mandibular/alveolar nutrient canals (NC) as a radiographic anatomical/diagnostic landmark — a structure the wiki had zero prior coverage of. This cross-sectional study broadens the sibling paper [[radiology/kumar-2014-incidence-nutrient-canals-hypertensive]] (a smaller, single-disease hypertension-only study) into a three-way multi-disease comparison (diabetes mellitus, hypertension, chronic periodontitis vs healthy controls), giving the wiki its first multi-condition NC prevalence dataset.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study (n=200 of 800 screened, India, 10 months) comparing nutrient canal (NC) prevalence on mandibular anterior IOPARs across four groups: healthy controls, diabetes mellitus (DM), hypertension (HTN), and chronic periodontitis.

NC prevalence was significantly higher in disease groups than controls (DM 84% vs control 20%, p=0.000004; HTN 66%, p=0.000003; periodontitis 52%, p=0.00085), with DM showing the highest prevalence overall (inter-group p=0.0028); NC presence correlated with disease duration in DM (p=0.047) and HTN (p=0.012) but not with age or gender in any group.

Elevated NC frequency on routine mandibular anterior IOPARs may serve as an ancillary screening clue for underlying undiagnosed DM/HTN/periodontitis, but the single-center design and reliance on chi-square comparisons without confounder adjustment limit generalizability.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Directly compares nutrient canal (NC) prevalence across three chronic systemic diseases (DM, HTN, chronic periodontitis) plus a healthy control group in a single cohort, rather than the single-disease designs common in prior NC literature.
- Reports a clear DM > HTN > periodontitis prevalence gradient with a statistically significant inter-group comparison (p=0.0028), giving a relative ranking of disease association strength not previously quantified together.
- Identifies disease duration (not age or gender) as significantly associated with NC presence in the DM and HTN cohorts, refining the clinical read on which patient-level factor actually predicts NC visibility.
- Locates most NCs (72.4-74.2%) beyond the root apex (periapical) rather than strictly interproximal, adding anatomic-location detail to the diagnostic landmark description.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Prospective cross-sectional study, single center, 10-month enrollment window (June 2019-April 2020).
- **Population**: 800 subjects screened; 200 enrolled (97 male, 103 female), age 20-70 years, presenting with anterior mandibular pain, deep proximal caries with dentinal involvement, or attrition (radiographs taken as part of routine treatment protocol, no extra radiographs for study purposes).
- **Groups (n=50 each)**: Group 1 healthy controls (no periodontitis signs, negative DM/HTN/systemic disease history); Group 2 DM (>1 year history, on medication); Group 3 HTN (>1 year history, on medication); Group 4 chronic periodontitis (bleeding on probing, pocket depth ≥4mm, plaque/calculus, tooth mobility). Each group subdivided by missing mandibular anterior teeth and interdental bone loss level (none / to middle 1/3 / to apical 1/3).
- **Exclusions**: age <20 or >70y, unwillingness to participate, coexisting DM+HTN+other systemic disease, pregnancy/lactation, poor-quality radiographs.
- **Radiographic technique**: IOPAR of the 43-33 mandibular anterior region, bisecting angle technique, 70 kVp, 10 mA, 0.6 sec exposure, standard size-2 E-speed film, manually processed; NC presence/number/location assessed with a magnifying glass.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 20.0; chi-square test for group comparisons; p<0.05 significance threshold. No confounder adjustment or multivariable modeling reported.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Overall NC prevalence by group**: DM 84% (42/50), HTN 66% (33/50), chronic periodontitis 52% (26/50), healthy controls 20% (10/50).
- **Disease vs control comparisons**: DM vs control p=0.000004; HTN vs control p=0.000003; periodontitis vs control p=0.00085 — all highly significant.
- **Inter-group comparison** (DM vs HTN vs periodontitis): p=0.0028, DM showing the highest NC prevalence.
- **Gender**: no significant association in any group (controls p=0.775; DM p=0.237; HTN p=0.195; periodontitis p=0.768).
- **Age**: no significant association in any group (controls p=0.509; DM p=0.536; HTN p=0.482; periodontitis p=0.090).
- **Disease duration**: significant association with NC presence in DM (p=0.047) and HTN (p=0.012).
- **Anatomic location**: 72.4-74.2% of NCs located beyond the root apex (periapical) rather than interproximal.
- **Edentulous subgroup (DM)**: all 8 DM patients missing lower anterior teeth showed NC presence.
- **Bone loss association**: DM and periodontitis groups showed the highest NC counts at more advanced interdental bone loss (apical 1/3); HTN group showed the highest NC counts at moderate (middle 1/3) bone loss.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single-center study — authors explicitly note results may not represent the broader population; recommend multicentric studies with larger samples.
- Small per-group sample size (n=50 per group, 200 total) limits precision of subgroup (age-band, bone-loss-level) comparisons.
- Statistical analysis limited to chi-square tests; no adjustment for potential confounders (e.g., smoking, oral hygiene status, medication effects) that could independently influence NC visibility.
- Radiographic detection of NC is inherently reader-dependent (magnifying-glass visual assessment) with no reported inter-observer reliability/kappa statistic.
- Cross-sectional design cannot establish causality between systemic disease and NC formation — only association; the vascular-collateralization mechanisms proposed (Isselbacher et al. for DM, Haslett et al. for HTN) are cited from prior physiology literature, not directly tested here.
- Gender and age associations for NC prevalence remain inconsistent across the wider literature this paper reviews (some studies report female predominance, others male, others age-related increase) — this study adds a non-significant data point to an already mixed evidence base.

## 6. Related Work

- Cites Hirschfield (1923) for the first radiographic description of nutrient canals.
- Cites multiple prior NC prevalence studies with widely varying control-group rates (Lovette 92%, Bali et al. 60%, Selarka et al. 35%, Patel et al. 42.5%, Kaur et al. 23%, Kaul et al. 57.5%, Pandarinath et al. 6%) — this study's 20% control prevalence sits in the middle of that range.
- Cites Isselbacher et al. for the proposed mechanism linking insulin deficiency/atherosclerosis to collateral vessel formation (higher NC in DM).
- Cites Haslett et al. for the proposed mechanism linking hypertensive arteriosclerosis/arteriolar changes to collateral vessel formation (higher NC in HTN).
- In this wiki: extends [[radiology/kumar-2014-incidence-nutrient-canals-hypertensive]], which examined NC prevalence in hypertensive patients only — this paper broadens that single-disease finding into a three-disease (DM/HTN/periodontitis) comparative framework and adds the DM > HTN > periodontitis prevalence ranking that the earlier single-disease study could not establish.

## 7. Glossary

- **Nutrient canal (NC)**: a radiolucent linear passage visible on intraoral radiographs, containing a neurovascular bundle (blood vessels, lymph vessels, nerves), most often seen on mandibular anterior IOPARs.
- **IOPAR (Intra-Oral Periapical Radiograph)**: a standard intraoral dental X-ray capturing the entire tooth (crown to root apex) and surrounding bone, used here to visualize NCs.
- **Bisecting angle technique**: an intraoral radiographic positioning technique that aims the X-ray beam perpendicular to an imaginary bisector between the film plane and the long axis of the tooth, used to minimize image distortion.
- **Chronic periodontitis (in this study)**: defined clinically by bleeding on probing, gingival recession, probing pocket depth ≥4mm, presence of plaque/calculus, and tooth mobility.
- **Interdental bone loss grading**: categorized in this study as none, up to the middle third of the root, or up to the apical third of the root, based on radiographic bone level relative to the root.
