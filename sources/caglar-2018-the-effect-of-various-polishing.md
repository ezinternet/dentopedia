## Why Ingested

Serves the **post-adjustment polishing protocol** axis of the 2026-11-12 `zirconia-occlusal-adjustment` surveillance topic: it is a direct three-arm comparison of zirconia-specific versus porcelain polishing kits after simulated diamond-bur occlusal adjustment, with profilometric surface roughness (Ra) and XRD phase analysis. Complements [[dental-materials/zirconia/mohammadi-bassir-2017-grinding-overglazing-polishing-zirconia]], which tested the same grind→polish sequence but added flexural strength as an outcome.

## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (J Adv Prosthodont 2018, Turkish group; 100 monolithic zirconia disks, n=20/group) simulating chairside occlusal adjustment with a diamond bur (2 × 10 s, water-cooled) and then comparing three polishing systems — Meisinger zirconia kit, EVE Diacera zirconia kit, EVE Diapol porcelain kit — by profilometry surface roughness (Ra), SEM, and XRD phase analysis.

Grinding raised Ra to 1.77 µm; the zirconia polishing kits reduced it to 0.28 µm (Meisinger) and 0.23 µm (EVE Diacera), while the porcelain kit reached only 0.78 µm (all polishing groups smoother than grinding, p<.05; Meisinger vs EVE Diacera p>.05); XRD showed no tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation in any group.

Clinically, chairside adjustment should be finished with a zirconia-specific polishing kit rather than a porcelain kit, and no phase transformation is expected when total polishing time is held short (~3 min) under water cooling — an in vitro study that did not measure flexural strength or wear.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: The effect of various polishing systems on surface roughness and phase transformation of monolithic zirconia
- **Authors**: Ipek Caglar, Sabit Melih Ates, Zeynep Yesil Duymus
- **Journal**: The Journal of Advanced Prosthodontics, 2018;10(2):132-139
- **DOI**: 10.4047/jap.2018.10.2.132
- **PMID / PMCID**: 29713434 / PMC5917105
- **Study type**: in vitro bench study (surface characterization, no mechanical or wear testing)
- **Setting**: Yeditepe/Istanbul (Turkish group); CAD/CAM milled monolithic zirconia (Katana Zirconia HT, Kuraray-Noritake)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantifies the roughness penalty of simulated occlusal adjustment on monolithic zirconia: Ra 1.77 µm after a single diamond bur used for 20 s under water coolant.
- Provides a same-protocol, three-arm comparison of **two zirconia-specific** polishing systems against **one porcelain** polishing system — a material-matching argument rather than a brand argument.
- Tests the polishing-time → phase-transformation axis directly: total polishing was capped at 3 minutes per specimen to avoid the local heating that prior work linked to the tetragonal→monoclinic (t→m) transformation.
- Reports negative XRD findings for both the grinding step and the polishing step, i.e. adjustment + short polishing is phase-safe in this model.
- Reference point for the widely cited Ra = 0.2 µm plaque-retention threshold (Bollen) applied to adjusted zirconia.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Specimens**: 100 disks, 10 mm diameter × 3 mm thickness, milled from presintered Katana Zirconia HT (Kuraray-Noritake) on a Yenadent DC40, sintered at 1500 °C for 7 h, then standardized with a silicon carbide rotary abrasive and 500/1500-grit papers.
- **Adjustment simulation**: 80 specimens ground with diamond rotary instrument 6830RL (Komet-Brasseler) in a high-speed Ti-Max X600L (NSK) handpiece, twice for 10 s (20 s total) under water coolant, same examiner.
- **Groups (n=20 each)**: C = untreated control; G = ground, unpolished; M = ground + Meisinger "Luster for zirconia" intraoral adjustment kit; E = ground + EVE Diacera zirconia kit; P = ground + EVE Diapol **porcelain** kit.
- **Polishing protocol**: each of the 3 instruments per kit applied 30 s in one direction, then 30 s at 90°, at 80% of the manufacturer's maximum RPM with a low-speed handpiece; total polishing ~3 min per specimen, same examiner.
- **Cleaning**: air-water spray 15 s, ultrasonic bath 1 min in distilled water, air dried.
- **Outcomes**: Ra (µm) by profilometer (Perthometer M2, Mahr), 3 measurements per specimen, arithmetic mean; surface topography by SEM at ×1000 (Zeiss EVO LS 10); phase composition by XRD (Rigaku Smartlab) with CuKα, 0.01° steps between 20 and 40 2θ, evaluating the monoclinic peak-intensity ratio.
- **Statistics**: power analysis (effect size 2; alpha .05; beta .20) → n=20/group; Shapiro-Wilk normality; one-way ANOVA + Tukey post hoc at P<.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Group | Treatment | Ra (µm) |
|---|---|---|
| G | Ground only (diamond bur 20 s) | 1.77 |
| P | Ground + EVE Diapol (porcelain kit) | 0.78 |
| M | Ground + Meisinger zirconia kit | 0.28 |
| E | Ground + EVE Diacera zirconia kit | 0.23 |

- Polishing system had a statistically significant effect on Ra (P<.05); **every** polishing group was significantly smoother than group G (P<.05).
- Both zirconia kits (M, E) were significantly smoother than the porcelain kit (P<.05); M vs E was **not** significant (P>.05).
- SEM: control surfaces showed periodic CAD/CAM milling texture; grinding produced parallel deep scratches along the bur path; polishing progressively removed them, but deep grinding grooves were not entirely eliminated and striations remained in group P.
- XRD: all groups produced similar diffraction patterns — neither the simulated occlusal adjustment nor the polishing sequence caused a t→m phase transformation.
- Threshold context: Ra = 0.2 µm (Bollen) cited as the bacterial-retention threshold. The paper reads M (0.28 µm) and E (0.23 µm) as clinically acceptable and P (0.78 µm) as above threshold — note that the reported means for M and E are in fact still numerically above 0.2 µm, so the "acceptable" verdict rests on the group comparison, not on crossing the threshold.
- Rationale offered for the porcelain kit's inferiority: porcelain polishers contain abrasives softer than zirconia, so they cannot cut a zirconia surface effectively.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single zirconia block type (Katana Zirconia HT); no coloring/staining, so shaded or multilayer materials are untested.
- **Flexural strength and wear were not measured** — the paper cannot say whether the polished surfaces preserve strength or reduce antagonist enamel wear.
- Strictly in vitro; no thermocycling, aging/LTD, saliva, or cyclic loading, so clinical adjustment conditions are only approximated.
- Polishing parameters (RPM, load, time) remain unstandardized across the literature, which the authors themselves flag as a barrier to cross-study comparison; one operator and 3 min total polishing were used deliberately.
- Only one specimen per group was examined by SEM, so the topographic observations are illustrative rather than quantified.
- The authors call for structured in vivo comparisons and for work linking polisher abrasive composition to polishing performance.

## 6. Related Work

- Mohammadi-Bassir et al. 2017 — same grind→polish question on Y-TZP, with flexural strength added; concludes polishing (not overglazing) remediates grinding damage.
- Tachibana et al. 2021 — polished Y-TZP minimized antagonist enamel wear across three contact modes; supplies the wear endpoint this study lacks.
- Janyavula et al. 2013 — polished zirconia caused the least enamel wear while glazed zirconia wore both itself and enamel.
- Park et al. 2014 — polished CAD/CAM zirconia caused the least antagonist wear; surface roughness was the primary wear predictor.
- Sarabi et al. 2024 — grinding/glazing/polishing combined with hydrothermal and mechanical degradation (LTD) on flexural strength.
- Ramos et al. 2016 — grinding increased monoclinic content and strength via compressive stress, reversed by heat treatment (contrast with the no-transformation finding here).
- Kosmac et al. 1999 — foundational grinding/sandblasting strength-loss data for Y-TZP.

## 7. Glossary

- **Monolithic zirconia (MZ)** — full-contour zirconia restoration with no veneering porcelain, directly exposed to the oral environment.
- **Y-TZP** — yttria-stabilized tetragonal zirconia polycrystal; the metastable tetragonal phase underpins transformation toughening.
- **t→m phase transformation** — stress- or heat-induced tetragonal-to-monoclinic conversion, accompanied by volume expansion; beneficial locally (compressive stress) but degrading when widespread (LTD).
- **LTD (low-temperature degradation)** — hydrothermal aging-driven progressive t→m transformation and strength loss.
- **Ra** — arithmetical mean surface roughness (µm); Ra ≈ 0.2 µm is the cited plaque-retention threshold.
- **XRD (X-ray diffraction)** — used here to compute the monoclinic peak-intensity ratio and thus detect phase change.
- **Chairside/intraoral adjustment** — grinding of a delivered restoration to correct occlusion and adaptation, which removes the glaze layer and roughens the surface.
