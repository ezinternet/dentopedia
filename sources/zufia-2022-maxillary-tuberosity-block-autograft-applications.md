# Applications of maxillary tuberosity block autograft

## Why Ingested
Extends the intraoral donor site literature held in [[wiki/bone-regeneration/benic-2014-horizontal-bone-augmentation-guided-regeneration]] by detailing a donor site (maxillary tuberosity) that is easier to harvest, requires no donor-site restoration, and uniquely enables the "triple graft" technique combining corticocancellous bone and connective tissue in a single flap — clinically relevant for immediate implant placement with simultaneous hard and soft tissue reconstruction.

## Three-line Summary
This case series (4 cases) describes clinical applications of maxillary tuberosity (MT) block autografts for treating small-to-moderate localized alveolar defects, including immediate implant placement into type II/III sockets, horizontal/vertical defects, and circumferential defects. In all four cases, MT block autograft (alone or as a "triple graft" with connective tissue) successfully reconstructed defects, with stable bone and soft tissue outcomes up to 6 years post-treatment and no donor site complications reported. MT block autograft offers advantages over other intraoral donor sites (easier harvest, less morbidity, no donor-site restoration needed), but is limited by small tuberosity size and variable anatomy requiring careful preoperative CBCT assessment.

MT 블록 자가이식편은 다른 구강내 공여부 대비 채취 용이성·이환율↓·공여부 재건 불필요 등의 장점이 있으나, 결절의 소량·가변 해부학으로 수술 전 콘빔 전산화 단층촬영 (Cone Beam Computed Tomography, CBCT) 평가가 필수입니다.

## 1. Document Information
- **Title**: Applications of maxillary tuberosity block autograft
- **Authors**: Juan Zufia, Francesc Abella Sans
- **Year**: 2022
- **Journal**: Journal of Esthetic and Restorative Dentistry, 34(7):1015–1028
- **DOI**: 10.1111/jerd.12911
- **Institution**: Private Practice, Madrid, Spain; Universitat Internacional de Catalunya
- **Evidence Level**: case-report (n=4)
- **Follow-up**: Up to 6 years

## 2. Key Contributions
- Demonstrates four distinct clinical scenarios where MT block autograft is viable: (1) type II socket immediate implant, (2) type III socket with circumferential defect, (3) horizontal ridge defect, (4) combined vertical/horizontal defect.
- Introduces and describes the "triple graft" technique — a single tuberosity harvest yielding corticocancellous bone block + particulate bone + connective tissue — enabling simultaneous hard and soft tissue augmentation (Immediate Dentoalveolar Restoration, IDR).
- Documents up to 6-year radiographic and clinical stability with no donor-site complications (no oroantral communication, hematoma, or nerve injury).
- Compares MT donor site to retromolar, chin, and external sites: MT offers no need for donor-site restoration, less neurovascular risk, and suitability for patients who have had prior third molar extraction.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Study design**: Prospective case series (n=4); no control group.
- **Patient ages**: 38, 55, 50, 46 years.
- **Surgical approach**: Full-thickness crestal incision distal to the maxillary second molar; mucoperiosteal flap reflection; connective tissue graft harvested from palatal aspect of tuberosity; corticocancellous block harvested using 1-cm flat chisel and surgical hammer.
- **Fixation**: Graft secured with cortical screws, pins, or implant primary stability.
- **Adjuncts**: Collagen membrane and/or particulate bone substitute in some cases; primary tension-free closure in all cases.
- **Evaluation**: Clinical and radiographic (CBCT + periapical X-ray) follow-up at multiple intervals up to 6 years.
- **Outcomes assessed**: Implant survival, defect resolution (radiographic), peri-implant soft tissue health (probing depth, recession), donor-site morbidity.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- All 4 implants placed after augmentation were osseointegrated and clinically functional at last follow-up.
- Stable buccal bone plates and healthy peri-implant mucosa at final follow-up (up to 6 years); no significant marginal bone loss reported qualitatively.
- No donor-site complications in any of the 4 cases.
- "Triple graft" (Case 1 and 2) provided simultaneous bone reconstruction and keratinized tissue augmentation in a single surgical step.
- Preoperative CBCT was essential for assessing tuberosity volume; cases were selected accordingly.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Case series (n=4); very low evidence level; cannot generalize.
- No comparison group (vs. other intraoral donor sites, xenografts, or allografts).
- Outcomes are narrative/qualitative; limited quantitative volumetric bone data.
- Selection bias: cases selected based on confirmed tuberosity availability.
- Follow-up up to 6 years is insufficient for implant long-term survival claims.
- Variable tuberosity anatomy may restrict applicability to a subset of patients.

## 6. Related Work
- Horizontal block augmentation (retromolar, chin): Benic 2014 describes comparable intraoral autograft sites with different morbidity profiles.
- Triple graft in IDR context: intersects with the Versah protocol (CBCT-driven site selection, IDR II pathway).
- GBR with collagen membrane: standard adjunct in all cases.

## 7. Glossary
- **MT (Maxillary Tuberosity, 상악 결절)**: Posterior convexity of the maxilla distal to the last molar; intraoral bone graft donor site.
- **Triple Graft (삼중 이식편)**: Single-harvest technique yielding corticocancellous block + particulate bone + connective tissue from the tuberosity.
- **IDR (Immediate Dentoalveolar Restoration, 즉시 치아-치조골 재건)**: Protocol combining immediate implant placement with simultaneous bone and soft tissue reconstruction.
- **CBCT (Cone Beam Computed Tomography, 콘빔 전산화 단층촬영)**: 3D imaging modality essential for preoperative tuberosity volume assessment.
- **GBR (Guided Bone Regeneration, 유도 골재생)**: Membrane-based technique to confine and promote bone regeneration at the defect site.
- **Type II socket**: Post-extraction socket with intact buccal wall but buccal bone wall height loss <50%.
- **Type III socket**: Post-extraction socket with buccal wall defect/perforation.
