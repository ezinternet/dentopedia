## Why Ingested

Fills a gap the umbrella review [[post-and-core/fathi-2022-prosthetic-restoration-endodontically-treated-teeth-umbrella]] flags — restoration guidelines for endodontically treated teeth lean on laboratory data — with clinical cohort evidence specific to anterior teeth, and identifies concrete structural thresholds (wall count, dentin thickness ratio, palatal support) for when direct resin composite is a safe alternative to a full-coverage crown.

## Three-line Summary

This retrospective cohort study evaluated 263 endodontically treated anterior teeth (157 restored with direct resin composite, 106 with full-coverage crowns) from dental records of patients treated between 2007-2019 at Mahidol University, Thailand, with a mean recall period of 38 months, comparing survival rates from unrestorable fracture and identifying predisposing factors using Kaplan-Meier and Cox proportional-hazard models.

The survival rate of teeth restored with full-coverage crowns was significantly higher (99.1%) than those with direct resin composite (90.4%) (P<0.01). Significant predisposing factors for unrestorable fracture in the resin composite group included less than three walls of remaining cervical tooth structure (HR: 8.95; 95% CI: 1.43-56.01), root dentin thickness-to-canal width ratio <1:1:1 (HR: 12.98; 95% CI: 1.55-108.73), loss of posterior-teeth support (HR: 9.05; 95% CI: 1.68-48.77), and parafunctional habits (HR: 19.37; 95% CI: 2.25-166.71).

For anterior teeth without these risk factors, direct resin composite restorations showed survival rates comparable to crowns. The presence of palatal cervical tooth structure in maxillary anterior teeth significantly improved survival, suggesting that resin composite can be a successful restoration in selected cases, though long-term adhesive stability requires further investigation.

## 세줄요약

이 후향적 코호트 연구는 태국 마히돈 대학에서 2007~2019년 사이에 치료받은 263개의 근관치료된 전치(직접 레진 수복 157개, 완전피개관 106개)를 평균 38개월간 추적 관찰하여, 회복불가능한 파절의 생존율을 비교하고 위험인자를 Kaplan-Meier 및 Cox 비례위험모형으로 분석하였다.

완전피개관의 생존율(99.1%)이 직접 레진 수복(90.4%)보다 유의하게 높았다(P<0.01). 직접 레진 수복군에서 회복불가능한 파절의 유의한 위험인자는 경부 치질 구조물이 3개 미만인 경우(위험비: 8.95; 95% CI: 1.43-56.01), 치근 상아질 두께 대 근관 폭 비율이 1:1:1 미만(위험비: 12.98; 95% CI: 1.55-108.73), 후치 지지 상실(위험비: 9.05; 95% CI: 1.68-48.77), 그리고 이갈이 습관(위험비: 19.37; 95% CI: 2.25-166.71)이었다.

이러한 위험인자가 없는 치아에서는 직접 레진 수복이 완전피개관과 비슷한 생존율을 보였다. 상악 전치에서 구개측 경부 치질 구조물의 존재가 생존율을 유의하게 향상시켰으며, 이는 선택된 증례에서 직접 레진 수복이 성공적일 수 있음을 시사하나, 장기적 접착 안정성에 대한 추가 연구가 필요하다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First clinical study directly comparing survival rates of anterior endodontically treated teeth restored with direct resin composite versus full-coverage crowns, with separate analysis for anterior teeth.
- Identified four significant predisposing factors for unrestorable fracture in resin composite-restored anterior teeth: remaining cervical tooth structure <3 walls, root dentin thickness-to-canal width ratio <1:1:1, loss of posterior teeth support, and parafunctional habits.
- Demonstrated that resin composite restorations can achieve survival rates comparable to crowns (94.3% vs 99.1%) when none of the identified risk factors are present.
- Showed that maxillary anterior teeth with palatal cervical tooth structure have survival rates (95.8%) similar to those with complete cervical structure (94.5%), while those without palatal wall had significantly lower survival (78.6%).
- Provided clinical guidelines for restoration selection based on identified risk factors rather than restoration type alone.
- Demonstrated 5-year cumulative survival of resin composite restorations still exceeded 80%, even in a cohort with mixed risk profiles.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: Retrospective cohort study
- Study period: 2007-2019
- Setting: Endodontic Postgraduate Clinic, Faculty of Dentistry, Mahidol University, Bangkok, Thailand
- Eligibility: Anterior endodontically treated teeth with mature roots, restored with single-unit direct resin composite or full-coverage crown, having occluding pair, and at least 12-month recall period
- Exclusion: Preoperative root resorption/crack/fracture, restored with veneers, bone loss >50% of root length, history of post removal before retreatment, procedural errors, ongoing orthodontic treatment
- Sample: 263 teeth (157 DRC, 106 FCC) from 155 females and 108 males (age 11-79, mean 56±15.5 years)
- Follow-up: 12-144 months, mean 38±21.4 months
- Data sources: Dental records and radiographs
- Variables: Age, gender, tooth type/location, restoration type, restorative surfaces, remaining cervical tooth structure walls, posterior teeth support, adjacent teeth, post type and length, cervical root dentin-to-canal ratio, parafunctional habits
- Outcomes: Survival from unrestorable fracture (tooth extraction due to extensive destruction)
- Statistics: Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, log-rank test, Cox proportional-hazard multivariate model; significance level P<0.05
- Subgroup analysis: Effect of remaining cervical tooth structure sites on survival of maxillary anterior teeth

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Overall survival rate —** 93.9% (247/263 teeth) at mean 38 months follow-up; 16 teeth extracted due to unrestorable fracture.
- **Primary comparison (DRC vs FCC) —** FCC survival: 99.1% (105/106); DRC survival: 90.4% (142/157); P<0.01 (log-rank test).
- **Cumulative survival (DRC) —** 98.7% at 12 months, decreasing to 80.6% at 72 months.
- **Cumulative survival (FCC) —** 100% at 12-48 months, 93.33% at 60-72 months.
- **Predisposing factors for DRC (multivariate Cox):** Less than 3 cervical walls: HR 8.95 (95% CI: 1.43-56.01, P=0.02); no posterior support: HR 9.05 (95% CI: 1.68-48.77, P=0.02); cervical root dentin ratio <1:1:1: HR 12.98 (95% CI: 1.55-108.73, P=0.02); parafunctional habits: HR 19.37 (95% CI: 2.25-166.71, P<0.01).
- **Subgroup analysis (maxillary anterior DRC):** Complete cervical structure: 94.5%; partial with palatal wall: 95.8%; partial without palatal wall: 78.6%; no cervical structure: 44.4%. Partial without palatal vs complete: HR 6.21 (95% CI: 1.23-31.48, P=0.03); no structure vs complete: HR 16.78 (95% CI: 3.74-75.21, P<0.01).
- **Comparative analysis:** DRC without risk factors showed comparable survival to FCC (no significant difference, P>0.05 for all comparisons except parafunctional habit group with P=0.05).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single-center retrospective design limits generalizability of findings.
- Relatively short mean follow-up (38 months) may not capture long-term failure patterns, particularly adhesive degradation over time.
- Single incident of fracture in FCC group prevented statistical analysis of predisposing factors for crown-restored teeth.
- Inability to analyze mandibular anterior teeth subgroup due to insufficient fracture events (only 2 cases).
- Lack of randomization may introduce selection bias in restoration assignment (clinician decision-making factors not controlled).
- No blinding in outcome assessment, potentially introducing detection bias.
- Parafunctional habits were self-reported or clinically observed without objective measurement.
- Long-term degradation of adhesion between resin composite and dentin requires further investigation.
- Authors recommend randomized controlled trial to confirm retrospective findings.

## 6. Related Work

- Sorensen and Martinoff (1984) — Reported separate survival rates for anterior and posterior ETT; found no significant difference between full-coverage and direct restorations, though survival rates were marginally different (87.5-97.5% vs 85.4-94.4%).
- Skupien et al. (2016) — RCT comparing crowns vs composite for post-retained restorations; found significantly higher survival for crowns, though all composite restorations included fiber posts (unlike the present study).
- Fransson et al. (2016) — Swedish population study of root-filled teeth survival; combined anterior and posterior data, found better survival with crown coverage.
- Signore et al. (2009) — Long-term study of maxillary anterior ETT restored with fiber posts and full-ceramic crowns; found higher survival trend for teeth with 3-4 remaining coronal walls but not statistically significant.
- Ng et al. (2006) — Laboratory study demonstrating that remaining coronal tooth structure location (particularly palatal wall) affects fracture resistance of restored ETT; results consistent with present clinical findings.

## 7. Glossary

- **DRC (Direct Resin Composite)** : 직접 레진 수복 — 치아에 직접 적층하여 수복하는 레진 재료
- **FCC (Full-Coverage Crown)** : 완전피개관 — 치관 전체를 덮는 보철 수복물 (예: 전부 도재관, 도재-금속관)
- **ETT (Endodontically Treated Teeth)** : 근관치료된 치아 — 치수강이 제거되고 근관이 충전된 치아
- **CEJ (Cemento-Enamel Junction)** : 백악질-법랑질 접합부 — 치관부와 치근부의 경계 부위
- **Ferrule Effect** : 페룰 효과 — 남아있는 치경부 치질 구조물이 임플란트/수복물의 지대주 역할을 하여 파절 저항성을 향상시키는 현상
