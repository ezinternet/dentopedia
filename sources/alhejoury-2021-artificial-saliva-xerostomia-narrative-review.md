## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (J Pharm Bioallied Sci 2021, no stated systematic search strategy) surveying artificial saliva and sialogogue options for therapeutic management of xerostomia and salivary gland hypofunction (SGH).

Catalogues topical stimulants (flavored gums, citric acid), systemic sialogogues (pilocarpine 5–10mg 3–4x/day, cevimeline 30mg/day, anetholetrithione, yohimbine, interferon-α lozenges), saliva substitutes (mucin/carboxymethylcellulose-based, Recaldent, xylitol/Biotene/BioXtra products), and emerging approaches (electrostimulation devices, aquaporin-1 gene therapy, salivary pacemakers).

As a narrative (not systematic) review with no stated search methodology or risk-of-bias assessment, its claims should be read as a practical treatment-options catalogue rather than graded evidence; pilocarpine/cevimeline dosing and contraindications are the most actionable, clinically vetted details.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Provides a clinician-oriented catalogue of therapeutic options for xerostomia/SGH management, organized into topical stimulants, systemic sialogogues, saliva substitutes, and emerging technologies.
- Summarizes the physicochemical requirements of artificial saliva (viscosity/pseudoplasticity matching normal saliva; mucin-based formulations closer to natural saliva than carboxymethylcellulose/glycerol-based Newtonian-fluid formulations).
- Lists concrete dosing regimens for the main systemic sialogogues: pilocarpine (5–10mg, 3–4x/day), cevimeline (30mg/day), anetholetrithione (25mg, 3x/day), and interferon-α lozenges (150 IU, 3x/day).
- Surveys emerging/experimental approaches: intraoral electro-stimulation devices (3–4-fold salivary flow increase), aquaporin-1 (AQP1) gene therapy for postradiation SGH (Baum et al.), and implantable salivary pacemaker devices (Salitron, GenNarino, SaliPen, Saliwell Crown).
- Frames the overall approach within the "Minimally Invasive Dentistry" preventive philosophy.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Narrative review; no PRISMA flow, no stated database search strategy, no explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria, and no formal quality/risk-of-bias appraisal are reported in the text provided.
- **Scope**: General dental/oral-medicine management of xerostomia and hyposalivation, covering pharmacological (topical + systemic) and non-pharmacological (substitutes, devices, gene therapy) interventions.
- **Population framing**: General xerostomia patients (medication-induced, radiotherapy-induced, Sjögren's syndrome, age-related/senile hypofunction) are discussed together rather than analyzed as separate subgroups with comparative outcome data.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Saliva physiology**: normal saliva provides lubrication, digestion aid, taste enhancement, mucosal moisturizing, irrigation/cleansing, and antimicrobial/immunological functions; hypoptyalism impairs speech, chewing, and swallowing and promotes caries/gingival inflammation.
- **Artificial saliva composition**: buffering agents + cellulose derivatives + flavoring agents; mucin-with-xanthan-guar-gum or mucin-alone formulations have physiochemical (viscoelastic) properties closer to natural saliva than carboxymethylcellulose- or glycerol-based Newtonian alternatives; viscoelasticity assessed via rheometers (creep/recovery, dynamic tests).
- **Topical stimulants**: flavored/sugar-free chewing gums and lozenges (sorbitol, xylitol); citric acid is an effective stimulant but risks demineralization/caries in dentate patients via calcium chelation.
- **Systemic sialogogues (mainly Sjögren's syndrome)**: pilocarpine 5–10mg 3–4x/day (adverse effects: diaphoresis, flushing, polyuria; contraindicated in uncontrolled asthma, narrow-angle glaucoma, acute iritis); cevimeline 30mg/day (more M3-selective, favored in postradiation xerostomia); anetholetrithione 25mg 3x/day (useful in minor/senile salivary dysfunction); yohimbine (weaker evidence, more effective than anetholetrithione in patients on psychotropics per cited studies); interferon-α lozenges 150 IU 3x/day (meta-analysis cited showing efficacy with minimal side effects and immunomodulatory benefit in Sjögren's syndrome).
- **Saliva substitutes/commercial products**: Recaldent Gum (casein phosphopeptide–amorphous calcium phosphate, remineralization); Spry Dental Defense System (xylitol, reduces S. mutans with extended use); Biotene and BioXtra (protein enzyme systems for lubrication/hygiene). Chewing gums increase flow and provide cariostatic/remineralizing benefit but have limited effect on saliva's biochemical composition.
- **Emerging technologies**: acupuncture (improves parotid blood circulation, benefit shown in Sjögren's/radiotherapy/idiopathic xerostomia); intraoral electro-stimulators (3–4-fold salivary flow increase, mouthguard-like or implant-fixed devices); AQP1 gene therapy (viral-vector delivery, increased flow in postradiation SGH patients); salivary pacemakers (Salitron, GenNarino, SaliPen, Saliwell Crown); intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) reduces oral dryness compared with conventional radiotherapy.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No systematic search methodology reported** — the review does not state databases searched, search terms, date range, or inclusion/exclusion criteria, so publication/selection bias cannot be assessed.
- **No risk-of-bias or quality appraisal** of the underlying primary studies/trials it cites (e.g., the interferon-α meta-analysis, pilocarpine/cevimeline trials, electrostimulation and gene-therapy studies) — treat cited efficacy figures as reported, not independently graded.
- **Heterogeneous population pooling** — xerostomia etiologies (Sjögren's syndrome, radiotherapy, medication-induced, age-related) are discussed together without stratified comparative outcome data.
- **No comparative effectiveness ranking** across the many agents/devices listed; the review is descriptive rather than evaluative.
- Authors do not propose a specific future research agenda beyond general endorsement of continued development of substitute/device technologies.

## 7. Glossary

- **Xerostomia**: subjective sensation of oral dryness, most often but not always accompanying reduced salivary flow.
- **SGH (Salivary Gland Hypofunction)**: objective reduction in salivary gland output; may occur with or without subjective xerostomia.
- **Sialogogue**: an agent that stimulates salivary secretion (e.g., pilocarpine, cevimeline).
- **Pseudoplasticity**: shear-thinning viscosity behavior characteristic of normal saliva, important for lubrication function; artificial saliva formulations aim to reproduce this property.
- **Cholinomimetic**: a drug that mimics acetylcholine action at muscarinic receptors (e.g., pilocarpine, cevimeline), stimulating residual salivary gland tissue.
- **AQP1 (Aquaporin-1)**: water-channel protein used in an experimental gene-therapy approach to restore salivary flow in postradiation gland hypofunction.
- **IMRT (Intensity-Modulated Radiotherapy)**: radiotherapy technique that spares salivary gland tissue better than conventional radiotherapy, reducing postradiation xerostomia severity.
