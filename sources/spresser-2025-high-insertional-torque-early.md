## Why Ingested

The high-torque half of a deliberate contrast pair on insertion torque (IT) and early implant failure. This prospective observational study from the same VA North Texas group found that implants placed at IT ≥60 N·cm carried ~9× the early-failure odds of a 30–59 N·cm control group — the high end of the curve. Paired with [[wiki/implants/survival/carr-2022-low-insertional-torque-early]] (same institution and senior author, IT <30 N·cm → ~14× early failure), the two papers bracket the relationship from both ends: low torque and high torque each raise early-failure risk, jointly implying a biphasic curve with 30–59 N·cm as the safe middle window. It also extends [[wiki/overviews/high-insertion-torque-primary-stability-crestal-bone-overview]], whose evidence for high-torque harm has until now been post-hoc-classified MBL/crestal-bone data rather than a prospective early-failure outcome.

## Three-line Summary

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Oral Surgery, Oral Medicine, Oral Pathology and Oral Radiology 2025;140:539-543
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.oooo.2025.06.006
- **PMID**: 40645843
- **Institution**: Veterans Affairs North Texas Health Care System, Dallas, TX, USA

## 2. Key Contributions
- Provides the high-torque mirror of Carr 2022: a prospective early-failure signal at IT ≥60 N·cm, in the direction opposite to the "higher torque = better stability" intuition.
- Uses a 30–59 N·cm active control rather than an arbitrary low-torque comparator, so the finding is specifically about *excessive* torque above the stable window.
- Mechanistic framing: the authors propose that high torque, despite improving primary stability, may drive bone resorption, impaired osseointegration, and soft-tissue loss.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Prospective observational study
- **Setting**: VA North Texas Health Care System, Dallas, TX
- **n**: 207 implants in 95 patients
- **Exposure groups**: IT ≥60 N·cm (high-torque) vs 30–59 N·cm (control)
- **Outcome**: Early implant failure — exfoliation, mobility during stage 2 surgery, or need for explantation prior to prosthetic loading
- **Statistics**: odds ratio with 95% CI

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- High-torque (IT ≥60 N·cm) vs control (30–59 N·cm): **odds ratio = 9.00** (95% CI 1.06–76.18), **P = 0.043** (statistically significant)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Odds-ratio CI spans 1.06 to 76.18 — a barely-significant lower bound; the effect size is very imprecisely estimated.
- Abstract-only ingest: full text not retrieved, so site, bone quality, implant system, and loading protocol are unknown.
- The authors attribute the mechanism (bone resorption / impaired osseointegration / soft-tissue loss) as a proposed explanation, not a measured endpoint in this study.

## 6. Related Work
- Carr-2022 (same institution, same senior author): the low-torque counterpart — IT <30 N·cm carried ~14× the early-failure hazard vs ≥30 N·cm; together these define a U-shaped torque–failure relationship.
- High-insertion-torque-primary-stability-crestal-bone-overview: synthesis whose high-torque harm evidence (Marconcini, Aldahlawi) is MBL/crestal-bone based; this paper adds a prospective early-failure outcome.
- Baldi-2018: high-torque arm (>50 N·cm, mean 68.2 N·cm) gained no ISQ benefit and held both implant failures — consistent direction at a similar torque magnitude.

## 7. Glossary
- **IT**: Insertional/insertion torque — the rotational force (N·cm) required to seat an implant; a readout of the bone that resists it.
- **N·cm (Ncm)**: Newton-centimetre, the unit of insertion torque.
- **Odds ratio (OR)**: Ratio of odds of early failure between the high-torque group and the control group.
- **Early implant failure**: Failure before prosthetic loading — exfoliation, mobility at stage-2 surgery, or explantation prior to loading.
