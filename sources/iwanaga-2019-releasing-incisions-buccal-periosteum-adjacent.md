## Why Ingested

This is the only anatomical-safety paper the wiki holds on the periosteal releasing incision (PRI) — every clinical PRI paper in this batch (and [[suture-wound-closure/plonka-2017-flap-designs-flap-advancement-implant-therapy]]'s advancement taxonomy, which quantifies PRI as the single largest advancement contributor at +5.5 mm) implicitly relies on operators avoiding the vessel this cadaveric study locates. Iwanaga 2019 supplies the missing danger-zone measurement: the inferior labial artery (branch of the facial artery) sits closest to the periosteum at the PRI midpoint — between the first and second molar or 10 mm mesial to the retromolar-pad apex — giving PRI technique papers a precise anatomical boundary they otherwise only gesture at qualitatively ("avoid excessive bleeding").

## Three-line Summary

Cadaveric anatomical dissection study (14 sides, 7 fresh-frozen Caucasian cadaveric heads, mean age 75.9 ± 10.8 years) mapping the course of the facial artery/inferior labial artery (ILA) relative to a standardized periosteal releasing incision (PRI) on the mandibular buccal periosteum.

The ILA was identified in the fat tissue lateral to and close to the periosteum on all 14 sides; it was closest to the periosteum at the PRI midpoint — approximately between the first and second molar teeth or 10 mm mesial to the retromolar-pad apex — with a mean diameter of 2.72 ± 0.26 mm.

Clinical implication: PRI should be made without invading tissue deep to (underneath) the periosteum, particularly in the first/second-molar danger zone, to avoid injuring the ILA/facial artery; abstract-only ingestion, no living-tissue hemodynamics captured.

## 세줄요약

사체(cadaveric) 해부 연구(14면, 신선동결 백인 사체 머리 7구, 평균 연령 75.9±10.8세)로, 하악 협측 골막에 표준화된 골막이완절개(Periosteal Releasing Incision, PRI) 시행 시 안면동맥(facial artery)/하순동맥(Inferior Labial Artery, ILA)의 주행을 매핑함.

14면 전부에서 ILA가 골막에 인접한 지방조직 내에서 확인됐고, PRI 중간지점 — 대략 제1·2대구치 사이 또는 磨牙後패드(retromolar pad) 첨부에서 근심 10mm — 에서 골막과 가장 가까웠으며 평균 직경은 2.72±0.26mm였음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First (per the literature this wiki holds) cadaveric anatomical mapping of the facial artery/inferior labial artery (ILA) course specifically relative to the PRI maneuver on the mandibular buccal periosteum.
- Localizes a precise **danger zone**: the ILA is closest to the periosteum at the PRI midpoint, operationalized two ways — (a) between the first and second molar teeth area, or (b) 10 mm mesial to the apex of the retromolar pad.
- Reports vessel caliber (mean ILA diameter 2.72 ± 0.26 mm) relevant to bleeding severity if transected — a vessel of this size can produce clinically significant intra-/postoperative bleeding, not just capillary oozing.
- Gives a concrete technical corrective: keep the PRI within the periosteum (do not invade tissue deep to/underneath the periosteum), rather than the vaguer "release carefully" guidance found in technique papers.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Specimens**: 14 sides from 7 fresh-frozen Caucasian cadaveric heads (2 female, 5 male donors); mean age at death 75.9 ± 10.8 years.
- **Procedure**: PRI performed with a no. 15c blade under a surgical microscope, standardized to the mandibular buccal periosteum adjacent to the lower molar teeth.
- **Dissection**: fat tissue lateral to the periosteum was slightly dissected to expose the facial artery/its branch.
- **Measurements**: diameter of the facial artery (or relevant branch); closest point-of-approach between the tooth position and the artery; the artery was then traced proximally to clarify its origin (i.e., confirmed as a branch of the facial artery).
- **Confidence**: `in-vitro` — per this wiki's established convention that human fresh-frozen cadaver dissection studies are classified as ex-vivo bench-type anatomical studies (see [[implants/versah-protocols/rittipakorn-2025-clockwise-osseodensification-primary-stability-cadaveric]] for the precedent), not `animal` or `in-vivo`.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- The **inferior labial artery (ILA)** was identified in the fat tissue lateral to and close to the periosteum on **all 14 sides** (100%).
- The ILA was **closest to the periosteum at the midpoint of the PRI** — approximately between the first and second molar teeth area, or **10 mm mesial to the apex of the retromolar pad**.
- **Mean ILA diameter**: 2.72 ± 0.26 mm.
- **Bottom line / clinical recommendation**: dentists should make the PRI without invading the tissue underneath (deep to) the periosteum, to avoid transecting the ILA/facial artery branch.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Cadaveric/ex-vivo model** — no living-tissue hemodynamics, blood flow, or individual vascular variation under physiologic pressure was captured; bleeding severity in vivo if the ILA is transected is inferred, not directly observed.
- **Elderly Caucasian sample** (mean age 75.9 ± 10.8 years) — vascular position/course in an elderly, likely partially edentulous or resorbed-ridge population may not directly generalize to younger surgical patients with different alveolar bone height and soft-tissue thickness.
- **Small sample**: 7 cadavers / 14 sides — anatomical variation in artery origin, branching pattern, and exact depth is not statistically characterized beyond mean ± SD of diameter and a qualitative "closest point" description.
- **Abstract-only ingestion**: full text not retrieved (no PMCID), so per-cadaver data, figures, and any additional inferential statistics beyond what is reported in the abstract are unavailable and not reconstructed here.
- Does not test whether the maxillary/other regions of PRI carry a comparable vascular risk — the danger zone described is specific to the mandibular buccal periosteum adjacent to the molars.

## 7. Glossary

- **PRI (periosteal releasing incision)**: an incision through the periosteum (leaving the overlying flap/mucosa intact) that releases tension and allows coronal advancement of a mucoperiosteal flap for tension-free primary wound closure, most often in GBR.
- **Facial artery**: a branch of the external carotid artery that courses along the face, giving off branches (including labial arteries) near the mandible and lips.
- **Inferior labial artery (ILA)**: a branch of the facial artery supplying the lower lip; in this study, the vessel identified in the fat tissue lateral to the periosteum, closest to it at the PRI midpoint.
- **Retromolar pad**: the soft-tissue pad located distal to the last mandibular molar, used here as a fixed anatomical landmark ("10 mm mesial to the apex of the retromolar pad") to describe the danger-zone location independent of tooth presence/absence.
- **Danger zone (PRI, mandibular)**: the region between the first and second molar teeth (or 10 mm mesial to the retromolar-pad apex) where the ILA is closest to the periosteum and therefore at highest risk of injury during a deep PRI.
