## Why Ingested

Provides in vitro comparative data on diamond grinder cutting efficiency across zirconia and resin-based composite CAD/CAM materials, quantifying weight removal, wear degradation over serial cuts, and SEM-confirmed diamond attrition patterns. Essential bench data for clinical decisions on bur selection and replacement timing when adjusting or reducing zirconia restorations.

→ [[dental-materials/zirconia/rosentritt-2024-cutting-efficiency-of-diamond-grinders]]

## Three-line Summary

Diamond grinders used on sintered 3Y-TZP zirconia and resin-based composite CAD/CAM blanks were compared for cutting efficiency under a standardized 160 g load in an in vitro model (n = 10 per material/grinder).
Significant inter-bur differences in initial weight removal existed for both materials; zirconia required five times longer grinding time to achieve weight removal comparable to composite.
SEM confirmed diamond grain rounding and loss as the dominant wear mechanism; a special Z-Cut grinder with harder diamond grit maintained removal level but at a lower absolute rate, while conventional burs showed greater cutting depth initially but faster degradation.

## 세줄요약

소결 3Y-TZP 지르코니아 (Yttrium-stabilized Tetragonal Zirconia Polycrystal, 3Y-TZP)와 레진강화복합재 (Resin-Based Composite, RBC) CAD/CAM 블록에서 160 g 일정 하중 조건으로 다이아몬드 버 (Diamond Grinder)의 절삭 효율을 시험관 내 비교하였다 (n = 10).
버 종류 간 초기 삭제량 차이가 두 재료 모두에서 유의했으며, 지르코니아에서 동일한 삭제량을 얻으려면 복합레진보다 5배 긴 시간이 필요했다.
주사전자현미경 (Scanning Electron Microscopy, SEM)으로 다이아몬드 입자 마모 및 탈락이 절삭 저하의 주원인임을 확인했고, Z-CUT 전용 버는 낮은 절대 삭제량이지만 4회 사용 후에도 삭제량을 유지하는 내구성을 보였다.

## Document Information

- **Study type**: In vitro comparative experiment
- **Materials tested**: 3Y-TZP zirconia (Cercon base 30 colored; Dentsply) sintered at 1350 °C; resin-based composite (Grandio blocs 14L A3.5 HT; VOCO)
- **Grinders tested**: NTI-Kahla (Z881-016C-FG "ABACUS", 881-016C-FG, 881-014TC-FG "TURBO", 881-014C-FG, K881-016M-FG "Z-CUT", 881-016M-FG) and Komet Dental (6881.314.016, 6881.314.014, 881.314.016)
- **Load**: 160 g standardized via special handpiece holder
- **Protocol**: Composite — 8 cuts × 40 s each; Zirconia — 4 cuts × 5 min each; n = 10 per material-grinder combination
- **Statistics**: One-way ANOVA, Bonferroni post-hoc, Pearson correlation (α = 0.05, SPSS)
- **Outcomes**: Weight removal per cut (g), SEM of bur surface and material surface before/after

## Key Contributions

- Quantified that grinding zirconia to equivalent weight removal requires 5× longer time than composite under identical grinder and load conditions
- Demonstrated significant inter-bur variation in initial removal for composite (p = 0.009) and zirconia (p < 0.001)
- Identified progressive cutting efficiency decline for composite grinders (significant decrease by cut 3–7) and zirconia standard grinders (significant decrease by cut 4)
- Showed positive Pearson correlation (r = 0.673, p < 0.001) between cuts and removal in zirconia group, suggesting cumulative bur conditioning
- SEM confirmed diamond grain rounding and complete particle loss (ZB16) as dominant degradation mode; special Z-Cut (ZS16) retained diamond geometry with no surface wear traces
- Conventional burs for composite (not zirconia-specific) can match special zirconia burs in initial removal, echoing prior literature

## Methodology

**Specimens**: Zirconia cubes (12 × 14 × 18 mm) sintered at 1350 °C from pre-colored blanks; composite blanks used as-delivered.

**Grinding apparatus**: Custom handpiece holder applying constant 160 g load; cuts made across specimen surface in standardized linear strokes.

**Grinder groups**:
- Composite group (8 burs): conventional (CA16/CA14/CB16/CB14) and special-coating (CS16/CS14 "ABACUS", CT14 "TURBO") at 1.4 mm and 1.6 mm diameters
- Zirconia group (4 burs): conventional (ZA16/ZB16) and special (ZS16 "Z-CUT") at 1.6 mm; standard (ZK16) from second manufacturer

**Measurement**: Precision balance (MXX-612, Denver Instrument) weighed specimens before each cut and after to yield per-cut weight removal.

**SEM**: Bur surfaces before and after all cuts; material surfaces after grinding — evaluated for diamond wear morphology and workpiece surface integrity.

## Key Results

### Composite group (8 cuts × 40 s)

| Bur | First cut removal | 8th cut removal | Significant decrease vs baseline |
|-----|-----------------|-----------------|----------------------------------|
| CS16 (ABACUS) | 0.25 ± 0.05 g | — | Cut 3 |
| CS14 (ABACUS) | — | — | Cut 3 |
| CA14 | — | — | Cut 4 |
| CB14 | — | — | Cut 4 |
| CB16 | — | 0.09 ± 0.05 g | Cut 6 |
| CA16 | — | — | Cut 7 |

- Baseline (cut 1) differed significantly across burs (p = 0.009)
- 8th cut also differed across burs (p = 0.049)
- No removal–cut-number correlation (Pearson: −0.27, p = 0.333)
- Diameter (1.4 vs 1.6 mm) within same bur type: no significant difference (p = 0.069)

### Zirconia group (4 cuts × 5 min)

| Bur | First cut removal | Notes |
|-----|-----------------|-------|
| ZA16 (conventional) | 0.21 ± 0.05 g | No significant decrease over 4 cuts (p ≥ 0.056) |
| ZB16 (conventional) | — | Significant decrease by cut 4 (p = 0.006); SEM showed complete diamond loss |
| ZS16 (Z-CUT special) | ~0.10 g estimated | No significant decrease (p = 1.000); SEM: no wear traces |
| ZK16 (Komet standard) | — | Lower initial removal; decrease over cuts |

- Weight removal range: 0.07 ± 0.01 g (ZS16, cut 3) to 0.21 ± 0.05 g (ZA16, cut 1)
- Positive correlation: cuts vs removal (Pearson 0.673, p < 0.001)
- Initial removal ~20 mg for conventional vs ~10 mg for Z-Cut special
- Zirconia took 5× longer grinding time for comparable weight removal to composite

## Limitations

- Only weight-loss criterion assessed; no volumetric analysis or surface roughness (Ra) measurement
- Overall weight-removal levels were low, limiting clinical extrapolation
- Composite and zirconia grinding time protocols differed substantially (40 s vs 5 min), making direct cut-for-cut comparison indirect
- Single load (160 g) tested; clinical applied forces vary
- One type of 3Y-TZP and one RBC used; results may not generalize to 4Y/5Y-PSZ, translucent monolithic zirconia, or other composites
- No water-spray cooling volume variation studied

## Glossary

- **3Y-TZP (Yttrium-stabilized Tetragonal Zirconia Polycrystal, 3Y-TZP)**: Dense zirconia ceramic stabilized with 3 mol% yttria; high flexural strength (~1200 MPa) and hardness (~1200 HV), standard for posterior zirconia restorations.
- **CAD/CAM (Computer-Aided Design/Computer-Aided Manufacturing, CAD/CAM)**: Digital workflow for milling dental restorations from pre-fabricated blanks using CNC milling machines.
- **Diamond grinder (Diamond Grinder)**: Rotary dental instrument with diamond abrasive particles bonded to a metal shank; classified by grit size (ultra-fine to coarse) for different reduction tasks.
- **SEM (Scanning Electron Microscopy, SEM)**: High-resolution surface imaging technique used here to characterize diamond wear morphology and workpiece surface topography.
- **Weight removal (Weight Removal)**: Mass of substrate abraded per grinding step; primary outcome metric in this study, measured in grams using a precision balance.
- **Z-CUT**: Proprietary NTI-Kahla bur with harder, geometrically defined diamond grit and special nickel bond designed specifically for sintered zirconia.
