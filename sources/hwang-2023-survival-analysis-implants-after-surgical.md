## Why Ingested

Supplies the severity-stratified long-term survival curve that surgical peri-implantitis reviews keep calling for — and finds resective and regenerative surgery statistically indistinguishable in every stage, which is the decision the technique literature usually argues about → [[implants/peri-implantitis/monje-2025-long-term-surgical-treatment-outcomes]] and [[implants/peri-implantitis/ramanauskaite-2026-factors-influencing-effectiveness-surgical-therapy]].

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective survival analysis with Kaplan-Meier and Cox models (BMC Oral Health 2023;23:308, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital; 227 implants in 89 patients treated surgically for peri-implantitis between July 2003 and April 2021; mean follow-up 4.3 years, retrospectively registered KCT0008225).

Cumulative survival fell steeply with initial bone-loss severity — 70.7% (stage 1, <25% bone loss), 48.9% (stage 2, 25-50%) and 21.3% (stage 3, >50%), with hazard ratios 2.25 and 4.59 versus stage 1 — while resective versus regenerative surgery showed no significant difference overall (57.0% vs 44.2%, P = 0.444) or within any stage.

Bone-loss percentage of fixture length is therefore the usable preoperative prognostic marker for surgical peri-implantitis therapy, and it predicts outcome independent of which surgical technique is chosen.

## 세줄요약

카플란-마이어·콕스 모형을 쓴 후향 생존분석 (BMC Oral Health 2023;23:308, 분당서울대; 2003년 7월~2021년 4월 임플란트주위염 수술 227개/환자 89명, 평균 추적 4.3년, 후향 등록 KCT0008225).

누적 생존율은 초기 골소실 정도에 따라 급감 — 1기 (<25%) 70.7%, 2기 (25-50%) 48.9%, 3기 (>50%) 21.3%, 위험비 (Hazard Ratio, HR)는 1기 대비 2.25·4.59; 반면 절제술 (Resective Surgery) vs 재생술 (Regenerative Surgery)은 전체 (57.0% vs 44.2%, P=0.444)에서도 각 기별로도 유의차 없음.

따라서 임플란트 길이 대비 골소실률 (Bone Loss Rate)이 술 전 예후 예측 지표로 유용하며, 어떤 수술법을 택하든 예후를 바꾸지 않음.

## Key Contributions
- Provides severity-stratified long-term survival after peri-implantitis surgery using a simple, reproducible metric — bone loss as a percentage of fixture length (<25%, 25-50%, >50%).
- Quantifies the prognostic gradient: cumulative survival 70.7% / 48.9% / 21.3%, median survival unreached / 8.18 / 3.63 years, HR 1.00 / 2.25 / 4.59.
- Shows resective versus regenerative surgery produced statistically indistinguishable survival overall and within every stage — technique choice did not rescue severe cases nor blunt the severity effect.
- Reports unusually low absolute survival (47.8% overall, 94.9% at 1 year falling to 65.7% at 5 years) and attributes it to the retrospective setting, loose maintenance and permissive surgical indications.
- Adds the 1-, 3- and 5-year staged survival table (stage 3: 90.4% → 58.8% → 45.7%) that is directly usable for consent conversations.

## Methodology
**Design.** Retrospective chart review of all patients who underwent peri-implantitis surgery at a single institution between July 2003 and April 2021. 266 implants in 101 patients screened; 227 implants in 89 patients analyzed after excluding 13 implants in patients with no postoperative visit and 26 implants surgically retreated more than once.

**Severity classification.** Stage 1 = bone loss <25% of fixture length; stage 2 = 25-50%; stage 3 = >50%. Distribution: 79 stage 1, 70 stage 2, 78 stage 3.

**Technique.** Resective surgery 102 implants (44.9%); regenerative surgery 125 implants (55.1%).

**Outcomes and analysis.** Implant failure as the event; Kaplan-Meier curves with log-rank tests (Bonferroni correction, α = 0.016) and Cox proportional hazards models (proportional hazards assumption confirmed graphically). Median survival time, estimated mean survival time, HR and 95% CI reported. Cox modelling by technique was not performed because the log-rank tests were non-significant.

## Limitations
Retrospective, single centre, no standardized maintenance protocol — the authors attribute the low absolute survival to absent strict supportive care and permissive surgical indications, which makes the absolute numbers probably pessimistic relative to a well-maintained cohort. Multiple surgical retreatment cases were excluded (n = 26), so the worst cases are removed from the analysis, while simultaneously biasing the population toward single-procedure outcomes. Diagnosis was by probing depth ≥4 mm plus radiographic bone loss without a unified case definition, and 0.25 N probing force could not always be applied. Stage assignment used one bone-loss threshold scheme that is not the 2017 World Workshop staging, so cross-study comparison is indirect. Technique assignment was not randomized and the resective/regenerative split differs sharply by stage (stage 3: 20 resective vs 58 regenerative), so residual confounding by defect morphology remains even though no difference was found. Device/surface and prosthetic factors were not modelled.

## Glossary
- **Resective surgery:** implantoplasty / bone recontoing / apically positioned flap aimed at eliminating the defect and facilitating hygiene.
- **Regenerative surgery:** debridement plus graft/membrane intended to fill the intraosseous defect.
- **Stage 1/2/3 (this paper):** bone loss <25% / 25-50% / >50% of fixture length.
- **HR:** Hazard Ratio.
- **Cumulative survival rate:** Kaplan-Meier estimate at the end of observation.
- **Censored:** implant still in function (or lost to follow-up) without a failure event.
