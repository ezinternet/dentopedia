## Why Ingested

Pilot study evaluating whether chairside polishing results of monolithic zirconia crowns differ across dental practitioner groups (students, junior dentists, senior dentists, dental technicians), directly relevant to chairside occlusal adjustment protocols.

→ [[dental-materials/zirconia/kozmacs-2017-polishing-of-monolithic-zirconia-crowns]]

## Three-line Summary

A pilot study at University Witten/Herdecke assigned 24 participants (6 per group: dental students, dentists <2 years, dentists >2 years, dental technicians) to polish CAD/CAM monolithic zirconia crowns using a two-step Komet polishing kit under two time conditions (unrestricted vs. 15-second per step).
No statistically significant difference in Ra (surface roughness average) was found between any practitioner group, using Mann–Whitney U testing; technicians achieved the greatest median roughness reduction (25.4 nm) in trial 1, while experienced dentists led in the 15-second trial (41.35 nm).
The authors conclude that chairside polishing of monolithic zirconia is reliable regardless of experience level when the application protocol is followed.

## Key Contributions

1. **Experience level does not significantly affect polishing outcome**: Despite trends favoring higher-experience groups, no statistically significant difference in Ra was detected between any of the four practitioner groups (students, junior dentists, senior dentists, technicians).
2. **Polishing duration is not correlated with result**: Restricting polishing time to 15 seconds per step (vs. unlimited) did not significantly change outcomes; Spearman's rho showed no significant correlation.
3. **Practical implication**: Chairside polishing with a two-step commercial kit (Komet Dental) can be reliably performed in everyday clinical practice without laboratory re-glazing.
4. **Measurement method**: Used Alicona Infinite Focus System G3 optical profilometry with 10 linear + 10 area measurements per specimen (ISO 11562), offering high resolution (vertical resolution 450 nm, Lc = 25 µm).

## Methodology

- **Specimens**: 48 CAD/CAM monolithic Y-TZP zirconia crowns (3M Lava Plus High Translucency, 3% yttrium-stabilized) fabricated by one operator; occlusal adjustment standardized (5 strokes, red ring diamond bur 20,000 rpm)
- **Polishing kit**: 2-step Komet Dental zirconia polisher (blue 94018C + white 94018F, both 6000 rpm)
- **Trials**: T1 = no time limit; T2 = 15 seconds per polisher step
- **Participants**: 24 total (n=6 per group); all received 20-min standardized briefing with handout
- **Roughness measurement**: Ra (roughness average) by two blinded independent investigators (I1, I2); linear measurements 100 µm, area 50×50 µm², section 510×510 µm
- **Statistics**: Kolmogorov–Smirnov normality test → non-parametric; Mann–Whitney U (independent groups); Wilcoxon signed-rank (within-group T1 vs. T2); Spearman's rho (time vs. result correlation); p < 0.05

## Key Results

| Group | Trial | Investigator | Median ΔRa (nm) | IQR (nm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dental technicians | T1 | I1 | 25.4 | 10.15–35.26 |
| Dental students | T1 | I1 | 6.72 | 4.7–17.9 |
| Experienced dentists (>2y) | T2 | I1 | 41.35 | 7.77–54.11 |

- All groups achieved surface roughness reduction after polishing
- No statistically significant between-group differences (Mann–Whitney, p ≥ 0.05)
- No significant correlation between polishing duration and Ra outcome (Spearman's rho)
- Both investigators showed consistent trends and similar values

## Limitations

- Pilot study design with small sample size (n=6 per group)
- No antagonist wear data collected; clinical relevance of polishing outcome to enamel abrasion is not established
- In-vitro only; clinical conditions (saliva, patient movement, access) not simulated
- Contact pressure and movement speed were not standardized or measured
- Single polishing kit (Komet) tested; results may not generalize to other systems

## Glossary

- **Ra (Roughness Average)**: Mean absolute deviation of surface profile from mean line; most common surface roughness parameter
- **Lc value**: Spatial frequency cutoff for high-pass filter in profilometry; 25 µm used here
- **Y-TZP**: Yttria-stabilized tetragonal zirconia polycrystals; the dominant dental zirconia type
- **Alicona IFS G3**: Optical profilometry system using focus variation; non-contact measurement
- **CAD/CAM**: Computer-aided design/computer-aided manufacturing; digital fabrication pathway for ceramic restorations
