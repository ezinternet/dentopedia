## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (JARO 2023) summarizing recent developments in diagnosing and treating somatosensory tinnitus (ST) — tinnitus influenced by somatosensory input from the cervical spine and/or temporomandibular area.

A 4-criterion clinical decision tree diagnoses ST with 82.2% accuracy (82.5% sensitivity, 79% specificity); once diagnosed, musculoskeletal physical therapy targeting the cervical spine, the temporomandibular area, or both (counseling + exercise + manual techniques) reduces tinnitus severity/loudness in most patients, with rare total remission.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Summarizes a validated 4-criterion clinical decision tree for diagnosing somatosensory tinnitus (ST), reporting 82.2% diagnostic accuracy, 82.5% sensitivity, and 79% specificity.
- Frames ST treatment around musculoskeletal physical therapy (counseling, exercises, manual techniques) directed at cervical spine dysfunction, temporomandibular disorders, or both.
- Notes that other proposed techniques exist but require further investigation in larger RCTs.

## 7. Glossary

- **Somatosensory tinnitus (ST)**: a tinnitus subtype where somatosensory input from the head-neck region (cervical spine, temporomandibular area) is an influencing factor.
- **Decision tree (ST diagnosis)**: a 4-criterion clinical algorithm used to identify patients whose tinnitus is likely somatosensory in origin.
- **Musculoskeletal physical therapy**: combination of counseling, exercise, and manual techniques aimed at restoring normal cervical spine / temporomandibular function.
