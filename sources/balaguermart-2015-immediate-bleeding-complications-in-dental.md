## Why Ingested
Systematic review of immediate bleeding complications after implant placement, focused on mandibular anterior zone, sublingual artery damage, lingual plate perforation, and long implants (≥15 mm) — extends understanding of implant surgical risk; relates to [[oral-surgery/medina-2018-bleeding-dental-extractions-liver-cirrhosis]].

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review of 15 case reports/series (486 articles screened) on severe immediate bleeding after implant placement, mean patient age 60 years.
The mandibular canine region was the most common site, caused by lingual cortical plate perforation damaging the sublingual artery, with implants measuring 15 mm or longer.
Management prioritized securing the airway (41% intubation, 47% tracheotomy), followed by hematoma drainage and artery ligation; no fatalities were reported.

## 세줄요약
임플란트 식립 후 즉시 발생한 중증 출혈에 대한 체계적 문헌고찰 (486편 스크리닝 → 15편 포함), 평균 환자 연령 60세.
출혈 부위는 하악 견치부가 가장 많았고, 원인은 설측 피질골 천공으로 인한 설하동맥 (sublingual artery) 손상이며, 임플란트 길이는 15 mm 이상.
치료는 기도 확보(삽관 41%, 기관절개 47%)를 우선하고 혈종 배액 및 동맥 결찰로 출혈 조절; 사망 보고 없음.

- **Journal**: Medicina Oral, Patología Oral y Cirugía Bucal, 2015;15(3):e428–? (date 2015-03-01)

- **Institution**: University of Valencia, Spain

## 2. Key Contributions
- Identifies mandibular canine region as highest bleeding-risk zone in implant surgery.
- Links lingual cortical plate perforation → sublingual artery damage as the dominant mechanism.
- Associates bleeding with implants ≥15 mm in length.
- Documents emergency airway-first management protocol.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review (PubMed + Embase, English, up to May 2014).
- **n**: 486 articles screened → 28 after dedup → 15 included.
- **Outcomes**: Time to hemorrhage, implant length, cause of bleeding, treatment.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Most common site: mandibular canine (6 cases), then incisor (2), premolar (1); half unspecified.
- Cause specified in 7 series: lingual plate perforation → sublingual artery damage.
- Implant length (when specified): always ≥15 mm.
- Bleeding timing: immediate 70.6%; up to 7 h post-op.
- Treatment: gauze pressure effective in 1 case; intubation 41%; tracheotomy 47%; hematoma drainage; artery ligation (facial/lingual arteries when needed due to anastomoses).
- Upper jaw: one case post indirect sinus lift, posterior superior alveolar artery (PSAA) damage, Caldwell-Luc window + cauterization.
- No fatalities. No heavy bleeding reported in pterygoid region.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- All case reports/series (n=1 each except two with n=2) → possible underreporting.
- Excluded anticoagulated patients.
- Implant length often unspecified.

## 6. Related Work
- Mardinger 2007: median distance sublingual/submental arteries to alveolar crest 15 mm incisor/canine, 19 mm molar; 4 mm (chin) / 2 mm (canine/molar) to lingual plate.
- Katsumi 2013: four anatomical types of sublingual/submental branches; types II–IV closer to lingual plate → higher risk.

## 7. Glossary
- **Sublingual artery**: branch of lingual artery irrigating floor of mouth; runs close to lingual cortical plate in anterior mandible.
- **Lingual cortical plate perforation**: breach of the lingual mandibular bone during drilling/placement, the main cause of severe bleeding.
- **Posterior superior alveolar artery (PSAA)**: maxillary artery branch involved in rare upper-jaw bleeding post sinus lift.
- **Floor of the mouth (FOM)**: space beneath tongue; hematoma here can threaten airway.
