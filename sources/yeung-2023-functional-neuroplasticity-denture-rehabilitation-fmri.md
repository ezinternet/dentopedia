## Why Ingested

Extends the "masticatory disuse → central/peripheral consequences, and whether prosthetic rehabilitation reverses them" thesis to the **brain (central) side**. The wiki already holds the peripheral/bone-side analogue — occlusal hypofunction drives alveolar bone loss and is partly reversible ([[bone-biology/xu-2016-sclerostin-wnt-occlusal-hypofunction-alveolar-bone]], [[bone-biology/liu-2015-occlusal-hypofunction-recovery-mandibular-alveolar-bone-rats]]) — and the tooth-loss ↔ cognition/frailty link ([[geriatric-dentistry/dibello-2022-oral-frailty-indicators-adverse-health-outcomes]], [[geriatric-dentistry/silva-2026-oral-health-alzheimers-disease-life-course]]). This systematic review supplies the human fMRI evidence that denture rehabilitation restores sensorimotor cortical activation, closing the disuse→rehabilitation loop on the neural side. Ingested to anchor a future overview on masticatory disuse and its reversibility.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PICO + 3-database search + RoB, no meta-analysis) of nine fMRI studies (n≈4–20 each; eight complete-edentulous, one partial-edentulous) on how complete dentures, implant-supported overdentures, and implant-supported fixed dentures change task-evoked brain activation.

During jaw-clenching, implant-supported fixed dentures were associated with the highest activation in primary sensorimotor cortex (precentral/postcentral gyri) vs overdentures and complete dentures; new complete dentures produce a transient activation rise that returns to baseline after a ~3-month habituation; gum-chewing results were mixed (overdentures sometimes LOWER than complete dentures, attributed to more stable/regular chewing).

Clinically, restoring occlusion does remap sensorimotor cortical activation, but this is functional neuroplasticity (a BOLD-signal change), NOT demonstrated cognitive benefit — samples are tiny, paradigms heterogeneous, several studies used subjective signal grading, and no meta-analysis was possible.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (PICO + 3개 DB 검색 + 비뚤림평가, 메타분석 불가) — 9편의 기능적 자기공명영상 (functional MRI, fMRI) 연구(각 n≈4–20; 8편 완전무치악, 1편 부분무치악)를 종합해 총의치·임플란트지지 피개의치·임플란트지지 고정성의치가 과제유발 뇌활성을 어떻게 바꾸는지 정리.

이악물기 (Jaw-clenching) 시 임플란트지지 고정성의치가 일차감각운동피질(중심전회 PreCG·중심후회 PostCG)에서 가장 높은 활성과 연관됐고, 새 총의치는 일시적 활성 증가 후 약 3개월 적응기 (habituation)를 거쳐 기저선으로 복귀; 껌씹기 (Gum-chewing)는 결과가 혼재(피개의치가 총의치보다 오히려 낮은 활성 — 더 안정적·규칙적 저작 때문으로 해석).

임상적으로 교합 회복이 감각운동피질 활성을 재구성(재배선)하는 것은 맞지만, 이는 기능적 신경가소성 (functional neuroplasticity, BOLD 신호 변화)일 뿐 인지기능 향상이 입증된 것은 아님 — 표본이 매우 작고 패러다임이 이질적이며 일부 연구는 주관적 신호 등급을 사용, 메타분석 불가.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. First systematic collation of the fMRI evidence on denture rehabilitation (complete denture vs implant overdenture vs implant-supported fixed denture), framing prosthetic rehabilitation as a driver of **functional neuroplasticity**.
2. Establishes a rehabilitation-hierarchy signal for jaw-clenching: implant-supported **fixed** dentures > implant-supported overdentures > complete dentures in evoked primary sensorimotor (PreCG/PostCG) activation.
3. Documents a **habituation trajectory**: new complete dentures transiently increase PreCG/PostCG activation (peak ~1 week, T2) that returns to baseline by ~3 months (T3), paralleling behavioral chewing-efficiency/bite-force adaptation.
4. Surfaces a **paradigm-dependent contradiction**: gum-chewing studies sometimes show overdentures with LOWER activation than complete dentures (interpreted as more stable, kinematically regular chewing → less cortical recruitment), the opposite direction to the clenching hierarchy.
5. Rigorous critical appraisal — flags subjective signal-grading ("no/mild/strong signal"), unblinded assessment, unadjusted multiple comparisons (Padmanabhan MMSE), and the impossibility of neuroimaging meta-analysis (no reported brain coordinates in 6/9 studies; only 3 reported coordinates).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **PICO**: Population = patients with missing teeth; Intervention = tooth replacement by fixed/removable dentures; Comparison = different tooth-replacement methods; Outcome = task-evoked brain activity.
- **Search**: 2023-02-01, Web of Science + Scopus + PubMed; string `((denture* OR overdenture*) AND (fMRI OR "functional magnetic resonance" OR "functional MRI"))`; no language/type filters. 31 records → 15 duplicates removed → 16 assessed → excluded (non-English n=3; no tooth replacement n=3; no comparison n=1) → **9 included**. Citation chasing confirmed completeness.
- **Risk of bias**: RoB 2 for the single randomized crossover (Shoi); RoBANS for the eight non-randomized studies.
- **Study designs pooled**: 7 within-group (longitudinal, same patients across denture states) + 2 between-group (cross-sectional/retrospective). Only 3 collected a pre-intervention (old-denture or edentulous) baseline.
- **Tasks**: jaw/denture-teeth clenching (most common) and gum-chewing (next); single-study tasks — teeth-tapping, lip-pursing, memory recall, continuous performance task, go/no-go.
- **Synthesis**: narrative only — heterogeneity + missing MNI/Talairach coordinates precluded coordinate-based (ALE-style) meta-analysis (needs ≥17–20 studies with whole-brain coordinates).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Jaw-clenching (n=5 studies):**
- *Luraschi* (n=11, longitudinal, old→new complete dentures, T0–T3): PreCG + PostCG (bilateral) activated across clenching/tapping/lip-pursing; clenching activation **rose significantly at T2 (1 wk) vs T0, then returned to baseline at T3 (3 mo)**. Chewing efficiency improved T1→T2→T3; max bite force rose T2→T3; overall T0→T3 changes non-significant.
- *Tan* (n=4): switching complete denture → implant overdenture caused an **initial DECREASE** in sensorimotor activation at 1 week, largely recovering by 6 weeks — opposite initial direction to Luraschi's new-complete-denture increase.
- *Verma* (n=12, subjective signal grading): implant mandibular overdenture (3 mo) → stronger signals in PreCG, PostCG, hippocampus, insula, frontal/temporal lobe vs complete denture.
- *Bhattacharjee* (n=18, 3 groups): implant-supported **fixed** dentures → significantly more PreCG/PostCG, angular gyrus, temporal-lobe activation; but **no** group difference in active tactile sensibility or oral stereognosis.
- *Yan* (n=20, cross-sectional): higher proportion of fixed-denture patients showed PreCG/PostCG activation during clenching vs overdenture/complete denture (socioeconomic confounding unadjusted).

**Gum-chewing (n=4 studies):**
- *Kimoto* (n=4): implant overdenture → **reduced** bilateral prefrontal activation vs complete denture (attributed to more stable, kinematically regular chewing).
- *Padmanabhan* (n=10): overdenture → reduced prefrontal vs edentulous; vs complete denture, overdenture had higher insula but LOWER hippocampus. Memory-recall task: overdenture → higher hippocampus + prefrontal vs complete denture. MMSE overdenture 25.5 > complete denture 24.0 > edentulous 20.5 (median) — **but** unadjusted for 3 pairwise Wilcoxon tests; at Bonferroni p<0.017 the CD-vs-overdenture difference is non-significant.
- *Nakasato* (n=14): new > old complete dentures → higher PreCG, prefrontal, putamen, cerebellum, inferior parietal; masseter EMG higher with new dentures and correlated with fMRI in hippocampus/parahippocampal/PostCG; neuropsych (TMT-A, RAVLT, R-OCFT) improved — but **no** statistical link tested between cognition and mastication/fMRI.
- *Shoi* (n=11, only partial-edentulous study): full dental-arch RPD > shortened-arch RPD → higher bilateral medial/middle frontal gyri activation during chewing, with better food comminuting/mixing and subjective chewing.

**Bottom line hierarchy**: clenching → fixed > overdenture > complete denture (more activation is framed as "better", restored peripheral feedback); chewing → direction reverses in places (more stable prosthesis = less activation). Sample sizes ≤20 throughout (median taste/food fMRI study n=35 by comparison).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No cognitive-benefit proof.** fMRI BOLD activation changes are neuroplastic remapping, not demonstrated cognitive improvement; cognitive/MMSE signals are preliminary, confounded (practice effects, no denture-removal re-test, unadjusted multiplicity, small n).
- Tiny samples (n=4–20), heterogeneous designs/tasks, mostly within-subject without true baseline; two between-group studies unadjusted for socioeconomic confounding (fixed dentures ≈ wealthier patients).
- Methodological weakness: 3 studies graded fMRI signal subjectively (no/mild/strong) rather than statistical whole-brain analysis; 6/9 reported no brain coordinates → **meta-analysis impossible**.
- Direction-of-change is task-dependent and not mechanistically resolved (higher activation is not unambiguously "better").
- Future work called for: whole-brain coordinate reporting, larger cohorts, food-hardness/taste/food-picture paradigms, appetite neuropeptide/hormone (leptin/ghrelin/orexin) correlation, subcortical (hypothalamus/basal ganglia) coverage, and true edentulous-removal control conditions.

## 7. Glossary

- **fMRI (functional MRI)** — non-invasive imaging of brain activity via blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) signal.
- **Functional neuroplasticity** — task-evoked change in the magnitude/location of brain activation after a peripheral change (here, prosthetic rehabilitation); a signal change, not proof of cognitive gain.
- **PreCG (precentral gyrus)** — primary motor cortex.
- **PostCG (postcentral gyrus)** — primary somatosensory cortex.
- **Implant-supported overdenture** — removable denture retained/supported by implants (commonly maxillary complete denture + 2-implant mandibular overdenture).
- **Implant-supported fixed denture** — non-removable full-arch implant prosthesis.
- **Shortened dental arch** — arch replaced only to the premolars (molars not restored).
- **Habituation** — adaptation period (~3 months here) after which evoked activation normalizes.
- **RoB 2 / RoBANS** — risk-of-bias tools for randomized and non-randomized studies, respectively.
- **MMSE** — Mini-Mental State Examination (cognitive screen).
