## Why Ingested

This paper proposes a novel materials-engineering route to use **unmodified (intact) MTA** itself as a root canal sealer — not as an obturation core material, and not as a reformulated MTA-based sealer product — by instead hydrophilizing the **gutta-percha (GP) surface** (atmospheric plasma + cetylpyridinium chloride, CPC, surfactant treatment) so that it wets and seals against hydrophilic MTA. This is a distinct angle from the existing calcium-silicate/bioceramic premixed-sealer cluster (CeraSeal, BioRoot, NeoSealer, TotalFill) already in the wiki, which modifies the *sealer formulation*, not the core material surface. Wikilinked to [[wiki/overviews/ceraseal-bioceramic-sealer-clinical-material-synthesis]] as the existing bioceramic-sealer material synthesis this paper's finding contrasts/extends — same MTA-family chemistry, but via a completely different sealer-delivery strategy. Also wikilinked to [[wiki/endodontics/kumar-2022-cytotoxicity-bioc-ceraseal-mtafillapex-ahplus]], whose MTT-assay data show that MTA-Fillapex (an MTA-based sealer modified with salicylate-resin additives) plateaus at only moderate (~60%) recovered viability versus >90% for additive-free calcium-silicate sealers — the exact additive-driven biocompatibility cost this paper cites as its rationale for keeping MTA intact and modifying the GP surface instead.

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro materials study (flattened gutta-percha, GC Gutta-percha Bar Plus, n=9/group for contact angle, n=4/group for XPS/XRD; plus n=3/group single-rooted-tooth root canal specimens) testing atmospheric air plasma (PT), 100 mM cetylpyridinium chloride immersion (CT), and combined plasma+CPC (PC) surface treatment of gutta-percha to enable intact, unmodified MTA to function as a root canal sealer without added setting-modifier/handling components.

Combined plasma+CPC treatment reduced GP water contact angle from 113.27°±5.64 (untreated, hydrophobic) to ~22° (hydrophilic) immediately after treatment and showed the most stable hydrophilic effect at 8 weeks (versus hydrophobic recovery of plasma-only by 2 weeks); surface-treated GP + intact MTA groups showed significantly less dye penetration/interfacial voids at the sealer-GP interface than untreated GP (P<0.05), performing comparably to a conventional ZnO-based sealer (Canals-N, P>0.05), while CPC significantly prolonged MTA setting time but did not significantly alter compressive strength or flowability.

This in-vitro proof-of-concept suggests hydrophilized GP paired with additive-free intact MTA as a biocompatibility-motivated alternative to modified MTA-based sealers, pending clinical validation, micro-CT confirmation of 3D interfacial gaps, and resolution of MTA's sub-ISO flowability.

## 세줄요약

In-vitro 재료학 연구(GP 판상 시편, GC Gutta-percha Bar Plus, 접촉각 n=9/군, XPS·XRD n=4/군; 단근치 근관 시편 n=3/군)로 대기압 플라즈마(PT)·100mM 세틸피리디늄 클로라이드(Cetylpyridinium Chloride, CPC) 침지(CT)·플라즈마+CPC 병용(PC) 표면처리를 통해 무기질삼산화물응집체 (Mineral Trioxide Aggregate, MTA)를 첨가제 없이 그대로("intact") 근관 실러로 사용할 수 있는지 평가했다.

플라즈마+CPC 병용처리는 GP 접촉각을 113.27°±5.64(미처리, 소수성)에서 처리 직후 약 22°(친수성)로 낮췄고, 8주 후에도 가장 안정적인 친수성을 유지(플라즈마 단독은 2주 내 소수성 회복)했으며, 표면처리 GP+intact MTA 군은 미처리 GP군보다 실러-GP 계면 색소침투/공극이 유의하게 적어(P<0.05) 기존 ZnO계 실러(Canals-N)와 대등한 결과를 보였으나(P>0.05), CPC는 MTA 경화시간을 유의하게 연장시켰고(압축강도·유동성은 유의차 없음).

이 in-vitro 개념증명은 첨가제 없는 intact MTA + 표면처리 GP 조합을 첨가제 기반 MTA 실러의 생체적합성 대안으로 제시하나, 임상 검증·micro-CT를 통한 3차원 계면 간극 확인·MTA의 ISO 유동성 기준 미달 문제 해결이 필요하다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Proposes using **intact (unmodified) MTA** as a root canal sealer by hydrophilizing the **gutta-percha surface** instead of adding setting-modifier/handling components to the MTA formulation — reframing the sealer-affinity problem as a GP-surface problem rather than an MTA-reformulation problem.
- Combines two hydrophilizing treatments — **atmospheric air plasma** and **cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC)** cationic surfactant immersion — and shows plasma-before-CPC (PC) gives the most durable hydrophilic effect (least hydrophobic recovery over 8 weeks) of the three treatments tested.
- XPS evidence (N1s/Cl2p bands, r=0.99 correlation between surface free energy and CPC-characteristic peak intensity) mechanistically confirms CPC molecular attachment to the GP surface as the driver of wettability improvement, distinct from plasma's transient -NH2 group effect.
- Demonstrates that surface-treated GP + intact MTA achieves interfacial sealing (dye penetration) comparable to a conventional ZnO-based sealer, while avoiding the additive-driven cytotoxicity risk documented for modified MTA-based sealers elsewhere in the literature.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **GP specimens**: Flattened GC Gutta-percha Bar Plus (1.5 mm thick) in 4 groups — control/untreated, plasma (PT), CPC (CT), plasma+CPC (PC). n=9 (contact angle), n=4 (XPS), n=4 (XRD).
- **Surface treatment**: Atmospheric air plasma (PIB-10, 6 mA, 1 min); CPC solution 100 mM (358 mg CPC monohydrate/10 mL distilled water), 5-min dip, mild air-blow dry.
- **Wettability**: Contact angle (Phoenix Alpha, 5 µL water droplet, ImageJ) and derived surface free energy (SFE) measured immediately, and at 2/4/8 weeks post-treatment.
- **Surface chemistry**: XPS (C1s/O1s/Cl2p/N1s, ESCA-850, Mg Kα) to characterize surface bonding; pure-CPC spectra used as a reference to confirm CPC surface attachment in CT/PC groups.
- **MTA physicochemistry**: XRD of MTA powder (TMR-MTA Mielle) mixed with water vs 100 mM CPC (day 1, day 7); ISO 6876:2012 flowability; Vicat-needle setting time; compressive strength (cylindrical specimens, days 1 and 7, Autograph AG-IS).
- **Interfacial sealing**: Single-rooted extracted teeth, single-cone GPP #80 obturation; Groups A (untreated GP+MTA) through D (surface-treated GP+MTA) vs Group E (conventional ZnO-based sealer, Canals-N) as reference, n=3/group. Coronal methylene blue dye penetration (7 days), horizontal cross-sectional grinding, dye-penetration distance and sealer-GP/sealer-dentin interfacial void assessment under light microscopy + ImageJ.
- **Stats**: SPSS v26; one-way ANOVA + Tukey/Games-Howell; Mann-Whitney U for non-normal two-group comparisons; P<0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Contact angle**: control 113.27°±5.64 (hydrophobic) → PT 22.21°±4.42, CT 19.54°±5.24, PC 21.99°±6.08 immediately post-treatment (all P<0.05 vs control).
- **Durability of effect**: PT reverted toward control (hydrophobic recovery) by 2 weeks; **PC remained significantly lower than control even at 8 weeks** — the most stable hydrophilic surface of the three treatments.
- **XPS**: PT showed a single N1s peak (400.5 eV, -NH2); CT/PC showed N1s (402.2 eV + subpeak) and Cl2p (~197 eV) bands matching pure-CPC spectra — confirms CPC coating in CT/PC groups. SFE correlated with N1s/Cl2p intensity at r=0.99 across CT/PC groups.
- **MTA physicochemistry with CPC as mixing liquid**: only **setting time** significantly different (prolonged, P<0.05); compressive strength lower at day 1 but higher at day 7 with CPC (not significant overall, P>0.05); flowability not significantly different between mixing liquids, though **neither met the ISO 6876 diameter >20 mm threshold**.
- **Dye penetration**: Group A (untreated GP + intact MTA) had **significantly higher** dye penetration/interfacial void formation than all other groups (P<0.05); Groups B–D (surface-treated GP) were **not significantly different** from Group E (ZnO reference, P>0.05); dentin-wall/sealer interface dye penetration showed no group differences (P>0.05, intact MTA already bonds dentin well).
- **Void formation** at the sealer-GP interface was observed only in Group A (untreated GP).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Interfacial sealing assessed by 2D dye penetration/serial cross-sections, not micro-CT — no true 3D void/gap quantification.
- In-vitro bench study only; does not replicate long-term intraoral/clinical conditions (humidity cycling, occlusal loading, bacterial challenge).
- Sample sizes based on prior studies/pilot data without a formal power analysis — statistical power should be interpreted cautiously.
- MTA flowability (with either water or CPC as mixing liquid) fell short of the ISO 6876 requirement, a formulation gap the authors flag for further work.
- No cytotoxicity/biocompatibility testing was performed in this paper itself — the additive-avoidance rationale is motivated by prior literature, not confirmed here directly.

## 6. Related Work

- Motivated by literature (cited in Introduction) showing MTA-based sealer additives (methylcellulose, calcium chloride, propylene glycol) can affect gingival-fibroblast cell viability — the concern this paper's whole design tries to sidestep by keeping MTA intact.
- Prior plasma-treatment literature on hydrophobic-recovery kinetics of plasma-modified polymer surfaces, consistent with this paper's PT-group finding.
- Prior CPC/surfactant literature on root-canal wettability enhancement, extended here to the GP core material itself rather than dentin or irrigants.
- Contrasts with the wiki's premixed calcium-silicate/bioceramic sealer cluster (CeraSeal, Bio-C, MTA-Fillapex, TotalFill, BioRoot), which solves sealer-GP affinity by reformulating the sealer rather than treating the GP surface.

## 7. Glossary

- **MTA (Mineral Trioxide Aggregate, 무기질삼산화물응집체)** — calcium-silicate-based biomaterial, biocompatible/bioactive, historically used for root-end filling, pulp capping, apexification, perforation repair; here proposed as an intact (unmodified) root canal sealer.
- **Intact MTA** — MTA used without added setting-modifier or handling-improvement components, to avoid additive-driven cytotoxicity.
- **GP (Gutta-Percha)** — 1,4-trans-polyisoprene; the standard root-canal core obturation material; naturally hydrophobic and poorly adhesive.
- **Plasma treatment (PT)** — atmospheric air plasma surface modification; improves wettability/roughness transiently (hydrophobic recovery over weeks).
- **CPC (Cetylpyridinium Chloride, 세틸피리디늄 클로라이드)** — cationic surfactant (hydrophilic pyridinium ring + Cl⁻ counterion, hydrophobic C16 chain) used to hydrophilize the GP surface.
- **PC group** — combined plasma-then-CPC treatment; showed the most durable hydrophilic effect.
- **SFE (Surface Free Energy, 표면자유에너지)** — derived from contact angle; higher/lower values track wettability changes over time.
- **XPS (X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy)** — surface elemental/chemical-bonding analysis used to confirm CPC attachment on GP.
- **Canals-N** — conventional zinc-oxide (ZnO)-based root canal sealer used as the comparator/reference in the interfacial sealing experiment.
- **Hydrophobic recovery** — the tendency of plasma-treated polymer surfaces to revert toward their original hydrophobicity over time.
