## Why Ingested

Provides 5-year retrospective evidence (n=321) that direct resin composite with cuspal coverage achieves 94.1% tooth survival for cracked teeth — directly challenging the prevailing assumption that indirect restorations are required. Identifies periodontal lesion from crack propagation (OR=10.52) as the dominant independent risk factor for tooth loss, stronger than endodontic treatment (OR=2.67). Complements [[kaur-2026-cracked-tooth-syndrome-umbrella-review]] (which surveys evidence base) and [[zhang-2024-cracked-teeth-treatment-outcomes-sr-ma]] (SR/MA on treatment outcomes).

## One-line Summary

Retrospective 5-year study (n=321): cuspal coverage direct resin composite achieved 94.1% tooth survival; periodontal lesion (OR=10.52) dominates risk over fracture type or vitality.

## 한줄요약

후향적 5년 연구(n=321): 교두피개 직접 레진 복합 수복으로 치아 생존율 94.1% 달성; 치주 병소(OR=10.52)가 골절 유형이나 치수 활력보다 더 강한 독립적 위험인자.

## Key Contributions

1. Demonstrates that direct resin composite cuspal coverage can achieve comparable survival to indirect restorations at 5 years (94.1%), challenging the indirect-first paradigm.
2. Identifies periodontal lesion from crack propagation (OR=10.52, 95% CI 4.1–27.1) as the strongest independent predictor of tooth loss — overshadowing fracture type, vitality, and pulp calcification.
3. Endodontic treatment as secondary independent risk factor (OR=2.67, 95% CI 1.04–6.89), consistent with reduced prognosis of endodontically treated cracked teeth.
4. Establishes that fracture type (full vs partial cusp coverage design) was NOT a statistically significant predictor of tooth loss (p>0.05).

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective clinical study; single operator (private practice, January 1991–December 2018)
- **Ethics**: Declaration of Helsinki; university ethics committee approval
- **Inclusion**: Posterior cracked teeth, ≥5-year follow-up, no pre-existing endodontic treatment
- **Sample**: 321 patients (mean age 53.9 years); 79 maxillary premolars, 21 mandibular premolars, 78 maxillary molars, 143 mandibular molars
- **Interventions**:
  - Full cusp coverage direct resin composite
  - Partial cusp coverage (cracked cusp only) + box-shaped reinforcement of opposing walls
  - No indirect restorations in any group
- **Primary outcome**: Tooth survival at 5 years
- **Statistical analysis**: Stepwise logistic regression to identify predictors of tooth loss

## Key Results

- **Tooth survival at 5 years**: 94.1% (302/321 teeth); 19 teeth extracted
- **Root canal treatments**: 126/321 (39.2%) performed during follow-up
- **Periodontal lesions**: 41/321 (12.8%) developed due to crack deepening
- **Risk factors for tooth loss** (stepwise logistic regression):
  - Periodontal lesion: OR=10.52, 95% CI 4.1–27.1, p<0.0001
  - Endodontic treatment: OR=2.67, 95% CI 1.04–6.89, p=0.04
  - Fracture type: not significant (p>0.05)
  - Pulp calcification: not significant (p>0.05)
  - Tooth vitality: not significant (p>0.05)
  - Apical periodontitis: not significant (p>0.05)
- Both direct restoration types (full vs partial cusp coverage) showed similar clinical performance

## Limitations

- Retrospective single-operator design; selection bias possible
- No direct comparison arm with indirect restorations
- Private practice setting may not represent general population
- Abstract-only source; full methodology details not available (no PMC full text)
- Long recruitment span (1991–2018) with possible evolution of materials and technique

## Glossary

- **Cuspal coverage**: Restorative design that covers and protects one or more cusps to prevent further crack propagation
- **OR (Odds Ratio)**: Measure of association; OR=10.52 means ~10.5× higher odds of tooth loss with periodontal lesion
- **Stepwise logistic regression**: Statistical method sequentially selecting predictors that significantly improve model fit
