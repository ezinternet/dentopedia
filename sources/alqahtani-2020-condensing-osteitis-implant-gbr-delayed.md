## Why Ingested

First PubMed-indexed case report demonstrating a GBR + delayed implant placement protocol for large condensing osteitis (골화성 골염, Condensing Osteitis, CO) in the mandible. Provides a concrete clinical instance for [[implants/survival/taghsimi-2022-implant-hyperdense-jaw-lesions-narrative-review]], which reviews hyperdense jaw lesion management broadly, and contrasts with [[implants/survival/kim-2026-dental-implant-osteoporosis-osteosclerosis]] on bone density considerations. The protocol (atraumatic extraction → immediate GBR → 6-month healing → implant) represents one end of the management spectrum, with immediate basal implant being the other end documented in the literature.

## Three-line Summary

A mandibular right second premolar (No. 45) presented with a large radiopaque osseous mass at the root apex, diagnosed as condensing osteitis based on clinical and radiologic findings. The tooth was atraumatically extracted under local anesthesia, immediately followed by guided bone regeneration (골유도재생술, Guided Bone Regeneration, GBR). A dental implant was placed 6 months after healing, completing a staged GBR + delayed implant protocol for large CO.

## 세줄요약

하악 우측 제2소구치(No. 45) 근첨부에 거대 방사선불투과성 골성 종괴(골화성 골염, Condensing Osteitis, CO)가 발견됨. 무외상 발치 후 즉시 골유도재생술(GBR)을 시행하고 6개월 치유 후 임플란트를 식립하는 단계적 프로토콜을 적용함. GBR+지연 식립 접근이 대형 골화성 골염 부위의 임플란트 치료를 위한 선택지임을 임상 증례로 제시한 최초 문헌.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Demonstrates feasibility of dental implant placement after treatment of large condensing osteitis
- Documents sequential protocol: atraumatic extraction → immediate GBR → 6-month healing → implant placement
- Differentiates condensing osteitis from other radiopaque jaw lesions (bone-borne vs. tissue-borne)
- Contrasts with immediate basal implant approaches (e.g., Hassan 2023) — two valid protocols at opposite ends of staging spectrum

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

**Patient presentation:**
- Mandibular right second premolar (tooth No. 45)
- Radiopaque mass attached to root apex
- Differential diagnosis: bone-borne (condensing osteitis, cemento-ossifying fibroma, sclerosing osteomyelitis) vs. tissue-borne diseases

**Definitive diagnosis:** Condensing osteitis — based on:
- Bone density characteristics
- Trabeculation pattern on radiograph

**Treatment sequence:**
1. Atraumatic extraction under local anesthesia
2. Immediate guided bone regeneration (GBR) at the extraction site
3. 6 months healing period
4. Dental implant placement

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Successful dental implant placement following GBR protocol
- No complications reported
- Outcome: osseointegrated implant (implied by completion of case report)

## 7. Glossary

- **골화성 골염 (Condensing Osteitis, CO):** A localized bone sclerosis reaction to low-grade pulpal infection, presenting as a radiopaque periapical mass
- **골유도재생술 (Guided Bone Regeneration, GBR):** Membrane-barrier technique to regenerate bone defects prior to or concurrent with implant placement
- **무외상 발치 (Atraumatic Extraction):** Extraction technique preserving alveolar bone, typically using periotomes/luxators
- **지연 식립 (Delayed Implant Placement):** Implant placement after complete or near-complete socket healing (≥4 months post-extraction)
