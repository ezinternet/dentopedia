## Abstract

Aim: The aim of this review was to evaluate the survival rate of axial and tilted implants in rehabilitation of edentulous jaws using all on four concept. Setting and Design: Systematic Review. Materials and Methods: A literature review was performed in MEDLINE, PubMed Central (PMC), Google scholar, Embase, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials. Hand searches were conducted of the bibliographic of related journals and systematic reviews. A total of 380 articles were obtained from the intial screening process. Of these articles, 25 articles fulfilled the inclusion criteria. The authors performed evaluation of articles independently as well as data extraction and quality assessment. Statistical Analysis Used: Qualitative analysis. Results: The major prosthetic complication was the fracture of the acrylic prosthesis. The mean cumulative survival rate of implants (72-132 months) were 94% to 98%. The prosthesis survival rate (12 months) was between 99% to 100%. The averaged bone loss was 1.3 ±0.4 mm (12-60 months). No Significant difference was found between survival rates of axial and tilted implants nor between maxilla and mandible. Conclusion: All on four concept can be employed successfully in the edentulous patients with resorbed ridges while improving their quality of life and reducing morbidity. However, randomized clinical trials with large sampling size and long term follow up should be incorporated.

## Introduction

The occurrence of edentulism among elderly patients has been shown to have a negative impact on their quality of life. It is a debilitating and irreversible condition leading to functional impairment and physical, psychological, and social disability. The treatment options available for these patients are complete dentures and removable or fixed implant-supported prosthesis. Prosthetic rehabilitation of completely edentulous patients with implants is a reliable mode of treatment, but its success depends on the availability of good quality and quantity of bone. Patients with severe resorption of the alveolar bone require prior surgical intervention such as bone augmentation and sinus lift procedures for a successful outcome. These techniques increase patient morbidity and treatment fees and can have associated complications. To overcome these disadvantages, the concept of "All-on-four™" was introduced by Paulo Malo in 2003. This concept demonstrates placing two anterior implants in an axial position and two posterior implants with a tilt of up to 45° to support a full-arch fixed restoration. Bone grafting is avoided by tilting the posterior implants, thus utilizing the available bone.

## Materials and Methods

Search: MEDLINE, PMC, Google Scholar, Embase, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials. Keywords: "All on 4," "All-on-four," "tilted implant," "angled implant," "upright implant," "axial implant," "four implants," "edentulous patient," "edentulous mandible," "edentulous maxilla". Inclusion: Clinical trials reporting survival rates of axial/tilted implants, prosthesis survival, bone level changes, minimum 1-year follow-up. Exclusion: Systematic reviews, case reports, biomechanical trials, finite-element analyses, >4 implants, zygomatic/pterygoid implants. Only human studies. Data collected: author, study design, number of jaws, total implants, maxilla/mandible distribution, implant survival, prosthesis survival, marginal bone loss. 380 articles screened, 70 considered, 45 excluded, 25 included (14 prospective, 11 retrospective).

## Results

**Included Studies (25):** Agliardi et al. (2010, 2010), Babbush et al. (2011), Butura et al. (2011), Capelli et al. (2007), Crespi et al. (2012), Francetti et al. (2008, 2012), Hinze et al. (2010), Landázuri-Del Barrio et al. (2013), Maló et al. (2007, 2012, 2012, 2005, 2015, 2015, 2003), Galindo et al. (2012), Balshi et al. (2014), Di et al. (2013), Malo et al. (2011), Agliardi et al. (2014), Cavalli et al. (2012), Lopes et al. (2015), Browaeys et al. (2015), Krennmair et al. (2013), Maló et al. (2015).

**Implant Survival Rates:**
- Cumulative survival rates (72-132 months): 94% to 98%
- No significant difference between axial and tilted implants
- No significant difference between maxilla and mandible
- Maló et al. (2011): CSR 98.3% at 12mo, 96.3% at 96mo (mandible)
- Butura et al. (2011): Axial SR 99.54%, Tilted SR 99.7%
- Capelli et al. (2007): CSR 100% both axial and tilted

**Prosthesis Survival Rates:**
- 12 months: 99% to 100%
- Most common complication: fracture of provisional acrylic prosthesis
- Other: wear of opposing dentition, prosthetic screw loosening in bruxers

**Marginal Bone Loss:**
- Average: 1.3 ± 0.4 mm (12-60 months)
- No significant difference between axial and tilted implants
- No significant difference between maxilla and mandible
- Maló et al. (2015): Axial 1.74mm, Tilted 1.76mm at 5 years
- Krennmair et al. (2013): Axial 1.17±0.26mm, Tilted 1.24±0.8mm at 5 years

## Discussion

The All-on-4 concept offers predictable treatment for edentulous patients while eliminating regenerative procedures. Benefits: fixed prosthesis on day of surgery, shorter treatment time, lower cost vs conventional modalities. No statistically significant difference in survival rates of axial vs tilted implants. Due to absence of RCTs, efficacy cannot be definitively evaluated, but prognosis appears excellent. Marginal bone loss <1.5mm average for 12 months. Prosthesis complications mainly acrylic fracture in bruxers - recommend metal framework reinforcement.

## Conclusion

All-on-4 treatment concept is a viable approach for edentulous jaws with cost-effective treatment, decreased treatment times, and higher patient quality of life. Implant SR 98% to 94.8% for 72-132 months follow-up. Marginal bone loss 1.5-1.7mm for axial and tilted implants at 12-60 months. Prosthesis SR 99-100% at 12 months. Proper patient selection, thorough evaluation, and good surgical skills essential for predictable outcomes.
