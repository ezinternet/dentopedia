## Why Ingested

치위생사 스케일링 술기(2026-07-20 사용자 요청) 토픽스윕의 일부 — 초음파 스케일링 중 에어로졸 노출을 실제로 통제하는 변수(흡인 기구 선택)를 다룬 감염관리 공학 논문. [[dental-handpiece/matys-2020-dental-aerosol-hazard-risk-dental]]가 마네킹 벤치 모델로 7종 흡인 방식 vs 3가지 시술(우식제거·스케일링·연마)을 비교했다면, 본 논문은 그 설계를 **extends**한다 — 실시간 연속 파티클센서(AUC 기반) 방법론을 도입하고, "능동추종형(dynamic)" vs "고정형(static/hands-free)" 흡인기구라는 명시적 분류축을 세워 6종 구강내 흡인기구를 초음파 스케일링과 하이스피드 핸드피스 양쪽에서 비교했다. 결론은 Matys와 정합적(핸드피스가 스케일러보다 에어로졸 발생량이 많고, 고용량 능동추종 흡인이 배경수준까지 저감) — 별도의 실측 데이터로 같은 방향의 근거를 강화한다.

## Three-line Summary

Controlled bench (dental mannikin) study at the University of Glasgow Dental Clinical Research Facility, real-time particulate sensors (2× Plantower PMS5003, 0.3–10 μm), comparing 6 intra-oral suction devices (dynamic vs static) against no suction during ultrasonic scaling and high-speed handpiece use, n=5 replicates per device.

Without suction, the high-speed handpiece generated 3.4× more particles/min than the ultrasonic scaler (single-sensor setup); every suction device significantly reduced particle counts vs no suction (p<0.001) for both procedures, but only dynamic (instrument-following) high-volume devices — standard HVE, Purevac® (±lightweight hose) — brought counts to near/below background, while static/hands-free devices (DryShield®, ReLeaf™) and standard low-volume suction showed periodic particle "spikes."

Suction-device *type* (dynamic vs static), not just presence/absence of suction, is the operationally controllable determinant of aerosol escape; the study is manikin-based (no saliva/breathing/coughing) with a single operator for the ultrasonic arm, so inter-operator and patient-factor variability remain untested.

## 세줄요약

University of Glasgow Dental Clinical Research Facility의 마네킹 벤치 연구 — 실시간 파티클센서(Plantower PMS5003 2대, 0.3–10 μm)로 초음파 스케일링과 하이스피드 핸드피스 사용 시 구강내 흡인기구 6종(능동추종형 vs 고정형)과 무흡인을 비교(기구당 n=5 반복).

흡인이 없을 때 하이스피드 핸드피스가 초음파 스케일러보다 분당 입자수 3.4배 많았고(단일센서), 모든 흡인기구가 무흡인 대비 유의하게 입자수를 감소시켰으나(p<0.001) 표준 고용량흡인기(HVE)·Purevac® 같은 **능동추종형(dynamic)** 고용량 흡인만 배경수준 근처까지 저감시켰고, DryShield®·ReLeaf™ 같은 **고정형(static/hands-free)** 및 표준 저용량흡인(LVE)은 간헐적 입자 "스파이크"를 보였다.

흡인 유무보다 흡인기구의 **유형**(능동추종 vs 고정형)이 실질적으로 통제 가능한 에어로졸 노출 결정요인이라는 결론 — 마네킹 기반 연구로 타액·호흡·기침 등 환자 요인과 술자간 변이는 미검증(초음파 시술은 단일 술자로만 진행).

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study (per the authors) to compare a wide panel of *commonly used* intra-oral suction devices in a real-time, continuous-monitoring format across two distinct AGPs (ultrasonic scaling and high-speed handpiece use) in the same protocol.
- Introduces an explicit **dynamic** (instrument-following: standard HVE, Purevac® ± lightweight hose) vs **static** (hands-free, fixed position: DryShield®, Ivory® ReLeaf™, standard LVE) suction-device taxonomy.
- Quantifies that high-speed handpiece use generates 3.4× more particles/min than ultrasonic scaling in the absence of suction (single-sensor setup).
- Shows all tested suction devices significantly reduce particle counts vs no suction, but only dynamic high-volume devices reduce counts to near/below background for *both* AGPs — static/low-volume devices leave periodic escape "spikes" despite a significant average reduction.
- Uses a normalised, background-subtracted AUC (area-under-curve) particle-count metric that captures continuous escape patterns over the procedure, not just a pre/post snapshot.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Setting**: University of Glasgow Dental Clinical Research Facility; dental mannikin head mounted on a dental unit with standard built-in high/low-volume suction, coolant, and air-turbine functions; separate portable ultrasonic scaling unit. Room assessed at **zero air changes/hour**, windows closed, no mechanical ventilation (deliberate "worst-case" ventilation scenario).
- **Sensors**: 2× Plantower PMS5003 laser-scattering particulate sensors (0.3–10 μm range, reading per 0.1 L air intake) wired to Raspberry Pi single-board computers; custom in-house Bash pipeline → Microsoft Excel / GraphPad Prism analysis.
- **Two sensor arrangements**: "single sensor" (10 cm above the central incisors with a funnel attachment; half-mouth 4-minute scale, right side only) and "dual sensor" (nose + chin, 5 cm from incisal edges; full-mouth 8-minute scale, both sides, 1-minute equipment-transfer pause).
- **Ultrasonic scaling protocol**: Cavitron Touch® Scaling System + Cavitron® DualSelect™ + Powerline® 1000 30K insert; 0.89% saline coolant at 18.0 mL/min; 100% power; predetermined supragingival scaling pattern (parallel to long axis at the gingival margin, equal time per tooth), single solo operator (PW), as is standard hygienist practice.
- **High-speed handpiece protocol**: Midwest Stylus® Plus handpiece with flat-end cylindrical diamond bur; 0.89% saline coolant at 68.0 mL/min; 3 depth cuts × 3 surfaces (labial/incisal/palatal) on the upper-right central incisor over 90 s, mimicking an anterior crown preparation; operator (KP) + assistant (RD), as is standard restorative practice.
- **Suction devices, ultrasonic arm** (6 devices + no-suction control, n=5 each): dynamic = standard HVE, Purevac® HVE System with lightweight hose/adapter ["Purevac (+H)"], Purevac® HVE Mirror Tip direct to port ["Purevac (−H)"]; static = DryShield® Isolation System, Ivory® ReLeaf™ hands-free device, standard low-volume suction (LVE) lingual to the lower incisors.
- **Suction devices, handpiece arm** (3 devices + control, n=5 each): standard HVE, Purevac® (±H), DryShield®.
- **Outcome**: "normalised particle count" = AUC(procedure reading) − AUC(background reading), correcting for ambient particle-level drift between experiments.
- **Statistics**: one-way ANOVA with Tukey's post-hoc test, mean ± SD (n=5 per device), significance threshold α = 0.05 (MS Excel + GraphPad Prism).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Baseline (no suction)**: high-speed handpiece generated **3.4× more particles/min** than the ultrasonic scaler in the single-sensor setup; a comparable amount in the dual-sensor setup.
- **Ultrasonic scaling, both sensor setups**: statistically significant reduction in normalised particle count with *any* suction device vs no suction (p<0.001). Dynamic devices (standard HVE, Purevac ±H) reduced counts to near/below background; static/low-volume devices (standard LVE, DryShield, ReLeaf) remained consistently above background with visually distinct spikes concentrated at the upper/lower labial regions. In the dual-sensor setup, all dynamic devices significantly outperformed standard LVE and ReLeaf; in the single-sensor setup Purevac (+H) significantly outperformed standard LVE.
- **High-speed handpiece, both sensor setups**: every suction device tested produced a significant reduction vs no suction (p<0.001). Dynamic devices again reduced counts to near/below background; DryShield allowed escape of significantly more particles than all dynamic devices (p<0.001) in both setups, with spikes concentrated during labial and incisal-edge preparation.
- Exact mean ± SD normalised particle-count values are reported in the original with powers-of-ten exponents that were **not recoverable from the extracted full-text** (superscript characters dropped in PMC text extraction) — the qualitative pattern (dynamic ≈ background; static/LVE > background, with significance levels) above is reproduced faithfully, but exact magnitudes should be checked against the original figures/tables if needed.
- Pattern was consistent across both the single- and dual-sensor setups and both AGP types.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Manikin-based simulation — cannot fully model patient-derived factors (saliva, coughing/breathing, access difficulty), though the authors cite prior work showing non-contaminated and salivary-contaminated instruments produce comparable particle-distribution profiles during ultrasonic scaling.
- Aerosol plume volume/directionality is inherently unpredictable; only 2–3 sensors positioned near the oral cavity, which may miss some escaping particles — a necessary compromise to preserve operator access.
- Single operator (PW) for all ultrasonic-scaling replicates — inter-operator variability not explored (flagged explicitly as future work).
- Dynamic-suction positioning followed manufacturer IFU precisely, which the authors note "may not always be possible to replicate in all situations" clinically.
- Deliberately used a "worst-case" zero-air-changes/hour, no-mechanical-ventilation environment, unlike prior studies using consistent mechanical ventilation (though those largely focused on fallow-time rather than direct particle-escape measurement).
- Terminology inconsistency across the AGP literature (particles vs aerosols vs splatter vs fallow-time) is flagged by the authors as a likely confounder when comparing studies.
- Future work proposed: operator-variability studies; additional/expanded sensor positions.

## 6. Related Work

- Authors state their real-time, real-world-scenario findings are consistent with Ehtezazi et al. and with [[infection-control/dudding-2022-clinical-observational-analysis-aerosol-emissions]] (both cited in Discussion as reaching similar conclusions on the pattern of particle escape and effective high-volume-suction mitigation).
- Cites prior work (not separately held in this wiki) showing combined intra-oral high+low suction reduces both ultrasonic- and handpiece-generated aerosol to background immediately post-AGP, while cautioning aerosol exposure cannot be fully eliminated.
- Cites prior work (not separately held in this wiki) quantifying that high-speed handpieces generate ≥10× more aerosol particles than ultrasonic scaling, and that high-volume intra-oral suction mitigates ultrasonic-scaling aerosol to background.

## 7. Glossary

- **AGP**: Aerosol-Generating Procedure — dental procedure (e.g., ultrasonic scaling, high-speed handpiece use) presumed to generate airborne particles capable of carrying respiratory pathogens.
- **HVE**: High-Volume Evacuator/suction — wide-bore, high-flow intraoral suction.
- **LVE**: Low-Volume Evacuation — narrow-bore suction (e.g., saliva ejector-type), lower flow than HVE.
- **Dynamic suction**: Device actively repositioned by the operator/assistant to follow the aerosol-generating instrument throughout the procedure (e.g., standard HVE, Purevac®).
- **Static suction**: Hands-free device fixed in one intraoral position for the whole procedure (e.g., DryShield®, Ivory® ReLeaf™).
- **Normalised particle count**: This study's primary outcome — background-subtracted AUC of particle-sensor readings across the duration of the AGP.
- **Plantower PMS5003**: Laser-scattering particulate-matter sensor detecting particles 0.3–10 μm in diameter, read every 0.1 L of air intake.
- **Fallow-time**: Mandated post-AGP interval before an operatory can be re-entered/reused, intended to allow aerosol to settle/clear.
