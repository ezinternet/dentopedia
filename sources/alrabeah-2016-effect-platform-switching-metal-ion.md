## Why Ingested

기존 wiki의 platform switching(PS) 근거는 골보존 효과를 미세간극-염증 거리화(microgap-inflammation distancing)나 응력 재분배(stress redistribution, [[wiki/implants/mbl/yadav-2025-finite-element-analysis-platform-switching]])로 설명하지만, 부식(corrosion)에 의한 금속이온 유출(metal ion release) 경로는 다루지 않았던 gap. 본 in vitro 연구는 platform-matched 그룹이 platform-switched 그룹보다 유의하게 많은 금속이온을 유출한다는 것을 ICP-MS로 정량화해, PS의 골보존 효과에 세 번째 기전(부식 산물 감소)을 추가.

## Three-line Summary

In vitro accelerated-corrosion study: 42 commercially pure titanium (CPTi) implant cylinders connected to either titanium-alloy (Ti-6Al-4V) or cobalt-chrome (CoCr) abutments at three platform diameters (6mm matched [M], 5mm switched [SW], 4mm switched [S]), plus unconnected controls, statically immersed in 1% lactic acid (pH 2.3, ISO 10271) for 7 days at 37°C, with released Ti/V/Al/Co/Cr/Mo quantified by ICP-MS and interfacial surfaces examined by SEM/EDX before and after immersion.

Platform-matched groups released significantly more metal ions than platform-switched groups for vanadium, aluminium (titanium-abutment groups) and cobalt, chromium, molybdenum (CoCr-abutment groups) (p<0.05, roughly twofold higher), with the decrease proportional to the amount of platform mismatch for Co, Cr, and Mo; titanium was the highest-released element overall regardless of group. SEM showed active pitting corrosion on implant and abutment interfacial surfaces in all groups, most pronounced near the outer borders of the contacting platform.

Findings provide the first indirect evidence that reduced metal-ion/corrosion-product release — not only mechanical stress redistribution or microgap-distancing — may partly explain platform switching's clinically observed crestal bone preservation, since a wider mismatch moves the corroding interface further from peri-implant tissue.

## 세줄요약

In vitro 가속부식(accelerated corrosion) 연구 — 순티타늄(Commercially Pure Titanium, CPTi) 임플란트 42개를 티타늄합금(Ti-6Al-4V) 또는 코발트-크롬(CoCr) 지대주(abutment)에 3가지 플랫폼 직경(6mm 매칭[M], 5mm 스위칭[SW], 4mm 스위칭[S])으로 연결, 비연결 대조군 포함, 1% 젖산(lactic acid, pH 2.3, ISO 10271)에 37°C 7일 침지 후 유도결합플라즈마 질량분석(Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry, ICP-MS)으로 Ti/V/Al/Co/Cr/Mo 이온을 정량, 주사전자현미경(Scanning Electron Microscopy, SEM)·에너지분산형X선분광(Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy, EDX)으로 접촉면 관찰.

플랫폼 매칭(platform-matched) 그룹이 플랫폼 스위칭(platform-switched) 그룹보다 바나듐·알루미늄(티타늄 지대주군), 코발트·크롬·몰리브덴(CoCr 지대주군) 이온 유출이 유의하게(p<0.05, 약 2배) 많았고, Co·Cr·Mo는 부정합(mismatch) 정도에 비례해 감소; 티타늄은 전 그룹에서 유출량이 가장 많은 원소.

SEM상 전 그룹 접촉면(특히 외곽부)에서 활성 피팅 부식(pitting corrosion) 관찰; 이는 플랫폼 스위칭의 임상적 치조정골 보존 효과가 기계적 응력 재분배·미세간극 거리화뿐 아니라 부식 산물(금속이온) 감소로도 일부 설명될 수 있음을 시사하는 첫 간접 증거.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study (per authors) to directly compare elemental metal-ion leakage between platform-matched and platform-switched implant-abutment couples of varying mismatch amounts, using two clinically relevant abutment materials (Ti-6Al-4V and CoCr).
- Establishes a dose-response-like relationship: for Co, Cr, and Mo, ion release decreased proportionally with increasing platform mismatch (6mm→5mm→4mm), paralleling the clinical dose-response Canullo reported between mismatch amount and marginal bone loss (MBL).
- SEM/EDX evidence that corrosion is most active at the outer borders of the implant-abutment contacting interface — the region closest to peri-implant soft tissue — supporting a plausible mechanistic link between interface geometry and biological exposure to corrosion products.
- Proposes a third mechanistic pathway (reduced corrosion/metal-ion release) alongside the two dominant existing theories (biomechanical stress theory, bacterial/biologic-width theory) for why platform switching preserves crestal bone.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: In vitro accelerated-corrosion (static immersion) study, ISO 10271-compliant.
- **Specimens**: 42 CPTi (Grade II, ASTM F67-13) implant cylinders (6mm × 10mm), 36 connected to abutments + 6 unconnected. Abutments: 24 Ti-6Al-4V (Grade 5) prefabricated abutments (18 connected + 6 unconnected) and 24 CoCr abutments made via CAD/CAM laser-melting (18 connected + 6 unconnected).
- **Groups (n=6 each, 9 groups total, 54 specimens)**: TM (Ti abutment, 6mm matched), TSW (Ti, 5mm switched), TS (Ti, 4mm switched), UT (unconnected Ti abutment, 4mm); CM (CoCr, 6mm matched), CSW (CoCr, 5mm switched), CS (CoCr, 4mm switched), UC (unconnected CoCr abutment, 4mm); UI (unconnected implants).
- **Surface prep**: platform surfaces wet-polished (4000-grit SiC), ultrasonicated in 70% ethanol, rinsed in deionized water; all implant/abutment surfaces except the contacting platform coated with resin to confine corrosion to the interfacial area.
- **Immersion**: assembled with hexed titanium screws, hand-tightened; immersed in fresh 1% lactic acid (0.1 mol/L lactic acid + 0.1 mol/L NaCl, pH 2.3) at 1mL solution per cm² surface area, sealed, incubated 37°C × 7 days (static). Parallel fully-coated reference specimen used to establish background impurity levels.
- **Ion quantification**: ICP-MS (Varian/Bruker 800-MS) for Ti, V, Al, Co, Cr, Mo; results in ppb.
- **Surface analysis**: SEM (FEI) and EDX (Inca 400) on 2 representative specimens per subgroup (12 total), examined before and after immersion at 5 spot areas per surface.
- **Statistics**: Levene's test for homogeneity; one-way ANOVA + Bonferroni-corrected multiple comparisons (homogeneous data) or Kruskal-Wallis (non-homogeneous); Mann-Whitney U for connected-vs-unconnected comparisons. α=0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

*(Note: PDF/text extraction stripped scientific-notation exponents from ICP-MS values throughout the source full text — e.g. "440 × 10" and "1 250 × 10" lost their superscript power-of-ten. Values below are reported exactly as extracted; relative comparisons and significance are unaffected since all values share the same missing exponent and the paper's own group-to-group comparisons are reported at face value.)*

- **Titanium (Ti)**: highest-released element in every group (p<0.05), ranging from 440×10 ppb (UT) to 1250×10 ppb (TM, highest). No significant difference in Ti release between TM and the switched Ti-abutment groups (TSW/TS).
- **Vanadium (V)** (Ti-abutment groups): TM=60×10 ppb (highest), significantly higher than TSW=36×10 and TS=38×10 (p<0.05).
- **Aluminium (Al)** (Ti-abutment groups): TM=67×10 ppb, significantly higher than TSW=57×10 and TS=59×10 (p<0.05).
- **CoCr-abutment groups**: Ti release higher in CS (993×10) than CM, but NS difference vs CM (p>0.05); CS significantly higher than CSW (p<0.05).
- **Cobalt (Co)**: highest in CM=219×10 ppb (p<0.05); decreased significantly as CoCr abutment platform size decreased, lowest in CS=85×10 ppb.
- **Chromium (Cr) and Molybdenum (Mo)**: same pattern as Co — highest in CM, significant decrease as CoCr abutment size decreased (p<0.05).
- **Unconnected vs connected**: UI (unconnected implants) showed significantly higher Ti release (998×10 ppb) than connected CM and CSW groups (p<0.05), but no difference from CS (p>0.05). UC group Co release (103×10 ppb) significantly higher than CS (p<0.05); no significant Cr/Mo difference between UC and CS. UT group V (17×10) and Al (10×10) significantly lower than TS group (p<0.05).
- **Overall pattern (Discussion)**: platform-matched groups released approximately twofold higher V, Co, Cr, and Mo than platform-switched groups within each abutment material; differences statistically significant for V, Al, Co, Mo, and Cr. Decrease was proportional to the amount of mismatch for Co, Cr, and Mo specifically.
- **SEM**: active pitting corrosion on interfacial (contacting) surfaces of both implants and abutments in all test groups post-immersion, most pronounced near the outer borders of the platform surfaces.
- **EDX**: interfacial implant surfaces showed >99.9% Ti composition pre- and post-immersion with no cross-deposition of abutment elements (Co/Cr/Mo/Al/V) onto implants, nor Ti deposition onto CoCr abutments — indicating corrosion products are released into the surrounding environment rather than redepositing at the interface.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Static immersion test only — does not model fretting/mechanical-loading corrosion, which the authors note would likely increase corrosion product release further.
- No electrochemical testing (e.g., potentiodynamic polarisation) to assess electrical potential/galvanic current directly.
- 1% lactic acid (pH 2.3, ISO 10271) is an accelerated/aggressive test medium, not a full physiological simulation (vs. 0.9% NaCl, phosphate-buffered saline, or artificial saliva); chosen per prior literature (Yoneyama et al.) as most reliable for Ti ion release quantification.
- Machined CPTi cylinders used as implant proxies rather than commercial implant geometries/surface finishes — noted by authors as a possible limitation, though precedented in prior corrosion literature.
- Gold-alloy abutments (which would reverse titanium's anode/cathode role in galvanic corrosion) were not tested — authors suggest this as a future direction.
- High variance in ICP-MS Ti measurements attributed to method sensitivity, sample prep, and instrument detection limits.
- Biological consequence of the measured ion concentrations is inferred from prior cell-culture literature (e.g., Zijlstra et al. on Co/Cr effects on osteoblasts), not directly tested in this study — the authors explicitly frame the bone-loss link as indirect evidence requiring further mechanistic (cell pathway) research, which they state is already underway by the same group.

## 7. Glossary

- **Platform switching (PS)**: connecting a narrower-diameter abutment to a wider-diameter implant platform, repositioning the implant-abutment interface inward and away from the implant's outer edge.
- **Galvanic corrosion**: electrochemical corrosion arising from contact between dissimilar metals (here, CPTi implant vs. Ti-alloy or CoCr abutment) in an electrolyte; corrosion increases as the cathode surface area exceeds the anode surface area.
- **ICP-MS (Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry)**: highly sensitive elemental analysis technique used to quantify trace metal ion concentrations (here in ppb) in the immersion solution.
- **Pitting corrosion**: localized corrosion producing small cavities/pits on a metal surface, distinct from uniform surface corrosion; observed via SEM at the implant-abutment interface in this study.
- **EDX (Energy Dispersive X-ray spectroscopy)**: surface elemental-composition analysis technique paired with SEM imaging, used here to check for cross-deposition of abutment-derived elements onto implant surfaces and vice versa.
