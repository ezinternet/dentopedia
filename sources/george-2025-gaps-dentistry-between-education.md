## Why Ingested

This wiki holds several pages on how well dental *trainees* convert education into clinical decisions — [[behavioral-dentistry/communication-relationship/iyer-2026-shared-decision-making-knowledge-attitude-dental-trainees]] (SDM knowledge/attitude gaps in senior students/residents) and [[practice-management/cangul-2026-full-pulpotomy-adoption-determinants-trainee-decision-making]] (what predicts trainee willingness to act independently) — but nothing on the *systematic theory–practice gap* new graduates perceive at the moment of entering independent practice. This qualitative study names that gap from the graduate's own voice and frames it as a workforce-readiness problem (mentorship, supervision, curriculum integration), which anchors the education→practice transition strand inside practice-management. It also feeds the broader theme that the profession's own preparation is uneven, echoing [[overviews/evidence-appraisal-toolkit]]'s premise that clinicians must be trained to reason with uncertainty rather than memorize protocols.

## Three-line Summary

Qualitative exploratory study (one-on-one semi-structured interviews, n=10 new Bachelor of Dental Surgery graduates in a 1-year Certificate in General Dentistry programme at Christian Medical College, Vellore, India, Feb–Jul 2023) probing perceived gaps across three domains: knowledge, skills, and practice.
All 10 participants reported feeling unprepared for independent unsupervised practice immediately after graduation; manual thematic analysis (content validity index 0.90) yielded six themes — existence of the theory–practice gap, its impact on practice preparedness, curriculum integration, the preceptor/role-model role, direct supervision, and patient safety.
Graduates specifically named deficits in practice management, medical emergencies, jurisprudence, and care of medically compromised patients; closing the gap requires an integrated competency-based curriculum, structured mentorship, and supervised hands-on experience.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First explicit description of the **theory–practice gap in undergraduate dentistry** as perceived by graduates themselves — the authors note the concept has long been described in nursing (since 1943), medicine, and paramedics, but "is yet to be described clearly" in dentistry.
- Maps the gap onto **three operational domains**: knowledge (theoretical understanding), skills (procedural competence), practice (application in real-world settings).
- Identifies **six themes** via grounded/emergent thematic analysis and links them to practice preparedness and **patient safety**.
- Proposes concrete educational remedies: curriculum integration (case-based/patient-based learning), competency-based redesign, preceptor development, direct supervision, reflection-based assessment, bridging programmes.
- Tabulates education gaps vs practice gaps vs recommendations (Table 2) usable as a checklist for curriculum committees.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: qualitative exploratory study, 5 months (Feb–Jul 2023), Dental Unit 1, Department of Dental and Oral Surgery, CMC Vellore.
- **Participants (n=10)**: new BDS graduates from the 2022–23 batch of the Certificate in General Dentistry (CGD) programme — a 1-year clinical service training programme in basic general/hospital dentistry. Purposive + convenience sampling. Demographics: ages 23–30; 6 female / 4 male; 8 with nil prior clinical experience, 2 with 1 year.
- **Instrument**: validated semi-structured interview guide covering knowledge/cognitive, psychomotor/skills, patient-care, and practice-readiness domains (Appendix 1); developed from literature review and expert panel review; scale- and item-level content validity averaged **0.90**; revised per biostatistician feedback.
- **Data collection**: one-on-one interviews by a supervisor/clinical guide (positioned as facilitator, not expert); audio recorded on password-protected machine; field notes after each session; manual transcription.
- **Analysis**: manual thematic analysis by the principal researcher using grounded and emergent theory — preliminary codes → formal codes → themes/subthemes (refs 26, 27).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **100% (10/10) of participants** acknowledged the theory–practice gap and agreed they felt unprepared for unsupervised independent practice straight after graduation; all wanted additional training before independent practice.
- **Six themes** (Table 2 / Figure 2):
  1. *Existence of gap* — unpreparedness due to weak applicatory/analytical skills, mentorship, critical thinking.
  2. *Education gaps* — written-exam-oriented preparation overwhelming theory content with minimal hands-on/patient work; low emphasis on clinical reasoning, decision making, dental management of medically compromised patients; medico-legal knowledge deficit; named curriculum holes: jurisprudence, medical emergencies, practice management.
  3. *Practice gaps* — insufficient skill exposure, communication/soft skills, consultation, treatment planning, procedural competence; quota-based admission system; absent clinical mentorship.
  4. *Practice preparedness* — integrate mentorship into clinical system, bridging courses, one-to-one direct supervision, formative/summative evaluation beyond theory, feedback-driven learning with self-assessment checklists.
  5. *Recommendations for gap closure* — theory-practice integration in planned curriculum, faculty development, competence-based curriculum, customized feedback.
  6. *Patient safety linkage* — inability to apply theory in real situations endangers patients (e.g., participant unable to handle a medical emergency in a medically compromised patient once out of the teaching-hospital umbrella).
- **Contextual benchmarks cited** (from other studies, not this cohort): India — 65% of graduates lacked confidence to start practice immediately, 86% wanted post-graduation; Australia — 85% wanted to work in an established practice, 93% wanted a mentor before opening their own; Saudi Arabia — 40% felt unprepared (traditional vs integrated curriculum comparison).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Small sample (n=10)**, purposive/convenience sampling at a single tertiary centre — explicitly not generalizable; intent was hypothesis/theme generation, not statistical generalizability.
- **Self-report bias** inherent to qualitative interviews; participants were interviewed by their own supervisor/clinical guide (mitigated by framing the researcher as facilitator and neutral reporting).
- Interviewer dual relationship and principal-researcher-only coding (no second coder / inter-rater reliability reported).
- Future work called for: larger, in-depth multi-centre studies; local-context solutions; implementation research on mentorship/preceptor structures.

## 7. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Theory–practice gap | Inability to relate and apply academic/research knowledge to actual clinical practice |
| Practice preparedness | Readiness for the complexities of practice — multidimensional: competence, confidence, capability, adaptability (Aggarwal et al.) |
| CGD programme | Certificate in General Dentistry — 1-year clinical service training in basic routine and hospital-based dentistry at CMC Vellore |
| Preceptor | A teacher standing alongside the student, linking workplace reality with academic idealism without compromising professional ideals |
| Reality/transition shock | Distress when graduates realize the difference between ideal classroom learning and real clinical practice |
| Content validity index (CVI) | Averaged expert-rating measure of instrument relevance; 0.90 reported here |
| Grounded and emergent theory | Qualitative analysis approach where codes/themes are derived from data while allowing emergent themes beyond predefined ones |
