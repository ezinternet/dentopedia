## Why Ingested

This is a 10-year (2012-2021) literature overview of *systemic* xerostomia management — cytoprotective/antioxidant drugs (amifostine, CoQ10, vitamin E/C, lycopene), para-sympathomimetics (pilocarpine, cevimeline, bethanechol), Sjögren's-targeted biologics (rituximab, epratuzumab, belimumab, interferon alpha), and traditional/complementary medicine (East Asian herbal decoctions, Western herb mixtures). It complements [[wiki/oral-medicine/kim-2023-xerostomia-cellular-targets]] — where Kim 2023 explains *why* current sialagogues have off-target effects at the cellular/receptor level (GPCR/IP3R/SOCE/AQP5 pathway), this overview catalogues the *clinical-trial-level* evidence (or lack thereof) for every systemic drug class tried against xerostomia, plus the TM/CM side that Kim does not cover. Found while surveying hyposalivation/xerostomia dental-management papers in the wiki.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative overview (Current Oncology 2023, literature search Jan 2012–Jan 2021 across PubMed/EMBASE/Web of Science/ScienceDirect/Google Scholar) summarizing 10 years of systemic pharmacological and traditional-medicine (TM) approaches to xerostomia prevention/treatment, mainly in head-and-neck cancer (HNC) and Sjögren's syndrome (SS) populations.

Amifostine (cytoprotective, free-radical scavenger) is the most-discussed HNC-radiotherapy preventive agent but has unclear net benefit and frequent side effects (nausea, hypotension, vomiting); pilocarpine/cevimeline/bethanechol (para-sympathomimetics) and Sjögren's biologics (rituximab, epratuzumab, belimumab, interferon alpha) show low-to-inconsistent efficacy with substantial side-effect burden; TM (East Asian herbal decoctions, Western herb/mouthwash mixtures) has too few and too low-quality clinical trials to validate efficacy or safety.

No systemic chemical treatment or TM approach currently has robust evidence for preventing or resolving xerostomia — clinical guidelines remain absent, and management of xerostomia and its complications is described by the authors as "a very significant void in daily clinical practice."

## 세줄요약

내러티브 개관 (Current Oncology 2023, 2012–2021년 10년간 문헌 검토: PubMed/EMBASE/Web of Science/ScienceDirect/Google Scholar) — 두경부암(Head and Neck Cancer, HNC) 및 쇼그렌증후군(Sjögren's Syndrome, SS) 환자 중심의 구강건조증(Xerostomia) 전신적 약물치료·전통의학(Traditional Medicine, TM) 접근법 요약.

아미포스틴(Amifostine, 세포보호제·활성산소 소거제)이 HNC 방사선치료 예방제로 가장 많이 논의되었으나 순이익 불명확·부작용(오심·저혈압·구토) 흔함; 필로카르핀(Pilocarpine)·세비멜린(Cevimeline)·베타네콜(Bethanechol, 부교감신경흥분제)과 쇼그렌 생물학적제제(리툭시맙(Rituximab)·에프라투주맙(Epratuzumab)·벨리무맙(Belimumab)·인터페론알파)는 효과 낮거나 일관성 없고 부작용 부담 큼; TM(동아시아 한약 탕제, 서양 허브·구강세정제 혼합물)은 임상시험 수·질이 너무 부족해 효능·안전성 검증 불가.

현재 어떤 전신 약물치료나 TM도 구강건조증 예방·해결에 확고한 근거 없음 — 임상 가이드라인 부재 지속; 저자들은 구강건조증과 그 합병증 관리를 "일상 임상 실무의 매우 중대한 공백"으로 규정.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Summarizes 10 years (Jan 2012–Jan 2021) of clinical experience with systemic pharmacological agents (cytoprotective, antioxidant, para-sympathomimetic, para-sympatholytic, biological) for xerostomia prevention/treatment, mainly in HNC radiotherapy and SS populations.
- Identifies amifostine and its antioxidant analogues as the most-studied *preventive* agents against radiotherapy-induced xerostomia, while flagging unresolved controversy over whether amifostine compromises cancer-treatment survival outcomes.
- Consolidates the mAChR-subtype mechanism (M1–M5, M3 dominant) explaining why para-sympathomimetics (pilocarpine, cevimeline, bethanechol) cause broad systemic side effects (indiscriminate stimulation of all 5 subtypes) rather than acinar-selective secretion.
- Catalogues the full roster of Sjögren's-syndrome biologics tested against xerostomia/lacrimal dysfunction (rituximab, epratuzumab, belimumab, ianalumab, baminercept, abatacept, efalizumab, TNF/IL-6/IL-1 inhibitors, interferon alpha) with efficacy/toxicity outcomes for each.
- First half of the review to separately treat East Asian TM (herbal decoctions/formulas) and Western TM (herb/mouthwash mixtures) for xerostomia/oral-mucositis, highlighting that Western TM evidence is almost entirely in vitro/cell-line rather than clinical.
- Bottom-line conclusion: no systemic chemical drug class and no TM approach currently has robust evidence for preventing or resolving xerostomia — a clinical-guideline void persists.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Narrative literature overview (not a registered systematic review; no PRISMA flow diagram, no formal eligibility criteria beyond date range, no risk-of-bias/GRADE assessment reported in the retrieved text).
- **Search period**: January 1, 2012 – January 30, 2021.
- **Databases**: PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar.
- **Scope**: systemic (oral/IV) pharmacological compounds and traditional/complementary medicine approaches for xerostomia prevention and treatment; excludes local/topical salivary substitutes and mechanical/device-based sialogogue methods (covered by other papers, e.g., artificial saliva reviews).
- **Populations covered**: predominantly HNC radiotherapy patients and primary/secondary Sjögren's syndrome patients; general drug-induced xerostomia (>400 xerogenic medications) discussed as background etiology.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Xerostomia epidemiology**: ~30% incidence in population aged ≥65, rising to 61% with comorbidity/polypharmacy; up to 40% of HNC radiotherapy patients develop temporary/permanent salivary impairment; 45.9% of SARS-CoV-2-infected subjects reported xerostomia.
- **Pilocarpine**: Cochrane review (Davis et al.) — efficacy vs placebo statistically unclear; side effects at 2.5–10 mg up to 3×/day: headache 15%, urinary frequency 14%, vasodilatation 12%, dizziness 10%, dyspepsia 10%, nausea 8%, asthenia 8%, diarrhea 5% (dominant cause of study withdrawal).
- **Cevimeline**: 2 RCTs found "quite effective" (less side-effect detail reported than pilocarpine).
- **Bethanechol**: increased basal salivary flow vs artificial saliva in irradiated HNC patients, but limited by hyperthyroidism/peptic ulcer/asthma/bradycardia/hypotension/coronary disease/epilepsy/Parkinsonism contraindications.
- **Amifostine**: mixed results — some trials show reduced post-radiotherapy xerostomia; others show no clear benefit vs IV saline placebo; adverse effects include vomiting, hypotension, nausea, allergic reactions; debate persists over impact on overall/progression-free/disease-free survival and QoL.
- **Antioxidants**: CoQ10 (ubiquinone/ubiquinol) increased salivary production in SS patients, ubiquinol more effective; Vitamin E (± Vitamin C 500 mg) improved salivary pH/potassium/amylase/flow rate and VAS scores during radiotherapy; lycopene-enriched virgin olive oil outperformed isolated lycopene for dry-mouth relief.
- **Sjögren's biologics**: rituximab — improved saliva/lacrimal flow and reduced extra-glandular manifestations early, but benefit lost by 48 weeks (only ocular-dryness VAS remained significant); epratuzumab — improved lacrimal/unstimulated saliva flow but infusion reactions/infections; belimumab — no change in unstimulated saliva flow, caused infections incl. pneumococcal meningitis; ianalumab/baminercept — low efficacy, associated toxicity; abatacept — controversial (mixed inflammatory-marker results, lupus-like cutaneous lesions); efalizumab — withdrawn (multifocal leukoencephalopathy risk); infliximab/etanercept/tocilizumab/anakinra — no significant benefit vs placebo; interferon alpha — improved oral/ocular dryness and unstimulated flow in some trials (possibly via AQP5 upregulation) but limited by high GI adverse-effect rate.
- **East Asian TM**: Plantago major, Kampo mixtures, Zingiber officinale (ginger), quercetin, Malva sylvestris + Althaea officinalis (4-week trial, significant VAS efficacy vs artificial saliva), and multi-herb decoctions (Shennongbaijie, Xuanmaizengyehuadu, Yunnanbaiyao, Jiaweizengye, Sanganhuayin) show subjective dry-mouth improvement during HNC radiotherapy in some trials (not always statistically significant), but overall very-low-to-moderate quality evidence with high risk of bias.
- **Western TM**: 15–73% of cancer patients (European data) self-medicate with TM against radio/chemotherapy side effects; EMA/HMPC herbal-recognition thresholds require >30 years established use + >15 years documented safety; most European studies are in vitro/cell-line (antimicrobial/anti-inflammatory/antioxidant assays against oral pathogens incl. Candida spp.), with very few clinical trials (e.g., Aloe vera + honey; propolis + Ficus carica + Malva sylvestris + Althaea officinalis mouthwash; sage/Echinacea sprays for pharyngitis/mucositis/sore throat).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **This is a narrative overview, not a systematic review** — search strategy (databases, date range) is stated, but no PRISMA flow diagram, no explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria beyond date, and no formal risk-of-bias or GRADE certainty assessment is reported. Its conclusions should be read as a clinical-experience synthesis, not graded systematic evidence — treat individual drug/herb efficacy claims as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.
- Core finding across every systemic drug class reviewed (amifostine, antioxidants, pilocarpine/cevimeline/bethanechol, Sjögren's biologics): none achieves robust, guideline-worthy prevention or resolution of xerostomia; efficacy signals are inconsistent across trials and consistently offset by side-effect burden.
- TM evidence (both East Asian and Western) is too sparse and methodologically weak (small samples, blinding/allocation-concealment/dropout reporting deficiencies; Western TM mostly in vitro rather than clinical) to validate efficacy or rule out interference with concomitant chemotherapy/radiotherapy.
- Authors explicitly state that no clinical guideline for xerostomia management currently exists and call this "a very significant void in daily clinical practice."
- Future work implied: rigorous RCTs (particularly for TM, which currently lacks them almost entirely), formal risk-of-bias appraisal of the existing pharmacological literature, and translational studies bridging the in vitro Western-TM evidence to clinical trials.

## 7. Glossary

- **Xerostomia**: subjective sensation of dry mouth, from reduced saliva quantity and/or altered composition; clinically defined at resting flow ≤0.1 mL/min or stimulated flow ≤0.5 mL/min.
- **HNC (Head and Neck Cancer)**: cancer population in which radiotherapy-induced salivary gland damage is the most prevalent cause of xerostomia.
- **Amifostine**: organic thiophosphate cytoprotective prodrug; active metabolite WR-1065 scavenges free radicals and protects membranes/DNA in normal tissue during radio/chemotherapy.
- **ROS (Reactive Oxygen Species)**: oxidative-stress mediators implicated in salivary gland damage in Sjögren's syndrome, systemic sclerosis, and radiotherapy.
- **mAChR (Muscarinic Acetylcholine Receptor)**: 5 subtypes (M1–M5) on acinar cells; M3 is the principal driver of secretion via the Gq–PLC–IP3–Ca²⁺ pathway; non-selective agonism by pilocarpine/cevimeline/bethanechol across subtypes causes systemic side effects.
- **Pilocarpine, Cevimeline, Bethanechol**: para-sympathomimetic sialagogue drugs.
- **SS (Sjögren's syndrome)**: chronic autoimmune exocrinopathy with lymphocytic infiltration of salivary/lacrimal glands, the target population for the biologics reviewed (rituximab, epratuzumab, belimumab, etc.).
- **TM (Traditional Medicine) / CM (Complementary Medicine)**: WHO-defined health practices based on cultural theories/experience predating modern pharmacology; reviewed here in East Asian (herbal decoctions/Kampo) and Western (herb infusions/mouthwashes) forms.
- **VAS (Visual Analogue Scale)**: patient-reported symptom-severity measure used across most of the trials cited.
