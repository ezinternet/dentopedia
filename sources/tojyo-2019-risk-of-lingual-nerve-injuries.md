## Why Ingested

We already hold a broad scoping review of IAN/lingual nerve injury ([[wiki/oral-surgery/kammerer-2024-ian-lingual-nerve-diagnosis-treatment-review]]) but no paper isolating **which mandibular third molar impaction pattern** carries the highest lingual nerve injury risk with matched-literature control-group comparisons. Tojyo et al. 2019 fills that gap with a 79-patient microsurgical-repair case series compared against six published control cohorts, identifying distoangular impaction as the strongest risk factor — a concrete refinement of the general risk framework in the Kämmerer review.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective case-control study of 79 patients (24 males, 58 females; mean age 36.5y) who underwent microsurgical repair of severe iatrogenic lingual nerve injury after mandibular third molar removal, with impaction type (Winter's / Pell and Gregory classification) compared against six literature control cohorts.

Distoangular impaction was significantly overrepresented in the lingual nerve injury group (30.8% vs 0.2–15.8% in most control groups, P as low as <0.0001 in one comparison), and female sex (72.1% vs 47–59% in controls, P=0.001) and older age (mean 36.5y vs 27–34y in controls, P<0.0001 in three of four comparisons) were also significantly associated with injury; injury side (right vs left) showed no significant association.

Distoangular impaction of the mandibular third molar in women in their 30s–50s may be a higher-risk configuration for severe lingual nerve injury during extraction, but the control groups were literature-derived and heterogeneous (not a matched contemporaneous cohort), and only 26/79 cases had orthopantomographs available for classification.

## 세줄요약

하악 제3대구치 발치 후 심한 의인성 설신경 손상으로 미세수술적 봉합술을 받은 79명(남 24, 여 58; 평균연령 36.5세)을 대상으로 매복 양상(Winter 분류·Pell and Gregory 분류)을 6개 문헌 대조군과 비교한 후향적 환자-대조군 연구.

원심경사(Distoangular) 매복이 설신경 손상군에서 유의하게 높았고(30.8% vs 대조군 대부분 0.2–15.8%, 일부 비교에서 P<0.0001), 여성(72.1% vs 대조군 47–59%, P=0.001)과 고령(평균 36.5세 vs 대조군 27–34세, 4개 비교 중 3개에서 P<0.0001)도 유의한 연관성을 보였으며, 손상측(좌/우)은 유의한 연관이 없었다.

30–50대 여성에서 원심경사 매복 하악 제3대구치는 발치 시 중증 설신경 손상의 고위험 조합일 수 있으나, 대조군이 동시대 매칭 코호트가 아닌 이질적 문헌 자료이고 26/79례만 파노라마 방사선으로 분류 가능했다는 한계가 있다.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Risk of lingual nerve injuries in removal of mandibular third molars: a retrospective case-control study
- **Journal**: Maxillofacial Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery, 2019, 41:40
- **Authors**: Itaru Tojyo, Takashi Nakanishi, Yukari Shintani, Kenjiro Okamoto, Yukihiro Hiraishi, Shigeyuki Fujita — Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Wakayama Medical University, Japan
- **DOI**: 10.1186/s40902-019-0222-4
- **Study type**: Retrospective case-control study (single-center case series vs. literature-derived control groups)
- **Ethics**: Approved by regional ethical review board of Wakayama Medical University (Protocol ID 1698); general patient consent obtained

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantifies, in a dedicated microsurgical-repair case series (n=79, all severe/permanent lingual nerve injuries requiring surgical nerve restoration — not the full spectrum of transient injuries), the association between third molar impaction type and severe lingual nerve injury risk.
- Directly compares this severe-injury cohort against six independently published control-group datasets on sex, age, injury side, and Winter's classification distribution, rather than relying on a single reference population.
- Proposes an anatomical mechanism for why distoangular impaction elevates lingual nerve risk: the distal/lingual crown position increases exposure of the lingual plate and nerve during bone removal and tooth dislocation (illustrated in the paper's Fig. 3).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective chart review, March 2003–November 2016, of patients who underwent microsurgical lingual nerve repair at Wakayama Medical University after lingual nerve injury sustained during mandibular third molar removal at *other* facilities.
- **Indication for microsurgery**: (1) witnessed nerve transection, OR (2) all of: two-point discrimination >20mm, absent temperature/taste/pin-prick sensation, and Semmes-Weinstein monofilament asymmetry, each persisting >3 months post-injury.
- **Surgical technique**: Intraoral mucosal incision + lingual flap reflection; optical magnifying glasses/operating microscope; neuroma and scar excision; direct end-to-end epineural suture (8-0/9-0 nylon, ≥8 sites); no nerve grafts required in any case.
- **Variables collected**: sex, age, injury side; impaction type by Winter's classification and depth/space by Pell and Gregory classification (available for only 26/79 cases with orthopantomographs).
- **Statistics**: Chi-squared test (categorical) and t-test (continuous), P<0.05 significance, versus six literature control-group datasets (Almendros-Marqués 2006, Uematsu 2015, Cheung/Cheng 2010, Blondeau 2007, Smith 2013, Oguma 2013).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Cohort**: 79 patients total; sex-comparison subset 23 males/56 females (some tables report 24/58); mean age 36.5y (range 15–67).
- **Sex**: Female 72.1% (LNIG) vs. 52.7–61% across 5 control groups; P=0.001 to 0.07 depending on comparator (significant in 4/5).
- **Age**: Mean 36.5y vs. 27.2–33.9y across 4 control groups; P<0.0001 in 3/4 comparisons, P=0.0619 in 1/4.
- **Injury side**: Right 55.7% / Left 44.3% vs. control right 49.7% / left 50.3%; P=0.28 (not significant).
- **Winter's classification** (n=26 with imaging): Distoangular 30.8%, Horizontal 30.8%, Mesioangular 19.2%, Vertical 11.5%, Inverted 3.8%, Linguoangular 3.8%, Buccoangular 0%.
- **Distoangular comparison vs. controls**: LNIG 30.8% vs. control range 0.2–31.0%; significant in most comparisons (P=0.009, 0.036, <0.0001, <0.0001), not significant vs. Smith 2013 (P=0.979, whose control also had high distoangular rate of 31.0%).
- **Pell and Gregory**: Position B (69.2%) and Class II (61.5%) most common depth/space categories.
- Distolingual bone was shaved in 5 of 8 distoangular cases in this series, supporting the proposed mechanism.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Control groups are heterogeneous literature cohorts (different populations, eras, surgeons, and outcome definitions), not a matched contemporaneous control — the authors explicitly flag this as a limitation.
- Only 26/79 cases (33%) had orthopantomographs available for Winter's/Pell and Gregory classification, limiting the imaging-based analysis to a small subset.
- Whether a lingual flap was raised or a lingual retractor used during the index extraction was unknown for this cohort (unlike Cheung et al., which found flap-raising was not significantly associated with deficit).
- Single-center, single-country (Japan) cohort of only severe/surgically-repaired injuries — cannot estimate absolute incidence or generalize to milder/transient lingual nerve injuries.
- Authors call for long-term multi-institutional prospective studies given the rarity of severe lingual nerve injury.

## 6. Related Work

- Cheung et al. 2010 (4338 cases, prospective) — largest control cohort used; found distoangular impaction significantly increased LN deficit risk (P<0.001) but no sex association.
- Valmaseda-Castellón et al. 2000 — lingual angulation associated with LN injury (OR=4.39), consistent with this paper's distoangular mechanism (distoangular position anatomically implies lingual angulation).
- Jerjes et al. 2010 — contrasting finding of horizontal impaction as highest-risk and male sex as a risk factor, illustrating population/technique heterogeneity across studies.
- See also [[wiki/oral-surgery/kammerer-2024-ian-lingual-nerve-diagnosis-treatment-review]] for a broader diagnosis-to-treatment framework covering both IAN and lingual nerve injury.

## 7. Glossary

- **LNIG**: Lingual nerve injury group (the study cohort)
- **CG**: Control group (literature-derived comparison cohorts)
- **Winter's classification**: Angulation-based classification of impacted third molars (distoangular, horizontal, mesioangular, vertical, inverted, buccoangular, linguoangular) based on angle between occlusal plane and tooth long axis
- **Pell and Gregory classification**: Depth (Position A/B/C, relative to occlusal plane) and available space (Class I/II/III, relative to ascending ramus) classification of impacted mandibular third molars
- **Two-point discrimination (2-PD)**: Neurosensory test measuring the minimum distance at which two simultaneous touch points are perceived as distinct
- **SWM test**: Semmes-Weinstein monofilament test, a graded tactile threshold neurosensory assessment
