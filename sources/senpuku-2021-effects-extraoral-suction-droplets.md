## Why Ingested

치위생사 스케일링 술기 감염관리 축(사용자 요청 2026-07-20 배치 인제스트)의 일부로, 구강외 흡인(extraoral suction)이 실제 치과 진료실 환경에서 생물학적 지표(ATP 발광, 세균 CFU)로 측정한 비말·에어로졸 오염 저감 효과를 정량화한다. [[dental-handpiece/matys-2020-dental-aerosol-hazard-risk-dental]]가 3D 마네킹 인비트로 실험에서 광학입자계수기로 흡인 장치(HVE·구강외진공 포함)의 물리적 에어로졸 저감을 측정한 것을 본 논문은 저자 스스로 밝히듯("the effects by suctions were physically assessed and biological significance could not be evaluated") 실제 치과 진료실·생체 오염 지표로 extends한다 — 동일하게 스케일링을 에어로졸 발생원으로 삼되 마네킹이 아닌 실제 치과의사·치위생사가 건강한 자원자에게 시행한 모의 스케일링을 사용했다.

## Three-line Summary

Field study (3 healthy volunteers, 2 private-clinic + 1 university-hospital sessions, July–October 2020) comparing bacterial ATP (RLU) and colony-forming-unit (CFU) contamination around the dental chair across three suction conditions during 10-min simulated ultrasonic scaling: A = no suction, B = oral suction only, C = oral + extraoral suction.

Oral suction (B) and oral+extraoral suction (C) significantly reduced RLU at the left-desk position and reduced colony counts behind the chair head vs. no suction (A); extraoral suction (C) uniquely and significantly outperformed oral suction alone (B) at the left-behind-chair-head position, and no significant colonies appeared on BHI (oral-bacteria) plates, implying most droplets/aerosols originated from the ultrasonic scaler waterline rather than saliva.

Protection from combined oral+extraoral suction was spatially limited to the left and posterior sides of the dental chair head for a right-handed operator — the position where a chairside dental assistant typically stands — suggesting extraoral suction's main added value is protecting the assistant, with only 3 volunteers/sessions and no ethics-committee submission limiting generalizability.

## 세줄요약

건강한 자원자 3명 대상 실제 진료실 현장 연구(2020년 7~10월, 개인 치과의원 2회+대학병원 1회) — 10분 모의 초음파 스케일링(Ultrasonic Scaling) 중 흡인 조건 3군(A=무흡인, B=구강내흡인만, C=구강내+구강외흡인)별로 치과 유니트 주변 세균 ATP(RLU)·집락형성단위(Colony-Forming Unit, CFU) 오염도를 비교했다.

구강내흡인(B)·구강내+구강외흡인(C) 모두 좌측 데스크 위치 RLU와 체어 뒤쪽 집락수를 무흡인(A) 대비 유의하게 감소시켰고, 구강외흡인(C)은 체어 좌후방 위치에서 구강내흡인 단독(B)보다도 유의하게 우수했다; BHI(구강세균용) 배지에서는 유의한 집락이 없어, 비말·에어로졸이 타액보다 초음파 스케일러 급수관 물에서 주로 기원했음을 시사한다.

구강내+구강외흡인의 방어 효과는 우측잡이 술자 기준 체어 좌측·후방으로 공간적으로 제한되었으며 이는 체어사이드 보조인력이 주로 서는 위치와 일치해 구강외흡인의 핵심 이득이 보조인력 보호에 있음을 시사하나, 자원자 3명·세션 3회에 그치고 윤리위원회 심의 절차를 별도로 거치지 않아 일반화에 한계가 있다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First (within this wiki) biological-endpoint (ATP/RLU + bacterial CFU) field assessment of extraoral suction efficacy, complementing prior physical/particle-count bench studies.
- Demonstrates a graded suction hierarchy: no suction (A) > oral suction alone (B) > oral + extraoral suction (C) for surface contamination around the dental chair.
- Localizes protective effect of oral+extraoral suction to specific chair-side positions (left desk, behind chair head, left-behind chair head) rather than uniform reduction everywhere.
- Identifies that extraoral suction's statistically distinct benefit over oral suction alone appears specifically at the left-behind-chair-head position — plausibly protecting the chairside assistant.
- Suggests (via BHI vs R2A plate contrast) that simulated-scaling droplets/aerosols are dominated by ultrasonic scaler waterline water rather than patient saliva.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: field/simulated-scaling study, not a bench (manikin) or randomized clinical trial. Real dental offices/hospital rooms; real dentists and dental hygienists; 3 healthy volunteers (31–52 years old).
- Sites/sessions: AO1 Dental Clinic (Tokyo) ×2 sessions + Nihon University Graduate School of Dentistry at Matsudo ×1 session; conducted 30 July–8 October 2020.
- Intervention: simulated scaling (scaler did not contact teeth/calculus) for 10 min per session, using an ultrasonic scaler with dental-chair oral suction and an extraoral suction unit (ARTEO, Tokyo Giken Inc.).
- Groups: A = no oral suction, no extraoral suction; B = oral suction, no extraoral suction; C = oral suction + extraoral suction.
- CFU sampling: open BHI agar (1 plate) and R2A agar (2 plates) placed radially around the chair at 0.5–1 m intervals; opened before scaling, closed 10 min after scaling ended; BHI incubated 48 h/37 °C, R2A incubated 96–120 h at 25 °C and 35 °C.
- ATP sampling: LuciPac pen swabs at 14 named positions around the dental chair (e.g., behind left desk, bracket-table bottom/top button, scaler tube surface, extraoral suction arm(s), chair shoulder/holding side, monitor, spittoon side, sidewall), reacted with luciferin/luciferase in a Lumitester luminometer, reported as relative light units (RLU); initial cleanliness threshold set at <500 RLU.
- Statistics: one-way ANOVA and Kruskal–Wallis across the 3 groups (SPSS 24), Bonferroni post hoc for pairwise comparisons; all experiments repeated independently 3 times; significance at p<0.05.
- No formal ethics-committee submission — justified by reference to a similar prior study previously reviewed under National Institute of Infectious Diseases project code 424.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- RLU: only the No. 5 position (left desk to the dental chair head) showed a significant excess over the 500-RLU cleanliness threshold in group A; RLU there was significantly lower in B and C than A (ANOVA); C trended lower than B but the B–C difference was not significant. Several low-signal positions (No. 1, 2, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11 in the RLU dataset) were excluded as too low to analyze.
- CFU (R2A, heterotrophic/waterline bacteria): group A's highest colony counts (~10 colonies) occurred at positions No. 1, No. 4, and No. 7; position No. 5 was excluded (no significant increase). Group comparison: R2A colony counts significantly lower in B and C vs. A at position No. 7 (ANOVA).
- Post hoc pairwise: at the position behind the dental chair head (No. 1), colony counts were significantly lower in B and C than A. At the position left-behind the dental chair head (No. 7), colony counts were significantly lower in C than in either A or B — i.e., extraoral suction added a measurable benefit beyond oral suction alone specifically at this position.
- BHI (oral-bacteria-selective) plates showed no significant colony differences among groups (data not shown by the authors); water from all three test sites contained <500 CFU/mL heterotrophic bacteria on R2A, supporting the authors' inference that ultrasonic-scaler waterline water, not saliva, was the dominant droplet/aerosol source in this simulated-scaling setup.
- Overall spatial pattern: protection from combined oral+extraoral suction was confined to the left and posterior sides of the dental chair head for a right-handed dentist/hygienist — the position typically occupied by a chairside dental assistant.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 3 healthy volunteers across 3 sessions (2 clinic + 1 university hospital) — small sample, likely underpowered for many of the 14 ATP positions (7 were excluded as too low-signal to analyze).
- Simulated scaling only (no actual calculus/tooth contact), so droplet/aerosol composition may not fully replicate real scaling with genuine subgingival/supragingival calculus and blood.
- No formal ethics-committee review was obtained for this specific study (justified by a prior related approval, project code 424) — a methodological caveat for reproducibility/oversight.
- CFU and RLU position numbering across the two assays (open-plate radial layout vs. the 14-point ATP swab grid) is not fully harmonized in the text, complicating exact spatial cross-referencing between the two endpoints.
- Only bacterial/ATP surrogates were measured — no direct viral (e.g., SARS-CoV-2) assay, consistent with the general physical/biological assessment gap the authors themselves note in the literature.
- Right-handed operators only; left-handed operator ergonomics (and thus spatial protection pattern) were not assessed.

## 6. Related Work

- Matys & Grzech-Leśniak 2020 (in-vitro manikin, wiki: [[dental-handpiece/matys-2020-dental-aerosol-hazard-risk-dental]]) — physical particle-count assessment of suction modalities (including extraoral vacuum) during ultrasonic scaling and other procedures; Senpuku 2021 explicitly frames itself as adding biological verification where such physical studies could not.
- Allison et al. 2024 (narrative review, wiki: [[dental-handpiece/allison-2024-bioaerosols-airborne-transmission-dental-clinic]]) — quantifies local exhaust/extraoral suction efficacy at ~89–93% aerosol reduction at 0.1 m as part of a broader mitigation hierarchy; consistent in direction with Senpuku 2021's finding that extraoral suction adds benefit beyond oral suction alone.
- Ghoneim et al. 2024 (CDHA/ADHA consensus, wiki: [[dental-handpiece/ghoneim-2024-aerosol-generating-procedures-control-mitigation]]) — recommends HVE/suction combined with PPE and ventilation for AGP infection control; Senpuku 2021 provides field-level biological evidence for the suction component of that consensus.

## 7. Glossary

- Extraoral suction: an external vacuum unit (e.g., ARTEO) positioned outside the patient's mouth near the dental chair to capture escaping droplets/aerosols, distinct from intraoral (oral) high-volume/saliva-ejector suction.
- Oral suction: standard dental-chair intraoral suction (high-volume evacuator or saliva ejector) used during treatment.
- ATP (adenosine triphosphate): energy-carrying molecule present in all living cells; rapidly degrades after cell death, so its presence indicates recent/active biological material on a surface.
- RLU (relative light unit): the bioluminescence measurement output of an ATP luminometer (via the luciferin/luciferase reaction), proportional to ATP/microbial biomass on a sampled surface.
- CFU (colony-forming unit): a measure of viable bacterial/fungal cells, counted as colonies grown on culture media.
- BHI agar: brain heart infusion agar, a nutrient-rich medium favoring growth of oral/host-associated bacteria.
- R2A agar: a low-nutrient medium favoring growth of heterotrophic (often waterline-associated) bacteria.
- Simulated scaling: scaler instrument activated and moved as in real periodontal scaling but without contacting teeth or calculus, used here purely to generate a standardized aerosol/droplet source.
