## Three-line Summary

Multicenter, double-blind, parallel-group RCT (RETHINK, Japan) in 400 adults aged 65 or older with hip/knee osteoarthritis pain, comparing acetaminophen 1800 mg/day (n=191) with NSAIDs (loxoprofen 180 mg/day, n=98, or celecoxib 200 mg/day, n=99) for 8 weeks.

Worst-pain (BPI item 3) change was -1.79 with acetaminophen versus -1.94 with NSAIDs (difference 0.14, 95% CI -0.33 to 0.61), so non-inferiority (margin 0.6) was not demonstrated by a hair; gastrointestinal disorders occurred in 17.1% vs 25.9%, and GI disorders led to discontinuation in 0.5% (acetaminophen) vs 8.2% (loxoprofen) and 6.1% (celecoxib).

Non-dental, older-adult, 8-week chronic-use population: the GI-tolerability signal (and the early GSRS rise with loxoprofen at week 2) is informative for NSAID-intolerant patients, but extrapolation to short-course dental analgesia is limited.

## 세줄요약

일본 다기관 이중맹검 평행군 무작위대조시험 (RETHINK): 65세 이상 고관절·슬관절 골관절염 환자 400명에서 아세트아미노펜 (acetaminophen) 1800 mg/일 (n=191)과 NSAID (록소프로펜 (loxoprofen) 180 mg/일 n=98, 셀레콕시브 (celecoxib) 200 mg/일 n=99)를 8주간 비교.

최악 통증 (BPI 3번 문항) 변화는 아세트아미노펜 -1.79 대 NSAID -1.94 (차이 0.14, 95% CI -0.33~0.61)로 비열등성 (margin 0.6)은 근소하게 입증되지 못함; 위장관 장애는 17.1% 대 25.9%, 위장관 장애로 인한 중단은 아세트아미노펜 0.5% 대 록소프로펜 8.2%, 셀레콕시브 6.1%.

비치과 고령자 8주 만성 투여 연구이므로 단기 치과 진통 처방으로의 외삽은 제한적이나, NSAID 위장관 불내성 환자에서 아세트아미노펜 전환의 근거 신호로 참고할 수 있음.

- **Journal**: Osteoarthritis and Cartilage Open 2026;8(3):100850
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ocarto.2026.100850
- **Institution**: Kyushu University Hospital and four other Japanese institutions (Fukuoka)
- **Registration**: jRCTs071200112; funded by Ayumi Pharmaceutical Corporation (sponsor had no control over data analysis or interpretation per authors; several authors are sponsor employees/consultants)

## 2. Key Contributions
- First double-blind RCT comparing acetaminophen with NSAIDs specifically in adults aged 65 or older with hip/knee osteoarthritis (authors' claim).
- Reports per-NSAID (loxoprofen vs celecoxib) GI discontinuation, GSRS trajectory, eGFR and blood pressure changes alongside the pooled comparison.
- Primary non-inferiority endpoint narrowly missed (upper CI 0.61 vs margin 0.6), with pain reduction exceeding the MCID (0.9) in all arms.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group non-inferiority RCT; 5 Japanese sites; enrolled May 2021 to June 2023.
- **n**: 400 randomized and analyzed as FAS (acetaminophen 191; NSAIDs 197 = loxoprofen 98 + celecoxib 99). PPS 177 vs 162.
- **Population**: age 65 or older, ACR-criteria hip/knee OA, BPI item 3 of 3 or more; exclusions included IBD, kidney disease, uncontrolled hypertension, heavy alcohol use, current ulcer/reflux treatment. Prophylactic PPI/H2RA/mucosal protectants and rescue analgesics were prohibited.
- **Interventions**: acetaminophen 600 mg three times daily (1800 mg/day; not the maximum approved dose); loxoprofen 60 mg three times daily; celecoxib 100 mg twice daily; 8 weeks, double-dummy-style placebo.
- **Outcomes**: primary BPI item 3 change at week 8 (non-inferiority margin 0.6); secondary BPI at week 4, SF-8, GSRS, eGFR, blood pressure, liver enzymes, adverse events.

- GSRS: mean rise at week 2 was 0.01 (acetaminophen) vs 0.08 (NSAIDs), most notable in the loxoprofen subgroup; scores then plateaued. Authors suggest caution with NSAIDs even for 4 weeks or less.
- Reported GI disorders included dyspepsia, gastric ulcer, abdominal pain and GERD. Hepatobiliary adverse event: one grade 1 case in the acetaminophen group.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- No placebo arm; non-inferiority missed by 0.01 on a deliberately conservative margin; acetaminophen dosed below the maximum approved dose.
- GI outcomes via GSRS and adverse-event reporting, not endoscopy; bleeding not confirmed, likely underestimating GI events.
- Per-NSAID comparisons were exploratory (sample size powered for pooled NSAIDs vs acetaminophen); no per-arm GI event counts beyond discontinuations in the main text.
- ALT elevation with acetaminophen not followed up for reversibility.
- Non-dental: older adults, chronic OA pain, 8-week continuous dosing, in Japan; extrapolation to short-course dental/postoperative analgesia is limited.
- Industry-sponsored (Ayumi Pharmaceutical).

## 7. Glossary
- **BPI item 3**: Brief Pain Inventory worst-pain-in-24-hours 0-10 rating.
- **GSRS**: Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale, 15 items.
- **MCID**: minimal clinically important difference (0.9 for OA pain NRS per authors).
- **FAS / PPS**: full analysis set / per-protocol set.
