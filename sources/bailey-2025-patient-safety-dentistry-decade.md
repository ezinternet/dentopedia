Direct extension of an existing overview pillar.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review / perspective (BDJ 2025) by the same authors as Bailey 2021, marking 10 years in the field: summarises paradigm shifts (Safety I → Safety II, blame → just culture), practical frameworks (HALT/IMSAFE, pre-clinic huddles, hierarchy management), and policy landmarks (PSIRF 2023-24, NHS primary care patient safety strategy September 2024, Project Sphere).

Wrong-tooth extraction was removed from the NHS "never events" list in 2021 due to insufficient systemic barriers; checklists alone are ineffective without a positive safety culture and team engagement; UK healthcare faced culture-of-blame in dentistry, with fear of reporting incidents due to litigation/regulatory concerns.

Outlook: access crisis (DIY dentistry, oral cancer), facial aesthetics expansion, provisional overseas registration, and increasing AI/technology use are new patient safety frontiers; gamified education has improved early-career knowledge; next decade will demand curriculum and legislative reform.

## Key Contributions

- **Safety I vs Safety II**: Safety I = retrospective, studying infrequent failures; Safety II = why things go well, resilience-focused — more nuanced model than Swiss cheese
- **Human factors acronyms**: HALT (Hungry Angry Late Tired) + IMSAFE (Illness Medication Stress Alcohol Fatigue Emotions) for recognising unsafe staff states
- **Wrong-tooth extraction removed from never events (2021)**: systemic barriers insufficient to prevent it; highlights limits of binary "never event" classification
- **PSIRF (2023–24)**: Patient Safety Incident Response Framework — requires NHS Trusts; changes from Datix-style reporting to "learn from patient safety events" (LFPSE); supports patient/family engagement
- **NHS primary care patient safety strategy (Sept 2024)**: explicitly includes primary dental care; aims to give dentists access to National Care Records Service to reduce reliance on patient-declared histories
- **Project Sphere**: NHS England + Chief Dental Officer initiative specifically targeting dental safety culture; published huddle sheets June 2023
- **Freedom to speak up guardians**: all NHS organisations required; response to high-profile failures (Lucy Letby case cited)
- **Burnout/wellbeing**: NHS absence rate 5.6% in 2022; 170,000 staff left or considering leaving due to workload; Maslow-based NHS staff wellbeing needs framework
- **Hierarchy and CRM**: crew resource management (CRM) from aviation; Korean Air 6509 anecdote (junior didn't speak up to senior) as hierarchy risk illustration; dental-specific mitigation: induction, huddles, visible name/role badges

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative review / perspective
- **Scope**: 10-year retrospective (2014–2025) plus forward look
- **Author vantage**: lead author Bailey is a practising dental professional in the UK secondary care and academic setting

## Key Results / Content

- 98,000 deaths/yr from medical errors (USA 2000 landmark report); patient safety incidents could be #3 cause of death after heart disease and cancer; up to 50% of adverse events preventable; 15% of OECD hospital expenditure attributable to safety failures
- Checklist use in dentistry still largely confined to oral surgery in specialist/academic settings; checklists alone are insufficient without safety culture + team engagement
- Second-victim concept has generated controversy (some patients object to "victim" labelling); six-stage cycle described post-incident; early support shortens recovery trajectory
- Patient safety culture in dentistry lags medical specialties, especially in general dental practice; fear of litigation and regulatory action suppresses reporting — "punishment myth"

## Limitations

- Narrative perspective, no systematic search
- Authors' experience based in UK secondary care and academic settings — may not generalise to community dental practice or other health systems
- No quantitative data; relies on policy documents and the authors' own experiential claims

## Glossary

- **Safety II**: approach focusing on why most care goes well (resilience, adaptive capacity), complementing Safety I's focus on failures
- **PSIRF**: Patient Safety Incident Response Framework — NHS framework replacing previous Datix-focused blame-based reporting, rolled out 2023–24
- **Project Sphere**: NHS England / Chief Dental Officer initiative developing dental-specific patient safety tools and culture change
- **HALT / IMSAFE**: acronyms capturing physiological/psychological states that predispose clinical unsafe acts
- **CRM**: Crew Resource Management — originally aviation concept, adapted to healthcare to empower junior team members to raise concerns with senior colleagues
