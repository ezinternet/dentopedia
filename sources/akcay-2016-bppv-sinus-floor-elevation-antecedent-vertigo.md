## Why Ingested

사용자 요청으로 진행 중인 상악동거상술 관련 어지럼증(vertigo)/양성돌발성두위현훈(Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo, BPPV) 문헌 표적 조사(targeted sweep)의 일환으로 인제스트. 기존 [[sinus-lift/transcrestal/stacchi-2022-transcrestal-sinus-complications-low-rbh]]는 7개 센터 n=430의 합병증 발생률 데이터에서 BPPV를 0.5%로 보고했으나 위험인자를 특정하지 않았다. 본 증례보고는 그 발생률 데이터를 **사전 어지럼증 병력(prior vertigo history)이 있는 환자에서 지연·재발성 BPPV로 이어질 수 있다**는 실행 가능한 술전 스크리닝 시사점으로 확장한다 — 즉 "이 환자에게 과거 어지럼증 병력이 있는가?"를 문진에 추가해야 한다는 임상적 함의를 제공하는, 발생률 논문들과는 구별되는 각도의 근거다.

## Three-line Summary

Case report (n=1, 43-year-old female) of benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) immediately following osteotome sinus floor elevation (OSFE) with simultaneous implant placement, in a patient with an undisclosed history of two prior vertigo episodes over 9 years.

Standard same-night treatment (nootropic medication + canalith repositioning procedure) failed to resolve symptoms; the patient required readmission after 1 month and recovered only after repeated repositioning maneuvers, nine doses of IV serous fluid, and piracetam over a 10-day course, with no recurrence thereafter.

Prior vertigo history is proposed as a risk factor for protracted/refractory post-OSFE BPPV, supporting pre-operative screening for vertigo history before osteotome-based transcrestal sinus lift procedures — abstract-only, full text not retrieved.

## 세줄요약

골절제기 상악동거상술(Osteotome Sinus Floor Elevation, OSFE) 및 동시 임플란트 식립 직후 발생한 양성돌발성두위현훈(Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo, BPPV) 증례보고(n=1, 43세 여성) — 환자는 지난 9년간 2회의 어지럼증 병력이 있었으나 사전에 알려지지 않았음.

수술 당일 밤 표준치료(향정신성 약물 + 이석정복술(Canalith Repositioning Procedure, CRP))로 호전되지 않아 1개월 후 재입원, 반복 정복술·정맥 수액 9회·피라세탐(piracetam) 투여를 포함한 10일간의 치료 후 완치, 이후 재발 없음.

기존 어지럼증 병력이 OSFE 후 지연·난치성 BPPV의 위험인자로 제시되며, 골절제기 기반 경치조골 상악동거상술 전 어지럼증 병력에 대한 술전 스크리닝의 근거가 됨 — 초록만 확보, 전문 미확보(abstract-only — full text not retrieved).

## 2. Key Contributions
- Documents a case of protracted, refractory BPPV following osteotome sinus floor elevation (OSFE) requiring 10 days of repeated treatment, in contrast to the typically transient/self-limiting BPPV reported in most OSFE case series.
- Identifies a prior vertigo history (2 episodes over 9 years, undisclosed until post-procedure questioning) as a plausible predisposing factor for a protracted course.
- Reinforces the mechanistic hypothesis that vibratory forces from osteotome/mallet malleting plus head hyperextension during OSFE displace otoliths into the semicircular canals.
- Frames prevention/management recommendations around pre-operative vertigo-history screening.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Single case report (n=1)
- **Databases**: Not applicable (case report, not a review)
- **n**: 1 patient
- **Outcomes**: Resolution of vertigo/nausea following OSFE + simultaneous implant placement; treatment course and duration to resolution

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Immediate onset of intense vertigo and nausea after OSFE + simultaneous implant placement.
- Same-night treatment (nootropic drug + canalith repositioning procedure by a specialist) — no improvement.
- Readmission after 1 month due to incomplete recovery.
- Resolution achieved after repeated repositioning maneuvers + 9 doses of IV serous fluid + piracetam administration.
- Total treatment duration: 10 days; no recurrence of dizziness after successful treatment.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Single case report — no causal inference possible; cannot estimate incidence or generalize the "prior vertigo history → protracted course" association beyond this one patient.
- Full text not retrieved (PMC full_text field empty); abstract-only summary — mechanism section, imaging/vestibular test details, and full discussion/prevention recommendations not captured here.
- No comparator group or systematic risk-factor analysis; hypothesis-generating only.

## 7. Glossary
- **BPPV (Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo)**: Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo — transient vertigo triggered by head position change, caused by displaced otoconia (otoliths) in the semicircular canals.
- **OSFE (Osteotome Sinus Floor Elevation)**: Osteotome sinus floor elevation — a transcrestal technique using osteotomes and a mallet to elevate the sinus floor and place implants simultaneously.
- **Canalith Repositioning Procedure (CRP)**: Also known as the Epley maneuver; a sequence of head/body positions used to move displaced otoconia out of the semicircular canals back into the vestibule.
- **Piracetam**: A nootropic drug used adjunctively in some vertigo/vestibular disorder management protocols.
