## Three-line Summary

- Organization-authored ADA chairside guide (©2024; FDA/HHS U01FD007151) operationalizing the conditional recommendations for postoperative pain after SIMPLE and SURGICAL tooth extraction(s) in adolescents (12–<17 y), adults (17–<65 y), and older adults (≥65 y).
- First line (both scenarios): NSAID alone (e.g., ibuprofen 400 mg or naproxen sodium 440 mg) OR NSAID + acetaminophen (e.g., 500 mg); surgical-only second line adds (Option 1) or replaces (Option 2) with acetaminophen + acetaminophen-opioid combination at lowest effective dose, fewest tablets, duration rarely >3 days; acetaminophen-only branch when NSAIDs contraindicated; corticosteroids suggested AGAINST for surgical pain; optional supplemental long-acting local anesthetic before discharge.
- Simple extractions are non-opioid ONLY (opioids recommended against); wherever opioids appear they demand informed consent, PDMP review, no routine just-in-case prescriptions, and extreme caution in adolescents 12–<17.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Scenario-split architecture**: separate recommendation sets for SIMPLE extraction(s) (non-opioid ONLY, opioids recommended against) vs SURGICAL extraction(s) (non-opioid first-line INSTEAD OF opioids, with a rare-instance opioid-containing second line).
- **Dual first-line options** with divergent escalation logic — Option 1 escalates by ADDING tablets; Option 2 by REPLACING the initial prescription — preventing double-prescribing errors.
- **Opioid minimization engineered into the flow**: opioids enter only at surgical second line, only combined with acetaminophen (never alone), at lowest effective dose / fewest tablets / shortest duration rarely exceeding 3 days; routine delayed just-in-case prescriptions explicitly NOT recommended.
- **Explicit suggestion AGAINST** adding oral, submucosal, or intramuscular corticosteroids to standard analgesic therapy for surgical extraction pain (Conditional, Very low certainty).
- **Supplemental local anesthetic recommendation** (0.5% bupivacaine or 4% articaine regimens) as an optional complementary pre-discharge intervention.
- **Six good practice statements** plus a cautions box printing safety constants (max daily doses, cumulative-acetaminophen warning, co-prescribing exclusions, adolescent caution) on the same page as the prescribing decisions.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Inherited from the parent guideline (GRADE framework): every recommendation carries strength (all Conditional here) and certainty of evidence (Low or Very Low). The panel defined age ranges (adolescents 12–<17, adults 17–<65, older adults ≥65) and issued good practice statements alongside graded recommendations. The chairside edition adds no new synthesis — it renders the recommendations as a two-column clinical pathway (Simple vs Surgical extraction), footnotes, GRADE interpretation tables, and good-practice statements. Contraindication language follows FDA's 2011 guidance-for-industry definition ("only known hazards, not theoretical possibilities"). Funded by FDA/HHS [U01FD007151]; intended to inform clinical decision making, never to restrict legitimate prescribing or coverage.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

All recommendations Conditional; certainty Low unless noted.

- **Rec 1 — Surgical extraction(s)**: post-procedural non-opioid analgesics as first-line therapy INSTEAD OF opioid analgesics (Low).
  - 1.1 Initiate with NSAID alone (e.g., ibuprofen 400 mg or naproxen sodium 440 mg) OR NSAID + acetaminophen (e.g., 500 mg) (Low).
  - 1.2 Rare instances, NSAID-alone inadequate → ADD to the NSAID: one tablet 325 mg acetaminophen PLUS one tablet of a 325 mg acetaminophen-opioid combination (e.g., hydrocodone 5–7.5 mg or oxycodone 5 mg) at lowest effective dose, fewest tablets, shortest duration rarely exceeding 3 days (Low).
  - 1.3 Rare instances, NSAID+acetaminophen inadequate → REPLACE the initial prescription with an NSAID + 325 mg acetaminophen + acetaminophen-opioid combination tablet (same opioid principles) (Low).
  - 1.4 NSAIDs contraindicated → acetaminophen alone at full therapeutic dose (e.g., 1,000 mg) OR the 325 mg acetaminophen + acetaminophen-opioid combination scheme (same opioid principles) (Low).
  - 1.5 Suggests AGAINST adding oral, submucosal, or intramuscular corticosteroids to standard analgesic therapy (**Very low** certainty). Corticosteroids for inflammatory complications (trismus, facial swelling, infection) and IV administration are out of scope.
- **Rec 2 — Simple extraction(s)**: post-procedural non-opioid analgesics ONLY; recommends AGAINST opioid analgesics (Low).
  - 2.1 Same dual first line as surgical: NSAID alone OR NSAID + acetaminophen (Low).
  - 2.2 NSAIDs contraindicated → acetaminophen alone at full therapeutic dose (e.g., 1,000 mg) (Low). No opioid escalation exists in this scenario.
- **Rec 3 — Simple or surgical**: suggests pre-discharge supplemental local anesthetic — 0.5% bupivacaine + 1:200,000 epinephrine by block or infiltration, OR 4% articaine + 1:100,000/1:200,000 epinephrine by infiltration — instead of 2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine or 3% mepivacaine (Low). Goal: immediate in-visit pain relief; complementary to, never a replacement for, analgesics.
- **Good practice statements (6)**: counsel expected pain manageable and discuss past experience/preferences/values before prescribing; review medical/social history (incl. illicit/recreational drug use), current medications, supplements to avoid overdose and drug-drug interactions; minimum effective dosage principle + avoid routine delayed just-in-case opioid prescriptions; if opioids prescribed after failed NSAID ± acetaminophen, counsel storage and disposal; review state PDMP for co-prescribed controlled substances and prioritize nonopioids in chronic-opioid patients; special care prescribing opioids in substance use disorder incl. communication with other providers.
- **Cautions**: max daily doses ibuprofen 2,400 mg / naproxen sodium 1,100 mg / acetaminophen 4,000 mg; acetaminophen-opioid combination pills contain BOTH drugs — total acetaminophen ≤4,000 mg/day; combination scheme NOT offered to patients on gabapentinoids, CNS-active medications (benzodiazepines, antidepressants, anticonvulsants, narcotics), or already taking opioids for other medical reasons; opioid informed consent required (physiological dependence, misuse risk, respiratory depression, driving/machinery effects) — critical in adolescents/young adults even after a single prescription; EXTREME CAUTION in adolescents 12–<17 y (caregiver consent advised).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- All recommendations Conditional with Low or Very Low certainty — shared decision-making is mandatory, not optional; no strong recommendations exist on this page.
- The opioid-containing second line applies only to surgical extraction(s); evidence does not support it for simple extraction(s).
- Corticosteroids are addressed only as adjuncts for surgical-extraction pain itself: management of inflammatory complications (trismus, facial swelling, infection) and IV administration are explicitly out of scope.
- Contraindication determination follows FDA's known-hazards-only standard — clinicians must document the clinical basis when declaring NSAIDs contraindicated.
- Chairside format omits effect sizes and the underlying trial network; cite the parent guideline and its NMA base for quantitative claims.

## 7. Glossary

- **NSAID** — nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen sodium); first-line analgesic class throughout both pathways
- **APAP / acetaminophen** — paracetamol; used alone (NSAID-contraindicated branch) or combined with NSAID ± opioid
- **Acetaminophen-opioid combination tablet** — fixed-dose pill containing 325 mg acetaminophen plus an opioid (e.g., hydrocodone 5–7.5 mg or oxycodone 5 mg)
- **Simple vs surgical extraction** — uncomplicated forceps/elevator removal vs extraction requiring flap/sectioning/bone removal; determines whether any opioid-containing second line exists
- **Just-in-case (delayed) prescription** — opioid prescribed preemptively "in case" breakthrough pain occurs; NOT recommended
- **PDMP** — Prescription Drug Monitoring Program; state database of controlled-substance dispensing
- **GRADE** — Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation; framework behind strength (Strong/Conditional) and certainty (High/Moderate/Low/Very Low)
