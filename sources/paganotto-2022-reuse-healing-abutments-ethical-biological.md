## Why Ingested

Anchors the practice-reality/ethics pole of the healing-abutment (HA) reuse controversy: while [[infection-control/bidra-2019-should-healing-abutments-cover-screws]] and the in-vitro decontamination papers debate *whether* residual protein/biofilm can be removed and *how*, this survey documents that in the field reuse is already near-universal (98.1%), risk perception is low (53.3% see no limitation), and manufacturer guidance is essentially absent (95.3% received none). It is the epidemiological/behavioral counterweight to the bench evidence, and it exposes the informed-consent gap (94.5% do not tell the patient) that the biological papers do not measure.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional survey of 202 Brazilian implantologists: 98.1% reuse healing abutments (cost-driven, 71.2%), 94.5% do not inform the patient, all disinfect + autoclave, and 95.3% received no manufacturer guidance — reuse is de facto standard practice with low risk perception.

## 1. Document Information

- **Type**: Cross-sectional survey (online questionnaire, Google Forms)
- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical and Experimental Dentistry, 2022;14(10):e822-e826
- **Setting**: Implantology specialists registered at the Regional Council of Dentistry of Rio Grande do Sul (CRO/RS), Brazil
- **Sampling**: 1,147 registered implantologists → GPower sample-size calc (203 needed) + 40% non-response buffer = 284 randomly selected (Randomizer software); 202 valid responses
- **Data collection**: Sept–Nov 2021, email + WhatsApp, max 2 submissions
- **Reliability**: Cronbach's alpha 0.7–0.8 (acceptable)
- **Questionnaire**: 3 sections — informed consent; demographics; reuse/disinfection/sterilization routines, risk perception, manufacturer information

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantifies the **prevalence of HA reuse** in a specialist population — 98.1%, i.e. near-universal.
- Documents the **informed-consent gap**: 94.5% of practitioners do not inform the patient that the HA is reused.
- Shows an **absence of manufacturer guidance** (95.3% received none), tying the low risk perception to a training/information deficit.
- Characterizes **actual cleaning/sterilization routines** in practice (enzymatic detergent + ultrasonic bath dominant; universal autoclave), providing the behavioral backdrop against which the in-vitro decontamination studies should be read.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Online 3-section questionnaire distributed to a randomized sample of CRO/RS-registered implantology specialists. Sample size derived from GPower (203) with a 40% non-response buffer (→284 invited). Demographic section (sex, age, practice ownership) plus a routines/perception section covering reuse frequency, cleaning method, sterilization method, patient disclosure, perceived limitations, and receipt of manufacturer guidance. Descriptive statistics on the 202 respondents; internal consistency assessed with Cronbach's alpha (0.7–0.8).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Demographics (n=202)**
- 75.2% men, 24.8% women; mean age 42.7 (SD 8.4); modal group 41–50 yr (45.5%)
- 89.6% own a private practice; 53% hold >1 specialty

**Reuse behavior**
- Reuse HAs: **98.1%**; do not reuse: 1.9%
- Reasons: **cost 71.2%**, practicality 26%, difficulty of availability 1.9%
- Reuse "countless times" 98.1% (reuse repeatedly 91.8%); reuse only once 8.2%
- Do NOT inform the patient about reuse: **94.5%**; inform: 5.5%

**Cleaning / sterilization**
- ALL respondents disinfect after use
- Cleaning: enzymatic detergent + ultrasonic bath **50.7%**, ultrasonic bath only 23.3%, water + detergent 21.9%, sodium bicarbonate blasting 1.4%, NaOCl 1.4%
- Sterilization: **ALL autoclave**; none use glutaraldehyde

**Risk perception / guidance**
- Received NO manufacturer guidance on reuse: **95.3%**
- Perceived limitations: **none 53.3%**, increased roughness/corrosion 20%, remaining organic matter 17.8%, cross-infection **8.9%**

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single-region (Rio Grande do Sul) specialist sample — generalizability to other countries/regulatory contexts is limited.
- Self-report survey: no biological/microbiological measurement of the reused HAs; behavior and perception, not outcomes, are captured.
- Cannot establish clinical harm; complements but does not replace the in-vitro residual-protein/biofilm and RCT decontamination evidence.
- Highlights a need for manufacturer guidance, standardized decontamination protocols, and clarification of the informed-consent obligation.

## 7. Glossary

- **Healing abutment (HA)**: transmucosal component screwed onto the implant during the healing/soft-tissue-maturation phase to shape the emergence profile; sits in the mouth 30–180 days.
- **Autoclave**: steam sterilization (typically 121–134 °C under pressure); the sterilization step used by all respondents — sterilizes but does not remove adhered organic matter/biofilm.
- **Enzymatic detergent + ultrasonic bath**: common cleaning combination; cavitation plus proteolytic enzymes intended to dislodge organic debris before sterilization.
- **Cross-infection**: transmission of microorganisms between patients via an inadequately reprocessed shared device — the biological hazard perceived by only 8.9% here.
- **Cronbach's alpha**: internal-consistency reliability coefficient (0–1); 0.7–0.8 is acceptable.
