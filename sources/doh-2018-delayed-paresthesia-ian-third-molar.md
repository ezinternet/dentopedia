## Why Ingested

임플란트가 아닌 제3대구치 발치 후 지연성(2주) 하치조신경 지각이상 증례 — [[oral-surgery/nerve-injury/sarikov-2014-ian-injury-third-molar-review]]가 다룬 발치 관련 IAN 손상에 헤모글로빈 유리·국소염증이라는 새로운 병태생리 기전 가설을 더한다.

## Three-line Summary

This case report describes a 53-year-old woman who developed delayed paresthesia of the inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) 2 weeks after surgical extraction of the left lower third molar, with symptoms lasting 5 weeks before complete resolution.

Neurosensory testing at onset showed decreased sensation on the affected side (brush direction test 7/10, pin prick 5/10 vs. control 10/10) and two-point discrimination of 15 mm (control 12 mm); postoperative CBCT revealed discontinuity of the upper cortical layer of the inferior alveolar canal.

The patient was diagnosed with neuropraxia of the IAN based on the temporary nature of symptoms and complete recovery within 6–8 weeks, with pathophysiology attributed to hemoglobin release and local inflammatory processes following partial loss of the blood clot, similar to alveolar osteitis.

## 세줄요약

본 증례보고는 53세 여성에서 좌측 하악 제3대구치 외과적 발치 2주 후 지연성 하치조신경 (Inferior Alveolar Nerve, IAN) 지각이상이 발생하여 5주간 지속된 후 완전히 회복된 사례를 기술한다.

발병 당시 신경감각검사에서 침범측의 감각 저하(솔방향검사 7/10, 침자극검사 5/10 vs. 대조측 10/10)와 2점 식별검사 15 mm(대조측 12 mm)를 보였고, 술후 CBCT에서 하치조관 상방 피질골의 불연속이 관찰되었다.

증상의 일시적 특성과 6~8주 내 완전회복을 바탕으로 하치조신경의 신경실용증 (neuropraxia)으로 진단되었으며, 혈액손실에 따른 헤모글로빈 유리와 국소 염증과정이 병태생리로 제시되었고 이는 건성치조와 유사하였다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Presents a rare case of delayed IAN paresthesia occurring 2 weeks after third molar surgery, with complete resolution at 6 weeks.
- Documents neurosensory testing results (brush direction test, pin prick, two-point discrimination) at onset and recovery.
- Identifies partial loss of blood clot (alveolar osteitis-like presentation) without hematoma organization or bone fragments.
- Proposes hemoglobin release and local inflammation, rather than clot organization or bone fragment compression, as the pathophysiologic mechanism.
- Demonstrates radiologic evidence of upper cortical layer discontinuity of the inferior alveolar canal on postoperative CBCT.
- Classifies the injury as neuropraxia based on Seddon's classification, with complete recovery within expected 6–8 week timeframe.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: Case report of a single patient with delayed IAN paresthesia following surgical extraction of mandibular third molar.
- Patient: 53-year-old woman with chronic pericoronitis of left lower third molar, no significant medical history.
- Procedure: IAN block anesthesia with 2 ampoules of 2% lidocaine with 1:100,000 epinephrine, followed by surgical extraction and suturing; no intraoperative complications and IAN not visualized.
- Assessment: Clinical examination, subjective sensory reporting, skin mapping of affected area, and neurosensory testing (brush direction test, pin prick test on visual analog scale, two-point discrimination test) performed on left chin with unaffected right side as control, following Poort et al. (2009) recommendations.
- Imaging: Preoperative panoramic radiography and CBCT; postoperative CBCT to evaluate cortical bone integrity.
- Diagnosis: Delayed paresthesia of IAN, classified as neuropraxia based on Seddon's classification (temporary conduction block, full recovery within 6–8 weeks).
- Treatment: Anti-inflammatory drugs and steroids prescribed; follow-up at 1 week and 1 month post-diagnosis.
- Outcomes: Resolution of extraction site pain at 1 week; complete resolution of paresthesia by patient report and subjective testing at 6 weeks post-onset.
- Statistics: Descriptive case report; no inferential statistical analysis performed.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Primary outcome — Time to symptom onset:** Delayed paresthesia onset at 2 weeks postoperatively.
- **Primary outcome — Symptom duration:** Paresthesia lasted approximately 5 weeks (from 2 weeks to 6 weeks postoperatively).
- **Primary outcome — Recovery:** Complete resolution by patient report and subjective testing at 6 weeks post-onset.
- **Neurosensory testing (at onset, affected side vs. control):** Brush direction test: 7/10 vs. 10/10; Pin prick test: 5/10 vs. 10/10; Two-point discrimination: 15 mm vs. 12 mm (normal).
- **Radiologic finding:** Postoperative CBCT showed discontinuity of upper cortical layer of the inferior alveolar canal.
- **Clinical finding:** Partial loss of blood clot at extraction site without swelling or redness; no submandibular lymphadenopathy.
- **Classification:** Neuropraxia (Seddon classification) based on temporary conduction block and full recovery within 6–8 weeks.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single case report with limited generalizability.
- No histologic or microbiologic confirmation of the proposed pathophysiologic mechanism (hemoglobin release, inflammation).
- The role of alveolar osteitis in delayed paresthesia is speculative; no direct evidence linking clot loss to nerve injury.
- No long-term follow-up beyond complete recovery to assess for recurrence or late sequelae.
- Limited objective measure of nerve function recovery beyond subjective patient report and basic sensory testing.
- No comparison group or alternative treatment evaluation.

## 6. Related Work

- Seddon 1943 — Three types of nerve injury: neuropraxia, axonotmesis, and neurotmesis; foundational classification system.
- Borgonovo et al. 2012 — Reported three cases of delayed paresthesia after third molar extraction; proposed compression by clot, fibrous organization, and bone fragments as possible etiologies.
- Flanagan 2002 — Delayed onset of altered sensation following dental implant placement; associated hemoglobin degradation and iron release with delayed neuropathy.
- Goldberg and Galbraith 1984 — Late onset of mandibular and lingual dysesthesia secondary to postextraction infection; proposed bacterial invasion or inflammation of neural sheath.
- Tolstunov and Pogrel 2009 — Delayed paresthesia of IAN after extraction of mandibular third molar; case report and possible etiology discussion.
- Kipp et al. 1980 — Retrospective study of 1,477 third molar surgeries; delayed paresthesia represented only 5% of 60 paresthesia cases.

## 7. Glossary

- **IAN (Inferior Alveolar Nerve)**: 하치조신경 — 삼차신경의 제3분지로, 하악 치아, 하순, 턱, 뺨의 감각을 지배하는 신경.
- **Neuropraxia**: 신경실용증 — Seddon 분류에서 가장 경미한 말초신경 손상으로, 신경전도 차단으로 인한 일시적 감각기능 상실을 특징으로 하며 평균 6~8주 내 완전회복됨.
- **CBCT (Cone Beam Computed Tomography)**: 콘빔 컴퓨터단층촬영 — 치과 영역에서 3차원적 해부학적 평가에 사용되는 방사선 검사법.
- **Alveolar osteitis**: 건성치조 — 발치 후 치조와에서 혈병의 부분적 또는 완전 소실로 인한 술후 통증 상태.
- **Two-point discrimination test**: 2점 식별검사 — 두 개의 점을 동시에 자극하여 환자가 두 점을 구별할 수 있는 최소 거리를 측정하는 신경감각검사.
