## Why Ingested

Fills the "still missing" gap in [[wiki/overviews/unopposed-tooth-overeruption-overview]] — the only CE decision-review that explicitly answers "when should a clinician replace a missing second molar with an implant?" using chewing efficiency, super-eruption, and occlusal-interference evidence, directly extending [[wiki/occlusion/faggion-2011-evidence-restore-posterior-bounded-edentulous-grade]]'s GRADE finding that replacement is not automatically mandatory.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative literature review assessing whether a missing second molar warrants implant restoration, synthesizing data on chewing efficiency, super-eruption, and occlusal interferences.

First-molar occlusion alone provides ~90% masticatory efficiency; ~20% of unopposed posterior teeth extrude ≥2 mm, yet over-eruption degree is NOT strongly related to occlusal interference incidence.

Replacement is driven by patient preference, not a clinical mandate — monitoring with preventive management of supraeruption is a valid default when the patient perceives no masticatory deficit.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantifies that first-molar occlusion alone restores ~90% of chewing efficiency, making second-molar replacement an optional quality-of-life upgrade rather than a functional necessity.
- Synthesizes super-eruption data: frequent occurrence but only ~20% extrude ≥2 mm, and degree of overeruption is not a reliable predictor of occlusal interference.
- Reframes the clinical decision as patient-preference–driven: if the patient perceives no deficit, replacement is not indicated; if they notice a gap or reduced chewing, an implant is appropriate.
- Notes that supraeruption can complicate future restorative cases — preventive monitoring is warranted even when immediate replacement is deferred.

## 7. Glossary

- **Super-eruption / supraeruption / over-eruption**: Coronal migration of a tooth unopposed by an antagonist; used interchangeably in this literature.
- **Masticatory efficiency**: Proportion of normal chewing function retained; first-molar contact alone achieves ~90%.
- **Occlusal interference**: Premature contact during mandibular closure or excursion that disrupts smooth occlusal function, a potential downstream consequence of super-eruption.
- **Jumping distance / buccal gap**: Not addressed in this paper — distinct from the gap grafting literature.
