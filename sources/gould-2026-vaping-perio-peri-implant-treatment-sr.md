## Three-line Summary

SR (no meta-analysis), databases including Ovid MEDLINE/PubMed/Scopus/WoS through June 2026 — evaluating the effect of vaping (e-cigarettes) on periodontal and peri-implant treatment outcomes.

Vaping users may exhibit worse clinical and immunological treatment responses vs non-smokers, with outcomes appearing similar to conventional cigarette smokers; definitive causal conclusions cannot be drawn.

Certainty of evidence was low to very low due to limited studies, observational designs, heterogeneous exposure definitions, and short follow-up; meta-analysis was not feasible.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First SR specifically evaluating vaping's effect on periodontal AND peri-implant treatment outcomes
- Vape users may exhibit outcomes similar to cigarette smokers — worse than non-smokers
- Certainty of evidence: low to very low (observational designs, small studies, heterogeneous exposure definitions)
- PICO: vaping (P) + perio/peri-implant treatment (I) vs smokers/former smokers/non-smokers (C) → clinical and immunological outcomes (O)
- Cannot draw definitive causal conclusions; patterns are suggestive only
- Clinical implication: treat vaping with same caution as conventional smoking in perio/peri-implant management

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: SR (no MA — insufficient data for pooling)
- Databases: Ovid MEDLINE, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science; no date restrictions; search through June 30, 2026
- Inclusion: studies on vaping and perio/peri-implant treatment outcomes (gingivitis, periodontitis, peri-implantitis)
- Risk of bias: Newcastle-Ottawa Quality Assessment Scale
- PRISMA-compliant

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Vape users: possible association with less favorable clinical and immunological responses vs non-smokers
- Vape users vs cigarette smokers: outcomes appear similar (both worse than non-smokers)
- Immunological: vaping may affect oral cytokine levels (IL-1β, PGE2) — less studied than smoking
- Certainty: low to very low due to limited studies, observational designs, heterogeneous vaping exposure definitions, short follow-up
- MA not feasible due to heterogeneity in study design, outcome measures, and exposure definitions

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Small number of eligible studies — SR without MA
- Predominantly observational designs
- Heterogeneous vaping exposure (frequency, duration, nicotine content) across studies
- Short follow-up in most studies
- Need: longitudinal/interventional studies with larger diverse populations; specific vape aerosol constituent effects on host immunity

## 6. Related Work
- Cigarette smoking as perio risk factor is well-established (central sensitization, inflammatory pathways)
- Reactive oxygen species from e-vapor → DNA damage and gingival fibroblast impairment (in vitro/observational)
- Delayed healing and higher post-treatment complication rates reported for vapers in some studies

## 7. Glossary
- Vaping/e-cigarette: electronic nicotine delivery system; vaporizes nicotine solution (propylene glycol, vegetable glycerin, flavoring)
- Peri-implant disease: peri-implant mucositis (soft tissue only) or peri-implantitis (bone loss)
- Newcastle-Ottawa Scale: non-randomized study risk-of-bias tool
