## Why Ingested

First 6-month update of the living systematic review on COVID-19 oral manifestations, expanding the evidence base to 183 studies and 64,876 patients. Introduces xerostomia (43%) as a new major finding alongside the taste disorder triad and oral mucosal lesions. Pairs with the original 2020 living SR.

## Three-line Summary

6-month update of the COVID-19 oral manifestations living SR (183 studies, 64,876 patients) establishing taste disorders (38%), xerostomia (43%), and oral mucosal lesions as the triad of common oral COVID-19 manifestations.

## 세줄요약

COVID-19 구강 증상 살아있는 체계적 문헌고찰 6개월 업데이트(183편, 64,876명): 미각장애 38%·구강건조증 43%·구강점막병변을 3대 주요 구강 소견으로 확립.

- **Type**: Systematic review (living SR first update), meta-analysis
- **Journal**: Journal of Dental Research, 2021;100(12):1321–1329
- **DOI**: [10.1177/00220345211029637](https://doi.org/10.1177/00220345211029637)
- **PMID**: 34324825
- **Institution**: Laboratory of Oral Histopathology, Health Sciences Faculty, University of Brasilia, Brazil (lead); multiple Brazilian institutions

## 2. Key Contributions

- Expands evidence base from 40 (original 2020 SR) to 183 studies, 64,876 patients globally
- Provides pooled prevalence of taste disorder subtypes: hypogeusia 34%, dysgeusia 33%, ageusia 26%
- Introduces xerostomia as a new major outcome with pooled prevalence 43%
- Confirms strong association between taste disorders and positive COVID-19 test (OR = 7.54, high certainty)
- Identifies association between taste disorders and mild/moderate COVID-19 severity (OR = 1.63, low certainty) and female sex (OR = 1.77, low certainty)
- Catalogues oral mucosal lesion patterns: aphthous-like > herpes-like > candidiasis > glossitis/depapillation/geographic tongue > parotitis > angular cheilitis
- Proposes the triad: xerostomia + taste dysfunction + oral mucosal lesions as common COVID-19 oral manifestations

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: Living systematic review — first 6-month update of PROSPERO CRD42020184468
- Literature search: 6 databases + gray literature (expanded from original SR)
- Inclusion: Studies reporting oral signs/symptoms in COVID-19 patients, any language
- Risk of bias: Joanna Briggs Institute appraisal tools
- Evidence certainty: GRADE assessment
- Statistical method: Meta-analysis with pooled prevalence, odds ratios, 95% CI
- Full text not retrieved; summary based on abstract

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

*Abstract-only*

- 183 studies, 64,876 COVID-19 patients worldwide included
- Overall taste disorder prevalence: 38% (95% CI 22%–56%; I²=98%)
  - Hypogeusia: 34%; Dysgeusia: 33%; Ageusia: 26%
- Taste disorders vs. positive COVID-19 test: OR 7.54 (95% CI 5.24–10.86; I²=93%; p<0.00001) — high certainty
- Taste disorders vs. mild/moderate severity: OR 1.63 (95% CI 1.33–1.99; I²=69%; p<0.0001) — low certainty
- Taste disorders vs. female sex: OR 1.77 (95% CI 1.26–2.48; I²=79%; p=0.001) — low certainty
- Xerostomia (new finding): 43% (95% CI 36%–50%; I²=71%)
- Oral mucosal lesions predominate on tongue, lips, and palate; most likely represent co-infections

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- High statistical heterogeneity (I²=98% for overall taste disorders) limits precision
- Certainty of evidence for severity and sex associations rated low
- Oral mucosal lesions likely represent co-infections/secondary manifestations, not direct SARS-CoV-2 causation
- More studies needed to confirm direct SARS-CoV-2 infection role in oral cavity

## 7. Glossary

- **Living systematic review (살아있는 체계적 문헌고찰, Living SR)**: SR updated at regular intervals as new evidence emerges
- **Xerostomia (구강건조증, Xerostomia)**: Subjective sensation of dry mouth
- **Dysgeusia (미각이상, Dysgeusia)**: Distorted taste perception
- **Hypogeusia (미각감소, Hypogeusia)**: Reduced taste sensitivity
- **Ageusia (미각소실, Ageusia)**: Complete loss of taste
- **GRADE**: Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluations — framework for rating certainty of evidence
- **Angular cheilitis (구각염, Angular cheilitis)**: Inflammation at corners of the mouth
- **Glossitis (설염, Glossitis)**: Inflammation of the tongue
