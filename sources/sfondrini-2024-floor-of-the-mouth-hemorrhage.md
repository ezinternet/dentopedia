## Why Ingested

Fills the sublingual-artery / floor-of-mouth hemorrhage gap: mechanism, incidence context, airway compromise, and management. → [[oral-surgery/sfondrini-2024-floor-of-the-mouth-hemorrhage]]

## Three-line Summary

Two cases of life-threatening floor-of-mouth hemorrhage after interforaminal mandibular implant surgery (one with GBR); both patients developed airway compromise requiring ICU hospitalization and nasotracheal intubation, with spontaneous hemostasis and complete hematoma resorption by ~2 weeks. The classic mechanism is lingual cortical plate perforation during drilling injuring the sublingual artery; a novel reported mechanism is ascending mental artery injury during periosteal release incision in GBR, with hematoma spreading to the floor of the mouth through the lingual flap incision. Management prioritizes bimanual compression, immediate hospital transfer, and airway security (intubation/tracheostomy), with surgical evacuation/ligation or embolization reserved for large nonrestrictive hematomas; prevention centers on preoperative CT, buccolingual drilling angulation, wide flap reflection, meticulous hemostasis, short implants, and a 2 h postoperative observation period.

## 세줄요약

하악 전치간부(interforaminal) 임플란트 수술(1건은 GBR 동반) 후 발생한 구강저 출혈 2예; 두 환자 모두 혈종이 구강저 연조직으로 빠르게 퍼져 혀를 후방·상방으로 밀어 기도 폐쇄가 발생해 ICU 입원 및 비강기관삽관이 필요했고, 출혈은 자연 지혈되어 약 2주 내 혈종이 완전 흡수되었다. 고전적 기전은 drilling 중 설측 피질골 천공으로 설하동맥(sublingual artery)이 손상되는 것이며, 이 보고에서는 GBR의 골막 방출 절개(periosteal release incision) 중 상승성 정신동맥(ascending mental artery) 손상으로 구강저로 혈종이 전파되는 새로운 기전도 제시한다. 관리는 이중수지압박(bimanual compression)·즉시 병원 이송·기도 확보(삽관/기관절개)를 우선하고, 큰 비제한성 혈종에만 수술적 배액·결紮/색전술을 사용하며, 예방으로는 수술 전 CT, 협설측 drilling 각도 조정, 넓은 피판 반사, 정밀 지혈, 단축 임플란트, 2시간 수술 후 관찰을 강조한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Documents two cases of life-threatening floor-of-mouth hemorrhage after interforaminal mandibular implant surgery (one with simultaneous GBR).
- Reinforces classic mechanism: lingual cortical plate perforation during drilling → sublingual artery injury.
- First report of floor-of-mouth hematoma secondary to vestibular soft-tissue (ascending mental artery) injury during GBR periosteal release incision, with hematoma spreading to the floor of the mouth via the lingual flap incision.
- Provides management hierarchy (bimanual compression → hospital transfer → airway security; spontaneous hemostasis common; surgical evacuation/ligation or embolization for large nonrestrictive hematomas) and prevention strategies.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Descriptive case report of two patients treated in different outpatient dental offices, subsequently transferred to IRCCS Policlinico San Matteo ICU. Each case includes clinical presentation, imaging (orthopantomography, CT, MR angiography), management, and follow-up. Literature review informs mechanism and prevention discussion. No controlled design; incidence not quantifiable.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Case 1**: 52-year-old male, 4 implants in interforaminal knife-edge ridge; delayed onset swelling/elevation of floor of mouth → airway obstruction; mobile emergency unit performed prompt tracheal intubation; ICU; angiography/CT showed no active bleeding (robust extravascular clots → spontaneous hemostasis); nasotracheal tube removed day 3; discharged; complete hematoma resorption at 15-day follow-up. CT: insufficient mandibular thickness, lingual cortical plate absence around two implants; implant length 10–13 mm, diameter 3.5 mm. Multiple dehiscences/fenestrations → malpractice.
- **Case 2**: 69-year-old female, GBR + 2 implants in symphyseal region; delayed onset copious bleeding/swelling displacing tongue cranially → progressive airway obstruction; nasotracheal intubation; CT/MR angiography showed active bleeding initially then none ~12 h later; no lingual cortical perforation; wide buccal hematoma with floor-of-mouth involvement. Tube removed day 3; discharged day 3 post-intubation; infection of residual hematoma/xenograft at 1 week (antibiotics 7 days); surgical toilet at 2 weeks; healed by 1 month.
- **Mechanism/anatomy**: sublingual artery (2 mm diameter) arises from lingual artery, courses between genioglossus and mylohyoid, reaches lingual anterior cortical plate via middle/lateral lingual canals; anastomoses with submental artery (facial artery branch) running on mylohyoid surface; terminal branch = ascending mental artery to chin/lip. Loose adipose/connective tissue of floor of mouth does not contain bleeding; hematoma spreads to submandibular space and tongue → upper airway obstruction.
- **Onset**: bleeding may be immediate or delayed (reflective vasoconstriction + epinephrine in local anesthetic → clot; delayed onset via compensatory vasodilation, thrombus loss).
- **Management**: immediate bimanual compression at suspected perforation site + urgent hospital transfer; airway control = observation / oro- or nasotracheal intubation / tracheostomy or cricothyroidotomy; once airway secured, most cases achieve spontaneous hemostasis via hematoma pressure; avoid surgical drainage (may promote bleeding); surgical evacuation + ligation (intra-/extraoral) or endovascular embolization for large nonrestrictive hematomas.
- **Prevention**: preoperative CT (implant size/angulation, intrabony vascular canals); adjust drilling angulation buccolingually in atrophic ridges; wide lingual/vestibular flap reflection; accurate hemostasis after periosteal incision; short implants (≤10 mm) recommended in anterior atrophic mandible; observation ≥2 h post-surgery; patient education on warning signs (protruding tongue, hemorrhage, respiratory distress).
- **Implant length**: no need for implants >10 mm or bicortical placement if properly inserted.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Case report (n=2); no incidence data (incidence difficult to ascertain; few cases reported; no RCTs).
- Case 1 details limited (external dental office; no surgical protocol/anatomy info).
- Case 2 lost to follow-up after healing.
- Novel GBR mechanism is inferred (not histologically/angiographically confirmed as ascending mental artery).
- Further expert protocols/guidelines needed for anterior mandible implant surgery.

## 6. Related Work

- Balaguer-Martí et al. systematic review: most frequent cause = lingual cortical plate perforation during drilling → sublingual artery injury.
- One prior case of periosteum tear during lingual flap elevation without cortical interruption.
- Krenkel and Holzner: first report of floor-of-mouth bleeding.
- Antithrombotic-seminar overview references lingual cortical perforation/sublingual-submandibular concavity hazard.

## 7. Glossary

- **Floor of the mouth (FOM, 구강저)**: sublingual region; loose tissue space where hematoma spreads.
- **Sublingual artery (설하동맥)**: lingual artery branch, ~2 mm diameter, courses between genioglossus and mylohyoid, reaches lingual anterior cortex via MLC/LLC.
- **Ascending mental artery (상승성 정신동맥)**: terminal branch of submental artery (facial artery) curving to chin/lip; injured in GBR periosteal release case.
- **Middle/lateral lingual canal (MLC/LLC, 중앙/측방 설측관)**: intrabony canals carrying sublingual artery branches.
- **GBR (Guided Bone Regeneration, 유도골재생)**: bone augmentation requiring periosteal release incisions.
- **Knife-edge ridge (칼날형 치조제)**: horizontally compromised atrophic ridge increasing perforation risk.
