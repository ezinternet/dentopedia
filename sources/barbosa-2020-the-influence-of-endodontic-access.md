## Why Ingested
This laboratory study directly compares three access cavity design strategies (traditional, conservative, and truss) on mandibular molars—extending [[endodontics/anatomy/dioguardi-2024-access-cavity-designs-canal-orifice-scoping|Dioguardi 2024's access cavity scoping review]] with outcome data on instrumentation efficacy, microbial control, and fracture resistance. The findings challenge the clinical adoption of minimally invasive access approaches in molars and inform the [[endodontics/overviews/endodontic-access-cavity-decision-tree|access cavity decision tree overview]].

## Three-line Summary
In vitro study (n=30 extracted mandibular molars) comparing three access cavity designs (Traditional Endodontic Cavity/TEC, Conservative Endodontic Cavity/CEC, Truss Access Cavity/TAC) on canal shaping, bacterial control, filling quality, and fracture resistance.
Conservative access cavities provided no clinical advantage over traditional cavities across instrumentation, microbial reduction, or fracture resistance; conservative methods showed larger unprepared canal surface areas and greater remaining root filling material in the pulp chamber.
Findings suggest traditional straight-line access remains the evidence standard for routine endodontic treatment in molars; conservative/truss designs sacrifice instrumentation completeness without demonstrable benefit.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Direct three-way laboratory comparison of access cavity designs on mandibular molars across instrumentation, microbiological, filling, and biomechanical outcomes
- Quantitative demonstration that conservative/truss cavities leave larger percentages of uninstrumented canal wall (P < 0.05) without functional benefit
- Evidence that remaining root filling material in the pulp chamber is significantly higher in conservative/truss groups (P < 0.05), suggesting cleaning disadvantage
- Confirmation that fracture resistance is independent of access cavity design (P > 0.05), contrary to some conservative-design advocacy

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In vitro laboratory study
- **Sample**: 30 extracted intact mandibular molars
- **Group Assignment**: Three equal groups (n=10 each) matched by micro-CT anatomical features
  - TEC (Traditional Endodontic Cavity) — reference standard
  - CEC (Conservative Endodontic Cavity) — minimally invasive straight-line access
  - TAC (Truss Access Cavity) — conservative design with remaining dentin bridges
- **Bacterial Contamination**: *Enterococcus faecalis* suspension (21-day biofilm incubation)
- **Instrumentation Protocol**: 
  - Reciproc Blue R25, then Reciproc Blue R40
  - 0.5% NaOCl irrigation (8 mL per instrument)
  - Final flush: 2 mL 0.5% NaOCl + 2 mL 17% EDTA + 2 mL 0.5% NaOCl
- **Microbial Sampling Points**: S1 (baseline), S2 (post-R25), S3 (post-R40), S4 (post-final irrigation)
- **Imaging**: Micro-CT scanning pre-instrumentation, post-instrumentation, post-filling
- **Outcome Measures**:
  - Microbial reduction (CFU/mL, negative binomial regression)
  - Unprepared canal surface area (%)
  - Dentin removal volume (%)
  - Canal transportation and centering ability
  - Root filling voids (%)
  - Remaining filling material in pulp chamber (volume)
  - Fracture resistance testing (post-restoration)
- **Statistical Analysis**: Type I negative binomial and beta 0-1 inflation regression models for microbiological data; ANOVA and Tukey tests for instrumentation/filling/fracture (α=0.05)

### Main Findings (Quoted from Abstract/Results)
> "Conservative access cavities did not offer any advantage in comparison with the traditional endodontic cavities in any of the parameters considered. Furthermore, conservative methods were associated with larger percentages of unprepared canal surface area and larger volumes of remaining root filling material within the pulp chamber."

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **Single anatomical site**: Only mandibular molars tested; maxillary molars and single-rooted teeth may have different outcomes
- **Laboratory model**: Extracted teeth, *in vitro* bacterial inoculation, mechanical loading — does not fully replicate the complexity of pulpal tissue, periapical ligament, or dynamic clinical environment
- **Fixed instrumentation protocol**: Results specific to Reciproc Blue reciprocating files; outcomes may differ with other instrumentation systems (ProTaper, WaveOne, continuously rotating instruments)
- **No clinical operator variation**: Standardized access preparation; experienced and inexperienced operators may achieve different results with conservative designs
- **Short-term restoration**: Temporary restoration used for fracture testing; long-term restoration durability and coronal seal not assessed
- **No follow-up**: No investigation of long-term periapical healing, retention of root filling material over time, or fracture/recontamination at distance

## 7. Glossary
- **Reciproc Blue R25 / R40**: Nickel-titanium reciprocating files (sizes 25 and 40) with blue thermomechanical treatment for enhanced flexibility and cyclic fatigue resistance
- **NaOCl**: Sodium hypochlorite irrigant (0.5% concentration in this study) — antimicrobial, tissue-dissolving agent
- **EDTA**: Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (17% concentration in this study) — chelating agent for smear layer removal
- ***Enterococcus faecalis***: Gram-positive facultative anaerobic bacterium; common endodontic pathogen resistant to many intracanal medicaments
- **Micro-CT**: Microcomputed tomography — high-resolution imaging for precise measurement of canal geometry, instrumentation efficacy, and filling voids
- **Canal Transportation**: Lateral deviation of the original canal axis during instrumentation
- **Centering Ability**: Ability of an instrument to remain centered within the canal walls during preparation
- **TEC (Traditional Endodontic Cavity)**: Straight-line access with full removal of the pulp chamber roof; establishes direct line of sight and instrument access to all canal orifices
- **CEC (Conservative Endodontic Cavity)**: Minimally invasive access cavity preserving marginal ridges and some coronal dentin; aims to conserve tooth structure but reduces visibility and instrumentation access
- **TAC (Truss Access Cavity)**: Conservative variant with dentin "trusses" (supportive dentin bridges) maintained between the access opening and pulp chamber; highest conservation of coronal structure but greatest restriction to instrumentation visibility
- **Unprepared Surface Area**: Percentage of original canal wall that remains untouched after instrumentation — higher values indicate incomplete shaping
- **Pulp Chamber Filling Voids**: Air gaps within the root filling material inside the pulp chamber — higher values suggest inadequate obturation or condensation
