## Why Ingested

Batch ingest of dental-tinnitus PMC literature to build `tmj` category coverage of the tinnitus-TMD relationship; the wiki previously held only one narrow cross-sectional paper, [[wiki/tmj/ma-2025-tmd-tinnitus-chewing-side-preference-correlation]], on chewing-side preference and tinnitus laterality. This 2024 narrative review (77 publications, 2009–2024) is the most comprehensive synthesis in the batch and the only one proposing a concrete multidisciplinary diagnostic algorithm, making it the most directly clinically actionable addition.

Source: data retrieved from PubMed / PubMed Central ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm13237346), PMID 39685804, PMCID PMC11642320).

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (SANRA-compliant search of PubMed/Google Scholar/ClinicalKey, 2009–2024, 77 of 30,621 initially retrieved publications qualifying) synthesizing the coexistence of somatosensory tinnitus (ST) and temporomandibular disorders (TMD).

Tinnitus prevalence in TMD patients ranges 2–59% and is ~8x more common than in the general population; among tinnitus patients, TMJ disorders occur at 19% frequency overall and 36% in high-severity tinnitus; the review proposes a multidisciplinary diagnostic algorithm and reports tinnitus improvement in 43–86% of cases after TMD therapy.

Clinicians should consider TMD as an occult cause of otologic symptoms when ENT/audiological workup is unremarkable, and interdisciplinary cooperation (otolaryngologist, audiologist, neurologist, psychiatrist, dentist, physiotherapist) is recommended — but the review itself notes causality between TMD and tinnitus remains unproven (association, not confirmed mechanism).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Comprehensive synthesis of the anatomical, physiological, and clinical evidence linking somatosensory tinnitus (ST) and TMD, covering 77 publications spanning 2009–2024.
- Consolidates six mechanistic theories linking TMD and otologic/tinnitus symptoms: joint phylogenetic/ontogenetic development, topographical proximity, joint sensory innervation, joint motor innervation, neuromodulation, and stress.
- Compiles the diagnostic criteria strongly suggestive of ST (tinnitus modulation by head/jaw/neck/eye movement or myofascial trigger-point pressure; concurrent cervical/head/shoulder pain; trauma-preceded onset; TMD; bruxism/clenching/dental disease).
- Proposes a **multidisciplinary diagnostic algorithm** for tinnitus/TMD co-management — the paper's central clinically actionable output.
- Aggregates prevalence and treatment-outcome statistics (tinnitus 8x more common in TMD, 19–36% TMJ-disorder frequency in tinnitus patients, 43–86% tinnitus improvement after TMD therapy).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Narrative review complying with SANRA (Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles) recommendations.
- **Search**: PubMed, Google Scholar, ClinicalKey databases; keyword combinations (tinnitus AND TMD; tinnitus AND stress/etiology/diagnosis; TMD AND diagnosis/stress/etiology; somatosensory tinnitus AND TMD/etiology/diagnosis; temporomandibular dysfunction; stress; bruxism), extended via reference lists and selected books.
- **Screening**: 30,621 articles initially found → 2,356 after duplicate removal → 106 assessed for eligibility → 50 rejected for lack of relevance → **77 publications** qualified (PRISMA flow diagram in the original). Both opinion and research papers, in Polish and English, years 2009–2024.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Prevalence**: tinnitus prevalence in the general population 5.1–42.7% (most-cited range 10–15%); tinnitus prevalence in TMD patients 2–59%; tinnitus reported ~8x more common in TMD sufferers vs. general population.
- **Reciprocal frequency**: among tinnitus patients, TMJ disorders present at 19% frequency overall, rising to 36% in patients with high-severity tinnitus.
- **Symptom co-occurrence**: in patients with concomitant TMD and oto-vestibular disorders, ear pain occurred in ~50% and tinnitus in 45.5% (one study) vs. 52.1% tinnitus prevalence (Porto de Toledo et al.); masticatory muscle pain was the most common comorbid TMD finding among tinnitus patients, with acoustic TMJ symptoms/muscle fatigue in ~33%.
- **Laterality**: ipsilateral localization of unilateral TMD and unilateral otologic symptoms was significantly more common than contralateral; tinnitus patients are 3x more likely to report myofascial pain than non-tinnitus patients; correlation between tinnitus laterality and body-pain side ≈54%.
- **Six linking-theory framework**: (1) joint phylogenetic/ontogenetic development (shared first-pharyngeal-arch origin of medial pterygoid, tensor veli palatini, tensor tympani); (2) topographical proximity (discomalleolar ligament, condylar pressure on auriculotemporal nerve, chorda tympani injury from posterior condylar displacement); (3) joint sensory innervation (trigeminal, facial, glossopharyngeal, vagus nerves, C2–C3, sympathetic carotid plexus); (4) joint motor innervation (trigeminal mandibular branch co-innervates masseter and tensor tympani); (5) neuromodulation/cross-modal plasticity in the dorsal cochlear nucleus as a multi-sensory integration site; (6) stress as a shared predisposing/aggravating factor for both TMD and tinnitus.
- **Treatment outcomes**: multimodal therapy (TMJ/cervical/masticatory-muscle exercises, manual therapy, physical therapy, acrylic splint, patient education) is the most effective conservative approach; cervico-mandibular manual therapy improved physical, psychological, and tinnitus/TMD-related impairment measures; tinnitus resolution or improvement reported in **43–86%** of cases after TMD therapy.
- **Multidisciplinary diagnostic algorithm** (proposed): patients presenting with audiovestibular symptoms (ear pain, tinnitus, dizziness, sudden hearing impairment) first undergo otolaryngological + audiological evaluation and imaging; if these do not explain the symptoms, evaluation extends to the stomatognathic system (dentist), cervical spine/musculoskeletal assessment (physiotherapist), and psychological/psychiatric screening (given stress's shared etiological role); presence of ST-suggestive criteria (modulation by jaw/neck/head/eye movement, myofascial trigger points, TMD/bruxism, trauma-preceded onset, symmetric audiometry with asymmetric tinnitus) route the patient into a coordinated otolaryngologist–audiologist–neurologist–psychiatrist–dentist–physiotherapist care team; discordant laterality between unilateral TMD and unilateral tinnitus argues against a TMD-tinnitus link in that individual.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative (not systematic) review — no formal risk-of-bias/quality appraisal of included studies beyond SANRA adherence.
- Underlying primary literature is heterogeneous in diagnostic criteria for both ST and TMD, limiting pooled quantitative synthesis (hence the wide 2–59% prevalence range).
- Authors explicitly state it remains unclear whether TMD and tinnitus coexist independently, are causally related, or share a common upstream driver (e.g., stress) — causality unresolved.
- Relatively few studies address ST and TMD simultaneously; call for more homogeneous diagnostic criteria and better-designed interdisciplinary studies.
- Authors note dentists and otolaryngologists often lack interprofessional awareness/training in this cross-specialty area, itself an implementation barrier to the proposed algorithm.

## 6. Related Work

- Costen (1934): first clinical description linking TMJ/masticatory-system disorders to ear symptoms.
- Michiels et al. (2017): defined three criteria groups strongly suggestive of ST with somatosensory-system modulation.
- Porto de Toledo et al.: reported tinnitus prevalence of 52.1% among a TMD-referred population (cited within this review as a comparator prevalence figure).
- International Consensus on Assessment of Bruxism Status (2018): sleep vs. awake bruxism definitions, cited as a TMD-development risk factor.
- DC/TMD (Diagnostic Criteria for TMD): referenced framework for classifying TMD into pain-related, intra-articular, and complex-symptom categories.

## 7. Glossary

- **Somatosensory (somatic) tinnitus (ST)**: subjective tinnitus subtype whose onset/perception can be triggered or modulated by somatic maneuvers or pressure in the temporomandibular or cervical-spine region, implicating non-otologic (somatosensory) input to the auditory pathway.
- **Dorsal cochlear nucleus (DCN)**: brainstem auditory-pathway nucleus proposed as the site of multi-sensory (auditory + somatosensory) integration underlying ST.
- **Tinnitus modulation**: temporary change in tinnitus frequency, intensity, or location provoked by head/neck/jaw/eye movement, somatic maneuvers, or myofascial trigger-point pressure.
- **TMJD / MPD / CSD**: the review's three-way TMD subclassification — temporomandibular joint disorder, myofascial pain disorder, cervical spine dysfunction.
- **Cross-modal plasticity**: compensatory reorganization in which loss of sensory input in one modality (e.g., auditory) is partly filled by input from another modality (e.g., somatosensory), proposed as a ST mechanism.
- **SANRA**: Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles — quality framework this review's methodology follows.
