## Three-line Summary
Systematic review + meta-analysis of the literature on corticosteroid (CS) administration in oral surgery (dental extraction) and orthognathic surgery, pooling clinical trials on edema, pain (analgesia), infection risk, and neuroregeneration, plus a non-pooled narrative review of other side effects.

In oral surgery, CS produced a significant decrease in edema and pain (both P<.0001) with no significant increase in infection risk (relative risk 1.0041, P=.89); local methylprednisolone ≥25mg was expected to yield significant edema reduction. In orthognathic surgery, IV methylprednisolone ≥85mg was sufficient for significant edema reduction, and several trials suggested a neuroregeneration effect, though this could not be statistically tested.

Only a minimal risk of chronic adrenal suppression was seen in oral surgery, versus an elevated risk of avascular osteonecrosis, steroid-induced psychosis, and adrenal suppression in orthognathic surgery, with no reports of decreased wound healing in either setting — but this page is built entirely from the structured abstract (paywalled full text), so pooled effect sizes, confidence intervals, heterogeneity statistics, and the included-study list are not available here.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Pools multiple clinical trials to give a statistically significant, quantified verdict (P<.0001) on CS reducing both edema and pain after oral surgery — moving the question from "individual trials suggest" to "meta-analytically confirmed."
- Establishes rough dose thresholds from the pooled literature: local methylprednisolone ≥25mg (oral surgery edema) and IV methylprednisolone ≥85mg (orthognathic surgery edema).
- Directly tests and quantifies the infection-risk counterargument against CS use (RR 1.0041, P=.89) — addressing the most common clinical objection to perioperative steroid use with a pooled null result rather than a single trial's underpowered null.
- Extends the evidence base beyond third-molar extraction into orthognathic (orthopedic jaw) surgery, including a preliminary (non-statistical) signal for a neuroregeneration benefit and a distinct, more severe side-effect profile (avascular osteonecrosis, steroid psychosis).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review of the literature + meta-analysis (SR+MA) for the edema/pain/infection outcomes; a non-pooled "simple review" for the broader side-effect profile.
- **Databases**: not stated in the retrieved abstract (search strategy is in the paywalled body).
- **n**: not stated in the retrieved abstract — number of included trials/patients is in the paywalled body/tables, not the structured abstract.
- **Outcomes**: primary predictor = CS administration (yes/no, and dose where reported); outcome variables = edema, pain, infection (all meta-analyzed); neuroregeneration and other adverse effects (narrative only, not pooled).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Oral surgery, edema**: most trials showed a significant decrease after CS, P<.0001; local methylprednisolone ≥25mg injection expected to produce a significant decrease.
- **Oral surgery, pain**: several trials showed a significant decrease after CS, P<.0001.
- **Oral surgery, infection**: CS associated with a slightly higher relative risk (RR=1.0041) but not statistically significant (P=.89) — authors conclude CS can be given with no increased infection risk.
- **Orthognathic surgery, edema**: IV methylprednisolone ≥85mg appeared sufficient for a significant decrease.
- **Orthognathic surgery, neuroregeneration**: several trials pointed toward a benefit, but "no statistical analysis could be performed" (authors' own caveat — this is a qualitative signal, not a pooled effect).
- **Side effects, oral surgery**: minimal risk of chronic adrenal suppression.
- **Side effects, orthognathic surgery**: elevated risk of avascular osteonecrosis, steroid-induced psychosis, and adrenal suppression.
- **Wound healing**: no reports of decreased healing in either surgical context.
- No pooled effect-size point estimates, 95% confidence intervals, heterogeneity (I²/Q) statistics, forest plots, or a PRISMA-style included-study count were retrievable — these live in the paywalled full text/tables, not the structured abstract this page is built from.

## 6. Related Work
- tamgadge-2025 (RCT): dexamethasone 4mg IM preoperative vs placebo for third-molar surgery pain/swelling/trismus — an independent, later confirmation of this paper's oral-surgery edema/pain conclusion, at the single-trial level.
- bakri-2024 (RCT): prednisolone single-dose vs 3-day taper for third-molar surgery — tests a dosing-*schedule* question this SR+MA did not address (it only reports whether/how-much CS helps and rough dose thresholds, not single-vs-tapered administration).
- satpathi-2024 (narrative review): general corticosteroid-in-dentistry review whose post-extraction-edema dosing claim aligns with this paper's pooled finding.

## 7. Glossary
- **CS**: Corticosteroid — the paper's own abbreviation, used throughout the abstract for the drug class under study.
- **Methylprednisolone**: intermediate-acting synthetic glucocorticoid; the only named corticosteroid in the retrieved abstract, with dose thresholds reported for local injection (oral surgery) and IV administration (orthognathic surgery).
- **Avascular osteonecrosis (AVN)**: bone death from disrupted blood supply; reported here as an elevated-risk side effect specific to the orthognathic-surgery (not oral-surgery) arm.
- **Neuroregeneration**: nerve recovery/regrowth; relevant to orthognathic surgery's known risk of neurosensory disturbance (e.g., after sagittal split osteotomy) — this paper reports a suggestive but statistically untested signal.
- **Relative risk (RR)**: ratio of an event's probability between two groups; here, RR=1.0041 for infection (CS vs. no CS), a negligible and non-significant (P=.89) difference.
