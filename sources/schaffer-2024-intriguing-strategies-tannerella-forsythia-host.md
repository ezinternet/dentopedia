## Three-line Summary

*Tannerella forsythia*, the least-studied red-complex member, fields a molecular arsenal — S-layer O-glycosylation, BspA, NanH sialidase, KLIKK proteases, OMVs, and LPS — that modulates innate immune defense without achieving keystone-pathogen equivalence to *P. gingivalis*. Its defining survival adaptation is MurNAc auxotrophy, enforcing biofilm co-dependency. The S-layer glycosylation pattern is the pivotal immunological switch: native nonasaccharide enables persistence; truncation triggers Th17-mediated clearance and reduced bone loss.

## Core Argument

*Tannerella forsythia* expresses several unique virulence factors that modulate host immune defense. Its current status is not equivalent to *Porphyromonas gingivalis* as a keystone pathogen — the evidence for active host-microbiome dysbiosis manipulation is incomplete — but its molecular toolkit (T9SS, O-glycosylation, S-layer, BspA, sialidase, proteases, OMVs, LPS) collectively dampens innate immunity and enables persistence in the subgingival niche.

## Survival Strategies and Cellular Infrastructure

- *T. forsythia* and *P. gingivalis* both belong to the phylum *Bacteroidota* and are late colonizers of oral biofilm
- Both use the **Type 9 Protein Secretion System (T9SS)** to export virulence factors with a C-terminal structural targeting domain across the outer membrane
- Both extensively **O-glycosylate** cell surface proteins with a species-specific glycan; glycosylation facilitates host persistence likely through molecular mimicry of host sialic acid via bacterial nonulosonic acids
- Deletion of T9SS signal peptidase PorU in *T. forsythia* reduced pro-inflammatory mediator production by macrophages and gingival fibroblasts upon infection
- Unique trait: *T. forsythia* is auxotrophic for **N-acetylmuramic acid (MurNAc)** — it cannot synthesize this essential cell wall sugar and must scavenge it from cohabiting bacteria; this dependency makes it resistant to fosfomycin (no MurAB enzymes)

## Virulence Factors

### Sialidase (NanH)
- *T. forsythia* encodes a dedicated **sialic acid utilization operon** with sialidase NanH, transporter NanT, and esterase NanS
- NanH cleaves terminal sialic acid from host glycoproteins (mucins), exposing hidden epitopes on epithelial cells and enhancing attachment
- NanT (sialic acid transporter) loss diminishes attachment and survival during KB cell infection
- NanS collaborates with *P. gingivalis* sialidase to acquire sialic acid from diacetylated substrates, demonstrating red-complex interdependency

### Proteases (KLIKK family, miropin, karylisin/mirolysin)
- PrtH proteases: associated with attachment loss
- Trypsin-like cysteine protease: arginine- and citrulline-specific activities
- **KLIKK proteases**: secretory, target collagen, gelatine, elastin, casein; transcripts detected in nearly all GCF samples where *T. forsythia* was present
- **Miropin**: deactivates neutrophil elastases and cathepsin G → impairs antibacterial PMN function
- **Karylisin**: inhibits complement activation at multiple stages; degrades cathelicidin (with mirolysin)
- Net effect: broader protein substrate range but less potent degradation of host cytokines compared to *P. gingivalis* gingipains

### S-layer (TfsA/TfsB)
- *T. forsythia* is covered by a 2D-crystalline S-layer formed by self-assembly of TfsA and TfsB proteins
- Each protein is multiply modified by a **nonasaccharide** at the phylum-wide D(S/T)(A/I/L/M/T/V/S/C/G/F/N/E/Q/D/P) motif
- S-layer knockout studies:
  - S-layer-deficient mutant showed **earlier** onset of pro-inflammatory mediator production in macrophages and gingival fibroblasts
  - Induced monocyte chemoattractant protein (MCP) and GM-CSF production by oral epithelial cells
  - Exhibited significantly reduced adherence to human gingival epithelial cells
- Glycosylation pattern of S-layer is critical: the native nonasaccharide ensures persistence; truncated glycosylation (Δ mutant lacking trisaccharide branch with nonulosonic acid) elicited robust **Th17 response** and reduced periodontal bone loss in mice
- Immune priming with the Th17-biasing strain triggered a productive neutrophil response, limiting persistence and alveolar bone loss
- Glycosylated S-layer is recognized by macrophage-inducible C-type lectin receptors (primarily in myeloid cells)
- In a subgingival multispecies biofilm model, the glycosylated S-layer governs *T. forsythia* positioning within the biofilm and co-localization with *P. gingivalis*

### BspA (Bacterial Surface Protein A)
- Glycosylated leucine-rich repeat (LRR) cell surface antigen
- Triggers cytokine production in macrophages and dendritic cells via **CD14/TLR2-dependent** mechanisms; TLR2 activation mediated through sequence motif GC(S/T)GLXSIT
- BspA-deficient mutant caused significantly less alveolar bone loss in mouse gavage model
- Bone loss linked to Th2 response activation and reduced in TLR2-knockout animals
- BspA genotype prevalence elevated in chronic and aggressive periodontitis patients
- *T. forsythia* and BspA induce foam cell formation in THP-1 macrophages and promote atherosclerotic lesions in ApoE-knockout mice → systemic health implications

### Outer Membrane Vesicles (OMVs)
- Induce TNF-α and IL-8 in U937 macrophages; IL-6, IL-8, and MCP-1 in hPDL mesenchymal stromal cells (concentration-dependent)
- Host cell response to OMVs is comparable to or stronger than whole-bacterium infection
- Mediated through TLR2 activation
- OMV cargo proteins are predictably O-glycosylated, linking glycosylation to virulence packaging

### Lipopolysaccharide (LPS)
- Rough-type LPS; induces inflammatory response in macrophages (concentration-dependent)
- *T. forsythia* LPS stimulates cytokine production in whole blood from periodontitis patients
- Co-stimulation with *P. gingivalis* or *T. denticola* LPS synergistically elevates IL-1β and TNF-α

## Cell-Specific Host Responses

### Epithelial Cells
- *T. forsythia* invades oral epithelial cells via PI3K activation by BspA
- BspA (surface-associated and secreted) induces IL-8 via TLR2
- NanH sialidase mediates attachment and invasion of H357 squamous cell carcinoma line
- In vivo (ex vivo): invasion of buccal epithelium demonstrated
- No effect on apoptosis in OKF6/hTERT-2 cells (contrast: *P. gingivalis* and some commensals induce apoptosis)
- HOK-16B keratinocytes: IL-24 production via ROS; IL-1α via NLRP10; IL-1Rα, IL-8, VEGF production
- *T. forsythia* slightly inhibits epithelial cell migration (weaker than *P. gingivalis* and *T. denticola*)

### PMNs (Polymorphonuclear Leukocytes)
- PMNs can phagocytose non-opsonized *T. forsythia* → ROS production
- *T. forsythia* adheres to and invades dental follicle mesenchymal stem cells → diminishes PMN chemotaxis, phagocytic activity, and NET formation
- Unlike *P. gingivalis*, subcutaneous infection of mice with *T. forsythia* does not inhibit neutrophil migration to the infected site
- Clinical study: negative correlation between *T. forsythia* prevalence/levels in subgingival plaque and PMN phagocytosis and superoxide anion production

### Macrophages
- *T. forsythia*, OMVs, and LPS elicit IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-8 in human U937 macrophages
- Murine macrophages: dose-dependent IL-6, MCP-1, IL-23 production
- Co-infection with *P. gingivalis* synergistically enhances IL-6 (not other cytokines)
- *T. forsythia* induces macrophage apoptosis via caspase-1 activation + release of fibronectin and HSP60
- Contrast with *P. gingivalis*: *T. forsythia* infection increases both gene expression AND protein content; *P. gingivalis* increases gene expression but with low cytokine protein levels (gingipain proteases degrade host proteins more efficiently)

### Mesenchymal Stromal Cells (MSCs)
- *T. forsythia* induces IL-6, IL-8, MCP-1 gene and protein expression in gingival and PDL-derived MSCs
- Similar effects with *T. forsythia* LPS and OMVs

## Comparison with *P. gingivalis*

| Feature | *T. forsythia* | *P. gingivalis* |
|---|---|---|
| Keystone status | Not established | Established (low-abundance manipulator) |
| Protease cytokine degradation | Limited | High (gingipain-mediated "chemokine paralysis") |
| Complement evasion | Karylisin inhibits multiple stages | Multiple complement evasion strategies |
| Cathelicidin degradation | Mirolysin + karylisin | Gingipains |
| IL-8 manipulation | BspA-TLR2 upregulation | Local chemokine paralysis (downregulation) |
| Neutrophil inhibition | Indirect (via dental follicle MSCs) | Direct (PMN migration block) |
| Mouse infection model | Ligature-based limited; *P. gingivalis* used more | Extensively studied |
| MurNAc biosynthesis | Auxotrophic (dependent on cohabitors) | Independent |

## Biofilm Interplay
- *T. forsythia* scavenges NOD-2 ligands secreted by *P. gingivalis* → diminishes NOD-2-mediated activation of oral epithelial cells
- *T. forsythia* inhibits *P. gingivalis* invasion into oral epithelial cells
- *T. forsythia* synergistically augments alveolar bone loss within experimental periodontitis model (ligature + *P. gingivalis* + *T. forsythia*)
- Virulence influenced by ecological conditions: temperature, pH, iron availability; clinical isolates differ from ATCC 43037 in IP-10 activation

## Limitations and Gaps
- Most knowledge from monoculture or mouse infection models; subgingival plaque is polymicrobial
- Limited *T. forsythia*-specific mouse periodontitis models
- Cannot conclusively classify *T. forsythia* as a keystone pathogen analogous to *P. gingivalis*
- Strain-level variation (clinical isolates vs. ATCC 43037) not well characterized
