## One-line Summary
RCT (COIR 2026): 62 patients, SIP permanent molars; full pulpotomy ± adjunctive cryotherapy; 24-month success 92.9% vs 92.3% (per-protocol) — no long-term benefit from cryotherapy.

## 한줄요약

## 2. Key Contributions
- 24-month RCT follow-up for full pulpotomy in SIP — longer than most pulpotomy VPT RCTs
- Tests adjunctive intracanal cryotherapy alongside pulpotomy (comparing the two strategies)
- Establishes high 24-month success (~92%) for full pulpotomy in SIP permanent molars
- Cryotherapy adds no long-term benefit to pulpotomy success in SIP

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: RCT (parallel arm)
- Population: 62 patients, symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP), permanent molars
- Intervention: Full pulpotomy + adjunctive cryotherapy
- Control: Full pulpotomy alone
- Follow-up: 24 months
- Outcome: Clinical and radiographic success (per-protocol analysis)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- 24-month success: 92.9% (cryotherapy group) vs 92.3% (no cryotherapy) — PP analysis
- No statistically significant difference between groups
- Both groups demonstrate high success rates for full pulpotomy in SIP at 24 months

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Single center; operator-blinded assessment may be challenging
- 24-month follow-up still limited for VPT longevity assessment; 5-year data needed
- Small sample per group (n≈31/arm); powered for equivalence, not superiority

## 6. Related Work
- Full pulpotomy for SIP increasingly supported (see mtalsi-2026 umbrella review)
- Cryotherapy as adjunct has evidence in pain reduction (huang-2026) but not in VPT success

## 7. Glossary
- SIP: Symptomatic Irreversible Pulpitis
- Full pulpotomy: complete coronal pulp removal (vs partial/shallow)
- PP (per-protocol) analysis: success assessed only in patients who completed per-protocol treatment
