## Three-line Summary

Comprehensive narrative review (Bioactive Materials 33:129-156, 2024; CC BY-NC-ND) covering biomaterial scaffold design for maxillofacial bone tissue engineering, organized into physical properties (architecture: pore geometry, pore size, interconnectivity, mechanical strength) and biological functionalization (growth factors — BMP-2, VEGF, FGF; stem cells — BMSCs, ADSCs, iPSCs; surface modification — bioceramics, nanoparticles).

Tables 1–3 compile clinical trial evidence for alveolar bone defects (including Ossix Volumax for ridge augmentation), jawbone defects, and periapical defects, providing a practical evidence map with study type, scaffold material, defect size, and outcome.

The review identifies directional vascularization and patient-specific manufacturing (3D printing, biofabrication) as the two principal unresolved challenges for maxillofacial BTE translation.

## 2. Scaffold Physical Design Framework

### 2a. Architecture Parameters

| Parameter | Clinical Relevance | Range cited |
|---|---|---|
| Pore size | Cell infiltration, vascularization | 200–500 µm optimal |
| Interconnectivity | Nutrient/waste exchange | >50% porosity recommended |
| Surface roughness | Cell adhesion, protein adsorption | nano-scale preferred |
| Mechanical strength | Load-bearing sites | Must match cortical (GPa) or cancellous (~1-5 MPa) |

### 2b. Geometric Architecture Types

- **Random porous** (conventional freeze-dry, electrospun): isotropic pores, poor directional control
- **Gradient porous** (3D-printed, SLA): mimics bone-marrow density transition
- **Fiber/membrane architectures** (collagen, PLGA): suitable for membranes, ridge augmentation
- **Tubular/channel scaffolds**: designed for directed vascularization

### 2c. Mechanical Considerations

| Bone type | Compressive modulus | Scaffold target |
|---|---|---|
| Cortical | 7–30 GPa | ~1–10 GPa (ceramics) |
| Cancellous | 0.02–0.5 GPa | hydrogel + ceramic composite |
| Soft tissue adjacent | Low | collagen scaffolds (Ossix type) |

## 3. Biological Functionalization

### 3a. Growth Factors

| Factor | Role | Delivery method cited |
|---|---|---|
| BMP-2 (Bone Morphogenetic Protein-2) | Osteoinduction | Collagen sponge, microspheres, 3D-printed scaffold |
| VEGF (Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor) | Vascularization | Sequential release systems |
| FGF (Fibroblast Growth Factor) | Proliferation, angiogenesis | Heparin-loaded scaffold |
| PDGF | Early healing, bone formation | Commercial membrane coating |

### 3b. Stem Cell Types

| Cell type | Source | Application |
|---|---|---|
| BMSCs (Bone Marrow Stromal Cells) | Iliac crest | Gold standard; limited availability |
| ADSCs (Adipose-Derived Stem Cells) | Liposuction | More accessible; comparable osteogenic potential |
| iPSCs (Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells) | Skin/blood | Future direction; immunogenic risk |
| PDL stem cells | Extracted tooth | Locally available; promising for alveolar bone |

### 3c. Surface Modification

- **Bioceramics**: hydroxyapatite (HA) coating → enhanced osteoconductivity
- **Nanoparticles**: silver (antimicrobial), zinc oxide (angiogenic)
- **Bioactive glass**: ionic dissolution → local BMP stimulation

## 4. Clinical Evidence Tables (Summary from Paper)

### Table 1 — Alveolar Bone Defects (selected entries relevant to dentistry)

| Scaffold material | Study type | Defect | Key finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ossix Volumax (collagen) | Case series | Ridge augmentation | Volume increase; simplified GBR |
| rhBMP-2/ACS | RCT | Alveolar bone | Superior bone fill vs. autograft |
| DBBM + collagen membrane | RCT | Socket preservation | Less ridge resorption vs. no treatment |
| PLGA microspheres | Pilot | Periodontal | Sustained release BMP-2; bone gain |
| HA/TCP composite | RCT | Ridge augmentation | Comparable to autograft |

### Table 2 — Jaw Bone Defects

Covers reconstruction after resection (tumor, cyst), trauma, congenital. Major scaffold types: Ti mesh + bone graft, vascularized fibula flap, printed HA scaffolds. Main metric: volumetric defect fill, functional occlusion.

### Table 3 — Periapical Defects

Covers apical lesions, apicoectomy defects. Primarily: PRF, collagen cones, HA putty. Short follow-up (3–6 months most studies).

## 5. Key Unresolved Challenges (per review)

1. **Vascularization**: scaffold ossification fails in large defects (>1 cm) without pre-vascularization; current solutions (VEGF co-delivery, channeled scaffolds) insufficient for clinical translation
2. **Patient-specific manufacturing**: 3D-printed scaffolds need CT/CBCT-to-print pipeline; regulatory pathway immature
3. **Long-term degradation matching**: scaffold should degrade at the same rate as new bone forms; mismatch leads to early collapse or fibrous encapsulation
4. **Immune response**: macrophage polarization (M1→M2) critical for scaffold integration; not routinely assessed in trials

## 6. Limitations of Review

- Narrative (non-systematic) — selection bias in included studies
- Heterogeneous outcome measures across trials — no meta-analysis possible
- Focus on maxillofacial; excludes periodontal scaffold literature largely
- Several trials small n (<20 patients)
