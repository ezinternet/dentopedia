## Why Ingested

Classic review paper directly cited as evidence for the keratinized vs non-keratinized barrier differences in [[overviews/oral-mucosal-epithelial-turnover-overview]]. Squier 1991 is the canonical reference for the **lipid intercellular permeability barrier** mechanism that accounts for why non-keratinized sites (buccal, floor of mouth) have higher permeability and faster turnover, while keratinized sites (gingiva, hard palate) are least permeable.

## One-line Summary

Narrative review explaining that oral mucosa shows site-specific regional variation in permeability, with keratinized gingiva and hard palate being least permeable due to a lipid-type intercellular barrier, and non-keratinized lining mucosa being most permeable.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Establishes that oral mucosa is **not uniformly permeable** — contra the common assumption that it acts like gut epithelium
- Demonstrates that keratinized areas (gingiva, hard palate) have the **lowest permeability** due to lipid composition of the intercellular barrier in superficial epithelial layers
- Non-keratinized lining mucosa (buccal, floor of mouth, soft palate) is **most permeable** — directly correlates with its faster turnover rate
- Discusses how regional permeability differences relate to **mucosal disease susceptibility** (e.g., ulceration patterns)
- Highlights the clinical utility of non-keratinized high-permeability sites for **transmucosal drug delivery**

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

From abstract:
- Keratinized (gingiva, hard palate) = **least permeable**
- Non-keratinized lining (buccal, floor of mouth) = **most permeable**
- Permeability variation reflects differences in **lipid types in the intercellular barrier** of superficial epithelial layers
- Regional variation linked to differences in prevalence of certain mucosal diseases
- Non-keratinized sites suitable for systemic drug delivery via transmucosal route

## 7. Glossary

- **Intercellular permeability barrier**: lipid-rich extracellular spaces in superficial epithelium layers controlling molecular passage
- **Lining mucosa** (피복점막): non-keratinized, flexible mucosa (buccal, floor of mouth, soft palate, alveolar mucosa)
- **Masticatory mucosa** (저작점막): keratinized, attached mucosa (attached gingiva, hard palate)
