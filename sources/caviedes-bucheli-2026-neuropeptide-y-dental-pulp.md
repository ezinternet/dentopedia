## Why Ingested
PRISMA-guided systematic review (92 studies) establishing NPY as the primary counter-regulatory neuropeptide in dental pulp, directly extending [[endodontics/anatomy/caviedes-bucheli-2008-neuropeptides-dental-pulp-silent]] with receptor-level mechanistic detail (Y1/Y2 Gi/Go signalling) and stage-dependent caries expression data unavailable in the 2008 narrative review.

## Three-line Summary
PRISMA-guided narrative synthesis of 92 studies on NPY biology in dental pulp, spanning in vitro, in vivo (rat/cat), ex vivo human tissue, and clinical observations from inception to February 2026.
NPY, predominantly released from sympathetic perivascular fibres, acts via Gi/Go-coupled Y1/Y2 receptors to suppress SP/CGRP-driven vasodilation, reduce intrapulpal pressure, inhibit TRPV1-mediated nociception, and stimulate tertiary dentinogenesis via BMP-2/fibroblast crosstalk; NPY and Y1 expression peak in mild-to-moderate caries and collapse in advanced lesions with sympathetic fibre loss.
The excitatory (SP/CGRP) ↔ inhibitory (NPY) balance governs pulp survival: adequate NPY tone prevents irreversible pulpitis, but no RCTs exist on NPY-targeted interventions — current evidence is largely immunohistochemical without functional validation in human clinical models.

## 2. Key Contributions
1. **NPY as master homeostatic regulator**: Positions NPY — not merely a vasoconstrictor — as the primary intrinsic anti-excitatory axis in dental pulp, operating via Gi/Go signalling that collectively suppresses SP/CGRP-driven neurogenic inflammation.
2. **Receptor-resolved mechanism (Y1 vs Y2)**: Y1 (postsynaptic, vascular smooth muscle + nociceptors) → vasoconstriction + direct antinociception via TRPV1 inhibition + CGRP suppression. Y2 (presynaptic) → SP/CGRP spillover brake (inferred from other tissues; Y2 protein not yet confirmed in human pulp tissue).
3. **Stage-dependent caries model**: NPY and Y1 expression peak in mild-to-moderate caries (protective programme), then collapse in advanced lesions as sympathetic fibres are destroyed — explaining why late-stage pulpitis becomes self-perpetuating.
4. **Reparative NPY signalling**: NPY/Y1 on pulpal fibroblasts upregulates BMP-2 → odontoblast differentiation + tertiary dentinogenesis + angiogenesis, linking sympathetic neuropeptide to regenerative capacity.
5. **Dentinal hypersensitivity framing**: Proposes NPY release during restorative procedures as an endogenous analgesic mechanism limiting procedure-associated pain, challenging the view that restorative stimuli elicit only pro-nociceptive responses.

## 3. Methodology
- **Design**: PRISMA-guided narrative synthesis (not meta-analysis due to study heterogeneity)
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science, Scopus (inception → February 2026); supplemented by grey literature, Google Scholar, ClinicalTrials.gov
- **Inclusion**: Experimental (in vitro/in vivo/ex vivo), human and animal tissue, reviews — all providing molecular/cellular/functional data on NPY, Y1/Y2, or associated neuropeptides (SP, CGRP, VIP) in pulp inflammation/pain
- **Exclusion**: Case reports, editorials, studies lacking molecular/cellular NPY data, non-neurogenic dentinal hypersensitivity studies
- **Selection**: Two independent reviewers; third reviewer for discrepancies
- **n included**: 92 studies from 140 initially retrieved

## 4. Key Results

### NPY Receptor Pharmacology in Pulp
| Receptor | Location | G protein | cAMP | Key function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Y1 | VSM, sensory neurons, fibroblasts, odontoblasts | Gi/Go | ↓↓ | Vasoconstriction, analgesia, dentinogenesis |
| Y2 | Presynaptic (inferred from other tissues) | Gi/Go | ↓↓ | Limits SP/CGRP release |
| Y5 | Pulp (limited data) | Gi/Go | ↓ | Possible adjunct role |

### Contrast with Excitatory Neuropeptides
- SP (NK1/Gq → ↑IP3/Ca²⁺) → vasodilation, plasma extravasation, mast-cell degranulation
- CGRP (CALCRL/RAMP1/Gs → ↑↑↑cAMP) → potent vasodilation, TRPV1 phosphorylation/sensitisation
- NPY (Y1-Y2/Gi/Go → ↓cAMP, closes presynaptic VGCC) → opposes all above

### Stage-Dependent NPY/Y1 in Caries
- **Healthy pulp**: Baseline NPY localised to perivascular fibres; SP > NPY concentration
- **Mild-moderate caries**: NPY and Y1 expression highest; fibres extend into odontoblastic layer and predentine; BMP-2-mediated tertiary dentinogenesis active
- **Advanced caries**: Sympathetic fibre destruction → NPY collapse → unrestrained SP/CGRP → irreversible pulpitis

### Antinociceptive Mechanisms
- Y1 activation inhibits TRPV1-expressing nociceptors → raises activation threshold
- NPY suppresses CGRP release from sensory terminals → limits peripheral sensitisation
- NPY-mediated vasoconstriction reduces intrapulpal pressure → reduces mechanical nociceptor loading
- NPY present at low picomolar levels, increases with inflammation/trauma

### VIP Counterpoint
- VIP (parasympathetic, vasodilatory) expression modestly increases in mild-moderate caries — compensatory mechanism fine-tuning microcirculation against NPY vasoconstriction; NPY expression is ~2× VIP in healthy pulp reflecting sympathetic > parasympathetic fibre density

## 5. Limitations
1. **Descriptive evidence base**: Majority of evidence is immunohistochemical — confirms spatial expression but not receptor activation or downstream signalling efficacy
2. **Limited human in vivo data**: Mechanistic data largely from rat/cat models with significant species differences (continuous eruption, pulpal anatomy, receptor profiles)
3. **No RCTs**: Zero randomised controlled trials on NPY-based interventions in dentistry
4. **Y2 receptor gap**: No confirmed Y2 protein expression in human dental pulp tissue; Y2 role is inferred from other neurogenic inflammation models
5. **Functional causality unproven**: NPY quantification (picomolar range) is technically challenging; real-time dynamics during procedures remain unstudied

## 7. Glossary
- **NPY (Neuropeptide Y, 뉴로펩타이드 Y)**: 36-amino-acid sympathetic neuropeptide co-released with noradrenaline from perivascular fibres; primary endogenous vasoconstrictor and anti-inflammatory modulator in dental pulp
- **Y1 receptor**: Postsynaptic Gi/Go-coupled NPY receptor on vascular smooth muscle, sensory neurons, fibroblasts, and odontoblasts; mediates vasoconstriction, antinociception, and reparative dentinogenesis
- **Y2 receptor**: Presynaptic Gi/Go-coupled NPY autoreceptor/heteroreceptor; limits SP/CGRP release from sensory terminals (inferred role in pulp)
- **SP (Substance P, 물질 P)**: Pro-inflammatory sensory neuropeptide; NK1/Gq → plasma extravasation, mast-cell degranulation
- **CGRP (Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide, 칼시토닌 유전자 관련 펩타이드)**: Most potent endogenous vasodilator in craniofacial tissues; CALCRL/RAMP1/Gs → TRPV1 sensitisation
- **TRPV1**: Transient receptor potential vanilloid 1; thermosensitive nociceptor channel inhibited by Y1 activation
- **Neurogenic inflammation (신경성 염증)**: Neuropeptide-mediated vascular response (vasodilation, plasma extravasation) triggered antidromically from sensory C fibres without classical leukocyte activation
- **BMP-2 (Bone Morphogenetic Protein-2)**: Growth factor upregulated via NPY/Y1/fibroblast signalling → odontoblast differentiation, tertiary dentinogenesis
- **Intrapulpal pressure (수내압)**: Hydrostatic pressure within the rigid dentinal chamber; elevated by vasodilation → venous stasis → local hypoxia/necrosis
