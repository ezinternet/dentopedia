## Why Ingested
This review establishes the widely cited threshold surface roughness (R_a ~0.2 µm) below which bacterial plaque retention is not expected to increase further; it compares initial roughness of oral hard materials and effects of treatment modalities. It reinforces concepts found in [[dental-materials/restorative-margin-periodontal-interface-overview]] regarding surface roughness driving biofilm colonization.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Proposes threshold surface roughness R_a ≈ 0.2 µm for bacterial plaque retention.
- Compares initial surface roughness of various oral hard materials and effects of treatment modalities (polishing, scaling, brushing, condensing, glazing, finishing).
- Suggests material-specific treatment approaches needed to achieve/maintain smooth surfaces.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
Medline search; inclusion criteria: (1) stated threshold surface roughness values and reputed change due to different manipulation techniques, or (2) included standardized surface conditions comparable to treated surface. (Details limited to abstract.)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Threshold suggested: R_a ≈ 0.2 µm below which no further reduction in bacterial accumulation expected.
- Above threshold, increased roughness associated with increased plaque accumulation (raising caries/periodontal inflammation risk).
- Range of surface roughness wide across materials; treatment impact material-dependent.
