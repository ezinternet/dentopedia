## One-line Summary
SR (Niger J Clin Pract 2026): 5 RCTs; MINB up to 93% anesthetic success for mandibular premolars with SIP vs IANB; clinically relevant advantage despite non-significant statistical difference; MINB = faster onset and less injection pain.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First SR comparing MINB vs IANB specifically for mandibular permanent premolars with SIP
- 5 RCTs included — limited evidence base but provides pooled directional estimate
- MINB success up to 93% vs IANB (lower rates in SIP); not statistically significant difference
- MINB advantages: significantly faster onset + less injection pain vs IANB
- Clinically relevant regardless of statistical threshold — MINB viable alternative when IANB fails

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: SR (systematic review; no meta-analysis possible)
- Databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, WoS, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar, ClinicalTrials.gov; search up to Sept 5, 2025
- Inclusion: RCTs, human adults, mandibular permanent premolars, SIP requiring NSRCT, comparing MINB vs IANB
- n: 5 RCTs met inclusion criteria
- ROB: assessed independently by two reviewers

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- MINB anesthetic success: up to 93% for SIP mandibular premolars
- Comparison vs IANB: directional superiority for MINB, not statistically significant
- MINB advantages (statistically significant vs IANB):
  - Faster onset of anesthesia
  - Significantly less injection pain
- IANB: well-documented failure rates in SIP due to central sensitization and inflammatory effects

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Only 5 RCTs — limited evidence base
- Heterogeneity in outcome definitions and local anesthetic agents across studies
- Non-significant primary outcome (success rate) limits definitive conclusions

## 6. Related Work
- IANB failure in SIP is a recognized clinical problem (central sensitization, inflammatory pH changes)
- Mental nerve innervates mandibular premolars and lower incisors — MINB directly targets the regional afferent pathway

## 7. Glossary
- MINB: Mental/Incisive Nerve Block — injection at mental foramen targeting mental and incisive nerve branches
- IANB: Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block — conventional mandibular block at lingula
- SIP: Symptomatic Irreversible Pulpitis
- NSRCT: Non-Surgical Root Canal Treatment
