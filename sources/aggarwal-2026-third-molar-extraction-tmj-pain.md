## Why Ingested

발치(특히 제3대구치 발치)가 TMJ 통증을 악화시키는지 완화시키는지는 임상에서 반복적으로 나오는 논쟁적 질문이며, 이 PRISMA 2020 SR+MA(pooled RR/OR=0.49)는 기존 [[wiki/tmj/kapos-2020-temporomandibular-disorders-aetiology-diagnosis-review]]의 다인성 병인론에 "발치"라는 구체적 술식 변수를 계량화해 추가한다. 또한 치료적 발치(치료적 맥락) vs 예방적 제3대구치 발치를 구분해 결론을 내는 점에서 [[wiki/tmj/valesan-2021-tmd-prevalence-sr-ma]]의 유병률 데이터와 대비해 임상 의사결정에 참고할 수 있다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO-registered) of 8 studies (2 RCTs, 1 prospective cohort, 2 retrospective/matched cohorts, 2 cross-sectional, 1 SR; n=42 to >34,000) examining dental extraction (predominantly third molar) versus non-extraction/pre-extraction controls on TMJ pain/TMD outcomes.

Pooled meta-analysis of 6 studies showed extraction was associated with a significant reduction in TMJ pain/symptoms (RR/OR=0.49, 95% CI 0.31-0.66, p<0.00001, I2=34%), i.e., extraction patients were ~51% less likely to have TMJ pain than non-extraction controls, though certainty of evidence was low-to-moderate (GRADE).

Evidence is dominated by third molar extraction (not orthodontic premolar extraction, for which data were minimal/absent); therapeutic extraction (e.g., supraerupted/interfering molars in TMD patients) improved symptoms, while prophylactic third molar removal caused common but usually transient short-term postoperative morbidity (trismus 92%, myofascial pain 69% at 1 week in one RCT).

## 세줄요약

PRISMA 2020 기반 체계적 문헌고찰+메타분석(PROSPERO 등록)으로, 발치(주로 제3대구치)와 TMJ 통증/TMD 증상의 관계를 다룬 8편(RCT 2, 전향적 코호트 1, 후향적/매칭 코호트 2, 단면연구 2, SR 1편; n=42~34,000명 이상)을 분석했다.

6편의 정량 메타분석 결과 발치군이 비발치군 대비 TMJ 통증/증상 위험이 유의하게 낮았다(pooled RR/OR=0.49, 95% CI 0.31-0.66, p<0.00001, I2=34%) — 발치군이 약 51% 덜 통증을 경험, 다만 GRADE 근거수준은 low-to-moderate.

근거 대부분이 교정 소구치 발치가 아닌 제3대구치 발치에 편중되어 있으며, 치료적 발치(TMD 환자의 과맹출/간섭 대구치 제거)는 증상을 개선시킨 반면 예방적 제3대구치 발치는 흔하지만 대개 일시적인 단기 술후 이환(1주차 개구제한 92%, 근막통 69%, 한 RCT 기준)을 동반했다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First protocol-registered SR+MA specifically framing the "extraction vs TMJ pain" debate as split between the *orthodontic premolar extraction* controversy (historically debated, but evidence-poor) and the *third molar extraction* literature (evidence-rich but a different clinical scenario) — and explicitly cautions against conflating the two.
- Pooled quantitative synthesis (6 studies) shows a significant *protective* association between (predominantly third molar) extraction and reduced TMJ pain — contradicting several earlier narrative conclusions that extraction is neutral-to-harmful for TMJ outcomes.
- Distinguishes therapeutic extraction (in patients with established TMD, e.g., removal of supraerupted/interfering molars) from prophylactic extraction (routine/asymptomatic third molar removal), showing divergent risk-benefit profiles: therapeutic extraction relieves symptoms, prophylactic extraction causes common short-term morbidity that is usually transient.
- Applies formal risk-of-bias tools per study design (RoB 2.0 for RCTs, ROBINS-I for non-randomized studies, NOS for observational studies) plus GRADE certainty rating across outcomes.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review, prospectively registered (PROSPERO CRD420250655883).
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane Library, Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, plus Google Scholar (gray literature), ClinicalTrials.gov, CTRI (India). Search through August 2025, no language restriction.
- **Eligibility**: Studies evaluating third molar or other extraction with TMJ pain/TMD outcomes (short-term morbidity or long-term diagnosis), comparator = no extraction / pre-extraction baseline / non-extraction controls. Excluded: studies of surgical TMD management (arthrocentesis, arthroscopy, open joint, TMJ replacement), case reports/series.
- **Screening**: Two independent reviewers, two-stage (title/abstract then full-text), third-reviewer arbitration for disagreements. Zotero used for deduplication.
- **Risk of bias**: RoB 2.0 (RCTs), ROBINS-I (non-randomized interventional), NOS (observational); GRADE for overall certainty.
- **Synthesis**: Narrative synthesis for all 8 studies; random-effects meta-analysis (RevMan Web) for 6 studies with sufficient homogeneity. Dichotomous outcomes pooled as RR with 95% CI; effect direction standardized/inverted as needed so RR/OR<1 = favors extraction (less TMJ pain). Heterogeneity via chi-square (p<0.10) and I2; substantial heterogeneity (I2>50%) to trigger subgroup analysis (age, extraction type, comparator type, follow-up duration); sensitivity analysis by excluding high-RoB studies; publication bias via funnel plot/Egger's test if ≥10 studies (not reached here — 6 studies pooled).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 8 studies met eligibility: 2 RCTs, 1 prospective cohort, 2 retrospective/matched cohorts, 2 cross-sectional, 1 systematic review. Sample sizes 42 to >34,000. Follow-up: baseline-only (cross-sectional) up to 6 years (longitudinal cohorts).
- 6/8 studies primarily evaluated third molar extraction; 1 evaluated therapeutic extraction of supraerupted maxillary third molars in TMD patients; no RCTs specifically evaluated orthodontic premolar extraction.
- **Pooled quantitative result (6 studies)**: RR/OR = 0.49 (95% CI 0.31-0.66), Z=5.49, p<0.00001; I2=34% (moderate heterogeneity), chi2=10.20 (p=0.07); REML tau2=0.01 (low). Largest weight contributors: Huang and Rue (29.5%), Gururaj et al. (27.6%), Barbosa et al. (25.4%).
- Individual study findings:
  - Gururaj et al. (RCT-like, therapeutic extraction of supraerupted/distoverted molars): 96% reduction in pain symptoms vs non-extraction (p<0.05); improved joint clicking, headache, neck pain, and patient satisfaction.
  - Munawar et al. (mandibular third molar surgery): short-term pain increase; myofascial pain 69% at 1 week (p<0.005), trismus 92% at 1 week; some persisted to 6 months; small sample → low meta-analytic weight (<5%) but highest short-term risk (RR=5.00, wide CI).
  - Huang et al.: non-significant increase in TMD incidence post-extraction (RR 1.4, 95% CI 0.9-2.2).
  - Huang and Rue: significant association, adjusted RR 1.6 (95% CI 1.3-2.0); ~23% of TMD cases attributable to extraction (large insurance-registry cohort).
  - Barbosa et al. (cross-sectional, n=1,381, RDC/TMD criteria): ORs 1.30-2.15 across diagnostic subgroups (p=0.041-0.006); history of third molar removal → higher odds of reporting TMD symptoms.
  - Uppgaard et al. (multisite RCT, mandibular support device vs manual): no significant functional difference between support methods; fully impacted molars predicted higher postoperative pain (OR 3.44, p=0.004); judged low RoB, retention >95%.
  - Damasceno et al. (prior SR of 7 studies): OR 1.81-2.15, RR ~2.1 for TMD after extraction; low-to-moderate certainty.
- Risk of bias: cross-sectional studies moderate-to-low-moderate risk; cohort studies (Huang et al., Huang and Rue) low-to-moderate risk; Munawar et al. moderate-to-high risk; Uppgaard et al. (RCT) low risk.
- Overall GRADE certainty: low-to-moderate for most outcomes, mainly due to observational design predominance and heterogeneity.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Evidence base overwhelmingly derives from **third molar extraction**, not orthodontic **premolar** extraction — the historically debated orthodontic scenario has essentially no direct RCT evidence in this review; conclusions cannot be extrapolated to elective orthodontic extraction.
- Heterogeneous diagnostic criteria across studies (RDC/TMD vs DC/TMD).
- Predominance of observational designs; most outcomes graded low/very-low certainty by GRADE → findings are "suggestive rather than confirmatory."
- Authors call for future SRs to stratify by extraction type (therapeutic vs prophylactic; premolar vs third molar) and by follow-up duration (short-term morbidity vs long-term risk), using standardized DC/TMD criteria, CBCT imaging, and patient-centered outcomes (OHIP, QoL).
- Publication bias assessment (funnel plot/Egger's test) was not performed because the pooled analysis included only 6 studies (below the pre-specified ≥10 threshold).

## 6. Related Work

- Contrasts with Kim et al. and Luther (cited within): no significant association between orthodontic extraction-based treatment and TMD onset.
- Contrasts with Jerjes et al.: third molar removal frequently associated with short-term postoperative TMJ pain/dysfunction but no consistent long-term adverse effect.
- Contrasts with Damasceno et al. (prior SR, 7 studies): pooled OR 1.81-2.15 suggesting extraction *increases* TMD risk — this review's inclusion of therapeutic-extraction RCT data (Gururaj et al.) reverses the pooled direction toward a protective effect.

## 7. Glossary

- **TMD**: Temporomandibular disorder(s).
- **TMJ**: Temporomandibular joint.
- **RDC/TMD**: Research Diagnostic Criteria for Temporomandibular Disorders.
- **DC/TMD**: Diagnostic Criteria for Temporomandibular Disorders (updated RDC/TMD).
- **RoB 2.0**: Cochrane Risk of Bias tool version 2, for randomized trials.
- **ROBINS-I**: Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies - of Interventions.
- **NOS**: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, for observational study quality.
- **GRADE**: Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluations — certainty-of-evidence framework.
- **RR/OR**: Risk ratio / odds ratio; pooled effect estimate coded so <1 favors extraction (less TMJ pain).
- **I2**: Statistical heterogeneity measure (percentage of variance across studies attributable to heterogeneity rather than chance).
- **REML tau2**: Restricted maximum likelihood estimate of between-study variance in a random-effects meta-analysis.
- **Pericoronitis**: Inflammation of soft tissue surrounding a partially erupted tooth (commonly mandibular third molar), implicated here as a possible mediator of TMJ symptom exacerbation via protective muscle splinting.
