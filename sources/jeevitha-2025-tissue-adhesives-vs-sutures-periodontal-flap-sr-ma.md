## Why Ingested

[[overviews/suture-wound-closure-decision-ladder]]가 2026-06-04 종합 시점에 명시한 최대 공백이 "SR+MA 부재"였다 — 이 카테고리는 그때까지 개별 RCT·in-vitro·case-report만 15편이었다. 이 논문은 PROSPERO 등록(CRD42023444615) SR+MA로 그 공백을 정면으로 메운다. 동시에 [[suture-wound-closure/pabst-2024-cyanoacrylate-tissue-adhesive-coronally-advanced-flap]]의 in-vitro 역학적 결론(cyanoacrylate TA가 봉합보다 CAF 변연 안정성 우수)을 임상 wound-healing index 근거로 독립 재확인한다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42023444615, PRISMA 2020) of 10 split-mouth/parallel RCTs (255 patients, 2013–2023) comparing tissue adhesives (mostly cyanoacrylate, 2 fibrin sealant/glue) vs sutures (mostly 3-0 silk) for wound closure in periodontal flap surgery, using validated wound-healing indices (Wachtel EWHI, Huang WHI, Landry HI) at 7/14/21 days.

Pooled forest-plot analysis found no statistically significant difference in wound-healing index between adhesives and sutures at any timepoint (overall SMD −0.50, 95% CI −1.42 to 0.42, p=0.29, I²=94%; dichotomous healing RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.19–3.00), though 7/10 individual studies numerically favored adhesives at 1 week, and adhesives showed consistently lower postoperative pain (7/7 studies) and better esthetics (3/3 assessed) with faster hemostasis and fewer inflammatory cells on histology.

GRADE certainty was moderate for both outcomes, downgraded for strongly suspected publication bias (Egger's intercept p=0.003; funnel-plot asymmetry); RoB-2 found low risk of bias in 80% of trials; authors conclude tissue adhesives are "better or comparable" to sutures but caution the clinical-superiority framing is not statistically supported by the primary pooled estimates.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 + 메타분석(SR+MA, PROSPERO CRD42023444615, PRISMA 2020) — 치주판막수술(periodontal flap surgery) 창상폐쇄에서 조직접착제(주로 cyanoacrylate, 2편은 fibrin sealant/glue) vs 봉합(주로 3-0 실크)을 비교한 스플릿마우스/평행군 RCT 10편(환자 255명, 2013–2023), 표준화된 창상치유지수(Wachtel EWHI·Huang WHI·Landry HI)를 7/14/21일에 측정.

통합 포레스트플롯 분석은 모든 시점에서 접착제-봉합 간 창상치유지수 통계적 유의차 없음(전체 SMD −0.50, 95% CI −1.42~0.42, p=0.29, I²=94%; 이분형 치유 RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.19–3.00)을 보였으나, 개별 연구 10편 중 7편은 1주차에 접착제가 수치상 우세했고, 술후 통증(7/7편)·심미성(평가한 3/3편)에서 접착제가 일관되게 우수, 조직학적으로 지혈이 빠르고 염증세포가 적음.

GRADE 확실성은 두 결과 모두 중등도(moderate) — 강한 출판편향 의심(Egger 절편 p=0.003, 깔때기그림 비대칭)으로 하향; RoB-2상 시험 80%가 낮은 비뚤림위험; 저자들은 접착제가 봉합과 "동등하거나 우수"하다고 결론짓지만 이 임상적 우위 서술이 1차 통합추정치의 통계적 유의성으로는 뒷받침되지 않는다는 점에 주의.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review (per the authors) specifically comparing tissue adhesives vs sutures for wound closure in periodontal flap surgery (prior SRs/MAs on tissue-adhesive-vs-suture existed for third-molar cyanoacrylate use, oral mucosal incisions generally, and gingival-recession/free-gingival-graft surgery, but not periodontal flap surgery specifically).
- Aggregates 10 RCTs (8 quantitatively pooled) spanning cyanoacrylate esters (isoamyl-, N-butyl-, 2-octyl-cyanoacrylate) and fibrin sealant/autologous fibrin glue against 3-0/4-0 silk sutures.
- Reports a clear directional split between the primary SMD/RR pooled estimates (not statistically significant, high heterogeneity I²=85–96%) and secondary patient-centered outcomes (pain, esthetics, hemostasis, histology) that consistently favored adhesives across the individual trials that measured them.
- Quantifies publication-bias risk formally (Egger's regression, Begg-Mazumdar, Rosenthal fail-safe N=848, trim-and-fill) rather than a qualitative funnel-plot impression alone.

## 3. Methodology

- PRISMA 2020-compliant search: PubMed, Cochrane Library, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, Embase, trial registries, hand search, 2013–2023, no language restriction. 252 records → 196 duplicates removed → 56 screened → 35 excluded at title/abstract → 21 sought full text (2 unobtainable) → 19 assessed → 9 excluded (no wound-healing outcome, wrong population, or wrong comparison) → 10 included qualitatively, 8 quantitatively.
- PICOS: adults 18–60 undergoing periodontal flap surgery; tissue adhesive vs suture for wound closure; outcome = wound closure %/dehiscence via validated indices; RCTs/clinical trials/split-mouth trials only (reviews, cohort, cross-sectional excluded).
- Two independent reviewers (Rayyan, blinded) with third-reviewer arbitration for both screening and extraction; RoB assessed with Cochrane RoB-2 (not blinded to authors).
- Random-effects inverse-variance SMD for continuous wound-healing-index outcomes; Mantel-Haenszel random-effects RR for dichotomous outcomes; I²>40% = substantial heterogeneity; GRADEpro for certainty rating.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Timepoint | SMD (95% CI) | p | I² |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 days | −0.25 (−1.80 to 1.30) | 0.75 | 96% |
| 14 days | −0.77 (−1.96 to 0.43) | 0.21 | 85% |
| 21 days | −0.96 (−2.54 to 0.63) | 0.24 | 93% |
| Overall pooled | −0.50 (−1.42 to 0.42) | 0.29 | 94% |

Dichotomous wound-healing (2 studies): RR 0.75 (95% CI 0.19–3.00, p=0.68, I²=0%); pooled healing ~88% (adhesive) vs ~86% (suture) at day 7.

RoB-2: 8/10 trials low risk overall; 2/10 (Chandra 2021, Soundarajan 2021) "some concerns" in the randomization domain.

Publication bias: Egger's intercept −14.10 (p=0.003, suggesting small-study effects); Begg-Mazumdar tau −0.29 (p=0.069, non-significant); Rosenthal fail-safe N=848 (Fisher's method 3,169); trim-and-fill minimal adjustment.

GRADE: moderate certainty for both the mean-difference and risk-ratio wound-healing outcomes, downgraded specifically for suspected publication bias (not for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or imprecision, which were all rated "not serious").

Secondary/patient-centered findings (not meta-analyzed, narratively synthesized across studies): 7/7 studies assessing pain found lower postoperative pain/discomfort with adhesives; 3/3 studies assessing esthetics favored adhesives; 2 histology studies found reduced inflammatory cells/increased connective-tissue fibers with adhesives; 2 studies found faster hemostasis with adhesives; 1 study (Pulikkotil 2014) found reduced IL-1β/IL-8 in gingival crevicular fluid with fibrin sealant.

The paper also reports, in its Discussion, a differently-framed "pooled effect size" (g=0.42, 95% CI 0.18–0.66, p<0.001, favoring adhesives) that is not reconcilable from the extracted text with the non-significant primary SMD/RR forest-plot results — flagged here as an internal inconsistency in the source rather than resolved by inference.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- High heterogeneity (I²=85–96%) across all pooled continuous-outcome timepoints, driven by differing suture materials/gauges, adhesive chemistries, flap techniques (conventional mucoperiosteal vs modified Widmann vs full-thickness), and outcome scales (EWHI vs WHI vs HI, not identical instruments).
- Publication bias strongly suspected (Egger p=0.003, asymmetric funnel), the explicit reason for the GRADE moderate (not high) rating on both outcomes.
- All included trials excluded medically compromised patients — no evidence on systemic-disease modifiers of adhesive-vs-suture wound healing.
- Small individual trial sizes (n=10–50 patients); largest single meta-analytic contrast (dichotomous RR) rests on only 2 studies.
- Authors' own conclusion ("better or comparable") arguably overstates what the non-significant pooled SMD/RR support; the paper's real contribution is stronger on secondary patient-centered outcomes (pain, esthetics) than on the primary wound-healing-index endpoint.
- Authors call for larger, standardized-protocol RCTs before adhesives can be said to replace sutures in periodontal flap surgery.

## 7. Glossary

- **Wound Healing Index (WHI)** — Huang et al.'s clinical scale scoring gingival edema, erythema, suppuration, discomfort, and flap dehiscence 2 weeks after coronally advanced flap.
- **Early Wound Healing Index (EWHI)** — Wachtel et al. 2003 scale for 1–2 week post-surgical evaluation of flap closure completeness, fibrin, and necrosis after intrabony-defect surgery.
- **Healing Index (HI)** — Landry et al.'s scale assessing tissue color, palpation response, granulation tissue, suppuration, and incision margin at 2 and 4 weeks.
- **Cyanoacrylate tissue adhesive** — monomeric ester that rapidly polymerizes on contact with tissue moisture, forming an areal (surface) bond; isoamyl-, N-butyl-, and 2-octyl-cyanoacrylate variants used across the included trials.
- **Standardized Mean Difference (SMD)** — effect-size metric expressing the difference between two group means in pooled standard-deviation units, used here because included trials measured wound healing on different index scales.
- **GRADE (certainty of evidence)** — framework (via GRADEpro) rating confidence in a pooled estimate as very low/low/moderate/high, here downgraded from high to moderate for suspected publication bias.
