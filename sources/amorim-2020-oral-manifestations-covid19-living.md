## Why Ingested

Original living systematic review establishing the epidemiology of COVID-19 oral manifestations (40 studies, 10,228 patients): gustatory impairment as the most common finding (45%), with detailed subtype prevalence and association with COVID-19 diagnosis, disease severity, and sex. Foundation paper for the 2021 6-month update.

## Three-line Summary

Original living systematic review (40 studies, 10,228 patients) demonstrating gustatory impairment as the most prevalent COVID-19 oral manifestation (45%) and characterizing oral mucosal lesion patterns.

## 세줄요약

COVID-19 구강 증상 최초 살아있는 체계적 문헌고찰(40편, 10,228명): 미각장애가 가장 흔한 구강 소견(45%)으로 확인, 구강점막병변의 임상 양상과 발생 시기를 최초 기술.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First living SR to systematically synthesize COVID-19 oral manifestation prevalence
- Establishes gustatory impairment as the most common oral manifestation (45%)
- Provides pooled prevalence for taste disorder subtypes: dysgeusia 38%, hypogeusia 35%, ageusia 24%
- Demonstrates strong association between taste disorders and COVID-19 (OR 12.68), mild/moderate severity (OR 2.09), and female sex (OR 1.64)
- Characterizes oral mucosal lesion morphology: white/erythematous plaques, ulcers, blisters, petechiae, desquamative gingivitis
- Establishes anatomic distribution: tongue > palate > lips > gingiva > buccal mucosa
- Describes temporal pattern: in mild cases, oral lesions appear before/at onset of respiratory symptoms; in severe/hospitalized cases, 7–24 days after onset

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: Living systematic review (PRISMA-compliant)
- Literature search: 6 databases + gray literature, all languages
- Inclusion: Studies reporting oral signs/symptoms in COVID-19 patients
- Study types included: 33 cross-sectional, 7 case reports (n=40 total)
- Risk of bias: Joanna Briggs Institute appraisal tools
- Evidence certainty: GRADE assessment
- Statistical method: Meta-analysis with pooled prevalence, odds ratios, 95% CI
- Sample: 10,228 patients (4,288 male, 5,770 female, 170 unknown) from 19 countries
- Full text not retrieved; summary based on abstract

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

*Abstract-only*

- 40 studies included; 10,228 patients from 19 countries
- Gustatory impairment overall: 45% (95% CI 34%–55%; I²=99%)
  - Dysgeusia: 38%; Hypogeusia: 35%; Ageusia: 24%
- Taste disorders vs. COVID-19 diagnosis: OR 12.68 (95% CI 6.41–25.10; I²=63%; p<0.00001)
- Taste disorders vs. mild/moderate severity: OR 2.09 (95% CI 1.25–3.49; I²=66%; p=0.005)
- Taste disorders vs. female sex: OR 1.64 (95% CI 1.23–2.17; I²=70%; p=0.0007)
- Oral mucosal lesions: variable morphology — white/erythematous plaques, irregular ulcers, small blisters, petechiae, desquamative gingivitis
- Lesion timing: mild cases — before or concurrent with respiratory onset; hospitalized cases — 7–24 days after onset

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Very high heterogeneity (I²=99% for gustatory impairment prevalence)
- Early pandemic data: 40 studies, predominantly cross-sectional and case reports
- Oral mucosal lesions interpreted as likely co-infections/secondary manifestations rather than direct SARS-CoV-2 effect
- Living SR design anticipates continuous updates (see 2021 6-month update)

## 7. Glossary

- **Living systematic review (살아있는 체계적 문헌고찰, Living SR)**: SR designed for regular updates as new evidence emerges
- **Gustatory impairment (미각장애, Gustatory impairment)**: Any abnormality in taste perception (umbrella term for dysgeusia, hypogeusia, ageusia)
- **Desquamative gingivitis (박리성 치은염, Desquamative gingivitis)**: Erythematous, erosive, or blistering gingivitis
- **Petechiae (점상출혈, Petechiae)**: Small pinpoint hemorrhages in mucosa
- **PRISMA**: Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses
- **GRADE**: Evidence certainty grading framework
- **PROSPERO**: International register of systematic reviews (CRD42020184468)
