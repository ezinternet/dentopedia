## Three-line Summary

Prospective observational study at two Australian hospital-based dental clinics; 95 heparinized patients received 185 dental extractions, with bleeding events tracked over the 48-hour post-procedure window.

Six patients (6.3%) had minor bleeding within 48 hours, all controlled by gauze pressure alone; bivariate analysis found unfractionated heparin (vs low-molecular-weight heparin) and a shorter interval since the last heparin dose were associated with higher bleeding rates (p ≤ 0.01).

Heparin therapy need not be interrupted or dose-adjusted for dental extraction when careful local hemostatic measures are used — though unfractionated heparin and extraction timed close to the last dose warrant extra hemostatic attention.

## 세줄요약

호주 병원 기반 치과 2곳의 전향적 관찰연구 — 헤파린 (Heparin) 투여 환자 95명이 발치 185건을 받았고, 시술 후 48시간 동안 출혈 사건을 추적했다.

48시간 내 6명 (6.3%)에서 경미한 출혈이 발생했고 전원 거즈 압박만으로 지혈됨; 이변량 분석에서 미분획헤파린 (Unfractionated Heparin, UFH)이 저분자량헤파린 (Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin, LMWH)보다, 그리고 마지막 헤파린 투여와 가까운 시점의 발치일수록 출혈률이 높았다 (p ≤ 0.01).

국소 지혈을 충실히 하면 헤파린 중단이나 용량 변경 없이 발치가 가능하다 — 다만 미분획헤파린 사용 중이거나 마지막 투여 직후 발치하는 경우엔 지혈에 더 신경 써야 한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Addresses an explicitly stated guideline gap: management protocols exist for warfarin, DOACs, and antithrombotics, but guidance for **injectable anticoagulants (heparins)** is limited, leaving dentists and physicians uncertain about extraction safety and delaying care.
- Provides prospective incidence data for post-extraction bleeding in heparinized patients (6.3% of 95 patients over 185 extractions), all events minor.
- Identifies two risk stratifiers within heparin therapy: heparin type (unfractionated vs low-molecular-weight) and proximity of the extraction to the last heparin dose.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: prospective, observational.
- **Setting**: two Australian hospital-based dental clinics.
- **Population**: 95 heparinized patients undergoing dental extraction; 185 teeth extracted in total.
- **Outcome window**: bleeding events within 48 hours post-procedure.
- **Outcome**: frequency and severity of post-procedure bleeding; management required for each event.
- **Analysis**: bivariate analysis of bleeding rate against heparin type and interval since last heparin dose.
- **Note**: abstract-only ingest — detailed inclusion/exclusion criteria, the specific local hemostatic protocol, bleeding-severity grading scale, and exact per-subgroup denominators are not available from the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Cohort**: 95 heparinized patients, 185 dental extractions.
- **Bleeding**: 6 patients with minor bleeding within 48 h = **6.3%** (patient-level rate).
- **Severity**: all bleeding episodes were minor and managed with direct gauze pressure to the site — no escalation reported.
- **Heparin type**: unfractionated heparin (UFH) associated with higher post-extraction bleeding than low-molecular-weight heparins (LMWH), **p ≤ 0.01**.
- **Timing**: extractions completed closer to the last heparin dose had higher bleeding rates, **p ≤ 0.01**.
- **Authors' conclusion**: extractions can be completed safely on injectable anticoagulants with careful local hemostatic measures; interruption or dose modification of heparin is unnecessary.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Observational (no control/comparator arm of non-anticoagulated or heparin-interrupted patients), so the 6.3% rate is not benchmarked against a within-study baseline.
- Modest cohort (95 patients, 6 events) — the bivariate UFH-vs-LMWH and dose-timing signals rest on very few events and were not reported as adjusted for confounders in the abstract.
- Two hospital-based clinics in Australia; hospital inpatients on heparin are a specific case-mix and generalization to community dental practice is uncertain.
- 48-hour observation window may miss later delayed bleeding.
- **Ingest limitation**: built from the PubMed abstract only; the local hemostatic protocol, severity-grading criteria, and subgroup numerators/denominators could not be verified against full text.

## 6. Related Work

- Warfarin / VKA extraction management protocols — described by the authors as already well established, in contrast to the heparin gap this study targets.
- DOAC extraction protocols — likewise established; see the wiki's DOAC pages for the continuation-vs-interruption evidence base.

## 7. Glossary

- **Heparin**: injectable (parenteral) anticoagulant potentiating antithrombin; unlike oral agents it is used in hospitalized, bridging, and dialysis settings.
- **UFH (Unfractionated Heparin)**: standard heparin, typically IV infusion, monitored by aPTT; shorter, more variable action. Higher bleeding rate in this study.
- **LMWH (Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin)**: e.g., enoxaparin; subcutaneous, more predictable pharmacokinetics, usually unmonitored. Lower bleeding rate here.
- **Enoxaparin**: the LMWH named among this paper's keywords.
- **Post-operative hemorrhage**: bleeding after the procedure; here defined within a 48-hour window and graded as minor when controlled by gauze pressure.
- **Bivariate analysis**: two-variable association test (here, bleeding vs heparin type, and bleeding vs dose-to-extraction interval).
