## Why Ingested

Serves the **timing** axis of the 2026-11-12 `zirconia-occlusal-adjustment` surveillance topic ("how much adjustment is tolerable — and when"): it is the first held study to put the *same* diamond-bur adjustment before vs after crystallization in two glass-ceramics, showing that the moment of adjustment, not the material, controls the strength loss. Unlike the zirconia grinding literature (where post-adjustment polishing/overglazing is the remediation lever, e.g. [[dental-materials/zirconia/mohammadi-bassir-2017-grinding-overglazing-polishing-zirconia]]), here polishing did **not** restore roughness or strength — the defect had to be sealed by the crystallization firing itself. Anchors the ZLS/LDS side of the batch against [[dental-materials/ceramic/moeeduddin-2020-firing-etching-zls-theracem-bond]].

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro biaxial flexural strength study (Materials 2025; 4 groups × n = 15 discs, 1.2 × 15 mm) of zirconia-reinforced lithium silicate (ZLS, Suprinity) and lithium disilicate (LDS, IPS e.max CAD) adjusted with an extra-fine cylindrical diamond bur (3099 FF) either before crystallization (AC) or after crystallization plus polishing (CA).

Abrasion after crystallization roughly halved strength in ZLS (232.1 → 115.0 MPa) and significantly lowered LDS (244.3 MPa when abraded before crystallization); two-way ANOVA showed the abrasion/crystallization sequence, not the material, was the only significant factor, and final surface roughness (표면거칠기, Ra) remained higher than baseline in every group despite polishing.

Clinical implication: adjust/try-in glass-ceramic restorations in the pre-crystallized state whenever the workflow allows, because post-crystallization diamond adjustment leaves subsurface cracks that polishing cannot repair — and the paper does not simulate glazing, aging, or cyclic loading.

## 세줄요약

시험관 연구(Materials 2025, 4군 × n = 15, 직경 15 mm × 두께 1.2 mm 디스크): 지르코니아 강화 리튬실리케이트 (Zirconia-reinforced Lithium Silicate, ZLS — Suprinity)와 리튬디실리케이트 (Lithium Disilicate, LDS — IPS e.max CAD)를 초미세 원통형 다이아몬드 버(3099 FF)로 결정화 전(AC) 또는 결정화 후 연마까지 시행(CA)해 이축 굴곡강도 (Biaxial Flexural Strength, BFS)를 비교.

결정화 후 연삭 시 ZLS는 232.1 → 115.0 MPa로 약 절반, LDS도 결정화 전 연삭(244.3 MPa)보다 유의하게 낮았고, 이원배치 분산분석 (Two-way ANOVA)에서 유의 인자는 재료가 아니라 연삭·결정화 순서뿐이었으며, 연마 후에도 최종 표면거칠기 (Surface Roughness, Ra)는 모든 군에서 초기값보다 유의하게 높았다.

임상적 의미: 글라스세라믹은 가능하면 결정화 전(pre-crystallized) 상태에서 교합조정·시적을 끝내야 하며, 결정화 후 다이아몬드 조정은 연마로 회복되지 않는 표면하 균열을 남긴다 — 단 유약 재소성·에이징·반복하중은 이 연구에서 평가되지 않았다.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Clinical Adjustment of Zirconia-Reinforced Lithium Silicate and Lithium Disilicate Restorations Should Be Performed Before Crystallization
- **Authors**: Baroudi K, de Almeida NR, de Abreu LS, Wandscher VF, Ramos NC, Padmanabhan V, Bucholz CA, Amaral M, Beatty MW
- **Journal**: Materials (MDPI), 2025;18(9):1944
- **DOI**: 10.3390/ma18091944 — PMID 40363448, PMCID PMC12072546
- **Study type**: in-vitro bench study (biaxial flexural strength + profilometry + Weibull analysis)
- **Materials**: ZLS (Suprinity, Vita Zahnfabrik) and LDS (IPS e.max CAD, Ivoclar Vivadent), milled pre-crystallized blocks

## 2. Key Contributions

1. Isolates **adjustment timing** (before vs after crystallization) as an independent variable — most adjustment studies vary the *instrument* or the *polishing system*, not the sequence.
2. Shows the timing effect is material-independent: two-way ANOVA found a significant effect only for abrasion/crystallization moment, with no significant material effect and no interaction.
3. Quantifies the penalty for the routine chairside habit of adjusting after crystallization: ZLS drops from 232.1 MPa to 115.0 MPa (≈50%).
4. Demonstrates that silicone/rubber polishing after diamond abrasion does **not** restore roughness — a direct contrast with the zirconia paradigm where polishing is the remediation step.
5. Reports the mechanism (glass-phase melting during the crystallization firing partially seals pre-existing defects), which reconciles the result with the established "crystallization heals cracks" finding in LDS and the weaker/absent effect in ZLS.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Specimen fabrication**: pre-crystallized blocks rounded to 15 mm cylinders, sectioned with a diamond blade under water cooling to 1.2 ± 0.2 mm discs, finished with 600-grit SiC paper on both faces.
- **Baseline roughness**: contact profilometer (SurfTest SJ-310, Mitutoyo), 0.25 mm cutoff, 0.5 mm/s, three parallel readings per disc (Ra, µm).
- **Groups (n = 15 each, 60 discs total)**:
  - AC — diamond abrasion **before** crystallization: ZLS-AC, LDS-AC
  - CA — crystallization **then** diamond abrasion: ZLS-CA, LDS-CA
- **Adjustment simulation**: cylindrical extra-fine-grit diamond bur (3099 FF, KG Sorensen) in a high-speed handpiece, manual pressure, until a permanent marker on the surface was fully removed. Deliberately **not** standardized in time/pressure, to mimic real clinical adjustment.
- **Post-abrasion polishing**: AC group — silicone diamond discs (DF → DFF, 8090 D, Viking), 1 min side-to-side; CA group — rubber points (Viking), 1 min.
- **Crystallization**: Programat EP 5000 furnace (Ivoclar Vivadent), manufacturer cycle for each material.
- **Strength test**: piston-on-three-ball biaxial flexure — three 1.6 mm metallic spheres on a 10 mm diameter circle at 120°, 1.6 mm flat cylindrical tip, 0.5 mm/min crosshead. The **abraded surface faced downward**, i.e. was loaded in tension (worst-case orientation).
- **Analysis**: Kolmogorov–Smirnov normality; paired t-test (initial vs final Ra); independent t-test (roughness increase between materials; BFS between AC/CA within material); two-way ANOVA (material × timing) + Tukey post hoc; Weibull module (m) and characteristic strength (σ0) per ENV 843-5; SEM of representative surfaces and fracture origins; fragment count/origin analysis.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Roughness (Ra)**: final Ra was significantly higher than initial Ra in **all** groups and both materials (paired t-test). The *magnitude* of the roughness increase did not differ significantly between ZLS and LDS within either timing scenario.
- **Biaxial flexural strength (BFS)** — figures stated in the text: LDS-AC 244.3 MPa; ZLS-AC 232.1 MPa; ZLS-CA 115.0 MPa. LDS-CA was significantly lower than LDS-AC (exact mean not stated in the extracted text). Abrasion after crystallization produced the lowest values for both materials.
- **Two-way ANOVA**: significant influence only for the moment of abrasion/crystallization; material and the interaction were not significant.
- **Weibull**: reliability module m similar across all groups (overlapping CIs); characteristic strength σ0 significantly decreased when the material was crystallized first and adjusted afterwards.
- **Fracture patterns**: more fragments with higher strength (minimum 2 fragments at 50–150 MPa, maximum 6 fragments at 280 MPa); **every** fracture originated at the abraded surface, which was under tension; SEM showed subsurface cracks and sharper defects in the CA (post-crystallization) groups.
- **Benchmarking against unadjusted literature values**: LDS 343.57–378.88 MPa and ZLS ~289.1 MPa (up to 507.53 MPa) — all higher than any adjusted group here, i.e. damage was irreversible even with pre-crystallization abrasion + polishing.
- **Power**: calculated as ≈1.0 (4 levels, α = 0.05, n = 15, SD 34.22, maximum intergroup difference 129 MPa).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Pilot/preliminary** by the authors' own framing; flat discs distribute load differently from anatomical crowns, so absolute MPa values are not crown predictions.
- No **glazing** simulation — the authors note glaze re-application is likely the more effective repair for post-crystallization adjustment, citing prior LDS work.
- No **aging** (hydrothermal/LTD), no **cyclic loading/fatigue**, no humid environment or antagonist contact — all directly relevant to slow crack growth in the mouth.
- Roughness characterized by **Ra only**; Sa/Sz/Rt and 3D profilometry recommended.
- Abrasion was intentionally unstandardized (marker-removal endpoint), so bur pressure/time varied between specimens — clinically realistic but adds variance.
- Adjustments were made on discs, not on cemented restorations; cementation and adhesive support (which raise glass-ceramic strength) were not modeled.

## 6. Related Work

- Builds on the LDS pre-crystallization defect-healing literature (crack/defect reduction after crystallization firing) and its inconsistent replication in ZLS.
- Contrasts with the zirconia adjustment literature, where post-grinding polishing or overglazing *is* the repair step: [[dental-materials/zirconia/mohammadi-bassir-2017-grinding-overglazing-polishing-zirconia]].
- Complements wear-side adjustment evidence in [[dental-materials/zirconia/alfrisany-2019-occlusal-adjustments-zirconia-enamel-wear]] (finishing state governs antagonist wear).
- Shares the ZLS crystallization-firing variable with [[dental-materials/ceramic/moeeduddin-2020-firing-etching-zls-theracem-bond]] (firing cycles → bond strength).
- Material-selection context: [[dental-materials/ceramic/cuzic-2025-clinicians-guide-material-selection-all-ceramics]].

## 7. Glossary

- **ZLS (Zirconia-reinforced Lithium Silicate)** — glass-ceramic with lithium metasilicate/orthophosphate crystals plus tetragonal zirconia filler in a glassy matrix (지르코니아 강화 리튬실리케이트).
- **LDS (Lithium Disilicate)** — glass-ceramic crystallizing to ~70% Li2Si2O5 (리튬디실리케이트).
- **Crystallization firing (결정화 소성)** — post-milling furnace cycle that grows the crystalline phase and improves strength/optics.
- **AC / CA** — abrasion-then-crystallization / crystallization-then-abrasion.
- **Biaxial flexural strength (BFS, 이축 굴곡강도)** — strength measured by piston-on-three-ball loading of a disc; standard for brittle dental ceramics (ENV 843-5).
- **Ra (표면거칠기, arithmetic mean roughness)** — 2D profilometric roughness average.
- **Weibull module m / σ0** — scatter parameter (reliability) and characteristic strength (63.2% failure probability).
