## Why Ingested

Fills the "external trauma / sport as TMD risk factor" gap in the TMD shelf: [[tmj/chan-2022-myogenous-tmd-diagnosis-treatment-review]] frames myofascial TMD around bruxism and central sensitization but not athletic exposure. It also adds a TMD-outcome angle to the mouthguard evidence in [[dental-trauma/agarwal-2025-impact-mouthguards-prevention-dentofacial-injuries]], which covers only dentofacial injury.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies (cohort and cross-sectional; NIH quality tools) comparing temporomandibular disorder (TMD) symptoms between athletes and non-athletes.

Symptoms (myofascial pain, clicking, musculoskeletal symptoms) were comparably prevalent in athletes and non-athletes, and pooled comparisons showed statistically insignificant differences; mouthguard use was associated with a significant reduction in TMD symptoms.

Only the abstract was available, so pooled estimates, study counts and heterogeneity are unknown; sport-specific prevalence differed, but athletic participation per se is not shown to raise TMD risk.

## 세줄요약

관찰연구(코호트·단면연구; NIH 질 평가 도구)를 대상으로 운동선수와 비선수 간 측두하악장애 (Temporomandibular Disorders, TMD) 증상을 비교한 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석.

근막통 (myofascial pain)·관절음·근골격계 증상의 유병률은 선수와 비선수에서 비슷했고 합산 비교에서 통계적으로 유의한 차이가 없었으며, 마우스가드 (mouthguard) 사용 시 TMD 증상이 유의하게 감소했다.

초록만 확보되어 합산 추정치·연구 수·이질성은 알 수 없으며, 종목별 유병률은 달랐으나 운동 참여 자체가 TMD 위험을 높인다고 볼 근거는 제시되지 않았다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Systematic appraisal of sports participation as a TMD exposure.
- Meta-analyses found no significant athlete vs non-athlete difference.
- Reports a significant TMD-symptom reduction with mouthguard use.
- Reports different prevalence rates by sport.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Databases: PubMed, ScienceDirect, Dimensions, Google Scholar, Cochrane Library, ERIC.
- Pre-specified eligibility criteria; quality assessed with NIH tools for observational cohort and cross-sectional studies.
- Systematic data extraction; meta-analysis of athlete vs non-athlete comparisons.
- Number of studies, participants and effect measure: not stated in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- TMD symptoms (myofascial pain, clicking, musculoskeletal) comparably prevalent in athletes and non-athletes, some studies contrasting.
- Meta-analyses: statistically insignificant differences between athletes and non-athletes.
- Mouthguard use: significant reduction in TMD symptoms.
- Different prevalence rates across sports. No numeric values available (abstract-only).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Abstract-only: no numbers, heterogeneity, risk-of-bias results or publication-bias assessment could be read.
- Included designs are observational; inherent confounding. Mouthguard finding direction of causality and mouthguard type are unknown from the abstract.
- Google Scholar as a database is a methodological weakness (reviewer note, not from the abstract).

## 7. Glossary

- TMD: temporomandibular disorders, pain/dysfunction of masticatory muscles and TMJ.
- Myofascial pain: muscle-origin pain with trigger points.
- Mouthguard: intraoral protective appliance used in contact sports.
- NIH quality assessment tools: checklists for observational cohort and cross-sectional studies.
