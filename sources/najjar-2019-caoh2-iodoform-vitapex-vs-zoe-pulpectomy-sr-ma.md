## Three-line Summary


Systematic review and meta-analysis (15 studies included, 10 in meta-analysis, 1,669 primary teeth) comparing Ca(OH)2/iodoform paste (Vitapex/Metapex) vs.

ZOE as pulpectomy root filling material in primary teeth: no significant difference in clinical/radiographical success at 6 and 12 months, but ZOE (and ZOE/iodoform combined with Ca(OH)2) showed statistically significantly higher success rates than Ca(OH)2/iodoform at ≥18-month follow-up in high-quality studies.

## 1. Document Information

- Journal: Clinical and Experimental Dental Research, 2019 Jun (Wiley/Wiley Open Access)
- DOI: 10.1002/cre2.173; PMID 31249711; PMCID PMC6585588
- Systematic review registration: PROSPERO CRD42016037563
- Conflict of interest: none declared by authors
- Funding: not reported in retrieved text

## 2. Key Contributions

- First meta-analysis (per the authors) to directly compare Ca(OH)2/iodoform paste against ZOE (and ZOE/iodoform, and ZOE/iodoform combined with Ca(OH)2) for primary tooth pulpectomy outcomes.
- Synthesizes 15 studies (1,669 primary teeth: 337 anterior, 1,332 molars) across follow-up periods from 1 week to 30 months.
- Stratifies analysis by study quality (modified CONSORT 2010 checklist, 27-point scale) revealing that conclusions differ substantially between high-quality and moderate-quality studies — a methodological contribution beyond the raw pooled result.
- Provides a clinical recommendation stratified by expected tooth exfoliation timing: Ca(OH)2/iodoform for teeth nearing exfoliation; ZOE or ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 otherwise.
- Confirms (via subgroup analysis) that irrigation solution type (NaOCl vs. others) and molar type (mandibular-only vs. maxillary+mandibular) do not significantly affect success rates in the ZOE group.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: PRISMA-guided systematic review + meta-analysis; registered PROSPERO CRD42016037563.
- **Search**: PubMed/MEDLINE (261), Google Scholar (3,850), Scopus (89) — 5,000 titles total; initial search April 2016, updated search January 2018 (added 1 study). No language or year restriction (search strategy initially limited 2003–2017 window, later broadened).
- **Inclusion criteria**: RCTs and non-randomized clinical trials comparing clinical and/or radiographical outcomes of Ca(OH)2/iodoform vs. ZOE pulpectomy in primary teeth of healthy children.
- **Exclusion criteria**: cross-sectional, retrospective, laboratory, animal studies; permanent teeth; traumatized teeth; primary teeth without a successor; special-needs populations.
- **Screening**: two independent reviewers (title → abstract → full text); Cohen's kappa 0.92 (96.29% agreement) for study selection, kappa 0.82 (98.13% agreement) for data extraction; disagreements resolved by a third reviewer.
- **Study flow**: 5,000 titles → 602 duplicates removed → 27 full-text reviewed → 11 excluded (3 no comparison group, 3 reviews, 1 not compared with ZOE, 4 Ca(OH)2 without iodoform) → 16 included → 1 further excluded after translation (double-counted data) → **15 studies in final SR**, **10 in meta-analysis**.
- **Materials studied**: Ca(OH)2/iodoform as Metapex (4 studies) or Vitapex (10 studies); 1 study did not specify manufacturer.
- **Success criteria**: Clinical — no pain, no swelling, no abscess, no pain on percussion, and/or decreased mobility. Radiographical — decrease/absence of radiolucency vs. preoperative baseline (no change in radiolucency counted as success in several studies).
- **Quality appraisal**: modified CONSORT 2010 checklist (27 points; ≥19 low risk/high quality, 10–18 moderate risk, ≤9 high risk/low quality) — 2 studies high-quality, 11 moderate-quality, 2 low-quality.
- **Statistics**: Review Manager (RevMan) 5.3; Mantel–Haenszel method for pooled odds ratios (OR) and 95% CI; I² heterogeneity statistic (>25%/50%/75% = low/moderate/high); fixed-effect model if no heterogeneity, random-effects (DerSimonian & Laird) if heterogeneous; significance threshold p<0.05; funnel plots + Egger's test for publication/small-study-effect bias; chi-squared comparison of high- vs. moderate-quality study success rates.
- **Subgroup/sensitivity analyses**: by intracanal irrigation type (NaOCl vs. other), tooth type (mandibular-only molars vs. maxillary+mandibular), and study quality tier.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Across all 15 studies (raw ranges, not pooled): clinical success 70–100% (Ca(OH)2/iodoform), 77–100% (ZOE), 57–100% (ZOE/iodoform), 88–100% (ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2). Radiographical success 61–100%, 75–100%, 79–100%, 81–100% respectively.

**Pooled meta-analysis (Ca(OH)2/iodoform vs. comparator), overall:**

| Follow-up | vs. ZOE — clinical OR (95% CI) | vs. ZOE — radiographical OR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| 6 months | 1.84 (0.51–6.61), p=0.35 (NS) | 0.71 (0.34–1.51), p=0.38 (NS) |
| 12 months | 0.78 (0.21–2.88), p=0.07 (NS) | 0.39 (0.07–2.35), p=0.31 (NS) |
| ≥18 months | 0.52 (0.17–1.59), p=0.25 (NS) | 0.13 (0.06–1.61), p=0.16 (NS) |

No significant differences vs. ZOE/iodoform or ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 subgroups at any timepoint in the overall pooled analysis.

**Quality-stratified findings (the key clinically actionable result):**
- At 6-month radiographical success, high-quality studies favored ZOE over Ca(OH)2/iodoform (OR 0.10, 95% CI 0.01–0.83, p=0.03); moderate-quality studies showed no difference.
- At 12-month follow-up, high-quality studies showed statistically significantly higher clinical and radiographical success for ZOE and ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 vs. Ca(OH)2/iodoform (p<0.05); moderate-quality studies showed no difference.
- At ≥18 months, high-quality studies: clinical OR 0.21 (95% CI 0.07–0.69, p=0.010) vs. ZOE, and OR 0.10 (95% CI 0.02–0.45, p=0.003) vs. ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 — both favoring the comparator over Ca(OH)2/iodoform. Radiographical success also favored ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 (OR 0.09, 95% CI 0.03–0.30, p=0.0001) but not ZOE alone (p=0.48).
- Heterogeneity: strong at 12 months (clinical I²=45%, radiographical I²=62%) and ≥18 months (clinical I²=58%, radiographical I²=42%); low at 6 months.
- Egger's test: no significant small-study effect except radiographical success at ≥18 months (p=0.02), suggesting possible publication bias there.
- Subgroup analyses (irrigation type, tooth type) showed no significant effect on outcomes.

**Level of evidence / recommendation**: Because no significant overall difference was found ≥18 months, the authors state Ca(OH)2/iodoform, ZOE, and ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 "may be used interchangeably" (level Ib, grade A) — but recommend ZOE or ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 for teeth with longer expected lifespan (level Ia, grade A), reflecting the high-quality-study-driven signal that ZOE-based materials outperform Ca(OH)2/iodoform over longer follow-up.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 2 of 15 studies were rated high-quality (low risk of bias); most of the pooled evidence comes from moderate-quality studies with small sample sizes, no sample-size calculation, unclear design, or incomplete randomization/blinding.
- Moderate-to-high heterogeneity at 12- and ≥18-month follow-up, attributable to differing eligibility criteria, dropout handling, radiographical assessment methods, study design (RCT vs. non-randomized, blinded vs. unblinded), and clinical protocol variation (irrigation, visit number, restorative material, tooth type, follow-up timing).
- Funnel-plot asymmetry at 12- and ≥18-month periods suggests possible publication bias; Egger's test confirmed this quantitatively for ≥18-month radiographical outcomes only.
- Five studies had to be excluded from the meta-analysis (of the 15 in the SR) due to missing or incorrect data — reducing the effective evidence base for pooled estimates.
- Authors explicitly call for more high-quality RCTs with long-term follow-up before a definitive material recommendation can be made.
- The review only covers Ca(OH)2/iodoform pastes marketed as Metapex or Vitapex; it does not generalize to other calcium hydroxide-based root filling formulations without iodoform.

## 6. Related Work

- Pozos-Guillén et al. — SR+MA on intracanal irrigation solutions in primary pulpectomy, cited for concordant finding that irrigant choice does not significantly affect success.
- Barja-Fidalgo et al. — permanent-tooth pulpectomy success by tooth type (maxillary vs. mandibular), cited as concordant precedent for the present study's tooth-type subgroup finding.
- Al-Namankany et al.; Rajasekharan, Vandenbulcke & Martens — cited regarding generally poor RCT reporting quality in pediatric dentistry journals, contextualizing why only 2/15 included studies were high-quality.
- CONSORT group (Moher et al.; Schulz et al.) — cited regarding the general finding that low-quality RCTs can overestimate treatment effect by up to 35%, motivating the quality-stratified subgroup analysis presented here.

## 7. Glossary

- **Ca(OH)2/iodoform paste**: A resorbable root canal filling paste combining calcium hydroxide (antibacterial, healing-promoting) with iodoform (radiopaque, resorbable, antibacterial); marketed as Vitapex (Neo Dental Chemical Products, Japan) or Metapex (Meta Biomed, South Korea).
- **ZOE (zinc oxide eugenol)**: Traditional primary-tooth pulpectomy root filling material; disadvantages include low resorption rate and potential periapical irritation/necrosis and successor tooth bud deflection if not resorbed appropriately.
- **Pulpectomy**: Complete removal of pulp tissue from crown and root canals, followed by canal filling — indicated for irreversible pulpitis or pulp necrosis in primary teeth with a permanent successor.
- **PROSPERO**: International prospective register of systematic reviews.
- **CONSORT**: Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials checklist, used here (modified, 27-point) for study quality appraisal.
- **Mantel–Haenszel method**: Statistical method for pooling odds ratios across studies in meta-analysis.
- **I² statistic**: Measure of heterogeneity across pooled studies (percentage of variability due to true differences rather than chance).
- **Egger's test**: Statistical test for funnel-plot asymmetry, used to detect publication/small-study bias.
