## Why Ingested

The wiki holds several primary systematic reviews on implant antibiotic prophylaxis ([[wiki/drug/antibiotics/torof-2023-antibiotic-dental-implant-procedures-sr-ma]], [[wiki/drug/antibiotics/esposito-2013-antibiotics-dental-implant-placement-cochrane]], [[wiki/drug/antibiotics/momand-2024-antibiotic-prophylaxis-early-implant-failure]]) but no procedure-stratified guideline that turns those pooled findings into a prescribable drug/dose/timing table — and, critically, none that spell out what to give a **penicillin-allergic** patient across the different implant procedure types. This SEI consensus (the first clinical practice guideline on preventive antibiotic therapy in implant procedures) fills that gap and contextualizes the sinus-lift axis alongside [[wiki/overviews/sinus-augmentation-perioperative-pharmacology-overview]].

## Three-line Summary

Consensus report / clinical practice guideline from the Spanish Society of Implants (SEI): a 12-member expert panel plus 3 external reviewers graded the evidence (SIGN levels of evidence + GRADE recommendation grades, PRISMA-structured, MEDLINE Jan 2020–Feb 2021) across 6 PICO questions covering implant placement without anatomical constraints, immediate implants, sinus lifts, guided bone regeneration, the prosthetic phase, and penicillin-allergic patients.

Core recommendations: preoperative 2–3 g amoxicillin 1 h before surgery for routine placement (GRADE A; not prescribing is also acceptable, GRADE B), no postoperative/perioperative extension, no prosthetic-phase antibiotics (GRADE D); for penicillin-allergic patients the panel recommends azithromycin 500 mg 1 h preoperatively for placement (GRADE C, avoid clindamycin — up to 6× higher failure/infection risk), azithromycin/clarithromycin/metronidazole regimens for immediate implants (GRADE D), and ciprofloxacin 500 mg/12 h × 9 days for sinus lifts (GRADE D).

Evidence for every procedure except routine placement is weak (LoE 2+ to 4, few or no control groups); PAT reduces early implant failure (OR 0.08–0.45; NNT 24–50) but not postoperative infection (NNT 143), and the whole document is framed as an antimicrobial-stewardship corrective to habitual over-prescription.

## 세줄요약

스페인 임플란트학회(SEI)의 합의 보고서 / 임상진료지침 — 전문가 12인 패널 + 외부 검토자 3인이 SIGN 근거수준 + GRADE 권고등급으로 근거를 평가(PRISMA 구조, MEDLINE 2020-01~2021-02), 6개 PICO 질문(해부학적 제약 없는 식립, 즉시 임플란트, 상악동 거상, 골유도재생술(Guided Bone Regeneration, GBR), 보철 단계, 페니실린 알레르기 환자)을 다룸.

핵심 권고: 통상 식립은 수술 1시간 전 아목시실린(Amoxicillin) 2~3 g 술전 단회(GRADE A; 미투여도 허용 GRADE B), 술후·주술기 연장 불필요, 보철 단계 항생제 불필요(GRADE D); 페니실린 알레르기 환자는 식립 시 아지트로마이신(Azithromycin) 500 mg 술전 1시간(GRADE C, 클린다마이신(Clindamycin)은 실패·감염 위험 최대 6배로 회피), 즉시 임플란트는 아지트로마이신/클라리트로마이신/메트로니다졸 요법(GRADE D), 상악동 거상은 시프로플록사신(Ciprofloxacin) 500 mg/12h × 9일(GRADE D).

통상 식립을 제외한 모든 술식의 근거는 약함(근거수준 2+~4, 대조군 희소/부재); 예방적 항생제요법(Preventive Antibiotic Therapy, PAT)은 조기 임플란트 실패는 줄이나(OR 0.08~0.45; 치료필요수 NNT 24~50) 술후 감염은 못 줄임(NNT 143), 문서 전체가 관행적 과잉처방에 대한 항균제 스튜어드십 교정의 틀로 서술됨.

## 1. Document Information

- **Journal**: Antibiotics (Basel) 2022;11(5):655
- **DOI**: 10.3390/antibiotics11050655
- **PMID / PMCID**: 35625298 / PMC9138127
- **Institution**: Spanish Society of Implants (SEI — Sociedad Española de Implantes); lead authors Complutense University of Madrid / University of Seville, Spain
- **Type**: Consensus report summarizing the SEI clinical practice guideline; endorsed by SEGER, SOPIO, SIOLA, FIIO and the General Council of Dentists of Spain (12 Dec 2021)
- **Methodology frameworks**: PRISMA (structure), SIGN checklists + level-of-evidence grading (1++ to 4), GRADE (recommendation grades A–D)

## 2. Key Contributions

- First published clinical practice guideline / consensus on preventive antibiotic therapy (PAT) specifically stratified by dental-implant procedure type.
- Provides an explicit drug/dose/posology table for **penicillin-allergic** patients that differs by procedure (immediate implant, sinus lift, routine placement), rather than a single blanket alternative.
- Formally recommends **against** clindamycin as the reflex penicillin-allergy substitute in implant surgery, citing observational evidence of markedly higher implant failure and infection.
- Frames PAT prescribing as an antimicrobial-resistance / stewardship problem: quantifies that PAT prevents one early failure per 24–50 healthy patients treated but does not reduce postoperative infection (NNT 143).
- Recommends **no** antibiotic prophylaxis for the implant prosthetic phase (second-stage surgery, impressions, prosthesis delivery).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Consensus report / clinical practice guideline; expert panel of 12 dentists/stomatologists + 3 independent external reviewers.
- **Evidence appraisal**: two reviewers per study using SIGN critical-appraisal templates, disagreements resolved by a third; studies ranked 1++ / 1+ / 1- / 2++ / 2+ / 2- / 3 / 4.
- **Recommendation grading**: GRADE approach → A (≥1 meta-analysis/SR/CT rated 1++, or body of 1+ with high consistency); B (2++ directly applicable, or extrapolated from 1++/1+); C (2+ directly applicable, or extrapolated from 2++); D (level 3 or 4, or extrapolated from 2+).
- **Search**: MEDLINE via PubMed, elaborated January 2020–February 2021; separate individualized search strategy and filters per PICO question; reference lists hand-searched. Evidence pyramid descended (or extrapolated from tooth/periodontal/endodontic data) where implant-specific literature was absent.
- **Scope**: 6 PICO questions — (1) placement without anatomical constraints, (2) immediate implants, (3) sinus lifts with 1- or 2-stage placement, (4) GBR/bone augmentation with 1- or 2-stage placement, (5) prosthetic phase, (6) penicillin-allergic patients / antibiotics other than amoxicillin.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**PICO 1 — routine placement, healthy patients (best evidence base: 11 SR/MA, one rated 1++):**
- All regimens reduced early implant failure vs no PAT/placebo (OR 0.08–0.45); significant benefit only for **preoperative** antibiotics (LoE 1+).
- No added benefit from amoxicillin + clavulanic acid, or from postoperative/perioperative dosing — only added adverse-reaction risk (PAT patients ~1.8% higher adverse-reaction risk).
- Absolute early-failure reduction modest: 1.8–4% (LoE 1++). NNT to prevent one early failure 24–50; NNT to prevent one postoperative infection 143 (100 for preoperative amoxicillin, 143 for postoperative).
- Romandini network meta-analysis: 3 g amoxicillin 1 h preoperatively is the most effective protocol (OR 0.41); the common 2 g regimen has only a 0.2% probability of being the best.
- **Recommendation**: 2–3 g amoxicillin 1 h preoperatively (GRADE A); not prescribing is not a wrong approach in certain simple cases in healthy patients (GRADE B).

**PICO 2 — immediate implants (6 SR, LoE 2++):**
- Immediate implant failure 5.1% vs 1.1% for delayed placement (all failures early); trend to lower survival when postoperative PAT not given (RR 0.93). One study: 5× higher wound-complication risk in immediate implants (26.1% vs 5.3%).
- Regimens across studies heterogeneous (total PAT duration 6–14 days; up to 31 days in one review); no specific protocol validated. Lang et al.: annual failure 1.9% preoperative-only vs 0.5% postoperative vs 0.8% perioperative (p = 0.002).
- **Recommendation (GRADE D overall)**: loading dose 2 or 3 g amoxicillin 1 h before surgery (GRADE B) + 500 mg/8 h for 5–7 postoperative days (GRADE D), extrapolated from endodontic prescribing.
- **Penicillin-allergic (confirmed true allergy), GRADE D**: azithromycin 500 mg 1 h before → 250 mg/24 h × 5–7 days; **or** clarithromycin 500 mg 1 h before → 250 mg/12 h × 5–7 days; **or** metronidazole 1 g 1 h preoperatively → 500 mg/6 h × 5–7 days.

**PICO 3 — sinus lifts (very limited evidence, LoE 2+ and 4):**
- PAT does not significantly change implant or graft failure vs no PAT (Zinser). Schneiderian membrane perforation rate 18.3–23.5%; perforation raises graft-failure odds (OR 16.82) and sinusitis/infection risk (11.3% vs 1.4%).
- Clindamycin associated with 6% graft failure vs 0% with amoxicillin (LoE 3). Sinus isolates show greater resistance to macrolides, fosfomycin, penicillin G; sensitive to amoxicillin/clavulanate, ampicillin, ciprofloxacin.
- Carreño-Carreño: 227 lateral-window sinus lifts, no preoperative PAT, postoperative amoxicillin 1 g/12 h stopped at 48 h in 81.9% (sterile subsinus cultures).
- **Recommendation**: no perforation → 2–3 g amoxicillin 1 h preoperatively sufficient (GRADE B). Assume perforation (usually unpredictable) → amoxicillin/clavulanic acid 875/125 mg/12 h starting 1 day preoperatively, then 875/125 mg/8 h for 7 days (GRADE D).
- **Penicillin-allergic, GRADE D**: ciprofloxacin 500 mg/12 h × 9 days (macrolides not justified because sinus flora is macrolide-resistant; an expert panel's clarithromycin + metronidazole suggestion is explicitly not endorsed).

**PICO 4 — GBR / bone augmentation (1 SR rated 1++, 4 RCTs):**
- SR (1++): PAT improves postoperative infection rate; unclear whether a single dose suffices or dosing must extend past surgery day.
- Payer multicentre RCT (1++): perioperative amoxicillin (2 g preop + 500 mg/8 h × 3 days) vs placebo — no significant difference in failure; suppuration higher in control clinically. RCTs otherwise used clindamycin 600 mg preop ± 300 mg/6 h × 1 day.
- **Recommendation**: preoperative PAT reduces postoperative infection vs perioperative (GRADE A); single dose 2 or 3 g amoxicillin 1 h before surgery to reduce single-stage implant failure and bacterial contamination of grafted particles, also for two-stage (GRADE C). No procedure-specific penicillin-allergy regimen given here — the PICO 6 azithromycin recommendation covers GBR (its scope includes native bone with simultaneous GBR).

**PICO 5 — prosthetic phase:**
- No implant-specific studies; extrapolated from periodontal surgery (NNT to prevent one infection = 203 in access/regenerative surgery; 10th European Workshop: systemic peri-/postoperative PAT not indicated in periodontal plastic surgery, though extensive surgery may warrant local or systemic PAT).
- **Recommendation**: PAT in second-stage surgery, impression-taking and prosthesis placement is not justified (GRADE D).

**PICO 6 — penicillin-allergic patients / non-amoxicillin antibiotics for placement (5 observational studies; allergy confirmed by testing in only 1):**
- Clindamycin in "allergic" patients vs amoxicillin in non-allergic: implant failure RR 3.34–3.84, patient-level failure 5.2% vs 18.9% (Salomo-Coll: 8% vs 24.7% at implant level), HR 2.16 (French), OR 3.10 (French follow-up: failure 0.8% vs 2.1%, postoperative infection 0.6% vs 3.4% — 6× higher). Wagenberg & Froum: 5.7× higher immediate-implant failure from infection with clindamycin (8.5% vs 3%).
- Azithromycin 500 mg vs amoxicillin 2 g (both 1 h preop): detectable azithromycin in gingival/peri-implant crevicular fluid at day 6 (amoxicillin undetectable); azithromycin also lowers G-CSF, IL-6, IL-8, MIP-1, IP-10 during healing and has higher bioavailability than amoxicillin or clindamycin.
- **Recommendation**: clindamycin carries a significantly elevated risk of osseointegration failure and up to 6× higher infection risk; immediate implants are further compromised in these patients (GRADE C). Pending further study, **azithromycin 500 mg 1 h before surgery** is recommended (GRADE C).

**Conclusion**: PAT is indicated for all implant surgical procedures except the prosthetic phase; type and dose must be adapted to the procedure; PAT never replaces or relaxes aseptic/sterile technique.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Shortage of studies with control groups — external validity of most recommendations (especially PICO 2–6) must be confirmed by future trials.
- Evidence for immediate implants, sinus lifts, GBR and the penicillin-allergic population is largely LoE 2+ to 4; several regimens are extrapolated from endodontic, periodontal or chronic-sinusitis literature rather than implant trials.
- Penicillin allergy in the underlying studies was almost always self-reported (allergy testing in only 1 of 5 PICO 6 studies), inflating the apparent penalty of the "allergic" label; true confirmed penicillin allergy is far rarer.
- No dose established for ciprofloxacin as implant/sinus prophylaxis (the 500 mg/12 h × 9 days figure is borrowed from chronic-sinusitis treatment).
- Newer placebo-controlled, double-blinded RCT-only syntheses (held in this wiki: momand-2024, dubedout-2025) find no significant early-failure benefit even for routine placement, tempering this consensus's GRADE A for PICO 1.

## 6. Related Work

- Torof 2023 (SR+MA): preoperative single 2 g amoxicillin for implant placement, no benefit from postoperative extension — this consensus turns that into a graded, procedure-stratified protocol.
- Esposito 2013 (Cochrane): preoperative amoxicillin 2–3 g reduces implant failure (RR 0.33, NNT 25); a foundational input to PICO 1.
- Momand 2024 / Dubedout 2025: placebo-RCT-restricted syntheses finding no significant early-failure reduction — partial counterweight to PICO 1's GRADE A.
- Mohammadpour 2025 (scoping SR, oral surgery): single-dose amoxicillin standard, no >24 h extension, clindamycin only for penicillin allergy — independently converges with this consensus.
- Díaz 2025 (umbrella review, sinus lift antibiotics): prophylaxis benefit concentrated in high-risk sinus lifts (membrane perforation, lateral window) — consistent with PICO 3's perforation-contingent escalation.

## 7. Glossary

- **PAT (Preventive Antibiotic Therapy)**: preventive administration of antibiotics in healthy patients to avoid early implant failure and/or postoperative infection; the consensus's preferred term over "antibiotic prophylaxis".
- **DI**: dental implant.
- **GBR (Guided Bone Regeneration)**: bone augmentation using a barrier membrane, with or without simultaneous implant placement.
- **SIGN**: Scottish Intercollegiate Guidelines Network — critical-appraisal checklists and level-of-evidence grading (1++ to 4) used here.
- **GRADE**: Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation — recommendation grades A (strongest) to D (weakest) in this document.
- **NNT (Number Needed to Treat)**: patients who must receive PAT to prevent one adverse event vs control; 24–50 for early implant failure, 143 for postoperative infection.
- **SEI**: Sociedad Española de Implantes (Spanish Society of Implants).
- **Schneiderian membrane**: sinus mucosal lining; perforation during sinus lift markedly raises graft infection/failure risk (OR 16.82).
- **True penicillin allergy**: allergy confirmed by specific testing, as opposed to a self-reported/self-declared label — the distinction the consensus stresses before committing a patient to inferior alternatives.
