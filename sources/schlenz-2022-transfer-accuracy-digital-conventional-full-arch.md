## Why Ingested
Fills a specific gap in the [[digital-workflow/alkadi-2023-intraoral-scanner-accuracy-factors]] factor review: fixed orthodontic appliances (buccal brackets ± wire, metal vs ceramic) are not among the factors that review catalogs, and this in vitro study is the first to test them against a proper reference (not a best-fit superimposition) per ISO 5725-1 trueness/precision methodology.

## Three-line Summary
In vitro reference aid–based study (phantom head, human-tooth mandibular model, 12 repetitions × 5 settings) comparing five intraoral scanners (CS3600, Primescan, Trios 4, Medit i500, Emerald S) and conventional alginate impression (CAI) for full-arch transfer accuracy, with and without fixed orthodontic appliances (FOAs: metal/ceramic brackets, ± wire).
All IOSs showed lower deviations than CAI in every bracket setting (IOS range 28±23 to 141±140 µm vs. CAI 103±103 to 212±204 µm), except natural teeth (group A) where CAI was most accurate (21±20 µm); Medit i500 had the largest deviations among IOSs but still beat CAI; Primescan was fastest overall (92–115 s) while CAI+plaster-casting took longest (224–238 s); all group/technique comparisons p<0.001.
Clinically, IOS full-arch scanning is recommended over alginate for patients with fixed orthodontic appliances on both accuracy and time-efficiency grounds, but the in vitro design (no saliva, single trained operator, occlusal-surface-only reference) means clinical re-verification is still needed.

## 세줄요약
기준체(reference aid) 기반 in vitro 연구(팬텀헤드, 실치아 하악 모델, 5개 세팅 × 12회 반복): 5종 구강스캐너(CS3600, Primescan, Trios 4, Medit i500, Emerald S)와 알지네이트 인상(CAI)의 전악(full-arch) 전이정확도(transfer accuracy)를 고정성 교정장치(Fixed Orthodontic Appliance, FOA: 금속/세라믹 브라켓, 와이어 유무) 유무별로 비교.
브라켓이 있는 모든 세팅에서 구강스캐너가 CAI보다 편차가 작았음(구강스캐너 28±23~141±140 µm vs CAI 103±103~212±204 µm), 단 자연치(A군)에서는 CAI가 가장 정확(21±20 µm); Medit i500이 구강스캐너 중 편차가 가장 컸지만 CAI보다는 우수; Primescan이 전체 중 가장 빠름(92–115초), CAI+석고모형 제작이 가장 오래 걸림(224–238초); 모든 군/기법 간 비교 p<0.001.
임상적으로 고정성 교정장치 환자에서는 정확도·시간효율 양쪽 모두 알지네이트보다 구강스캐너가 권장되나, in vitro 설계(타액 없음, 단일 숙련 술자, 교합면 기준체만 사용)라 임상 재검증이 필요.

- **Journal**: Clinical Oral Investigations (2023) 27:273–283 (online first 15 Sept 2022; received 1 Mar 2022, accepted 6 Sept 2022)

- **Institution**: Department of Prosthodontics and Department of Orthodontics, Dental Clinic of the Justus Liebig University Giessen, Germany

## 2. Key Contributions
- First study using a standardized reference aid–based method (ISO 5725-1 trueness/precision) rather than best-fit superimposition to assess transfer accuracy of full-arch impressions in the presence of fixed orthodontic appliances (FOAs).
- Systematically varied bracket material (metal vs. ceramic) and wire presence/absence across 5 test-model settings (A: natural teeth; B: metal brackets, no wire; C: metal brackets + wire; D: ceramic brackets, no wire; E: ceramic brackets + wire), each captured by 5 IOSs plus CAI, 12 repetitions per group.
- Demonstrated that all 5 tested IOSs outperform conventional alginate impression (CAI) in every FOA setting, despite CAI being most accurate on natural teeth without appliances.
- Quantified required chair-side/laboratory time per technique per group, showing IOSs (except Medit i500) require 16% less time scanning brackets+wire than brackets alone, while CAI accuracy degrades sharply with wire present.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In vitro comparative study (bench/laboratory, phantom head simulation) — not a clinical trial.
- **Test model**: Frasaco ANA-4 mandibular model modified with real extracted human teeth (5–5) embedded in acrylic resin; ethics approval Ref. no. 143/09, Justus Liebig University Giessen.
- **Groups (n=5)**: A) natural teeth; B) metal brackets (Tip-Edge PLUS Stainless Steel) without wire; C) metal brackets + wire (0.46 mm/.018″); D) ceramic brackets (Tip-Edge PLUS Aesthetic) without wire; E) ceramic brackets + wire. Molar bands cemented on first molars.
- **Reference**: 4 high-precision bearing spheres (ISO 3290-1 steel balls, roundness 5000±5.63 µm) bonded to occlusal surfaces; reference dataset established via 10 measurements on a coordinate measurement machine (CMM, Thome Präzision) and stored as IGES; linear distances D1_2, D1_3, D1_4, D2_3, D2_4, D3_4 between the 4 spheres (S1–S4) used as the accuracy metric.
- **Impression techniques (n=6)**: 5 IOSs — CS3600 ("CAR"), Primescan ("PRI"), Trios 4 POD wireless ("TIO"), Medit i500 ("MED"), Planmeca Emerald S ("EME") — same fixed scan path (occlusal → oral → buccal) each; plus conventional alginate impression (CAI, Cavex Orthotrace) cast in type IV dental stone (Fujirock EP) and measured on the CMM.
- **n**: 300 IOS scan datasets (12 repetitions × 5 groups × 5 scanners) analyzed in GOM Inspect (v2020, best-fit sphere construction, Gauss 3-sigma); 60 plaster casts (12 × 5 groups) measured by CMM for CAI.
- **Outcomes**: Trueness (mean deviation from reference) and precision (SD) per ISO 5725-1 for the 6 linear inter-sphere distances; required time (s) for impression taking and, for CAI, additional plaster-casting time (CPC).
- **Statistics**: SPSS v26, GENLINMIXED procedure (two-/three-way ANOVA, heterogeneous variances), one-way ANOVA per group/distance with 6-level technique factor, non-parametric Kruskal–Wallis due to extreme outliers; α=0.05. Single operator (M.M.) for all impressions and measurements — no inter/intra-rater reliability computed.

Both null hypotheses were rejected: significant differences existed between the 6 techniques for transfer accuracy (p<0.001 in all groups) and for required time (p<0.001 in all groups). Maximum deviation outliers were <0.25 mm for IOSs across all groups. CAR, PRI, TIO, and MED needed up to 16% less scanning time for brackets+wire vs. brackets alone (possible wire-as-guidance effect); CAI accuracy, by contrast, degraded substantially once wire was present (group C: 202±163 µm; group E: 212±204 µm).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- In vitro phantom-head design lacks clinical patient factors: saliva flow, jaw/tongue/cheek movement, challenging anatomy.
- Only occlusal-surface reference spheres used (no buccal/lingual reference structure currently exists in the literature); buccal bracket-region accuracy itself was not directly measured against a reference.
- Single experienced operator performed all impressions and measurements — no inter/intra-rater reliability assessed.
- Authors explicitly call for clinical re-investigation to verify these in vitro findings, particularly regarding the influence of saliva.

## 6. Related Work
- Kuhr et al. 2016 / Schmidt et al. 2020, 2021: established the reference aid–based method this study builds directly on for assessing full-arch impression transfer accuracy in patients and phantom models.
- Song & Kim 2020: in vitro study with artificial saliva found lowest deviation in the no-FOA group (opposite direction from this study) and found resin/metal brackets worse than ceramic — concordant on bracket-material direction but discordant on whether FOA presence increases or the no-FOA group is best; attributed by the current authors to the artificial-saliva reference method used by Song & Kim.
- Heo & Kim 2021: found ceramic brackets had the highest deviations (opposite of the concordance seen with Song & Kim and this study), using best-fit superimposition rather than a proper reference — a methodological difference the current authors flag as a likely cause.
- Kang et al. 2021 / Kim et al. 2021 (in vivo, best-fit superimposition, before/after bracket bonding): shorter intercanine distance showed less deviation than longer intermolar distance, consistent with this study's finding of D1_2/D3_4 (short) vs. D1_4 (long intermolar) deviation pattern.
- [[digital-workflow/alkadi-2023-intraoral-scanner-accuracy-factors]]: narrative review of IOS accuracy factors (arch size, tooth presence, operator experience, powder, technology) — does not include fixed orthodontic appliances as a factor; this paper extends that factor list.

## 7. Glossary
- **FOA**: Fixed Orthodontic Appliance — buccal brackets (metal or ceramic) with or without archwire, bonded to teeth.
- **CAI**: Conventional Alginate Impression.
- **CPC**: Conventional Plaster model Casting (time to cast the alginate impression in dental stone, excluding setting/hardening time).
- **Trueness**: Closeness of agreement between the mean of measurement results and the reference (true) value, per ISO 5725-1.
- **Precision**: Closeness of agreement between independent measurement results under specified conditions (expressed here as SD), per ISO 5725-1.
- **Reference aid**: A test jig with 4 high-precision bonded spheres whose inter-sphere distances, measured independently by CMM, serve as the ground-truth reference against which impression/scan datasets are compared — avoids the error-masking of best-fit superimposition.
