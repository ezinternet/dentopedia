## Why Ingested

The soft-tissue companion to [[wiki/immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2013-ridge-alterations-post-extraction-esthetic]] — same group, same 8-week post-extraction CBCT / 3D-superimposition design, same thin-vs-thick bone phenotype split, but measuring the facial **soft tissue** rather than the bone. The 2013 page reports the 7.5 mm vertical bone loss that drives the phenotype split and concludes that thin-wall sites should be redirected away from immediate placement; this paper shows that the *same* 7.5 mm bone loss is masked by a ~7-fold spontaneous soft-tissue thickening in that exact phenotype, so soft-tissue-only assessment reports no phenotype difference at all (1.6 mm vs 1.1 mm total loss, NS). That is a clinical reframing of the held 2013 bottom line (`refines`: the bone finding stands, the soft-tissue read of it changes) and it is not present anywhere in the folder's review page [[wiki/immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2017-dimensional-bone-soft-tissue-post-extraction]], which covers the bone biology but not the masking effect (`extends`).

## Three-line Summary

Prospective clinical study (J Dent Res 2015;94(9 Suppl); the abstract does not report the sample size) — sequential 3-dimensional digital surface model superimposition of the facial soft tissue and the underlying bone in esthetic-zone extraction sites across an 8-week healing period, with digital impressions plus CBCT, stratified by thin vs thick bone phenotype.

Baseline soft tissue thickness at extraction was essentially identical between phenotypes (0.7 mm thin, 0.8 mm thick) but then diverged: thin phenotypes thickened ~7-fold by 8 weeks while thick phenotypes showed no change in soft tissue dimensions, so measured vertical soft tissue loss reached only 1.6 mm and **concealed 7.5 mm of underlying vertical bone resorption** — with no significant difference in total tissue loss between thin and thick phenotypes at 2, 4, 6 or 8 weeks.

More than 51% of the dimensional alterations had already occurred within the first 2 weeks of healing; the spontaneous thickening is proposed as advantageous for subsequent bone regeneration and implant therapy in high-esthetic-demand sites, but the practical corollary is that a clinically acceptable soft-tissue contour is not evidence of adequate bone.

## 세줄요약

전향적 임상 연구 (J Dent Res 2015;94(9 Suppl), 초록에 표본수 미기재) — 심미부 발치 부위에서 디지털 임프레션 (Digital Impression) + 선형단층촬영 (Cone Beam Computed Tomography, CBCT)을 이용한 **순차적 3차원 (3-Dimensional, 3D) 디지털 표면 모델 중첩 (Superimposition)** 으로 순측 연조직과 기저골을 8주 치유기 간 추적, 박벽형(thin bone phenotype) vs 후벽형(thick bone phenotype) 비교.

발치 당시 연조직 두께는 두 표현형에서 거의 동일(박벽형 0.7 mm, 후벽형 0.8 mm)했으나 8주 후 갈렸다 — 박벽형에서만 연조직 두께가 **약 7배 증가**하고 후벽형에서는 변화가 없어, 측정된 수직 연조직 소실은 1.6 mm에 그쳤고 그 아래 **7.5 mm의 수직 골소실을 가려버렸다(masking)**. 그 결과 총 조직 소실은 2·4·6·8주 모두에서 박벽형과 후벽형 간 유의한 차이가 없었다.

치유 2주 이내에 치수 변화의 51% 이상이 이미 발생했고, 이러한 자발적 연조직 증厚的 두꺼워짐은 심미적 요구도가 높은 부위의 후속 골재생·임플란트 치료에 유리할 수 있으나 — 실무적으로는 **임상적으로 괜찮아 보이는 연조직 윤곽이 골 충분성의 증거가 아니다**는 함의가 남는다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study to analyse the **interplay** between facial soft tissue dimensions and underlying bone anatomy during post-extraction healing, rather than bone change alone
- Establishes that baseline facial soft tissue thickness does **not** discriminate thin from thick bone phenotypes (0.7 vs 0.8 mm) — phenotype is a bone-based, not a soft-tissue-based, classification
- Quantifies spontaneous soft tissue thickening as a **phenotype-specific** response: ~7-fold increase in thin-bone sites, no change in thick-bone sites
- Demonstrates that this thickening is a **confounder of outcome measurement**, not merely a cosmetic event: it reduces measured vertical soft tissue loss to 1.6 mm while 7.5 mm of vertical bone resorbs underneath, and it erases the thin-vs-thick difference in total tissue loss at every timepoint
- Quantifies the **timing** of postextraction dimensional change: >51% occurs within the first 2 weeks

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Prospective clinical study; sequential 3-dimensional digital surface model superimposition (no randomisation, no control arm — thin vs thick phenotype is a pre-existing anatomical stratification)
- **Modality**: Digital impressions (soft tissue surface) + CBCT (underlying bone), superimposed over time
- **n**: **not stated in the abstract** — do not assume the 2013 sibling's n=39; the abstract reports no recruitment, inclusion criteria, drop-outs, or per-phenotype counts
- **Window**: 8-week healing period; measurements at 2, 4, 6 and 8 weeks are reported for the total-tissue-loss comparison
- **Outcomes**: facial soft tissue thickness, vertical soft tissue loss, vertical bone resorption, total tissue loss (soft + bone combined), compared between thin and thick bone phenotypes
- **Instrumentation detail**: impression/scanning protocol, voxel size, registration method, and the exact definition of the thin/thick thickness cutoff are all inside the paywalled full text and are not available from the abstract

| Measurement | Thin bone phenotype | Thick bone phenotype |
|---|---|---|
| Soft tissue thickness at extraction | 0.7 mm | 0.8 mm — abstract: "similar" |
| Soft tissue dimension change over 8 wk | **~7-fold increase in thickness** | unchanged |
| Vertical soft tissue loss (measured) | **1.6 mm** (net, after thickening) | not given numerically |
| Underlying vertical bone resorption | **7.5 mm** (concealed by the thickening) | not given numerically |

- No significant difference in **total tissue loss** between thin and thick phenotypes at **2, 4, 6 and 8 weeks**
- **>51%** of the dimensional alterations occurred within the **first 2 weeks** of healing
- No p-values, confidence intervals, or per-phenotype group sizes appear in the abstract. Do **not** import a thick-phenotype number from elsewhere: the 1.1 mm figure that usually travels with this result is a **bone**-loss value from the 2013 sibling study ([[wiki/immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2013-ridge-alterations-post-extraction-esthetic]]), not a soft-tissue-loss value measured in this paper

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only ingest** — no full text retrieved (paywalled ITI supplement). Method detail (phenotype cutoff, impression/scanning protocol, registration accuracy, statistical test used) is unavailable, so the reliability of the 0.7 mm / 0.8 mm / 1.6 mm / 7.5 mm figures cannot be checked against dispersion measures
- **Sample size not reported** in the abstract — statistical power, phenotype subgroup sizes, and drop-outs are unknown, so the "no significant difference between phenotypes" result must be read without knowing whether it is a true null or an underpowered one
- Single research group, single centre (implied), and a young-adult anterior-maxilla population typical of the Bern ITI case series — generalisability to older patients, thick-periodontal-phenotype sites, or posterior sites is untested
- The masking effect is itself a **measurement limitation** of the whole postextraction-dimension literature: any trial or cohort using soft tissue landmarks alone to grade postextraction change will systematically under-detect the underlying bone deficit in thin-wall sites
- No intervention arm — the study characterises spontaneous healing only, and the "advantages for subsequent bone regeneration and implant therapies" claim is an inference from biology, not a tested outcome (no graft, no implant, no randomised comparison)
- Follow-up stops at 8 weeks; the durability of the thickened tissue, and whether it persists through implant placement and long-term function, is not addressed

## 6. Related Work

- Chappuis 2013 (JDR Clin Res Suppl 2013;92(12 Suppl)): same group/design, bone-side twin of this study — 7.5 mm vs 1.1 mm vertical bone loss by phenotype; this paper confirms the same 7.5 mm and explains why soft-tissue metrics do not see it (`refines`)
- Chappuis, Araújo & Buser 2017 (Periodontol 2000): same group's review of the bone/soft-tissue biology — carries the <1 mm facial wall prevalence and ~2 mm stability threshold but not the soft-tissue masking finding (`extends`)
- Chappuis et al. 2018 (J Dent Res): 10-year outcome of Type 2 placement plus simultaneous contour augmentation with GBR, facial bone wall 0 → 1.67 mm — the treatment-side counterpart to the unmasked bone deficiency documented here
- Buser et al. 2017 (Periodontol 2000): companion ITI placement-timing review; supplies the selection-criteria framework against which the masked phenotype should be judged

## 7. Glossary

- **Thin bone phenotype**: an anatomical classification of the extraction site by facial/buccal bone wall thickness at the time of extraction (used here to stratify sites; the abstract does not state the cutoff value)
- **Digital surface model superimposition**: registering serial digital impressions against each other to quantify millimetre-scale change in soft tissue contour over time
- **CBCT superimposition**: registering serial cone beam CT datasets against each other to quantify bone dimensional change over time
- **Spontaneous soft tissue thickening**: increase in facial mucosal thickness occurring without any surgical or grafting intervention during postextraction healing
- **Masking**: the situation where soft tissue compensation offsets a hard tissue deficit, so a soft-tissue-based measurement underestimates true tissue loss
- **Total tissue loss**: the combined vertical loss of soft tissue plus underlying bone at a given site and timepoint — the metric that reveals no phenotype difference here
