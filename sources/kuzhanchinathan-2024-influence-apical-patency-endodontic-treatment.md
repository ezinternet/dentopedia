## Why Ingested

This 2024 systematic review is the first SR to aggregate clinical evidence on apical patency (AP) and endodontic treatment outcome, filling a gap in the [[endodontics/shaping/shaping|shaping]] literature where AP was previously discussed mainly in technique-focused or in vitro studies.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (5 clinical studies, 4370 roots) evaluating whether maintaining apical patency during endodontic treatment influences healing. Maintaining AP was associated with a 2-fold increase in healing outcome with long-term follow-up. The authors conclude AP likely enhances endodontic outcome, but acknowledge the limited number of studies and heterogeneous designs.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review specifically synthesizing clinical studies on the influence of apical patency on endodontic treatment outcome.
- Demonstrates a 2-fold increase in healing when AP is maintained, based on qualitative synthesis of 5 studies.
- Identifies the evidence gap: only 1 RCT among the included studies; the rest are prospective clinical studies.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Systematic review (qualitative synthesis; no meta-analysis due to heterogeneity).
- **PROSPERO registration**: CRD42022374966.
- **Databases searched**: PubMed, Cochrane Library, LIVIVO, LILACS, EBSCO host (inception to July 2024).
- **Risk of bias**: Assessed with Cochrane Collaboration tool and ROBINS-I tool; all 5 included studies had low risk of bias.
- **PRISMA flow**: 314 articles screened → 5 included.
- **Included studies**: Published 2011–2021, from UK, Turkey, and US. Only 1 RCT (Arslan et al.); remaining 4 were prospective clinical studies.
- **Follow-up range**: 1–4 years.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Total roots analyzed**: 4370 across 5 studies.
- **Primary finding**: Maintaining apical patency resulted in a 2-fold increase in healing outcome with long-term follow-up.
- **Supporting studies**: 3 of 5 studies showed a significant positive influence of AP on outcome.
- **Heterogeneity**: Study designs, follow-up durations, and outcome measures varied, precluding meta-analysis.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 5 studies met inclusion criteria; evidence base is thin.
- Only 1 RCT (Arslan et al.); the rest are prospective clinical studies with inherent selection bias potential.
- Follow-up durations ranged from 1 to 4 years — longer follow-up needed for definitive conclusions.
- Heterogeneity across studies prevented quantitative meta-analysis.
- Future long-term clinical studies, ideally RCTs, are needed to confirm the association.

## 7. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Apical patency (AP)** | Maintaining a small file (typically #10 K-file) at or beyond the apical constriction during canal instrumentation to prevent apical blockage |
| **Healing outcome** | Periapical healing assessed clinically and/or radiographically after endodontic treatment |
| **Cochrane tool** | Risk of bias assessment tool for randomized controlled trials |
| **ROBINS-I** | Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies of Interventions — tool for assessing non-randomized studies |
| **PROSPERO** | International prospective register of systematic reviews |
