## Why Ingested

The wiki already holds strong evidence that disclosing-agent-based instruction outperforms verbal or model teaching ([[periodontics/oral-hygiene-instruction/choi-2017-dental-plaque-control-oral-hygiene-education-korea-sr]]) and treats "showing patients their own plaque as feedback" as the shared active ingredient of oral hygiene instruction ([[overviews/oral-hygiene-instruction-overview]]), but it had no primary study of *patient self-checking* with a disclosing solution, and none in an institutionalized-children population. This 2018 prospective program adds exactly that context, so the disclosing-solution mechanism can be cited from a concrete primary study rather than only inferred from the synthesis.

## Three-line Summary

Prospective single-arm (before–after) 5-month oral health program in nine schoolchildren (six girls; grades 1–6) without untreated decayed teeth living in a children's home in Japan, combining group and individual instruction with 3 months of weekly self-checking using plaque disclosing solution.

Mean Plaque Control Record (PCR) fell from 66.1% at baseline and 60.7% at 1 month to 38.7% after 3 months of self-checking (P = 0.009 and P = 0.007, paired t-test), and Patient Hygiene Performance (PHP) fell from 2.8 at baseline and 2.7 at 1 month to 1.4 at 4 months (P = 0.012 and P = 0.018, Wilcoxon signed-rank test).

Self-checking with disclosing solution may improve oral hygiene among schoolchildren in a children's home, but the study is small (n = 9), uncontrolled, and the improvement ratio did not correlate with school grade (r = 0.63, P = 0.070).

## 세줄요약

일본의 아동양육시설(children's home)에 거주하며 미처치 우식이 없는 학령기 아동 9명(여아 6명, 1~6학년)을 대상으로, 집단·개별 교육과 3개월간 주 1회 치태착색용액 자가검진을 결합한 전향적 단일군(전후비교) 5개월 구강보건 프로그램.

평균 치태조절기록(Plaque Control Record, PCR)은 기저 66.1%·1개월 60.7%에서 자가검진 3개월 후 38.7%로 감소(P = 0.009, P = 0.007, 대응 t검정)했고, 환자위생수행도(Patient Hygiene Performance, PHP)는 기저 2.8·1개월 2.7에서 4개월째 1.4로 감소(P = 0.012, P = 0.018, 윌콕슨 부호순위검정).

치태착색용액 자가검진은 아동양육시설 학령기 아동의 구강위생을 개선할 수 있으나, 표본 9명·대조군 없음·학년과 개선율 무상관(r = 0.63, P = 0.070)이라는 한계가 있다.

- **Study period**: 2016–2017

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study, to the authors' knowledge, to evaluate the possible effects of self-checking of oral health among children residing in a children's home.
- Shows that a 5-month program incorporating 3 months of weekly self-checking with plaque disclosing solution (alongside group and individual instruction) significantly reduced both PCR and PHP.
- Reports an improvement ratio (PCR at 4 months / PCR at 1 month) of 0.40 ± 0.35 and its non-significant correlation with school grade.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Prospective single-arm (before–after) interventional program, no control group.
- **Setting**: One children's home in Japan; 2016–2017.
- **n**: 9 schoolchildren (6 girls) without untreated decayed teeth; grades 1 (n = 2), 3 (n = 1), 4 (n = 3), 5 (n = 1), 6 (n = 2).
- **Program (5 months)**:
  1. Baseline group oral health education after plaque staining (20-min play on caries etiology and cleaning method, using large educational teeth model and toothbrush).
  2. Individual tooth cleaning instruction 1 month later, based on oral hygiene status after staining.
  3. Self-care after plaque staining: plaque examined with disclosing agent once a week for 3 months; PCR and PHP recorded weekly by a dental hygienist after self-checking following brushing; no individual instruction given.
  4. Individual tooth cleaning instruction again after 3 months of self-care.
  5. Final oral hygiene instruction / report 1 month later.
  - All group and individual instruction delivered by one dental hygienist.
- **Outcomes**: PCR (mesial, distal, facial, lingual surfaces at gingival margin) and PHP; self-administered oral health questionnaire.
- **Statistics**: paired t-test, Wilcoxon signed-rank, Cochran's Q, Friedman, McNemar, Mann–Whitney U, Pearson's correlation; JMP Pro 12 (SAS Institute); P < 0.05.
- **Ethics**: Approved by the Ethical Committee of Hiroshima University (No. 1814); informed consent from all participants and individuals in parental authority.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Baseline | 1 month | 4 months (after 3 months self-checking) | P |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean PCR | 66.1% | 60.7% | 38.7% | 0.009 vs baseline; 0.007 vs 1 month (paired t-test) |
| Mean PHP | 2.8 | 2.7 | 1.4 | 0.012 vs baseline; 0.018 vs 1 month (Wilcoxon) |
| Improvement ratio (PCR 4 mo / PCR 1 mo) | — | — | 0.40 ± 0.35 (range 0.0–1.0) | — |

- PCR gradually decreased throughout the first 5 weeks of the 3-month self-checking period; no characteristic changes were evident afterward.
- PCR and PHP at 4 months were lower in girls than boys, without a significant difference.
- Improvement ratio vs school grade: PCR r = 0.63, P = 0.070; PHP r = 0.39, P = 0.30 (both non-significant).
- Questionnaire: 6/9 (66.7%) brushed three times a day throughout; brushing in front of a mirror was 55.6% (baseline) → 66.7% (1 month) → 44.4% (4 months). Use of additional cleaning appliances besides a toothbrush was 0%, 0% and 66.7% at baseline, 1 month and 4 months (P = 0.002, Cochran's Q; 4 months vs baseline P = 0.031, McNemar); dental floss was a common additional appliance among six children at 4 months. Monthly toothbrush replacement rose from 18.2% to 55.6% (not significant). Using toothpaste every time rose from 88.9% to 100% (P = 0.37); fluoride toothpaste use was 44.4% → 66.7% → 55.6% (P = 0.47).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No control group without self-checking, so the specific contribution of self-checking cannot be separated from the accompanying group and individual instruction.
- Very small sample (n = 9) from a single children's home.
- All instruction delivered by one dental hygienist.
- Older schoolchildren showed greater PCR improvement and girls better PCR/PHP than boys at 4 months, but grade–improvement correlation was not significant (r = 0.63, P = 0.070).
- Authors call for a larger number of participants and a control group to clarify the significance of self-checking with disclosing solution.

## 6. Related Work

- Choi 2017 ([[periodontics/oral-hygiene-instruction/choi-2017-dental-plaque-control-oral-hygiene-education-korea-sr]]): Korean SR concluding disclosing-agent-based instruction is more effective than verbal or model-based education — this primary study extends that evidence to a Japanese children's-home population and a patient self-check protocol.
- Mitani 2024 ([[periodontics/oral-hygiene-instruction/mitani-2024-effectiveness-novel-oral-hygiene-instruction]]): plaque-record-guided oral hygiene instruction; both use plaque indices to steer instruction, but Mitani targets deep-pocket sites while this study adds weekly patient self-checking.

- Sooampon 2026 ([[periodontics/oral-hygiene-instruction/sooampon-2026-dc-red-33-erythrosine-plaque-disclosure-rct]]): addresses the disclosing-agent/dye side of plaque visualization used here.
- Overview ([[overviews/oral-hygiene-instruction-overview]]): cross-paper synthesis of oral hygiene instruction.

## 7. Glossary

- **PCR (Plaque Control Record)**: index recording presence of plaque on mesial, distal, facial and lingual surfaces at the gingival margin; number of plaque-containing surfaces divided by total available surfaces.
- **PHP (Patient Hygiene Performance)**: plaque index in which six tooth surfaces are divided into five areas after disclosing, scored 0–5 per tooth surface (O'Leary-type debris scoring adapted by Podshadley & Haley).
- **Plaque disclosing solution/agent**: dye applied to teeth to make plaque visually identifiable to the patient.
- **Children's home (아동양육시설)**: residential care institution for children receiving minimal family support; in Japan about 30,000 children lived in such homes per the 2013 MHLW survey cited by the authors.
- **Improvement ratio**: ratio of PCR at 4 months to PCR at 1 month; lower values indicate greater improvement.
