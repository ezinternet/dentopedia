## Why Ingested

This foundational systematic review and meta-analysis addresses a critical esthetic challenge in immediately placed implants — the risk of midfacial recession (MFR) and papillary height (PH) loss. This paper provides quantitative evidence comparing soft-tissue outcomes between immediate and conventional implant placement, and evaluates key modifiers (biotype, immediate provisionalization) that directly inform clinical decision-making in the esthetic zone. Reinforces and quantifies findings referenced in [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/overview]].

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 12 studies (106 articles screened) examining soft-tissue changes around immediately placed implants (IIP) with ≥12 months follow-up after functional loading, comparing IIP vs conventional implant placement (CIP) and stratifying by gingival biotype and restoration timing.
MFR was slightly but non-significantly less in CIP vs IIP (MD −0.064 mm, P=0.687); thick biotype IIP showed significantly less MFR (MD −0.478, P<0.001) and better papillary height (MD −0.287, P<0.001) vs thin biotype; immediate provisionalization showed non-significant MFR benefit (MD 0.253, P=0.384) but significant PH advantage (MD −0.519, P=0.028) over conventional restoration.
Thick gingival biotype and immediate provisionalization are key modifiers favoring soft-tissue stability in IIP; findings qualified by high heterogeneity and limited qualified studies, warranting cautious interpretation in clinical application.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Journal of Periodontology, September 2017; 88(9):876-886
- **DOI**: 10.1902/jop.2017.160698
- **Databases searched**: Cochrane, MEDLINE (through January 2016)
- **Lead institution**: University of California, USA

## 2. Key Contributions
- First comprehensive quantitative evidence on soft-tissue outcomes specifically around immediately placed implants using meta-analytic pooling
- Demonstrated that gingival biotype is a significant predictor of soft-tissue stability in IIP (thick > thin), with a clinically measurable MFR and PH difference
- Provided quantitative support for immediate provisionalization as a modifier that significantly improves papillary height maintenance (MD −0.519 mm, P=0.028)
- Identified methodological gaps: limited qualified studies (n=12 from 106 screened) and high heterogeneity, highlighting the need for higher-quality RCTs in this domain

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review with meta-analysis
- **Databases**: Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, MEDLINE (electronic search up to January 2016), plus hand search
- **Eligibility**: Studies reporting clinical outcomes (MFR and/or PH) in IIP with ≥12 months follow-up after functional loading
- **Quality assessment**: Four independent reviewers assessed data quality and methodology using established bias-assessment tools
- **Synthesis**: Meta-analysis using random-effects models (comprehensive meta-analysis software) accounting for sample size and treatment-group heterogeneity
- **Outcomes**: (1) MFR: IIP vs CIP; (2) MFR and PH: thick vs thin biotype in IIP; (3) MFR and PH: immediate provisionalization vs conventional restoration

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
**IIP vs Conventional Implant Placement (CIP):**
- MFR: MD −0.064 mm (95% CI, ns; P=0.687) — no significant difference
- Papillary height (cumulative): MD −0.396 mm (P=0.010) — significantly better maintained in CIP
- Distal papillary height (DPH): MD −0.765 mm (P<0.001) — significantly better in CIP
- Mesial papillary height (MPH): MD −0.285 mm (ns; P=0.256)

**Gingival Biotype Effect in IIP (thick vs thin):**
- MFR: MD −0.373 mm (ns; P=0.243) — trend toward less recession in thick biotype, not statistically significant
- When pooled data stratified by biotype:
  - MFR (thick): MD −0.478 mm (P<0.001) — significantly less in thick
  - Cumulative PH (thick): MD −0.287 mm (P<0.001) — significantly better in thick
  - MPH (thick): MD −0.288 mm (P<0.001)
  - DPH (thick): MD −0.310 mm (P<0.001)

**Immediate Provisionalization vs Conventional Restoration in IIP:**
- MFR: MD 0.253 mm (ns; P=0.384) — no significant difference
- Cumulative PH: MD −0.519 mm (P=0.028) — significantly better maintained with immediate provisionalization

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **High heterogeneity**: Acknowledged by the authors; comprehensive meta-analysis software accounted for sample-size variation and different treatment groups, but heterogeneity remained substantial
- **Limited qualified studies**: Only 12 studies from 106 screened articles met rigorous inclusion criteria, reducing statistical power
- **Follow-up duration**: Only studies with ≥12 months follow-up included; longer-term (5-10 year) outcomes not systematically reviewed
- **Study-design variability**: Mix of prospective cohort, retrospective, and case-series designs in the meta-analyses; few RCTs
- **Publication bias risk**: Hand search supplemented electronic databases, but risk of selective reporting not formally assessed
- **Causality**: Observational nature limits causal inference; thick biotype may be a proxy for other factors (e.g., bone width, operator skill)

## 7. Glossary
- **Midfacial recession (MFR)**: Labial/buccal soft-tissue margin migration apically from the incisal/occlusal edge or from baseline; measured in mm; clinically significant when >1 mm
- **Papillary height (PH)**: Height of the interdental soft tissue fill between implant/abutment and adjacent tooth; measured in mm; subdivided into mesial (MPH) and distal (DPH) components
- **Gingival biotype**: Classification of soft-tissue thickness and form; thick biotype = >2 mm keratinized mucosa with fibrotic quality; thin biotype = <2 mm, fine and scalloped contours; predictor of esthetic outcome and recession risk
- **Immediate implant placement (IIP)**: Implant insertion at the time of tooth extraction (Type 1 timing per Hammerle et al.)
- **Immediate provisionalization**: Placement and loading of a provisional or definitive restoration on the implant within hours to days of extraction, in contrast to delayed restoration after osseointegration
- **Heterogeneity**: In meta-analysis, variation in effect sizes across studies beyond what is expected by chance; I² >75% considered substantial
