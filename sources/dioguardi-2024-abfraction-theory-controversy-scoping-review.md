## Three-line Summary

Registered PRISMA-ScR scoping review with ROBINS-I risk-of-bias appraisal (1,449 records screened, only 6 studies included) — the most rigorous appraisal of the abfraction theory to date — directly critiquing Duangthip 2017 for conflating abfraction with erosion/abrasion and relying on lab-weighted evidence.

The 6 included studies provide insufficient evidence to either confirm or refute the aetiological role of occlusal loads in NCCL development; "clues" exist but require prospective longitudinal studies that control for coexisting erosion/abrasion before any conclusion is possible.

NCCLs affect ~10–40% of adults over 30, with premolars predominating; the clinical implication is that abfraction-based occlusal adjustment cannot be justified on current evidence, and a multifactorial etiology should guide lesion management.

NCCL은 30세 이상 성인 ~10–40%에서 발생하며 소구치가 주로 이환; 현재 근거로는 abfraction 기반 교합조정을 정당화할 수 없으며, 다인성 병인이 임상적 병소 관리를 안내해야 함.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Most rigorous (registered, PRISMA-ScR) appraisal of abfraction to date; included only 6 of 1449 articles correlating NCCL progression to applied forces.
- Directly critiques Duangthip 2017: that SR did not separate true abfraction from pure erosion/abrasion lesions, lab-weighted, and acknowledged no clinical studies link occlusal stress to NCCLs.
- Concludes evidence is insufficient to confirm OR refute occlusal-load aetiology; "clues" exist but need prospective longitudinal confirmation considering coexisting erosion/abrasion.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- PRISMA-ScR scoping review; PubMed + Scopus, keywords "abfraction" AND "NCCL"; 1449 identified → 6 included. ROBINS-I for risk of bias.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- 6 included studies; none provided sufficient evidence to confirm/refute occlusal-load role in abfraction.
- Prevalence framing: NCCLs ~10-40% of adults >30, premolars predominant.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Scoping (not full SR+MA); very small included set; heterogeneous lesion definitions. Calls for prospective longitudinal studies isolating abfraction from erosion/abrasion.

## 6. Related Work

## 7. Glossary
