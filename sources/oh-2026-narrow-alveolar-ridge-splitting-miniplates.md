## Why Ingested

Case series presenting a modified alveolar ridge-splitting technique with miniplate fixation, directly extending the evidence base in [[bone-regeneration/lin-2023-alveolar-ridge-split-implants-sr-ma]], which identified lack of hardware-fixation detail as a limitation across pooled studies. The modification (intermittent horizontal osteotomies + miniplates) addresses controlled expansion and buccal plate fracture prevention — a practical gap alongside the one-stage vs two-stage comparison in [[bone-regeneration/khanum-2024-one-stage-vs-two-stage-ridge-splitting-sr-ma]].

## Three-line Summary

Case series (3 patients) describes a modified alveolar ridge-splitting technique using miniplates and miniscrews for controlled expansion of narrow alveolar ridges with simultaneous implant placement.

The technique uses sagittal, vertical, and intermittent horizontal osteotomies with piezoelectric surgery (Surgystar X), followed by miniplate fixation to prevent buccal plate fracture; successful expansion and osseointegration were achieved in all cases at up to 24-month follow-up.

Modified ridge splitting with metal fixation is effective for horizontal augmentation in narrow ridges, but requires careful case selection, additional surgical time, and hardware removal surgery.

## 1. Document Information

- **Type:** Case series
- **Journal:** J Implantol Appl Sci, 30(2):98–114
- **Institution:** Dr. Oh's Dental Clinic, Korea
- **Sample:** 3 patients with narrow alveolar ridges
- **Follow-up:** Up to 24 months

## 2. Key Contributions

- Describes a modified multistaged alveolar ridge-splitting (ARS) technique incorporating piezoelectric osteotomies (sagittal, vertical, and intermittent horizontal cuts) combined with titanium miniplate and miniscrew fixation to control expansion and prevent buccal plate fracture.
- Demonstrates applicability in both mandibular posterior (unidirectional) and maxillary posterior (bidirectional: buccal + palatal) narrow ridges.
- Reports simultaneous implant placement with autogenous bone chips (harvested from trimming) used for GBR defect fill.
- No implant failures or complications at follow-up in all 3 cases.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Study design:** Prospective case series (n=3); no control group.
- **Device:** Piezoelectric surgery unit (Surgystar X) for all osteotomies.
- **Osteotomy sequence:** Sagittal crestal cut → vertical releasing cuts → intermittent horizontal cuts (every 2–3 mm) to allow incremental, controlled lateral expansion without complete fracture.
- **Fixation:** Titanium miniplates and miniscrews placed buccally to maintain the expanded gap; hardware removed after osseous consolidation.
- **Simultaneous implant placement** at time of ridge expansion; augmentation with autogenous bone chips ± GBR membrane.
- **Evaluation:** CBCT at baseline, post-expansion, and follow-up; clinical assessment of healing and implant stability.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Case 1 (mandibular posterior):** Narrow ridge 3.4–5.7 mm expanded to accommodate 4.0–5.0 mm diameter implants; bone fill and osseointegration confirmed at 24 months.
- **Case 2 (maxillary posterior):** Bidirectional buccal + palatal expansion for narrow maxillary ridge; successful simultaneous implant placement.
- **Case 3 (maxillary multiple sites):** Staged ridge splitting across multiple maxillary sites; all implants osseointegrated at follow-up.
- **Complications:** None reported across all 3 cases.
- Miniplates prevented buccal plate displacement and fracture, providing predictable expansion.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Case series with no control group; evidence level: case-report/case series.
- Small sample size (n=3); cannot provide statistical estimates of implant survival or complication rates.
- Additional surgical time and hardware costs compared to conventional ridge splitting.
- Requires a second surgery for hardware removal.
- Not suitable for patients with severe vertical deficiency or thin osseous biotype.
- Longer-term RCT or prospective cohort studies needed to compare with conventional ridge splitting.

## 7. Glossary

- Ridge splitting (치조제 분할술, ARS): Surgical division of the alveolar crest to expand a narrow ridge, creating space for implant placement.
- Miniplate (미니플레이트): Small titanium plate used for skeletal fixation; here used to hold the expanded buccal segment.
- Miniscrew (미니스크류): Small titanium screw anchoring the miniplate.
- Piezoelectric surgery (초음파 골절단술): Ultrasonic bone-cutting device enabling precise selective osteotomy while protecting soft tissues.
- GBR — Guided Bone Regeneration (골유도재생술): Barrier membrane technique to contain bone graft and promote new bone formation.
- Intermittent horizontal osteotomy (간헐적 수평 골절단): Partial horizontal cuts perpendicular to the sagittal split to allow controlled incremental expansion.
