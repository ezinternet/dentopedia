## Why Ingested

치과 chairside에서 가장 흔한 의료응급인 미주신경성 실신 (Vasovagal Syncope, VVS)을 위험인자·예방·P–CAB–D 처치·회복·기립성 저혈압 관리까지 치과 맥락으로 통합한 최신 서술적 고찰이 필요했다. 기존 [[wiki/drug/systemic-disease/malave-2022-vasovagal-reactions-during-interventional-pain]]가 통증 중재 시술 영역에 머문 것을 치과 시술(삼차신경-심장 반사, 국소마취 주입)로 확장하고, [[wiki/drug/systemic-disease/smereka-2019-preparedness-and-attitudes-towards-medical]]의 "VVS가 치과 응급 1위" 역학을 독립적으로 재확인한다.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (5 databases, July 1997–October 2025) of vasovagal syncope in dental settings, synthesizing etiology, trigeminocardiac/psychogenic pathophysiology, risk factors, prevention, P–CAB–D management, and recovery criteria.
Vasovagal syncope accounts for more than 60% of dental-office emergency incidents and over 50% of all syncope cases; local anesthetic administration triggers approximately 75% of episodes and 39% occur before the procedure begins, with male adolescents 2.69-fold more likely to faint after dental injection.
Management is positioning-based (supine, legs level or slightly elevated; Trendelenburg 15–30° remains controversial) with atropine 0.5 mg IV up to 3 mg for symptomatic bradycardia and ephedrine 5 mg IV (max 50 mg) for hypotension with heart rate >60 beats/min; evidence is narrative, not systematic, and tables were not captured in the extracted text.

## 세줄요약

서술적 고찰 (Narrative Review): 5개 데이터베이스(Google Scholar·EMBASE·PubMed·ScienceDirect·Cochrane)에서 1997년 7월–2025년 10월 문헌을 검색해 치과에서의 미주신경성 실신 (Vasovagal Syncope, VVS)의 병인·병태생리·위험인자·예방·처치를 통합.
VVS는 치과 응급 발생의 60% 이상, 전체 실신의 50% 이상을 차지하며 국소마취제 투여가 약 75%의 삽화를 유발하고 39%는 시술 시작 전에 발생, 남자 청소년은 치과 주사 후 실신 위험이 2.69배.
처치는 체위(앙와위, 다리를 머리와 같은 높이 또는 약간 높게; 제렌델렌부르크 체위 (Trendelenburg Position) 15–30°는 논란) 중심이며 증상성 서맥에 아트로핀 (Atropine) 0.5 mg IV 최대 3 mg, 심박수 >60/min 저혈압에 에페드린 (Ephedrine) 5 mg IV 최대 50 mg; 근거 수준은 서술적 고찰이고 표 1–7은 추출 텍스트에 포함되지 않았다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- 치과 진료실 특이적 VVS 역학을 한자리에 모음: 전체 의료응급의 60% 이상, 전체 실신의 50% 이상, 치과의사 45%가 연 1.2건 경험, 36%가 대처 준비 부족을 호소.
- 삼차신경-심장 반사 (Trigeminocardiac Reflex, TCR) 경로를 해부학적으로 정리 — 상악신경 (CN V2)·하악신경 (CN V3) 자극 → 가서리신경절 (Gasserian Ganglion) → 삼차신경 감각핵 → 연수 망상체 경유 → 모호핵 (Nucleus Ambiguus)·미주신경 배측운동핵 → 방실결절 부교감 항진 → 서맥·저혈압, 드물게 일과성 무수축.
- 편도신경성 경로(중심편도핵 → 고립로핵 (Nucleus Tractus Solitarius)·rostral ventrolateral medulla 억제)를 별도 기전으로 구분해 정신성 실신을 설명.
- 예방을 3층으로 구조화: 사전 선별(ASA III 이상) → 심리·행동 중재(코라 불안척도 (Corah Dental Anxiety Scale), CBT, 대항압박 수기) → 약물(의식하 진정, 국소마취 기법 최적화).
- 처치를 P–CAB–D 프로토콜로 표준화하고, 2020 AHA·2021 ERC 소생술 지침과 유럽소생위원회 (European Resuscitation Council, ERC) 아트로핀 용량, 미국 식품의약국 (U.S. Food and Drug Administration, FDA) 에페드린 용량을 치과 상황에 매핑.
- 회복·재발 위험 계층화(Table 7)와 기립성 저혈압 (Orthostatic Hypotension, OH) 별도 관리(의자 약 10° 기울임)를 포함.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Narrative (non-systematic) literature review.
- **Databases**: Google Scholar, EMBASE, PubMed, ScienceDirect, Cochrane Database — search window July 1997 to October 2025.
- **Search terms**: "vasovagal syncope", "vasovagal prevention", "anxiolytic vasovagal", "syncope dental management", "dental emergencies", plus combinations with "anxiolytic behavior management", "sedation management", "anticholinergic pharmacology".
- **Inclusion**: published guidelines, systematic reviews, RCTs, cohort/cross-sectional/case-control/interventional studies, relevant textbooks; adults and children undergoing dental procedures under local anesthesia or conscious sedation.
- **Exclusion**: non-English publications, editorials, animal studies.
- **n**: not applicable (no pooled sample); qualitative synthesis of cited studies.
- **Outcomes**: none measured — the review reports epidemiology, mechanism, risk factors, preventive strategies, diagnostic workup, and stepwise management.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Epidemiology / burden**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Dentists encountering a medical emergency during career | 3 of 4 |
| VVS share of reported dental-office incidents | >60% |
| VVS share of all syncope cases | >50% |
| Dentists managing ≥1 syncope case | 45%, mean 1.2 incidents/year |
| Practitioners feeling inadequately prepared | ~36% |
| Typical recovery time (uncomplicated VVS) | 1–2 min |
| Orthostatic hypotension prevalence, adults >60 y | ~20% |
| Cardiac vs noncardiac syncope mortality | 18–33% vs 0–12% |

**Triggers and at-risk groups**

- Local anesthetic administration: approximately 75% of reported syncope episodes.
- 39% of episodes occur before the procedure starts (anticipatory anxiety).
- Young adult males 16–35 years are over-represented; male adolescents are 2.69-fold more likely to experience syncope after dental injection than female adolescents.
- Medications (especially NSAIDs and sedatives) increase susceptibility; medication-related causes account for 5–15% of syncope cases.

**Clinical phases / physiology**

- Syncope phase: heart rate may fall below 50 beats/min; blood pressure may drop to as low as 30/15 mmHg; respiration may become irregular or apneic; convulsive movements may mimic seizure.
- Post-syncopal symptoms may persist several hours, occasionally up to 24 h.
- Delayed recovery flags: >2 min to regain consciousness, or >15–20 min for complete recovery → consider hyperventilation, hypoglycemia, anemia, seizure.

**Prevention**

- Physical counterpressure (leg raising, leg crossing) aborted syncope in 7 of 8 patients with prodromal symptoms.
- 60% lidocaine tape applied ~3 h before venipuncture facilitates IV access in pediatric/special-needs sedation.
- Slow injection ≈1 cartridge/min; buffered/warmed solution, topical anesthesia, fine-gauge or computer-controlled delivery to reduce vasovagal responses.
- Epinephrine-containing local anesthetics require caution in patients with prior syncope (paradoxical hypotension/bradycardia).

**Management (P–CAB–D)**

| Item | Dose / parameter |
|---|---|
| Chest compression rate | 100–120/min |
| Compression:ventilation | 30:2 adults and children (single rescuer); 15:2 pediatric (two rescuers) |
| Supine positioning | legs level with or slightly above head (ADA-supported) |
| Trendelenburg | feet 15–30° above head — controversial (regurgitation, airway obstruction, cerebral edema, fall risk) |
| Oxygen, conscious | 6–10 L/min |
| Oxygen, unconscious (bag-valve mask) | 10–15 L/min |
| Vital signs monitoring interval | every 5 min |
| Atropine (symptomatic bradycardia, HR <60 with hypotension) | 0.5 mg IV every 3–5 min, max 3 mg (ERC) |
| Ephedrine (HR >60 with hypotension) | 5 mg IV, max cumulative 50 mg (FDA) |
| Orthostatic hypotension, next visits | dental chair inclined ~10° |
| Urgent cardio/neuro referral | age <10 or >40 with unexplained syncope and no prodrome |

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Narrative, not systematic**: no PRISMA protocol, no search-string documentation beyond term lists, no risk-of-bias appraisal, no evidence grading — effect sizes are cited from heterogeneous sources without synthesis.
- **Tables 1–7 are cited in the body but absent from the extracted PMC text** (risk-factor table, trigger tables, ASA screening table, differential-diagnosis table, recurrence-risk table). Every number above therefore comes from body prose only; table-level detail could not be verified.
- Most quantitative claims are single-study and undated in-text (e.g. 75% local-anesthetic trigger share, 2.69-fold male adolescent risk, 7-of-8 counterpressure success), so their provenance cannot be traced without the reference list.
- Pharmacological recommendations are extrapolated from resuscitation and anesthesia literature (ERC, AHA, FDA) rather than dental-specific trials; no RCT evidence for atropine/ephedrine in dental VVS.
- Prevention evidence is mechanistic/expert-opinion based: no comparative data on CBT vs sedation vs counterpressure in dental populations.
- Authors call for research on virtual reality and artificial intelligence for stress-induced syncope — a gap statement, not a finding.

## 7. Glossary

- **VVS (Vasovagal Syncope)**: reflex-mediated transient loss of consciousness from neurally induced hypotension and bradycardia after a trigger stimulus.
- **TCR (Trigeminocardiac Reflex)**: trigeminal afferent stimulation (CN V2/V3) producing vagal efferent bradycardia and hypotension.
- **OH (Orthostatic Hypotension)**: blood pressure fall on standing from venous pooling with inadequate sympathetic compensation.
- **P–CAB–D**: Position → Circulation–Airway–Breathing → Definitive treatment, the review's dental syncope algorithm.
- **Trendelenburg Position**: feet elevated 15–30° above the head; controversial in syncope because of regurgitation/airway/fall risks.
- **Corah Dental Anxiety Scale**: validated instrument for screening dental anxiety in new patients.
- **Tilt-table test**: controlled postural-change provocation used to identify the etiology of unexplained syncope.
