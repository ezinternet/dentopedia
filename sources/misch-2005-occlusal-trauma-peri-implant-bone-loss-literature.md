## Why Ingested

The occlusion folder asks whether occlusal overload drives peri-implant bone loss, and the wiki's [[occlusion/fukushima-2016-controversy-with-respect-occlusion]] page records that the question is contested. This Misch 2005 review is the implant-side argument that overload does increase marginal bone loss, and it should be read against the controversy page.

## Three-line Summary

Selective literature review (MEDLINE-assisted plus hand search; Implant Dentistry, 2005) on occlusal overload and peri-implant marginal bone loss — abstract-only ingest, full text not retrieved.

The authors combine biomechanics, bone physiology and animal and clinical reports to argue that occlusal overload on implants may increase the incidence of marginal bone loss.

The conclusion is a narrative inference, not a quantified association; the abstract gives no effect sizes and the question remains contested in the wiki.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Argues for a force-management approach to occlusal planning around implants.
- Integrates engineering, bone-physiology and clinical sources in one narrative.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

MEDLINE-assisted and hand search of English peer-reviewed literature and textbooks; selective review across cellular biomechanics, bone mechanics, animal studies and clinical reports.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Qualitative: occlusal overload "may increase" marginal bone loss. No numeric effect size in the abstract.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Selective review; the abstract does not show inclusion criteria or grading.
- Full text not retrieved; the cited animal and clinical evidence is not verified here.
- Published before the wiki's occlusion-overload evidence base was assembled; the controversy page is the current reading.

## 7. Glossary

- **Occlusal overload**: functional or parafunctional loads exceeding the tissue's adaptive capacity. **Marginal bone loss (MBL)**: crestal bone loss around the implant neck.
