## Why Ingested

Found while mining the reference list of Wen et al. 2026 ("The effect of dental floss sequence on plaque removal efficacy and periodontal health"), whose introduction opens by framing dental plaque as a complex biofilm of diverse oral microorganisms. This Nature Reviews Microbiology paper (Baker et al. 2024) is the authoritative, current background review on oral microbiome diversity and biogeography that underlies that framing — it updates and extends the field synthesized in [[wiki/oral-microbiology/lamont-2018-oral-microbiota-dynamic-communities-host]] (same journal's prior generation review), adding species-level CLASI-FISH biogeography (e.g., S. mutans "rotund" architecture linked to localized demineralization), the ecological role of candidate phyla radiation (CPR) bacteria/Saccharibacteria, and expanded systemic-disease mechanistic detail (F. nucleatum Fap2/FadA, P. gingivalis gingipain-tau, TIGIT/CEACAM1 immune evasion).

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Nature Reviews Microbiology, 2024) synthesizing recent advances in human oral microbiome diversity (>700 bacterial species across 7 phyla, candidate phyla radiation/CPR bacteria, microeukaryotes, archaea, viruses) and species-level biogeography via CLASI-FISH microscopy.

Site-specific spatial organization (e.g., Streptococcus salivarius/mutans/sanguinis occupying distinct buccal/plaque/tongue niches; S. mutans "rotund" biofilm architecture driving localized demineralization) links microbial community structure directly to caries, periodontal disease, oral cancer, and systemic diseases (Alzheimer, cardiovascular disease, colorectal cancer, rheumatoid arthritis, IBD) via bacterial translocation and immunomodulation.

Clinical implication: oral disease pathogenesis (caries, periodontitis) is increasingly understood as ecological/network-level dysbiosis rather than single-pathogen infection, supporting prebiotic/probiotic strategies (e.g., nitrate-reducing bacteria) over single-target antimicrobial approaches; limitation is that most systemic-disease mechanistic links remain animal-model-based with high human inter-individual variability.

## 세줄요약

Nature Reviews Microbiology 2024 내러티브 리뷰로, 인체 구강 미생물군집 다양성(700종 이상, 7개 문, 후보문방사선군(CPR) 세균 포함 진핵미생물·고세균·바이러스)과 CLASI-FISH 현미경 기반 종 수준 생물지리학(biogeography)을 종합.

부위별 미생물 공간구조(예: S. salivarius/mutans/sanguinis가 각각 협측점막/치태/설배부에 특이적으로 분포, S. mutans의 "rotund" 생물막 구조가 국소 탈회를 유발)가 우식·치주질환·구강암 및 전신질환(알츠하이머, 심혈관질환, 대장암, 류마티스관절염, 염증성장질환)과 세균 전위(translocation)·면역조절 기전으로 직접 연결됨.

임상적 의미: 우식·치주염 발병기전은 단일 병원체 감염이 아닌 생태학적·네트워크 수준 이상증(dysbiosis)으로 이해되어 프리바이오틱/프로바이오틱 전략(예: 질산염 환원균)을 뒷받침하나, 전신질환 연관 기전의 상당수는 동물모델 근거이며 인체 개인차가 크다는 한계가 있음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Comprehensive update on oral microbiome taxonomic diversity beyond bacteria: candidate phyla radiation (CPR) bacteria (Saccharibacteria/TM7, Absconditabacteria/SR1, Gracilibacteria/GN02), microeukaryotes (fungi, amoebae, flagellates), archaea (Methanobrevibacter oralis), and viruses (>60,000 phage species-level groups; Herpesviridae/Anelloviridae in saliva).
- Species-level biogeography via CLASI-FISH: demonstrates that oral biofilms are highly spatially organized (e.g., Corynebacterium-Streptococcus "corncob" architecture), revising the classic Fusobacterium-nucleatum-as-bridge model of plaque development.
- Streptococcus genus niche partitioning: S. salivarius (buccal mucosa), S. mutans (dental plaque), S. sanguinis (tongue dorsum) — resolved via short-read metagenomics.
- Mechanistic detail on oral-systemic disease links: F. nucleatum Fap2/FadA in colorectal cancer, P. gingivalis gingipain-mediated tau degradation in Alzheimer disease, TIGIT/CEACAM1-mediated immune evasion, citrullination-driven rheumatoid arthritis autoimmunity.
- Introduces the "slow" inflammatory responder phenotype in the experimental gingivitis model (in addition to previously known high/low responders).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative synthesis review drawing on culture-independent metagenomic sequencing, single-cell sequencing, FISH-based microscopy (including CLASI-FISH), metatranscriptomics, metaproteomics, and metabolomics literature. Not a systematic review/meta-analysis; no formal quality appraisal or PRISMA methodology — represents expert synthesis of the current oral microbiome literature by leading researchers in the field (Forsyth Institute, OHSU, JCVI, UC San Diego, U Washington, U Buffalo).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- >700 bacterial species across 7 phyla (Actinomycetota, Bacteroidota, Bacillota, Fusobacteriota, Pseudomonadota, Saccharibacteria, Spirochaetota) constitute the core oral bacteriome.
- CPR bacteria account for >25% of global bacterial diversity; TM7x (oral Saccharibacteria) has an ultrasmall (200-300 nm) cell and an epiparasitic lifestyle dependent on Actinomyces odontolyticus host.
- >90% of humans chronically carry Herpesviridae/Anelloviridae detectable in saliva.
- ~90% of US oral squamous cell carcinomas are HPV-driven; >90% of nasopharyngeal carcinomas are EBV-associated.
- Dysbiotic Candida-rich mycobiome prevalent in OSCC; higher Malassezia abundance correlates with better OSCC survival (potential prognostic biomarker).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative (non-systematic) review — no quantitative pooled estimates.
- Many oral-systemic disease mechanisms (e.g., trained innate immunity, gut colitis exacerbation by oral pathobiont-specific T cells, placental microbiota) are established only in animal models, with acknowledged high human inter-individual variability limiting translation.
- Authors call for: large-scale longitudinal studies incorporating oral alongside gut sampling; improved in vitro model systems resembling in vivo taxonomic/behavioral complexity; time-course studies on microbial growth/turnover, phage predation loss, and dispersion rates.

## 7. Glossary

- **CPR bacteria (Candidate Phyla Radiation)**: large monophyletic bacterial superphylum group, mostly uncultivated, often with ultrasmall cells and epiparasitic/episymbiotic lifestyles.
- **CLASI-FISH**: Combinatorial Labelling and Spectral Imaging Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization — microscopy technique enabling simultaneous identification of many bacterial taxa within intact biofilm samples at micrometre resolution.
- **Biogeography (microbial)**: the spatial distribution and organization of distinct microbial taxa across different anatomical niches/microenvironments.
- **Epiparasite/epibiont**: an organism (e.g., TM7x) living attached to the surface of, and metabolically dependent on, a host organism.
- **Rotund architecture**: a densely packed, extracellular-scaffold-embedded biofilm morphology observed for S. mutans associated with localized low pH and enamel demineralization.
