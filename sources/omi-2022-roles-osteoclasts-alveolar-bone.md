## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Genesis 2022;60(8-9):e23490, Omi M & Mishina Y) organised around the osteoclastic bone-resorptive process within alveolar bone remodeling — no primary data, no patient or animal cohort, no reported n.

The abstract advances three claims with no quantitative support: the osteoclast is a large multinucleated hematopoietic-derived cell responsible for bone resorption whose balance with osteoblast formation maintains bone homeostasis; the alveolar bone differs from other skeletal bones in embryonic cellular origin, form of ossification, and the presence of teeth and periodontal tissues; and excessive osteoclastic resorption is a prominent feature of jaw bone disease such as periodontitis.

No effect sizes, pooled estimates, or any quantitative result are available (narrative review, not an SR/MA), and the abstract names no signalling pathway or drug — so the therapeutic content is a stated perspective rather than an evidence-weighed conclusion.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Frames the alveolar bone as a remodeling compartment that is **not interchangeable with generic skeletal bone**, distinguishing it on three grounds: embryonic cellular origin, form of ossification, and the presence of teeth and periodontal tissues. The stated rationale is that understanding this uniqueness is necessary to maintain oral homeostasis.
- Identifies the osteoclast as the central effector cell of the resorptive arm — large, multinucleated, hematopoietic in origin — and stresses that homeostasis rests on the *balance* between osteoclastic resorption and osteoblastic formation rather than on either cell in isolation.
- Names excessive osteoclastic resorption as a defining feature of jaw bone disease, with **periodontitis given as the exemplar**; this is what lifts the mechanism out of general bone biology and into dentistry.
- Makes the mechanism-to-therapy argument explicitly: understanding the mechanisms of osteoclastic bone resorption is a precondition for the effective treatment of jaw bone disease, and inhibiting osteoclast formation and the bone-resorptive process is stated as the therapeutic target.
- Closes with perspectives on **osteoclast-targeted therapies** and **regenerative approaches** associated with bone diseases in the jaw — the regenerative arm is asserted as a perspective only, with no approach, material, or technique named in the abstract.

## 7. Glossary

- **Osteoclast (파골세포)**: large multinucleated cell of hematopoietic origin responsible for bone resorption; the bone-resorbing effector cell of the remodeling cycle.
- **Osteoblast (조골세포)**: the bone-forming cell; the balance between osteoblastic formation and osteoclastic resorption is what maintains bone homeostasis.
- **Alveolar bone / alveolar process (치조골)**: the part of the jawbone that holds the teeth and supports oral functions.
- **Alveolar bone remodeling (치조골 재형성)**: the coupled resorption-and-formation turnover of alveolar bone; the review argues it has features unique to the jaw.
- **Bone homeostasis (골 항상성)**: the maintained equilibrium between osteoclastic bone resorption and osteoblastic bone formation.
- **Osteoclast-targeted therapy (파골세포 표적 치료)**: intervention aimed at inhibiting osteoclast formation and the bone-resorptive process; named in the abstract as a therapeutic target and a perspective, with no agent specified.
