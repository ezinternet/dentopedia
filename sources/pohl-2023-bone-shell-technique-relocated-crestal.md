## Why Ingested

Ingested alongside a batch of periosteal-releasing-incision (PRI) papers, but this is not itself a PRI paper — it is a horizontal-ridge-augmentation *technique* paper (autologous bone shell from a relocated crestal segment) that explicitly claims to **eliminate the need for PRI** rather than study PRI itself. It is the closest technique-level counterpart to [[implants/vertical-ridge-augmentation/wang-2025-mucoperiosteal-flap-healing-vertical-bone-titanium-mesh]] — both report a design that reaches tension-free closure without a periosteal releasing incision, but by different mechanisms (Wang: deliberate open healing over a clean Ti-membrane; Pohl: donor site = recipient site, so there is no flap advancement to relieve in the first place). Kept as counter-evidence/context for the PRI-topic batch: a case series where the surgical design itself removes the clinical problem PRI exists to solve.

## Three-line Summary

Preliminary case series (Int J Periodontics Restorative Dent 2023;43(5):596-605; 5 consecutive edentulous patients, 20 sites, severe mandibular horizontal ridge atrophy with adequate ridge height) testing a bone shell technique using the coronal segment of the knife-edge ridge itself, relocated laterally as an autologous shell, with 1-4 year follow-up.

Average horizontal bone gain was 3.6 ± 0.76 mm at 1 mm below the newly formed ridge crest and 3.4 ± 0.92 mm at 5 mm below crest; ridge volume was sufficiently restored in all 20 sites to allow staged implant placement, with only 2/20 sites needing an additional hard-tissue graft at implant placement.

Because donor and recipient sites are the same, the authors report periosteal releasing incisions and flap advancement were not required for primary closure — a donor-site-free, PRI-free alternative to conventional bone-shell/block techniques harvested from the external oblique ridge, symphysis, sinus wall, or palate; abstract-only, very small n.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Describes a bone shell technique that uses the **coronal segment of the atrophic ridge itself** as the shell, rather than harvesting a cortical plate from a remote donor site (external oblique ridge, symphysis, lateral sinus wall, or palate — the donor sites the authors cite as conventional).
- Because the harvested segment is repositioned at its own site, the authors report **no periosteal releasing incisions and no flap advancement are needed** for primary wound closure, which they attribute to reduced muscle tension and consequently lower wound dehiscence risk.
- Reports quantified horizontal bone gain (3.6 mm / 3.4 mm at 1 mm / 5 mm below crest) sufficient for staged implant placement in all 20 treated sites, with a low additional-grafting rate (2/20) at implant placement.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Preliminary case series (uncontrolled, single-arm).
- **Population**: 5 consecutive edentulous patients, 20 treatment sites, severe horizontal mandibular ridge atrophy (anterior) with adequate ridge *height* (vertical dimension not the deficiency being treated).
- **Technique**: The coronal (knife-edge) segment of the atrophic ridge is horizontally sectioned and relocated/repositioned laterally as an autologous bone shell over particulate graft material at the same donor/recipient site; staged implant placement follows once ridge volume is restored.
- **Follow-up**: 1 to 4 years.
- **Outcomes measured**: horizontal bone gain at 1 mm and 5 mm below the newly formed ridge crest; need for additional hard-tissue grafting at implant placement.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Horizontal bone gain: **3.6 ± 0.76 mm** at 1 mm below the newly formed ridge crest; **3.4 ± 0.92 mm** at 5 mm below crest.
- Ridge volume was **sufficiently restored in all 20 sites** to enable implant placement in a staged approach.
- **2 of 20 sites** required additional hard-tissue grafting at the time of implant placement.
- Qualitative/procedural claim (not a numeric outcome in the abstract): primary wound closure was achieved **without periosteal releasing incisions or flap advancement**, attributed to same-site donor/recipient design and reduced muscle tension.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Very small sample (n=5 patients, 20 sites), single-arm, uncontrolled case series — no comparison group against conventional remote-donor-site bone shell technique, ridge-split, or GBR.
- Single institution/technique description; generalizability to other atrophy patterns (vertical deficiency, posterior mandible/maxilla, insufficient ridge height) is not established — the technique specifically requires adequate ridge *height* to have a coronal knife-edge segment available to relocate.
- Abstract-only ingestion: no reported dehiscence rate, complication rate beyond the 2/20 additional-graft figure, histologic data, or statistical testing; the "no PRI needed" claim is stated by the authors as a procedural advantage, not measured against a PRI comparator arm within this study.
- No implant-level survival/loading outcome reported in the abstract (endpoint is ridge restoration adequate for staged placement, not post-loading implant survival).

## 7. Glossary

- **Bone shell technique**: horizontal/vertical ridge augmentation method using a thin autologous cortical bone plate ("shell") fixed lateral to the deficient ridge, with particulate graft packed in the space between shell and native bone.
- **Knife-edge ridge**: severely horizontally atrophic alveolar ridge with adequate vertical height but a narrow, sharp crestal profile — too thin for implant placement without augmentation.
- **Coronal segment / relocated crestal segment**: the top (crestal) portion of the knife-edge ridge, horizontally sectioned off and repositioned laterally as the bone shell in this technique — donor and recipient site are the same location.
- **Periosteal releasing incision (PRI)**: a horizontal incision through periosteum (leaving mucosa intact) that releases flap tension, allowing coronal advancement for tension-free primary wound closure — the maneuver this technique's design avoids needing.
- **Staged approach**: ridge augmentation performed first, followed by a separate later surgery for implant placement once bone has healed/matured (as opposed to simultaneous augmentation + implant placement).
