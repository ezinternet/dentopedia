## Why Ingested

Long-term (10–15 year) survival data for endodontically treated cracked teeth is scarce. This retrospective study provides the most extended follow-up cohort to date, directly complementing the shorter-term evidence in [[cracked-tooth/zhang-2024-cracked-teeth-treatment-outcomes-sr-ma]] and [[cracked-tooth/dong-2026-nomogram-cracked-tooth-rct-5year-survival]]. Preoperative probing depth as a primary survival predictor has direct clinical triage implications.

## Three-line Summary

A retrospective cohort of 204 endodontically treated cracked teeth followed for 10–15 years found overall survival rates of 66% at 10 years and 55% at 15 years.

Preoperative probing depth (PD) was the dominant prognostic factor: PD <4 mm yielded 76%/64% survival at 10/15 years, while PD ≥6 mm carried aHR=3.19 for extraction.

Success rates (asymptomatic + complete radiographic healing) declined steeply from 64% to 37% between years 10 and 15; endodontic treatment of cracked teeth with PD <4 mm is a viable long-term option.

## Key Contributions

1. Provides 15-year survival data for endodontically treated cracked teeth — the longest follow-up cohort in the literature.
2. Identifies preoperative probing depth (PD) as the primary statistically significant prognostic factor for tooth survival.
3. Quantifies hazard ratios for PD thresholds (4–5 mm: aHR 2.48; ≥6 mm: aHR 3.19), enabling evidence-based triage decisions.
4. Documents success rate decline from 64% at 10 years to 37% at 15 years, distinguishing survival (tooth present) from success (asymptomatic + complete radiographic healing).
5. Demonstrates that teeth with favorable PD (<4 mm) achieve survival comparable to non-cracked teeth (76% at 10 yr, 64% at 15 yr).

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective review of clinical and radiographic records
- **Setting**: Patients with endodontically treated cracked teeth seen for 10–15 year review
- **Inclusion**: 196 patients with 204 endodontically treated cracked teeth
- **Outcomes**:
  - **Survival**: tooth present at follow-up
  - **Success**: asymptomatic with complete radiographic healing
- **Statistical method**: Cox proportional hazards regression; multivariable analysis with adjusted hazard ratios (aHR)
- **Key variable**: Preoperative probing depth (PD) categorized as <4 mm, 4–5 mm, ≥6 mm

## Key Results

### Survival Rates (all teeth)
- 10-year survival: **66%** (95% CI: 60%–73%)
- 15-year survival: **55%** (95% CI: 48%–63%)

### Survival Rates by Probing Depth
- PD <4 mm: **76%** at 10 yr, **64%** at 15 yr
- PD 4–5 mm: aHR = **2.48** (95% CI: 1.41–4.38, P = .002)
- PD ≥6 mm: aHR = **3.19** (95% CI: 1.56–6.54, P = .002)

### Success Rates (all teeth)
- 10-year success: **64%**
- 15-year success: **37%**

### Additional Prognostic Factors
- Older age: modest independent risk factor for survival (aHR = **1.03** per year, 95% CI: 1.00–1.05, P = .026)

### Clinical Conclusion
Endodontic treatment of cracked teeth with favorable preoperative PDs (<4 mm) demonstrates long-term survival comparable to non-cracked teeth and is a viable long-term treatment option.

## Glossary

- **Survival**: Tooth is present at follow-up (regardless of symptoms or radiographic status)
- **Success**: Tooth is asymptomatic with complete radiographic healing
- **PD (Probing Depth, 치주낭 깊이)**: Periodontal pocket depth measured preoperatively
- **aHR (Adjusted Hazard Ratio)**: Multivariable-adjusted estimate of relative risk of tooth loss over time
- **Cox proportional hazards regression**: Survival analysis method accounting for time-to-event and multiple covariates
