## Why Ingested

The cracked-tooth cluster holds pooled and narrative statements that CBCT is imperfect for showing a vertical root fracture ([[wiki/cracked-tooth/shokri-2024-is-cone-beam-computed-tomography]], [[wiki/cracked-tooth/patel-2025-position-statement-longitudinal-cracks-fractures]]) but mostly from in vitro data, plus one clinical CBCT-vs-PA bone-defect study ([[wiki/cracked-tooth/alaugaily-2022-cbct-patterns-bone-loss-clinical]]). This single-centre clinical series, with extraction-confirmed fractures, adds in vivo CBCT-vs-intraoral-radiography detection rates by fracture direction (bucco-lingual vs mesio-distal) and a tooth-type/core-type profile of VRF in root-filled teeth.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-centre diagnostic/descriptive study (Nippon Dental University, Niigata; CBCT 2017-2019): 50 patients with extraction-confirmed root fractures in endodontically treated teeth, of which 36 were vertical root fractures (VRF; 72%) analysed for cause, core construction, tooth type, fracture direction, and detectability on intraoral radiography vs CBCT (two radiologists, consensus read).

All 36 VRFs occurred in non-traumatized teeth; VRF counts did not differ by core type (metal / composite resin / no core, p = 0.558) but differed by tooth type (premolars most, p = 0.005) and direction (bucco-lingual about twice mesio-distal, p = 0.046; in premolars 76.2% vs 23.8%, p = 0.016). CBCT detected 91.7% of bucco-lingual and 100% of mesio-distal VRFs, whereas intraoral radiography detected 25% of bucco-lingual and none of the mesio-distal ones (both p < 0.001); two bucco-lingual VRFs were missed on CBCT because of metal artifacts.

The authors conclude CBCT is an adequate method for diagnosing VRF in root-filled teeth, but the design (only extraction-confirmed fractures, no fracture-free comparison teeth, n = 36, single centre, consensus read at the time of imaging) yields sensitivity only and cannot give specificity or true risk estimates for tooth type or core type.

## 세줄요약

후향적 단일기관 진단·기술 연구 (일본치과대학 니가타; CBCT 2017-2019) — 발거 후 파절이 확인된 근관치료치 환자 50명 중 수직치근파절 (Vertical Root Fracture, VRF) 36건(72%)을 대상으로 원인, 코어 (Core) 종류, 치아 종류, 파절 방향, 구내 방사선사진 (Intraoral radiography) 대 콘빔 CT (Cone-Beam CT, CBCT) 검출률을 분석 (방사선과 의사 2인 합의 판독).

VRF 36건 모두 외상 없는 치아였고, 코어 종류(금속·복합레진·코어 없음) 간 차이는 없었으나 (p = 0.558) 치아 종류(소구치 최다, p = 0.005)와 파절 방향(협설 파절이 근원심 파절의 약 2배, p = 0.046; 소구치에서 76.2% 대 23.8%, p = 0.016)에서 차이가 있었음. CBCT 검출률은 협설 파절 91.7%, 근원심 파절 100%, 구내 방사선사진은 협설 25%, 근원심 0% (모두 p < 0.001); 협설 파절 2건은 금속 아티팩트 (Artifact) 때문에 CBCT에서 놓침.

저자는 CBCT가 근관치료치 VRF 진단에 적절하다고 결론짓지만, 발거로 확인된 파절만 포함하고 파절 없는 비교 치아가 없으며 n = 36의 단일기관·촬영 당시 합의 판독 설계라 민감도만 산출되고 특이도나 치아·코어 종류별 실제 위험 추정은 불가능함.

## 2. Key Contributions

- In vivo, extraction-confirmed series showing VRF in root-filled teeth was dominated by premolars and by the bucco-lingual direction (anatomical explanation offered: thin mesio-distal dimension, oval canals prepared round, thinning remaining dentin).
- Direction-specific detection rates: intraoral radiography saw about one quarter of bucco-lingual VRFs (the ones whose plane is parallel to the beam) and no mesio-distal VRFs; CBCT saw nearly all (91.7% / 100%).
- Practical advice: set the X-ray beam parallel to the suspected bucco-lingual fracture plane when intraoral radiography is used; remove metal before CBCT when feasible because metal artifacts caused the only CBCT misses.
- Null finding on core construction (metal vs composite resin vs no core) among fractured teeth.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective review of CBCT images (Jan 2017 - Aug 2019) in a university hospital; fractures confirmed after extraction. Ten non-endodontically treated fractured teeth were excluded.
- **n**: 50 patients (18 male, 32 female; age 16-86, mean 58.7 +/- 16.0 y) with root fractures in endodontically treated teeth; 36 VRF analysed (14 horizontal fractures excluded from the VRF analysis).
- **Imaging**: all patients had intraoral radiography first (HELIODENT Plus, 70 kV, 7 mA), then CBCT (Fine Cube, Yoshida; standard mode 90 kV, 4 mA, FOV 81 x 81 mm, 16.8 s, slice thickness 0.144 mm; axial, cross-sectional, parasagittal MPR and 3D).
- **Reading**: two radiologists independently, disagreements resolved by consensus; detectability decided by consensus at the time each image was taken.
- **Outcomes**: cause (traumatized vs not), core construction (metal core, composite resin core, none), tooth type (anterior / premolar / molar), fracture direction (bucco-lingual vs mesio-distal), and detectability by intraoral radiography vs CBCT.
- **Statistics**: chi-square when expected counts > 5; cross-tabulation with Yates correction and residual analysis for detection ability; SPSS 17.0, alpha 0.05.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Stated: small number of root-fractured teeth; larger samples needed.
- Inferred: only teeth with a confirmed fracture were analysed, with no fracture-free or equivocal comparison group, so only sensitivity is estimable; specificity and false-positive behaviour of CBCT in this sample are unknown. Reading was by consensus at the time of imaging in teeth already clinically suspected, which may inflate apparent performance relative to blinded reads.
- Inferred: the tooth-type, direction and core-type comparisons are chi-square tests on the distribution of fractured teeth only, without denominators of teeth at risk (all root-filled premolars, molars, or metal-core teeth); they describe where fractures were seen, not relative risk. The "no effect of core construction" result therefore cannot be read as equivalence of core types.
- Inferred: results rest on one CBCT unit/protocol (standard mode, 0.144 mm) and the authors did not test high-resolution mode; metal-artifact misses were limited to two cases.
- Source inconsistency: PubMed lists three authors (Mizuhashi, Watarai, Ogura) while the PMC full text lists Testarelli as a fourth; PMC byline used here.

## 7. Glossary

- **VRF (vertical root fracture)**: longitudinal root fracture that starts in the canal or root surface and runs along the root axis; most common in root-filled teeth.
- **Bucco-lingual / mesio-distal fracture**: fracture plane running through the cheek-tongue vs the front-back dimension of the root; intraoral radiographs see the fracture only when the beam is nearly parallel to its plane.
- **CBCT (cone-beam computed tomography)**: 3D imaging that removes superimposition but is degraded by beam hardening near metal and gutta-percha.
- **Metal artifact**: streaks/dark bands from dense materials (metal cores, posts) that can hide or mimic a fracture line.
- **Verification (work-up) bias**: bias that arises when only teeth with a confirmed reference diagnosis (here, extraction-confirmed fractures) are analysed, so sensitivity is estimable but specificity is not.
- **Core construction**: build-up of the coronal tooth part on a root-filled tooth (metal cast core, composite resin core, or none).
