## Why Ingested

Cited as the source of the "early failures account for about 2–6% of implants placed" benchmark in an internal clinic implant-audit report (DentWeb cohort, 2026-10). The 2–6% figure is a background statement in this paper's introduction (refs 10–18), not the authors' own result. Their own early-failure rate is 4.8%. The paper also adds a small general-practice cohort to [[overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]], alongside [[implants/survival/guarnieri-2025-analysis-risk-factors-related-early]] and [[implants/survival/yari-2023-risk-factors-early-implant-failure]].

## One-line Summary

Retrospective single-practice cohort (Germany, 2012–2017; 186 bone-level implants in 106 patients, one implant system, two dentists): 9 implants (4.8%) failed before the final prosthesis; short implants (<10 mm; OR 5.8) and augmentation (OR 5.5) were associated with early failure.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Gives a practice-based (non-academic, non-referral) early-failure rate: 4.8% at implant level, at the upper end of the cited 2–6% range.
- Shows two implant-related risk factors after accounting for clustering within patients: augmentation (OR 5.5, 95% CI 1.4–21.7) and implant length <10 mm (OR 5.8, 95% CI 1.3–26.4).
- All failures were posterior, and 8 of 9 were in the maxilla. These were only trends in the models (posterior OR 6.1, maxilla OR 5.6, both not significant).
- Smoking, diabetes, periodontitis history, insertion torque (all ≥20 Ncm) and healing mode were not associated with early failure in this small sample. The authors estimate about 3,169 implants would be needed to detect a smoking effect.
- Restates the definition of early failure: no or inadequate osseointegration before functional loading, mechanistically distinct from late failure.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design:** retrospective cohort. Anonymized records of all implant cases in one German dental practice, 2012–2017.
- **Implant system:** a single bone-level system (blueSky, Bredent Medical).
- **Operators:** two dentists. D1 had completed an implantology curriculum and had 4–9 years of experience over the period. D2 graduated in 2015 and was supervised by D1.
- **Protocol:** minimum insertion torque 20 Ncm. Patients with a periodontitis history needed ≥3 months of stability after treatment. Abutments were torqued to 25 Ncm. The surgeon chose the loading time based on primary stability and augmentation; some high-torque implants were loaded immediately.
- **Population:** 206 implants in 116 patients were placed.
  - Excluded: 7 one-piece 2.8 mm implants (3 patients), and 13 implants in 7 patients who were not yet restored or left the practice.
  - Analysed: 186 implants in 106 patients, about 90% response.
  - Patients: mean age 60.6 (SD 12.7) years, 58.5% female, about 20% smokers, about 10% diabetic, 26% with a periodontitis history.
- **Outcome:** successful osseointegration (yes/no) at incorporation of the final prosthesis. Failure means loss before the final prosthesis was attached. Mean healing time before loading was 147.8 (SD 81.9) days: 154.3 days in the maxilla, 137.8 days in the mandible.
- **Predictors:**
  - Patient: age, gender, number of diseases and medications (median split), smoking, diabetes, periodontitis history.
  - Implant: length (<10 mm), diameter (<4 mm), anterior/posterior, maxilla/mandible, torque (<30 Ncm), augmentation (sinus-floor elevation or any grafting, spreading or splitting), open/closed healing.
- **Statistics:**
  - Chi-squared tests for bivariate comparisons.
  - Univariate mixed models with patient as a random factor, to account for implants clustered within patients.
  - Firth-corrected logistic regression when a model did not converge.
  - No multivariable model. p-values were interpreted descriptively. Analysed in SAS 9.4.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Primary outcome:** 9/186 implants (4.8%) failed before the final prosthesis.
- **Bivariate (chi-squared):**

  | Factor | Failure rate | p |
  |---|---|---|
  | Length <10 mm vs ≥10 mm | 17.6% vs 3.6% | 0.010 |
  | Augmentation vs none | 13.2% vs 2.7% | 0.007 |
  | Maxilla vs mandible | 7.1% vs 1.4% | 0.072 |
  | Posterior vs anterior | 6.3% vs 0% | 0.092 |
  | Closed vs open healing | 6.1% vs 0% | 0.113 |
  | Smokers vs non-smokers | 6.8% vs 4.2% | 0.484 |
  | Diabetes vs none | 5.3% vs 4.8% | 0.927 |
  | Torque <30 vs ≥30 Ncm | 4.8% vs 4.8% | 1.000 |

- **Mixed models (odds ratio, 95% CI):**
  - Augmentation 5.5 (1.4–21.7), p = 0.0174
  - Short implant 5.8 (1.3–26.4), p = 0.0230
  - Maxilla 5.6 (0.7–47.6), p = 0.11
  - Posterior 6.1 (0.3–111.3, Firth), p = 0.22
  - Smoker 1.7 (0.4–7.1), p = 0.49
- Failed implants were placed with 20–45 Ncm (mean 32 Ncm). No immediately loaded implant failed.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Author-stated:**
  - Retrospective design with a small sample.
  - Smoking quantity and periodontal diagnosis were not available.
  - Possible non-responder bias (about 90% response).
  - Operator experience varied.
- **Additional:**
  - Only 9 events, so confidence intervals are very wide (e.g., posterior OR 0.3–111).
  - Only univariate models. Augmentation, short implants, posterior site and maxilla are likely correlated (all are markers of deficient bone), so they cannot be separated.
  - The outcome is ascertained at final-prosthesis incorporation, so follow-up differs between implants.
  - Single implant system and a two-operator practice, which limits generalisability.
  - Indication bias: short implants are chosen where bone height is limited.

## 7. Glossary

- **Early failure:** no or inadequate osseointegration before functional loading. Here, loss before the final prosthesis was attached.
- **Open/closed healing:** healing abutment through the mucosa (one-stage) vs cover screw under the mucosa with re-exposure (two-stage).
- **Augmentation:** sinus-floor elevation or any grafting, including bone spreading or splitting.
- **Firth correction:** penalised likelihood logistic regression for sparse events or separation.
- **D1/D2:** the two operating dentists.
