## Why Ingested

기존 [[occlusion/didier-2026-occlusal-contacts-ios-vs-carbon-paper-concordance]]와 [[occlusion/rovira-lastra-2026-articulating-paper-accuracy-occlusal-points]]는 개별 교합접촉점(occlusal contact point) 검출의 IOS-vs-전통방법 일치도를 다뤘다. 본 논문(Wang 2024, n=10)은 다른 축 — 최대 교두감합위(MIP)의 상하악 간 공간적 관계(inter-arch distance) 자체의 정확도(trueness)를 안면궁 전이(facebow transfer) 기준과 비교한다. 결론은 "3종 IOS 모두 유의차 없음(equivalence)"이지만, n=10·표준편차가 군간 차이를 압도하는 상황에서 유의차 없음을 등가성으로 해석하는 것은 통계적으로 위험하다는 점이 이 논문을 넣는 핵심 이유 — 위키에 "긍정적 IOS 결과"를 무비판적으로 쌓지 않기 위한 대조 사례.

## Three-line Summary

Clinical cross-sectional trueness study (n=10 participants with good occlusal relationship and healthy TMJ, Sichuan University) comparing three intraoral scanners (Trios 3, Carestream 3600, Aoralscan 3) against a facebow-transfer + mechanical-articulator + lab-scan control for recording maximal intercuspal position (MIP), using summed inter-arch distances at incisor/canine/first-molar regions.

All three IOS showed no statistically significant difference from control on overall or regional deviation (ANOVA p=0.89–0.98); however, n=10 with SDs (±6.4 mm on ~39 mm sums) far exceeding the between-group differences (~0.6–1.4 mm) makes this a severely underpowered test to detect clinically meaningful differences — non-significance here is not evidence of equivalence.

All three scanners read systematically SMALLER than the control on every measure, a consistent one-directional pattern across independent devices that is hypothesis-generating (possible systematic IOS bias in MIP capture) but not statistically supported by this study's own significance tests.

## 세줄요약

임상 단면 정확도(trueness) 연구(n=10, 정상 교합·건강한 측두하악관절, 사천대학교): 3종 구강스캐너(IOS; Trios 3, Carestream 3600, Aoralscan 3)를 안면궁 전이(facebow transfer)+기계식 교합기+실험실 스캐너 대조군과 비교하여 최대 교두감합위(Maximal Intercuspal Position, MIP)의 절치/견치/제1대구치 부위 상하악 간 거리 합을 측정.

3종 IOS 모두 대조군과 전체·부위별 편차에서 통계적으로 유의한 차이 없음(ANOVA p=0.89–0.98). 그러나 n=10에 표준편차(약 39mm 합계값에 ±6.4mm)가 군간 차이(약 0.6–1.4mm)를 압도하는 상황이라 임상적으로 의미 있는 차이를 검출하기엔 심각하게 검정력 부족(underpowered) — 유의차 없음이 곧 등가성(equivalence)의 근거는 아니다.

3종 스캐너 모두 대조군보다 일관되게 작은 값을 보였다(모든 측정치에서 방향 일치) — 독립된 3개 기기에서 같은 방향의 편향이 반복된 것은 가설을 생성할 만한 관찰이지만, 이 연구의 유의성 검정으로는 뒷받침되지 않는 소견이다.

## Summary

According to PubMed, ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.7518/hxkq.2024.2023277); PMID 38597082; PMCID PMC11034409) this study from the State Key Laboratory of Oral Diseases, West China Hospital of Stomatology, Sichuan University, asked whether three intraoral scanners (Trios 3, Carestream 3600, Aoralscan 3) can reproduce the spatial inter-arch relationship at maximal intercuspal position (MIP) as truely as a conventional facebow-transfer + mechanical-articulator + laboratory-scan workflow. Ten participants with good occlusal relationships and healthy TMJ were scanned with each device following its manufacturer-endorsed MIP workflow; the outcome was the sum of measured inter-arch distances at paired incisor, canine, and first-molar landmarks (overall D, and regional D-incisor/D-canine/D-first-molar), compared after best-fit alignment to the control casts.

ANOVA found no statistically significant difference between any of the three IOS and the control for the overall sum or any regional sum (p=0.96, 0.98, 0.96, 0.89). The authors concluded that, with standardized scanning protocols, all three IOS demonstrated "comparable trueness" to the conventional method for recording MIP. This conclusion should be read cautiously: a non-significant ANOVA in a sample of n=10, with standard deviations (~±6.4 mm on a ~39 mm sum) that dwarf the observed between-group differences (~0.6–1.4 mm), is exactly the signature of an underpowered test — absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, and no power calculation or pre-specified equivalence margin is reported in the retrieved abstract to justify the equivalence framing.

## Key Contributions

- **Frames IOS accuracy for MIP as a spatial inter-arch-distance question**, distinct from occlusal-contact-POINT detection (the axis Didier 2026 and Rovira-Lastra 2026 address) — a genuinely different outcome measure within the same clinical problem (recording MIP digitally).
- **Reports directionally consistent under-measurement**: all three independent IOS systems (different manufacturers, different scan engines) read smaller inter-arch sums than the facebow/articulator control on every one of four measures — worth flagging as hypothesis-generating even though not statistically tested for direction.
- **Illustrates a common pitfall in equivalence claims**: "no significant difference, n=10" is treated by the authors as "comparable trueness... fulfilling clinical requirements," without an equivalence margin or power justification visible in the retrieved text — a caution point for reading similar small-n IOS validation studies in this wiki.

## Methodology

- **Design**: clinical cross-sectional trueness study, single center (West China Hospital of Stomatology, Sichuan University, Chengdu).
- **Population**: 10 participants with good occlusal relationships and a healthy temporomandibular joint (the easiest possible case; excludes worn, crowded, or compromised dentitions).
- **Control group**: facebow transfer + MIP bite registration → maxillary/mandibular stone casts mounted on a mechanical articulator → scanned with a desktop (laboratory) scanner to obtain reference digital casts. Measurement points placed at bilateral central incisors, canines, and first molars on the control casts; inter-arch distances at matching points summed to give D (overall) and D-incisor/D-canine/D-first-molar (regional).
- **Experimental groups**: three intraoral scanners — Trios 3, Carestream 3600, Aoralscan 3 — each used to scan the same participants intraorally at MIP, following that manufacturer's own endorsed scanning workflow.
- **Alignment**: control casts (with measurement points already placed) were best-fit aligned to each experimental group's digital casts; D, D-incisor, D-canine, D-first-molar recomputed on the aligned control casts for paired comparison.
- **Statistics**: ANOVA comparing control vs. each experimental group's D, D-incisor, D-canine, D-first-molar.
- **Power/equivalence margin**: not stated in the retrieved abstract; full text was not retrievable (see Deviation note below), so this could not be verified against the original Methods section.

## Results

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control (facebow + articulator) | 39.58 ± 6.40 | 13.64 ± 3.58 | 14.91 ± 2.85 | 11.03 ± 1.56 |
| Trios 3 | 38.99 ± 6.60 | 13.42 ± 3.66 | 14.55 ± 2.87 | 11.03 ± 1.69 |
| Carestream 3600 | 38.57 ± 6.36 | 13.56 ± 3.68 | 14.45 ± 2.85 | 10.55 ± 1.41 |
| Aoralscan 3 | 38.16 ± 5.69 | 13.03 ± 3.54 | 14.23 ± 2.59 | 10.90 ± 1.54 |
| ANOVA p-value | 0.96 | 0.98 | 0.96 | 0.89 |

- No statistically significant difference between control and any IOS on any measure.
- Every IOS group's mean is numerically SMALLER than the control on all four measures (Aoralscan 3 smallest overall; Carestream 3600 smallest on first-molar).
- Authors' conclusion: "with standardized scanning protocols, the three intraoral scanners demonstrated comparable trueness to traditional methods in recording MIP, fulfilling clinical requirements" — an equivalence claim resting on non-significance in n=10, not on a demonstrated equivalence margin.

## Limitations and Future Work

- **Underpowered for equivalence**: n=10 with SDs (~±6.4 mm) far larger than the between-group differences (~0.6–1.4 mm); no power calculation or equivalence margin found in the retrieved abstract. A true difference of this magnitude could easily be missed.
- **Control is not ground truth**: the facebow-transfer + bite-registration + stone-cast + lab-scan chain carries its own compounding error at each step; "comparable to conventional" is not the same as "accurate."
- **Best-case population**: 10 participants with good occlusion and healthy TMJ — results do not generalize to worn, crowded, or compromised dentitions where MIP registration is clinically harder.
- **Scope limited to inter-arch relationship trueness**, not detection of individual occlusal contact points (see Didier 2026, Rovira-Lastra 2026 for that axis).
