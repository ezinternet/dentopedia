## Why Ingested

Risk factors for bisphosphonate (BP)-associated MRONJ are well characterized, but dedicated denosumab analyses were sparse and small (prior largest comparison n=14). This retrospective single-center cohort (n=128, Munich 2011–2020) is one of the first adequately powered three-group comparisons — denosumab-only controls, denosumab-MRONJ, and BP→denosumab-switch MRONJ — quantifying demographic, co-medication, systemic-disease, and oral-inflammatory risk factors with logistic-regression odds ratios. It directly complements the Korean low-dose osteoporosis cohorts [[drug/mronj/jung-2022-denosumab-mronj-osteoporosis-5year]] and [[drug/mronj/cho-2026-clinical-characteristics-osteonecrosis-jaw-related]] (both of which it anticipates on BP-switch neutrality and dose dependence), extends the breast-cancer findings of [[drug/mronj/yokoo-2025-denosumab-mronj-breast-cancer-bone]] with adjusted ORs, and supplies the risk-factor evidence base referenced by [[drug/mronj/ruggiero-2022-aaoms-mronj-position-paper-update]] staging/prevention guidance.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center cohort (n=128 denosumab patients, Munich 2011–2020): 40 controls without MRONJ, 46 with MRONJ on denosumab alone, 42 with MRONJ after a bisphosphonate→denosumab switch.

Logistic regression identified hormonal therapy (OR 6.33 [1.41–28.43]), chemotherapy (OR 2.97), hypertension (OR 2.96), breast cancer (OR 2.83), denture use (OR 2.74), periodontal disease (OR 2.46), apical ostitis (OR 2.04), and longer ART duration (OR 1.07/month) as significant MRONJ risk factors; high-dose 120 mg denosumab predominated in MRONJ groups (p<0.01).

The BP→denosumab switch did not affect MRONJ onset (p=0.86), while drug holiday (OR 0.24 [0.07–0.79]) and dental implants present before ART (OR 0.85) were associated with reduced risk.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First sizeable (n=128) three-group risk-factor analysis for denosumab-associated MRONJ, adding a BP→denosumab switch arm (n=42) to the classic case/control design
- Quantified adjusted risk factors unique to denosumab: hormonal therapy OR 6.33, chemotherapy OR 2.97, hypertension OR 2.96, breast cancer OR 2.83, denture use OR 2.74, periodontal disease OR 2.46, apical ostitis OR 2.04, ART duration OR 1.07 per month
- Demonstrated dose dependence: 120 mg (oncologic) denosumab strongly concentrated in MRONJ groups (76.1% and 71.4% of groups II/III vs 32.5% of controls, p<0.01)
- Showed BP→denosumab switching did not influence MRONJ onset (p=0.86) — consistent with the same group's prior surgical-outcome work
- Counterintuitive safety signal: dental implants present before ART were NOT associated with increased MRONJ risk (OR 0.846, i.e., slightly protective), attributed to better oral hygiene/fewer periodontitis cases among implant patients
- Drug holiday around dental procedures associated with >75% MRONJ risk reduction (OR 0.241), though based on only 13 treated patients — flagged by authors as hypothesis-generating

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Screening/enrollment**: 273 consecutive denosumab-treated patients identified; 145 excluded (head-and-neck irradiation, maxillofacial metastatic bone disease, missing follow-up/panoramic X-ray/medical history); 128 included
- **Groups**: I control (n=40, denosumab, no MRONJ); II Test 1 (n=46, denosumab + MRONJ, 49 lesions); III Test 2 (n=42, BP→denosumab switch + MRONJ, 48 lesions)
- **Inclusion**: ≥1 denosumab dose; MRONJ per AAOMS criteria (groups II/III); panoramic X-ray; ≥12-month follow-up; complete medical history
- **Variables**: demographics (age, sex, smoking, alcohol), underlying disease (cancer type, osteoporosis, bone metastasis), ART details (dose 120/60 mg, duration, switch), co-medication (chemo/molecular-targeted, corticosteroid, hormonal, antiangiogenic), comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, cardio-pulmonary, thyroid, rheumatic, infectious), intraoral findings (teeth, retained roots, implants pre-ART, periodontal disease, apical periodontitis, cysts, dentures, dental procedures)
- **Care pathway**: yearly prophylactic follow-up per 2018 German MRONJ guidelines (extraction of non-preservable/periodontally compromised teeth and implants, cyst excision, denture adjustment); surgical MRONJ protocol by single surgeon (CP) with amoxicillin/clavulanate 875/125 mg from 1 week preop up to 3 weeks postop (clindamycin 600 mg if allergic); no investigator-recommended drug holiday (prescriber-initiated in 13 cases)
- **Statistics**: SPSS v21 + R 4.0.3; Kolmogorov–Smirnov normality; t-test, chi-square, ANOVA/Kruskal–Wallis with Bonferroni post hoc; binary logistic regression for risk factors

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Cohort profile (n=128)**: female 61.7%; median age 72.2 y; cancer 67.2% (breast 31.3%, prostate 24.2%), osteoporosis 44.5%; bone metastasis 67.2%; denosumab 120 mg 60.9% / 60 mg 39.1%; prior BP 32.8% (zoledronate 16.4%); median ART duration 38.7 months; median follow-up 14.0 months. MRONJ present in 88 (68.8% by design); mandible 68.0% / maxilla 32.0% (9 patients both jaws); surgery in 92.0%.

**Univariate significance vs control**: underlying disease (p=0.01), denosumab dosage (p<0.01), co-medications (p=0.05), vascular diseases (p=0.04), dental inflammations; abstract-level: chemo/hormonal therapy p=0.02, breast cancer p=0.03, corticosteroids p=0.04, hypertension p=0.02, diabetes mellitus p=0.04, periodontal disease p=0.03, apical ostitis p=0.02, denture use p=0.02; medication switch p=0.86 (NS).

**Logistic regression — risk-elevating predictors**:

| Variable | Odds ratio [95% CI] | p |
|---|---|---|
| Hormonal therapy | 6.33 [1.41–28.43] | 0.016 |
| Chemotherapy (or molecular targeted) | 2.97 [1.18–7.46] | 0.021 |
| Hypertension | 2.96 [1.22–7.16] | 0.016 |
| Breast cancer | 2.83 [1.12–7.12] | 0.027 |
| Denture use | 2.74 [1.20–6.28] | 0.017 |
| Periodontal disease | 2.46 [1.12–5.40] | 0.026 |
| Apical ostitis | 2.04 [0.92–4.51] | 0.018 |
| Duration of ART (per month) | 1.07 [1.00–1.13] | 0.037 |

**Risk-decreasing variables**:

| Variable | Odds ratio [95% CI] | p |
|---|---|---|
| Drug holiday | 0.241 [0.073–0.792] | 0.019 |
| Dental implants prior to ART | 0.846 [0.189–1.018] | 0.043 |

Sex was NOT a risk factor despite 61.7% female enrollment; smoking and alcohol were non-significant.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small sample size; retrospective design with post-hoc-defined inclusion window
- Heterogeneous antiresorptive intake, underlying disease, and immunomodulatory co-medication; multifactorial regression models accounting for covariance are left to future confirmatory studies
- Pure intervention groups will become increasingly difficult to assemble
- Drug-holiday finding rests on only 13 patients — authors call for a large prospective trial
- Authors' future-work framing: current data usable for power estimation of a prospective trial; interdisciplinary decision-making for discontinuation (no German guideline recommendation exists for breast/prostate cancer or myeloma)

## 6. Related Work

- Prior denosumab comparison was limited to n=14 (Okuma et al.), which found prolonged intake and sex influential; Wick et al. replicate the duration effect weakly (OR 1.07) and attribute apparent sex effects to indication mix (breast cancer/osteoporosis skew female)
- BP-MRONJ risk-factor literature (chemotherapy, corticosteroids, dental procedures, apical ostitis, periodontal disease, female sex/breast cancer) correlates well with the denosumab findings here — supporting inflammation-driven pathogenesis common to both drug classes despite divergent pharmacokinetics (BP accumulates in hydroxyapatite vs denosumab cleared within weeks)
- Dental-implant evidence in BP users is contradictory (risk factor in some overviews/Jacobsen; not confirmed in the Korean Ryu/Kim/Kwon cohort); this study extends the neutral-to-protective position to denosumab
- Meta-analytic evidence that drug holidays do not reduce MRONJ risk is mostly BP-based; the pharmacology of denosumab (reversible RANKL inhibition) reopens the question for short periprocedural holidays

## 7. Glossary

- **MRONJ** — Medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw; exposed jawbone >8 weeks in antiresorptive-exposed patients without radiation/metastasis history (AAOMS criteria)
- **Denosumab (DNO)** — Human monoclonal RANK-ligand antibody; subcutaneous, non-accumulating, effect reversible within weeks; 120 mg q4wk oncologic (Xgeva) vs 60 mg q6mo osteoporosis (Prolia)
- **Bisphosphonate (BP)** — Hydroxyapatite-binding antiresorptive acting via mevalonate-pathway osteoclast toxicity; zoledronate/alendronate/ibandronate in this cohort
- **ART** — Antiresorptive therapy (BP or denosumab)
- **Apical ostitis** — Apical periodontitis; periapical bone inflammation from pulp necrosis
- **Drug holiday** — Prescribed interruption of ART around invasive dental procedures
- **SRE** — Skeletal-related event (fracture, spinal cord compression, need for radiotherapy/surgery) — the oncologic risk traded off against drug holidays
