## Why Ingested

Provides quantitative eruption-success rates for maxillary and mandibular third molars following orthodontic second-molar extraction, along with predictors of failure (Nolla developmental stage, patient age) — directly relevant to the clinical decision of whether to extract second molars in orthodontic treatment and expect third-molar replacement. Complements [[wiki/oral-surgery/third-molar/sun-2026-third-molar-health-management-expert-consensus]] on third-molar management indications.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study of 48 orthodontic patients (128 extracted second molars) using panoramic radiographs to evaluate spontaneous third-molar eruption success (defined as proximal contact with first molar and inter-tooth angle ≤35°) over a median 3–4 years.
Maxillary third molars achieved successful eruption in 96.2% of cases; mandibular third molars in 66.2%; unsuccessful mandibular eruptions were predominantly caused by excessive mesial tilting or absence of proximal contact; both outcomes worsened with higher Nolla developmental stage and older patient age.
Maxillary second-molar extraction for orthodontic purposes can reliably be replaced by the third molar; mandibular outcomes are less predictable, especially when Nolla stage exceeds 8 or patients are older at time of extraction.

## 세줄요약

교정 목적으로 제2대구치 (second molar)를 발거한 48명(발거 치아 128개)을 파노라마 방사선 사진으로 추적한 후향적 연구로, 성공 기준은 제1대구치와의 인접 접촉 형성 및 두 치아간 각도 ≤35°였고 중앙 맹출 기간은 3–4년이었다.
상악 제3대구치 (maxillary third molar)의 성공 맹출률은 96.2%, 하악 제3대구치 (mandibular third molar)는 66.2%였으며, 하악 실패 사례의 대부분은 과도한 근심 경사 또는 인접 접촉 불형성에 기인했고, 높은 놀라 발육 단계 (Nolla developmental stage) 및 고령이 실패 위험인자였다.
상악은 교정 목적의 제2대구치 발거 후 제3대구치로 신뢰성 있게 대체 가능하나, 하악은 예측성이 낮으며 특히 Nolla 8단계 초과 시 근심 경사 실패 위험이 높아 주의가 필요하다.

## Key Contributions

- Established quantitative success rates for spontaneous third-molar eruption after orthodontic second-molar extraction: 96.2% maxillary, 66.2% mandibular
- Operationalized a reproducible success criterion: proximal contact with adjacent first molar + inter-tooth angle ≤35° on panoramic radiograph
- Identified Nolla developmental stage >8 at time of extraction as a predictor of open proximal contact (maxillary) and eruption failure (mandibular)
- Showed that older patient age at extraction correlates with higher risk of unsuccessful third-molar eruption
- Characterized the predominant failure mechanism in mandibular third molars as excessive mesial tilting and/or absent proximal contact

## Methodology

- **Study design**: Retrospective cohort study
- **Sample**: 48 patients (age 11–23 years at extraction), 128 permanently extracted second molars (orthodontic indication)
- **Intervention**: Second-molar extraction during or before orthodontic treatment
- **Outcome assessment**: Panoramic radiographs taken before extraction and after third-molar eruption; position evaluated by two measures — proximal contact with adjacent first molar, and inter-tooth angle
- **Success definition**: Proximal contact present AND angle between third molar and first molar ≤35°
- **Follow-up**: Median eruption time 3–4 years (interquartile range 2 years)
- **Predictor variables**: Nolla developmental stage (1–10 scale of tooth development), patient age at extraction
- **Radiographic method**: Panoramic radiography; reproducibility referenced from Larheim & Svanaes 1986 for rotational panoramic angular/linear measurements

## Key Results

| Jaw | Successful eruption rate | Main failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Maxillary | 96.2% | Open proximal contact when Nolla stage >8 |
| Mandibular | 66.2% | Excessive mesial tilting or absent proximal contact |

- Maxillary third molars uprighted and successfully replaced second molars in the vast majority of cases
- When Nolla developmental stage >8 (close to fully formed), proximal contact could remain open in maxillary cases
- Most mandibular third molars also uprighted satisfactorily; unsuccessful eruptions were due to excessive mesial tilting or failure to establish proximal contact
- Unsuccessful eruption in both jaws was more common in older patients and those with higher Nolla developmental stages at time of extraction
- The study included 57 citations in literature and has received 57 citations per article metrics

## Limitations

- Retrospective design precludes randomization or control group with alternative extraction patterns
- Small and heterogeneous sample (48 patients, mixed ages 11–23)
- Single institution; no data on operator variability or concurrent orthodontic mechanotherapy details
- Panoramic radiographs cannot fully characterize three-dimensional position (e.g., buccal/lingual tipping)
- No functional occlusal outcomes reported beyond radiographic position criteria

## Glossary

- **Nolla developmental stage**: 10-stage radiographic classification (Nolla 1960) for permanent tooth development — stage 1 (crypt present) through stage 10 (apex closed); stage 8 = three-quarters root complete; used to time extraction for optimal third-molar eruption outcome
- **Spontaneous eruption**: Unassisted migration and clinical eruption of a tooth without orthodontic traction, into its natural position in the arch after adjacent tooth removal
- **Mesial tilting**: Anterior inclination of a tooth (here, third molar) measured as an angle on panoramic radiograph; excessive mesial tilting was the primary failure mechanism for mandibular third molars
- **Proximal contact**: Physical interproximal contact between adjacent teeth; absence indicates failure of the third molar to fully replace the extracted second molar
- **Inter-tooth angle**: Angle measured between the third molar and the first molar on panoramic radiograph; ≤35° defined successful upright position
- **Retrospective cohort**: Study design using existing records/radiographs of patients who already received treatment, comparing outcomes without prospective randomization
