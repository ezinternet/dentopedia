## Why Ingested

[[periodontics/donertas-2026-gbt-subgingival-debridement-gcf-biomarkers]] tracks specific GCF cytokine responses to debridement; this scoping review supplies the broader biomarker landscape — mapping which GCF/saliva bone-turnover markers (IL-1β, sclerostin, OPG, IL-34, OC, TNF-α, IL-6, MMP-9) span periodontitis, MRONJ, and osteoporosis, and where they overlap. It also cross-connects periodontitis biomarkers to MRONJ risk, extending [[drug/mronj/kwon-2023-mronj-position-papers-what-changed]] toward non-invasive oral-fluid diagnostics, and to alveolar-bone RANKL/OPG/sclerostin biology in [[bone-biology/xu-2016-sclerostin-wnt-occlusal-hypofunction-alveolar-bone]].

## Three-line Summary

Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR, 37 observational studies) of oral-fluid bone-turnover biomarkers across three jawbone diseases: periodontitis, MRONJ, and osteoporosis.

GCF markers IL-1β, sclerostin, OPG, and IL-34 dominate the literature (mainly periodontitis and osteoporosis), while saliva markers OC, IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, OPG, and MMP-9 span all three diseases — extensive overlap enables saliva as a convenient non-invasive sampling medium for bone-turnover assessment.

Clinical implication: oral fluids offer accessible multi-biomarker diagnostics for jawbone diseases, though no single marker is oral-cavity-exclusive — systemic contribution complicates specificity, and multi-marker panels are required for clinical translation.

- **Search window**: August 2014 – August 2024, five databases (Web of Science, EBSCOhost Dentistry & Oral Sciences Source, Cochrane Library, Scopus, PubMed) + grey literature (Google Scholar)

## 2. Key Contributions

1. First scoping review to consolidate bone-turnover biomarkers specifically obtainable from **oral fluids (saliva) and GCF** — rather than blood/serum — across the three jawbone-turnover diseases: periodontitis, MRONJ/BRONJ, and osteoporosis.
2. Answers two research questions: (i) which bone-turnover biomarkers are collectable from oral fluid/GCF in these diseases, and (ii) the similarities/differences among biomarkers across diseases.
3. Identifies the **most significant markers**: GCF → IL-1β, sclerostin, OPG, IL-34; saliva → OC, IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, OPG, MMP-9.
4. Highlights extensive **overlap** between GCF and saliva biomarkers, and argues saliva is the more convenient sampling medium because GCF is a component of saliva.
5. Frames periodontitis as a shared inflammatory crossroads with MRONJ and osteoporosis — periodontal inflammation is a risk factor for MRONJ, and IL-1β/IL-6/TNF-α are common to all three.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Framework**: JBI scoping-review manual; reported per PRISMA-ScR. Registered in OSF (20 Sept 2024).
- **Eligibility**: Studies investigating bone-turnover biomarkers of jawbone diseases (osteoporosis, MRONJ, periodontitis) using oral fluid/GCF; English; full text available; RCT, case-control, analytical observational (prospective/retrospective cohort), cross-sectional, or case-series. Minimum ≥10 cases and ≥10 controls. Excluded: systematic reviews, case reports, commentaries, editorials, letters.
- **Screening tools**: Rayyan (rayyan.ai) + EndNote X20; two independent reviewers; disagreements by consensus/third reviewer.
- **Data extraction**: study type, subject count, case/control characteristics, disease, assessment tools, and bone-turnover markers in GCF vs saliva.
- **Risk of bias**: RoBANS 2 across 7 domains (comparability of target group, target group selection, confounders, exposure measurement, blinding of assessors, outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Screening flow**: 14,965 database + 34 other citations → limited to last 10 years, duplicates removed → 5,030 + 26 → eligibility → 136 + 26 → final **37 articles** included. Most studies addressed periodontitis, then MRONJ/BRONJ, then osteoporosis.

**GCF markers (18 studies, mostly periodontitis)**:
- Periodontitis: sclerostin ↑ (2 studies; inhibits osteoblast bone formation); TRAP/TRAcP-5b ↑ (osteoclast resorption); DPD ↑ (osteoclast activity); interleukins, TNF-α, MMPs ↑ scaling with disease stage (healthy < gingivitis < periodontitis). IL-34 ↑ (Batra 2019; Guruprasad & Pradeep 2018). IL-1β and IL-37 ↑ (Görgün 2021).
- **MRONJ**: **no human GCF bone-turnover biomarker studies identified** — only an animal model (IL-36α ↑ in inflamed periodontal tissue). A clear evidence gap.
- Osteoporosis: OPG ↑ in post-menopausal osteoporosis 3–6 months after bisphosphonate treatment (Özden 2017, likely BP slowing bone loss); IL-6 and RANKL ↑ in combined chronic periodontitis + osteoporosis.

**Saliva markers (24 studies, mostly periodontitis)**:
- Periodontitis: CTX, OC, ON ↑ (correlate with PPD, BOP, bone loss); IL-1β, IL-6, MMP-8 ↑; RANKL/OPG ratio ↑; OSM, LIF, IL-11 ↑ (fall after treatment); MIP-1α, MMP-2, MMP-9 ↑.
- **MRONJ**: unlike GCF, saliva has many candidate markers — MMP-9, TNF-α, IL-6 ↑ (also drivers of periodontal bone loss); periodontal inflammation suggested as MRONJ risk factor.
- Osteoporosis: BAP, OC, NTX, CTX ↑ vs controls; calcium, OC, ALP ↑ in male osteoporotic patients.

**Bone-turnover marker taxonomy (Table 3)**: formation (osteoblast) = OC, BAP, (P1NP*); regulators = RANKL, OPG, sclerostin; resorption (osteoclast) = CTX, NTX, TRAcP-5b, DPD, calcium, ICTP. (*P1NP, BSP, OP were NOT captured in any included study.)

**Overlap / conclusion**: IL-1β most extensively studied in both GCF and saliva. RANKL/OPG ratio and MMPs identified as resorption markers in both. No biomarker was oral-cavity-exclusive — all are systemic, complicating precise oral-fluid-only diagnosis, though they reflect overall health status. Most-frequently studied salivary proteins: OC and CTX.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Diagnosis of complex oral diseases needs **more than one biomarker**; single-marker accuracy is hard to prove — a core limitation for clinical translation.
- GCF literature is heavily skewed to periodontitis; MRONJ and osteoporosis GCF data are sparse (no human GCF MRONJ studies at all).
- All included studies were non-randomized observational; every study showed high risk of bias for "confounders" and "blinding of assessors"; two also for "comparability of target group."
- No biomarker is oral-cavity-specific (all systemic), limiting diagnostic specificity from oral fluid alone.
- Future: integrate bone-turnover markers with genomic/transcriptomic/proteomic and pharmacological-response indicators for early diagnosis; identify disease-specific markers for osteoporosis and MRONJ beyond the shared periodontitis panel.

## 7. Glossary

- **GCF** — Gingival crevicular fluid; inflammatory exudate from periodontal tissues, collected via perio paper/filter strips; derived from blood.
- **Oral fluid / saliva** — blood filtrate containing molecular biomarkers; GCF is a component; passive drooling is the most accurate collection method.
- **MRONJ / BRONJ** — Medication-related (formerly bisphosphonate-related) osteonecrosis of the jaw.
- **IL-1β / IL-6 / TNF-α** — pro-inflammatory cytokines linked to periodontal bone loss and shared across all three diseases.
- **Sclerostin** — osteocyte-derived Wnt inhibitor; suppresses osteoblast activity/bone formation.
- **OPG / RANKL** — bone-turnover regulators; RANKL drives osteoclast resorption, OPG inhibits it by binding RANKL; the RANKL/OPG ratio indexes resorptive activity.
- **IL-34** — significant GCF periodontitis biomarker.
- **OC (osteocalcin) / BAP** — bone-formation (osteoblast) markers.
- **CTX / NTX / TRAcP-5b / DPD / ICTP** — bone-resorption (osteoclast/collagen-breakdown) markers.
- **MMP-8 / MMP-9 / MMP-2** — matrix metalloproteinases; extracellular-matrix degradation and bone resorption.
- **RoBANS 2** — Revised Risk of Bias Assessment Tool for Non-Randomized Studies (7 domains).
- **PRISMA-ScR** — PRISMA extension for Scoping Reviews.
