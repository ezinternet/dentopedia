## Why Ingested
Foundational 2015 narrative review of bone cell biology — the four bone cell types, the three-phase basic multicellular unit (BMU) remodeling cycle, the RANKL/RANK/OPG axis, osteocyte mechanosensing, and the endocrine functions of bone — ingested to anchor the canonical biology underlying later remodeling and alveolar-bone pages. ## Three-line Summary
2015 narrative review consolidating the structure and function of four bone cell types (osteoblasts, bone lining cells, osteocytes, osteoclasts) within the three-phase BMU-driven remodeling cycle.
Osteocytes (90–95% of bone cells, lifespan up to 25 years) act as mechanosensors through the lacunocanalicular system and orchestrators of remodeling; RANKL/RANK/OPG governs osteoclastogenesis and osteocyte apoptosis chemotactically recruits osteoclasts.
Bone is framed as an endocrine organ — undercarboxylated osteocalcin acts on pancreas, adipose tissue, testis and brain, and osteocyte-derived FGF23 regulates phosphate — while resorption/formation imbalance underlies osteoporosis and osteopetrosis.

## 세줄요약
2015년 서술적 리뷰(narrative review)로 골조직의 4종 세포(조골세포, 골표면세포, 골세포, 파골세포)와 BMU 기반 3단계 골개량(bone remodeling) 사이클을 정리.
골세포(Osteocyte)는 전체 골세포의 90–95%, 수명 최대 25년으로 라쿠나-캐나리쿨라 시스템(lacunocanalicular system)을 통한 기계감지(mechanosensing)와 골개량 조율을 담당하고, RANKL/RANK/OPG 축이 파골세포 형성을 조절하며 골세포 사세포(apoptosis)가 파골세포를 화학유인(chemotaxis)함.
골은 내분비 기관으로도 기술되어 저카복실화 오스테오칼신(undercarboxylated osteocalcin)이 췌장·지방조직·고환·뇌에 작용하고 골세포 유래 FGF23이 인 대사를 조절하며, 개량 불균형이 골다공증(osteoporosis)·골경화증(osteopetrosis)으로 이어짐.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Defines bone as a mineralized connective tissue with four cell types — osteoblasts, bone lining cells, osteocytes and osteoclasts — and lists its roles: locomotion, support/protection of soft tissues, calcium and phosphate storage, and harboring bone marrow.
- Describes the remodeling cycle in three phases — (1) osteoclast resorption, (2) reversal/transition, (3) osteoblast formation — coordinated by the basic multicellular unit (BMU).
- Details osteoblast differentiation from mesenchymal stem cells: Runx2 as master gene (Runx2-null mice lack osteoblasts), with Dlx5 and osterix (Osx) crucial; ALP-positive preosteoblasts; FGF, microRNAs and connexin 43 as modulators.
- Describes matrix synthesis in two steps: organic matrix deposition (type I collagen, OCN, osteonectin, BSP II, osteopontin, decorin, biglycan) then mineralization in vesicular (30–200 nm matrix vesicles, annexin calcium channels, ALP-generated phosphate) and fibrillar phases producing hydroxyapatite.
- Establishes osteocytes as the most abundant (90–95%) and longest-lived (up to 25 years) bone cells, with up to 50 cytoplasmic processes per cell forming the lacunocanalicular system; osteocyte surface estimated 400-fold larger than the Haversian/Volkmann systems and >100-fold larger than trabecular bone surface.
- Identifies osteocyte mechanotransduction (piezoelectric effect; primary cilium/polycystins 1–2 and focal-adhesion/actin-associated complexes; ATP, NO, Ca²⁺, PGE₂, PGI₂) and positions osteocytes as remodeling orchestrators via sclerostin, DKK-1, RANKL and apoptosis-driven chemotaxis.
- Lays out the RANKL/RANK/OPG system as the key mediator of osteoclastogenesis, with M-CSF/cFMS, NFATc1, DC-STAMP, PU.1, cFos, MITF, TRAP and cathepsin K, and four osteoclast membrane domains (sealing zone, ruffled border, basolateral and functional secretory domains).
- Reviews osteoclast–osteoblast coupling (semaphorins, ephrins) and notes the endocrine functions of bone: undercarboxylated osteocalcin effects on pancreas, adipose tissue, testis and brain, and osteocyte FGF23 regulation of phosphate, giving rise to osteoimmunology.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Narrative (non-systematic) review article of current data on bone cell structure/function and the local and systemic factors influencing bone remodeling; illustrated with light and electron micrographs of rat alveolar bone (Figures 1–5) and a schematic of the RANKL/RANK/OPG system.
- **n**: Not applicable — no cohort, no patients, no reported sample size; synthesis of in vitro and in vivo literature.
- **Outcomes**: Qualitative mechanistic synthesis — cell lineages and differentiation, matrix mineralization, mechanosensing, osteoclastogenesis, cell–cell coupling, and systemic/endocrine effects; no quantitative endpoints or effect sizes reported.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Osteoblasts comprise 4–6% of total resident bone cells; osteocytes comprise 90–95% and have a lifespan up to 25 years.
- Four proposed osteocyte maturation stages: osteoid-osteocyte, preosteocyte, young osteocyte, mature osteocyte; mature osteocytes downregulate OCN, BSP II, collagen type I and ALP and upregulate DMP1 and sclerostin.
- Matrix vesicles are 30–200 nm in diameter; osteocytes extend up to 50 cytoplasmic processes per cell.
- Osteocyte surface area estimated 400-fold larger than all Haversian and Volkmann systems and more than 100-fold larger than trabecular bone surface.
- Mechanical loading stimulates osteocytes to produce anabolic factors (PGE₂, prostacyclin/PGI₂, NO, IGF-1); unloading upregulates sclerostin and DKK-1 (osteoblast inhibitors) and osteoclastogenesis-stimulating factors.
- Sclerostin (SOST gene product) negatively regulates bone formation by antagonizing Lrp5 in the Wnt/β-catenin pathway.
- Resorption/formation imbalance: excessive osteoclast resorption without matching formation → bone loss and osteoporosis; the reverse → osteopetrosis.
- Osteoclasts from long-bone marrow form faster than those from the jaw, possibly reflecting site-specific marrow cellular composition.
- Undercarboxylated osteocalcin regulates pancreatic insulin secretion/sensitivity and β-cell proliferation, adipose adiponectin expression, testicular testosterone synthesis, and hippocampal monoamine/GABA balance; osteocyte FGF23 acts on parathyroid gland and kidney to lower circulating phosphate.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- The authors explicitly note that bone lining cell functions are "not completely understood."
- Mechanisms converting mechanical stimuli into biochemical signals in osteocytes are "not well known"; only two candidate mechanisms (cilium/polycystins and focal-adhesion/actin complexes) are proposed.
- The precise osteoclastogenesis-stimulating factors produced by osteocytes remain uncertain — candidates include RANKL/OPG ratio, VEGF, CCL2, HMGB1 and M-CSF; future studies required.
- Direct contact between mature osteoblasts and osteoclasts has not been demonstrated in vivo and remains controversial.
- Bone's endocrine function is described as still poorly understood; the review is qualitative and reports no quantitative effect sizes.

## 7. Glossary
- **BMU**: Basic Multicellular Unit — the temporary anatomical structure of coordinated osteoclasts, osteoblasts, osteocytes and bone lining cells that carries out one remodeling cycle.
- **Osteoblast**: Cuboidal, mesenchymal-derived bone-forming cell that secretes osteoid and drives its mineralization.
- **Bone lining cell**: Quiescent, flat osteoblast covering inactive bone surfaces; produces OPG and RANKL and helps couple resorption to formation.
- **Osteocyte**: Terminal, long-lived cell embedded in a lacuna; mechanosensor and remodeling orchestrator connected by cytoplasmic processes in canaliculi.
- **Osteoclast**: Terminally differentiated multinucleated hematopoietic-lineage cell that resorbs bone via sealing zone and ruffled border.
- **RANKL/RANK/OPG**: Axis controlling osteoclastogenesis — RANKL (from osteoblasts/osteocytes/stromal cells) binds RANK on precursors; OPG is the decoy inhibitor.
- **Sclerostin (SOST)**: Osteocyte-derived negative regulator of bone formation that antagonizes Lrp5 in Wnt/β-catenin signaling.
- **DMP1**: Dentin matrix protein 1 — osteocyte marker upregulated on maturation.
- **E11/gp38 (podoplanin)**: Protein highly expressed in embedding osteocytes, implicated in dendrite formation.
- **Matrix vesicle**: 30–200 nm vesicle released from the osteoblast apical membrane that initiates mineralization by concentrating calcium and phosphate.
- **Lacunocanalicular system**: Network of osteocyte lacunae and canaliculi housing cytoplasmic processes, enabling intercellular signaling and mechanosensing.
- **Piezoelectric effect**: Conversion of mechanical stimuli into biochemical signals, here attributed to osteocytes.
- **Osteocalcin (OCN)**: Vitamin K-dependent bone matrix protein; the undercarboxylated form acts as an endocrine hormone on multiple organs.
- **FGF23**: Osteocyte-derived fibroblast growth factor that acts on parathyroid gland and kidney to reduce circulating phosphate.
