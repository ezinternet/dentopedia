## One-line Summary
Review (Micron 2005) synthesizing histological sequence of peri-implant osteogenesis — blood clot → afibrillar calcified layer → woven bone → reparative trabecular bone → lamellar bone — and contrasting health vs osteoporotic (senile/postmenopausal) healing trajectories.

## 한줄요약

## 2. Key Contributions
- First detailed histological mapping of peri-implant bone type sequence (woven → trabecular → lamellar) with cellular mechanisms at each stage
- Distinction between contact osteogenesis (implant surface → host bone) and distant osteogenesis (host bone → implant): both coexist
- Afibrillar calcified layer (comparable to lamina limitans) appears within first day — earliest implant–bone interface material
- Osteoporosis prolongs woven bone phase and impairs lamellar conversion → mechanism for higher implant failure in osteoporosis
- Lamellar bone at osseointegrated implant is not static: continuous 1 mm-zone peri-implant turnover persists life-long

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: Narrative review of animal and human histological studies (SEM, light microscopy)
- Population: Titanium endosseous dental implants (various micro-rough surfaces)
- Focus conditions: normal bone healing and osteoporotic bone healing
- Key techniques reviewed: SEM, backscattered electron imaging, confocal microscopy of fluorescent labels

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
**Healing timeline:**
- Day 0: blood clot fills gap; fibrin network; platelet activation
- Day 1: afibrillar calcified layer (≈lamina limitans) on implant surface; mesenchymal cells + pre-osteoblasts adhere
- Days 1–3: collagen fibrils of osteoid deposited on afibrillar layer
- Days 3–7: woven bone (섬유골, Woven Bone) forms immediately adjacent to implant (de novo = contact osteogenesis)
- Week 1–2: reparative trabecular bone (수복성 소주골, Reparative Trabecular Bone) with large marrow spaces rich in vessels + mesenchymal cells
- Weeks 6–12: lamellar bone (층판골, Lamellar Bone) gradually replaces trabecular bone
- Months to years: continued bone-to-implant contact remodeling; lamellar BIC = mature osseointegration

**Contact vs distant osteogenesis:**
- Contact osteogenesis: new bone nucleated from implant surface outward; requires early osteoblast adhesion
- Distant osteogenesis: new bone advances from host bone margin toward implant; the classic view
- Both occur simultaneously; contact osteogenesis accelerated by micro-rough surfaces

**Osteoporosis effects:**
- Reduced osteoblast differentiation potential
- Prolonged woven bone phase; impaired lamellar conversion
- Lower BIC values at standard healing timepoints
- Trabecular bone loss → reduced primary stability at implant placement

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Most cited studies pre-2005; surface modification literature now far more extensive
- Osteoporosis model largely based on ovariectomized rodents and rabbit — human data limited
- Quantitative BIC data not meta-analyzed; values cited range widely across studies

## 7. Glossary
- **Contact osteogenesis**: bone formation initiated at implant surface, proceeding outward toward host bone
- **Distant osteogenesis**: bone formation initiated at host bone surface, advancing toward implant
- **Afibrillar calcified layer (lamina limitans)**: thin, collagen-poor calcified film deposited on implant within first day; earliest bone–implant interface material
- **Reparative trabecular bone**: transient woven-bone-derived trabecular structure with large marrow spaces; precursor to lamellar bone
- **De novo bone formation**: new bone formed without pre-existing bone template; distinct from appositional growth
