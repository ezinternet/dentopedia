## Why Ingested

기존 위키의 임플란트 로딩 프로토콜 논문들(단일 임플란트 중심의 [[implants/rismanchian-2025-immediate-nonimmediate-loading-umbrella-review]] 등)은 대부분 임상적 결과(생존율·MBL)를 종합하는 우산리뷰다. 본 Heiderich 2020은 그와 결이 다르게, "multiple implant" 로딩 시기를 다룬 SR 21편 자체의 **방법론적 질과 비뚤림 위험**(AMSTAR/ROBIS)을 평가한 메타-방법론 우산리뷰로, rismanchian-2025가 인용·종합하는 근거 기반이 실제로 얼마나 견고한지에 대한 상위 검증 레이어를 제공한다. Rismanchian 2025가 "생존율·MBL 유의차 없음"이라는 결론을 내릴 때, Heiderich 2020은 그 결론의 신뢰도가 (당시 기준) 개별 SR 40%가 high risk of bias였다는 사실에 의해 제한될 수 있음을 보여준다 — 즉 두 논문은 같은 임상 질문("즉시 vs 재래식 로딩, 차이가 있는가")에 대해 서로 다른 층위(임상 결과 vs 근거의 질)에서 답한다.

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella systematic review of 21 systematic reviews (2004–2019, search to Dec 2019) on loading-time protocols (immediate vs conventional) for multiple dental implants in totally/partially edentulous patients, assessed with AMSTAR (methodological quality) and ROBIS (risk of bias).

13 SRs reported similarity between loading techniques, 2 were inconclusive, 6 favored conventional loading; 8/21 SRs were high risk of bias, 12 low risk, 1 uncertain — restricting to only the 12 low-risk-of-bias SRs still shows no significant difference in loading time outcomes.

Nearly 40% of the underlying SR evidence base was high risk of bias (poor study-selection restrictions, inadequate meta-analytic synthesis, only 1/21 published an excluded-studies list), so clinicians should weight loading-time conclusions by the source SR's risk-of-bias tier, not just its existence.

## 세줄요약

다수 임플란트(multiple dental implants)의 완전/부분 무치악 환자 로딩 시기(즉시 vs 재래식)를 다룬 체계적 문헌고찰 21편(2004–2019년 출판, 2019년 12월까지 검색)에 대해 AMSTAR(방법론적 질)와 ROBIS(비뚤림 위험)를 적용한 우산 체계적 문헌고찰.

13편은 로딩 방식 간 유사성을 보고, 2편은 결론 미확정, 6편은 재래식 로딩이 더 낫다고 보고; 21편 중 8편이 high risk of bias, 12편 low risk, 1편 uncertain risk — low risk of bias 12편만으로 한정해도 로딩 시기 간 유의한 차이는 없었다.

기저 SR 근거의 거의 40%가 high risk of bias(부적절한 연구선정 제한, 부실한 메타분석적 종합, 21편 중 1편만 제외논문 목록 공개)였으므로, 임상의는 로딩 시기 결론을 SR의 존재 여부가 아니라 그 SR의 비뚤림 위험 등급으로 가중해서 받아들여야 한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First umbrella review (per the authors) specifically evaluating methodological quality (AMSTAR) and risk of bias (ROBIS) of SRs on multiple-implant loading-time protocols, rather than pooling their clinical outcome conclusions directly.
- Demonstrates that raw vote-counting across SRs (13 similarity / 2 inconclusive / 6 favor-conventional) is misleading unless stratified by each SR's risk-of-bias tier — after restricting to the 12 low-risk-of-bias SRs, only 2 favored conventional loading and 1 was inconclusive, with the remaining 9 (of the low-risk group) showing similarity.
- Identifies the specific methodological failure modes driving high risk of bias: single-database searches, unexplained language/date restrictions, absent or inadequate meta-analytic synthesis/heterogeneity handling, and near-universal omission of an excluded-studies list (only 1/21 reported one).
- Chose AMSTAR (original) over AMSTAR-2 because AMSTAR-2 is designed only for SRs of RCTs, while several included SRs also incorporated retrospective/prospective observational studies — a deliberate tool-selection rationale worth noting for future umbrella-review methodology.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Umbrella systematic review (overview of systematic reviews), PRIO-guided, PROSPERO-registered.
- **Search**: MEDLINE (PubMed) and Scopus searched through December 31, 2019; grey literature via OpenGrey; manual reference-list checking. No language or date restriction on the search itself.
- **Eligibility**: (1) about multiple dental implants; (2) mentions moment of loading; (3) is a systematic review. Immediate loading defined as prosthesis placed immediately after implant placement surgery; conventional loading defined as prosthesis placement 4 months (mandible) to 6 months (maxilla) after implant placement.
- **Selection/extraction**: Two independent reviewers for screening (CH, SN) and two for data extraction (CH, TKT), discordances resolved by a third reviewer (TG); inter-reviewer kappa = 0.8 at screening. Duplicate-dataset SR updates: only the most complete version retained.
- **Quality/bias appraisal**: AMSTAR (methodological quality) and ROBIS (risk of bias), each applied independently by two further reviewers (FMM, RCS).
- **Synthesis**: Qualitative/narrative only — no new quantitative pooling was performed across the 21 SRs (the authors explicitly judged formal meta-analysis unnecessary given SR heterogeneity and the low-risk subset's consistent "no difference" signal).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Screening**: 757 records identified → 609 unique after dedup → 50 selected on title/abstract → 21 SRs eligible after full-text exclusion criteria; none additional found via OpenGrey or manual search.
- **Included SR characteristics**: published 2004–2019; primary-study counts per SR ranged 6–120; most searched >1 database; 7/21 did not clearly report number of reviewers; only 8/21 had no language restriction; nearly all included RCTs/non-RCTs, some also prospective studies; arch coverage: 5 SRs unspecified, 8 both arches, 5 mandible-only, 3 maxilla-only; 7/21 reported no explicit follow-up criterion.
- **Clinical conclusions across 21 SRs**: 13 similarity between IL/conventional, 2 inconclusive, 6 favor conventional.
- **AMSTAR/methodological quality**: most SRs scored well on a priori protocol, dual-reviewer selection, comprehensive search, grey-literature search, and reporting included-study characteristics; ~half did not assess included-study quality or use it in drawing conclusions; 12/21 performed no meta-analysis or publication-bias check; only 1/21 listed excluded studies; only 3/21 reported a conflict-of-interest statement.
- **ROBIS risk of bias**: 8/21 high risk, 12/21 low risk, 1/21 uncertain risk. Among high-risk SRs: 3 favored conventional, 4 showed similarity, 1 inconclusive. Among low-risk SRs: 10 showed similarity, 2 favored conventional, 1 inconclusive. The single uncertain-risk SR showed similarity.
- **Bottom line**: restricting to low-risk-of-bias evidence only, there is no significant difference in implant loading time outcomes — but ~40% of the total SR evidence base was high risk of bias, meaning naive reliance on "more SRs agree" without risk-of-bias stratification would be misleading.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Purely qualitative/narrative synthesis — no new pooled effect size across the 21 SRs, so precision of the "no difference" conclusion is bounded by consistency among included SRs rather than a formal quantitative estimate.
- Search restricted to two databases (MEDLINE/Scopus) plus OpenGrey; no additional grey-literature or manually found articles were identified, raising a residual (if judged low by the authors) publication-bias/completeness concern.
- Most included SRs did not specify anterior/posterior arch location despite known bone-density differences (posterior maxilla lowest, anterior mandible highest), and overdenture-implant interconnection status (splinted vs stand-alone) was often unclear — both plausible effect modifiers left unresolved at the SR level.
- The umbrella review itself does not report a formal AMSTAR/ROBIS appraisal of its own methodology (no self-critique via a tool like AMSTAR-2/ROBIS on the umbrella review), an inherent limitation of first-generation umbrella-review methodology circa 2020.
- Authors note immediate-loading primary literature volume still lags conventional loading and its methodological quality "is still far from optimal," flagging a persistent evidence gap for future higher-quality primary RCTs and updated SRs/MAs.

## 7. Glossary

- **AMSTAR**: A MeaSurement Tool to Assess systematic Reviews — appraises methodological quality/rigor of a systematic review's conduct (protocol, search comprehensiveness, dual review, etc.).
- **ROBIS**: Risk Of Bias In Systematic reviews — a tool assessing risk of bias in the SR's study-selection, data-collection, and synthesis/interpretation domains (distinct from methodological-quality checklists like AMSTAR).
- **Umbrella review**: A systematic review of systematic reviews ("overview of reviews"), aggregating and appraising multiple SRs on a related question rather than primary studies.
- **Immediate loading (IL)**: Prosthesis installed immediately after implant placement surgery.
- **Conventional/delayed loading**: Prosthesis installation delayed ~4 months (mandible) to ~6 months (maxilla) after implant placement to allow osseointegration.
- **PRIO**: Preferred Reporting Items for Overview of systematic reviews — reporting guideline for umbrella reviews used in this study.
