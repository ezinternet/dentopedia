## Why Ingested

Ingested 2026-07-20 as part of a 2-paper batch on 치과의사/치위생사 번아웃 (dentist/dental-hygienist burnout). [[wiki/overviews/dentist-burnout-mental-health-overview]] already synthesizes 7 papers on this topic and explicitly flags its axis-2 gap as "DCP(위생사·치료사) 데이터 극히 부족" (DCP data extremely sparse) — this paper was picked specifically because it helps fill that gap rather than duplicate existing coverage. It is the first paper in this wiki to directly compare psychological distress *and* burnout prevalence between dentists and a DCP-adjacent role (dental nurses) within one study, partially answering the open question [[wiki/professional-wellbeing/kaki-2022-health-wellbeing-clinical-dental-professionals]] raises — "What's the burnout prevalence in hygienists vs. dentists? Different exposure profiles ... should produce different patterns" — though the compared role (Chinese hospital dental nurses/chairside assistants) and health system (China vs. UK) differ from Kaki's DCP population.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional survey by the Chongqing Stomatological Association (February–March 2021) of 3,020 Chinese dental medical staff (1,855 dentists, 1,165 dental nurses) across 180 institutions in 11 provinces, using the Chinese GHQ-12 (psychological distress), the Chinese Maslach Burnout Inventory (CMBI), and job-stress/satisfaction and career-choice-regret measures.

Dentists reported higher psychological distress than dental nurses (25.7% vs. 20.8%, p=0.003) and higher burnout prevalence (28.9% vs. 23.6%); high job stress was the single strongest independent risk factor for both roles (dentists OR 3.017; nurses OR 3.37), but dental nurses carried a broader risk-factor profile — age ≥35 (OR 2.069), working >55 h/week (OR 2.433), low job satisfaction (OR 1.742), and poor medical environment (OR 2.2) — none of which were significant for dentists.

Cross-sectional, self-report design precludes causal inference and the sample is limited to 11 of China's provinces; the authors conclude that dentist- and nurse-specific (not uniform) intervention strategies are needed.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Occupational Differences in Psychological Distress Between Chinese Dentists and Dental Nurses
- **Authors**: Zhang Y, Yan L, Long H, Yang L, Wang J, Liu Y, Pu J, Liu L, Zhong X, Xin J.
- **Journal**: Frontiers in Psychology (2022)
- **DOI**: 10.3389/fpsyg.2022.923626 | **PMID**: 35846642 | **PMCID**: PMC9285401
- **Institution**: Stomatological Hospital of Chongqing Medical University, China
- **Study type**: Cross-sectional, self-administered questionnaire survey
- **Data collection**: Chongqing Stomatological Association, February–March 2021, 180 institutions across 11 provinces of China

## 2. Key Contributions

- One of few studies in the literature (per the paper's own framing) to directly compare psychological distress between dentists and dental nurses within one sample/instrument set, rather than studying either role in isolation.
- Large sample (n=3,020) spanning 11 Chinese provinces and 180 institutions — far larger than the entire DCP evidence base Kaki 2022 found for the UK (12 studies total).
- Fits separate multivariable risk-factor models by role, showing dental nurses carry a broader/more distinct risk-factor profile (age, long hours, job satisfaction, medical environment) than dentists, despite dentists having the higher overall prevalence of both distress and burnout.
- Uses four instruments in one survey (GHQ-12, CMBI, Consultants' Mental Health Questionnaire, career-choice-regret items), allowing burnout to be modeled as a risk factor for psychological distress in each role (dentists OR 2.171; nurses OR 1.772) rather than only reporting burnout prevalence as an isolated endpoint.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional, self-administered questionnaire.
- **Sampling**: Chongqing Stomatological Association survey, February–March 2021; 11 provinces, 180 institutions; 3,020 valid questionnaires (1,855 dentists, 1,165 dental nurses).
- **Instruments** (5-part questionnaire):
  1. Demographics
  2. Chinese General Health Questionnaire-12 (GHQ-12) — psychological distress, cutoff ≥4
  3. Chinese Maslach Burnout Inventory (CMBI) — 22 items across three domains
  4. Consultants' Mental Health Questionnaire — job stress/satisfaction
  5. Career-choice-regret items
- **Analysis**: Descriptive prevalence plus multivariable analysis (odds ratios reported), stratified into separate models for dentists and dental nurses.
- **Gap in available text**: the exact composite cutoff defining "burnout" prevalence (28.9%/23.6%, e.g., high-EE-only vs. all-three-domain-positive) is not spelled out in the condensed source text retained for this ingest — flagged here rather than assumed; re-check the full PMC article if the precise CMBI cutoff becomes clinically relevant.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Psychological distress (GHQ-12 ≥4), overall**: 23.8%
- **Psychological distress, dentists vs. dental nurses**: 25.7% vs. 20.8% (4.9-point gap, p=0.003)
- **Burnout prevalence (CMBI), dentists vs. dental nurses**: 28.9% vs. 23.6%
- **Dentist risk factors for psychological distress** (multivariable OR): high job stress 3.017 (highest); burnout 2.171; career-choice regret 2.05; lack of sufficient personal time 1.735; lower income (OR not specified in available text).
- **Dental nurse risk factors for psychological distress** (multivariable OR): high job stress 3.37 (highest); lack of sufficient personal time 2.347; working >55 h/week 2.433; poor medical environment 2.2; age ≥35 2.069; low job satisfaction 1.742; burnout 1.772; lower income (OR not specified in available text).
- **Shared risk factors across both roles**: lower income, burnout, high job stress, lack of sufficient personal time.
- **Nurse-only significant risk factors** (not significant for dentists): age ≥35, working hours >55 h/week, low job satisfaction, poor medical environment.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Selection bias**: only 11 of China's ~34 provincial-level divisions were sampled; generalization to the whole country is uncertain.
- **Self-report questionnaire**: recall and social-desirability bias possible for all four instruments.
- **Cross-sectional design**: precludes causal inference — e.g., burnout is modeled as a risk factor for psychological distress, but the reverse direction (or bidirectional feedback) cannot be ruled out.
- **Occupational-label caveat**: "dental nurses" here reflects the Chinese hospital chairside-nursing/assistant role, not a confirmed match to UK-style independently-practicing dental hygienists/therapists (see [[wiki/professional-wellbeing/kaki-2022-health-wellbeing-clinical-dental-professionals]]) — cross-system comparisons of this paper's nurse data against non-Chinese DCP literature should be made cautiously.
- **Future work** (per Discussion): role-specific rather than generic intervention design, given dental nurses' broader risk-factor profile and their reported "subordinate" collaborative relationship with dentists plus mismatched professional-development expectations among older nurses.

## 7. Glossary

- **GHQ-12**: General Health Questionnaire-12, a screening instrument for psychological distress; Chinese version, cutoff ≥4 used in this study.
- **CMBI**: Chinese Maslach Burnout Inventory, a 22-item adaptation of the Maslach Burnout Inventory across three domains.
- **DCP**: Dental Care Professional — umbrella term (used in the UK/international literature, see Kaki 2022) for hygienists, therapists, clinical dental technicians, and orthodontic therapists; this paper's "dental nurses" are a related-but-distinct chairside role within the Chinese hospital system.
- **OR**: Odds ratio, multivariable-adjusted.
- **Psychological distress vs. burnout**: two related but distinct constructs measured in this study — psychological distress (GHQ-12, general mental-health screening) and burnout (CMBI, occupation-specific exhaustion/depersonalization/accomplishment construct); the paper models each as a risk factor for the other.
