## Why Ingested

Batch ingest of dental-tinnitus PMC literature to build out `tmj` category coverage of the tinnitus–TMD relationship; the wiki previously held only one narrow cross-sectional paper, [[wiki/tmj/ma-2025-tmd-tinnitus-chewing-side-preference-correlation]], on chewing-side preference. This is the most recent (2025) PRISMA-compliant systematic review covering both the association evidence and the mechanistic/therapeutic angle, updating the evidence base with a decade (2014–2024) of literature across three databases.

## Three-line Summary

PRISMA-registered (PROSPERO CRD 641352) systematic review of 12 studies (from 976 initial records across PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science, 2014–2024) on the association between temporomandibular disorders (TMDs) and subjective tinnitus in adults.

A consistent, moderate-to-strong association was found across studies (e.g., painful TMD vs. tinnitus OR = 7.3, 95% CI 3.50–15.39; TMD diagnosed in 97.8% of somatosensory-tinnitus patients in one cohort), explained by somatosensory/neuroplastic cross-modal mechanisms (dorsal cochlear nucleus convergence of auditory and somatosensory input), with TMD-directed therapy (manual therapy, stabilization splints, education) producing measurable tinnitus symptom improvement in several trials.

Evidence quality is limited by small samples, absent/weak control groups, cross-sectional designs, and diagnostic heterogeneity, so the review calls for standardized DC/TMD-based criteria and larger RCTs before causality or definitive treatment protocols can be established.

## 세줄요약

PRISMA 등록(PROSPERO CRD 641352) 체계적 문헌고찰로, PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science 3개 데이터베이스에서 2014–2024년 10년치 문헌 976편을 검토해 최종 12편을 정성분석에 포함, 성인의 측두하악장애 (Temporomandibular Disorders, TMD)와 주관적 이명(subjective tinnitus)의 연관성을 평가했다.

여러 연구에서 일관되게 중등도~강한 연관성이 확인되었으며(통증성 TMD-이명 OR = 7.3, 95% CI 3.50–15.39; 체성감각 이명 환자군에서 TMD 진단율 97.8%), 이는 배측 와우핵(dorsal cochlear nucleus)에서 청각-체성감각 경로가 교차 수렴하는 신경가소성 (Neuroplasticity) 및 체성감각 이명(somatosensory tinnitus) 기전으로 설명되며, 도수치료·교합안정장치·환자교육 등 TMD 표적 치료가 여러 임상시험에서 이명 증상 개선을 보였다.

다만 소표본, 대조군 부재/취약, 단면연구 설계, 진단기준 이질성이 근거의 질을 제한하므로, DC/TMD 표준 진단기준 적용과 대규모 RCT가 인과관계 규명과 치료 프로토콜 확립에 필요하다고 결론짓는다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First comprehensive systematic review (per the authors) consolidating clinical/observational evidence on the bidirectional relationship between subjective tinnitus and TMD (with cervical spine disorders discussed as a related somatosensory contributor, though the final included-study set focuses on TMD).
- Synthesizes 12 heterogeneous studies (cross-sectional, cohort, and RCT/secondary-analysis designs) spanning prevalence/correlation studies and interventional (manual therapy, splint, education) studies.
- Articulates a mechanistic framework: somatosensory tinnitus modulation via cross-modal auditory–somatosensory convergence at the dorsal cochlear nucleus, providing anatomical/functional rationale for why TMJ/masticatory muscle dysfunction can alter tinnitus perception.
- Applies formal risk-of-bias tools (Cochrane RoB for RCTs, ROBINS-I for non-randomized studies) across included studies — a step often missing in prior narrative reviews of this topic.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Protocol**: PRISMA-compliant; registered at PROSPERO, CDR ID 641352.
- **Databases**: PubMed (n = 675), Scopus (n = 251), Web of Science (n = 50) — 976 total records identified; no additional studies from manual search.
- **Search terms**: "tinnitus" AND "TMJ" (Boolean), English-language only.
- **Timeframe**: October 2014 – October 2024 (most recent decade).
- **Inclusion criteria**: human studies, open access, English language, adult populations.
- **Exclusion criteria**: case reports, animal studies, in vitro studies, reviews, off-topic articles, pediatric trials. Criteria structured under the PICO framework.
- **Screening flow**: 976 identified → 211 duplicates removed → 765 title/abstract screened → 642 excluded → 123 reports sought for full-text retrieval (all retrieved) → 111 excluded at full-text stage → **12 studies included** for qualitative synthesis. (Note: the extracted text states "26 records for further consideration" immediately before "123 reports requested," an apparent internal inconsistency/OCR artifact in the PRISMA flow numbers in the source article; the final included count of 12 is unambiguous and consistent throughout the Results/Discussion.)
- **Quality appraisal**: Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool (RCTs) and ROBINS-I (non-randomized studies), assessed across seven bias domains. Most studies rated "low risk" or "some concerns"; higher risk concentrated in confounding factors (D1) and selection bias (D3); outcome measurement (D6) generally well-handled; some reporting-bias uncertainty (D7).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Prevalence/association studies:
- Maciejewska-Szaniec et al. (2017), n = 246 TMD patients: 36.18% had otologic symptoms; tinnitus in 14.63% of the total group; hypersensitivity to sound in 10.57%.
- Peleg et al. (2022), n = 51 tinnitus patients: bruxism in 66.7%, TMD in 27.5%; 92.8% of TMD patients also had bruxism; tinnitus + TMD + bruxism associated with highest anxiety scores; tinnitus severity correlated with depression and inversely with age.
- Fernandes et al. (2014), n = 261 women: painful TMD strongly associated with tinnitus (OR = 7.3, 95% CI 3.50–15.39, p < 0.001); sleep bruxism more weakly associated (OR = 1.9, 95% CI 1.16–3.26, p = 0.0163); painful TMD without SB still strongly associated (OR = 6.7); painful TMD + SB associated with more severe tinnitus (OR = 7.0).
- Manfredini et al., n = 250 TMD patients: 30.4% reported tinnitus, higher with masseter myalgia and TMJ osteoarthritis; age-stratified associations varied by symptom.
- Didier et al., n = 97 somatosensory-tinnitus patients / 50 TMD patients: TMD diagnosed in 97.8% of tinnitus patients studied.
- Ralli et al., n = 226 chronic tinnitus patients: history of TMD dysfunction/tinnitus somatic modulation associated with TMD presence, but no direct correlation between TMD and tinnitus etiology found.
- Kijak et al., n = 331 (mostly women): petrotympanic fissure position and mandibular condyle dislocation significantly correlated with tinnitus incidence; posterior/intracranial condyle positions more common in tinnitus patients.

Interventional studies:
- Mahmoudian et al. (2023), n = 6 male patients: SMTDI scores fell from 34.50 ± 1.3 to 24.37 ± 8.6 (p = 0.0001) after TMD therapy; VAS tinnitus intensity/annoyance/awareness significantly improved (p < 0.05); audiometric thresholds unchanged; bruxism resolved in 3/6.
- Plaza-Manzano et al. (2020), n = 61 (secondary analysis of RCT): higher baseline tinnitus severity predicted better outcomes (13–41% of variance); higher baseline tinnitus handicap predicted better manual-therapy outcomes (45% of variance); lower baseline temporalis pressure pain thresholds predicted worse outcomes (10.5–41% of variance).
- De la Serna et al. (multicenter RCT): exercise+education+manual therapy (EEMT) group showed significant improvements in pain, tinnitus handicap, TMD disability, quality of life, depression, pain sensitivity, and mandibular range of motion vs. exercise+education (EE) alone.
- Michiels et al. (RCT, immediate- vs. delayed-start): conservative TMD treatment (jaw-use education, relaxation, stabilization splints) significantly and durably reduced tinnitus-related distress in patients with somatic tinnitus.
- Arruda de Souza Alcarás et al. (2023), n = 19: mean THI score 37.8 (moderate QoL impact); tinnitus duration and frequency correlated with greater THI impact.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small sample sizes across most included studies (some n < 20).
- Frequent absence of control groups, limiting causal inference.
- Predominantly cross-sectional designs, precluding assessment of symptom progression.
- Diagnostic heterogeneity: inconsistent TMD/tinnitus/bruxism diagnostic criteria and outcome measures across studies; reliance on clinical (non-instrumental) bruxism diagnosis in some studies.
- English-language-only inclusion may introduce publication/language bias.
- 10-year search window may exclude older foundational or newer post-cutoff studies.
- No uniform systematic quality assessment applied consistently across the literature base as a whole.
- Authors recommend: larger longitudinal studies, standardized diagnostic frameworks (e.g., DC/TMD), objective audiological measures, integrated psychological assessment, and well-powered RCTs with control arms.

## 7. Glossary

- **TMD**: Temporomandibular disorder(s) — conditions affecting the temporomandibular joint(s) and masticatory muscles.
- **Subjective tinnitus**: Tinnitus perceived only by the patient (vs. objective tinnitus, audible to an external observer), commonly attributed to central auditory neuroplastic changes.
- **Somatosensory tinnitus**: A subtype of subjective tinnitus modulated by muscle contraction or movement of the neck, head, or jaw, implicating cross-modal auditory–somatosensory pathway interaction.
- **Dorsal cochlear nucleus**: Brainstem structure receiving convergent auditory and somatosensory input, proposed as the anatomical substrate for somatosensory tinnitus modulation.
- **DC/TMD**: Diagnostic Criteria for Temporomandibular Disorders — standardized diagnostic framework referenced as a target for future methodological standardization.
- **THI**: Tinnitus Handicap Inventory — patient-reported outcome measure of tinnitus-related quality-of-life impact.
- **SMTDI**: Steigerwald/Maher TMD Disability Index — a TMD-specific disability outcome measure used in one included interventional study.
- **ROBINS-I**: Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies of Interventions — tool used for quality appraisal of non-RCT studies in this review.
- **PPT**: Pressure pain threshold — quantitative sensory measure used as a predictor variable in one included RCT secondary analysis.
