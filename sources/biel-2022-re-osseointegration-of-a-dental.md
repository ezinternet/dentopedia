## Three-line Summary
Single case report (n=1): a mandibular implant that lost osseointegration through aseptic loosening in a 79-year-old female breast-cancer patient was treated solely by occlusal correction.
Re-osseointegration was confirmed clinically and radiographically after 8 months and remained stable over a 21-month follow-up.
Suggests reducing occlusal forces may permit re-osseointegration, but n=1 with concurrent antineoplastic chemotherapy as an uncontrolled confounder and no evidence-based therapy concept for aseptic loosening yet.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Demonstrates (n=1) that a dental implant which had lost osseointegration through aseptic loosening can re-establish osseointegration after occlusal correction alone — no surgical/graft intervention performed.
- Frames aseptic implant loosening as a poorly understood process causing sudden implant failure for which **no evidence-based therapy concept exists**.
- Explicitly limits its own claim: reduction of occlusal forces "may have" facilitated re-osseointegration, and the concurrent antineoplastic chemotherapy (breast carcinoma) or other unknown influences cannot be ruled out.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Single case report (n=1)
- **Databases**: None (clinical case)
- **n**: 1 patient — 79-year-old female with mamma carcinoma, mandibular implant
- **Intervention**: Correction of the occlusion only (sole treatment performed)
- **Outcomes**: Clinical and radiographic re-osseointegration at 8 months; stability of the result through the follow-up period

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **n=1**: a single case cannot establish efficacy or causality — very low evidence level; no control, no comparator.
- Confounding is acknowledged by the authors themselves: concurrent antineoplastic chemotherapy for breast carcinoma (or unknown factors) may have contributed; occlusal forces were not reported as measured in the abstract.
- Abstract-only source, and the original article is German — methodology detail, imaging specifics, and discussion cannot be verified from this record.
- No evidence-based therapy concept for aseptic loosening exists yet; controlled studies are needed before occlusal correction can be recommended as a treatment.

## 6. Related Work
- chang-2013: literature review concluding that whether excessive occlusal load causes loss of osseointegration remains unproven — this case is the anecdotal reverse-direction observation (load reduction → re-osseointegration), consistent with but not resolving that gap.
- sadowsky-2019: narrative review of occlusal overload and implant failure (Isidor monkey: 6/8 overloaded implants lost osseointegration) — this case supplies the single clinical mirror-image datapoint.
- mojaver-2025: SR quantifying overload–peri-implant pathology associations — population-level context against which this n=1 anecdote sits.
- ramanauskaite-2023 / hakkers-2026: "re-osseointegration" in their context means surgical reconstructive defect fill after peri-implantitis — a different mechanism and intervention from non-surgical re-osseointegration after aseptic loosening (terminological boundary, not the same claim).

## 7. Glossary
- **Aseptic Loosening (무균증 느슨해짐)**: implant loosening and loss of osseointegration without infection, leading to sudden implant failure; poorly understood, no evidence-based therapy.
- **Re-osseointegration (재골결합)**: re-establishment of bone-to-implant contact after osseointegration has been lost.
- **Occlusal Correction (교합 조정)**: adjustment of occlusal contacts/forces — the sole intervention in this case.
- **Mamma Carcinoma (유방암)**: breast cancer; the patient's concurrent condition treated with antineoplastic chemotherapy.
