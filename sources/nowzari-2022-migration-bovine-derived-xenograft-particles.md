## Why Ingested

PMC 전문텍스트 "graft material displacement/scattering within the maxillary sinus" 표적 sweep의 일부. [[sinus-lift/lateral/mordenfeld-2010-msfa-dpbb-biopsies-11year]]가 11년 human biopsy로 "DPBB(=Bio-Oss)는 흡수되지 않는다"는 조직학적 증거를 제시했다면, 본 case series는 그 비흡수성이 실제 임상에서 어떻게 나타나는지(peri-implant sulcus로의 입자 이동/노출, 만성염증, 연조직 천공) 7개 증례로 보여준다. 동일 저자군(Rodriguez, Nowzari)의 자매 논문 [[sinus-lift/lateral/rodriguez-2019-long-term-risks-complications-bovine]] (장기 위험·합병증 전반)에 대해, 본 2022년 논문은 "migration" 현상에 초점을 좁힌 후속 보고다.

## Three-line Summary

Case series (n=7 consecutive patients, single private-practice cohort, JISP 2022) of anterior maxillary/mandibular implant sites grafted with bovine-derived xenograft (Bio-Oss/Bio-Oss Collagen) for socket preservation or contour augmentation, followed 2–6 years post-grafting.

In all 7 cases, intact bovine xenograft particles were clinically and/or radiographically detected migrating into or surfacing within the peri-implant sulcus, from as early as the healing-abutment stage up to 6 years post-grafting — despite the "slow-turnover" resorption concept, no case showed biodegradation of the particles.

Bovine bone xenograft is not biodegradable in humans; migrated/intact particles can surface in the peri-implant sulcus, act as a chronic-inflammation/bacterial-reservoir risk, and clinicians should be aware that surgical removal may be needed to resolve complications — though most cases here reached an acceptable esthetic/functional outcome despite intact particles.

## 세줄요약

증례 시리즈 (n=7, 단일 개인 치과의원 코호트, JISP 2022) — 전치부 상악·하악 임플란트 부위에 발치와 보존 또는 윤곽증대 목적으로 우골유래 이종골이식재 (Bio-Oss/Bio-Oss Collagen)를 사용, 이식 후 2–6년 추적.

7례 모두에서 유착부착 (intact) 이종골 입자가 치유지대주 단계부터 이식 후 6년까지 임상적·방사선학적으로 임플란트주위구 (peri-implant sulcus)로 이동하거나 표면에 노출됨 — "서서히 회전(slow-turnover)"되는 흡수 개념과 달리 어떤 증례에서도 생분해가 관찰되지 않음.

우골유래 이종골이식재는 인체에서 생분해되지 않으며, 이동된/유착된 입자가 임플란트주위구에 노출될 수 있고 만성염증·세균저장소 위험으로 작용할 수 있어 술자는 필요 시 외과적 제거를 고려해야 하나, 본 증례 대부분은 입자가 남아있음에도 심미·기능적으로는 수용 가능한 결과에 도달함.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Presents 7 consecutive human clinical cases documenting bovine-derived xenograft (Bio-Oss) particle migration into/surfacing within the peri-implant sulcus over 2–6 years, directly observed clinically and radiographically (not just histologically).
- Directly challenges the "slow-turnover"/"slow-resorbing" marketing concept for bovine bone substitutes — the authors state no study has ever demonstrated complete biodegradation and replacement of bovine bone substitutes in humans, and particles have been found unchanged even after 20 years.
- Proposes a mechanistic explanation involving host inability to biodegrade xenograft particles (citing Seok et al.'s animal histology/EDX evidence of distant lymphatic migration of Bio-Oss to submandibular lymph nodes) and cytokine-mediated (TNF-α, IL-1β, TGF-β, IL-10) chronic inflammatory response to persistent intact particles.
- Raises the clinical-awareness point that particle migration/surfacing is often subclinical/asymptomatic (most patients had no discomfort) yet still represents a potential long-term bacterial-reservoir and chronic-inflammation risk requiring clinician vigilance and, in some cases, surgical removal to resolve complications.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Retrospective/prospective consecutive case series from a single private periodontal-implant practice; 7 healthy adult patients (ages 34–80, 5 female/2 male), no systemic disease exclusions reported beyond "healthy."
- All patients: anterior maxillary (6 cases) or mandibular (1 case) sites, mix of immediate-implant-with-immediate-loading protocols (Cases 3, 6, 7) and delayed/staged protocols (Cases 1, 2, 4, 5); all received bovine-derived xenograft (Bio-Oss or Bio-Oss Collagen) for socket/contour augmentation, most also covered with a collagen membrane (Bio-Gide) and/or connective-tissue soft-tissue graft.
- Outcome ascertainment: clinical inspection at healing-abutment removal / provisional or definitive restoration insertion / long-term follow-up (2–6 years), plus periapical radiographs. No histology was performed in this series (contrast with Seok et al.'s animal histologic work cited in the discussion, and with human biopsy studies such as Mordenfeld 2010).
- Written informed consent obtained from all patients; local ethical approval obtained; no funding/COI disclosed.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Case | Age/sex | Site | Protocol | Time to particle detection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 34F | Max R central incisor | Socket graft → delayed implant + contour graft | At final restoration (~3y f/u) |
| 2 | 70F | Max R lateral incisor | Immediate implant + contour graft | Healing abutment (2mo); more at 3y |
| 3 | 45F | Max R central incisor | Immediate implant + immediate load | 3mo (temp removal); surfacing at 6y |
| 4 | 55F | Max R central incisor | Delayed implant (6mo) + contour graft | At final restoration (3y post-graft) |
| 5 | 62F | Mand L lateral incisor | Immediate implant + contour graft | 2y post-graft |
| 6 | 71M | Max R central incisor | Immediate implant + immediate load | 2mo (impression); still present at 3y |
| 7 | 80M | Max anterior (multi-implant) | Immediate implant + adjacent implants | 4y post-graft |

- Common finding across all 7 cases: intact (not resorbed/remodeled) xenograft particles clinically visible and/or radiographically confirmed "embedded"/"floating" in the peri-implant sulcus — none showed evidence of resorption despite follow-up intervals as long as 6 years.
- Most patients were asymptomatic; a minority had minor esthetic buccal-contour depression. No case in this series required removal, though the authors state removal is the typical resolution pathway for symptomatic xenograft complications (citing their own prior work, Rodriguez & Nowzari).
- Reported literature context cited by authors: peri-implantitis prevalence 1–47%, peri-implant mucositis prevalence 19–65%; peri-implantitis onset typically within 3 years of function.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Case series design (n=7, single practice, no control/comparator group) — cannot establish incidence, causal migration mechanism, or dose-response; purely descriptive/hypothesis-generating.
- No histologic or microbiologic confirmation of the "migrated" particles in this series (unlike the animal histology study by Seok et al. cited in discussion) — clinical/radiographic identification alone.
- No standardized outcome measure for "migration" (definition relies on visual/radiographic detection at variable follow-up intervals per case, not a fixed protocol).
- Authors explicitly frame this as raising awareness rather than quantifying risk; call for further research into the biodegradation and long-term biologic fate of bovine xenografts in humans.

## 6. Related Work

- Seok et al. — animal (histology + energy-dispersive X-ray microanalysis) study demonstrating distant lymphatic migration of Bio-Oss particles, plus a human case of lymphatic-node foreign material morphologically consistent with xenograft.
- Rodriguez & Nowzari — prior work on surgical resolution of xenograft-related complications by particle removal (companion/earlier paper in this wiki: [[sinus-lift/lateral/rodriguez-2019-long-term-risks-complications-bovine]]).
- [[sinus-lift/lateral/mordenfeld-2010-msfa-dpbb-biopsies-11year]] — 11-year human biopsy evidence independently supporting non-resorption of DPBB (deproteinized bovine bone) particles.
- [[sinus-lift/lateral/sartori-2003-msfa-bio-oss-10year-case-report]] — contrasting single-case biopsy trajectory suggesting progressive Bio-Oss resorption over 10 years (marked as `contradicts` on the Mordenfeld page).

## 7. Glossary

- **Xenograft**: bone graft material derived from a different species (here, bovine).
- **DPBB (deproteinized bovine bone)**: processed bovine-derived bone mineral scaffold (e.g., Bio-Oss) stripped of organic/protein content, used as an osteoconductive graft material.
- **Slow-turnover / slow-resorbing concept**: the marketed/assumed property that bovine xenograft particles are gradually resorbed and replaced by host bone over time — challenged by this paper.
- **Biodegradation**: host-mediated breakdown/removal of graft material, primarily via phagocytosis.
- **Peri-implant sulcus**: the soft-tissue crevice surrounding a dental implant, analogous to the gingival sulcus around a natural tooth.
- **Foreign body reaction**: chronic inflammatory host response to a persistent, non-degradable implanted material.
