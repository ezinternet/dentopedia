## Why Ingested

The wiki's handheld/portable-X-ray coverage so far is entirely about radiation-dose safety — [[wiki/radiology/berkhout-2015-justification-and-good-practice-using]] (EADMFR justification consensus) and [[wiki/radiology/makdissi-2016-effects-device-position-operators-radiation]] (operator-dose phantom study) — but neither addresses whether handheld units actually produce diagnostically adequate images. This is the first PMC-available paper in the wiki to evaluate **image quality and clinical utility** of a handheld device (Nomad Pro 2) against formal quality criteria, in the specific context that motivates portable radiography in the first place: domiciliary/nursing-home dental care for immobile patients. It extends the dose-focused literature with the outcome that actually justifies the exposure trade-off — diagnostic yield.

## Three-line Summary


Prospective quality-assessment study of 127 single-tooth radiographs taken with a handheld Nomad Pro 2 device in German nursing-home domiciliary dental care, graded by 3 dentists against German Federal Dental Association criteria: 80% showed no quality defects, periapical bone was diagnostic in 86%, and unexpected clinically important secondary findings appeared in 61% — quality was comparable to conventional wall-mounted units.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Mobile Dental Radiology—Evaluation of Quality Requirements for Radiographs Applying Handheld Mobile Radiography
- **Authors**: Amina Maria Geibel, Constanze Keutel, Daniela Kildal, Margrit-Ann Geibel
- **Journal**: Gerodontology, 2025;43(1):119–124
- **DOI**: 10.1111/ger.70023 | **PMID**: 41002018 | **PMCID**: PMC12872978
- **Study type**: Prospective clinical quality-assessment study (single-arm, descriptive comparison to a published 2012 wall-mounted-unit study)
- **Setting**: German retirement/nursing-home domiciliary dental care; Ethics Committee of University Ulm approval (no. 149/09), per Declaration of Helsinki

## 2. Key Contributions

- First formal quality evaluation (per national quality-criteria catalogue) of a handheld mobile X-ray device used specifically in domiciliary/nursing-home dental care for immobile, seriously ill, or disabled patients — a population where conventional wall-mounted radiography is often impossible.
- Demonstrates that handheld mobile radiography achieves diagnostic image quality **comparable to, and on some metrics better than**, conventional wall-mounted units and previously published inpatient/stationary benchmarks.
- Quantifies the diagnostic yield of enabling bedside radiography: 61% of images revealed treatment-relevant secondary/incidental findings (e.g., apical osteolysis, root remnants) that would otherwise go undetected in patients unable to attend a practice — with direct legal/ethical relevance, since surgical procedures without radiographic justification are legally contestable in this setting.
- Uses a multi-rater design (3 dentists of differing specialisation/experience) to control for evaluator-experience bias in quality grading.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Device**: Nomad Pro2 (Dexis/KaVo) handheld intraoral X-ray unit, 60 kV / 2.5 mA, meeting German Dental Association minimum specifications (imaging voltage ≥60 kV, resolution ≥5 line pairs/mm, focus-to-tube-end distance ≥200 mm). Shorter exposure time than a stationary unit (0.12 s anterior / 0.16 s molar vs. 0.25 s / 0.32 s stationary).
- **Sample**: 127 single-tooth radiographs, all taken by one experienced dentist familiar with the device, during routine domiciliary dental care in a retirement/nursing-home setting.
- **Indications**: root remnants (pre-surgical), caries examination, periodontitis examination, implantology.
- **Consent/anonymisation**: written informed consent obtained (from patient or legal guardian/next of kin); images anonymised with a key file accessible only to the treating dentist.
- **Raters**: 3 dentists of differing specialisation/experience — one oral and maxillofacial surgery specialist, one oral surgery specialist, one second-year conservative dentistry trainee — assessing independently to test the influence of professional experience on findings.
- **Quality criteria**: German Federal Dental Association standard catalogue — central beam centring, phosphor storage plate (PSP) quality defects, foreign bodies/overlaps in the beam path, contrast/sharpness sufficiency. Osseous structures assessed for treatment relevance: alveolar border, interradicular septum, periodontal ligament (PDL) assessability, periapical bone structure.
- **Comparator**: descriptive comparison against a previously published (2012) study of wall-mounted X-ray devices at a university hospital, and against other published stationary/inpatient quality benchmarks.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Overall quality**: 101/127 (80%) radiographs showed no qualitative limitations.
- **Periradicular/periapical bone structure**: diagnostic in 109/127 (86%); in <4% of images, bony structures (alveolar ridge, dentin-enamel junction, pulp chamber) could not be assessed with sufficient accuracy.
- **Field of view**: sufficiently covered the justifying indication in 107/127 (84%); inadequate in 20/127 (16%).
- **Secondary/incidental findings**: noted in 78/127 (61%) of radiographs — clinically important unexpected findings (e.g., apical osteolysis, root remnants) only detectable via radiography.
- **Specific defect rates (of all 127 images)**: overexposure 9% (n=11); superimposition 3% (n=4); PSP not aligned occlusally 43% (n=54); PSP quality issues (scratches/folds/wear) 47% (n=60); X-ray beam center not centred 28% (n=36); motion artefacts 4% (n=5); foreign-body superimposition 0%; angulation too steep 1% (n=1).
- **Comparison to stationary units**: 107/127 (83%, anterior/premolar/molar combined) showed no diagnostically relevant quality defects, matching or exceeding published inpatient/stationary benchmarks — German quality criteria (crown-to-apex visibility, periodontal bone structure, interradicular septum) were met in >96% of radiographs, better than the 83% reported in non-mobile environments. Field of view adequacy (84%) was comparable to 86% in normal (non-mobile) environments. Qualitative defect rate (16%) matched the stationary-condition rate (17%) reported elsewhere.
- **Motion artefacts**: only 4% — an acceptable rate given anticipated difficulty imaging limited-compliance patients at the bedside; no evidence of increased motion artefacts versus wall-mounted units, and the handheld device's shorter exposure time may even reduce motion-artefact risk.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Not a direct head-to-head comparison between handheld and wall-mounted units on the same patients — comparison to the 2012 wall-mounted-unit study and other published benchmarks is descriptive/indirect.
- All images taken by a single experienced operator — controls for operator-skill variance but limits generalisability to less-experienced operators; the authors note operator training/experience (citing Nietschke et al.) is crucial for image quality.
- Findings on perpendicularity/quality vary across the cited comparator literature: one study (Lommen et al.) found significantly better perpendicularity for the handheld Nomad Pro 2, while other bitewing/molar-premolar comparisons found no significant quality difference between Nomad Pro 2 and a Heliodent Plus wall-mounted unit — the evidence base on handheld-vs-wall-mounted quality is still mixed across studies.
- Device (Nomad Pro2 and similar handheld units) is not yet approved for use in Germany, partly attributed to a lack of quality studies — this paper is offered as evidence toward that regulatory gap.
- Future work: direct within-patient handheld-vs-wall-mounted comparison; broader multi-operator studies; assessment of whether direct digital detectors or stricter PSP quality control further reduce the ~16% defect rate.

## 7. Glossary

- **Nomad Pro2**: a CE-marked handheld, battery-powered intraoral dental X-ray unit (Dexis/KaVo) with an internal backscatter radiation shield, used widely in the US, Netherlands, and Sweden.
- **PSP (Phosphor Storage Plate)**: reusable digital radiographic detector plate that must be correctly positioned/handled to avoid quality defects (scratches, folds, misalignment).
- **Domiciliary dental care**: dental treatment delivered on-site to patients (e.g., in nursing/retirement homes) who cannot attend a conventional dental practice.
- **Justifying indication**: the clinical reason (per radiographic justification principle) that must be met by the field of view/image content for the radiograph to be considered adequate.
