## Why Ingested

The wiki already holds a meta-analysis of the *bone lid* technique, which repositions the excised lateral sinus window back over the antrostomy as an autologous barrier (→ [[sinus-lift/lateral/schiavon-2022-bone-lid-technique-lateral-sinus]]), but nothing on the alternative fate of that same piece of cortical bone: fixing it laterally as an autologous onlay shield over a particulate xenograft during simultaneous implant placement. This case series supplies the first clinical numbers (n=8, 24 implants, 2.55 ± 1.47 mm horizontal gain at ~19.5 months) for that "SPAM" variant, which matters because it removes the second donor site that autogenous block grafting otherwise requires.

## Three-line Summary
(Case series (n = 8 patients, 24 implants) introducing the Sinus-Plate Augmentation Method (SPAM): in maxillary posterior sites with Class C horizontal resorption, the vestibular cortical wall of the lateral sinus window is harvested with piezosurgery and re-fixed with an 8- to 10-mm screw as an autologous lateral onlay plate over a particulate xenogeneic graft, combined with external sinus lifting and simultaneous implant insertion.)
(CBCT horizontal alveolar width rose from 5.99 ± 1.71 mm pre-operatively to 9.28 ± 1.20 mm post-operatively and settled at 8.37 ± 1.50 mm at a mean 19.5 ± 6.98 months (9.8% / 0.91 ± 1.06 mm remodeling loss), leaving a statistically significant net gain of 2.55 ± 1.47 mm, with 100% implant survival and no biological or prosthetic complications.)
(SPAM is feasible for moderate-to-severe posterior maxillary atrophy without a second donor site and with low morbidity, but n = 8, non-consecutive sampling, mixed graft/membrane brands and variable follow-up (12–36 months) keep this at case-series strength; thin plates risk fracture if the fixation screw is over-tightened.)

## 2. Key Contributions
- Proposes SPAM (Sinus-Plate Augmentation Method): the cortical plate of the lateral sinus window — normally discarded or repositioned over the antrostomy — is harvested and re-fixed as an autologous lateral onlay plate over a particulate xenogeneic graft, eliminating the second donor site (ramus/iliac) that autogenous block grafting requires.
- Reports the first clinical outcome data for the technique: 24 implants in 8 patients, 100% implant survival, no biological or prosthetic complications, and a significant CBCT horizontal bone gain of 2.55 ± 1.47 mm over a mean 19.5 months.
- Quantifies the remodeling loss of the augmented site as 9.8% (0.91 ± 1.06 mm) between the immediate post-operative scan and follow-up, contrasting favourably with the 2.3 ± 1 mm resorption cited for membrane/autogenous-block GBR.
- Describes the anatomy-driven mechanical rationale: the curved sinus plate acts as a space-maintaining shield and its curvature eases fixation at the recipient site.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: prospective-style single-operator case series (8 patients), no control group, no ethical approval required per the authors.
- **Population**: 8 partially edentulous patients (7 female, 1 male; age 52–71, mean 57.37 ± 7.44 years; 2 smokers < 10 cigarettes/day; FMPS < 30%), all with moderate-to-severe horizontal posterior maxillary resorption, Allen 1985 Class C (mild-to-moderate). Exclusions: head/neck radiation, cardiovascular disease needing antibiotic prophylaxis, organ transplantation, infectious disease.
- **Surgical protocol**: crestal incision with papilla preservation, full-thickness flap with two vertical releasing incisions → piezo osteotomy of the lateral sinus window (Piezotome CUBE, Acteon) → window plate harvested and stored in saline → Schneiderian membrane elevation → sinus grafted with bovine (BioOss, 3 patients) or porcine (THE Graft, Purgo, 5 patients) natural bone mineral at 0.25–1 mm and 1–2 mm granulations, covered with a collagen membrane (BioGide 3 / THE Cover Flex 5) → 2–5 implants placed per site (24 total; MIS V3, MIS Seven, Dentium Superline, Dentium Bright, Straumann BLX; Ø 3.3–4.5 mm, 11–16 mm) → the harvested plate fixed to the alveolar bone with an 8–10 mm screw (Meisinger) and the plate-to-bone gap filled with the same particulate xenograft → bioresorbable collagen membrane over the whole augmented area, fixed with PTFE mattress sutures; periosteal incision for tension-free primary closure.
- **Post-operative care**: 0.12% chlorhexidine twice daily for 2 weeks, amoxicillin 875 mg + clavulanic acid 125 mg and ibuprofen 400 mg/8 h for 7 days, sutures removed at 2 weeks. Prosthetic reconstruction by another dentist 6–12 months post-operatively. Material brands selected according to patients' financial means.
- **Outcomes**: CBCT horizontal alveolar width (vestibulo-oral, at implant shoulder level, OnDemand3D, one examiner) pre-operatively, immediately post-operatively and at 12–36 months; clinical peri-implant parameters (PPD, bleeding on probing, oral hygiene); implant survival; biological/prosthetic complications.
- **Follow-up**: 6/12/18 months for six patients; one patient at 6 and 12 months; one at 6 and 36 months (mean reported follow-up 19.5 ± 6.98 months; Table 1 lists 18 months for patients 1–6 and 12 months for patients 7–8).

- Remodeling between immediate post-op and follow-up: 0.91 ± 1.06 mm = 9.8% of the post-operative width, versus 2.3 ± 1 mm resorption reported for membrane/autogenous-block GBR in the papers cited by the authors.
- Healing: uneventful in all 8 patients, no complications at grafted sites; tenderness and light-to-moderate swelling for 3–5 days in all patients.
- Clinical: PPD ≤ 5 mm and no profuse bleeding on probing at every follow-up; no peri-implant mucositis/peri-implantitis, no vertical bone loss ≥ 1 mm beyond expected remodeling; no ceramic fracture, abutment fracture/loosening or decementation.
- Survival: 24/24 implants (100%) over the evaluated follow-up.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Only 8 patients, non-consecutive sampling, heterogeneous materials (two xenograft brands, two membrane brands, five implant systems chosen by patient finances) and variable follow-up (12–36 months), with one internal inconsistency between the abstract ("one patient 36 months") and Table 1 (patients 7 and 8 at 12 months).
- Single operator, single centre, single CBCT examiner, no control group and no histology/histomorphometry — the "autologous plate" contribution to the gain cannot be separated from the particulate xenograft.
- Technique-specific mechanical risk acknowledged by the authors: plate fracture when the cortical plate is very thin or the fixation screw is over-tightened, and instability when primary implant stability or sufficient plate fixation cannot be achieved; remodeling loss must be anticipated in planning.
- Authors call for larger cohorts and randomized comparisons against other GBR techniques used adjunctively with sinus lifting.

## 6. Related Work
- schiavon-2022: bone lid technique SR+MA — same donor bone (the lateral window), opposite destination (repositioned over the antrostomy as barrier vs. re-fixed laterally as onlay plate).
- scaini-2025: narrative state-of-the-art on lateral window SFE with ridge augmentation — the clinical context SPAM sits in (indications, piezo, graft choice, complications).
- jue-2025: split-mouth RCT of autogenous ramus bone vs. BBM + 25% local autogenous bone — the morbidity benchmark (second donor site) that SPAM claims to avoid.

## 7. Glossary
- **SPAM (Sinus-Plate Augmentation Method)**: harvesting the vestibular cortical wall of the lateral sinus window and re-fixing it as an autologous lateral onlay plate over a particulate graft.
- **Sinus plate**: the thin cortical bony wall of the maxillary sinus lateral window, elevated during external sinus lifting.
- **Lateral ridge augmentation**: horizontal (vestibulo-oral) bone augmentation of the alveolar ridge, here as an onlay shield technique.
- **CBCT horizontal bone width**: vestibulo-oral ridge width measured on CBCT at the implant shoulder level.
- **Allen 1985 Class C**: moderate-to-severe horizontal maxillary ridge resorption classification cited by the authors for patient selection.
- **FMPS (Full-Mouth Plaque Score)**: whole-mouth plaque index, < 30% in all included patients.
- **PPD (Probing Pocket Depth)**: peri-implant probing depth, ≤ 5 mm at all follow-ups here.
