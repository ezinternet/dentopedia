## Why Ingested

→ [[local-anesthesia/gulnahar-2023-articaine-lidocaine-ianb-implant-surgery-rct]]

Largest RCT (n=577 patients, 1185 implants) directly comparing 4% articaine vs 2% lidocaine for IANB in posterior mandibular implant surgery. Provides definitive evidence that both agents are equivalent in pain control for implant placement, settling the clinical debate about articaine superiority in this specific context.

## Summary

RCT comparing 4% articaine + 0.006mg/mL epinephrine vs 2% lidocaine + 0.0125mg/mL epinephrine for IANB in posterior mandible implant surgery. Two university hospital clinics, Feb 2019 – Mar 2022. 634 enrolled, 57 excluded (required 2nd injection), 577 analyzed.

No significant difference in:
- VAS during injection: articaine median 1 (range 0-4) vs lidocaine median 1 (range 0-8), p=0.778
- VAS during surgery: articaine median 1 (range 0-3) vs lidocaine median 1 (range 0-7), p=0.302
- Lip numbness time: lidocaine 3.06±3.22min vs articaine 2.96±3.09min, p=0.678

Significant differences:
- Mandibular canal-implant apex distance: articaine 2.28±0.75mm vs lidocaine 2.45±0.86mm (p<0.05) — articaine placed closer
- Release incision rate: articaine 20.3% (51/252) vs lidocaine 12.3% (40/325), p<0.05

Despite articaine group having implants placed anatomically closer to the mandibular canal and requiring more release incisions, pain perception was identical between groups.

## Clinical Implications

- 2% lidocaine 1:100,000 epi IANB provides equivalent anesthesia to 4% articaine for posterior mandible implant surgery
- Either agent is suitable; cost, availability, and risk profile (articaine: higher paresthesia risk at 4% concentration) should guide choice
- IANB technique quality and proximity to nerve are primary determinants of success — not the agent
- Correlation between injection pain and surgical pain suggests pre-op pain management matters

## Limitations

- Two-center, two-surgeon study — inter-operator variability
- ~9% excluded for requiring second injection (balanced between groups)
- No blinding of operators
- No long-term follow-up
