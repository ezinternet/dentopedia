## Why Ingested
Broad narrative synthesis of ARP materials/techniques (bone substitutes → PRF/platelet concentrates → collagen membranes → bioactive/injectable hydrogels) that extends the modality-level scope of [[wiki/bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/avila-ortiz-2019-alveolar-ridge-preservation-interventions]] with patient-reported and cost-effectiveness data. Its uniformly positive framing (high 5–7yr implant survival, VAS 94/100 aesthetic satisfaction) sits in tension with [[wiki/bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/mardas-2023-alveolar-ridge-preservation-overtreatment]]'s scenario-dependent "ARP as overtreatment" critique — notable because this paper's own body text (§III.C, citing ref. [52]) already concedes ARP shows "no difference... regarding implant loss or success" versus unassisted healing, an internal nuance the abstract/conclusion do not carry forward.

## Three-line Summary
(Line 1: Narrative review (Gangneung-Wonju National University, Korea) synthesizing the evolution of alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) materials — allo/xenograft bone substitutes, platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) and other platelet concentrates, collagen membranes, bioactive and injectable hydrogel systems — and techniques.)
(Line 2: Reports high long-term implant survival (100% at 5–7 years in one cohort; 93.7% patient-level in another, n=108/308 implants), high 1-year aesthetic satisfaction (VAS 94/100), and a positive cost-effectiveness link between barrier-membrane spend ($12–$189) and reduced horizontal/vertical ridge resorption.)
(Line 3: As a non-systematic narrative synthesis with no defined article-selection criteria, it cannot adjudicate between conflicting primary findings it cites — e.g., one cited study reports ARP shows no difference vs. unassisted healing on implant success, which sits awkwardly beside the review's generally pro-ARP framing.)

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Maxillofacial Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery 2024;46:14
- **DOI**: 10.1186/s40902-024-00425-w
- **Institution**: Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, College of Dentistry, Gangneung-Wonju National University, Gangneung 25457, Republic of Korea

## 2. Key Contributions
- Organizes ARP materials into four families — bone substitutes (allograft/xenograft/synthetic), platelet concentrates (PRP/PRF/A-PRF/C-PRF/CGF), collagen membranes, and bioactive/3D-printed/injectable-hydrogel materials — with representative primary studies for each.
- Consolidates patient-reported outcome data rarely aggregated elsewhere: VAS pain at suture removal = 17 (SD 11, range 5–40); VAS aesthetic satisfaction at 1 year = 94 (SD 6, range 85–100), the latter linked to tooth-supported surgical guides.
- Consolidates a cost-effectiveness picture: bone graft substitutes US$46.2–$140, socket sealing materials US$12–$189; barrier-membrane spend (not graft spend) correlates with reduced ridge resorption; DFDB-based ARP cost only 22.4% of iliac-crest-autograft-based ARP in one comparative study.
- Surfaces a long-term implant-survival range across cited cohorts (90.5–100%) alongside one cited finding that ARP shows no difference vs. unassisted healing on implant loss/success — an internal nuance not reflected in the abstract's framing.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Narrative review — no PRISMA workflow, no stated database list, no explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria or quality appraisal.
- **Databases**: Not specified (non-systematic).
- **n**: Not applicable (narrative synthesis of ~70 cited primary/secondary sources).
- **Outcomes**: Bone regeneration/resorption, pain and wound healing, implant placement/stability, long-term implant survival, patient satisfaction and quality of life, cost-effectiveness.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Allograft-based procedures: highest bone percentage at 3 months (54.4%) in one cited SR; xenograft-based procedures lowest at 5 months (23.6%).
- Additional bone augmentation needed in 48.1% of non-grafted extraction sites vs. 11.5% of ARP sites.
- 288-patient ARP + immediate-implant cohort (3–60 month follow-up): 9.5% (26/274) complications; 90.5% uncomplicated implant survival.
- 108-patient retrospective cohort (xenograft ARP, 308 implants): 93.7% patient-level implant survival (13/308 implants lost in 10/108 patients); 5-year marginal bone loss ≈2.2 mm/patient.
- Separate cohort: 100% implant survival at 5–7 years, favorable peri-implant bone and soft-tissue health.
- Ridge width/height at 12 months comparable to 4-month post-ARP outcomes — delaying implant placement up to 1 year does not compromise the healed ridge.
- Pain VAS at suture removal: 17 (SD 11, range 5–40). Aesthetic VAS at 1 year: 94 (SD 6, range 85–100).
- Bone graft substitute cost: US$46.2–$140; socket sealing material cost: US$12–$189; membrane spend (not graft spend) significantly correlated with reduced horizontal/vertical resorption.
- DFDB-based ARP total cost = 22.4% of iliac-crest-autograft-based ARP cost in one health-economic comparison.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Narrative (non-systematic) design — no defined article-selection criteria, so source quality/heterogeneity is not controlled or graded.
- No standardized outcome-measurement protocol across the cited literature, limiting direct cross-study comparability (explicitly acknowledged by the authors as the review's central limitation).
- Authors disclose ChatGPT was used for sentence editing (Acknowledgements) — an editorial-assistance disclosure, not a content-generation one, but worth noting for provenance.
- Positive framing in the abstract/conclusion does not fully carry forward an internal caveat present in its own body text (§III.C: ARP showed no significant difference vs. unassisted healing on implant loss/success in one cited study) — see [[wiki/bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/mardas-2023-alveolar-ridge-preservation-overtreatment]] for a paper that centers this exact caveat.

## 6. Related Work
- avila-ortiz-2019 (SR+MA, 22 RCTs, 9 ARP modalities): this review's materials/techniques narrative extends that modality framework with newer bioactive/injectable materials and patient-reported/cost data.
- che-2024 (Korean Dental Association narrative review): independent, contemporaneous narrative review reaching a similarly positive ARP synthesis — corroborating rather than overlapping source material.
- suarez-lopez-del-amo-2022 (AAP biologics consensus SR): reaches a compatible conclusion on platelet concentrates/biologics — histomorphometric/healing benefit without consistent dimensional superiority.
- mardas-2023 (ARP-overtreatment critical review): the clearest point of tension — see Why Ingested and Limitations above.

## 7. Glossary
- **ARP (Alveolar Ridge Preservation)**: Interventions performed at the time of tooth extraction to minimize post-extraction resorption of the alveolar ridge and preserve hard/soft tissue for future implant placement.
- **PRF (Platelet-Rich Fibrin)**: Second-generation autologous platelet concentrate prepared from whole blood without anticoagulants, yielding a fibrin clot rich in platelets, leukocytes, and growth factors.
- **DBBM (Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral)**: Xenograft bone substitute derived from processed bovine bone, used as an osteoconductive scaffold in ARP.
- **VAS (Visual Analogue Scale)**: A continuous 0–100 patient-reported outcome scale used here for pain intensity and aesthetic satisfaction.
- **CGF (Concentrated Growth Factor)**: A platelet-concentrate variant prepared via a distinct centrifugation protocol from PRF, used adjunctively for bone regeneration.
- **MSC-EC-F127DA**: An injectable, photocurable bone-repair hydrogel (F127 diacrylate) loaded with mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) and vascular endothelial cell (EC) spheroids, cited as a minimally invasive bioactive-material example.
