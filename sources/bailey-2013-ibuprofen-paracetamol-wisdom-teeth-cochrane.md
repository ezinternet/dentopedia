# Ibuprofen and/or Paracetamol for Pain Relief After Surgical Removal of Lower Wisdom Teeth

## Three-line Summary

Cochrane intervention review of 7 RCTs (2241 participants) comparing ibuprofen, paracetamol, and their combination for postoperative pain after mandibular third molar extraction. Ibuprofen 400 mg is superior to paracetamol 1000 mg alone (RR for ≥50% pain relief at 6 h: 1.47, 95% CI 1.28–1.69). Combined paracetamol + ibuprofen (Nuromol) shows promising additive benefit versus either drug alone (RR 1.77, 95% CI 1.32–2.39 vs paracetamol).

## Why Ingested

This is the definitive Cochrane review on ibuprofen vs paracetamol for post-third-molar pain — the most-cited reference for analgesic selection after wisdom tooth surgery. It provides the NNT values and RR comparisons that anchor every subsequent RCT in this niche. Essential for the analgesics subcategory and for any clinical consult on post-extraction pain management. ## Summary

Bailey et al. (2013) conducted a Cochrane Intervention Review assessing the efficacy of ibuprofen and/or paracetamol for pain relief after surgical removal of impacted mandibular third molars. Seven RCTs involving 2241 participants met inclusion criteria. Ibuprofen 400 mg was significantly more effective than paracetamol 1000 mg for ≥50% pain relief at 6 hours (RR 1.47, 95% CI 1.28–1.69; NNT = 4). The combination of paracetamol 1000 mg + ibuprofen 400 mg (marketed as Nuromol) demonstrated encouraging results compared to paracetamol alone (RR 1.77, 95% CI 1.32–2.39; NNT = 5) and ibuprofen alone (RR 1.21, 95% CI 1.05–1.40; NNT = 7). Evidence quality was rated high for most comparisons. Rescue medication use was consistently lower with ibuprofen-based regimens.

## Key Contributions

- First Cochrane review to systematically compare ibuprofen vs paracetamol head-to-head for third-molar postoperative pain
- Established NNT values: ibuprofen 400 mg NNT = 4 (vs paracetamol 1000 mg) for ≥50% pain relief at 6 h
- Quantified the additive benefit of paracetamol + ibuprofen combination (NNT = 5 vs paracetamol alone; NNT = 7 vs ibuprofen alone)
- High-quality evidence base cited by virtually all subsequent analgesic RCTs in oral surgery
- Demonstrated that ibuprofen 400 mg should be preferred over paracetamol 1000 mg as monotherapy for post–third-molar extraction pain

## Methodology

Cochrane Intervention Review following standard Cochrane Collaboration methods. Studies were identified through CENTRAL, MEDLINE, EMBASE, and hand-searching of relevant journals. Inclusion criteria: RCTs comparing ibuprofen, paracetamol, or their combination in adults undergoing surgical removal of impacted mandibular third molars. Primary outcome was participant-reported pain relief (≥50% reduction) at 6 hours, dichotomized from TOTPAR (Total Pain Relief) or SPID (Sum of Pain Intensity Differences) scores. Secondary outcomes included pain intensity at 6 h, use of rescue medication, and adverse events. Risk of bias was assessed using Cochrane's risk-of-bias tool. Data were synthesized using fixed-effect and random-effects meta-analysis with calculation of NNT and RR with 95% CIs.

## Results

- **Ibuprofen 400 mg vs paracetamol 1000 mg**: RR for ≥50% pain relief at 6 h = 1.47 (95% CI 1.28–1.69); NNT = 4; high-quality evidence
- **Paracetamol 1000 mg + ibuprofen 400 mg vs paracetamol alone**: RR = 1.77 (95% CI 1.32–2.39); NNT = 5; encouraging
- **Paracetamol 1000 mg + ibuprofen 400 mg vs ibuprofen alone**: RR = 1.21 (95% CI 1.05–1.40); NNT = 7; encouraging
- **Ibuprofen 400 mg vs placebo**: RR = 3.11 (95% CI 2.49–3.88); NNT = 2; high-quality evidence
- **Paracetamol 1000 mg vs placebo**: RR = 2.11 (95% CI 1.67–2.66); NNT = 3; high-quality evidence
- Rescue medication use: significantly lower with ibuprofen-based regimens than paracetamol alone
- Adverse events: no significant differences between groups; generally mild (GI symptoms)
