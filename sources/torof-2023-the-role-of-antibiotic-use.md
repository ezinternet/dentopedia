## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 16 RCTs evaluating antibiotic prophylaxis around third molar (M3) extraction — independent of antibiotic type, dose, or timing — with primary outcome of postoperative infection; antibiotics reduced infection risk by 69% overall (p<0.001), but no effect was found on dry socket, intraoral inflammation, wound dehiscence, haematoma, or lymphadenopathy.

Antibiotic use was safe (adverse event RR 0.96, NS); however, routine prophylaxis was not supported for uncomplicated procedures in healthy patients, while evidence remains controversial for patients with comorbidities or immunosuppression.

This SR provides the most comprehensive evidence to date that antibiotics reduce M3 extraction infection but do not prevent dry socket — a clinically important distinction.

## 세줄요약

16편 RCT SR+MA: 제3대구치(M3) 발치 전후 항생제(Antibiotic) 효과 평가 (약제·용량·시기 불문), 주결과 술후 감염; 항생제가 감염 위험 69% 유의 감소(p<0.001), 그러나 건조치조염(Dry Socket)·염증·창상 열개·혈종·림프절병증에 효과 없음.

부작용 RR 0.96(통계적 유의성 없음) — 안전성 확인; 그러나 건강한 환자의 단순 발치 시 일상적 예방투여 미지지, 동반질환·면역저하 환자에서는 논쟁 지속.

항생제가 M3 발치 감염은 줄이나 건조와는 예방하지 못한다는 임상적으로 중요한 구별 제시.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Antibiotics reduced M3 extraction infection by 69% (statistically significant, p<0.001)
- Antibiotics did NOT prevent dry socket (alveolar osteitis) — no significant difference
- Adverse events profile favourable: RR 0.96 (NS)
- Routine prophylaxis not supported for uncomplicated M3 extraction in healthy patients
- NNT calculable for infection reduction (significant outcome)
- Comorbidities/immunosuppression remain controversial — calls for future studies

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- PRISMA-P; PROSPERO CRD42021269522; University of Wolverhampton ethics approval
- Databases: PubMed, Science Direct, Cochrane, plus reference bibliographies
- Search to November 30, 2021
- Population: healthy adults, any M3 extraction
- Intervention: any oral/systemic antibiotic vs placebo/control/no antibiotic
- 494 records → 64 selected → 16 RCTs met criteria (14 in MA, 2 narrative)
- Effect measure: RR (Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel); NNT for significant outcomes
- Quality: EPHPP tool; moderate–strong global rating across all studies

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
| Outcome | Studies | Key Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Postoperative infection | 12 RCTs (n=2,069) | AB reduced infection by 69% (p<0.001); 34/1,186 vs 78/883 |
| Dry socket / alveolar osteitis | 7 RCTs | No significant difference between groups |
| Adverse events | 8 studies (148 events total) | RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.58–1.59), NS |
| Other outcomes (swelling, pain, wound dehiscence, etc.) | Various | No significant benefit |

- Specific findings from individual studies: Lacasa et al. — infection rate significantly higher in placebo (16%) vs single-dose (5.3%) or 5-day pre-emptive (2.7%); difficulty/ostectomy increased infection risk (p=0.027); age and operative time were confounders

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Heterogeneity in antibiotic types, doses, and timing across trials
- Most studies excluded patients with comorbidities — cannot generalize
- Short follow-up periods in many studies
- Inconclusive pain reduction data
- Future studies should focus on comorbid/immunosuppressed patients specifically

## 6. Related Work and Context
- Aligns with Camps-Font 2024 NMA: antibiotics reduce infection but routine use not justified in healthy patients
- Extends evidence to: antibiotics ≠ dry socket prevention (important clinical distinction)
- Surgical difficulty (ostectomy, >10 min) and age increase infection risk and may justify prophylaxis

## 7. Glossary
- **M3**: Third molar (wisdom tooth)
- **Alveolar osteitis / dry socket**: Painful postoperative complication from premature clot loss; antibiotics do NOT prevent it
- **Infection (postoperative)**: Purulent discharge, wound infection, wound separation — antibiotic-preventable
- **EPHPP**: Effective Public Health Practice Project quality assessment tool

## Why Ingested

Establishes that antibiotics reduce M3 extraction infection risk (69% reduction) but do NOT prevent dry socket — a critical clinical distinction that informs targeted, non-routine prophylaxis.
