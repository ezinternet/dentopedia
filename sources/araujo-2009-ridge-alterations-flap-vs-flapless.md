## Why Ingested

Foundational Araújo/Lindhe dog histology establishing that post-extraction ridge resorption is driven by intra-socket bundle-bone biology, not flap trauma — a mechanistic anchor for [[overviews/bone-regeneration-socket-biology-and-arp-critique]] and the rationale baseline for ARP in [[overviews/socket-preservation-arp-overview]]. Rebuts the claim that flapless extraction alone preserves the buccal plate.

## Three-line Summary

Dog histology study (5 mongrels, split-mouth design: flap elevation vs flapless extraction, 6-month healing) testing the hypothesis that flapless extraction preserves the buccal plate by minimizing periosteal vascular disruption.

Ridge alterations occurred in both groups with comparable magnitude; bundle bone resorption — the dominant driver of buccal vertical loss — proceeded regardless of whether a mucoperiosteal flap was raised.

Flapless extraction alone is NOT an alveolar ridge preservation substitute; the finding directly rebuts a widely held clinical assumption and reinforces that intra-socket biology drives ridge collapse.

## 세줄요약

개 조직학 연구(5마리, 분할구강 설계: 판막 거상 vs 무피판 발치, 6개월 치유) — 무피판 발치가 순측 골판을 보존한다는 가설 검증.

양 군에서 치조제 변화 발생, 크기 유의차 없음; bundle bone 흡수(순측 수직 소실의 주원인)는 판막 거상 여부와 무관하게 진행.

무피판 발치는 치조제 보존술 대체 불가 — 치조 내 생물학이 치조제 흡수를 주도한다는 기존 가정을 직접 반박한 연구.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Tests the then-popular clinical claim that **"flapless" extraction preserves the buccal plate** by minimizing periosteal vascular disruption.
- Result rebuts this: ridge resorption magnitude is comparable between flap and flapless sides at 6 months in dogs.
- Reinforces the 2-phase resorption mechanism (Araújo 2005): bundle bone loss is intrinsic to tooth removal, not flap reflection.
- Indirectly argues that "flapless" alone is not an ARP substitute.

## 3. Methodology
- 5 mongrel dogs.
- Mandibular 2nd premolars hemi-sected; mesial roots retained as control.
- Distal root extraction with split-mouth randomization:
  - One side: full-thickness mucoperiosteal flap elevation, then extraction.
  - Contralateral: flapless extraction.
- Soft tissue closed with interrupted sutures both sides.
- 6 months healing → euthanasia, biopsy → ground sections (×4 per site: 2 from mesial root, 2 from healed socket), stained for histomorphometry.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Abstract reports: ridge alterations occurred in both groups; magnitude not meaningfully different.
- Bundle bone resorption (the dominant contributor to buccal vertical loss) occurred whether or not a flap was raised.
- Histologic appearance of healed sockets similar between flap and flapless sides.
- (Detailed mm-level data is in the full text which is not in this PDF; cited values in subsequent SRs report comparable buccal vertical loss between groups.)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Small n (5 dogs) → low precision for between-group difference.
- Single follow-up (6 months); short-term healing dynamics not resolved.
- Mongrel dogs only; species translation to humans imperfect (humans have different bone turnover).
- PDF in this collection is abstract-only — for clinical citation, retrieve full text for exact mm values and statistical tests.

## 7. Glossary
- **Flapless extraction**: Tooth removal without raising a mucoperiosteal flap, intended to preserve periosteal blood supply.
- **Bundle bone**: Tooth-anchored cortical bone of the inner socket wall; resorbs after extraction due to loss of functional load.
- **Split-mouth design**: Both interventions performed in the same animal on contralateral sides, controlling for systemic variability.
