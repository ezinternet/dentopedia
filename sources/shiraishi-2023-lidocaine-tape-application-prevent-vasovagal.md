## Three-line Summary

Case series (n = 2) from a Japanese dental anesthesiology department: two patients with dental phobia and prior vasovagal syncope during vaccination, venipuncture, and dental treatment under local anesthesia, both scheduled for dental treatment under intravenous sedation.

Both had VVS again at venipuncture with a 24-gauge indwelling needle; pain was judged the main trigger, and 60% lidocaine tape applied 3 hours before venipuncture at their next visits allowed comfortable IV catheter placement without any VVS.

The observation is hypothesis-generating only — abstract-level reporting, two patients, no control, no VVS recurrence data beyond the next visit — and it concerns venipuncture for sedation, not dental local anesthetic injection.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Documents a concrete pre-procedural protocol for needle-triggered VVS in dentistry: 60% lidocaine tape, applied 3 hours before venipuncture.
- Argues from the case sequence that **pain**, rather than anxiety alone, was the operative trigger — the same patients had VVS at venipuncture despite being scheduled for sedation, and the tape alone changed the outcome.
- Positions topical local anesthetic as a preventive measure usable *before* the IV line exists, i.e. in patients in whom sedation cannot yet be started because access is the failing step.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Case series (n = 2), single institution, dental anesthesiology.
- **Participants**: Two patients, both with histories of dental phobia and of VVS during vaccination, venipuncture, and dental treatment with local anesthetics; both scheduled for dental treatment under IV sedation.
- **Index event**: VVS during venipuncture using a 24-gauge indwelling needle.
- **Intervention**: 60% lidocaine tape applied 3 hours before venipuncture at the next dental visit.
- **Outcome**: Successful, comfortable placement of the IV catheter without onset of VVS.
- **No control arm, no randomisation, no reported haemodynamics, no follow-up beyond the described visits.**

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only**: PMC body extraction returned empty; every statement here comes from the PubMed abstract. Details of both cases (age, sex, sedation regimen, monitoring, exact time course) are unavailable.
- n = 2, uncontrolled and unblinded; the "success" could reflect expectation, habituation to the venipuncture setting, or co-interventions at the next visit.
- Outcome is a single binary observation per patient; no recurrence rates, no pain scores, no plasma lidocaine data.
- Scope is **venipuncture for IV sedation**, not dental local anesthetic injection — the 3-hour tape timing has no bearing on intraoral infiltration or block technique.
- 3-hour application is impractical for unscheduled/urgent visits; the authors do not address this.

## 7. Glossary

- **VVS (Vasovagal Syncope)**: neurally mediated reflex faint triggered by pain, anxiety, or orthostatic stress; the most frequent complication during dental treatment.
- **Lidocaine tape**: topical 60% lidocaine preparation applied to intact skin to anaesthetise the venipuncture site.
- **Indwelling needle**: peripheral IV catheter (here 24-gauge) placed for venous access.
- **IV sedation**: intravenous conscious sedation used for dental treatment in anxious/phobic patients.
