## Why Ingested

FEA comparing implant length, diameter, bicortical anchorage, and sinus augmentation in posterior maxillary bone quantities A and C. Directly extends [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/zheng-2025-ultrashort-implants-posterior-maxilla-bicortical-osfe-fea]] and [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/seker-2014-bicortical-short-graft-long-tilted-fpd-fea-posterior-maxilla]] by providing a diameter variable (4 vs 5 mm) alongside length and bicortical status.

## Three-line Summary

3D FEA comparing cortical bone stress in posterior maxillary molar region; bone quantity A (normal height) and bone quantity C (atrophic) models; implant lengths and diameters varied alongside bicortical vs sinus augmentation approaches; principal stress as outcome.
Bone quantity A: 4 mm diameter × 6 mm length showed greatest (worst) stress; bone quantity C: 5 mm diameter × 6 mm length + bicortical anchorage yielded much lower stress than 4 mm × 13 mm + sinus augmentation.
For atrophic posterior maxilla (bone quantity C) with sufficient ridge width: short wide implant (5 mm diameter) + bicortical anchorage is preferable to longer implant with sinus augmentation from a biomechanical standpoint.

## 세줄요약

3D FEA, 후방 상악 구치부; 골 형태 A(정상)·C(위축) 모델; 임플란트 길이·직경 × 쌍겹질 고정 vs 상악동 거상 비교; 주응력.
골 형태 A: 4 mm 직경 × 6 mm 길이가 최고 응력(불리); 골 형태 C: 5 mm × 6 mm 쌍겹질이 4 mm × 13 mm + 상악동 거상보다 훨씬 낮은 응력.
위축 후방 상악에서 충분한 골폭 있을 때: 짧고 넓은 임플란트 (5 mm) + 쌍겹질 고정이 상악동 거상 장임플란트보다 생역학적으로 유리.

## Key Contributions

- Diameter effect quantified: 5 mm > 4 mm diameter for bicortical short implants in atrophic bone
- Bicortical 6 mm + 5 mm diameter outperforms sinus-augmented 13 mm + 4 mm diameter in bone quantity C
- Bone quantity A: short implants show higher stress than quantity C models (paradox — normal height not ideal for short implants without bicortical)
- Framework positions short bicortical implants as a viable alternative to sinus augmentation

## Key Results

- Bone quantity C, 5 mm × 6 mm + bicortical: stress **much smaller** than 4 mm × 13 mm + sinus augmentation
- Bone quantity A, 4 mm × 6 mm: **greatest** degree of stress distribution (highest risk)
- Recommendation: 6 mm implant + increased diameter (4→5 mm) for bone quantity C where ridge width permits

## Limitations

- Abstract-only; specific stress values not available
- Single-unit FEA; no FPD configuration
- CAD model geometry (not CT-derived) — may not reflect clinical anatomy variation
