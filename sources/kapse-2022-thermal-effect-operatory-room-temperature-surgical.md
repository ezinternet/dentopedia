## Why Ingested

기존 다인자 발열 SR인 [[implants/chauhan-2018-biomechanical-factors-heat-generation-osteotomy]]가 "irrigation 온도는 강력한 인자, drilling depth·diameter는 미확정"이라 정리했는데, 본 in vitro thermographic 연구(Kapse 2022, goat mandible)는 room temperature라는 새 변수를 추가하고 irrigant temperature·drill diameter·drilling depth를 동일 실험 내에서 직접 통계 비교(paired t-test)해 그 프레임을 정량적으로 보강한다.

## Three-line Summary

In vitro thermographic study (64 goat-mandible bone specimens, 2 room temperatures × 2 drill diameters × 2 depths × 2 irrigant temperatures) evaluating heat generation during implant osteotomy.

Only irrigant temperature significantly affected bone temperature (5°C: 24.5–24.8°C vs 25°C: 29.3–29.7°C, p=0.003); operating room temperature (25°C vs 30°C), drill diameter (4.2 vs 5.6 mm), and drilling depth (10 vs 13 mm) showed no statistically significant differences.

Cooled saline (5°C) is the dominant, clinically actionable lever for controlling osteotomy-site heat; the study is ex vivo on nonvital goat bone (no living bone-healing endpoint) and is limited to two discrete levels per factor.

## 세줄요약

In vitro 열화상 연구(goat mandible 골편 64개, 실온 2단계 × 드릴 직경 2단계 × 깊이 2단계 × 관주액 온도 2단계 조합)로 임플란트 osteotomy 시 발열을 평가.

관주액(irrigant) 온도만 골 온도에 유의한 영향(5°C: 24.5–24.8°C vs 25°C: 29.3–29.7°C, p=0.003); 수술실 온도(25°C vs 30°C), 드릴 직경(4.2 vs 5.6mm), 드릴링 깊이(10 vs 13mm)는 통계적으로 유의한 차이 없음.

차가운 saline(5°C)이 osteotomy 부위 발열 조절의 지배적·임상적으로 실행 가능한 변수 — 본 연구는 비생체 goat bone ex vivo라 실제 골치유 endpoint는 측정 불가하며 각 인자당 2단계 값만 비교한 한계가 있음.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Journal of Indian Society of Periodontology 2022;26(1):32-36
- **DOI**: 10.4103/jisp.jisp_791_20
- **Institution**: Department of Periodontology, V.Y.W.S. Dental College and Hospital, Amravati, Maharashtra, India

## 2. Key Contributions
- First study (per authors) to isolate the effect of ambient operating-room temperature (25°C vs 30°C) on osteotomy-site bone temperature — found no significant effect.
- Confirms, within a single controlled experiment, that irrigant temperature is the dominant modifiable factor (p=0.003) while drill diameter and drilling depth are not significant (p>0.05 in all comparisons).
- Uses graphite-spray-coated goat mandible specimens + laser/IR thermometer with 0.95 emissivity correction to standardize cortical/cancellous measurement.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In vitro (ex vivo) controlled experimental study, blinded outcome evaluator/statistician.
- **Specimens**: 64 goat mandible bone blocks (15 mm × 15 mm), 32 in Group A (room temp 25°C) and 32 in Group B (room temp 30°C).
- **Variables crossed**: drill diameter (4.2 mm, 5.6 mm) × drilling depth (10 mm, 13 mm) × irrigant temperature (4–5°C, 25°C), n=4 per subgroup.
- **Equipment**: conventional physiodispenser handpiece, 800 rpm, external saline irrigation, laser/infrared thermometer, graphite spray (0.95 emissivity) to normalize cortical/cancellous readings.
- **Statistics**: Student's t-test; significance at p<0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Baseline bone specimen temperature: 20.24 ± 0.60°C.
- Irrigant temperature (25°C room, Group A): 25°C saline 29.28 ± 1.78°C vs 5°C saline 24.48 ± 0.78°C (p=0.003*).
- Irrigant temperature (30°C room, Group B): 25°C saline 29.70 ± 1.85°C vs 5°C saline 24.83 ± 0.80°C (p=0.003*).
- Drill diameter (25°C room): 4.2 mm 25.93 ± 2.29°C vs 5.6 mm 27.83 ± 3.39°C (p=0.38, NS).
- Drill diameter (30°C room): 4.2 mm 26.28 ± 2.30°C vs 5.6 mm 28.25 ± 3.47°C (p=0.37, NS).
- Drilling depth (25°C room): 10 mm 26.38 ± 2.96°C vs 13 mm 27.38 ± 3.12°C (p=0.65, NS).
- Drilling depth (30°C room): 10 mm 26.78 ± 3.06°C vs 13 mm 27.75 ± 3.15°C (p=0.67, NS).
- Intergroup (room temp 25°C vs 30°C): no statistically significant difference.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- In vitro study on nonvital (ex vivo) goat bone — cannot measure actual downstream effect on bone healing/osseointegration.
- Only two discrete levels tested per factor (diameter, depth, irrigant temp, room temp) — may miss dose-response or threshold effects.
- Only external irrigation tested (internal/combined irrigation excluded to avoid clogging/contamination bias).
- Single operator, single drilling speed (800 rpm) — findings may not generalize across speeds/operators.
- Authors call for further studies with more variable levels and larger sample sizes.

## 6. Related Work
- Chauhan et al. 2018 (biomechanical factors SR): irrigation type/temperature "strong" evidence factor — this study directly confirms with controlled data.
- Aquilanti et al. 2023 (in vitro heat generation study): also found irrigation/saline temperature to be a significant heat modulator (10°C vs warmer) while irrigation volume was not — Kapse 2022 reinforces the temperature-over-volume pattern with a different (ex vivo bone) model.
- Tabrizi et al. 2017 (cited in Discussion): drilling speed/depth did not significantly affect bone viability — consistent with this study's depth findings.
- Misir et al. 2009 (cited in Discussion): combined (internal+external) irrigation produced higher jaw bone temperature than external irrigation alone — supports the authors' choice to use external-only irrigation.

## 7. Glossary
- **Osteotomy**: Surgical cutting/drilling of bone to prepare an implant site.
- **Emissivity**: A material's efficiency in emitting thermal radiation, used to calibrate infrared thermometry (graphite spray = 0.95 emissivity in this study).
- **Thermal osteonecrosis**: Bone tissue death caused by excessive heat, classically triggered above 47°C sustained for >1 minute.
- **Physiodispenser**: A motor-driven dental handpiece control unit used for implant surgery, allowing controlled speed/torque and irrigation.
