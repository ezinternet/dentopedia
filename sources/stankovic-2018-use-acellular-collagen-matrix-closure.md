## Why Ingested

The wiki's periosteal-releasing-incision (PRI) batch is otherwise entirely technique-execution literature (laser vs scalpel PRI, vertical-incision-free PRI, PRI-quantification SRs) — all of it assumes PRI is being performed and asks how. Stankovic 2018 is ingested as the opposite pole: a technique proposal for *avoiding* PRI altogether by covering the GBR wound with an acellular (porcine) collagen matrix (Mucograft) instead of scoring the periosteum for buccal flap advancement, giving the batch a non-incision alternative for comparison. It parallels the "avoid the vertical/periosteal incision" strategy already explored (from a different angle — open healing without flap closure) in [[implants/vertical-ridge-augmentation/wang-2025-mucoperiosteal-flap-healing-vertical-bone-titanium-mesh]] and sits at the "minor advancement, PRI-skippable" end of [[suture-wound-closure/plonka-2017-flap-designs-flap-advancement-implant-therapy]]'s advancement-stratum taxonomy.

## Three-line Summary

Technique paper (J Stomatol Oral Maxillofac Surg 2018;119(5):446-449; abstract-only, no PMCID) proposing acellular porcine collagen matrix (Mucograft) as a cover for bone substitute + barrier membrane in guided bone regeneration (GBR), applied in at least one described surgical case.

The stated rationale: covering the open oral wound with the collagen matrix's compact outer layer avoids scoring the periosteum, submucosa, and muscle layer for buccal flap advancement — i.e., periosteal-releasing incisions (PRI) are skipped entirely, and the flap is repositioned to its original level without cutting into attached keratinized gingiva.

No patient count, follow-up duration, or outcome measurements are reported in the abstract; this is a rationale/technique-proposal paper illustrating an approach, not a controlled or quantified outcome study.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Proposes covering the GBR wound (bone substitute + barrier membrane) with an acellular collagen matrix (Mucograft) as a substitute for periosteal-releasing-incision-based buccal flap advancement.
- Frames PRI avoidance as a soft-tissue-preservation strategy: skipping periosteal/submucosal/muscle scoring lets the flap return to its original position without impairing attached keratinized gingiva.
- Names the clinical scenario this is meant to address — shallow vestibulum, short attached gingiva, and strong muscle pull, situations where conventional buccal flap advancement risks reducing or eliminating attached gingiva and, by extension, threatening long-term implant soft-tissue health.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

No quantitative outcome data are reported in the abstract. The claims are qualitative and rationale-level only:

- Periosteal-releasing incisions (PRI) were avoided by using the collagen matrix to cover the open wound instead of scoring the periosteum/submucosa/muscle for flap advancement.
- The flap was repositioned to its original level.
- Attached keratinized gingiva was not impaired by the procedure.

## 7. Glossary

- **Mucograft / acellular collagen matrix**: a porcine-derived, cell-free collagen matrix with a compact (dense) outer layer and a porous inner layer, marketed for use in open healing (non-submerged) situations where primary flap closure over the wound is not achieved.
- **Attached keratinized gingiva**: the band of firmly bound, keratinized gingival tissue immediately coronal to the mucogingival junction; its width is a soft-tissue health marker around teeth and implants, and can be reduced by aggressive flap-advancement incisions.
- **Open healing**: a wound-management approach in which a surgical site heals without primary (flap) closure over it, relying instead on a barrier material (here, the collagen matrix) to protect the underlying graft/membrane while secondary-intention healing occurs.
- **Periosteal-releasing incision (PRI)**: a horizontal incision through the periosteum (and often submucosa/muscle) at the base of a mucoperiosteal flap, used to gain flap mobility for tension-free advancement and primary closure in GBR.
