## Three-line Summary

In vitro study: hyperhydrophilic (alkali/NaOH-treated) sand-blasted acid-etched (SBA) titanium vs untreated SBA titanium, exposed to partially heparinized human whole blood, assessing blood clot formation, platelet activation, complement activation, and macrophage/monocyte adhesion.

Increasing blood-surface exposure time increased platelet activation, clot formation, and complement activation regardless of surface treatment; alkali-treated (hyperhydrophilic) surfaces recruited significantly fewer macrophages/monocytes than untreated surfaces and produced a qualitatively different clot architecture (thick, organized, layered fibrin network vs thin, patchy, unstructured on untreated Ti).

This is the mechanistic reference Becker 2019 cites for why hydrophilic/plasma-activated titanium surfaces alter early blood-contact immune behavior — but it does not itself measure cytokine gene expression or a 24-hour reversal, so it supports a candidate hypothesis (time-dependent complement/coagulation accumulation) rather than confirming Becker's specific finding.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Establishes that blood-surface **exposure time itself** (independent of surface treatment) drives increasing platelet activation, clot formation, and complement activation — a time-dependent baseline any surface-treatment study must be read against.
- Shows hyperhydrophilic (alkali-treated) titanium recruits significantly fewer macrophages/monocytes than untreated titanium — consistent with the early anti-inflammatory direction seen in plasma-treated surfaces (e.g., Becker 2019's 1–8 h suppression).
- Documents a structurally distinct, thicker, more organized fibrin clot architecture on hydrophilic surfaces, proposed (not proven) to support later tissue integration.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- SBA titanium disks, alkali (NaOH)-treated (hyperhydrophilic) vs untreated.
- Exposed to partially heparinized human whole blood.
- Endpoints: blood clot formation/architecture (SEM), platelet activation, complement system activation (C5a and related markers), macrophage/monocyte adhesion count.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Increasing exposure time → more activated platelets, more clot formation, stronger complement activation (both surfaces, direction not surface-specific).
- Macrophage/monocyte count: significantly lower on alkali-treated vs untreated SBA Ti.
- Clot architecture: untreated Ti — thin (<15 µm), patchy, unstructured, lacking large fibrin networks. Alkali-treated — thick (>30 µm), layered, organized fibrin network with a continuous surface cover; nucleated cells adhere close to the surface, platelets entrapped above in the fibrin mesh.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only ingest** — full text not retrieved (no PMC deposit; journal is open-access on its own site but outside this pipeline's sanctioned acquisition tools). Quantitative statistics (n, p-values, exact time points) not available at this tier.
- Alkali/NaOH surface treatment, not plasma — a different surface-modification method than the plasma cluster; relevance to Becker 2019 is by analogy (both produce hyperhydrophilic surfaces altering blood-contact behavior), not identical mechanism.
- Does not measure cytokine gene expression (IL1-β, TNF-α) or any timepoint beyond what's needed to show the exposure-time trend — cannot directly confirm or refute Becker's specific 24-h reversal, only supplies a plausible contributing mechanism (time-dependent complement/coagulation accumulation).
- In vitro ex vivo model; no in vivo or clinical osseointegration outcome in this paper (though the authors reference separate in vivo work suggesting alkali-treated SBA performs better).

## 7. Glossary

- SBA = Sand-Blasted, Acid-etched (샌드블라스트-산에칭) titanium surface.
- Complement system (보체계) = innate immune cascade (C5a etc.) activated by foreign-surface contact, distinct from but crosstalking with the coagulation cascade.
- Hyperhydrophilic = 초친수성, contact angle near 0°, produced here via alkali (NaOH) treatment rather than plasma.
