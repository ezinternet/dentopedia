## Why Ingested

Foundational conceptual review covering burnout definition, etiology, antecedents, consequences, prevention, and measurement instruments across all occupations. Fills the definitional gap in [[wiki/overviews/dentist-burnout-mental-health-overview]] where the WHO/ICD-11 burnout framing and the reason for prevalence estimate heterogeneity were unverified — this paper supplies both. Also provides the instrument taxonomy (MBI-HSS vs MBI-GS vs CESQT etc.) needed to interpret why Jin-2014 (subscale EE 41.2%), Long-2023 (pooled full-syndrome 13%), and Zhang-2022 (CMBI 28.9%) cannot be directly compared.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review synthesizing burnout definition, six etiological theories, organizational and individual antecedents, individual/organizational consequences, primary/secondary/tertiary prevention strategies, and 10+ validated measurement instruments across all occupational groups.
Maslach's 3-dimension model (emotional exhaustion → depersonalization → reduced professional efficacy) remains dominant; WHO/ICD-11 classifies burnout as occupational phenomenon (not a disease); prevalence estimates legitimately range from 3.2% to 91.4% depending on instrument and cutoff applied.
No clinically validated diagnostic threshold exists for any instrument; most interventions target individuals despite burnout being primarily driven by organizational factors.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Establishes the construct history: Freudenberger (1974) → Maslach (1976) → Maslach & Jackson (1981/1986) 3-dimension model → ICD-11 (2019) occupational phenomenon
- Catalogues 10+ generic and occupation-specific burnout instruments with their dimensional structure and cultural validation status
- Explains why prevalence estimates vary enormously: each instrument has different dimensions, scoring, and no cross-validated diagnostic cutoff
- Reviews 6 complementary etiological theories (not antagonistic — each adds explanatory power)
- Identifies the primary prevention gap: burnout is organizational in origin; most interventions incorrectly target individuals

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Narrative review + Special Issue editorial
- **Scope**: All occupational groups (not healthcare/dentistry-specific)
- **Structure**: Definition → subtypes → theories → antecedents → consequences → prevention → instruments → Special Issue summary (21 papers)
- **No systematic search reported** — expert-curated, not SR/MA methodology

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Definition (Maslach & Jackson 1986)

Burnout = psychological syndrome with three dimensions:
| Dimension | Description |
|---|---|
| Emotional Exhaustion (EE) | Depletion of emotional resources from chronic work demands |
| Depersonalization/Cynicism (DP) | Distancing attitudes toward work/clients (coping mechanism) |
| Reduced Professional Efficacy (PA) | Feelings of incompetence and failure at work |

Causal order (longitudinal evidence): EE → DP; PA may be antecedent or consequence.

### ICD-11 Status

WHO classified burnout as occupational phenomenon in ICD-11 (2019) — exclusive to occupational context, NOT a disease or mental disorder.

### Prevalence Heterogeneity

- US surgery residents: 3.2%–91.4% depending on burnout definition applied
- Source of variation: different instruments, different dimensions used, no validated diagnostic cutoff for any instrument

### Burnout Subtypes (Montero-Marín)

| Subtype | Work pattern | Core emotion | Coping |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frenetic | Overloaded, high dedication | Personal abandonment | Problem-focused (active) |
| Under-challenged | Monotonous, understimulating | Boredom/indifference | Escapist/avoidant |
| Worn-out | Lack of recognition/control | Hopelessness | Passive (behavioral disconnection) |

Progressive deterioration: frenetic → under-challenged → worn-out (more longitudinal studies needed).

### Etiological Theories (6, complementary)

1. **Social Cognitive**: self-efficacy doubts → efficacy crises → low PA → EE → DP
2. **Social Exchange**: lack of reciprocity in work relationships → EE → DP as coping
3. **Organizational**: organizational stressors + inadequate coping. Two models disagree on order: Golembiewski (DP first) vs Cox (EE first)
4. **JD-R**: demands > resources → exhaustion. Demands → EE directly; resources → DP inversely
5. **Structural**: coping failure under chronic stress → PA + EE → DP
6. **Emotional Contagion**: shared burnout state across workgroups via social synchronization

### Measurement Instruments

| Instrument | Items | Dimensions | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| MBI-HSS | 22 | EE, DP, PA | Human services workers; original tool |
| MBI-GS | 16 | Exhaustion, Cynicism, Efficacy | All occupations; most widely used |
| CESQT/SBI | 20 | Enthusiasm, Psychic burnout, Indolence, Guilt | Spanish origin; adds guilt dimension |
| CBI | – | Personal, Work-related, Client-related | Context-free assessment |
| Oldenburg | – | Exhaustion, Disengagement | 2 dimensions only |
| BCSQ-36/12 | 36/12 | Frenetic/Under-challenged/Worn-out | Subtype-based |
| BAT | 33 | 4 core + 2 secondary | Most comprehensive construct |
| SMBM | 14 | Physical fatigue, EE, Cognitive weariness | Biomarker-linked |

**Critical gap**: None of the above instruments have clinically validated diagnostic cutoffs → burnout "prevalence" depends entirely on which threshold the researcher chose.

### Antecedents Summary

Organizational (triggers): work overload, emotional labor, lack of autonomy, role ambiguity/conflict, inadequate supervision, lack of social support, poor working hours
Individual (moderators): neuroticism (+), extraversion (-), external locus of control (+), Type A (+), high expectations (+); age (-), gender (EE/low PA in women, DP in men)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative (not systematic) review — selection bias possible
- No meta-analytic quantification of antecedent effect sizes
- Insufficient longitudinal data on subtype progression
- Need for biomarker validation of self-report instruments (salivary cortisol)
- Need for culturally validated cutoffs across instruments

## 7. Glossary

- **Burnout**: ICD-11 occupational phenomenon; syndrome of EE + DP + reduced PA from chronic workplace stress
- **Emotional Exhaustion (정서적 소진, EE)**: depletion of emotional resources — the core/first dimension
- **Depersonalization/Cynicism (비인격화, DP)**: distancing from work/clients as EE coping mechanism
- **MBI-GS**: Maslach Burnout Inventory–General Survey; 16-item, all-occupation version; most widely used
- **JD-R**: Job Demands-Resources theory; burnout when chronic demands exceed available resources
- **Diagnostic cutoff (진단 절단값)**: validated clinical threshold for burnout diagnosis — absent in ALL current instruments
