## Why Ingested

The wiki's clindamycin-vs-implant-failure signal currently rests on [[wiki/drug/antibiotics/salgado-peralvo-2021-penicillin-allergy-risk-factor-early-implant-failure]] — a systematic review *without* meta-analysis — and the 2026-09-02 surveillance overview [[wiki/overviews/penicillin-allergy-dental-antibiotic-overview]] explicitly flags a living inconsistency: several held pages still name clindamycin as the first-line penicillin-allergy substitute ([[wiki/drug/antibiotics/torof-2023-antibiotic-dental-implant-procedures-sr-ma]]). This 2023 SR+MA is the first quantitative pooling of that exact comparison (10,853 implants; OR 3.30, 95% CI 2.58–4.22) and pairs the failure signal with an actionable, risk-stratified penicillin-allergy delabeling protocol for implant patients.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD42022343716; PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science) of 4 retrospective cohort studies covering 10,853 implants — 1,210 placed under clindamycin for self-reported penicillin allergy vs 9,643 under amoxicillin.

Clindamycin-substituted patients were over three times more likely to undergo implant failure (OR 3.30, 95% CI 2.58–4.22, p < .00001), with cumulative failure 11.0% (95% CI 3.5–22.0%) vs 3.8% (95% CI 1.2–7.7%); comparative heterogeneity I² = 0%, but trial sequential analysis required 17,421 patients vs the 10,853 pooled.

Evidence is limited to retrospective studies with unverified (self-reported) allergy, so penicillin allergy, clindamycin, or their combination cannot be separated — the paper therefore proposes a penicillin-allergy delabeling protocol (low-risk: single 250 mg oral amoxicillin challenge + 1 h observation).

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰·메타분석(PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD42022343716; PubMed·Scopus·Web of Science) — 후향 코호트(RETrospective cohort) 4편, 임플란트 10,853개(자가보고 페니실린 알레르기로 클린다마이신 1,210개 vs 아목시실린 9,643개).

클린다마이신 투여군의 임플란트 실패 위험이 3배 이상 높음 (교차비 Odds Ratio, OR 3.30, 95% CI 2.58–4.22, p < .00001), 누적 실패율 11.0% (95% CI 3.5–22.0%) vs 3.8% (1.2–7.7%), 비교 이질성 I² = 0% — 단 시험순차분석(Trial Sequential Analysis, TSA) 필요 표본은 17,421명으로 실제 10,853명 미달.

근거는 알레르기 미확진 후향 연구 4편에 그쳐 알레르기·클린다마이신·이 조합 중 무엇이 원인인지 분리 불가 — 저자들은 페니실린 알레르기 라벨 해제(Delabeling) 프로토콜(저위험군은 경구 아목시실린 250 mg 단회 투여시험 + 1시간 관찰)을 제안.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First meta-analysis to quantify the association: patients self-reporting penicillin allergy and given clindamycin instead of amoxicillin have 3.3× higher odds of implant failure (OR 3.30, 95% CI 2.58–4.22, p < .00001) across 10,853 implants.
- Pooled proportional estimates: clindamycin group 11.0% (95% CI 3.5–22.0%) failed vs amoxicillin group 3.8% (95% CI 1.2–7.7%) — 114/1,210 vs 252/9,643 implants.
- Proposes a risk-stratified penicillin-allergy delabeling protocol for dental implant patients (low-risk oral amoxicillin 250 mg challenge; higher-risk skin testing with benzylpenicilloyl polylysine then challenge; SCAR patients excluded).
- Trial sequential analysis (TSA) shows the significance boundary was already crossed after the second study, yet required information size (RIS = 17,421) is unreached — a concrete sample-size target for future prospective trials.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis, PRISMA 2020 guidelines, PROSPERO CRD42022343716; comparative pooled analysis (fixed-effects) plus proportional meta-analyses (Freeman–Tukey transformation, random effects, MedCalc 20.218).
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science; first search 22 June 2022, last search 19 September 2022, plus hand-search of reference lists.
- **Eligibility**: English-language cohort/case-control/observational retrospective studies; adults >18 needing non-traumatic extraction followed by implant treatment; test group = self-reporting penicillin allergy. Excluded: case reports/series, reviews, head-and-neck radiotherapy, uncontrolled systemic/bone disease, animal/cell-line studies, multi-antibiotic cases.
- **n**: 572 records screened → 24 full texts → 4 studies included; 10,853 implants (1,210 clindamycin / 9,643 amoxicillin).
- **Outcomes**: implant failure proportion per group; pooled odds ratio; heterogeneity (I²), Egger's test, fail-safe N, sensitivity analysis (leave-one-out), meta-regression (follow-up, publication year), TSA with RIS.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Comparative effect**: OR = 3.30 (95% CI 2.58–4.22), p < .00001; I² = 0% (no heterogeneity); Egger's test p = .379 (no detected publication bias); fail-safe N = 83.
- **Proportional pooled failure**: clindamycin 11.0% (95% CI 3.5–22.0%, I² = 96.2%, 114/1,210 failures); amoxicillin 3.8% (95% CI 1.2–7.7%, I² = 98.3%, 252/9,643 failures). (Discussion quotes 10.5%, 95% CI 4.6–18.5% for the clindamycin pool.)
- **Per-study failure (test vs control)**: Wagenberg & Froum 2006 (USA, 364/1,561 implants, 16-y follow-up) 8.52% vs 2.95%; French et al. 2016 (Canada, 470/5,106, 10-y) 2.10% vs 0.80%; Salomó-Coll et al. 2018 (Spain, 175/2,572, 1-y) 18.86% vs 5.17%; Zahra et al. 2022 (USA, 201/404, 1-y) 19.90% vs 8.40%.
- **Meta-regression**: no significant correlation with clinical follow-up (p = .629) or publication year (p = .931); zero studies trimmed.
- **TSA**: RIS = 17,421 vs actual 10,853; cumulative Z-curve crossed the trial sequential significance boundary after the second study (French et al. 2016) — statistically significant but underpowered by TSA criteria.
- **Antibiotic regimens**: amoxicillin 2 g 1 h pre-op (3 of 4 studies); clindamycin 600 mg 1 h pre-op (French, Salomó-Coll) or 300 mg q6h × 7 d post-op (Salomó-Coll); Zahra did not report clindamycin posology.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Only 4 included studies, all retrospective and all based on *self-reported* penicillin allergy without clinical/laboratory confirmation — allergy status and antibiotic choice are confounded, so causation cannot be assigned to either.
- TSA shows the pooled sample (10,853) falls short of the RIS (17,421); publication-bias tests lack power with <10 studies.
- Very high heterogeneity in the proportional pools (I² 96.2–98.3%) and wide variation in effect size; primary data were insufficient for subgroup analysis or for separating early from late failures.
- Heterogeneous clindamycin dosing across studies (one study unreported posology); biological mechanism for clindamycin's effect (osteoblast toxicity, resistant Prevotella/MRSA selection) remains unproven.

## 7. Glossary
- **SRPA (Self-Reported Penicillin Allergy)**: a penicillin-allergy label reported by the patient without confirmatory testing; ~7% of the population carries it, <5% have active clinically significant hypersensitivity.
- **Delabeling**: removal of the penicillin-allergy designation from the patient record after testing (oral challenge ± skin testing).
- **TSA / RIS (Trial Sequential Analysis / Required Information Size)**: monitoring technique estimating how many patients a meta-analysis needs before a significant result is reliable; RIS here = 17,421.
- **Freeman–Tukey transformation**: double-arcsine transformation used to pool single-arm proportions in the proportional meta-analyses.
- **Benzylpenicilloyl polylysine skin test (Pre-Pen®)**: penicillin skin-testing reagent for higher-risk patients; negative predictive value near 100%.
- **1-1-1 criterion**: urticaria within 1 h of the first beta-lactam dose and resolving within 1 day — defines a higher-risk (IgE-mediated) history requiring skin testing before challenge.
