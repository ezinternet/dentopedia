## Why Ingested

[[overviews/treatment-planning-decision-variability-overview]] explicitly names its own blind spot: *"Variability outside endodontics is unmapped here… Whether the same threefold gap appears in restorability judgements, periodontal prognosis, or implant-versus-bridge planning is unknown from these sources."* This survey (150 periodontists/oral surgeons/prosthodontists, India) is the first evidence this wiki holds that the same clinician-dependent variability the overview documents for extraction-vs-retention in endodontics ([[practice-management/dufey-portilla-2026-treatment-decision-making-apical-periodontitis-clinician-groups]]) also shows up in an entirely different decision — implant soft-tissue augmentation technique and threshold choice. It also surfaces a finding the overview's endodontic sources never raised: that moderate clinical outcomes are widely accepted in daily practice but rarely judged "publishable," which is a direct statement about publication bias in the very evidence base clinicians calibrate against.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional questionnaire survey (Bharati Vidyapeeth Dental College and Hospital, India, January–March 2025; n=150: 65 periodontists, 45 oral surgeons, 40 prosthodontists; 63.3% male, most with 11–15 years' implantology experience and 11–20 implants placed per month) on clinical decision thresholds, technique preference, failure attribution, and outcome calibration for peri-implant soft tissue augmentation (STA).

Most clinicians across all three specialties intervened at 1–2 mm keratinized tissue width (no significant interspecialty difference) and preferred connective tissue grafting (CTG) over xenogeneic collagen matrix or free gingival graft in a thin-mucosa scenario (p=0.027); most estimated STA failure at 5–10% and favored shared 50/50 responsibility for unexplained failure; CTG confidence was higher among males (3.43±0.79 vs 3.13±0.90, p=0.041) and biomaterial confidence varied significantly by specialty (periodontist 2.80±0.94 vs oral surgeon 2.53±0.97, p=0.004); patient dissatisfaction despite clinical success was the single most commonly reported complication (41.3%), ahead of graft necrosis (36.7%) and infection (28.0%).

This is a self-reported, convenience-sampled, single-country survey — every figure is perceived failure rate, self-rated confidence, or stated preference, not measured performance; its sharpest finding is structural rather than clinical — moderate outcomes were accepted as personally successful by 65–71.1% of respondents but judged publishable by only 40–48.9%, meaning the literature clinicians read to calibrate their own expectations is itself skewed toward excellent-outcome cases.

## 세줄요약

인도 횡단면 설문조사(Bharati Vidyapeeth Dental College and Hospital, 2025년 1–3월; n=150: 치주과전문의(Periodontist) 65명·구강외과의(Oral Surgeon) 45명·보철전문의(Prosthodontist) 40명; 남성 63.3%, 대다수 11–15년 임플란트 경력·월 11–20개 임플란트 식립) — 임플란트 주위 연조직증대술(Soft Tissue Augmentation, STA)의 임상적 개입 기준·술식선호·실패귀인·결과보정에 관한 설문.

세 전문분과 모두 각화조직폭(Keratinized Tissue Width, KTW) 1–2mm에서 증대술을 시행한다는 응답이 다수였고(전문분과 간 유의차 없음), 얇은 점막 시나리오에서는 결합조직이식(Connective Tissue Graft, CTG)을 이종콜라겐기질(Xenogeneic Collagen Matrix)이나 유리치은이식(Free Gingival Graft, FGG)보다 유의하게 선호(p=0.027); 대다수가 STA 실패율을 5–10%로 추정했고 원인불명 실패 시 술자·환자 50/50 공동책임을 선호; CTG 자신감은 남성이 유의하게 더 높았고(3.43±0.79 vs 3.13±0.90, p=0.041), 생체재료 자신감은 전문분과별로 유의한 차이(치주과 2.80±0.94 vs 구강외과 2.53±0.97, p=0.004); 임상적으로 성공했음에도 환자 불만족(41.3%)이 이식편괴사(36.7%)·감염(28.0%)보다 더 흔한 합병증으로 보고됨.

자가보고·편의표본·단일국가 설문이므로 여기 실린 모든 수치는 실제 시행결과가 아니라 지각된 실패율·자가평정 자신감·희망 선호일 뿐이다; 가장 날카로운 소견은 임상적이라기보다 구조적이다 — 중등도(moderate) 결과는 응답자의 65–71.1%가 "개인적으로는 성공"으로 받아들였지만 "출판 가능"으로 판단한 비율은 40–48.9%에 그쳤다. 즉 술자가 자신의 기대치를 보정하는 데 쓰는 그 문헌 자체가 이미 우수한 결과 쪽으로 편향돼 있다는 뜻이다.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **First wiki evidence of clinician-decision variability outside the endodontic extraction-vs-retention scenario.** 150 implant clinicians (periodontists, oral surgeons, prosthodontists) rated the same five domains: intervention thresholds, technique preference, failure attribution, subjective vs. objective outcome evaluation, and confidence/training.
2. **Quantifies the gap between clinically acceptable and publishable outcomes.** Scenario 2 (moderate outcome, ≈2 mm keratinized tissue, ≈1.5 mm thickness) was rated a personal success by 65.0–71.1% of respondents across specialties but publishable success by only 40.0–48.9% — the same clinicians, the same case, a different bar depending on the audience.
3. **Isolates where specialty training does and does not predict practice.** No interspecialty difference in intervention thresholds (KTW p=0.253, thickness p=0.737) or smoker-technique modification (p=0.530), but significant specialty differences in technique choice for a thin-mucosa scenario (p=0.027), confidence in xenogeneic collagen matrix (p=0.004, periodontist 2.80±0.94 vs oral surgeon 2.53±0.97, adjusted p=0.0027), and primary training source (p=0.003).
4. **Names patient-clinician outcome divergence directly**: patient dissatisfaction despite clinically acceptable outcomes was the single most frequently reported complication (41.3%), ahead of graft necrosis (36.7%) and infection (28.0%) — clinician-defined success and patient-defined success are shown to diverge, without patient-reported data ever being collected.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional, questionnaire-based, single-center (India), January–March 2025. Institutional ethics approval BVDU/DCH//09/2024-25; Declaration of Helsinki; informed consent from all participants.
- **Sample**: Non-probability convenience sample via professional networks, institutional contacts, and digital platforms. 180 clinicians invited, 150 completed (83.3% response rate). Minimum target of 120 set pragmatically (no formal power calculation — exploratory design, no prior effect-size data across domains).
- **Population**: 150 dental professionals actively involved in implant placement/restoration with ≥1 year clinical experience — 65 periodontists (43.3%), 45 oral surgeons (30.0%), 40 prosthodontists (26.7%); 95 male (63.3%), 55 female (36.7%); postgraduate residents not recruited as a separate subgroup.
- **Instrument**: Structured, self-administered questionnaire, 5 domains (demographics/practice characteristics; clinical decision thresholds; failure attribution and economic considerations; subjective vs. objective outcome evaluation via clinical scenarios; confidence/training/complications). Content validity reviewed by a 5-expert panel; piloted on 15 clinicians; internal consistency of Likert items Cronbach's α = 0.82.
- **Scenario design**: Scenario 1 = poor outcome (<1 mm keratinized tissue, thin mucosa, graft contraction); Scenario 2 = moderate outcome (≈2 mm keratinized tissue, ≈1.5 mm thickness); Scenario 3 = excellent outcome (>3 mm keratinized tissue, >2 mm thickness) — hypothetical, verbally described, not standardized photographs.
- **Statistics**: IBM SPSS v20. Shapiro-Wilk normality check; Pearson's chi-square / Fisher's exact for categorical associations; Mann-Whitney U (2 groups) / Kruskal-Wallis H (>2 groups, with Bonferroni-corrected post hoc Dunn's test) for ordinal Likert data. Significance p<0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Keratinized tissue threshold prompting augmentation**: 1–2 mm the modal response across all specialties (periodontist 43.1%, oral surgeon 40.0%, prosthodontist 40.0%; χ²=7.82, p=0.253 — no significant interspecialty difference).
- **Buccal thickness threshold**: 1–2 mm again modal (38.5% / 37.8% / 40.0%; p=0.737).
- **Technique choice, thin/translucent mucosa scenario**: CTG preferred across all groups (periodontist 64.6%, oral surgeon 53.3%, prosthodontist 55.0%; χ²=14.28, p=0.027).
- **Smoker technique modification**: no significant interspecialty difference (p=0.530); most (50.0–58.5%) switched to a more invasive technique rather than avoiding augmentation or referring.
- **Perceived STA failure rate**: modal response 5–10% (male 44.2%, female 47.3%; no sex difference, p=0.921).
- **Cost attribution for unexplained CTG failure**: shared 50/50 modal response (male 49.5%, female 45.5%; p=0.734).
- **Legal dispute involvement**: low overall (male 15.8%, female 12.7%; p=0.470).
- **Personal vs. publishable success gradient (Scenarios 1/2/3)**: Scenario 1 (poor) personal success 9.2–15.6% vs. publishable 2.5–6.7%; Scenario 2 (moderate) personal success 65.0–71.1% vs. publishable 40.0–48.9%; Scenario 3 (excellent) personal success 95.0–97.8% vs. publishable 93.3–95.4%. No significant interspecialty difference at any scenario (p>0.05 throughout); authors flag a ceiling effect reducing discriminatory power for Scenario 3 comparisons.
- **CTG confidence by sex**: male 3.43±0.79 vs. female 3.13±0.90 (4-point Likert; Mann-Whitney U=2388.50, p=0.041).
- **Xenogeneic collagen matrix confidence by specialty**: periodontist 2.80±0.94, oral surgeon 2.53±0.97, prosthodontist 2.68±0.96 (Kruskal-Wallis H=11.24, p=0.004; post hoc periodontist vs. oral surgeon adjusted p=0.0027, no other pairwise difference significant).
- **Complications (last 2 years, multiple response, n=150 base)**: patient dissatisfaction despite clinical success 41.3% (highest), graft necrosis 36.7%, infection 28.0%, severe postoperative bleeding 21.3%; no significant interspecialty difference (p=0.859 for dissatisfaction).
- **Training source**: significant interspecialty difference (χ²=19.72, p=0.003) — private training most common overall (34.7%) and especially among oral surgeons (48.9%) and prosthodontists (45.0%), while periodontists more often reported formal postgraduate training (38.5%).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Self-reported, questionnaire-based, convenience sample.** Every outcome here is *perceived* failure rate, *self-rated* confidence, and *stated* preference — not measured graft survival, not observed technique, not verified complication incidence. A clinician's estimate that STA fails 5–10% of the time is not a failure rate; it is what that clinician believes and is willing to report.
- **Cureus, n=150, single institution/region (India), no outcome data, no patient data.** The authors themselves call the results "hypothesis-generating," not confirmatory, and explicitly flag the cross-sectional/convenience design as precluding causal inference.
- **The sex difference in CTG confidence (3.43 vs. 3.13, p=0.041) is a difference in self-rated confidence, not skill or clinical results** — a 0.30-point gap on a 4-point Likert scale at p just under 0.05 with n=150 (95 male / 55 female) is a fragile result and should not be read as a competence finding.
- **Specialty is self-selected, not randomized** — periodontists, oral surgeons, and prosthodontists differ in training pathway (confirmed here, p=0.003), case mix, and referral pattern, all of which confound any specialty-level comparison of confidence or technique choice.
- **Scenarios were verbally described, not standardized photographs** — the authors acknowledge this may itself have introduced interpretation variability for subjective parameters (contour, thickness, esthetic asymmetry), on top of the variability the study set out to measure.
- **Ceiling effect on Scenario 3 (excellent outcome) comparisons** reduces the discriminatory power of the "no interspecialty difference" findings at that scenario — near-universal agreement (93–98%) leaves little room for a group difference to appear even if one exists.
- **No standardized visual assessment tool or longitudinal data** — the authors call for multicenter studies with validated outcome criteria as the natural next step.

## 6. Related Work

- **Fürhauser et al. and Belser et al.** (cited by the authors) — soft tissue characteristics significantly influence esthetic outcomes around implants, supporting the clinical relevance of the thresholds surveyed here, though without settling a universal cutoff.
- **Cited systematic reviews on soft-tissue substitutes** — support that xenogeneic collagen matrices can approach autogenous graft outcomes but with less predictable long-term stability, a plausible driver of the significant specialty variation in xenogeneic confidence found here.
- **Pink esthetic score literature** (cited by the authors) — invoked as a parallel example of the documented gap between clinically acceptable and publication-standard esthetic outcomes, which this survey's Scenario 2 finding (65–71% personal vs. 40–49% publishable) reproduces for soft-tissue augmentation specifically.

## 7. Glossary

- **STA** — Soft tissue augmentation around dental implants.
- **CTG / FGG** — Connective tissue graft / free gingival graft, the two autogenous augmentation techniques compared against xenogeneic collagen matrix.
- **KTW** — Keratinized tissue width, the primary threshold parameter surveyed (Q4: <1 mm / 1–2 mm / >2 mm / no augmentation based on KTW).
- **"Personal" vs. "publishable" success** — the study's two outcome-acceptance criteria: acceptable in daily practice vs. acceptable for peer-reviewed reporting, assessed against the same three hypothetical scenarios.
- **Shared 50/50 responsibility** — the modal response for cost attribution when an unexplained CTG failure occurs, contrasted with attribution shifting toward the patient when a risk factor (smoking) is present.
