## Abstract

The aim of the present study was to evaluate and compare the traditional “All on Four” technique with digital smile designed computer-aided “All on Four” rehabilitation; with a 4-years follow-up. The protocol was applied to a total of 50 patients randomly recruited and divided in two groups. Digital protocol allows for a completely virtual planning of the exact position of the fixtures, which allows one to perform a flapless surgery procedure with great accuracy (mini-invasive surgery) and also it is possible to use virtually planned prostheses realized with Computer-Aided Design/Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAD/CAM).

## Introduction

Dental implants are increasingly popular for the rehabilitation of partially and totally edentulous patients, demonstrating optimal clinical outcomes and high levels of patients' satisfaction. Among the several factors that might affect their clinical outcomes, the type of workflow (traditional vs. digital) is nowadays widely investigated. The “All on Four” treatment concept was introduced by Malo et al. in 2003, demonstrating the possibility of rehabilitating edentulous jaws with four implants, two axial and two tilted, avoiding bone grafting procedures. The traditional workflow involves conventional impressions, manual model fabrication, and surgical guide creation based on wax-up. The digital workflow integrates intraoral scanning, virtual smile design, computer-aided implant planning, and guided surgery with CAD/CAM prostheses.

## Materials and Methods

Fifty patients were randomly allocated to two groups (n=25 each): Traditional protocol and Digital protocol. Inclusion criteria: edentulous maxilla/mandible, sufficient bone for 4 implants, willingness for immediate loading. Exclusion: systemic diseases, smoking >10 cigs/day, bisphosphonates, untreated periodontitis. Traditional group: conventional impressions, stone models, wax-up, surgical guide, flap surgery, immediate provisional prosthesis. Digital group: intraoral scan, digital smile design (DSD), virtual implant planning, 3D-printed surgical guide, flapless guided surgery, CAD/CAM immediate provisional prosthesis. Implants: NobelActive (Nobel Biocare). Follow-up at 12, 24, 36, 48 months. Outcomes: implant survival, marginal bone level (MBL), complications, patient satisfaction (VAS).

## Results

**Implant Survival (4 years):** Traditional: upper axial 100%, upper tilted 96.67%, lower axial 100%, lower tilted 95%. Digital: upper axial 100%, upper tilted 100%, lower axial 100%, lower tilted 100%. No statistically significant difference between groups (p>0.05).

**Marginal Bone Level (mean ± SD, mm):**
- Traditional total: 1.04±0.29 (12m), 1.08±0.28 (24m), 1.10±0.27 (36m), 1.12±0.26 (48m)
- Digital total: 0.68±0.13 (12m), 0.75±0.13 (24m), 0.79±0.11 (36m), 0.83±0.11 (48m)
- p<0.0001 at all timepoints favoring digital

**Complications (48 months):** Traditional: implant failures 2 (2%), unscrewing 3 (3%), provisional fractures 2. Digital: implant failures 0, unscrewing 1 (1%), provisional fractures 2.

**Patient Satisfaction:** Digital smile previsualization 93% very effective, mock-up 98%, guided surgery 94%, immediate loading 92%. Traditional: mock-up 45%, surgery 71%, immediate loading 95%.

## Discussion

Digital workflow showed significantly less marginal bone loss (0.83 vs 1.12 mm at 4 years, p<0.0001) and zero implant failures vs 2% in traditional. Flapless guided surgery preserves peri-implant tissues. Digital smile design improves patient communication and satisfaction. Limitations: single center, modest sample size, single implant system. The 4-year data confirm digital workflow superiority for All-on-4 rehabilitations.

## Conclusion

Digital smile designed computer-aided All-on-4 rehabilitation demonstrates superior marginal bone preservation and patient satisfaction compared to traditional workflow at 4-year follow-up, with 100% implant survival in the digital group.
