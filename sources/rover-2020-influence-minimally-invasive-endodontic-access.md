## Why Ingested
This in vitro study evaluates how minimally invasive (MI) endodontic access cavity design affects root canal instrumentation quality, filling integrity, and fracture resistance compared to traditional access cavities — directly extending the access cavity design literature assessed in [[endodontics/anatomy/kapetanaki-2021-access-cavity-designs-endodontic-review]] and [[endodontics/anatomy/ribeiro-2022-guided-endodontics-static-dynamic-review]].

## Three-line Summary
In vitro comparative study of 40 extracted human mandibular incisors grouped by access cavity design (traditional vs. minimally invasive) and instrumentation system (TRUShape vs. MTwo), evaluated by micro-CT before/after root canal preparation and filling.
Minimally invasive access cavities were significantly associated with higher void density in root canal fillings compared to traditional access (P < 0.05); no significant differences in canal preparation parameters, hard-tissue debris accumulation, or fracture resistance among groups.
While MI access cavities preserve tooth structure, they may compromise root canal filling homogeneity and quality, a trade-off requiring clinical vigilance in the filling phase.

## 2. Key Contributions
- **Void density trade-off**: Minimally invasive access cavities were associated with significantly more voids in root canal fillings (P < 0.05), suggesting a trade-off between tooth structure preservation and filling quality.
- **No differences in preparation efficacy**: Access cavity location/design did not significantly impact canal transportation, centring ratio, hard-tissue debris accumulation, or canal volume changes during instrumentation.
- **Fracture resistance neutral**: Despite filling voids, minimally invasive cavities did not reduce tooth fracture resistance compared to traditional cavities (P > 0.05).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In vitro experimental comparative study
- **Sample**: 40 extracted intact human mandibular incisors (pre-selected via periapical radiographs, matched by anatomical features)
- **Experimental groups** (n = 10 each):
  - T/TRU: Traditional access + TRUShape preparation
  - T/MT: Traditional access + MTwo preparation
  - MI/TRU: Minimally invasive access + TRUShape preparation
  - MI/MT: Minimally invasive access + MTwo preparation
- **Evaluation**: Micro-computed tomography (pre-selection, after preparation, after filling)
- **Outcomes**:
  - Root canal volume and area
  - Noninstrumented canal areas
  - Canal transportation and centring ratio
  - Accumulated hard tissue debris
  - Voids in root canal fillings
  - Remnants of filling material in pulp chamber
  - Fracture resistance (after restoration)
- **Statistical analysis**: Shapiro–Wilk, one-way ANOVA, Bonferroni post-hoc (α = 0.05)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **Extracted teeth only**: Study used non-vital extracted teeth, which may not fully replicate clinical conditions (vascular dynamics, PDL, proprioception).
- **Mandibular incisors only**: Results may not generalize to posterior teeth or maxillary teeth with different anatomy and access geometry.
- **Short-term observation**: No follow-up beyond immediate post-filling assessment; long-term void progression or micro-leakage not evaluated.
- **Single filling technique**: Only gutta-percha/sealer used; other filling materials (bioceramic, thermoplastic) not tested.
- **Instrumentation time/apical force not controlled**: MI vs. traditional access may impose different tactile feedback and operator force profiles, not quantified.

## 7. Glossary
- **Minimally invasive (MI) access cavity**: Contracted or orifice-directed access preparation that removes less coronal dentin than traditional designs; preserves marginal ridges and pulp chamber walls.
- **Traditional access cavity**: Standard bilateral symmetrical cavity preparation with broad pulp chamber access; maximum removal of coronal dentin.
- **Canal transportation**: Lateral deviation of the canal path during instrumentation, measured as axial displacement from the original canal center.
- **Centring ratio**: Proportion of canal cross-section that remains centered after instrumentation (0 = maximum transportation, 1.0 = perfect centering).
- **Voids**: Air pockets or incomplete filling material condensation within the root canal system, detected via micro-CT.
- **Hard-tissue debris**: Dentin chips and pulp tissue fragments generated during instrumentation and not removed by irrigation.
- **Micro-CT**: Micro-computed tomography; high-resolution 3D imaging for quantifying internal tooth anatomy and filling defects.
- **TRUShape**: Rotary NiTi instrument system (proprietary design for shaping curved canals).
- **MTwo**: Rotary NiTi instrument system (alternative shaping geometry).
- **Gutta-percha**: Natural rubber polymer; standard root canal filling material (typically combined with an epoxy sealer).
