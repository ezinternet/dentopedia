## Three-line Summary

Meta-analysis of RCTs (Anesth Prog 2015; 6 RCTs identified, 4 with adequate data, n=420 patients) evaluating whether preemptive oral NSAID administration reduces postoperative pain following surgical third-molar removal.

Pooled analysis showed no statistically significant reduction in postoperative pain with preemptive NSAIDs versus placebo or no preemptive dose (P=0.2227).

The negative finding challenges routine preemptive NSAID prescribing for third-molar surgery; the evidence base is limited by small included-study count (4 for meta-analysis) and high methodological heterogeneity.

## 2. Key Contributions
1. Tests common clinical hypothesis (pre-emptive analgesia for third molar surgery).
2. Negative finding: preemptive NSAID does NOT show significant pain reduction.
3. Challenges the practice of pre-op NSAID dosing in this indication.

## 3. Methodology
- PubMed search; 704 articles → 6 selected (n=420 subjects)
- 4 studies included in meta-analysis (2 excluded for inadequate numeric data)
- Low risk of bias (Cochrane criteria); high methodological heterogeneity

## 4. Key Results
[근거강함] Preemptive NSAID vs placebo/no preemptive: NS (n=298, P=0.2227, OR not significant)

## 5. Limitations
- Small included study sample (4 for meta-analysis)
- High methodological heterogeneity
- Mostly older RCTs
