3-arm RCT (J Clin Periodontol 2026): 25 patients; CTG and VCMX both significantly increased soft-tissue thickness vs no augmentation at 6 months; CTG superior to VCMX for thickness gain; similar patient-reported outcomes but higher donor-site morbidity for CTG.

## Three-line Summary

3-arm parallel RCT, 25 patients, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki — comparing CTG vs volume-stable collagen matrix vs no augmentation for soft-tissue augmentation at maxillary anterior implant placement, with combined clinical, CBCT, histological, and patient-reported outcomes.

Both CTG and VCMX significantly increased soft-tissue thickness vs no augmentation at 6 months; CTG was superior to VCMX for thickness gain; donor-site morbidity was higher with CTG and willingness to repeat the procedure was lower in the CTG group.

Small per-group sample (n=8–9); 6-month follow-up; histological data at 3 months; larger trials needed to confirm long-term stability differences between CTG and VCMX.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First 3-arm RCT (CTG vs VCMX vs no augmentation) with combined clinical/CBCT/histological/PROMs
- Both CTG and VCMX increase soft-tissue thickness (STT) vs control; CTG superior to VCMX
- Histological integration: both VCMX and SCTG showed favorable integration at 3 months
- VCMX: lower donor-site morbidity, mild inflammation consistent with physiological response
- CTG: lower willingness to repeat procedure vs VCMX despite comparable PROMs for pain

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: 3-arm parallel RCT; superiority framework
- Setting: Postgraduate Clinic, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki; maxillary anterior implant placements
- n: 25 patients (CTG n=8, VCMX n=9, control n=8); sample size powered for 1mm STT difference
- Inclusion: systemically healthy adults, maxillary anterior implant, ridge width ≤6 mm, KOM ≥2 mm, no severe soft-tissue deficiency
- Exclusion: heavy smokers (>10 cigs/day), untreated periodontitis, need for bone augmentation
- Outcomes: STT by ultrasonography, BBT by CBCT, clinical parameters, histological biopsy at 3 months, PROMs
- Follow-up: 6 months (STT and BBT); histology at 3 months

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- STT (6 months): both CTG and VCMX significantly increased vs control; CTG > VCMX for thickness
- BBT (buccal bone thickness): reduction in all groups including augmented — extent varies
- Histology: VCMX showed remodeling with mild inflammation at 3 months; CTG showed well-organized connective tissue network
- PROMs: comparable pain and analgesic intake between CTG and VCMX
- Willingness to repeat: lower for CTG group (donor-site morbidity effect)
- Complications: CTG — donor-site morbidity; VCMX — mild inflammation, secondary healing; control — none

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Small sample size (n=25) limits generalizability
- Short 6-month follow-up — long-term stability of VCMX uncertain
- Uneven implant distribution across groups
- Immediate STA performed — comparative timing studies needed

## 6. Related Work
- SCTG remains gold standard; VCMX (e.g., Fibro-Gide) reduces donor-site morbidity
- Thin phenotype (<2 mm STT) associated with marginal bone resorption and esthetic compromise

## 7. Glossary
- CTG/SCTG: (Sub-)epithelial connective tissue graft
- VCMX: Volume-stable collagen matrix (e.g., Fibro-Gide — porcine collagen, maintains volume)
- STT: Soft-tissue thickness (assessed by ultrasonography)
- BBT: Buccal bone thickness (assessed by CBCT)
- PROMs: Patient-reported outcome measures
- KOM: Keratinized oral mucosa
