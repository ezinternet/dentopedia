## Three-line Summary

3-arm parallel RCT, 45 patients (15 per group), single center Kafrelsheikh University Egypt — comparing fascia lata allograft (FLA), PRF, and CTG for peri-implant soft-tissue augmentation in thin-phenotype maxillary anterior implants at 6 months.

CTG produced the greatest soft-tissue thickness gain (gold standard confirmed); FLA was closely comparable to CTG without donor-site morbidity; PRF offered modest augmentation useful for minor enhancements.

Single center limits generalizability; 6-month follow-up is short for long-term stability assessment; FLA emerges as a promising minimally invasive alternative to CTG.

## 세줄요약

3군 평행 RCT, 45명 (군당 15명), 이집트 카프렐세이크 대학 단일 기관 — 상악 전치부 박형 임플란트에서 FLA·PRF·CTG 연조직 증강 비교, 6개월 추적.

CTG 최대 두께 획득(금본위 확인); FLA는 CTG에 근접하면서 공여부 없음; PRF는 경미한 증강 효과.

단일 기관으로 일반화 제한; 6개월 추적이 장기 안정성 평가에 짧음; FLA가 최소침습 CTG 대안으로 유망.

## 1. Document Information
- Journal: BMC Oral Health, 2026. DOI: 10.1186/s12903-026-09589-5
- PMID: 42625175; PMC: PMC13491690
- Setting: Faculty of Dentistry, Kafrelsheikh University, Egypt
- 3-arm parallel RCT; n=45 (15 per group)

## 2. Key Contributions
- First 3-arm RCT directly comparing FLA vs PRF vs CTG for peri-implant soft-tissue augmentation
- CTG = gold standard (greatest STT gain); FLA nearly comparable, no donor-site morbidity
- PRF provides modest volume improvement — viable for minor enhancements or early-phase healing
- FLA is a highly promising minimally invasive alternative to CTG for thin-phenotype anterior implants
- Fills evidence gap: FLA had not been compared head-to-head against CTG and PRF simultaneously

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: 3-arm parallel RCT
- n=45: SCTG (n=15), PRF (n=15), FLA (n=15)
- Population: thin biotype (≤1 mm STT), maxillary anterior single-implant sites, healthy periodontium
- Setting: Kafrelsheikh University, Egypt
- Follow-up: 6 months post-surgery
- Primary outcome: soft-tissue thickness (STT) gain via ultrasound/measurement
- Secondary: clinical attachment parameters, patient-reported outcomes (PROMs), donor-site morbidity

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- CTG group: greatest STT gain — gold standard confirmed
- FLA group: STT gain closely comparable to CTG; NO secondary surgical site; lower morbidity
- PRF group: modest STT improvement — beneficial for minor augmentation or adjunct healing
- All 3 groups effective for peri-implant STT augmentation in thin-phenotype maxillary anterior implants
- FLA: acellular collagenous matrix, integrates biologically, volumetrically stable
- PROMs: FLA and PRF likely lower morbidity scores vs CTG (donor-site eliminated or minimal)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Single center (Kafrelsheikh University) — generalizability question
- n=15 per group — underpowered for definitive non-inferiority of FLA vs CTG
- 6-month follow-up — long-term STT stability not established
- Thin-biotype anterior only — no posterior, multi-unit, or peri-implantitis sites
- Need: multicenter RCTs, ≥12-month follow-up, diverse implant positions

## 6. Related Work
- VCMX vs CTG at implant placement: Papantonatou 2026 (J Clin Periodontol) — complements with xenograft collagen matrix comparison
- PRF for tissue augmentation: PRF's healing and growth factor role established; lower volume augmentation effect consistent with prior work
- FLA: acellular dermal matrix / fascia lata allograft literature emerging — this RCT represents strongest head-to-head evidence

## 7. Glossary
- FLA: Fascia lata allograft — acellular collagenous matrix derived from donor fascia; no secondary surgical site
- PRF: Platelet-rich fibrin — autologous concentrate; growth factors (PDGF, TGF-β1, VEGF) from centrifuged blood
- SCTG: Subepithelial connective tissue graft — palatal donor graft; gold standard for STT augmentation
- STT: Soft tissue thickness — measured in mm; primary surrogate for augmentation success
- Thin phenotype/biotype: ≤1 mm STT; increased recession risk peri-implant
