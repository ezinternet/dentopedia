## Why Ingested

Adds an electrolyte/magnesium angle to the held masticatory-muscle-pain evidence, which so far covers mainly manual therapy, botulinum toxin, and general myogenous-TMD management (see [[tmj/chan-2022-myogenous-tmd-diagnosis-treatment-review]] and [[botulinum-toxin/abdulrahman-2026-botulinum-toxin-myofascial-pain-masseter-temporalis]]); it is also a useful caution that the evidence is indirect (non-TMD cramp populations).

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review with meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD420251120631; 13 RCTs/experimental studies, 10 magnesium) of electrolyte supplementation for muscle cramps and myalgia in non-TMD populations, with a prespecified, hypothesis-generating extrapolation to TMD myalgia; no trial in TMD patients was found.

Oral magnesium reduced cramp frequency in pregnancy (4 trials, N about 364; pooled RR 1.35, 95% CI 1.05-1.74, P=.02) but not in adult nocturnal/persistent leg cramps (4 trials, N about 396; MD -0.42 cramps/week, 95% CI -1.15 to 0.31, P=.26); calcium and potassium had no eligible trials, sodium-based drinks helped only in exercise and cirrhosis contexts.

The adult null result is a negative signal for systemic magnesium in TMD myalgia; the authors favor testing targeted local magnesium in TMD trials, but all TMD relevance is indirect extrapolation and not clinical evidence.

## 세줄요약

근육 경련·근육통에 대한 전해질 보충 효과를 본 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis, SR+MA; 무작위대조시험 13편, 마그네슘 10편)으로, 측두하악장애 (Temporomandibular Disorder, TMD) 근육통으로의 외삽은 가설 생성 수준이며 TMD 환자 대상 시험은 없었다.

경구 마그네슘은 임신 중 경련 빈도를 줄였으나 (4편, 약 364명; 통합 위험비 1.35, 95% CI 1.05-1.74, P=.02), 성인 야간·지속성 다리 경련에서는 효과가 없었다 (4편, 약 396명; 평균차 -0.42회/주, 95% CI -1.15~0.31, P=.26); 칼슘·칼륨은 적격 시험이 없었고 나트륨 음료는 운동·간경변 상황에서만 효과가 있었다.

성인 경련의 무효 결과는 TMD 근육통에 대한 전신 마그네슘 보충에 불리한 신호이며, 저자들은 국소 마그네슘 투여 시험을 제안하나 TMD 관련성은 모두 간접적 외삽일 뿐 임상 근거가 아니다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic synthesis of electrolyte supplementation (magnesium, sodium, calcium, potassium) for muscle cramps/myalgia with explicit TMD framing.
- Finds no RCT of electrolytes in TMD myofascial pain; extrapolation is prespecified as hypothesis-generating.
- Separates pregnancy cramps (magnesium effective) from adult nocturnal/persistent cramps (no pooled effect), and argues the latter is closer to TMD patients.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, January 1995 to August 2025 (last search 2025-08-15); human, English.
- Eligibility: randomized or quasi-randomized trials of oral, intravenous, or topical electrolytes for cramps or myalgia; exclusions for dialysis-dependent renal disease, arrhythmia, preeclampsia/eclampsia, bowel preparation.
- Two reviewers screened and extracted; RoB 2; random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird); I2 and Cochran Q; meta-analysis only when at least 2 comparable studies.
- Selection: 75 records, 34 full texts, 13 included (n 9 to 184 per study).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 10 magnesium trials, 3 multi-electrolyte/sodium beverage studies; no calcium or potassium monotherapy trial.
- Pregnancy (4 RCTs, about 364 women): pooled RR about 1.35 (95% CI 1.05-1.74, P=.02) for magnesium vs placebo on cramp frequency; 3 of 4 trials positive, Araujo (magnesium citrate) no difference.
- Adult nocturnal/persistent leg cramps (4 RCTs, nearly 400 participants): MD -0.42 episodes/week (95% CI -1.15 to 0.31, P=.26); Barna (magnesium oxide monohydrate, 60 days) positive, Frusso, Roffe, Roguin Maor not.
- Intravenous magnesium sulphate: no effect on rest cramps in older adults (Garrison); high-dose pretreatment reduced suxamethonium fasciculations and postoperative myalgia (Ojo). Neither pooled.
- Sodium/multi-electrolyte: raised cramp stimulation threshold (Earp), reduced electrically induced cramp susceptibility (Lau), reduced cramp severity in cirrhosis with pickle juice (Tapper; no change in sleep or HRQoL). Not pooled.
- Risk of bias: pregnancy trials mostly low; Frusso and Roffe some concerns; small crossover studies some concerns; overall moderate quality.
- TMD: a recent RCT (cited by the authors, not part of the review) of local magnesium sulphate injection into the masseter is described as reducing pain and improving function in TMD myalgia.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Heterogeneity in population, magnesium salt (citrate, oxide, bisglycinate, sulphate), and duration; small samples; some older trials with incomplete reporting.
- Extrapolation from systemic cramps to TMD is inferential; evidence for sodium, calcium, potassium is sparse.
- Source inconsistency: the abstract reports the pregnancy pooled estimate as RR 1.35 for a reduction in cramp frequency without stating the direction convention (an RR above 1 would usually indicate more events); the text does not clarify. Treat the magnitude with caution. Nocturnal N is given as about 396 (abstract) and nearly 400 (text).
- Future: well-powered placebo-controlled magnesium trials in TMD myofascial pain, systemic and local routes.

## 7. Glossary

- Myalgia: muscle pain; in TMD, local myalgia, myofascial pain, and myofascial pain with referral.
- NMDA receptor: glutamate receptor blocked by magnesium; involved in central sensitization.
- Nociceptor sensitization: lowered firing threshold of pain-sensing neurons.
- RoB 2: Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool for randomized trials.
- PROSPERO: international register of systematic review protocols.
