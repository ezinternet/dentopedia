## Why Ingested

This SR addresses a concrete chairside protocol question — how many days a patient should wear each aligner tray before advancing — that is directly actionable at the operatory level, unlike most clear-aligner papers in this wiki that focus on biomechanics or material properties. It complements [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/nakornnoi-2024-aligner-trimline-biomechanics-tooth-movement-sr]] (trimline/force-delivery design) by addressing the *temporal* dimension of aligner activation rather than the mechanical design dimension, and provides the evidence backbone for any future wear-compliance or trimline-plus-schedule synthesis.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42021288179) of 6 studies (3 RCT, 3 non-RCT; total analyzed n=449) comparing expedited clear aligner wear protocols (7-day, 10-day) against the conventional 14-day protocol for orthodontic tooth movement (OTM) efficacy in adults (Invisalign only).

4/6 studies (2 RCT, 2 retrospective) found no significant difference in OTM efficacy between expedited and conventional schedules; 2 studies (1 RCT, 1 prospective cohort) found the 14-day protocol significantly more accurate for specific posterior movements (maxillary intrusion/distal tipping/buccal torque, mandibular intrusion/extrusion); meta-analysis was precluded by heterogeneity.

Evidence certainty was "very low" for OTM efficacy (GRADE) and "high" for pain perception (no difference between 10-day and 14-day, 1 RCT); authors propose a hybrid wear protocol (shorter wear for simple movements, 14-day for complex ones) as a clinically pragmatic compromise.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (PROSPERO CRD42021288179), 6개 연구 (RCT 3편, 비RCT 3편; 총 분석 n=449)로 성인(Invisalign 전용)에서 단축 투명교정장치 착용 프로토콜(7일, 10일)과 기존 14일 프로토콜 간 치아이동(OTM) 효율성을 비교.

6편 중 4편(RCT 2편, 후향적 2편)은 단축 프로토콜과 기존 프로토콜 간 유의한 차이 없음; 2편(RCT 1편, 전향적 코호트 1편)은 특정 후방 치아이동(상악 압하/원심경사/협측토크, 하악 압하/정출)에서 14일 프로토콜이 유의하게 더 정확; 이질성으로 메타분석 불가.

OTM 효율성에 대한 근거수준은 "매우 낮음"(GRADE), 통증 지각에 대해서는 "높음"(10일 vs 14일 차이 없음, RCT 1편); 저자들은 단순 이동엔 단축 착용, 복잡한 이동엔 14일 착용을 결합한 하이브리드 착용 프로토콜을 임상적으로 실용적인 절충안으로 제안.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review (per authors) specifically synthesizing comparative evidence on expedited (7-day/10-day) vs. conventional (14-day) clear aligner wear/activation protocols.
- Applies formal risk-of-bias tools (Cochrane RoB 2.0 for RCTs; Newcastle–Ottawa Scale for non-RCTs) and GRADE certainty rating to a topic previously assessed only in individual primary studies.
- Synthesizes across both primary outcome (OTM efficacy via 3D superimposition or occlusal indices) and secondary outcome (pain perception via VAS).
- Proposes a "hybrid aligner-wear protocol" — 7-day wear for simpler movements, 14-day wear for movements shown to need it (posterior intrusion, distal tipping, torque) — as an actionable synthesis rather than a blanket recommendation.
- Offers biological rationale (inflammatory mediator/RANKL release kinetics) for why shortening wear time may not compromise OTM efficacy for most movements.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- PRISMA-adherent systematic review, protocol registered in PROSPERO (CRD42021288179).
- **Search**: MEDLINE (PubMed), Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, Google Scholar, DOAJ, Cochrane Library, OpenGrey, ClinicalTrials.gov; no language/date restriction; initial search October 2023, updated to 18 February 2024. 9076 records identified → 34 full-text reviewed → 6 studies included.
- **PICOS**:
  - Population: adults (>18y) undergoing clear aligner therapy in both arches
  - Intervention: any manufacturer's aligners with activation schedule <14 days
  - Comparison: same appliance, 14-day activation schedule
  - Primary outcome: OTM efficacy (occlusal indices or digital superimposition)
  - Secondary outcome: pain perception (VAS/questionnaires)
  - Study designs: RCT, prospective, retrospective; excluded in vitro, case reports, narrative reviews, studies using auxiliary acceleration methods
- Two independent assessors (J.M., E.P.) for screening (Rayyan software), extraction, and RoB.
- RoB: Cochrane RoB 2.0 (RCTs, 5 domains) and Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (non-RCTs, 3 domains: selection/comparability/outcome).
- Certainty of evidence: GRADE Pro tool, independently by two assessors.
- Meta-analysis was planned only if substantial homogeneity existed; it was precluded due to significant methodological/reporting heterogeneity — synthesis is qualitative/descriptive only.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Primary outcome — OTM efficacy:**
- 4/6 studies (Zhao 2023 RCT, Clements 2003 RCT, Stephens 2022, Wei 2023 — 2 RCT + 2 retrospective) found **no significant difference** (P>0.05) in OTM efficacy between expedited and conventional protocols.
- 2/6 studies (Al-Nadawi 2020 RCT, Castroflorio 2023 prospective cohort) found the **14-day protocol significantly more efficacious** (P<0.05):
  - Al-Nadawi: maxillary posterior intrusion (0.3 vs 0.4mm), distal crown tip (2.8° vs 3.9°), buccal crown torque (2.8° vs 4.4°) — 14-day > 7-day; mandibular posterior intrusion (0.3 vs 0.4mm) and extrusion (0.2 vs 0.3mm) — 14-day > 7-day & 10-day. No significant differences for any anterior tooth movements in either arch, or for 10-day vs 14-day comparisons in the maxilla.
  - Castroflorio: 14-day protocol reduced "lack of correction" by 12% more than 7-day (P=0.009); 7-day vs 10-day difference not significant.
- GRADE certainty for OTM efficacy: **very low** (both 7-day vs 14-day and 10-day vs 14-day comparisons) — downgraded for serious RoB and very serious inconsistency (contradictory findings between studies using similar comparisons).

**Secondary outcome — pain perception:**
- Zhao 2023 (RCT): no significant difference in VAS pain scores between 10-day and 14-day groups at any timepoint (day 1, 2, 3, 7 of each stage); pain peaked on day 1 of stage 1 in both groups.
- GRADE certainty: **high**.

**Treatment completion (Bollen 2003):** 14-day subjects showed a non-significant trend toward higher completion rate (37%) vs 7-day (21%), P<0.2.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Author-stated: substantial methodological heterogeneity across included studies precluded quantitative meta-analysis; only descriptive/qualitative synthesis possible.
- 2 of 6 studies (both RCTs comprising a large share of RCT evidence) had high risk of bias, lowering overall certainty.
- Only 6 studies met inclusion criteria out of 9076 initially screened records — a narrow evidence base for a widely-practiced clinical protocol change (7-day wear is now an industry-default Invisalign recommendation).
- No studies assessed potential adverse effects (root resorption, periodontal compromise) of expedited wear schedules — flagged explicitly as an open safety question given the accelerated force application.
- Study population restricted to adults (>18y) treated with Invisalign only — generalizability to adolescents or non-Invisalign aligner systems unknown.
- Small individual study sample sizes (as low as n=12–14 per arm in Bollen/Clements) and substantial sample attrition (up to 70% non-completion in Bollen 2003).

## 6. Related Work

- Align Technology's 2016 internal analysis (>200 Invisalign patients) recommending 7-day wear, cited as the origin of the expedited-protocol trend but not itself peer-reviewed.
- Zheng et al. 2017 (Orthod Craniofac Res) SR+MA on clear aligner efficiency/effectiveness/stability generally.
- Ke et al. 2019 (BMC Oral Health) comparing clear aligner vs fixed appliance treatment effectiveness.
- Krishnan et al. 2017 and Kapoor et al. 2014 — cited for biological rationale (inflammatory mediator and RANKL release kinetics) explaining why shortened wear may not compromise OTM for most tooth movements.
- Peter & George 2022 (AJODO) — cited regarding environmental impact of aligner tray consumption, relevant to the "hybrid protocol reduces tray count" argument.

## 7. Glossary

- **OTM** — Orthodontic Tooth Movement
- **CAT** — Clear Aligner Therapy
- **RoB** — Risk of Bias
- **RCT** — Randomized Controlled Trial
- **GRADE** — Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation
- **VAS** — Visual Analog Scale (pain measurement)
- **PAR index** — Peer Assessment Rating index (occlusal outcome scoring)
- **AII index** — Average Incisor Irregularity index
- **RANKL** — Receptor Activator of Nuclear factor Kappa-B Ligand (bone resorption marker)
- **Activation schedule / wear protocol** — the interval (days) a patient wears a single aligner tray before switching to the next in the series
- **Hybrid aligner-wear protocol** — proposed approach mixing shorter wear (7-day) for simple movements with longer wear (14-day) for movements shown to need more time (e.g., posterior intrusion/torque)
