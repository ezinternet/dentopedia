## Why Ingested

An umbrella review pooling 8 systematic reviews (5130 implants, 1448 models/patients) with AMSTAR-2 quality grading and a random-effects meta-analysis of non-duplicate primary studies — the first overview-of-reviews synthesis on robot-assisted implant accuracy we hold, one level above the individual RCT/cohort comparisons already in [[digital-workflow/wei-2025-autonomous-robotic-surgery-dynamic-navigation]] and the [[overviews/robotic-vs-navigation-implant-accuracy]] synthesis (which found the robot's reliable advantage is angular, not linear/coronal). This paper instead reports a significant pooled coronal-deviation advantage for robots (SMD −0.97), which is worth reconciling against that overview's "angular-only" thesis in a future refresh.

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (AMSTAR-2-graded) of 8 systematic reviews covering 5130 implants in 1448 models/patients, comparing robot-assisted dental implant surgery accuracy to dynamic/static computer-assisted or freehand placement.

Robot-assisted surgery showed lower apical and angular deviations than comparator methods across included reviews; pooled random-effects meta-analysis of coronal deviation favored robots (SMD −0.97, 95% CI −2.27 to −0.32; P<.01), though individual-review coronal findings were mixed and robotic automation levels varied (fully automatic, semi-automatic, passive).

Clinically robot-assisted placement appears to improve positional accuracy (apical, coronal, angular) over dynamic/static guided or freehand implant placement, but the evidence base is thin (only 8 eligible reviews from 269 records) and heterogeneous across robot automation levels, limiting confidence in the pooled coronal effect.

임상적으로 로봇보조 식립은 동적/정적 가이드 또는 프리핸드 대비 위치 정확도(첨단부·관상부·각도)를 개선하는 것으로 보이나, 근거기반이 얇고(269건 중 8개 리뷰만 최종 포함) 로봇 자동화 수준 간 이질성이 커 관상부 풀링 효과의 확신도는 제한적이다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First umbrella review (SR-of-SRs) specifically on robot-assisted dental implant surgery positional accuracy, applying AMSTAR-2 critical appraisal to the included systematic reviews.
- Performed a random-effects meta-analysis on non-duplicate primary studies pooled from the included reviews (rather than simply narratively summarizing review-level conclusions), addressing double-counting of overlapping primary studies — a known weakness of prior single-layer SRs in this space.
- Explicitly stratifies findings by level of robotic automation (fully automatic, semi-automatic, passive), flagging that accuracy claims are not uniform across robot types.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Umbrella review of systematic reviews (with or without meta-analysis), following JBI umbrella-review methodology and PRISMA 2020 reporting.
- **Databases**: PubMed-MEDLINE, Science Direct, Scopus, Embase, searched to April 2025, by two independent reviewers (Rayyan used for screening).
- **Quality appraisal**: AMSTAR 2 (A MeaSurement Tool to Assess systematic Reviews 2).
- **n**: 269 records screened → 8 systematic reviews included for final analysis, covering 5130 implants placed in 1448 models/patients.
- **Outcomes**: Apical deviation, coronal deviation, angular deviation of robot-assisted vs dynamic-CAIS, static-CAIS, or freehand implant placement. Random-effects model used to account for between-study heterogeneity in the pooled meta-analysis of non-duplicate primary studies.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Robot-assisted implant surgeries showed better overall accuracy than dynamic or static computer-assisted surgeries or freehand surgery.
- Apical and angular deviations: consistently lower with robot-assisted surgery across included reviews.
- Coronal deviation: mixed results at the individual-review level, but pooled meta-analysis of non-duplicate primary studies favored robots — SMD −0.97, 95% CI (−2.27, −0.32), P<.01.
- Automation heterogeneity: reviews included fully automatic, semi-automatic, and passive robotic systems, which were not uniformly superior — automation level affects the size/consistency of the accuracy advantage.
- Conclusion as stated by authors: robot-assisted implant surgeries achieved better positional accuracies (apical, coronal, angular) than dynamic or static computer-guided or freehand implant placement.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Only 8 systematic reviews (from 269 initially screened records) met inclusion criteria — a narrow evidence base for an umbrella review, with likely primary-study overlap across the 8 reviews despite the deduplication attempt.
- Wide confidence interval on the pooled coronal SMD (−2.27 to −0.32) signals substantial heterogeneity; the authors themselves note "mixed results" at the review level for coronal deviation.
- Robotic automation level (fully automatic vs semi-automatic vs passive) is a major confound not fully separated in the pooled analysis — mirrors the wiki's existing finding (Chen 2025 semi-autonomous vs Yu/Wei 2025 more autonomous systems) that automation level changes which accuracy component improves.
- No linkage of positional-accuracy differences to hard clinical outcomes (osseointegration, survival, prosthetic fit, MBL) — consistent with the gap already flagged in [[overviews/robotic-vs-navigation-implant-accuracy]].
- Full text was not accessible (paywalled abstract page); this source page is built from the structured abstract, reference list, and PubMed metadata only — some sub-analysis details (e.g., per-review AMSTAR-2 scores, forest plots) are not captured here.

## 7. Glossary
- **Umbrella review**: A systematic review of systematic reviews, synthesizing conclusions across multiple existing SRs/SR+MAs on a topic.
- **AMSTAR 2**: A critical appraisal tool for assessing the methodological quality of systematic reviews of randomized or non-randomized studies.
- **SMD**: Standardized mean difference, an effect-size metric used to pool continuous outcomes (e.g., deviation in mm/degrees) measured differently across studies.
- **Apical/coronal/angular deviation**: Standard implant-placement accuracy metrics — linear deviation at the implant apex, linear deviation at the coronal (platform) point, and angular deviation of the implant long axis from the planned trajectory.
- **r-CAIS**: Robotic computer-assisted implant surgery.
