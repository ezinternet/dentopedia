## Why Ingested

The wiki holds only review-level statements that physical counterpressure maneuvers prevent vasovagal syncope ([[wiki/drug/systemic-disease/li-2026-syncope-in-dentistry-risk-factors]], [[wiki/drug/systemic-disease/malave-2022-vasovagal-reactions-during-interventional-pain]]) and no dental study that actually tests a maneuver as prophylaxis. This pilot trial supplies that missing primary dental-extraction data point, while also marking how thin the evidence is (n=30, no blinding described).

## Three-line Summary

Single-centre pilot trial (n=30 adults aged 18–75 y with prior syncope and dental anxiety, 15 per group) teaching leg raise + leg fold maneuvers before and during extraction versus conventional extraction.
Post-treatment syncope occurred in 5/15 (33.3%) controls vs 0/15 (0%) in the maneuver group (chi-square 6.000, p=0.014), and giddiness in 9/15 (60.0%) vs 3/15 (20.0%) (p=0.025); post-op systolic BP and pulse were higher in the maneuver group (136 ± 8.281 vs 109.33 ± 8.837 mmHg; 80.07 ± 4.728 vs 68.93 ± 3.515 bpm, both p<0.001).
The result is hypothesis-generating only — 30 patients, no blinding or allocation-concealment detail, syncope judged from clinical signs, and a significant baseline age imbalance (p=0.026) that the groups were never adjusted for.

## 세줄요약

단일기관 예비 연구(n=30, 이전 실신 병력과 치과 불안이 있는 18–75세 성인, 군당 15명) — 발치 전·중에 다리 들기 (Leg Raise) + 다리 접기 (Leg Fold) 신체 술기를 교육한 군과 통상 발치군을 비교.
술후 실신은 대조군 5/15명(33.3%) vs 술기군 0/15명(0%) (카이제곱 6.000, p=0.014), 어지럼증은 9/15명(60.0%) vs 3/15명(20.0%) (p=0.025); 술후 수축기 혈압과 맥박은 술기군에서 더 높았다(136 ± 8.281 vs 109.33 ± 8.837 mmHg; 80.07 ± 4.728 vs 68.93 ± 3.515 bpm, 모두 p<0.001).
가설 생성 수준의 근거 — 30명, 눈가림·배정 은폐 기술 없음, 실신은 임상 징후로 판정, 기저 나이 분포가 유의하게 달랐으나(p=0.026) 보정하지 않았다.

## 1. Document Information

- **Journal**: Cureus 2023;15(2):e34488
- **DOI**: 10.7759/cureus.34488 (PMID 36874341, PMCID PMC9982758)
- **Institution**: Tamil Nadu Government Dental College and Hospital, Chennai, India
- **Type**: Pilot comparative study (two groups of 15; abstract says "randomly divided", Methods gives no sequence generation or allocation concealment)
- **Ethics**: informed consent obtained from all patients (as stated)

## 2. Key Contributions

- First dental-extraction study to use a physical counterpressure maneuver (leg raise + leg fold performed by the seated patient) as **prophylaxis** rather than as abortive treatment of an established faint.
- Reports a large absolute difference in post-treatment syncope (33.3% → 0%) in a deliberately high-risk sample: 100% prior syncope history, dental anxiety, extraction under local anesthesia.
- Ties the maneuver's rationale to the large-muscle-mass argument (greater isometric muscle mass → larger sympathetic/BP response), citing non-dental counterpressure literature (hand grip, squatting, isometric arm contraction).
- Provides a practical chairside protocol: 5 leg raises to ≥15 cm each leg, then 5 knee-extension folds per leg, taught before local anesthesia and repeated at intervals during extraction.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: single-centre, two-arm pilot study; 30 patients "divided" into 15 + 15.
- **Population**: 18–75 y, previous history of syncope **and** dental anxiety. Exclusions: musculoskeletal disease, suspected/overt heart disease with likely cardiac syncope, orthostatic hypotension, non-syncopal loss of consciousness, cognitive/physical inability, doubtful compliance, inaccessible follow-up, pregnancy.
- **Intervention (Group I, n=15)**: education + rehearsal of leg raise and leg fold before extraction; performed before local anesthesia, at regular intervals intraoperatively, and if required postoperatively. Sudden jerky movements discouraged.
- **Control (Group II, n=15)**: conventional extraction. Rescue for either arm: stop procedure, reverse Trendelenburg chair, standard syncope protocol.
- **Outcomes**: blood pressure (manual sphygmomanometer), SpO2 and pulse rate (pulse oximeter) pre-/intra-/postoperatively; clinical signs and symptoms (syncope, giddiness, sweating, cold clammy skin). Diagnosis of vasovagal syncope referenced to ESC guideline definition.
- **Statistics**: chi-square for categorical comparisons, paired t-test within the maneuver group, independent-samples t-test between groups.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Sample size**: 30 patients, explicitly a pilot; the 5/15 vs 0/15 difference carries wide uncertainty (no confidence interval for the risk difference reported).
- **Allocation**: the abstract says patients were "randomly divided", but the Methods only state they "were divided" — no sequence generation, no allocation concealment, and no blinding of patient, operator or outcome assessor is described anywhere. The significant baseline age imbalance (p=0.026) is consistent with weak or absent randomization.
- **Outcome ascertainment**: syncope/giddiness recorded as clinical signs and symptoms by an unblinded clinician; no pre-specified definition of giddiness, and no time-stamped relationship between a maneuver and the outcome.
- **Inference mismatch**: the between-group post-op BP/HR comparison has no between-group *baseline* comparison, so higher maneuver-group values could partly reflect baseline or age differences, and the rise in BP/HR is equally compatible with exercise-induced sympathetic activation as with "improved hemodynamics".
- **Confounding by attention**: the maneuver group received extra education, rehearsal, and repeated reminders — a co-intervention that could reduce anxiety independently of the maneuver.
- **Generalisability**: single centre in India, one operator team, one extraction type; no follow-up, no reporting of which maneuver (leg raise vs leg fold) drove the effect.
- Text note: the retrieved PMC text ends at the Conclusions section; the reference list and any dedicated limitations section were not included in the extracted text.
- The authors' claims ("proves that syncope ... had been prevented", "risk-free") overstate what a 30-patient unblinded pilot can support.

## 6. Related Work

- **Alizadeh et al.** — compared hand grip and squatting maneuvers in patients with prior syncope; reported reduced long-term vasovagal syncope versus control (cited as the conceptual basis).
- **Kim et al.** — squatting with muscle tensing raised BP more effectively than hand grip.
- **Brignole et al.** — isometric arm contraction aborted impending vasovagal syncope by raising systemic BP.
- **Seals** — BP response to isometric exercise scales with contracting muscle mass (rationale for using leg muscles).
- **Malave 2022** — review listing counterpressure (squatting, leg crossing) as part of VVS management rather than prophylaxis.
- **Li 2026** — dental review listing counterpressure maneuvers among preventive strategies but without dental-extraction trial data.

## 7. Glossary

- **Vasovagal syncope (VVS)**: neurally mediated transient loss of consciousness with hypotension and relative bradycardia from cerebral hypoperfusion.
- **Physical counterpressure maneuver**: isometric skeletal-muscle contraction (leg raise, leg fold, hand grip, squatting, leg crossing) used to raise venous return and systemic BP.
- **Leg raise maneuver**: seated patient lifts each leg 5 times to a minimum height of 15 cm.
- **Leg fold maneuver**: seated patient folds and stretches each leg 5 times at the knee, avoiding jerky movements.
- **Reverse Trendelenburg**: chair position with head higher than feet; stated in this paper as the rescue position for developing syncope (note: conventional rescue is supine/Trendelenburg).
