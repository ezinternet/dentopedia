## Why Ingested

Documents a rare but instructive presentation of TMJ retrodiscal tissue pathology — a posterior ganglion cyst manifesting as position-dependent hearing loss — expanding the differential diagnosis for TMD-adjacent otologic symptoms. The mechanical explanation (closed-mouth posterior translation compressing the external auditory canal via retrodiscal tissue adhesion) is clinically novel. Reinforces [[tmj/kim-2012-diagnosis-treatment-temporomandibular-disorder]] (retrodiscal tissue role in TMJ pathology).

## Three-line Summary

A 72-year-old woman presented with 3 years of bilateral preauricular pain and near-complete left hearing loss that occurred exclusively during mandibular occlusion and resolved immediately on mouth opening. MRI revealed a ~7 × 10 mm left posterior TMJ ganglion cyst adherent to the retrodiscal tissues; dynamic EAC narrowing was confirmed on physical examination. Preauricular open excision achieved full hearing recovery and pain resolution at 2-year follow-up with no recurrence.

## Key Contributions

1. **Novel mechanical mechanism**: Closed-mouth posterior condylar translation causes retrodiscal cystic mass to shift anteriorly, functionally narrowing the EAC — a previously undescribed pathomechanism linking TMJ position to otologic symptoms.
2. **Dynamic EAC examination**: Physical demonstration that EAC caliber changes with mandibular position is the key bedside diagnostic maneuver for this entity.
3. **Histopathologic distinction**: Confirmed ganglion cyst (no synovial lining; gelatinous fluid; fibrous wall) vs. synovial cyst (synovial lining present; potential joint communication) — distinction requires histopathology, not MRI alone.
4. **Surgical outcome**: Complete excision via preauricular approach with facial nerve monitoring yielded full recovery at 2 years, supporting open excision as definitive treatment.
5. **Literature synthesis**: Reviews all prior reported TMJ ganglion/synovial cysts with otologic manifestations; notes largest pathologic series suggests synovial cysts may predominate when immunohistochemistry is applied.

## Methodology

**Study type**: Single case report with narrative literature review.

**Patient**: 72-year-old woman; 3-year history of bilateral preauricular pain, left tinnitus, and aural fullness. Primary complaint: near-complete left hearing loss occurring only when mandible in occlusion, resolving immediately on mouth opening.

**Past medical history**: Osteoarthritis, fibromyalgia, depression. Surgical history: rhytidectomy (facelift) 2008; Mohs surgery left conchal region 2016.

**Clinical examination**:
- Dynamic narrowing of left EAC confirmed with mandibular closure on direct otoscopy
- Maximum mouth opening: 52 mm with mild discomfort; no clicking or crepitus
- Cranial nerves V and VII intact bilaterally

**Imaging**: MRI — bilateral anterior disc displacement with reduction (ADDwR); left posterior meniscal/retrodiscal cyst approximately 7 mm AP × 10 mm craniocaudal.

**Surgical approach**: Preauricular approach through prior rhytidectomy scar; continuous facial nerve monitoring; cyst found adherent to posterior articular disc and retrodiscal tissues; small disc portion excised en bloc with cyst; EAC wall preserved.

**Pathology**:
- Gross: 2.4 × 2.1 × 1.0 cm cystic structure; homogeneous white cut surface; clear gelatinous fluid
- Histopathology: fibrous connective tissue wall with NO synovial lining → confirmed ganglion cyst

**Follow-up**: 4 months (substantial hearing improvement, EAC patent bilaterally); 2 years (hearing normalized, preauricular pain resolved, no recurrence).

**Pathogenesis (from literature review)**:
- Prevailing theory: myxoid degeneration of periarticular collagenous connective tissue from chronic stress, repetitive microtrauma, or degenerative change
- Posterior location adjacent to EAC enables otologic manifestation
- Mechanical mechanism: closed-mouth → posterior condylar translation + retrodiscal tissue tension → cyst shifts anteriorly → EAC functional obstruction

**Differential diagnosis for posterior TMJ mass**:
- Synovial cyst, ganglion cyst, parotid lesion, inflammatory pseudocyst, synovial chondromatosis, neoplasm
- Otologic DDx: exostoses, osteoma, cholesteatoma

## Limitations

- Single case report; no controlled follow-up; generalizability limited
- MRI cannot reliably distinguish ganglion vs. synovial cyst — histopathology required
- Prior surgical history (rhytidectomy, Mohs) may have altered anatomy and contributed to cyst formation; this confounds pathogenesis interpretation
- Literature review scope limited; the authors note that the largest pathologic review suggests periarticular TMJ cysts may be more frequently synovial than ganglion when immunohistochemistry is applied, introducing uncertainty about true prevalence

## Glossary

- **Ganglion cyst**: Cystic structure with fibrous wall and gelatinous content, lacking synovial lining; arises from myxoid degeneration of periarticular connective tissue
- **Synovial cyst**: Cystic structure lined by synovial cells; may communicate with the joint space; distinguished from ganglion cyst by histopathology
- **External auditory canal (EAC)**: The bony and cartilaginous channel leading from the auricle to the tympanic membrane; its anterior wall is immediately posterior to the TMJ
- **Retrodiscal tissue**: Highly vascularized and innervated bilaminar zone posterior to the articular disc; a common site of pain and pathology in TMD
- **Anterior disc displacement with reduction (ADDwR)**: Common TMJ disc disorder in which the disc is anteriorly displaced at rest but recaptures normal position on mouth opening
- **Preauricular approach**: Surgical incision anterior to the ear, used for TMJ access; can follow prior rhytidectomy scar
- **Myxoid degeneration**: Accumulation of mucoid ground substance in connective tissue, proposed mechanism for ganglion cyst formation
- **Dynamic EAC obstruction**: Functional narrowing of the EAC that varies with mandibular position, as opposed to fixed structural obstruction
