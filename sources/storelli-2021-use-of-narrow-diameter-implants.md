## Abstract

The objective of the present review is to assess the implant survival, marginal bone loss, and biomechanical features of narrow-diameter implants (2.5-3.5 mm) supporting or retaining full-arch fixed or removable restorations. Three operators screened the literature (PubMed, Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar) and performed a hand search on the main journals that focus on implantology until 24 March 2019. Only articles that considered full-arch restorations supported or retained by narrow-diameter implants (2.5-3.5 mm) were considered if they have a minimum of 10 patients and a mean follow-up of at least 6 months. The outcome variables were survival of implants and marginal bone loss. The review was performed according to the PRISMA statements. Risk of bias assessment was evaluated. Failure rates were analyzed using random effect Poisson regression models to obtain the summary estimate of 5-year survival rate and marginal bone loss.

A total of nine papers were finally selected, reporting a high survival rate of the implants. Eight studies focused only on the mandible while one study reported data from both mandible and maxilla. All studies reported on removable restorations; none focused on fixed rehabilitations. The estimated survival rate for 5 years of follow-up was calculated to be 92.25% for the implants. The estimated marginal bone loss after 5 years was calculated to be 1.40 mm.

## Introduction

Narrow-diameter implants (NDIs) have been proposed as an alternative for rehabilitation of completely edentulous patients, particularly in cases with limited bone width or financial constraints. This systematic review evaluates the clinical outcomes of NDIs (2.5-3.5 mm) supporting full-arch restorations.

## Materials and Methods

### Search Strategy
Electronic search of PubMed, Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar until March 24, 2019, plus hand search of major implantology journals (2016-2019). Search terms: (edentulous) AND (small diameter implant OR narrow implant OR mini-implant OR transitional implant OR temporary implant OR provisional implant).

### Inclusion Criteria
- Studies published in English
- Human studies
- Sample size ≥ 10 patients
- Completely edentulous patients rehabilitated with small-diameter implants (2.5-3.5 mm)
- Full-arch removable or fixed restorations
- Follow-up > 6 months
- Prospective cohort, RCT, retrospective case-control, case series

### Exclusion Criteria
- Duplicate cohorts with different follow-ups
- Case reports, animal or in vitro models

### Data Extraction
Study design, implant manufacturer, restoration data, implant/prosthesis survival rates, marginal bone loss, patient satisfaction.

### Risk of Bias Assessment
Cochrane Collaboration's tool across 7 domains.

### Statistical Analysis
Random effect Poisson regression models for 5-year survival rate and MBL estimates. Life table analysis.

## Results

### Included Studies
Nine studies (885 implants total):
- 8 mandibular only, 1 both mandible and maxilla
- All removable restorations (overdentures), no fixed rehabilitations
- Study designs: 5 prospective RCTs, 2 prospective cohorts, 1 retrospective cohort, 1 case series
- Implant systems: Straumann, IMZ, Ankylos, BioHorizons, etc.
- Diameter range: 2.5-3.5 mm (most 3.0-3.3 mm)

### Implant Survival
- **5-year estimated cumulative survival rate: 92.25%** (weighted mean 96.75%, median 98.35%, 95% CI: 90.87-100%)
- Life table analysis: 95.85% at 120 months
- Range across studies: 81.7% to 100% at 5 years

### Marginal Bone Loss (MBL)
- **5-year estimated mean MBL: 1.40 mm** (weighted mean 1.40 mm, median 1.24 mm, 95% CI: 0.55-2.03 mm)
- Range across studies: 0 mm to 2.82 mm at 5 years

### Prosthesis Survival
- Reported in 4 studies: 100% in 3 studies, 1 study NR

### Biomechanical Features
- Limited data; some studies reported on prosthetic complications (screw loosening, fracture)

## Discussion

NDIs show high survival rates (92.25% at 5 years) for full-arch removable restorations in edentulous patients. MBL of 1.40 mm at 5 years is within acceptable limits. However, all included studies were on removable restorations; no data on fixed full-arch rehabilitations. Most studies were mandibular. High heterogeneity in implant systems, diameters, and study designs.

## Conclusions

Narrow-diameter implants (2.5-3.5 mm) represent a viable prosthetic alternative for completely edentulous patients, particularly for mandibular overdentures, with 5-year implant survival of 92.25% and MBL of 1.40 mm. Further research needed on fixed full-arch rehabilitations and maxillary applications.

## Key Points
- 9 studies, 885 implants, all removable restorations
- 5-year implant survival: 92.25% (95% CI 90.87-100%)
- 5-year MBL: 1.40 mm (95% CI 0.55-2.03 mm)
- No fixed full-arch data; predominantly mandibular
- High heterogeneity limits definitive conclusions
