## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Universiti Malaya, Spec Care Dentist 2025) of dental management for patients with common hematological disorders — anemia, hemophilia, von Willebrand disease, leukemia, and multiple myeloma — and their associated bleeding tendency.

Covers oral manifestations (delayed wound healing, mucosal ulcers, increased bleeding risk), diagnostic criteria for each disorder, the implications of concurrent anticoagulant and antiplatelet therapy on dental procedures, and the dentist–hematologist collaborative pathway.

Orientation and scoping material for risk assessment, not citable effect evidence: the review is qualitative and reports no incidence rates, effect sizes, or numeric hemostatic thresholds.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Consolidates five distinct hematological conditions — anemia, hemophilia, von Willebrand disease, leukemia, multiple myeloma — into a single dental-management frame organized around bleeding tendency rather than around the individual disease.
- Bridges disease-intrinsic coagulopathy and drug-induced coagulopathy: explicitly addresses how anticoagulant and antiplatelet therapy interacts with dental procedures in these patients, rather than treating them as separate problems.
- Frames the dentist–hematologist collaboration as a required pathway, not an optional consult, and points to diagnostic criteria as the shared language for it.

## 7. Glossary

- **Hemophilia**: X-linked inherited deficiency of clotting factor VIII (hemophilia A) or factor IX (hemophilia B), causing impaired secondary hemostasis and delayed, prolonged bleeding.
- **von Willebrand Disease (vWD)**: the most common inherited bleeding disorder; deficient or defective von Willebrand factor impairs platelet adhesion and destabilizes factor VIII — affects primary hemostasis.
- **Multiple Myeloma (MM)**: malignant plasma-cell proliferation; associated with anemia, bone lesions, and bleeding tendency via thrombocytopenia and paraprotein interference with coagulation.
- **Leukemia**: malignancy of white blood cell precursors; oral bleeding tendency arises chiefly from thrombocytopenia and marrow failure, compounded by chemotherapy-induced mucositis.
- **Anemia**: reduced hemoglobin/red-cell mass; oral signs include mucosal pallor, glossitis, and impaired wound healing.
- **Local hemostatic measures**: site-level hemostasis adjuncts (sutures, gelatin sponge, oxidized cellulose, tranexamic acid) used to control bleeding without altering systemic therapy.
- **Antiplatelet agent**: drug inhibiting platelet aggregation (e.g., aspirin, clopidogrel) — affects primary hemostasis.
- **Anticoagulant**: drug inhibiting the coagulation cascade (e.g., warfarin, direct oral anticoagulants, heparin) — affects secondary hemostasis.
