## Why Ingested
This systematic review is the first comprehensive assessment of patient-reported outcomes (PROMs) comparing immediate and early/conventional loading protocols for immediately placed single implants — a gap in the IIP evidence base that had focused on clinical outcomes (survival, MBL) rather than patient perspective. Directly informs [[immediate-implant/loading-protocol]] decisions from patient satisfaction and quality-of-life standpoint, and extends [[immediate-implant]] by explicitly synthesizing PROMs literature.

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review of 9 studies (1 RCT, multiple cohorts) comparing immediate vs early/conventional loading on PROMs in partially edentulous single-tooth IIP patients (up to May 2017).
Overall, patient satisfaction was high with little difference between loading protocols; qualitative evidence indicated positive impact on OHRQoL from immediate implant placement and loading.
Limited comparative PROM data prevents definitive protocol preference, but immediate loading appears well-accepted from patient perspective and warrants clinical consideration.

- **Institution**: University of Texas Health Science Center at San Antonio; University of Maryland

## 2. Key Contributions
- First SR specifically answering PICO question on PROMs comparing immediate vs early/conventional loading for Type 1 (immediately placed) implants
- Synthesis of heterogeneous PROM instruments (VAS, OHRQoL scales, satisfaction surveys) across studies with limited head-to-head comparative data
- Evidence that patient satisfaction was overall high regardless of loading protocol in single-tooth edentulous spaces
- Identification of paucity of comparative PROM data as limiting factor for evidence-based protocol preference

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review (PROSPERO #49604, registered Oct 2016)
- **Databases**: OVID MEDLINE, PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews (search up to May 2017)
- **Study inclusion**: RCTs, prospective/retrospective cohorts, case series (n ≥ 5) on immediate implant placement with any loading protocol
- **Population**: Partially edentulous patients (single-tooth gap)
- **Outcomes**: Patient-Reported Outcome Measures (PROMs) — quality of life (OHIP-14, OHRQoL), visual analog scales (VAS), patient satisfaction surveys
- **n**: 1,102 references screened; 9 studies included (3 allowed comparative data; 1 was RCT)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Heterogeneous data prevented meta-analysis
- Only 3 of 9 studies provided comparative data between immediate and early/conventional loading
- Across PROMs (VAS, satisfaction indices): little difference in patient outcomes between immediate and early/conventional loading protocols
- Patient satisfaction was uniformly high across both loading protocols
- Qualitative evidence: positive impact on OHRQoL following immediate implant placement and loading
- No formal synthesis of QoL scores due to heterogeneity of instruments used

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Limited number of comparative studies (only 3 of 9)
- Heterogeneity of PROMs instruments (different scales, timing, patient populations) prevented meta-analysis
- Majority of included studies were observational (case series, retrospective cohorts); only 1 RCT
- Lack of standardized PROM assessment across studies
- Reporting: most studies reported only descriptive statistics without formal effect sizes
- Recommendation: future immediate-loading RCTs should include standardized PROM instruments (e.g., OHIP-14, validated VAS) to enable better comparative synthesis

## 7. Glossary
- **PROM**: Patient-Reported Outcome Measure — direct report of patient's health status without clinician interpretation
- **OHRQoL**: Oral Health–Related Quality of Life — patient's perception of oral health impact on daily functioning/well-being
- **OHIP-14**: Oral Health Impact Profile–14, validated QoL instrument
- **VAS**: Visual Analog Scale — 0–10 scale for patient-reported symptom/satisfaction rating
- **Type 1 implant**: Immediately placed implant (extraction socket placement at time of tooth removal)
