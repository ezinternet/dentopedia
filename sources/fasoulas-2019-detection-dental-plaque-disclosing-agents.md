## Why Ingested

This paper supplies the population-level "where does plaque actually sit" evidence behind disclosing-agent-based oral-hygiene instruction — it maps biofilm detection sites by age, socioeconomic status (nationality proxy), BMI, and caries (DMFT) in 588 schoolchildren, extending the plaque-index methodology surveyed in [[periodontics/oral-hygiene-instruction/delia-2023-dental-biofilm-indices-narrative-review]]. It reinforces the wiki's held default that disclosing agents belong at the centre of plaque-control education ([[periodontics/oral-hygiene-instruction/choi-2017-dental-plaque-control-oral-hygiene-education-korea-sr]]) by showing which surfaces and subgroups such instruction should be targeted at.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study of 588 public-school children (295 boys / 293 girls, aged 4–18 y) in a rural area of northern Greece (school year 2017–2018) that mapped dental biofilm detection sites using chewable two-tone disclosing tablets.

Age/education level, socioeconomic status (nationality proxy), BMI, and caries (DMFT) were each significantly associated with where plaque was detected (e.g., preschool vs other grades: upper posterior palatal p < 0.0001; overweight/obese: upper posterior buccal p = 0.0014; severe caries DMFT 6–10: upper posterior palatal p < 0.0001, lower posterior buccal p < 0.0001), whereas sex was the only variable with no effect on any detection surface (all p > 0.05).

Disclosing agents localize plaque to specific oral surfaces and can therefore target oral-hygiene instruction and program evaluation in schoolchildren, but the convenience-sampled cross-sectional design cannot establish causality.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study (per the authors) to evaluate the association between dental biofilm *detection location* and sex, age, socioeconomic status, BMI, and oral health status (DMFT index, orthodontic disorder, periodontal disease) in schoolchildren aged 4–18.
- Produces a site-level map of biofilm detection across 11 tooth surfaces plus the tongue (upper/lower anterior labial and lingual; upper/lower posterior buccal, lingual, occlusal; tongue).
- Confirms the research hypothesis for age, socioeconomic status, BMI, and oral health, but rejects it for sex — sex was the only variable with no significant impact on any detection surface.
- Shows that tongue (dorsal) biofilm is independent of caries status: it was high even in caries-free children (91.5% at DMFT = 0) and did not differ across DMFT categories (p = 0.6018).
- Supports disclosing agents as an auxiliary tool in school oral-hygiene programs, both to guide use of hygiene tools and to evaluate interventions.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional observational study.
- **Setting**: 14 public schools, rural northern Greece, school year 2017–2018; 58 sessions covering preschool (2 grades), primary (6), middle (3), and high school (3); 58 visits.
- **Participants**: n = 588 (295 boys, 293 girls), aged 4–18 y. Children with learning disabilities and special healthcare needs were excluded.
- **Demographics**: sex, date of birth, school class, nationality provided by school directors; nationality used as the socioeconomic element (foreign children often from migrant families).
- **Anthropometry**: portable seca scale + height measure; BMI = weight/height² (kg/m²); classified with WHO child/adolescent BMI growth curves (underweight / normal / overweight / obese).
- **Oral health**: extraoral and intraoral examination (dentist); DMFT divided into no caries (DMFT = 0), moderate (DMFT = 1–5), severe (DMFT = 6–10); orthodontic disorder (OD) as absence/presence; periodontal diagnosis as absence/presence (presence = bleeding on probing with or without attachment loss). Exam kits: Carl Martin mirror, explorer, WHO perio-probe.
- **Biofilm detection**: two-tone disclosing agents (Mira-2-Ton tablets, Hager & Werken, Duisburg, Germany); children chewed the tablet 30–60 s; presence/absence of biofilm recorded *without* scoring quantity (convenience sampling). Thin/recent biofilm stains red, thicker/older biofilm stains blue.
- **Recording regions**: each jaw split into anterior (buccal, lingual) and posterior (buccal, lingual, occlusal) plus the tongue surface.
- **Statistics**: biofilm detection tabulated as multiple responses against demographic, body (BMI), and dental (DMFT) variables, plus orthodontic and periodontal effects; likelihood ratio chi-square to test whether each response rate differs across grouping levels; individual tests for observed vs expected; α = 0.05. Software: Minitab® 18.1 and JMP 13.2.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Baseline (whole sample): 44.0% had no caries, 44.8% had no periodontitis, 69.4% had no orthodontic disorder.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Convenience sample**: schoolchildren were recruited from the county/area where the researchers worked (selection bias).
- **Orthodontic diagnosis** based on extraoral/intraoral examination only, without panoramic or lateral cephalometric radiographs.
- **Periodontal diagnosis** recorded only as presence/absence, without further disease classification.
- **Biofilm recorded as presence/absence only** (no score/quantity), limiting severity analysis.
- **Cross-sectional/association design** — cannot establish causality, and the authors note several findings (foreign vs Greek sites, BMI sites, periodontal sites) had no comparable prior literature.
- Authors call for further investigation and public-health programs on proper oral hygiene to limit inequalities and ignorance.

## 6. Related Work

- **delia-2023** (dental biofilm indices narrative review): supplies the plaque-index/disclosing-agent taxonomy and lineage that this study applies at the population level.
- **choi-2017** (Korean SR of plaque control and oral hygiene education): concludes disclosing-agent-based instruction is more effective than verbal/model-based education; this paper adds *where* plaque sits by subgroup, complementing that effectiveness evidence.
- **choi-2013** (domestic-literature SR, same author lineage): predecessor reading of the disclosing-agent OHI evidence.
- **sooampon-2026** (erythrosine vs D&C Red No. 33 disclosure RCT): disclosing-agent formulation comparison; this paper reports the clinical use of a two-tone tablet (Mira-2-Ton).
- **guo-2026** (digital plaque-assessment SR+MA): shows disclosing agents raise correlation with reference methods (r 0.53 → 0.81), context for the continued role of chemical disclosure.

## 7. Glossary

- **DBP / biofilm**: dental bacterial plaque (dental biofilm) — a thin yellowish-white coating of microbial colonies in a self-produced extracellular matrix adhering to dental surfaces; the main cause of caries and periodontal disease.
- **Disclosing agent**: dye (iodine, gentian violet, erythrosine, basic fuchsin, fast green, food dyes, fluorescein, two-tone) that colours biofilm to make it visible; intensity reflects plaque thickness.
- **Two-tone disclosing agent**: tablet/solution that stains thin new plaque one colour (red) and thicker old plaque another (blue) — here Mira-2-Ton chewable tablets.
- **DMFT**: decayed, missing, and filled teeth index; categorised here as no caries (0), moderate (1–5), severe (6–10).
- **BMI**: body mass index (kg/m²), classified against WHO child/adolescent growth curves.
- **OD**: orthodontic disorder (absence/presence).
- **BOP**: bleeding on probing; used to define periodontal presence in this study.
- **Likelihood ratio chi-square**: test used to determine whether the rate of each biofilm-detection response differs across grouping levels (α = 0.05).
