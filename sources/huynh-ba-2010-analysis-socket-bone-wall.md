## Why Ingested

Ingested to close a real gap in this wiki's `immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment` folder, which until now held **only CBCT datasets** (root-position classifications, incisive-canal proximity, bone-thickness norms) and no *intraoperative, directly measured* socket-wall morphometry. This paper is the direct intraoperative counterpart: it measures the buccal and palatal bony walls at the moment of extraction in exactly the sites that are candidates for immediate implant placement (IIP), and it is the primary-study origin of the "≥2 mm buccal wall" criterion that the folder's narrative pages keep citing. It independently reproduces [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2013-ridge-alterations-post-extraction-esthetic]]'s 0.8 mm anterior buccal thickness from a different cohort using a different modality, which is why it is filed here rather than in the esthetic-soft-tissue folder that houses the Chappuis pages.

**Category routing note (boundary case, resolved by method).** Two sibling folders plausibly claimed this paper: `immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment` and `immediate-implant/gap-grafting`. Its conclusion ("in most clinical situations, augmentation procedures are needed") reads like a grafting paper, but **routing principle #3 in `wiki/_meta/categories.md` classifies by method/procedure, not by clinical conclusion**. The method here is bone-wall dimension measurement — a pure morphometry with **no grafting material, no membrane, no jumping-gap protocol, no augmentation technique and no implant outcome reported**. Filing it under `gap-grafting` would also gut that folder's scope, since *every* anterior-maxilla anatomy paper ends with "and then you will need augmentation." `anatomic-assessment` matches on all three of its defining features: buccal plate thickness, anterior maxilla, and an anatomy-only dataset used for IIP case selection. **Chosen: `immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment`.**

**Evidence-level note.** The paper is nested in "an ongoing prospective randomized-controlled multicenter clinical study," but *this paper* reports no randomized comparison, no control arm and no outcome — it is a single-timepoint cross-sectional measurement of 93 sites. Labelled `cross-sectional`, consistent with all 16 other anatomy-only pages in the folder.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional intraoperative morphometric study (n = 93 extraction sites, maxillary esthetic zone — canine-to-canine and premolars; recorded as part of an ongoing prospective randomized-controlled multicenter trial, Clin Oral Implants Res 2010) measuring buccal and palatal socket bone-wall width at extraction.

Mean buccal wall 1 mm vs palatal 1.2 mm (P<0.05); anterior (canine-to-canine) buccal mean 0.8 mm vs posterior/premolar 1.1 mm (P<0.05), with 87% of anterior buccal walls ≤1 mm and only 3% 2 mm wide, against 59% and 9% respectively in premolar sites.

If the ≥2 mm minimal-buccal-width criterion is valid, only a limited number of anterior maxillary sites meet it, so augmentation is needed in most cases — but the record is abstract-only: no instrument, no measurement level, no standard deviations, no implant outcome.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Provides the **intraoperative** counterpart to the CBCT bone-thickness datasets already held in this wiki: the buccal and palatal socket walls were *measured at extraction* in the very sites that are IIP candidates, rather than inferred from pre-operative scans of intact dentition.
- **Direct prevalence figures**, not just means: 87% of anterior (canine-to-canine) buccal walls were ≤1 mm and only 3% were 2 mm wide; the corresponding premolar figures were 59% and 9%. This converts "the buccal wall is thin on average" into a case-selection statement about how often a site fails a thickness threshold.
- Supplies an **anterior-vs-premolar gradient within the maxilla** (0.8 mm vs 1.1 mm buccal, P<0.05), i.e. the esthetic-zone risk is position-dependent rather than uniform across the arch.
- Frames the clinical conclusion conditionally and honestly ("*if* the criterion of a minimal buccal bone width of 2 mm … *is valid*"), treating the threshold as an assumption imported from prior animal/human work rather than something this study tested.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional intraoperative morphometric measurement, nested inside "an ongoing prospective randomized-controlled multicenter clinical study on immediate implant placement." This paper reports **no randomized comparison and no control arm** — the parent trial's design belongs to the parent trial's outcome papers, not to this one.
- **Setting/Sites**: Extraction sites in the **esthetic zone of the maxilla** — anterior teeth and premolars, analysed separately as *anterior (canine to canine)* and *posterior (premolar)* subgroups.
- **n**: **93 extraction sites**. Whether this equals 93 sites in 93 patients or fewer patients contributing multiple sites is **not stated in the abstract**; the anterior-vs-premolar subgroup n's are also not reported.
- **Intervention/Measurement**: Width of the buccal and palatal bony walls recorded at extraction. The instrument (caliper, periodontal probe, etc.), the reference level (crest, mid-root, or a fixed distance apical to the crest) and the number of levels measured are **not stated in the abstract**.
- **Outcomes**: Buccal and palatal bone-wall width (mean, plus the distribution of buccal width against 1 mm and 2 mm cut-points). **No implant outcome of any kind is reported** — the abstract stops at anatomy.
- **Databases**: N/A (primary clinical study; no search component).
- **Statistics**: The abstract attaches (P<0.05) to the buccal-vs-palatal mean difference and to the anterior-vs-posterior mean difference; exact p-values, tests used and dispersion measures are not given.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Parameter | Value | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Mean buccal bony wall width (all sites) | **1 mm** | vs palatal, P<0.05 |
| Mean palatal bony wall width (all sites) | **1.2 mm** | vs buccal, P<0.05 |
| Mean buccal width — anterior (canine to canine) | **0.8 mm** | vs posterior, P<0.05 |
| Mean buccal width — posterior (premolar) | **1.1 mm** | vs anterior, P<0.05 |
| Anterior buccal walls ≤1 mm | **87%** | — |
| Anterior buccal walls 2 mm wide | **3%** | — |
| Premolar buccal walls ≤1 mm | **59%** | — |
| Premolar buccal walls 2 mm wide | **9%** | — |

- The buccal wall is **thinner than the palatal wall** at the same sites (1 vs 1.2 mm), i.e. the defect-prone wall is the facial one.
- The buccal wall is **thinner anteriorly than in premolar sites** (0.8 vs 1.1 mm), so the "esthetic zone" is the higher-risk end of the same gradient.
- Reading the anterior distribution: ≤1 mm (87%) and exactly 2 mm (3%) leave **10% unaccounted for** in the abstract — the 1–2 mm band and anything >2 mm are not itemised.
- Background premise the authors accept: animal and human research has shown that immediate implant placement **fails to prevent** post-extraction socket dimensional change, and a minimal buccal width of **1–2 mm** has been *suggested* as necessary to maintain a stable vertical dimension of the alveolar crest. The paper tests the anatomy against that premise; it does not test the premise.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only record.** Full text not retrieved; instrument, measurement level, subgroup n's, dispersion and statistical detail are unrecoverable from what was ingested.
- **The 2 mm criterion is assumed, not tested.** The conclusion is explicitly conditional — the study shows how few anterior sites *would* satisfy the threshold, but cannot say whether 2 mm is in fact the right threshold.
- **Anatomy only, no outcome.** No implant survival, marginal bone level, buccal recession or esthetic score is reported, so the page cannot be used to argue that augmentation improves results — only that the anatomy usually leaves a gap that has to be managed.
- **No comparator group.** Sites were not split by whether they actually went on to receive immediate placement with or without augmentation, so the morphometry cannot be linked to outcome in this dataset.
- **Selection and geography not described.** The abstract does not state why the teeth were extracted, the patient age/sex distribution, or the participating countries — all of which shape buccal plate thickness.
- **Sites vs patients ambiguity** (93 sites; patient count unstated) means the effective sample size for any site-level comparison is uncertain.

## 6. Related Work

- **Reinforces** [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2013-ridge-alterations-post-extraction-esthetic]] — Chappuis/Buser's CBCT study reports a baseline central buccal thickness of 0.8 ± 0.5 mm; this multicenter intraoperative series arrives at 0.8 mm for the anterior region from a different cohort, a different modality and a different time point (at extraction rather than post-extraction). Independent data, same number.
- **Anatomy side of** [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2017-dimensional-bone-soft-tissue-post-extraction]] — that narrative review states the "facial bone wall <1 mm in 90% of anterior maxilla" figure and the ~2 mm facial-wall threshold that this paper tests site-by-site at extraction. Cited as context, **not** as independent confirmation: the review is downstream of the primary literature and cannot corroborate it.
- **Intraoperative complement to** [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/altarawneh-2018-bone-dimensions-anterior-maxilla-cbct]] — same anatomical question, opposite end of the workflow: AlTarawneh maps CBCT bone dimensions in *healthy* anterior maxillary dentition at several apicocoronal levels; this paper measures the walls intraoperatively in teeth actually being extracted for IIP.
- **Puts a prevalence number on the pooled estimate in** [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/shafizadeh-2021-alveolar-bone-thickness-healthy-sr]] — that SR+MA reports anterior buccal plates averaging ≤1 mm; this study quantifies how many sites sit at or below that level (87% anteriorly).
- **Same morphometric question, wider map in** [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/dossantos-2019-buccal-bone-root-inclination-alveolar-cbct]] — dos Santos correlates buccal plate thickness with root inclination across both jaws; this paper reports wall width alone and adds the anterior/premolar split.
- **Where the "augmentation is needed" conclusion lands procedurally** — the *action* implied by this paper's conclusion is the jumping-gap/GBR literature in `immediate-implant/gap-grafting` (e.g. [[immediate-implant/gap-grafting/sanz-2017-bone-graft-gap-immediate-implant-rct]]), but that is where the follow-on evidence lives, not where this method belongs.
- **Same author, unrelated question** — [[immediate-implant/loading-protocol/huynh-ba-2018-immediate-loading-vs-early-conventional]] is Huynh-Ba's 2018 systematic review on patient-reported outcomes of loading timing. Shared first author and the shared IIP context only; different study, different question, different evidence type. Not a version of this trial.

## 7. Glossary

- **Socket bone wall (bony wall)**: the residual alveolar bone plate bounding an extraction socket on each side — here the **buccal** (facial, cheek-facing) and **palatal** plates of a maxillary socket.
- **Buccal / palatal**: buccal = toward the cheek (facial/labial side of the arch); palatal = toward the palate (lingual side of the maxillary arch).
- **Esthetic zone**: the anterior maxillary segment visible during smiling — in this paper defined as anterior teeth and premolars, and split into *anterior* (canine to canine) and *posterior* (premolar) subgroups.
- **Immediate implant placement (IIP)**: insertion of an implant into the extraction socket at the time of tooth removal (ITI Type 1 / "Type 1" placement).
- **Augmentation**: a procedure (grafting, barrier membrane, block graft) intended to create or preserve bone contour around an implant where the native bone is deficient.
- **Morphometry**: measurement of the dimensions (size, thickness, width) of anatomical structures — here, a single-timepoint survey of socket wall thickness with no outcome follow-up.
- **P<0.05 as used here**: the abstract attaches this marker to the stated mean difference it follows (buccal vs palatal; anterior vs posterior). The exact tests and p-values are not reported in the abstract.
