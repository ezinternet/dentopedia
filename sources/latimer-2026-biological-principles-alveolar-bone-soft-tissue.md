## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Journal of Periodontal Research, 2026; Harvard Dental School with Cha Jae-Kook of Yonsei as co-author) synthesizing biological principles governing oral tissue regeneration across alveolar bone, gingiva, and periodontium, covering wound healing biology, regenerative biomaterials, defect classification, and emerging technologies.

The same 4-stage wound healing framework (hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, remodeling) applies to all three oral tissues but is uniquely modulated by masticatory mechanical forces, polymicrobial biofilm, and complex immunological dynamics; immunoengineering to shift macrophage polarization from pro-inflammatory M1 to pro-regenerative M2, along with bioprinted customized scaffolds and organ-on-chip disease modeling, are identified as next-generation therapeutic strategies.

Controlling the inflammatory phase through macrophage polarization (M1→M2) is the most actionable lever for improving oral regenerative outcomes; the 4-phase framework from Hämmerle 2014 remains the clinical foundation while emerging technologies address the oral-specific challenges that slow progression through these phases.

## One-line Summary
Review (2026, Harvard/Yonsei): oral tissue regeneration is governed by 4 sequential wound-healing stages (hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, remodeling) modulated by mechanical, microbiological, immunological, and biochemical factors in the oral microenvironment.

- Reviewed natural vs. synthetic matrices and biologic adjuncts in oral regenerative therapy
- Presented defect classification systems as treatment planning guides
- Outlined emerging technologies: bioprinted scaffolds, immunoengineering, organ-on-chip platforms

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Hemostasis**: initial platelet aggregation and fibrin matrix formation — primary scaffold for subsequent cell migration (직접적 가피 형성 단계)
- **Inflammation**: regulated by oral microbiome and immune cells; dysregulation causes chronic non-healing wounds
- **Proliferation**: fibroblast/keratinocyte activity, angiogenesis; driven by growth factors (PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF)
- **Remodeling**: collagen crosslinking and architecture restoration; most vulnerable to mechanical disruption in the oral cavity
- Natural regenerative capacity varies by tissue type and defect morphology
- Customized bioprinted scaffolds and immunoengineering identified as next-generation approaches

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Review article; no primary outcome data
- Emerging technologies (organoids, gene therapy) remain pre-clinical
- Long-term safety and cost-effectiveness not yet established

## 7. Glossary
- **Immunoengineering**: modulating immune response to shift from pro-inflammatory (M1) to pro-regenerative (M2) macrophage phenotype
- **Organ-on-chip**: microfluidic platform replicating periodontal tissue mechanics for drug/biology testing
- **Defect classification**: morphological categorization (e.g., 1-wall vs. 3-wall bony defect) to guide regenerative material selection
