## Why Ingested

The `drug/anticoagulants` hemostasis literature is dominated by synthetic agents (chitosan, TXA, Gelfoam); this is the first RCT in the wiki testing plant-derived tannin-based hemostatics (green tea tannin extract, methanolic extract, aqueous extract) against saline gauze in molar extractions, providing a natural-product datapoint for the hemostatic toolkit.

## One-line Summary

4-arm parallel RCT (n=64, healthy molar extractions) showing green tea tannin extract reduces hemostasis time from 61.56 min (saline gauze) to 5.62 min, with aqueous (8.44 min) and methanolic (7.50 min) extracts also dramatically superior (p<0.001).

## 2. Key Contributions

- First RCT in this wiki evaluating three distinct green tea extract formulations (tannin, methanolic, aqueous) as socket hemostatics after molar extraction
- All three extracts markedly superior to saline gauze control in hemostasis time (p<0.001); tannin extract fastest at 5.62 min
- Provides mechanistic context: tannins precipitate proteins, astringent effect on vessels, and polyphenols inhibit platelet aggregation inhibitors — coagulation cascade augmented
- Adverse events not detailed in abstract; patient population systemically healthy

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

*(Abstract-level detail only)*

- **Design**: Parallel-group RCT; 4 arms; n=64 (16 per arm); Pakistan
- **Population**: Healthy adults requiring molar extraction; systemically healthy; no anticoagulants or antiplatelets
- **Interventions**: G1 green tea tannin extract; G2 methanolic green tea extract; G3 aqueous green tea extract; G4 saline-soaked gauze (control)
- **Primary outcome**: hemostasis time in minutes (from extraction to cessation of bleeding)
- **Statistics**: significance p<0.001

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Group | Mean hemostasis time (min) |
|---|---|
| Tannin Extract (TE) | 5.62 |
| Methanolic Extract (ME) | 7.50 |
| Aqueous Extract (AE) | 8.44 |
| Saline Gauze (control) | 61.56 |

- All three extract groups significantly faster than control (p<0.001)
- Tannin extract fastest; difference between extract groups not stated in abstract
- No adverse events specifically mentioned in abstract

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only**: sample size, allocation concealment, blinding, exact formulation concentrations, and adverse events unavailable
- Healthy patients only — no anticoagulated or bleeding-disorder data
- Control arm (saline gauze only) not representative of clinical standard of care; standard dry gauze would be a better comparator
- 61.56 min control hemostasis time is unusually long for healthy patients — likely reflects minimal gauze pressure protocol rather than passive hemostasis
- No healing, pain, or dry socket outcomes reported
- Formulation standardisation (tannin % by weight, extraction method) not described in abstract

## 6. Related Work

- Patil 2025: 5-arm RCT in healthy patients comparing Botroclot, TXA, chitosan, adrenaline, gauze — places TE hemostasis speed in context
- Natural hemostatics: tannic acid from oak gall has established astringent/coagulant properties (historical usage)

## 7. Glossary

- **Tannin Extract (TE)**: polyphenolic compounds (gallotannins, catechins) precipitating proteins; astringent — vasoconstriction + fibrin precipitation
- **Catechin / EGCG (Epigallocatechin Gallate, 에피갈로카테킨 갈레이트)**: green tea polyphenol; antioxidant, platelet modulation
- **Aqueous Extract (AE)**: water-soluble green tea fraction; lower polyphenol concentration than methanolic
- **Methanolic Extract (ME)**: organic-solvent fraction; higher polyphenol/catechin yield than aqueous extraction
