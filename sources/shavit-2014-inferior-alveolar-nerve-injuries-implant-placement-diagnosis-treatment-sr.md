## Three-line Summary

This systematic review analyzed 6 studies (n=157 patients) to assess diagnostic timing and treatment outcomes for inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) injuries following dental implant placement, searching MEDLINE (PubMed) from December 2004 to March 2014.

Subjective neurosensory testing was the most commonly used diagnostic method (64% of cases); early diagnosis within 36 hours was achieved in 47% of cases, while 43% were diagnosed very late (>6 months), with full recovery observed in cases treated before 48 hours post-injury.

Early diagnosis and treatment (implant removal within 36 hours, corticosteroid therapy, canal decompression) are critical for favorable outcomes; however, severe injuries (neurotmesis) may not recover regardless of timing, and the review is limited by missing exact diagnosis times and small case numbers.

## 세줄요약

본 체계적문헌고찰은 하치조신경 (Inferior Alveolar Nerve, IAN) 손상 환자 157명을 포함한 6개 연구를 분석하였으며, 2004년 12월부터 2014년 3월까지 MEDLINE (PubMed)을 검색하여 임플란트 식립 후 진단 시점과 치료 결과를 평가하였다.

주관적 신경감각검사가 가장 흔히 사용된 진단 방법이었고 (64%), 손상 후 36시간 이내 조기 진단은 47%에서 이루어졌으며, 43%는 매우 늦게 (>6개월) 진단되었고, 손상 후 48시간 이내 치료한 경우 완전 회복이 관찰되었다.

조기 진단과 치료 (36시간 이내 임플란트 제거, 코르티코스테로이드, 하악관 감압술)는 좋은 예후에 필수적이나, 중증 손상 (신경단절, neurotmesis)은 시기에 관계없이 회복이 어려울 수 있으며, 본 연구는 정확한 진단 시점 누락과 적은 증례 수 등의 한계가 있다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Systematic review of 6 studies (157 patients) on inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) injury following dental implant placement
- Subjective neurosensory testing (light touch, sharp/blunt discrimination, two-point discrimination) was the most commonly used diagnostic method (64%)
- Early diagnosis within 36 hours achieved in 47% of cases; 43% diagnosed very late (>6 months)
- Full recovery observed when treatment initiated before 48 hours post-injury
- No clinician in reviewed studies used objective methods (masseter inhibitory reflex, somatosensory evoked potentials)
- Phentolamine mesylate proposed as reversal agent for local anesthesia to enable immediate postoperative sensory assessment
- Early implant removal (<36 hours) recommended if radiographic evidence of mandibular canal contact

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: Systematic review
- Databases: MEDLINE (PubMed)
- Search window: December 2004 to March 2014
- PRISMA compliance: Yes, according to PRISMA guidelines
- Inclusion criteria: Human studies, English language, observational studies and case series on IAN injury following implant placement
- Exclusion criteria: Literature reviews, animal studies, studies not mentioning diagnostic methods, neurosensory deficit not from implant, articles not describing diagnostic procedures, no IAN injury
- Included studies: 6 (n=157 patients)
- Bias assessment: Cochrane Collaboration's tool for assessing risk of bias
- Study types: Diagnostic methods (1), treatment outcomes (2), clinical presentation (2), risk factors/management guidelines (1)
- Outcomes assessed: Time to diagnosis, diagnostic methods used, treatment timing, treatment type, recovery outcomes
- Statistics: Descriptive analysis; SPSS and Excel used in some studies

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Primary outcome — Time to diagnosis:** 47% diagnosed within 36 hours; 43% diagnosed very late (>6 months); 8% diagnosed within 36-48 hours (early but not critical window)
- **Full recovery rate:** Cases treated before 48 hours showed full recovery except severe injuries
- **Subjective testing prevalence:** 64% of diagnostic methods used were subjective (Poort et al. 2009)
- **Recovery outcomes by timing:** <24 hours (2 patients) full recovery; <48 hours (1 patient) full recovery; <72 hours (1 patient) no recovery; >6 days (2 patients) no recovery; 6-12 months (13 patients) no recovery; >12 months (10 patients) no recovery
- **Treatment outcomes:** Kubilius et al. (2004): 65/68 patients full recovery with treatment within 2-48 hours; Juodzbalys et al. (2011): 5/16 full, 5/16 near-full, 6/16 incomplete recovery with 36-hour treatment
- **Severe injury no recovery:** 6 patients (Kubilius et al. 2004) treated >1 week post-injury with no recovery
- **Seddon classification:** Neurapraxia (mildest, best prognosis), axonotmesis, neurotmesis (most severe)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Limited quantity of cases analyzed (n=157)
- Exact time of diagnosis not mentioned in all cases
- Some articles reported patients referred to specialists (authors did not perform the surgery, therefore were not first to diagnose)
- Heterogeneity in diagnostic methods and outcome reporting across studies
- Limited use of objective diagnostic methods (none used in reviewed studies)
- Sex scission and low number of patients in some included studies
- Postoperative neurosensory examination by single examiner in some studies
- Operation not performed by authors in several included studies
- Future research needed on objective diagnostic methods (MIR, SEPs, blink reflex) and standardized early diagnosis protocols

## 6. Related Work

## 7. Glossary

- **IAN (Inferior Alveolar Nerve)**: 하치조신경. 하악골 내 하악관을 주행하는 삼차신경 제3지의 가지로, 하악 치아와 하순, 턱 부위의 감각을 담당
- **Neurapraxia**: 신경실행. 가장 경미한 신경 손상으로, 축삭의 연속성은 유지되며 예후가 가장 좋음
- **Axonotmesis**: 축삭단절. 축삭은 손상되었으나 신경초는 보존된 상태
- **Neurotmesis**: 신경단절. 가장 심각한 신경 손상으로 신경섬유와 신경초 모두 절단됨
- **MIR (Masseter Inhibitory Reflex)**: 저작근 억제 반사. 삼차신경 하악지의 기능 평가에 사용되는 신경생리학적 검사 (특이도 99%, 민감도 51%)
- **SEPs (Somatosensory Evoked Potentials)**: 체성감각 유발전위. 말초신경 자극 후 두피에서 기록되는 뇌의 전기적 활동
- **TPD (Two-Point Discrimination)**: 두점식별검사. 두 개의 날카로운 끝으로 피부를 자극하여 두 점을 식별할 수 있는 최소 거리를 측정하는 감각검사
- **Phentolamine mesylate**: 펜톨라민 메실레이트. 국소마취제의 혈관수축 효과를 역전시키는 약물로, 수술 직후 신경손상 평가를 가능하게 함
