## Why Ingested
치주 미세수술 (Periodontal Microsurgery)의 창상 치유를 혈류 관류 (Perfusion) 관점에서 재해석하는 가설 구축적 고찰 — 기존 [[overviews/periosteal-elevation-alveolar-blood-supply-overview]]의 판막 거상·재혈관화 종합에 최신 비침습적 관류 영상 (Non-invasive Perfusion Imaging)층을 추가하고, [[bone-regeneration/zanettabarbosa-1993-laser-doppler-flowmetry-of-blood]]의 관류 측정 개념을 레이저 스페클 대비 영상 (Laser Speckle Contrast Imaging, LSCI)·초음파 (Ultrasound, US)로 확장한다. LSCI/US의 미세수술 적용 근거가 없다는 공백을 명시적으로 문서화한다는 점에서 인제스트 가치가 있다.

## Three-line Summary
Hypothesis-building narrative review (two reviewers, Jan 2022–Apr 2025, five databases) evaluating non-invasive blood perfusion imaging for periodontal microsurgery; the literature search found no direct empirical evidence for LSCI/US in microsurgical periodontal wound healing.
LSCI (~10 μm spatial resolution, 113 fps, ~1 mm depth) and US (B-mode + color flow, 64 μm axial resolution) emerged as the leading complementary modalities; an illustrative grafted-ridge case showed mid-occlusal ~220 PU versus facial apical ~360 PU perfusion.
The authors hypothesize microsurgery improves healing by limiting surgical trauma and flap tension ( flap elevation can cut perfusion by 50% at 24 h ); large controlled human studies and updated diagnostic protocols are required.

## 세줄요약
가설 구축적 서술적 고찰 (Hypothesis-building Narrative Review; 검토자 2명, 2022-01~2025-04, 데이터베이스 5개) — 치주 미세수술 (Periodontal Microsurgery)의 비침습적 혈류 관류 영상 (Non-invasive Blood Perfusion Imaging) 평가; 미세수술 창상 치유에 LSCI/US를 적용한 직접 경험적 근거는 없음.
레이저 스페클 대비 영상 (Laser Speckle Contrast Imaging, LSCI; 공간해상도 ~10 μm, 113 fps, 깊이 ~1 mm)과 초음파 (Ultrasound, US; B-mode + 컬러플로우, 축해상도 64 μm)가 상보적 기기로 도출; 이식 치조제 증례에서 중앙 교합부 ~220 PU 대 협측 첨단부 (Apical, API) ~360 PU 관류 차이.
판막 거상 시 24시간 내 관류 50% 감소 — 미세수술은 외상·판막 장력을 줄여 관류를 보존한다는 가설; 대규모 대조 인체 연구와 진단 프로토콜 정립이 필요함.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First framework positioning LSCI and US as complementary, real-time chairside perfusion-imaging tools for periodontal/peri-implant microsurgery: LSCI for superficial projection perfusion, US for deeper cross-sectional anatomy + flow direction/velocity/volume.
- Documents the central evidence gap explicitly — the search returned no hits on LSCI or US for microsurgical periodontal wound-healing evaluation — then constructs a testable central hypothesis: microsurgery encourages early reperfusion by reducing surgical trauma and managing flap tension.
- Proposes a standardized ROI-based diagnostic framework, illustrated by a clinical case of a grafted edentulous ridge (maxillary site #8, 5 months post-augmentation) that spatially correlates US and LSCI perfusion.
- Integrates quantitative flap-tension thresholds into the perfusion–healing model (residual tension ≤0.1 N / 10 g desirable; 7-0 polypropylene tensile strength ~3.7 N / 370 g acts as an over-tightening safeguard).
- Reviews modality physics, advantages/limitations, and the periodontal literature for both techniques, and notes sex-related vascular reactivity differences (males: higher baseline flow, larger ischemic areas).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Hypothesis-building narrative review (explicitly chosen over systematic review; inherent bias acknowledged), conducted by two reviewers (A.R. and O.K.), plus one illustrative exploratory clinical case.
- **Databases**: NLM PubMed, Embase, EBSCOhost CINAHL, EBSCOhost Dentistry and Oral Sciences Source, Wiley Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials. Search window January 2022 to April 2025.
- **n**: No fixed study count reported (narrative synthesis). Underlying human cohorts include LSCI studies of n = 7, 21 and 31 healthy subjects; a US human pilot of n = 5 (CAF + CTG); a periapical-surgery cohort of n = 15; and mini-pig preclinical work (n = 8). One illustrative case: 30-year-old female, post-extraction ridge preservation.
- **Outcomes**: Gingival/soft-tissue perfusion (LSCI perfusion units, PU; US color-flow velocity/power and color pixel density CPDV/CPDP), reperfusion time, inflammation, wound-healing scores, soft-tissue thickness.
- **Inclusion / exclusion**: human or preclinical in vivo imaging quantifying blood circulation in oral/periodontal tissues; ex vivo, reviews/consensus reports, retrospective studies and non-English publications excluded.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **LSCI principle**: ~785 nm infrared laser scanned over a 2D ROI; ~10 μm spatial and 113 fps temporal resolution; ~1 mm superficial quantification (Table 1 lists penetration as ~100 μm — internal inconsistency in the paper); 1.8 × 2.9 cm field of view; 30 s recordings at 25 fps; arbitrary color-coded PU (blue–cyan low → green–yellow moderate → orange–red high).
- **US principle**: B-mode grayscale + color-flow overlay (velocity = red/blue by direction; power = single red hue proportional to moving blood). L30-8 linear array: 64 μm axial resolution, 12–24 MHz second harmonic, ~30 × 18 × 12 mm intraoral footprint.
- **LSCI human studies**: Fazekas 2018 (n=21) — resting flow higher apical to papillae; horizontal compression caused more coronal ischemia; post-occlusive hyperemia more pronounced and longer in males. Mikecs (n=31) — 30 s compressions, superior labial artery collateral plexuses, males with higher baseline flow and larger ischemic areas. Molnár 2018/2019 (n=7 each) — good test–retest reproducibility; elevated postoperative flow at palatal donor sites after connective-tissue harvest with delayed normalization; reperfusion time strongly correlated with healing scores. Palombo 2024 RCT — sutureless EGG harvesting gave faster hyperemia resolution (peak day 7) vs sutures, not statistically significant.
- **US human/preclinical**: pilot (n=5, CAF + CTG) — CV/CP rose at 1 week and 1 month, peaking 199.25% above baseline, then declined by 6 and 12 months. Tavelli 2025 RCT — tunneling (TUN) gave better interproximal perfusion and earlier graft vascularization than CAF, correlating with 12-month soft-tissue outcomes. Tikku — B-mode/color Doppler outperformed radiography for periapical lesion healing (n=15). Mini-pig compression study — significantly greater inflammatory perfusion increases in males (weeks 2, 4, 6, 8) than females (week 4 only).
- **Illustrative case**: both modalities showed lower perfusion at the mid-occlusal crest than facial/palatal-lingual ROIs (~220 PU mid-occlusal); on facial view crestal bone (CB) ~280 PU vs apical (API) ~360 PU — consistent with an apico-coronal vascular pattern.
- **Surgical determinants**: elevating a mucoperiosteal flap can reduce baseline perfusion by 50% at 24 h; longer vertical incisions relative to flap base width correlate with poorer revascularization; residual tension and surgical trauma are inter-related.
- **Central finding**: literature searches revealed no hits on LSCI or US for microsurgical periodontal wound-healing evaluation — the hypothesis remains theoretical.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Hypothesis-building (narrative) review carries inherent selection bias; no formal PRISMA/RoB/GRADE appraisal of the synthesized evidence; reviews, consensus reports and retrospective studies were excluded.
- No direct empirical evidence supports LSCI/US in periodontal microsurgery — large-scale controlled human studies and updated diagnostic protocols are essential before clinical adoption.
- LSCI limitations: arbitrary perfusion units, limited/shallow penetration (~1 mm; Table 1 states ~100 μm), large sensor (molars difficult to image), direct line-of-sight requirement, sensitivity to working distance, patient movement, gingival temperature and measurement angle on curved arches.
- US limitations: requires gel coupling medium, user dependency, sensitivity to probe pressure and scanning angulation, anatomical accessibility, and difficulty reproducing the exact location across time points without a guide.
- Confounding: site- and individual-specific supraperiosteal plexus distribution and sex-related vascular reactivity (males higher baseline flow, larger ischemic areas) reduce predictability.
- The clinical case is explicitly exploratory and not evidence of clinical effectiveness; systemic modifiers (smoking, diabetes) were excluded from the discussion.

## 6. Related Work
- zanettabarbosa-1993 (bone-regeneration): laser Doppler flowmetry of blood perfusion in mucoperiosteal flaps covering membranes — the closest modality-adjacent perfusion-measurement study; this review extends perfusion assessment from laser Doppler (animal, bone augmentation) to LSCI/US in human periodontal microsurgery.
- Periosteal elevation & alveolar blood supply overview (overviews): synthesis of flap elevation → vascular injury → revascularization; this review adds non-invasive perfusion imaging as a measurement/monitoring layer.
- Di Gianfilippo 2021 (ref 3): SR/MA reporting a 30% greater chance of complete root coverage with microsurgery vs macrosurgery — the outcome evidence underlying the microsurgery hypothesis.
- Tavelli 2025 (ref 26): Doppler ultrasonographic evaluation of revascularization after CTG at implant sites (TUN vs CAF RCT) — direct human US evidence cited for perfusion-guided soft-tissue outcomes.
- Rodriguez 2024 (ref 13, PLoS One): systematic review of oral wound-healing indices — the authors' prior work on healing assessment that this imaging framework aims to complement.

## 7. Glossary
- **LSCI (Laser Speckle Contrast Imaging)**: non-contact optical technique estimating superficial blood flow (~785 nm infrared laser) from motion-blurred speckle patterns; outputs arbitrary perfusion units (PU).
- **US (Ultrasound)**: non-ionizing, cross-sectional chairside imaging using acoustic wave reflection/scattering; B-mode for anatomy, color-flow velocity/power for perfusion.
- **PU (Perfusion Units)**: arbitrary, color-coded relative units of LSCI-measured blood flow (blue–cyan low; orange–red high).
- **CPDV / CPDP (Color Pixel Density–Velocity / –Power)**: US color-flow quantification metrics computing the density of velocity- or power-weighted flow pixels.
- **B-mode (Brightness Mode)**: grayscale US anatomical image; hypoechoic = soft tissue, hyperechoic = hard tissue.
- **OM (Operating Microscope)**: magnification + coaxial illumination platform enabling minimally invasive microsurgical flap handling.
- **Flap tension**: residual mechanical tension in a reflected flap; correlated with soft-tissue hypoxia/ischemia and a driver of additional releasing incisions.
- **Primary wound closure**: tension-free edge approximation essential for predictable periodontal regeneration.
- **EGG / CTG / CAF / TUN**: epithelialized gingival graft / connective-tissue graft / coronally advanced flap / tunneling technique.
- **ROI (Region of Interest)**: standardized area (mid-occlusal, facial, palatal/lingual, crestal bone, apical) used to correlate US and LSCI perfusion.
