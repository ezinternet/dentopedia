## Why Ingested

Non-healing extraction sockets are a clinical scenario where the differential diagnosis extends far beyond routine alveolar osteitis (dry socket). This paper fills a critical gap in the wiki: a systematic case-series review specifically mapping which systemic diseases and malignancies underlie persistent socket non-healing, with a diagnostic framework for general practitioners. The wiki's oral-surgery holdings focus primarily on implant site preparation, bone augmentation, and immediate implant protocols; this paper adds the index-suspicion layer needed when a socket simply refuses to heal.

The findings are directly relevant to clinical triage: distinguishing a benign delayed healing from a socket harboring undiagnosed primary or metastatic malignancy or medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ) determines whether the clinician proceeds with conservative management or urgently refers. No existing oral-surgery page in the wiki addresses this differential-diagnosis workflow.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review / case-series synthesis of 50 reported non-healing extraction socket cases (28F, 22M; mean age 55.2 years; 71 teeth) identified across five major databases.

Malignancy (primary and metastatic) was the most frequent definitive diagnosis, followed by medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw; both categories require urgent escalation rather than routine socket management.

Clinicians should maintain a low threshold for biopsy and systemic investigation when an extraction socket fails to show healing signs after a reasonable interval, as the socket may be the first presentation of an undiagnosed serious disease.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Provides the first focused case-series synthesis mapping specific systemic diagnoses behind non-healing extraction sockets
- Quantifies the diagnostic distribution: malignancy (primary + metastatic) ranks first; MRONJ ranks second
- Establishes that non-healing socket is a potential herald sign of undiagnosed systemic disease or malignancy
- Offers a differential diagnosis framework applicable in general dental practice (UK context: >18 million extractions/year)
- Highlights that the average patient age of 55.2 years overlaps with peak malignancy incidence, reinforcing vigilance in older patients

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Study type**: Narrative review / case-series review
- **Databases searched**: PubMed, Embase, Scopus, ProQuest, Google Scholar
- **Search term**: "non-healing extraction socket"
- **Inclusion filters**: human subjects, English language
- **Cases identified**: 50 non-healing extraction socket cases; 71 extracted teeth
- **Demographics**: 28 women, 22 men; mean age 55.2 years
- **Limitations**: case-series synthesis (no control group, no incidence data, publication bias likely); full-text unavailable for quantitative breakdown of individual diagnoses

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Most common diagnosis**: Malignancy (primary and metastatic)
- **Second most common**: Medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ)
- Other serious systemic disorders accounted for remaining cases
- Non-healing sockets may present weeks to months after extraction without obvious cause
- Clinical implication: routine management of a "slow-healing" socket is inappropriate when healing fails entirely — biopsy and systemic workup are indicated
