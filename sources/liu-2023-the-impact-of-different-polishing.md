## Why Ingested

This paper quantifies heat generation during intraoral polishing of Y-TZP crowns at clinically relevant speeds, directly testing the pulp damage threshold. It is the first study to use a water-circulation model simulating intrapulpal blood flow for this question, providing stronger validity than prior room-temperature in vitro designs. It supports evidence-based speed selection for chairside occlusal adjustment of zirconia restorations.

→ [[dental-materials/zirconia/liu-2023-the-impact-of-different-polishing]]

## Three-line Summary

Polishing Y-TZP (3M Lava Esthetic) crowns with Meisinger coarse or fine polishers at 20,000 RPM consistently exceeded the 5.5°C pulpal damage threshold (≥8°C rise in 2 min) in a novel water-circulation in vitro model. At 10,000 RPM, heat generation was significantly lower (p < .0001) and stayed below threshold for most groups. Clinicians should avoid 20,000 RPM intraoral polishing of zirconia and prefer low-speed, intermittent protocols.

## 세줄요약

Y-TZP 크라운을 20,000 RPM으로 연마 시 치수 손상 역치(5.5°C 상승)를 초과(2분 내 ≥8°C 상승)하는 발열이 발생했다. 10,000 RPM에서는 대부분 역치 이하로 유의하게 낮았다(p < .0001). 임상적으로 고속 구내 연마를 피하고 저속·간헐 연마 프로토콜을 권장한다.

## Document Information

- **Study type**: In vitro
- **Material tested**: Y-TZP (3M™ Lava™ Esthetic, ZrO₂ + 5 mol% Y₂O₃, 800 MPa flexural strength)
- **Polishers**: Meisinger coarse (DCA04 204 040) and fine (DCA10 204 040) diamond polishers — Ivoclar Vivadent 2-step zirconia system
- **Polishing speeds**: 10,000, 15,000, 20,000 RPM (n = 10 cusps/group; total n = 120)
- **Model**: Two plastic mandibular first molars with different reduction depths (Tooth 1: 1.0–1.5 mm; Tooth 2: <1.0 mm); water-circulation system (37°C) simulating intrapulpal temperature and blood flow
- **Institution**: University of Otago / University of Auckland (NZ)
- **DOI**: 10.1002/cre2.778

## Key Contributions

1. First study simulating intra-pulpal blood flow (via sous vide-heated water pump) during Y-TZP polishing — improves ecological validity over static-water or room-temperature designs.
2. Demonstrated that 20,000 RPM (the manufacturer's maximum recommended speed) produces clinically dangerous levels of heat (≥8°C rise) for both coarse and fine polishers.
3. SEM analysis showed coarse polishers more effectively removed grinding-induced surface grooves; fine polishers less effective even at higher speeds.
4. Crown thickness differences (1.0–1.5 mm vs <1.0 mm) influenced heat transfer at 10,000 and 15,000 RPM but not at 20,000 RPM.

## Methodology

- **Specimen preparation**: 24 CAD/CAM-milled Y-TZP crowns (Ceramill Motion 2), sintered and glazed (IPS Ivocolor / Programat P310)
- **Pre-polishing grinding**: Diamond bur at 30,000 RPM for 30 s (no water coolant) to simulate intraoral adjustment
- **Polishing protocol**: 2 min at each speed without coolant; manufacturer-recommended time is 60 s
- **Temperature measurement**: K-type thermocouple wire on intaglio surface of crown; EasyLog USB data logger; heat generation = max temp − initial temp
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk + Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality tests; Kruskal-Wallis test with multiple comparisons; p < .05 significant (Prism 9)
- **SEM**: Zeiss Sigma 300VP FESEM at 5 kV; 15 nm Au-Pd coating

## Key Results

| Group | Tooth 1 ΔT (2 min) | Tooth 2 ΔT (2 min) |
|---|---|---|
| Coarse 10k RPM | 3.50°C | 5.10°C |
| Coarse 15k RPM | 6.90°C | 4.50°C |
| Coarse 20k RPM | **8.40°C** | **8.00°C** |
| Fine 10k RPM | 5.71°C | 5.35°C |
| Fine 15k RPM | 6.35°C | 4.40°C |
| Fine 20k RPM | **8.45°C** | **8.25°C** |

- Pulp damage threshold: 5.5°C (Zach & Cohen 1965)
- 20,000 RPM exceeded threshold within first 60 s for both crowns (ΔT ≥ 6°C at 60 s)
- Significant differences between 10,000 and 20,000 RPM: p < .0001 for all comparisons
- No significant difference between coarse vs fine at 20,000 RPM for either crown (p > .99 between crowns)
- Fine polishing at 10k/15k RPM: difference between two crown designs (p < .0001 or p = .0138)
- SEM: Coarse polisher particles ~20–50 μm; fine ~5–10 μm; higher speed caused more polisher tip wear

## Limitations

- In vitro only (plastic typodont teeth, not real teeth with enamel/dentin/cement)
- Cement layer, remaining enamel thickness, humidity variation not controlled
- Only one polisher brand (Meisinger) and one zirconia brand (3M Lava Esthetic)
- Applied force not standardized beyond clamp-height control
- 2 min exceeds manufacturer recommendation; clinical relevance of extended duration is interpretive

## Glossary

- **Y-TZP**: Yttria-stabilized Tetragonal Zirconia Polycrystal — dental zirconia stabilized with ~5 mol% Y₂O₃
- **Pulp damage threshold**: 5.5°C rise above baseline body temp (37°C) → pulpal inflammation/necrosis risk (Zach & Cohen 1965)
- **Phase transformation (zirconia)**: Tetragonal → monoclinic transformation triggered by heat >350°C or mechanical stress; weakens material
- **CAD/CAM**: Computer-Aided Design / Computer-Aided Manufacturing — milling workflow for ceramic restorations
- **SEM**: Scanning Electron Microscopy
