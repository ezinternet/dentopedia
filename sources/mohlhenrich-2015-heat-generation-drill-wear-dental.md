## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (English-language MEDLINE/PubMed studies, Jan 2000–Feb 2014; 41 articles found, 27 on temperature increase during implant site preparation) of heat generation and drill wear during dental implant osteotomy preparation.

Measured temperatures ranged 28.4–64.4°C across studies (thermocouples in 18, infrared thermography in 7, immunohistochemistry for cell viability in 3); drill wear after preparing 50 sites produced a significant temperature increase and a small shift in bone-cell protein physiological balance — nearly all studies were in vitro, in non-vital bovine/porcine bone, at low evidence level.

Heterogeneous study designs meant meta-analysis was not appropriate; the authors recommend standardizing future work on axial load 2 kg, drilling speed 1500 rpm, irrigation, standard artificial bone blocks, and infrared thermography — this abstract-only ingest carries no PMC full text (see Limitations).

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Establishes the empirical temperature range this wiki's whole osteotomy-thermal cluster cites against**: 28.4–64.4°C across 27 heat-focused studies, measured by thermocouple (18 studies) or infrared thermography (7 studies).
- **First systematic mapping of measurement methodology** in this literature — thermocouple vs infrared thermography vs immunohistochemical cell-viability assays (3 studies) — a methodological taxonomy later SRs (chauhan 2018, chakraborty 2024, saxena 2024) implicitly build on.
- **Quantifies the drill-wear effect at a literature level**: after preparation of 50 sites, drill wear produced a significant temperature increase alongside a small shift in the physiological protein balance of bone cells — an early, literature-level anchor for the more specific single-study wear findings later held ([[wiki/implants/osteotomy-thermal/sorgato-2025-drill-bit-wear-temperature]]).
- **Names the field's central methodological problem early**: study-design heterogeneity across the 41→27 screened articles made meta-analysis inappropriate — a limitation every later SR in this folder (chauhan 2018, chakraborty 2024) independently re-confirms.
- **Proposes a standardization protocol** (2 kg axial load, 1500 rpm drilling speed, irrigation, standard artificial bone blocks, infrared thermography) intended to make future studies poolable — a benchmark against which later methodology can be checked.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Systematic review (no meta-analysis).
- **Databases**: MEDLINE/PubMed only.
- **Search window**: January 2000 – February 2014, English-language studies only.
- **Screening**: 41 articles identified → 27 related to temperature increase during implant site preparation were the primary analytic set (drill wear reported separately, from 50 prepared sites across the included literature).
- **Outcome measures across included studies**: real-time temperature (thermocouple, n=18 studies; infrared thermography, n=7 studies); immediate cell viability via immunohistochemistry (n=3 studies).
- **Evidence appraisal**: authors characterize the included body of evidence as basic research at a low level of evidence, predominantly in vitro, with osteotomies usually prepared in non-vital bovine or porcine bone rather than living human bone.
- **NOT recoverable from the abstract**: individual study citations, per-study n or exact protocols, formal risk-of-bias scoring instrument (if any), and any quantitative pooled estimate (none was computed).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only ingest.** No PMC copy exists; per-study data, the search strategy's exact string, and any risk-of-bias appraisal tool are unavailable to this wiki. Obtaining the full text would materially upgrade this page.
- **Low level of evidence throughout the included body.** The authors themselves characterize the underlying literature as basic research at a low evidence level — this SR summarizes in vitro/bench data, not clinical outcome data.
- **Non-vital animal bone, not living human bone.** Most included studies used non-vital bovine or porcine osteotomy models, which do not capture perfusion, vascularity, or in vivo cooling present in a living surgical site.
- **No meta-analysis possible.** Heterogeneous study designs across the 27 temperature studies prevented pooling — the review is narrative/qualitative only.
- **Pre-2014 literature cutoff.** The February 2014 search cutoff means osseodensification, contemporary piezosurgery protocols, and modern guided-sleeve drilling systems studied in later SRs (chakraborty 2024, saxena 2024, jain 2024) are not reflected here.
- **Future work (author-stated)**: standardize study design around axial load 2 kg, drilling speed 1500 rpm, irrigation, standard artificial bone blocks, and infrared thermography to make future studies comparable/poolable.

## 7. Glossary

- **Osteotomy (골절개, 임플란트 식립부 준비)**: the surgical drilling procedure that prepares a socket in bone for implant placement; the heat source under study.
- **Thermocouple**: a temperature sensor using the junction of two dissimilar metals to generate a voltage proportional to temperature difference; the most common real-time intraosseous heat-measurement method in this literature (18/27 studies here).
- **Infrared thermography**: non-contact temperature measurement via infrared radiation imaging of the bone surface during drilling; used in 7/27 included studies.
- **Immunohistochemistry (IHC)**: antibody-based staining technique; used here in 3 studies to assess immediate bone-cell viability after drilling-induced heat exposure.
- **Non-vital bone (bovine/porcine)**: bone harvested from a deceased animal, used as an ex vivo/in vitro osteotomy model; lacks the perfusion and living cellular response of an in vivo surgical site.
- **Drill wear**: progressive dulling/degradation of a drill's cutting edges with repeated use, associated in this review with increased frictional heat generation.
