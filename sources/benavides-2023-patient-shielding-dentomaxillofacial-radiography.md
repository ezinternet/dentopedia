## Three-line Summary

AAOMR consensus position statement (2023) reviewing dosimetric and epidemiological evidence for patient contact shielding (gonadal, pelvic, fetal, and thyroid) across all standard dentomaxillofacial radiographic modalities (intraoral, panoramic, cephalometric, CBCT).

Recommends discontinuing gonadal/pelvic/fetal shielding (no radiation-induced heritable effects in humans; negligible gonad/fetus dose) and thyroid shielding (negligible thyroid cancer risk; collars can degrade images and necessitate retakes) for all dentomaxillofacial radiography.

Routine contact patient shielding is no longer justified for any standard dental radiographic exam; clinicians should prioritize ALARA through beam collimation and technique selection rather than lead aprons, and regulators should update outdated mandates.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Recommends discontinuing gonadal, pelvic, and fetal shielding for all dentomaxillofacial radiography (no radiation-induced heritable effects in humans; negligible gonad/fetus dose).
- Recommends NOT using thyroid shielding for intraoral, panoramic, cephalometric, and CBCT imaging (negligible thyroid cancer risk; collars can degrade images / require retakes).
- Calls for state/local regulators to update rules accordingly.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Ad hoc committee review of radiation-protection monographs and studies reporting gonad, breast, and thyroid doses from dentomaxillofacial imaging.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

On contemporaneous-dose evidence, contact shielding provides negligible benefit and can interfere with diagnosis; unequivocal guidance to stop routine patient shielding.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Position statement (consensus, not graded SR); US-centric regulatory framing; does not override local law where shielding is still mandated.
