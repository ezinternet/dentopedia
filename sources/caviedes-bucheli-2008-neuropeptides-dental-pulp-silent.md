## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (J Endod 2008; abstract-only access) synthesizing evidence on five principal dental pulp neuropeptides — Substance P (SP), Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide (CGRP), Neurokinin A (NKA), Neuropeptide Y (NPY), and Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide (VIP) — covering pulp physiology, release during dental procedures, neuroplasticity, receptor pharmacology, and therapeutic targets.

These neuropeptides govern normal pulp homeostasis and vascular regulation; their release during operative stimuli and pulpal injury initiates neurogenic inflammation that can cascade to pulp necrosis and periapical lesion expansion; receptor antagonists (NK1, NK2, CGRP-R) are proposed as pharmacologic targets for controlling pulpal and periapical inflammation.

This 2008 foundational review established the neuropeptide framework that the same group extended to the endogenous opioid system (2025) and NPY specifically (2026); a key limitation is the non-systematic narrative design with literature coverage through approximately 2007.

## Paper Type

Narrative review — comprehensive synthesis of neuropeptide biology in dental pulp, covering physiology, release during dental procedures, neuroplasticity, receptor pharmacology, and therapeutic targets.

## Key Findings (from abstract)

1. **Five core neuropeptides reviewed**: Substance P (SP), Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide (CGRP), Neurokinin A (NKA), Neuropeptide Y (NPY), and Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide (VIP) in dental pulp.
2. **Pulp physiology role**: Neuropeptides participate in normal pulp homeostasis, vascular regulation, and immune modulation.
3. **Release during dental procedures**: Neuropeptides are released by pulp sensory nerves in response to operative stimuli, caries, and pulpal injury.
4. **Neuroplasticity in extensive injury**: Pulp/dentin injury induces neuropeptide plasticity — altered expression levels and distribution.
5. **Receptor-mediated pulp/immune cell interactions**: Neuropeptides act via specific receptors on pulp cells and immune cells to modulate inflammation.
6. **Neurogenic inflammation → pulp necrosis**: The cascade of neuropeptide-driven neurogenic inflammation can culminate in pulp necrosis.
7. **Periapical lesion growth**: Centrally and peripherally released neuropeptides contribute to periapical lesion development and expansion.
8. **Central neuropeptides in dental pain**: Discusses centrally released neuropeptides as mediators of dental pain perception.
9. **Pharmacologic targets**: Receptor antagonists (NK1, NK2, CGRP receptor antagonists) proposed as potential analgesic/anti-inflammatory therapies.

## Methods

Narrative literature review — no systematic search protocol or PRISMA reported. Synthesizes experimental and clinical evidence available through ~2007.

## Key Definitions / Terminology

- **SP (Substance P, 물질 P)**: Tachykinin neuropeptide; primary mediator of neurogenic inflammation and pain in dental pulp.
- **CGRP (Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide, 칼시토닌 유전자 관련 펩타이드)**: Potent vasodilator co-released with SP; marker of pulpal nerve fiber density.
- **NKA (Neurokinin A, 뉴로키닌 A)**: Tachykinin co-stored with SP; acts on NK2 receptors.
- **NPY (Neuropeptide Y, 뉴로펩타이드 Y)**: Sympathetic co-transmitter with vasoconstricting and anti-inflammatory properties.
- **VIP (Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide, 혈관작용장폴리펩타이드)**: Parasympathetic neuropeptide with vasodilatory and immunomodulatory effects.
- **Neurogenic inflammation (신경성 염증)**: Inflammation initiated by sensory nerve activation and neuropeptide release, independent of immune cell recruitment.

## Limitations

- Abstract-only access; full methodology, figure data, and detailed clinical recommendations not available.
- Narrative (non-systematic) review — subject to selection bias.
- 2008 publication; some receptor pharmacology and plasticity mechanisms have since been refined.
