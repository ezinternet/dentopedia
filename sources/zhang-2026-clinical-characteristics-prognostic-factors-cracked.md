## Why Ingested

Large retrospective cohort (n=561) providing quantitative data on crack direction–failure associations, two critical pulpitis progression windows, and independent prognostic factors for early-stage cracked teeth. Extends [[cracked-tooth/zhang-2024-cracked-teeth-treatment-outcomes-sr-ma]] and [[cracked-tooth/dong-2026-nomogram-cracked-tooth-rct-5year-survival]] with real-world clinical epidemiology.

## One-line Summary (English)

Retrospective cohort of 561 cracked teeth identifies distal marginal ridge involvement and composite resin restoration as key failure predictors, with two critical windows (0–1 month, 3–6 months) for pulpitis progression.

## 1. Document Information

- **Type**: Retrospective cohort study
- **Journal**: Journal of Dentistry, Vol 169, p106659
- **Date**: 2026-03-25
- **Institution**: Taihe Hospital
- **Study period**: June 1, 2021 – December 31, 2024
- **Sample**: 561 patients with cracked teeth; subgroup of 67 early-stage cases

## 2. Key Contributions

- Establishes two critical periods for pulpitis progression: **0–1 month** (48.97% of cases) and **3–6 months** (27.84%), indicating the urgency of early intervention.
- Identifies **distal marginal ridge** involvement (39.45% of cracks) as the location with the highest failure rate (16.81%, χ²=16.285, P<0.05).
- Full-coverage crown yields significantly better 1-year success (78.05%) than composite resin (50.00%) for early-stage cracked teeth (χ²=5.676, P<0.05).
- Multivariable logistic regression confirms two independent protective factors: full-coverage crown (OR=5.57, 95%CI=1.44–21.56) and normal pulp status (OR=4.52, 95%CI=1.23–16.69).
- Provides epidemiological profile: 40–49-year age group most affected (29.59%); maxillary first molars most commonly involved (37.01%); occlusal discomfort predominant symptom (72.73%).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort, single-center (Taihe Hospital)
- **Enrollment**: 561 patients diagnosed with cracked teeth, June 2021–December 2024
- **Variables collected**: Age, sex, tooth position, crack direction, symptom duration, treatment modality
- **Primary outcome**: 1-year treatment success (normal occlusal function, absence of symptoms, no tooth mobility)
- **Subgroup analysis**: 67 early-stage cracked teeth treated with composite resin or full-coverage crowns
- **Statistical methods**: Chi-square test, Fisher's exact test (bivariate); multivariable logistic regression (prognostic factors)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single-center retrospective design limits generalizability
- Subgroup for prognostic analysis limited to 67 patients — wider multicenter RCT needed
- 1-year follow-up is relatively short; longer-term outcomes unknown
- No blinding in outcome assessment inherent to retrospective design
- Treatment allocation not randomized (clinician/patient choice)

## 7. Glossary

- **Cracked tooth syndrome**: A tooth hard tissue injury with cracks that may involve pulp and/or periodontal structures, presenting with occlusal discomfort and/or thermal sensitivity
- **Distal marginal ridge**: The posterior ridge of the occlusal surface; involvement associated with higher failure rates in this study
- **Full-coverage crown**: A restoration covering all tooth surfaces, providing circumferential protection against crack propagation
- **Pulpitis progression window**: A critical time period when untreated cracked teeth are most likely to develop pulpal inflammation
- **OR (Odds Ratio)**: Multivariable logistic regression estimate of the association between a predictor and the outcome (treatment success)
