## Why Ingested

This SR+MA pools 6 retrospective studies (233 adults) on Invisalign's transverse-expansion predictability by tooth region, complementing [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/fonseca-planells-2026-clear-aligner-maxillary-expansion-growing]] (which compares aligners to conventional expanders in *growing* patients) by covering the *adult* population and a different outcome definition — achieved-vs-ClinCheck-planned percentage rather than achieved-vs-conventional-expander difference. It also sits alongside [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/de-la-rosa-gay-2025-expansion-predictability-clear-aligner]], a single-clinic adult cohort on the same question, and the two disagree on which tooth region is most reliable (see Related Work below) — a useful tension for a future clear-aligner-expansion overview.

## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42024584778, 5 databases, Jan 2014–Aug 2024; 6 retrospective studies, 233 adults treated with Invisalign for maxillary transverse expansion, achieved vs ClinCheck-predicted expansion by tooth region).

Pooled predictability — canine 72.01% (95% CI 59.67–84.35%, I²=74.7%), 1st premolar 80.73% (73.78–87.68%, I²=43.5%), 2nd premolar 78.74% (70.41–87.08%, I²=57.4%), 1st molar 71.57% (62.41–80.73%, I²=50.2%); developed- vs developing-country subgroup 77.22% vs 58.53% (p=.015, exploratory).

Achieved expansion is consistently less than programmed across all regions (~0.72–0.81 mm per programmed mm), so some overcorrection may be warranted, but authors decline to prescribe a universal number given only 6 retrospective studies and substantial canine-region heterogeneity/low-certainty evidence.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First SR+MA to pool Invisalign maxillary transverse-expansion *predictability* (achieved/predicted ratio) specifically by anatomical tooth region (canine, 1st premolar, 2nd premolar, 1st molar) in adults.
- Quantifies that predictability is not uniform across the arch: highest in the premolar regions, lowest and most heterogeneous in the canine region.
- Exploratory subgroup analyses (country classification, measurement landmark, measurement software) surface candidate — but not confirmed — sources of the substantial canine-region heterogeneity.
- GRADE certainty rated per region (low for canine, high for 1st premolar, moderate for 2nd premolar and 1st molar), making explicit that the headline SR+MA label does not mean uniformly strong evidence.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review + random-effects (DerSimonian–Laird) meta-analysis; protocol registered PROSPERO CRD42024584778.
- **Databases**: PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Library; search window January 1, 2014 – August 1, 2024.
- **n**: 269 records → 126 after dedup → 15 after title/abstract screening → 6 studies included after full-text review (233 patients total; 117 F / 59 M among studies reporting sex).
- **Included study design**: all 6 included studies were retrospective (Houle 2017 Australia n=64; Zhao 2017 China n=31; Zhou 2020 China n=20; Lione 2021 Italy n=28; Bowman 2023 Australia n=33; Tien 2023 Australia n=57).
- **Outcomes**: percentage of achieved maxillary transverse expansion relative to ClinCheck-predicted expansion, at intercanine, 1st interpremolar, 2nd interpremolar, and 1st intermolar widths.
- **Risk of bias**: ROBINS-I (all studies non-randomized) — 2 studies overall low risk, 4 overall moderate risk (mainly confounding, participant selection, outcome measurement, and selective reporting concerns).
- **Certainty of evidence**: GRADE, per-outcome (see Results).
- **Heterogeneity**: Cochran Q + I², thresholds ~25/50/75% = low/moderate/substantial. Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis; Egger regression as an exploratory small-study-effect check (funnel plots not used, <10 studies per outcome).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Pooled predictability (random-effects)**:
  - Canine: 72.01% (95% CI 59.67–84.35%); I²=74.7% (p=.001) — substantial heterogeneity.
  - 1st premolar: 80.73% (95% CI 73.78–87.68%); I²=43.5% (p=.115) — low-to-moderate heterogeneity.
  - 2nd premolar: 78.74% (95% CI 70.41–87.08%); I²=57.4% (p=.039) — moderate heterogeneity.
  - 1st molar: 71.57% (95% CI 62.41–80.73%); I²=50.2% (p=.074) — moderate heterogeneity.
- **Subgroup — country (1st molar only)**: developed countries 77.22% (95% CI 70.34–84.10%, I²=0.0%) vs developing countries 58.53% (95% CI 45.20–71.86%, I²=32.0%); between-subgroup p=.015. Only 2 studies in the developing-country subgroup — authors explicitly flag this as exploratory, not a socioeconomic-status claim.
- **Subgroup — measurement landmark (1st molar only)**: palatal cusp tip 75% (66–84%), buccal cusp tip 78% (69–87%), central fossa 51% (34–69%); between-subgroup p=.031. After excluding the single central-fossa study, palatal vs buccal cusp-tip difference is not significant (p=.655).
- **Subgroup — Geomagic software (3 studies)**: canine 82.42% (75.86–88.98%, I²=84.0%), 1st premolar 80.70% (73.61–87.78%, I²=12.3%), 2nd premolar 78.06% (70.61–85.51%, I²=0.0%), 1st molar 75.12% (67.30–82.93%, I²=0.0%); no significant difference among regions within this subgroup (p=.528).
- **Sensitivity**: leave-one-out stable for premolar/1st-molar pooled estimates; does not resolve canine-region heterogeneity.
- **Publication bias (Egger, exploratory, n=6 per outcome)**: 1st premolar t=−2.56, p=.064; 2nd premolar t=−2.75, p=.051; 1st molar t=−1.58, p=.190; canine t=−2.83, p=.047 (suggestive of small-study effects in canine only; underpowered).
- **GRADE certainty**: intercanine LOW (downgraded for suspected publication bias + inconsistency); 1st interpremolar HIGH; 2nd interpremolar MODERATE (downgraded for risk of bias + heterogeneity); 1st intermolar MODERATE (same downgrades).
- **Clinical translation**: for each 1 mm of ClinCheck-programmed expansion, ~0.72–0.81 mm was achieved across canine/premolar/1st-molar regions — achieved expansion is consistently below programmed. Individual studies (Zhou 2020, Tien 2023) recorded >100% predictability in some cases (achieved exceeded planned), illustrating individual variability the pooled estimate cannot capture.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Only 6 retrospective, non-randomized studies (no RCTs), overall risk of bias low-to-moderate; no individual patient-level data (age, sex, malocclusion severity, attachment design, programmed-expansion magnitude could not be analyzed).
- Studies differed in anatomical landmark (buccal cusp tip, palatal cusp tip, central fossa), measurement software (Geomagic variants, 3-Matic, Viewbox, MeshLab), and follow-up/protocol details — a source of clinical and methodological heterogeneity the authors could not fully quantify.
- Subgroup analyses (country, landmark, software) are exploratory, based on very small numbers of studies (as few as 1–3 per subgroup); the country-based split is study-level (ecological), not individual socioeconomic or compliance data, and should not be read as a compliance or SES claim.
- Egger's test for small-study effects had limited statistical power (only 6 studies per outcome); funnel plots were not used at all (below the ~10-study threshold the authors considered reliable).
- Canine-region heterogeneity (I²=74.7%) remains unexplained after leave-one-out and all subgroup analyses — authors point to rotational/transverse movement difficulty at the canine and incompletely-reported attachment/rotation data as plausible but unconfirmed drivers.
- Authors explicitly do not recommend a universal overcorrection prescription; call for prospective, multicenter studies with standardized landmarks and reported aligner/attachment protocols.

## 6. Related Work
- [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/fonseca-planells-2026-clear-aligner-maxillary-expansion-growing]]: SR+MA on clear-aligner vs conventional-expander transverse expansion in *growing/mixed-dentition* patients (aligners under-expand vs conventional expanders, mostly dentoalveolar). Population complement — this paper (xianggang-2026) covers *adults* and a different outcome (achieved-vs-planned %, not aligner-vs-comparator mm difference).
- [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/de-la-rosa-gay-2025-expansion-predictability-clear-aligner]]: single-clinic retrospective cohort (98 adults) modeling the same broad question (Invisalign expansion predictability) with a multilevel GLMM. That study found the canine the *most reliable* (lowest absolute mm error) maxillary tooth region, and the 1st/2nd premolar and 1st molar comparatively worse — the opposite ranking from this SR+MA's pooled percentage predictability, where canine is *tied lowest* with 1st molar and premolars are highest. The two studies use different outcome metrics (absolute mm discrepancy vs achieved/predicted %) and de la Rosa-Gay's study postdates this SR+MA's August 2024 search cutoff, so it was not among the 6 pooled studies — a genuine open tension on canine-region reliability in adults, not resolved by either paper alone.
- [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/goncalves-2023-invisalign-upper-incisor-accuracy-sr]]: SR of Invisalign predicted-vs-achieved accuracy by *movement type* (torque, tip, rotation, extrusion) in maxillary incisors — a different tooth region and a movement-type rather than expansion-magnitude lens, but the same underlying software-prediction-accuracy question.

## 7. Glossary
- **ClinCheck**: Align Technology's proprietary software that visualizes the planned tooth-movement sequence and predicted final positions for an Invisalign case.
- **Predictability (as used here)**: the percentage of achieved maxillary transverse expansion relative to the expansion predicted by ClinCheck (achieved/predicted × 100); not a measure of accuracy against a "true" target, but against the software's own plan.
- **ROBINS-I**: Risk of Bias in Non-randomized Studies of Interventions — the risk-of-bias tool used because all 6 included studies were non-randomized.
- **GRADE**: Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation — framework for rating certainty of evidence per outcome (high/moderate/low/very low).
- **ICW / FIPW / SIPW / FIMW / SIMW**: intercanine width / 1st interpremolar width / 2nd interpremolar width / 1st intermolar width / 2nd intermolar width — the transverse measurements used across the included studies.
