## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (J Dent Sci 2024, Seoul National University), 13 patients / 14 implant displacements into the maxillary sinus retrieved by endoscopic-assisted bony-window surgery.

Displacement AFTER functional loading caused significantly worse sinus opacification (3.6 vs 1.7, p=0.019) and osteitis (wall ≥3 mm in 2/5) than perioperative displacement; timing was the only variable independently predicting both opacification and wall thickness (p=0.013), and all 14 cases had inadequate site preparation despite residual bone <5 mm.

Clinical implication: prevent displacement with site-specific bone grafting when residual bone is thin, and remove displaced implants promptly — especially post-loading ones — before osteitis sets in; opacification resolved (0.4) by 6 months after removal.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. Quantifies the **impact** of a displaced implant on the sinus (opacification score, wall thickness/osteitis) — not just the removal technique.
2. Shows **timing of displacement** (post-loading > perioperative) is the dominant independent predictor of sinus pathology.
3. Documents a **site-preparation gap** (no grafting/short implants in any case despite residual bone <5 mm) as a modifiable risk driver.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Retrospective cohort of patients who underwent retrieval of an implant displaced into the maxillary sinus with pre/postop panoramic, Waters, and CT imaging. Modified endoscopic-assisted sinus surgery with a microplate-fixed, repositioned bony window. Radiographic metrics: sinus opacification (0–4), sinus wall thickness (≥3 mm = osteitis). Statistics: Mann–Whitney U, Fisher exact, Wilcoxon signed-rank, univariate linear regression (p<0.05). n = 13 patients (14 displacements; one patient twice).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Demographics**: 8M/5F, mean age 64.6 ± 9.9; 10 smokers; 4 hypertension, 3 diabetes. Original sites: 2nd molar 64.3%, 1st molar 35.7%.
- **Timing**: 9 perioperative, 5 post-loading. **Retention**: 7 <3 mo, 7 >3 mo. Residual bone height 4.7 ± 1.9 mm (perioperative 3.9 vs post-loading 5.9, p=0.067).
- **Opacification**: preop mean 2.4 ± 1.5; post-loading 3.6 ± 0.9 vs surgery 1.7 ± 1.3 (**p=0.019**); dropped to 0.4 ± 0.8 at 6 months post-removal (**p=0.002**).
- **Wall thickness / osteitis**: 1.6 ± 0.8 mm (displaced) vs 1.1 ± 0.2 mm (normal, p=0.002); post-loading 2.3 vs perioperative 1.2 (p=0.198 ns); **osteitis (≥3 mm) in 40% (2/5) of post-loading**, 0% perioperative.
- **Regression**: opacification ← age (p=0.034), diabetes (p=0.025), **timing** (p=0.013); wall thickness ← **timing only** (p=0.013).
- **Risk profile**: mean residual bone <5 mm yet **no site preparation / short-implant use in any of 14 cases** — echoes Galindo-Moreno (~73% no grafting) and an SR (>half of 182 patients no pre-implant surgery).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small n (rare complication); no histopathology of removed inflammatory tissue.
- Osteitis extent/treatment consensus lacking; several comparisons underpowered (ns despite trends).
- Larger cohorts with histopathology needed to clarify osteitis impact and management.

## 7. Glossary

- **Sinus opacification score (0–4)** — radiographic grading of maxillary-sinus haziness (0 none → 4 total).
- **Osteitis** — inflammatory neo-osteogenesis thickening the sinus wall (≥3 mm), associated with poorer endoscopic-sinus-surgery prognosis.
- **Ostiomeatal complex** — the drainage pathway of the maxillary sinus; obstruction predisposes to sinusitis.
- **Type IV bone** — thin cortex + low-density cancellous bone typical of the posterior maxilla.
