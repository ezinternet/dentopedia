## Why Ingested

Surveillance hit (bone-graft-material topic): the only evidence we hold that isolates the *surface design* of an osteoinductive calcium phosphate (CaP) from its bulk composition — a preclinical sheep maxillary sinus floor augmentation (MSFA) experiment contrasting submicron needle-shaped with submicron grain-shaped CaP, followed by a small randomized clinical arm (n=10) against autologous bone graft (ABG). It qualifies the material-class ranking in [[sinus-lift/lateral/somngam-2025-new-bone-formation-biphasic-calcium]] (autograft 12.33% above BCP) and sits in line with [[sinus-lift/lateral/trimmel-2021-relative-performance-various-biomaterials]], where the "autograft is the gold standard" hypothesis was rejected; it also supplies the osteoinductivity caveat that is missing from [[bone-regeneration/zhao-2021-bone-grafts-substitutes-dentistry-review]], where all available synthetic substitutes were described as satisfying only osteoconductivity.

## Three-line Summary

Translational study: a preclinical sheep model of maxillary sinus floor augmentation (MSFA) comparing an osteoinductive calcium phosphate with submicron needle-shaped topography (BCPN, MagnetOs Granules) against submicron grain-shaped calcium phosphate (BCPG) and autologous bone graft (ABG) as controls, followed by a prospective randomized controlled trial (RCT) of 10 patients with two-stage implant placement.
Both materials were highly biocompatible with direct bone apposition and bone formation as early as 3 weeks; BCPN formed significantly more bone than BCPG at the 12-week endpoint, and only BCPN reached an equivalent amount of bone formation in the available space with a greater proportion of calcified material (bone + graft material) than ABG, while clinically implant stability, bone height, new bone in trephine core biopsies and overall clinical outcome were comparable to ABG.
Osteoinductive calcium phosphate is a promising graft substitute for MSFA, but its bone-forming potential depends on the design of its surface features (needle > grain); the clinical arm is small (n=10) and this record is abstract-only, so no effect sizes, p-values or implant-survival figures are reported here.

두 재료 모두 높은 생체적합성 (biocompatibility)과 재료 표면에 직접 부착하는 골 형성 (direct bone apposition)을 보이며 3주부터 골형성이 시작되었고, 12주 시점 BCPN이 BCPG보다 유의하게 많은 골을 형성함. 이용 가능 공간 내 골형성량에서 ABG와 동등한 수준에 이르고 석회화물질 (bone + graft material) 비율은 더 높았으며, 임플란트 안정성 (implant stability)·골높이 (bone height)·트레파인 코어 생검 (trephine core biopsy) 신생골 (new bone) 형성과 전반적 임상 결과는 ABG와 비교 가능 (comparable)했음.
골유도성 인산칼슘은 MSFA 이식재 대체재로 유망하나 골형성 잠재력은 표면 특징 설계 (surface feature design)에 의존함 (나침형 > 입자형). 임상 부분은 10명으로 표본이 작고 본 기록은 초록 전용 (abstract only)이므로 효과크기·p값·임플란트 생존율 수치는 기재하지 않음.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Head-to-head test of two submicron CaP surface designs in the same species and site (needle-shaped BCPN vs grain-shaped BCPG), isolating surface design as the experimental variable.
- Bone formation detected as early as 3 weeks post-implantation with direct bone apposition against both materials in the sheep model.
- BCPN formed significantly more bone than BCPG at the 12-week endpoint, and was the only material that reached equivalence with the "gold standard" ABG in bone formed within the available space, with a greater proportion of calcified material (bone + graft material).
- Small prospective randomized clinical validation (n=10, two-stage implant placement) showing comparability of BCPN with ABG on implant stability, bone height, trephine-biopsy new bone and overall clinical outcome.
- Design-dependence of osteoinductive performance stated as the paper's conclusion: bone-forming potential of an osteoinductive calcium phosphate depends on the design of its surface features.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: two-part translational study — (i) preclinical animal experiment, (ii) prospective randomized controlled clinical trial.
- **Preclinical model**: sheep model of MSFA. Arms: BCPN (calcium phosphate with submicron needle-shaped topography; MagnetOs Granules, Kuros Biosciences BV), BCPG (calcium phosphate with submicron grain-shaped topography), and autologous bone graft (ABG) as control. Study endpoint 12 weeks; bone formation already observed at 3 weeks.
- **Preclinical endpoints**: biocompatibility, bony ingrowth and direct bone apposition against the material, amount of bone formation in the available space, and proportion of calcified material (bone + graft material) in the maxillary sinus.
- **Clinical trial**: 10 patients, prospective, randomized, controlled; BCPN vs ABG in MSFA with two-stage implant placement. Outcomes: implant stability, bone height, new bone formation in trephine core biopsies, overall clinical outcome. Registered as NL6436.
- **Artifact limitation**: only the structured abstract was retrieved, so allocation concealment, blinding, exact timepoints, histomorphometric thresholds, effect sizes, confidence intervals and p-values are not available in this record.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **Abstract-only record**: no full text (closed access, no OA copy located), so no effect sizes, CIs, p-values, implant-survival numbers or methodological detail (randomization, blinding, allocation concealment) are available here.
- **Small clinical sample (n=10)**: precision and generalizability are limited; no power calculation is reported in the abstract.
- **Preclinical-to-clinical translation gap**: the ABG "gold standard" comparison is animal data; the clinical arm is a feasibility-sized RCT, and the abstract does not state the surgical approach (lateral window vs transcrestal) or follow-up duration.
- **Industry affiliation**: two authors (de Groot FG, de Bruijn JD) are affiliated with Kuros Biosciences BV, the manufacturer of MagnetOs Granules — a relevant conflict-of-interest consideration when this page is cited.
- **Missing outcome domains**: no long-term data on graft volume stability/resorption, implant survival figures, cost or patient-reported outcomes in the retrieved record.
- **Future work (author direction)**: the paper's conclusion points to surface-feature design as the lever for osteoinductive performance, implying that grafting-material selection in MSFA should be made at the level of surface design, not bulk composition.

## 6. Related Work
- Somngam et al. 2025 — SR+MA network meta-analysis: autograft ranked above BCP for new bone formation (+12.33%); the present study qualifies that class-level ranking by showing BCP performance is surface-design dependent.
- Trimmel et al. 2021 — Bayesian NMA of 34 RCTs rejecting the "autograft alone is the gold standard" hypothesis; consistent with this study's finding of a CaP substitute comparable to autograft.
- Jue et al. 2025 — split-mouth RCT (50 patients, 198 implants) of autograft vs bovine bone mineral with 25% local bone in lateral MSFA: the same autograft-vs-substitute clinical question at larger scale and 12-month follow-up.
- Del Fabbro et al. 2025 — SR of 49 RCTs finding equivalent implant survival across graft materials; consistent with this study's comparability claim.
- Alkandari et al. 2025 — SR+MA of BCP vs DBBM histomorphometry: positions conventional BCP among synthetic grafts, in contrast to the osteoinductive CaP tested here.
- Zhao et al. 2021 — narrative review of graft substitutes stating that available synthetic substitutes satisfy only osteoconductivity; the osteoinductive claim for one CaP design qualifies this.

## 7. Glossary
- **MSFA (Maxillary Sinus Floor Augmentation)**: surgical elevation of the maxillary sinus floor to create vertical bone height in the atrophic posterior maxilla.
- **Osteoinductive (골유도성)**: material property by which an implant induces progenitor cells to differentiate toward osteoblasts (bone-forming lineage).
- **Osteoconductive (골전도성)**: material property by which the implant surface supports bone growth along it without itself inducing osteoblastic differentiation.
- **BCPN / BCPG**: two calcium phosphate materials of identical bulk type but different submicron surface topography — needle-shaped (BCPN) and grain-shaped (BCPG).
- **Direct bone apposition**: bone growing in direct contact with the material surface without intervening soft tissue.
- **Available space**: the residual sinus cavity volume into which new bone can form after grafting.
- **Calcified material (bone + graft material)**: total mineralized fraction in the grafted area, combining newly formed bone and remaining graft particles.
- **Trephine core biopsy**: hollow-cylinder bone sample taken at implant site for histomorphometric analysis of new bone.
- **Two-stage implant placement**: grafting and implant placement as two separate surgical sessions.
- **ABG (Autologous Bone Graft)**: bone harvested from the same patient, used here as the "gold standard" control.
