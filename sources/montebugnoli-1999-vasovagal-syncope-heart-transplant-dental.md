## Why Ingested

The dental vasovagal literature the wiki holds — [[drug/systemic-disease/malave-2022-vasovagal-reactions-during-interventional-pain]] and [[drug/systemic-disease/li-2026-syncope-in-dentistry-risk-factors]] — explains vasovagal syncope (VVS) through the Bezold–Jarisch reflex, whose efferent limb is vagal bradycardia plus sympathetic withdrawal, and [[drug/systemic-disease/arakeri-2010-trigeminocardiac-reflex-hypothesis-syncope-extraction]] routes it through cardiac vagal afferents. This report is the natural-experiment test of that limb: if a completely denervated transplanted heart still produces a vasodepressor collapse, the efferent vagal limb cannot be the whole mechanism.

## Three-line Summary

Case series (3 episodes in 3 heart transplant patients undergoing periodontal surgery, University of Bologna, Italy; abstract-only, full text not retrieved) used as a natural experiment on the mechanism of vasovagal syncope, since a transplanted donor heart is completely deprived of vagal and sympathetic innervation.
Every episode produced a dramatic fall in systolic blood pressure (137 ± 5 mmHg → 76 ± 3.6 mmHg) but, unlike normal subjects, no relevant heart-rate change (96.7 ± 4.5 → 102.6 ± 7.6 beats/min); management is not described in the abstract.
The authors conclude that the heart plays only a marginal role in the pathogenesis of vasovagal syncope and that a vasovagal reaction can develop without the bradycardia usually reported as its primary symptom.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Documents vasovagal syncope in 3 heart transplant patients during periodontal surgery, i.e. in patients whose donor heart has no vagal or sympathetic innervation.
- Reports the haemodynamic signature of those episodes: a large fall in systolic blood pressure with no relevant change in heart rate.
- Argues from that dissociation that the heart itself has only a marginal role in the pathogenesis of vasovagal syncope.
- States explicitly that a vasovagal reaction can occur without bradycardia, the symptom usually described as its primary manifestation.
- Reframes the debate over C-fiber mechanoreceptors in the heart and cardiac vagal afferents as insufficient to explain the vasodepressor response on their own.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only**: this page is built from the PubMed abstract; the full text was not retrieved, so methods, monitoring detail, per-patient data, and management are unavailable.
- **n = 3, single centre**: no control group, no denominator (the number of transplant patients operated on without syncope is unknown), so no incidence can be estimated.
- **Incomplete denervation**: the abstract's premise is *complete* denervation, but transplanted hearts can show partial or heterogeneous reinnervation and the patients' immunosuppression, denervation status, and time since transplant are not reported here — denervation is asserted, not measured in this report.
- **Haemodynamics only**: blood pressure and heart rate are the only reported variables; there is no cardiac output, stroke volume, or vascular-resistance measurement, so the vasodepressor mechanism is inferred rather than characterised.
- **Metadata discrepancy**: the stored artifact's header lists the second author as "Testa S", while the verbatim PubMed record in the same file lists "Montanari G"; this page follows the PubMed record.
- Because vasodepressor syncope without bradycardia is the expected pattern when the efferent vagal limb is absent, the report constrains but does not settle where the reflex originates.

## 6. Related Work

- **Malave 2022** (`drug/systemic-disease/malave-2022-vasovagal-reactions-during-interventional-pain`): the Bezold–Jarisch account this report stresses — cardiac mechanoreceptors → vagal afferents → parasympathetic overdrive with bradycardia and hypotension.
- **Li 2026** (`drug/systemic-disease/li-2026-syncope-in-dentistry-risk-factors`): dental VVS review whose management branch keys atropine to symptomatic bradycardia and whose pathophysiology lists cardiac vagal outflow; this report is the case in which that branch would have nothing to treat.
- **Arakeri 2010** (`drug/systemic-disease/arakeri-2010-trigeminocardiac-reflex-hypothesis-syncope-extraction`): places the afferent trigger in trigeminal (CN V2/V3) afferents; the transplant observation concerns the efferent side, so the two hypotheses are not mutually exclusive.

## 7. Glossary

- **Vasovagal syncope (VVS)**: neurally mediated transient loss of consciousness with hypotension, usually with bradycardia.
- **Denervation (cardiac)**: loss of vagal and sympathetic nerve supply to the transplanted donor heart.
- **Vasodepressor response**: fall in blood pressure without a proportionate fall in heart rate.
- **C-fiber mechanoreceptors**: unmyelinated cardiac afferents proposed to trigger the vasodepressor reflex.
- **Cardiac vagal afferents**: vagal sensory fibres carrying cardiac signals to the medullary cardiovascular centre.
- **Systolic blood pressure (SBP)**: peak arterial pressure; the reported haemodynamic index in this abstract.
