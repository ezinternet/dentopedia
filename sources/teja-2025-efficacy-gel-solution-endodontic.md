## One-line Summary
SR of 37 studies: solution-based endodontic irrigants are superior to gel-based formulations in antimicrobial efficacy, smear layer removal, instrument safety, and overall clinical performance; gels cause dentin-gel sludge and increased file separation risk.

## 한줄요약
SR (37편): 용액형 근관 세정제가 젤형보다 항균·세정·기구 안전성 전반에서 우위 — 젤-NaOCl 슬러지가 기구 피로도·파일 분리 위험 증가; 젤 사용 시 반드시 충분한 용액 세정 후속 필요.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 37 studies (in vitro, ex vivo and clinical) comparing gel-based with solution-based endodontic irrigants, synthesised narratively without pooling.

Solutions gave better smear layer removal, cleaning and antimicrobial activity, whereas gels mixed with dentinal debris into sludge that increased torsional and cyclic fatigue and file separation risk; clinical trials showed no gel advantage in bacterial load or postoperative pain.

Use solution irrigants as the primary irrigant and, if a gel is used for lubrication, follow it with thorough solution irrigation; the evidence is heterogeneous with few high-quality clinical RCTs.

## 세줄요약

젤형과 용액형 근관 세정제를 비교한 37편 (in vitro, ex vivo, 임상)의 체계적 문헌고찰로, 풀링 없이 서술적으로 종합했다.

용액형이 도말층 제거·세정력·항균 효과에서 우수했고, 젤은 상아질 잔사와 섞여 슬러지를 만들어 비틀림·반복 피로와 파일 파절 위험을 높였으며, 임상시험에서 젤은 세균 감소나 술 후 통증에서 이점이 없었다.

용액형을 기본 세정제로 쓰고 윤활 목적으로 젤을 쓰면 반드시 용액 세정을 충분히 이어서 한다; 연구 간 이질성이 크고 고품질 임상 RCT는 적다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First SR directly comparing gel vs solution formulations of the same endodontic disinfectants (NaOCl, EDTA, CHX) during shaping
- Gel-based NaOCl forms dense "dentine-gel sludge" with dentinal debris → increased torsional/cyclic fatigue and file separation risk
- Solution-based formulations consistently superior for smear layer removal, antimicrobial activity, and canal cleaning efficiency
- Gels do offer glide-path lubrication advantage and may reduce apical extrusion in some models — limited clinical benefit
- Clinical trials showed no significant advantage of gels in bacterial load reduction or postoperative pain
- Gel use should always be followed by thorough solution irrigation

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review; narrative synthesis (heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis)
- **Study types included**: RCTs, in vitro, ex vivo, and clinical studies
- **Inclusion**: Studies comparing gel- vs solution-based formulations (EDTA gel, NaOCl gel, CHX gel) during root canal shaping
- **Outcomes**: Smear layer/debris removal, instrument separation, cyclic/torsional fatigue, bacterial reduction, debris extrusion, postoperative pain
- **Studies included**: 37
- **Risk of bias**: Not formally reported; heterogeneity across study designs noted

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
**In vitro / ex vivo:**
- Gel formulations → ↑ dentinal debris retention, ↑ torsional/cyclic fatigue, ↑ file separation risk (consistent across studies)
- Solution formulations → superior smear layer removal, better cleaning efficiency, stronger antimicrobial activity
- Gels reduced apical extrusion in some models (glide-path lubrication benefit)

**Clinical trials:**
- No significant advantage of gels in bacterial load reduction
- Postoperative pain scores comparable between gel and solution groups
- NaOCl solutions provided better antimicrobial outcomes overall

**Key mechanism**: gel + dentinal debris → sludge → instrument clogging → fatigue fracture; solutions flush debris more effectively

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Narrative synthesis only (no pooled quantitative analysis)
- Study heterogeneity across design types limits cross-comparability
- Limited high-quality RCTs in clinical domain
- Future needs: standardized shaping protocols to enable meta-analysis; gel-specific RCTs with file separation and bacterial load as co-primary outcomes

## 7. Glossary
- **Dentin-gel sludge**: Dense paste formed when gel-based irrigants mix with dentinal debris — impairs canal cleaning and instrument safety
- **Cyclic fatigue**: Repeated bending stress causing NiTi file fracture; worsened by sludge obstructing rotational movement
- **Glide-path lubrication**: Gel benefit — reduces initial file friction in narrow canals; clinical relevance debated
- **CHX gel**: Chlorhexidine gluconate in gel vehicle; antibiofilm but cytotoxic concerns on dentin
