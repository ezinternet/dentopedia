## Why Ingested

This PRISMA-compliant systematic review synthesizes evidence on CAT effects on masticatory muscles and TMJ, addressing a gap identified in [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/luo-2024-factors-alveolar-bone-changes-clear-aligner]] and other CAT pages which focus on periodontal/bone outcomes but not neuromuscular adaptation. The review confirms temporary muscle activity increase during aligner wear with normalization post-treatment — an important patient counseling point for CAT tolerance expectations.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA-Cochrane; 6 studies, n=122, mean age 22–35 years) evaluating masticatory muscle activity, bite force, and TMD signs during clear aligner therapy.

Clear aligners caused short-term increase in muscle activity & pain at treatment onset (T1), which reverted to baseline at follow-up; bite force temporarily reduced but normalized; no significant TMD sign/symptom increase.

Findings support patient education that transient muscle soreness and altered bite force are expected adaptive responses during CAT, not pathological sequelae, pending larger RCTs with standardized outcome measures.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **Synthesized evidence on CAT & masticatory muscle dynamics:** First systematic review of its kind consolidating electromyography (EMG) and myoelectric activity data during aligner wear across 6 clinical studies with low-to-moderate heterogeneity.

2. **Confirmed temporary muscle adaptation pattern:** Clear pattern emerged—transient elevation of masseter/temporalis activity at treatment onset (T0→T1) with adaptive normalization by T2 (follow-up weeks to months), contradicting initial concern that CAT might persistently alter muscle function.

3. **Bite force reversibility established:** Temporary reduction in bite force during active aligner wear (especially maxillary aligner placement) normalized post-treatment, supporting the hypothesis that force reduction is a mechanical/occlusal transient, not neuromuscular pathology.

4. **Low TMD risk signal:** Review did not support increased incidence of TMD signs/symptoms with CAT, though studies were heterogeneous in TMD assessment methodology. Nuanced conclusion: orthodontic treatment does not *increase* TMD risk *de novo*, but may affect pre-existing TMD natural history (insufficient evidence for definitive statement).

5. **Methodological quality documented:** All 6 included studies showed low risk of bias on Cochrane ROB-2 tool; systematic use of ROB-2 for bias assessment ensures internal validity of synthesized findings.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

**Search Strategy (PRISMA-adherent):**
- Databases: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane Library, CINAHL, PsycINFO, Google Scholar
- Search terms: Boolean combinations of ("Aligner orthodontic treatment" OR "Clear aligner treatment" OR "Invisalign") AND ("Masticatory anomalies" OR "Masticatory disorders" OR "Temporomandibular disorder" OR "TMD") AND ("Clinical study" OR "Observational study" OR "Cohort study")
- Filters: English-language, clinical studies on humans, published papers (no reviews, editorials, case reports as primary inclusions)
- Study selection: Dual-reviewer screening (title/abstract → full-text eligibility) with disagreement resolution by third reviewer
- Timeframe: Multi-decade coverage (studies 2017–2023 included)

**Inclusion Criteria (PECO framework):**
- **P (Population):** Human individuals, any age, any gender
- **E (Exposure):** Clear aligner orthodontic treatment (removable thermoplastic appliances correcting malocclusion)
- **C (Comparator):** None required; single-arm studies acceptable
- **O (Outcome):** Changes in masticatory muscle activity, bite force, TMD signs/symptoms, or oral function

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Studies not clearly defining aligner treatment or outcome measurement
- Reviews, editorials, commentaries, case reports (small n<10), case-only studies
- Pre-existing conditions (rheumatoid arthritis, jaw trauma) that independently impact TMD phenotype

**Data Extraction:** Standardized form designed per study; two reviewers extracted independently with third-party arbitration on disagreement. Variables: study ID, location, year, population demographics (n, age, gender), sample size range (10–31), study design (longitudinal/retrospective/prospective), follow-up duration (9 days–8 months), assessment method (EMG, bite-force transducer, clinical exam), outcome measures.

**Risk of Bias Assessment:** Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (ROB-2) tool applied to all 6 studies; assessed domains: randomization bias, allocation concealment, blinding of participants/personnel/outcome assessors, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting, other bias. Result: all 6 rated low risk overall (see Fig. 2: predominantly green, minimal yellow "unclear" ratings).

**Synthesis:** Qualitative narrative synthesis (heterogeneous outcome measures precluded meta-analysis); findings organized by outcome category (muscle activity, bite force, TMD, pain/discomfort).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### **Muscle Activity (Electromyography/Masseter-Temporalis Activity)**

| Study | Design | Finding | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liu et al (2017) | Longitudinal, 3 timepoints (T0, T1, T2) | sEMG & sMMG activity ↑ baseline→T1 (at appliance placement); ↑ at MVC (p<0.05); anterior TA & SCM activity significantly higher at T1 vs T0 (p<0.05); normalized T2 | Transient muscle exertion adaptation during aligner insertion; remodeling/neural accommodation by 3-month follow-up |
| Manfrendini et al (2018) | Retrospective sleep study (3 phases: pre, during, post-treatment) | sEMG during sleep: no significant difference in sMMG variables (sleeping masseter activity unchanged) | Muscle activity during rest (sleep) unaffected by aligner presence — adaptation limited to functional/conscious mastication |
| Nota et al (2019) | Longitudinal 3 timepoints (T0, T1, T2) | sEMG masseter at mandibular rest: statistically significant reduction at T1 vs baseline, returned to baseline T2; pain at clenching reported in 12.5% at T1 (↑ 25% at T1) | Baseline bite posture altered transiently during aligner adaptation; muscle soreness reversible |
| Paes et al (2023) | Preliminary prospective, 8-month follow-up (anterior temporals) | sEMG↑≈30% RMS at p<0.001 during aligner treatment; bite force reduced <20% (p<0.05); clenching discomfort ↑ in higher %age | Aligner material (polyurethane) sensitized masticatory muscles; soft-tissue irritation contributed |
| Pittar et al (2023) | Prospective, students with HPA/LPA categorization; evaluated cafeine/alcohol/motorway stress | sEMG MMA: ↓ in mean contraction amplitude (p=0.091, nonsignificant); OD increased (p=0.048, high-HPA subset); TMD symptoms present throughout both groups | Stress/psychosocial factors confounded aligner effect; aligner-specific muscle effect modest when anxiety controlled |
| Tran et al (2020) | Multi-site prospective, 4-week follow-up; assessed pain thresholds (3 phases: passive, active, dummy) | Tooth pain at all phases; masticatory pressure pain thresholds ↑ during active phase (higher soreness vs dummy); pain weakly correlated anxiety (r=0.423, p=0.08) | Pain during active aligner phase driven by mechanical/orthodontic stimulus, not anxiety; soreness resolves post-aligner removal |

**Summary:** Transient elevation of masseter & temporalis EMG activity during aligner wear (first 1–3 months), with normalization by 3–8 months follow-up. No persistent neuromuscular dysfunction; pattern consistent with muscle adaptation to altered occlusal contacts and force vectors.

### **Bite Force**

- **Reduced during aligner wear** (~20% decrease in maximal voluntary contraction, p<0.05; noted in Paes et al)
- **Mechanism:** Aligner material interposition reduces direct dental contact efficiency; polyurethane compliance absorbs force
- **Reversibility:** Normalized post-treatment (return to baseline at follow-up phases)
- **Clinical implication:** Patients may experience reduced chewing efficiency temporarily; counseling about softer diet during initial weeks advised

### **Temporomandibular Disorder (TMD) Signs & Symptoms**

- **Pain:** Significantly increased at T1 (treatment onset) — 47.5–100% of patients reported mild tooth pain, masticatory muscle soreness
- **TMD diagnosis/dysfunction:** No significant increase in TMD incidence at follow-up; existing TMD not exacerbated by CAT
- **Condylar positioning & jaw relation:** No significant changes observed; aligner did not alter vertical dimension or condylar seating (limited data)
- **Conclusion:** CAT does not pose *de novo* TMD risk in healthy patients; transient pain is expected and resolves

### **Occlusal Adjustment & Parafunctional Habits**

- Liu et al: Oral Behavior Checklist (OBC) decreased T0→T1 (reducing parafunctional clenching/bruxism), then ↑ modestly T2 (baseline restoration)
- Interpretation: Aligner presence constrains orofacial habits; habit improvement during treatment is temporary

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

### **Study Limitations (Author-Acknowledged)**

1. **Small sample sizes:** 6 studies with n ranging 10–31 (total n≈122). Heterogeneous age, gender ratios, follow-up duration (9 days–8 months). Smallest n=10 (Liu 2017 Brazil), largest n=31 (Pittar 2023 New Zealand).

2. **Heterogeneous outcome measures:** No standardized EMG reporting (peak amplitude vs. RMS vs. integrated EMG); different pain/discomfort scales (visual analog scale, Likert, ordinal); inconsistent TMD diagnostic criteria (DC/TMD, RDC/TMD, clinical exam only). Precluded meta-analysis → qualitative synthesis only.

3. **Short follow-up periods:** Median follow-up ≤8 months; longest was 8 months (Paes). Insufficient to assess long-term neuromuscular adaptation (>2 years post-treatment) or delayed TMD onset.

4. **Confounding factors insufficiently controlled:** Parafunctional habits (bruxism, clenching), stress/anxiety, caffeine, concurrent medication, airway/sleep quality, pre-treatment TMD severity. Pittar et al attempted cafeine/alcohol/stress stratification but sample sizes precluded robust analysis.

5. **Aligner type & wear protocol heterogeneity:** Studies did not standardize material (polyurethane vs. proprietary formulations), thickness, retention (wear h/day), replacement schedule. Aligner fit quality (retention level) likely modulates muscle response but not systematically reported.

6. **Lack of objective assessment in some domains:** Most studies relied on self-reported pain/discomfort (prone to expectation bias); objective measures (algometry, pressure pain threshold quantification) sparse.

7. **Publication bias risk:** Review did not include grey literature; non-English studies excluded. Negative/null results underrepresented.

### **Future Research Recommendations**

1. **Prospective RCTs with standardized protocols:**
   - Fixed aligner material, wear duration (h/day), replacement schedule
   - Standardized EMG protocol (bipolar surface electrodes, reference electrode placement, normalization to MVC)
   - Longer follow-up (12–24 months) to assess persistence of adaptation

2. **Mechanistic studies:**
   - Biomechanical FEA of aligner force vectors & muscle activation patterns
   - Electrophysiology (motor unit recruitment, fatigue resistance)
   - Neuroimaging (fMRI of motor cortex during aligner vs. control)

3. **TMD phenotyping:**
   - Use standardized DC/TMD diagnostic criteria across all sites
   - Measure condylar position (CBCT), disc status (MRI) before/after
   - Stratify by pre-treatment TMD status (no TMD vs. mild vs. pain-related diagnosis)

4. **Biomarkers of muscle adaptation:**
   - Serum muscle enzymes (creatine kinase), myokine profiles (IL-6, TNF-α)
   - Muscle biopsy (fiber-type shift, mitochondrial density) — invasive but mechanistically informative

5. **Patient-centered outcomes:**
   - Masticatory efficiency questionnaires & objective comminution assays
   - Oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) during treatment vs. baseline
   - Long-term chewing comfort & dietary habits

## 6. Related Work

### **Same Topic — Clear Aligners & Neuromuscular / Masticatory Effects**

- **Luo et al (2024)** — Factors of alveolar bone changes; relevant for bone resorption under similar muscle/force loading during CAT
- **Zhuo et al** — Roller Coaster Effect in extraction CAT; posterior open bite risk related to vertical force vectors affecting jaw closure muscles
- **Myofunctional/elastodontic orthodontics** (separate category) — Contrasts with CAT: myofunctional appliances are passive/elastic; CAT active/thermoplastic; different muscle recruitment patterns

### **TMD & Orthodontic Treatment**

- Leite et al, Fernandez et al (cited in review) — Systematic reviews on TMD & fixed-appliance orthodontics; found inconsistent TMD outcomes across modalities
- Alam et al (cited) — Meta-analysis on MAD (mandibular advancement devices) and TMD; comparable neuromuscular assessment but different treatment modality

### **Masticatory Muscle Physiology & Adaptation**

- Core references on myofascial pain, SMAUG (slow/sleep-time masticatory muscle activity) biomechanics, stress/anxiety links (cited in text: pp. 6–8)

## 7. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **CAT** | Clear Aligner Therapy — removable thermoplastic appliance-based orthodontic treatment |
| **EMG / sEMG** | Electromyography / surface electromyography — non-invasive measurement of muscle electrical activity |
| **sMMG** | Sleep-time Masticatory Muscle Activity — EMG recorded during sleep (baseline/resting state) |
| **MVC** | Maximal Voluntary Contraction — peak force exerted during maximum clench; EMG normalized to MVC |
| **RMS** | Root Mean Square — standard EMG amplitude metric (microvolts) |
| **MMP** | Mandibular Postural Position — resting jaw position without conscious effort |
| **TA** | Anterior Temporalis muscle |
| **SCM** | Sternocleidomastoid muscle (accessory mastication) |
| **OBC** | Oral Behavior Checklist — questionnaire of parafunctional habits (clenching, bruxism) |
| **OD** | Occlusal Discomfort — subjective pain/soreness rating |
| **DC/TMD** | Diagnostic Criteria for Temporomandibular Disorders (research classification) |
| **RDC/TMD** | Research Diagnostic Criteria for TMD (predecessor to DC/TMD) |
| **ROB-2** | Risk of Bias 2 (Cochrane tool) — assessment of internal validity in RCTs & observational studies |
| **PRISMA** | Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews & Meta-Analyses — reporting standard |
| **PECO** | Population, Exposure, Comparator, Outcome — framework for defining research question |
| **HPA / LPA** | High-Parafunctional Activity / Low-Parafunctional Activity — participant stratification by habit severity |
