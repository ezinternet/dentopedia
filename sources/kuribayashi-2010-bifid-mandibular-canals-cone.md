## Why Ingested

This is the original/foundational sectional-imaging study of the bifid mandibular canal (BMC) — the abstract itself states there had been "no systematic studies evaluating the bifid mandibular canal using sectional imaging" prior to this work. It applies the Nortjé et al. classification to cone beam CT (CBCT) data, establishing the CBCT-era incidence baseline (15.6%) that later cross-modality studies build on. The wiki already holds [[wiki/radiology/wamasing-2018-imaging-bifid-mandibular-canal]], which uses high-resolution MRI (a different modality) to characterize BMC and found a much lower incidence (6.4%) with BMCs largely invisible on CT/panoramic radiography — this 2010 CBCT paper is the earlier sectional-imaging anchor that the 2018 MRI paper's cross-modality comparison implicitly extends. Ingested now to fill that foundational gap and give the wiki's BMC cluster a CT-modality data point alongside the existing MRI one.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cone beam CT (CBCT) study of 252 patients (301 mandible sides, imaged October 2004–September 2005) evaluating the incidence and configuration of the bifid mandibular canal (BMC) using the Nortjé et al. classification.

BMC was present in 47/301 sides (15.6%) — Type I in 2, Type II in 40, Type III in 0, Type IV in 5 — with the accessory canal ≥50% of the main canal's diameter in 23 cases and <50% in 24 cases.

CBCT detected BMC at a markedly higher rate than prior panoramic-radiograph studies, supporting CBCT as a suitable modality for detailed pre-surgical evaluation of BMC in the posterior mandible; abstract-only source limits verification of methodology detail beyond what is stated.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First described-as-systematic evaluation of the bifid mandibular canal (BMC) using sectional (cone beam CT) imaging, rather than 2D panoramic radiography.
- Applies the established Nortjé et al. four-type classification scheme to CBCT data, and additionally grades accessory-canal diameter relative to the main canal (≥50% vs <50%).
- Reports a substantially higher BMC incidence (15.6%) than earlier panoramic-radiograph-based studies, attributed to CBCT's superior sectional resolution.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Retrospective review of CBCT scans from 252 patients (301 evaluable mandible sides) imaged between October 2004 and September 2005. Each CBCT dataset was assessed for presence and configuration of a bifid mandibular canal. Bifurcation patterns were classified into the four Nortjé et al. types (I–IV). Where a bifid canal was present, the accessory canal's diameter was further categorized as either ≥50% or <50% of the main mandibular canal's diameter. (No further detail on scanner model, reader number/blinding, or statistical methods is available beyond the abstract.)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 47 of 301 mandible sides (15.6%) showed a bifid mandibular canal.
- Type distribution: Type I — 2 cases; Type II — 40 cases; Type III — 0 cases; Type IV — 5 cases.
- Accessory canal diameter: ≥50% of main canal in 23 cases; <50% of main canal in 24 cases.
- This 15.6% incidence is described by the authors as markedly higher than previously reported using panoramic radiography.

## 7. Glossary

- **Bifid mandibular canal (BMC)**: An anatomic variant in which the mandibular canal (containing the inferior alveolar neurovascular bundle) splits into two or more branches.
- **Cone beam CT (CBCT)**: A 3D sectional radiographic imaging modality commonly used in dentomaxillofacial diagnostics, offering higher spatial detail of bony canals than 2D panoramic radiography.
- **Nortjé et al. classification**: A four-type (I–IV) classification scheme describing different bifurcation configurations of the mandibular canal.
