## Why Ingested

User requested evidence on blood-supply loss of alveolar bone after periosteal elevation (골막 박리에 따른 치조골 혈행 손실); this SR is the review backbone of that topic — its introduction explicitly frames mucoperiosteal flap reflection as "compromis[ing] the vascular supply of bone," leading to crestal bone loss, and it aggregates the flapped-vs-flapless clinical-parameter literature (pooling [[implants/soft-tissue/kim-2009-blood-vessels-peri-implant-mucosa]] as one of its 10 included studies) on the soft-tissue axis that [[implants/chrcanovic-2014-flapless-vs-conventional-implant-surgery-ma]]'s failure/MBL axis does not cover.

## Three-line Summary

PRISMA systematic review (PubMed/Medline, Embase, Cochrane; search updated May 2016) of 10 prospective controlled studies — 6 animal, 4 human, 334 implants with 3 failures — comparing clinical parameters of implants placed in healed sites with flapped vs flapless surgery.

Animal studies consistently favored the flapless technique on every analyzed parameter (probing depth 1.0 vs 1.7 mm, inflammation 0/12 vs 5/12 implants, less recession at 4–8 weeks), while human studies showed only a trend toward flapless (significantly higher probing depth and gingival index in flap groups) with no consensus.

The flapless advantage is short-term, soft-tissue-level, and animal-dominated evidence; human data remain too sparse for consensus, so the authors call for more human research.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review focused on **clinical parameters** (gingival index, plaque index, probing depth, recession, mucosal/epithelial attachment height, keratinized gingiva, papilla index) of flapped vs flapless implants in healed sites — the two prior SRs (e.g. Vohra et al.) covered only marginal bone loss.
- Separates the **animal and human evidence streams**, assessing them with dedicated risk-of-bias tools: SYRCLE's tool (10 entries) for animal studies, the Cochrane RoB tool (7 domains) for human studies.
- States the mechanistic rationale verbatim: reflection of the mucoperiosteal flap **compromises the vascular supply of bone**, which may lead to crestal bone loss and long-term aesthetic complications — the basis for flapless surgery preserving peri-implant circulation and accelerating recovery.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: PRISMA systematic review, narrative synthesis (no meta-analysis); animal and human studies analyzed separately.
- **Databases**: PubMed/Medline, Embase, Cochrane Library; English-language dental journals; search updated May 2016.
- **n**: 889 initial hits → 42 after title/abstract → **10 included studies** (6 animal experimental, 4 human clinical); 334 implants total (range 22–60 per study), 3 implant failures; all single/partial rehabilitation; all mandibular except one study (Bashutski et al.).
- **Inclusion**: prospective controlled design comparing flap vs flapless in healed sites, >10 implants per group, ≥1 clinical parameter, survival rate specified, ≥1 week follow-up. **Exclusion**: case reports, SRs/technical notes, immediate placement, simultaneous bone regeneration.
- **Outcomes**: animal — flap design, GI, mucosal height, epithelial attachment, recession, PD, inflammation; human — PI, GI, recession, PD, papilla index (PPI), keratinized gingiva (KG).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Study-level**: follow-up 8 weeks–24 months (one study only 1 week); flap design — 8 studies midcrestal flap with intrasulcular incision, 2 trapezoidal flap; flapless protocol — 8 studies tissue punch, 2 transmucosal without incision, 1 mini-incision of 5 mm.

**Animal studies (flapless favored overall)**:
- GI (1 study): postoperative inflammation score **0 (flapless) vs 0.9 (flap)**.
- PD (1 study): **1.0 mm (flapless) vs 1.7 mm (flap)**, significantly greater in flap group.
- Inflammation (Kim et al., included study): flapless sites **0/12** implants with inflammatory signs vs **5/12** flap sites red and oedematous.
- Mucosal height: thicker at flap sites in one study (3.5 vs 2.2 mm), but significantly higher at lingual flapless sites in another; epithelial attachment higher with flapless punch in one study (2.27 vs 1.38 mm; lowest 1.1 mm transmucosal), higher with flap in another (2.2 vs 1.2 mm), no difference in a third.
- Recession (1 study): no significant difference at 2 weeks; **less pronounced in flapless at 4 and 8 weeks** (minor epithelial contraction, better aesthetics).

**Human studies (trend to flapless, no consensus)**:
- PD (2 studies): **significantly higher in flap group** in both.
- GI (4 studies): significantly higher in flap groups at 3 and 9 months in 2 studies; decrease at 1 and 4 weeks in the other 2.
- PI (3 studies): higher in flapped implants, difference no longer significant at 15 months (2 studies) and at 1–2 weeks (1 study).
- KG (2 studies): significant between-group differences in one (mean 0.86 mm in flap group), both groups losing KG with greater flap-group loss over time; in the other, flapless 4.2→3.7 mm and flapped 4.5→4.0 mm by abutment connection, stable at 24 months.
- PPI (1 study): significantly larger increase in flapless group at 6 and 9 months; no difference in thin biotypes; thick-biotype flapless patients trended toward greater papilla fill at 9 months (not significant at 15 months). Recession: no significant differences at any time point.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No meta-analysis possible — heterogeneous parameters and study designs; risk of bias largely "unclear" because surgical trials cannot blind operators/patients.
- Evidence is animal-dominated (6/10 studies) with short-term follow-up; few human studies exist, so no consensus on which technique gives better human clinical parameters.
- Flapless drawbacks acknowledged: no visualization of anatomical landmarks/vital structures, potential thermal osseous damage from obstructed external irrigation, inability to contour bone or manage dehiscences/fenestrations, increased misplacement risk, keratinized gingiva loss — hence cautious patient selection based on surgeon skill and experience.

## 6. Related Work

- **Chrcanovic et al. 2014** ([[implants/chrcanovic-2014-flapless-vs-conventional-implant-surgery-ma]]): the wiki's flapless-vs-conventional meta-analysis on the failure/infection/marginal-bone-loss axis — this SR covers the complementary clinical-parameter axis.
- **Vohra et al.**: prior SR (10 clinical studies) concluding marginal bone loss is comparable between techniques, with 4 studies showing significantly less crestal bone loss in flapless groups; cited here as the review the authors extend beyond bone loss.
- **Kim et al. 2009** ([[implants/soft-tissue/kim-2009-blood-vessels-peri-implant-mucosa]]): included canine study whose inflammation data (0/12 vs 5/12) is pooled into this review's animal results.
- **Becker et al., Jeong et al., Lee et al.**: individual flapless-cohort studies cited in the introduction (minimal crestal bone changes at 2 years; greater peri-implant bone height at flapless sites; superior mucosal profile maintenance in 44 patients).

## 7. Glossary

- **Mucoperiosteal Flap**: full-thickness flap elevating periosteum off the alveolar bone to expose the ridge; its reflection is the maneuver that compromises bone vascular supply.
- **Flapless Surgery**: implant placement without flap elevation — via tissue punch, transmucosal drilling, or minimal incision.
- **GI (Gingival Index)**: gingival inflammation score around implants.
- **PD (Probing Depth)**: peri-implant sulcus probing depth.
- **KG (Keratinized Gingiva)**: width of keratinized tissue around the implant.
- **PPI (Papilla Index)**: scoring of interproximal papilla fill adjacent to implants.
