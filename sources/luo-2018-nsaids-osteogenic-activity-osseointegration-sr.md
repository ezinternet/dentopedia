## Why Ingested

This is the PRISMA/PROSPERO systematic review that provides the crucial COUNTERPOINT to the animal/in-vitro alarm about NSAIDs and osseointegration: human clinical studies show no effect while experimental animal and in vitro studies show impairment, and it frames the effect along a dose / duration / COX-2-selectivity axis. It balances the overview and directly complements the narrative review in [[wiki/drug/analgesics/etikala-2019-nsaids-periodontal-implant-therapy-review]].

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD42016051448; 1999-2018 search across Ovid/PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science; 875 titles → 79 full text → 13 included; 2 in vitro on titanium, 2 RCTs + 1 retrospective clinical, 7 animal; no meta-analysis due to heterogeneity) of NSAID effects on osseointegration.

Osseointegration is NOT negatively affected by NSAIDs in human clinical studies (two ibuprofen 600 mg 4×/day for 7 days RCTs showed no marginal-bone difference at 3 and 6 months), which contrasts with in vitro and animal data where long-term selective COX-2 inhibition reduced bone ingrowth, BIC, and density; short-term (2 weeks) did not.

Evidence is insufficient to conclude a relationship between NSAID use and early implant failure; most included studies are at high risk of bias and no human clinical study has tested a selective COX-2 inhibitor — a key gap.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD42016051448; 1999-2018 Ovid/PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science 검색; 875편 → 전문 79편 → 13편 포함; in vitro 2·RCT 2+후향 1·동물 7; 이질성으로 메타분석 없음)이 비스테로이드소염제 (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs, NSAIDs)의 골유착 (Osseointegration) 영향을 종합.

인체 임상연구에서는 골유착이 NSAIDs로 부정적 영향을 받지 않음 (ibuprofen 600 mg 1일 4회 7일 투여 RCT 2편에서 3·6개월 변연골 수준 차이 無) — 이는 in vitro·동물 데이터(장기 COX-2 선택적 억제가 골내성장·골-임플란트 접촉률 (Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC)·골밀도 감소; 단기 2주는 무영향)와 상반됨.

NSAID 사용과 조기 임플란트 실패의 관계를 결론짓기엔 근거 부족; 포함 연구 대부분 높은 편향위험, 인체에서 COX-2 선택적 억제제를 검증한 임상연구가 전무한 것이 핵심 공백.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Directly juxtaposes human clinical evidence against experimental (in vitro + animal) evidence and finds they point in opposite directions.
- Central message: osseointegration does not appear negatively affected by NSAIDs in human clinical studies, contrasting with in vitro and animal studies; insufficient evidence to link NSAID use to early implant failure.
- Reframes the question along a DOSE / DURATION / COX-2-SELECTIVITY axis rather than "NSAIDs yes/no".
- Identifies a key gap: no human clinical study has tested a selective COX-2 inhibitor on osseointegration.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
Systematic review following PRISMA with a protocol registered in PROSPERO (CRD42016051448). Four databases searched (Ovid, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science) over 1999-2018. Screening reduced 875 titles to 79 full-text articles to 13 included studies (2 in vitro on titanium surfaces, 2 clinical trials + 1 retrospective clinical study, 7 animal studies). No meta-analysis was performed because of heterogeneity in model type, agent, dose, duration, and outcome measures.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Human RCTs (Alissa; Sakka): ibuprofen 600 mg 4×/day for 7 days → NO significant difference in marginal bone level at 3 and 6 months.
- Human retrospective (Winnett): perioperative NSAID cohort (n=60) had 119 failed implants vs non-NSAID cohort (n=44) with 78 failures — associates NSAID exposure with failure.
- In vitro (titanium): Boyan et al. — NSAIDs reduced osteoblast PGE2 on rough Ti (PGE2 favorable for proliferation/differentiation); Arpornmaeklong — indomethacin + celecoxib inhibited rat calvarial osteoblast growth dose-dependently (celecoxib dose-dependent).
- Animal: long-term selective COX-2 (meloxicam 3 mg/kg/day 60 d; rofecoxib 12.5 mg/kg/day continuous 6 wk) significantly reduced bone ingrowth / BIC / bone density → failed osseointegration; SHORT-term (2 wk) rofecoxib/meloxicam did NOT. COX-2-knockout mice (Chikazu): minimal BIC. Diclofenac 5 d delayed peri-implant healing (Pablos) but not in Cai / Salduz. Animal data inconsistent; effect depends on dose, duration, COX-2 selectivity.
- Prior SRs: Gomes et al. — osseointegration impaired by conventional NSAIDs; Kalyvas et al. — short-term post-op NSAIDs do not negatively impact. Both agree prolonged / long-term use impairs.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Most included studies at HIGH risk of bias.
- No human clinical study has tested a selective COX-2-selective inhibitor on osseointegration — the exact agent class implicated in animal harm is untested in humans.
- Animal pharmacokinetics differ from human, limiting translation of the animal harm signal.
- Heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis; conclusions are qualitative.
- Future work: adequately powered human trials of selective COX-2 inhibitors, standardized dose/duration reporting, and long-term osseointegration outcomes.

## 7. Glossary
- PRISMA — Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses; reporting standard for systematic reviews.
- PROSPERO — international prospective register of systematic review protocols (this review: CRD42016051448).
- BIC — Bone-to-Implant Contact; the proportion of implant surface in direct contact with bone, a key osseointegration metric.
- PGE2 — Prostaglandin E2; a prostaglandin (COX product) favorable to osteoblast proliferation and differentiation in bone repair.
- COX-2 — Cyclooxygenase-2; the inducible cyclooxygenase upregulated in early bone repair; target of selective NSAIDs (e.g., celecoxib, rofecoxib, meloxicam).
- Bone harvest chamber — an experimental device implanted in animals to sample and quantify new bone ingrowth for measuring osseointegration.
