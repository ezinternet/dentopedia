## Why Ingested

Establishes that high-speed coarse-grit diamond bur grinding of 3Y-TZP zirconia without subsequent polishing induces the highest degree of tetragonal→monoclinic (t→m) phase transformation and lowest flexural strength, while polishing after any grinding reduces surface roughness and partially reverses monoclinic phase accumulation. Directly supports occlusal adjustment protocols for zirconia prostheses.

→ [[dental-materials/zirconia/lee-2019-the-effects-of-surface-grinding]]

## Three-line Summary

Surface grinding of 3Y-TZP zirconia with high-speed diamond burs induces tetragonal-to-monoclinic phase transformation and increased surface roughness; coarse-grit grinding without polishing produces the most severe changes. Subsequent polishing with an NTI ceramic polisher significantly reduces both monoclinic phase fraction and surface roughness in the coarse-bur group. All groups retained flexural strength >500 MPa, meeting ISO 6872 clinical acceptability, but coarse grinding without polishing produced significantly lower strength than polished groups.

## 세줄요약

고속 다이아몬드 버로 3Y-TZP를 연삭하면 정방정→단사정(t→m) 상변환과 표면 거칠기가 증가하며, 연마 없이 조립 버를 사용한 경우 변화가 가장 심하다. NTI 세라믹 폴리셔로 연마하면 단사정상 비율과 표면 거칠기가 유의하게 감소한다. 모든 군이 500 MPa 이상의 굴곡강도를 유지해 임상적으로 허용 가능하지만, 연마 없이 조립 버를 사용하면 연마군보다 유의하게 낮은 강도를 보인다.

## Key Contributions

1. Demonstrates that high-speed coarse-grit diamond bur grinding (150 µm, Komet Zr 6881) without polishing causes significantly higher t→m phase transformation and lower flexural strength compared to polished groups.
2. Polishing after coarse grinding effectively reduces monoclinic phase fraction by removing the shallow surface layer where monoclinic phase concentrates.
3. Fine-grit bur grinding (50 µm, Komet Zr 8881) produces less phase transformation; additional polishing shows no statistically significant benefit in the fine-bur condition.
4. All four treatment groups exceeded the ISO 6872 clinical threshold of 500 MPa for posterior dental ceramics.
5. XRD confirms t→m transformation (not rhombohedral) across all groups, with Xm ordered: Control > Group 3 ≥ Group 1 ≥ Group 2.

## Methodology

**Design**: In vitro, four-group comparative study (n=10/group)

**Specimen fabrication**: 40 disc-shaped specimens (∅15 mm × 1.25 mm) with central cylindrical protrusion (∅3 mm × 1 mm) milled from 3Y-TZP monolithic block (Prettau, Zirkonzahn) via CAD/CAM to simulate localized occlusal contact.

**Groups**:
| Group | Bur | Polish |
|-------|-----|--------|
| Control | Coarse (150 µm, Komet Zr 6881) | None |
| Group 1 | Coarse (150 µm) | Yes |
| Group 2 | Fine (50 µm, Komet Zr 8881) | Yes |
| Group 3 | Fine (50 µm) | None |

**Grinding protocol**: High-speed handpiece at 300,000 rpm with water spray; protrusion removed until thickness 1.25 ± 0.02 mm. **Polishing protocol**: Low-speed handpiece at 20,000 rpm, 10 seconds, dry (NTI ceramic polisher).

**Measurements**:
- Surface morphology: FE-SEM (Hitachi S4700)
- Surface roughness Ra: 3D-OM (Nikon LV150L)
- Phase transformation: XRD (X'pert powder, PANalytical); Garvie-Nicholson method for monoclinic fraction (Xm)
- Flexural strength: Biaxial flexural strength (ISO 6872); universal testing machine (DSC-500, Shimadzu), 0.5 mm/min

**Statistics**: One-way ANOVA + Games-Howell post-hoc (P < .05)

## Key Results

**Surface roughness** (Ra, ordered highest to lowest): Control > Group 3 > Group 1 > Group 2

**Phase transformation (Xm, monoclinic fraction)**:
- All groups showed t→m transformation
- Order: Control > Group 3 ≥ Group 1 ≥ Group 2
- Control significantly higher than Groups 1 and 2 (P < .001)

**Flexural strength** (ordered highest to lowest): Group 2 ≥ Group 1 ≥ Group 3 ≥ Control
- Control significantly lower than Groups 1 and 2 (P < .001 implied)
- Fine-bur groups (2 vs 3): no significant difference from polishing
- **All groups >500 MPa** — clinically acceptable per ISO 6872

**Fracture morphology**: Polished groups (1, 2) showed crack initiation from surface progressing inward; unpolished groups (Control, 3) showed fractures originating from ground surface defects.

**Interpretation**: Polishing removes the superficial monoclinic-enriched layer created by coarse grinding, restoring tetragonal phase dominance and improving strength. Fine bur creates less surface damage, so polishing benefit is marginal.

## Limitations

- Single clinician performed grinding/polishing; pressure variability despite controlled protocol
- No fatigue strength testing; biaxial flexural strength does not fully replicate masticatory cyclic loading
- Disc geometry does not fully replicate crown anatomy
- One brand of 3Y-TZP (Prettau/Zirkonzahn); generalizability to other formulations uncertain
- No assessment of aging (hydrothermal low-temperature degradation) after grinding

## Glossary

- **3Y-TZP**: 3 mol% yttria-stabilized tetragonal zirconia polycrystal — most common dental zirconia
- **t→m phase transformation**: tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation; accompanied by 3–5% volume expansion that creates compressive stress (toughening mechanism) but can weaken if excessive
- **Xm**: monoclinic phase fraction by Garvie-Nicholson XRD method
- **Biaxial flexural strength**: piston-on-three-ball test per ISO 6872 for dental ceramics
- **Ra**: average surface roughness
- **Garvie-Nicholson method**: XRD peak-intensity formula to calculate monoclinic volume fraction
