## Why Ingested

이 SR은 2024년 8월 AO/AAP Consensus Conference(Oakbrook, IL)를 위해 위촉된 8편의 사전위촉 SR 중 하나로, 이미 위키에 있는 상위 합의문 [[implants/peri-implantitis/wang-2025-ao-aap-consensus-peri-implant-diseases-conditions]]가 인용하는 "수술적·임플란트 관련 위험요인" 근거 원본이다. 합의문의 요약 서술(implant malposition, unfavorable prosthetics 등)을 실제 OR/HR 수치와 개별 연구 단위로 뒷받침하기 위해 ingest.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (34 studies; 21 surgical-factor studies with 2752 patients/7591 implants, 13 implant-factor studies with 1192 patients/4072 implants; MEDLINE/PubMed + Cochrane Library search through October 2023) qualitatively synthesizing surgical- and implant-related predictors of peri-implant mucositis and peri-implantitis.

Inadequate three-dimensional implant position (malposition) was the strongest and most consistent surgical-related risk factor for peri-implantitis (e.g., Canullo OR=48.2; Schuldt-Filho implant-to-implant distance <3mm OR=8.6x; Kumar apico-coronal depth ≥6mm OR=8.5), while implant macro-/micro-design (system, surface roughness, tissue- vs bone-level) showed no consistent superiority across comparative studies; a short (<1.5mm) restorative-margin-to-crestal-bone distance emerged as a possible implant-related predisposing factor (OR≈2.3).

High study heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis (qualitative synthesis only, PROSPERO CRD501977); clinicians should prioritize precise 3D implant positioning and consider tissue-level/transmucosal-abutment designs, since implant-related factors' evidence base remains too sparse/inconsistent to recommend a specific system or surface.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰(34편; 수술요인 21편[환자 2752명/임플란트 7591개], 임플란트요인 13편[환자 1192명/임플란트 4072개]; 2023년 10월까지 MEDLINE/PubMed+Cochrane 검색)로 임플란트주위점막염·임플란트주위염의 수술적·임플란트 관련 예측인자를 정성적으로 종합.

부적절한 3차원 임플란트 위치(오식립)가 임플란트주위염의 가장 강력하고 일관된 수술 관련 위험요인이었고(Canullo OR=48.2; 임플란트간 거리<3mm 시 Schuldt-Filho OR=8.6배; Kumar 치조정하 깊이≥6mm OR=8.5), 임플란트 거대/미세디자인(시스템·표면거칠기·조직수준 vs 골수준)은 비교연구 전반에서 일관된 우위를 보이지 못했으며, 보철변연-치조정골 거리<1.5mm가 임플란트 관련 소인 가능성으로 부상(OR≈2.3).

연구간 이질성이 커서 메타분석은 불가했고(정성적 종합만, PROSPERO CRD501977), 임상적으로는 정밀한 3차원 임플란트 식립과 조직수준/경점막 지대주 디자인 고려가 우선이며, 임플란트 관련 요인의 근거는 아직 특정 시스템·표면을 권고할 만큼 충분·일관되지 않다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Synthesizes 34 clinical studies (RCTs, prospective/retrospective cohorts, cross-sectional, case-control) linking specific surgical- and implant-related exposures to peri-implant mucositis/peri-implantitis incidence, prevalence, and OR/HR.
- Identifies **implant malposition** (3D position relative to bone envelope and adjacent implants/teeth) as the most robust surgical-related predictor of peri-implantitis, with effect sizes ranging from OR 2.6 up to OR 48.2 across studies.
- Identifies a **short restorative-margin-to-crestal-bone distance (<1.5mm)** as a plausible implant-related predisposing factor for peri-implantitis (OR=2.29), supporting tissue-level/transmucosal-abutment designs over deep subcrestal placement of bone-level implants.
- Finds **no consistent evidence of superiority** for any specific implant macro-design (tissue- vs bone-level), micro-design (surface roughness, hybrid vs fully rough, laser-microtextured collar), or implant system in preventing peri-implant disease — comparative RCTs/cohorts were largely null or contradictory.
- Notes bone regeneration/grafting at implant placement carries a modest incremental peri-implantitis risk (OR=2.35, and 2.3x for failed regeneration specifically), attributed to the difficulty of fully restoring buccal bone dimension and periosteal/endosteal blood supply after augmentation.
- Surgical timing (immediate vs early vs delayed placement) showed inconsistent associations — some studies found immediate placement riskier (Jemt 1.8x; Ortiz-Echeverri OR=2.61), others found no difference or even favorable outcomes with immediate placement + immediate provisionalization.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **PECOS framework**: Population = adult humans with ≥1 functioning implant diagnosed with mucositis/peri-implantitis per AAP/EFP 2017 World Workshop or clear composite clinical+radiographic criteria; Exposure/Comparison = surgical- and/or implant-related local confounders; Outcome = incidence/prevalence/progression rate of peri-implant disease, plus OR/HR for mucositis and peri-implantitis; Study design = RCTs, prospective/retrospective cohorts, case-control, ≥10 patients, ≥12-month follow-up after final prosthesis delivery.
- **Search**: MEDLINE via PubMed + Cochrane Library, through October 2023, no language/year restriction; supplemented by Cochrane Grey Literature Database (New York Academy of Medicine, per AMSTAR checklist) and citation-chasing of included studies/reviews.
- **Screening**: Single reviewer (AM) title/abstract screen; second reviewer (SB) full-text assessment; disagreements resolved by discussion, third-author (HLW/PSR) arbitration if unresolved.
- **Data extraction (duplicate, 2 independent reviewers)**: citation/year, experimental group, sample size, factor, case definition, mucositis/peri-implantitis rate at patient- and implant-level, OR/HR, concluding remarks.
- **Screening yield**: 4296 records after de-duplication → 71 full-text assessed → 34 included in qualitative synthesis (21 surgical-factor studies, 13 implant-factor studies).
- **Synthesis**: Narrative/qualitative only — meta-analysis was not performed due to high cross-study heterogeneity in case definitions, exposures, and designs.
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB2 tool for RCTs (low / some concerns / high); NIH quality assessment tool for cohort/case-series/cross-sectional studies (score out of 14 or 10).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Surgical-related factors (Table 1, n=21 studies)

| Author (year) | Factor | Design | n patients / implants | Key finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canullo (2016/2017) | Implant position (malposition) | Retrospective case-control | 63 / 232 (2016); 53 / 231 (2017) | OR=48.2 (95% CI 11.4–204.1) for inadequate position; malposition-related peri-implantitis = 47% (2016) and 17.1% (2017) of all peri-implantitis cases |
| Canullo (2016) | Bone regeneration (failed reconstruction) | Retrospective case-control | — | OR=2.3; failed-regeneration-related peri-implantitis = 58.2% of cases |
| Schuldt-Filho (2014) | Implant-to-implant distance <3mm | Retrospective cohort | 27 / 161 | OR=8.6 (peri-implantitis rate 8.6x vs ≥3mm apart); implant-level OR=2.98 (95% CI 1.34–7.70, p=0.006) |
| Kumar (2018) | Apico-coronal position ≥6mm from adjacent CEJ | Retrospective case-control | 86 / 222 | OR=8.5 (95% CI 6.7–32.6, p<0.05) |
| Obreja (2021) | Subcrestal position (1–3mm) | Cross-sectional | 200 / 657 | Highest mucositis rate (66.5% patient-level); OR=5.33 in periodontitis-history patients (95% CI 0.22–0.94, p=0.003) |
| Jemt (2017) | Immediate vs staged placement | Retrospective cohort | 1017 / 3082 | OR=1.88 (immediate); OR=1.32 for number of implants |
| Ortiz-Echeverri (2023) | Immediate placement / deficient site / short implant (≤11mm) | Cross-sectional | 155 / 279 | Immediate placement OR=2.61 (95% CI 1.4–5.1, p=0.004); deficient alveolar width OR=2.9 (95% CI 1.8–4.6, p<0.001); implant length ≤11mm OR=2.4 |
| Obreja (2021) | Simultaneous CTG (connective tissue graft) | Cross-sectional | 19 vs 36 | Protective: lower BOP (p=0.02) and PPD (p=0.01) with graft |
| Bienz (2023) | Soft tissue grafting | Retrospective case-control | 8 vs 10 | No clear benefit shown (small n) |
| Zhang (2020) | Thick vertical soft tissue (periodontitis-history patients) | Prospective cohort | 65 / 159 | OR=2.5 — thicker crestal soft tissue was adverse, not protective, in periodontitis-susceptible patients |
| Kolerman (2023) | Simultaneous GBR + immediate placement | Retrospective cohort | 42 / 75 | Effective for mandibular incisors (low complication rate) |
| Kütan (2014) RCT | Subcrestal (1mm) vs crestal position | RCT | 28 vs 28 | More marginal bone resorption after year 3 with subcrestal, but not associated with biological complications (low RoB) |

### Overall risk-of-bias

- Surgical-factor studies: cross-sectional/cohort/case-series fulfilled ~60% of NIH quality items (weakest: sample-size justification, blinding, uniformity); RCTs generally low risk of bias.
- Implant-factor studies: ~70% of NIH items fulfilled; 25% of RCTs low risk of bias, 75% had blinding-related concerns.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No meta-analysis performed — high clinical/methodological heterogeneity across case definitions (mucositis/peri-implantitis thresholds varied from 0.5–3mm MBL across studies, pre- vs post-2017 AAP/EFP World Workshop definitions) precluded pooling.
- Most surgical-factor evidence is retrospective/cross-sectional (confounding, recall/selection bias); only 2 RCTs among the 21 surgical-factor studies.
- Implant-related factor evidence is sparse (13 studies) and largely comparative-null — insufficient power/duration to detect true differences between implant systems/surfaces.
- Single-reviewer initial screening (AM) with second-reviewer full-text check, rather than dual independent screening from the outset — some risk of missed studies.
- Restorative-margin-to-crestal-bone distance finding (OR=2.29) derives from a single referenced study within the discussion rather than a dedicated table row — flagged here as the paper's own hedge ("some of the evidence would suggest").
- Authors call for standardized case definitions and prospective, adequately powered studies isolating single surgical/implant variables to strengthen causal inference.

## 7. Glossary

- **AO/AAP**: Academy of Osseointegration / American Academy of Periodontology.
- **PECOS**: Population, Exposure, Comparison, Outcomes, Study design — systematic review inclusion framework (adapted PICO).
- **PROSPERO**: International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews.
- **BOP/SUP**: Bleeding on probing / suppuration.
- **MBL**: Marginal bone loss.
- **PPD**: Pocket probing depth.
- **NIH quality assessment**: US National Institutes of Health tool for rating quality of case-series, cohort, and cross-sectional studies.
- **RM-AC**: Restoration margin to alveolar bone crest distance — a radiographic risk marker (see basak-2024, IDRA framework).
- **GBR**: Guided bone regeneration.
- **CTG**: Connective tissue graft.
