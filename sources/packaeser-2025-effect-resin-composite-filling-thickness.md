## Three-line Summary
In-vitro fatigue/monotonic bench study (n=100 lithium disilicate discs bonded to zirconia abutments) comparing 5 groups of screw-access-hole sealing composite thickness (Ctrl/PTFE-only, +0.5mm, +1.0mm, +1.5mm, +2.0mm).

No difference in monotonic fracture load across groups, but cyclic fatigue failure load was significantly higher for 1.5mm and 2.0mm groups vs Ctrl/0.5mm/1.0mm (0.5mm: 1093N = Ctrl: 1120N = 1.0mm: 1127N < 1.5mm: 1426N = 2.0mm: 1307N, p≤0.05).

Clinical implication: more than half of the screw-access hole depth (>1.5mm) should be filled with resin composite to improve fatigue resistance of lithium disilicate crowns cemented over zirconia abutments; abstract-only, no PDF/PMC full text, so methodological detail beyond the abstract is not verifiable here.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Quantifies a dose-response relationship between screw-access-hole sealing composite thickness and cyclic fatigue failure load in zirconia-abutment/lithium-disilicate restorations.
- Identifies a threshold (>1.5 mm of composite fill) above which fatigue resistance significantly improves, versus no benefit from thinner fills (0.5–1.0 mm) relative to PTFE-only control.
- Shows monotonic (single-load-to-fracture) testing fails to detect this effect — only cyclic fatigue testing reveals the difference, underscoring that static bench tests may not predict clinical (fatigue) performance for this protocol.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In-vitro laboratory bench study (monotonic + cyclic fatigue mechanical testing, plus finite element analysis and SEM)
- **Databases**: N/A (not a systematic review)
- **n**: 100 lithium disilicate discs (Ø10mm, 1mm thick; IPS e.max CAD) bonded to zirconia abutments (Ø10mm, 3mm thick, 2.5mm screw-access hole; IPS e.max ZirCAD MO), 5 groups; monotonic n=5/group, fatigue n=15/group
- **Outcomes**: Monotonic crack-initiation load (1.0 mm/min loading rate); cyclic fatigue failure load (staircase protocol: 100N × 5000 cycles, then +100N every 10,000 cycles at 20Hz) via Kaplan-Meier/Mantel-Cox survival analysis

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Monotonic testing: no significant differences between groups (One-way ANOVA/Tukey).
- Fatigue failure load ranking: 0.5mm (1093N) = Ctrl (1120N) = 1.0mm (1127N) < 1.5mm (1426N) = 2.0mm (1307N), p ≤ 0.05.
- Groups sealed with ≤1.0mm composite (or PTFE tape alone) performed statistically the same; groups with ≥1.5mm composite fill performed significantly better in fatigue.

## 7. Glossary
- **Screw Access Hole (SAH)**: The channel through a screw-retained (or hybrid cement-retained-over-screw-retained-abutment) restoration that allows access to the abutment/prosthetic screw, typically sealed with PTFE tape and resin composite after screw tightening.
- **Fatigue Strength (피로강도)**: The maximum cyclic load a material/restoration can withstand for a defined number of loading cycles before failure, as distinct from monotonic (single-load) fracture strength.
