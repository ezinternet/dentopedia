## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PRISMA; 13 systematic reviews encompassing 178 primary studies) of root migration after mandibular third molar coronectomy — prevalence, magnitude, timing, determinants, and clinical significance (abstract-only — full text not retrieved).

Migration prevalence ranged from 6% to nearly 100%; it began within 3-6 months, was fastest in the first postoperative year, and stabilised thereafter, with mean distances of 2-4 mm; younger age, female sex, and specific root/impaction characteristics predicted greater migration, and excessive displacement occasionally caused root exposure, pain, and secondary surgery.

Authors frame "controlled root migration" and propose a conceptual (unvalidated) risk stratification model to personalise follow-up and minimise secondary interventions; the abstract reports no pooled estimate, AMSTAR 2 appraisal, or overlap statistics.

## 1. Document Information

- **Type**: Umbrella review (systematic review of systematic reviews), PRISMA-guided
- **Journal**: Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery 2026;30(1), epub 2026-04-29
- **Included**: 13 systematic reviews, 178 primary studies

## 2. Key Contributions

- Consolidates SR-level evidence on root migration after coronectomy (prevalence, distance, timing, predictors, clinical consequences).
- Introduces "controlled root migration" and a conceptual risk stratification model integrating key predictors.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Umbrella review per PRISMA; databases searched for systematic reviews reporting root migration after mandibular third molar coronectomy; data extracted on prevalence, magnitude, timing, associated factors, clinical outcomes. Quality appraisal tool, overlap handling, and registration not stated in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 13 SRs / 178 primary studies.
- Prevalence 6% to nearly 100%.
- Onset within 3-6 months; fastest in first postoperative year; stabilises thereafter.
- Mean migration distance generally 2-4 mm.
- Greater migration: younger age, female sex, specific root or impaction characteristics.
- Most migrated roots asymptomatic; excessive displacement occasionally → root exposure, pain, secondary surgery.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Abstract-only: no effect sizes for predictors, no definition of migration, no AMSTAR 2 or overlap (CCA) data available. Prevalence range (6% to ~100%) indicates heterogeneity in definition/imaging. The risk model is conceptual and unvalidated. Likely overlap with [[oral-surgery/third-molar/di-spirito-2025-re-intervention-rate-timing-indications]] primary studies (not verifiable from abstract).

## 7. Glossary

- Coronectomy (치관절제술): intentional crown removal leaving roots in situ to spare the IAN.
- Root migration (치근 이동): coronal drift of retained roots away from the nerve.
- Umbrella review: systematic review of systematic reviews.
