## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of lipophilic statins (simvastatin, lovastatin, atorvastatin) and their preclinical bone-anabolic effects via BMP-2/Smad, OPG/RANKL, and NF-κB signaling pathways relevant to implant osseointegration.
Hyperlipidemia impairs osseointegration through reduced osteoblastic function, increased osteoclastogenesis, and compromised collagen turnover; statins theoretically counteract these effects.
Human evidence remains inconclusive — modest BMD gains without confirmed fracture reduction; large-scale stratified RCTs with controlled statin delivery are needed.

## Summary

abstract-only — full text not retrieved

Statins, widely prescribed for dyslipidemia, may exert bone-modifying effects through coordinated stimulation of osteoblast activity and suppression of osteoclastogenesis. Lipophilic statins — simvastatin, lovastatin, and atorvastatin — enhance osteoblast differentiation and matrix synthesis via BMP-2/Smad and Ras-PI3K-Akt-Erk signaling in preclinical models, while attenuating osteoclast development through the OPG/RANKL axis, NF-κB inhibition, and p38 MAPK blockade. Human data remain inconclusive: modest BMD increases have been reported but no confirmed fracture risk reduction. In implant dentistry, hyperlipidemia is linked to impaired osseointegration via reduced osteoblastic function, increased osteoclast activity, and compromised collagen turnover. The interplay of obesity, lipid metabolism, and skeletal biology introduces confounders requiring patient stratification. Biological plausibility of statin-mediated peri-implant bone enhancement is supported, but definitive clinical translation awaits large-scale stratified trials with controlled delivery and extended follow-up.
