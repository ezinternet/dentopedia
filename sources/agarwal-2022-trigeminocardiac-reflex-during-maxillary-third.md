## Why Ingested

위키에는 미주신경성 실신(VVS) 쪽 근거는 이미 두껍지만([[drug/systemic-disease/li-2026-syncope-in-dentistry-risk-factors]], [[drug/systemic-disease/malave-2022-vasovagal-reactions-during-interventional-pain]]), "발치 중 갑자기 의식소실 + 서맥"을 VVS가 아니라 **삼차신경-심장 반사(Trigeminocardiac Reflex, TCR)**로 감별하는 쪽은 [[drug/systemic-disease/kumari-2025-hemodynamic-fluctuations-during-tooth-extraction]]의 혈역학 곡선뿐이었다. 이 증례보고는 상악 제3대구치 발치 중 발생한 2예로 TCR의 구심로(CN V2/V3)·원심로(미주신경)를 명시하고, **자극 제거 = 치료**라는 VVS와 다른 처치 분기를 chairside 수준에서 못박기 때문에 인제스트한다.

## Three-line Summary

Case report / small case series (n=2: 44-year-old and 29-year-old women, both systemically healthy) of sudden unresponsiveness during non-surgical extraction of a maxillary right third molar under local anesthesia, retrospectively attributed by the authors to trigeminocardiac reflex (TCR) rather than vasovagal syncope.

Case 1: pulse 33–42 bpm, respiratory rate 8–9/min, BP 90/70 mmHg immediately after extraction, recovering to HR 60–65 bpm and BP 100/80 mmHg by 30–40 min; Case 2: pulse unrecordable and no respiration on standing up after extraction, responding after one deep gasp, normal within 15–20 min (BP 110/70 mmHg) — both discharged the same day, neither needing atropine.

The teaching point is mechanistic and chairside: TCR (afferent CN V2/V3 → brainstem → vagal efferent) is treated first by stopping the surgical stimulus, whereas vasovagal syncope is managed positionally and psychologically — but with n=2, no ECG, and a retrospective label, this paper establishes the differential, not the incidence.

## 세줄요약

증례보고/소규모 증례군(n=2: 전신질환 없는 44세·29세 여성) — 국소마취 하 상악 우측 제3대구치 비외과적 발치 중 갑작스러운 무반응이 발생했고, 저자들은 이를 미주신경성 실신(Vasovagal Syncope, VVS)이 아닌 삼차신경-심장 반사(Trigeminocardiac Reflex, TCR)로 후향적으로 판정했다.

증례 1: 발치 직후 맥박 33–42회/분, 호흡 8–9회/분, 혈압 90/70 mmHg → 30–40분 뒤 HR 60–65회/분·BP 100/80 mmHg로 회복; 증례 2: 발치 후 일어나려다 맥박 촉지 불가·무호흡, 깊은 숨 한 번 후 반응 회복, 15–20분 내 정상(BP 110/70 mmHg) — 둘 다 당일 귀가, 아트로핀 불필요.

핵심은 기전과 처치 분기: TCR(구심성 CN V2/V3 → 뇌간 → 원심성 미주신경)은 **자극 중단이 1차 치료**이고 VVS는 체위·심리적 처치가 중심이다 — 다만 n=2, 심전도 미기록, 후향적 판정이라 이 논문이 확립하는 것은 감별의 틀이지 발생률이 아니다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Reports two consecutive episodes of sudden loss of consciousness with objective vital-sign collapse (HR 33–42 bpm; unrecordable pulse plus apnea) during **routine non-surgical maxillary third molar extraction** under local anesthesia — i.e. TCR without general anesthesia and without major surgery.
- Makes the mechanistic case that these events are TCR, not vasovagal syncope: the authors apply the classic >20% fall in mean arterial pressure and heart rate coinciding with trigeminal stimulation as the defining criterion.
- Lays out the reflex arc explicitly — short/long ciliary nerves → ciliary ganglion → gasserian ganglion → sensory nucleus of the trigeminal nerve in the floor of the fourth ventricle → internuncial fibers of the reticular formation → motor nucleus of the vagus → depressor fibers to the myocardium.
- States the treatment hierarchy that differs from VVS: identify bradycardia → **cease the procedure to withdraw the stimulus** (reverses most cases) → atropine or glycopyrrolate only if bradycardia is severe or persists after stimulus removal.
- Reinforces intraoperative vital-sign monitoring for even minor oral procedures, since neither patient reported any discomfort beforehand and the event was therefore unpredictable.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Case report / small case series (n=2), no protocol, no consecutive sampling, no control group; both cases treated at the authors' practice and described in narrative form.
- **Population**: Case 1 — 44-year-old female, insignificant medical history, grossly decayed maxillary right third molar, 2 days of premedication, non-surgical extraction with luxators and extraction forceps under local anesthesia. Case 2 — 29-year-old female, insignificant medical history, deeply carious maxillary right third molar, prior antibiotics/anti-inflammatories from a physician, non-surgical extraction with forceps after 2% lignocaine with adrenaline.
- **Data collected**: opportunistic chairside pulse, respiratory rate, and blood pressure during and after the collapse; both patients transferred to an intensive care unit for observation.
- **Outcome of interest**: onset/reversal of bradycardia, hypotension, apnea, and unconsciousness; response to stimulus withdrawal and supportive care.
- **Comparative frame**: narrative discussion against Schaller et al. (1999, intracranial trigeminal stimulation), oculocardiac reflex literature, Arakeri & Arali 2010 (400 extractions under local anesthesia; "dentocardiac reflex"), Arasho et al. (management algorithm), and Rashad et al. (heart-rate recording during extraction).
- **No statistics**: no denominators, no incidence estimate, no inferential testing.

| Intervention | Oxygen 5 L/min, IV line, ICU observation | ICU transfer and observation |
| Recovery | HR 60–65 bpm, RR 12–14/min, BP 100/80 mmHg at ~30–40 min | Vitals within normal limits, BP 110/70 mmHg at 15–20 min |
| Disposition | Discharged same day, fair condition | Discharged same day at patient's request, fair condition |
| Atropine / glycopyrrolate | Not given | Not given |

- Both episodes met the authors' applied criterion of a >20% negative change in vitals coinciding with trigeminal-nerve stimulation.
- In both cases the phenomenon reversed once the stimulus was removed; no further pharmacologic management was required.
- The authors note that Case 2 was initially managed as a presumed vasovagal syncope and only re-labelled as TCR in retrospect.
- Arakeri & Arali's 2010 series is cited as the precedent that TCR can occur under local anesthesia (their series covered 400 extractions under local anesthesia), not only under general anesthesia where sympathetic reflexes are depressed.
- The paper states that psychological variation is **not yet proven** to be a risk factor for TCR, while conceding that psychological control may still be desirable for safety.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **n=2, no denominator** — no incidence, no risk-factor estimate, and no comparison group; the two cases cannot establish how often TCR occurs during maxillary third molar extraction.
- **Retrospective diagnostic labelling** — no ECG, no continuous monitoring trace, and in Case 1 the BP/HR values are single chairside readings; in Case 2 the pulse was simply "not recordable". The distinction from vasovagal syncope therefore rests on the authors' interpretation, not on an objective electrophysiologic record.
- **Case 2 confounded by posture** — collapse occurred while rising from the chair, which is also the classic trigger of vasovagal/orthostatic syncope; the paper does not address this competing explanation.
- **No prodrome data** — absence of reported discomfort is used as evidence for unpredictability, but prodromal symptoms were not systematically elicited, and prodrome is a discriminator that favours VVS.
- **Internal inconsistency** — the abstract calls the pathophysiology of vasovagal syncope "fully elucidated", whereas the discussion says it "remains to be fully elucidated"; the paper also equates dentocardiac reflex, oculocardiac reflex, and TCR under one umbrella without resolving whether TCR-mediated syncope is a subset of VVS or a separate entity.
- **No treatment trial** — both patients recovered without atropine/glycopyrrolate, so the paper provides no data on when anticholinergics are needed beyond citing Arasho et al.
- **Future work called for**: research on the relationship between psychological state and TCR occurrence, and on prophylaxis/management, which the authors describe as still unsettled.

## 6. Related Work

- **Schaller et al. (1999)**: first demonstrated TCR from stimulation of the intracranial portion of the trigeminal nerve and consolidated the reflexes under the single term TCR.
- **Arakeri & Arali (2010)**: reported TCR after maxillary first molar extraction and studied 400 extractions under local anesthesia, coining "dentocardiac reflex" for TCR-mediated syncope.
- **Arasho et al.**: systematic management algorithm for TCR, reproduced as the paper's Figure 2.
- **Rashad et al.**: recorded heart rate during extractions and attributed intraoperative HR/BP changes to TCR, recommending monitoring.
- **Li 2026 / Malave 2022 (wiki)**: VVS-centred reviews whose differential this paper narrows by naming the surgical-stimulus (CN V2/V3) branch and its distinct first-line treatment.

## 7. Glossary

- **TCR (Trigeminocardiac Reflex, 삼차신경-심장 반사)**: sudden parasympathetic dysrhythmia, sympathetic hypotension, apnea, or gastric hypermotility triggered by stimulation of any sensory branch of the trigeminal nerve; classically defined as a >20% fall in mean arterial pressure and heart rate coinciding with trigeminal stimulation.
- **OCR (Oculocardiac Reflex, 안구-심장 반사)**: bradycardia from stimulation of the ophthalmic division (CN V1) during ocular surgery; the historical prototype of TCR.
- **Dentocardiac reflex (치성-심장 반사)**: term introduced by Arakeri & Arali for TCR-mediated syncope during dental extraction under local anesthesia.
- **VVS (Vasovagal Syncope, 미주신경성 실신)**: neurally mediated transient loss of consciousness with a psychogenic/orthostatic trigger, prodrome, and positional first-line management.
- **Gasserian ganglion (가서 신경절)**: the trigeminal ganglion, relay station on the TCR afferent path.
- **Internuncial fibers (개재신경섬유)**: small reticular-formation fibers connecting the trigeminal sensory nucleus to the vagal motor nucleus — the central link of the reflex arc.
- **Glycopyrrolate (글리코피롤레이트)**: anticholinergic alternative to atropine for severe or persistent reflex bradycardia.
