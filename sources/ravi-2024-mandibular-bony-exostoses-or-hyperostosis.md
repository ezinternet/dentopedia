## Why Ingested

무치악 환자에서 하악 골성 외골증(bony exostosis)이 MRONJ·osteoradionecrosis와 임상적으로 혼동될 수 있음을 다룬 증례로, 기존 [[wiki/drug/mronj/cho-2026-clinical-characteristics-osteonecrosis-jaw-related]]가 다루는 약제관련 악골괴사의 감별진단 축을 보강하기 위해 인제스트. 본 위키에 아직 exostosis/tori 관련 페이지가 없어 신규 주제로 추가.

## Three-line Summary

Case report, n=1, 70-year-old edentulous male with a lingual mandibular bony exostosis discovered on routine dental check-up.

Brownish-yellow, bony-hard, non-tender, sessile protuberance (~1×1.5 cm) at the lingual left mandibular second molar region; orthopantomogram showed a radiopaque mass blending with adjacent cortical bone; diagnosis based on history and clinical exam alone (no CBCT, no biopsy).

Careful history-taking (no bisphosphonate/radiotherapy exposure) distinguished this benign, treatment-not-required exostosis from osteonecrosis/osteoradionecrosis — a key limitation is the absence of confirmatory imaging or histopathology.

## 세줄요약

증례보고, n=1, 무치악 70세 남성에서 정기 치과검진 중 발견된 설측 하악 골성 외골증(exostosis).

좌측 하악 제2대구치 부위 설측에 갈색-노란색의 단단하고 무통성인 유경성 융기(약 1×1.5cm)가 관찰되었고, 파노라마 방사선사진에서 주변 피질골과 밀도가 유사한 방사선불투과 종괴가 확인되었으며, 병력·임상소견만으로 진단(CBCT·조직검사 미시행).

비스포스포네이트·방사선치료 병력 부재를 확인하는 세심한 병력청취로 이 양성 외골증을 골괴사(MRONJ)/방사선골괴사와 감별했으며, 확진 영상·조직검사가 없었다는 것이 핵심 한계다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Documents a rare presentation of a lingual mandibular bony exostosis in an edentulous elderly patient, discovered incidentally.
- Provides a compact differential-diagnosis table distinguishing bony exostoses from dense bone island, bone spur, chronic osteomyelitis, osteoma/osteochondroma, osteosarcoma, osteonecrosis, and osteoradionecrosis.
- Reinforces that history-taking (absence of bisphosphonate/antiresorptive or radiotherapy exposure) is often sufficient to rule out osteonecrosis/osteoradionecrosis in a benign exostosis presentation, without requiring biopsy.
- Summarizes epidemiologic background: oral exostosis prevalence ~14.4% (Sawair et al., Jordan, n=618), palatal exostosis 25–47.5% across dry-skull populations by ethnicity (Nery et al.), and 1.8% among twins (Auškalnis et al.).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Single clinical case description: history-taking, extraoral/intraoral clinical examination, palpation, and orthopantomography (OPG). No CBCT, no histopathology — diagnosis made on clinical + radiographic grounds alone, explicitly acknowledged as a limitation by the authors.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Lesion: ~1 × 1.5 cm, brownish-yellow, bony-hard, sessile, non-tender mass on the lingual aspect of the left mandibular second molar region, above the mylohyoid ridge.
- OPG: radiopaque mass whose density blends with adjacent cortical bone (a distinguishing radiographic feature from osteoma/osteochondroma, which show a dense outer cortical border, and from osteosarcoma's sunburst periosteal reaction).
- No treatment performed; patient asymptomatic and declined biopsy/excision.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single case; no CBCT or histopathologic confirmation — diagnosis is presumptive.
- No follow-up data on lesion growth or long-term outcome.
- Etiology of exostoses remains incompletely established; proposed mechanisms (occlusal overload, subpontic hyperostosis theory, genetic predisposition, gingival inflammation) are cited from prior literature, not tested in this case.

## 7. Glossary

- **Exostosis**: benign, non-neoplastic, slow-growing bony outgrowth on the cortical surface of the jaw.
- **Torus mandibularis / torus palatinus**: exostoses at specific anatomic sites (lingual mandibular premolar-canine region; hard palate midline, respectively).
- **Subpontic hyperostosis**: bone formation beneath a fixed-prosthesis pontic attributed to occlusal stress.
- **MRONJ**: medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw.
