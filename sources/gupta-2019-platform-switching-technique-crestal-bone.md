## Why Ingested

플랫폼 스위칭(Platform Switching, PLS)이 치조정골(crestal bone)을 보존하는 **기전**(inward IAJ shift, 염증세포침윤 이동, 생물학적 폭경 relocation)을 2005–2017년 문헌으로 정성적(qualitative) 종합한 서베일런스 리뷰. ## Three-line Summary

Qualitative/narrative systematic review (PubMed + Google Scholar, 2005–2017, no meta-analysis) of platform switching (PLS) and its effect on crestal bone loss around dental implants, synthesizing human clinical studies, immediate-placement case series, PS-vs-non-PS comparisons, and finite element analysis (FEA) reports.

Across the reviewed literature, PLS implants consistently showed less marginal/crestal bone loss than platform-matched implants (e.g., Hürzeler et al.: 0.22mm PLS vs 2.02mm non-PLS; Cappiello et al.: 0.95±0.32mm vs 1.67±0.37mm), attributed mechanistically to inward relocation of the implant-abutment junction (IAJ) shifting the inflammatory cell infiltrate away from crestal bone and increasing the microgap-to-bone distance.

The review concludes PLS is a major contributing factor limiting crestal bone resorption and should be used in clinical practice, but reports no pooled statistic of its own — its evidence base is individually-reported study numbers (mostly Biomet 3i implants, short follow-up ≤2 years) rather than a computed summary effect.

## 세줄요약

정성적(qualitative) 체계적 문헌고찰(PubMed + Google Scholar, 2005–2017, 메타분석 없음) — 플랫폼 스위칭(Platform Switching, PLS)이 임플란트 주위 치조정골소실(crestal bone loss)에 미치는 영향을 인체 임상연구·즉시식립 증례·PLS vs 비-PLS 비교·유한요소분석(Finite Element Analysis, FEA) 문헌으로 종합.

검토된 문헌에서 PLS 임플란트는 일반형(platform-matched) 임플란트보다 일관되게 적은 변연골/치조정골 소실을 보였음 (예: Hürzeler 등 — PLS 0.22mm vs 비-PLS 2.02mm; Cappiello 등 — 0.95±0.32mm vs 1.67±0.37mm); 기전은 임플란트-지대주 접합부(Implant-Abutment Junction, IAJ)의 내측 이동이 염증세포침윤을 치조정골에서 멀어지게 하고 미세간극(microgap)-골 거리를 늘리는 것으로 설명.

리뷰는 PLS가 치조정골 흡수를 제한하는 주요 요인이며 임상에서 사용되어야 한다고 결론짓지만, 자체 통합(pooled) 통계치는 제시하지 않음 — 근거는 개별 연구가 보고한 수치(대부분 Biomet 3i 임플란트, 추적기간 2년 이하)를 그대로 나열한 서술적 종합에 가까움.

## Key Contributions

- Articulates the mechanistic explanation for PLS's crestal bone-preserving effect: inward shift of the IAJ moves the inflammatory cell infiltrate (ICT) toward the implant's central axis and away from crestal bone; connective tissue thickens laterally with increased local blood flow; ICT remains confined above the implant platform level, so the biologic width does not need to relocate apically (i.e., no compensatory bone remodeling/loss).
- Table 1 tabulates nine human studies (as of the ~2009 evidence base cited) comparing PLS vs control bone resorption/change, implant systems, and abutment/implant diameter ratios (Canullo, Calvo-Guirado ×3, Prosper et al., Rodríguez-Ciurana, Cappiello, Canullo & Rasperini, Hürzeler).
- Separately reviews three literature clusters: (1) immediate implant placement + PLS (Canullo; Calvo-Guirado; Canullo & Rasperini; Baumgarten; Gardner), (2) PLS-vs-non-PLS head-to-head comparisons (Salamanca; Hürzeler; Cappiello), and (3) PLS effect on inter-implant distance/interproximal bone peak (Rodríguez-Ciurana, implants placed <3mm apart).
- Summarizes five FEA reports on PLS biomechanics, distinguishing butt-joint (Replace Select, 3i) vs taper-joint (Straumann, Astra, Ankylos) implant designs under vertical/diagonal loading (10–250N, 15°–30°).
- Discussion proposes combining Morse taper connections (which reduce microgap width) with PLS (which increases microgap-to-bone distance) as complementary strategies for crestal bone preservation.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Qualitative/narrative systematic review — search of PubMed and Google Scholar, 2005–2017, keywords "crestal bone preservation," "crestal bone loss," "PLS," "small diameter abutments," "immediate implant placement and PLS," and "loading in implants."
- **Exclusion criteria**: unpublished papers, letters to the editor, historical reviews, non-English case reports, studies with no results, and studies with abstract only (full article not yet published).
- **No PRISMA flow diagram, risk-of-bias/quality appraisal, or formal pooled meta-analysis is reported.** A "Statistical analysis" section states that collected data were processed in SPSS 20 with unpaired Student's t-test / ANOVA — but this describes methods used by the *individual included studies*, not a new pooled analysis performed by the review authors; the Results section is a narrative synthesis that reports each source study's own numbers and p-values as-is.
- Synthesis is organized around: mechanism (biologic width/IAJ), a summary table of 9 human studies, immediate-placement subgroup, PLS-vs-non-PLS subgroup, inter-implant-distance subgroup, and FEA subgroup.

## Key Results

- **Mechanism**: Ericsson et al. cited for the ~1mm healthy connective tissue zone encircling bone; Lazzara & Porter hypothesized that inward IAJ shift moves the inflammatory infiltrate away from crestal bone. Jansen et al. cited for inflammatory infiltrate extending ~0.5–0.75mm coronal and ~0.5–0.75mm apical to the IAJ, always separated from bone by ~1mm of healthy connective tissue.
- **Human studies table (as of the review's ~2009-anchored evidence base)**: most reports (6 of 9) used Biomet 3i (the PLS-originating) implant systems; longest observation ~2 years; most reports concluded PLS positively affects crestal bone preservation. Example values: Canullo — PLS 0.3±0.157mm vs control 1.19±0.138mm; Prosper et al. — 98.3% of PLS implants had no crestal bone loss at 12 months vs 66.1% control (p<0.001).
- **Immediate placement**: Canullo — PLS group 0.3±0.16mm resorption vs non-PLS 1.19±0.35mm (p<0.005) at ~25 months. Canullo & Rasperini — 0.78±0.36mm vs a 1.7mm reference value, over 18–36 months. Baumgarten — PLS helps prevent anticipated bone loss when ≥~3mm soft-tissue depth is available.
- **PLS vs non-PLS comparisons**: Hürzeler et al. — mean crestal bone loss 0.22mm (PLS) vs 2.02mm (non-PLS); concluded ≥0.45mm abutment reduction per side is sufficient to avoid peri-implant bone loss. Cappiello et al. — 0.95±0.32mm (PLS) vs 1.67±0.37mm (non-PLS). Salamanca et al. — PLS showed greater vertical (0.93±1mm, p<0.05) and horizontal (0.50±0.56mm) marginal bone gain than platform-matching (0.23±0.58mm / 0.22±0.53mm).
- **Inter-implant distance**: Rodríguez-Ciurana — 41 pairs of PLS implants placed <3mm apart showed mean vertical bone resorption 0.62mm, horizontal 0.60mm, with the interproximal bone peak extending 0.24mm coronal to the imaginary IAJ-to-IAJ line — PLS helped preserve the interproximal bone peak versus conventional restorations.
- **FEA**: five reports (as of ~2009) modeled butt-joint vs taper-joint implant/abutment designs under vertical and diagonal loading; findings framed as biomechanical support for PLS's stress-distribution rationale rather than new bone-loss quantification.
- **Conclusion**: "PLS helps to prevent the bone loss around the implants, and thus this concept can be used in clinical practice" whenever clinical situations permit.

## Limitations

- Search restricted to two databases (PubMed, Google Scholar) and to 2005–2017 — no Cochrane/EMBASE, no papers before 2005 or after 2017, so the review may miss both foundational early PLS work and later (2018+) higher-quality RCTs/SR+MAs.
- No PRISMA flow diagram, no explicit risk-of-bias or study-quality appraisal tool is described.
- **Not a meta-analysis** — no pooled effect size, no forest plot, no heterogeneity statistic (I²). Numbers quoted are lifted directly from each source study, which used heterogeneous implant systems, abutment/platform diameter ratios, follow-up durations (up to ~2 years), and measurement methods (digital X-ray in most cases) — not statistically comparable across studies.
- Majority of underlying human studies (6/9 in the core table) used Biomet 3i implants, the system that originated the PLS concept — a potential vendor/design concentration bias in the underlying evidence base the review synthesizes.
- Underlying primary evidence is largely small case series, cohort studies, and a few RCTs rather than large multicenter RCTs; the review does not independently re-verify or grade this evidence.
- The "Statistical analysis" (SPSS, unpaired t-test/ANOVA) described in Methods does not correspond to any statistic actually computed and reported by the review authors in Results — likely boilerplate describing how the *included* studies analyzed their own data.

## Glossary

- **Platform Switching (PLS)**: use of a smaller-diameter abutment on a larger-diameter implant platform/collar, shifting the implant-abutment junction (IAJ) inward toward the implant's central axis.
- **Implant-Abutment Junction (IAJ)**: the interface/microgap between the implant platform and the abutment.
- **Inflammatory Cell Infiltrate (ICT)**: the zone of inflammatory cells that forms around the IAJ microgap due to bacterial colonization; its position relative to crestal bone is central to the PLS mechanism.
- **Biologic width**: the dimension of soft tissue (junctional epithelium + connective tissue) that re-establishes itself around an implant/tooth; crestal bone remodels to accommodate it if the ICT encroaches too close to bone.
- **Finite Element Analysis (FEA)**: computational biomechanical modeling method used to study stress distribution in implant-abutment systems under loading, here used to evaluate PLS designs.
