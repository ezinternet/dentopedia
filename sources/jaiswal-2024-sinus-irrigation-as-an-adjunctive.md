## Why Ingested

The wiki's odontogenic maxillary sinusitis (OMS) surgical-management coverage — [[wiki/oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/kim-2019-definition-management-odontogenic-maxillary]] (Caldwell-Luc, FESS, MESS comparison) and [[wiki/overviews/odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-overview]] (source removal ± FESS) — holds no outcome data for the minimally invasive intraoral small-lateral-window irrigation option. This retrospective series (n=21) adds CBCT-volumetric and symptom outcomes for iatrogenic OMS (implant, extraction, sinus graft), extending that surgical-management picture and giving a quantitative anchor for the irrigation arm.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center case series (n=21, Pusan National University Dental Hospital, 2016–2022) of intraoral sinus irrigation through a small lateral window for odontogenic maxillary sinusitis not improved by antibiotics, with at least 2 months of follow-up.

Radiopacity on CBCT decreased in 16/21 patients (76.1%; 2 unchanged, 3 increased), mean reduction 40.15%, and symptoms improved in 17/21 (80.9%) with no worsening; extraction-related cases improved most (5/5) and implant-related cases least (5/7 radiographic).

Irrigation looks effective as a minimally invasive adjunct for iatrogenic OMS, but there was no control group, the sample was small, and follow-up was short, so no comparison with antibiotics alone or with Caldwell-Luc/FESS can be drawn.

## 세줄요약

후향적 단일기관 증례군 연구(n=21, 부산대학교치과병원, 2016–2022): 항생제에 호전되지 않은 치성 상악동염 (Odontogenic Maxillary Sinusitis, OMS) 환자에게 구강내 소형 측방창(small lateral window) 상악동 세척술을 시행하고 최소 2개월 추적.

CBCT 불투명도(radiopacity)는 21명 중 16명(76.1%)에서 감소(2명 불변, 3명 증가), 평균 감소율 40.15%; 증상은 17명(80.9%)에서 호전되었고 악화는 없었음; 발치 관련군은 5/5로 가장 좋았고 임플란트 관련군은 영상 5/7로 가장 낮았음.

의인성 OMS에서 최소침습 보조요법으로 효과가 있어 보이나, 대조군이 없고 표본이 작으며 추적이 짧아 항생제 단독이나 Caldwell-Luc·내시경 부비동 수술 (Functional Endoscopic Sinus Surgery, FESS)과의 우열은 판단할 수 없음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Reports volumetric (three-dimensional, ImageJ) CBCT quantification of opacified sinus volume before and after intraoral sinus irrigation, rather than 2D radiographic judgement alone.
- Stratifies radiographic and symptomatic response by iatrogenic etiology (implant-related, tooth extraction, sinus graft, other).
- Describes a modified window position: a small lateral window at least 10 mm above the alveolar crest (3-mm round bur), chosen to limit future restrictions for implant surgery or bone grafting, instead of the conventional thin area below the infraorbital foramen.
- Positions sinus irrigation as a less invasive alternative to Caldwell-Luc, which the authors say has longer hospital stay, bone-wall defects and postoperative maxillary cyst risk.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective review of an electronic database; single center. The abstract says patients were "surveyed"; the Discussion names the retrospective nature as a limitation.
- **Sample**: 21 patients with OMS who underwent sinus irrigation between 2016-01-02 and 2022-06-30 with at least 2 months of follow-up. Excluded: sinusitis due to medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ), cysts, polyps, tumors, and infections.
- **Selection context**: The Background states the aim as evaluating patients who did not improve with antibiotic therapy. All patients received antibiotics for 1 week (extended another week if improving); if no improvement, source removal and irrigation were selected.
- **Surgery**: Vestibular or intrasulcular incision with vertical releasing cuts, periosteal detachment, small lateral window (3-mm round bur, at least 10 mm above the alveolar crestal bone). Inflammatory sinus membrane and foreign bodies were removed, the sinus was irrigated with saline or iodine solution and packed for hemostasis, and antibiotics were prescribed afterwards. 16 patients had general anesthesia with nasotracheal intubation.
- **Imaging**: CBCT (ProMax, Planmeca; 20 x 19 cm field of view, 110 kVp, 4.0 mA, 24 s, 1-mm slices). Opaque area (S) measured by counting opaque pixels in ImageJ per slice; volume V = sum(S x h); degree of sinusitis = Vt0/Vs (opaque volume / total sinus volume), with Vt1 the post-irrigation opaque volume.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 16.0; independent t-test (male vs female age), chi-square (etiology associations); p < 0.05.
- **Protocol gaps (full-text re-read 2026-10-12)**: irrigation is described at surgery before packing only; no serial postoperative irrigation, irrigant volume, iodine concentration, device, postoperative antibiotic agent/duration, or packing-removal timing is stated. Anesthesia: general 16, monitored 2, local 3. Conventional window site (thinnest lateral area below the infraorbital foramen) was deliberately avoided in favour of ≥10 mm above the crest. Authors attribute rising opacity in implant/graft cases to bone particles not washed out.
- **Outcomes**: radiographic opacity volume change; clinical symptom improvement. The exact timing of the postoperative CBCT is not stated beyond the inclusion rule of at least 2 months of follow-up.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Demographics (n=21)**: 16 male (76.1%), 5 female (23.8%); mean age 53.71 +/- 15.27 years (range 26–80); male 51.9 +/- 14.1, female 59.4 +/- 19.1, difference not significant (p = 0.8). Anesthesia: general 16, monitored 2, local 3. Oroantral fistula present in 7 (33.3%).

**Etiology (Table 2)**: implant-related 7 (33.3%: implant placement 4, fixture removal 2, peri-implantitis 1); tooth extraction 5 (23.8%); bone graft after sinus augmentation 7 (33.33%); other 2 (9.52%: sinus invasion/fracture of the lateral wall, fixation plate invading the sinus after open reduction, endodontic material). Chi-square etiology vs patients p = 0.74.

**Radiographic response**: 16/21 (76.1%) decreased, 2 unchanged, 3 increased. Mean decrease in opacity 40.15% (abstract and Conclusion; the unit, relative vs percentage points, is not defined in the text). By etiology: implant 5/7 decreased and 2 increased; extraction 5/5 decreased; bone graft 5/7 decreased and 1 increased; other: fixation-plate case unchanged, endodontic-material case decreased. Etiology vs degree of improvement p = 0.29 (not significant).

**Pre-irrigation opaque volume ratio (share of sinus volume)**: implant 67.8%; extraction 69.7%; bone graft 81.6% (highest); other 44.6%.

**Post-irrigation volume ratios as printed in the text**: extraction 11.2% (described as the greatest drop); bone graft 27.4%; implant 41.8%; other 32.2% (described as the smallest reduction). The source sentence is garbled, so these values are read as post-irrigation ratios by inference; the pre-to-post pairing should be re-checked against Fig. 4 before citing.

**Clinical symptoms**: 17/21 (80.9%) improved, 4 (19%) did not, none worsened. By etiology: implant 6/7 (one patient had postoperative facial pain); extraction 5/5; bone graft 5/7 (2 continued to have discomfort); other: endodontic-material case improved, fixation-plate case did not.

**Authors' explanation for non-response**: opacity increased in some implant and graft patients possibly because bone particles were not easily washed out and remained in place.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Retrospective, single-center, n=21, no control group (no antibiotics-only, Caldwell-Luc or FESS comparator); effectiveness cannot be separated from concurrent source removal.
- Short and variable follow-up (minimum 2 months); long-term recurrence is not reported. The authors name long-term follow-up as a limitation.
- Mixed etiologies with 2 to 7 patients per group and no multivariable analysis; nonsignificant chi-square results (p = 0.29, p = 0.74) are underpowered to detect subgroup differences.
- Internal inconsistencies in the text: the implant subgroup is 3 perforation + 3 fixture removal + 1 peri-implantitis in the prose but 4 + 2 + 1 in Table 2 (Table 2 used here); the "other" group is said to be unchanged in one sentence but includes a decreased case in the next; the post-irrigation ratio sentence is ambiguous; the abstract says 5 females (23%) vs 23.8% in the Results.
- Conclusion states effectiveness for "acute odontogenic sinusitis caused by iatrogenic disease", but the cohort comprised patients refractory to antibiotics with at least 2 months of follow-up; the acute/chronic split is not reported.
- Most procedures (16/21) were done under general anesthesia, so the local-anesthesia, outpatient advantage claimed in the Background was shown in only 3 patients.
- Authors call for further studies to generalize results.

## 6. Related Work

- Kim SM 2019 (single-author narrative review, in wiki): compares Caldwell-Luc, FESS and MESS; this paper adds irrigation-specific outcome data.
- Gang et al. (cited in the Discussion): chronic maxillary sinusitis healed within 8–12 weeks of Caldwell-Luc followed by irrigation, no major complications at 36 months.
- Kim et al. case report (cited): opaque images decreased significantly at 2 weeks after lateral irrigation in two post-implant patients.
- Matthias 2015 (cited, 174 cases): oroantral fistula after extraction the most common cause (60 cases, 34.5%), peri-implantitis 9 (5.2%), sinus lift 4 (2.3%); contrasts with this series where sinus graft was jointly most common (33.3%).
- Kim et al. (cited, n=27): implant-related 10 (37%) and extraction-related 8 (29.6%).

## 7. Glossary

- **OMS (Odontogenic Maxillary Sinusitis)**: maxillary sinusitis arising from dentoalveolar disease or dental procedures.
- **Sinus irrigation / lavage**: opening the lateral wall of the maxillary sinus and washing with saline (or iodine solution).
- **Small lateral window**: here a 3-mm bur window at least 10 mm above the alveolar crest.
- **Radiopacity volume ratio (Vt0/Vs)**: opaque volume on CBCT divided by total sinus volume; the study's measure of sinusitis severity.
- **Caldwell-Luc procedure**: classical anterior-wall maxillary sinus approach to remove diseased mucosa.
