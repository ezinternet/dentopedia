## Why Ingested

Ingested to fill a critical gap in the wiki: no prior human histology paper documents peri-implant bone necrosis (PIBN) from **overcompression** in clinical tissue. This 2025 case series (University of Michigan) is the first held paper to present biopsy-confirmed human histological evidence of bone necrosis from bone overcompression during placement — and contrasts it with histologically similar peri-implantitis-associated necrosis — directly supporting the differential diagnosis framework in [[wiki/overviews/implant-failure-mechanism-xray-differential-overview]].

## Three-line Summary

Case series (5 patients, 10 implants) of peri-implant bone necrosis (PIBN): 3 early cases (overcompression at placement) and 2 late cases (peri-implantitis sequela), all managed by implant + necrotic bone removal.

Two cases biopsy-confirmed histologically — both showed the same necrosis pattern: multiple nonvital bone fragments, empty osteocyte lacunae, acute and chronic inflammatory cell infiltrate; all 5 patients resolved without recurrence.

PIBN is rare but clinically critical; etiology bifurcates between mechanical (bone overcompression at insertion) and inflammatory (peri-implantitis); both mechanisms produce a convergent histological picture that can appear radiographically similar as a "crateriform" defect.

## Key Contributions

- First human clinical case series to histologically document PIBN arising from **bone overcompression** at time of placement (3 early cases).
- Establishes a two-pathway PIBN classification: (1) mechanical — overcompression → early ischemic necrosis; (2) inflammatory — peri-implantitis sequela → late necrosis.
- Provides the histological signature of PIBN: **empty osteocyte lacunae + nonvital bone fragments + mixed inflammatory infiltrate** — the same pattern regardless of etiology, explaining why imaging alone cannot differentiate.
- Demonstrates successful management: surgical removal of implant + necrotic bone; no recurrence in any of the 5 cases.
- Clinical implication: PIBN must be suspected in both early (<1 month) and late (>1 year) failure — timing is the most useful distinguishing feature between the two pathways.

## Methodology

Case series design. 5 patients, 10 total failed implants. Cases 1–3: PIBN presenting early (immediately after placement, presumed overcompression). Cases 4–5: PIBN presenting 1–5 years post-placement (peri-implantitis associated). Two cases underwent histopathological biopsy of the necrotic tissue. All treated by implant removal + surgical debridement of necrotic bone. Follow-up confirmed resolution without recurrence.

## Limitations

- Abstract-only ingest; full histological images, implant details (torque values, bone density), and case demographics not recoverable.
- Very small sample (n=5 patients); cannot estimate PIBN incidence or validate risk factors.
- Retrospective case series; no comparison group, no standardized diagnostic criteria.
- Causal attribution (overcompression vs peri-implantitis) based on clinical timing and judgment, not biomechanical measurement.

## Glossary

- **PIBN (Peri-Implant Bone Necrosis)**: necrosis of the alveolar bone immediately surrounding a dental implant, rare complication distinct from peri-implantitis
- **Empty osteocyte lacunae**: hallmark histological sign of osteonecrosis — lacunar spaces devoid of viable osteocyte nuclei
- **Bone sequestrum (bone sequestrae)**: dead, devitalized bone fragment surrounded by inflammatory tissue; visible as a radiopaque fragment separated from vital bone
- **Overcompression necrosis**: ischemic bone death from excessive compression stress during implant insertion, occluding intraosseous vasculature
