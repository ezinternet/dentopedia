## Why Ingested
First-page evaluation of dental-dedicated MRI (ddMRI, low-field 0.55 T) for TMJ assessment, a brand-new modality category for this wiki. It extends [[wiki/radiology/willershausen-2025-low-field-mri-pediatric-dental]] (same 0.55 T low-field platform, pediatric dental indication) to TMJ diagnostics and anchors the [[wiki/radiology/mri/liedke-2026-tmj-structures-dental-dedicated-mri]] page with head-to-head comparison to CBCT (the reference modality) for bony TMJ changes.

## Three-line Summary
Line 1: Cross-sectional observational feasibility study (n = 24 patients / 48 TMJs) comparing dental-dedicated MRI (ddMRI, 0.55 T, 7-channel dental coil) with CBCT, assessed independently by three observers for hard- and soft-tissue TMJ conditions (STARD 2015).
Line 2: Mean inter-observer κ was 0.36 (ddMRI) vs 0.50 (CBCT); most prevalent finding was anterior disc displacement (58.3%); no significant differences between CBCT and ddMRI for hard-tissue conditions (McNemar p > 0.05), though CBCT detected bone changes more often (2.1%–41.7%) than ddMRI (0%–31.3%).
Line 3: ddMRI is feasible, radiation-free, and depicts bone plus disc/joint fluid at point-of-care, but larger clinically correlated studies are needed before it replaces CBCT-guided TMJ diagnostics.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Journal of Oral Rehabilitation 2026;53(2):149–159
- **DOI**: 10.1111/joor.70071
- **Institution**: Section for Oral Radiology and Endodontics, Department of Dentistry and Oral Health, Aarhus University, Denmark; Section for Oral Radiology, Department of Stomatology, Federal University of Santa Maria, Brazil

## 2. Key Contributions
- First feasibility evaluation of dental-dedicated MRI (ddMRI) for the diagnostic assessment of TMJ hard- and soft-tissue conditions.
- Head-to-head comparison of ddMRI with CBCT for bony TMJ conditions: no statistically significant differences (p > 0.05), supporting ddMRI as an adjunct modality.
- ddMRI depicts osseous components while also imaging the articular disc and joint fluid, without ionizing radiation, at point-of-care.
- Quantitative reproducibility data for ddMRI TMJ evaluation: mean inter-observer κ 0.36 (vs 0.50 CBCT), intra-observer κ 0.444–1.
- Prevalence profile of TMJ findings in a referred TMD population: anterior disc displacement most common at 58.3%.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Cross-sectional observational feasibility study, reported per STARD 2015 guidelines; ethics approval #1-10-72-101-22.
- **n**: 24 patients, 48 TMJs (intra-observer subset: 5 patients / 10 TMJs); 3 observers (>5 years TMJ imaging experience, 1 year ddMRI experience).
- **Imaging**:
  - CBCT: X1 unit (Copenhagen Imaging, 90 kVp, 10 mAs, 15 × 5 cm FOV, 0.20 mm voxel).
  - ddMRI: Siemens MAGNETOM Free.Max 0.55 T with 7-channel dental-dedicated receive coil (RAPID Biomedical, flexible jaw-side extensions). Sequences: PD (TSE), T1 (TSE), T2 (TSE), PD-FS, T2-STIR; oblique sagittal and coronal planes; PD/T1/T2 at 0.2 × 0.2 × 2.5 mm, PD-FS 0.4 × 0.4 × 2.5 mm, T2-STIR 0.4 × 0.4 × 2.0 mm.
- **Scoring**: 5-point scale (1 = normal → 5 = diseased; 0 = not evaluable) for flattening, cortical erosion, subcortical sclerosis, osteophyte, subcortical cyst, loose body, disc position (sagittal + coronal). Joint fluid: dichotomous. Scores dichotomized into normal (1–2) vs diseased (3–5).
- **Outcomes**: Inter-/intra-observer agreement (Cohen's Kappa + raw agreement), condition prevalence per modality, CBCT-vs-ddMRI comparison via McNemar test (two-sided p halved for conservative one-sided interpretation; α = 0.05). Sample-size plan: non-inferiority limit 10%, 45 TMJs for 80% power.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Inter-observer agreement** (n = 48): mean κ 0.36 (range −0.021 to 0.733) for ddMRI vs 0.50 (range 0.143–1) for CBCT; mean raw agreement 81.3% (39.6–100) vs 85.9% (60.4–100).
- **Intra-observer agreement** (n = 10): κ 0.444–1 (raw 70–100%) ddMRI vs 0.545–1 (raw 80–100%) CBCT.
- **Prevalence** (n = 48): most prevalent = anterior disc displacement 58.3% (sagittal; coronal 18.8%). CBCT bone prevalence 2.1% (subcortical cyst) to 41.7% (condylar flattening); ddMRI 0% (subcortical cyst, temporal cortical erosion) to 31.3% (condylar flattening).
- **Per-condition prevalence & p (ddMRI vs CBCT)**: Flattening temporal 4.2% vs 12.5% (p=0.125); cortical erosion temporal 0 vs 6.3% (p=0.250); subcortical sclerosis temporal 12.5% vs 6.3% (p=0.625); flattening condyle 31.3% vs 41.7% (p=0.332); cortical erosion condyle 8.3% vs 10.4% (p=1.000); subcortical sclerosis condyle 10.4% vs 12.5% (p=1.000); osteophyte condyle 22.9% vs 27.1% (p=0.625); subcortical cyst condyle 0 vs 2.1% (p=1.000); loose body 0 vs 0 (nd).
- **Missing data**: CBCT 2 TMJs (temporal bone, FOV positioning); ddMRI 3 TMJs (disc position) and 6 TMJs (joint fluid) judged inadequate.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Feasibility design: statistical comparison (McNemar) is underpowered relative to the non-inferiority plan (45 TMJs needed; 48 available with conservative two-sided analysis) — results are image-to-image, no clinical correlation/demographic data.
- Modest reproducibility likely reflects observer inexperience with a recently introduced modality plus subjectivity of disc-position scoring; regular (not weighted) Kappa was deliberately used, representing a "worse-case scenario".
- ddMRI sequence protocols were non-consensus (system was developed during the study; 3–5 sequences per patient), and some structures were not assessable in some cases.
- Larger, clinically oriented validation studies are required before firm conclusions on ddMRI diagnostic performance.

## 6. Related Work
- Willershausen 2025: same 0.55 T low-field platform applied to pediatric dental imaging; together support low-field dental MRI as a radiation-free point-of-care modality.
- Chana 2026: ultrasound as another radiation-free TMJ imaging alternative (juvenile idiopathic arthritis), complementing ddMRI in point-of-care TMJ assessment.
- Prior CBCT-vs-MRI TMJ literature (cited as refs 3, 8–11): CBCT consistently shows higher osseous-change detection than conventional MRI — the gap this ddMRI study targets with a dental-optimized coil.

## 7. Glossary
- **ddMRI (Dental-Dedicated MRI)**: low-field (0.55 T) MRI using dental-dedicated receive coils (e.g., 7-channel jaw coil) to enable point-of-care dentomaxillofacial imaging at lower cost than 1.5/3 T hospital systems.
- **CBCT (Cone-Beam Computed Tomography)**: reference imaging modality for osseous TMJ components; uses ionizing radiation and cannot depict the disc.
- **ADD (Anterior Disc Displacement)**: articular disc displaced anteriorly relative to the condyle; the most prevalent condition (58.3%) in this study.
- **STARD 2015**: Standards for Reporting Diagnostic Accuracy Studies — reporting guideline followed here.
- **McNemar test**: paired-proportions test used to compare CBCT vs ddMRI diagnosis counts on the same 48 TMJs.
- **PD-FS / T2-STIR**: proton-density fat-suppressed and short tau inversion recovery sequences used for joint fluid / cystic lesion depiction.
