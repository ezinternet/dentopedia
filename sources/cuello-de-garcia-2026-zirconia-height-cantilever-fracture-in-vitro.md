## Why Ingested

No held paper directly tested whether varying the zirconia veneering height (2, 4, 6mm) on a titanium framework affects cantilever fracture resistance. This addresses a specific clinical design question: does increasing zirconia height provide additional structural benefit? The finding that it does not — and that screw fracture dominates — redirects clinical attention to screw design and fixation protocols.

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro bench study, 45 specimens (3 groups n=15 each: 2mm, 4mm, 6mm zirconia height over Ti framework) representing cantilever full-arch prostheses; cyclic loading 1.2M cycles at 200N followed by static loading to fracture; failure mode and fracture resistance recorded.

Fracture resistance 849±142N (2mm), 862±158N (4mm), 871±133N (6mm) — no significant difference (P>.05); most common failure: prosthetic screw fracture 86.7%; zirconia fracture 13.3%; zero Ti framework fractures.

Zirconia height (2–6mm) does not affect fracture resistance of cantilever full-arch prostheses on Ti framework; screw fracture is the dominant failure mode, suggesting future focus should shift to screw design and fixation protocols.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Null finding: zirconia height 2–6mm has no significant effect on cantilever fracture resistance over Ti framework
- Dominant failure mode shift: prosthetic screw fracture (86.7%) >> zirconia fracture (13.3%) >> Ti framework fracture (0%)
- Redirects clinical design focus from veneering height to screw design and fixation protocols
- Cyclic fatigue methodology (1.2M cycles) mimics realistic masticatory loading before static failure testing
- Clinically: thinner zirconia (2mm) is not structurally inferior — allows more space for hygiene access

## 5. Clinical Interpretation

The null finding on zirconia height has practical implications:

1. **Space allocation**: When vertical prosthetic space is limited, the 2mm minimum zirconia height is structurally equivalent to 6mm — clinicians can allocate the saved space to improved hygiene access or additional Ti framework bulk
2. **Failure mode insight**: 86.7% screw fracture means the structural weak link is the fixation system, not the veneering material or even the framework — future engineering efforts should target screw fatigue resistance
3. **Ti framework robustness**: Zero Ti framework fractures across all 45 specimens — current Ti framework design is not the limiting factor under this loading protocol

## 6. Limitations

- In-vitro — no saliva, temperature cycling, or bone/implant interface simulation
- Single Ti framework design tested — results may not generalize to other framework geometries
- Abstract only — specific screw specifications, cantilever length, and statistical methods details unavailable
- 200N cyclic load may not represent the full range of parafunctional forces (bruxism can exceed 700N)
