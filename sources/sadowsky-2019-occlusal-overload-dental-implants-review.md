## Why Ingested
The 2019 anchor narrative review of the occlusal-overload literature: it compiles the conflicting animal data (Isidor's monkey overload vs Heitz-Mayfield/Kozlovsky dog studies) and the bruxism–failure clinical link (OR 2.71) that [[wiki/occlusion/mojaver-2025-occlusal-overload-peri-implant-health-sr]] later synthesized at systematic-review level, and its "prosthetic-design dogma is not evidence-based" stance (high C/I ratio, cantilevers, non-splinted designs) is reinforced by the later SR [[wiki/occlusion/di-fiore-2022-periimplant-bone-loss-overload-occlusal-analysis]].

## Three-line Summary
Non-systematic narrative review of preclinical (monkey, dog) and clinical literature asking whether occlusal overload causes peri-implant bone loss and implant failure.

Animal studies conflict (Isidor: 6/8 overloaded monkey implants lost osseointegration; Heitz-Mayfield dogs: no marginal bone loss, BIC 72.6% vs 73.9%), while clinical data link bruxism to failure (OR 2.71; 5-year survival down to 72%) yet show high crown-to-implant ratios (2–3:1), cantilevers, and non-splinted designs perform as well as conventional alternatives.

Avoiding these designs based on the natural-tooth model is not evidence-based; preclinical research must establish patient-specific load thresholds (>4000 microstrain fatigue failure vs 1000–3000 microstrain anabolic window).

## 2. Key Contributions
- Single-author synthesis showing the animal-model evidence on overload-induced bone loss is conflicting (monkey studies positive, recent dog studies negative), and that the biological fatigue threshold for peri-implant bone remains undefined.
- Compiles the clinical bruxism evidence: 20–35.9% of patients generate loads capable of perimplant microfracture; bruxers have OR 2.71 for implant failure and 5-year survival of 72% in one retrospective cohort.
- Argues that prosthetic restrictions inherited from the natural-tooth model — avoiding crown-to-implant ratios of 2–3:1, cantilevers, and non-splinted restorations — are not supported by evidence, and have driven unnecessarily invasive/costly treatment.
- Frames occlusal overload within Frost's mechanostat: >4000 microstrain indexed as fatigue-failure threshold vs 1000–3000 microstrain anabolic bone-stimulating window, modulated by bone density (Lekholm & Zarb types 1–4).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Non-systematic narrative review (author states this as the study's main limitation)
- **Scope**: Preclinical literature (monkey and dog overload experiments) + clinical studies (bruxism, crown-to-implant ratio, cantilevers, splinting)
- **n**: Not applicable (narrative; ~63 references)
- **Outcomes**: Peri-implant bone level, osseointegration loss, implant survival/failure, bone-to-implant contact, microstrain thresholds
- **Ethics note**: Randomized/prospective human overload trials are deemed unethical (Helsinki accords), so no controlled human occlusal studies exist

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
| Finding | Value |
|---|---|
| Isidor monkey study: loss of osseointegration under overload | 6/8 loaded implants after 18 months |
| Miyata monkey study: bone resorption threshold (superstructure gap) | ≥180 µm after 4 weeks loading |
| Heitz-Mayfield dog split-mouth: BIC overloaded vs control | 73.9% vs 72.6% (not statistically different); no marginal bone loss |
| Kozlovsky dog study: effect of supra-occlusion without inflammation | No MBL/osseointegration loss; BIC increased |
| Patients generating microfracture-level forces (bruxism prevalence range) | 20–35.9% |
| Bruxers vs non-bruxers: implant failure odds ratio | 2.71 (98 bruxers + matched controls) |
| Bruxism 5-year retrospective implant survival (Chitumalla) | 90% (1y) → 85% (3y) → 72% (5y) |
| Mean bite force in bruxers | 827 ± 620 N (Nishigawa: ~790 N, mean contact duration 7.1 s) |
| Normal mastication load pattern | 0.23–0.3 contacts/s, 1–2 Hz, ~9–17 min/day |
| Implant tactile sensitivity vs natural teeth | ~8-fold lower (no periodontal ligament feedback) |
| Microstrain: fatigue-failure threshold vs anabolic window | >4000 vs 1000–3000 microstrain |
| Crown-to-implant ratios reported successful (anabolic bone response) | 2–3:1 |
| Duyck bending-moment experiment | 14.7 N × 50 mm = 73.5 Ncm, 2520 cycles at 1 Hz → crater-like lateral bone defects |
| Cantilever arm length associated with marginal bone loss | ≥8 mm |
| Vigolo 10-year split-mouth (44 patients), splinted vs non-splinted | No difference in crestal bone loss (no bruxers in cohort) |
| Naert 16-year cohort: 644 implants (235 single crowns, 409 splinted) | 95.8% survival; no significant hazard-rate difference by design |
| Peri-implantitis burden cited | Up to 20% of implant patients |

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Non-systematic review methodology (author-stated); heterogeneous source studies with confounding variables (overload induction method, bone quality, hygiene regimens).
- No controlled human studies on implant occlusal overload are ethically feasible; conclusions rest on animal models that poorly simulate lateral human forces (dogs cannot perform lateral movements).
- Key studies (Vigolo, Naert) had no reported bruxer counts, limiting subgroup inference.
- Future work: preclinical overload experiments and finite element analyses to establish bone-microstrain thresholds and patient-specific prosthetic design guidelines.

## 7. Glossary
- **Occlusal overload**: Mechanical loading exceeding the peri-implant bone's physiologic tolerance, hypothesized to cause microfracture, resorption, and possibly loss of osseointegration.
- **Microstrain (μstrain)**: Unit of tissue-level bone deformation; >4000 indexed as fatigue-failure threshold, 1000–3000 as anabolic (bone-strengthening) window.
- **Crown-to-implant ratio (C/I ratio)**: Ratio of clinical crown height to implant length; 2–3:1 reported not to compromise bone levels.
- **Bruxism**: Parafunctional grinding/clenching generating high-magnitude, prolonged occlusal forces; principal clinical risk factor for overload.
- **Bone-to-implant contact (BIC)**: Histomorphometric percentage of implant surface in contact with mineralized bone.
- **Lekholm & Zarb classification**: Bone quantity (A–D) and quality (type 1–4) scheme used to stratify implant site bone.
