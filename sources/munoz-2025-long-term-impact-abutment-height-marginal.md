## Why Ingested
7-year RCT directly testing the mechanistic claim behind TL vs BL outcomes: does a taller transmucosal abutment (3mm vs 1mm, simulating supragingival IAJ position) reduce MBL in subcrestally placed BL implants? This is the strongest long-term evidence for the biological width/IAJ position hypothesis that underpins [[overviews/tissue-level-vs-bone-level-implant-outcomes]]. Extends [[implants/mbl/scognamiglio-2026-influence-implant-type-clinical-radiologic]] with an RCT design testing the same mechanism.

## One-line Summary
7-year RCT (37 subjects, 63 subcrestally placed BL implants; 1-mm vs 3-mm definitive abutment) found 3-mm abutments had significantly less total MBL at 7 years (mesial: -0.27 vs -0.42mm, p<0.001; distal: -0.33 vs -0.35mm, p<0.001), and descriptively lower peri-implantitis rate (3.2% vs 18.2%), though the latter did not reach statistical significance (p=0.158).

## 한줄요약
7년 무작위 대조시험 (37명/63 임플란트; 1mm vs 3mm 최종 지대주, 하방 1.5mm 식립): 3mm 지대주에서 7년 변연골 소실 (Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)이 유의하게 적음 (근심 -0.27 vs -0.42mm, p<0.001); 임플란트 주위염 (Peri-implantitis) 발생률 기술적으로 낮음 (3.2% vs 18.2%) 이나 통계 유의 미달 (p=0.158).

## 2. Key Contributions
- Longest RCT (7yr) testing abutment height effect on MBL in subcrestally placed implants
- Mechanistic test: 3mm abutment positions microgap supracrestally (resembling TL IAJ) → significantly less total MBL
- Peri-implantitis descriptive trend: 18.2% (1mm) vs 3.2% (3mm) — clinically meaningful even if not statistically significant given small n
- KM width <2mm independently linked to higher peri-implant disease risk regardless of abutment height
- Abutment height effect on MBL attenuates beyond 1 year: surgery-to-7yr significant, 1yr-to-7yr only mesial difference significant (p=0.049), distal NS (p=0.749)

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Design: 7-year RCT follow-up (builds on prior 1-year study, same cohort)
- Population: 37 subjects (63 implants) at 7yr; originally 65 subjects/99 implants at 1yr; 43% dropout
- Placement: Subcrestalljy 1.5mm; definitive abutments 1mm (control) vs 3mm (test)
- Implant: Klockner system, two-piece, placed posteriorly
- Radiographic: paralleling cone technique, individualized silicone keys, blinded calibrated examiner (ICC=0.99)
- Statistics: IBM SPSS v28; Mann-Whitney U test; Bonferroni correction; no multivariable regression (small n, multicollinearity)
- PIMT: 3-6 month intervals; 83.8% non-compliant or erratically compliant at 7yr (COVID-19 disruption cited)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Comparison | 1mm abutment | 3mm abutment | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mesial MBL (surgery→7yr) | -0.42 ± 0.50 mm | -0.27 ± 1.32 mm | <0.001 |
| Distal MBL (surgery→7yr) | -0.35 ± 0.38 mm | -0.33 ± 1.39 mm | <0.001 |
| Mesial MBL (1yr→7yr) | -0.22 ± 0.48 mm | -0.24 ± 1.34 mm | 0.049 |
| Distal MBL (1yr→7yr) | -0.10 ± 0.45 mm | -0.33 ± 1.38 mm | 0.749 (NS) |
| Peri-implantitis at 7yr | 6/33 (18.2%) | 1/31 (3.2%) | 0.158 (NS) |
| Peri-implant mucositis | 18/33 (54.6%) | 23/31 (74.2%) | NS |

Additional findings:
- BoP increase (1→7yr) greater in 1mm group at mesio-buccal (p=0.020) and buccal (p=0.038) sites
- KM width: peri-implant mucositis cases had ~1mm less KM than healthy implants in both groups (1mm: p=0.010; 3mm: p<0.001)
- Posterior mandible implants with 1mm abutment: 7× higher risk of remaining disease vs posterior maxilla (p=0.039)
- Mucosal thickness >2mm with 3mm abutment linked to higher mucositis risk (p=0.004)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- High dropout rate (43%): 65 → 37 patients; COVID-19 cited; attrition bias possible
- Small n limits multivariable regression; Bonferroni correction reduces power further
- Single implant system (Klockner) — generalizability to other platforms unclear
- 83.8% PIMT non-compliance → confounds disease outcomes
- Single university center (UIC Barcelona)
- Prior 1-year data published separately (Muñoz et al.) — full longitudinal picture requires both papers

## 6. Related Work
- Muñoz et al. (prior meta-analysis): long abutments reduce MBL by 0.27-0.33mm — corroborated by this RCT
- Galindo-Moreno et al.: initial bone loss >0.44mm predicts progressive loss at 18mo
- Windael et al.: implants with early bone loss ≥0.5mm are 5× more likely to develop peri-implantitis
- Derks et al.: peri-implantitis more likely when restorative margin ≤1.5mm from bone crest
