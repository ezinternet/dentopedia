## Why Ingested

The wiki's platform-switching cluster treats crestal-bone preservation as a property of the implant–abutment offset itself — [[wiki/implants/mbl/gupta-2019-platform-switching-crestal-bone-loss-sr]] tabulates 9 comparative studies all favoring platform switching (PS), and [[wiki/implants/mbl/dhir-2012-significance-clinical-relevance-biologic-width]] already argues biologic width needs soft-tissue room. This comparative clinical study supplies the missing **conditional**: PS preserved bone only in thick mucosa (≤2 mm threshold), while thin mucosa lost 1.17 mm at 1 year despite switching. Its ≥2 mm threshold is already cited as an inclusion criterion in [[wiki/implants/mbl/verma-2024-comparison-bone-loss-submerged-nonsubmerged]], but the primary source was not held until now.

## Three-line Summary

Comparative clinical study (abstract-only) of 80 patients receiving 80 platform-switched 4.1 mm bone-level Straumann implants, allocated by initial mucosal thickness to thin (≤2 mm, n=40) or thick (>2 mm, n=40), with radiographic follow-up to 1 year.

Thin-mucosa implants lost 0.79 mm of crestal bone at 2 months and 1.17 mm at 1 year, versus 0.17 mm and 0.21 mm in thick mucosa; between-group differences were significant (p<.001) at both time points.

Platform switching does not prevent crestal bone loss when mucosal tissue is thin at implant placement — its bone-preserving effect is conditional on thick (≥2 mm) soft tissue.

## 세줄요약

비교 임상연구(초록만 확보) — 플랫폼 스위칭(Platform Switching, PS) 4.1 mm 골수준 임플란트(Straumann) 80개를 식립한 80명을 초기 점막 두께에 따라 얇은 군(≤2 mm, n=40)과 두꺼운 군(>2 mm, n=40)으로 나누어 1년까지 방사선 추적.

얇은 점막군은 2개월 0.79 mm, 1년 1.17 mm의 치조정골 소실을 보인 반면 두꺼운 점막군은 0.17 mm, 0.21 mm였고, 두 시점 모두 군 간 차이는 유의(p<0.001).

식립 시 점막이 얇으면 플랫폼 스위칭(PS)이 변연골소실(Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)을 막지 못한다 — 골보존 효과는 두꺼운(≥2 mm) 연조직에 조건부적이다.

## 1. Document Information

- **Journal**: Clinical Implant Dentistry and Related Research 2015;17(6):1228–1236 (first published online 28 March 2014)
- **DOI**: 10.1111/cid.12222
- **Institution**: Not stated in the retrieved abstract-only PDF
- **Implant system**: Institut Straumann AG, Basel, Switzerland (bone-level, 4.1 mm diameter, platform-switched)

## 2. Key Contributions

- First clinical comparison to isolate **initial vertical soft-tissue thickness** as a modifier of platform-switching's crestal-bone outcome — earlier PS studies had not addressed it (paper's own stated gap).
- Quantifies a 2 mm mucosal-thickness boundary: thin (≤2 mm) versus thick (>2 mm), with a ~5.6-fold difference in 1-year crestal bone loss (1.17 mm vs 0.21 mm).
- Shows early divergence — the between-group difference is already large at 2 months (0.79 vs 0.17 mm) and persists to 1 year, locating the effect in early healing/remodeling rather than late loading.
- Directly qualifies the general "PS preserves crestal bone" claim: the technique maintains bone only when soft tissue is thick at placement.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Comparative clinical study (prospective radiographic follow-up; not described as randomized — cases allocated by measured mucosal thickness).
- **Databases**: Not applicable (clinical study, not a review).
- **n**: 80 patients (38 male, 42 female; mean age 44 ± 3.34 years), 80 implants (one per patient).
- **Groups**: Group 1 = thin soft tissue ≤2 mm (n=40); Group 2 = thick soft tissue >2 mm (n=40).
- **Intervention**: One-stage placement of 4.1 mm bone-level platform-switched implants; screw-retained restorations.
- **Outcomes**: Radiographic crestal bone loss at four time points — after implant placement, 2 months after healing, after restoration, and 1-year post-reconstruction follow-up.
- **Statistics**: Mann–Whitney U-test; significance set at p ≤ .05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Time point | Thin mucosa (≤2 mm), n=40 | Thick mucosa (>2 mm), n=40 | Between-group |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 months after healing | 0.79 mm crestal bone loss | 0.17 mm crestal bone loss | p < .001 |
| 1-year post-reconstruction | 1.17 mm crestal bone loss | 0.21 mm crestal bone loss | p < .001 |

- Absolute 1-year difference: 0.96 mm more bone loss in thin mucosa (~5.6× the thick-mucosa value).
- Direction of the effect is established by 2 months and does not reverse — thick mucosa retained near-baseline crestal bone throughout ("minimal remodeling" per the authors).
- No implant-level or patient-level covariate adjustment is reported in the available abstract; only the two thickness strata are compared.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only source**: the retrieved Wiley PDF contains the abstract, reference list, and publisher boilerplate but no full Methods/Results body — all numbers above come from the abstract verbatim; subgroup details, radiographic measurement method, and dropout accounting are not recoverable.
- Group allocation by tissue thickness rather than randomization leaves open confounding between mucosa thickness and other patient/site factors.
- Single implant system (Straumann bone-level, 4.1 mm) and a single 2 mm cut-off — generalizability to other connections, diameters, and a continuous thickness–bone-loss relationship is untested here.
- One-year follow-up only; no data on longer-term crestal stability or peri-implant disease.
- Paper's own framing calls for further research on how initial soft-tissue thickness drives crestal bone loss.

## 7. Glossary

- **Platform Switching (PS)**: Restoring a bone-level implant with an abutment narrower than the implant platform, shifting the implant–abutment junction horizontally inward away from the crestal bone.
- **Crestal / marginal bone loss (MBL)**: Radiographic reduction in the peri-implant alveolar bone height adjacent to the implant platform; the primary outcome here.
- **Vertical soft-tissue thickness**: Mucosal thickness measured at the implant site at placement; ≤2 mm defined thin, >2 mm thick in this study.
- **Biologic width**: The combined connective-tissue and epithelial attachment dimension above the alveolar crest; needs soft-tissue space to form without apical bone remodeling.
- **Implant–abutment junction (IAJ)**: The microgap interface where the inflammatory cell infiltrate forms; platform switching relocates it medially.
