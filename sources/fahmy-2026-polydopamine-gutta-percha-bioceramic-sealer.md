## Why Ingested

This paper is a bioinspired-coating counterpart to [[wiki/endodontics/sarilaksmi-2026-intact-mta-sealer-gutta-percha-surface-modification]] ingested in the same batch — both address gutta-percha's poor wettability/adhesion to hydrophilic sealers via surface modification (polydopamine here vs plasma+CPC there), but this one pairs the coated gutta-percha with a bioceramic sealer using the clinically standard single-cone technique rather than intact MTA. It also directly extends the existing GP/sealer wettability-contact-angle cluster anchored by [[wiki/endodontics/kunam-2024-wettability-ahplus-ceraseal-guttaflow]] (which ranked AH Plus > Ceraseal > Guttaflow bioseal on dentin wettability) by testing a novel coating intervention rather than comparing stock sealer chemistries.

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study (14 extracted mandibular second premolars, single-cone technique) comparing polydopamine (PDA)-coated versus uncoated gutta-percha with a bioceramic sealer (CeraSeal) for sealing ability (apical dye penetration, n=7/group), interfacial adaptation (ESEM, n=3/group), and wettability (contact angle, n=14/group).

PDA-coated gutta-percha showed significantly lower apical dye penetration than uncoated gutta-percha (3.04 +/- 0.59 mm vs 4.30 +/- 1.11 mm, p=0.041), no detectable ESEM interfacial gaps in any root region after 28 days in simulated body fluid versus persistent and enlarging gaps (up to 2.8 micrometers) in the uncoated group, and a markedly lower contact angle indicating much-improved wettability (28.75 +/- 9.42 degrees vs 77.42 +/- 27.10 degrees, p<0.001).

Bioinspired PDA coating is a low-cost, simple, retreatment-compatible surface modification that may improve long-term obturation quality with bioceramic sealers, but conclusions are limited to a small in-vitro dye-penetration/SEM design pending clinical, micro-CT, and long-term durability validation.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First wiki paper to test **polydopamine (PDA)**, a mussel-inspired bioadhesive coating, as a gutta-percha surface modification to enhance interfacial behavior with a **bioceramic sealer** (CeraSeal) under the **single-cone** technique.
- Demonstrates PDA deposition is confirmable via ATR-FTIR (new catechol/amine peaks), ESEM (uniform darkening at 72 h dip-coating), and EDX (C 55%, O 24%, N 15%), and that 72 h dip-coating outperforms 24 h.
- Shows PDA-coated gutta-percha promotes bioactivity in simulated body fluid (SBF) — calcium/phosphorus (calcium-phosphate-like) deposits on ESEM/EDX after 14 days — whereas uncoated gutta-percha shows no comparable mineral deposition.
- Provides converging tri-method evidence (dye penetration, ESEM interfacial gap, contact angle) that PDA coating improves sealing ability, adaptability, and wettability versus uncoated gutta-percha with the same bioceramic sealer.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Materials**: Dopamine hydrochloride (Sigma-Aldrich) in TRIS buffer (pH 8.5, 2 mg/mL) for PDA solution; gutta-percha single cones (Conform Fit, Dentsply Sirona) matched to ProTaper Next X3; CeraSeal (Meta Biomed), a premixed calcium-silicate bioceramic sealer; laboratory-prepared simulated body fluid (SBF, Kokubo/ISO 23317:2012 protocol); 1% methylene blue dye.
- **Coating procedure**: Gutta-percha cones abraded (#1000 SiC paper), cleaned (acetone/ethanol/deionized water, ultrasonic), dried, then dip-coated in dopamine solution for 24 h or 72 h at 37 degrees C (color changes colorless -> pale brown -> deep brown as PDA self-polymerizes); rinsed and air-dried. 72 h coating was selected for all downstream experiments based on characterization results.
- **Characterization** (9 cones for chemical/morphological; 6 for bioactivity): ATR-FTIR (Nicolet iS50) for chemical confirmation; ESEM (FEI Quanta 3D 200i) for surface morphology/uniformity; EDX for elemental composition; bioactivity assessed via ESEM/EDX after 14-day SBF immersion.
- **Sealing ability test** (n=7/group): 14 freshly extracted, sound-root mandibular second premolars (single straight canal, closed apex), decoronated to 14 mm, shaped with ProTaper Next X1-X3 (5.25% NaOCl + 19% EDTA gel + saline irrigation), randomized to uncoated or PDA-coated gutta-percha + CeraSeal, single-cone obturation (X3 30/.07), stored 1 week (37 degrees C, 100% humidity) for sealer setting. After 14-day SBF immersion, roots were varnished (apical 3 mm exposed), immersed in 1% methylene blue (48 h), sectioned longitudinally, and apical dye penetration measured (stereomicroscope, ImageView software) by a blinded examiner.
- **Adaptability test** (n=3 roots/group, 3 time points): interfacial gaps (micrometers) between gutta-percha and sealer measured on ESEM at coronal/middle/apical transverse sections at baseline, 14 days, and 28 days of SBF immersion; qualitative/descriptive (no inferential statistics given small sample).
- **Wetting ability test** (n=14 discs/group): thermoplasticized gutta-percha discs (10 mm x 1 mm, uncoated or PDA-coated) mounted and analyzed with an optical tensiometer (Theta Flow, Attension); a sessile droplet of CeraSeal dispensed and contact angle calculated via the spherical cap model, triplicate readings averaged per specimen.
- **Stats**: Kolmogorov-Smirnov/Shapiro-Wilk normality checks; independent Student's t-test for dye penetration and contact angle (SPSS v20); significance at p <= 0.05. Sample size via PS Power and Sample Size Program (alpha 0.05, power 80%), inflated from minimums of 3 (sealing) and 5 (wetting) per group to 7 and 14 respectively for dropout/robustness.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Characterization**: ATR-FTIR of PDA-coated gutta-percha showed new broad O-H/N-H bands (3400-3700 cm^-1) and a catechol aromatic C=C peak (~1480 cm^-1) not present in uncoated gutta-percha, with intensity increasing from 24 h to 72 h. ESEM confirmed uniform, continuous PDA layer at 72 h (fine-grained/nodular morphology, ~50-200 nm thick); EDX of the coated layer: carbon 55%, oxygen 24%, nitrogen 15% (plus trace zinc/barium from the underlying gutta-percha matrix).
- **Bioactivity in SBF (14 days)**: uncoated gutta-percha showed only filler particles (Zn 18.47%, Ba 2.53%, C 51.67% by EDX) with no new mineral phase; PDA-coated gutta-percha developed hemispherical globules/flake-like crystals with EDX-detected calcium (0.11%) and phosphorus (0.05%), consistent with early calcium-phosphate-like deposition.
- **Sealing ability (apical dye penetration, mm, mean +/- SD)**: uncoated 4.30 +/- 1.11 vs PDA-coated 3.04 +/- 0.59; **p=0.041** (significant).
- **Adaptability (interfacial gap, micrometers, descriptive)**:
  - Baseline: uncoated 1.9 (coronal) - 2.64 (apical, largest); PDA-coated 1.5 (coronal), 1.6 (middle), 2.1 (apical, still largest but smaller than uncoated).
  - Day 14: uncoated 1.59 (coronal) -> 2.0 (middle) -> 2.4 (apical, largest); PDA-coated 0.7 (coronal) -> 1.3 (middle) -> 2.0 (apical, largest).
  - Day 28: uncoated 1.5 (coronal) -> 2.3 (middle) -> 2.8 (apical, largest, worsening over time); PDA-coated **no detectable gaps in any root region** — the key adaptability finding.
- **Wetting ability (contact angle, degrees, mean +/- SD)**: uncoated 77.42 +/- 27.10 vs PDA-coated 28.75 +/- 9.42; reported as p=0.000001 in text and p=0.0001 in the results table (both far below the 0.05 threshold; treat as p<0.001).
- **Bottom line**: null hypothesis rejected on all three outcomes — PDA coating significantly reduced apical dye penetration, eliminated detectable SEM interfacial gaps by day 28, and markedly lowered the contact angle with the bioceramic sealer versus uncoated gutta-percha.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small sample sizes per arm (sealing ability n=7, adaptability n=3, though power-calculated); adaptability analysis was explicitly descriptive/qualitative with no inferential statistics.
- Single bioceramic sealer (CeraSeal) and single gutta-percha coating duration (72 h) tested; findings may not generalize to other sealers or coating protocols.
- Dye penetration is a simple, standardized but lower-resolution leakage method versus fluid filtration, bacterial leakage models, or micro-CT; sectioning for SEM risked artifacts (microcracks, partial detachment) despite water cooling.
- In-vitro only — no clinical, thermal-cycling, long-term durability, handling, or retreatability outcome data; authors explicitly call for in-vivo and long-term studies plus micro-CT-based volumetric assessment before clinical translation.
- Operator blinding during obturation was not feasible (coated vs uncoated cones differ visibly in color), though the dye-penetration examiner was blinded.

## 6. Related Work

- Park and Jung; Yu et al.: PDA coating increased surface hydrophilicity on PEEK spinal implants and reduced water contact angle on zirconia (Y-TZP) respectively — cited as precedent for PDA's cross-substrate hydrophilization effect, extended here to gutta-percha.
- Ferreira et al.: plasma-treated gutta-percha retained the same core ATR-FTIR peaks post-treatment — concordant with this study's finding that PDA coating does not alter gutta-percha's underlying chemical structure.
- Tay et al.: resin-based sealers showed more coronal than apical interfacial gaps (attributed to shrinkage from greater coronal sealer volume) — contrasted here, where the bioceramic-sealer groups (both coated and uncoated) showed the opposite gradient (largest gaps apically).
- Prior commercial coated-gutta-percha systems (methacrylate-resin-based, bioceramic-coated, glass-ionomer-coated, silver-mesoporous-calcium-silicate) are noted as inconsistent/nonhomogeneous in coating distribution — framing PDA as a more uniform alternative surface-modification strategy.
- Kardon et al.: no significant sealing-ability difference between single-cone and warm vertical compaction with bioceramic sealers — cited as rationale for using the single-cone technique in this study's design.

## 7. Glossary

- **Polydopamine (PDA)** — a mussel-inspired, catechol/amine-rich polymer formed by oxidative self-polymerization of dopamine; used here as a thin (~50-200 nm), adherent, hydrophilizing coating on gutta-percha.
- **CeraSeal** — premixed calcium-silicate-based (bioceramic) root canal sealer (Meta Biomed, Korea); hydrophilic, forms hydroxyapatite via reaction with dentinal moisture, expands on setting ("self-seal").
- **Single-cone technique** — obturation using one master gutta-percha cone matched to the final file size plus sealer, without lateral/vertical compaction; relies heavily on sealer interfacial adaptation.
- **Simulated body fluid (SBF)** — laboratory-prepared solution with ion concentrations approximating human blood plasma, used to assess in-vitro bioactivity/apatite-forming ability.
- **ESEM** — environmental scanning electron microscope; used here to visualize gutta-percha-sealer interfacial gaps and surface morphology/coating uniformity.
- **EDX** — energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy; elemental composition analysis paired with SEM imaging.
- **ATR-FTIR** — attenuated total reflectance Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy; chemical/functional-group characterization technique used to confirm PDA deposition.
- **Contact angle** — angle formed by a liquid droplet on a solid surface; lower angle indicates better wetting/adaptation, measured here via the spherical cap model on an optical tensiometer.
