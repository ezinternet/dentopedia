## Why Ingested

No held paper measured operative time differences between screw-retained and conometric retention for full-arch prostheses. This in-vitro study provides the first time-comparison data, identifying that the two systems have inverse efficiency profiles: screw-retained is faster for delivery (provisional preference), conometric is faster for maintenance check-ups (permanent preference). Relevant for clinical workflow planning and recall scheduling.

[[implants/full-arch/jacotti-2026-conometric-vs-screw-operative-time-in-vitro]]
## Three-line Summary

In-vitro comparison of screw-retained vs conometric technique (Orbit, Bionica system) for All-on-4 and All-on-6 full-arch prostheses; activation phase established 15Ncm as the optimal torque for conometric coping activation without deformation; time phase compared delivery and check-up times across operators with different experience levels.

Screw-retained faster at delivery (All-on-4: 6min 4s vs 13min 56s; All-on-6: 8min 0s vs 18min 37s); conometric faster at check-up (All-on-4: 9min 2s vs 0min 30s; All-on-6: 12min 41s vs 0min 43s); conometric less operator-dependent.

Screw-retained preferred for provisional restorations (faster delivery); conometric preferred for permanent restorations (faster check-ups); 15Ncm activation torque produces no coping deformation.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First time-comparison data between screw-retained and conometric for full-arch prostheses
- Establishes optimal conometric activation torque: 15Ncm (no coping deformation; <10Ncm insufficient retention, >15Ncm causes deformation at lower values)
- Identifies inverse efficiency: screw faster delivery, conometric faster maintenance
- Conometric approach more standardized and less operator-experience dependent
- Practical recommendation: screw-retained for provisionals, conometric for finals

## 3. Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | In-vitro experimental (bench study) |
| System | Orbit implant-prosthetic connection (Bionica) — allows both techniques on same platform |
| Phase 1 | Activation: torque range 10–30Ncm, pull-off force measured per torque value |
| Phase 2 | Time comparison: delivery and check-up, multiple operators (different experience) |
| Configurations tested | All-on-4, All-on-6 |
| Primary outcome | Operative time (delivery, check-up), coping deformation |

## 4. Key Results

### Activation Phase

| Torque | Coping condition |
|---|---|
| 10–14 Ncm | Insufficient data / lower retention |
| **15 Ncm** | **No coping deformation** — optimal activation |
| >15 Ncm | Deformation observed |

### Time Phase

| Configuration | Metric | Screw-retained | Conometric |
|---|---|---|---|
| All-on-4 | Delivery | **6 min 4 s** | 13 min 56 s |
| All-on-6 | Delivery | **8 min 0 s** | 18 min 37 s |
| All-on-4 | Check-up | 9 min 2 s | **0 min 30 s** |
| All-on-6 | Check-up | 12 min 41 s | **0 min 43 s** |

### Operator Dependence

Conometric technique showed less variability between operators of different experience levels — the friction-fit activation is more standardized than torque-dependent screw placement.

## 5. Clinical Interpretation

The **inverse efficiency** profile has direct implications for prosthetic planning:

- **Provisional phase**: Screw-retained preferred — faster delivery reduces chair time during initial placement when adjustments and remakes are more likely
- **Permanent phase**: Conometric preferred — the 6-monthly recall hygiene appointments take ~0.5 min vs 9–12 min per arch with screw access, compounding significant time savings over years
- **Activation torque 15Ncm**: A practical protocol parameter — consistent with the screw-retained torque values commonly used (15–25Ncm), making hybrid transitions on the same Orbit platform feasible

## 6. Limitations

- In-vitro — does not capture real clinical variables (patient anatomy, saliva, soft tissue access, restoration bulk)
- Single system tested (Orbit/Bionica) — may not generalize to other conometric systems
- Sample size and statistical analysis details not available from abstract
- Long-term retention stability of conometric under masticatory load not assessed
