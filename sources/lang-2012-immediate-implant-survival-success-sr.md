## Why Ingested

Foundational, highly-cited (Lang et al. 2012, Clin Oral Implants Res) SR pooling 46 prospective studies on Type I (immediate) implant survival/success — a landmark quantitative baseline (annual failure rate 0.82%) that predates and complements [[immediate-implant/esposito-2010-fresh-extraction-sockets-immediate-cochrane]]'s RCT-only Cochrane review; adds a much larger prospective-cohort evidence base and factor analysis (extraction reason, antibiotics, position, loading) not covered by the Cochrane RCT pool.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 46 prospective studies (mean follow-up 2.08 years) on Type I immediate implant placement into fresh extraction sockets, searched via MEDLINE/Cochrane Library (1991–July 2010).

Annual failure rate 0.82% (95% CI 0.48–1.39%), translating to 2-year survival of 98.4% (97.3–99%); of five factors analyzed (extraction reason, antibiotic use, implant position, loading type), only postoperative antibiotic use significantly reduced failure; marginal bone loss was mostly <1 mm and occurred predominantly in year 1.

Clinical implication: immediate implant survival is high, but success (biological/technical/aesthetic complications) was difficult to assess due to sparse reporting — more long-term studies with standardized complication reporting are needed, with special attention to aesthetic outcomes.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Clinical Oral Implants Research 2012;23(Suppl 5):39–66

- **Institution**: Faculty of Dentistry, The University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong SAR, China (Lang also affiliated with University of Bern, Switzerland)

## 2. Key Contributions
- Pooled quantitative survival estimate (annual failure rate 0.82%, 2-year survival 98.4%) across 46 prospective studies of Type I immediate implants — one of the earliest large-scale quantitative syntheses specific to immediate placement
- Factor analysis identifying postoperative antibiotic use as the only significant predictor of survival among 5 candidate factors (reason for extraction, antibiotic regimen, anterior/posterior and maxilla/mandible position, loading protocol)
- Documented the timeline of soft- and hard-tissue change: soft tissue changes concentrated in the first 3 months after restoration then stabilize by year 1; marginal bone loss predominantly occurs in year 1 and is generally <1 mm
- Flagged persistent controversy over whether platform-switching preserves hard tissue better than conventional platforms — unresolved as of this review
- Highlighted a methodological gap: "success" (as opposed to survival) could not be reliably assessed because biological, technical, and aesthetic complications were inconsistently reported across the included studies

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review with quantitative pooling (weighted means via inverse-variance method; STATA for survival-rate computation) — no forest-plot meta-analysis of RCTs, since almost all included studies were prospective (non-comparative) cohorts
- **Databases**: MEDLINE (PubMed) and the Cochrane Library, 1991 to July 2010
- **n**: 46 prospective studies included (mean follow-up 2.08 years); a large parallel list of excluded studies is documented with exclusion reasons (e.g., not reporting on immediate implants, mean follow-up <1 year, unclear immediate-implant survival subgroup)
- **Outcomes**: implant/prosthesis survival and success rates; prevalence of biological, technical, and aesthetic complications; magnitude of soft- and hard-tissue dimensional change

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Annual failure rate: 0.82% (95% CI 0.48–1.39%)
- 2-year survival rate (derived): 98.4% (95% CI 97.3–99%)
- Of 5 factors analyzed (reason for extraction, antibiotic use, implant position [anterior vs. posterior, maxilla vs. mandible], type of loading): only antibiotic regimen significantly affected survival — courses of postoperative antibiotics associated with lower failure rates
- Success rate: not reliably quantifiable — biological/technical/aesthetic complication reporting was too scarce/heterogeneous across included studies
- Soft tissue changes: occurred mostly in the first 3 months after provision of the restoration, stabilizing toward the end of year 1
- Marginal bone loss: predominantly occurs in year 1 post-placement, generally <1 mm in magnitude
- Platform-switching vs. conventional platform: hard-tissue preservation benefit remains controversial/unresolved in the pooled evidence

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Predominantly non-comparative prospective cohort data (not RCT-based), limiting causal inference on the 5 analyzed factors
- Success (vs. survival) could not be robustly evaluated due to inconsistent complication reporting across studies
- Search cutoff July 2010 — does not capture more recent evidence on socket-shield, immediate provisionalization, or CTG-based esthetic protocols
- Authors explicitly call for more long-term studies with standardized outcome/complication reporting, with particular attention to aesthetic outcomes

## 7. Glossary
- **Type I implant placement**: Implant placed immediately into a fresh extraction socket at the same surgical visit as extraction
- **Annual failure rate**: Number of implant failures per implant-year of follow-up, used to derive cumulative survival estimates
- **Marginal bone loss (MBL)**: Radiographically measured loss of crestal bone height around an implant over time
- **Platform switching**: Prosthetic/implant design where the abutment diameter is narrower than the implant platform, hypothesized to reduce peri-implant crestal bone loss
