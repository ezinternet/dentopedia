## Why Ingested

The wiki holds strong evidence that interdental brushes (IDBs) are the most effective interdental aid (e.g. [[interdental-cleaning/kotsakis-2018-network-meta-analysis-interproximal]] network meta-analysis; [[interdental-cleaning/worthington-2019-home-use-interdental-cleaning]] Cochrane), but that IDB *handling* is a recurring failure point — patients struggle with insertion, positioning, and reaching approximal cervical areas, especially where guide surfaces are missing. This RCT (Spindler 2026) introduces a novel adjunct — a customized 3D-printed cleaning splint with funnel-shaped guide openings — that directly targets that handling gap, extending the IDB-efficacy evidence base with a device that improves *how well the gold-standard aid is actually delivered*. It reinforces the IDB-as-first-line position while adding a technology solution for anatomically challenging cases (missing adjacent teeth, partial edentulism).

## Three-line Summary

Randomized clinical crossover pilot RCT (n=30 periodontitis patients, age 21–82) comparing customized 3D-printed cleaning splints plus interdental brushes (CS+IDBs) versus IDBs alone, each participant as own control over 2-week intervention/washout phases.

CS+IDBs produced a markedly greater plaque reduction than IDBs alone (median ∆Quigley-Hein index −1.38 vs −0.23, p<0.001; random-effects ANCOVA CS estimate −0.62, p<0.001); gingival-inflammation (GI) and bleeding-on-probing (BOP) benefits were significant overall but reached significance only in younger participants (AG1 ≤40 y), not in seniors (AG2 ≥65 y).

Cleaning splints are a promising handling adjunct to raise IDB effectiveness — notably enhancing cleaning where adjacent teeth are missing — but this is an exploratory, unblinded, 2-week pilot with a ~360 € per-device cost, so long-term adherence and periodontal impact remain unproven.

## 1. Document Information

- **Type**: Randomized controlled clinical trial (parallel crossover, exploratory pilot)
- **Journal**: Clinical and Experimental Dental Research, 2026; 12(3):e70392
- **Registration**: German Clinical Trials Register (DRKS00034674); CONSORT 2025 compliant
- **Ethics**: Heidelberg University Faculty of Medicine (No. S-387/2023)
- **Setting**: Department of Conservative Dentistry, Heidelberg University, Germany (Oct 2023–Aug 2024)
- **Conflict of interest**: Two authors (Zenthöfer, Rues) hold a non-commercial European patent on the investigated cleaning splints; no external funding reported.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **First clinical (in-vivo) evidence** for cleaning splints — prior support was in-vitro typodont only (Rues et al.). Converts a bench concept into an RCT-tested adjunct.
- Demonstrates that a **customized 3D-printed guide splint** with funnel-shaped triangular openings significantly improves IDB plaque removal (∆QHI −1.38 vs −0.23) without obstructing brush insertion.
- Shows the effect is **independent of manual dexterity** (apraxia score no influence, no CS×apraxia interaction), suggesting the guidance mechanism (not just user skill) drives the benefit.
- Identifies **enhanced effectiveness precisely where IDBs struggle most** — interdental spaces with missing adjacent teeth (significant CS×gap interaction) — the anatomically challenging cases motivating the design.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Randomized parallel crossover; each participant served as both experimental (CS+IDBs) and control (IDBs alone) at different time points. Unblinded block randomization stratified by age (sealed envelopes). Blinding not feasible.
- **Population**: 30 periodontitis patients completing (31 enrolled, 1 dropout — cardiovascular event). Age-stratified: AG1 ≤40 y (n=15) and AG2 ≥65 y (n=15). Inclusion: informed consent, ≥2 teeth in one jaw. Exclusion: recent antibiotics, severe systemic conditions.
- **Splint fabrication**: Intraoral scan (Primescan) → 3D-printed splint (Freeprint Splint 2.0, Detax). Funnel-shaped guide openings at each interdental space; triangular cross-section incircle radius 0.5 mm > passage hole diameter; guide surfaces along terminal teeth / edentulous-adjacent surfaces; guide-to-tooth distance 1.0 or 2.0 mm.
- **Protocol**: 2-week washout → baseline (T0) → 2-week intervention → reassess → 2-week washout → crossover 2-week intervention → final. Cylindrical IDBs (Curaprox) individually sized by passage hole diameter (max 3 sizes/patient); straight in-and-out motion. Elmex toothpaste standardized during washouts.
- **Outcomes**: Primary — Quigley-Hein plaque index (QHI, Turesky modification). Secondary — Gingival Index (GI, Löe & Silness), BOP, PPD, CAL, at 4 sites/tooth mesial+distal. Modifiers: Goldenberg Apraxia Test (motor skills), MEDAS (diet), PSS-10 (stress).
- **Statistics**: Approximal space as primary unit; two-step aggregation to patient level. No carry-over detected → pooled analysis. Wilcoxon signed-rank (within), Mann–Whitney (between). Random-effects ANCOVA (Patient ID random; fixed: intervention, baseline, apraxia, MEDAS, PSS; interactions for gaps and anterior/posterior). SAS JMP v18.2.1, α=0.05. Exploratory — p-values interpreted descriptively (no a priori power calculation).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Primary (QHI)**: median ∆QHI −1.38 (CS+IDBs) vs −0.23 (IDBs alone), p<0.001. 2-week QHI: CS+ 1.48 vs CS− 2.87 (intergroup p<0.001). Effect consistent across AG1 (2W p=0.006) and AG2 (2W p<0.001). Within AG1, a significant QHI reduction occurred ONLY when CS was used.
- **Random-effects ANCOVA**: CS estimate −0.620 (95% CI −0.731 to −0.510), p<0.001; higher baseline QHI → greater reduction (−0.434, p<0.001); apraxia no effect (p=0.854), no CS×apraxia interaction.
- **GI**: CS+ 0.26→0.13 (p<0.001) vs CS− 0.31→0.24 (p=0.089); overall intergroup p=0.002. Significant within AG1 (p=0.010) but not decisively driving AG2 intergroup (p=0.080). ANCOVA CS estimate −0.084 (p=0.023); MEDAS diet quality associated with reduced GI (−0.052, p=0.033).
- **BOP**: CS+ 12.77%→6.27% (p<0.001) vs CS− 11.25%→9.83% (p=0.078); overall intergroup p=0.034, significant in AG1 (p=0.020) not AG2 (p=0.213). ANCOVA CS estimate −0.019 (p<0.001); notable PSS×CS interaction (−0.003, p=0.001) — stress modulates CS-supported BOP reduction.
- **Missing teeth / region**: Gaps reduced plaque removal (+0.107, p=0.002) but significant CS×gap interaction (−0.126, p<0.001) → CS *more* effective in gap areas. Front teeth slightly better regardless of treatment (−0.070, p=0.008); no CS×tooth-area interaction (splint neutralized region effect).
- **Safety**: No harms or adverse events related to the interventions.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **2-week intervention/washout phases only** — no long-term adherence, handling-improvement-with-practice, or durable periodontal-outcome conclusions.
- **Exploratory pilot**, feasibility-based sample size, no a priori power → results are hypothesis-generating; p-values descriptive.
- **Unblinded** examiner (observer bias) and aware participants (performance bias).
- University-based periodontitis population → limited generalizability.
- No microbiological analysis; toothbrush type not standardized (background plaque variability).
- **Cost ~360 € per customized device** plus maintenance/storage burden — real barriers to adoption; long-term durability not systematically assessed.
- Future: long-term cost-effectiveness, durability, patient-reported handling/maintenance outcomes; application in nursing-home / vulnerable / manually-limited populations.

## 6. Related Work

- Rues et al. — in-vitro typodont precursor showing improved approximal cleaning with the splint (the bench basis this RCT clinically tests).
- Passia and Kern; Kern — cleaning splints improving proximal access for double crown prostheses and implants (prosthesis-specific designs; this splint is adaptable to all dentitions).
- Sälzer et al.; Sanz et al. — IDBs as the most effective interdental aid for gingivitis reduction (rationale for optimizing IDB delivery rather than replacing it).
- Wolff et al.; Slot et al. — documented IDB handling problems (bending, distortion, incorrect insertion, soft-tissue trauma) that the splint is designed to solve.

## 7. Glossary

- **CS (Cleaning Splint)**: Customized 3D-printed intraoral appliance with funnel-shaped guide openings to direct interdental brush insertion into the approximal space.
- **IDB (Interdental Brush)**: Cylindrical/conical brush inserted between teeth for mechanical interdental plaque removal; the recognized gold-standard interdental aid.
- **QHI (Quigley-Hein plaque index, Turesky modification)**: Ordinal plaque-scoring index (0–5) per tooth surface; the study's primary plaque outcome.
- **GI (Gingival Index, Löe & Silness)**: Ordinal index of gingival inflammation severity.
- **BOP (Bleeding on Probing)**: Percentage of periodontal sites bleeding on gentle probing; marker of gingival/periodontal inflammation.
- **PHD (Passage Hole Diameter)**: Clinically assessed diameter of an interdental space used to select IDB size and to dimension the splint's guide opening.
- **MEDAS (Mediterranean Diet Adherence Screener)**: 14-point validated short diet-quality questionnaire.
- **PSS-10 (Perceived Stress Scale)**: 10-item validated perceived-stress questionnaire.
- **Apraxia score (Goldenberg test)**: Measure of manual/motor coordination skill.
