## Three-line Summary

Narrative literature review (MEDLINE 1990–June 2003; 31 studies included, ≥10 cases; survival/success studies required ≥12 months follow-up) of immediate and delayed (early) implant placement after tooth extraction, covering extraction-socket healing biology, clinical procedures, and outcomes.

All included studies reported implant survival but none reported formal clinical success; peri-implant defects showed high regenerative potential, with small horizontal defects (≤2 mm) often healing by spontaneous bone fill around rough-surface implants while larger/damaged defects required barrier membranes and grafts; immediate and delayed-immediate implants showed short-term survival comparable to implants in healed ridges.

Clinical implication: immediate/early placement can be predictable for short-term survival, but long-term evidence on peri-implant tissue health, prosthesis stability, and esthetic outcomes was limited at the time, and treatment choice must balance infection control/soft-tissue management advantages of delayed approaches against ridge resorption during healing.

포함 연구 모두 임플란트 생존율(survival)은 보고했으나 임상적 성공(success) 보고는 없었고, 임플란트 주위 결손은 재생 잠재력이 높아 수평 결손 ≤2 mm에서는 거친 표면 임플란트 주변 자발적 골충전이 가능하나 더 큰/손상 결손은 차폐막·골이식 등 병행 증대가 필요했으며, 즉시·지연 즉시 식립의 단기 생존율은 치유 치조제 식립과 유사했다.

임상적 의미: 즉시/조기 식립은 단기 생존 측면에서 예측 가능할 수 있으나, 장기 조직 건강·보철 안정성·심미 결과 근거는 당시 제한적이었고, 지연 식립의 감염 해소·연조직 확보 이점과 치유 기간 중 치조제 흡수 증가를 함께 고려해야 한다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Synthesized biologic basis of extraction-socket healing (histologic stages, dimensional changes) as context for timing of implant placement.
- Reviewed clinical procedures and outcomes for immediate and delayed/early placement, including defect morphology, augmentation needs, and soft-tissue considerations.
- Highlighted that peri-implant defects have strong regenerative potential: horizontal defects ≤2 mm may heal by spontaneous fill with rough-surface implants, whereas larger or damaged defects generally require barrier membranes and grafts.
- Emphasized evidence gap: survival was commonly reported, but standardized long-term success outcomes (peri-implant tissue health, prosthesis stability, esthetics) were lacking.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Narrative literature review.
- **Search**: MEDLINE, 1990–June 2003; bibliographies of prior reviews and identified studies also screened.
- **Inclusion**: Randomized/nonrandomized clinical trials, cohort studies, case-control studies, and case reports with ≥10 cases; studies reporting success/survival required ≥12 months follow-up.
- **Included evidence**: 31 articles identified. Most were short-term and not randomized by timing/augmentation method.
- **Outcomes considered**: Implant survival/success, peri-implant defect healing/regeneration, clinical procedures, and esthetic/soft-tissue considerations.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Survival vs success**: All included studies reported survival data; no reports on formal clinical success outcomes.
- **Defect healing**: Peri-implant defects had high potential for healing by bone regeneration irrespective of healing protocol and augmentation method (as synthesized by authors).
- **Horizontal defect threshold**: Sites with horizontal defects (HD) ≤2 mm healed by spontaneous bone fill when rough-surface implants were used.
- **Augmentation need**: HD >2 mm or damaged socket walls required concomitant augmentation (barrier membranes and bone grafts).
- **Timing trade-offs**: Delayed placement allowed resolution of local infection and increased soft-tissue area/volume for flap adaptation, but ridge resorption during healing could increase augmentation requirements.
- **Comparative survival**: Immediate and delayed-immediate implants appeared predictable with short-term survival rates comparable to implants placed in healed ridges.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Evidence base available at review time was dominated by short-term, often nonrandomized studies with heterogeneous protocols.
- Lack of standardized reporting on long-term clinical success, peri-implant tissue health, prosthesis stability, and esthetic outcomes.
- Authors’ synthesis supports need for better-controlled, longer-term studies with consistent outcome definitions.

## 7. Glossary
- **Immediate implant placement**: Implant placed into a fresh extraction socket at or near the time of tooth extraction.
- **Delayed/early placement**: Implant placement after some period of socket healing rather than immediately at extraction.
- **Horizontal defect (HD)**: Gap/defect dimension in the horizontal plane between implant surface and socket wall.
- **Guided bone regeneration (GBR)**: Use of barrier membranes with/without graft materials to promote bone regeneration in defects.
- **Implant survival vs success**: Survival indicates the implant remains in function/present; success typically requires additional criteria (tissue health, stability, esthetics, lack of complications).
