## Why Ingested

The whole anterior-maxilla buccal-wall literature we hold is CBCT-derived — pooled ranges in [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/shafizadeh-2021-alveolar-bone-thickness-healthy-sr]] ("often ≤1 mm"), [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/altarawneh-2018-bone-dimensions-anterior-maxilla-cbct]] and [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/dossantos-2019-buccal-bone-root-inclination-alveolar-cbct]] — and clinical CBCT cannot resolve bone reliably below roughly its voxel size. This study re-measures the same buccal/palatal dimensions by **micro-CT on dry skulls**, which resolves sub-millimetre bone without beam-hardening or partial-volume error, so it qualifies the magnitude of the existing CBCT estimates (see `relations: refines` on the wiki page) rather than merely repeating them. It also directly stress-tests the ~2 mm facial-wall premise in [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2017-dimensional-bone-soft-tissue-post-extraction]] by showing that at every level from crestal to 9 mm the buccal wall rarely exceeds 0.5 mm.

## Three-line Summary

Cadaveric ex-vivo micro-CT morphometric study (Clin Implant Dent Relat Res 2023;25(2):261-270; PMID 36596566) — 72 dry human skulls scanned at the South African Nuclear Energy Corporation, with axial planes created over every alveolus/tooth from canine to canine and buccal/palatal bone dimensions measured at crestal, 3 mm, 6 mm and 9 mm levels.

Buccal bone rarely exceeded 0.5 mm and consisted of bundle bone only, for all investigated teeth at all levels; up to a third of teeth showed buccal fenestrations; palatal bone was thicker than buccal and increased from <1 mm at crestal level up to 3.77 mm (centrals), 4.56 mm (laterals) and 5.43 mm (canines) at the 9 mm level.

Clinical implication: immediate implant placement in the anterior maxilla carries a genuine anatomic risk because the facial bone is essentially all bundle bone and is fenestrated in up to a third of teeth, so thorough per-site planning and an individualised approach are required; the limitation is that this is a cadaveric bone-only morphometric dataset, not a clinical cohort — the abstract reports no soft tissue, no clinical outcome, no voxel size, and no skull demographics or statistics.

## 세줄요약

시체(ex-vivo) 미세전산단층촬영(micro-CT) 형태계측 연구 — 남아프리카 원자력발전공사(South African Nuclear Energy Corporation)에서 건조 인체 두개골 72개를 스캔하고, 상악 견치~견치 구역의 각 치조·치아마다 축면(axial plane)을 생성하여 협측·구개측 골 두께를 치아경사부(crestal)·3 mm·6 mm·9 mm 수준에서 측정함.

협측골(buccal bone)은 조사한 모든 치아의 모든 수준에서 0.5 mm를 거의 넘지 않았고 전부 다발뼈(bundle bone)만이었으며, 최대 1/3의 치아에서 협측 개구부(fenestration)가 관찰됨. 구개측(palatal) 골은 협측보다 두꺼워 치아경사부에서 <1 mm로 시작해 9 mm 수준에서 중앙치 3.77 mm, 측치 4.56 mm, 견치 5.43 mm에 이름.

임상적 함의: 협측골이 사실상 다발뼈뿐이고 최대 1/3에서 개구부를 보이므로 상악 전치부 즉시식립(immediate implant placement)은 실제 해부학적 위험을 안고 있어 부위별 철저한 계획과 개별적 접근이 요구됨. 한계는 생존 환자 임상 코호트가 아닌 건조 두개골cadaveric 자료이며, 초록 기반이라 연조직·임상결과·복셀 크기·두개골 인구 특성 및 통계는 보고되지 않았다는 점.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Buccal alveolar bone in the anterior maxilla is quantified at **micro-CT** resolution rather than clinical CBCT resolution — the abstract's explicit motivation, since "many CBCT studies have assessed alveolar bone dimensions in the anterior maxilla, with varying results."
- First-in-kind claim (as stated by the authors) that buccal bone **rarely exceeded 0.5 mm** and was composed of **bundle bone only** for *all* investigated teeth at *all* measured levels.
- Quantifies **buccal fenestrations in up to a third of teeth** — a per-site risk datum that most CBCT studies in this wiki report only qualitatively.
- Provides palatal bone reference values by tooth type at the 9 mm level (central 3.77 mm, lateral 4.56 mm, canine 5.43 mm), i.e. the bone actually available for primary stability in an immediately-placed implant.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: ex-vivo cadaveric morphometric (anatomic) study — micro-CT measurement of alveolar bone dimensions. Not a clinical cohort, not an intervention study.
- **Specimens**: 72 human dry skulls. Teeth analysed: anterior maxilla, **canine to canine** (maxillary incisors and canines). Number of teeth/alveoli actually measured is not stated in the abstract.
- **Imaging**: micro-CT (high-resolution), scanned at the South African Nuclear Energy Corporation. Voxel size, tube settings and field of view are not reported in the abstract.
- **Post-processing**: specialised software for 3-D rendering, segmentation and visualisation of the reconstructed volume data. Software name/version is not given in the abstract.
- **Measurement protocol**: axial planes created over each alveolus/tooth; buccal and palatal bone dimensions measured at four apicocoronal levels — crestal, 3 mm, 6 mm and 9 mm.
- **Outcomes**: buccal bone thickness, palatal bone thickness, buccal fenestration presence, and bone composition (bundle bone) at each level and tooth type.
- **Not reported in the abstract**: inter-/intra-rater reliability, blinding, statistical tests, p-values, CIs, mean vs median vs range for the quoted values, and any correlation analysis.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Measurement | Result (verbatim from abstract) |
|---|---|
| Buccal bone thickness | Rarely exceeded **0.5 mm** |
| Buccal bone composition | **Bundle bone only** for all investigated teeth at all levels |
| Buccal fenestrations | **Up to a third of teeth** |
| Palatal vs buccal | Palatal **thicker than buccal** |
| Palatal at crestal level | **<1 mm** |
| Palatal at 9 mm — central incisor | **3.77 mm** |
| Palatal at 9 mm — lateral incisor | **4.56 mm** |
| Palatal at 9 mm — canine | **5.43 mm** |

- Direction of change: palatal bone thickness **increases** apico-coronally (from <1 mm at the crestal level to the 3.77–5.43 mm range at 9 mm); buccal bone shows no such gradient, staying ≤0.5 mm at every level.
- The 3 mm and 6 mm palatal values are **not given** in the abstract — only the crestal (<1 mm) and 9 mm endpoints are reported.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **Abstract-only record**: measurement uncertainty, the segmentation protocol, whether the quoted values are means or medians, and all supporting statistics are unavailable, so the numbers cannot be checked against the paper's tables.
- **Cadaveric, bone-only**: dry skulls have no mucosa, periosteum or connective tissue, so the measured buccal thickness is not the same quantity as the *clinical* buccal plate-plus-soft-tissue envelope a CBCT-based (living-patient) study sees. This inflates the apparent discrepancy between this paper and CBCT series and is the main reason the two sets of numbers should not be pooled directly.
- **Population transferability unknown**: age, sex, ancestry and health status of the 72 skulls are not reported; age in particular matters because buccal plate thickness and alveolar dimensions change with age.
- **Morphometry only**: no implant was placed, so the study quantifies anatomic feasibility/risk but provides no outcome data (survival, marginal bone loss, esthetic score).
- **Single time point**: no post-extraction measurement, so the dataset cannot speak to resorption dynamics (cf. the ≤1 mm thin-wall → 7.5 mm vertical loss finding in [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2013-ridge-alterations-post-extraction-esthetic]]).
- Authors' own conclusion, stated as the forward-looking implication: a thorough, individualised planning approach is required to avoid complications and achieve stable long-term esthetic and functional results.
- As the abstract notes, micro-CT skull data cannot be applied to a living patient without accounting for the resolution difference — the higher the resolution, the thinner the measured buccal wall appears.

## 7. Glossary
- **Micro-CT (micro-computed tomography)**: high-resolution 3-D X-ray tomography of excised tissue or bone at the micrometre scale — far finer voxel size than clinical CBCT, and free of beam-hardening and partial-volume effects, but only possible on specimens that can be physically scanned.
- **Bundle bone**: tooth-dependent alveolar bone proper, a thin mineralised layer fused to root cementum; it is the first bone lost after extraction and is the reason a post-extraction facial wall remodels.
- **Buccal / labial bone**: the facial cortical plate of the alveolar process (labial in the anterior, buccal posteriorly).
- **Fenestration**: a circumscribed window where the root is denuded of bone but the overlying soft tissue/periosteum remains intact, as opposed to a dehiscence where bone and soft tissue are both missing.
- **Crestal level**: measurement taken at the alveolar crest; 3/6/9 mm denote apically displaced axial levels from the crest.
- **Immediate implant placement (IIP)**: type-1 placement into the extraction socket at the time of tooth removal; its prerequisite is sufficient apical alveolar bone for primary stability.
