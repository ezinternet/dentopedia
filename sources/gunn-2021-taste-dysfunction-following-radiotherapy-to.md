## Why Ingested

Systematic review with meta-analysis directly quantifying the prevalence (~96% acute, ~25% long-term) of radiation-induced taste dysfunction (미각장애, Taste Dysfunction) in head and neck cancer (두경부암, Head and Neck Cancer, HNC) radiotherapy — primary evidence base for the radiation dose–taste alteration relationship alongside [[oral-medicine/salivary-chemosensory/morelli-2023-radiation-dose-taste-alteration-hnscc]].

## Three-line Summary

A systematic review of 31 studies finding ~96% acute and ~25% long-term radiation-induced taste dysfunction in HNC radiotherapy, with dose to the anterior tongue and oral cavity as key predictors and proton therapy or bite blocks as mitigation strategies.

## 세줄요약

31편 체계적 고찰로 두경부 방사선치료(방사선요법, Radiotherapy, RT) 후 급성 미각장애 유병률 약 96%, 장기 약 25%를 메타분석하고, 전방 2/3 혀·구강에 대한 방사선량이 핵심 예측인자임을 확인하여 양성자치료와 바이트블록 등 선량 감소 전략의 근거를 제시한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Meta-analysed prevalence: acute taste dysfunction ~96% (95% CI 64–100%) by objective measures; ~79% (95% CI 65–88%) by subjective measures
- Long-term dysfunction (beyond acute phase): ~25% of patients
- Identified anterior two-thirds of tongue and oral cavity irradiation as anatomic predictors of worse taste loss
- Proposed dose constraint concept for taste preservation (gustatory organs-at-risk)
- Evidence that proton beam therapy and customized bite blocks reduce dose to gustatory field → less taste loss
- Established taste dysfunction as clinically significant: associated with weight loss and reduced quality-of-life (삶의 질, Quality of Life, QoL)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

*Abstract-only*

- **Acute taste dysfunction prevalence:** ~96% (95% CI 64–100%) objective; ~79% (95% CI 65–88%) subjective
- **Long-term taste dysfunction:** ~25% of patients
- **Recovery:** Majority show at least partial recovery from acute dysfunction
- **Sequelae:** Weight loss; reduced quality-of-life
- **Anatomic predictor:** Irradiation of oral cavity and anterior two-thirds of tongue → greater dysfunction
- **Dose-sparing strategies:** Proton beam therapy; customized bite blocks → reduced dose to gustatory field → reduced taste loss
- **Evidence gap:** Authors note pressing need for randomized studies or large prospective cohorts with confounder adjustment

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Included studies are heterogeneous in design, RT technique, and outcome assessment (objective vs. subjective)
- Wide 95% CIs (especially objective: 64–100%) reflect heterogeneity
- Evidence base relies largely on observational studies; randomized evidence limited
- Full-text not retrieved for this wiki entry
- Authors' call: prospective RCTs or large cohort studies with adequate confounder adjustment needed

## 7. Glossary

- **Dysgeusia (미각이상):** Distorted or unpleasant taste
- **Gustatory organs-at-risk:** Structures receiving radiation dose that contribute to taste function (taste buds on tongue, palate, epiglottis)
- **Proton beam therapy:** RT modality with steep dose falloff (Bragg peak) allowing dose reduction to organs at risk
- **Bite block:** Custom dental device that displaces tongue/oral structures out of the radiation field
- **Acute vs. long-term dysfunction:** Acute = during/immediately after RT; long-term = persisting beyond 6 months
