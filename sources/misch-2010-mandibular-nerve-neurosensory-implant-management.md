## Three-line Summary

Narrative review and expert protocol (Misch International Implant Institute survey, n=73% of dentists reporting postoperative paresthesia/dysesthesia/anesthesia) covering IAN neurosensory impairment etiology, classification, and management after dental implant surgery.

Reported incidence ranges 0–44%; protocol divides management into 4 time-based periods: intraoperative, 1-week, and 12-week postoperative, with pharmacologic (topical/oral dexamethasone, NSAIDs) and surgical (implant removal, microneurosurgery referral) decision branches.

Neurosurgery referral is indicated for observed nerve transection intraoperatively, dysesthesia/complete anesthesia at 1-week, or no sign of improvement by 12 weeks; immediate implant removal and topical dexamethasone (4 mg/mL) are the key intraoperative interventions.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **Standardized management protocol**: First organized protocol specifically addressing IAN neurosensory impairment after implant surgery divided into 4 time-based periods.
2. **Terminology unification**: Adopts Association for the Study of Pain nomenclature — paresthesia (altered, not unpleasant), dysesthesia (altered, unpleasant), anesthesia (total loss) — replacing inconsistent historical usage.
3. **Nerve anatomy for clinicians**: Reviews IAN histology (7,000–12,000 axons, 18–21 fascicles in third-molar region reducing to 12 at mental foramen) to contextualize injury severity.
4. **Classification mapping**: Aligns Seddon (3-stage) and Sunderland (5-stage) classifications with clinical decision thresholds meaningful to general dentists.
5. **Neurosensory testing battery**: Provides standardized nociceptive (pin-prick, temperature) and mechanoreceptive (static touch, direction of movement, two-point discrimination at 6 mm) examination protocol.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

This is an expert narrative review and protocol paper based on:
- Literature review of peripheral nerve anatomy, nerve injury classification (Seddon 1943; Sunderland 1951), and pharmacologic management.
- Clinical experience from the Misch International Implant Institute, including survey data indicating 73% of dentists encountered postoperative neurosensory impairment.
- Integration of pharmacologic evidence (dexamethasone, NSAIDs, antidepressants, anticonvulsants) and surgical treatment literature.

**Protocol structure (Table 3 — Neurosensory Deficit Treatment Algorithms)**:

| Time | If: | Pharmacologic | Treatment | Referral? |
|------|-----|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| Intraoperative | Nerve injury suspected | Dexamethasone 4 mg/mL topically 1 mL + 6-day oral course (4 mg × 2 tab AM × 3d, then 1 tab × 3d) | Cryotherapy 1 wk; implant repositioning if encroachment | No |
| Intraoperative | Known nerve transection | Same as above | Implant removal/reposition | **Yes — immediately** |
| 1 week post-op | Any neurosensory deficit | High-dose NSAIDs (ibuprofen 600–800 mg TID × 3 wk) | Palliative; repeat exam every 2 wk | Yes if dysesthesia or complete anesthesia |
| 12 weeks post-op | No sign of improvement | PRN | Palliative | **Yes** |

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- IAN contains 7,000–12,000 axons arranged in 18–21 fascicles (third-molar region) reducing to ~12 at the mental foramen.
- As a polyfascicular nerve, IAN withstands stretch injury better than monofascicular/oligofascicular nerves and has greater recovery potential.
- Reported incidence of nerve injury after implant procedures: **0–44%** (highly variable).
- 73% of dentists surveyed at Misch Institute reported experiencing postoperative neurosensory impairment.
- Two-point discrimination threshold: normal = 6 mm (used as baseline for mechanoreceptive testing).
- Topical dexamethasone (4 mg/mL, 1–2 mL applied 1–2 min) directly at the surgical site — no reported morbidity; significant improvement in recovery observed.
- Oral dexamethasone regimen: 4 mg × 2 tabs AM × 3 days, then 1 tab AM × 3 days, followed by ibuprofen 600–800 mg TID × 3 weeks.
- Neurosensory improvement most often occurs by 2–3 months; if not improved by 3–4 months, microneurosurgery window is narrowing.
- Cryotherapy: intense extraoral ice × 24 hours postoperatively, then episodic for 1 week — minimizes edema-induced secondary nerve compression.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative review / expert opinion without systematic search methodology or GRADE evidence assessment.
- No randomized trial data on the protocol's efficacy — recommendations based on published literature and institutional experience.
- Pharmacologic dosing (dexamethasone, ibuprofen) not validated in RCTs specific to implant-related nerve injury.
- The Sunderland/Seddon classifications are noted as difficult to apply clinically without direct surgical visualization of the nerve.
- Outcomes of the proposed protocol are not reported with denominator data.

## 6. Related Work

- Seddon HJ (1943) — 3-stage nerve injury classification (neuropraxia, axonotmesis, neurotmesis).
- Sunderland S (1951) — 5-stage subclassification of nerve injuries.
- Association for the Study of Pain nomenclature (referenced) — standardized neurosensory terminology.
- Pogrel MA and colleagues — microneurosurgery outcomes literature cited in discussion.

## 7. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Paresthesia | Altered sensation that is NOT unpleasant (IASP definition) |
| Dysesthesia | Altered sensation that IS unpleasant |
| Anesthesia | Total loss of feeling or sensation |
| Allodynia | Pain from a stimulus that normally does not provoke pain |
| Hypoesthesia | Decreased sensitivity to stimulation |
| Hyperesthesia | Increased sensitivity to stimulation |
| Neuropraxia (Seddon I) | Conduction block, no axonal degeneration — best prognosis |
| Axonotmesis (Seddon II) | Axonal injury with degeneration/regeneration potential |
| Neurotmesis (Seddon III) | Severe traction or complete transection — limited recovery |
| Endoneurium | Basal lamina + collagen surrounding individual nerve fibers |
| Perineurium | Multilayered sheath surrounding fascicles; maintains intrafascicular pressure |
| Epineurium | Outer connective tissue encasing all fascicles; structural continuity |
| Polyfascicular | Nerve with ≥10 fascicles and abundant interfascicular tissue (IAN type) |
| Two-point discrimination | 6 mm = normal threshold for mandibular nerve territory |
| IAN | Inferior alveolar nerve — most commonly injured nerve in implant dentistry |
