## Why Ingested

Fills a genuine gap: the wiki holds no prior page on TMJ arthritis of rheumatic/autoimmune origin specifically. This is the treatment side of that gap — per the authors' own claim, the first systematic review to evaluate conservative/rehabilitative pain-relief approaches specifically in rheumatic-disease patients with TMJ arthritis. Companion to [[tmj/tenorio-2026-ultrasonography-tmj-rheumatoid-arthritis-scoping-review]] (detection side of the same disease) and relevant to [[tmj/park-2024-dextrose-prolotherapy-tmd]] since dextrose perineural injection is one of the four interventions this review covers.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42024517524) evaluating rehabilitative/conservative pain-relief approaches for TMJ arthritis specifically in patients with rheumatic diseases (rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, Sjögren's syndrome, fibromyalgia, common variable immunodeficiency, chronic polyarthritis), searching PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science through February 25, 2024.

The yield was brutal: of 479 records, only 5 studies (1996–2021, n=11–91, four rheumatoid-arthritis-predominant plus one systemic-lupus-erythematosus cohort plus one mixed-diagnosis cohort) met inclusion criteria; interventions were four heterogeneous approaches — intra-articular corticosteroid injection (± lidocaine, ± irrigation) in three studies, dextrose subcutaneous perineural injection in one, and low-level laser therapy (LLLT) in one — assessed with the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Checklist, with GRADE quality ranging from low to high across studies.

The authors' own conclusion is the honest one: these approaches "might be effective" for pain relief, but the heterogeneity of the approaches and the low quality of the included studies "do not allow to draw certain conclusions" — this is a scoping-level evidence gap, not an endorsement of any specific technique, and the review does not address the safety of local joint injection in patients concurrently on systemic immunosuppressive therapy.

## 세줄요약

류마티스 질환(류마티스 관절염(Rheumatoid Arthritis, RA), 전신홍반루푸스(Systemic Lupus Erythematosus, SLE), 강직성척추염, 건선성 관절염, 쇼그렌증후군, 섬유근육통, 흔한변이형면역결핍증(Common Variable Immunodeficiency, CVID), 만성다발관절염 등)을 가진 환자의 측두하악관절(Temporomandibular Joint, TMJ) 관절염에 대한 재활적·보존적 통증완화 접근법의 효과를 평가한 체계적 문헌고찰(Systematic Review, SR)(PROSPERO 등록번호 CRD42024517524).

성과는 냉정하게 빈약하다 — PubMed·Scopus·Web of Science 479건 검색 중 최종 5편(1996~2021년, 대상자 11~91명; RA 중심 4편 + SLE 1편 + 혼합진단 1편)만 포함됐고, 4가지 개입(관절강내 코르티코스테로이드 주사(±리도카인, ±관절세척) 3편, 덱스트로스 피하 신경주위 주사 1편, 저출력레이저치료(Low-Level Laser Therapy, LLLT) 1편)이 시도됐으며, JBI(Joanna Briggs Institute) 비평평가체크리스트로 평가한 GRADE 근거 질은 낮음~높음까지 연구마다 혼재했다.

저자들 스스로의 결론이 핵심이다 — 이들 접근법은 "효과가 있을 수 있다(might be effective)"이지만 "개입의 이질성과 포함 연구의 낮은 질 때문에 확정적 결론을 내릴 수 없다"; 이는 특정 술기에 대한 지지가 아니라 근거 공백을 보여주는 scoping 수준의 결과이며, 면역억제 전신치료를 받는 환자에게 국소 관절 주사를 놓는 것의 안전성·상호작용은 이 리뷰가 다루지 않는다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review, per the authors, to evaluate conservative/rehabilitative approaches for pain relief specifically in rheumatic-disease patients with TMJ arthritis (as opposed to prior reviews on TMD prevalence in rheumatic disease, or on TMJ arthrocentesis/injection in the general TMD population).
- Documents that the entire evidence base for this specific question is 5 studies published 1996–2021 (n=11–91 each), spanning only four interventions: intra-articular corticosteroid injection (± lidocaine, ± irrigation, 3 studies), dextrose subcutaneous perineural injection (1 study — Elshoura et al. 2020), and LLLT (1 study — Benli et al. 2021).
- Applies the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist per study design (RCT / quasi-experimental / case series) and GRADE quality rating; only Benli et al. (RCT) reached "high" GRADE (11/13), while Trieger et al. (case series) was "low" (6/10) and the remaining three were "moderate."
- States explicitly that heterogeneity of interventions, conditions, and outcomes precluded meta-analysis, and that the low quality of included studies "do not allow to draw certain conclusions regarding the efficacy of these approaches" — the review's headline finding is the near-absence of evidence, not an efficacy ranking.
- Cites a related SR (Hysa et al., 56 papers) reporting TMJ-involvement prevalence in rheumatic disease of 8–70% depending on outcome measure, with symptoms typically appearing within 5 years of RA onset — useful epidemiological context this review itself does not re-derive.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **PICOS**: Participants = patients diagnosed with a rheumatic disease involving the TMJ; Intervention = rehabilitative approaches aimed at reducing pain; Comparison = placebo/sham/other interventions; Outcome = TMJ pain intensity (VAS or NRS); Study design = case series, cohort, cross-sectional, RCT.
- **Search**: PubMed (189 records), Scopus (266), Web of Science (24) — total 479, searched from inception to February 25, 2024, plus manual reference-list search of prior related SRs. Registered on PROSPERO (CRD42024517524); conducted per PRISMA and Cochrane Handbook guidance.
- **Exclusions**: pediatric/adolescent-growth patients, prior TMJ trauma, congenital/neoplastic TMJ conditions, cross-over designs, non-English studies, full-text unavailable (posters/conference abstracts), animal studies.
- **Screening funnel**: 479 → 115 duplicates removed → 364 title/abstract screened → 19 full-text screened → 3 not retrievable + 11 excluded for not meeting eligibility → **5 included**.
- **Quality assessment**: JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist (separate tool variants for RCT, quasi-experimental, and case-series designs), by two independent reviewers with third-reviewer arbitration on disagreement; GRADE approach used to rate evidence quality per study.
- No meta-analysis was performed — the authors state explicitly that heterogeneity of rehabilitative protocols, conditions, interventions, and outcomes made pooled quantitative synthesis impossible.

**Discrepancy in disease composition (source-data issue, reproduced not smoothed over)**: The abstract states "Four studies assessed patients affected by rheumatoid arthritis, **one systemic sclerodermia**, and one included patients affected by ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, Sjögren's syndrome, fibromyalgia, common variable immunodeficiency, and chronic polyarthritis." The Results body text and Table 2, however, both identify the fifth study (Benli et al.) as **systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)**, not systemic sclerosis (SSc) — two different diseases. This appears to be an error in the abstract that contradicts the paper's own body/table. Additionally, the stated composition (4 RA + 1 SLE/SSc + 1 mixed = 6) does not sum to the 5 included studies; the explanation, confirmed against Table 2, is that Alstergren et al.'s cohort is counted in both the "RA" bucket (9/22 of its patients had RA) and the "mixed" bucket (the same cohort also included AS, PA, Sjögren's, CVID, and unspecified polyarthritis/fibromyalgia patients) — i.e. one study is double-counted across the two category labels rather than there being a sixth study.

**Interventions overall**: intra-articular corticosteroid injection (3/5 studies, ± lidocaine, ± irrigation), dextrose subcutaneous perineural injection (1/5), LLLT (1/5). Only 2 of 5 studies included a control/comparison group. All patients remained on systemic therapy for their rheumatic disease throughout (NSAIDs, glucocorticoids, methotrexate, leflunomide, hydroxychloroquine, etanercept per Table 2) — the review reports what systemic drugs each cohort was on but does **not** analyze or discuss any interaction between the local rehabilitative intervention and concurrent systemic immunosuppression; that safety question is left unaddressed.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 5 studies met inclusion after a strict PICOS filter — authors acknowledge this "could negatively affect the clinical relevance of the study," attributing it to strict eligibility criteria combined with genuine scarcity of literature on this specific question.
- Heterogeneity across interventions, rheumatic diagnoses, and outcome measures made meta-analysis impossible; only a narrative synthesis was performed.
- Low overall study quality (JBI/GRADE ranging low–moderate, with only one RCT reaching "high") "do not allow to draw a certain conclusion regarding the real efficacy of the analyzed treatment."
- Only 2/5 studies had a control group; follow-up windows were short (2–6 weeks, one study to a median 6.3 weeks) — no study reports long-term (>3 month) outcomes.
- The review does not address the interaction between local rehabilitative interventions (esp. intra-articular corticosteroid injection) and patients' concurrent systemic immunosuppressive therapy — a real gap for a dentist deciding whether to inject a TMJ in a patient on biologics/DMARDs.
- Authors call for further high-quality studies with larger samples to characterize efficacy of rehabilitative approaches in this population.

## 6. Related Work

- Cites Hysa et al. (56 papers) on TMD prevalence in immune-mediated rheumatic disease (8–70% depending on outcome measure; symptoms often within 5 years of RA onset; number of involved joints, global pain, and TNFα level as predictors) — the epidemiological complement to this review's treatment focus.
- Cites Vallon et al. (long-term, 12-year follow-up in 21 RA patients) as supporting evidence for corticosteroid intra-articular injection efficacy and safety, outside this review's own 5-study inclusion set.
- Cites Maddali Bongi et al. on 12-week home-exercise vs. combined physiotherapist-led rehabilitation protocols in systemic sclerosis patients with TMJ impairment — a study on exercise-based rehabilitation in SSc that did not meet this review's PICOS inclusion criteria but is discussed narratively.
- Companion to [[tmj/tenorio-2026-ultrasonography-tmj-rheumatoid-arthritis-scoping-review]] (imaging/detection of TMJ involvement in RA — the diagnostic counterpart to this review's treatment focus) and to [[tmj/ravelo-2025-tmj-replacement-degenerative-disease-sr]] (surgical replacement, the escalation pathway when conservative approaches like those catalogued here fail).

## 7. Glossary

- **TMD (Temporomandibular Disorders)**: 측두하악관절장애 — cluster of musculoskeletal conditions affecting the TMJ, masticatory muscles, and associated structures.
- **RD (Rheumatic Diseases)**: 류마티스 질환 — heterogeneous group of conditions affecting the musculoskeletal system (joints, tendons, ligaments, connective tissue), including autoimmune arthritides.
- **RA (Rheumatoid Arthritis)**: 류마티스 관절염 — chronic autoimmune inflammatory arthritis; the most-represented diagnosis in this review's included studies.
- **SLE (Systemic Lupus Erythematosus)**: 전신홍반루푸스 — systemic autoimmune disease; diagnosis of the Benli et al. cohort per body text/Table 2 (abstract states "systemic sclerodermia" — a discrepancy, see Section 4).
- **JBI (Joanna Briggs Institute) Critical Appraisal Checklist**: 조안나브릭스연구소 비평적평가체크리스트 — study-design-specific risk-of-bias tool used here for RCT, quasi-experimental, and case-series designs.
- **GRADE**: 근거등급 평가체계 (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluations) — used to rate evidence quality per included study.
- **LLLT (Low-Level Laser Therapy)**: 저출력레이저치료 — low-intensity laser applied to masticatory muscles for pain modulation; one of the four interventions catalogued in this review.
- **VAS/NRS (Visual Analogue Scale / Numerical Rating Scale)**: 시각아날로그척도/숫자평정척도 — patient-reported pain-intensity measures, the primary outcome across all 5 included studies.
- **CVID (Common Variable Immunodeficiency)**: 흔한변이형면역결핍증 — primary immunodeficiency disorder; diagnosis present in one patient within the Alstergren et al. mixed cohort.
