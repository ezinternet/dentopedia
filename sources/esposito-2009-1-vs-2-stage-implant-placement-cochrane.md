## Why Ingested

This Cochrane systematic review (originally published 2007, last amended 2009, withdrawn 2018) was the only Cochrane-branded synthesis on **one-stage vs two-stage implant placement** (submerged vs non-submerged healing) — a foundational surgical-technique question. It is missing from the wiki even though several later SRs on the same comparison exist (e.g., [[implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]], [[implants/mbl/al-amri-2016-crestal-bone-loss-submerged]], [[implants/survival/troiano-2018-early-late-failure-submerged]]). Ingesting the original Cochrane version, despite its withdrawal, lets the evidence trail show how the evidence base evolved: the 2009 Cochrane update included 3 new studies but still found the evidence insufficient; the 2018 withdrawal flagged it as out-of-date. The current wiki already has later non-Cochrane SRs that supersede it.

## Three-line Summary

Cochrane systematic review (search to January 2009) comparing one-stage (transmucosal/non-submerged) versus two-stage (submerged) implant placement for replacing missing teeth.

Only one eligible RCT (Barber 1996, n=40) was identified and included in the 2009 update — the original 2007 version had zero included studies. The single RCT showed no significant difference in implant failure between one-stage and two-stage placement at 1-year follow-up.

The review was **withdrawn in 2018** (Issue 5) by Cochrane Oral Health because it was substantially out-of-date, did not meet current Cochrane methodological standards, and the authors were unable to update it. Conclusions are correct only as of the 2009 publication date.

- **Journal**: Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2009, Issue 1, Art. No. CD006698 (amended from 2007 Issue 3 publication)
- **DOI**: 10.1002/14651858.CD006698.pub3
- **Publication history**: Protocol first published Issue 3, 2007; Review first published Issue 3, 2007; Amended 12 June 2008 (format conversion); New search 9 March 2009 (3 new included studies); Minor edit 17 September 2009; **Withdrawn 23 May 2018 (Issue 5, 2018)**.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First (and only) Cochrane-branded systematic review directly comparing one-stage vs two-stage implant placement — the foundational surgical-technique question for submerged vs non-submerged healing.
- The 2009 update identified and included the Barber 1996 RCT (n=40), which the original 2007 version had excluded — a methodological note on how eligibility criteria shifts affected evidence inclusion.
- Explicitly withdrawn in 2018 with a transparent reason: "substantially out-of-date and does not meet current Cochrane methodological and reporting standards" — a rare example of Cochrane formally retracting a review for obsolescence rather than error.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cochrane systematic review (intervention review). The 2009 version (pub3) updated searches to January 2009.
- **Search**: Cochrane Oral Health's Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, EMBASE (to January 2009); no language restrictions.
- **Eligibility**: RCTs comparing one-stage (implant placed with transmucosal healing abutment, no second-stage surgery) vs two-stage (implant submerged, second-stage uncovering surgery) placement in partially or fully edentulous patients.
- **Included studies**: 1 RCT (Barber 1996, n=40) — originally excluded in 2007 version, included after re-assessment in 2009.
- **Outcomes**: Implant failure, prosthesis failure, marginal bone loss, complications, patient satisfaction (as per standard Cochrane Oral Health protocol).
- **Risk of bias**: Assessed per Cochrane Handbook (random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Studies (n) | Result | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implant failure (1 year) | 1 RCT (40) | No significant difference between 1-stage vs 2-stage | NS (single small trial, underpowered) |
| Marginal bone loss | Not reported / insufficient data | — | — |
| Peri-implant soft tissue outcomes | Not reported / insufficient data | — | — |
| Prosthesis failure | Not reported / insufficient data | — | — |
| Complications | Not reported / insufficient data | — | — |

- **Critical limitation**: Only **one small RCT** (Barber 1996, n=40) met inclusion criteria — the evidence base was (and remains in this withdrawn version) critically insufficient for any meta-analysis or firm conclusion.
- The review authors' 2009 conclusion: "There is no evidence of a difference in implant failure between one-stage and two-stage surgery... The evidence is insufficient to permit any conclusion."

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Critically underpowered evidence base**: Only 1 RCT with 40 participants — far too small to detect clinically meaningful differences in implant failure or MBL.
- **Withdrawn status**: The review was formally withdrawn in 2018 (Cochrane Database Issue 5, 2018) because it was "substantially out-of-date and does not meet current Cochrane methodological and reporting standards." Authors were unable to update it.
- **Superseded by later non-Cochrane SRs**: Subsequent systematic reviews with broader inclusion criteria (more studies, longer follow-up) have addressed this question — see Related Work.
- **No meta-analysis possible**: With only one included study, no quantitative synthesis was performed.
- **Outdated search**: Last search January 2009; 15+ years of new RCTs are missing.

## 6. Related Work

- [[implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]] — 2018 SR+MA (6–8 RCTs): submerged vs non-submerged healing; no implant/prosthesis failure difference, but submerged showed significantly greater MBL (MD 0.12 mm, P=0.04). Partially supersedes this withdrawn Cochrane review.
- [[implants/survival/troiano-2018-early-late-failure-submerged]] — 2018 SR+MA + TSA (11 studies): non-submerged healing had 2% higher early failure, no late failure difference; MBL 0.13 mm favored non-submerged. Supersedes this Cochrane review.
- [[implants/mbl/al-amri-2016-crestal-bone-loss-submerged]] — 2016 SR (13 studies, 6 human): no MBL difference between submerged vs non-submerged in human studies (5/6 no difference). Supersedes this Cochrane review.
- [[implants/wu-2018-submerged-nonsubmerged-internal-hexagonal]] — 2018 retrospective (114 implants, 5y): no survival/MBL difference; lower evidence level but consistent with later SRs.
- [[implants/mbl/verma-2024-comparison-bone-loss-submerged-nonsubmerged]] — 2024 RCT (30 implants): submerged healing had significantly less MBL (0.18 mm) vs non-submerged anatomical healing abutment (0.34 mm) at 3 months.

## 7. Glossary

- **One-stage (non-submerged/transmucosal) placement**: Implant placed with a healing abutment protruding through the mucosa; no second-stage uncovering surgery required.
- **Two-stage (submerged) placement**: Implant placed with a cover screw, fully submerged under the mucosa; requires a second surgery to uncover and place a healing abutment.
- **Withdrawn (Cochrane)**: A review formally removed from the Cochrane Library because it is obsolete/methodologically outdated; the last published version remains accessible but is flagged as not meeting current standards.
- **Barber 1996**: The single RCT included in this review (n=40, mandibular overdenture implants, 1-year follow-up).
