## Why Ingested

측방접근 상악동거상술에서 DBBM(Bio-Oss) 입자 크기(대입자 1–2mm vs 소입자 0.25–1mm)가 조직형태계측 결과에 영향을 주는지 직접 다룬 SR+MA가 위키에 없었다. 최근 인제스트된 [[wiki/bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/yu-2026-bone-particle-size-arp-periodontally-compromised-molars]]가 발치와보존술(ARP) 맥락에서 "입자 크기는 부차적 변수"라는 결론을 냈는데, 본 논문(Li 2023, 4개 RCT 메타분석)이 상악동거상술이라는 별개 시술 맥락에서 같은 결론(대·소입자 간 결합조직·신생골·잔류이식재 비율에 유의차 없음)을 독립적으로 재확인하는지 확인하기 위해 인제스트했다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA/PROSPERO CRD42022379384) of 4 RCTs (n=94 patients, 44 large-particle vs 50 small-particle implant sites) comparing large-granule (1–2 mm) vs small-granule (0.25–1 mm) deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM, Bio-Oss) for histological outcomes after maxillary sinus floor elevation.

No significant differences were found between large- and small-particle DBBM in percentage of connective tissue (WMD −5.76%, 95% CI −12.96 to 1.45, p=0.117), percentage of newly formed bone (WMD +5.15%, 95% CI −1.56 to 11.86, p=0.132), or percentage of residual xenograft (WMD +1.89%, 95% CI −2.10 to 5.88, p=0.353).

DBBM particle size (large vs small granule) does not significantly affect histological healing outcomes in sinus floor elevation, but the evidence base is limited to only 4 RCTs with substantial heterogeneity in two of the three outcomes.

## 세줄요약

체계적 고찰 + 메타분석 (PRISMA/PROSPERO CRD42022379384, 4개 RCT, n=94명, 대입자군 44부위 vs 소입자군 50부위): 상악동거상술(Maxillary Sinus Floor Elevation) 후 대입자(1–2mm) vs 소입자(0.25–1mm) 단백질제거우골이식재(Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral, DBBM, Bio-Oss)의 조직형태계측 비교.

결합조직 비율(WMD −5.76%, p=0.117), 신생골 비율(WMD +5.15%, p=0.132), 잔류 이종골이식재 비율(WMD +1.89%, p=0.353) 모두 대·소입자군 간 유의한 차이 없음.

DBBM 입자 크기(대·소입자)는 상악동거상술의 조직학적 치유 결과에 유의한 영향을 미치지 않으나, 근거는 4개 RCT뿐이고 3개 지표 중 2개에서 이질성(heterogeneity)이 상당히 커 결론 해석에 제한이 있음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First (and to date, only) SR+MA to isolate **DBBM granule/particle size** (large 1–2 mm vs small 0.25–1 mm Bio-Oss) as the sole variable and test its effect on histological outcomes after maxillary sinus floor elevation.
- Pooled 4 RCTs (94 patients total) reporting all three histomorphometric outcomes: % connective tissue (CT), % newly formed bone (NFB), % residual xenograft (RX).
- Found no statistically significant difference between large- and small-particle DBBM across all three outcomes, concluding the two preparations are histologically equivalent — though the individual studies disagreed in direction (Testori 2011 and Kamolratanakul 2022 favored large particle for new bone; Molon 2018 found no difference; Chackartchi 2011 found similar results for both).
- Discusses clinical trade-offs independent of histology: large particles may shorten surgical time and reduce graft volume needed, but increase void space (infection risk); small particles allow finer control of graft volume/space but increase material/patient burden.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Protocol**: Registered a priori in PROSPERO (CRD42022379384); reported per Cochrane Handbook + PRISMA statement.
- **Search**: PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, Scopus, Web of Science; Boolean strings combining ("maxillary sinus"/"sinus" AND "floor elevation"/"lift"/"floor augmentation"/"augmentation"/"floor") AND ("xenograft"/"bovine bone"/"Bio-Oss"/"inorganic bovine bone"/"deproteinized bovine bone matrix"). No language/year restriction. Last search February 2023.
- **Inclusion**: RCTs in humans; maxillary sinus lift in healthy patients with inadequate posterior bone height; large (1–2 mm) OR small (0.25–1 mm) DBBM used for augmentation.
- **Exclusion**: Animal studies; meta-analyses, case reports, proceedings, retrospective/cohort studies, personal communications, studies without control groups; studies lacking a relevant evaluation index; duplicates.
- **Screening**: Two independent reviewers (XL, SCL); disagreements resolved by discussion with other authors.
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane Collaboration tool (7 domains; low/high/unclear per domain, then an overall low/moderate/high rating per study). Assessed with RevMan 5.4.
- **Outcome measures**: % connective tissue (CT), % newly formed bone (NFB), % residual xenograft (RX) from histomorphometric biopsy analysis.
- **Statistics**: Stata 15.1 + RevMan 5.4.1; weighted mean difference (WMD) with 95% CI; heterogeneity via Chi²-based Q-test and I² statistic; random-effects model used when I² > 50% (with p < 0.1 for the Q-test), otherwise fixed-effects.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Search yield**: 149 records → 72 after de-duplication (EndNote 20) → 4 RCTs included after full-text screening (68 excluded).
- **Study characteristics**: All 4 studies were RCTs; n per study 20–32 (total 94 patients); implants — large-particle group total 44 (range 10–13/study), small-particle group total 50 (range 10–19/study).
- **Risk of bias**: All 4 declared randomized; allocation concealment reported except Testori (2013 — labelled "Tiziano" in text); blinding reported only in Chackartchi (2011) and Kamolratanakul (2022, labelled "Paksinee"); complete data/no selective reporting in all except Molon (2018); Chackartchi (2011) and Testori (2013) flagged unclear risk of other bias.
- **% Connective tissue**: Random-effects (I²=65.7%, p=0.033); WMD = −5.76 (95% CI −12.96 to 1.45), p=0.117 — not significant.
- **% Newly formed bone**: Random-effects (I²=75.3%, p=0.007); WMD = +5.15 (95% CI −1.56 to 11.86), p=0.132 — not significant.
- **% Residual xenograft**: Fixed-effects (I²=0.0%, p=0.415); WMD = +1.89 (95% CI −2.10 to 5.88), p=0.353 — not significant.
- Bottom line: large- and small-particle DBBM produced histologically equivalent outcomes across all three measures, providing a reference for treating particle size as a non-critical selection factor.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 4 RCTs available — authors explicitly state "it is difficult to make a conclusion from limited evidence from four studies. More clinical evidence was needed."
- Substantial heterogeneity for CT (I²=65.7%) and NFB (I²=75.3%) outcomes, driven by differences in biopsy site (lateral sinus wall in Testori/Molon vs. alveolar crest/implant site trephine core in Chackartchi/Kamolratanakul), timing (6–9 months across studies), and possible inclusion of native autogenous bone in trephine cores.
- Blinding was largely absent/unclear across included studies, and allocation concealment reporting was incomplete in one study.
- No implant survival, ISQ, or long-term clinical outcome data were part of this histology-focused review.
- Authors note the four included studies individually disagreed in direction (some favoring large particle for new bone formation), which the pooled non-significant result partly masks — individual study heterogeneity in surgical technique, sample selection, and specimen-collection method were cited as contributing factors.

## 6. Related Work

- Chackartchi et al. — large vs. small bovine bone in sinus floor lift, similar clinical/histological results at 6–9 months.
- Testori et al. — large-granule DBBM showed more new bone by histomorphometry at 6–8 months (lateral wall biopsy).
- Molon et al. — immunohistochemical staining (osteocalcin, VEGF, TRAP) showed no significant difference between particle sizes.
- Kamolratanakul et al. — large-particle DBBM induced more angiogenic expression and new bone, but similar clinical outcomes overall.

## 7. Glossary

- **DBBM**: Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral — xenograft bone substitute derived from calf bone, retains natural trabecular/porous structure after removal of organic components.
- **Bio-Oss®**: Commercial DBBM product (Geistlich, Switzerland); available as large granules (1–2 mm) and small granules (0.25–1 mm).
- **WMD**: Weighted Mean Difference — pooled effect size for continuous outcomes in meta-analysis.
- **CT / NFB / RX**: Percentage of Connective Tissue / percentage of Newly Formed Bone / percentage of Residual Xenograft — the three histomorphometric outcome measures used.
- **I² statistic**: Measure of heterogeneity across pooled studies (>50% generally considered substantial).
