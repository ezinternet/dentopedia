## Why Ingested

This SR+MA supplies the pooled, placebo-controlled evidence tier for the corticosteroids/third-molar cluster: [[wiki/drug/analgesics/tamgadge-2025-preoperative-dexamethasone-third-molar-pain-swelling-trismus]] showed a single split-mouth RCT where preoperative dexamethasone beat placebo on pain, trismus, and swelling — this paper pools 10 RCTs (of 17 reviewed, 730 patients) and independently confirms the same direction of effect for pain and trismus at the meta-analytic level.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis of placebo-controlled RCTs (PubMed/Scopus/Cochrane, PRISMA-structured) on corticosteroids for pain, oedema, and trismus after impacted third molar surgery — 1223 studies screened, 17 included qualitatively (730 patients, 15–45 y), 10 pooled quantitatively via Review Manager.

Corticosteroids significantly reduced pain (mean difference −17.38, 95% CI −24.81 to −9.95, P=0.002) and trismus/improved mouth opening (mean difference 6.10, 95% CI 3.42 to 8.77, P<0.00001) versus placebo; route of administration did not affect outcomes except the submucosal route, and preoperative timing was superior to postoperative specifically for trismus control.

This is placebo-controlled, pooled-level confirmation of corticosteroid efficacy for third-molar postoperative sequelae — but only the abstract was retrievable, so no pooled oedema/swelling effect size or heterogeneity (I²) statistic can be reported from this ingest.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Pools 10 RCTs (of 17 reviewed qualitatively, 730 patients total) via Review Manager meta-analysis to produce placebo-controlled pooled effect sizes for pain and trismus after third molar surgery — the highest evidence tier available in this cluster.
- Analyzes effect modifiers beyond simple drug-vs-placebo: route of administration (only the submucosal route differed from the rest) and timing (preoperative vs postoperative), isolating that preoperative timing specifically drives the trismus benefit.
- PRISMA-structured search across PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane Library (1223 records screened → 17 qualitative → 10 meta-analyzed).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis (pooling performed in Review Manager / RevMan)
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library; PRISMA-structured reporting
- **n**: 1223 studies identified → 17 studies in qualitative synthesis (730 patients, aged 15–45) → 10 studies in quantitative meta-analysis
- **Outcomes**: postoperative pain, oedema/swelling, trismus (mouth opening) after third molar surgery; effect-modifier analyses for corticosteroid drug type, administration route, and administration timing (pre- vs postoperative)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Pooled effect (corticosteroid vs placebo) | 95% CI | P value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain | Mean difference −17.38 | −24.81 to −9.95 | 0.002 |
| Trismus (mouth opening) | Mean difference 6.10 | 3.42 to 8.77 | <0.00001 |
| Oedema/swelling | Not stated in abstract (full text not retrieved) | — | — |

- Route of administration: no significant effect on outcomes with the exception of the submucosal route (abstract does not specify direction/magnitude of the submucosal difference).
- Timing: preoperative corticosteroid administration was superior to postoperative administration specifically for trismus control.
- Number of studies actually pooled for each individual outcome (pain vs trismus vs oedema), forest-plot data, heterogeneity (I²), and risk-of-bias assessment are not present in the abstract text and could not be transcribed.

## 7. Glossary
- **Trismus**: restricted mouth opening from masticatory-muscle spasm/inflammation, typically measured as maximal interincisal distance (mm)
- **Mean difference (MD)**: pooled meta-analytic effect-size statistic — the average difference in a continuous outcome between intervention and control arms across combined studies
- **PRISMA**: Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses, the reporting standard this review followed
- **Review Manager (RevMan)**: Cochrane Collaboration's software for performing and displaying meta-analyses
- **Submucosal route**: drug injected beneath the oral mucosa, distinct from intramuscular/intravenous/oral routes
