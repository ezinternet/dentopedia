## Why Ingested

The wiki holds no page on whether clinicians looking at the *same tooth* actually decide the same thing — every existing practice-management page (e.g. [[practice-management/nosrat-2025-endodontist-gp-nsrct-clinical-approaches-cohort]]) measures how endodontists vs. GDPs *execute* a procedure once the decision to treat is already made. This paper measures the decision itself: 431 clinicians shown the same standardised periapical radiograph chose among retreatment, apical surgery/intentional replantation, or extraction, and GDPs and dental students extracted at 3–4× the odds of endodontists (OR 4.37 and 4.41 respectively, both p<0.001). It is the clinician-side mirror that any patient-facing shared-decision-making page implicitly assumes is stable — this paper shows it is not.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional vignette study (Chile, 2023–2024, n=431: 47 dental students, 349 general dental practitioners (GDP), 35 endodontists) presenting seven standardised periapical radiographs of teeth with post-treatment apical periodontitis and asking clinicians to choose retreatment, apical surgery/intentional replantation, or extraction.

GDPs and students were significantly more likely than endodontists to choose extraction (OR 4.37 and 4.41, p<0.001 for both); GDPs aged 36–45 (OR 1.99, p=0.003) and those in the public sector (22.96% vs. 15.24% private) extracted more; every tooth-level variable — indirect restoration (GDP OR 2.12, p<0.001), inadequate root filling (GDP OR 1.81, p<0.001), medium (GDP OR 7.26, p<0.001) and large (GDP OR 1.56, p=0.001) lesions — pushed GDPs toward extraction, while no endodontist chose extraction regardless of lesion size.

The design measures stated intent from a radiograph-only vignette, not delivered treatment or outcome, and cannot separate skill/confidence/self-selection from the authors' framing that the endodontist's answer is the "correct" one; the public-sector association nonetheless hints that resource/access constraints, not just knowledge gaps, shape the generalist extraction bias.

## 세줄요약

칠레 횡단면 비네트(vignette) 연구(2023–2024, n=431: 치과대학생 47명·일반치과의사(General Dental Practitioner, GDP) 349명·근관전문의(Endodontist, E) 35명) — 근관재치료 후 재발한 치근단주위염(Post-Treatment Apical Periodontitis, AP) 치아의 표준화된 치근단 방사선사진 7장을 보여주고 근관재치료(Retreatment)·치근단수술/의도적재식(Apical Surgery/Intentional Replantation)·발치(Extraction) 중 하나를 선택하게 함.

GDP와 학생이 근관전문의보다 발치를 유의하게 더 많이 선택(승산비(Odds Ratio, OR) 각각 4.37·4.41, 둘 다 p<0.001); 36–45세 GDP(OR 1.99, p=0.003)와 공공부문 근무자(22.96% vs 사설 15.24%)가 더 많이 발치를 선택; 간접수복물(GDP OR 2.12, p<0.001)·부적절한 근관충전(GDP OR 1.81, p<0.001)·중등도(GDP OR 7.26, p<0.001) 및 큰(GDP OR 1.56, p=0.001) 치근단병소 등 모든 치아 관련 변수가 GDP를 발치 쪽으로 밀었고, 근관전문의는 병소 크기와 무관하게 단 한 건도 발치를 선택하지 않음.

이 연구는 방사선사진만 보고 밝힌 '의도'를 측정한 것이지 실제 시행된 치료나 예후가 아니며, 술기·자신감·자기선택 효과와 "근관전문의의 답이 정답"이라는 저자들의 전제를 설계상 분리할 수 없음; 다만 공공부문 연관성은 발치 편향이 지식 부족만이 아니라 자원·접근성 제약의 결과일 수 있음을 시사.

## 1. Document Information

- **Authors**: Dufey-Portilla N, Vera I, Peña-Bengoa F, Bórquez G, Cáceres C, Nagendrababu V, Dummer PMH, Abella F, Garcia-Font M
- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical and Experimental Dentistry, 2026;18(4):e483-e493 (e-pub 2026-03-30)
- **DOI**: 10.4317/jced.63471
- **PMID**: 42136705 / **PMCID**: PMC13170950
- **Study type**: Cross-sectional, analytical, observational vignette/survey study
- **Setting**: Nationwide (all 16 regions), Chile; ethics approval Universidad Andrés Bello Resolution 163/23

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **First national quantification of clinician-group extraction bias for post-treatment AP in Chile.** 431 respondents (99.7% response rate) across dental students, GDPs, and endodontists rated the same 7 standardised radiographic scenarios.
2. **Direction and size of the bias, not just significance.** GDP and student extraction odds vs. endodontists: OR 4.37 (p<0.001) and OR 4.41 (p<0.001); endodontist overall extraction rate as low as 5.3%, no endodontist extracted regardless of lesion size.
3. **Isolates which tooth-level cues drive generalist extraction**: indirect restoration, inadequate root canal filling, and lesion size all independently and significantly raised GDP extraction odds (all p≤0.001), while endodontists were comparatively unmoved by the same cues.
4. **Surfaces a health-systems signal, not just a knowledge-gap story**: public-sector GDPs extracted more (22.96%) than private (15.24%) or mixed-practice (15.95%) GDPs — consistent with long public-system wait times shaping the decision independent of clinical knowledge.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional, PROBE-guideline-adherent, anonymous nationwide survey (Google Forms + in-person), July–September 2023.
- **Sample**: Minimum required n=379 (95% CI, 5% precision, population 31,943: 1,600 final-year students, 28,495 GDPs, 1,848 endodontists); achieved n=431 (47 students, 349 GDPs, 35 endodontists) after excluding 13 incomplete/non-compliant responses.
- **Instrument**: 11 items — clinician characteristics (group, age category, years of experience, workplace) + 7 case-based single-forced-choice items. Each case: identical clinical vignette (50-year-old, systemically healthy, pain on biting, previously root-filled tooth, positive percussion, normal soft tissue) paired with a standardised periapical radiograph manipulated to vary root canal filling quality (per European Society of Endodontology criteria), restoration type (direct/indirect), and apical lesion size (small <3mm, medium 4–5mm, large >5mm — categories not disclosed to participants). Time since original treatment deliberately omitted to avoid anchoring heterogeneity (acknowledged as a limitation).
- **Options offered**: (A) root canal retreatment, (B) apical surgery/intentional replantation, (C) extraction. Analysis dichotomized A/B as "save and treat" vs. C as "extract."
- **Statistics**: Generalized linear mixed-effects logistic regression (random intercept per participant, 7 repeated measures) for the primary extraction outcome; fixed effects = professional group + age, experience, workplace, lesion size, restoration type, root-filling status. Simple logistic regression used for group-specific associations. R v4.3.1; two-sided p<0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Group vs. extraction (reference: endodontists)**: GDP OR 4.37 (p<0.001); students OR 4.41 (p<0.001). Endodontist extraction rate ~5.3% overall; comparable published benchmark (Rodríguez et al.) reported E 5.63% vs. GDP 14.69% (threefold gap) — this study's GDP figure was 17.03%.
- **Age**: GDPs aged 36–45 y more likely to extract (OR 1.99, p=0.003); no significant effect of years of experience on group differences, though GDPs with ≥20 y experience descriptively favored preservation (not statistically significant).
- **Workplace (GDP only)**: public sector 22.96% extraction vs. private 15.24% vs. mixed 15.95%. Neither students nor endodontists were represented in the public sector, limiting cross-group comparison on this variable.
- **Restoration type**: indirect restoration → GDP extraction 24.35% (OR 2.12, p<0.001); students 24.11% (descriptive). Direct restorations associated with lower extraction across groups.
- **Root canal filling quality**: inadequate filling → GDP OR 1.81 (p<0.001); student extraction rate reached 23.4% under this condition; endodontists comparatively unaffected (7.43%).
- **Apical lesion size**: GDP medium lesion OR 7.26 (p<0.001), large lesion OR 1.56 (p=0.001); students also showed a preference for extraction with large lesions (descriptive). No endodontist extracted regardless of lesion size.
- **Response rate**: 99.7% (444/445 approached), participants from all 16 Chilean regions.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Vignette, not real practice**: measures stated single-choice intent from a standardised radiograph in a hypothetical 50-year-old with no comorbidities — no clinical exam, no patient preference, no cost/access/referral constraints, no actual treatment or outcome data. The authors' own framing (that reducing extraction is unambiguously desirable) assumes the endodontist's answer is correct; the design cannot establish this, only that it differs.
- **Selection into group is not randomized** — endodontists chose endodontics; the gap could reflect skill, confidence, equipment access, referral-base effects, or self-selection into the specialty, not (or not only) superior judgment.
- **Time-since-treatment deliberately withheld** from all scenarios to avoid anchoring bias — acknowledged by the authors as reducing realism, since experienced clinicians typically weigh treatment age.
- **No CBCT used** (reflects limited public-system availability in Chile) — may understate real diagnostic capability where CBCT is accessible.
- **Patient-level factors excluded** entirely (systemic health, socioeconomics, lifestyle) — a real-world extraction decision weighs these alongside the radiograph.
- **Single country, no cross-national comparison** possible due to lack of comparable regional data; findings may not generalize outside Chile's specific public/private dual system.
- **Public-sector representation gap**: only GDPs were sampled from the public sector, so the workplace effect cannot be cleanly compared across all three groups.

## 6. Related Work

- **Rodríguez et al.** — CBCT's effect on GDP/endodontist decision-making after failed RCT; comparable direction (E 5.63% vs. GDP 14.69% extraction, threefold gap) but did not find a significant experience effect either, mirroring this study's null on years-of-experience.
- **Lee et al.** — underfilled root canals associated with higher GDP extraction rates, consistent with this study's OR 1.81 finding.
- **Lixandru et al.** — public dental system wait times as a driver of patient/clinician urgency, cited to explain the higher public-sector GDP extraction rate here.
- **Cochrane review (surgical vs. non-surgical retreatment outcomes)** — cited by the authors as not clearly favoring one modality, underscoring that the "correct" answer this study implicitly assumes (endodontist-style preservation) is not itself settled by outcome evidence.

## 7. Glossary

- **AP** — Apical periodontitis; here specifically post-treatment (persistent/recurrent) AP in a previously root-filled tooth.
- **DS / GDP / E** — Dental student / general dental practitioner / endodontist, the three compared professional groups.
- **"Save and treat"** — Composite outcome combining root canal retreatment and apical surgery/intentional replantation, contrasted against extraction.
- **PROBE** — Preferred Reporting Items for Observational Studies in Endodontics, the reporting guideline followed.
- **OR** — Odds ratio from the mixed-effects logistic regression (random intercept per participant across 7 repeated case ratings).
