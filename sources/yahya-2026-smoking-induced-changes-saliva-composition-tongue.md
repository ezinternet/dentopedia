## Three-line Summary
Cross-sectional study of 78 adults (38 smokers, 40 nonsmokers) at Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia comparing salivary flow, pH, buffering capacity, and tongue coating (Miyazaki Index) by smoking status.
Smokers showed significantly lower salivary buffering capacity (p = 0.002) and greater tongue-coating thickness (p = 0.008) and distribution area (p = 0.000); salivary flow and pH did not differ significantly.
Authors propose integrating tongue-coating and saliva analysis into caries risk assessment, framing smoking as a modifiable behavioural factor influencing salivary defence and oral ecological balance.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Simultaneously assessed salivary flow, pH, buffering capacity, and tongue coating in smokers vs nonsmokers — factors rarely evaluated together.
- Identified reduced buffering capacity and thicker/more extensive tongue coating as the smoking-associated signals, with flow and pH unchanged.
- Positioned tongue coating and saliva parameters as adjunctive biomarkers for caries risk assessment and smoking-cessation-oriented preventive protocols.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: cross-sectional, purposive (non-probability) sampling; adult smokers and nonsmokers at UKM Dental Polyclinics
- **Sample size calculation**: based on Rad et al. (2010) xerostomia 39% (smokers) vs 12% (nonsmokers); α = 0.05, β = 0.2, 80% power → 80 required; +10% dropout → 88 planned (44/group); **final analysed n = 78 (38 smokers, 40 nonsmokers)** due to incomplete data/attrition
- **Inclusion**: no systemic illness and no medication affecting saliva flow; smokers = cigarette use > 6 months
- **Saliva**: resting and stimulated (paraffin-wax) flow volumetric; stimulated pH by indicator strips (red 5.0–5.8, yellow 6.0–6.6, green 6.8–7.8); buffering capacity by colourimetric strips scored very low 0–5, low 6–9, normal/high 10–12
- **Tongue coating**: Miyazaki Index — thickness (0–2), colour (0–3), distribution area (0–3) across nine tongue-dorsum sections; three examiners, inter-rater ICC = 0.889
- **Smoking status confirmation**: Fagerström Test for Nicotine Dependence (FTND) and carbon monoxide analysis
- **Statistics**: chi-square and Mann–Whitney U tests, p < 0.05 significant; bivariate only (no multivariate adjustment)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Salivary parameters (Mann–Whitney U)**: buffering capacity significantly lower in smokers (n = 37 smokers, 40 nonsmokers; mean ranks 31.43 vs 46.00; p = 0.002). Resting saliva p = 0.308; stimulated saliva p = 0.654; saliva pH p = 0.244 — all non-significant.
- **Tongue coating (chi-square)**: severe coating 54.3% of smokers vs 20.0% of nonsmokers (p = 0.008); > two-thirds distribution area 37.3% vs 18.7% (p = 0.000 as reported); colour not significant (p = 0.075). Note: published Table 5 cell counts and percentages are internally inconsistent (e.g. >2/3 area 28 vs 14 with total printed as 33); p-values and printed percentages are transcribed verbatim.
- **Baseline (Table 3)**: mean age 31.89 ± 12.33 (smokers) vs 28.57 ± 9.89 (nonsmokers), p = 0.193; all smokers male vs 40.0% male nonsmokers (p = 0.000); ethnicity p = 0.043; education p = 0.612.
- **Interpretation**: reduced buffering may reflect altered bicarbonate/phosphate secretion or oxidative-stress protein modification; tongue coating acts as a reservoir for cariogenic species (Streptococcus mutans, Lactobacillus).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Purposive sampling from a university clinic — selection bias, limited generalizability.
- All smokers were male while nonsmokers included females — sex imbalance is a potential confounder for salivary measures.
- No multivariate adjustment for age, gender, oral hygiene, diet, alcohol, socioeconomic status, or smoking intensity (pack-years).
- pH and buffering measured by colourimetric strips — less precise than laboratory biochemical assays; variability may reduce sensitivity.
- Cross-sectional design precludes temporal/causal inference; final n = 78 vs planned 88 modestly reduced power. Authors call for longitudinal and multivariable studies.

## 7. Glossary
- **Salivary Buffering Capacity (타액 완충능)**: saliva's ability to neutralise bacterial acids and stabilise plaque pH; scored here 0–12 by colourimetric strips.
- **Tongue Coating (설태)**: layer of desquamated cells, debris, and microorganisms on the tongue dorsum; scored by Miyazaki Index (thickness/colour/area).
- **Miyazaki Index**: nine-section tongue-dorsum scoring system for coating thickness (0–2), colour (0–3), and distribution area (0–3).
- **FTND (Fagerström Test for Nicotine Dependence)**: standard instrument confirming nicotine dependence/smoking status.
- **Caries Risk Assessment (충치 위험평가)**: process of identifying individuals at elevated caries risk; here proposed to include saliva and tongue-coating measures.
