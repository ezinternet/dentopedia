## Three-line Summary

Retrospective chart review (J Am Acad Dermatol, 2019; 15 critically ill inpatients seen by dermatology 2009–2017) describing acute inflammatory edema, a likely underrecognized variant of pseudocellulitis.

It presents as bilateral erythematous edematous plaques, most commonly on the thighs and lower abdomen and sparing pressure areas, with a predilection for high body mass index and fluid overload; the authors propose a three-part pathogenesis: acute volume overload, impaired lymphatic return, leading to dermal edema, microtears in connective tissue and an influx of inflammation.

Case series of 15 with no comparison group; it is a lookalike of cellulitis in critical illness, not evidence about infectious cellulitis itself, and the abstract only is available.

## 1. Document Information

- Journal: Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology 81(4):931–936 (published 2019-06-01)
- Design: retrospective chart review, UCSF inpatient dermatology consultation service, 2009–2017
- Full text not retrieved (abstract only)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Describes a pseudocellulitis entity found in critically ill patients.
- Proposes a three-step mechanism for it.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Charts of 15 patients (9 women, 6 men, age 52–73) reviewed.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Bilateral erythematous edematous plaques, thighs and lower abdomen, sparing areas of increased pressure; high BMI and fluid overload.
- Proposed pathogenesis: acute volume overload, in patients with impaired lymphatic return, leads to dermal edema, microtears in connective tissue and an influx of inflammation.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- n=15, single service, no comparison group; abstract only.
- Concerns critically ill inpatients; not relevant to odontogenic facial infection.
