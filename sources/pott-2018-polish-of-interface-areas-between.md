## Why Ingested

Intraoral polishing of zirconia after occlusal adjustments and chipping repairs is a common clinical scenario. This study directly addresses how to polish interface areas between zirconia, veneering ceramic, and composite — providing both material-specific and sequence recommendations for chairside polishing systems.

→ [[dental-materials/zirconia/pott-2018-polish-of-interface-areas-between]]

## Three-line Summary

Fifty plates each of zirconia, silicate-ceramic, and nano-hybrid composite were ground to simulate intraoral adjustment surfaces, then polished with three diamond-containing intraoral systems (Dimanto/VOCO single-step, Komet two-step, Busch two-step) or lab controls (glaze or machine polish); surface roughness Ra was measured by profilometry.

All three systems reduced zirconia Ra to values statistically equivalent to glaze firing (Zlab), with post-polishing Ra < 0.20 µm; silicate-ceramic was polished adequately only by the single-step VOCO system (Sv); composite was rougher after all intraoral systems than after lab polish (Clab), though VOCO and Komet systems were equivalent to each other.

For interface areas spanning multiple materials, the recommended sequence is: zirconia-specific polisher first, then veneering-ceramic polisher, then composite polisher — aligning the abrasive grit progression with material hardness.

## 세줄요약

지르코니아(50개)·규산염 세라믹(50개)·나노하이브리드 복합레진(50개) 플레이트를 자동 연삭 후, 3종 다이아몬드 함유 구강 내 폴리싱 시스템(Dimanto 1단계, Komet 2단계, Busch 2단계) 또는 실험실 대조군(글레이즈 소성/기계 연마)으로 처리하고 프로파일로미터로 표면조도(Ra)를 측정했다.

지르코니아는 3종 시스템 모두 Ra < 0.20 µm로 감소시켜 글레이즈 소성군(Zlab)과 통계적 차이 없었으나, 규산염 세라믹은 VOCO 단일단계 시스템(Sv)만 유의하게 감소시켰고 복합레진은 모든 구강 내 폴리싱 시스템에서 실험실 연마(Clab)보다 거칠었다.

인터페이스 영역(지르코니아-세라믹-복합레진) 폴리싱 순서: 지르코니아용 → 비니어링 재료용 → 복합레진용 순으로 경도 순서에 맞춰 적용한다.

## Key Contributions

1. All three tested diamond-containing intraoral polishing systems produce clinically acceptable zirconia Ra (< 0.20 µm), equivalent to glaze firing — no statistically significant difference (P > .899 across all vs Zlab).
2. Among tested systems, only the single-step VOCO Dimanto system significantly reduced silicate-ceramic Ra vs both Sgrit and Slab controls; two-step systems (Komet, Busch) actually increased roughness of silicate-ceramic compared to control.
3. None of the intraoral systems achieved lab-level composite polish (Clab), but Dimanto and Komet produced statistically equivalent results on composite (P = .991).
4. Cross-material comparisons establish that after polishing with the VOCO system, zirconia and silicate-ceramic reach comparable Ra (Zv vs Sv: P > .999), enabling a matched-roughness finish at the interface.
5. Recommended polishing sequence for mixed-material interfaces: zirconia polisher → veneering ceramic polisher → composite polisher.

## Methodology

- **Specimens**: 50 plates each of:
  - Zirconia: CAD/CAM InCoris Maxi-S (Sirona), 8.5×6.0×2.0 mm
  - Silicate-ceramic: Ivoclar Vivadent, 12.5×10.5×2.0 mm
  - Nano-hybrid composite: GrandioSO (VOCO), cast in zirconia-plate silicone moulds; light-cured at 1,200 mW/cm²
- **Ground** uniformly to 165 µm SiC grit (PowerPro 5000, Buehler), 50 N force under water cooling
- **Groups (15 total)**:
  - Controls: Zgrit/Sgrit/Cgrit (ground only); Zlab/Slab (glaze fired); Clab (machine polished to 9 µm SiC)
  - Experimental (n=9): v = Dimanto (VOCO, single-step), k = Komet Zirkonpoliersystem (two-step), b = Busch Zirkon-/Keramikpoliersystem (two-step); each applied at 6,000 rpm, 2 N × 30 s pre-polish then 1 N × 30 s high-gloss finish, under water cooling
- **Roughness**: Dektak 150 profilometer; 5 parallel scans × 3,600 µm, 0.33 µm resolution; Ra arithmetic mean
- **Statistics**: SPSS v24; Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality test; Levene homogeneity; ANOVA; Scheffé post-hoc; α = .05

## Key Results

| Group | Material | Ra (µm) direction vs control |
|---|---|---|
| Zv, Zk, Zb | Zirconia | Significantly reduced vs Zgrit (P < .001); NOT significant vs Zlab (P > .899) |
| Sv | Silicate-ceramic | Significantly reduced vs both Sgrit and Slab (P = .022); but NOT vs Sgrit (P = .487) |
| Sk | Silicate-ceramic | Significantly INCREASED vs all groups (P < .001) |
| Sb | Silicate-ceramic | Comparable to Slab (P = .576), less rough than Sk |
| Cv, Ck, Cb | Composite | NOT significantly different from Cgrit; significantly rougher than Clab (P < .014) |

- Pre-polish grit control Zgrit Ra = 0.31 µm → Zv/Zk/Zb Ra < 0.20 µm (consistent with enamel Ra 0.27 ± 0.19 µm)
- Plaque-adhesion threshold ≤ 0.20 µm (Bollen 1997): zirconia polishing achieves this; composite polishing does not

## Limitations

1. Flat plate geometry: clinical crowns/bridges have curved surfaces; profilometry required flat specimens
2. Manual polishing by one experienced dentist — operator variability in clinical practice not captured
3. Only Ra (arithmetic mean) measured — Rz (mean peak-to-valley) and other parameters not evaluated
4. Only three diamond-based systems tested; other commercially available systems not included
5. No ageing, thermal cycling, or wear simulation post-polishing; long-term durability unknown
6. Interface polishing was modeled by within-group Ra comparisons between materials polished with the same system; true combined-interface specimens were not fabricated

## Related Work

- Kou et al. — zirconia polishing systems reduce roughness (cited as confirming current results)
- Hmaidouch et al. — no significant difference between glazed and fine-polished zirconia (consistent with Zv/Zk/Zb ≈ Zlab finding)
- Park et al. — "zirconia polishing systems showed excellent ceramic surface polishing" (consistent)
- Bollen et al. 1997 — plaque-adhesion reduction below Ra 0.20 µm (threshold applied in discussion)
- Literature ranges: silicate-ceramic Ra 0.24–0.79 µm; zirconia 0.12–0.20 µm; composite 0.14–0.86 µm (this study falls within those ranges)

## Glossary

- **Ra**: Arithmetic mean of absolute surface roughness deviations — the primary profilometry outcome
- **FPD (고정성 보철물, Fixed Partial Denture)**: Multi-unit crown-and-bridge restoration
- **CAD/CAM**: Computer-aided design/manufacturing; used to mill zirconia blocks
- **LTD (저온열화, Low-Temperature Degradation)**: Aging phenomenon of Y-TZP zirconia — not directly studied here but motivates water-cooling during polishing
- **Dimanto**: VOCO's single-step diamond-containing rubber-point polishing system marketed for zirconia
- **Scheffé procedure**: Conservative post-hoc test for all pairwise comparisons following ANOVA
