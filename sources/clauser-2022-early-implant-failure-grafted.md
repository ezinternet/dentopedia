## One-line Summary
SR+MA (10 cohort studies) found bone grafting significantly associated with early implant failure (OR 1.50, 95% CI 1.06–2.13) vs non-grafted sites; other risk factor data were insufficient for pooling.

## 한줄요약

## 2. Key Contributions
- First dedicated SR+MA specifically addressing the EARLY failure (pre-loading) risk in grafted vs non-grafted sites
- Pooled OR 1.50 fills a methodology gap: prior evidence was single-cohort or mixed with late failure data
- Applied Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for cohort study quality — systematic bias assessment
- Defined early failure strictly as lack of osseointegration detected prior to functional loading (clear, reproducible outcome)
- Signals that bone graft procedures may negatively impact initial osseointegration, not just long-term outcomes

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Study design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis (SR+MA)
- **Database**: MEDLINE via PubMed only
- **Screening**: 231 articles → full-text review → 10 included in quantitative meta-analysis
- **Outcome (primary)**: Early implant failure = lack of osseointegration prior to functional loading
- **Statistical method**: Odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence interval; random-effects model (implied)
- **Quality assessment**: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for cohort studies
- **Reviewers**: Two independent reviewers
- **Limitation**: Other risk factors (e.g., bone quality, smoking) in grafted context insufficient for pooling

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Primary outcome**: Bone grafting → early implant failure
  - OR **1.50** (95% CI 1.06–2.13) — statistically significant
- 10 studies included for quantitative meta-analysis; 231 full-texts reviewed
- Data for other EIF risk factors in grafted sites: insufficient for quantitative pooling
- Conclusion: "significant positive association between bone grafting procedures and early implant failure"

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Single database (MEDLINE/PubMed) — other databases (Cochrane, Scopus, Embase) excluded, potential selection bias
- Only 10 studies included — relatively small evidence base
- Heterogeneity level not reported in abstract — likely an issue given different graft types, sites, timing
- Graft type (autograft vs xenograft vs alloplast), graft timing (simultaneous vs staged), and site (maxilla vs mandible) not separable in pooling
- Future: stratified analyses by graft material, site, and simultaneous vs delayed protocol needed

## 6. Related Work
- Gurbanov 2024 — long-term (≥10 yr) survival in grafted vs non-grafted (different timepoint; ISR similar at 10y)
- Yari 2023 — retrospective cohort: augmented site OR 2.24 for EIF (consistent direction, higher point estimate)
- Bonsmann 2025 — nested case-control: sinus augmentation independently raises EIF
- Fouda 2020 — SR+MA of EIF by alveolar site (anterior maxilla highest at 6.2%)

## 7. Glossary
- **EIF (Early Implant Failure)**: loss of osseointegration before functional prosthetic loading
- **Grafted site**: alveolar site that received bone graft (GBR, sinus lift, ridge augmentation) prior to or simultaneous with implant placement
- **Newcastle-Ottawa Scale**: quality assessment tool for non-randomized cohort studies (0–9 stars)
- **OR 1.50**: grafted sites have 50% higher odds of EIF vs non-grafted; note this is OR not RR — actual risk increase lower in low-prevalence settings
