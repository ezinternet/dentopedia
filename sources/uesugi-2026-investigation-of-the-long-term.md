## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort study (Int J Implant Dent 2026, n=203 Japanese patients, 323 zygomatic + 600 conventional implants, mean follow-up 8.3 years, range 3–13) evaluating long-term outcomes of combined zygomatic-conventional full-arch rehabilitation on the All-on-4 concept. Zygomatic implant (ZI) survival (95.9%) was significantly lower than conventional implant (CI) survival (98.7%) at the implant level (p=0.0178); sinusitis was the dominant ZI failure mode (6/9 failure cases, 66.7%). Palatal platform position (HR=18.2, p=0.026) and presence of systemic disease (HR=14.9, p=0.012) were the strongest independent risk factors in multivariable Cox regression.

## Abstract

This retrospective cohort study investigated the long-term prognosis (3–13 years) of full-mouth implant prosthetics combining zygomatic implants (ZIs) and conventional implants (CIs) based on the all-on-four concept in 203 Japanese patients. A total of 323 ZIs and 600 CIs were placed. Immediate loading with provisional restoration was performed. Final prostheses were titanium frameworks with ceramic or acrylic crowns at 6 months. Follow-up was every 3–6 months. Cumulative survival rates were estimated via Kaplan-Meier. Risk factors analyzed at implant and patient levels using log-rank and Cox regression.

## Discussion Highlights

- ZI survival significantly lower than CI at **implant level** (95.9% vs 98.7%), not patient level
- **Palatal platform position = major risk** (HR 18.2, p=0.026); biomechanics: palatal emergence → off-axis loading, hygiene difficulty, sinus membrane proximity
- **Systemic disease = patient-level risk** (HR 14.9, p=0.012); circulatory diseases drive this (90% survival)
- Smoking: univariate significance lost in multivariable — confounding with systemic disease
- Sinusitis is the dominant ZI failure mode (6/9 cases), especially with palatal platform
- Long follow-up (up to 13 yr) reveals late failures — need extended monitoring
- Japanese cohort (Malo protocol + AGA) — generalizability to other populations/ethnicities unclear
- Limited failure events (11 ZI failures) → multivariable results exploratory

## Clinical Implications

| Finding | Clinical Action |
|---|---|
| Palatal ZI platform → 18× failure risk | Avoid palatal platform when possible; prefer vestibular/ridge |
| Systemic disease (esp. circulatory) → 15× patient-level risk | Rigorous medical optimization pre-op; intensified maintenance |
| Sinusitis = main failure mode | Pre-op sinus assessment; postoperative sinus surveillance; consider vestibular platform to reduce sinus penetration |
| Late failures up to 13 yr | Lifelong 3-6 month maintenance mandatory |
| CI survival excellent (98.7%) | Maximize conventional implants when bone permits; reserve ZIs for truly atrophic maxillae |
