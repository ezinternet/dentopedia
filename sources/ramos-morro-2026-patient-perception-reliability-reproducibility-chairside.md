## Why Ingested
Systematic review on the same three axes (patient perception, reliability/reproducibility, chairside time) as [[digital-workflow/christopoulou-2022-intraoral-scanners-orthodontics-critical-review]], but in a general adult population against alginate/polyether/PVS/silicone comparators (not orthodontic-scope), with a later search date (November 19, 2024) and formal PRISMA 2020 + QUADAS-2 methodology — checked for whether it resolves Christopoulou's "contradictory" accuracy/duration verdict.

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review (PRISMA 2020, OSF-registered protocol; Medline/PubMed, EBSCO, Web of Science, Scopus searched November 19, 2024; 10 cross-sectional studies, 2016–2024, n=5–50 participants each) comparing intraoral scanners (IOS) against conventional impressions on accuracy, chairside time, and patient comfort in adolescent/adult (≥18y) populations with full or near-full dentition.

No meta-analysis was possible (outcome heterogeneity); accuracy findings across the 10 studies remained mixed (several favored conventional impressions for full-arch/posterior/long-span measurements, one favored IOS, several found both comparable), while the 2 studies that measured chairside time and the 2 that measured patient comfort (VAS) both found significantly shorter time and higher comfort for IOS.

Clinical implication: findings apply mainly to young, fully dentate, healthy adults — QUADAS-2 showed unclear/high risk of bias in patient selection across most included studies, so the review stops short of a firm accuracy verdict and calls for standardized protocols and more diverse populations.

## 세줄요약
체계적 문헌고찰(PRISMA 2020, OSF 사전등록; Medline/PubMed·EBSCO·Web of Science·Scopus, 2024년 11월 19일 검색; 횡단관찰 연구 10편, 2016–2024년, 연구당 n=5–50명): 청소년/성인(18세 이상) 대상 구강스캐너(Intraoral Scanner, IOS) vs 재래식 인상의 정확도·의자옆시간(chairside time)·환자 편의(comfort) 비교.

이질성으로 메타분석 불가; 10편의 정확도 결과는 혼재(다수는 전악·후방부·장거리 계측에서 재래식 인상 우위, 1편은 IOS 우위, 다수는 양자 동등), 의자옆시간을 측정한 2편과 환자 편의(VAS)를 측정한 2편은 모두 IOS에서 유의하게 짧은 시간·높은 편의도를 보고.

임상적 의미: 결과는 주로 젊고 완전 치열을 가진 건강한 성인에 국한 — QUADAS-2 상 대부분 연구의 환자선정 영역에서 위험도가 불명확/높음으로 나타나, 확정적 정확도 결론 대신 표준화된 프로토콜과 다양한 인구집단 대상 추가연구를 촉구함.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Applies PRISMA 2020 + OSF pre-registration and QUADAS-2 risk-of-bias appraisal (with inter-rater Cohen's kappa: patient selection 0.88, index test 1.00, reference standard 0.89, flow/timing 1.00, general risk 0.63) to the accuracy/time/comfort literature — a higher methodological bar than Christopoulou 2022's non-systematic critical review.
- Explicitly documents why meta-analysis was not attempted: accuracy was measured via linear/angular distances, shell-to-shell deviation, colorimetric mapping, and qualitative assessment — methods judged not directly poolable.
- Isolates that only 2 of the 10 included studies (Sfondrini et al., Janosi et al.) actually measured chairside time, and the same 2 measured patient comfort via VAS — both outcomes are thin evidence despite the review's title emphasis.
- Surfaces a "chairside time" definitional problem: Sfondrini et al.'s isolated chairside-only comparison (5 min 49 s digital vs 7 min 32 s conventional) is a much smaller gap than the total-time comparison including processing (5 min 49 s digital vs 22 min 6 s conventional), showing most of the IOS time advantage comes from eliminating lab/gypsum processing, not from a much faster chairside procedure itself.
- Flags a comfort-assessment-timing confound: Sfondrini et al. administered VAS immediately after each procedure while Janosi et al. administered it only after both procedures were complete, which the authors argue may inflate IOS's apparent comfort advantage via recall bias and recency/social-desirability effects.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review, PRISMA 2020, OSF-registered protocol; no meta-analysis (heterogeneity precluded pooling)
- **Databases**: Medline/PubMed, EBSCO (D&OSS), Web of Science, Scopus; searched November 19, 2024; English/Spanish language; no publication-date restriction
- **n**: 269 records → 153 duplicates removed → 87 excluded at screening → 27 assessed for eligibility → 17 further excluded → 10 studies included (published 2016–2024 per Results; the Discussion section states "2015 and 2023" — a discrepancy within the source text itself)
- **Risk-of-bias tool**: QUADAS-2 (patient selection, index test, reference standard, flow and timing domains), applied after reviewer calibration; repeated after a 4-month interval to assess intra-rater reliability
- **Outcomes**: Accuracy (ISO 5725 trueness + precision definition), chairside time (definition varied across studies — some included tray selection/digitalization, some did not), patient comfort (validated VAS questionnaires)
- **Population**: Adolescent/adult participants ≥18 years, full or near-full dentition (edentulous spans >2 teeth excluded, completely edentulous excluded, systemic disease/craniofacial anomaly/limited cooperation excluded); sample sizes 5–50 per study; mean age 26.4 years (range 18–36) where reported; studies from Germany (n=5), South Korea (n=2), Italy (n=1), Romania (n=1), Switzerland (n=1)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Accuracy** (10/10 studies): mixed — several studies (Schmidt et al., Kuhr et al., Onbasi et al., Schmidt et al. [2nd]) found conventional impressions more accurate for full-arch/posterior/long-span measurements; Zimmermann et al. found IOS significantly more accurate overall (p<0.05); Sfondrini et al., Lee and Park, Sun et al., Janosi et al. found both techniques clinically comparable. Ender et al. found conventional impressions had fewer overall deviations, though all IOS deviations stayed under 100 µm.
- **Chairside/total time** (2/10 studies): Sfondrini et al. — total time digital 5 min 49 s vs conventional 22 min 6 s (p=0.001); isolated chairside-only time 5 min 49 s digital vs 7 min 32 s conventional; processing time 14 s digital vs 14 min 34 s conventional. Janosi et al. — total time digital 12 min vs conventional 75.5 min (p=0.001).
- **Patient comfort/VAS** (2/10 studies): Sfondrini et al. — mean VAS 9.14 digital vs 2.57 conventional (p<0.001), largest gaps in comfort and gag-reflex domains. Janosi et al. — mean VAS 9.02 digital vs 6.5 conventional (p=0.001).
- **QUADAS-2 inter-rater agreement (Cohen's kappa)**: patient selection 0.88, index test 1.00, reference standard 0.89, flow and timing 1.00, general risk 0.63 (moderate).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- No meta-analysis possible; heterogeneous accuracy-measurement methods, chairside-time definitions, and comfort-assessment timing across the 10 included studies limit direct comparison.
- Chairside time and patient comfort were each assessed in only 2 of the 10 included studies, both using similar IOS models (3Shape TRIOS 3/TRIOS 3 Mono) and alginate comparators — a narrow evidence base for the review's own title emphasis on chairside time and perception.
- Population is skewed toward young (mean 26.4y), fully dentate, systemically healthy adults with good oral hygiene; findings do not generalize to elderly, partially/fully edentulous, or complex prosthodontic populations — the authors explicitly flag this.
- QUADAS-2 showed unclear or high risk of bias in patient selection across most studies (small samples, non-randomized impression order in all but Zimmermann et al.), and unclear applicability in several domains.
- Publication-bias assessment was not possible without meta-analysis; language restricted to English/Spanish.
- Authors call for standardized chairside-time and comfort-assessment-timing protocols, larger/more diverse populations (including edentulous and craniofacial-anomaly patients), and further study of operator experience, scanner type, and environmental factors.

## 6. Related Work
- christopoulou-2022: Narrative/critical review answering closely related questions (IOS accuracy, reproducibility, duration, comfort, preference) in an orthodontic-specific population against alginate/PVS, searched to October 2020; Ramos-Morro 2026 reinforces Christopoulou's "contradictory" accuracy verdict and consistent patient-preference-for-digital finding with a later, broader (non-orthodontic-specific), more methodologically rigorous (PRISMA+QUADAS-2) evidence base, but does not resolve the accuracy contradiction and only thinly resolves chairside time (2/10 studies).
- singh-2025: Umbrella review (sr+ma) on device-ranked full-arch accuracy for prosthodontic/restorative indications (TRIOS 3, Primescan) — different clinical question (device selection for restorations vs general diagnostic-impression comparison) and different evidence base (10 SRs vs 10 primary studies).
- alkadi-2023: Narrative review of accuracy-influencing factors (arch size, edentulism, operator experience, scanner technology) — complementary mechanistic explanation for why Ramos-Morro's own included studies show more IOS error in full-arch/posterior regions (stitching-error accumulation), a mechanism Ramos-Morro's discussion also invokes.

## 7. Glossary
- **QUADAS-2**: Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies-2 — risk-of-bias/applicability appraisal tool for diagnostic accuracy studies, applied here across patient selection, index test, reference standard, and flow/timing domains.
- **VAS**: Visual Analog Scale — patient-reported comfort/preference rating scale used in the two chairside-time studies.
- **Shell-to-shell (S2S) deviation**: A 3D surface-superimposition method for quantifying the geometric difference between a digital and a reference (scanned gypsum) model.
- **Chairside time**: Time to complete the impression/scanning procedure itself, as distinct from downstream processing (e.g., gypsum model fabrication) — a distinction the review found inconsistently applied across its included studies.
