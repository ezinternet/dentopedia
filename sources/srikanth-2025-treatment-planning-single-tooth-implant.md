## Why Ingested

이 논문은 보철지향적(prosthetically driven) 단일치 임플란트 치료계획을 **4개 부위(상악 전치·후치, 하악 전치·후치)로 분해**해, 골밀도(Density)–골량(Dimension)–교합력의 부위별 우선순위를 하나의 표로 통합한 서술적 고찰이다. 이미 보유한 [[implants/raghavan-2025-bone-density-implant-dentistry-review]]는 **골밀도 분류 체계와 프로토콜 개조**만 다루고 부위별 치수 기준이 없으며, [[implants/joshi-2025-implant-tooth-proximity-sr-ma]]는 임플란트-인접치 근접 위험만 다루므로, 두 페이지의 공백(부위별 치수·해부학적 안전거리·외관 임플란트 심미)을 이 논문이 채운다. 또한 [[implants/loading-protocol/zhou-2021-placement-loading-protocols-single-implants-sr]]의 ITI 부위별 의사결정 행렬을 임플란트 치수·생물학적 폭·심지대와 연결하는 실무 가이드를 제공한다.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (J Maxillofac Oral Surg 2025;24:886–898, 65 citations) consolidating single-tooth implant treatment planning into four anatomic regions — maxillary anterior/posterior, mandibular anterior/posterior — with region-specific bone density, dimensional minimums, and occlusal priorities.

Universal dimensional minimums are tabulated: mesiodistal space 6–7 mm, buccolingual ridge width ≥6 mm (≥1.5 mm bone on every side), interocclusal clearance 5–7 mm, ≥1.5 mm to the adjacent tooth root, ≥3 mm interimplant distance, and ≥3 mm implant shoulder to free gingival margin; the "triangle of bone" concept places the fixture to bisect buccolingual bone volume rather than follow the socket trajectory.

Region priorities diverge sharply: maxillary anterior is D3 with ~2 mm facial bone and platform 3–4 mm apical to the gingival margin (esthetic-driven); maxillary posterior is D4 requiring under-drilling by ~0.5 mm, ≥10 mm vertical bone or sinus elevation, and >4–6 months healing; mandibular anterior is D1–D2 needing 3–4 months only but risking thermal osteonecrosis; mandibular posterior is D2 with a 2 mm IAN safety margin, 5 mm from the mental foramen, a 1:1 crown-to-implant ratio, and reported success >95%.

Clinical value is a single-page regional decision aid; the principal limitation is that it is a narrative synthesis with no pooled effect sizes, no systematic search strategy, and no patient-level outcome data — its papilla (crest-to-contact ≤5 mm) and success (>95%) figures are inherited from cited primary studies rather than generated.

## 세줄요약

서술적 고찰(Narrative Review, J Maxillofac Oral Surg 2025;24:886–898, 인용 65편): 단일치 임플란트 치료계획을 상악 전치·후치, 하악 전치·후치 4개 부위로 분해해 부위별 골밀도(Misch D 등급), 치수 최소 기준, 교합 우선순위를 정리한 임상 가이드.

보편 기준 — 근원심 간격 6–7 mm, 협순골 치조 폭 ≥6 mm(모든 면에 ≥1.5 mm 잔존골), 교합 간 여유 5–7 mm, 인접치 치근까지 ≥1.5 mm, 임플란트 간 ≥3 mm, 플랫폼에서 자유치연까지 ≥3 mm. "뼈의 삼각형(triangle of bone)" 개념으로 소켓 궤적 대신 협순골 부피를 이등분하도록 배치.

부위별 우선순위 상이 — 상악 전치는 D3, 안면골 ~2 mm, 치은연 3–4 mm 아피칼 플랫폼(심미 우선); 상악 후치는 D4로 최종 드릴을 임플란트보다 ~0.5 mm 언더사이즈, 수직골 ≥10 mm 또는 상악동 거상술 필요, 치유 >4–6개월; 하악 전치는 D1–D2로 3–4개월이면 충분하나 열괴사 위험; 하악 후치는 D2, 하악관 2 mm·mental foramen 5 mm 안전거리, 크라운:임플란트 1:1, 보고 성적률 >95%.

임상 가치는 한 장짜리 부위별 의사결정 표이며, 핵심 한계는 효과크기 풀링·체계적 검색 전략·환자 수준 결과가 없는 **서술적 종합**이라는 점이다. 치대(<5 mm)·성공률(>95%) 수치는 인용된 1차 연구에서 차용한 것이지 본 논문에서 산출한 값이 아니다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **부위별(4-region) 계획 프레임워크** — "universal principles + site-specific priorities" 이분법. 상악 전치는 심미(E)적 요구가 지배적, 후치는 교합 하중·역학적 고려가 지배적이라는 명시적 대비.
- **"Triangle of bone" 배치 개념 도입** — CBCT 횡단면에서 협순골 폭(저변)과 치조 중심(첨점)으로 정의된 삼각형을 임플란트가 이등분하도록 배치. 자연 소켓 궤적(socket trajectory)을 그대로 따르면 임플란트 오정렬 또는 협측 cortical plate 천공이 초래되므로, 밀도 높고 부피 큰 궤적으로 정렬한다고 설명(Ganz 1995 인용).
- **치수 최소 기준의 표(Table 1, Table 8) 통합** — 근원심 6–7 mm, 협순 ≥6 mm, 교합 간 5–7 mm, 인접치 ≥1.5 mm, 임플란트 간 ≥3 mm, 임플란트 어깨-자유치연 ≥3 mm.
- **부위별 요약 표 4개(Table 2–5)** — 상악 전치 / 상악 후치 / 하악 전치 / 하악 후치 각각의 골질(D 등급), 해부학적 고려, 외과적 핵심, 심미 우선순위, 위험을 1장에 압축.
- **프로토콜 선택 프레임** — 1단계 vs 2단계, 유막 vs 무막, 즉시식립, 즉시부하의 장단점·적응증을 Table 7로 정리.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Narrative review / clinical guide — no PRISMA, no registered protocol, no risk-of-bias assessment
- **Databases**: Not stated (no search strategy, no inclusion/exclusion criteria reported)
- **n**: 65 references cited (Refs 1–65, mostly textbooks and classic reviews; no pooled dataset)
- **Outcomes**: No primary outcome. Outputs are dimensional minimums, regional bone-density assignments, anatomical safety margins, and protocol indications.
- **Structure**: Pre-treatment evaluation → surgical considerations (4 regions) → surgical protocols → prosthetic considerations → conclusion. 9 tables carry the operative content; figures illustrate the prosthetic-driven workflow, triangle of bone, and anatomy landmarks.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### 4.1 Universal dimensional minimums (Table 1, Table 8)

| Dimension | Recommended minimum | Clinical relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Mesiodistal space | 6–7 mm | Standard-diameter implant placement |
| Buccolingual ridge width | ≥6 mm | Ensures ≥1.5 mm bone around implant on all sides |
| Interocclusal (vertical) space | 5–7 mm | Abutment + crown components |
| Implant-to-adjacent tooth | ≥1.5 mm | Avoids PDL damage, preserves papilla |
| Between adjacent implants | ≥3 mm | Bone preservation, soft-tissue esthetics |
| Implant shoulder to free gingival margin | ≥3 mm | Supports natural emergence profile |
| Bone encirclement all sides | ≥1.5 mm (1.5–2 mm ideal) | Long-term osseointegration + soft-tissue contour |

### 4.2 Regional summary (Tables 2–6)

| Region | Bone density (Misch) | Esthetic concern | Ideal BL width | Min MD space | Surgical key point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maxillary anterior | D3 (Type III) | High | ≥6 mm | 6–7 mm | Palatal placement, platform 3–4 mm apical to gingival margin; ~2 mm facial bone |
| Maxillary posterior | D4 (Type III/IV) | Low | ≥6–7 mm | 6–7 mm | Under-drill osteotomy; sinus elevation if vertical bone <10 mm |
| Mandibular anterior | D1–D2 (Type I/II) | Moderate | ≥5.5–6 mm | 5.5–6 mm | Careful spacing, avoid lingual perforation, bone tapping in D1 |
| Mandibular posterior | D2 (Type I/II) | Low | ≥6–7 mm | 6–7 mm | CBCT-guided angulation, 2 mm IAN safety, gradual drilling |

### 4.3 Region-specific operative numbers

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Papilla fill threshold (bone crest → contact point) | ≤5 mm favors papilla; ≥6 mm → papilla present in only ~50% of cases (Choquet 2001) |
| Maxillary anterior platform depth | 3–4 mm apical to planned gingival margin; >5 mm apical → long clinical crown + gingival shadowing |
| Maxillary anterior lateral-incisor implant | ~3.0–3.5 mm diameter, placed slightly shallower than a central incisor |
| Maxillary posterior vertical bone | ≥10 mm to place an implant; 6–8 mm crestal bone → indirect sinus lift with simultaneous placement |
| Maxillary posterior sinus floor buffer | 1–2 mm to avoid membrane trauma |
| Maxillary posterior osteotomy | Final drill ~0.5 mm smaller than implant diameter (under-preparation) |
| Maxillary posterior healing | >4–6 months before loading; immediate loading only if primary stability >35 Ncm and out of heavy occlusion |
| Mandibular anterior healing | 3–4 months (short, due to excellent primary stability) |
| Mandibular anterior implant depth | 12–15 mm; ≥1–2 mm above lower border |
| Mandibular anterior canine implant | >4 mm diameter, ~13 mm length where space allows |
| Mandibular posterior IAN safety margin | ~2 mm between implant apex and superior border of the canal |
| Mandibular posterior mental foramen | Posterior edge of implant ≥5 mm anterior to the mental foramen |
| Mandibular posterior implant size | 4.5–6 mm diameter preferred; 5–6 mm wide implants need ~7–8 mm ridge width |
| Crown-to-implant ratio | ~1:1 as the biomechanical goal |
| Immediate placement primary stability | Insertion torque >30–35 Ncm |
| Reported posterior mandible success | >95% when load and anatomy are respected |

### 4.4 Protocol comparison (Table 7)

| Protocol | Pros | Cons | Indications |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-stage surgery | Fewer surgeries, less morbidity, shorter treatment | Higher soft-tissue complication risk in esthetic areas | Non-esthetic sites, good bone, primary stability |
| Two-stage surgery | Better implant protection, ideal for grafted sites | Second surgery, longer treatment | Esthetic/grafted sites, soft bone, augmentation cases |
| Flap | Direct bone visualization, enables GBR | More trauma, longer recovery | Sites needing grafting or ridge modification |
| Flapless | Preserves blood supply, less discomfort, faster healing | Limited visibility, misplacement risk without guidance | Ideal bone/soft tissue, guided surgery available |
| Immediate placement | Preserves soft/hard tissue, shorter treatment | Requires primary stability, difficult soft-tissue handling | Fresh sockets with intact walls |
| Immediate loading | Faster to final restoration, patient convenience | Needs high insertion torque, failure if overloaded | Selected cases with sufficient primary stability |

### 4.5 Occlusal scheme (Table 9)

| Aspect | Recommended approach | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Centric contacts | Centralized, light, ideally in central fossa | Minimizes overload at bone-implant interface |
| Eccentric contacts | Minimized or eliminated, especially posteriorly | Reduces screw loosening and bone loss |
| Cuspal anatomy | Flat cusp inclines | Lowers shear stress |
| Occlusal table | Narrower | Decreases lateral load transmission |
| Guidance scheme | Canine guidance or mutually protected occlusion | Protects posterior restorations |
| Posterior disclusion | Posterior implants disclude during excursions | Prevents occlusal trauma |
| Force direction | Axial loading preferred; no cantilevers/off-axis | Long-term biomechanical stability |

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No systematic search strategy** — databases, search terms, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and study-count flow are absent, so selection bias cannot be assessed; the citations are heavily weighted toward classic textbooks (Misch) and pre-2010 reviews.
- **No quantitative synthesis** — no pooled effect sizes, no confidence intervals, no GRADE. Every number is transcribed from a cited primary study (e.g., the ~50% papilla rate from Choquet 2001, the >95% success rate from Cristea 2023).
- **No patient-level outcome data** — no survival/failure rates, no marginal bone loss (MBL), no peri-implantitis incidence for the single-tooth indication, and no comparison of single-tooth implants against resin-bonded bridges or conventional FPDs despite that being the clinically relevant alternative.
- **Anatomy numbers lack CBCT-evidence grading** — safety margins (2 mm IAN, 5 mm mental foramen, ≥1.5 mm to root) are presented as fixed rules without the diagnostic accuracy data that would justify the thresholds.
- **Flapless vs flap advantage is asserted without the failure signal** — the table credits flapless surgery with faster healing and less discomfort but omits the reported ~75% higher relative failure risk, which a clinician weighing the choice needs.
- **Future work**: digital/CAD-CAM guided surgery integration, personalization of implant design to bone density, and long-term maintenance protocols.

## 6. Related Work

- **Raghavan 2025** ([[implants/raghavan-2025-bone-density-implant-dentistry-review]]): narrative review of the same Misch D1–D4 classification with CBCT Hounsfield thresholds; that paper covers density → protocol, this one supplies the missing per-region dimensions and safety margins.
- **Zhou 2021** ([[implants/loading-protocol/zhou-2021-placement-loading-protocols-single-implants-sr]]): ITI Group 2 SR computing weighted survival by site × placement × loading; provides the evidence base that this narrative guide's site-specific protocol table approximates.
- **Joshi 2025** ([[implants/joshi-2025-implant-tooth-proximity-sr-ma]]): SR+MA establishing the ≥1.5 mm implant-to-root margin and quantifying pulpal damage in 20–25% of contact cases — the pooled evidence behind this paper's fixed dimensional rule.
- **Chrcanovic 2014** ([[implants/chrcanovic-2014-flapless-vs-conventional-implant-surgery-ma]]): meta-analysis of flapless vs flapped surgery (RR 1.75, 95% CI 1.07–2.86 for failure); relevant because Table 7 of the present paper lists flapless without this risk signal.
- **Choquet 2001**: primary retrospective source of the crest-to-contact-point ≤5 mm papilla threshold quoted throughout the anterior sections.
- **Ganz 1995**: original "triangle of bone" formulation cited as the basis for the placement concept.

## 7. Glossary

- **Prosthetically driven (restoration-driven) planning**: 최종 보철(크라운)의 위치를 먼저 진단상 왁스업(wax-up)으로 결정한 뒤 그 위치에 맞추어 임플란트를 배치하는 치료계획 원칙.
- **Triangle of bone**: CBCT 횡단면에서 협순골 폭을 저변, 치조 중심을 첨점으로 하는 삼각형. 임플란트가 이를 이등분하도록 배치하면 골 부피 분포가 최적화되고 cortical plate 천공이 최소화된다.
- **Gingival biotype (치은 표현형)**: 치음이 두껍고 평평한 양성 표현형(두꺼운 유형) 또는 얇고 부채꼴 모양의 음성 표현형(얇은 유형). 심예측가능성과 재발(recession) 위험을 좌우한다.
- **Crown-to-implant ratio (크라운-임플란트 비)**: 크라운 높이를 임플란트 길이로 나눈 비율. 이 논문은 하악 후치에서 약 1:1을 역학적 목표로 제시.
- **Platform switching (플랫폼 스위칭)**: 어댑션이 임플란트 플랫폼보다 좁은 연결 구조로, 임플란트 주변 순환 골·연조직 두께를 증가시켜 경주부 응력을 줄이는 설계.
- **Angulated screw channel (각도 스크루 채널)**: 전치부에서 시멘트 지합을 피하면서 보철철거 가능성을 유지하기 위한 비가시 위치 스크루 접근.
- **Misch bone density D1–D4**: D1 조밀피질골, D2 두꺼운 피질 + 거친 해면골, D3 얇은 피질 + 미세 해면골, D4 미세 해면골만 존재. 상악 전치 D3, 상악 후치 D4, 하악 전치 D1–D2, 하악 후치 D2가 전형.
- **Insertion torque (삽입 토크)**: 즉시식립·즉시부하 판단의 대표 안정성 지표. 이 논문은 30–35 Ncm 이상을 기준으로 제시.
- **Under-drilling (언더사이즈 드릴링)**: 최종 드릴 직경이 임플란트 직경보다 작아 이식이면서 스레드가 해면골을 압축해 초기 안정성을 높이는 드릴링법(상악 후치 D4 권고).
- **Bone tapping (본태핑)**: 밀도 높은 D1/D2 골에서 스레드를 사전 절삭해 삽입 토크와 골내 미세파괴를 줄이는 술식.
- **Emergence profile (진출 형태)**: 치연에서 치아 형태로 자연스럽게 이어지는 연조직 굴곡. 이온 전치 platform 3–4 mm 아피칼 배치와 관계.
- **Bicortical stabilization (양측피질고정)**: 임플란트가 양측 cortical plate를 모두 engaging하여 얻는 초기 안정화.
- **Underlingual / lingual undercut (설측 함요)**: 하악골 내면에서 골이 안쪽으로 파인 부분. 수직으로 드릴링 시 설측 cortical plate 천공 및 설골하 혈관 손상 위험.
- **MRONJ**: 약물연관 골괴사병(Medication-Related Osteonecrosis of the Jaws) — 비스포스포네이트·덴오수맙 사용 환자에서의 위험.
