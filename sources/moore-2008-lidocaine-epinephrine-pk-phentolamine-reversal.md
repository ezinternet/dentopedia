## Three-line Summary

Phase 1 pharmacokinetic trial (n=16, 4 treatment groups, Anesth Prog 2008) measuring PK parameters of lidocaine/epinephrine in a dental context with and without phentolamine mesylate reversal of soft-tissue numbness.

Phentolamine (alpha-adrenergic antagonist) removed epinephrine-mediated vasoconstriction, accelerating systemic lidocaine absorption from the injection site — raising Cmax slightly while delaying Tmax.

The safety-relevant implication is that phentolamine reversal does not cause toxic lidocaine accumulation, but clinicians should be aware of the modestly increased systemic absorption when using it in dose-sensitive patients.

## Summary

Phase 1 pharmacokinetic trial (n=16, 4 treatment groups; Anesth Prog 2008) measuring PK parameters of lidocaine/epinephrine in a dental context with and without phentolamine mesylate reversal.

Four treatments: 1 carpule lidocaine/epi → phentolamine (1L1P); 1 carpule phentolamine IV (1Piv); 4 carpules lidocaine/epi → 2 carpules phentolamine (4L2P); 4 carpules lidocaine/epi without reversal (4L). Key finding: phentolamine delays lidocaine Tmax while slightly increasing Cmax — alpha-adrenergic blockade removes epi vasoconstriction, accelerating systemic absorption from injection site.

## Key PK Data

- **Phentolamine effect**: delays lidocaine Tmax (slower initial rise), but slightly increases Cmax by blocking vasoconstriction
- **Phentolamine Tmax**: 7 min (IV) vs 11–15 min (submucosal)
- **t½, CL, Vd**: similar across all treatment groups
- **Clinical implication**: phentolamine reversal of LA increases systemic lidocaine bioavailability — not a LAST risk at therapeutic doses but mechanistically important
- **Dental dose context**: 1 carpule = 1.8 mL of 2% lidocaine = 36 mg; 4 carpules = 144 mg; typical 70 kg patient max = 7 mg/kg × 70 kg = 490 mg = ~13–14 carpules
