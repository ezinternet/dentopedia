## Why Ingested

2026-09-29 흡연과 조기 임플란트 실패 서베일런스에서 확인. 기존 [[implants/survival/fan-2024-smoking-early-implant-failure-sr-ma]]의 정량 메타분석(조기 실패 OR 2.59)을 2020-2023 발표 연구의 질적 종합으로 보완하며, 조기·후기 실패와 용량-반응을 함께 다뤄 [[implants/survival/naseri-2020-smoking-levels-implant-failure-sr-ma]]의 흡연량 근거와 짝을 이룸. 메타분석은 수행되지 않았음.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (33 studies, 29,519 implants, more than 18,301 patients; PubMed and Cochrane search of 2020-2024 literature, mostly retrospective) on smoking and dental implant failure.

25 of 33 studies found a significant association between smoking and implant failure; 7 studies assessed early failure (e.g., OR 2.14; OR 2.07 early vs 1.48 late), and dose-response findings included OR 18.3 for more than 10 cigarettes per day; no pooled estimate was calculated.

Smoking appears to raise both early and late failure risk and reduction of daily consumption may help, but heterogeneity, single-database searching, and lack of confounder control limit the strength of the conclusions.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review, SR; 메타분석 없음), 연구 33편, 임플란트 29,519개, 환자 18,301명 이상 (2020-2024 문헌, 대부분 후향 연구) — 흡연과 임플란트 실패의 관계.

33편 중 25편이 흡연과 임플란트 실패의 유의한 연관을 보고; 조기 실패 평가 7편 (예: OR 2.14; 조기 OR 2.07 vs 후기 OR 1.48), 용량-반응은 하루 10개비 초과 시 OR 18.3; 통합 추정치는 산출되지 않음.

흡연은 조기·후기 실패 위험을 모두 높이는 것으로 보이며 흡연량 감소도 도움이 될 수 있으나, 연구 간 이질성·단일 데이터베이스 검색·교란변수 미통제로 결론의 강도는 제한적.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Update of the smoking and implant failure literature restricted to 2020-2024 publications, with no meta-analysis.
- Separates early implant failure (EIF; 7 studies, varying definitions) from later failure and reports dose-response findings.
- 25 of 33 studies significant; the 8 non-significant studies covered 11,864 implants in 5475 patients (about one third of the total).
- No study on e-cigarettes met the inclusion criteria.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Search**: advanced PubMed and Cochrane library search, (dental implant) AND (smoking) AND (implant failure) plus MeSH (dental implants) AND (smoking), run 03.03.2024, restricted to 2020-2024. Limitations section says only one database was searched.
- **Selection**: 564 records; 117 after date restriction; 33 included. Screening by HLS and JI, disagreements resolved with CW. Excluded, among others: 21 reviews, 2 experimental studies, 1 comment, 2 ongoing trials, 2 full texts not retrievable, 6 non-English, 23 without implant failure data, 10 without smoker vs non-smoker failure rates, 1 with inaccurate published data.
- **Eligibility**: human prospective/retrospective studies (RCT, non-randomized, cohort, observational) classifying smokers vs non-smokers with at least ten implants; excluded head and neck cancer, free flaps, failed sites, zygoma and mini implants, student-placed implants, studies excluding smokers.
- **Outcome**: implant failure defined as loss of the implant; effect measures were mean difference, OR, HR as reported by the studies.
- **Risk of bias**: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (max 9 points).
- **Meta-analysis**: not performed, because of heterogeneity in designs, comparison groups, outcome definitions, follow-up times, confounder handling, and effect measures without CIs.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 33 studies; 29,519 implants; more than 18,301 patients; sample sizes 22 to 4247 patients; follow-up 1 to 22 years; most retrospective; 4 multicentre studies.
- Risk of bias: 1 study high risk, 4 with some concerns, 28 low risk (NOS 7-8).
- 25/33 significant association with failure; 8/33 not.
- Early failure: 7 studies, "early" defined from 3-4 months to 12 months or until loading; 2 found no association; one found OR 2.140 (P < 0.001); Nagao et al. OR 2.07 early vs OR 1.48 late loss; one study 1.049 odds per additional pack-year (P < 0.001).
- Dose-response: more than 10 cigarettes/day, risk of implant loss multiplied by 18.3 (survival time decreased 4.2%, P = 0.017); heavy smokers (>10 pack-years) HR 1.81 (P = 0.039).
- Long term: 94% implant survival at 15 years in one study; another reported smoking HR 36.35 (P = 0.001); one study OR 5.35 (years 1-4) and OR 4.66 (after 4 years).
- Large studies: 1420 implants in 826 patients, smoking had the highest failure proportion versus other medical risk factors such as diabetes (P < 0.05); 1820 implants in 771 patients, failure 13.5% vs 4.4% (P = 0.027), 5.2 times greater risk.
- Implant brand: 8 studies no significant difference; one (Lazaro-Abdulkarim) found 2 of 5 brands with lower risk. Prosthesis type: only one study significant.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No meta-analysis; qualitative synthesis only, no certainty-of-evidence (GRADE) rating reported.
- Publication bias, heterogeneity, differing study quality; a single database (per Limitations) limits retrieval.
- Smoking frequency rarely assessed; no e-cigarette studies; confounding not consistently controlled.
- Internal inconsistencies in the text: "Four of the 32 included studies" (33 elsewhere); search dated to 2024 but all included studies published 2020-2023.
- Future work: smoking frequency, cessation interventions, e-cigarettes and other tobacco products.

## 7. Glossary

- **EIF**: early implant failure, defined variably (3-4 months to 12 months or until loading) in the included studies.
- **NOS**: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, 9-point quality tool for cohort and case-control studies.
- **Pack-year**: packs smoked per day multiplied by years smoked.
- **Dose-response**: rising failure risk with rising cigarette consumption.
