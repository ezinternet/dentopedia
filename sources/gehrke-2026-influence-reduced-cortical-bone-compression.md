## Why Ingested
This is the in vivo counterpart to the same author's 2021 bench-only healing-chamber paper ([[wiki/implants/osteotomy-thermal/gehrke-2021-healing-chambers-macrogeometry-low-density-drilling]]): it holds the osteotomy protocol constant at 2.8 mm for 3 mm implants and varies only implant macrogeometry, then supplies the 21-day rabbit-tibia BIC/histomorphometry the 2021 study could not. It also tests the torque ≠ stability dissociation claimed in [[wiki/overviews/implant-macrogeometry-clinical-outcomes-overview]] on a histologic endpoint rather than an ISQ endpoint, converting that overview's "lower torque yet equivalent ISQ" finding into a "lower torque yet better bone healing" result.

## Three-line Summary
Combined in vitro + in vivo preclinical study: 40 titanium prototype implants (3 × 6 mm, grade IV) split into Control (standard cylindrical macrogeometry) vs Test (macrogeometry with healing chambers), insertion torque measured in polyurethane blocks (n = 10/group), then 21-day healing in rabbit tibia with intra-animal paired design (5 rabbits, 4 implants each = n = 10/group).

Test implants seated at significantly lower insertion torque (5.70 ± 0.85 vs 8.01 ± 0.82 Ncm, p < 0.0001) yet achieved higher bone-to-implant contact (Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC) at the two deeper cervical levels — C2 59.30% vs 40.30%, C3 42.10% vs 17.90% (p = 0.0002) — with more bone tissue (BT) and less collagen tissue (CT) and marrow space (MS) at C2/C3, plus a higher radiographic blue-channel (high-density) contribution in both the lateromedial (27.36% vs 9.73%) and proximodistal (42.59% vs 29.39%) directions.

Clinically it suggests that lowering insertion torque via macrogeometry rather than tolerating dense cortical compression may be the better trade — but it is a 21-day rabbit study in a 3 mm non-clinical-sized implant with an identical surface treatment on both arms, so the authors explicitly cannot separate reduced compression from the healing chambers' own clot-stability / haptotaxis / contact-guidance effects, and human long-term validation is still absent.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Isolates implant macrogeometry as the single manipulated variable by holding the osteotomy constant (2.8 mm drill, 6 mm depth, 800 rpm, 1 kg bench pressure) across both arms — separating macrogeometry from the drilling-protocol effects that confound much of the osteometry literature.
- First in vivo histology for this specific healing-chamber cylindrical design: shows the LOWER-torque design achieving higher BIC, not merely equal stability.
- Adds a non-destructive radiographic density readout (thermal pseudocolour + RGB channel decomposition in a 2 × 3 mm peri-implant ROI) alongside histology, using the same group's previously published method (Gehrke et al., *J Funct Biomater* 2025, 16, 415).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Combined in vitro + in vivo preclinical experimental study; intra-animal (paired) in vivo design — both Test and Control implants placed in each rabbit, animals serving as their own controls.
- **n**: 40 implants total (3 × 6 mm, commercially pure titanium grade IV, titanium-oxide microparticle + sandblasting surface, Implacil/Osstem). 20 per group. In vitro IT arm n = 10/group; in vivo arm 5 rabbits × 4 implants = 20 implants, n = 10/group.
- **Test implant**: cylindrical with healing chambers — circular holes between threads, chamber diameter 0.6 mm, chamber depth 0.5 mm. **Control**: unaltered cylindrical, thread depth 0.5 mm and pitch 1 mm in both.
- **In vitro**: polyurethane foam synthetic bone blocks (ASTM F1839-08 standard material), 95 × 45 × 35 mm, 1 mm cortical at 40 PCF + 10 PCF low-density medullary layer mimicking rabbit tibia; lance drill then 2.8 mm cylindrical drill to 6 mm; computerized digital torque meter CME-30 (Filizola) with 0.002° angular resolution; peak torque value per implant analysed.
- **In vivo**: adult New Zealand rabbits (Oryctolagus cuniculus), 3.5–4.0 kg, 15-day acclimation, University of Rio Verde Ethics Committee protocol 04/20, ARRIVE + CONCEA Res. 15/2013, 3Rs. Anaesthesia ketamine 35 mg/kg + xylazine 5 mg/kg IM, local 4% articaine with epinephrine 1:100,000, meloxicam 0.2 mg/kg + morphine 5 mg/kg, prophylactic Pentabiotic 84,000 IU/kg. Right tibia: Test proximal / Control distal; left tibia inverted, so P/D position is counterbalanced. Euthanasia at 21 days with 3% pentobarbital 50 mg/kg IV.
- **Outcomes**: (primary) in vitro peak insertion torque; (primary) BIC% by histomorphometry at three transverse cervical levels C1/C2/C3 within the first 3 mm (IsoMet 1000, Technovit 7200 VLC historesin, picrosirius hematoxylin, ImageJ 1.52v); (secondary) BT/CT/MS area fractions in a 4 × 6 mm ROI by digital colour segmentation with examiner cross-verification; (secondary) radiographic RGB channel percentages in a 2 × 3 mm lateral peri-implant ROI, lateromedial and proximodistal projections (IriX-ray DX 3000 + RVG First sensor, Fiji/ImageJ 2.9.0).
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk then parametric; paired Student's t-tests for all Control-vs-Test comparisons; Cohen's dz; post hoc power > 99% at α = 0.05.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- In vitro IT was measured in synthetic polyurethane blocks, not in living bone — the authors state the link from bench torque to in vivo healing is indirect by construction (the rationale was that surgical torque meters are analogue and inaccurate).
- Only one timepoint (21 days); no removal-torque, ISQ, micro-CT or mechanical push-out endpoint, and no long-term or loaded endpoint.
- Macrogeometry is not isolated from the healing chambers' other biological actions — the authors explicitly decline to attribute the gain to compression reduction alone, citing clot stability, cell selection, haptotaxis, contact guidance and strain distribution as competing explanations.
- Same surface treatment (TiO₂ microparticles + sandblasting) on both arms and a 3 × 6 mm prototype size that does not correspond to any clinical implant dimension, so transfer of the magnitude of effect is uncertain.
- n = 5 rabbits; the paired design was chosen for power under the 3Rs reduction principle rather than by a priori sample-size calculation.
- Rabbits remodel bone far faster than humans and have anisotropic tibia geometry; the authors call for long-term human studies before clinical application.
- The manuscript reports paired tests for insertion torque, although the bench arm has no within-animal pairing (foam blocks are independent) — the pairing rationale stated in §2.9 applies to the in vivo and radiographic arms.

- Gehrke et al. 2021 (*J Indian Soc Periodontol*): same first author, same healing-chamber concept, but in vitro only and with drilling technique as a variable — this paper supplies the in vivo histology and fixes drilling as a constant.
- Gehrke et al. 2024 (*Bioengineering* 11:1155): same group's preclinical cervical-design study; prior work in the same series.
- Gehrke et al. 2023 (*Medicina* 59:168): randomized clinical trial comparing insertion torque and implant stability of two macrogeometries — the clinical antecedent of this bench/animal sequence.
- Gehrke et al. 2022 (*Sci Rep* 12:538): insertion torque values and marginal bone loss in sheep mandibles — the source of the "high IT alone does not cause marginal bone loss" position cited in the Discussion.
- Gehrke et al. 2025 (*J Funct Biomater* 16, 415): the group's RGB-radiograph method (reference 39) applied here.
- Chanquini/Bhattacharya et al. 2025 (*Prosthet Dent*): human split-mouth RCT of the same healing-chamber concept — lower insertion torque with equivalent ISQ; the clinical counterpart to this animal result.
- Tabassum et al. 2021 (*Saudi Dent J*) and Di Stefano et al. 2018 (*J Appl Oral Sci*): the opposing immediate-mechanical tradition — undersizing raises insertion torque and raises immediate BIC; contrast with the 21-day healing-timepoint finding here.
- Ratiu et al. 2025 (*Medicina* 62:72): early osseous proliferation in spiraled healing chambers in rabbit cortical bone.
- Ferreira Balan et al. 2024 / Ferri et al. 2024 (*Bioengineering*): blade-equipped implants controlling lateral cortical pressure — an alternative route to the same "reduce cortical compression" goal.

## 7. Glossary
- **Healing chamber**: circular voids cut between implant threads (0.6 mm diameter, 0.5 mm deep here) intended to reduce cortical compression and provide space for clot organization and bone ingrowth.
- **Insertion Torque (IT)**: peak torque recorded during implant seating; a press-fit measure of mechanical primary stability. Here the highest value attained during insertion was taken.
- **Bone-to-Implant Contact (BIC)**: percentage of the implant surface in direct contact with bone, measured on transverse sections; the standard histologic osseointegration metric.
- **Cortical bone compression**: lateral mechanical strain the implant inflicts on dense, poorly vascularized cortical bone; excessive compression is proposed to cause microfracture and delay regeneration.
- **Histomorphometry**: quantitative tissue-area measurement (bone tissue / collagen tissue / marrow space fractions) by colour segmentation of stained sections.
- **RGB channel decomposition**: thermal pseudocolour mapping of radiograph greyscale so that pixel intensity — proportional to X-ray attenuation by calcium and phosphate — is binned into red (low), green (intermediate) and blue (high) density channels.
- **Intra-animal (paired) design**: both implants placed in each animal so the animal is its own control, removing between-subject biological variance; tested with paired Student's t-tests.
