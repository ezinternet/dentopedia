## Why Ingested
발치경 vs 피에조톰의 직접 비교 RCT — [[oral-surgery/pujarern-2026-minimally-invasive-extraction-socket]]의 기법 비교를 수치로 보강; 근관치료 실패치에 특화된 최초 RCT로 임상 결정 근거 제공.

## One-line Summary
Double-blind RCT (n=100, endodontically failed single-rooted teeth): periotome was faster (5.78 vs 12.8 min, p<0.01) with better immediate pain control, but piezotome caused less immediate marginal bone loss (0.083 vs 0.54 mm, p<0.007); 6-month bone loss not significantly different between groups.

## 한줄요약
이중맹검 RCT (n=100, 근관치료 실패 단근치): 발치경이 더 빠르고(5.78 vs 12.8분, p<0.01) 즉시 통증 조절 우수; 피에조톰은 즉시 변연골 소실 더 작음(0.083 vs 0.54 mm, p=0.007); 6개월 후 골소실은 두 군 차이 없음.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First RCT comparing periotome vs piezotome specifically for endodontically treated teeth
- Establishes periotome as faster, lower-cost option with equivalent long-term bone outcomes
- Piezotome shows superior immediate marginal bone preservation despite longer operative time
- At 6 months, neither technique significantly outperforms the other on bone volume

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Design: Prospective, double-blind, randomized controlled trial
- Population: 100 patients (58F, 42M), age 20–55 yr (mean 32.47); single-rooted teeth with failed root canal treatment requiring extraction
- Randomization: Computer-generated; 50 per group (periotome vs piezotome)
- Periotome group: Hu-Friedy periotome in modified pen grasp, 20° to tooth long axis, sever PDL fibers from cervical to 2/3 root length on all 4 surfaces → extraction with appropriate forceps
- Piezotome group: SATLEC ACTEON, LC 2 tips, ultrasonic blade inserted between bone and root along all 4 surfaces, apical increments 2–4 mm
- Outcomes: Operation duration, VAS pain (0–7 days postop), marginal bone loss (immediate, Hu-Friedy probe), bone loss at 6 months (CBCT vs preop landmarks), gingival laceration, complications
- Follow-up: 96 patients analyzed (4 lost to follow-up, 2 per group)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Operation time: Piezotome 12.8 ± 7.4 min vs periotome 5.78 ± 4.0 min (p<0.01)
- Pain reduction day 7: Periotome group significantly better (p=0.002)
- Immediate marginal bone loss: Piezotome 0.083 ± 0.24 mm vs periotome 0.543 ± 0.25 mm (p=0.007)
- Bone loss 6 months — height: piezotome 4.21 ± 0.26 mm vs periotome 3.71 ± 1.24 mm (NS)
- Bone loss 6 months — buccolingual width: piezotome 2.85 ± 1.28 mm vs periotome 2.67 ± 2.24 mm (NS)
- Gingival laceration: Grade 1 in 2 periotome + 1 piezotome (NS)
- No significant dry socket or buccal plate fracture in either group

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Only single-rooted teeth — multi-rooted extractions not studied
- Sample size limits statistical power for subgroup comparisons
- Short follow-up beyond 6 months not assessed
- One of few studies comparing these two techniques; larger multicenter RCTs recommended

## 6. Related Work
- Vercellotti: Developed piezosurgery with claims of minimal soft-tissue damage
- Bortoluzzi: Longer surgery → increased postoperative pain (supports periotome advantage)
- Tsai et al.: Piezotome showed better attachment level preservation (contrasts with this study)
- Melek & Noureldin: Piezotome required more time than periotome — consistent finding

## 7. Glossary
- **Periotome**: Thin-bladed instrument combining mini-scalpel and miniature elevator; severs PDL by wedging motion along root axis
- **Piezotome (piezosurgery)**: Ultrasonic piezoelectric device using micro-vibrations (25–30 kHz) to cut bone; selective cutting — damages only hard tissue
- **PDL (Periodontal Ligament)**: Fibrous connective tissue attaching root cementum to alveolar bone; severance is key to atraumatic extraction
- **Marginal bone loss**: Difference between pre- and post-extraction crestal bone height measured by probing/CBCT
