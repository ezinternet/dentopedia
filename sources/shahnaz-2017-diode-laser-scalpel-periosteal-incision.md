## Why Ingested

The same Tehran group (Moslemi, Khorsand, Fekrazad — with Aysan Shahnaz herself a co-author) previously published [[suture-wound-closure/moslemi-2016-periosteal-releasing-incision-diode-laser-gbr]], an uncontrolled 17-patient case series showing diode-laser periosteal releasing incision (PRI) achieves tension-free closure with low morbidity, but with no scalpel comparator to isolate the laser's contribution. This 2017 paper is the randomized follow-up: an 18-patient RCT with Shahnaz now first author, adding the missing scalpel-control arm and directly testing whether laser PRI reduces postoperative morbidity (pain/NSAID use/swelling) versus conventional scalpel technique during implant surgery with simultaneous bone grafting, while also confirming implant survival is not compromised.

## Three-line Summary

Preliminary RCT (Lasers Med Sci 2017; n=18 implant patients undergoing simultaneous bone grafting; Tehran University of Medical Sciences) comparing diode laser (810 nm) vs scalpel periosteal releasing incision for flap advancement.

VAS pain score (days 1-4), NSAID consumption (days 1-3), and swelling intensity (days 1-5) were all significantly lower in the laser group vs scalpel control (all P<0.05); one scalpel-group patient had ecchymosis; all implants survived in function at 6 months in both groups.

Laser PRI reduces early postoperative morbidity relative to scalpel PRI without harming implant survival, but the trial is small, single-center, and abstract-only.

## 세줄요약

예비 무작위대조시험 (RCT; Lasers Med Sci 2017; 임플란트 동시 골이식 환자 n=18; 테헤란 의과대학) — 다이오드 레이저(810 nm) vs 메스 골막이완절개(periosteal releasing incision, PRI) 비교.

VAS 통증점수(1-4일), NSAID 복용률(1-3일), 부종 강도(1-5일) 모두 레이저군이 메스 대조군보다 유의하게 낮음(전부 P<0.05); 메스군 1명 반상출혈(ecchymosis) 발생; 6개월 시점 양군 모두 임플란트 생존율 100%.

레이저 PRI는 임플란트 생존에 악영향 없이 술후 초기 이환율을 낮추나, 소규모·단일기관·초록만 확보된 예비 연구로 근거 수준에 한계.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Adds the **randomized scalpel-control arm** missing from the same group's earlier [[suture-wound-closure/moslemi-2016-periosteal-releasing-incision-diode-laser-gbr]] case series, converting an uncontrolled observation into a comparative RCT.
- Extends laser-PRI evidence from a **GBR ridge-augmentation context** (Moslemi 2016) to **implant placement with simultaneous bone grafting**, and switches the laser wavelength from 940 nm to 810 nm.
- Reports a **consistent multi-domain morbidity benefit** (pain, NSAID consumption, swelling) for laser over scalpel across the early postoperative window, not just a single endpoint.
- Confirms **no detrimental effect on implant survival** at 6 months of loading, addressing the safety question a morbidity-only case series could not answer.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Preliminary (pilot) randomized clinical trial, two parallel arms (test = laser, control = scalpel).
- **n**: 18 patients scheduled for dental implant placement with simultaneous bone grafting, randomly assigned to test or control groups.
- **Intervention**: Diode laser, 810 nm, 2 W, pulse interval 200 μs, pulse length 100 μs, 400-μm initiated fiber tip, used to sever the periosteum for a tension-free flap.
- **Control**: Scalpel periosteal incision, same clinical goal (tension-free flap).
- **Outcomes measured over 6 postsurgical days**: VAS pain score, NSAID consumption rate, swelling intensity, ecchymosis.
- **Long-term outcome**: implant survival rate assessed 6 months after loading.
- Randomization method, allocation concealment, blinding, and statistical test details are not reported in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **VAS pain score**: significantly lower in laser group during postoperative days 1-4 (P<0.05).
- **NSAID consumption**: significantly lower in laser group during postoperative days 1-3 (P<0.05).
- **Swelling intensity**: significantly lower in laser group during postoperative days 1-5 (P<0.05).
- **Ecchymosis**: one patient in the scalpel control group; none reported for laser group.
- **Implant survival**: all implants successful in function at 6 months post-loading in both groups — no detrimental effect of laser incision on implant survival.
- Exact numeric VAS/NSAID/swelling values and per-day means are not available from the abstract alone.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Small preliminary trial** (n=18 total, split across two arms) — explicitly labeled "preliminary" by the authors; likely underpowered for anything beyond the reported morbidity endpoints.
- **Single-center** (Tehran University of Medical Sciences), single author group — same investigators as the earlier uncontrolled case series, so independent replication is still lacking.
- **Abstract-only ingestion**: randomization/blinding methodology, exact effect sizes, and full statistical reporting are unavailable and not reconstructed.
- **Short follow-up** for implant survival (6 months of loading) — does not address longer-term marginal bone loss or peri-implant outcomes.

## 6. Related Work

- Direct predecessor / uncontrolled arm: [[suture-wound-closure/moslemi-2016-periosteal-releasing-incision-diode-laser-gbr]] — same author group's 940-nm diode-laser PRI case series (n=17, GBR context, no comparator); this RCT adds the scalpel-control arm that page lacked.
- Technique-context systematic review: [[suture-wound-closure/plonka-2017-flap-designs-flap-advancement-implant-therapy]] — quantifies periosteal releasing incision as the single largest contributor to flap advancement (+5.5 mm) under the PASS principle; this RCT tests an instrument-level variation (laser vs scalpel) of that same maneuver.

## 7. Glossary

- **PRI (periosteal releasing incision)**: an incision through the periosteum at the base of a mucoperiosteal flap that releases tension, allowing flap advancement for tension-free primary closure.
- **Diode laser**: a semiconductor laser (here, 810 nm) used as a soft-tissue cutting instrument, an alternative to a scalpel blade.
- **VAS (visual analogue scale)**: a patient-reported pain-intensity scale, typically a 0-100 mm line anchored "no pain" to "worst pain."
- **Ecchymosis**: subcutaneous bruising/discoloration from blood leaking into tissue, a marker of surgical/vascular trauma.
- **NSAID**: nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug, used here as a rescue-analgesic consumption proxy for postoperative pain.
