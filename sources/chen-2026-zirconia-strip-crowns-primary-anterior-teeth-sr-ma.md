## Why Ingested

사용자가 요청한 PMC 전문(全文) 논문으로, 유전치(primary anterior teeth) 우식 수복에서 prefabricated zirconia crown (PZC) vs resin-bonded composite strip crown (RCSC) 비교의 SR+MA (RCT 9편, 치아 1746개) — 기존 [[wiki/caries/chavhan-2026-zirconia-vs-ssc-primary-dentition-sr-ma]]가 유구치(molar)에서 PZC vs stainless steel crown (SSC)을 다룬 것과 짝을 이루어, 전치부(anterior) PZC vs RCSC 비교라는 별개 임상 의사결정 축을 보강한다.

## Three-line Summary

SR + meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024625706; 9 RCTs, 1746 primary anterior teeth, children aged 3–6 years) directly comparing prefabricated zirconia crowns (PZC) with resin-bonded composite strip crowns (RCSC) for restoration of primary anterior teeth.

PZC was significantly superior for secondary caries prevention (RR=0.41), crown retention (RR=0.15), restoration failure (RR=0.07), and plaque accumulation (RR=0.08); however, PZC caused significantly more wear of opposing teeth than RCSC (RR=7.58, favoring RCSC); 8/9 studies were low risk of bias.

PZC is the stronger restorative choice for primary anterior teeth on most clinical outcomes, but its zirconia hardness creates an opposing-tooth wear tradeoff; surface polish rather than bulk hardness appears to drive this wear clinically.

## 세줄요약

SR + 메타분석(PROSPERO CRD42024625706; RCT 9편, 유전치 1746개, 3–6세 아동) — prefabricated zirconia crown (PZC)과 resin-bonded composite strip crown (RCSC)의 유전치(primary anterior teeth) 수복 효과를 직접 비교.

PZC가 이차우식 예방(RR=0.41), 크라운 유지(RR=0.15), 수복 실패(RR=0.07), 플라크 축적(RR=0.08)에서 유의하게 우수; 단, 대합치 마모는 PZC가 RCSC보다 유의하게 많아(RR=7.58, RCSC 유리); 9편 중 8편이 낮은 비뚤림 위험.

대부분 임상 결과에서 PZC가 유전치 수복의 더 강한 선택이지만, 지르코니아 경도로 인한 대합치 마모 절충이 존재; 연마도(surface polish)가 bulk 경도보다 임상 마모를 결정하는 주요 요인으로 제시.

## 1. Document Information

- Journal: BMC Oral Health
- Publication date: 2026-01-30
- PROSPERO registration: CRD42024625706
- Funding/COI: not stated in retrieved text (standard BMC disclosure applies); underlying included studies — 6/9 non-profit funded, 1/9 industry-funded (NuSmile, PZC materials), 2/9 not declared.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First meta-analysis directly comparing PZC vs RCSC (as opposed to PZC vs stainless steel crown, which dominates prior primary-molar literature) specifically for **anterior** primary teeth.
- Quantifies 8 outcomes across effectiveness (secondary caries, crown retention, restoration failure, marginal fit, color match) and adverse effects (wear of opposing teeth, plaque, gingival bleeding).
- Identifies publication bias specifically in the crown-retention outcome (Egger's p=0.041), correcting via trim-and-fill while flagging the pooled retention advantage as likely an upper-bound estimate.
- Highlights a methodological gap common to all 9 included studies: none stratified outcomes by specific anterior tooth position (central vs lateral incisor), meaning pooled estimates represent an average across the "primary anterior cohort" rather than position-specific effects.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: PRISMA-guided systematic review + meta-analysis; PROSPERO-registered (CRD42024625706).
- Databases: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, searched to 2025-06-21; English-language only.
- Population: children <13 years with primary anterior teeth requiring crown restoration (caries or trauma); all 9 included studies ultimately enrolled children aged 3–6 years.
- Intervention/Comparator: PZC vs RCSC.
- Study design eligibility: RCTs only.
- Risk of bias: Cochrane RoB 2.0, independently rated by 2 reviewers, third reviewer for disagreements.
- Statistics: Stata 16; risk ratios (RR) with 95% CI; fixed-effects (inverse-variance) when I²≤50%/p≥0.10, random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird) when heterogeneous; leave-one-out sensitivity analysis; funnel plot + Egger's test for outcomes with ≥5 studies; trim-and-fill correction for detected bias.
- Screening: 3760 records retrieved, 1630 duplicates removed, 2112 excluded at full-text stage, 9 RCTs (1746 teeth) included from 6 countries (Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Canada, India, Egypt, UAE).
- RoB result: 8/9 studies low overall risk; 1/9 high risk (unclear randomization).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Studies (n teeth) | Model | RR (95% CI) | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Secondary caries | 5 (636) | fixed | 0.41 (0.18–0.92) | 0.030 |
| Crown retention | 5 (820) | fixed | 0.15 (0.07–0.33) | <0.001 |
| Restoration failure | 3 (241) | fixed | 0.07 (0.02–0.22) | <0.001 |
| Wear of opposing teeth | 4 (652) | fixed | 7.58 (2.73–21.07) | <0.001 (favors RCSC) |
| Color mismatch | 4 (734) | random | 0.24 (0.05–1.21) | 0.084 (NS) |
| Gingival bleeding | 3 (446) | fixed | 0.09 (0.01–1.73) | 0.109 (NS) |
| Plaque occurrence | 2 (358) | fixed | 0.08 (0.01–0.23) | <0.001 |
| Marginal fit | 2 (286) | random | 0.50 (0.05–8.70) | 0.672 (NS) |

RR values <1 favor PZC except for wear of opposing teeth, where RR>1 indicates PZC has greater antagonist wear. Sensitivity analysis (leave-one-out) showed no material change to effect sizes for outcomes with >3 studies. Publication bias detected only for crown retention (Egger's p=0.041); trim-and-fill correction confirmed stability but authors flag the retention advantage as a likely overestimate/upper-bound.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Marginal fit and plaque index each based on only 2 studies — authors explicitly caution these need further validation.
- Publication bias in crown-retention outcome; asymmetric funnel plot.
- No study stratified outcomes by anterior tooth position (central vs lateral incisor) — pooled effects are an average across the anterior cohort, potentially masking clinically relevant positional differences.
- English-language restriction introduces potential language bias.
- High risk of performance/detection bias across most studies (blinding of subjective outcomes like patient/parent satisfaction infeasible given visibly distinct materials).
- Significant heterogeneity (I²) noted for several outcomes attributed to differing baseline caries risk, operator skill, adhesive brand, and follow-up duration — fixed-effects pooling should be read as an average across variable contexts.
- Grey-literature search did not comprehensively cover conference abstracts/dissertation databases.
- Authors call for future RCTs with standardized outcome reporting, tooth-position-stratified subgroup data, and surface-modification techniques for PZC to reduce opposing-tooth wear.

## 6. Related Work

- Cites prior work establishing zirconia hardness differential vs enamel (Vickers Hardness ~1000–1400 HV for zirconia vs ~300–400 HV enamel) as the theoretical basis for opposing-tooth wear concern, contrasted against a cited 2-year clinical trial finding polished 5Y-TZP zirconia crowns did not show significantly greater enamel wear than natural antagonists — suggesting surface polish, not bulk hardness, drives clinical wear outcomes.
- Discusses composite resin hardness (~50–80 HV, lower than enamel) and its paradoxical susceptibility to uneven wear patterns that may accelerate antagonist wear despite lower intrinsic hardness.
- References emerging "bionic gradient zirconia/resin composite" materials as a future direction to optimize both wear resistance and biocompatibility.
