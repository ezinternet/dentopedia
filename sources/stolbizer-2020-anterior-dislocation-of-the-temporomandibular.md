## Why Ingested

위키에 급성 악관절 탈구 (acute TMJ dislocation) 복원 자체를 다룬 페이지가 하나도 없었고, [[tmj/calixtre-2015-manual-therapy-tmd-pain-rom-sr]]·[[tmj/demelo-2020-manual-therapy-myofascial-pain-tmd-sr]]은 근막통형 TMD의 통증·개구량에만 초점을 둔다. 이 논문은 42예의 무마취 수동복원 (manual reduction) 성적과 45° 하방후방 지속압 + 환자 주도 개폐·측방 운동의 단계별 기법을 제공해, 기존 히포크라테스식 기법 (>85%) 외에 통증 없는 대체 경로를 수동치료 계열 근거로 넓힌다. 또한 표준 폐쇄(pop)-gate인 후두강직 (trismus, 입 못 닫음) 방향과 반대인 입 잠김(개구 고정·하악전방 돌출) 응급을 [[tmj/falletti-2026-update-trismus-etiology-diagnosis-treatment]]와 별개 축으로 보완한다.

## Three-line Summary

Uncontrolled retrospective case series of 42 patients (out of 45 acute/chronic anterior TMJ dislocations treated 2011–2018 at the University of Buenos Aires dental emergency service; trauma-related cases excluded) describing a simplified non-traumatic manual reduction technique: seated patient, operator thumbs behind the last lower molars, gentle steady 45° downward-posterior pressure held 1–2 min, then patient-performed opening/closing and lateral jaw movements — no sedation or local/general anaesthesia, one operator only.

Reduction (full condylar repositioning into the glenoid fossa) succeeded in 100% of cases, taking 1.5–7 min in 40 of 42 patients; 12 cases needed up to 3 repetitions of the whole cycle (two 1-min rest intervals) and the 2 hardest cases — both referred after failed reduction elsewhere, dislocated for 2 h and 3 h — took up to 15 min and 6 repetitions; all 42 patients reported no pain during the manoeuvre.

A 23/42 subgroup (55%) arrived after ≥1 failed Hippocratic attempt elsewhere, so the series is enriched for exactly the patients in whom repeat conventional reduction usually fails — but with no control group, no force measurement and no pain-score quantification, the 100% figure is an uncontrolled single-arm success rate and cannot establish superiority over the conventional method.

## 세줄요약

무대조 후향 환자 연쇄 (uncontrolled retrospective case series, n=42) — 2011–2018년 부에노스아이레스 치과대학 응급실에서 치료한 45예 급성·만성 전방성 악관절 탈구 중 외상성 제외 42예를 대상으로, 의식진정·국소·전신마취 없이 단일 술자가 시행한 비외상적 수동복원 (non-traumatic manual reduction) 기법을 기술함.

복원률 100%(42/42), 소요시간은 42예 중 40예에서 1.5–7분; 12예는 전체 사이클을 최대 3회 반복(1분 휴식 2회 삽입) 필요했고, 타 기관 복원 실패 후 전원되어 2시간·3시간 경과된 2예는 최대 15분·6회 반복 소요. 전원 환자 23/42(55%)는 타원에서 1회 이상 히포크라테스식 복원에 실패한 경우였음.

용어 표기: 전방성 하악탈구 (Anterior Mandibular Dislocation, AMD)·히포크라테스 양수내 양손법 (Hippocratic bimanual intraoral method, 성공률 >85%)·수동복원 (manual reduction)·비외상적 기법 (non-traumatic technique)·복원 후 안정화 (post-reduction stabilisation). 대조군·적용력 측정·통증 점수화(경증/중증/중증)가 모두 없어 100%는 단일군 성공률일 뿐 히포크라테스 기법보다 우수함을 입증하지 못하며, 저자 스스로 통증·소요시간·적용력 지표로 시험군/대조군 임상시험을 요구함.

- **DOI**: 10.1080/10669817.2019.1704516
- **Institution**: Department of Maxillofacial Surgery and Traumatology II & III, School of Dentistry, University of Buenos Aires, Argentina (Emergency and Patient Referral Department)
- **Period covered**: 2011–2018 (394,000 patients received oral care at the School of Dentistry in that window; 45 TMJ dislocations were treated in the Emergency Service)
- **Ethics**: University Ethics Committee Resolution 026/2011, Declaration of Helsinki, written informed consent from all patients

## 2. Key Contributions
- Describes a **simplified non-traumatic manual reduction technique** in which the operator never changes the intensity or direction of pressure: the operator's thumbs rest behind the last lower molars applying a gentle, steady 45° downward-posterior pressure, while the **patient** performs the opening/closing (up to 5×) and lateral (up to 5×) movements that actively reposition the condyles with the elevator muscles relaxed.
- Reports the technique on a **42-patient series the authors state is the largest published for anterior spontaneous dislocation reduction** — prior literature, they note, consists of isolated cases or series of only 7–20 patients, and most published variants (devices, general anaesthesia, traction, surgery) increase rather than reduce procedural complexity.
- Positions patient **anxiety/tension as the main treatment obstacle**: forceful stretching triggers a myotatic reflex causing sudden mouth closure (and possible injury to the operator's thumbs), and pain anticipated on a second attempt makes repeat Hippocratic reduction likely to fail.
- Thumbs are deliberately placed **behind, not on, the last molars' occlusal surface**, explicitly to prevent the patient biting the operator's fingers.
- Unilateral cases are handled by placing the thumbs bilaterally but applying pressure **only on the dislocated side**.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Uncontrolled retrospective case series / technique report (single centre, single operator group, no control arm)
- **Setting**: Emergency Service, School of Dentistry, University of Buenos Aires; outpatient, no procedural sedation, no local or general anaesthesia
- **n**: 42 patients (male and female, acute and chronic anterior TMJ dislocation); drawn from 45 TMJD cases treated 2011–2018 out of 394,000 oral-care patients
- **Exclusions**: trauma-related TMJ dislocation
- **Recorded variables**: time to achieve condylar repositioning, number of times the procedure had to be repeated, subjective pain perception (mild / moderate / severe)
- **Success definition**: full repositioning of the condyle in the glenoid cavity
- **Comparator**: none — no group treated with the conventional Hippocratic method (the authors name this as the report's principal limitation)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **No control group** — the authors state this explicitly: there is no cohort treated with the most common (Hippocratic) technique to serve as a control, so the 100% success rate cannot be attributed to the technique rather than to the single-operator setting, patient selection, or learning curve.
- **No force quantification** — the applied force is described qualitatively ("gentle, steady"), never measured, so the technique cannot be standardised or reproduced between operators.
- **Pain not scored numerically** — only a subjective mild/moderate/severe category was recorded, and every patient fell in the "no pain" category without an instrument (no VAS/NRS), so the pain claim is unverified.
- **No follow-up / recurrence data** — post-reduction stabilisation, splinting, or recurrence rates are not reported, so the paper speaks only to immediate reduction, not to durable success.
- **Single centre, single-team, unblinded** — one emergency physician group in one dental school; generalisability to other settings, operators, or non-dental emergency departments is untested.
- **Not the first such report** — the authors cite prior series of 7–20 cases, so novelty rests on sample size and the patient-driven movement idea, not on being the first non-traumatic variant.
- **Authors' own requested next step**: controlled clinical trials measuring complete reduction, time spent, applied-force magnitude and pain intensity in test vs control arms across different dislocation conditions, plus standardised multi-centre reporting so a meta-analysis of the competing techniques becomes possible.

## 6. Related Work
- [[tmj/calixtre-2015-manual-therapy-tmd-pain-rom-sr]] (Calixtre 2015, `sr`): technique-stratified manual therapy for TMD pain/ROM — this series carries the same "hands-on TMJ manipulation" family into a different indication (acute joint dislocation) and a different mechanism (patient-driven active movement under sustained passive pressure rather than therapist-delivered mobilisation).
- [[tmj/demelo-2020-manual-therapy-myofascial-pain-tmd-sr]] (de Melo 2020, `sr`): MT for masticatory myofascial pain with a home-programme comparator — same manual-therapy lineage, but outcomes are pain relief, not condylar repositioning.
- [[tmj/falletti-2026-update-trismus-etiology-diagnosis-treatment]] (Falletti 2026, `narrative-review`): trismus (mouth will not *open*) management algorithm; anterior TMJ dislocation is the mirror-image presentation (mouth will not *close*, with prognathism) and is not covered there — this page fills that gap in the acute TMJ emergency pathway.

## 7. Glossary
- **Anterior mandibular dislocation (AMD)**: displacement of the condylar head out of the glenoid fossa beyond the articular eminence that does not reduce spontaneously; patients present with mandibular prognathism and inability to close the mouth.
- **Hippocratic bimanual intraoral method**: the classical 5th-century BC technique — thumbs intraorally on the molars, downward force to clear the articular eminence, then re-seating the condyles in the glenoid fossa; success rate above 85% but perceived as traumatic.
- **Myotatic reflex**: the stretch reflex that converts forceful muscle stretching into sudden involuntary mouth closure, the mechanism behind both patient pain and operator thumb injury in conventional reduction.
- **Articular eminence**: the bony rim of the glenoid fossa the condyle must clear before it can be re-seated; its morphology (along with capsule/ligament laxity and muscle hyperactivity) is a predisposing factor.
- **Trismus**: restricted mouth *opening* (< 35 mm interincisal) — the opposite functional deficit to the locked-open jaw of anterior dislocation.
- **Post-reduction stabilisation**: the unaddressed next step after a successful reduction (splinting, recurrence prevention), not reported in this series.
