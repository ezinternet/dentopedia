## Three-line Summary
ACOG Committee Opinion No. 569 (2013), a multidisciplinary consensus statement by ACOG, ADA, HRSA, and the National Maternal and Child Oral Health Resource Center, synthesizes published literature and meta-analyses on oral health throughout pregnancy; at the time of publication, 56% of US women had not visited a dentist during pregnancy (2007–2009 data).

Dental care—including radiographs, local anesthesia, and most medications—is endorsed as safe across all trimesters; periodontal treatment during pregnancy does NOT reduce preterm birth or low birth weight based on multiple meta-analyses; endocarditis prophylaxis is limited to AHA highest-risk cardiac patients only.

Untreated oral disease poses greater risk to mother and fetus than dental treatment itself; maternal-to-infant Streptococcus mutans transmission via utensil sharing is a modifiable caries risk factor warranting counseling.

## One-line Summary
ACOG Committee Opinion (2013) concludes that dental care—including dental X-rays, local anesthesia, and most medications—is safe throughout pregnancy, and untreated oral disease poses greater risk than dental treatment.

## 한줄요약
ACOG 위원회 의견서(2013)는 치과 X선·국소마취·대부분의 치과 약물이 임신 중 안전하며, 구강 질환 방치가 치과 치료보다 더 큰 위험이라고 결론짓는다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Authoritative multidisciplinary position statement (OB/GYN + Dentistry)
- Explicitly endorses dental care safety during all trimesters
- Addresses periodontal disease–preterm birth relationship: notes no benefit of periodontal treatment on preterm birth/low birth weight in recent meta-analyses
- Provides specific guidance on antibiotic prophylaxis (AHA criteria only), endocarditis risk, oral cancer screening
- Recommends counseling on maternal-to-infant transmission of cariogenic bacteria via sharing utensils

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Document type: Committee Opinion (consensus/position statement)
- Evidence base: Narrative review of published literature, meta-analyses, and guidelines from AHA, ADA, USPSTF
- Reviewed by: Oral Health Care During Pregnancy Advisory Committee

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Dental visits during pregnancy: 35% of US women did not have a dental visit in past year; 56% did not visit dentist during pregnancy (2007–2009 data)
- Periodontal disease + preterm birth: Recent meta-analyses show NO benefit of periodontal treatment for reducing preterm birth or low birth weight
- Periodontal disease association with preeclampsia: Conflicting evidence
- Safety statement: "Oral health care, including use of radiographs, pain medication, and local anesthesia, is safe throughout pregnancy"
- Fluoride: Recommended for cavity prevention during pregnancy
- Endocarditis prophylaxis: Only for patients at highest cardiac risk (AHA criteria)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Published 2013 — some evidence base predates recent systematic reviews
- Observation-based data on dental visit rates; no intervention trial component
- Does not address specific drug categories in detail (e.g., specific antibiotic choices, analgesic safety by trimester)
- Periodontal treatment studies are heterogeneous; statement should be updated as evidence accrues

## 6. Related Work
- 2000 US Surgeon General's report: Oral Health in America
- AHA endocarditis prophylaxis guidelines
- USPSTF oral cancer screening evidence review
- Multiple meta-analyses on periodontal treatment and pregnancy outcomes (Offenbacher, Michalowicz)

## 7. Glossary
- **ACOG**: American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists
- **Committee Opinion**: ACOG's mechanism for rapid guidance on emerging or underrepresented issues; less rigorous than Practice Bulletins
- **Endocarditis prophylaxis**: Pre-procedural antibiotic to prevent infective endocarditis in high-risk cardiac patients
- **Periodontal disease**: Infection of supporting structures of teeth; associated with systemic inflammatory burden
- **Maternal-to-infant transmission**: Transfer of Streptococcus mutans from mother to infant via saliva sharing (utensils, kissing)
