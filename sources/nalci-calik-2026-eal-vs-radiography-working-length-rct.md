## One-line Summary
RCT (BMC Oral Health 2026): 125 patients, 4 EALs vs periapical radiography; no significant pain differences between methods for working length determination.

## 한줄요약
RCT (BMC Oral Health 2026): 125명, 4종 EAL vs 방사선 사진; 작업장 결정 방법 간 유의한 통증 차이 없음.

## 1. Document Information
- Journal: BMC Oral Health, 2026. DOI: 10.1186/s12903-026-09451-8

## 2. Key Contributions
- Multi-arm RCT directly comparing 4 different EALs vs periapical radiography in clinical practice
- Primary outcome is patient pain/experience during working length determination
- Demonstrates no significant patient-reported difference between EAL and radiographic methods
- Provides head-to-head data across 4 EAL devices in the same clinical trial

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: RCT (multi-arm)
- Population: 125 patients requiring root canal treatment
- Interventions: 4 different EAL devices (brand names not specified in abstract)
- Control: periapical radiography for working length determination
- Primary outcome: patient pain/discomfort during working length procedure
- Statistics: comparative analysis across 5 groups (4 EALs + radiography)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- No significant pain differences between any of the 4 EALs and periapical radiography
- All methods appear equivalent in terms of patient discomfort during the procedure
- Specific EAL accuracy data not reported in abstract (primary outcome was pain)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Primary outcome was pain, not accuracy — limited data on working length accuracy per method
- EAL brand names/generations not specified in abstract
- Single center; patient selection criteria not detailed in abstract

## 6. Related Work
- EAL accuracy well-established (see kaur-2024, pisano-2024); this RCT adds patient experience data
- Pain during WL determination is relevant patient-centered outcome rarely studied in RCT format

## 7. Glossary
- EAL: Electronic Apex Locator — device measuring electrical impedance to locate apical constriction
- Working length (WL): distance from reference point to apical termination for instrumentation
