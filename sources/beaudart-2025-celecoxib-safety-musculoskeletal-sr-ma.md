## Why Ingested

This umbrella review pools 16 systematic reviews to quantify celecoxib's safety profile (GI/CV/renal) across the same OA/RA/AS population that [[drug/analgesics/zingel-2025-nsaids-cardiovascular-risk-inflammatory-arthritis]] examined narratively — where Zingel argued NSAIDs are not cardiovascular-risky (and may be cardioprotective) in RA/AS, Beaudart supplies the pooled meta-analytic numbers (celecoxib vs non-selective NSAID cardiovascular mortality RR 0.75) that reinforce that claim specifically for a COX-2-selective agent, directly informing dental perioperative NSAID selection for these patients.

## Three-line Summary

This umbrella review included 16 systematic reviews (all based on randomized controlled trials) to evaluate celecoxib safety in osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, and ankylosing spondylitis, searching MEDLINE, Cochrane Central, and Scopus through November 2024. Celecoxib demonstrated superior gastrointestinal safety compared with non-selective NSAIDs, with a pooled risk ratio for gastroduodenal ulcers of 0.22 (95% CI 0.15–0.32, I² = 0%) from one high-quality meta-analysis; cardiovascular outcomes were generally similar to non-selective NSAIDs, although one meta-analysis showed reduced cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.57–0.99, p = 0.04) with celecoxib, and renal adverse events appeared lower with celecoxib than placebo (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.66–0.94, p < 0.05).

Despite consistent gastrointestinal safety advantages, most included reviews (14/16) were rated critically low quality by AMSTAR-2, and evidence certainty was downgraded to moderate for gastroduodenal ulcers and low for all other outcomes due to risk of bias, imprecision, and inconsistency. The authors recommend individualized risk-benefit assessment and future integration of both randomized and observational data to better inform long-term safety in high-risk populations.

## 세줄요약

이 총괄적 문헌고찰(umbrella review)은 16개의 체계적 문헌고찰(모두 무작위 대조 임상시험 기반)을 포함하여 골관절염(osteoarthritis, OA), 류마티스 관절염(rheumatoid arthritis, RA), 강직성 척추염(ankylosing spondylitis, AS)에서 셀레콕십(celecoxib)의 안전성을 평가했으며, 2024년 11월까지 MEDLINE, Cochrane Central, Scopus를 검색했습니다. 셀레콕십은 비선택성 비스테로이드성 항염증제(non-selective NSAIDs)보다 우수한 위장관 안전성을 보였으며, 하나의 고품질 메타분석에서 위십이지장 궤양(gastroduodenal ulcers)에 대한 통합 위험비(RR)는 0.22 (95% CI 0.15–0.32, I² = 0%)였습니다; 심혈관 결과는 비선택성 NSAID와 대체로 유사했으나, 한 메타분석에서 셀레콕십이 심혈관 사망률을 감소시켰고(RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.57–0.99, p = 0.04), 신장 이상반응은 셀레콕십에서 위약보다 낮았습니다(RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.66–0.94, p < 0.05).

일관된 위장관 안전성 이점에도 불구하고, 포함된 대부분의 문헌고찰(14/16)이 AMSTAR-2에서 매우 낮은 품질(critically low quality)로 평가되었고, 위십이지장 궤양에 대한 근거 확실성은 중간(moderate)으로, 다른 모든 결과는 비뚤림 위험, 불정밀성, 불일치로 인해 낮음(low)으로 하향 조정되었습니다. 저자들은 개별화된 위험-이득 평가와 고위험 집단에서의 장기 안전성을 더 잘 평가하기 위해 무작위 및 관찰 연구 자료의 통합을 권장합니다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Celecoxib was consistently associated with a 78% lower risk of gastroduodenal ulcers compared with non-selective NSAIDs (RR 0.22, 95% CI 0.15–0.32, I² = 0%; moderate certainty evidence)
- Cardiovascular mortality was significantly reduced with celecoxib compared with non-selective NSAIDs (RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.57–0.99, p = 0.04), but myocardial infarction and stroke risks were not significantly different
- Renal composite events (dysfunction, peripheral edema, hypertension) were lower with celecoxib than placebo (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.66–0.94, p < 0.05)
- All-cause mortality was reduced with celecoxib compared with non-selective NSAIDs in one meta-analysis (Cheng et al.), though another meta-analysis reported no statistical analysis
- Most included reviews (14 of 16) were rated as critically low quality by AMSTAR-2, limiting confidence in overall findings
- Evidence certainty (GRADE) was moderate for gastroduodenal ulcers and low for all other outcomes due to risk of bias, imprecision, and inconsistency
- PRECISION trial data (a large long-term RCT) demonstrated celecoxib's non-inferiority and potential superiority in composite major toxicity compared with ibuprofen and naproxen

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design:** Umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses (with or without meta-analysis) following PRISMA 2020 guidelines and Joanna Briggs Institute manual for umbrella reviews
- **Protocol:** Registered on Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/jn52b/) prior to conduct; no deviations
- **Databases & search:** MEDLINE (Ovid), Cochrane Central Systematic Reviews (Ovid), Scopus; searched November 2024; supplemented by manual bibliography searches and expert consultation; consultation of Epistemonikos to ensure no missed references
- **Inclusion criteria:** Systematic reviews with/without meta-analysis; adult humans with OA, RA, and/or AS; any dose/duration of celecoxib; reported cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, renal safety, or all-cause mortality; published in peer-reviewed journals; randomized and non-randomized designs included (though all identified were RCT-based); English language only
- **Exclusion criteria:** Mixed populations without separate analyses for OA/RA/AS; non-peer-reviewed publications
- **Study selection:** Two independent reviewers screened titles/abstracts and full texts; conflicts resolved by consensus
- **Data extraction:** One reviewer extracted using standardized form, second reviewer verified; authors contacted if data missing
- **Quality appraisal:** AMSTAR-2 tool used by two independent reviewers; 16-item checklist with 7 critical domains; reviews categorized as high, moderate, low, or critically low quality
- **Evidence synthesis:** Narrative synthesis; GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation) adapted for umbrella reviews to assess strength of evidence for each outcome
- **Number of included studies:** 16 systematic reviews/meta-analyses (all based on RCTs); 13 (81.25%) in OA, 11 (68.75%) in RA, 4 (25%) in AS
- **Comparators:** Placebo (13 studies), non-selective NSAIDs (naproxen, diclofenac, ibuprofen) (13 studies), non-selective NSAIDs + proton pump inhibitors (2 studies)
- **Quality of included reviews:** 2 rated high quality; 14 rated critically low quality
- **Outcomes assessed:** Cardiovascular (mortality, MI, stroke, composite events); gastrointestinal (gastroduodenal ulcers, complaints/events, bleeding/perforation/obstruction); renal (composite events, raised creatinine); all-cause mortality
- **Statistics:** Risk ratios (RR), odds ratios (OR), Peto odds ratios, 95% confidence intervals, I² heterogeneity, p-values

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Cardiovascular outcomes:**
  - Cardiovascular mortality: celecoxib vs placebo — RR 3.02 (95% CI 0.36–25.27, I² = 0%, p = 0.31); celecoxib vs non-selective NSAIDs — RR 0.75 (95% CI 0.57–0.99, I² = 0%, p = 0.04) [Cheng et al., 2021]
  - Myocardial infarction: celecoxib vs placebo — RR 1.87 (95% CI 0.39–8.90, I² = 0%, p = 0.43); celecoxib vs non-selective NSAIDs — RR 1.08 (95% CI 0.88–1.33, I² = 0%, p = 0.46) [Cheng et al.]
  - Stroke: celecoxib vs placebo — RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.13–6.92, I² = 0%, p = 0.97); celecoxib vs non-selective NSAIDs — RR 0.94 (95% CI 0.71–1.24, I² = 10%, p = 0.64) [Cheng et al.]
  - MI + stroke combined: celecoxib vs placebo — Peto OR 3.40 (95% CI 0.73–15.88, I² = 0%, p = 0.12) [Puljak et al., 2017]
  - Cardiovascular events (unspecified): celecoxib vs placebo — OR 1.12 (95% CI 0.66–1.90, I² = 0%, p = NS) [Zeng et al., 2015]; celecoxib vs NSAIDs+PPIs — RR 1.67 (95% CI 0.78–3.59, I² = 0%, p = NS) [Wang et al., 2011]

- **Gastrointestinal outcomes:**
  - Gastroduodenal ulcers: celecoxib vs non-selective NSAIDs — RR 0.22 (95% CI 0.15–0.32, I² = 0%; from Fidahic et al., 2017, high-quality meta-analysis based on 5 studies, 1568 patients)
  - Gastroduodenal ulcers: celecoxib vs placebo — significantly higher with celecoxib [Ashcroft et al., 2001]
  - Gastrointestinal complaints (abdominal pain, diarrhea, dyspepsia, nausea): inconsistent across studies; some showed celecoxib superior to non-selective NSAIDs, others showed no difference or worse than placebo
  - GI bleeding/perforation/obstruction: fewer with celecoxib than NSAIDs [Rostom et al., 2007], but not supported by Deeks et al. (2002) or Jarupongprapa et al. (2013)
  - GI events (unspecified): higher odds with celecoxib than placebo — OR reported but CI NR [Zeng et al., 2015]

- **Renal outcomes:**
  - Composite renal events (dysfunction, peripheral edema, hypertension): celecoxib vs placebo — RR 0.79 (95% CI 0.66–0.94, p < 0.05) [Zhang et al., 2006, 25 studies]
  - Raised creatinine (>1.3× upper limit of normal): celecoxib vs placebo — RR 1.65 (95% CI 0.69–4.0, p = NS) [Moore et al., 2005, 5 studies, n = 2,776]; celecoxib vs non-selective NSAIDs — RR 0.78 (95% CI 0.46–1.3, p = NS) [Moore et al., 2005, 9 studies, n = 15,319]

- **All-cause mortality:**
  - Celecoxib vs placebo: no significant change in risk [Cheng et al., 2021]
  - Celecoxib vs non-selective NSAIDs: reduced risk [Cheng et al., 2021]; but not supported by Wang et al. (2011) — 6 deaths in each group, no statistical analysis, total N not reported

- **GRADE certainty:**
  - Gastroduodenal ulcers vs non-selective NSAIDs: moderate certainty (downgraded for risk of bias in primary studies)
  - All other outcomes: low certainty (downgraded for imprecision and/or inconsistency)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Most included systematic reviews (14/16) were rated as critically low methodological quality by AMSTAR-2, reducing confidence in overall findings
- Majority of included reviews did not provide detailed participant demographics or comorbidities, limiting subgroup analyses for high-risk populations (elderly, cardiovascular/renal disease)
- Reliance on RCT-derived data only (observational studies excluded) may limit generalizability to routine clinical practice and detection of rare/long-term adverse events
- Systematic reviews often excluded observational studies which could provide complementary information on long-term and rare events
- Substantial heterogeneity across studies in populations, interventions, outcome definitions complicates interpretation
- Short follow-up periods in many RCTs may underestimate long-term or rare adverse events
- Potential publication bias (especially industry-sponsored trials) cannot be ruled out
- AMSTAR-2 severe downgrading may overstate limitations when core methodological components (selection, extraction, bias assessment) are adequate
- Deliberate restriction to OA/RA/AS limits generalizability to other musculoskeletal conditions
- Future research should integrate high-quality randomized and observational data, and systematic reviews synthesizing evidence from different study designs are warranted

## 6. Related Work

- Nissen et al. (2016) — PRECISION trial: large pragmatic RCT demonstrating non-inferiority of celecoxib vs naproxen and ibuprofen for cardiovascular outcomes in OA/RA patients at moderate cardiovascular risk (N Engl J Med 375:2519-29)
- Solomon et al. (2017) — Secondary analysis of PRECISION: celecoxib associated with significantly lower incidence of composite major toxicity (cardiovascular, GI, renal events, all-cause mortality) than ibuprofen and naproxen; NNH 82 for ibuprofen, 135 for naproxen vs celecoxib (Am J Med 130:1415-22)
- Yeomans et al. (2018) — PRECISION GI sub-analysis: confirmed significantly lower incidence of clinically significant GI events and iron deficiency anemia with celecoxib (Aliment Pharmacol Ther 47:1453-63)
- Ruschitzka et al. (2017) — PRECISION-ABPM sub-study: significantly lower 24-h systolic blood pressure increase and new-onset hypertension with celecoxib vs ibuprofen (Eur Heart J 38:3282-92)
- Obeid et al. (2022) — PRECISION renal outcomes: celecoxib associated with lower incidence of renal events (acute kidney injury, hospitalization for heart failure/hypertension) than ibuprofen and naproxen (Eur Heart J 8:611-21)
- McGettigan and Henry (2011) — Observational study: celecoxib cardiovascular risk RR 1.17 (95% CI 1.08–1.27), lower than diclofenac or rofecoxib, comparable to ibuprofen at typical doses (PLoS Med 8:e1001098)
- Silverstein et al. (2000) — CLASS study: gastrointestinal toxicity of celecoxib vs NSAIDs (JAMA 284:1247-55)
- Topol (2004) — Rofecoxib withdrawal and FDA issues (N Engl J Med 351:1707-9)
- Antonioli et al. (2025) — Observational study: increased postoperative heart failure risk with perioperative celecoxib in type 2 diabetes patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty (J Arthroplasty 40:S184-7)
- Kim et al. (2025) — Nationwide cohort: no significant CV or GI safety advantage of celecoxib over non-selective NSAIDs in AS patients (younger lower-risk population) (Scand J Rheumatol 54:204-12)

## 7. Glossary

- **COX-2 (Cyclooxygenase-2)**: 사이클로옥시게나제-2 — 효소로, 염증 매개체(프로스타글란딘) 합성에 관여하며, 선택적 COX-2 억제제는 위장관 보호 효과를 유지하면서 염증을 감소시킴
- **NSAID (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug)**: 비스테로이드성 항염증제 — 진통, 해열, 항염 효과를 가진 약물군
- **PPI (Proton Pump Inhibitor)**: 프로톤 펌프 억제제 — 위산 분비를 억제하는 약물; NSAID 관련 위장관 손상 예방에 사용
- **GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation)**: 근거 평가 등급 체계 — 근거의 확실성을 평가하고 권고 강도를 결정하기 위한 도구
- **AMSTAR-2 (A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews)**: 체계적 문헌고찰의 방법론적 질 평가 도구 — 16개 항목, 7개 핵심 영역으로 구성
- **Umbrella review**: 총괄적 문헌고찰 — 특정 주제에 대한 여러 체계적 문헌고찰의 결과를 종합하여 평가하는 연구 방법론
- **PRECISION trial (Prospective Randomized Evaluation of Celecoxib Integrated Safety vs Ibuprofen or Naproxen)**: 셀레콕십, 이부프로펜, 나프록센의 심혈관 안전성을 비교한 대규모 무작위 대조 임상시험
- **Gastroduodenal ulcer**: 위십이지장 궤양 — 위 또는 십이지장 점막에 발생한 궤양성 병변
