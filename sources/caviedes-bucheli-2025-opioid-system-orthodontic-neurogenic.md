## Why Ingested
This PRISMA-guided comprehensive review maps the entire endogenous opioid counter-system that suppresses orthodontic-induced neurogenic pulpal inflammation — explaining why most orthodontic patients remain asymptomatic despite measurable SP/CGRP upregulation. It directly extends the nociception molecular mechanism framework captured in [[orofacial-pain/kim-2024-trigeminal-sensory-system-orofacial-pain]] by adding the antinociceptive (opioid/SST/endocannabinoid) dimension to the existing pro-inflammatory neuropeptide evidence.

## Three-line Summary
PRISMA-guided comprehensive review; 38 studies selected from 819 identified (PubMed/Scopus/ISI Web of Science, searched through December 2024); focus on endogenous opioid modulation of orthodontic-induced dental pulp neurogenic inflammation.
Endogenous opioids (β-endorphin, methionine-enkephalin, dynorphin, SST, endocannabinoids) act via MOR/DOR/KOR receptors and anti-inflammatory cytokine cascades (IL-10, TGF-β) to suppress SP/CGRP-driven neurogenic inflammation; severe forces can overwhelm these mechanisms, leading to asymptomatic irreversible pulpitis or necrosis.
Clinically, moderate forces (56–180 g) with extended reactivation intervals (~40–50 days) allow opioid system recovery; forces >180 g risk exceeding the pulp's antinociceptive capacity.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First comprehensive PRISMA/AMSTAR review specifically mapping endogenous opioid counter-system in orthodontic pulpal neurogenic inflammation
- Establishes force-threshold framework: moderate 56–180 g (opioid system compensates) vs. severe >180 g (overwhelms antinociceptive capacity)
- Identifies 8 opioid peptide categories across 38 studies: β-endorphin (12), SST (7), methionine-enkephalin (5), endocannabinoids (5), leu-enkephalin (3), dynorphin (3), orphanin (3), anti-inflammatory cytokines (1)
- Maps both peripheral and central antinociceptive pathways activated by orthodontic forces
- Explains receptor heterodimerization (MOR–NK1, MOR–DOR, CB1–DOR) as mechanistic basis for variable pain experience among patients

## 3. Methodology
- **Design**: PRISMA + AMSTAR-guided comprehensive literature review
- **Databases**: PubMed (304 hits), Scopus (305), ISI Web of Science (210) + manual search (21) = 840 total; after deduplication 572 retained
- **Final selection**: 38 articles after excluding irrelevant (450) and non-criteria (73)
- **Search terms**: "dental pulp," "tooth pulp," "opioids," "neurogenic inflammation," "orthodontic movement" combined with each opioid peptide keyword
- **Inclusion**: reviews/clinical trials on dental pulp × endogenous opioids; cell-line studies on orthodontic opioid expression; Q1–Q4 SCImago-indexed English journals meeting AMSTAR/PRISMA standards
- **Exclusion**: non-pulp/non-opioid focus; pharmacological opioid interventions; non-indexed journals; poor methodological transparency
- **Search period**: up to December 2024; two independent reviewers + arbitration by third reviewer

## 4. Key Results

### Peripheral mechanisms (moderate forces, 56–180 g)
- Orthodontic forces sensitize Aδ and C-fibres → upregulate SP, CGRP, NKA → neurogenic inflammation with plasma extravasation
- Counter-response: immune cells (neutrophils, macrophages, lymphocytes) in pulp/PDL express β-endorphins → MOR activation on Aδ fibres → hyperpolarization (GIRK K⁺ channels) → antinociception
- Methionine-enkephalin rapidly depleted then upregulated proportionally to force intensity; β-endorphin/methionine-enkephalin both show increasing trends at 24 h and 7 days

### Central mechanisms (severe forces, >180 g)
- Monoaminergic descending system activated: locus coeruleus (noradrenergic), dorsal raphe/periaqueductal grey (serotonergic) inhibit caudal nucleus via Laminae I, II, V
- Enkephalinergic system activated by nociception from tooth movement
- Serotonin inhibits SP release in superficial trigeminal nucleus layers

### Somatostatin (SST) system
- SST-immunoreactive fibres identified in human dental pulp
- SST acts pre-synaptically to inhibit neuropeptide release (SP, CGRP) and inhibits nociceptor sensitization via SST2 receptors

### Endocannabinoid system
- CB1 receptors found in trigeminal ganglion; CB2 in immune cells
- Anandamide, 2-AG synthesized by B lymphocytes, PMNs, NK cells, macrophages, platelets
- Severe/long-term orthodontic forces may upregulate CB1 mRNA; CB1–DOR heterodimerization enhances antinociception in neuropathic conditions

### Bradykinin & monoaminergic systems
- Bradykinin activates noradrenergic fibres in locus coeruleus pre-synaptically → reduces trigeminal nociceptive transmission
- MOR–5HT1A heterodimerization: serotonin agonists paradoxically antagonize morphine antinociception

### Anti-inflammatory cytokines
- Opioids polarize macrophages toward M2 → IL-10, TGF-β, IL-4, IL-13 production
- Opioids inhibit NF-κB and MAPK → reduce TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, iNOS
- IL-10 restricts neurogenic inflammation; IL-10/TGF-β–opioid interaction promotes pulp mineralization as defense

### Opioid receptor mechanism of action
- Gαi/o-coupled MOR/DOR/KOR → inhibit adenylyl cyclase → ↓cAMP → GIRK K⁺ channel opening → hyperpolarization
- Also modulate Nav channels, inhibit PKA → reduce p38/ERK → ↓proinflammatory cytokines
- Heterodimerization: MOR–NK1 (SP promotes MOR internalization → reduced efficacy in chronic inflammation); MOR–DOR (allosteric enhancement of ligand binding)

## 5. Limitations
- Narrative/comprehensive review design — no meta-analytic pooling of effect sizes
- Heterogeneous animal and human studies; varied force magnitudes, tooth types, and measurement methods
- Limited direct evidence for cannabinoid system at pulp level; mostly extrapolated from central pain research
- Anti-inflammatory cytokines section based on only 1 directly qualifying study
- No established consensus on optimal orthodontic force thresholds in the literature
- Complex receptor heterodimerization mechanisms remain incompletely understood in the pulp context

## 7. Glossary
- **SP (Substance P)**: pro-inflammatory neuropeptide released by Aδ/C fibres; promotes vasodilation, plasma extravasation, immune cell recruitment
- **CGRP**: Calcitonin gene-related peptide; vasodilatory neuropeptide co-released with SP during neurogenic inflammation
- **NKA (Neurokinin A)**: tachykinin neuropeptide with pro-inflammatory actions similar to SP
- **MOR**: μ-opioid receptor; primary receptor for β-endorphin and endomorphins; Gαi/o-coupled
- **DOR**: δ-opioid receptor; primary receptor for enkephalins
- **KOR**: κ-opioid receptor; primary receptor for dynorphins
- **SST (Somatostatin)**: inhibitory neuropeptide; suppresses SP/CGRP release and nociceptor sensitization
- **β-endorphin**: endogenous opioid peptide derived from POMC; antinociceptive; produced by pulp immune cells
- **Methionine-enkephalin (Met-enkephalin)**: short-lived endogenous DOR agonist; rapidly upregulated after orthodontic stimulus
- **Dynorphin**: KOR agonist; complex excitatory/inhibitory effects depending on receptor and context
- **Endocannabinoids**: anandamide, 2-AG; CB1/CB2 agonists with peripheral and central antinociceptive properties
- **GIRK**: G-protein–coupled inward-rectifier K⁺ channels; mediate opioid-induced hyperpolarization
- **Neurogenic inflammation**: inflammation initiated and maintained by sensory nerve activity and neuropeptide release (without microbial trigger)
- **Heterodimerization**: functional coupling of two different receptor types altering each other's pharmacology
- **POMC**: proopiomelanocortin; precursor for β-endorphin and ACTH
- **PENK**: proenkephalin; precursor for enkephalins
- **PDYN**: prodynorphin; precursor for dynorphins
