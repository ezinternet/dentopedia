## Why Ingested

측방 상악동거상술의 이식재 비교 근거는 [[sinus-lift/lateral/trimmel-2021-relative-performance-various-biomaterials]](베이지안 네트워크 메타분석)와 [[sinus-lift/lateral/del-fabbro-2025-sinus-augmentation-materials-lateral-approach]](생존율 중심 SR)로 이미 채워져 있으나, 두 페이지 모두 "얼마나 높이 이식했는가"라는 술자 조절 변수 — 잔존골높이 (Residual Bone Height, RBH)와 수직 이식량 (Vertical Graft Dimension) — 가 신생골 형성 (New Bone Formation, NBF)과 이식재 수축 (Graft Shrinkage)을 좌우하는지를 다루지 않았다. 본 SR(Pesce 2021, RCT 21편)은 그 축을 회귀분석으로 정량화해, 이식재 선택표 위에 "이식량 자체가 결과 변수"라는 층을 얹는다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD42020199261; PubMed/MEDLINE + Scopus + CENTRAL, April 2020–January 2021, no language/year limit) of 21 RCTs — 412 patients and 533 maxillary sinuses (319 for the NBF question, 301/302 for the shrinkage question) — testing whether residual bone height and vertical graft size drive new bone formation and graft shrinkage after lateral sinus augmentation at ≥6 months.

Graft volume reduction at 6 months was lowest for xenograft (XG 7.30 ± 15.49%) and highest for autogenous bone (AU 41.71 ± 12.63%), with alloplast 27.82 ± 15.58%, allograft 30.23 ± 1.61% and AU+AP 26.68 ± 11.03% (only AU vs XG, p=0.009, and AG vs XG, p=0.048, significant); NBF was unrelated to RBH (slope +2.11%/mm, 95% CI −2.19 to 6.43, R²=0.04, p=0.32) but fell significantly with each millimetre of postoperative vertical height (slope −1.56%/mm, 95% CI −2.50 to −0.61, R²=0.33, p=0.0023).

Only 3 of 21 RCTs were at overall low risk of bias, no meta-analysis was possible (≤2 studies per material comparison), and the volumetric data came from 11 heterogeneous CBCT studies — so the "graft less, form more bone" message is a directional regression signal, not a prescriptive threshold.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Shifts the sinus-graft question from *which material* to *how much material*: quantifies the regression of NBF on residual bone height (non-significant) and on postoperative vertical graft dimension (significant), making graft height an explicitly operator-controllable outcome determinant.
- Gives material-specific 6-month volumetric contraction figures from 11 CBCT RCTs — XG 7.30 ± 15.49%, AU 41.71 ± 12.63%, AP 27.82 ± 15.58%, AG 30.23 ± 1.61%, AU+AP 26.68 ± 11.03% — allowing a direct shrinkage ranking where no meta-analysis could be pooled.
- Explicitly reports that no meta-analysis was possible (never more than two studies per material comparison) and that only 3 of 21 RCTs were at low risk of bias — an unusually candid precision statement for a sinus-lift review.
- Reconciles its RBH null result with the histological mechanism literature (bone forms from the peripheral bony walls centripetally; NBF falls with distance from the residual floor, citing Beck et al.), and with prior biopsy studies reporting RBH-independence.
- Counters the "over-augment to compensate for shrinkage" habit (Kühl et al.) by suggesting that excessive vertical augmentation itself lowers NBF at second-stage surgery.
- Reports a low implant-failure count — 10 of 620 implants — with 3 in AG, 3 in AP and 3 in XG sinuses, plus one unspecified failure excluded from the group breakdown.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: systematic review (PRISMA), PROSPERO CRD42020199261. Meta-analysis *planned* (trigger = ≥3 studies per comparison) but **not performed** because no material comparison had more than two studies.
- **PICO 1**: atrophic posterior maxilla requiring lateral sinus augmentation; any biomaterial vs another material or vs augmentation without material; outcomes = new bone formation, implant survival, complications. **PICO 2**: same population; outcome = CBCT-measured graft volumetric change at ≥6 months.
- **Eligibility**: RCTs only (split-mouth and parallel); ≥1 follow-up at 6 months post-surgery; ≥5 patients treated; English. Excluded: non-randomized studies, case series/series pilots, retrospective/cohort designs, reviews, animal/in vitro, duplicate data, trans-sinus implants, short-vs-long implant comparisons.
- **Search window**: April 2020 – January 2021; no year or language exclusion applied at search stage (English required at eligibility).
- **Selection**: two reviewers (G.G., L.M.) independently; disagreements to a third (P.P.).
- **Data extraction**: two authors (L.M., G.G.) into a predefined Excel sheet — study design, country, sponsorship, operators, smoking status, patients/sinuses, implant number and timing, follow-up, graft material class, membrane use, failures, complications, RBH, volumetric gain, histomorphometry. Graft classes were coded as AU, XG, AG, AP, autologous tissue graft (ATG), biological material (BIO), spontaneous healing (SH).
- **Material-defined subgroups**: linear bone gain = postoperative minus preoperative measurement (mm ± SD); volumetric contraction = 6-month volume vs immediate post-surgery volume (mm³ and %).
- **Statistics**: GraphPad Prism 5.1. Simple linear regression of NBF% on mean RBH and on postoperative bone height (PBH = RBH + vertical graft dimension); material-specific regressions attempted only for AP (n=6) and XG (n=11) because of small subgroup sizes; unpaired t-test for inter-material shrinkage comparisons.
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane Handbook domains (sequence generation, allocation concealment, performance, detection, attrition, reporting), rated low / unclear / high.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No meta-analysis**: the pre-specified trigger (≥3 studies per material comparison) was never met, so every shrinkage number is a descriptive mean of study-level percentages rather than a pooled estimate; the wide SDs (e.g. XG 7.30 ± 15.49%) indicate the mean is unstable.
- **Few low-RoB trials**: 3 of 21 overall low risk; allocation concealment unclear/high in 16 of 21 — a domain that matters most for split-mouth histomorphometry.
- **Unit-of-analysis and correlation caveats**: the regressions use *study-level mean* NBF% against *study-level mean* RBH/PBH, not patient-level data; ecological regression cannot establish an individual-level relationship, and no adjustment for material, healing time or sinus morphology was made in the overall model.
- **Heterogeneous biopsy protocols**: biopsy site, healing period (6 to 9 months) and histomorphometric parameters varied; distance from the residual floor was not standardised.
- **Sinus morphology is uncontrolled**: the authors name sinus dimensions as a key confounder (citing Stacchi et al.) that could independently change NBF%.
- **Volumetric method heterogeneity**: CBCT segmentation thresholds and timing of the baseline scan differed across the 11 studies; only 5 material groups were compared, composites were left unpooled.
- **Smoking status mixed** (10 of 21 studies included smokers) with no stratified analysis.
- **Data gaps**: authors of 10 studies were contacted for missing data, only 3 replied — extraction therefore depends on reported values, and some outcomes (implant timing, membrane use, implant counts) were not reported in a substantial minority.
- **Authors' own call**: prospective RCTs are needed that deliberately vary vertical graft height while holding material constant, and that relate graft volume to histomorphometric outcome, since current literature "lacks RCTs, reviews, and meta-analyses assessing the correlation between vertical bone gain and histomorphometric outcomes."

## 6. Related Work

- **Trimmel et al. 2021** (Bayesian network meta-analysis, 34 RCTs): ranks 28 biomaterial subgroups by histomorphometric NBF%; confirms no single material dominates and autogenous bone is not clearly the gold standard at 5–8 months. Pesce does not re-rank materials but adds the dimensional axis (RBH, graft height) that the NMA cannot express.
- **Del Fabbro et al. 2025** (SR, 49 RCTs, 1,722 augmentations): implant survival 84.6–100% with no material-class difference — the endpoint Pesce only reports descriptively (10/620 failures).
- **Starch-Jensen et al.** (meta-analysis of autogenous graft resorption) and **Shanbhag et al.** (SR) are cited by Pesce as externally confirming its autogenous-bone resorption figure, and Shanbhag additionally reports that graft volume reduction does not affect two-stage implant survival.
- **Mazzocco et al.** (XG ~7% at 8 months delayed placement; ~13% simultaneous), **Gutekin et al.** (DBBM 8.14 ± 3.76% at 6 months) — close to Pesce's XG 7.30%; whereas **Salem et al.** (23.8 ± 15.9%) and **Zhang et al.** (22.7%) report substantially higher bovine xenograft resorption, a discrepancy Pesce does not resolve.
- **Beck et al.** (histological thin-ground sections): NBF decreases with distance from the residual sinus floor, with fibrous-encapsulated residual graft in the apical region — the mechanistic explanation Pesce adopts for its negative graft-height regression.
- **Pignaton et al.**: RBH and sinus width did not influence NBF at 8 months with anorganic bovine bone — concordant with Pesce's RBH null.
- **Kühl et al.**: advocated over-augmentation in two-stage lifts to pre-compensate shrinkage; Pesce's regression implies the opposite trade-off (more height, less relative NBF), which the authors frame as a hypothesis requiring prospective testing.

## 7. Glossary

- **RBH (Residual Bone Height)**: preoperative vertical bone height of the native sinus floor, measured on CT/CBCT; the primary anatomical constraint for lateral versus transcrestal approach.
- **PBH (Postoperative Bone Height)**: RBH plus the vertical dimension of the graft placed, i.e. total augmented height measured after surgery.
- **NBF (New Bone Formation)**: histomorphometric percentage of the biopsy area occupied by newly formed vital bone.
- **Graft shrinkage / volumetric contraction**: percentage loss of graft volume at ≥6 months relative to the immediate postoperative CBCT volume.
- **AU / AG / XG / AP**: autogenous bone / allograft (FDBA, DFDBA) / xenograft (e.g. DBBM) / alloplast (synthetic: HA, β-TCP, BCP, bioactive glass, polymers).
- **BIC (Bone-to-Implant Contact)**: fraction of the implant surface in direct contact with bone; cited as the histological link between NBF% and implant survival.
- **RoB (Risk of Bias)**: Cochrane domain-based quality rating (low / unclear / high) applied to each included RCT.
- **PRISMA / PROSPERO**: reporting guideline for systematic reviews / prospective international register of systematic-review protocols.
