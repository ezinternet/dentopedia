## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 37 CBCT studies (34 in meta-analysis) characterising the PSAA in the lateral sinus wall across 11,402+ sinuses from multiple populations.
Mean diameter 1.06 mm (55% < 1 mm, only 2% > 2 mm); detection rate 79%; location 64% intraosseous; mean distance from sinus floor 8.85 mm, from alveolar crest 16.71 mm.
Severe hemorrhage from PSAA injury during sinus augmentation is not a substantial clinical problem; only the 2% of arteries exceeding 2 mm diameter pose a risk of excessive (non–life-threatening) bleeding.

## 세줄요약

후상치조동맥 (Posterior Superior Alveolar Artery, PSAA)을 측방 상악동 측벽에서 평가한 콘빔전산화단층영상 (Cone Beam Computed Tomography, CBCT) 기반 체계적 고찰·메타분석 (37편, 11,402건+ 상악동).
평균 직경 1.06 mm (55%가 <1 mm, >2 mm는 2%뿐); 검출률 79%; 위치: 골내형 64%; 동저 거리 8.85 mm, 치조정 거리 16.71 mm; 남성에서 직경·거리 모두 유의하게 큼.
상악동거상술 (Sinus Floor Augmentation, SFA) 중 PSAA 손상으로 인한 중증 출혈은 실질적 임상 문제가 아님 — 반응성 혈관수축과 소구경이 출혈량을 제한; 직경 >2 mm 혈관(2%)만이 과다출혈 위험.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First CBCT-based SR+MA of PSAA since Varela-Centelles 2015, incorporating 8 years of new data.
- Establishes that only 2% of PSAAs (diameter > 2 mm) risk serious hemorrhage — the rest are manageable.
- Quantifies sex difference: males have higher detection rate (OR 1.34) and larger artery diameter (+0.14 mm).
- Integrates distance data: window < 8 mm from sinus floor, < 15 mm from alveolar crest is the safety envelope.
- Explicitly concludes: "life-threatening hemorrhage from PSAA injury is not a genuine clinical concern."

## 3. Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA)
- **Databases**: Web of Science, MEDLINE, Scopus, Embase; gray literature; searched to May 2022
- **n**: 37 studies included; 34 in meta-analysis
- **Quality**: 13 high / 22 moderate / 2 low (5-item binary scale)
- **Statistical**: Random-effects for heterogeneous outcomes; CMA v2.2.064; meta-regression for sex

## 4. Key Results

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Detection rate (31 studies, 11,402 sinuses) | **79%** (72%–84%) |
| Male vs female detection OR | **1.34** (1.17–1.55) |
| Mean diameter (16 studies, 3,757 sinuses) | **1.06 ± 0.05 mm** (0.96–1.16 mm) |
| Diameter < 1 mm | **55%** (42%–68%) |
| Diameter 1–2 mm | **41%** (31%–53%) |
| Diameter > 2 mm | **2%** (1%–5%) |
| Location: intraosseous | **64%** (57%–69%) |
| Location: intrasinus/submucosal | **30%** (24%–37%) |
| Location: superficial | **5%** (3%–7%) |
| Distance from alveolar crest (17 studies) | **16.71 ± 0.49 mm** (15.75–17.68 mm) |
| Distance from sinus floor (9 studies) | **8.85 ± 0.40 mm** (8.06–9.65 mm) |

**Surgical safety rules derived:**
- Lateral window superior border < 15 mm from alveolar crest (mean PSAA at 16.71 mm).
- Lateral window height < 8 mm from sinus floor (mean PSAA at 8.85 mm from floor).
- Edentulous patients have shorter crest-to-PSAA distance (alveolar resorption).

## 5. Bleeding Management Implications

The paper does not study hemostatic techniques (electrocautery, compression, packing) directly. The implication of the anatomy data is:
- 55% of PSAAs are < 1 mm — these bleed trivially and are stopped by vasoconstriction or gauze pressure.
- 41% are 1–2 mm — manageable with standard techniques.
- 2% are > 2 mm — risk excessive (non-life-threatening) bleeding; pre-planned management needed.
- No paper in this SR+MA compared electrocautery vs other methods.

**What the CBCT data supports for C5**: "Electrocautery can control PSAA bleeding" is a plausible claim for small arteries, but no held evidence directly tests it. The seminar claim that electrocautery is THE method is an unsupported specific recommendation on top of a generally correct observation (most PSAA bleeds are manageable).

## 6. Limitations

- All included studies cross-sectional (no intervention studies).
- High heterogeneity in all meta-analysed outcomes.
- Potential for CBCT-based underestimation of diameter < 0.5 mm arteries.
- Cadaveric vs CBCT comparison still inconclusive.

## 8. Glossary

- **IO (intraosseous)**: PSAA runs inside the lateral osseous wall — highest injury risk during osteotomy.
- **IS (intrasinus/submucosal)**: PSAA runs between the sinus membrane and bone — injury risk during membrane detachment.
- **SF (superficial/extraosseous)**: PSAA on the outer cortex — lowest intraoperative risk.
- **Detection rate**: probability of CBCT visualising the PSAA; true prevalence is 100% (cadaveric confirmation).
