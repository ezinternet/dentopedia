## Why Ingested

Existing clear-aligner biomechanics/predictability pages such as [[wiki/orthodontics/clear-aligner/nakornnoi-2024-aligner-trimline-biomechanics-tooth-movement-sr]] and [[wiki/orthodontics/clear-aligner/inchingolo-2023-clear-aligner-distalization-predictability-sr]] synthesize predictability data mostly at the level of trimline design and distalization movements across aligner systems generically. This paper adds planned-vs-achieved predictability data specifically for the **Invisalign Lite** product line (a limited-movement, lower-aligner-count Invisalign variant), covering overjet, overbite, arch depth, and incisor labiolingual inclination — movement types not centrally addressed in those pages. It is a useful data point for setting realistic patient expectations with the Lite protocol specifically, rather than full Invisalign or generic CAT systems.

## One-line Summary

Retrospective analysis of 122 adult Invisalign Lite patients found planned overjet, arch depth, and mandibular incisor inclination closely matched achieved outcomes, but overbite and maxillary incisor labiolingual inclination showed statistically and clinically significant planned-vs-achieved discrepancies (abstract-only — full text not retrieved).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantifies planned-vs-achieved accuracy specifically for the **Invisalign Lite** appliance (a lower-aligner-count, limited-movement product variant), which is less studied than standard Invisalign in the predictability literature.
- Uses Bland-Altman plots to characterize agreement, including detection of a **proportional bias** for overbite correction (larger initial overbite → larger discrepancy).
- Separately reports maxillary vs mandibular incisor labiolingual inclination accuracy, finding an asymmetry: mandibular incisors tracked planned values closely, while maxillary incisors consistently under-achieved (more lingually inclined / less proclined than planned).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

*Note: This section is reconstructed entirely from the abstract; the full Materials and Methods section was not retrieved.*

- Retrospective data pulled from the Align Technology treatment-planning application (ClinCheck-type planning data) for adult patients meeting inclusion criteria on overjet and overbite.
- Metrology software used to measure pretreatment, planned, and achieved arch depth and labiolingual inclination of maxillary and mandibular central incisors.
- Outcome comparisons: planned vs achieved overjet, overbite, arch depth (across subgroups), and incisor labiolingual inclination (maxillary and mandibular separately).
- Agreement analysis via Bland-Altman plots (mean difference ± SD, limits of agreement, visual inspection for proportional bias).
- A clinically acceptable interval of ±0.5 mm was used as the threshold for overjet.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

*All figures below are as reported in the abstract; no additional methodological detail (e.g., statistical test names beyond what's stated, full subgroup breakdowns) is available.*

- **Sample**: 122 patients met selection criteria; 95 (79.5%) were female.
- **Overjet**: mean planned-achieved difference +0.183 mm (SD 0.557); did not exceed the ±0.5 mm clinically acceptable interval.
- **Overbite**: mean difference +0.746 mm (SD 0.857); Bland-Altman plot suggested a **proportional bias** — larger discrepancies with larger initial overbite.
- **Arch depth**: differences remained within an acceptable range across all subgroups; no statistically significant differences (p > .05) between planned and achieved.
- **Mandibular incisor labiolingual inclination**: achieved closely matched planned, mean difference -1.088° (SD 2.78°).
- **Maxillary incisor labiolingual inclination**: mean difference -2.905° (SD 2.671°) — a consistent tendency for achieved values to be lower (less proclined/more lingually positioned) than planned.
- **Conclusion (as stated)**: overjet, arch depth, and mandibular incisor labiolingual inclination outcomes closely matched planned values; overbite and maxillary incisor labiolingual inclination showed statistically and clinically significant planned-vs-achieved differences.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only ingestion**: full text (Methods detail, statistical tests, subgroup tables, discussion of mechanism, limitations as stated by authors) was not retrieved. This source should be revisited if/when full text becomes available.
- Single appliance system studied (Invisalign Lite) — findings may not generalize to standard Invisalign or other clear aligner systems (see [[wiki/orthodontics/clear-aligner/inchingolo-2023-clear-aligner-distalization-predictability-sr]] for broader system-level distalization predictability).
- Retrospective design drawn from treatment-planning software data — subject to selection bias (only patients meeting overjet/overbite inclusion criteria included) and no control for aligner wear compliance, a known confound in achieved-vs-planned discrepancy literature.
- Mechanism behind the overbite proportional bias and the maxillary-specific inclination under-achievement is not explained in the abstract; likely candidates (biomechanical limits of aligner intrusion/extrusion for overbite, insufficient attachment design for maxillary incisor torque) are not discussed here — would need full text or related aligner biomechanics literature (e.g. [[wiki/orthodontics/clear-aligner/nakornnoi-2024-aligner-trimline-biomechanics-tooth-movement-sr]]) for mechanistic interpretation.

## 7. Glossary

- **Invisalign Lite**: An Invisalign product variant using a reduced number of aligners, marketed for mild-to-moderate malocclusion correction with limited planned tooth movement per stage.
- **Overjet**: Horizontal (anteroposterior) overlap of the maxillary incisors over the mandibular incisors.
- **Overbite**: Vertical overlap of the maxillary incisors over the mandibular incisors.
- **Arch depth**: A measure of anteroposterior dental arch dimension, typically referenced from incisor position to a posterior landmark.
- **Labiolingual inclination**: The buccolingual/labiolingual tip angulation of an incisor relative to its long axis or a reference plane.
- **Bland-Altman plot**: A statistical method for assessing agreement between two measurement methods (here, planned vs achieved) by plotting the difference against the mean, used to detect bias including proportional bias.
