## Summary

This article is a narrative summary of the Cochrane systematic review CD004624 (Bailey et al. 2013), published in the British Dental Journal as a Verifiable CPD Paper. It presents the same evidence base — 7 double-blind RCTs involving 2241 participants — comparing single-dose oral analgesics for pain relief after surgical removal of impacted mandibular third molars. The BDJ summary distills the Cochrane findings for a general dental audience, highlighting ibuprofen 400 mg as superior to paracetamol 1000 mg and the combination product (ibuprofen 200 mg + paracetamol 400 mg, Nuromol) as providing the greatest pain relief. Evidence quality was rated as high across the primary comparisons.

## Key Findings

- **Ibuprofen 400 mg vs paracetamol 1000 mg**: Ibuprofen was significantly more effective — 47% more patients experienced at least 50% pain relief (RR 1.47, 95% CI 1.28–1.69; NNT = 4).
- **Combination (ibuprofen 200 mg + paracetamol 400 mg) vs paracetamol 1000 mg**: The combination product (marketed as Nuromol) was 77% more effective (RR 1.77, 95% CI 1.32–2.39; NNT = 3).
- **Aspirin 600 mg or 900 mg vs paracetamol 1000 mg**: No significant difference (RR 1.15, 95% CI 0.93–1.42).
- **Codeine 60 mg + paracetamol 600 mg vs paracetamol 600 mg**: Non-significant trend (RR 1.24, 95% CI 0.97–1.60).
- **Single doses were effective**: For most analgesics, approximately 1 in 4 patients experienced at least 50% pain relief over 6 hours (placebo response was much lower at ~1 in 10).
- Evidence base: 7 studies, 2241 participants, all double-blind RCTs. High quality evidence.

## Clinical Relevance

The BDJ summary emphasizes that ibuprofen 400 mg is the recommended first-line analgesic for post-third-molar extraction pain based on superior efficacy, tolerability, and affordability. The combination tablet offers the highest efficacy but at greater cost. Paracetamol alone is less effective than ibuprofen.
