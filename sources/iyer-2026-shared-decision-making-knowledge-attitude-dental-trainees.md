## Why Ingested

[[overviews/treatment-planning-decision-variability-overview]] ends by arguing that because the profession's own recommendation varies ~3× by training, "recommend, then discuss" negotiates from an unstable anchor — which makes structured shared decision-making (SDM) more necessary, not less. This paper supplies the piece that overview names as missing: whether the next generation of clinicians is equipped or willing to practice it. It extends [[behavioral-dentistry/communication-relationship/elwyn-2025-shared-decision-making-primer-clinicians]]'s clinician-facing "principle-to-practice gap" down into dental education, showing the gap is present — and structurally worse — before practice even begins.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional, self-administered questionnaire study (n=176: 3rd-year students/D3 46, 4th-year students/D4 57, interns 38, residents 35; mean age 24.17 ± 2.13) at a single dental college in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia (Aug 2024–Feb 2025), assessing dental trainees' knowledge and attitude toward shared decision-making (SDM).

Knowledge of SDM was "less than satisfactory" across all groups; 6 of 7 attitude items differed significantly by educational level, with the least-experienced (D3) students far more likely to agree patients should trust clinicians to decide unilaterally (OR 13.00, p=0.01, CI 2.21–74.30) and that SDM threatens their clinical expertise (OR 4.20, p=0.05, CI 1.98–17.90); one reported D4 estimate (OR 1.17, p=0.02, CI 1.17–14.50) has a point estimate identical to its own confidence-interval lower bound — an internal inconsistency present in the source's own Table 4, reproduced here as printed rather than silently corrected.

Sample size is modest and split four ways (smallest cell D3 n=46), single-institution/single-city, self-reported attitudes rather than observed consultations, and most confidence intervals are very wide — treat point estimates as directional, not precise.

지식은 전 집단에서 "불충분(less than satisfactory)" 수준이었으나 태도 문항 7개 중 6개가 교육 수준에 따라 유의하게 갈렸다. 경험이 가장 적은 D3 학생은 "환자는 임상의가 대신 모든 결정을 내리도록 믿어야 한다"에 동의할 오즈비(Odds Ratio, OR)가 13.00(p=0.01, CI 2.21–74.30), "SDM이 자신의 임상 전문성을 의심받게 만든다"에 동의할 오즈비가 4.20(p=0.05, CI 1.98–17.90)으로 유의하게 높았다. 보고된 D4 수치 하나(OR 1.17, p=0.02, CI 1.17–14.50)는 점추정치가 신뢰구간(Confidence Interval, CI) 하한과 동일한 원문 자체의 내부 불일치로, 임의 교정 없이 원문 그대로 반영·표기함.

표본은 4개 군으로 쪼개져 다소 작고(최소 셀 D3 n=46), 단일 기관·단일 도시이며, 실제 진료 관찰이 아닌 자기보고 태도이고, 대부분의 신뢰구간이 매우 넓어 점추정치를 정밀값이 아니라 방향성으로만 해석해야 한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study of its kind in the region (per the authors) assessing SDM knowledge and attitude specifically among dental undergraduates, interns, and residents — prior regional SDM work (e.g. [[behavioral-dentistry/communication-relationship/asaad-2019-shared-decision-making-sdm-dentistry]]) addresses practicing clinicians or the field generally, not the training pipeline.
- Separates *knowledge* (broadly poor across all levels, largely insensitive to training stage) from *attitude* (sharply stratified by training stage) — the paper's central finding is that the two behave differently and only one improves with experience.
- Uses multinomial regression against a residents reference category to isolate which specific attitude beliefs — not just an overall "favorability" score — drive the gap: distrust of patient involvement, perceived threat to expertise, and time burden.
- Validated instrument: content validity (Lynn's S-UA = 0.86) and test-retest reliability (ICC = 0.82) reported for the adapted questionnaire, with a 9-respondent (5%) pilot excluded from final analysis.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Descriptive cross-sectional study, STROBE-guided, single site (College of Dentistry, King Saud bin Abdulaziz University for Health Sciences, Riyadh).
- **Sampling**: Nonprobability, purposive sampling of all senior dental students in the D3 and D4 undergraduate years, plus interns and residents (i.e., four training-level strata, not just "senior students and residents" as the title's short form suggests — see Sample composition note below). Sample size estimated via G*Power (v3.1.9.4) from a prior-literature proportion of 27%, 95% CI, 90% power.
- **Instrument**: Questionnaire adapted (minor demographic modifications) from two prior published instruments; distributed via Google Forms with weekly email reminders. Two components of 7 items each: knowledge (yes/no/not sure) and attitude (5-point Likert, strongly disagree → strongly agree).
- **Statistics**: Descriptive stats (frequency/percentage); Pearson's chi-square to screen for significant association between knowledge/attitude items and exploratory variables (gender, GPA, education level); items significant at chi-square (p<0.05) carried forward into multinomial regression, with **residents** as the reference category (highest educational level) and **strongly disagree** as the reference response category. SPSS v20.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Sample**: n=176; D3 46 (26.14%), D4 57 (32.39%), interns 38 (21.59%), residents 35 (19.88%); female 99 (56.25%), male 77 (43.75%); GPA 4–5 in 164 (93.18%) — GPA variability in the sample is therefore very limited.
- **Knowledge**: assessed as "less than satisfactory" overall. Only 2 of 7 knowledge items showed significant association with an exploratory variable, both weak: GPA with "majority of patients do not want to engage in SDM" (χ², p=0.04) and education level with "SDM interventions have a variable effect on the treatment option" (χ², p=0.04). No significant gender effect on any knowledge item.
- **Attitude**: 6 of 7 items significant by education level at chi-square screening (p=0.01–0.03); no significant gender or GPA effect on attitude items. Multinomial regression (reference = residents) findings, as printed in Table 4:
  - "SDM and informed consent mean the same thing" — D4 less likely to disagree than residents (OR 0.30, p=0.04, CI 0.09–0.96); interns similarly (OR 0.23, p=0.01, CI 0.07–0.75).
  - "Patients should trust clinicians to make all decisions on their behalf" — D3 more likely to feel neutral vs. residents (OR 13.00, p=0.01, CI 2.21–74.30); D4 more likely to feel neutral (OR 25.6, p=0.01, CI 4.50–145.4) **and** more likely to somewhat agree (OR 1.17, p=0.02, CI 1.17–14.50).
  - "SDM may cause patients to question my clinical expertise" — D3 more likely to somewhat agree (OR 4.20, p=0.05, CI 1.98–17.90); D4 more likely to feel neutral (OR 6.37, p=0.01, CI 1.42–28.60).
  - "SDM is challenging because patients ask me to decide for them" — D3 more likely to strongly agree (OR 19.50, p=0.01, CI 2.19–173.40).
  - "SDM is unrealistic because it takes too much time" — D3 more likely to strongly agree (OR 6.40, p=0.02, CI 1.20–34.01).
- **⚠ Source-data anomaly (reproduced, not corrected)**: the "somewhat agree" D4 row for "patients should trust clinicians to make all decisions on their behalf" is printed in the source's Table 4 as **OR 1.17, 95% CI 1.17–14.50** — the point estimate is numerically identical to its own lower confidence bound, which is not a mathematically normal regression output (for OR=1.17 with a lower bound at 1.17, the estimate would sit at the very edge of its own interval). This exact figure also appears in the abstract verbatim, so it is not a one-off typesetting slip local to the table — it is the number the authors reported. Most plausibly a mistyped B-coefficient-to-OR conversion or a transposed digit (β=1.42 is given alongside it, and exp(1.42)=4.14, not 1.17), but the source gives no erratum. Reproduced here exactly as printed; logged as a `source-data-issue` deviation. Do not treat 1.17 as a precise, load-bearing estimate.
- **Wide CIs generally**: several attitude ORs carry CIs spanning an order of magnitude or more (e.g. 2.21–74.30, 2.19–173.40, 4.50–145.4). With n=176 split across four educational strata (smallest D3 n=46), these are imprecise estimates of direction and rough magnitude, not precise effect sizes.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Single institution, single city**: all 176 respondents from one dental college in Riyadh; the authors' own framing ("first of its kind in the region") signals this is a starting data point, not a regional estimate.
- **Nonprobability/purposive sampling**: authors note this yields a smaller, less representative sample frame than random sampling would.
- **Self-reported attitudes, not observed behavior**: the instrument measures what trainees *say* they believe about SDM, not how they behave with an actual patient in an actual consultation — nobody watched these trainees deliver care.
- **Limited GPA variability**: 93.18% of respondents had GPA 4–5, constraining the power of GPA-based comparisons regardless of significance found.
- **Recall/information bias risk**: authors note SDM is not yet part of the curriculum, so responses may reflect informal exposure rather than structured learning.
- **Wide confidence intervals** throughout the attitude regression results (see above), a direct consequence of small per-stratum n.
- **Source-data anomaly**: the D4 "somewhat agree" OR for the "patients should trust clinicians" item (OR 1.17, CI 1.17–14.50) is internally inconsistent as printed (see Results); flagged rather than corrected, `source-data-issue` deviation logged.
- **Sample-composition nuance**: the title's shorthand "senior students and residents" covers four distinct strata (D3, D4, interns, residents) per the Methods, not two — worth stating precisely rather than repeating the title's compression.
- **Future work** (per authors): extend data collection regionally (Middle East/Eastern Mediterranean) rather than single-institution; integrate SDM formally into dental curricula and re-assess.

## 7. Glossary

- **SDM (Shared Decision-Making, 공유의사결정)**: collaborative process in which clinician and patient jointly reach a treatment decision, integrating best evidence with the patient's values and preferences.
- **D3 / D4**: 3rd-year / 4th-year undergraduate dental students (Saudi dental curriculum), i.e. the two most junior clinically-active cohorts in this sample.
- **Intern / Resident**: post-undergraduate training stages, more clinically experienced than D3/D4; residents served as the regression's reference category.
- **OR (Odds Ratio, 오즈비)**: multinomial-regression-derived odds of a given response category (e.g. "somewhat agree") vs. the reference response ("strongly disagree"), for a given education-level group vs. the reference group (residents).
- **GPA (Grade Point Average)**: cumulative academic performance measure; treated here as an exploratory variable against knowledge/attitude responses.
- **S-UA (Scale-level Universal Agreement)**: content-validity index reflecting the proportion of expert raters agreeing an item is relevant; 0.86 here is described as optimal.
- **ICC (Intraclass Correlation Coefficient)**: test-retest reliability statistic; 0.82 here indicates good reliability of the adapted questionnaire.
