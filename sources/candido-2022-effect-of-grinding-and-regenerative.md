## Why Ingested

The 2026-11-12 `zirconia-occlusal-adjustment` topic already holds the "grinding creates monoclinic phase, heat treatment reverses it" strand ([[dental-materials/zirconia/ramos-2016-grinding-heat-treatment-zirconia-flexural]], [[dental-materials/zirconia/kosmac-1999-grinding-sandblasting-flexural-strength-zirconia]]) and the "which finishing sequence after adjustment" strand ([[dental-materials/zirconia/mohammadi-bassir-2017-grinding-overglazing-polishing-zirconia]], [[dental-materials/zirconia/sarabi-2024-grinding-ltd-flexural-strength-zirconia]]). What was missing is the downstream cost of the regenerative heat treatment itself: this paper asks whether the 900 °C/1000 °C firing used to restore the tetragonal phase damages the zirconia–porcelain interface of a *veneered* (bilayer) crown — the axis that decides whether "regenerative HT after adjustment" is a safe chairside/lab add-on or only acceptable for monolithic restorations.

→ [[dental-materials/zirconia/candido-2022-effect-of-grinding-and-regenerative]]

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro interface-fracture study (J Adhes Dent 2022; bar-shaped Lava Frame Y-TZP, 6 groups × 45 specimens, n=13 bilayer specimens per group for four-point bending) testing 0.3 mm diamond-stone grinding (20,000 rpm, no irrigation) followed by regenerative heat treatment at 900 °C/60 min or 1000 °C/30 min.

Grinding raised the monoclinic fraction from 1.2 to 2.6 vol% and Ra from 0.17 to 1.16 µm (p<0.005), while both heat treatments drove monoclinic to 0.0 vol% and left Ra unchanged (p=0.22); the strain energy release rate of the zirconia/porcelain interface was statistically equal in all six groups (6.8/6.6/7.6 J/m² for C/C900/C1000 vs 8.3/8.0/5.7 J/m² for G/G900/G1000, p>0.05).

Both heat-treatment protocols fully restored the tetragonal phase without weakening veneer adhesion, so regenerative HT is compatible with veneered zirconia — but Ra stayed ~1.0 µm and the absolute interface toughness (~6–8 J/m²) is far below metal-ceramic, and this is bench data without cyclic loading.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Directly measures the **cost of regenerative heat treatment to the interface**, not just to the bulk zirconia: the zirconia/porcelain energy release rate (G, Charalambides four-point bend method) is unchanged by either 900 °C/60 min or 1000 °C/30 min.
- Reproduces the classic grinding → t→m transformation on a **partially sintered, then fully sintered** Lava Frame Y-TZP (1.2 → 2.6 vol% monoclinic) and shows **complete reversal to 0.0 vol%** at both temperatures.
- Documents an accompanying **cubic-phase increase after grinding** (12.9 → 31.3 vol%) that the authors interpret as a stress-related rhombohedral (r) phase, reversed to 14.2/11.5 vol% after HT.
- Separates **roughness from phase state**: grinding roughens (Ra 0.17 → 1.16 µm, p<0.005) but heat treatment does *not* smooth it back (1.16 → 1.07/1.01 µm, p=0.22) — restoration of crystallography is not restoration of surface finish.
- Provides a **negative adhesion result**: neither grinding (p=0.52) nor HT (p=0.29) improved interface toughness, contradicting the claim that grinding alone improves porcelain bonding.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Material**: partially sintered Y-TZP blocks (Lava Frame, 3M Oral Care) cut into bars 25×5×1.9 mm (ground arm) and 25×5×1.5 mm (control arm); sintered per manufacturer protocol (Lava 200 furnace) to final 20×4×1.5 mm (G) and 20×4×1.2 mm (C), accounting for ~20% linear shrinkage and 0.3 mm planned substance loss.
- **Groups (n=45 each)**: C = no grinding, no HT; C900 = HT 900 °C/60 min; C1000 = HT 1000 °C/30 min; G = ground; G900; G1000.
- **Grinding**: automated device, medium-grit cylindrical diamond (MasterCeram 133-104-SDN) in an electric prosthetic micromotor at ~20,000 rpm, **without water irrigation**, 0.3 mm depth — a deliberately severe, clinically-unfavourable adjustment.
- **Heat treatment**: isothermal in a preheated laboratory ceramic oven (AluminiPress), 900 °C/60 min or 1000 °C/30 min, bench cooling.
- **Surface characterization**: XRD (Bruker D8 Advance, Cu Kα, 20–80°, 0.02° step) with **Rietveld refinement** for m/t/c volume fractions; arithmetic mean roughness Ra (Mitutoyo SJ 400, 0.01 µm resolution, 2.5 mm length, 3 readings/specimen, measured perpendicular to grinding lines); optical profilometry (Nanovea PB1000, 500×500 µm, 5 µm pitch, Gwyddion 2.48); SEM (FEI Inspect F50) for topography, crack path and fracture surface (backscattered mode for phase contrast).
- **Bilayer build-up**: low-fusing nano-fluorapatite porcelain (IPS e.max Ceram) — liner (~0.1 mm) + wash + 2 dentin layers + glaze, fired per manufacturer schedule (Programat P310), final 20.0×4.0×2.4 mm with a **1:1.9 zirconia:porcelain thickness ratio**; a 0.7 mm central notch cut in the veneer to fix crack-start length.
- **Interface test**: four-point bending (EMIC DL2000, 5 kN cell, 0.1 mm/min) to steady-state crack propagation; G computed with the Charalambides equation using E=200 GPa/ν=0.32 (zirconia) and E=70 GPa/ν=0.27 (porcelain).
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk, then two-way ANOVA + Tukey (α=0.05) on Ra, P and G (BioEstat 5.0).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Group | Monoclinic (vol%) | Tetragonal (vol%) | Cubic (vol%) | Ra (µm) | P (N) | G (J/m²) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C (control) | 1.2 | 85.9 | 12.9 | 0.17 ± 0.04 | 51.4 ± 10.9 | 6.8 ± 2.1 |
| C900 | 0.0 | 82.3 | 17.7 | 0.18 ± 0.03 | 48.6 ± 8.7 | 6.6 ± 1.4 |
| C1000 | 0.0 | 82.5 | 17.5 | 0.18 ± 0.03 | 52.2 ± 7.5 | 7.6 ± 1.3 |
| G (ground) | 2.6 | 66.1 | 31.3 | 1.16 ± 0.24 | 55.8 ± 3.8 | 8.3 ± 2.0 |
| G900 | 0.0 | 85.8 | 14.2 | 1.07 ± 0.08 | 55.0 ± 7.3 | 8.0 ± 2.7 |
| G1000 | 0.0 | 88.5 | 11.5 | 1.01 ± 0.11 | 46.0 ± 8.5 | 5.7 ± 1.7 |

- **Phase transformation**: grinding increased monoclinic content (1.2 → 2.6 vol%) and cubic/rhombohedral fraction (12.9 → 31.3 vol%); HT extinguished the monoclinic phase (0.0 vol%) in both C and G arms and returned tetragonal fraction to ~82–88 vol%.
- **Roughness**: grinding effect significant (p<0.005); HT effect not significant (p=0.22); no interaction (p=0.15). G1000 gave the numerically lowest ground-surface Ra (1.01 µm) but with no statistical separation.
- **Topography (SEM)**: ground surfaces showed longitudinal grooves parallel to grinding direction, deformations, scale-like structures, debris and microcracks; HT produced only subtle edge rounding/chip smoothing, no groove removal.
- **Interface fracture**: interaction term significant for P (p=0.02) and G (p=0.005), but no significant main effect of grinding (P p=0.42; G p=0.52) or HT (P p=0.15; G p=0.29). All G values ~5.7–8.3 J/m².
- **Fracture mode**: mixed cohesive/adhesive — residual liner porcelain remained attached to zirconia in all groups (similar phase-contrast images), crack initiated at the notch and propagated along the interface with focal zirconia detachment.
- **Benchmark against metal-ceramic**: authors cite ~39.4 J/m² (Ni-Cr/porcelain) and ~80 J/m² (gold-treated Ti/porcelain) from the literature — zirconia/porcelain at ~6–8 J/m² is roughly 5–10× lower, i.e., intrinsically delamination-prone.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Bar-shaped specimens, not crown-shaped: geometry and porcelain thickness distribution differ from a real restoration.
- No cyclic/fatigue loading — a one-shot monotonic four-point bend cannot capture chewing fatigue or LTD-driven slow crack growth.
- No in vivo/clinical arm; no thermocycling or artificial aging before the interface test.
- Grinding used a single severe protocol (0.3 mm, 20,000 rpm, **dry**), so the results bound the worst case rather than typical fine occlusal adjustment.
- XRD only n=2 and profilometry n=2 per group; interface toughness variance is large (±2.7 J/m²), so equivalence is asserted by non-significance rather than by an equivalence margin.
- Authors call for crown-shaped specimen testing and clinical studies.

## 7. Glossary

- **Y-TZP** — yttria-stabilized tetragonal zirconia polycrystal; the dental zirconia grade tested (Lava Frame).
- **t→m transformation** — tetragonal-to-monoclinic phase transformation; stress-induced, accompanied by ~3–5% volume expansion that generates compressive stress (transformation toughening) but also microcracking.
- **Regenerative heat treatment (HT)** — isothermal firing after grinding intended to reverse m→t and restore the original crystallographic state.
- **Rietveld refinement** — full-pattern XRD fitting used here to quantify m/t/c volume fractions.
- **Ra** — arithmetic mean surface roughness (µm); measured perpendicular to grinding grooves.
- **Energy release rate (G)** — fracture-mechanics measure of interface toughness (J/m²); higher = stronger zirconia/porcelain adhesion.
- **Charalambides test** — four-point bending of a notched bilayer used to extract interfacial G from the steady-state plateau load.
- **r phase** — rhombohedral phase; a stress-deformed tetragonal variant the authors invoke to explain the cubic-fraction increase after grinding.
