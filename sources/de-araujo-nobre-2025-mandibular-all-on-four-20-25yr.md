## Why Ingested

The longest available follow-up dataset (20–25 years) for the All-on-4 mandibular protocol in the wiki. Provides the very-long-term endpoint for implant cumulative survival rate (CSR) and marginal bone loss (MBL) trajectory that complements [[wiki/implants/full-arch/shao-2026-all-on-four-vs-all-on-six-sr-ma]], which synthesizes medium-term data. Critical for answering patient questions about lifetime durability of the All-on-4 concept.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (n=181 patients, 724 implants, mean follow-up 264 months / range 240–321 months) of immediate full-arch mandibular All-on-4 rehabilitation.

Implant and prosthesis cumulative survival rates were 90% and 92% at up to 25 years; mean MBL was 1.71 mm at 20 years and 2.28 mm at 25 years; mechanical complications occurred in 70.7% of patients.

Biological complications (24.9% patient-level) and smoking are key risk factors for failure; the protocol is confirmed safe and reliable over the very long term despite high mechanical complication incidence.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Longest follow-up (20–25 years) published for the All-on-4 mandibular protocol
- Establishes very-long-term survival benchmarks: implant 90%, prosthesis 92%
- Quantifies MBL trajectory at 20 and 25 years with SD
- Identifies multivariable risk indicators for failure, MBL >3 mm, and biological/mechanical complications via Cox regression and binary logistic regression

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort, single-center (Portugal)
- **Population**: 181 patients (108F/73M), mean age 59.2 ± 11.9 years; fully edentulous mandible
- **Intervention**: All-on-4 concept — 2 axial anterior + 2 tilted (posterior) implants per patient; 724 implants total, all in immediate function
- **Primary outcomes**: Implant survival and success, prosthesis survival
- **Secondary outcomes**: MBL at 20-year and 25-year follow-up; mechanical and biological complications
- **Statistical analysis**: Kaplan-Meier for survival; Cox regression (implant failure risk indicators); binary logistic regression (MBL >3 mm at 20yr, biological and mechanical complications)
- **Follow-up**: Mean 264 months (range 240–321); 100 patients lost to follow-up; 77 at 20-yr FU, 3 at 25-yr FU

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Implant cumulative survival rate (CSR) | 90% at up to 25 years |
| Prosthesis survival | 92% at up to 25 years |
| Mean MBL at 20 years | 1.71 ± 1.98 mm |
| Mean MBL at 25 years | 2.28 ± 2.07 mm |
| Mechanical complications (patient level) | 70.7% |
| Biological complications (patient level) | 24.9% |
| Biological complications (implant level) | 11.6% |

**Risk indicators identified by multivariable analysis:**

| Outcome | Risk indicators |
|---|---|
| Implant failure (Cox regression) | Biological complications; type of restorative material |
| Mechanical complications (logistic) | Male sex |
| Biological complications (logistic) | Smoking; previous failure of a contiguous implant |

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- High attrition: 100/181 patients lost to follow-up (55.2%); only 77 at 20-yr FU, 3 at 25-yr FU — survivor bias possible
- Retrospective, single-center design
- Abstract-only source — detailed statistical tables (HR, OR, 95% CI) not available for this summary
- Restorative material types not specified in abstract — a key risk indicator remains qualitatively uncharacterized without full text

## 7. Glossary

- **All-on-4**: Full-arch implant-supported fixed prosthesis concept using 4 implants (2 axial anterior, 2 distally tilted posterior)
- **Immediate function / immediate loading**: Prosthesis connected within 48–72 hours of implant placement
- **Cumulative Survival Rate (CSR)**: Kaplan-Meier estimate of survival probability at a given time point
- **Marginal Bone Loss (MBL)**: Radiographic bone resorption at the implant-bone interface, measured from baseline
- **Biological complications**: Peri-implant infections, peri-implantitis; tissue-level failures
- **Mechanical complications**: Prosthetic fractures, screw loosening, abutment failures; hardware-level failures
