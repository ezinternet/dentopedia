## Why Ingested

This systematic review bridges the implant-iip-loading evidence gap by centering patient-reported outcomes (PROMs)—discomfort, satisfaction, esthetics—on the placement/loading timing decision, a complementary angle to clinician-measured survival/recession in [[wiki/immediate-implant/pommer-2021-maxillary-single-tooth-timing-protocols-sr-ma]] (same special issue, Clinical Oral Implants Research 2021;32(Suppl 21)). No strong evidence emerges that placement or loading timing alone drives patient satisfaction, but full-arch edentulous immediate loading shows transient patient preference over early/delayed protocols at short follow-up. Essential for framing timing protocols as trade-offs between clinical outcomes and patient experience.

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review of 35–40 randomized/prospective trials evaluating patient perception (PROMs: discomfort, satisfaction, esthetics) of immediate vs early vs conventional/delayed implant placement and loading.
No strong evidence that placement or loading timing alone influences patient discomfort, functional/esthetic satisfaction, or overall satisfaction with implant treatment; some weak evidence for transient patient preference for immediate loading in edentulous full-arch cases at short follow-up.
Timing protocols are not driven by patient preference alone; clinical outcomes (bone loss, esthetics) remain primary decision drivers; long-term PROM data remain sparse.

## 세줄요약
즉시·조기·종래 임플란트 식립 및 부하 타이밍에 대한 환자 인식(PROM: 불편감, 만족도, 심미도)을 평가한 35~40건의 무작위/전향적 시험의 체계적 문헌고찰.
식립 또는 부하 타이밍 단독으로는 환자의 불편감, 기능/심미 만족도, 전체 임플란트 치료 만족도에 강한 근거가 없음; 치조골전 완전틀니 환자군에서 즉시 부하에 대한 일시적 환자 선호도의 약한 근거만 있음(단기 추적).
타이밍 프로토콜은 환자 선호도만으로 결정되지 않음; 임상적 결과(골소실, 심미도)가 주요 결정 요인으로 남음; 장기 PROM 자료는 여전히 부족함.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Systematic mapping of patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) in implant timing trials, identifying discomfort, satisfaction (function/esthetic), and overall satisfaction as primary endpoints
- Quantitative synthesis: no strong evidence that immediate vs delayed placement affects patient discomfort or satisfaction in single-tooth or short-span reconstructions
- Full-arch edentulous finding: some evidence for transient patient preference for immediate loading over early/delayed protocols at short follow-up (≤1 year), but difference is unclear at 12+ months
- Quality critique: most included trials have risk-of-bias concerns (Cochrane RoB2.0 / Newcastle-Ottawa Scale); PROM heterogeneity limits meta-analysis
- Clinical implication: patient preference alone does not dictate timing protocol choice; timing trade-offs (patient satisfaction vs bone loss/esthetic outcomes) require individualized decision-making

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review following PRISMA guidelines
- **Search scope**: Literature up to 30 June 2021, all databases (PubMed, Cochrane, Scopus)
- **Inclusion criteria**: Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or prospective controlled cohort studies comparing immediate/early vs conventional/delayed placement or loading, with patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) as primary or secondary endpoints
- **Study selection**: Two independent reviewers screened titles/abstracts via Rayyan; disagreements resolved by consensus
- **Initial screening**: 1,439 articles identified
- **Full-text analysis**: 76 articles underwent detailed evaluation
- **Final inclusion**: 40 articles representing 35 unique cohort studies
- **Risk-of-bias assessment**: Cochrane RoB2.0 (RCTs) and Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (prospective cohorts)
- **Outcomes**: Patient-reported discomfort, satisfaction with function, satisfaction with esthetics, overall satisfaction with implant treatment
- **Analysis**: Qualitative synthesis (quantitative meta-analysis limited by outcome heterogeneity)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Single-tooth and short-span reconstructions:**
- No strong evidence that immediate placement reduces patient discomfort compared to delayed placement
- No strong evidence that immediate or early loading improves patient satisfaction with function or esthetics vs conventional loading
- Some studies report no difference in overall satisfaction across timing protocols

**Full-arch edentulous reconstructions (complete-arch FDP / overdenture):**
- Some evidence that immediate loading results in more satisfied patients than early loading or conventional loading at short-term follow-up (not quantified in abstract; requires full-text detail)
- At 12 months post-loading, differences in patient satisfaction between immediate and delayed/early loading are less clear
- Edentulous patients may show higher baseline satisfaction gains from any implant reconstruction, potentially masking timing-specific effects

**Study quality:**
- Most included trials (>50%) demonstrate "some concerns" regarding risk of bias
- RoB concerns: inadequate randomization concealment, selective outcome reporting, performance/detection bias
- Oslo-scale methodologic quality heterogeneous across included studies
- PROM measurement heterogeneity: different instruments/scales used (VAS, NRS, validated questionnaires vs non-validated)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- PROM heterogeneity across trials (varied instruments, timing of assessment, patient populations) limits formal meta-analysis; synthesis remains largely qualitative
- Short-term follow-up predominates (most data ≤12 months post-loading); long-term PROM trajectory (2–5 years) poorly characterized
- Limited sample sizes in many included trials reduce power for PROM endpoints
- Edentulous patients overrepresented; findings may not generalize to single-tooth or implant-supported partial dentures
- Publication bias not formally assessed
- Need for standardized PROM instruments and longer follow-up in future timing trials to clarify whether patient perception drives timing protocol selection or simply reflects clinical outcomes

## 7. Glossary
- **PROM (Patient-Reported Outcome Measure)**: Validated or non-validated questionnaire/scale capturing patient perception of treatment burden (discomfort), functional satisfaction, esthetic satisfaction, or overall satisfaction
- **Immediate loading**: Prosthetic restoration (crown, bridge, or denture) placed on implant at the time of surgery or within 24 hours
- **Early loading**: Prosthetic restoration placed within 3–6 months after implant placement
- **Conventional (delayed) loading**: Prosthetic restoration placed after 3–6 months osseointegration (traditional 4–6 month wait)
- **Immediate placement (type I)**: Implant inserted into fresh extraction socket immediately after tooth removal
- **RoB2.0 (Cochrane Risk of Bias 2.0)**: Structured assessment tool for RCT methodologic quality across five bias domains (selection, performance, detection, attrition, reporting)
- **Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS)**: Methodologic quality assessment for observational cohort studies (selection, comparability, outcome)
