## Why Ingested

First systematic review in this wiki specifically synthesizing MRI for dental, oral, and maxillofacial trauma (radiation-free imaging), with a new category [[wiki/radiology/willershausen-2025-low-field-mri-pediatric-dental]]. It consolidates the MRI-trauma evidence base ([[wiki/radiology/dewake-2023-mri-occlusal-trauma-pdl]] reinforces its dental-trauma soft-tissue claims) and provides the framework within which modern CT-like (UTE/ZTE) and Black Bone MRI protocols — the radiation-free counterweights to this wiki's large CT/CBCT dose literature — can be judged.

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42024588224) of 29 in vivo studies (982 trauma patients, 0.5–83 y, 1991–2023) evaluating MRI for dental, oral, and maxillofacial trauma: 12 orbital, 10 condylar/subcondylar/TMJ, 5 mandibular, 1 temporal bone, 1 dental trauma.
MRI matches CT sensitivity for orbital floor fracture detection and IAN lesion detection (>90%, confirmed intraoperatively), provides disc assessment feasible in ~90% of cases, and CT-like MRI reaches inter/intra-reader agreement κ 0.92–0.98 for mandibular fracture classification.
MRI is a promising radiation-free adjunct for pediatric/pregnant patients (ALADAIP), but remains complementary — not a standalone replacement for CT/CBCT in acute trauma because it underperforms on fine osseous detail such as microfractures.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First/consolidating SR evaluating state-of-the-art radiation-free MRI (CT-like UTE/ZTE + Black Bone sequences) across the full spectrum of dental, oral, and maxillofacial trauma, compared to X-ray-based imaging.
- Distinct modality- and protocol-oriented conclusions per trauma subsite: orbital, condylar/TMJ, mandibular, temporal bone, dental.
- Introduces the ALADAIP ("as low as diagnostically acceptable being indication-oriented and patient specific") rationale for MRI-first imaging in young, radio-sensitive trauma patients.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review without meta-analysis (PRISMA), registered PROSPERO CRD42024588224
- **Databases**: MEDLINE, EMBASE, BIOSIS, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, LILACS, BBO Dentistry
- **n**: 29 studies (982 patients); initial hits 1160 → 986 after dedup → 14 excluded at full-text
- **Search window**: up to November 2024; English/German; inclusion: in vivo studies, case series ≥5 cases, ED/outpatient admissions, full text
- **Quality tools**: adapted STROBE (18 criteria; low ≥15, moderate 11–14, high ≤10) + QUADAS-2

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Study characteristics: 59% prospective, 41% retrospective, 79% comparative; field strengths 0.23/0.5/1.5/3 T; acquisition 6–53 min; MRI at post-traumatic, postoperative, or both stages.
- Risk of bias: low in 22 studies, moderate in 6, high in 1 (Kleinheinz 2000, 8/18 = 44%).
- Orbital: MRI matches CT sensitivity for orbital floor fractures but is less effective for small/associated fractures (Freund 2002); preoperative detection of retained foreign bodies only ~50% for both CT and MRI (Nasr 1999); MRI-derived 3D-printed orbital models as accurate as CT for blowout margins, less accurate for zygomaticomaxillary fractures, no significant difference in plate-bending accuracy (Cooper 2020).
- Condylar/TMJ: disc assessment feasible in ~90% of cases (Zheng 2016; table: 87% of cases successfully assessed postoperatively); >70% of discs on the fractured side remained orthotopic with no fixed anterior dislocation, excursion limitation in 30% of treated joints (Neff 2000).
- Mandibular: traumatic IAN lesions detected in >90% of cases, all confirmed intraoperatively (Kress 2003); CT-like MRI (3D T1 GRE) identified and classified all mandibular fractures with inter/intra-reader agreement κ range 0.92–0.98 and no significant difference in fracture-dislocation assessment (Feuerriegel 2023b).
- Dental: 3 T MRI + 20-channel head/neck coil (14:46 min) distinguished pulp from surrounding structures and evaluated reperfusion/vitality in children, potentially avoiding unnecessary root canal treatment (Assaf 2015, n=7, 8–17 y).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- No meta-analysis; heterogeneous MR protocols, field strengths, coils, and outcome measures across studies.
- Many included studies >5 years old, predating recent MRI advances; scant data for zygomatic and dental fracture types in vivo.
- Longer scan times, motion artifacts (children, severe pain), limited depiction of microfractures, high cost/availability limits in emergency settings.
- Future: standardization of scan parameters/field strengths/coils, RCTs vs X-ray gold standard, head-to-head protocol comparisons.

## 6. Related Work
- Feuerriegel 2023b, Burian 2020, Kress 2003: CT-like/Black Bone MRI for mandibular fracture + inferior alveolar nerve assessment.
- Assaf 2015: dental trauma MRI for pulp vitality — overlaps with this wiki's clinical dental-trauma pages on pulp necrosis.
- Willershausen 2025, Dewake 2023: radiation-free MRI pages already in this wiki — independently consistent with the SR's pediatric emphasis and MRI's soft-tissue/PULP depiction role.

## 7. Glossary
- **Black Bone MRI**: MR protocol rendering cortical bone dark against soft tissue, giving CT-like bone depiction without ionizing radiation; limited where "black bone" meets "black air" (maxillary sinus).
- **CT-like MRI (UTE/ZTE)**: ultra-short / zero echo time sequences producing CT-analogous images of mineralized tissue; less susceptible to metal artifacts than Black Bone MRI but poorer on microfractures and pure soft-tissue protocols.
- **NMCNR**: nerve–muscle contrast-to-noise ratio (Burian 2020 quantitative IAN assessment).
- **ALADAIP**: "as low as diagnostically acceptable, being indication-oriented and patient specific" — indication-driven radiation minimizing principle.
