Adds a structured, PRISMA-ScR-based evidence layer under the bubalo overview's scaffold-material claims.

## Three-line Summary

Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR) of 23 clinical studies (2015–2024; 9 RCTs, 1 prospective, 2 pilot, 5 case series, 5 retrospective, 1 clinical trial) on customized digital (CAD/CAM, 3D-printed) workflows for alveolar bone regeneration/ridge augmentation.

Workflow is CBCT→DICOM→STL→CAD design→3D print; titanium mesh (16/23 studies, mostly grade IV/V, fabricated via DMLS 9 studies, SLM 7 studies, or 5-axis milling 5 studies) dominates, with PEEK mesh and 3D-printed alloplastic/hydroxyapatite bone grafts as alternatives; average mesh thickness 0.5 mm; one RCT (Kim et al., n=60) showed 3D-printed customized bone graft gave higher % bone volume vs conventional block graft at 5 months, and another study (Wang et al., n=24) showed customized allogeneic blocks gave greater horizontal bone gain and less resorption plus shorter operative time vs autogenous blocks.

Clinical implication: digital customization improves surgical precision/efficiency and appears to modestly improve some radiographic outcomes and reduce operative time versus conventional membranes/grafts, but the evidence base is still limited (few RCTs, heterogeneous protocols, no meta-analysis performed), so protocol standardization and long-term outcome data are still needed.

임상적 의미: 디지털 맞춤화는 수술 정밀도·효율을 높이고 일부 방사선학적 결과와 수술시간을 개선하는 것으로 보이나, RCT 수가 적고 프로토콜이 이질적이며 메타분석이 이루어지지 않아 근거 수준은 아직 제한적 — 프로토콜 표준화와 장기 추적 데이터가 필요.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First review to systematically map the *digital workflow itself* (software, file formats, printing technique, sterilization method) for customized 3D-printed scaffolds/meshes used in alveolar bone regeneration, rather than only comparing clinical outcomes.
- Documents a common cross-study workflow: CBCT scan → DICOM → STL conversion → 3D visualization/planning software (e.g., Mimics, Btk-3D) → mesh/graft CAD design → 3D printing.
- Catalogs specific software (Mimics, Btk-3D, 3D Slicer, Yxoss CBR/ReOss, TRIOS 3, Dent-Plan), materials (titanium grade IV/V, PEEK, alloplastic hydroxyapatite/β-TCP, allogenic bone), printing methods (DMLS, SLM, five-axis milling), and sterilization protocols (autoclave 135°C 15–20 min, 2.4% glutaraldehyde 12 h, ethylene oxide) actually reported in the 23 included studies.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- PRISMA-ScR-guided scoping review; PICO question: in adult patients undergoing bone regeneration, what is the impact of customized digital 3D planning/printing workflows on clinical and radiographic outcomes vs conventional methods?
- Databases: MEDLINE (PubMed), Cochrane Library, Scopus, Web of Science; final search 27 February 2025.
- Inclusion: RCTs, non-randomized trials, cohort studies, case series, retrospective studies, English, 2015-01-01 to 2025-01-01, reporting ≥1 clinical or radiographic outcome.
- Exclusion: in vitro, animal studies, commentaries, narrative reviews, unpublished data, expert opinion, isolated case reports.
- Two independent reviewers (SE, LPB) screened via Covidence; third reviewer (MHAS) arbitrated disagreements. Data extraction independently performed and verified.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 23 studies (2015–2024): 9 RCTs, 1 prospective study, 2 pilot studies, 5 case series, 5 retrospective case studies, 1 clinical trial.
- Software: Mimics used in 6/19 studies with reported software; Btk-3D in 5 studies; others used 3D Slicer, Yxoss CBR, TRIOS 3, Dent-Plan; 4 case series did not specify software.
- Materials: 16 studies used titanium alloy (grade IV or Ti64/grade V variants); 2 studies used PEEK mesh; 3 studies developed 3D-printed bone graft materials (alloplastic hydroxyapatite/β-TCP, nanohydroxyapatite, milled allogenic bone).
- Mesh thickness range 0.1–2 mm, average ~0.5 mm (7 studies did not specify).
- Mesh design: 16 studies used perforated titanium mesh; 3 used occlusive titanium mesh; 1 occlusive PEEK mesh; only 2 studies specified pore size (1 mm diameter).
- Printing method: DMLS in 9 studies, SLM in 7 studies, five-axis milling in 5 studies; 1 study did not specify.
- Sterilization: autoclave 135°C/15–20 min in 3 studies; 2.4% glutaraldehyde/12 h in 3 studies; remainder unspecified or pre-sterilized by manufacturer.
- Kim et al. RCT (n=60): 3D-printed customized alloplastic bone graft vs conventional block bone graft for GBR post-extraction — significantly higher % bone volume and smaller tissue surface area at 5 months in 3D-printed group; no other significant differences.
- Wang et al. (n=24): customized allogeneic bone blocks vs autogenous bone blocks — greater horizontal bone gain, less horizontal resorption at 1 mm below crest, and shorter operative time at 6 months in the customized group.
- Mounir et al. RCT (n=16): custom PEEK mesh vs prebent titanium mesh — no statistically significant difference in 3D bone gain % or linear dimensional change between groups.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Scoping review, not a systematic review with meta-analysis — no pooled quantitative synthesis of outcomes; heterogeneous protocols/materials/follow-up prevent direct cross-study comparison.
- Number of RCTs remains limited relative to case series/retrospective designs; several studies did not report software, sterilization method, or pore size, limiting reproducibility assessment.
- Authors explicitly flag the need for further RCTs directly comparing 3D-customized bone/BR techniques for advantages, complications, and long-term (beyond ~5–6 month) outcomes, and call for a future systematic review/meta-analysis specifically comparing bone graft materials.

## 7. Glossary

- **CAD/CAM**: Computer-Aided Design / Computer-Aided Manufacturing.
- **DICOM**: Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine — CBCT scan output format.
- **STL**: Standard Triangulation Language — 3D mesh file format used for design/printing.
- **DMLS**: Direct Metal Laser Sintering — additive manufacturing technique fusing metal powder layer-by-layer.
- **SLM**: Selective Laser Melting — powder-bed-fusion additive manufacturing technique using a high-powered laser.
- **PEEK**: Poly Ether-Ether Ketone — polymer used as an alternative to titanium mesh, elastic modulus similar to bone.
- **PICO**: Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome — structured review question framework.
- **PRISMA-ScR**: Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews.
