## Why Ingested

This case documents the pathophysiology of natural-ostium reclosure/recurrence in odontogenic maxillary sinusitis (OMS) and a mucosa-preserving conservative management (nasoantral window in the inferior nasal meatus + apicoectomy) that endoscopic sinus surgery cannot deliver — it complements our sinus-infection/otolaryngology pages that frame implant/endodontic OMS from the ENT and surgical side. Reinforces [[sinus-lift/lateral/nam-2014-fess-intraoral-implant-related-sinusitis]] (excretory-passage/ostial patency rationale) and relates to [[sinus-lift/lateral/kim-2019-maxillary-implants-otolaryngologist-perspective]] (OMS management from the otolaryngologist perspective).

## Three-line Summary

Single case report (n=1, 54-year-old man) of severe OMS from underfilled teeth #25/#26 spreading to the frontal sinus, treated by conservative maxillary sinus management (CMSM) — Caldwell–Luc access, mucosa preservation, a large inferior-meatus nasoantral window, plus apicoectomy and retrograde filling — with complete recovery at 2 years and preserved teeth and sinus function.

## 1. Document Information

- **Type**: Case report (single patient) — abstract + full text retrieved from PubMed Central.
- **Authors / Journal**: Ohyama Y, Ogawa M, Yokoo S. *Case Reports in Dentistry* 2022;2022:1614739.
- **PMID / PMCID / DOI**: 35935340 / PMC9348962 / 10.1155/2022/1614739.
- **Scope**: Proposes and illustrates "conservative maxillary sinus management" (CMSM) as an alternative to both classical maxillary sinus radical surgery (MSRS) and endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) for severe odontogenic maxillary sinusitis.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Articulates the **pathophysiologic failure modes** of the two established options for OMS: MSRS removes the maxillary sinus mucosa (MSM) and causes postoperative maxillary cyst (POMC), deformation, sclerosis and loss of function decades later; ESS relies on the **natural ostium**, which gives an inadequate excretory tract/ventilation hole and **recloses easily** because the OMS causative site sits distally *below* the natural ostium and pus pools in the inferior meatus by gravity.
- Describes **CMSM** (attributed to Yokoo et al.): a Caldwell–Luc approach that **preserves the thickened MSM** (pseudostratified ciliated epithelium) and creates a **large nasoantral window in the inferior nasal meatus** to secure a gravity-appropriate excretory passage and ventilation, while avoiding nasolacrimal duct damage.
- Adds **apicoectomy + retrograde root-canal filling of the causative teeth** — a step ESS cannot perform — allowing simultaneous cure of the odontogenic source and tooth preservation.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: single-patient case report; no comparator, no cohort.
- **Diagnosis**: 54-year-old man referred from otolaryngology; chief complaint pain radiating from the cheek to the forehead; cheek tenderness without swelling. Teeth #25 and #26 (FDI) root-canal-treated but **underfilled**, apices exposed to the sinus on panoramic radiograph. CT showed opacification extending from the maxillary sinus to the **frontal sinus**.
- **Prior failed treatment**: endodontic (re)treatment + long-term antibiotics did not resolve symptoms.
- **Surgical procedure (CMSM)**: general anesthesia; 5–10 mm gingivobuccal-fold incision; mucoperiosteal flap; cortical bone removed at the canine fossa for maxillary sinus access. MSM at the protruding root apices of #25/#26 removed, then **apicoectomy**; missing root filling in the buccal-distal canal of #26 → **retrograde filling with 4-META/MMA-TBB resin**. Saline irrigation; remaining **thickened MSM preserved**. **Large nasoantral window** cut in the inferior nasal meatus (vertical position guided by the palatal wall; created with saw and hammer in the raised area atraumatically, then enlarged with bone forceps). Acromycin (tetracycline) gauze packed into the sinus via the window, removed at 1 week. **Clarithromycin for 1 month** postoperatively.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Two years postoperatively**: recovery from OMS observed; anatomy and function of the maxillary sinus **completely restored**; nasoantral window **open**; causative teeth **preserved**.
- No quantitative outcomes beyond the single-case follow-up are reported.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **n = 1** case report — lowest evidence tier; no control, no radiographic scoring, single operator/technique.
- Authors explicitly state that **further investigation in a larger patient group** is needed to establish CMSM as a treatment option.
- Follow-up limited to 2 years; POMC is described as arising *decades* after MSRS, so the POMC-prevention claim for CMSM is mechanistic/theoretical rather than demonstrated over that timescale.

## 6. Related Work

- MSRS (classical radical surgery) and its long-term sequela POMC.
- ESS with natural-ostium windowing (the current common minimally invasive standard for OMS) — critiqued here for ostial reclosure and inadequate excretory patency.
- CMSM as developed by Yokoo et al. (prior work by the same group).

## 7. Glossary

- **OMS** — odontogenic maxillary sinusitis; sinusitis of dental origin (here, underfilled root canals with apices exposed to the sinus).
- **MSM** — maxillary sinus mucosa; pseudostratified ciliated epithelium responsible for mucociliary clearance and ventilation.
- **MSRS** — (classical) maxillary sinus radical surgery; removes the MSM.
- **POMC** — postoperative maxillary cyst; a late complication of MSRS.
- **ESS** — endoscopic sinus surgery; minimally invasive, natural-ostium–based.
- **CMSM** — conservative maxillary sinus management; the mucosa-preserving Caldwell–Luc + inferior-meatus nasoantral-window technique proposed here.
- **Nasoantral window** — surgical opening between the maxillary sinus and nasal cavity (here in the inferior nasal meatus) for drainage/ventilation.
- **Caldwell–Luc approach** — transoral (canine-fossa) access to the maxillary sinus.
- **4-META/MMA-TBB resin** — an adhesive resin used here for retrograde root-end filling.
