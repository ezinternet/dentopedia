## Why Ingested

Resolves the retrodiscal region into its two distinct posterior attachments (superior vs inferior) and quantifies how each differs mechanically and biochemically from the disc — anatomical granularity the other retrodiscal-tissue pages ([[wiki/tmj/coombs-2017-structure-function-relationships-temporomandibular-retrodiscal]], [[wiki/tmj/yuan-2026-distinct-mural-cells-fibroblasts-drive]]) treat as a single "bilaminar zone." Establishes tissue-engineering design criteria and a functional division of labor (stiff posterior inferior attachment anchors the disc to the condyle; compliant posterior superior attachment permits translation over the eminence). abstract-only — full text not retrieved (empty PMC body).

## Three-line Summary

Structural, tensile, biochemical, and collagen-crosslinking characterization of the two posterior attachments (posterior superior attachment, PSA; posterior inferior attachment, PIA) and the lateral disc in the Yucatan minipig, a standard large-animal TMJ model. The PIA was 2.13× stiffer and 2.30× stronger than the PSA, 1.93× more collagenous by dry weight, and 1.78× more crosslinked; the PSA was the most heterogeneous and fattiest region, while the lateral disc had the highest glycosaminoglycan content (1.48–5.39× the attachments) and the most anisotropic collagen. The attachments are less fibrocartilaginous than the disc but still mechanically load-bearing, supporting a division of function — the stiffer PIA anchors the disc to the condyle during articulation while the softer PSA allows the disc to translate over the articular eminence.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **Separates the retrodiscal region into PSA and PIA** and shows they are mechanically and biochemically distinct — not a homogeneous "bilaminar zone."
2. **Quantitative functional division of labor**: stiff PIA = disc-to-condyle anchor; compliant PSA = permits anterior translation over the articular eminence.
3. **Gold-standard tissue-engineering design criteria** for the TMJ disc complex (target moduli, collagen/GAG content, crosslink density per region).
4. **Establishes the Yucatan minipig disc-complex baseline** — a widely accepted TMJ animal model.
5. Shows the **disc attachments are less fibrocartilaginous than the disc** (lower GAG, lower collagen in PSA) yet still contribute meaningfully to mechanical stability.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Model**: Yucatan minipig; three locations — PSA, PIA, lateral disc.
- **Assays**: tensile testing (stiffness, strength); collagen fiber alignment / anisotropy; biochemistry (collagen per dry weight, glycosaminoglycan per dry weight); collagen crosslinking (per dry weight); tissue heterogeneity and fat-vacuole proportion.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Comparison | Result |
|---|---|
| PIA vs PSA — tensile stiffness | PIA 2.13× stiffer |
| PIA vs PSA — tensile strength | PIA 2.30× stronger |
| Collagen per dry weight | PIA 1.93× and lateral disc 1.91× > PSA |
| Crosslinking per dry weight | PIA 1.78× > PSA |
| GAG per dry weight | lateral disc 1.48× > PIA; 5.39× > PSA |
| Collagen alignment | lateral disc most aligned/anisotropic; both attachments primarily mediolateral |
| Heterogeneity / fat vacuoles | PSA highest of the three locations |

- Functional interpretation: PIA anchors the disc to the mandibular condyle; PSA (softer, fattier, more heterogeneous) allows disc translation over the eminence.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Yucatan minipig, ex vivo — species and post-mortem differences from human tissue.
- Healthy tissue only; no displaced/remodeled comparison.
- Six attachments named in the abstract but only PSA, PIA, and lateral disc characterized here.
- abstract-only page: results limited to the abstract's ratios; absolute values not captured.

## 7. Glossary

- **TMJ disc complex**: the articular disc plus its attachments (six named: anterior, posterior superior, posterior inferior, medial, lateral, and the anterior capsular/pterygoid connection)
- **Posterior superior attachment (PSA)**: superior lamina of the bilaminar zone — connects the posterior disc to the temporal bone / posterior glenoid; elastic, compliant
- **Posterior inferior attachment (PIA)**: inferior lamina of the bilaminar zone — connects the posterior disc to the mandibular condyle; collagenous, stiffer
- **Glycosaminoglycan (GAG)**: negatively charged polysaccharides that bind water and give fibrocartilage its compressive resistance; high in disc, low in attachments
- **Collagen crosslinking**: covalent bonds between collagen molecules that increase tensile strength and stiffness
- **Anisotropy**: direction-dependence of a material property (here, collagen alignment and mechanical response)
