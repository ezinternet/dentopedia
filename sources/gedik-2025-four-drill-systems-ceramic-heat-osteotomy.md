## Summary

In vitro study (Istanbul University Faculty of Dentistry) comparing four osteotomy systems: conventional stainless steel (CS, Straumann 5-step), velodrill (VD, Straumann 6-step), ceramic zirconia drill (CD, Z-Systems 4-step), and bone-enhancing drill (BE, Legend Group 8-step). 160 osteotomies (40/system) in fresh bovine rib cortical bone, standardized at 800 rpm, 2 kg axial load, 50 mL/min saline irrigation (23°C), infrared thermography (FLIR E6-XT). Final osteotomy: 4mm diameter × 10mm depth.

Key results:
- Ceramic (CD): highest mean temperature 24.82 ± 2.69°C (range 23.10–33.80°C); longest drilling time 17.07 ± 6.82s
- Velodrill (VD): 24.45 ± 1.15°C, 10.24 ± 4.89s
- Bone-enhancing (BE): 24.40 ± 1.50°C, 9.87 ± 4.68s
- Conventional SS (CS): 23.79 ± 1.08°C (lowest), 10.11 ± 5.94s
- CD vs all others on drilling time: p<0.0001; CD vs CS temperature: p=0.0723 (NS), but CD showed highest peak (33.80°C)
- CS significantly lower temp than VD (p<0.0001) and BE (p=0.0125) overall
- One ceramic drill fractured at 26th repetition (brittleness)
- All systems remained below 47°C necrosis threshold under standard irrigation conditions

Mechanism discussion: ceramic drills' low thermal conductivity impairs heat dissipation; prolonged contact time → progressive heat accumulation; velodrill distributes cutting across multiple diameters → reduced net resistance; BE displaces rather than removes bone.

Limitations: surface IR thermography only (not intraosseous); no histologic validation; no torque measurement; single bone density type; in vitro only.
