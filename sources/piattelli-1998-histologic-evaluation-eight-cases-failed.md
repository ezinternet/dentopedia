## Why Ingested

Ingested as the foundational human histological evidence for bone overheating as an early implant failure mechanism. Piattelli 1998 is one of the earliest and most cited papers to document the specific microscopical pattern of overheating-induced failure in human tissue (bone sequestra, absent periimplant bone regeneration, inflammatory infiltrate in the implant gap). Directly supports [[wiki/overviews/implant-failure-mechanism-xray-differential-overview]] Mechanism 1 and provides the histological signature that differentiates thermal necrosis from overcompression necrosis.

## Three-line Summary

Case series of 8 human implant failures attributed to bone overheating during osteotomy preparation; histological ground sections showed a consistent 6-feature microscopical pattern including bone sequestra, absence of peri-implant bone regeneration, and inflammatory infiltrate between bone and implant surface.

The surrounding bone in all cases was compact and mature — suggesting an established cortical site where heat generated during drilling exceeded the 47°C/1-minute thermal-osteonecrosis threshold — and bacteria were found around both the implant and necrotic bone, indicating secondary infection of the necrotic tissue.

This early Piattelli series established the histological fingerprint for drill-induced overheating failure and prompted standardization of irrigation and drill-speed protocols; its six-feature pattern remains the reference against which subsequent overheating studies are calibrated.

## 세줄요약

인간 임플란트 실패 8증례 증례군: 골절개 시 골 과열(Bone Overheating)이 주요 원인으로 추정되며, 전 증례에서 동일한 6개 현미경적 소견이 관찰됨 — 골 격리편(bone sequestra), 임플란트 주위골 재생 없음, 골-임플란트 간 염증세포 침윤.

주위골은 전 증례에서 치밀하고 성숙된 피질골 — 드릴링 시 과열(>47°C)이 발생하기 쉬운 고밀도 부위; 괴사골 주변에 세균 집락도 확인, 이차 감염을 시사.

Piattelli 그룹이 열 괴사 실패의 조직학적 지문을 최초 기술한 기초 논문 — 이후 모든 골절개 발열 연구에서 현미경적 기준으로 참조됨.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes the **6-feature histological fingerprint of overheating failure** in human tissue: (1) bone sequestra; (2) no peri-implant bone regeneration; (3) inflammatory infiltrate in bone–implant gap; (4) no organization of peri-implant bone clot; (5) compact, mature surrounding bone; (6) bacteria around implant and necrotic bone.
- First systematic human case series attributing early failure to drilling overheating — preceding most laboratory thermal-threshold studies that cite it.
- Distinguishes the overheating pattern from the peri-implantitis pattern: no bacteria in peri-implantitis early stages; bacteria are secondary in overheating (they colonize pre-existing necrotic tissue).
- Compact surrounding bone as a risk factor: all 8 cases involved dense cortical bone — supporting the mechanism that high bone density impairs heat dissipation.

## Methodology

Case series: 8 retrieved failed implants from clinical practice; ground histological sections prepared for each. Assessment: microscopical evaluation of peri-implant tissue for specified histological features. No comparison group; attribution to overheating is inferential (no intraoperative temperature data).

## Key Results

| Feature | Present in all 8 cases |
|---|---|
| Bone sequestra | Yes |
| Absence of peri-implant bone regeneration | Yes |
| Inflammatory infiltrate in implant–bone gap | Yes |
| No organization of peri-implant bone clot | Yes |
| Compact, mature surrounding bone | Yes |
| Bacteria around implant/necrotic bone | Yes |

## Limitations

- Abstract-only ingest; full histological images, implant brand/type, operative details (irrigation use, drill speed, torque values) not recoverable.
- No intraoperative temperature measurements — overheating is inferred from histological appearance, not proven.
- Very small series (n=8); publication bias toward "explainable" failures.
- No control implants; cannot calculate rates or compare patterns quantitatively.

## Glossary

- **Bone sequestrum**: a fragment of dead, devitalized bone separated from viable bone by a zone of inflammation; hallmark of osteonecrosis
- **Peri-implant bone clot**: the fibrin–blood clot that initially forms around an implant and must organize into woven bone for osseointegration — absent organization is a failure of early healing
- **Thermal osteonecrosis**: bone cell death caused by sustained heat (>47°C for ≥1 minute) during surgical site preparation
