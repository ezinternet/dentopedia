## Why Ingested

Fills the last evidence gap in [[wiki/overviews/unopposed-tooth-overeruption-overview]] — the only longitudinal in-vivo micro-CT rat study that directly quantifies the overeruption *rate* (µm/day) and compares young vs adult with a matched control group, corroborating [[wiki/occlusion/fujita-2009-supraposition-unopposed-molars-young-adult-rats]]'s age-effect finding with a higher-resolution longitudinal design.

## Three-line Summary

Longitudinal in-vivo micro-CT study (56 Wistar rats, 4-week-old vs 26-week-old, 12-week follow-up) measuring eruption rate and alveolar bone adaptation of mandibular first molars after maxillary right molar extraction.

Young unopposed molars erupted at 172 ± 67 µm/day (highest rate, most marked in first 3 weeks post-extraction); overloaded adult molars did NOT erupt; clinical crown height in young unopposed was 4.5-fold higher than controls.

Young animals erupt much faster and earlier than adults — confirming the age effect first shown by Fujita 2009, with the critical window being the first 3 weeks post-extraction.

## 세줄요약

종단 생체내 미세전산화단층촬영 (Micro-CT, in vivo) 연구(흰쥐 56마리, 4주령 vs 26주령, 12주 추적) — 상악 우측 대구치 발치 후 하악 제1대구치의 정출률 (Eruption Rate)과 치조골 적응 측정.

어린 쥐(무대합군): 정출률 172 ± 67 µm/일 — 발치 후 **첫 3주**에 가장 두드러진 후 점차 대조군 수준(12.9 ± 6.8 µm/일)으로 수렴; 과부하 성체 대구치는 실험기간 동안 정출 없음; 어린 무대합군 임상치관고 (Clinical Crown Height)는 대조군의 **4.5배**.

젊은 동물이 성체보다 정출 속도·양 모두 크며 발치 직후 3주가 가장 위험한 창(window) — Fujita 2009 연령 효과를 종단 설계로 재확인.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First longitudinal in-vivo micro-CT study directly measuring daily overeruption *rate* (µm/day) in young vs adult rats under a controlled protocol with matched controls.
- Quantifies the critical window: overeruption most marked in first 3 weeks post-extraction, then gradually decelerates toward control level.
- Confirms that adult overloaded (contralateral) molars do NOT erupt — only unopposed young molars show major overeruption.
- Alveolar bone adapts upward but lags behind tooth eruption, resulting in net increase in clinical crown height (4.5-fold vs controls in young group).
- Corroborates Fujita 2009 age-effect finding with a more rigorous longitudinal design and daily-rate metric.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Longitudinal in-vivo animal study (micro-CT)
- **Animals**: 56 male Wistar rats — 28 young (4-week-old) + 28 adult (26-week-old); each age group split into 16 experimental (maxillary right molar extraction) + 12 controls
- **Follow-up**: 12 weeks, regular scanning intervals
- **Reference point**: Mandibular canal (stable bony landmark)
- **Tooth groups**: Unopposed right mandibular first molars (experimental) vs overloaded left (contralateral) vs control

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Group | Eruption rate |
|---|---|
| Young unopposed (peak period) | 172 ± 67 µm/day |
| Young — later period (returned to control level) | 12.9 ± 6.8 µm/day |
| Adult overloaded (contralateral) | 0 µm/day (did not erupt) |
| Clinical crown height, young unopposed vs control | 4.5-fold higher |
| Critical window | First 3 weeks post-extraction |

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Rat model — eruption kinetics in rodents (continuously erupting teeth) differ from human secondary eruption; magnitude not directly translatable to clinical mm values.
- Abstract-only retrieved — specific statistical methods and inter-group comparisons for adult unopposed molars not available.
- 12-week study: long-term plateau beyond the experimental period not assessed.

## 7. Glossary

- **Eruption rate (µm/day)**: Daily rate of tooth coronal migration; allows direct comparison across time points and age groups.
- **Clinical crown height**: Visible portion of the crown above the gingival margin; increases when tooth erupts faster than alveolar bone adapts.
- **In vivo micro-CT**: High-resolution computed tomography in a living animal, enabling longitudinal tracking without sacrifice at each time point.
- **Overloaded tooth**: Contralateral molar that receives increased occlusal load when the antagonist is extracted — expected to erupt less or not at all.
