## Why Ingested

[[drug/anticoagulants/guardieiro-2023-chitosan-cellulose-hemostasis-dapt-rct]] establishes chitosan dressings as a local hemostatic option in antithrombotic patients at the human-RCT level; this paper adds the preclinical/materials layer beneath that claim — an antibacterial-modified chitosan variant (quaternized carboxymethyl chitosan/polydopamine, QCD) tested against a commercial gelatin sponge in an anticoagulated rat extraction model. Ingested primarily as a bench-to-animal counterpoint to the human local-hemostasis toolkit in [[oral-surgery/dinkova-2025-local-hemostasis-oral-surgery-review]], with the explicit caveat that its title's clinical framing outruns its evidence.

## Three-line Summary

Anticoagulated rat tooth-extraction model plus in vitro characterisation of a quaternized carboxymethyl chitosan/polydopamine composite sponge (QCD) — not a study of human anticoagulated patients, despite the title's wording.

In rats, QCD reported blood loss 0.011 ± 0.001 g and hemostasis time 59.667 ± 4.163 s versus a commercial gelatin sponge at 0.019 ± 0.001 g and 87.000 ± 4.082 s; the quaternary ammonium modification gave antibacterial activity in vitro, and the hemostatic advantage was attributed to rapid blood absorption and enhanced blood-cell/platelet adhesion.

There is no human data in this paper: the clinical claim in its title and closing sentence is an extrapolation from a rodent model and benchtop assays, and no comparison was made against tranexamic acid or oxidized cellulose.

항응고 처치된 흰쥐(rat) 발치 모델과 in vitro 실험으로 평가한 4급 암모늄화 카르복시메틸 키토산/폴리도파민 복합 지혈 스폰지(Quaternized Carboxymethyl Chitosan/Polydopamine, QCD) 연구 — 제목과 달리 항응고 환자를 대상으로 한 임상연구가 아니다.

쥐 모델에서 QCD는 출혈량 0.011 ± 0.001 g, 지혈시간 59.667 ± 4.163초로 상용 젤라틴 스폰지(0.019 ± 0.001 g, 87.000 ± 4.082초)보다 우수했고, 4급 암모늄 변형으로 in vitro 항균능을 보였으며, 저자들은 빠른 혈액흡수와 혈구·혈소판 부착 증가를 기전으로 제시한다.

사람 데이터는 전혀 없다 — 제목과 결론 문장의 임상적 함의는 설치류 모델과 벤치탑 실험에서의 외삽이며, 트라넥삼산(Tranexamic Acid, TXA)이나 산화셀룰로스(Oxidized Cellulose)와의 비교도 없다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Describes a quaternized carboxymethyl chitosan/polydopamine composite sponge (QCD) intended to combine mechanical robustness, biocompatibility, hemostatic performance, and intrinsic antibacterial activity in one material.
- Adds a **quaternary ammonium modification** as the antibacterial mechanism, motivated by the polymicrobial oral environment and post-extraction infection risk — a property standard gelatin sponges lack.
- Provides a head-to-head preclinical comparison against a commercial gelatin sponge in an **anticoagulated** extraction model, i.e. under the impaired-hemostasis condition that matters clinically, rather than in healthy animals.
- Proposes a mechanism for the hemostatic advantage: rapid blood absorption plus enhanced adhesion of blood cells and platelets, promoting clot formation.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

*(Abstract-level detail only; sample sizes, randomisation, blinding, anticoagulant agent/dose, and statistical methods are not recoverable without full text.)*

- **Material**: quaternized carboxymethyl chitosan / polydopamine composite sponge, abbreviated QCD.
- **Comparator**: commercial gelatin sponge ("Gel group").
- **In vitro arm**: hemostatic performance assays and antibacterial testing (MeSH includes Microbial Sensitivity Tests, i.e. susceptibility testing).
- **In vivo arm**: tooth extraction test in **anticoagulated rats** (MeSH: Rats, Sprague-Dawley; Male). The anticoagulant agent, dose, and route are not stated in the abstract.
- **Outcomes measured**: blood loss (g) and hemostasis time (s).
- **Mechanistic assessment**: blood absorption rate; adhesion of blood cells and platelets to the sponge.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

All values below are **rat-model values**, not clinical outcomes.

| Outcome (anticoagulated rat extraction) | QCD | Commercial gelatin sponge |
|---|---|---|
| Blood loss | 0.011 ± 0.001 g | 0.019 ± 0.001 g |
| Hemostasis time | 59.667 ± 4.163 s | 87.000 ± 4.082 s |

- QCD was reported as significantly outperforming the gelatin sponge on both outcomes; the exact test statistic and p-value are not in the abstract.
- **In vitro**: QCD showed markedly enhanced hemostatic and antibacterial performance versus the Gel group; the quaternary ammonium modification was credited with the antibacterial activity.
- **Proposed mechanism**: rapid blood absorption and enhanced blood-cell/platelet adhesion promoting clot formation.
- **Reported precision is the authors' own.** Three decimal places on a hemostasis time near 60 seconds (59.667 s) and on a blood loss near 10 milligrams (0.011 g) imply a measurement resolution the method almost certainly does not have; the 59.667/87.000 figures read as means of very small n rather than as precise quantities. Treat the *direction* of the difference as the finding, not the digits.
- **Absolute effect sizes are tiny in real terms**: an 8-milligram difference in blood loss and roughly 27 seconds of hemostasis time in a rat socket. Whether either scales to a human extraction socket is untested here.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Rat model.** The in vivo evidence is a rodent tooth-extraction model; rat socket size, blood volume, coagulation physiology, and oral flora differ substantially from human. Nothing here establishes clinical efficacy.
- **No human data whatsoever**, despite the title and closing sentence framing the work around "anticoagulated patients". Any downstream citation of this page as clinical evidence is a misuse.
- **Small effect sizes in absolute terms** — an 8 mg blood-loss difference and ~27 s hemostasis-time difference, with implausibly tight reported precision (see §4).
- **No comparison against tranexamic acid or oxidized cellulose**, the two agents that dominate the real-world local-hemostasis decision in anticoagulated extraction. The only comparator is a commercial gelatin sponge.
- **Antibacterial claim tested in vitro only.** No in vivo infection endpoint, no post-extraction infection rate, no oral-biofilm model reported; the anti-infective benefit is inferred, not demonstrated.
- **Abstract-only ingest**: sample size, anticoagulant protocol, randomisation/blinding, statistical methods, biocompatibility and degradation data, and any adverse findings are unavailable.
- **Future work implied**: large-animal or human feasibility work, comparison against TXA and oxidized cellulose, and an in vivo infection endpoint would be needed before any clinical inference.

## 7. Glossary

- **Chitosan**: deacetylated derivative of chitin; a cationic polysaccharide with intrinsic hemostatic activity mediated largely by electrostatic attraction of negatively charged erythrocyte membranes and platelets, independent of the coagulation cascade — which is why it is of interest in anticoagulated patients.
- **Carboxymethyl chitosan (CMC)**: water-soluble chitosan derivative with carboxymethyl groups added, improving solubility and processability into sponges/films.
- **Quaternized (quaternary ammonium modification)**: grafting permanently positively charged quaternary ammonium groups onto the polymer; the cationic charge disrupts bacterial membranes, conferring antibacterial activity.
- **Polydopamine (PDA)**: mussel-inspired adhesive polymer formed by dopamine self-polymerisation; used to improve wet adhesion and mechanical integrity of the composite.
- **Gelatin sponge**: absorbable porous gelatin hemostat (e.g. Gelfoam/Spongostan), the standard socket-filling comparator; acts mainly as a physical scaffold for clot formation.
- **Hemostasis time**: time from wound creation/agent placement to cessation of visible bleeding; here measured in a rat extraction socket.
- **Sprague-Dawley rat**: outbred albino laboratory rat strain, the model animal used for the in vivo arm.
- **TXA (Tranexamic Acid)**: antifibrinolytic agent used topically (mouthwash, powder, soaked gauze) for post-extraction bleeding; **not** compared against in this study.
- **Oxidized cellulose (Surgicel)**: absorbable topical hemostat acting via low pH and a physical clot matrix; **not** compared against in this study.
