## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 12 systematic reviews of RCTs (search May 2012–May 2017; 8/12 low risk of bias per ROBIS) evaluating implant prosthetic rehabilitation in partially edentulous patients with bone atrophy — bone augmentation, short/tilted/zygomatic implants, sinus lift, and IAN transposition.

Short implants (≤8 mm) showed a notable decrease in complications versus long implants with bone augmentation; no augmentation/sinus-lift technique or membrane/bone-substitute type was shown superior to another for implant or prosthetic failure; no RCT data existed for zygomatic implants, tilted implants, or IAN transposition.

Evidence is not robust enough to determine the single best treatment for atrophic partially edentulous ridges — for vertical defects, short implants should be preferred when feasible given fewer complications, but long-term follow-up data are lacking; no quantitative meta-analyses were performed (qualitative synthesis only).

## 세줄요약

부분무치악 환자의 골위축(bone atrophy) 부위 임플란트 보철 재활에 관한 RCT 기반 체계적 고찰 12편을 종합한 우산형 고찰(umbrella review, ROBIS로 8/12편 저위험 평가).

짧은 임플란트(≤8mm)는 골증대 동반 긴 임플란트 대비 합병증이 뚜렷이 적었고, 골증대·상악동거상술 기법 간 혹은 차폐막·골이식재 종류 간 임플란트/보철 실패율 차이는 없었으며, 관골(zygomatic) 임플란트·경사 임플란트·하치조신경 이전술에 대한 RCT 근거는 전무했다.

위축된 부분무치악 부위의 "최선의 치료"를 확정할 만큼 근거가 견고하지 않으며, 수직 결손에서는 가능하면 짧은 임플란트를 우선 고려할 것을 제안하나 장기추적 자료 부족이 한계(정량적 메타분석은 수행되지 않음 — 정성적 종합).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Umbrella review restricted to systematic reviews of **RCTs only** (not observational studies), reducing confounding-driven bias relative to typical umbrella reviews of mixed-design SRs.
- Applies **ROBIS** (not AMSTAR) as the risk-of-bias tool for the included SRs — 8 of 12 included reviews judged low risk of bias.
- Spans the full breadth of atrophic-ridge implant interventions in one synthesis: bone augmentation procedures, short implants, tilted implants, zygomatic implants, sinus lift, and inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) transposition — rather than a single-technique comparison.
- Explicitly flags evidence gaps: **zero RCT-based SR data** exists comparing zygomatic implants, tilted implants, or IAN transposition against alternatives.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Search of systematic reviews of RCTs from MEDLINE, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, and PROSPERO, restricted to the **prior 5 years** (May 2012–May 2017), supplemented by manual search of SRs.
- Included: SRs of RCTs on partially edentulous adults with bone defects, evaluating bone augmentation, short/tilted/zygomatic implants, sinus lift, or IAN transposition.
- Outcomes assessed: prosthetic and implant failure, complications, radiographic/clinical peri-implant bone level change, esthetic/functional satisfaction, vestibular peri-implant soft-tissue recession.
- Data extraction independently by two authors using predefined fields; risk of bias assessed with **ROBIS**.
- No quantitative pooling (meta-meta-analysis) was performed — synthesis is qualitative/narrative across the 12 included SRs.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 12 systematic reviews of RCTs included; 8/12 rated low risk of bias by ROBIS.
- **Short implants (≤8 mm)**: notable decrease in complications vs. long implants placed with bone augmentation procedures (vertical-defect scenario).
- **Sinus lift / bone augmentation technique comparisons**: no technique demonstrated superiority over another for implant or prosthetic failure across the reviewed RCTs.
- **Membranes / bone substitutes (horizontal augmentation)**: use of a membrane appeared to increase hard-tissue regeneration, but different membrane types or bone substitute materials did not differ substantially in outcome; no difference detected in prosthesis/implant failure or complications.
- **Zygomatic implants, tilted implants, IAN transposition**: no RCT-based comparative data available at all.
- **Vertical bone augmentation in atrophic posterior mandible**: no conclusions possible on technique choice because quantitative meta-analyses were not performed by the underlying reviews.

## 7. Glossary

- **Umbrella review**: a review that summarizes multiple existing systematic reviews (a "review of reviews") rather than primary studies.
- **ROBIS**: Risk Of Bias In Systematic reviews — a tool for assessing the risk of bias of systematic reviews themselves (as opposed to AMSTAR, which assesses methodological quality more broadly).
- **IAN transposition**: inferior alveolar nerve transposition/lateralization, a surgical technique to allow posterior mandibular implant placement in the presence of a superficial mandibular canal.
- **Bone atrophy (partially edentulous)**: horizontal and/or vertical alveolar ridge deficiency following tooth loss, requiring augmentation or alternative implant strategies (short/tilted/zygomatic implants) for prosthetic rehabilitation.
