## Why Ingested

Directly relevant to clinical practice of adjusting and bonding zirconia restorations. Compares the effect of airborne particle abrasion (APA) and grinding on the mechanical properties (flexural strength, fracture toughness, fatigue resistance) of both high translucency (HT) and low translucency (LT) zirconia — a key question when intraoral adjustment of monolithic zirconia crowns is performed.

## Three-line Summary

LT zirconia (3Y-TZP, Cercon/Lava) has significantly higher biaxial flexural strength (~1247–1271 MPa) than HT zirconia (4Y-PSZ, Katana/BruxZir, ~813–834 MPa), but HT zirconia shows higher fracture toughness and better fatigue resistance. Low-pressure APA (50 µm alumina, 0.5 bar) increased flexural strength in all groups except BruxZir, while surface grinding consistently and significantly decreased both flexural strength and fracture toughness in all zirconia types. Cyclic fatigue (10⁶ cycles) further reduced residual flexural strength and Weibull reliability for all materials, with LT zirconia losing proportionally more strength (17–32%) than HT zirconia (11–14%).

## 세줄요약

저투명 지르코니아(LT, 3Y-TZP)는 고투명 지르코니아(HT, 4Y-PSZ)보다 이축 굴곡강도가 현저히 높으나(~1247 vs ~813 MPa), HT 지르코니아는 파절인성과 피로저항성이 더 우수하다. 저압 입자분사연마(APA, 50 µm 알루미나, 0.5 bar)는 BruxZir를 제외한 모든 군에서 굴곡강도를 유의하게 증가시킨 반면, 연삭(그라인딩)은 모든 지르코니아 종류에서 굴곡강도와 파절인성을 유의하게 감소시켰다. 주기적 피로부하(10⁶회)는 모든 군의 잔류 굴곡강도와 Weibull 신뢰성을 추가 감소시켰으며, LT 지르코니아(17–32%)가 HT 지르코니아(11–14%)보다 더 큰 감소를 보였다.

## Key Contributions

1. First study to compare both APA and grinding effects across four clinically relevant zirconia brands spanning both HT and LT categories simultaneously.
2. Demonstrated that low-pressure APA (0.5 bar) is beneficial or neutral for flexural strength, countering concerns about APA-induced surface damage.
3. Showed HT zirconia has paradoxically higher fracture toughness than LT zirconia despite lower flexural strength — attributed to larger grain size and longer crack paths.
4. Provided Weibull reliability data showing HT zirconia has lower moduli (less predictable fracture), clinically relevant for restoration planning.
5. Quantified fatigue degradation: LT zirconia loses more residual strength after 10⁶ cycles than HT zirconia, especially after grinding (25–32% reduction).

## Methodology

- **Materials**: 2 LT zirconia (Cercon base 3Y-TZP, Lava Frame 3Y-TZP) and 2 HT zirconia (Katana HT 4Y-PSZ, BruxZir 4Y-PSZ)
- **Specimens**: 480 discs milled and sintered to 12 mm diameter × 1.2 mm thickness (ISO 6872:2015)
- **Surface treatments**:
  - Control: as-sintered
  - APA: 50 µm alumina particles at 0.5 bar (AquaCare Twin sandblaster)
  - Grinding: 400 grit SiC paper under 300 g load with water cooling
- **Measurements**:
  - Surface roughness (Ra, Rv, Rp) via non-contact laser profilometer
  - Grain size via SEM (100 grains measured per type)
  - Biaxial flexural strength: piston-on-3-balls setup (ISO 6872:2015), n=20/subgroup
  - Fracture toughness (KIC): SEM measurement of critical defect size (cr) from fractured surfaces
  - Cyclic fatigue: 10⁶ cycles at 40% of mean initial strength in 37°C distilled water (ACTA fatigue tester), then residual strength measured
  - Weibull modulus: Quinn and Quinn method, Weibull++ v21 software
- **Statistics**: 3-way ANOVA, Tukey HSD post-hoc, α = 0.05

## Key Results

### Surface Roughness
- APA: no significant change in Ra vs control for any group
- Grinding: significantly increased Ra, Rv, Rp in all groups (p < 0.001)
- HT zirconia had higher Ra after APA than LT, attributed to larger grain pullout

### Initial Biaxial Flexural Strength (MPa)
| Material | As-sintered | APA | Grinding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cercon (LT) | 1247.5 ± 25.8 | 1451.5 ± 34.9 | 909.7 ± 22.9 |
| Lava (LT) | 1271 ± 14.1 | 1442.4 ± 24.3 | 973.4 ± 23.9 |
| Katana (HT) | 812.8 ± 27.9 | 860.3 ± 18.2 | 692.3 ± 16.8 |
| BruxZir (HT) | 833.5 ± 13.2 | 858.3 ± 17.1 | 686.5 ± 13.2 |

- LT zirconia significantly higher than HT in all conditions (p < 0.001)
- APA significantly increased strength in all groups except BruxZir
- Grinding significantly decreased strength in all groups

### Fracture Toughness (KIC, MPa·m^1/2)
- HT zirconia: higher KIC than LT zirconia
- APA: significantly increased KIC in all types except BruxZir
- Grinding: significantly decreased KIC in all types (p < 0.001)

### Fatigue and Residual Strength
- LT zirconia reduction after 10⁶ cycles: 17% (as-sintered), 13–16% (APA), 25–32% (grinding)
- HT zirconia reduction: 11–14% (as-sintered and APA), 13–14% (grinding)
- HT zirconia more resistant to cyclic fatigue degradation overall

### Weibull Modulus
- Values range: 6.7–14.8 (within dental ceramic range of 5–15)
- Grinding group: higher m than as-sintered in some cases (similar flaw size distribution)
- HT zirconia: lower m values = less predictable fracture pattern

## Limitations

- Cylindrical disc geometry does not simulate clinical crown anatomy
- Only two brands per translucency category tested (supra-HT and ultra-HT zirconia not included)
- No crystallographic analysis (XRD) performed — tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation not quantified
- In vitro — extrapolation to clinical outcomes requires caution

## Glossary

- **Biaxial flexural strength (이축 굴곡강도)**: Strength measured under biaxial stress state using piston-on-3-balls; more representative of clinical loading than uniaxial tests
- **Fracture toughness / KIC (파절인성)**: Material's resistance to crack propagation (MPa·m^1/2)
- **APA (Airborne Particle Abrasion, 입자분사연마)**: Sandblasting with alumina particles to roughen surface for bonding
- **Weibull modulus (바이블 계수)**: Statistical reliability measure; higher = more predictable fracture behavior
- **LT zirconia (저투명 지르코니아)**: 3Y-TZP, tetragonal phase dominant, high strength, opaque
- **HT zirconia (고투명 지르코니아)**: 4Y/5Y-PSZ, mixed tetragonal-cubic phases, translucent, lower strength but higher toughness
- **t-m transformation (정방정→단사정 상변태)**: Stress-induced phase transformation providing transformation toughening in zirconia
