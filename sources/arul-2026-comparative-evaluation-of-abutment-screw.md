## Why Ingested

Abutment screw loosening (incidence ~12.7%) remains the most common implant prosthetic complication; existing wiki pages such as [[prosthetic-materials/abutment-screw/nithyapriya-2018-factors-loss-preload-dental-implants]] catalogue preload-loss factors, and [[prosthetic-materials/abutment-screw/coelho-2024-preload-removal-torque-screw-coatings-laboratory]] tests silicone/PTFE coatings — but no ingested paper directly compares a medical-grade anaerobic threadlocker (methacrylate, Nobil Grip) against an industrial-grade threadlocker (dimethacrylate, Loctite 243) and an uncoated control under both static and simulated-chewing cyclic loading. This paper fills that gap and provides the first in-vitro evidence ranking methacrylate > dimethacrylate > control for detorque retention.

## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (n=30 implant-abutment assemblies, 3 groups × 2 loading conditions) comparing detorque values after threadlocker application (none / dimethacrylate Loctite 243 / methacrylate Nobil Grip) under static storage (1 month) and cyclic chewing simulation (240,000 cycles, 50 N, 48 h moist, ≈1 year of clinical function).

Methacrylate yielded the highest detorque values (static: 36.46 ± 0.95 Ncm; dynamic: 33.22 ± 0.34 Ncm), followed by dimethacrylate (32.84 ± 1.12 / 29.98 ± 1.05 Ncm) and control (28.80 ± 0.96 / 25.84 ± 0.63 Ncm); all pairwise differences statistically significant (P < 0.0005); cyclic loading consistently reduced detorque across all groups.

Incorporating medical-grade methacrylate threadlocker may reduce screw-loosening risk in high-load or challenging clinical situations (bruxism, posterior implants, long-span screw-retained prostheses), but in vitro findings need clinical validation.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First in vitro study to directly compare a medical-grade anaerobic methacrylate adhesive (Nobil Grip™) against an industrial-grade dimethacrylate threadlocker (Loctite® 243) and an untreated control for abutment screw retention.
- Demonstrates that methacrylate > dimethacrylate > control under both static (1 month) and dynamic cyclic loading (240,000 cycles), with all pairwise comparisons statistically significant.
- Establishes that cyclic loading reduces detorque values across all groups, reinforcing the clinical relevance of threadlocker application for functional load-bearing situations.
- Suggests that medical-grade (biocompatible, FDA-context) methacrylate threadlockers are clinically preferable over industrial-grade (non-FDA-approved for intraoral use) dimethacrylate adhesives.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In vitro prospective observational study; single operator; institutional ethics board approval.
- **Sample**: 30 Adin implant analogs (12.6 mm × 3.7 mm) with straight abutments (4.5 mm diameter, 8.5 mm height, 1 mm gingival height); PLA jig fabricated by FDM; bench vice stabilization.
- **Groups (n=10 each)**:
  - Control (C): no threadlocker; torqued to 35 Ncm + retorque at 10 min
  - Dimethacrylate (D): Loctite® 243 (Henkel, USA); industrial-grade; cured 24 h anaerobic
  - Methacrylate (M): Nobil Grip™ (Nobil Metal, Italy); medical-grade; cured 24 h anaerobic
- **Each group subdivided (n=5)**:
  - Static: room-temperature dry storage 1 month
  - Dynamic: chewing simulator 240,000 cycles at 50 N, 16 mm/s, 48 h moist (simulating 1 year clinical function; per Steiner et al. 2009 protocol)
- **Outcome**: Detorque value (Ncm) measured with digital torque meter at end of each loading condition.
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk normality, one-way + two-way ANOVA, Tukey HSD post hoc; α=0.05; G*Power 3.1.9.4 sample size (power=80%, effect size=0.60).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Group | Condition | Detorque mean ± SD (Ncm) | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control | Static (1 month) | 28.80 ± 0.96 | 27.60–29.99 |
| Dimethacrylate | Static (1 month) | 32.84 ± 1.12 | 31.44–34.23 |
| Methacrylate | Static (1 month) | 36.46 ± 0.95 | 35.27–37.64 |
| Control | Dynamic (240 k cycles) | 25.84 ± 0.63 | 25.04–26.63 |
| Dimethacrylate | Dynamic (240 k cycles) | 29.98 ± 1.05 | 28.66–31.29 |
| Methacrylate | Dynamic (240 k cycles) | 33.22 ± 0.34 | 32.79–33.64 |

- Overall static mean: 32.70 ± 3.37 Ncm vs dynamic: 29.68 ± 3.20 Ncm (P = 0.000)
- Pairwise under static: methacrylate–control Δ = 7.66 Ncm (P < 0.0005); methacrylate–dimethacrylate Δ = 3.62 Ncm (P < 0.0005); dimethacrylate–control Δ = 4.04 Ncm (P < 0.0005)
- Pairwise under dynamic: methacrylate–control Δ = 7.38 Ncm (P < 0.0005); methacrylate–dimethacrylate Δ = 3.24 Ncm (P < 0.0005); dimethacrylate–control Δ = 4.14 Ncm (P < 0.0005)
- Highest single value: methacrylate static 37.50 Ncm; lowest: control dynamic 24.90 Ncm

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- In vitro only — controlled laboratory environment does not replicate salivary exposure, thermal cycling, or diverse occlusal loading patterns.
- Static subgroup evaluated at only 1 month; long-term retention behavior of threadlockers uncharted.
- Single implant-abutment system (Adin, internal connection); results may not generalize to other connection geometries.
- Repeated insertion/removal cycles — a common clinical reality — not evaluated.
- Sample size is small (n=5 per subgroup), adequate for the stated power but limits precision of effect estimates.
- No biological safety evaluation of threadlockers in peri-implant tissues.

## 6. Related Work
- Arshad et al. (2017): industrial adhesive vs control — present study's detorque values higher in both control (28.80 vs 17.60 Ncm static) and methacrylate groups, attributed to chemical differences and medical-grade formulation.
- Bratu et al. (2019): screw sealer reduces loosening risk — consistent with present findings.
- Nithyapriya et al. (2018): catalogues preload-loss factors; threadlocker as surface-coating strategy maps onto that framework.
- Kim (2018): cyclic loading diminishes detorque values — corroborated by present dynamic vs static comparison.
- Coelho et al. (2024): GapSeal vs PTFE coatings vs control — comparable paradigm (surface treatment to improve retention); different material class (sealants vs anaerobic adhesives).

## 7. Glossary
- **Threadlocker / Anaerobic adhesive**: A polymer that polymerizes in the absence of oxygen and presence of metal ions, filling microscopic thread-to-thread voids and increasing frictional resistance against loosening.
- **Dimethacrylate (Loctite® 243)**: Industrial-grade medium-strength anaerobic adhesive; not FDA-approved for intraoral use; used historically in dental research as a screw-loosening inhibitor.
- **Methacrylate (Nobil Grip™)**: Medical-grade anaerobic adhesive with documented biocompatibility; used clinically on threaded components of attachments, orthodontic devices, and implant screws.
- **Detorque value**: The torque (Ncm) required to unscrew an abutment screw; a proxy for the degree to which screw retention has been preserved.
- **Preload**: The axial clamping force generated when a screw is tightened; the primary determinant of resistance to loosening.
- **Settling effect (embedment relaxation)**: Collapse of micro-surface asperities on mating threads under initial torquing, causing preload loss within the first minutes to hours after tightening.
- **Cyclic loading (chewing simulation)**: 240,000 cycles at 50 N, 16 mm/s in a moist environment over 48 h — simulates approximately 1 year of masticatory function per Steiner et al. 2009.
