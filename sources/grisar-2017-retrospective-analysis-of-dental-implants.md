## Why Ingested

Cited by [[implants/survival/krisam-2019-factors-affecting-the-early-failure]] as one of the references (10–18) behind its background statement that early failures account for about 2–6% of implants placed. Ingested to make that benchmark traceable and to extend the evidence base of [[overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]].

## One-line Summary

Retrospective cohort of 509 consecutive patients (1,139 implants) at University Hospitals Leuven, 2012–2014: early failure 4.6% of implants (6.5% of patients), associated with smoking, male sex, total edentulism, implant diameter and bone augmentation (abstract only).

## 2. Key Contributions

- University-hospital early-failure benchmark: 4.6% implant level, 6.5% patient level (early success 95.4%).
- Adds male sex and total edentulism to the usual smoking/augmentation risk factors.
- Describes the referral case mix: 80.1% partially edentulous, posterior mandible single-tooth the most common indication (24.4%).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Retrospective cohort of 509 consecutive patients (240 men, 269 women; median age 58) treated 2012–2014 in the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, University Hospitals Leuven, Belgium; 1,139 implants; 152 implants (13.3%) with augmentation or sinus elevation. Outcomes: indications, early implant failure and associated risk factors. Statistical methods and failure definition are not stated in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Early failure: 52/1,139 implants (4.6%) in 33/509 patients (6.5%); early success 95.4%. Associated factors: smoking, male gender, total edentulism, implant diameter, bone augmentation surgery (effect sizes, CIs and direction for diameter not available in the abstract).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Abstract only: definition of "early", statistical model, clustering handling and effect sizes unknown. Hospital referral population (oral surgery department) may differ from general practice.
