## One-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (N=325, up to 24.8yr) comparing ceramic in/-onlays vs. cast gold partial crowns: both achieved ~91–93% survival at 23.5 years, with CIOs failing by ceramic fracture (6.7%) and CGPCs by endodontic complications (8.8%).

## 한줄요약

후향 코호트(N=325, 최대 24.8년): 세라믹 인레이/온레이(CIO) vs 골드 부분크라운(CGPC) — 23.5년 생존율 양군 약 91~93%, CIO는 세라믹 파절(6.7%), CGPC는 근관합병증(8.8%)이 주요 실패 원인.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Longest published follow-up for cast gold partial crowns (up to 24.8 years, mean 13.9yr)
- Direct head-to-head comparison of CIO vs CGPC — both made by supervised undergraduate students
- CGPC annual failure rate 0.7%/yr provides first quantitative long-term benchmark for gold partial crowns
- Endodontically treated teeth performed significantly worse (p=.02) in both material groups
- Both materials achieved FDI-acceptable clinical quality in ~70% of restorations at final recall

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Study design**: Retrospective cohort, single-center (University Hospital Würzburg)
- **Sample**: 325 patients; 296 restorations assessed (CIO n=155, CGPC n=163 at recall)
- **CIO material**: IPS Empress I (Ivoclar Vivadent, leucite-reinforced glass ceramic), n=161 allocated
- **CGPC material**: Degunorm (DeguDent, gold alloy), n=164 allocated
- **Operator**: Supervised undergraduate students (standardized protocol)
- **Follow-up**: Up to 24.8 years, mean 13.9 ± 3.8 years
- **Outcomes**: USPHS criteria (modified); clinical + radiographic assessment; Kaplan-Meier survival curves
- **Teeth**: Pre-molars and molars (posterior only); vital and endodontically treated included

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Survival Rates (Kaplan-Meier)

| Material | Mean service time | Survival |
|---|---|---|
| CIO | 15.2 yr | 93.9% |
| CIO | 23.5 yr | 91.7% |
| CGPC | 14.9 yr | 92.6% |
| CGPC | 23.5 yr | 91.8% |

### Success Rates

| Material | Mean service time | Success |
|---|---|---|
| CIO | 14.5 yr | 92.1% |
| CGPC | 14.9 yr | 84.2% |

### Annual Failure Rates

| Material | Annual failure rate |
|---|---|
| CIO | 0.5%/yr |
| CGPC | 0.7%/yr |

### Complications

| Complication | CIO (n=149) | CGPC (n=147) |
|---|---|---|
| Ceramic fracture | 6.7% | — |
| Endodontic complication | 2.7% | 8.8% |
| Secondary caries | 4.7% | 4.8% |
| Tooth fracture | 1.3% | — |
| Decementation | — | 2.0% |

**Key finding**: Endodontically treated teeth restored with CIO or CGPC had significantly less success vs vital teeth (p=.02).

**Quality**: CIOs 71.8% (107/149) and CGPCs 68% (100/147) rated good/excellent with no significant difference between material types.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single center, single operator type (supervised undergraduates) — may not generalize to specialist/private practice
- No randomization (retrospective cohort design)
- CIO material = leucite-reinforced glass ceramic (Empress I, now largely replaced by lithium disilicate) — not modern LD ceramic
- Dropout not reported; 325 patients allocated vs 296 assessed implies some attrition
- Primary-care level technique may underestimate materials' potential

## 7. Glossary

- **CGPC**: Cast Gold Partial Crown — conventional precious metal alloy partial coverage restoration
- **CIO**: Ceramic In-/Onlay — indirect ceramic partial coverage restoration
- **USPHS criteria**: US Public Health Service evaluation criteria for direct and indirect restorations
- **Degunorm**: Gold alloy for cast restorations (DeguDent/Dentsply Sirona)
- **IPS Empress I**: Leucite-reinforced pressable glass ceramic (Ivoclar Vivadent, introduced 1991)
