## Why Ingested
Demonstrates that strict protocols (under-preparation, socket grafting/sealing, delayed loading) achieve ≥95% success even when primary stability is entirely absent — challenges the dogma that primary stability is always mandatory. Directly reinforces [[implants/survival/cobo-vazquez-2018-effect-lack-primary-stability-survival]] on the same clinical question with a larger, multi-system dataset.

## Three-line Summary
Retrospective study of 97 implants (Neodent GM Helix, Biotem AR, MegaGen AnyOne) placed without primary stability (IT <10 N·cm or intraoperative spinning) in 63 patients at a private practice in Qazvin, Iran (2021–2024).
Overall success rate was 95.9% (93/97; 95% CI 89.9–98.4%), with immediate placements achieving 100% (23/23) vs 94.6% (70/74) for delayed, and no significant difference among implant systems (p=0.91).
Strict case selection, conservative loading, and modern bioactive surfaces can compensate for absent primary stability in selected cases, though the authors do not advocate replacing primary stability as a general practice.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First multi-system retrospective (Neodent GM Helix, Biotem AR, MegaGen AnyOne) demonstrating ≥95% success with IT <10 N·cm across three implant brands in a single private practice
- Immediate placements (n=23, all with grafting/sealing) achieved 100% success despite absent primary stability — no late failures observed
- Controlled systemic conditions (diabetes, hypertension, hypothyroidism) did not increase failure risk (p=0.61)
- All four early failures (≤4 weeks) occurred exclusively in posterior delayed placements, not in anterior or immediate sites

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Retrospective chart review
- **Setting**: Single private periodontal practice, Qazvin, Iran
- **Period**: January 2021 – September 2024
- **n**: 63 patients, 97 implants (Neodent GM Helix n=35, Biotem AR n=39, MegaGen AnyOne n=23)
- **Definition of no primary stability**: Insertion torque <10 N·cm and/or observable spinning during cover-screw placement
- **Outcomes**: Implant success per modified Albrektsson/Buser criteria (no mobility, no pain/suppuration/infection, no peri-implant radiolucency, in function ≥6 months post-loading)
- **Follow-up**: >6 months post-loading for 88 implants (90.7%); exactly 6 months for 9 implants (9.3%)
- **Statistics**: Wilson 95% CI, Fisher's exact and χ² tests

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Retrospective design from a single private practice by a single operator — generalizability limited
- No ISQ/RFA data; primary stability assessed only by tactile/clinical criteria (insertion torque <10 N·cm, spinning)
- No bone quality (Lekholm-Zarb) distribution reported, though under-preparation was used for type III/IV sites
- 6-month follow-up minimum — long-term survival data unavailable
- All implants two-stage (submerged) — results may not apply to one-stage or immediate-loading protocols

## 7. Glossary
- **Primary Stability**: Mechanical anchorage of a dental implant in bone at placement, measured by insertion torque or ISQ
- **Insertion Torque (IT)**: Rotational force required to seat an implant; <10 N·cm defined as absence of primary stability in this study
- **Secondary (Biological) Stability**: Stability achieved through new bone formation at the implant surface during healing
- **Modified Albrektsson/Buser Criteria**: Success criteria requiring no mobility, no pain/suppuration, no peri-implant radiolucency, implant in function ≥6 months post-loading
- **Two-stage (Submerged) Protocol**: Implant placed and covered with mucosa, uncovered at second-stage surgery (~3 months)
- **ISQ (Implant Stability Quotient)**: Resonance frequency analysis-derived measure of implant stability (not used in this study)
- **Lekholm-Zarb Classification**: Bone quality classification (type I–IV) based on cortical and trabecular bone density
