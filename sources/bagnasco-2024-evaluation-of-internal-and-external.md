## Abstract

Background: Full-arch immediate loading rehabilitations are now a widely used rehabilitation method that guarantees predictable medium- and long-term results. Numerous factors can influence its success and stability in the medium and long term. Among these the implant-abutment connection seems to play an important role, however there is still little information on which is the most suitable in this type of treatment.

Purpose: The aim of the present multicenter split-mouth controlled trial is to evaluate whether external hexagonal connections (EHC) and internal hexagonal connections (IHC) can influence success, bone resorption and peri-implant parameters in immediate-load full-arch rehabilitations.

Materials and Methods: Twenty patients were rehabilitated with immediately loaded fixed full-arch rehabilitations. All the implants presented the same macro- and micro-topography but different implant-abutment connection. IHC were used in one randomly selected side of the jaw and EHC was used in the other side. Outcome measures were implant survival rate, peri-implant marginal bone loss (MBL), plaque index (PI), probing depth (PD), and bleeding on probing (BoP) evaluated at 3, 6, 12, 36, and 72-month post-loading. Technical and biological complications were recorded.

Results: In 20 patients, 43 EHC and 40 IHC implants were placed. Between 32 and 72 months of follow-up two patients withdrew (died) and no implants were lost. The cumulative implant survival rate was 100% for both groups. Mean MBL at 72 months was -2.0 mm (SD 0.8) for EHC and -1.9 mm (SD 0.7) for IHC (p=0.608). No statistically significant differences were found between the two groups at any time point for MBL, PD, BoP, or PI. MBL had a gradual increase throughout the follow-up period. Between 0 and 12 months it increased slightly to a value of 1.6 mm for EHC and 1.7 for IHC. This value increased slightly to a resorption of 2 mm in the EHC group and 1.9 mm in the IHC group at 72 months of follow-up.

Conclusions: Both external and internal hexagonal connections showed excellent and comparable clinical outcomes in immediately loaded full-arch rehabilitations over a 6-year follow-up period. The type of implant-abutment connection did not significantly influence implant survival, marginal bone loss, or peri-implant health parameters.

## Introduction

Immediately loaded full-arch rehabilitations allow aesthetic, phonetic, masticatory, and social functions to be re-established in 24-48 h, with high patient satisfaction. Research has demonstrated the effectiveness and long-term sustainability of this treatment modality. The success criteria have evolved over time, with marginal bone loss (MBL) gaining importance alongside osseointegration. The implant-abutment connection design may influence force distribution, screw loosening, and microbiological leakage. External hexagon (EHC) was the first connection introduced but showed issues with force distribution and screw loosening. Internal hexagon connections (IHC) were developed to address these limitations. In vitro studies show more beneficial outcomes with IHC, but clinical evidence in immediate-load full-arch is limited.

## Materials and Methods

### Study Design
Multicenter, randomized, split-mouth controlled trial with 6-year follow-up. Registered protocol.

### Participants
- 20 patients (completely edentulous or with terminal dentition)
- Immediately loaded fixed full-arch rehabilitations
- Split-mouth: IHC on one randomly selected side, EHC on contralateral side
- Same macro- and micro-topography for both implant types

### Interventions
- **EHC group**: External hexagonal connection implants
- **IHC group**: Internal hexagonal connection implants
- Immediate loading protocol (provisional prosthesis within 24-48 h)
- Definitive prosthesis at 3-6 months

### Outcome Measures
**Primary**: Implant survival rate, marginal bone loss (MBL)
**Secondary**: Plaque index (PI), probing depth (PD), bleeding on probing (BoP), technical/biological complications

### Follow-up Schedule
3, 6, 12, 36, and 72 months post-loading

### MBL Measurement
Intraoral digital periapical radiographs with long parallel cone technique. Implant-abutment interface as reference. Digital software (OrisWin DG) calibrated using implant diameter.

### Statistical Analysis
- Poisson regression for implant failure at patient level
- Kaplan-Meier for survival probability comparison
- Linear mixed model with random intercept for longitudinal MBL, PD, BoP, PI

## Results

### Patient and Implant Numbers
- 20 patients enrolled
- 43 EHC implants, 40 IHC implants placed
- 2 patients withdrew (died) between 32-72 months
- No implant losses in either group

### Implant Survival
- **Cumulative implant survival rate: 100% for both EHC and IHC at 72 months**
- No statistically significant difference (Kaplan-Meier)

### Marginal Bone Loss (MBL)
| Time | EHC (mm) | IHC (mm) | p-value |
|------|----------|----------|---------|
| 3 mo | -1.1 (0.7) | -1.1 (0.8) | 0.868 |
| 6 mo | -1.3 (0.8) | -1.4 (0.8) | 0.541 |
| 12 mo | -1.6 (0.8) | -1.7 (0.8) | 0.565 |
| 36 mo | -1.7 (0.7) | -1.9 (0.7) | 0.355 |
| 72 mo | -2.0 (0.8) | -1.9 (0.7) | 0.608 |

- **No statistically significant difference at any time point**
- Gradual increase from baseline to 72 months
- EHC showed slightly lower MBL from 6-50 months; IHC showed slightly lower from 50-72 months (non-significant)

### Peri-implant Parameters (72 months)
| Parameter | EHC | IHC | p-value |
|-----------|-----|-----|---------|
| PD (mm) | 2.0 (1.2) | 1.9 (1.0) | 0.836 |
| BoP (0-4) | 1.7 (0.9) | 1.6 (1.1) | 0.732 |
| PI (0-4) | 2.2 (1.2) | 2.2 (1.3) | 0.878 |

- No significant differences at any time point

### Complications
- **Biological**: No peri-implantitis cases reported
- **Technical**: Screw loosening, framework fractures recorded but no significant difference between groups
- During 36-72 month period: no biological nor prosthetic complications in either group

## Discussion

Both EHC and IHC demonstrated excellent long-term outcomes (100% survival at 6 years) in immediately loaded full-arch rehabilitations. The lack of significant difference in MBL (2.0 vs 1.9 mm at 6 years) suggests connection type does not critically influence bone remodeling in this clinical scenario.

Previous in vitro studies favored IHC for reduced microbial leakage and screw loosening, but this clinical trial did not translate those findings into significant clinical differences. The split-mouth design controlled for patient-level confounders. MBL progression pattern (rapid first year, then gradual) aligns with literature.

Limitations: Small sample size (n=20), single implant system (Sweden & Martina), split-mouth design may have carry-over effects, no power calculation reported.

## Conclusions

In immediately loaded full-arch rehabilitations, both external and internal hexagonal connections yield comparable and excellent clinical outcomes over 6 years: 100% implant survival, similar MBL (~2 mm), and comparable peri-implant health. Connection type does not appear to be a determining factor for success in this protocol.

## Key Points
- RCT, split-mouth, 20 patients, 6-year follow-up
- 43 EHC vs 40 IHC implants, same topography
- 100% survival both groups at 72 months
- MBL at 72 mo: EHC 2.0 mm, IHC 1.9 mm (p=0.608)
- No differences in PD, BoP, PI at any time
- No peri-implantitis; technical complications similar
- Connection type not a critical factor for all-on-x immediate loading success
