## Why Ingested

First TMJ-wiki page on psoriatic arthritis (PsA) — a third inflammatory-arthritis entity alongside rheumatoid arthritis ([[tmj/tenorio-2026-ultrasonography-tmj-rheumatoid-arthritis-scoping-review]]) and juvenile idiopathic arthritis ([[tmj/pawlaczyk-kamienska-2026-facial-asymmetry-mandibular-hypomobility-jia-tmj]]). Fills a gap: none of the 68 existing TMJ pages covered PsA. Its mechanistic account (PsA as an enthesitis/bone-marrow-edema-driven spondyloarthropathy) contrasts with the synovium-driven degenerative model in [[tmj/song-2023-understanding-tmj-osteoarthritis-pathophysiology]] and [[tmj/kim-2023-treatment-tmj-osteoarthritis]], and its conservative/pharmacological therapy discussion is a plausible companion to [[tmj/aiello-2025-tmj-arthritis-rheumatic-diseases-rehabilitative-pain-relief-sr]] (whose included studies explicitly span rheumatic-disease TMJ arthritis, PsA among them).

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (no systematic search protocol beyond a keyword search across PubMed/Web of Science/Scopus through September 2023, no risk-of-bias assessment) on psoriatic arthritis (PsA) involving the TMJ: pathogenesis, diagnostic signs, therapy, and a tentative look at sex/gender differences.

Pathogenesis is HLA-linked (HLA-B27, HLA-Cw6, IL23R) with TNF-α/IL-17/IL-23-driven synovial hypoxia-angiogenesis-erosion cascade and enthesitis/proliferative bone lesions as a PsA-typical radiologic differentiator from osteoarthritis (central-only erosion) and rheumatoid arthritis (faster degeneration course); the authors state outright that epidemiological data on TMJ involvement in PsA "are hard to find" and that few studies address therapeutic management or quality-of-life impact.

Therapy is pharmacology-first (NSAIDs/steroids → methotrexate → anti-TNF/IL-12/23/IL-17 biologics) with surgery (arthrocentesis, arthroplasty, condylotomy, custom TMJ replacement) reserved for refractory severe cases; the paper's practical conclusion is that PsA-TMJ needs multidisciplinary coordination (rheumatology/dermatology/dentistry, plus gynecology in reproductive-age or pregnant women) for diagnosis and management — a referral signal, not a splint-and-wait situation.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First synthesis in this wiki of PsA's TMJ pathogenesis as an enthesitis/bone-marrow-edema-driven, HLA- and cytokine- (TNF-α, IL-17, IL-23) mediated process, distinct from the synovium-centric degenerative cascade described for TMJ osteoarthritis.
- States a specific radiological differentiator versus osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis: in OA, erosions localize centrally on the condyle; in PsA, erosive lesions can occur centrally *or* at the articular-surface margins, with proliferative bone lesions/enthesitis a PsA-typical sign; RA is distinguished by a faster degeneration course (per Wang et al., as cited).
- Lays out a therapy ladder (pharmacological first-line NSAIDs/steroids → methotrexate for moderate-severe disease → biologics [anti-TNF: infliximab, etanercept, adalimumab, golimumab; IL-12/23: ustekinumab; IL-17: brodalumab] → surgery [arthrocentesis, arthroplasty, condylotomy, custom total TMJ replacement] as last resort).
- Explicitly names the evidentiary gap: "epidemiological data on the involvement of the TMJ are hard to find," and few studies address therapeutic management or quality-of-life impact — the paper is candid about its own limits rather than overstating what a narrative review of scarce literature can support.
- Frames PsA-TMJ management as inherently multidisciplinary (rheumatology, dermatology, dentistry, and — for reproductive-age/pregnant women — gynecology/obstetrics), given medication safety concerns in pregnancy/breastfeeding and disease-course variability with hormonal state.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Narrative/comprehensive review. Databases: PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus. Search terms: "Psoriasis", "Psoriatic arthritis", "Temporomandibular joint", "Temporomandibular disorders", "Pathogenesis", "Gender differences". English-language items published through September 2023.
- Inclusion: clinical studies, epidemiological studies, reviews/systematic reviews, case series, case reports. Exclusion: conference proceedings, journal abstracts, non-English articles.
- Two independent reviewers (S.B., D.G.) screened items; disagreements resolved by a third author (S.M.). Where information was missing/unclear, corresponding authors of source studies were contacted; items were excluded if clarification was unavailable.
- No PRISMA flow diagram, no formal risk-of-bias/quality assessment, no meta-analysis — consistent with a narrative rather than systematic review despite the structured search/screening steps described.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Epidemiology**: PsA affects ~30% of psoriasis patients; general-population PsA prevalence 0.1–1%; joint involvement (any) present in ~25% of psoriasis/PsA patients combined. TMJ-specific epidemiological data are explicitly stated to be scarce/"hard to find" — no TMJ-specific prevalence figure is given in the retrieved text.
- **Pathogenesis cascade** (general PsA, extended to TMJ): HLA loci (HLA-B27, HLA-Cw6) + environmental triggers → synovial angiogenesis (tortuous, dilated, immature vessels) → tissue hypoxia despite increased vascularity → cytokine release (IL-17, IL-22, IL-23) → fibroblast-like synoviocyte proliferation/invasion (a "tumor-like" pattern) → cartilage fibrillation/chondrocyte apoptosis + osteoclast-mediated bone erosion at articular margins. IL-22 also implicated in osteoblastic bone proliferation (Wnt-3a, BMP4 up-regulation) — the coexistence of erosive and proliferative bone change is a PsA hallmark.
- **Diagnostic signs (TMJ-specific)**: arthralgia, crepitus, rarely bilateral ankylosis; nonspecific accompaniments include orofacial pain, headache, masticatory myalgia, sleep disturbance. Imaging: cortical erosion, joint-space narrowing, flattening of articular surfaces, osteophytes; CT for bone detail, MRI for soft tissue/bone marrow edema. Radiological differentiator from OA/RA as above.
- **Sex/gender data — general PsA (not TMJ-specific unless noted)**: literature on overall PsA sex ratio is contradictory (Love et al.: female > male in an Icelandic cohort; Nossent et al.: male > female) — the paper does not resolve this. Males reportedly show more peripheral-joint disease; females more axial-pattern disease (spine, sacroiliac joints, **and TMJ is grouped into this axial category by the authors**, without an independent TMJ-specific dataset cited for that grouping). Females report higher pain/fatigue/psychological distress; HLA-B27 (axial-associated) reported more prevalent in males. One cited cohort (Wilson et al.): mean age 43.3 ± 17.1 years, 50% female — no TMJ-specific breakdown given.
- **Sex/gender data — TMD in general (not PsA-specific)**: the paper leans on general-TMD sex-difference literature to support its "TMJ is likely no exception" framing — Stinson et al. (estrogen affects condylar morphology/health), Hauru et al. (low estradiol → altered proteoglycan levels in condylar cartilage, via FTIR imaging), Christidis et al. (higher masseter serotonin-receptor expression in women, proposed pain-sensitivity mechanism), Quinelato et al. (estrogen acts on bone/pain/serotonin receptors). None of these four studies are PsA-specific; they establish general TMD sex-hormone biology that the authors extrapolate to the PsA-TMJ case rather than report as direct PsA-TMJ findings.
- **Net assessment**: no PMJ-TMJ-specific sex-stratified epidemiological or outcome dataset is reported anywhere in the retrieved full text. The gender-difference material is either (a) general-PsA data with TMJ verbally appended to the "axial" category, or (b) general-TMD sex-hormone biology applied by extrapolation. This is consistent with the title's own hedge, "eventual gender differences."

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative review: no PRISMA flow, no risk-of-bias grading, no pooled/quantitative synthesis; study-selection transparency is limited to a two-reviewer/third-tiebreak description.
- Authors' own admission: TMJ-specific epidemiological data on PsA involvement are hard to find; few studies exist on therapeutic management or quality-of-life impact — the review can support pathogenesis/diagnostic-sign/clinical-awareness claims but not prevalence, diagnostic-accuracy, or treatment-effect-size claims.
- Sex/gender section is explicitly the paper's own future-research priority: gender-specific biomarkers, gender-specific treatment guidelines, large-scale trials with female-focused endpoints, and hormonal-intervention (e.g., HRT) studies on TMJ symptoms are all named as unmet needs — not established findings.
- No discussion found in the retrieved text of TMJ-PsA prevalence estimates, treatment-outcome effect sizes, or comparative accuracy of diagnostic imaging modalities beyond qualitative description.

## 7. Glossary

- **PsA (Psoriatic Arthritis)**: 건선관절염 — chronic autoimmune inflammatory arthritis associated with psoriasis.
- **TMJ (Temporomandibular Joint)**: 측두하악관절.
- **Enthesitis**: 부착부염 — inflammation at tendon/ligament bone-insertion sites; a pathogenetic and radiological hallmark distinguishing spondyloarthropathies (including PsA) from synovium-centric arthritides like RA.
- **HLA-B27 / HLA-Cw6**: 사람백혈구항원 아형 — genetic susceptibility loci for PsA, HLA-B27 specifically linked to axial-pattern disease.
- **TNF-α (Tumor Necrosis Factor alpha)**: 종양괴사인자-알파 — pro-inflammatory cytokine central to PsA synovitis and a biologic-therapy target.
- **IL-17 / IL-23**: 인터루킨-17/23 — pro-inflammatory cytokines driving PsA's osteoclast-mediated bone erosion; also biologic-therapy targets (IL-17: brodalumab; IL-12/23: ustekinumab).
- **DMARDs (Disease-Modifying Antirheumatic Drugs)**: 질병조절 항류마티스제 — e.g., methotrexate; pregnancy/breastfeeding safety is a named management consideration.
- **PASI (Psoriasis Area Severity Index)**: 건선 중증도 평가지수 — used in the paper to link skin-disease severity to TMJ-arthritis risk.
- **Arthrocentesis / Arthroplasty / Condylotomy**: TMJ 관절강세정술 / 관절성형술 / 과두절단술 — surgical options ordered from minimally invasive to most invasive for refractory PsA-TMJ disease.
