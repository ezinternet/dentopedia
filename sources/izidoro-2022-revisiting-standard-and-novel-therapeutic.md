## Why Ingested

Comprehensive narrative review covering ISBOR classification of halitosis, its multifactorial etiology, and both standard and novel treatments (mechanical, chemical, probiotics, VSC neutralization); complements the epidemiological scope of [[halitosis/silva-2017-estimated-prevalence-halitosis-sr]] and the drug-induced halitosis work in [[halitosis/mortazavi-2020-drug-related-halitosis-systematic]] by providing a clinically actionable treatment framework.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of halitosis etiology (ISBOR classification: genuine/delusional; 85–90% intra-oral origin) and treatments (mechanical > chemical > probiotics) with emphasis on multidisciplinary management for extra-oral causes.

## 세줄요약

ISBOR 분류(진성/망상성 구취; 85–90% 구강 내 원인)와 기계적·화학적·프로바이오틱 치료법을 다루는 종합 서술적 리뷰로, 구강 외 원인에는 다학제적 접근이 필요함을 강조한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **ISBOR classification framework**: Organizes halitosis as genuine (physiological / pathological intra-oral or extra-oral) vs. delusional (pseudohalitosis / halitophobia), providing a clinician-friendly decision tree.
2. **Epidemiological anchor**: Prevalence 2.4–78%; 85–90% intra-oral origin; tongue coating the dominant single contributor.
3. **Four-phase intra-oral treatment ladder**: (1) mechanical reduction → (2) chemical reduction → (3) VSC neutralization → (4) masking, with tongue scrapers shown to achieve 75% VSC reduction vs. 45% with toothbrush.
4. **Chemical treatment hierarchy**: Chlorhexidine (CHX) 0.2% = gold standard (43% VSC reduction, 50% organoleptic score reduction); CHX + CPC + zinc lactate combination produces synergistic effect.
5. **Probiotics evidence**: Streptococcus salivarius K12, Lactobacillus salivarius, and Weissella cibaria show potential for sustained VSC inhibition as adjuvant therapy.
6. **Extra-oral multidisciplinary pathway**: ENT, gastroenterologist, endocrinologist, and clinical psychologist involvement indicated by origin; halitophobia requires psychiatric referral.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Study design**: Comprehensive narrative review (not systematic); no protocol registration reported.
- **Classification system used**: International Society for Breath Odor Research (ISBOR) taxonomy.
- **Scope**: Etiology (intra-oral and extra-oral), risk factors (behavioral, xerostomia, medication), and treatment (mechanical, chemical, probiotic, VSC transformation, masking, extra-oral management).
- **VSC focus**: H₂S and methyl mercaptan account for ~90% of VSC in bad breath; dimethyl sulfide minor component; anaerobic Gram-negative bacteria (T. denticola, P. gingivalis, F. nucleatum, P. intermedia, etc.) are primary producers.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Treatment modality | Efficacy metric | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Tongue scraper vs. toothbrush | VSC reduction | 75% vs. 45% |
| CHX 0.2% | VSC reduction (daytime) | 43% |
| CHX 0.2% | Organoleptic score reduction | 50% |
| CHX + CPC + zinc lactate (0.05%/0.05%/0.14%) | Organoleptic score | Significant reduction (Cochrane) |
| Zinc (0.3%) + CHX (0.025%) | H₂S drop at 3 h | 0.75% |
| Essential oils (Listerine) | Odor reduction duration | ~25% for 3 h |
| Chlorine dioxide | Odor reduction at 4 h | 29% |

- Intra-oral causes: tongue coating > periodontal disease > odontogenic factors.
- Extra-oral causes: ENT ~10%; gastrointestinal/endocrine ~5%; GI tract alone only ~0.5% of all halitosis.
- Tonsilloliths confer 10-fold increased risk of abnormal VSC levels.
- Xerostomia (hyposalivation) — most common cause is medication (anticholinergics, antihistamines, antipsychotics, anxiolytics, antidepressants, antihypertensives, diuretics, narcotics); ~25% of elderly affected.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative review design limits systematic evidence appraisal; no PRISMA or quality scoring of included studies.
- Authors note inconsistencies in existing evidence and call for robust RCTs comparing standard vs. innovative therapies (especially probiotics and novel VSC neutralizers).
- Probiotic evidence is largely in vitro and in vivo pilot studies; clinical translation protocols not established.
- CHX long-term use limited by tooth staining and taste; alternatives with equivalent efficacy lacking strong RCT support.

## 7. Glossary

- **VSC (Volatile Sulfur Compounds, 휘발성 황화합물)**: H₂S, methyl mercaptan (CH₃SH), dimethyl sulfide; primary odorants in halitosis.
- **ISBOR (International Society for Breath Odor Research)**: Organization providing canonical halitosis classification.
- **Genuine halitosis (진성 구취)**: Objectively detectable oral malodor exceeding socially acceptable level; divided into physiological and pathological subtypes.
- **Pseudohalitosis (가성 구취)**: Patient-perceived malodor not detected by examiners; resolves with counseling.
- **Halitophobia (구취 공포증)**: Persistent conviction of bad breath despite negative clinical findings; requires psychiatric management.
- **CHX (Chlorhexidine, 클로르헥시딘)**: Gold-standard antibacterial mouthrinse for halitosis; mechanism: disrupts bacterial cell membranes.
- **CPC (Cetylpyridinium Chloride, 세틸피리디늄 클로라이드)**: Quaternary ammonium antiseptic; synergistic with CHX and zinc.
- **Tonsillolith (편도 결석)**: Calcified deposits in tonsillar crypts; 10-fold increased VSC risk.
- **Fetor hepaticus (간성 구취)**: Sweet excremental breath in hepatic failure from lung excretion of waste metabolites.
- **Trimethylaminuria (트리메틸아민뇨증, "fish odour syndrome")**: Genetic disorder causing fishy breath/body odor; dietary and antibiotic management.
