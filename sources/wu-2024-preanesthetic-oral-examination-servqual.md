## Why Ingested

First patient-experience evaluation of a phase-III interdisciplinary quality improvement program (preanesthetic oral examination, PAOE) designed to prevent perioperative dental injuries — quantifying the service-quality cost patients perceive when a safety program adds a dental step before general anesthesia. It operationalizes SERVQUAL (perceptions only) with path analysis in dentistry, complementing the satisfaction-dimension framework in [[wiki/behavioral-dentistry/patient-reported-outcomes/ebnahmady-2015-patient-satisfaction-surveys-dental-school]] and the patient-safety program perspective in [[wiki/practice-management/padmanabhan-2024-patient-safety-dentistry-narrative]].

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional SERVQUAL survey (BMC Oral Health 2024; n=416: 286 PAOE vs 130 control; Cronbach's α=0.861) at Kaohsiung Medical University Hospital found PAOE patients scored lower on reliability (β=-0.074, P=0.003), responsiveness (β=-0.148, P=0.006), and empathy (β=-0.140, P=0.011) than voluntary dental patients.

Reliability (β=0.655, P<0.001) and responsiveness (β=0.147, P=0.008) directly drove overall satisfaction; absolute satisfaction remained high in both groups (PAOE 6.17±1.208 vs control 6.39±0.792 on a 7-point scale), and 83.9% of PAOE patients agreed the pre-anesthesia oral exam was necessary.

Conclusion: an interdisciplinary perioperative dental-injury prevention program slightly lowers perceived service quality but is a "win-win" for patient safety and malpractice-claim reduction; waiting-time management and patient-centered education are the improvement levers.

## 세줄요약

단면적 SERVQUAL 설문(BMC Oral Health 2024; n=416: 마취전 구강검사(PAOE)군 286명 vs 대조군 130명, Cronbach's α=0.861)에서 PAOE군이 신뢰성(β=-0.074, P=0.003), 응답성(β=-0.148, P=0.006), 공감(β=-0.140, P=0.011) 차원에서 유의하게 낮았다.

신뢰성(β=0.655, P<0.001)과 응답성(β=0.147, P=0.008)이 환자 만족도에 직접 효과를 냈고, 절대 만족도는 양군 모두 높게 유지되었다(PAOE 6.17±1.208 vs 대조 6.39±0.792, 7점 척도), PAOE 필요성 동의는 83.9%.

학제간 치과손상 예방 프로그램은 지각된 서비스 품질을 소폭 낮추지만 영향은 제한적 — 환자 안전·의료소송 감소의 윈윈 전략이며, 개선 레버는 대기시간 관리와 환자 중심 교육이다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First satisfaction/service-quality assessment of a **phase-III PAOE program** (extended Nov 2018 to all general-anesthesia patients) where prior phases I–II were evaluated only by dental-injury incidence
- Modified SERVQUAL (22 items, 5 dimensions, 7-point Likert, **perceptions only** — expectation–perception gaps not measured), α=0.861
- Path-analysis model (AMOS 26) linking patient type → SERVQUAL dimensions → satisfaction with excellent fit (CFI=0.996, TLI=0.989, SRMR=0.038, RMSEA=0.031)
- Identifies reliability and responsiveness as the two satisfaction-driving dimensions — i.e., waiting time and information/communication, consistent with preoperative-clinic literature
- Documents clinical yield of PAOE: tooth mobility 13.3%, fixation 8.4% among 286 referred surgical patients

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Program context**: surgeon refers elective GA patients to family dentistry; intern examines → resident double-checks → visiting staff triple-checks; mobile teeth get wire fixation or extraction; mobility grade uploaded to Hospital Information System. Dental injury incidence fell 0.0255% (Nov 2016–Oct 2018) → 0.0144% (Nov 2018–Jul 2020)
- **Groups**: PAOE referrals (n=286) vs voluntary family-dentistry attendees (n=130); 416 valid questionnaires after excluding incomplete/reverse-item errors
- **Instrument**: demographics + perspectives (satisfaction, recommendation, PAOE necessity; 7-point Likert) + adapted SERVQUAL perceptions (tangibles 4, reliability 5, responsiveness 4, assurance 4, empathy 5 items; 9 reverse-scored)
- **Analysis**: χ² for categorical comparisons, independent t-test for dimension scores (SPSS 20); path analysis with fit cutoffs CFI/TLI ≥0.95, SRMR <0.08, RMSEA <0.06 (AMOS 26); medical-university students excluded from path model

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Satisfaction**: total 6.24±1.093; PAOE 6.17±1.208 vs control 6.39±0.792. "Agree" (5–7): 82.9% PAOE vs 97.7% control (P<0.001); no-response 7.0% vs 0%
- **Recommendation**: total 6.16±1.266; agree 80.4% vs 94.6% (P<0.001). **PAOE necessity**: 83.9% agree, 9.8% non-committal/disagree, 6.3% no answer
- **SERVQUAL totals (max)**: tangibles 25.28 (28), reliability 31.93 (35), responsiveness 23.40 (28), assurance 25.37 (28), empathy 30.75 (35); means 6.32/6.39/5.85/6.34/6.15 — responsiveness lowest
- **Group contrasts (PAOE vs control)**: tangibles 25.07±3.44 vs 25.74±2.85 (P=0.056 NS); reliability 31.47±4.42 vs 32.92±3.15 (P<0.001); responsiveness 22.45±7.00 vs 25.48±4.48 (P<0.001); assurance 25.05±4.31 vs 26.05±2.57 (P=0.004); empathy 29.81±7.56 vs 32.82±4.61 (P<0.001)
- **Path coefficients**: PAOE→reliability β=-0.074 (P=0.003), PAOE→responsiveness β=-0.148 (P=0.006), PAOE→empathy β=-0.140 (P=0.011); reliability→satisfaction β=0.655 (P<0.001), responsiveness→satisfaction β=0.147 (P=0.008); older age→lower responsiveness (β=-0.122, P=0.038), higher education→higher responsiveness (β=0.155, P=0.011)
- **Model fit**: χ²=19.765, df=15, P=0.181, SRMR=0.038, RMSEA=0.031 (90% CI 0.000–0.065), CFI=0.996, TLI=0.989
- **PAOE clinical characteristics**: top referral sources otolaryngology 29.7% and OB/GYN 15.0%; first surgery at KMUH 71.0%; mobility 13.3%, fixation 8.4%

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Convenience sampling → potential sample bias
- Perceptions-only design: expectation–perception gaps (original SERVQUAL gap scores) not assessed
- Control group younger, more educated, less employed — sociodemographic confounding handled via path covariates but residual imbalance possible
- Future work named by authors: reasonable waiting times, patient-centered education on perioperative dental-injury risk, interprofessional education in continuing and undergraduate programs

## 6. Related Work

- Kuo et al. 2016 (J Chin Med Assoc) — phases I–II QI program: injury rate 0.108% → 0.051% → 0.009% over 64,718 anesthetics; this paper extends to phase III patient experience
- Pakdil & Harwood 2005 — SERVQUAL in a preoperative assessment clinic; same finding that communication is most positive and waiting time most negative
- Japanese perioperative oral management literature (fee schedule 2012; Shin 2019; Kurasawa 2020, 2022) — reduced postoperative pneumonia/mortality/costs, the outcome-side counterpart to PAOE's injury-prevention side
- Parasuraman, Zeithaml & Berry 1988 — original SERVQUAL instrument

## 7. Glossary

- **PAOE (Preanesthetic Oral Examination)**: dentist-performed oral exam before general anesthesia to detect/manage vulnerable teeth and reduce perioperative dental injury
- **SERVQUAL**: Parasuraman et al. service-quality instrument — 22 items across tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy; originally measures expectation–perception gaps
- **Perioperative dental injury**: dental damage sustained during anesthesia/intubation or during recovery before full return of consciousness; leading anesthesia-related malpractice claim source
- **Path analysis**: structural equation modeling of direct/indirect standardized effects (β) among observed variables
- **Cronbach's α**: internal-consistency reliability coefficient; 0.861 here indicates good consistency
