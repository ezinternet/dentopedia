## Why Ingested

The conventional GBR literature prescribes 3–9 months before re-entry, but the optimal timing remains undetermined. This canine study directly tests whether early re-entry at 2 months (after membrane resorption is complete) compromises bone regeneration compared to conventional 4-month re-entry — filling a gap identified in [[overviews/gbr-simultaneous-implant-placement-contour-dehiscence-overview]] on simultaneous GBR and re-entry timing.

## Three-line Summary

Animal study (6 mongrel dogs, 12 bilateral mandibular buccal dehiscence defects, 5×5×3 mm) randomized to re-entry at 2 months (early) or 4 months (late) after simultaneous GBR and implant placement, euthanized at 5 months for histomorphometric and micro-CT analysis.
No significant differences in new bone area (1.98 vs 0.88 mm², p=0.063), 골-임플란트 접촉률 (Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC) (53.80% vs 54.15%, p>0.999), defect height resolution (68.80% vs 64.80%, p=0.438), or peri-implant mucosal dimensions between groups; early group showed non-significantly higher mucosal volume (75.36 vs 50.05 mm³) and denser soft-tissue collagen.
Early second-stage surgery at 2 months does not compromise GBR outcome and may enable earlier soft-tissue maturation, allowing a more timely progression to the prosthetic phase.

신생골 면적 (1.98 vs 0.88 mm², p=0.063), 골-임플란트 접촉률 (Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC) (53.80% vs 54.15%, p>0.999), 결손 해소율 (68.80% vs 64.80%, p=0.438), 임플란트 주위 점막 두께·높이·부피 모두 두 군 간 통계적 유의차 없음; 조기군에서 점막 부피 비유의 우세 (75.36 vs 50.05 mm³) 및 교원질 밀도 높음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First preclinical study directly comparing early (2-month) vs conventional (4-month) re-entry surgery following simultaneous GBR with submerged implant healing using histomorphometric and 3D micro-CT endpoints.
- Demonstrates that re-entry surgery at the biologically sound 2-month point (after full resorbable collagen membrane resorption, ~6 weeks) does not disrupt ongoing bone regeneration or osseointegration.
- Shows that early re-entry may produce denser, more mature connective tissue architecture in the peri-implant mucosa due to an additional 3-month period of soft-tissue maturation vs only 1 month in the late group.
- Documents one implant failure (fibrin incarceration) in the late group, suggesting that extended submerged healing is not inherently safer.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Randomized, controlled animal experiment (split-mouth design within each dog); ARRIVE 2.0 guidelines
- **Subjects**: 6 male mongrel dogs, 15–24 months old, ~15 kg; bilateral mandibular premolar extraction → 8-week healing → standardized buccal dehiscence defects (5 mm length × 5 mm height × 3 mm depth)
- **Implants**: Bone-level, 3.5 × 8 mm (Superline III, Dentium, Korea), SLA surface; primary stability ≥30 N; submerged healing with cover screws
- **GBR**: EDC-crosslinked collagen membrane (Collagen membrane P, Genoss) + synthetic biphasic calcium phosphate (BCP: 60% HA + 40% β-TCP, Osteon III, Genoss); membrane fixed with 2 titanium bone tacks; periosteal releasing incision + primary closure
- **Groups**: Early re-entry (2 months postop, n=6) vs Late re-entry (4 months postop, n=6); all euthanized 5 months after GBR
- **Outcomes — Hard tissue**: New bone area (mm²), residual graft area (mm²), mineralised tissue area (mm²), BIC (%), first BIC from implant shoulder (fBIC, mm), bone crest-to-implant shoulder distance (BC-S, mm), defect height resolution (%), buccal bone thickness 1–5 mm apical to shoulder
- **Outcomes — Soft tissue**: Mucosal height (mm), mucosal thickness at 0/1/2 mm above platform (mm), junctional epithelium height (mm), 3D mucosal volume by micro-CT (mm³)
- **Statistics**: Wilcoxon signed-rank test (nonparametric paired); p<0.05 significant; 5 matched pairs analyzed (one implant failure in late group excluded)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Hard tissue (early vs late, median [IQR]):**

| Outcome | Early (2 mo) | Late (4 mo) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| New bone area (mm²) | 1.98 [1.53–2.98] | 0.88 [0.09–1.79] | 0.063 |
| Residual graft area (mm²) | 1.55 [0.98–2.39] | 1.80 [1.12–1.88] | >0.999 |
| Mineralised tissue area (mm²) | 3.78 [2.77–4.99] | 2.10 [1.80–3.36] | 0.063 |
| BIC (%) | 53.80 [40.88–63.10] | 54.15 [45.21–66.02] | >0.999 |
| fBIC from shoulder (mm) | 1.61 [1.53–3.11] | 3.44 [2.57–4.60] | 0.063 |
| BC-S (mm) | 1.56 [1.15–2.50] | 1.76 [1.22–2.77] | 0.438 |
| Defect height resolution (%) | 68.80 [50.00–77.00] | 64.80 [44.60–75.60] | 0.438 |

All buccal bone thickness levels (1–5 mm apical): NS. None of the implants in either group was covered by buccal bone at 1 mm apical to the implant shoulder.

| Mucosal thickness at 1 mm (mm) | 1.61 [0.90–1.90] | 0.87 [0.23–1.09] | 0.063 |
| Mucosal thickness at 2 mm (mm) | 0.48 [0–0.91] | 0.00 [0.00] | 0.25 |

| Junctional epithelium height (mm) | 1.93 [1.02–2.13] | 1.35 [0.98–1.39] | 0.125 |
| 3D mucosal volume (mm³) | 75.36 [42.03–86.19] | 50.05 [41.00–68.47] | 0.313 |

**Histology:** Early group — denser, more mature collagen fibers in lamina propria; more complex collagen fiber arrangement parallel and perpendicular to implant long axis. Late group — loose fibrous arrangement, lower density and complexity. Collagen membrane undetectable (fully resorbed) by 5 months in both groups.

**Implant failure:** One implant in the late group failed due to fibrin incarceration (not osseointegrated).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small sample size (n=5 matched pairs after one exclusion); statistical equivalence not confirmed due to wide confidence intervals exceeding the prespecified ±1.0 mm² margin for new bone formation.
- Acute-type standardized defects may not fully replicate heterogeneous chronic clinical conditions.
- No clinical parameters (probing depth, mucosal recession) measured; limits baseline-to-endpoint comparisons.
- Absence of an equivalence test means non-inferiority cannot be formally declared despite consistent trends.
- Animal model; human RCT validation required before clinical protocol changes.
- Bone crest was below the implant shoulder in most specimens, potentially masking intergroup differences in the region most affected by surgical access.

## 7. Glossary

- **GBR (Guided Bone Regeneration, 골유도재생술)**: Technique using a barrier membrane to seclude a bone defect from fibrous tissue invasion, allowing bone-forming cells to populate the defect space.
- **BIC (Bone-to-Implant Contact, 골-임플란트 접촉률)**: Percentage of implant surface directly in contact with bone on histological sections; primary indicator of osseointegration quality.
- **fBIC (First Bone-to-Implant Contact, 최초 골-임플란트 접촉점)**: Distance from the implant shoulder to the most coronal point of bone contact on the buccal aspect; indicator of crestal bone preservation.
- **BCP (Biphasic Calcium Phosphate, 이상성 인산칼슘)**: Synthetic bone substitute composed of 60% hydroxyapatite and 40% β-TCP; osteoconductive scaffold with controlled resorption.
- **EDC crosslinked membrane (EDC 가교 콜라겐 차폐막)**: Collagen membrane crosslinked with 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC); provides bioinert degradation products and extended barrier function (~6 weeks).
- **Fibrin incarceration (섬유소 포위)**: Failure mode where fibrin clot surrounds and isolates the implant, preventing bone contact and osseointegration.
