## Why Ingested

This 1996 case-control study directly tests whether orthodontic extraction of upper second permanent molars causes over-eruption of the now-unopposed lower second permanent molars — a foundational clinical question for the unopposed tooth overeruption topic synthesized in [[wiki/overviews/unopposed-tooth-overeruption-overview]] and addressed empirically in [[wiki/occlusion/craddock-2004-incidence-overeruption-occlusal-interferences-unopposed]]. It provides early quantitative evidence using travelling microscope measurements on study models, and also evaluates whether upper third molar eruption compensates for the lost antagonist.

## Three-line Summary

Case-control study comparing vertical molar positions (travelling microscope on study models) in subjects who had upper second permanent molars extracted orthodontically versus age- and sex-matched controls.
The distal aspect of the lower second permanent molar over-erupted significantly in the extraction group compared to controls (P<0.001 right, P<0.05 left); mesial over-eruption was moderated by the distal position of the upper first permanent molar (P<0.01).
Upper third molar eruption did not prevent lower second molar over-eruption, and most erupted third molars adopted an unfavorable contact position with the first permanent molar, raising concern about orthodontic upper second molar extraction as routine practice.

## 세줄요약

상악 제2대구치 (upper second permanent molar)를 교정적 이유로 발치한 군과 대조군(연령·성별 매칭)에서 주행 현미경 (travelling microscope)으로 연구 모형 상 구치부 수직 위치를 비교한 증례-대조 연구 (case-control study)이다.
발치군에서 하악 제2대구치 (lower second permanent molar) 원심면이 유의하게 정출 (overeruption, P<0.001 우측, P<0.05 좌측)되었으며, 근심면 정출은 상악 제1대구치의 원심 위치에 의해 영향을 받았다(P<0.01).
상악 제3대구치 (upper third molar)의 맹출은 하악 제2대구치 정출을 방지하지 못하였고, 대부분의 제3대구치는 제1대구치와 불량한 접촉 위치(unfavorable contact position)로 맹출하여, 교정적 상악 제2대구치 발치의 일상적 적용에 대한 우려를 제기한다.

## Key Contributions

- Quantitative evidence (travelling microscope on study casts) that orthodontic upper second molar extraction leads to clinically and statistically significant distal over-eruption of the opposing lower second molar
- Demonstrates that the upper first permanent molar's distal position moderates mesial (but not distal) over-eruption of the lower second molar
- Shows upper third molar eruption does NOT compensate for the lost antagonist — most erupt into unfavorable occlusal contact with the first molar
- Establishes a case-control framework for the unopposed-molar overeruption literature, predating larger observational cohort studies

## Methodology

- **Design**: Case-control study
- **Experimental group**: Subjects who had undergone orthodontic extraction of upper second permanent molars as part of a previous course of orthodontic treatment
- **Control group**: Subjects matched for age and sex, without upper second molar extraction
- **Outcome measurement**: Upper and lower impressions → study models → travelling microscope (validated precision instrument; reference: Bhatia & Harrison 1987, BJO 14:147–153) to objectively assess vertical positions of posterior teeth
- **Outcomes**: Vertical position of lower second molar (mesial and distal aspects), influence of upper first molar position, effect of upper third molar eruption status
- **Statistical analysis**: Methods per Dahlberg 1940; P-values reported for group comparisons

## Key Results

| Measurement | Finding | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Distal aspect, lower 2nd molar (right) | Over-erupted in experimental vs control | P < 0.001 |
| Distal aspect, lower 2nd molar (left) | Over-erupted in experimental vs control | P < 0.05 |
| Mesial aspect, lower 2nd molar | Over-eruption moderated by distal position of upper 1st molar | P < 0.01 |
| Upper 3rd molar eruption | Did NOT affect lower 2nd molar over-eruption | Not significant |
| Upper 3rd molar timing | Majority erupted ≥3 years post-extraction | Descriptive |
| Upper 3rd molar position | Majority in unfavorable contact with upper 1st molar | Descriptive |

## Limitations

- Abstract-only access; exact sample sizes (n), mean over-eruption magnitudes, and follow-up duration not determinable from available text
- Author full name unknown from available text (listed only as R. Smith)
- Retrospective design: extraction decisions and timing not controlled; heterogeneity in orthodontic treatment protocols possible
- Travelling microscope measures vertical position on casts, not direct in-vivo radiographic measurement — cannot capture alveolar bone changes
- No data on the magnitude of over-eruption in millimeters visible from the abstract

## Glossary

- **Travelling microscope**: A precision optical measurement instrument used to objectively measure distances on dental casts; validated for this purpose by Bhatia & Harrison (1987)
- **Over-eruption / supraeruption**: Pathologic continued eruption of a tooth beyond the occlusal plane when its antagonist (opposing tooth) is absent
- **Experimental group**: Subjects who had upper second permanent molars extracted as part of orthodontic treatment
- **Distal aspect**: The surface of a tooth facing away from the midline — here, the posterior face of the lower second molar
- **Mesial aspect**: The surface of a tooth facing toward the midline — here, the anterior face of the lower second molar
- **Unfavorable contact position**: Occlusal relationship where the erupted third molar does not provide stable, functional antagonist contact with the opposing tooth
