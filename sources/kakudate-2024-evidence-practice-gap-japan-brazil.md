## Why Ingested

Evidence-practice gap (EPG) 배치 인제스트 — 동일한 National Dental PBRN EPG 측정 도구(3문항)를 일본·브라질 치과의사에게 동시 투여해 국제 비교를 만든 최초 연구. MID(minimal intervention dentistry) 실행률이 국가별로 크게 다르며(전체 일치율 브라질 56% vs 일본 38%), 그 격차의 예측인자가 **정보원의 질**(일본: 영어 학술지 OR 2.44 / 브라질: 인터넷 OR 0.39)이라는 점이 핵심. [[caries/banihani-2021-minimal-intervention-primary-teeth-carious-umbrella-review]]가 "무엇이 근거 있는 MID 기법인가"를 정의한다면, 이 논문은 "그 근거가 실제 진료에 얼마나 적용되고 있는가"의 측정값을 제공해 위키의 MID 스토리에 practice-side 절반을 채운다. [[caries/urquhart-2019-nonrestorative-treatments-caries-network-meta-analysis]]의 비복원적 치료 근거와 함께 읽으면 권고↔실행 간격의 양단이 연결된다.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional web survey of 136 Japanese and 110 Brazilian dentists (Dec 2021–Jun 2022) using a 3-question EPG tool (restorative threshold, deep caries excavation, caries risk assessment) to measure concordance with published MID evidence.

Overall concordance (all 3 answers evidence-consistent) was significantly higher in Brazil (56%, 61/110) than Japan (38%, 51/136; p=0.007); the single largest divergence was caries risk assessment (Brazil 91% vs Japan 68%, p<0.001), while deep-caries management favored Japan numerically (81% vs 75%, NS).

On adjusted logistic regression, frequent use of English-language scientific journals predicted HIGHER overall concordance in Japan (OR 2.44, 95% CI 1.13–5.29), whereas frequent Internet use predicted LOWER concordance in Brazil (OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.16–0.92) — pointing to a "language gap" in Japan and an information-quality gap in Brazil.

## 세줄요약

일본 136명·브라질 110명 치과의사 대상 웹 설명 단면연구(2021.12–2022.6)로, 수복 기준·깊은 우식 처리·우식위험도평가(Caries Risk Assessment, CRA) 3문항으로 최소개입치료(Minimal Intervention Dentistry, MID) 근거 일치율을 측정.

전체 일치율(3문항 모두 근거 부합)은 브라질 56%(61/110) > 일본 38%(51/136, p=0.007); 개별 문항 중 유의한 차이는 CRA뿐(브라질 91% vs 일본 68%, p<0.001), 깊은 우식 관리는 오히려 일본이 우세(81% vs 75%, 유의하지 않음).

보정 로지스틱 회귀에서 일본은 영어 학술지 빈용 시 일치율 상승(OR 2.44, 95% CI 1.13–5.29), 브라질은 인터넷 정보 빈용 시 일치율 하락(OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.16–0.92) — 일본엔 '언어 갭', 브라질엔 '정보 신뢰성 갭'이 EPG의 축.

## 1. Document Information

- **Type**: Cross-sectional study (web-delivered questionnaire survey of practicing dentists)
- **Journal**: Operative Dentistry, 2024 March;49(2):127–135 (NIH author manuscript; PMID 38196080, PMC10984213)
- **Population**: Restorative dentists recruited via JDPBRN websites/e-mails (Japan, 7 regions, response rate 74% = 136/185) and e-mail/social networks (Brazil, 5 regions; denominator indeterminate due to social-media recruitment)
- **Survey window**: December 2021 – June 2022; questionnaires in Japanese/Portuguese (English reference version public on dentalpbrn.jp)
- **Funding**: JSPS KAKENHI 20K10274; NIH U19-DE-22516, U19-DE-28717
- **Conflict of interest**: none declared

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **First same-questionnaire international comparison** of the MID evidence-practice gap between Japan and Brazil, run concomitantly with the instrument originally developed by the US National Dental PBRN (Norton et al., 2014).
2. **Quantifies the gap direction**: Brazil outperforms Japan on overall MID concordance (56% vs 38%) — reversing the naive expectation that high-income research infrastructure implies better evidence uptake.
3. **Identifies information-source signatures**: Japanese dentists rely more on textbooks (92% vs 72%), non-academic journals (67% vs 21%), seminars/workshops (86% vs 58%); Brazilian dentists read English scientific journals far more (82% vs 49%) — all p<0.001.
4. **Proposes two remediable mechanisms**: a "Language Gap" (FSI classifies Japanese as Category IV ≈ 2,200 class-hours to professional English proficiency vs Portuguese Category I ≈ 600–750) driving Japan's EPG, and unreliable Internet information hampering Brazilian dentists.
5. **Documents secular improvement in caries risk assessment (CRA)**: Japan 26% (2011–12) → 68% (2021–22); Brazil 36% (2014–15) → 91% — +42 and +55 percentage points respectively.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional; identical web questionnaire fielded simultaneously in both countries (Helsinki-compliant; informed consent obtained).
- **EPG measurement tool** (modified from Norton et al. 2014, National Dental PBRN Practice Impact Questionnaire):
  - Q1 Restoration diagnosis & treatment — radiographic proximal lesion depth warranting operative restoration instead of preventive/non-surgical care (consistent = restore only into-dentin lesions).
  - Q2 Deep caries diagnosis & treatment — options when excavation goes deeper than anticipated near the mesio-buccal pulp horn of a mandibular first molar (consistent = stop removal near pulp horn and remove decay elsewhere; inconsistent = remove all decay / initiate endodontic treatment).
  - Q3 Caries risk assessment — whether patients are risk-assessed in any way (consistent = yes).
- Each answer coded 1 (consistent with literature) or 0 (inconsistent); **overall concordance** = consistent on all three (primary outcome).
- **Dentist characteristics**: years since graduation, gender, practice type, practice busyness ('busy' vs 'not busy'), and 8 information-source items dichotomized as Higher (Frequently/Sometimes) vs Lower (Rarely/Never) frequency.
- **Statistics**: Chi-square for country comparisons; country-stratified multiple logistic regression (adjusted ORs, 95% CI) for overall concordance; alpha 0.05; SPSS 23; available-case analysis; ~100 participants targeted via the 1:10 events-per-variable rule.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Measure | Japan (n=136) | Brazil (n=110) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 restoration threshold concordant | 63% (86/136) | 73% (80/110) | 0.149 |
| Q2 deep caries concordant | 81% (110/136) | 75% (82/110) | 0.299 |
| Q3 caries risk assessment performed | 68% (93/136) | 91% (98/108) | <0.001 |
| **Overall concordance (all 3)** | **38% (51/136)** | **56% (61/110)** | **0.007** |

(PubMed abstract states Brazil overall as 55%; the manuscript's Table 3 reports 56% — 61/110 = 55.5% rounded.)

- **Information sources (higher-frequency use)**: textbooks JP 92% vs BR 72%; non-academic journals 67% vs 21%; seminars/workshops 86% vs 58% (all p<0.001); English scientific journals JP 49% vs BR 82% (p<0.001); colleague 82% vs 82%; Internet 62% vs 59% (NS).
- **Adjusted logistic regression** (each country; adjusted for graduation year, gender, practice type, busyness):
  - Japan: frequent reading of English scientific journal articles → OR 2.44 (95% CI 1.13–5.29), p=0.024 for higher overall concordance.
  - Brazil: frequent use of Internet information → OR 0.39 (95% CI 0.16–0.92), p=0.032 (lower concordance). Textbook OR 7.18 in Japan but CI crossing 1 (0.85–60.4).
  - Model predictive accuracy 64.7% (Japan), 68.3% (Brazil). Gender not associated in either country.
- **Demographics**: mean years since graduation 18.5±13.7 (JP) vs 16.1±11.3 (BR); male 84% (JP) vs female 71% (BR); self-employed 52% vs 48%; "busy" practices 56% (JP) vs "not busy" 71% (BR).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Sampling bias**: no random sampling; Japan recruited via JDPBRN (network-affiliated volunteers), Brazil denominator unknown because of social-media recruitment.
- **Information bias**: all practice data are self-reports; social desirability may inflate reported concordance/CRA.
- **No causal inference**: cross-sectional design cannot establish that information sources cause concordance differences.
- **Generalizability caveats**: gender structure differs sharply between countries (84% male JP vs 71% female BR), though distributions matched national dentist demographics.
- **Future work**: longitudinal/repeated EPG measurement; a common internationally comparable EPG scale; multilingual studies testing the Language-Gap hypothesis; interventions building critical appraisal of online information (Brazil) and facilitating access to English-language evidence (Japan).

## 7. Glossary

- **EPG (evidence-practice gap)**: the measurable discrepancy between published scientific evidence and clinicians' actual decisions.
- **MID (minimal intervention dentistry)**: philosophy aiming at maximal preservation of healthy tooth structure and lifelong tooth retention — earliest possible lesion control, conservative excavation, prevention-first management (FDI-endorsed).
- **Concordance**: proportion/indicator that a dentist's answer matches the response classified as consistent with the literature; "overall concordance" requires consistency on all three questions.
- **CRA (caries risk assessment)**: structured judgment of a patient's future caries risk used to individualize prevention intensity.
- **PBRN (practice-based research network)**: infrastructure linking community practitioners with researchers to study care as really delivered; JDPBRN = Japan Dental PBRN; National Dental PBRN = US network.
- **Selective caries removal**: stopping excavation at firm/leathery dentine near the pulp instead of removing all softened tissue — the evidence-consistent deep-caries answer in Q2.
