## Why Ingested

Only SR focused specifically on *late* (chronic, ≥1 week post-op) iatrogenic paresthesia treatments in the IAN/LN territory — distinguishing preemptive/adjunctive use from true therapeutic intervention. Fills the "what works for established paresthesia" gap. Links to [[oral-surgery/lee-2015-inferior-alveolar-nerve-injury-management]] for IAN injury context.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 7 RCTs examining therapeutic modalities for late (≥1 week post-procedural) IAN/LN paresthesia following dental procedures.

Photobiomodulation (PBM), vitamin B12, and corticosteroids (prednisolone 3–6 weeks post-op) each showed acceleratory effects on sensory recovery; PBM was superior to PBM acupuncture; no gold standard established.

All included studies had small sample sizes and high methodological risk of bias, limiting the strength of recommendations.

## 세줄요약

치과 시술 후 발생한 만성(≥1주) 하치조신경/설신경 감각이상의 치료 방법에 대한 7개 RCT 체계적 문헌고찰.

광생물조절(PBM), 비타민 B12, 코르티코스테로이드(술 후 3–6주 prednisolone)가 각각 감각 회복을 촉진하는 효과를 보였으며, PBM이 PBM침술보다 우월; 단, 표준 치료법은 미확립.

포함된 연구들의 표본 크기가 작고 방법론적 비뚤림 위험이 높아 권고 수준이 낮음.

## 1. Document Information

- **Type**: Systematic review
- **Journal**: Maxillofacial Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery
- **Year**: 2024
- **DOI**: 10.1186/s40902-024-00438-5
- **PROSPERO**: CRD42023447657
- **Databases searched**: PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science (up to December 4, 2023)
- **Studies included**: 7 RCTs (from 3122 initial hits)

## 2. Key Contributions

- First SR restricted to *late* paresthesia (onset ≥1 week post-procedure) — excludes preemptive/adjunctive treatments, isolating true therapeutic evidence
- Covers all major modalities: PBM, electroacupuncture, vitamin B12 + pyrimidine ribonucleotides, corticosteroids (prednisolone), microsurgery (none met inclusion)
- Risk-of-bias assessed via JBI checklist; randomization, allocation concealment, and data analysis were highest-bias domains
- Concludes PBM, vit. B12, and corticosteroids can accelerate recovery, but evidence quality is low

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **PICO**: Patients with iatrogenic late paresthesia (IAN/LN, ≥1 week post-dental-procedure) | Therapeutic interventions | Control/sham/alternative | Sensory recovery outcomes
- **Inclusion**: RCTs, human subjects, late paresthesia (≥1 week), controlled design
- **Exclusion**: preemptive use, adjunctive-during-procedure use, retrospective/cohort/case reports, no control group
- **Dual independent screening** (EK + MH); disagreements resolved by group discussion
- **Risk of bias**: JBI RCT checklist; visualized with robvis

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Study | Nerve | Cause | Delay | Treatment | Key Finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salari 2022 | IAN | Orthognathic surgery (neurotmesis) | ≥6 months | PBM vs sham (12 sessions/6 wk) | PBM improved light touch & 2-point discrimination; no EPT/thermal difference |
| Oliveira 2021 | IAN | Third molar/implant (NM) | <90 days | PBM vs PBM-acupuncture vs vit. B12 combo (20 sessions/10 wk) | All improved; PBM & medication superior to PBM-acupuncture; no difference PBM vs medication at 4 wk |
| Miloro 2018 | IAN+LN | Third molar, implant, LA injection (NM) | >90 days | PBM vs sham (20 sessions/3 mo) | No significant difference between groups |
| Oliveira 2017 | IAN | Orthognathic + genioplasty (NM) | 7 days | Electroacupuncture vs control (30 sessions/4 mo) | Tactile sense improved; no difference in 2-point discrimination or EPT |
| Seo (Japan) | IAN | Craniomaxillofacial surgery | 3–6 weeks | Prednisolone (tapering) | Prednisolone 3–6 weeks post-op accelerates sensation recovery |
| Scandinavian studies (×2) | IAN/LN | Various | Various | PBM variants | Significant PBM benefit in some outcomes |

**Incidence data**: IAN NSD 0.26–8.4%; LN NSD 0.1–22%; persistent NSD annual incidence 18.67% (95% CI 14.54–22.79) per Padmanabhan meta-analysis.

**Mechanism rationale for treatments**:
- PBM: photobiomodulation promotes axonal regeneration and reduces inflammation
- Vit. B12: essential for myelin synthesis; cytidine monophosphate + UTP combination supports nerve membrane repair
- Prednisolone: anti-inflammatory, reduces post-injury edema compressing nerve; window of 3–6 weeks post-op identified as optimal
- Electroacupuncture: minimal evidence; one study showed tactile improvement only

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 7 RCTs met inclusion (out of 3122 screened)
- Small sample sizes across all studies (n = 13–26 per arm typically)
- High methodological bias: especially randomization, allocation concealment, blinding
- Heterogeneity in nerve type, injury cause, injury severity (neuropraxia vs neurotmesis), delay-to-treatment, outcome measures — meta-analysis not performed
- No microsurgery RCTs met inclusion criteria (real-world evidence only)
- Recommended: larger RCTs following GCP guidelines

## 6. Related Work

- Seddon classification (1942): neuropraxia / axonotmesis / neurotmesis — severity predicts recovery probability
- Padmanabhan meta-analysis: 18.67% persistent NSD annual incidence
- Leung: NSD severity moderately correlated with OHRQoL in IAN/LN; not in neuralgia patients

## 7. Glossary

- **NSD**: Neurosensory disturbance
- **IAN**: Inferior alveolar nerve
- **LN**: Lingual nerve
- **PBM**: Photobiomodulation (laser therapy)
- **NM**: Nerve manipulation (injury type category)
- **Late paresthesia**: Paresthesia persisting ≥1 week post-procedure (SR's operational definition)
- **Neuropraxia**: Demyelination with intact nerve continuity (best prognosis)
- **Axonotmesis**: Axon interrupted, connective tissue intact (intermediate)
- **Neurotmesis**: Complete nerve severance (worst prognosis)
- **EPT**: Electric pulp test
- **JBI**: Joanna Briggs Institute
- **GCP**: Good Clinical Practice
