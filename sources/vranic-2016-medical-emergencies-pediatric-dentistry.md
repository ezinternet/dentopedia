## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (abstract-only retrieval) from the University of Zagreb on medical emergencies in paediatric dentistry, with no study population, no n, and no reported frequencies. It classifies causes as toxic/allergic drug reactions (local anaesthetics most often toxic; antibiotics, usually penicillin, most often allergic), stress-mediated vasovagal syncope, underlying disease (acute asthma attack, diabetic ketoacidosis, hypoglycaemia, seizures), and accidents (foreign-body aspiration with airway obstruction); failure to intervene can progress to cardiac and respiratory arrest requiring cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR). Its only actionable content is the reminder that every procedure and drug dosage must be scaled to the child's age — all quantitative claims must be sourced elsewhere.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Frames paediatric dental emergencies by aetiology: drug reactions (toxic vs allergic), stress response, underlying systemic disease, and accidents.
- Specifies that the dominant drug reaction pattern differs by drug class — local anaesthetics mainly cause toxic reactions, antibiotics (usually penicillin) mainly cause allergic reactions.
- Names vasovagal syncope as the archetypal stress-mediated emergency in the dental setting.
- Lists the disease-specific emergencies a paediatric dentist must recognise: acute asthma attack, diabetic ketoacidosis, hypoglycaemia, seizures.
- Adds foreign-body aspiration with respiratory obstruction as the accident category, and states the progression pathway to cardiac/respiratory arrest requiring CPR.

## 7. Glossary

- **Vasovagal syncope (VVS)**: neutrally mediated reflex fainting triggered by stress/pain, the paradigm stress-related dental emergency.
- **Toxic reaction**: dose-dependent adverse effect of a drug (here, chiefly local anaesthetics), distinct from an immune-mediated allergy.
- **Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)**: acute hyperglycaemic decompensation producing ketonaemia and acidosis; a paediatric systemic emergency.
- **Hypoglycaemia**: low blood glucose, which can present as altered consciousness or seizures in a child.
- **Foreign-body aspiration**: inhalation of material into the airway, causing obstruction; an accident-type emergency in paediatric dentistry.
- **CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation)**: the rescue protocol for cardiac/respiratory arrest, the terminal pathway named by this review.
