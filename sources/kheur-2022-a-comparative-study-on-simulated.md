## Why Ingested

Addresses two sequential chairside decision axes for monolithic zirconia restorations: (1) which abrasive agent (diamond bur, zirconia-specific diamond points, tungsten carbide bur) causes the least damage during occlusal adjustment grinding, and (2) which subsequent polishing protocol (diamond paste, ceramic polishing kit, novel zirconia slurry) best restores surface quality after diamond-bur grinding. By testing both grinding and polishing variables in a two-phase design — measuring surface roughness (Ra), Vickers hardness (VH), flexural strength (MPa), SEM morphology, and XRD crystal structure — this paper is the most complete comparative framework in the zirconia-occlusal-adjustment surveillance set.

## Three-line Summary

Two-phase in vitro study (Materials 2022, n = 140 sintered Y-TZP specimens 15 × 3 × 3 mm, 3M ESPE Lava, 1500 °C 2 h): Phase 1 compared diamond burs, zirconia-specific diamond points, and tungsten carbide burs for grinding to 1.5 mm and 1.0 mm residual thickness, measuring Ra, Vickers hardness (VH), and flexural strength plus SEM/XRD; Phase 2 tested three polishing protocols (diamond paste, Shofu ceramic polishing kit, novel low-temperature-resintered zirconia slurry) on diamond-bur-ground specimens.

Tungsten carbide burs, despite producing the smoothest surface (Ra ≈ 0.40 µm, comparable to control 0.27 µm), reduced flexural strength catastrophically — from 978 MPa to 496 MPa at 1.5 mm and 196 MPa at 1.0 mm — and SEM revealed fine grinding-direction cracks; diamond burs roughened surfaces (Ra ≈ 0.81 µm) but did not significantly reduce flexural strength at 1.5 mm; no T→M phase transformation was detected by XRD in any group.

After diamond-bur grinding, diamond polishing paste (Signum HP diamond + Z-Shine wheel) was the best polishing option — restoring Ra to 0.24 µm, below the 0.27 µm control — while the porcelain polishing kit was acceptable (Ra 0.32 µm, not statistically different from control), and the novel zirconia slurry failed completely (Ra 2.81 µm); all polishing groups at 1.5 mm residual thickness showed only insignificant flexural-strength reduction.

## 세줄요약

2상 시험관내 (In vitro) 연구 (Materials 2022, n = 140, Y-TZP 시편 15 × 3 × 3 mm, 3M ESPE Lava, 1500 °C 2 h 소결): 1상은 다이아몬드 버, 지르코니아 전용 다이아몬드 포인트, 텅스텐 카바이드 버로 잔류 두께 1.5·1.0 mm까지 연삭 후 표면거칠기 (Ra), 비커스 경도 (Vickers hardness, VH), 굴곡강도 (flexural strength) + SEM/XRD 비교; 2상은 다이아몬드 버 연삭 후 3종 연마프로토콜(다이아몬드 연마 페이스트, Shofu 도자기 연마 키트, 신규 저온 소결 지르코니아 슬러리) 비교.

텅스텐 카바이드 버는 표면을 가장 매끄럽게 (Ra ≈ 0.40 µm) 했으나 굴곡강도를 치명적으로 저하시킴 — 978 MPa → 1.5 mm에서 496 MPa, 1.0 mm에서 196 MPa — SEM에서 연삭 방향 미세균열 확인; 다이아몬드 버는 Ra 증가 (≈ 0.81 µm)를 보였으나 1.5 mm에서 굴곡강도 유의감소 없음; XRD에서 어느 군도 정방정→단사정 (T→M) 상전이 (phase transformation) 없음.

다이아몬드 버 연삭 후 최적 연마는 다이아몬드 연마 페이스트 (Ra 0.24 µm, 대조군 0.27 µm보다 낮음); 도자기 연마 키트는 허용 수준 (Ra 0.32 µm); 지르코니아 슬러리는 실패 (Ra 2.81 µm); 잔류두께 1.5 mm에서 모든 연마군의 굴곡강도 감소는 통계적으로 유의하지 않음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Establishes a **bur-type hierarchy for chairside grinding**: diamond burs are the preferred agent — they roughen the surface (Ra ↑, significant) but do not reduce flexural strength significantly at 1.5 mm; zirconia-specific diamond points perform similarly; tungsten carbide burs are contraindicated because they halve flexural strength despite producing a smooth surface.
- Identifies a **critical thickness threshold**: at 1.5 mm residual thickness all burs except tungsten carbide preserve flexural strength; at 1.0 mm all test groups show significant flexural-strength reduction — establishing a minimum safe residual thickness for occlusal adjustment.
- Demonstrates that **diamond polishing paste restores Ra below the unground control** (0.24 µm vs 0.27 µm) after diamond-bur grinding, outperforming the ceramic polishing kit (acceptable) and the novel zirconia slurry (failed).
- Provides **negative XRD evidence**: no T→M phase transformation after any grinding or polishing protocol — ruling out transformation toughening or degradation as a mechanism.
- Tests a **novel indigenously developed zirconia slurry** (pre-sintered zirconia + glazing liquid + 450 °C resintering) and shows it worsens surface quality (Ra 2.81 µm), closing this research direction.
- Uses a **two-phase sequential design** that separates grinding-agent selection (Phase 1) from polishing-protocol optimization (Phase 2), making it directly applicable to a step-by-step clinical protocol.

## 3. Methodology

- **Specimens**: 140 presintered Y-TZP blanks (3M ESPE Lava) milled at presintered stage then sintered at 1500 °C for 2 h; final dimensions 15 × 3 × 3 mm; divided into groups of n = 20.
- **Phase 1 — grinding agents** (4 groups, residual thicknesses 1.5 mm and 1.0 mm):
  - Group I: control (no grinding)
  - Group II: diamond bur (medium grit 100–125 µm)
  - Group III: zirconia-specific diamond points (medium grit)
  - Group IV: tungsten carbide finishing bur (8-bladed)
  - Water irrigation during all grinding; customized assembly for controlled grinding; 1 bur per specimen.
- **Phase 2 — polishing protocols** (best bur from Phase 1 = diamond bur; 3 polishing groups at 1.5 mm and 1.0 mm):
  - Group D: diamond polishing paste (Signum HP diamond, Kulzer) + polishing wheel (Z-Shine, Dental Creations)
  - Group P: ceramic polishing kit (Shofu Dental Porcelain Laminate)
  - Group S: novel zirconia slurry (presintered zirconia particles in glazing liquid 1:1) + 450 °C resintering 7 min
  - Polishing: 30 s stroke movements by single operator, 10,000–12,000 rpm.
- **Surface roughness (Ra)**: contact profilometer (Perthometer SP6, Mahr) with MarSurf XR20 software; velocity 0.1 mm/s, Lc = 0.25 mm, Lm = 2.4 mm; 3 measurements per specimen, perpendicular to grinding direction.
- **Vickers hardness (VH)**: Vickers tester (Struers), load 9.807 N, 15 s dwell; 3 measurements per specimen.
- **Flexural strength**: ISO 6872, universal testing machine (Instron 5566), 0.5 mm/min crosshead speed; circular fixture + 2 mm tungsten piston.
- **SEM**: Zeiss MERLIN FE-SEM; specimens Pt-coated; multiple magnifications.
- **XRD**: Bruker D8 Advance, CuKα (1.54 Å), 2θ 20°–80°, step 0.03°, 0.6 s/step; Karakoca and Yilmaz method for relative monoclinic phase quantification.
- **Statistics**: ANOVA + Bonferroni post-hoc, α = 0.05; Anderson–Darling normality test.

## 4. Key Results

### Phase 1 — Grinding agent comparison

| Group | Ra (µm) 1.5 mm | Ra sig. vs control | Flexural strength (MPa) 1.5 mm | FS sig. vs control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I: Control | 0.27 ± 0.03 | — | 978 ± 24.81 | — |
| II: Diamond bur | 0.81 ± 0.26 | p = 0.000 ↑ | 901.6 ± 28.07 | p > 0.05 (NS) |
| III: Zirconia diamond | 0.85 ± 0.35 | p = 0.000 ↑ | 840.3 ± 35.44 | p > 0.05 (NS) |
| IV: Tungsten carbide | 0.40 ± 0.32 | p = 0.824 (NS) | 496.2 ± 23.45 | p = 0.000 ↓ |

At 1.0 mm residual thickness: all groups showed significant flexural-strength reduction (p = 0.000).
XRD: no T→M phase transformation in any group; asymmetric broadening of tetragonal peak attributed to lattice distortion.

### Phase 2 — Polishing protocol comparison (after diamond-bur grinding)

| Group | Ra (µm) 1.5 mm | Ra vs control | Flexural strength (MPa) 1.5 mm | FS vs control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control | 0.27 ± 0.03 | — | 978 ± 24.81 | — |
| D: Diamond paste | 0.24 ± 0.05 | p = 0.000 ↓ (better) | 896.7 ± 48.56 | p > 0.05 (NS) |
| P: Ceramic polishing kit | 0.32 ± 0.25 | p > 0.05 (NS) | 882.7 ± 47.11 | p > 0.05 (NS) |

At 1.0 mm residual thickness: all polishing groups showed significant flexural-strength reduction (p = 0.000).
Vickers hardness: no significant differences between groups or within groups after polishing.

## 5. Limitations

- In vitro only: rectangular bar specimens (15 × 3 × 3 mm) do not represent crown geometry or cemented conditions; no cyclic fatigue or aging.
- Grinding depth was fixed (to 1.5 mm and 1.0 mm residual); no intermediate thicknesses tested.
- Polishing duration standardized at 30 s; optimal duration for each system not determined.
- Novel zirconia slurry tested without independent reproducibility data; slurry composition not standardized.
- No antagonist wear testing; clinical longevity of chairside-polished zirconia not addressed.
- Single zirconia brand (3M ESPE Lava); results may not generalize to high-translucency or cubic-phase zirconia.

## 7. Glossary

- **Ra (arithmetic average roughness, 산술평균거칠기)**: mean deviation of surface profile from the centerline; clinical threshold for zirconia ≈ 0.2–0.3 µm.
- **Flexural strength (굴곡강도)**: maximum stress before fracture under three-point or biaxial bending; ISO 6872 standard for dental ceramics.
- **Vickers hardness (VH, 비커스 경도)**: hardness measured by diamond-pyramid indentation; units HV.
- **T→M phase transformation (정방정→단사정 상전이)**: tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation in Y-TZP under stress or grinding heat; associated with ~4.5% volume increase that can either toughen (transformation toughening) or degrade the material.
- **XRD (X-ray diffraction, X선 회절분석)**: crystallographic analysis to detect relative monoclinic phase fraction and crystal structure changes.
- **SEM (scanning electron microscopy, 주사전자현미경)**: surface topography imaging at high magnification.
- **Low-temperature degradation (LTD, 저온열화)**: hydrothermal aging of Y-TZP causing T→M transformation at mouth temperature in the presence of water.
- **Zirconia slurry (지르코니아 슬러리)**: in this study, an indigenously developed paste of presintered zirconia particles in glazing liquid (1:1 by mass), applied and resintered at 450 °C.
