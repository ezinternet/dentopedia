## Three-line Summary

Animal study (anesthetized male rats, single-unit recording in trigeminal ganglion) testing whether fremanezumab (TEV-48125, a humanized anti-CGRP monoclonal antibody) differentially affects Aδ versus C-fiber meningeal nociceptors activated by cortical spreading depression (CSD).

Fremanezumab pretreatment selectively decreased the percentage of Aδ neurons activated by CSD, but had no effect on C-fiber neuron activation — the first identification of an anti-migraine drug selective for Aδ-fibers peripherally.

This peripheral Aδ-selectivity is proposed to explain the drug's previously reported selective inhibition of central high-threshold (HT, predominant Aδ input) but not wide-dynamic-range trigeminovascular neurons, and may explain incomplete efficacy in some migraine patients; abstract-only — full text not retrieved (PMC returned empty body for this PMCID).

## 2. Key Contributions

- First demonstration that a clinically used anti-migraine biologic (anti-CGRP mAb) pharmacologically dissociates Aδ from C-fiber meningeal nociceptors — Aδ inhibited, C unaffected.
- Mechanistically links a peripheral finding (Aδ-selective nociceptor inhibition) to a previously reported central finding (selective inhibition of high-threshold but not wide-dynamic-range trigeminovascular dorsal horn neurons) via the predominant Aδ input to HT neurons.
- Offers a candidate explanation for incomplete/heterogeneous clinical efficacy of CGRP-mAbs across migraine patients (those relying more on a C-fiber/WDR pathway may respond less).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Single-unit extracellular recording from mechanosensitive primary afferent meningeal nociceptors in the trigeminal ganglion of anesthetized male rats. Cortical spreading depression (CSD) was induced as the physiological activator of meningeal nociceptors (established migraine-relevant stimulus). Neurons were classified as Aδ (thinly myelinated) or C-fiber (unmyelinated) by conduction velocity. Fremanezumab (TEV-48125) was administered as pretreatment; the percentage of Aδ vs C neurons subsequently activated by CSD was compared to control/vehicle. This design directly extends the authors' prior study of central (dorsal horn) trigeminovascular neuron responses to CGRP-mAb, now probing the peripheral (trigeminal ganglion) level.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Fremanezumab pretreatment: selective decrease in the percentage of **Aδ** neurons activated by CSD.
- No effect of fremanezumab on the percentage of **C-fiber** neurons activated by CSD.
- Interpreted alongside the authors' earlier report: fremanezumab selectively inhibits **high-threshold (HT)** but not wide-dynamic-range central trigeminovascular neurons — consistent with HT neurons receiving predominantly Aδ input.
- Conclusion: the headache-preventive site of action of CGRP-mAbs is likely peripheral, at Aδ meningeal nociceptors, and the Aδ-HT pathway may be sufficient to generate headache in a subset of patients.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Abstract-only extraction: quantitative results (exact % changes, sample sizes, p-values, dose) are not captured here because PMC returned an empty full-text body for this PMCID.
- Animal model only (rats); translation to human migraine patients is inferential.
- Male rats only — no assessment of sex differences, which are clinically important in migraine (female-predominant disease).

## 6. Related Work

- Builds directly on the authors' own prior report of CGRP-mAb selective inhibition of central high-threshold (not wide-dynamic-range) trigeminovascular dorsal horn neurons (cited within this abstract as "we recently tested...").
- Complements [[orofacial-pain/wilke-2020-chloride-underrated-ion-nociceptors]] (DRG nociceptor chloride-channel repertoire) by providing a trigeminal-ganglion, fiber-type-selective pharmacological dissociation rather than a channel-repertoire catalog.
- Contrasts mechanistically with botulinum toxin type A (BoNT-A) approaches to migraine/TMD myogenous pain in `wiki/botulinum-toxin/` — BoNT-A acts on SNARE-mediated neurotransmitter/neuropeptide release machinery at nerve terminals broadly, whereas fremanezumab acts by sequestering circulating CGRP ligand with apparent fiber-type selectivity; the two are not directly compared in this paper but represent distinct migraine-prevention mechanisms in the wiki.

## 7. Glossary

- **CGRP (Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide)**: Neuropeptide abundant in primary afferent sensory neurons, strongly implicated in migraine pathophysiology.
- **CGRP-mAb**: Humanized monoclonal antibody that sequesters CGRP, reducing its availability; class includes fremanezumab.
- **Fremanezumab (TEV-48125)**: A specific CGRP-mAb studied here for migraine prevention.
- **Aδ fiber**: Thinly myelinated primary afferent fiber, faster conducting than C-fibers, associated with high-threshold (HT) central neuron input.
- **C fiber**: Unmyelinated primary afferent fiber, slow conducting.
- **Cortical Spreading Depression (CSD)**: A wave of neuronal/glial depolarization across the cortex, used experimentally as the migraine-relevant trigger to activate meningeal nociceptors.
- **High-Threshold (HT) neuron**: A class of second-order trigeminovascular dorsal horn neuron requiring strong stimuli for activation; receives predominant Aδ input.
- **Wide-Dynamic-Range (WDR) neuron**: A class of second-order trigeminovascular dorsal horn neuron responsive across a broad stimulus intensity range.
- **Meningeal nociceptor**: Primary afferent sensory neuron innervating the cranial dura mater, implicated as the origin of migraine headache pain.
