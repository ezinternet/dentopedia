## Three-line Summary
Single-center cross-sectional study (University of Genova, Italy; BMC Oral Health 2024) of 93 patients, adapting the Ivy bleeding-time test to the oral cavity to compare intraoral bleeding time during a professional oral hygiene (scaling/polishing) session across six drug-exposure groups: VKA (warfarin/acenocoumarol), NOAC-DTI (dabigatran), NOAC-AntiXa (apixaban/rivaroxaban), SAPT, DAPT, and a drug-free control.

Mean bleeding time was longest for VKA (203 s) and DAPT (190 s) — both significantly higher than control (50 s, p<0.001) — and DTI (120 s) was also significant (p=0.004), while AntiXa (98 s) and SAPT (105 s) did not differ significantly from control; time since last dose (<5 h vs ≥5 h) showed no significant within-group difference, though NOAC groups showed a non-significant trend toward shorter bleeding time closer to the next dose.

None of the prolonged bleeding interfered with completion of the hygiene session (gauze pressure alone sufficed in every case), supporting uninterrupted anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy for routine scaling; the authors speculate 2–3 h before the next NOAC dose as a candidate (non-mandatory) scheduling window, but the study is single-center, unpowered, and did not record periodontal parameters (BOP%, PD, plaque index).

## 2. Key Contributions
- Adapts the classic Ivy bleeding-time test (volar-forearm incision) to the oral cavity for the first time in this context: the first bleeding-on-probing site (probing 1.6→2.6, vestibular) becomes the bleeding-time assay site, timed with 15-second gauze-blot intervals until spontaneous hemostasis.
- Six-arm head-to-head comparison (VKA, NOAC-DTI, NOAC-AntiXa, SAPT, DAPT, drug-free control) rather than the more common two-arm continue-vs-discontinue design, letting drug classes be ranked against each other and against a true untreated baseline.
- Measures bleeding during a **non-surgical, routine** procedure (professional oral hygiene / scaling) rather than extraction or implant surgery — the clinical setting dental hygienists actually work in day to day.
- Reports that despite VKA and DAPT producing bleeding times roughly 2–4× the control, none required anything beyond gauze tamponade — no sutures, local hemostatic drugs, or cautery were needed in any group.
- From a secondary, non-significant timing analysis, proposes 2–3 hours before the next NOAC dose as a candidate scheduling window that avoids both peak anticoagulation and medication interruption.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: single-center cross-sectional study, dental clinic setting, Italy; ethics approval CERA 2024/08; written informed consent.
- **Recruitment**: patients on regular biannual dental-hygiene recall, screened during a scheduled professional oral hygiene session.
- **Inclusion criteria**: adults, regular follow-up, no heavy calculus, no contraindication to the planned hygiene procedure, natural teeth or tooth-supported restorations, on any anticoagulant or antiplatelet.
- **Exclusion criteria**: age <18; pregnant/lactating; myocardial infarction within 6 months; immunosuppression; uncontrolled diabetes; head/neck radiation history; periodontitis patients; fully edentulous or implant-supported-restoration patients.
- **Groups** (n, M/W, mean age [range]):
  | Group | Drug(s) | n (M/W) | Mean age (range) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | VKA | warfarin, acenocoumarol | 27 (4/23) | 62 (35–90) |
  | NOAC-DTI | dabigatran | 15 (2/13) | 58 (40–83) |
  | NOAC-AntiXa | apixaban, rivaroxaban | 13 (2/11) | 60 (40–91) |
  | SAPT | aspirin or clopidogrel | 12 (9/3) | 56 (40–74) |
  | DAPT | aspirin + clopidogrel | 12 (12/0) | 56 (40–74) |
  | Control | none | 14 (5/9) | 49 (20–60) |

  (Total N=93; a mention of "16 healthy patients" appears earlier in the Methods text but does not reconcile with Table 1's Control n=14 or with the stated total of 93 — Table 1 and the stated total are used here as the internally consistent figures.)
- **Drug-use rationale** (Table 2): atrial fibrillation (34 patients) most common indication, followed by ischemia (23), deep vein thrombosis (15), valvulopathy (3), other (4).
- **Procedure**:
  1. 1-minute chlorhexidine mouth rinse.
  2. Full-mouth bleeding-on-probing assessment, vestibular surface, tooth 1.6 → 2.6, with a calibrated periodontal probe (PCP UNC 15, Hu-Friedy), by operator "DP."
  3. First bleeding site identified → chronometer started by a second, independent operator ("LP") → gentle sterile-gauze pressure applied every 15 seconds → time to spontaneous hemostasis recorded as the "Ivy time" (seconds).
  4. Professional oral hygiene performed: ultrasonic mechanical instrumentation (piezoelectric scaler, EMS), polishing paste (Cleanic, Kerr) with rubber cup, hand scaler as needed for interproximal calculus, by expert dental hygienist "LP."
  5. Session closed with oral-hygiene motivation.
- **Data recorded**: drug indication, dosage, time elapsed since last intake, and Ivy time (seconds), logged in Excel.
- **Statistics**: analyst blinded to group assignment; non-parametric methods used throughout because small subgroup sizes precluded assuming Gaussian distribution — Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn's post-hoc for multiple comparisons, or two-way ANOVA with Dunnett post-hoc; data reported as median [IQR] unless stated otherwise; α = 0.05.
- **Secondary analysis**: patients split at the median time-from-last-dose (5 hours) into <5 h vs ≥5 h subgroups, compared within each drug category.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Bleeding time by group (Table 3)**
| Category | Group | Mean bleeding time (s) | Range (s) | vs. Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control | — | 50 | 39–61 | — |
| NOAC | AntiXa (apixaban, rivaroxaban) | 98 | 74–160 | not significant |
| Antiplatelet | SAPT | 105 | 50–120 | not significant |
| NOAC | DTI (dabigatran) | 120 | 60–155 | p = 0.004 |
| Antiplatelet | DAPT | 190 | 143–240 | p < 0.001 |
| Conventional anticoagulant | VKA (warfarin, acenocoumarol) | 203 | 140–281 | p < 0.001 |

- VKA bleeding time was more than 2× the control mean, the largest gap of any group.
- Within NOAC, rivaroxaban produced the longest bleeding time, followed by dabigatran, then apixaban (drug-level ordering reported in Discussion, not broken out numerically in the tables).
- Within antiplatelets, aspirin and clopidogrel monotherapy produced similar bleeding times (SAPT), while the combination (DAPT) nearly doubled it.
- **Timing sub-analysis**: median time since last dose was 5 hours; no statistically significant within-category difference between <5 h and ≥5 h groups. VKA bleeding time remained >200 s regardless of timing (VKA activity is not meal/dose-interval sensitive at this scale — consistent with warfarin's 48–72 h half-life). NOAC groups showed a non-significant trend toward shorter bleeding time when measured ≥5 h post-dose, motivating the authors' 2–3 h pre-next-dose scheduling suggestion.
- No clinically significant bleeding episode required escalation beyond gauze tamponade in any group, including DAPT.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **No power analysis**: the authors state no comparable prior study existed to base a sample-size calculation on.
- **Periodontal parameters not recorded**: percentage of bleeding on probing, probing depth, and plaque index were not captured, despite BOP being used operationally to locate the assay site; mitigated somewhat by all patients being under strict biannual recall.
- **Cross-sectional, single time-point design**: no longitudinal or interventional (e.g., continue-vs-interrupt) arm; causal inference about drug effect relies on between-group comparison only.
- **Small, unequal subgroups** (n = 12–27 per drug group; n = 14 control), which is also why non-parametric statistics were required.
- **Single-center, single geography** (Italy) — generalizability to other populations/health systems untested.
- **Local hemostasis limited to gauze tamponade** by protocol; sutures, local hemostatic drugs, and cautery were named as available escalation options but were not needed and thus not evaluated.
- **Time-since-dose analysis is a post-hoc median split** (5 h), not a pre-registered pharmacokinetic-interval design — the 2–3 h scheduling suggestion is explicitly framed as speculation, not a tested protocol.
- Internal inconsistency in the Methods text between "16 healthy patients" and the Table 1 control n=14 (see note in §3) — not flagged by the authors, noted here for traceability.

## 6. Related Work
Papers cited in the Discussion for context (not independently verified against this wiki's holdings unless a wikilink is given):
- Rubino et al. — retrospective study; minimal postoperative bleeding in invasive periodontal procedures regardless of antiplatelet/anticoagulant status, medication continuation/discontinuation, comorbidity, or smoking.
- Darvish et al. — systematic review, 11 studies; minor oral surgery generally safe on DOACs, continue-vs-discontinue debate still unresolved.
- Schrodi et al. (2002) and Royzman et al. (2004) — aspirin (325 mg/day and 81–325 mg/day respectively, 1 week) significantly increased bleeding-on-probing percentage.
- Almiñana-Pastor et al. — found no correlation between anticoagulant use and bleeding on probing.
- Sandhu et al. — 513 surgical episodes / 1,001 extractions on uninterrupted anticoagulants/antiplatelets; 95.9% no postoperative bleeding, 4.1% required local-measures-only intervention.
- López-Galindo — systematic review, 7 studies / 931 patients; minor bleeding predominant across DOAC/VKA/no-therapy groups; DOACs judged safe for extraction without regimen alteration.
- Manfredini et al. — PRISMA-ScR review; discontinuation's thromboembolic risk judged to outweigh the hemorrhagic risk of continuation.
- Zaib et al. — local tranexamic acid reduces postoperative bleeding risk in anticoagulated dental patients.
- Campana et al. — systematic review; autologous platelet concentrates reduce postoperative bleeding, shorten hemostasis time, ease pain, speed healing in anticoagulated extraction patients.
- Berton et al. — clinical study; L-PRF is a safe, effective hemostatic agent for VKA/DOAC patients undergoing single-tooth extraction.
- RE-LY and ARISTOTLE trials — cited as showing comparable postoperative dental bleeding rates between warfarin and NOAC.

## 7. Glossary
- **Ivy test / bleeding time**: classic hemostasis assay (standardized skin incision, timed to spontaneous cessation); here adapted to an intraoral bleeding-on-probing site instead of the volar forearm.
- **VKA (Vitamin K Antagonist)**: warfarin, acenocoumarol — inhibits synthesis of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors; requires INR monitoring.
- **NOAC (Novel/Non-vitamin K Oral Anticoagulant)**: umbrella term used here for DTI and AntiXa agents; no routine coagulation monitoring required.
- **DTI (Direct Thrombin Inhibitor)**: dabigatran — inhibits free and fibrin-bound thrombin and thrombin-induced platelet aggregation.
- **AntiXa (direct Factor Xa inhibitor)**: apixaban, rivaroxaban (endoxaban/edoxaban also named as class member) — inhibits factor Xa in the coagulation cascade.
- **SAPT (Single Antiplatelet Therapy)**: monotherapy with acetylsalicylic acid or clopidogrel.
- **DAPT (Dual Antiplatelet Therapy)**: combined acetylsalicylic acid + clopidogrel.
- **INR (International Normalized Ratio)**: ratio of patient prothrombin time to a standardized control, adjusted for the International Sensitivity Index; used to monitor VKA activity.
- **BOP (Bleeding on Probing)**: gingival bleeding elicited by periodontal probing; used here to locate the Ivy-test assay site, not as an independently recorded periodontal outcome.
