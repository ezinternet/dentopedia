## Why Ingested

기존 [[wiki/radiology/wamasing-2018-imaging-bifid-mandibular-canal]]은 고해상도 MRI로 이분 하악관(Bifid Mandibular Canal, BMC) 발생률(6.4%)을 보고했지만 삼분 하악관(Trifid Mandibular Canal, TMC)이나 후구치관(retromolar canal)의 세부 분류·계측은 다루지 않았다. 본 논문(Cuozzo 2024)은 CT/CBCT 123명(246 하악관)을 대상으로 BMC/TMC 통합 발생률(53.6%)을 보고하고, Naitoh 분류(type I–IV)와 von Arx 후구치관 아형(A/B/C) 및 폭·위치 계측치까지 제공해 기존 페이지를 방사선 양식(MRI→CT/CBCT)과 분류 세밀도 양면에서 확장한다.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective CT/CBCT study (n=123 patients, 246 mandibular canals) evaluating prevalence and anatomical classification of bifid mandibular canals (BMCs) and trifid mandibular canals (TMCs), with detailed sub-classification and measurement of retromolar canals.

53.6% of patients (66/123) had at least one BMC or TMC; site-level prevalence was 36.2% for BMC and 4.5% for TMC, with forward canal (type III, 12.6%) and retromolar canal (type I, 10.2%) the most common accessory-canal subtypes; retromolar canals were significantly wider (1.44±0.65 mm) than other accessory canal types.

Accurate CT/CBCT evaluation of accessory mandibular canals — especially the retromolar canal, whose branch point, width, and course were characterized in detail — is recommended before posterior mandibular surgery to reduce neurovascular complication risk; limitation: single-center retrospective sample of pre-existing scans referred mostly for third-molar/implant planning, not a screened healthy population.

## 세줄요약

후향적 CT/CBCT 연구(환자 123명, 246개 하악관)로 이분 하악관(Bifid Mandibular Canal, BMC)과 삼분 하악관(Trifid Mandibular Canal, TMC)의 발생률 및 해부학적 분류를 평가하고, 특히 후구치관(retromolar canal)의 세부 아형과 계측치를 제시했다.

전체 환자의 53.6%(66/123)가 BMC 또는 TMC를 1개 이상 보유했으며, 부위(반측하악골) 단위 발생률은 BMC 36.2%, TMC 4.5%였고, 전방관(forward canal, type III, 12.6%)과 후구치관(type I, 10.2%)이 가장 흔한 아형이었으며, 후구치관의 평균 폭(1.44±0.65mm)이 다른 아형보다 유의하게 넓었다.

후구치관의 분지점·폭·주행을 포함한 정밀 CT/CBCT 평가는 하악 후방부 수술 시 신경혈관 손상 위험을 줄이기 위해 권고되나, 본 연구는 제3대구치 발치·임플란트 계획을 위해 이미 촬영된 스캔을 활용한 단일기관 후향적 표본이라 건강인 무작위 표본을 대표하지 않는다는 한계가 있다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Reports a combined BMC+TMC patient-level prevalence of 53.6% (66/123) using CT/CBCT, one of the higher figures in the literature range (15.6%–66.5% cited for BMC alone).
- Adopts the Naitoh et al CBCT-based classification (retromolar/type I, dental/type II, forward/type III, buccolingual/type IV) for BMC subtyping and additionally applies the von Arx classification (vertical/type A, curved/type B, horizontal/type C) specifically to retromolar canals.
- First formal quantitative characterization in this dataset of retromolar canal course/position (distance to second molar, height, width, distance from mandibular foramen to branch point, and width of the parent mandibular canal at the branch point).
- Documents a rare, non-classifiable temporal crest canal (TCC) variant in one hemimandible, originating from an accessory foramen anterosuperior to the mandibular foramen.
- No statistically significant sex or side (right/left) differences found in accessory canal prevalence or subtype distribution.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Retrospective collection of CT/CBCT scans of patients ≥18 years old, performed for odontostomatological indications (tooth extraction, implant planning) with FOV showing the full mandibular arch.
- Exclusions: scans not showing the entire mandibular canal path (mandibular foramen to mental foramen), and scans with osteolytic/osteosclerotic lesions in the posterior mandible.
- Of 138 candidate scans, 15 excluded (10 for incomplete canal visualization, 5 for osteolytic lesions posterior to mandible), leaving 123 patients (246 mandibular canals; 64 female/52%, 59 male/48%; mean age 31.4±11.62 years, range 18–78).
- Two calibrated examiners (V.P., L.G.) evaluated multiplanar reconstruction (MPR) images in RadiAnt DICOM Viewer, with a second-pass 3D volumetric reconstruction analysis in SimPlant Pro 18 for confirmatory visualization of accessory canal course and retromolar foramen identification. Interexaminer agreement assessed by kappa (κ ≥0.90 accepted).
- Each mandibular canal classified as normal / bifid / trifid based on number of branching accessory canals (0/1/2); BMC subtype per Naitoh classification; retromolar canals further subclassified per von Arx (vertical/curved/horizontal) and measured for width and positional landmarks.
- Statistics: chi-squared/Fisher's exact test for prevalence differences by gender/side/type; ANOVA for width comparisons across accessory canal types; significance at p<0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Overall prevalence**: 53.6% of patients (66/123) had ≥1 BMC or TMC; 46.8% in females (30/64) vs 61% in males (36/59) — not statistically significant (p=0.148).
- **Bilaterality**: 51.5% (34/66) of affected patients had accessory canals bilaterally (27 bifid-bifid, 4 bifid-trifid, 3 trifid-trifid pairs).
- **Site-level (hemimandible, n=246) prevalence**: BMC 36.2% (89/246), TMC 4.5% (11/246). Patient-level: BMC 50.4% (62/123), TMC 6.5% (8/123).
- **BMC subtype distribution (site-level)**: forward canal (type III) 12.6%, retromolar canal (type I) 10.2%, dental canal (type II) 6.2% (also reported as 6%), buccolingual canal (type IV) 3.7%; 3.7% (9/246) unclassifiable (8 sites: accessory branch from inferior wall running antero-inferiorly; 1 site: temporal crest canal, TCC).
- **Width by type**: retromolar canal mean 1.44±0.65 mm — significantly wider than anterior/forward canals (1.08±0.26 mm; p=0.043); no other significant width differences between subtypes. Overall mean accessory canal width 1.21±0.44 mm.
- **Retromolar canal detail** (n=25 canals from 19/123 patients, 15.5%; 6 bilateral): 60% vertical (type A, n=15), 40% curved (type B, n=10), 0% horizontal (type C).
  - Distance, retromolar foramen midpoint to 2nd molar: 15.7±3.5 mm (range 11–29.3 mm)
  - Height of retromolar canal: 12.9±2.7 mm (range 9.6–21.5 mm)
  - Width of retromolar canal: 1.03±0.28 mm (range 0.45–1.47 mm)
  - Distance, mandibular foramen to branch point: 12.18±5.32 mm (range 2.7–23.3 mm)
  - Width of parent mandibular canal at branch point: 3.12±0.67 mm (range 1.96–4.44 mm)
- Findings for retromolar canal position/width closely match von Arx et al's prior measurements (15.2±2.4 mm / 11.4±2.7 mm / 1±0.31 mm).
- No significant gender (p=0.145) or side (p=0.742) differences in accessory canal type distribution.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single-center, retrospective design using pre-existing scans (mostly referred for third-molar extraction/implant planning), not a screened random/healthy population — may bias prevalence upward toward patients with suspected canal-tooth proximity.
- Sample size (123 patients) modest; authors explicitly call for larger studies for stronger evidence.
- New radiographic exams were not obtained (to avoid additional radiation), so image quality/FOV depended on original referral indication.
- 3.7% of accessory canals were not classifiable under the adopted (Naitoh) system, indicating the classification does not capture full anatomical variability (e.g., inferior-wall antero-inferior branches, the rare temporal crest canal).

## 6. Related Work

- Naitoh et al — original CBCT-based BMC classification (types I–IV) adopted here.
- von Arx et al — retromolar canal subclassification (vertical/curved/horizontal) and positional measurement methodology, closely replicated in this study with concordant results.
- Orhan et al — another CBCT BMC prevalence/subtype study, cited for comparison (discordant for overall BMC prevalence, concordant for type III/I dominance).
- Prior systematic review cited for BMC mean length/diameter benchmarks (12.38±2.92 mm / 1.64±0.46 mm) and BMC prevalence range (15.6–66.5%) across the literature.
- [[wiki/radiology/wamasing-2018-imaging-bifid-mandibular-canal]] — MRI-based BMC/nutrient-canal incidence study; this CT/CBCT paper provides a complementary, higher-resolution-classification dataset using a different (and more widely available) imaging modality.

## 7. Glossary

- **BMC (Bifid Mandibular Canal)**: mandibular canal variant with one accessory branch in addition to the main canal.
- **TMC (Trifid Mandibular Canal)**: mandibular canal variant with two accessory branches.
- **Retromolar canal (type I)**: accessory canal branching off in the ramus region, running anterosuperiorly to open at the retromolar foramen in the retromolar fossa; associated with surgical complication risk during third-molar/implant surgery.
- **Dental canal (type II)**: accessory canal bifurcating toward the root apex of the second or third molar.
- **Forward canal (type III)**: accessory canal arising from the superior wall of the mandibular canal, running forward parallel to the main canal.
- **Buccolingual canal (type IV)**: accessory canal arising from the buccal or lingual wall of the mandibular canal.
- **Temporal crest canal (TCC)**: rare accessory canal originating from an accessory foramen anterior/cranial to the mandibular foramen, opening at a bony foramen in the temporal crest region; presumed to carry the long buccal nerve and vessels.
- **MPR (Multiplanar Reconstruction)**: CT/CBCT image analysis technique combining axial, coronal, and sagittal planes.
