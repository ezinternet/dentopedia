## Why Ingested

First Aa-specific page in wiki. Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans (Aa) is the primary pathogen in localized aggressive periodontitis (LAP). This review covers CDT, LtxA, LPS, host immune modulation, and alveolar bone loss mechanisms — essential for understanding non-red-complex periodontal pathogens, complementing the red-complex-centric [[oral-microbiology/hajishengallis-2012-psd-model-periodontal-disease]].

## Three-line Summary

Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans (Aa) deploys multiple virulence factors — leukotoxin (LtxA), cytolethal distending toxin (CDT), and LPS — to evade host immunity, drive pathologic inflammation, and disrupt alveolar bone homeostasis via osteoclast-osteoblast uncoupling.

The pathogen interacts sequentially with non-hematopoietic barriers (gingival epithelium, fibroblasts), myeloid immune cells (neutrophils, macrophages, dendritic cells), and lymphoid cells (B and T cells), activating MAPK/NF-κB cascades, NLRP3 inflammasome, and RANKL/OPG imbalance, culminating in bone loss.

Localized aggressive periodontitis (LAP) is caused predominantly by serotype b (JP2 clone) with a 530-bp deletion in the ltxA promoter driving excessive leukotoxin; alveolar bone loss results from both increased osteoclastogenesis and impaired osteoblastogenesis.

## 세줄요약

응집간균 (Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans, Aa)은 백혈구독소 (Leukotoxin, LtxA), 세포독성팽창독소 (Cytolethal Distending Toxin, CDT), 내독소 (LPS) 등 다중 독성인자를 통해 숙주 면역을 회피하고 병적 염증을 유발해 치조골 항상성을 붕괴시킨다.

병원균은 비조혈계 장벽(치은 상피·섬유아세포), 골수계 면역세포(호중구·대식세포·수지상세포), 림프계 세포(B·T세포)와 순차 상호작용하여 MAPK/NF-κB, NLRP3 인플라마좀, RANKL/OPG 불균형을 활성화하고 궁극적으로 치조골 소실 (Alveolar Bone Loss)을 유발한다.

국소 공격성 치주염 (Localized Aggressive Periodontitis, LAP)은 주로 혈청형 b (JP2 클론, ltxA 프로모터 530-bp 결실)가 원인이며, 골 소실은 파골세포 (Osteoclast) 활성 증가와 조골세포 (Osteoblast) 활성 억제가 동시에 작용한 결과이다.

## Key Findings

### Organism Classification and Serotypes
- Gram-negative, capnophilic (CO2-requiring) coccobacillus; facultative anaerobe
- Serotypes a through g; serotypes a, b, c most isolated from diseased sites
- Serotype b JP2 clone: 530-bp deletion in ltxA operon promoter → excessive leukotoxin production → enhanced virulence in LAP
- Scaling and root planing alone poorly achieves Aa depletion; systemic antibiotics or surgery required

### Primary Virulence Factors

**LtxA (Leukotoxin)**
- Surface-associated and secreted; kills PMNs, monocytes, T cells
- Mechanism: binds LFA-1 (CD18/CD11b) on leukocytes → cofilin dephosphorylation, actin depolymerization, lysosomal-mediated cell death
- Pore formation in monocyte membrane → ATP leak → P2X7 receptor activation → K+ influx → NLRP3 inflammasome → caspase-1 → IL-1β, IL-18 secretion
- Serotype b JP2 strains: enhanced cytotoxicity against neutrophils; conflicting evidence on PMN phagocytosis evasion

**CDT (Cytolethal Distending Toxin)**
- CdtA, CdtB, CdtC holocomplex
- Induces G2/M cell cycle arrest in monocytes; promotes apoptosis
- Upregulates RANKL on gingival fibroblasts and PDL cells
- Induces IL-1β and IL-18 via NLRP3/ASC/caspase-1/ROS/ATP/K+ inflammasome pathway
- Detected in majority of Swedish and Brazilian Aa isolates

**LPS (Lipopolysaccharide)**
- Recognized by TLR2, TLR4 (primary), TLR5; TLR4 is principal receptor
- Activates MyD88 → IκB-α phosphorylation → NF-κB activation
- Activates all three MAPKs: p38, JNK, ERK in macrophages
- p38/MK2 axis: MK2 phosphorylates TTP, preventing ARE-mediated mRNA degradation → cytokine translation (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-10, IL-8, PGE)
- MKP-1 (anti-inflammatory phosphatase): dephosphorylates p-p38, p-JNK → dampens cytokine production and inflammatory infiltrate
- Concentration-dependent effects on osteoblasts: 0.1 µg/mL LPS suppresses BSP (osteoblast differentiation marker); 0.01 µg/mL LPS enhances it
- LPS at 1 µg/mL inhibits murine calvaria DNA synthesis by 30% and collagen by 40%

**Other Virulence Factors**
- PGA (poly-N-acetylglucosamine exopolysaccharide): enhances colonization, biofilm formation, decreases monocyte viability
- OMP29 (outer membrane protein): stimulates RANKL; surface RANKL on T cells primed with OMP29 essential for osteoclastogenesis
- CPA-c (capsular polysaccharide antigen, serotype c): induces osteoblast apoptosis via Fas system independent of Bcl-2/NO
- CagE: type IV secretion system homologue; induces apoptosis in primary human osteoblast cultures

### Non-Hematopoietic Host Barriers (Section I)

**Gingival Epithelium**
- Aa migrates through three layers of human gingival epithelium
- Upregulates TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8; IL-8 is CXCR1/CXCR2 ligand → recruits neutrophils
- Induces apoptosis via caspase 3/7 pathway; Smad2 phosphorylation via TGF-βR1 signaling
- Disruption of epithelium → recruitment of hematopoietic immune cells

**Gingival and PDL Fibroblasts**
- Secrete IL-6, IL-8 in response to Aa
- Aa does not directly stimulate IL-1β and TNF-α from fibroblasts, but both cytokines stimulate RANKL via p38 MAPK on stromal cells
- PDL fibroblasts: Aa activates p38 and JNK phosphorylation

### Hematopoietic Immune Response (Section II)

**Chemokines**
- CCL3 (MIP-1α), CCR5, CXCR3, IP-10 (CXCL10) elevated in aggressive periodontitis tissue biopsies
- CXCL1 (neutrophil chemoattractant, binds CXCR1/CXCR2) — first chemokine mRNA upregulated post-inoculation
- CCL3 elevated → prognostic marker for bone loss in Aa-colonized subjects

**Neutrophils (PMNs)**
- First responders; Aa-specific IgG enhances PMN phagocytosis and killing
- Non-oxidative proteases: cathepsin G (more bactericidal than elastase), elastase
- Cathepsin G: greater bactericidal activity against Aa than elastase
- ROS production induced by Aa; self-aggregating Aa with rough fimbriated surface may impair phagocytosis
- Intracellular azithromycin accumulation in neutrophils → decreased Aa survival

**Macrophages**
- TLR2 + TLR4 double deficiency: more prominent blunting of TNF-α, IL-6 than single deficiency
- MyD88 deficiency further reduces cytokine production → other receptors also propagate inflammation
- Aa stimulates IL-12p40 (MyD88-dependent) → Th1 polarization
- MK2 attenuation in rat model → decreased inflammatory infiltrate and bone loss
- MKP-1 overexpression → decreased TRAP+ osteoclasts and alveolar bone loss

**Monocytes / Inflammasome**
- LtxA and CDT: primary activators of NLRP3 inflammasome → caspase-1 → IL-1β, IL-18
- Serotype a: upregulates NLRP3, attenuates NLRP6 → increased IL-1β (CDT- and LtxA-independent mechanism in one study)
- Conflicting evidence: one study (serotype b) — cathepsin B and ROS, not NLRP3/caspase-1, attenuate IL-1β
- JAK2/STAT3 axis: Aa induces cell cycle arrest in G1 phase in macrophages; SOCS3 overexpression normalizes cycle

**Dendritic Cells**
- Monocytes from LAP patients spontaneously differentiate into CD11c+ DCs
- Aa = most potent IL-12p70 inducer among periodontal pathogens
- Serotype b: more preferentially elicits IL-12, IL-1β, IL-23, CCR5, CCR6 via TLR2 (serotypes a, c rely more on TLR4)

**B and T Lymphocytes**
- B cells: activated B cells (MHC Class II+) increase in draining lymph nodes; plasma cells secrete opsonic IgG2a against Aa
- T cells: CD3+CD4+ and CD3+CD8+ increase at 1 week post-infection; Th1/Th2/Th17/Treg all implicated
- Serotype b: most robust Th1 and Th17 response via DCs; serotype-dependent Th phenotype
- Treg (CCR4, IL-4, CCL22 pathway): suppresses inflammation and attenuates bone loss — CCR4/IL-4/CCL22 deficiency → exacerbated bone loss

### Alveolar Bone Homeostasis (Section III)

**RANKL/OPG Axis**
- 91.7% of subjects with vertical bone loss radiographically were Aa-positive
- RANKL (expressed on fibroblasts, osteoblasts, T cells) > OPG → net bone resorption
- Aa induces RANKL via: CDT, LPS, OMP29 on gingival fibroblasts/PDL cells; T cell OMP29 priming
- OPG loss via Treg depletion → enhanced bone resorption
- NFATc1: master transcription factor of osteoclastogenesis; activated by RANKL-RANK signaling

**Direct Osteoclastogenesis**
- LPS + TNF-α → p38/JNK MAPK → direct osteoclast formation from dOCPs
- CXCL1/CXCL2 (MKP-1 regulated): dampen LPS-induced osteoclastogenesis
- IL-1R signaling critical: IL-1R antagonist → decreased osteoclast formation and bone loss
- Bone remodeling sequence disrupted: Activation-Resorption-Reversal-Formation uncoupled

**MMPs and Bone Degradation**
- Aa induces MMP-1, -2, -9 and TIMP genes in murine infection model
- LPS → secreted MMP-1, -3, -9 and TIMP-1 from peripheral blood monocytes
- Aa has direct bone-degrading collagenase activity; binds saliva-coated hydroxyapatite

**Osteoblast Impairment**
- Aa impairs osteoblast proliferation, collagen synthesis, differentiation, and promotes apoptosis
- LPS (1 µg/mL): inhibits DNA synthesis 30%, collagen synthesis 40% in calvaria explants
- Capsular material (LPS-free): inhibits DNA/collagen synthesis at 10 ng/mL
- CPA-c: osteoblast apoptosis via Fas system (independent of Bcl-2, NO)
- NLRP3 knockdown (siRNA) attenuates Aa-induced osteoblast apoptosis in MG63 cells
- CagE secreted protein: significant osteoblast apoptosis in primary human cultures
- Conclusion: alveolar bone loss = increased osteoclastogenesis + decreased osteoblastogenesis (dual mechanism)

## Study Design
- Type: narrative review
- Scope: comprehensive review of Aa pathogenesis across non-hematopoietic barriers, hematopoietic immune cells (myeloid and lymphoid), and osteoimmunology
- Evidence base: predominantly in vitro and murine pre-clinical models; some human clinical studies (LAP patients)
- Limitations: majority of mechanistic findings are in vitro; many strain/serotype-specific discrepancies; mast cell and NK cell data very limited

## Clinical Relevance
- Serotype b JP2 clone: highest virulence, most robust Th1/Th17 response → consider serotyping in LAP management
- Antibiotic adjunct essential: SRP alone inadequate for Aa elimination; systemic antibiotics (azithromycin shown to enhance PMN killing) recommended
- RANKL/OPG and IL-1R axes: potential therapeutic targets for bone loss attenuation
- Dual bone remodeling disruption (osteoclast ↑ + osteoblast ↓): periodontal bone loss cannot be addressed by anti-resorptive therapy alone
