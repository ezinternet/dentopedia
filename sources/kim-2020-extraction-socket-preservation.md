## Why Ingested

Provides the Korean OMFS-society framing of the extraction socket preservation (ESP) controversy — including a 4-class socket defect classification and the "ESP does not change implant success or MBL" position — which sits against the ARP-overtreatment critique → [[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/mardas-2023-alveolar-ridge-preservation-overtreatment]] and complements the same-genre Korean primer → [[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/che-2024-alveolar-ridge-preservation-review]].

## Three-line Summary

Editor's-opinion narrative review (J Korean Assoc Oral Maxillofac Surg 2020;46:435-439, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital OMFS) summarizing extraction socket preservation (ESP) indications, graft materials, socket-sealing options, and implant timing.

Post-extraction ridge width shrinks about 50% within 1 year (5-7 mm average, two-thirds in the first 3 months); ESP reduces but does not prevent resorption, does not change implant success or marginal bone loss, and implants are recommended at least 4 months after ESP.

ESP is indicated selectively — esthetic sites, severe residual wall destruction, and delayed implant placement — while no consensus standard protocol exists and primary closure is not essential as long as the procedure stays minimally traumatic.

## 세줄요약

종설 (Editor's Opinion, 대한구강악안면외과학회지 2020;46:435-439, 분당서울대 구강악안면외과): 발치와 보존술 (Extraction Socket Preservation, ESP)의 적응증·이식재·소켓 밀봉·임플란트 식립 시기를 정리.

발치 후 1년 내 치조제 폭이 약 50% 감소 (평균 5-7 mm, 2/3가 첫 3개월 내); ESP는 흡수를 줄이지만 완전히 막지는 못하며 임플란트 성공률·변연골소실 (Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)에는 영향이 없고, 식립은 ESP 후 최소 4개월 뒤 권장.

ESP는 심미 부위·잔존 골벽의 심한 결손·지연 식립 예정인 경우에 선택적으로 시행하며, 표준 프로토콜 합의는 없고 1차 봉쇄 (primary closure)는 필수가 아니되 최소 침습이 중요함.

## Key Contributions
- Quantifies post-extraction disuse atrophy: ~50% ridge width loss within 1 year, 5-7 mm average, two-thirds inside the first 3 months.
- Presents a 4-class extraction-socket defect classification (Class I 4-wall → Class IV 1-wall with vertical and horizontal deficiency) as the basis for deciding whether ESP is needed.
- States the negative-view position explicitly: ESP reduces but cannot prevent resorption, and grafting may impair natural bone healing.
- Explicit clinical bottom line: ESP does not affect implant success rate or marginal bone loss; primary closure is not essential; implant placement ~4 months after ESP.
- Buccal bone wall thickness is named the single most important prognostic factor — ESP recommended at ≤1 mm with slowly resorbing substitutes (DBBM, alloplast).

## Methodology
Literature-based narrative review (Editor's Opinion; no systematic search, no pooling). Structure: (1) the ESP controversy (positive vs negative view), (2) surgical technique, (3) bone graft materials by origin, (4) socket sealing materials, (5) timing of implant placement, (6) seven-point summary.

## Key Results
- **Ridge change after extraction:** ~50% width reduction within 1 year; 5-7 mm average loss; two-thirds within 3 months; maxillary buccal resorption more pronounced in molars; mandibular buccal loss exceeds lingual.
- **Historical reference figures cited:** 2009 SR — 3.87 mm horizontal / 1.67 mm vertical loss in the first 3 months; 2012 RCT — >60% of total resorption within 6 months.
- **ESP effect size cited (Avila-Ortiz et al.):** ESP group had significantly less resorption than simple extraction — 1.89 mm horizontal, 2.07 mm labial vertical, 1.18 mm lingual vertical; effect best in the maxilla, where it can avoid/minimize sinus elevation and allow flapless surgery.
- **Socket class I-IV:** I = intact 4-wall; II = 3-wall (1 dehiscence/fenestration); III = type 1 adequate height/inadequate width, type 2 two intact walls; IV = 1-wall with inadequate height and width.
- **Indications:** esthetic concern, severe destruction of residual bone walls after extraction, delayed implant treatment.
- **Materials:** autogenous (limited by resorption), xenogeneic (PBBM/DBBM — 82.3% new-bone socket fill at 9 months in Artzi's histomorphometry; DPBM comparable to DBBM), synthetic (HA, BCP, bioactive glass, calcium sulfate, Bioplant HTR), allogeneic (FDBA, putty/gel forms), growth factors (rhBMP-2, PRP/PRF, P-15, VEGF).
- **Socket sealing:** autogenous free gingival graft/connective tissue, acellular dermal matrix, resorbable collagen membrane (BCP + cross-linked collagen tolerated early exposure), non-resorbable dPTFE, collagen sponge (Bio-Col).
- **Timing:** new bone grows from the recipient bed up to the lower third of the defect; ~4 months healing recommended, primary stability should come from basal bone, not the graft.

## Limitations
Editor's opinion, not a systematic review — no search strategy, no risk-of-bias, no quantitative synthesis. Evidence claims are borrowed from cited SRs/RCTs without appraisal. No new data.

## Glossary
- **ESP:** Extraction Socket Preservation — bone grafting at the time of extraction to limit ridge atrophy.
- **ARP:** Alveolar Ridge Preservation — used synonymously with ESP.
- **DBBM/PBBM:** Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral / Porous Bovine Bone Mineral — slowly resorbing xenograft.
- **DPBM:** Deproteinized Porcine Bone Mineral.
- **BCP:** Biphasic Calcium Phosphate.
- **MBL:** Marginal Bone Loss.
- **dPTFE:** Dense Polytetrafluoroethylene (non-resorbable membrane).
