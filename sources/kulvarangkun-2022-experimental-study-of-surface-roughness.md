## Why Ingested

Directly relevant to chairside occlusal adjustment finishing protocols — evaluates whether material-specific ceramic polishing sets can restore clinically acceptable surface roughness (Ra <0.2 μm) after diamond bur grinding across three major ceramic types. Cross-material comparison (feldspathic porcelain, lithium disilicate, zirconia) with XRD compositional analysis of polishing wheels adds abrasive science context.

→ [[dental-materials/zirconia/kulvarangkun-2022-experimental-study-of-surface-roughness]]

## Three-line Summary

Lithium disilicate and zirconia extraoral polishing sets (Jota) achieved significantly lower surface roughness than the feldspathic porcelain set and glazing across all three ceramic types. Ra values after LiDi/Zirconia polishing were below the 0.2 μm bacterial-adhesion threshold and below the 0.25–0.5 μm tongue-detection range, meeting clinical acceptability. The feldspathic polishing set, with larger diamond abrasive particles confirmed by SEM and XRD, produced the roughest surfaces and is not recommended as a sole finishing step.

## 세줄요약

리튬 디실리케이트 및 지르코니아 연마 세트(Jota)는 세 종류 세라믹 모두에서 장석질 세라믹 세트 및 글레이징보다 유의하게 낮은 표면 거칠기(Ra)를 달성하였다. 연마 후 Ra 값은 세균 부착 임계치(<0.2 μm) 및 혀 감지 범위(0.25–0.5 μm) 모두를 하회하여 임상적으로 허용 가능하였다. SEM·XRD 분석으로 장석질 연마 세트의 연마 입자가 가장 커서 표면 긁힘이 가장 심하였음이 확인되었다.

## Document Information

- **Study type**: In vitro experimental (n = 120 ceramic specimens)
- **Ceramics tested**: Feldspathic porcelain (Vintage PRO, Shofu), lithium disilicate (IPS e.max CAD, Ivoclar Vivadent), zirconia (Prettau Zirconia, Zirkonzahn)
- **Polishing systems**: Jota kit 1429 (feldspathic set), Jota kit 1433 (lithium disilicate set), Jota kit 1434 (zirconia set); two rubber wheels per set
- **Control**: Glazed surface (manufacturer protocol)
- **Instrumentation**: Profilometer (Talysurf Intra 50), SEM (JSM-6610), XRD (AERIS, Malvern Panalytical), EDS
- **Statistical method**: One-way ANOVA with Dunnett's T3 post hoc test, p <0.05

## Key Contributions

1. First study to include the lithium disilicate–specific polishing set alongside feldspathic and zirconia sets for cross-material comparison.
2. Demonstrated LiDi and Zirconia polishing sets are functionally equivalent in Ra outcomes (no significant difference between the two) across all three ceramic substrates.
3. Provided XRD/EDS compositional evidence: diamond (C) is the main abrasive in all wheels; zirconia set also contains TiO₂, Fe₂O₃, SiC — but polishing outcomes correlate more with abrasive particle size than elemental composition.
4. Confirmed clinical threshold: Ra <0.2 μm (bacterial adhesion prevention per Bollen et al.) was achieved by LiDi and Zirconia sets on all ceramics; feldspathic set failed this threshold for feldspathic porcelain ceramic (Ra 0.6319 μm).
5. Polishing with LiDi/Zirconia sets outperformed glazing — counter to the assumption that glazing restores the smoothest surface.

## Methodology

- **Specimen dimensions**: 15 × 20 × 2 mm; sintered per manufacturer instructions
- **Grinding protocol**: Fine diamond bur (46 μm, Komet), 10,000 rpm, 30 s, 1 bur per 10 specimens
- **Polishing protocol**: Slow-speed handpiece (8,000 rpm), 1 N pressure, 10 passes per wheel in one direction, custom-made standardized device; 1 bur per 10 specimens
- **Roughness measurement**: Ra (μm), 4 mm traversal, 3 points per specimen, mean ± SD reported
- **Subgroups**: n = 10 per ceramic × 4 treatment groups = 40 specimens per ceramic; 120 total
- **Polishing wheel composition**: confirmed by XRD qualitative analysis (10–100°, step 0.01°) and EDS morphology

## Key Results

| Ceramic | Glaze (control) | Feldspathic set | LiDi set | Zirconia set |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feldspathic porcelain | 0.3481 ± 0.2505 μm | 0.6319 ± 0.1145 μm | **0.0476 ± 0.0307 μm** | 0.0590 ± 0.0187 μm |
| Lithium disilicate | 0.1630 ± 0.0601 μm | 0.1217 ± 0.0481 μm | **0.0162 ± 0.0084 μm** | 0.0177 ± 0.0601 μm |
| Zirconia | 0.0981 ± 0.0170 μm | 0.2750 ± 0.0756 μm | **0.0185 ± 0.0044 μm** | 0.0230 ± 0.0189 μm |

- LiDi and Zirconia polishing sets: **not significantly different** from each other on any ceramic
- Feldspathic set and glaze: **not significantly different** from each other (both inferior)
- Feldspathic polishing set exceeded 0.5 μm threshold for feldspathic porcelain ceramic only
- SEM confirmed: feldspathic polishing set had largest abrasive particles → deepest scratches
- XRD: diamond (C) dominant in all; TiO₂ in feldspathic + zirconia sets; SiC exclusive to zirconia set

## Limitations

- Custom standardized device does not replicate clinical hand-polishing variation or contoured crown geometry
- Flat-surface specimens only — clinical restorations are curved
- Surface roughness only; flexural strength, translucency, and fatigue behavior not assessed
- Single brand per ceramic and polishing system — generalizability across brands unclear
- No oral aging or thermocycling — does not address long-term surface changes

## Glossary

- **Ra**: Arithmetical mean surface roughness (μm) — primary output metric
- **Profilometer**: Contact instrument using diamond stylus to measure surface topography
- **SEM (Scanning Electron Microscopy, 주사전자현미경)**: High-magnification surface imaging
- **XRD (X-ray Powder Diffraction, X선 분말회절)**: Crystal phase / elemental composition analysis
- **EDS (Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy, 에너지분산X선분석)**: Elemental mapping of polishing wheel morphology
- **SiC (Silicon Carbide, 탄화규소)**: Second-hardest dental abrasive (Hv 1,800–2,000 kg/mm²); found in zirconia polishing set only
- **Feldspathic porcelain (장석질 도재)**: Glass-matrix ceramic with leucite crystals (~10 μm grain size)
- **Lithium disilicate (리튬 디실리케이트)**: IPS e.max CAD — elongated crystal grain 0.2–1 μm
- **Zirconia (지르코니아)**: Polycrystalline ceramic — ~0.3 μm grain size after final firing
