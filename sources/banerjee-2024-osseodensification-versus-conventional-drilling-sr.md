## Why Ingested
Systematic review comparing OD and conventional drilling for implant stability — adds to the growing body of OD evidence alongside existing wiki entries such as [[implants/osseodensification/gaspar-2022-implant-stability-osseodensification-conventional-sr-ma]] and [[implants/osseodensification/inchingolo-2021-osseodensification-implant-site-osteotomy-sr-ma]]. Provides a 2024 JIPS review with PRISMA methodology and bias assessment using Newcastle-Ottawa Scale and Cochrane RoB tool.

## One-line Summary
SR (n=8 studies, 170 patients, 334 implants) finds OD superior to conventional drilling in ISQ, bone development, and torque across multiple clinical settings.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Synthesizes 8 studies from Egypt (4), India (3), Brazil (1) — geographically distinct cohorts
- Confirms OD superiority in primary stability, torque, and bone growth over conventional drilling
- Uses dual bias tools (Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for observational, Cochrane RoB for RCTs) — more rigorous than single-tool reviews
- Identifies high risk in blinded outcome assessment, allocation concealment, and random sequence generation — flags methodological weaknesses in OD RCT evidence base
- Establishes that 7/8 studies placed implants in maxilla with OD

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: Systematic review following PRISMA guidelines
- Databases: PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, OpenGrey, Google Scholar (through July 2023)
- PICO: Patients needing implants (P), OD drilling (I), conventional drilling (C), implant stability + crestal bone levels (O)
- Inclusion: RCTs and non-RCTs comparing OD vs conventional drilling; reporting ISQ, insertion torque, or other stability methods
- Exclusion: No direct comparison, non-English, animal studies, case reports, reviews
- Records: 113 identified → 83 screened → 26 full-text assessed → 8 included
- Exclusions: retracted (1), unspecified comparative group (2), animal (6), observational (5), unclear results (3), piezo comparison (1)
- Bias: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (observational) + Cochrane RoB (RCTs)
- Meta-analysis: Not conducted due to anticipated heterogeneity

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Total: 8 studies, 170 patients, 334 implants
- OD superior to conventional drilling in: ISQ values, bone development, insertion torque
- All studies in maxilla (7) or maxilla + mandible (1)
- Statistical analysis: lowest bias risk for statistical methods; higher risk in blinding, allocation concealment, randomization
- Notable inclusion: Arafat & Elbaz: posterior maxilla sinus lift study — OD showed significantly higher ISQ vs standard drilling
- Notable inclusion: Aloorker et al.: split-mouth design, posterior maxilla

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- No meta-analysis due to heterogeneity — limits pooled quantitative conclusions
- Only 8 studies with 170 patients — small evidence base
- Predominantly maxillary studies (7/8) — limited mandibular data
- Geographic concentration (Egypt, India, Brazil) — applicability to other bone types uncertain
- High risk of bias in key RCT quality domains across included studies

## 7. Glossary
- **ISQ (Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ)**: Dimensionless scale 1–100 from resonance frequency analysis (공명주파수분석, RFA); higher = greater implant stability
- **Newcastle-Ottawa Scale**: Quality assessment tool for non-randomized observational studies
- **Cochrane Risk of Bias tool**: Standardized RCT bias assessment across domains (randomization, blinding, etc.)
