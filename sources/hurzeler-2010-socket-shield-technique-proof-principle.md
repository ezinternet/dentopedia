## Three-line Summary

Proof-of-principle animal experiment (1 beagle dog, mandibular 3rd/4th premolars; 4 implants, 4-month healing) testing partial buccal root retention ("socket-shield technique") combined with immediate implant placement after hemisecting teeth and retaining the buccal distal root fragment ~1 mm coronal to the buccal bone plate.

All four implants osseointegrated without histologic inflammatory reaction; the retained root fragment showed no resorption, maintained a physiologic periodontal ligament attachment to the buccal bone plate, and exhibited newly formed cementum on the lingual/treated surfaces and in areas contacting the implant.

Clinical implication: retaining the buccal root aspect during immediate implant placement did not interfere with osseointegration and suggested a potential strategy to preserve the buccal bone plate, but as a proof-of-principle (single animal, short-term) it required further histologic and long-term clinical evidence.

## 세줄요약

개념증명 동물실험(Hürzeler 2010, J Clin Periodontol; 비글견 1마리, 하악 제3·4소구치 반절제 후 원심치근 협측편을 협측 골판 상방 약 1 mm 높이로 잔존시킨 socket-shield technique + 즉시식립, 임플란트 4개·치유 4개월).

4개 임플란트 모두 조직학적 염증 없이 골유착되었고, 잔존 치근편은 흡수 없이 협측 골판과 생리적 치주인대 부착을 유지했으며 설측/처치면 및 임플란트 접촉 부위에서 신생 백악질 형성이 관찰되었다.

임상적 의미: 즉시식립 시 협측 치근편 잔존이 골유착을 방해하지 않고 협측 골판 보존에 도움이 될 가능성을 제시했으나, 단일 동물·단기 proof-of-principle 근거이므로 추가 조직학·장기 임상 연구가 필요하다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Introduced and histologically demonstrated the **socket-shield technique (SST)**: retain buccal root fragment during immediate implant placement to preserve periodontal attachment and buccal bone.
- Showed in a beagle dog model that osseointegration can occur adjacent to a retained tooth fragment without inflammatory complications.
- Documented cementum formation on treated dentin surfaces and on implant surfaces in contact areas, suggesting biologic compatibility of the interface.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Proof-of-principle preclinical animal experiment.
- **Sample**: 1 beagle dog; mandibular third and fourth premolars hemisected.
- **Intervention**: Buccal fragment of the distal root retained ~1 mm coronal to buccal bone plate; enamel matrix derivative (EMD) applied; titanium implant placed lingual to tooth fragment either with or without contact to buccal fragment; healing abutment connected.
- **Follow-up/analysis**: 4 months after implant placement; histologic evaluation and backscatter scanning electron microscopy (BSEM).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- All 4 implants osseointegrated without histologic inflammatory reaction.
- Retained tooth fragment: no resorptional processes observed.
- Buccal side of fragment: attached to buccal bone plate by physiologic periodontal ligament.
- Lingual/treated side of fragment: newly formed cementum detected; where implant contacted fragment, newly formed cementum demonstrated directly on implant surface (per abstract/supporting description).
- Overall conclusion in abstract: retaining buccal root aspect did not appear to interfere with osseointegration and may be beneficial in preserving buccal bone plate.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Proof-of-principle level: single animal, small number of sites (4 implants), short-term (4 months).
- No clinical outcome data; technique safety/complications (e.g., shield exposure/mobility, infection) not assessable from this model.
- Authors stated need for further histologic evidence and long-term follow-up before general recommendation.

## 7. Glossary
- **Socket-shield technique (SST)**: Partial root retention technique in which a buccal root fragment (shield) is left in situ during immediate implant placement to preserve periodontal attachment and buccal bone.
- **Bundle bone**: Tooth-dependent alveolar bone lining the socket; resorbs after extraction/loss of periodontal ligament.
- **Enamel matrix derivative (EMD)**: Biologic material used to promote periodontal/regenerative responses.
- **BSEM (backscatter scanning electron microscopy)**: Imaging modality used to assess mineralized tissue interfaces.
