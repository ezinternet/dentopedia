## Why Ingested

First systematic review to comprehensively evaluate both implant diameter and length on FEA-derived stress distribution, providing higher-level synthesis than single FEA studies such as [[implants/kavitha-2023-outcome-implant-diameter-length-distribution]] and [[implants/singh-2024-influence-implant-design-length-stress]]. Resolves the long-debated clinical question of which dimension dominates peri-implant stress.

## Three-line Summary

(Line 1: Systematic review of 40 FEA in-vitro studies (PubMed/Embase/Scopus/WoS, 2003–2023; PROSPERO CRD42022382211) evaluating how implant diameter and length affect bone stress distribution.)
(Line 2: Diameter primarily governs cortical bone stress and crestal overloading while length governs cancellous bone stress; diameter is the dominant factor overall — short implants with larger diameter generated lower stresses than longer implants with smaller diameter.)
(Line 3: Clinically, wider-diameter implants should be prioritized over longer implants for stress reduction, especially in high-load areas; in low-density bone, adequate length gains increasing relevance.)

## 2. Key Contributions
- First comprehensive SR to simultaneously evaluate FEA evidence on both implant diameter and length, synthesizing 40 studies (2003–2023)
- Established that diameter primarily controls cortical bone stress (crestal overloading) while length primarily controls cancellous bone stress
- Quantified that short implants with larger diameter generate lower stresses than longer implants with smaller diameter — directly challenging the clinical default of preferring longer implants
- Identified low-density bone as the key modifier where implant length gains increasing relevance for stress reduction

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review (PRISMA-compliant; no meta-analysis due to heterogeneity of FEA models)
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, SciELO, Open Access Theses (grey literature)
- **Search period**: October 2003 – October 2023
- **n**: 852 records identified → 40 studies met inclusion criteria
- **Eligibility**: Peer-reviewed English FEA in-vitro mathematical studies evaluating both implant diameter and length
- **Outcomes**: Von Mises stress (MPa), microstrain (μStrain) in cortical and cancellous bone; crestal stress; stability factor
- **Bone models**: D1–D4 (Type I–IV) bone quality; most studies used Type II bone

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Diameter effect: primarily on cortical bone stress and peri-implant crestal overloading; wider diameter → reduced stress concentration at bone-implant interface
- Length effect: primarily on cancellous (trabecular) bone stress; length gains relevance as bone density decreases (D3/D4 bone)
- Diameter > length: short implants with larger diameter generated lower stresses than longer implants with smaller diameter under both static and immediate loading FEA models
- Only 5 of 40 studies used immediate loading; 17 applied axial load only, 23 applied multi-directional loading
- Only 1 study conducted experimental validation of FEM; 11 used convergence analysis
- Most studies used titanium (Grade 4 or Ti-6Al-4V) and von Mises stress as the primary metric
- Implant designs varied (threaded, cylindrical, tapered, conical) across posterior maxilla and mandible models predominantly

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- All included studies are in-vitro FEA with no pH simulation, temperature effects, biofilm, or anisotropic bone material properties (most models assumed isotropic materials)
- Only 1 of 40 studies performed experimental validation of the FEM model, limiting clinical transferability
- High heterogeneity in boundary conditions, implant systems, loading conditions, and FEA software prevents quantitative pooling (no meta-analysis possible)
- Future studies should standardize FEA methodology (convergence analysis, experimental validation) and explore interaction effects between diameter and length numerically
- Cantilever length, thread features, abutment collar height, and implant system identified as additional influential factors requiring investigation

## 6. Related Work
- Kavitha 2023: single 3D FEA study on diameter and length under immediate loading — Qiu SR synthesizes this evidence level
- Singh 2024: 2D FEA on implant design and length in posterior maxilla — consistent with Qiu's length-governs-cancellous-bone finding
- Chang 2024: FEA optimization on implant design across D1–D4 bone — concurrent independent confirmation that bone density modifies dimensional requirements
- Leblebicioglu-Kurtulus 2022: FEA on implant design and bone density — foundational FEA paper this SR likely subsumes

## 7. Glossary
- **FEA (Finite Element Analysis)**: In-silico numerical simulation dividing a structure into finite elements to compute stress/strain distribution under applied loads
- **Von Mises stress**: Scalar stress criterion combining all stress components, used to predict material yielding/failure at the bone-implant interface
- **Cortical bone**: Dense outer bone layer at the crestal region; primary load-bearing area sensitive to implant diameter
- **Cancellous/Trabecular bone**: Spongy inner bone; more sensitive to implant length for load distribution
- **D1–D4 bone**: Lekholm-Zarb bone density classification (D1 = densest cortical, D4 = soft trabecular); D4 shows greatest stress sensitivity to implant length
- **Short implant**: Generally defined as ≤10 mm (some definitions ≤8 mm) — finding is that wider diameter compensates for reduced length
- **Narrow implant**: Diameter ≤3.5 mm — generates higher stress than wider implants under same loading
