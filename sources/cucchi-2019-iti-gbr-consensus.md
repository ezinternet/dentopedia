## Why Ingested

ITI GBR Symposium 2016 Bologna consensus — first published statements and clinical recommendations specifically for GBR. Provides comprehensive guidelines on patient selection, membrane choice, bone grafting, fixation, membrane removal timing, complication classification, and soft-tissue management. Directly relevant to [[wiki/overviews/gbr-barrier-membrane-overview]] and [[wiki/overviews/bone-regeneration-protocol-ladder]] for GBR decision-making.

## Three-line Summary

Expert consensus from closed meeting of 14 experienced GBR clinicians (Bologna, October 2016) reviewing systematic/narrative reviews on GBR clinical management. Formulated statements and clinical recommendations across 13 domains: patient selection, blood supply, antibiotic treatment, flap passivation, timing of implant placement, implant surface, barrier membranes, bone grafts, membrane fixation, timing of membrane removal, healing complications, soft-tissue management, GBR in scarred/esthetic sites. Concluded GBR is predictable for vertical/horizontal defects when strict protocols followed; healing >6 months (9 months for severe defects); soft-tissue management critical for long-term success.

## 세줄요약

이탈리아 볼로냐에서 2016년 10월 개최된 GBR 심포지엄의 폐쇄적 합의 회의(14명 전문가) 결과로, GBR 임상 관리의 13개 영역(환자 선택, 혈액 공급, 항생제, 판막 수동화, 식립 시기, 임플란트 표면, 차폐막, 골 이식재, 막 고정, 막 제거 시기, 치유 합병증, 연조직 관리, 반흔/심미 부위 GBR)에 대한 진술과 임상 권고 도출. GBR은 엄격한 프로토콜 준수 시 수직/수평 결손에 예측 가능한 기법; 치유 기간 6개월 이상(중증 결손 9개월) 권고; 연조직 관리가 장기 성공의 또 다른 핵심 요소. 증거 수준은 주로 전문가 의견과 체계적 고찰 기반이며, 향후 RCT 필요성 강조.

## Key Contributions

- First published consensus statements and clinical recommendations specifically for GBR procedures
- Defined GBR as any reconstructive technique using nonresorbable/resorbable barrier + particulate bone graft (excludes onlay/inlay grafts, sinus lift)
- Adopted Wang & Al-Shammari defect classification (Class H horizontal, V vertical, C combined; subdivided small <3mm, medium 4–6mm, large >7mm)
- PASS principles (Primary closure, Angiogenesis, Space maintenance, Stability) as mandatory framework
- Specific membrane recommendations: resorbable for horizontal/small vertical; nonresorbable (PTFE, Ti-reinforced) for large vertical defects
- Bone grafting: allograft/xenograft alone for horizontal with cell-occlusive membrane; xenograft/allograft + autogenous bone for vertical with space-maintaining device
- Membrane fixation: osteosynthesis screws/titanium pins on buccal and lingual/palatal sides recommended
- Nonresorbable membrane removal timing: 4–6 months (simultaneous implants), 6+ months (lateral GBR), 6–12 months (vertical GBR)
- Complication classification for nonresorbable membranes (Fontana et al.): Class I–IV based on exposure size, infection status, timing
- Soft-tissue management: KT width >2 mm recommended around implants in augmented sites
- Scarred sites: Urban classification (vestibule depth × periosteum integrity) with specific flap techniques
- Esthetic sites: staged approach, submerged healing, soft-tissue augmentation, esthetic peri-implant plastic surgery
- Identified research gaps: compromised patients, optimal antibiotic regimen, cortical perforation parameters, membrane fixation methods, long-term soft-tissue outcomes

## Methodology

- **Design**: Expert consensus meeting (closed meeting)
- **Participants**: 14 experienced GBR clinicians/surgeons, speakers at Second GBR Symposium Bologna 2016
- **Evidence base**: 8 systematic/narrative reviews prepared before meeting (Retzepi & Donos; Rakhmatia et al.; Clementini et al.; Carbonell et al.; Al-Nawas & Schiegnitz; Millinkovic & Cordaro; Lutz et al.; Keestra et al.)
- **Process**: Evidence review → interactive discussion → formulation of statements, clinical recommendations, future research implications
- **Scope**: GBR defined as barrier membrane (resorbable/nonresorbable) + particulate bone graft; excluded onlay/inlay grafts, sinus lift

## Key Results

### Patient Selection
- Smoking: light smokers only (<10 cigarettes/day); 80–90% implant survival but increased implant loss/peri-implantitis risk
- Diabetes: HbA1c <7% (53 mmol/mol) mandatory before GBR; controlled diabetes shows similar bone matrix formation but lower BIC
- MRONJ risk (antiresorptive/antiangiogenic therapy): follow AAOMS position papers; drug holiday ≥2 months before/1 month after for oral BPs >4 years or with corticosteroids

### Antibiotic Treatment
- Prophylaxis recommended for all GBR: loading dose 1hr pre-op + postoperative 7 days
- Suggested regimen: 2g amoxicillin+clavulanic acid + 500mg metronidazole 1hr pre-op; then 1g amoxicillin+clavulanic acid q8h ×7 days + 250mg metronidazole q8h ×4 days
- Evidence on antibiotic timing/choice/dose poorly documented; research needed

### Flap Passivation
- Tension-free primary closure critical (PASS "P")
- Mandible: easier (lingual + buccal flaps); Maxilla: buccal rotational, coronally positioned palatal sliding, split palatal rotated, palatal advanced flaps
- Urban et al. classification for anterior maxilla: 4 scenarios (vestibule depth × periosteum integrity)

### Blood Supply
- PASS principles mandatory: Primary closure, Angiogenesis, Space maintenance, Stability
- Cortical perforation/decortication for marrow communication and bleeding into defect
- Controversy on whether perforation accelerates or reduces regeneration; no data on optimal hole diameter/configuration/extent

### Implant Surface
- Rough surfaces (Sa 1–2 μm) associated with stronger bone response than smooth (Sa <0.5) or very rough (Sa >2)
- Simultaneous GBR should use rough-surfaced implants to enhance osseointegration
- Possible long-term peri-implantitis risk with rough surfaces in augmented areas; more data needed

### Timing of Implant Placement
- Systematic review (Clementini et al.): simultaneous 87.7% success (61.5–100%), staged 86.4% (75–98.3%); survival 98.9% vs 100%
- Short follow-up, no meta-analysis possible; clinician preference guides choice
- Staged advantages: less operative time, no primary stability needed, better vascularization, predictable marginal bone, prosthetic guidance
- Simultaneous advantages: native bone assessment, implant-guided augmentation, better membrane/graft/clot stabilization

### Barrier Membranes
- **Nonresorbable**: e-PTFE (5–30 μm pores, bacterial infiltration risk) vs d-PTFE (0.2–0.3 μm pores, eliminates bacterial infiltration); Ti-reinforced for space maintenance in large defects
- **Resorbable**: native collagen (enzymatic degradation, unpredictable resorption) vs cross-linked collagen (longer barrier function, reduced biocompatibility)
- Recommendation: resorbable for horizontal/potentially small vertical; nonresorbable for large vertical defects

### Bone Grafting
- Autogenous bone (AB) = gold standard (osteogenic, osteoinductive, osteoconductive)
- DBBM most used xenograft (osteoconductive scaffold)
- 1:1 DBBM:AB + PTFE membrane successful for vertical augmentation
- AB+DBBM+collagen membrane successful for horizontal augmentation
- Review (Al-Nawas & Schiegnitz): implant survival 97.4% (BSM), 98.6% (AB), 100% (BSM+AB); independent of biomaterial
- Recommendations: horizontal → allograft/xenograft alone + cell-occlusive membrane; vertical → xenograft/allograft + AB + cell-occlusive membrane + space-maintaining device; large defects → autogenous particulate bone

### Membrane Fixation
- Stability critical to avoid graft micromovement; rigid fixation → faster, more organized bone formation
- RCT: significant complications when membranes lacked adequate fixation
- Osteosynthesis screws/titanium pins on buccal and lingual/palatal sides recommended
- Optimal fixation method for human bone regeneration needs research

### Timing of Membrane Removal (nonresorbable)
- Systematic review: horizontal simultaneous 2–8 months, non-simultaneous 3–10 months; vertical 4–12 months (both)
- Working group: lateral GBR ≥6 months (BSM±AB); simultaneous implants 4–6 months; vertical GBR 6–12 months
- High heterogeneity, no clear surgical conclusions; more research needed

### Healing Complications (Fontana et al. classification for e-PTFE)
- **Class I**: early exposure <3mm, no pus → leave 1 month max, 0.2% CHX gel BID, close follow-up
- **Class II**: early exposure >3mm, no pus → immediate removal (bone usually not compromised)
- **Class III**: early exposure with pus → immediate removal, curette infected particles/inflammatory tissue
- **Class IV**: abscess without exposure → immediate removal, curette graft, local antibiotic wash
- Late exposure (>3 months): immediate removal regardless of infection
- d-PTFE/Ti mesh/resorbable may need different management

### Soft-Tissue Management
- KT width >2 mm recommended around augmented-site implants: prevent early marginal bone loss, facilitate hygiene, improve plaque/gingival scores
- Medium/long-term (5–10yr) studies needed on soft-tissue augmentation effects on stability/esthetics/PROMs
- Methods: apically positioned flap + FGG/CTG; coronally advanced flap + CTG; collagen matrices with apical/coronal flaps

### GBR in Scarred Sites
- Urban classification: 4 scenarios (deep/shallow vestibule × native/scarred periosteum)
- Techniques: remote/safety flap, periosteal incision, elastic fiber separation, periosteoplasty, muscle-releasing incision, papillary shifting, double-layer suturing

### GBR in Esthetic Sites
- Staged implant placement, submerged healing with soft-tissue augmentation
- Resorbable membranes for horizontal small defects; nonresorbable/Ti mesh for horizontal large + vertical defects
- Re-entry with/without soft-tissue augmentation + esthetic peri-implant plastic surgery

## Limitations

- Evidence largely based on expert opinion, systematic reviews of heterogeneous studies, case series
- No meta-analyses possible due to interstudy heterogeneity and missing data
- Short follow-up in many cited studies
- Compromised patient protocols based on AAOMS/limited data, not GBR-specific trials
- Optimal antibiotic regimen, cortical perforation parameters, membrane fixation methods not established
- Long-term (≥5–10yr) soft-tissue augmentation outcomes lacking

## Glossary

- **GBR (Guided Bone Regeneration, 골유도재생술)**: 차폐막과 골 이식재를 이용해 골 결손을 재생하는 술식
- **PASS principles**: Primary closure (무장력 1차 봉합), Angiogenesis (혈관신생), Space maintenance (공간 유지), Stability (안정성) — GBR 성공의 4대 원칙
- **DBBM (Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral, 탈단백 우골 광물질)**: 이종골 골이식재의 대표격, 골전도성 공간유지 재료
- **e-PTFE / d-PTFE**: expanded/high-density 폴리테트라플루오로에틸렌 차폐막 (비흡수성)
- **KT (Keratinized Tissue, 각화조직/각화점막)**: 임플란트 주위 연조직 건강과 관련된 임상 지표
- **MRONJ (Medication-Related Osteonecrosis of the Jaw, 약제 관련 턱뼈 괴사)**: 항흡수제/항혈관신생제 치료 환자에서 발생하는 골 괴사
- **FGG / CTG**: Free Gingival Graft (유리치은이식) / Connective Tissue Graft (결합조직이식)
- **BIC (Bone-to-Implant Contact, 골-임플란트 접촉률)**: 임플란트 표면과 골의 직접 접촉 비율
