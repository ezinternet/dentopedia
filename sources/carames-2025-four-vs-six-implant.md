## Why Ingested

4 vs 6 임플란트 IFCD(implant-supported fixed complete denture) 생존 논쟁에 대해 기존 [[complete-denture/difrancesco-2021-maxillary-overdentures-four-six-implants-sr-ma]] (overdenture, SR+MA)는 유의차 없음을 보고했으나, 표본이 작고 splinted bar-retained overdenture로 국한됐다. 본 논문(Caramês 2025)은 943명·5989개 임플란트, 최대 17년 추적, CDSS(clinical decision support system) 기반 systematic treatment protocol을 적용한 대규모 retrospective cohort로 fixed(screw-retained) IFCD에서 동일한 결론(생존율 유의차 없음)을 재확인·확장한다.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort study of 943 patients receiving 5989 immediately loaded implants (2431 quadrants) supporting four- or six-implant full-arch fixed complete dentures (IFCDs), with mean follow-up 5.0 ± 3.2 years (range 0–17), using a standardized anatomy-driven clinical decision support system (CDSS).

Overall 2-year and 5-year cumulative survival rates were comparable between four and six IFCDs (2-year: 98.6% vs 98.8%, p=0.362; 5-year: 98.4% vs 98.7%, p=0.343); multilevel Cox models found jaw type (maxilla HR 1.815, p=0.037) and age (HR 1.026/year, p=0.030) — not implant number or alveolar atrophy classification — as significant predictors of implant loss.

Under a systematically applied patient-centered CDSS, four and six IFCDs achieve comparably high mid-to-long-term implant survival regardless of jaw or statistical model, though prosthetic/technical complications were not evaluated (beyond scope).

## 세줄요약

943명·5989개 임플란트(2431개 구획)를 대상으로 4개 또는 6개 임플란트로 지지되는 즉시부하 전악 고정성 보철(implant-supported fixed complete denture, IFCD)을 비교한 후향적 코호트 연구로, 평균 추적기간 5.0±3.2년(0–17년), 표준화된 해부학 기반 임상의사결정지원시스템(clinical decision support system, CDSS)을 적용했다.

전체 2년·5년 누적 생존율은 4개 임플란트군과 6개 임플란트군 사이 유의차가 없었으며(2년: 98.6% vs 98.8%, p=0.362; 5년: 98.4% vs 98.7%, p=0.343), 다수준 콕스 모델에서 임플란트 개수나 치조골 위축 분류가 아닌 악골 종류(상악 HR 1.815, p=0.037)와 연령(HR 1.026/년, p=0.030)이 유의한 임플란트 상실 예측인자였다.

체계적인 환자 중심 CDSS 지침 하에서는 악골이나 통계 모델과 무관하게 4개·6개 IFCD 모두 높고 비교 가능한 중장기 임플란트 생존율을 보이나, 보철적·기공적 합병증은 본 연구 범위 밖으로 평가되지 않았다.

- **Title**: Four vs. Six Implant Full-Arch Restorations—A Direct Comparative Retrospective Analysis in a Large Controlled Treatment Cohort
- **Authors**: João Manuel Mendez Caramês, Helena Cristina Oliveira Francisco, Filipe Araújo Vieira, Gonçalo Bártolo Caramês, Jorge Nuno do Rosário Martins, Duarte Nuno da Silva Marques
- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Medicine (MDPI), 2025, 14, 4237
- **DOI**: 10.3390/jcm14124237
- **Study type**: Retrospective cohort, single private clinic (Implantology Institute, Lisbon, Portugal), November 2004 – November 2021
- **Funding/Affiliation**: Faculdade de Medicina Dentária, Universidade de Lisboa; LIBPhys-FCT

## 2. Key Contributions

- Largest direct comparative dataset to date on four vs. six implant IFCDs (943 patients, 5989 implants, up to 17-year follow-up) treated under a single standardized, anatomy-driven CDSS rather than empirical operator judgment.
- First study to systematically analyze implant number/distribution impact on IFCD survival within a structured decision-making framework (previously described Carames Classification, CC, of alveolar atrophy Classes I–V).
- Uses causal directed acyclic graph (DAG) methodology to derive Canonical Adjustment Sets for confounding in multilevel Cox proportional hazard models — a more rigorous confounder-selection approach than typical stepwise/univariate adjustment in prior implant-survival literature.
- Reports both overall and jaw-specific (maxilla vs mandible) analyses, plus qualitative failure-pattern characteristics (early vs late loss, clustering).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective analysis of clinical records, STROBE-guided reporting, IRB/Ethics Committee approved (II2021-05), Declaration of Helsinki.
- **Population**: 943 patients, 5989 implants, 2431 restored quadrants; convenience sample from patients treated November 2004–November 2021.
- **Treatment protocol**: All patients treated per a previously published standardized, anatomy- and patient-centered workflow. Full-arch screw-retained IFCDs with immediate loading, conceptually similar to All-on-4/All-on-6, with four or six implants assigned per arch based on the CDSS.
- **CDSS / Carames Classification (CC)**: Anatomic criteria (vertical/horizontal alveolar dimensions at 3 predefined positions per quadrant) define maxillary/mandibular classes I (no atrophy) to V (severe atrophy); Option A/B restorative strategies mapped to CC class plus risk factors (systemic conditions, smoking, bruxism) determine implant number, position, angulation, grafting need, and prosthesis type.
- **Exclusions**: Uncontrolled systemic/local/psychological conditions, uncontrolled diabetes, pregnancy/nursing, history of head/neck radiation, history of IV bisphosphonate therapy.
- **Imaging/surgery standardization**: Identical CBCT protocol (Planmeca Promax, 0.20 mm voxel, 80 kV, 15 mA, 12 s); one experienced surgeon (J.M.M.C.) plus clinic prosthodontists for all patients; acrylic immediate provisionals, definitive porcelain-veneered/monolithic zirconia/ceramic/acrylic-metal hybrid prosthesis at 6 months; recall at 2-week intervals for 12 weeks post-op, then 4-month intervals.
- **Statistics**: Kaplan–Meier survival analysis with log-rank tests (Sidak-adjusted for multiple comparisons); single-predictor and multilevel Cox proportional hazard models (patient as random effect) with confounders selected via causal DAG-derived Canonical Adjustment Sets; significance threshold p ≤ 0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Overall implant loss**: four IFCD 1.61% (42/2608) vs six IFCD 1.21% (41/3381), p=0.240 (not significant).
- **Maxilla**: 2.4% vs 1.4% loss (p=0.130); **Mandible**: 1.4% vs 0.6% loss (p=0.109).
- **Kaplan–Meier CSRs**: Overall 2-yr 98.6% (4-impl) vs 98.8% (6-impl), p=0.362; 5-yr 98.4% vs 98.7%, p=0.343. Maxilla 2-yr 97.7% vs 98.6% (p=0.084, most pronounced difference); 5-yr 97.5% vs 98.4% (p=0.105). Mandible 2-yr 98.9% vs 99.4% (p=0.224); 5-yr 98.6% vs 99.4% (p=0.136).
- **Single-predictor Cox HRs**: four IFCD vs six IFCD showed 1.624× (CI 0.886–2.976, p=0.117) risk at maxilla and 1.959× (CI 0.806–4.762, p=0.138) at mandible — neither significant.
- **Overall multilevel Cox model (Canonical Adjustment Set)**: restoration type (4 vs 6 IFCD) HR 1.678 (CI 0.908–3.102, p=0.098, NS); jaw type (maxilla vs mandible) HR 1.815 (CI 1.037–3.175, p=0.037, significant); age HR 1.026/year (CI 1.003–1.050, p=0.030, significant). Anatomic classification (CC), implant position, bone regeneration, implant length, gender, bruxism, cigarettes/day, comorbidities: all non-significant.
- **Jaw-level multilevel Cox models**: Maxilla — 4 vs 6 IFCD HR 1.294 (CI 0.327–5.122, p=0.710, NS); age HR 1.034/year (p=0.038, significant). Mandible — 4 vs 6 IFCD HR 1.905 (CI 0.651–5.570, p=0.240, NS); bone regeneration trended (HR 2.211, p=0.060) and cigarettes/day trended (HR 1.045, p=0.054), neither significant.
- **Failure pattern**: mandibular four-implant restorations failed later on average (476.3 ± 713.6 days, 67% early, 26% clustered) than six-implant (111.8 ± 44.8 days, 83% early, 67% clustered), though differences not statistically significant (p=0.401, p=0.151).
- **Patient-level loss**: 68/943 patients (7.2%) had ≥1 implant failure; no significant between-group differences at maxilla (7.8% vs 6.8%, p=0.805) or mandible (4.7% vs 2.8%, p=0.397).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Retrospective, single-center, single-surgeon design limits generalizability; convenience sample (not randomized between 4 vs 6 groups — assignment driven by CDSS/anatomy, introducing confounding by indication that the DAG-adjustment approach attempts but cannot fully eliminate).
- Prosthetic and technical complications (screw loosening, veneer chipping, framework fracture) were explicitly out of scope — survival-only outcome; conclusions cannot be extended to overall restoration success/complication burden.
- Some subgroup comparisons (e.g., mandibular failure timing/clustering) were qualitatively distinct but underpowered for statistical significance given low absolute event counts (n=83 total implant losses across the cohort).
- No CC I events observed in maxilla and no CC V events in mandible, forcing reference-category substitutions in Cox models for those jaw-specific analyses.
- Authors note findings should be interpreted within the context of the specific CDSS/Carames Classification protocol used; may not generalize to less standardized treatment-decision pathways in other practices.

## 7. Glossary

- **IFCD**: Implant-supported fixed complete denture — screw-retained full-arch prosthesis supported by dental implants.
- **CDSS**: Clinical decision support system — here, a structured protocol mapping anatomic/risk-factor assessment to a defined treatment (implant number/position/grafting) decision.
- **CC (Carames Classification)**: Alveolar atrophy classification (Classes I–V, least to most severe) based on vertical/horizontal ridge dimensions, used within the CDSS to guide implant treatment planning.
- **CSR**: Cumulative survival rate, derived via Kaplan–Meier analysis.
- **DAG / Canonical Adjustment Set**: Causal directed acyclic graph methodology used to systematically select the minimal sufficient confounder set for a given exposure–outcome relationship in the Cox models, reducing risk of over- or under-adjustment/collider bias.
- **STROBE**: Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology — reporting guideline followed for this study.
