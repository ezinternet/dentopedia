## Why Ingested

This is a broad narrative-review synthesis of clear aligner therapy (CAT) biomechanics across the four most biomechanically demanding movement types — useful as an anchor/overview reference tying together the single-topic papers already in the wiki, such as [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/nakornnoi-2024-aligner-trimline-biomechanics-tooth-movement-sr]] (trimline as a third predictability determinant), [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/tang-2025-evaluating-the-effectiveness-of-clear]] (curve-of-Spee leveling in extraction vs non-extraction CAT), and [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/cao-2025-clear-aligner-biomechanics-finite-element-analysis-sr]] (FEA studies organized by movement direction). It also explicitly names the "Roller Coaster Effect" in extraction-case CAT, directly relevant to any future ingest on that specific phenomenon.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Chinese-language, 华西口腔医学杂志/West China J Stomatol 2026;44(1):17–24) synthesizing biomechanical mechanisms and clinical strategies for four complex clear aligner therapy (CAT) tooth-movement designs — molar distalization, arch expansion, vertical tooth movement, and extraction-space closure.

Overall CAT expression rate (실제 이동량/설계 이동량) averages only ~50%, with molar distalization performing best (58–75%), vertical movement worst (13–52.9%), and arch expansion / extraction cases showing large tipping-dominant deviations requiring anchorage reinforcement (mini-screws, overcorrection, torque compensation) to approach bodily movement.

Clinical implication: CAT treatment planning for these four complex movement types should build in movement-specific overcorrection, attachment design, and mini-screw-assisted anchorage from the outset rather than relying on mid-treatment refinement, since the review is narrative (no formal quality appraisal or pooled statistics) and expression-rate figures come from heterogeneous single-center/FEA studies.

중국어 내러티브 리뷰(华西口腔医学杂志/West China J Stomatol 2026;44(1):17–24)로, 무托槽 투명교정(Clear Aligner Therapy, CAT)의 4가지 복잡 치아이동 디자인 — 구치 원심이동, 확장, 수직적 치아이동, 발치공간 폐쇄 — 에 대한 생체역학 기전과 임상전략을 종합.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Proposes a unified clinical decision-support framework organizing CAT biomechanics literature around four common complex treatment designs: molar distalization, arch expansion, vertical tooth movement, and extraction cases — rather than the single-technique focus (e.g., one anchorage site, one traction method) of prior literature.
- Synthesizes quantitative expression-rate (实现率) data across all four movement types, giving clinicians a comparative sense of which movement types are biomechanically reliable (molar distalization) vs. unreliable (vertical movement, molar-region arch expansion).
- Names and explains three CAT-specific biomechanical phenomena: the "Watermelon Seed Effect" (西瓜籽效应 — force distributed unevenly across occlusal/buccal/lingual surfaces due to asymmetric crown geometry), the "Pendulum Effect" (钟摆效应 — lingual tipping + extrusion of anterior teeth during posterior segment retraction), and the "Roller Coaster Effect" (过山车效应 — mid-arch open bite from anterior/posterior premature contact during extraction space closure).
- Consolidates clinical strategy evidence (overcorrection design, attachment selection/placement, mini-screw-assisted anchorage, intra-/inter-arch elastics) with quantitative parameters (e.g., specific overcorrection angles, anchorage-preparation degrees) drawn from the underlying primary studies.
- Identifies AI-assisted individualized treatment planning and multidisciplinary materials/engineering integration (e.g., 3D-printed aligners with regionally customized thickness/mechanical properties) as the two priority directions for future CAT development.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative review structure, organized into four movement-type sections (each with a "biomechanics and risk factors" subsection followed by a "strategy optimization" subsection), plus a concluding "reflections and outlook" section. No explicit systematic search strategy (databases, search terms, date range), no PRISMA flow diagram, no inclusion/exclusion criteria, and no formal risk-of-bias/quality appraisal tool are reported in the extracted text — this is a narrative synthesis despite Chinese-abstract language ("系统性回顾") suggesting otherwise. Evidence base draws on retrospective/prospective clinical studies, finite element analysis (FEA) studies, in-vitro simulator studies, and case reports, cited throughout via numbered references (~90 citations visible in extracted text).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Molar distalization** (Section 1):
- Maxillary first molar expression rate: 69%; maxillary second molar: 75%
- Mandibular first molar: 58%; mandibular second molar: 74%
- Maxillary molar distalization of 2.25 mm did not significantly alter axial inclination (bodily movement achieved)
- Third molar presence reduces overall distalization efficiency by 17% (FEA, Kuguoglu et al.)
- Palatal mini-screw placement improves distalization efficiency and torque control vs. buccal placement

**Arch expansion** (Section 2):
- Canine/premolar region expansion expression rate: ~70% (canine), ~50% (premolar); first molar region only ~29%
- CT-based overall (bodily) tooth movement expression rate averaged only 36.35% despite crown-level expression of 70% (Zhou et al.) — most expansion is tipping, not bodily movement
- Mixed dentition expansion expression rate approaches 80%, higher than permanent dentition
- 1.5× overcorrection raised expression rates from baseline (51.86%–84.17% depending on tooth position) to 75.16%–122.37%; adding 1.3° crown-lingual torque compensation reduced these gains back to 56.15%–87.80%

**Vertical tooth movement** (Section 3):
- Lower anterior intrusion expression rate: 34%; upper central incisor intrusion: only 13%
- Molar extrusion expression rate: 35–40%
- Curve-of-Spee leveling in deep-bite CAT cases: average expression rate only 52.9%
- Open-bite molar intrusion averaged only 1 mm
- Combined buccal + palatal mini-screws achieved 1–2 mm absolute molar intrusion (author's own group data) but increased palatal root resorption risk

**Extraction cases** (Section 4):
- Maxillary arch shows greater anchorage loss than mandibular: incisor torque/canine axial inclination loss up to 10°; molar mesial movement can exceed 2 mm (1.24–2.28× the mandibular value)
- Each 1 mm increase in anterior overbite associated with an additional 1.5° mesial molar tipping (Ren et al., retrospective study)
- Anchorage-preparation ("备抗") design: molars typically need 8°–10° pre-programmed distal tip (for 1–2 mm planned movement); canines 15°–20° (for 6 mm planned movement)
- Labial mini-screws in Class II division 2 patients achieved 69.8% expression for incisor labial tipping and 53.3% for distal movement (Yan et al.)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

**Author-acknowledged limitations / future directions:**
- Current CAT biomechanical understanding remains insufficient for reliable, predictable outcomes in all four complex movement categories, especially vertical tooth movement.
- Future direction 1: AI-assisted individualized CAT planning — using imaging-derived root/alveolar bone relationship data to predict tooth-movement difficulty by tooth position and movement type, enabling computer-assisted quantitative anchorage control and 3D overcorrection design, plus AI-assisted monitoring of clinical follow-up.
- Future direction 2: deeper integration of CAT with materials science and engineering — particularly 3D-printing technology allowing region-specific customization of aligner sheet thickness and mechanical properties to close the gap between virtual design and actual clinical tooth movement.

**Additional limitations identified by the ingesting reviewer:**
- No systematic search methodology, PRISMA diagram, or formal risk-of-bias appraisal reported — this is a narrative review despite "systematic review" language in the abstract; conclusions should be weighted accordingly (lower evidence tier than a true SR).
- Quantitative expression-rate figures are pooled narratively across studies with heterogeneous initial movement magnitudes, staging protocols (step size), attachment designs, and measurement methods (crown-level vs. CBCT/3D-model overall tooth movement) — direct cross-study numeric comparison should be made cautiously.
- Most cited primary evidence is retrospective, FEA/in-vitro, or small case series; few RCTs are cited.

## 7. Glossary

- **CAT (无托槽隐形矫治)**: Clear Aligner Therapy
- **实现率 / Expression rate (Predictability)**: Ratio of actual achieved tooth movement to software-planned (ClinCheck-type) tooth movement
- **矫治效率 / Efficiency**: The absolute amount of tooth movement actually achieved by CAT
- **西瓜籽效应 (Watermelon Seed Effect)**: The biomechanical phenomenon whereby CAT aligners apply force simultaneously across occlusal, buccal, and lingual tooth surfaces, producing an uneven, hard-to-predict combined force vector due to asymmetric crown morphology
- **钟摆效应 (Pendulum Effect)**: Lingual tipping and extrusion of anterior teeth as a reactive/unwanted movement during posterior tooth retraction or distalization
- **过山车效应 (Roller Coaster Effect)**: Mid-arch premature occlusal contact between anterior and posterior segments during extraction-space closure, producing an open bite in the middle of the arch
- **三角隙效应 / 楔形效应 (Triangular gap / wedge effect)**: Biomechanical effect by which aligner coverage design amplifies molar intrusion effectiveness (roughly 1:1.2 ratio described in the paper)
- **备抗 (Anchorage Preparation)**: Pre-programming a tooth (e.g., molar or canine) with intentional distal tip in the software design so that, as retraction forces act, the tooth trends toward bodily movement rather than excessive tipping
- **IMPA**: Incisor Mandibular Plane Angle
- **FMA**: Frankfort Mandibular Plane Angle
- **Power Ridge / Power Arm**: Attachment-adjacent aligner design features used for torque control (Power Ridge) or to control rotation during canine distal movement (Power Arm)
- **IPR (Interproximal Enamel Reduction)**: 邻面片切, interproximal stripping used to manage crowding/space
- **MSE (Maxillary Skeletal Expander)**: Bone-borne palatal expansion device referenced for comparison against CAT-based hybrid expansion designs
