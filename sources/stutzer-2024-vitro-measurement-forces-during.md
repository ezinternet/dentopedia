## Why Ingested

Ingested as part of the 2026-07-20 치위생사 스케일링 술기 (dental hygienist scaling technique) topic sweep. Extends [[wiki/periodontics/dvorska-2026-mechanical-instrumentation-hard-tissue-sr]] — that systematic review quantifies ultrasonic-scaler-associated hard-tissue substance loss and surface roughness in vitro, while this study supplies the upstream mechanistic driver: actual force magnitude, direction, and temporal dynamics applied by trained dental hygienists and dentists with a contemporary piezoelectric scaler. Also refines the force variable that [[wiki/periodontics/lodigkeit-2026-periodontal-instrumentation-enamel-cementum-review]] names only qualitatively as an operator-dependent risk factor, by quantifying it for a specific device.

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro biomechanical study: 10 dental hygienists and 6 dentists debrided soft (Model A, ~4.6 HV) and hard (Model B, ~24 HV) artificial dental calculus using a piezoelectric ultrasonic scaler (EMS PIEZON EN-060), with applied force recorded via a custom 3-axis force-sensor rig.

Mean total force was 0.34 N (hygienists) / 0.28 N (dentists) on soft calculus and 0.63 N / 0.57 N on hard calculus (hard vs soft: 1.85x, p=0.04 for hygienists; 2.04x, p=0.06 for dentists); force did not differ significantly between professions, was predominantly perpendicular to the surface (2.7–13.7x the parallel component), and exceeded the 0.5 N literature-recommended limit 14–56% of debridement time depending on calculus hardness.

Even trained, experienced professionals frequently exceed the recommended lateral-force ceiling, especially on hard calculus, suggesting force-feedback training aids could reduce iatrogenic root-surface damage risk; findings are limited by a small sample (one institution withdrew consent) and an in-vitro rig likely underestimating true in-vivo forces.

## 세줄요약

인비트로(In-Vitro) 생체역학 연구 — 치위생사 10명·치과의사 6명이 3축 힘센서(Three-axis Force Sensor) 실험대에서 압전형 초음파 스케일러(Piezoelectric Ultrasonic Scaler, EMS PIEZON EN-060)로 연질(Model A, 약 4.6 HV)·경질(Model B, 약 24 HV) 인공치석(Artificial Calculus)을 제거하며 가해진 힘을 기록.

평균 총력(Total Force)은 연질치석에서 치위생사 0.34 N·치과의사 0.28 N, 경질치석에서 0.63 N·0.57 N(경질/연질 비 1.85배 p=0.04[치위생사], 2.04배 p=0.06[치과의사]); 직종 간 유의차 없음, 힘은 표면 수직 방향이 압도적(평행 성분 대비 2.7~13.7배), 문헌 권장 상한 0.5 N을 치석 경도에 따라 전체 시간의 14~56% 초과.

숙련된 술자도 특히 경질 치석에서 권장 측방력(Lateral Force) 상한을 자주 초과 — 힘 피드백(Force Feedback) 훈련 보조기구가 의원성(Iatrogenic) 치근면 손상 위험을 줄일 수 있음을 시사; 표본이 작고(기관 1곳 참여 철회) in-vitro 실험대가 실제 임상보다 힘을 과소평가할 가능성이 한계.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study to quantify the magnitude, direction, AND temporal dynamics (rate of change) of force applied during debridement with a contemporary **piezoelectric** ultrasonic scaler — prior force data (Ruppert et al.) covered only magnetostrictive scalers.
- Shows applied force is predominantly perpendicular to the treated surface (2.7–13.7x larger than the parallel/lateral component), not lateral as the "0.5 N lateral force" recommendation implicitly frames it.
- Demonstrates calculus hardness, not professional role, is the dominant driver of applied force: force roughly doubles on hard vs soft artificial calculus, while dentists and dental hygienists apply statistically indistinguishable forces.
- Quantifies how often trained, experienced operators exceed the literature-recommended 0.5 N force threshold (Flemming et al.) — up to 56% of debridement time on hard calculus.
- Provides force-rate-of-change data (spikes up to ~62 N/s), relevant to future force-feedback device design and instrument-tip fatigue.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Apparatus**: custom-built measurement rig — base plate, three-axis force sensor (ZM3DW-AL 10N, Anhui Zhimin) with amplifier, NI USB-6366 data-acquisition module, handrest, video camera. Sensor validated pre/post each measurement set against a calibrated spring scale.
- **Calculus models**: Model A — thin (~0.1 mm) soft artificial calculus, mean hardness 4.6 HV (±0.8), gypsum-based, on a rectangular glass slab. Model B — thin hard artificial calculus, mean hardness 24 HV (±4.9), resin-based, on a circular glass plate.
- **Instrument**: piezoelectric ultrasonic scaler (PIEZON EN-060 LED handpiece + PIEZON PS tip, Electro Medical Systems, Nyon, Switzerland).
- **Participants**: 10 dental hygienists and 6 dentists, all self-reported experienced with this specific device; each debrided a defined area (10×10 mm on Model A; 3.5×6.5 mm on Model B) on both models, choosing their own vibration intensity/irrigation settings.
- **Data processing**: MATLAB R2021a — raw sensor signal filtered and offset-corrected; contact window isolated; force decomposed into a 3-axis coordinate system aligned to the calculus-model surface (Fx, Fy=perpendicular, Fz) and total force F=√(Fx²+Fy²+Fz²).
- **Statistics**: Wilcoxon rank-sum tests (profession comparison; Model A vs B comparison) via MATLAB Statistics and Machine Learning Toolbox v9.4. A priori power calculation: n=5/group gives power=0.93 to detect a 0.25 N difference by two-sample pooled t-test.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Dental hygienists (n=10) | Dentists (n=6) |
|---|---|---|
| Mean total force, soft calculus (Model A) | 0.34 N (SD 0.18; range 0.13–0.59) | 0.28 N (SD 0.33; range 0.06–0.95) |
| Mean total force, hard calculus (Model B) | 0.63 N (SD 0.40; range 0.28–1.64) | 0.57 N (SD 0.17; range 0.34–0.76) |
| Hard vs soft force ratio | 1.85x (p=0.04) | 2.04x (p=0.06) |
| Hygienist vs dentist force | soft p=0.26; hard p=1.00 (not significant) | — |
| Perpendicular : parallel force ratio | 12.6x (Model A) / 3.3x (Model B) | 13.7x (Model A) / 2.7x (Model B) |
| % of debridement time total force >0.5 N | Model A 23%; Model B 54% | Model A 14%; Model B 56% |
| Mean maximum rate of force change | 11.9 N/s (A) / 29.7 N/s (B) | 15.1 N/s (A) / 39.5 N/s (B) |

- Maximum single-instant force recorded: 1.83 N (Model A) and 2.22 N (Model B).
- Maximum rate of change recorded: up to 61.8 N/s (Model B).
- Work patterns varied qualitatively between operators — some used repeated single-direction strokes, others maintained continuous back-and-forth contact.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small sample (10 hygienists, 6 dentists); one participating institution withdrew consent shortly before measurements, further limiting recruitment and statistical power — authors state results "may not be generally valid."
- In-vitro bench setup (flat surface, unobstructed access) likely **underestimates** true in-vivo force — operators self-reported expecting somewhat higher forces intraorally; also lower than Ruppert et al.'s in-vivo magnetostrictive-scaler measurements (0.77 N hygienists / 1.00 N dentists).
- Artificial calculus models (gypsum/resin-based, Vickers-hardness-characterized) approximate but do not perfectly replicate natural calculus mechanical properties.
- Single piezoelectric device tested (EMS PIEZON EN-060) — generalization to other piezoelectric brands/tip designs or to magnetostrictive systems is uncertain.
- Future work proposed by authors: real-time force-feedback devices that warn operators when a force threshold is exceeded, to test whether feedback reduces applied force and improves clinical (tissue-preservation) outcomes; in-vivo replication.

## 6. Related Work

- Ruppert et al. (pilot study cited in Introduction/Discussion; not yet in this wiki) — measured force applied by 10 dentists and 10 dental hygienists debriding a bicuspid in vivo with a **magnetostrictive** scaler (Cavitron SPS): mean positive force 0.77 N (hygienists) / 1.00 N (dentists), higher than this study's in-vitro piezoelectric measurements. Authors attribute the difference to easier bench access and this cohort's greater device-specific training.
- Flemming et al. (cited recommendation; not yet in this wiki) — literature basis for the 0.5 N lateral-force limit used here as the safety threshold operators are evaluated against.
- [[wiki/periodontics/dvorska-2026-mechanical-instrumentation-hard-tissue-sr]] — in-vitro SR quantifying the hard-tissue substance-loss/roughness outcome side of the same force-driven instrumentation-damage mechanism this study measures on the input (force) side.
- [[wiki/periodontics/lodigkeit-2026-periodontal-instrumentation-enamel-cementum-review]] — narrative review naming force as one of several unquantified operator-dependent variables behind ultrasonic-scaling cementum loss; this study supplies that missing quantification for a contemporary device.

## 7. Glossary

- **Piezoelectric ultrasonic scaler** (압전형 초음파 스케일러) — periodontal instrument using piezoelectric crystals to vibrate a metal tip at ultrasonic frequency, producing predominantly linear tip motion; used here (EMS PIEZON EN-060) as distinct from magnetostrictive systems.
- **Magnetostrictive ultrasonic scaler** (자기변형형 초음파 스케일러) — alternative ultrasonic scaler technology using a metal stack driven by magnetostriction, producing more elliptical tip motion (e.g., Cavitron SPS, used by Ruppert et al.).
- **Debridement** (데브리망) — mechanical removal of calculus and biofilm from the tooth/root surface.
- **Total force (F)** (총력) — resultant magnitude of the 3-axis force vector, F=√(Fx²+Fy²+Fz²).
- **Lateral / parallel force** (측방력) — force component parallel to the treated surface; the dimension the 0.5 N literature recommendation targets.
- **Vickers hardness (HV)** (비커스 경도) — material-hardness scale used here to characterize artificial calculus stiffness (Model A ~4.6 HV soft vs Model B ~24 HV hard).
- **Rate of change of force (dF/dt)** (힘 변화율) — speed of force fluctuation during debridement; relevant to tip stress and potential patient discomfort.
