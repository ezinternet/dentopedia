## Why Ingested

치과 응급상황 문헌은 대개 개원가 설문([[drug/systemic-disease/smereka-2019-preparedness-and-attitudes-towards-medical]])이거나 실신 위주 리뷰([[drug/systemic-disease/li-2026-syncope-in-dentistry-risk-factors]])라서, "병원 구강응급실에 실제로 실려 온 중증 증례"의 질병 구성비는 비어 있었다. 이 후향적 20년 코호트는 중증 구강응급 92예의 진단 분포와 소생 처치·사망률을 직접 보고해 그 공백을 채운다 — 특히 혈관미주신경성 실신(VVS)이 중증 응급에서 차지하는 몫이 13예(14.1%)로, 개원가 발생빈도 1위라는 통념과 층위가 다르다는 점을 수치로 못박는다.

## Three-line Summary

Single-centre retrospective review (n=92, January 2006 – September 2025) of complete-record critical cases rescued in the Department of Oral Emergency, Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology, Beijing, China; mean age 47 years (4–88, median 49), 62 male (67.4%).

Maxillofacial haemorrhage (35/92, 38.0%) and cardiovascular accidents (22/92, 23.9%) together made up 61.9% (57/92) of rescues, followed by upper airway obstruction (17 cases) and vasovagal syncope (13 cases) together 32.6% (30/92); ECG monitoring 98.9%, oxygen 96.7% and venous access 78.3% were the commonest supportive measures, rescue succeeded in 91.3% (84/92) and 8 patients died.

The abstract reports no transfer-to-ward/hospitalisation rate and no procedure-level trigger distribution, and the single tertiary Chinese centre limits generalisability — but the case mix is the actionable signal: in a hospital oral-emergency setting the critical load is dominated by bleeding and cardiovascular events, not by vasovagal syncope.

## 세줄요약

중국 베이징 단일 3차 병원 구강응급실 후향적 연구 (n=92, 2006년 1월–2025년 9월): 평균 47세 (4–88, 중앙값 49), 남 62명 (67.4%).

악안면 출혈 35예 (38.0%)와 심혈관 사고 22예 (23.9%)가 전체의 61.9% (57/92)를 차지했고, 다음으로 상기도 폐색 17예와 혈관미주신경성 실신 (VVS) 13예가 합산 32.6% (30/92)였다; 심전도 모니터링 98.9%·산소 투여 96.7%·정맥로 확보 78.3%가 주요 지지처치였고 구조 성공률 91.3% (84/92), 사망 8예였다.

초록에는 병동 전원·입원율과 시술별 유발 분포가 미보고이며 단일 중국 3차기관이라 일반화에 한계가 있지만, 핵심 메시지는 병원 구강응급의 중증 부하가 실신이 아니라 출혈과 심혈관 사고 중심이라는 점이다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Reports the 20-year (2006–2025) disease composition of genuine *critical* oral-emergency cases at a hospital-based oral emergency department, a denominator that dental-office surveys cannot provide.
- Quantifies the dominance of maxillofacial haemorrhage (38.0%) and cardiovascular accidents (23.9%) — 61.9% combined — with upper airway obstruction (17) and vasovagal syncope (13) as the next tier.
- Documents the actual rescue toolkit used: ECG monitoring/oxygen/venous access near-universal, tracheal intubation as the primary airway measure, adrenaline the most-used drug, and a 91.3% rescue success rate with 8 deaths.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective clinical analysis of patients with complete medical records
- **Setting**: Department of Oral Emergency, Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology (single tertiary centre, Beijing, China)
- **Period**: January 2006 – September 2025
- **n**: 92 critical cases
- **Data collected**: primary diagnosis, age, sex, rescue measures and drugs, disease outcome
- **Outcomes analysed**: disease composition, key characteristics of clinical treatment, disease outcome (survival/death)
- **Not reported in abstract**: transfer/hospitalisation rate, procedure-level trigger distribution, denominator of all emergency visits, case definition for "critical"

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only**: full text not retrieved (Chinese-language journal, no PMC full text); subgroup definitions, case-selection criteria and the total emergency-visit denominator are unavailable.
- **Single-centre tertiary setting**: one hospital-based oral emergency department in Beijing — a referral centre for maxillofacial trauma, tumour bleeding and space infection; case mix cannot be extrapolated to community dental clinics, where vasovagal syncope dominates.
- **"Critical" is preselected**: 92 cases over ~20 years is a denominator of rescued critically ill patients only, so the reported proportions describe the composition *within* critical cases, not the incidence of any emergency.
- **No transfer/hospitalisation or procedure-trigger data** in the abstract, so the operational question "which procedure precedes a critical event" remains unanswered.
- **Heterogeneous 20-year span**: drug formularies, resuscitation protocols and imaging changed substantially between 2006 and 2025 with no era-stratified analysis reported.
- Abstract-internal inconsistency noted: the English text gives both mean 47 and median 49 years; the Chinese abstract reports median 49 (range 4–88) only. The Chinese text also prints venous access as "8.3% (72/92)", which contradicts its own numerator/denominator; the English 78.3% was used.

## 6. Related Work

- Smereka 2019 ([[drug/systemic-disease/smereka-2019-preparedness-and-attitudes-towards-medical]]): Polish dentist survey where VVS was the most frequent office emergency (46.30%) — the contrast with 13/92 here is a denominator difference (all office events vs rescued critical cases).
- Li 2026 ([[drug/systemic-disease/li-2026-syncope-in-dentistry-risk-factors]]): narrative review stating VVS exceeds 60% of dental-office emergency incidents, with local anaesthesia the dominant trigger (~75%) — supplies the mechanism/trigger layer that this abstract lacks.
- Jevon 2020 ([[drug/systemic-disease/jevon-2020-management-anaphylaxis-dental-practice-update]]): anaphylaxis protocol; this cohort lists anaphylactic shock among the indications for adrenaline but gives no case count.

## 7. Glossary

- **혈관미주신경성 실신 (Vasovagal Syncope, VVS)**: reflex-mediated transient loss of consciousness with bradycardia/vasodepression; the commonest dental-office emergency.
- **악안면 출혈 (Maxillofacial Haemorrhage)**: bleeding in the maxillofacial region; here mostly trauma- or tumour-related and frequently progressing to shock.
- **상기도 폐색 (Upper Airway Obstruction)**: obstruction at or above the larynx; in this cohort driven by tumour mass and space infection.
- **출혈성 쇼크 (Haemorrhagic Shock)**: circulatory failure from acute blood loss; 17/35 of the haemorrhage cases.
- **심폐소생술 (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation, CPR)**: chest compression/ventilation protocol; applied to all 15 cardiac-arrest patients.
- **윤상갑상연골절개술 (Cricothyrotomy)**: surgical airway through the cricothyroid membrane, used as a rescue step after failed intubation.
