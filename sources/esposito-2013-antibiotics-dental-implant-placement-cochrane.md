## Why Ingested

This Cochrane SR (pub4, last substantively updated June 2013, reaffirmed "stable" in 2019) is the foundational trial-level evidence behind the now-standard "single preoperative 2g amoxicillin" implant-prophylaxis recommendation, yet it was missing from the wiki even though two newer analyses that build on and dispute it — [[drug/antibiotics/torof-2023-antibiotic-dental-implant-procedures-sr-ma]] (reinforces the same conclusion) and [[drug/antibiotics/momand-2024-antibiotic-prophylaxis-early-implant-failure]] (a stricter placebo-controlled/double-blind-only re-analysis that found no significant benefit) — were already ingested. Adding the original Cochrane synthesis closes that gap and lets the wiki show, evidence-trail style, exactly how the RCT-inclusion criteria shift the conclusion from RR 0.33 (significant) to RR 0.66 (non-significant).

## Three-line Summary

Cochrane systematic review + meta-analysis (search to 17 June 2013) of 6 RCTs, 1162 participants, comparing prophylactic antibiotics vs placebo/no antibiotics at dental implant placement.

Antibiotics (all amoxicillin regimens; mostly a single 2g or 3g preoperative oral dose) significantly reduced implant failures (RR 0.33, 95% CI 0.16–0.67, P=0.002, I²=0%; NNTB=25); prosthesis failure was borderline significant (RR 0.44, 95% CI 0.19–1.00); postoperative infections and adverse events showed no significant difference.

Evidence graded moderate quality; no trials compared different antibiotics or dosages, and the one trial evaluating different prophylaxis durations was high risk of bias with zero events in any arm — postoperative antibiotic benefit and optimal regimen remain unknown.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First (and, as of this ingest, still the) Cochrane-branded meta-analysis directly quantifying the benefit of prophylactic systemic antibiotics at dental implant placement, providing the trial-level data (6 RCTs) that virtually all subsequent narrative reviews and guideline statements on implant antibiotic prophylaxis cite.
- Produces a GRADE summary-of-findings table translating the pooled RR into an NNT (25) framed around a 6% baseline implant-failure rate in the no-antibiotic arm — a clinically interpretable number-needed-to-treat that has propagated widely into implant dentistry teaching.
- Explicitly flags the unanswered questions that later reviews ([[drug/antibiotics/torof-2023-antibiotic-dental-implant-procedures-sr-ma]], [[drug/antibiotics/momand-2024-antibiotic-prophylaxis-early-implant-failure]]) subsequently tried to resolve: postoperative-antibiotic benefit, and comparative antibiotic/dose effectiveness.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cochrane systematic review with random-effects meta-analysis (Mantel-Haenszel, dichotomous outcomes, risk ratios with 95% CI); adverse events pooled with a fixed-effect model.
- **Search**: Cochrane Oral Health's Trials Register (to 17 June 2013), CENTRAL (Cochrane Library 2013, Issue 5), MEDLINE via OVID (1946–17 June 2013), EMBASE via OVID (1980–17 June 2013); no language or date restrictions.
- **Eligibility**: RCTs with ≥3 months follow-up comparing prophylactic antibiotic regimens vs no antibiotics in adults (>18y) undergoing dental implant placement. Exclusions included cardiac-endocarditis risk, artificial joints, immune compromise, diabetes, head/neck radiotherapy, penicillin allergy, active infection at the implant site, recent/concurrent antibiotic use, IV bisphosphonate therapy, pregnancy/breastfeeding, long-term NSAID use, and bleeding disorders — i.e., the pooled effect applies to otherwise-healthy, uncomplicated implant patients.
- **Outcomes**: implant failure, prosthesis failure, postoperative infection, adverse events (GI upset, hypersensitivity, etc.).
- **Risk of bias / duplicate process**: screening, risk-of-bias assessment, and data extraction performed in duplicate and independently by two review authors.
- **Included studies (6 RCTs, 1162 participants)**: three trials of 2g preoperative amoxicillin vs placebo (927 participants: Esposito 2008a, Esposito 2010a, and one other); one trial of 3g preoperative amoxicillin vs placebo (55 participants, Caiazzo 2011); one trial of 1g preoperative + 500mg qid ×2 days vs no antibiotics (80 participants, Abu-Ta'a 2008); one 4-arm trial (100 participants, Nolan 2013) comparing 2g preoperative only, 2g preoperative + 1g bid ×7 days, 1g postoperative bid ×7 days, and no antibiotics.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Studies (n participants) | RR (95% CI) | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implant failures | 6 (1162) | 0.33 (0.16–0.67) | P=0.002, significant; NNTB=25 (95% CI 14–100) at 6% baseline failure |
| Prosthesis failures | 5 (1107) | 0.44 (0.19–1.00) | P=0.05, borderline |
| Postoperative infections | 6 (1162) | 0.69 (0.36–1.35) | NS |
| Adverse events | 6 (1162) | 1.00 (0.06–15.85) | NS (only 2 minor AEs total, 1 in placebo arm) |

- Heterogeneity across all pooled comparisons was low (I²=0%).
- The 4-arm duration-comparison trial (Nolan 2013, 100 participants) was judged high risk of bias and produced no conclusive information — zero implant/prosthesis failures, infections, or adverse events occurred in any of the four 25-participant arms.
- No trials compared different antibiotic agents or different antibiotic dosages head-to-head.
- Overall body of evidence (antibiotics vs placebo/no antibiotics, 6 trials): moderate quality (GRADE ⊕⊕⊕⊝).
- Authors' bottom line: a single oral dose of 2g or 3g amoxicillin, given one hour preoperatively, significantly reduces dental implant failure in ordinary (non-medically-compromised) conditions; postoperative antibiotic benefit and comparative antibiotic choice remain unknown.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small evidence base: only 6 trials, all using amoxicillin exclusively — no data on alternative antibiotics (e.g., for penicillin-allergic patients) or dose-ranging.
- The only duration-comparison trial was high risk of bias and underpowered (zero events per 25-participant arm), so postoperative antibiotic questions remain unanswered by this review.
- Esposito, the lead author, is also first author of two of the six included trials (declared as a conflict of interest, though not involved in quality-assessing those trials).
- Cochrane subsequently classified the review as "not a priority for updating" — the 2019 reaffirmation carries no new trials; readers should cross-check newer, methodologically stricter syntheses (see Related Papers on the wiki page) for whether the effect holds under tighter RCT-inclusion criteria.

## 7. Glossary

- **RR (Risk Ratio)**: ratio of event probability in the intervention (antibiotic) group vs the comparison (no-antibiotic/placebo) group; RR<1 favors antibiotics.
- **NNTB (Number Needed to Treat for Benefit)**: number of patients who need to receive the intervention for one additional patient to benefit (here, avoid an implant failure).
- **GRADE**: system for rating certainty/quality of a body of evidence (high/moderate/low/very low) used in Cochrane summary-of-findings tables.
- **CENTRAL**: Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, a core Cochrane search source.
