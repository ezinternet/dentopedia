## Why Ingested
칼슘 인산염 접착 시멘트로 과삭제 골창에서 임플란트 1차 안정성을 달성하는 혁신적 접근 — [[overviews/implant-primary-stability-arp-macrogeometry-low-density-overview]]에서 다루는 저밀도/불량 골 상황에서의 1차 안정성 확보 전략 확장.

## Three-line Summary
In vitro proof-of-concept: calcium phosphate adhesive cement restores primary implant stability in oversized osteotomies (cement gap 0.5–3 mm), achieving removal torques >32 Ncm in all configurations — the immediate loading threshold.
Stability scales with implant surface area and diameter (66.5–170.1 mm²), not cement gap size; trisodium citrate extends working time without reducing final strength; failure mode is adhesive at the cement-implant interface.
Pre-clinical only (sawbone, no in vivo data), but establishes a route to primary stability in poor-fit beds without press-fit — directly relevant to oversized or post-augmentation osteotomies.

## 세줄요약
In vitro: 칼슘 인산염 접착 시멘트가 과삭제 골창(0.5–3 mm 간격) 전 구성에서 제거 토크 >32 Ncm(즉시 부하 기준치) 달성.
안정성은 임플란트 표면적·직경(66.5–170.1 mm²)에 비례하며 시멘트 간격과 무관; 구연산삼나트륨이 작업 시간 연장(p≤0.01), 최종 강도 불변; 파절은 시멘트-임플란트 계면(접착 파절).
전임상 단계이나, 압박 끼워맞춤 없이 저밀도 또는 이식 후 골창에서 1차 안정성을 확보하는 대안 경로 제시.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First in vitro characterization of calcium phosphate-based adhesive cement as alternative to IT-based primary stability
- Shows removal torque (surrogate for primary stability) scales with implant surface area and diameter — not cement gap size
- All measured removal torques exceeded 32 Ncm immediate loading threshold
- Identifies failure mode: cement-implant interface (adhesive failure), not cement-bone interface
- Demonstrates trisodium citrate extends working time without sacrificing final strength

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: In vitro, sawbone blocks (bone surrogate)
- Adhesive: calcium phosphate cement based on phosphoserine adhesion (glue-like viscous liquid)
- Implants: Straumann TL 4.1mm (4mm, 10mm), TLX 3.75mm (6mm, 12mm), TLX 4.5mm (10mm)
- Variables: cement gap size (0.5, 1, 1.5, 3 mm), curing time (2–15 min), implant geometry
- Outcome: removal torque (Ncm) as surrogate for primary stability
- Statistics: Wilcoxon rank-sum test (non-parametric; groups did not all adhere to normal distribution)
- Also tested trisodium citrate variant to extend handling time

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- All removal torques above 32 Ncm (immediate loading threshold) at 15 min curing
- Primary factors: implant surface area and diameter (endosteal area: TL 4.1×4mm=66.5 mm² → TLX 3.75×12mm=170.1 mm²)
- Cement gap size (0.5–3 mm): no significant difference in median removal torque; larger gaps (≥1mm) showed wider data spread
- Curing time: full strength at 15 min; 2-min still developing strength
- Trisodium citrate: extends working time significantly (p≤0.01 at 2, 5 min); full strength matches at 15 min
- Failure mode: adhesive at cement-implant interface (dusty residue on implant surface)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Sawbone does not replicate in vivo bone biology or healing
- Removal torque is a bench surrogate — not validated as predictor of clinical primary stability for adhesive systems
- Sample size not specified for normal distribution testing → non-parametric stats required
- No comparison with standard insertion torque in same preparation
- No in vivo or clinical data — pre-clinical proof-of-concept only

## 6. Related Work
- Insertion torque as primary stability surrogate: standard approach (Meredith et al., Javed & Romanos)
- RFA (ISQ) as primary stability measure (Osstell): does not measure mechanical interlocking directly
- Calcium phosphate cements in orthopedics/dentistry: phosphoserine-based adhesion under wet conditions

## 7. Glossary
- **Removal torque**: torque required to unscrew an implant — used as surrogate for primary stability when insertion torque not measurable
- **Calcium phosphate adhesive cement**: viscous liquid cement with glue-like properties; bonds to titanium and wet bone surfaces via phosphoserine
- **Oversized osteotomy**: preparation larger than implant diameter — removes press-fit mechanism
- **Trisodium citrate**: added to extend cement working/handling time
