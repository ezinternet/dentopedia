## Why Ingested

[[drug/systemic-disease/smereka-2019-preparedness-and-attitudes-towards-medical]]의 2019 폴란드 설문과 **동일한 도구 계보**(요르단 Abu Al Ghanam 도구를 번역·역번역)를 쓴 2025 레바논 전국 표본으로, VVS가 응급상황 1위라는 순위가 국가·표본을 바꿔도 유지되는지를 독립적으로 검증한다. 이 페이지가 필요한 이유는 "치과 응급 대비" 일반론이 아니라 [[overviews/dental-vasovagal-syncope-overview]]의 역학 표에 넣을 **VVS 특정 분모**(12개월 내 응급 경험자 161명 중 91명)와 VVS를 포함한 경증 응급군의 **자신감 수치**를 제공하기 때문이다.

## Three-line Summary

A nationally distributed online cross-sectional survey of 442 Lebanese dentists (both dental associations, Aug–Oct 2024) measured emergency experience, clinic equipment/drugs, training, and self-rated capability; the instrument was adapted from the Jordanian survey used in comparable regional studies.

Vasovagal reactions ranked first among emergencies in the preceding 12 months — 91 of the 161 dentists (56.5%) who had encountered any emergency — ahead of hypoglycemia (55.9%) and orthostatic hypotension (54.0%), while major emergencies were rarer (22.4% ever) and cardiac arrest rare (1.8% ever). Preparedness was thin (emergency kit 38.2%, defibrillator 5.9%, staff trained 18.8%) and no VVS-specific confidence figure was reported — capability was measured only for the bundled "minor medical conditions" category, at 69.0% capable.

Its value to the vault is denominator discipline plus a documented training cliff: the 56.5% is a share of dentists with a recent emergency (20.6% of all 442), and 71.3% had undergraduate emergency training but only 39.6% postgraduate and 7.9% within the past year, with no Lebanese regulation mandating BLS for dentists or staff.

## 세줄요약

레바논 치과의사 442명(양 치과의사협회 명부, 2024년 8–10월 온라인 설문)을 대상으로 한 전국 횡단면 조사 — 도구는 요르단 선행 연구 것을 번역·역번역해 사용.

최근 12개월 내 응급상황을 겪은 161명 중 **미주신경성 실신 (Vasovagal Syncope, VVS)이 91명(56.5%)으로 1위**, 저혈당증 55.9%, 기립성 저혈압 54.0% 순이며 중증 응급은 22.4%(경력 전체), 심정지는 1.8%에 그침. 대비 수준은 낮고(응급키트 38.2%, 제세동기 5.9%, 스태프 교육 18.8%) **VVS 단독 자신감 수치는 보고되지 않음** — 역량은 "경증 응급군" 묶음으로만 측정되어 69.0%가 대응 가능.

이 논문의 위키 내 가치는 분모 규율(56.5%는 최근 응급을 겪은 치과의사의 비율이고 전체 442명 기준으로는 20.6%)과 졸업 후 재교육 단절(학부 71.3% vs 졸업 후 39.6%, 최근 1년 내 7.9%)의 문서화이며, 레바논에는 치과의사·스태프 BLS를 의무화한 규정이 없다.

- **Design**: Cross-sectional national online survey (self-administered questionnaire, English + French)

## 2. Key Contributions

- First national assessment of medical-emergency preparedness among Lebanese dentists; Lebanon has **no regulation mandating first-aid/BLS training** for dentists or staff, while all three dental schools embed BLS in the curriculum (7 h at Lebanese University via Lebanese Red Cross; 14 h at Saint-Joseph University; 14 h at Beirut Arab University) — i.e. a training cliff at graduation.
- Quantifies the frequency hierarchy in a fresh national sample: **vasovagal reactions first (56.5% of those with any emergency in the past 12 months)**, hypoglycemia second (55.9%), orthostatic hypotension third (54.0%).
- Separates three preparedness axes — equipment/drugs, dentist training, and **clinic staff** training + emergency response plan (EMRP) — and shows the staff axis is the weakest (18.8% staff trained, 16.1% have an EMRP).
- Logistic models link prior defibrillator use and postgraduate training to higher capability across minor, major and cardiac-arrest outcomes.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Sampling frame**: all dentists registered with the two Lebanese Dental Associations (LDA) of Beirut and Tripoli; questionnaire link distributed **via WhatsApp** from the LDA phone databases, with four reminders at two-week intervals.
- **Sample size calculation**: Epi Info StatCalc, population 7,000 (per LDA), expected frequency 61.1%, 95% CL, α = 5% → required n = 347.
- **Achieved n = 442** completed questionnaires. **No response rate is reported** anywhere in the text (denominator 442/~7,000 cannot be converted to a true response rate because reach/delivery is unknown).
- **Response-bias check**: gender/age distribution compared against LDA population figures — significant difference, with over-representation of females and under-representation of dentists aged >60 (attributed to online Google Forms collection).
- **Instrument**: adapted from the tool used in the Jordanian study (Abu Al Ghanam et al.) — participant characteristics; professional characteristics; emergency experience (minor: VVS, hypoglycemia, orthostatic hypotension; major: asthma, seizures, allergy, foreign-body inhalation, ACS; cardiac arrest; oxygen/defibrillator/BP-monitor/emergency-drug use; any emergency in past 12 months + type); equipment and drug availability; training; plus added items on staff training, EMRP presence, and EMRP simulation frequency. Translated EN→FR, back-translated; pilot-tested with 5 dentists.
- **Analysis**: SPSS v25; chi-square/Fisher bivariate screens, then dimension-wise logistic regressions (preparedness / experience / training) with a minimum of 20 individuals per included variable, significant variables entered into final models. Capability Likert responses dichotomized: "fully capable" + "capable enough" = capable.

**Denominator note (important for synthesis use)**: the 56.5% VVS figure is the proportion **among dentists who had at least one emergency in the past 12 months**, not the proportion of all dentists and not the proportion of emergency episodes. The comparable Polish figure (smereka-2019, 46.30%) is reported as the share of **all surveyed dentists** experiencing ≥1 case in 12 months — the two percentages are therefore not on the same denominator and must not be placed in one column without a label.

**VVS-specific confidence/preparedness is NOT reported.** Capability was surveyed only for bundled categories and specific procedures (Figure 3, values not in the text): 69.0% (n = 305) capable for "minor medical conditions (VVS, orthostatic hypotension, hypoglycemia)". The Discussion adds a stricter reading — "nearly 25.0%" at *full* capability for those same minor conditions — but no separate VVS item appears anywhere in the text or tables. Any VVS-specific confidence claim attributed to this paper would be an invention.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No response rate reported**, and the 442/~7,000 relationship is not a response rate (WhatsApp broadcast reach is unknown). Representatives cannot be assessed from the text alone.
- **Response bias confirmed by the authors**: female over-representation, >60 y under-representation versus LDA population figures.
- **Self-report only** — equipment, drugs, training and capability are all self-assessed; social desirability bias plausibly inflates preparedness. Knowledge was explicitly **not** tested.
- **Internal numeric inconsistency in the published text**: the Results prose gives "74.2% of participating dentists (n = 132)" for minor problems while Table 2 gives 328 (74.2%). 74.2% of 442 = 328, so the tabulated value is used here; the prose `n = 132` is treated as a typographical error in the source.
- **Capability was not itemized for VVS** (bundled with hypoglycemia and orthostatic hypotension), and Discussion-level "~25% full capability / ~5% major / ~1% cardiac arrest" figures are rounded and not traceable to the truncated Figure 3 in the retrieved text. Treat those three as approximate.
- Cross-sectional design; no knowledge or skills testing; no patient-level outcomes.
- Briefing/paper DOI mismatch (briefing `…2024.07.010` vs file `…2024.06.010`) — file value used.

## 6. Related Work

- Abu Al Ghanam et al. (Jordan) — instrument source; 87.8% of Jordanian dentists wanted further training, 27.2% had undergraduate emergency training.
- Smereka et al. 2019 (Poland) — VVS 46.30% of dentists, the closest European counterpart.
- Varoni et al. (Italy) — average satisfaction with own emergency capability.
- Studies from Saudi Arabia (one-third lacked confidence with emergency drugs; 45.0% proficient at CPR) and Riyadh (77.0% of staff BLS-certified under a mandate) — used as the policy-contrast group.
- AHA/ERC — annual BLS recommendation that Lebanon's non-mandatory regime fails to meet.

## 7. Glossary

- **VVS (Vasovagal Syncope, 미주신경성 실신)**: neurally mediated reflex faint; here the most frequently encountered dental-office emergency.
- **EMRP (Emergency Medical Response Plan, 응급대응계획)**: written clinic-level plan assigning roles during an emergency; present in 16.1% of clinics here.
- **Minor vs major emergency (this paper's operational split)**: minor = VVS, hypoglycemia, orthostatic hypotension; major = asthma attack, seizures, allergy, foreign-body inhalation, acute coronary syndrome. Cardiac arrest is counted separately.
- **LDA (Lebanese Dental Association, 레바논치과의사협회)**: the two registration bodies (Beirut, Tripoli) that supplied the sampling frame.
- **BLS (Basic Life Support, 기본생명구조술)**: the level of training the three Lebanese dental schools provide and the level AHA/ERC recommend refreshing annually.
