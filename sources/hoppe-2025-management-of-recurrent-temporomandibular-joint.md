## Why Ingested

위키 주제인 **급성 악관절 탈구의 복원 (reduction of acute TMJ dislocation)** 에서 복원은 1단계, 재발 방지가 2단계인데, [[tmj/abrahamsson-2020-treatment-of-temporomandibular-joint-luxation]]는 재발성 주사 요법에 국한된 RCT 5편만 다루고 소아 재발 관리 전체(보존·미세침습·수술)를 다루지 않는다. 이 논문은 **소아(<18세) 재발성 악관절 탈구 (recurrent TMJ dislocation) 치료 9편(사례보고 5·케이스시리즈 3·케이스-컨트롤 1)을 PRISMA로 정직하게 정량화**해 "소아에서는 어떤 방법을 실제로 보고했으며 근거가 어디까지인가"라는 공백을 메운다. 특히 결론이 "소아라는 이유만으로 수술을 금기라고 할 근거는 없다(no evidence)"로 끝난다는 점은 근거 등급(hierarchy) 자체를 다시 잡아주는 내용이다. 또한 이 논문이 재발성에서 본 기준선 — 결절절제술 (eminectomy)·도트리 수술 (Dautrey's procedure) 은 포함 9편 중 2편에서만 보고된 마지막 resort — 이 [[tmj/prechel-2018-the-treatment-of-temporomandibular-joint]]의 성인 재발 22% 기준과 나란히 읽혀야 한다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251139493), searching BASE, PubMed and Scopus on 21 September 2025 with PICOS eligibility: 268 records identified (102 BASE / 96 PubMed / 70 Scopus) → 168 after deduplication → 34 full texts → 24 excluded and 1 unobtainable → **9 studies included (5 case reports, 3 case series, 1 case-control; 10 paediatric patients tabulated, 2 excluded from outcome measures)**. Oxford CEBM levels: only 4 publications above level 5 (one level 3, three level 4).

Conservative and minimally invasive methods dominate the paediatric literature — botulinum toxin type A (BoNT-A) injection into the lateral pterygoid muscle is the most frequently reported minimally invasive approach and autologous blood injection nearly as frequent — while **invasive treatment appeared in only 2 of the 9 studies** (French 2017 eminectomy, Gadre 2010 Dautrey's procedure), both used as last resort and both recurrence-free at their follow-ups (6 and 36/28 months). Dislocation frequency before treatment reached >40 episodes in 2 weeks (Stark) and >20 in 2 months (Sicard); after treatment the reported series were 0 episodes at 33–36 months (Coser, Yoshida, Gadre), 2 episodes in 6 months (Sicard), and recurrence requiring repeat injections at 4 and a further 4 months (Stark).

Clinical implication: conservative and minimally invasive methods are the preferred first-line in children given low risk and better psychological tolerance, interdisciplinary input (psychology, neurology) recurs in the successfully resolved cases — but heterogeneity across design, intervention and outcome measures makes effectiveness comparisons impossible, only 1 of the 5 pre-specified outcome measures (dislocation frequency) was reported consistently, and no long-term evidence supports choosing surgery, which is why the authors explicitly refuse to call age alone a contraindication to operative treatment.

소아 재발성 악관절 탈구 (recurrent TMJ dislocation) 에서는 보존적·미세침습법이 압도적으로 많이 보고되었고, 그중 **미세침습법의 최다 보고는 외항교근 (lateral pterygoid muscle) 에 대한 보툴리눔 독소 A형 (botulinum toxin type A, BoNT-A) 주사**, 자가혈액 주사 (autologous blood injection) 가 거의 같은 빈도로 뒤따랐다. 수술은 9편 중 2편(French 2017 결절절제술 (eminectomy), Gadre 2010 도트리 수술 (Dautrey's procedure) )에서만, 두 편 모두 마지막 수단으로 선택되어 각각 6개월·36/28개월 추적에서 재발이 없었다. 치료 전 탈구 빈도는 2주간 40회 초과(Stark), 2개월간 20회 초과(Sicard)였고, 치료 후에는 33~36개월 재발 0회(Coser·Yoshida·Gadre), 6개월간 2회(Sicard), 주사 반복이 필요한 재발(4개월 후 재발, 재주사 후 다시 4개월 후 재발; Stark)이 보고되었다.

임상적 함의: 위험이 낮고 심리적 수용성이 좋은 보존·미세침습법이 소아의 1차 선택이며, 성공적으로 해소된 사례들에서 **학제 간(multidisciplinary) 접근 — 심리·신경과 협업 — 이 반복적으로 등장**한다. 그러나 연구설계·중재·结局지표의 이질성 때문에 방법 간 우열 비교가 불가능하고, 사전 지정한 5개 지표 중 **탈구 빈도 하나만 일관되게 보고**되었으며 수술 선택을 뒷받침할 장기 근거가 없다 — 그래서 저자들은 "소아라는 이유만으로 수술을 금기라고 할 근거는 없다"고 명시한다. 또한 이 논문은 급성 복원 자체가 아니라 그 뒤 단계인 **재발 방지**를 다루며, 소아 급성 복원 시 아산화질소·프로포폴 진정 하에서 자연 복원(spontaneous reduction)이 보고된 사례(Proppofol 진정 중自发적 복원, Sicard)가 있어 [[tmj/lin-2026-rapid-reduction-of-temporomandibular-joint]]의 환자 주도 급성 복원 맥락과 연결된다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Quantifies the paediatric evidence vacuum**: 25 years of literature (2000–2025) yielded just 9 usable publications on recurrent TMJ dislocation in under-18s — the authors call this "an unsatisfactory level of scientific attention," and it is the first PRISMA-registered count of the topic.
- **Deliberately refuses to pool**: because all 9 studies are case-level designs with heterogeneous outcomes, the review tabulates rather than meta-analyses, and its headline conclusion is that "consistent conclusions regarding the effectiveness of different treatment methods … could not be drawn."
- **BoNT-A into the lateral pterygoid is named as the most-reported minimally invasive route** in children, with autologous blood injection nearly as frequent — the mechanism stated (reduced excessive lateral pterygoid activity) is the same muscle targeted in [[botulinum-toxin/bae-2025-ultrasonographic-assessment-lateral-pterygoid-muscle]].
- **Bounds the surgical question explicitly**: only 2 of 9 studies report invasive treatment (eminectomy, Dautrey's), both as last resort with recurrence-free follow-ups, and the authors conclude the current literature does **not** justify calling age alone a contraindication to surgery in children — a direct check on the "children are too young to operate" intuition.
- **Names the sedation/anaesthesia layer for paediatric reduction**: nitrous oxide before reduction or BoNT injection (Mohan, Sicard, Stark), light IV sedation (French), propofol sedation (Sicard), local anaesthetic before arthrocentesis or reduction (Coser, Stark) — and reports a spontaneous reduction under propofol sedation, i.e. sedation alone sometimes sufficed.
- **Sets an outcome-measure standard for future work**: all five measures (dislocation frequency, acoustic symptoms, mandibular opening path, pain intensity, range of jaw abduction) pre- and post-treatment should be reported; the planned mandibular-opening-path measure was in the end examined by **none** of the 9 studies and had to be dropped.
- **Flags the risk-of-bias instrument behaviour**: κ ≈ 0.10 despite ~78% reviewer response overlap — an artefact of both reviewers over-selecting "Yes," with PABAK 0.56 and AC1 Gweta 0.71 recovering moderate-to-high concordance.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: systematic review, PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251139493; **no meta-analysis** (narrative synthesis + tabulation only).
- **Databases**: BASE, PubMed, Scopus — searched 21 September 2025. BASE 102 records, PubMed 96, Scopus 70 → **268 identified** → 168 after duplicates → 34 full-text screened → 1 full text unobtainable (Lee & Chou 2009) → 24 excluded (Table A2) → **9 included**.
- **Eligibility (PICOS)**: patients <18 with recurrent TMJ dislocation (studies mixing children and adults allowed); any treatment; any or no comparator; ≥1 of five required measures (dislocation frequency, acoustic symptoms, mandibular opening path, pain intensity, range of abduction, each pre/post); published 2000–2025 in scientific journals. Excluded: cadaver/animal studies, unclear diagnosis or undefined treatment, dislocation treated as part of broader therapy, preprints, conference abstracts, pre-2000 papers.
- **Designs included**: 5 case reports, 3 case series, 1 case–control study — levels of evidence graded on the Oxford CEBM 2011 scale (one level 3, three level 4, five level 5).
- **Screening/extraction**: Rayyan, two reviewers (K.C., A.H.) independently screened titles/abstracts and full texts, all disagreements carried to full text, third author adjudicated; data extracted into a Google Sheets form.
- **Risk of bias**: Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal checklists matched to design (case–control, case series, case report); two reviewers (A.H., M.C.); agreement quantified as % agreement, Cohen's κ, PABAK, AC1 Gweta.
- **Comparators**: none — every included study is uncontrolled, so no head-to-head comparison exists in the paediatric literature.

### Risk of bias

- **Low**: Triantafillidou 2012 (case–control, all JBI criteria met), Stark 2015 (case report, all criteria met).
- **Moderate**: case series Coser 2015, Yoshida 2018, Gadre 2010 — mostly criteria met but consistently lacking appropriate statistical analysis, plus unclear inclusion reporting and, in some cases, measurement reliability.
- **Moderate**: case reports Mohan 2022, Sicard 2018, French 2017, Martínez-Pérez 2004 — incomplete reporting in key areas.
- **Inter-reviewer agreement**: ~78% response overlap; Cohen's κ ≈ 0.10 (inflated chance agreement from both reviewers selecting "Yes"); PABAK = 0.56; AC1 Gweta = 0.71.

### Contextual figures cited in the Discussion

- TMDs affect up to **31% of adults and 11% of children** (Valesan 2021); projected by 2050 to reach **44% globally / 37% in Europe** (Zieliński 2025).
- The majority of TMJ dislocations are themselves recurrent: **62.5%** (Papoutsis 2018) and **61.9%** (Tarhio 2023) — the latter matching [[tmj/tarhio-2023-causes-and-treatment-of-temporomandibular]].
- Paediatric joint hypermobility affects **8–39% of school-aged children**, benign form peaking at ages **3–10** and declining with age; hypermobility predisposes to later TMD.
- Predisposing factors listed: Ehlers-Danlos and other connective-tissue disorders, neuromuscular disease (muscular dystrophy, myotonia), seizure or involuntary-movement disorders, chronic corticosteroid therapy, and — in children specifically — trauma.
- Autologous blood injection follow-up shows **no radiographic or functional evidence of ankylosis**.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Stated limitations: very few eligible studies and very few participants; predominantly case reports → low overall level of evidence; heterogeneity in design, intervention and outcome measures making direct comparison impossible; reporting bias cannot be excluded.
- Only **4 of 9 publications rated above Oxford level 5**, and **no study evaluated a surgical technique against a comparator**.
- Only 1 of the 5 pre-specified outcome measures (dislocation frequency) was reported consistently; mandibular opening path was measured by no included study at all.
- The authors' own unresolved position: no consistent conclusion on relative effectiveness, and **age alone cannot be treated as a contraindication to surgery** in children on current evidence.
- Recommended: any design of further study is warranted, with all five measures (dislocation frequency, acoustic symptoms, mandibular opening path, pain intensity, range of jaw abduction) reported before and after treatment; randomised designs are hampered by rarity, and the authors note surgery in children carries growth, joint-physiology and ankylosis concerns, so early mobilisation and physiotherapy are advocated over immobilisation.

## 6. Related Work

- Abrahamsson 2020 (Clin Oral Investig, RCT-only SR, 8 trials / 338 patients) — closest sibling: recurrence-prevention injections, but adults and mixed ages, RCT design, no paediatric sub-analysis. This paper is the paediatric counterpart and cites it.
- Tarhio 2023 (Clin Oral Investig, retrospective n=260) — real-world recurrence 61.9%, cited here as the recurrence-rate benchmark.
- Prechel 2018 (Dtsch Arztebl Int, clinical practice guideline) — adult/child CPG; recurrence 22% after the first episode; eminectomy framed within a guideline algorithm.
- Hoppe 2025 paper's unique contribution vs all three above: paediatric-only eligibility, a BoNT-A-first preference, and an explicit refusal to treat age as a surgical contraindication.
- Saini 2024 (PLoS ONE, SR+MA of botulinum toxin in TMD) — corroborates BoNT as the best-supported minimally invasive TMJ intervention in general, though in adult/mixed TMD rather than paediatric recurrent dislocation.
- Bae 2025 (US assessment of the lateral pterygoid) — the imaging basis for the muscle that this review identifies as the BoNT-A target.
- Chana 2026 (US for TMJ in juvenile idiopathic arthritis, SR) — paediatric TMJ imaging context; same under-18 imaging/measurement problem.
- Papoutsis 2018 (OAEM, Swiss urban ED retrospective) — recurrent fraction 62.5%, source of the majority-recurrent claim.

## 7. Glossary

- **Recurrent TMJ dislocation**: repeated anterolateral displacement of the mandibular condyle beyond the articular eminence, trapped in front of it and not self-reducible; distinguished from acute (single episode) and chronic (untreated/prolonged) luxation, and from bilateral vs unilateral.
- **Eminectomy / 결절절제술 (eminectomy)**: surgical removal of the articular eminence to block the condyle's re-dislocation path; one of only two invasive methods appearing in the included literature.
- **Dautrey's procedure**: surgical elevation of the height of the articular eminence (eminence osteotomy) to prevent re-dislocation; the other invasive method in the included literature.
- **Intermaxillary fixation (IMF) / 악간 고정**: wiring the teeth together to immobilise the jaw and limit dislocation; not usable in children without erupted teeth or in edentulous patients.
- **Botulinum toxin type A (BoNT-A) / 보툴리눔 독소 A형**: presynaptic acetylcholine-release blocker injected into the lateral pterygoid muscle to reduce excessive muscle activity contributing to recurrent dislocation.
- **Autologous blood injection (ABI) / 자가혈액 주사**: injection of the patient's own blood to provoke a localised inflammatory and fibrotic response that increases joint stability; no ankylosis on follow-up.
- **Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal checklists**: design-specific risk-of-bias tools used here for case–control, case series and case report studies.
- **PABAK / AC1 Gweta**: chance-corrected inter-rater agreement statistics that, unlike Cohen's κ, are robust to skewed response distributions such as both reviewers answering "Yes".
