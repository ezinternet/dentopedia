- **Title**: Comparison of 4- or 6-implant supported immediate full-arch fixed prostheses: A retrospective cohort study of 217 patients followed up for 3–13 years
- **Authors**: Yan Zhang, Sha Li, Ping Di, Yu Zhang, Aozhou Wu, Ye Lin
- **Journal**: Clinical Implant Dentistry and Related Research, 2022;25(2):381–397, doi:10.1111/cid.13170
- **PMID**: 36522852
- **Institution**: Department of Oral Implantology, Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology, Beijing, China; Department of Epidemiology, Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health
- **Study design**: Retrospective cohort study; clinician questionnaire
- **Source**: pubmed-abstract

## 2. Key Contributions

- Largest single-center retrospective cohort directly comparing All-on-4 vs All-on-6 with 3–13 year follow-up (217 patients, 1222 implants, 271 FAFPs).
- Demonstrates no significant overall difference in implant survival, prosthesis survival, complications, or MBL between All-on-4 and All-on-6.
- Critical finding: for high-risk subgroups (elderly, opposing natural/fixed dentition, smoking, bruxism, long cantilever, low bone density, all-acrylic provisional), All-on-6 shows better outcomes in some measurements.
- Uniquely captures clinician decision-making: medium-experienced clinicians and prosthodontists significantly preferred All-on-6 (P<0.05).
- Provides long-term (up to 13yr) head-to-head comparison data that feeds into SR+MA literature.

## 3. Methodology

- **Population**: 217 patients, 1222 implants, 271 FAFPs (202 prostheses on 4 implants [All-on-4], 69 prostheses on 6 implants [All-on-6])
- **Follow-up**: 3–13 years
- **Outcomes**: implant survival, prosthesis survival, biological/technical complications, implant marginal bone loss (MBL)
- **Covariates analyzed**: age, gender, jaw, opposing dentition, smoking, bruxism, bone quantity/quality, cantilever length (CL), prosthesis material, oral hygiene
- **Clinician questionnaire**: 6 surgeons + 3 prosthodontists with >5 years experience; assessed patient/clinician-related influences on implant number choice
- **Statistics**: Cox proportional hazards (implant survival), odds ratio (prosthesis survival/complications), MBL comparison at 1yr, 5yr, 10yr

## 4. Key Results

**Overall comparisons (All-on-4 vs All-on-6): NO significant differences**

| Outcome | All-on-4 vs All-on-6 | p |
|---|---|---|
| Implant survival (implant level) | HR = 1.0 (95%CI 0.8–1.2) | 0.96 |
| Implant survival (prosthesis level) | HR = 0.8 (95%CI 0.3–1.8) | 0.54 |
| Prosthesis survival | OR = 0.8 (95%CI 0.3–2.8) | 0.56 |
| Biological complications | OR = 0.9 (95%CI 0.5–1.8) | 0.78 |
| Technical complications (provisional) | OR = 1.3 (95%CI 0.7–2.3) | 0.42 |
| Technical complications (definitive) | OR = 1.1 (95%CI 0.6–2.2) | 0.33 |
| MBL @1yr | — | 0.65 |
| MBL @5yr | — | 0.28 |
| MBL @10yr | — | 0.14 |

**High-risk subgroup exceptions (All-on-6 more predictable in some measurements)**:
- Elderly patients
- Opposing natural teeth or fixed dentition
- Smoking
- Bruxism
- Long cantilever length
- Low bone density
- All-acrylic provisional prostheses

**Clinician preference (questionnaire)**:
- Medium-experienced clinicians: significantly preferred All-on-6 (P<0.05)
- Implant prosthodontists: significantly preferred All-on-6 (P<0.05)
- Note: experienced implant surgeons showed no significant preference

## 5. Limitations

- Retrospective, non-randomized design; All-on-4 vs All-on-6 assignment based on surgeon judgment
- Single center (Peking University); limited external validity
- Abstract-only ingest; full subgroup analysis detail not available
- All-on-6 group significantly smaller (69 vs 202 prostheses) — potentially underpowered for subgroup analyses
