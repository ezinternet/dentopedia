## Why Ingested

기존 [[infection-control/eswaramurthy-2021-contemporary-sterilization-protocols-healing-abutments]]의 SR 결론은 "어떤 프로토콜도 100% virgin(unused) 표면을 재현하지 못한다"였다. 본 in vitro 연구(Çetinsoy 2026, 이 배치 중 최신)는 그 서사를 정면으로 다듬는(refine) 반례 축이다 — precleaning(enzymatic detergent soak) → US + 1% NaOCl → autoclave의 3-step 프로토콜이 성숙 biofilm을 99.7% 제거하고 SEM/EDX상 표면이 미사용 HA와 유사함을 보여, "완전 제거 불가"라는 부정 서사를 "precleaning + US + NaOCl이면 최소 1회 재사용 사이클은 가능"으로 조건화한다. "autoclave-only" 대조군이 54.4%에 그친다는 점은 [[infection-control/burioni-2024-could-dental-material-reuse-play]](autoclave-only는 잔류 단백질 지속) 및 [[infection-control/naghsh-2024-comparison-four-decontamination-procedures-reusing]](NaOCl + air polishing 효과적)과 결이 맞는다.

## One-line Summary

In vitro study on 95 retrieved titanium healing abutments (2 brands): after enzymatic-detergent precleaning, ultrasonication in 1% sodium hypochlorite + autoclave removed 99.7% of mature biofilm (vs 54.4% for autoclave-only control), with SEM/EDX surface morphology and elemental composition resembling unused abutments.

## 한줄요약

환자에서 회수한 티타늄 healing abutment 95개(2개 브랜드) in vitro 연구: 효소세제(ED) 전세척 후 1% 차아염소산나트륨(NaOCl) 내 초음파 세척 + 오토클레이브가 성숙 biofilm을 99.7% 제거(오토클레이브 단독 대조군 54.4% 대비)했고, SEM/EDX상 표면 형태·원소조성이 미사용 abutment와 유사 — precleaning + US + NaOCl이면 최소 1회 재사용 사이클은 실현 가능함을 시사.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. Directly compares five decontamination protocols on **clinically retrieved** healing abutments (HAs) carrying **mature** biofilm (4–6 weeks intraoral), not lab-inoculated surfaces.
2. Isolates the effect of the debridement agent by holding autoclaving constant across groups — showing that **autoclave alone is the weakest option** (mean 54.4% debridement).
3. Identifies **1% sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) + ultrasonication (US)** as the standout, removing 99.7% of biofilm, and confirms via SEM/EDX that this does not degrade the titanium surface (morphology + elemental composition resembling unused HAs).
4. Frames a concrete, low-cost **three-step clinical protocol** (enzymatic-detergent soak → US in 1% NaOCl → autoclave) positioned as a fallback for **at least one reuse cycle** where placing a new HA is not feasible.
5. Demonstrates the result holds across **two distinct HA macrogeometries** (Brand1 99.6%, Brand2 99.9%), addressing a common criticism that reuse-decontamination studies test a single design.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Samples**: 95 titanium HAs of two distinct brands/designs, retrieved from patients after 4–6 weeks in situ.
- **Precleaning**: all HAs immersed in enzymatic detergent (ED) before group allocation.
- **Randomization**: into 5 groups —
  1. **Control** — autoclave only.
  2. US in 40°C tap water + autoclave.
  3. US in 40°C **1% sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl)** + autoclave.
  4. US in 80°C **citric + maleic + lactic acid (CA)** solution + autoclave.
  5. US in 40°C **enzymatic detergent (ED)** + autoclave.
- **Outcome measurement**: HAs stained and photographed (lateral + occlusal views) before and after decontamination; residual stained area quantified as a proxy for retained contamination.
- **Surface analysis**: scanning electron microscopy (SEM) for morphology and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX/EDS) for elemental composition, compared against unused HAs.
- *(abstract-only — exact staining agent, quantification method, US duration/frequency, and statistical model are not recoverable from the abstract.)*

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Autoclave-only control**: lowest debridement, mean **54.4%**, highest residual contamination.
- **1% NaOCl + US**: almost complete removal, mean **99.7%** (Brand1 99.6%, Brand2 99.9%), outperforming all other groups (p < 0.05).
- **SEM/EDX**: NaOCl-decontaminated surface morphology and elemental composition **similar to unused HAs** — i.e., no detectable surface alteration from the protocol.
- Result **independent of HA macrogeometry** (both brands ≈99.6–99.9%).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **In vitro** design: measures visible/stained biofilm removal and surface chemistry, **not** biological/inflammatory response of peri-implant tissue to a reused HA (contrast with in vitro detoxification studies reporting persistent inflammation).
- **Abstract-only** here: residual-protein/endotoxin assays, microbiological culture, and long-term clinical peri-implant outcomes are not reported in the retrievable text.
- Conclusion is explicitly bounded to **at least one reuse cycle** and to situations "where placing unused HAs is not feasible" — not a general endorsement of unlimited reuse.
- Two brands only; generalization to all HA alloys/surface treatments and to screw-access internal channels (harder to debride) is untested.

## 7. Glossary

- **Healing abutment (HA)**: transmucosal component placed on an implant during soft-tissue healing; frequently retrieved and reused in practice.
- **Mature biofilm**: structurally developed, matrix-embedded microbial community (here from 4–6 weeks intraoral), more resistant to removal than freshly adhered cells.
- **Ultrasonication (US)**: cavitation-based mechanical cleaning in a liquid bath; here paired with different chemical solutions.
- **Sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl)**: oxidizing, protein-dissolving disinfectant; used at 1% here.
- **Enzymatic detergent (ED)**: proteolytic precleaning solution that loosens organic debris before the main decontamination step.
- **SEM / EDX (EDS)**: scanning electron microscopy (surface morphology) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (elemental composition), used to check for surface alteration.
- **Debridement (%)**: proportion of stained contaminant area removed after treatment.
