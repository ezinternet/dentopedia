## Why Ingested

Ingested as part of a 2026-07-20 topic sweep on dental hygienist scaling technique (치위생사 스케일링 술기). This is the wiki's first curette-ergonomics/instrument-design paper; it extends [[professional-wellbeing/kaki-2022-health-wellbeing-clinical-dental-professionals]] — which found that Dental Care Professional wellbeing evidence is concentrated in job-satisfaction and career-trajectory factors and largely silent on physical/musculoskeletal determinants — by supplying controlled physiological evidence (sEMG, grip-force, fatigue) for a workplace-level (meso) determinant, instrument handle design, that Kaki's review identified as under-studied.

## Three-line Summary

Randomized crossover bench study (typodont/manikin model), 12 dental hygienists, comparing a novel adaptive-handle curette (silicone-covered core, adjustable up to a 30° bend) against a conventional rigid stainless-steel curette (both configured as Barnhart 5/6 universal curettes) during simulated calculus removal.

The adaptive curette produced significantly less total and per-muscle surface electromyography (sEMG) work, preserved pinch/grasp strength post-instrumentation (vs. a significant strength drop with the conventional curette), and scored significantly better on comfort/fatigue VAS, blade adaptation, and grasp-video analysis (all p<0.05).

Findings support handle adaptability — spreading instrumentation load over a larger hand/finger contact area — as a design strategy to reduce musculoskeletal burden during periodontal instrumentation, but this is a first typodont-model study; in-vivo clinical-patient studies are described as already underway.

## 세줄요약

무작위 교차설계(crossover) 벤치 연구(typodont/마네킹 모델), 치과위생사 12명 대상, 새로운 적응형(어댑티브) 손잡이 큐렛(실리콘 코팅 코어, 최대 30° 굴곡 조절) vs 기존 경성 스테인리스 스틸 큐렛(둘 다 Barnhart 5/6 유니버설 큐렛) 비교, 인공 치석 제거 과제로 평가.

적응형 큐렛이 총 근활동량 및 개별 근육별 표면근전도(surface Electromyography, sEMG) 작업량을 유의하게 감소시켰고, 기존 큐렛 사용 후 유의하게 감소한 집기력·파지력(pinch/grasp strength)이 적응형 큐렛에서는 유지됐으며, 편안함·피로도 시각아날로그척도(Visual Analog Scale, VAS)·블레이드 적합도·파지 영상분석에서도 유의하게 우수했음(모두 p<0.05).

손가락·손 접촉면적을 넓혀 부하를 분산시키는 손잡이 적응형 설계전략이 치주기구조작 중 근골격계 부담을 줄일 수 있음을 시사하나, typodont 모의모델의 첫 연구로, 실제 환자 대상 임상연구는 저자들에 따르면 현재 진행 중.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First controlled comparison of an adjustable-handle ("adaptive") curette against a conventional rigid stainless-steel curette using simultaneous objective (sEMG, pinch/grasp dynamometry, video analysis) and subjective (VAS comfort/fatigue) measures.
- Demonstrates convergence between objective and subjective ergonomic measures — a gap the authors note is often absent or contradictory in prior hand-tool ergonomics literature.
- Video analysis links handle adaptability to better clinical technique markers (correct blade angulation twice as often, less joint hypermobility/hyperextension), not just comfort.
- Establishes a reusable 20-minute-washout, randomized 1:1 sequence, within-subject (each hygienist as own control) protocol for future instrument-ergonomics comparisons, validated in a pilot sub-study (2 testers, repeated 3x per instrument) confirming sEMG/VAS return to baseline between arms.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Randomized, within-subject crossover bench study; sequence of curette use randomized 1:1 (random.org). Not blinded (curette appearance made blinding infeasible).
- **Testers**: 12 practicing dental hygienists (11 female, 1 male; 10 right-handed, 2 left-handed), age 24–68 (mean 36.1), employed 3–5 days/week clinically; experience ranged 2–5 yr (n=3), 6–10 yr (n=3), ≥11 yr (n=6). Excluded: recent (≤6 mo) hand/wrist/finger injury or diagnosed upper-extremity MSD.
- **Model**: Artificial calculus (Kilgore Dental Calculus Set) applied supra-/sub-gingivally to 32 artificial teeth in a typodont, standardized to a fixed 18-hour calculus-hardening interval before each session; typodont mounted on a manikin face fixed to a clinical dental chair.
- **Instruments**: Curette A = prototype Barnhart 5/6 with a central, universally bendable (up to ~30°) core under a silicone overlay ("adaptive" handle). Curette B = conventional stainless-steel Barnhart 5/6 curette (similar working end/length/weight, smaller handle diameter, matched to popular commercial curette dimensions).
- **Protocol**: Each hygienist scaled 8 typodont segments per arm (1 min/segment, fixed sequence covering anterior facial/lingual and posterior buccal/lingual sextants), 8 min total scaling per arm, with a 20-min rest between arms (duration set from a prior 2-tester pilot confirming full sEMG/VAS return to baseline). 1-min instructional video + 5-min accustomization period before first use of the adaptive curette.
- **Outcome capture**: sEMG (FREEEMG, BTS Engineering) from 4 hand/forearm muscles (APB, FDI, FPL, EDC), normalized to each muscle's 15-s maximum voluntary isometric contraction (MVC); signal rectified/filtered (2nd-order Butterworth, 10 Hz high-pass), total workload = integrated area under the curve. Pinch/grasp strength via Jamar dynamometer, 3 measurements before and 3 after each arm. VAS (0–10) for fatigue/comfort immediately post-arm. Video recording of hand/grasp/blade position throughout, scored for grasp type, joint hypermobility, blade-to-tooth angulation (target 70°), and shank-to-long-axis parallelism. Spontaneous tester comments recorded. Gloves were not worn (would have interfered with sEMG electrodes) — flagged by the authors as a limitation with follow-up studies underway.
- **Statistics**: Paired t-tests (SPSS 19); repeated-measures ANOVA with covariates (sequence, age/experience) to test robustness; significance α=0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Comfort/fatigue (VAS)**: Curette A significantly better than B in every category (hand, wrist, finger comfort; overall fatigue), p<0.05.
- **Pinch/grasp strength**: No pre-scaling difference between instruments (p>0.05). Post-scaling, both pinch and grasp strength dropped significantly with curette B but were preserved (no significant drop) with curette A (p<0.05).
- **sEMG total/per-muscle work**: Curette A required significantly less total muscle work and significantly less work in each of the 4 individual muscles (APB, FDI, FPL, EDC) than curette B (p<0.05).
- **Video analysis**: Joint hypermobility/hyperextension occurred significantly more often with curette B (p<0.05). Blade-shank parallelism to the tooth's long axis and blade adaptation to the tooth surface were both significantly better with curette A (p<0.05); a correct 70° blade angle was achieved twice as often with curette A as with curette B.
- **Covariates**: Neither years of clinical experience, handedness, nor instrumentation sequence significantly affected any outcome (p>0.05 for all) — attributed partly to the 20-min inter-arm washout.
- **Qualitative**: 5/12 testers spontaneously reported feeling lower pinch/grasp force with curette A; 2 reported better access and lingual-surface blade adaptation; 1 wanted more accustomization time; 1 disliked the silicone's "rubbery" feel and preferred a stiffer surface.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single first-of-its-kind study; only one design variable (adaptive/bendable handle + silicone) was isolated, but the prototype also differs from the comparator in handle diameter and surface material, so the authors explicitly attribute the effect to a *bundle* of design differences, not the adaptive mechanism alone.
- Bench/typodont model, not real patients — no scaling-duration or clinical-outcome (calculus-removal completeness, root-surface effect) endpoints were measured; authors state more extensive in-vivo patient studies are underway.
- Unblinded (instrument appearance made blinding impossible) — a performance/expectation bias risk the authors acknowledge but do not quantify.
- No glove use (methodologically required to keep sEMG electrodes functional), which limits generalization to typical clinical infection-control conditions; glove-compatible follow-up studies are described as in progress.
- Tactile-feedback quality (silicone vs. metal) was not formally evaluated despite one tester's negative comment; planned for future comparison.

## 6. Related Work

The paper situates itself within a broader dental/occupational ergonomics literature establishing that handle diameter, hardness, texture, weight, and thermal conductivity all affect hand-tool ergonomic performance, evaluated via EMG/sEMG, goniometry/inclinometry/kinematics/accelerometry (posture), and pinch/grasp dynamometry (force decay with use). It also draws on non-dental hand-tool ergonomics research (sewing, gardening, workmen's tools) showing that spreading contact area/force over a larger surface lowers pressure-per-unit-area and improves comfort — the mechanistic rationale offered for why the adaptive handle reduced sEMG work in this study. ## 7. Glossary

- **sEMG (surface Electromyography)**: Non-invasive recording of muscle electrical activity via skin-surface electrodes; used here to quantify muscle "work" during scaling.
- **MVC (Maximum Voluntary Isometric Contraction)**: A 15-second maximal muscle contraction recorded per muscle to normalize (100% reference) subsequent sEMG readings.
- **VAS (Visual Analog Scale)**: 0–10 subjective rating scale (0 = no fatigue/discomfort, 10 = extreme fatigue/discomfort).
- **Typodont**: An artificial dentition model mounted in a manikin, used to simulate intraoral instrumentation without a live patient.
- **Barnhart 5/6**: A universal curette design (double-ended, angulated for both anterior and posterior surfaces) used as the common working-end platform for both test instruments.
- **APB / FDI / FPL / EDC**: Abductor pollicis brevis, first dorsal interosseous, flexor pollicis longus, extensor digitorum communis — the four hand/forearm muscles instrumented with sEMG.
- **CTS (Carpal Tunnel Syndrome)**: A median-nerve compression disorder cited in the Introduction as highly prevalent among dental hygienists (~45% report symptoms, ~10% diagnosed).
- **MSD (Musculoskeletal Disorder)**: Umbrella term for the occupational hand/wrist/arm/neck/back conditions this ergonomic design intervention targets.
