## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort study (n=287 orthognathic surgery patients, Tel-Aviv Sourasky Medical Center, 2010–2024) analyzing incidence, anatomical distribution, and postoperative treatment outcomes of trigeminal nerve injuries.

Neurosensory deficits occurred in 5.9% of cases (17/287, 95% CI 3.7–9.3%, 88.2% mandibular/CN V3); of the treated subset, LLLT (970 nm diode laser, 8/17 patients) produced subjective improvement in 75% vs 33% with no treatment, while corticosteroid (preoperative dexamethasone 20 mg in 82.2%) and vitamin B complex (B1/B2/B3/B6/B12, one patient) were used adjunctively but complete sensory restoration was rarely achieved with any modality.

No standardized step-by-step pharmacologic/physical-therapy protocol exists for post-orthognathic IAN/trigeminal neurosensory deficit management; retrospective design, small event count (n=17), and non-standardized LLLT/vitamin-B regimens limit causal inference — findings are hypothesis-generating for LLLT as the most promising adjunct.

## 세줄요약

후향적 코호트 연구(n=287, 정악교정수술 환자, Tel-Aviv Sourasky Medical Center, 2010–2024) — 삼차신경(trigeminal nerve) 손상의 발생률·해부학적 분포·치료 결과 분석.

감각이상은 5.9%(17/287, 95% CI 3.7–9.3%, 88.2% 하악/CN V3)에서 발생; 치료군 중 저출력레이저치료(LLLT, 970nm 다이오드 레이저, 8/17명)는 무치료군 대비 주관적 호전율 75% vs 33%로 우세했으나, 코르티코스테로이드(수술 전 덱사메타손 20mg, 82.2%)·비타민B 복합제(B1/B2/B3/B6/B12, 1명)를 포함한 어떤 치료법으로도 완전한 감각 회복은 드물었음.

정악교정수술 후 하치조신경/삼차신경 감각이상에 대한 표준화된 약물·물리치료 프로토콜은 부재; 후향적 설계·적은 사례 수(n=17)·비표준화된 LLLT/비타민B 용법이 인과추론을 제한 — LLLT가 가장 유망한 보조요법이라는 가설생성적(hypothesis-generating) 결과.

## 2. Key Contributions

- One of the few studies reporting real-world incidence (5.9%) and treatment outcomes of trigeminal nerve injury specifically in the **orthognathic surgery** context (most prior IAN-injury literature focuses on third-molar extraction or implant placement).
- Directly compares three adjunctive treatment modalities in the same clinical cohort: low-level laser therapy (LLLT), vitamin B complex supplementation, and corticosteroids (perioperative dexamethasone).
- Explicitly states — after a literature search — that **no published step-by-step protocol exists** for managing IAN/trigeminal neurosensory deficits after orthognathic surgery or third-molar extraction, reinforcing the same evidence gap noted by [[oral-surgery/nerve-injury/lee-2015-inferior-alveolar-nerve-injury-management]].
- Identifies operative time (230 min vs 198 min median) as a potential surrogate marker for neural traction risk.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Single-center retrospective cohort, complete census of orthognathic surgery patients at TASMC, Jan 2010–Dec 2024.
- **Population**: 287 procedures (56% female); 106 single-jaw, 181 bimaxillary; 55.1% LeFort-I + IVRO combination, 30% isolated LeFort-I; 39 (13.5%) with genioplasty.
- **Primary outcome**: Postoperative trigeminal neurosensory deficit (CN V2/V3 distribution), clinician-documented complaint ± objective bedside testing (light touch, two-point discrimination) when available.
- **Exposure variables**: Osteotomy type (Le Fort I / BSSO/SSO / IVRO), genioplasty, operative time, mandibular movement (mm), bad split occurrence, IAN exposure/manipulation.
- **Treatment variables**: Preoperative dexamethasone (20 mg standard), postoperative LLLT (970 nm diode, 2 W continuous, 10 s/point, 20 Hz, ~10–20 J/cm² per point, 6–8 sessions over 3–4 weeks), vitamin B complex (B1 100mg/B2 100mg/B3 100mg/B6 100mg/B12 200μg daily × 30 days), or no treatment.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 28.0; Shapiro-Wilk, Kruskal-Wallis, Mann-Whitney, Fisher's exact, Chi-square; two-sided α=0.05. Sensitivity analysis excluding incomplete-follow-up cases.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Incidence**: 17/287 (5.9%, 95% CI 3.7–9.3%) neurosensory deficits; 15/17 (88.2%) mandibular (CN V3), 2/17 (11.8%) maxillary/infraorbital (CN V2).
- **Demographics of affected**: 58.8% female, median age 22 (IQR 20–29); no significant epidemiological predictors identified.
- **Preoperative steroid**: 14/17 (82.2%) received dexamethasone 20 mg prophylaxis.
- **Postoperative treatment outcomes** (of 17):
  - LLLT alone (n=8, 47.06%): 6/8 (75%) subjective improvement, none achieved complete restoration.
  - LLLT + vitamin B (n=1): improvement without full recovery.
  - No treatment (n=9): 3/9 (33%) improvement without full recovery, 3/9 (33%) persistent deficit, 3/9 (33%) follow-up data unavailable.
- **Risk factors**: Higher incidence with BSSO or IVRO+LeFort-I combinations vs isolated LeFort-I; median operative time longer in injured group (230 min, IQR 210–255) vs overall cohort (198 min, IQR 180–220); no significant difference in mandibular movement magnitude (4.3±1.3mm injured vs 4.5±1.7mm non-injured); bad split (n=1) and intraoperative nerve manipulation (n=2) all showed partial-only transient recovery.
- **Cited external RCT**: a 20-patient RCT comparing LLLT vs vitamin B12 for post-third-molar-extraction IAN paresthesia found significantly greater neurosensory improvement (VAS + light touch/two-point discrimination) with LLLT.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Retrospective design; incomplete follow-up in 4/9 untreated patients (information bias).
- Neurosensory assessment mostly subjective; objective testing (current perception threshold, two-point discrimination) not routinely applied.
- Very small event count (n=17) precluded statistical testing of risk factors or treatment efficacy — associations (LLLT 75% vs no-treatment 33% improvement) are descriptive, not adjusted/powered comparisons.
- No standardized LLLT or vitamin B dosing regimen across patients, limiting generalizability.
- Authors call for prospective studies to validate a structured, conservative-care management pathway (documentation, serial neurosensory testing, symptom-guided pharmacotherapy, time-based reassessment).

## 7. Glossary

- **LLLT**: Low-Level Laser Therapy — photobiomodulation intended to reduce inflammation and promote nerve regeneration.
- **BSSO/SSO**: Bilateral Sagittal Split Osteotomy — mandibular ramus osteotomy technique, higher IAN injury risk.
- **IVRO**: Intraoral Vertical Ramus Osteotomy — alternative mandibular osteotomy, lower reported IAN injury risk than BSSO.
- **CN V2/V3**: Maxillary/mandibular divisions of the trigeminal nerve (cranial nerve V).
- **Methylcobalamin**: Activated form of vitamin B12; promotes neurite outgrowth/neuronal survival in nerve-injury models.
- **Neuropraxia**: Mildest nerve injury grade — conduction block without axonal disruption, typically fully reversible.
