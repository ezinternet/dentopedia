## Three-line Summary

Controlled instrumented study in five healthy subjects comparing three intra-oral tracing-plate inclinations (horizontal at 0 degrees to the occlusal plane, fully inclined along the line from the condylar point to the stylus at the occlusal plane, and half-inclined) during Gothic arch and tapping movements (n=30), with incisal and condylar points captured by a 6-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) jaw movement tracking system.

Repeated-measures one-way ANOVA found the mean position of tapping points significantly different among the appliances: the half-inclined appliance recorded the 30 tapping points in a convergent area nearer to the intercuspal position (IP), and tap contact points lay anterior to the Gothic arch apex in every appliance.

Plate inclination therefore changes the condylar position registered by a tapping registration, but the abstract gives no numeric effect sizes, p-values or millimetre offsets and the sample is only five subjects, so the direction of the optimum is supported while its magnitude is not.

## 세줄요약

건강한 성인 5명(n=5)을 대상으로 구내 추적판(Intra-oral Tracing Plate) 경사도 3종 — 수평(교합평면에 대해 0°), 완전경사(과상돌기점에서 교합평면의 스타일러스 위치로 그은 선의 각도), 반경사 — 을 비교한 계측 연구; 고딕아치(Gothic Arch) 및 탭핑운동(Tapping Movement, n=30) 중 절치점(Incisal Point)과 과상돌기점(Condylar Point)을 6자유도(6 Degrees of Freedom, 6-DOF) 하악운동 추적시스템으로 기록.

반복측정 일원분산분석(Repeated-measures One-way ANOVA)에서 30개 탭핑점의 평균 위치는 기기 간 유의하게 달랐다; 반경사(Half-inclined) 기기가 탭핑점을 최대교두접촉위(Intercuspal Position, IP)에 가장 가까운 수렴 영역에 기록했고, 모든 기기에서 탭핑 접촉점은 고딕아치 정점보다 전방이었다.

추적판 경사도는 탭핑 기록으로 등록되는 과상돌기 위치를 실질적으로 바꾸지만, 초록에는 수치적 효과크기·p값·mm 오차가 없고 표본이 5명뿐이어서 최적 경사의 방향만 지지되고 크기는 확인되지 않는다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Isolates **tracing-plate inclination** as the single manipulated device variable inside one intra-oral tracing-device concept, rather than comparing different jaw-relation recording modalities.
- Tracks both the **incisal point and the condylar point** with a 6-DOF system, tying a device setting directly to the registered *condylar* position instead of relying on the incisal tracing alone.
- Identifies the **half-inclined plate** as the setting whose tapping points converge nearest to the intercuspal position (IP).
- Reports that **tap contact points are anterior to the Gothic arch apex for all three inclinations** — a caution against treating the Gothic arch apex and the tapping-registered position as interchangeable.
- Standardizes the test geometry (30 mm interincisal distance, head Camper plane horizontal, 30 tapping movements) so the three inclinations are compared under one controlled posture.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: three appliances differing in tracing-plate inclination, tested within the same healthy subjects.
- **Subjects**: five healthy subjects.
- **Plate inclinations compared**:
  1. *Horizontal* — 0 degrees to the occlusal plane;
  2. *Inclined* — the angle formed by drawing a line from the condylar point to the stylus position at the occlusal plane;
  3. *Half-inclined* — half of that inclined angle.
- **Movements**: Gothic arch tracing and tapping movements (n=30) at a 30 mm interincisal distance, with the head positioned so the Camper plane was horizontal.
- **Instrumentation**: 6-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) jaw movement tracking system recording incisal and condylar points.
- **Outcomes**: location of the Gothic arch apex; distribution and mean position of the 30 tapping points relative to intercuspal position (IP), evaluated at the incisal and condylar points and compared across the three appliances.
- **Statistics**: repeated-measures one-way ANOVA.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Result as reported in the abstract |
|---|---|
| Mean position of tapping points | Significantly different among the three appliances (repeated-measures one-way ANOVA) |
| Half-inclined appliance | Tapping points fell in a convergent area nearer to intercuspal position (IP) than with the other appliances |
| Gothic arch apex vs tap contacts | In all appliances the contact points of the tapping movement were anterior to the Gothic arch apex |

- The abstract reports **no numeric effect sizes, no p-values, no angular or millimetre offsets, and no condylar-coordinate values**; the only quantitative facts available are the design parameters (n=5 subjects, n=30 tapping movements, 30 mm interincisal distance, three inclinations including a 0-degree plate).
- Direction of effect is available (half-inclined closest to IP; tap contacts anterior to apex); magnitude is not.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only — full text not retrieved.** No PMCID exists for this record; the full Methods, tables, figures and discussion were not accessed, so any detail beyond the structured abstract is unavailable.
- **Very small sample (n=5 healthy subjects).** The repeated-measures design helps within-subject comparison, but five subjects give low precision and limit generalization to edentulous patients, for whom jaw-relation registration is actually performed.
- **No numeric results in the abstract.** There are no effect sizes, p-values, variances or coordinate values — the "significantly different" statement and the convergence finding cannot be quantified or independently checked at this level.
- **Healthy dentulous subjects, not the clinical population.** Optimum condylar position in complete-denture jaw-relation registration may behave differently without periodontal proprioception and with unstable record bases.
- **Apex-versus-tap discordance unexplained at abstract level.** That tap contacts were anterior to the Gothic arch apex in every appliance is reported without a quantified offset or a mechanistic explanation in the retrieved text.
- Future work would require the full text to recover the actual condylar-point displacements and to test the half-inclined setting in edentulous patients.

## 7. Glossary

- **Intra-oral tracing device** — an appliance with a tracing plate and stylus worn in the mouth to graphically register mandibular border and functional movements for jaw-relation recording.
- **Tracing plate inclination** — the tilt of that plate relative to the occlusal plane; compared here at horizontal (0 degrees), fully inclined, and half-inclined.
- **Gothic arch (Gothic Arch Tracing)** — the arrowhead-shaped graphic produced by protrusive and lateral mandibular excursions; its apex is conventionally read as the retruded/centric reference point.
- **Tapping movement (Tapping Movement)** — repeated small vertical closure taps used to sample the repeatable closure position rather than a guided border position.
- **Condylar point (Condylar Point)** — a tracked reference point representing the mandibular condyle, here recorded alongside the incisal point.
- **6-DOF jaw tracking** — six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) recording of three translations and three rotations of the mandible.
- **Intercuspal position (IP)** — maximum intercuspation, used here as the reference from which the 30 tapping points were measured.
- **Camper plane (Camper Plane)** — the anatomical reference plane through the nasal spine and the auditory meatus, leveled horizontally to standardize head posture.
