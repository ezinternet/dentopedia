## One-line Summary

Narrative review of orthodontically forced eruption (extrusion) for restorative purposes — crown lengthening/ferrule when caries or fracture violates the supracrestal tissue attachment (biologic width), and implant site development — detailing indications, contraindications, PDL tension biology, Salama and Hochman classifications, appliances, and ~1–2 mm/month extrusion protocol.

## 한줄요약

교정적 정출(Forced Eruption, 강제맹출)의 수복 목적 활용을 다룬 내러티브 리뷰 — 우식·파절이 초치조상부 조직부착(생물학적 폭경, biologic width)을 침범했을 때의 크라운연장·페룰 확보와 임플란트 식립부 개발; 적응증·금기·치주인대(PDL) 장력 생물학·Salama/Hochman 분류·장치·정출 속도(1–2 mm/월) 정리.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Consolidates when forced eruption is a valid alternative to resective crown lengthening for **biologic-width (SCTA) violation** and inadequate **ferrule** (target: 1.5–2 mm ferrule + ~4.5 mm supra-alveolar structure, ≈2 mm of which is biologic width).
- Contrasts the two opposite goals of extrusion: for **ferrule** the bone/soft tissue must NOT follow → fiberotomy; for **implant site development** the bone SHOULD follow → PDL tension deposits crestal bone.
- Summarizes two classifications: **Salama 1993** extraction-site (Type 1/2/3, Type 2→1 convertible, Type 3 contraindicated) and **Hochman** soft-tissue response (bone sounding vs keratinized tissue).
- Practical protocol: extrusion 1–2 mm/month, forces ~15 g anterior / ~50 g posterior, fixed vs clear-aligner appliances, 1–6 month stabilization.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Narrative literature review (no PRISMA search protocol, no meta-analysis) synthesizing historical + clinical + animal evidence.
- Organized by indication: (1) ferrule/crown lengthening, (2) implant site development; plus biology, methodology/efficacy.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Advantages vs crown-lengthening surgery**: preserves supporting bone, spares adjacent teeth, avoids recession/sensitivity, better crown-to-root ratio (4:5 → 4:4 vs 5:4), superior esthetics.
- **Disadvantages**: prolonged treatment; possible (prophylactic) endodontics with large extrusion + occlusal reduction; fiberotomy reduces but may not prevent coronal attachment migration.
- **Absolute contraindication**: absence of a healthy attachment apparatus (active periodontitis) — extrusion then causes only coronal relocation + clinical attachment loss. Also ankylosis, hypercementosis, vertical root fracture, root proximity.
- **Efficacy evidence**: Berglundh 1991 dog — 4.5 mm extrusion/16 wk, PD unchanged, recession 0.5 mm; Somar SR — 0.5–1 mm/month, bone-augmentation efficacy 69–100%, Amato ~65% keratinized-tissue augmentation.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative (not systematic) review — no pooled estimates, selection/citation not protocolized.
- Paucity of controlled studies on long-term stability of extruded sites and on fiberotomy's efficacy in obviating additional crown lengthening.
- No systematic review yet giving evidence-based guidelines on indications/management/complications (authors state intent to pursue).

## 7. Glossary

- **Forced eruption / orthodontic extrusion**: tractional force on the PDL that moves a tooth coronally; tension zone stimulates crestal bone apposition.
- **Ferrule**: 1.5–2 mm circumferential band of sound tooth structure coronal to the margin that reinforces an endodontically treated, restored tooth.
- **Supracrestal tissue attachment (SCTA) / biologic width**: junctional epithelium + connective tissue attachment (~2 mm) that a restorative margin must not invade.
- **Fiberotomy (supracrestal)**: severing supracrestal connective-tissue fibers to prevent bone/soft tissue from following the extruded tooth (used for crown lengthening).
- **Salama classification (1993)**: extraction-site defect types 1/2/3 by residual bone morphology and regenerative potential.
- **Hochman classification**: soft-tissue response to extrusion based on sulcus/pocket depth, MGJ position, and bone sounding.
