## Why Ingested

The wiki held no page on implant *fixture fracture* as a distinct failure mode, and this series supplies the preoperative warning-sign sequence (repetitive screw loosening → marginal bone loss → fracture at the third thread) that converts an unpreventable catastrophe into a scheduled intervention → [[implants/survival/song-2021-implant-complications-bruxism-patients]] and [[prosthetic-materials/abutment-screw/kim-2019-astra-implant-dissection-solutions]].

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective case series (Maxillofac Plast Reconstr Surg 2020;42:13, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital; 13 fractured implants in 12 patients removed 2007-2015) analyzing crown/implant ratio, time to fracture, preceding symptoms, treatment and replacement outcomes.

Fractures clustered in posterior sites (10 molars, 3 premolars; 9 of 12 patients male), mean crown/implant ratio was 0.83:1, the mean interval from prosthesis delivery to fracture was 61.7 months, and screw loosening, marginal bone loss or peri-implant disease preceded fracture in most cases.

Every fracture required fixture removal and 12 of 13 sites were re-implanted with 100% survival over a mean 54.6 months, so the practical yield is the warning-sign triad — repetitive screw loosening, progressive marginal bone loss, and posterior overload — as a trigger for intervention before the fixture breaks.

## Key Contributions
- Characterizes the clinical profile of fixture fracture in a Korean population: overwhelmingly posterior (10 molar, 3 premolar of 13), predominantly male (9 of 12), mean age at fracture 59.3 years.
- Documents the predecessor-symptom sequence — screw loosening (repetitive in 3 of 4 affected implants), peri-implantitis/mucositis, and peri-implant bone resorption — reported in parallel in the majority of cases.
- Reports the crown/implant ratio in fractured cases (mean 0.83:1) and the mean time from prosthesis delivery to fracture (61.7 months, ~5 years).
- Shows fracture is a salvageable event: all fractured fixtures were removed, 12 of 13 sites re-implanted, and none of the replacements failed over a mean 54.6 months.
- Identifies parafunction as a co-factor in 2 of 12 patients (sleep bruxism; attrition from a strong chewing habit) and an unmanaged hard splint in the illustrative case.

## Methodology
Retrospective review of all patients who had a fractured implant fixture removed at a single institution between 2007 and 2015. Records and radiographs (panoramic + periapical) were used to collect: sex, age, implant type, diameter and length, bone graft material, parafunction, presence of a mesiodistal cantilever (single crowns), crown/implant ratio, type of upper prosthesis, time of fracture, clinical symptoms preceding fracture, treatment of the fractured implant, and complications.

Crown/implant ratio measured on radiographs; in 5 of the 6 implants placed elsewhere an estimated value was used to correct for radiographic enlargement, and one implant could not be measured because the fractured segment was lost. Replacement implants were followed with ISQ (Osstell) primary/secondary stability.

## Key Results

**Demographics / site (n = 12 patients, 13 implants)**

| Variable | Value |
|---|---|
| Sex | 9 male / 3 female |
| Maxilla / mandible | 6 / 7 |
| Molar / premolar | 10 / 3 |
| Mean age at fracture | 59.3 years |
| Placed at own hospital / elsewhere | 7 / 6 |

**Implant dimensions (n = 12 measured)**

| Diameter | n | Length | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.8 mm | 2 | 10 mm | 1 |
| 4.0 mm | 3 | 11.5 mm | 4 |
| 4.5 mm | 5 | 12 mm | 4 |
| 5.0 mm | 2 | 13 mm | 3 |

**Prosthetics and timing.** 6 single implant crowns, 7 splinted prostheses; one mesiodistal cantilever; mean crown/implant ratio 0.83:1 (n = 12); mean healing time from placement to prosthesis completion 5.9 months; mean interval from prosthesis completion to fracture 61.7 months.

**Preceding symptoms.** Screw loosening in 4 of the 7 implants placed in-house (repetitive in 3 of those 4); peri-implantitis in 3 and mucositis in 1; peri-implant bone resorption in 3 of the 6 implants placed elsewhere plus screw loosening in 1; periodontal problems in 6 of 13; BOP positive in only 1.

**Parafunction.** 2 of 12 patients — sleep bruxism in one, attrition from a strong chewing habit in the other. In the bruxism case a hard resin splint was made but not worn, with repetitive screw loosening at 42 and 48 months, a feeling of hypo-occlusion at 59 months, and bilateral fixture fracture confirmed.

**Treatment and outcome.** All 13 fractured fixtures removed (trephine/surgical bur); 12 of 13 sites re-implanted; no replacement implant failed or was removed during a mean follow-up of 54.6 months. Complications were confined to the residual bone defect created by fixture removal.

## Limitations
Small series (13 implants) from a single centre, so no incidence denominator — the paper cannot estimate fracture rate, only describe the phenotype. Six implants were placed elsewhere and their systems could not be identified, and 5 of those 6 crown/implant ratios were estimated rather than measured. One implant could not be measured at all. There is no control group of intact implants, so the reported crown/implant ratio (0.83:1) cannot be interpreted as a risk threshold. Retrospective symptom ascertainment depends on chart quality. The recommendation to act on warning signs is plausible but not tested here — no data are presented on how many implants with identical symptoms did *not* fracture.

## Glossary
- **C/I ratio:** Crown/implant ratio — clinical crown height divided by implant length above bone.
- **Fixture fracture:** Fracture of the implant body itself, usually at the third thread, requiring removal.
- **ISQ:** Implant Stability Quotient (Osstell RFA).
- **Mesiodistal cantilever:** Prosthetic extension beyond the most distal implant support.
- **Parafunction:** Bruxism/clenching or habitual overload beyond normal masticatory function.
- **Trephine bur:** Hollow bur used to core out a fractured fixture with surrounding bone.
