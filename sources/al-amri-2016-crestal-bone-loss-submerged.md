## Why Ingested

This is the earliest systematic review held in the wiki on the submerged-vs-nonsubmerged healing axis, and it was retrieved specifically to sit against [[wiki/implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]] — the later SR+MA that reports a *statistically significant* pooled bone-loss difference (MD 0.12 mm; 95% CI 0.00–0.24) in the same direction. Read together they show the clinical bottom line converging even as the statistical one diverges: Al Amri 2016 found no significant crestal bone-loss difference at all (5 of 6 human studies null), while Moustafa Ali's own authors judged their significant 0.12 mm effect **not clinically relevant**. It also fills a scope the newer design-factor reviews left open: [[wiki/implants/mbl/mortazavi-2021-bone-loss-tissue-bone-level-implants]] treats *design* levers on marginal bone loss (platform switching, Morse taper, SLActive) and says nothing about the surgical healing protocol, and Al Amri explicitly **excluded** platform-switching studies, which fixes the boundary of what its null can be applied to.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (13 studies — 6 human + 7 animal, all university-based; searched 1986 through October 2015; King Saud University, Riyadh) assessing crestal bone loss (CBL) around submerged vs nonsubmerged dental implants, framed as the focused question "Does crestal and subcrestal placement of dental implants influence crestal bone levels?"

No significant difference in CBL was found between submerged and nonsubmerged implants: 5 of 6 human studies reported no significant difference (n = 8–84 individuals, follow-up 1–5 years; CBL 0.17–0.9 mm at test sites vs 0.02–1.4 mm at control sites), and 4 of 7 dog studies were likewise null (follow-up 2–6 months).

The evidence does not support choosing submerged vs nonsubmerged healing to protect crestal bone — but the null is a vote count with no pooled effect estimate, 7 of 13 studies are short-term animal work, and platform-switching studies were excluded, so the conclusion cannot be extended to modern platform-switched implants.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **First review on this question** — the author states that to their knowledge no systematic review of CBL around submerged vs nonsubmerged implants existed, so this fills a stated gap rather than updating one
- **Explicitly framed as a focused question**, not a narrative sweep: "Does crestal and subcrestal placement of dental implants influence crestal bone levels?" — placing placement-depth (crestal vs subcrestal) and healing protocol (submerged vs nonsubmerged) inside one question
- **Wide evidence window** (1986 through October 2015) covering the full modern implant era
- **Deliberate exclusion of platform-switched implant studies** — the single most consequential scope decision in the review, since it carves out the design class that later became the dominant MBL literature (see `## 5. Limitations`)
- **Null result reported without a pooled estimate** — no meta-analysis, so the synthesis is a directional vote count with reported CBL ranges rather than an effect size, confidence interval, or heterogeneity statistic
- **Separates human from animal evidence explicitly** in the results, and reports the species, age range, and follow-up window of the animal component

## 3. Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review **without** meta-analysis (narrative/qualitative synthesis of reported CBL values)
- **Focused question**: "Does crestal and subcrestal placement of dental implants influence crestal bone levels?"
- **Databases**: **not named in the abstract** — only the search window and the keyword combinations are reported; do not assume specific databases were searched
- **Search window**: 1986 through October 2015
- **Keywords**: different combinations of *crestal*, *sub-crestal*, *bone loss*, *dental implant*, *submerged*, *nonsubmerged*
- **Supplementary searching**: reference lists of potentially relevant original **and** review articles were hand-searched to identify further studies
- **Exclusions**: letters to the editor, case reports, commentaries, studies on **platform-switched implants**, and studies published in languages other than English
- **Included**: 13 studies — 6 human, 7 animal — all performed at universities
- **Outcomes**: crestal bone loss (mm) at test vs control sites
- **Risk of bias / certainty / publication bias**: no appraisal tool, no GRADE, no funnel plot is mentioned in the abstract; whether one was applied cannot be determined from the abstract

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Studies included | 13 total (6 human, 7 animal), all university-based |
| Human sample size | 8–84 individuals |
| Human follow-up | 1–5 years |
| CBL at test sites | 0.17–0.9 mm |
| CBL at control sites | 0.02–1.4 mm |
| Human studies reporting no significant difference | **5 of 6** |
| Animal species | Dogs only, mean age 1 to ~2 years |
| Animal follow-up | 2–6 months |
| Animal studies reporting no significant difference | **4 of 7** |
| Review conclusion | "No significant difference in CBL was found around submerged and nonsubmerged dental implants." |

**How to read these numbers honestly:**
- The 0.17–0.9 mm (test) and 0.02–1.4 mm (control) figures are **cross-study ranges of minimum and maximum reported values**, not paired group means. The control maximum (1.4 mm) exceeds the test maximum (0.9 mm), but this **cannot** be read as "submerged implants lost less bone" — the ranges come from different studies and no within-study contrast is reported in the abstract.
- "5 of 6 human studies reported no significant difference" is a **vote count**. Without per-study effect sizes it cannot distinguish a true absence of effect from an underpowered test failing to detect a small one.
- **4 of 7 animal studies** were null, so 3 animal studies did not report a null result. The abstract does not state what those 3 reported — no direction, magnitude, or significance level can be attributed to them.
- No p-value, confidence interval, effect size, or heterogeneity statistic appears anywhere in the abstract.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Stated or structural (readable from the abstract):
- **No meta-analysis and no pooled effect estimate** — the synthesis is a vote count plus value ranges, so the null cannot be quantified or power-checked.
- **Over half the evidence base is animal** — 7 of 13 studies, all in dogs, with 2–6 months of follow-up against 1–5 years in the human studies (a 20–60× shorter window). Canine bone remodeling is faster than human, so these studies cannot speak to long-term crestal stability.
- **Platform-switching studies explicitly excluded** — the conclusion is scoped to conventional (non-platform-switched) implants and cannot be transferred to the platform-switched designs that dominate the newer MBL literature held in this wiki (e.g. [[wiki/implants/mbl/mortazavi-2021-bone-loss-tissue-bone-level-implants]]).
- **English-language restriction** — a language bias that cannot be assessed without the full text.
- **All 13 studies were performed at universities** — generalizability to community/private practice is unaddressed.
- **Small and short human samples** — as few as 8 individuals and as little as 1 year of follow-up; because most crestal bone loss occurs in the first year, a 1-year window is the minimum informative duration and longer remodeling is unobserved.
- **The focused question bundles two variables** — crestal vs subcrestal placement *and* submerged vs nonsubmerged healing — and the abstract does not separate their contributions, so a null for the pair cannot be assigned to either factor alone.

*(inferred)* Additional gaps from abstract-only retrieval:
- No per-study table, no risk-of-bias instrument, no certainty rating, no publication-bias assessment, and no study-level data are retrievable; the full text would be required to appraise the review's own rigor before its null is relied on.
- The wide, overlapping CBL ranges with no pooled estimate mean the review **cannot exclude a small but real protocol effect** — consistent with the later pooled estimate of MD 0.12 mm reported by [[wiki/implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]].

## 6. Related Work

- [[wiki/implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]] — Moustafa Ali 2018 · sr+ma · **same question, opposite statistical direction.** That SR+MA (6 RCTs) reports **significantly more** MBL with submerged placement (MD 0.12 mm; 95% CI 0.00–0.24; I² = 0%, P = .04), where this review found **no significant difference**. Reconciling them honestly: this review is 2 years earlier, includes 7 animal studies and no meta-analysis, and excludes platform-switching; the later one is RCT-only and pooled but judged underpowered with 4 of 6 trials at high risk of bias — and its own authors call the 0.12 mm difference **not clinically relevant**, which is consistent with this review's null. **No typed `relations:` edge is written here** because both pages were ingested in the same parallel batch; a later ingest can add `type: contradicts` on this older page.
- [[wiki/implants/pirc-2026-one-piece-two-piece-implants-15year-rct]] — Pirc 2026 · rct (15–17-year follow-up) — the long-term design contrast. One-piece implants (no implant-abutment interface at bone) showed MBL 0.08 mm vs 1.53 mm for two-piece, a magnitude far exceeding any protocol effect, indicating that **eliminating the interface** matters more than whether the flap is closed over it.
- [[wiki/implants/mbl/mortazavi-2021-bone-loss-tissue-bone-level-implants]] — Mortazavi 2021 · sr (38 trials) — the design-factor counterpart. Covers platform switching, Morse taper, and SLActive as MBL levers; silent on healing protocol. Its conclusion that design modifiers dominate is *complemented* by this review's null on the procedural axis, but the two reviews share no included studies worth reconciling numerically here (this review excludes platform-switching trials outright).
- [[wiki/implants/mbl/kim-2022-abutment-connection-mbl-survival]] — Kim 2022 · retrospective (374 implants, 5 y) — identifies the abutment **connection** (external hex 1.23 vs internal 0.72 mm at 1 year) as an MBL driver in loaded implants. Together with this review's null on flap design, it points to interface/connection handling rather than submerged-vs-nonsubmerged as the more plausible crestal bone lever.
- [[wiki/implants/mbl/dhir-2012-significance-clinical-relevance-biologic-width]] — Dhir 2012 · narrative-review · supplies the mechanism this review's null sits on top of (microgap position relative to crestal bone driving ~2 mm of apical resorption), and is the natural explanation for why two-stage submerged surgery per se does not add bone loss.

**Supersession check: no.** This review's clinical bottom line (submerged vs nonsubmerged does not meaningfully change crestal bone loss) is not overturned by any page held in this wiki; the later pooled result converges on the same clinical reading. No `superseded_by` is set.

## 7. Glossary

- **Crestal Bone Loss (CBL)**: Apical resorption of bone at the implant shoulder, measured in millimetres on periapical radiographs; the primary outcome of this review and conventionally ≈ the same construct as marginal bone loss (MBL) in the implant literature.
- **Submerged implant**: A two-stage protocol in which the implant is fully covered by mucoperiosteal flap at placement and only exposed at a second surgery after healing, so the implant-abutment interface sits away from the crestal bone during the healing period.
- **Nonsubmerged implant**: A one-stage protocol in which the implant is exposed to the oral cavity (or fitted with a healing abutment) at placement, leaving the implant-abutment interface adjacent to the crestal bone from the start.
- **Crestal vs subcrestal placement**: Implant shoulder positioned at, or apically below, the crestal bone margin — the second variable folded into this review's focused question alongside submerged vs nonsubmerged healing.
- **Platform switching**: Use of a narrower abutment than the implant platform, shifting the implant-abutment junction inward and away from the crestal bone; **explicitly excluded** from this review's evidence base, which is why its null cannot be generalized to platform-switched implants.
- **Vote count synthesis**: Reporting "how many studies found a significant difference" instead of pooling effect sizes — the format this review uses, which cannot separate a true null from an underpowered null.
