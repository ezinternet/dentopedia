## Why Ingested

Classic review challenging the assumption that keratinizing the sulcular epithelium would improve its barrier function — directly relevant to the [[overviews/oral-mucosal-epithelial-turnover-overview]] discussion of why JE remains non-keratinized and why barrier function in the sulcus is not simply a matter of cornification. Squier argues here that JE's permeability is not determined by keratinization status, and that attempts to keratinize JE could compromise epithelial attachment.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Journal of Periodontology, 1981; 4 pages) challenging the prevailing clinical hypothesis that keratinizing sulcular/junctional epithelium (JE) would strengthen its barrier against bacterial antigens — theoretical synthesis of available permeability and epithelial attachment biology, no original experimental data.

Non-keratinized epithelia can resist penetration of certain substances independently of cornification; more critically, epithelial attachment to the tooth surface is a property of undifferentiated (non-keratinized) epithelial cells — inducing keratinization would likely disrupt attachment, making the intervention biologically counterproductive.

JE's non-keratinized, permeable state is a biological necessity, not a deficiency to be corrected; this 1981 conceptual framework predates IRX1/PITX1 molecular characterization and the JE stem cell niche concept but remains foundational for understanding why JE maintains its unique undifferentiated phenotype.

## One-line Summary

Narrative review arguing that keratinization of sulcular/junctional epithelium would not necessarily confer greater impermeability and could be counterproductive because non-keratinized epithelia can also resist molecular penetration, and because JE's attachment function depends on its undifferentiated state.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Challenges the **prevailing clinical assumption** (1981) that keratinization of sulcular epithelium would improve its barrier to antigens and bacterial products
- Argues non-keratinized epithelia can also resist penetration of certain substances — barrier is not solely determined by cornification
- Points to the fundamental tension: **JE's attachment function requires an undifferentiated state** — inducing differentiation (= keratinization) could disrupt epithelial attachment to the tooth
- Implies that JE's **permeable, undifferentiated** state is biologically necessary, not a deficiency
- Historical document establishing the biological rationale for JE as a **necessary permeable barrier**, not a weak link to be strengthened

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

From abstract:
- Non-keratinized epithelia have **barrier properties** that resist penetration of certain substances — keratinization not uniquely necessary for impermeability
- Barrier properties emerge as a **concomitant of epithelial differentiation**
- Epithelial attachment to tooth is a property of **relatively undifferentiated epithelial cells**
- Keratinizing JE would likely cause **loss of epithelial attachment** — biologically counterproductive

## 7. Glossary

- **Sulcular epithelium** (열구상피): non-keratinized epithelium lining the gingival sulcus lateral wall
- **Junctional epithelium** (접합 상피, JE): epithelium sealing the base of the gingival sulcus to the tooth surface; non-keratinized; fastest-renewing oral epithelium
- **Epithelial attachment** (상피부착): the functional connection of JE to the tooth surface via hemidesmosomes and internal basal lamina
