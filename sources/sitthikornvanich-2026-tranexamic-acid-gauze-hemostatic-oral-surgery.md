## Why Ingested

기존 트라넥삼산(Tranexamic Acid, TXA) 근거는 임상 형태에 몰려 있다 — [[drug/anticoagulants/bergo-2026-topical-tranexamic-acid-powder-dentistry]]의 정제 분쇄 분말, [[drug/anticoagulants/swissdentj-2026-gelatin-sponge-tranexamic-acid-anticoagulated]]의 젤라틴 스폰지 병용, [[drug/anticoagulants/inchingolo-2024-anticoagulation-therapy-dental-practice-sr]]의 TXA 양치액 1차 요법. 이 논문은 그 문헌들이 반복해서 지적하는 **용량 편차(dosage variability) 문제**를 제조 단계에서 규격화한 ready-to-use 동결건조 거즈로 접근한 벤치 연구로, 전달 형태(delivery format) 축을 위키에 추가한다. 단, 환자 대상 임상 팔은 없다.

## Three-line Summary

Bench fabrication study (Naresuan University, Thailand) of a ready-to-use freeze-dried tranexamic acid (TXA)-impregnated cotton gauze at 2.5% and 5% (w/v), characterised by FESEM/FTIR, tensile testing, UV-vis drug release, MTT cytotoxicity on 3T3 fibroblasts, and in vitro blood clotting time (BCT) / plasma recalcification time (PRT) on citrated blood from 6 healthy volunteers.

Both TXA gauzes shortened BCT and PRT significantly vs plain gauze (p<0.05) with 2.5% statistically indistinguishable from 5%, released ~87% of loaded TXA within the first minute (burst complete by 3 min), preserved 3T3 fibroblast viability (75.2%/83.7% at 24 h vs QuikClot 48.4%), and retained hemostatic bioactivity unchanged across 180 days at both 30 °C/65% RH (ICH Zone IV) and 4 °C.

This is preclinical bench evidence only — no patient was treated, no anticoagulated subject was studied, and the authors explicitly name "the absence of in vivo validation" as a limitation; the gauze is a manufacturable prototype whose clinical efficacy in anticoagulated patients remains entirely untested.

## 세줄요약

태국 나레수안대 벤치 연구 — 동결건조로 트라넥삼산(Tranexamic Acid, TXA)을 코팅한 즉시사용(ready-to-use) 면 거즈 2.5%·5% (w/v)를 제작해 FESEM/FTIR 특성분석, 인장시험, UV-vis 약물방출, 3T3 섬유아세포 MTT 세포독성, 그리고 건강 지원자 6명의 구연산 처리 혈액을 쓴 시험관 내 혈액응고시간(Blood Clotting Time, BCT)·혈장재석회화시간(Plasma Recalcification Time, PRT)으로 평가.

두 TXA 거즈 모두 순수 거즈 대비 BCT·PRT를 유의하게 단축(p<0.05)했고 2.5%와 5% 사이엔 차이가 없었으며, 담지 TXA의 약 87%가 1분 내 방출(버스트는 3분에 종료), 3T3 세포생존율 유지(24시간 75.2%/83.7% vs QuikClot 48.4%), 30 °C/65% RH(ICH Zone IV)와 4 °C 양 조건 180일간 지혈 활성 변화 없음.

**이건 전임상 벤치 근거일 뿐이다** — 치료받은 환자도, 연구된 항응고 환자도 없고 저자들 스스로 "생체 내(in vivo) 검증 부재"를 한계로 명시한다; 제조 가능한 시제품일 뿐 항응고 환자에서의 임상 유효성은 전혀 검증되지 않았다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that a simple, factory-scalable freeze-drying process can convert ordinary medical cotton gauze into a prefabricated, dose-standardised TXA hemostatic dressing — directly targeting the dosage-variability and preparation-burden problem of chair-side TXA soaking and patient-dissolved TXA tablet mouthwashes.
- Establishes dose equivalence at the bench: 2.5% (w/v) matched 5% on hemostatic performance and biocompatibility, arguing the lower load suffices and halving drug exposure/cost.
- Provides 180-day real-time (not accelerated) stability data under ICH Zone IV hot/humid conditions — the shelf-life and transportability argument that liquid TXA formats cannot make.
- Benchmarks against QuikClot combat gauze, the only commercially available prefabricated hemostatic gauze, showing markedly better fibroblast biocompatibility (though QuikClot clotted faster via its kaolin mechanism).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Fabrication**: 2.5 mL of TXA solution at 25 mg/mL or 50 mg/mL applied to a 5 × 5 cm² 100% cotton medical gauze in a Petri dish → 5 °C for 1 h → −80 °C overnight → freeze-dried at −50 °C for 48 h. Theoretical loading 62.5 mg (2.5% gauze) and 125 mg (5% gauze) per 25 cm² piece.
- **Characterisation**: FESEM (10 kV, WD ~10.3 mm) for fiber-surface morphology; ATR/FTIR (4000–400 cm⁻¹) for chemical integrity of TXA.
- **Mechanical**: universal testing machine (Instron), 2 mm/min, ASTM D882-12, 40 mm gauge × 20 mm; Young's modulus from linear slope; dry and wet states; n=3 technical triplicate.
- **Blood source**: 6 healthy volunteers aged 18–65 (n=6 biological donors), 10 mL venous blood each, 21-G butterfly, used immediately. Excluded: anticoagulants/antiplatelets/NSAIDs/SSRIs within 10 days, pregnancy/breastfeeding, hereditary or acquired coagulation disorders. **No anticoagulated patient was studied.**
- **Fluid absorption**: 5 min immersion in PBS (pH 7.4) or citrated whole blood at 37 °C, drained at 45° for 1 min, gravimetric.
- **Drug release**: full-size gauze in 30 mL PBS (pH 7.4) at 37 ± 0.5 °C; sampling at 1, 3, 5, 10, 15, 30, 60 min with equal-volume replacement; UV-vis at 220 nm; standard curve Y = 0.1676X + 0.0361, R² = 0.9993; n=3.
- **Cytotoxicity**: MTT on 3T3 mouse fibroblasts, indirect contact (0.2 g/mL extract, 24 h at 37 °C), read at 24 h and 72 h, absorbance 540 nm; medium-only control.
- **BCT**: gauze immersed 5 min in PBS at 37 °C → 100 µL aliquot + 250 µL of 3.2% citrated whole blood, vortex 10 s, 3 min at 37 °C → 25 µL of 0.25 M CaCl₂ → tube tilted every 15 s until flow stopped.
- **PRT**: platelet-poor plasma (5000 rpm, 12 min); 250 µL PPP + 100 µL aliquot, 3 min at 37 °C → 25 µL of 0.25 M CaCl₂ → inspected every 15 s for silky fibrin thread.
- **Fibrin network**: platelet-rich plasma (100×g, 10 min, 4 °C) + CaCl₂ on sample surface, 60 min at 37 °C, 2% glutaraldehyde fixation, ethanol gradient dehydration, FESEM.
- **Stability**: packed in sterilisation roll inside opaque zip-lock bags; two conditions — 30 °C/65% RH (ICH Zone IV long-term) and 4 °C/65% RH; sampled at 0, 15, 30, 90, 180 days; BCT as the bioactivity readout.
- **Comparator**: QuikClot combat gauze (Teleflex) as positive control, chosen as the only commercial prefabricated hemostatic gauze despite its different kaolin/intrinsic-pathway mechanism.
- **Statistics**: one-way ANOVA + Tukey post-hoc, n=6 biological donors, p<0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Coating confirmed**: gross appearance unchanged; FESEM showed irregular TXA molecules accumulating on fiber surfaces, densifying with concentration. FTIR retained TXA peaks at 1532 cm⁻¹ (amide N-H), 1380 cm⁻¹ (carboxylic), 1008 cm⁻¹ (C-N), intensifying with concentration — drug functional structure not altered by lyophilisation.
- **Mechanical**: dry Young's modulus 2.71 (plain) → 2.00 (2.5%) → 2.10 MPa (5%), a significant reduction vs plain but independent of TXA concentration; wet 3.22 / 3.29 / 3.59 MPa. Authors judge this adequate, citing commercial hemostatic dressings at 0.24–0.95 MPa dry.
- **Fluid absorption**: plain gauze ~750% of its own weight; both TXA gauzes significantly lower and decreasing with TXA concentration, but the full range (500–800%) sits inside the 100–900% ideal band for medical gauze.
- **Drug release**: **~87% of the loaded TXA released within the first minute**, remaining ~13% over minutes 2–3 — burst complete by 3 min; 100% cumulative release at 60 min equilibrium, implying ~100% loading efficiency and confirming the 62.5 mg / 125 mg actual doses. 2.5% and 5% profiles were similar in shape.
- **Cytotoxicity (3T3, % viability)** — 24 h: plain 100, 2.5% TXA 75.23, 5% TXA 83.68, QuikClot 48.43. 72 h: plain 90.49, 2.5% TXA 72.82, 5% TXA 79.42, QuikClot 45.73. **Only QuikClot was significantly toxic**; TXA itself induced no notable fibroblast toxicity.
- **BCT and PRT**: both TXA gauze groups significantly shorter than plain gauze (p<0.05); **2.5% vs 5% showed no significant difference**; QuikClot significantly shorter than all others (p<0.05), consistent with its kaolin-driven intrinsic-pathway activation. Concordant BCT/PRT results indicate the effect spans both the overall cascade and the intrinsic pathway.
- **Fibrin architecture (FESEM)**: both TXA gauzes produced visibly denser fibrin networks with barely visible inter-fibril gaps and embedded red blood cells and platelets, vs the loose, wide-gapped networks on plain gauze *and* on QuikClot — the structural correlate of TXA's antifibrinolytic clot stabilisation.
- **Stability over 180 days**: BCT across all groups, timepoints, and both storage conditions ranged **6.25–8.75 min with no significant difference from day-0 freshly prepared gauze**, at both 30 °C/65% RH and 4 °C/65% RH. Hemostatic bioactivity therefore survives six months anywhere in the 4–30 °C range.
- **Headline engineering conclusion**: 2.5% (w/v) is sufficient — it matches 5% on physicochemical structure, biocompatibility, and hemostatic efficiency, so the lower dose maximises cost-effectiveness and minimises drug exposure.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No in vivo validation** — the authors state this first and explicitly. Planned next steps: rat tail amputation model (bleeding time, total blood loss), then rodent intraoral wound and porcine dental extraction models to introduce salivary flow and mechanical forces, **before** any clinical trial in patients on oral anticoagulants.
- **No clinical arm whatsoever**; the conclusion's framing as "promising… especially for patients on anticoagulant therapy" is an aspiration extrapolated from bench data, not a demonstrated clinical outcome. Blood donors were explicitly screened to *exclude* anticoagulant and antiplatelet users.
- **Single non-oral cell line**: 3T3 mouse fibroblasts only; the authors call for human gingival fibroblasts and oral keratinocytes to model the oral mucosa.
- **Sterilisation unresolved**: samples were only UV-treated for the cell assay; gamma irradiation, ethylene oxide, and autoclaving could degrade both the cotton fiber structure and TXA chemistry. Sterility assurance requires separate study.
- **Burst-only kinetics**: release is essentially complete in 3 min because the drug sits on fiber surfaces. The authors note a theoretically ideal material would sustain release ~3 h to protect against delayed fibrinolysis; a second-generation burst-plus-sustained design is left to future work.
- **Stability data are laboratory-scale and 180 days only** — commercial registration would need certified stability studies over broader regulatory conditions.
- **Comparator mismatch**: QuikClot works by a different (kaolin, intrinsic-pathway) mechanism, so it is a functional benchmark rather than a mechanistic control. The authors note that a direct comparison against freshly chair-side-impregnated TXA gauze — the actual incumbent practice — is still missing and is needed to prove lyophilisation preserves bioactivity.
- Small donor pool (n=6) and technical triplicates for material assays.
- Cost-effectiveness and large-scale manufacturing viability remain to be assessed.

## 6. Related Work

- Wang et al. — topical TXA at 100 mg/mL for 10 min did not significantly affect human fibroblast viability or proliferation; >20 mg/mL with extended exposure may be cytotoxic to tendon cells and osteoblasts.
- El Halawany et al. — alginate/nano-hydroxyapatite composite aerogel loaded with TXA; protection against alveolar osteitis and enhanced bone formation in animal models. The present authors position their cotton-gauze approach as the low-cost, clinically familiar alternative to such "high-tech" scaffolds.
- Physically crosslinked trilaminate dressings and TXA scaffolds — other advanced TXA delivery systems, limited by production cost and application complexity.
- Prior literature on gelatin sponges (Gelfoam, Surgifoam/Spongostan), oxidized regenerated cellulose (Surgicel), and collagen plugs (Colla-Plug), with documented adverse effects from retained material: allergic reaction, adjacent-tissue inflammation, delayed healing, abscess formation, interference with bone regeneration, increased postoperative pain.
- ICH stability guideline for Zone IV hot/humid climates — the framework used for the 30 °C/65% RH long-term condition.

## 7. Glossary

- **TXA (Tranexamic Acid)**: synthetic lysine derivative, antifibrinolytic; blocks the lysine-binding sites of plasminogen, inhibiting plasminogen activation and fibrinolysis, thereby stabilising an already-formed clot rather than accelerating its formation.
- **BCT (Blood Clotting Time)**: in vitro whole-blood coagulation assay; recalcified citrated blood is tilted at fixed intervals until flow ceases. Reflects both cellular and plasma components, i.e. the overall cascade.
- **PRT (Plasma Recalcification Time)**: time for fibrin thread formation after adding Ca²⁺ back to citrated platelet-poor plasma; isolates plasma factor activity (intrinsic pathway) by excluding blood cells and platelets.
- **FESEM (Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscopy)**: high-resolution electron microscopy used here for fiber-surface coating and fibrin network architecture.
- **ATR/FTIR (Attenuated Total Reflectance / Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy)**: identifies chemical functional groups; used to confirm TXA was incorporated without structural degradation.
- **MTT assay**: colorimetric cell-viability test based on mitochondrial reduction of tetrazolium; here on 3T3 mouse fibroblasts by indirect extract contact.
- **Freeze-drying (lyophilisation)**: freezing then sublimating solvent under vacuum, depositing the dissolved drug onto the substrate — here the mechanism by which TXA is fixed onto cotton fibers.
- **ICH Zone IV**: International Conference on Harmonisation climatic zone for hot/humid regions; long-term storage testing condition of 30 °C/65% RH.
- **Burst release**: rapid initial liberation of drug, here ~87% within 1 minute — desirable for hemostats, which must act within the seconds-to-minutes early hemostasis window, unlike most sustained-release systems.
- **QuikClot combat gauze**: commercial kaolin-impregnated hemostatic gauze activating factor XII / the intrinsic pathway; used here as the only available prefabricated-gauze benchmark despite known cytotoxicity to oral soft tissue.
- **PPP / PRP (Platelet-Poor / Platelet-Rich Plasma)**: centrifugation fractions used for PRT and fibrin network imaging respectively.
