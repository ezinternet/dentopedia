## Why Ingested

Aβ(A-beta)·Aδ(A-delta)·C 섬유와 하치조신경(Inferior Alveolar Nerve, IAN) 관련 논문 요청에 따라 PubMed에서 탐색. 본 논문은 IAN/설신경(Lingual Nerve, LN) 손상 후 전기생리학적 평가(피부 무반응기, Cutaneous Silent Period, CSP)에서 통증성 자극이 Aδ·C 섬유를, 무통성 자극이 Aβ 섬유를 각각 활성화한다는 섬유 유형별 자극 프로토콜을 명시적으로 채택·보고한 임상연구로, 기존 [[oral-surgery/lee-2015-inferior-alveolar-nerve-injury-management]] (스테로이드 증례)와 [[overviews/trigeminal-injury-neuropathic-pain-cascade-overview]] (말초-중추 캐스케이드 종합)에 신경손상 후 비침습적 치료(LLLT/TENS) 옵션과 섬유 특이적 전기생리 평가라는 새 축을 추가한다.

## Three-line Summary

Exploratory non-randomized clinical study (n=27; 18 inferior alveolar, 9 lingual nerve injuries after mandibular third-molar surgery) comparing low-level laser therapy (LLLT), TENS, and placebo, using VAS symptom scores and fiber-type-specific cutaneous silent period (CSP) electromyography (painful stimuli → Aδ/C fibers; painless → Aβ fibers).

LLLT produced significantly greater clinical improvement (pain, burning, speech, biting, taste) than TENS or placebo, while CSP electrophysiological recovery remained statistically non-significant across groups.

LLLT is a promising non-invasive option for neurosensory recovery after trigeminal nerve injury, but the design is exploratory and non-randomized with a small cohort, and the clinical–electrophysiological dissociation limits mechanistic conclusions.

## 세줄요약

비무작위 탐색 임상연구(n=27; 하악 제3대구치 발치 후 하치조신경 18·설신경 9 손상) — 저출력 레이저치료(LLLT)·경피신경전기자극(TENS)·위약을 VAS 증상점수와 섬유 유형별 피부 무반응기(CSP) 근전도(통증성 자극 → Aδ·C 섬유, 무통성 → Aβ 섬유)로 비교.

LLLT가 TENS·위약보다 유의하게 우수한 임상적 회복(통증·화끈거림·발음·물림·미각)을 보였으나, CSP 전기생리학적 회복은 군 간 통계적으로 유의하지 않았음.

LLLT는 삼차신경 손상 후 감각회복의 유망한 비침습 옵션이나, 설계가 소규모·비무작위 탐색적이고 임상–전기생리 해리가 있어 기전적 결론은 제한됨.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Directly documents a fiber-type-specific stimulation protocol for trigeminal electrophysiological testing: painful stimuli → Aδ and C fibers; painless stimuli → Aβ fibers — used to interpret cutaneous silent period (CSP) responses after IAN/LN injury.
- Head-to-head comparison of two non-invasive neurosensory-recovery modalities (LLLT vs TENS) in a real clinical cohort of post-third-molar-surgery nerve injury, rather than the single-arm case reports that dominate this niche.
- Reports a nerve-type interaction: LLLT produced greater symptomatic improvement in lingual nerve injuries than in inferior alveolar nerve injuries, a subgroup signal not previously quantified in the wiki's IAN literature.
- Distinguishes clinical (VAS symptom) recovery from electrophysiological (CSP) recovery — the two did not move together, which is a methodologically important dissociation for future IAN-injury outcome studies.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: prospective, non-randomized, pragmatic-allocation clinical study (not RCT); IRB-approved.
- Population: 27 patients with persistent postoperative IAN (n=18) or LN (n=9) paresthesia after impacted mandibular third molar surgery, no systemic disease, no spontaneous recovery during follow-up.
- Allocation (clinically driven, not randomized): IAN — 6 LLLT, 10 TENS (5 of these later received adjunct LLLT), 2 placebo. LN — 6 LLLT, 3 placebo.
- LLLT: GaAlAs 830 nm continuous-wave diode laser, 100 mW, 40 s/point (4 J/point, 4–10 J/cm²), 3 anatomical points along the injured nerve, 5x/week for 4 weeks (20 sessions), started after resolution of postoperative edema/trismus.
- TENS: 50–100 Hz, 40–75 μs pulse duration, 10–30 mA, biphasic rectangular waveform, 2 extraoral points x 5 min, 5x/week for 4 weeks (20 sessions).
- Electrophysiology: 4-channel EMG (Nicolet Viking-IV), monopolar recording from orbicularis oculi (bilateral), stimulation at supraorbital/mental foramen and intraoral retromolar trigone; cutaneous silent period (SP1+SP2 total duration) measured for masseter and mylohyoid muscles under painful (Aδ/C-fiber) and painless (Aβ-fiber) stimulus conditions, pre- and post-treatment.
- Outcome measures: composite total VAS score (sum of per-symptom 0–10 ratings) as primary clinical outcome; individual symptom VAS scores as secondary/exploratory; CSP duration as electrophysiological outcome.
- Statistics: Wilcoxon signed-rank (within-group pre/post) and Mann-Whitney U (between-group, subgroup) tests; Holm-Bonferroni correction for secondary individual-symptom comparisons; primary outcome and CSP not multiplicity-corrected.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- LLLT group: statistically significant pre-post improvement in numbness, pain, stabbing, electric shock, tension, stiffness, crawling, tingling, burning, speech difficulty, accidental biting, taste disturbance (p<0.05); no significant change in itching, tickling, salivation, vibration, warm/cold sensation.
- TENS group: significant improvement only in numbness, tension, crawling, cold sensation, tingling, burning, speech difficulty, taste disturbance — narrower symptom coverage than LLLT.
- LLLT vs TENS (between-group): significantly greater improvement with LLLT in tension, stiffness, crawling, cold sensation, speech difficulty, salivation, accidental biting.
- Nerve-type subgroup (LLLT-treated): LN patients had higher baseline severity and more residual symptoms (biting, crawling, speech difficulty, taste disturbance) than IAN patients even post-treatment, but showed significant improvement in biting, speech difficulty, taste disturbance; IAN patients improved in biting, crawling sensation, speech difficulty.
- Placebo group: no significant pre-post change in any parameter.
- CSP: healthy side significantly longer than pathological side for masseter/mylohyoid across nearly all painful and painless tasks at baseline (p<0.05, one exception p=0.066). Post-treatment, masseter CSP showed no significant change on the pathological side; mylohyoid CSP significantly prolonged only for painful soft bite, painful maximal force, and painless maximal force tasks (p<0.05) — i.e., electrophysiological recovery was partial/muscle-specific and not statistically robust overall.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Non-randomized, clinically-driven (not random) treatment allocation → small, uneven group sizes (as few as n=2 placebo-IAN) and unadjusted baseline comparability.
- Crossover contamination: 5 TENS patients later received adjunct LLLT, complicating clean TENS-vs-LLLT attribution.
- Single, relatively short post-treatment follow-up timepoint — delayed recovery (especially CSP) may be uncaptured.
- Composite VAS score is not a validated neurosensory instrument; unblinded design risks expectation bias.
- CSP assessment is exploratory (reflects inhibitory reflex integrity, not direct proof of nerve regeneration); authors explicitly note CSP results are not confirmatory of the clinical improvement.
- Small overall sample (n=27) limits power, especially for nerve-type subgroup analysis.
- Authors flag a discrepant prior report (Miloro et al.) finding no LLLT-vs-placebo difference — attributed to differing laser parameters/timing/design, underscoring the field's protocol heterogeneity.

## 6. Related Work

- Miloro et al. — cited as a contrasting study finding no significant LLLT-vs-placebo difference after IAN/LN injury.
- General photobiomodulation mechanism literature (mitochondrial ATP enhancement, anti-inflammatory modulation, axonal repair without thermal damage) cited to support LLLT's biological plausibility.

## 7. Glossary

- **IAN**: Inferior Alveolar Nerve (하치조신경)
- **LN**: Lingual Nerve (설신경)
- **LLLT**: Low-Level Laser Therapy / photobiomodulation (저출력 레이저치료)
- **TENS**: Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation (경피신경전기자극)
- **CSP**: Cutaneous Silent Period — EMG-measured inhibitory reflex silent interval following sensory nerve stimulation, used to probe trigeminal afferent pathway integrity
- **VAS**: Visual Analog Scale (0–10 symptom severity rating)
- **Aβ (A-beta) fiber**: large-diameter, fast-conducting myelinated fiber; mediates non-painful tactile/proprioceptive sensation (activated here by painless CSP stimuli)
- **Aδ (A-delta) fiber**: thin myelinated fiber; mediates fast, sharp pain (activated here, with C fibers, by painful CSP stimuli)
- **C fiber**: unmyelinated fiber; mediates slow, dull/burning pain and thermal sensation (activated here, with Aδ fibers, by painful CSP stimuli)
