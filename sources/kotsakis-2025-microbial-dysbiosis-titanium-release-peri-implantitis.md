## Why Ingested

기존 [[wiki/implants/peri-implantitis/smeets-2014-peri-implantitis-definition-etiology-review]]는 peri-implantitis의 정의·역학·CIST 치료 프레임을 다루지만 임플란트 특이적 병인 기전(왜 항균 치료 반응률이 periodontitis보다 낮은가)은 다루지 않는다. 본 Critical Review(Kotsakis & Ganesan, JDR 2025)는 이 공백을 메우는 최신 기전 종설로, 티타늄 임플란트 부식산물(i-TiPs)이 숙주-미생물-생체재료(H-M-B) 삼각구도를 통해 dysbiosis를 유발한다는 abiotic exposome 가설을 정리한다. 논문 본문이 dysbiosis 개념 정의에 [[wiki/oral-microbiology/hajishengallis-2012-psd-model-periodontal-disease]]를 직접 인용하므로, 두 페이지 모두와 연결한다.

## Three-line Summary

Invited critical/narrative review synthesizing human, animal, and in vitro evidence on titanium implant degradation products (implant-derived titanium particles, i-TiPs) as a co-etiologic "abiotic exposome" in peri-implantitis, alongside the microbial biofilm.

i-TiPs are detected in 75–100% of peri-implantitis cases (rare in health); titanium concentration inversely correlates with microbiome alpha-diversity and drives a Veillonella parvula-enriched dysbiotic shift, while activating inflammasome, TLR, and C3-complement pathways independent of bacterial LPS.

Because titanium particles constitute an abiotic danger signal that antimicrobial monotherapy cannot address, and because abrasive mechanical cleaning (e.g., titanium brushes) paradoxically increases particle release, the authors argue future peri-implantitis therapy needs host-modulatory strategies (e.g., complement inhibitor AMY-101) targeting titanium-mediated inflammation, not biofilm control alone.

## 세줄요약

초청 Critical Review로, 임플란트 티타늄 부식산물(implant-derived titanium particles, i-TiPs)이 미생물 생체막과 함께 peri-implantitis의 공동 병인("abiotic exposome")으로 작용한다는 근거를 인간·동물·in vitro 연구를 종합해 정리했다.

i-TiPs는 peri-implantitis 병소의 75~100%에서 검출되며(건강 부위에서는 드묾), 티타늄 농도는 미생물총 알파다양성과 역상관 관계를 보이고 Veillonella parvula가 우세해지는 dysbiosis 전환을 유도하며, 세균 LPS와 무관하게 inflammasome·TLR·C3 보체 경로를 활성화한다.

티타늄 입자는 항생제 단독요법으로 제거할 수 없는 abiotic danger signal이며, 티타늄 브러시 등 연마성 기계적 세척이 오히려 입자 방출을 증가시키므로, 저자들은 생체막 관리만이 아니라 티타늄 매개 염증 경로(예: 보체억제제 AMY-101)를 표적하는 host-modulatory 전략이 필요하다고 주장한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Frames peri-implantitis pathogenesis as a triangle of reciprocal host-microbiome-biomaterial (H-M-B) interactions, distinct from the purely plaque-centric model of periodontitis.
- Synthesizes the "titanium particle exposome" hypothesis: implant-derived titanium particles (i-TiPs), released via biocorrosion, tribocorrosion, or iatrogenic corrosion, act as an abiotic danger signal that activates inflammasome, TLR, and complement pathways independent of bacterial LPS.
- Traces the microbiome-sequencing literature (culture-based → 16S pyrosequencing → 16S Illumina MiSeq → whole-genome/strain-level metagenomics → meta-transcriptomics) showing a consistent, implant-specific dysbiotic signature distinct from periodontitis.
- Explains why peri-implantitis is resistant to antimicrobial-only therapy (<50% disease resolution) while single-rooted-tooth periodontitis responds well to mechanical/antibiotic treatment — because titanium particles constitute a persistent abiotic trigger that antimicrobials cannot remove.
- Highlights the paradox that abrasive mechanical implant-surface cleaning (titanium brushes, some ultrasonic instrumentation) increases titanium particle release and perpetuates inflammation, whereas non-abrasive nylon-brush cleaning does not.
- Proposes host-modulatory therapy (e.g., the C3 complement inhibitor AMY-101) as the logical future direction, rather than antimicrobial monotherapy.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative/critical review (not a systematic review or meta-analysis; no PRISMA methodology, no formal search strategy or risk-of-bias assessment reported). The authors synthesize:
- Cross-sectional human studies quantifying titanium particles in peri-implant plaque/tissue (exfoliative cytology, spatially resolved immunohistochemistry, ICP-MS/dissolved titanium assays).
- In vitro corrosion/tribocorrosion studies of titanium implant surfaces under simulated inflammatory/microbial conditions.
- Orthopedic literature on titanium-wear-particle-induced osteolysis (inflammasome activation, osteoclast activation) as mechanistic precedent.
- Microbiome-sequencing studies spanning four technological eras: pyrosequencing (Kumar et al. 2012), 16S Illumina MiSeq (Sanz-Martin et al. 2017), whole-genome/strain-level metagenomics (Ghensi et al. 2020; Song et al. 2024; Bazzani et al. 2024), and meta-transcriptomics/host-microbe correlation (Ganesan et al. 2022).
- One randomized clinical trial (Daubert et al. 2023) comparing abrasive vs. non-abrasive implant-cleaning methods and their effect on titanium release.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- i-TiPs detection frequency in peri-implantitis tissue/plaque: 75–100% depending on assay (rarely present in health).
- Daubert et al. 2018: titanium concentration in peri-implant plaque inversely correlated with microbiome alpha-diversity; disease samples showed titanium-concentration-dependent enrichment of *Veillonella parvula*; titanium concentration significantly separated healthy vs. diseased community structure by beta-diversity ordination.
- Ganesan et al. 2022 (meta-transcriptomics): 4- to 200-fold upregulation of microbial genes for biofilm thickness, heme transport/utilization, and gram-negative cell membrane synthesis in peri-implantitis, correlated with host genes for mucosal zinc-finger proteins, apoptosis, membrane transport, inflammation, and cell-cell communication.
- Daubert et al. 2023 RCT: mechanical contact abrasive implant cleaning significantly increased titanium release in peri-implant plaque at 8 weeks post-treatment; non-abrasive nylon-brush cleaning did not.
- Disease resolution rates for standard antimicrobial/mechanical debridement of peri-implantitis: largely <50% (Garaicoa-Pazmino et al. 2023), contrasted with high success rates for scaling/root planing ± antibiotics in single-rooted-tooth periodontitis (López et al. 2000).
- Shared upregulated pathways in peri-implantitis transcriptome: endosomal-lysosomal, inflammasome, and C3 complement pathways (Martin et al. 2022; Pettersson et al. 2017; Ganesan et al. 2022).
- Titanium microparticle size range in peri-implant tissue: submicron scale up to ~10 µm, sufficient for cellular internalization and endosomal-lysosomal activation.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative/critical review — not systematic; selection of cited studies is author-curated, no formal quality appraisal.
- Causality/temporality unresolved: the authors explicitly state available data cannot determine whether biomaterial breakdown/titanium release precedes, coincides with, or follows the dysbiotic microbial shift.
- Heterogeneity across the microbiome literature (disease case definitions, sampling strategies, sequencing platforms/regions, reference databases) limits direct comparability between studies cited.
- Zirconia implant particle release/dysbiosis is acknowledged as an emerging parallel phenomenon but explicitly excluded from this review's scope (titanium only).
- Host-modulatory therapy (AMY-101 complement inhibitor) is discussed as a promising direction but has only been tested for gingival inflammation, not yet peri-implantitis-specific clinical outcomes.
- Standardization of microbial sampling/sequencing methodology and further elucidation of host-microbial-biomaterial crosstalk are called out as prerequisites for developing targeted interventions.

## 6. Related Work

- Berglundh et al. 2018 (2017 World Workshop consensus) — defines peri-implantitis as plaque-associated but the current review challenges "plaque-associated" as insufficient given resistance to plaque-focused treatment.
- Herrera et al. 2023 (EFP S3 guideline) — recommends antimicrobial mechanical debridement as primary peri-implantitis therapy; this review argues its <50% success rate reflects the unaddressed titanium exposome.
- Hajishengallis and Lamont 2012 — source of the "dysbiosis" definition (shift from health-associated to disease-associated microbial community) applied throughout this review; corresponds to [[wiki/oral-microbiology/hajishengallis-2012-psd-model-periodontal-disease]] in this wiki.
- Albrektsson et al. 2023 — osteoimmune regulation framework underlying the host-microbiome-biomaterial (H-M-B) triangle model used throughout.
- Kotsakis and Olmedo 2021 (Periodontol 2000) — prior foundational statement that "peri-implantitis is not periodontitis," cited as the conceptual predecessor to this review.
- Daubert et al. 2018, 2023 — the key primary studies (titanium-stratified microbiome analysis; RCT on abrasive vs. non-abrasive cleaning) underpinning this review's central claims.

## 7. Glossary

- **i-TiPs (implant-derived titanium particles)**: Microscopic titanium particles (submicron to ~10 µm) released from a dental implant's surface via biocorrosion, tribocorrosion, or mechanical abrasion, found in peri-implant plaque and tissue.
- **Exposome**: The totality of environmental (non-genetic, non-purely-microbial) factors influencing disease progression or severity; here, titanium degradation products are framed as an "abiotic exposome."
- **Host-microbiome-biomaterial (H-M-B) triangle**: Conceptual model in which peri-implant health/disease is determined by reciprocal interactions among host immunity, the peri-implant microbiome, and the implant biomaterial itself — distinguishing peri-implantitis pathogenesis from periodontitis.
- **Biocorrosion / tribocorrosion / iatrogenic corrosion**: Three mechanisms of titanium implant surface breakdown — biocorrosion (biological-fluid-driven degradation), tribocorrosion (combined corrosion + mechanical wear, e.g., at the implant-abutment microgap), iatrogenic corrosion (clinician-caused, e.g., abrasive instrumentation).
- **Dysbiosis**: Shift of a microbial community from a health-associated composition toward a disease-associated one (definition per Hajishengallis and Lamont 2012).
- **Inflammasome**: Multiprotein innate-immune signaling complex responsible for IL-1β release; activated by titanium particles independent of bacterial LPS, as well as by periodontal pathogens.
- **C3 complement pathway / AMY-101**: Complement cascade component implicated in peri-implant inflammation; AMY-101 is an experimental C3-targeted complement inhibitor discussed as a candidate host-modulatory therapy.
- **Repassivation**: The capacity of titanium's native oxide layer to reform/self-heal after minor surface disruption, limiting particle release unless overwhelmed by anaerobic inflammatory conditions or repeated abrasion.
