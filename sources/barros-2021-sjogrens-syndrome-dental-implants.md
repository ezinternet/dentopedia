## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (AMSTAR-2 appraisal of systematic reviews; search to October 2020; 4 included SRs pooling 722 implants in 189 Sjögren's syndrome patients) evaluating the methodological quality of evidence on dental implants in Sjögren's syndrome.

Pooled implant success rate across the 4 SRs was 95.22% (follow-up 45.2 ± 23.8 to 125.5 months), but AMSTAR-2 rated 3 of the 4 SRs as critically low quality and 1 as low quality.

Dental implants appear clinically favorable in Sjögren's syndrome, but the underlying systematic-review evidence base is methodologically weak, so the reassuring success-rate figures should be interpreted with caution pending better-designed primary and secondary studies.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First (per authors) umbrella review to formally appraise the methodological quality of existing SRs on dental implants in Sjögren's syndrome patients using AMSTAR-2.
- Quantifies the aggregate evidence: 4 SRs, 722 implants, 189 patients, 95.22% success rate, follow-up 45.2 ± 23.8 to 125.5 months.
- Surfaces a critical-appraisal gap: despite favorable outcome numbers, 3/4 SRs were critically low quality and 1/4 low quality by AMSTAR-2 — none rated moderate or high.
- Reframes the clinical question from "do implants succeed in SS?" (yes, apparently) to "how much should we trust that yes?" (not much, methodologically).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: umbrella review (systematic review of systematic reviews), methodological quality appraisal only — abstract does not describe a formal outcome meta-analysis, only quality grading of the 4 included SRs via AMSTAR-2.
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, LILACS, Science Direct, DARE/Cochrane, searched to October 2020.
- **Screening**: 833 articles initially identified → inclusion/exclusion criteria applied → 4 SRs retained for detailed analysis.
- **Quality appraisal tool**: AMSTAR 2 (A MeaSurement Tool to Assess systematic Reviews, version 2).
- Abstract does not specify number of independent reviewers, risk-of-bias tool details beyond AMSTAR-2, or PROSPERO registration — these details are not recoverable without full text.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Included evidence**: 4 systematic reviews, comprising 722 implants placed in 189 Sjögren's syndrome patients.
- **Follow-up**: minimum 45.2 ± 23.8 months, maximum 125.5 months.
- **Success rate**: 95.22% pooled across the 4 SRs.
- **AMSTAR-2 quality grading**: 3 of 4 SRs critically low quality; 1 of 4 SRs low quality. No SR achieved moderate or high quality.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Abstract-only source (no PMC full text available) — Methods detail (reviewer count, specific AMSTAR-2 domain failures, PROSPERO registration, exact 4 SRs included) cannot be extracted from the abstract alone; if deeper methodological detail is needed later, the Wiley PDF would need to be obtained.
- Only 4 SRs met inclusion criteria out of 833 initially identified articles — a small evidence base for an umbrella review.
- The authors themselves flag that all included SRs were low or critically-low quality by AMSTAR-2, meaning the reassuring 95.22% success figure rests on a weak methodological foundation (this is the paper's central message, not a shortcoming of the umbrella review itself).
- No RCTs are implied among the underlying primary studies (consistent with [[implants/almeida-2017-dental-implants-sjogren-syndrome-sr]], which found none among its 6 included studies).

## 7. Glossary

- **Umbrella review**: a systematic review whose unit of analysis is other systematic reviews, typically used to summarize and appraise the overall evidence landscape on a topic.
- **AMSTAR-2**: A MeaSurement Tool to Assess systematic Reviews, version 2 — a 16-item critical-appraisal instrument yielding an overall confidence rating (high / moderate / low / critically low) for a systematic review's methodological quality.
- **Sjögren's syndrome (SS)**: chronic autoimmune exocrinopathy causing lymphocytic infiltration of salivary and lacrimal glands, producing xerostomia (dry mouth) and xerophthalmia (dry eyes); relevant to implants because oral dryness alters the peri-implant soft-tissue and biofilm environment.
- **DARE**: Database of Abstracts of Reviews of Effects, a Cochrane-affiliated database of systematic review abstracts (now archived/discontinued but historically used for SR searches).
