## Why Ingested

Covers mechanisms and clinical evidence for methylcobalamin/cyanocobalamin/hydroxycobalamin (vitamin B12) in neuropathic pain and nerve regeneration — supports B12 use as adjunct for iatrogenic inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) paresthesia after third molar extraction or implant surgery. Supplies the pharmacological mechanism behind the medical-adjunct arm of [[overviews/ian-nerve-injury-management-overview]], where B12 is listed as an adjunct without a mechanistic anchor, and complements the treatment-efficacy evidence in [[oral-surgery/keykha-2024-iatrogenic-paresthesia-mandibular-treatment-sr]].

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of PubMed animal studies and clinical trials using pharmacological doses (>100 µg) of vitamin B12 for pain treatment.

Multiple animal studies confirm nerve regeneration (axonal growth, Schwann cell differentiation, BDNF upregulation), COX enzyme inhibition, TRPV1 receptor modulation, and synergy with NSAIDs and opioids; clinical RCTs show efficacy for low back pain (pain −87%, disability −82% with IM cyanocobalamin 1000 µg/day × 2 wk) and neuralgia.

Optimal dosing and regimen remain undefined; more large placebo-controlled trials needed, but favorable safety profile supports adjunct use.

## 세줄요약

비타민 B12(메틸코발라민/시아노코발라민)의 통증 치료 효과를 동물실험 및 임상시험 문헌을 정리한 서사적 고찰.

동물실험에서 신경재생(축삭성장·슈반세포 분화·BDNF 상향), COX 억제, TRPV1 조절, NSAIDs·아편유사제와의 상승작용 확인; 임상 RCT에서 만성요통(통증 −87%), 신경통(VAS −60%), 당뇨병성 신경병증에서 유의한 개선 보고.

최적 용량·투여경로·기간 미확립; 대규모 이중맹검 RCT 부족하나 안전성 우수해 보조치료로 고려 가능.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First compiled narrative synthesis of B12 mechanisms (nerve regeneration, COX inhibition, TRPV1 modulation, neurotransmitter potentiation) and clinical trial data across multiple pain conditions
- Highlights synergistic potential of B12 with NSAIDs (diclofenac) and opioids (morphine tolerance reduction)
- Contextualizes B12 as non-opioid adjunct in the context of the opioid epidemic
- Reviews dental-adjacent indication: aphthous stomatitis (sublingual B12 1000 µg/day × 6 months → pain duration −68%, number of outbreaks −72%)

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- PubMed narrative review; no systematic search protocol or PRISMA; no risk-of-bias assessment
- Included: primary animal mechanism studies + clinical trials with pharmacological doses (>100 µg)
- Excluded: dietary supplementation studies, non-pain B12 uses
- Forms of B12 reviewed: cyanocobalamin (oral/IM), methylcobalamin (oral/IM/injection), hydroxycobalamin (oral)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Animal Studies — Mechanisms
| Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Nerve regeneration | Axonal growth + Schwann cell differentiation in nerve crush models; ↑ BDNF; ↑ nerve conduction velocity |
| COX inhibition | B12 deficiency → COX2 upregulation; B12 reduces 2nd phase of formalin-induced orofacial pain similarly to NSAIDs; central + peripheral COX effects in hot-plate/writhing models |
| TRPV1 modulation | Methylcobalamin reduces heat hyperalgesia via ↓ TRPV1 ion influx in mice |
| Neurotransmitter potentiation | B1+B6+B12 → ↑ noradrenaline/5-HT pain inhibitory response; ↓ homocysteine → restores neurotransmitter synthesis |
| Opioid synergy | B12 + morphine → significant ↓ morphine tolerance and dependence; B1+B6+B12 + morphine > morphine alone |

### Clinical Trials
| Condition | Regimen | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Chronic low back pain (RCT) | IM cyanocobalamin 1000 µg/day × 2 wk | Pain −87%, disability −82% |
| Chronic low back pain (RCT) | IM methylcobalamin 500 µg 3×/wk × 2 wk | Pain −31%, disability −27% |
| Low back pain + diclofenac | Oral B-complex incl. B12 | Synergistic: improved function or reduced diclofenac dose |
| Compressive neuralgia (RCT) | Oral hydroxycobalamin 2 mg 3×/day | VAS −60%; +nucleotides → −69% |
| Herpetic neuralgia (RCT) | IM methylcobalamin 500 µg/day | 60% of subjects ≥50% pain reduction |
| Chemotherapy-induced neuralgia | IM methylcobalamin 500 µg q.o.d. × 10 doses, then oral 500 µg 3×/day × 2 mo | Pain −23% |
| Diabetic neuropathy (RCT DBPC) | Oral methylcobalamin 1500 µg/day × 3 mo | Pain −70%; improved 2-point discrimination, nerve conduction |
| Aphthous stomatitis (RCT) | Sublingual cyanocobalamin 1000 µg/day × 6 mo | Pain duration −68%, number of outbreaks −72%, pain-free 74.1% vs 32% placebo |
| Morton's neuralgia (case series) | Weekly IM B12 × interspaces | 73% of 85 patients resolved |

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No systematic review protocol; risk of selection bias
- Many included clinical trials lack placebo arm or have small n
- Optimal B12 form (cyanocobalamin vs methylcobalamin vs hydroxycobalamin), dose, route, and duration undefined
- Mechanism data largely from animal models; human mechanistic data sparse
- No RCT data on IAN/dental nerve injury specifically

## 6. Related Work

- Methylcobalamin PLCL conduit study (peripheral nerve regeneration) — extends the regeneration mechanism to a dental/surgical context
- B-vitamin combinations for neuralgia and low back pain (multiple Japanese/Chinese RCTs cited within)

## 7. Glossary

- **Methylcobalamin**: Active methyl-donor form of vitamin B12; most studied for neurological uses
- **Cyanocobalamin**: Synthetic form of B12; most stable but must be converted to active forms in body
- **Hydroxycobalamin**: Hydroxyl-bound form; also active; sometimes preferred for IV/IM
- **BDNF**: Brain-derived neurotrophic factor — promotes nerve growth and repair
- **COX**: Cyclooxygenase — enzyme mediating prostaglandin/pain signaling; inhibited by NSAIDs
- **TRPV1**: Transient receptor potential vanilloid 1 — capsaicin/heat pain receptor
- **SAMe**: S-adenosyl methionine — major methyl donor in body, produced via B12-dependent methylation
