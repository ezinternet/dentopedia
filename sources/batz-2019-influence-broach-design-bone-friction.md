## Why Ingested

Ingested to put a **measurement** under a claim the wiki has so far carried only on patent prose: that **face geometry — not working direction — decides whether an instrument compacts bone or cuts it**. [[wiki/implants/huwais-2017-autografting-tool-enhanced-flute-profile]] (the Densah/Versah PCT patent) asserts a dual-face flute in which a negative-rake "densifying face" burnishes and a "cutting face" slices, but it contains no data — all effects are inventor assertions. Bätz holds the working direction fixed at *extraction* and varies only sharpness, finding that **blunt extraction densified +117% over sharp extraction (P = .005)** — i.e. an instrument travelling in the direction that "should" cut still densifies if its geometry is blunt. That reframes the direction-vs-geometry reading of [[wiki/implants/huwais-2017-novel-osseous-densification-osteotomy-primary-stability]], whose CW=extract / CCW=densify result is equally well explained by rotation *selecting which face engages* rather than by direction being causal in itself. It is also the first held quantitative support for the mechanistic premise of [[wiki/implants/versah-protocols/rittipakorn-2025-clockwise-osseodensification-primary-stability-cadaveric]], which ran a densifying bur clockwise (cutting direction) on the hypothesis that its non-sharp geometry compacts anyway, but could only show densification qualitatively on SR-µCT.

Second reason: it is the **only held paper that quantifies densification *depth* as a parameter separable from densification *magnitude*** (higher depth for compaction, P = .001; higher maximum densification for blunt extraction, P = .008). Depth is the nearest existing proxy for the lateral-vs-apical distribution question raised by the HaeNaem condensing-screw patent [[wiki/implants/kim-2019-double-spiral-condensing-screw-implant]] — though Bätz tests no dental instrument and nothing about that patent is validated here.

Cross-specialty ingest follows the precedent of [[wiki/implants/timon-2019-thermal-osteonecrosis-bone-drilling-orthopedic]] (orthopaedic bone-drilling review held in `implants/`). **Transfer limits are severe and stated throughout**: broaches are impacted axially, not rotated; the loading is quasi-static, not 800–1500 rpm; and bovine trabecular cuboids are not jaw bone. The mechanism is suggestive, not transferable as a clinical number.

## Three-line Summary

Quasi-static bench experiment on bovine trabecular bone cuboids comparing three total hip arthroplasty (THA) broach designs — compaction, blunt extraction, and sharp extraction — with broaching forces measured and micro-computed tomography (micro-CT) performed before and after testing.

Compaction and blunt extraction broaching increased total bone densification by 121% and 117% over sharp extraction (P = .005), arising from greater densification depth for compaction (P = .001) and greater maximum densification for blunt extraction (P = .008); sharp extraction friction coefficients exceeded the other two by 38% and 37% (P < .001), and blunt extraction produced fewer large debris particles than compaction (P = .005).

Blunt and compaction geometries densified to a similar extent despite opposite working directions, implicating geometry rather than direction of travel; higher sharp-extraction friction implies lower periprosthetic fracture risk at equal implantation force — but this is an orthopaedic, quasi-static, ex vivo bovine model, and abstract-only in this wiki.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Isolates geometry from working direction.** By comparing *blunt* extraction against *sharp* extraction — same direction of travel, different sharpness — the design shows the direction an instrument works in does not by itself determine whether it densifies. Blunt extraction densified +117% over sharp extraction (P = .005), statistically alongside compaction (+121%).
- **Decomposes densification into two separable parameters**: *depth* of densification (greater for compaction, P = .001) and *maximum* densification (greater for blunt extraction, P = .008). Total densification was similar for the two, but distributed differently — the first quantitative treatment of densification distribution the wiki holds.
- **Quantifies broaching friction coefficients** and links them to periprosthetic fracture risk: sharp extraction exceeded compaction and blunt extraction by 38% and 37% (P < .001), which the authors read as *lower* fracture risk for sharp extraction at equal implantation force (more of the applied force is absorbed in friction rather than driving cortex strain).
- **Measures debris particle size** as a design outcome: blunt extraction produced fewer large particles than compaction (P = .005), which the authors argue may favour osseointegration.
- Establishes an orthopaedic bench line of evidence on the same physical process dentistry calls osseodensification, independent of the dental OD literature and its inventor-driven conflicts of interest.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: quasi-static bench simulation of femoral cavity preparation. Not rotary drilling and not impaction at surgical rates — a controlled, slow-loading laboratory analogue.
- **Specimen**: bovine trabecular bone cuboids (ex vivo). MeSH indexing lists Cattle; Femur.
- **Groups**: three broach *models* representing (1) **compaction**, (2) **blunt extraction**, (3) **sharp extraction** designs. These are simplified geometric models standing in for commercial broach families, not the commercial broaches themselves.
- **Measurements**:
  - Broaching forces during the simulated preparation → **friction coefficients**.
  - **Micro-CT** scans acquired *before and after* testing → bone densification parameters (total densification, densification depth, maximum densification) and **microstructure of the broached bone bed**.
  - **Size of debris particles** pushed into the bone.
- **n / statistics**: sample size per group, exact test, and effect estimates are not recoverable from the abstract (full text not retrieved). Reported P values are .005, .001, .008, and < .001 as noted below.
- **Outcomes**: friction coefficient; total/maximum/depth densification; debris particle size; inferred periprosthetic fracture risk.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

*(abstract-level only — full text not retrieved; no means/SDs available)*

| Outcome | Finding | P |
|---|---|---|
| **Friction coefficient** | Sharp extraction **> compaction by 38%** and **> blunt extraction by 37%** | < .001 |
| **Total bone densification** | Compaction **+121%** and blunt extraction **+117%** vs sharp extraction | .005 |
| **Densification depth** | Higher for **compaction** | .001 |
| **Maximum densification** | Higher for **blunt extraction** | .008 |
| **Large debris particles** | **Fewer** with blunt extraction than compaction | .005 |

**Authors' interpretation (verbatim sense):**
- Higher friction coefficients indicate a **decreased periprosthetic fracture risk with sharp extraction broaches** for equal implantation forces.
- The blunt extraction and compaction designs investigated **densified the bone to a similar extent**.
- Blunt extraction broaching **may support better osseointegration** due to smaller bone debris particles.

**Reading for this wiki (inference, not authors' claim):** the blunt-vs-sharp extraction contrast is the load-bearing comparison. Both travel in the same direction; only sharpness differs; only the blunt one densifies. Whatever "direction" contributes in a rotary dental bur, this result says direction is not sufficient on its own — geometry is doing the work. That is a *hypothesis-strengthening* observation for dentistry, not a dental finding.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only in this wiki.** No PMC record and no PDF; means, SDs, n, and all figures are unavailable. Every number above is the abstract's own.
- **Orthopaedic, not dental.** Femoral cavity preparation for uncemented hip stems. No dental bur, no Densah/Versah, no HaeNaem, no implant osteotomy of any kind is tested. Nothing here validates any dental device.
- **Broaching ≠ drilling.** A broach is driven axially (impaction/extraction strokes); a dental densifying bur *rotates* at 800–1500 rpm. "Extraction direction" in a broach and "clockwise rotation" in a bur are analogous only at the level of *which face engages bone*, not mechanically equivalent.
- **Quasi-static.** Slow controlled loading, not surgical-rate impaction or high-speed rotation. Strain-rate-dependent bone behaviour (visco-elasticity, the strain-hardening window the OD literature relies on) is not reproduced.
- **Bovine trabecular cuboids**, ex vivo — uniform geometry, no cortex-in-situ boundary conditions, no biology, no healing, no irrigation thermal effects. Jaw bone (D1–D4, thin cortex, sinus proximity) differs substantially.
- **Broach *models***, not commercial broaches — geometry is idealized to isolate the variable, which strengthens internal validity and weakens direct transfer.
- **No temperature measured**, despite friction being the primary measured force quantity — so this contributes nothing to the drilling-heat question ([[wiki/implants/timon-2019-thermal-osteonecrosis-bone-drilling-orthopedic]]).
- **Fracture risk is inferred, not tested.** No fractures were produced or counted; the periprosthetic fracture conclusion is a force-balance argument from friction coefficients.
- Future work implied: dynamic (impaction-rate) testing, cortex-inclusive models, in vivo confirmation, and — for dentistry — an equivalent geometry-vs-direction factorial on rotary burs in jaw-like bone, which no held paper has yet run.

## 7. Glossary

- **Broach / broaching** — in total hip arthroplasty, a tapered rasp-like instrument driven **axially** (impacted in, extracted out) to prepare the femoral cavity for an uncemented stem. Distinct from rotary drilling: there is no rotation.
- **Compaction broach** — broach geometry that compresses trabecular bone outward into the cavity wall rather than removing it, densifying the bone bed.
- **Extraction broach** — broach whose teeth act on the extraction (pull-out) stroke. Subdivided here into **sharp extraction** (cuts and removes bone) and **blunt extraction** (compacts despite travelling in the same direction) — the contrast that isolates geometry from direction.
- **Osseodensification (OD) / 골밀도화** — preparing an implant site by compacting bone outward into the walls instead of excising it, raising peri-site bone mineral density. Called osseodensification in both the dental (Densah/Versah) and this orthopaedic literature.
- **Densification depth** — how far the zone of increased density extends from the prepared surface into surrounding bone (greater for compaction here, P = .001). Distinct from magnitude.
- **Maximum densification** — the peak density achieved in the densified zone (greater for blunt extraction here, P = .008). Total densification can be similar while depth and maximum differ — the two are separable.
- **Friction coefficient** — ratio of tangential to normal force at the broach–bone interface. Higher friction absorbs more of the applied implantation force, leaving less to drive cortex strain — the basis of the paper's fracture-risk inference.
- **Periprosthetic fracture** — fracture of the bone surrounding a prosthesis, here the femoral cortex during or after stem implantation; the principal clinical hazard motivating this study.
- **Quasi-static** — loading applied slowly enough that rate-dependent (visco-elastic, inertial) effects are negligible. The opposite of surgical-rate impaction or high-speed rotary drilling.
- **Micro-CT (micro-computed tomography)** — high-resolution X-ray tomography resolving trabecular microstructure; used here before/after broaching to quantify densification.
- **Debris particles** — bone fragments displaced and pushed into the bone bed during preparation. Particle size matters because smaller particles are argued to favour osseointegration (blunt extraction produced fewer large particles than compaction, P = .005).
