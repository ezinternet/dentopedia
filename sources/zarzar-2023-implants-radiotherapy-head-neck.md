## Why Ingested

이 umbrella review(Zarzar 2023, SR 15편, 24,996개 임플란트/5,487명, 2022년 7월까지 검색)는 이미 위키에 있는 [[implants/pacheco-2025-dental-implant-survival-irradiated]](Pacheco 2025, SR 11편, 73,674개 임플란트/16,193명, PROSPERO 등록, 2024년 2월까지 검색)와 **동일한 임상 질문**(두경부암 방사선치료 환자의 임플란트 생존율 vs 비조사 환자)을 다룬 **독립적인 별개 논문**이다(다른 DOI·다른 저자·다른 저널 — 번역 제목 중복 아님, 확인 완료). Zarzar가 시간상 먼저(2023) 발표됐지만 위키에는 나중에(2026-07) 들어왔으므로, Pacheco 페이지를 먼저 읽고 관계를 판단했다.

두 논문의 방향성은 일치한다: Zarzar 86.2% vs 95.2%(조사 vs 비조사), Pacheco 81.52% vs 94.64% — 독립적으로 동일한 결론(방사선치료가 임플란트 생존율을 낮춘다)을 재확인. 그러나 Pacheco가 (1) PROSPERO 사전등록, (2) 더 넓은 4개→4+grey lit 데이터베이스, (3) 더 최신 검색 시점(2024-02 vs 2022-07), (4) 훨씬 큰 정량 통합 표본(48,563 vs 24,996), (5) AMSTAR 2 평가의 내적 일관성(11편 전부 critically low vs Zarzar의 15편 중 1편만 high — 이 불일치는 실제로 published comment[PMID 37191480, DOI 10.1111/scd.12875]가 방법론적 의문을 제기한 지점)에서 더 우수하다. 임상적 결론 자체는 뒤집히지 않고 강화(reinforce)되었으므로 `superseded_by: pacheco-2025-dental-implant-survival-irradiated` / `superseded_scope: partial`로 표시 — Zarzar는 폐기가 아니라 "현재 임상결정은 Pacheco를 우선 참조, Zarzar는 초기 독립 확증 자료로 보존"의 의미.

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (15 systematic reviews, 24,996 dental implants across 5,487 head-and-neck-cancer patients, search through July 2022, four databases: PubMed/Lilacs/Cochrane/Google Scholar) comparing dental implant success in irradiated vs. non-irradiated patients.

Success rate was 86.2% in patients who underwent radiotherapy vs. 95.2% in patients who did not; only 1 of the 15 included systematic reviews was rated high methodological quality by AMSTAR 2.

Implant rehabilitation remains a valid therapy after head-and-neck radiotherapy, but the low methodological quality of most included SRs (14/15 not high quality) limits confidence — a later, larger, PROSPERO-registered umbrella review (Pacheco 2025) independently reinforces this same direction of effect with a much bigger evidence base.

## 2. Key Contributions

- One of the earlier umbrella reviews to directly compare dental implant outcomes between head-and-neck-cancer patients who did vs. did not receive radiotherapy, pooling 15 systematic reviews.
- Applies AMSTAR 2 appraisal across all 15 included SRs, reporting only 1 as high methodological quality — an explicit evidence-certainty caveat.
- Provides pooled success-rate figures (86.2% irradiated vs 95.2% non-irradiated) across 24,996 implants in 5,487 patients, an early quantitative anchor point later superseded in scale by Pacheco 2025's 48,563-implant meta-analytic synthesis.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Umbrella review of systematic reviews. Search: PubMed, Lilacs, DARE/Cochrane, and Google Scholar, through July 2022, using descriptors "Radiotherapy," "Dental implants," and "Head and Neck Cancer." 958 records identified in the initial search; after applying inclusion/exclusion criteria, 15 systematic reviews were selected. Methodological quality of the 15 included SRs assessed with the AMSTAR 2 tool. No PROSPERO registration reported in the abstract (unlike Pacheco 2025's PROSPERO CRD42023406059).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Abstract-only ingestion (full text not retrieved — Wiley/Special Care in Dentistry is not open access; no PMC full text available). Limitations reported/implied: no PROSPERO pre-registration stated; only 4 databases searched (narrower than Pacheco 2025's PubMed/Cochrane/Embase/Web of Science + grey literature); search cutoff July 2022 (roughly 18 months earlier than Pacheco's February 2024 cutoff); AMSTAR 2 rating of only 1/15 SRs as high quality was itself contested by a published comment (PMID 37191480) raising methodological questions about the appraisal. The authors themselves call for further, better-designed studies to strengthen clinical confidence.

## 7. Glossary

- **Umbrella review**: A systematic review of systematic reviews, synthesizing evidence one level above individual primary studies.
- **AMSTAR 2**: A 16-item critical appraisal tool for assessing the methodological quality of systematic reviews (7 critical domains).
- **Osseointegration**: Direct structural and functional connection between living bone and the surface of a load-bearing implant.
