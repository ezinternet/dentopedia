## Why Ingested

최초의 체계적 문헌고찰(SR)로, 근관치료된 치아의 파절저항에서 FRC vs conventional composite·inlay·fiber post·endocrown 간 대비를 정량화한다. 기존 [[post-and-core/izadi-2020-core-materials-fracture-resistance-parapost]]는 FRC vs ParaPost 포스트만 비교했으나, 이 논문은 18편의 RCT-in-vitro를 종합해 FRC > conventional hybrid composite 근거의 강도("strong evidence" 15편)를 확립하고, bulk fill·endocrown·ceramic inlay 등과의 비교에서는 근거 수준이 낮음을 명시한다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 18 in-vitro studies (796 initial search, high heterogeneity, no meta-analysis) on fracture resistance of endodontically treated posterior teeth: fiber-reinforced composites (EverX Posterior, Ribbond, Vectris) showed higher fracture resistance than conventional hybrid/nanohybrid composites in 15 studies (strong evidence);

limited or conflicting evidence vs. bulk fill, moderate evidence vs. fiber posts & inlays, and no evidence available for endocrowns;

major risk of bias across included studies.

## 세줄요약

18편의 인비트로 파절저항 연구에 대한 체계적 문헌고찰(796편 검색, 높은 이질성, 메타분석 불가): 섬유강화복합레진(EverX Posterior, Ribbond, Vectris)이 재래식 하이브리드/나노하이브리드 복합레진보다 파절저항이 높음(15편, strong evidence);

벌크필 대비 제한적/상충 근거, 파이버 포스트·인레이 대비 중간 수준 근거, 엔도크라운과의 비교 근거 없음;

포함 연구 대부분 높은 편향 위험.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review to evaluate fracture resistance of FRC in endodontically treated (ETT) teeth.
- Comprehensive literature search (Jan 2000 — May 2023): MEDLINE, Scopus, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar, LILACS.
- Risk of bias assessment using modified JBI & CRIS (13 parameters for in vitro studies).
- Stratified evidence synthesis by material comparison: FRC vs conventional hybrid, bulk fill, inlays, fiber posts, endocrowns, ceramics, amalgam, silorane.
- Material-specific analysis: EverX Posterior vs. Ribbond vs. Vectris vs. Interlig performance.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Protocol**: PRISMA Statement; registered PROSPERO CRD42021295212.
- **Search strategy (Jan 2000–May 2023)**: MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar, LILACS, plus manual reference-list search.
- **Search terms**: Compound Boolean query (Short fiber-reinforced composite OR short fiber composite OR EverX Posterior OR Ribbond OR polyethylene fiber ribbon) AND (nanocomposite OR nanofilled composite OR fiber post OR inlay OR crown OR endocrown) AND (endodontically treated teeth OR structurally compromised teeth OR conventional/traditional access cavity) AND (fracture resistance OR fracture toughness OR flexural strength).
- **Inclusion criteria**: 
  - In vitro studies only.
  - Fully formed extracted human teeth, endodontically treated.
  - Intervention: Fiber-reinforced composites (FRC).
  - Comparison: conventional hybrid/nanohybrid composites, fiber posts, crowns, endocrowns, ceramic inlays.
  - Outcome: Fracture resistance measured by universal testing machine.
- **Exclusion criteria**: Non-English publications, studies using fiber only on occlusal surface or single direction, anterior teeth with incisal fracture simulation, FRC used only as substrate for inlay/onlay, entirely removed crowns without cavity specification.
- **Study selection**: Two independent reviewers; data extraction table; risk-of-bias assessment (13 custom parameters per JBI & CRIS guidelines).
- **Heterogeneity assessment**: High heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis; qualitative synthesis only.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Overall Results
- **Total studies retrieved**: 796 (electronic search) + manual reference search.
- **Studies included**: 18 (qualitative analysis only; meta-analysis not feasible due to high heterogeneity).
- **Studies excluded**: 778 (flowchart provided; Table 2 lists 7 detailed exclusion reasons).

### FRC vs. Conventional Hybrid/Nanohybrid Composites
- **Findings**: 16 included studies compared FRC to conventional hybrid/nanohybrid composites.
- **Result**: 15 studies reported FRC fracture resistance > conventional hybrid composites (P<0.05); 3 studies found no significant difference (P>0.05).
- **Evidence strength**: **Strong evidence** for FRC superiority.

### FRC vs. Bulk Fill Composites
- **Findings**: 5 studies investigated bulk fill (conventional, flowable, SDR bulk fill, etc.).
- **Result**: Conflicting evidence — One study found no significant difference between conventional hybrid, bulk fill, and FRC; one study reported SDR bulk fill > FRC; two studies showed FRC > bulk fill.
- **Evidence strength**: **Limited/conflicting evidence**.

### FRC vs. Fiber Posts + Core Build-up
- **Findings**: Limited studies; one comparison of Ribbond (FRC strip) vs. PWFP (polyethylene woven fiber post) in MODP cavities.
- **Result**: EverX Posterior (packable FRC) possessed higher fracture strength than Ribbond (fiber-ribbon FRC) in some comparisons.
- **Evidence strength**: **Moderate evidence**.

### FRC vs. Inlays
- **Findings**: Limited comparative data.
- **Result**: Moderate evidence for inlay performance vs. FRC core build-up.
- **Evidence strength**: **Moderate evidence**.

### FRC vs. Endocrowns
- **Finding**: No studies directly compared fracture resistance of FRC-restored teeth to endocrowns.
- **Evidence strength**: **No evidence**.

### FRC vs. Amalgam
- **Findings**: One study reported FRC > amalgam for core material in MOD cavities; amalgam failures involved root fracture (unfavorable).
- **Evidence strength**: **Moderate evidence**.

### FRC vs. Silorane Composites
- **Findings**: One study showed FRC > silorane.
- **Evidence strength**: **Insufficient evidence**.

### Material-Specific Findings

#### EverX Posterior
- 13 studies used EverX Posterior (packable short e-glass fiber composite).
- 8 studies reported EverX Posterior enhanced fracture resistance (strong evidence).
- 1 study found no significant difference between EverX Posterior and conventional hybrid composites.
- 1 study reported SDR bulk fill > EverX Posterior.
- 3 studies comparing Ribbond (fiber-ribbon FRC) vs. EverX Posterior found EverX Posterior superiority (consistent findings).

#### Ribbond (Polyethylene Fiber Ribbon)
- 9 studies used Ribbond (high-molecular-weight polyethylene fiber, cold-gas-plasma treated).
- Ribbond increased fracture strength compared to conventional hybrid composites and Vectris in 3 studies.
- Inconsistent results when compared to other fiber composites.
- EverX Posterior generally showed higher fracture resistance than Ribbond in head-to-head comparisons.

#### Vectris & Interlig
- Limited data; 2–3 studies each.
- EverX Posterior showed higher fracture resistance than Interlig in one comparison.
- Composite impregnated with Interlig fibers showed higher fracture resistance than Ribbond in one study (limited evidence).

## 5. Limitations

- **In vitro static loading** (crosshead speed ~1 mm/min) does not simulate dynamic/chewing/fatigue oral loading; no thermocyclic aging in majority of studies.
- **High heterogeneity** of tooth selection (variety of teeth, sizes, pre-extraction status, storage media, storage duration), cavity designs (MOD, MODP, access-cavity dimension variations), fiber placement (packable vs. strips), cement/bonding systems, and testing parameters (load angle, specimen geometry, crosshead speed) precluded meta-analysis and subgroup quantitation.
- **Small sample sizes** per study (n=10–20 per group typical); some groups may lack statistical power.
- **Majority of studies (moderate to high risk of bias)**: Bias assessment revealed concerns in study design reporting, randomization transparency, baseline-group matching, and outcome-measurement blinding.
- **Limited clinical translation**: In vitro fracture resistance does not directly predict clinical longevity or failure rate in vivo; no RCTs of clinical outcomes (survival, complications) included.
- **Access cavity design variation**: Not all studies specified traditional vs. conservative access cavity design; confounds assessment of cavity-design effect on FRC restorations.
- **No direct comparison data** for FRC vs. endocrowns, limiting clinical decision-making on when to use FRC core build-up vs. adhesive endocrown restorations.

## 6. Related Work

- **Predecessor fracture-resistance comparisons**: Makade et al. (2013), Asadzadeh Aghadaee et al. (2013), Fokkinga et al. (metal > glass fiber > control ETT), Ahn & Sorensen (composite materials performance).
- **FRC material science**: Garoushi et al. (short fiber composites, anisotropic properties), Vallittu (high-aspect-ratio fillers), Bijelic-Donova et al. (bilayered FRC restorations, chewing fracture resistance, fatigue performance).
- **Access cavity design**: Özyürek et al. (2018) — traditional vs. conservative access cavity design effects on fracture strength (included in this SR).
- **Endodontic access cavity consequences**: Cuspal deflection, cusp breakage risk, proprioception impairment — foundational in ETT fracture-resistance literature.
- **Restorative outcomes**: Need for permanent coronal restoration post-endodontic treatment; microleakage prevention (Marchionatti et al.).
- **Other core materials**: Photo Core, LuxaCore, Core Max II (Izadi et al., Ahn & Sorensen) — core build-up material effects on FRC fracture resistance minimal in some studies.
- **Vertical root fracture (VRF)**: Tang et al. (2010), Khasnis et al. (2014) — complications of under-restored ETT.

## 7. Glossary

- **Fiber-reinforced composite (FRC)**: Composite resin containing embedded glass, polyethylene, or other high-modulus fibers to increase fracture resistance and prevent crack propagation.
- **EverX Posterior**: Packable short e-glass fiber composite (e.g., 17.5 wt% e-glass), manufacturer-formulated for bulk application in posterior restorations of ETT.
- **Ribbond**: Ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene fiber ribbon, cold-gas-plasma treated for bonding; applied as strips and stabilized with flowable/composite overlay (non-packable).
- **Vectris, Interlig**: Fiber-reinforced composite strips/strips (similar application to Ribbond); limited in this SR.
- **Fracture resistance**: Peak load (N) or flexural strength (MPa) at specimen fracture under universal testing machine; higher values indicate greater resistance to fracture.
- **MOD cavity**: Mesio-occlusal-distal cavity design (remaining buccal and lingual cusps); MODP = MOD + pulpal access.
- **Endodontically treated (ETT)**: Tooth that has undergone root canal treatment and is missing all or most of the pulp and pulp tissue.
- **Hybrid composite**: Conventional resin composite with mixed filler particle sizes (macrofiller, microfiller blend), typically without fiber reinforcement.
- **Nanohybrid composite**: Composite with nanosized filler particles; resin-matrix composite without fiber reinforcement.
- **Bulk fill composite**: Resin composite formulated for larger bulk increments (>4 mm) with reduced shrinkage and flowable or regular viscosity (e.g., SDR, SonicFill, Tetric EvoCeram Bulk Fill).
- **Endocrown**: Adhesive restoration for ETT without a core, relying on adhesive bonding to remaining tooth structure; uses etched occlusal/pulpal surface (no ferrule).
- **PRISMA Statement**: Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses; guideline for SR methodology and reporting.
- **JBI & CRIS guidelines**: Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) & Checklist for Reporting In vitro Studies (CRIS); risk-of-bias assessment tools for in vitro studies.

## 8. Evidence Level & Clinical Takeaway

- **Evidence level (this SR)**: Moderate overall — 18 in vitro studies, high heterogeneity, mostly moderate-to-high risk of bias, no meta-analysis feasible, strong evidence only for FRC > conventional hybrid composites.
- **Clinical implication**: FRC core build-up materials (EverX Posterior, Ribbond) are supported by in vitro fracture-resistance data over conventional composite cores for posterior ETT, particularly in high-stress situations (large MOD cavities, heavy occlusal loading). **However**, lack of RCT evidence and clinical outcome studies means no definitive superiority in long-term survival or cost-effectiveness; material selection should also account for clinical feasibility, operator skill, and patient factors.
- **Research gap**: Need for prospective clinical cohort studies or RCTs comparing FRC vs. conventional composite core build-up vs. endocrown restorations in similar cavity-design cohorts, with long-term survival and fracture-mode outcome measures.
