## Why Ingested

The sweep's remaining open question was whether heavy drinking worsens the *infectious* complications of jaw surgery, not just healing and bleeding. This 20-year series is the wiki's only page that puts alcohol habit into a complication model for jaw osteomyelitis, and it does so with an effect large enough to change a consent conversation: 41.2% of drinkers had a major complication versus 10.8% of non-drinkers. It also documents implant-induced osteomyelitis as a distinct aggressive subtype, which ties the page back to the implant category.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort of 54 patients with histologically confirmed osteomyelitis of the jaw treated over 20 years at a single university hospital (2002–2022).

Alcohol habit (7/17, 41.2% of complicated cases vs 4/37, 10.8%; p = 0.025), smoking (p = 0.018), malnutrition (p = 0.027), and implant-induced osteomyelitis (p = 0.003) were significantly associated with major complications, which occurred in 17 patients (31.5%).

Clinical bone exposure predicted a refractory course (p = 0.030) while a dental cause or periosteal reaction predicted success — making alcohol one of four pre-treatment flags for a complicated course.

## 1. Document Information

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Medicine 2023;12(14):4715
- **DOI**: 10.3390/jcm12144715
- **Design**: retrospective cohort, STROBE-reported
- **Setting**: Division of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Geneva University Hospitals, Switzerland
- **Ethics**: approval no. 2022-01756 (University Hospitals of Geneva); patient consent waived for de-identified data
- **Period**: 2002–2022

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Puts alcohol into a complication model, not just an aetiology list.** Prior literature named alcohol as a predisposing factor for *developing* osteomyelitis; this study associates it with *major complications* once the disease exists (p = 0.025).
- **Quantifies the contrast in absolute terms**: 41.2% of patients with an alcohol habit had a major complication versus 10.8% without.
- **Identifies implant-induced osteomyelitis as an aggressive subtype** (p = 0.003; 41.2% of complicated cases were implant-related), tying the page to the implant literature.
- **Separates predictors of complication from predictors of refractory course** — alcohol/smoking/malnutrition/implant aetiology for the former; bone exposure for the latter.

## 3. Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective cohort of all patients diagnosed with OMJ over 20 years, histologically confirmed, complete records; no age limit.
- **Exclusions**: prior head-and-neck irradiation, antiresorptive or anti-angiogenic medication, malignant disease of the oral cavity or metastatic to the jaws (i.e. MRONJ and malignancy excluded).
- **Variables**: demographics, medical and dental history, smoking and alcohol habits, clinical presentation, Zurich classification (acute / secondary chronic / primary chronic), microbiology, radiology, treatment, follow-up.
- **Outcomes**: (1) major complications — pathological fracture, deep neck abscess, cutaneous fistula; (2) treatment outcome — success versus partial/no resolution.
- **Statistics**: Fisher's exact test for categorical bivariate associations; Kruskal–Wallis for non-normal continuous variables; α = 0.05; R 4.2.1.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Variable | No complication (n = 37) | Complication (n = 17) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alcohol habit | 4 (10.8%) | 7 (41.2%) | 0.025 |
| Smoking habit | 14 (37.8%) | 13 (76.5%) | 0.018 |
| Malnutrition | 0 (0.0%) | 3 (17.6%) | 0.027 |
| Recent implant placement | 2 (5.4%) | 7 (41.2%) | 0.003 |
| Symptoms duration (median) | 2.0 months | 1.0 month | 0.016 |

- Cohort: 54 patients, mean age 47.5 ± 18.9 years, 29 female (53.7%); acute 7 (13.0%), secondary chronic 35 (64.8%), primary chronic 12 (22.2%).
- Alcohol habit overall: 11 patients (20.4%); smoking 27 (50.0%).
- Major complications: 17 patients (31.5%) — deep neck abscess, pathological fracture, or cutaneous fistula.
- Treatment success: 48 patients (88.9%); decortication was first-line in 30 (60.0%).
- **Refractory course**: clinical bone exposure associated with unfavourable outcome (p = 0.030); dental history (p = 0.015) and periosteal reaction (p = 0.036) with success; amoxicillin allergy trended (p = 0.051).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Small, single-centre, retrospective.** 54 patients and 17 events; Fisher's exact tests on cells of 0–7 patients are unstable, and alcohol's p = 0.025 rests on a 4-versus-7 split.
- **"Alcohol habit" is a binary clinical note**, not a quantified intake — no grams, no AUDIT score, no separation of current versus former drinking.
- **Confounding with smoking and malnutrition is severe.** Alcohol, smoking, and malnutrition were all significant in the same model-adjacent table, and drinkers are likely to be smokers; the study does not report a multivariable model that separates them.
- **Aetiology is a treatment variable too** — patients who developed osteomyelitis after implant placement may have been referred later or treated differently, so the implant association may partly reflect referral pathway.
- **Excluded populations** (irradiated, antiresorptive-treated) mean this is not a generalisable "jaw infection" population.

## 7. Glossary

- **OMJ (osteomyelitis of the jaw)**: progressive inflammatory infection of jaw bone and marrow.
- **Zurich classification**: divides OMJ into acute osteomyelitis, secondary chronic osteomyelitis (SCO), and primary chronic osteomyelitis (PCO).
- **Major complication**: here defined as pathological fracture, deep neck abscess, or cutaneous fistula.
- **Refractory course**: partial or no resolution despite treatment.
- **Decortication**: surgical removal of the cortical plate to allow vascular ingress and drainage — first-line here in 60% of patients.
- **Fisher's exact test**: exact test for 2×2 contingency tables, used here because of small cell counts.
