[[wiki/radiology/lingam-2023-common-errors-subjective-quality-panoramic]]이 실측한 "자세 오류가 최우선 흔한 오류" 결론과 독립적으로 일치하며(재확인), [[wiki/radiology/devlin-2013-object-position-magnification-panoramic-radiography]]의 초점골(Focal Trough) 왜곡·확대 이론을 임상적 오류 분류 체계로 확장한다.

## Three-line Summary
Narrative literature review (PubMed search, 2014) cataloguing the most common patient-positioning errors in panoramic radiography with each error's image consequence and correction.
Patient positioning is the most frequent panoramic error class; re-examination causes cited are head turned right/left (24.84%), head in front of the focal trough (21.15%), head tilted forward (21.21%), and head behind the focal trough (20.30%); correct setup requires the occlusal plane at -20° to -30° from horizontal and the tongue against the palate.
Lets operators recognize and correct each misposition (flattened/reversed occlusal curve, tooth overlap, condyle signs, ghost images) to avoid repeat exposures and unnecessary radiation.

## 세줄요약
파노라마 방사선 사진(Panoramic Radiography)에서 가장 흔한 환자 자세 오류(Positioning Error)의 유형·영상 결과·교정법을 정리한 서술적 문헌고찰(Narrative Review, PubMed 검색, 2014).
환자 자세 설정이 가장 흔한 오류이며, 재촬영 원인별 비율은 두부 좌우 회전 24.84%, 초점골(Focal Trough) 전방 위치 21.15%, 전방 경사 21.21%, 초점골 후방 위치 20.30%; 올바른 경사면(Occlusal Plane)은 수평선 대비 -20°~-30°, 혀는 구개에 밀착.
머리·턱·혀·자세의 각 오류가 초점골 이탈로 블러·중첩·확대왜곡·유령상(Ghost Image)을 유발하는 기전을 제시해, 반복 촬영과 불필요한 방사선 노출을 줄이는 참고가 된다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Categorizes panoramic positioning errors by anatomical axis (focal-trough antero-posterior, lateral tilt/turn, chin elevation/depression, tongue position, posture/movement) and gives the image signature of each.
- Reports relative frequency of head-misposition causes among re-examined cases (21.15% anterior, 24.84% side turn, 21.21% forward tilt, 20.30% posterior).
- Summarizes the geometric basis: panoramic screen-film is 10–60-fold more sensitive than fluoroscopic film, but the focal layer has limited dimensions, so minor positioning errors cause unequal vertical/horizontal magnification, tooth overlap, and loss of sharpness.
- Provides chairside recognition cues (occlusal-plane curvature, hyoid overlap, condyle signs, palatoglossal air space, ghost images) to correct errors and avoid repeat exposures.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Narrative literature review
- **Databases**: PubMed (search terms "radiographic positioning errors" and "panoramic radiographs")
- **n**: Not applicable — no quantitative synthesis; error frequencies are second-hand citations (Dhillon et al 2012, Kaviani et al 2008)
- **Outcomes**: Classification of common positioning errors, their radiographic consequences, and corrective technique

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Decreasing order of most commonly observed panoramic errors (oral radiology institutes): positioning → exposure factors → artifact appearance → technical performance.
- Share of re-examination cases by cause of head misposition: turned right/left 24.84%; in front of focal trough 21.15%; tilted forward 21.21%; behind focal trough 20.30%.
- Correct setup: Frankfort plane parallel to ground, median sagittal plane perpendicular to ground, chin in frontal cup, tongue against palate, occlusal plane -20° to -30° from horizontal; chin positioned so the tragus-to-outer-canthus line is parallel to the ground.
- Panoramic screen-film is 10–60-fold more sensitive than film used in fluoroscopic X-ray.
- Positioning error → image consequence (from text):
  - Head in front of focus → anterior dental arches blurred/shortened/narrowed; premolar and ramus-column overlap.
  - Head behind focus → anterior teeth blurred and horizontally expanded; condyles at the lateral film edges.
  - Head tilted back → occlusal plane flat or reversely curved, upper incisor apices out of focus, condyles projected out of the imaged area (increased intercondylar distance).
  - Head tilted forward → occlusal plane excessively curved, lower incisor apices out of focus, hyoid bone overlapping the anterior mandible, upper condylar regions may vanish (narrowing intercondylar distance).
  - Lateral tilt → asymmetric structures (slope-side reduced in size), proximal surfaces substantially overlapped.
  - Head turn → teeth extended on one side of the midline and shortened on the other; one ascending ramus much larger; condyles unequal in size.
  - Chin elevated → occlusal plane flat or inverted, radiopaque palate shadow over maxillary tooth roots, mandible widened.
  - Chin depressed → teeth overlap, symphysis may disappear, condyles project past the upper image edge, mandible shortened.
  - Tongue not on palate / lips open → radiolucent band at the apex of upper teeth; palatoglossal air space obscures the apical region of maxillary teeth, risking misdiagnosis of the central incisor apex.
  - Posture/movement → ghost image: blurred zones and large step defects at the inferior border of the mandible.
  - Ghost-image sources (rotational panoramic only): vertebrae, rami, hyoid bone; metallic materials, crowns, wires, containment plates, earrings, necklaces, machine parts.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Narrative review: no systematic search protocol, no explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria, no quality assessment of included studies — conclusions are qualitative.
- Error-frequency figures are second-hand (Dhillon et al 2012, Kaviani et al 2008), not the authors' own measurements.
- Some positioning errors are acknowledged as inevitable (patient physical stature, facial asymmetry, inability to follow instructions).

## 7. Glossary
- **Focal Trough (초점골/Image Layer)**: the curved 3-D zone in front of a panoramic receptor within which structures are acceptably sharp; objects outside it are blurred, magnified, or ghosted.
- **Ghost Image (허상)**: a blurred, often contralateral artifact of a radiopaque object between the X-ray source and the center of rotation; occurs only in rotational panoramic acquisition.
- **Occlusal Plane (경사면)**: the plane through the occlusal surfaces; correctly angulated -20° to -30° from the horizontal for panoramic radiography.
- **Frankfort Plane**: the reference plane through orbitale and porion, used as the horizontal landmark for patient positioning.
- **Palatoglossal Air Space**: the dark radiolucent air gap between the tongue dorsum and the hard/soft palate when the tongue is not pressed against the roof of the mouth.
