## Why Ingested
Basic-science perspective on why oral mucosal wounds heal faster and with less scarring than skin — mechanistic background relevant to periodontal soft-tissue surgery, donor-site healing, and graft outcomes. Extends the fibroblast-level wound-healing biology already held in [[periodontics/huynh-2016-saline-rinsing-gingival-fibroblast-wound-healing]] by framing it against the oral-vs-dermal comparison.

## Three-line Summary
(Line 1) Perspective/narrative review (Wound Repair and Regeneration, 2013) contrasting scar-forming dermal wound healing with the scarless, accelerated healing of oral mucosal wounds.
(Line 2) Oral wounds show fewer immune mediators, blood vessels, and profibrotic mediators, but more bone-marrow-derived cells, faster reepithelialization, and faster fibroblast proliferation than dermal wounds; ECM differences noted in procollagen I and tenascin-C expression.
(Line 3) The review frames scarless oral healing as a template for future antifibrotic therapy but does not itself report new experimental data — it synthesizes prior comparative studies.

## 7. Glossary
- **ECM (Extracellular Matrix, 세포외기질)**: the non-cellular structural network (collagen, fibronectin, proteoglycans) that provides scaffolding during tissue repair.
- **Tenascin-C (테나신-C)**: an ECM glycoprotein upregulated during wound healing/tissue remodeling, implicated in fibrosis and scar formation.
- **Procollagen I (전콜라겐 I)**: the precursor form of type I collagen, the principal collagen deposited in scar tissue.
- **Reepithelialization (재상피화)**: the process of epithelial cell migration and proliferation to resurface a wound.
- **Bone marrow-derived cells (골수유래세포)**: circulating progenitor/immune cells recruited from bone marrow to a wound site, contributing to repair and, in some tissues, reduced scarring.
- **Scarless healing (무흉터 치유)**: wound repair that regenerates tissue architecture without fibrotic scar formation, characteristic of fetal skin and oral mucosa.
