## Why Ingested

Extends the smoking–periodontal harm axis of [[overviews/smoking-tobacco-periodontal-implant-overview]] from active smokers to secondhand exposure, a population the wiki held no evidence on. Where [[periodontics/apatzidou-2022-cigarette-smoking-periodontal-implant-review]] explains the mechanism in smokers, this SR+MA quantifies periodontitis risk (OR 1.47) in people who never smoked — and its cotinine-vs-self-report contrast makes exposure misclassification the methodological lever to watch.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (14 studies, 115,925 non-smoking adults, search to June 2025) testing whether environmental tobacco smoke (ETS/secondhand smoke) exposure is associated with periodontitis and tooth loss in people who have never smoked themselves.

ETS exposure was significantly associated with periodontitis (OR 1.47, 95% CI 1.22-1.76) but not with tooth loss (OR 1.03, 95% CI 0.80-1.31); the association was stronger with biomarker (cotinine)-based exposure assessment than self-report, and stronger in men than women.

Clinically, this extends the smoking-periodontal harm framework beyond active smokers to household/occupational secondhand exposure — relevant for periodontal risk counselling of non-smoking patients living with smokers, and a public-health argument for smoke-free environments as a periodontitis-prevention measure.

## 세줄요약

체계적 고찰+메타분석(연구 14편, 비흡연 성인 115,925명, 2025년 6월까지 검색) — 간접흡연(환경 담배연기, ETS) 노출과 비흡연자의 치주염·치아상실 연관성 검증.

ETS 노출은 치주염과 유의하게 연관(OR 1.47, 95% CI 1.22–1.76)되었으나 치아상실과는 무관(OR 1.03, 95% CI 0.80–1.31); 코티닌 기반 객관적 노출평가에서 자가보고보다 강한 연관성, 여성보다 남성에서 강한 연관성.

임상적으로 흡연-치주 손상 틀을 능동 흡연자를 넘어 가정·직장 내 간접흡연 노출자까지 확장 — 비흡연 환자 중 동거 흡연자가 있는 경우 치주 위험 상담에 참고, 금연구역 정책의 치주염 예방 근거로도 활용 가능.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First meta-analytic quantification (14 studies, >115K participants) of ETS-periodontitis association restricted to genuine non-smokers, removing active-smoking confounding.
- Identifies cotinine-based (biomarker) assessment as yielding stronger, more reliable associations than self-report — a methodological signal for future ETS research.
- Reveals a sex-differential association (men positive, women null) with plausible biological/behavioral explanations (nicotine metabolism, exposure under-reporting, hormonal modulation).

## 3. Methodology
PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, Cochrane Library searched for English-language publications to June 9, 2025. 14 studies, 115,925 adults (18+) included. OR with 95% CI as main effect measure; subgroup analyses by region, age, sex, exposure-assessment method (self-report vs cotinine), adjustment for diabetes/oral hygiene, and study recency.

## 4. Key Results
[확인, 관찰연구 기반] ETS exposure vs periodontitis: OR 1.47 (95% CI 1.22-1.76), significant. ETS exposure vs tooth loss: OR 1.03 (95% CI 0.80-1.31), null. Cotinine-based assessment > self-report in association strength. Men: significant association; women: no association. Studies ≥2015 showed somewhat weaker effects than earlier studies (possible confounding control improvement or publication bias in earlier literature). Substantial heterogeneity present; age-stratified subgroup analysis not possible due to inconsistent reporting.

## 5. Limitations
- Predominantly cross-sectional design — causality/temporality cannot be established (reverse causation considered unlikely but not excluded).
- Dose-response relationship not evaluable due to insufficient exposure-dose/duration data.
- English-language restriction may introduce language bias.
- Substantial unexplained heterogeneity despite extensive subgroup analysis.
- Self-reported ETS exposure (majority of studies) is an information-bias source; residual confounding from unmeasured environmental exposures (air pollution, occupational hazards) not addressable.
