## Why Ingested

Ingested as the historical-but-current pharmacology anchor for the analgesics shelf: this narrative review codifies the NSAID-first, opioid-as-addition doctrine and the equianalgesic dosing tables that later quantitative syntheses on this wiki set out to test. It independently reinforces [[wiki/drug/analgesics/pozzi-2011-pain-management-dentists-ibuprofen]] at the class level and supplies the qualitative baseline against which [[wiki/drug/analgesics/moore-2015-single-dose-oral-analgesics-cochrane-overview]] later measured single-dose efficacy.

## Three-line Summary

- Narrative continuing-education review (single author, Anesthesia Progress 57(2):67-79, 2010) covering nonopioid and opioid analgesic pharmacology for acute and postoperative dental pain; no systematic search reported.
- Core claims: NSAIDs are generally superior to conventional-dose opioids for musculoskeletal (including dental) pain; analgesic ceilings sit at ibuprofen 400 mg and aspirin/acetaminophen 1000 mg; equianalgesic oral doses run codeine 200 mg = hydrocodone 30 mg = oxycodone 20 mg = morphine 30 mg PO (= morphine 10 mg IM).
- Prescribes a stepped regimen — around-the-clock ibuprofen 400-800 mg and/or acetaminophen 500-1000 mg first, opioid added only for breakthrough pain — while acknowledging its own evidence base is narrative expert synthesis rather than systematic review.

## 세줄요약

- 단일 저자의 서술형 문헌고찰(narrative review, Anesthesia Progress 57권 2호, 2010)로, 급성·술후 치과 통증의 진통제 약리학을 비오피이드(nonopioid)와 오피이드(opioid) 계열로 나눠 정리했다.
- 핵심 주장: 근골격계(치과 포함) 통증에서는 상용량의 비스테로이드성 소염제(NSAID)가 오피이드보다 generally 우수하며, 진통 천장(ceiling)은 이부프로펜(ibuprofen) 400 mg, 아스피린(aspirin)·아세트아미노펜(acetaminophen) 1000 mg; 경구 등가진통용량(equianalgesic dose)은 코드인(codeine) 200 mg = 하이드로코돈(hydrocodone) 30 mg = 옥시코돈(oxycodone) 20 mg = 모르핀(morphine) 30 mg.
- 임상 적용: 시계 기반 정기투여(''around-the-clock'') 이부프로펜 400-800 mg 및/또는 아세트아미노펜 500-1000 mg을 1단계로 하고 돌발통에만 오피이드를 추가하는 단계별 처방 제시; 단 체계적 검색이 없는 2010년 전문가 종합이라는 한계 명시.

## 1. Document Information

- Journal: Anesthesia Progress 57(2):67-79, 2010 (issue season "Summer"; copyright American Dental Society of Anesthesiology; ISSN 0003-3006)
- Article type: Continuing Education review article (Part 1 of a series; chronic pain deferred to Part 2)
- Author: Daniel E. Becker, DDS — Associate Director of Education, General Dental Practice Residency, Miami Valley Hospital, Dayton, Ohio
- DOI: 10.2344/0003-3006-57.2.67 | PMID: 20553137
- Scope: basic pharmacology of analgesic classes, relative efficacy for dental pain, dosage strategies stratified by anticipated pain intensity

## 2. Key Contributions

- Reframes the classic opioid/nonopioid distinction: both classes act centrally and peripherally; the true distinguishing feature is mechanism — receptor activation (opioids) versus interruption of prostaglandin synthesis (nonopioids).
- Establishes the analgesic ceiling doctrine: dose-response curves for nonopioids plateau (ibuprofen 400 mg; aspirin and acetaminophen 1000 mg) whereas mu-opioid agonists show unlimited dose-dependent efficacy.
- Tabulates equianalgesic doses (adapted from Facts and Comparisons 2009), arguing against the traditional textbook figure that oxycodone 30 mg PO is equipotent with morphine 10 mg IM and defending oxycodone 20 mg as the appropriate oral equivalent.
- Details the CYP2D6 metabolism cascade: codeine is effectively a prodrug (~10% demethylated to morphine, credited with its entire analgesic effect); hydrocodone's effect is shared between parent drug and hydromorphone metabolite; oxycodone acts almost entirely as parent drug, making it preferable under CYP2D6 inhibition.
- Provides a stepped prescribing algorithm (Table 4) keyed to anticipated pain intensity, with around-the-clock nonopioids as the foundation and opioids titrated as additions, never replacements.
- Consolidates NSAID safety pharmacology relevant to dentistry: GI mucosal injury, antiplatelet effects (irreversible only for aspirin), renal constraints, leukotriene-shunt pseudoallergy, and drug interactions (aspirin-ibuprofen competition, lithium, methotrexate, SSRIs).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Narrative expert review in continuing-education format; no systematic literature search, no inclusion criteria, no meta-analytic pooling are reported.
- Structure proceeds by drug class: nonopioids (NSAIDs including COX-2 selective agents, then acetaminophen) followed by opioids (receptor pharmacology, dependence/tolerance/addiction distinctions, agent-by-agent considerations including codeine derivatives, meperidine, propoxyphene, pentazocine, tramadol), closing with regimen selection.
- Evidence base is citation-driven: randomized trials of dental pain (Forbes 1990; Fricke 1992; Van Dyke 2004), textbook references (Goodman and Gilman's 11th ed.; Drug Facts and Comparisons 2009), The Medical Letter treatment guidelines, and the Oregon Evidence-Based Practice Center COX-2/NSAID drug class review (Chou 2006).
- Figures carry conceptual weight: Figure 1 (arachidonic acid cascade and prostanoid functions), Figure 2 (ceiling-effect dose-response curves), Figure 3 (morphine/codeine molecular structures and CYP2D6 demethylation), Figure 4 (NSAID versus opioid analgesia after third molar impaction surgery, redrawn from Van Dyke et al.).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Class-level efficacy claim: "for decades studies have repeatedly found that NSAIDs are generally superior to opioids at conventional dosages" for musculoskeletal pain, dental pain included.
- Analgesic ceilings: ibuprofen 400 mg; aspirin and acetaminophen 1000 mg (Figure 2); a single 200-400 mg ibuprofen dose suffices for pain/fever while 1600-2400 mg/day is required for adequate inflammation suppression.
- Van Dyke-derived benchmark (Figure 4, third molar impaction patients): oxycodone 5 mg alone was "no more effective than placebo," ibuprofen 400 mg provided clearly greater relief, and adding oxycodone 5 mg to ibuprofen improved analgesia further (opioid-nonopioid synergy).
- Combination rationale: NSAID plus acetaminophen yields greater analgesic efficacy than either agent alone and "may obviate the need for opioids."
- Equianalgesic anchors: 10 mg morphine IM = 75 mg meperidine = 120 mg codeine parenterally; oral equivalents morphine 30 mg (3:1 ratio), codeine 180-200 mg, hydrocodone 30 mg, oxycodone 20 mg; trial support cited (oxycodone 10 mg comparable to codeine 100 mg; hydrocodone 10 mg approximately equipotent to codeine 60 mg).
- Acetaminophen toxicity thresholds: hepatotoxic metabolite accumulation above 200-250 mg/kg per 24 hours; maximum 4 g/day in healthy adults, 2 g/day advised for chronic alcoholism.
- Interaction magnitudes: nonaspirin NSAIDs raise GI bleeding risk twofold-threefold with clopidogrel and fourfold-fivefold with warfarin; aspirin-ibuprofen antiplatelet competition managed by separating doses 1-2 hours, though clinical relevance questioned (thromboxane inhibition reduced by only 1% after 10 days concurrent use in one cited study).
- Metabolite hazards: normeperidine (elimination half-life 15-20 hours vs 3 hours for parent meperidine; oral bioavailability ~25%) makes oral meperidine "a very poor choice"; norpropoxyphene half-life 30 hours limits propoxyphene to short-term use.
- Population variability: roughly 5-10% of Caucasians are CYP2D6 poor metabolizers (blunted codeine response) and an estimated 1-7% show elevated CYP2D6 activity (heightened sensitivity).
- Stepped regimens (Table 4): Step 1 — ibuprofen 400-800 mg tid/qid or equivalent NSAID and/or acetaminophen 500-1000 mg qid, prescribed continuously, not PRN; Step 2 — add oxycodone 5-10 mg or morphine 15 mg q4h PRN, or pentazocine/naloxone 50 mg or tramadol 50 mg q4h PRN, or fixed opioid-acetaminophen combinations (with the 4 g/day acetaminophen cap flagged).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Explicitly narrative: no systematic search strategy or formal quality appraisal; several pivotal statements rest on textbook compendia and single trials.
- The author concedes equianalgesic dose studies for codeine derivatives are "sparse," and that precise analgesic-ceiling doses for most NSAIDs other than ibuprofen have not been determined.
- Era-bound content: tramadol described as not a controlled substance in the United States (status has since changed), COX-2 cardiovascular controversy framed with 2006-era evidence, and propoxyphene still marketed (later withdrawn in the US).
- Chronic pain management, analgesic adjuncts (antidepressants, anticonvulsants), and the second half of the topic are deliberately deferred to Part 2 of the series.
- Guidance for patients already on long-term physician-managed opioid therapy (do not add opioids; request dose adjustment from the treating physician) is presented without cited outcome data.

## 6. Related Work

- Dionne et al. 1983 and Jackson/Moore/Hargreaves 1989 — cited as repeated demonstrations that preoperative NSAID administration decreases postoperative pain intensity (basis for the preemptive analgesia argument).
- Van Dyke et al. 2004 (Clin Ther) — source of the Figure 4 dataset comparing ibuprofen 400 mg, oxycodone 5 mg, and their combination after third molar impaction.
- Hyllested et al. 2002 (Br J Anaesth) — qualitative review supporting the superior efficacy of NSAID-acetaminophen combination over either alone.
- Chou et al. 2006, Oregon Evidence-Based Practice Center — drug class review underpinning the claim that no convincing evidence favors one NSAID over another in efficacy or safety.
- Becker DE and Phero JC 2005 (Anesth Prog 52:140-149) — prior article by the same author from which Table 4's stepped approach is adapted.
- Gutstein HB and Akil H, Goodman and Gilman's 11th ed. — source for opioid receptor pharmacology (mu/kappa effects, meperidine metabolism).

## 7. Glossary

- Prostanoids: family of cyclooxygenase products (prostaglandins, thromboxanes, prostacyclin) mediating pain, fever, inflammation, and protective physiological functions.
- Ceiling effect: plateau of the dose-response curve beyond which higher doses confer no additional analgesia (nonopioids), contrasted with the unlimited dose-response of mu-opioid agonists.
- Equianalgesic dose: dose of one opioid producing analgesia equivalent to a reference dose of another (standard reference: morphine 10 mg IM).
- Agonist-antagonist opioid: agent agonizing kappa receptors while antagonizing mu receptors (e.g., nalbuphine, pentazocine); limited analgesic ceiling, lower abuse potential, can precipitate withdrawal in opioid-dependent patients.
- CYP2D6: cytochrome P450 isoenzyme responsible for O-demethylation of codeine (to morphine), hydrocodone (to hydromorphone), oxycodone (scant, to oxymorphone), and tramadol (to active M1 metabolite).
- Normeperidine: non-analgesic CNS/cardiovascular stimulant metabolite of meperidine with a 15-20 hour elimination half-life; accumulates with repeated dosing.
- Wind-up: amplification of central pain interpretation triggered by unblocked nociceptive input once local anesthesia wanes; rationale for establishing NSAID levels preemptively.
- Preemptive analgesia: administration of analgesic before tissue injury/anesthesia reversal so prostaglandin synthesis is inhibited before nociceptive traffic resumes.
- Aspirin/NSAID intolerance: pseudoallergic reaction driven by leukotriene shunting (not IgE-mediated hypersensitivity); preferred terminology over "allergy."
- Around-the-clock (clock-based) dosing: fixed-interval administration regardless of pain presence, as opposed to PRN dosing, prescribed for Step 1 nonopioids.
