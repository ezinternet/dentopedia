## Why Ingested

[[wiki/overviews/treatment-planning-decision-variability-overview]] establishes that generalists extract ~3x more often than endodontists and speculates this reflects "a lack of awareness or confidence in conservative treatment options" — but names no mechanism, because no paper in that synthesis measures one. This paper is the first the wiki holds that does: a multivariable model of what actually predicts a trainee's willingness to adopt a conservative pulp-preserving option (full pulpotomy), isolating procedural experience and confidence in bleeding control as independent predictors while knowledge/awareness variables (year of study) dropped out of the final model. It extends [[wiki/practice-management/dufey-portilla-2026-treatment-decision-making-apical-periodontitis-clinician-groups]] one step upstream — that paper shows *that* decisions diverge by training level; this one proposes *why*.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional questionnaire study (Dicle University, Türkiye, n=255 fourth- and fifth-year dental students, response rate 85.0%) presenting a standardised symptomatic-irreversible-pulpitis vignette (tooth 36, carious exposure) and using multivariable binary logistic regression to identify independent predictors of self-reported willingness to use full pulpotomy as a definitive treatment.

Overall 55.7% reported willingness to use full pulpotomy definitively, but only 45.1% actually chose a pulpotomy-based approach when forced to pick a treatment for the vignette (vs. 28.6% single-visit pulpectomy+obturation, 24.7% pulpectomy+Ca(OH)₂); in the adjusted model, having previously *performed* a pulpotomy (OR 2.51, 95% CI 1.18–5.33) and a mandatory-rubber-dam attitude (OR 1.78, 95% CI 1.01–3.14) independently raised willingness, while reporting an implementation barrier (OR 0.29, 95% CI 0.15–0.58) and uncertainty about barriers (OR 0.06, 95% CI 0.02–0.25) independently lowered it; year of study, observed-only exposure, and hemostasis/bleeding-control requirement were **not** independently significant in the Results section — despite the Abstract listing "clinical year" and "confidence in bleeding control" as independently associated, a discrepancy this source flags rather than resolves.

This is undergraduate stated intent on a hypothetical single-centre vignette, not delivered treatment or an outcome, and the model explains only 24.0% of variance (Nagelkerke R²) with 66.3% classification accuracy — the predictors identified are real but the model is a distinctly weak one, and the paper itself does not address whether full pulpotomy actually works (see [[wiki/endodontics/vpt/mtalsi-2026-pulpotomy-symptomatic-irreversible-pulpitis-mature-umbrella]] for that separate question).

## 세줄요약

횡단면 설문 연구(터키 Dicle 대학, 4·5학년 치과대학생 n=255, 응답률 85.0%) — 표준화된 증상성 비가역성 치수염(Symptomatic Irreversible Pulpitis) 비네트(치아 36, 우식 노출)를 제시하고, 완전 치수절단술(Full Pulpotomy)을 최종치료로 사용할 의향의 독립 예측인자를 다변량 이분형 로지스틱회귀로 분석.

전체 55.7%가 완전 치수절단술을 최종치료로 쓸 의향을 밝혔으나, 실제 비네트에서 치수절단 기반 접근을 선택한 비율은 45.1%뿐(단일내원 발수+충전 28.6%, 수산화칼슘 근관貼(Ca(OH)₂) 발수 24.7%); 보정모델에서 과거 치수절단술 시행 경험(승산비(Odds Ratio, OR) 2.51, 95% 신뢰구간(Confidence Interval, CI) 1.18–5.33)과 러버댐(Rubber Dam) 필수 사용에 대한 태도(OR 1.78, 95% CI 1.01–3.14)가 의향을 독립적으로 높였고, 시행 장벽 인지(OR 0.29, 95% CI 0.15–0.58)와 장벽에 대한 불확실성(OR 0.06, 95% CI 0.02–0.25)이 독립적으로 낮췄으며, 학년·관찰만 경험·지혈 요구도는 본문 Results에서 유의하지 않았다 — Abstract는 "학년"과 "지혈 조절 자신감"을 독립 연관인자로 나열해 본문과 상충하는데, 이 source는 이를 해소하지 않고 그대로 flag함.

이는 학부생의 가상 단일기관 비네트에서의 stated intent(의향 진술)이지 실제 시행된 치료나 예후가 아니며, 모델은 분산의 24.0%(Nagelkerke R²)만 설명하고 분류정확도 66.3%로 뚜렷이 약한 모델이다 — 완전 치수절단술이 실제로 효과가 있는지는 이 논문이 다루지 않는 별개 질문([[wiki/endodontics/vpt/mtalsi-2026-pulpotomy-symptomatic-irreversible-pulpitis-mature-umbrella]] 참조).

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **First mechanism-level determinant analysis of pulpotomy adoption the wiki holds.** Rather than describing what trainees prefer, it isolates *why* via multivariable logistic regression (n=255, 142 events, EPV adequate) — a distinct evidentiary layer above the descriptive-only survey work already in `practice-management/`.
2. **Procedural experience beats academic seniority.** Prior *performance* of pulpotomy (not just observation, not year of study) was the strongest positive predictor (OR 2.51) — year of study dropped out of the adjusted model despite a univariable class-level difference trend (48.7% vs. 57.2% willingness, p=0.153, itself not significant).
3. **A protocol-adherence attitude (rubber dam) tracks with willingness to adopt a different protocol-driven procedure (pulpotomy)**, suggesting a general disposition toward structured/evidence-based practice rather than procedure-specific knowledge. This directly parallels the endodontist/GDP rubber-dam gap already documented in [[wiki/practice-management/nosrat-2025-endodontist-gp-nsrct-clinical-approaches-cohort]].
4. **Barriers and uncertainty are the dominant negative predictors**, and uncertainty (OR 0.06) has a far larger effect size than any positive predictor — though the subgroup is small (n=22) and the authors themselves flag it for cautious interpretation.
5. **Surfaces its own abstract/body inconsistency**, useful as a caution for any downstream synthesis: the Abstract names "clinical year" and "confidence in bleeding control" as independently associated with willingness, but Results §3.5 explicitly states these were **not** independently significant in the final model (all p>0.05). See Deviation Log.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional, single-centre questionnaire; 19 structured items across diagnostic criteria, treatment preference, clinical experience, perceived barriers, postoperative management. Piloted on n=15 (excluded from final analysis), reviewed by two endodontists + one restorative dentistry specialist for face validity.
- **Population**: Fourth- and fifth-year dental students at Dicle University who had completed ≥1 clinical rotation in endodontics and restorative dentistry. Distributed to 300 eligible students; 255 valid responses (85.0% response rate). 117 (45.9%) fourth-year, 138 (54.1%) fifth-year.
- **Vignette**: 22-year-old female, nocturnal spontaneous pain, tooth 36, radiographic deep caries, pulp exposure during caries removal — a standardised hypothetical scenario, not an actual patient encounter.
- **Outcome**: Binary — willingness to use full pulpotomy as a *definitive* treatment in the scenario ("Yes"/"No"), distinct from the separate forced-choice treatment-selection item (pulpotomy-based / single-visit pulpectomy+obturation / pulpectomy+Ca(OH)₂ / other).
- **Statistics**: Chi-square / Fisher–Freeman–Halton for class-level comparisons; multivariable binary logistic regression for the primary outcome, predictors entered via univariable screening (p<0.10) + clinical relevance; dummy coding for pulpotomy-experience (reference: never performed) and barriers (reference: no barrier). VIF/tolerance checked, no problematic multicollinearity. Model fit: likelihood-ratio test p<0.001, Hosmer–Lemeshow p=0.403, Nagelkerke R²=0.240, classification accuracy 66.3%. Sample size via G*Power (w=0.30, α=0.05, power=0.80) and EPV principle (142 events / predictors, ratio >10).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Diagnostic criteria cited** (may select multiple): spontaneous pain 69.4%, prolonged pain to heat 50.6%, percussion sensitivity 46.7%, radiographic findings 43.9%.
- **Clinical exposure**: 52.5% encounter symptomatic irreversible pulpitis cases at least weekly.
- **Scenario treatment choice**: pulpotomy-based 45.1%, single-visit pulpectomy+obturation 28.6%, pulpectomy+Ca(OH)₂ dressing 24.7%, other 1.6%.
- **Capping material preference**: MTA 57.3%, Biodentine 12.9%, calcium hydroxide 8.2%.
- **Overall willingness to use full pulpotomy definitively**: 55.7% — a figure 10.6 percentage points above the 45.1% who actually selected a pulpotomy-based approach in the same vignette; the source does not cross-tabulate the two items, so whether this reflects "would use it in principle but chose differently here" or a genuine measurement mismatch between the two questions cannot be resolved from the text as retrieved.
- **Barriers** (n=255): lack of practical experience 20.4% (52), concerns about long-term success 16.5% (42), limited access to materials 13.3% (34), insufficient theoretical knowledge 10.2% (26), uncertainty regarding barriers 8.6% (22), lack of confidence 3.5% (9).
- **Class-level (4th vs. 5th year)**: willingness 48.7% vs. 57.2% (p=0.153, NS); rubber dam attitude 50.4% vs. 54.3% (p=0.583, NS); MTA preference 55.6% vs. 58.7% (p=0.566, NS); procedural difficulty ("no difficulty") 22.2% vs. 35.5% (p=0.0129, significant); hemostasis duration distribution (p=0.0299, significant).
- **Adjusted multivariable model** (outcome: willingness to adopt full pulpotomy definitively):

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Undergraduate stated intent, not clinical behaviour or outcome.** The authors explicitly state results "should not be interpreted as evidence that participants would necessarily make the same decisions in real-world practice."
- **Single-centre, cross-sectional** — no causal inference; experience and confidence are entangled (a student who has performed pulpotomies is more willing, but the arrow could run either way, and self-selection into extra procedural exposure is plausible).
- **Weak explanatory model**: Nagelkerke R²=0.240, i.e., ~76% of variance in willingness is unexplained by the measured predictors; classification accuracy 66.3% is modest. The authors attribute the gap to unmeasured self-efficacy, perceived competence, and tolerance for uncertainty.
- **Small subgroup for the largest effect size**: the "uncertain about barriers" group is n=22, and the authors themselves caution the OR 0.06 estimate should be read cautiously.
- **Questionnaire not a validated psychometric instrument**; knowledge level, self-efficacy, and perceived competence were not directly measured with standardized tools.
- **Abstract/body inconsistency** (see Deviation Log below): treat the Abstract's claim that "clinical year" and "confidence in bleeding control" were independently associated with willingness as **unsupported by the Results section as retrieved** — the regression table language explicitly places both in the non-significant list.
- **Efficacy of full pulpotomy itself is out of scope** — this paper is entirely about adoption/willingness, not outcome. See [[wiki/endodontics/vpt/mtalsi-2026-pulpotomy-symptomatic-irreversible-pulpitis-mature-umbrella]] and [[wiki/endodontics/vpt/taha-2017-mta-pulpotomy-mature-permanent-carious]] for the separate efficacy evidence.

## 6. Related Work

- Cites Zhu et al. (RCT, comparable 12-month outcomes for full pulpotomy vs. RCT, less time/cost, less early pain) and Ather et al. (pooled pulpotomy success >80%) as the outcome evidence this paper does not itself generate.
- Cites Colloc et al. and Yi et al. — conventional RCT remains predominant among US/UK practitioners despite rising openness to pulpotomy, echoing the willingness/behaviour gap this paper's own 55.7%-vs-45.1% split hints at.
- Cites Edwards et al. — insufficient training, outcome uncertainty, and lack of confidence as pulpotomy barriers in primary dental care, consistent with this paper's barrier-driven negative predictors.

## 7. Glossary

- **VPT** — Vital Pulp Therapy; biologically oriented alternative to full pulp removal.
- **Full pulpotomy** — removal of the entire coronal pulp (as opposed to partial pulpotomy), followed by capping of the radicular pulp stump.
- **MTA** — Mineral Trioxide Aggregate, a calcium silicate-based capping material.
- **EPV** — Events-Per-Variable, a regression-model-adequacy heuristic (≥10 recommended).
- **Nagelkerke R²** — pseudo-R² for logistic regression model fit; here 0.240 (weak-to-moderate).
