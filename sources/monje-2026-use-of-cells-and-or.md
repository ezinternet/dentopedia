## Why Ingested

[[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/suarez-lopez-del-amo-2022-efficacy-biologics-alveolar-ridge]]의 AAP Best Evidence Consensus (2022, Monje A 공동저자)는 생체치료제 (biologic)를 "조직 질 향상제(tissue-quality enhancer)"로 규정하면서 치조제 치수(dimensional) 개선 효과는 없다고 결론냈으나, 이질성이 커 메타분석을 포기하고 정성 종합에 그쳤다. 본 논문(Monje 2026)은 같은 senior author가 후속으로 수행한 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis, SR+MA)으로, 검색을 2024-11-01까지 확장하고 생체치료제+스캐폴드(scaffold) 조합의 치수·조직형태계측(histomorphometric) 효과량을 정량화한다 — 골증대(ridge augmentation) 치조골 폭(ridge width, RW) WMD = 0.30 mm (p < 0.001), 치조제 보존술(alveolar ridge preservation, ARP) RW WMD = 0.48 mm (p = 0.035)·치조골 높이(ridge height, RH) WMD = 0.40 mm (p < 0.001), 상악동거상술(maxillary sinus floor elevation, MSFA) RH WMD = 0.32 mm (p = 0.878). 또한 기존 위키에 없던 세포치료 (cell therapy, 골수 유래 배아간엽줄기세포) 축을 별도 PICO로 정리해 "안전하지만 우월성은 입증 미완"을 명시하므로, [[bone-regeneration/bubalo-2026-bone-substitutes-alveolar-ridge-augmentation]]가 서술형으로만 다루던 adjunct biologic 축을 정량 근거로 보강한다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review with meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024605421; MEDLINE/EMBASE/Cochrane CENTRAL to 1 Nov 2024, manual screening update 21 Sep 2025) of adjunctive biologics or cell therapy combined with scaffolds in alveolar bone augmentation for implant site development — 56 studies in qualitative synthesis, and 23 RCTs (n = 1078 patients) meta-analyzed: ridge augmentation 6 RCTs (n = 172), ridge preservation 9 RCTs (n = 455), sinus floor elevation 8 RCTs (n = 451).

Biologics gave a statistically significant but small dimensional advantage over scaffold-only controls in ridge augmentation (RW WMD = 0.30 mm, p < 0.001, I² = 0%; PRP subset 0.52 mm, p = 0.015, I² = 11%; RH not significant, WMD = 0.39 mm, p = 0.155) and in ridge preservation (RW WMD = 0.48 mm, p = 0.035, I² = 74%; RH WMD = 0.40 mm, p < 0.001, I² = 5%; rhBMP-2 RW WMD = 1.08 mm, p = 0.02, I² = 84.6%), with less non-mineralized tissue (p = 0.009), but no dimensional benefit in sinus floor elevation (RH WMD = 0.32 mm, p = 0.878, I² = 87%) despite significantly less residual graft (WMD = −5.54, p < 0.001); no major adverse events were reported for biologics or cell therapy.

Effect sizes are sub-millimetre and procedure-specific, heterogeneity reaches I² = 74–98% in several pools, publication bias was detected for ridge-preservation height (Egger p = 0.010) and sinus mineralized tissue (p = 0.022), all participants were systemically healthy, and cell-therapy superiority could not be validated because meta-analysis was infeasible — so the adjunctive biologic is best read as a modest add-on for ridge augmentation and ridge preservation, and as unsupported for sinus floor elevation.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis, SR+MA; PROSPERO CRD42024605421, MEDLINE·EMBASE·Cochrane CENTRAL 2024-11-01까지 + 2025-09-21 수동 갱신): 스캐폴드(scaffold)와 병용한 생체치료제(biologic) 및 세포치료(cell therapy)의 치조골 증강(alveolar bone augmentation) 유효성·안전성 평가 — 정성 종합 56편, 정량 분석 대상 무작위대조시험(Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) 23편(환자 n = 1078; 골증대 6편 n = 172, 치조제 보존 9편 n = 455, 상악동거상 8편 n = 451).

생체치료제 병용군이 스캐폴드 단독 대조군 대비 골증대에서 치조골 폭(ridge width, RW) 유의한 소폭 우위(WMD = 0.30 mm, p < 0.001, I² = 0%; 혈소판풍부혈장(platelet-rich plasma, PRP) 부분집단 0.52 mm, p = 0.015; 치조골 높이(ridge height, RH)는 유의하지 않음 WMD = 0.39 mm, p = 0.155), 치조제 보존술(alveolar ridge preservation, ARP)에서 RW WMD = 0.48 mm(p = 0.035, I² = 74%)·RH WMD = 0.40 mm(p < 0.001, I² = 5%)·재조합인간골형성단백질-2(recombinant human bone morphogenetic protein-2, rhBMP-2) RW WMD = 1.08 mm(p = 0.02)로 우위를 보였고 비광화 조직(non-mineralized tissue)이 감소(p = 0.009)했으나, 상악동거상술(maxillary sinus floor elevation, MSFA)은 RH WMD = 0.32 mm(p = 0.878)로 치수상 이득이 없었고 잔존 이식재(residual graft)만 감소(WMD = −5.54, p < 0.001)했다 — 생체치료제·세포치료 모두 중대 이상반응(major adverse event)은 보고되지 않았다.

효과량은 1 mm 미만의 소폭·시술 특이적이며 조직형태계측·치조제 보존 메타분석에서는 이질성이 I² = 74–98%까지 높고 교란편향(publication bias)이 검출되어(RH Egger p = 0.010, 상악동거상 광화조직 p = 0.022) 해석에 주의가 필요하다. 전 포함 환자가 전신건강(systemically healthy)이어서 당뇨병(diabetes mellitus)·자가면역(autoimmune) 질환 환자에 대한 적용 근거는 별도 문헌이 필요하며, 세포치료는 자료 수와 이질성 때문에 메타분석이 불가해 우월성이 입증되지 않았다 — 결론적으로 생체치료제는 골증대·치조제 보존에서의 소폭 보조제(adjunct)로, 상악동거상술에서는 근거가 없다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Quantifies what the 2022 AAP Best Evidence Consensus could not.** The authors explicitly frame this review as the quantitative successor to the 2022 AAP BEC (Suárez-López del Amo & Monje — same senior author), which was limited to qualitative synthesis because of heterogeneity; here 23 RCTs (n = 1078) are meta-analyzed with random-effects WMDs, I² and Egger's tests.
- **Splits the question into two PICO sets**: biologics (PICO 1, meta-analyzable) and cell therapy (PICO 2, meta-analysis judged infeasible). This is the first review in this corpus to treat mesenchymal-cell therapy as a separate evidence axis in alveolar bone augmentation rather than folding it into "biologics".
- **Procedure-stratified effect sizes**, not a single pooled answer: ridge augmentation, ridge preservation and sinus floor elevation are reported separately, and within each, agent-specific subsets are given for PRP (ridge augmentation), rhBMP-2 (ridge preservation) and autologous blood-derived products (ridge preservation).
- **Histology is meta-analyzed, not merely described**: % mineralized tissue, % non-mineralized tissue and % residual graft are pooled separately for ridge preservation and sinus floor elevation — the residual-graft signal (fewer residual particles with biologics) is the paper's most consistent histological finding.
- **Safety and PROs carried through every subgroup**: no major adverse events for biologics or cell therapy, with the one consistent toxicity signal being increased facial/intra- and extra-oral edema and prolonged swelling with rhBMP-2.
- **Implant-level outcomes acknowledged as out of scope for pooling**: marginal bone loss to 60 months and ISQ at placement and 18 months are reported narratively as comparable with and without adjunctive biologics.
- **Methodological recommendations**: adherence to the ID-COSM reporting standard for studies evaluating biologics or cell therapy in bone augmentation for implant site development.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

**Design**: systematic review with meta-analysis (PROSPERO-registered, PRISMA-reported). Two focused PICO questions — (1) biologics + scaffold vs scaffold ± guided bone regeneration (GBR) membrane; (2) cell therapy + scaffold/GBR vs scaffold/GBR without cell therapy.

**Eligibility**: randomized or non-randomized clinical trials with parallel-arm or split-mouth design, and prospective single-arm case series with ≥10 subjects per arm; systemically healthy subjects; any re-assessment modality for dimensional, histologic, PRO and adverse-event outcomes; no minimum follow-up. Excluded: retrospective or cross-sectional cohorts, case series, case reports, heavy smokers (≥10 cigarettes/day), and presence of (or lack of assessment for) systemic disease that could affect bone healing.

**Intervention classes (PICO 1)**: enamel matrix derivatives, bone morphogenetic proteins, autologous blood-derived products, platelet-derived growth factor — each combined with a scaffold, with or without GBR using resorbable or non-resorbable barrier membranes, simultaneous or staged to implant placement. Growth and differentiation factor-5 was excluded as not broadly commercialized.

**Outcomes**: primary — vertical and/or horizontal bone gain (radiographic or clinical) of residual defects (simultaneous placement) or at surgical re-entry (staged placement). Secondary — implant placement feasibility, implant stability (insertion torque / implant site development), implant survival, biological complications, adverse effects, patient-reported outcomes (PROs), clinician-reported outcomes and healing time, histomorphometry, and clinician-assessed subjective bone quality/density.

**Search**: MEDLINE (OVID), EMBASE (OVID), Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, and the Cochrane Oral Health Group Trials Register, for reports published up to 1 November 2024, plus grey literature (New York Academy of Medicine Grey Literature Report) and the NIH ClinicalTrials.gov registry; bibliographies of candidate papers were hand-searched. Two reviewers (AM, MS) searched independently and extracted data (M.S., C.S.); pivotal missing data were requested from corresponding authors and otherwise recorded as not reported (N.R.). An updated manual screening on 21 September 2025 added two further cell-therapy studies.

**Risk of bias**: RoB 2 (2019 version) for RCTs; ROBINS-I version 2 for non-randomized studies.

**Synthesis**: random-effects model with weighted mean difference (WMD) as the global effect measure, reported with Z-statistics, p-values and 95% confidence intervals; Cochran's Q for heterogeneity and I² (25% / 50% / 75% thresholds for low / medium / high); funnel plots with Egger's test for publication bias; subset analyses per individual biologic wherever data allowed. Network meta-analysis was not performed because control-group composition was too variable to support a validated NMA, and meta-analysis for cell-based therapies was judged infeasible given limited sample size and data heterogeneity. Software: Metafor in R 4.3.1; significance level α = 0.05. Evidence strength/recommendation grading used criteria adapted from the American Dental Association (ADA) Clinical Practice Guidelines.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Screening flow**: 3,532 records after duplicate removal, plus 9 additional records from reference lists = 3,541 evaluated; 80 assessed at full text; 66 included in qualitative analysis per the Results text (the abstract reports "68 eligible … 56 selected"). Thirty-one articles (n = 1,369) met the initial quantitative criteria and 8 were excluded for using biologics without a scaffold, leaving 23 RCTs (n = 1,078) in the meta-analysis: ridge augmentation 6 RCTs (n = 172), ridge preservation 9 RCTs (n = 455), sinus floor elevation 8 RCTs (n = 451). The 21 September 2025 manual update brought qualitative synthesis to 56 studies.

### 4.1 Ridge augmentation — biologics

| Outcome | Studies | Effect (WMD) | p | I² | Publication bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ridge width | 5 | +0.30 mm | < 0.001 | 0% | funnel symmetric, Egger p = 0.916 |
| Ridge width, PRP subset | 2 | +0.52 mm (reported as "WCD") | 0.015 | 11% | — |
| Ridge height | 2 | +0.39 mm | 0.155 (NS) | 0% | — |

Histologic meta-analysis was not feasible (insufficient reporting). Complications: 4 studies reported — no major complication in either arm, but more facial swelling with rhBMP-2; no major adverse events. PROs: 2 studies — reduced post-operative discomfort with platelet-rich fibrin (PRF); greater intra-/extra-oral sensitivity at suture removal and at 4 months with rhBMP-2; the control group showed lower willingness to repeat the surgery. Risk of bias was mid-to-low across the pool, with common concerns about absent trial registration and omitted complication reporting.

### 4.2 Ridge augmentation — cell therapy

Four studies; meta-analysis not feasible. Stem cells seeded on a synthetic bioabsorbable bone graft outperformed autogenous blocks on volume gain (p = 0.03); the remaining studies showed no superiority of cell therapy + scaffold over scaffold alone. No study reported histologic outcomes. Peri-operative adverse events: 2 studies — no major post-operative complications, but more dehiscences with stem-cell GBR than control (not statistically significant). PROs: 2 studies — high satisfaction and willingness to recommend.

### 4.3 Ridge preservation — biologics

| Outcome | Studies | Effect (WMD) | p | I² | Publication bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ridge width | 7 | +0.48 mm | 0.035 | 74% | funnel asymmetry, Egger p = 0.054 |
| Ridge width, rhBMP-2 subset | 3 | +1.08 mm | 0.02 | 84.6% | — |
| Ridge height | 6 | +0.40 mm | < 0.001 | 5% | **publication bias detected, Egger p = 0.010** |
| Ridge height, rhBMP-2 subset | 3 | +0.42 mm | 0.09 (marginal) | — | — |
| % mineralized tissue | 5 | +7.46% | 0.427 (NS) | 98% | Egger p = 0.379 |
| % non-mineralized tissue | 2 | −14.9% (i.e. control higher) | 0.009 | 77% | Egger p = 0.893 |
| % residual graft | 5 | −5.01% | 0.172 (NS) | 97% | Egger p = 0.948 |

Cell therapy (1 study): significant improvement in linear ridge height at 6 weeks (but not 12 weeks) versus conventional GBR, with a tendency toward less simultaneous bone grafting needed at early re-entry. Histology at 6 weeks showed a trend toward higher bone volume fraction and bone mineral density on micro-CT and higher bone area/tissue area on histomorphometry versus GBR; these differences were absent at 12 weeks. Adverse events: 3 studies — minor complications did not differ between groups, erythema most frequent, no major adverse events; cell-therapy data from 1 study showed no serious adverse events. PROs: 2 studies — lower post-operative VAS discomfort with biologics and high willingness to repeat the intervention.

### 4.4 Maxillary sinus floor elevation — biologics

| Outcome | Studies | Effect (WMD) | p | I² | Publication bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ridge height | 2 | +0.32 mm | 0.878 (NS) | 87% | funnel symmetric, Egger p = 0.672 |
| % mineralized tissue | 6 | +3.08% | 0.334 (NS) | 87% | **publication bias detected, Egger p = 0.022** |
| % non-mineralized tissue | 5 | — | 0.19 (NS) | 70% | — |
| % residual graft | 5 | −5.54 | < 0.001 | 21% | — |

Cell therapy: 4 studies reported dimensional changes with no difference from control; 3 studies reported histology with no significant difference in mineralized tissue or residual particles, one showing a higher percentage of mature bone with cell therapy. Adverse events: rhBMP-2 was associated with increased extra- and intra-oral edema and prolonged post-operative swelling, while post-operative pain and rash were substantially less common; no major adverse events. PROs: PRP was associated with increased willingness to undergo the procedure again. Four studies were rated high risk of bias (mostly for lack of a described randomization process); one non-randomized study was high risk because of substantial baseline imbalance in residual bone height.

### 4.5 Implant-related outcomes (not pooled)

Marginal bone loss on periapical radiographs was similar in simultaneously grafted sites with and without adjunctive biologics up to 60 months. Implant stability quotient (ISQ) was comparable between test and control at placement and at 18-month follow-up. Post-operative discomfort (VAS) was comparable, with only minor differences in swelling. No study of cell therapy met the inclusion criteria for implant-related outcomes.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Clinical and methodological heterogeneity is the dominant limitation.** Biologic type, surgical intervention, scaffold choice, application timing and dosage were inconsistent across trials; because of this, subset meta-analyses by biologic × scaffold combination were not feasible. The authors themselves advise cautious interpretation of the favorable pooled results and recommend reading each individual clinical scenario.
- **Non-randomized and comparative studies were admitted** to increase sample size and weight, with the acknowledged cost that "the quality and consistency of the outcomes might be partly sacrificed".
- **No within-patient clustering adjustment was possible**: some trials reported site-level outcomes, others patient-level summaries, and the available data did not allow clustering to be modelled.
- **Biologic preparation protocols were under-reported** (centrifugation method and time differ between autologous blood-derived products), limiting generalizability; the same is true for scaffold-specific biologic performance.
- **Generalizability ceiling**: every included patient was systemically healthy, so the review cannot speak to patients in whom wound healing or immune function is impaired (e.g. diabetes mellitus, autoimmune dysfunction) — the authors direct clinicians to seek additional literature and use clinical judgement for those groups.
- **Regression to the mean** is a specific statistical caveat raised for subgroups showing large versus minimal effect sizes.
- **Publication bias and imprecision are not negligible**: Egger's test flagged ridge-preservation ridge height (p = 0.010) and sinus mineralized tissue (p = 0.022), with I² of 74–98% in several pools, and the ridge-augmentation ridge-width pool was built on only five studies.
- **Cell-therapy claims cannot be graded**: meta-analysis was infeasible, so the "safe and effective" cell-therapy conclusion rests on small numbers of heterogeneous studies, and the authors themselves caution about toxicity, neoplasm formation and undesired immune reactions reported in the wider medical literature.
- **Reporting inconsistencies internal to the paper** (relevant when the numbers are reused downstream): the abstract describes 68 articles eligible and 56 selected while the Results report 66 included and 54 in qualitative synthesis before the September 2025 update; the Methods state the search was carried out "without language restrictions" whereas the electronic-search section states it was restricted to English-language publications; and the abstract attributes the ridge-augmentation benefit to PRP (p < 0.001) whereas the body assigns p < 0.001 to the overall biologic pool and p = 0.015 to the PRP subset.
- **Future work**: standardized protocols with proper randomization under equal clinical scenarios; adherence to the ID-COSM reporting standard; better characterization of biologic preparation protocols; longer follow-up (included assessments ranged from 3 to 18 months); and dedicated study of systemically compromised patients.

## 7. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| ISD (implant site development) | Procedures creating a recipient site adequate for implant placement in the optimal 3D position |
| Biologic | Therapeutic agent with biological activity (EMD, BMPs, autologous blood-derived products, PDGF) administered to enhance regeneration |
| Cell therapy | Use of bone-derived mesenchymal stem cells with a scaffold, as opposed to acellular biologics |
| EMD (enamel matrix derivative) | Enamel-derived protein fraction used as a bone-regeneration adjunct |
| rhBMP-2 (recombinant human bone morphogenetic protein-2) | Osteogenic growth factor, usually delivered on a collagen sponge |
| PRP / PRF / PRGF | Autologous blood-derived concentrates: platelet-rich plasma / platelet-rich fibrin / plasma rich in growth factors |
| GBR (guided bone regeneration) | Compartmentalization principle using barrier membranes to exclude fibroblasts and epithelium |
| Scaffold | Particulate graft or block that maintains space and is resorbed as bone replaces it (DBBM, allograft, autograft, alloplast) |
| RW / RH (ridge width / ridge height) | Horizontal and vertical residual-ridge dimensions, the primary outcomes of the review |
| WMD (weighted mean difference) | Pooled between-group difference in mm (or percentage points) between test and control |
| I² | Percentage of total variability attributable to between-study heterogeneity (25/50/75% = low/medium/high) |
| RoB 2 / ROBINS-I | Risk-of-bias tools for randomized and non-randomized studies respectively |
| PROs (patient-reported outcomes) | Pain, swelling, satisfaction, willingness to repeat or recommend |
| ISQ (implant stability quotient) | Resonance-frequency measure of implant stability, reported narratively in this review |
| ID-COSM | Reporting standard recommended for studies evaluating biologics or cell therapy in bone augmentation for ISD |
