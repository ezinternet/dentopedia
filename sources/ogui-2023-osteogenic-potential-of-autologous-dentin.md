## Why Ingested

The wiki's autologous dentin graft (ADG) evidence base for alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) currently rests on a pooled SR+MA ([[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/feng-2024-autogenous-particulated-dentin-graft-arp]]) and a 2026 DDM-vs-DBM RCT performed in premolar/molar sites ([[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/xu-2026-demineralized-dentin-matrix-versus-xenograft-arp-rct]]); none of the held pages quantify osteoinductive or inflammatory markers inside human grafted sockets. This RCT fills that gap in the esthetic zone: per the authors it is among the first human immunohistochemical reports of TNF-α and BMP-4 after ADG, and it adds a hard-tissue comparator arm (BX+AB) to the pilot human histology series in [[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/minetti-2020-alveolar-socket-preservation-autologous-graft]].

## Three-line Summary

Prospective randomized controlled clinical trial (37 patients: 20 ADG vs 17 bovine xenograft + autologous bone; 37 CBCT scans, 22 bone biopsies) comparing autologous dentin graft (ADG) with bovine xenograft mixed with autologous bone (BX+AB) for alveolar ridge preservation in the esthetic zone of the maxilla.

CBCT dimensions and histomorphometry were comparable between groups (test-group new bone 72.55 ± 12.14%; no significant differences in new bone, residual biomaterial or soft tissue), while immunohistochemistry showed significantly higher TNF-α intensity in the new-bone area (p = 0.0003) and around residual biomaterial (p = 0.0027) and higher BMP-4 around biomaterial particles (p = 0.0001) in the BX+AB group.

ADG is biocompatible and achieved osteoconductive/osteoinductive performance comparable to the gold-standard mixture while removing the autologous-bone harvest (shorter chair time, lower cost, less morbidity); the small single-center sample (37 patients, 22 biopsies, one surgeon), 4-month horizon and unblinded CBCT measurement limit certainty.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **First-in-kind human immunohistochemistry on ADG.** Per the authors, this is "one of the first immunohistochemical reports on the use of ADG in humans" and "one of the first studies to investigate TNF-α and BMP-4 expression in human alveolar bone" — the wiki held no page that quantifies osteoinductive or inflammatory markers inside human grafted sockets.
2. **Direct comparison against a hard-tissue benchmark.** ADG (autogenous dentin, prepared chairside) was tested against bovine xenograft (cerabone®) **mixed with locally harvested autologous bone** (BX+AB) — the combination the introduction calls the current practice standard for slowing autologous-bone resorption (mixed with xenograft + GBR membrane reduces autologous graft resorption by 50%, per the cited literature).
3. **Biopsy-based, not just radiographic.** 22 trephine bone biopsies (12 test / 10 control) taken at the 4-month re-entry allowed histomorphometry (new bone / residual biomaterial / soft tissue) plus quantitative immunostaining (mean grey value) in two anatomic compartments (new-bone area and around residual particles).
4. **A mechanism-level explanation of the between-group difference.** The only significant differences were in marker intensity, not in bone quantity: higher TNF-α (new bone p = 0.0003; around particles p = 0.0027) and higher BMP-4 around particles (p = 0.0001) in the BX+AB group, which the authors attribute to the added autologous bone (osteoinductivity) and to a stronger inflammatory reaction to xenogeneic material (active remodeling) that did not impair bone formation.
5. **A practical argument for ADG in the esthetic zone.** Same clinical outcome as the mixture, with no second donor site: shorter chair time (graft prepared by the assistant during surgery), no additional autologous bone harvest (less morbidity, fewer postoperative complications), lower cost.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: prospective randomized controlled clinical trial; 42 patients screened, 37 met inclusion criteria and gave written consent; 37 randomized (test n = 20, control n = 17). Randomization used a free online randomizer (randomizer.org, accessed 30 July 2022) performed **after flap elevation**.
- **Participants**: adults > 18 years, healthy, referred for extraction of at least one hopeless tooth in the anterior maxilla followed by implant treatment. Excluded: smokers (> 10 cigarettes/day), uncontrolled systemic disease, prior therapeutic head/neck radiotherapy, pregnancy or lactation. One extraction site per participant (maxillary incisors, canines, premolars). Preoperative periodontal treatment, oral hygiene instruction, antibiotic prophylaxis (875 mg penicillin + 125 mg clavulanic acid, or 600 mg clindamycin on allergy), 0.2% chlorhexidine rinse.
- **Surgical protocol (both arms)**: atraumatic extraction, socket curettage, bone-wall integrity check (only sockets with three intact walls included), full-thickness flap elevation, resorbable collagen membrane (Biogide®, Geistlich) placed under the buccal flap, primary closure with a palatal connective tissue graft and resorbable 6.0 monofilament sutures.
- **Test material — ADG**: extracted teeth cleaned with a high-speed carbide bur (fillings, calculus, caries, periodontal ligament, discoloured dentin and part of enamel removed), ground in a Smart Dentin Grinder (SDG, KometaBio) programmed for **300–1200 µm** particles, immersed 5 min in an alcohol-based basic cleaner, dried, rinsed twice in sterile phosphate-buffered saline. The protocol used follows the manufacturer protocol that yields a **demineralized dentin matrix (DDM)** — the authors note DDM's irregular surface and exposed collagen fibres favour osteoblast adhesion.
- **Control material — BX+AB**: bovine xenograft cerabone® (botiss biomaterials; bovine trabecular bone, > 1200 °C heat treatment, high porosity) mixed with autologous bone harvested locally around the surgical site.
- **Follow-up**: clinical checks at 10, 30 and 60 days; at 4 months patients were called for implant treatment (BEGO Semados RSXPro); all patients proceeded to implant therapy and all mucosa/bone had healed by 4 months.
- **Radiology**: CBCT (Planmeca Promax 3D; 0.3 mm resolution; 8.5 s scan, 4 s exposure, 120 kV, 5 mA) before extraction and at 4 months, identical configuration; ridge width defined as the distance between the most prominent buccal and oral points; 37 scans (20 test / 17 control) measured by a single investigator (M.O. — the operating surgeon).
- **Biopsy**: 22 trephine biopsies (diameter 2.3–2.8 mm) harvested at 4 months during implant bed preparation, fixed in 4% buffered paraformaldehyde.
- **Histology**: decalcification (Osteofast 2), paraffin embedding, 5 µm sections, H&E; 10× photomicrographs analysed in ImageJ v1.53 for percentages of new bone (NB), residual biomaterial (BM) and soft tissue (ST).
- **Immunohistochemistry**: anti-TNF-α (rabbit polyclonal, ab6671) and anti-BMP-4 (rabbit polyclonal, ab124715); 0.3% H₂O₂, citrate-buffer antigen retrieval, DAB visualization (ab64238); 200× photomicrographs processed in cellSense v3.2 (intensity separation → black/white conversion → background subtraction → mean grey value) in two regions of interest (new bone; around residual particles). Histology and IHC were performed **blind** by one investigator (O.C.P.).
- **Statistics**: Kolmogorov–Smirnov test (data normally distributed); mean ± SD; Student's t-test (two-tailed); IBM SPSS Statistics 25; p < 0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Clinical**: uneventful healing in all patients; 5 test and 3 control patients reported moderate discomfort, pain and swelling; no exposed wounds and no leakage of biomaterial into the oral cavity; at 4 months oral mucosa and underlying bone had healed completely in all patients, and all transitioned to implant therapy.
- **CBCT (37 scans)**: no statistically significant difference between groups in the dimensional change of alveolar ridge width from pre-extraction to 4 months (group means are tabulated in Table 5 of the paper, which is not part of the retrieved text artifact and therefore not reproduced here).
- **Histology (22 biopsies)**: new bone (NB) extended from the apical to the coronal part of every specimen in both groups, with lamellar organization and numerous osteocytes in lacunae; residual biomaterial lay in close contact with NB in both groups; fibroblasts were present in soft tissue; **no inflammatory reaction was observed in any group**. Histomorphometry showed no statistically significant differences between groups in the percentages of NB, BM and ST (Table 3).
- **Histomorphometric benchmark (test group)**: **new bone 72.55 ± 12.14%** at 4 months. The authors place this alongside a clinical study of ADG under GBR reporting 63% new bone and 25% residual biomaterial at 7 months.
- **Immunohistochemistry**: TNF-α- and BMP-4-positive cells were present in the new-bone area in both groups, in osteocytes embedded in new bone and in osteoblasts on the bone surface; intensity was measured both in the new-bone area and around residual biomaterial particles.
- **Significant between-group differences (BX+AB > ADG)** — the only significant results of the trial:
  - TNF-α intensity, new-bone area — **p = 0.0003**
  - TNF-α intensity, around residual biomaterial — **p = 0.0027**
  - BMP-4 intensity, around residual biomaterial — **p = 0.0001**
- **Literature comparisons drawn by the authors**: Pang et al. compared Bio-Oss® xenograft with ADG and found no significant difference in preserved bone volume (consistent with this study); Li et al. reported comparable marginal bone resorption with ADG vs BX at 6 and 18 months; Pohl et al. reported alveolar ridge width changes of −1.38 ± 1.24 mm (1 mm below crest), −0.82 ± 1.13 mm (3 mm) and −0.43 ± 0.89 mm (5 mm) for ADG with platelet-rich fibrin (PRF).
- **Background figures cited**: horizontal ridge loss of 29–63% and vertical loss of 11–22% in the first 6 months after extraction; with a buccal wall ≤ 1 mm an alveolus loses 7.5 mm in height at 2 months vs 1 mm when > 1 mm (Chappuis et al.); autologous-bone complications 0.5–10.5% with rapid resorption in 5–28%; xenografts carry a theoretical prion-transmission risk, while only BMPs can independently induce de novo bone formation at orthotopic and heterotopic sites (Yeomans & Urist, 1967).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Authors' stated limitations**: the number of analysed CBCT scans and bone biopsies is small; only the anterior maxilla (esthetic zone — incisors, canines, premolars) was studied, which the authors argue justifies the smaller sample; the TNF-α/BMP-4 differences require further investigation and should be the focus of future clinical studies.
- **Additional appraisal-level limitations (derived from the reported methods)**:
  - **No allocation concealment is reported** — randomization was performed with a free online randomizer after flap elevation, with no sequence concealment, registration, or blinding of the surgeon; the trial reports no intention-to-treat analysis and no CONSORT flow accounting beyond screening (42 → 37).
  - **CBCT measurement was not blinded** — the single investigator who measured ridge dimensions (M.O.) is the operating surgeon; only the histology/IHC readouts were masked.
  - **No graft-free control** — the comparator is a mixture (xenograft + autologous bone), so the study does not separate the effect of ADG from the effect of the added autograft, nor does it test ADG against a pure xenograft.
  - **Short horizon** — the readout ends at 4 months, at the point of implant placement; no implant survival, marginal bone or esthetic outcome is reported.
  - **Per-protocol biopsies** — 22 of 37 patients contributed histology (12 test / 10 control); the per-group numbers of the morphometric and immunohistochemical comparisons are not stated in the text artifact.
  - **Single center, single surgeon** — generalizability is limited to the anterior maxilla and to one operator's technique.
- **Future work suggested by the authors**: clinical studies designed to investigate the significant TNF-α and BMP-4 differences between the two materials.

## 7. Glossary

- **ADG (Autologous Dentin Graft)**: autogenous graft made from the patient's own extracted tooth, ground into particles; here 300–1200 µm, EDTA-demineralized to yield a **DDM (Demineralized Dentin Matrix)**.
- **BX+AB (Bovine Xenograft + Autologous Bone)**: control arm — cerabone® bovine trabecular bone xenograft mixed with locally harvested autologous bone.
- **DDM (Demineralized Dentin Matrix)**: demineralized tooth matrix; irregular surface with exposed collagen fibres favouring osteoblast adhesion.
- **ARP (Alveolar Ridge Preservation)**: grafting the extraction socket to limit post-extraction ridge resorption.
- **NB / BM / ST**: new bone, residual biomaterial, soft tissue (histomorphometric compartments).
- **TNF-α (Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha)**: pro-inflammatory cytokine required to initiate bone healing and, with RANKL, involved in bone remodeling; here measured by IHC intensity (mean grey value).
- **BMP-4 (Bone Morphogenetic Protein-4)**: TGF-β-family member; with BMP-2 and BMP-7 it is one of only three BMPs that stimulate bone formation, and its presence is the histologic evidence of osteoinductivity.
- **Osteoconduction vs osteoinduction**: growth along a scaffold vs induction of new bone from undifferentiated mesenchymal cells.
- **Mean grey value**: image-analysis output used here to quantify immunostaining intensity.
- **CBC (Bone-Implant Contact)** is not assessed in this trial — the readout ends at implant placement.
