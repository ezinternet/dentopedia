## Three-line Summary


Examiner-blind split-mouth RCT (30 patients, 60 NCCL restorations, 18-month follow-up) found no significant difference between rubber dam and cotton roll/retraction cord isolation for retention rate (73% vs 73% at 18 months), gingival damage, patient preference, or chairside time.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Influence of Isolation Method of the Operative Field on Gingival Damage, Patients' Preference, and Restoration Retention in Noncarious Cervical Lesions
- **Authors**: Alessandro D Loguercio, Issis Luque-Martinez, Alessandro H Lisboa, Cristian Higashi, Vania A Oliveira Queiroz, Rodrigo O Rego, Alessandra Reis
- **Journal**: Operative Dentistry, 2015, 40-6, 581-593
- **DOI**: 10.2341/14-089-C
- **Study type**: Examiner-blind, split-mouth randomized clinical trial (RCT)
- **Setting**: Clinic of the School of Dentistry, State University of Ponta Grossa (Paraná, Brazil), April–November 2010

## 2. Key Contributions

- Directly compares rubber dam (RD) vs cotton rolls/retraction cord (CR/RC) isolation specifically for noncarious cervical lesion (NCCL) restorations, a setting where dental-retainer placement is considered more challenging than for other cavity classes.
- Split-mouth, examiner-blind, prospective design (most prior evidence on this question was observational, not prospective).
- Reports retention, gingival damage (bleeding, laceration, insertion level, sensitivity), patient preference, and chairside time as a coordinated outcome set, evaluated with FDI criteria out to 18 months.
- Incidentally documents an unusually low overall 18-month retention rate (73%) for the one-step self-etch adhesive (GO!, SDI) used, independent of isolation method.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Examiner-blind, split-mouth RCT; CONSORT-reported; local ethics approval (protocol 6291/06 / approval code 06231/09).
- **Participants**: 30 subjects (12 men, 18 women; mean age 45y) each with a matched pair of similar-sized Class V NCCLs on opposing sides of the same arch, no undercuts, occlusal contacts present, ≤50% cavo-surface margin in enamel. Of 51 screened, 21 excluded (poor oral hygiene, periodontitis criteria 2/3, heavy bruxism, spontaneous hypersensitivity).
- **Intervention**: In each patient, isolation method for the two matched lesions was randomized by coin toss — one restored under rubber dam (RD, dental retainer No. 212) and the other under cotton rolls/retraction cord (CR/RC, mouth retractor + retraction cord) — both restored same-day with the same self-etch adhesive (GO!, SDI) and composite resin (Ice, SDI), no cavity preparation/bevel per ADA guidelines. Operators were calibrated dental residents (>10y clinical experience); patient/operator not blinded to isolation method, but the outcome examiner was.
- **Outcome measures**: Patient preference (post-hoc question); gingival laceration (yes/no) immediately and at 1 week; gingival bleeding/insertion level (Löe & Silness Gingival Index) at 1 week; gingival sensitivity (yes/no) at 1 week; chairside time (isolation start to final polish); FDI criteria (marginal staining, fractures/retention, marginal adaptation, postoperative sensitivity, recurrent caries) at baseline, 6, 12, 18 months, scored by two blinded, calibrated examiners (≥85% intra/inter-examiner agreement required).
- **Statistics**: McNemar test (preference, anesthesia need, laceration, bleeding, sensitivity); Wilcoxon signed-rank test (gingival insertion level); paired Student t-test (chairside time); Fisher exact test (FDI ratings between groups at each recall) and McNemar test (each recall vs baseline); α=0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Retention**: 6-month 93% (79–98%) both groups; 12-month RD 90% (74–96%) vs CR/RC 86% (70–95%); 18-month RD 73% (55–86%) vs CR/RC 73% (55–86%) — no significant difference at any timepoint (P>0.05), but both groups showed significant decline from baseline at 12 and 18 months (P<0.05). Overall 18-month retention across both groups was only 73%.
- **Patient preference**: RD preferred by 15/28 (54%, 95% CI 36–71%), CR/RC by 13/28 (46%, 30–64%) — not significant (P=0.86); 2 patients slept during the procedure and expressed no preference.
- **Chairside time**: RD 20.8±5.2 min vs CR/RC 21.2±5.2 min (P=0.77).
- **Anesthesia need**: ~70% both groups, no difference (P=1.00).
- **Gingival damage**: laceration RD 63% vs CR/RC 37% (P=0.64); 1-week bleeding RD 60% vs CR/RC 47% (P=0.57); 1-week sensitivity RD 43% vs CR/RC 27% (P=0.52) — none significant, though RD trended numerically higher for laceration/bleeding.
- **Gingival insertion level**: median 1mm (0–3) at baseline for both groups; no significant baseline (P=0.82) or 1-week (P=0.56) difference between groups; damage reversible (1-week level ≈ baseline for both).
- **FDI criteria (marginal staining, fractures/retention, marginal adaptation)**: no significant between-group difference at any recall (P>0.05); no postoperative sensitivity or recurrent caries observed in either group at any timepoint.
- **Conclusion**: Null hypothesis accepted — CR/RC isolation performs similarly to RD for NCCL adhesive restorations across all measured outcomes.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Convenience sample from a single university clinic; operators were experienced (>10y) residents supervised by an expert clinician — results may not generalize to less-experienced practitioners or private-practice settings (a recognized limitation given practice-based research shows lower rubber dam adoption/skill).
- Patient/operator not blinded to isolation method (only the outcome examiner was), and preference was assessed only immediately post-procedure, not longitudinally.
- Generalizable only to low-caries-risk patients (poor oral hygiene/active caries were exclusion criteria).
- The unusually low 18-month retention (73%) for the specific adhesive (GO!, SDI, a HEMA-free one-step self-etch system) appears to reflect adhesive-specific bonding weaknesses (thin hybrid layer, phase separation, low degree of conversion reported in prior lab studies) rather than an isolation-method effect — authors note other studies report ~85% retention at 3 years for the same product family with longer light-cure times and selective enamel etching, neither of which was used here.
- Authors flag that whether isolation-method equivalence holds in practice-based settings (less-trained clinicians) or for cavity types with more challenging moisture control remains to be tested.

## 6. Related Work

- Miao et al. 2021 (Cochrane review, general restorative treatment) — broader evidence on rubber dam isolation across restorative contexts; this study narrows to the NCCL-specific clinical scenario.
- Heintze & Rousson 2012 meta-analysis (105 studies, Class II restorations) — found a nonsignificant trend toward higher retention with rubber dam, consistent with this study's direction (if not magnitude) of effect.
- Daudt et al. 2013 — prior NCCL isolation-method study cited as showing rubber dam causes significantly higher immediate gingival recession that resolves by 1 week, consistent with this study's laceration/bleeding findings being non-persistent.
- van Dijken & Hörstedt 1987 — earlier study on rubber dam vs cotton roll effects on marginal adaptation of composite fillings to acid-etched enamel.

## 7. Glossary

- **NCCL (Noncarious Cervical Lesion)**: tooth-structure loss at the cervical (gumline) region not caused by caries (e.g., abfraction, abrasion, erosion).
- **RD**: Rubber dam isolation.
- **CR/RC**: Cotton rolls/retraction cord isolation.
- **FDI criteria**: World Dental Federation clinical evaluation criteria for restorations (esthetic, functional, biological properties), rated VG/GO/SS/UN/PO.
- **Split-mouth design**: within-patient comparison where each patient receives both interventions (here, one per matched lesion), controlling for patient-level confounders.
- **One-step self-etch adhesive**: adhesive system combining etching and priming in a single application step (here, GO!, SDI Limited).
