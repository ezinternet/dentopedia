## Why Ingested

Most virtual-interocclusal-record (VIR) accuracy studies in this wiki (e.g. [[occlusion/didier-2026-occlusal-contacts-ios-vs-carbon-paper-concordance]] vs carbon paper) validate the digital record against an imperfect clinical proxy. Mei 2022 instead scans a mechanically-articulated resin master cast with an **industrial CT system** as the reference standard — an independent ground truth, not another imperfect method — then tests 9 defined intraoral-scanner (IOS) scanning strategies against it. The finding that accuracy is *regional* (best near the scanned span, worse with wider/full-arch spans) gives the wiki its first technique-parameter-level guidance on how to place a bite scan, not just whether IOS is "accurate enough."

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro accuracy study (five pairs of reference cubes embedded in a volunteer's digitized dentitions, printed as resin casts, articulated at maximal intercuspal position, MIP) that used an industrial computed tomography (CT) system — not carbon paper or a second intraoral scanner — as the reference virtual interocclusal record (VIR), then compared nine intraoral-scanner (IOS) scanning strategies against it via six reference-point deviations on the XY-plane.

Accuracy was regional, not global: right-posterior-only scanning (RP) gave the smallest deviation on the right side, bilateral-posterior scanning (BP) gave the smallest deviation on the left side and across the full arches, and left-posterior scanning (LP) gave the (weakly, non-significantly) smallest anterior deviation; wider spans and full-arch scans (CA1/CA2) produced larger deviations than confined unilateral scans.

The practical rule: where the buccal-bite scan is placed determines where the resulting virtual articulation is accurate, so BP is the safest general-purpose default for full-arch work, while single-side posterior work (e.g. one implant crown) is best served by scanning only that side; because the reference was an independent industrial CT rather than an imperfect proxy, this in-vitro result carries more methodological weight than its design tier alone would suggest.

## 세줄요약

가상 교합기록(Virtual Interocclusal Record, VIR) 정확도 실험실 연구 — 지원자 치열의 디지털 모형에 기준 큐브 5쌍을 삽입해 레진 모형으로 출력하고, 최대교두감합위(Maximal Intercuspal Position, MIP)로 교합기에 부착한 뒤, 산업용 컴퓨터단층촬영(Computed Tomography, CT) 장비로 스캔한 결과를 기준 VIR로 삼았다 — 카본지나 두 번째 구강스캐너가 아니라 독립적 산업용 CT를 기준으로 삼은 점이 특징. 이후 9가지 구강스캐너(Intraoral Scanner, IOS) 스캔 전략을 이 기준과 비교했다(상악 기준 큐브의 기준점 6개, XY평면까지의 편차로 측정).

정확도는 부위별로 달랐다 — 우측 구치부는 우측 구치부만 스캔(RP)이 편차 최소, 좌측 구치부와 전체 치열은 양측 구치부 스캔(BP)이 편차 최소, 전치부는 좌측 구치부 스캔(LP)이 (통계적으로는 CA2 대비만 유의한 약한 차이로) 편차 최소였다. 스캔 범위가 넓을수록, 특히 전체 치열 스캔(CA1/CA2)일수록 편차가 컸다.

실무 규칙: 협측 교합 스캔을 어디에 위치시키느냐가 가상 교합의 정확도가 어디서 나오는지를 결정한다 — 전체 치열 작업의 기본값으로는 BP(양측 구치부)가 가장 안전하고, 단측 구치부 작업(예: 임플란트 단일 크라운)은 해당 측만 스캔하는 것이 낫다. 카본지·교합지 같은 불완전한 대리 기준이 아니라 독립적 산업용 CT를 기준으로 삼았기 때문에, 이 실험실 연구는 연구설계 등급(in-vitro) 이상의 방법론적 무게를 가진다.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **Independent ground truth, not an imperfect proxy.** The reference VIR came from an industrial CT scan (Zeiss Metrotom 1500 G3) of the mechanically-articulated resin casts, not from carbon paper, articulating paper, or a second IOS pass. This is methodologically stronger than the agreement-vs-imperfect-reference designs the wiki otherwise holds (e.g. [[occlusion/didier-2026-occlusal-contacts-ios-vs-carbon-paper-concordance]] vs carbon paper).
2. **Nine scanning strategies systematically compared**: right posterior (RP), left posterior (LP), bilateral posterior (BP), right quadrant (RQ), left quadrant (LQ), bilateral quadrant (BQ), complete arch 1 (CA1), complete arch 2 (CA2), and anterior (ANT). The precise tooth-boundary definitions of each strategy were given only in a figure/table that the PMC full-text extraction did not return (see Methodology note below); the group names and the 90-sample (9 groups × 10 repetitions) design are confirmed in the body text.
3. **Region-specific accuracy law**: deviation is smallest near the scanned span and larger away from it — the discussion attributes this to a "tilting effect toward the site of the VIR of a single section" (citing Edher), i.e. a rotational/lever-type error propagating from the unscanned region. This mechanism is explicitly stated for the right-posterior/left-posterior asymmetry (RP/RQ: δR ≪ δL; LP: δL ≪ δR); it is not explicitly re-derived for the anterior/LP finding, which was also the statistically weakest result (see below).
4. **Actionable default**: BP (bilateral posterior) is the best general-purpose choice when the full arch matters; single-side posterior restorative work (e.g. one implant or single-crown case) is best served by scanning only the ipsilateral side, and RP ≈ RQ / LP ≈ LQ (no significant difference), so the shorter quadrant scan is recommended for time-saving without an accuracy penalty.
5. **Full-arch scans are consistently worse than confined scans.** CA1 and CA2 (two full-arch scanning directions, not significantly different from each other — direction alone does not matter) had larger δD, δR, and δL than the confined unilateral/bilateral-posterior strategies, attributed to image-stitching error accumulating over a wider captured span.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Reference cast preparation**: One volunteer (male, 20 y, full dentition, stable MIP, normal overjet/overbite) scanned with TRIOS 3 (3Shape) → STL. Five pairs of reference cubes (cubes 1–10) added digitally to the labial/buccal gingival surface of the maxillary and mandibular casts in Geomagic 15.0. The upper surfaces of the five mandibular cubes (1–5) define the XY-plane (paralleled laterally). STL files 3D-printed into acrylic-resin master casts (DLP1080E printer).
- **Articulation**: Resin casts articulated in MIP (full-dentition determined) in a mechanical articulator (Artex CPR, Amann Girrbach), then locked in hinge movement to prevent mandibular deviation during closure.
- **Reference VIR**: The fully articulated resin casts scanned by an industrial CT system (Metrotom 1500 G3, Zeiss) — reported to have measurement accuracy comparable to a coordinate measuring machine — exported as STL. This is the ground truth against which all 9 IOS strategies are compared.
- **Investigated VIRs**: Each of the 9 strategies scanned once per repetition (10 repetitions per group = 90 STL samples total), IOS used per manufacturer instructions, **no rescanning**. The reference cubes themselves were excluded from the VIR scan (only teeth scanned) specifically to avoid the known confound that artificial landmarks bias IOS accuracy.
- **Outcome measurement**: Six reference points (points 1–6; three-plane intersections, Gaussian best-fit) on the five maxillary reference cubes (cubes 6–10); distance from each point to the XY-plane (distances 1–6) measured in GOM Inspect 2020. Deviation of each investigated sample from the CT reference at distances 1–6 = δ1–δ6. Grouped as: δR = mean(δ1,δ2) (right posterior), δA = mean(δ3,δ4) (anterior), δL = mean(δ5,δ6) (left posterior), δD = mean(δ1–δ6) (full arches).
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk (all four outcomes non-normally distributed) → Kruskal-Wallis across the 9 groups, Bonferroni-corrected post hoc pairwise comparisons, α = .05.
- **Extraction note**: the PMC full-text return dropped figure/table numbers and several in-text cross-references (e.g. "displayed in," "(and)"), which is where the exact per-strategy tooth-boundary definitions and the numeric δ values (in µm) would have appeared. The body text confirms exact strategy names, sample sizes, and statistical comparisons, but not the raw magnitudes — only the general statement that deviations fell within the literature's previously reported 60–200 µm trueness/precision range for complete-arch digital scans.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome (region) | Smallest-deviation group | NOT significantly different from | Significantly worse comparators |
|---|---|---|---|
| δR — right posterior | **RP** | RQ, BP, BQ | LP, LQ, ANT, CA1, CA2 |
| δL — left posterior | **BP** | LP, LQ, BQ | RP, RQ, ANT, CA1, CA2 |
| δA — anterior | **LP** (weak effect) | all groups except CA2 | CA2 only |
| δD — full arches | **BP** | RP, LP, RQ, LQ, BQ, ANT | CA1, CA2 |

- Within RP and RQ, δR was significantly smaller than δL (right-sided scan → right-sided accuracy advantage); within LP, δL was significantly smaller than δR (mirror effect); LQ showed no significant δR/δL difference.
- CA1 vs CA2 (the two full-arch scanning directions) did not differ significantly on any outcome — scanning direction itself does not matter, only span/section strategy does.
- The anterior/δA result is the statistically weakest finding in the paper: LP was only significantly better than CA2, and not different from the other seven groups — this nuances the "LP best for anterior" headline considerably.
- Exact numeric δ values (µm) per group were not recoverable from the returned full text (see Methodology note); only the rank/significance structure above is confirmed.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work (as stated by the authors, plus wiki-added caveats)

- Single volunteer, single IOS system (TRIOS 3) — design does not replicate clinical variability (soft tissue, saliva, mandibular flexure, muscle-driven jaw closure) and may not generalize to other scanners.
- Reference cubes, not natural tooth anatomy, served as the measured landmarks; cubes were excluded from the VIR scan itself to avoid landmark bias, but the underlying casts are full dentitions, not edentulous ridges.
- The abstract's closing phrase — "Appropriate scanning strategies of VIR should be selected for different regions of interest **and edentulous situations**" — is a generalized recommendation in the Conclusion, not a tested condition of this study. The Discussion cites another group's (Ren's) finding that edentulous spans of 3+ missing posterior teeth affect VIR accuracy, but this study's own design used a fully dentate cast throughout; **do not extrapolate these specific strategy recommendations to edentulous jaws** — that is outside what this design tested.
- This study did not explicitly exclude scanning-process errors intrinsic to the IOS itself.
- Authors call for further clinical studies validating the indications across a full digital workflow, and for assessing accuracy when the digital-cast alignment step is done manually vs automatically.

## 6. Related Work (as cited in the paper)

- **Solaberrieta et al.** — used an industrial 3D scanner (not an IOS) and full-dentition-scan sectioning to recommend combining right+left lateral sections; results don't necessarily transfer to IOS-based capture, which is the gap this study fills.
- **Revilla-León et al.** — recommended a bilateral interocclusal record of only 2–3 teeth over 4 teeth, but did not test spans wider than 4 teeth (this study's wider-span comparisons extend that finding).
- **Ren; Iwaki** — a single missing tooth in complete-arch scanning does not affect VIR accuracy; Ren additionally found that unilateral/bilateral extended edentulous spans (3+ missing posterior teeth) and extended anterior edentulous spans do affect accuracy.
- **Edher** — reported a "tilting effect" toward the site of a single-section VIR in full-dentition quadrant-vs-complete-arch comparisons; cited as the likely mechanism for this study's regional accuracy pattern.

## 7. Glossary

- **VIR** — Virtual interocclusal record: the 3D digital record of the relationship between maxillary and mandibular casts in a target occlusal position (e.g. MIP).
- **MIP** — Maximal intercuspal position, the recording position used throughout this study.
- **IOS** — Intraoral scanner (here TRIOS 3, 3Shape).
- **RP / LP / BP / RQ / LQ / BQ / CA1 / CA2 / ANT** — the 9 scanning strategies: right posterior, left posterior, bilateral posterior, right quadrant, left quadrant, bilateral quadrant, complete arch (direction 1), complete arch (direction 2), anterior.
- **δR / δA / δL / δD** — mean deviation (vs the industrial-CT reference) for the right posterior, anterior, left posterior, and full-arch regions respectively.
- **Industrial CT reference standard** — an independent metrology-grade ground truth (Zeiss Metrotom 1500 G3), distinct from clinical proxies like carbon/articulating paper used in most other VIR/occlusion-agreement studies in this wiki.
