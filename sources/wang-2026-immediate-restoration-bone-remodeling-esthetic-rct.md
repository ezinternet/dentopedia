## Three-line Summary

Parallel-group randomized controlled trial (n=20, anterior maxilla trauma requiring single immediate implant placement; Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital) comparing immediate restoration using the patient's native crown stabilized with fiber-reinforced composite (FRC) splints versus conventional delayed restoration after immediate implant placement (IIP).

At 4–5 months (CBCT/ISQ follow-up), implant survival was 100% in both groups with complete soft-tissue closure; no significant intergroup differences were found in peri-implant bone changes at the implant neck (P=0.878) or 2 mm apically (P=0.667), and mean ISQ values were similar (75.6 ± 2.4 vs 74.1 ± 3.4, P=0.271).

Clinical implication: an FRC-splinted native-crown immediate restoration (esthetic rehabilitation without direct implant loading) showed no additional adverse effect on early peri-implant bone remodeling or osseointegration in this pilot RCT, but the short observation period and small sample warrant cautious interpretation.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Evaluated a **non-loaded immediate esthetic restoration** protocol after IIP using the patient's native crown bonded to adjacent teeth with FRC splints.
- Provided RCT pilot data comparing this approach vs delayed restoration on early peri-implant bone remodeling and stability.
- Addressed scenarios where conventional immediately loaded provisionalization is limited by insufficient primary stability.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Parallel-group randomized controlled clinical trial (pilot/exploratory).
- **Population**: 20 participants with traumatic injuries requiring single immediate implant placement in the maxillary anterior (esthetic zone).
- **Interventions**:
  - Experimental: immediate restoration using patient's native crown stabilized with FRC splints (no direct occlusal loading on implant).
  - Control: conventional delayed restoration.
- **Primary outcome**: Labial bone plate change (CBCT) at baseline and 4–5 months postoperatively.
- **Secondary outcomes**: Implant survival, implant stability quotient (ISQ), gingival healing/soft-tissue closure.
- **Timepoint**: 4–5 months postoperative assessment.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Survival**: 100% in both groups.
- **Soft tissue**: Complete soft-tissue closure within 4–5 months in all cases; no significant intergroup differences reported.
- **Bone remodeling (CBCT)**: No significant intergroup differences at implant neck (P=0.878) or 2 mm apically (P=0.667).
- **ISQ**: Mean 75.6 ± 2.4 (experimental) vs 74.1 ± 3.4 (control), P=0.271 (NS).
- **Conclusion (authors)**: No additional adverse effects on early peri-implant bone remodeling or osseointegration within observation period; potential alternative for immediate esthetic rehabilitation after IIP, especially when primary stability is limited.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Pilot/exploratory sample (n=20) and short follow-up (4–5 months).
- Outcomes focused on early bone remodeling/ISQ; longer-term esthetic, prosthetic, and biologic outcomes not established in this report.
- Generalizability limited to described trauma/IIP context and protocol.

## 7. Glossary
- **IIP (Immediate implant placement)**: Implant placement into a fresh extraction socket at the time of tooth extraction.
- **FRC splint (fiber-reinforced composite splint)**: Resin-bonded splint reinforced with fibers used to stabilize teeth/restorations.
- **ISQ (Implant Stability Quotient)**: Resonance frequency analysis measure of implant stability.
- **CBCT (cone-beam computed tomography)**: 3D radiographic imaging used to assess peri-implant bone dimensions.
