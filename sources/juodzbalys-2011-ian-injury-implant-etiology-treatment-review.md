## Why Ingested

하악 임플란트 식립 중 발생하는 하치조신경 (Inferior Alveolar Nerve, IAN) 손상의 원인, 기전, 증상, 진단, 치료를 포괄적으로 정리한 초기 문헌 리뷰 — 136편 검토 기반으로 임상 가이드라인을 최초로 체계화한 참조 논문. [[oral-surgery/lee-2015-inferior-alveolar-nerve-injury-management]] 등 이후 관련 문헌들이 이 분류 체계를 인용함.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative literature review (136 sources, 1972–2010) synthesizing IAN injury aetiology, mechanisms, clinical presentation, diagnostic methods, and treatment in the context of dental implant placement.

IAN injury incidence reported as 0–40%; classified into intraoperative (mechanical, thermal, chemical) and postoperative (thermal, infection, haematoma/scarring) causes; nerve damage grades range from neuropraxia to neurotmesis.

Pre-surgical CBCT-based planning, maintaining ≥2 mm safety margin from mandibular canal, and early intervention (implant removal/repositioning within 30–36 h if persistent symptoms) are the key clinical takeaways.

## 세줄요약

문헌 서술적 고찰 (136편, 1972–2010) — 임플란트 식립 관련 하치조신경 손상의 원인·기전·증상·진단·치료를 종합 정리.

IAN 손상 발생률 0–40% 보고; 원인은 수술 중(기계적·열적·화학적)과 수술 후(열·감염·혈종/반흔)로 분류; 신경 손상 등급은 신경실조(neuropraxia)부터 신경절단(neurotmesis)까지.

술 전 CBCT 계획·하악관 ≥2 mm 안전 거리 확보, 증상 지속 시 30–36시간 내 임플란트 제거·재위치가 핵심 임상 권고사항.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic classification of IAN injury aetiology into intraoperative (mechanical, thermal, chemical) and postoperative (thermal, infection, haematoma) categories
- Proposed structured clinical examination protocol for sensory nerve assessment (subjective complaint, neurosensory tests: tactile, pain, two-point discrimination, directional, thermal)
- Developed clinical management guidelines: presurgery (risk assessment, CBCT, safety margin), intraoperative (avoiding MC), and postoperative (monitoring timeline, pharmacologic and surgical management)
- Incidence range: 0–40% across published series; IAN is the most commonly injured nerve (64.4%), followed by lingual nerve (28.8%)

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative review: searched PubMed, Embase, Cochrane using keywords: "inferior alveolar nerve injury," "inferior alveolar nerve damage," "paresthesia," "inferior alveolar nerve repair." English-only, 1972–November 2010. Manual search of anatomy, implant, periodontal, and oral surgery journals and textbooks. Clinical studies, human anatomy, and physiology studies included.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Aetiology — intraoperative**:
- Mechanical: injection needle (hematoma/nerve transection), implant drill (partial/full MC intrusion, thermal injury), implant itself (pressure, chronic neuropathy if adjacent), scalpel (mental nerve), soft-tissue retraction
- Thermal: bone overheating without irrigation → inflammation → secondary ischaemia
- Chemical: cytotoxic local anaesthetic or irrigation → direct nerve degeneration

**Aetiology — postoperative**:
- Thermal, peri-implant infection (inflammation → ischaemia), haematoma/scarring → ischaemia

**Nerve injury classification** (Seddon/Sunderland):
- Neuropraxia (Grade I): reversible conduction block
- Axonotmesis (Grades II–IV): axon disruption, variable recovery
- Neurotmesis (Grade V): complete severance; requires microsurgical repair

**Clinical symptoms**: paraesthesia, hypoesthesia, anaesthesia, dysaesthesia, hyperaesthesia, or allodynia in the IAN/mental nerve distribution (lower lip, chin, gingiva)

**Sensory testing battery**: subjective patient report + tactile (light touch, brush stroke), pain (pinprick), two-point discrimination, thermal testing

**Safety margin**: ≥2 mm from implant apex to mandibular canal superior cortex recommended (radiographic)

**Management timeline**:
- Immediate (within 30–36 h): if suspected direct nerve damage → remove/reposition implant
- Pharmacologic: corticosteroids (dexamethasone) + B-vitamins (B1, B6, B12) ± NSAIDs for anti-inflammatory support
- Observation: if no improvement at 3 months → consider microsurgical exploration
- Microsurgery: neurolysis, neurorrhaphy, or nerve grafting for Grade IV–V injuries

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative (not systematic) review — heterogeneous studies, no meta-analysis, no pooled incidence
- Publication date 2010; CBCT guidance since then has become more evidence-based
- No standardised reporting of nerve injury outcomes across included studies
- Management recommendations expert/consensus-level, not from RCTs

## 6. Related Work

- Worthington 1995 (incidence and complications of implant surgery) — cited as motivation
- Seddon 1943 / Sunderland 1951 — nerve injury classification framework applied
- Later updates: Lee 2015 management guidelines, subsequent IAN systematic reviews

## 7. Glossary

- **IAN**: Inferior Alveolar Nerve (하치조신경) — the main sensory branch of the mandibular division of CN V3
- **MC**: Mandibular Canal (하악관) — bony canal containing the IAN and inferior alveolar artery
- **Neuropraxia**: reversible conduction block without axon loss
- **Axonotmesis**: axon disruption with intact endoneurium; spontaneous recovery possible
- **Neurotmesis**: complete nerve transection; microsurgical repair required
- **Paraesthesia**: altered sensation (tingling, numbness) — most common IAN injury symptom
- **Neurolysis**: surgical decompression/freeing of the nerve from surrounding scar tissue
- **Neurorrhaphy**: direct end-to-end suture repair of a severed nerve
