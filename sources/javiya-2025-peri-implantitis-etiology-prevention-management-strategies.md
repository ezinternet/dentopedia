## Three-line Summary

PRISMA 2020 systematic review (no meta-analysis) of human clinical studies on peri-implantitis etiology, prevention, and management — PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Cochrane Library, English-language studies from January 2010 to March 2025, two independent reviewers.

Peri-implantitis prevalence ranges from 10% to over 40%; biofilm (chiefly P. gingivalis, T. denticola, T. forsythia) with periodontitis history, smoking, poor glycemic control and poor plaque control drive risk, structured supportive maintenance significantly reduces incidence, and guided bone regeneration yields better bone fill than resective surgery.

Qualitative synthesis only — no pooled effect sizes, no included-study count reported and no prospective registration, and heterogeneous diagnostic criteria across the literature limit cross-study comparison.

## 2. Key Contributions
- PRISMA 2020-guided systematic review framing peri-implantitis as a multifactorial disease spanning etiology, prevention, and stage-based management in one document (search to March 2025).
- Confirms biofilm as the chief precipitant (Gram-negative anaerobes P. gingivalis, T. denticola, T. forsythia) while positioning host susceptibility (genetics, diabetes, smoking) and mechanical factors (occlusal overload, implant–abutment junction micromovement, foreign-body reaction to surface particles) as modifiers of initiation and severity.
- Identifies past periodontitis as one of the strongest predictors, alongside excess cement, plaque-retentive prosthetic design, insufficient keratinized mucosa, rough/subcrestal surfaces, and absent professional maintenance.
- Stage-based treatment synthesis: non-surgical modalities (debridement, antiseptics, local antibiotics, laser) offer limited benefit confined to early disease; surgical intervention is typically required for extensive bone loss, with guided bone regeneration (GBR) producing better bone fill than resective treatment.
- Flags novel prevention directions (nanoscale/hydrophilic/antibacterial implant surfaces, probiotics, photodynamic therapy, host modulation) as promising but supported mainly by laboratory or short-term evidence.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review per PRISMA 2020 guidelines, qualitative synthesis (no meta-analysis); not prospectively registered.
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Cochrane Library; MeSH + free-text terms with Boolean operators; hand-searched reference lists of included articles and relevant reviews.
- **Search window**: January 2010 – March 2025, English language only.
- **Inclusion**: Human clinical studies (RCTs, controlled clinical trials, prospective/retrospective cohorts, case-control) addressing etiology, risk factors, prevention, or treatment of peri-implantitis; defined diagnostic criteria and measurable outcomes; ≥6-month follow-up for interventional trials.
- **Exclusion**: Animal/in vitro studies, case reports, case series <10 participants, narrative reviews, expert opinions, conference abstracts without peer-reviewed data, mucositis-only studies without bone loss, non-English publications.
- **Screening**: Two independent reviewers (Author A/B), discrepancies resolved by consensus or a third reviewer (Author C); standardized data-extraction sheet (author, year, country, design, sample size, population, diagnostic criteria, intervention, follow-up, outcomes).
- **n**: Number of included studies not reported in the article; Table 1 summarizes 8 key studies (Renvert 2018, Schwarz 2012, Carcuac 2017, Derks 2015, Heitz-Mayfield 2018, Froum 2022, Ata-Ali 2011, Smeets 2014).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Prevalence**: reported 10% to >40%, varying with diagnostic criteria, population, and follow-up length.
- **Etiology**: microbial biofilm central to pathogenesis — Gram-negative anaerobes predominate; mechanical stress (occlusal overload), implant–abutment junction micromovement, and foreign-body reactions to surface particles contribute; host susceptibility (genes, diabetes mellitus, smoking) modulates initiation/severity.
- **Risk factors**: history of periodontitis (strongest predictor), smoking, poor glycemic control, poor oral hygiene, excess restorative cement, plaque-retentive prosthetic design, mucosa type with insufficient keratinized mucosa, rough and subcrestally positioned implants, and absence of regular professional maintenance — all significantly correlated with higher peri-implantitis occurrence.
- **Prevention**: early risk assessment, individualized oral hygiene programs, regular maintenance therapy — patients in long-term supportive care showed significantly reduced incidence and severity; surface modifications (hydrophilic/antibacterial coatings) limit early biofilm attachment but evidence is mainly laboratory/short-term.
- **Treatment**: non-surgical modalities give limited benefit in early disease; surgical procedures typically required for extensive lesions/bone loss; GBR augmentation produced better bone fill than resective treatment (consistent with Schwarz 2012 in Table 1); adjuncts (probiotics, photodynamic therapy, host modulation) supportive but of limited additional outcome; no single decontamination method consistently superior (Carcuac 2017 RCT).
- **No pooled statistics**: no meta-analytic effect sizes, confidence intervals, or p-values computed.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Stated: lack of standardized diagnostic criteria and inconsistent disease thresholds, follow-up times, and outcome measures make cross-study comparison and guideline development difficult.
- Stated: lack of long-term randomized controlled trials despite high disease prevalence; findings remain qualitative due to inter-study heterogeneity; English-only, grey literature excluded.
- Stated in methods: not registered prospectively.
- Inferred: included-study count and PRISMA flow numbers not reported, no named risk-of-bias tool or per-study quality summary, and Table 1 (8 studies) is illustrative rather than a complete evidence table — so the review cannot by itself shift clinical bottom lines.

## 7. Glossary
- **Peri-implantitis**: biofilm-associated pathological condition with inflammation and progressive bone loss around osseointegrated dental implants (implant counterpart of periodontitis).
- **Peri-implant mucositis**: reversible inflammatory process limited to the peri-implant mucosa without bone loss; may progress to peri-implantitis.
- **Biofilm**: structured microbial community (here dominated by Gram-negative anaerobes) that triggers the host-mediated inflammatory-immune response around the implant.
- **Supportive peri-implant therapy (SPT)**: structured long-term maintenance program (probing, radiographic monitoring, plaque control) that reduces peri-implantitis incidence and progression.
- **Guided bone regeneration (GBR)**: barrier-membrane + graft surgical technique used in contained peri-implant defects; associated with greater bone fill than resective surgery.
- **Resective surgery**: access-flap/osteoplasty approach removing peri-implantitis lesion without rebuilding bone; alternatives to regenerative GBR in advanced defects.
