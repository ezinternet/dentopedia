## Why Ingested

Requested while surveying cervical (NCCL) resin restoration retention loss: this umbrella review is the highest-level synthesis we hold that places posterior and NCCL direct restorations side by side and states that an RMGIC sandwich base improves NCCL retention. It broadens the GIC-only umbrella [[glass-ionomer/panetta-2024-gic-longevity-umbrella-review]] to composites and ormocers, and sits beside the NCCL adhesion evidence in [[resin-bonding/chaple-gil-2026-adhesive-strategies-etching-nccl-sr-nma]] → [[dental-materials/fernandez-2026-direct-restorations-longevity-umbrella-review]].

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 16 systematic reviews/meta-analyses (2012–2025; >15,000 restorations; follow-up 6–120 months) on direct restorations (conventional composite, bulk-fill, ormocer, GIC/RMGIC) in posterior teeth and non-carious cervical lesions (NCCLs), appraised with AMSTAR 2 and GRADE.

Conventional composites showed >90% 5-year survival (especially with multi-step adhesives); bulk-fill failure <7% in short-medium term; ormocer composites failed more (12.1%, mainly marginal degradation); GICs had lower survival; the RMGIC sandwich technique improved NCCL retention.

Longevity depends on material class, bonding strategy and patient risk; the adhesive protocol was a key prognostic factor across all materials. Only the abstract was available, so per-review numbers, AMSTAR 2/GRADE ratings and overlap handling could not be checked.

## 세줄요약

우산 리뷰 (Umbrella review): 체계적 문헌고찰·메타분석 16편 (2012–2025, 수복물 >15,000개, 추적 6–120개월)을 AMSTAR 2와 GRADE로 평가해 구치부 및 비우식성 치경부 병소 (Non-Carious Cervical Lesion, NCCL)의 직접수복 재료 (복합레진·벌크필·오모서·글래스아이오노머)의 임상 수명을 종합했다.

기존 복합레진은 5년 생존율 90% 초과 (특히 다단계 접착제), 벌크필은 단·중기 실패율 7% 미만, 오모서 (Ormocer) 복합레진은 실패율 12.1%로 유의하게 높았고 (주로 변연 열화), 글래스아이오노머 (GIC)는 생존율이 낮았으며, 레진강화형 글래스아이오노머 (RMGIC) 샌드위치 술식이 NCCL 유지력을 개선했다.

수명은 재료 종류·접착 전략·환자 위험도에 좌우되며 접착 프로토콜이 전 재료에 걸친 핵심 예후인자다; 초록만 확보되어 개별 리뷰 수치·AMSTAR 2/GRADE 등급·중복 처리는 확인하지 못했다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Consolidates 16 SR/MAs on direct restoration longevity across composite, bulk-fill, ormocer and GIC/RMGIC materials.
- Covers both posterior restorations and NCCLs in one framework.
- Identifies the adhesive protocol as a key prognostic factor across all materials.
- Reports that the RMGIC sandwich technique improved retention in NCCLs.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- **n**: 16 SRs/MAs (2012–2025), >15,000 restorations, follow-up 6–120 months
- **Quality/certainty**: AMSTAR 2 (methodological quality), GRADE (certainty)
- **Outcomes**: survival rates, failure types, comparative risk estimates (RR, OR)
- Databases, search dates, registration and overlap handling: not in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Conventional composites: >90% 5-year survival, especially with multi-step adhesives.
- Bulk-fill resins: similar short-medium-term outcomes, failure rates <7%; long-term data limited.
- Ormocer-based composites: significantly higher failure rate (12.1%), primarily marginal degradation.
- GICs: lower survival, but useful in high caries risk or moisture-challenged conditions.
- RMGIC sandwich technique: improved retention in NCCLs.
- Conclusion: conventional and bulk-fill composites remain the most reliable; RMGICs and sandwich approaches are effective in specific indications.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Abstract-only ingest: AMSTAR 2/GRADE ratings, per-review effect sizes and the comparator for the "improved retention" claim are not available.
- Overlap of primary studies among the 16 included reviews cannot be assessed from the abstract.
- Authors note long-term bulk-fill data remain limited.

## 7. Glossary
- **Umbrella review**: systematic review of systematic reviews.
- **NCCL**: non-carious cervical lesion (cervical wear lesion without caries).
- **RMGIC**: resin-modified glass ionomer cement.
- **Sandwich technique**: RMGIC/GIC base (dentin replacement) covered by composite.
- **Ormocer**: organically modified ceramic-based composite.
- **AMSTAR 2 / GRADE**: tools for review methodological quality / certainty of evidence.
