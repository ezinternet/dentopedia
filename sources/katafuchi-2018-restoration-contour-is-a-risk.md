## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional radiographic analysis (University of Washington; 96 patients/225 implants in the source data set, 83 patients/168 implants analysed; mean follow-up 10.9 years) of restoration emergence angle and convex/concave contour as risk indicators for peri-implantitis.

In bone-level implants, peri-implantitis prevalence was 31.3% at emergence angle >30° vs 15.1% at ≤30° (p = .04); convex contour combined with >30° gave 37.8% with a significant angle × profile interaction (p = .003), while tissue-level implants showed no such association.

Supports keeping the restoration emergence angle ≤30° and avoiding convex contours specifically in bone-level implants; observational, abstract-only evidence — an association, not causation.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Positions **restoration contour** — emergence angle plus convex/concave profile — as a risk indicator for peri-implantitis, assessed radiographically with a long (mean 10.9-year) follow-up window.
- Identifies a **30° threshold**: in bone-level implants, >30° carried roughly double the peri-implantitis prevalence of ≤30° (31.3% vs 15.1%).
- Demonstrates an **angle × profile interaction**: a convex profile with >30° reached 37.8% prevalence (interaction p = .003).
- Shows the effect is **implant-design-conditional**: no angle–peri-implantitis correlation in tissue-level implants.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional radiographic analysis of a clinical data set (not a cohort followed prospectively for this question).
- **Population**: 96 patients with 225 implants in the source data set; 83 patients with 168 implants met inclusion criteria.
- **Groups**: bone-level vs tissue-level implants.
- **Measurements**: restoration emergence angles and restoration profiles (convex or concave) from radiographs; peri-implantitis diagnosed from probing depth plus radiographic bone loss.
- **Statistics**: generalized estimating equations (GEE), accounting for correlation among implants within patients.
- **Note**: full methodological detail (angle measurement protocol, calibration, case-definition thresholds, exclusions) is not available in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Comparison (bone-level implants) | Peri-implantitis prevalence | p |
|---|---|---|
| Emergence angle >30° | 31.3% | .04 vs ≤30° |
| Emergence angle ≤30° | 15.1% | reference |
| Convex profile + angle >30° | 37.8% | interaction p = .003 |

- Tissue-level implants: no significant emergence-angle/peri-implantitis correlation (no figures in the abstract).
- Authors' conclusion: >30° is a significant risk indicator and convex profile adds risk **for bone-level implants only**.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only ingest**: effect estimates with confidence intervals, the full case definition, measurement reproducibility and excluded-case reasons could not be verified against the paper body.
- Cross-sectional design supports association only; temporality and causation are not established.
- Implant-level clustering was handled with GEE, but residual confounding by restorative, hygienic and patient factors remains possible.
- Subgroup sizes and the tissue-level null result are reported without numbers in the available abstract — absence of significance is not equivalence.

## 7. Glossary

- **Emergence Angle (EA)**: angle between the restoration/abutment transmucosal contour and the implant's long axis; the exposure in this study, thresholded at 30°.
- **Emergence Profile / Restoration Profile**: the convex or concave shape of the restoration as it exits the mucosa.
- **Bone-level vs tissue-level implant**: implant neck positioned at the crestal bone vs above it at the soft-tissue level; the effect modifier in this study.
- **Generalized estimating equations (GEE)**: regression method for correlated/clustered observations — here, multiple implants per patient.
- **Peri-implantitis**: inflammatory peri-implant condition with probing depth and radiographic bone loss; the outcome as defined in the abstract.
