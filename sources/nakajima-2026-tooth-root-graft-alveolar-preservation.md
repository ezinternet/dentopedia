## Why Ingested

This paper was mined as a reference from [[wiki/bone-regeneration/bubalo-2026-bone-substitutes-alveolar-ridge-augmentation]], a narrative review on bone substitutes for alveolar ridge augmentation. It provides a direct animal head-to-head between an autogenous tooth root (demineralized dentin matrix) graft and a collagenated xenograft for alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) — filling the review's cited evidence gap with quantitative histomorphometric data on residual-graft persistence and vital-bone proportion, which bears directly on the review's discussion of xenograft resorption kinetics vs. tooth-derived material biology.

## Three-line Summary

Within-animal comparative histomorphometric study in 6 Beagle dogs (23 analyzable sockets) comparing tooth root–derived graft (Tooth Transformer automated device) vs. collagenated equine xenograft (OsteOXenon + Heart membrane) for alveolar ridge preservation after bilateral mandibular premolar extraction, evaluated at 3 months.

Tooth root graft sites showed near-complete replacement by vital bone (65.6 ± 9.1% total bone, residual graft only 0.5 ± 1.1%) versus xenograft sites (57.8 ± 17.2% total bone, 19.7 ± 16.0% residual graft remnants, p = 0.032); xenograft sites retained a slightly taller buccal crest (12.3 ± 1.8 mm vs 11.8 ± 1.7 mm, p = 0.031) and greater peripheral bone extension (0.52 ± 0.23 mm vs 0.20 ± 0.12 mm, p < 0.0001).

Tooth-derived grafts favor "vital bone" regeneration with minimal residual biomaterial while xenografts favor slightly better buccal contour stability but leave persistent hybrid-bone particles — a trade-off with plausible (but not directly tested, no implants placed) implications for subsequent implant osseointegration; small animal sample (n=6 dogs, 3-month single time point) limits generalizability.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First direct histological comparison between extraction sockets grafted exclusively with a tooth-root-derived (dentin-based) particulate graft and sockets grafted with a collagenated xenograft, in the same within-animal design.
- Quantifies residual-graft persistence and vital vs. immature/mature bone proportions at 3 months post-ARP for both materials, isolating a "vital bone" vs. "hybrid bone" regeneration pattern.
- Demonstrates biocompatibility and effective decontamination of an automated chairside tooth-processing device (Tooth Transformer) even when preliminary handling of the extracted tooth is non-sterile — no inflammatory infiltrates observed in any specimen.
- Provides granular coronal-region histomorphometry (the region most relevant to future implant placement), showing xenograft sites retain measurable residual graft coronally (20.3 ± 16.5%) versus none in tooth-graft sites (p = 0.044).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Animals**: 6 adult female Beagle dogs (~6 years, ~11.5 kg), 3-week quarantine, housed at CeBioVet (Lugo, Spain). Ethical approval: Rof Codina Foundation / Xunta de Galicia, authorization 01/20/LU-001; ARRIVE-compliant.
- **Design**: Bilateral extraction of 3rd and 4th mandibular premolars; mesial sockets randomly grafted with either tooth root graft or collagenated xenograft (2 sockets per material per dog) — 24 total sites (12/group), 1 excluded for buccal bone fracture → 23 analyzed (12 tooth-graft, 11 xenograft).
- **Sample size**: G*Power a priori calculation (paired design, α=0.05, power 80%, Cohen's dz≈1.0) → 6 animals required.
- **Randomization/blinding**: Digital randomization by an investigator not involved in surgery; sealed opaque envelopes; histological sections coded/blinded for analysis.
- **Materials**: OsteOXenon (equine cortical-cancellous bone granules, 0.25–1.0 mm, enzyme-deantigenated) + Heart collagen membrane (equine pericardium) for xenograft group; Tooth Transformer automated device (fragmentation, demineralization, detoxification of extracted tooth root, ~25 min turnaround) for tooth-graft group, also covered with collagen membrane. No fixation used; semi-submerged 4-0 Vicryl closure.
- **Follow-up**: 3 months, then euthanasia (IV sodium pentobarbital overdose) and histologic processing (Technovit 7200 VLC resin embedding, Levai–Laczkó staining, ~50 µm sections along buccolingual plane).
- **Outcome measures**: Clinical (UNC probe socket depth/width, Iwanson caliper buccal wall thickness) at baseline; histomorphometric (Nikon Eclipse + NIS-Elements) — lingual/buccal crest height, L–B vertical discrepancy, % mature bone, % immature bone, % residual graft, % soft tissue, both whole-ROI and coronal-region-specific.
- **Statistics**: Shapiro–Wilk normality; paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank as appropriate; α=0.05; animal-level averaging of paired sockets (GraphPad Prism v10.6.1).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small sample (6 dogs, 23 analyzable sockets); ethical constraints limited power to detect subtler differences.
- Single healing interval (3 months) — early/intermediate healing only, no long-term remodeling data.
- Single histological section per site — no 3D volumetric characterization.
- No ungrafted control sockets (would have reduced within-animal comparison power).
- No immunohistochemistry — inflammatory assessment limited to conventional histology.
- No direct physicochemical characterization of the processed tooth graft material.
- No implants placed — osseointegration implications remain speculative extrapolation from prior literature, not directly tested.
- Animal (dog) model — extrapolation to human clinical protocols should be cautious; authors explicitly frame findings as hypothesis-generating.
- Future work proposed: longer follow-up, micro-CT 3D volumetric analysis, implant-based validation models, region-specific analyses.

## 6. Related Work

- Cites prior human clinical/multicenter studies on tooth-derived graft materials produced with automated chairside devices, reporting limited residual graft and high vital bone percentages consistent with this animal study's findings.
- References literature on impaired implant osseointegration in the presence of slowly resorbing xenograft residues after sinus floor elevation, used to frame the clinical-relevance discussion of "hybrid bone" vs. "vital bone" regeneration.
- Cites emerging ridge-preservation strategies using periosteal separation / cortical lamina or high-density PTFE membrane interposition to mechanically shield buccal bone during healing.
- Directly relevant to [[wiki/bone-regeneration/bubalo-2026-bone-substitutes-alveolar-ridge-augmentation]] as a primary-data animal study supporting/extending that review's discussion of tooth-derived vs. xenogeneic bone substitutes.

## 7. Glossary

- **ARP (Alveolar Ridge Preservation)**: procedures performed at time of tooth extraction to limit post-extraction ridge resorption, typically via socket grafting ± membrane.
- **Xenograft**: bone graft material derived from a different species (here, equine bone).
- **Collagenated xenograft**: xenograft processed to retain/add a collagen component for improved handling and cellular adhesion.
- **Tooth root graft / demineralized dentin matrix**: autogenous graft material produced by fragmenting, demineralizing, and detoxifying a patient's own extracted tooth root.
- **Tooth Transformer**: an automated chairside device that fragments, demineralizes, and decontaminates extracted teeth to produce an autologous graft material.
- **L–B discrepancy**: vertical difference between lingual and buccal crest bone peaks after healing, a marker of buccal plate resorption/remodeling asymmetry.
- **Woven/immature bone vs. lamellar/mature bone**: histological bone maturation stages; woven bone is a disorganized early form, lamellar bone is organized, mature bone.
- **Corticalization**: formation of a dense cortical bone layer, here observed at the coronal socket entrance.
- **ROI (Region of Interest)**: the standardized histomorphometric measurement area used for quantitative analysis.
