## Why Ingested

National Dental Practice-Based Research Network study quantifying real-world antibiotic prescribing patterns after root canal treatment across 153 US dentists (GDs and endodontists), revealing persistent gaps between guideline recommendations and clinical practice — reinforces [[drug/antibiotics/mendez-millan-2024-antibiotic-overprescription-apical-periodontitis-sr-ma]] and extends evidence on antibiotic stewardship implementation.

## Three-line Summary

Prospective observational study: 153 dentists (104 GDs, 49 endodontists) enrolled 1,723 patients undergoing non-surgical root canal treatment; outcomes: antibiotic prescribing patterns and associated dentist/patient/clinical factors.

Antibiotics prescribed to 322 (19%) patients post-RCT; patients with apical periodontitis (OR=1.56, p=.004) or tenderness to palpation (OR=1.40, p=.046) more likely to receive antibiotics; younger dentists (<45 years, OR=0.47, p=.02), non-Hispanic White (OR=0.40, p=.01), and those in large/academic practices (OR=0.25, p=.007) significantly less likely to prescribe.

Clinical implication: nearly 1 in 5 post-RCT patients receive antibiotics despite guideline recommendations limiting systemic antibiotic use to systemic signs/medical risk factors; prescribing variation by dentist demographics and practice setting suggests opportunity for targeted antibiotic stewardship intervention.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Real-world prescribing epidemiology**: Quantified post-RCT antibiotic prescribing rate (19%) across diverse US practice settings, filling evidence gap on implementation of guideline-discordant prescribing in endodontics.
- **Dentist-level predictors**: Younger age, non-Hispanic White ethnicity, and affiliation with large/academic practices independently associated with lower antibiotic prescribing — suggests knowledge/training and organizational context influence stewardship.
- **Patient/clinical predictors**: Apical periodontitis and tenderness to palpation drove prescribing beyond guideline indications; sociodemographic patterns (higher education, non-minority status) associated with lower antibiotic receipt — identifies equity gaps in prescribing.
- **Guideline-practice gap**: Documented persistent deviation from American Association of Endodontists (AAE) and professional guidelines despite decades of antibiotic-stewardship messaging; most prescribing lacked documented systemic signs/symptoms.

---

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Prospective practice-based research network (PBRN) study, multi-institutional (PREDICT project, National Dental PBRN).
- **Setting**: US general dental practices and endodontic practices, 153 providers.
- **Participants**: 
  - Dentists: 104 general dentists (GDs), 49 endodontists
  - Patients: 1,723 enrolled; 137 excluded (68 ineligible, 6 no email, 3 lost contact, 60 withdrew); final cohort = 1,723 (method text inconsistency noted: initially 1,860 enrolled)
- **Data collection**: Consecutive enrollment during 6-month study period; clinical, radiographic, demographic data recorded by practitioners.
- **Primary outcome**: Systemic antibiotic prescribed after non-surgical RCT (yes/no).
- **Secondary analysis**: Restricted to vital pulp + no swelling (clinical situation where antibiotics not indicated).
- **Statistical method**: Generalized estimating equations (GEE) to account for clustering within dentists; adjusted for dentist, patient, and clinical characteristics; Wald chi-square tests for categorical.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Unmeasured systemic involvement**: Data on fever, malaise, lymphadenopathy, cellulitis, or immunosuppression not collected — cannot fully assess guideline concordance; some prescribing may have had undocumented systemic indications.
- **Self-reported prescribing**: Practitioners reported whether antibiotics were prescribed; no verification via pharmacy records or patient medication review.
- **Practitioner dropout / missing data**: 137 of 1,860 enrolled patients excluded; mechanisms (ineligibility vs refusal) not fully detailed.
- **Cross-sectional variability**: 6-month enrollment window; temporal trends in prescribing not captured.
- **No clinical outcome data**: Postoperative pain, infection rate, flare-up, or treatment success not reported — prescribing rationale not matched to clinical course.
- **Causality**: Observational design; associations do not establish whether dentist characteristics cause lower prescribing, or whether unmeasured confounders (e.g., practice population acuity) explain variation.
- **Generalizability**: PBRN participants may not represent all US dentists; practice type / geographic oversampling unknown.

## 6. Related Work

- **Mendez-Millan et al. 2024** (systematic review + meta-analysis): Antibiotic prescribing in apical periodontitis and acute apical abscess shows persistent overprescribing vs guidelines; Law 2026 adds real-world PBRN data complementing SR evidence.
- **Prior PBRN / survey studies**: Previous surveys (cited in introduction, pre-2022) documented high antibiotic prescribing in non-indicated scenarios (vital pulp, localized infection); Law 2026 extends with multivariate analysis and practice-setting stratification.
- **AAE / ESE guidelines** (cited in abstract): American Association of Endodontists and European Society of Endodontology recommend systemic antibiotics only for systemic signs (fever, cellulitis, lymphadenopathy) or medical risk factors; Law 2026 quantifies deviation.
- **Antimicrobial resistance / stewardship**: Global concern (Colombia, Finland, Australia, Germany cited as international parallels); dentists account for ~10% of outpatient antibiotic prescriptions in US; Law 2026 provides US PBRN baseline for future stewardship intervention trials.

## 7. Glossary

- **RCT**: Root canal treatment / non-surgical root canal therapy — endodontic disinfection, medication, and gutta-percha obturation of the pulp chamber and root canal system.
- **PBRN**: Practice-based research network — consortium of real-world dental practices (solo, group, academic) that enroll patients and share de-identified data for pragmatic studies.
- **Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE)**: Statistical method that accounts for clustering (multiple patients per dentist) when analyzing repeated-measures or correlated data; preferred over standard logistic regression for clustered binary outcomes.
- **Apical periodontitis (AP)**: Inflammatory lesion at the root apex, detected radiographically (periapical radiolucency) or clinically (swelling, drainage, tenderness); indicates chronic or acute pulpal/periapical infection.
- **Vital pulp**: Pulp tissue showing positive response to sensibility testing (electric test, cold stimulus); indicates living tissue and absence of pulpitis (reversible or irreversible).
- **Guideline-discordant prescribing**: Antibiotic prescription pattern that deviates from evidence-based clinical-practice guidelines (AAE 2017, ESE 2018) — e.g., routine post-RCT antibiotics in absence of systemic involvement.
- **Antibiotic stewardship**: Healthcare program aimed at reducing unnecessary/inappropriate antibiotic use, preserving drug efficacy, and limiting antimicrobial resistance development.
- **Systemic involvement** (endodontic context): Evidence of infection beyond the tooth/localized periodontal ligament — fever, malaise, lymphadenopathy, cellulitis, or immunocompromised state — indications for systemic antibiotic therapy per guidelines.
