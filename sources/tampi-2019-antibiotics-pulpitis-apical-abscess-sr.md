## Three-line Summary

Companion SR+MA to the ADA 2019 CPG (Lockhart 2019): updated 2 prior Cochrane reviews for new RCTs (none found), ultimately including 3 RCTs and 8 observational reports; trial estimates for pain, swelling, and analgesic use at 7 days showed both benefit and harm at very low to low certainty across all target conditions (SIP, PN-SAP, PN-LAAA).

No statistically significant benefit from antibiotics as adjuncts to DCDT or alone was demonstrated for patient-reported outcomes; the pooled pain-intensity differences were consistently near zero (MD ≈ 0.09–0.39 on 0–3 VAS at 24–72 h) with wide CIs crossing both benefit and harm.

Additional population-level harms from antibiotic prescribing (antibiotic resistance, Clostridioides difficile infection, anaphylaxis) carry potentially large magnitude at very low to moderate certainty, providing the harm context underpinning the CPG's strong conditional non-prescribing recommendations.

## Why Ingested

Companion evidence synthesis paper to Lockhart 2019 (ADA CPG, DOI 10.1016/j.adaj.2019.08.020). While Lockhart 2019 states the recommendations, Tampi 2019 is the underlying SR+MA that documents the full evidence base, GRADE evidence profiles, forest plots, and harm data that justify those recommendations. Together these two papers form the complete ADA 2019 antibiotic stewardship pair.

## Objectives

Summarize evidence on the effect of antibiotic therapy (alone or as adjuncts to DCDT) versus no antibiotic therapy for:
1. Symptomatic Irreversible Pulpitis (SIP) ± Symptomatic Apical Periodontitis (SAP)
2. Pulp Necrosis + Symptomatic Apical Periodontitis (PN-SAP)
3. Pulp Necrosis + Localized Acute Apical Abscess (PN-LAAA)

Also assess additional harms from antibiotic use at the population level.

## Methods

### Study Design
Systematic review with meta-analysis (PRISMA-compliant). Updated 2 pre-existing systematic reviews (Aminoshariae 2016, Agnihotry 2019) to identify new RCTs. Separate search for SRs informing harm outcomes.

### Search Strategy
Databases: MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, CINAHL. Updates searched through 2019.

### Participants
Immunocompetent adults ≥18 years with SIP ± SAP, PN-SAP, or PN-LAAA, with no comorbidities. Immunocompromised patients excluded.

### Evidence Assessment
GRADE approach for certainty of evidence. Risk of bias using Cochrane tool (RCTs). Pairs of reviewers independently conducted selection, extraction, and appraisal.

## Included Studies

### Randomized Controlled Trials (3 total)

| Study | Condition | N | Intervention | Comparator | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fouad 1996 | PN-LAAA | 31 | Penicillin 500 mg + DCDT (partial/total pulpectomy ± I&D) | Placebo + DCDT | 3 days |
| Nagle 2000 | SIP (± SAP) | 40 | Penicillin 500 mg q6h × 7d + ibuprofen ± acetaminophen/codeine | Placebo + analgesics | 7 days |
| Henry 2001 | PN-SAP | 41 | Penicillin 500 mg q6h × 7d + total pulpectomy + analgesics | Placebo + pulpectomy + analgesics | 7 days |

No new RCTs were identified on updating the 2 prior Cochrane reviews.

### Observational Studies for Harm (8 total)
Four sources of additional harm data:
- **Antibiotic resistance / mortality**: Mainous 2011 (NHDS 1997–2006: US hospitalizations for resistant infections); CDC 2013 (national AR burden report)
- **C. difficile infection (CDI)**: Chitnis 2013 (community-associated CDI, EIP surveillance); Hicks 2015 (US antibiotic prescriptions cross-sectional)
- **Anaphylaxis / hypersensitivity**: Studies on penicillin anaphylaxis rates from dental prescribing context
- Three additional reports on antibiotic prescribing patterns and outcomes

## Results

### SIP ± SAP (Antibiotics Alone — No DCDT Available)

**Nagle 2000** (N=40, penicillin vs placebo, no DCDT performed):
- Pain intensity 7d: No meaningful difference (VAS data; very low certainty)
- Analgesic use — ibuprofen tablets: MD −0.40 (95% CI −4.23 to 3.43), p=0.84 — no difference
- Analgesic use — acetaminophen/codeine tablets: MD 2.45 (95% CI −1.23 to 6.13), p=0.19 — no significant difference
- No adverse effects reported by investigators

### PN-SAP / PN-LAAA (Antibiotics as Adjuncts to DCDT)

**Pain intensity (pooled, Fouad 1996 + Henry 2001, N=29–43)**:
- At 24h: MD 0.09 (95% CI −0.37 to 0.55), p=0.69; I²=0%
- At 48h: MD 0.39 (95% CI −0.13 to 0.91), p=0.14; I²=0%
- At 72h: MD 0.12 (95% CI −0.32 to 0.56), p=0.59; I²=0%
- All differences near zero; CIs cross both benefit and harm; **very low to low certainty**

**Intraoral swelling** (pooled estimates): Similar pattern — CIs crossing no effect and both directions; very low certainty.

**No studies** met criteria for SIP ± SAP with DCDT available (as adjunct design).

### GRADE Certainty Summary

| Outcome | Condition | Certainty | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain (VAS) | SIP, no DCDT | Very low | Benefit and harm both possible |
| Pain (VAS) | PN-SAP/LAAA + DCDT | Very low to low | Benefit and harm both possible |
| Intraoral swelling | PN-SAP/LAAA + DCDT | Very low | Benefit and harm both possible |
| Analgesic use | SIP | Very low | No difference |

### Additional Population-Level Harms

| Harm Domain | Key Finding | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Antibiotic-resistant infection mortality | ~23,000 deaths/year in US (CDC 2013) | Moderate |
| C. difficile infection | 14% of community-associated CDI preceded by antibiotic use; dentists are a contributing prescriber class | Low |
| Anaphylaxis (penicillin) | Serious/fatal reactions from dental prescriptions documented | Very low to low |
| Antibiotic prescribing volume | ~50 million antibiotic prescriptions/year from US dentists; 30–85% potentially inappropriate in dental settings | Moderate |

"The magnitude of additional harms related to antibiotic use for any condition were potentially large."

## Conclusions

- For all 3 target conditions, trial evidence showed both potential benefit and harm for pain and swelling outcomes at 7 days, at very low to low certainty.
- No statistically significant benefit from antibiotic adjunct therapy was demonstrated across any pooled analysis.
- Population-level harms carry potentially large magnitude and are supported by moderate-certainty evidence in some domains.
- Further RCT evidence is needed; current evidence base is extremely limited (3 small trials, all using penicillin).
- Findings directly underpin the ADA CPG (Lockhart 2019) recommendations for non-prescribing in SIP and conditional non-prescribing in PN-SAP/PN-LAAA.

## Limitations

- Only 3 RCTs identified total; very small sample sizes (N=29–43 per pooled analysis)
- All RCTs used penicillin; no evidence for amoxicillin, clindamycin, or azithromycin in these conditions
- High risk of bias in all 3 RCTs (attrition bias, selective reporting)
- Heterogeneity in DCDT performed across trials
- No head-to-head antibiotic comparisons; no dose-response data
- Harm outcomes extrapolated from observational sources not specific to dental antibiotic prescribing for pulpal conditions
