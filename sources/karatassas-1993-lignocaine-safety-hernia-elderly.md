## Three-line Summary

Prospective pharmacokinetic study (n=14 elderly patients, Aust NZ J Surg 1993) measuring plasma lidocaine concentrations after 7 mg/kg infiltration anesthesia with adrenaline for inguinal hernia repair.

Peak plasma concentration ranged 0.23–0.90 mg/L (mean 0.54 mg/L at 7 mg/kg), less than one-fifth of the 5 mg/L toxicity threshold, demonstrating a large safety margin even at the FDA maximum dose in elderly patients.

This real-world PK study supports the FDA 7 mg/kg limit as a conservative ceiling for lidocaine with epinephrine, providing a quantified safety buffer reference for clinical dosing decisions.

## Summary

Prospective PK study (n=14 elderly patients; Aust NZ J Surg 1993) measuring plasma lidocaine concentrations after infiltration anesthesia (7 mg/kg with adrenaline) for inguinal hernia repair.

Peak plasma lidocaine (normalized to 7 mg/kg): range 0.23–0.90 mg/L, mean 0.54 mg/L. With toxicity threshold at 5 mg/L, the maximum observed concentration was less than one-fifth of the threshold — demonstrating a large safety margin even at the FDA maximum dose.

## Key Data

- **Dose studied**: approaching 7 mg/kg (FDA maximum for infiltration with epinephrine)
- **Peak Cmax (normalized to 7 mg/kg)**: 0.23–0.90 mg/L, mean 0.54 mg/L
- **Toxicity threshold**: 5 mg/L (lignocaine toxicity)
- **Safety margin**: maximum observed Cmax/threshold = 0.90/5 = 0.18 → 5.5x safety margin
- Patient population: elderly (reduced hepatic function, lower plasma proteins) — conservative estimate
- No toxic symptoms observed in any patient

## Note on Toxicity Threshold Discrepancy

Karatassas 1993 uses 5 mg/L (5 μg/mL) as toxicity threshold; Klein 2016 uses 6 μg/mL. Both thresholds are cited in the literature; 5 mg/L is the older threshold for serious toxicity (mild toxicity begins ~3-4 μg/mL by some estimates). Klein's 6 μg/mL is for mild early toxicity (subjective symptoms). Both support the same clinical conclusion: standard dental doses produce concentrations far below either threshold.
