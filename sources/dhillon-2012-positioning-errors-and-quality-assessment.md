## Why Ingested

위키의 파노라마 quality thread에서 가장 오래된 정량 앵커가 빈다. [[radiology/lingam-2023-common-errors-subjective-quality-panoramic]]이 2023년 리야드(단일/대형 기관)의 panorama부터 자세오류 유병률을 잰다면, 본 2012년 인도 단일 치과대학 연구는 디지털 파노라마 도입 초기의 기관급 감사 결과(무오류 11%·진단 불가 24.9%)를 제공해 "자세오류가 만연하다"는 결론이 기관·시대를 가로질러 독립적으로 반복된다는 근거를 보강한다. 아울러 불량 영상의 재촬영이 암 위험(백만 건당 0.21–1.9건)과 연결된다는 선량/반복 관점을 제시해, [[radiology/elkhateeb-2022-psp-image-plate-artifacts-prevalence]]의 구내 PSP 재촬영 감사와 같은 "피할 수 있는 재촬영" 스레드를 파노라마 쪽에서 지지한다.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective audit of 1,782 digital panoramic radiographs (Strato 2000D; India, Jan 2007–Mar 2010, single dental college), grading 9 positioning-error categories and overall diagnostic quality (excellent / diagnostically acceptable / unacceptable).

89% of images carried ≥1 positioning error; only 11.0% were error-free; the most common error was failure to position the tongue against the palate (55.7%) and the least common was patient movement (1.6%); 24.9% of images were diagnostically unacceptable — exceeding the ≤10% benchmark — while 64.1% were acceptable.

Substantial room for improvement exists in patient positioning and operator–patient communication; repeat radiography from non-diagnostic images carries a cancer risk estimated at 0.21–1.9 cases per million examinations.

## 세줄요약

인도 단일 치과대학에서 2007년 1월–2010년 3월 촬영된 디지털 파노라마 1,782장(Strato 2000D)을 대상으로 9가지 자세 오류(Positioning Error)와 진단 화질(excellent/진단 가능/진단 불가)을 평가한 후향적 감사.

영상의 89%가 자세 오류 1개 이상을 보였고 무오류는 11.0%뿐; 가장 흔한 오류는 혀를 구개에 붙이지 않은 것(55.7%), 가장 드문 오류는 촬영 중 움직임(1.6%); 진단 불가 영상은 24.9%로 권고 기준(≤10%)을 초과, 진단 가능(acceptable)은 64.1%.

환자 자세 잡기·술자와 환자의 소통 개선 여지가 크며, 비진단 영상의 재촬영은 백만 건당 0.21–1.9건의 암 발생 위험과 연관된다.

## 1. Document Information

- **Journal**: Imaging Science in Dentistry 2012;42(4):207-12
- **DOI**: 10.5624/isd.2012.42.4.207
- **Institution**: Department of Oral Medicine and Radiology, ITS Centre for Dental Studies and Research, Ghaziabad, India (with Teerthanker Mahavir Dental College, Kothiwal Dental College, IDST Dental College)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantifies positioning-error frequency in a single-center Indian digital-panoramic cohort (n=1,782): 89% of images had ≥1 positioning error, none were error-free in more than 11% of cases.
- Separates forward (18.3%) and backward (30.0%) patient positioning as distinct errors, where Rushton et al. had combined them (58.8% combined).
- Reports an unacceptable-image rate (24.9%) numerically between Brezden & Brooks (18.2%) and Rushton et al. (33%), contextualized by sampling frame (claims submissions vs multi-center daily practice vs single center).
- Adds a radiation-protection framing: repetition of panoramic examinations carries a cancer risk estimated at 0.21–1.9 cases per million examinations.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: retrospective audit of archived radiographs (radiographs from inactive patient files).
- **n**: 1,782 panoramic radiographs (Jan 2007–Mar 2010), adults ≥18 years; pediatric (<18 y) and edentulous patients excluded; first image only when multiple taken; repeated images excluded.
- **Equipment**: Strato 2000D (Villa Sistemi Medicali, Milan, Italy), 50–80 kVp, 4–10 mA, 15 s exposure, magnification 1.23; taken by 2 radiographers with >3 years' experience; viewed digitally on a Dell 15-inch 1280×800 monitor in subdued lighting.
- **Assessment**: a single professor of oral and maxillofacial radiology (>12 y experience) scored 9 positioning-error categories (chin tipped high/low, slumped, patient forward/backward, tongue not against palate, movement, head tilted, head turned to one side) and overall quality (excellent / diagnostically acceptable / unacceptable) per the Guidelines on Radiology Standards for Primary Dental Care; 25% random subsample (446 images) re-rated after 3 months.
- **Outcomes**: positioning-error frequency distribution, diagnostic-quality distribution, intra-examiner repeatability (% agreement and kappa; Altman categories).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 196/1,782 (11.0%) radiographs error-free ("excellent"); 1,586 (89.0%) had ≥1 positioning error.
- Most common error: tongue not positioned against the palate — 993 (55.7%); least common: patient movement during exposure — 28 (1.6%).
- Error ranking: tongue 55.7% > slumped 35.0% (624) > backward 30.0% (534) > forward 18.3% (326) > chin high 17.9% (319) > head turned 17.4% (310) > chin low 16.2% (289) > head tilted 12.7% (227) > movement 1.6% (28).
- Diagnostic quality: excellent 196 (11.0%); diagnostically acceptable 1,142 (64.1%); unacceptable 444 (24.9%) — exceeding the ≤10% benchmark; the ≥70% "excellent" target was far from met.
- Intra-examiner reliability: positioning errors kappa 0.77 (92% agreement); quality assessment kappa 0.63 (83% agreement) — both "good".

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single center; all images from one department and two technicians — sampling frame differs from multi-center studies (Rushton: 41 dentists, 33% unacceptable).
- Processing/handling artifacts not considered (digital machine); clinical context (patient stature, swelling/facial asymmetry) not considered.
- Adults only — pediatric positioning error rates may differ; repeated images excluded from analysis.
- Single experienced observer; no inter-examiner reliability.

## 6. Related Work

- lingam-2023: newer, larger (n=2,629) Riyadh audit of the same phenomenon — also finds the tongue error most common, a small error-free minority, and unacceptable rates exceeding the ≤10% benchmark (independently confirms).
- elkhateeb-2022: intraoral PSP artifact audit — same "avoidable, operator-driven repeats cost dose" thread on the intraoral side.

## 7. Glossary

- **Positioning error**: deviation in patient placement/orientation in the panoramic machine that produces characteristic geometric distortions of the image.
- **Focal trough (image layer)**: the limited three-dimensional zone of sharpness in panoramic radiography; errors in positioning manifest as distortion outside it.
- **Ghost image**: an artifact arising from a radiopaque structure between the X-ray source and the center of rotation, projected to the contralateral side.
- **Diagnostically unacceptable**: an image found to be non-diagnostic and requiring repetition.
