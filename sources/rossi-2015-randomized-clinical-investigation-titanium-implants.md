## Why Ingested

위키의 platform switching (PS) 관련 페이지 대부분은 [[wiki/implants/mbl/juan-montesinos-2022-platform-switching-conventional-sr-ma]]가 확립한 것처럼 PS의 변연골소실(Marginal Bone Level, MBL) 감소 이점을 지지하는 방향으로 편중되어 있다. 이 논문은 그와 반대로 PFS와 StE(platform-matching, 표준) 임플란트 간 방사선학적·임상적 차이가 통계적으로 유의하지 않다고 보고하는 RCT로, 균형 잡힌 근거 그림을 위해 의도적으로 인제스트한다 — cherry-picking을 피하기 위한 반대증거(counter-evidence) 확보 목적.

## Three-line Summary

Pilot RCT (23 patients, 21 PFS + 18 StE titanium implants, single-center-equivalent multi-investigator design, 6-month follow-up) testing whether platform switching (PFS) improves crestal bone preservation and clinical outcome versus standard (StE) implants of the same base design.

No statistically significant difference in marginal bone level (Wilcoxon two-sample test) was found between PFS and StE implants at any time point (AC through 6 months), nor in pocket depth or attachment height; bone stabilized approximately 1 mm below the implant shoulder in both groups.

Authors conclude it seems unlikely that the already-optimal clinical/radiological outcome of the tested standard (no-PFS) implant can be improved by introducing PFS — a null/contrarian finding relative to most PS-favoring literature.

## 세줄요약

파일럿 RCT(환자 23명, 플랫폼 스위칭(Platform Switching, PFS) 21개 + 표준형(StE) 18개 티타늄 임플란트, 다수 연구자 참여, 6개월 추적) — PFS가 StE(표준, non-PFS) 임플란트 대비 치조정골 보존 및 임상 결과를 개선하는지 검정.

모든 시점(AC~6개월)에서 변연골 수준(Marginal Bone Level, MBL)에 PFS-StE 군 간 통계적 유의차 없음(Wilcoxon 이표본검정); 탐침깊이(Pocket Depth, PD)·부착고(Attachment Height, AH)도 유의차 없음; 두 군 모두 골이 임플란트 숄더 약 1mm 하방에서 안정화.

저자들은 이미 최적화된 표준(non-PFS) 임플란트의 임상·방사선학적 결과가 PFS 도입으로 더 개선될 가능성은 낮다고 결론 — 대부분의 PS 우호적 문헌과 대비되는 null/반증적 결과.

## 2. Key Contributions

- One of the few individually-reported RCTs in this wiki's PS collection that finds **no significant difference** between PFS and StE (platform-matching) implants — directly counters the pooled/qualitative trend of [[wiki/implants/mbl/juan-montesinos-2022-platform-switching-conventional-sr-ma]] and other RCTs in this collection.
- Directly quotes the Atieh systematic review/meta-analysis it draws on for context: of 10 controlled clinical trials (1,239 implants, 12–60 month follow-up) pooled by Atieh, only 1 trended toward PFS benefit, 4 found no difference, and 5 found platform-matched implants significantly superior for marginal bone preservation — evidence that PS-null and even PS-negative RCTs already existed in the literature prior to this trial.
- Reports a matched "split-mouth"-style case (same patient, PFS implant at position 15, StE implant at position 25) as an illustrative example of clinically equivalent outcomes between the two designs.
- Surgeons were blinded to implant type (PFS vs StE) until after patient screening — reduces selection bias risk relative to some earlier PS RCTs.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Prospective, randomized, controlled pilot clinical trial; no prospective sample size calculation (empirically selected sample size); 6-month follow-up from abutment connection (AC).
- **Patients**: 23 partially edentulous patients enrolled Jan 2010–Mar 2012, randomized to 21 PFS (test) and 18 StE (control) implants (Thommen Medical AG ELEMENT RC system, platform Ø5.0mm, lengths 8.0/9.5/11.0mm). Two randomized patients could not undergo surgery and were discontinued; 21 patients completed the 6-month follow-up.
- **Site**: All implants placed in posterior mandible/maxilla premolar-molar sites (FDI x4–x7); 12 implants (31%) maxilla, 27 (69%) mandible. Inclusion required ≥3 months healed alveolus, ≥7mm horizontal bone width, moderate smokers (<10 cig/day) and history of periodontitis allowed.
- **Blinding**: Surgeons blinded to implant assignment until after screening (randomization code revealed post-screening).
- **Surgical protocol**: Two-stage (submerged) placement, implants set ~0.5mm subcrestal (machined-structured border 0.5mm below bone crest); second-stage (AC) surgery after 4–12 weeks healing; functional loading 6 weeks after AC.
- **Radiographic measurement**: Standardized parallel-technique periapical X-rays via customized jig bite blocks, digitized and measured (ImageJ, NIH v1.49m); mesial + distal implant-shoulder-to-first-bone-contact distance averaged. Timepoints: AC, 4-week follow-up, 6-week (restoration/loading), 3-month recall, 6-month (end of study).
- **Clinical measurement**: Pocket depth (PD) and attachment height (AH) via calibrated probe at AC, 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months.
- **Statistics**: Descriptive statistics (Excel 2013); parametric (t-tests) and non-parametric (Wilcoxon two-sample) tests (SAS 9.3.1).
- **Ethics**: Declaration of Helsinki (2008) principles; written informed consent; both implant types CE-certified and used within approved indications — formal ethics board approval not sought as the intervention itself was not deemed additional research risk.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Quantitative bone-to-implant distance (mm, mesial+distal averaged, mean ± SD):

| Time point | PFS mean (SD) | StE mean (SD) |
|---|---|---|
| Abutment connection (AC) | 0.63 (0.68) | 0.79 (0.48) |
| 4-week follow-up | 0.81 (0.50) | 1.18 (0.39) |
| 6-week (restoration/loading) | 1.06 (0.61) | 1.22 (0.62) |
| 3-month recall | 0.93 (0.69) | 1.14 (0.63) |
| 6-month (end of study) | 0.88 (0.76) | 1.06 (0.51) |

- **No statistically significant difference between PFS and StE at any time point** (Wilcoxon two-sample test), though PFS showed a numerically lower mean bone loss at every timepoint.
- Bone level stabilized ~1mm below the implant shoulder (at/above the first implant thread) for both groups by 6 months — consistent with prior 5-year outcome data on this same implant line.
- PD and AH changes were similar between groups; all PD values remained <4mm (healthy); AH loss trended slightly lower for StE than PFS (opposite direction from the bone-level trend, also not significant).
- No implant losses. Complications: 1 partially exposed cover screw (PFS, healed uneventfully, no infection), 1 patient pregnancy (PFS group) leading to discontinued X-ray follow-up for that patient.
- For 13/39 implants, insertion torque of 30–45 Ncm was recorded (good bone quality); 10 sites (6 PFS, 4 StE) had prior non-coronal bone augmentation.
- Discussion contextualizes against a cited meta-analysis of 28 publications (1,216 PFS + 1,157 platform-matched implants, not itself held in this wiki) that found significantly less MBL with PFS, increasing with longer follow-up and larger platform-abutment mismatch — the authors note their own null result does not confirm this trend, at least at 6 months with this specific implant design.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Pilot study: no prospective sample size/power calculation; small sample (23 patients, 39 implants) likely underpowered to detect a modest PFS effect of the magnitude reported in larger meta-analyses (e.g., ~0.2–0.5mm).
- Short follow-up (6 months only) — authors explicitly note long-term RCT evidence for PFS is still lacking, and this trial does not address whether PFS effects might emerge later.
- Both implant types already achieve a very favorable clinical/radiological baseline (this implant line's documented 5-year outcomes are good), which may create a ceiling effect masking a PFS advantage that could be more visible against a less-optimized StE control.
- No formal ethics board review (deemed unnecessary given CE certification and non-experimental risk framing) — a methodological gap relative to more rigorously governed trials.
- Authors explicitly call for a larger-scale, longer-term RCT to confirm or reject the PFS-superiority hypothesis.

## 6. Related Work

- [[wiki/implants/mbl/juan-montesinos-2022-platform-switching-conventional-sr-ma]] — 2022 SR+MA finding PS significantly reduces MBL (MD 0.255mm, p<0.05); this RCT's null 6-month result directly contradicts that pooled effect at the level of an individual trial.
- [[wiki/implants/mbl/strietzel-2015-platform-switching-mbl-sr-ma]] — same-year (2015) SR+MA pooling 22 studies, finding PS significantly superior (0.49mm vs 1.01mm MBL); this RCT was not part of that pooled analysis and stands as a contemporaneous counterexample.
- [[wiki/implants/mbl/enkling-2013-platform-switching-bone-level-3year-rct]] — individual 3-year multi-center RCT reporting significantly less bone loss with PFS; discordant with this trial's null 6-month finding (different implant systems and follow-up length, so not directly comparable, but illustrates that individual RCTs in this literature disagree on PFS efficacy).
- [[wiki/implants/mbl/messias-2019-platform-switching-mbl-5year-rct]] — 5-year equivalence RCT finding PS 0.23mm lower MBL (p=0.025); also discordant with this trial's null result, though again over a much longer follow-up.

## 7. Glossary

- **Platform Switching (PFS/PS)**: use of a prosthetic abutment narrower than the implant platform diameter, shifting the implant-abutment microgap inward and away from the crestal bone.
- **StE (Standard implant)**: this paper's term for the control (platform-matching) implant — abutment diameter matches the implant platform diameter, no inward microgap shift.
- **Abutment Connection (AC)**: second-stage surgical procedure exposing the implant and connecting the prosthetic abutment; used as the 0-mm radiographic reference level in this study.
- **Pocket Depth (PD)**: distance from the mucosal margin to the base of the peri-implant sulcus.
- **Attachment Height (AH)**: clinical measure of soft-tissue attachment level around the implant.
- **Wilcoxon two-sample test**: non-parametric statistical test used here (alongside parametric t-tests) to compare bone level and clinical parameters between PFS and StE groups.
