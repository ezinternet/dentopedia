## Why Ingested

[[drug/mronj/ruggiero-2022-aaoms-mronj-position-paper-update]] left AAOMS without consensus on denosumab drug holidays (surgery 3–4 mo post-dose only if a holiday is elected), while [[drug/mronj/jung-2022-denosumab-mronj-osteoporosis-5year]] found binary pre/post-extraction timing non-significant. This paper is the first sized cohort (n=258, 185 interventions) to quantify a **continuous** timing variable against MRONJ risk in osteoporosis-dose denosumab (Denosumab) patients, finding delay independently protective (OR 0.35) and zero MRONJ when surgery occurred ≥4 mo post-injection — direct quantitative refinement of the position-paper guidance and the ECTS/Campisi windows.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center cohort (n=258 denosumab-treated osteoporosis patients, Rabin Medical Center, Israel; 91 patients undergoing 185 dentoalveolar interventions) testing whether timing of surgery relative to the last 60 mg denosumab injection affects MRONJ risk.

MRONJ occurred in 5.43% (14/258) of patients (7.57% of interventions); intervention delay was independently protective at the intervention level (OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.16–0.77, p=.0089; univariate OR 0.26, p=.0008), mean delay was 2.03 ± 0.75 mo in MRONJ vs 5.74 ± 0.95 mo in non-MRONJ interventions, and no MRONJ occurred when surgery was ≥4 mo after the last injection.

Cumulative antiresorptive duration was the other independent risk factor (intervention-level OR 1.33, 95% CI 1.07–1.65, p=.0101; patient-level OR 1.25, p<.001) while the number of denosumab injections was not; authors advise avoiding elective bone-related surgery within the first 4 mo post-injection (optimal 4–5 mo), arguing this balances MRONJ avoidance against rebound vertebral fracture risk better than the ECTS 5–6 mo window.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First quantification of a **continuous** "dentoalveolar intervention delay" (months between last denosumab dose and surgery) as an independent MRONJ predictor in osteoporosis-dose denosumab patients: OR 0.26 (95% CI 0.12–0.57, p=.0008) univariate; OR 0.35 (95% CI 0.16–0.77, p=.0089) after GEE multivariable adjustment — a 65% odds reduction per unit increase.
- Empirical floor effect: **zero MRONJ cases when surgery was performed ≥4 mo after the most recent denosumab injection**, grounding a proposed 4–5 mo optimal surgical window.
- Demonstrates that **timing outweighs cumulative exposure** for denosumab: number of injections was not a significant predictor at either level, attributed to denosumab's non-integration into hydroxyapatite bone matrix (reversible RANKL inhibition, serum decline ~4 mo post-Cmax, terminal half-life ~25–30 d).
- Identifies sequential antiresorptive therapy as the dominant confounder: 100% of the 14 MRONJ patients had prior prolonged bisphosphonate exposure (mean >9 yr ARD before denosumab), yielding a 5.43% overall MRONJ rate consistent with Everts-Graber real-world transition data.
- Proposes a clinically actionable schedule that explicitly prices in the rebound-fracture dilemma: surgery at 4–5 mo keeps the next denosumab dose near month 7–7.5, versus month 8–9 under the ECTS 5–6 mo window once 6–8 wk mucosal healing is added.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort at a tertiary OMFS center (Rabin Medical Center—Beilinson Hospital, Israel). Two analysis levels: patient level (n=258) and intervention level (91 patients, 185 dentoalveolar interventions).
- **Cohort**: Osteoporosis patients on denosumab 60 mg SC q6mo; mean age 71.35 ± 8.26 yr; 91.47% female; mean 6.33 ± 5.05 injections; mean total ARD duration 9.16 ± 5.46 yr. Comorbidities: hypertension 44.57%, hyperlipidemia 35.66%, diabetes mellitus 20.54%.
- **Exposure**: Dentoalveolar intervention delay (months from last denosumab administration to surgery); procedures included extractions (mean 1.54 ± 1.89 per treatment), bone grafts (5.41%), implants placed/exposed, jaw location (mandible 48.65% / maxilla 51.35%).
- **Outcome**: MRONJ per AAOMS definition (exposed/probeable bone >8 wk, no radiation/metastatic history).
- **Statistics**: Univariable screening (p<.1 threshold), multicollinearity assessment, then multivariable modeling — generalized estimating equations (GEE) at the intervention level (accounts for within-patient clustering), logistic regression at the patient level. Significance p<.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Overall MRONJ**: 5.43% of patients (14/258); 7.57% of interventions (14/185).
- **Intervention delay (the headline finding)**: MRONJ group 2.03 ± 0.75 mo vs non-MRONJ group 5.74 ± 0.95 mo (cohort mean 5.37 ± 1.49 mo). Univariate OR 0.26 (0.12–0.57), p=.0008; multivariable GEE OR 0.35 (0.16–0.77), p=.0089. Zero MRONJ when delay ≥4 mo.
- **ARD duration**: patient-level univariable p=.005, multivariable OR 1.25 (1.10–1.41), p<.001; intervention-level univariate OR 1.19 (1.05–1.36), p=.0077; GEE OR 1.33 (1.07–1.65), p=.0101.
- **Implants**: previous implant presence at site increased risk (univariate OR 2.45, 1.02–5.86, p=.044; GEE OR 6.30, 0.81–49.15, p=.079 — attenuated). Implant exposure and bone graft: no MRONJ cases (N/A in models).
- **Non-significant**: number of denosumab injections (both levels), procedure type (extractions yes/no OR 3.36, p=.100; extractions count p=.533), jaw location (maxilla OR 1.77, p=.250 despite 9.47% maxillary event rate), age at intervention level (p=.0525 borderline; patient level OR 1.09, p=.067).
- **BP pretreatment**: all 14 MRONJ patients (100%) had prior prolonged oral BP exposure — framed as synergistic sequential-therapy risk, not pure denosumab effect.
- **Pharmacokinetic anchor**: CTX falls >80% within 5 d of dosing and begins rising ~4 mo; serum denosumab declines biphasically (terminal half-life ~25–30 d) — aligning the 4-mo safe threshold with turnover recovery.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Confounding by prior BP exposure**: 100% of MRONJ cases had prolonged BP pretreatment; observed risk reflects cumulative sequential antiresorptive therapy, not denosumab alone. BP subclass not evaluated (vast majority oral BPs).
- **Retrospective single-center design** at a tertiary referral center — referral bias inflates the 5.43% MRONJ rate relative to community figures.
- **Timing recommendation is clinical extrapolation**: the 4–5 mo optimum derives from a zero-event threshold plus pharmacokinetic reasoning, not a prospective comparison of scheduled windows.
- **Maxilla finding unexplained**: higher-than-expected maxillary involvement proposed via vascular transport/inflammation mechanisms (VEGF, IL-1α/β, TNF-α) — hypothesis requiring dedicated study.
- Authors call for **prospective studies** to validate surgical timing recommendations and enable individualized care; oncology-dose (120 mg monthly) patients are explicitly out of scope.

## 7. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Denosumab (Dmab) | Human monoclonal antibody against RANKL; osteoporosis dose 60 mg SC every 6 mo; does not bind hydroxyapatite, so bone turnover recovers fully after clearance |
| MRONJ | Medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw: exposed/probeable maxillofacial bone >8 wk in antiresorptive-exposed patients without radiation or metastatic disease |
| Dentoalveolar intervention delay | Months elapsed between the last denosumab injection and the dental surgical procedure — the study's primary timing variable |
| Rebound effect | Accelerated bone loss and multiple vertebral fracture risk after denosumab discontinuation/delay, driven by rapid turnover recovery |
| GEE | Generalized estimating equations; regression accommodating within-patient clustering of multiple interventions |
| Sequential antiresorptive therapy | Prior bisphosphonate course followed by denosumab; skeletal BP retention plus reversible Dmab suppression creates additive MRONJ susceptibility |
| Pragmatic window (Campisi) | Hypothesized 5–7 mo post-injection interval during which bone turnover recovery permits lower-risk dental surgery |
| ECTS window | European Calcified Tissue Society recommended surgical window of 5–6 mo post-denosumab injection |
