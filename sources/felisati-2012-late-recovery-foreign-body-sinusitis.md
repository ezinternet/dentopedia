## Three-line Summary

Case report (n=1, 55-year-old male) with recurring, antibiotic-resistant ethmoidomaxillary sinusitis after right lateral-window maxillary sinus floor augmentation, caused by bone graft infection and displacement of xenograft material into the maxillary sinus.

Combined nasal endoscopy + oral surgery removal of the infected graft achieved apparent resolution, but sinusitis relapsed and CT confirmed residual graft fragments; a second, more precise endoscopic local treatment led to spontaneous expulsion of the fragments 6 hours later and prompt recovery.

Displaced/infected xenograft can cause a late, antibiotic-refractory sinusitis that surgical drainage alone may not fully clear on the first attempt — CT follow-up is warranted when symptoms persist, and successful re-augmentation is possible 6 months after resolution.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Documents a rare but instructive complication pathway: bone graft infection combined with intra-sinus displacement of previously placed xenograft material, producing late, antibiotic-resistant ethmoidomaxillary sinusitis after lateral-window sinus floor augmentation.
- Demonstrates that first-line combined endoscopic + oral surgical removal of infected/displaced graft may not fully clear symptoms — relapse occurred despite an apparently successful initial procedure and 2 weeks of postoperative antibiotics.
- Reports a favorable long-term outcome: after residual graft fragments were confirmed on CT and treated with a second, more targeted endoscopic local medication, the fragments were spontaneously expelled within 6 hours, and the patient later successfully underwent re-augmentation at 6 months.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Single case report. A 55-year-old male presented with recurring sinusitis symptoms following a right maxillary sinus floor augmentation performed for implant purposes. Diagnostic workup identified antibiotic-resistant ethmoidomaxillary sinusitis attributable to (1) infection of the bone graft and (2) displacement of the previously inserted xenograft material into the maxillary sinus. Treatment consisted of a combined surgical approach: nasal endoscopy paired with oral (intraoral) surgery to remove the infected graft material and re-establish sinus drainage. Postoperative course included 2 weeks of antibiotic therapy plus local medications. On relapse, CT imaging was used to confirm residual grafting fragments, followed by a second, more precise endoscopic local treatment.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Initial combined nasal-endoscopic + oral-surgical removal of infected graft: apparently successful immediately post-procedure, but sinusitis relapsed.
- Relapse persisted despite 2 weeks of antibiotic therapy and local medications.
- CT scan confirmed persistence of grafting fragments in the maxillary sinus.
- After a second, more accurate endoscopic local medication, the patient spontaneously expelled the graft fragments 6 hours post-treatment and recovered promptly.
- A repeat maxillary sinus floor augmentation was performed successfully 6 months after full recovery, with no further complications reported in the abstract.

## 7. Glossary

- **Ethmoidomaxillary sinusitis**: Inflammation/infection involving both the ethmoid and maxillary sinuses.
- **Xenograft**: Bone graft material derived from a different species (e.g., bovine-derived), commonly used in maxillary sinus floor augmentation.
- **Maxillary sinus floor augmentation (MSFA)**: Surgical procedure (often lateral-window approach) to increase bone height in the posterior maxilla for implant placement by grafting beneath the sinus membrane.
- **Foreign body sinusitis**: Sinus infection/inflammation caused by a retained foreign material (here, displaced graft particles) acting as a nidus for infection.
- **Nasal endoscopy**: Minimally invasive endonasal visualization/instrumentation technique used here in combination with oral surgery to access and clear the maxillary sinus.
