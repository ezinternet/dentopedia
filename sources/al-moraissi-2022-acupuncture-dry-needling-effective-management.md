## Why Ingested

The wiki holds dry-needling evidence for myofascial TMD that ranks the technique favorably against botulinum toxin ([[tmj/menendez-torre-2023-dry-needling-vs-manual-therapy-tmd-nma]]) and a stepwise recommendation that places dry needling among minimally invasive options ([[tmj/chan-2022-myogenous-tmd-diagnosis-treatment-review]]). This network meta-analysis adds the placebo-controlled question, asking whether acupuncture or dry needling beat active or inactive placebo for masticatory muscle pain.

## Three-line Summary

Network meta-analysis of RCTs (searched to September 2019) comparing acupuncture, dry needling, and active or inactive placebo for masticatory muscle pain (TMD-M); outcomes were pain intensity, pressure pain threshold (PPT), and maximum mouth opening (MMO).

Neither acupuncture nor dry needling differed from active or inactive placebo for pain intensity or PPT (p > .05); dry needling increased MMO versus placebo (very low-quality evidence).

The authors conclude that the placebo effect accounts for most of the treatment effect in TMD-M rather than a specific therapeutic effect of needling; numbers of trials and pooled effect sizes are not available from the abstract.

## 세줄요약

무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT)의 네트워크 메타분석 (Network Meta-Analysis, NMA, 2019년 9월까지 검색): 저작근 통증 (masticatory muscle pain, TMD-M)에서 침술 (acupuncture)·건침 (dry needling)을 능동·비활성 위약과 비교; 결과변수는 통증 강도, 압통역치 (Pressure Pain Threshold, PPT), 최대 개구량 (Maximum Mouth Opening, MMO).

침술·건침 모두 통증 강도와 PPT에서 능동·비활성 위약과 차이 없음 (p > .05); 건침만 위약 대비 MMO 증가 (근거 질 매우 낮음).

저자들은 치료효과의 대부분이 침 자체의 특이 효과가 아닌 위약 효과 (placebo effect)로 설명된다고 결론; 포함 시험 수와 합성 효과크기는 초록에서 확인 불가.

## 1. Document Information

- Journal: Journal of Oral Rehabilitation 2022;50(1):87-97 (epub 2022-11-03). Source: PubMed, PMID 36314254, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/joor.13382).
- Authors: Essam Ahmed Al-Moraissi (Thamar University, Yemen), Greg Goddard (UCSF), Nikolaos Christidis (Karolinska Institutet).
- Article types per PubMed: Review, Network Meta-Analysis.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Placebo-anchored NMA of needling therapies for masticatory muscle pain, including both active and inactive placebo comparators.
- Reports no pain or PPT benefit of acupuncture or dry needling over placebo; attributes most of the effect to placebo response.
- Identifies a short-term MMO gain with dry needling versus placebo, rated very low-quality evidence.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

RCTs published until September 2019 comparing dry needling, acupuncture, and inactive or active placebo in TMD-M patients. Outcomes: post-treatment pain intensity, PPT, MMO. Risk of bias assessed with the Cochrane tool. Mean differences analysed in a frequentist NMA using STATA; direct pairwise meta-analysis also reported. Number of included trials and participants not stated in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- NMA and direct pairwise meta-analysis: no difference of acupuncture or dry needling versus active or inactive placebo for pain intensity and PPT (p > .05).
- Dry needling significantly increased MMO versus placebo (very low-quality evidence).
- Numeric pooled estimates, confidence intervals, and ranking values are not in the abstract and are not reproduced here.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Abstract-level only. The search ended September 2019 (publication in 2022), so later RCTs are not captured. The abstract flags very low-quality evidence for the MMO finding. Risk-of-bias results and heterogeneity are not available in the abstract.

## 7. Glossary

- Network meta-analysis (NMA): simultaneous direct and indirect comparison of several interventions.
- Dry needling: insertion of a needle into a myofascial trigger point without injectate.
- Pressure pain threshold (PPT): minimum pressure at which pressure becomes pain.
- Maximum mouth opening (MMO): largest interincisal opening.
- TMD-M: masticatory muscle temporomandibular disorder.
