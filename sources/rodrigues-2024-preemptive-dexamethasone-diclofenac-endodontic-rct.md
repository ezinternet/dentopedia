## Why Ingested

Directly addresses one of the most clinically challenging scenarios in endodontics: achieving adequate anesthesia in mandibular molars with irreversible symptomatic pulpitis. Provides RCT-level evidence for preemptive pharmacological strategies (dexamethasone and diclofenac potassium) that improve inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) success and reduce postoperative pain. Relevant to [[wiki/drug/analgesics/rodrigues-2024-preemptive-dexamethasone-diclofenac-endodontic-rct]].

## Three-line Summary

Preemptive oral dexamethasone (4 mg) or diclofenac potassium (50 mg), administered 60 minutes before IANB with 4% articaine, significantly increased anesthetic success in mandibular molars with irreversible symptomatic pulpitis compared to placebo (39.3% and 21.4% vs. 3.6%).

Both drugs reduced postoperative pain versus placebo at 24, 48, and 72 hours; dexamethasone was additionally superior at the 6-hour mark, while diclofenac showed intermediate behavior at 6–12 hours with no significant difference from either group.

No statistically significant difference was found between dexamethasone and diclofenac in anesthetic success rates, suggesting either is a viable preemptive adjunct, with dexamethasone offering earlier postoperative pain relief.

하악 구치부 비가역적 증상성 치수염 환자에서 하치조신경 차단마취 (Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block, IANB) 60분 전 경구 덱사메타손 (Dexamethasone) 4 mg 또는 디클로페낙 칼륨 (Diclofenac Potassium) 50 mg 투여 시, 마취 성공률이 위약군(3.6%) 대비 각각 39.3%, 21.4%로 유의하게 향상되었다.

두 약물 모두 위약 대비 수술 후 24, 48, 72시간 통증을 유의하게 감소시켰으며, 덱사메타손은 6시간 시점에서 추가적으로 우월한 진통 효과를 보였고, 디클로페낙은 6–12시간에 중간적 양상을 나타냈다.

마취 성공률에서 두 약물 간 유의한 차이는 없어, 두 가지 모두 선제적 보조제로 활용 가능하나 덱사메타손이 조기 수술 후 통증 조절에 더 유리한 것으로 결론지을 수 있다.

## Document Information

- **Journal**: International Endodontic Journal
- **Citation**: Vol 57, Issue 5, Pages 520–532, 2024
- **Study design**: Randomized double-blind placebo-controlled clinical trial
- **Sample size**: 84 patients (28 per group)
- **Setting**: Mandibular molars with irreversible symptomatic pulpitis

## Key Contributions

1. Demonstrates that preemptive corticosteroid (dexamethasone) or NSAID (diclofenac potassium) significantly improves IANB success rate in hot teeth — a well-known clinical problem.
2. Quantifies the magnitude of effect: dexamethasone 39.3% vs. diclofenac 21.4% vs. placebo 3.6% — all three differ significantly, but drug-vs-drug difference is not significant.
3. Provides time-resolved postoperative pain data at 6, 12, 24, 48, and 72 hours, delineating the temporal profile of each intervention.
4. Identifies dexamethasone's early advantage (6 h) over placebo, with diclofenac showing intermediate, non-significant behavior at that early timepoint.

## Methodology

- **Population**: 84 patients with mandibular molars diagnosed with irreversible symptomatic pulpitis
- **Groups (n=28 each)**:
  - Dexamethasone 4 mg (oral, 60 min preoperative)
  - Diclofenac potassium 50 mg (oral, 60 min preoperative)
  - Placebo (oral, 60 min preoperative)
- **Anesthetic protocol**: IANB with 4% articaine + 1:200,000 epinephrine
- **Success criteria**: Cold thermal test at 15 min post-injection; modified Numerical Rating Scale (mNRS) for pain
- **Preoperative pain assessment**: Cold thermal test + mNRS
- **Postoperative monitoring**: mNRS at 6, 12, 24, 48, and 72 hours
- **Blinding**: Double-blind; randomized allocation
- **Treatment**: Single-session endodontic treatment + provisional restoration

## Key Results

| Outcome | Dexamethasone | Diclofenac | Placebo | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IANB success rate | 39.3% | 21.4% | 3.6% | <0.001 |
| Postoperative pain at 6 h | Superior | Intermediate (NS) | Reference | <0.001 (dexa vs placebo) |
| Postoperative pain at 12 h | NS | NS | Reference | >0.05 |
| Postoperative pain at 24–72 h | Superior | Superior | Reference | <0.001 |
| Dexamethasone vs. Diclofenac (IANB success) | — | NS | — | >0.05 |

- IANB baseline success in irreversible symptomatic pulpitis is extremely low (~3.6% placebo), consistent with the "hot tooth" literature.
- Both active treatments improved success, but even dexamethasone only reached ~39.3% — supplemental anesthesia strategies remain necessary in many cases.

## Limitations

- Abstract-only source; full methodology details (randomization, allocation concealment, dropout rates) not fully verifiable.
- IANB success rate remained low even with preemptive medication (~39%), underscoring that pharmacological premedication alone is insufficient in all cases.
- Single-session endodontic treatment may not reflect all clinical scenarios.
- Sample size (n=28/group) may limit detection of smaller between-drug differences.
- No long-term follow-up beyond 72 hours.

## Glossary

- **IANB**: 하치조신경 차단마취 (Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block) — standard mandibular nerve block technique
- **Irreversible symptomatic pulpitis**: 비가역적 증상성 치수염 — inflamed pulp with pain that does not resolve; "hot tooth" scenario
- **Preemptive analgesia**: 선제적 진통 (Preemptive Analgesia) — administering analgesics before painful stimulus to blunt central sensitization
- **mNRS**: modified Numerical Rating Scale — pain scale used in this study
- **Articaine**: 아티카인 (Articaine) — amide local anesthetic used at 4% concentration with epinephrine
- **Dexamethasone**: 덱사메타손 (Dexamethasone) — glucocorticoid; anti-inflammatory corticosteroid
- **Diclofenac potassium**: 디클로페낙 칼륨 (Diclofenac Potassium) — NSAID; potassium salt for faster absorption than sodium salt
