## Why Ingested

혈소판의 성분(platelets, leukocytes)과 성장인자(growth factors) 구성을 다루는 논문 요청에 대응해 PMC 풀텍스트로 인제스트. 기존 위키에는 PRF의 개별 임상 결과나 특정 세대(PRF 단독) 기전을 다룬 논문은 많지만, PRP·PRF·CGF **세 세대를 나란히 비교**하며 각 성장인자(TGF-β, BMP, VEGF, PDGF)의 분자 신호전달·방출 동역학·세포 구성(CD34+/CD45+/CD105+)까지 통합 정리한 기초 생물학 리뷰가 없었다. [[bone-regeneration/cho-2026-prf-bone-regeneration-mechanisms-scoping-review]]가 PRF 단일 세대의 기전(골면역학·대식세포 극성전환)을 깊이 다룬 반면, 본 논문(Giannotti 2023)은 PRP→PRF→CGF 3세대 전체의 제조 프로토콜·혈소판 농축배율(3–5배→4–8배→15.5–17.8배)·성장인자 방출기간(1시간→7–10일→28일)을 비교해 범위를 확장(extends)한다.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (University of Salento/CNR, Genes 2023) comparing the three generations of autologous platelet concentrates (APCs) — platelet-rich plasma (PRP), platelet-rich fibrin (PRF), and concentrated growth factors (CGF) — across preparation protocols, growth-factor composition (TGF-β, BMP, VEGF, PDGF signaling), and clinical applications.

Platelet/growth-factor concentration rises across generations (PRP 3–8× whole blood, A-PRF 17.8×, CGF 15.5×) with progressively longer growth-factor release: PRP releases nearly all content within 1 hour, PRF sustains release 7–10 days, A-PRF up to 14 days, CGF up to 28 days (VEGF peak ~day 14, TGF-β1/BMP-2 peak ~day 21); CGF also retains CD34+/CD45+/CD105+ progenitor-like cells capable of osteoblast and endothelial differentiation.

CGF is framed as the most effective current-generation APC (highest growth-factor content, simplest preparation, sustained delivery kinetics) with the widest clinical evidence in oral surgery/dental implantology, bone-defect regeneration, and facial rejuvenation — but the review is narrative (no PRISMA/systematic search protocol), and conflicting CGF-implant-stability findings (Palermo 2023 positive vs Özveri Koyuncu null) are attributed to technique differences (implant-surface permeation vs socket placement) rather than resolved by pooled evidence.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Provides a unified molecular map of the four growth-factor families central to APC biology — TGF-β (Smad2/3/4), BMP (RA-Smad1/5/8 + Smad4), VEGF-A (VEGFR-1/2 → PLC/PI3K/Akt/MAPK), and PDGF (tyrosine-kinase receptors → Src/PI3K/PLC-γ/RAS) — tying each to specific regenerative functions (angiogenesis, osteoblast chemotaxis, ECM synthesis, chemotaxis of inflammatory cells).
- Systematically contrasts preparation protocols (centrifugation speed/time/tube composition) for PRP (two-stage, 5600→2400 rpm, anticoagulant + bovine thrombin/CaCl₂), PRF (single-stage, 3000 rpm/10 min, no anticoagulant, endogenous thrombin), and CGF (alternating-speed Medifuge protocol, 13 min total) and their subtypes (P-PRP/L-PRP; P-PRF/L-PRF/I-PRF/A-PRF).
- Quantifies platelet-concentration fold-increase over whole blood across generations (PRGF ~3×, PRP 4–8×, A-PRF 17.8×, CGF 15.5×) and links this to growth-factor release kinetics reported across multiple independent studies (Masuki, Qiao, Park, Schär, Kobayashi, Borsani, Honda, Stanca, Isobe).
- Characterizes the CGF-resident cell population (CD34+/CD45+/CD105+, Oct3/4+/Nanog+) and its capacity to differentiate into osteoblasts (Stanca et al.) and endothelial progenitor-like cells (Calabriso et al.), providing a cellular (not just acellular growth-factor) mechanism for CGF's regenerative activity.
- Tabulates head-to-head clinical application evidence (facial rejuvenation, ear/nose cartilage reconstruction, bone-defect repair, dental implantology, neural differentiation) with generation-specific outcome comparisons.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative literature review (not a formal systematic review — no PRISMA flow diagram, database list, or search date range reported). Structured in four main sections: (1) growth-factor biology background; (2) APC generations (PRP/PRF/CGF) — preparation, subtypes, comparison of devices/growth-factor release/cellular content/clinical applications; (3) allogeneic platelet gel treatments; (4) conclusions. Draws on primary in-vitro/in-vivo studies (including several from the authors' own group — Stanca, Calabriso, Giannotti) plus device/protocol literature.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Generation | Platelet fold vs whole blood | Growth-factor release duration | Peak/notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| PRP (PRGF variant) | ~3× | ~1 hour (bolus) | Supports only the initial regeneration phase |
| PRP (standard) | 4–8× | ~1 hour (bolus) | Unstable L-PRP matrix disintegrates during culture |
| PRF | — | 7–10 days | More consistent/sustained release than PRP |
| A-PRF | 17.8× | up to 14 days | Softer, denser fibrin clot than P-PRF/L-PRF |
| CGF | 15.5× | up to 28 days | VEGF peak ~day 14; TGF-β1/BMP-2 peak ~day 21, remain high to day 28; MMP-2/9 peak ~day 7 |

CGF vs PRP/PRF: 1.5× more VEGF than PRP/PRF (Park et al.); similar TGF-β/PDGF/VEGF/IGF-1 to PRF but both significantly higher FGF-b than PRP (Qiao et al.); CGF and A-PRF contain similar growth-factor amounts, both higher than PRP (Masuki et al.).

Dental implantology outcomes: CGF accelerates osseointegration and improves implant stability (Shetye et al.; Palermo et al. — implant pre-permeated with CGF before insertion) vs a null CGF-on-implant-stability finding when CGF was placed in the osteotomy site rather than on the implant surface (Özveri Koyuncu et al.) — a technique-dependent discrepancy, not resolved by pooled data in this narrative review.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative (not systematic) review — no reproducible search protocol, risk of selective citation, no formal risk-of-bias or GRADE appraisal of the underlying primary studies.
- Regulatory context is Italy/EU-specific (Directive 2002/98/EC blood-component framework); classification of APCs as "blood derivatives" vs "medicinal products" varies by country, limiting direct generalizability of the regulatory discussion.
- Several cited studies are in-vitro/animal or from the authors' own research group (Stanca, Calabriso, Giannotti et al.), so the CGF-superiority conclusion partly reflects the authors' own prior findings rather than fully independent replication.
- The Özveri Koyuncu vs Palermo/Shetye implant-stability discrepancy is explained post hoc by technique difference (implant surface permeation vs osteotomy placement) without a head-to-head trial isolating that variable.
- The authors call for further characterization of APC composition and exploration of additional application fields (only briefly touched: neural regeneration via CGF, Borsani et al. 2020).

## 7. Glossary

- **APC (Autologous Platelet Concentrate)**: umbrella term for PRP/PRF/CGF — biomaterials derived from centrifuging a patient's own blood, containing concentrated platelets, leukocytes, and growth factors.
- **PRP (Platelet-Rich Plasma)**: first-generation APC; two-stage centrifugation with anticoagulant + exogenous thrombin/CaCl₂ activation; rapid (~1 hr) growth-factor release.
- **PRF (Platelet-Rich Fibrin)**: second-generation APC; single-stage centrifugation without anticoagulant, endogenous fibrin clot; sustained (7–14 day) growth-factor release.
- **CGF (Concentrated Growth Factor)**: third-generation APC; alternating-speed centrifugation (Medifuge); densest fibrin scaffold, highest growth-factor content, longest (up to 28-day) release, retains CD34+/CD45+/CD105+ progenitor-like cells.
- **TGF-β/BMP/VEGF/PDGF**: the four growth-factor families detailed — transforming growth factor-beta (wound healing/ECM/osteoblast chemotaxis), bone morphogenetic proteins (osteogenic differentiation), vascular endothelial growth factor (angiogenesis), platelet-derived growth factor (chemotaxis/proliferation of repair cells).
- **I-PRF (Injectable PRF)**: liquid-state PRF variant (low-speed, short centrifugation) usable for soft-tissue/mucosal injection, e.g., facial rejuvenation.
