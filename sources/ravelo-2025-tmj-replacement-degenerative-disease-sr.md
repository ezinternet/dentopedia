## Why Ingested

The wiki's only prior page on total TMJ replacement (TJR), [[wiki/tmj/guzman-2025-tmj-replacement-customized-prostheses]], is a single-institution n=6 case series with no comparator arm. This systematic review sits above it in the evidence hierarchy — 16 studies, 760 participants, ROBINS-I risk-of-bias + GRADE certainty assessment — and becomes the page a reader should land on first for "what does the evidence say about TJR in degenerative/inflammatory TMJ disease," with the case series providing the granular single-center detail underneath it. It also connects the surgical endpoint to the wiki's inflammatory-arthritis TMJ pages, since juvenile idiopathic arthritis and rheumatoid arthritis appear among the indications, and sits at the far end of the same conservative-to-surgical ladder as the companion ingest [[wiki/tmj/aiello-2025-tmj-arthritis-rheumatic-diseases-rehabilitative-pain-relief-sr]] (conservative pain management in TMJ arthritis).

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (Cochrane Handbook methods, PRISMA reporting; PubMed/Embase/Scopus/Web of Science + gray literature, 1997–July 2024) of total TMJ replacement in 579 participants (of 760 total analyzed) with degenerative and/or inflammatory joint disease — osteoarthritis most frequent, followed by osteoarthrosis, juvenile idiopathic arthritis, and rheumatoid arthritis; ROBINS-I used for risk of bias in the 16 non-randomised included studies, GRADE for certainty of evidence.

Preoperative maximum mouth opening averaged 24.32 ± 5.8 mm with 76.18% of participants reporting moderate-to-severe pain and a soft/liquid diet; postoperatively all participants reported reduced or absent pain, progression to a solid diet, and mouth opening improved to 40.74 ± 3.1 mm; prosthesis replacement due to system failure occurred in only 4.07%. These are uncontrolled before-after outcomes — every included study lacked a control group and blinding, all 16 were GRADE-rated low-quality evidence, so the improvement cannot be attributed to the prosthesis with the confidence the raw numbers imply.

The review's most clinically useful finding is a negative one: it was not possible to identify the best timing for joint replacement, whether measured by time since diagnosis, time since starting non-surgical treatment, or number of prior surgeries — the question "when do we stop conserving and operate" remains unanswered by the current evidence base.

## 세줄요약

코크란 핸드북(Cochrane Handbook) 방법론과 PRISMA 보고 기준을 따른 체계적 문헌고찰(PubMed·Embase·Scopus·Web of Science + 회색문헌, 1997~2024년 7월)로, 퇴행성 및/또는 염증성 관절질환 환자 579명(전체 분석대상 760명 중)의 전악관절치환술(total TMJ replacement)을 다룸 — 진단은 골관절염(osteoarthritis)이 가장 흔했고 골관절증(osteoarthrosis), 소아특발성관절염(juvenile idiopathic arthritis, JIA), 류마티스관절염(rheumatoid arthritis, RA) 순; 비무작위 연구 16편의 비뚤림위험(risk of bias)은 ROBINS-I, 근거등급(certainty of evidence)은 GRADE로 평가.

수술 전 최대 개구량(maximum mouth opening)은 평균 24.32 ± 5.8 mm였고 76.18%가 중등도~중증 통증과 연식/유동식 식이를 보고했으며, 수술 후 전원이 통증 감소·소실과 고형식 회복을 보고했고 개구량은 40.74 ± 3.1 mm로 개선; 시스템 실패로 인한 보철물 재치환은 4.07%에 불과. 다만 이는 대조군 없는 전후비교(before-after) 결과이며 포함된 16편 전부가 대조군·눈가림 없이 GRADE 낮은 근거등급으로 평가되어, 수치가 시사하는 만큼의 확신으로 개선을 보철물 자체에 귀속시킬 수 없음.

이 리뷰의 가장 유용한 정직한 소견은 음성 결과다 — 진단 후 경과시간·비수술적 치료 시작 후 경과시간·이전 수술 횟수 중 어느 기준으로도 관절치환술의 최적 시행 시점을 특정할 수 없었으며, "언제 보존적 치료를 멈추고 수술해야 하는가"라는 임상의의 실제 질문에 현재 근거는 답하지 못함.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Synthesizes 16 studies (760 participants total, 1997–2024 search window) on total TMJ replacement, the largest and highest-tier (SR with ROBINS-I + GRADE) evidence base in this wiki for alloplastic TJR, above the existing single-center case series [[wiki/tmj/guzman-2025-tmj-replacement-customized-prostheses]].
- Quantifies indication-stage severity (mean preop opening 24.32 ± 5.8 mm; 76.18% moderate-to-severe pain, soft/liquid diet) and postoperative outcome (opening 40.74 ± 3.1 mm, universal pain reduction/resolution, diet progression to solids) across the pooled cohort.
- Explicitly reports the prosthesis system-failure/replacement rate as low (4.07% of 760), consistent with prior TJR-survival literature (~97% survival, per Lima et al. cited within).
- States a clear negative finding with direct clinical relevance: no reliable predictor of optimal replacement timing (time since diagnosis, time since non-surgical treatment start, or number of prior surgeries) could be identified from the pooled evidence.
- Names juvenile idiopathic arthritis and rheumatoid arthritis among the TJR indications, linking the surgical end-stage to the wiki's inflammatory-arthritis TMJ pages rather than only the osteoarthritis literature.
- Runs and reports a formal ROBINS-I risk-of-bias assessment and GRADE certainty rating for all 16 included non-randomised studies — a methodological rigor the underlying case-series literature (including [[wiki/tmj/guzman-2025-tmj-replacement-customized-prostheses]]) does not itself provide.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Systematic review per the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, reported per updated PRISMA items; PROSPERO registration 602516.
- PICO: P = participants >15y with degenerative and/or inflammatory TMJ disease; I = total TMJ replacement with prostheses; C = indications and use of TMJ prostheses; O = clinical outcomes ≥12 months post-installation.
- Search: PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, 1997–July 2024 (1997 chosen because U.S. regulation began requiring TMJ-implant manufacturers to submit premarket safety/efficacy data that year); gray literature via Google Scholar and Open Access Theses and Dissertations (OATD); no language or study-design restriction.
- Two independent reviewers (V.R., S.O.), Kappa index 0.617; third reviewer (R.S.) for discrepancy resolution. Duplicates removed via Mendeley; missing data requested from authors with a six-week response window (unresolved fields marked "not described").
- Inclusion: human patients >15y, degenerative/inflammatory TMJ disease (or ≥50% of sample if mixed pathology), joint prosthesis replacement (± concurrent orthognathic surgery), ≥12-month postoperative follow-up. Exclusion: sample <10 patients, animal/cadaver studies, not full text.
- Risk of bias: ROBINS-I tool for non-randomised studies across 7 domains (confounding, participant selection, exposure measurement, post-exposure interventions, missing data, outcome measurement, outcome reporting), rated low/moderate/serious/no-information.
- Certainty of evidence: GRADE profiler (GRADEpro v7.11.0) per Cochrane/GRADE Working Group criteria (High/Moderate/Low/Very low).
- Heterogeneity: Cochran Q test found significant heterogeneity across included studies (p = 0.001), which combined with inconsistent outcome reporting/measurement across studies precluded meta-analysis — narrative synthesis only.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Screening yield**: 687 identified → 242 duplicates removed → 445 title/abstract screened → 23 full-text reviewed → 16 included (7 excluded: 4 not full text, 2 <12-month follow-up, 1 did not use total TMJ prostheses as definitive treatment). Gray literature: 665 → 650 excluded at title/abstract → 5 full-text reviewed, all excluded (did not meet inclusion criteria).
- **Cohort**: 760 participants total; 78 (10.26%) male, 682 (89.73%) female; age range 15–75y, mean 39.7 ± 9.16y; follow-up 12–120 months across studies (10 studies with 2–5y follow-up, 4 studies with 5–10y follow-up). Study origin: 50% US, 31.24% UK, plus Australia, Canada, China. 8 prospective / 8 retrospective; 12 quasi-experimental, 2 cohort, 2 non-randomised clinical trial designs; none blinded.
- **Prostheses**: 1231 total fitted (874 customized, 357 stock); 828 bilateral, 308 unilateral (left side more prevalent); TMJ Concepts most used, followed by Zimmer Biomet and Christensen.
- **Indication/diagnosis subgroup (n=579/760, 76.18%)**: degenerative and/or inflammatory joint disease — osteoarthritis most frequent, then osteoarthrosis, juvenile idiopathic arthritis, and rheumatoid arthritis. Preoperative maximum mouth opening 24.32 ± 5.8 mm (range 18–36.4 mm); soft-to-liquid diet; pain distribution: 574 moderate-to-severe, 123 moderate, 21 mild-to-moderate (one 42-participant study reported no clinical features at diagnosis).
- **System-failure replacement**: only 31/760 (4.07%).
- **Pretreatment**: of 619 participants across 9 studies reporting prior treatment, 327 (52.82%) had prior surgical intervention (arthroscopy, discectomy, steroid infiltration ± condylar repositioning), 39 had drug-only conservative treatment (Sarlabous et al.), 210 (33.92%) had no prior treatment; 43.02% of the full sample had some joint intervention before TJR.
- **Postoperative outcome (pooled, all participants)**: reduced or absent pain, diet progression to solids, mouth opening improved to 40.74 ± 3.1 mm. At long follow-up (>5y) opening decrease of only 0.5–1 mm was observed (2–5 mm decrease at ≤5y), neither considered clinically significant; no study reported joint changes or need for revision at long-term follow-up.
- **Orthognathic co-surgery**: 7/16 studies combined TJR with orthognathic surgery (24.47% of total sample, 186 participants), of whom 91.65% had bilateral prostheses.
- **Risk of bias (ROBINS-I, 16 studies)**: confounding — 12 moderate, 4 serious (differing follow-up timing across arms); selection — 4 serious (differential dropout across follow-up visits); intervention classification — 3 moderate, 12 serious (quasi-experimental design); deviation from intended intervention — low risk in all; missing data — low in all but 2 studies; outcome measurement/reporting — low risk in all (specific inclusion/exclusion criteria). Overall: 3 studies moderate risk, 2 studies high risk (rest not explicitly stated in retrieved text beyond domain breakdown).
- **GRADE**: all 16 included studies rated **low certainty of evidence**, each with at least one high-risk domain — the review states findings are therefore "ambiguous" and results need careful evaluation.
- **Heterogeneity**: Cochran Q test significant (p = 0.001) — studies not homogeneous; meta-analysis was judged inappropriate given this heterogeneity plus inconsistent/insufficiently reported statistics across studies.
- **Design caveat (critical)**: every quantitative gain reported above is a within-group before-after comparison; no included study had a control/comparator arm, and none blinded investigators, surgeons, or outcome assessors. The GRADE-low rating applies to all 16 studies without exception.
- **Negative finding (headline)**: it was not possible to identify the best timing for joint replacement surgery based on time since diagnosis, time since starting non-surgical treatment, or number of previous surgeries — the pooled evidence cannot answer the clinically central question of when to move from conservative/minimally-invasive management to TJR.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No control/comparator group in any included study; no blinding anywhere in the evidence base — before-after change cannot be causally attributed to the prosthesis alone with the certainty the raw magnitude of change (e.g., ~16 mm opening gain) might suggest.
- All 16 studies GRADE-low certainty; heterogeneity (Cochran Q p = 0.001) precluded meta-analysis, so no pooled effect size is available — only narrative synthesis.
- Attrition at longer follow-up intervals is severe in several source studies (e.g., 3 of an original cohort remained at 120 months in one study), limiting confidence in the durability claims beyond ~5 years even though the direction of the reported trend (small, non-clinically-significant further opening loss) is consistent across studies.
- No standardized reporting of statistical dispersion/central tendency across included studies; qualitative measures were common, limiting quantitative synthesis.
- The review authors explicitly flag that optimal TJR timing cannot be derived from current evidence — future prospective, controlled, standardized-outcome studies are needed to answer that question.

## 6. Related Work

- Cites Lima et al. (systematic review with meta-analysis) reporting ~97% prosthesis survival at ≥12-month follow-up, consistent with this review's 4.07% system-failure-replacement figure.
- Cites Wolford et al. and Mercuri et al. on long-term (10–14y) TJR safety/effectiveness, both noted as limited by small long-term follow-up cohorts (55 participants via questionnaire; email survey, respectively).
- Cites Hsieh et al. on facial morphology differences in juvenile idiopathic arthritis with vs. without TMJ involvement, and Kim et al. (SR + meta-analysis) on orthognathic surgery outcomes combined with TJR.
- Directly relevant to [[wiki/tmj/guzman-2025-tmj-replacement-customized-prostheses]] (single-center n=6 case series, same clinical intervention, no comparator) — this SR is the evidence-hierarchy tier above it.
- Relevant to [[wiki/tmj/aiello-2025-tmj-arthritis-rheumatic-diseases-rehabilitative-pain-relief-sr]] (companion ingest; SR of conservative pain management for TMJ arthritis in rheumatic disease, only 5 low-quality studies found) — together the two reviews describe a treatment ladder where conservative care is nearly evidence-free and the surgical end reports large uncontrolled gains but no evidence on when to escalate.

## 7. Glossary

- **TJR (Total Joint Replacement)**: 전악관절치환술 — alloplastic replacement of both the condylar and fossa/glenoid components of the TMJ with a prosthesis.
- **ROBINS-I (Risk Of Bias In Non-randomised Studies of Interventions)**: 비무작위 중재연구의 비뚤림위험 평가도구 — 7-domain tool for assessing bias in non-RCT intervention studies.
- **GRADE (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluations)**: 근거등급 평가체계 — framework for rating certainty of evidence (High/Moderate/Low/Very low).
- **PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses)**: 체계적 문헌고찰 보고 표준.
- **JIA (Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis)**: 소아특발성관절염 — chronic childhood-onset inflammatory arthritis that can involve the TMJ and drive condylar destruction.
- **RA (Rheumatoid Arthritis)**: 류마티스관절염 — autoimmune inflammatory polyarthritis, a TJR indication in this review's cohort.
- **Cochran Q test**: 코크란 Q 검정 — statistical test for heterogeneity across studies in a systematic review/meta-analysis.
- **Maximum mouth opening (interincisal opening)**: 최대 개구량 — distance between maxillary and mandibular incisal edges at maximal jaw opening, a standard TMJ function outcome measure.
