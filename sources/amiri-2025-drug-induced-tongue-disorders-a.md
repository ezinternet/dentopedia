## Why Ingested

Comprehensive review cataloging drug classes (antineoplastics, anti-infectives) causing tongue discoloration, black hairy tongue, dysgeusia, and glossitis — directly extends the medication-induced chemosensory and oral mucosal adverse-effect framework alongside [[oral-medicine/salivary-chemosensory/kohli-2023-medication-induced-xerostomia-orofacial-pain]].

## Three-line Summary

A narrative review of 208 case reports identifying drug classes and clinical presentations of drug-induced tongue disorders, with antineoplastic/immunomodulating agents and anti-infectives as the most implicated.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Systematic catalog of drug-induced tongue disorders across 208 case reports/case series (1980–2022)
- Identified antineoplastic and immunomodulating agents and anti-infectives for systemic use as the most common culprit drug classes
- Most frequent presentations: tongue discoloration and black hairy tongue (BHT)
- Demonstrated that good oral hygiene plus drug discontinuation resolves most cases
- Clinical utility: aids recognition and management to prevent patient alarm and drug non-compliance

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

*Abstract-only*

- 208 case reports/series included
- Most implicated drug classes: antineoplastic/immunomodulating agents; anti-infectives for systemic use
- Most common tongue disorders: tongue discoloration; black hairy tongue (BHT)
- Management: discontinue offending drug + maintain good oral hygiene → significant improvement or complete resolution
- Drug-induced tongue disorders characterized as "not rare" adverse drug reactions; majority benign

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative (not systematic) review design; potential selection bias in case report literature
- Relies on published case reports/series — incidence underestimated (publication bias toward unusual cases)
- No formal quality appraisal or meta-analysis
- Causality often not rigorously established in individual case reports
- Full-text not retrieved for this wiki entry

## 7. Glossary

- **Black hairy tongue (BHT):** Elongation and discoloration of filiform papillae, often caused by antibiotics or antineoplastics disrupting oral flora
- **Dysgeusia:** Distorted or unpleasant taste perception
- **Ageusia:** Complete loss of taste
- **Hypogeusia:** Reduced taste sensitivity
- **Glossitis:** Inflammation of the tongue
- **Glossodynia / stomatodynia:** Burning sensation of the tongue/mouth (synonym: burning mouth syndrome)
