## Why Ingested

Gutta-percha (GP) cones are contaminated during storage/handling and cannot be heat-sterilised, so chairside chemical decontamination is a mandatory but under-standardised obturation step — yet [[endodontics/obturation/surana-bhandari-2026-single-cone-hydraulic-sealer-irrigation-protocol]] and other single-cone workflow pages treat cone asepsis only in passing. This SR is the ANCHOR of a 4-paper GP-cone-decontamination cluster that pins down which agent (NaOCl vs CHX), concentration, and exposure time actually decontaminate cones, extending the obturation literature toward the cross-infection-control gap. Pairs directly with the chairside-disinfection sibling [[endodontics/bracciale-2020-bacterial-contamination-gutta-percha-chairside-disinfection]].

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA, INPLASY-registered; 309 records screened → 7 in vitro studies included) comparing NaOCl vs chlorhexidine (CHX) for gutta-percha (GP) cone chairside decontamination against E. faecalis, S. aureus, and C. albicans at various concentrations and exposure times.

Higher-concentration NaOCl (5.25%/6%) eliminates E. faecalis and S. aureus from GP cones within 1–5 min and is the gold standard; CHX data are limited but CHX (± cetrimide) appears promising, particularly against Candida albicans where it was the only solution effective without agitation; 5/7 studies rated high risk of bias.

The fragmented, heterogeneous in vitro evidence (single-species testing, varying concentrations/times) prevents meta-analysis and definitive protocol formulation; a clinically practical minimum is 1-min immersion in 5.25% NaOCl before cone placement.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰(PRISMA, INPLASY 등록; 309편 스크리닝 → 체외 연구 7편 포함) — 거타퍼차(gutta-percha, GP) cone의 의장소독에서 차아염소산나트륨(NaOCl) vs 클로르헥시딘(chlorhexidine, CHX)의 항균 효능을 E. faecalis·S. aureus·C. albicans 대상으로 비교.

고농도 NaOCl(5.25%/6%)이 1–5분 내 E. faecalis·S. aureus를 제거(금본위)하며, CHX(±cetrimide)는 Candida albicans에 agitation 없이 효과적인 유일한 물질로 유망하나 E. faecalis 데이터는 부족; 7편 중 5편이 높은 비뚤림 위험.

단일 균종 실험·농도/시간 이질성으로 메타분석 불가하고 확정적 프로토콜 제시가 어려우며, 임상적 최소 기준으로 5.25% NaOCl 1분 침지가 권장됨.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **First systematic review** to compare NaOCl vs CHX for GP cone decontamination specifically against *E. faecalis*, *S. aureus*, and *C. albicans* at 0–10 min exposure intervals.
- Consolidates fragmented in vitro evidence into a clinical bottom line: **higher-concentration NaOCl remains the gold standard** for GP cone disinfection, eliminating common bacterial pathogens within 1–5 min.
- Flags **CHX (especially with additives such as cetrimide) as a promising alternative**, particularly where fungal contamination is a concern and where NaOCl's deteriorative effect on GP elasticity is undesirable.
- Exposes methodological heterogeneity (species tested, concentrations, exposure times, CFU quantification method) that undermines cross-study comparison and blocks evidence-based protocol formulation.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **PICO**: In GP cones (P), does disinfection with NaOCl (I) vs CHX (C) achieve greater decontamination at specific time intervals (O)?
- **Databases (6)**: PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, SciELO, Scopus, LILACS; plus manual hand-search. Last search 30 Sep 2024. No publication-year restriction; English-language only.
- **Search terms**: ((gutta-percha cones) OR (gutta-percha points) OR (gutta-percha)) AND ((decontamination) OR (disinfection)) AND ((solutions) OR (chemical agents)).
- **Selection**: Two reviewers (R.A., E.N.) independent, duplicate; third reviewer (I.D.) for disagreements. Reference lists screened.
- **Data extraction** into standardised Excel: author, year, sample size, irrigants, microbial cultures, methodology, efficacy-evaluation method, results.
- **Risk of bias**: modified Cochrane tool, 6 parameters — sample-size calculation, randomisation, blinding (examiner + outcome assessment), control group, standardisation, antimicrobial-efficacy measurement (CFU quantification); rated low / moderate (some concerns) / high.
- All 7 included studies were **in vitro**; efficacy uniformly assessed by colony-forming-unit (CFU) quantification (only 2/7 used digital counting).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Screening funnel**: 308 database records + 1 hand-searched = 309; 93 duplicates removed → 216 title/abstract screened → 32 full-text → **7 included** (25 full-texts excluded for methodological limitations, 5 for unavailable full text, 179 irrelevant).
- **Risk of bias**: 5/7 high, 2/7 moderate. Worst parameters: sample-size calculation (7/7), outcome-assessment blinding (7/7), randomisation (5/7).
- **Included studies (7)**: Pauletto 2024 (98 cones, *C. albicans*); Al-Jobory 2021 (40, *E. faecalis*); Asnaashari 2020 (70, *S. aureus*, incl. cold plasma comparator); Chandrappa 2014 (280, *E. faecalis* + *S. aureus*); Brito-Júnior 2012 (60, *E. faecalis*); Rai 2019 (180, *E. faecalis* + *S. aureus*); Banka 2024 (60, *E. faecalis*).
- **NaOCl**: 5.25% NaOCl completely inhibited *E. faecalis* (CFU = 0) immediately and at 7 days (Al-Jobory); 5.25% NaOCl > 2% CHX against *E. faecalis* at 1 min (Banka, ~8.5× greater CFU reduction, p = 0.003); 6% NaOCl effective against *E. faecalis* within 3 min (Rai). Effect is concentration- and time-dependent.
- **Exceptions / conflicts**: neither 5.25% NaOCl nor 2% CHX eliminated *E. faecalis* after 5 min in Chandrappa; Brito-Júnior found no significant difference between 2.5% NaOCl and 2% CHX at 5 min. NaOCl less effective on facultative anaerobes.
- **CHX**: limited data on *E. faecalis*; 2% CHX effective against *E. faecalis* at 5 min in some studies; CHX + cetrimide eliminated *E. faecalis* within 1–5 min (Rai). Against **fungi**, 2% CHX was the only solution effective **without agitation** at both 1 and 5 min for *C. albicans* (Pauletto); CHX shows fungicidal + fungistatic action.
- **Bottom line**: higher-concentration NaOCl eliminates *E. faecalis* and *S. aureus* within 1–5 min (gold standard); CHX (± additives) is promising, especially against fungal species.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only **7 in vitro studies**, all moderate/high risk of bias — no meta-analysis possible (qualitative synthesis only).
- Marked heterogeneity: microbial species, irrigant concentrations, exposure times, and CFU-quantification accuracy vary across studies, blocking direct comparison.
- 5/7 studies tested only a single microorganism, whereas endodontic microbiota is polymicrobial and cooperative (mono-species testing underestimates real disinfection difficulty).
- Only 3/7 studies assessed multiple/extended exposure times (30 s–5 min); limited concentration range tested.
- Only 2/7 used digital CFU counting; manual counting is error-prone.
- **Future work**: more homogeneous, high-quality in vitro models with a broader range of endodontic pathogens (including coexisting species), more irrigant concentrations, and wider time intervals; further investigation of CHX (± additives) as a GP disinfectant against fungal species.

## 6. Related Work

- Sibling cluster (GP-cone decontamination): Bracciale et al. 2020 (chairside GP disinfection, bacterial contamination), Teles et al. 2024 (GP points MRSA/MSSA contamination), Raheja et al. 2025 (NaOCl / silver / chitosan nanoparticle GP cone disinfection).
- Obturation context: single-cone bioceramic/hydraulic-sealer workflows where cone asepsis precedes filling.
- Asnaashari 2020 within-study comparator: low-pressure radiofrequency cold plasma (LRFCP) as a non-chemical GP disinfection alternative (links to cold-plasma endodontics).

## 7. Glossary

- **GP (gutta-percha)**: century-old root-canal filling material; biocompatible, cost-effective; cannot be heat-sterilised, so requires chemical chairside decontamination.
- **NaOCl (sodium hypochlorite)**: broad-spectrum irrigant; oxidises sulfhydryl groups in bacterial enzymes, disrupts membranes; concentration- and time-dependent activity.
- **CHX (chlorhexidine digluconate)**: bactericidal via cytoplasmic precipitation/protein cross-linking; fungicidal + fungistatic; non-deteriorative to GP elasticity.
- **E. faecalis (Enterococcus faecalis)**: persister species linked to failed/recurrent root-canal treatment; survives nutrient deprivation and alkalinity.
- **S. aureus (Staphylococcus aureus)**: most prevalent GP-cone storage/glove-handling contaminant.
- **C. albicans (Candida albicans)**: opportunistic fungal pathogen in persistent endodontic infections.
- **CFU (colony-forming unit)**: gold-standard quantification of viable microbial cells; compares treated vs untreated controls.
- **MIC**: minimum inhibitory concentration.
- **Cetrimide**: cationic surfactant that enhances CHX antibacterial efficacy.
- **LRFCP**: low-pressure radiofrequency cold plasma; non-chemical GP disinfection comparator.
