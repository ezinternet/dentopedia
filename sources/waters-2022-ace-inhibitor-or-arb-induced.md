## Three-line Summary

Two dental patients on ACE inhibitor or ARB antihypertensives took their medication within 24 hours of general anesthesia for oral surgery, then developed persistent profound intraoperative hypotension refractory to conventional vasopressors (ephedrine, phenylephrine). Both cases were successfully rescued with vasopressin administration. The mechanism is depletion of the renin-angiotensin system by ACE-I/ARB, rendering sympathomimetics ineffective, while vasopressin acts via V1 receptors independently of that pathway.

## Abstract

Two case reports present the use of vasopressin for treating refractory hypotension associated with continued angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor (ACEI) or angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) therapy prior to general anesthesia for oral surgery. Both patients were treated in an ambulatory dental surgery clinic and took either their ACEI or ARB medication for hypertension within 24 hours prior to undergoing an intubated general anesthetic. Persistent profound hypotension was encountered intraoperatively that was refractory to treatment with traditional methods. However, the ACEI- or ARB-induced refractory hypotension was successfully managed with the administration of vasopressin.

## Key Points

- **Setting**: Ambulatory dental surgery clinic, intubated general anesthesia
- **Drug class**: ACE inhibitor OR ARB (antihypertensives)
- **Timing**: Medication taken within 24 hours prior to surgery (both cases)
- **Intraoperative event**: Persistent profound hypotension refractory to conventional vasopressors
- **Failed treatments**: Traditional vasopressors (ephedrine, phenylephrine implied by "traditional methods")
- **Rescue agent**: Vasopressin — successfully resolved hypotension in both cases
- **Mechanism**: ACE-I/ARB deplete renin-angiotensin system → conventional sympathomimetics fail → vasopressin via V1 receptors bypasses depleted pathway

## Clinical Implications

- Consider withholding ACE-I or ARB 24 hours before dental general anesthesia
- Vasopressin should be available as a rescue agent in dental GA settings for patients on these medications
- When conventional vasopressors fail in an ACE-I/ARB patient, vasopressin is the evidence-based alternative
