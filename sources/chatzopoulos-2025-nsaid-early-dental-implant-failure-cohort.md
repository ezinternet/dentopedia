## Why Ingested

This is the largest human cohort to date on the NSAID → early dental-implant-failure question (12,943 patients, 49,997 implants), ingested to fill the human-evidence gap flagged in [[wiki/drug/analgesics/etikala-2019-nsaids-periodontal-implant-therapy-review]], which noted the human evidence on NSAIDs and osseointegration was poor and conflicting. It supplies the adjusted odds-ratio numbers an NSAID-osseointegration overview needs.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective EHR cohort of 12,943 patients receiving 49,997 implants (NSAID group n=3133 vs non-NSAID control n=9810), testing whether common NSAID use is associated with early implant failure (removal within six months).

After adjustment for age, gender, diabetes and osteoporosis, ibuprofen raised the odds of early failure at both implant level (OR 2.29, 95% CI 1.48–3.55) and patient level (OR 2.87, 95% CI 1.83–4.51); naproxen was significant at implant level (OR 2.65, 95% CI 1.22–5.75) but not at patient level (p=0.0689).

Signals caution in post-operative analgesic planning after implant placement, but this is an association from a retrospective design with confounding by indication (NSAID users were older and had more diabetes and osteoporosis) — not proof of causation.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Largest known real-world human cohort addressing the NSAID → osseointegration / early implant failure question, moving the debate beyond animal models and small clinical trials.
- Quantifies adjusted odds ratios for two of the most common OTC NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) against early implant failure, at both implant and patient levels.
- Reports an association that persists after adjustment for major confounders (age, gender, diabetes, osteoporosis).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort using an EHR database.
- **Population**: 12,943 patients receiving 49,997 implants; NSAID group n=3133 vs non-NSAID control n=9810.
- **Primary outcome**: Early implant failure, defined as implant removal within six months of placement.
- **Analysis**: Multivariable logistic regression odds ratios adjusting for age, gender, diabetes, and osteoporosis; analyses conducted at both implant and patient levels.
- **Baseline imbalance**: The NSAID group was significantly older (62.8 vs 58.7 y, p<0.0001) and had higher prevalence of diabetes (22.0% vs 16.7%, p<0.0001) and osteoporosis (11.0% vs 7.0%, p<0.0001).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

After adjustment:

- **Implant-level** — Ibuprofen OR 2.29 (95% CI 1.48–3.55; p<0.0001); Naproxen OR 2.65 (95% CI 1.22–5.75; p=0.0137).
- **Patient-level** — Ibuprofen OR 2.87 (95% CI 1.83–4.51; p<0.0001); Naproxen p=0.0689 (not significant).
- **Conclusion**: Ibuprofen, and to a lesser extent naproxen, was associated with a significantly increased risk of early dental implant failure, independent of the other risk factors examined.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Retrospective design** — no randomization; the reported effect is an association, not causation.
- **Confounding by indication** — NSAID users were older and had more diabetes and osteoporosis; whatever prompted NSAID use (e.g., inflammatory/painful conditions) may itself track with failure risk, and residual confounding beyond the four adjusted variables is likely.
- **EHR data** — dose, duration, timing relative to placement, and adherence are not detailed in the abstract; outcome ascertainment depends on record completeness.
- **Naproxen** signal was inconsistent across levels (significant at implant level, NS at patient level), so its estimate is less robust.
- Full text was not retrieved (abstract-only), so subgroup analyses and methodological detail beyond the abstract are unavailable.

## 7. Glossary

- **NSAID** (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug): analgesic/anti-inflammatory class (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen) that inhibits cyclooxygenase and prostaglandin synthesis.
- **Osseointegration**: direct structural and functional connection between living bone and the surface of a load-bearing implant.
- **Early implant failure**: here defined as implant removal within six months of placement (failure of initial healing/osseointegration).
- **Odds Ratio (OR)**: measure of association between an exposure and an outcome; OR >1 indicates higher odds of the outcome in the exposed group.
- **Confounding by indication**: bias in which the reason a treatment is prescribed is itself associated with the outcome, distorting the apparent drug–outcome relationship.
- **EHR** (Electronic Health Record): digital patient record system used here as the data source.
