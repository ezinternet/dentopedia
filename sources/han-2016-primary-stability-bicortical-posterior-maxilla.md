## Why Ingested

Systematic in vitro validation of the four common stability metrics (ISQ, PTV, IT, RT) across monocortical vs bicortical conditions and four residual bone heights in posterior maxilla simulation. Provides the mechanistic groundwork linking bicortical fixation to ISQ advantage that is observed clinically in [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/bhave-2023-unicortical-bicortical-anchorage-posterior-maxilla]], and also highlights that IT ≠ ISQ — a clinically important caveat.

## Three-line Summary

In vitro 2-factor ANOVA (2 cortical conditions × 4 RBH: 3/5/8/12 mm), n=80 polyurethane bone blocks; Dentium Superline 4.3×10 mm implants; ISQ, PTV, IT, RT measured.
Bicortical condition showed significantly higher ISQ and PTV vs monocortical at ALL bone heights (p<0.001); B8 group (8 mm RBH bicortical) had highest ISQ (81.03) and IT; significant interaction between cortical condition × RBH for all metrics.
ISQ and PTV are clinically more reliable stability indicators than IT/RT; bicortical fixation improves stability across all RBH levels, and "tight hand feel" (IT) does not reliably predict ISQ-confirmed stability.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic 2-factor design isolating cortical condition AND RBH effects on 4 stability metrics
- ISQ/PTV reliably reflect bicortical advantage across all RBH; IT/RT do not consistently track ISQ
- IT paradox: M3 (3 mm monocortical) highest IT due to tapered implant body engaging apical cortex → clinical trap
- B12 group: bicortical NOT achieved (10 mm implant in 12 mm RBH) → demonstrates length must exceed RBH for bicortical
- Long-term marginal bone loss: prior Brånemark data showed no difference mono- vs bicortical → stability difference may not translate to survival

## Methodology

- **Model**: Polyurethane blocks (Sawbones), cancellous density 0.32 g/cm³; cortical epoxy 1 mm top/bottom
- **Monocortical (M)**: Cancellous only, single cortical plate opposite crest; subgroups M3/M5/M8/M12
- **Bicortical (B)**: Cancellous with cortical plates on both surfaces; subgroups B3/B5/B8/B12
- **Implant**: Dentium Superline 4.3 mm × 10 mm, tapered, SLA surface
- **Measurements**: ISQ (Osstell, 4 directions averaged), PTV (Periotest M, 3×4), IT (peak, digital gauge), RT (pull-out torque)
- **Statistics**: 2-factor ANOVA; post-hoc Tukey; simple main effects with Bonferroni correction; Pearson r between methods

- ISQ/PTV: bicortical > monocortical at all RBH (p<0.0125, adjusted)
- IT: bicortical > monocortical at RBH ≥5 mm; highest in M3 (tapered geometry artifact)
- ISQ–PTV correlation: Pearson r=−0.97 (p<0.001)
- ISQ–IT correlation: moderate r=0.58 (p<0.001); ISQ–RT: r=0.12 (NS)

## Limitations

- In vitro only; polyurethane ≠ human bone (no vascularization, no healing response)
- B12 group failed to achieve bicortical fixation (10 mm implant doesn't span 12 mm bone)
- Single implant design (Dentium Superline); different macrogeometries may differ

## Glossary

- **Monocortical**: Single cortical plate opposite to implant insertion site (crestal side bare)
- **Bicortical**: Cortical plates on both implant surfaces (crestal + apical/sinus-floor)
- **ISQ (Implant Stability Quotient)**: Osstell RFA-derived 0–100 score; >70 high stability
- **PTV (Periotest Value)**: −8 to +50; lower = stiffer; <0 = clinically stable
- **IT (Insertion Torque)**: Peak rotational resistance during implant insertion (Ncm)
- **RT (Removal Torque)**: Force required to unscrew implant; measures bone anchorage
