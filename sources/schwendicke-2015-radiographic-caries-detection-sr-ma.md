## Why Ingested

Landmark SR+MA (117 studies, 13,375 teeth, 19,108 surfaces) providing systematic quantitative evidence for radiographic caries detection accuracy stratified by lesion depth and location. Directly answers the clinical question of when proximal caries becomes radiographically visible on bitewing/PA — the largest synthesis on this topic in the dental literature as of 2015. Foundational reference for [[caries/caries]] and [[radiology/radiology]] on the sensitivity limits of bitewing radiography.

## Three-line Summary

Radiographic caries detection has high specificity (0.87–0.97) but poor sensitivity for initial and enamel lesions (Se ~0.24–0.42), improving only modestly for dentin lesions (Se 0.36–0.56) and reaching acceptable sensitivity (>0.60) for cavitated proximal lesions. Meta-analysis of 117 studies with 13,375 teeth confirms that bitewing/periapical radiography reliably detects caries late — primarily once lesions are cavitated or well into dentin. For high-caries-risk populations, supplementary detection methods beyond radiography are warranted for initial lesions.

## 세줄요약

방사선 사진을 이용한 우식 검출은 특이도 (specificity)가 0.87–0.97로 높지만, 초기 우식 (initial caries) 및 법랑질 우식의 민감도 (sensitivity)는 0.24–0.42에 불과하다. 상아질 우식 (dentin caries)은 민감도 0.36–0.56, 와동형성 우식 (cavitated caries)은 인접면 (proximal)에서 >0.60으로 허용 가능한 수준에 이른다. 117개 연구(13,375치아)를 포괄한 이 메타분석은 방사선 검출이 우식이 진행된 이후에야 신뢰할 수 있음을 확인하며, 고위험군에선 추가 검출 수단이 필요함을 지지한다.

## Key Contributions

1. Largest radiographic caries detection SR+MA at time of publication: 117 studies, 13,375 teeth, 19,108 surfaces
2. Stratified pooled estimates by both **location** (occlusal vs. proximal) and **lesion depth** (initial, dentin, cavitated) — enabling stage-specific clinical guidance
3. Confirms the classical teaching that radiography detects caries late (after enamel–dentin junction breach in most proximal cases)
4. Provides the quantitative threshold: cavitated proximal lesions consistently achieve Se >0.60 and Sp >0.90 — the first depth at which radiography is reliably useful
5. QUADAS-2 risk-of-bias assessment applied; majority of included studies rated high risk of bias — limiting certainty of estimates

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- **Databases**: Medline, Embase, Cochrane Central + grey literature
- **Inclusion**: Studies reporting sensitivity/specificity of radiographic detection of natural primary caries; clinical or in vitro conditions; permanent or primary teeth
- **Excluded**: Secondary caries, root caries, studies without reference standard histology or excavation
- **Risk of bias**: QUADAS-2 tool
- **Analysis**: Random-effects meta-analysis; pooled sensitivity, specificity, diagnostic odds ratios (DORs)
- **Stratification**: (1) location — occlusal vs. proximal; (2) lesion depth — any/initial (enamel), dentin, cavitated
- **Scale**: 947 records identified → 442 full-text reviewed → 117 studies included

## Key Results

### Pooled Accuracy by Lesion Type and Location

| Lesion depth | Location | Pooled sensitivity | Pooled specificity | DOR magnitude |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Initial (any/enamel) | Proximal | ~0.24 | 0.70–0.84 | Low |
| Initial (any/enamel) | Occlusal | ~0.42 | ~0.97 | Low |
| Dentin | Proximal | 0.36 (95% CI 0.24–0.49) | 0.87–0.89 | Moderate |
| Dentin | Occlusal | 0.56 (95% CI 0.53–0.59) | 0.87–0.95 | Moderate |
| Cavitated | Proximal | >0.60 | >0.90 | High |
| Cavitated | Occlusal | No studies available | — | — |

### Clinical Interpretation

- **Enamel/initial lesions**: ~24–42% detected — most enamel caries MISSED radiographically; high false-negative rate
- **Dentin lesions**: 36–56% detected — improvement over enamel but still suboptimal
- **Cavitated proximal lesions**: >60% detected — radiography reliable at this stage
- **Specificity**: Consistently high (0.87–0.97) — a positive radiographic finding strongly suggests true caries; low false-positive rate maintained across all depth categories
- **Clinical conclusion**: Bitewing/PA radiography most diagnostically useful once caries has breached the enamel–dentin junction; supplementary non-radiographic methods warranted for initial lesion detection in high-risk patients

## Limitations

- Majority of included studies rated **high risk of bias** on QUADAS-2 — reduces certainty of pooled estimates
- Abstract-only source for this summary — specific subgroup n-counts and CIs not fully extractable
- Most studies on permanent teeth — generalizability to primary dentition limited
- No studies on cavitated occlusal lesions — gap in evidence base
- Heterogeneity expected but exact I² values not available from abstract
- In vitro and in vivo studies pooled — reference standard and verification conditions vary

## Glossary

- **DOR (Diagnostic Odds Ratio)**: Single summary statistic for diagnostic test accuracy; combines sensitivity and specificity; higher = better discrimination
- **QUADAS-2**: Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies tool, version 2; assesses risk of bias in four domains (patient selection, index test, reference standard, flow and timing)
- **Random-effects meta-analysis**: Accounts for heterogeneity between studies; assumes study-specific true effects vary around an overall mean
- **Initial lesion**: Earliest detectable caries stage, typically confined to enamel; also termed enamel caries or E1/E2 ICDAS
- **Cavitated lesion**: Caries with surface breakdown — clinical/radiographic cavity present; corresponds to ICDAS 5–6
