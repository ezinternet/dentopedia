## Why Ingested

기존 [[drug/systemic-disease/malave-2022-vasovagal-reactions-during-interventional-pain]]은 시술 중 미주신경성 실신 (Vasovagal Syncope, VVS)의 병태생리·위험인자·처치 프로토콜을 정리했지만 "동일 세션 내 재발"과 "외관상 회복 후 조기 재개"의 위험은 다루지 않는다. 본 증례는 발치 세션 하나에서 무실신 에피소드 → 10분 뒤 실신으로 악화된 실제 타임라인을 기록해, malave-2022의 "이전 병력 = 최강 위험인자" 주장과 재개 금지 권고를 치과 임상 시나리오로 구체화한다.

## Three-line Summary

Single case report (n=1, 33-year-old female, no medical history) of recurrent vasovagal syncope during a surgically complicated mandibular third molar extraction, with a documented vasovagal episode during a previous simple extraction.

Two episodes occurred in one session — the first without loss of consciousness (malaise, pallor, nausea), the second with transient loss of consciousness roughly 10 min after treatment resumed; a later appointment completed the extraction without syncope using prodrome-triggered interruption and supine positioning.

The authors propose a three-phase model (trigger accumulation → incomplete autonomic reset → premature re-exposure with escalation) and stress hypersalivation as a parasympathetic prodromal marker, but no blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation, or local anesthetic dose was recorded at any point.

## 세줄요약

단일 증례보고(n=1, 33세 여성, 특이 전신병력 없음) — 하악 제3대구치 발치가 기술적으로 어려워진 상황에서 미주신경성 실신 (Vasovagal Syncope, VVS)이 같은 세션 내 재발한 사례이며, 이전 단순 발치 때도 실신 병력이 있었다.

첫 에피소드는 의식 소실 없이(권태감·창백·오심) 발생했고, 술식을 재개한 약 10분 뒤 두 번째 에피소드가 일과성 의식 소실과 함께 발생했으며, 이후 별도 내원에서 전구증상 조기 인지와 앙와위 자세로 실신 없이 발치를 완료했다.

저자들은 3단계 모델(자극 축적 → 불완전한 자율신경 재설정 → 조기 재노출과 악화)을 제안하고 과다침분비 (Hypersalivation)를 부교감 전구 표지로 강조하지만, 혈압·심박수·산소포화도·국소마취제 용량은 전 구간에서 기록되지 않았다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Documents **two consecutive vasovagal episodes within one operative session**, the second more severe (transient loss of consciousness) than the first (no LOC), with the escalation occurring only ~10 min after treatment was resumed.
- Argues that **apparent clinical recovery ≠ autonomic stability**: citing continuous autonomic monitoring data, parasympathetic dominance may persist for **15–30 min** despite normal vital signs, and sympathetic compensation becomes exhausted with repeated episodes.
- Elevates **hypersalivation** to a prodromal warning sign of imminent recurrence — its persistence after the first episode's "recovery" is read as ongoing autonomic instability.
- Proposes a three-phase model of recurrence adapted to dentistry: (1) trigger accumulation (sustained nociception, inadequate analgesia, anxiety), (2) incomplete autonomic reset, (3) premature re-exposure and escalation.
- Reiterates the practical rule: **never resume treatment if prodromal signs persist**; if substantial work remains, defer to a separate appointment.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: single-patient case report with clinical narrative; no comparator, no protocolized measurement schedule.
- **n**: 1 patient (33-year-old female, no relevant medical history).
- **Setting**: emergency dental visit → surgical extraction of right mandibular third molar (symptomatic irreversible pulpitis) → follow-up appointment for retained roots.
- **Data collected**: narrative chronology of signs/symptoms, sequence of clinical interventions, and healing/follow-up outcome. Vital signs and anesthetic doses were **not** systematically recorded (explicitly stated in the paper's Limitations).
- **Analysis**: qualitative synthesis of the observed sequence against established autonomic physiology; the three-phase model is presented as a conceptual application, not a physiological demonstration.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No objective physiological data**: blood pressure, heart rate, and oxygen saturation were not recorded during the syncopal episodes, so the proposed pathophysiology (and the 15–30 min autonomic instability window) is imported from the literature, not measured in this patient.
- Single case (n=1), single operator, retrospective narrative — no control, no protocolized intervention sequence; causal claims about "premature resumption" rest on a temporal coincidence in one patient.
- Local anesthetic agent, concentration, and total dose are not reported, so an adrenaline-related or dose-related contribution cannot be assessed or excluded.
- The timeline in the paper is given as a prose list without clock times except the "~10 minutes after resuming" interval; no duration of the first episode or of the total appointment is stated.
- **Hint–paper mismatch (logged)**: the ingest briefing asked Results to report vitals and doses; the paper contains neither, so the Results table reports absence explicitly rather than inferring values.

## 6. Related Work

- **Malave 2022** ([[drug/systemic-disease/malave-2022-vasovagal-reactions-during-interventional-pain]]): narrative review of vasovagal reactions in interventional pain procedures — prior VVS history as the strongest risk factor, Bezold–Jarisch pathophysiology, Trendelenburg/supine management, antimuscarinic options; this case is its dental instantiation.
- **Kumari 2025** ([[drug/systemic-disease/kumari-2025-hemodynamic-fluctuations-during-tooth-extraction]]): prospective BP/HR/SpO2 time-course during extraction in normotensive adults — the mean-curve counterpart to this case's unmeasured episodic collapse.
- **Smereka 2019** ([[drug/systemic-disease/smereka-2019-preparedness-and-attitudes-towards-medical]]): dental-office emergency preparedness survey, where syncope is the most frequently encountered emergency.
- **Jevon 2020** ([[drug/systemic-disease/jevon-2020-management-anaphylaxis-dental-practice-update]]): differential diagnosis of adult collapse in the dental chair (anaphylaxis vs. vasovagal).

## 7. Glossary

- **Vasovagal syncope (VVS)**: neurally mediated reflex faint from excessive parasympathetic activation plus sympathetic withdrawal → bradycardia, vasodilation, cerebral hypoperfusion.
- **Prodrome**: pre-syncopal symptom cluster (nausea, diaphoresis, pallor, dizziness, hypersalivation, tunnel vision, warmth, impending doom).
- **Hypersalivation**: increased salivation via facial (VII) and glossopharyngeal (IX) parasympathetic outflow; used here as a bedside marker of vagal predominance.
- **Autonomic reset**: return of autonomic balance after an episode — the paper's point is that clinical recovery precedes it by 15–30 min.
- **Cardioneuroablation**: catheter-based vagal denervation reserved for refractory, specialist-managed cases.
