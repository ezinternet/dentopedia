## Why Ingested

The wiki has no page on bite/interocclusal registration MATERIALS — a daily chairside decision (which material to seat a bite registration in) that has so far only been touched indirectly through [[complete-denture/satin-2023-occlusal-vertical-dimension-transfer-cad-cam-dentures]] (in-vitro vertical-transfer error, but for CAD/CAM complete-denture fabrication, not fixed-prosthodontic bite registration). This 3-page in-vitro supplement study (Rani 2024) directly compares four interocclusal materials — Ramitec (polyether), CADbite and Jet Bite (polyvinyl siloxane), Aluwax (wax) — for vertical-relationship accuracy, filling that gap despite its caveats (supplement format, bench-only design, abstract with no numeric results — full text mined for actual deviation values/CIs/p-values below). A companion clinical study, `tank-2024-interocclusal-registration-materials-vertical-accuracy-centric-relation` (Tank B et al., n=30, centric relation), is being ingested in the same batch and asks the same question in patients — see relations on the wiki page.

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro bench study (n=10 interocclusal records per material, 40 total; ideal maxillary/mandibular casts with mock preparations on teeth 46/47/48, semi-adjustable articulator, Medit T500 lab scanner) comparing four bite registration materials — polyether (Ramitec), two polyvinyl siloxane/addition silicone products (CADbite, Jet Bite), and wax (Aluwax) — for their capability to reproduce the vertical relationship after demounting and remounting the cast using the record.

All four materials produced a small but statistically significant deviation from the control (no-material, direct-contact) reading (Ramitec 0.136, CADbite 0.152, Jet Bite 0.165, Aluwax 0.939 — unit not stated in the source; all p≤0.001 vs. control); the three elastomeric/polyether materials did not differ significantly from each other (pairwise p=0.90–0.99), while Aluwax deviated far more than all three (mean difference ~0.77–0.80 vs. each, p<0.001 for every Aluwax comparison).

This is a 3-page journal-supplement in-vitro study with mock preparations only (no patient, no muscle/closure-force variability, no soft tissue), and the paper's own conclusion overstates a "best" material despite a nonsignificant difference among the top three — clinically, wax should be avoided for accurate vertical-relationship transfer, but the choice among the elastomeric/polyether options is not evidence-differentiated by this study.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **First wiki page on bite-registration/interocclusal-record MATERIALS** — an everyday chairside question the wiki did not previously have a dedicated page on.
2. **Quantifies deviation-from-control by material** with ANOVA + Tukey HSD: wax (Aluwax) deviates far more from the control reading than polyether or polyvinyl-siloxane/addition-silicone materials (mean difference ~0.77–0.94 vs. the smaller ~0.14–0.17 range for the elastomeric/polyether group; unit unstated in source).
3. **Class-level, not brand-level, evidence**: within the elastomer/polyether group (Ramitec, CADbite, Jet Bite), no material was significantly more accurate than another (pairwise p=0.90–0.99) — the paper's "polyether was best" conclusion is not supported by its own pairwise statistics.
4. **Confirms material-class identity for each brand from the Methods** (not inferred from brand name alone): Ramitec = polyether; CADbite and Jet Bite = polyvinyl siloxane / addition silicone; Aluwax = wax.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: in-vitro (bench) comparative study; not a clinical trial.
- **Sample**: ideal maxillary and mandibular casts, mock tooth preparation on teeth 46, 47, 48, mounted on a semi-adjustable articulator in maximum intercuspation. 4 groups × 10 interocclusal records = 40 records total. n = interocclusal records, **not patients** — no patient, muscle activity, closure-force variability, or soft tissue in this design.
- **Materials tested**: Group A Ramitec (polyether, 3M ESPE); Group B CADbite (polyvinyl siloxane, Ivoclar Vivadent); Group C Jet Bite (polyvinyl siloxane, Coltene Whaledent); Group D Aluwax (wax, Dental Products Co.).
- **Procedure**: baseline (control) scan of the casts in direct occlusal contact using a Medit T500 Dental Lab Scanner → interocclusal record made, with 50 lb of load applied to standardize seating pressure → mandibular cast demounted → remounted using the record → rescanned ("final reading") → deviation from control computed. Repeated 10× per material.
- **Statistics**: one-way ANOVA (5 groups: control + 4 materials) + Tukey HSD post-hoc pairwise comparisons, α=0.05.

| CADbite vs. Jet Bite | −0.013 | −0.107 to 0.080 | 0.994 (ns) |
| Ramitec vs. Aluwax | −0.802 | −0.896 to −0.709 | <0.001 |

| Jet Bite vs. Aluwax | −0.774 | −0.867 to −0.681 | <0.001 |

Overall ANOVA (5 groups): F(4,45) = 261.498, p < 0.001. **Unit of measurement is not stated in the source text** (plausibly mm given the scanner/vertical-dimension context, but unconfirmed — treat as unverified).

The paper's own Discussion is internally inconsistent: it states "when the materials were compared within the group, the mean value was significant (0.005), but when the materials were compared between the groups, it showed an insignificant value (1.407)." These numbers are the ANOVA mean-square values from Table 1 (within-groups MS=0.005, between-groups MS=1.407), not p-values — and the ratio of those two mean squares is exactly what produces the highly significant F=261.498 reported in the same table. Calling the between-groups result "insignificant" contradicts the paper's own Table 1; this reads as confused/mistyped wording, flagged here rather than silently corrected. The Conclusions section states polyether "was found to be the best," but Ramitec/CADbite/Jet Bite were **not** significantly different from each other (p=0.90–0.99) — the ranking is not supported by the paper's own statistics.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- In-vitro only: no patient, no muscle/closure-force variability, no soft tissue; n=10 records/material, not patients.
- 3-page supplement format: no Discussion depth beyond a few sentences, no explicit stated unit for the deviation values, effect sizes limited to mean-difference/CI from Tukey HSD.
- Only teeth 46/47/48 mock preparation tested — a posterior fixed-prosthodontic quadrant scenario; results should not be extrapolated to full-arch or edentulous interocclusal registration (see [[complete-denture/de-sousa-ervolino-2023-centric-relation-recording-techniques-edentulous]] for that separate population).
- Internally inconsistent Discussion sentence describing Table 1's mean-square values as "significant"/"insignificant" in a way that contradicts the table's own ANOVA result (see Results above) — flagged, not silently corrected.
- The Conclusions overstate a "best" material (polyether) despite nonsignificant pairwise differences among the top three materials — read the ranking language with that caveat.
- Future: clinical (in-vivo) replication — see the companion `tank-2024-interocclusal-registration-materials-vertical-accuracy-centric-relation` study in centric relation; unit-of-measurement clarification from the authors would strengthen reproducibility.

## 7. Glossary

- **Interocclusal record / bite registration** — a material used to capture and transfer the occlusal relationship between opposing casts to an articulator.
- **Vertical relationship / vertical dimension** — the vertical position of the mandible relative to the maxilla at a given occlusal contact.
- **Semi-adjustable articulator** — a mechanical device simulating jaw movement, used to mount and relate casts.
- **Polyether** — an elastomeric bite-registration material class (e.g., Ramitec).
- **Polyvinyl siloxane (PVS) / addition silicone** — another elastomeric bite-registration material class (e.g., CADbite, Jet Bite).
- **ANOVA / Tukey HSD** — statistical test for comparing means across more than two groups, and the post-hoc pairwise comparison test used to identify which specific group pairs differ.
