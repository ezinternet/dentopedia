## Why Ingested

Healthcare-specific burnout narrative review explicitly anchoring WHO/ICD-11 definition ("syndrome resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed") and confirming the 3-dimension construct in a clinical practitioner context. Complements [[wiki/professional-wellbeing/edu-valsania-2022-burnout-review-theory-measurement]] (general theory/measurement) by adding healthcare epidemiology (~50% prevalence globally), MBI-HSS clinical cutoffs (EE ≥27, DP ≥10, PA ≤33), burnout-vs-depression distinction, and the ABC prevention framework — filling the clinical-translation gap between theory and practice. South African setting introduces a low/middle-income healthcare context missing from the existing burnout cluster.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review targeting practising healthcare professionals: WHO/ICD-11 burnout definition, global epidemiology (~50% prevalence), contributing factors, clinical assessment with MBI-HSS cutoffs, and primary/secondary/tertiary prevention strategies including the ABC approach.
Nearly half of healthcare providers globally experience burnout; medical students exceed 50%; MBI-HSS high-burnout thresholds are EE ≥27 / DP ≥10 / PA ≤33; outpatient providers show more EE than inpatient; no definitive gender difference in large systematic reviews.
Burnout is REVERSIBLE when recognized early; the primary prevention gap — organizational interventions — remains underused; burnout and depression overlap but are conceptually distinct (burnout = work-specific only).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### ICD-11 Definition

WHO ICD-11: burnout = "syndrome conceptualised as resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed." **NOT a medical condition.** Three dimensions:

| Dimension | Korean | Clinical description |
|---|---|---|
| Emotional exhaustion (EE) | 정서적 소진 | Feelings of energy depletion |
| Depersonalisation (DP) | 비인격화 | Increased mental distance, negativity, cynicism toward work |
| Reduced professional accomplishment (PA) | 직업효능 저하 | Diminished sense of personal achievement |

### Prevalence

| Population | Burnout prevalence |
|---|---|
| Healthcare providers (global) | ~50% (nearly half) |
| Medical students | >50% during academic journey |
| Highest-risk specialties | Urology, EM, anaesthesiology, surgical subspecialties, radiology, internal medicine |
| Gender | No definitive difference in large SRs (some studies show women slightly more affected) |
| Outpatient vs inpatient | More EE in outpatient; DP and reduced PA do not differ significantly |

### MBI-HSS Clinical Cutoffs (High Burnout Thresholds)

| Subscale | Items | Total range | High-burnout threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emotional Exhaustion (EE) | 9 | 0–54 | ≥ 27 |
| Depersonalisation (DP) | 5 | 0–30 | ≥ 10 |
| Personal Accomplishment (PA) | 8 | 0–48 | ≤ 33 |

### Burnout vs. Depression

| Feature | Burnout | Depression |
|---|---|---|
| Specificity | Work-specific | Not necessarily occupational |
| Overlap | Fatigue, low energy, negativity, functional impairment | Same |
| Relationship | Can predispose to depression; can coexist | Can exist without workplace stress |
| Clinical implication | Screening tools/interventions must target both without conflating |  |

### Contributing Factors

Organizational: workload overload, administrative burden, excessive EHR use, poor work-life balance, productivity pressure, inadequate leadership support, lack of autonomy, value misalignment
Individual: lack of meaningful work, poor collegial support, limited scheduling flexibility
South Africa-specific: fiscal austerity (hiring freezes, overcrowding), workplace violence, xenophobia

### Prevention Framework

| Level | Aim | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Prevent onset | Healthy staffing, flexible rosters, EHR optimisation, leadership training |
| Secondary | Detect/intervene early | Quarterly MBI screening, stress-management training, early counselling |
| Tertiary | Treat/rehabilitate | Psychological care, return-to-work plans, peer support |

**ABC approach** (emotional intelligence framework):
- **A (Applying)**: self-awareness — recognise limits, stress signals
- **B (Balancing)**: equilibrium across family, social, spiritual, leisure
- **C (Cultivating)**: healthy routines, consistency, lifestyle choices

### Individual Management Strategies

Mindfulness, meditation, gratitude journaling, regular exercise, self-care, stress-management programmes
Social connection, reconnecting with professional purpose, mentorship (novice ↔ end-of-career)
Setting limits, regular short breaks > infrequent extended leave, periodic digital disconnection

### Organizational Strategies

Align institutional and individual values, fair compensation, streamline EHR documentation, delegate non-clinical tasks, offer flexible arrangements (part-time, remote consultations)

### Prognosis

**Favorable** when recognized early and managed through personal resilience + systemic reforms.
If unaddressed → escalation to depression, substance misuse, suicide.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative (not systematic) review — potential selection bias
- South African context limits direct generalizability to higher-income settings
- No de novo data — synthesis/commentary only
- No meta-analysis of intervention effectiveness

## 7. Glossary

- **Burnout (ICD-11)**: occupational phenomenon from chronic unmanaged workplace stress; 3 dims: EE + DP + reduced PA; not a medical condition
- **MBI-HSS**: Maslach Burnout Inventory — Human Services Survey; 22 items; cutoffs EE ≥27 / DP ≥10 / PA ≤33 = high burnout
- **ABC approach**: Applying (self-awareness) / Balancing (work-life) / Cultivating (habits) — emotional intelligence-based prevention model
- **Primary prevention (1차 예방)**: eliminate organizational burnout causes before onset
- **Depersonalisation (비인격화)**: mental distancing from work/patients; a coping response to EE
- **LMIC context**: low/middle-income country — South African healthcare system with austerity, staffing shortages, workplace violence as additional burnout drivers
