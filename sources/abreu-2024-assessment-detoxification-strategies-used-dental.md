## Why Ingested

The healing-abutment (HA) reuse debate splits into two axes: a *surface-cleanliness* axis (can residual debris/protein be removed?) and a *biologic-response* axis (does a cleaned surface still provoke inflammation?). Most papers we hold argue on the cleanliness axis — [[infection-control/burioni-2024-could-dental-material-reuse-play]] shows residual protein persists in vitro but stops there. Abreu 2024 supplies the missing biologic-response endpoint: even the best-cleaned used HAs (Groups D/E, lowest residual protein) still triggered high inflammatory cytokine secretion from human macrophages. This makes it the strongest anti-reuse pole of the batch and the direct biologic counterweight to reuse-permissive protocols such as [[infection-control/kyaw-2023-effect-chemical-electrochemical-decontamination-protocols]].

## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (IJOMI 2024; University of Illinois Chicago; 50 used healing abutments, n=10 per group) testing 5 enzymatic-cleaner decontamination protocols (Groups A–E) and measuring residual debris/protein plus human primary macrophage inflammatory cytokine response vs new sterile controls.

Groups D and E (prophy jet + ultrasonic + enzymatic cleaner / prophy jet + autoclave) removed the most surface debris and had the lowest residual protein, but ALL five cleaned groups still triggered high macrophage cytokine secretion for up to 5 days — equivalent to non-decontaminated used surfaces.

Decontamination and sterilization cannot restore immunological inertness to a used healing abutment; authors conclude clinicians should not reuse HAs regardless of cleaning protocol. Abstract-only; exact cytokine values unavailable.

## 세줄요약

In vitro 연구 (IJOMI 2024; 일리노이 대학교 시카고; 사용 힐링어버트먼트 50개, 군당 n=10): 5가지 효소세정제 기반 오염제거 프로토콜(A–E군) 후 잔여 debris·단백질 및 사람 대식세포 (macrophage) 염증성 사이토카인 (cytokine) 반응 비교.

D·E군(공기연마 + 초음파 / 공기연마 + 멸균)이 표면 debris·잔여 단백질 최소 제거 효과; 그러나 5개 세정군 모두 신품 대조 대비 5일간 대식세포 사이토카인 분비 높게 유지 — 재세정·재멸균이 면역학적 불활성 복원 불가.

어떤 세정 프로토콜로도 사용된 힐링어버트먼트의 면역 반응 정상화 불가 → 저자: "재사용 금지"; 초록만 수집, 사이토카인 개별 수치 불명.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Adds the **biologic-response endpoint** that the surface-cleanliness literature on HA reuse lacks: it is not enough to show a used HA can be made visually clean and low-protein — the paper tests whether a decontaminated surface still provokes host inflammation.
- Demonstrates a **decoupling** between macroscopic/proteomic cleanliness and biologic inertness: the groups that removed the most debris/protein (D, E) were NOT the groups that avoided inflammation — ALL cleaned groups still induced high cytokine secretion.
- Delivers the **strongest explicit anti-reuse recommendation** in the reuse-controversy set: clinicians should not reuse HAs even after decontamination and sterilization.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Specimens**: 50 healing abutments collected after 2–4 weeks of intraoral use (uHAs), randomized into 5 groups (A–E; n=10 each). Control = 10 new sterile HAs.
- **Decontamination strategies** (all enzymatic-cleaner-based, agents available in most clinical settings):
  - **Group A**: enzymatic cleaner foam + autoclave
  - **Group B**: ultrasonic bath with enzymatic cleaner + autoclave
  - **Group C**: prophy jet + enzymatic cleaner foam + autoclave
  - **Group D**: prophy jet + ultrasonic bath with enzymatic cleaner + autoclave
  - **Group E**: prophy jet + autoclave
- **Residual biomaterial**: Micro BCA protein assay for residual protein; Phloxine B staining + macroscopic examination for debris.
- **Inflammatory potential**: human primary macrophages exposed to the HAs; supernatant assayed for a 9-cytokine/chemokine profile via multiplex bead assay, tracked for up to 5 days.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- All test groups differed in visual decontamination compared to new controls (i.e., none matched a virgin surface).
- **Groups D and E** = most effective surface debris removal and lowest residual protein concentration; **Group A** = least effective. (abstract-only — no per-group numeric values reported.)
- **However**: compared to new controls, **ALL test groups showed high inflammatory cytokine secretion** on multiplex assay for up to 5 days.
- **Bottom line**: enzymatic-cleaner decontamination failed to re-establish an inert HA surface or prevent an in vitro inflammatory immune response. Authors conclude clinicians should NOT reuse HAs even after attempts to decontaminate and sterilize.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **In vitro only** — a human macrophage monoculture inflammatory readout, not a peri-implant clinical outcome (marginal bone loss, mucositis, soft-tissue health). Whether the observed cytokine secretion translates to worse clinical peri-implant response is untested here.
- **Small groups** (n=10 per arm) and enzymatic-cleaner strategies only — does not test chemical/electrochemical or NaOCl-based protocols that other papers report as effective for surface cleanliness.
- **Abstract-only ingestion**: exact residual-protein values, individual cytokine identities/magnitudes, and statistical detail are unavailable and not reconstructed.

## 7. Glossary

- **uHA (used healing abutment)**: a healing abutment recovered after intraoral service, being assessed for potential reuse.
- **Enzymatic cleaner**: detergent containing proteolytic/lipolytic enzymes used to digest organic debris (protein, biofilm) off instruments before sterilization.
- **Prophy jet / air polishing**: air-powder abrasive stream used to mechanically remove surface deposits.
- **Micro BCA protein assay**: colorimetric assay quantifying residual protein concentration on a surface.
- **Phloxine B**: a stain used to visualize residual organic debris/protein on the abutment surface.
- **Multiplex bead assay**: bead-based immunoassay measuring many cytokines/chemokines simultaneously from one supernatant sample.
- **Cytokine/chemokine**: signaling proteins secreted by immune cells (here, macrophages) whose elevated levels indicate an inflammatory response.
