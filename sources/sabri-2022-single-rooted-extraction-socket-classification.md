## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of extraction-socket (ES) classifications with a proposal for a new system combining morphologic and patient-related factors; searches covered PubMed, EMBASE, and SCOPUS; abstract only (full text not retrieved).

Thirteen ES classifications were identified; the most frequently repeated hard/soft tissue factors were buccal bone dehiscence, interproximal bone, gingival recession, and soft tissue phenotype, while patient-related factors such as systemic conditions and smoking received minimal attention.

The proposed system's inclusion of patient-level risk factors addresses a documented blind spot in anatomy-only case-selection frameworks such as the sagittal root position (SRP) classifications held in this wiki.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Critically appraises **13 existing single-rooted extraction socket classifications**.
- Identifies the recurrent core morphologic parameters: buccal bone dehiscence, interproximal bone, gingival recession, soft tissue phenotype.
- Documents the **under-representation of patient-related factors** (systemic conditions, smoking) in prior morphologic classifications.
- Proposes a **new classification system** combining prior morphologic cornerstones with patient-related factors, aligned with contemporary implant-dentistry consensus.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Systematic search across PubMed, EMBASE, and SCOPUS for single-rooted extraction socket classification systems.
- Critical appraisal of retrieved classifications to extract the most repeated hard- and soft-tissue factors and to identify gaps.
- New system synthesized from the "cornerstones" of prior classifications plus patient-level domains; detailed categories are in the full text (not retrieved).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **13** extraction-socket classifications detected.
- **Most repeated hard/soft tissue factors**: buccal bone dehiscence, interproximal bone, gingival recession, soft tissue phenotype.
- **Gap**: minimal attention to patient-related factors (systemic conditions, smoking) in the prior systems.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Abstract-only ingest: the proposed classification's actual categories, definitions, and any validation data are in the full text and are not reproduced here.
- As a review-and-proposal, it does not itself provide outcome data to validate the new system's predictive value.
- Applies to single-rooted sockets; molar/multirooted socket morphology is out of scope.
