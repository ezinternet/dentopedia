## One-line Summary
In-vitro study (10 rotary cutting instrument groups × n=30, Macor substrate, 300 cuts, electric handpiece vs Part I air-turbine) showing that with the electric handpiece, the tested carbide bur outperformed all tested diamond burs in cutting efficiency (highest advancement rate 0.17 mm/s, lowest load), and overall electric handpiece yielded higher cutting efficiency and lower pulp-chamber temperature than the air-turbine handpiece.

## 한줄요약
인비트로 실험(회전절삭기구 10종, Macor 블록, 전동 핸드피스 vs 에어터빈 비교): 전동 핸드피스에서 카바이드 버가 다이아몬드 버 전체 대비 절삭효율 최고(진행속도 최대·하중 최소); 전동 핸드피스 전체 평균 절삭효율 에어터빈보다 높고 치수강 온도 낮음.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Establishes that **handpiece type (electric vs air turbine) significantly interacts with rotary cutting instrument type** — the optimal bur choice depends on which handpiece is used.
- Shows that with the **electric handpiece, the tested carbide bur (Great White Ultra) outperforms all tested diamond burs** in cutting efficiency (rate of advancement 0.17 mm/s, lowest applied load 108.35 g), reversing the common assumption that diamond always outperforms carbide.
- Demonstrates **electric handpiece superiority over air turbine** overall: higher mean advancement rate (0.12 vs 0.11 mm/s), higher load (124.53 vs 121.7 g), and lower pulp-chamber temperature (26.68°C vs 28.37°C) — likely attributed to greater torque.
- Documents **temperature reduction** in simulated pulp chamber for all tested burs with electric handpiece vs baseline, suggesting better thermal safety with electric.
- Provides SEM characterisation of bur surface pre-use, post-use (uncleaned), and post-ultrasonic cleaning — supporting sterilization workflow decisions.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In-vitro bench study, comparative (Part II of series)
- **Substrate**: Macor blocks (machinable glass-ceramic used as tooth analog) — 75 blocks, 4 cuts each = 300 total cuts
- **Handpiece**: Electric handpiece (Intramatic Lux K200); compared against air-turbine data from Part I (same protocol)
- **Rotary cutting instruments (10 groups, n=30 each)**:
  - 9 diamond: 7 multi-use, 2 disposable
  - 1 carbide: Great White Ultra
- **Outcomes**: Rate of advancement (mm/s), applied load (g), temperature in simulated pulp chamber (°C vs baseline)
- **Morphology**: SEM at 3 time points per group (pre-use / post-use uncleaned / post-ultrasonic clean)
- **Statistics**: 2-way ANOVA (group × handpiece interaction); 1-way ANOVA + Tukey's post-hoc (α=.05) for significant main effects
- **Part I**: Air-turbine data — not held in wiki (same journal, same series; paywalled)
- **Limitation (abstract)**: Macor is not natural tooth — enamel/dentin cutting may differ; single carbide bur tested; specific brand names for diamond groups not extractable from abstract

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Carbide (Great White Ultra) vs all diamonds — electric handpiece**:
  - Rate of advancement: carbide 0.17 mm/s = **highest** of all 10 groups
  - Applied load: carbide 108.35 g = **lowest** of all 10 groups
  - → carbide bur most efficient with electric handpiece on Macor substrate
- **Electric vs air-turbine (all instruments combined)**:
  - Mean temperature: electric 26.68°C vs turbine 28.37°C (electric **lower**)
  - Rate of advancement: electric 0.12 mm/s vs turbine 0.11 mm/s (electric **higher**)
  - Applied load: electric 124.53 g vs turbine 121.7 g (electric **higher**)
  - → electric handpiece superior on all three metrics
- **Pulp chamber temperature**: all burs showed **reduction vs baseline** with electric handpiece
- **Interaction significant**: handpiece type × rotary cutting instrument group interaction was statistically significant (2-way ANOVA)
- *Note*: Effect sizes, exact p-values, and per-diamond-group breakdowns not available from abstract

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Abstract-only: individual diamond group data, confidence intervals, and exact p-values inaccessible
- Macor substrate (glass-ceramic) does not replicate enamel (harder) or dentin (viscoelastic) cutting; results may not transfer directly to clinical tooth preparation
- Single carbide bur model (Great White Ultra) — cannot generalise to carbide burs as a class
- Standardised constant load/speed protocol may not replicate clinician's variable operative technique
- Temperature measured in simulated pulp chamber, not actual pulp; thermal transfer may differ
- Part I (turbine data) not held — cannot fully reconstruct the instrument-by-instrument turbine vs electric comparison table

## 6. Related Work

## 7. Glossary
- **Macor**: Machinable glass-ceramic (Corning); commonly used in dental bench studies as tooth analog — harder than dentin, softer than enamel
- **Rate of advancement (mm/s)**: Distance the bur advances into the substrate per unit time at controlled load — primary cutting efficiency metric in this series
- **Applied load (g)**: Force applied to the bur during cutting; lower load for same advancement = more efficient cutting
- **Great White Ultra**: Specific tungsten carbide bur brand (SS White Dental) used as the sole carbide comparator in this study
- **Intramatic Lux K200**: Electric micromotor handpiece (KaVo) used in Part II; provides higher and more constant torque than air-turbine
- **Part II context**: Part I (same authors, 2009) tested the same 10 instrument groups with air-turbine only; Part II adds electric handpiece and enables 2-way comparison
