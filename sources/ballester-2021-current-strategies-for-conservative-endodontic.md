## Why Ingested

Provides the first systematic review and meta-analysis evidence weighing four endodontic access cavity design strategies (traditional, conservative, ultraconservative, truss) on fracture resistance and clinical outcomes. The wiki holds individual RCTs on conservative access cavity preparation ([[endodontics/anatomy/rivera-2020-conservative-access-upper-molars]], [[endodontics/anatomy/rover-2020-ultraconservative-mandibular-incisors]]) but lacks a cross-design synthesis to guide practitioner selection. This SR establishes that marginal ridge preservation status is the critical moderator of mechanical outcome, and that diagnostic visibility (operating microscope + ultrasonic troughing) is mandatory for conservative designs.

## Three-line Summary

PRISMA systematic review and meta-analysis of 33 clinical studies comparing four endodontic access cavity (AC) designs: traditional (TradAC), conservative (ConsAC), ultraconservative (UltraAC), and truss (TrussAC) on fracture resistance, endodontic outcomes, procedural errors, and treatment time.

Key findings: ConsAC/TrussAC achieve significantly higher fracture resistance than TradAC only when all marginal ridges are preserved (P < 0.05); no advantage when one or more ridges are lost (P > 0.05); ConsAC carries significantly higher risk of undetected canals without operating microscope and ultrasonic troughing (P < 0.05); TrussAC lacks sufficient evidence; UltraAC applicable only in limited cases.

Clinical recommendation: maintain AC extent as small as practical without jeopardizing treatment quality; marginal ridge status and diagnostic visibility determine optimal design per case.

## 세줄요약

PRISMA 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (임상연구 33편): 네 가지 근관접근동 설계 비교 — 전통적(TradAC) vs 보존적(ConsAC) vs 초보존적(UltraAC) vs 트러스(TrussAC) 설계의 파절저항성·근관치료 결과·수술상 오류·처치시간.

핵심 결과: ConsAC/TrussAC는 **모든 변연능선이 보존된 경우에만** TradAC 대비 파절저항성이 유의하게 높음(P < 0.05); 1개 이상 능선 상실 시 유의한 차이 없음(P > 0.05); ConsAC는 근관 발견경미경·초음파 트로프 없으면 미발견 근관 위험 유의하게 높음(P < 0.05); TrussAC 근거 부족; UltraAC 제한된 경우에만.

임상 권고: 근관치료 질 손상 없는 범위에서 접근동 범위 최소화 — 변연능선 상태·진단 시인성이 개별 선택을 결정.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Systematically categorizes four endodontic AC designs: TradAC (conventional straight-line), ConsAC (contracted/minimally invasive), UltraAC (ultra-minimally invasive/orifice-directed), TrussAC (truss-shaped with marginal ridge retention via occlusal-layer preservation).
- First meta-analysis directly comparing fracture resistance and clinical outcomes across designs (33 included studies from 356 identified records).
- Establishes marginal ridge preservation as the critical outcome moderator: ConsAC/TrussAC offer fracture advantage only with complete ridge preservation; advantage disappears with ridge loss.
- Identifies diagnostic visibility (operating microscope + ultrasonic troughing) as mandatory for conservative designs to achieve comparable canal detection to traditional access.
- Concludes against routine adoption of TrussAC (insufficient evidence) and restricts UltraAC to limited clinical scenarios.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Search Strategy**: PubMed and Web of Science (dates not explicitly stated in abstract); PRISMA 2009 guidelines.
- **Selection Criteria**: Studies comparing fracture resistance and/or endodontic outcomes (canal detection, root canal filling, cleaning/disinfection, treatment time, apical debris extrusion, procedural errors) between different AC designs; in vitro and clinical studies.
- **Data Extraction & Quality Assessment**: Two independent reviewers; risk of bias assessment (ROBINS-I / RoB-2 implied).
- **Meta-analysis**: Conducted for fracture resistance and root canal detection; significance level P = 0.05.
- **Heterogeneity Management**: Acknowledged; prevented pooling of some outcomes.
- **Included Studies**: 33 articles (exact breakdown: in vitro biomechanics, clinical RCTs, retrospective cohorts); moderate global risk of bias.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Fracture Resistance (Primary Outcome)

**vs. Traditional Access**:
- **ConsAC/TrussAC with all marginal ridges preserved**: significantly higher fracture resistance (P < 0.05)
- **ConsAC/TrussAC with one or two ridges lost**: no significant difference in fracture resistance (P > 0.05)
- **UltraAC**: shows strength advantage in some studies; insufficient evidence for routine recommendation

### Root Canal Detection & Visibility

- **ConsAC without magnification aids**: significantly higher risk of undetected canals (P < 0.05)
- **ConsAC + operating microscope + ultrasonic troughing**: comparable canal detection to TradAC
- **MB2 (middle mesial) canal detection**: improved with magnification and correct AC design

### Other Outcomes Discussed

- **Cleaning/Disinfection**: Conservative AC designs require extra care to ensure complete instrumentation; similar outcomes achievable with proper technique
- **Procedural Errors**: No significant difference across designs with competent technique
- **Treatment Time**: Conservative designs may add minimal time (not quantified in abstract)
- **Apical Debris Extrusion**: No significant difference noted across designs
- **Root Canal Filling & Sealing**: Comparable success across designs when proper visibility and technique maintained

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Methodological Heterogeneity**: Studies employed different AC techniques, tooth types, measurement protocols — precluded full meta-analysis for some outcomes (qualitative synthesis required)
- **Risk of Bias**: Moderate global assessment; some included studies are in vitro and may not reflect clinical reality
- **Study Design Mix**: In vitro, prospective, retrospective, small sample sizes — limits generalizability
- **Limited RCT Data**: Majority of studies are biomechanical in vitro; high-quality RCT evidence comparing clinical outcomes across designs remains sparse
- **Tooth Type Variability**: Most studies focus on molars; limited data for incisors, canines, premolars
- **Restoration Type**: Impact of coronal restoration type and core thickness on fracture outcomes only partially addressed
- **Operator Skill & Visualization**: AC design advantage heavily dependent on magnification use and experience — not controlled across all studies

**Future Directions**:
- Standardized, multicenter RCTs comparing AC designs with uniform outcome measures
- Long-term clinical follow-up studies (5–10 years) on survival and apical healing
- Prospective trials integrating CBCT-guided access with conservative designs
- Investigation of ultraconservative access in single-canal teeth (anterior teeth) vs multirooted teeth
- Systematic evaluation of restoration design (height, material, core) interaction with AC design on fracture risk

## 7. Glossary

- **TradAC (Traditional Access Cavity)**: Conventional straight-line access, full occlusal clearance, pulp chamber floor exposure; gold standard for visibility and instrumentation
- **ConsAC (Conservative Access Cavity)**: Contracted/minimally invasive access, occlusal surface minimization while maintaining canal orifice visibility; aim to preserve marginal ridges and cuspal anatomy
- **UltraAC (Ultraconservative Access Cavity)**: Orifice-directed or "access-to-orifice" design; minimal occlusal instrumentation; highest dentin preservation but greatest visibility challenge
- **TrussAC (Truss Access Cavity)**: Retains marginal ridge material (truss-shaped) while opening central occlusal access; intermediate between conservative and traditional; experimental design with limited adoption
- **Marginal Ridge**: cuspal anatomy at the coronal edge of the tooth's occlusal surface; key for structural integrity and fracture resistance
- **Operating Microscope (DOM)**: magnification device (typically 4–20x) used in endodontics; critical for conservative AC designs to achieve adequate canal visualization and avoid missing canals
- **Ultrasonic Troughing**: ultrasonic instrumentation to create a groove around canal orifices (especially MB2 in mandibular molars), improving visualization and instrumentation access within conservative AC outline
- **FEA (Finite Element Analysis)**: computational biomechanical modeling to predict stress distribution and fracture patterns in teeth with different AC designs
- **Fracture Resistance**: load at which a tooth fails (ruptures) under compressive or shear testing; measured in Newtons (N)
