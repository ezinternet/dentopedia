## Why Ingested

→ [[local-anesthesia/jain-2025-psa-buccal-infiltration-maxillary-molar-extraction]]

Large RCT (n=150) directly comparing PSA nerve block vs buccal infiltration for maxillary molar extraction, both using 2% lidocaine 1:100,000 epi. Provides evidence that buccal infiltration is preferred (equivalent efficacy, fewer complications) — relevant to extraction technique guidance in the lidocaine clinical tool.

## Summary

Double-blind RCT, n=150 (75/group), ASA I healthy adults, non-surgical maxillary second and third molar extractions. Both groups: 1.8mL of 2% lidocaine 1:100,000 epinephrine.
PSA block group vs buccal infiltration group.

Primary outcomes:
- VAS: PSA 2.11±1.21 vs infiltration 2.02±1.30 (p=0.71, NS)
- VDS: comparable (p=0.96, NS)
- Supplemental anesthesia: PSA 10.7% vs infiltration 12.0% (p=0.74, NS)

Secondary outcomes:
- Complications: PSA 4.0% (hematoma, trismus, positive aspiration) vs infiltration 0%, p=0.03
- Analgesic use within 3h: comparable (p=0.48, NS)

## Clinical Implications

- Buccal infiltration = equivalent anesthesia to PSA block for maxillary molar extraction
- PSA block carries 4% complication risk (hematoma, trismus, positive aspiration) — clinically significant
- Buccal infiltration: simpler technique, safer, no aspiration risk
- Recommendation: buccal infiltration is the preferred technique for routine maxillary molar extraction
- Both techniques used standard 2% lidocaine 1:100,000 epi — no dose advantage to PSA block

## Limitations

- ASA I only (healthy adults) — generalizability to medically compromised limited
- Non-surgical extractions only
- No long-term follow-up
