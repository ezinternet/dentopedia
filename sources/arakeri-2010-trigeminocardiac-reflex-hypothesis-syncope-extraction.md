## Why Ingested

This is the paper that first put the trigeminocardiac reflex (TCR) forward as the mechanism of extraction-induced syncope — the historical origin of a claim that later dental reviews now state routinely (see [[drug/systemic-disease/li-2026-syncope-in-dentistry-risk-factors]]). It is needed as the *origin* node of that claim, not as evidence for it: a 2010 hypothesis paper carries no outcome data, so the wiki must keep it distinct from the observational work that later tests the reflex.

## Three-line Summary

Hypothesis paper (Med Hypotheses 2010), no prospective outcome data reported in the retrieved abstract: the authors describe one case of trigeminocardiac reflex after maxillary first-molar extraction and state that they then observed 400 extractions under local anesthesia.
The proposal is that the trigeminocardiac reflex — usually described under general anesthesia, when sympathetic reflexes are blunted — also occurs under local anesthesia during maxillary molar extraction ("dento-cardiac reflex") and mediates syncope; the abstract reports no incidence, heart-rate, or outcome figures.
Clinically it supplies a mechanistic hypothesis for a well-known event (transient loss of consciousness during extraction), but it is a hypothesis only and must not be cited as proof that TCR causes syncope.

국소마취 하에서도 상악 대구치 발치 중 TCR(이른바 치성-심장 반사 (Dento-cardiac Reflex))이 발생해 실신을 매개할 수 있다는 가설을 제시하며, 초록에는 발생률·심박수·결과 수치가 전혀 없다.
발치 중 일시적 의식소실 (Transient Loss Of Consciousness, TLOC)에 대한 기전 가설을 제공하지만 가설일 뿐이며, TCR이 실신을 일으킨다는 증거로 인용해서는 안 된다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Proposes the trigeminocardiac reflex (TCR) as the mechanism of syncope during tooth extraction, rather than an unqualified vasovagal explanation.
- Argues that the reflex is not exclusive to general anesthesia (where sympathetic reflexes are blunted) but can also be elicited under local anesthesia in maxillary molar extraction.
- Introduces the label "dento-cardiac reflex" for this dental-specific afferent trigger.
- Positions the trigger site specifically at maxillary molars (the reported index case was a maxillary first molar).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Hypothesis paper built on a single clinical observation plus an unspecified observation series.
- **Index observation**: one case of trigeminocardiac reflex after extraction of a maxillary first molar.
- **Series described in the abstract**: 400 extractions under local anesthesia, studied "to know the relation between trigeminocardiac reflex and syncope".
- **Outcomes**: the abstract names no measured outcome (no heart rate, blood pressure, incidence, or syncope count) — only the stated aim.
- **Controls / blinding / statistics**: none reported in the abstract; the design as described does not support causal inference.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- No numerical result is available: the retrieved abstract reports neither the incidence of TCR in the 400 extractions nor how many episodes of syncope occurred.
- The only concrete claim is mechanistic — TCR can occur under local anesthesia during maxillary molar extraction and may mediate syncope.
- Because no denominators-to-events figures exist in the accessible text, this page deliberately carries no effect estimate (an honest gap, not an omission).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Hypothesis-level only** — the paper's own genre (Med Hypotheses) is argument, not measurement; it cannot establish that TCR causes syncope.
- **Abstract-only artifact** — full text not retrieved; the design of the 400-extraction observation, its monitoring method, and any results are unknown to this page.
- **Single index case** — one post-extraction TCR episode is a starting observation, not a rate.
- **No comparison group** — syncope during extraction has competing explanations (psychogenic vasovagal, orthostatic, local-anesthetic effects) that a case-plus-series narrative cannot separate.
- **What is needed**: monitored prospective studies recording heart rate/blood pressure continuously during extraction, which is what later observational work provides; without that, the TCR attribution remains unverified.

## 6. Related Work

- li-2026 ([[drug/systemic-disease/li-2026-syncope-in-dentistry-risk-factors]]): current dental narrative review that lists TCR (CN V2/V3 afferents → vagal outflow → bradycardia/hypotension) as one of two syncope pathways; it states the mechanism as established background, which is exactly the layer this 2010 hypothesis paper sits beneath.
- malave-2022 ([[drug/systemic-disease/malave-2022-vasovagal-reactions-during-interventional-pain]]): procedural vasovagal review whose mechanism account is Bezold-Jarisch/venous-pooling centred; Arakeri's proposal is an alternative, extraction-specific afferent route to the same bradycardia-hypotension endpoint.
- Later observational work on TCR during maxillary third-molar surgery (Agarwal et al. 2022) is *not held in this vault* — it is the kind of monitored study that could test this hypothesis, and the absence of that page is a stated gap, not a hidden one.

## 7. Glossary

- **TCR (Trigeminocardiac Reflex, 삼차신경-심장 반사)**: bradycardia ± hypotension elicited by stimulation of a trigeminal branch (here, dental afferents), via trigeminal sensory nucleus → vagal outflow.
- **Dento-cardiac reflex (치성-심장 반사)**: the authors' term for TCR triggered by dental/extraction stimuli.
- **TLOC (Transient Loss Of Consciousness, 일시적 의식소실)**: umbrella term the paper uses for the clinical event it is trying to explain.
- **Vasovagal syncope (미주신경성 실신)**: the conventional label for fainting in dental settings; the paper questions whether its pathophysiology is fully explained.
