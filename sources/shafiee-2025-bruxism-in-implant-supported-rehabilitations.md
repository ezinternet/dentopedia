## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (1990–2024 literature) synthesizing bruxism-related complications and management in implant-supported rehabilitations, covering biological, technical, and treatment strategy dimensions.

Bruxism patients show 2.2–4.7 fold increased implant failure risk (Chrcanovic: 13.0% vs 4.6%, HR 3.396); occlusal splints reduce implant stress by 33–73% at loads of 300–900 N; splinted prosthetic designs consistently outperform non-splinted in biomechanical performance.

Evidence for peri-implant bone loss is limited (slight association only); technical/mechanical complications (screw loosening, fractures) are the primary documented concern; well-designed RCTs remain lacking for most management strategies.

이갈이 환자에서 임플란트 실패 위험 2.2–4.7배 증가 (Chrcanovic: 13.0% vs 4.6%, HR 3.396); 교합 스플린트(Occlusal Splint)는 300–900 N 하중에서 응력을 33–73% 감소; 연결(Splinted) 보철 설계가 비연결(Non-splinted)보다 생체역학적으로 우수.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **Failure risk quantification**: Synthesizes that bruxing patients have 2.2–4.7 fold higher implant failure risk vs non-bruxing patients, with Chrcanovic (n=2,670 patients, 10,096 implants) reporting 13.0% vs 4.6% failure (HR 3.396; 95% CI 1.314, 8.777; P=0.012).
2. **Splint biomechanics**: Teixeira photoelastic study shows occlusal splints reduce stress by 33.22% at 300 N, 66.66% at 600 N, 73.33% at 900 N — protective effect scales with load magnitude.
3. **Prosthetic design evidence**: Systematic reviews demonstrate splinted designs lose less bone and have lower failure rates vs non-splinted (Shah et al.); splinting more effective than occlusal devices alone for abutment screw stress reduction (Silva et al.).
4. **Bone loss nuance**: Peri-implant bone loss association with bruxism is NOT well-established — systematic reviews (Bertolini, Di Fiore) find only slight relationships and insufficient evidence for causality.
5. **Primary failure mode**: Mechanical overload/fracture of implant components (implant body fractures, screw loosening, framework failures) is the dominant failure mechanism in bruxism patients — not peri-implantitis.
6. **Management taxonomy**: Comprehensive framework — behavioral (CBT, stress management), pharmacological (botulinum toxin, muscle relaxants), dental (splints, prosthetic design, strategic placement, occlusal scheme).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

**Study design**: Narrative review; no PRISMA protocol or formal risk-of-bias assessment. Covers publications January 1990 – December 2024 across 6 databases + Google Scholar. Includes clinical studies, SRs, meta-analyses, case series. Excludes single case reports, animal studies, in vitro studies without clinical relevance.

**Bruxism diagnosis criteria**: Uses international consensus grading — "possible," "probable," "definite" sleep or awake bruxism. Notes that standardized diagnostic criteria remain inconsistently applied across included studies.

**Biomechanical evidence sources**: Primarily finite element analysis (FEA) studies and photoelastic analysis; limited prospective clinical trials specifically in implant patients with bruxism.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Implant Failure Risk

| Source | Population | Bruxing Failure | Non-bruxing Failure | Effect Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Häggman-Henrikson et al. | Probable bruxing patients | — | — | OR 2.2 (95% CI 1.337–3.583, p=0.002) |
| Chrcanovic et al. | 2,670 pts, 10,096 implants | 13.0% | 4.6% | HR 3.396 (95% CI 1.314–8.777, p=0.012) |
| Chrcanovic (technical) | Bruxing subgroup | Higher mechanical complications | — | OR 2.71 (95% CI 1.25–5.88) |
| Meta-analysis range | Multiple SRs | — | — | 2.2–4.7 fold increased risk |

### Occlusal Splint Stress Reduction (Teixeira photoelastic)

| Load | Stress Reduction |
|---|---|
| 300 N | 33.22% |
| 600 N | 66.66% |
| 900 N | 73.33% |

Note: FEA studies (Dos Santos Marsico, Silva, Henrique) confirm splints reduce stress in implants while transferring loads to bone tissues; oblique loading stresses increase 4–19% in absence of splints.

### Bruxism Prevalence

- Sleep bruxism (Bruxisme du Sommeil): 8–16% of adults
- Awake bruxism (Bruxisme Éveillé): 22–31% of adults
- Bruxing forces: 6–10× normal occlusal function

### Peri-implant Bone Loss

Limited evidence; Bertolini SR found insufficient evidence for causal relationship; Bredberg (matched groups) found limited differences in marginal bone loss patterns; Di Fiore SR found inconclusive evidence.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

**Study limitations:**
- Narrative review design — no formal protocol, no PRISMA, no risk-of-bias assessment
- Heterogeneous bruxism diagnostic criteria across included studies
- Most evidence from retrospective studies and FEA; limited prospective clinical trials
- Technical complications evidence primarily from one high-quality case-control study (Chrcanovic)
- Behavioral and pharmacological management evidence minimal for implant-specific populations
- Limited long-term outcome data for management strategies

**Future directions noted by authors:**
- Well-designed RCTs evaluating long-term management strategy outcomes
- Standardized diagnostic criteria development
- Investigation of patient-specific factors (intensity, compliance, site factors)
- Interdisciplinary approaches integrating behavioral and dental management

## 7. Glossary

- **이갈이 (Bruxism)**: 수면 또는 각성 시 반복적 턱 근육 활동 (저작·이갈기·조이기)
- **교합 스플린트 (Occlusal Splint)**: 치아에 착용하는 아크릴 장치로 과기능 시 임플란트 응력 분산
- **연결 보철 (Splinted Prosthesis)**: 인접 임플란트를 연결한 보철; 비연결(non-splinted)보다 생체역학적으로 우수
- **골-임플란트 접촉 (Osseointegration)**: 임플란트와 골의 직접 결합 — 치주인대 완충 없이 힘이 직접 전달됨
- **유한요소분석 (Finite Element Analysis, FEA)**: 임플란트 주위 응력 분포 시뮬레이션 방법론
- **상호 보호 교합 (Mutually Protected Occlusion)**: 전치부와 구치부가 서로 보호하는 교합 형태; 임플란트 보철에 권장
