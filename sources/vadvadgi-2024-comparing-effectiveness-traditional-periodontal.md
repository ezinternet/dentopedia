## Why Ingested

Ingested as part of the 2026-07-20 "치위생사 스케일링 술기" (dental hygienist scaling technique) topic sweep. This RCT provides direct empirical (randomized) evidence for the treatment-hierarchy claim in [[periodontics/non-surgical-instrumentation/unknown-2009-non-surgical-and-surgical-periodontal]] — that non-surgical therapy (hygienist-delivered scaling and root planing) and surgical therapy produce broadly comparable clinical improvement — while extending it with quantified patient-reported comfort/satisfaction data the 2009 narrative review did not provide.

## Three-line Summary

Two-arm RCT (n=120 adults, moderate-to-severe periodontal disease, multi-center India + Saudi Arabia) comparing traditional periodontal surgery (flap surgery/grafts, by periodontal surgeons) against non-surgical therapy (scaling and root planing, by trained dental hygienists), with clinical (pocket depth, attachment level) and patient-reported (pain, discomfort, satisfaction) outcomes at baseline, 3, and 6 months.

Per the tables and the PubMed-indexed abstract, pocket-depth reduction was larger with surgery (6.8→3.7 mm, Δ−3.1 mm) than non-surgical therapy (6.7→4.0 mm, Δ−2.7 mm), and attachment-level gain likewise favored surgery (Δ−2.1 vs Δ−1.6 mm); non-surgical therapy scored lower pain (2.1 vs 3.6/10) and discomfort (2.9 vs 4.2/10) with similar satisfaction (8.4 vs 7.8/10).

Key limitation: the PMC full text is internally inconsistent — its own narrative Results paragraphs cite a *different* set of numbers than Tables 1–3 (and the tables/figures are explicitly captioned "arbitrary and for illustrative purposes only"), and the Methods section leaves template placeholders (clinic name, recruitment dates, statistics-software name) unfilled — so quantitative claims should be treated with caution; see Limitations.

## 세줄요약

2군 무작위 대조군 연구(Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) (성인 120명, 중등도-중증 치주질환, 인도·사우디아라비아 다기관) — 전통적 치주수술(flap surgery/graft, 치주전문의 시행) 대 비외과적 치료(스케일링·치근활택술, Scaling and Root Planing, SRP, 훈련된 치위생사 시행) 비교, 임상지표(치주낭 깊이, 임상부착수준)와 환자보고지표(통증·불편감·만족도)를 기저·3개월·6개월에 측정.

표 및 PubMed 등재 초록 기준으로 치주낭 깊이(Periodontal Pocket Depth, PPD) 감소는 수술군에서 더 큼(6.8→3.7mm, Δ−3.1mm) 대 비외과군(6.7→4.0mm, Δ−2.7mm), 임상부착수준(Clinical Attachment Level, CAL) 개선도 수술군 우세(Δ−2.1 대 Δ−1.6mm); 비외과군은 통증(2.1 대 3.6/10)·불편감(2.9 대 4.2/10)이 낮고 만족도(8.4 대 7.8/10)는 유사.

핵심 한계 — PMC 전문(全文) 자체가 내적으로 불일치함: 본문 서술문의 수치가 Table 1–3 수치와 다르고(표·그림은 "arbitrary and for illustrative purposes only"로 명시), Methods 절에 병원명·모집기간·통계 소프트웨어명 등 템플릿 placeholder가 미기재로 남아있어 수치 해석에 주의가 필요함 (Limitations 참조).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Direct RCT-level, head-to-head comparison of traditional periodontal surgery (flap surgery + grafts) versus dental-hygienist-delivered non-surgical therapy (SRP) in moderate-to-severe periodontal disease (n=120), tracking both clinical parameters and patient-centered outcomes in parallel.
- Quantifies an efficacy-vs-comfort trade-off: surgery shows the larger reduction in pocket depth and the larger gain in clinical attachment level at 6 months; non-surgical therapy shows meaningfully lower pain and discomfort scores with comparable patient satisfaction.
- Adds patient-reported outcome data (pain, discomfort, satisfaction on 1–10 scales) to a treatment-hierarchy question that older syntheses (e.g., [[periodontics/non-surgical-instrumentation/unknown-2009-non-surgical-and-surgical-periodontal]]) framed mainly in terms of clinical parameters.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Two-arm parallel RCT; examiners (periodontists conducting clinical measurements and patient-centered data collection) were blinded.
- **Population**: 120 adult patients diagnosed with moderate to severe periodontal disease; informed consent obtained from all participants.
- **Randomization**: Participants randomly assigned to traditional surgery group (TSG) or non-surgical therapy group (NSTG); randomization method and allocation-concealment procedure are not described in the available text.
- **Interventions**:
  - TSG: traditional periodontal surgery (flap surgery and grafts when indicated), performed by experienced periodontal surgeons following standard protocols.
  - NSTG: scaling and root planing using ultrasonic and hand instruments, administered by trained dental hygienists.
- **Outcome measures**: Periodontal pocket depth (mm) and clinical attachment level (mm) at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months; patient-reported pain, discomfort, and satisfaction via standardized questionnaires (1–10 scale) at each follow-up visit.
- **Statistics**: Descriptive statistics (means, SD); independent t-tests and Chi-square tests for TSG-vs-NSTG comparisons; α = 0.05. The statistical software used is not named in the available text (template placeholder "[Name of Statistical Software]" left unfilled).
- **Reporting gaps**: recruiting clinic name and exact recruitment start/end dates are likewise unfilled template placeholders in the available full text; no CONSORT flow diagram, dropout/attrition figures, sample-size/power calculation, or effect-size confidence intervals are reported in the text obtained.

| CAL baseline → 6 mo | 7.2 → 5.1 mm (Δ −2.1 mm) | 7.1 → 5.5 mm (Δ −1.6 mm) |
| Pain (1–10) | 3.6 | 2.1 |
| Discomfort (1–10) | 4.2 | 2.9 |
| Satisfaction (1–10) | 7.8 | 8.4 |

No p-values or confidence intervals are reported for these between-group comparisons in the available text, despite the Methods stating t-tests/Chi-square were used. The PubMed-indexed abstract's own conclusion states surgery produced the greater reduction in pocket depth and attachment loss; the article's Discussion/Conclusion sections instead emphasize that "both...are effective," without explicitly stating surgical superiority — a further internal inconsistency (see Limitations).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Internal numerical inconsistency**: the narrative Results paragraphs report PPD change of −2.4 mm (TSG) / −2.2 mm (NSTG) and CAL change of −2.3 mm (TSG) / −2.1 mm (NSTG) and pain/discomfort/satisfaction of 4.2/4.8/7.6 (TSG) and 2.7/3.3/8.2 (NSTG) — all different from Tables 1–3 and from the PubMed abstract (reproduced above). Readers should not treat either number set as unambiguously "the" result without access to the original tables/figures.
- **"Arbitrary" value disclaimers**: the Results section states three separate times that the figures/tables "are arbitrary and for illustrative purposes only" — it is unclear whether this is leftover boilerplate from a figure-generation step or an admission that displayed values are not the actual trial data.
- **Unfilled manuscript placeholders**: "[Name of Dental Clinic]", "[Start Date]", "[End Date]", and "[Name of Statistical Software]" appear literally in the Methods section, indicating incomplete manuscript preparation and/or minimal copyediting/peer review before publication.
- **Very short article**: published as a 3-page item (pp. S936–S938) in a supplement issue (Vol 16, Suppl 1), consistent with a conference-proceedings-style short communication rather than a full RCT report — no randomization/allocation-concealment method, no CONSORT diagram, no attrition/dropout accounting, no effect sizes or confidence intervals, no sample-size justification.
- **Follow-up limited to 6 months**; no data on long-term stability, re-treatment/maintenance needs, or cost.
- **No blinding of patients or treating clinicians** described (only outcome examiners are stated as blinded), which is expected given surgery cannot be sham-controlled, but is not discussed as a limitation in the source text.

Given these issues, this paper is best used as a directional data point (both traditional surgery and non-surgical hygienist-delivered SRP produce clinically meaningful improvement; non-surgical therapy is better tolerated) rather than as a source of precise effect-size estimates.

## 6. Related Work

The paper situates itself within the standard periodontal literature establishing both flap surgery/grafts and scaling-and-root-planing as "established treatment approaches" for periodontal disease, citing prior work on the efficacy of both modalities and on reduced pain/discomfort with non-surgical therapy, without naming specific comparator studies in the extracted text. Within this wiki, it sits alongside [[periodontics/non-surgical-instrumentation/unknown-2009-non-surgical-and-surgical-periodontal]], a narrative review that establishes non-surgical therapy as first-line and surgery as adjunctive with a mandatory post-SRP re-evaluation before deciding on surgery — a treatment-hierarchy framework this RCT's design (parallel-arm, not stepped) does not directly test but whose "both approaches improve clinical parameters" conclusion it is broadly consistent with.

## 7. Glossary

- **TSG**: Traditional Surgery Group — the surgical treatment arm (flap surgery, grafts).
- **NSTG**: Non-Surgical Therapy Group — the scaling-and-root-planing treatment arm.
- **PPD**: Periodontal Pocket Depth, measured in mm.
- **CAL**: Clinical Attachment Level, measured in mm.
- **SRP**: Scaling and Root Planing — mechanical removal of plaque/calculus from tooth and root surfaces, the core procedure of non-surgical periodontal therapy.
- **Flap surgery**: Surgical reflection of the gingiva to access and debride root surfaces and underlying bone directly, sometimes combined with grafting.
