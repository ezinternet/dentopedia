# Sources: Nayak 2025 — Commensalism of Fusobacterium nucleatum

## Why Ingested

F. nucleatum is the canonical bridging species in subgingival biofilm. We already have [[oral-microbiology/mcilvanna-2021-fusobacterium-nucleatum-oral-cancer]] covering F. nucleatum in oral cancer. This paper fills the periodontal virulence angle — commensal-to-pathobiont transition, FadA adhesin mechanistics, immune evasion, and butyric acid duality. Extends our F. nucleatum coverage beyond cancer into its foundational periodontal role.

## Key Findings

### Taxonomy & Structure
- Gram-negative, spindle-shaped rod; name from Latin "fusus" (spindle) + "bacterion" (rod)
- Five subspecies: nucleatum, polymorphum, vincentii, animalis, fusiforme — each with unique rRNA gene sequence

### Culture & Metabolism
- Requires nutrient-rich media; primarily uses amino acids (glutamate, histidine, aspartate)
- Ferments glutamate and lysine → acetate and butyrate
- Metabolic byproducts (butyrate, propionate, ammonium ions) inhibit gingival fibroblast proliferation
- Produces volatile sulfur compounds (H₂S, methyl mercaptan) — major contributors to halitosis

### Beneficial / Commensal Role
- Butyric acid: energy source for anti-inflammatory cells; promotes transcription of protease inhibitors released by neutrophils
- Stimulates mucosal epithelial cells to produce human beta-defensin-2 (and to lesser extent hBD-3) — effective against gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria and some viruses

### Bridging Species Role (Pathogenicity)
- Links early colonizers (gram-positive, e.g., Streptococcus) with late colonizers (gram-negative periodontal pathogens)
- Surface adhesins: FomA, RadD, Fap2, Aid1, CmpA — facilitate coaggregation and biofilm formation
- Infects diverse host cells: endothelial cells, fibroblasts, epithelial cells
- Releases extracellular vesicles with bioactive compounds for bacterial communication

### Primary Virulence Factor: FadA
- Pre-FadA + mature FadA → active FadA complex
- Binds E-cadherin → invasion of host cells, disruption of cell adhesion and intercellular communication
- FadA highly conserved in oral F. nucleatum; variable in extraoral sites → potential diagnostic marker for oral strains
- FadA interacts with retinoic acid-inducible gene I receptor → NF-κB pathway activation → inflammatory response
- Amyloid FadA: produced under disease/stress conditions (not healthy states); enhances biofilm formation, acid tolerance, bacterial attachment; induces bone loss in rat periodontal tissues

### Other Virulence Mechanisms
- **LPS**: recognized by TLRs on gingival epithelial cells and fibroblasts → NLRP3 inflammasome activation → IL-1β production → bone resorption
- **65 kDa serine protease**: degrades host ECM (provides nutrients for bacterial survival); cleaves IgA chain → immune evasion
- **Butyric acid (dual role)**: low concentrations = anti-inflammatory energy source; elevated levels = ROS production in osteoblasts → bone-forming cell damage

### Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT)
- F. nucleatum (with other gram-negative pathogens) induces EMT in gingival epithelial cells
- Upregulates Snail-1; suppresses E-cadherin → disrupts epithelial cell adhesion → deeper tissue invasion

### Immune Microenvironment
- Subspecies nucleatum and animalis inhibit neutrophil superoxide production → prevents oxidative neutrophil death
- Induces necrosis and apoptosis in neutrophils → reduces immune cell numbers at infection site
- Increases IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, HMGB1 → promotes macrophage infiltration → accelerates gingival inflammation

### Systemic Involvement
- **Preterm birth**: hematogenous transmission from maternal oral cavity to intrauterine environment
- **Colorectal cancer**: higher F. nucleatum concentrations in carcinomas vs normal tissue
- **Other systemic infections**: oropharyngeal and disseminated via bloodstream from oral primary site

## Evidence Assessment
- Narrative review; no systematic search protocol or PRISMA
- Evidence_level: narrative_review
- Strong mechanistic synthesis; clinical recommendations limited by review design
