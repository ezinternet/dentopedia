## Why Ingested

Found while surveying hyposalivation/xerostomia dental-management literature. This RCT's scope is broader than hyposalivation specifically — it covers a full basket of oncology-therapy oral complications (mucositis, mucosal lesions, gingivitis/periodontitis, dental caries, TMJ dysfunction), with xerostomia being only one of the monitored complications rather than the paper's focus. It is included here as a rare RCT-level supportive-care/monitoring-model reference (structured teledentistry protocol vs standard oncology-unit care) in an area otherwise dominated by observational and narrative evidence, not as a hyposalivation-mechanism paper. It adds a monitoring/delivery-model layer to [[overviews/oral-mucositis-cancer-therapy-overview]], which covers pharmacological and photobiomodulation prevention but not how complications are detected between oncology visits, and sits beside [[oral-medicine/salivary-chemosensory/sardellitti-2023-xerostomia-pharmacological-traditional-medicine-overview]] on the xerostomia side of the same complication basket.

## Three-line Summary

Prospective RCT (n=105 randomized, 90 completed; breast/lung/colon cancer patients starting chemotherapy or combined therapy; Oncology Hospital of Kifissia, Athens, Greece, March 2022–November 2023) comparing a structured telemedicine/e-health teledentistry monitoring protocol against standard oncology-unit oral care advice, with assessments at baseline (T0), mid-treatment (T1), and end of treatment (T2).

The telemedicine group had significantly lower rates of mucosal lesions (OR 7.59 at T2, p<0.001), gingival bleeding (OR 35.25, p<0.001), gingivitis (OR 38.83, p<0.001), dental caries (OR 26.11, p<0.001), and moderate/advanced periodontitis (OR 8.25, p<0.001) at end of treatment, plus higher toothbrushing frequency (OR 5.52, p<0.001); no group differed at baseline and TMJ disorders showed no significant difference at any timepoint.

Structured teledentistry monitoring (91.8% adherence, 100% referral follow-through) is a low-cost, high-effectiveness model for oncology oral-complication prevention/early detection, but the design confounds "more structured follow-up" with "digital monitoring" per se, blinding was infeasible, and differential mortality (14 control vs 1 intervention deaths) reduced the final sample without registry pre-registration.

## 세줄요약

전향적 무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) (등록 105명, 완료 90명; 유방암/폐암/대장암 항암화학요법 또는 병용요법 시작 환자; 그리스 아테네 Kifissia 종양병원, 2022년 3월–2023년 11월) — 원격의료/이헬스(e-health) 구조화 원격치과(teledentistry) 모니터링 프로토콜 vs 표준 종양내과 구강관리를 기준선(T0)·치료중기(T1)·치료종료(T2)에서 비교.

원격의료군은 치료종료 시점에서 점막병변(mucosal lesion, OR 7.59, p<0.001), 치은출혈(gingival bleeding, OR 35.25, p<0.001), 치은염(gingivitis, OR 38.83, p<0.001), 치아우식(dental caries, OR 26.11, p<0.001), 중등도-중증 치주염(periodontitis, OR 8.25, p<0.001) 발생률이 유의하게 낮았고 칫솔질 빈도(OR 5.52, p<0.001)는 유의하게 높았음; 기준선에서는 군간 차이 없었고 턱관절장애(TMJ disorder)는 전 시점에서 유의차 없음.

구조화 원격치과 모니터링(순응도 91.8%, 의뢰 후속조치 100%)은 종양환자 구강합병증 예방·조기발견에 저비용·고효과적 모델이나, 설계상 "더 구조화된 추적관찰" 효과와 "디지털 모니터링 자체" 효과가 혼재되어 있고 눈가림이 불가능했으며 대조군에서 사망(14명)이 개입군(1명)보다 많아 최종표본이 줄었고 임상시험 사전등록도 없었음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First prospective RCT conducted in Greece to comprehensively investigate oral health in oncology patients with solid tumors (breast, colorectal, lung) during active antineoplastic treatment, per the authors.
- Demonstrates that a structured telemedicine/e-health teledentistry monitoring protocol (scheduled synchronous + asynchronous teleconsultations, digital symptom reporting, MASCC/ISOO-guided in-person referral) significantly reduces the incidence of mucosal lesions, gingival bleeding, gingivitis, dental caries, and periodontitis compared with usual oncology-unit oral-care advice.
- Reports high adherence to the digital monitoring protocol (91.8% of scheduled remote communications completed) and 100% follow-through on dental referrals triggered by remote symptom reports.
- Frames teledentistry as a low-cost, scalable model for integrating oral care into oncology treatment without additional dedicated dental infrastructure, relevant to national cancer-control policy (Europe's Beating Cancer Plan, 2022).
- Documents a broad panel of oncology-therapy oral complications (mucositis/mucosal lesions, gingivitis, periodontitis, dental caries, TMJ dysfunction, candidiasis observation in denture wearers) rather than a single-complication study.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Prospective, randomized, interventional (2-arm) clinical trial; computer-generated randomization by an independent statistician with concealed allocation until baseline (T0) data confirmed.
- **Setting**: Two oncology clinics/outpatient departments, Oncology Hospital of Kifissia, Athens, Greece; ethics approval from the hospital (101/18-02-2022) and University of West Attica (20256/15-03-2022).
- **Period**: March 2022–November 2023 (21-month recruitment/follow-up).
- **Population**: 105 patients with solid tumors of breast, lung, or colon, treatment-naive, initiating chemotherapy or combination therapy; ECOG 0–2; adult; Greek-language competent; basic smartphone/computer ability (or cohabitant able to use one). Excluded: cognitive impairment, ECOG 3–4, severe comorbidity, age <18, other tumor types, prior antineoplastic therapy.
- **Arms**: Control (n=56) — standard oncology-unit advice to see a dentist pre-treatment, routine care thereafter. Intervention (n=49) — structured individualized oral-health management: baseline hygiene education, e-health platform training, scheduled synchronous/asynchronous teleconsultations between T0/T1/T2 clinical visits, MASCC/ISOO-guided management of reported symptoms, referral to hospital Dental Department or private dentist when indicated.
- **Assessment timepoints**: T0 (baseline/pre-treatment), T1 (mid-treatment), T2 (end of treatment) — timing individualized to each patient's oncology regimen but consistent in sequence across participants.
- **Outcome instruments**: Periodontal exam (PPD, CAL, BOP, Gingival Index, Plaque Index; 6 sites/tooth; single calibrated periodontist, same probe/unit throughout); Oral Mucosa Rating Scale (OMRS) + CTCAE v5.0 grading for mucosal alterations; patient-reported xerostomia; categorical toothbrushing frequency; caries by clinical/radiographic exam (initial/moderate/advanced). QoL instruments (EORTC QLQ-C30, EORTC QLQ-OH15, HADS) were also collected but analyzed separately as part of a broader doctoral protocol — not reported in this paper.
- **Statistics**: Kolmogorov–Smirnov normality test; independent t-test/Chi-square for baseline homogeneity; Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) for longitudinal categorical outcomes (group × time interaction), reported as OR (95% CI); SPSS v21.00; two-sided p<0.05 significance; complete-case sensitivity analysis performed.
- **Blinding**: Not feasible for participants or the treating dentist (nature of the intervention); single blinded-to-nothing examiner performed all standardized clinical/radiographic exams to minimize measurement variability.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Enrollment/attrition**: 105 randomized (intervention 49, control 56) → 90 analyzed at T2 (15 deaths: 14 control, 1 intervention, between T1–T2).
- **Baseline homogeneity**: no significant between-group differences in sex, age, education, marital status, employment, tumor site, treatment type, ECOG, metastases, or surgical history (all p>0.05); borderline difference in toothbrushing frequency (p=0.058).
- **Mucosal lesions** (control vs telemedicine): baseline OR=1.01 (0.44–2.33) p=0.977; mid-therapy OR=2.20 (1.00–4.89) p=0.053; end of therapy OR=7.59 (2.98–19.37) **p<0.001**.
- **Gingival bleeding**: baseline OR=0.93 (0.43–2.02) p=0.853; mid-therapy OR=19.20 (2.44–151.16) **p<0.001**; end of therapy OR=35.25 (4.44–280.15) **p<0.001**.
- **Gingivitis**: baseline OR=0.93 (0.43–2.02) p=0.853; mid-therapy OR=20.92 (2.67–164.25) **p<0.001**; end of therapy OR=38.83 (4.89–308.25) **p<0.001**.
- **Dental caries**: baseline OR=0.93 (0.43–2.02) p=0.853; mid-therapy OR=3.12 (1.12–8.72) **p=0.030**; end of therapy OR=26.11 (3.27–208.76) **p<0.001**.
- **Moderate/advanced periodontitis**: baseline OR=1.63 (0.74–3.59) p=0.224; mid-therapy OR=3.63 (1.52–8.67) **p<0.001**; end of therapy OR=8.25 (2.50–27.18) **p<0.001**.
- **TMJ disorders**: no significant difference at any timepoint — baseline OR=1.21 (0.55–2.70) p=0.627; mid-treatment OR=1.58 (0.70–3.54) p=0.267; end of treatment OR=2.04 (0.83–5.00) p=0.120.
- **Toothbrushing frequency (2–3×/day)**, telemedicine vs control: baseline OR=2.44 (1.00–6.03) p=0.058; mid-therapy OR=3.60 (1.45–8.96) **p=0.006**; end of therapy OR=5.52 (1.96–15.51) **p<0.001**.
- **Complete-case sensitivity analysis**: consistent with the primary GEE analysis; conclusions unchanged.
- **Adherence**: control group — 12/56 (21.4%) reported a dentist visit during the study. Intervention group — 147 remote communications scheduled, 135 completed (**91.8%** adherence); 14/49 (28.6%) reported oral symptoms remotely; 6/49 (12.2%) referred for dental evaluation; 6/6 (**100%**) referred patients attended.
- **Descriptive percentages** (Discussion): mucosal lesions at mid-treatment 51% (telemedicine) vs 69.6% (control); at end of therapy 27.1% (telemedicine) vs 73.8% (control).
- **Prosthetic note**: mostly fixed restorations; 19 patients wore complete removable dentures; oral candidiasis descriptively observed among denture wearers (not analyzed as an independent variable).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Differential mortality between arms (14 control vs 1 intervention deaths, T1–T2) reduced final sample and is a potential confound, though a sensitivity analysis excluding deceased participants gave consistent results; mortality was not a predefined outcome.
- Blinding of participants and the examining dentist was not feasible given the nature of the intervention (a structural limitation common to all behavioral/telehealth RCTs).
- The intervention group received more structured follow-up overall (not just digital monitoring) — the observed benefit reflects a comprehensive telehealth-supported oral-care model rather than isolating the effect of digital monitoring alone.
- Not prospectively registered in a public clinical trial registry.
- Single-center (Athens, Greece) design; generalizability to other healthcare systems/populations not established.
- QoL outcomes (EORTC QLQ-C30/OH15, HADS) were collected but not analyzed/reported in this paper (reserved for separate doctoral-protocol publication) — so the effect on patient-reported quality of life is not yet available from this paper.
- Future work: report the QoL/psychological-distress analysis separately; extend to other cancer types/settings; disentangle the relative contribution of digital monitoring vs increased contact frequency.

## 7. Glossary

- **Teledentistry**: use of telecommunications and digital health platforms for remote oral-health screening, diagnosis, consultation, treatment planning, and monitoring.
- **MASCC/ISOO**: Multinational Association of Supportive Care in Cancer / International Society of Oral Oncology — issuing bodies for oral mucositis/oral-complication management guidelines referenced for the intervention protocol.
- **CTCAE v5.0**: Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0 — grading scale for mucosal-lesion severity used in this study.
- **OMRS**: Oral Mucosa Rating Scale — clinical instrument used to evaluate oral mucosal alterations.
- **GEE (Generalized Estimating Equations)**: statistical model for longitudinal/repeated-measures categorical data accounting for within-subject correlation across time (T0/T1/T2), used here to compare group × time interaction effects.
- **ECOG**: Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status scale (0=fully active to 4=completely disabled); used as an eligibility/homogeneity criterion.
- **PPD/CAL/BOP/GI/PI**: probing pocket depth, clinical attachment level, bleeding on probing, Gingival Index, Plaque Index — standard periodontal-examination parameters.
- **EORTC QLQ-C30 / QLQ-OH15**: European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer core and oral-health-specific quality-of-life questionnaires (collected but analyzed separately from this paper).
- **HADS**: Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale — psychological-distress instrument (collected but analyzed separately from this paper).
