This SR+MA (22 studies, 165,220 participants in the pooled analysis) supplies that number and, more usefully, quantifies how much of the estimate is instrument artefact (I² = 100%). → [[occlusion/franco-2025-prevalence-self-assessed-masticatory-disorders]]

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review with meta-analysis of prevalence (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42020202495) — 22 population-based questionnaire studies of community-dwelling adults aged ≥60 years were included and 21 entered the meta-analysis, pooling 165,220 participants across 10 databases plus grey literature.

Pooled prevalence of self-assessed masticatory disorders was 36% (95% CI 0.28–0.43; I² = 100%), with individual study estimates spanning 4.3% to 61.7%; heterogeneity was not explained by mean age on meta-regression, persisted in definition-based subgroups, and a 1%-margin sensitivity analysis (4 studies) moved the estimate to 33% (95% CI 19–48%; I² = 100%).

Roughly one in three community-dwelling older adults self-reports a masticatory disorder, but the estimate is a public-health planning figure rather than a screening threshold, because the near-total statistical heterogeneity is driven by non-standardised questionnaires that no subgroup could resolve.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review to estimate the prevalence of masticatory disorders specifically in community-dwelling older adults (authors' own claim: "This is the first systematic review to estimate the prevalence of masticatory disorders in community-dwelling elderly individuals").
- Produces a pooled prevalence of 36% (95% CI 0.28–0.43) from 21 studies / 165,220 participants, against a background of earlier single-study estimates of 14.3%–49.7%.
- Documents that the heterogeneity is total (I² = 100%) and *not* attributable to mean age — meta-regression non-significant — nor resolvable by subgrouping on the four operational definitions used in the literature ("chewing difficulty", "chewing disability", "chewing problem", "chewing discomfort").
- Makes the methodological variability itself the finding: only 1 of 22 included studies defined what constitutes chewing dysfunction, and instruments were validated in few or none — the authors' actionable conclusion is a call for standardised, ideally validated self-report instruments combined with objective assessment, not a screening recommendation.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: PRISMA-compliant SR+MA; protocol registered PROSPERO CRD42020202495.
- **Databases**: Cinahl, Embase, Lilacs, Livivo, PubMed/Medline, Scopus, Web of Science, plus grey literature (Google Scholar, Open Grey, Proquest, incl. theses/dissertations). MeSH/EMTREE-derived terms; no language and no publication-date restriction. References managed in EndNote Web.
- **Eligibility**: original population-based cross-sectional/ecological studies reporting prevalence of self-assessed masticatory disorders in community-dwelling adults aged ≥60 years, both sexes, sampled so that the population was representative of the source population, using a self-reported symptom questionnaire (validated or not). Excluded: mixed-age samples without age-stratified data, objective masticatory performance tests (colorimetric capsules, colour-changing gum, sieving, test foods), nursing-home/home-care populations, prosthesis-evaluation studies, non-population samples, and all non-original document types.
- **Screening**: two independent reviewers (I.L.F., L.C.P.T.) at title/abstract then full-text stage; third reviewer (R.C.) for disagreements.
- **Risk of bias**: Joanna Briggs Institute Checklist for Cross-Sectional Studies (prevalence and cohort study variants used for those designs).
- **Statistics**: meta-analysis of proportions, random-effects (inverse-variance weights), DerSimonian–Laird tau estimator, I² for heterogeneity, 95% CIs by Clopper–Pearson; RStudio 1.2.1335. Planned sensitivity analysis by required sample size (5% and 1% margins of error, 95% CI, infinite-population assumption), random-effects meta-regression on mean age (α = 0.05), subgroup analysis by the four definition groups, and publication bias by funnel plot + Egger's test.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Total heterogeneity unexplained**: I² = 100% survived meta-regression on mean age, definition-based subgrouping, and the two sensitivity scenarios; the authors attribute it to methodological differences, absent case definitions, and non-standardised/non-validated instruments.
- **Self-report only**: no objective masticatory assessment in most included studies — recall bias, subjectivity, and question wording are uncontrolled; under-reporting of conditions the participant does not perceive is possible.
- **No case definition**: only 1 of 22 studies defined chewing dysfunction; several omitted even the question asked, which the authors say hampered tabulation.
- **No sex or denture-status subgroup analysis was performed** — only mean age (meta-regression) and the four definition groups were explored; denture use appears inside some item stems (e.g. Cho & Kim 2019, Moon & Hong 2017, Park 2013, Shin 2022 asked about teeth/dentures/gums) but is never stratified. This is a gap relative to what the paper's own framing invites.
- **No routine-screening recommendation is made.** The authors stop at a prevalence estimate plus a call for standardised instruments, combined subjective + objective assessment, and use of the figure to inform public-health and preventive strategies; they do not propose a screening threshold, a screening interval, or a screening instrument.
- **Internal inconsistencies in the source** (reproduced verbatim, not corrected): (a) abstract "Of the 7008 articles identified" vs Results/Figure 1 "A total of 8761 articles were found"; (b) abstract reports the meta-regression as "(p < 0.05)" while Results reports the mean-age effect as non-significant "(p > 0.05)"; (c) Table 1 lists Kida et al. 2007 under "Norway" but the data-collection site as Pwani/Dar es Salaam, Tanzania, and lists Srinarupat et al. 2022 under "Tokyo" with the country as Thailand; (d) mean age is "not reported" for three Korean studies (Cho & Kim 2019, Kim 2023, Park 2013).
- **Scope limits**: community-dwelling only — nursing-home and home-care populations were excluded by design, so the 36% cannot be transported to institutionalised elders; studies of prosthesis wearers evaluated for prosthodontic treatment were also excluded.
- **Future work (authors')**: combine subjective and objective measurement, adopt specific validated self-report questionnaires per guideline recommendations, and standardise criteria to reduce heterogeneity.

## 7. Glossary

- **Masticatory disorder**: here, a *self-reported* chewing difficulty/disability/problem/discomfort captured by questionnaire — not a measured masticatory performance deficit.
- **Community-dwelling**: living in the community (own home or non-institutional setting), as opposed to nursing-home or home-care populations, which were excluded.
- **I² (inconsistency index)**: proportion of total variation across studies attributable to heterogeneity rather than chance; 100% means essentially all variability is between-study.
- **DerSimonian–Laird estimator**: method-of-moments estimator of between-study variance (tau²) used in the random-effects pooling.
- **Clopper–Pearson interval**: exact binomial confidence interval used here for the 95% CIs of proportions.
- **JBI Checklist for Cross-Sectional Studies**: Joanna Briggs Institute critical-appraisal instrument used for risk-of-bias assessment.
- **Meta-regression**: regression of effect size on study-level covariates (here mean age) to test whether a covariate explains heterogeneity.
