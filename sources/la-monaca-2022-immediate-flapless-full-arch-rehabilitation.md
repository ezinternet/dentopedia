## Why Ingested

기존 [[wiki/implants/del-fabbro-2022-full-arch-tilted-axial-implants-sr-ma]]가 tilted vs axial 임플란트의 MBL 차이를 SR+MA 수준에서 보고했는데, 본 논문(La Monaca 2022)은 동일 주제를 단일기관 최장 10년 추적 후향적 연구로 all-on-4(2 axial+2 tilted) vs all-on-6(전체 axial) 직접 비교 및 회귀분석(임플란트 길이·반대측 자연치·협측골/치은두께)까지 제공해 개별 위험인자 근거를 보강한다.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center cohort (n=28 edentulous patients, 32 prostheses [17 all-on-4/15 all-on-6], 164 implants) evaluating immediate, flapless, computer-guided (NobelGuide) full-arch rehabilitation over a mean follow-up of 6.46 ± 2.236 years (range 1-10 years).

Cumulative implant survival was 89.7% for all-on-4 vs 99.0% for all-on-6 (7 vs 1 failure); prosthesis success was only 51.5% overall (58.8% all-on-4/43.8% all-on-6) despite 88.2% prosthesis survival; marginal bone loss reached 1.38±1.28 mm at 5 years and 2.09±0.56 mm at 10 years, with no significant MBL difference between all-on-4 vs all-on-6 or tilted vs axial implants, but significantly more MBL with natural-tooth opposing dentition (1.70 vs 1.01 mm, p=0.017).

All-on-6 showed markedly fewer biological complications than all-on-4 (1.0% vs 10.3% incidence, p=0.014), suggesting a 6-implant configuration may better tolerate implant loss and reduce peri-implant disease risk over long-term follow-up, though prosthesis "success" (free of any modification) was low in both groups, reflecting the burden of long-term prosthetic maintenance.

## 세줄요약

단일기관 후향적 코호트 (n=28명 무치악 환자, 32개 보철물[all-on-4 17개/all-on-6 15개], 임플란트 164개) — 즉시부하·무피판·컴퓨터 가이드(NobelGuide) 전악 재건을 평균 6.46±2.236년(범위 1-10년) 추적.

누적 임플란트 생존율은 all-on-4 89.7% vs all-on-6 99.0% (실패 7개 vs 1개); 보철 성공률은 전체 51.5%로 낮음(all-on-4 58.8%/all-on-6 43.8%; 보철 생존율은 88.2%); 변연골소실(MBL)은 5년 1.38±1.28mm, 10년 2.09±0.56mm이며 all-on-4 vs all-on-6, tilted vs axial 간 유의차 없으나 반대측 자연치가 있으면 유의하게 더 큼(1.70 vs 1.01mm, p=0.017).

생물학적 합병증은 all-on-6에서 뚜렷이 적음(1.0% vs 10.3%, p=0.014) — 6본 식립이 장기적으로 임플란트 소실·주위염 위험을 낮출 가능성을 시사하나, 두 군 모두 무수정 보철 "성공률"은 낮아 장기 보철 유지관리 부담이 크다는 한계가 있음.

- **Journal**: Clinical Implant Dentistry and Related Research, 2022;24:831-844
- **DOI**: 10.1111/cid.13134
- **Institution**: Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Sciences, Sapienza University of Rome, Italy (Oral Surgery Unit, Policlinico Umberto I)
- **Registration**: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05307029; Institutional Board approval n.63/2021

## 2. Key Contributions

- Reports one of the longest follow-up periods (up to 10 years, mean 6.46 years) for computer-guided (NobelGuide), flapless, immediate-loading full-arch rehabilitation with fixed prostheses on 4 or 6 implants.
- Directly compares all-on-4 (2 axial + 2 tilted implants) vs all-on-6 (6 axial implants) cohorts within a single protocol/center, controlling for surgical technique.
- Provides a multiple backward stepwise linear regression identifying implant length, opposing natural dentition, buccal bone thickness, and crestal soft tissue thickness as significant predictors of 5-year marginal bone loss.
- Documents biological and prosthetic complication rates with detailed case-level narrative of implant failures and peri-implantitis management.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Single-center retrospective cohort, patients treated June 2009-December 2019, data collected in 2021.
- **Population**: 28 edentulous patients (20 female/8 male, mean age 67.75±8.627 years), 32 prostheses (17 all-on-4/15 all-on-6), 164 implants.
- **Inclusion**: edentulous or hopeless-teeth mandibles/maxillae, ASA-1/ASA-2 general health, ≥1-year functional loading, adherence to 6-month recall.
- **Exclusion**: compromised systemic conditions, head/neck irradiation or chemotherapy within 2 years, bisphosphonate therapy, metabolic bone disease, insufficient bone volume, mouth opening <50mm.
- **Protocol**: Double-scan CT/CBCT technique with radiopaque markers on a radiographic guide/denture, transferred to Procera NobelGuide software (Nobel Biocare) for virtual planning, stereolithographic surgical template fabrication, flapless implant placement, and prefabricated all-acrylic resin fixed complete prosthesis for immediate loading.
- **Groups**: Group 1 = 4 implants (2 axial + 2 tilted); Group 2 = 6 axial implants. Choice based on clinical/radiographic features (not randomized).
- **Outcomes measured**: implant survival, prosthesis success/survival, marginal bone loss (MBL), biological complications, prosthetic complications.
- **Statistics**: Kaplan-Meier survival curves, multiple backward stepwise linear regression for MBL predictors (p=0.05 to enter, p=0.10 to leave), adjustment for age, gender, jaw, implant location/angulation, MUA height.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Implant survival**: 89.7% all-on-4 (7 failures) vs 99.0% all-on-6 (1 failure) at mean 6.46-year follow-up.
- **Prosthesis success** (function without modification): 51.5% cumulative (58.8% all-on-4/43.8% all-on-6, p=0.358, NS).
- **Prosthesis survival**: 88.2% all-on-4 (affected by 7 implant failures in 2 prostheses); no failures in all-on-6 (p=0.231, NS).
- **Marginal bone loss**: 1.38±1.28 mm at 5 years, 2.09±0.56 mm at 10 years; no significant difference all-on-4 (1.56±1.61mm) vs all-on-6 (1.20±0.85mm) (p=0.104); no significant difference tilted (1.22±1.29mm) vs axial (1.44±1.27mm) (p=0.385) at 5 years.
- **Opposing dentition effect**: natural teeth opposing (1.70±1.73mm MBL) significantly greater than implant-supported opposing prosthesis (1.01±0.62mm) (p=0.017); no difference vs removable complete denture opposing (1.29±0.65mm, p=0.447).
- **Regression predictors of 5-year MBL**: implant length (β=-0.200, p=0.002, protective), opposing natural teeth (β=0.980, p<0.001, risk), buccal bone thickness (β=-0.383, p=0.070, trend protective), crestal soft tissue thickness (β=0.351, p=0.018, risk).
- **Biological complications**: 1.0% all-on-6 (1 mucositis) vs 10.3% all-on-4 (7 implants with peri-implantitis in 2 subjects), p=0.014 — significant.
- **Prosthetic complications**: 3 detachments, 10 chippings/fractures, 3 severe occlusal wears; no implant/prosthesis/framework fractures or abutment/screw loosening-fracture.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Retrospective, non-randomized design; group allocation (4 vs 6 implants) based on clinician judgment, introducing selection bias.
- Single center, single surgical protocol (NobelGuide), limiting generalizability to other guided-surgery systems.
- Relatively small sample (28 patients, 32 prostheses) with unequal group sizes.
- Prosthesis "success" defined strictly (no modification at all), producing low success rates that may not reflect clinically acceptable long-term function.
- No control group with conventional (non-guided, delayed-loading) protocol for direct comparison.

## 6. Related Work

- Compares directly to Lopes et al. (all-on-4/NobelGuide, 7-year follow-up: 94.5% implant/97.8% prosthesis survival) and other computer-guided flapless immediate-loading cohorts cited in Discussion.
- Complements [[wiki/implants/del-fabbro-2022-full-arch-tilted-axial-implants-sr-ma]] (SR+MA of tilted vs axial full-arch implants) — that meta-analysis found axial implants had significantly lower MBL than tilted; this study found no significant tilted-vs-axial MBL difference at the single-cohort level, illustrating cohort-level heterogeneity within the broader SR+MA signal.

## 7. Glossary

- **NobelGuide®**: Nobel Biocare's computer-guided implant surgery protocol using CT/CBCT-based virtual planning, stereolithographic surgical templates, and prefabricated immediate-loading prostheses.
- **MUA**: Multi-unit abutment.
- **MBL**: Marginal bone loss, measured radiographically at the implant-bone interface.
- **All-on-4**: Full-arch fixed prosthesis supported by 4 implants (typically 2 axial + 2 distally tilted).
- **All-on-6**: Full-arch fixed prosthesis supported by 6 implants (typically all axial).
- **Flapless surgery**: Implant placement without raising a mucoperiosteal flap, guided by a surgical template.
- **Double-scan technique**: CT/CBCT protocol scanning both patient (wearing radiographic guide) and the guide alone, matched via radiopaque markers, to derive prosthetically-driven implant planning.
