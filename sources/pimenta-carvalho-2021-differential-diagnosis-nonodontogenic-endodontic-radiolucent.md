## Why Ingested

기존 [[wiki/endodontics/diagnosis/karamifar-2020-endodontic-periapical-lesion-an-overview]]는 치근단병소를 이미 치성(endodontic) 기원으로 전제하고 육아종(granuloma) vs 낭종(cyst) 감별을 다루는데, 본 논문은 그 전제 자체 — "이 방사선투과상이 애초에 치성 병소가 맞는가" — 를 검증하는 한 단계 앞선 safety-net 감별진단 리뷰로, 임상적 오진 예방에 직접 관련된다.

## Three-line Summary

Critical review of PubMed case reports (1978–2020, n=426 screened → 111 relevant → 30 distinct nonendodontic entities) misdiagnosed, mimicked, or masqueraded as chronic apical periodontitis, periapical granuloma, radicular cyst, or dentoalveolar abscess.

Of the 30 nonendodontic entities, 83.3% mimicked chronic apical periodontitis and 16.7% mimicked dentoalveolar abscess; malignant tumors (primary + metastatic) accounted for 43.3% of entities; 68.4% of the nonendodontic lesions showed a negative pulp sensibility/vitality response, and swelling did not reliably discriminate endodontic from nonendodontic origin.

Clinical parameters (pain, swelling, pulp response) alone are insufficient to rule out nonendodontic pathology at a periapical radiolucency — a systematic differential-diagnosis workup (pulp vitality testing correlated with imaging and clinical course) should precede assuming an endodontic origin, especially before or during root canal treatment.

통증·부종·치수 반응 같은 임상 지표만으로는 근단 방사선투과상에서 비치성 병소를 배제하기 부족하며, 근관치료를 전제하기 전에 체계적 감별진단(치수생활력검사 + 영상 + 임상경과 상관분석)이 선행되어야 함 — 특히 근관치료 시작 전후 시점에서 중요.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Systematically catalogs 30 distinct nonendodontic ("nonodontogenic" in the title sense of not arising from pulp/periapical infection) pathological entities across five groups: primary malignant tumors, metastatic tumors, noninflammatory odontogenic lesions (cysts + tumors), nonodontogenic cysts, bone-related lesions, and anatomic defects — all reported in the literature as clinically/radiographically mimicking endodontic periapical lesions.
- Quantifies how often each clinical parameter (pain, swelling, pulp vitality) is present or absent across the 30 entities, showing none of them reliably discriminates endodontic from nonendodontic origin on its own.
- Highlights pulp sensibility/vitality testing as the single most useful (though imperfect) discriminator: a nonendodontic lesion overlying a tooth with a **positive** pulp response should raise strong suspicion of a nonendodontic process, since inflammatory endodontic lesions require pulp necrosis.
- Proposes a diagnostic flowchart (Figure 1) summarizing which nonendodontic entities present as apical-periodontitis-like vs abscess-like radiolucencies.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

**Search strategy**: PubMed (MEDLINE) search for case reports (1978–2020) using predefined terms — "Acute and Chronic Apical Periodontitis," "Acute Dentoalveolar Abscess," "Acute Apical Periodontitis," "Periapical Abscess," "Chronic Apical Periodontitis," "Periapical Granuloma," "Radicular Cyst," and "Chronic Dentoalveolar Abscess" — each combined with "Misdiagnosed," "Mimicking," "Masquerading," or "Simulating."

**Inclusion criteria**: well-documented case reports of nonendodontic pathological entities in the periapical location, published in English in dental/medical journals, with detailed symptoms, signs, imaging findings, and diagnosis.

**Exclusion criteria**: reviews, clinical trials, and case reports with incomplete clinical/radiographic/microscopic data. When multiple case reports described the same entity, only the most recent was retained for analysis.

**Appraisal process**: 426 articles were initially retrieved; 111 were judged relevant after applying inclusion criteria. Duplicate-entity reports were consolidated (n=81 excluded as redundant), leaving 30 distinct pathological entities, grouped by anatomical variation, nonodontogenic cysts, odontogenic lesions, bone-related lesions, and malignancies.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Overall distribution (n=30 entities)**:
- Malignant tumors: 43.3% (n=13) — 77% (n=10) primary tumors, 23% (n=3) metastatic tumors.
- Noninflammatory odontogenic lesions (cysts + tumors): 23.1% (n=7).
- Nonodontogenic cysts: 9.9% (n=3).
- Bone-related lesions: ~8% (n=2, cemento-osseous dysplasia and ossifying fibroma).
- Anatomic defects: 6.6% (n=2, maxillary sinus variation and Stafne bone cavity).
- Giant cell lesion, hamartoma/lymphangioma, pseudotumor: remaining entities.

**Mimicry pattern**: of the 30 entities, 83.3% (n=25) mimicked chronic/acute apical periodontitis, and 16.7% (n=5) mimicked dentoalveolar abscess.

**Clinical parameter breakdown (all 30 entities pooled)**:
- Pain present in 53.3% (n=16); absent in 46.7% (n=14).
- Swelling present in 56.6% (n=17); absent in 43.4% (n=13).
- Pulp vitality/sensibility testing (PVT): **positive** in 31.6% (n=6), **negative** in 68.4% (n=13, among those tested).

**Malignancy subgroup**: pain was reported in 84.6% (n=11/13) of malignancies — the highest pain prevalence of any subgroup — while swelling occurred in 61.5% (n=8/13). Malignant primary tumors had a mean age of 37.2 years (range 16–72); metastatic tumors had a mean age of 50.3 years (range 40–62), notably older, consistent with typical metastatic-disease demographics.

**Discriminating-feature analysis**: neither pain nor swelling reliably separated endodontic from nonendodontic lesions — both were common in malignancies and in true endodontic disease. The best (though still imperfect) discriminator was pulp sensibility/vitality: a **positive** pulp response accompanying a periapical radiolucency argues strongly against an inflammatory endodontic origin (since apical periodontitis/abscess require pulp necrosis), whereas a **negative** response does not rule out a nonendodontic lesion, since 68.4% of the nonendodontic entities also tested negative — likely from secondary pulp necrosis due to tumor/lesion invasion or unrelated coincidental pulp necrosis.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Evidence base is entirely case reports/case series (lowest evidence tier) — no cohort or prospective comparative data; publication bias likely over-represents dramatic/rare malignant presentations relative to true population prevalence.
- English-language-only inclusion criterion may have excluded relevant non-English case literature.
- "Most recent report retained" rule for duplicate entities discards earlier case detail and could bias which specific case-level features (e.g., a particular age or radiographic pattern) are represented per entity.
- Denominators for PVT vary (not all 30 cases had PVT reported/performed), so the 31.6%/68.4% split is out of a subset, not the full n=30 — the paper's own tables show "NI" (no information) entries.
- No proposed decision-tree validation cohort — the diagnostic flowchart is a synthesis heuristic, not a validated diagnostic instrument.

## 7. Glossary

- **PVT (Pulp Vitality/Sensibility Test)**: 치수 생활력/감각 검사 — cold, electric, or other stimulus testing of pulp responsiveness; a positive response indicates a clinically vital (non-necrotic) pulp.
- **Nonodontogenic / nonendodontic lesion**: a pathological entity in the jaw not arising from odontogenic/pulp-periapical infectious processes (e.g., malignancy, bone dysplasia, anatomic variant).
- **Dentoalveolar abscess**: acute or chronic purulent infection of endodontic origin at the periapex.
- **Apical periodontitis (acute/chronic)**: inflammatory periapical lesion secondary to pulp necrosis and root canal infection.
