## Why Ingested

This rapid review updates [[lamont-2018-routine-scale-and-polish-periodontal-health]] (Cochrane SR) with 2018–2023 evidence and incorporates the EFP S3 Clinical Practice Guidelines for supportive periodontal therapy (SPT). Commissioned by Health Canada to inform the Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP) rollout, it specifically addresses routine S&P effectiveness, optimal recall intervals, and equity gaps for underserved/low-income populations. It directly extends [[manresa-2018-supportive-periodontal-therapy-cochrane-sr]] by synthesizing Manresa's finding of no eligible RCTs for SPT intervals and placing it in the context of subsequent EFP guideline recommendations. Relevant to [[farooqi-2015-periodontal-maintenance-recall-interval-sr]] (reinforces recall interval evidence) and [[stahli-2024-clinical-evaluation-novel-protocol-supportive]] (reinforces SPT protocol evidence).

## Three-line Summary

A 2024 rapid review (8 studies, low risk of bias) commissioned for Health Canada found that routine dental scaling and polishing (S&P) provides no statistically or clinically significant benefit for gingivitis or pocket depth in low-risk regular dental attenders over 3 years, but observational data link regular scaling to reduced tooth loss (HR 1.87 without scaling) and lower AMI incidence (HR 0.543 with regular scaling). For adults with treated periodontitis, EFP S3 guidelines issue a Grade A strong recommendation for SPT at 3–12 month intervals tailored to risk profile, with high-risk patients requiring 3-monthly intervals and lower-risk patients 6–12 monthly; long-term SPT (≥5 years) reduces tooth loss and minimizes clinical attachment loss. No studies were found for adults with inequitable access to care.

## Key Contributions

1. **Updates Lamont 2018 Cochrane SR** with 2018–2023 evidence across two distinct adult populations
2. **Synthesizes EFP S3 Clinical Practice Guidelines** (Grade A recommendations) for SPT intervals and risk-based recall
3. **Identifies equity gap**: no studies exist for adults with inequitable access to dental care — a critical gap for Canadian Dental Care Plan policy
4. **Integrates cost-benefit evidence**: 6-monthly S&P with personalized OHI has largest incremental net benefit; SPT in diabetes patients reduces healthcare costs (€8.04/quarter)
5. **Provides risk-based recall framework**: high-risk 3-monthly, lower-risk 6–12 monthly based on smoking, HbA1c, PPD, BoP

## Methodology

- **Type**: Rapid review (not full systematic review); no meta-analysis performed; publication bias not assessed
- **Search period**: 2018–2023 (updating Lamont 2018 Cochrane SR)
- **Inclusion criteria**: Randomized controlled trials and systematic reviews on S&P/PMPR for adult populations
- **Included studies**: 8 studies (4 for all adults with no/early periodontitis; 4 for periodontitis SPT population)
- **Risk of bias**: All included studies rated low risk of bias
- **Populations examined**:
  1. All adults (no/early periodontitis), regular dental attenders
  2. Adults with history of treated periodontitis (SPT population)
  3. Adults with inequitable access — no studies found

## Key Results

### Population 1: All Adults (No/Early Periodontitis)

**Clarkson et al. 2021 (IQuaD trial)** — Multicenter cluster RCT, 63 UK general dental practices, 1,877 randomized, 1,327 analyzed, 3-year follow-up:
- Compared 6-monthly S&P, 12-monthly S&P, and no S&P
- **No statistically or clinically significant difference** between groups for gingival inflammation at any interval
- Patients in scaling groups reported higher self-perceived oral cleanliness

**Lamont 2018 Cochrane SR** (baseline evidence):
- S&P vs no S&P: little or no difference in gingivitis or probing pocket depth
- Small calculus reduction with S&P
- Patients valued regular scaling (OHRQoL component)

**Lee et al. 2019 (Korean cohort, n=557, 40–75y, 3-year follow-up)**:
- Regular scaling vs no scaling → tooth loss incidence HR **1.87** (95% CI 1.03–3.38) higher without scaling

**Kao et al. 2021 (Taiwan claims data, n=14,328, 50–64y, 13-year follow-up)**:
- AMI incidence: 3.5% (no scaling) vs 1.9% (regular scaling)
- HR = **0.543** (95% CI 0.441–0.670) for AMI with regular scaling
- Annual costs lower with regular scaling group

### Population 2: Adults with Treated Periodontitis (SPT)

**Manresa 2018 Cochrane SR**: No eligible RCTs found for SPT vs monitoring-only or different SPT intervals

**EFP S3 Clinical Practice Guidelines (Grade A — Strong Recommendation)**:
- Supragingival PMPR in Step 1 therapy for Stage I–IV periodontitis
- Regular SPT to reduce tooth loss long-term (≥5 years) and minimize CAL loss — evidence from SR of 17 prospective cohorts
- SPT intervals: 3–12 months, tailored to individual risk profile
- High-risk patients (smoking, elevated HbA1c, PPD ≥6mm, BoP >30%): **3-monthly** intervals
- Lower-risk patients: **6–12 monthly** intervals
- Repeated individualized oral hygiene instruction (OHI) as part of every SPT visit

**Smits et al. 2020 (Netherlands claims, n=937,704, 7-year follow-up)**:
- SPT in diabetes patients → **€8.04 reduction** in healthcare costs per quarter

### Population 3: Adults with Inequitable Access
- **No studies identified** — critical evidence gap for CDCP policy

### Cost-Benefit Analysis
- 6-monthly S&P with personalized OHI: largest incremental net benefit when health + non-health costs are included (WTP analysis)
- Cost savings from reduced tooth loss and cardiac events offset S&P costs over long term

## Limitations

- Rapid review methodology — not a full systematic review
- No meta-analysis performed
- Publication bias not assessed
- No studies identified for populations with inequitable access to care
- Observational studies (tooth loss, AMI) cannot establish causality

## Glossary

- **S&P**: Scaling and Polishing — supragingival calculus removal and tooth surface polishing
- **PMPR**: Professional Mechanical Plaque Removal — broader term encompassing all professional mechanical approaches to biofilm/calculus removal
- **SPT**: Supportive Periodontal Therapy — long-term maintenance for patients with treated periodontitis
- **IQuaD trial**: Improving Quality of Dental care — large UK multicenter RCT on S&P frequency
- **CDCP**: Canadian Dental Care Plan — federal dental coverage program for underserved Canadians
- **EFP**: European Federation of Periodontology
- **Grade A**: EFP evidence grade — strong recommendation based on high-quality evidence
- **WTP**: Willingness-to-pay — economic threshold used in cost-benefit analysis
- **OHRQoL**: Oral Health-Related Quality of Life
- **OHI**: Oral Hygiene Instruction
- **BoP**: Bleeding on Probing
- **PPD**: Probing Pocket Depth
- **CAL**: Clinical Attachment Loss
- **AMI**: Acute Myocardial Infarction
- **HR**: Hazard Ratio
