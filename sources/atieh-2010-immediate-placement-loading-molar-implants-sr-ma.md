## Three-line Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated (1) immediate placement of single implants in fresh molar extraction sockets (9 studies, 1,013 implants: survival 99.0%; no significant difference immediate vs delayed loading, RR 0.30, P=0.16) and (2) immediate restoration/loading of single molar implants in healed sites (7 studies, 188 implants: survival 97.9%; no significant difference, RR 3.0, P=0.33). Favorable marginal bone level changes in the immediate loading group were detected at 12 months (MD -0.31mm, 95%CI -0.53 to -0.096, P=0.005), suggesting a potential advantage beyond mere equivalence in survival.

## Overview

This systematic review with two separate meta-analyses assessed (1) immediate placement (Type 1) of single implants in fresh molar extraction sockets and (2) immediate restoration/loading of single molar implants in healed sites. Literature searched through November 2008 across major electronic databases including Cochrane Oral Health Group Trials Register; MA methodology per Academy of Osseointegration Workshop guidelines.

## Study Design

- **Review type**: Systematic review + two independent meta-analyses
- **Population**: Single implants in molar regions
- **MA-1 axis**: Immediate placement into fresh molar extraction sockets (placement timing)
- **MA-2 axis**: Immediate restoration/loading of implants in healed molar sites (loading timing)
- **Search cutoff**: November 1, 2008
- **Region focus**: Primarily mandibular molar region

## MA-1 — Immediate Placement in Fresh Molar Sockets

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Studies included | 9 |
| Total implants | 1,013 |
| Implant survival rate | 99.0% |
| Immediate vs. delayed loading RR | 0.30 (95% CI: 0.05–1.61) |
| P-value | 0.16 (not significant) |

**Conclusion**: No statistically significant difference in survival between immediate and delayed loading/restoration in freshly extracted molar sockets.

## MA-2 — Immediate Restoration/Loading in Healed Molar Sites

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Studies included | 7 |
| Total implants | 188 |
| Implant survival rate | 97.9% |
| Immediate vs. delayed loading RR | 3.0 (95% CI: 0.33–27.16) |
| P-value | 0.33 (not significant) |
| MBL at 12 months (immediate loading) | Mean diff −0.31 mm (95% CI: −0.53 to −0.096), P=0.005 |

**Conclusion**: No significant survival difference; however, immediate loading group showed statistically significantly favorable (less) marginal bone loss at 12 months vs. delayed loading.

## Key Findings

- Both immediate placement (99.0%) and immediate restoration/loading (97.9%) protocols show high survival in molar regions
- Neither placement timing nor loading timing significantly affected survival outcomes
- The MBL advantage at 12 months in the immediate loading cohort of healed-site molar implants (MA-2) is a notable secondary finding: mean −0.31 mm less bone loss vs. delayed loading
- Scope limited primarily to mandibular molar region — generalizability to maxillary molars is limited

## Clinical Relevance

Supports both immediate placement and immediate loading/restoration as viable protocols for molar implant replacement with acceptable survival rates. The MBL finding suggests possible biologic benefit of functional loading stimulus on peri-implant bone in the posterior mandible.
