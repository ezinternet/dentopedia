## Why Ingested

Our odontogenic-sinusitis pages describe the Caldwell–Luc procedure (CLP) mainly as a morbid, mucosa-stripping approach ([[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/kim-2019-definition-management-odontogenic-maxillary]]) and show one mucosa-preserving, inferior-meatal-window alternative only as a single case report ([[sinus-lift/lateral/ohyama-2022-novel-management-severe-odontogenic-maxillary]]). This two-arm trial (20 vs 20) adds comparative, same-setting data for a mucosa-preserving approach (inferior meatal antrostomy plus catheter saline irrigation) versus CLP after enucleation of a large odontogenic cyst that involves the sinus — a scenario not otherwise covered in the wiki.

## Three-line Summary

Self-described prospective randomized controlled trial (n=40; 20 sinus irrigation vs 20 Caldwell–Luc, Fujian, China, 2012–2018) of large odontogenic cysts (median diameter about 44 mm) involving the maxillary sinus with unilateral maxillary sinusitis, all treated by cyst enucleation.

Irrigation (100 mL saline twice daily via an epidural catheter through an inferior meatal antrostomy, 2 weeks) vs Caldwell–Luc: mean VAS pain on day 1 was 2.50 (1.43) vs 4.15 (2.78), fever above 38.0 °C on day 2 in 1/20 (5%) vs 8/20 (40%), and severe swelling in 2/20 (10%) vs 6/20 (30%); all P<0.05 per the authors. All patients were cured, CT showed the sinus cleared at 8–12 weeks in both arms, and no cyst recurrence was seen at 36 months.

Irrigation appears to cause less postoperative morbidity than Caldwell–Luc with equivalent cure, but the trial is small and single-center, does not describe randomization, allocation concealment or blinding, and reports no objective mucosal or mucociliary measurement.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Compares a mucosa-preserving approach (cyst enucleation, inferior meatal antrostomy, 2-week catheter saline irrigation) with the traditional Caldwell–Luc (CDL) procedure in large odontogenic cysts (OCs) that involve the maxillary sinus and are accompanied by unilateral maxillary sinusitis.
- Reports significantly less postoperative facial swelling, pain (VAS) and temperature elevation after irrigation, with all patients in both arms cured.
- Proposes irrigation as an alternative to standard CDL ("fewer complications, reduced trauma, restoration of the mucosa") — the "restoration of the mucosa" wording is a conclusion; no direct mucosal measurement is reported in the results.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: "prospective, randomized controlled design"; IRB approved (School and Hospital of Stomatology, Fujian Medical University); informed consent obtained. The text does not describe the randomization method, allocation concealment or blinding.
- **Participants**: 40 patients with large OCs involving the maxillary sinus, enrolled 2012-01-01 to 2018-01-31; randomized to group A (n=20, sinus irrigation) and group B (n=20, control, traditional CDL). Involved teeth were checked for vitality; non-vital teeth received endodontic treatment before surgery.
- **Inclusion criteria**: (1) preoperative clinical diagnosis of odontogenic cyst of the maxilla; (2) otolaryngologist-diagnosed unilateral maxillary sinusitis; (3) preoperative CT showing the cyst extending into the sinus with complete obliteration of the maxillary sinus; (4) cyst found adhered to the sinus mucosa during surgery; (5) histopathology confirming OC.
- **Surgery (both groups)**: intravenous-inhalation anesthesia; gingival-fold or intrasulcular incision with vertical releasing cuts; cyst with its non-separable adherent sinus membrane enucleated.
- **Group A**: sinus mucosa left in place as far as possible; after thorough enucleation and hemostasis, inferior meatal antrostomy (IMA) with an antrostomy knife at the thinnest bony portion of the cyst wall (per CT); an epidural catheter passed through the antrostomy into the cyst cavity and sutured near the base of the nose; irrigation with 100 mL normal saline via a 20-mL syringe through the catheter twice a day (head lowered, fluid exiting through the nose); catheter removed 2 weeks after surgery.
- **Group B**: standard CDL — diseased and inflamed sinus membranes enucleated and stripped; sinus cavity packed for hemostasis.
- **Common**: intraoral incision closed with absorbable sutures; cefazolin 2000 mg intravenously twice daily for 3 days; sinus precautions for 2 weeks.
- **Outcomes**: facial swelling (day 2), pain by VAS (day 1), temperature elevation (day 2), rhinorrhea/postnasal drip, nasal obstruction, paresthesia, CT clearance of chronic maxillary sinusitis, cyst recurrence; longest follow-up 36 months.
- **Protocol gaps (full-text re-read 2026-10-12)**: not stated — irrigation pressure, who irrigated and where (inpatient vs home), antibiotic after day 3, antrostomy closure management (82% of 367 antrostomies closed at 3 months is quoted from other literature), catheter-related problems. The catheter sits in the cyst cavity (cyst-surgery scenario). "Restoration of the mucosa" in the conclusion was not measured. Future plan: catheter via the natural ostium with FESS.
- **Statistics**: Wilcoxon rank sum test (swelling), t-test (VAS), chi-squared test (temperature); cyst diameter reported by quartile method because non-normal. Exact P values are not given (only P<0.05).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Randomization method, allocation concealment, blinding and sample-size justification are not described despite the "randomized controlled" label; no trial registration is mentioned in the supplied text.
- Small sample (20 per arm), single center, 6-year enrollment window, three different cyst types pooled (radicular cyst 23, dentigerous cyst 14, keratocyst 3 across both arms).
- Internal inconsistencies in the text: the VAS severity bands are 1–2 / 3–4 / 5–6 in the text but 1–3 / 4–6 / 7–10 in Tables 2–3 (the counts are identical); the swelling-grade definitions in the text differ from those in the tables; sex labels read "males" twice per group; the sentence "(Tables 1, 2)" for temperature points to the wrong tables. Counts were transcribed as given.
- Outcomes are early postoperative reactions plus CT clearance; "restoration of the mucosa" and "protect the function of the maxillary sinus" are asserted in the Discussion/Conclusion but no mucosal histology, endoscopy or mucociliary function measurement is reported. The pain, swelling and fever comparisons are the only tested between-group differences; no between-group test for CT clearance is given (both 100% cured).
- Group A carries a catheter for 2 weeks while Group B has sinus packing; antibiotic regimen was identical (cefazolin 3 days).
- The authors plan to use functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) to place the catheter through the natural ostium in future work.

## 6. Related Work

- Kim 2019 (narrative review; [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/kim-2019-definition-management-odontogenic-maxillary]]): lists CLP morbidity (postoperative maxillary cyst, paresthesia, sinus wall sclerosis); this trial reports higher swelling, pain and fever after CDL than after irrigation but no paresthesia in either arm.
- Ohyama 2022 (case report; [[sinus-lift/lateral/ohyama-2022-novel-management-severe-odontogenic-maxillary]]): mucosa-preserving management with a large inferior-meatus nasoantral window — the same principle as the inferior meatal antrostomy used here.
- Dumitrescu 2021 ([[endodontics/diagnosis/dumitrescu-2021-cbct-periapical-lesions-maxillary-sinus]]): periapical lesions (including cysts) and thickened maxillary sinus mucosa on CBCT.
- Overview: [[overviews/odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-overview]].
- Within the supplied text the authors cite: transnasal endoscopic cyst surgery is limited by narrow field and dead angles and cannot do root-end resection and retrograde filling; the inferior meatal antrostomy was reported closed in 82% of 367 cases at 3 months (their reference 14).

## 7. Glossary

- **Odontogenic cyst (OC)**: destructive jaw cyst arising from odontogenic epithelium; inflammatory (radicular cyst) or developmental (dentigerous cyst, keratocyst).
- **Caldwell–Luc procedure (CDL)**: traditional intraoral sinus approach in which diseased sinus membrane is stripped and the cavity packed.
- **Inferior meatal antrostomy (IMA)**: window made from the inferior nasal meatus into the maxillary sinus, here used to pass the irrigation catheter and allow drainage.
- **Chronic maxillary sinusitis (CMS)**: here, unilateral sinusitis accompanying the cyst, diagnosed by an otolaryngologist.
- **Visual Analog Scale (VAS)**: patient-rated pain scale.
