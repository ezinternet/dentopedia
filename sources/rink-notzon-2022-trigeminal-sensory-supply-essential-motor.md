## Why Ingested

기존 신경재생 페이지([[wiki/oral-surgery/zhang-2020-methylcobalamin-plcl-nerve-regeneration-conduit]], [[wiki/oral-surgery/lin-2007-ginkgo-biloba-nerve-regeneration-rat]])는 모두 도관·약리학적 lever(비타민B12, 은행잎 추출물)로 신경재생을 촉진하는 접근이다. 본 리뷰(Rink-Notzon 2022)는 정반대 lever — 삼차신경(V) 감각 입력 — 를 다룬다: 단순 수기자극(manual/vibrissal stimulation)만으로 안면신경(CN VII) 운동 재신경분포의 특이도와 휘스킹 기능회복이 개선됨을 보여, 비약리적·임상적용 가능한 보조요법 가설을 제시한다.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of the authors' own Wistar-rat facial nerve transection/anastomosis program (multiple experimental series, vibrissal whisking analysis + immunohistochemistry) testing whether trigeminal (V) sensory input affects facial (VII) motor recovery.

Excising ipsilateral infraorbital nerve worsened whisking recovery and abolished the benefit of manual stimulation; conversely, manual/vibrissal stimulation of intact trigeminal afferents restored synaptic density in the facial nucleus, reduced pathological polyinnervation (53%→22%), and produced near-normal whisking amplitude recovery.

Trigeminal sensory input is a necessary co-factor for good facial motor reinnervation — a rat-only finding whose translation to human facial-palsy rehabilitation (e.g., post-parotidectomy, Bell's palsy, iatrogenic facial nerve injury) remains a hypothesis, not clinical evidence.

## 세줄요약

저자들 자신의 위스타 랫트 안면신경 절단-봉합 프로그램(여러 실험 시리즈, 진동모(vibrissae) 휘스킹 분석 + 면역조직화학)을 정리한 내러티브 리뷰로, 삼차신경(V) 감각 입력이 안면신경(VII) 운동회복에 미치는 영향을 검증한다.

동측 안와하신경(Infraorbital Nerve, IOn) 절제는 휘스킹 회복을 악화시키고 수기자극(Manual Stimulation, Mstim)의 이득을 무효화한 반면, 온전한 삼차신경 구심로에 대한 수기·진동모 자극은 안면신경핵의 시냅스 밀도를 회복시키고 병적 다중신경지배(polyinnervation, 53%→22%)를 감소시키며 거의 정상에 가까운 휘스킹 진폭 회복을 이끌었다.

삼차신경 감각 입력은 양호한 안면 운동 재신경분포에 필요한 보조인자이나, 랫트 모델에 국한된 소견으로 인간 안면마비 재활(이하선절제술 후, 벨마비, 의인성 안면신경 손상 등)로의 임상적 전환은 아직 가설 단계이며 근거는 아니다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Synthesizes several of the authors' own rat facial-nerve-transection experimental series into a unified hypothesis: trigeminal (V) sensory afferents are necessary — not merely adjunctive — for accurate and functional facial motor (VII) reinnervation.
- Demonstrates that excising the sensory infraorbital nerve (IOn) abolishes or reverses the benefit of manual/vibrissal stimulation on whisking recovery, establishing a causal (not just correlative) role for trigeminal input.
- Links behavioral outcome (whisking amplitude), muscle-level histology (polyinnervation of motor endplates), and brainstem histology (synaptophysin-positive perisomatic synapses on facial motoneurons) into one coherent explanatory framework.
- Rules out muscle neurotrophic factors (FGF2, IGF1, NGF) as the mechanism for Mstim's benefit, narrowing the likely mechanism to synaptic/central rather than peripheral trophic effects.
- Offers direct clinical translation reasoning: massage-based sensory stimulation of the trigeminal-innervated skin/mucosa may substitute for elaborate physiotherapy techniques after combined facial/trigeminal nerve injury.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Multiple linked rat (Wistar) experimental series, each following broadly the same architecture: unilateral facial nerve (Fn) transection-and-suture (Fn-n), with or without concurrent infraorbital nerve (IOn) transection/excision (ipsilateral or contralateral) and/or a post-injury sensory-stimulation intervention (manual stimulation/massage of the whisker pad = Mstim; removal of contralateral vibrissal hairs to force ipsilateral use = Vstim).

Outcome measures across series:
- Video-based motion analysis of vibrissal whisking amplitude and angular velocity (functional recovery).
- Histology of the levator labii superioris (LLS) muscle — class III-tubulin/α-bungarotoxin staining to quantify polyneuronal innervation of motor endplates (normally monoinnervated; pathological reinnervation is polyneuronal).
- Retrograde tracer double-labeling (Fluoro-Gold, DiI, Fast Blue applied to distal nerve branches or target muscle) to map myotopic accuracy/specificity of motoneuron regeneration in the facial nucleus.
- Synaptophysin immunofluorescence with grayscale densitometric quantification of perisomatic synaptic coverage on retrogradely-labeled facial motoneurons (measure of synaptic stripping/restoration).
- Sandwich ELISA for muscle neurotrophic factor proteins (FGF2, IGF1, NGF) in LLS muscle.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Fn-n alone: whisking amplitude falls from ~62±13° (intact) to 19±6°; IOn ablation does not change amplitude further but eliminates the benefit of Mstim (Mstim + IOn-ipsi-ex amplitude 14±6°, worse than no treatment).
- Double lesion (Fn-n + IOn-n) with post-op stimulation (Vstim/Mstim/both): amplitude improves from 11±4° (no treatment) to 28-32° (~50% of intact, but significantly better than untreated).
- Buccal-nerve-only reinnervation accuracy (myotopic specificity by retrograde tracer): Bn-n alone or + ipsilateral IOn excision leaves ~48-56% correctly targeted motoneurons; contralateral IOn excision (forcing ipsilateral sensory-side use) raises correct targeting to 69% and reduces double-labeled (mistargeted) motoneurons to ~8%.
- Whisker-pad-muscle-specific retrograde double-labeling: only 27% of motoneurons reinnervating the whisker pad after Bn-n were from the original pool (73% "ectopic"); this rose to 41% with contralateral IOn excision (vs 32% with ipsilateral IOn excision, p=0.01).
- Mstim after Fn-n (single lesion, sensory intact) restored synaptophysin-positive synaptic pixel counts to near-intact levels (33×10^n vs 34.3×10^n intact, vs 29.2×10^n handling-only, p=0.007) and reduced polyinnervation from 53±10% to 22±5%, with near-complete restoration of whisking amplitude.
- FGF2/IGF1/NGF muscle protein levels did not differ between Mstim and handling-only groups at the 2-month endpoint — ruling out these three neurotrophic factors as the mechanism.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- All data are from Wistar rat facial/trigeminal nerve models (facial nerve transection ± infraorbital nerve manipulation); there is no human clinical trial data in this review. Translation of the "contralateral IOn excision improves accuracy" finding has literally no direct clinical application (the authors state this explicitly), and even the more translatable finding (Mstim benefit) is extrapolated from rat massage protocols, not tested in human facial-palsy patients within this paper.
- The mechanism by which Mstim improves target reinnervation and reduces polyinnervation remains only partially elucidated — synaptic counts on motoneuron somata were not restored by Vstim/Mstim in the double-lesion (Fn-n+IOn-n) experiments even though function improved, implying synaptic efficacy rather than synapse number, which was not directly measured.
- Neurotrophic factor analysis was a single 2-month endpoint; earlier time-course changes cannot be excluded.
- The review synthesizes results across several original studies (some using different rat strains/paradigms) rather than presenting a single unified confirmatory experiment; readers should treat it as a synthesis with associated citation-tracing burden for effect-size verification of individual claims.
- Authors explicitly note a prior paper replicated their manual-stimulation finding "without permission" and without citing their original work — a scientific-integrity aside, not a methodological limitation, but relevant to citation practice when using this literature.

## 6. Related Work

- Pavlov et al. — manual stimulation of denervated (not reinnervating) muscle worsened outcome, contrasted against this group's finding that Mstim after nerve repair (with intact sensory afferents) helps; the review interprets this contrast as evidence that stimulation timing/context (denervated vs reinnervating with intact afferents) determines benefit vs harm.
- Angelov et al. 1999 — source of the buccal-branch/infraorbital-nerve double-lesion figures reproduced in this review (myotopic organization disruption/restoration).
- English et al. — sex-specific exercise-training studies showing retention of excitatory glutamatergic synaptic inputs on axotomized lumbosacral motoneurons, cited as a parallel mechanism supporting the Mstim-preserves-synapses hypothesis.
- Studies on facial-nerve-crush functional recovery cited as replicating (without attribution) the group's original manual-stimulation findings.
- Human clinical rehabilitation literature on conditioning rehabilitation, EMG/neuromuscular re-education, and reduced synkinesis after facial nerve repair, cited as indirect clinical corroboration.

## 7. Glossary

- **Facial nerve (Fn, CN VII)** — motor cranial nerve innervating mimetic (facial expression) muscles; in the rat model, innervates the whisker-pad (vibrissal) musculature.
- **Trigeminal nerve (V)** — sensory (and masticatory motor) cranial nerve; its second branch (maxillary) gives rise to the infraorbital nerve, which supplies sensory innervation to the vibrissal whisker pad.
- **Infraorbital nerve (IOn)** — end branch of the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve; provides the sole sensory supply to the rat whisker pad (vibrissal hairs, muscles, fur).
- **Axotomy** — surgical/traumatic transection of an axon, here of facial or infraorbital nerve fibers.
- **Motoneuron / facial motoneuron** — the axotomized cell body in the facial nucleus (brainstem) whose axon is cut and must regrow to reinnervate a target muscle.
- **Polyinnervation (polyneuronal innervation)** — pathological reinnervation state where a single motor endplate receives input from more than one axon/axonal branch (normal adult state is mono-innervation).
- **Synaptic stripping** — post-axotomy detachment/loss of presynaptic terminals from the motoneuron soma and dendrites, quantifiable via synaptophysin immunostaining.
- **Whisking amplitude** — the angular range of rhythmic vibrissal (whisker) protraction-retraction movement, the primary functional-recovery readout in this rat model.
- **Vstim / Mstim** — vibrissal stimulation (forced use via contralateral whisker removal) / manual stimulation (massage) of the whisker pad, the two sensory-stimulation interventions tested.
- **Myotopic organization** — the normally precise spatial mapping within the facial nucleus of motoneuron cell bodies to the specific peripheral nerve branch/muscle they innervate; disrupted after nerve transection-regeneration ("misdirected reinnervation").
