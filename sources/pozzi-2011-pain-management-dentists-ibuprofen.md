## Why Ingested

Comprehensive pharmacology review of ibuprofen in dentistry — covers COX-1/COX-2 mechanism, postoperative pain across dental disciplines (extraction, implant, periodontal, orthodontic, bleaching), safety profile, and drug interactions. Provides foundational analgesic knowledge that supports evidence-based pain management decisions across the wiki. Reinforces analgesic protocols referenced in [[drug/analgesics/bailey-2013-ibuprofen-paracetamol-wisdom-teeth-cochrane]].

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of ibuprofen pharmacology and clinical applications in dentistry. Ibuprofen is a nonselective COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor with analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and antipyretic properties; it is the most widely studied NSAID in postoperative dental pain. The review covers dosing, safety, drug interactions, and specific dental indications including third molar surgery, implant placement, periodontal treatment, orthodontic pain, and tooth whitening sensitivity.

## 세줄요약

치과에서의 이부프로펜(Ibuprofen) 약리학 및 임상 적용을 종합한 리뷰. 이부프로펜은 비selective 사이클로옥시제나제-1/2 (COX-1/COX-2) 억제제로, 진통·소염·해열 효과가 있으며, 수술 후 치과 통증에서 가장 널리 연구된 비스테로이드성 항염증제 (NSAID)다. 제3대구치 (third molar) 수술, 임플란트 식립, 치주 처치, 교정 통증, 치아 미백 민감도에 대한 용량·안전성·약물상호작용을 다룬다.

## Key Contributions

- Comprehensive overview of ibuprofen pharmacology including S(+)/R(-) enantiomer metabolism, COX-1/COX-2 dual inhibition mechanism, and FAAH inhibition leading to endocannabinoid modulation
- Systematic review of ibuprofen efficacy across dental disciplines: third molar extraction, implant surgery, periodontal scaling/root planing, orthodontic separator placement, and bleaching-induced sensitivity
- Dosing guidance: 200-400 mg for mild-moderate dental pain; 400-800 mg for moderate-severe; max 3200 mg/day prescription
- Safety profile comparison: lower GI/cardiovascular risk than COX-2 selective inhibitors; drug interactions with aspirin, ACE inhibitors, lithium, methotrexate, warfarin
- Preemptive analgesia evidence: preoperative ibuprofen reduces postoperative pain and analgesic consumption

## Methodology

- Design: Narrative review
- Scope: English and Italian literature on ibuprofen in dentistry
- Search: Not specified (narrative)
- No meta-analysis performed

## Key Results

- Ibuprofen 400 mg provides T½ of 2.1 hours; peak plasma in ~2 hours (tablets), <15 min (granules)
- Postoperative dental pain: ibuprofen 400 mg superior to acetaminophen 1000 mg in multiple RCTs
- Preemptive dosing: 400-600 mg given before surgery reduces postoperative pain scores and rescue medication use
- 50-60% of R(-) ibuprofen converted to active S(+) form in gut/liver
- Ibuprofen inhibits FAAH → increases anandamide levels → synergistic analgesic effect via CB1/CB2 receptors
- GI tolerability: better than aspirin; comparable to other propionic acid derivatives

## Limitations

- Narrative review (not systematic); no explicit search strategy or inclusion criteria
- Published 2011 — does not include newer NSAID evidence or formulations
- Primary focus on Western populations/dosing; limited mention of pharmacogenomic variability

## Glossary

- **NSAID (비스테로이드성 항염증제)**: Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug; class including ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac
- **COX (사이클로옥시제나제)**: Cyclooxygenase enzyme; COX-1 constitutive (GI/platelet), COX-2 inducible (inflammation)
- **FAAH (지방산아마이드가수분해효소)**: Fatty acid amide hydrolase; degrades endocannabinoids; inhibited by ibuprofen
- **TOTPAR (전체통증완화총점)**: Total pain relief; standard outcome in analgesic RCTs
- **Preemptive analgesia (선제적 진통)**: Analgesic given before painful stimulus to prevent central sensitization
