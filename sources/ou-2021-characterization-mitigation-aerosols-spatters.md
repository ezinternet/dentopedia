## Why Ingested

Ingested as part of the 2026-07-20 치위생사 스케일링 술기 (dental hygienist scaling technique) topic sweep. Provides device-level, size-resolved capture-efficiency numbers for aerosol-mitigation devices during ultrasonic scaling, giving quantitative engineering grounding to the qualitative device synthesis in [[wiki/dental-handpiece/ghoneim-2024-aerosol-generating-procedures-control-mitigation]] (extends: introduces the extraoral local extractor (ELE) as a hands-free device outperforming HVE, outside that paper's 2012–2022 review window).

## Three-line Summary

Bench simulation study using a dental manikin and two in-situ optical methods (digital inline holography, DIH; laser sheet imaging, LSI) to characterize droplet size/velocity from simulated ultrasonic supragingival scaling and to evaluate the capture efficiency of aerosol-mitigation devices.

More than 99% of droplets measured 12–200 μm and over 65% traveled at less than 1 m/s; a saliva ejector (SE) reduced overall aerosol/spatter signal by 63%, a high-volume evacuator (HVE) by 88%, and an extraoral local extractor (ELE) by up to 96% at its high-flow setting with the nozzle 14 cm from the manikin's mouth.

A properly positioned, hands-free ELE outperformed HVE and SE without requiring a chairside assistant, but capture efficiency fell sharply as nozzle distance increased (ELE high-flow: 96%→56% when moved from 14 cm to 18 cm), and the manikin-only bench design cannot itself confirm reduced infectious risk.

## 세줄요약

치과 마네킹과 두 가지 실시간 광학기법 — 디지털 인라인 홀로그래피(Digital Inline Holography, DIH), 레이저 시트 이미징(Laser Sheet Imaging, LSI) — 을 이용해 모의 초음파 치은연상 스케일링에서 발생하는 물방울 크기·속도를 특성화하고 에어로졸(Aerosol) 저감 장치의 포집효율을 평가한 실험실 벤치 연구.

물방울의 99% 이상이 12–200 μm 범위였고 65% 이상이 속도 1 m/s 미만이었으며; 타액흡입기(Saliva Ejector, SE)는 전체 신호를 63%, 고용량흡인(High-Volume Evacuation, HVE)은 88%, 구강외 국소흡인장치(Extraoral Local Extractor, ELE)는 노즐을 마네킹 입에서 14 cm에 두고 고유량으로 가동했을 때 최대 96%까지 저감시켰다.

손이 자유로운 ELE를 적절히 배치하면 보조인력 없이도 HVE·SE보다 우수한 저감 효과를 냈지만, 노즐 거리가 멀어지면(14cm→18cm) 포집효율이 급격히 떨어졌고(ELE 고유량 96%→56%), 마네킹만을 이용한 벤치 연구이므로 실제 감염 위험 감소까지 입증한 것은 아니다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First reported characterization of **both** droplet size *and* velocity for ultrasonic-scaler-generated aerosols/spatter using in-situ optical methods, rather than surface-collection (fluorescent/chromatic indicator or microbiological culture) methods, which the authors argue undersample small aerosols (< ~50 μm).
- Combines a near-field method (DIH, size/speed at the source) with a far-field method (LSI, broader-field plume/spatter visualization) as complementary characterization and mitigation-evaluation tools.
- First quantitative capture-efficiency evaluation of an extraoral local extractor (ELE) for ultrasonic scaling, including the effect of flow setting and nozzle-to-mouth distance — a device the authors state had no published effectiveness evidence before this study.
- Demonstrates that ultrasonic-scaler droplets travel at low average velocity (>65% < 1 m/s), supporting point-of-source suction strategies over whole-room ventilation for this specific AGP.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Laboratory bench simulation (not a clinical/patient study). A dental manikin with thermoplastic teeth was positioned supine to mimic a patient.
- **Procedure simulated**: A dental hygienist performed simulated supragingival scaling of the facial surfaces of the maxillary central incisors (teeth #8, #9) and the lingual surfaces of the mandibular central incisors (teeth #24, #25) — stated by the authors to be the regions generating the most spatter/aerosol clinically — using an ultrasonic scaler (Cavitron Plus with 30K FSI-1000 inserts; Dentsply Sirona) at medium power, with a reported water flow of 36 (SD 2) mL/min [source text renders the unit as "mm/min," almost certainly a plain-text extraction artifact for mL/min]. 3 trials × 10-second videos per site.
- **DIH (digital inline holography)**: Near-field setup (laser + digital camera + beam-expanding/collimating/condensing optics); sample volume 15.6 × 13.0 × 250 mm, centered ~8 mm above the manikin's oral cavity; 35 frames/s, 13-μs exposure. Hologram processing followed the hybrid method of Shao et al. (image enhancement → digital reconstruction → particle segmentation → postprocessing) to obtain particle size distributions.
- **LSI (laser sheet imaging)**: Far-field setup (high-resolution camera + image intensifier + imaging lens + light-sheet generator), light sheet positioned just above the oral cavity/below the nose, camera 60 cm away with a 40 × 40 cm field of view. Images were converted to gray scale, background-subtracted, and segmented by an entropy filter; total scattered-light pixel intensity (vs. a no-mitigation control trial) yielded each device's capture efficiency.
- **Mitigation devices tested**: saliva ejector (SE, hygienist-operated); high-volume evacuator (HVE, second-person-operated); extraoral local extractor (ELE; DentalPro Aerosol UVC, BOFA International) at two flow settings — approximately 100 m³/h (59 ft³/min, "low") and 220 m³/h (129 ft³/min, "high") [source text strips the cubic-unit superscript, rendering as "mm/h"/"m/h"] — and two nozzle distances (14 cm and 18 cm from the manikin's mouth). SE, HVE, ELE, and combinations (ELE+SE, ELE+HVE) were compared against a no-mitigation control.
- **Supplementary verification**: An aerodynamic particle sizer (TSI 3321) sampled residual (dried) particles 0.7–3 μm at the DIH measurement location to cross-check small-particle capture.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Droplet size (DIH)**: > 99% of droplets fell within 12–200 μm; spatter > 200 μm was rare and depended heavily on scaler-tip position/movement. Number-mode size was 55 μm (facial surfaces, teeth #8/#9) vs. 34 μm (lingual surfaces, teeth #24/#25). Total droplet concentration: 114 particles/cm³ (teeth #8/#9) vs. 42 particles/cm³ (teeth #24/#25) — the lower lingual-site concentration attributed to the more confined intraoral space.
- **Droplet velocity (DIH)**: > 65% of droplets < 1 m/s; < 2% > 3 m/s. No dominant direction of travel was found (omnidirectional, consistent with high-frequency scaler-tip oscillation).
- **Size-dependent capture efficiency (DIH)**: All devices except SE captured > 95% of droplets up to 90 μm; efficiency fell to ~80% at 120 μm and ~50% at 150 μm. Efficiency at > 180 μm could not be quantified reliably owing to irregular large-spatter generation.
- **Overall capture efficiency (LSI)**: SE 63% (lowest); ELE low-flow (14 cm) 74%; HVE 88%; ELE high-flow (14 cm) 96% (best single device). Combining ELE-high-flow with SE (94%) or HVE (95%) gave no meaningful additional benefit over ELE alone.
- **ELE positioning effect**: Moving the ELE nozzle from 14 cm to 18 cm dropped capture efficiency from 74%→38% (low-flow) and from 96%→56% (high-flow).
- **Small-particle cross-check**: > 95% capture measured by the aerodynamic particle sizer for residual (post-evaporation) particles 0.7–3 μm, consistent with the DIH-based size-dependent capture curve.
- **Noise**: Ambient noise (measured 10 cm above the manikin's chest) rose from 63.2 dB (baseline) to 76.9 dB (scaler on) to 80.3 dB (scaler + ELE high-flow). At the manikin's ear (nearer the ELE), noise rose from 81.1 dB (scaler only) to 86.8 dB (scaler + ELE high-flow).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Bench/manikin simulation only — no live patients, no microbiological or pathogen-load sampling; the authors explicitly frame their emission profiles as a "worst-case scenario" requiring future biological risk assessment, since SARS-CoV-2 infective-dose knowledge was limited at the time.
- Only two intraoral sites (facial maxillary and lingual mandibular incisors) and one scaler/insert/power/water-flow setting were tested by a single hygienist; generalization to other teeth, scaler units, or operators is untested.
- DIH's near-field FOV could not statistically characterize droplets/spatter > 180 μm owing to their irregular, infrequent occurrence.
- Authors flag water-cooled high-speed handpieces and combined point-of-source + HVAC mitigation as needed future investigations.
- ELE's higher airflow produced measurably higher ambient/near-patient noise than SE or HVE, a practical trade-off not otherwise quantified against comfort or communication.

## 6. Related Work

- Positions itself against prior aerosol/spatter studies that used surface-collection methods (fluorescent or nonfluorescent chromatic indicators, microbiological culture media), which the authors argue undersample aerosols smaller than ~50 μm.
- Cites evidence that SARS-CoV-2-relevant particles can be as small as 0.06–0.14 μm, motivating a full-spectrum (not just large-droplet) characterization approach.
- Notes HVE is already reported elsewhere to reduce aerosol contamination considerably, but that ELEs were marketed during COVID-19 without published effectiveness or design/configuration evidence prior to this study.
- Adopts the hybrid hologram-processing method of Shao and colleagues for DIH particle-size-distribution extraction.

## 7. Glossary

- **AGP (Aerosol-Generating Procedure)**: A dental/medical procedure (e.g., ultrasonic scaling, high-speed handpiece use) that produces airborne droplets/aerosols capable of carrying pathogens.
- **DIH (Digital Inline Holography)**: Near-field optical imaging technique used here to measure the size and speed of individual particles close to the aerosol source.
- **LSI (Laser Sheet Imaging)**: Far-field optical imaging technique used here to visualize and quantify the broader spatial spread/concentration of an aerosol plume via scattered light.
- **SE (Saliva Ejector)**: A low-volume, hygienist-operated intraoral suction device.
- **HVE (High-Volume Evacuator)**: A high-flow intraoral suction device typically requiring a second operator (assistant) to hold and position.
- **ELE (Extraoral Local Extractor)**: An extraoral, fixed-position, high-flow suction/filtration device positioned near (but outside) the patient's mouth; can be operated hands-free relative to the treating hygienist.
- **Capture efficiency**: The percentage reduction in measured aerosol/spatter signal (droplet count by size, or total scattered-light intensity) when a mitigation device is used, relative to a no-mitigation control trial.
