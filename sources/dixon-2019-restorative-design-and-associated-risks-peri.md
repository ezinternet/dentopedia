## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Periodontology 2000, 2019; 81(1):167–178) from the University of Washington on surgical and restorative design — emergence profile, implant design/position, and implant–abutment interface manipulation — as factors associated with peri-implant inflammatory disease.

The review states that peri-mucositis and peri-implantitis prevalence may reach 40%–65% and 20%–47%, respectively, and frames etiology as multifactorial, with prosthetic/positional parameters modifying the host response alongside the inflammatory nature of disease.

Surgical and restorative design is presented as a modifiable target for lowering disease prevalence; this is an abstract-only ingest (no PMC record or open-access copy), so no design-specific thresholds or effect sizes are available here.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Positions inflammatory peri-implant conditions as highly prevalent (up to 40%–65% mucositis, 20%–47% peri-implantitis) and multifactorial in etiology.
- Adds three design-domain considerations beyond classic risk factors: initial surgical implant placement position, disruption of the biologic interface by implant–abutment interface manipulation, and prosthetic design.
- Reviews the concepts of the **natural and prosthetic emergence profile** together with implant design and position, alongside other restorative concepts tied to implant complications and disease.
- Emphasizes the **temporal nature** of disease progression around implants as a reason to account for these parameters in surgical–restorative planning.
- Frames the surgeon–restorative team's design decisions as a route to reducing the prevalence of both peri-mucositis and peri-implantitis.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Study type: narrative review (PubMed `Review` publication type); no patient cohort, no sample size reported in the abstract.
- Scope: Surgical and restorative design variables related to peri-implant mucositis/peri-implantitis, including emergence-profile concepts, implant design/position, and the implant–abutment interface.
- Search strategy, inclusion criteria, number of included studies, and any quantitative synthesis are **not stated in the abstract** and cannot be verified from an abstract-only artifact.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Peri-mucositis prevalence "may be as high as 40%–65%" and peri-implantitis "20%–47%" (authors' summary of current analyses; reported verbatim as ranges, not as this review's own measured rates).
- Direction of effect is qualitative: implant position, implant–abutment interface manipulation, and prosthetic design "may also influence" the host response and the diagnosis of inflammatory states.
- No comparative groups, effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values, or design thresholds (e.g., emergence-angle cut-offs) are present in the retrieved abstract.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only:** Full text was not available (no PMC record; closed access), so all content is limited to the PubMed abstract — no details on search method, study selection, or the review's specific design recommendations.
- Prevalence figures are presented as "may be as high as" and are definitions- and population-sensitive; they should not be read as pooled estimates from this review.
- The paper is a narrative review, not primary data or a systematic review/MA — it supplies a conceptual frame, not effect estimates.
- Later quantitative work (Atieh 2023 SR+MA, Monje 2025 SR, Pirc 2026 narrative review with cases) is where testable design thresholds and risk magnitudes appear; this 2019 review supplies the framing.

## 7. Glossary

- **Peri-implant mucositis (임플란트주위점막염):** Inflammatory condition of the peri-implant soft tissue without bone loss.
- **Peri-implantitis (임플란트주위염):** Inflammatory condition around an implant with progressive crestal bone loss.
- **Emergence profile (출현윤곽, EP):** Contour of the restoration/abutment as it emerges through the peri-implant soft tissue.
- **Implant–abutment interface (임플란트-지대주 연결부, IAI):** Junction between fixture and abutment; manipulation is proposed here to disrupt the biologic interface.
- **Biologic interface (생물학적 계면):** Soft-tissue–implant interface whose disruption is linked to inflammatory states.
