## Why Ingested

Systematic review of fiber-reinforced composite (FRC) as core material for post-obturation restoration of endodontically treated teeth — directly addresses material selection and fracture-resistance optimization for [[post-and-core|endodontic restorations]]. Establishes comparative efficacy of different fiber types (polyethylene vs. glass fiber) and design factors (retention slots, placement location).

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 25 in vitro studies evaluating fiber-reinforced composite (FRC) as core material for post-endodontic restoration, comparing against other core materials and intact teeth.

FRC core materials increase fracture resistance of endodontically treated teeth; polyethylene and short fiber-reinforced composites show greater resistance than glass FRC; fracture resistance further increases with retention slots and occlusal-third placement.

FRC does not achieve fracture resistance equivalent to intact teeth; most fractures are favorable (enamel-dentin level) and repairable rather than catastrophic.

## 세줄요약

근관충전 후 코어 재료로서의 섬유보강 복합레진(FRC)의 파절저항성을 평가한 25개 논문의 체계적 문헌고찰.

FRC 코어는 근관치료 치아의 파절저항성을 증가시키며, 폴리에틸렌 및 단섬유 복합레진이 유리섬유 FRC보다 우수; 유지홈 및 교합면 상부 배치로 저항성 추가 증가.

FRC는 건전 치아 수준의 파절저항성에 도달하지 못하나, 대부분의 파절은 호의적(미관상 회복 가능)이며 재앙적 파절이 드문 편.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Systematic review establishing FRC as effective core material for increasing fracture resistance of endodontically treated teeth compared to conventional materials (amalgam, composite without reinforcement)
- Evidence that polyethylene and short fiber-reinforced composites provide superior fracture resistance compared to glass fiber-reinforced composites
- Identification of design optimization factors: retention slots and placement on occlusal-third surfaces significantly increase fracture resistance
- Classification and analysis of fracture modes (favorable vs. catastrophic) — predominantly favorable fractures at enamel-dentin junction supporting clinical repairability
- Characterization of material-specific properties: polyethylene fibers modify stress distribution via monoblock effect; glass fibers provide aesthetic advantage but lower mechanical performance

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Systematic review without meta-analysis
- **Databases**: PubMed, EBSCO Host, Scopus, Google Scholar, Hinari, plus manual library search
- **Search Period**: 1 January 2000 – 30 November 2019
- **Inclusion Criteria**: In vitro studies on human extracted teeth evaluating FRC as core material for fracture resistance using universal testing machine
- **Exclusion Criteria**: Review articles, abstracts, case reports, letters to editor, in vivo studies, studies published before 2000
- **n**: 157 articles identified; 25 studies included in final analysis (after title screening, duplicate removal, abstract screening, and full-text assessment)
- **Study Design Covered**: Primarily in vitro mechanical testing studies; experimental designs using extracted human teeth

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Total studies reviewed**: 157 identified → 55 after title review → 27 after duplicate removal → 26 full texts assessed → 25 included
- **Primary finding**: FRC as core material increases fracture resistance of endodontically treated teeth, but does not match intact tooth fracture resistance
- **Fiber type comparison**: Polyethylene fiber-reinforced and short fiber-reinforced composites demonstrated greater fracture resistance than glass FRC
- **Design optimization**: Fracture resistance increased when FRC combined with retention slots and placement on occlusal-third surfaces of cavities
- **Fracture mode**: Favorable fractures predominated (at enamel-dentin level and adhesive fractures), indicating repairable rather than catastrophic failure patterns
- **Material limitations**: FRC core materials did not achieve fracture resistance equivalent to intact teeth even with optimization

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Limited to in vitro studies; no clinical long-term follow-up data on survival, complication rates, or patient outcomes
- Heterogeneous study designs and testing methodologies preventing formal meta-analysis
- Restricted search period (2000–2019); updates needed for recent materials and fiber technologies (e.g., Ever X Posterior introduced after cutoff)
- Anatomical variables across studies (single-rooted vs. multi-rooted, dentin thickness, cavity preparation designs) not controlled for meta-analysis
- Limited mechanistic data on stress distribution and fiber-matrix interactions
- No standardized testing protocols across included studies limiting direct comparisons
- Need for in vivo prospective cohort studies validating in vitro findings in clinical practice

## 6. Related Work

## 7. Glossary

- **FRC (Fiber-Reinforced Composite)**: Composite resin reinforced with different fiber types (polyethylene, glass, short fiber) used as core material to increase fracture resistance
- **Polyethylene Fibers**: Unidirectional or multidirectional fibers providing monoblock effect and stress distribution along tooth axis
- **Glass Fibers**: Bidirectional fibers providing reinforcement and aesthetic advantage but lower mechanical performance than polyethylene fibers
- **Short Fiber-Reinforced Composite**: FRC containing discontinuous short fibers (e.g., Ever X Posterior) for increased load-bearing capacity and crack prevention
- **Monoblock Effect**: Mechanical adhesion creating unified structure that dissipates stress along long axis of tooth
- **Post-obturation Restoration**: Final restoration placed after root canal obturation, consisting of core build-up and crown/coverage restoration
- **Fracture Resistance**: Mechanical property measuring tooth's ability to withstand occlusal forces without fracture; tested to failure in universal testing machine
- **Favorable Fracture**: Repairable fracture occurring at or coronal to enamel-dentin junction; does not require extraction
- **Retention Slots**: Grooves or slots placed in cavity walls to provide mechanical retention for core material and increase overall fracture resistance
- **Ever X Posterior**: Commercial FRC product with multidirectional discontinuous fibers; dentin-substitute material for endodontic core restoration
