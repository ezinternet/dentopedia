## Why Ingested

The wiki holds measurement-side pages for single posterior implant restorations — [[gozen-2025-occlusal-force-distribution-digital-analysis-implant-in-vivo]] (occlusal force distribution) and [[mao-2024-occlusal-changes-implant-supported-single-crowns]] (occlusal contact drift over 12 months) — and masticatory-performance evidence only for *removable* prostheses and shortened dental arches ([[liang-2015-effects-removable-dental-prostheses-masticatory]]). This paper fills the missing functional-outcome slot: a 12-month head-to-head of single-piece implant crown vs three-unit metal-ceramic FPD, measured with the almond-comminution (sieving) method.

## Three-line Summary

Prospective, non-randomized parallel-group observational study (consecutive allocation, n=60, 30/30; single centre in India, March 2021–December 2023) comparing masticatory performance (MP%, almond comminution + four-sieve dry sieving) of single-piece titanium implant crowns vs conventional three-unit metal-ceramic FPDs for one posterior tooth, at 3, 6 and 12 months post-loading.

MP% was significantly higher for implant crowns at every timepoint: 28.4 ± 4.2 vs 22.6 ± 3.8 at 3 months, 32.1 ± 3.7 vs 25.4 ± 3.9 at 6 months, 35.7 ± 3.9 vs 28.3 ± 4.1 at 12 months (all t-test p = 0.001); both groups improved over time (repeated-measures ANOVA F = 25.71 implants, F = 15.73 FPDs, p = 0.001 each).

The authors conclude single-piece implant crowns give better and faster functional adaptation, but the design is observational with consecutive (not randomized) allocation, so the ~6 MP%-point gap cannot be read as a causal treatment effect.

MP%는 모든 시점에서 임플란트군이 유의하게 높았다 — 3개월 28.4 ± 4.2 vs 22.6 ± 3.8, 6개월 32.1 ± 3.7 vs 25.4 ± 3.9, 12개월 35.7 ± 3.9 vs 28.3 ± 4.1 (모두 p = 0.001); 양군 모두 시간에 따라 유의하게 개선 (반복측정 분산분석 (Repeated-measures ANOVA) F = 25.71 vs 15.73, p = 0.001).

저자들은 단일체 임플란트 크라운이 더 우수하고 더 빠른 기능 적응을 보인다고 결론내렸으나, 연속 배정(무작위화 없음) 관찰연구이므로 약 6 MP%-point 차이를 치료 효과로 단정할 수 없다.

- **Journal**: Cureus 2026;18(1):e101229
- **DOI**: 10.7759/cureus.101229 (PMID 41669575; PMCID PMC12884674; CC BY 4.0)
- **Institution**: Department of Prosthodontics, Crown and Bridge — study site stated as RKDF Dental College and Research Centre, Bhopal, India (first author affiliated with Bharati Vidyapeeth Dental College, Sangli, India)
- **Study period**: March 2021 – December 2023 (recruitment, treatment, 12-month follow-up)

## 2. Key Contributions

- First head-to-head clinical comparison of **single-piece (one-piece) implant crowns** vs conventional **three-unit metal-ceramic FPDs** for a single posterior tooth, using a natural test food (almond) rather than artificial chewing material.
- Reports a full three-point functional trajectory (3/6/12 months post-loading), not a single endpoint — allowing the "implant adaptation is faster" claim to be tested rather than asserted.
- Quantifies both an intergroup gap (absolute MP% difference of 5.6–7.4 points, i.e. ~25–26% relative superiority as stated in the discussion) and intragroup time effects (ANOVA F 25.71 vs 15.73).
- Provides an Indian single-centre dataset for the single-tooth replacement decision, where most comparative functional data come from two-piece implants or multi-unit restorations.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Prospective, **parallel-group observational** study — *not* randomized. "Eligible participants were consecutively allocated" to Group 1 or Group 2 (n = 30 each). No randomization sequence, allocation concealment, or ITT analysis is described.
- **Blinding**: participants and operators could not be blinded (distinct treatments); a single trained outcome assessor was blinded to group assignment and performed all functional assessments. Examiner calibration reported qualitatively ("intraclass correlation values above the acceptable limits") without numeric ICC.
- **Population**: 60 partially edentulous adults, 25–60 years, needing one posterior tooth (premolar or first molar); inclusion required ≥10 mm bone height and ≥6 mm width on CBCT, healthy FPD abutments, no bruxism; exclusions included HbA1c >7%, >10 cigarettes/day, head-and-neck radiotherapy, TMD.
- **Sample size**: G*Power 3.1.9.2, effect size 0.64 borrowed from Kumar et al., 30 per group, 80% power, α = 0.05.
- **Interventions**:
  - Group 1 — Equinox Myriad One-Piece Implant System (Equinox Medical Technologies, Amersfoort, Netherlands), diameter 3.75–4.2 mm, length 10–12 mm, described as placed "following a two-stage surgical protocol" with healing caps and three months of osseointegration, then cemented monolithic zirconia crowns on the integrated abutment.
  - Group 2 — three-unit metal-ceramic FPD (Wiron 99 base alloy, Bego; VMK Master porcelain, VITA), polyvinyl siloxane impressions (Aquasil Ultra), glass-ionomer cementation (GC FujiCEM 2).
- **Masticatory efficiency test (almond comminution + dry sieving)**:
  - Five whole almonds, 20 mandibular strokes at a comfortable pace, stroke count verified by the examiner.
  - Mouth rinsed with 50 mL water, rinse added to the collected fragments; sample poured onto a stainless-steel sieve (≈175 × 78 × 40 mm) with filter paper, then 500 mL water passed through to remove saliva and prevent agglomeration.
  - Dried at 130 °C for 40 min; separated on a four-sieve stack (4.0, 2.8, 2.0, 1.0 mm; Granutest/Telastem, ABNT-approved) on a gypsum vibrator for 60 s; each fraction weighed on a precision balance.
  - MP = fraction passing the 2.8 mm sieve and smaller (P1) relative to total dry mass (Pt), computed with the Kapur & Soman formula. Higher MP% = more effective comminution.
- **Timepoints**: 3, 6 and 12 months post-prosthetic loading. (A baseline MP% appears in Table 1; see § 5 for the internal inconsistency with the stated lack of baseline assessment.)
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk for normality; independent-samples t-tests for intergroup MP% at each timepoint; repeated-measures ANOVA + Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise tests for within-group change; χ²/t for baseline comparability; α = 0.05; SPSS 23.0.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Baseline comparability (Table 1): age 44.7 ± 8.3 vs 45.1 ± 7.9 y (p = 0.82); male 17 (56%) vs 16 (53%) (p = 0.79); premolar 12 (40%) vs 11 (37%) (p = 0.77); edentulism duration 6.8 ± 2.4 vs 6.5 ± 2.1 months (p = 0.63); "baseline MP%" 21.8 ± 4.1 vs 21.3 ± 3.8 (p = 0.64). All 60 patients completed the study.

Intergroup MP% (independent-samples t-test):

| Timepoint | Single-piece implant (n=30) | Conventional FPD (n=30) | t | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 months | 28.4 ± 4.2 | 22.6 ± 3.8 | 5.60 | 0.001 |
| 6 months | 32.1 ± 3.7 | 25.4 ± 3.9 | 6.82 | 0.001 |
| 12 months | 35.7 ± 3.9 | 28.3 ± 4.1 | 7.16 | 0.001 |

Intragroup change (repeated-measures ANOVA, Table 3): implants F = 25.71, p = 0.001; FPDs F = 15.73, p = 0.001.

Pairwise Bonferroni comparisons (Table 4): implants 3→6 mo t = −4.92, 3→12 mo t = −5.40, 6→12 mo t = −8.16; FPDs 3→6 mo t = −3.88, 3→12 mo t = −3.74, 6→12 mo t = −6.28 — all p = 0.001 (negative t = improvement).

Derived observations (not stated as such by the authors): the absolute gap widened from 5.8 points at 3 months to 7.4 points at 12 months; and the FPD group's 12-month mean (28.3%) sits only marginally above the implant group's 3-month mean (28.4%) — i.e. the FPD cohort needed 12 months to reach the functional level the implant cohort showed at 3 months.

No implant survival/failure, marginal bone level, probing depth, restoration complication, bite-force or patient-reported outcome data are reported in the Results, although the Introduction and Methods both name maximum bite force as a co-objective.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Design (honest statement)**: this is a **prospective observational parallel-group study with consecutive allocation, not a randomized controlled trial**. There is no randomization, no allocation concealment, no neutral/no-treatment control, and no mention of confounder adjustment beyond baseline table comparison. Treatment choice was effectively systematic, so indication/selection bias cannot be excluded — the group difference cannot be attributed to the restoration type with RCT-level confidence.
- **Cureus review model**: peer reviewed but rapid-review; the manuscript passed Cureus's streamlined review, which does not substitute for the CONSORT/STROBE-level methodological scrutiny a specialty journal would apply. Several reporting gaps are consistent with this (see below).
- **Internal inconsistencies in the report**: (a) the implants are described as *single-piece* yet placed "following a two-stage surgical protocol" with healing caps, which contradicts the one-piece/one-stage rationale argued in the Introduction; (b) the stated limitation "did not include … baseline assessment of masticatory efficiency prior to prosthetic rehabilitation" contradicts Table 1, which reports a baseline MP%; (c) maximum bite force is announced as an objective but never reported.
- **Missing standard clinical outcomes**: no implant survival, marginal bone loss, peri-implant health, prosthetic complications (chipping/decementation), or FPD abutment outcomes. A functional superiority claim without survival/biological outcomes is one-sided — [[mao-2024-occlusal-changes-implant-supported-single-crowns]] and the occlusal-overload literature show the implant side is not risk-free.
- **Statistical reporting**: between-group tests were run separately at three timepoints without multiplicity control (only the *within*-group pairwise tests got Bonferroni); exact p-values are truncated to 0.001 (no CIs, no effect sizes with CIs); no mixed/repeated-measures model with a group × time interaction term is reported, so "the implant group improved more" rests on comparing two separate F-values rather than a formal interaction test.
- **Measurement**: single test food (almonds), single 20-stroke regimen, single examiner, one sieving run per timepoint — no within-subject repeatability data are given (ICC cited without a number), and MP% is a ratio measure sensitive to drying/sieving protocol.
- **Generalizability**: single centre, Indian population, 30 per arm, 12-month horizon, one specific one-piece implant system and one FPD material combination; results do not extend to two-piece implants, cantilever/reshaped-arch designs, or long-term (>5 y) behaviour. Absolute MP% at 12 months (35.7%) remains far below reported natural-dentition values.
- **Authors' own note**: the Discussion itself records conflicting literature (Kumar et al.: no significant MP difference between single-tooth implant and FPD; Altayyar et al.: similar), and the Conclusion asks for randomized multicentre trials with baseline assessments and longer follow-up before definitive recommendations — the paper does **not** claim to settle the question.

## 6. Related Work

- **Kumar et al.** (cited) — no statistically significant MP/efficiency difference between single teeth replaced by implants vs FPDs; directly conflicts with the present result and is the single most important counterweight to this paper's conclusion.
- **Altayyar et al.** (cited) — similar null finding for Kennedy Class I/II fixed-implant vs conventional removable partial prostheses.
- **Fueki et al.** (cited SR) — implant overdentures improve comminution in edentulous resorbed ridges; supports the rigid-osseointegration mechanism but in a different prosthetic class.
- **Montoya-Carralero et al.** (cited) — two-colour gum test: reduced hue variance and better perceived chewing with implant prostheses.
- **Fontijn-Tekamp et al.** (cited) — 1-year longitudinal masticatory gains in implant overdenture wearers (time-dependent cortical adaptation), the reference frame for the 3/6/12-month trajectory here.
- **Zhang et al.** (cited) — relative occlusal force of implant crowns in light-occlusion cohort inferior to normal occlusion; relevant to how occlusal scheme modulates the functional result.
- Key mechanism the authors rely on: implants bypass the periodontal ligament (PDL), so protective proprioceptive reflexes that limit force on tooth-supported prostheses are absent.

## 7. Glossary

- **Masticatory performance (MP%)**: the fraction of a standardized test food reduced below a threshold particle size after a fixed number of chewing strokes — here the proportion of dried almond mass passing a 2.8 mm sieve, per the Kapur & Soman formula.
- **Comminution test**: a chewing-efficiency assay using a natural or artificial test food, followed by sieving and weighing of particle-size fractions (distinct from two-colour chewing-gum mixing-ability tests and from gummy-jelly methods).
- **Single-piece (one-piece) implant**: an implant whose fixture and transgingival abutment are a single manufactured unit, eliminating the implant–abutment microgap; the restoration is cemented directly onto the integrated abutment.
- **Fixed partial denture (FPD)**: a tooth-supported multi-unit fixed prosthesis; here a three-unit metal-ceramic bridge replacing one posterior tooth, requiring preparation of both adjacent teeth.
- **Repeated-measures ANOVA with Bonferroni post-hoc**: a within-subject test of change across ≥3 timepoints, with a family-wise error correction applied to the pairwise comparisons.
