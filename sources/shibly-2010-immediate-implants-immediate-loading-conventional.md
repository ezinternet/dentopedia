## Why Ingested

This RCT evaluates loading-protocol outcomes in immediately-placed implants, comparing immediate vs conventional loading at 1-year follow-up—a foundational benchmark for loading-protocol classification. Extends [[immediate-implant/loading-protocol/wittneben-2023-immediate-implant-loaded-single-crown-sr]] by providing an independent clinical trial of immediate loading in a periodontally compromised cohort.

## Three-line Summary

Randomized clinical trial (n=60 patients) comparing immediate vs conventional loading of dental implants placed immediately after extraction in patients with periodontal disease history.

Immediate loading (group A): 96.6% survival, 0.99 ± 0.22 mm bone gain; conventional loading (group B): 93.3% survival, 0.75 ± 0.17 mm bone gain; differences not statistically significant (p > 0.5).

Both immediate and delayed loading protocols showed similar outcomes for osseointegration and stability; however, conventional loading resulted in greater mucogingival junction displacement (65% vs 15% coronally displaced sites).

## 2. Key Contributions
- Randomized clinical trial of loading-timing protocols in immediately-placed implants within a periodontally compromised patient population
- Demonstrates equivalence of immediate vs conventional loading for osseointegration and radiographic bone stability at 1-year follow-up
- Highlights soft-tissue management differential: transmucosal (immediate loading) vs submerged with primary closure (conventional loading) impact on mucogingival junction position

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Randomized controlled trial (RCT)
- **Population**: 60 patients with history of periodontal disease seeking immediate implant treatment to replace a hopeless tooth
- **Intervention Groups**:
  - Group A (immediate loading): Immediate implants with provisional crown
  - Group B (conventional loading): Immediate implants with cover screw and delayed loading after 3 months
- **Common to Both Groups**: Mucoperiosteal flap, atraumatic extraction, immediate implant placement, allograft bone, collagen barrier membrane
- **Follow-up**: 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively
- **Primary Outcomes**: Implant survival rate, radiographic bone level change
- **Secondary Outcomes**: Mucogingival junction (MGJ) displacement, soft-tissue position

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Immediate Loading (Group A) | Conventional Loading (Group B) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-year implant survival rate | 96.6% (29/30) | 93.3% (28/30) | ns |
| Bone level change (mm ± SD) | 0.99 ± 0.22 | 0.75 ± 0.17 | > 0.5 (ns) |
| MGJ coronally displaced at 1-year | 15% (4/26 sites) | 65% (17/26 sites) | significant |

**Key Finding**: Both loading protocols achieved high survival rates with stable radiographic bone levels; however, the transmucosal approach (immediate loading) resulted in greater MGJ coronal displacement prevention compared to the submerged protocol with primary closure.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Relatively small sample size (n=60) limited to single-tooth extraction sites
- Short follow-up period (1 year); long-term bone and soft-tissue stability unknown beyond this timeframe
- Periondontally compromised population; findings may not generalize to periodontally healthy patients
- Study conducted in the early 2000s; newer implant designs and biomaterials not evaluated
- Lack of prospective soft-tissue esthetic measures beyond MGJ position (Pink Esthetic Score/White Esthetic Score not reported)

## 7. Glossary
- **Immediate Implant (Type 1)**: Dental implant placement into an extraction socket at the time of tooth extraction
- **Immediate Loading**: Placement of an occlusal/functional restoration (provisional crown) on an implant at the time of implant placement or within 24 hours
- **Conventional/Delayed Loading**: Placement of restoration after a healing period, typically 3–6 months (submerged healing) or 2–4 months (transmucosal)
- **Mucogingival Junction (MGJ)**: The boundary between the free and attached gingiva; used as a landmark for soft-tissue position measurement
- **ISQ (Implant Stability Quotient)**: Resonance frequency analysis (RFA) measure of implant stability; not explicitly measured in this trial but related to primary stability assessment
- **Osseointegration**: Direct bone–implant contact without intervening fibrous tissue; assessed radiographically via bone level stability and survival rate
