## Why Ingested

Existing MTLD-accuracy work in this wiki measures a wrench once on a test bench, so it cannot say whether a torque-limiting device (TLD) stays trustworthy over a service life. This paper is the first in the folder to track each manufacturer's spring-style mechanical torque-limiting device (MTLD) across 40 cycles of mechanical loading plus steam sterilization, and it qualifies the blanket "all wrenches miss the set value by >10%" claim by showing one manufacturer (Astra Tech) sitting well inside the ±10% tolerance band (median 3.33%) while two others (Implance 11.43%, DIO 16.67%) sit outside it — refining [[wiki/prosthetic-materials/abutment-screw/marenzi-2026-torque-limiting-devices-accuracy-manufacturers]].

## Three-line Summary
In vitro bench study: 9 spring-style mechanical torque-limiting devices (3 each from Implance, Astra Tech, DIO), each mounted on its own manufacturer's implant in a resin block and cycled 40 times through mechanical loading (10 reps per target torque) plus 134 °C steam sterilization, measured with a calibrated digital torque tester (±0.2% FS) by a single investigator.
Torque accuracy was strongly manufacturer-dependent and drifted with service life: overall median percentage deviation 3.33% (Astra Tech), 11.43% (Implance), 16.67% (DIO), with measurements falling outside the ±10% tolerance band in 5%, 60%, and 95% of readings respectively (χ²=99.241, p<0.001); experimental cycle affected torque difference in all three brands (p=0.004 / <0.001 / 0.007) with a significant cycle × manufacturer interaction (p<0.001).
Clinically, spring-style design alone does not guarantee tolerance — the specific MTLD–implant-system combination must be verified at the exact torque used for both placement (35 Ncm) and abutment screw tightening (25 Ncm) — but the ±10% exceedance here is a property of brand and service history, not of spring-style geometry per se; limits are 3 devices per manufacturer, one operator, and mechanical loading confounded with sterilization.

## 2. Key Contributions
- **Lifecycle dimension, previously missing**: the only in-folder study that follows the *same* physical MTLD through 40 repeated cycles of mechanical loading **and** steam sterilization, isolating cycles 0/10/20/30/40 as a priori analysis points — prior accuracy work (Marenzi 2026; McCracken 2010; L'Homme-Langlois 2015) measured new or single-use wrenches.
- **Manufacturer identity, not design class, drives accuracy**: spring-style construction alone does not confer tolerance. Astra Tech stayed inside ±10% on 95% of readings (median 3.33%) while DIO missed on 95% (median 16.67%) and Implance on 60% (median 11.43%), under one identical protocol.
- **System-specific, not one-size-fits-all, verification**: torque difference differed significantly between target settings for Astra Tech (p<0.001) and DIO (p<0.001) but not Implance (p=0.088) — accuracy at one clinical torque value does not predict accuracy at another.
- **Negative deformation finding**: the pre-defined exclusion criterion (indicator failing to return to its zero reference, i.e. plastic deformation/permanent set) was **never triggered** in any device at any measurement point, arguing against spring take-up as the mechanism of drift over 40 cycles.
- **Unexplained direction reversal**: Astra Tech's torque difference *progressively decreased* with cycling (median 2.5 → 1.0 Ncm from cycle 0 to 40, ηp²=0.634) — the opposite of wear-induced drift — which the authors flag as unexplained and needing material analysis.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: In vitro bench study, repeated-measures (within-device) design; not randomized and not blinded.
- **Devices (n=9)**: 3 spring-style MTLDs each from Implance (AGS Medikal, İstanbul, Türkiye), Astra Tech Implant System (Dentsply Sirona Implants, Mölndal, Sweden), and DIO Implant (DIO Corporation, Busan, South Korea). Each device given a unique ID.
- **Fixture**: manufacturer-specific implants inserted into CAD/CAM-fabricated resin blocks at simulated crestal bone level (Implance 4.3 × 10 mm; Astra Tech 4.2 × 11 mm; DIO 4.0 × 10 mm); each MTLD used with its own manufacturer-supplied mounting component (mount driver/adapter).
- **Target torques**: 25 and 35 Ncm for Implance and Astra Tech; 20 and 35 Ncm for DIO (25 Ncm absent from DIO's scale, so nearest marked value used). 35 Ncm = clinically relevant implant-placement torque; 25 Ncm = prosthetic abutment screw tightening.
- **Cycle protocol (×40)**: baseline measurement → mechanical fatigue (10 consecutive loading repetitions at *each* target torque = 20 reps/cycle) → post-fatigue measurement → steam sterilization (Kronos B23, Newmed; 134 °C, 2.1 bar, 5 min). Cycle 0 = first assessment straight from packaging, before any autoclave step. Three measurements per stage, mean used.
- **Measurement**: calibrated digital torque tester (Mark-10 Cap Torque Tester, model MTT01-100, USA; accuracy ±0.2% of full scale), peak mode, zeroed before each set. Torque applied horizontally in a clockwise direction with the distal phalanx of the index finger at a pre-defined grip point until the indicator rod aligned with the target mark; scale read at 90° to minimize parallax. A standardized zero mark was created by one operator for MTLDs lacking a factory-etched zero (visual guide only — no functional component modified). All measurements by a single investigator.
- **Outcomes**: (a) torque difference = |T_measured − T_target| (Ncm), used within manufacturer; (b) percentage deviation = |T_measured − T_target| / T_target × 100 (%), used between manufacturers; (c) exceedance of the ±10% tolerance threshold (counted as measurements, n=60 per manufacturer).
- **Sample size**: G*Power 3.1.9.2; large standardized effect f=0.40 (Cohen, no preliminary data available); 3 groups, 10 repeated measurements (5 cycle levels × 2 time points), r=0.50, α=0.05, 80% power → 9 devices = 3 per manufacturer. (The PDF prints "α = 0.05%", an evident typo for α=0.05.)
- **Statistics**: R v4.4.1; normality by Shapiro-Wilk (not met); **aligned rank transform (ART) mixed ANOVA** in ARTool with device ID as a random grouping factor — fixed effects cycle (0/10/20/30/40), stage (baseline vs post-fatigue), and target torque within each manufacturer; manufacturer × cycle model for percentage deviation. Post hoc via estimated marginal means (emmeans) with **Holm** correction; effect sizes as partial η². Reliability by ICC(3,1) (two-way mixed, consistency, single-measure). Manufacturer × ±10% exceedance by Pearson χ² followed by pairwise column-proportion Z tests with Bonferroni correction. Values reported as median (min–max); p<0.05 significant.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **No device met the exclusion criterion** — no indicator failed to return to its zero reference at any point across 40 cycles, so no evidence of plastic deformation or permanent set.
- **Median percentage deviation from target torque (overall, all cycles/stages/settings)**: Astra Tech **3.33%**, Implance **11.43%**, DIO **16.67%**.
- **±10% tolerance exceedance** (n=60 measurements per manufacturer; χ² = 99.241, p<0.001; all pairwise manufacturer differences significant): Astra Tech **5%** (3/60), Implance **60%** (36/60), DIO **95%** (57/60); pooled 96/180 = 53.3%.
- **ART mixed ANOVA, within-manufacturer torque difference**: cycle significant in all three — Implance F=4.697, p=0.004, ηp²=0.320; Astra Tech F=17.344, p<0.001, ηp²=0.634; DIO F=4.155, p=0.007, ηp²=0.294. **Measurement stage (baseline vs post-fatigue) was never significant** (p=0.311 / 0.059 / 0.243), i.e. the drift came from the cycle as a whole rather than the fatigue bout alone. Target torque value significant for Astra Tech (F=13.459, p<0.001, ηp²=0.252) and DIO (F=316.580, p<0.001, ηp²=0.888), both with larger deviation at 35 Ncm; not significant for Implance (p=0.088). No 2- or 3-way interactions within manufacturers (all p>0.05).
- **Median torque difference (Ncm) across cycles 0/10/20/30/40**: Implance 3.08 / 3.33 / 3.83 / 2.92 (cycle 30 highest; letters a-ab-b-a, only 30 differs from 10 and 40); Astra Tech 1.17 / 0.92 / 1.00 / 0.67 (cycle 0 highest, monotonic improvement; a-b-cd-bc-d); DIO 5.09 / 4.34 / 4.84 / 4.34 (a-ab-a-b). Per-setting medians at 35 Ncm were far worse than at the lower setting for DIO (median difference 6–7 Ncm vs 2.7–4 Ncm at 20 Ncm).
- **ART mixed ANOVA, percentage deviation**: cycle p<0.001, manufacturer p<0.001, stage p=0.081 (n.s.), **cycle × manufacturer interaction p<0.001** — the drift pattern was not the same across brands. Remaining interactions non-significant (cycle × stage p=0.878, stage × manufacturer p=0.943, three-way p=0.870). Holm-adjusted pairwise cycle differences at 0 vs 20, 0 vs 40, 10 vs 40, 30 vs 40; Astra Tech lowest and DIO highest, with all three manufacturers differing pairwise.
- **Reliability ICC(3,1)**: Implance 0.993 (0.989–0.996), Astra Tech 0.992 (0.987–0.995), DIO 0.998 (0.997–0.999), all p<0.001 — excellent, so the between-manufacturer spread is real device behaviour, not measurement noise.
- **Manufacturer documentation context**: DIO specifies prosthetic tightening of 20 Ncm (N platform) and 30–35 Ncm (R/W platforms) with 35 Ncm listed separately for fixture placement — the deviation seen at 35 Ncm argues for verifying even "official" settings. For Implance, no clearly component-specific torque recommendations are publicly documented, limiting interpretation of its deviation.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Only 3 independent MTLDs per manufacturer — enough for within-device repeated measures, but manufacturer-level findings need confirmation with more devices and additional implant systems.
- All torque applications by a single investigator: improves standardization but limits generalizability to other operators.
- Mechanical loading and steam sterilization were bundled into one cycle, so their individual effects on accuracy cannot be separated.
- No material, microscopic, or deformation analysis — the mechanism or onset of spring behaviour change (including the unexplained Astra Tech improvement) could not be determined.
- The in vitro model cannot reproduce restricted access, angulation, humidity, temperature, biological contamination, or patient-specific bone; clinical failure and biological outcomes were not evaluated.
- Authors call for manufacturers to publish calibration intervals and expected accuracy limits for use/sterilization cycle counts, to make observed deviations interpretable at the chairside.

## 6. Related Work
- **Marenzi et al. 2026** (doi 10.1186/s12903-026-07852-3): 3 manufacturers' MTLDs, 15/20/25 Ncm, UTM, single session — found universal under-delivery of 11–29.3%. This paper's ±10% exceedance rates qualify that magnitude claim (Astra Tech 5% exceedance) while adding the service-life dimension Marenzi never measured; no brand overlap, so the two coexist rather than compete.
- **Fayaz et al. 2014** (Dental Res J 11(1):74–80, cited here as ref 11): friction-style MTLDs stayed within ±10% after the first sterilization cycle, then became more variable. Those devices and this protocol differ in design class and in whether mechanical fatigue was included, so the divergence maps onto device-specific rather than universal behaviour.
- **Mahshid et al. 2012 / 2013**: pre-sterilization spring-style MTLDs remained within ±10% of target; the present study is the longer-horizon counterpart (40 cycles with loading).
- **Cehreli et al. 2004**: slight torque-output decrease with clinical use of manual torque devices — directionally consistent with the Implance/DIO drift.
- **McCracken et al. 2010**: no significant mean difference between spring-style and toggle-style devices but greater variability in toggle-style, with unknown cycle counts.
- **Shiba et al. 2021**: viewing angle affects torque accuracy and repeatability of beam-type implant torque wrenches — supports the 90° read-through protocol used here.
- **Vallée et al. 2008**: MTLD accuracy differs by device design and manufacturer — the finding this study extends across a cycling protocol.

## 7. Glossary
- **MTLD (Mechanical Torque-Limiting Device)**: a hand instrument that mechanically caps the torque applied to an implant fixture or prosthetic screw at a dialed value, preventing over-tightening.
- **Spring-style / beam-type MTLD**: design in which a spring-steel beam deflects against a graduated scale, so one instrument covers a range of torques; contrasted with friction-style/toggle (click) wrenches.
- **Torque difference**: |T_measured − T_target| in Ncm — the absolute miss against the dialed value.
- **Percentage deviation (PerDev)**: |T_measured − T_target| / T_target × 100 — scale-free, used for cross-manufacturer comparison.
- **±10% tolerance band**: the de facto acceptability threshold used across the MTLD accuracy literature; measurements outside it are counted as "exceedance".
- **Insertion torque value (ITV)**: torque at implant placement, used as an objective marker of primary stability and of immediate-loading suitability.
- **Cycle (experimental cycle)**: one full loop of baseline measurement → mechanical fatigue loading → post-fatigue measurement → steam sterilization, repeated 40 times.
- **ART (aligned rank transform) ANOVA**: nonparametric ANOVA for mixed/repeated designs, combining rank-based alignment with classical F-tests.
- **ICC(3,1)**: two-way mixed-effects, consistency, single-measurement intraclass correlation — repeatability across repeated measurements on the same device.
- **Partial η²**: proportion of variance in the outcome explained by the given factor.
- **Torque preload**: clamping force generated in the implant–abutment screw joint by applied tightening torque; too little promotes loosening and fracture, too much deforms the screw or threads.
