## Why Ingested
Top-tier longevity synthesis for the GIC cluster; supports [[glass-ionomer/panetta-2024-gic-longevity-umbrella-review]] and grounds the GIC overview's evidence-quality caveats.

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42022320602) of 13 systematic reviews (from 132 initial references across MedLine/PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus) evaluating GIC longevity as a definitive restorative material in primary and permanent teeth, follow-up 6 months–6 years.

None of the 13 SRs met all AMSTAR-2 criteria or used an a priori design; ROBIS rated 7/13 at low risk of bias and 1 at very high risk; the pooled GRADE assessment was Class II recommendation / Level B evidence.

GIC, RMGIC, and compomer show broadly comparable clinical performance to composite/amalgam with material- and technique-dependent exceptions (e.g., silver-reinforced GIC underperforms, compomer often outperforms conventional GIC), but the weak methodological quality of the underlying SR pool precludes a definitive recommendation.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First umbrella review approaching GIC exclusively (both primary and permanent teeth), per the authors.
- Umbrella-level GIC longevity synthesis across dentitions, GIC subtypes (conventional, RMGIC, HVGIC, compomer, silver- and zirconia-reinforced), and delivery technique (conventional vs. ART).
- Quantifies weak underlying SR quality with dual tools (AMSTAR-2 methodological quality, ROBIS risk of bias) plus a pooled GRADE recommendation/evidence-level rating.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
Umbrella review (systematic review of systematic reviews), PROSPERO CRD42022320602, reported per PRIO. Two independent reviewers (AP, ACVMM) for screening/extraction/risk-of-bias; a third reviewer (TFN) resolved conflicts.

PICO(S): P — primary and permanent teeth with restorations; I — GIC restorations; C — comparison between different GIC types, composite, and amalgam; O — restoration longevity; S — systematic review of clinical trials.

Databases: MedLine/PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, searched 28 April 2022, no language or date restriction; reference lists of eligible studies also hand-searched.

Eligibility: inclusion required (1) systematic review of clinical trials, (2) evaluation of clinical longevity of GIC in primary/permanent teeth as a restorative material. Excluded: studies on root caries or non-carious cervical lesions, critical/narrative reviews, letters, and guidelines.

Quality/bias tools: AMSTAR-2 (methodological quality) and ROBIS (risk of bias), both independently rated by ACVMM and AP. GRADE used to assign an overall level of evidence (A/B/C) and class of recommendation (I/IIa/IIb/III) to the body of evidence.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
Search yielded 132 references (37 MedLine/PubMed, 51 Scopus, 44 Web of Science); 41 duplicates removed; 67 excluded on title/abstract; 24 assessed in full text; 11 further excluded; **13 SRs included**.

Only 6/13 stated a PICO question and 7/13 reported protocol registration; 2/13 did not report a search strategy; all 13 stated inclusion/exclusion criteria; 4/13 included non-English-language results. Follow-up across the 13 SRs ranged 6 months–6 years.

AMSTAR-2: none of the 13 SRs satisfied all criteria and none used an a priori design; most defined a search strategy and 8/13 excluded grey literature; 4 SRs did not use a quality-analysis tool (negative rating); 2 SRs used no risk-of-bias tool; 2 SRs (Amorim et al.; Mickenautsch et al.) disclosed a conflict of interest (related to GIC-technique teaching); 3 SRs did not control for publication bias; 4 SRs did not document included-study scientific quality.

ROBIS: 2/13 very low risk of bias; 3/13 positive on all criteria except one unclear domain; 5/13 positive on 3/5 domains (rated low risk); 1/13 (Ruengrungsom et al.) high risk of bias; 1/13 very high risk of bias (negative on all domains) — netting **7/13 SRs at low risk of bias overall**.

GRADE (pooled across the evidence base): degree of recommendation **Class II** (conflicting evidence on GIC clinical performance/longevity vs. comparators); level of evidence **Level B** (derived from less-robust meta-analyses and single RCTs).

By-subtype findings extracted from the included SRs:
- **Conventional HVGIC** (permanent teeth): high survival 2–6 years for surface texture, marginal discoloration/adaptation, anatomic form. ART/HVGIC showed high 5-year survival for single-surface posterior restorations but inconclusive results for multi-surface. ART techniques ranged 29.6–100% survival for single-occlusal restorations (4 months–6 years) and 30.6–100% for multi-surface (6 months–2 years); ART annual failure rates were higher in primary than permanent dentition in both single- and multi-surface restorations (2 studies); main failure modes: marginal defects, restoration loss, excessive wear, retention loss.
- **HVGIC with resin coating (HVGIC/RC)**: comparable to conventional GIC and composite up to 5 years in Class I/II (fracture toughness, retention, abrasion resistance); one SR found inferior longevity vs. composite.
- **RMGIC**: good option for Class I/II; outperformed HVGIC and composite in some SRs (survival, fluoride release, biologic considerations); other SRs found no significant difference vs. conventional GIC, composite, compomer, or amalgam. One SR (Manisha et al.) found RMGIC and HVGIC had less favorable compressive strength/microleakage than zirconia-reinforced GIC, while compomer and conventional GIC had greater compressive strength and lower microleakage than zirconia-reinforced GIC.
- **Silver-reinforced GIC (SRGIC)**: lower survival, higher secondary-caries recurrence.
- **Compomer**: statistically better than conventional GIC for median survival time, surface texture, marginal discoloration, tooth decay, and fatigue/fracture resistance (RMGIC no significant difference in the same SR); other SRs found compomer outperformed both conventional GIC and RMGIC on survival, marginal adaptation, surface roughness, and form.
- Across dentitions, **Class II restorations showed higher failure rates than Class I**, regardless of material or technique; cavity type and operator experience strongly influenced survival.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
Heterogeneity across the 13 included SRs: study-count range 3–67 primary studies per SR (6 SRs ≤11 studies; 3 SRs 15–24 studies; 4 SRs 34–67 studies); GIC type/composition varied; 5 SRs searched ≤2 databases; 2 SRs had incomplete search-strategy reporting; 6 SRs did not report duplicate removal; 1 SR did not state reviewer count; 3 SRs lacked a risk-of-bias assessment; 3 SRs lacked a quality assessment; 5 SRs used very short follow-up (as low as 4–6 months). Unclear/high risk of bias among most included SRs precluded meta-analysis in this umbrella review.

Future-study recommendations from the authors: well-designed RCTs with adequate random-sequence generation and allocation concealment; longer follow-up (>6 years); sample-size calculations accounting for expected dropout; reporting of participants' caries risk, tooth/cavity class, caries depth/extension, operator experience, isolation technique, and detailed material/technique description; inclusion of patient-reported outcomes (discomfort, pain, OHRQoL) alongside classical restoration-survival endpoints.

## 6. Related Work
Contrasted in the Discussion with prior umbrella/systematic reviews with a broader restorative-material scope or a compomer focus in primary teeth: Andas et al. (compomers similar to other direct-restoration materials in primary teeth) and Amend et al. (all studied materials had acceptable mean failure rates and were recommendable for primary-tooth caries restoration). GIC-vs-amalgam and GIC-vs-composite longevity SRs generally.

## 7. Glossary
- Umbrella review: review of systematic reviews.
- AMSTAR-2: SR methodological-quality tool.
- ROBIS: risk-of-bias-in-systematic-reviews tool.
- GRADE: framework for grading quality of evidence and strength of recommendations (here: Class of recommendation I/IIa/IIb/III; Level of evidence A/B/C).
- PRIO: Preferred Reporting Items for Overview of systematic reviews (umbrella-review reporting guideline).
- ART: Atraumatic Restorative Treatment (hand-instrument, minimally invasive GIC placement technique).
- HVGIC / RMGIC / SRGIC: high-viscosity / resin-modified / silver-reinforced glass ionomer cement.
