## Why Ingested

Earliest case series demonstrating that early implant removal (within 36 h) promotes IAN recovery — foundational evidence for the implant-related nerve injury management algorithm. Complements [[oral-surgery/nerve-injury/lee-2015-inferior-alveolar-nerve-injury-management]] by providing the primary case-based rationale for the 36-hour intervention window.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective 4-case series at King's College Hospital of IAN injury following mandibular implant placement, with early removal (18 h to 4 days post-injury).

Cases 1 and 2 (removal at 18 h and 36 h) regained near-complete sensory recovery; Cases 3 and 4 (removal at 2 and 4 days) suffered persistent complete numbness with functional problems.

Early implant removal within 36 hours of IAN compromise appears critical for preventing permanent neuropathy; adjunctive corticosteroid and high-dose NSAID therapy also appears effective.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First case-based evidence supporting the 36-hour threshold for implant removal to prevent permanent IAN neuropathy
- Demonstrates the role of adjunctive corticosteroids and high-dose NSAIDs in IAN injury management
- Proposes a practical clinical algorithm for suspected/actual implant-related IAN nerve injuries
- Highlights medico-legal implications (incidence 0–40% in the literature) and emphasizes informed consent

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Four patients referred to a specialist oral surgery department over two years with implant-related IAN injury. Data collected via health records. Sensory disturbance assessed clinically (IAN dermatome testing). All implants removed by reverse-torque technique with the implant driver. Removal timing: Case 1 = 18 h, Case 2 = 36 h, Case 3 = 2 days, Case 4 = 4 days.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Case | Age/Sex | Removal timing | Pain (neuropathic) | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 55F | 18 h | Yes | Near-complete recovery; adjunctive NSAID + steroid |
| 2 | — | 36 h | Yes | Near-complete recovery |
| 3 | — | 2 days | Yes | Persistent complete numbness; functional problems |
| 4 | — | 4 days | Yes | Persistent complete numbness; functional problems |

- 3/4 cases experienced neuropathic pain in addition to numbness
- Cases 1–2: outcome favorable; Cases 3–4: outcome poor
- Critical window identified: **<36 hours** from injury to implant removal

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Very small sample (n=4); no control group; retrospective design
- Heterogeneous cases (timing, anatomy, adjunctive treatments vary)
- Cannot exclude spontaneous recovery independent of removal
- Authors call for prospective studies and standardized management guidelines
- Lingual nerve vs IAN comparison acknowledged as imperfect; IAN more vulnerable to ischaemic injury within bony canal

## 6. Related Work

- Hegedus & Diecidue 2006: recommended immediate implant removal and delayed re-placement
- Blackburn 1990, Mason 1988: 90% of lingual nerve injuries resolve by 8–10 weeks (not directly comparable to IAN)
- Literature: 25% of iatrogenic paraesthesias suffer permanent effects
- After 3 months: permanent central and peripheral changes occur; microsurgical window closes

## 7. Glossary

- **IAN**: Inferior alveolar nerve — mandibular branch of V3 (trigeminal) running within the mandibular canal
- **Neuropathic pain**: Pain arising from nerve injury; includes allodynia, dysesthesia, spontaneous pain
- **Allodynia**: Pain response to normally non-painful stimulus
- **Dysesthesia**: Unpleasant abnormal sensation, spontaneous or evoked
- **Hypoesthesia**: Reduced but not absent sensation
- **Reverse-torque removal**: Standard technique — re-engage implant driver and run handpiece in reverse to unscrew implant
- **IAN dermatome**: Skin/mucosa territory innervated by IAN: ipsilateral lower lip, chin skin, vermilion, lower teeth, buccal gingiva of lower quadrant
