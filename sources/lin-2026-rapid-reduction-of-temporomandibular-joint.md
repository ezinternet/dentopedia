## Why Ingested

위키에 급성 악관절 탈구 (temporomandibular joint dislocation, TMJ dislocation)의 복원 자체를 다룬 페이지가 한 건도 없었고, [[tmj/falletti-2026-update-trismus-etiology-diagnosis-treatment]]의 아관긴장증 (trismus) 알고리즘은 "입이 벌어지지 않는" 방향만 다룬다. 본 논문은 응급실 기반 환자자가 유도하는 복원 (patient-guided reduction, PGR) 기법을 83예로 보고해 그 반대쪽("입이 닫히지 않는") 미충족 영역을 채우고, 통증 없이 무마취·무진정으로 끝나는 급성 복원 경로를 제시한다 — 이는 만성 TMD에서 환자 주도 요법을 1차 옵션으로 두는 [[tmj/ferland-2026-patient-education-self-management-tmd-sr-ma]]의 환자중심 파라다임을 응급 복원으로 이식한 것이라 두 페이지가 서로를 보완한다.

## Three-line Summary

Single-centre, single-operator retrospective case series (83 consecutive acute TMJ dislocation patients, January 2017 – December 2022, emergency department of Jiangxi Provincial People's Hospital) describing a two-step patient-guided reduction (PGR) manoeuvre in which the operator holds gentle sustained downward thumb traction on the external oblique ridges while the patient actively opens the mouth with head extension (Step 1) and then closes the mouth with head flexion (Step 2), without sedation or anaesthesia.

100% of 83 dislocations met the primary outcome — complete condylar repositioning within the protocol's 5-minute window (simple dislocations n = 81 fully reduced; the 2 condylar-fracture cases achieved only temporary palliative alignment as a bridge to surgery) — at a mean reduction time of 150 ± 51.78 s (range 28–280 s, median 146 s, IQR 124–183 s), with no case reaching the 300-s ceiling, no sedation required in any simple case, no iatrogenic injury, and success in all 12 patients (14.5%) who had already failed at least two Hippocratic attempts elsewhere.

The evidence is uncontrolled and single-operator, the 100% success rate is explicitly attributed by the authors to favourable case selection and 20+ years of operator experience, pain was reconstructed from chart narrative rather than VAS/NRS, 12 lateral cases (14.5%) bypassed the imaging-first protocol, and no return within 30 days could not exclude recurrence handled elsewhere — so the manoeuvre is a promising technique report, not a demonstrated improvement on the 86.2–86.7% Hippocratic success rate it is benchmarked against.

## 세줄요약

단일 센터·단일 술자 후향 환자 연쇄 (retrospective case series, n=83, 2017년 1월–2022년 12월, Jiangxi Provincial People's Hospital 응급실) — 술자가 외골유두 (external oblique ridge)에 엄지를 구멍 내리고 부드럽고 지속적인 하방견인 (downward traction)을 유지한 채, 환자가 능동적으로 움직이는 2단계 환자자가 유도하는 복원 (patient-guided reduction, PGR) 기법: 1단계는 개구+두정부전 (mouth opening + head extension), 2단계는 폐구+두부굴곡 (mouth closing + head flexion).

83예 전원이 5분 관찰창 내 완전 복원에 성공(단순 탈구 n=81 완전 복원, 과두파편 동반 2예는 최종 수술 전 임시정렬의 완화적 성격) — 평균 복원시간 150 ± 51.78초(범위 28–280초, 중앙값 146초, IQR 124–183초), 300초 프로토콜 상한에 도달한 사례 0건, 단순 탈구 전례에서 진정·마취 필요 없음, 의인성 손상·합병증 0건, 타 기관 히포크라테스식 (Hippocratic maneuver) 2회 이상 실패 후 내원한 난치 12예(14.5%)도 전부 성공.

무대조·단일 술자 설계로 100% 성공률은 저자 스스로도 유례적 환자 선별과 20년 이상 경력 술자의 효과로 해석하며, 통증은 VAS/NRS 없이 차트 서술로만 파악했고 측방탈구 12예(14.5%)는 영상 우선 프로토콜을 우회했으며 30초 내 재발(acute recurrence) 부재는 타 기관 처치분 배제 불가 — 히포크라테스 기법의 86.2–86.7% 성공률 대비 우위를 입증한 것이 아니라 유망한 기법 보고다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **A head-movement axis added to patient-driven reduction.** The distinctive element versus other "let the patient move the jaw" variants is that the patient's head is moved in opposition to the mouth: mouth opening is paired with head extension, mouth closing with head flexion. The authors propose this actively modifies the condylar-skull angle so the condyle bypasses the articular eminence on re-entry — a mechanism claim the paper itself labels theoretical (no cine-MRI, no EMG).
- **Protocol-level success ceiling rather than an open-ended attempt.** Success is pre-defined as complete repositioning inside a 5-minute window without procedural sedation or anaesthesia, with a mandated conversion to conventional manual reduction under local anaesthesia or sedation if that window is exceeded. No case in the series hit the 300-s limit, so the authors argue the 100% rate is free of censoring bias.
- **A quantified refractory subgroup.** 12 patients (14.5%) who had already failed at least two Hippocratic attempts at referring facilities were reduced successfully, with a mean interval of 4.2 h (range 2–12 h) from the last failed attempt to successful reduction — a direct head-to-head style contrast with the conventional method, though without a control arm.
- **First extraoral variant of the guided protocol with a time signal.** In 3 anterior cases (3.6%) with extreme intraoral sensitivity, the clinician applied the same movement protocol with pressure applied externally over the condylar area, reportedly reducing in approximately 30 s while removing the clinician's exposure to being bitten. The authors call this preliminary and keep the intraoral approach as the primary recommendation.
- **Atypical dislocation types included rather than excluded.** 16 lateral (19.3%) and 5 posterior (6.0%) dislocations — both with dedicated CT figures — were managed by the same protocol, extending the technique's claimed scope beyond the anterior type that dominates the literature.
- **Honest negative-space reporting.** The deviation from the imaging-first protocol (12/83, 14.5%) is stated with the three clinical criteria that justified it, and fracture-associated use is explicitly framed as palliative rather than definitive, with a recommendation *against* routine use in suspected or confirmed fracture.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective case series at a single emergency department, January 2017 – December 2022; consecutive inclusion of all eligible patients treated in the period. No sample-size pre-calculation for statistical power ("aiming for sufficient clinical representation").
- **n**: 83 consecutive patients (24 male, 59 female); mean age 44.64 ± 18.87 y (range 19–85 y). Inclusion required age ≥16 y — the observed minimum of 19 y is consistent.
- **Inclusion**: acute TMJ dislocation with consent for use of anonymised clinical data, photographs and radiographs. Imaging (CT/X-ray) was the standard protocol for suspected traumatic dislocation, except a pragmatic exception for 12 lateral-dislocation cases presenting in severe distress with a clear history of failed prior manual reduction, where immediate guided reduction was chosen to prioritise pain relief; post-reduction CT was then performed in all of them to confirm repositioning and exclude occult fracture.
- **Exclusion**: history of TMJ tumour or systemic disease affecting the TMJ; chronic TMJ dislocation; previous TMJ surgery; inability to follow instructions due to cognitive impairment.
- **Diagnosis**: clinical first (inability to close the mouth, pain, palpable condylar displacement), with dislocation direction classified as anterior / posterior / lateral; radiographic confirmation for non-anterior dislocations or suspected associated fracture.
- **Primary outcome**: successful reduction = complete anatomical repositioning of the condyle into the articular fossa within a 5-minute observation window, achieved without procedural sedation or anaesthesia.
- **Secondary outcome / measurement**: reduction time, recorded by an **independent observer (Fei Lin)** from the onset of sustained downward traction in Step 1 to audible or palpable condyle re-entry at the end of Step 2.
- **Protocol failure rule**: if reduction is not achieved within 5 min, the protocol mandated transition to conventional manual reduction under local anaesthesia or sedation.
- **Operator**: a single senior clinician with over 20 years of experience in oral and maxillofacial surgery performed **all 83** procedures, deliberately to hold technical constant during initial validation; investigators attended calibration sessions for technique and data-recording consistency.
- **Post-reduction care**: soft diet for 2 weeks, avoidance of wide yawning and biting hard foods, chin support while yawning, analgesics as needed; patients discharged immediately with no mandatory scheduled follow-up.
- **Data source**: existing clinical records plus a secondary review of the electronic medical record (EMR) for up to 6 months post-procedure; data available from the corresponding author on reasonable request; no funding received.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Technique as executed.** Patient seated facing the clinician with the mandible at the clinician's elbow level; both thumbs placed intraorally as posteriorly as possible on the external oblique ridge, remaining fingers extraoral for stability. **Step 1 (unlocking/disengagement)**: patient opens the mouth while simultaneously raising the head, as the clinician applies gentle, continuous downward traction with the thumbs — aiming to relax the masseter and pterygoid muscles and disengage the condyle from the temporal bone. **Step 2 (repositioning)**: under the sustained downward pressure the patient closes the mouth while simultaneously lowering the head, which glides the condyle back into the articular fossa; the thumbs can then be withdrawn slowly without risk of being bitten.

**Benchmarks quoted by the authors.** TMJ dislocation incidence ≈25 per 100,000 per year; traditional Hippocratic maneuver success rate 86.2–86.7%, often perceived as unpleasant, ergonomically demanding, and risking operator thumb injury from the myotonic reflex.

**Proposed mechanism (labelled theoretical).** (1) Synchronised gentle traction with voluntary mouth opening and head extension relaxes the mandibular elevators — masseter and medial pterygoid — reducing protective guarding and avoiding the myotonic reflex that sudden forceful pressure provokes. (2) Coordinated head extension/flexion dynamically modifies the condylar-skull angle, giving the condyle a favourable orientation to bypass the articular eminence, while the patient's own neck strength individualises the traction force. (3) Minimising external force and using the patient's proprioception is argued to spare the TMJ capsule and temporomandibular ligament (TML) from over-extension, preserving post-reduction stability — explicitly unvalidated.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Design and bias (authors' own)**: retrospective single-ED series, no control arm, selection and information bias from record-based data collection; 100% success attributed to single-operator bias by one clinician with >20 years' experience; generalisability to novice operators or other settings unproven.
- **Pain unmeasured**: no VAS or NRS recorded, so the "less painful than Hippocratic" claim rests on chart descriptors and cannot support comparison.
- **Long-term outcomes absent**: only an immediate-success endpoint plus 30-day return-to-ED; no prospective range-of-motion, chronic-pain or recurrence data, and recurrence treated at other facilities cannot be excluded.
- **Anatomy unaddressed**: the technique reduces the acute event but does not modify predisposing ligamentous laxity or articular eminence morphology.
- **Fracture cases (n = 2)**: palliative alignment only; the authors do not recommend routine application with suspected or confirmed fracture, and mandate pre-reduction CT/MRI in all traumatic presentations.
- **Reporting ambiguity (inferred, not stated by the authors)**: the paper reports "12 cases" for both the imaging-protocol deviation (Methods, lateral dislocations) and the refractory-after-failed-Hippocratic subgroup (Results) — both 12/83 (14.5%) — without stating whether these are the same 12 patients. Given 16 lateral dislocations were treated, the two subgroups cannot be assumed identical. Readers should not sum them.
- **Future work proposed by the authors**: multi-centre prospective randomised trials against conventional manoeuvres, standardised pain scales, long-term follow-up with objective physiological data (dynamic cine-MRI, EMG) to test the muscle-relaxation mechanism, and separate validation of the extraoral variant in a larger cohort.

## 7. Glossary

- **TMJ dislocation / luxation**: condyle displaced out of the articular (glenoid) fossa and locked there, preventing normal jaw movement; ~25 per 100,000 per year. Anterior (74.7%) ≫ lateral (19.3%) > posterior (6.0%) in this series.
- **PGR (patient-guided reduction)**: the technique reported here — operator maintains gentle sustained downward thumb traction while the patient actively performs the two-step mouth/head movement sequence.
- **Hippocratic maneuver**: conventional manual reduction — patient seated, mandible at or below the operator's elbow level, operator's thumbs intraorally on lower molars or external oblique ridge pressing down then back, assistant stabilising the head; reported success 86.2–86.7%, and the benchmark this paper positions against.
- **External oblique ridge**: the bony ridge on the lateral mandibular body; the thumb contact point for maximum leverage in this technique.
- **Myotonic (myotatic) reflex**: involuntary protective muscle contraction triggered by sudden forceful muscle stretch, the mechanism by which forceful Hippocratic manipulation can abort a reduction and, in the operator's case, snap the thumb.
- **Condylar-skull angle**: the geometric relationship the paper proposes to manipulate via head extension/flexion to let the condyle clear the articular eminence on re-entry; a theoretical mechanism, unvalidated by cine-MRI or EMG.
- **TML (temporomandibular ligament)**: capsuloligamentous structure whose preservation is claimed to reduce the risk of chronic instability.
- **IQR**: interquartile range — here 124–183 s around a 146 s median.
