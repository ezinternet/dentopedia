## Why Ingested

This 2-year RCT directly compares immediate vs. conventional loading of immediately-placed implants in periodontally compromised patients, addressing the core loading-protocol evidence axis. It reinforces [[immediate-implant/loading-protocol/wittneben-2023-immediate-implant-loaded-single-crown-sr]] and extends the evidence base for bone regeneration outcomes specific to periodontal-disease-history patients — a population not well-represented in early loading studies.

## Three-line Summary

2-year RCT (n=60: 30 immediate loading, 30 conventional/delayed loading) of immediately placed implants in extraction sites of periodontally compromised patients; both groups received allograft bone and barrier membrane, stratified by loading protocol.

Immediate loading group: 96.7% survival, mean bone gain 1.19 mm at 2 years; conventional loading group: 93.3% survival, mean bone gain 1.0 mm. No significant differences in bone gain or papilla index change between groups (P >0.05).

Immediate loading of immediately-placed implants in periodontal-disease-history patients yielded similar bone regeneration and soft-tissue esthetics to delayed loading; primary closure and delayed loading were not critical for bone gain in this cohort.

## 세줄요약

2년 RCT (n=60: 즉시부하 30명, 지연부하 30명), 치주질환 병력 환자의 즉시식립 임플란트, 골이식·차단막 병용, 부하프로토콜 비교.

즉시부하: 96.7% 생존율, 평균 골신득 1.19 mm (2년); 지연부하: 93.3% 생존율, 평균 골신득 1.0 mm. 골신득과 유두지수 변화 무차이 (P >0.05).

치주질환 병력 환자의 즉시식립·즉시부하 임플란트는 지연부하와 유사한 골재생·연조직 심미 결과 제공; 1차 봉합·지연부하는 이 집단에서 골신득을 위해 필수적이지 않음.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Direct RCT comparison of immediate vs. conventional loading for immediately-placed single implants in periodontally compromised patients — a population with higher baseline bone loss at baseline
- Bone regeneration quantified at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months post-placement using radiographic linear measurement
- Esthetic assessment via papillary index (PI) blinded evaluation at 3, 6, 12, 24 months
- Demonstration that primary closure and delayed loading are not prerequisite for bone gain in immediate implant placement with bone graft support
- Evidence that immediate loading does not compromise bone regeneration outcomes vs. conventional loading in this subpopulation

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Prospective, randomized controlled trial (RCT); parallel-group allocation
- **Population**: Patients with history of periodontal disease requiring single tooth extraction and replacement via immediate implant placement
- **Sample**: 72 patients enrolled, 60 completed with implant placement (30 immediate loading, 30 conventional loading); 2-year follow-up completed
- **Surgical Protocol**: 
  - All patients: tooth extraction, immediate implant placement in same visit
  - All patients: periodontal flap, allograft bone placement, collagen barrier membrane
  - Immediate loading group: temporary crown placed at insertion
  - Conventional loading group: primary closure without crown, conventional loading after 3-month osseointegration
- **Evaluation Points**: 3, 6, 12, and 24 months post-placement
- **Primary Outcomes**: 
  - Radiographic bone level change (linear measurement from baseline to each time point)
  - Implant survival (no mobility, no radiographic peri-implant radiolucency)
  - Papillary esthetic score (papilla index change)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Immediate Loading (n=30) | Conventional Loading (n=30) | P-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implant Survival Rate (2 yr) | 96.7% (29/30) | 93.3% (28/30) | NS |
| Mean Bone Gain (2 yr) | 1.19 mm | 1.00 mm | >0.05 (NS) |
| Mean Bone Gain (baseline to 1 yr) | 1.05 mm* | 0.95 mm* | <0.001 (within-group, paired) |
| Bone Gain 1–2 yr | 0.14 mm | 0.05 mm | Minimal change |
| Papilla Index (baseline to 1 yr) | Significant decrease | Significant decrease | <0.001 (within both groups) |
| Papilla Index (1–2 yr) | Minimal change | Minimal change | NS |

*Bone gain was predominantly achieved in the first postoperative year in both groups; minimal additional gain occurred from month 12 to 24.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Single-tooth replacement only; immediate-loading evidence may not generalize to multi-unit/full-arch cases
- Relatively small sample size (n=60); moderate power for detecting non-inferiority
- Periodontally compromised cohort limits generalizability to patients with no periodontal history or different disease severity
- Esthetic evaluation limited to papilla index; does not include soft-tissue contour, buccal-plate integrity, or midfacial recession quantification beyond blinded PI
- No direct histologic validation of bone regeneration; bone changes inferred from 2D radiography (superimposition may obscure true 3D changes)
- No implant stability quotient (ISQ) or resonance frequency analysis (RFA) data; primary stability magnitude not reported, potentially confounding early loading safety interpretation
- No interim analysis for failure trends; if survival differences emerged by 12 months, study continued to 24 months despite potential non-equivalence signal

## 7. Glossary
- **Immediate Implant (Type 1)**: Implant placement into fresh extraction socket at time of tooth removal, in the same surgical appointment.
- **Immediate Loading**: Functional load (temporary or definitive crown) placed on implant within 1–2 weeks of implant placement; distinct from "immediate provisionalization" (temporary crown for esthetics, non-functional contact).
- **Conventional Loading**: Osseointegration period of 3–6 months without load, followed by abutment connection and crown insertion.
- **Papillary Index (PI)**: Blinded visual assessment of interdental papilla fill at implant-crown margin; scored on ordinal scale (1 = complete fill, lower = recession).
- **Allograft Bone**: Demineralized or mineralized allogeneic (human cadaver-derived) bone graft material; used here for socket grafting to augment bone volume.
- **Barrier Membrane**: Collagen-based resorbable or non-resorbable (e-PTFE) membrane placed over bone graft to protect graft and guide bone regeneration (GBR principle).
