## Why Ingested

First case in this wiki documenting a detailed day-by-day management protocol for IANB-induced trismus over a 50-day recovery arc. Clinically relevant for IANB complication counselling and for building a trismus emergency workflow. Complements broader trismus etiology and management content (see [[tmj/falletti-2026-update-trismus-etiology-diagnosis-treatment]]).

## Three-line Summary

A 59-year-old woman developed severe trismus (18 mm opening) within 12 hours of receiving two IANB injections of 2% lidocaine with epinephrine; conservative management with warm compresses, jaw exercises, NSAIDs, and corticosteroids yielded no improvement through day 12 but progressive recovery thereafter, reaching full function (42 mm) by day 50. The case documents six proposed mechanisms for IANB-related trismus — medial pterygoid penetration, intramuscular hematoma, needle-track infection, local anesthetic myotoxicity, barbed needle injury, and volume-induced tissue expansion — and underscores that early, sustained physical therapy is the core driver of recovery. Chronic trismus (failure to manage acutely) risks scar tissue formation and irreversible restriction, making early recognition and structured exercise protocols essential.

## 세줄요약

59세 여성이 하치조신경차단 (Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block, IANB) 2회 시술 후 12시간 내에 아관긴장증 (Trismus, 개구량 18 mm)이 발생하였으며, 온찜질·운동요법·비스테로이드성 소염진통제 (Nonsteroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs, NSAIDs)·스테로이드 복합 치료로 12일까지 개선 없다가 이후 점진적 회복, 50일째 완전 회복(42 mm)에 이르렀다. 익돌근 관통, 혈종 (Hematoma), 감염, 국소마취제 근독성 (Myotoxicity), 바브 침 손상, 과다 주입 등 6가지 기전을 제시하며, 초기 구강운동 치료가 핵심 회복 인자임을 강조하였다. 급성기 관리 실패 시 반흔 조직 (Scar Tissue) 형성→만성 아관긴장증으로 진행될 위험이 있어 조기 진단과 단계별 운동 프로토콜이 필수적이다.

## Key Contributions

1. **Day-by-day management protocol**: Detailed timeline from symptom onset to full recovery — rare level of granularity for IANB trismus.
2. **Six-mechanism taxonomy** for IANB-induced trismus (see Key Results).
3. **Outcome-annotated exercise regimen**: Specific exercises with frequency and hold duration that can be reproduced in clinical practice.
4. **Pharmacological sequencing**: Oral NSAIDs + muscle relaxant + venotonic (days 1–7) → IM NSAID + corticosteroid combination (days 8–12) → exercise escalation (days 12–50).
5. **Contextualizes dexamethasone evidence**: Cites mixed literature on DEX for trismus specifically (vs. oedema) and notes submucosal route superiority per Kaewkumnert et al.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Single retrospective case report
- **Patient**: 59-year-old female, no significant medical history
- **Procedure**: Restorative treatment tooth 47 (lower right first molar); two IANB injections totaling ~3.4 cc Persocaine® (2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine)
- **Outcome measures**: Interincisal opening (mm), lateral movement (mm), oedema resolution, pain on palpation

## Key Results

### Presentation at Day 1

| Parameter | Finding |
|---|---|
| Interincisal opening | 18 mm (normal ≥ 35 mm women / ≥ 40 mm men) |
| Right lateral movement | 9 mm |
| Left lateral movement | 3 mm |
| Right lower face | Oedema |
| TMJ palpation | Painful |
| Medial pterygoid palpation | Painful |
| Onset | 12 hours post-IANB |

### Management Protocol Table

| Days | Intervention | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1–7 | Warm application daily; jaw exercises ×10/day (opening/closing, hold 5 s ×3/day; laterality ×10/day; protrusion ×5/day); Muscerol 2 (paracetamol + orphenadrine) 2×3/day; Profinal (ibuprofen 400 mg) 1×3/day; Reparil (aescin 20 mg) 2×3/day | Oedema resolved day 7; bruising appeared day 4; mouth opening unchanged at 18 mm |
| 8–12 | IM Difen B12® (diclofenac + beclomethasone + cyanocobalamin) daily ×5; Augmentin® added day 10 (suspected infection) | Oedema fully resolved; mouth opening worsened to 13 mm (forced exercise effect) |
| 12–50 | Increased exercise frequency | 15 mm (D12) → 17 mm (D13) → 19 mm (D14) → 28 mm (D30) → 42 mm (D50); full recovery maintained at 1-month follow-up |

### Six Mechanisms of IANB-Related Trismus

1. **Medial pterygoid penetration** → muscle spasm
2. **Intramuscular hematoma** — injury to inferior alveolar artery/vein → pterygomandibular space hematoma
3. **Needle-track infection** — delayed onset 2–3 days post-injection
4. **Local anesthetic myotoxicity** — direct cellular toxicity of the anesthetic agent
5. **Barbed needle tip** — tearing of muscle fibers on withdrawal
6. **Large volume injection** into restricted pterygomandibular space → tissue expansion / pressure necrosis

### Natural History Context

- Acute iatrogenic trismus typically resolves in **2–6 weeks** (range 4–20 weeks)
- Chronic trismus: severe trauma, delayed/inadequate management, local infections, TMD, radiotherapy, neoplastic processes, connective tissue disorders → scar tissue → progressive restriction

## Limitations

- Single case; no control or comparison group
- Cannot distinguish treatment effect from natural healing trajectory
- Protocol includes multiple simultaneous interventions — impossible to isolate which component drove recovery
- Long recovery (50 days) without clear inflection point attributable to specific treatment change
- No imaging (MRI/CT) to confirm underlying mechanism (hematoma vs. muscle spasm vs. other)

## Related Work

- Falletti 2026 — broad trismus etiology, diagnosis, and treatment update; provides reference framework for this case's classification
- Kaewkumnert et al. (cited) — submucosal DEX superior to IM for post-third-molar trismus
- Referenced dexamethasone studies for oedema reduction after third molar surgery (4 mg submucosal)

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Trismus | Restricted mouth opening; clinically significant below 35 mm (women) / 40 mm (men) |
| IANB | Inferior alveolar nerve block — mandibular anesthesia technique targeting the inferior alveolar nerve at the mandibular foramen |
| Pterygomandibular space | Potential space bounded by medial pterygoid muscle and mandibular ramus; site of IANB deposition |
| Persocaine® | 2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine formulation |
| Muscerol 2 | Paracetamol + orphenadrine (muscle relaxant + analgesic combination) |
| Reparil / aescin | Venotonic agent; used for oedema reduction |
| Difen B12® | IM formulation of diclofenac + beclomethasone (corticosteroid) + cyanocobalamin (B12) |
| Myotoxicity | Direct muscle cell toxicity from chemical agents, including local anesthetics |
