## Why Ingested

This is the consensus-level ADA clinical practice guideline itself, ingested at user request as the authoritative synthesis sitting on top of the evidence reviews this wiki already holds. The panel's own commissioned systematic reviews are [[wiki/drug/analgesics/miroshnychenko-2023-dental-extraction-pain-network-meta]] (82-RCT network meta-analysis of analgesics after extraction) and [[wiki/drug/analgesics/miroshnychenko-2023-injectable-topical-anesthetics-acute-pain-sr]] (local/topical anesthetics) — this guideline translates those reviews into graded recommendations (GRADE + Evidence-to-Decision) for two scenarios: post-extraction pain and temporary toothache management when definitive care is not immediately available. Ingesting the polished final package completes the review→guideline chain so clinical consults can cite consensus-level guidance, not only the underlying SRs.

## Three-line Summary

ADA clinical practice guideline (JADA 2024;155(2):102-117), GRADE + Evidence-to-Decision, built on 4 systematic reviews (opioid/nonopioid analgesics, local anesthetics, corticosteroids, topical anesthetics), covering post-extraction pain and temporary toothache management in adolescents (12–<17 y), adults (17–<65 y), older adults (≥65 y).

Nonopioid first-line throughout: NSAID alone (ibuprofen 400 mg or naproxen sodium 440 mg) ± acetaminophen 500 mg; opioids reserved as add-on/replace steps (APAP 325 mg + APAP-opioid combo, lowest dose/fewest tablets/duration rarely >3 days); against corticosteroid addition (conditional, very low) and against opioids after simple extraction (conditional, low).

All recommendations conditional at low/very low certainty — guidance rests on a beneficial net balance (efficacy + opioid-specific serious harms), not high-certainty effect estimates; clinicians must individualize with shared decision making.

비오피이드(NSAID) 전 구간 1차요법: NSAID 단독(이부프로펜 400 mg 또는 나프록센나트륨 440 mg)±아세트아미노펜 500 mg; 오피이드는 추가·대체 단계로만(APAP 325 mg+APAP-오피이드 복합제, 최저유효용량·최소 정제수·3일 초과 드물게); 코르티코스테로이드 추가는 반대(조건부, 매우 낮음), 단순 발치 후 오피이드 사용도 반대(조건부, 낮음).

모든 권고가 조건부이며 확실성은 낮음~매우 낮음 — 지침은 고효과 추정치가 아니라 유효성+오피이드 고유 중대 위해의 순이익 판단에 근거하므로, 임상에서는 공유 의사결정으로 개별화해야 한다.

## 1. Document Information
- Journal: JADA 2024;155(2):102-117 (February 2024)
- DOI: 10.1016/j.adaj.2023.10.009
- Design: Evidence-based clinical practice guideline (CPG)
- Convened by: American Dental Association Science and Research Institute, University of Pittsburgh, University of Pennsylvania (with McMaster University methodological support); funded by an FDA grant (Congressional mandate following the 2020 National Academies report)
- Panel: 14 members (clinicians, researchers, epidemiologists, pharmacologists, public health dentists, patient representative); methodological team of 16 researchers; meetings December 2020–December 2021
- Scope: acute dental pain after simple and surgical tooth extraction(s) (including impacted mandibular third molars) AND temporary management of toothache (symptomatic reversible or symptomatic irreversible pulpitis ± symptomatic apical periodontitis, or pulp necrosis with symptomatic apical periodontitis, or acute apical abscess) when definitive dental treatment is not immediately available
- Age bands defined by the panel: adolescents (12–<17 years), adults (17–<65 years), older adults (≥65 years)

## 2. Key Contributions
- First FDA-funded, systematically developed ADA CPG for acute dental pain pharmacotherapy across both extraction and toothache scenarios
- Nonopioid medications established as first-line therapy in both scenarios; opioids reserved strictly for inadequate first-line response or NSAID contraindication
- Complete stepwise opioid-sparing ladder (initiate → add → replace → contraindicated path) with explicit numeric guardrails (max daily doses: ibuprofen 2,400 mg, naproxen sodium 1,100 mg, acetaminophen 4,000 mg)
- Recommends AGAINST routine delayed ("just-in-case") opioid prescriptions; recommends supplemental long-acting local anesthetics before discharge as a complementary strategy
- Recommends AGAINST adding oral/submucosal/intramuscular corticosteroids to standard analgesic therapy for postoperative pain
- 7 good practice statements on counseling expectations, history review, minimum effective dosage, storage/disposal counseling, PDMP checks, chronic-opioid patients, and substance use disorder precautions

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- 4 systematic reviews and meta-analyses conducted to inform the guideline: (1) opioid and nonopioid analgesics, (2) local anesthetics, (3) corticosteroids (oral/submucosal/intramuscular), (4) topical anesthetics
- GRADE approach for certainty assessment; GRADE Evidence-to-Decision Framework for recommendation formulation; NASEM methodological standards
- Searches: MEDLINE via PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, gray literature from database inception through September 30, 2021; RCTs prioritized for benefit, observational data used for long-term opioid harms; Cochrane RoB 2.0 and ROBINS-I for risk of bias
- Three guidance types produced: (1) recommendation statements with strength + certainty, (2) good practice statements supported by extensive indirect evidence, (3) panel remarks; consensus-first, vote (simple majority) only if consensus failed
- Patient values informed via a primary values-and-preferences study (31 stakeholder organizations engaged twice: question framing + external review; plus public comment period)
- Where direct toothache evidence was lacking, indirect evidence from extraction-pain RCTs was used explicitly

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Analgesics after surgical extraction(s): 82 RCTs, high-to-moderate certainty — NSAIDs alone (ibuprofen or naproxen sodium) or combined with acetaminophen more effective than opioids; opioids increase dizziness, drowsiness, nausea, vomiting plus severe harms (long-term persistent use, overdose, hospitalization, mortality); ibuprofen and acetaminophen may slightly increase drowsiness (low–very low certainty)
- Corticosteroids: 40 RCTs, low–very low certainty — trivial pain reduction vs no corticosteroids; undesirable effects uncertain (very low certainty) → recommendation against addition
- Long-acting LA: 7 RCTs, very low certainty — bupivacaine before discharge could reduce rescue analgesia need 8–48 h postoperatively; negligible difference vs short-acting LA (2% lidocaine, 3% mepivacaine) for adverse effects (moderate certainty); no clinically significant difference found between bupivacaine and 4% articaine (very low certainty)
- Topical anesthetics for toothache: 10% benzocaine (2 RCTs) or 20% (3 RCTs) increases proportion with substantial pain reduction at ≥2 consecutive time points (20–30 min) vs placebo vehicle (low certainty); 20% vs 10% probably negligible benefit (moderate certainty)
- Toothache direct evidence: none found for analgesics or LAs → indirect extrapolation from extraction RCTs
- Values and preferences: most people place high value on avoiding any substance use disorder risk even with mild/moderate/severe pain; a smaller group accepts increased opioid risk to manage severe pain; patients value agency and personalized discussion

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- No direct evidence for temporary toothache management — analgesic and LA recommendations rest on indirect extraction-pain RCTs
- Adverse-effect certainty mostly low to very low even where efficacy certainty was moderate-high; future studies should report precise patient-important adverse-event estimates with larger samples
- Out of scope: IV corticosteroids; corticosteroids for inflammatory complications (trismus, facial swelling, infection); antibiotics; definitive dental treatment itself
- Recommendations apply only when definitive dental treatment is not immediately available (Box 2) — they are a bridge, not a substitute or reason to delay definitive care
- Panel urges research access/policy work: reconcile emergency-medicine→dentistry referral flow, integrate dental and emergency medical services, intensify preventive oral health care

## 7. Glossary
- CPG: Clinical practice guideline
- GRADE: Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation
- EtD: Evidence-to-Decision framework (GRADE)
- MME: Morphine milligram equivalents
- NSAID: Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug
- APAP: Acetaminophen (paracetamol)
- PDMP: Prescription drug monitoring program
- NSRCT: Nonsurgical root canal treatment
- OUD: Opioid use disorder
- Definitive dental treatment: pulpectomy, nonsurgical root canal treatment, incision and drainage of abscess, tooth extraction
- Conditional recommendation: most patients would want the suggested course of action but many would not; clinician helps each patient decide consistent with values/preferences
- Just-in-case prescription: delayed opioid prescription written for anticipated breakthrough pain (routinely not recommended)
