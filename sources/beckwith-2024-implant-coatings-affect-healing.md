## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (11 systematic reviews included) using PICOS/PROSPERO-registered protocol, AMSTAR 2 and ROBIS quality/bias assessment, evaluating whether dental implant surface coatings (calcium phosphate/hydroxyapatite, bisphosphonate, bioactive glass) affect healing outcomes.

Weak evidence that coatings (chiefly HA/calcium phosphate) improve osseointegration and reduce marginal bone loss (MBL); weak evidence for progressive complications with calcium phosphate coatings; included reviews showed considerable heterogeneity, small sample sizes, low confidence, and high risk of bias.

Abstract-only — full text not retrieved; current evidence is too weak/heterogeneous to guide coating selection, and long-term multicenter controlled trials with standardized protocols are needed.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Umbrella-review-level synthesis of 11 systematic reviews evaluating implant coatings (calcium phosphate/hydroxyapatite including brushite and bioabsorbable nano-HA, bisphosphonate, bioactive glass) and their effect on implant healing outcomes.
- Applies dual quality/bias instruments (AMSTAR 2 for the included SRs' methodological quality, ROBIS for risk of bias) — a higher evidentiary bar than a single SR typically applies to itself.
- Explicitly flags that the included body of SR evidence is low-confidence and high-risk-of-bias, tempering any positive-sounding coating claims found in the primary literature.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- PICOS-structured question; protocol pre-registered with PROSPERO (CRD42022321926).
- Databases searched: MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, ScienceDirect.
- Duplicate independent screening (title/abstract, then full text), duplicate independent quality/bias assessment (AMSTAR 2, ROBIS), duplicate independent data extraction via predefined form.
- Outcome domains of interest across included SRs: marginal bone loss (MBL), bone-to-implant contact (BIC), survival/success rates.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 11 systematic reviews met inclusion criteria.
- Most frequently assessed coatings: calcium phosphate-based (hydroxyapatite [HA], brushite, bioabsorbable nano-HA), followed by bisphosphonate coatings, then bioactive glass coatings.
- Most frequently assessed outcomes: MBL, BIC, survival/success rates.
- Considerable heterogeneity and small sample sizes across the included reviews' primary studies.
- Quality/bias appraisal: low confidence in the included reviews overall, high risk of bias.
- Bottom line: weak evidence that coatings improve osseointegration and reduce MBL; weak evidence of progressive complications specifically with calcium phosphate coatings.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Umbrella-review-of-SRs design inherits and compounds the weaknesses of primary studies underlying each included SR (small samples, heterogeneous protocols).
- Authors explicitly call for long-term, multicenter, controlled clinical trials with improved standardization and bias control before coating effects can be considered established.
- Abstract-only ingestion here — full text (methods detail on the AMSTAR 2/ROBIS scoring per included SR, and the specific effect estimates or ranges reported for MBL/BIC/survival) not retrieved; if finer detail is needed later, the JOMI PDF should be sourced directly.

## 7. Glossary

- **Umbrella review**: A systematic review of systematic reviews — synthesizes conclusions across multiple SRs on a topic rather than pooling primary-study data directly.
- **AMSTAR 2**: A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews (version 2) — critical appraisal instrument for the methodological quality of systematic reviews.
- **ROBIS**: Risk Of Bias In Systematic reviews — tool assessing risk of bias in the conduct of a systematic review.
- **MBL**: Marginal bone loss — radiographic/clinical measure of peri-implant crestal bone change over time.
- **BIC**: Bone-to-implant contact — histomorphometric measure of direct bone apposition to the implant surface.
- **HA**: Hydroxyapatite — a calcium phosphate mineral used as a bioactive implant surface coating.
