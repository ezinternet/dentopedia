## Why Ingested

Found while surveying hyposalivation/xerostomia dental-management literature. Xerostomia is only one of several oral conditions this comprehensive narrative review covers in Alzheimer's disease (AD) patients — alongside periodontal disease, root caries, traumatic mucosal lesions, and prosthesis-related complications — but it is directly relevant to the wiki's existing geriatric-dentistry/xerostomia coverage (e.g. [[wiki/geriatric-dentistry/kumar-2025-nutritional-status-dry-mouth-older-adults]] and [[wiki/geriatric-dentistry/halasabalu-kalgeri-2026-polypharmacy-oral-health-elderly]]) by adding a dementia-specific, stage-adapted clinical management lens on top of the general polypharmacy/older-adult xerostomia framework already in the wiki.

## Three-line Summary

Comprehensive narrative review (27 studies, 1997–2025, from PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Scielo, and Web of Science; no PRISMA/systematic protocol) synthesizing oral health conditions and stage-specific dental management strategies for Alzheimer's disease (AD) patients, with emphasis on caregiver integration.

AD patients commonly present periodontal disease, root caries, xerostomia/hyposalivation, and traumatic (denture-related) mucosal lesions; evidence does not support strong bidirectional causality between AD and oral disease, pointing instead to shared antecedent factors (biological aging, chronic inflammation) producing a "syndemic" pattern of parallel decline.

Effective care requires stage-adapted strategies (conventional preventive/restorative care in early AD → minimally invasive/ART in moderate AD → palliative infection/comfort control in advanced AD) plus structured caregiver behavioral training (hand-over-hand technique, short positive commands, music-based distraction); as a narrative (non-systematic) review, no quantitative pooling or risk-of-bias/GRADE assessment was performed.

## 세줄요약

포괄적 내러티브 리뷰(narrative review) (27편, 1997–2025년, PubMed/MEDLINE·Scopus·Scielo·Web of Science; PRISMA 체계적 프로토콜 아님) — 알츠하이머병(Alzheimer's Disease, AD) 환자의 구강건강 상태 및 병기별(stage-specific) 치과관리 전략을 종합, 보호자(caregiver) 통합을 강조.

AD 환자는 치주질환(periodontal disease)·치근우식(root caries)·구강건조증/침분비저하(xerostomia/hyposalivation)·의치 관련 외상성 점막병변을 흔히 보임; AD와 구강질환 간 강한 양방향 인과관계는 근거 부족하며, 오히려 생물학적 노화·만성 염증 등 공통 선행요인에 의한 "신드믹(syndemic)" 동시 악화 패턴으로 설명됨.

효과적 관리는 병기별 맞춤 전략 필요(경도: 통상적 예방·수복 치료 → 중등도: 최소침습/ART → 중증: 완화적 감염관리·안위 중심) + 구조화된 보호자 행동훈련(핸드오버핸드 기법, 짧은 긍정적 구두지시, 음악 기반 주의전환); 내러티브(비체계적) 리뷰이므로 정량적 메타분석이나 비뚤림위험(risk-of-bias)/GRADE 평가는 수행되지 않음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1111/scd.70179](https://doi.org/10.1111/scd.70179)), synthesizes 27 studies (1997–2025) into three thematic domains: (1) clinical oral manifestations of AD, (2) stage-adapted dental management, and (3) the caregiver's role.
- Proposes a "syndemic" rather than causal model: AD and oral disease (periodontitis, caries) share antecedent exposures (aging, chronic inflammation, social determinants) rather than one causing the other; frames the relationship as a self-reinforcing cycle (Figure 1) — AD progression → reduced autonomy + medication-related xerostomia → periodontitis/caries → chronic pain, masticatory dysfunction, nutritional compromise, systemic inflammation → reinforces frailty.
- Provides an explicit stage-by-stage dental management table (Table 1: early/moderate/advanced AD) mapping behavioral profile → clinical guidance → treatment objective → dental planning → recommended strategy.
- Details 8 concrete caregiver-oriented behavioral techniques (positioning, short positive communication, hand-over-hand technique, chaining/priming, music-based distraction, de-escalation, adaptive tools, denture care) grounded in preserved procedural/emotional memory despite cognitive decline.
- Explains the mechanistic basis of AD-associated xerostomia: autonomic dysfunction independent of medication, compounded by acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine) being routinely co-prescribed with anticholinergic/xerostomic antidepressants and antipsychotics.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Comprehensive narrative review following the Green et al. framework (not PRISMA/systematic review methodology) — appropriate for integrating heterogeneous evidence (observational, qualitative, clinical reports, reviews) into a clinically-oriented synthesis.
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Scielo, Web of Science; searched November 2025. MeSH + free-text terms combined with Boolean operators and truncation; complementary "caregiver*" search arm. No language or year restriction.
- **Eligibility**: Studies addressing oral health conditions, dental management, preventive/therapeutic dental care, or caregiver roles in older adults with AD. Excluded: other-dementia-only focus, purely pharmacological/neurological/laboratory topics, editorials/letters/opinion without clinical relevance.
- **Screening**: Two independent reviewers (M.G.S., M.B.V.) screened titles/abstracts; disagreements resolved by consensus or a third reviewer (M.A.L.F.); author/journal/COI information concealed during screening; reference lists of included studies also hand-searched.
- **Synthesis**: Qualitative, organized into 3 thematic domains (clinical manifestations; adapted dental management; caregiver role) — no quantitative pooling, no formal risk-of-bias tool, no GRADE certainty rating.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Search yield**: 640 records (MEDLINE 238, Web of Science 241, Scopus 157, SciELO 4) → 203 duplicates removed → 12 studies met eligibility from database search + 15 additional via manual/reference-list search → **27 studies included**, published 1997–2025.
- **Study-design mix**: observational (n=7), literature reviews (n=7), systematic/scoping reviews (n=6), non-randomized interventional/pilot studies (n=4), RCTs (n=2), experimental study (n=1).
- **Periodontitis**: chronic systemic inflammatory source in AD; periodontal pathogens hypothesized to translocate to CNS (microglial activation, oxidative stress) but epidemiological evidence insufficient for bidirectional causality; declining manual dexterity/immunosenescence → greater plaque/gingival bleeding vs cognitively healthy older adults.
- **Root caries**: AD patients show a "third peak" of cariogenic activity concentrated on exposed root surfaces (vs coronal-caries stabilization typical of general older-adult populations); significantly higher decayed-surface counts and root-caries prevalence vs cognitively intact controls, driven by AD-specific oral dysbiosis (increased acidogenic/aciduric taxa, reduced diversity) plus reduced salivary buffering (critical pH for dentin ~6.2–6.7 vs enamel ~5.5).
- **Xerostomia/hyposalivation**: driven by both AD-related autonomic dysfunction (independent of medication, present even in early AD) and cumulative polypharmacy — acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine) commonly combined with anticholinergic/xerostomic antidepressants/antipsychotics; reduced buffering accelerates caries and erosion and favors aciduric/opportunistic colonization.
- **Traumatic mucosal lesions**: denture stomatitis occurs >2x more often in AD/dementia vs cognitively healthy older adults, driven by ill-fitting dentures (weight loss/muscle atrophy), continuous overnight wear, and poor hygiene; hyaluronic acid gel (0.2–0.3%) plus denture adjustment accelerates healing vs adjustment alone.
- **Stage-specific management table (Table 1)**: Early AD — conventional dental care, up to 50-min appointments, prioritize curative/clinical interventions, patient + caregiver hygiene education. Moderate AD — mandatory caregiver presence, ~30-min morning appointments, minimally invasive techniques (ART — atraumatic restorative treatment), fluoride-releasing materials, possible conscious sedation, intensive caregiver hygiene training. Advanced AD — emergency treatment only, palliative/assisted hygiene, denture removal if risk, cariostatic agents (e.g. SDF — silver diamine fluoride) for non-restorative caries control, multidisciplinary planning focused on comfort/infection control.
- **Caregiver behavioral toolkit**: positioning/head stabilization (11–12 o'clock caregiver position); short single-step positive-affect commands ("Open," "Good," "All done"); hand-over-hand technique (most robustly supported for apraxia/resistance, exploits preserved procedural memory); chaining/priming (sensory cues before motor task); music-based distraction (engages preserved emotional/auditory memory, reduces psychomotor agitation); rescuing/de-escalation (interrupt care before verbal refusal becomes physical aggression); adaptive tools (electric toothbrushes, high-fluoride toothpaste, mouth props for trismus/tonic bite); denture care (daily extraoral cleaning + mandatory overnight removal).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Narrative, not systematic review**: follows the Green et al. narrative-synthesis framework rather than PRISMA methodology — no registered protocol (no PROSPERO), no formal risk-of-bias assessment (e.g., no Newcastle-Ottawa/AMSTAR-2/Cochrane RoB tool applied to individual studies), and no GRADE certainty-of-evidence rating. Findings should be read as an expert qualitative synthesis, not a quantitatively pooled or bias-adjusted estimate.
- **No meta-analysis / no quantitative pooling**: prevalence and association statements (e.g., "more than twice as high," "significantly higher") are drawn from individual heterogeneous source studies rather than a pooled effect size with confidence intervals.
- **Heterogeneous evidence base**: included study designs span case reports/pilot studies to RCTs and reviews (only 2 of 27 are RCTs; 1 experimental study); conclusions on mechanistic links (e.g., periodontal pathogen CNS translocation, neuroinflammation) rely substantially on experimental/mechanistic rather than clinical-epidemiological evidence.
- Causality between periodontitis/oral infection and AD progression remains unestablished — authors explicitly frame the relationship as a shared-antecedent "syndemic" rather than bidirectional causal link, and call for further longitudinal studies to clarify mechanistic pathways between oral inflammation, neuroinflammation, and cognitive decline.
- **Future work** flagged by authors: longitudinal studies on oral-inflammation/neuroinflammation/cognition links; broader adoption of teledentistry for remote triage and caregiver education/support; earlier integration of dental assessment into the medical care pathway at AD diagnosis.

## 7. Glossary

- **AD (Alzheimer's Disease)**: leading cause of dementia worldwide; progressive irreversible cognitive decline, executive dysfunction, behavioral changes; ~5% prevalence at age 65–74, up to 33.4% at >85 years.
- **Syndemic**: synergistic co-occurrence of two or more conditions driven by shared antecedent/social determinants rather than one directly causing the other — the paper's proposed model for AD–oral disease co-occurrence.
- **Xerostomia / hyposalivation**: subjective dry-mouth sensation / objectively reduced salivary flow; in AD driven by both autonomic dysfunction and cumulative anticholinergic/xerostomic polypharmacy.
- **Root caries "third peak"**: epidemiological concept describing a distinct late-life surge of cariogenic activity on exposed root surfaces (after gingival recession/attachment loss), superimposed on the stabilized coronal-caries pattern of general adult populations.
- **ART (Atraumatic Restorative Treatment)**: minimally invasive restorative technique (hand instruments, fluoride-releasing materials) recommended for moderate-stage AD patients who cannot tolerate conventional rotary instrumentation.
- **SDF (Silver Diamine Fluoride)**: topical cariostatic agent used for non-restorative caries arrest, recommended in advanced-stage AD for palliative infection control without operative intervention.
- **Hand-over-hand technique**: caregiver guides the patient's hand while holding a toothbrush/denture, providing proprioceptive feedback that activates preserved procedural motor memory in patients with apraxia.
- **Denture stomatitis**: chronic inflammatory condition of denture-bearing mucosa, often Candida-associated; occurs >2x more frequently in AD/dementia patients than cognitively healthy older adults.
- **Apraxia**: loss of the ability to perform learned purposeful movements despite intact motor function — a key cognitive deficit the caregiver behavioral toolkit (hand-over-hand, chaining/priming) is designed to work around.
