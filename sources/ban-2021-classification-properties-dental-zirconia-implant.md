## Three-line Summary

Review (Materials 2021;14:4879) presenting the most comprehensive classification of dental zirconia: 12 types organized by four axes — yttria content (3Y/4Y/5Y), monochromatic vs. polychromatic color, uniform vs. hybrid composition, and monolayer vs. multilayer architecture — covering both implant fixture (ATZ, NanoZR) and superstructure applications.

ATZ (alumina-toughened zirconia) and NanoZR are optimal for implant fixtures requiring maximum mechanical strength; yttria-stabilized zirconia (3Y→5Y-TZP) covers superstructure needs; higher yttria = higher translucency + lower strength + lower LTD resistance; mirror-polished zirconia has least bacterial adhesion among dental ceramics, with soft tissue response superior to other ceramics.

Adequate selection requires matching yttria content (strength vs. esthetics priority) to clinical region and restoration design; zirconia fixtures achieve osseointegration comparable to titanium after surface modification (sandblasting + acid etching); LTD risk is clinically manageable even for 3Y-TZP under intraoral conditions.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First comprehensive 12-type classification of dental zirconia (yttria content × mono/polychromatic × uniform/hybrid × mono/multilayer)
- ATZ (alumina-toughened zirconia) and NanoZR: adequate for implant fixtures due to high mechanical strength despite poor esthetics
- Yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) for superstructures: sufficient esthetics, classified by Y content (3Y → 5Y)
- Established that higher yttria content = higher translucency + lower mechanical strength (confirmed independently by Kui 2023, Aboushelib 2026)
- Low temperature degradation (LTD/aging): decreases with yttria content; even 3Y has sufficient durability in oral conditions
- Mirror-polished zirconia: least bacterial adhesion + best soft tissue response among dental ceramics

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Study design:** Literature review (narrative)
- **Scope:** Zirconia as (1) implant fixtures and (2) implant superstructures — covers mechanical, esthetic, and biological properties plus surface modification for osseointegration
- **Topics:** Phase transformation, fracture strength, low-temperature degradation, antagonist wear, bacterial adhesion, surface treatments for fixtures

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**12-type classification framework:**
- Axis 1: Yttria content (3Y / 4Y / 5Y)
- Axis 2: Monochromatic vs Polychromatic (gradual color layers)
- Axis 3: Uniform vs Hybrid composition
- Axis 4: Monolayer vs Multilayer (gradient translucency/strength)

**Mechanical properties by yttria content:**
| Yttria | Flexural strength | Translucency | LTD resistance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3Y-TZP | Highest | Lowest | Good |
| 4Y-TZP | Medium | Medium | Moderate |
| 5Y-TZP | Lowest | Highest | Low |

**Fracture strength considerations:**
- Fracture strength of superstructures depends critically on occlusal contact region
- Mirror polishing: significantly reduces antagonist wear (zirconia is hardest dental material)

**Implant fixtures:**
- Surface modification required for osseointegration: roughening, activation, coating
- Sandblasting + acid etching most common for fixture surface

**Soft tissue/bacterial properties:**
- Mirror-polished zirconia: least bacterial adhesion + better soft tissue adhesion vs other ceramics

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative review — no systematic search
- Long-term in vivo fixture data limited relative to titanium
- Polychromatic/multilayer zirconia blocks: clinical longevity data still accumulating at time of publication
