## Abstract

Statement of the Problem: The application of digital impressions for complete-arch implant supported fixed dental prostheses (FDP) remains controversial, and data from a systematic review with meta-analysis comparing intraoral scanning (IOS) and stereophotogrammetry (SPG) remain limited.

Purpose: To evaluate and compare the accuracy of currently available digital technologies, specifically IOS and SPG, in capturing complete-arch implant impressions.

Materials and Methods: An electronic and manual search was conducted on May 4, 2024, across PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL databases following PRISMA guidelines. The search targeted studies (excluding case reports) that assessed the in vivo, in vitro, or ex vivo accuracy of IOS and SPG for complete-arch implant impressions. Two investigators screened eligible studies using the QUADAS-2 tool. Accuracy was the primary outcome, including linear, angular, surface deviations, and inter-implant distance. Three meta-analyses were performed on angular deviations trueness, surface deviations trueness, and surface deviations precision using a random-effect model.

Results: Thirteen studies (3 in vivo and 10 in vitro) met inclusion criteria, displaying methodological heterogeneity (8 analyzing surface, 3 linear, 8 angular, and 3 inter-implant distance deviations). The studies evaluated seven IOS (Aoralscan 3, Carestream 3600, iTero Element 2, iTero Element 5D, Primescan, Trios 3, and Trios 4) and two SPG devices (PIC and ICam4D). The number of implants ranged from 4 to 8 per arch. SPG showed higher accuracy compared to IOS in 10 out of 13 studies. IOS showed higher trueness and lower precision in one in vitro study, higher accuracy in one in vitro study, and comparable trueness results were detected by one in vivo study. Meta-analysis of angular deviation trueness (4 studies): Cohen's D = 1.20, p = 0.02, favoring SPG. Meta-analysis of surface deviation trueness (4 studies): effect size = 3.426, p < 0.05, favoring SPG. Meta-analysis of surface deviation precision (4 studies): effect size = 4.893, p < 0.05, favoring SPG. High heterogeneity (I² > 94%) was observed in all meta-analyses.

Conclusions: Within the limitations of this study, SPG demonstrated superior accuracy compared to IOS for complete-arch implant impressions in terms of trueness and precision for both angular and surface deviations. However, high heterogeneity and limited number of studies warrant cautious interpretation.

## Introduction

To deliver a passive fit screw-retained complete arch implant-supported fixed dental prostheses (FDP), an accurate implant impression is mandatory. The capability of recording the implant coordinates is defined by accuracy, composed of trueness and precision (ISO5725-1). Continuous technology improvements have made digital implant impressions a valid alternative to conventional impressions.

Two digital technologies are available for complete-arch implant impressions: intraoral optical surface scan (IOS) and extraoral stereophotogrammetry (SPG). The accuracy of IOS has been proven reliable for single crowns and short-span FDPs. However, the IOS 3D reconstruction algorithm, which acquires and stitches consecutive 3D images, is less efficient and potentially inaccurate in case of clinical scenarios with unstable reference points, long-span edentulous ridges, or completely edentulous arches.

SPG uses extraoral cameras to capture the position of scan bodies attached to implants, avoiding the stitching process inherent to IOS. Several recent studies have compared the accuracy of SPG and IOS, but their application for complete-arch digital implant impression remains controversial. This systematic review and meta-analysis assesses and compares the accuracy of IOS versus SPG for complete-arch implant impressions.

## Materials and Methods

### Search Strategy
- Databases: MEDLINE, PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL
- Search date: May 4, 2024 (electronic), May 20, 2024 (manual)
- Search terms: ("Photogrammetry" OR "stereophotogrammetry") AND ("implant dentistry" OR "Dental Prosthesis") AND ("Implant digital impression" OR "Intraoral...")
- PRISMA 2020 guidelines followed
- PROSPERO registration: CRD42024490844

### Inclusion Criteria
- English language
- Accuracy of complete-arch digital implant impressions with SPG and IOS
- Proper sample size calculation
- Published after 2018 (novelty of technologies)
- Outcomes: linear, angular, surface deviations, inter-implant distance
- In vivo, in vitro, or ex vivo studies

### Exclusion Criteria
- Duplicate data from same authors
- Systematic reviews, commentaries, letters, case reports, animal models
- Studies without proper reference scan methodology

### Quality Assessment
- QUADAS-2 tool for diagnostic accuracy studies
- Two independent investigators

### Statistical Analysis
- Random-effects model for meta-analysis
- Cohen's D as effect size
- Minimum 3 studies required for meta-analysis
- Heterogeneity: Q statistic, I² index
- Sensitivity analysis performed
- SPSS version 28

## Results

- 413 titles identified
- 341 excluded after duplicate removal and title screening
- 72 abstracts reviewed
- 59 full-text articles excluded
- **13 articles included for qualitative analysis** (3 in vivo, 10 in vitro)

### Qualitative Results
- **SPG superior to IOS in 10/13 studies**
- IOS superior in 1 in vitro study (Demirel 2023)
- IOS higher accuracy in 1 in vitro study (Revilla-León 2021)
- Comparable trueness in 1 in vivo study (Fu 2023 with prefabricated aids)

### Meta-analysis Results

#### Angular Deviation Trueness (4 in vitro studies)
- **Cohen's D = 1.20, p = 0.02** (favoring SPG)
- High heterogeneity: Q = 69.34, df = 2, p < 0.001, I² = 97%
- Sensitivity analysis: excluding Tohme 2021 outlier → significant, reduced heterogeneity

#### Surface Deviation (RMS) Trueness (4 in vitro studies)
- **Effect size = 3.426, p < 0.05** (favoring SPG)
- High heterogeneity: Q = 35.60, df = 2, p < 0.001, I² = 95%
- Required exclusion of Tohme 2021 for homogeneity

#### Surface Deviation (RMS) Precision (4 in vitro studies)
- **Effect size = 4.893, p < 0.05** (favoring SPG)
- High heterogeneity: Q = 42.87, df = 3, p < 0.001, I² = 98%

## Discussion

SPG consistently outperformed IOS in accuracy (trueness and precision) for complete-arch implant impressions across both in vitro and in vivo studies. The meta-analyses confirmed statistically significant differences favoring SPG for angular trueness, surface trueness, and surface precision.

Clinical implications: SPG may be preferred for complete-arch cases where passive fit is critical. However, IOS with prefabricated scan aids showed comparable results in one clinical study (Fu 2023). The high heterogeneity (I² > 94%) reflects variations in study design, devices, reference scanners, and analysis methods.

Limitations: High heterogeneity, small number of studies for meta-analysis (4 each), mostly in vitro data (only 3 in vivo), no publication bias tests due to few studies, reference scan methodology differences between in vitro (direct digitalization) and in vivo (indirect via models).

## Conclusions

SPG demonstrates superior accuracy compared to IOS for complete-arch digital implant impressions in terms of both trueness and precision for angular and surface deviations. However, high heterogeneity and limited evidence base warrant cautious interpretation. Further well-designed clinical studies are needed.
