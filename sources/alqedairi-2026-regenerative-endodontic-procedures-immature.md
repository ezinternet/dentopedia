## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Cureus 2026; PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science, 2004–2025) synthesizing the biological mechanisms (SCAP, scaffold, growth factors) and clinical outcomes of regenerative endodontic procedures (REP) in permanent necrotic immature teeth, including a representative-studies table spanning Banchs & Trope 2004 to Song 2025.

Periapical healing was 85–94%, continued root development 74–81%, and pulp sensibility recovery only 25–70% (least predictable); PRP/PRF scaffolds outperform blood clot alone for dentinal wall thickening; intracanal calcification develops in ~18–22% of treated teeth over the medium term.

Current REP outcomes represent "biologically supported repair" rather than true pulp regeneration (histological tissue is cementum/bone-like, not organized pulp-dentin complex); long-term data beyond 5 years are scarce, and agreed core outcome sets are lacking.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Consolidates the biological rationale for regenerative endodontic procedures (REPs) — stem cell sources (SCAP, DPSC, PDLSC, BM-MSC), scaffold biology (blood clot vs PRP/PRF vs i-PRF), and growth factor delivery (TGF-β, PDGF, VEGF, BMPs; EDTA-mediated dentin matrix release)
- Lays out a stepwise clinical protocol (patient selection, first-visit disinfection, second-visit scaffold induction and coronal seal) with rationale for each material choice (DAP over TAP, Biodentine over MTA for discoloration/handling)
- Synthesizes prognostic factors across two decades of evidence: age ≤12 years, preoperative root dimensions, infection duration, coronal seal quality, and scaffold choice
- Provides a representative-studies table spanning 2004 (Banchs & Trope) to 2025 (Song et al.), tracing the evolution of protocols and reported outcome ranges
- Frames current REP outcomes explicitly as "biologically supported repair" rather than true pulp regeneration, based on histological evidence

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative literature review, not a systematic review. Databases searched: PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, publication window 2004–2025. Search terms: regenerative endodontics, revascularization, immature permanent teeth, pulp regeneration, stem cells of the apical papilla, platelet-rich plasma, platelet-rich fibrin, apexification. No PRISMA flow diagram, no formal risk-of-bias/quality appraisal tool applied — the author explicitly flags this limitation.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Periapical healing: 85–94% across studies (comparable to apexification)
- Continued root development (apical closure, root lengthening, dentinal wall thickening): 74–81% of cases
- Pulp sensibility recovery: 25–70%, least predictable outcome, not a reliable proxy for true pulp regeneration
- PRP/PRF scaffolds show superior dentinal wall thickening vs blood clot alone in controlled trials
- Intracanal calcification develops in ~18–22% of treated teeth over the medium term
- Representative milestone studies cited: Banchs & Trope 2004 (n=2, case report); Bose et al. 2009 (n=16, retrospective, root lengthening 81%, sensibility recovery 56%); Jeeruphan et al. 2012 (n=45, retrospective, REP superior to apexification for root development); Kahler et al. 2014 (n=16, prospective, periapical healing 100%); Alagl et al. 2017 (n=40, RCT, PRP > blood clot for root length/wall thickness); Swaikat et al. 2023 (SR+MA of 32 studies, REP superior to apexification for root length and wall thickening, comparable periapical healing); Theekakul et al. 2024 (n=68, prospective, root development 79%, periapical healing 94%, calcification 18%); Song et al. 2025 (n=85, retrospective, periapical healing 91%, root development 76%, calcification 22%)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Author-stated limitations:
- Not a systematic review — no PRISMA, no formal risk-of-bias assessment
- Tissue formed inside REP-treated canals is histologically cementum-/bone-/PDL-like, not organized pulp-dentin complex; odontoblast-like cells lining canal walls are uncommon
- Radiographic "root development" may reflect cementum/bone deposition from periapical cells rather than true dentinogenesis
- Outcome definitions (apical closure, root development, success) are inconsistent across studies
- Most published work is retrospective with only 12–24 month follow-up; long-term data beyond 5 years are scarce
- Patient-reported outcomes rarely recorded
- Antibiotic paste use in young patients raises antimicrobial stewardship concerns
- PRP/PRF preparation requires centrifugation equipment and trained personnel not universally available

Future directions identified: direct delivery of defined SCAP populations rather than passive recruitment via bleeding; engineered scaffolds (injectable hydrogels, peptide-based matrices, chitosan-based nanofibrous scaffolds, self-assembling peptide nanofibers) for controlled growth-factor release; dental stem cell banking from exfoliated deciduous teeth; CBCT-based root-dimension measurement over periapical radiography; agreed core outcome sets for REP trials; multicenter prospective studies with ≥5-year follow-up as the most pressing evidence gap.

## 6. Related Work

- Banchs & Trope (2004) — foundational case report establishing the blood-clot revascularization concept
- Jeeruphan et al. (2012), Kahler et al. (2014), Alagl et al. (2017), Song et al. (2025) — cohort/RCT evidence base cited in the outcome synthesis
- Swaikat et al. (2023) — SR+MA of 32 studies comparing REP vs apexification, cited as the strongest synthesis of root-development superiority
- AAE and ESE position statements/guidelines recommending REP as treatment of choice for necrotic immature permanent teeth

## 7. Glossary

- **REP**: Regenerative Endodontic Procedure
- **SCAP**: Stem Cells of the Apical Papilla
- **DPSC**: Dental Pulp Stem Cells
- **PDLSC**: Periodontal Ligament Stem Cells
- **BM-MSC**: Bone Marrow-derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells
- **PRP**: Platelet-Rich Plasma
- **PRF**: Platelet-Rich Fibrin
- **i-PRF**: Injectable Platelet-Rich Fibrin
- **TAP**: Triple Antibiotic Paste
- **DAP**: Double Antibiotic Paste (ciprofloxacin + metronidazole)
- **MTA**: Mineral Trioxide Aggregate
- **AAE**: American Association of Endodontists
- **ESE**: European Society of Endodontology
- **CEJ**: Cementoenamel Junction
