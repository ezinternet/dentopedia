## Why Ingested
임플란트 "성공(success)"의 정의·기준을 다룬 초기 체계적 문헌고찰로, 위키가 이미 보유한 정의 기준 문서([[wiki/implants/survival/misch-2008-implant-success-survival-failure-icoi-pisa]], [[wiki/implants/survival/misch-1998-implant-quality-scale-health-disease-continuum]])의 근거층을 채운다. 성공기준을 임플란트·연조직·보철·환자만족 4수준으로 나눠 정리한 최초 SR로, 생존율 수치를 비교하는 후속 연구들의 기준선 역할을 한다.

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review (MEDLINE/PubMed) of RCTs and prospective studies on roughened-surface implants with ≥5-year follow-up, analyzing success at implant, peri-implant soft-tissue, prosthetic, and patient-satisfaction levels.
The most frequently reported implant-level success criteria were mobility, pain, radiolucency and peri-implant bone loss (>1.5 mm); soft-tissue criteria were suppuration and bleeding; prosthetic criteria were technical complications/prosthetic maintenance, adequate function and esthetics; patient criteria were discomfort/paresthesia, satisfaction with appearance, and ability to chew/taste.
Implant success should ideally be judged on a long-term primary outcome of the implant-prosthetic complex as a whole rather than on implant-level criteria alone.

## 세줄요약
체계적 문헌고찰 (MEDLINE/PubMed) — 거친 표면(roughened surface) 임플란트, 최소 5년 추적 RCT·전향 연구를 대상으로 임플란트·주위 연조직·보철·환자만족 4수준에서 성공기준을 분석.
가장 빈번히 보고된 임플란트 수준 성공기준은 동요도(mobility)·통증·방사선 투과상·주위 골소실(>1.5 mm)이었고, 연조직은 화농(suppuration)·출혈, 보철은 기술적 합병증/보철 유지관리·기능·심미, 환자만족은 불편감/감각이상·외모 만족·저작/미각 능력이었다.
임플란트 성공은 임플란트 단독 기준이 아니라 임플란트-보철 복합체 전체의 장기적 일차 결과로 평가해야 한다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First systematic attempt to catalogue and structure the criteria actually used to define implant "success" across the literature.
- Splits success into four evaluation levels — implant, peri-implant soft tissue, prosthetic, and patient satisfaction — rather than the single implant-level outcome traditionally used.
- Reports the most frequently used criteria per level (mobility, pain, radiolucency, bone loss >1.5 mm at implant level; suppuration, bleeding at soft-tissue level; technical complications, function, esthetics at prosthetic level).
- Argues that success should be evaluated as a long-term primary outcome of the implant-prosthetic complex as a whole.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review (no meta-analysis).
- **Databases**: MEDLINE/PubMed electronic search.
- **Inclusion criteria**: randomized controlled trials and prospective studies reporting implant dentistry outcomes; roughened-surface implants only; at least 5-year follow-up.
- **Outcomes**: frequency of success criteria reported at the implant, peri-implant soft-tissue, prosthetic, and patient-satisfaction levels.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Implant level** (most frequent criteria): mobility, pain, radiolucency, peri-implant bone loss (>1.5 mm).
- **Peri-implant soft-tissue level**: suppuration, bleeding.
- **Prosthetic level**: occurrence of technical complications / prosthetic maintenance, adequate function, esthetics over the 5-year period.
- **Patient-satisfaction level**: discomfort and paresthesia, satisfaction with appearance, ability to chew/taste.
- No pooled survival/success percentage is reported in the abstract (abstract-only retrieval).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Abstract-only retrieval — the full body (search count, included-study number, risk-of-bias handling) is not available in this PDF.
- The review is restricted to roughened-surface implants with ≥5-year follow-up, so it does not describe criteria used in short-term or machined-surface studies.
- Heterogeneity of success definitions across studies limits quantitative pooling (implied by the descriptive design).

## 7. Glossary
- **Success (implant dentistry)**: an outcome state judged by multiple criteria (mobility, pain, radiolucency, bone loss, etc.), distinct from mere survival (implant still in place).
- **Peri-implant bone loss**: radiographic crestal bone level change around an implant; >1.5 mm was the most frequently applied threshold in this review.
- **Roughened-surface implant**: implant with a textured (e.g., SLA/TiO-blasted) surface, as opposed to machined; an inclusion criterion here.
- **Prosthetic maintenance**: technical complications and repairs required over the follow-up period; a prosthetic-level success criterion.
- **Paresthesia**: altered sensation (e.g., in the lower lip) reported as a patient-satisfaction criterion alongside discomfort.
