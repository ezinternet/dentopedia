## Why Ingested

No held paper evaluated the "hybrid modification" of metal-resin prostheses with zirconia molars/quadrants (Zrm-ISFP) as a cost-effective middle ground between full metal-resin and full zirconia full-arch prostheses. This provides 5-year RCT-quality comparative evidence showing that adding zirconia molars significantly reduces acrylic tooth fractures while maintaining the repairability and cost benefits of metal-resin design.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective comparison of 29 bimaxillary ISFP patients (14 Zrm-ISFP with zirconia molars, 15 r-ISFP without) over 5 years; PRTC incidence, implant/prosthesis survival, MBL, and temporal distribution of complications (−1yr, 1–3yr, 3–5yr) compared.

100% implant and prosthesis survival in both groups; acrylic-tooth fracture (p<0.001) and repair (p=0.015) significantly lower in Zrm-ISFP; PRTC count: r-ISFP −1yr:9/1–3yr:23/3–5yr:34 vs Zrm-ISFP −1yr:2/1–3yr:3/3–5yr:7; rebasing, cleaning, screw hole repair, MBL, implant complications NS.

Zirconia molar modification reduces acrylic tooth complications by >4× over 5yr while preserving metal-resin repairability and cost advantages; a practical hybrid solution between metal-acrylic and full-zirconia.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First 5-year comparative data for Zrm-ISFP (metal-resin + zirconia molars) vs standard r-ISFP
- Strong PRTC reduction: 34 vs 7 total events at 3–5yr post-loading (r-ISFP vs Zrm-ISFP)
- Confirms that zirconia molar modification does not affect implant/prosthesis survival, MBL, or structural complications
- Temporal analysis shows r-ISFP complication burden accelerates over time (9→23→34), while Zrm-ISFP stays flat (2→3→7)
- Practical implementation: zirconia in molar quadrants only, not full-arch — cost savings + repairability preserved
