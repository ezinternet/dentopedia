## Why Ingested

Mayo Clinic 14-year cohort (8,540 implants) that tests patient factors, **surgical manipulations**, and systemic conditions/medications together in one Cox model for first-year implant failure — the largest single-institution test of the surgical-manipulation axis (bone augmentation, socket preservation, xenogenic material, alveoloplasty) held here, and a strong null result for systemic conditions and medications after adjustment. Reinforces the grafted/prepared-site signal in [[implants/survival/yari-2023-risk-factors-early-implant-failure]] and [[implants/survival/clauser-2022-early-implant-failure-grafted]], and the surgical-complication-to-failure link in [[implants/survival/krisam-2019-factors-affecting-the-early-failure]]. J Prosthodont is paywalled (not in PMC) → abstract-based (abstract-only).

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-institution Cox-regression cohort (Mayo Clinic, 2000–2014; 8,540 implants) analyzing first-year (early) implant failure against patient factors, preplacement surgical manipulations, and systemic conditions/medications.

362 implants (4.2%) failed within the first year at a mean of 129 (SD 96) days; bone augmentation only (HR 1.45, 95% CI 1.02–2.05, p=0.04), socket preservation (HR 2.67, 95% CI 1.33–5.38, p=0.006), and xenogenic material (HR 2.12, 95% CI 1.11–4.04, p=0.02) raised risk, alveoloplasty only at placement lowered it (HR 0.33, 95% CI 0.17–0.65, p=0.001), and any surgical complication was overwhelmingly associated with failure (HR 15.84, 95% CI 11.10–22.61, p<0.001).

After adjustment for age, sex, and implant era, no single or multiple medical condition(s) or medication(s) increased first-year failure risk; the authors recommend targeting surgical complications to reduce early failure, but abstract-only ingest prevents verifying model details and complication definitions.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Largest held single-institution test of **preplacement surgical manipulations** as early-failure risk factors, with effect sizes (HR + 95% CI + p) for bone augmentation, socket preservation, xenogenic material, and alveoloplasty.
- Quantifies the association between **any first-year surgical complication and early implant failure** (HR 15.84) — the dominant signal in the model and the authors' stated prevention target.
- Reports a largely **null result for systemic conditions and medications** after adjustment for age, sex, and implant era — notable against the many single-factor cohort claims elsewhere in this literature.
- Provides an era-controlled design (implant "era" adjustment) across a 2000–2014 window.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: retrospective cohort with survival (time-to-event) analysis.
- Setting: Department of Dental Specialties, Mayo Clinic; consecutive adult patients with dental implants seen 2000–2014.
- Data sources: institutional database + individual medical records.
- Analysis: Cox proportional hazards regression; associations summarized as hazard ratio (HR) with 95% confidence interval (CI).
- Outcome: first implant failure within the first year post-implantation (early failure).
- n: 8,540 implants identified.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Early failure: 362/8,540 implants (4.2%) within the first year, mean (SD) 129 (96) days after placement.
- On univariate analysis, most candidate predictors did **not** influence first-year failure.
- Preplacement surgical manipulations associated with **increased** early failure:
  - Bone augmentation only — HR 1.45 (95% CI 1.02–2.05), p=0.04
  - Socket preservation — HR 2.67 (95% CI 1.33–5.38), p=0.006
  - Xenogenic material — HR 2.12 (95% CI 1.11–4.04), p=0.02
- Associated with **decreased** early failure:
  - Alveoloplasty only at placement — HR 0.33 (95% CI 0.17–0.65), p=0.001
- Surgical complications within the first year: 318 implants (3.7%), mean (SD) 110 (114) days after placement; any surgical complication was significantly associated with early implant failure — HR 15.84 (95% CI 11.10–22.61), p<0.001.
- After adjustment for age, sex, and implant era: **no** single or multiple medical condition(s) and **no** single or multiple medication(s) increased first-year failure risk.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single-institution (Mayo Clinic) retrospective cohort; tertiary-referral case mix may not generalize.
- **Abstract-only ingest** — full model details (covariate list, multivariable vs univariate reporting, complication definitions, event counts per subgroup) not verifiable here.
- The dominant complication→failure association (HR 15.84) is not causal: complications and failure may share upstream causes (poor bone quality, infection), and complication as a time-varying covariate within the same year as failure risks reverse causation.
- Systemic-condition null must be read against the specific conditions/medications and coding available in the record; absence of association is not proof of no effect.

## 6. Related Work

- Grafted/prepared-site early-failure risk (Yari 2023 multivariate cohort; Clauser 2022 SR+MA pooling OR 1.50 for grafted sites) — consistent direction with this study's augmentation/socket-preservation signals.
- Surgical-complication-driven early failure (Krisam 2019; general early-failure risk models in the same literature).
- Conventional risk-factor syntheses for early implant failure (Chrcanovic 2016; Manzano 2016) — this study's systemic null contrasts with their positive systemic signals.

## 7. Glossary

- **Early implant failure**: in this study, implant failure within the first year after placement.
- **Socket preservation**: ridge-preservation grafting of an extraction socket at/after extraction.
- **Xenogenic material**: graft material of non-human (usually bovine/porcine) origin.
- **Alveoloplasty**: surgical reshaping/smoothing of the alveolar ridge, here performed at implant placement.
- **Cox proportional hazards regression**: survival model estimating hazard ratios for time-to-event outcomes.
- **HR (hazard ratio)**: ratio of instantaneous failure hazard between groups; HR >1 = increased risk.
