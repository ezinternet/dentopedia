## Why Ingested

Ingested because it is the **large-n preoperative CBCT source** behind the single most-repeated
anatomical claim in this wiki's immediate-implant pages — "the facial bone wall in the anterior
maxilla is thin" — and because the two Chappuis pages that repeat it
([[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2013-ridge-alterations-post-extraction-esthetic]],
[[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2017-dimensional-bone-soft-tissue-post-extraction]])
**cite this paper as one of their sources** rather than reproducing it, so the wiki currently holds
its conclusions without holding the study. This page restores that primary datum in the folder that
routes it ([[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment]], whose scope covers "labial/buccal plate
thickness … anatomy-only datasets … with no site-specific anchorage technique or implant outcome"),
and it sits upstream of the folder's existing intraoperative counterpart
[[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/huynh-ba-2010-analysis-socket-bone-wall]] (same senior
author, Lindhe; 93 extraction sites measured at the moment of extraction vs 250 intact dentitions
measured preoperatively).

**Citation-direction check (verified against the PDFs already in `papers/`).** Three held pages

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional CBCT morphometric study (n = 250 subjects aged 17–66, all maxillary front teeth present, iCAT unit, Clin Oral Implants Res 2011) measuring anterior maxillary facial bone-wall thickness at 1, 3 and 5 mm apical to the facial bone crest, plus the distance from the cemento-enamel junction (CEJ) to that crest.

The CEJ-to-facial-crest distance ranged **1.6–3 mm**, and the facial bone wall was **≤1 mm thick at most measured locations in every tooth site examined**, with **close to 50% of sites at ≤0.5 mm**.

Because such a thin wall may undergo marked dimensional diminution after extraction, facial bone thickness must be assessed before tooth removal and before planning rehabilitation in the anterior maxilla — but the record is abstract-only: no per-tooth breakdown, no dispersion or p-values, no implant outcome.

## 세줄요약

횡단적 CBCT(Cone-Beam Computed Tomography) 형태계측 연구 (n = 250명, 17–66세, 상악 전치 모두 보유, iCAT 장비, Clin Oral Implants Res 2011) — 전방 상악의 순측골벽(facial bone wall) 두께를 순측골융기(apical) 1·3·5 mm 위치에서 계측하고, 치아경계연결부(Cemento-Enamel Junction, CEJ)~순측골융기 간 거리도 함께 측정.

CEJ~순측골융기 거리는 1.6–3 mm였고, 순측골벽은 조사한 **모든** 치아 부위의 대부분 위치에서 **1 mm 이하**였으며 **약 50% 부위는 0.5 mm 이하**에 머물렀다.

이러한 박벽은 발치 후 치수의 뚜렷한 감소를 겪을 수 있으므로 발치 전, 그리고 전방 상악 보철 재활 계획 전에 순측벽 두께를 반드시 평가해야 한다 — 단, 전문 미확보 상태로 부위별 내역·표준편차·p값·임플란트 성적은 보고되지 않는다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **A prevalence statement, not a mean.** The headline is that the facial wall is ≤1 mm "in most
  locations in all tooth sites examined" and ≤0.5 mm at close to half of sites — i.e. *how often* an
  anterior site fails a thickness threshold, which a mean alone cannot express.
- **Unusual n for this question.** 250 subjects in one CBCT dataset, an order of magnitude larger
  than the other anterior-maxilla morphometric series held in this folder (93 extraction sites in
  `huynh-ba-2010`; 39 patients in `chappuis-2013`).
- **Three fixed apical levels (1, 3, 5 mm below the crest)** rather than a single level, so the
  gradient of thinning with depth is part of the design — although the abstract reports the
  thickness conclusion only in aggregate, not per level.
- **A second, separate measurement: CEJ-to-crest distance (1.6–3 mm)**, the supracrestal
  tissue-attachment dimension, which none of the buccal-plate-thickness papers in this folder
  report alongside thickness.
- **Explicit transfer of the anatomy to a planning rule.** The conclusion is not merely descriptive:
  the thin wall "must be considered before tooth removal and the planning of rehabilitation in the
  anterior segment of the dentition in the maxilla" — i.e. preoperative CBCT morphometry as
  immediate-implant case selection.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional CBCT morphometric survey — single timepoint, no intervention, no
  comparator, no follow-up, no outcome.
- **n**: **250 subjects**, aged **17–66 years**, all of whom had **all maxillary front teeth
  present**. Written informed consent was obtained. Whether 250 subjects corresponds to 250 measured
  sites or more (the abstract notes the scan "included all tooth and edentulous sites in the
  dentition") is **not stated**.
- **Setting/population**: not stated beyond the age range and the intact-anterior-dentition
  inclusion criterion; no country, center or ethnicity is given in the abstract.
- **Imaging**: cone-beam CT on the **iCAT** unit; images acquired with iCAT software and processed
  on a computer. Voxel size and any dose parameters are **not stated** — which matters, because
  sub-millimetre wall thickness sits at the resolution limit of many CBCT protocols.
- **Measurements (two)**:
  1. the **distance between the CEJ and the facial bone crest**;
  2. the **thickness of the facial bone wall**, assessed at **three positions relative to the facial
     bone crest — 1, 3 and 5 mm apical to the crest**.
- **Outcomes**: purely morphometric. **No implant outcome of any kind** is reported — no survival,
  no marginal bone level, no recession, no esthetic score.
- **Databases**: N/A (primary clinical study; no search component).
- **Statistics**: **none reported.** The abstract contains no test, no p-value, no mean, no standard
  deviation and no confidence interval; results are given as ranges and proportions only.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance CEJ → facial bone crest | **1.6–3 mm** |
| Facial bone wall thickness — most locations, all tooth sites | **≤1 mm** |
| Sites with facial bone wall ≤0.5 mm | **close to 50%** |
| Measurement levels | 1, 3 and 5 mm apical to the facial bone crest |
| n | 250 subjects (17–66 years, all maxillary front teeth present) |

- **The thin-wall finding is arch-wide, not site-specific**: the abstract states ≤1 mm holds "in
  most locations in **all** tooth sites examined", i.e. no tooth position in the anterior maxilla
  escapes it.
- **Half the sites are thinner than 0.5 mm**, so the "≤1 mm" figure is a floor on the problem, not a
  ceiling — the thinnest half of sites have essentially no facial plate to preserve.
- **Position within the tooth**: the conclusion is stated for "most tooth sites in the anterior
  maxilla" and is not broken down by level (1 vs 3 vs 5 mm) in the abstract, so whether the wall
  thins further apically **cannot be stated from this record**.
- **Clinical inference the authors draw**: a wall this thin "may undergo marked dimensional
  diminution following tooth extraction", hence the pre-extraction planning rule.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only record.** Full text not retrieved; per-tooth and per-level breakdowns, dispersion,
  statistics, voxel size and reliability are unrecoverable from what was ingested.
- **Anatomy only, no outcome.** The paper predicts dimensional loss; it does not measure it. No
  extraction cohort, no follow-up scan, no implant placed — the causal chain from "thin wall" to
  "post-extraction resorption" is asserted from prior literature, not tested here.
- **The ≤1 mm / ≤0.5 mm thresholds are descriptors, not decision rules.** The paper identifies how
  thin walls usually are; it does not test any minimum thickness against an outcome, so it cannot
  license "therefore augment".
- **Sub-millimetre measurement against finite CBCT resolution.** Half the sites measure ≤0.5 mm while
  the voxel size is unreported, so the precision of the thinnest measurements — exactly the values
  that drive the conclusion — cannot be appraised from the abstract.
- **Population and selection undescribed.** No country, center, sex distribution, reason for tooth
  loss or inclusion/exclusion criteria beyond "all maxillary front teeth present"; facial wall
  thickness is known to vary by population and by reason of extraction, and none of that is
  recoverable here.
- **Sites vs subjects unresolved.** The scan covered all tooth *and* edentulous sites in the
  dentition, so the effective sample size behind the proportions is ambiguous from the abstract.

- **Cited by** [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2017-dimensional-bone-soft-tissue-post-extraction]] —

  ≤1 mm" from an independent sample. (AlTarawneh lists this paper among its references; the
  *measurement* is still an independent cohort, which is what `reinforces` requires.)
- **Reinforces** [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/huynh-ba-2010-analysis-socket-bone-wall]] —
  the intraoperative counterpart in the same folder: 87% of anterior buccal walls ≤1 mm measured

  dentitions preoperatively. Different cohort, different modality, different time point, concordant
  prevalence. **Caveat:** Lindhe J is the last author of both, so they are same-lab rather than
  fully independent — the corroboration is of the *number* across two distinct datasets, not of an
  untouched replication.
- **Pooled norms** [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/shafizadeh-2021-alveolar-bone-thickness-healthy-sr]] —
  that SR+MA reports anterior buccal plates averaging ≤1 mm with thickness increasing posteriorly;
  this page supplies the preoperative prevalence distribution behind that mean. Kept in prose: a
  pooled estimate is not an independent confirmation of one of its contributing datasets.
- **Cited by** [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/dossantos-2019-buccal-bone-root-inclination-alveolar-cbct]] —
  verified: that paper attributes "a thinner buccal bone wall will lead to a more severe" resorption
  to Tomasi and to "Januário et al."; it then measures buccal thickness against root inclination in

- **Outcome side of the same question** [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/yang-2019-labial-bone-thickness-esthetics-iipp]] —

## 7. Glossary

- **Facial bone wall (facial / buccal plate)**: the thin facial cortical plate of the alveolar bone
  bounding the tooth socket on the lip/cheek side — here the structure whose thickness is the object
  of the study.
- **Cemento-enamel junction (CEJ)**: the line where the enamel cap meets the root cementum; used
  here as the fixed reference point from which the distance to the facial bone crest was measured.
- **Bone crest (facial bone crest)**: the most coronal edge of the facial alveolar plate; the second
  reference point, and the origin of the three apical measurement levels.
- **Apical / coronal**: toward the root apex (apical, deeper) vs toward the crown/gingiva (coronal,
  more superficial). "1, 3, 5 mm apical to the crest" = three depths below the crestal edge.
- **Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT)**: volumetric 3D X-ray imaging used here on the iCAT unit;
  the modality shared by nearly every other page in this folder, which is why it is the natural
  preoperative tool for this measurement.
- **Facial bone crest distance from the CEJ (1.6–3 mm)**: the supracrestal tissue-attachment
  dimension — the band of soft tissue and supporting bone between the crestal edge and the CEJ.
- **Morphometry**: single-timepoint measurement of anatomical dimensions; the design of this paper,
  which yields prevalence statements but no outcome data.
