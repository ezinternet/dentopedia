## Three-line Summary

Three-month prospective service evaluation at Liverpool University Dental Hospital: 26 patients on DOAC or antiplatelet therapy assessed, 24 of whom underwent extractions (most three or fewer routine extractions).

Patient-knowledge gaps dominated the findings — 6 of 26 patients had received no initial information about their medication, only 11 of 26 were aware of its bleeding risks, and 3 patients altered their own regimen against clinician advice.

Bleeding outcomes were unremarkable at this scale (4 patients with minor day-one bleeding, all self-managed; none beyond day one), so the reportable signal is the education and documentation gap, not any bleeding rate.

핵심은 환자 지식 공백: 26명 중 6명은 약에 대한 초기 설명을 전혀 못 받았고, 출혈 위험을 인지한 사람은 26명 중 11명뿐이었으며, 3명은 술자 지시를 거슬러 스스로 복약을 변경했다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Adds the **patient-side knowledge dimension** to a literature that is almost entirely clinician-facing: what the patient actually understood about their own DOAC/antiplatelet medication before extraction.
- Documents a concrete safety hazard — **3 patients self-altered their anticoagulant/antiplatelet regimen against clinician advice**, an event class that discontinuation-vs-continuation protocol studies structurally cannot observe.
- Frames the intervention target as **multimodal information delivery and robust documentation**, rather than a further refinement of the drug-interruption protocol.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: prospective service evaluation (audit-type), not a controlled study; no control group and no randomisation.
- **Setting/period**: Liverpool University Dental Hospital, three months.
- **Participants**: 26 patients on anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy; 24 proceeded to extraction. Most needed three or fewer routine extractions.
- **Data collected**: patient demographics, DOAC/antiplatelet agent, treatment plan, and the clinician's recorded advice on peri-operative medication management.
- **Patient measure**: questionnaire on understanding of their own medication (whether they had been given information; whether they were aware of bleeding risk) — self-reported.
- **Follow-up**: telephone contact on day 1 and day 5 post-extraction to capture complications.
- **Haemostasis**: local haemostatic measures used; the authors report these as effective. Specific agents/techniques are not enumerated in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

All figures are raw counts out of 26 assessed / 24 extracted. They are deliberately **not** expressed as percentages — at this n, a percentage implies a precision the design cannot support and invites misquotation as a rate.

- **Underwent extractions**: 24 of 26 patients; most required three or fewer routine extractions.
- **No initial information about their medication**: 6 patients.
- **Aware of the bleeding risk of their medication**: only 11 patients.
- **Altered their regimen against clinician advice**: 3 patients.
- **Minor day-one bleeding**: 4 patients, all self-managed.
- **Bleeding beyond day one**: none reported.
- **Authors' conclusion**: enhanced patient education, guideline adherence, and robust documentation are the levers for improving outcomes in this group; local haemostatic measures minimised complications.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Single UK dental-hospital service evaluation** — findings describe one unit's practice and patient population, not a generalisable estimate.
- **n=26** (24 extracted): every count is small; no figure here should be converted to a rate or pooled with cohort data.
- **No control group** and no comparator arm (e.g. continued vs interrupted therapy).
- **Self-reported patient understanding**, collected by questionnaire — subject to recall and social-desirability effects, and not validated against what was actually said at the consultation.
- **Not designed or powered to estimate bleeding incidence**; the day-1/day-5 telephone follow-up captures complications for service-improvement purposes only.
- DOAC and antiplatelet patients are analysed together, so drug-class-specific inference is not available.
- Future work implied by the authors: multimodal patient information delivery and improved documentation, with re-audit.

## 6. Related Work

- Prospective DOAC extraction cohorts (e.g. Izzetti et al. 2024) establish that bleeding is manageable when therapy is continued with local haemostasis — but assess the clinician's protocol, not the patient's comprehension.
- Systematic reviews of anticoagulation in dental practice (e.g. Inchingolo et al. 2024) synthesise guideline positions whose real-world adherence this evaluation audits.
- Local haemostasis reviews inform the measures credited here with minimising complications.
- Clinician-survey work on anticoagulant management addresses the same guideline-adherence question from the practitioner side; this paper is its patient-side counterpart.

## 7. Glossary

- **DOAC (Direct Oral Anticoagulant, 직접경구항응고제)**: anticoagulants directly inhibiting a single coagulation factor — dabigatran (thrombin/factor IIa), rivaroxaban/apixaban/edoxaban (factor Xa).
- **Antiplatelet therapy (항혈소판요법)**: agents inhibiting platelet aggregation (e.g. aspirin, clopidogrel); a distinct mechanism from anticoagulation, often grouped with it in bleeding-risk protocols.
- **Service evaluation (진료 서비스 평가)**: a UK NHS audit-family design assessing current care against standards within one service. It is not a research study — no hypothesis test, no control group, no power calculation.
- **Local haemostatic measures (국소 지혈 조치)**: socket-level bleeding control — atraumatic technique, sutures, oxidised cellulose or gelatin sponge, pressure packs, tranexamic acid mouthwash.
- **Multimodal information delivery (다중매체 정보 제공)**: giving the same medication guidance through more than one channel (verbal, written leaflet, digital) to improve retention.
