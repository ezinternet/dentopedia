## Three-line Summary

Study evaluating the diagnostic performance of artificial intelligence (AI) language models for cracked tooth syndrome (CTS) across 50 clinical scenarios, comparing AI outputs to established clinical diagnostic criteria.

AI models demonstrated variable performance across the 50 scenarios; specific accuracy metrics suggest AI can assist in CTS diagnosis but does not yet match expert clinical judgment in complex or atypical presentations.

The study highlights both the potential and current limitations of AI in CTS diagnosis, suggesting AI as a supplementary decision-support tool rather than a standalone diagnostic system.

## 세줄요약

AI 언어모델의 균열치증후군(Cracked Tooth Syndrome, CTS) 진단 성능을 50개 임상 시나리오에서 평가한 연구; AI 출력값을 확립된 임상 진단 기준과 비교.

AI 모델은 50개 시나리오에서 가변적 성능을 보였으며, 특히 복잡·비전형 증례에서 전문가 임상 판단에 미치지 못함.

AI는 CTS 진단 보조 의사결정 지원 도구로 잠재력이 있으나 독립적 진단 시스템으로는 현재 한계가 있음을 결론.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First study evaluating AI language model performance specifically for CTS diagnosis using standardized clinical scenarios
- 50-scenario benchmark provides reproducible evaluation framework
- Characterizes AI strengths (pattern recognition in typical presentations) and limitations (complex/atypical cases)
- Frames AI as decision-support tool, not replacement for clinical examination
- Provides foundation for future AI-assisted CTS diagnostic tools

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Design: Experimental performance evaluation study
- AI models evaluated: Large language models (specific models not specified in abstract)
- Benchmark: 50 standardized CTS clinical scenarios
- Reference standard: Established CTS diagnostic criteria (presumably AAE/ESE guidelines)
- Outcome: AI diagnostic agreement with reference standard per scenario

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- AI models: variable performance across 50 scenarios
- AI performs better on typical/classic presentations; worse on complex/atypical cases
- Specific accuracy metrics (sensitivity, specificity, agreement %) not extractable from abstract
- Conclusion: AI = supplementary decision support, not standalone diagnostic system

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Abstract-only: specific AI model names, accuracy values, and scenario distributions not available
- Reference standard may vary by reviewer — CTS diagnosis is inherently observer-dependent
- 50 scenarios: adequate for proof-of-concept but limited generalizability
- Rapidly evolving AI landscape — study AI models may already be outdated at publication
