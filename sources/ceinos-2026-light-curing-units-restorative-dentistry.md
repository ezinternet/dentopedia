## Why Ingested
First comprehensive SANRA-compliant narrative review (2026) to address LCU performance, selection criteria, and emerging optical functions as a unified clinical framework — fills a gap alongside existing RCT/in-vitro literature on individual parameters. Provides authoritative guidance on why manufacturer-reported irradiance is insufficient and how polywave 3rd-generation LED LCUs should be selected.
→ [[resin/lehmann-2026-distance-exposure-mode-degree-conversion-rbc]]

## Three-line Summary
Line 1: SANRA-guided narrative review of PubMed/MEDLINE (n=181 references, January 2000–February 2026) synthesising LCU technology evolution, selection criteria, and adjunctive optical features across third-generation polywave LED, monowave LED, QTH, PAC, and laser systems.
Line 2: Polywave 3rd-generation LED LCUs outperform monowave devices for mixed Norrish type I/II photoinitiator composites (modest but consistent advantage in degree-of-conversion and hardness meta-analyses); curing adequacy depends on delivered radiant exposure, spectral match, beam homogeneity, and effective tip diameter — not on manufacturer-stated irradiance; clinical factors (distance, angulation, maintenance, battery state, training) often override device-generation effects; intrapulpal temperature threshold of 5.5 °C not exceeded by ≤2–3 standard curing cycles; fluorescence/transillumination adjuncts have insufficient independent evidence.
Line 3: Clinicians should select third-generation polywave LED LCUs as the conservative default for routine practice, validate delivered radiant exposure (not nominal irradiance), and extend curing time rather than rely on "boost" modes; integrated diagnostic functions (fluorescence, NIR, beam-shaping) are appealing but await independent validation.

## Key Contributions
- Establishes that **radiant exposure (J/cm²)** — not manufacturer-stated irradiance (mW/cm²) — is the clinically meaningful parameter for LCU evaluation
- Documents that high-irradiance "boost" modes may deliver *less* total energy to the restoration than standard modes at recommended exposure times
- Confirms third-generation polywave LED LCUs as the most versatile default for mixed Norrish type I/II photoinitiator systems
- Clarifies that for camphorquinone-dominant materials, well-maintained 2nd-generation monowave LEDs perform comparably if radiant exposure is adequate
- Details safety thresholds: intrapulpal ΔT ≤5.5 °C, not exceeded by ≤2–3 standard polywave curing cycles; air syringe cooling effective
- Highlights beam homogeneity and effective tip diameter as critical variables invisible to conventional dental radiometers
- Identifies clinical determinants (distance 2–4 mm → significant irradiance drop; angulation; maintenance; battery state; training) as often more decisive than device generation
- Reviews "no-finishing concept" and "multiluting concept" protocols with preheated viscous resin composites, noting adequate conversion achievable under ≥2–3 mm restorations
- Flags independent evidence gap for integrated fluorescence, NIR transillumination, and beam-shaping accessories

- **Design**: Clinically-oriented narrative review; SANRA quality checklist applied
- **Database**: PubMed/MEDLINE; single database; January 2000–February 2026
- **References**: n=181 final (128 structured + 53 citation tracking/expert)
- **Three search equations**: (1) LCU technology evolution; (2) device selection & clinical optimization; (3) adjunctive optical features

- **Limitations**: Single database, English-language only, single screener, no formal RoB assessment, evidence predominantly in vitro

## Key Results
- **Photoinitiator absorption peaks**: CQ ~468 nm (Norrish type II); TPO ~380 nm, PPD ~410 nm, Ivocerin 400–420 nm (Norrish type I) — polywave LCU required for type I activation
- **Depth penetration**: Violet wavelengths penetrate less deeply than blue due to Rayleigh scattering — relevant for bulkfill/deep cavities
- **Meta-analytic support**: Recent SR/MAs support modest polywave advantage in degree-of-conversion and hardness for mixed photoinitiator composites
- **Thermal safety**: ΔT ≤5.5 °C threshold; protected by remaining dentin thickness, pulpal microvascularization; not exceeded by ≤2–3 standard polywave curing cycles; air syringe reduces risk
- **Luting protocol**: Adequate conversion under restorations ≥2–3 mm achievable with prolonged irradiation; beyond this range predictability is material-dependent
- **Clinical variables outweigh generation**: Distance, angulation, maintenance (debris/chips), battery state, operator training are primary determinants of delivered energy
- **Radiometers**: Conventional dental radiometers cannot detect spatial beam inhomogeneity — periodic relative comparison more useful than absolute irradiance reading

## Limitations
- Single PubMed database; English only; temporal restriction to 2000–2026
- Single-author screening — potential selection/interpretation bias
- Sections on adhesive luting and adjunctive features heavily rely on citation snowballing and expert knowledge (lower reproducibility)
- No formal risk-of-bias assessment; evidence predominantly in vitro
- No long-term clinical outcome data for multifunctional LCUs

## Glossary
- **Radiant exposure (J/cm²)**: Total light energy delivered per unit area — the clinically meaningful curing parameter (irradiance × time)
- **Radiant exitance (mW/cm²)**: Power per unit area emitted from the LCU tip surface — what manufacturers typically report
- **Norrish type I photoinitiator**: Directly cleaved by light absorption (TPO, PPD, Ivocerin); requires violet wavelengths ~380–420 nm; higher molar extinction coefficient than CQ
- **Norrish type II photoinitiator**: Requires photosensitizer + coinitiator interaction (camphorquinone + amine); absorbs ~400–500 nm, peak ~468 nm
- **Polywave LED LCU (3rd generation)**: Dual violet (395–415 nm) + blue (450–470 nm) emission peaks; compatible with both Norrish type I and II systems
- **Beam homogeneity**: Spatial uniformity of radiant exitance across the emitting tip — asymmetric LED chip placement creates "hot spots" invisible to standard radiometers
