## Why Ingested

The wiki holds substantial efficacy evidence on anticoagulated extraction ([[drug/anticoagulants/inchingolo-2024-anticoagulation-therapy-dental-practice-sr]], [[drug/anticoagulants/dou-2025-bleeding-dentoalveolar-doac-vka-sr-ma]]) but nothing measuring what general dentists actually do — this national survey supplies that practice-behaviour axis. It documents where reported practice concentrates its uncertainty (direct oral anticoagulants, DOACs), which is exactly where the wiki's synthesis pages carry their strongest recommendations.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional online questionnaire administered to general dentist (GD) members of the US National Dental Practice-Based Research Network, measuring self-reported pre- and post-procedure practice for patients taking vitamin K antagonists (VKA) and direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs).

47.6% (VKA) and 41.2% (DOAC) of surveyed GDs reported "always" consulting the patient's physician before an extraction; 27.1% (VKA) and 8.7% (DOAC) reported using laboratory tests to assess bleeding risk, with International Normalized Ratio (INR) preferred by 68.4% of those testing VKA patients while most respondents were "unsure" which test applies to DOACs.

This is evidence about practice variation and knowledge gaps rather than about what works: reported management diverges most sharply around DOACs, where respondents could not name an assessment test, marking DOAC management as the point where practitioner uncertainty concentrates.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantifies **self-reported practice** — not clinical efficacy — of US community general dentists managing anticoagulated patients, split explicitly by VKA versus DOAC.
- Localises the knowledge gap: for DOAC patients, the modal response about which laboratory test to use was "unsure", whereas VKA practice converges on INR (68.4% of those who test).
- Provides a national picture of which hemostatic agents are actually reached for in community practice (cellulose 65.1%, collagen 60.6%, epinephrine infiltration 60.6%).
- Establishes a practice-behaviour baseline against which guideline-dissemination or continuing-education efforts could be measured.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: cross-sectional online questionnaire (survey-based assessment).
- **Sampling frame**: general dentist members of the US National Dental Practice-Based Research Network. Respondent count, response rate, and respondent demographics are not stated in the retrieved abstract.
- **Exposure of interest**: patients taking VKA (e.g., warfarin) versus patients taking DOACs.
- **Domains queried**:
  1. interaction/consultation with the patient's medical professional prior to dental treatment;
  2. use of laboratory tests to assess bleeding risk, and which test;
  3. measures used to control bleeding (hemostatic agents);
  4. self-rated comfort level delivering dental care to these patients.
- **Outcome type**: proportions of respondents reporting each practice. **No patient-level clinical outcomes (bleeding events, complications) were measured.**
- **Analysis**: descriptive proportions as reported; the retrieved abstract does not describe inferential modelling.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

All values are proportions of surveyed general dentists reporting the practice.

| Domain | VKA patients | DOAC patients |
|---|---|---|
| "Always" consult patient's physician before extraction | 47.6% | 41.2% |
| Use laboratory tests to assess bleeding risk before extraction | 27.1% | 8.7% |
| Preferred test among those testing | INR — 68.4% | most respondents "unsure" which test |

Most-used hemostatic agents (reported, both drug classes):

| Agent | Reported use |
|---|---|
| Cellulose-based | 65.1% |
| Collagen-based | 60.6% |
| Epinephrine infiltration (vasoconstrictor) | 60.6% |

- Authors' stated conclusions: GDs are uncertain about the best approach for DOAC patients; INR is not routinely used by most GDs to assess bleeding risk in VKA patients; the commonly used hemostatic agents are cellulose-based, collagen-based, and vasoconstrictors.
- Comfort level with delivering care was queried but the retrieved abstract reports no numeric result for it.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Self-reported practice, not observed behaviour.** Responses capture what dentists say they do; questionnaire self-report is subject to recall error and social-desirability effects, and may diverge from chart-documented practice.
- **US practice-based research network sample.** Generalisability to other health systems — including Korea, where referral patterns, medical-consultation norms, and insurance-driven laboratory access differ — is limited. Practice-based research network members may also be more research-engaged than the average community dentist.
- **Response and selection bias.** Response rate, respondent N, and non-responder characteristics are not available in the retrieved abstract, so the direction and magnitude of non-response bias cannot be assessed.
- **No patient outcomes measured.** The survey records no bleeding events, no complication rates, and no clinical results — it cannot support any statement about whether a reported practice is safe or effective.
- **Abstract-only ingestion.** Full text was not retrieved, so denominators, subgroup breakdowns, questionnaire wording, and the comfort-level results are unavailable here; re-ingest if the full text becomes accessible.
- **Future work** implied: mapping where reported practice diverges from current evidence, and testing whether targeted education changes DOAC-specific practice.

## 6. Related Work

- Systematic reviews of anticoagulation management in dental practice establishing that most simple extractions can proceed without interrupting therapy — the evidence backdrop against which this survey's reported practice can be read.
- Comparative syntheses of bleeding after dentoalveolar surgery in DOAC versus VKA patients.
- Reviews of local hemostatic measures in oral surgery, which characterise the agents this survey found in common use (oxidized cellulose, collagen sponges, vasoconstrictor-containing local anesthesia).
- Practice-based research network survey literature more broadly, of which this is an instance methodologically.

## 7. Glossary

- **VKA (Vitamin K Antagonist)**: anticoagulant class acting by inhibiting vitamin K-dependent clotting factor synthesis (e.g., warfarin); monitored by INR.
- **DOAC (Direct Oral Anticoagulant)**: anticoagulants directly inhibiting a single factor — dabigatran (thrombin/IIa), rivaroxaban/apixaban/edoxaban (factor Xa). Not reliably monitored by INR.
- **INR (International Normalized Ratio)**: standardized prothrombin time ratio; a valid monitor for VKA therapy, not for DOACs.
- **GD (General Dentist)**: the survey's respondent population — community-practising general dentists, as distinct from oral surgeons or hospital-based specialists.
- **National Dental Practice-Based Research Network**: US network of practising dentists recruited for clinical and practice research, funded via NIDCR.
- **Cross-sectional survey**: observational design measuring characteristics at one time point; here it measures reported practice, so it yields evidence about behaviour and knowledge, not about treatment efficacy.
- **Oxidized cellulose / collagen hemostat**: resorbable local hemostatic materials placed into an extraction socket.
