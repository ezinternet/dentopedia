## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (PMC full text) of the evidence on extending a shortened dental arch (SDA) with a free-end saddle removable partial denture (FESRPD), covering the biomechanical problem, clinical outcome trials, patient attitudes, and case-selection factors.

The mixed tooth–mucosa support of a distal-extension denture rotates under load because mucosa is far more compressible than periodontal ligament — worse in the mandible, where the hard palate is unavailable to spread load — and the outcome literature is correspondingly negative: extending an SDA of three to five occlusal units produced no evident improvement in chewing, comfort, esthetics, dentition stability or TMJ function, no difference in tooth loss at 38 months or 5 years, and cantilevered resin-bonded bridges were more comfortable and acceptable at a lower biological price.

Case selection is where the paper earns its place: the proportion of SDA patients who actually seek restoration rises from 3% (second molars missing) to 58% (both molars) to 93% (premolars missing), and Witter's ladder recommends no prosthetic extension for slightly shortened arches, exceptional extension for moderately shortened ones, and extension of extremely shortened arches only back to the moderately-shortened level — where a fixed bridge may suffice.

## 세줄요약

서술적 문헌고찰 (PMC 전문): 단축치열 (Shortened Dental Arch, SDA)을 원심 유리단 국소의치 (Free-End Saddle Removable Partial Denture, FESRPD)로 연장하는 것의 근거를 생역학·임상결과·환자태도·케이스 선택 인자 네 축으로 정리했다.

원심 유리단 의치는 치아–점막 **혼합 지지** 때문에 하중 하에서 회전한다 — 점막이 치주인대 (Periodontal Ligament, PDL)보다 훨씬 잘 압축되기 때문이며, 경구개로 하중을 분산할 수 없는 **하악에서 더 심하다**; 결과 문헌도 부정적이다: 교합단위 3~5개의 SDA를 연장해도 저작·편안함·심미·치열 안정성·턱관절 기능에 뚜렷한 개선이 없었고, 38개월·5년 시점 치아상실 발생률에도 차이가 없었으며, 캔틸레버 접착성 브릿지가 **더 편하고 수용도가 높으면서 생물학적 대가는 더 낮았다**.

이 논문의 값어치는 **케이스 선택**에 있다 — 실제로 보철을 원하는 SDA 환자 비율은 제2대구치만 결손 시 3%, 제1·2대구치 결손 시 58%, 소구치까지 결손 시 93%로 올라가며, Witter의 3단계는 경도 단축은 **연장 근거 없음**, 중등도는 예외적으로(주로 심미), 고도 단축은 연장하되 **중등도 수준까지만** — 그 정도면 고정성 브릿지로 충분할 수 있다고 권고한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Explains the mechanism** behind distal-extension RPD failure rather than only reporting outcomes: mixed PDL/mucosa support → differential displacement → rotation about the distal rest axis → abutment stress and residual ridge resorption.
- **Locates the anatomical asymmetry**: the mandible lacks the palatal load-spreading area, so mandibular FESRPDs rotate more and resorb more.
- **Supplies a demand gradient** (Fueki: 3% / 58% / 93%) that converts "does the patient need this" into a measurable question.
- **Supplies a decision ladder** (Witter: slightly / moderately / extremely shortened) with an explicit ceiling — restore only back to moderately shortened.
- **Identifies the strongest compliance predictor**: whether the denture replaces **anterior** teeth (Jepson; Armellini).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative review. No search strategy, inclusion criteria, or risk-of-bias appraisal reported. Literature organised into four sections: the biomechanical problem of FESRPDs; clinical investigations of outcome; patients' attitudes; and factors affecting case suitability. Findings are attributed to named primary studies rather than pooled.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Demand gradient (Fueki) — proportion of SDA subjects seeking prosthetic restoration**

| Extent of shortening | Seeking restoration |
|---|---|
| Second molar(s) missing | **3%** |
| First and second molars missing | **58%** |
| Premolar(s) missing | **93%** |

Significant predictors of seeking restoration: young age, more missing occlusal units, asymmetric arch, existing chewing complaint.

**Witter's case-selection ladder**

| Category | Definition | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Slightly shortened | Molars missing | **No evidence supports prosthetic extension** |
| Moderately shortened | Molars + first premolar missing | Extend in exceptional cases, particularly esthetic |
| Extremely shortened (incl. asymmetric) | Molars + premolars missing | Should be extended — but only back to *moderately shortened*; a fixed bridge may suffice |

**Outcome findings**

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Nijmegen group | Extending SDA of 3–5 occlusal units by FESRPD produced no evident improvement in chewing, comfort, esthetics, dentition stability, TMJ function or functional habits |
| — | No significant difference in tooth-loss incidence at 38 months; confirmed at 5 years |
| Leak | Small socio-functional impact of replacement until fewer than three posterior functional units |
| Aras | No significant difference in masticatory performance with vs without FESRPD; in *extremely* shortened arches FESRPD did improve performance, though not to dentate levels |
| Creugers | Mandibular FESRPDs do not affect TMJ function and restore only a fraction of occlusal support in 3–5 unit SDAs |
| RCTs | Cantilevered resin-bonded bridges effective alternative at **lower biological price**; more comfortable and acceptable than bilateral FESRPDs |
| Systematic review (cited) | Evidence insufficient to conclude relative effectiveness of FPD vs RPD for SDA |

**Patient attitudes**

- Compliance significantly related to presence of **anterior** replacement teeth; never-worn dentures more likely to have none (Jepson).
- Positive QoL impact only when the denture replaced anterior teeth (Armellini).
- Patients place little value on acrylic or metal RPDs replacing **molars** in an SDA (Nassani).
- Multicentre RCT: RPD improved OHIP-49 at 6 and 12 months — but **only 10 of 17** RPD participants completed 12-month follow-up, and there was **no significant difference** in oral health rating versus SDA-concept treatment.

**Satisfaction predictors (Knezović Zlatarić, n = 205)**: Kennedy classification, base material, base shape and support type did **not** significantly affect general satisfaction or comfort; higher education associated with less satisfaction with esthetics and hygiene; males with mandibular RPDs less satisfied with chewing; more missing mandibular teeth → less oral comfort. Arch distribution: maxilla 57.4% Kennedy I / 28.8% II; mandible 75.2% I / 17.1% II.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Narrative, not systematic**: no search strategy or risk-of-bias appraisal; selection of cited work is not auditable.
- Published 2013; predates current digital removable workflows and the more recent SDA trial reports.
- The two summary tables — the most directly clinically usable artefacts in the paper — **did not survive extraction** (see Document Information). Anyone needing them must open the PMC article.
- Several attitude studies cited (Ikebe; Nassani) used **hypothetical scenarios** in **non-SDA populations**, a caveat the review itself flags.
- The multicentre RCT's OHIP-49 improvement rests on 10 completers of 17 — the review reports this honestly, and it should not be cited as robust.
- The authors call for a cost-effectiveness study of RPD treatment for SDA patients; that gap is still open.
- Reference numerals were stripped in extraction, so primary sources are identified by author name only.

## 7. Glossary

- **SDA (단축치열, Shortened Dental Arch)**: "a dentition with a reduction of occlusal units starting posteriorly."
- **Occlusal unit (교합단위)**: "a pair of antagonist teeth that supports the occlusion" (e.g. premolars, molars).
- **FESRPD (원심 유리단 국소의치, Free-End Saddle Removable Partial Denture)**: distal-extension RPD with no posterior abutment, supported jointly by teeth and mucosa; Kennedy class I (bilateral) or II (unilateral).
- **Kennedy classification (케네디 분류)**: classification of partially edentulous arches by the position of edentulous spaces relative to remaining teeth.
- **Resin-bonded bridge (접착성 브릿지)**: minimally invasive fixed prosthesis bonded to enamel, used here as a cantilever to extend the arch.
- **Biological price (생물학적 대가)**: irreversible cost to tooth structure and supporting tissue exacted by a restorative choice.
- **Normative vs subjective need (규범적 필요 vs 주관적 필요)**: clinician-assessed need versus patient-perceived need; their divergence explains non-use.
