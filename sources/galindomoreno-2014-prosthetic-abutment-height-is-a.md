## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (131 patients; 308 of 315 implants analysed after 7 exclusions) of bone-level Astra Tech internal conical connection implants in the posterior maxilla, relating abutment height (<2 vs ≥2 mm) to radiographic marginal bone loss (MBL) at loading, 6 and 18 months.

Short-abutment implants lost more bone: mesial MBL 0.180/0.565 mm vs long 0.063/0.295 mm at 6/18 months; MBL rates 0.069/0.049 vs 0.039/0.024 mm/month (abutment effect F(1,1216) = 58.25, p < .01; continuous height p < .001).

Bone loss was faster in the first 6 months than over the next 12; grafted bone, periodontitis and smoking also raised MBL rates — supporting ≥2 mm abutments, within the limits of a non-randomized panoramic-radiograph cohort.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Names abutment height as the strongest measured factor** for early MBL: F(1,1216) = 58.25, p < .01, and significant also as a continuous predictor (p < .001).
- **Supplies absolute bone-loss values** for short vs long abutments at 6 and 18 months (mesial 0.180/0.565 vs 0.063/0.295 mm; distal 0.224/0.704 vs 0.073/0.312 mm), not just a direction.
- **Quantifies the non-linear post-loading pattern**: initial rate (loading→6 mo) 0.069 vs 0.049 mm/month, later rate (6→18 mo) 0.049 vs 0.024 mm/month for short vs long abutments.
- **Adjusts for competing host/site factors** — age, sex, smoking, periodontal status, restoration type, bone substratum, implant width/length — in a linear mixed model; grafted bone (p < .01/.013), periodontitis (p < .001) and smoking (p < .001) independently raised MBL rates.
- **Tests the biologic-width hypothesis** by proposing that a taller abutment increases the crown-to-bone distance and leaves more room for soft-tissue adaptation, reducing microgap-inflammation-mediated crestal resorption.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective correlational study; consecutive patients from 2 private practices, restorations placed September 2008 – July 2010; protocol approved by the University of Granada ethics committee.
- **Eligibility**: Age 18–85 yr; ≥2 splinted implants in a screw-retained prosthetic structure in the posterior maxilla; ≥5 mm remnant crestal bone; all surgery by the lead author (P.G.-M.).
- **Implants**: One system only — Astra Tech AB (Mölndal, Sweden), all inserted at bone level.
- **Sample**: 131 patients, 315 implants placed; 7 implants from 3 patients excluded for missing data → **final n = 308 implants** (137 long/LA = 44.5%; 171 short/SA = 55.5%). Demographics: 67 men (52.3%) / 61 women (47.7%); 45 smokers (35.2%) / 83 non-smokers (65.6%); periodontitis 80 (62.5%) / no periodontitis 48 (37.5%).
- **Exposure**: Uni-abutment height 0, 0.5, 1, 2 or 4 mm (manufacturer values); SA <2.00 mm, LA ≥2.00 mm; also analysed as a continuous variable.
- **Covariate definitions**: Smoking recorded at surgery as 0, 0–10, or >10 cigarettes/day; periodontitis = ≥4 sites with clinical attachment loss (CAL) ≥3 mm (Michigan O probe); restoration type = full-arch, fixed partial denture, or overdenture.
- **Outcome measurement**: Standardized digital panoramic radiographs (Kodak ACR-2000) at implant placement, at final restoration delivery (baseline/loading), and at 6 and 18 months post-loading; one independent calibrated examiner; Dent-A-View v1.0; mesial/distal linear measurements from implant platform to crestal bone, corrected using known implant height/width.
- **Statistics**: Descriptive statistics, ANOVA, exact chi-square, linear mixed model with scaled-identity repeated-covariance matrix (Schwarz Bayesian information criterion); Bonferroni correction when required. MBL rate = (MBL at 18 mo − MBL at 6 mo)/elapsed months (mm/month); initial rate = (MBL at 6 mo − MBL at baseline)/6. Confounders controlled: age, sex, smoking, periodontal status, restoration type, bone substratum, implant width and length.
- **Not in the supplied PDF**: the electronic-only supplemental appendix (Appendix Tables 1–3).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Authors' stated limitations**: retrospective design prevented homogenizing the sample (statistical methodology only partly compensates); panoramic radiography is less sensitive than periapical techniques for MBL but was chosen to capture posterior-maxilla features such as bone type; periapical imaging there would need the bisector technique, whose angulation variation alters measurements.
- **Design constraints (inferred)**: non-randomized abutment selection; bone-level internal-connection implants in the posterior maxilla only; one calibrated examiner and one implant system improve internal consistency but limit generalizability.
- **Source-data issues, reproduced verbatim and NOT silently corrected** (see `logs/ingest-deviations.md`, 2026-11-25): Table 2's caption calls the second interval "between 6 and 12 Months Post-loading" although the study reads 6 and 18 months and the text says "from 6 to 18 mos post loading"; the table's Effect column says "Abut x Time < .01" while the Results text says the abutment × time interaction was **not** significant, F(1,1216) = 0.64, p > .4; the no-periodontitis second-period value is printed as "0.026 (0.0.04)"; and Table 2 labels the smoking row "Slope (mm/y)" while the Results text reports 0.0009 mm/month. These need the electronic appendix/original tables to resolve.
- **Not retrievable from the supplied artifact**: Appendix Tables 1–3 (electronic-only supplement), so no per-characteristic standard errors beyond Table 2 and no full adjusted model coefficients.

## 7. Glossary

- **Marginal bone loss (MBL)**: Radiographic crestal bone loss; here measured mesially and distally at baseline, 6 and 18 months post-loading, with rates expressed in mm/month.
- **Short / long abutment (SA/LA)**: Prosthetic abutment height <2.00 mm vs ≥2.00 mm; available heights were 0, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 mm.
- **Internal conical connection**: Implant–abutment interface with an internal taper (Astra Tech, bone-level here).
- **Linear mixed model**: Adjusted analysis with repeated-covariance structure for mesial/distal and 6-/18-month observations.
- **Bone substratum**: Pristine native bone vs grafted bone (including grafted maxillary sinus — an author keyword).
- **Non-linear MBL trend**: Faster bone loss in the first 6 post-loading months (0.069 mm/month SA) than in months 6–18 (0.049 mm/month SA).
