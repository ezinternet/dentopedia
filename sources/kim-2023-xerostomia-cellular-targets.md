## Three-line Summary

Narrative mechanistic review (Int J Mol Sci 2023, single-author) tracing salivary secretion at the cellular level — parasympathetic neurotransmitter → GPCR (M3 mAChR) → PLC/IP3 → ER Ca²⁺ release + store-operated Ca²⁺ entry (SOCE) → AQP5 apical trafficking — and framing GPCR, IP3R, SOCE, and AQP5 as druggable cellular targets in xerostomia.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Provides a **cell/molecular-level map of salivation** underlying clinical xerostomia, complementing epidemiologic/clinical dry-mouth literature.
- Defines the **GPCR → IP3R → SOCE → AQP5 axis** as the core secretory signaling pathway and as the set of therapeutic targets.
- Explains **why current sialagogues (pilocarpine, cevimeline) work but have systemic side effects** (broad mAChR expression) and why radiation-induced hyposalivation responds poorly.
- Catalogs **causes** (drugs >400, radiation, Sjögren's, aging, tumors) with quantitative prevalence and structural aging data.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative mechanistic review of salivary gland physiology. Structure: gland/duct anatomy → polarized acinar cell biology → GPCR-mediated Ca²⁺ signaling → prevalence/etiology → palliative therapies. Evidence drawn from knockout-mouse studies, human labial-gland expression data, and RNA-seq cellular heterogeneity.

- **Anatomy**: parotid (serous, stimulated), submandibular (mixed, unstimulated majority), sublingual (mucous); tubarial glands proposed 2020; minor glands (lip biopsy for Sjögren's).
- **Acinar polarity**: tight junctions permit unidirectional secretion; ducts convert isotonic → hypotonic saliva (secrete HCO3⁻/K⁺, reabsorb Na⁺/Cl⁻).
- **Signaling**: mAChR/α1-adrenergic → PLC → IP3 + DAG → IP3R (apical IP3R2/R3) ER Ca²⁺ release → SOCE (STIM1 + Orai1/TRPC1) sustained influx → K⁺/Cl⁻ channel activation → NaCl in lumen → water via AQP5.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Daily saliva **0.5–1.5 L**, 90% from three major glands.
- Persistent xerostomia prevalence **10–50%** (conservative ~20%); women up to 30%, older adults up to 50%.
- **M1/M3 double-KO mice**: salivation completely lost regardless of pilocarpine dose (single KO reduced only at low 1 mg/kg dose).
- **IP3R2/IP3R3 double-KO mice**: severely impaired pilocarpine salivation; die within 4 weeks without wet food.
- **AQP5-KO mice**: ~60% reduction in pilocarpine-induced saliva.
- Resting acinar **[Ca²⁺]i ≈ 50–100 nM** (below K⁺/Cl⁻ channel activation threshold).
- **Aging** acinar volume loss: ~30% submandibular, ~25% sublingual, ~12% parotid; TUNEL-positive apoptosis rises with age.
- Burden: ~550,000 head-and-neck radiation patients/year; >4 million Sjögren's patients worldwide; >400 xerogenic drugs.
- **GPR39** (zinc-liganded): zinc gargle increased salivation even in Sjögren's/hyposalivation groups. **P2Y2R agonist diquafosol** approved for dry eye (Korea/Japan, Diquas).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No permanent FDA-approved cure** for salivary gland hypofunction; management is palliative (sialagogues, saliva substitutes, mouth rinses with ≤4 h relief).
- Systemic muscarinic agonists cause off-target effects (nausea, diarrhea, sweating, bronchoconstriction, hypotension, bradycardia); radiation hyposalivation responds minimally.
- Saliva substitutes cannot mimic antibacterial properties of natural saliva.
- Author calls for **acinar-cell-specific GPCR agonists** (mAChR, P2Y2R) to regulate Ca²⁺ signaling with fewer systemic effects.

## 7. Glossary

- **Xerostomia** — subjective feeling of dry mouth; may or may not accompany measurable hyposalivation.
- **Acinar cell** — polarized secretory cell producing the primary isotonic saliva; marked by AQP5 and Mist1.
- **GPCR / M3 mAChR** — G-protein-coupled receptor; M3 muscarinic acetylcholine receptor is the key parasympathetic trigger of salivation.
- **IP3R** — inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor; releases Ca²⁺ from the ER (apical IP3R2/IP3R3).
- **SOCE** — store-operated Ca²⁺ entry; STIM1 (ER sensor) + Orai1/TRPC1 sustain Ca²⁺ influx for prolonged secretion.
- **AQP5** — aquaporin 5; apical water channel whose Ca²⁺-triggered trafficking enables water secretion.
- **Sialagogue** — drug that stimulates saliva (pilocarpine, cevimeline).
