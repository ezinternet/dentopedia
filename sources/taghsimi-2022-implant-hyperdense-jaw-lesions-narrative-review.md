## Why Ingested

The only narrative review in the wiki covering implant outcomes across all major hyperdense jaw lesion types — including condensing osteitis (CO), idiopathic osteosclerosis (IO), odontoma, cementoblastoma, osteoid osteoma, osteoblastoma, and cemento-osseous dysplasia (COD). Provides the single cross-lesion synthesis (n=19 cases, 84.2% success) that allows clinicians to stratify implant risk by lesion biology. See also [[implants/survival/kim-2026-dental-implant-osteoporosis-osteosclerosis]] for osteosclerosis-specific outcomes.

## Three-line Summary

A narrative review of 19 case reports/series (PubMed, Google Scholar, Science Direct; 1978–2022) found an overall 84.2% implant success rate across hyperdense jaw lesions, with osteoid osteoma, odontoma, cementoblastoma, condensing osteitis, and idiopathic osteosclerosis permitting implantation without major complications. Cemento-osseous dysplasia carries implant failure risk (3 of 19 total failure cases were FCOD), and osteoblastoma is contraindicated due to aggressive behavior and en bloc resection requirements. No unified implantation protocol exists; outcomes varied by lesion type, placement timing (immediate vs. delayed), and drilling heat management.

## 세줄요약

총 323편 문헌에서 포함 기준을 충족한 19편(증례보고·증례연구)을 분석한 결과, 과방사성 악골 병변 부위 임플란트의 전체 성공률은 84.2%였다. 골양골종(Osteoid Osteoma, OO)·치아종(Odontoma)·백악질모세포종(Cementoblastoma)·치밀성 골수염(Condensing Osteitis, CO)·특발성 골경화증(Idiopathic Osteosclerosis, IO) 부위에서는 합병증 없이 식립 가능하였으나, 골모세포종(Osteoblastoma)은 공격적 성장과 전절제 필요성으로 금기이고 시멘토골성 이형성증(Cemento-Osseous Dysplasia, COD)은 실패 사례 3건이 모두 이 유형에서 발생하였다. 표준 식립 프로토콜 부재 및 관찰 기간 불일치로 비교근거가 제한적이며, 드릴링 중 과열 방지가 핵심 합병증 예방 인자로 강조되었다.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **First narrative synthesis** of implant outcomes across all major hyperdense jaw lesion types (8 categories)
2. **84.2% overall success rate** derived from 19 included case reports/series
3. **Risk stratification by lesion type**: safe (OO, odontoma, cementoblastoma, CO, IO) vs. risky (COD) vs. contraindicated (osteoblastoma)
4. **Identification of key failure factors**: inadequate pre-op diagnosis, peri-lesional blood supply disruption, drilling overheating, intralesional placement in COD
5. **No clinical guidelines exist** for implantation in hyperdense lesion areas — this review is the first attempt at systematization

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Search databases**: PubMed, Google Scholar, Science Direct
- **Time range**: 1978 to January 2022
- **Search terms**: "dental implantation" combined with 10 lesion-specific terms (osteoid osteoma, odontoma, osteoblastoma, cementoblastoma, cemento-osseous dysplasia, exostosis, idiopathic osteosclerosis, condensing osteitis, neoplasms, radiopaque lesion)
- **Articles identified**: 323
- **Exclusion**: 304 (non-English=2, irrelevant=300, no full text=2)
- **Included**: 19 (all case reports or case series)
- **Study design**: Narrative review (not systematic; no PRISMA, no meta-analysis)
- **Breakdown by lesion**: osteoid osteoma=2, odontoma=5, osteoblastoma=0, cementoblastoma=2, COD=7, condensing osteitis=1, idiopathic osteosclerosis=2

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Overall Outcome
- **84.2% success** (16/19 reported cases); 3 failures, all in FCOD (= 15.8% failure rate within reported cases)

### By Lesion Type

| Lesion | Cases | Outcome | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Osteoid osteoma | 2 | Success | Gardner syndrome case: 7yr follow-up stable |
| Odontoma | 5 | Success | Immediate and delayed both feasible |
| Osteoblastoma | 0 | Contraindicated | Aggressive, en bloc resection required |
| Cementoblastoma | 2 | Success (conditional) | Tooth + lesion removal required first; immediate or delayed |
| Cemento-osseous dysplasia | 7 | Mixed | 3 failures; overheating and poor blood supply are risks |
| Condensing osteitis | 1 | Success | Tooth extraction → GBR → 6-month wait → implant |
| Idiopathic osteosclerosis | 2 | Success | 1–2 yr follow-up stable; protocol varies by lesion size/position |

### Critical Technical Points
- All successful implantations used standard sterile protocol with **avoidance of bone overheating** as the paramount criterion
- In FCOD: 3-stage protocol recommended (drilling with abundant cooling → 3-week seal → 3-month healing before loading)
- In IO: small/apical lesions → adjust implant length/angulation; large coronal lesions → direct placement; extremely dense → remove and delay
- CBCT superior to panoramic/periapical for pre-op lesion characterization
- Histopathological examination mandatory to confirm diagnosis and rule out malignant transformation

### Failure Mechanism in COD
- Impaired vascularization of dysplastic tissue → reduced regenerative capacity
- Drilling necrosis risk → infection and sequestrum
- Chronic osteomyelitis as secondary complication (Shin et al. case)
- Oliveira et al. (2014): failure at 6 months; no pre-op imaging available to assess baseline lesion state

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

1. Very small evidence base: only 19 case reports/series over 44-year span
2. Publication bias: authors suspect negative results are underreported
3. No unified implantation protocol — direct comparison across cases impossible
4. Heterogeneous follow-up periods across included cases
5. No RCTs or controlled studies exist; systematic review not feasible at current evidence level
6. Authors call for additional systematic research before clinical guidelines can be established

## 7. Glossary

- **Condensing osteitis (CO)**: Chronic focal sclerotic osteomyelitis; periapical radiopaque lesion; 2–6.5 mm; associated with non-vital teeth or arsenic exposure
- **Idiopathic osteosclerosis (IO)**: Localized compact bone increase in cancellous bone; unknown etiology; 1–3 cm (up to 7 cm); not associated with non-vital teeth; longitudinally stable or decreasing
- **Cemento-osseous dysplasia (COD)**: Fibro-osseous replacement; three types — focal (FCOD), periapical, florid; avascular dysplastic tissue increases implant failure risk
- **Osteoblastoma**: Benign osteoblastic tumor >2 cm; aggressive form tends to recur; en bloc resection = treatment of choice; no implant cases in literature
- **Cementoblastoma**: Rare benign mesenchymal tumor; fused to tooth root; recurrence rate 5.9–37.1%; tooth + lesion removal required before implant
- **Osteoid osteoma**: Rare benign bone tumor <1.5 cm nidus; low recurrence; low implant failure risk
- **CBCT**: Cone Beam Computed Tomography; superior accuracy vs. panoramic for hyperdense lesion characterization
- **GBR**: Guided Bone Regeneration; used in CO case before delayed implantation
