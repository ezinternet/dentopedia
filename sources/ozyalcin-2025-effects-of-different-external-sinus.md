## Why Ingested

The wiki holds plenty of evidence on *perforation risk* by osteotomy instrument ([[sinus-lift/lateral/lee-2023-impact-sinus-floor-elevation-techniques]], [[sinus-lift/lateral/monje-2016-sinus-membrane-perforation-reamer-drilling]]) but almost nothing that puts surgeon comfort, operating time and patient-reported outcomes (pain, analgesic use, edema, OHIP-14) on the same three-arm patient RCT. This paper supplies that head-to-head comparison of guided piezosurgery vs a dedicated lateral-window bur vs conventional burs, with the useful null result that pain/edema did not differ.

## Three-line Summary

Prospective three-arm RCT (27 patients, 30 lateral sinus lifts, 10 sinuses per arm) at Ordu University, Turkey, comparing guided piezosurgery (P), a dedicated lateral-window osteotomy bur/reamer (L) and conventional rotary burs (C) for perioperative complications and patient/clinician comfort.

Membrane elevation time (p = 0.015) and surgeon's field visibility (p < 0.001) differed significantly — the guided-piezo column had the shortest total operating time (16.80 ± 4.76 vs 22.3 ± 8.90 and 24 ± 8.99 min) and 9/10 "perfect visibility" ratings — while window osteotomy time (p = 0.058), total time (p = 0.135), pain, analgesic consumption, edema (p = 0.868/0.179/0.242), patient experience (p = 0.387) and OHIP-14 change (p = 0.912) showed no significant between-group differences.

Guided piezosurgery is the ergonomic/procedural winner but not a patient-comfort winner; the authors suggest dedicated lateral-window burs may be preferable where postoperative edema is expected, though this rests on a small, non-significant difference.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Head-to-head patient RCT of three lateral-window osteotomy strategies, including a **guided (3D-printed) piezosurgery** arm — a combination rarely tested against a dedicated bur and conventional burs in the same trial.
- Separates intraoperative time into **window osteotomy** vs **membrane elevation** and shows the difference between techniques lives in elevation time (p = 0.015), not in cutting time (p = 0.058).
- Reported **Schneiderian membrane perforation counts by arm**: 3 conventional, 2 dedicated-bur, 1 guided piezo (the perforations that occurred among the 33 initially eligible patients; 6 such patients were excluded, leaving 27).
- Adds patient-reported endpoints that RCTs on this topic usually omit: 7-day VAS pain diary, analgesic counts, five-landmark facial edema, 3-point expectation/experience scale, and OHIP-14 — all null between arms.
- Documents a **surgeon-side benefit that does not translate into patient-side benefit**, which is the clinically interesting split.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: prospective, randomized, controlled clinical trial; computer-generated simple randomization in sealed opaque envelopes; single surgeon; blinded statistical analyst only (surgeon and patient unblindable because a guide was used in one arm); CONSORT-reported.
- **Setting**: single centre, Ordu University, Turkey.
- **Unit of randomization**: the **sinus to be lifted**, not the patient (30 sinuses in 27 patients).
- **Arms** (n = 10 sinuses each):
  - **C — conventional**: tungsten carbide + diamond round burs (Meisinger) with rotary motor (NSK SurgicPro), sterile saline irrigation.
  - **L — reamer / dedicated bur**: burs designed for lateral sinus lift (Dentium Advanced Sinus Kit).
  - **P — guided piezo**: piezo surgical device (NSK Variosurg 3) plus a 3D-printed resin surgical guide (NextDent 5100), designed from CBCT DICOM → STL (3D Slicer), intraoral scan (TRIOS 4) and MeshMixer planning.
- **Inclusion**: posterior maxilla needing sinus lift, Cawood & Howell class V–VI, residual bone height < 6 mm, ≥18 years, no prior preprosthetic/implant surgery, CBCT within 1 month.
- **Exclusion**: systemic disease contraindicating surgery, pregnancy/lactation, immune disorders/substance abuse, head-and-neck radiotherapy, existing sinus pathology or prior sinus surgery, >10 cigarettes/day, antibiotics/steroids within 30 days, uncooperative, **and Schneiderian membrane perforation** (perforated cases were dropped, not analysed).
- **Standardization**: same synthetic graft in all arms (NTM-Cera, ZeSa); same post-op regimen (amoxicillin-clavulanate 1000 mg, dexketoprofen 25 mg, benzydamine + chlorhexidine mouthwash); sutures out day 7.
- **Sample size**: G*Power 3.1.9.2 — ≥8/group (pain), ≥7/group (edema), ≥9/group (operating time) at 95% confidence and ~81–82% power → ≥9 per group targeted, 10 enrolled.
- **Outcomes**: window osteotomy time, membrane elevation time, total intraoperative time (chronometer, minutes); surgeon access on a 4-point scale (0 very poor → 3 excellent); VAS pain (10 cm) from 6 h to day 7; analgesic consumption to day 7; facial edema from 5 landmark distances (tragus, pogonion, lip commissure, gonion, lateral canthus, nasal border) pre-op, day 2, day 7; patient expectation-vs-experience 3-point scale at day 7; OHIP-14 pre-op vs day 7 (Turkish validated version).
- **Statistics**: Shapiro–Wilk; one-way ANOVA / Kruskal–Wallis between groups; repeated-measures ANOVA / Friedman within group; Wilcoxon / paired t for OHIP-14 change; chi-square for categorical; α = 0.05; SPSS 29.0.2.0.

Column 1 is identifiable as the **guided piezosurgery arm** because it holds the 9/10 "perfect visibility" ratings and the shortest total time, both of which the Discussion attributes to guided piezo surgery. Per-group values should therefore be read as column-level rather than label-level.

- **Pain**: VAS declined to <1 mean in every arm by day 7; no between-group difference in VAS change (p > 0.05), significant within-group decline in all arms.
- **Analgesic consumption**: no between-group difference (p > 0.05); within-group differences significant in P and highly significant in C and L; mean intake fell below 1 tablet by day 6 in all arms.
- **Edema**: no significant change overall — pre-op vs day 2 p = 0.868, pre-op vs day 7 p = 0.179, day 2 vs day 7 p = 0.242.
- **Patient experience (day 7)**, p = 0.387: better than expected 7 / 9 / 5; same as expected 2 / 1 / 3; worse than expected 1 / 0 / 2.
- **OHIP-14**: no within-group change pre-op → day 7 (p > 0.05) and no between-group difference in the change score (p = 0.912); the Discussion nonetheless states the guided-piezo arm showed the least perioperative deterioration in quality of life.
- **Membrane perforations (Discussion)**: 3 conventional, 2 dedicated bur, 1 guided piezo — i.e. an absolute gradient favouring the guided approach, but no statistical test reported and these cases were excluded from outcome analysis.
- **Authors' conclusion**: guided piezo preferable for operating time, field access and quality of life; dedicated lateral-window burs possibly preferable where greater edema is expected (e.g. thick lateral sinus wall).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Small sample** (n = 10 sinuses per arm), powered on pain/edema/time only — the patient-reported nulls are underpowered for small effects, and the edema "advantage" of the dedicated bur is a non-significant observation the conclusion leans on.
- **Perforated cases were excluded rather than analysed**, so the technique's most relevant complication is reported only as a raw count without statistics — and exclusion removes the worst-case outcomes from the comfort data.
- **Unit of randomization is the sinus**, not the patient, with 3 patients contributing two sinuses — non-independence not addressed in the analysis.
- **Blinding impossible** for surgeon and patient (guide visible), and the surgeon scored their own access; VAS/OHIP-14 are subjective.
- **Single surgeon, single centre**, Turkish-speaking cohort — generalizability limited; buccal plate thickness/density, membrane thickness, vestibule depth, mouth opening and phenotype were not measured.
- **Retrospectively registered** (NCT06601816 after recruitment), so no pre-specified primary outcome.
- No implant-level outcome (survival, ISQ, histomorphometry) is reported — the trial stops at the perioperative week.

## 6. Related Work

- **Delilbasi & Gurler [5]**: prior piezo vs conventional comparison in 23 direct sinus lifts — longer piezo procedure time, better visibility, lower early pain/edema; this trial's access finding agrees, its pain/edema finding does not, and it credits the guide (not the piezo alone) for the shorter time.
- **Jordi et al. [18]**: meta-analysis of membrane perforation — 24% rotary vs 8% piezoelectric; contextualizes the 3/2/1 perforation gradient here.
- **Lozada et al. [8]**: 17 patients with a single dedicated lateral-window bur, 1 perforation (5.8%) — the direct ancestor of arm L.
- **Barone et al. [7]**: more perforations with piezo than conventional (non-significant), plus the cavitation/bloodless-field argument — the counterpoint to the piezo-advantage narrative.
- **Mandelaris & Rosenfeld [21]; Cho et al. [24]**: the surgical-guide lineage (prototype guide; digital navigation) that arm P operationalizes.
- **Mardinger et al. [25]**: 76 patients, health-related quality-of-life recovery after conventional sinus lift — the benchmark for the OHIP-14 endpoint used here.

## 7. Glossary

- **External (lateral) sinus lifting**: maxillary sinus floor elevation through an antrostomy in the lateral wall of the sinus, as opposed to a crestal/transcrestal approach.
- **Guided piezosurgery**: piezoelectric (ultrasonic) bone cutting performed through a 3D-printed surgical guide that fixes the lateral-window outline.
- **Dedicated lateral-window bur (reamer)**: a bur/kit designed specifically for lateral sinus wall osteotomy (here Dentium Advanced Sinus Kit), intended to stop at the membrane.
- **Schneiderian membrane**: the sinus mucosal lining elevated to create the graft space; its perforation is the index intraoperative complication.
- **VAS**: visual analogue scale, 0–10 here, for pain intensity.
- **OHIP-14**: Oral Health Impact Profile-14, 14-item oral health-related quality-of-life questionnaire, 0–56 (higher = worse).
- **Cawood & Howell class V–VI**: severely to completely resorbed edentulous ridge classes requiring augmentation before implant placement.
