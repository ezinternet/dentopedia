## Three-line Summary

Largest published MA comparing 4% articaine vs 2% lidocaine (36 RCTs, 3,088 cases; BMC Anesthesiol 2026; PROSPERO CRD420251110177), stratified by infiltration vs nerve block technique with four primary outcomes: onset, duration, pain intensity, and success rate.

Articaine achieves faster onset (infiltration −1.34 min; nerve block −0.27 min) and longer duration (infiltration +18.68 min; nerve block +53.63 min) than lidocaine, and lower pain scores for infiltration (−4.41 VAS) — but anesthetic success rates are NOT significantly different in either technique (RR 1.03 infiltration; RR 1.06 nerve block).

The key clinical message: articaine ≠ higher success rate over lidocaine; advantages are pharmacokinetic (onset/duration) rather than procedural success, and agent selection should be based on duration requirement and individual patient factors — not assumed superiority.

## 세줄요약

최대 규모 아티카인 vs 리도케인 비교 MA (36 RCTs, 3,088례; BMC Anesthesiol 2026) — 침윤마취/신경차단 각각 4가지 결과지표 층화 분석.

아티카인: 발현 빠름(침윤 −1.34분; 신경차단 −0.27분), 지속 길음(침윤 +18.68분; 신경차단 +53.63분), 침윤 통증 낮음(−4.41 VAS) — 그러나 마취 성공률은 두 술식 모두 유의차 없음 (RR 1.03 침윤; RR 1.06 신경차단).

임상 핵심 메시지: **아티카인 ≠ 더 높은 성공률**; 이점은 약동학적(발현/지속)이며, 제제 선택은 시술 소요시간·개별 환자 요인 기반이어야 함 — 보편적 우월성 가정 금지.

## Study Design

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Systematic review and meta-analysis |
| Databases | PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane (inception–July 2025) |
| Included RCTs | 36 (infiltration: 19; nerve block: 17) |
| Total cases | 3,088 (infiltration: 1,862; nerve block: 1,226) |
| Comparison | 4% articaine vs 2% lidocaine (both with epinephrine) |
| Primary outcomes | Onset time, duration, pain intensity (VAS), success rate |
| Statistical | Random-effects; MD (continuous), RR (dichotomous) |

## Paresthesia Safety Note

Some pharmacovigilance datasets report disproportionate paresthesia reports with 4% formulations (including articaine). The included RCTs are not powered for rare adverse events. Evidence does not confirm causal association; absolute risks appear low. Many clinicians prefer lidocaine for IANB due to this ongoing debate.

## Pharmacological Explanation

Articaine advantages (when they exist):
- Higher lipid solubility + pKa closer to physiologic pH → enhanced tissue diffusion
- 4% concentration → more active molecules per volume → faster onset
- Ester side chain → rapid plasma esterase hydrolysis → short systemic half-life

Why success rates don't differ despite pharmacologic advantages:
- Ceiling effects in routine (non-inflamed) cases
- Technique and anatomy dominate nerve block success
- Variable success definitions across trials dilute detection of small differences

## Clinical Insights

1. **Onset/duration = modest practical benefit** — 1.34 min faster onset for infiltration; 53.63 min longer nerve block duration. Both are potentially useful (anxious patients; lengthy procedures) but not decisive for most cases.

2. **Success rate equality** — choosing articaine over lidocaine to achieve higher success is NOT supported by this evidence. Both achieve comparable procedural success.

3. **Infiltration analgesic advantage** — articaine significantly reduces intraoperative pain for infiltration cases (MD −4.41 VAS), particularly for molar extractions (MD −2.38).

4. **IANB duration** — 53.63 min longer with articaine for nerve blocks = clinically meaningful if post-op analgesia is desired; less relevant for short procedures.

5. **Cost-effectiveness logic** — if lidocaine is cheaper and success rates are equivalent, lidocaine is the cost-effective default; articaine justified when duration extension is needed or bone density impedes infiltration.
