## Why Ingested

Multicenter prospective case-control providing the first direct comparison of tissue-level vs bone-level short implants (5–7 mm) in the posterior maxilla. Crucially, bicortical engagement of the implant apex in the sinus floor was included as a predictor in multivariate analysis; it emerged as a significant positive correlate of insertion torque (IT) at placement, consistent with [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/han-2016-primary-stability-bicortical-posterior-maxilla]] and [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/ahn-2012-implant-stability-preparation-methods-bicortical-monocortical]].

## Three-line Summary

Prospective multicenter case-control, 58 patients / 71 short implants (36 tissue-level 5–6.5 mm, 35 bone-level 7 mm), posterior maxilla, mean RBH 6.2 ± 2.3 mm; primary outcome = physiological bone remodeling (PBR) at T1 (5 months) and T2 (12 months of loading).
Tissue-level implants showed significantly less PBR than bone-level at both timepoints (T1: 0.11 vs 0.34 mm, p=0.004; T2: 0.30 vs 0.55 mm, p=0.003); bicortical engagement correlated significantly with IT but not with ISQ or PBR in multivariate models; thin mucosal thickness (≤2 mm) was the dominant PBR driver.
Bicortical engagement is a positive IT predictor; short abutment height (≤2 mm) and crown emergence angle >30° drive late bone remodeling in bone-level implants.

## Key Contributions

- Bicortical engagement = significant IT predictor (multivariate), confirming in vitro findings clinically
- Bicortical engagement NOT a significant predictor of ISQ, PBR(T0–T1), or PBR(T1–T2) — no direct stability or bone remodeling benefit in multivariate model
- Tissue-level short implants (microgap above crest) → less PBR than bone-level: clinically actionable design choice
- Thin mucosal thickness (≤2 mm) at placement is the dominant early PBR driver — independent of implant type
- Short abutment height (≤2 mm) significantly increases late PBR in both tissue- and bone-level implants
- Emergence angle >30° is additional late PBR risk factor in bone-level implants only

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective case-control (STROBE guidelines)
- **Implants**: M2 Zeros (Busan, Korea), 4 mm diameter; tissue-level (5, 6.5 mm), bone-level (7 mm, placed 1 mm subcrestal)
- **n**: 59 enrolled → 58 analyzed (1 dropout); 71 implants total
- **Settings**: 3 centers, June 2020 – May 2023
- **Outcomes**: IT, ISQ (Osstell Smartpeg #27), periapical radiograph PBR at T0/T1/T2
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk, t-test / Wilcoxon, multiple linear regression (Enter method)
- **Bicortical predictor**: yes/no (radiographic apex at/through sinus floor cortex)

## Key Results

| Outcome | Tissue-level | Bone-level | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISQ T0 | 66.8 ± 9.3 | 64.7 ± 10.3 | 0.42 |
| IT T0 (N·cm) | 41.9 ± 14.0 | 40.8 ± 11.3 | 0.56 |
| ISQ T1 | 69.5 ± 7.0 | 71.4 ± 6.5 | 0.10 |
| PBR T0–T1 (mm) | **0.11 ± 0.27** | 0.34 ± 0.35 | **0.004** |
| PBR T0–T2 (mm) | **0.30 ± 0.23** | 0.55 ± 0.42 | **0.003** |

Multivariate predictors:
- PBR(T0–T1): thin mucosa ≤2 mm (both groups, p<0.001)
- Bicortical engagement: significant for IT (positive), NOT for ISQ or PBR
- PBR(T1–T2): short abutment ≤2 mm (both groups); emergence >30° (bone-level only)

## Limitations

- 1-year follow-up only; long-term PBR and survival data unavailable
- Bone-level implants placed 1 mm subcrestally — subcrestal vs crestal placement confounds the comparison
- Relatively small n per group (35–36)
- No stratification by RBH or bicortical engagement rate reported

## Glossary

- **Physiological Bone Remodeling (PBR)**: Peri-implant crestal bone level change measured radiographically; positive = coronal remodeling, negative = apical (resorption)
- **Supracrestal tissue adhesion**: Biological seal (avg 2.7 mm) formed at implant transgingival zone; insufficient mucosa → bone resorption to create space
- **Crown emergence angle**: Angle of prosthetic crown transitional contour to implant long axis; >30° increases lateral force transfer to crestal bone
