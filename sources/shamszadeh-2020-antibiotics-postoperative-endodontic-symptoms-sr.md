## Why Ingested

Fills the gap on whether prophylactic antibiotics reduce post-operative pain or swelling after root canal treatment in patients with necrotic pulp. Directly relevant to antibiotic stewardship in endodontics. Related to [[di-spirito-2022-endodontic-pain-management-overview]].

## Three-line Summary

Prophylactic oral antibiotics do not significantly reduce post-operative pain or swelling following endodontic treatment in adults with pulpal necrosis. A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (n=690) for pain and 4 RCTs (n=149) for swelling found no significant SMDs at any time point (6, 12, 24, 48, 72 h). Current evidence does not support antibiotic prophylaxis for managing post-endodontic symptoms.

## 세줄요약

예방적 항생제 (prophylactic antibiotics)는 괴사 치수 (pulpal necrosis) 환자의 근관치료 후 통증 및 부종 감소에 유의미한 효과가 없다. 통증에 관한 8건의 무작위 대조시험 (Randomised Controlled Trial, RCT) (n=690)과 부종에 관한 4건의 RCT (n=149)에서 6·12·24·48·72시간 모든 시점의 표준화 평균차 (Standardised Mean Difference, SMD)가 통계적으로 유의하지 않았다. 현 근거는 근관치료 후 증상 예방 목적의 항생제 처방을 지지하지 않는다.

## Key Contributions

- First SR/MA to pool RCT evidence specifically on prophylactic antibiotics for post-endodontic symptoms in necrotic pulp cases
- Quantifies null effect across five time points for pain and three time points for swelling using SMD with 95% CI
- Supports antibiotic stewardship by providing high-level evidence against routine prophylaxis in non-complicated necrotic pulp cases

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs
- **Search**: Up to April 2020; all RCTs comparing oral antibiotics vs. placebo for post-endodontic symptoms
- **Outcomes**: Post-operative pain; post-operative swelling; combined pain+swelling
- **Statistical method**: Standardised mean differences (SMDs) with 95% CI; random-effects inverse-variance model
- **Heterogeneity**: Additional analysis performed when P < .05
- **Pain pool**: 8 RCTs, n = 690
- **Swelling pool**: 4 RCTs, n = 149

## Key Results

### Post-operative Pain (8 RCTs, n=690)

| Time point | SMD | 95% CI | P value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 h | −0.008 | −0.279 – 0.264 | .95 |
| 12 h | −0.080 | −1.39 – 1.23 | .90 |
| 24 h | −0.044 | −0.29 – 0.20 | .72 |
| 48 h | 0.18 | −0.26 – 0.62 | .42 |
| 72 h | −0.050 | −0.33 – 0.23 | .723 |

### Post-operative Swelling (4 RCTs, n=149)

| Time point | SMD | 95% CI | P value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 h | 0.29 | −0.72 – 1.32 | .56 |
| 48 h | −0.23 | −0.98 – 0.51 | .54 |
| 72 h | −0.03 | −1.25 – 1.33 | .96 |

**Conclusion**: Prophylactic antibiotics confer no statistically significant benefit for pain or swelling at any measured time point.

## Limitations

- Small number of included RCTs, especially for swelling (n=4)
- Heterogeneity in antibiotic types, dosing regimens, and treatment protocols across studies
- Combined pain+swelling outcome had insufficient data for pooling
- Mostly single-visit vs. multi-visit designs not fully separated in subgroup analysis
- Abstract-only extraction (full text not available); internal risk-of-bias tables not assessable

## Glossary

- **SMD (Standardised Mean Difference)**: Effect size measure allowing pooling of studies using different pain/swelling scales; SMD near 0 = no effect
- **Random-effects model**: Accounts for between-study heterogeneity; conservative pooling
- **Prophylactic antibiotics**: Antibiotics given before/during treatment to prevent (not treat existing) infection or symptoms
- **Pulpal necrosis**: Death of the dental pulp, typically due to infection or trauma; common indication for root canal treatment
