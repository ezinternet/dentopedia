## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional pilot study, n=122, adult dental patients at a single center in Tirana, Albania (mean age 32.3±12.7y).

Overall oral tori prevalence 48.4% (torus mandibularis 39%, alveolar bone exostosis 16%, torus palatinus 14%), peaking in the 18–29 age group (54.2% of cases); no significant association with gender or bruxism.

Prevalence was markedly higher than most prior population reports (2.1–38%), highlighting large population-to-population variability; small single-center sample (n=122) and incomplete vitamin D/calcium self-report data limit generalizability.

## 세줄요약

단면연구(cross-sectional pilot), n=122, 알바니아 티라나 단일기관 성인 치과 환자(평균연령 32.3±12.7세).

전체 구강 torus 유병률 48.4%(하악골융기 torus mandibularis 39%, 치조골외골증 16%, 구개융기 torus palatinus 14%)로 18–29세군에서 최다(54.2%); 성별·이갈이(bruxism)와 유의한 연관 없음.

기존 인구 보고(2.1–38%)보다 뚜렷이 높은 유병률로 인구군 간 큰 편차를 보여주며, 소규모 단일기관 표본(n=122)과 불완전한 비타민D·칼슘 자가보고 데이터가 한계.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First published prevalence data for oral tori/exostoses in an Albanian population — fills a country-level data gap.
- Reports prevalence and morphology separately for torus mandibularis (TM), torus palatinus (TP), and alveolar bone exostosis (ABE), including shape distributions (TP: flat 71.4%, spindle 18.2%, lobular 18.2%; TM: bilateral solitary 47.9%).
- Notes that having one type of oral exostosis (per cited literature) roughly doubles the odds of having another, suggesting a shared underlying predisposition across TM/TP/ABE.
- Discusses candidate etiologic factors beyond genetics: occlusal stress, high-altitude hypoxia, vitamin D/calcium status, and air pollution (speculative), alongside the established Osteogenic-Periosteal Stretch Hypothesis.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Single-center, single-examiner cross-sectional clinical study at Diamond Dental Hospital, Tirana (Feb–Apr 2024). Ethics approval (Council of Ethics UMT No. 375/1), written consent. Adults ≥18y, Albanian ethnicity, dentulous or edentulous, no orthodontic history; excluded questionable/tumor-like lesions and post-surgical incomplete healing. Visual + palpation exam for TP/TM/ABE at standard anatomic sites; self-report questionnaire for vitamin D, calcium, and bruxism. Chi-square tests for group comparisons (SPSS 26.0).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Overall oral tori prevalence: 48.4% (n=59/122).
- By type: TM 39.3%, ABE 16%, TP 11.5% (some overlap/combination cases; no TP+ABE combination observed).
- Peak age group: 18–29 years (54.2% of tori cases) — contrasts with several other population studies reporting peaks in older (e.g., 60–69y) groups.
- No significant association with gender (p=0.359–0.968 across TM/TP/ABE pattern comparisons) or with bruxism/malocclusion (p=0.745/0.813).
- Prevalence (48.3%) far exceeds most comparator studies cited (range 2.1–38%), underscoring wide population variability in oral tori occurrence.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small sample (n=122), single center, single examiner — limited generalizability and possibly limited power to detect gender/bruxism associations.
- Vitamin D and calcium data were self-reported and frequently missing/incomplete; authors recommend objective lab-based measurement in future studies.
- Speculative etiologic links (air pollution, altitude) are proposed but not directly tested in this design.

## 7. Glossary

- **Torus mandibularis (TM)**: bony exostosis on the lingual mandible above the mylohyoid ridge, canine-premolar region.
- **Torus palatinus (TP)**: midline palatal bony exostosis, classified by shape (flat/spindle/nodular/lobular) and size.
- **Alveolar bone exostosis (ABE)**: buccal/labial bony outgrowth, typically distal premolar-molar region.
- **Osteogenic-Periosteal Stretch Hypothesis**: proposed mechanism limiting mandibular deformation via localized torus formation.
