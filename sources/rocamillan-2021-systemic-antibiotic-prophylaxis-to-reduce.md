## Why Ingested

This is the earlier positive-direction SR+MA in the implant-prophylaxis family that the wiki currently holds only as a *cited reference* inside [[drug/antibiotics/dubedout-2025-antibiotic-prophylaxis-early-complications]] (ref 36 — "supported a single prophylactic dose, but only 4/11 studies low risk of bias") and never as its own page. Ingesting it closes a real gap: it is the primary source of the widely-quoted **RR 0.30 / single-preop-dose-is-enough** position, and without the page that position lives in the wiki only as paraphrase. It reinforces [[drug/antibiotics/torof-2023-antibiotic-dental-implant-procedures-sr-ma]] and [[drug/antibiotics/esposito-2013-antibiotics-dental-implant-placement-cochrane]] (benefit direction, single preoperative dose) while standing as the *counterweight* the placebo-only reviews [[drug/antibiotics/momand-2024-antibiotic-prophylaxis-early-implant-failure]] and [[drug/antibiotics/dubedout-2025-antibiotic-prophylaxis-early-complications]] deliberately excluded — 7 of its 11 RCTs carried "some concerns" on RoB 2, which is exactly the design-filter difference that explains the discordant conclusions.

## Three-line Summary

PRISMA SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42021248348; PubMed/MEDLINE, Scielo, Cochrane CENTRAL to 9 Apr 2021) of 11 RCTs, 2144 patients / 4037 implants, ≥3-month follow-up; early implant failure was the outcome.

Antibiotic prophylaxis significantly reduced early implant failure (RR 0.30, 95% CI 0.19–0.47, p<0.00001; I²=0%, p=0.54) — failure 1.55% antibiotic vs 4.61% no-antibiotic/placebo — and a single preoperative dose alone was sufficient (RR 0.34, 95% CI 0.21–0.53); preoperative vs pre+postoperative regimens showed no difference (RR 0.57, 95% CI 0.21–1.55, NS).

The authors conclude a single preoperative dose is indicated in healthy patients and that postoperative continuation is not justified; the clinical ceiling is the evidence quality — only 4/11 RCTs were low risk of bias, the rest carried "some concerns" (especially deviations from intended interventions), and the sample was small.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Provides the largest **pooled RR for antibiotic prophylaxis vs no prophylaxis/placebo** in this literature (RR 0.30) with **zero statistical heterogeneity** (I²=0%, p=0.54), which is why it became the citation of record for "the effect is large and real."
- Separates the **regimen question** from the yes/no question: preoperative-only vs pre+postoperative is not significantly different (RR 0.57, NS) — i.e. postoperative continuation adds nothing.
- Isolates the **single preoperative dose** as a stand-alone comparison and shows it is sufficient (RR 0.34, 95% CI 0.21–0.53).
- Reports the raw failure proportions that anchor absolute-effect reasoning across the wiki: **1.55% (antibiotic) vs 4.61% (no antibiotic/placebo)**.
- Adds a mechanistic **metronidazole hypothesis**: genes encoding metronidazole resistance are less frequent in peri-implant tissue than beta-lactam resistance genes, and implant-failure/peri-implantitis flora includes Gram-negative anaerobes — so metronidazole deserves direct study.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: PRISMA systematic review + meta-analysis; protocol pre-registered on PROSPERO (CRD42021248348).
- **PICO**: P — partially or totally edentulous patients undergoing implant surgery; I — any systemic antibiotic, any dose/duration/timing; C — different antibiotic/dose/duration/timing, or placebo/no antibiotic; O — early implant failure (implant removed before prosthetic loading for mobility or infection).
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Scielo, Cochrane Central Trials Database; English or Spanish; January 2000 – April 2021; searches run 9 April 2021; plus a hand search of reference lists.
- **Search string**: ("antibiotics" OR "anti-bacterial agents" OR "amoxicillin") AND ("dental implant" OR "implantology" OR "implant placement" OR "implant failure") — all [All Fields].
- **Inclusion**: RCTs; ≥3 months follow-up; regimen clearly described; partial/totally edentulous patients. **Exclusion**: local (not systemic) antibiotic application; patients requiring prophylaxis for medical reasons.
- **n**: 11 RCTs (published 2008–2019) — 2144 patients, 4037 implants; 5 single-center (327 patients / 624 implants), 6 multicenter (1817 patients / 3413 implants); 4 in university centers, 7 in private practice. Mean follow-up 4.5 months (range 3–10).
- **Comparators**: 5 RCTs antibiotic vs **placebo** (1028 patients / 1874 implants); 3 RCTs antibiotic vs **no antibiotic** (627 patients / 1358 implants); 3 RCTs compared **different regimens only** (489 patients / 805 implants).
- **Regimens**: amoxicillin in every study (clindamycin 600 mg 1 h preop for penicillin-allergic patients in one). Standard single dose = **2 g amoxicillin 1 h preop**, but one study used 1 g and another 3 g. Postoperative-only regimens ran 7 days; pre+postoperative regimens were 7 days in two studies and short-course 2–3 days in three.
- **Statistics**: Review Manager (RevMan) 5.4; **risk ratio** with 95% CI; fixed-effect model (used because all pooled I² < 40%; the stated rule was random-effects if I² ≥ 40%); significance p < 0.05; heterogeneity by Chi² and I²; **unit of analysis = the dental implant**.
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane **RoB 2** across 5 domains (randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, outcome measurement, selection of the reported result); plotted with Robvis.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Study selection

749 records identified → 710 titles/abstracts read → 698 excluded → 12 full texts + 2 hand-searched (14 evaluated) → 3 excluded (1 quasi-randomized [25], 1 outcome not early implant failure [24], 1 follow-up only 8 weeks [23]) → **11 included**.

### Risk of bias (RoB 2)

| Category | Studies |
|---|---|
| Low overall risk of bias | 4 |
| "Some concerns" overall | 7 |
| Highest-risk domain | Deviations from intended interventions |
| Lowest-risk domains | Missing outcome data; measurement of the outcome |

### Individual-trial direction

- Of the 8 RCTs comparing prophylaxis vs no antibiotic/placebo, **only one found a statistically significant difference** (the trial comparing preoperative amoxicillin 2 g *or* clindamycin 600 mg vs no antibiotic). The others nonetheless recorded fewer early failures in the antibiotic arms.
- Four RCTs compared preoperative vs pre+postoperative: **no statistically significant difference in any**.
- One trial compared postoperative-only vs preoperative and vs pre+postoperative: no significant difference.

### Absolute failure rates

| Arm | Early implant failure |
|---|---|
| Antibiotic groups | 1.55% |
| No antibiotic / placebo groups | 4.61% |

### Meta-analyses (fixed-effect)

| Comparison | Studies | RR (95% CI) | p | Heterogeneity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Antibiotic prophylaxis (any regimen) vs none/placebo | 8 | **0.30 (0.19–0.47)** | <0.00001 | I²=0%, p=0.54 |
| Preoperative-only vs pre+postoperative | 4 | 0.57 (0.21–1.55) | 0.27 | I²=0%, p=0.37 |
| Single preoperative dose vs no antibiotic | 6 | **0.34 (0.21–0.53)** | <0.00001 | I²=0%, p=0.61 |

**Critical caveat on the regimen meta-analysis**: in two of the four studies in the pre- vs pre+postoperative comparison there were **no implant failures in either arm**, so the effect was not estimable there — the pooled result rested on only two studies [17,19].

### Comparison with concurrent syntheses (as reported by the authors)

- Canullo et al. — prophylaxis prevents early implant failure in healthy patients (concordant).
- Romandini et al. — prophylaxis necessary, but evidence insufficient for a specific dose; **postoperative antibiotics not justified** (concordant on the postoperative point).
- Kim et al. — prophylaxis reduces early-failure risk, but a standardized protocol still needs research.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Small sample** across the pooled evidence base, with low event rates producing wide confidence intervals.
- **Unclear/some-concerns risk of bias in 7 of 11 RCTs**, especially blinding of participants and researchers and deviations from intended interventions — this is precisely the design weakness that the later placebo-controlled-only reviews (Momand 2024, Dubedout 2025) filtered out, and it is the most likely source of the discordance between this page's large positive RR and their null results.
- Few RCTs meet even the 3-month minimum follow-up for this question.
- **No RCTs compared different preoperative amoxicillin doses**, so dose selection is unsupported by direct evidence.
- No subgroup analysis by **surgical complexity** (simple vs complex), by **grafting/sinus-lift involvement**, or by antibiotic class beyond the amoxicillin/clindamycin pair.
- Included RCTs were healthy patients only — the authors argue this makes the protective effect *indisputable* in at-risk patients, but that is an inference, not a measured result (no at-risk population was studied).
- Briefing-value audit (per INGEST.md Step 2 rule): all numbers on this page were read directly from the PMC artifact; the briefing's DOI values (10.3390/antibiotics10060798 / 10.1080/10717544.2018.1477855) disagree with the artifact and were **not** used. No briefing-vs-paper numeric discrepancy otherwise.
- Future work proposed by the authors: separate healthy vs systemically-diseased populations, differentiate oral-disease profiles, compare simple vs complex implant surgery, and run RCTs of different single preoperative doses; also investigate **metronidazole** and **local antibacterial delivery systems** for the osseointegration window.

## 6. Related Work

- Torof 2023 (Medicina) — SR+MA reaching the same regimen conclusion (single preoperative amoxicillin 2 g; no postoperative extension).
- Esposito 2013/2026 Cochrane — the long-running Cochrane line on antibiotics at implant placement; concordant on benefit direction and on single-dose sufficiency.
- Momand 2024 (BMC Oral Health) — placebo-controlled, double-blinded, low/moderate-RoB RCTs only, finding **no** significant benefit (RR 0.66, NS; NNT 143); this review's inclusion of 7 "some concerns" RCTs is the leading explanation for the divergence.
- Dubedout 2025 (Med Buccale Chir Buccale) — the page that already cited this review as ref 36; double-blind placebo-only, also null.
- Romandini et al. (network meta-analysis) — dose/regimen ranking; cited here as concordant on the "no postoperative antibiotics" conclusion.
- Canullo et al.; Kim et al. — concurrent positive-directed syntheses cited by the authors.

## 7. Glossary

- **Early implant failure**: loss of a dental implant before or during abutment connection / before prosthetic loading, due to failure of osseointegration; ~2% of cases overall in the authors' framing.
- **Late implant failure**: loss after prosthetic loading.
- **RR (risk ratio)**: ratio of failure risk in the antibiotic arm to that in the control arm; RR 0.30 = 70% relative risk reduction.
- **Fixed-effect model**: pooling that assumes one common true effect; used here because all I² values were < 40%.
- **I²**: proportion of between-study variability attributable to heterogeneity rather than chance; 0% here indicates consistent effects across trials.
- **RoB 2**: Cochrane risk-of-bias tool (version 2) for randomized trials — 5 domains, classified low / some concerns / high.
- **PRISMA**: Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses.
- **PROSPERO**: international prospective register of systematic review protocols.
