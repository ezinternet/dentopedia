## Why Ingested

No held paper evaluated segmented full-arch designs combined with a fully digital workflow for mandibular ISFCDs. Segmented prostheses distribute mechanical stress differently than monolithic frameworks; this provides 3-year survival and complication data for a clinically relevant alternative to one-piece monolithic full-arch construction.

## Three-line Summary

Prospective consecutive series of 14 patients (119 implants, 14 segmented mandibular ISFCDs) fabricated using fully digital workflow (intraoral scan, digital design, milling/printing); followed 24–48 months (mean 36mo); primary outcomes: ISR, PSR, MBL; secondary: biological/technical complications and PROMs.

ISR and PSR 100%; mean MBL 0.71±0.24mm; peri-implant mucositis 4.20% (5 implants), peri-implantitis 0.84% (1 implant); zero biomechanical complications (no framework fractures, screw loosening, or chipping); patient satisfaction 9.2/10 VAS.

Segmented mandibular ISFCDs with full digital workflow showed excellent 3-year outcomes with no biomechanical failures; the segmented design combined with digital manufacture appears reliable for mandibular full-arch rehabilitation.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First prospective data specifically on segmented full-arch mandibular ISFCDs with fully digital workflow
- 100% ISR and PSR at 3 years — benchmark outcome
- Zero biomechanical complications: no framework fractures, screw loosening, or chipping
- MBL 0.71mm at mean 36mo — within clinically acceptable range
- Peri-implantitis 0.84% — notably low; peri-implant mucositis 4.2%
- High patient satisfaction (9.2/10 VAS)

## 5. Limitations

- Small sample (n=14 patients) — underpowered for rare complication detection
- Prospective consecutive series (no control group) — no comparison to monolithic or non-segmented designs
- Abstract only — segmented design details (number of segments, connector design, material) unavailable
- Mean follow-up 36mo — intermediate-term; 5–10yr data needed
