## Why Ingested

Cochrane systematic review (5th update) providing high-certainty RCT-level evidence on optimal dental recall intervals in primary care adults. Directly relevant to clinical scheduling policy and supports [[periodontics/farooqi-2015-periodontal-maintenance-recall-interval-sr]], which established recall interval evidence for periodontal maintenance patients. Fee 2020 extends that evidence base by examining caries, gingival health, and OHRQoL outcomes across 6-month, 24-month, and risk-based recall strategies in a general primary care population.

## Three-line Summary

Cochrane SR (5th update, 2 RCTs, n=1736) comparing dental recall intervals of 6-month, 24-month, and risk-based in primary care patients (Norway: children/adolescents; UK: adults).
In adults (4-year follow-up, high certainty), risk-based and 24-month recall showed little/no difference vs 6-month recall for caries surfaces (MD −0.60 to 0.15), gingival bleeding, and OHRQoL (OHIP-14).
Extending recall intervals beyond 6 months is not clinically detrimental for adults; evidence for children remains insufficient and very low certainty.

## 세줄요약

코크란 체계적 고찰(5차 업데이트, 2 RCT, n=1736)로 1차 의료기관 성인 및 소아·청소년에서 치과 검진 간격 (Dental Recall Interval, DRI) 6개월·24개월·위험 기반 리콜 (Risk-based Recall)을 비교하였다.
성인 4년 추적(고확실성)에서 위험 기반 및 24개월 DRI는 6개월 대비 우식 경험 지수 (DMFS/dmfs), 치은 출혈, 구강건강 관련 삶의 질 (Oral Health-Related Quality of Life, OHRQoL)에서 임상적으로 의미 있는 차이가 없었다.
성인에서 6개월 초과 간격으로 연장해도 임상적 손실이 없으나, 소아·청소년 근거는 매우 낮은 확실성으로 불충분하다.

## Key Contributions

1. First high-certainty RCT evidence that risk-based and 24-month recall intervals are equivalent to 6-month recall for adult primary care patients across caries, gingival bleeding, and OHRQoL outcomes.
2. Demonstrated that risk-based recall (individualized by clinical risk assessment) performs no better or worse than fixed 6-month recall — challenging the rationale for individualized scheduling as a quality improvement over extended fixed intervals.
3. Confirmed that extending recall intervals is not clinically detrimental for adults, providing policy-level justification for moving away from the universal 6-month recall recommendation.
4. Highlighted the persistent evidence gap for children and adolescents, where available RCT evidence remains very low certainty.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review of randomized controlled trials
- **Search**: Cochrane Oral Health Group Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase (dates not specified in abstract; 5th update of 2005 review)
- **Inclusion criteria**: RCTs assessing different dental recall intervals in primary care settings
- **Included studies**: 2 RCTs, n=1736 total
  - **RCT 1 (Norway)**: Public dental clinic; participants <20 years old (3-5 year olds and 16-20 year olds analyzed separately); 12-month vs 24-month recall; 2-year follow-up; very low certainty evidence
  - **RCT 2 (UK)**: General dental practices; adults (regular attenders, ≥1 dental visit in previous 2 years); 6-month vs 24-month vs risk-based recall; 4-year follow-up; high-certainty evidence
- **Primary outcomes**: Caries increment (DMFS/dmfs), gingival bleeding, OHRQoL (OHIP-14), adverse effects
- **Secondary outcomes**: Moderate-to-extensive caries prevalence (ICDAS 3-6)
- **Risk of bias**: Assessed per Cochrane standards; certainty rated by GRADE

## Key Results

### Adults (UK RCT, 4-year follow-up)

| Comparison | Outcome | MD / RR (95% CI) | n | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Risk-based vs 6-month | Caries surfaces (DMFS) | MD 0.15 (−0.77 to 1.08) | 1478 | High |
| Risk-based vs 6-month | Gingival bleeding | MD 0.78% (−1.17% to 2.73%) | 1472 | High |
| Risk-based vs 6-month | OHRQoL (OHIP-14) | MD −0.35 (−1.02 to 0.32) | 1551 | High |
| Risk-based vs 6-month | Caries prevalence (ICDAS 3-6) | RR 1.04 (0.99–1.09) | — | Moderate |
| 24-month vs 6-month | Caries surfaces (DMFS) | MD −0.60 (−2.54 to 1.34) | 271 | Moderate–High |
| 24-month vs 6-month | Gingival bleeding | MD −0.91% (−5.02% to 3.20%) | 271 | Moderate–High |
| 24-month vs 6-month | OHRQoL (OHIP-14) | MD −0.24 (−1.55 to 1.07) | 305 | Moderate–High |
| Risk-based vs 24-month | Caries / gingival bleeding | Probably little/no difference | — | Moderate |

### Children/Adolescents (Norway RCT, 2-year follow-up)

| Age group | Outcome | MD (95% CI) | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3–5 years | dmfs increment | MD 0.90 (−0.16 to 1.96) | Very low |
| 16–20 years | DMFS | MD 0.86 (−0.03 to 1.75) | Very low |

Note: Confidence intervals cross zero for both age groups; unclear if differences are clinically important.

### Overall Conclusion

For adults in primary care, risk-based recall = 6-month recall = 24-month recall in terms of caries surfaces, gingival bleeding, and OHRQoL. Extending beyond 6-monthly intervals is not clinically detrimental in adults.

## Limitations

1. Only 2 eligible RCTs identified despite broad search — evidence base is narrow.
2. Children/adolescent evidence is very low certainty; results cannot be generalized to pediatric populations.
3. UK adult RCT included only regular dental attenders (≥1 visit in prior 2 years), limiting applicability to irregular attenders or high-risk populations.
4. Full text not accessible (Cochrane paywall); content derived from structured abstract only — detailed subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses not available.
5. Comparator recall intervals are fixed (6, 24 months) or risk-stratified per protocol; results may not apply to ultra-short (<6 month) or ultra-long (>24 month) intervals.
6. No evidence on cost-effectiveness of recall strategies.

## Glossary

- **DMFS**: Decayed, Missing, Filled Surfaces — permanent dentition caries index
- **dmfs**: decayed, missing, filled surfaces — primary dentition caries index
- **OHIP-14**: Oral Health Impact Profile (14-item) — validated OHRQoL instrument
- **ICDAS 3-6**: International Caries Detection and Assessment System scores 3–6 (moderate to extensive lesions)
- **Risk-based recall**: Recall interval individualized by clinician assessment of patient caries and periodontal risk
- **OHRQoL**: Oral Health-Related Quality of Life
