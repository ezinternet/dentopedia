## One-line Summary
Narrative review of 30 studies (mostly in vitro) on biomimetic zinc-carbonate hydroxyapatite (Zn-CHA) oral-care products: evidence of enamel/dentin remineralization, erosion protection, and dentin-tubule occlusion for hypersensitivity, but few RCTs and moderate risk of bias.

## 한줄요약
생체모방 zinc-carbonate hydroxyapatite(Zn-CHA) 구강제품 30편(대부분 in vitro) 서술적 리뷰: 법랑질/상아질 재광화·침식보호·상아세관 폐쇄(과민증)에 효과 신호, 그러나 RCT 부족·중등도 비뚤림.

## 1. Document Information
- Biomimetics (Basel) 2023;8(1):71. MDPI. PMC9944842. DOI 10.3390/biomimetics8010071.
- Pavia (Butera/Scribante) + Bologna (Lelli) groups. Search PubMed/Scopus 2003–2023; 5065 → 2076 de-duplicated → 30 included.
- Type: literature review with a flowchart and risk-of-bias appraisal (not a PROSPERO-registered SR-MA).

## 2. Key Contributions
- Frames Zn-CHA as "biomimetic" for two reasons: (1) ionic substitution within the apatite crystal, (2) chemical/physical identity (shape, size, ionic substitution) to tooth apatite → high affinity/adhesion to enamel and dentin.
- Groups the 30 studies into enamel protection/remineralization (21) and dentin-hypersensitivity management (9).
- Positions Zn-CHA against the two incumbent systems: fluoride (forms less-soluble fluorapatite) and CPP-ACP (inhibits demin, promotes remin of early lesions, but "less effective than traditional fluoride toothpastes").

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Keywords: "zinc hydroxyapatite"/"hydroxyapatite"/"apatite"/"biomimetic" AND "enamel"/"dentin".
- Most included studies in vitro on extracted human or bovine teeth; interventions were toothpastes (~86%) and mouthwashes (~10%); a few clinical trials.
- Mechanistic rationale: non-stoichiometric Zn-carbonate composition, micro-aggregate size (high surface area), low crystallinity, elongated morphology, and surface disorder → increased solubility + affinity for natural mineral. Carbonate substitutes the PO4 site (raises solubility). Zn substitutes a fraction of Ca → slow antibacterial Zn2+ release.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Enamel protection (erosion): multiple in vitro studies (Poggio, Colombo, Bradna, Alessandri Bonetti) show Zn-CHA toothpastes deposit acid-resistant mineral layers and protect erosively challenged enamel; some comparators (conventional NaF) were not inferior, and results varied by product/experimental model.
- Enamel remineralization: Tschoppe (bovine) — non-fluoride Zn-CHA toothpaste gave MORE remineralization of caries-like lesions than fluoride toothpaste; Lelli (8-wk clinical, SEM) — HA-rich coating deposition; Hegazy RCT in children — Zn-CHA mouthwash remineralized early lesions comparably to fluoride mouthwash. Also proposed to prevent secondary caries at restoration margins (Butera).
- Dentin/hypersensitivity: Zn-CHA occludes dentinal tubules with some acid resistance; clinical trials (Orsini) showed reduced DH at 4/8 weeks and even after a 3-day regimen; Butera trials — greater DH/pain reduction than conventional fluoride toothpaste, and effect in MIH.
- Risk of bias: overall moderate; most evidence in vitro; few RCTs.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Predominantly in vitro; small number and heterogeneity of clinical trials; some experimental models (highly polished enamel, single acid exposure) likely underestimated Zn-CHA. Undeclared HA percentages in many products. Calls for more clinical studies.

## 7. Glossary
- **Zn-CHA (zinc-carbonate hydroxyapatite)**: biomimetic apatite with Zn2+ (for Ca2+) and CO3 (for PO4) substitutions; core active ingredient of BioRepair-type products.
- **CPP-ACP**: casein phosphopeptide–amorphous calcium phosphate; calcium/phosphate delivery complex.
- **Fluorapatite (FA/FHA)**: less-soluble apatite formed when fluoride substitutes OH.
- **DH**: dentin hypersensitivity. **MIH**: molar-incisor hypomineralization.
