## Why Ingested
Split-mouth RCT directly comparing a wound-dressing material (allogenic amnion-chorion membrane, ACM) against standard collagen dressing (CLD) at the palatal free gingival graft (FGG) donor site — the same procedural family as [[suture-wound-closure/shakiliyeva-2025-conventional-modified-sling-suture-free-gingival-graft]] (FGG suture/graft technique), but this paper addresses donor-site wound-dressing choice rather than suture pattern, filling a gap in the wiki's FGG donor-site-healing coverage.

## Three-line Summary
Pilot split-mouth RCT (n=19 participants, 2 palatal donor sites each) randomized to allogenic amnion-chorion membrane (ACM, test) vs collagen dressing (CLD, control) after free gingival graft (FGG) harvest, evaluated clinically and histologically at 4, 10, 14, 21, 28, 45, and 60 days post-op.
No significant differences were found between ACM and CLD in wound area reduction, epithelialization, color change, pain, sensibility, epithelium/keratin thickness, inflammatory cell/fibroblast/blood vessel counts, or collagen density at any time point.
ACM was comparable, not superior, to collagen dressing for palatal FGG donor site healing — both are equally viable dressing options; registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03713073).

## 2. Key Contributions
- First (per abstract) split-mouth RCT directly comparing allogenic amnion-chorion membrane (ACM) against collagen dressing (CLD) at the palatal FGG donor site, with paired within-patient design controlling for individual healing variability
- Combines clinical outcomes (wound area, epithelialization, color match, bleeding, pain, sensibility) with histomorphometric analysis (epithelium/keratin thickness, inflammatory cells, fibroblasts, blood vessels, collagen density) from biopsies taken at baseline and 10 days post-op
- Long observation window (up to 60 days) spanning early wound closure through late remodeling

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Pilot, split-mouth, randomized controlled trial (human)
- **n**: 19 participants, 38 palatal donor sites (right + left, one ACM one CLD per patient) undergoing FGG harvest
- **Outcomes**: Clinical — wound area size, epithelialization degree, color match, bleeding/hemostasis, pain, sensibility. Histological — epithelium/keratin layer thickness, inflammatory cell count, fibroblast count, blood vessel count, collagen density (from biopsies at baseline and day 10)
- **Timepoints**: Days 4, 10, 14, 21, 28, 45, 60 post-operatively
- **Registration**: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03713073

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
No numeric tables were retrievable from the abstract-only text. Per the reported summary: no statistically significant differences between ACM and CLD groups in reduction of wound area size, degree of epithelialization, color change, pain, sensibility, thickness of epithelium and keratin layer, number of inflammatory cells/fibroblasts/blood vessels, or density of collagen — across all evaluation timepoints (4–60 days).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Full text (Introduction, detailed Methods, Discussion, numeric results tables, and figures) was not retrieved — only the abstract, background/methods/results/conclusions summary, and plain-language summary were available from the PDF
- Pilot-scale trial (n=19) — likely underpowered to detect small-to-moderate differences between dressings; a null/equivalence result from a pilot should not be read as definitive non-inferiority
- Split-mouth design controls for inter-patient variability but the histological biopsy sub-analysis timepoint (day 10 only) limits characterization of later remodeling phases (day 21–60 clinical-only)

## 7. Glossary
- **ACM (Amnion-Chorion Membrane)**: Allogenic (donor-derived) placental membrane graft used as a biologic wound dressing, containing extracellular matrix components and growth factors
- **CLD (Collagen Dressing)**: Standard-of-care collagen-based wound dressing used to protect open palatal donor sites during secondary-intention healing
- **FGG (Free Gingival Graft)**: Autogenous keratinized soft-tissue graft harvested from the palate, leaving an open donor-site wound that heals by secondary intention
- **Split-mouth design**: Within-patient randomized trial design where two comparable sites (here, right and left palate) in the same patient receive different treatments, controlling for patient-level confounders
- **Histomorphometry**: Quantitative microscopic measurement of tissue structures (e.g., epithelium thickness, cell counts, collagen density) from biopsy sections
