## Why Ingested

Fills a gap this wiki did not previously cover: rheumatoid arthritis (RA)-driven TMJ involvement and how ultrasonography (US) performs as a detection tool for it, as opposed to TMJ osteoarthritis of non-rheumatic origin (already covered by [[tmj/song-2023-understanding-tmj-osteoarthritis-pathophysiology]] and [[tmj/kim-2023-treatment-tmj-osteoarthritis]]). Ingested alongside two companion papers in the same batch — [[tmj/chana-2026-ultrasound-tmj-juvenile-idiopathic-arthritis-sr]] (same imaging question, pediatric JIA population) and [[tmj/aiello-2025-tmj-arthritis-rheumatic-diseases-rehabilitative-pain-relief-sr]] (treatment side of TMJ arthritis in rheumatic disease) — so that detection and management evidence for rheumatic TMJ involvement sit next to each other.

## Three-line Summary

This scoping review (PRISMA-ScR, OSF-registered protocol) maps the evidence on ultrasonography (US) for evaluating temporomandibular joint (TMJ) involvement in rheumatoid arthritis (RA): of 88 records screened, only 5 studies (154 RA patients total, mostly women with moderate-to-high disease activity) met inclusion criteria.

US consistently identified joint effusion, synovial thickening, and disc alterations across the included studies; only one diagnostic-accuracy study (Guiducci et al. 2025, n=50) reported high performance — 92.4% sensitivity and 90.3% specificity for joint effusion versus MRI — while the same single study found markedly weaker performance for disc displacement (72%) and condylar surface irregularities (53%), and associations between US findings and clinical indices (DAS28, HAQ) or pain were observed only inconsistently across studies.

As a scoping review, this work maps rather than ranks or pools evidence (no meta-analysis performed); the authors' own verdict is that US is a practical, non-invasive, affordable adjunct/initial examination that should complement rather than replace MRI, particularly for structural assessment — with methodological heterogeneity, small samples, and marked operator dependence flagged as the main limitations of the underlying evidence.

## 세줄요약

이 스코핑 리뷰(PRISMA-ScR 준수, OSF 사전등록)는 류마티스 관절염(Rheumatoid Arthritis, RA) 환자의 측두하악관절(Temporomandibular Joint, TMJ) 침범을 평가하는 초음파(Ultrasonography, US)의 근거를 지도화한다 — 88건 스크리닝 중 단 5편(RA 환자 총 154명, 대부분 여성, 중등도~고도 질병활성도)만 포함 기준을 충족했다.

포함된 연구들에서 US는 관절삼출(joint effusion), 활막비후(synovial thickening), 디스크 변화를 일관되게 식별했다. 단 1편의 진단정확도 연구(Guiducci 등 2025, n=50)만이 관절삼출에 대해 MRI 대비 민감도 92.4%·특이도 90.3%라는 높은 수치를 보고했으며, 같은 연구에서 디스크 변위(72%)와 과두 표면 불규칙성(53%)에 대한 성능은 뚜렷이 낮았다. 임상지표(질병활성도 점수 28, Disease Activity Score-28, DAS28; 건강평가설문, Health Assessment Questionnaire, HAQ)·통증과 US 소견 간 연관성은 연구 간 비일관적으로 관찰되었다.

스코핑 리뷰라는 설계 특성상 이 논문은 근거를 서열화·통합(메타분석)하지 않고 지도화만 한다. 저자들의 결론은 US가 실용적이고 비침습적이며 저렴한 보조/초기 검사로서 특히 구조적 평가에서 MRI를 대체하기보다 보완해야 한다는 것이며, 방법론적 이질성·소규모 표본·뚜렷한 시술자 의존성(operator dependence)을 근거 기반의 주된 한계로 지적했다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First dedicated evidence map specifically on US for TMJ evaluation in RA (as opposed to TMJ osteoarthritis broadly or juvenile idiopathic arthritis), using the COCOPOP (Condition–Context–Population) framework and PRISMA-ScR methodology.
- Quantifies just how thin the evidence base is for a common systemic disease: 88 records screened → only 5 studies → 154 RA patients total, spanning 2003–2025 and 3 countries (Italy, Egypt, Turkey).
- Isolates the widely-cited 92.4%/90.3% sensitivity/specificity figure for joint effusion to a single diagnostic-accuracy study (Guiducci et al. 2025, n=50) against MRI, and shows that the same study's performance drops sharply for disc displacement (72%) and condylar surface irregularities (53%) — diagnostic capacity varies by anatomical target, not a single number for "US accuracy in RA."
- Foregrounds operator dependence and total absence of a standardized TMJ-specific US protocol (transducer frequency, probe positioning, patient position, static vs. dynamic acquisition, examiner background) as a first-order limiter of reproducibility, not incidental noise.
- Notes that associations between US findings and clinical/serological indices (DAS28, HAQ, pain) were inconsistent — imaging findings do not reliably track how the patient feels or composite systemic disease activity, since DAS28 itself does not include TMJ assessment.
- Positions US explicitly as a complementary, non-invasive, radiation-free adjunct/initial examination — not a replacement for MRI, especially for structural (disc, bone) assessment.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- PRISMA-ScR (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews) methodology; protocol registered on OSF (10.17605/OSF.IO/UQWSH).
- Search strategy: COCOPOP (Condition–Context–Population), Condition = TMJ alterations, Context = assessed by US, Population = individuals diagnosed with RA.
- Databases searched (October 2025, no language/date restriction): PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, plus gray literature (OpenGrey, Google Scholar — first 200 results by relevance) and reference-list screening. Monthly automated search alerts activated post-search.
- Eligible: observational designs (cross-sectional, cohort, case-control) and clinical trials evaluating TMJ changes with US in RA patients, any disease activity/age/sex/ethnicity. Excluded: case reports/series, conference abstracts, letters, narrative/systematic reviews, animal/in vitro studies, studies with other concomitant autoimmune/musculoskeletal TMJ-affecting disease.
- Two independent reviewers screened titles/abstracts and full texts (EndNote deduplication → Rayyan screening); disagreements resolved by discussion or a third reviewer. Non-English/Portuguese/Spanish articles machine-translated. Corresponding authors contacted up to 3 times for inaccessible full texts or incomplete data.
- Data synthesized descriptively/narratively in tabular form — no meta-analysis, no pooled effect size, no formal risk-of-bias tool (explicitly stated as out of scope for a scoping review, though the authors do discuss methodological quality qualitatively in the Discussion).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 88 records retrieved → 40 after deduplication → 7 selected for full-text review → 5 studies included (1 inaccessible despite author contact, 1 lacked RA-group absolute data, 6 gray-literature hits were duplicates).
- 154 RA patients total across the 5 studies (Melchiorre 2003 Italy n=22; El-Melegy 2017 Egypt n=20; Elshoura 2020 Egypt n=11; Becenen Durmuş 2025 Turkey n=51 + 51 controls; Guiducci 2025 Italy n=50). Predominantly female where reported; mean age 45.4–53 years where reported for the RA subgroup specifically.
- **Diagnostic accuracy — from ONE study only** (Guiducci et al. 2025, n=50, vs. MRI as reference): joint effusion 92.4% sensitivity / 90.3% specificity; disc displacement 72%; condylar surface irregularities 53%. An earlier study (Melchiorre 2003) found US detected more pathological changes than MRI overall but with lower specificity (75.0%), particularly for condylar changes.
- Egyptian studies: US detected TMJ erosions in 57.5%, effusion in 62.5%, disc displacement in 52.5% of cases, with detection rates comparable to but slightly below MRI (77.5% vs. 82.5% for MRI).
- Turkish study: RA group had greater articular disc thickness than controls (p<0.001); proposed a diagnostic cut-off of 1.55 mm disc thickness for dysfunction; effusion more frequent in RA (7.8%) than controls, statistically significant on one side (p=0.041) but not the other (p=0.169).
- Associations between US findings and DAS28/mHAQ/pain were significant in some studies (El-Melegy 2017: effusion and disc displacement significantly associated with DAS28 and mHAQ) but inconsistent overall — erosions detected by US alone did not consistently correlate with clinical measures, and structural changes were sometimes found in asymptomatic patients.
- **[미검증-flag for reader]**: the 92.4%/90.3% figure is a single-study, single-target (joint effusion) result versus MRI, not a pooled estimate — this scoping review performed no meta-analysis and this figure should never be cited as "ultrasound's sensitivity/specificity for RA TMJ involvement" generally.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Sparse evidence base**: only 5 studies / 154 patients for a disease affecting ~0.5–1% of the global population with TMJ involvement reported in 40–55% of RA patients — the thinness of the base is itself a finding, not just a caveat.
- **Operator dependence**: transducer frequency (7–16 MHz across studies), probe positioning, patient position (supine vs. seated), static vs. dynamic acquisition, and examiner background (radiologist, rheumatologist, sonographer) varied across every included study with no standardized TMJ-specific US protocol — this is presented as the limitation most likely to determine whether results transfer to a new practice, since US performance is a property of the operator, not the machine.
- Predominantly cross-sectional designs with small samples; only one study included a healthy control group; few studies directly compared against MRI.
- Examiners were often not blinded to clinical status in the included primary studies, raising observer-bias risk; confounders (disease duration, pharmacological treatment, dental/occlusal status) were not uniformly controlled.
- As a scoping review, no formal risk-of-bias assessment or GRADE certainty rating was conducted — a explicit design choice, not an oversight, but it means findings cannot be graded for certainty the way a systematic review + meta-analysis could.
- Disc displacement — where US performs worse against MRI — is noted by the authors as not the primary pathological feature of RA (which is driven by autoimmune synovial inflammation), so this shortfall matters less clinically than it might first appear.
- Authors call for well-designed longitudinal studies, standardized imaging protocols (acquisition + interpretation), and integration with clinical/serological measures (DAS28, HAQ, RF/anti-CCP) to define the diagnostic and prognostic value of US in this population.

## 6. Related Work

- Cites a prior systematic review reporting that US has acceptable accuracy for joint effusion and disc displacement generally, but limited/unvalidated performance for condylar bone changes requiring CBCT as reference standard — consistent with this scoping review's own effusion-vs-bone performance gap.
- Cites Zaman et al. (2024, meta-analysis) and Su et al. on general TMJ US vs. MRI performance: US performs well for effusion/active inflammation, MRI remains superior for overall TMJ dysfunction detection and structural abnormalities — same pattern as found here, but in a non-RA-specific population.
- Draws an explicit parallel to pediatric juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA) literature (Kirkhus et al.) on US detecting synovitis-related capsular widening with moderate MRI correlation, and flags that non-validated cut-off values and scan-plane/measurement-level variability affect diagnostic performance similarly in that population — directly relevant to [[tmj/chana-2026-ultrasound-tmj-juvenile-idiopathic-arthritis-sr]], whose broader sensitivity range (21–85%) across targets in JIA sits below this review's single-study 92.4% for effusion in adult RA; the gap is better read as a target-difficulty hypothesis (effusion easier to see than condylar bone change) than as evidence that US performs better in RA than in JIA.
- Complements [[tmj/aiello-2025-tmj-arthritis-rheumatic-diseases-rehabilitative-pain-relief-sr]], which covers rehabilitative/pain-relief management of TMJ arthritis in rheumatic disease — this paper is the detection side of the same clinical problem.
- Sits alongside the wiki's existing TMJ osteoarthritis pathophysiology/diagnosis and treatment reviews ([[tmj/song-2023-understanding-tmj-osteoarthritis-pathophysiology]], [[tmj/kim-2023-treatment-tmj-osteoarthritis]]) as the RA-specific counterpart — those cover TMJ OA generally; this maps the autoimmune/inflammatory subset.

## 7. Glossary

- **RA (Rheumatoid Arthritis)**: 류마티스 관절염 — chronic systemic autoimmune disease with persistent synovial inflammation and progressive joint damage.
- **TMJ (Temporomandibular Joint)**: 측두하악관절.
- **US (Ultrasonography)**: 초음파 검사 — non-invasive, radiation-free, real-time, operator-dependent imaging modality.
- **MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)**: 자기공명영상 — reference standard for TMJ soft-tissue/early inflammatory changes.
- **CBCT (Cone-Beam Computed Tomography)**: 콘빔 전산화단층촬영 — better suited to bone assessment, less sensitive for early inflammatory change.
- **DAS28 (Disease Activity Score-28)**: 질병활성도 점수 28 — composite RA systemic disease-activity index; does not include TMJ assessment.
- **HAQ / mHAQ (Health Assessment Questionnaire)**: 건강평가설문(변형) — patient-reported functional status measure.
- **ACR/EULAR (American College of Rheumatology/European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology) 2010 criteria**: RA 분류기준.
- **RF (Rheumatoid Factor) / ACPA (Anti-Citrullinated Protein Antibody)**: 류마티스인자/항시트룰린화단백질항체 — RA autoantibodies used for seropositivity.
- **PRISMA-ScR (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews)**: 스코핑 리뷰용 PRISMA 확장 보고 지침.
- **COCOPOP (Condition–Context–Population)**: 스코핑 리뷰 검색전략 프레임워크.
- **OMERACT (Outcome Measures in Rheumatology)**: 류마티스 결과지표 표준화 그룹 — source of the standardized US scoring systems some included studies used.
