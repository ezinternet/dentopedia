## Why Ingested

The wiki's only brand-comparison page, [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/telles-2025-clear-aligner-brand-virtual-setup-comparison]], compares Invisalign, ClearCorrect, 3M Clarity, and Spark — but only at the *virtual treatment setup* level (planned aligner/attachment counts), not achieved-vs-predicted clinical accuracy. This paper adds Eon Aligner (Eon Dental, Amman, Jordan), a brand not previously covered anywhere in the wiki, and measures actual predicted-vs-achieved tooth movement head-to-head against Invisalign — filling both a brand gap and a methodology gap (real accuracy, not just plan output).

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study (n=80 recruited [40 Invisalign, 40 Eon Aligner], 4 excluded for absence of teeth; Riyadh Elm University Department of Orthodontics, Jan 2023–Jan 2025) comparing software-predicted (final treatment-plan step) versus achieved (pre-refinement, first aligner set only) tooth movement across interpremolar width, intercanine width, mesiodistal rotation of incisors, and horizontal/vertical movements of canines and premolars, measured via 3Shape Clear Aligner Studio superimposition.

Both brands showed good-to-excellent agreement for transverse width changes (interpremolar/intercanine, mean differences 0.06–0.34 mm, near Camardella's 0.4 mm clinical-relevance threshold) with no significant between-brand difference in magnitude; Eon Aligner outperformed Invisalign for mesiodistal rotation (differences 1.9–4.2°, though both brands still showed significant predicted-vs-achieved bias) and for horizontal movement (mean differences Invisalign 0.206–0.341 mm vs Eon 0.015–0.24 mm); vertical movement predictability was poor for both, with Invisalign described as "unreliable" and Eon as moderate but fluctuating (except first premolars).

Both clear aligner systems require systematic over-correction for most measured tooth movements — vertical/intrusive corrections in particular should not be planned with aligners alone — and this is the first study to directly compare Eon Aligner's clinical predictability against Invisalign.

## 세줄요약

후향적 연구(n=80 모집[Invisalign 40명, Eon Aligner 40명], 결손치로 4명 제외; Riyadh Elm University 교정과, 2023년 1월–2025년 1월) — 소프트웨어 예측(치료계획 마지막 단계) 대 실제 달성(리파인먼트 이전, 초기 교정장치 세트만) 치아이동을 소구치간 폭경(interpremolar width)·견치간 폭경(intercanine width)·절치 근원심 회전(mesiodistal rotation)·견치·소구치의 수평/수직 이동으로 비교, 3Shape Clear Aligner Studio 중첩(superimposition)으로 측정.

양 브랜드 모두 횡적 폭경 변화는 양호~우수한 일치도(평균차 0.06–0.34mm, Camardella 임상적 유의성 기준 0.4mm에 근접)로 브랜드 간 크기 차이 없음; Eon Aligner가 근원심 회전(차이 1.9–4.2°, 다만 양쪽 다 유의한 예측-달성 편향 존재)과 수평이동(평균차 Invisalign 0.206–0.341mm vs Eon 0.015–0.24mm)에서 Invisalign보다 우수; 수직이동은 양쪽 모두 불량(Invisalign "신뢰불가", Eon은 제1소구치 제외 변동성 있는 보통 수준).

두 시스템 모두 측정된 대부분의 치아이동에서 체계적 과교정(over-correction)이 필요하며 — 특히 수직/압하(intrusive) 이동은 얼라이너 단독 계획에 의존해선 안 됨 — 이 연구는 Eon Aligner의 임상적 예측도를 Invisalign과 직접 비교한 최초 연구다.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Dent J (Basel) 2026;14(4):240
- **DOI**: 10.3390/dj14040240
- **Institution**: Department of Orthodontics, Riyadh Elm University, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia

## 2. Key Contributions
- First study to directly compare the clinical predictability of Eon Aligner against Invisalign (stated explicitly by the authors).
- Isolates first-aligner-set (pre-refinement) accuracy only, avoiding the confound of mid-course/refinement corrections — the same design principle used in [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/saif-2026-clear-aligner-anterior-3d-accuracy]] for Invisalign alone.
- Shows Eon Aligner outperforming Invisalign specifically on rotational and horizontal-movement accuracy, while both remain poor on vertical-movement accuracy — a movement-type asymmetry, not a uniform brand advantage.
- Anchors transverse-width discrepancies (0.06–0.34 mm) against Camardella's published clinical-relevance threshold (>0.4 mm) and flags incisor rotation discrepancies (1.9–4.2°) as potentially clinically significant (contact-point/black-triangle risk).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Retrospective cohort, single institution (Riyadh Elm University), Jan 2023–Jan 2025
- **n**: 80 cases recruited (40 Invisalign, 40 Eon Aligner); 4 excluded for absence of teeth. Eon group: 25F/15M; case mix 8 spacing, 24 crowding, 6 crowding+Class II, 1 crowding+Class III, 1 spacing+Class II. Invisalign group: 23F/17M; case mix 22 crowding, 7 spacing, 7 crowding+Class II, 1 crowding+Class III, 2 spacing+Class II, 1 spacing+Class III. Minimal IPR planned (0.2–0.3 mm/tooth, anterior only).
- **Models**: 3 STL scans per patient — initial, predicted (final treatment-plan step), achieved (pre-refinement, first-aligner-set outcome)
- **Measurement**: 3Shape Clear Aligner Studio superimposition on 3 stable reference points (right/left second-molar mesiobuccal cusp tip + right second-molar central fossa); horizontal movement measured occlusally, vertical frontally, mesiodistal rotation as inter-line angle; intercanine/interpremolar width as cusp-tip/fossa distances
- **Reliability**: single calibrated examiner, 10 readings × 5 measures × 2 time points (2 weeks apart); ICC 0.766–0.892 (satisfactory)
- **Outcomes**: interpremolar width, intercanine width, mesiodistal rotation (central + lateral incisors), horizontal and vertical movement (canines, premolars)
- **Statistics**: ICC for predicted-vs-achieved agreement (poor <0.5, moderate 0.5–0.75, good 0.75–0.9, excellent 0.9–1.0); Wilcoxon matched-pairs (within-brand predicted-vs-achieved and initial-vs-achieved); Welch's t-test (between-brand comparison of mean-difference magnitude); Bonferroni correction when >10 comparisons; α=0.05; SPSS v23.0

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Movement | Invisalign agreement | Eon Aligner agreement | Predicted-vs-achieved bias | Between-brand magnitude difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interpremolar width | Good | Good | Not significant (both) | Not significant |
| Intercanine width | Excellent | Excellent | Invisalign: not significant; Eon: significant | Not significant |
| Mesiodistal rotation (incisors) | Lower agreement | Better agreement than Invisalign | Significant (both) | Not significant |
| Horizontal movement | Moderate | Good–excellent | Significant (both), except Eon 2nd premolar | Invisalign significantly higher discrepancy (1st premolar, canines) |
| Vertical movement | Poor / "unreliable" | Poor–moderate, fluctuating (except 1st premolar) | Significant (both) | Invisalign significantly higher discrepancy (both premolars) |

(Exact p-values are not reproduced in the extracted PMC full text — only significance/non-significance and ICC agreement bands were recoverable; Bonferroni correction was applied per the Methods.)

Numeric differences reported in the Discussion:
- Interpremolar/transverse width: mean differences 0.06–0.34 mm (both brands) — near Camardella's 0.4 mm clinical-relevance threshold.
- Incisor mesiodistal rotation: mean differences 1.9–4.2° — authors consider this range potentially clinically significant (contact-point alignment, black-triangle risk).
- Horizontal movement: mean differences Invisalign 0.206–0.341 mm vs Eon Aligner 0.015–0.24 mm (Eon's range at or below Charalampakis et al.'s published 0.20–0.25 mm; Invisalign's range slightly above it).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Retrospective design; causal inference limited; authors call for RCTs assessing Eon Aligner specifically.
- 40/group considered adequate but authors recommend larger, more generalizable cohorts.
- Confounders not isolated: biological response variability, aligner-wear compliance, attachment shape/size, anatomy.
- 3Shape Clear Aligner Studio may lose precision on very small discrepancies.
- Palatal rugae (gold-standard maxillary superimposition landmark) were unavailable in these scans; second molars were used instead, risking undetected unwanted movement between initial and achieved models.
- Upper and lower jaw data were pooled rather than analyzed intra-arch.
- No evaluation of post-treatment stability (long-term outcome unknown).
- Multiple treating orthodontists (bias mitigated via standardized protocol, objective digital measurement, and examiner calibration).

## 6. Related Work
- Morales-Burruezo et al.: high predictability for both inter-premolar and inter-canine width expansion with Invisalign — consistent with this study's transverse-width findings.
- Zhou et al.: Invisalign's greatest expansion efficacy in the premolar area.
- Lione et al. and Grunheid et al.: maxillary arch expansion may not be fully attained in non-extraction cases — contrasts with this study's achieved transverse expansion.
- Solano-Mendoza et al.: reported limitations predicting Invisalign width expansion in posterior segments — contrasts with this study, attributed by the authors to aligner-material/design evolution since that report.
- Camardella et al.: >0.4 mm in transverse/sagittal planes = clinically relevant threshold, used here to contextualize the 0.06–0.34 mm differences as within/near tolerance.
- Caruso et al.: over-estimation of predicted movement is inevitable for Invisalign — cited to explain the significant predicted-vs-achieved bias seen even where agreement (ICC) was excellent.
- Kravitz et al.: clear aligners perform best within linear translations; predictability drops with movement magnitude/complexity — cited to frame the study's own linear-vs-rotational-vs-vertical accuracy gradient.
- Charalampakis et al.: horizontal-movement mean discrepancies of 0.20–0.25 mm — used as the direct numeric comparator for this study's 0.206–0.341 mm (Invisalign) and 0.015–0.24 mm (Eon) findings; also reported vertical adjustments as unreliable without supplemental stabilization, consistent with this study's vertical-movement results.
- Krieger et al.: Invisalign ineffective at controlling vertical movements, especially anterior intrusion — consistent with this study's "unreliable" Invisalign vertical-movement finding.
- Drake et al.: clear aligners are optimized for linear displacement, not vertical displacement, due to insufficient force vectors — cited as a biomechanical explanation for the vertical-movement weakness observed in both brands here.

## 7. Glossary
- **CAT**: Clear Aligner Therapy
- **ICC**: Interclass (intraclass) Correlation Coefficient — used here both for examiner reliability and for predicted-vs-achieved model agreement (poor/moderate/good/excellent bands)
- **IPR**: Interproximal Reduction
- **STL**: Stereolithography (digital model file format)
- **Watermelon seed effect**: term cited from the literature for aligner slippage/expulsion behavior under bodily-movement force, used to help explain software-prediction error in the Discussion
