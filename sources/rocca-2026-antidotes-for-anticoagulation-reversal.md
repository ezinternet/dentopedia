## Why Ingested

Requested 2026-11-27 for the reversal-agent gaps in [[overviews/antithrombotic-seminar-evidence-check-overview]] (claims E2 warfarin and E3 enoxaparin → protamine). It is a current NEJM review covering every class — VKA, heparins, dabigatran, factor Xa inhibitors — and it states that **no specific antidote exists for low-molecular-weight heparin**. That qualifies the older reviews already held ([[drug/anticoagulants/yee-2019-emergency-reversal-of-anticoagulation]], [[drug/anticoagulants/aldhaeefi-2023-practical-guide-for-anticoagulant-and]]), which list protamine for LMWH but with only ~50–60% reversal — and it bears on the seminar's enoxaparin–protamine mapping. → [[drug/anticoagulants/choudhary-2025-bleeding-thrombosis-noac-dental-management]]

## Three-line Summary

NEJM narrative review (2026) of pharmacologic reversal strategies for anticoagulant-associated major bleeding, organized by anticoagulant and antidote pharmacology, regulatory indication and evidence quality.
Protamine neutralizes unfractionated heparin but no specific antidote exists for LMWH or fondaparinux; 4F-PCC effectively reverses VKAs; idarucizumab reverses dabigatran (delayed rebound possible); andexanet alfa targets factor Xa inhibitors but efficacy-safety, rebound, perioperative use and cost concerns drive off-label 4F-PCC use.
For dentistry, it confirms the antidote map while placing every agent in the major-bleeding setting — dosing, lab monitoring, thrombotic risk and perioperative guidance remain unsettled.

## 7. Glossary

- **Protamine sulfate**: cationic protein that binds and neutralizes heparin.
- **Fondaparinux**: synthetic pentasaccharide factor Xa inhibitor (indirect).
- **Rebound**: return of anticoagulant activity after the antidote wears off.
- **Off-label**: use outside the regulator-approved indication.
