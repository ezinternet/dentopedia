## Why Ingested

This single-blind RCT directly tests *when* to floss relative to brushing (before/after/during) using RMNPI, the same zonal plaque index used in the 5-paper toothbrush/dentifrice synthesis at [[overviews/toothbrush-design-dentifrice-plaque-index-methodology-synthesis]] (that synthesis itself was mined from this paper's reference list). It also sits alongside [[interdental-cleaning/jung-2025-flossing-performance-plaque-removal]] (which asked whether *floss technique quality* predicts plaque removal) as a second angle on the same open question — whether procedural details of flossing (technique vs. sequence/timing) meaningfully change outcomes beyond flossing itself.

## Three-line Summary

Single-blind RCT, n=54 Chinese university students, three parallel groups comparing flossing before brushing (FB), after brushing (BF), or during brushing (BFB, mid-brushing) for 21 days, measuring RMNPI plaque index, bleeding index (BI), and probing depth (PD).

Mid-brushing flossing (BFB) produced the largest RMNPI reduction at day 7 (p=0.039, driven by a significant BF-vs-BFB difference) and significantly greater BI reduction at both day 7 (p=0.028) and day 21 (p=0.015) versus the other sequences; by day 21 the RMNPI advantage disappeared (p=0.933) and PD showed no group differences at either time point (p=0.742, p=0.422).

Flossing midway through brushing may give an early gingival-bleeding benefit and is a safe, promotable self-care variant, but the plaque-index advantage is not durable to 21 days and likely reflects a Hawthorne/novelty effect rather than a lasting biofilm-control difference — sequence appears to matter far less than the act of flossing itself.

- **Affiliation**: Department of Periodontology, School and Hospital of Stomatology, Guangzhou Medical University, Guangzhou, China
- **Journal**: BMC Oral Health (2026) 26:1053
- **DOI**: 10.1186/s12903-026-08392-6
- **Trial registration**: ChiCTR2500108845 (retrospectively registered, Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, 2025-08-09)
- **Study type**: Single-blind, three-arm parallel RCT

## 2. Key Contributions

- First RCT to compare three flossing *sequences* relative to brushing — floss-before-brushing (FB), floss-after-brushing (BF), and floss-*during*-brushing (BFB, i.e., brush-floss-brush) — rather than only the traditional "floss-first vs. brush-first" dichotomy examined by prior studies (Mazhari et al., Torkzaban et al.).
- Broadens the participant pool beyond dental/dentistry students (used by prior floss-sequence trials) to general university students, improving external validity.
- Uses a fine-grained, 9-zone RMNPI (buccal + lingual, zones A–I) scanned via intraoral scanner (3Shape TRIOS 3) and quantified in Adobe Illustrator, allowing separate reporting of total, interdental, and marginal plaque reduction.
- Explicitly discusses the Hawthorne effect as the likely explanation for the day-7-but-not-day-21 plaque-index signal, citing Niere et al. as corroborating evidence that observation-driven behavior change fades within ~2 weeks.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Single-blind (examiners blinded to group; participants aware of assignment), 3-arm parallel RCT, CONSORT 2025-compliant.
- **Sample**: 60 volunteers recruited, 54 completed (29 M / 25 F); inclusion = university students in Guangzhou, gingival health or gingivitis, ≥20 teeth, no recent antibiotics/fluoride/salivary-affecting medication; exclusion = dental cleaning within 6 months, orthodontic appliances, periodontitis.
- **Sample size**: Powered from a 6-person pilot (95% CI, 80% power) → 54 required, 60 recruited to allow for attrition.
- **Groups (18 each)**: FB = 1 min floss → 2 min full-mouth brushing; BF = 1 min brush → 1 min floss → 1 min brush (mid-brushing/BFB — note paper's own group-letter/abbreviation mapping: Group A=FB, Group B=BF [floss after brushing], Group C=BFB [floss during brushing]); all protocols capped at 3 minutes total, Bass technique for brushing, Woodpecker plaque-disclosing agent + standardized floss/toothpaste/toothbrush supplied.
- **Randomization/blinding**: Sealed-envelope A/B/C allocation by the experiment leader only; examiners scoring RMNPI/BI/PD blinded to group assignment; 4 gender-balanced batches.
- **Outcomes**: RMNPI (total, interdental, marginal) at baseline/day 7/day 21; bleeding index (BI, 0–5 scale); probing depth (PD, 6 sites/tooth) at baseline/day 7/day 21.
- **Statistics**: R v4.4.0; Shapiro-Wilk normality, Levene's homogeneity, repeated-measures ANOVA with Tukey HSD post-hoc; α = 0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Baseline**: No significant between-group differences in RMNPI, BI, PD, age, or gender (all p>0.3).
- **RMNPI**: Significant reduction at day 7 across all groups combined (p=0.039), largest in BFB; Tukey post-hoc found the BF-vs-BFB difference significant at day 7 (mean diff 0.108, 95% CI [0.004, 0.210], p=0.039). No significant difference at day 21 (p=0.933). Interdental and marginal sub-scores never reached significance at either timepoint.
- **BI**: Significant within-group and interaction effects; BFB showed greater BI reduction than the other two sequences at both day 7 (p=0.028; FB-vs-BFB post-hoc diff 0.233, 95% CI [0.005, 0.462], p=0.045) and day 21 (p=0.015; largest post-hoc diff BF-vs-BFB 0.360, 95% CI [0.070, 0.651], p=0.012).
- **PD**: No significant between-group, within-group, or interaction effects at either timepoint (p=0.742 day 7, p=0.422 day 21); PD numerically increased slightly across all groups.
- **Safety**: No adverse events (sensitivity, allergic reaction, mucosal injury) in any arm across 21 days.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single-center, homogeneous 18–22-year-old university-student sample with healthy/mildly-inflamed gingiva — limits generalizability to older, periodontally compromised, or less compliant populations.
- 21-day follow-up is short; authors note PD changes may need ≥3 months to manifest, and note the plaque-index signal itself may be a Hawthorne/novelty artifact rather than a durable effect.
- RMNPI records presence/absence per zone and cannot quantify plaque *volume*, which the authors flag as a possible reason BI and RMNPI diverged.
- Findings on floss-before-vs-after-brushing contradict two earlier crossover studies (Mazhari et al.; Torkzaban et al.) that found floss-first superior — authors attribute the discrepancy partly to using dental students (higher oral-health literacy) vs. general university students in this trial.
- No true "no-flossing" control arm — all three arms floss, so the trial isolates *sequence*, not the marginal value of flossing itself.

## 6. Related Work

- Mazhari et al. and Torkzaban et al. (both cited, not yet in this wiki) — earlier floss-before-vs-after-brushing crossover trials in dental students, both favoring floss-first; this trial's null/opposite result for the FB-vs-BF comparison motivates re-examination in a broader population.
- [[interdental-cleaning/jung-2025-flossing-performance-plaque-removal]] — asks whether flossing *technique quality* predicts plaque removal; complements this trial's focus on flossing *timing*.

## 7. Glossary

- **RMNPI**: Rustogi Modified Navy Plaque Index — a 9-zone (A–I) buccal/lingual plaque-presence index used for fine-grained localization of plaque (interdental vs. marginal).
- **FB / BF / BFB**: Floss-Before-brushing / Brush-then-Floss (floss after brushing) / Brush-Floss-Brush (floss during/mid-brushing).
- **BI**: Bleeding Index, 0 (healthy) to 5 (spontaneous bleeding) ordinal scale of gingival inflammation.
- **PD**: Probing Depth, distance from free gingival margin to pocket base, measured at 6 sites/tooth.
- **Hawthorne effect**: Behavior change (here, improved oral hygiene) driven by awareness of being observed rather than by the intervention itself; proposed explanation for the day-7-only RMNPI signal.
- **ChiCTR**: Chinese Clinical Trial Registry.
