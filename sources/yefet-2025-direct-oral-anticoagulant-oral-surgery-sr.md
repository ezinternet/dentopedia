## Why Ingested

DOAC-specific systematic review restricted to oral surgery (extraction, implants, soft-tissue surgery). Extends [[drug/anticoagulants/inchingolo-2024-anticoagulation-therapy-dental-practice-sr]] (all anticoagulant classes) and independently points the same direction as [[drug/anticoagulants/moldovan-2023-anticoagulant-oral-surgery-bleeding-sr]] and [[drug/anticoagulants/dou-2025-bleeding-dentoalveolar-doac-vka-sr-ma]]; adds a timing (trough-level scheduling) angle.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (no meta-analysis stated in the abstract): 628 records screened, 17 studies on DOAC-treated patients (dabigatran, rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban) undergoing oral surgery.

Minor to moderate bleeding was relatively common, severe bleeding requiring hospitalization was rare, and most events were managed with standard local hemostatic measures.

Continuing DOAC was deemed safe with local management, and scheduling when DOAC levels are lowest is advised; only the abstract was available, so no pooled estimates are known.

## 2. Key Contributions

Restricts inclusion to DOAC-only cohorts (other anticoagulants and combined therapy excluded) to isolate DOAC-specific outcomes in oral surgery, and offers recommendations on timing and perioperative strategy.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Systematic review; 628 articles identified, 17 met inclusion criteria. Procedures: tooth extractions, dental implants, soft tissue surgery. Search databases, risk-of-bias tool, and registration are not stated in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Abstract-level only: minor to moderate bleeding relatively common; severe bleeding requiring hospitalization rare; most bleeding controlled with standard local hemostasis; continuation of DOAC deemed safe; schedule procedures at lowest DOAC levels. No rates or effect sizes are given in the abstract.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Full text not retrieved; no quantitative data, risk-of-bias, or certainty grading available. "Relatively common" bleeding is not quantified.
