## 2. Key Contributions
- First SR+MA quantifying accessory canal vertical distribution specifically for root-end resection level planning
- Tooth-type specific data: molars vs anterior teeth differ substantially
- 2mm resection adequate for majority of molar accessory canals (75.8%)
- 3mm resection needed for anteriors to achieve >95% accessory canal capture
- Provides evidence-based rationale for classic 3mm resection guideline and introduces tooth-type nuance

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: SR+MA
- n: 19 studies included (11 for meta-analysis)
- Outcomes: proportion of accessory canals within apical 2mm, 3mm of root tip; stratified by tooth type (anterior vs posterior/molar)
- Databases: standard systematic search

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Within apical 2mm: 75.8% of accessory canals for molars
- 3mm resection needed for anteriors: captures >95% of accessory canals
- Differential by tooth type: molars can be managed with 2mm; anteriors require 3mm for comprehensive resection

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Heterogeneity in accessory canal detection methods (microCT, histology, clearing-staining) across studies
- Limited studies per tooth type; subgroup analyses based on small numbers
- No clinical outcome data correlating accessory canal capture with periapical healing

## 6. Related Work
- Classic ESE/AAE recommendation: 3mm root-end resection standard
- This SR provides the anatomical justification but notes tooth-type variation

## 7. Glossary
- Accessory canals: lateral/secondary canals branching from the main root canal
- Root-end resection level: mm of root apex removed during apicoectomy
- MA: meta-analysis subset of the 19 included studies (11 studies had extractable data)
