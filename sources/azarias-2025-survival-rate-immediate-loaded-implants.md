## Why Ingested

[[wiki/immediate-implant/alqutaibi-2026-root-analog-dental-implants]] (root-analog implant SR) cites conventional immediate-implant survival benchmarks of 83.7–100% when contrasting its RAI findings. This review (Azarias 2025) is one of the underlying evidence bodies for that benchmark range — a literature review of 15 studies on immediately loaded Branemark-protocol (full-arch, screw-retained) prostheses reporting survival >93%. Mined to give the RAI comparator a traceable, paper-level source rather than a bare number.

## Three-line Summary

Literature review (15 included studies, 546 screened; PubMed/MEDLINE 2006–Feb 2024) of human clinical studies evaluating implants immediately loaded (≤48h) with a Branemark protocol (full-arch, screw-retained) prosthesis in fully edentulous patients.

All 15 included studies reported implant survival rates above 93% (range ~93.9–100%) across follow-ups of 1–10 years; marginal bone loss stayed clinically acceptable (<2 mm at 1 year) in all studies that reported it; moderate-to-high primary stability (insertion torque ≥30 N·cm, ISQ ≥60 in the studies reporting it) was typical.

Immediate loading of full-arch implant-supported prostheses (including the All-on-4 concept) is supported as a viable, high-survival treatment by this body of literature, but the review itself is a narrative/literature review (defined PubMed search and inclusion criteria, no PRISMA flow, no formal risk-of-bias/quality appraisal, no meta-analysis) — treat the pooled ">93%" figure as a descriptive range across heterogeneous studies, not a meta-analytic estimate.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Consolidates 15 human clinical studies (2003–2022 publication dates; searched 2006–Feb 2024) reporting survival of implants immediately loaded (within 48 hours, most same-day) with a Branemark protocol (full-arch, screw-retained) prosthesis.
- Confirms the "All-on-4" concept (4 implants, 2 distally tilted posterior + 2 axial anterior) as a viable variant that avoids bone grafting and reduces implant number/cost compared to the traditional 5–6 implant Branemark protocol.
- Extracts and tabulates per-study implant number, follow-up duration, survival rate, waiting period before prosthesis delivery, and marginal bone loss where reported (Table 1, 15 rows).
- Synthesizes primary-stability thresholds from the included literature: insertion torque <30 N·cm = low, 30–44 N·cm = moderate, ≥45 N·cm = high; ISQ <60 = low, 60–64 = medium-low, 65–69 = medium-high, ≥70 = high. Concludes moderate primary stability (torque 30–44 N·cm or ISQ ≥60) is sufficient for immediate loading of Branemark protocol prostheses (contrasted with the 45 N·cm high-stability recommendation for single immediately loaded implants).
- Highlights an inconsistency in the literature: most included studies do not report whether prosthesis cantilever lengths respected the 1.5× anteroposterior-spread rule for "clinically acceptable" cantilevers; only Mohamed et al (2022) explicitly verified this.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Not applicable in the technical sense — this is a clinical literature review, not a computational/engineering paper. Search methodology: electronic search of PubMed/MEDLINE database, 2006 to February 2024, using MeSH descriptors "completely edentulous" and "immediate loading." Inclusion: human clinical articles in English evaluating implant survival rate after immediate loading with a Branemark protocol prosthesis. 546 articles identified; 15 met inclusion criteria. No PRISMA flow diagram, no dual-reviewer screening process, and no formal risk-of-bias/quality appraisal tool are reported — this places the paper as a narrative/literature review with a defined but not fully systematic search protocol, not a formal systematic review.

- All 15 studies: implant survival >93%; provisional and definitive prosthesis survival was 100% in essentially all studies that reported it, even when acrylic provisional prostheses fractured (fractures were repairable).
- Marginal bone loss: all studies reporting this metric found values <2 mm at 1-year follow-up, considered clinically acceptable.
- Notable stability finding: Bergkvist et al (2009) loaded implants at a mean ISQ of 50.6 (below the commonly cited 60 threshold) and still achieved 98.2% survival at 3 years — suggesting the ISQ≥60 immediate-loading threshold may be more conservative than strictly necessary in full-arch/Branemark-protocol loading (as opposed to single-implant immediate loading).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Explicitly a narrative literature review, not a systematic review with meta-analysis: no PRISMA methodology, no formal quality/risk-of-bias appraisal, no pooled statistical estimate — the ">93%" figure is a qualitative floor across heterogeneous study designs (retrospective and prospective), not a meta-analytic pooled rate.
- Substantial heterogeneity across included studies in implant systems, torque/ISQ reporting completeness, number of implants per arch, cantilever length reporting, and follow-up duration (1–10 years) — direct comparison of survival percentages across rows is not statistically rigorous.
- Most included studies do not report whether cantilever lengths met the clinically-recommended 1.5× rule, limiting conclusions about prosthetic design safety.
- Windael et al (2018) did not report insertion torque; Li et al (2009) used a wide torque range (20–50 N·cm) mixing low- and high-stability implants without stratified survival outcomes — obscures the torque-survival relationship in that specific dataset.

## 7. Glossary

- **Branemark protocol prosthesis**: A full-arch, screw-retained, fixed implant-supported prosthesis, traditionally requiring 5–6 (mandible) or 6+ (maxilla) implants.
- **All-on-4**: A reduced-implant-count (4 implants: 2 axial anterior + 2 distally tilted posterior) variant of the Branemark protocol that avoids bone grafting in atrophic arches.
- **Immediate loading**: Loading (i.e., delivering a functional/provisional prosthesis on) an implant within 48 hours of placement.
- **Primary stability**: The mechanical/frictional stability of an implant immediately after placement, before biological osseointegration occurs; measured by insertion torque (N·cm) or resonance frequency analysis (ISQ).
- **ISQ (Implant Stability Quotient)**: A 1–100 scale output of resonance frequency analysis (RFA) quantifying implant stability.
- **Marginal bone loss (MBL)**: Radiographically measured vertical bone level change around the implant neck over time.
