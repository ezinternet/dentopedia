## Three-line Summary

In vivo multi-species study (rabbit, dog, human — femoral cortex; human clinical data from Richards plate fixation surgery) measuring drilling temperature at 0.5 mm from the drill hole under ~20,000 rpm with saline cooling.

Cortical thickness drove temperature linearly across species: rabbit 1.5 mm cortex → 40°C; dog 3.5 mm cortex → 56°C; human femur 6.5 mm cortex → 89°C; and in the same canine femur, the medial cortex ran 9°C hotter than the lateral cortex because saline could not reach it.

Animal model temperatures are NOT applicable to the human clinical situation — even with saline cooling, human cortical bone drilling can reach temperatures far above the necrosis threshold.

## 세줄요약

in vivo 다종 비교 연구 (토끼·개·인간 — 대퇴골 피질골; 인간 임상 데이터는 Richards plate 고정술 중 측정): ~20,000 rpm 식염수 냉각 조건에서 드릴 홀에서 0.5 mm 떨어진 지점의 온도 측정.

피질골 두께가 온도를 선형적으로 결정: 토끼 1.5 mm → 40°C; 개 3.5 mm → 56°C; **인간 대퇴골 6.5 mm → 89°C**. 동일 개 대퇴골의 원위 피질골은 근위보다 9°C 높음(식염수 도달 불가).

동물 실험 온도를 인간 임상에 그대로 적용할 수 없다 — 식염수 냉각이 있어도 인간 피질골 드릴링에서 괴사 역치를 훨씬 초과하는 온도가 발생한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First multi-species in vivo demonstration that cortical bone thickness — not drill type or cooling — is the primary determinant of heat generation.
- Establishes the absolute temperature ceiling problem in human surgery: 89°C under conditions identical to those producing only 40°C in rabbits.
- Shows that cooling access failure (medial vs lateral cortex, +9°C) is an independent heat risk beyond cortical thickness.
- Provides the data foundation that makes direct animal-to-human thermal extrapolation untenable, a point repeated in virtually every subsequent drilling heat review.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Species / models**: rabbit femur, dog femur, human femur (pertrochanteric fracture fixation, Richards plate)
- **Measurement point**: thermocouple at 0.5 mm from drill hole periphery
- **Conditions**: ~20,000 rpm, saline irrigation
- **Human context**: intraoperative measurement during internal fixation surgery — not implant osteotomy
- **Limitation**: abstract-only; exact n per group, thermocouple type, and saline flow rate not extractable

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Species | Cortical thickness | Temperature at 0.5 mm |
|---|---|---|
| Rabbit | 1.5 mm | 40°C |
| Dog | 3.5 mm | 56°C |
| Human femur | 6.5 mm | **89°C** |

- Canine medial cortex (cooling-inaccessible): +9°C vs lateral cortex under same conditions.
- Authors explicitly conclude: animal experiment temperatures are not applicable to clinical situations; very high temperatures may arise in patients even with saline cooling.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Human data from orthopedic plate fixation, not dental implant osteotomy — mandibular cortical thickness (~1–3 mm) differs substantially from femoral cortex.
- Abstract only; sample sizes, statistical analysis, and exact methodology not confirmable.
- 20,000 rpm (orthopedic) is 10–13× higher than dental implant protocol (1,500–2,000 rpm) — direct numeric extrapolation to dental drilling is inappropriate.
- Does not address the 47°C necrosis threshold (that is Eriksson & Albrektsson 1983, a separate paper not held in this wiki).

## 6. Related Work

- Timon & Keady 2019 — narrative review (orthopedic) synthesizing this paper alongside others; 50°C identified as orthopedic threshold.
- Eriksson & Albrektsson 1983 (not held) — rabbit tibia chamber study establishing 47°C/1 min necrosis threshold; directly paired with this paper in clinical teaching.
- Eriksson & Adell 1986 (not held) — clinical dental implant osteotomy temperatures; reported peak 33.8°C in human mandible under implant-protocol drilling.
- Kniha et al. 2020 SR (not held) — systematic review of 25 animal studies confirming 47–55°C/1 min range; explicitly notes clinical translation gap documented here.

## 7. Glossary

- **Richards plate**: orthopedic fixation device for pertrochanteric hip fractures; the clinical setting in which the human measurements were taken.
- **Cortical bone (Cortex, 피질골)**: the dense outer shell of bone; its thickness is the dominant variable in this study.
- **Thermal osteonecrosis (열 골괴사)**: irreversible bone cell death caused by heat exceeding the viability threshold.
