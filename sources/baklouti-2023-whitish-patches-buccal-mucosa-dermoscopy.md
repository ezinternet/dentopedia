## Why Ingested

교흔(morsicatio labiorum/buccarum)의 임상 감별진단 도구(dermoscopy)를 다루는 케이스 리포트로, 역학 데이터 중심의 [[oral-medicine/normal-variants/nautiyal-2024-prevalence-keratosis-oral-cavity]]를 보완한다. 실제 임상에서 oral lichen planus와의 오진 위험(steroid 치료 무반응 사례)을 구체적으로 보여줘 chairside 감별 기준을 제공.

## Three-line Summary

Case report (n=1; 46-year-old woman, 2-year history of whitish lip plaques previously misdiagnosed as oral lichen planus unresponsive to topical steroids) describing dermoscopic characterization of morsicatio labiorum.

Dermoscopy revealed whitish/yellowish structureless areas, dotted-vessel erosions, and loose removable white scales without Wickham striae, distinguishing it from oral lichen planus and enabling diagnosis without biopsy.

This is reportedly the first dermoscopic report specifically of morsicatio labiorum; absence of Wickham striae is the key negative differentiator from OLP, but diagnostic-accuracy data (sensitivity/specificity) are lacking.

## 세줄요약

증례 보고(n=1; 46세 여성; 2년간 구강편평태선으로 오진되어 국소 스테로이드에 반응 없던 교순증(morsicatio labiorum) 사례) — 교순증의 더모스코피 특성 기술.

더모스코피 소견: 백황색 무구조 영역, 점상혈관을 동반한 미란, 쉽게 제거되는 느슨한 백색 인설 + Wickham striae 부재 → 구강편평태선과 감별 진단, 조직검사 없이 진단 가능.

교순증에 대한 최초 더모스코피 보고로 알려지며, Wickham striae 부재가 OLP 감별의 핵심 음성 소견이나 민감도·특이도 데이터는 아직 없음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First reported dermoscopic characterization specifically of morsicatio labiorum (per authors' claim).
- Demonstrates a practical, non-invasive tool to differentiate self-inflicted cheek/lip-biting lesions from oral lichen planus (OLP) and other whitish mucosal disorders, avoiding unnecessary biopsy.
- Highlights that patients are frequently unaware of their own biting habit, which contributes to diagnostic delay/misdiagnosis.

## 3. Methodology

- Single case report; 46-year-old woman, no relevant medical history, 2-year history of asymptomatic whitish lip plaques previously treated (without improvement) as presumed OLP with topical steroids.
- Clinical exam: scaly, jagged, irregular whitish patches with thin erosions on inner lip mucosa.
- Dermoscopic exam: Dermlite DL4 (3Gen) handheld dermatoscope.
- Diagnosis confirmed by patient's own admission of chronic lip-biting behavior linked to a nervous/anxious temperament.

## 4. Results

- Dermoscopy showed: whitish/yellowish structureless areas and lines; small superficial erosions with dotted vessels; loose, easily removable white scales.
- Key negative finding: absence of Wickham striae (pathognomonic for OLP) and absence of other OLP-associated vascular patterns (linear/hairpin vessels, pigmented structures, hyperkeratotic-bordered erosions) — this negative pattern was the deciding differentiator.
- Management: patient counseling to stop lip-biting; psychiatric referral considered for refractory/compulsive cases (not required in this patient).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single case — descriptive, hypothesis-generating only; no comparative diagnostic-accuracy data (sensitivity/specificity) for dermoscopy vs. biopsy in morsicatio.
- Authors call this the first dermoscopic report of morsicatio labiorum specifically (as opposed to morsicatio buccarum more generally) — further case series needed to validate reproducibility of the described pattern.

## 6. Related Work

- Complements the larger epidemiologic study [[oral-medicine/normal-variants/nautiyal-2024-prevalence-keratosis-oral-cavity]], which found morsicatio buccarum the least common of three innocuous keratotic lesions (0.9%) — the rarity may partly reflect underrecognition, which this case illustrates.
- Practical companion to [[oral-medicine/normal-variants/gupta-2023-prevalence-distribution-oral-mucosal-nepal]] for chairside differentiation of buccal/labial "normal variant" white lesions from pathologic mimics (notably OLP).

## 7. Glossary

- **Morsicatio labiorum / buccarum**: chronic self-inflicted biting of the lips / buccal mucosa causing gray-white desquamative keratotic patches; a benign, behaviorally-driven mucosal alteration, often subconscious.
- **Wickham striae**: fine white lace-like reticular pattern pathognomonic of oral lichen planus, visible on dermoscopy/mucoscopy.
- **Dermoscopy (mucoscopy)**: non-invasive handheld magnified/polarized examination technique adapted from skin dermoscopy for mucosal lesion differential diagnosis.
