## Why Ingested

The wiki held no dedicated synthesis on alcohol as an implant-failure risk factor — alcohol appeared only as an incidental covariate inside multi-factor pages (e.g. [[implants/survival/da-rocha-coelho-2021-evaluation-of-the-association]], where it was null). This 2026 SR+MA is the first page in the wiki that quantifies the dose-response directly, and it is the evidence anchor for the new clinical overview on alcohol and oral surgery.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA; PubMed, Embase, Cochrane, Web of Science to 12 Dec 2024) of 9 observational studies (54–747 participants each) on alcohol consumption and dental implant failure.

Overall alcohol exposure showed no association with implant failure (OR 0.87, 95% CI 0.50–1.51, p = 0.61), but the dose split: heavy consumption increased failure (OR 2.78, 95% CI 2.08–3.91, p < 0.001) while low-to-moderate consumption was associated with lower risk (OR 0.40, 95% CI 0.21–0.76, p = 0.01).

The clinical signal is heavy drinking, not any drinking — and the apparent protection at low doses is explicitly flagged by the authors as confounder-prone and not a basis for advising patients to drink.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (PRISMA; PubMed·Embase·Cochrane·Web of Science, 2024-12-12까지): 관찰연구 9편 (연구당 54–747명)에서 음주와 임플란트 실패의 연관성을 분석.

전체 음주군은 임플란트 실패와 유의한 연관이 없었으나 (교차비 (Odds Ratio, OR) 0.87, 95% 신뢰구간 (Confidence Interval, CI) 0.50–1.51), 용량별로 갈려 과량 음주는 실패 위험 증가 (OR 2.78, 95% CI 2.08–3.91, p < 0.001), 저·중등도 음주는 낮은 위험 (OR 0.40, 95% CI 0.21–0.76, p = 0.01)과 연관.

임상 신호는 "음주 전체"가 아니라 과량 음주이며, 저용량의 겉보기 보호효과는 저자들이 교란 가능성으로 명시적으로 경고한 값이므로 환자에게 음주 권고의 근거로 쓸 수 없음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First meta-analytic estimate of the alcohol–dental implant failure association that separates **heavy** from **low-to-moderate** exposure instead of pooling all drinkers into one binary variable — the pooling is exactly what hid the signal in earlier single-cohort studies.
- Quantifies the heavy-drinking odds (OR 2.78, 95% CI 2.08–3.91) at a magnitude comparable to smoking in peri-implant disease meta-analyses.
- Reports the low-to-moderate inverse association (OR 0.40) but frames it as hypothesis-generating, naming confounding (healthier-drinker selection, socioeconomic status, dental attendance) as the likely explanation.
- Sensitivity analyses reported as robust, with no significant publication bias detected.

## 3. Methodology

- **Design**: systematic review + meta-analysis of observational studies (cohort/case-control/cross-sectional designs eligible).
- **Databases**: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science; search cut-off 12 December 2024.
- **Inclusion**: observational studies reporting the association between alcohol consumption and dental implant failure.
- **Included studies**: 9, each with 54 to 747 participants.
- **Outcome**: dental implant failure; exposure stratified post hoc into heavy vs low-to-moderate consumption.
- **Analysis**: random-effects pooling of ORs; subgroup analysis by consumption level; sensitivity analyses; publication-bias assessment.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Exposure stratum | OR (95% CI) | p |
|---|---|---|
| Overall alcohol consumption | 0.87 (0.50–1.51) | 0.61 |
| Heavy alcohol consumption | 2.78 (2.08–3.91) | < 0.001 |
| Low-to-moderate consumption | 0.40 (0.21–0.76) | 0.01 |

- No significant publication bias detected; sensitivity analyses preserved the direction and magnitude of the heavy-drinking estimate.
- The included studies were small (54–747 participants), all observational, and heterogeneous in how alcohol exposure was defined — the SR does not report a pooled dose threshold in grams/day.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Observational evidence only: no causal inference, and residual confounding by smoking, periodontitis history, diabetes, and socioeconomic status is likely in every included study.
- Small primary studies (maximum 747 participants) and few of them (9) — subgroup estimates rest on a subset of the nine.
- Alcohol exposure definitions were not harmonised (units, frequency, "alcoholism" vs intake), so the heavy/low-moderate split is the review's construct, not a validated threshold.
- No adjustment for implant-level confounders (site, bone quality, loading protocol) was possible in a study-level meta-analysis.
- The low-to-moderate "protective" estimate should not be translated into patient advice; the authors themselves caution against it.

## 7. Glossary

- **OR (Odds Ratio)**: ratio of the odds of implant failure in drinkers vs non-drinkers; > 1 means higher odds with exposure.
- **Heavy alcohol consumption**: the review's upper exposure stratum; defined per included study rather than by a single grams/day cut-off.
- **Low-to-moderate consumption**: lower exposure stratum, pooled separately from heavy drinking.
- **Observational study**: cohort, case-control, or cross-sectional design — association only, no randomisation.
- **Publication bias**: tendency for positive findings to be published; assessed here with standard funnel-plot/Egger-type methods (reported as not significant).
- **Sensitivity analysis**: re-running the pooled estimate under different assumptions to test robustness.
