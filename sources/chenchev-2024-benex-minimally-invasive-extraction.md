## Why Ingested
Benex 수직발치 시스템의 협측골 보존 효과를 정량화한 RCT — 기존 [[oral-surgery/pujarern-2026-minimally-invasive-extraction-socket]] 서술 리뷰에 임상 수치 근거 보강.

## One-line Summary
Crossover RCT (n=56): Benex vertical extractor preserved buccal cortical plate in 95% of cases vs 71.8% for conventional forceps (p<0.05), with similar extraction success rates and better early wound healing.

## 한줄요약
교차 RCT (n=56): Benex 수직발치기 협측 피질골 보존율 95% vs 전통 발치겸자 71.8% (p<0.05), 발치 성공률은 동등, 초기 치유는 Benex 우위.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First RCT directly measuring buccal cortical plate preservation rate with Benex vs forceps
- Quantifies 23.2 percentage-point advantage (95% vs 71.8%) in plate preservation
- Demonstrates equivalent extraction success scores (4.61 Benex vs 4.55 forceps)
- Shows significantly better early wound healing at day 10 by Landry's index
- Identifies endodontically treated teeth and multi-rooted teeth as Benex failure risk factors

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Design: Crossover randomized controlled trial
- Setting: Department of Oral Surgery, Medical University — Plovdiv; 12-month recruitment (Jan 2022–Jan 2023)
- Population: 56 adult patients (mean age 37.2 ± 8.1 yr); any tooth indicated for closed extraction
- Intervention (Group II, n=28): Benex extractor — vertical pull via screw-post drilled into root canal, support disk on adjacent teeth, gradual clockwise screw traction
- Control (Group I, n=28): Conventional extraction forceps; multi-rooted teeth sectioned in both groups for standardization
- Randomization: Block randomization
- Primary outcomes: Extraction success (1–5 scale, Choi/Patel), buccal cortical plate preservation (intact/partial/missing by probing depth difference), VAS pain (days 0,1,3,7), Landry Wound Healing Index (days 3,7,10)
- Statistics: Independent samples t-test, Chi-square, repeated-measures ANOVA, Mann-Whitney U

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Buccal cortical plate preserved: Benex 95% vs forceps 71.8% (p<0.05, Chi-square)
- Partial fracture (<4 mm loss): ~25% of forceps group vs near 0% Benex
- Extraction success mean: Benex 4.61 vs forceps 4.55 (NS)
- VAS pain day 7: Benex marginally better (NS)
- Wound healing day 10: 100% Very Good/Excellent Benex vs 78.6% forceps (significant)
- Literature context: Muska et al. overall success 83%; Hong et al. 85.4% success, surgical extraction rate 6% (Benex) vs 22% (forceps)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Short follow-up (10 days only) — no long-term bone volume assessment
- Small sample size (n=28 per group)
- Endodontically treated and multi-rooted teeth had higher Benex failure (root weakened by filing/caries)
- Multi-rooted molar access difficult due to device bulk
- Convenience sampling — selection bias risk

## 6. Related Work
- Muska et al.: Benex 111 extractions, 83% success, 44% would have required surgical extraction
- Hong et al.: 323 Benex extractions, 85.4% success, 2.2× higher failure in multi-rooted teeth
- Krug et al.: Root perforation in 3/51 Benex cases — complete periodontal healing
- Pujarern 2026: narrative review covering Benex among 10 minimally invasive techniques

## 7. Glossary
- **Benex**: Vertical extraction system by Hager & Meisinger; uses screw-post inserted into drilled root canal, pulled vertically via hand screw
- **LWHI (Landry Wound Healing Index)**: 5-category wound assessment (tissue color, bleeding on palpation, granulation tissue, pus, gingival margin) at days 3, 7, 10
- **Buccal cortical plate**: Facial bony wall of extraction socket; loss correlates with ridge resorption and implant difficulty
