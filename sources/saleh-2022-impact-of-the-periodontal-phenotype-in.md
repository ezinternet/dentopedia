## Three-line Summary

Single-arm prospective clinical trial (26 subjects, 52 adjacent posterior teeth) measuring vertical/horizontal bone changes on teeth included in a full-thickness mucoperiosteal flap (FTF) during posterior implant surgery, using superimposed CBCT at baseline (T0) and 12 months (T1) with periodontal phenotype from digital-scan/DICOM superimposition.
Vertical bone loss was significant (P = 0.013) and greatest at mesial (−0.31 ± 0.30 mm) and facial (P < 0.05) sites; horizontal changes 5 mm subcrestally did not differ by location (P = 0.086), while bone-width loss was greatest closest to the crest (P = 0.001).
Overall bone loss was clinically insignificant (≤0.4 mm) with no soft-tissue-thickness correlation — baseline bone thickness (>2 mm appears protective) governs remodeling, and FTF shows no deleterious effect on the adjacent-tooth periodontium (abstract-only report).

## 세줄요약

단일군 전향적 임상시험(Prospective clinical trial; 26명, 인접치 52개) — 후방 임플란트 수술 시 전층점막골막판(Full-Thickness mucoperiosteal flap, FTF)에 포함된 인접치의 수직·수평 골 변화를 기준(T0)·12개월(T1) 콘빔전산화단층촬영(Cone-Beam Computed Tomography, CBCT) 중첩으로 측정하고, 치주 표현형은 디지털 스캔·DICOM 중첩으로 평가.
수직 골소실은 유의했고(P = 0.013) 근심(−0.31 ± 0.30 mm)·협측(P < 0.05)이 가장 컸으며, 치조정 아래 5 mm 수평 변화는 부위 간 차이가 없었고(P = 0.086) 치조정에 가장 가까운 곳에서 골폭 소실이 가장 컸다(P = 0.001).
전체 골소실은 임상적으로 미미(≤0.4 mm)했고 연조직 두께와 상관이 없어 기저 골 두께(>2 mm에서 방어 효과 추정)가 재형성을 좌우 — FTF는 인접치 치주조직에 유해 영향이 없음(초록만 확보).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Prospective clinical evidence that FTF elevation during posterior implant surgery produces only minimal (≤0.4 mm) marginal bone change on the adjacent natural teeth at 12 months.
- Bone remodeling after FTF is governed by baseline **bone** thickness rather than **soft tissue** thickness — no correlation was found between soft tissue thickness and bone changes.
- Suggests a possible protective threshold of **>2 mm baseline bone thickness** against horizontal bone loss.
- Site-specific pattern: vertical loss greatest at mesial and facial aspects; crestal bone-width loss greatest immediately below the crest.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Single-arm prospective clinical trial (no unflapped control arm).
- **Databases**: Not applicable (clinical trial, not a secondary study).
- **n**: 26 subjects / 52 adjacent teeth (the tooth mesial and the tooth distal to each implant site).
- **Intervention / setting**: Patients receiving implant surgery in the posterior area; at implant site preparation an FTF was extended one tooth mesial and one tooth distal to the planned site, and elevated both facially and lingually.
- **Outcomes**: Vertical and horizontal linear bone changes on both adjacent teeth measured on superimposed CBCT images at T0 (before implant placement) and T1 (12 months). Periodontal phenotype assessed by superimposing baseline digital models and DICOM files.
- **Measurement scope**: As the retrieved artifact contains the abstract but not the full Methods, exact statistical model, assessor calibration, and error-of-measurement details are not available.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Result (abstract-reported) |
|---|---|
| Vertical bone change, T0 → T1 | Statistically significant (P = 0.013) |
| Mesial vertical change | −0.31 ± 0.30 mm; significantly greater than other sites |
| Facial vertical change | Significantly greater (P < 0.05) |
| Horizontal dimensional change, 5 mm subcrestal | Similar among different locations (P = 0.086) |
| Bone-width loss | Higher closest to the crest (P = 0.001) |
| Soft tissue thickness ↔ bone change | No correlation |
| Baseline bone thickness | Appears to influence the extent of horizontal bone remodeling; preventive effect suggested for >2 mm |
| Overall magnitude, vertical and horizontal | Clinically insignificant (≤0.4 mm) |

Bottom line: marginal bone changes in maxillary and mandibular posterior teeth 12 months after FTF are very minimal, influenced mainly by bone rather than soft tissue thickness; FTF does not appear deleterious to the adjacent-tooth periodontium.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only source** — the retrieved PDF is a Wiley Online Library print/landing artifact containing the abstract and 46 references but no Methods/Results body or tables; all numbers above are from the abstract alone.
- Single-arm design with no unflapped control group — the FTF effect cannot be fully separated from implant-site preparation, postsurgical healing, or natural remodeling.
- Small sample (26 subjects / 52 teeth) and 12-month follow-up only; longer-term stability is unknown.
- CBCT-derived linear measurements are inherently limited by voxel resolution and scatter/blooming — an inferred method limitation, not explicitly stated in the abstract.
- The "preventive effect" of >2 mm bone thickness is phrased tentatively ("maybe expected") by the authors and needs confirmation.

## 7. Glossary

- **FTF (Full-Thickness mucoperiosteal flap)**: flap elevated with the periosteum included, exposing the underlying alveolar bone; here extended one tooth mesial and distal and elevated facially and lingually.
- **Periodontal phenotype (치주 표현형)**: the combined gingival (soft tissue) phenotype and underlying bone morphology; assessed here by digital model–DICOM superimposition.
- **CBCT (Cone-Beam Computed Tomography)**: three-dimensional radiographic modality used for superimposed linear bone measurement at T0 and T1.
- **Subcrestal (치조정 아래)**: below the alveolar crest; horizontal changes were measured 5 mm subcrestally.
- **Bone-width loss (골폭 소실)**: reduction in the bucco-lingual/palatal linear width of the crestal bone, greatest immediately below the crest in this study.
