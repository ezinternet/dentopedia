## Why Ingested
Directly compares the two dominant final-restoration options for endodontically treated posterior teeth (full veneer crown vs full cuspal coverage onlay) — a comparison our existing [[wiki/post-and-core/suksaphar-2017-survival-fracture-ett-crown-resin-sr]] page addresses only for crown-vs-direct-resin-composite, not crown-vs-onlay. Also independently corroborates (narrower ETT-specific population, weaker narrative-only synthesis) the onlay-favorable-with-comparable-survival finding already established at higher evidence tier by [[wiki/inlay/wang-2022-onlays-vs-full-crowns-posterior-sr-ma]].

## Three-line Summary
This systematic review (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42022287217) searched MEDLINE/EMBASE via Ovid, PubMed, and Cochrane CENTRAL (1946–April 2022) plus Google Scholar citation chaining, screening 1707 records down to 11 included in-vivo studies (RCTs, prospective and retrospective cohorts) comparing full veneer crowns vs full cuspal coverage onlays on endodontically treated posterior teeth.
Onlay survival ranged 95–100% at 2 years and 90.7–100% at 3 years (success 86.6–96.6% at 2yr, 86.6–96% at 3yr); full veneer crown survival ranged 87.8% (>2yr) to 95.1% (3yr) and 84–97.73% (5–10yr), with success 91.11–92.64% (5yr) and 60% (6yr) — broadly overlapping ranges, no meta-analysis was possible (protocol/material heterogeneity), so synthesis is narrative only, and 3/11 studies were rated serious risk of bias (ROBINS-I/RoB-2).
The authors conclude onlays are favourable over full veneer crowns in the short-to-midterm for restoring endodontically treated posterior teeth, given comparable survival combined with substantially less tooth-structure removal — but flag that onlay failures, while more often material-fracture-related, may also be more retrievable than crown failures.

## 세줄요약
근관치료 구치의 최종 수복 방법 두 축(전장관 vs 교두피개 온레이)을 비교한 체계적 문헌고찰(PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42022287217)로, MEDLINE·EMBASE(Ovid)·PubMed·Cochrane CENTRAL(1946–2022.4) 및 Google Scholar 인용추적을 통해 1707편 중 11편(RCT·전향/후향 코호트)을 최종 포함하였다.
온레이 생존율은 2년 95–100%, 3년 90.7–100%(성공률 2년 86.6–96.6%, 3년 86.6–96%)였고, 전장관 생존율은 2년 초과 87.8%, 3년 95.1%, 5–10년 84–97.73%(성공률 5년 91.11–92.64%, 6년 60%)로 — 두 군의 범위가 상당 부분 겹쳐 메타분석은 불가능했고(프로토콜·재료 이질성) 서술적 종합에 그쳤으며, 11편 중 3편은 심각한 편향위험(ROBINS-I/RoB-2 serious risk of bias)으로 평가되었다.
저자들은 근관치료 구치 수복 시 생존율이 비슷하면서 치질 삭제량이 현저히 적다는 이유로 단기~중기적으로 온레이가 전장관보다 유리하다고 결론 내렸으나, 온레이 실패는 재료 파절이 주된 원인이면서도 전장관 실패보다 회수 가능성(retrievability)이 더 높을 수 있다고 부연하였다.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: European Journal of Prosthodontics and Restorative Dentistry 2024;32(2):45-55
- **DOI**: 10.1922/EJPRD_2547Lane11
- **Institution**: University of Edinburgh, Edinburgh Dental Institute, UK

## 2. Key Contributions
- First systematic review specifically framing the endodontically-treated-posterior-tooth restoration question as full veneer crown vs full cuspal coverage onlay (rather than crown vs direct composite, or a broader all-posterior-teeth onlay-vs-crown comparison).
- Tabulates survival/success across 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, and 12-year timepoints for both restoration types across 11 primary studies, exposing that no study reports onlay outcomes beyond 3 years — a clear evidence gap.
- Discussion identifies retrievability of onlay failures (mostly material fracture, often repairable in place) as a potential secondary advantage beyond raw survival/success percentages.
- Flags post-core-crown pooling (posts and non-post crowns analyzed together in most included studies) as a confound limiting clean crown-vs-onlay comparison.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: PRISMA-guided systematic review, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42022287217); narrative synthesis (no meta-analysis — protocol/material heterogeneity across studies)
- **Databases**: MEDLINE and EMBASE via Ovid, PubMed, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL); supplemented by Google Scholar forward-citation chaining
- **n**: 1707 records screened → 19 sought for retrieval → 11 studies included (RCTs, prospective cohort, retrospective cohort); 8 excluded (listed with reasons); 3 further non-English studies discussed narratively but excluded from quantitative results (translation unavailable)
- **Outcomes**: Survival (tooth retention regardless of intervention) and success (tooth + restoration retention without repair/replacement need)
- **Risk of bias**: ROBINS-I (non-randomized cohort studies) and RoB-2 (RCTs), summarized via ROBVIS; 9/11 moderate risk, 3/11 serious risk. Quality also cross-checked with CASP checklists for cohort studies and RCTs.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
Onlays: survival 95–100% (2yr), 90.7–100% (3yr); success 86.6–96.6% (2yr), 86.6–96% (3yr). No onlay data beyond 3 years in any included study.
Full veneer crowns: survival 87.8% (>2yr), 95.1% (3yr), 84–97.73% (5–10yr); success 91.11–92.64% (5yr), 60% (6yr).
Materials for onlays: lithium disilicate (89.6–96.6% success at 3yr), feldspathic ceramic (90.7% at 3yr), PICN (97% at 3yr) — no significant difference between feldspathic and PICN in the one study comparing them directly (χ²=1.645, P=.20).
Failure mode: onlay failures predominantly material fracture (retrievable in most cases per Xiao 2020, Lu 2019, Ferrari 2019); crown failures spanned root fracture, post/core fracture, debonding, periradicular pathology, and crown dislodgement (Ferrari 2012 reported 19 root fractures, 18 post/core fractures, 16 debondings, 34 periradicular pathology cases, 46 dislodgements across its cohort).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- No meta-analysis possible; narrative synthesis only, weaker than a pooled quantitative estimate.
- 3/11 included studies rated serious risk of bias; most studies pool teeth restored with and without posts, confounding the crown-vs-onlay comparison with a post-vs-no-post effect.
- No onlay follow-up data beyond 3 years exists in the included literature — long-term (5yr+) onlay-vs-crown comparison remains an open question.
- 3 additional foreign-language studies (Li 2015, Di Iorio 2016, Qiang 2018) were excluded from quantitative synthesis due to translation unavailability, despite reporting data broadly consistent with the review's conclusion.

## 6. Related Work
- wang-2022-onlays-vs-full-crowns-posterior-sr-ma: broader (not ETT-restricted) SR+MA on the same onlay-vs-full-crown question, using meta-analysis (higher evidence tier) and finding statistically equivalent 5-year survival (93.5% vs 94.2%) with higher onlay fracture-complication rates — directionally consistent with this paper's narrative conclusion, though this paper's ETT-specific scope and shorter follow-up window narrow and partially refine that broader finding.
- suksaphar-2017-survival-fracture-ett-crown-resin-sr: adjacent SR on the same ETT-restoration-choice question, but comparing crown vs direct resin composite rather than crown vs onlay.
- dekuijper-2023-clinical-performance-direct-composite-resin: SR+MA on direct composite vs indirect restoration (mostly crowns, few onlays) for ETT posterior teeth; shares this paper's ETT-posterior population but a different comparator axis (direct vs indirect, not onlay vs crown specifically).

## 7. Glossary
- **Full Veneer Crown (FVC)**: A complete-coverage indirect restoration covering all remaining coronal tooth surfaces, requiring circumferential axial reduction.
- **Full Cuspal Coverage Onlay**: An indirect restoration covering all occlusal cusps for protection but without the circumferential axial wall reduction of a full crown — a more conservative, minimally invasive alternative.
- **Survival vs Success (this paper's definitions)**: Survival = retention of the tooth in the mouth irrespective of restoration status; Success = retention of both tooth and restoration without need for any repair or replacement.
- **ROBINS-I / RoB-2**: Cochrane risk-of-bias tools for non-randomized studies of interventions (ROBINS-I) and randomized trials (RoB-2), respectively.
