## Why Ingested

Fills a real hole: the wiki has 68 TMJ pages and, before this ingest, not one addressed juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA). TMJ involvement in JIA is frequently silent — clinical symptoms and radiologic findings diverge — and the mandibular condyle is a growth centre, so unrecognized inflammation damages craniofacial development before it ever becomes symptomatic. A dentist, not the treating rheumatologist, is often the person positioned to notice the asymmetric face, the deviating opening path, and the limited mouth opening. This is a single-author narrative review anchored on one illustrative case (a 9-year-old girl), so it cannot support prevalence or diagnostic-accuracy claims — it supports awareness of clinical signs. The companion systematic review [[tmj/chana-2026-ultrasound-tmj-juvenile-idiopathic-arthritis-sr]] (same ingest batch) answers the diagnostic-accuracy question (ultrasound vs. MRI/CBCT) that this paper does not attempt.

## Three-line Summary

Case-based narrative review (CABARET-format, single author) synthesizing 30 studies on TMJ involvement in juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA), anchored on an illustrative case: a 9-year-old girl with 5-year polyarticular JIA and no reported TMJ symptoms, found on examination to have facial asymmetry, mandibular deviation, and restricted mouth opening, with CBCT showing unilateral condylar flattening and a shortened mandibular ramus.

Reported TMJ involvement prevalence in JIA ranges 17%–over 90% depending on diagnostic method; clinical symptoms alone are unreliable (some literature-cited studies found no MMO difference between JIA patients with vs. without TMJ arthritis), while facial asymmetry (23–41%) and mandibular deviation (up to 62%) are reported more consistently as objective correlates of radiographic damage; the case's own maximal mouth opening (MMO) was 30 mm (normal ≥35 mm under age 10), deviation 5 mm to the right (pathological ≥2 mm), and CBCT scored 3/4 on Billiau's grading scale for the affected condyle vs. 0 for the unaffected side.

Clinically, the paper argues facial asymmetry and mandibular hypomobility should be treated as primary screening red flags for TMJ involvement in any child with JIA regardless of symptoms, since MRI (the gold standard for active synovitis) is often inaccessible; but as a single-case narrative review with no risk-of-bias assessment, it supports awareness of signs, not diagnostic accuracy — that question belongs to [[tmj/chana-2026-ultrasound-tmj-juvenile-idiopathic-arthritis-sr]].

## 세줄요약

CABARET 형식의 케이스 기반 서술적 문헌고찰(단독 저자)로, 30편의 선행연구를 종합하며 대표 사례로 다낭관절형 소아 특발성 관절염(Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis, JIA) 5년 병력의 9세 여아(측두하악관절 관련 증상 무보고)를 제시 — 진찰상 안면비대칭·하악 편위·개구 제한이 있었고 CBCT에서 편측 과두 편평화와 하악지 단축이 확인됨.

JIA의 측두하악관절(Temporomandibular Joint, TMJ) 침범 유병률은 진단법에 따라 17%~90% 이상으로 보고되며, 임상 증상만으로는 신뢰도가 낮음(TMJ 관절염 유무에 따른 최대개구량(Maximal Mouth Opening, MMO) 차이가 없었다는 인용 연구도 존재); 반면 안면비대칭(23~41%)과 하악 편위(최대 62%)는 방사선학적 손상과 비교적 일관되게 연관되는 객관적 소견으로 보고됨 — 본 사례의 MMO는 30mm(10세 미만 정상 ≥35mm), 우측 편위 5mm(병적 기준 ≥2mm), CBCT상 이환측 과두는 Billiau 등급 3/4(비이환측은 0).

임상적으로 이 논문은 MRI(활동성 활막염 진단의 표준)에 대한 접근성이 제한적인 현실에서, 증상 유무와 무관하게 안면비대칭과 하악 운동제한을 JIA 환아의 1차 선별 적신호로 취급해야 한다고 주장하나, 단일 사례 기반 서술적 문헌고찰로 비뚤림위험평가가 없어 진단정확도가 아닌 '징후 인지'만을 뒷받침함 — 진단정확도 질문은 [[tmj/chana-2026-ultrasound-tmj-juvenile-idiopathic-arthritis-sr]]이 담당.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First wiki page on TMJ involvement in juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA) — closes a category gap across 68 pre-existing `tmj/` pages, none of which addressed JIA.
- Frames TMJ arthritis in JIA as frequently "silent": structural/radiographic damage (condylar flattening, ramus shortening) can be present without pain, crepitus, or subjective complaint, and clinical symptom presence/absence correlates poorly with radiographic severity across the cited literature.
- Positions facial asymmetry and mandibular deviation/hypomobility — not pain — as the practical, low-cost screening red flags a general or pediatric dentist can pick up at a routine visit, ahead of specialist imaging referral.
- Documents a concrete diagnostic example: an asymptomatic 9-year-old with unilateral CBCT damage (Billiau grade 3 vs. 0) detected purely through morphofunctional examination (facial photograph landmarking + MMO/deviation/lateral-excursion/protrusion measurement with a ruler and pencil-line technique).
- Explicitly names the mandibular condyle as a growth centre: unilateral inflammation there produces asymmetric ramus growth (measured condylion-to-gonion) rather than just local joint damage, tying TMJ screening to craniofacial developmental outcomes, not just joint pain management.
- Cites the standardized Clinical Orofacial Examination (COFE) protocol (Stoustrup et al.) as a structured tool for incorporating this screening into routine rheumatology follow-up.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Case-based narrative review, developed per the "CAse-BAsed REview sTandards (CABARET)" framework — literature synthesis built around and interpreted through a single representative clinical case, not a systematic review or meta-analysis.
- **Literature search**: PubMed (MEDLINE), Scopus, and Web of Science, studies published up to June 2025; keywords "juvenile idiopathic arthritis," "temporomandibular joint," "TMJ," "arthritis," "facial asymmetry," "mandibular mobility," "CBCT," combined with Boolean AND/OR.
- **Inclusion criteria**: original studies in JIA patients; clinical and/or radiological TMJ assessment; population under 20 years of age; full text available in English.
- **Exclusion criteria**: reviews/meta-analyses/editorials/conference abstracts; adult-only (≥20y) populations; studies unrelated to TMJ arthritis in JIA.
- **Selection**: single author, non-blinded, non-independent (no second reviewer) — 30 studies included in the narrative synthesis; reference-list snowballing performed but yielded no additional eligible articles.
- **Risk of bias**: explicitly none performed, consistent with the review's narrative (not systematic) character.
- **Case component**: single 9-year-old female patient with 5-year polyarticular JIA history, examined for TMJ involvement despite no reported TMJ symptoms; informed consent from legal guardian; ethics approval from Poznan University of Medical Sciences Ethical Committee (No. 1255/18), per Declaration of Helsinki.
- **Case examination protocol**: en-face photograph with anatomical landmarks (trichion, ophryon, pronasale, subnasale, gnathion) for facial-symmetry assessment; palpation of masticatory muscles/TMJ for tenderness and joint sounds; MMO measured twice with a millimeter ruler between upper/lower central incisor incisal edges; mandibular deviation and lateral excursions measured via pencil-line midline tracking from centric occlusion; protrusive movement measured as anterior mandibular translation; CBCT performed for bony morphometric analysis, graded per Billiau's TMJ radiographic grading scale.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Literature-reported prevalence and clinical-sign rates (cited studies, not this paper's own data):**

| Source (as cited) | Population/measure | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| — (general) | TMJ involvement in JIA | Prevalence 17% to >90%, depending on diagnostic criteria/imaging/duration/population |
| Savioli et al. | Helkimo Index | TMJ dysfunction in 94% of JIA patients; ~half grade II/III (moderate–severe); only 30% grade I (mild) |
| Mohamed et al. | Symptom/sign survey | TMJ-related symptoms in 35%; pain 17.5%; crepitus 7.5%; clinical signs 62%; reduced MMO most common sign (25%) |
| Leksell et al. | Symptom survey | Pain at rest 22%; pain during chewing 71%; pain during mouth opening 68%; reduced MMO in 32% |
| Pawlaczyk-Kamieńska et al. (prior study, cited) | Unilateral TMJ arthritis cohort | Reduced MMO in 58%; facial asymmetry in 23%; mandibular deviation >2 mm in 46.15% (mostly toward the more-affected joint) |
| Stoll et al.; Koss et al. | MMO comparison | No significant MMO difference between JIA patients with vs. without TMJ arthritis (limited diagnostic specificity of MMO alone) |
| Collin et al. | CBCT | TMJ deformities in 39% of JIA patients; significantly associated with reduced MMO |
| Keller et al. | Facial asymmetry/deviation | Facial asymmetry in 37–41% of JIA patients (vs. healthy controls); mandibular deviation during opening in up to 62% (vs. 16% in controls) |
| Müller et al.; Weiss et al. | MRI | TMJ involvement detection rate up to 80% |
| Jank et al. | Conventional ultrasound | Disc displacement visualized in 55.5% of examined TMJs |
| Assaf et al.; Tonni et al. | High-resolution ultrasound (HR-US) | Enhanced visualization of synovial width/condylar irregularities; HR-US lateral periarticular space measurements correlated with MRI inflammation findings, but SRs flag limited sensitivity for early synovitis and heterogeneous protocols |

- Proposed clinical marker: MMO <40 mm suggested (by the cited Pawlaczyk-Kamieńska et al. prior study) as a sensitive indicator of structural TMJ involvement.
- Correlation pattern (cited): mandibular/chin deviation direction corresponds to the side with more radiographic damage; earlier disease onset associates with greater restriction of lateral movement toward the less-affected side; no association found with disease duration itself; MMO positively correlated with mandibular protrusion.
- No direct association found (cited prior study) between severity of condylar radiographic damage and presence/severity of clinical symptoms — some patients with major structural deformity were asymptomatic, others with mild structural change were symptomatic.

**Index case (this paper's own data, 9-year-old girl, 5-year polyarticular JIA, no reported TMJ symptoms):**

- MMO: 30 mm (reduced; normal ≥35 mm for children <10 years)
- Mandibular deviation on opening: 5 mm to the right (pathological threshold ≥2 mm)
- Lateral excursion: 2 mm to the left, 7 mm to the right (normal ≥6 mm) — restricted left-side mobility
- Protrusive movement: 1 mm (normal ≥4 mm) — markedly restricted
- Palpation: no tenderness, no joint sounds (no clicking/crepitus)
- CBCT: right condyle — flattening + subcortical bone defects, Billiau's TMJ radiographic grading scale score 3; left condyle — preserved morphology and cortical integrity, score 0; right mandibular ramus — reduced vertical height (condylion-to-gonion) vs. contralateral side, consistent with impaired unilateral growth
- Diagnosis: unilateral TMJ arthritis, "silent" morphofunctional presentation (no subjective symptoms despite significant clinical and radiographic findings)
- No MRI was performed in this case, so active synovitis could not be confirmed/excluded — only chronic structural damage was documented via CBCT.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single-case narrative review — the case cannot support prevalence, sensitivity/specificity, or generalizability claims; it is illustrative, not statistical evidence.
- Literature selection performed by a single, non-independent reviewer with no formal risk-of-bias assessment — appropriate for a narrative review but means included-study quality is not graded.
- No MRI performed on the index case, so the paper cannot distinguish active synovitis from chronic burnt-out structural damage in that patient.
- No long-term follow-up of the index case — disease trajectory and response to any subsequent treatment are unknown.
- Findings may not generalize across JIA subtypes (this is a polyarticular case) or across disease stages.
- Authors call for improved diagnostic methods, earlier detection strategies, and longitudinal studies of "silent" TMJ arthritis's impact on long-term joint function and quality of life.

## 7. Glossary

- **JIA (Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis, 소아 특발성 관절염)**: chronic childhood arthritis of unknown cause persisting >6 weeks, onset before age 16; can involve the TMJ alongside or instead of other joints.
- **TMJ (Temporomandibular Joint, 측두하악관절)**: the jaw joint; in this paper, the site of silent inflammatory damage in JIA.
- **MMO (Maximal Mouth Opening, 최대개구량)**: maximum interincisal opening distance; a simple chairside functional measure.
- **CBCT (Cone Beam Computed Tomography, 콘빔전산화단층촬영)**: high-resolution bone imaging; detects chronic structural change (flattening, erosion, asymmetry) but not active synovitis.
- **MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging, 자기공명영상)**: gold standard for detecting active synovial inflammation, effusion, pannus, and bone marrow edema in TMJ arthritis, but limited by access/cost/sedation needs in young children.
- **COFE (Clinical Orofacial Examination)**: standardized protocol (Stoustrup et al.) for assessing MMO, lateral excursions, palpation tenderness, joint sounds, and facial asymmetry.
- **Billiau's TMJ radiographic grading scale**: CBCT-based scoring system for condylar structural damage severity (0 = normal, used up to 3 in this case).
- **Condylion / Gonion**: cephalometric landmarks (highest point of the condylar head; angle of the mandible) used here to measure mandibular ramus height and asymmetry.
- **CABARET (CAse-BAsed REview sTandards)**: reporting framework for case-based narrative reviews.
