## Why Ingested

The immediate-loading subcategory needs a biomechanical rationale alongside survival data. This Misch 2004 Part II review supplies the stress-reduction logic for the transitional restoration (patient factors, cantilevers, occlusal direction, contact intensity, surface area), which the [[immediate-implant/loading-protocol/markovic-2024-immediate-vs-early-loading-immediately-placed]] outcome study does not give.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Implant Dentistry, 2004), Part II, on the biomechanical rationale for immediate loading of implants in edentulous and partially edentulous patients — abstract-only ingest, full text not retrieved.

Overload risk is reduced by controlling stress on the transitional restoration, influenced by patient factors, implant position, cantilevers, load direction, contact intensity and diet, and by increasing load-distribution area through implant size, design and surface.

The abstract gives no clinical outcome data; it is a biomechanical rationale, and its recommendations should not be cited as proof that immediate loading is safe.
