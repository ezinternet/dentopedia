## Why Ingested

The wiki's porcine-versus-bovine xenograft evidence stops at 6-month histology/CBCT ([[bone-regeneration/bhatavadekar-2022-comparative-assessment-of-bovine-versus]], [[bone-regeneration/wang-2025-porcine-bovine-xenograft-msfa-arp-sr-ma]]) and at socket preservation / sinus floor elevation — the SR+MA explicitly excluded horizontal GBR, and no held paper reports **implant-level long-term (≥5-year) outcomes after horizontal ridge augmentation (HRA) with a porcine-derived xenograft**. This paper supplies that missing time horizon (mean 67.5 months after loading) in a single-surgeon cohort, which is what a clinician actually needs when choosing between autogenous block, DBBM and porcine particulate for a narrow ridge.

## Three-line Summary

Single-center retrospective cohort (Seoul National University Bundang Hospital, 2014–2017; 28 patients, 49 implants) of simultaneous horizontal ridge augmentation (HRA) with porcine bone-derived xenograft (The Graft; Purgo Biologics) plus implant placement, followed a mean 67.5 ± 10.3 months after prosthesis loading.

Marginal bone loss (MBL) was 0.23 ± 0.36 mm at 1 year and 0.40 ± 0.45 mm at final follow-up; 2/49 implants were lost (survival 95.9%) and 3 further implants failed Albrektsson success criteria (success 89.8%), while the 24 sites grafted with porcine material alone achieved 100% survival and 100% success (MBL 0.23 mm at 1 year, 0.41 mm at 65.8 months).

Porcine xenograft holds up over 5+ years in HRA with MBL comparable to published bovine/autogenous horizontal-augmentation cohorts, but all 5 failures clustered in ridge-split plus mixed-graft sites where postoperative infection occurred — a warning about case complexity, not about the graft species, and the study never measured horizontal bone width.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the **first ≥5-year implant-level outcome data for horizontal ridge augmentation using a porcine-derived xenograft**, a graft species that until now was supported here only by 4–6-month histology and CBCT width data.
- Separates a **porcine-only subgroup (n=24)** from a **mixed-graft subgroup (n=25)** and shows the two behave very differently — 100% survival/success versus 92.0% survival / 80.0% success — making case complexity, not material species, the dominant explanatory variable.
- Reports **membrane use has no measurable effect** on MBL in either the whole cohort or the porcine-only subset (12 vs 12 split, P=0.443 at 1 year and P=0.843 at final), consistent with Gielkens et al.'s null finding.
- Isolates **infection as the single mechanism of every failure**: all 5 failed/compromised sites occurred in 2 patients, all were ridge-split plus mixed-graft sites, and all had perioperative infection with a causal link traced to prosthesis loading.
- Reports **ISQ trajectory** (primary 72.1 ± 8.9 → secondary 76.9 ± 10.8) for simultaneously grafted implants, i.e. no stability dip below the immediate-loading threshold even with simultaneous HRA.

## Methodology

**Design.** Single-center, single-operator (one OMFS surgeon) retrospective cohort; radiographic and chart review by two dentists not involved in surgery. Consecutive patients operated 2014–2017 who met criteria and had >5 years of follow-up.

**Inclusion.** (1) horizontal bone volume deficiency at the implant site; (2) simultaneous implant placement with HRA using porcine graft, follow-up >5 years; (3) age >20 years.

**Exclusion.** (1) alcohol addiction or heavy smoking (>10 cigarettes/day); (2) severe periodontal disease unresponsive to periodontal therapy; (3) uncontrolled diabetes or hypertension; (4) severe liver or kidney disease; (5) head-and-neck radiotherapy.

**Surgery.** Prophylactic cephalosporin 500 mg ×2, NSAID (Naxen) 500 mg, mucosal protectant (Stillen) 60 mg plus 0.12% chlorhexidine gargle from one day pre-op; 2% lidocaine with 1:100,000 epinephrine; crestal + releasing incisions, full-thickness flap; **selective ridge splitting** where the exposed ridge was judged too narrow (Tolstunov & Hicke technique); bone-level titanium implants placed per CBCT planning; buccal cortical decortication with a 2-mm burr; porcine graft packed on the buccal aspect; **autogenous tooth bone (Auto BT) and/or allograft added when the defect was severe**; resorbable collagen membrane over all graft material at operator discretion when a large volume was used; tension-free suturing, sutures out at 7–10 days. Prosthesis (crown or bridge) by two prosthodontists at a mean 8.9 months.

**Outcomes.** ISQ (Osstell Mentor) at placement and at second-stage/impression; MBL measured on periapical radiographs (parallel technique, OC100 CR / Heliodent DS, INFINITT PACS 3.0) from implant shoulder to the mesial and distal uppermost bone contact, mean of the two, at prosthesis delivery / 1 year / final; survival and success per **Albrektsson et al. 1986** (no mobility, no radiolucency, no symptoms, <0.2 mm bone loss per year after year 1 and <1.5 mm total); operation-related and postoperative complications tabulated.

**Statistics.** PASW Statistics 18; Mann–Whitney for MBL by membrane use and by mixed-graft use; log-rank for survival/success by the same two factors; α = 0.05. The porcine-only subgroup (n=24) was re-analyzed the same way for membrane use.

**Note on sample accounting.** 49 sites in 28 patients; follow-up and prosthesis-timing data are reported for **n=47** (the 2 lost implants removed from those means).

### Complications (Table 4)

5 sites in 2 of 28 patients, all with perioperative infection, all ridge-split + mixed-graft cases:

## Limitations

**Stated by the authors**

- Retrospective and not fully controlled: mixed-graft use, membrane use, implant length/diameter all varied; subgroup samples too small.
- Only **vertical bone loss** was measured radiographically; **horizontal width change was never quantified** — CBCT was judged unjustifiable annually, so horizontal change was substituted with clinical soft-tissue contour assessment. For a study whose whole premise is horizontal augmentation, this is the single most important gap.
- Pocket depth change could not be analyzed (only PD >5 mm was recorded, per Karoussis criteria).
- No histology or histomorphometry, so graft resorption/turnover of the porcine material is inferred only from MBL.

**Inferred / structural**

- **Confounding by indication is explicit and admitted**: membranes and mixed grafts were used in the larger, more invasive defects, so the membrane and mixed-graft comparisons describe case severity rather than material effect. The porcine-only 100% result is confounded in the opposite direction (small defects).
- **Survival/success comparison to the literature is cross-study**, against mixed autogenous+bovine cohorts with 2–3-year follow-up (Mordenfeld, Hellem, Meloni) and allograft cohorts (Le & Borzabadi-Farahani) — no concurrent control arm and no bovine/autogenous arm in this cohort.
- "More than 5 years" is slightly overstated: the minimum follow-up after loading is **53.7 months (4.5 years)**, with only the mean (67.5) and maximum (86.9) above 5 years.
- The 3 "success failure" implants' actual MBL values are never given, so the distance beyond the 0.2 mm/year threshold cannot be judged.
- Still photographs (Fig. 2) are the only documentation of graft volume; no volumetric or superimposition analysis.
- ISQ secondary timing is heterogeneous by design (second-stage surgery *or* impression), weakening it as a stability trajectory.
- 3 smokers were retained (heavy smoking >10/day excluded) — light smoking is a residual confounder for MBL.
- Statistical approach is unadjusted pairwise (Mann–Whitney/log-rank), with no multivariable model for site, arch, implant length, or defect severity.

## Glossary

- **HRA (Horizontal Ridge Augmentation)** — surgical increase of buccolingual alveolar width, here by buccal particulate grafting ± membrane ± ridge splitting at the time of implant placement.
- **MBL (Marginal Bone Loss)** — radiographic change in the crestal bone level measured from the implant shoulder to the first bone-to-implant contact, averaged over mesial and distal aspects.
- **ISQ (Implant Stability Quotient)** — 1–100 resonance-frequency-analysis index of implant stability; higher is more stable (≥70 conventionally quoted for immediate loading).
- **Albrektsson success criteria** — 1986 composite: no mobility, no peri-implant radiolucency, no symptoms, and <0.2 mm bone loss per year after the first year (<1.5 mm total).
- **Ridge splitting (bone-splitting osteotomy)** — longitudinal buccolingual splitting of a narrow crest with expansion, used here in 6 sites judged too narrow for the planned implant.
- **Auto BT (autogenous tooth bone graft)** — graft material prepared from the patient's extracted tooth (Korea Tooth Bank), used as an autogenous adjunct in severe defects.
