## Why Ingested

Mechanistic in vitro evidence for why bicortical placement improves stability: cortical bone thickness and density (not total bone height) are the key predictors of bone-implant micromotion in simulated sinus-region bicortical placement. Directly supports the decision framework in [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/bhave-2023-unicortical-bicortical-anchorage-posterior-maxilla]] and [[wiki/sinus-lift/transcrestal/albadani-2024-graftless-sinus-lifting-simultaneous]] regarding when non-augmented bicortical placement can achieve sufficient primary stability.

## Three-line Summary

In vitro μCT + torsional micromotion study, n=17 pig scapula bone segments simulating bicortical posterior maxilla; Straumann SLA 4.1 RN 10 mm implants placed with standardized osteotomy.
Primary stability (bone-implant micromotion) did NOT correlate with total bone thickness (p=0.827) but tended to improve with higher bone mineral density (BMD) or greater implant length embedded in cortical bone (both p≈0.07–0.09); minimum micromotion ~4 μm when cortical engagement ≥2 mm and BMD >1.0 g/cm³.
Clinicians should focus on cortical bone quality and achieving ≥2 mm implant-in-cortex engagement (not just total RBH) when planning non-augmented bicortical implants in atrophic posterior maxilla.

## Key Contributions

- Isolates cortical bone metrics from total RBH as predictors of bicortical primary stability
- Minimum micromotion threshold ~4 μm at cortical engagement ≥2 mm + BMD >1.0 g/cm³
- BMD from μCT predicts primary stability better than gross dimensions
- 5/17 specimens failed to withstand 15 Ncm torque (all had low insertion torque or <2 mm cortical engagement)
- Novel torsional micromotion measurement method (validated to ±1 μm accuracy)

## Methodology

- **Model**: Pig scapulae (n=17 segments, max 40×60×8 mm); two-cortex sandwich resembles posterior maxilla bicortical anatomy
- **Implant**: Straumann Standard SLA® Tissue Level, 4.1 RN, 10 mm length
- **Drill protocol**: 2.8 mm pilot → 3.5 mm pilot (just through coronal cortex); standard bicortical sequence
- **μCT**: SkyScan 1076, 18 μm resolution; BMD, cortical thickness, spongeous thickness quantified
- **Stability metric**: Torsional bone-implant micromotion via 3D optical marker tracking (±15 Ncm, 3 cycles, 2°/min)
- **Correlation**: Pearson's r between micromotion and BMD, total thickness, cortical thickness

## Key Results

- Micromotion NOT correlated with total bone thickness (r correlation, p=0.827)
- Micromotion tended to decrease with increasing BMD (p=0.095) and cortical bone length (p=0.071)
- Lowest micromotion: ~4 μm at cortical engagement >2 mm and BMD >1.0 g/cm³
- 5 specimens loosened before 15 Ncm: all had insertion torque <15–25 Ncm and/or <2 mm in cortical bone
- Cortical bone embedded length ranged 1.8–2.5 mm across successful specimens

## Limitations

- Animal model (pig scapula) not identical to human posterior maxilla anatomy
- Small n=17; some heterogeneity in specimen preparation
- No clinical validation of the μm-level micromotion threshold
- Single implant design and length tested

## Glossary

- **BMD (Bone Mineral Density)**: Mineral content per unit bone volume (g/cm³); determines resistance to torsional load
- **Micromotion**: Relative movement between implant and bone at interface under load; >150 μm → fibrous encapsulation risk
- **Bicortical placement**: Implant spanning from crestal cortex through cancellous bone into the opposite (sinus floor) cortex
- **Cortical engagement length**: Length of implant embedded within cortical bone (excluding cancellous zone)
