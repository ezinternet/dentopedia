## Why Ingested

Adds a mechanistic, cell-level animal study to the insertion-torque (IT) cluster: it shows what high IT does to peri-implant bone (osteocyte death, micro-fractures, more resorption, less formation), which reinforces the histologic high-torque harm in [[wiki/implants/isq/trisi-2011-high-low-implant-torque-histology-sheep]] and gives biological grounding to the clinical high-torque questions synthesized in [[wiki/overviews/high-insertion-torque-primary-stability-crestal-bone-overview]].

## Three-line Summary
(Line 1: animal experimental study; titanium micro-implants placed in murine femurs with low vs high insertion torque, torque scaled to approximate clinically sized implants; finite element modeling of strain, stiffness testing, and molecular/cellular/histomorphometric analyses at multiple time points)
(Line 2: osteotomy alone creates a narrow zone of dead and dying osteocytes, not significantly enlarged by low IT; high IT more than doubles this zone, with micro-fractures, increased bone resorption and decreased bone formation)
(Line 3: high IT creates high interfacial stress and strain associated with peri-implant bone damage, so the authors conclude it should be avoided; abstract-level only, no numeric torque values or sample sizes available)

## 2. Key Contributions
- Quantifies how peri-implant bone responds to strains and stresses produced by low vs high insertion torque (IT).
- Reports that osteotomy preparation alone produces a narrow zone of dead and dying osteocytes in peri-implant bone.
- Reports that high IT more than doubles that zone and is accompanied by micro-fractures, increased resorption and decreased bone formation.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: animal experimental (murine femur) with finite element modeling
- **Intervention**: titanium micro-implants inserted with low and high IT, torque values scaled to approximate clinically sized implants
- **Outcomes**: strain distribution/magnitude (finite element modeling); primary and secondary implant stability (stiffness tests); apoptosis, mineralization, resorption and collagen matrix deposition (molecular, cellular and histomorphometric analyses at multiple time points)
- **n**: not available in the abstract

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Osteotomy preparation: narrow zone of dead and dying osteocytes in peri-implant bone.
- Low IT: zone not significantly enlarged.
- High IT: zone more than doubled; micro-fractures, increased bone resorption, decreased bone formation.
- No numeric torque values, strain values or p-values are given in the abstract.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Abstract-only: full text not retrieved, so methods detail, sample sizes and numbers are unavailable.
- Murine micro-implant model with scaled torque; extrapolation to clinically sized human implants is by scaling only.

## 7. Glossary
- **Insertion torque (IT)**: torque applied when seating an implant, used clinically as an index of primary stability.
- **Osteocyte apoptosis**: programmed death of bone cells in peri-implant bone, here used as a marker of damage.
- **Finite element modeling (FEM)**: computational method used to calculate strain and stress distribution around the implant.
