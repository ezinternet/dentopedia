## Why Ingested

Directly compares six zirconia surface treatments (polishing, grinding, sandblasting, chemical etching, laser structuring, plasma etching) on tetragonal-to-monoclinic (t→m) phase transformation, surface roughness, wettability, and surface free energy — providing head-to-head data for framework preparation decisions in porcelain-fused-to-zirconia restorations.

→ [[dental-materials/zirconia/mielak-2024-alternative-treatments-for-zirconium-oxide]]

## Three-line Summary

Six surface treatment methods on 3Y-TZP zirconia were evaluated by XRD, roughness, wettability, and microscopy; chemical etching induced the least monoclinic phase (1.8%) but failed roughness requirements, while sandblasting induced the most (20.8%) yet gave the best surface free energy. Laser structuring and plasma etching offered a favorable balance — low phase transformation (7.9% and 4.8% monoclinic) with adequate surface geometry and wettability. Laser structuring is recommended as the superior alternative to sandblasting: controllable, economical, and structurally customizable.

## 세줄요약

6가지 지르코니아 표면처리(연마·연삭·샌드블라스팅·화학부식·레이저·플라즈마)를 XRD·거칠기·습윤성으로 비교했으며, 화학부식이 단사정상 변태 최소(1.8%)이나 거칠기 불충분, 샌드블라스팅은 최대(20.8%)이나 표면자유에너지 최대였다. 레이저 구조화(7.9%)와 플라즈마 에칭(4.8%)은 낮은 상변태와 적절한 표면 형상을 동시에 달성했다. 레이저 구조화가 제어 용이성·경제성·구조 맞춤 가능성 측면에서 가장 권장된다.

## Document Information

- **Study type**: In vitro laboratory study
- **Sample**: 30 cylindrical 3Y-TZP zirconia samples (Ceramill Zi; Amann Girrbach AG), diameter 25 mm, height 8 mm; sintered at 1450°C
- **Groups**: 6 treatment groups (n = 5 each): polishing, grinding, sandblasting (110 µm Al2O3, 0.4 MPa), 40% HF chemical etching (30 min), CO2 laser structuring, dry plasma etching
- **Outcome measures**: XRD (Garvie-Nicholson method for monoclinic%), SEM microscopy, contact angle (Krüss FM40 EasyDrop, water and diiodomethane), surface free energy (Owens-Wendt), surface roughness Ra (Mitutoyo SJ-410, ISO 21920-3:2021)

## Key Contributions

1. First side-by-side comparison of six zirconia surface preparation methods on the phase transformation ratio, evaluated simultaneously with roughness, wettability, and surface free energy
2. Demonstrates that laser structuring and plasma etching uniquely combine low t→m phase transformation with customizable surface geometry — not achievable with mechanical methods
3. Quantifies that sandblasting (the clinical standard) produces 20.8% monoclinic phase, suggesting long-term risk for ceramic chipping and framework degradation
4. Documents chemical etching (40% HF) as phase-safest option but practically unusable due to non-wettable surface (contact angle 101.5°) and near-zero roughness
5. Provides surface free energy and polar/dispersive component data to predict ceramic-framework wetting at firing temperatures

## Methodology

**Materials**: Ceramill Zi 3Y-TZP (Amann Girrbach AG), zirconia content ≥99%; sintering with universal program (8°C/min to 1450°C, 2 h dwell, ~10 h total cycle, ~21% shrinkage).

**Surface treatments**:
- Polishing: successive grits 120→2500, 3 µm diamond suspension final
- Grinding: defined abrasive sequence
- Sandblasting (abrasive blasting): 110 µm Al2O3, 0.4 MPa pressure
- Chemical etching: 40% HF, 30 min
- Laser structuring: CO2 laser
- Dry plasma etching: requires masking mesh for patterned geometry

**XRD**: Empyrean diffractometer (Malvern Panalytical), CuKα 1.54056 Å, 40 kV / 45 mA; phase ratio by Garvie-Nicholson (G-N) method.

**Wettability**: Contact angles with distilled water and diiodomethane (0.3 µL drops, n=5); surface free energy by Owens-Wendt model.

**Roughness**: Mitutoyo SJ-410, ISO 21920-3:2021.

## Key Results

### Phase Transformation (XRD — monoclinic % by weight)

| Treatment | Monoclinic % |
|---|---|
| Sandblasting | 20.8% |
| Grinding | 16.9% |
| Polishing | 16.8% |
| Laser structuring | 7.9% |
| Plasma etching | 4.8% |
| Chemical etching (40% HF) | 1.8% |

Mechanical methods (polishing, grinding, sandblasting) all exceeded 16% monoclinic; chemical/energy-based methods stayed below 8%.

### Surface Roughness

Sandblasting and grinding produced surface irregularities ~5 µm depth — considered optimal for ceramic anchoring. Laser-cut grooves achieved similar depth with adjustable width/spacing. Plasma etching yielded ~1 µm depth (adjustable). Polishing and chemical etching produced only tenths-of-micron irregularities — insufficient for mechanical anchoring.

### Wettability (Water Contact Angle)

| Treatment | Water CA (°) | Surface Free Energy (mJ/m²) |
|---|---|---|
| Sandblasting | 30.3 ± 1.6 | 64.4 ± 3.0 |
| Laser structuring | 50.3 ± 1.5 | 50.3 ± 3.8 |
| Plasma etching | 52.2 ± 1.6 | 49.3 ± 0.7 |
| Grinding | — | ~30–38 |
| Polishing | 86.8 ± 2.3 | ~30–38 |
| Chemical etching | 101.5 ± 0.7 | ~38 |

Chemical etching produced non-wettable surface (CA > 90°) with near-zero polar component (0.9 mJ/m²), making it clinically unusable for porcelain layering.

### Conclusions

- Recommended treatments: laser structuring and plasma etching
- Laser structuring: easier to control, more economical, more varied structural possibilities
- Plasma etching: requires masking mesh for defined patterns — additional constraint
- Sandblasting remains a valid clinical choice for ceramic bond but carries long-term t→m transformation risk

## Limitations

- Small n per group (n = 5)
- In vitro only — no bond strength testing to veneering ceramics, no aging/fatigue data
- Wettability measured at room temperature; ceramic-framework bonding occurs at firing temperatures (behavior may differ)
- 40% HF concentration and 30 min etching time — not standard clinical protocol; longer etching unexplored
- Plasma etching parameters (time, power) and laser parameters not standardized across published literature
- Sandblasting time effect on monoclinic phase not investigated

## Glossary

- **t→m phase transformation**: tetragonal-to-monoclinic polymorphic transformation in zirconia; causes ~3–5% volume increase, compressive stress (strengthening near cracks) but also microcrack formation and long-term structural weakening
- **3Y-TZP**: 3 mol% yttria-stabilized tetragonal zirconia polycrystal — most common dental zirconia grade
- **Garvie-Nicholson (G-N) method**: XRD calculation formula for quantifying monoclinic phase fraction in zirconia
- **Surface free energy (γs)**: thermodynamic measure of surface reactivity; polar component governs water-based ceramic flow, dispersive component governs behavior at firing temperatures
- **Owens-Wendt model**: two-liquid contact angle method for calculating polar and dispersive components of surface free energy
- **LTD (Low-Temperature Degradation)**: spontaneous t→m transformation at 65–400°C in humid environments (aging); separate but related degradation pathway
