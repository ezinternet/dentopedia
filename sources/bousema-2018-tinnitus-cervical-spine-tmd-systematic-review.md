## Why Ingested

배치 인제스트: 치과-이명(tinnitus) PMC 문헌을 모아 `tmj` 카테고리의 이명-TMD 연관성 근거 기반을 구축하는 작업. 기존 위키에는 편측저작(chewing-side preference)에 국한된 좁은 범위의 단면연구 [[wiki/tmj/ma-2025-tmd-tinnitus-chewing-side-preference-correlation]] 한 편뿐이었다. 본 논문은 이명과 TMD/경추부장애(Cervical Spine Disorders, CSD) 사이의 정량적 승산비(Odds Ratio, OR) 연관성을 확립한 2018년 SR+MA(체계적 문헌고찰+메타분석)로, 이번 배치의 다른 신규 논문들이 근거로 삼는 토대(anchor) 역할을 한다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis of 24 studies (22 independent, from 2,139 initial records; Medline/Embase/Pedro, 1966–Sep 2017) examining the association between subjective tinnitus and cervical spine disorders (CSD) or temporomandibular disorders (TMD).

Tinnitus patients showed higher odds of CSD (OR 2.6, 95% CI 1.1–6.4) and a bidirectional association with TMD, with odds ratios ranging from 2.3 (95% CI 1.5–3.6) for arthrogenous TMD to 6.7 (95% CI 2.4–18.8) for unspecified TMD; myogenous TMD OR was 4.1 (95% CI 2.1–8.1).

Evidence quality was low overall (no high-quality studies; only 2/22 blinded assessors, 3/22 randomized selection) and publication bias likely inflated several pooled estimates (trim-and-fill reduced the unspecified-TMD OR from 6.7 to 4.5), so the association — while statistically significant — should be treated as weak evidence pending better-designed studies.

## 세줄요약

24편(독립 연구 22편, 초기 2,139편에서 선별; Medline/Embase/Pedro, 1966–2017년 9월) 대상 체계적 문헌고찰+메타분석(SR+MA)으로 주관적 이명(subjective tinnitus)과 경추부장애(Cervical Spine Disorders, CSD) 또는 턱관절장애(Temporomandibular Disorders, TMD)의 연관성을 분석.

이명 환자는 CSD 승산비(Odds Ratio, OR) 2.6(95% CI 1.1–6.4)로 유의하게 높았고, TMD와는 양방향(bidirectional) 연관성이 확인되어 관절성(arthrogenous) TMD OR 2.3(95% CI 1.5–3.6)부터 미분류(unspecified) TMD OR 6.7(95% CI 2.4–18.8)까지 범위를 보였으며, 근육성(myogenous) TMD OR은 4.1(95% CI 2.1–8.1)이었다.

전체적으로 근거 수준이 낮았고(고품질 연구 없음; 평가자 눈가림 2/22, 무작위 선정 3/22) 출판편향(publication bias)이 일부 통합추정치를 부풀렸을 가능성이 있어(trim-and-fill 적용 시 미분류 TMD OR 6.7→4.5로 감소), 통계적으로 유의하지만 "약한 근거(weak evidence)"로 해석해야 한다.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Association Between Subjective Tinnitus and Cervical Spine or Temporomandibular Disorders: A Systematic Review
- **Authors**: E.J. Bousema, E.A. Koops, P. van Dijk, P.U. Dijkstra
- **Journal**: Trends in Hearing, 2018
- **DOI**: 10.1177/2331216518800640
- **PMID**: 30269683 / **PMCID**: PMC6168723
- **Article type**: Systematic Review + Meta-Analysis

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review (to the authors' knowledge) quantifying the association between subjective tinnitus and CSD or TMD using pooled odds ratios.
- Establishes that the tinnitus–TMD relationship is **bidirectional** (tinnitus patients have more TMD; TMD patients have more tinnitus), while the tinnitus–CSD relationship is only demonstrated as **unidirectional** (no included study examined tinnitus prevalence in CSD patients vs. controls).
- Stratifies TMD into arthrogenous (TMDa), myogenous (TMDm), and unspecified subtypes, and a combined CSD+TMD category, each with separate pooled ORs.
- Applies funnel-plot publication-bias assessment with trim-and-fill correction, showing several pooled ORs are likely inflated.
- Proposes a brainstem-level explanatory mechanism (trigeminal–cochlear nucleus convergence) and notes the association is largely ipsilateral.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Databases**: Medline, Embase, Pedro; publication window 1966 to September 2017.
- **Search terms**: Tinnitus AND (Temporomandibular Joint OR Jaw OR Cervical Vertebrae OR Neck Muscles OR Facial Muscles OR Neck Pain OR Masticatory Muscles OR Musculoskeletal OR Trigger point OR combinations of Head/Jaw/Neck/Shoulder with Muscle/Pain/Trauma/injuries).
- **Screening**: Titles/abstracts screened by first author; full texts independently assessed by two observers (interobserver Cohen's kappa = 0.70, 86% absolute agreement).
- **Inclusion**: studies where tinnitus and CSD or TMD were primary or secondary outcomes, compared against a control group.
- **Exclusion**: reviews, letters to the editor, animal studies, n<10, trauma-caused neck/jaw disorders.
- **No language restrictions**; translation performed as needed (none required in this review).
- **Methodological quality**: 9 criteria (modified from a validated quality tool), each scored [+]=Yes / [–]=No / [?]=Cannot be determined, reported per-item (not summed) because item weight against bias is not uniform. Criteria covered: clear research question, defined study population, comparable control recruitment, valid/consistent case definitions, differentiation of patients from controls, random selection, matching, valid/consistent exposure measures, and assessor blinding.
- **Statistical synthesis**: Comprehensive Meta-Analysis V3 (Biostat); random-effects model; summary statistic = odds ratio; funnel plots per association with nonparametric trim-and-fill imputation to adjust for publication bias.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Search yield**: 2,139 records (PubMed 1,069; Embase 1,053; Pedro 17) → 1,581 after dedup → 64 full-texts assessed → 24 met inclusion → 22 independent studies included after removing 2 duplicate-dataset articles.
- **Study design**: 20 cross-sectional, 2 longitudinal (baseline data used). 12 studies compared CSD/TMD presence by tinnitus status; 10 compared tinnitus presence by TMD status; none examined tinnitus presence by CSD status alone.
- **Category breakdown**: tinnitus–CSD (5 studies), tinnitus–CSD+TMD combined (3 studies), tinnitus–TMD unspecified (6 studies), tinnitus–TMDa (11 studies), tinnitus–TMDm (7 studies).
- **Pooled odds ratios**:
  - Tinnitus–CSD: OR 2.6 (95% CI [1.1, 6.4]); individual study ORs 1.2–10.9.
  - Tinnitus–CSD+TMD combined: OR 5.5 (95% CI [3.4, 9.0]); individual ORs 4.9–7.0; 67–83% of tinnitus prevalence in these specialized-hospital cohorts.
  - Tinnitus–TMD (unspecified): OR 6.7 (95% CI [2.4, 18.8]); individual ORs 1.7–44.3; 37–88% of TMD-clinic patients perceived tinnitus.
  - Tinnitus–TMDa (arthrogenous): OR 2.3 (95% CI [1.5, 3.6]); individual ORs 1.2–9.9; 20–59% (specialized clinics) to 21–48% (other settings) tinnitus prevalence.
  - Tinnitus–TMDm (myogenous): OR 4.1 (95% CI [2.1, 8.1]); individual ORs 1.3–10.0.
- **Laterality**: 3 studies examined side-concordance; two found predominantly ipsilateral tinnitus-TMD/trigger-point co-occurrence (contralateral TMJ asymptomatic in 94% in one study); a third found 56.5% laterality concordance (p<.001) between tinnitus side and the side with most myofascial trigger points.
- **Publication bias**: Funnel-plot asymmetry suggested publication bias for CSD+TMD, TMD (unspecified), and TMDm associations. Trim-and-fill adjustment reduced pooled ORs: CSD+TMD 5.5→4.9; TMD (unspecified) 6.7→4.5; TMDm 4.1→3.3.
- **Quality assessment**: criteria met per study ranged 1–7 of 9 (kappa=0.33, 64% absolute agreement). Only 2/22 studies used blinded assessors, 3/22 randomly selected patients/controls, 5/22 matched for age/gender, 16/22 recruited patients and controls from the same population. Only 8/22 studies used well-described/validated instruments (all used RDC/TMD). Tinnitus was most often assessed via a single non-validated question (16/22 studies), with no severity data collected in any study.
- **GRADE**: overall level of evidence rated **low** for both the tinnitus–CSD and tinnitus–TMD associations.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only one author performed the initial title/abstract screening, risking missed studies (mitigated partially by reference-list checking, which yielded no additional studies).
- Tinnitus was almost always assessed by a single non-validated question rather than a validated instrument, so severity data are entirely absent and its effect on the association is unknown.
- Modified quality-assessment tool had low interobserver kappa (0.33) despite acceptable absolute agreement (64%); a planned meta-regression of quality vs. outcome was abandoned because quality-criteria distributions were too skewed.
- No high-quality studies were identified for either association — every included study had at least one critical unmet quality criterion (commonly blinding or group comparability).
- Recruitment heterogeneity (e.g., specialized TMD-clinic patients vs. general-dental-care controls) may introduce selection bias not fully captured by the quality tool.
- The tinnitus–CSD relationship is demonstrated only unidirectionally; no included study tested whether CSD patients have higher tinnitus prevalence than controls — a clear gap for future primary research.
- Authors recommend future studies improve blinding and group comparability, use validated instruments for tinnitus and CSD symptoms, and evaluate CSD/TMD laterality relative to the side of perceived tinnitus.
- The review does not address treatment effects — whether treating CSD or TMD reduces tinnitus was outside its scope.

## 6. Related Work

- Somatosensory tinnitus modulation literature (two-thirds of tinnitus patients can modulate loudness/pitch via neck, head, or jaw muscle contraction) motivating the review's premise.
- Anatomical/functional connections between the trigeminal spinal nucleus and the cochlear nucleus (CN) in the medulla, proposed as the neural substrate for the tinnitus-CSD/TMD association and its ipsilateral tendency.
- RDC/TMD (Research Diagnostic Criteria for Temporomandibular Disorders) as the most consistently used validated diagnostic instrument across included studies.
- Companion wiki paper: [[wiki/tmj/ma-2025-tmd-tinnitus-chewing-side-preference-correlation]], a 2025 cross-sectional study that narrows in on chewing-side preference and TMJ-area pain as specific correlates of tinnitus within a TMD population — a mechanistic/clinical extension of the broad OR associations established here.

## 7. Glossary

- **CSD (Cervical Spine Disorders)**: neck-region musculoskeletal disorders including pain, tenderness, and myofascial trigger points.
- **TMD (Temporomandibular Disorders)**: disorders of the temporomandibular joint and/or masticatory muscles.
- **TMDa (arthrogenous TMD)**: TMD of joint origin — disc displacement, joint pain/sounds.
- **TMDm (myogenous TMD)**: TMD of muscular origin — myofascial pain/tenderness of masticatory muscles.
- **Subjective tinnitus**: tinnitus audible only to the patient (as opposed to objective tinnitus, audible to an observer).
- **Somatosensory tinnitus**: tinnitus modulated by somatosensory input (neck/head/jaw muscle contraction).
- **RDC/TMD**: Research Diagnostic Criteria for Temporomandibular Disorders, a validated diagnostic instrument.
- **Trim-and-fill**: a nonparametric statistical method to estimate and adjust for publication bias in meta-analysis by imputing theoretically missing studies.
- **GRADE**: Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation — framework for rating overall quality/certainty of evidence.
