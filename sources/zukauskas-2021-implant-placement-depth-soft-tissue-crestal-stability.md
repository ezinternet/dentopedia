## Why Ingested

Adds a same-research-group counterpoint to the wiki's crestal-bone cluster: whereas [[wiki/implants/mbl/linkevicius-2015-crestal-bone-stability-soft-tissue-thickening-matching]] isolated soft-tissue thickness at a matching connection and found thin mucosa costly, this case-control study combines platform switching, subcrestal placement, and thin tissue in one arm and reports *more* crestal bone loss than matching-connection supracrestal implants in thick tissue. It bears directly on the STH × placement-depth interaction summarized in [[wiki/overviews/platform-switching-marginal-bone-preservation-synthesis]], and is ingested as a confounded clinical signal rather than as an isolated platform-switching comparison.

## Three-line Summary

Case-control clinical trial (66 patients, two-piece internal-hex implants) comparing early crestal bone changes around platform-switched implants placed subcrestally in thin soft tissue with matching-connection implants placed supracrestally in thick soft tissue.

At 2 months mean bone loss was 0.2 mm (SD 0.22 mm; range 0.1–1.2 mm) in controls versus −0.69 mm (SD 0.65 mm; range 0–2.6 mm) in the test group (P < .05); at 1 year the values were 0.28 mm (SD 0.36 mm) versus −0.6 mm (SD 0.55 mm).

Platform-switched subcrestal implants in thin tissue showed significantly more bone loss than non-switched supracrestal implants in thick tissue — but connection geometry, placement depth, and soft-tissue thickness all differed between groups, so the effect cannot be attributed to any single variable.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Reports early (2-month) and 1-year crestal bone changes for platform-switched subcrestal implants in thin soft tissue — a combination underrepresented in the platform-switching literature held in this wiki.
- Direct head-to-head clinical comparison of two multi-factorial treatment packages (platform switching + ~1.5 mm subcrestal + thin tissue versus matching connection + ~0.5–1 mm supracrestal + thick tissue), with a significant early difference.
- Demonstrates that a platform-switched design does not guarantee less crestal bone loss when placed subcrestally in thin soft tissue — the reported direction runs opposite to the usual platform-switching bone-preservation narrative, though the design cannot isolate why.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Case-control comparative clinical trial (as labeled in the abstract).
- **n**: 66 patients with two-piece internal-hex dental implants; control n = 33, test n = 33.
- **Control group**: horizontally matching implant–abutment connection, placed approximately 0.5–1 mm supracrestally, surrounded by thick soft tissue.
- **Test group**: platform-switched implants, placed approximately 1.5 mm subcrestally, surrounded by vertically thin soft tissue.
- **Outcomes**: clinical examinations and intraoral radiographs; mean crestal bone loss at 2 months and 1 year, with statistical analysis (significance threshold P < .05).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Three variables change simultaneously between groups — connection geometry (platform-switched vs matching), placement depth (~1.5 mm subcrestal vs ~0.5–1 mm supracrestal), and soft-tissue thickness (thin vs thick) — so no single factor can be identified as the cause of the difference.
- Case-control assignment by tissue condition rather than randomization; no adjustment for confounders is reported in the abstract.
- Only 2-month and 1-year radiographic outcomes are reported; no survival, probing-depth, esthetic, or patient-reported outcomes appear in the abstract.
- The negative test-group means with non-negative ranges are internally inconsistent as printed; the sign convention is undefined in the abstract, so the magnitude should not be over-interpreted.
- Full text was not retrieved; any methodological detail beyond the abstract (sample-size calculation, blinded measurement, radiographic calibration, bone-loss definition) is unavailable.

## 7. Glossary

- **Platform switching (PS)**: Restoring a wider implant platform with a narrower abutment, moving the implant–abutment junction horizontally inward away from the crestal bone.
- **Subcrestal placement**: Positioning the implant platform below the alveolar crest (here approximately 1.5 mm), as opposed to supracrestal placement approximately 0.5–1 mm above it.
- **Crestal bone stability / marginal bone loss (MBL)**: Radiographic change in the peri-implant crestal bone level, used here as the primary outcome at 2 months and 1 year.
- **Soft-tissue thickness (vertically thin/thick)**: The vertical dimension of the peri-implant mucosa; vertically thin tissue is the test-group condition here.
- **Matching connection**: A horizontally matched implant–abutment interface (no platform offset), used in the control group.
- **Internal hex**: A two-piece implant–abutment connection geometry used in all implants in this trial.
