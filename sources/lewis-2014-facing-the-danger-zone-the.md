## Three-line Summary

Single case report (emergency department, USA): a 31-year-old man with recurrent right periorbital and malar swelling, where contrast maxillofacial CT showed preseptal cellulitis without a defined abscess but bedside ultrasound found a small abscess close to the globe.

A specialist then performed incision and drainage; he received IV antibiotics for 2 days and was discharged improved without complication (n=1; no accuracy statistics).

Illustrates that physical exam can miss an occult abscess and that ultrasound can add information when CT does not, especially near the eye and nose; one case, authors' interpretation, and no stated dental source.

## 1. Document Information

- Journal: Case Reports in Emergency Medicine, vol. 2014, article 935283 (published 2014-01-16)
- Design: case report with a short educational background and discussion
- Full text: PMC (PMC4006571), read in full (about 5,900 characters)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Argues that exam alone is often insufficient to tell cellulitis from cellulitis-with-abscess and that bedside ultrasound can rule an abscess in or out.
- Emphasizes the "danger zone" (triangle formed by the nasal bridge and the mouth corners), where venous drainage via the ophthalmic veins to the cavernous sinus can carry infection intracranially.
- Documents one case where ultrasound found an abscess that contrast CT did not.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Narrative case presentation: history, vitals (afebrile; 98.4°F), exam (right periorbital and malar erythema, edema, overlying pustule with scant serous exudate), labs (WBC 9.3, CRP 2.07), maxillofacial CT with contrast, bedside ultrasound.
- Background text states the exam signs: cellulitis = erythema, edema, warmth; abscess suspected when a tender fluctuant area is palpated within the cellulitis.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Contrast CT: preseptal cellulitis, no specific abscess. Bedside ultrasound: small soft-tissue abscess in close proximity to the globe.
- Outcome: incision and drainage by a facial specialist, hospitalization with IV antibiotics for 2 days, improvement, discharge.
- No sensitivity, specificity or comparison counts are given.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- n=1; the authors' interpretation that ultrasound is "accurate" is not tested by any data in the paper.
- The infection is not attributed to a dental source (chronic swelling for months, prior facial cellulitis with abscess requiring I&D 1.5 years earlier); it is a facial soft-tissue infection, so applying it to odontogenic infection is an extrapolation.
- Ultrasound is operator-dependent, as the authors themselves note.

## 6. Related Work

- Authors state CT with IV contrast is considered by many the gold standard for abscess, but it is not always available and carries radiation, contrast and cost.
- Wiki: [[oral-surgery/frid-2025-diagnostic-accuracy-and-clinical-impact]] and [[oral-surgery/ghali-2021-fascial-space-odontogenic-infections-ultrasonography]].

## 7. Glossary

- Danger zone: facial triangle from the nasal bridge to the corners of the mouth with venous communication to the cavernous sinus
- Preseptal cellulitis: infection of the eyelid and periorbital tissue anterior to the orbital septum
- I&D: incision and drainage
