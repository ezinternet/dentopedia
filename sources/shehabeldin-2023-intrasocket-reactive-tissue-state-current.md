## Why Ingested
The wiki already holds cohort-level evidence that intrasocket reactive tissue (IRT) can be kept and used as a socket seal or primary closure material — [[immediate-implant/gap-grafting/liu-2019-reactive-soft-tissue-primary-closure-posterior]], [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/deng-2024-posterior-open-wound-healing-immediate-implant]], [[immediate-implant/gap-grafting/liu-2022-reactive-soft-tissue-primary-closure-2year]] — but no held page frames the **removal-vs-preservation decision itself**, its histopathological/biological rationale, and the debridement instruments involved. This Wang group review (Int J Oral Implantol 2023) is ingested to supply that decision frame and to anchor the RST cohort pages to a mechanism-level reference.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Int J Oral Implantol 2023, PMID 37158179) by the Wang group summarising current knowledge on intrasocket reactive tissue (IRT) in extraction socket healing from a histopathological and biological perspective, discussing the mechanisms by which *residual* IRT can act either favourably or unfavourably on healing — abstract-only ingest.

It then overviews the hand and rotary instruments currently used for IRT debridement, argues the case for preserving IRT as a socket sealing material, and presents clinical cases in which IRT was either removed or preserved after extraction and before alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) — no pooled or quantitative outcome data are reported in the abstract.

Bottom line: whether IRT is a contaminant to be debrided or a free autologous seal to be kept is presented as a **site- and tissue-dependent clinical decision**, not a fixed rule, and the authors explicitly state that future studies are needed to test the suggested beneficial effects of IRT on socket healing outcomes.

## 세줄요약

서술적 리뷰 (Narrative Review; Int J Oral Implantol 2023, PMID 37158179, Wang 그룹): 발치와내 반응성 조직 (Intrasocket Reactive Tissue, IRT)이 발치와 치유에 미치는 영향을 조직병리학·생물학 관점에서 정리하고, **잔존 IRT가 치유에 유리하게도 불리하게도 작용할 수 있는 기전**을 논의 — 초록만 인제스트 (abstract-only ingest).

이어서 IRT 제거 (Debridement)에 쓰이는 수기·회전 기구들을 개관하고, IRT를 소켓 밀봉재 (Socket Seal)로 보존하는 접근과 그 이점을 논증하며, 치조제 보존 (Alveolar Ridge Preservation, ARP) 전에 IRT를 제거한 경우와 보존한 경우의 임상 증례를 제시 — 초록에는 통합·정량적 결과 수치가 없다.

결론: IRT를 오염물로 보고 제거할지, 자가 밀봉재로 보고 보존할지는 **고정된 규칙이 아니라 부위·조직 상태에 따른 임상 결정**이며, 저자들은 IRT의 치유 촉진 효과를 검증할 향후 연구가 필요하다고 명시한다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Frames **IRT as a decision variable rather than a disposal problem**: the review's organising question is whether residual intrasocket tissue should be removed or deliberately preserved, and it presents both directions as clinically legitimate depending on the case.
- Supplies the **histopathological and biological rationale** for IRT's dual effect — the abstract states the review explains mechanisms by which residual IRT can influence healing positively *or* negatively, without specifying which mechanism maps to which outcome (that detail is in the full text, outside this ingest).
- Consolidates the **instrument inventory** for IRT debridement at the level of two families — hand instruments and rotary instruments — which the abstract does not enumerate further.
- Advances **IRT as an autologous socket sealing material**, connecting the tissue already present in the socket to the socket-seal question that this wiki otherwise covers only through graft/matrix materials.
- Provides **illustrative clinical cases** of both strategies (removal vs preservation) executed *after extraction and before ARP*, i.e. it situates the decision inside an ARP workflow rather than in unassisted healing alone.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
This is a **narrative review**, so there is no protocol to report, and the abstract describes none:
- **Design**: narrative/expert review of current knowledge (the abstract uses "This review focuses on…", "It summarises the current knowledge…", "It provides an overview…").
- **Structure as described in the abstract** (four components):
  1. Histopathological and biological summary of IRT.
  2. Mechanisms by which *residual* IRT can positively or negatively affect socket healing.
  3. Overview of hand and rotary instruments used for IRT debridement.
  4. Preservation of IRT as a socket sealing material, with its proposed benefits, plus clinical cases of removal *or* preservation before ARP.
- **Evidence base**: the abstract reports no databases searched, no date range, no inclusion/exclusion criteria, no number of studies, and no appraisal instrument — consistent with a narrative review.
- **Case material**: clinical cases are mentioned but no patient count, site distribution, follow-up period, or outcome measurement is given in the abstract.
- **Not reported in the abstract**: author affiliations, funding, conflicts of interest, and whether any histopathological material was newly generated versus summarised from the literature.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
The abstract reports **no quantitative outcome data** — no effect sizes, no percentages, no sample sizes, and no meta-analytic pooling. What it does state, qualitatively:

|---|---|---|
| IRT and healing | IRT has an *impact* on extraction socket healing and must be understood histopathologically and biologically before any removal/preservation decision | not quantified in abstract |

| Debridement | Hand and rotary instruments are the two instrument families currently used for IRT debridement; the review gives an overview of them | not quantified in abstract |
| IRT as seal | Preserving IRT as a **socket sealing material** may offer benefits, which the review argues for | not quantified in abstract |

Methodological stance worth carrying forward: the review does not resolve the debate — it **legitimises both directions** and converts the question from "is debridement mandatory?" into "which tissue, in which socket, for which reconstructive goal?"

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **Narrative review by design**: no systematic search, no explicit eligibility criteria, no risk-of-bias or certainty appraisal (GRADE or equivalent), and no quantitative synthesis. Any statement of effect here is expert synthesis, not pooled estimate.
- **Abstract-only in this wiki**: the specific instruments, the mechanistic details, the case outcomes, and the authors' own caveats are all in the full text, which was not available at ingest time. Nothing above should be cited as if the full text had been read.
- **No numbers at all**: the abstract reports no sample sizes, no healing dimensions, no histomorphometry and no follow-up durations, so this page cannot support any quantitative claim about IRT removal or preservation.
- **Selection/illustration risk**: clinical cases in a narrative review are chosen to illustrate the authors' framework; without a denominator they cannot estimate how often either strategy succeeds.
- **Authors' own stated gap**: "Future studies are needed to investigate the suggested beneficial effects of intrasocket reactive tissue on socket healing outcomes" — i.e. the preservation arm of the argument is a hypothesis, not an established benefit.

## 7. Glossary
- **Intrasocket reactive tissue (IRT)** — the tissue that forms or persists within an extraction socket as a reactive response; in this review's frame it is the object of the removal-versus-preservation decision. (The term overlaps with "reactive soft tissue (RST)" as used in the Liu/Deng cohort pages.)
- **Socket seal** — deliberate closure/covering of the fresh extraction socket orifice — with a graft, membrane, matrix, or, in this review's argument, the patient's own IRT — to protect the healing wound and the underlying ridge.
- **Alveolar ridge preservation (ARP)** — grafting/sealing a socket at the time of extraction to attenuate the dimensional remodelling that follows tooth loss; the workflow in which this review places its cases.
- **Debridement** — mechanical removal of tissue and debris from the socket wall, performed with hand and rotary instruments; the review's second focus after IRT biology.
- **Socket healing** — the sequenced post-extraction repair of the socket (clot → granulation tissue → woven bone fill → modelling/remodelling and soft-tissue coverage); the outcome against which IRT's positive or negative effect is judged.
