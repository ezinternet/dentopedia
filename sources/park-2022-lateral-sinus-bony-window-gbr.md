## Why Ingested

Extends the intraoral-donor theme for sinus-adjacent GBR (see [[bone-regeneration/park-2022-previously-grafted-sinus-intraoral-donor]]) by repurposing the lateral sinus bony window itself — material routinely discarded or repositioned during lateral approach maxillary sinus augmentation — as an autogenous block graft for wide post-extraction defects, thereby eliminating the need for a separate donor site.

## Three-line Summary

This case series (4 patients) describes a novel technique using the lateral sinus bony window, obtained during maxillary sinus augmentation (MSA), as an autogenous block bone graft for GBR in wide post-extraction defects adjacent to the sinus.
In all four cases, the lateral sinus bony window was successfully adapted to adjacent post-extraction defects (alone or with particulate graft) and covered with resorbable collagen membrane. At 6-month re-entry, the grafted bone was well integrated and complete bone filling of defects was observed.
Within the limitations of this case series, the lateral sinus bony window appears to be a safe and effective intraoral donor site for GBR in wide defects adjacent to sinus-augmented sites, eliminating the need for a second surgical site.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Introduces the concept of repurposing the lateral sinus bony window as an autogenous cortical block graft for GBR in wide post-extraction defects adjacent to the sinus, eliminating the need for a separate donor site.
- All 4 cases showed complete bone filling of defects at 6-month re-entry; implants placed successfully in all cases.
- Technique demonstrated safe adaptation of the window bone alone or combined with particulate xenograft (탈단백 소골 무기질, Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral, DBBM), covered with resorbable collagen membrane.
- Stable clinical and radiographic outcomes at 12–24-month follow-up across all cases.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Study Design**: Case series (4 patients)
- **Patient selection**: All patients scheduled for simultaneous lateral approach MSA and implant placement in an adjacent area with a wide post-extraction defect
- **Procedure**: During MSA, the lateral bony window was carefully preserved rather than discarded. After sinus grafting, the window was adapted to the adjacent post-extraction defect as a block autograft (with or without additional particulate bone/DBBM), then covered with a resorbable collagen membrane
- **Re-entry**: 6 months post-GBR for implant placement; bone integration assessed clinically
- **Follow-up**: 12–24 months, clinical and radiographic

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Case series (n=4): low evidence level; not generalizable
- No comparison group vs alternative GBR methods or separate donor sites
- Applicable only to patients simultaneously undergoing lateral MSA; technique requires concurrent MSA to obtain the window
- Short follow-up (12–24 months)
- Narrative description with limited quantitative bone volume data
- Larger RCTs or prospective studies needed to confirm efficacy and compare with conventional block grafting

## 6. Related Work
