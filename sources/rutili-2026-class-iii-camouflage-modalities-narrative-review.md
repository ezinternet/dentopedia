## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (SANRA-compliant, non-systematic 5-database search to Sep 2025) synthesizing 128 studies (110 case reports/series, 18 observational) on camouflage treatment for adult skeletal Class III malocclusion across all modalities — extraction protocols, labial/lingual fixed appliances, self-ligating systems, clear aligners, Carrière Motion III (CM3), MEAW, and skeletal-anchorage (TAD/miniplate) distalization.

86% of included evidence is level 4–5 (case report/small series); clear aligners achieve lower-arch distalization of only 2–3 mm with ~74% crown-level but ~47% root-level efficiency for the first molar (predominantly tipping, not bodily movement), and aligners are positioned as most valuable for high-esthetic-demand adults or periodontally compromised adults, while skeletal anchorage (buccal-shelf miniscrews, ramal plates) is favored for larger sagittal corrections; overall Class III camouflage relapse rate is ~30%, with extraction-based camouflage showing superior long-term stability over non-extraction protocols.

Clinical implication: current evidence for aligner-based Class III camouflage is exploratory (limited to case reports/small prospective studies) — appropriate for mild-to-moderate, low/normo-angle, high-compliance adults, but movement-predictability limits (torque/vertical control, root-level distalization) mean it should not be presented as equivalent to fixed-appliance or TAD-assisted alternatives for more severe or bodily-movement-heavy cases; the review's overall quality is limited by its non-systematic search and absence of risk-of-bias assessment.

## 세줄요약

SANRA 준수 narrative review(5개 데이터베이스, 2025년 9월까지 비체계적 검색)로 성인 골격성 Class III 부정교합의 camouflage 치료를 발치 protocol·순측/설측 고정장치·self-ligating·투명교정·Carrière Motion III(CM3)·MEAW·골격성 anchorage(TAD/miniplate) 전 modality에 걸쳐 종합(128편, case report/series 110편 + 관찰연구 18편).

포함 근거의 86%가 level 4–5(증례보고급)이며, 투명교정은 하악 원심이동이 2–3mm에 그치고 제1대구치 치관 수준 효율 ~74% 대비 치근 수준 ~47%(주로 tipping, 실제 체이동 아님)로 심미 요구가 높거나 치주 취약 성인에 유리한 반면, 큰 시상적 보정이 필요하면 골격성 anchorage(협측 shelf 미니스크류, ramal plate)가 우세; Class III camouflage 전체 재발률은 약 30%이며 발치 기반 camouflage가 비발치보다 장기 안정성이 우수.

임상적 의미: 투명교정 기반 Class III camouflage 근거는 아직 탐색적(증례보고/소규모 전향연구 수준)이며 경도~중등도, 저각/평균각, 순응도 높은 성인에 적합하나, 토크·수직조절·치근 수준 원심이동 등 이동 예측성 한계로 인해 중증 혹은 체이동 위주 증례에서 고정장치나 TAD 병용을 대체할 만큼의 동등성을 주장할 수 없음; 리뷰 자체도 비체계적 검색과 risk-of-bias 미평가라는 방법론적 한계를 가짐.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First comprehensive narrative synthesis (post-dating prior SRs) to explicitly incorporate **clear aligner mechanics** and **skeletal anchorage systems** alongside classic fixed-appliance/extraction camouflage strategies for adult Class III.
- Proposes a **4-parameter diagnostic framework** for predicting camouflage success: (1) skeletal discrepancy magnitude vs. dentoalveolar compensatory capacity, (2) mandibular symphysis morphology + incisor inclination limits, (3) vertical growth pattern, (4) soft-tissue profile response.
- Reports quantitative benchmarks not previously aggregated in one place: aligner lower-molar distalization efficiency (74% crown / 47% root), miniplate vs. miniscrew failure rates (7.3% vs. 16.4%), ramal-plate distalization magnitude (2.0–3.6 mm), and overall Class III camouflage relapse rate (~30%).
- Frames appliance choice along an esthetics-vs-predictability axis: fixed appliances (best torque/vertical control, least esthetic) → lingual appliances (esthetic + good torque, technically demanding) → clear aligners (most esthetic, weakest torque/vertical predictability).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Non-systematic search across 5 databases; 306 duplicates removed → 315 unique screened → 103 excluded at title/abstract → 212 full-text assessed → **128 studies included** (110 case reports/small series [level 4–5 OCEBM], 18 observational [13% retrospective level 3, 1% prospective level 2]; 2 observational studies shared the same patient sample).
- Quality appraisal used the **Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine (OCEBM) 2011** framework qualitatively; no formal risk-of-bias tool, no meta-analysis — explicitly a qualitative/narrative synthesis.
- Studies were thematically grouped by (1) extraction vs. non-extraction protocol and (2) appliance type: labial fixed, self-ligating, lingual, clear aligner, Carrière Motion III (CM3), MEAW, skeletal-anchorage-assisted.
- Included growing-patient and surgical-treatment studies were explicitly excluded (adult, non-surgical camouflage only).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Evidence quality**: 86% level 4–5 (case report/series), 13% level 3 (retrospective), 1% level 2 (prospective) — overall evidence strength "low."

**Extraction-based approaches**: premolar extraction (commonly upper 2nd + lower 1st premolar) is the traditional protocol; single lower-incisor extraction is an alternative for mild crowding with favorable Bolton ratio; molar extraction (1st/2nd/3rd) is a less common alternative, with 3rd-molar extraction associated with lower SNB and greater counterclockwise mandibular rotation vs. premolar extraction.

**Non-extraction / appliance-specific findings**:
- **Labial fixed + Class III elastics**: relies on spontaneous dentoalveolar compensation (upper incisor proclination / lower incisor retroclination); compliance-dependent; side effects include upper molar extrusion, lower molar distal tipping, increased LAFH.
- **Self-ligating**: ~1.5° less incisor proclination vs. conventional brackets; no clear systematic-review advantage besides reduced archwire-removal chair time; evidence limited to case reports.
- **Lingual appliances**: superior esthetics, effective torque control post-lower-extraction, limited transverse expansion capacity; evidence limited to case reports.
- **Clear aligners** (§3.5.4, primary axis of interest for this wiki):
  - Lower-arch distalization achievable range **2–3 mm**; efficiency ~**74% at crown level, 47% at root level** for the first molar, ~74% crown-level for the second molar; movement is predominantly **distal tipping, not bodily translation**.
  - The 1.5 mm inherent occlusal disclusion from aligner thickness aids anterior-crossbite correction.
  - Useful in hyperdivergent Class III patients for vertical control during Class III elastics, though the claimed posterior-intrusive effect is limited because masticatory loading mostly occurs when the aligner is out.
  - In hyperdivergent open-bite Class III cases, TADs in the lower arch can assist distalization while avoiding mandibular clockwise rotation.
  - Favorable for adults with periodontal disease (better plaque control, less decalcification/attachment-loss risk than fixed appliances).
  - One prospective study: aligners + Class III elastics in mild Class III modestly improved facial profile (↑upper lip projection, lower-arch distalization) without altering lower facial third.
  - Material science note: PETG (higher initial stiffness, more stress relaxation) vs. TPU (better elastic recovery, sustained force) affects distalization predictability; optimized attachment geometry helps counteract tipping toward more bodily movement.
- **Carrière Motion III (CM3)**: bilateral bars canine↔1st molar + Class III elastics to upper posterior anchorage; achieves a "Class I platform" via posterior mandibular segment distalization + counterclockwise occlusal-plane rotation; retrospective data show minimal sagittal skeletal change, ~2.5 mm LAFH increase, ~2 mm anterior maxillary-molar / posterior mandibular-molar displacement, −3.1° occlusal-plane rotation — confirming a primarily dentoalveolar mechanism.
- **MEAW**: 5-phase protocol (alignment → interference elimination → functional mandibular position → occlusal-plane reconstruction → physiologic occlusion); best for mild-moderate skeletal Class III with mesially-tipped posterior teeth; combining with maxillary TADs improves high-angle/open-bite case control.
- **Skeletal anchorage (TAD/miniplate)**: interradicular miniscrews limited to ≤2–3 mm distalization; buccal-shelf miniscrews allow more; ramal plates achieve **2.0–3.6 mm** first-molar distalization with more bodily movement and less tipping than miniscrews, but induce clockwise mandibular rotation. **Failure rates: miniplates 7.3% vs. miniscrews 16.4%** (systematic review cited). Mylohyoid ridge/submandibular fossa anatomically limit distal movement to ~3 mm; CBCT recommended pre-treatment.

**Stability/relapse**: ~37% of anterior sites in orthodontically treated adults show >2 mm alveolar bone loss (Lupi et al.); overall Class III camouflage sagittal relapse rate ~**30%** (overjet reduction to <1 mm or occlusal re-emergence); extraction-based camouflage shows statistically superior long-term stability vs. non-extraction. Aligner-based camouflage: "favorable short-term outcomes, but concerns remain regarding long-term stability of complex movements (distalization, torque control, incisor compensation)" — an explicit caution not present in either of the wiki's existing single-case reports.

**Diagnostic thresholds for camouflage-vs-surgery borderline cases**: ANB ≤ −4°, incisor–mandibular plane angle ≤ 83° associated with better surgical outcomes; Wits appraisal and Holdaway angle used to discriminate feasible-camouflage candidates.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Explicitly a **narrative, not systematic, review**: no formal risk-of-bias assessment, no meta-analysis, non-exhaustive/non-reproducible search strategy by design (though PRISMA-flow-diagram reporting was used).
- 86% of underlying evidence is case-report/small-series level — the review itself repeatedly flags that conclusions about aligner-based Class III camouflage specifically are provisional pending prospective, multicenter, randomized studies.
- Two included observational studies drew from the same patient sample (partial double-counting risk in the evidence base, acknowledged by authors).
- No aligner-specific relapse or long-term (>2–3 year) follow-up data were identified within the review — mirrors the exact gap the two wiki case reports (7-year and 17-month follow-up, respectively) only partially address given their n=1 design.

## 7. Glossary

- **Camouflage (orthodontic)**: dentoalveolar compensation approach that improves occlusal function/facial harmony via controlled tooth movement without correcting the underlying skeletal discrepancy.
- **CM3 (Carrière Motion III)**: bilateral canine-to-first-molar bar appliance + Class III elastics used to distalize the posterior mandibular segment and establish a "Class I platform."
- **MEAW**: Multiloop Edgewise Archwire technique — individualized stainless-steel archwire with multiple loops for 3D tooth-movement control, used for complex Class II/III/open-bite cases.
- **OCEBM**: Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine 2011 Levels of Evidence framework.
- **SANRA**: Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles — reporting-quality checklist used for this review's structure.
- **Wits appraisal / Holdaway angle**: cephalometric parameters used to discriminate camouflage-feasible from surgery-indicated borderline Class III cases.
- **Buccal shelf / ramal plate**: skeletal-anchorage sites in the posterior mandible used for TAD-assisted lower-arch distalization.
