## Why Ingested

Posterior implants face higher forces and poorer bone, and the `implants` folder needs a source on design adaptations for those sites. This Misch 1999 review proposes increasing surface area through diameter and thread design rather than length, and it is the design-level counterpart to the short-implant rationale in [[implants/short-narrow/misch-2005-short-dental-implants-literature-review]].

## Three-line Summary

Review (Implant Dentistry, 1999) on implant design for posterior sites, arguing that surface area, via diameter and thread design, matters more than length for stress distribution — abstract-only ingest, full text not retrieved.

The abstract cites a finite element analysis supporting length as a secondary parameter, and states that conventional diameter-based designs add only about 30% surface area while posterior forces are up to >300% higher.

The 30% and >300% figures are the author's stated comparisons, not outcome data; the abstract does not report survival or failure rates.
