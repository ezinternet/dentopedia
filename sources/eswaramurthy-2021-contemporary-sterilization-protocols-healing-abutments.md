## Why Ingested

Anchor systematic review for the healing-abutment (HA) reuse controversy batch. It supplies the top-of-hierarchy SR framing — 812→8 studies, steam autoclave as the dominant resterilization method, and the key negative finding that *no* protocol restores a 100% virgin surface — which reinforces the practice-level infection-control gate in [[infection-control/patino-marin-2025-sterilization-disinfection-dental-practices]] and complements the surgical HA-cover-screw guidance in [[infection-control/bidra-2019-should-healing-abutments-cover-screws]]. Together with the in-vitro residual-contaminant papers ([[infection-control/burioni-2024-could-dental-material-reuse-play]], [[infection-control/abreu-2024-assessment-detoxification-strategies-used-dental]]) it establishes the "reuse only after thorough decontamination + resterilization, never assume virgin surface" position.

## Three-line Summary


Systematic review (5 databases, 812→8 studies) of healing-abutment resterilization protocols for reuse: steam autoclave was the most widely used method, but no protocol achieved a 100% virgin surface;

thorough decontamination + resterilization is recommended before reuse.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Compiles and organizes the most common healing-abutment (HA) sterilization/resterilization techniques for reuse, addressing the stated gap that adequate references describing detailed HA reuse protocols are lacking.
- Establishes at systematic-review level that **steam autoclave is the most widely used** resterilization method for HAs.
- Delivers the central negative finding that **not a single protocol achieved a 100% virgin (contaminant-free) surface** — reuse can reduce but not eliminate residual contamination.
- Frames reuse as a cost-effectiveness question that must be gated by thorough surface decontamination followed by resterilization, and informed by cost and patient preference.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Systematic review with risk-of-bias assessment.
- **Databases (5)**: MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, Web of Science, Google Scholar.
- **Search window**: January 2000 – December 2020.
- **Search variables**: dental implant, healing abutment, contaminate, contamination, reuse, sterilization.
- **Inclusion criteria**: minimum sample size 10 HAs (5 per group); English language.
- **Screening yield**: 812 articles identified → **8 studies included**.
- Meta-analytic pooling / quantitative synthesis details: abstract-only (not available from the abstract).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **812 → 8** studies included after screening.
- **Steam autoclave** = most widely used form of resterilization across the included studies.
- **No protocol achieved a 100% virgin surface** on the healing abutments (residual traces of microorganisms/contaminants persisted after every evaluated protocol).
- Per-protocol efficiency percentages, per-study residual-contaminant quantities, and risk-of-bias ratings of the 8 studies: abstract-only (not reported in the abstract).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 8 studies met inclusion criteria from 812 identified — a small, heterogeneous evidence base.
- Because no protocol restores a virgin surface, the SR cannot endorse any single "safe" reuse protocol; it recommends thorough decontamination + resterilization as the pragmatic minimum.
- Reuse remains a decision balancing cost-effectiveness against residual-contamination risk, cost, and patient preference.
- Detailed limitation/future-work discussion: abstract-only (full text not retrieved).

## 7. Glossary

- **Healing abutment (HA)**: transmucosal component placed on an implant during soft-tissue healing to shape the peri-implant mucosa; manufacturer-designated single-use in most systems but commonly reused in practice.
- **Resterilization**: repeating a sterilization cycle (e.g., steam autoclave) on a previously used device to render it reusable.
- **Virgin surface**: the original, never-contaminated surface state of a device; "100% virgin surface" means complete removal of all prior biological/microbial traces.
- **Steam autoclave**: moist-heat sterilization using pressurized saturated steam; the most common HA resterilization modality in this review.
- **Decontamination**: physical/chemical removal of contaminants (protein, biofilm, debris) from a surface, a prerequisite step distinct from — and preceding — sterilization.
