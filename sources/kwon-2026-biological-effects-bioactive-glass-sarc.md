## Why Ingested

This paper directly evaluates the biological effects (cytotoxicity, odontogenic differentiation, mineralization) of bioactive glass (BAG)-modified self-adhesive resin cements (SARCs) on dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs), comparing experimental formulations (BG0–BG5) against the commercially available TheraCem and RelyX U200. It provides in-vitro evidence that BAG incorporation at 1–2 wt% enhances late odontogenic differentiation without cytotoxicity, and critically shows that TheraCem — a supposedly "bioactive" SARC — performs worst on mineralization endpoints. Relevant to understanding SARC biocompatibility in deep cavity situations.

## Three-line Summary

Experimental self-adhesive resin cements (SARCs) containing 0–5 wt% bioactive glass (BAG) were tested on human dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs) for cytotoxicity, odontogenic differentiation, and mineralization. All groups showed comparable short-term cell viability (MTT, 24–72 h), while BAG-modified groups (especially BG2–BG5) significantly upregulated late odontogenic markers (DMP1, BSP) and promoted mineral nodule formation — most effectively at 1–2 wt% BAG. TheraCem exhibited the lowest mineralization of all groups throughout the study, despite its calcium silicate composition.

## 세줄요약

실험적 자가접착형 레진 시멘트(SARC)에 0–5 wt% 바이오액티브 글라스(BAG)를 첨가해 치수줄기세포(DPSC) 세포독성·상아질 형성 분화·광물화를 평가했다. 세포생존율(MTT 24–72 h)은 전 군 간 유의차 없었으며, BAG 함유군(특히 BG2–BG5)은 후기 상아질 분화 마커(DMP1, BSP)를 유의하게 상향하고 광물화 결절 형성을 촉진했고 1–2 wt%에서 최적 효과를 보였다. TheraCem은 칼슘 실리케이트 성분임에도 ARS 기준 광물화 수치가 가장 낮았다.

## Key Contributions

1. Demonstrates that BAG incorporation into SARCs up to 5 wt% does not compromise short-term DPSC viability.
2. Shows BAG at 2–5 wt% significantly upregulates late odontogenic markers DMP1 and BSP at transcript and protein (IF) levels.
3. Identifies an optimal BAG bioactive window of 1–2 wt% for mineral nodule formation (ARS staining, day 28) — a non-linear, biphasic dose-response.
4. Critically shows TheraCem (TC) yields the lowest ARS-quantified mineralization of all tested materials throughout the experiment, calling into question its "bioactive" clinical positioning.
5. Provides mechanistic hypothesis: moderate Ca²⁺/silicate ion release at 1–2 wt% BAG creates a favorable milieu; higher concentrations may cause hyperalkalinity (>pH 9.0) that suppresses ALP activity and inhibits calcium phosphate precipitation.

## Methodology

- **Study design**: In-vitro eluate-based model (ISO 10993-12; disk 5 mm × 2 mm, light-cured 40 s, 37 °C/5% CO₂ for 24 h)
- **Materials**: BG0–BG5 (0–5 wt% BAG, experimental; Mediclus), TheraCem (TC; Bisco), RelyX U200 (RU; 3M ESPE)
- **Cells**: Human DPSCs (CefoBio, Seoul; passage 5)
- **Assays**:
  - MTT assay: 1:2 and 1:4 eluate dilutions, 24 h and 72 h
  - qRT-PCR: Early markers (DSPP, RUNX2, COL1) and late markers (DMP1, BSP) at days 3, 7, 14
  - Immunofluorescence (IF): DMP1, BSP protein on day 7; quantified by ImageJ mean gray value
  - Alizarin Red S (ARS) staining: Mineralized nodule formation at days 14, 21, 28; quantified by O.D. at 560 nm
- **Statistics**: One-way ANOVA + Tukey post-hoc; n=3 (triplicate); p<0.05 threshold

Key findings:
- **Null hypothesis 1 accepted**: No cytotoxicity differences (MTT)
- **Null hypotheses 2 & 3 rejected**: BAG groups showed enhanced differentiation/mineralization with time-dependent increase
- **BG1–BG2 optimal**: Highest ARS O.D. at day 28; BG3–BG5 show discrepancy (high mRNA but lower mineral due to excessive ion release/hyperalkalinity)
- **TheraCem lowest ARS**: Even below GM (basal medium) group at early time points; proposed mechanism: excessive alkalinity + residual monomer elution

## Limitations

1. Eluate-based in vitro model cannot replicate the dentin-pulp complex dynamics (remaining dentin thickness, tubule permeability, outward pulpal pressure)
2. 2D single-cell culture system — does not capture native pulp tissue multicellular interactions
3. No direct measurement of ion release kinetics or physical properties of BAG-modified formulations
4. No quantification of pH changes in culture medium over time to verify alkalinity hypothesis
5. No in vivo animal study to validate the 1–2 wt% optimal window for clinical pulpal healing
6. Short observation window for MTT (max 72 h) — cannot exclude later-onset toxicity

## Glossary

- **SARC**: Self-Adhesive Resin Cement — simplified luting material that eliminates separate etch/prime/bond steps
- **BAG**: Bioactive glass (originally Larry Hench, 1969) — releases Ca²⁺ and silicate ions on aqueous exposure, promoting hydroxycarbonate apatite formation
- **DPSC**: Dental Pulp Stem Cell — multipotent stem cells from dental pulp; key to tertiary dentin formation and pulp regeneration
- **DMP1**: Dentin Matrix Protein 1 — late odontogenic differentiation marker; involved in extracellular matrix organization and mineral deposition regulation
- **BSP**: Bone Sialoprotein — late odontogenic marker; stored intracellularly before secretion and proteolytic processing
- **DSPP**: Dentin Sialophosphoprotein — early-to-mid odontogenic marker; cleaved into DSP and DPP
- **RUNX2**: Runt-related transcription factor 2 — early osteogenic/odontogenic transcription factor
- **COL1**: Collagen type I alpha 1 — structural extracellular matrix component; early marker
- **ARS**: Alizarin Red S staining — quantifies mineralized nodule formation (calcium deposits); O.D. at 560 nm
- **MTT**: Methylthiazol tetrazolium assay — measures metabolic activity as proxy for cell viability
- **qRT-PCR**: Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction — gene expression quantification
- **IF staining**: Immunofluorescence staining — protein-level detection using antibodies
- **OM**: Odontogenic medium (positive differentiation control); **GM**: Growth medium (basal/negative control)
