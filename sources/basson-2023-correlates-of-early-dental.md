## Why Ingested
This large Israeli health-provider cohort (585 early-failure cases drawn from 29,378 patients, 1:1 matched controls) is one of the largest patient-level datasets on early implant failure among the pages we hold, and it independently reproduces the smoking signal already pooled in [[wiki/implants/survival/fan-2024-smoking-early-implant-failure-sr-ma]] while adding a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) exposure signal and a strong dose-response for number of implants — both framed within the risk-factor picture assembled in [[wiki/overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]].

## Three-line Summary
Historical nested case-control study (Clalit health-provider electronic medical records, Israel, 2015–2020; 585 individuals with implant failure ≤6 months post-placement, matched 1:1 to controls, drawn from 29,378 eligible patients) examining patient-level disease, drug, and dental clinical correlates of early implant failure.

Clinical factors were the significant correlates — number of implants per person (two implants OR 1.53; ≥4 implants OR 3.33; P<.01), maxillary location (OR 1.59, 95% CI 1.2–2.04), and lifetime smoking (OR 1.57, 95% CI 1.09–2.26); Crohn's disease, myocardial infarction, and osteoporosis were only borderline.

The systemic-disease and medication signals were weak or absent in this cohort (IBD 1.5% vs 0.02%, P=.059; PPI use within 6 months 40.0% vs 32.5%, P=.028 univariate), reinforcing that implant count, site, and smoking carry the actionable early-failure signal here — but abstract-only retrieval means the adjusted multivariate model and its CI structure cannot be fully audited.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: The International Journal of Oral & Maxillofacial Implants 2023;38(5):897-906
- **DOI**: 10.11607/jomi.10199
- **PMID**: 37847831
- **Institution**: Sheba Medical Center / Tel Aviv University and Clalit Health Services, Israel (state-mandated health provider)

## 2. Key Contributions
- Patient-level correlates of early implant failure (≤6 months / up to extraction) modelled in a very large Israeli electronic-medical-record cohort, not a single clinic.
- Quantifies a **dose-response by number of implants per person** (2 implants OR 1.53; ≥4 implants OR 3.33, P<.01) — an exposure axis rarely reported in early-failure studies.
- Reports a **maxillary location** effect (OR 1.59, 95% CI 1.2–2.04) and a **lifetime smoking** effect (OR 1.57, 95% CI 1.09–2.26) as the clinical correlates that reached significance.
- Fails to confirm most systemic-disease and drug associations (Crohn's, myocardial infarction, osteoporosis borderline; PPIs / IBD driven by univariate prevalence differences), narrowing what should be treated as a patient-level medication risk in this population.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Historical, nested case-control study using electronic medical records.
- **Setting/population**: Adult members of a large, state-mandated Israeli health provider (Clalit), implants placed 2015–2020; 29,378 eligible patients.
- **Cases**: Individuals with implant extraction (implant failure) occurring up to 6 months after implantation — 585 identified. Mean age 53 years [42,62]; 51.3% female, 49.7% male.
- **Controls**: Matched 1:1 to cases.
- **Variables**: demographics; clinical information (number, location, and complicated status of implants); underlying diseases (osteoporosis, diabetes, hypertension, inflammatory bowel disease [IBD], myocardial infarction [MI]); drug purchases (chemotherapy, bisphosphonates, selective serotonin inhibitors, antihypertensive medications, proton pump inhibitors [PPIs], disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs, corticosteroids, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs).
- **Analysis**: Univariate and adjusted multivariate conditional logistic regression; odds ratios (ORs).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **Abstract-only retrieval** (`full_text: false`): the adjusted multivariate model, model covariates, and CI structure cannot be audited; only the ORs quoted in the abstract are transcribed here.
- **Patient-level unit of analysis, not implant-level**: 585 failing individuals represent an unknown number of failing implants; implant-level failure rate cannot be computed from the abstract.
- **Case definition = extraction up to 6 months**, so "early failure" here is a 6-month window, broader than the pre-loading/pre-abutment definitions used in several other wiki cohorts — direct cross-study comparison of failure rates is unsafe.
- **Medication exposure measured by drug purchase records** (dispensing/purchase proxy), which may overstate actual intake; PPI and IBD signals are univariate prevalence differences at P≈.03–.06.
- Authors themselves call for **larger patient-level studies** to examine individual and combined effects of diseases, medications, and clinical factors.

## 6. Related Work
- Basson 2023 (this paper): early-failure correlates at patient level in a national Israeli EMR cohort.
- Fan 2024 ([[wiki/implants/survival/fan-2024-smoking-early-implant-failure-sr-ma]]): pooled smoking → early-failure OR 2.59; this cohort's adjusted OR 1.57 is directionally consistent but smaller.
- Yari 2023 ([[wiki/implants/survival/yari-2023-risk-factors-early-implant-failure]]): single-center multivariable cohort; smoking OR 1.84, posterior maxilla OR 2.96, immediate provisional OR 3.42 — anatomy/protocol axis that Basson's patient-level model does not include.
- Manzano 2016 ([[wiki/implants/survival/manzano-2016-risk-factors-in-early-implant]]): earlier early-failure risk-factor cohort; comparable multivariable framing.
- Chrcanovic 2016 ([[wiki/implants/survival/chrcanovic-2016-factors-influencing-early-dental-implant]]): Swedish specialist-clinic early-failure cohort; smoking patient-level OR 3.27 — effect size larger than Basson's.
- Masri 2024 ([[wiki/drug/systemic-disease/masri-2024-early-implant-failure-antihypertensive-medications]]): antihypertensive medication exposure at patient level — the same class of EMR drug-purchase signal that Basson found weak for most drugs.
- Overview ([[wiki/overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]]): synthesis of early-failure risk factors into which this page feeds.

## 7. Glossary
- **EIF**: Early implant failure — implant loss in the early post-placement window; here operationalised as extraction within 6 months.
- **IBD**: Inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's disease / ulcerative colitis).
- **MI**: Myocardial infarction.
- **PPI**: Proton pump inhibitor (e.g. omeprazole) — acid-suppressing medication.
- **SSRI**: Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor — referred to in the abstract as "selective serotonin inhibitors".
- **DMARDs**: Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs.
- **Adjusted multivariate conditional logistic regression**: Matched case-control regression that estimates ORs while conditioning on the matched sets and holding other covariates constant.
