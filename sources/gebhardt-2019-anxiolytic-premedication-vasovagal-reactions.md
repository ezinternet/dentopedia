## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-centre chart analysis of 2747 outpatients receiving low-dose intrathecal anaesthesia (IA) 2008–2017, comparing vasovagal reactions in patients with vs without anxiolytic premedication (1–2 mg midazolam IV, n=1291).

Vasovagal incidents during IA application occurred in 314 patients — 15.0% without premedication (217) vs 7.5% with premedication (97), P<0.0001; readiness-for-discharge time was not prolonged (mepivacaine P=0.5886; chloroprocaine P=0.1555) and was shorter with prilocaine (P=0.0002).

Anxiolytic premedication halves vasovagal reactions without delaying home readiness in intrathecal anaesthesia, but this is a non-dental, non-randomised cohort — applying it to the dental chair is extrapolation, not dental evidence.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantifies, in a large real-world cohort, an association between anxiolytic premedication and roughly halved vasovagal reaction incidence during application of intrathecal anaesthesia.
- Directly tests the standing concern that anxiolytic premedication in day surgery delays recovery and discharge — found no delay (and a faster readiness with prilocaine).
- Provides the concrete incidence figures (15.0% → 7.5%) that narrative reviews cite qualitatively when recommending premedication/VVS prophylaxis.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: retrospective analysis of anaesthesia records (chart review, non-randomised by treatment).
- **Setting**: single university centre, outpatient surgery under low-dose intrathecal anaesthesia (IA).
- **Period**: January 2008 – June 2017.
- **n**: 2747 patients; 1291 received anxiolytic premedication.
- **Exposure**: anxiolytic premedication, 1–2 mg midazolam intravenously.
- **Primary outcomes**: incidence of vasovagal reactions (blood pressure and/or heart rate decrease, need for cardiovascular activating medication) documented during application of IA; time from intrathecal injection of local anaesthetic until readiness for discharge.
- **Subgroup axis**: local anaesthetic used (mepivacaine, chloroprocaine, prilocaine).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only**: full text was paywalled; effect sizes, adjustment for confounders, and the actual definition thresholds for "vasovagal reaction" could not be verified. Percentages and p-values here are exactly as printed in the abstract.
- **Retrospective and non-randomised**: premedication was given at clinical discretion, so channeling bias (more anxious patients receiving premedication, or vice versa) is uncontrolled in what we can read; no multivariable adjustment is described in the abstract.
- **Population is not dental**: outpatient surgery under intrathecal (spinal) anaesthesia — a procedure with its own prominent vagal trigger (dural puncture, sympathetic block). Applying this to chairside dental care is extrapolation.
- **The claim that premedication does not delay discharge may be local-anaesthetic dependent** — prilocaine showed a *shorter* time, which the abstract reports without mechanism.
- **Metadata discrepancy**: the ingested briefing listed authors as "Kiefer P, Bussen D" and a title without "intrathecal"; the PubMed record reads "Kiefer K, Weiss C, Schmittner MD" and includes "outpatient intrathecal anaesthesia" in the title. This page follows the PubMed record (the artifact in `papers/`).

## 7. Glossary

- **Vasovagal reaction (미주신경성 반응)**: reflex bradycardia and/or hypotension triggered by pain, anxiety or mechanical stimulation; the precursor event to vasovagal syncope.
- **Intrathecal anaesthesia (IA, 척수마취)**: local anaesthetic injected into the cerebrospinal fluid (subarachnoid space) — "spinal anaesthesia".
- **Anxiolytic premedication (항불안제 전처치)**: sedative/anxiolytic given before the procedure to reduce anxiety; here 1–2 mg midazolam IV.
- **Home readiness (퇴원 준비도)**: the point at which discharge criteria are met after day-surgery anaesthesia.
- **Mepivacaine / chloroprocaine / prilocaine (메피바카인 / 클로로프로카인 / 프릴로카인)**: amide/ester local anaesthetics used for the intrathecal injection in the three subgroups.
