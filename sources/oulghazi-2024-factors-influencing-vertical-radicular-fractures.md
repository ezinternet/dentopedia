## Why Ingested
Fills the metal/cast-post failure-mechanism gap in the post-and-core category: it supplies the biomechanical "why" (ferrule, post length, elastic modulus, luting cement, occlusal fatigue) behind vertical root fracture under metal posts, extending [[cracked-tooth/patel-2022-present-status-future-directions-vertical]] (VRF aetiology in root-filled teeth) and reinforcing the ferrule-first message of [[post-and-core/naumann-2017-ferrule-first-post-second-systematic-review]]; its bench data on cast-post fracture load also qualify the fiber-over-metal conclusion of [[post-and-core/wang-2019-fiber-posts-vs-metal-posts-severely-damaged]].

## Three-line Summary
Scoping review (PubMed/Medline + Scopus 2013–2023, English/French; 1,464 records screened, 18 studies included: 8 labeled FEA, 4 laboratory experimental, 3 in vitro, 2 retrospective, 1 RCT) of factors behind sub-prosthetic vertical root fracture (VRF) in teeth restored with metallic post-and-core.
Key findings: maxillary premolars and mandibular molars are most predisposed; a 2 mm ferrule significantly raises fracture resistance (FEA); high-modulus alloys transmit nearly all stress to dentin; resin-modified glass-ionomer luting gave the highest fracture resistance in one bench study; longer posts may protect against apical VRF; the single RCT found cast-metal 1.2%/yr vs fiber 1.7%/yr annual failure with no significant difference in root fracture risk.
Evidence base is mostly FEA/in vitro with no quality appraisal and internally inconsistent inclusion criteria, so the recommendations (ferrule preservation, post length, cement choice) are hypothesis-grade rather than clinical-outcome evidence.

## 세줄요약
범위 문헌고찰 (PubMed/Medline + Scopus 2013–2023, 영어·프랑스어; 1,464편 검토 후 18편 포함: 유한요소분석 8·실험실 4·시험관 3·후향 2·RCT 1): 금속 포스트-코어 수복치의 보철 하방 수직 치근파절 (Vertical Root Fracture, VRF) 관련 인자를 정리.
주요 결과: 상악 소구치·하악 대구치가 호발; 2 mm 페룰 (ferrule)이 파절저항을 유의하게 증가 (유한요소분석); 고탄성계수 합금은 응력을 거의 전부 상아질로 전달; 한 실험에서 레진강화형 글라스아이오노머 합착이 파절저항 최고; 긴 포스트가 치근단 VRF 예방에 유리할 수 있음; 유일한 RCT에서 연간 실패율 금속 1.2% vs 파이버 1.7%, 치근파절 위험 유의차 없음.
근거 대부분이 유한요소분석·시험관 연구이고 질 평가가 없으며 선정 기준에 내적 모순이 있어, 제시된 권고(페룰 보존·포스트 길이·시멘트 선택)는 임상 결과 근거가 아닌 가설 수준이다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Consolidates, for metal/cast post-and-core only, the variables proposed to drive sub-prosthetic VRF: tooth predisposition, intracanal preparation, ferrule, post length/diameter/elastic modulus, luting cement, occlusal fatigue.
- Frames ferrule height of 2 mm as the key protective factor (FEA, Mosharaf 2023) and records a counter-result (Samran 2015: ferrule location not significant in mandibular premolars).
- Reports failure-mode contrast from bench data: cast posts risk irreparable apical fracture under high load, fiber posts typically fail by repairable coronal fracture (Rippe 2014).
- Identifies "danger zones" (mesial root of mandibular molars, buccal root of bifurcated maxillary premolars) where excess dentin removal predisposes to fracture/perforation.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Scoping review (no formal protocol registration or risk-of-bias appraisal reported).
- **Databases**: PubMed/Medline and Scopus, 2013 to June 2023, plus hand-searching; English/French only.
- **n**: 1,464 records identified; 18 studies included (Table 1: FEA/laboratory/in vitro n = 1–119 specimens or patients; Liao 2017 retrospective n=65 teeth/58 patients; Sugaya 2015 n=304 fractured teeth; Sarkis-Onofre 2020 RCT n=119 patients/183 posts).
- **Outcomes**: Fracture resistance/stress distribution (bench/FEA), fracture location/frequency (retrospective), survival/annual failure rate (RCT).
- Four-step selection: title, abstract, full text, analysis; extraction of author, date, design, n, objective, intervention.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
| Factor | Finding (study) |
|---|---|
| Predisposition | Maxillary premolars and mandibular molars most affected (Sarkis-Onofre 2020; Cohen); mandibular first molar > maxillary second premolar > maxillary central incisor (Liao 2017); narrow, flat roots at risk |
| Intracanal preparation | Manual preparation produced more cervical dentin irregularities/cracks than rotary; prosthetic preparation did not remove them (Rippe 2014); preparation type itself did not change fracture resistance |
| Ferrule | 2 mm ferrule significantly raises fracture resistance (Mosharaf 2023 FEA); ferrule eliminates the effect of post length/material on stress (Kainose 2014); ferrule location NS in mandibular premolars (Samran 2015); 10 mm post doubles resistance vs 5 mm without ferrule, but no length effect with 2 mm ferrule (Dejak 2013) |
| Post length | Longer posts: fewer apical fractures, no link with cervical fractures (Sugaya 2015); shorter metal posts raise apical stress and debonding risk (Kainose 2014); high residual coronal height permits shorter posts (Mosharaf 2023) |
| Post material | Stainless steel highest stress > zirconia > titanium > glass fiber > carbon fiber (Madfa 2014); high-modulus alloys transmit nearly all stress to dentin (Ona 2013); cast posts highest fracture load in thin-walled teeth without ferrule (Iemsaengchairat 2021); cast > fiber under oblique load (Rippe 2014); titanium > fiber for small-diameter posts (Veeraganta 2020) |
| Post diameter | Larger diameter more fracture-resistant in vitro (Veeraganta 2020) but limit to <50% of root diameter to preserve dentin |
| Luting cement | RMGIC highest fracture resistance (Torres-Sanchez 2013); zinc phosphate higher fracture resistance, composite resin more restorable failure modes (Dabbagh 2016); do not bond deeper than 5–7 mm into canal |
| RCT | Cast metal 1.2%/yr vs fiber 1.7%/yr failure at 5 yr; 23 failures, 16 posterior (mostly premolars), 10 root fractures, 5 dislodgements; no significant difference in root fracture risk (Sarkis-Onofre 2020) |
| Occlusion | Repetitive stress propagates surface cracks (fatigue fracture); bruxism a risk factor but less determinant than for coronal cracks |

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Evidence dominated by FEA and in vitro studies; only 1 RCT and 2 retrospective studies, so no clinical-outcome pooling.
- No risk-of-bias appraisal and no registered protocol; selection criteria are internally contradictory (inclusion list contains grey literature, book chapters and non-English/French publications while language is restricted).
- Table 1 labels Abdulmunem/Dabbagh 2016 (60 extracted incisors, physical loading) as FEA; the Discussion attributes a "three-year clinical follow-up" ferrule figure (6.67% vs 26.20% damage) to an FEA study (Mosharaf 2023) without a clear primary source — treat as unverified.
- Conclusions about post length and cement are drawn from single studies with conflicting results.

## 7. Glossary
- **Vertical root fracture (VRF)**: longitudinal root fracture running parallel to the long axis, usually starting in the canal and extending outward.
- **Ferrule**: circumferential band of crown (≥ 1.5–2 mm) embracing sound cervical dentin.
- **Sub-prosthetic fracture**: root fracture beneath an existing crown/post-core restoration.
- **Finite element analysis (FEA)**: computational stress modeling of tooth/restoration geometry.
- **Elastic modulus mismatch**: difference in stiffness between post material and dentin that concentrates stress at the interface.
- **Fatigue fracture**: crack propagation from surface defects under repetitive sub-failure loading.
