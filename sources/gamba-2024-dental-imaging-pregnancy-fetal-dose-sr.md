## Why Ingested

Dental-specific systematic review linking fetal doses from intraoral/panoramic/CBCT to fetal thresholds — the dental anchor for the threshold comparison in the pregnant-staff guide.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PubMed/Scopus/WoS, May 2023; RoBANS) of 7 studies — 3 retrospective cohorts of pregnant women and 4 female-phantom dosimetry studies — on fetal safety of intraoral, panoramic and CBCT imaging.

A single intraoral digital examination gives ~6–7 µGy to the fetus — about 14,000× below the ~100 mGy embryonic-death threshold and 42,000× below the 300 mGy threshold for intellectual disability/microcephaly; no childhood cancer risk has been identified below 10 mGy.

Dental imaging in pregnancy should not be restricted when clinically indicated and no evidence supports lead aprons or thyroid shields; the low-birth-weight signal (Hujoel) carried high risk of bias and was contradicted by later data.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰(PubMed·Scopus·WoS 2023-05, RoBANS) — 임신부 후향 연구 3편 + 여성 팬텀 선량 연구 4편, 총 7편으로 구내·파노라마·CBCT의 태아 안전성 평가.

디지털 구내촬영 1회 태아 선량 약 6–7 µGy — 배아 사망 역치(~100 mGy)의 약 1/14,000, 지적장애·소두증 역치(300 mGy)의 약 1/42,000. 10 mGy 미만에서 소아암 위험은 확인되지 않음.

임상적 적응증이 있으면 임신부 치과촬영을 제한하지 말아야 하며, 납 앞치마·갑상선 차폐가 필요하다는 근거는 없다. 저체중아 연관(Hujoel)은 비뚤림 위험이 높고 이후 자료로 반박됨.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Dental fetal doses are in the µGy range — 4–5 orders of magnitude below tissue-reaction thresholds.
- Background radiation over a pregnancy (~0.9 mGy) exceeds any single dental examination fetal dose by >100×.
- Lead apron reduces fetal dose 39–97% but is not needed at these absolute doses.
- Evidence gap: no study defines a safe number of dental examinations per pregnancy.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Systematic review; inclusion of cross-sectional/longitudinal studies of dental imaging in pregnant women or phantoms representing the uterus; risk of bias by RoBANS; qualitative-quantitative synthesis (no meta-analysis).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Studies included | 7 (3 retrospective, 4 phantom) |
| Single intraoral digital fetal dose | ~6–7 µGy |
| Kelaranta 2016 fetal doses | 0.009–6.9 µGy (no shield); 0.005–2.1 µGy (shield) |
| Rottke 2013 | 17.3–85.4 µGy |
| Embryonic death threshold (0–9 days) | ~100 mGy (≈14,000× dental dose) |
| Intellectual disability/microcephaly (8–25 wk) | 300 mGy (≈42,000×) |
| Childhood cancer | Not identified below 10 mGy; EAR 6%/Gy |
| Background during pregnancy | ~0.9 mGy |
| Lead apron fetal dose reduction | 39–97% |

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Only 7 heterogeneous studies; phantom studies dominate; high risk of bias in exposure measurement for retrospective studies; patient-focused, not occupational.

## 7. Glossary

- Tissue reaction (deterministic effect) — threshold effect (pregnancy loss, malformation, microcephaly, intellectual disability)
- Stochastic effect — probability scales with dose, no threshold assumed (childhood cancer)
- mGy / µGy / nGy — absorbed dose (1 mGy = 1,000 µGy = 1,000,000 nGy); for X-rays 1 mGy ≈ 1 mSv
