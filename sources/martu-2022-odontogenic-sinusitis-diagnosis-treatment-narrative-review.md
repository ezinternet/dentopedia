## Why Ingested

This 2022 narrative review provides a comprehensive ENT-focused update on odontogenic sinusitis (OS) etiology, microbiology, diagnosis (emphasizing CBCT over 2D imaging), and treatment algorithms including FESS vs Caldwell-Luc comparison. It reinforces [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/craig-2022-odontogenic-sinusitis-state-art]]'s dual-confirmation diagnostic paradigm and extends the iatrogenic etiology data (55.97% iatrogenic) cited in [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/de-corso-2022-sinonasal-complications-dental-disease]].

## Three-line Summary

A narrative review synthesizing recent data on odontogenic sinusitis (OS) epidemiology, etiology, microbiology, diagnosis, and treatment. OS accounts for 10–14% of maxillary sinusitis (up to 40%) and 75% of unilateral cases; iatrogenic causes (55.97%) now exceed infectious causes, with sinus lifts, implants, and extractions as leading triggers. CBCT is the diagnostic gold standard (detects dental pathology in 67% vs 14% on periapical radiographs); treatment requires dental source control ± antibiotics ± endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS), which offers 96.5% success with lower morbidity than Caldwell-Luc.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **Updated epidemiology**: Confirms OS represents 10–14% of maxillary sinusitis (up to 40% in some series) and 75% of unilateral maxillary sinusitis; peak incidence 40–60 years, slight female predominance (57.7%).
2. **Etiology shift quantified**: Iatrogenic causes now dominate at 55.97% (sinus lift complications, malpositioned implants, endodontic overfilling, root displacement during extraction, orthognathic surgery) vs infectious causes (periapical/periodontal/endo-perio).
3. **Microbiology update**: Polymicrobial flora—aerobic predominance in acute OS, anaerobic in chronic; fungal/biofilm role in treatment resistance discussed.
4. **Diagnostic hierarchy established**: Clinical (unilateral symptoms + fetid odor) → nasal endoscopy → imaging (CBCT > CT > panoramic > periapical); CBCT detects dental etiology in 67% of cases missed on 2D radiographs.
5. **Treatment algorithm**: Source control (dental: RCT/extraction/periodontal/foreign body removal) is mandatory; antibiotics adjunctive; FESS preferred over Caldwell-Luc (mucosa preservation, ostium patency, 96.5% success, lower morbidity).
6. **Oroantral fistula (OAF) management**: Size-timing algorithm (<3 mm observation, <5 mm primary closure, ≥5 mm flap, >10 mm palatal rotation); buccal fat pad flap superior for medium-large defects.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Narrative review of recent literature (references 1–108, spanning 1988–2022).
- **Search/Selection**: Not explicitly described as systematic; authors synthesize "recent data" across epidemiology, etiology, microbiology, clinical presentation, diagnosis (clinical, endoscopic, radiological), and treatment (medical, dental, surgical).
- **Scope**: Covers maxillary sinus anatomy, OS definition/classification, epidemiology, etiology (infectious, iatrogenic, cystic, traumatic, tumoral), microbiology, clinical diagnosis, radiological diagnosis (comparing periapical, panoramic, CT, CBCT), medical treatment (antibiotics), dental treatment (source control), surgical treatment (FESS vs Caldwell-Luc), and OAF management.
- **No formal quality assessment** of included studies (narrative review limitation).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Domain | Key Finding |
|---|---|
| **Epidemiology** | OS = 10–14% of maxillary sinusitis (up to 40%); 75% of unilateral maxillary sinusitis; age 40–60 peak; F:M ≈ 1.3:1 |
| **Etiology** | Iatrogenic 55.97% (sinus lift, implants, endo overfill, extraction complications, orthognathic); infectious (periapical 25.1%, periodontal 8.3% per Lechien 2014 n=674); cysts, trauma, tumors |
| **Anatomy** | 2nd molar roots closest to sinus floor (1.97 mm); 1st molar most frequently associated (35.6% of OS cases) |
| **Microbiology** | Acute: aerobic (Streptococcus, Haemophilus, Moraxella); Chronic: anaerobic (Prevotella, Porphyromonas, Fusobacterium); Fungi/biofilms → antibiotic resistance |
| **Clinical Dx** | Unilateral nasal obstruction/discharge + facial pain + fetid odor; anterior rhinoscopy (yellow-green pus); nasal endoscopy superior |
| **Radiological Dx** | Mucosal thickening >3 mm (or >2 mm with symptoms) = pathological; CBCT detects dental pathology in 67% vs 14% on periapical; 60% periapical pathology missed on 2D |
| **Medical Tx** | Antibiotics alone insufficient for treatable odontogenic lesions; culture-guided therapy ideal; amoxicillin/clavulanate common empiric choice |
| **Dental Tx** | Source control mandatory: RCT, extraction, periodontal therapy, foreign body removal (overfill, displaced roots, implants) |
| **Surgical Tx** | FESS: 96.5% success (De Corso 2022 n=262), mucosa preservation, ostium patency, < morbidity vs Caldwell-Luc; Caldwell-Luc reserved for foreign bodies, cysts, tumors, fungal ball, spread to adjacent sinuses/pterygomaxillary fossa |
| **OAF Closure** | <3 mm spontaneous; <5 mm primary suture; ≥5 mm flap (buccal fat pad preferred, NMA OR 15 vs alternatives); >10 mm palatal rotation; acute primary closure ~95% vs chronic ~67% |

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Narrative review design**: No systematic search strategy, no formal risk-of-bias assessment, no quantitative synthesis — evidence level inherently lower than SR/MA.
- **Heterogeneous cited studies**: Mix of retrospective series, case reports, and few prospective studies; effect sizes not pooled.
- **Microbiology section**: Based on older and newer studies without clear temporal separation; resistance patterns may not reflect current local epidemiology.
- **Treatment comparisons**: FESS vs Caldwell-Luc data from non-randomized studies; no RCT comparing FESS vs medical+dental management alone.
- **OAF algorithms**: Based on case series and expert opinion; no high-level evidence for size cutoffs.
- **No discussion of**: Cost-effectiveness, patient-reported outcomes (SNOT-22), long-term recurrence rates after FESS, or role of sinus lavage/irrigation protocols (e.g., socket lavage, trans-implant lavage).

## 7. Glossary

- **OS**: Odontogenic Sinusitis — maxillary sinus inflammation originating from dental/alveolar pathology.
- **Schneiderian membrane**: The mucosal lining of the maxillary sinus (sinus membrane).
- **FESS**: Functional Endoscopic Sinus Surgery — minimally invasive endoscopic approach to restore sinus drainage/ventilation.
- **Caldwell-Luc**: Classic open surgical approach to maxillary sinus via canine fossa; higher morbidity, mucosa-stripping.
- **OAF**: Oroantral Fistula — epithelialized communication between oral cavity and maxillary sinus.
- **CBCT**: Cone-Beam Computed Tomography — 3D imaging with lower dose than CT, high resolution for dentoalveolar-sinus complex.
- **Rhinosinusitis**: Inflammation of nasal and sinus mucosa (preferred term over "sinusitis" alone).
- **Endo-perio lesion**: Combined endodontic and periodontal infection communicating via accessory canals or apical foramen.
- **Mucosal thickening**: Radiological sign of sinus inflammation; >3 mm (or >2 mm with symptoms) considered pathological.
- **Biofilm**: Structured microbial community embedded in extracellular matrix, conferring antibiotic resistance.
