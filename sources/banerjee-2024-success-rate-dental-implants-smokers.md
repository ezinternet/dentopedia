## Why Ingested

The wiki already holds several individual SRMAs on smoking and implant outcomes — [[wiki/implants/fan-2024-smoking-early-implant-failure-sr-ma]] (OR 2.59 early failure), [[wiki/implants/mustapha-2022-smoking-dental-implants-sr-ma]], and [[wiki/implants/naseri-2020-smoking-levels-implant-failure-sr-ma]] (dose-response) — but none of them appraise the *quality of the SR/MA evidence base itself*. This 2024 umbrella review (Banerjee et al.) is a "review of reviews": it AMSTAR-2-appraises 17 SRs (10 with meta-analyses) on smoking and implant success, including Mustapha 2022 and Naseri 2020 as component reviews, and finds only 1/17 "high quality," 7 "moderate," 7 "low," 2 "critically low." It reinforces the direction and magnitude of the effect-size literature already in the wiki while adding a meta-evidence-quality lens that changes how confidently the smoking→implant-failure link should be stated in patient counseling. It directly extends and quality-grades the same evidence cluster synthesized in [[wiki/overviews/smoking-tobacco-periodontal-implant-overview]].

This umbrella review should also be read alongside a separate 2018 paper on the same clinical question from a different angle — a dedicated risk-of-bias assessment of systematic reviews on implants in smokers (Oliveira Neto et al., being ingested in parallel as `oliveira-neto-2018-risk-bias-systematic-reviews-smokers`). Where Banerjee 2024 assesses the *success-rate evidence* directly via AMSTAR-2 across a decade-plus of SRMAs (2006-2024), the 2018 paper critiques *SR methodology quality* specifically — the two are complementary meta-evidence appraisals of the same clinical question, one broader/more recent, one focused on methodological rigor at an earlier time point.

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella systematic review (17 SRs, 10 with meta-analyses, 2006-2024, PROSPERO CRD42024575748) assessing the strength of evidence for dental implant success/osseointegration in smokers vs non-smokers, quality-appraised with AMSTAR 2.

AMSTAR 2 scoring: 1/17 "high quality," 7/17 "moderate," 7/17 "low," 2/17 "critically low"; 15/17 reviews reported statistically significant associations between smoking and implant failure/marginal bone loss, with a clear dose-response signal (≥10 cigarettes/day) reported by the included Naseri 2020 review.

The overall evidence base points to lower success and higher osseointegration failure in smokers, but the authors caution that only one included SRMA reached "high" methodological quality — smoking is not an absolute contraindication, but informed consent and cessation counseling are warranted given the strength-of-evidence ceiling.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First umbrella review specifically on smoking and dental implant success/osseointegration, synthesizing 17 SRs (10 with MAs) spanning 2006-2024.
- Applies AMSTAR 2 (16-item checklist) to grade methodological quality of every included SR, producing a quality-stratified map of the evidence (1 high / 7 moderate / 7 low / 2 critically low).
- Consolidates effect estimates across reviews in one table (RR/OR range ~1.87-2.92, most 95% CIs excluding 1), giving a "meta-evidence" view rather than a single pooled effect.
- Surfaces the dose-response finding (≥10 cigarettes/day threshold, Naseri 2020) and mechanistic explanations (TNF-alpha/IL-1b elevation, nicotine's suppression of osteoblast-related gene expression, oxidative stress, RANKL-RANK-OPG pathway interference) drawn from the underlying reviews.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Umbrella review per PRISMA 2020; PROSPERO-registered (CRD42024575748).
- **Search**: PubMed/Medline, Google Scholar, ScienceDirect, Cochrane Library, EBSCOhost; Jan 2006-Aug 2024; MeSH terms combining implant/smoking/success-failure/bone-loss concepts.
- **Screening**: Two independent reviewers (UB, SR) for title/abstract; three reviewers (UB, SR, NJ) for full text; disagreements resolved by a fourth reviewer (PD).
- **Inclusion**: SRs and SR+MAs (English, 2006-Aug 2024) reporting success rate/osseointegration outcomes with qualitative or quantitative synthesis. **Exclusion**: literature/scoping reviews, cohort studies, case reports/series, non-English, pre-2006, no definite outcomes.
- **Quality appraisal**: AMSTAR 2 (16 items; yes=1, partial yes=0.5, no=0), summed to 1-4 critically low / 5-8 low / 9-12 moderate / 13-16 high; scored independently by three reviewers, consensus via a fourth.
- **Yield**: 2,563 SRMAs identified → 364 after dedup/title-abstract screening → 24 met eligibility on full text → 17 finally included (7 excluded for unavailable full text).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **17 SRs included, 10 with meta-analyses.** Primary-study counts per SR ranged 4-292; implant counts per SR ranged from 80 to 35,511 where reported.
- **AMSTAR 2 quality distribution**: Moraschini 2015 = 16 (High); Ismail 2021 and Strietzel 2007 = 12 (Moderate); Chambrone 2015, Akel 2019 = 12 (Moderate); Ghanem 2017 = 11.5 (Moderate); Mustapha 2022 = 11 (Moderate); Farronato 2022 = 9 (Moderate); Alfadda 2018 = 8, Caggiano 2022 = 8 (Low); Chrcanovic 2015 = 7.5, Naseri 2020 = 7.5 (Low); Akram 2018 = 7, Telleman 2011 = 7 (Low); Al-Qamshaa 2022 = 5 (Low); Turri 2016 = 4.5 (Critically low); Hinode 2006 = 4 (Critically low).
- **Effect sizes reported across MAs (RR/OR, 95% CI)**: Mustapha 2022 RR 2.91 (2.06-4.00); Alfadda 2018 RR 2.92 (1.76-4.83); Chrcanovic 2015 RR 2.23 (1.96-2.93); Naseri 2020 RR 2.45 (1.42-4.22); Akel 2019 RR 2.24 (1.9-2.64); Hinode 2006 RR 2.17 (1.67-2.83); Strietzel 2007 RR 2.38 (1.93-2.93); Chambrone 2015 RR 1.87 (1.35-2.58); Akram 2018 RR 2.49 (1.01-3.97, waterpipe smokers); Moraschini 2015 RR 2.53 (as reported, marginal bone loss/failure).
- **Dose-response**: Naseri 2020 found significantly elevated implant failure risk (p=0.001) at ≥10 cigarettes/day vs non-smokers, but no significant difference between 15 vs 20 cigarettes/day subgroups.
- **15 of 17 SRs reported statistically significant associations** between smoking and implant failure/MBL/peri-implant outcomes; 2 did not report p-values.
- Overall strength-of-evidence categorization (derived from AMSTAR 2 tier): "high" in 1, "mediocre/moderate" in 7, "low" in 9 of the included studies.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Conclusions are bounded by the number and quality of available SRMAs — only 1/17 achieved "high" AMSTAR 2 quality, so the umbrella review's own certainty ceiling is capped by its lowest-quality inputs.
- Included reviews pool heterogeneous primary study designs (RCTs, case-control, retrospective, prospective) with variable confounder control (smoking status/duration/quantity often self-reported and inconsistently defined across reviews).
- Seven identified reviews were excluded for unavailable full text, a potential source of selection bias.
- Limited reporting on sex-based subgroup effects; multiple SRs lacked meta-analyses, and the authors call for future SRs/MAs stratified by gender and smoking frequency.
- As an umbrella review, it inherits (rather than resolves) publication bias and confounding present in the primary literature underlying each component SR.

## 7. Glossary

- **Umbrella review (UR)**: a systematic review of systematic reviews/meta-analyses ("synthesis of reviews"), used when the primary SRMA literature on a topic has become too voluminous to synthesize narratively.
- **AMSTAR 2**: Assessment of Multiple Systematic Reviews Tool 2, a 16-item critical appraisal instrument for SRs (with or without meta-analysis), yielding a quality tier of critically low / low / moderate / high.
- **MBL**: Marginal bone loss, radiographic bone loss around the implant neck/collar, a standard implant-success proxy.
- **BIC**: Bone-to-implant contact, a histomorphometric measure of osseointegration.
- **RANKL-RANK-OPG pathway**: the receptor activator of NF-kB ligand / receptor / osteoprotegerin signaling axis governing osteoclast differentiation and activity, implicated in smoking-related bone metabolism disruption.
