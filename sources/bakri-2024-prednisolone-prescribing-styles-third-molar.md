## Why Ingested

Direct follow-up to a chairside consult on whether prednisolone and dexamethasone differ in dental use — the wiki held only a narrative-review potency table ([[drug/analgesics/satpathi-2024-corticosteroids-dentistry-review]]) and a dexamethasone-only RCT ([[drug/analgesics/tamgadge-2025-preoperative-dexamethasone-third-molar-pain-swelling-trismus]]), with no prednisolone-specific dental trial. This RCT fills that gap directly.

## Three-line Summary

Randomized split-mouth single-blind clinical trial (n=15, 18–30y, bilateral impacted mandibular third molars, Jazan University) comparing two oral prednisolone prescribing styles after surgical extraction: single dose (25 mg once) vs tapered dose (5 mg three times/day day 1, twice/day day 2, once/day day 3).

Tapered dosing produced significantly greater postoperative mouth opening and significantly less facial swelling (corner-of-mouth-to-tragus and canthus-to-mandibular-angle distances) at days 1/3/7 than the single dose; pain VAS showed no significant difference between regimens at any timepoint.

Supports a 3-day tapered oral prednisolone regimen over a single postoperative dose for reducing swelling/trismus after third-molar extraction — limited by very small sample (n=15), single-center design, and no placebo arm (both groups received a corticosteroid).

## 세줄요약

무작위 분악(split-mouth) 단일맹검 임상시험(n=15, 18–30세, 양측 매복 하악 사랑니, Jazan University): 술후 경구 프레드니솔론(Prednisolone) 처방 스타일 2종 비교 — 단일용량(25mg 1회) vs 체감용량(tapered dose, 5mg을 1일차 3회·2일차 2회·3일차 1회).

체감용량군이 단일용량군 대비 술후 개구량(day 1/3/7)이 유의하게 크고, 안면부종(구각-이주 거리 및 외안각-하악각 거리)이 유의하게 적음; 통증 VAS는 두 군 간 전 시점에서 유의차 없음.

사랑니 발치 후 부종·개구제한 감소에는 단일고용량보다 3일 체감용량 경구 프레드니솔론 요법을 지지 — 매우 작은 표본(n=15)·단일기관·위약군 부재(양쪽 다 스테로이드 투여)가 한계.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Journal of the Korean Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons 2024;50(1):27-34
- **DOI**: 10.5125/jkaoms.2024.50.1.27
- **Institution**: Jazan University College of Dentistry, Saudi Arabia

## 2. Key Contributions
- First trial (per the authors) to directly compare two *oral* prescribing styles of prednisolone (rather than steroid vs placebo, or steroid vs different steroid) for third-molar postoperative sequelae.
- Demonstrates a dose-timing effect independent of total drug identity: tapering reduces swelling/trismus more than a single bolus dose, even though both regimens use the same total-ish drug class.
- Explicit route rationale: oral prednisolone is positioned as a practical alternative to IM/IV corticosteroid dosing for clinicians without injection access (implantologists, periodontists, GPs).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Randomized, split-mouth (within-subject), single-blind (data collectors blinded to allocation) clinical trial.
- **n**: 15 patients (8F/7M), bilateral impacted mandibular third molars, Pell & Gregory Class II Position B; surgeries ≥21 days apart per side.
- **Groups**: A = prednisolone 25 mg PO single postoperative dose; B = prednisolone 5 mg PO tapered over 3 days (3×/day → 2×/day → 1×/day). Both arms also received standard ibuprofen 400 mg TID ×3 days.
- **Outcomes**: maximum mouth opening, facial swelling (COM-TRG and canthus-mandibular angle distances), pain VAS — measured preop, immediately postop, and days 1/3/7.
- **Stats**: Shapiro-Wilk normality check; unpaired t-test for continuous measures; chi-square for pain category comparison; α=0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
| Outcome | Result | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Mouth opening (day 1/3/7) | Group B (tapered) > Group A (single dose) | Significant, all 3 timepoints |
| Facial swelling, COM-TRG | Greater decrease over time in Group B | Significant |
| Canthus-mandibular angle | Greater decrease over time in Group B | Significant |
| Pain VAS | No difference between groups | Not significant, any timepoint |

Background pharmacology cited in Discussion (not this study's own data): prednisolone ≈5–6× more potent than hydrocortisone; methylprednisolone ≈5× more potent than hydrocortisone with an intermediate half-life of 18–30h; effective corticosteroid range ≈80–625 mg hydrocortisone-equivalent (16–125 mg methylprednisolone-equivalent); swelling in steroid-treated patients typically peaks on postoperative day 3, motivating a ≥3-day course.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Very small sample (n=15) — authors note findings need confirmation in a larger trial.
- No placebo/no-steroid arm — this trial isolates *dosing style*, not whether prednisolone itself works vs no drug.
- Single-center, single surgeon (reduces inter-operator variance but limits generalizability).
- Both groups also received background ibuprofen, so absolute swelling/trismus reduction can't be attributed to prednisolone alone.
- Cites conflicting prior literature on prednisolone efficacy for this indication (Bierne & Holland 1993, Jepsen & Andreasen 1991 positive; Ozok & Uslu 2007, Yilmaz & Ozturk 2004 null) — this trial does not resolve that prior conflict, only the dosing-style question within a positive-effect framing.

## 6. Related Work
- Al-Khateeb et al. (cited in Discussion, not separately ingested): RCT of dexamethasone vs methylprednisolone for third-molar surgery — found methylprednisolone superior for pain/trismus reduction.
- [[drug/analgesics/tamgadge-2025-preoperative-dexamethasone-third-molar-pain-swelling-trismus]]: parallel single-dose dexamethasone-vs-placebo RCT for the same procedure — different drug, different question (does a corticosteroid help at all, vs which oral prednisolone schedule helps more).
- [[drug/analgesics/satpathi-2024-corticosteroids-dentistry-review]]: narrative review's potency table lists prednisolone at 4× hydrocortisone; this paper's Discussion cites 5–6× — a minor reference-figure discrepancy between two secondary sources, not a primary-data conflict.

## 7. Glossary
- **Split-mouth design**: within-subject trial design comparing two interventions on the left vs right side of the same patient, controlling for inter-patient variability.
- **COM-TRG**: corner-of-mouth-to-tragus distance, a facial-swelling proxy measurement.
- **Tapered dose**: a corticosteroid regimen that decreases in frequency/amount over successive days rather than stopping abruptly.
- **Pell and Gregory classification**: system describing impacted third-molar depth/angulation relative to the mandibular ramus and adjacent tooth.
