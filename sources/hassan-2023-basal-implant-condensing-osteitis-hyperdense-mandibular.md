## Why Ingested

Case report demonstrating single-visit extraction and immediate basal implant placement at a mandibular molar site occupied by condensing osteitis (CO). The hyperdense sclerotic lesion was deliberately used as an osseofixation anchor rather than removed. Claims to be the first published case of KOC Plus basal implant placed directly into CO. Provides a practical case-level instance that complements the narrative review by [[implants/survival/taghsimi-2022-implant-hyperdense-jaw-lesions-narrative-review]] and adds immediate-placement technique detail relevant to the condensing osteitis section of [[implants/survival/kim-2026-dental-implant-osteoporosis-osteosclerosis]].

## Three-line Summary

An 18-year-old male with a 13.82 × 9.91 mm condensing osteitis lesion at mandibular right first molar (tooth 46) underwent same-day atraumatic extraction and immediate KOC Plus basal implant placement, using the hyperdense sclerotic bone as an osseofixation substrate. Conventional implant placement was contraindicated due to buccal plate loss and low crestal bone density; guided bone regeneration would have delayed rehabilitation 5–6 months. At the reported follow-up, no mobility was observed and the fixed prosthesis met functional requirements, supporting basal implantology as a viable single-visit rehabilitation pathway when crestal bone is compromised.

## 세줄요약

하악 우측 제1대구치(46번) 부위에 13.82 × 9.91 mm 크기의 골성화 골염(condensing osteitis, CO) 병소가 존재하는 18세 남성 환자에서, 협측골판 소실 및 낮은 치조골 밀도로 인해 일반 임플란트 즉시식립이 불가하여 KOC Plus 기저 임플란트를 CO 병소를 골고정(osseofixation) 기질로 활용해 동일 내원일에 식립하였다. 골유도재생술(GBR)을 시행할 경우 5~6개월의 대기가 필요하나, 기저 임플란트는 즉시 보철 장착을 가능하게 하여 치료 기간을 단축하였다. 추적관찰에서 동요도 없이 고정성 보철이 기능적 요건을 충족하였으며, 이는 CO를 포함한 과밀도 병소 부위에서의 기저 임플란트 식립 가능성을 지지하는 최초의 증례 보고이다.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **First reported case** of KOC Plus basal implant placed directly into a condensing osteitis lesion, exploiting the hyperdense bone as an osseofixation substrate.
2. Demonstrates a single-visit extraction-plus-immediate-implant workflow that avoids the 5–6-month GBR waiting period required for conventional implants when buccal plate is deficient.
3. Provides surgical detail (DOS1/DOS2/DOS3 sequential drilling, 80 N/cm insertion torque, abutment neck bending for angulation correction) applicable to KOC Plus placement in sclerotic bone.
4. Reinforces the basal implantology principle that osseofixation in cortical bone can substitute for crestal osseointegration in cases of ridge atrophy or buccal plate loss.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Patient**: 18-year-old systemically healthy male, secondary caries with prior RCT on tooth 46, no tenderness on percussion, no mobility.
- **Imaging**: CBCT — well-defined radiopaque mass, 13.82 × 9.91 mm, at root apices of 46; loss of lamina dura; partial buccal plate loss exposing mesial root.
- **Differential diagnoses considered**: Condensing osteitis, osteosclerosis, cementoblastoma.
- **Final diagnosis**: CO with asymptomatic persistent apical periodontitis.
- **Surgical steps**: Sulcular incision → full-thickness mucoperiosteal flap → atraumatic extraction with periotomes → single pilot osteotomy (pathfinder drill) → sequential DOS1/DOS2/DOS3 drilling → implant bed preparation → KOC Plus insertion → torque 80 N/cm → abutment angulation adjustment by neck bending → immediate postoperative CBCT verification → fixed prosthesis delivery.
- **Prophylaxis**: Amoxicillin 500 mg, 1 hour preoperatively.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single case report; no control group, no quantitative follow-up data (ISQ, BIC, radiographic bone loss).
- Follow-up duration not explicitly stated — outcome durability unknown.
- Device-specific: findings apply to KOC Plus (bicortical screw design); may not generalize to other basal implant systems.
- Authors acknowledge large-scale RCT evidence for basal implants is still lacking.
- Future: prospective case series with defined follow-up periods and standardized outcome metrics (ISQ, marginal bone level) in CO sites are needed.

## 6. Related Work

- Garg et al. — survival comparison of 34 endosseous IL vs 18 basal IL implants; both reported satisfactory results (cited in text, no explicit citation number given).
- Ghalaut et al. — complete mouth rehabilitation with 18 single-piece basal implants; immediate loading into extraction sockets and healed bone.
- Anuradha et al. — immediately loaded basal implants in compromised bone; 100% survival, no infection, no mobility at 6 months.
- Ahmad et al. — 14 basal implants in cleidocranial dysplasia patient; immediately loaded 3 days post-placement.
- Awadalkreem et al. — basal implants for gunshot injury rehabilitation; good peri-implant health at 5 years.

## 7. Glossary

- **Basal implant (기저 임플란트)**: Single-piece implant designed to engage cortical (basal) bone rather than cancellous crestal bone; also called corticobasal implant.
- **KOC Plus**: King of Compression Plus — a specific basal implant design by Simpladent featuring compression and bicortical apical screws for osseofixation.
- **Osseofixation (골고정)**: Mechanical fixation in cortical bone without requiring biologic osseointegration at the crestal level; the primary stability mechanism in basal implantology.
- **Condensing osteitis (골성화 골염, CO)**: Reactive bone sclerosis at a tooth apex triggered by low-grade chronic pulpal inflammation; radiographically a well-defined dense radiopaque mass.
- **DOS drills**: Sequential drilling system (DOS1, DOS2, DOS3) used for KOC Plus implant bed preparation.
- **Bicortical screw**: The apical component of KOC Plus that engages a second cortex for enhanced primary stability.
