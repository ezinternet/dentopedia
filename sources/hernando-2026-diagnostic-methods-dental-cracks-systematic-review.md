## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (12 studies) evaluating the diagnostic accuracy of various methods for detecting dental cracks; optical coherence tomography (OCT) demonstrated the highest diagnostic accuracy among the technologies assessed.

Single-method approaches showed inferior performance compared to multimodal strategies combining clinical examination, bite testing, transillumination, dye penetration, and imaging — reinforcing existing frameworks for crack detection protocols.

The review concludes that a standardized multimodal approach incorporating emerging technologies such as OCT should guide future clinical protocols for dental crack diagnosis.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First systematic review aggregating 12 studies on diagnostic accuracy of multiple crack detection methods
- OCT identified as highest-accuracy single technology for dental crack detection
- Establishes evidence base for multimodal approach superiority over single-method diagnosis
- Highlights absence of standardized diagnostic protocols as a major clinical gap
- Provides framework for future development of standardized crack diagnosis guidelines

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Design: Systematic review (12 studies included)
- Databases: Not specified in abstract; standard systematic review methodology assumed
- PICO: Patients with dental cracks; comparisons of diagnostic methods; accuracy outcomes
- Methods evaluated: OCT, clinical visual exam, bite test, transillumination, dye penetration, CBCT, confocal microscopy, and other modalities
- Outcome measures: Diagnostic accuracy (sensitivity, specificity, AUC where reported)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- OCT demonstrated highest diagnostic accuracy among individual methods
- Multimodal approach (combination strategies) outperformed single-method diagnosis
- Specific accuracy metrics not detailed in available abstract
- Standardized protocols identified as lacking across included studies

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Abstract-only: specific accuracy values per method not extractable
- Heterogeneity likely high across 12 studies (different crack types, reference standards)
- Future need: standardized protocols enabling cross-study accuracy comparison
- OCT remains expensive and not universally available in general practice

## 6. Related Work

## 7. Glossary
- OCT: Optical Coherence Tomography — light-based cross-sectional imaging with micron resolution
- Multimodal approach: combination of ≥2 diagnostic methods for crack detection
- Diagnostic accuracy: composite of sensitivity + specificity performance
