## Why Ingested

치과 응급상황 문헌은 거의 전부 **분자(numerator)만** 보고한다 — "치과의사의 46.3%가 지난 1년간 VVS를 경험"([[drug/systemic-disease/smereka-2019-preparedness-and-attitudes-towards-medical]]) 같은 설문 기반 빈도이거나, 개별 증례([[drug/systemic-disease/kawauchi-2025-vasovagal-syncope-with-sinus-arrest]])다. 이 논문은 위키에 없던 **분모(denominator)** — 8년간 1,146,929건의 치과 외래 방문 — 를 제공해, [[drug/systemic-disease/li-2026-syncope-in-dentistry-risk-factors]]가 정성적으로만 서술한 "VVS가 압도적 1위"를 방문당 발생률로 수치화한다.

## Three-line Summary

Single-center retrospective observational study of all medical emergencies requiring an emergency call and i.v. line at the dental outpatient department of Okayama University Hospital, Japan, over 8 years (April 2012–March 2019), with a denominator of 1,146,929 dental outpatient visits.
42 emergencies occurred (0.0037% of visits; derived 3.66 per 100,000 visits, ~1 per 27,300), dominated by vasovagal syncope (26/42, 61.9%), followed by dehydration (4, 9.5%), hypoglycemia (4, 9.5%), seizure (3, 7.1%) and unexpected bleeding (3, 7.1%); 19 (45.2%) occurred during extraction.
90.5% recovered and went home the same day while 9.5% were hospitalised, but the case definition (emergency call + i.v. line) means this is a floor estimate of all syncopal episodes, not a true incidence of vasovagal syncope.

## 세줄요약

일본 오카야마대학병원 치과 외래의 8년(2012-04–2019-03) 단일기관 후향적 관찰연구 — 응급 호출과 정맥로 확보가 필요했던 증례 42건, 분모는 치과 외래 방문 1,146,929건.
발생률 0.0037% (방문당 10만 건당 3.66건, 약 27,300방문당 1건)이며 미주신경성 실신 (Vasovagal Syncope, VVS)이 26/42 (61.9%)로 1위, 탈수 4건 (9.5%), 저혈당 4건 (9.5%), 발작 3건 (7.1%), 예기치 않은 출혈 3건 (7.1%) 순; 발치 중 발생이 19건 (45.2%).
90.5%는 당일 회복·귀가, 9.5%는 입원했으나 "응급 호출 + 정맥로"라는 증례 정의 때문에 이는 전체 실신의 발생률이 아니라 **하한 추정치(floor estimate)**다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Supplies the **denominator** the dental-emergency literature lacks: 1,146,929 dental outpatient visits over 8 years, giving a per-visit incidence (0.0037%) rather than a per-dentist survey frequency.
- Types the numerator by **emergency category with counts**: VVS 26/42 (61.9%), dehydration 4 (9.5%), hypoglycemia 4 (9.5%), seizure 3 (7.1%), unexpected bleeding 3 (7.1%), other 2 (4.8%).
- Adds **timing** as a third axis alongside type and treatment: before treatment 17 (40.5%), during 12 (28.6%), after 13 (31.0%) — i.e. a third of events happen after the procedure ends.
- Documents the **rescue pathway and disposition**: 14 cases (33.3%) needed emergency-department support; 38 (90.5%) recovered same-day, 4 (9.5%) hospitalised; no cardiac arrest in the cohort.
- Quantifies the **pre-treatment group's risk profile**: older (median 59 vs 26 years, P = 0.030) and far more often comorbid (88.2% vs 15.4%, P < 0.001) than the post-treatment group.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Single-center, retrospective, observational chart review (electronic medical records)
- **Period**: April 1, 2012 – March 31, 2019 (8 years)
- **Setting**: Dental outpatient department of Okayama University Hospital — an "Advanced Treatment Hospital" (50 departments, 853 beds, of which 12 departments / 20 beds are dental); ~165,000 dental patients per year, 150 dental units in 2019
- **Denominator**: 1,146,929 dental outpatient visits (the paper's wording is "1,146,929 patients visited"; this is a visit count, not unique individuals)
- **Case definition (critical)**: patients requiring medical intervention that necessitated an **emergency medical call and establishment of an i.v. line**; cases were extracted from records where an i.v. line was used, then reviewed by the authors
- **Variables**: age, sex, underlying disease, emergency type, timing of onset, type of dental treatment, practitioners involved, medications, disposition
- **Timing groups**: "before" = from scheduled appointment to start of treatment; "during" = start to end of treatment; "after" = end of treatment to home/discharge
- **Statistics**: medians with IQR (continuous), n (%) (categorical); Kruskal–Wallis for continuous, Fisher's exact for categorical; P < 0.05; SPSS 14.0 + Excel

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Single university hospital** — a large "Advanced Treatment Hospital" whose dental population plausibly carries more severe systemic disease than a community clinic; the authors state the results may not generalise to smaller outpatient clinics. Direction of bias is upward for both the comorbidity mix and possibly the event severity mix.
- **The case definition is the real limit**: an event counts only if it triggered an **emergency call plus i.v. line establishment**. Simple syncope managed with supine positioning and observation — by far the most common real-world scenario — is invisible here. **0.0037% is therefore a floor estimate for emergencies-as-defined, not the incidence of vasovagal syncope, and absolutely not the incidence of all adverse events.**
- No denominator breakdown by treatment type, so the extraction-specific risk (19/42 of events) cannot be converted into a per-extraction rate.
- Very small numerator (42) means the type-specific percentages (e.g. 7.1% = 3 cases) and the timing-group comparisons are fragile; several table cells have expected counts below 5 for Fisher's exact test.
- The 42 cases are listed individually with age/sex/comorbidity in Table 1, which is a re-identification risk in a single-center series (handled here by reporting aggregates only).
- The `after` group definition (end of treatment → home/discharge) mixes true post-procedural events with events during recovery/observation, and the "before" group includes pre-appointment waiting-room events; timing is therefore administrative, not physiological.
- Retrospective record review: emergency type was assigned by the treating team, so misclassification between "vasovagal syncope", "dehydration" and "other" is possible — the dehydration label in particular is a clinical attribution without a measured fluid deficit.

## 6. Related Work

- **Li 2026** ([[drug/systemic-disease/li-2026-syncope-in-dentistry-risk-factors]]): narrative review of VVS etiology, risk factors, prevention and P–CAB–D management. It states VVS accounts for >60% of dental-office emergencies; this paper supplies the quantitative denominator behind that statement, and its 61.9% is the same order of magnitude.
- **Smereka 2019** ([[drug/systemic-disease/smereka-2019-preparedness-and-attitudes-towards-medical]]): 419 Polish dentists surveyed; 46.30% encountered VVS in 12 months and 80.66% made zero EMS calls. Survey-based "dentist-years" denominators vs this paper's "visits" denominator.
- **Choufani 2025** ([[drug/systemic-disease/choufani-2025-lebanese-dentists-preparedness-to-deal]]): Lebanese dentists' self-reported preparedness — the training-side counterpart to this incidence-side measurement.
- **Kawauchi 2025** ([[drug/systemic-disease/kawauchi-2025-vasovagal-syncope-with-sinus-arrest]]): a single severe VVS case with sinus arrest — the tail of the severity distribution that this cohort's 9.5% hospitalisation rate summarises.
- **Bhagat 2023** ([[drug/systemic-disease/bhagat-2023-leg-raise-leg-fold-maneuver-syncope-extraction]]): physical counterpressure manoeuvre for extraction-related syncope; this paper's 45.2% extraction share is the population in which such a manoeuvre would be applied.
- Prior survey literature cited by the paper: 19–44% of dentists report ≥1 emergency per year; ~90% mild, 8% severe/life-threatening; VVS encountered by 37.6–66.9% of dentists, hypoglycemia 5.6–37.1%, anaphylactic shock 0.4–2.1%, cardiac arrest 0.2–6.1%. Broadbent et al. attributed syncope to anxiety about general treatment (35.7%), pain/fear of local anesthesia (31.4%), anxiety about oral surgery such as extraction (18.6%), and sight of blood (5.7%).

## 7. Glossary

- **Vasovagal syncope (VVS, 미주신경성 실신)**: neurally mediated transient loss of consciousness from reflex vasodilation/bradycardia; the dominant dental-office emergency.
- **Emergency call system (응급 호출 체계)**: in-hospital paging that summons emergency physicians and staff to the dental chairside; here part of the case definition.
- **i.v. line (정맥로)**: peripheral intravenous access; the paper's primary first step in emergency cases and the practical filter that defines the numerator.
- **Floor estimate (하한 추정치)**: an incidence that can only undercount, because cases below the case-definition threshold are never captured.
- **Disposition (전원·귀가)**: final outcome — home vs hospital admission (here 90.5% vs 9.5%).
- **Trendelenburg position (트렌델렌버그 체위)**: head-down tilt; the paper explicitly does not recommend it for dental-chair syncope (regurgitation/airway risk, cerebral swelling, fall risk).
- **Kruskal–Wallis test**: non-parametric comparison of continuous variables across the three timing groups.
- **Fisher's exact test**: exact test for 2×k categorical tables, used because of small expected counts.
