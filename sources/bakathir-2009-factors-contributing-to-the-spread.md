## Three-line Summary

Prospective multicentre pilot study (West of Scotland, n=25 patients with severe spreading odontogenic infection) of social, systemic and microbial factors in the pathogenesis of spreading infection.

Most infections were tooth- or root-related; 80% were tobacco smokers, 72% came from deprived areas, 5 were intravenous drug users and 4 chronic alcohol users; raised CRP at admission indicated severity; inappropriate prior antibiotics without surgical drainage were common; cultures were dominated by strict anaerobes (anaerobic streptococci, Prevotella, Fusobacterium).

Small pilot with no comparison group, so the factors are associations only, and the abstract leaves open whether bacterial factors turn a localized abscess into a spreading infection.

## 1. Document Information

- Journal: Sultan Qaboos University Medical Journal 9(3), published 2009-12-19 (PMC3074787)
- Design: prospective multicentre pilot study, 6-month recruitment
- Full text: PMC record exists, body empty in retrieval; abstract used

## 2. Key Contributions

- Documents social and lifestyle associations (smoking, deprivation) with spreading odontogenic infection.
- Reports anaerobe-dominant microbiology and the prior-antibiotics-without-drainage pattern.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- 25 patients with severe odontogenic infection recruited over six months; admission temperature, hematology and biochemistry; demographics, social and medical histories; microbiology samples; management recorded.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Smokers 80%; deprived areas 72%; 5 IV drug users; 4 chronic alcohol abuse; 6 with underlying systemic disorders; 2 at high risk of malnutrition.
- Raised C-reactive protein at admission useful as severity indicator.
- Inappropriate prior antibiotic treatment without surgical drainage was common.
- Microbiology: predominance of strict anaerobes — anaerobic streptococci, Prevotella, Fusobacterium.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- n=25, no comparison group (association only), abstract only.
- The authors suggest molecular characterization of the flora to see whether bacterial factors convert a localized dentoalveolar abscess into a serious spreading infection, and further study of malnutrition.
