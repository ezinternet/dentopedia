## One-line Summary
SR of 10 trials (5 RCT + 5 CCT, n=1,331): continuing oral anticoagulation (VKA or NOAC) did not increase bleeding in most dental procedures; heparin bridging increased bleeding; recommend continuation with local hemostats.

## 한줄요약
SR(10편, n=1,331): 대부분의 치과 시술에서 항응고제(VKA·NOAC) 유지가 출혈을 증가시키지 않으며, 헤파린 브리징은 출혈을 오히려 증가시켜 권고하지 않음.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Provides procedure-specific recommendations (extraction, implant, periodontal, biopsy, frenectomy, apicectomy)
- Synthesizes evidence that heparin bridging is associated with higher bleeding without reducing thromboembolic events
- NOAC (dabigatran, rivaroxaban) data: comparable to VKA safety profile for dental procedures
- Recommends continuation for vast majority of dental procedures with local hemostatic agents
- Identifies gap: limited RCT data specifically for NOAC in invasive dental procedures

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review
- **Databases**: PubMed (1996–2016)
- **Included**: 10 clinical trials (5 RCT, 5 CCT)
- **n total**: 1,331 participants
- **Comparison**: Continued anticoagulation vs. interrupted vs. heparin bridging
- **Local hemostats evaluated**: tranexamic acid mouthwash, oxidized cellulose, gelatin sponge, fibrin adhesives, suturing

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Continuing anticoagulation: did NOT increase bleeding in most trials
- Thromboembolic events: NONE reported in any study after interruption (follow-up too short/inconsistent to assess)
- Thromboembolic risk when stopping: 0.7–1.1% reported in broader literature
- Heparin bridging: associated with INCREASED bleeding incidence
- INR-therapeutic VKA patients: safe to continue for minor procedures
- NOAC: 4-year cross-sectional data — no significant bleeding when continuing for dental procedures

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Search limited to PubMed; no EMBASE/Cochrane
- Heterogeneous outcomes across studies; no meta-analysis possible
- Most studies focus on extraction; implant/periodontal data sparse
- Follow-up too short to capture thromboembolic events
- Limited NOAC-specific data (especially apixaban, edoxaban)

## 6. Related Work
- Madrid 2009 (oral implant SR): same conclusion, OAT continuation safe
- Moldovan 2023 (SR broader): updated guidance
- Lee 2024 (cohort n=537): confirms VKI and AF as highest risk factors
- Xiang 2026 (SR+MA DOAC): DOAC continuation for minimal-bleeding procedures

## 7. Glossary
- **VKA**: Vitamin K antagonist (warfarin)
- **NOAC**: Novel/direct oral anticoagulant
- **CCT**: Controlled clinical trial (non-randomized)
- **INR**: International Normalized Ratio
- **Bridging**: Transition from oral to parenteral (heparin) anticoagulation periprocedurally
