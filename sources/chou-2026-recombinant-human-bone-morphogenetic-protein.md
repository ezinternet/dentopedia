## Why Ingested

The wiki's donor-site axis ([[overviews/autogenous-bone-graft-donor-site-selection-overview]]) ranks *where* to harvest bone and treats iliac crest (ICBG) as the durable extraoral fallback, while the only rhBMP-2 synthesis we hold ([[overviews/extraction-socket-biologic-agents-prf-collagen-rhbmp2-healing-overview]]) is confined to adult extraction sockets. This 2026 systematic review is the first paper we hold that tests whether the harvest can be skipped altogether — rhBMP-2-infused allograft versus ICBG in pediatric craniofacial reconstruction — so it fills the gap for the one population where a second (iliac crest) surgical site is the default.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (21 included studies — 6 RCTs, 2 prospective, 10 retrospective, 3 case reports; PubMed, Ovid and Web of Science searched 2008–2023) comparing rhBMP-2-infused allograft against iliac crest bone graft (ICBG) in pediatric craniofacial reconstruction.

For alveolar cleft repair, 4 of 12 studies favored rhBMP-2 for bone formation, 1 favored ICBG and 7 found no difference; operative time was shorter with rhBMP-2 in all 5 comparative studies and hospitalization was shorter in 7 of 8, while 5 of 12 complication studies favored ICBG and cost was mixed across 4 studies.

rhBMP-2 appears comparable, if not favorable, to ICBG for pediatric alveolar cleft repair while shortening operative time and hospital stay, but 18 of 21 studies address this single procedure, the search ends in 2023, and long-term pediatric efficacy and safety remain unconfirmed.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review we hold asking whether rhBMP-2-infused allograft can *replace* (not merely supplement) autologous bone graft in pediatric craniofacial reconstruction.
- Head-to-head framing against the pediatric standard of care — iliac crest bone graft (ICBG) — across five outcome domains: bone formation efficacy, complications, operative time, length of hospitalization and financial burden.
- Separates the dominant procedure (alveolar cleft repair, 18 of 21 studies) from the sparse evidence on calvarial defect (2) and mandibular continuity defect (1) reconstruction.
- Names the adverse-event trade-off explicitly: facial edema led the rhBMP-2 complications, donor-site morbidity led the ICBG complications.
- States the generalizability limit in the authors' own conclusion: long-term follow-up is needed, particularly for craniofacial procedures other than alveolar cleft repair.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Databases**: PubMed, Ovid, Web of Science — search window 2008–2023.
- **Eligibility**: randomized controlled trials, prospective or retrospective case series, and case reports on rhBMP-2 in pediatric craniofacial reconstruction. Reviews, animal studies and basic science studies were excluded.
- **Screening**: 211 articles screened → 21 included (6 RCTs, 2 prospective, 10 retrospective, 3 case reports).
- **Synthesis method**: narrative, direction-of-effect vote counting per outcome domain (the abstract reports no pooled effect estimate, no heterogeneity statistic and no meta-analysis). Search string, duplicate handling, and risk-of-bias appraisal are not visible in the abstract.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Full text not retrieved** — this page is built from the PubMed abstract only. The complete search strategy, risk-of-bias assessment, per-study data tables and any GRADE rating are not visible here.
- **Search window closes in 2023** although the paper appears in 2026 — literature published after 2023 was not eligible.
- **No meta-analysis** — results are unweighted votes on the direction of effect, so a large and a small trial each count as one; no heterogeneity estimate, no pooled effect, no certainty rating.
- **Design heterogeneity** — the 21 studies span RCTs down to 3 case reports; the case-report arm cannot contribute comparative inference.
- **Procedure concentration** — 18 of 21 studies are alveolar cleft repair, so the conclusion cannot be carried to calvarial or mandibular continuity reconstruction (the authors say this explicitly).
- **No long-term data** — efficacy and safety confirmation beyond the reported follow-up horizons is pending; growth-related safety and ectopic bone are not addressed in the abstract.
- **Cost signal unresolved** — mixed across 4 studies, so the economic argument for avoiding the iliac crest harvest is not established.
- **Future work named by the authors**: long-term follow-up to confirm efficacy and safety, especially for other craniofacial reconstruction procedures.

## 7. Glossary

- **rhBMP-2** — Recombinant Human Bone Morphogenetic Protein-2; osteoinductive growth factor delivered on a graft carrier.
- **ICBG** — Iliac Crest Bone Graft; autologous cortical/cancellous block harvested from the anterior iliac crest, the conventional graft for pediatric alveolar cleft repair.
- **Alveolar cleft repair** — surgical closure of the bony cleft left by a cleft lip/palate alveolar defect, with graft placement into the cleft.
- **Osteoinduction** — induction of undifferentiated mesenchymal cells toward osteoblastic differentiation.
- **Donor-site morbidity** — complications attributable to the harvest wound (pain, seroma, gait or sensory disturbance) rather than to the graft.
