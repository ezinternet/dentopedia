## Why Ingested

Most flap-advancement papers this wiki holds are single-arm case series (e.g. laser PRI) or a narrative/SR taxonomy of techniques ([[suture-wound-closure/plonka-2017-flap-designs-flap-advancement-implant-therapy]]) rather than head-to-head trial data. Ogata 2013 is one of the few papers in the collection with an actual randomized, prospective comparative trial pitting one named flap-advancement technique directly against another (double-flap incision, DFI, vs the conventional periosteal releasing incision, PRI) on both advancement amount and morbidity — the exact RCT-level evidence Plonka's later SR narratively synthesizes but does not itself generate.

## Three-line Summary

Prospective, randomized clinical trial (n=23 Seibert Class III vertical/horizontal ridge defects; Tufts University School of Dental Medicine, Boston) comparing double-flap incision (DFI) vs conventional periosteal releasing incision (PRI) for flap advancement during vertical ridge augmentation.

DFI achieved significantly greater flap advancement than PRI (9.64 ± 0.92 mm vs 7.13 ± 1.45 mm, P < .001) and lower pain/swelling/bleeding VAS scores (P = .019 / .010 / .061), with fewer postoperative complications (DFI 1 site vs PRI 5 sites, P < .082, not statistically significant).

DFI facilitated advancement and reduced morbidity relative to PRI, positioning it as a potential alternative technique; abstract-only ingestion (no PMC, full text not retrieved) — exact surgical steps of DFI, statistical methods, and per-defect data are unavailable beyond the abstract.

## 세줄요약

전향적 무작위 임상시험 (n=23, Seibert Class III 수직·수평 치조제 결손; 미국 터프츠 치과대학 치주과)으로, 이중피판절개 (Double-Flap Incision, DFI) vs 기존 골막이완절개 (Periosteal Releasing Incision, PRI)의 수직골증대 시 피판 전진 효과를 비교.

DFI군이 PRI군보다 피판 전진량이 유의하게 컸고(9.64±0.92mm vs 7.13±1.45mm, P<.001), 통증·부종·출혈 VAS 점수도 더 낮았으며(P=.019/.010/.061), 합병증 발생도 DFI 1부위 vs PRI 5부위로 적었으나(P<.082) 통계적 유의성은 없음.

DFI가 PRI 대비 전진량 증가·이환율 감소 효과를 보여 PRI의 대안 기법이 될 잠재력이 있으나, 초록만 확보(전문 미확보, PMC 없음)되어 DFI의 구체적 술식 단계·통계 방법·부위별 세부 데이터는 확인 불가.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Provides **head-to-head RCT-level evidence** comparing two named flap-advancement techniques (DFI vs PRI) on the same outcome set (advancement amount, complication incidence, patient-reported morbidity) — a comparative-trial design that most other flap-advancement papers in this wiki (single-arm case series, SR taxonomy) lack.
- Quantifies a **~2.5 mm advancement advantage** for DFI over conventional PRI (9.64 mm vs 7.13 mm) in the same Seibert Class III defect population, directly measured with a UNC-15 periodontal probe.
- Demonstrates a **morbidity trade-off in DFI's favor**: lower pain, swelling, and bleeding VAS scores, and a directionally (though not significantly) lower complication count.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Prospective, randomized clinical trial; incision technique (DFI vs PRI) randomly assigned per defect site.
- **Population**: 23 vertical and horizontal ridge defects, Seibert Class III (combined horizontal + vertical ridge deficiency), undergoing vertical ridge augmentation.
- **Intervention arms**: double-flap incision (DFI) vs conventional periosteal releasing incision (PRI) for flap advancement.
- **Primary outcome**: amount of flap advancement, measured with a UNC-15 probe as the difference between the initial elevated flap position and the final advanced flap position.
- **Secondary outcomes**: incidence of postsurgical complications (premature membrane exposure, infection, paresthesia, continuous discomfort) at follow-up visits; patient discomfort via visual analog scale (VAS) for pain, swelling, and bleeding.
- **Abstract-only caveat**: number of follow-up visits, exact VAS administration timing, and statistical test methodology are not stated in the abstract and are not reconstructed here.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | DFI | PRI | P value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flap advancement (mm) | 9.64 ± 0.92 | 7.13 ± 1.45 | < .001 |
| Pain VAS | 1.55 ± 1.21 | 3.75 ± 2.63 | .019 |
| Swelling VAS | 1.91 ± 0.94 | 3.25 ± 1.29 | .010 |
| Bleeding VAS | 0.40 ± 0.12 | 1.16 ± 0.34 | .061 |
| Postoperative complications (sites) | 1 | 5 | < .082 (n.s.) |

- Premature membrane exposure: 2 sites (PRI), 1 site (DFI).
- Paresthesia, infection, and continuous discomfort: 1 site each, all in the PRI group.
- **Bottom line**: DFI outperformed conventional PRI on advancement magnitude and all three morbidity VAS metrics (bleeding narrowly missed significance), and trended toward fewer complications without reaching significance at this sample size.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Small sample**: n=23 defects total, split across two arms — likely underpowered for the complication-incidence comparison (P < .082 despite a 5:1 event ratio).
- **Single-center**: Tufts University School of Dental Medicine only; generalizability to other operators/settings untested.
- **Abstract-only ingestion**: exact DFI surgical technique description, statistical methodology, defect-site anatomic distribution (maxilla vs mandible split), and follow-up schedule are unavailable and not reconstructed from memory.
- No stated blinding of outcome assessors; VAS is inherently patient-reported and unblinded to technique received.

## 7. Glossary

- **DFI (double-flap incision)**: the flap-advancement technique tested here as an alternative to conventional PRI; involves a modified incision approach (exact steps not detailed in the abstract) intended to achieve greater advancement with less morbidity.
- **PRI (periosteal releasing incision)**: the conventional/reference technique — an incision through the periosteum at the base of a reflected flap that releases its inelastic attachment, allowing coronal advancement for tension-free primary closure.
- **Seibert Class III defect**: a combined horizontal AND vertical alveolar ridge deficiency (Seibert's classification of ridge defects), the most complex of the three Seibert classes.
- **UNC-15 probe**: a periodontal probe with 1 mm markings up to 15 mm, used here to directly measure the linear distance of flap advancement.
- **VAS (visual analog scale)**: a patient-reported outcome instrument (typically a 0–10 or 0–100 line) used to quantify subjective symptom intensity — here, pain, swelling, and bleeding.
