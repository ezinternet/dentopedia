## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional method-comparison study (n=81 fully dentate volunteers, healthy oral function; 4 independent operators; fifth/final part of a programmatic series) using a central bearing point system and rapid closing movements to record the neuromuscular mandibular position, then comparing it on a custom electronic measuring articulator against centric relation (CR, defined by central-bearing registration at the Gothic arch tip) and maximal intercuspal position (MI).

Reproducibility of the neuromuscular registration averaged 0.52 ± 0.16 mm (range 0.04–2.53 mm); the condylar spatial distance from MI averaged 0.88 ± 0.30 mm (range 0.12–5.98 mm) and from CR averaged 0.83 ± 0.27 mm (range 0.10–7.89 mm).

The neuromuscular position is neither MI nor CR — a third, distinct position — and the authors conclude registration of the neuromuscular adduction field should not be recommended for prosthodontic restorations in dentate or edentulous patients.

## 세줄요약

단면 방법비교 연구(완전 유치악·건강한 구강기능 81명; 독립 술자 4인; 과두위치 연구 5부작 중 5부·최종편): 중심베어링포인트 시스템과 급속 폐구운동으로 신경근 하악위를 기록한 뒤, 커스텀 전자 계측 교합기로 중심위(Centric Relation, CR — 고딕아치 첨점에서의 중심베어링 등록으로 정의)·최대교두감합위(Maximal Intercuspal position, MI)와 비교했다.

신경근 등록의 재현성은 평균 0.52 ± 0.16 mm(범위 0.04–7.89 mm 중 재현성 자체는 0.04–2.53 mm); MI-신경근위 간 과두 공간거리는 평균 0.88 ± 0.30 mm(범위 0.12–5.98 mm), CR-신경근위 간은 평균 0.83 ± 0.27 mm(범위 0.10–7.89 mm)였다.

신경근위치는 MI도 CR도 아닌 제3의 위치이며, 저자들은 유치악·무치악 환자의 보철 수복에 신경근 흡착역(adduction field) 등록을 권장할 수 없다고 결론지었다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Unusually large, well-operator-controlled design for this literature: 81 fully dentate, healthy-function subjects, 4 independent operators, each registration repeated twice — the fifth and final part of a programmatic series on TMJ condylar positions.
- Establishes the neuromuscular adduction position as a **third, distinct mandibular position** — measurably different from both MI and CR, not a synonym or approximation of either.
- Reports dispersion, not just central tendency: means look small (<1 mm) but ranges reach 5.98 mm (MI) and 7.89 mm (CR) — the tails, not the means, carry the paper's negative conclusion.
- Makes explicit that its CR standard is itself a specific methodological choice — central bearing point registration at the tip of the Gothic arch tracing — not a neutral ground truth.
- Categorical clinical recommendation: neuromuscular adduction-field registration should not be used for prosthodontic restorations, in dentate patients directly studied and — by extrapolation the study does not test — edentulous patients too.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional method-comparison study; fifth and final part of a comprehensive investigation of TMJ condylar positions.
- **Sample**: 81 fully dentate subjects with healthy oral function.
- **Neuromuscular position recording**: central bearing point system + rapid closing movements, performed independently by 4 operators; most frequent adduction point determined; recordings repeated twice per subject.
- **CR position**: maxillary casts mounted via individual facebow transfer; mandibular casts mounted using a central bearing point registration at the tip of the Gothic arch tracing — this operational definition of CR is a substantive methodological choice, not a neutral reference.
- **MI position**: recorded from the subject's own maximal intercuspation.
- **Measurement**: custom-made electronic measuring articulator determined the spatial distance between the condyles across CR, MI, and the newly determined neuromuscular position.
- **Statistics**: means ± SD and ranges reported for reproducibility and for each pairwise condylar distance (right/left averaged); no inferential test reported in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Reproducibility of neuromuscular registration** (R/L averaged): 0.52 ± 0.16 mm, range 0.04–2.53 mm.
- **MI ↔ neuromuscular position**: 0.88 ± 0.30 mm, range 0.12–5.98 mm.
- **CR ↔ neuromuscular position**: 0.83 ± 0.27 mm, range 0.10–7.89 mm.
- Conclusion: neuromuscular position ≠ MI and ≠ CR; not recommended as a prosthodontic registration reference.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Abstract-only ingest — methodology detail (e.g., exact rapid-closure protocol, operator training, statistical tests) not verifiable beyond what the abstract states.
- Sample is fully dentate with healthy oral function only, n=81, apparently single research group/series — the "edentulous patients" clause in the conclusion is an extrapolation beyond the enrolled population, not a tested claim.
- Means are small (<1 mm) and could read as clinically tolerable in isolation; the paper's own conclusion rests on the wide upper tails (up to 5.98 mm and 7.89 mm), which the abstract does not further stratify (e.g., by subject, by operator, or by which side is discordant).
- The study's CR reference is itself defined by central-bearing/Gothic-arch registration — readers who reject gothic-arch-derived CR as ground truth would need to reinterpret the CR-vs-neuromuscular comparison accordingly.

- [[occlusion/kwak-2024-masticatory-positioning-occlusal-stability-full-mouth]] — refines: Kwak's chewing-pattern-derived therapeutic position (CM-H, recorded during actual hard-food mastication) was clinically stable over 10 years; Utz's rapid-closure/central-bearing-derived neuromuscular position was not reproducible or usable — the two results bound *which* muscle/function-derived registration methods can be trusted, rather than contradicting each other outright (different registration tasks).
- [[occlusion/fukushima-2016-controversy-with-respect-occlusion]] — related, not typed as contradicts: Fukushima's proposed alternative to CR is a stable ICP where habitual closing movements terminate free of premature contact, verified chairside on the patient's own natural bite — not an instrument-derived adduction-field position. ## 7. Glossary

- **Neuromuscular position**: mandibular adduction position recorded via a central bearing point system and rapid closing movements, representing the most frequent adduction point across repeated trials — distinct from MI and CR in this study.
- **Central bearing point registration**: intraoral tracing device (Gothic arch) used here both to define CR (at the tracing apex) and, via rapid closure, to capture the neuromuscular position.
- **Gothic arch tip**: the apex of the pantographic tracing, used in this study as the operational CR reference.
