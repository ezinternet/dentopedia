## Why Ingested
치과 상담 동의율 세트 인제스트: 일선 치과의사들이 SDM을 어떻게 이해(혹은 오해)하는지 질적으로 포착한 첫 연구 중 하나. SDM을 informed consent와 혼동하는 실무 패턴을 기존 [[behavioral-dentistry/communication-relationship/asaad-2019-shared-decision-making-sdm-dentistry]] 리뷰의 "구현 격차" 주장에 현장 증거로 연결하며, [[overviews/patient-consultation-communication-protocol]] 의 6단계 상담 프레임이 실제로 어디서 무너지는지 진단한다.

## One-line Summary
Qualitative study (n=9 GDPs, UK) found SDM is systematically confused with informed consent, with three barriers: communication style limits, regulatory fear driving defensive information-dumping, and NHS time pressure.

## 한줄요약
질적 연구(n=9, 영국 일반치과): SDM을 informed consent와 혼동·실제 실무에서 정보 전달로 대체; 장벽 3개 — 소통 습관, 규제 두려움, NHS 시간 제약.

## 1. Document Information
- Journal: British Dental Journal, Vol 232, Issue 4, pp. 227–231, February 2022
- Institution: University of Bedfordshire; private dental surgery, Hemel Hempstead, UK
- Ethics: University of Bedfordshire ethical approval
- Funding/COI: not disclosed

## 2. Key Contributions
- Demonstrated empirically that UK GDPs conflate SDM with informed consent in daily practice
- Identified three overarching barrier themes: (1) communication style, (2) perceptions of regulatory bodies, (3) dental care structure (NHS vs. private)
- Showed that "information provision" dominates over "collaborative discussion" — patients given lists of options and risks without elicitation of preferences
- Documented that fear of litigation redirects communication from SDM toward defensive documentation
- Recommended standardized decision-making aids to support GDPs in structuring SDM

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Design: Qualitative (interpretive paradigm), semi-structured interviews
- Sampling: purposive, via private dental online forum (Facebook)
- n: 9 GDPs; 6 men, 3 women; experience 3–16 years post-foundation; 8 mixed-practice (NHS + private), 1 fully private
- Data collection: Skype interviews, 25–45 min each (avg 30 min); ~4 hours total; recordings transcribed within 48 hours
- Analysis: thematic analysis (3 overarching themes, coded by first author)
- Data saturation: reached at n=9

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- All 9 participants believed the patient should make the final decision → consistent with informed consent model, not SDM
- Most provided all options/risks/benefits as a list (information provision mode), not as collaborative exploration
- Conflicting statements: participants said they practice SDM but described informed consent process when probed
- Regulatory fear: fear of litigation altered communication toward defensive information-dumping
- Guideline conflict: NHS England, GDC, and Dental Protection gave different messages; practitioners confused
- Time: NHS system cited as structurally incompatible with SDM's conversation demands
- Experience moderates: longer-tenured GDPs in stable practices described more organic SDM due to rapport built over time

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Very small sample (n=9); not generalizable quantitatively
- All UK-based; regulatory and NHS context limits cross-national applicability
- Self-report on communication practice (no observation); social desirability bias possible
- No patient perspective
- Future: observation studies of actual consultation behavior; develop and test SDM aids for UK dental practice

## 7. Glossary
- GDP: General Dental Practitioner — equivalent to Korean 일반 치과의사 (비전문의)
- SDM: Shared Decision Making — collaborative decision process involving clinician and patient
- GDC: General Dental Council — UK dental regulatory body
- Montgomery ruling: 2015 UK Supreme Court ruling requiring disclosure of "material risks" meaningful to the specific patient (vs. what a reasonable doctor would disclose)
- NHS: National Health Service — UK public healthcare; time/fee constraints limit SDM implementation
