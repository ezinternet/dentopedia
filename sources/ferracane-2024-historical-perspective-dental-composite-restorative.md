## Three-line Summary


Single-author historical-perspective review (Ferracane, J Funct Biomater 2024) tracing dental resin-based composite (RBC) evolution — from 1950s silicate/PMMA acrylics through Bowen's 1962 Bis-GMA and Buonocore's acid-etch, to the transitions chemical→UV→visible/CQ→LED light-curing, low-shrinkage monomer and nano/hybrid filler refinements, bulk-fill (4–5 mm) composites, and the emerging "bioactive"/self-healing frontier — with amalgam framed as the posterior material composites gradually displaced.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Provides a **chronological spine** for the wiki's scattered composite pages — organizes RBC history along three axes: polymerization initiation, monomer chemistry, and filler technology.
- Fixes the **founding milestones**: PMMA acrylics (early 1950s) → Bowen's Bis-GMA (US patent 3,066,112, 1962; literature 1963) → Buonocore acid-etch enamel bonding.
- Traces the **light-curing lineage**: chemical two-paste → UV (~1970) → visible-light CQ/amine (late 1970s–80s) → QTH → LED (dominant by mid-2000s) → laser/PAC/poly-wave.
- Reframes **polymerization shrinkage** as a *stress* problem (shrinkage resisted by adhesion), motivating low-shrinkage monomers (silorane, ormocer, TCD-urethane) and bulk-fill stress-relief chemistries.
- Sets the **future agenda**: "bioactivity" (antimicrobial/anti-biofilm/ion-releasing remineralizing) and self-healing composites, with a candid note that clinical superiority is largely unproven.

## 3. Methodology

- Narrative historical review; not a systematic search. Author-selected literature to identify the significant, commercially realized changes to initiators, monomers, and fillers.
- Organized in four evolutionary sections + a future-directions section; illustrates each change with named commercial products.

## 4. Key Results (timeline highlights)

- **Pre-composite**: silicate cements; PMMA (PMMA powder + MMA liquid, amine–peroxide cure) — high shrinkage, no bonding, thermal mismatch, MMA pulpal toxicity, weak.
- **1962 Bis-GMA (Bowen)**: methacrylate+epoxide hybrid → stronger, stiffer, less shrinkage, tooth-like thermal expansion, esthetic. Diluted with TEGDMA.
- **Bonding**: Buonocore phosphoric-acid enamel etch → mechanical adhesion + margin seal. Early use anterior-only; posterior stayed amalgam.
- **Initiators**: self-cure → UV (benzoin ether, <400 nm, penetration/UV-exposure limits) → visible CQ+amine (~468 nm) → "less-yellow" PPD/BAPO/TPO/Ivocerin. Diphenyliodonium boosts CQ.
- **Lights**: QTH (inefficient, heat) → LED (420–500 nm, 800–2000 mW/cm², battery) → laser/PAC (1990s) → Monet LED-laser; poly-wave for multi-initiator materials. Exposure reciprocity discredited as a general rule.
- **Monomers/low-shrinkage**: larger monomers (DX511/Kalore, TCD-urethane/Venus Diamond, N'Durance); silorane ring-opening (Filtek LS — reduced shrinkage, only equivalent clinically, needs dedicated adhesive); ormocer (Admira/Definite/Ceram-X — slightly less successful posteriorly). BPA not formulated but possible via dimethacrylate hydrolysis → "BPA-free" claims.
- **Self-adhesive**: Activa poor w/o adhesive (discontinued at <1 yr), acceptable with adhesive in primary teeth; SureFil One reasonable but discontinued (supply).
- **Bulk-fill (early 2010s)**: 4–5 mm single cure; low-stress requirement; SDR Flow (flowable + cap); refractive-index matching + larger particles for depth; covalent-adaptable-network stress relief (Filtek One, Tetric PowerFill). Perform as well as incremental conventional.
- **Fillers**: large quartz/glass (poor wear/polish) → 40–50 nm "microfill" (gloss, weak) → hybrid → microhybrid → nanohybrid → true nanofill (Filtek Supreme) → supra-nanofill spherical 100–300 nm (Estelite/Omnichroma, single-shade). Radiolucent silica → radiopaque Ba/Al/Sr/Zr glasses. Fiber/whisker reinforcement (Alert, everX Posterior).
- **Future**: bioactivity (MDPB/Clearfil Protect Bond, Nobio Infinix, ion-releasing Activa/Beautifil/Cention N alkasite) — reduced in-vitro mineral loss but no proven clinical superiority; self-healing composites still research-only.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative, single-author selection — not a systematic review; no quantitative synthesis or risk-of-bias appraisal.
- Composite-centric; amalgam, GIC, and ceramics appear only as context, not covered as parallel histories (so "아말감부터" is framed through the composite lens, not an amalgam material history).
- Emphasizes commercialized products; many experimental systems and discontinued products limit forward applicability.
- Author repeatedly notes bioactive/low-shrinkage/self-adhesive claims lack demonstrated clinical superiority — future long-term RCTs needed.

## 7. Glossary

- **RBC (Resin-Based Composite)**: tooth-colored direct restorative of a polymerizable resin matrix reinforced with inorganic filler particles.
- **Bis-GMA (Bowen's resin)**: bisphenol-A glycidyl dimethacrylate (1962); large cross-linking monomer that founded modern composites, usually diluted with TEGDMA.
- **Camphorquinone (CQ)**: yellow visible-light photoinitiator absorbing ~468 nm, activated with a tertiary amine co-initiator.
- **Polymerization shrinkage stress**: internal stress when curing contraction is resisted by adhesion to the cavity — the true driver of marginal gaps/microleakage (vs shrinkage magnitude alone).
- **Bulk-fill composite**: formulation curable in 4–5 mm increments via enhanced depth of cure and low polymerization stress.
- **Ormocer / silorane**: alternative low-shrinkage matrices — organically modified ceramic (sol–gel) and cationic ring-opening oxirane, respectively.
- **Bioactive composite**: material intended to add benefit (antimicrobial/anti-biofilm/ion-release remineralization) beyond passive restoration; definition not standardized.
