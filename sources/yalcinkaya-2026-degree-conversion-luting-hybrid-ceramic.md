## Three-line Summary

In-vitro FTIR study (n=7 per group, 63 specimens total) evaluating degree of conversion of dual-cure resin cement, flowable composite, and bulk-fill composite polymerized beneath hybrid ceramic (Cerasmart) CAD/CAM substrates at 1.5/2.0/2.5 mm thicknesses, finding flowable composite had the highest DC at ≤2.0 mm but the lowest at 2.5 mm, while dual-cure resin cement gave the most stable DC across all thicknesses and the highest DC at 2.5 mm.

- Study type: in-vitro laboratory study

## 2. Key Contributions
- First direct three-way comparison of dual-cure resin cement, flowable composite, and bulk-fill composite polymerization efficiency beneath the same hybrid ceramic CAD/CAM material across three clinically relevant restoration thicknesses.
- Quantifies optical property changes (translucency parameters RTP_ab/RTP_00, opalescence, contrast ratio) of Cerasmart hybrid ceramic across 1.5–2.5 mm and links them mechanistically to the degree-of-conversion decline of the luting materials underneath.
- Demonstrates a statistically significant material × thickness interaction effect (p<0.001) — the ranking of "best" luting material for DC flips depending on restoration thickness, rather than one material being universally superior.
- Identifies a clinically meaningful esthetic threshold: the 1.5→2.0 mm thickness step exceeds translucency perceptibility/acceptability thresholds, while 2.0→2.5 mm stays within acceptable limits (non-linear/curvilinear translucency-thickness relationship).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
In-vitro factorial study. One hybrid ceramic CAD/CAM block (CeraSmart 270, shade A2 HT, GC) sectioned into substrates of 16×12 mm at three thicknesses (1.5, 2.0, 2.5 mm; diamond disc, low-speed water-cooled sectioning; thickness verified with digital caliper ±0.01 mm). Three luting materials from the same manufacturer (Tokuyama): dual-cure resin cement (Estecem II Plus), light-cured flowable composite (Estelite Universal Flow), and light-cured bulk-fill composite (Estelite Bulk-Fill Flow).

Optical properties: 6 substrates per thickness group (uncemented) measured spectrophotometrically (VITA Easyshade Advance 4.0) over standardized black/white backgrounds under CIE Lab* D65 illuminant, 45:0 geometry, triplicate readings averaged. Relative translucency parameter by CIELab (RTP_ab) and CIEDE2000 (RTP_00), opalescence (OP), and contrast ratio (CR) calculated from standard formulas; RTP differences evaluated against literature perceptibility (TP: 0.6/1.3) and acceptability (TA: 2.6/4.4) thresholds for RTP_ab/RTP_00 respectively.

Degree of conversion: luting materials (n=7 per material per thickness = 63 total specimens) polymerized through each substrate thickness in a standardized jig (Mylar strips + aluminum foil spacer with central opening, black glass plate + black cardboard holder) using a polywave LED curing unit (Valo X, 1000 mW/cm², 385–515 nm) at 0 mm distance, 90° angle. Dual-cure cement: 20 s light-cure plus standardized seating pressure (5 s finger pressure, then 25 N load for 2.5 min). Flowable composite: 20 s cure. Bulk-fill composite: 10 s cure (per manufacturer instructions). Final luting-layer thickness standardized to 80 μm. Specimens stored dry/dark at 37°C for 24 h before analysis. DC measured by ATR-FTIR (Perkin Elmer Spectrum Two, diamond ATR, 32 scans, 400–4000 cm⁻¹, resolution 4 cm⁻¹) comparing cured vs uncured spectra using the 1634 cm⁻¹ (methacrylate C=C) / 1608 or 1592 cm⁻¹ (aromatic C=C internal reference) peak-height ratio.

Statistics: Shapiro-Wilk normality testing (DC data non-normal → robust ANOVA with Bonferroni pairwise comparisons); optical parameters analyzed by one-way ANOVA + Tukey's test; significance set at p<0.05; post-hoc power analysis via G*Power 3.1.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
**Optical properties** (all p<0.001 across thickness): RTP_ab decreased from 11.21±1.4 (1.5mm) to 7.9±1.5 (2.0mm) to 6.06±1.1 (2.5mm); RTP_00 decreased from 16.51±1.5 to 11.52±1.5 to 8.46±1.2; opalescence decreased from 7.35±0.6 to 6.73±0.2 to 5.87±0.5; contrast ratio increased from 0.63±0.04 to 0.74±0.05 to 0.81±0.04. Translucency differences exceeded perceptibility/acceptability thresholds for the 1.5–2.0 mm and 1.5–2.5 mm comparisons, but the 2.0–2.5 mm difference stayed within the acceptability limit.

**Degree of conversion** (material × thickness interaction p<0.001):
- 1.5 mm: flowable composite highest (36.57±1.8%), then dual-cure cement (28.01±5.2%), then bulk-fill (23.34±3.6%) — dual-cure vs bulk-fill not significantly different; flowable significantly higher than both others (p<0.05).
- 2.0 mm: flowable composite still highest, statistically similar to dual-cure cement (27.16±2.2%); bulk-fill lowest (22.69±2.9%), not significantly different from dual-cure.
- 2.5 mm: dual-cure resin cement highest (30.88±2.5%), then bulk-fill (19.50±5.9%), then flowable composite lowest (11.69±2.2%) — flowable DC dropped significantly from its 1.5/2.0 mm values (p<0.05).
- Dual-cure cement and bulk-fill composite showed no significant DC differences across the three thicknesses (p>0.05) — i.e., relatively thickness-stable — whereas flowable composite DC was highly thickness-dependent.
- Post-hoc power: sample size 63, large effect size f=2.75, power ≈1.000 at α=0.05.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
Authors explicitly state: controlled in-vitro conditions do not replicate the clinical environment (no salivary contamination, thermal fluctuation, occlusal loading, pulpal temperature, or oral pH variation simulated). They call for further clinical trials and in-situ evaluations to validate applicability under real-world conditions. Additional limitations identifiable from the methodology: single hybrid ceramic material/shade tested (Cerasmart A2 HT only, not generalizable to other CAD/CAM materials or shades); single light-curing unit/protocol; DC measured only at 24 h (no long-term/aging DC or bond-strength correlation reported in this paper).

## 6. Related Work
The paper situates itself against prior literature on ceramic-thickness-dependent light attenuation and DC reduction (cited as reducing light transmittance by up to 50% at 2 mm for some materials), prior reports of a curvilinear (Wu et al.) translucency-thickness relationship, and prior reports that self-adhesive dual-cure resin cements show lower DC than composite-based alternatives under ceramic substrates of varying thickness. It also references the broader bulk-fill composite literature on filler/photoinitiator optimization enabling cure depths up to 4 mm.

## 7. Glossary
- **DC (Degree of Conversion)**: percentage of C=C methacrylate double bonds converted to single bonds during polymerization, measured by FTIR peak-ratio method; higher DC = more complete cure, better mechanical/biological properties.
- **RTP_ab / RTP_00 (Relative Translucency Parameter)**: color-difference-based translucency metrics calculated over black vs white backgrounds using CIELab (ab) or CIEDE2000 (00) formulas; higher = more translucent.
- **OP (Opalescence Parameter)**: quantifies the blue-orange color shift characteristic of natural tooth structure/ceramics, derived from luminance differences over black/white backgrounds.
- **CR (Contrast Ratio)**: ratio of reflectance over black vs white backgrounds; higher CR = more opaque material.
- **TP / TA (Translucency Perceptibility / Acceptability thresholds)**: literature-derived thresholds below which a translucency difference is imperceptible (TP) or clinically acceptable (TA) to observers.
- **ATR-FTIR**: attenuated total reflectance Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy; used here to quantify residual unreacted C=C bonds pre/post cure.
- **Hybrid ceramic / resin nanoceramic / PICN**: CAD/CAM restorative material combining a ceramic matrix (silica/Ba-glass or zirconia particles) with a resin polymer network, intended to balance ceramic esthetics/strength with composite reparability/elasticity.
