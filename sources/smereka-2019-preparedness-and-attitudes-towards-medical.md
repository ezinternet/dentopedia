## Three-line Summary

A cross-sectional survey of 419 Polish dentists found vasovagal syncope (46.30%) as the most common dental office emergency, followed by orthostatic hypotension (18.85%) and hyperventilation crisis (18.61%); anaphylactic shock affected 16.23% and was among the least-common serious events. Despite this, major competence gaps exist: 74.47% lacked confidence in managing hypertensive crisis, 55.13% in anaphylaxis, and 41.29% in cardiac arrest. The study concludes that prevalence is comparable with other countries and calls for improved undergraduate/postgraduate emergency training.

## Abstract

**Background:** Medical emergencies in dental offices are considered a problem in most countries owing to dentists' concerns about emergency preparedness, practical skills, lifesaving equipment and staff availability. The prevalence of medical emergencies in dental offices and dentists' preparedness have been analysed in several countries but have never been studied in Poland.

**Aim:** To assess the prevalence of medical emergencies in dental offices in Poland, as well as dentists' preparedness and attitudes towards medical emergencies.

**Methods:** An 18-item questionnaire was completed by 419 dentists. It asked for information on their cardiopulmonary resuscitation training, availability of emergency medical equipment in the dental office, prevalence of medical emergencies and self-assessed competence in various dental office emergencies. Data were analysed using the Statistica 13.3 software package. Variability was measured with standard deviation. Pearson's linear correlation coefficient was used to determine correlation strength.

**Results:** The most common medical emergencies in Polish dental offices were:
- Vasovagal syncope (VVS): 46.30% of study participants experienced ≥1 case in preceding 12 months
- Orthostatic hypotension: 18.85%
- Hyperventilation crisis: 18.61%
- Mild allergic reactions: 16.23%
- Hypoglycaemia: 15.99%
- Seizures: 11.81%

Emergency Medical Service (EMS) call frequency (preceding 12 months):
- 0 calls: 80.66%
- 1 call: 12.65%
- 2 calls: 4.53%
- 3 calls: 1.20%
- 4 calls: 0.48%
- 5–10 calls: 0.48%
- >10 calls: 0%

Self-assessed competence gaps:
- Not confident in cardiac arrest management: 41.29%
- Not confident in hypertensive crisis management: 74.47%
- Not confident in asthma management: 55.61%
- Not confident in anaphylactic shock management: 55.13%
- Not confident in seizure management: 52.99%

**Conclusion:** The prevalence of medical emergencies in dental offices in Poland is comparable with that in other countries. A large number of dentists do not feel competent enough to manage medical emergencies. Better undergraduate and postgraduate training in medical emergencies is recommended, as well as broader availability of emergency medical equipment in the dental office.
