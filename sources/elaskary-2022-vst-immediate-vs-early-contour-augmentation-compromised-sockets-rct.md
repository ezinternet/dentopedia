## Why Ingested

First RCT directly comparing Vestibular Socket Therapy (VST) (즉시 식립 + 전정 접근 GBR) versus early implant placement + contour augmentation for compromised (nonrestorable) maxillary anterior sockets in the esthetic zone. Establishes VST's mid-facial soft tissue preservation advantage (-0.53 vs -1.87 mm, p<0.001) as the foundational RCT evidence for the technique. Extends [[wiki/immediate-implant/elaskary-2024-vst-immediate-implant-infected-sockets]] (2024 cohort that further tested VST in infected sockets), and provides comparator evidence for [[wiki/immediate-implant/ickroth-2025-immediate-implant-intact-nonintact-sockets]] (socket integrity classification outcomes).

## Three-line Summary

RCT (n=40, 1 year) comparing VST — immediate implant placement with GBR via vestibular access — versus early implant placement with conventional flap and contour augmentation in compromised maxillary esthetic-zone sockets.

VST showed significantly less mid-facial soft tissue change (-0.53 ± 1.17 mm) versus early placement + contour augmentation (-1.87 ± 0.69 mm; p<0.001); papilla and horizontal soft tissue changes also significantly less in VST; labial bone thickness increased in both groups with no between-group difference.

VST represents an innovative option for immediate implant placement in the esthetic zone, with better soft-tissue preservation than early placement + GBR, though limited by small sample (n=40), single center, and 1-year follow-up.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First RCT to directly compare VST (immediate implant + vestibular GBR) versus early implant placement + contour augmentation for compromised esthetic-zone sockets
- VST significantly reduced mid-facial soft tissue recession (-0.53 mm) compared to early placement + GBR (-1.87 mm; p<0.001)
- Papilla dimensions (mesial and distal) and horizontal soft tissue changes favored VST over early placement
- Labial bone thickness increased significantly intra-group in both techniques — neither approach caused bone loss
- Regression analysis identified VST use and increased coronal bone thickness as significant predictors of reduced mid-facial soft tissue change
- Overall implant survival near-equivalent: one failure in VST group (19/20 = 95%) vs 20/20 in control (100%)

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Parallel-group RCT; 1:1 allocation (n=20 VST, n=20 early placement + contour augmentation)
- **Population**: 40 adults with single non-restorable maxillary teeth in the esthetic zone (anterior); sockets classified as "compromised" (not specifically infected)
- **VST (Test)**: Atraumatic extraction → immediate implant placement → GBR performed through vestibular (labial) access incision, preserving the crestal gingival architecture
- **Control**: Atraumatic extraction → initial healing period (early placement protocol) → implant placement + GBR through conventional access flap + contour augmentation
- **Primary outcome**: Mid-facial soft tissue level change at 1 year
- **Secondary outcomes**: Mesial and distal papilla dimensions, horizontal soft tissue change, labial bone plate thickness (apical, middle, coronal levels via CBCT)
- **Follow-up**: 12 months post-loading
- **Single center; single operator** (inferred from institutional context)
- **Limitation**: Small sample (n=40), single center, 1-year follow-up only, abstract-only source

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small sample (n=20 per group) limits power to detect smaller effect sizes
- Single center, single operator — external validity uncertain
- 1-year follow-up only — longer-term soft tissue stability and bone thickness evolution unknown
- Abstract-only ingest — detailed methodology (randomization/allocation concealment, ITT analysis, blinding) not verifiable
- Population: "compromised" (non-restorable) sockets; not specifically infected — generalizability to infected sockets addressed separately by the 2024 cohort

## 7. Glossary

- **VST (Vestibular Socket Therapy)**: Immediate implant placement technique using a vestibular (labial/buccal) access incision for GBR, designed to preserve gingival architecture
- **Contour augmentation**: GBR procedure performed through a conventional access flap, used here with early (delayed) implant placement to rebuild buccal ridge contour
- **Mid-facial soft tissue change**: Vertical change in soft tissue at the mid-buccal aspect of the implant crown — primary esthetic outcome measure
- **Early implant placement**: Implant placed after initial soft tissue healing (typically 4–8 weeks post-extraction), but before complete bone fill
- **Compromised socket**: Extraction socket with some degree of tissue loss or bone deficiency (not necessarily infected); distinguished from intact socket (4-wall type)
