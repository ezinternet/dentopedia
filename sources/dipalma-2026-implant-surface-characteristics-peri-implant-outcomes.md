## Why Ingested
This paper directly fills a gap named in [[overviews/implant-surface-comparison]]'s "Wiki Coverage Caveat" section: "표면처리와 peri-implantitis 장기 발생률 연관 prospective (Komatsu 2026이 biofilm 억제 기전 제시 — 임상 발생률 연결 RCT 필요)." It is the first paper the wiki holds that pools 13 comparative clinical studies (6 RCT + 3 prospective/controlled + 4 retrospective/pooled cohort) specifically to test whether implant surface type predicts long-term peri-implant disease occurrence, and its conclusion — surface characteristics have a limited, context-dependent influence, with patient/site risk factors dominating long-term outcomes — is a nuancing counterweight to the wiki's largely surface-optimistic UV-PF/hydrophilic-surface literature (e.g. [[implants/surface/komatsu-2026-uv-photofunctionalization-surface-agnostic-biofilm-soft-tissue-sr]]) and reinforces the surgical-recurrence finding in [[implants/peri-implantitis/gardelis-2025-implant-surface-modifications-peri-implantitis]] that rough surfaces become a liability once disease is established.

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review (PRISMA/PROSPERO #1301140, ROBINS-I) of 13 comparative human clinical studies (668 records screened → 13 included; 6 RCT, 3 prospective/controlled, 4 retrospective/pooled cohort; 18–600 patients, 35–630 implants) on implant surface characteristics and peri-implant clinical/radiographic/microbiological outcomes, PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science 2015–2025.
Short/medium-term RCTs found minimal marginal bone loss (MBL) differences across surface types (SLA vs hydrophilic/CA, anodized/laser-microgrooved collars); long-term retrospective cohorts found rough surfaces associated with higher peri-implantitis odds (Ferrantino: OR 4.877, 95% CI 1.701–13.980; Raes: 12/42 TiUnite vs 3/41 turned at 5 years, p<0.01) but patient/site factors (smoking, periodontitis history, maintenance) outweighed surface effects; microbiological/biomolecular data were scarce.
Clinical implication: surface modifications should be treated as adjunctive, not determinant, factors in peri-implant health — long-term risk assessment and supportive care matter more than surface choice; microbiological evidence remains too thin for firm conclusions.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First wiki-held SR to pool comparative clinical evidence (not just narrative) on whether implant surface type predicts peri-implant disease occurrence/MBL, across RCT + prospective + retrospective designs.
- Reconciles the short-term "surfaces are equivalent" RCT literature with the long-term "rough surfaces raise peri-implantitis odds" retrospective literature by framing surface effect as real but secondary to patient/site risk factors.
- Explicitly flags the scarcity/heterogeneity of microbiological and biomolecular outcome data as a distinct evidence gap from clinical/radiographic outcomes.
- Proposes methodological standards for future trials (CONSORT, prospective registration, ≥3–5 year follow-up for peri-implantitis incidence, stratified randomization for smoking/periodontitis history, ID-COSM core outcome sets).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review of comparative clinical studies (no meta-analysis — heterogeneity precluded pooling), PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered (#1301140).
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science; English-language, 1 Jan 2015 – 1 Dec 2025.
- **n**: 668 records identified (PubMed 68, Scopus 157, WoS 443) → 129 duplicates removed → 539 screened → 13 studies included (18–600 patients; 35–630 implants).
- **Exclusions**: 15 animal studies, 72 in vitro/ex vivo, 22 SR/MA, 417 not aligned with objectives (insufficient sample size, non-comparative design, unclear outcome definitions, no relevant peri-implant outcome).
- **Quality assessment**: ROBINS-I (risk-of-bias tool for non-randomized studies), three-reviewer consensus (disagreements resolved by a third reviewer).
- **Outcomes**: primary — marginal bone loss (MBL), probing depth, bleeding on probing, peri-implantitis/peri-implant mucositis incidence; secondary — microbiological/biomolecular markers (bacterial load, inflammatory mediators), implant stability quotient (ISQ).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Study mix**: 6 RCT (Canullo 2024 nano-superhydrophilic bioactive surface in poor-quality bone; Guarnieri 2019 submerged vs nonsubmerged laser-microgrooved; Vílchez 2025 hydrophilic-modified SLA vs conventional SLA; Ko 2019 calcium-phosphate–coated vs uncoated SLA; Longhi 2025 anodized vs machined collars; Albertini 2021 loading protocols on thermo-chemically treated surfaces), follow-up 6 months–5 years.
- **3 prospective/controlled**: Carinci 2019 (antibacterial internal implant-abutment coating → reduced early bacterial load); Anitua 2017 and Şener-Yamaner 2017 (peri-implant bone/soft-tissue outcomes by surface/component modification).
- **4 retrospective/pooled cohort**: Raes 2018 (5-year split-mouth RCT, periodontitis-susceptible patients — peri-implantitis in 12/42 moderately rough TiUnite vs 3/41 minimally rough turned implants, p<0.01; cumulative survival 100% vs 97.6%); Ferrantino 2022 (630 implants, ~13-year mean follow-up — rough surfaces (Sa > 1 µm) OR 4.877, 95% CI 1.701–13.980 for peri-implantitis vs machined, adjusted for smoking/location); Hussain 2024 (patient-related factors > surface topography across two implant systems); Gnanajothi 2024 (limited short-term clinical differences across surface types).
- Short/medium-term RCTs: comparable MBL at 1 year (Ko, Vílchez) and up to 3 years (Guarnieri) across SLA vs hydrophilic/CA variants; anodized/laser-microgrooved collars showed no consistent advantage.
- Surface-related benefit was most evident in compromised scenarios: nano-superhydrophilic bioactive surfaces aided primary-to-secondary stability transition in low-density bone (Canullo); thermo-chemically treated surfaces performed well under early/immediate loading (Albertini).
- Microbiological data limited to Carinci 2019 (early bacterial-load reduction) and Raes 2018; too sparse/heterogeneous for firm conclusions.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- No meta-analysis possible — substantial heterogeneity in implant systems, surface classification, disease definitions, follow-up duration, outcome reporting.
- Many RCT/controlled studies had small samples and short follow-up (6–12 months), underpowered for late peri-implantitis endpoints; outcomes were often surrogate (early MBL, ISQ, plaque/bleeding indices) rather than disease incidence.
- Risk-of-bias: moderate-to-serious in several non-randomized/retrospective studies (confounding, selection bias); blinding often infeasible.
- Clustering at the patient level (multiple implants per patient) not consistently addressed, risking biased variance estimates.
- Future trials should follow CONSORT, be prospectively registered, use standardized peri-implant disease case definitions and ID-COSM core outcome sets, stratify randomization by smoking/periodontitis history, and follow ≥3–5 years when peri-implantitis incidence is the outcome.

## 7. Glossary
- **MBL (Marginal Bone Loss)**: radiographic loss of crestal bone height around an implant, the most common surrogate/early outcome measured in these trials.
- **ROBINS-I**: Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies of Interventions — the risk-of-bias tool used to appraise the non-RCT studies in this review.
- **PROSPERO**: International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews; this review is registered as #1301140.
- **ISQ (Implant Stability Quotient)**: resonance-frequency-analysis-derived measure of implant stability, used as a surrogate for the primary-to-secondary stability transition.
- **TiUnite**: a moderately rough anodized titanium implant surface (Nobel Biocare), used as the "moderately rough" comparator in the Raes 2018 split-mouth trial.
- **Sa (arithmetic mean roughness)**: a surface-roughness metric; Ferrantino 2022 used Sa > 1 µm as the threshold for "rough" surfaces in its cohort analysis.
