## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 2 systematic reviews aligned with the 2018 World Workshop Classification (WWC) framework for Stage III/IV periodontitis, using AMSTAR-2 quality appraisal and Corrected Covered Area (CCA) for overlap assessment; CCA=52.9% indicating substantial overlap between included SRs.

AMX+MTZ showed moderate-certainty evidence for greater CAL gain and PPD reduction (Lin et al., 45 RCTs, NMA); azithromycin evidence was insufficient for routine use (Jones & Hoyle, lower certainty, heterogeneous); doxycycline evidence too sparse for conclusions.

Findings support selective, not routine, antibiotic use in Stage III/IV periodontitis; antibiotics should complement, not replace, effective mechanical periodontal treatment — consistent with EFP S3-level clinical practice guidelines.

## 세줄요약

2018 세계치주학회의(World Workshop Classification, WWC) 기준 Stage III/IV 치주염 대상 체계적 문헌고찰 2편 우산리뷰(Umbrella Review): AMSTAR-2 질 평가 + 수정포함면적(CCA) 중복 평가, CCA=52.9% 상당한 중복.

AMX+MTZ: CAL 획득·PPD 감소에서 중등도 근거 질 우월 효과(Lin et al., RCT ~45편, 네트워크 메타분석); 아지스로마이신: 일상적 사용 지지 근거 불충분(Jones & Hoyle, 낮은 근거 질, 이질성); 독시사이클린: 근거 희소로 결론 불가.

Stage III/IV 치주염에서 항생제 사용은 선택적·비일상적 접근 지지; EFP S3 임상진료지침과 일치 — 기계적 치주치료를 보완하되 대체하지 않음.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First umbrella review restricted to SRs using 2018 WWC Stage III/IV framework
- CCA=52.9%: substantial overlap — same RCTs appear in both reviews; independent confirmation not valid
- AMX+MTZ moderate-certainty evidence from NMA (Lin et al., ~45 RCTs)
- Azithromycin: insufficient evidence for routine use
- Stage IV-specific evidence lacking — most underlying data are Stage III
- Consistent with EFP S3-level guidelines: selective, patient-centered antibiotic use

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD420261449383
- Databases: Embase, Google Scholar, LILACS, MEDLINE, PubMed, Scopus; June 2018 – June 2026
- Inclusion: SRs/MAs with quantitative meta-analysis, Stage III or IV periodontitis (2018 WWC), English only
- Exclusion: narrative reviews, protocols, conference abstracts, duplicate analyses
- 273 records → 2 eligible SRs included
- CCA formula: (N−r)/(rc−r) × 100; N=56, r=2, c=52 → CCA=52.9%
- AMSTAR-2 methodological quality assessment

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
| SR | Focus | Quality | Key Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lin et al. 2025 | AMX+MTZ, azithromycin, doxycycline NMA (~45 RCTs) | Moderate (AMSTAR-2) | AMX+MTZ: moderate-certainty CAL gain + PPD reduction |
| Jones & Hoyle 2022 | Azithromycin (fewer RCTs, pairwise MA) | Lower (AMSTAR-2) | Insufficient evidence for routine azithromycin use |

- CCA=52.9%: considerable overlap; conclusions not independent
- Stage IV periodontitis: insufficient dedicated evidence (data dominated by Stage III)
- EFP S3 guideline alignment: selective use only

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Only 2 SRs met eligibility (use of 2018 WWC criterion is very restrictive)
- CCA=52.9%: same primary trials counted twice
- Short follow-up (mostly <12 months) in underlying studies
- Stage IV-specific evidence absent
- Need: multicenter RCTs using 2018 WWC classification; living systematic reviews

## 6. Related Work and Context
- Aligns with EFP S3 guidelines: antibiotics in carefully selected patients only
- Ramachandra RCT (cited): azithromycin added no long-term benefit despite transient microbiological changes
- AMX+MTZ supported by multiple independent SRs (Khattri 2020, Zhao 2021, Kondreddy 2025)

## 7. Glossary
- **Umbrella review**: SR of SRs; synthesizes evidence across multiple systematic reviews
- **CCA (Corrected Covered Area)**: Measure of overlap in primary studies across SRs; 52.9% = substantial
- **AMSTAR-2**: Methodological quality appraisal tool for SRs
- **Stage III/IV periodontitis (2018 WWC)**: CAL ≥5 mm + bone loss to middle/apical third; Stage IV adds masticatory dysfunction, tooth loss, collapse

## Why Ingested

Most recent (2026) umbrella review synthesizing antibiotic evidence specifically for Stage III/IV periodontitis under the 2018 WWC — the current clinical classification system; directly informs which patients benefit from adjunctive antibiotics.
