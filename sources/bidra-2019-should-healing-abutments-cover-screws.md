## Why Ingested

Anchor systematic review for the "healing-abutment (HA) reuse" controversy batch — establishes the evidence baseline (6 observational studies, no clinical trials, no reported adverse events, conclusion = avoid *indiscriminate* reuse) against which the in-vitro/RCT sibling papers are read. Reinforces the broader sterilization/disinfection evidence in [[infection-control/patino-marin-2025-sterilization-disinfection-dental-practices]] and pairs directly with the surface-decontamination SR [[infection-control/eswaramurthy-2021-contemporary-sterilization-protocols-healing-abutments]].

## One-line Summary

Systematic review (6 observational studies, >300 used healing abutments): routine mechanical/chemical/steam methods often do NOT fully remove contaminants, but NO clinical study and NO reported adverse consequence exists — so indiscriminate HA/cover-screw reuse should be avoided until more evidence.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review to formally ask whether used healing abutments (HAs) and cover screws can be effectively resterilized for reuse, and whether any adverse consequences of reuse have been reported.
- Establishes the evidence baseline: only observational studies exist, no true clinical studies, and no reported patient harm — framing the controversy as an evidence gap rather than a demonstrated hazard.
- Provides the clinical bottom line that indiscriminate reuse should be avoided until supporting evidence is established, while acknowledging the cost-driven popularity of the practice.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Electronic search of the English-language literature (PubMed + Cochrane) by multiple independent investigators, with predetermined inclusion/exclusion criteria.
- Primary question: can HAs and cover screws be resterilized effectively for reuse? Secondary question: have any adverse biological/mechanical consequences to implants, or harm to patients, from reuse been reported?
- Screening: 657 titles initially identified → 6 articles met the objectives.
- (abstract-only — full methodological detail such as risk-of-bias assessment not retrieved.)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- All 6 included studies were **observational**, describing a total sample of **>300 used healing abutments** obtained from patients.
- **3 studies**: routine methods (mechanical, chemical, steam) for cleaning/sterilizing used HAs did **NOT** completely remove contaminants.
- **2 studies**: routine methods **supplemented with an additional regimen** achieved adequate decontamination.
- **1 study**: routine methods alone were sufficient for adequate decontamination.
- **NO** true clinical studies were identified.
- **NO** studies reported adverse consequences (infection, bone loss, mechanical complications, implant failure, or patient harm) from reuse of HAs or cover screws.
- **Conclusion**: limited evidence suggests routine methods may not completely remove contaminants; the biological/mechanical implication remains undetermined; indiscriminate reuse should be avoided until further evidence exists.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Evidence base is entirely observational, small (6 studies, >300 HAs), and heterogeneous in decontamination protocol; no meta-analysis performed.
- No clinical outcome studies — the clinical significance of residual contamination is unknown; absence of reported adverse events is not the same as demonstrated safety.
- Future work: controlled clinical studies correlating residual contamination with peri-implant biological/mechanical outcomes, and standardized decontamination protocols.

## 7. Glossary

- **Healing abutment (HA)**: transmucosal component placed on an implant during second-stage healing to shape peri-implant soft tissue; frequently removed and, in practice, reused.
- **Cover screw**: low-profile screw sealing the implant platform during submerged (two-stage) healing.
- **Indiscriminate reuse**: reuse without a validated decontamination protocol or patient-specific justification — the practice this review cautions against.
- **Observational study**: non-experimental study describing existing conditions (here, contamination on retrieved HAs) without a randomized intervention.
