## Why Ingested

위키에 clear-aligner 논문 52편이 있으나 attachment의 **회전(rotation)·정출(extrusion) 정확도**를 직접 정량화한 논문은 드물다 (대부분 FEA/기전 연구이거나 distalization·확장 등 다른 이동에 초점). 본 SR+MA(PROSPERO CRD42024546354, PRISMA 2020, 4편 정량통합)는 optimized vs conventional attachment를 canine derotation·전치부 extrusion·root angulation에서 직접 head-to-head 비교한 이 주제 첫 체계적 문헌고찰로, [[wiki/orthodontics/clear-aligner/nucera-2022-composite-attachments-clear-aligners-sr]]이 "extrusion 근거 없음"으로 지적한 gap을 정량 수치로 채운다. Canine derotation 정확도(61.2%/71.5%)는 [[wiki/orthodontics/clear-aligner/bhate-2025-cat-maxillary-molar-distalization-class-ii-sr]]의 대구치 derotation 정확도(62.7–77.5%)와 비교 맥락을 이룬다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024546354, PRISMA 2020; 7 studies qualitative synthesis / 4 quantitative meta-analysis; n=526 patients, ages 11–67, predominantly retrospective cohorts + 1 RCT) comparing optimized vs conventional clear aligner attachments for accuracy/efficacy of tooth movement.

No significant mean-difference in accuracy between optimized and conventional attachments for canine derotation (SMD −0.31, p=0.06, I²=0%) or anterior teeth extrusion (SMD 0.14, p=0.60, I²=76%); pooled accuracy was 61.2% (conventional) vs 71.5% (optimized) for canine derotation and 57.5% (conventional) vs 62.4% (optimized) for anterior extrusion — neither attachment type achieved the ClinCheck-predicted movement.

Optimized attachments show no proven accuracy advantage over conventional attachments for the movements studied, though they may shorten treatment time and provide root control via 3D bevel design; evidence is low-to-moderate certainty (mostly retrospective cohorts, high heterogeneity for extrusion), so attachment-type selection should not be based on accuracy alone.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 + 메타분석(PROSPERO CRD42024546354, PRISMA 2020; 정성종합 7편/정량 메타분석 4편; n=526명, 11–67세, 대부분 후향적 코호트 + RCT 1편) — 투명교정 optimized vs conventional attachment의 치아이동 정확도·효율 비교.

Canine derotation(SMD −0.31, p=0.06, I²=0%)과 전치부 extrusion(SMD 0.14, p=0.60, I²=76%) 모두 optimized·conventional 간 유의한 평균차 없음; 통합 정확도는 canine derotation에서 conventional 61.2% vs optimized 71.5%, 전치부 extrusion에서 conventional 57.5% vs optimized 62.4% — 어느 쪽도 ClinCheck 예측 이동량을 달성하지 못함.

Optimized attachment는 연구된 이동에서 정확도 우위가 입증되지 않았으나 치료기간 단축·3D bevel 기반 치근 조절 이점 가능성이 있음; 근거는 낮음~중등도 확실성(대부분 후향적, extrusion 이질성 높음)이라 attachment 선택을 정확도만으로 결정해서는 안 됨.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review to directly compare **optimized vs conventional Invisalign attachments** for efficacy/accuracy across multiple tooth-movement types (canine/premolar derotation, anterior extrusion, incisor intrusion, root angulation, mesiodistal angulation, vestibulolingual inclination).
- Quantitative meta-analysis (random-effects, DerSimonian-Laird) of 4 studies for canine derotation and anterior extrusion, reporting pooled accuracy percentages and SMDs with heterogeneity/certainty grading (GRADE).
- Confirms across attachment types that **neither optimized nor conventional attachments achieve the ClinCheck-predicted movement**, reframing "which attachment is more accurate" as secondary to "aligners under-express predicted movement regardless of attachment type."
- Identifies a movement-specific asymmetry: optimized attachments trend better for **rotation control** (esp. upper lateral incisor derotation, root control via 3D bevel), while larger/sharper conventional attachments trend better for **extrusion** in at least one study — suggesting attachment choice may be movement-specific rather than uniformly favoring "optimized."

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: PRISMA 2020 systematic review + meta-analysis; PROSPERO CRD42024546354.
- **PICO**: Adult Invisalign patients; optimized attachments vs conventional attachments; outcome = mean efficacy (predicted − achieved) and mean percentage accuracy.
- **Databases**: Cochrane Library, PubMed, Web of Science, SCOPUS, Google Scholar, trial registries; manual/hand search assisted by a librarian; no language filter; studies to January 2025.
- **Screening**: RAYYAN (AI deduplication tool); two independent reviewers (title/abstract κ=0.89, full-text and data extraction κ=0.88); disagreements resolved by a third reviewer.
- **Search yield**: 185 records → 141 duplicates removed → 44 title/abstract screened → 31 excluded → 13 full-text reviewed → **7 studies** included for qualitative synthesis, **4** for quantitative meta-analysis (IPR studies, no-comparison studies, in-vitro studies, and 3 systematic reviews excluded).
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB-2 for the single RCT (concerns re: deviations from intended intervention and missing outcome data); Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) cohort tool for the 6 retrospective studies (all low overall risk of bias), κ=0.86.
- **Data synthesis**: mean percentage accuracy via Kravitz formula — 100 − [(|predicted − achieved|)/|predicted|] × 100; pooled as weighted mean difference / SMD using a random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird) model; I² for heterogeneity; funnel plot for publication bias.
- **Certainty of evidence**: GRADE (GRADEpro GDT software).
- **Population**: 526 patients total, ages 11–67 (177 male, 349 female) across the 7 included studies; movement-type breakdown — 3 studies anterior teeth extrusion, 3 studies rotational movement (canine/premolar), 1 study incisor intrusion, 1 study root angulation.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Meta-analysis (pooled, random-effects):**

| Movement | Studies pooled | SMD | p-value | I² (heterogeneity) | Certainty (GRADE) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canine derotation | 2 | −0.31 | 0.06 (NS) | 0% | Low |
| Anterior teeth extrusion | 3 | 0.14 | 0.60 (NS) | 76% (high) | High |

**Pooled mean accuracy (Kravitz formula, %):**

| Movement | Conventional | Optimized |
|---|---|---|
| Canine derotation | 61.2% | 71.5% |
| Anterior extrusion (upper/lower) | 57.5% | 62.4% |

- Publication bias: funnel plot showed no significant bias for canine rotation; **significant publication bias for anterior teeth extrusion** (driven by one outlier study, Groody et al.).
- Root angulation (1 study, extraction cases): no significant difference (p>0.05) between conventional and optimized attachments for predicted vs actual root angulation changes in adjacent canines/premolars/molars, including after regression adjustment for wear protocol, age, sex, aligner number, and attachment presence.
- Incisor intrusion (1 study): no significant difference between optimized (G5 deep-bite) and conventional attachments; only 33–40% overall accuracy achievable for intrusion regardless of attachment.
- Mesiodistal angulation / vestibulolingual inclination (1 study, Hassanaly et al.): conventional vertical attachments increased mesiodistal angulation; conventional horizontal attachments increased vestibulolingual inclination.
- Context from prior literature cited in Discussion: overall canine derotation accuracy with Invisalign (any attachment) ranges 55–88.8% (vs Kravitz 2008's 35.8% and an earlier review's 29.1–49.7% for premolar/canine derotation); extrusion overall efficacy 54.6–76% regardless of attachment (Kravitz found extrusion the least accurate movement at 29.6%); one extrusion study found conventional attachments more accurate for lateral incisor extrusion (76% vs 62% optimized) attributed to larger size/sharper edges.
- Conclusions (as stated by authors): (1) no significant accuracy differences for most movements, neither attachment type achieves 100% of predicted movement; (2) optimized attachments show better control for upper lateral incisor rotation, conventional may enhance anterior extrusion due to size/edge sharpness; (3) root angulation/MD angulation/VL inclination differences between types are not clinically significant; (4) optimized attachments are biomechanically designed for better force application but superiority remains inconclusive — more high-quality RCTs needed.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Most included studies (6/7) are **retrospective cohort studies** — lower evidence grade than RCTs; only one RCT included, and it had risk-of-bias concerns (deviations from intended intervention, missing outcome data).
- Small number of studies poolable per outcome (2 for canine derotation, 3 for anterior extrusion) — wide confidence intervals, and high heterogeneity (I²=76%) for the extrusion pooled estimate limits interpretability despite the "high" GRADE certainty rating for that outcome.
- Significant publication bias detected for the extrusion meta-analysis.
- Movements with only single-study evidence (intrusion, root angulation, MD angulation/VL inclination) cannot be pooled and rely on one study's findings each.
- Authors explicitly call for parallel-arm RCTs following CONSORT guidelines, controlling for confounders (attachment size/shape, age, gender, wear protocol, number of attachments, race) to determine whether optimized attachments truly outperform conventional attachments.
- As with related Invisalign-accuracy literature, all data derive from the manufacturer's ClinCheck software as the "predicted" reference — a potential circularity/measurement limitation common to this entire evidence base (not specific to this SR, but inherited).

## 7. Glossary

- **ClinCheck**: Align Technology's proprietary software that generates the predicted (planned) tooth position/movement sequence for Invisalign treatment; used as the "predicted" reference in all accuracy calculations.
- **Optimized (SmartForce) attachment**: Align Technology's third-generation attachment, automatically placed by ClinCheck software and shaped to individual tooth morphology, with a distinct active pressure surface and relieved non-active surface.
- **Conventional attachment**: Manually-designed rectangular or ellipsoidal attachment, adjustable by the clinician in ClinCheck Pro for size, prominence, bevel, and position.
- **Kravitz formula**: Accuracy = 100 − [(|predicted − achieved|)/|predicted|] × 100; the standard percentage-accuracy metric in Invisalign outcome literature (Kravitz et al. 2008/2009).
- **Canine derotation**: Correction of a rotated canine tooth around its long axis toward the ClinCheck-planned position.
- **SMD (standardized mean difference)**: Pooled effect-size metric in meta-analysis expressing the difference between two groups in units of pooled standard deviation.
- **I² statistic**: Percentage of total variation across studies attributable to heterogeneity rather than chance; I²=0% indicates no heterogeneity, I²=76% indicates high heterogeneity.
- **PROSPERO**: International prospective register of systematic review protocols.
- **RoB-2**: Revised Cochrane Risk of Bias tool for randomized trials.
- **JBI (Joanna Briggs Institute) cohort tool**: Critical appraisal checklist for cohort studies.
- **GRADE**: Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluation — framework for rating certainty of evidence (very low/low/moderate/high).
