## Why Ingested

Ingested as part of a topical sweep on mandibular/alveolar "nutrient canals" as a radiographic marker of systemic disease. This case-control study is a **negative/contradicting result** relative to the hypertension-association literature: it found NO significant difference in nutrient-canal incidence between hypertensive (37.5%) and normotensive (53.1%) subjects (P = 0.209), directly contradicting [[radiology/kumar-2014-incidence-nutrient-canals-hypertensive]], which reported a significantly higher incidence of nutrient canals in hypertensive patients. Ingesting both papers together surfaces this conflict in the wiki rather than letting a single-paper narrative stand unchallenged.

## Three-line Summary

Case-control study (n=64: 32 hypertensive patients, 32 normotensive controls, Isfahan, Iran) examining periapical radiographs of the mandibular anterior cuspid–central incisor region for nutrient canal (NC) presence.

Nutrient canal incidence was 37.5% in hypertensive patients vs 53.1% in normotensive subjects — not statistically significant (P = 0.209); no association with HTN duration (P = 0.292) or control status (P = 0.144); NC presence correlated with older age overall (47.1 vs 42.6 years, P = 0.002).

Contradicts the "NC as a hypertension diagnostic clue" claim from other studies (e.g. Mani et al., and by extension the general narrative echoed in Kumar 2014-type findings); this paper aligns instead with Yilmaz et al. and Patni et al., who also found no NC–HTN association — the literature on this marker is genuinely split, not just underpowered.

영양관 발생률은 고혈압군 37.5% vs 정상혈압군 53.1%로 통계적으로 유의하지 않음 (P = 0.209); 고혈압 유병기간(P = 0.292)·조절 여부(P = 0.144)와도 무관; 전체 인구에서 NC 존재군이 더 고령(47.1 vs 42.6세, P = 0.002).

"영양관이 고혈압 진단 단서가 된다"는 일부 문헌(Mani et al. 등, Kumar 2014류 결과와 유사한 서사)과 정면으로 배치되며, NC-고혈압 무관을 보고한 Yilmaz et al.·Patni et al.과는 일치함 — 이 방사선학적 지표에 대한 문헌은 단순히 검정력 부족이 아니라 실제로 결과가 갈리는 상태.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Mandibular Anterior Nutrient Canals in Periapical Radiography in Relation to Hypertension
- **Authors**: Abdar-Esfahani M, Mehdizade M
- **Journal**: Nephro-Urology Monthly, 2013;6(1):e15292
- **DOI**: 10.5812/numonthly.15292 | **PMID**: 24719810 | **PMCID**: PMC3968996
- **Study type**: Case-control study (32 hypertensive vs 32 normotensive; radiologists blinded to clinical status)
- **Setting**: Cardiovascular research center and dental school, Isfahan University of Medical Sciences, Isfahan, Iran (ethics project no. 82100)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Blinded, prospectively-recruited case-control design (radiologists unaware of hypertensive/normotensive status) testing the specific claim that nutrient canal (NC) appearance on periapical radiographs can serve as a diagnostic clue for hypertension.
- Reports a **null result** with adequate sample-size justification (a priori power calculation, d=0.3, α=0.05, β=0.1), adding a well-designed negative study to a literature that is otherwise mixed between positive (Mani et al.) and negative (Yilmaz et al., Patni et al.) findings.
- Identifies age, not hypertension, as the variable significantly associated with NC presence — reframes NC as more plausibly an age-related anatomic/vascular finding than a hypertension marker.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Case-control. Hypertensive patients recruited from a cardiovascular research center; normotensive controls recruited from a dental school population presenting for dental problems.
- **Sample**: 64 participants aged 30–55; 32 confirmed hypertensive (cardiologist-diagnosed), 32 normotensive (simple randomization). Excluded: diabetes, periodontal disease, tuberculosis, rickets, pregnancy.
- **Imaging**: Periapical radiographs (Kodak E-speed film, long-cone bisecting-angle technique, 70 kVp/10 mA) of the mandibular anterior cuspid–central incisor region. Radiologists blinded to hypertensive/normotensive status.
- **Blood pressure assessment**: Standardized mercury sphygmomanometer, right arm, after 15 min rest, excluding recent tea/coffee/smoking/full bladder/exercise. HTN defined as ≥140/90 mmHg (Korotkoff phase 1 systolic, phase 5 diastolic).
- **Statistics**: SPSS v21; chi-square and Fisher's exact tests; significance at P < 0.05. Sample size pre-calculated (d=0.3, P1=0.35, P2=0.05, α=0.05, β=0.1).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **NC incidence**: hypertensive 37.5% (12/32) vs normotensive 53.1% (17/32); total 45.3% (29/64) — **not significant** (P = 0.209).
- **NC by sex within hypertensive group**: male 39.1% vs female 33.3% (P = 0.761) — no significant sex association. Normotensive male vs female also not significant (P = 0.804).
- **NC and HTN chronicity**: no association with duration of hypertension (P = 0.292) or blood-pressure control status (P = 0.144).
- **NC and age**: whole-cohort mean age with NC present 47.1 ± 3.2 y vs NC absent 42.6 ± 5.1 y (P = 0.002); within normotensive subgroup specifically, NC-present mean age 45.9 vs NC-absent 39.2 (P < 0.001). Within the hypertensive subgroup the age difference (48.8 vs 45.1) did not reach significance (P = 0.06).
- Baseline metabolic characteristics (BMI, fasting blood sugar, total cholesterol, triglycerides, HDL, LDL) were comparable between hypertensive and normotensive groups (all P > 0.05), ruling out confounding by these variables.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Small sample (n=64) despite a priori power calculation — a case-control study of this size cannot rule out a smaller true effect, though the direction of the point estimate (lower NC incidence in HTN, not higher) argues against a simple "underpowered positive" explanation.
- Cross-sectional radiographic assessment; no verification of NC nature (vascular canal vs artifact) via CBCT or histology — 2D periapical film has known limitations for identifying fine radiolucent canal structures.
- Excludes diabetes/periodontal disease/tuberculosis/rickets — improves internal validity for the HTN question but limits generalizability to real-world comorbid populations.
- Single-center Iranian cohort (Isfahan) aged 30–55; broader age range and multi-center replication needed.
- The paper's own discussion narrative ("frequency of nutrient canals ... in patients with HTN was more than those normotensive participants") appears to describe the opposite direction from its own Table 2 data (HTN 37.5% < normotensive 53.1%); readers should rely on the reported statistics (Table 2, P = 0.209) rather than the discussion's directional wording.

## 6. Related Work

- **Contradicts** [[radiology/kumar-2014-incidence-nutrient-canals-hypertensive]] — Kumar 2014 reports a significantly higher incidence of nutrient canals in hypertensive patients; this paper's null result (and even numerically lower NC incidence in the HTN group) directly conflicts with that finding.
- Cites and aligns with Yilmaz et al. and Patni et al., both reporting no NC–hypertension association.
- Cites Mani et al. and Patsakas et al. as representative of the positive-association camp (higher NC prevalence in hypertensive patients; sex-dependent findings), the same camp this paper's results push back against.
- Cites Bilge et al. for age-related NC prevalence, consistent with this paper's own age finding.

## 7. Glossary

- **Nutrient canal (NC)**: a radiolucent, mostly vertically-oriented canal-like structure seen on mandibular anterior periapical radiographs, thought to contain blood vessels/nerves; also called vascular channel, circulatory canal, or interdental canal.
- **HTN**: hypertension, defined here as blood pressure ≥140/90 mmHg.
- **Korotkoff phase 1 / phase 5**: auscultatory landmarks used to define systolic (first sound, phase 1) and diastolic (disappearance of sound, phase 5) blood pressure.
- **Case-control study**: observational design comparing an exposure/finding (here, NC presence) between a disease group (hypertensive) and a control group (normotensive), recruited based on outcome status.
