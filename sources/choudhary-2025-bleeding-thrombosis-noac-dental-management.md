## Why Ingested

기존 위키의 항응고제 근거는 출혈(bleeding) 쪽에 치우쳐 있는데 — [[drug/anticoagulants/xiang-2026-continuous-interrupted-doac-minimal-bleeding-sr-ma]]는 지속 vs 중단의 출혈 결과를, [[drug/anticoagulants/dou-2025-bleeding-dentoalveolar-doac-vka-sr-ma]]는 직접경구항응고제 (Direct Oral Anticoagulant, DOAC) vs 비타민K길항제 (Vitamin K Antagonist, VKA) 출혈을 다룬다 — 이 narrative review는 **반대편 팔인 혈전색전 위험**과 약동학(pharmacokinetics) 기반 최저혈중농도(trough) 타이밍을 명시적으로 저울에 올린다. 단, Cureus 게재 서술형 종설이라 근거 등급은 이 카테고리에서 가장 낮으므로 위 SR/MA들을 대체하는 근거가 아니라 *의사결정 틀*로만 사용한다.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative evidence synthesis (Cureus, 2025) of perioperative dental management in patients on novel oral anticoagulants (NOACs/DOACs); structured search of PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane, and Scopus to October 2025 yielded 412 de-duplicated records → 148 full-text → 86 included (RCTs, cohorts, SRs, consensus documents), explicitly non-PRISMA.

Synthesis position: uninterrupted NOAC therapy is supported for low-to-moderate-risk dental procedures when paired with local hemostatics (cited prospective extraction series: clinically significant bleeding 6.9%, all managed conservatively with no transfusion, hospitalization, or thromboembolic event; cited SR of 7 studies/931 patients found minor bleeding predominant; local tranexamic acid associated with 7% lower oral-bleeding risk); for higher-risk surgery the authors propose trough-timed omission of one dose for twice-daily agents and two for once-daily, without bridging.

Key limitation: this is a Cureus narrative review, not a systematic review — no meta-analysis, no risk-of-bias appraisal, no PRISMA flow, and its own limitations section concedes that most underlying evidence is observational with heterogeneous bleeding definitions; its recommendations must not be read as stronger than the SR/MA evidence already held in this category.

## 세줄요약

Cureus 게재 서술형 근거종합(narrative evidence synthesis, 2025) — 신경구항응고제 (Novel Oral Anticoagulant, NOAC = DOAC) 복용 환자의 치과 시술 전후 관리; PubMed/MEDLINE·Embase·Cochrane·Scopus를 2025년 10월까지 검색해 중복 제거 412건 → 전문검토 148건 → 최종 86건 포함(RCT·코호트·체계적문헌고찰·합의문), PRISMA 비준수임을 명시.

핵심 주장: 저-중위험 치과 시술은 국소 지혈(local hemostasis)을 병용하면 NOAC를 **중단하지 않고** 진행 가능 — 인용된 전향 발치 연구에서 임상적 유의 출혈 6.9%로 전부 보존적 관리(수혈·입원 없음, 혈전색전 사건 0건), 인용 SR(7편·931명)에서 주된 합병증은 경미 출혈, 국소 트라넥삼산 (Tranexamic Acid, TXA)은 구강 출혈 위험 7% 감소; 고위험 수술에서만 최저혈중농도에 맞춰 1일2회 제제는 1회분·1일1회 제제는 2회분을 거르되 브리징(bridging)은 하지 않는다.

한계: Cureus 서술형 종설로 메타분석·비뚤림위험 평가·PRISMA 흐름도가 없고 저자들 스스로 근거 대부분이 관찰연구이며 출혈 정의가 이질적이라고 인정 — 위키가 이미 보유한 SR/MA보다 강한 근거로 읽어서는 안 되며, 임상 의사결정 *틀*로만 활용할 것.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Frames dental NOAC management as a **two-sided balance**, giving the thromboembolic arm equal weight to the bleeding arm, rather than treating bleeding as the only outcome of interest.
- Ties the interruption decision explicitly to **pharmacokinetics and renal function**: half-life, renal clearance fraction, and dosing frequency determine how many doses (if any) to omit and when to schedule the appointment.
- Proposes a tiered procedural risk framework (low: supragingival scaling, routine restorations, single extraction → moderate: multiple extractions, single implant → high: extensive flap surgery, multiple implants) cross-tabulated against CHA₂DS₂-VASc thrombotic risk.
- Consolidates adjuncts into one framework: local hemostatics (topical TXA, gelatin sponge, oxidized cellulose, primary closure), minimally invasive technique (flapless implant placement, piezoelectric osteotomy, diode/erbium laser), and reversal agents as emergency-only.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: narrative evidence synthesis with a structured search; the authors state core PRISMA *principles* (structured search, predefined eligibility, dual independent screening, transparent selection reporting) were followed but that this is **not** a full PRISMA systematic review.
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, Scopus — inception to **October 2025**. No language restriction; non-English articles with English abstracts screened.
- **Search string**: ("direct oral anticoagulants" OR "NOAC" OR "DOAC" OR dabigatran OR rivaroxaban OR apixaban OR edoxaban) AND (dentistry OR "oral surgery" OR extraction OR implant OR periodontal) AND (bleeding OR hemorrhage OR thrombosis OR perioperative OR interruption OR reversal OR hemostasis).
- **Supplementary sources**: hand-searched reference lists; guidelines from the American Dental Association, European Society of Cardiology, and International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis; conference abstracts (AAOMS, IADR); targeted (non-exhaustive) grey literature.
- **Inclusion**: RCTs, prospective/retrospective cohorts, SRs, meta-analyses, clinical guidelines, expert consensus documents on perioperative dental management of NOAC/DOAC patients; last-decade publications prioritized.
- **Exclusion**: case reports (unless illustrating rare but critical complications), in vitro/animal-only studies, PK studies unrelated to dental procedures, articles without perioperative relevance, insufficient methodological detail.
- **Screening**: two independent reviewers on titles/abstracts; discrepancies resolved by consensus.
- **No quantitative synthesis**: no meta-analysis, no pooled effect estimates, no formal risk-of-bias or GRADE appraisal of included studies.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Article flow**

- 412 records after de-duplication → 148 selected for full-text assessment → **86 included**, 62 excluded (insufficient relevance, case-report-only evidence, or lack of NOAC-specific perioperative data).

**Bleeding figures cited from the included literature** (these are secondary citations, not new data)

- Prospective NOAC tooth-extraction study: clinically significant bleeding in **<10% of cases (6.9%)**, all managed conservatively — no transfusion, no hospitalization, and **no thromboembolic complications**.
- Prospective series of **49 patients** on rivaroxaban/apixaban/edoxaban/dabigatran: bleeding episodes more frequent with rivaroxaban and apixaban.
- López-Galindo and Grau-Benítez SR: **7 studies, 931 patients** — minor bleeding (immediate or delayed) was the predominant postoperative complication for both NOAC and VKA patients after uncomplicated extraction; interruption/modification judged unnecessary for simple extractions.
- Kwak et al. retrospective study, **120 NOAC patients** — most dental treatment performed safely without increased bleeding likelihood.
- Miclotte et al. prospective study, **26 NOAC patients vs 26 controls** — skipping the morning NOAC dose avoided excessive intra- and postoperative bleeding.
- Curto et al. — interruption of edoxaban generally not necessary for minor oral surgery.
- Local TXA SR: **7% lower risk** of oral bleeding with local tranexamic acid therapy.
- RCT comparisons of continued vs briefly interrupted regimens: **no meaningful difference in major bleeding**; minor seepage modestly increased with continued therapy.

**Timing / interval recommendations proposed by the authors**

- Twice-daily agents (apixaban, dabigatran): omit **one** dose when omission is indicated — holding the morning dose typically allows sufficient clearance by the following morning in normal renal function.
- Once-daily agents (rivaroxaban, edoxaban): omit **two** doses / allow a longer interval to reach trough; schedule in the **morning after skipping the prior evening dose**.
- High-risk extensive surgery: consider a **24–48 hour** preoperative pause plus postoperative TXA prophylaxis, with multidisciplinary planning.
- Postoperative **24–48 hour** follow-up to reduce rebleeding.
- **No bridging anticoagulation** — bridging carries its own hemorrhagic burden.

**Risk-stratification thresholds**

- Thrombotic risk: **CHA₂DS₂-VASc** in nonvalvular atrial fibrillation — higher scores indicate greater stroke vulnerability during brief interruption. The paper describes "elevated" scores as the trigger for brief dose omission at moderate procedural risk but **states no numeric cut-off**.
- Bleeding risk: **HAS-BLED** acknowledged as developed for spontaneous (not procedural) bleeding — described as a "useful, albeit imperfect" framework because components (hypertension, renal dysfunction, prior hemorrhage) overlap with procedural risk factors. **No numeric threshold given.**
- Renal function: preoperative **creatinine clearance (CrCl)** assessment required; dabigatran contraindicated at CrCl < 30 mL/min; edoxaban dose-reduced to 30 mg OD at CrCl 15–50 mL/min or low body weight.

**Pharmacokinetic table (agent → half-life / renal clearance / bioavailability / Tmax / reversal)**

- **Dabigatran** — direct thrombin (IIa) inhibitor; 150 mg BID (110 or 75 mg BID in some cases); t½ **12–17 h**; renal **~80%**; bioavailability ~6–7% (prodrug); Tmax ~1–2 h; reversal **idarucizumab**; trial RE-LY. Longest preoperative pause when CrCl reduced.
- **Rivaroxaban** — factor Xa inhibitor; 20 mg OD; t½ **5–13 h**; renal **~33%**; bioavailability ~80–100% with food; Tmax ~2.5–4 h; reversal **andexanet alfa or PCC**; trial ROCKET-AF.
- **Apixaban** — factor Xa inhibitor; 5 mg BID (2.5 mg BID if dose-reduction criteria); t½ **~12 h**; renal **~25%** (least renal dependence); bioavailability ~50%; Tmax ~3–4 h; reversal **andexanet alfa or PCC**; trial ARISTOTLE. Dental work often possible without interruption.
- **Edoxaban** — factor Xa inhibitor; 60 mg OD (30 mg OD if CrCl 15–50 mL/min or low weight); t½ **10–14 h**; renal **~50%**; bioavailability ~62%; Tmax ~1–2 h; reversal **andexanet alfa or PCC**; trial ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48.

**Reversal agents**

- Idarucizumab (dabigatran) neutralizes by active-site binding, restoring thrombin generation within minutes; dental-specific use **anecdotal**, reserved for uncontrolled postoperative oral bleeding refractory to local measures.
- Andexanet alfa (apixaban, edoxaban, rivaroxaban); ciraparantag (apixaban, rivaroxaban) limited by high cost, short duration, and rebound anticoagulation; prothrombin complex concentrates (PCC) as alternative when andexanet unavailable, but dental evidence sparse.
- **Routine reversal before elective procedures is unwarranted and potentially pro-thrombotic.**

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Journal and design tier**: Cureus is a low-barrier, author-pays journal, and this is a *narrative* synthesis — no meta-analysis, no pooled estimates, no formal risk-of-bias or GRADE appraisal, and no PRISMA flow diagram. Within this wiki's anticoagulant holdings it is the **weakest evidence tier**, below the SR/MA pages it overlaps with.
- **Citation-integrity problem in the retrieved text**: reference brackets are empty in the PMC full text (rendered as `[]`), so most quantitative claims above **cannot be traced to a specific primary study** from this article alone. Several cited figures (e.g., the 6.9% bleeding rate) coincide with numbers reported elsewhere in this category and should be verified against the primary source before use.
- **Author-conceded limitations**: most underlying studies observational, few RCTs powered for dental endpoints; heterogeneity in procedural definitions, bleeding classification, and NOAC regimens; long-term thrombotic outcomes after brief interruption underreported; renal impairment, polypharmacy, and antiplatelet co-administration underrepresented in trials; procedure-specific bleeding risk not granularly quantified; no cost-effectiveness evaluation.
- **No numeric thresholds** are supplied for CHA₂DS₂-VASc or HAS-BLED despite both being invoked as decision anchors — the "elevated score" trigger is left to clinician judgment.
- Some prose in the retrieved text is garbled (e.g., a truncated sentence in the dabigatran pharmacology paragraph), suggesting limited copy-editing.
- **Future work** proposed by the authors: prospective registries with standardized outcomes; randomized comparisons of continuation versus tailored interruption; validation of adjunctive technologies (flapless, piezoelectric, laser).

## 7. Glossary

- **NOAC (Novel Oral Anticoagulant)**: synonym used in this paper for DOAC — dabigatran, rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban.
- **DOAC (Direct Oral Anticoagulant)**: anticoagulant directly inhibiting a single coagulation factor — thrombin/IIa (dabigatran) or factor Xa (rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban).
- **CHA₂DS₂-VASc**: stroke-risk score in nonvalvular atrial fibrillation (congestive heart failure, hypertension, age ≥75 [2 pts], diabetes, stroke/TIA [2 pts], vascular disease, age 65–74, sex category).
- **HAS-BLED**: bleeding-risk score — hypertension, abnormal renal/liver function, stroke, bleeding history, labile INR, elderly (>65), drugs/alcohol. Developed for *spontaneous*, not procedural, bleeding.
- **Trough level**: the minimum plasma drug concentration in a dosing interval, immediately before the next dose — the window targeted for surgical scheduling.
- **Bridging**: substituting a short-acting parenteral anticoagulant (e.g., heparin) during oral-anticoagulant interruption; **not recommended** for dental procedures here.
- **TXA (Tranexamic Acid)**: antifibrinolytic; topical mouthwash inhibits fibrinolysis at the surgical site with minimal systemic absorption.
- **Idarucizumab / andexanet alfa / ciraparantag**: specific NOAC reversal agents (dabigatran / factor Xa inhibitors / factor Xa inhibitors, respectively).
- **PCC (Prothrombin Complex Concentrate)**: nonspecific reversal option containing vitamin K-dependent clotting factors.
- **CrCl (Creatinine Clearance)**: renal function measure governing NOAC elimination, dose reduction, and preoperative pause length.
- **Piezoelectric surgery**: ultrasonic bone cutting that spares soft tissue and reduces intraoperative blood loss versus rotary instrumentation.
- **Flapless implant placement**: implant placement without periosteal reflection, preserving vascular integrity.
