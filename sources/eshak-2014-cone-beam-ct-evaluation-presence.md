## Why Ingested

이 논문은 위키의 하악전치부 영양관(Nutrient Canal, NC) 시리즈([[radiology/kaur-2019-evaluation-mandibular-anterior-nutrient]], [[radiology/hasan-2022-prevalence-nutrient-canals-mandibular]], [[radiology/wamasing-2018-imaging-bifid-mandibular-canal]]) 대비 압도적으로 큰 표본(4051 CBCT 스캔)으로 상하악 전체의 부관(Accessory Canal, AC) 유병률을 평가한다 — 기존 시리즈가 IOPAR·MRI 기반 하악전치부 국소 평가에 그친 것과 달리, CBCT 기반 상하악 전영역 스크리닝이라는 방법론적 확장이다. 성별 분포(여성 42.9% vs 남성)와 상악/하악 비율(하악 29.8% > 상악 13.1%)이라는 대규모 기준값을 제공해 술전 수술 리스크 평가에 참고 가능.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective analysis of 4051 cone beam CT (CBCT) scans (2306 females, 1745 males, ages 7-88) from the University of Michigan School of Dentistry, evaluating the prevalence, location, and anatomical course of accessory canals (ACs) in the maxilla and mandible.

Accessory canals were identified in 1737 scans overall (42.9%; 1004 females, 733 males) — 532 in the maxilla (13.1%) and 1205 in the mandible (29.8%) — revealing a network of accessory canals connecting the inner and outer cortical plates of the jaws.

Given the high prevalence (especially in the mandible), clinicians should carefully assess for accessory canals prior to surgical intervention to reduce complication risk; this is an abstract-only source (full text not retrieved — PMC returned an empty body for this DMFR article), so methodological detail beyond the abstract (e.g., exact scan protocol, statistical testing, per-region breakdown) is unavailable.

## 세줄요약

미시간대학교 치과대학에서 촬영된 4051건의 콘빔 CT(Cone Beam CT, CBCT) 스캔(여성 2306명, 남성 1745명, 7-88세)을 후향적으로 분석하여 상하악 부관(Accessory Canal, AC)의 유병률·위치·주행 경로를 평가한 연구.

전체 스캔의 42.9%(1737건; 여성 1004건, 남성 733건)에서 부관이 확인되었고, 상악 532건(13.1%), 하악 1205건(29.8%)이었으며, 하악골의 내외측 피질골판을 연결하는 부관 네트워크가 확인되었다.

특히 하악에서 높은 유병률을 고려할 때 수술적 처치 전 부관 존재 여부를 신중히 평가해 합병증 위험을 줄여야 하나, 본 페이지는 초록만을 근거로 작성되었다(PMC가 이 DMFR 논문에 대해 빈 본문을 반환하여 전문 미확보) — 정확한 스캔 프로토콜·통계 검정·부위별 세부 분석 등은 확인 불가.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Largest known sample in the wiki's accessory/nutrient canal literature (n=4051 qualifying CBCT scans, from 4200 successive scans after exclusions).
- Establishes CBCT-based (rather than 2D IOPAR or MRI) prevalence figures for accessory canals across the entire jaw (maxilla + mandible), not just the mandibular anterior region.
- Reports a maxilla vs mandible split (13.1% vs 29.8%) and overall gender breakdown (1004/2306 females = 43.5%; 733/1745 males = 42.0%), suggesting no large gender disparity in overall prevalence despite the raw counts skewing female (larger female sample size, n=2306 vs 1745).
- Describes a network of accessory canals bridging the inner and outer cortical plates — relevant to surgical/implant planning to avoid vascular injury.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Retrospective analysis of 4200 successive CBCT scans at the School of Dentistry, University of Michigan, Ann Arbor, MI, patients aged 7-88 years, both genders. Exclusion criteria: severe ridge resorption, pre-existing implants, previously reported craniofacial malformations/syndromes, previous trauma/surgery history, inadequate image quality, and repeat scans from the same individual. After exclusions, 4051 scans qualified (2306 females, 1745 males). Scans were evaluated for the presence, location (maxilla vs mandible), and anatomical course of accessory canals. No further detail (CBCT unit/settings, voxel size, reader calibration, statistical test) is available from the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Overall AC prevalence: 1737/4051 scans (42.9%) — 1004 females, 733 males.
- Maxillary AC: 532 scans (13.1% of total) — 296 females, 236 males.
- Mandibular AC: 1205 scans (29.8% of total) — 708 females, 497 males.
- Mandible >> maxilla in AC prevalence (29.8% vs 13.1%).
- A network of accessory canals connecting inner and outer cortical plates was identified.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Abstract-only source: PMC returned an empty full-text body for this DMFR article (known DMFR-journal PMC quirk); no access to detailed methodology, statistical significance testing, subregion mapping, or discussion/interpretation beyond the abstract's summary statement.
- No inferential statistics reported in the abstract (no p-values comparing maxilla vs mandible or gender subgroups) — the 42.9%/13.1%/29.8% figures are descriptive prevalence only.
- Retrospective single-institution (University of Michigan) design; generalizability to other populations/CBCT protocols unverified.
- Does not report canal caliber, precise anatomical named-canal breakdown (e.g., incisive canal vs lingual vascular canals vs interforaminal canals — mentioned only as keywords, not detailed in the abstract text itself).

## 7. Glossary

- **Accessory canal (AC)**: a secondary bony canal in the jaws carrying neurovascular structures, distinct from the main mandibular canal or incisive canal.
- **Cone beam CT (CBCT)**: 3D dental/maxillofacial imaging modality providing cross-sectional visualization superior to 2D radiographs for detecting fine bony canals.
- **Nutrient canal**: a radiolucent channel through alveolar/cortical bone carrying vessels/nerves; used near-interchangeably with "accessory canal" in this literature.
- **Cortical plate**: the outer (buccal/labial) or inner (lingual/palatal) compact bone layer of the jaw.
