## Why Ingested

PRISMA-compliant SR (n=412, 9 studies, ROBINS-I) comparing clear aligner mandibular advancement (CAMA) to conventional functional appliances for skeletal Class II malocclusion in growing patients. Added to reinforce and corroborate [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/huang-2026-clear-aligner-mandibular-advancement-vs-functional-class-ii-sr-ma]], the primary SR+MA on this question (n=465, full text). Although Huang 2026 remains higher quality (meta-analysis, PROSPERO), this European-context SR independently arrives at the same equivalence conclusion, strengthening the evidence base from a different research group and journal.

## Three-line Summary

PRISMA SR (726 records → 9 studies, n=412 growing skeletal Class II patients; PubMed/ScienceDirect/Cochrane/Google Scholar, 2020–2025; PICO framework; ROBINS-I RoB) comparing clear aligners with mandibular advancement to conventional functional orthopedic appliances via cephalometric analysis.

Both aligner mandibular advancement and conventional functional appliances showed similar skeletal and dental correction — consistent reductions in overbite and overjet, increases in SNB, and decreases in ANB and Wits appraisal — with no significant differences reported across these parameters.

Aligners with mandibular advancement are an effective option for treating Class II malocclusions in growing patients with dentoalveolar and skeletal effects equivalent to conventional functional appliances, but methodological limitations and heterogeneity restrict evidence strength; this conclusion reinforces the findings of the larger Huang 2026 SR+MA (n=465).

## Summary

Abstract-only source (PubMed, PMID 42734614). Stomatologija (2026, Vol 28(1):2–9). Lithuanian orthodontic group (Klaipėda) conducted a PRISMA-aligned SR searching PubMed, ScienceDirect, The Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar for studies published 2020–2025 using a PICO framework. ROBINS-I was used for risk-of-bias assessment.

Starting from 726 identified articles, 9 studies involving 412 patients were included after deduplication and eligibility screening. All studies primarily used cephalometric analysis to evaluate skeletal and dental changes pre- and post-treatment.

Key findings from the abstract: despite variability in parameters and methodologies across studies, both aligner mandibular advancement and conventional functional appliances showed "similar skeletal and dental correction." The consistent outcomes were: reductions in overbite and overjet, increases in SNB, and decreases in ANB and Wits appraisal. The authors conclude that aligners with mandibular advancement are "an effective option for treating Class II malocclusions in growing patients" with "similar dentoalveolar and skeletal effects." Methodological limitations and heterogeneity among studies restrict the strength of the evidence.

This SR independently corroborates Huang 2026 SR+MA (9 studies, n=465, PROSPERO-registered, full-text, with quantitative meta-analysis), which also found no statistically significant difference in any sagittal skeletal parameter between CAMA and functional appliances. The present paper adds the perspective of a European clinical group and a different search window (2020–2025 only, vs Huang 2026's comprehensive historical search).
