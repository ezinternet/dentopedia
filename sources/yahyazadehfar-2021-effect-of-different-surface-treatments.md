## Why Ingested

Compares four surface treatment modalities for graded zirconia — control, sandblasting (SA), grinding with diamond bur (GB), and Er,Cr:YSGG laser (LS) — measuring surface roughness (Ra/Rz), monoclinic phase transformation (XRD), and biaxial flexural strength with Weibull analysis. Provides in-vitro evidence directly relevant to chairside zirconia surface preparation before cementation.

→ [[dental-materials/zirconia/yahyazadehfar-2021-effect-of-different-surface-treatments]]

## Three-line Summary

Er,Cr:YSGG laser treatment of graded dental zirconia produced the lowest extent of tetragonal-to-monoclinic phase transformation and the highest Weibull reliability modulus (m = 20.5) among the four tested methods, while mean flexural strength (994.5 MPa) was not significantly different from sandblasting or control. Grinding with a diamond bur generated markedly greater surface roughness (Ra 1.87 µm vs < 0.30 for other groups) and significant monoclinic phase increase, yet yielded the lowest Weibull modulus (12.2), indicating less predictable flaw distribution. No statistically significant correlation was found between either surface roughness or monoclinic phase content and biaxial flexural strength.

## 세줄요약

Er,Cr:YSGG 레이저 처리는 모노클리닉상 변환이 가장 낮고 Weibull 계수(m=20.5)가 가장 높아 굴곡강도 신뢰성이 우수하며, 굴곡강도 평균값(994.5 MPa)은 샌드블라스팅 및 대조군과 유의차 없었다. 버 그라인딩은 표면조도(Ra 1.87 µm)와 모노클리닉상 비율이 가장 높았으나 Weibull 계수(12.2)는 가장 낮아 결함 분포의 예측도가 떨어졌다. 표면조도 및 모노클리닉상 비율과 이축굴곡강도 사이에는 유의한 상관관계가 없었다.

- **Study design**: In-vitro comparative study
- **Sample**: 40 sintered graded zirconia disk-shaped specimens (n=10 per group); Shenzhen Upcera pre-sintered 98-mm block, sintered at 1450 °C
- **Groups**: Control (no treatment), Grinding with diamond bur (GB), Sandblasting with 110-µm Al₂O₃ at 2.5 bar (SA), Er,Cr:YSGG laser 1.5 W / 15 Hz (LS)
- **Outcomes**: Surface roughness Ra/Rz (optical microscope ISO 25178), XRD monoclinic phase %, biaxial flexural strength (ISO 6872:2008), Weibull analysis

## Key Contributions

1. **Er,Cr:YSGG laser as preferred surface treatment**: lowest monoclinic phase transformation (mean 1.2%, comparable to control 1.4%), highest Weibull modulus (m = 20.5), and characteristic strength of 1020.2 MPa.
2. **Diamond bur grinding is most damaging**: Ra 1.87 µm (vs ≤ 0.29 µm for others), monoclinic phase 4.7%, lowest Weibull modulus (12.2), highest flaw distribution variability.
3. **Sandblasting paradox**: despite significant monoclinic phase increase (mean 2.9%), SA yielded the numerically highest flexural strength (1023 MPa, significantly greater than control and GB) — suggesting surface compressive stress benefit can transiently offset phase-transformation penalty.
4. **No roughness–strength correlation**: Pearson's r non-significant for all four groups (P > 0.3), contradicting the assumption that roughening always degrades strength.
5. **Laser power optimisation**: pilot study established 1.5 W as the threshold below which microcracks and discoloration are avoided.

## Methodology

**Specimen fabrication**: Pre-sintered 98-mm zirconia blanks milled via CAD/CAM into 12 mm-diameter × 1.2 mm disks; sintered at 1450 °C; polished with 600/800/1200-grit SiC papers under 10-N load, 300 rpm, ≥ 30 min.

**Surface treatments**:
- **GB**: Diamond bur (108–120 µm particle), high-speed handpiece, 200 kPa, 20 s, water coolant, back-and-forth motion.
- **SA**: 110-µm Al₂O₃ particles, 2.5-bar pressure, 30-mm distance, 30 s.
- **LS**: Er,Cr:YSGG laser 2940 nm, 1.5 W, 15 Hz, 74 µs pulse, 1-mm non-contact, 80% water/80% air, 10 s; power selected via SEM pilot study on 4 extra disks at 1.5/2.25/3/3.75 W.

**Measurements**:
- Surface roughness: Keyence VHX2000 optical microscope, ×1000, ISO 25178; 5 measurements per specimen over 240-µm length; Ra and Rz reported.
- XRD: Rigaku Ultimate IV, 50 kV, 150 mA, 2θ 5–80°, 0.02° step; 3 specimens/group; Vm calculated via Garvie–Nicholson / Toraya equations.
- Biaxial flexural strength: Zwick Roell universal frame, 1000-N load cell, 1 mm/min; ISO 6872:2008 piston-on-3-ball; treated surface under tension.
- Weibull: 2-parameter analysis (Quinn & Quinn method); Weibull modulus m and characteristic strength σ₀ calculated by linear regression.

**Statistics**: One-way ANOVA + post-hoc Tukey (SPSS 22, α = 0.05); Pearson correlation between roughness/monoclinic % and flexural strength.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control | 0.19 ± 0.02 | 1.36 ± 0.14 | 1.4 ± 0.4 | 926.3 ± 65.5 | 16.5 | 955.3 |
| GB | 1.87 ± 0.50 | 7.46 ± 0.71 | 4.7 ± 0.2 | 909.8 ± 87.3 | 12.2 | 947.5 |
| LS | 0.29 ± 0.06 | 1.90 ± 0.37 | 1.2 ± 0.4 | 994.5 ± 56.8 | 20.5 | 1020.2 |
| SA | 0.23 ± 0.04 | 1.60 ± 0.37 | 2.9 ± 0.0* | 1023.0 ± 74.8 | 16.3 | 1023.0 |

\*Note: SA monoclinic SD = 0.0 appears to be a rounding/reporting artefact in the original table; actual range 4.27–4.33%.

- GB Ra and Rz significantly greater than all other groups (P < 0.005); no significant differences among control, LS, SA (P > 0.7).
- SA and GB monoclinic Vm significantly greater than control and LS (P < 0.001).
- SA flexural strength significantly > control (P = 0.02) and GB (P < 0.01); LS not significantly different from any group (P > 0.05).
- Pearson's r: roughness vs strength — non-significant (all groups P > 0.33); monoclinic % vs strength — non-significant (all groups P > 0.39).

## Limitations

- In-vitro only; no aging, fatigue, or thermocycling assessment — clinical durability unknown.
- Small n per group (n = 10 for mechanical; n = 3 for XRD) limits statistical power for phase analysis.
- Single zirconia brand (Upcera graded zirconia) — results may not generalize to 3Y-TZP, 5Y-PSZ, or other compositions.
- SA monoclinic phase SD anomaly (0.0 ± 0.0) suggests possible XRD data-reporting error.
- No adhesive bond-strength testing; surface treatment efficacy for cementation was not assessed.
- Laser pilot study used only visual SEM — no microcrack depth quantification.

## Related Work

- Martins et al. — laser > sandblasting for surface roughness in zirconia (contrasts with this study's finding of no significant difference).
- Kurtulmus-Yilmaz et al. — post-sintered grinding superior Weibull reliability (contradicts GB being lowest here).
- Kosmač et al. — highest Weibull modulus in control group, contradicting SA's strength advantage in this study.
- Çağlar & Yanıkoğlu — sandblasted and laser-treated specimens had higher flexural strength than control, consistent with SA result here.
- Hallmann et al. — sandblasting with 150-µm Al₂O₃ yielded lower flexural strength than control, contrasting with this study's SA finding; difference attributed to cubic-dominant zirconia phase in Hallmann specimens.

## Glossary

- **Ra**: Arithmetical mean surface roughness (µm).
- **Rz**: Mean peak-to-valley height (µm).
- **Vm**: Volumetric percentage of monoclinic phase; calculated from XRD peak intensities via Garvie–Nicholson formula.
- **Weibull modulus (m)**: Shape parameter of flaw-size distribution; higher m = narrower, more uniform flaw distribution = greater strength reliability.
- **Characteristic strength (σ₀)**: Stress at which 63.2% of specimens fail; Weibull scale parameter.
- **Biaxial flexural strength**: Piston-on-3-ball ISO 6872 configuration; treats surface under tension during loading.
- **Er,Cr:YSGG laser**: Erbium, chromium: yttrium-scandium-gallium-garnet, 2940 nm wavelength; ablates material via water-mediated microexplosion mechanism.
- **LTD (Low-Temperature Degradation)**: Hydrothermal aging of Y-TZP causing slow tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation at mouth-temperature moisture — not tested in this study.
