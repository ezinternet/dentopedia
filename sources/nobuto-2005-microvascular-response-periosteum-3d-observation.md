## Why Ingested

User requested an evidence base for blood-supply loss of the periosteum/alveolar bone after periosteal elevation; this paper is the early-phase (days 3–7) evidence — a 3-D ultrastructural characterization of the angiogenic response the periosteal vascular plexus mounts after mucoperiosteal flap elevation in dogs. It supplies the mechanistic healing-response half of the question that [[implants/soft-tissue/kim-2009-blood-vessels-peri-implant-mucosa]] addresses at the outcome level (flapless surgery preserving periosteal vascularity) and that the flap-advancement taxonomy of [[suture-wound-closure/plonka-2017-flap-designs-flap-advancement-implant-therapy]] presumes when full-thickness flaps are elevated and advanced.

## Three-line Summary

Canine experimental study (9 adult beagle dogs) tracking the angiogenic response of the periosteal vascular plexus after mucoperiosteal flap surgery at postoperative days 3, 5, and 7, using India-ink-injected histological specimens under light microscopy, transmission electron microscopy of ultrathin sections, and 3-D scanning electron microscopy of acryl plastic vascular casts.

By day 3, new vessels had formed through sprouting, bridging, and intussusception, plus blood island-like clusters of immature endothelial cells in the repaired tissue; by days 5–7, vascular casts showed the new vessels as sinus-like structures in the interstitium of the periosteal vascular plexus, gaining continuity and thinning their endothelium ultrastructurally as they matured.

The periosteal vasculature exhibits potent vessel-forming and repair activity after flap elevation — 3-D confirmation that the periosteal vascular plexus is a central revascularization engine of flap healing; limits: abstract-only ingestion, canine model, no quantitative blood-flow data.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Provides 3-dimensional clarification — via scanning electron microscopy of acryl plastic vascular casts — that the periosteal vascular plexus has an important role in the healing process after mucoperiosteal flap surgery.
- Documents that new-vessel formation after flap elevation proceeds through multiple concurrent angiogenic mechanisms: sprouting, bridging, and intussusception, with blood island-like structures of clustered immature endothelial cells appearing in the repaired tissue.
- Establishes the early morphological time-course of periosteal revascularization: day-3 sprouting/bridging/intussusceptive vessels and blood-island-like structures → days 5–7 sinus-like sinusoidal vessels with stereoscopic structure, increased continuity, and ultrastructurally thinned endothelium as vessels mature.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Animal experimental study; mucoperiosteal flap surgery performed on the dogs, and the periosteal vascular plexus was examined at postoperative days 3, 5, and 7.
- **n**: 9 adult beagle dogs.
- **Modalities**: (1) histological specimens with blood vessels injected with India ink, under a light microscope; (2) ultrathin sections under a transmission electron microscope; (3) acryl plastic vascular cast specimens under a scanning electron microscope (3-D observation).
- **Outcomes**: morphology and timing of new blood vessel formation in the periosteal vascular plexus (qualitative/ultrastructural endpoints; no quantitative metrics reported in the abstract).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Day 3 (ultrathin sections + vascular casts)**: new blood vessels, formed through sprouting, bridging, and intussusception, were observed; blood island-like structures consisting of clustered immature endothelial cells were noted in the repaired tissue.
- **Days 5–7 (3-D observation of vascular casts)**: the new blood vessels had a sinus-like morphology in the interstitium of the periosteal vascular plexus; these new sinusoidal vessels exhibited a stereoscopic structure with increased continuity as the blood vessels matured, and ultrastructurally the vascular endothelium was thinned.
- **Conclusion**: after mucoperiosteal flap elevation, the periosteal vasculature exhibited potent blood vessel-forming activity through various angiogenic mechanisms and through repair activity.
- No quantitative metrics (vessel counts, perfusion, blood-flow volumes) are reported in the abstract.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only**: paywalled J Periodontol 2005 article with no PMC record — full text not retrieved; per-timepoint group allocation, vessel-density or blood-flow quantification, and any statistical analysis in the body are unavailable and were not reconstructed.
- **Animal model**: adult beagle dogs — the morphology and timing of canine periosteal angiogenesis may not transfer directly to human alveolar bone healing.
- **Qualitative endpoints only**: the abstract reports morphological observation, not quantitative vascular or perfusion outcomes, so this page holds no numeric efficacy claims.
- **Early-phase window only**: observation is limited to postoperative days 3–7; later remodeling and maturation phases are not covered by this abstract.
- The abstract characterizes the periosteal plexus's healing response after flap elevation; it does not directly measure alveolar-bone blood-supply loss from elevation — that quantification must come from other papers in this evidence base.

## 7. Glossary

- **Periosteum (골막)**: the dense fibrous vascular connective-tissue membrane investing bone surfaces; its vascular plexus contributes to bone circulation and, in this study, is the structure that revascularizes the flap bed after elevation.
- **Mucoperiosteal flap (점막골막판)**: a full-thickness flap elevated with the attached periosteum off the underlying bone — the standard access/reflection flap in periodontal and implant surgery.
- **Periosteal vascular plexus (골막 혈관총)**: the network of blood vessels within the periosteum; the object of 3-D observation in this study.
- **Vascular cast / acryl plastic cast (혈관주조)**: vessels perfused with acrylic resin that hardens in the lumen; surrounding tissue is removed, leaving a 3-D replica of the vascular tree for scanning electron microscopy.
- **Sprouting / bridging / intussusception (싹돋움·가교·내감식)**: the three angiogenic mechanisms observed by day 3 — sprouting (new vessel extends as a bud from an existing vessel), bridging (vessels join across a gap), and intussusception (an existing vessel splits internally into new lumens).
- **Blood island (혈액섬)**: a cluster of immature endothelial cells forming an early vascular precursor; "blood island-like structures" were noted in the repaired tissue on day 3.
