## Why Ingested

Companion paper to [[pawlaczyk-kamienska-2026-facial-asymmetry-mandibular-hypomobility-jia-tmj]] (same ingest batch): that narrative review establishes that JIA TMJ involvement is often clinically silent and must be inferred from facial asymmetry/mandibular hypomobility, while this systematic review supplies the imaging-side answer — how well ultrasound (US), a non-radiation, sedation-free, chairside modality suited to children, actually performs against MRI at detecting that silent disease. No prior wiki page quantifies US diagnostic accuracy specifically in the pediatric JIA-TMJ population; [[tenorio-2026-ultrasonography-tmj-rheumatoid-arthritis-scoping-review]] answers the analogous question in adult rheumatoid arthritis, providing a useful population contrast.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA-DTA) of 8 diagnostic-accuracy studies (2007–2025; 312 JIA patients, 6 countries) evaluating B-mode and power Doppler ultrasound (US) against MRI (all 8 studies), CT, or CBCT (none) as the reference standard for temporomandibular joint (TMJ) arthritis in children with Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA).

B-mode US sensitivity ranged 21%–85% and specificity 36.4%–89% depending on which TMJ alteration was assessed (condylar changes, joint effusion, synovial thickness, lateral periarticular space/LPAS); GRADE certainty was LOW for sensitivity and MODERATE for specificity; none of the 8 studies fulfilled all QUADAS-2 criteria and no meta-analysis was possible due to methodological heterogeneity.

The sensitivity floor means a negative US does not rule out TMJ involvement in JIA; the authors' own conclusion frames US as a triage/adjunct tool alongside clinical exam to guide the need for MRI, not a replacement for MRI — attractive mainly because MRI in children often requires sedation and is costly/access-limited, while US is radiation-free, chairside, and repeatable.

## 세줄요약

JIA(소아 특발성 관절염) 환아의 측두하악관절 (Temporomandibular Joint, TMJ) 관절염을 초음파 (Ultrasound, US)로 평가한 진단정확도 체계적 문헌고찰(PRISMA-DTA), 2007~2025년 8편·312명·6개국, 기준표준은 전 연구 MRI(CT·CBCT는 기준표준으로 쓰인 연구 없음).

B-mode 초음파의 민감도(Sensitivity)는 21~85%, 특이도(Specificity)는 36.4~89%로 평가 대상(과두 변화, 관절 삼출, 활막 비후, 외측 관절주위공간/LPAS)에 따라 편차가 큼; GRADE 확실성 등급은 민감도 LOW·특이도 MODERATE; 8편 모두 QUADAS-2 전 항목을 충족하지 못했고 방법론적 이질성으로 메타분석은 불가능했음.

민감도 하한선이 낮다는 것은 초음파 음성 소견이 TMJ 침범을 배제하지 못한다는 뜻이며, 저자들도 초음파를 MRI 대체가 아닌 임상진찰과 병행하는 선별·보조(triage) 도구로 결론짓는다 — 소아 MRI는 진정(sedation)이 필요하고 비용·접근성 문제가 있는 반면, 초음파는 무방사선·체어사이드·반복시행이 가능하다는 것이 매력 요인.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic review to focus specifically on US diagnostic accuracy for TMJ arthritis in the pediatric JIA population (as opposed to adult TMD/RA populations), pooling 8 diagnostic-accuracy studies (312 patients, 2007–2025) with MRI as the uniform reference standard.
- Reports the honest range, not a pooled estimate: B-mode US sensitivity 21%–85%, specificity 36.4%–89%, varying by which TMJ alteration is targeted (condylar changes, joint effusion, synovial thickness/inflammation, LPAS) — explicitly states no meta-analysis was feasible due to heterogeneity in US protocols, transducer frequency, and outcome definitions.
- Assigns formal GRADE certainty: LOW for sensitivity, MODERATE for specificity — flags that a negative US finding cannot be trusted to exclude TMJ arthritis.
- Applies QUADAS-2 risk-of-bias/applicability assessment to all 8 studies; none fulfilled all criteria, though all showed low applicability concerns.
- Tests power Doppler US separately (1 study, Zwir et al.) and finds it non-useful for synovial inflammation detection (SN 0%, SP 36.4%) — distinguishes this from B-mode performance.
- Frames US's clinical role explicitly as adjunct/triage (alongside clinical exam, to guide need for MRI) rather than MRI replacement, while naming US's practical advantages for children: no ionizing radiation, no sedation requirement, chairside, low-cost, repeatable.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- PRISMA-DTA-compliant systematic review, registered on Open Science Framework.
- Databases: Medline, Embase, Cochrane, Web of Science, SCOPUS, plus partial gray literature (first 100 Google Scholar results by relevance). Searched June 26, 2024; updated July 24, 2025. No language/time restriction.
- Inclusion: diagnostic studies (RCT, cohort, observational) of 2D/3D US in children (<18y) with JIA, evaluated against MRI, CT, or CBCT as reference standard for TMJ arthritis (disc displacement, joint effusion, condylar changes).
- Exclusion: reviews/letters/case reports/case series/opinions/book chapters/conference abstracts; studies without reference-standard comparison; studies lacking SN/SP or ROC data; non-TMJ joint studies.
- Two-phase screening (Covidence) by two independent reviewers (J.C., F.T.A.), consensus-resolved; data extraction by one author, cross-checked by a second.
- Risk of bias/applicability: QUADAS-2, two independent raters (J.C., K.B.), third-reviewer (F.T.A., oral/maxillofacial radiologist, 8 years US experience) resolving disagreements.
- Certainty of evidence: GRADEpro (GRADE Summary of Findings).
- Planned quantitative meta-analysis of diagnostic test accuracy was abandoned due to high methodological heterogeneity (different TMD parameters assessed, different US frequencies used) — narrative/qualitative synthesis only.
- Search yield: 1198 records → 515 duplicates removed → 683 screened → 673 excluded at title/abstract → 11 full-text reviewed → 3 excluded → **8 studies included**.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

Included studies (2007–2025; Germany, Norway, Switzerland, Italy ×3, USA, Brazil; 312 JIA patients total, n=8–92 per study; all MRI reference standard; US 12–18 MHz except Zwir power Doppler at 6.7 MHz):

| Study | Country | n (JIA) | Design | US target | SN | SP | Other findings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Assaf et al. | Germany | 20 | Prospective | CE, condylar disc thickness, synovial thickness, JE, intra-articular space | — (no non-JIA comparator; detection-rate only) | — | 287 changes detected (35.9% of images); CE 77.5%, abnormal synovial thickness 34.4%, JE 12.5% |
| Farronato et al. | Italy | 46 (30 compared to MRI) | Prospective | Condylar morphology, disc position, soft tissue, dynamic function, JE (composite) | 85% | 80% | PPV 88%, NPV 75%; US abnormalities in 84.8% of JIA patients |
| Kirkhus et al. | Norway | 55 | Retrospective | Subcondylar/condylar capsular width (synovitis proxy) | 72% | 70% | Spearman ρ 0.347–0.483 (capsular width vs. MRI synovitis); ROC AUC 0.77, cutoff 1.2 mm |
| Müller et al. | Switzerland | 30 | Pilot | Joint effusion / contrast enhancement (active arthritis) | 23% | 89% | Detected only most severely affected TMJs; authors do NOT recommend US as early screening |
| Tonni et al. (pilot) | Italy | 8 JIA + 7 control | Pilot | LPAS, JE | — | — | LPAS higher in JIA group (p<0.001); no JE detected in either group |
| Tonni et al. (comparative) | Italy | 29 JIA + 28 control | Comparative | LPAS width | — | — | Spearman ρ=0.623 (p<0.05) vs. MRI |
| Weiss et al. | USA | 32 | Prospective | Chronic (condylar changes/erosions) vs. acute (effusion, synovial thickening) arthritis | Chronic: κ=0.12 (50% agreement); Acute: κ=0.00 (US detected none) | — | MRI found chronic in 69%, acute in 75% of patients; US found chronic in 28%, acute in 0% |
| Zwir et al. | Brazil | 92 | Observational | Synovial vascularity/inflammation (**power Doppler**, not B-mode) | 0% | 36.4% | PPV 0%, NPV 100%; MRI-positive inflammation in 64.7% of joints (119/184) |

**Pooled/abstract-level figures** (B-mode US only, 7 of 8 studies; excludes Zwir's power Doppler): SN 21%–85%, SP 36.4%–89% across all TMJ-alteration targets combined. The explicit 23% (Müller, joint effusion/active arthritis) is the lowest individually-reported B-mode sensitivity recoverable from the retrieved full text; the abstract's stated 21% floor could not be traced to a specific individual-study figure in the retrieved body text (possibly reported only in Supporting Information Appendix S4, GRADE certainty tables, not returned by the full-text retrieval) — reproduced as stated, not fabricated.

**GRADE certainty**: LOW for sensitivity, MODERATE for specificity (based on 6 of 8 studies with true-negative/false-positive data; Assaf and Farronato excluded from this specific assessment for lacking non-JIA comparators).

**QUADAS-2**: none of the 8 studies fulfilled all criteria; all showed low applicability concerns in patient selection, index test, and reference standard domains. Patient-selection RoB "low" in 6/8, "unclear" in 2/8; index-test RoB "low" in 5/8; reference-standard RoB "low" in 4/8; flow/timing RoB "low" in 6/8.

**No study used CBCT as reference standard** — all 8 used MRI (contrast-enhanced in most); this is flagged by the authors as a gap, since CBCT captures cortical bone detail MRI cannot.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No meta-analysis / pooled diagnostic accuracy estimate — high methodological heterogeneity (different TMJ alterations assessed, inconsistent operational definitions of JE/CE/synovial thickness, US frequencies 6.7–18 MHz) makes the SN/SP range the honest ceiling of what can be reported.
- Unclear risk of bias in index-test and reference-standard domains across several studies.
- No study used CBCT as reference standard, despite CBCT's superior bone-detail resolution relevant to condylar erosion/flattening — a stated gap for future work.
- Two of the three "negative" studies (Müller, Weiss) used lower-frequency (12 MHz) transducers and were conducted >15 years ago; authors speculate — but do not claim as proven — that modern higher-frequency US and greater operator experience may perform better today.
- Power Doppler (1 study, Zwir) found not diagnostically useful for TMJ synovial inflammation, unlike its established utility in other joints (hands, wrists, knees) in JIA — authors caution against generalizing from a single study.
- No standardized US acquisition/interpretation protocol exists; authors call for one, plus exploration of handheld/pocket US and AI-assisted image analysis for point-of-care screening where MRI access is limited.

- Cites a prior review (not separately ingested here) finding a "moderate association" between US and MRI TMJ findings in JIA children — this SR updates and quantifies that impression with formal diagnostic-accuracy figures and GRADE certainty.

## 7. Glossary

- **JIA (Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis, 소아 특발성 관절염)**: chronic inflammatory arthritis with onset before age 16; TMJ involved in >90% of cases during the disease course.
- **TMJ (Temporomandibular Joint, 측두하악관절)**: the joint assessed; JIA-related arthritis here is often clinically silent.
- **US (Ultrasound, 초음파)**: index test — non-ionizing, low-cost, chairside imaging; B-mode (grayscale) vs. power Doppler (blood-flow/vascularity) modes evaluated separately.
- **MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging, 자기공명영상)**: reference standard in all 8 included studies; downsides in children include cost, access, and need for sedation.
- **CBCT (Cone Beam Computed Tomography, 콘빔전산화단층촬영)**: not used as reference standard in any included study; superior for cortical bone detail but involves ionizing radiation and cannot assess soft-tissue inflammation.
- **QUADAS-2 (Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies-2)**: risk-of-bias/applicability tool for diagnostic accuracy studies, applied to all 8 studies here.
- **GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation)**: certainty-of-evidence framework; here LOW for sensitivity, MODERATE for specificity.
- **JE (Joint Effusion, 관절 삼출)** / **CE (Condylar Erosion, 과두 미란)** / **LPAS (Lateral Periarticular Space, 외측 관절주위공간)**: the specific TMJ alterations assessed by US across studies, each with inconsistent operational definitions.
- **SN/SP (Sensitivity/Specificity, 민감도/특이도)**: diagnostic accuracy metrics; SN is the proportion of true TMJ-arthritis cases correctly identified by US — the review's central finding is that this can be as low as ~21%–23% depending on target and study.
