## One-line Summary

Umbrella review (18 systematic reviews/meta-analyses, JBI + PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD42022335209) of the relationship between chronic kidney disease (CKD) and periodontal disease/treatment, synthesized into clinical management recommendations.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First umbrella review (as of the search date, April 2022) to appraise the quality of systematic reviews on the CKD–periodontal disease relationship using JBI/PRISMA 2020 methodology.
- Separates the evidence into three sub-questions: (1) association between oral health/periodontitis and CKD, (2) effect of periodontal treatment on CKD outcomes, (3) oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) in kidney failure patients.
- Translates heterogeneous SR-level evidence into clinically actionable recommendations for medical professionals, dental professionals, and aged-care workers managing CKD patients.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses (JBI umbrella review methodology + PRISMA 2020 reporting).
- Databases searched: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Dentistry & Oral Science Source, up to April 2022.
- Inclusion: systematic reviews/meta-analyses examining the relationship between CKD and periodontitis, or the effect of periodontal treatment on CKD outcomes.
- Screening yield: 371 studies identified → 18 systematic reviews met inclusion criteria.
- Breakdown of included SRs: 10 on oral health/periodontitis–CKD association, 5 on periodontal treatment effect on CKD outcomes, 2 covering both, 1 qualitative review of OHRQoL in kidney failure.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Findings indicate a bidirectional relationship between CKD and periodontal disease.
- Periodontal treatment showed potential to improve CKD outcomes in the included systematic reviews, though quality and heterogeneity of underlying evidence varied considerably.
- No single pooled effect size is reported in the abstract-level source (umbrella review qualitatively synthesizes across heterogeneous included SRs rather than re-running pooled meta-analysis); full-text would be needed for individual SR-level quantitative estimates.
- Clinically relevant management recommendations were generated for maintaining periodontal health in CKD patients, targeted separately at medical professionals, dental professionals, and aged-care workers.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Abstract-only source (`full_text: false`) — quantitative detail (individual SR quality scores, pooled estimates, specific recommendation text) is not captured here; re-visit if full text becomes accessible.
- Authors themselves note high heterogeneity in the quality of the 18 included systematic reviews, which limits the strength of any single conclusion.
- Search cutoff April 2022 — newer SRs on CKD-periodontal disease published since are not captured by this umbrella review.
