## Why Ingested

Foundational Cochrane SR+MA comparing 1-stage (non-submerged) vs 2-stage (submerged) implant placement protocols — the core surgical staging question that underpins loading-protocol decisions. Directly relevant to [[implants/loading-protocol/esposito-2013-loading-times-dental-implants-cochrane]] (loading timing) and extends the evidence base for surgical protocol selection in partially vs fully edentulous patients.

## Three-line Summary

Cochrane SR+MA (5 RCTs, n=239 patients, 761 implants) comparing 1-stage (non-submerged) vs 2-stage (submerged) implant placement with ≥6-month follow-up after loading.

No statistically significant differences in prosthesis failure (RR 1.87, 95% CI 0.33–10.40), implant failure (RR 1.39, 95% CI 0.59–3.27), marginal bone loss, complications, or soft-tissue recession between the two surgical protocols; evidence quality moderate (GRADE).

1-stage approach may be preferable in partially edentulous patients (avoids second surgery, shortens treatment); 2-stage submerged approach still indicated when primary stability is suboptimal, barriers used for GBR, or removable prostheses risk excessive forces on healing abutments in fully edentulous jaws.

## 세줄요약

코크란 SR+MA (5 RCT, 239명, 761 임플란트) — 1단계(비침지) vs 2단계(침지) 임플란트 식립 비교, 부하 후 ≥6개월 추적.

보철 실패(RR 1.87, 95% CI 0.33–10.40), 임플란트 실패(RR 1.39, 95% CI 0.59–3.27), 변연골 소실, 합병증, 연조직 퇴축 모두 통계적으로 유의한 차이 없음; 근거 수준 보통(GRADE).

부분 무치악에서는 1단계가 유리(2차 수술 회피, 치료기간 단축); 완전 무치악·초기고정 미흡·GBR 차폐막 사용·가철성 임시보철로 과도한 힘 전달 우려 시에는 2단계 침지법이 여전히 권장됨.

## Key Contributions

- First Cochrane systematic review specifically comparing 1-stage vs 2-stage surgical protocols for implant placement
- Standardized definitions: 1-stage = implant penetrates oral mucosa during healing; 2-stage = implant completely buried under mucosa requiring second surgery for abutment connection
- Meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (239 patients, 761 implants) across diverse clinical settings (partial/full edentulism, various implant systems)
- Moderate-quality GRADE evidence showing no statistically significant difference in any primary outcome
- Clinically important trend: more implant failures with 1-stage in fully edentulous mandibles (Becktor 2007: 17 vs 5 failed implants)
- Identified clinical decision framework: primary stability, GBR barriers, prosthetic loading risk as key factors for protocol selection

## Methodology

- **Design**: Cochrane systematic review with meta-analysis (pub2 — 2nd version)
- **Search**: Cochrane Oral Health Group's Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE (1966–21 Jan 2009), EMBASE (1980–12 Jan 2009); hand-searching 18 implant journals; contact with 55 manufacturers; no language restrictions
- **Inclusion**: RCTs comparing same implant system placed via 1-stage vs 2-stage; minimum 6-month follow-up after loading; parallel-group and split-mouth designs
- **Outcomes**: Prosthesis failure, implant failure (mobility + removal), radiographic marginal bone level change (parallel technique from surgery to 1 year), patient preference/aesthetics (split-mouth only), aesthetic evaluation by dentists, biological/mechanical complications up to prosthesis delivery
- **Analysis**: Random-effects models; risk ratios for dichotomous outcomes, mean differences for continuous; patient as statistical unit (split-mouth data combined using Elbourne 2002 method); Cochrane's test for heterogeneity, I² statistic; GRADE quality assessment
- **Bias assessment**: Allocation concealment, blinding of outcome assessors, completeness of follow-up; 4/5 trials high risk of bias, 1 low risk (Cordaro 2009)

## Key Results

| Comparison | Outcome | Effect | Significance | Trials (Patients) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Prosthesis failure | RR 1.87 (0.33–10.40) | NS | 3 (153) |
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Implant failure | RR 1.39 (0.59–3.27) | NS | 4 (210) |
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Marginal bone level change | No significant difference | NS | 3 |
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Complications | More frequent in 1-stage (Becktor 2007) | Descriptive | 3 |
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Soft tissue recession | No difference (Cordaro 2009) | NS | 1 |
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Keratinized tissue height | 1.1 mm less with 2-stage at 1 yr (Cordaro 2009) | P<0.05 | 1 |

- Total implants: 375 (1-stage, 121 patients) vs 386 (2-stage, 123 patients), counting split-mouth patients twice
- Implant failures: 23 implants failed in 11 patients (1-stage) vs 9 implants in 8 patients (2-stage)
- Becktor 2007 (edentulous mandibles): 17 implants failed in 6 patients (1-stage) vs 5 implants in 4 patients (2-stage) — clinically important trend
- Complications (Becktor 2007): soft tissue reactions 13 vs 2, pain 18 in 6 pts vs 0, other 13 vs 3 — all favoring 2-stage in edentulous mandibles
- GRADE evidence quality: **moderate** for prosthesis failure and implant failure (downgraded for high risk of bias in most trials, imprecision)

## Limitations

- Only 5 small RCTs included (total n=239 patients); underpowered to detect clinically important differences
- 4 of 5 trials at high risk of bias (inadequate allocation concealment, lack of blinding, unclear baseline comparability)
- No a priori sample size calculation in any trial; Cordaro 2009 only post-hoc for keratinized tissue outcome
- Follow-up limited to 6 months–1 year; long-term outcomes unknown
- Heterogeneous implant systems (IMZ TPS, Astra TiO₂-blasted, Brånemark turned, Straumann SLA), healing periods, prosthetic protocols
- Marginal bone level baseline measurements taken at prosthesis delivery (Ceccinato 2004) or 1 month after overdenture delivery (Heijdenrijk 2002) — not at implant placement
- Aesthetic outcomes assessed in only 1 trial (Cordaro 2009, post-extractive single implants)
- Split-mouth design (Barber 1996) required data transformation; original mixed design excluded from prior version

## Glossary

- **1-stage (non-submerged) placement**: Implant placed with transmucosal healing abutment/cover screw penetrating oral mucosa during osseointegration; avoids second surgery
- **2-stage (submerged) placement**: Implant completely buried under mucosa during healing; requires second minor surgery to connect healing abutment
- **Primary stability**: Mechanical stability at implant placement (insertion torque, ISQ); prerequisite for successful 1-stage healing
- **GBR (Guided Bone Regeneration)**: Barrier membrane technique for bone augmentation; often requires submerged healing
- **GRADE**: Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation — systematic approach to rating evidence quality (high/moderate/low/very low)
- **Split-mouth design**: Each patient receives both interventions (one per side); controls for inter-patient variability but requires specific statistical handling
