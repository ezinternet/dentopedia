This CBCT study quantifies accessory maxillary ostium (AMO) frequency and — clinically important — shows that PRIMARY maxillary ostium (PMO) obstruction, NOT AMO, is significantly associated with maxillary sinusitis (p < 0.001), extending our sinonasal-anatomy / ostiomeatal-complex coverage relevant to pre-sinus-lift screening. ## One-line Summary

Cross-sectional CBCT study (n = 244 scans; sinusitis vs normal groups) — AMO presence did NOT correlate with maxillary sinusitis (p = 0.104), but PMO obstruction was significantly more frequent in the sinusitis group (p < 0.001); AMO frequency was gender-dependent (p = 0.013).

## 2. Key Contributions

1. Directly separates two ostium-level findings on CBCT — accessory maxillary ostium (AMO, an extra opening in the fontanelle) vs primary maxillary ostium (PMO) patency/obstruction — and shows only the latter is tied to maxillary sinusitis.
2. Quantifies AMO in a mixed sinusitis / normal population (n = 244 scans) and demonstrates AMO carries no significant association with maxillary sinusitis (p = 0.104) or with any paranasal anatomical variation.
3. Identifies a gender effect on AMO presence (p = 0.013): higher in females in the normal group and in males in the sinusitis group.
4. Contrasts anatomical variations between groups — paradoxical concha (p < 0.001) and bifid concha (p = 0.017) were MORE frequent in the normal group, while Haller cell, nasal septal deviation, and concha bullosa did not differ.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional retrospective evaluation of CBCT scans.
- **Sample**: 244 CBCT scans — 134 females (54.9%) and 110 males (45.1%); mean age 34.16 ± 19.01 years.
- **Groups**:
  - Maxillary sinusitis group: mucosal thickening > 2 mm.
  - Normal group: normal mucosa or mucosal thickening < 2 mm.
- **Assessed on CBCT**: presence/absence of AMO; patency vs obstruction of the primary maxillary ostium (PMO); presence of paranasal-sinus anatomical variations (Haller cell, nasal septal deviation, concha bullosa, paradoxical concha, bifid concha).
- **Statistics**: independent t-test, Pearson Chi-square, Fisher's exact test; alpha = 0.05.

- Gender pattern: AMO more frequent in females within the normal group and in males within the sinusitis group.
- Bottom line: PMO obstruction — not AMO — is the ostium-level correlate of maxillary sinusitis in this sample.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Cross-sectional design — associations only, no causality between PMO obstruction and sinusitis direction can be inferred.
- Single-center CBCT sample; radiographic sinusitis defined by a > 2 mm mucosal-thickening threshold rather than clinical/ENT diagnosis, which may misclassify.
- Full-text body was not retrievable (abstract-only ingest): detailed AMO prevalence percentages, laterality, and per-group counts beyond those in the abstract are not captured here; re-ingest from the full text if finer numbers are needed.
- Mean age (~34 y) is relatively young for a sinus-lift/implant population, limiting direct transfer to older edentulous patients.

## 7. Glossary

- **Accessory Maxillary Ostium (AMO)**: An additional (secondary) opening in the maxillary sinus fontanelle, separate from the natural primary ostium; a common anatomical variant.
- **Primary Maxillary Ostium (PMO)**: The natural drainage opening of the maxillary sinus into the middle meatus via the ostiomeatal complex.
- **Ostiomeatal complex (OMC)**: The final common drainage pathway of the anterior paranasal sinuses; obstruction predisposes to sinusitis.
- **Haller cell**: An ethmoid air cell in the infraorbital region that can narrow the ostiomeatal complex.
- **Concha bullosa**: Pneumatization of the middle turbinate.
- **Paradoxical concha (PC)**: A middle turbinate curving laterally (paradoxically) rather than medially.
- **Bifid concha (BC)**: A middle turbinate split into two parts.
- **CBCT**: Cone-beam computed tomography.
