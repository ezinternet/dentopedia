## Why Ingested

Platform switching(PS) 근거 기반의 원류를 확보하기 위해 인제스트 — 이 컨센서스 리포트는 [[wiki/implants/mbl/juan-montesinos-2022-platform-switching-conventional-sr-ma]] 등 후속 SR+MA들이 정량화하는 "PS가 변연골 소실을 줄인다"는 결론을 2013년 시점에 두 개의 체계적 문헌고찰(Schwarz et al., Strietzel et al.)을 종합해 전문가 합의로 먼저 못박은 상위 권위 문헌(practice guideline)이다. 단일 1차 연구가 아니라 학회(Camlog Foundation) 공식 컨센서스라는 점에서 evidence_level이 다르다.

## Three-line Summary

Camlog Foundation International Expert Meeting (Rome, Jan 2013) consensus report synthesizing two systematic reviews — Schwarz et al. on implant-abutment connection type and machined-collar/microgap positioning, and Strietzel et al. on platform switching (PS) — into formal consensus statements and clinical recommendations on crestal bone level changes.

Machined-collar findings: supracrestal positioning of the smooth collar showed less crestal bone loss than subcrestal positioning; for the microgap specifically, a subcrestal position (≥1mm) was associated with more bone remodeling but better retained bony coverage of the rough surface than epi-/supracrestal placement; implant-abutment connection type (internal/external, conical/butt-joint) impact "lacks documentation." PS findings: despite high study heterogeneity, the evidence trend favors PS over platform matching to prevent/minimize peri-implant marginal bone loss, though clinical outcomes in humans have not as clearly shown the benefit seen in animal histology, and no negative effects of PS have been published.

Establishes the 2013 expert-consensus baseline — "a trend favoring platform switching" plus nuanced, position-dependent guidance on collar/microgap placement — that later quantitative SR+MAs and RCTs (e.g., MD ~0.25-0.5mm less MBL with PS) go on to confirm and sharpen.

## 세줄요약

Camlog Foundation 국제 전문가 회의(로마, 2013년 1월) 컨센서스 리포트 — 임플란트-지대주 연결부(Implant-Abutment Connection) 종류와 기계가공 칼라/미세간극(Machined Collar/Microgap) 위치를 다룬 Schwarz et al. 체계적 문헌고찰과, 플랫폼 스위칭(Platform Switching, PS)을 다룬 Strietzel et al. 체계적 문헌고찰 2편을 종합해 공식 합의문·임상권고안을 도출.

칼라/미세간극 소견: 매끈한 칼라를 치조정 상방(supracrestal)에 위치시킬 때가 치조정 하방(subcrestal)보다 골소실이 적음; 다만 미세간극 자체는 1mm 이상 치조정 하방 위치가 골개조는 더 크지만 거친 표면부의 골피개(bony coverage)를 더 잘 유지; 연결부 종류(내부형/외부형, 원추형/맞댐형)의 영향은 "문서화가 부족"하다고 명시. PS 소견: 연구 간 이질성(heterogeneity)이 높음에도, 근거의 경향은 PS가 일반형(Platform Matching) 대비 임플란트 주위 변연골 소실을 예방·최소화하는 쪽을 지지하며, 동물 조직학 연구의 유익함이 사람 임상연구에서는 그만큼 뚜렷하지 않으나 방향성은 긍정적이고 PS의 부정적 효과는 보고된 바 없음.

2013년 시점 전문가 합의 기준선("PS를 지지하는 경향" + 칼라/미세간극 위치별 세분화된 권고)을 확립한 문헌으로, 이후의 정량적 SR+MA·RCT들(예: PS로 MBL 약 0.25–0.5mm 감소)이 이를 재확인·구체화한다.

## 1. Document Information

- **Journal**: Clinical Oral Implants Research, 2013;25(11):1301–1303
- **DOI**: 10.1111/clr.12269
- **PMID**: 24147913 / **PMCID**: PMC4232323
- **Article type**: Consensus Statement / Practice Guideline — Camlog Foundation International Expert Meeting report, Rome, Italy, 18–19 January 2013
- **Structure**: Synthesizes two independently authored systematic reviews (Schwarz, Hegewald, Becker — implant-abutment connection & machined collar/microgap; Strietzel, Neumann, Hertel — platform switching), each presented to and debated by two separate expert working groups, with statements revised in plenary session before final approval.
- **Funding/COI**: Unrestricted grant from the Camlog Foundation, Basel, Switzerland. Alex Schaer, Frank Schwarz, and Thomas Taylor are Camlog Foundation Board members; Katja Nelson is a consultant to Camlog Biotechnologies AG.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Delivers formal, expert-panel-approved consensus statements (not just review conclusions) on three distinct but related questions: implant-abutment connection type, machined-collar/microgap position, and platform switching.
- Establishes the widely cited framing that the evidence shows "a trend favoring the platform switching concept to prevent or minimize peri-implant marginal bone loss," explicitly hedged by study heterogeneity.
- Provides a nuanced, non-obvious distinction between the machined *collar* (smooth part of the implant neck) and the *microgap* (implant-abutment junction) as positioning variables — supracrestal collar placement reduces bone loss, while subcrestal microgap placement, despite causing more remodeling, helps preserve bony coverage over the rough implant surface.
- States plainly that implant-abutment connection design (internal vs external, conical vs butt-joint) "lacks documentation" for its impact on crestal bone — a null/uncertain finding that is itself a contribution, distinguishing connection-type claims from collar/microgap and PS claims.
- Flags open questions still unresolved at time of writing: long-term effect of PS on soft-tissue health and biomechanical stability of the implant-abutment connection, and the limits of two-dimensional radiographic bone measurement used across the underlying studies.

## 3. Methodology

- **Format**: Structured consensus-development process, not a de novo systematic review. Two pre-circulated systematic reviews were presented in full (methodology, results, conclusions) to appointed international expert clinicians/researchers, who then split into two working groups.
- **Group 1 (implant-abutment connection / collar-microgap positioning)**: chaired by Gil Alcoforado, secretaries Katja Nelson and Alex Schär, rapporteur Frank Schwarz; reviewed the Schwarz, Hegewald & Becker systematic review.
- **Group 2 (platform switching)**: chaired by Thomas Taylor, secretary Florian Beuer, rapporteur Frank Peter Strietzel; reviewed the Strietzel, Neumann & Hertel systematic review.
- Each group independently drafted consensus statements and clinical recommendations, which were then presented in plenary session, revised per audience feedback, and formally approved.
- Output categories per topic: (1) focused question, (2) major findings/conclusions, (3) consensus statements, (4) clinical recommendations, (5) implications for future research.

## 4. Key Results

**Implant-abutment connection & machined collar/microgap (Schwarz et al. review):**
- Supracrestal positioning of the machined (smooth) collar — at both one-piece and two-piece implants — was associated with less crestal bone level change than subcrestal positioning of the smooth-to-rough border.
- For two-piece implants specifically, subcrestal positioning of the microgap relative to the alveolar crest may be favored over epicrestal insertion for retaining bony coverage of the rough surface — even though it is associated with more pronounced bone remodeling than epi-/supracrestal placement.
- Placing the smooth part of the implant below the alveolar crest "may lead to bone loss" — clinical recommendation: avoid subcrestal positioning of smooth implant parts; the smooth-rough border should ideally coincide with the adjacent alveolar bone level.
- Clinical recommendation on microgap: when the goal is retaining crestal bone coronal to the implant shoulder, a subcrestal microgap position may be favored over epi-/supracrestal — but subcrestal shoulder placement should be limited to entirely roughened two-part implants, given the detrimental effect of a smooth neck on remodeling.
- Implant-abutment connection type (internal/external, conical/butt-joint): evidence "lacks documentation" and does not allow robust conclusions; crestal bone remodeling has been observed with either configuration; microbial leakage may occur regardless of connection design. Clinical recommendation: choose a stable implant-abutment complex to minimize micromovement and microbial leakage.

**Platform switching (Strietzel et al. review):**
- Due to heterogeneity among included studies, results should be interpreted cautiously.
- Within the limits of available RCTs and prospective cohort studies, the tendency favors platform switching over platform matching to prevent/minimize peri-implant marginal bone loss.
- Animal histological studies support the biological rationale for PS, but human clinical outcomes have not as clearly demonstrated the benefit — though the trend is generally positive.
- No negative effects of PS have been published to date; concerns remain regarding thin soft-tissue-thickness scenarios and the two-dimensional radiographic measurement methods used in most included studies.
- Clinical recommendation: available evidence does not allow clear-cut statements on indications for PS; platform-switched restorations may be used wherever platform-matched restorations would be used, at clinician discretion.
- Unresolved: long-term effects of PS on soft-tissue health and on the biomechanical stability of the implant-abutment connection.

**Future research (both groups)**: call for more well-designed, randomized, parallel-arm controlled trials in humans, with uniform/comparable study design that either excludes or precisely documents confounding factors (patient/site characteristics, implant/abutment geometry, horizontal-offset dimension, surgical technique, prosthetic protocol, maintenance care).

## 5. Limitations

- Consensus-statement format, not a primary systematic review or meta-analysis conducted by this paper itself — it synthesizes two separately published reviews rather than presenting new pooled quantitative data (no MD/effect-size numbers are reported in this report itself).
- Explicitly acknowledges high heterogeneity among the underlying PS studies, limiting the strength of its own "trend favoring PS" conclusion.
- Camlog Foundation funding and board-member/consultant affiliations among several authors are disclosed as a potential conflict-of-interest consideration.
- Predates (by design) the more recent quantitative SR+MAs and multi-year RCTs that later provide effect-size estimates for PS's impact on marginal bone loss.
- Two-dimensional radiographic bone-level measurement, common across the underlying primary studies, is flagged by the panel itself as a methodological limitation.

## 7. Glossary

- **Platform Switching (PS)**: using a prosthetic abutment of smaller diameter than the implant platform, shifting the implant-abutment microgap inward/away from the crestal bone.
- **Machined Collar**: the smooth (non-roughened) portion of the implant neck, as distinct from the roughened body surface.
- **Microgap**: the implant-abutment junction/interface, a potential site of micromovement and microbial leakage.
- **Subcrestal / Epicrestal / Supracrestal positioning**: placement of the implant neck or microgap below, level with, or above the alveolar crest, respectively.
- **Crestal Bone Level Change**: radiographically measured vertical change in marginal/crestal bone height around an implant over time.
- **Implant-Abutment Connection**: the mechanical interface type joining implant and abutment (e.g., internal vs external hex, conical vs butt-joint).
