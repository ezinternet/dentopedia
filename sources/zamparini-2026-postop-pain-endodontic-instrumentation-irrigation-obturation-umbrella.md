## Why Ingested

This is the first umbrella review to synthesize POP evidence jointly across all three procedural phases (irrigation, instrumentation, obturation) over the last decade, giving a structural map for existing single-domain wiki pages. It directly extends [[endodontics/irrigation/hatipoglu-2025-irrigation-activation-postoperative-pain-network-meta]] (which ranked 7 irrigation-activation techniques by SUCRA for Day-1 pain) by situating that irrigation finding alongside instrumentation-kinematics and sealer-type evidence, and it reinforces [[wiki/overviews/endodontic-postoperative-pain-overview]]'s cross-cutting thesis that apical extrusion control — not any single technique choice — is the operative mechanism linking these domains. It also parallels [[endodontics/visit-protocol/vertucci-2026-single-vs-multiple-visit-endodontics-umbrella]] methodologically (both are 2026 umbrella reviews of endodontic SRs using AMSTAR-2), giving a second data point on how much overlap/heterogeneity limits umbrella-review conclusions in this field.

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 25 systematic reviews with meta-analysis (9 irrigation, 8 instrumentation, 8 obturation; 368 records screened, 2016–2025 window) synthesizing post-operative pain (POP) evidence across the three main procedural phases of non-surgical root canal treatment, AMSTAR-2 appraised, PROSPERO-registered (CRD420261331928).

Lower NaOCl concentrations (1–3% vs ≥5%), irrigant activation techniques, and intracanal cryotherapy were repeatedly linked to reduced short-term (6–48h) POP; rotary instrumentation systems were more often but not consistently associated with lower POP than reciprocating/manual; no clinically consistent POP difference was found between bioceramic/calcium silicate-based and resin-based obturation sealers.

Findings should be read as tentative directional trends rather than firm recommendations — obturation-domain reviews showed very high primary-study overlap (corrected covered area 17.6%) and irrigation/instrumentation domains showed high overlap (CCA 11.1%/12.5%), meaning concordant conclusions across reviews are not fully independent confirmations, and 4/25 SRs lacked protocol registration while 11/25 did not justify excluded studies.

## 세줄요약

근관치료 후 통증(Post-operative Pain, POP)에 관한 25편의 체계적 문헌고찰(systematic review, SR)+메타분석을 종합한 우산리뷰(umbrella review)로, 세정(irrigation, 9편)·기구조작(instrumentation, 8편)·충전(obturation, 8편)의 3개 시술 단계를 통합 분석했다(2016–2025년, 368편 스크리닝, AMSTAR-2 평가, PROSPERO 등록 CRD420261331928).

낮은 차아염소산나트륨(Sodium Hypochlorite, NaOCl) 농도(1–3% vs ≥5%), 세정 활성화(irrigation activation) 기법, 근관 내 냉동요법(intracanal cryotherapy)이 반복적으로 단기(6–48시간) POP 감소와 연관되었고, 회전형(rotary) 기구가 왕복형(reciprocating)·수동(manual) 기구보다 더 자주(그러나 일관되지는 않게) 낮은 POP와 연관되었으며, 바이오세라믹(bioceramic)/규산칼슘(calcium silicate) 계열 실러와 레진(resin) 계열 실러 간 임상적으로 유의한 POP 차이는 없었다.

충전 영역의 1차 연구 중복도(corrected covered area, CCA)가 17.6%(매우 높음)로 나타나 개별 SR 간 결론이 독립적 재확인이 아닐 수 있으며, 25편 중 4편은 프로토콜 미등록, 11편은 제외 연구 근거 미제시 등 방법론적 한계로 인해 본 소견은 확정적 임상권고가 아닌 잠정적 경향으로 해석해야 한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First umbrella review to jointly cover all three core procedural phases of non-surgical root canal treatment (irrigation, instrumentation, obturation) in relation to POP over a single 10-year window (2016–2025), rather than a single-domain focus (prior umbrella reviews focused narrowly on hydraulic sealers).
- Restricts inclusion to SRs *with meta-analysis only*, improving quantitative comparability across the 25 included reviews (vs narrative-only SRs).
- Introduces a formal overlap-of-evidence quantification (corrected covered area, CCA) per procedural domain, showing that irrigation/instrumentation overlap is "high" (11–12.5%) and obturation overlap is "very high" (17.6%) — a caution rarely made explicit in prior endodontic pain literature.
- AMSTAR-2 quality appraisal surfaces systemic weaknesses across the field: 4/25 SRs unregistered, 11/25 without justified exclusion lists, inconsistent risk-of-bias handling and publication-bias assessment.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Umbrella review per PRISMA guidelines and Choi et al. 2023 umbrella-review recommendations.
- **PIOS framework**: Population = patients undergoing non-surgical endodontic treatment; Intervention = instrumentation/irrigation/obturation techniques or strategies; Outcome = POP intensity/duration; Study = SR with meta-analysis.
- **Search**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, plus manual journal search; English language; 2016–2025; MeSH terms (Pain, Postoperative; Endodontics; Root Canal Therapy; Root Canal Irrigants; Sodium Hypochlorite; Chlorhexidine; Root Canal Filling Materials; Dental Instruments) combined with free-text pain/procedure terms.
- **Screening**: Two independent reviewers, title/abstract then full-text; disagreements resolved by discussion or third reviewer. 368 records → 204 duplicates removed → 164 screened → 113 excluded at abstract stage → 51 full-text assessed → 26 excluded → **25 SRs included** (9 irrigation, 8 instrumentation, 8 obturation).
- **Data extraction**: Standardized form, piloted/calibrated by 2 reviewers + 1 expert operator; fields = first author, year, pain-related factor, sample size, indication, publication period, pain measurement method, outcomes, evidence quality, limitations. No formal kappa calculated, but pilot calibration + duplicate extraction + consensus used to reduce misclassification.
- **Quality appraisal**: AMSTAR-2 (Yes/Partial Yes/No per item), applied to methodological quality/confidence of included SRs only (not to certainty of underlying primary-trial evidence).
- **Synthesis**: Qualitative narrative synthesis (no de novo meta-analysis of primary studies) due to cross-review heterogeneity; results from each SR's own meta-analyses summarized descriptively.
- **Overlap quantification**: Citation matrix (primary studies × SRs) per domain; corrected covered area (CCA) formula applied — thresholds: slight 0–5%, moderate 6–10%, high 11–15%, very high >15%.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Irrigation (9 SRs)**: lower NaOCl concentration (1–3% vs ≥5%) → less POP (2 SRs); irrigant activation/agitation → less POP at 6–48h vs syringe irrigation; NaOCl vs chlorhexidine → no significant POP difference (1 SR); intracanal cryotherapy → less POP at 6–24h (4 SRs, one specific to symptomatic apical periodontitis at 24h), difference fading by 48–72h/7d. Overlap: 36 unique primary studies, 68 total occurrences, **CCA 11.1% (high)**.
- **Instrumentation (8 SRs)**: apical patency vs non-patency → possible POP reduction at 24/48h (2 SRs, not always significant); foraminal enlargement → higher early POP vs conventional treatment (1 SR, necrotic teeth); instrumentation system comparisons (5 SRs) → rotary systems (esp. multi-file) more often linked to lower POP than reciprocating/manual, but 1 SR found no difference and 1 SR favored reciprocating. Overlap: 47 unique primary studies, 88 total occurrences, **CCA 12.5% (high)**.
- **Obturation (8 SRs)**: bioceramic/calcium silicate-based vs epoxy resin-based sealers → mostly no significant POP difference, especially at 3–7 days; some SRs show slightly lower 24h pain with bioceramic sealers (inconsistent); most recent SR (including bioactive glass sealers) → no difference at 24h/48h/7d; zinc oxide–eugenol, calcium hydroxide-based comparisons similarly null. Overlap: 43 unique primary studies, 96 total occurrences, **CCA 17.6% (very high)**.
- **AMSTAR-2 findings across all 25 SRs**: 4 without protocol registration; 11 without justified list of excluded studies; risk-of-bias assessed in all but often only partially per AMSTAR-2 criteria; publication bias frequently unassessed; funding sources inconsistently reported.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Marked cross-review heterogeneity in methodological quality, eligibility criteria, baseline diagnoses, treatment protocols, pain-assessment instruments, follow-up windows, denominators, and reporting standards.
- Evidence is predominantly short-term; long-term POP data for newer materials/techniques (hydraulic sealers, novel activation devices) remain scarce.
- No standardized pain-assessment terminology across primary trials limits cross-study comparability.
- High-to-very-high primary-study overlap (esp. obturation, CCA 17.6%) means concordant SR conclusions cannot be treated as independent confirmations.
- Certainty-of-evidence judgments were taken as reported by each included SR and were **not** reassessed de novo with GRADE at the umbrella-review level — the authors explicitly frame all associations as tentative directional patterns, not stable comparative conclusions or practice recommendations.
- Authors call for future high-quality SRs and adequately powered RCTs with standardized pain outcomes, clearly reported baseline diagnoses, and consistent follow-up.

## 7. Glossary

- **POP (Post-operative Pain)**: Pain experienced by the patient following endodontic treatment, typically assessed via VAS/NRS at defined time points (6h–7d).
- **Umbrella review**: A systematic review of systematic reviews, synthesizing and critically appraising evidence across multiple SRs/meta-analyses on a topic.
- **AMSTAR-2**: A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews (version 2) — 16-item checklist for appraising SR methodological quality/confidence.
- **Corrected Covered Area (CCA)**: A statistic quantifying the degree of overlap of primary studies across multiple systematic reviews; higher values indicate reviews draw more heavily on the same underlying trial pool.
- **NaOCl (Sodium Hypochlorite)**: Primary root canal irrigant valued for antimicrobial activity and organic tissue dissolution; concentration (commonly 1–6%) affects both antimicrobial efficacy and POP/extrusion risk.
- **Apical patency**: Maintaining canal patency at the apical foramen with a small file during instrumentation, without intentionally enlarging the foramen.
- **Foraminal enlargement**: Intentional widening of the apical foramen during instrumentation, distinct from patency maintenance.
- **Hydraulic calcium silicate sealer (HCSC) / bioceramic sealer**: Root canal sealer class based on calcium silicate chemistry, used increasingly as an alternative to epoxy resin-based sealers.
- **PROSPERO**: International prospective register of systematic review protocols.
