# Le Fur-Bonnabesse 2017 — Dental Pain Induced by Ambient Thermal Differential

## Why Ingested
Narrative review proposing a pathophysiological mechanism for an underrecognized form of dental pain — thermal allodynia triggered by cold-to-warm ambient temperature gradient — distinct from classical dentin hypersensitivity. Clinically relevant because patients are frequently misdiagnosed and standard DH treatments fail. See [[dentin-hypersensitivity/kaur-2026-dentin-hypersensitivity-enigma-revisited]].

Narrative review with three clinical case descriptions. No randomized data; authors propose a mechanistic hypothesis supported by existing literature on TRP channels, pulpal physiology, and pain neuroscience.

## Key Findings

### Clinical Presentation (3 Cases)
- All female, ages 49–55
- Chief complaint: diffuse, dull, throbbing dental pain beginning 15 min after returning indoors from prolonged cold outdoor exposure
- Pain peaks within 5–10 min, lasts 30 min to 3 hours then resolves completely
- VAS 6–8/10; pain not precisely localizable, crosses midline, sometimes spreads to opposite arch
- No pain while outdoors in cold; no response to air blast or dental probe; cold test (Endo-Ice) normal
- Electrical pulp test: at least one tooth with extremely low threshold (hyperresponsive) vs contralateral
- No history of typical DH (hot/cold food/beverage sensitivity)
- Pain partially relieved by paracetamol or ibuprofen
- Causal teeth: predominantly maxillary central incisors (11, 21); also 42 in one patient
- Previous DH treatments (desensitizing toothpaste, Duraphat, Gluma) had failed
- Confirmatory test: ice-cold water held ≥30 s then hot water ≥1 min reproduces similar pain
- Orthodontic wax applied to suspected teeth during outdoor activities prevented pain onset

### Distinguishing Features vs Typical DH
| Feature | Typical DH | Thermal Allodynia |
|---|---|---|
| Pain quality | Sharp, brief, acute, localized | Diffuse, dull, throbbing, sustained |
| Fiber type | Aδ-fibers (hydrodynamic) | C-fibers (sensitized nociceptors) |
| Stimulus | Direct stimulus (air blast, cold water) | Ambient thermal gradient (cold→warm) |
| Onset | Immediate | Delayed (~15 min) |
| Duration | Seconds | 30 min–3 hours |
| Season | Year-round | Autumn/winter only |
| Standard DH treatment | Effective | Ineffective |

## Pathophysiological Hypothesis

### Step 1 — Repeated cold→warm gradient exposure
Patients repeatedly go from cold outdoor to warm indoor environment. The thermal differential activates the tooth's thermomechanical response: enamel flexure (contraction with cold) → dentinal fluid shifts → mechanical shear on intratubular nerve endings and odontoblasts.

### Step 2 — Phenotypic changes in dental primary afferents
Repeated noxious stimulation causes neuroplastic changes (phenotypic changes) in peptidergic C-fiber nociceptors innervating the dental pulp. A "silent" or "low-grade" neurogenic inflammation state develops.

### Step 3 — C-fiber sensitization via TRP channel threshold shifts
- TRPA1 (cold-sensitive, threshold <25°C) and TRPV1 (heat-sensitive, threshold >43°C) are both expressed on dental pulp nociceptors and odontoblasts
- With peripheral sensitization, the activation threshold of TRPV1 is lowered
- C-fibers become responsive to previously innocuous warm temperatures (physiological room temperature)
- SP and CGRP released by activated peptidergic fibers → vasodilation, plasma extravasation → local neurogenic inflammation → further lowered thresholds (positive feedback)
- NGF upregulation in inflamed tissue sensitizes TRPV1, compounding heat hypersensitivity

### Step 4 — Central sensitization
Sustained peripheral C-fiber barrage to the trigeminal brainstem nuclear complex → neuroplastic alterations → central sensitization → diffuse, poorly localizable pain crossing midline.

### Supporting Mechanism — Dentinal Fluid Hydrodynamics
Lin et al showed that outward fluid flow velocity (cold→warm sequence) was 140% greater than with pure cooling alone. This enhanced hydrodynamic shear force on sensitized nociceptors amplifies neural discharge in already-primed teeth.

### Analogy — Bleaching Sensitivity
Bleaching agents diffuse through enamel into dentin → free radicals → direct TRPA1 channel activation on C-fibers → similar dull, unprovoked pain. This analogy supports the C-fiber/TRP mechanism.

### Analogy — Raynaud's Phenomenon
Pulpal blood flow (PBF) responds to temperature: vasoconstriction during cold → vasodilation on rewarming → possible transient increase in intrapulpal pressure → mechanical activation of thermo/mechanosensitive receptors on nerve endings and odontoblasts.

## Three Theories of Pulpal Nociception (Background)
1. **Neural theory**: Direct activation of pulpal nerve endings via nociceptive TRP channels (TRPV1, TRPV2, TRPA1, TRPM8, P2X2, P2X3)
2. **Odontoblastic transduction theory**: Odontoblasts act as sensory transducers; ATP released via PANX-1 → P2X3 receptors on pulpal neurons ("odontoblast hydrodynamic receptor" model — Shibukawa et al 2015)
3. **Hydrodynamic theory** (most widely accepted): Fluid movement in dentinal tubules → shear stress on mechanosensitive ion channels at pulp/dentin border

## Treatment
Effective treatment: increase the protective barrier (dentin/enamel thickness) to shield pulpal tissue — veneers or protective mouth guards for outdoor activities. TRP channel antagonists proposed as a novel avenue.

## Limitations
- Only 3 clinical cases; no control group; hypothesis not experimentally tested in this paper
- Exact contribution of pulpal blood flow changes to the mechanism remains unknown
- Pain direction specificity (cold→warm but NOT warm→cold) not fully mechanistically explained beyond threshold sensitization

## Key TRP Channels
- **TRPA1**: Cold/mechanosensitive, threshold <25°C; co-expressed with TRPV1; cold sensor, also activated by oxidative stress/free radicals
- **TRPV1**: Heat-sensitive, threshold >43°C (lowered to innocuous range with sensitization); activated by capsaicin and low pH (<6.0)
- **TRPM8**: Cold-sensitive, threshold <17°C
- **TRPV2**: Heat-sensitive, threshold >52°C

## Neuropeptides Involved
- **SP (Substance P)** and **CGRP**: Released by C-fiber activation → potent vasodilators → plasma extravasation → neurogenic inflammation → further sensitization
