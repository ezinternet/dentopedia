## Why Ingested

사용자 요청으로 지대주 높이 (Abutment Height)와 변연골소실 (Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)의 정량적 관계를 다룬 후향 연구를 추가한다. [[implants/mbl/galindomoreno-2014-prosthetic-abutment-height-is-a]]의 "짧은 지대주 = 더 큰 MBL" 방향을 같은 연구자 그룹이 별도 코호트에서 재확인하면서, MBL이 0이 되는 최소 지대주 높이 (PS 2.5 mm / 비-PS 3.0 mm)를 수치로 제시하고 플랫폼 스위칭 (Platform Switching, PS)에 따른 효과 차이를 더한다.

→ [[implants/mbl/spinato-2018-minimum-abutment-height-to-eliminate]]

## Three-line Summary

One-year retrospective study of 4.7-mm-diameter implants in the maxilla (control, conventional non-platform-switched: 25 patients; test, platform-switched: 26 patients), most with transcrestal sinus augmentation using mineralized xenograft, relating prosthetic abutment height to marginal bone loss in cement-retained restorations.

Mean mesial and distal marginal bone loss in the control group was significantly more than twice that of the test group at similar abutment height; regression showed an inverse abutment height–bone loss relationship in both groups, with the intercept 50% lower for the test group, and bone loss annulled at 2.5 mm (platform-switched) versus 3.0 mm (conventional).

Shorter abutments meant greater bone loss, and abutment height mattered more in platform-switched implants; bone loss did not differ between native and grafted maxillary bone (abstract-only).

## 세줄요약

4.7 mm 직경 상악 임플란트에 대한 후향 연구 (Retrospective Study) — 대조군 (기존 비-플랫폼 스위칭, 25명) 대 시험군 (플랫폼 스위칭 (Platform Switching, PS), 26명), 다수는 광화 이종골 (Xenograft)을 이용한 치조정접근 상악동 거상 (Transcrestal Sinus Floor Elevation, TSFE) 병행; 시멘트 유지형 보철에서 지대주 높이 (Abutment Height)와 변연골소실 (Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)의 관계를 분석.

대조군의 근·원심 평균 MBL은 시험군의 2배 넘게 유의하게 컸고 (지대주 높이는 유사), 회귀분석에서 양 군 모두 지대주 높이와 MBL이 유의한 역상관이었으며 시험군 회귀선의 절편이 50% 낮았고, MBL은 시험군 2.5 mm, 대조군 3.0 mm 지대주에서 0이 되었다.

지대주가 짧을수록 MBL이 컸고 지대주 높이의 영향은 PS 임플란트에서 더 컸으며, 자연골과 이식골 식립 임플란트 간 MBL 차이는 없었다 (초록만 확보).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantifies the abutment height at which marginal bone loss reaches zero: 2.5 mm for platform-switched implants and 3.0 mm for conventional implants.
- Shows an inverse linear relationship between abutment height and marginal bone loss in both groups, with a stronger abutment-height influence in platform-switched implants.
- Reports no statistically significant difference in marginal bone loss between implants in native maxillary bone and those in grafted (transcrestal sinus augmentation, mineralized xenograft) areas.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective study; radiographs immediately after implant placement, after delivery of the prosthetic restoration, and after 12 months of loading.
- **Implants**: 4.7-mm-diameter implants in maxillary bone, two types with a similar neck design, placed singly.
- **Groups**: Test group — platform-switched implants (26 patients); control group — conventional (non-platform-switched) implants (25 patients).
- **Augmentation**: Patients requiring bone augmentation underwent unilateral sinus augmentation using a transcrestal technique with mineralized xenograft.
- **Restoration**: Cement-retained prostheses (stated in the conclusion).
- **Analysis**: Linear regression of marginal bone loss on abutment height (mesial and distal).
- **Not available in the abstract**: implant brands, absolute bone-loss values in mm, regression coefficients, p-values, patient inclusion criteria.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Average mesial and distal marginal bone loss of the control group (25 patients) was significantly more than twice that of the test group (26 patients), while average abutment height was similar.
- Linear regression showed a statistically significant inverse relationship between marginal bone loss and abutment height in both groups.
- The regression-line intercept, mesially and distally, was 50% lower for the test group than for the control group.
- Marginal bone loss was annulled at an abutment height of 2.5 mm (test, platform-switched) and 3.0 mm (control, conventional).
- No statistically significant differences in marginal bone loss between implants in native maxillary bone and grafted areas.
- Authors' conclusion: the shorter the abutment, the greater the marginal bone loss in cement-retained prostheses; abutment height had greater influence in platform-switched than in non-platform-switched implants.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only**: absolute mm values, regression coefficients and exact p-values are not available here, so the "2.5 / 3.0 mm" thresholds cannot be checked against the underlying plots or confidence bands.
- **Retrospective design**; implant type (PS vs conventional) is confounded with implant system, as the groups used two different implant types with a similar neck design.
- **12-month horizon** after loading; durability beyond one year is unknown from this source.
- **Maxilla, 4.7-mm-diameter, cement-retained only** — transfer to mandible, other diameters or screw-retained restorations is not supported by this abstract.
- The thresholds come from regression fitting (where the line reaches zero loss), not from a prospective test of a cut-off.

## 7. Glossary

- **Abutment height** — transmucosal height of the prosthetic abutment above the implant platform.
- **Platform switching (PS)** — abutment narrower than the implant platform, moving the implant–abutment junction inward.
- **Marginal bone loss (MBL)** — radiographic crestal bone loss around the implant (mesial and distal here).
- **Transcrestal sinus augmentation** — sinus floor elevation through the crestal osteotomy (here with mineralized xenograft).
- **Annulled bone loss** — regression-line intercept with zero MBL (2.5 mm test, 3.0 mm control).
