## Why Ingested

Part of a five-paper sweep on abutment-screw tightening and torque. This paper adds a gap the wiki lacked: a direct torque-dose comparison (25/30/35/40 Ncm) on a single 6° cone morse joint, read out as SEM microgap rather than as preload or removal-torque loss. It extends the microgap-under-load picture of [[prosthetic-materials/abutment-screw/angermair-2024-microgap-abutment-displacement-implant-connection]] (microgaps exist before loading, regardless of cone angle) by asking how the tightening torque itself shapes that gap, and it provides independent bench support for the finding of [[prosthetic-materials/abutment-screw/pardal-pelaez-2017-preload-loss-abutment-screws-dynamic-fatigue]] that Morse-taper joints resist fatigue-related loosening well. → [[prosthetic-materials/abutment-screw/lorusso-2025-tightening-torques-cone-morse-fatigue]]

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro fatigue study of 80 implants with a 6° cone morse internal-hex joint (10 per group; 4 torque levels x loaded/unloaded control), embedded at 30° and tightened to 25, 30, 35 or 40 Ncm, with implant-abutment microgap measured on longitudinal sections by SEM and digital microscopy.

Microgap differed significantly across torque levels in all four conditions (Kruskal-Wallis p=0.0121 loaded flexion, 0.0027 loaded extension, 0.0337 control flexion, 0.0021 control extension), and 30 Ncm gave the smallest microgap; no joint failed and all loaded groups showed more distortion than unloaded controls.

The authors recommend 30 Ncm for this particular joint, but the evidence is a single-design bench test with n=10 per cell, no raw microgap values reported in the text, and internal inconsistencies in the loading protocol, so it supports direction of effect rather than a transferable torque number.

## 세줄요약

6° 콘 모스(cone morse) 내부 육각 연결 임플란트 80개(그룹당 10개; 토크 4수준 x 하중군/비하중 대조군)를 30°로 매립해 25·30·35·40 Ncm로 체결한 뒤, 종단면에서 주사전자현미경(SEM)·디지털 현미경으로 임플란트-어버트먼트 미세간극(microgap)을 측정한 in vitro 피로시험.

네 조건 모두에서 토크군 간 미세간극 차이가 유의했고(Kruskal-Wallis p=0.0121 하중 굴곡, 0.0027 하중 신전, 0.0337 대조 굴곡, 0.0021 대조 신전), 30 Ncm에서 미세간극이 가장 작았다. 파손된 연결부는 없었고, 하중군은 모두 비하중 대조군보다 변형이 컸다.

저자들은 이 연결부에 30 Ncm을 제안하지만, 단일 디자인 벤치 시험·셀당 n=10·본문에 원자료 수치 없음·하중 프로토콜의 내적 불일치 때문에 효과의 방향을 보여줄 뿐 다른 시스템에 옮길 토크 수치로는 쓸 수 없다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Compares four tightening torques (25/30/35/40 Ncm) on one 6° cone morse internal-hex joint using SEM-measured microgap as the outcome, with a matched unloaded control for each torque.
- Reports that the torque level, not only the cyclic load, changes the interface: torque groups differed significantly even without loading.
- Identifies 30 Ncm as the torque with the smallest microgap, with 35 and 40 Ncm showing tiny marks on the screw head and frictional metal flaking in the deep part of the cone.
- Argues for testing wider torque ranges than the usual manufacturer value, including potentially non-optimal torques.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: in-vitro cyclic fatigue test with SEM/optical morphometry; four torque groups, each with a loaded (Test) and unloaded (Control) arm.
- **Specimens**: 80 implants (CLC implant, Vicenza, Italy), 6° cone morse joint with hexagonal anti-rotational index and platform switching, SLA surface, internal conical contact length 0.80 mm, total internal chamber 2.30 mm; sample size by G*Power (effect size 0.45, alpha 0.05, power 0.80).
- **Set-up**: embedded in resin blocks at 30 degrees +/- 2; abutment torqued with a calibrated electronic torque meter; single-axis ZT-HIGH (IMADA) fatigue device, sinusoidal load control, Fmax 150 N and Fmin 9 N, 20 +/- 5 C and 65% +/- 4% humidity; loading frequency listed as 3 Hz for the 25 Ncm group and 15 Hz for the other three.
- **Outcome**: microgap at the two peripheral contact points (flexion and extension side) on longitudinally sectioned specimens (Technovit 7200 VLC), SEM plus transmitted-light digital microscopy; macroscopic naked-eye inspection of test groups.
- **Statistics**: Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn's post-test across torques; Mann-Whitney versus control; Wilcoxon for flexion versus extension; p<0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Macroscopic**: no coupling failure in any group; tiny marks on the screw head at 35 and 40 Ncm.
- **Microscopic**: 25 and 30 Ncm showed intact interfaces without deformation lines; 35 Ncm showed friction flaking in the deepest part of the connection; 40 Ncm showed evident frictional metal flaking in the apical angular surface of the abutment (250x), with the marginal seal still intact.
- **Kruskal-Wallis across torques** (Table 1): loaded flexion p=0.0121 (H=10.93); loaded extension p=0.0027 (H=14.17); control flexion p=0.0337 (H=8.693); control extension p=0.0021 (H=14.66).
- **Dunn's post-test** (Table 2, rank-sum differences): loaded extension 25 vs 30 Ncm 18.1 (p<0.01) and 25 vs 40 Ncm 15.25 (p<0.05); loaded flexion 30 vs 35 Ncm -16.60 (p<0.01); control flexion 25 vs 30 Ncm 13.95 (p<0.05); control extension 25 vs 30 Ncm 17.65 (p<0.01) and 30 vs 35 Ncm -15 (p<0.05). The sign pattern is consistent with 30 Ncm having the lowest-ranked microgap.
- **Loaded vs unloaded**: the text states the difference was significant and that loading caused more distortion (microgap larger than in unloaded controls).
- Absolute microgap values (micrometers) are given only in figures; they are not in the text retrieved.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single implant design and single cone angle (6°); no comparison to other connection types, implant dimensions or loading angles; authors call for different cone morse designs to be tested.
- Small sample (10 per cell) and a power calculation based on an assumed effect size of 0.45.
- Simulated fatigue in dry laboratory conditions at room temperature; no fracture-load testing or fractography.
- Internal inconsistencies in the report: implant diameter given as 3.5 mm in one place and 3.8 mm in another; the loading described as 1 x 10^4 loads in the abstract and methods but as an infinite-life limit of 1,000,000 cycles in the test description; the 25 Ncm group listed at 3 Hz versus 15 Hz for the others (a possible confound); one macroscopic result paragraph for 40 Ncm is labelled "Group II"; and the Discussion mentions a "significant reduction" in microgap with cyclic loading while the Results state that loading produced more distortion.
- Preload, removal torque and screw loosening were not measured, although the null hypothesis is phrased in terms of screw loosening.

## 6. Related Work

- Angermair 2024: synchrotron microgap measurement across cone angles under static load, finding microgaps even before load.
- Pardal-Pelaez 2017 (systematic review): post-fatigue torque loss and the better loosening resistance of Morse-taper internal connections.
- Gherke et al. (cited in the paper): 11° cone morse joint with the lowest microgap at 40 Ncm; the authors note optimal cone morse torques in the literature span roughly 20 to 35 Ncm depending on design.
- Bittencourt et al. (cited): conical versus internal-hex connections under cyclic loading for microleakage and loosening.

## 7. Glossary

- **Cone morse (Morse taper) joint**: cone-in-cone internal implant-abutment interface held by friction; here a 6° taper.
- **Microgap**: micrometer-scale space between implant and abutment at the joint, relevant to leakage and micromovement.
- **Flexion / extension points**: the compressed and tensioned sides of the joint under off-axis loading.
- **Kruskal-Wallis / Dunn's test**: non-parametric multi-group comparison and its pairwise post-test.
