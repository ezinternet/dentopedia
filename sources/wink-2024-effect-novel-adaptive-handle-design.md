## Why Ingested

Ingested as part of the 2026-07-20 치위생사 스케일링 술기 (dental hygienist scaling technique) topic sweep. Applies-to [[wiki/periodontics/lodigkeit-2026-periodontal-instrumentation-enamel-cementum-review]]: that narrative review ranks hand instrumentation as the most abrasive debridement modality for cementum/enamel but with the best tactile feedback among the three professional modalities — this pilot study quantifies the other, clinician-facing side of that same hand-instrumentation trade-off (musculoskeletal load on the hygienist), and shows an adaptive handle design can reduce it without apparently sacrificing tactile feedback.

## Three-line Summary

Pilot in vivo randomized crossover study (n=10 hygienists, 5 with musculoskeletal disorders [MSD] / 5 without) comparing the ergonomic performance of three Barnhart 5/6 periodontal curette handle designs — a novel adaptive silicone-overlay handle vs. two conventional rigid handles — during a standardized typodont scaling task.

The adaptive handle (Curette A) significantly reduced instrumentation-related fatigue and improved comfort in both groups, and significantly cut measured muscle work (surface EMG of 4 hand/forearm muscles) in non-MSD testers, without sacrificing tactile feedback; hygienists with MSDs expended roughly double the muscle work and reported ~68% worse comfort / ~99% worse fatigue than non-MSD colleagues regardless of which curette was used.

Pilot-scale (n=10) findings support adaptive/cushioned handle designs as a mitigation for MSD-related instrumentation burden, but the study used typodont manikins rather than live patients, could not blind testers to instrument identity, and awaits confirmation in larger follow-up trials already noted as underway by the authors.

## 세줄요약

파일럿 인비보(in vivo) 무작위 교차설계 연구(치위생사 n=10, 근골격계질환(Musculoskeletal Disorder, MSD) 있는 군 5명·없는 군 5명)로, 신형 적응형(Adaptive) 실리콘 오버레이 손잡이 큐렛과 기존 경성(rigid) 손잡이 큐렛 2종(Barnhart 5/6)의 인간공학적 성능을 표준화된 타이포던트(Typodont) 스케일링 과제로 비교.

적응형 큐렛(Curette A)은 양 군 모두에서 피로도를 유의하게 줄이고 편안함을 개선했으며, 비-MSD 군에서는 표면근전도(Surface Electromyography, sEMG, 손/전완 4개 근육) 측정 근육 작업량도 유의하게 감소시켰고 촉각 피드백은 손상되지 않았음; MSD 군은 큐렛 종류와 무관하게 비-MSD 군보다 근육 작업량이 약 2배, 편안함은 평균 68% 나쁘고 피로도는 평균 99% 나쁨.

소규모(n=10) 파일럿 결과로 적응형·쿠션형 손잡이 설계가 MSD 관련 시술 부담 완화에 도움될 수 있음을 시사하나, 실제 환자가 아닌 타이포던트 모형 사용, 술자 눈가림 불가능, 대규모 후속연구 필요라는 한계가 있음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study to evaluate the ergonomic performance of a novel adaptive/bendable-core, silicone-overlay curette handle head-to-head against two conventional rigid handle designs using a mixed objective (surface EMG) + subjective (VAS) design.
- One of the first studies to stratify instrument-ergonomics outcomes by clinician musculoskeletal-disorder (MSD) status, not just by instrument design.
- Quantifies the MSD burden itself: hygienists with MSDs expend significantly more muscle work and report ~68% worse comfort / ~99% worse fatigue than colleagues without MSDs, regardless of which curette is used.
- Adaptive Curette A significantly outperformed both rigid designs on comfort and fatigue in both MSD and non-MSD groups, and reduced measured muscle work in non-MSD testers, all while preserving tactile feedback (no significant cross-instrument difference in tactile-sensitivity scores).
- The wider, silicone-covered rigid handle (Curette C) outperformed the narrower resin rigid handle (Curette B) on palm comfort specifically — consistent with prior literature on handle diameter/cushioning.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Pilot in vivo, within-subject (randomized crossover) design; IRB-exempt (UC Irvine, de-identified data only).
- 10 right-handed hygienists, ≥5 years clinical practice at ≥3 days/week, all current or former periodontal-instrumentation instructors at six California dental hygiene schools (USC, West Coast University, Cypress College, Cerritos College, San Joaquin Valley College, Concorde College). 5 testers had a diagnosed instrumentation-related MSD/repetitive stress injury (RSI)/chronic hand pain; 5 had no hand/wrist/finger injury or disorder within the prior 6 months.
- Each tester used all 3 curette models, in a sequence randomized by research-randomizer software (randomizer.org), on typodont manikins seeded with standardized artificial calculus (hardened 18 h) and biofilm, mounted on an adjustable dental chair. A 20-minute rest period separated each arm (validated in a prior study to allow evaluation parameters to return to baseline); testers received a 20-second instructional video plus 5 minutes of familiarization for the novel handle.
- Standardized 8-minute scaling protocol per curette (1 minute × 8 tooth-surface segments spanning lower/upper anterior facial and lingual surfaces plus lower/upper right/left posterior buccal and lingual surfaces), identical sequence for all 3 curettes; each curette re-sharpened by the same instructor before each use.
- Outcomes: (1) surface EMG (sEMG) of 4 muscles used to grip/manipulate the instrument — Abductor Pollicis Brevis (APB), First Dorsal Interosseous (FDI), Flexor Pollicis Longus (FPL), Extensor Digitorum Communis (EDC) — normalized to each muscle's own 15-second maximum voluntary isometric contraction (MVC) baseline; signals rectified/filtered with a 2nd-order Butterworth filter (10 Hz high-pass). (2) Hard-copy VAS surveys (0 = best, 10 = worst) rating comfort, fatigue, and tactile sensitivity per instrument, plus open-ended comments. Testers could not be blinded to instrument identity (visually distinct handles), but all data extraction/analysis was performed by a single blinded investigator pre-calibrated to 95% reproducibility over 50 measurements.
- Statistics: General Linear Model (GLIM) with pairwise instrument comparisons, followed by Tukey's post hoc test (SPSS 19); significance level α < 0.05.
- Instruments compared (all Barnhart 5/6 universal curette pattern, stainless-steel tips): **Curette A** — in-house prototype with a flexible, universally adjustable core that adapts to hand/finger curvature, silicone overlay grip (fabricated in the authors' Engineering and Materials Science laboratory). **Curette B** — conventional rigid resin handle (Paradise Dental Technologies). **Curette C** — conventional rigid handle with a cushioned, thermally insulated silicone overlay (Iris 4696-500, Benco Dental).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Non-MSD testers**: Curette A significantly better than rigid Curettes B and C on all comfort and fatigue VAS categories; tactile-sensitivity scores did not differ significantly between instruments; Curette C significantly better than Curette B for palm comfort.
- **MSD testers**: Curette A significantly better than Curette B on all comfort and fatigue categories; Curette A vs. Curette C favored A on fatigue but did not reach significance; Curette C significantly better than Curette B for palm comfort; tactile sensitivity not significantly different across instruments.
- **MSD vs. non-MSD, pooled across all 3 instruments**: mean comfort was 57–74% worse (mean 68%) and mean fatigue was 97–100% worse (mean ~99%) in hygienists with MSDs.
- **Muscle work (sEMG)**: total muscle work to complete the standardized scaling task was significantly greater in MSD testers than non-MSD testers, independent of curette used. In non-MSD testers, Curette A required significantly less total muscle work than Curette B or C. In MSD testers, Curette A also trended lowest (Curette B highest), but the difference did not reach significance (inter-subject SD up to 28% in this group).
- All 10 testers completed the study in full protocol compliance (no dropouts).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Testers could not be blinded to instrument identity because of each curette's characteristic appearance; mitigated by blinding data extraction/analysis to a single calibrated investigator.
- Typodont/manikin substrate rather than live patients — trades ecological validity for a standardized, reproducible testing surface.
- Small pilot sample (n=10; 5 per subgroup); large inter-subject variability (SD up to 28%) limited statistical significance for some muscle-work comparisons in the MSD group.
- Only one curette pattern tested (Barnhart 5/6 universal curette); generalizability to other instrument shapes (e.g., Gracey curettes, ultrasonic tips) untested.
- Authors report expanded follow-up clinical studies already underway: larger sample size/scope/duration, plus new evaluation criteria for instrumentation efficacy/speed and hand-wrist-body positioning during use.

## 6. Related Work

- Discussion section cites two prior studies (footnote references stripped in the plain-text extraction used for this ingest) reporting improved ergonomic outcomes for an earlier iteration of the same adaptive/bendable handle vs. (i) a narrower-diameter rigid stainless-steel instrument and (ii) conventional rigid stainless-steel/resin/silicone curettes — consistent in direction with this study's findings.
- [[wiki/periodontics/lodigkeit-2026-periodontal-instrumentation-enamel-cementum-review]] characterizes the same "hand instrumentation" modality from the hard-tissue-abrasion side (hand > ultrasonic > air polishing for cementum loss), complementing this study's clinician-ergonomics side of the hand-instrumentation trade-off.
- Broader literature cited on: modified instrument weight/balance/shape/size/surface properties for ergonomics; wider, silicone-covered handles improving comfort/fatigue/tactile-work outcomes; and the general mechanical principle that a larger hand-tool contact area lowers force/area and reduces stress on hands and fingers.

## 7. Glossary

- **MSD (Musculoskeletal Disorder)**: work-related pain/dysfunction of muscles, tendons, nerves, or joints; common among dental clinicians due to repetitive, high-force, non-neutral hand postures during instrumentation.
- **RSI (Repetitive Stress Injury)**: cumulative-trauma injury from repeated movement/loading; used alongside MSD terminology in this paper.
- **sEMG (surface Electromyography)**: non-invasive recording of muscle electrical action potentials via skin-surface electrodes, used here to quantify muscle work during instrumentation.
- **MVC (Maximum Voluntary [isometric] Contraction)**: a brief maximal-effort muscle contraction used to normalize each muscle's sEMG signal to a 100% activity baseline.
- **VAS (Visual Analog Scale)**: 0–10 subjective rating scale (0 = best, 10 = worst) used here for comfort, fatigue, and tactile-sensitivity ratings.
- **GLIM (General Linear Model)**: statistical model used for pairwise instrument comparisons, followed by Tukey's post hoc test.
- **Barnhart 5/6**: a universal curette pattern usable on both anterior and posterior tooth surfaces; the base instrument pattern shared by all 3 tested curettes.
- **Typodont**: an artificial dental model (teeth mounted in a manikin) used to standardize the scaling substrate across testers and instruments.
