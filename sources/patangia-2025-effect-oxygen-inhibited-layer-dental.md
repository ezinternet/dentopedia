# Effect of Oxygen-Inhibited Layer of Dental Adhesives on Bond Strength: A Systematic Review

## Source Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD42024574190) evaluating the effect of the oxygen-inhibited layer (OIL) on bond strength of dental adhesives. Searched PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar (2000–2024). 71 records retrieved → 8 studies included (n=730 teeth: 450 bovine, 280 human). Meta-analysis not feasible due to heterogeneity (QUIN risk of bias: 40–70%, mostly medium risk).

**Key findings:**
- OIL **increases** bond strength with light-cured composite resin
- OIL **decreases** bond strength and increases bond failure with chemically-cured composite resin
- The etching mode (self-etch vs. total-etch) does not override the curing-mechanism-dependent direction of effect
- Surface free energy changes with OIL but cannot reliably predict bond strength outcomes
- Adhesive failure mode predominated regardless of OIL condition

**OIL characteristics:** Thickness 4–40 µm; influenced by monomer composition (HEMA → thicker), filler morphology, initiator system, and oxygen diffusion dynamics.

**OIL modification strategies evaluated:** ethanol application, extended curing time, physical barriers (Mylar strips), thermal activation (110–120 °C), phosphine additives (DPPS).

## Why Ingested

OIL effect on bonding is a clinically critical but underappreciated phenomenon. This SR is the first focused synthesis clarifying the directional effect: beneficial for light-cured composites (do NOT remove OIL), potentially harmful for self-cure/dual-cure scenarios. Directly relevant to layered restorations, indirect restorations cemented with dual-cure cements, and composite repair protocols.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA 2020, n=8 studies, 730 teeth: 450 bovine, 280 human) evaluating the effect of the oxygen-inhibited layer (OIL) of dental adhesives on bond strength.
OIL increases bond strength with light-cured composite resin; OIL decreases bond strength and increases failure with chemically-cured composite resin; OIL thickness ranges 4–40 µm.
Do not remove OIL before light-cured composite layering; for dual-cure/self-cure cementation, OIL management strategies (Mylar strips, ethanol, thermal activation) may be warranted, but high heterogeneity precludes meta-analysis.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (PRISMA 2020, n=8 연구, 730개 치아)로 치과 접착제의 산소억제층(Oxygen-Inhibited Layer, OIL)이 결합강도에 미치는 영향을 평가함.
OIL은 광중합(Light-Cured) 복합레진과의 결합강도를 증가시키지만, 화학중합(Chemically-Cured) 복합레진에서는 결합강도를 감소시키고 결합 실패를 증가시킴; OIL 두께 4–40 µm.
광중합 전 OIL 제거는 불필요; 자가중합·이중중합(Dual-Cure) 수복에서 OIL 제거 또는 물리적 차단이 유용할 수 있으나 이질성으로 메타분석(Meta-Analysis) 불가.
