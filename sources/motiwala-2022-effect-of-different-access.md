## Why Ingested

This network meta-analysis of in vitro fracture toughness data synthesizes evidence across 14 controlled studies comparing conservative endodontic cavities (CEC) against traditional endodontic cavities (TEC) and alternative designs. It ranks highest-evidence support for minimally invasive access decisions — directly informing [[endodontics/anatomy]] strategy selection and extending prior single-paper comparisons in [[overviews/endodontic-access-cavity-decision-tree]].

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review and network meta-analysis of 14 in vitro studies (10 studies, n=456 teeth evaluated for molar fracture resistance) comparing traditional vs. conservative endodontic access cavity designs and their effect on fracture toughness of endodontically treated teeth.

Conservative endodontic cavities (CEC) showed the least reduction in fracture resistance (MD: −365.59, 95% CI [−759.02, 27.83]) compared to intact teeth, while traditional cavities (TEC) caused the greatest reduction (MD: −927.52, 95% CI [−1304.80, −550.24]); CEC ranked second most favorable access design (SUCRA 51.4%) after intact teeth (85.4%).

Clinical bottom line: in vitro evidence supports conservative access cavity designs for preserving fracture resistance in endodontically treated teeth, though confidence was moderate and all studies were laboratory-based; clinical long-term outcome data needed.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Network meta-analysis methodology applied to endodontic access cavity designs — first to rank multiple cavity designs (TEC, CEC, truss, ninja) using SUCRA and formal confidence-in-evidence framework (CINeMA/GRADE)
- Quantifies fracture resistance differential: CEC preserves 360+ N more force than TEC when compared to intact baseline (MD difference: −365.59 vs −927.52)
- Demonstrates conservative cavity designs reduce structural compromise of endodontically treated teeth, supporting minimally invasive endodontic practice

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review + network meta-analysis of in vitro comparative studies
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, LILACS, OpenGrey, hand search (through September 2020)
- **n**: 844 articles screened; 14 studies included in SR; 10 studies (n=456 teeth) in network meta-analysis for molars
- **Inclusion**: In vitro studies on extracted endodontically treated human teeth comparing fracture toughness/resistance across access cavity designs
- **Exclusion**: Studies on single-rooted teeth (incisors/canines/premolars) in main network; clinical/observational data; non-English publications
- **Outcomes**: Primary = fracture resistance (MPa, N, or load-to-fracture); secondary = biomechanical properties
- **Risk of Bias**: Modified risk-of-bias tool for in vitro studies; overall moderate bias
- **Analysis**: Random-effects frequentist network meta-analysis with mean difference (MD) and 95% CI; SUCRA (Surface Under Cumulative RAnking Curve) for design ranking; CINeMA framework for evidence confidence (GRADE-adapted)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Fracture Resistance by Access Design (vs Intact Teeth Baseline)

| Access Design | Mean Difference (95% CI) | SUCRA Value | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intact teeth (reference) | 0 [baseline] | 85.4% | Ranked 1st (highest fracture resistance) |
| Conservative endodontic cavities (CEC) | −365.59 [−759.02, 27.83] | 51.4% | Ranked 2nd; 95% CI crosses zero (not significant) |
| Truss access cavities | −548.78 [−918.29, −179.27] | 40.2% | Ranked 3rd |
| Traditional endodontic cavities (TEC) | −927.52 [−1304.80, −550.24] | 15.3% | Ranked 4th (greatest compromise); CI excludes zero (significant) |
| Ninja cavities | —not enough data— | — | Insufficient studies for NMA |

**Key Magnitudes**: 
- CEC vs TEC difference: ~562 N preservation (−365.59 − (−927.52) = +561.93 N stronger with CEC)
- CEC confidence interval: includes zero, suggesting non-significant advantage over intact vs TEC (moderate-to-low confidence)

### Evidence Confidence (CINeMA/GRADE)
- Overall confidence: **low to moderate** across all comparisons
- Main limitations: Publication bias, inconsistency (heterogeneity I²), indirectness (all in vitro), small sample sizes per design comparison, high risk of bias in several included studies

### Study Quality
- Risk of bias: **Moderate overall**
- Included studies: heterogeneous outcomes, specimen preparation, test speeds, moisture conditions (e.g., dry vs wet, thermomechanical cycling), tooth types (mostly molars)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

### Limitations
- **In vitro only**: All 14 studies laboratory-based; no clinical long-term fracture/survival data to validate lab findings
- **Moderate to low confidence**: Multiple evidence-quality downgrades per CINeMA (publication bias, inconsistency, risk-of-bias distribution)
- **Molar-skewed**: Network meta-analysis focused on mandibular molars (10 studies, n=456); limited data on premolars, incisors (which have different anatomy/stress distributions)
- **Outcome heterogeneity**: Studies used different load-to-fracture protocols (compression vs shear), moisture states, thermomechanical cycling presence/absence, confounding restoration materials
- **Small sample sizes**: Several included studies had n=10–20 teeth per group; power/precision limited
- **Publication bias**: Hand-search yielded few unpublished/negative studies; database screening 844→14 may reflect selective reporting of significant findings
- **No long-term clinical validation**: Fracture resistance ≠ clinical fracture incidence; confounders (occlusal trauma, restoration type, patient factors, time-to-failure) not captured in vitro

### Future Work
- In vivo prospective cohort or RCT: fracture incidence/survival of CEC vs TEC-restored teeth over 3–5 years
- Standardized testing protocols: ISO 6874 or similar harmonization to reduce outcome heterogeneity
- Subgroup analysis by tooth type (premolar/molar) and restoration material
- Investigation of CEC technique learning curve and operator dependence (clinical adoption risk)
- Outcome: Extend SUCRA confidence intervals to clinical endpoints (pain-free survival, crack/cusp fracture event rates)

## 7. Glossary
- **CEC**: Conservative endodontic cavity — minimally invasive access removing only canal-obstructing dentin; preserves structural dentin in walls/roof/pulp chamber walls
- **TEC**: Traditional endodontic cavity — triangular/trapezoidal access removing dentin for visibility; larger outline on occlusal surface
- **Fracture resistance / Fracture toughness**: Maximum load (Newtons, N) or stress (MPa) before tooth fracture under axial or off-axis load (in vitro); proxy for clinical fracture risk (validation variable)
- **SUCRA**: Surface Under Cumulative RAnking Curve (0–100%) — probability of each design being ranked best/optimal across all pairwise comparisons in network; higher SUCRA = better rank
- **Network meta-analysis (NMA)**: Frequentist meta-analysis comparing 3+ treatments (access designs) in a network; provides both direct (head-to-head study data) and indirect (transitive through common comparator) evidence
- **CINeMA**: Confidence in Network Meta-Analysis framework — GRADE-adapted tool assessing methodological quality, evidence consistency, publication bias, and directness for NMA results
- **MD**: Mean difference — effect size for continuous outcomes (fracture load); negative MD = worse outcome (lower fracture resistance vs reference)
- **95% CI**: 95% confidence interval — range of plausible true effects; if CI crosses zero for a continuous outcome, the difference is not statistically significant at α=0.05
- **Thermomechanical cycling**: Laboratory aging protocol simulating oral temperature fluctuations and chewing forces over time (e.g., 10,000 cycles, 5–55°C) to approximate in vivo degradation
