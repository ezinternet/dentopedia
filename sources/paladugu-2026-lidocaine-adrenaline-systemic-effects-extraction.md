## Why Ingested

→ [[local-anesthesia/paladugu-2026-lidocaine-adrenaline-systemic-effects-extraction]]

RCT quantifying the physiological effects of adding adrenaline to 2% lignocaine during tooth extractions. Provides specific magnitude data (BP ΔSystolic +12.69%, blood glucose +14.63%, pulse rate +15.84%) relevant to the clinical safety section of the lidocaine dosage calculator and special patient population guidance.

## Summary

Prospective RCT, 110 patients, 5 months (Feb–Jun 2025), Vijayawada, India.
Group A: lignocaine 2% + adrenaline 1:80,000 (n=55)
Group B: lignocaine 2% plain (n=55)
Parameters measured at 3 timepoints: baseline, 10min post-anesthesia, post-extraction.

Key results — Group A vs Group B at post-extraction:
- Systolic BP: 138.20±10.45 vs 126.75±8.45 mmHg (p<0.001) → +12.69% from baseline
- Diastolic BP: 87.10±8.15 vs 79.85±7.10 mmHg (p<0.001) → +10.60%
- Blood glucose: 112.85±12.34 vs 101.90±10.55 mg/dL (p<0.001) → +14.63%
- Pulse rate: 88.40±7.20 vs 78.45±6.10 bpm (p<0.001) → +15.84%

Multiple regression: adrenaline use was the dominant predictor for all parameters.
Age was a significant predictor for BP (older patients → greater hypertensive response).
Gender and BMI were not significant.

Both groups were comparable at baseline (age, gender, BMI, fasting glucose).

## Clinical Implications

- Adrenaline-containing lidocaine causes significant transient elevations in BP, HR, and blood glucose
- Plain lidocaine: minimal systemic impact (within 1.85–4.19% change from baseline)
- Special caution warranted for: hypertensive patients, diabetics, elderly, cardiovascular disease
- Magnitude of change: ~11-12 mmHg SBP, ~7 mmHg DBP, ~14 mg/dL glucose, ~10 bpm HR
- Changes are transient but require monitoring in medically compromised patients
- In healthy adults, changes are generally safe; careful monitoring is standard protocol

## Limitations

- Single-center (India)
- 1:80,000 concentration (vs standard 1:100,000) — higher epi concentration
- Short study timeline; no long-term follow-up
- Relatively small n=110
