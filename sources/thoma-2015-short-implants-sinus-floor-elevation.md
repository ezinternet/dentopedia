## Why Ingested

This multicentre RCT directly addresses a key clinical question for posterior maxilla with reduced residual bone height: can **short implants (6 mm)** avoid sinus floor elevation (SFE) and achieve comparable patient-reported outcomes at 1 year as **longer implants (11–15 mm) with SFE**? The data inform treatment choice between reduced-dimension implants and augmentation procedures, anchoring [[implants/short-narrow/thoma-2015-short-implants-sinus-floor-elevation]] and contextualizing related work like [[sinus-lift/lateral/thoma-2024-short-6mm-vs-long-implants-sinus-elevation-10year-rct]].

## Three-line Summary

Multicentre RCT in 91 patients (101 implants) randomly assigned to short implants (6 mm) placed in residual bone height 6–8 mm, or longer implants (11–15 mm) placed after sinus floor elevation in residual bone height 3–6 mm, with follow-up at 1 year of loading.

At 1 year, patient-reported outcome measures (OHIP-49, visual analog scales for pain/function/aesthetics/general satisfaction) showed no statistically significant differences between groups; surgical morbidity differed with more invasive SFE in the long-implant group.

Demographics and baseline characteristics were comparable; the study establishes the trial framework for Part 2 (radiographic outcomes) and longer-term follow-up of the same cohort.

## 세줄요약

후방 상악에서 잔존골높이 6–8 mm인 경우 6 mm 단폭경 임플란트, 잔존골높이 3–6 mm인 경우 부비동저거상술(SFE) 후 11–15 mm 장폭경 임플란트를 무작위 배정한 다기관 RCT(91명, 101개 임플란트), 1년 하중 시 추적.

1년 시점에서 환자보고결과(OHIP-49, 통증/기능/심미/전반적 만족도 VAS)는 두 군 간 유의한 차이가 없었으며, 수술 합병증 측면에서는 부비동저거상술군에서 더 침습적이었음.

기저 특성은 양군 간 유사했고, 본 논문은 동일 코호트의 방사선학적 결과(Part 2) 및 장기 추적의 기틀을 제시함.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Randomized controlled multicentre study comparing short dental implants (6 mm) versus longer dental implants (11–15 mm) in combination with sinus floor elevation procedures. Part 1: demographics and patient‐reported outcomes at 1 year of loading
- **Authors**: Daniel S. Thoma, Nadja Zeltner, Irene Sanz-Martin, Ronald E. Jung, Christoph H.F. Hämmerle
- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Periodontology 2015;42(1):72–80
- **DOI**: 10.1111/jcpe.12323
- **Study design**: Multicentre randomized controlled trial (RCT)
- **Funding**: Dentsply Implants
- **Setting**: University clinics (multicentre)
- **Follow-up**: 1 year of loading (Part 1)

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **Direct comparison**: Head-to-head RCT comparing short implants (6 mm) without SFE versus longer implants (11–15 mm) with SFE in the posterior maxilla.
2. **Patient-reported focus (Part 1)**: Reports demographics and PROMs at 1 year (OHIP-49 and VAS for pain, function, aesthetics, general satisfaction), showing equivalence in patient experience.
3. **Treatment strategy question**: Evaluates whether avoiding augmentation by using short implants yields comparable patient-centered outcomes to the established long-implant + SFE approach.
4. **Trial framework**: Establishes a well-defined cohort for subsequent radiographic/histologic/long-term reports from the same trial.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Participants**: 91 patients randomly assigned, 101 implants placed (some bilateral). Inclusion based on residual bone height in posterior maxilla: short-implant group 6–8 mm; long-implant + SFE group 3–6 mm.
- **Intervention**: Test group — 6 mm dental implants (no sinus floor elevation). Control group — 11–15 mm implants after sinus floor elevation (lateral/window or internal approach as indicated per protocol; details in full text).
- **Randomization**: Multicentre RCT (allocation details in paper).
- **Outcomes (Part 1)**: Demographics, surgical/procedural parameters, and patient-reported outcomes (OHIP-49, VAS pain/function/aesthetics/general satisfaction) at 1 year of loading.
- **Analysis**: Between-group comparisons at 1 year.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Baseline**: Demographic and anatomical characteristics comparable between groups.
- **PROMs at 1 year**: OHIP-49 total scores and domain scores showed no statistically significant differences between short implants and long implants + SFE. VAS scores for pain, function, aesthetics, and general satisfaction also showed no significant between-group differences.
- **Morbidity**: The long-implant + SFE procedure was more invasive (expected), though patient-reported discomfort/satisfaction at 1 year did not differ significantly.
- **Conclusion (Part 1)**: At 1 year of loading, patient-reported outcomes were similar between 6 mm short implants and 11–15 mm implants placed with sinus floor elevation; clinical/radiographic outcomes reported separately (Part 2).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Short follow-up (1 year)**: Part 1 reports only 1-year PROMs/demographics; longer-term implant survival, MBL, complications, and cost-effectiveness require extended follow-up (addressed by subsequent reports of this trial, e.g. 3-year/10-year).
- **Part 1 scope**: No detailed radiographic bone-level outcomes in this paper (deferred to Part 2).
- **Generalisability**: Multicentre but protocol-driven with specific residual bone height windows; results may not generalize to all atrophic posterior maxilla scenarios.

## 6. Related Work

- **Same trial follow-up**: Thoma et al. Part 2 (radiographic outcomes) and later 3-year (Pohl et al. 2017, J Clin Periodontol) and 10-year (Thoma et al. 2024, Clin Oral Implants Res) reports.
- **Broader evidence**: SR/MA on short implants vs augmentation (e.g. Aleman et al. 2025, Toledano et al. 2022) contextualize these findings.
