## Why Ingested

Mined as a reference from [[wiki/bone-regeneration/bubalo-2026-bone-substitutes-alveolar-ridge-augmentation]], which lists demineralized tooth matrix / autologous dentin graft (ADG) as one of its seven bone-substitute categories but treats it briefly. This 2022 dedicated narrative review deep-dives the autogenous tooth bone graft (AUTO-BG) category — composition/biochemistry, preparation protocol (Smart Dentin Grinder), and per-application clinical outcomes (sinus lift, ridge augmentation, socket preservation, periodontal defects, alveolar cleft grafting, GBR) — giving the ADG row in Bubalo's Table 1 a fully sourced, evidence-detailed foundation.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review synthesizing the biology, preparation method, clinical applications, and complications of autogenous tooth bone graft (AUTO-BG), a technique using a patient's own extracted teeth (ground dentin/enamel) as an autogenous bone-graft substitute.

AUTO-BG shows biocompatibility and physical/biochemical properties resembling autogenous cortical bone (BMPs, type I collagen, calcium phosphates), with reported outcomes including ~5 mm ridge-width gain, ~96% implant survival after sinus augmentation, ~87% bone gain in GBR, and an overall implant failure rate of ~2.3–3.9% across pooled case series.

Complication rates (wound dehiscence, infection ~9.1% in one series, hematoma ~3.64%, marginal bone loss ~2.5 mm/5yr) are comparable to other graft sources, but the tedious chairside preparation, need for a specialized dentin grinder, limited graft quantity per tooth, and absence of long-term RCT data currently restrict AUTO-BG to a niche option for smaller craniofacial defects.

## 세줄요약

환자 자신의 발치 치아(분쇄된 상아질/법랑질)를 자가골 이식재 대용으로 사용하는 자가치아골이식(Autogenous Tooth Bone Graft, AUTO-BG)의 생물학·제작법·임상 적응증·합병증을 종합한 narrative review.

AUTO-BG는 자가 피질골(autogenous cortical bone)과 유사한 생체적합성·물리화학적 특성(BMP, type I 콜라겐, 인산칼슘)을 가지며, 보고된 결과로는 치조제 폭 증가 약 5mm, 상악동거상술 후 임플란트 생존율 약 96%, 골유도재생술(GBR)에서 골획득률 약 87%, 여러 case series를 종합한 전체 임플란트 실패율 약 2.3~4.0%.

합병증(창상개열, 감염 최대 9.1%, 혈종 3.64%, 5년간 변연골소실 약 2.5mm)은 타 이식재와 유사한 수준이나, 번거로운 chairside 제작 과정(전용 dentin grinder 필요), 치아당 확보 가능한 골이식재 양의 제한, 장기 RCT 데이터 부재로 인해 소규모 두개안면 결손에 국한된 틈새 옵션으로 남아있음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Consolidates the biochemical case for autogenous tooth bone graft (AUTO-BG): human dentin is 55% inorganic / 45% organic by weight, sharing near-identical composition to bone (type I collagen 95% of the organic collagenous fraction, non-collagenous proteins DSPP/osteopontin/osteonectin/bone sialoprotein, and BMPs/fibroblast growth factors embedded in the demineralized dentin matrix, DDM).
- Details the two preparation formats — block type (root-form for socket preservation; root-on type for ridge augmentation) and powder type (crown-type/enamel = osteoconductive; root-type/dentine = osteoinductive+osteoconductive) — and the standardized Smart Dentin Grinder (Kometa Bio) chairside protocol: grind → NaOH/ethanol cleanser 7–10 min → PBS wash 3 min → ready for grafting.
- Maps AUTO-BG across eight clinical application domains with outcome data per domain: bone/ridge augmentation, sinus augmentation, periodontal intrabony/furcation defects, GBR, alveolar bone grafting in cleft patients, socket preservation, and restorative/endodontic/pathology uses.
- Provides a structured complications catalogue (8 categories) with quantified incidence pooled across cited case series (Lee 2013, Kim 2014, Jeong et al., Schwarz et al., Ramanauskaite et al., Gharpure et al. systematic review), directly comparable to complication profiles of allograft/xenograft/alloplast.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Non-systematic narrative review. No PRISMA flow diagram, no formal quality/risk-of-bias appraisal, no meta-analysis. Evidence is a first-author narrative synthesis pooling case series, small clinical trials, one systematic review (Gharpure et al., animal-based primary-stability data), and histological/SEM/XRD physical-property studies (largely from a South Korean AUTO-BG research group: Kim, Lee, Jun, Bessho, Wang, Schwarz). Given the review's narrative (non-systematic) design, all quantitative figures below should be read as reported-in-source-study values, not pooled/meta-analytic estimates.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Physical/biochemical: AUTO-BG SEM surface morphology, XRD crystallinity, and Ca/P dissolution rate all resemble autogenous cortical bone; crown portion has higher Ca:P ratio and high-crystalline hydroxyapatite vs. root portion's lower-crystalline calcium phosphates.
- Bioactivity: DDM induces bone formation in ~4 weeks vs. 8–12 weeks for partial (≈30% mineral) DDM (Zhang et al.).
- Clinical timelines: graft fully replaced by normal bone in 12–15 months (Kim et al.); implants in AUTO-BG-augmented ridges typically loadable within 5–7 months.
- Sinus augmentation: ~5 mm bone-height gain, ~0.76 mm/year bone loss post-loading, ~96% implant survival (Shavit et al.).
- Ridge augmentation: ~5 mm alveolar width gain, ~0.1 mm/year annual resorption (Ramanauskaite et al.); implants placed within 26 weeks with adequate primary stability.
- GBR: ~87% net bone gain with no significant difference whether a membrane was used (Lee et al.).
- Socket preservation: 15% horizontal bone loss (Radoczy-Drajko et al.), negligible vertical loss.
- Implant density/volume: mean bone density 981 HU with AUTO-BG vs. 968 HU for Bio-Oss; ~60% new-bone-volume-to-total-bone-volume ratio (Jun et al.).
- ISQ: mean 67.3 at placement, improving to mean 75.5 at second-stage surgery.
- Complications (pooled across cited series): wound dehiscence 2/9 (Lee 2013) and 1/12 (Kim 2014) patients; infection up to 9.1% (Jeong et al., n=51, worst-case series; others report negligible); hematoma 3.64% overall; marginal/crestal bone loss ~2.5 mm over 5 years (Kim et al.) or 0.28±0.13 mm vertical / 0.15±0.08 mm horizontal at 4–6 months (Cenicante et al. review); loss of primary stability 11.6% pooled across 3 animal studies only (Gharpure et al. SR — not reported in human studies); implant failure rate 2.3–4.0% across pooled human case series (Jeong 3.9%, Kim 3.4%, Lee 4.0%; 165 implants in 72 patients, ~6 failures).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Explicitly narrative (non-systematic): no formal search protocol registration, no PRISMA diagram, no risk-of-bias/quality appraisal, no meta-analysis — carries selection-bias risk.
- Evidence base is dominated by a small number of research groups (mostly South Korean case series: Kim, Lee, Jeong, Jun) with overlapping patient cohorts likely underlying several cited outcome figures — limits independent-replication confidence.
- Human data on loss of primary stability is absent; the only quantified figure (11.6%) comes from three animal-based studies in Gharpure et al.'s systematic review.
- Practical limitations acknowledged by the authors themselves: tedious/technique-sensitive graft-acquisition process, requirement for a specialized dentin grinder (Smart Dentin Grinder), limited graft quantity obtainable per tooth (constrains use to smaller defects), and lack of long-term RCT data — all currently restrict AUTO-BG's routine clinical adoption.

## 7. Glossary

- **AUTO-BG / AUTO-BT**: Autogenous tooth bone graft — bone-graft material derived from a patient's own extracted teeth.
- **DDM**: Demineralized dentine matrix — acid-treated, absorbable collagen scaffold retaining embedded BMPs/growth factors.
- **DBM**: Demineralized bone matrix.
- **FDBA / DFDBA**: Freeze-dried bone allograft / demineralized freeze-dried bone allograft.
- **BMP**: Bone morphogenetic protein.
- **DSPP**: Dentin sialophosphoprotein.
- **PDGF / VEGF / IGF / EGF / TGF-β**: Growth factors present in dentin matrix mediating mesenchymal stem cell induction, angiogenesis, and bone formation.
- **PRP / PRF**: Platelet-rich plasma / platelet-rich fibrin — autologous growth-factor concentrates often combined with AUTO-BG.
- **ISQ**: Implant Stability Quotient — resonance-frequency-analysis measure of implant primary/secondary stability.
- **GBR**: Guided bone regeneration.
- **XRD**: X-ray diffraction (used to assess crystalline structure of graft material).
