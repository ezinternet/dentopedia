## Why Ingested

Independent animal data on the same question as [[implants/osseodensification/wang-2017-effects-of-condensation-on-peri]] (murine condensation model): here in a larger-animal (minipig) tibia model, osteotome preparation was followed by lower removal-torque stability and histologic peri-implant trabecular fractures at day 7. Also gives a pre-osseodensification osteotome baseline for [[implants/osseodensification/tian-2019-alveolar-ridge-expansion-osseodensification-osteotome]], which compares osseodensification with the Summers osteotome.

## Three-line Summary

Minipig tibial-condyle model (6 Gottinger minipigs, 56 SLA ITI cylinder implants): sites prepared by conventional drilling (control A) vs the osteotome technique (experimental B); evaluation at 7 and 28 days by resonance frequency measurement (RFM), removal torque and ground-section histology.

Biomechanical (removal-torque) stability was significantly higher in the drilled control group at day 7 (P = 0.004) and remained 47% higher at day 28, while RFM showed no significant group difference; histology showed fractured peri-implant trabeculae in the osteotome group at day 7 but not at day 28.

The authors conclude that the decreased stability with the osteotome technique is based on peri-implant microfractures; this is the authors' interpretation, and the abstract reports no direct test of mechanism or any human data.

## 세줄요약

미니피그 경골 과두 모델 (Gottinger 미니피그 6마리, SLA ITI 원통형 임플란트 56개): 통상 드릴 준비 (대조군 A) vs 골압축기 술식 (Osteotome technique, 실험군 B); 7일·28일에 공진주파수 측정 (Resonance Frequency Measurement, RFM), 제거 토크, 연마절편 조직 평가.

제거 토크 기반 안정성은 7일째 드릴 대조군이 유의하게 높았고 (P = 0.004), 28일에도 47% 높게 유지되었으나 RFM은 군 간 유의한 차이가 없었다; 조직 소견에서 골압축기군은 7일째 임플란트 주위 골소주 골절이 관찰되었고 28일에는 관찰되지 않았다.

저자들은 골압축기 술식의 안정성 저하가 임플란트 주위 미세골절 (Microfracture)에 기인한다고 결론지었다; 이는 저자의 해석이며 초록에는 기전의 직접 검증도, 사람 데이터도 없다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Compares osteotome vs conventional drill site preparation for SLA ITI cylinder implants in a minipig tibia model.
- Combines RFM, removal-torque testing and ground-section histology at 7 and 28 days.
- Authors attribute the lower stability with the osteotome technique to peri-implant microfractures.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Animal experiment, controlled two-technique comparison
- **Animals/implants**: 6 Gottinger minipigs; 56 implants in the cranial and caudal tibia condyle
- **Groups**: control A (drills) vs experimental B (osteotome technique)
- **Timepoints**: half of the animals sacrificed at day 7 and half at day 28 (per the abstract)
- **Outcomes**: RFM at placement and at sacrifice; removal torque ("biomechanical testing"); histology on 30-50 micrometre ground sections

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Day 7: biomechanical stability significantly higher in control A than in osteotome group B (P = 0.004).
- Day 28: control stability remained 47% higher than group B (the abstract prints the P value as "P > 0.001", apparently a typographical error; significance is described as significant).
- RFM: no significant difference between groups or over the experimental course.
- Histology: fractured trabeculae in peri-implant bone in the osteotome group at day 7, not at day 28.
- Authors' conclusion: decreased implant stability with the osteotome technique is based on microfractures in peri-implant bone.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Abstract-only ingest; per-group n, absolute torque values and quantitative histology are not captured.
- The microfracture explanation is the authors' conclusion drawn from co-occurrence of day-7 fractures and lower torque, not a demonstrated mechanism in the abstract.
- RFM and removal torque disagree on group differences in the abstract; the abstract does not discuss this.
- Animal model (minipig tibia); human applicability not addressed.

## 6. Related Work

## 7. Glossary
- **Osteotome technique**: site preparation by bone displacement/compaction with osteotomes instead of drilling.
- **RFM**: resonance frequency measurement of implant stability.
- **Removal torque**: torque needed to unscrew an integrated implant, used as a biomechanical stability measure.
