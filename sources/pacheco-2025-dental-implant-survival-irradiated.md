## Why Ingested

기존 [[implants/moy-2005-dental-implant-failure-rates-risk]]는 단일 후향적 코호트(UCLA, 4,680개 임플란트)에서 두경부 방사선을 임플란트 실패의 유의 위험인자(RR 2.73)로 보고했다. 본 umbrella review(Pacheco 2025)는 이를 11편의 SR(73,674개 임플란트, 그중 정량분석 48,563개)로 확장·재확인하여 조사율 격차(비조사 94.64% vs 조사 81.52%)를 수치화하고, 상악/이식골/방사선량/시기별 하위분석까지 제공한다 — 동일 방향의 결론을 훨씬 큰 근거 기반으로 보강.

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (11 systematic reviews, 73,674 dental implants across 16,193 patients with head and neck cancer, screened from 1,811 articles; PROSPERO CRD42023406059) comparing implant survival in irradiated vs. non-irradiated bone.

Pooled meta-analytic data (48,563 implants) showed 81.52% survival in irradiated bone (2,674/14,471 failures) vs. 94.64% in non-irradiated bone (1,825/34,092 failures), with lower survival also seen for irradiated maxilla vs. mandible and irradiated grafted vs. native bone.

All 11 included systematic reviews were rated critically low methodological quality by AMSTAR 2, so the umbrella review's conclusions — while directionally consistent (radiotherapy lowers implant survival) — carry weak certainty and should not by themselves drive clinical decision-making.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First umbrella review to consolidate 11 systematic reviews (2013–2023) directly comparing dental implant survival in irradiated vs. non-irradiated head-and-neck-cancer patients using a comparator-group design.
- Provides pooled quantitative survival figures (81.52% irradiated vs 94.64% non-irradiated across 48,563 implants) plus subgroup analyses: implantation site (maxilla vs mandible), bone type (grafted vs native), radiation dose, and RT-to-implant timing.
- Applies AMSTAR 2 critical appraisal across all 11 included SRs, exposing systematically low methodological quality in this literature — a key caveat for anyone citing implant-in-irradiated-bone survival numbers.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Umbrella review (review of systematic reviews), PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD42023406059.
- **Databases**: PubMed, Cochrane Library, Embase, Web of Science, plus manual/grey literature search (Google Scholar), inception to Feb 23, 2024, no language/date restriction.
- **n**: 1,811 articles screened → 45 full texts → 11 systematic reviews included (16,193 patients, 73,674 implants; 48,563 implants entering quantitative synthesis).
- **Outcomes**: Primary — dental implant survival percentage. Secondary — subgroup effects by implantation site, bone type (grafted/native), radiation dose, and interval between radiotherapy and implant placement. Methodological quality appraised with AMSTAR 2 (16 items, 7 critical domains).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Overall: 48,563 implants evaluated — irradiated 14,471 implants / 2,674 failures (survival 81.52%); non-irradiated 34,092 implants / 1,825 failures (survival 94.64%). Significant difference favoring non-irradiated bone supported by 11 meta-analyses.
- Maxilla vs mandible (irradiated): mandible survival higher (RR 3.16, 95% CI 1.76–5.68, p=0.0001, I²=70%; RR 5.96, 95% CI 2.71–13.12, p<0.00001, I²=33%); pooled maxilla 79.04% vs mandible 93.55% (n=3,378 implants). Shokouhi & Cerajewska (2022): maxilla OR 5.03 (95% CI 1.07–23.58, p=0.04).
- Grafted vs native bone (irradiated): native bone higher survival (OR 2.06, 95% CI 1.44–2.94, p<0.0001, I²=0%, follow-up ≥36 mo; OR 2.26, 95% CI 1.50–3.40, p<0.0001, I²=12%, follow-up ≥60 mo); one MA found no significant difference (RR 1.35, 95% CI 0.93–1.94, p=0.11). Pooled grafted 82.21% vs native 87.06% (n=1,740 implants).
- Radiation dose: trend toward higher survival at lower doses, not significant (RR 1.59, 95% CI 0.98–2.59, p=0.06, I²=47%); high-dose survival 82.05% vs low-dose 90.01% (n=1,057 implants).
- RT-to-implant timing: no significant difference placing implants ≤12 months vs >12 months post-RT (RR 1.37, 95% CI 0.76–2.45, p=0.29, I²=64%); ≤12 mo survival 84.26% vs >12 mo 88.51%.
- AMSTAR 2: all 11 included SRs rated critically low overall confidence.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- All 11 included SRs judged critically low methodological quality (AMSTAR 2) — umbrella-level conclusions inherit this weak certainty.
- Substantial heterogeneity across primary studies (implant systems, radiation protocols, follow-up duration, native vs grafted bone mix) and near-total absence of RCTs (mostly retrospective cohorts).
- Overlapping primary studies across the 11 included SRs were not formally quantified (citation-matrix/corrected covered area not reported), raising possible double-counting risk in pooled implant counts.
- Authors call for well-designed prospective studies with standardized individual-level data (implant-specific radiation dose, timing) and note the literature on implants placed *before* HNC diagnosis is essentially unexplored.

## 6. Related Work
- moy-2005 (Moy et al., 2005): single-institution retrospective cohort establishing head-and-neck radiation as an independent implant-failure risk factor (RR 2.73) — this umbrella review reinforces that finding at a much larger, multi-SR scale.
- scocca-2026 (Scocca et al., 2026): SR+MA specifically on zygomatic implants in head-and-neck-cancer patients — narrower implant-type scope, complementary rather than overlapping.
- hockova-2025: prospective study of short implants in microvascular free-flap-reconstructed HNC patients — a specific rehabilitation modality within the same irradiated-patient population.

## 7. Glossary
- **Umbrella review**: a systematic review that synthesizes findings across multiple existing systematic reviews/meta-analyses on the same or related questions.
- **AMSTAR 2**: A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews (2nd version) — 16-item critical appraisal instrument for SR methodological quality, yielding overall confidence ratings from high to critically low.
- **ORN**: osteoradionecrosis — necrosis of irradiated bone, a key adverse outcome tracked alongside implant survival in this literature.
- **HBO**: hyperbaric oxygen therapy — an adjunct sometimes used to improve tissue oxygenation before/after implant placement in irradiated bone.
- **RR / OR**: relative risk / odds ratio — effect-size measures reported by the pooled meta-analyses for survival comparisons.
