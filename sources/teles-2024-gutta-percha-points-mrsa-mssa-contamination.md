## Why Ingested

Gutta-percha (GP) points are the obturation gold standard but cannot be autoclaved, so chairside disinfection is the only sterility safeguard once a package is opened — this in-vitro study quantifies how contaminated in-use GP points actually are and confirms a 3% NaOCl/1-min protocol clears them. Part of a 4-paper GP-cone-decontamination cluster; it anchors the cross-infection-risk end of that cluster and complements the obturation-material evidence in [[endodontics/elmsmari-2025-single-cone-bioceramic-clinical-success-sr-ma]] by addressing the *sterility* of the cone rather than its clinical seal.

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro screen of 315 in-use GP points (2 brands, packages open 1 month) found only 6 (1.9%) contaminated (1 MRSA, 5 MSSA by PCR);

a 3% NaOCl 60-s chairside disinfection cleared all six (100%).

- **Authors**: Teles AM, Pina C, Cardoso IL, Tramontana A, Cardoso M, Duarte AS, Bartolomeu M, Noites R
- **Journal**: International Journal of Molecular Sciences (Int J Mol Sci), 2024
- **DOI**: 10.3390/ijms25168566 | **PMID**: 39201252 | **PMCID**: PMC11354985
- **Institution**: Universidade Católica Portuguesa (University Dental Clinic), Porto, Portugal
- **Study type**: In-vitro microbiology (classical culture + molecular/PCR identification)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantifies MRSA/MSSA contamination of GP points **sampled from packages already in clinical use for 1 month**, not freshly opened — the more realistic cross-infection scenario.
- Combines classical microbiology (BHI enrichment, chromogenic MRSA/MSSA selective medium, Gram/catalase/coagulase) with **PCR confirmation** (mecA-type MRSA gene, MSSA-specific gene), avoiding phenotype-only false calls.
- Demonstrates a simple, feasible **chairside disinfection protocol (3% NaOCl, 60 s, sterile-water rinse)** eliminates contamination in 100% of positive cones.
- Frames the finding for infection-control policy: contamination is low (<2%) but "cannot be considered negligible" given MRSA's nosocomial burden and Portugal's high European MRSA prevalence.
- Directly extends the same Porto group's earlier total-contamination work (Bracciale et al. 2020); Teles is a co-author on that paper, so this is a targeted MRSA/MSSA follow-up using the same sampling design.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Samples**: 315 GP points from 2 brands / 6 ISO gauges — main cones K20/K25/K30/K35 (R&S, Paris) and accessory A/B (Dentsply). All from boxes in use ≥4 weeks by pre-graduation students, ~8 appointments/week, stored per manufacturer temp/humidity specs.
- **Sampling cadence**: 32 main + 16 accessory points collected per day across 2 test packages, repeated over days until comparable per-gauge counts reached (design mirrors Bracciale 2020).
- **Contamination screen**: each cone in a sterile tube with 5 mL BHI, incubated 37 °C / 72 h, examined for turbidity (OD 600 nm). Controls: uninoculated BHI (C−), S. aureus ATCC 25923 (C+).
- **Phenotypic ID**: positive tubes subcultured on chromID MRSA / chromID S. aureus selective chromogenic medium (bioMérieux). Green = MRSA, beige = MSSA; confirmed by Gram-positive cocci morphology, catalase and coagulase positivity.
- **Molecular ID (PCR)**: MRSA confirmed via mecA-type resistance gene (527 bp product); mecA-negatives screened for the MSSA-specific gene (255 bp). Agarose-gel electrophoresis, ATCC positive/no-DNA negative controls.
- **Chairside disinfection (CPD)**: contaminated cones immersed in 3% NaOCl for 60 s, rinsed with 10 mL sterile distilled water, dried on sterile gauze, re-incubated in BHI 37 °C / 72 h; turbidity re-checked.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 25.0, binomial test on dichotomous variables, α = 0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Contamination rate**: 6 / 315 = **1.9%** positive for microbial growth; 98.1% uncontaminated.
- **Species split (PCR-confirmed)**: 1/6 (16.6%) MRSA (green phenotype), 5/6 (83.3%) MSSA (beige phenotype).
- All suspicious colonies were Gram-positive cocci, catalase- and coagulase-positive (consistent with S. aureus).
- **Disinfection efficacy**: 3% NaOCl / 60 s cleared **6/6 (100%)** — no turbidity on re-incubation.
- Contamination level (<2%) and 100% disinfection efficacy are both concordant with Bracciale et al. (2020).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **In-vitro** only; does not measure actual patient cross-infection or clinical endodontic outcomes.
- Target was a **narrow pair of species** (MRSA/MSSA) on selective media, so the true total microbial contamination of the cones is not captured — the low % partly reflects the restricted target.
- Single center, two brands, one exposure duration (1 month); no comparison of alternative chemical disinfectants or immersion times.
- NaOCl immersion caveats noted but not tested here: >1 min can cause loss of GP elasticity and chloride-crystal formation if not rinsed — disinfection time must be controlled.
- Authors call for future work comparing multiple chemical solutions (H₂O₂, chlorhexidine, ethyl alcohol, PVP-iodine, quaternary ammonium), assessing total contamination, decontamination time, and surface alterations, and screening for other persistent endodontic pathogens.

## 6. Related Work

- Bracciale et al. (2020) — the group's prior total-contamination/chairside-disinfection study on GP points; this paper is the MRSA/MSSA-specific follow-up with the same sampling design (Teles is a co-author on both).
- Siqueira (2001) — total microbial absence inside the root canal system after NSRCT is unachievable, motivating cone-sterility vigilance.
- Broader GP-cone decontamination literature comparing NaOCl, chlorhexidine, and other chemical agents for chairside disinfection before obturation.

## 7. Glossary

- **GP (Gutta-Percha) point/cone**: thermoplastic root-canal filling material, obturation gold standard; cannot be autoclaved due to its thermoplastic nature.
- **MRSA**: Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus — multidrug-resistant (β-lactams, tetracyclines, macrolides), a leading nosocomial pathogen.
- **MSSA**: Methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus — sensitive to standard β-lactams.
- **NSRCT**: Non-Surgical Root Canal Treatment.
- **RCS**: Root Canal System.
- **BHI**: Brain Heart Infusion — enrichment broth facilitating S. aureus growth.
- **Chromogenic selective medium (chromID)**: differential agar producing color-coded colonies (green = MRSA, beige = MSSA).
- **mecA**: gene conferring methicillin resistance, the molecular marker confirming MRSA by PCR.
- **CPD (Cleaning and Disinfection Procedure)**: the chairside 3% NaOCl 60-s immersion + sterile-water rinse protocol.
- **Catalase / coagulase tests**: phenotypic enzyme assays; both positive is characteristic of S. aureus.
