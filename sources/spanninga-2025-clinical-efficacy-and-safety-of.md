## Why Ingested

위키가 가진 골이식재 대체물 (bone graft substitute, BGS) 근거는 대부분 **재료군(narrative review) 수준**에 머물러 있었다. [[wiki/bone-regeneration/haugen-2026-bone-grafts-everything-you-need]]는 펩타이드 기능화 매트릭스(peptide-functionalized matrix, SmartBonePep)를 "향후 방향" 한 줄로만 언급하고 임상 효과 크기는 제시하지 않았으며, [[wiki/bone-regeneration/depace-2025-bone-regeneration-treatment-strategies-review]]는 SmartBone ORTHO를 상용 제품 카탈로그에 나열할 뿐 P-15 계열의 임상 결과를 담지 않았다. 본 논문은 **단일 펩타이드 첨가 이식대체물(ABM/P-15)의 임상적 유효성·안전성을 처음부터 끝까지 체계적으로 정제한 SR**로, 치과 부분(치주 골내 결손·치조제 보존·상악동 거상·임플란트 고정)이 `bone-regeneration` 카테고리의 "bone graft substitutes & biomaterials" 축에 정확히 대응하고, 척추·외상 부분은 **자가골(autologous bone graft, ABG)보다 빠른 유합(fusion)** 이라는 주장이 이 위키에 없던 교과(fields) 간 근거를 제공한다. [[wiki/overviews/bone-graft-material-selection-matrix-overview]]의 "골이식재 재료 선택 매트릭스"에 펩타이드 첨가 BGS라는 셀이 아직 비어 있었으므로, 이 페이지가 그 셀을 임상 수치로 채운다. 또한 이 논문은 유해사례 부재(no P-15-related adverse events)를 보고하는 반면, 위키가 가진 소(bovine) 유래 이식재 장기 합병증 사례 모음([[wiki/sinus-lift/lateral/rodriguez-2019-long-term-risks-complications-bovine]])은 ABM/P-15가 소 유래 무기질 기반이라는 점에서 별도의 안전성 맥락을 제공하므로 함께 읽어야 한다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic literature review (SR without meta-analysis; PROSPERO CRD42022306683, PRISMA; PubMed/Embase/Web of Science/Cochrane searched 13 May 2023) of 28 human clinical studies on ABM/P-15 — a P-15 cell-binding peptide on anorganic hydroxyapatite — used as a bone graft substitute: maxillofacial (18 manuscripts, 298 participants, 575 periodontal defects), spine (9 manuscripts = 7 studies, 710 participants) and trauma (1 study, 22 participants), against autologous bone graft (ABG), allograft, DBM, rhBMP-2, Bio-Oss and debridement controls.

Published results show equivalent safety and efficacy versus ABG at moderate-to-high quality (RoB2 low 46%, some concern 50%): cervical fusion 88.97% (129/145) vs ABG 85.82% (121/141) at 12 months (non-inferiority p = 0.0004) and 99.00% vs 98.20% at 72 months (p < 0.0001); periodontal defect fill 72.3% vs DFDBA 51.4% and debridement 40.3% (p < 0.05); lumbar fusion 50% (63/126) vs allograft 20% (23/114) at 12 months (p < 0.001); earlier fusion than ABG (97.73% vs 59.09% at 6 months) and than rhBMP-2 (mean 4.05 vs 10.0 months); and no P-15-related adverse events or allergic reactions in any study.

ABM/P-15 is a defensible autograft alternative where donor morbidity, volume or availability limits harvesting, and it is the only peptide-enhanced bone graft substitute the wiki holds with systematic clinical outcome data — but 8/20 RCTs carried some risk of bias and one high (randomisation by birthdate), the cervical evidence comes almost entirely from a single IDE trial, most included studies were partly funded by the product manufacturer, and heterogeneity of indications/outcome definitions plus the absence of any trauma RCT limit generalisability.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review, SR — 메타분석 없음; PROSPERO CRD42022306683, PRISMA 준수; PubMed·Embase·Web of Science·Cochrane Library에서 2023년 5월 13일 검색): 무기질 하이드록시아파타이트 (anorganic hydroxyapatite bone minerals, ABM) 표면에 콜라겐 I형 α-chain 유래 15아미노산 펩타이드 P-15를 흡착시킨 골이식재 대체물 (bone graft substitute, BGS)의 임상적 유효성·안전성 — 인간 임상 연구 28편(구강안과 18편/298명/치주 결손 575개, 척추 9편=7연구/710명, 외상 1편/22명), 대조군은 자가골 (autologous bone graft, ABG)·동종골(allograft)·탈회골기질(demineralized bone matrix, DBM)·재조합 사람 골형성단백질-2(recombinant human bone morphogenetic protein-2, rhBMP-2)·Bio-Oss·플랩 디브라이드먼트(flap debridement, FD).

ABM/P-15는 자가골 대비 **동등한 안전성·유효성**을 보였고(위험편향 low 46%, some concern 50%), 경추 유합 12개월 88.97%(129/145) vs 자가골 85.82%(121/141)(비inferiority p = 0.0004), 72개월 99.00% vs 98.20%(p < 0.0001), 치주 결손 충전(defect fill) 72.3% vs DFDBA 51.4%·FD 40.3%(p < 0.05), 요추 유합 12개월 50%(63/126) vs 동종골 20%(23/114)(p < 0.001)로 보고되었다. 자가골보다 빠른 유합(6개월 97.73% vs 59.09%)과 rhBMP-2·DBM 대비 빠른 유합(평균 4.05개월 vs 10.0·9.44개월)이 관찰되었고 **P-15 관련 이상반응과 알레르기 반응은 보고되지 않았다**.

ABM/P-15는 공여부 이환·이식량·가용성이 제한되는 상황에서 자가골의 대체 후보로 근거가 있으나, 20편 RCT 중 8편은 편향 우려가 있고 1편은 생년월일 기반 무작위배정으로 편향 위험이 높으며, 경추 근거가 단일 IDE 연구(Arnold 등)에 거의 집중되어 있고(대부분의 연구가 ABM/P-15 제조사의 일부 펀딩을 받아 발간 편향 가능), 적응증·종료점 정의가 이질적이며 외상에서는 RCT가 없어 일반화에 한계가 있다.

- **Scope note**: the review spans maxillofacial, spine and trauma surgery; ~2/3 of the included manuscripts (18/28) are dental/maxillofacial, and the spine (9) and trauma (1) strata are what make this the broadest evidence synthesis held in this wiki for a peptide-enhanced bone graft substitute.
- **Material under review**: ABM/P-15 — anorganic bovine/hydroxyapatite bone minerals (ABM) with the synthetic P-15 peptide (766GTPGPQGIAGQRGVV780, the cell-binding domain of the alpha-I chain of type I collagen) adsorbed onto the scaffold surface; commercial forms include i-FACTOR (i-FACTOR Bone Graft: ABM/P-15 particles 51.9%, sodium carboxymethylcellulose 1.5%, glycerin USP 7.0%, water USP 39.6%) and PepGen P-15.
- **Background context from the paper**: autologous bone graft is the biological gold standard (osteogenic + osteoinductive + osteoconductive) but is limited by donor-site morbidity, limited volume, variable bone quality and age-related loss of regenerative capacity; ~2.2 million bone grafting procedures are performed globally per year, forecast to rise ~13% annually.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **First systematic review of P-15 across indications** — the authors state this is the first SR to consider the full clinical evidence base for P-15 peptide in multiple surgical bone-regenerative procedures; no prior overview existed.
- **28 included studies spanning three anatomical strata** — maxillofacial (n = 18), spine (n = 9), trauma (n = 1), with 1,767 records screened down from 1,232 deduplicated titles/abstracts and 50 full texts.
- **Non-inferiority evidence against the gold standard in the largest trial held** — the Arnold IDE trial (n = 319) shows non-inferior fusion vs ABG at 12 months (p = 0.0004), 24 months and 72 months (p < 0.0001), plus a higher composite success rate at 1 year (69% vs 57%, p = 0.04) and 2 years (70% vs 56%, p = 0.03).
- **"Faster bone formation" quantified against two comparators** — versus ABG in PLIF (97.73% vs 59.09% fused at 6 months) and versus rhBMP-2/DBM in ILIF (mean time to fusion 4.05 vs 10.0 vs 9.44 months), the review's most distinctive claim.
- **Documented safety profile** — no allergic reaction or intolerability to ABM/P-15 in any assessed study; the complications reported (swelling, membrane exposure, tissue damage, graft migration, cage subsidence) were procedure- or comparator-attributed, and no serious complications of rhBMP-2 type (ectopic bone formation, osteolysis, retrograde ejaculation) were seen with ABM/P-15. The abstract states the same finding more cautiously, as "very few clinically relevant adverse events related to P-15 peptide".
- **Long-term follow-up included** — up to 72 months (cervical), 60 months (lumbar), 36 months (sinus augmentation) and 3 years (periodontal), enabling a long-term safety statement the authors make explicit.
- **Geographic breadth** — participants from 12 countries across four continents, cited by the authors as supporting generalisability.
- **Mechanistic framing** — P-15 as a *cell-binding* peptide that promotes osteogenic cell attachment, proliferation and differentiation and improves the osteoconductivity of the ABM scaffold through receptor-mediated binding (integrins, discoidin receptor 2, fibronectin), i.e. biological activity without the growth-factor risks that limit BMPs and the cost burden of rhBMP-2.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Registration / reporting**: PROSPERO CRD42022306683; reported per PRISMA with a completed PRISMA checklist in the supplementary material.
- **Databases and search date**: PubMed (Medline), Embase (OVID), Web of Science, Cochrane Library — all searched on **13 May 2023**.
- **Search string (condensed)**: surgery terms ("General Surgery", "Surgical procedures, operative", "reconstructive surgical procedures", "orthopedic procedures", "maxillofacial surgery", "cervicoplasty", "guided tissue regeneration", "regenerative medicine", "bone regeneration", "Spine", "Spinal Fusion", "Intervertebral Disc Displacement") AND ("cell-binding peptide P-15", "P-15 peptide enhanced bone graft", "synthetic peptide P-15"); filters: clinical, comparative, randomized controlled trials, observational and multicentre studies, English, full text, 1990–present.
- **Eligibility**: human clinical studies of spinal, maxillofacial or traumatological surgical interventions using P-15 peptide-enhanced bone graft, with **≥ 3 months follow-up**; exclusions: studies addressing malignancy or infection, and publication before 1990.
- **Screening**: two reviewers independently (BJS, TAH) screened title/abstract then full text; disagreements resolved by discussion or a third assessor (JJCA); Rayyan.ai used for screening; reference lists hand-searched for additional relevant studies.
- **Extraction**: standardised Excel template, two independent extractors (BJS, TAH), third-assessor arbitration; fields included DOI, design, level of evidence, implant location, experimental/control arms, intervention, participant numbers, plus age/sex/follow-up/diagnosis/measurements/adverse events/funding/COI where reported.
- **Clustering and outcomes**: studies clustered into spine / trauma / maxillofacial, tabulated per cluster and study design with narrative descriptions; primary outcome = **percentage of fusion or defect fill** where available, otherwise change in clinical attachment level (CAL) or time until consolidation.
- **Quality assessment**: RoB 2 for RCTs; MINORS for non-randomised studies (comparative max 24 points, non-comparative max 16; comparative high 19–24 / moderate 13–18 / low 0–12; non-comparative high 13–16 / moderate 9–12 / low 0–8).
- **Synthesis**: narrative/tabular — **no meta-analysis and no pooled estimates** are reported; the review is therefore classified `sr` (systematic review without meta-analysis).

- **Direction**: 13 of 16 maxillofacial comparator studies reported equal or significantly improved results with ABM/P-15; the abstract counts P-15 as effective in 11 maxillofacial studies. Benefit was concentrated in **periodontal osseous defects** (six RCTs vs debridement, PRP, DFDBA and ABM alone) and **ridge preservation** (reduced resorption vs no graft or acellular dermal matrix). ### 4.4 Trauma (1 study; 22 participants)

- Gomar 2007 pilot case series: two Spanish hospitals, June 2000 – October 2003, two patient series (9 and 13 patients; mean age 50 and 52; mean 14 months since initial diagnosis), long-bone delayed union/nonunion fractures fixed with screws-on-plates/nail plates/plaster plus particulate ABM/P-15 at the fracture site; no control arm.
- **Consolidation achieved in 20/22 (90%)**; bone bridging at a mean of **1.7 months**, full consolidation at **4 months**; histology on 5 patients confirmed the clinical/radiological result; one poor healing classification, one plate failure requiring a new plate with new P-15 material (then consolidated), one patient lost to follow-up at 12 months.

### 4.5 Methodological quality

- **20 RCTs assessed with RoB 2**: 11 low risk of bias; 8 with some concerns (randomisation description, protocol deviations, outcome-reporting/measurement selection); **1 high risk of bias** — Lauweryns & Raskin 2015, because randomisation was by birthdate, which RoB 2 does not accept as true randomisation.
- **8 non-randomised studies assessed with MINORS**: 2 high quality (Maharaj 2016 — 13; Sathe 2022 — 19), 6 moderate (Thompson 16, Scarano 16, Degidi 15, Mobbs 12, Gomar 11, Yukna 11). Quality losses traced to missing power calculations, absent control groups, unreported baseline data, absent endpoint-assessment blinding, missing statistical detail and no published protocol.
- **Abstract-level summary of evidence quality**: moderate (some concern 50%) to high (low risk 46%) quality clinical evidence supporting equivalence in safety and efficacy versus ABG.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

**Limitations reported by the review itself**

- **Heterogeneity** — clinical indications, outcome measures, study designs and even the definitions of fusion differ across the 28 studies, reducing generalisability; the authors note that inconsistent imaging and fusion criteria make cross-material comparison difficult.
- **Small maxillofacial samples** — per-manuscript means of 17 (range 2–33) participants and 32 (15–93) defects.
- **Single-trial dependence in the cervical stratum** — the cervical fusion population comes almost exclusively from the Arnold IDE exemption trial and its two follow-ups; 33 of 322 cervical participants were lost to follow-up in year 1, leaving 289 analysed (ABM/P-15 137, ABG 139).
- **Diabetes inclusion** — the IDE studies and Mobbs 2014 enrolled diabetic patients, so the reported fusion rates may be conservative.
- **Industry funding and publication bias** — most included studies were partly funded by the manufacturer of ABM/P-15, so unfavourable results may be unpublished.
- **Bias profile** — 8/20 RCTs with some concerns and 1 with high risk of bias; non-randomised studies lacked power calculations, protocols and blinded endpoint assessment.
- **Non-comparative arms** — some studies had no control (Scarano 2006, Maharaj 2016, Gomar 2007), so their results could not be statistically verified.
- **Search completeness** — the search (May 2023, four databases) missed a Korean-database study (Park et al.) reporting ABM/P-15 vs ABG fusion of 95.6% vs 86.9% in PLIF, which the authors cite from the discussion but could not include.

**Implications and research gaps stated by the authors**

- RCTs with large populations are needed before conclusions can be drawn for **sinus augmentation** and **implant fixation**.
- A **traumatology RCT** is required to assess efficacy and safety in fracture nonunion.
- **Systemic bone disease (e.g. osteoporosis) studies were excluded**, so no inference can be made about performance in suboptimal bone tissue.
- More high-level RCTs with biomaterial used in both arms and **standardised radiological assessment, preferably high-resolution CT**, are warranted.
- **Generalised conclusion**: ABM/P-15 is effective and safe in multiple surgical contexts; fusion was similar to rhBMP-2, DBM and ABG in lumbar and cervical fusion, with earlier fusion versus rhBMP-2, DBM and allograft in lumbar procedures, and no serious complications attributed to ABM/P-15.

## 7. Glossary

- **ABM/P-15** — anorganic bone minerals (hydroxyapatite) with the synthetic P-15 peptide adsorbed on the surface; the material under review (commercial names i-FACTOR, PepGen P-15).
- **P-15 peptide** — synthetic 15-amino-acid replicate (766GTPGPQGIAGQRGVV780) of the cell-binding domain of the alpha-I chain of type I collagen; promotes osteogenic cell attachment, proliferation and differentiation via integrin / discoidin receptor 2 / fibronectin binding.
- **BGS** — bone graft substitute.
- **ABG** — autologous bone graft (the biological gold standard; osteogenic + osteoinductive + osteoconductive).
- **FD** — flap debridement (open-flap debridement control arm).
- **GTR** — guided tissue regeneration.
- **PRP / PRF** — platelet-rich plasma / platelet-rich fibrin.
- **DFDBA / DBM** — demineralised freeze-dried bone allograft / demineralised bone matrix.
- **ADM** — acellular dermal matrix.
- **CAL** — clinical attachment level.
- **ACDF / ALIF / PLIF / ILIF** — anterior cervical discectomy and fusion / anterior lumbar interbody fusion / posterior lumbar interbody fusion / intervertebral lumbar interbody fusion.
- **IDE** — Investigational Device Exemption (the trial authorisation under which the Arnold cervical trials ran).
- **RoB 2** — Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomised trials; **MINORS** — methodological index for non-randomised studies.
- **PROSPERO / PRISMA** — prospective registry of systematic reviews / Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses.
- **Fusion** — radiological bridging bone across the intended fusion site; definitions varied between studies, a named source of heterogeneity in this review.
