## Why Ingested

Complements [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/di-spirito-2023-clear-aligner-periodontal-umbrella-review]] (umbrella review of 4 SRs) and [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/baneshi-2024-clear-aligners-malocclusion-sr-ma]] with the largest meta-analysis to date simultaneously quantifying all four patient-centred domains — PI, GI, PD, pain, and QoL — under one PRISMA-registered framework with GRADE certainty, temporal subgroup analysis, and comprehensive publication bias testing.

## One-line Summary

SR+MA (32 studies, 28 in meta-analysis) showing CA superior to FA across all periodontal indices (PI MD −0.437, GI MD −0.233, PD MD −0.332), pain (SMD −0.419), and short-term QoL (SMD −0.985 at 1 week) with GRADE moderate-to-high certainty.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First meta-analysis to simultaneously assess all five domains (PI, GI, PD, pain intensity, QoL) within a single statistical framework, enabling cross-outcome comparison
- Temporal subgroup analyses at 1 week, 10 days, 1 month, 6 months, 12 months reveal QoL benefit diminishes over time (12-month SMD −0.531, p=0.108, non-significant)
- GRADE certainty: high for GI and PD; moderate for PI, pain, and QoL
- Zero heterogeneity for periodontal indices (I² = 0% for GI and PD), reinforcing reliability of those pooled estimates
- Comprehensive publication bias assessment (Egger, Begg, trim-and-fill) — pain and PI show small-study effects; QoL and GI/PD do not

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: SR + meta-analysis following PRISMA 2020 guidelines
- Databases: PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, Embase, ScienceDirect + gray literature (ProQuest) + hand-search of American Journal of Orthodontics, European Journal of Orthodontics, Angle Orthodontist
- Date range: inception to March 2025
- Population: patients undergoing orthodontic treatment (any age/sex); excluded severe periodontal disease, craniofacial anomalies, orthognathic surgery
- Intervention: clear aligner therapy (Invisalign and similar removable systems)
- Comparator: fixed appliances (metal, self-ligating, ceramic, lingual brackets)
- Outcomes: PI, GI, PD (mean difference, MD), pain intensity and QoL (standardized mean difference, SMD)
- Study designs: RCTs, cohort studies, case-control studies (17 RCTs + 15 observational)
- Total N: 32 studies included; 28 in meta-analysis; sample sizes 28–145 per study
- Risk of bias: RoB 2 for RCTs; ROBINS-I for non-randomized studies
- Statistics: Comprehensive Meta-Analysis v3.7; random-effects models; subgroup analysis by follow-up duration and study design
- Pooling rule: PI/GI/PD pooled by MD on comparable clinical scales; pain/QoL by SMD across heterogeneous instruments (VAS, OHIP-14, McGill, OHRQoL-UK)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Periodontal outcomes (CA vs FA, negative = CA better):**
- Plaque Index (PI): MD = −0.437 (95% CI −0.507 to −0.367, p<0.001); I² = 39.88% (non-RCTs), 0% (RCTs)
- Gingival Index (GI): MD = −0.233 (95% CI −0.296 to −0.170, p<0.001); I² = 0%
- Probing Depth (PD): MD = −0.332 (95% CI −0.431 to −0.234, p<0.001); I² = 0%

**Pain:**
- Overall SMD = −0.419 (p<0.001), small-to-moderate effect; I² = 23.5% (low-to-moderate)
- Greatest advantage at 1 and 2 weeks (I² = 42.1% and 69.9%, respectively)

**Quality of Life:**
- 1 week: SMD = −0.985 (p<0.001), I² = 74% (high)
- 10 days: SMD = −0.895 (p<0.001)
- 1 month: SMD = −0.829 (p<0.001)
- End of treatment: SMD = −0.970 (95% CI −1.410 to −0.520, p<0.001)
- 12 months: SMD = −0.531 (95% CI −1.178 to 0.116, p=0.108) — NOT statistically significant

**Clinical significance caveat:**
- PD reduction of 0.332 mm is modest relative to periodontal thresholds (≤3 mm healthy; ≥6 mm severe)
- GI reduction of 0.233 ≈ 8% on 0–3 scale; no established MCID for orthodontic context
- PI reduction of 0.437 ≈ 15%; possibly influenced by study-specific hygiene protocols
- QoL SMD −0.985 at 1 week = large meaningful benefit

**Publication bias:**
- Pain: significant (Egger intercept −3.611, p=0.00019; Begg tau −0.591, p=0.00069); fail-safe N = 273
- PI: asymmetry confirmed; fail-safe N = 357
- GI: moderate bias (Begg tau −0.381); Egger not significant; fail-safe N = 82
- PD: no substantial bias by Egger; trim-and-fill imputed 2 missing studies; fail-safe N = 67
- QoL: minimal bias; fail-safe N = 898

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Substantial proportion of observational evidence (15/32 studies); residual confounding cannot be excluded
- Variability in appliance systems, OHI protocols, follow-up duration, and outcome instruments
- QoL outcomes pooled via SMD across different questionnaires (OHIP-14, OHRQoL-UK, VAS) reducing direct comparability
- No long-term periodontal stability or relapse data beyond 12 months
- Publication bias suggests overestimation particularly for pain-related outcomes
- No subgroup analysis by malocclusion severity or aligner system brand
- Future: powered long-term RCTs; standardized outcome reporting; 3D periodontal assessment; cost-effectiveness analysis

## 7. Glossary

- **PI (Plaque Index)**: quantitative measure of biofilm accumulation on tooth surfaces
- **GI (Gingival Index)**: 0–3 score assessing tissue appearance, contour, and bleeding response
- **PD (Probing Depth)**: sulcular depth in mm; ≤3 mm = healthy; 4–5 mm = mild; ≥6 mm = severe
- **SMD (Standardized Mean Difference)**: effect size used when studies use different measurement instruments; <0.2 = trivial, 0.2–0.5 = small, 0.5–0.8 = moderate, >0.8 = large
- **MD (Mean Difference)**: raw difference in means on same scale
- **GRADE**: Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation — certainty framework: high, moderate, low, very low
- **OHRQoL (Oral Health-Related Quality of Life)**: patient-reported impacts across functional, psychological, and social domains
- **OHIP-14**: 14-item Oral Health Impact Profile — validated QoL instrument
- **INPLASY**: International Platform of Registered Systematic Review and Meta-analysis Protocols
- **RoB 2**: Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool for RCTs
- **ROBINS-I**: Risk of Bias in Non-randomized Studies — Interventions tool
- **Trim-and-fill**: non-parametric publication bias correction method (Duval and Tweedie)
- **Fail-safe N**: number of null-result studies needed to nullify a finding

## Three-line Summary

- SR+MA (32 studies — 17 RCTs, 15 observational; 28 pooled; databases inception to March 2025; INPLASY 202530100) comparing clear aligner therapy versus fixed appliances across periodontal indices, pain, and quality of life.
- Clear aligners were significantly superior on all five outcomes: plaque index (PI) MD −0.437, gingival index (GI) MD −0.233 (I²=0%), probing depth (PD) MD −0.332 (I²=0%), pain SMD −0.419, and quality of life (QoL) SMD −0.970 at end of treatment; GRADE high for GI and PD, moderate for the remainder; QoL advantage non-significant at 12 months (SMD −0.531, p=0.108).
- Clear aligners produce consistently better short-term periodontal health and QoL than fixed appliances, but absolute periodontal differences are modest (PD reduction 0.332 mm) and QoL benefits fade by 12 months; publication bias likely inflates pain and PI estimates.
