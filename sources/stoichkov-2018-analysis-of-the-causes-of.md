## Three-line Summary
Retrospective clinical study of 101 patients with 218 fitted implants followed 3–10 years, analyzing causative factors of implant body fracture (bruxism, occlusion, cantilevers, planning, bone volume, implant diameter/number/inclination).
Implant fracture incidence was 2.3%; improper treatment planning, bruxism, and time of complication onset were the main factors, with typical size effect only for bruxism, occlusal errors, and their activity duration; fractures occurred most often with single crowns.
Implant fracture is rare but catastrophic: occlusal overload from bruxism or inadequate occlusion — especially in the first years after functional loading, in single crowns, and without implant-protected occlusion — is the dominant causal cluster.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Quantifies implant body fracture incidence at 2.3% in a 3–10 year follow-up cohort (218 implants) — one of the few etiology papers with a denominator, not just case reports.
- Separates biomechanical/physiologic overload factors (bruxism, occlusion, cantilevers) from prosthesis-planning factors (bone volume, implant area, diameter, number, inclination) in one tracked factor set.
- Identifies single crowns as the prosthesis type with the highest fracture concentration, in combination with bruxism and absent implant-protected occlusion.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Retrospective clinical study (chart review)
- **n**: 101 patients, 218 fitted implants, follow-up 3–10 years
- **Outcomes**: Implant body fracture incidence; association of tracked causative factors with fracture
- **Tracked factors**: parafunctional activity (bruxism), occlusion, cantilevers, prosthesis planning, available bone volume, implant area, implant diameter, number of implants, implant inclination
- **Statistics**: Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc Mann-Whitney test per group

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Implant fracture incidence | 2.3% of investigated cases |
| Main causative factors | Improper treatment planning, bruxism, time of complication onset |
| Typical size effect (significant) | Bruxism, occlusal errors, and their activity duration only |
| Prosthesis type most affected | Single crown prostheses (vs other implant-supported fixed dental prostheses) |
| Aggravating context | Parafunctional activities (bruxism) + lack of implant-protected occlusion |
| Critical time window | First years after functional load |

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Retrospective design; abstract does not report per-factor effect sizes, p-values, or fracture counts — full text not retrieved, so those numbers are unavailable and not invented.
- Rare-event cohort: 2.3% incidence implies only ~5 fracture events among 218 implants (derived, not stated) — limited per-factor statistical power despite Bonferroni-corrected testing; center count not stated in abstract.
- Abstract-only ingest; implant brand/macrogeometry, cantilever length, and occlusal measurement method are tracked factors in the design but their individual results are not visible in the abstract.

## 7. Glossary
- **Implant body fracture**: fracture of the osseointegrated implant fixture itself — the most severe mechanical complication, non-repairable, requires fixture removal.
- **Bruxism**: parafunctional activity (grinding/clenching) generating overload forces on implants.
- **Implant-protected occlusion (ImpOC)**: occlusal scheme minimizing lateral loads on implants (favor axial loading, mutually protected occlusion).
- **Cantilever**: unsupported extension of an implant-supported prosthesis magnifying occlusal forces.
- **Typical size effect**: standardizeable effect-size metric used in the study to compare factor strength across groups.
