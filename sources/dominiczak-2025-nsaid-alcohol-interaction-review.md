## Why Ingested

The wiki lacked a dedicated review on NSAID-alcohol interactions — a clinically important topic that the wiki's existing pages only touch tangentially (e.g., [[overviews/nsaid-aspirin-antiplatelet-interaction-overview]] covers NSAID-aspirin antiplatelet interaction, not NSAID-alcohol). This review fills the gap by synthesizing evidence on how alcohol affects NSAID pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics across GI, hepatic, and renal systems.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Quality in Sport 2025) surveying PubMed/Google Scholar literature on NSAIDs-alcohol interactions, covering pharmacokinetic (CYP2E1 induction, ADH competition) and pharmacodynamic mechanisms across GI, hepatic, renal, and cardiovascular systems.

Alcohol induces CYP2E1 and competes for ADH, altering NSAID metabolism; combined use exacerbates gastrointestinal mucosal damage, increases ulcer/GI bleeding risk, enhances nephrotoxicity, and may contribute to hepatotoxicity and inflammation-mediated carcinogenesis.

Clinical practice implications: healthcare providers should assess patient risk factors (GI history, renal impairment, cardiovascular disease) and counsel patients on avoiding alcohol while taking NSAIDs; future research should focus on safer analgesic alternatives with reduced systemic toxicity.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First comprehensive review specifically focused on NSAIDs-alcohol interactions (not NSAID-aspirin or NSAID-other drug)
- Mechanistically organized: covers pharmacokinetic (CYP2E1 induction, ADH competition, gastric emptying changes) and pharmacodynamic (additive GI toxicity, ROS production) pathways
- Systematically addresses three organ systems: gastrointestinal, hepatic, and renal consequences of combined NSAID-alcohol use
- Discusses emerging evidence linking chronic NSAID-alcohol use to inflammation-mediated carcinogenesis (colorectal cancer risk)
- Highlights genetic polymorphism influence on individual susceptibility to NSAID-alcohol interactions

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Narrative/literature review
- **Databases**: PubMed, Google Scholar
- **Search keywords**: "non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs", "alcohol", "COX-1", "COX-2"
- **Scope**: Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions between NSAIDs and ethanol; effects on GI, hepatic, renal, and cardiovascular systems

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- NSAIDs account for ~8% of all prescriptions globally; 30–50 million people use NSAIDs daily
- Alcohol metabolism primarily via ADH → acetaldehyde → acetate; CYP2E1 is the key enzyme for both ethanol and many NSAIDs
- **Pharmacokinetic interaction**: Alcohol induces CYP2E1 (chronic use) → accelerated NSAID metabolism → reduced efficacy or increased toxic metabolite formation
- **Pharmacodynamic interaction**: Combined local GI mucosal toxicity + prostaglandin inhibition → additive GI damage; alcohol disrupts gastric barrier → increased NSAID penetration and damage
- **GI risk**: NSAIDs + alcohol increase risk of ulcers and GI bleeding beyond either alone; risk factors include age >65, prior peptic ulcer, alcohol abuse
- **Renal risk**: Alcohol enhances NSAID-induced nephrotoxicity → increased AKI and CKD risk, especially in dehydrated or pre-existing renal impairment
- **Hepatic risk**: Alcohol-induced oxidative stress + NSAID use → elevated hepatotoxicity risk (via ROS and CYP2E1 metabolites)
- Emerging evidence: chronic NSAID-alcohol use may promote inflammation-mediated carcinogenesis, potentially increasing colorectal cancer risk

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- This is a narrative review, not a systematic review with meta-analysis — no quantitative pooled estimates
- Most evidence based on case-control studies and observational data; few prospective RCTs specifically testing NSAID-alcohol interactions
- Heterogeneity in NSAID types, doses, alcohol definitions, and study populations across reviewed literature
- Limited data on specific NSAIDs beyond ibuprofen and naproxen regarding alcohol interaction magnitude
- Future research needed on safer analgesic alternatives with reduced systemic toxicity and on genetic polymorphism contributions to individual susceptibility

## Glossary
- **NSAIDs**: Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs — inhibit cyclooxygenase (COX) enzymes to reduce prostaglandin synthesis
- **CYP2E1**: Cytochrome P450 2E1 — hepatic enzyme induced by chronic alcohol consumption; metabolizes both ethanol and many NSAIDs
- **ADH**: Alcohol Dehydrogenase — enzyme converting ethanol to acetaldehyde in the stomach and liver
- **UGIB**: Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding — major adverse effect of NSAIDs, exacerbated by alcohol
- **Pharmacokinetic interaction**: Alcohol alters absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion of NSAIDs (e.g., CYP2E1 induction)
- **Pharmacodynamic interaction**: Alcohol enhances NSAID effects/additive toxicity at the target site (e.g., additive GI mucosal damage)
- **ROS**: Reactive Oxygen Species — free radicals generated by alcohol metabolism; contribute to oxidative stress and tissue damage
