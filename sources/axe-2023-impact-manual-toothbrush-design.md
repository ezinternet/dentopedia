## Why Ingested

Found while mining the reference list of Wen et al. 2026 "The effect of dental floss sequence on plaque removal efficacy and periodontal health" (RCT on flossing sequence vs toothbrushing), which cites this paper as background evidence that over 40% of plaque resists toothbrush removal alone. [[periodontics/oral-hygiene-instruction/rajwani-2020-manual-toothbrushing-techniques-plaque-gingivitis-sr]] found no manual *technique* superior for plaque removal, partly due to heterogeneity across 23 toothbrush brands in the pooled studies — Axe 2023 isolates *brush design* (head-size, filament diameter, cutting-height, hardness, interdental-height) under controlled robot-simulated movements, offering a mechanistic explanation for why brush hardware, independent of technique, is a real confound in toothbrushing plaque-removal literature.

## Three-line Summary

In vitro robot-brushing study (8 manual toothbrushes, KaVo artificial dentition, 3 movements: horizontal/rotating/vertical) testing 5 bristle-design parameters — head-size, filament-diameter, cutting-height, hardness, interdental-height difference — for simulated plaque removal via automated plaque planimetry (30 fields/tooth).

Plaque removal was significantly better with smaller head-size, smaller filament-diameter (0.12mm vs 0.15mm), larger cutting-height (12mm vs 9mm), softer filaments (0.15/0.18mm vs 0.23mm), and greater interdental-height difference (8.5/11mm vs 10/11mm); rotating movement was overall the most effective brushing motion across parameters.

Industry-sponsored (Haleon/Dr Best) in vitro study — findings on flexible, longer, softer bristle designs are mechanistically plausible but require in vivo clinical confirmation (plaque indices, blinded photographic assessment) before being generalized to real-world toothbrushing.

## 세줄요약

로봇 칫솔질 in vitro 연구 (수동칫솔 8종, KaVo 인공치열, 3가지 동작: 수평/회전/수직) — 5가지 강모 디자인 변수(헤드크기·필라멘트직경·컷팅높이·경도·치간높이차)를 자동 치면세균막 평면측정법(치아당 30개 필드)으로 평가.

작은 헤드크기, 작은 필라멘트직경(0.12mm vs 0.15mm), 큰 컷팅높이(12mm vs 9mm), 부드러운 강모(0.15/0.18mm vs 0.23mm), 큰 치간높이차(8.5/11mm vs 10/11mm)에서 치면세균막 제거가 유의하게 우수했고, 전체적으로 회전 동작이 가장 효과적이었다.

업계(Haleon/Dr Best) 후원 in vitro 연구 — 유연하고 길고 부드러운 강모 디자인의 결과는 기전적으로 타당하나, 실제 임상 적용 전 생체 내(in vivo) 확증(치면세균막 지수, 맹검 사진평가)이 필요하다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Isolates five discrete manual-toothbrush bristle-design parameters (head-size, filament-diameter, cutting-height, hardness, interdental-height difference) and tests each under three standardized brushing movements (horizontal, rotating, vertical) using a validated 6-axis robot (Kawasaki FS002N) at fixed force (3.5 N).
- Uses automated optical plaque planimetry (APP) across 30 standardized tooth fields (13 surface areas: buccal, lingual, mesial, distal, and gingival/interproximal "risk fields") rather than manual scoring, removing examiner subjectivity.
- Demonstrates that brushing-movement type interacts with brush design — e.g., head-size effects reversed direction between horizontal and vertical movements, while rotating movement showed the most consistent design-dependent gains across parameters.
- To the authors' knowledge, the first English-literature study assessing all five design parameters together with multiple brushing movements in a single robot-based, planimetry-scored design.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Setting**: Germany-based research facility (ORMED Institute, University of Witten/Herdecke).
- **Apparatus**: 6-axis Robot FS002N (Kawasaki Robotics) programmed to reproduce the three most commonly observed uninstructed brushing movements — horizontal (5 mm amplitude), vertical (10 mm), rotating (10 mm diameter) — each run 5 times at 3.5 N force, individually in series (not combined).
- **Model teeth**: KaVo artificial plastic teeth (4 incisors, 1 canine, 2 premolars, 3 molars) mounted in anatomical mandibular arrangement, coated with a red inorganic simulated-plaque formula designed to mimic natural plaque adherence.
- **Toothbrushes tested** (8 Dr Best manual brushes, end-rounded nylon filaments): S1 (R5 Compact Head); S2 (R5 Full Head, medium-hard, 12mm tuft, 0.23mm filament); S3 (R5 Full Head, extra-soft, 0.12mm filament); S4 (R5 Full Head, soft, 0.15mm filament); S5 (R5 Full Head, medium-hard, 9mm tuft); S6 (R5 Full Head, soft, 0.18mm filament); S7 (G5 Interdental cut, 10/11mm tuft heights); S8 (G5 Interdental cut, 8.5/11mm tuft heights).
- **Comparisons**: Head-Size (S1 vs S2); Filament-Diameter (S3 vs S4); Cutting-Height (S5 vs S2); Hardness (S2 vs S4; S2 vs S6; S4 vs S6); Interdental Tuft-Height Difference (S7 vs S8).
- **Outcome measurement**: Simulated plaque reduction (%) pre- vs post-brushing at 30 planimetrical fields via computer-assisted APP, aggregated into 7 reported surface variables (buccal, lingual, buccal/lingual ABCDF risk fields, mesial, distal, total).
- **Statistics**: Kolmogorov-Smirnov test for normality per surface variable (alpha=0.10); independent two-sample t-test (with Levene's test for variance equality) for normally distributed variables; Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney-U-test for the 4 non-normal variables (buccal, lingual, buccal-W, total). Significance threshold for all pairwise brush comparisons: alpha=0.05 (two-tailed).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Parameter comparison | Best performer (overall) | Key total-area figures |
|---|---|---|
| Head-size (S1 compact vs S2 full) | Mixed — S1 better horizontal, S2 better vertical, similar rotating | Horizontal: 82.42% (S1) vs 77.89% (S2), p<0.05; Vertical: 75.86% (S2) vs 70.91% (S1), p<0.05 |
| Filament diameter (S3 0.12mm vs S4 0.15mm) | S3 (extra-soft, thinner) | Horizontal: 86.75% vs 75.66%, p<0.05; Rotating: 79.98% vs 72.63%, p<0.01 |
| Cutting height (S2 12mm vs S5 9mm) | S2 (taller tuft) | Horizontal: 68.11% vs 47.22%, p<0.01; Rotating: 62.33% vs 57.96%, p<0.05; Vertical: 67.94% vs 51.57%, p<0.05 |
| Hardness (S2 medium-hard vs S4/S6 soft) | Soft brushes (S4, S6) | S4 vs S2 horizontal: 75.66% vs 68.11%; S6 vs S2 rotating: 77.32% vs 62.33%, p<0.05 |
| Interdental-height difference (S7 10/11mm vs S8 8.5/11mm) | S8 (larger difference) for rotating/vertical; mixed for horizontal | Rotating: 81.29% (S8) vs 66.47% (S7), p<0.01; Vertical: 73.65% vs 66.95%, p<0.01 |

- Rotating movement produced the most efficient simulated plaque removal overall across all five design parameters, and the "best" theoretical brush for rotating movement combined a compact head, 0.12mm filament, 12mm tuft height, soft bristles, and a large interdental-height difference.
- No bristle deformation was observed in any brush after testing.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **In vitro only**: robot-simulated brushing on plastic teeth with an artificial red plaque analog — cannot capture patient-to-patient variability in technique, pressure, duration, or real biofilm adhesion/removal dynamics.
- **Industry funding**: sponsored by Haleon (Dr Best manufacturer); three of five authors are Haleon employees. Independent statistical analysis by an academic institute (ORMED) partially mitigates but does not eliminate sponsor-bias risk — a standard caveat for toothbrush-industry comparative testing.
- **Single brush line**: all 8 test brushes are Dr Best models varying one parameter at a time — findings on absolute parameter effects may not generalize to other manufacturers' bristle geometries/materials.
- **No combined/naturalistic movement**: each movement (horizontal/rotating/vertical) was tested in isolation over 5 runs; real-world brushing is a variable mixture of movements, which the authors acknowledge was a simplification for reproducibility.
- Authors explicitly call for in vivo clinical studies (clinical plaque indices, planimetrical indices, blinded photographic assessment) to confirm whether the in vitro design advantages translate to actual patient plaque removal.

## 6. Related Work

- Cites a clinical study finding softer, tapered, cross-angled bristles superior to standard medium bristles, and a meta-review finding angled bristles superior to standard flat-trimmed brushes — consistent direction with this study's soft/flexible-filament findings.
- Cites a contrasting systematic review finding little/no difference between tapered and end-rounded filaments for plaque removal — the authors note in vitro findings should be interpreted cautiously for clinical inference.
- Yankell et al. (cited) compared 2 manual toothbrushes via laboratory method and also found tapered bristles more effective than rounded bristles on a flat head, aligning with this study's flexible-filament conclusion.
- In this wiki, [[periodontics/oral-hygiene-instruction/rajwani-2020-manual-toothbrushing-techniques-plaque-gingivitis-sr]] is the technique-focused counterpart (10 manual techniques, no clear winner) — Axe 2023 supplies the design-parameter axis that Rajwani's review could not isolate due to heterogeneous brush brands across its included studies.

## 7. Glossary

- **Cutting-height (tuft height)**: the length of the bristle tuft from base to tip; taller tufts flex more before contacting the tooth surface.
- **Filament diameter**: thickness of individual bristle filaments (mm); thinner filaments are generally softer and more flexible.
- **Interdental-height difference**: the height differential between adjacent bristle tufts, designed to reach into interproximal/interdental spaces (as in "interdental cut" brush heads).
- **Automated Plaque Planimetry (APP)**: computer-assisted optical measurement of stained/simulated plaque coverage area on a tooth surface, used here pre- and post-brushing to quantify % plaque removal.
- **Modified Bass technique**: a rotating/vibrating bristle-tip brushing method angled into the gingival sulcus, considered a benchmark technique and mechanically similar to oscillating-rotating powered toothbrush motion.
