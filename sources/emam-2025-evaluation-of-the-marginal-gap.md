## Abstract

Background: The accuracy of digital impressions for full-arch implant-supported restorations can be influenced by the substructure framework material. This study evaluated the marginal gap and three-dimensional trueness of suprastructures generated from scanning two different substructure framework materials (zirconia and polyetheretherketone/PEEK) for full-arch implant-supported restorations.

Materials and Methods: This randomized controlled trial included 20 edentulous patients rehabilitated with full-arch implant-supported fixed prostheses. Ten patients received zirconia substructures and 10 received PEEK substructures. Digital impressions were taken using an intraoral scanner (Medit i700). The marginal gap and three-dimensional trueness of the milled suprastructures were evaluated using micro-CT and 3D analysis software. Statistical analysis used independent t-tests and Mann-Whitney U tests.

Results: The mean marginal gap for zirconia substructures was 42.3 ± 12.7 μm and for PEEK substructures was 58.9 ± 15.3 μm (p = 0.003). The three-dimensional trueness (RMS deviation) for zirconia was 28.4 ± 8.2 μm and for PEEK was 41.7 ± 10.5 μm (p = 0.001). Zirconia substructures demonstrated significantly better marginal adaptation and higher trueness compared to PEEK substructures.

Conclusions: Within the limitations of this study, zirconia substructure frameworks provide superior marginal fit and higher scanning trueness compared to PEEK frameworks for full-arch implant-supported restorations. The material properties of the substructure influence the accuracy of digital impressions and subsequent CAD/CAM fabrication.

## Introduction

Full-arch implant-supported fixed prostheses require passive fit to prevent mechanical and biological complications. The marginal gap between the prosthesis and implant platform is a critical parameter. Digital workflows using intraoral scanners (IOS) have become standard, but the accuracy can be affected by the scan body and substructure material properties. Zirconia and PEEK are two commonly used framework materials with different optical properties (zirconia: opaque, high refractive index; PEEK: translucent, lower refractive index). These properties may affect IOS accuracy due to light scattering and reflection differences. This RCT compares the marginal gap and 3D trueness of suprastructures fabricated from zirconia vs PEEK substructures.

## Materials and Methods

### Study Design
Randomized controlled trial, parallel-group, 20 patients. Ethics approval: Alexandria University (0796_11/2023). Registered.

### Participants
- 20 completely edentulous patients
- Maxilla or mandible
- 4-6 implants per arch (All-on-4/6 protocol)
- Random allocation: 10 zirconia, 10 PEEK substructures

### Interventions
- **Zirconia group**: Monolithic zirconia substructure (3Y-TZP)
- **PEEK group**: PEEK substructure (Juvora/PEEK)
- Same implant system, same scan bodies (Medit scan bodies)
- Same IOS: Medit i700
- Same CAD/CAM workflow (exocad, 5-axis milling)

### Outcomes
**Primary**: Marginal gap (μm) at implant-prosthesis interface
**Secondary**: 3D trueness (RMS deviation from reference), scanning time

### Marginal Gap Measurement
Micro-CT scanning of seated suprastructures. 3D analysis software (ImageJ2/3D Slicer). Measurements at 4 sites per implant (mesial, distal, buccal, lingual). Mean of all sites per patient.

### 3D Trueness Measurement
Reference scan: Industrial optical scanner (ATOS Core) of master model. Test scan: IOS scan of substructure in situ. Best-fit alignment, RMS deviation calculated.

### Statistical Analysis
- Normality: Shapiro-Wilk
- Independent t-test (parametric) / Mann-Whitney U (non-parametric)
- Significance: p < 0.05
- Sample size calculation: 80% power, α=0.05, effect size from pilot

## Results

### Patient Demographics
- 20 patients (12 male, 8 female)
- Mean age: 58.4 ± 9.2 years
- 12 maxillary, 8 mandibular arches
- Zirconia group: 10 patients (6 maxilla, 4 mandible)
- PEEK group: 10 patients (6 maxilla, 4 mandible)
- No dropouts

### Marginal Gap (μm)

| Group | Mean ± SD | Median (IQR) | Range | p-value |
|-------|-----------|--------------|-------|---------|
| Zirconia | 42.3 ± 12.7 | 40.1 (34.2–48.7) | 22–78 | **0.003** |
| PEEK | 58.9 ± 15.3 | 56.3 (49.1–65.8) | 35–92 | |

- Zirconia showed **28.2% smaller marginal gap** (mean difference 16.6 μm)
- Per-implant analysis: zirconia 41.8 ± 11.9 vs PEEK 57.4 ± 14.6 μm (p = 0.002)
- Maxilla vs mandible: no significant difference within groups

### 3D Trueness (RMS Deviation, μm)

| Group | Mean ± SD | Median (IQR) | Range | p-value |
|-------|-----------|--------------|-------|---------|
| Zirconia | 28.4 ± 8.2 | 26.7 (22.1–33.4) | 15–48 | **0.001** |
| PEEK | 41.7 ± 10.5 | 39.8 (34.2–47.9) | 25–62 | |

- Zirconia showed **31.9% better trueness** (mean difference 13.3 μm)
- Lower deviation = higher accuracy of digital impression → better prosthetic fit

### Scanning Time
- Zirconia: 142 ± 18 seconds per arch
- PEEK: 168 ± 22 seconds per arch
- p = 0.012 (zirconia faster to scan)

### Subgroup Analysis
- **Maxilla**: Zirconia gap 43.1 ± 13.2 vs PEEK 59.7 ± 16.1 (p = 0.008); Trueness 29.2 ± 8.7 vs 42.3 ± 11.2 (p = 0.004)
- **Mandible**: Zirconia gap 41.1 ± 12.1 vs PEEK 57.8 ± 14.2 (p = 0.021); Trueness 27.3 ± 7.6 vs 40.8 ± 9.4 (p = 0.011)
- Both jaws consistent: zirconia superior

## Discussion

Zirconia substructures yielded significantly smaller marginal gaps (42.3 vs 58.9 μm) and better 3D trueness (28.4 vs 41.7 μm RMS) compared to PEEK. Both values for zirconia are within clinically acceptable thresholds (< 50–100 μm for marginal gap). PEEK values approach/exceed some clinical thresholds.

Mechanism: Zirconia's opaque, high-refractive-index surface provides consistent light reflection for IOS (Medit i700 uses structured light/blue LED). PEEK's translucency causes subsurface scattering, reducing scan data quality. This aligns with prior in vitro studies (Yilmaz 2023, Revilla-León 2022) showing material-dependent scanning accuracy.

Clinical relevance: For full-arch prostheses where passive fit is paramount, zirconia substructures offer measurable accuracy advantages. However, PEEK has other benefits (shock absorption, radiolucency, lighter weight) that may outweigh accuracy differences in specific cases.

Limitations: Single IOS (Medit i700), single center, short-term (delivery only), no functional loading data, no long-term marginal gap stability.

## Conclusions

Zirconia substructure frameworks provide significantly better marginal adaptation (42.3 vs 58.9 μm) and higher 3D scanning trueness (28.4 vs 41.7 μm RMS) compared to PEEK for full-arch implant-supported restorations. Substructure material optical properties significantly influence IOS accuracy and subsequent prosthetic fit.

## Key Points
- RCT, 20 patients (10 zirconia, 10 PEEK), full-arch implant-supported
- IOS: Medit i700; Scan bodies: Medit; Milling: 5-axis
- Marginal gap: Zirconia 42.3 ± 12.7 μm vs PEEK 58.9 ± 15.3 μm (p = 0.003)
- 3D trueness (RMS): Zirconia 28.4 ± 8.2 μm vs PEEK 41.7 ± 10.5 μm (p = 0.001)
- Scanning time: Zirconia faster (142 vs 168 sec, p = 0.012)
- Both within acceptable clinical range, but zirconia superior
- Optical properties (opacity vs translucency) explain difference
