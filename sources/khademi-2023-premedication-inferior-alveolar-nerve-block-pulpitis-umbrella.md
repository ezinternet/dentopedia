## Why Ingested

기존 [[wiki/local-anesthesia/rujirawan-2025-combined-supplementary-ianb-irreversible-pulpitis-nma]]가 비가역 치수염에서 IANB 실패 시 주사 전략(보충/병용 주사)으로 성공률을 높이는 근거라면, 본 우산리뷰(umbrella review, 4편 SR 종합)는 주사 전이 아닌 전처치 약물(premedication) 경로로 접근한 상보적 근거 — 이부프로펜(ibuprofen) >400 mg 전처치가 IANB 성공률을 높인다는 결론을 보강한다.

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (review of systematic reviews) synthesizing 4 systematic reviews (3 with meta-analysis, 1 without; primary RCTs n=7–35 per SR) on premedication before IANB in adult patients with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis in molar teeth.

All four included SRs converged on the same conclusion — premedication with ibuprofen >400 mg significantly increases IANB anesthetic success (de Geus: +79% success vs ~20% placebo); ibuprofen/NSAIDs outperformed acetaminophen, and opioid/ketamine evidence was limited but promising (Jadad-algorithm best-evidence SR: Nagendrababu 2018, AMSTAR 2 "high").

Because all four SRs agreed, the umbrella review authors performed a qualitative/narrative synthesis rather than an additional umbrella-level meta-analysis; two of the four component SRs were AMSTAR 2 "low quality," and primary-RCT heterogeneity (diagnostic criteria, anesthetic volume, outcome measure) limits precision.

## 세줄요약

성인 하악 구치 증상성 비가역 치수염(symptomatic irreversible pulpitis) 환자에서 하치조신경차단(inferior alveolar nerve block, IANB) 전 전처치(premedication)를 다룬 체계적 문헌고찰 4편(3편 메타분석 포함, 1편 미포함; 개별 RCT 7~35편)을 종합한 우산리뷰(umbrella review).

포함된 4편 모두 이부프로펜(ibuprofen) >400 mg 전처치가 IANB 마취 성공률을 유의하게 높인다는 동일한 결론에 도달(de Geus: 성공률 위약군 대비 +79%p, 위약군 성공률 약 20%); 이부프로펜/NSAID가 아세트아미노펜보다 우월했고 오피오이드·케타민 근거는 제한적이나 유망함(JADAD 알고리즘 최적근거 SR: Nagendrababu 2018, AMSTAR 2 "high").

4편 SR의 결론이 일치했기 때문에 저자들은 우산리뷰 자체의 추가 메타분석 없이 질적 종합만 수행했으며, 4편 중 2편은 AMSTAR 2 "low quality"였고 개별 RCT 간 이질성(진단기준·마취량·평가지표 상이)이 정밀도를 제한한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First umbrella-level synthesis (as of the search date, September 2021) of all systematic reviews on premedication before IANB in irreversible pulpitis, resolving discordance across 4 SRs via the Jadad decision algorithm.
- Identifies ibuprofen >400 mg as the single premedication drug with converging support across independently conducted systematic reviews.
- Applies AMSTAR 2 to grade the 4 included SRs themselves (meta-level quality appraisal), flagging 2 of 4 as "low quality."

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Umbrella review per PRISMA; PICO question — does premedication (I) improve IANB success (O) vs placebo (C) in adults (P) with irreversible pulpitis in molar teeth?
- **Search**: PubMed (MEDLINE), Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Library up to 2021-09-15; no date restriction; English only; hand search of reference lists; grey literature via OpenGrey, ProQuest, WorldCat.
- **Eligibility**: included SRs had to report the anesthetic solution and premedication drug, and specify the method used to confirm anesthesia (no response to EPT, no pain during access/instrumentation). Excluded: network meta-analyses, narrative/critical reviews, letters, case reports/series, observational/clinical studies (i.e., only systematic reviews of RCTs were eligible units of analysis).
- **Selection/extraction**: two independent reviewers (S.B., N.M.), third-reviewer (A.K.) arbitration.
- **Quality appraisal**: AMSTAR 2 (16-item checklist, 7 critical domains); inter-rater kappa = 0.91 (95% CI 0.89–0.92).
- **Evidence reconciliation**: Jadad decision algorithm used to select the single best-evidence SR when reviews addressing the same question showed discordant conclusions.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 29 reviews identified → 9 duplicates removed → 16 excluded at title/abstract → 4 retained for full text → all 4 included: Shirvani (2017, MA, 16 RCTs/1900 pts, AMSTAR 2 "low"), Nagendrababu (2018, MA, 13 RCTs/1174 pts, AMSTAR 2 "high"), de Geus (2019, MA, 7 RCTs, AMSTAR 2 "high"), Karapinar-Kazandag (2019, SR without MA, 35 RCTs, AMSTAR 2 "low").
- Most primary RCTs used 2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine as the anesthetic solution.
- Nagendrababu (best-evidence SR by Jadad algorithm): ibuprofen >400 mg most effective, followed by diclofenac 50 mg and ketorolac 10 mg.
- de Geus: single 1 h pre-treatment ibuprofen dose raised success by 79 percentage points vs placebo (placebo success ~20%).
- Shirvani: preemptive analgesics improve intraoperative analgesia; co-administration strategy and anesthetic choice may modify effect; no association found between analgesic timing/dosage and effect.
- Karapinar-Kazandag: ibuprofen/other NSAIDs outperform acetaminophen; oral ketamine reduces cartridges needed and lowers postoperative pain.
- Bottom line: premedication with ibuprofen >400 mg is the only drug/dose combination with convergent, AMSTAR-2-high-quality support across independent SRs; ibuprofen <400 mg showed no significant effect.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Umbrella review performed no independent quantitative pooling across the 4 SRs (a meta-analysis was planned only if the reviews disagreed; since they agreed, only qualitative/narrative synthesis was done) — so this is a synthesis-of-syntheses, not a fresh statistical meta-analysis.
- Primary-RCT heterogeneity across the underlying trials: geography, sample size, operator experience, irreversible-pulpitis diagnostic criteria, anesthetic volume, vasoconstrictor concentration, injection rate.
- Outcome-measure inconsistency among primary RCTs (pulp sensitivity test/EPT vs patient-reported VAS pain during treatment) complicates cross-study interpretation.
- Language restriction to English introduces potential selection bias.
- None of the 4 included SRs reported funding-source information for their primary studies (AMSTAR 2 item 10 failed across all four).
- 2 of 4 included SRs rated "low quality" by AMSTAR 2, limiting confidence despite convergent conclusions.

## 6. Related Work

- Nagendrababu et al. (2018) — best-evidence SR by Jadad algorithm; ibuprofen >400 mg.
- de Geus et al. (2019) — ibuprofen-only SR+MA; +79% success vs placebo.
- Shirvani et al. (2017) — broader premedication SR+MA (NSAIDs, acetaminophen+opioid).
- Karapinar-Kazandag et al. (2019) — SR without MA; ibuprofen/NSAIDs, oral ketamine.

## 7. Glossary

- **IANB (Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block)**: 하치조신경차단 — the standard regional block for mandibular pulpal anesthesia.
- **Umbrella review**: 우산리뷰 — a review that treats other systematic reviews as its unit of analysis, synthesizing across them rather than across primary studies directly.
- **AMSTAR 2**: A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews, version 2 — 16-item critical-appraisal instrument for systematic reviews.
- **Jadad decision algorithm**: a structured method for choosing the best body of evidence when multiple systematic reviews on the same question reach discordant conclusions.
- **EPT (Electric Pulp Tester)**: 전기치수생활력검사기 — device used to confirm pulpal anesthesia by absence of sensory response.
- **Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis**: 증상성 비가역 치수염 — inflamed pulp with spontaneous/lingering pain, associated with elevated local inflammatory mediators that reduce local anesthetic efficacy.
