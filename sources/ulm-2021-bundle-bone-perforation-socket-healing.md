## Why Ingested

Labial bundle bone 흡수 서베일런스 2026-10-21. The wiki explains *that* bundle bone resorbs after extraction (Araújo 2005, two-phase model) but has no paper that characterises the structural reason **why the buccal plate loses more than the palatal**: this paper provides the histological explanation — the labial bundle bone at the incisors/canines is fused with the external cortical plate, leaving zero trabecular reservoir behind it, while the palatal wall retains retroalveolar trabecular bone. That asymmetry directly predicts asymmetric resorption.

The paper also introduces a practical intervention (bundle bone perforation) derived from the GBR decortication rationale, with explicit instructions on where to perforate and, crucially, where not to (buccal is off-limits because it is already ≤1 mm cortical with nothing behind it).

## Three-line Summary

Technical note + single case (University of Vienna, 2021; Clin Exp Dent Res 8(1):3-8), first describing the structural asymmetry of the alveolar bundle bone that explains why the buccal loses more after extraction, then proposing a perforation technique analogous to GBR decortication to improve vascularization of the socket.

The key anatomical finding: at the anterior incisors and canines the **labial bundle bone is fused with the external cortical plate of the alveolar process** — there is no trabecular compartment between them ("retroalveolar bone" absent); on the palatal/lingual side, a trabecular compartment always exists between the bundle bone and the cortical plate, giving the palatal wall both bone marrow resources and vascularity that the labial side simply lacks.

The practical intervention — 6-10 perforations of the palatal/lingual bundle bone with a <1 mm bur, 2-3 mm apart, extending into trabecular bone — is proposed to improve marrow access and blood inflow at socket healing or ARP; the buccal bundle bone is explicitly **spared** because it is fused with the cortex and ≤1 mm.

## 세줄요약

기술 보고 + 단일 증례 (빈 의대, 2021; Clin Exp Dent Res 8(1):3-8) — 치조 다발골 (bundle bone, 치조골 고유층 / 사상판 / 투명대) 의 구조적 비대칭이 발치 후 협측 소실을 악화하는 해부학적 이유를 기술하고, 이를 바탕으로 소켓 혈관화 개선 천공 술식을 제안.

핵심 해부: 전치부·견치의 **협측 다발골은 치조돌기 외측 피질판과 융합** — 그 사이에 해면골(교소골) 공간이 없음. 반면 구개측/설측은 다발골과 피질판 사이에 후방 치조 해면골("retroalveolar bone")이 존재하여 골수·혈관 자원이 풍부함. 이 비대칭이 협측의 더 큰 흡수를 설명함.

실용 술식: 발치 후 구개측/설측 다발골에 <1 mm 버로 6-10개 천공(간격 2-3 mm, 해면골까지 도달) → 골수 혈관 접근 개선 / 지혈·성장인자 방출 / Frost RAP 유도. **협측은 피질판과 융합(≤1 mm)이므로 천공 금지.**

Single case: tooth #11 (failing endodontically treated central incisor with buccal fistula), perforation technique followed by Bio-Oss Collagen + Mucograft Seal ridge preservation, BLT SLActive 4.1×12 mm implant at 9 months. No control group; histological assessment not performed.

## 2. Intervention / Measurement

**Anatomical characterisation:**
- Bundle bone thickness: **0.22–0.54 mm** (range cited from Hubar)
- At incisors/canines, labial bundle bone fuses with external cortical plate — **no retroalveolar trabecular bone** on buccal side
- Palatal/lingual: trabecular bone compartment ("retroalveolar bone", Sicher & Du Brul) is always present between bundle bone and inner cortical plate

**Perforation technique:**
- Instrument: round bur diameter <1 mm
- Location: palatal/lingual wall of socket; also mesial/distal when no adjacent teeth
- Number: 6-10 perforations per socket
- Spacing: ~2-3 mm between holes
- Depth: extend into trabecular bone to provoke bleeding; do NOT breach the external plate
- Buccal: explicitly excluded (fused cortex, ≤1 mm, no trabecular behind it)
- Can be used before unassisted healing OR before any ARP procedure

## 3. Results

Single case report — no histological or controlled data on perforation efficacy.

Clinical outcome: uneventful healing over 9 months, successful implant placement, crown delivery. No adverse events related to perforation. Multiple patients treated at MUV without complications (no standardised data reported).

## 4. Clinical Insights

The paper provides the anatomical rationale for an asymmetry that all prior papers (Araújo 2005, Chappuis 2013) document as outcome but do not mechanistically explain at the structural level:

> *"At the incisors and canines, the bundle bone of the labial surface often fuses with the external cortical plate of the alveolar process, whereas on the palatal/lingual side regularly a varying volume of trabecular bone is present between the bundle bone and the inner cortical plate."*

This fusion means the buccal socket wall is **entirely cortical** — bundle bone and cortex are the same structure — so when bundle bone is resorbed (phase 1 of Araújo's model), the entire buccal crest loses what little it had, with no marrow reservoir behind it to support revascularisation or osteogenesis. The palatal wall retains retroalveolar trabecular bone throughout, explaining its relative resistance to volume loss.

The proposed mechanism for perforation benefit:
1. **Improved vascularization** — opens marrow space, provokes bleeding and clot formation
2. **Growth factor / cytokine release** from bone marrow
3. **Frost's Regional Acceleratory Phenomenon (RAP)** — local bone remodeling acceleration
4. Analogous rationale to cortical decortication in GBR recipient sites

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single case, no controls, no histological data
- The structural claim (buccal fusion at anteriors) is cited from older anatomical texts (Sicher & Du Brul) and from Tsigarida et al., not directly measured in this study
- No data on whether perforation actually reduces alveolar ridge loss vs. ridge preservation without perforation
- Preclinical trials with micro-CT and histology were launched at time of publication — no follow-up results yet

## 6. Relations to Existing Wiki

- **Araújo 2005** ([[wiki/bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/araujo-2005-dimensional-ridge-alterations-tooth-extraction]]) described the two resorption phases but attributed differential buccal/lingual loss to bundle bone content, not to structural asymmetry — this paper adds the anatomical mechanism.
- **Todorovic 2023** ([[wiki/immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/todorovic-2023-buccal-palatal-alveolar-bone]]) confirmed via micro-CT that buccal bone is almost always <0.5 mm and bundle bone only; Ulm 2021 explains the *why* structurally.
- **Trombelli 2008** and **Cardaropoli 2003** establish the healing timeline; Ulm 2021 argues perforation accelerates that timeline via RAP.

## 7. Glossary

- **Bundle bone (다발골, 다발 골, 치조골 고유층)**: thin cortical bone lining the alveolus, penetrated by Sharpey fibres from the periodontal ligament. Synonyms: alveolar bone proper, cribriform plate, lamina dura.
- **Retroalveolar bone**: trabecular bone between the bundle bone and the inner cortical plate of the alveolar process; present palatally/lingually, absent labially in the anterior maxilla.
- **RAP (Regional Acceleratory Phenomenon)**: Frost's concept — local surgical trauma accelerates bone remodeling 2-10× for 3-6 months.
- **Decortication**: perforation or scoring of cortical bone to provoke bleeding and marrow access; standard preparatory step in GBR recipient sites.
