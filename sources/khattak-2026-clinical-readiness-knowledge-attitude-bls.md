## Why Ingested

Saudi Arabia cross-sectional survey (n=400) quantifying BLS/CPR knowledge, attitudes, and clinical readiness among practicing dentists; provides multivariate predictors of good knowledge (aOR) and confidence. First study in this wiki examining emergency preparedness via logistic regression in a Gulf/Middle Eastern dental workforce. Fills the drug/medical-emergencies category alongside international preparedness literature.

→ [[drug/systemic-disease/choufani-2025-lebanese-dentists-preparedness-to-deal]]

## Three-line Summary

A cross-sectional survey of 400 Saudi dentists found only 26% had good BLS/CPR knowledge, 7.5% had an AED available, and 44.5% had an emergency protocol in their clinic. Recent workshop attendance (aOR 4.91) and prior emergency experience (aOR 12.97) were the dominant predictors of good knowledge; the same workshop predictor was even stronger for CPR confidence (aOR 6.93). Findings support mandatory refresher training and clinic-level AED provision to bridge the attitude-behavior gap in dental emergency preparedness.

## 세줄요약

사우디아라비아 치과의사 400명 대상 단면 조사에서 기본소생술 (Basic Life Support, BLS) 지식 "양호" 비율은 26%에 불과했고, 자동제세동기 (Automated External Defibrillator, AED) 보유 클리닉은 7.5%에 그쳤다. 최근 5년 내 워크숍 수료(aOR 4.91)와 응급상황 경험(aOR 12.97)이 양호한 지식의 가장 강력한 예측인자였다. 긍정적 태도(90.5% 도덕적 의무 동의)와 실제 준비도 사이의 간극을 줄이기 위해 정기 재교육 및 AED 의무 비치가 필요하다.

## Document Information

- **Study type**: Cross-sectional survey
- **Study period**: Sep–Oct 2024
- **Setting**: Multiple regions of Saudi Arabia (private and government sectors)
- **Sample**: n=400 licensed dentists (convenience sampling)
- **Instrument**: 27-item validated questionnaire; Cronbach's α: knowledge 0.78, attitude 0.81, clinical readiness 0.76
- **Analysis**: SPSS v26; chi-square, t-tests, ANOVA; binary logistic regression

## Key Contributions

1. Quantifies that only 26% of Saudi dentists have "good" BLS/CPR knowledge; 33% have low knowledge.
2. Identifies recent workshop attendance and prior emergency encounter as the two dominant modifiable predictors (logistic regression, both p<0.001).
3. Documents critically low AED availability (7.5%) despite 85.5% of respondents agreeing CPR is critical.
4. Demonstrates that demographic variables (age, gender, designation, years of experience) are not significant predictors of good knowledge — training exposure is.
5. Provides comparable data to Lebanon (Choufani 2025) and Poland (Smereka 2019) surveys in a Gulf/Middle East context.

## Methodology

**Design**: Structured self-administered online questionnaire distributed via professional dental associations and social media (Sep–Oct 2024).

**Sample size calculation**: Minimum 385 (50% prevalence assumption, 95% CI, 5% margin of error); recruited 400.

**Questionnaire sections**:
1. Sociodemographics (6 items): age, gender, designation, experience, training history, emergency encounters
2. Knowledge (9 MCQ items): scored Low (1–3), Moderate (4–6), Good (7–9)
3. Attitudes (4 dichotomous items): professional obligation, relevance, mandatory training
4. Clinical readiness/confidence (8 items): clinic protocols, kit availability, AED, confidence per skill

**Statistical analysis**: Bivariate (chi-square, t-test, ANOVA) + binary logistic regression for predictors of (a) good knowledge and (b) high CPR confidence.

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional design: no causal inference
- Convenience sampling: potential self-selection bias (more training-conscious respondents)
- Self-reported confidence: may not reflect actual CPR skill performance
- No geographic distribution data within Saudi Arabia
- Actual CPR skill performance not assessed (simulation/manikin)

## Glossary

- **BLS (Basic Life Support)**: Sequence of chest compressions + rescue breathing + AED use for cardiac/respiratory arrest
- **CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)**: Manual chest compressions ± rescue breathing to maintain circulation
- **AED (Automated External Defibrillator)**: Portable device for automated rhythm analysis and defibrillation
- **aOR**: Adjusted odds ratio from multivariate logistic regression
- **Nagelkerke R²**: Pseudo-R² for logistic regression model fit
