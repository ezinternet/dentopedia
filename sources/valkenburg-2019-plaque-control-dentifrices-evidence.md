## Three-line Summary

Meta-review (umbrella review) of 10 systematic reviews (up to Feb 2017) synthesizing dentifrice active-ingredient evidence (triclosan, stannous fluoride, chlorhexidine, aloe vera) for plaque and gingivitis control in adults.

Toothbrushing with a standard fluoride dentifrice showed no added mechanical plaque-removal effect over brushing alone (DiffM 0.00, 95% CI [-0.05, 0.05], P=.91); triclosan-copolymer and stannous fluoride dentifrices showed strong GRADE-rated evidence of benefit for both plaque and gingivitis, chlorhexidine dentifrice showed weak evidence of a small benefit, and aloe vera very weak evidence.

Clinicians should not expect dentifrice per se to boost mechanical plaque removal, but triclosan or stannous fluoride formulations add meaningful chemical antiplaque/antigingivitis benefit over a plain fluoride dentifrice; considerable heterogeneity (up to 98%) across the underlying meta-analyses and industry-sponsorship/publication-bias risk limit precision of the pooled estimates.

## 세줄요약

성인 대상 치약(dentifrice) 활성성분(트리클로산, 주석불화물, 클로르헥시딘, 알로에베라)의 치태·치은염 조절 근거를 종합한 메타리뷰(우산 리뷰) — 2017년 2월까지의 체계적 문헌고찰 10편을 통합.

표준 불소 치약을 사용한 칫솔질은 치약 없이 칫솔질만 한 경우 대비 기계적 치태 제거에 유의한 부가효과가 없었으나(DiffM 0.00, 95% CI [-0.05, 0.05], P=.91), 트리클로산-코폴리머 및 주석불화물 치약은 치태·치은염 모두에서 강한(GRADE) 근거의 효과를 보였고, 클로르헥시딘 치약은 약한 근거의 소폭 효과, 알로에베라는 매우 약한 근거를 보였다.

치약 자체가 기계적 치태 제거를 늘려주지는 않지만 트리클로산·주석불화물 성분은 일반 불소 치약 대비 의미 있는 화학적 항치태·항치은염 효과를 더하며, 근거가 된 메타분석들의 상당한 이질성(최대 98%)과 산업 후원·출판 편향 위험은 통합 추정치의 정밀도를 제한하는 핵심 한계다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First dedicated **meta-review specifically on dentifrices** (companion to prior meta-reviews on mechanical home-care devices and on mouthwash chemical agents), closing the trio of home-care-product evidence syntheses.
- Quantifies, via weighted-mean pooling of DiffM values across multiple published meta-analyses, that **dentifrice itself contributes no significant mechanical plaque-removal benefit** over brushing alone — a frequently overlooked distinction between the vehicle (dentifrice) and its chemical actives.
- Produces GRADE evidence-strength summary table across four active ingredients (aloe vera, chlorhexidine, triclosan, stannous fluoride), ranking triclosan and stannous fluoride as "strong" evidence, chlorhexidine as "weak", and aloe vera as "very weak".
- Directly compares triclosan-copolymer vs stannous fluoride head-to-head (via Sälzer et al. and a corroborating network meta-analysis by Escribano et al.), concluding a clinically minor/inconclusive difference on gingival parameters but triclosan favored on the Q&H plaque index.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: A priori protocol; AMSTAR (2007) used for methodological rigor of the meta-review process itself.
- **Search**: Three databases (MEDLINE-PubMed, Cochrane/DARE, ADA EBD) to March 2017; MeSH/text terms "toothpastes"/"toothpaste"/"dentifrice"/"dentifrices" filtered to systematic review/meta-analysis; hand-search of reference lists; PROSPERO checked for in-progress reviews.
- **Screening**: Two independent reviewers (CV, DES), third reviewer (GAW) for disagreements; 205 unique reviews screened → 12 full texts → 2 excluded (1 narrative review, 1 non-systematic literature review) → **10 systematic reviews included**.
- **Quality appraisal**: Combined PRISMA + AMSTAR 27-item checklist scored as a percentage; risk-of-bias bands: 0-40% high, 40-60% substantial, 60-80% moderate, 80-100% low.
- **Quantitative synthesis**: Where ≥2 comparable meta-analyses reported the same clinical index, an overall weighted mean of the difference-of-means (DiffM) was calculated (SPSS v22), each meta-analysis weighted by its number of included comparisons; heterogeneity assessed via chi-squared/I².
- **Evidence grading**: GRADE framework (study design, risk of bias, consistency, precision, directness, publication bias, magnitude of effect) applied per active ingredient.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Mechanical effect of dentifrice** (Valkenburg et al., prior SR, 10 studies/20 comparisons): 49.2% plaque removed with dentifrice vs 50.3% without; DiffM 0.00, 95% CI [-0.05, 0.05], P=.91 → **no significant added mechanical effect**; GRADE = moderate quality.
- **Stannous fluoride (SnF)**: 4 SRs. Q&H plaque index DiffM range -0.112 to -0.31 (3-5 comparisons each, all significant); overall weighted mean 0.25 (SD 0.08), 95% CI (0.20, 0.29). Gingival index overall weighted mean 0.14 (SD 0.02), 95% CI (0.13, 0.15).
- **Triclosan-copolymer**: 6 meta-analyses (9-18 comparisons) on Q&H plaque index, DiffM -0.447 to -0.49 (significant); overall weighted mean 0.47 (SD 0.02), 95% CI (0.47, 0.48). Gingival index overall weighted mean (4 meta-analyses) 0.26 (SD 0.01), 95% CI (0.25, 0.26).
- **Chlorhexidine (CHX)**: Descriptive analysis (heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis in the primary SR) showed majority of CHX-dentifrice experiments significant for plaque; all bleeding-outcome studies favored CHX dentifrice. Two meta-analyses on Q&H plaque index gave overall weighted mean 0.73 (SD 0.07), 95% CI (0.70, 0.79). CHX **gel** vs placebo showed no clear trend, and CHX dentifrice was found less effective than CHX mouthwash.
- **Aloe vera**: only 2 RCTs identified, heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis; similar efficacy to control dentifrice reported in both; "not sufficiently defined."
- **Triclosan vs SnF head-to-head** (Sälzer et al., 15 studies): gingival index no significant difference (DiffM -0.04, P=.34); gingival bleeding significantly favored SnF (DiffM 0.02, P<.001); Q&H plaque index significantly favored triclosan (DiffM -0.29, P<.001); no difference on RMNPI index (P=.07). A subsequent network meta-analysis (Escribano et al.) corroborated triclosan favorability on Q&H (weighted mean difference -0.34, 95% CI [-0.56, -0.12]).
- **GRADE evidence profile summary**: Triclosan = strong (moderate magnitude); SnF = strong (moderate magnitude); CHX = weak (small magnitude); Aloe vera = very weak (very small magnitude).
- **Quality of included SRs**: 7/10 low risk of bias, 2 moderate, 1 substantial (scores 44-96%).
- **Practical implication stated by authors**: if pursuing both anticaries and antigingivitis effects, SnF would be a logical dentifrice choice, but its known staining side effect currently limits it as a routine universal recommendation.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Confounded vehicle vs active-ingredient effect**: dentifrice is never used in isolation from mechanical brushing, making the adjunctive chemical effect difficult to cleanly separate from mechanical action.
- **Placebo/control formulation confounds**: silica abrasive, SLS detergent, and flavoring agents in the "control" dentifrice can themselves affect outcome parameters and patient preference/compliance.
- **Considerable heterogeneity**: I² up to 98% for plaque scores (mostly ≥90%) and ≥70% for gingivitis scores across pooled meta-analyses — pooled DiffM point estimates should be interpreted cautiously.
- **Publication bias risk**: included SRs generally did not incorporate unpublished data, which the authors note likely inflates (over-estimates) the true pooled effect size.
- **Excluded ingredients**: baking soda, essential oils, sanguinarine, and zinc citrate were each represented by only a single 6-month study and were excluded from quantitative synthesis — evidence gap for these actives.
- **Author-suggested future work**: (1) evaluate dentifrice's effect on plaque *regrowth* specifically, since no adjuvant mechanical removal effect was found; (2) a structured cost-effectiveness analysis of specific-ingredient (more expensive) dentifrices vs plain fluoride dentifrice, given long-term-use cost implications.
- **Not addressed**: fluoride's anticaries effect (explicitly out of scope), dentin hypersensitivity actives, whitening/anticalculus dentifrices (mentioned narratively but not systematically pooled here).

## 7. Glossary

- **Dentifrice**: preparation used with a toothbrush to clean/polish teeth and deliver active ingredients (fluoride, antiplaque agents).
- **Triclosan (Tcs)**: nonionic chlorinated aromatic antibacterial/antifungal compound, often combined with a copolymer or zinc citrate to enhance retention/efficacy.
- **Stannous fluoride (SnF)**: tin-fluoride compound with both anticaries (remineralization) and antimicrobial/antiplaque properties; associated with tooth staining.
- **Chlorhexidine (CHX)**: cationic bisbiguanide antimicrobial, broad-spectrum against gram-positive/negative organisms and yeasts; gold-standard antiplaque agent in mouthwash form.
- **Q&H (Quigley-Hein) plaque index / S&L (Silness-Löe) / L&S (Löe-Silness) gingival index**: clinical indices used to score plaque extent and gingival inflammation.
- **DiffM**: difference of means; the pooled effect-size metric used throughout this meta-review's weighted-mean calculations.
- **GRADE**: Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation — framework for rating quality/strength of a body of evidence.
- **AMSTAR/PRISMA**: methodological-quality and reporting-quality checklists for systematic reviews.
- **Meta-review (umbrella review)**: a systematic review whose units of analysis are other systematic reviews/meta-analyses, rather than primary studies.
