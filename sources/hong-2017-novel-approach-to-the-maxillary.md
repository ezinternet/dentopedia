## Why Ingested

Adds an office-based, endoscope-free irrigation technique (canine-fossa antrostomy with tube drain and nasal suction) for graft-related maxillary sinusitis, which complements the ENT-heavy options already held: it offers a lower-tech counterpart to the FESS + intraoral approach in [[sinus-lift/lateral/nam-2014-fess-intraoral-implant-related-sinusitis]] and a canine-fossa (rather than lateral sinus-lift window or membrane-incision) route compared with [[sinus-lift/lateral/park-2024-surgical-drainage-simultaneous-sinus-floor]]. Part of a PubMed-text sweep on post-sinus-graft sinusitis management.

## Three-line Summary

Case report (n=1, 47-year-old patient) of acute right maxillary sinusitis diagnosed 10 days after crestal (osteotome) sinus elevation, bone graft and implant placement at a local clinic.

Treatment was explantation of the infected implant, removal of infected graft material, and four irrigation sessions 4 days apart through a canine-fossa antrostomy with a tube drain plus 21 days of antibiotics/decongestant/mucolytic/NSAID; headache resolved immediately after graft/implant removal, no pus after four sessions, and CT at 3.5 months was conclusive of recovery.

The authors present the technique as a patient-friendly, office-based alternative to Caldwell-Luc/ESS, but this is a single uncontrolled case with a blind technique, so the claimed advantages are not tested in the paper.

## 세줄요약

증례보고 (n=1, 47세): 지역 의원에서 치조정접근 (crestal, osteotome) 상악동 거상·골이식·임플란트 식립 10일 후 우측 급성 상악동염으로 의뢰됨.

감염된 임플란트 제거 및 감염 이식재 제거 후, 견치와 (canine fossa) 상악동 개창 (antrostomy)을 통한 관류를 4일 간격 4회 시행하고 21일간 약물 (항생제·충혈제거제·점액용해제·NSAIDs) 투여; 이식재·임플란트 제거 직후 두통 소실, 4회 관류 후 농 축적 없음, 3.5개월 CT에서 회복 확인.

저자들은 Caldwell-Luc/내시경 부비동 수술 (Endoscopic Sinus Surgery, ESS) 대비 환자 친화적인 외래 시술이라 주장하지만, 단일 증례·대조군 없음·맹시 (blind) 술식이라 주장한 장점은 논문 안에서 검증되지 않았다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Describes an office-based irrigation technique for post-sinus-graft maxillary sinusitis: lateral antrostomy through the canine fossa (described as the thinnest area of the anterior sinus wall), syringe aspiration of pus for culture, suction/irrigation tip to remove infected graft material, tube drain sutured to the gingiva, and copious saline irrigation through the drain.
- Adds a flexible silastic suction tip inserted into the ipsilateral nostril toward the ostio-meatal complex so saline does not flow out of the nose and mouth (the patient-comfort element of the technique).
- Argues, from literature rather than from its own data, that this avoids the need for an endoscope and is an alternative to Caldwell-Luc and ESS for odontogenic sinusitis generally, not only after sinus grafting.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: single case report; no comparison group
- **n**: 1 patient (47 years old); sex not stated in the supplied text
- **Presentation**: foul odor, tenderness on the right sinus, headache; gingival redness and swelling of the right buccal gingiva, pus discharge from the right nose, right lymphadenopathy; Waters' view showed haziness of the right maxillary sinus (acute sinusitis)
- **Surgical steps**: explantation of the infected implant; lateral antrostomy via canine fossa; pus aspirated and cultured; infected graft material removed with suction and irrigation tip and light saline irrigation; tube drain sutured to gingiva; silastic suction tip into the right nostril; copious irrigation through the drain
- **Schedule**: four consecutive irrigation sessions, 4 days apart; drain removed on the last irrigation day
- **Medication**: amoxicillin 500 mg, pseudoephedrine hydrochloride 60 mg, carbocisteine 750 mg and NSAIDs, three times daily for a total of 21 days
- **Outcomes**: symptom relief, pus accumulation, culture, follow-up CT

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Headache relieved immediately after removal of the infected graft material and implant.
- After four irrigation sessions, right buccal discomfort had diminished and pus accumulation was not observed.
- Culture on the first day of treatment grew alpha-hemolytic *Streptococcus viridans*.
- CT at 3.5 months showed radiolucency in the infected right maxillary sinus (figure legend: radiolucency on both sinuses, all right-sided symptoms relieved), read as conclusive of recovery.
- The abstract states patient satisfaction with less irrigation discomfort; no questionnaire, scale or numerical comfort outcome is reported in the text.
- Literature figures quoted in the background/discussion (not this study's data): odontogenic origin accounts for 10-12% of maxillary sinusitis and 30-40% of chronic maxillary rhinosinusitis; iatrogenic causes were 65.7% of odontogenic chronic maxillary sinusitis in the review by Lechien et al.; Caldwell-Luc recurrence 9-15%; oral antibiotics are effective against oral flora and sinus pathogens for only 21 to 28 days.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- n=1 with no comparator; recovery cannot be attributed to the irrigation route rather than to explantation, graft removal and 21 days of antibiotics.
- Follow-up is a single CT at 3.5 months; no longer-term recurrence data.
- Statements that the technique has lower complication rates, less blood loss, shorter operating time and lower cost than Caldwell-Luc/ESS are asserted in the Conclusions without data from this case.
- The authors acknowledge it is a blind technique requiring accurate anatomical knowledge of the maxillary sinus and sufficient irrigation.
- The index case followed a crestal (osteotome) lift, so the paper does not show the technique in lateral-window graft sinusitis, despite the generalization to all odontogenic sinusitis.
- Ostial patency/obstruction is not assessed in the text, which matters for the irrigation-outflow rationale discussed in other held cases.

## 6. Related Work

- Nam 2014 [[sinus-lift/lateral/nam-2014-fess-intraoral-implant-related-sinusitis]]: FESS + intraoral approach under general anesthesia for implant-related sinusitis; the higher-intervention counterpart.
- Park 2024 [[sinus-lift/lateral/park-2024-surgical-drainage-simultaneous-sinus-floor]]: intraoral-only drainage/irrigation as an alternative to FESS (intentional membrane incision during lateral-window lift).
- Ohyama 2022 [[sinus-lift/lateral/ohyama-2022-novel-management-severe-odontogenic-maxillary]]: Caldwell-Luc access with a nasoantral window argued on ostial pathophysiology; a different surgical philosophy for refractory odontogenic sinusitis.
- Lechien et al. (cited by the authors as ref. 4): iatrogenic etiology 65.7% of odontogenic chronic maxillary sinusitis (not independently held in the wiki).

## 7. Glossary

- **Canine fossa antrostomy**: opening made through the thin anterior sinus wall at the canine fossa to access the maxillary sinus for irrigation or surgery.
- **Crestal socket osteotome technique**: transcrestal sinus floor elevation using osteotomes through the implant osteotomy.
- **Ostio-meatal complex (OMC)**: drainage junction of the maxillary sinus into the middle meatus of the nose.
- **Caldwell-Luc**: classic open sinus surgery through the anterior wall; reported here with 9-15% recurrence and notable morbidity.
- **ESS**: endoscopic sinus surgery.
- **Waters' view**: plain-film radiograph used to image the paranasal sinuses.
