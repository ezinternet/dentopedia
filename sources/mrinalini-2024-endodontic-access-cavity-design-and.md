## Why Ingested
This systematic review and meta-analysis quantifies fracture resistance outcomes across access cavity designs (conventional vs. conservative/truss), providing level-1 evidence to inform the ongoing debate between traditional straight-line access and newer minimally invasive approaches — directly complements [[endodontics/anatomy/kapetanaki-2021-access-cavity-designs-endodontic-review]], [[endodontics/anatomy/dioguardi-2024-access-cavity-designs-canal-orifice-scoping]], and [[endodontics/shaping/rathke-2024-ex-vivo-minimally-invasive-endodontic]].

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA-registered) of 14 in vitro studies comparing fracture resistance of mandibular molars prepared with conventional endodontic access (CEA) vs. conservative (contracted) and truss access cavity designs.
Conservative and truss access cavities demonstrated significantly higher fracture resistance than conventional access (standardized mean difference [SMD] = 2.61, 95% CI 1.47–3.74, p < 0.001 for conservative; SMD = −1.26, 95% CI −1.81 to −0.71, p < 0.001 for truss, with I² = 92% and 65.6%, respectively).
Extent of access cavity preparation has a substantial impact on tooth fracture resistance; newer conservative and truss designs offer biomechanical advantages over traditional wide cavities despite reduced visibility.

## 2. Key Contributions
- **Comparative meta-analysis of access cavity fracture resistance**: Quantifies the effect size (SMD) of conservative and truss access designs relative to conventional access, addressing a key clinical trade-off: does tooth structure preservation offset reduced visibility and access?
- **Evidence-based support for minimally invasive access**: Both conservative (contracted) and truss designs yield superior fracture resistance, providing level-1 evidence to refute the assumption that wider cavities are necessary for biomechanical integrity.
- **Heterogeneity interpretation**: High heterogeneity (I² = 92% for conservative) indicates substantial variation across included studies, likely driven by tooth type, instrumentation system, and operator technique — highlights need for standardized methodology in comparative access-cavity research.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Study design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA 2020 guidelines; registered with Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/zmh6p).
- **Information sources**: Six databases (PubMed, Scopus, EBSCOHost, BVS, Wiley, Google Scholar) searched from January 2000 to July 2024; English-language studies only.
- **Search strategy**: Boolean operators combining terms: conventional/traditional/conservative/truss endodontic access + fracture resistance.
- **Inclusion criteria**: 
  - In vitro and ex vivo studies
  - Extracted mandibular molar teeth (intact, pre-selected)
  - Three or more access cavity designs compared
  - Fracture resistance outcome measured (load to fracture, MPa, N)
  - Mean ± SD reported or calculable
- **Exclusion criteria**: Narrative/literature reviews, case reports, clinical studies, non-English publications, missing data.
- **Quality assessment**: Modified QUIN (Quality Assessment Tool For In Vitro Studies) tool; classified into low, moderate, high risk of bias.
- **Statistical synthesis**: STATA software; standardized mean difference (SMD) with 95% confidence intervals; forest plots; heterogeneity (I²); significance level α = 0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
| Comparison | Standardized Mean Difference (SMD) | 95% CI | P-value | I² Heterogeneity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative access vs. Conventional** | 2.61 | 1.47 to 3.74 | < 0.001 | 92% |
| **Truss access vs. Conventional** | −1.26 | −1.81 to −0.71 | < 0.001 | 65.6% |

- **Selection process**: 243 articles identified; 14 met strict inclusion criteria (6 screened by full-text review).
- **Risk of bias**: 11 studies (78.6%) rated low risk; 3 studies (21.4%) rated moderate risk; none rated high risk.
- **Meta-analysis cohort**: All 14 studies included in quantitative synthesis (no studies excluded due to data quality).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **In vitro study population**: All included studies used extracted teeth (non-vital, no PDL, no vascular dynamics); results may not translate directly to clinical in vivo fracture behavior.
- **Limited to mandibular molars**: Inclusion criteria restricted to mandibular molar teeth; maxillary molars, premolars, and incisors (which have different anatomy and access geometry) not represented — generalization to other tooth types limited.
- **High heterogeneity**: I² = 92% for conservative vs. conventional comparison indicates substantial variation across studies; likely drivers include:
  - Different instrumentation systems (rotary, reciprocating)
  - Different tooth size/anatomy criteria for pre-selection
  - Operator technique variability (hand vs. powered instruments)
  - Post-loading (immediate vs. after restoration placement)
- **Study-design variability**: Included studies differ in filling techniques (single-cone vs. carrier-based), post-preparation restoration material, and loading protocols (compressive, 3-point bending, axial shear).
- **Missing clinical trial data**: No included studies were prospective clinical trials; level-1 clinical evidence (RCT) on access cavity design and long-term fracture resistance remains absent.
- **Publication bias**: Not formally assessed (funnel plot not reported); potential for unpublished null-result studies to be missing from the meta-analysis.

## 7. Glossary
- **CEA (Conventional Endodontic Access)**: Traditional straight-line access cavity that removes the entire roof of the pulp chamber for visualization and straight-line access to all canals; maximizes visibility but removes maximum tooth structure.
- **Contracted Access Cavity (CAC) / Conservative Access**: Smaller access cavity preserving pulp chamber roof and peripheral dentin (especially pericervical dentin, PCD); reduces tooth structure loss but restricts visibility and canal instrumentation angles.
- **Truss Design**: Preservation of intervening dentin between canal orifices (e.g., leaving dentin intact between mesial and distal canals) to maintain structural bridging; reduces cavity size while maintaining individual orifice access.
- **Fracture Resistance**: Load-bearing capacity of a tooth, typically measured as the maximum compressive/shear load (N or MPa) applied to cause failure or crack initiation; higher values indicate greater resistance to catastrophic fracture.
- **SMD (Standardized Mean Difference)**: Effect size metric used in meta-analysis to compare outcomes across studies with different measurement scales; accounts for between-study heterogeneity in measurement units and sample sizes.
- **I² (Heterogeneity Index)**: Statistical measure of variability among effect sizes in a meta-analysis, expressed as a percentage (0–100%); I² > 75% typically interpreted as high heterogeneity.
- **QUIN Tool**: Quality assessment instrument for in vitro experimental studies, used to rate risk of bias in study design, methodology, and reporting.
