## Why Ingested

No held paper specifically analyzed the effect of bruxism on long-term complication patterns in full-arch implant-supported FDPs with 4–10 year follow-up. Prior full-arch pages (de Araújo Nobre 2025/2026, Huber 2026 SR) mention bruxism as a risk factor but without prosthesis-level complication breakdowns. This single-center retrospective provides detailed complication profiles stratified by bruxism, retention type, cantilever, and prosthetic material over a real-world 4–10yr period.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study of 24 patients (34 arches, 180 implants, mean follow-up 5yr, range 4–9yr) with full-arch implant-supported FDPs; assessed prosthesis survival and success, complication types, and patient-related risk indicators including bruxism, smoking, DM, and prosthetic design variables.

Cumulative prosthesis survival 80% at 5yr and 60% at 10yr; complication-free success 14.7% at 5yr with a median complication-free time of 3 years (100% of prostheses had at least one minor complication); one implant lost (survival ~99.4%); bruxism significantly associated with chipping (p=0.049) and more minor technical complications in the mandible (p=0.015).

Cantilever length (5–7.5 mm) and material type did not significantly correlate with marginal bone loss or complication rates; long-term success necessitates meticulous pre-operative risk assessment and maintenance tailored to patient-specific factors including bruxism status.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Prosthesis-level complication profiles over 4–10 years in a real-world cohort — including success (complication-free) vs survival distinction
- First paper in this wiki to statistically link bruxism to chipping in full-arch FDPs (p=0.049)
- Median complication-free prosthesis time of 3 years reinforces that full-arch maintenance is a lifetime commitment
- Cantilever lengths 5–7.5 mm showed no significant MBL effect when within conservative limits

## 3. Methodology

- **Study design**: Single-center retrospective cohort
- **N patients**: 24 (13M/11F; mean age 58.17 ± 9.73yr; range 39–77yr)
- **N arches**: 34 (20 maxilla / 14 mandible); 180 implants (Straumann, Nobel Biocare, Nucleoss)
- **Angled implants**: 24 (12 maxilla / 12 mandible) — All-on-4 concept
- **Materials**: metal-ceramic 66.7%, zirconia-ceramic 29.2%, metal-acrylic 4.2%
- **Retention**: cemented 62.5%, screw-retained 37.5%
- **Follow-up**: Mean 5.21 ± 1.56yr (range 4–9yr)
- **Patient factors**: 45.8% smokers, 45.8% bruxers ("probable" by self-report + clinical exam), 16.7% DM, 54.2% periodontal history
- **Statistics**: Kaplan–Meier, Mann-Whitney U, Pearson chi-square, Bonferroni correction

## 4. Key Results

### Survival and Success

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Implant survival | ~99.4% (1/180 lost, peri-implantitis) |
| Prosthesis survival (5yr) | **80%** |
| Prosthesis survival (10yr) | **60%** |
| Prosthesis success 1yr (complication-free) | 61.8% |
| Prosthesis success 3yr | 26.5% |
| Prosthesis success 5yr | **14.7%** |
| Median complication-free time | 3 years |
| Major complication-free 5yr | 91.2% |
| Major complication-free 10yr | 58.1% |

### Complications (per prosthesis)

| Type | Incidence |
|---|---|
| Minor biological: gingival recession | 10 arches |
| Minor biological: inflammation | 15 arches |
| Minor technical: chipping/fracture | 25 arches |
| Minor technical: decementation | 10 arches |
| Minor technical: screw loosening | 3 arches |
| Major biological: peri-implantitis | 4 arches |
| Major biological: late implant failure | 1 |
| Major technical: prosthesis fracture | 3 arches |
| Major technical: screw fracture | 2 arches |
| Major technical: abutment fracture | 1 |

### Bruxism Effect

- Chipping in 16/25 chipping arches occurred in "probable" bruxers
- Bruxism → chipping: **p=0.049** (borderline significant)
- Bruxism → more minor complications in mandible: **p=0.015**
- All major technical complications and 1 late implant failure involved bruxers
- No statistically significant difference for material type or retention type vs complication rate

## 5. Limitations

- Small sample (n=24) — exploratory, underpowered for multivariable analysis
- Bruxism classified as "probable" only (no polysomnography)
- Heterogeneous follow-up (4–10yr); retrospective without standardized BOP
- Single center; generalizability limited
