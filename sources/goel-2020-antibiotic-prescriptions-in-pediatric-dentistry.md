## Why Ingested
Narrative review synthesizing antibiotic prescribing practices, indications, dosages, and adherence to guidelines in pediatric dentistry. Extends [[drug/antibiotics]] by focusing on age-specific considerations, inappropriate prescribing drivers (knowledge gaps, provider scarcity), and antimicrobial-resistance epidemiology in children harboring multidrug-resistant bacteria.

## Three-line Summary
Narrative review (2000–2019 literature) of antibiotic prescribing in pediatric dentistry, examining indications, dosages, duration, and adherence to clinical guidelines.
Global antibiotic consumption increased 65% from 2000–2015 (21.1→34.8 billion DDDs); dentists account for 7–11% of all antibiotics; ~1/3 of outpatient antibiotic prescriptions are unnecessary, with low guideline adherence among dentists.
Key gap: insufficient clinical knowledge of appropriate indications (pain relief, irreversible pulpitis, localized abscess) drives overuse and contributes to antibiotic resistance; early-life exposure creates obesity, Candida superinfection, enamel defects, and GI disturbances in children.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Comprehensive synthesis of antibiotic prescribing practices among pediatric dentists, dental students, and general dentists with global epidemiological context.
- Documented low adherence to professional clinical guidelines (wide variation in dosages and treatment durations; inconsistent with WHO/guideline recommendations).
- Identified drivers of inappropriate prescribing: insufficient clinical knowledge of indications, practitioner scarcity in rural areas (dentist–population ratio 1:150,000 in rural India vs WHO ideal 1:7,500), non-dentist providers (GPs, nurses) defaulting to antibiotics for acute dental problems.
- Quantified antimicrobial-resistance epidemiology: multidrug-resistant bacteria found in children as young as 4 years; methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) as exemplar of extensive resistance.
- Linked early-life antibiotic exposure to adverse outcomes: dysbiosis (microbiota alteration, obesity), Candida superinfection, enamel defects (early exposure to amoxicillin linked to permanent first molar & maxillary central incisor defects), photosensitivity, gastrointestinal disturbances (diarrhea more frequent with TID vs BID regimens).

- **Design**: Narrative literature review
- **Databases**: MEDLINE/PubMed, EBSCO host, Google Scholar; supplemented by manual textbook review
- **Search Strategy**: MeSH terms — "Antibacterial agents", "Antibacterial agents AND Pediatric Dentistry"
- **Time Period**: 2000–2019
- **Key Data**: Opinion-based synthesis from experienced researchers/clinicians; epidemiological statistics (e.g., Klein et al. 2018 global antibiotic consumption trends; English dental-prescription prevalence estimates)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Global Antibiotic Consumption Trends
- **2000–2015 increase**: 65% rise in global consumption (21.1 → 34.8 billion defined daily doses, DDDs)
- **Projected 2030**: if current annual growth continues, consumption estimated to rise 202% to 128 billion DDDs; consumption rate +161% to 41.1 DDDs per 1,000 inhabitants per day
- **Leading LMIC consumers**: India (3.2 → 6.5 billion DDDs, +103%), China (2.3 → 4.2 billion DDDs, +79%), Pakistan (0.8 → 1.3 billion DDDs, +65%)
- **Dental contribution**: 7–11% of all antibiotics prescribed by dentists

### Inappropriate Prescribing Rates & Guideline Adherence
- Approximately 1/3 of all outpatient antibiotic prescriptions are unnecessary
- **England prevalence**: 66.4% of all dental prescriptions are antibacterial drugs
- **Survey findings**: Low overall adherence to professional clinical guidelines among dental students, dentists, and pediatric dentists
- Wide variation in dosages & treatment durations inconsistent with guideline recommendations

### Clinical Indications Where Antibiotics Are Unnecessary
- Irreversible pulpitis with no systemic signs/symptoms
- Necrotic pulps with localized acute apical abscess
- Localized dentoalveolar abscess without fever/pyrexia
- **Rationale**: Lack of blood circulation to root canal prevents antibiotics reaching the infection site; ineffective for microorganism elimination. Pain relief requires analgesics/NSAIDs, not antibiotics. Edema requires anti-inflammatory drugs; purulence resolved by drainage/debridement.

### Appropriate Indications for Antibiotic Use
- Soft-tissue swelling of endodontic origin (alveolar abscess) WITH diffuse swelling AND pyrexia within 24 h (systemic response indicator)
- Acute facial cellulitis / acute apical abscess with spreading to interstitial/tissue spaces
- Contaminated oral/intraoral wounds with extrinsic bacteria, debris, foreign bodies
- Open fractures and joint injuries

### Antimicrobial Resistance Epidemiology
- Children as young as 4 years found to harbor multidrug-resistant bacteria in oral cavities
- MRSA widely known exemplar of extensive resistance (resistance to full range of available antibiotics)

### Adverse Effects of Inappropriate Pediatric Antibiotic Exposure
- **Metabolic**: Risk of developing type 2 diabetes in children due to sugar-containing liquid medications
- **Allergic/Respiratory**: Risk of allergy and asthma development in antibiotics-treated children
- **Microbiota dysbiosis**: Early-life antibiotic exposure alters intestinal microbiota → adverse long-term effects
  - Obesity
  - Candida superinfection
  - Photosensitivity
- **Developmental enamel defects**: Early amoxicillin exposure linked to permanent first molars & maxillary central incisor defects
- **GI disturbances**: Diarrhea more frequent with three-times-daily than twice-daily regimens

### The "4 D's" of Antimicrobial Therapy (Joseph & Rodvold)
1. Right **Drug**
2. Right **Dose**
3. **Deescalation** to pathogen-directed therapy
4. Right **Duration** of therapy

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Review period ends 2019; does not capture post-2019 antibiotic-stewardship evolution or newer guidelines
- Synthesizes predominantly opinion-based recommendations rather than meta-analyzed evidence (narrative review methodology)
- No formal risk-of-bias assessment of included literature (not a systematic review)
- Focuses on therapeutic (treatment) indications; prophylaxis protocols for high-risk patients (cardiovascular disease, immunosuppression) not deeply explored
- Calls for heightened clinician education on guideline adherence but does not evaluate implementation strategies or knowledge-translation interventions
- Epidemiological data heavily skewed toward India/South Asia; generalizability to other regions (North America, Western Europe) limited by source imbalance

## 7. Glossary
- **AMX/CLV**: Amoxicillin–clavulanic acid combination
- **Antibiotic resistance (AR)**: Microbial ability to survive exposure to antimicrobial agents; multidrug-resistant bacteria resistant to ≥3 antibiotic classes
- **Antibiotic stewardship**: Clinical practice ensuring appropriate antibiotic selection, dosing, duration, and de-escalation to reduce overuse and resistance emergence
- **Dentoalveolar abscess (DAA)**: Purulent infection of dental origin localized to alveolar bone and surrounding tissues; acute (localized, no systemic signs) vs diffuse (spreading, systemic involvement)
- **DDD (Defined Daily Dose)**: WHO standard unit for measuring antibiotic consumption; assumes average maintenance dose for the drug in its major indication for a typical adult
- **Irreversible pulpitis**: Inflammatory state of pulp tissue with no recovery potential; bacteria colonize the pulp; root canal treatment indicated
- **LMIC (Low- and Middle-Income Country)**: UN classification of countries by economic development; antibiotic consumption concentrated in LMICs
- **MRSA (Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus)**: Gram-positive bacterium resistant to β-lactam antibiotics; exemplar of extensive multidrug resistance
- **Necrotic pulp**: Clinically non-vital pulp tissue (no response to sensibility tests); colonized by gram-negative anaerobes; root canal treatment required
- **Orofacial infection**: Bacterial/viral infection affecting oral tissues, face, or surrounding structures (periodontitis, abscess, cellulitis, herpes)
- **Periapical abscess (apical abscess)**: Localized purulent infection at the apical region of a non-vital tooth; acute form (24–72 h, no systemic signs) vs chronic (granuloma/cyst)
- **Pyrexia (Fever)**: Body temperature elevation ≥38.0°C; indicator of systemic inflammatory/immune response to infection
- **Systemic manifestations**: Constitutional signs/symptoms indicating whole-body infection response (fever, malaise, lymphadenopathy, facial swelling, asymmetry)
