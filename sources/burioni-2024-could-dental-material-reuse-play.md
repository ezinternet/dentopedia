## Why Ingested

Part of the healing-abutment (HA) reuse controversy batch. [[infection-control/bidra-2019-should-healing-abutments-cover-screws]] (anchor SR) concluded routine reprocessing does not reliably return HAs to a virgin surface and cautioned against indiscriminate reuse; this in vitro study (Burioni 2024, full text) provides the quantitative counterpart — sterilization eradicates all viable bacteria (100% of reused HAs culture-negative) yet leaves measurably more residual surface protein on reused than new HAs (18.76 vs 9.35 µg/mL, mean diff 9.41 µg/mL, 95% CI 4.28–14.54, p<0.001). It reinforces the "sterile ≠ clean" caveat that underpins the anchor SR's caution.

## One-line Summary
In vitro study (TG=40 reused+sterilized vs CG=35 new Winsix titanium healing abutments): a decontamination+autoclave protocol achieved 100% sterility (no bacterial growth) but left significantly more residual surface protein on reused HAs (18.76 vs 9.35 µg/mL, mean diff 9.41, 95% CI 4.28–14.54, t(30)=4.52, p<0.001; ~12 µg protein/screw); clinical significance of the difference undetermined.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Could Dental Material Reuse Play a Significant Role in Preservation of Raw Materials, Water, Energy, and Costs? Microbiological Analysis of New versus Reused Healing Abutments: An In Vitro Study
- **Authors**: Burioni R, Silvestrini L, D'Orto B, Tetè G, Nagni M, Polizzi E, Gherlone EF
- **Journal**: Bioengineering (Basel) 2024;11(4):387
- **DOI**: 10.3390/bioengineering11040387 | **PMID**: 38671808 | **PMCID**: PMC11048622 (open-access full text)
- **Setting**: IRCCS San Raffaele Hospital, Milan, Italy (Sept 2022–Oct 2023)
- **Design**: In vitro comparative study; STROBE cohort reporting guideline
- **Study period question**: Does a decontamination + sterilization protocol render reused healing abutments as safe (sterile and clean) as new ones?

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **Decouples sterility from cleanliness.** Demonstrates that a reprocessing protocol can be 100% effective at eliminating viable bacteria while simultaneously failing to remove adherent surface proteins — the two endpoints are independent.
2. **Quantifies residual protein burden.** Provides Micro BCA (OD 562 nm) surface-protein measurements for reused vs new HAs, with effect size and 95% CI, on real patient-used screws.
3. **Frames reuse within sustainability.** Situates the reuse question against raw-material/water/energy/cost preservation and WHO 2030 sustainability goals, while showing that the microbiological/protein data do not yet justify routine reuse.
4. **Consolidates the "sterile ≠ clean" literature.** Aligns with Wadhwani (99% of sterilized HAs protein-contaminated), Stacchi, Sánchez-Garcés, Cakan, and Sennerby (in vivo rat macrophage response to used Ti) into a coherent caution.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Specimens**: Winsix healing abutment screws (Biosafin, Cefla, Imola, Italy). Test group (TG) = 40 abutments previously used in patients (one HA per patient, retrieved at implant reopening — 4–5 mo maxillary, 3 mo mandibular bone healing). Control group (CG) = 35 new abutments.
- **Reprocessing protocol**: all TG (and handling of CG) subjected to decontamination + steam sterilization.
- **Sterility assay**: extraction under laminar flow → deposit in Tryptone Soya Broth → incubate 24 h at 37 °C → turbidity read as bacterial growth.
- **Surface-protein assay**: Micro BCA protein assay, spectrophotometry at OD 562 nm; eluate volume 500 µL per screw.
- **Statistics**: SPSS v20; Shapiro-Wilk normality; one-way ANOVA + independent-samples t-test; Tukey adjustment; α = 0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Sterility**: NO turbidity in any sample (TG or CG) — protocol effective at eliminating infective agents/bacteria; reused HAs were culture-negative.
- **Residual surface protein (primary difference)**:
  - Reused (TG): **18.76 µg/mL** (SD 3.62)
  - New (CG): **9.35 µg/mL** (SD 2.15)
  - **Mean difference 9.41 µg/mL (95% CI 4.28–14.54)**; independent t-test **t(30)=4.52, p<0.001**
  - One-way ANOVA **F(2,45)=7.34, p<0.01**
- **Per-screw protein load**: average eluate protein in TG ≈ 23.88 µg/mL; with 500 µL solution → **~12 µg protein per screw**.
- **Interpretation ceiling**: authors state the **clinical significance of the new-vs-reused protein difference cannot be determined a priori** — residual denatured protein is a theoretical immune/inflammatory trigger but the threshold at which it matters clinically is unknown.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **In vitro only** — no clinical outcomes (peri-implant inflammation, marginal bone loss, implant survival) measured.
- **Single manufacturer / single HA system** (Winsix) — generalizability to other geometries/alloys unproven.
- **Protein presence ≠ harm** — no dose-response link between residual µg protein and adverse tissue response established; clinical significance undetermined.
- **Thread wear / biomechanical damage** from repeated cycles (a stated "disposable" rationale) was not assessed here.
- Authors call for study of alternative sterilization procedures and of the biomechanical implications of reuse.

## 6. Related Work

- **Bidra et al. (SR)**: 6 studies — 3 found routine methods insufficient, 2 achieved complete removal only with an additional regimen, 1 found routine sufficient. Anchor SR of this batch.
- **Wadhwani et al.**: 100 HAs from 8 clinics — 99% still protein/peptide-contaminated after sterilization → transmission concern.
- **Stacchi et al.**: 30 test HAs (mechanical+ultrasonic+autoclave) → 11 contaminated; all control HAs contaminated.
- **Sánchez-Garcés et al.**: 55 used+sterilized HAs — no bacterial growth but significant residual debris (total organic carbon). Direct methodological analogue.
- **Sennerby et al. (in vivo rat)**: used Ti screws → increased activated macrophages + fibrous encapsulation vs new Ti — biological rationale for the protein concern.
- **Cakan et al.**: 60 used+sterilized HAs — contaminated screw grooves in 10.5%, debris-filled driver slots in 5.2%.
- **Concordant 120-HA study**: autoclave alone vs +airflow polishing vs +NaOCl — minimal residue only with NaOCl, showing decontamination is achievable with specific adjuncts.

## 7. Glossary

- **Healing abutment (HA)**: transmucosal titanium screw placed after implant uncovering to shape the peri-implant soft tissue; manufacturer-labelled single-use (disposable).
- **Micro BCA assay**: bicinchoninic acid colorimetric protein quantification; absorbance read at OD 562 nm, proportional to total protein.
- **Sterility vs cleanliness**: sterility = absence of viable microorganisms; cleanliness = absence of residual organic matter (proteins, debris). Sterilization can achieve the former without the latter.
- **Residual surface protein**: denatured proteins/peptides from blood, saliva, plaque biofilm, and gingival epithelium adherent to titanium after use; a theoretical immune/inflammatory stimulus.
- **STROBE**: reporting guideline for observational/cohort studies.
- **TG / CG**: test group (reused) / control group (new).
