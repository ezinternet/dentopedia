## Why Ingested

Isolates toothbrush wear as a single variable (new vs. naturally 3-month-worn, same brush model, crossover design) with a concrete magnitude (15–21% greater plaque reduction with new brushes, strongest at proximal/marginal sites) — the wiki's first primary-study anchor for the standard "replace every 3 months" advice, complementing [[overviews/toothbrush-design-dentifrice-plaque-index-methodology-synthesis]].

## Three-line Summary

This single-center, single-examiner blind, randomized, crossover controlled clinical trial in 40 healthy university students (mean age 25.1 years) compared the single-use plaque removal efficacy of new versus 3-month-old naturally worn manual toothbrushes using the Patient Hygiene Performance (PHP) index.

New toothbrushes achieved significantly greater plaque reduction than 3-month-old brushes for whole-mouth sites (58.0% vs 42.9%, p=0.0001), proximal sites (45.2% vs 24.6%, p=0.0001), and marginal sites (54.0% vs 37.8%, p=0.0001), representing 15.1%, 20.6%, and 16.3% greater mean reductions respectively.

Worn toothbrushes are significantly less effective for plaque removal, particularly in proximal and marginal areas, supporting the recommendation that toothbrushes be replaced every 3 months to maintain optimal plaque control.

## 세줄요약

새 칫솔은 전악 구강 (58.0% vs 42.9%, p=0.0001), 인접면 (45.2% vs 24.6%, p=0.0001), 치은연변연부위 (54.0% vs 37.8%, p=0.0001)에서 3개월 사용 칫솔보다 각각 15.1%, 20.6%, 16.3% 더 높은 치면세균막 감소율을 보였다.

마모된 칫솔은 특히 인접면과 치은연변연 부위에서 치면세균막 제거효율이 유의하게 낮아, 최적의 치면세균막 관리를 위해 3개월마다 칫솔을 교체해야 한다는 권고를 지지한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Demonstrated that 3-month-old worn toothbrushes are significantly less effective than new toothbrushes for plaque removal in whole-mouth, proximal, and marginal sites.
- New brushes showed 15.1% greater whole-mouth plaque reduction compared to worn brushes (58.0% vs 42.9%, p=0.0001).
- Proximal site plaque reduction was 20.6% greater with new brushes versus worn brushes (45.2% vs 24.6%, p=0.0001).
- Marginal site plaque reduction was 16.3% greater with new brushes versus worn brushes (54.0% vs 37.8%, p=0.0001).
- No significant difference between brush types was found for non-proximal (p=0.071) and non-marginal sites (p=0.317).
- Linear correlations between progressive toothbrush wear and plaque score reduction were observed (r=-0.58 for whole-sites, r=-0.50 for marginal sites) but were not statistically significant.
- Brushing surface area of 3-month-old brushes increased by a mean of 50.6% (±27.8%) compared to new brushes, with a wide range of 0.2% to 112.1%.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: Single-center, single-examiner blind, randomized, crossover controlled clinical trial. Two experimental periods separated by a 2-week washout period.
- Subjects: 42 healthy subjects recruited (27 males, 13 females, mean age 25.1 years, range 23-37); 40 completed the study. All were undergraduate students at Seoul National University's Dental College.
- Inclusion criteria: Presence of all 6 Ramfjord teeth (16, 21, 24, 36, 41, 44), pocket depth ≤3mm on all tooth surfaces, no crowns/prostheses/orthodontic appliances, no restorations involving study tooth surfaces, healthy with no medical conditions requiring prophylactic antibiotics.
- Randomization: Subjects randomly assigned to two groups: Group I (New/Worn sequence, n=19) and Group II (Worn/New sequence, n=21).
- Blinding: Single examiner calibrated and blinded to toothbrush type used in both groups.
- Intervention: Subjects used the same toothbrush for 3 months to create naturally worn brushes. At experimental visits, subjects brushed with either new or 3-month-old brush using their familiar brushing technique for a single use. Brushing duration was not standardized; subjects used their habitual duration.
- Plaque assessment: Patient Hygiene Performance (PHP) index. Plaque disclosed with erythrosin solution (RED-COTE®, Butler, Chicago, USA) for 1 min. Six Ramfjord teeth scored (facial and lingual surfaces divided into 5 sections: mesial, distal, middle third subdivided into gingival, middle, occlusal). Sites evaluated: whole, proximal (mesial+distal), marginal (proximal+gingival), non-proximal, non-marginal.
- Toothbrush wear quantification: Brushing surface area measured using standardized digital photography and image analysis software (TDI Scope Eye®, Techsan Co., Ltd., Seoul, Korea). Increase in brushing surface area of 3-month-old brushes calculated compared to new brushes.
- Statistics: Paired t-tests for within-group and between-group comparisons. Pearson's correlation analysis for plaque scores and toothbrush wear. Two-sided p<0.05 considered statistically significant.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Primary outcome — Whole-mouth plaque reduction (%):** New brush: 57.96% (mean reduction 24.48, SD 6.83); Worn brush: 42.90% (mean reduction 17.83, SD 6.81); Difference between groups: 15.1% (p=0.0001)
- **Proximal site plaque reduction (%):** New brush: 45.21% (mean reduction 10.85, SD 4.26); Worn brush: 24.69% (mean reduction 5.90, SD 4.45); Difference between groups: 20.6% (p=0.0001)
- **Marginal site plaque reduction (%):** New brush: 54.04% (mean reduction 19.40, SD 6.04); Worn brush: 37.76% (mean reduction 13.50, SD 6.42); Difference between groups: 16.3% (p=0.0001)
- **Non-proximal site plaque reduction (%):** New brush: 74.76%; Worn brush: 67.76%; Difference between groups: 7.0% (p=0.071, not significant)
- **Non-marginal site plaque reduction (%):** New brush: 80.24%; Worn brush: 74.57%; Difference between groups: 5.7% (p=0.317, not significant)
- **Toothbrush wear (increase in brushing surface area):** Mean 50.6% (±27.8%), range 0.2% to 112.1% for 3-month-old brushes (n=40)
- **Correlation analysis:** Progressive wear vs plaque reduction: r=-0.58 for whole-sites (p=0.72); r=-0.50 for marginal sites (p=0.76) (not significant)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single-use design may not reflect long-term plaque control outcomes with repeated brushing over time.
- Brushing techniques were not standardized; subjects used their familiar techniques, introducing variability in brushing efficacy.
- PHP index may not adequately weight proximal and gingival sites; a different plaque index system (e.g., Modified Navy Plaque Index, Rustogi Modified Navy Plaque Index) that gives more weight to these areas is needed for more careful evaluation.
- The correlation between wear and plaque reduction was not statistically significant, possibly due to individual variability in brushing frequency, duration, force, and method.
- Study conducted on a gingivitis model in healthy young adults; results may differ in periodontitis patients where root surface anatomy is more three-dimensional.
- Only a 3-month wear period was evaluated; other time points might show different relationships between wear and efficacy.
- The examiner was single and calibrated; however, intra-examiner reliability data were not reported.

## 6. Related Work

- Kreifeldt et al. 1980 — Systematic study showing worn toothbrushes are significantly less effective at plaque removal than new brushes.
- Glaze and Wade 1986 — Found toothbrush age and wear related to reduced plaque control effectiveness.
- Warren et al. 2002 — Clinical investigation demonstrating decreased plaque removal efficacy with increasing toothbrush wear.
- McKendrick et al. 1971 — Analysis showing toothbrush age and wear, with average replacement age reported as 2.5-6 months.
- Daly et al. 1996 — Found plaque scores actually improved as initially new brushes became worn, suggesting wear may not be critical.
- Sforza et al. 2000 — Reported increased toothbrush wear was not associated with worsening plaque scores.
- Tan E and Daly CG 2002 — Found 3-month-old brushes as effective as new brushes in plaque removal.
- Conforti et al. 2003 — Provided data supporting that worn toothbrushes are less efficient than new brushes.
- Elliott et al. 1972 — Developed Modified Navy Plaque Index giving double scores to marginal gingival area.
- Rustogi et al. 1992 — Developed Rustogi Modified Navy Plaque Index for increased scoring efficiency in gumline and interproximal areas.

## 7. Glossary

- **PHP (Patient Hygiene Performance Index)**: 구강위생수행도 지수 — 치면세균막을 평가하는 지표로, 치아 표면을 5개 구획으로 나누어 세균막 유무를 점수화
- **Ramfjord teeth (Ramfjord Teeth)**: 램퍼드 치아 — 치주 평가를 위한 기준 치아 6개 (16, 21, 24, 36, 41, 44)
- **Brushing surface area (Brushing Surface Area)**: 칫솔질 면적 — 칫솔모의 외측열이 덮는 면적으로, 칫솔 마모도를 정량화하는 지표
- **Erythrosin solution (Erythrosin Solution)**: 에리트로신 용액 — 치면세균막을 염색하여 가시화하는 착색제 (RED-COTE®)
- **Washout period (Washout Period)**: 휴약기 — 두 실험 기간 사이에 설정된 2주간의 기간으로, 이전 칫솔 사용의 잔여 효과를 제거하기 위해 설계됨
- **Proximal site (Proximal Site)**: 인접면 — 치아의 근심면과 원심면을 포함하는 부위
- **Marginal site (Marginal Site)**: 치은연변연 부위 — 인접면과 치은연 부위를 포함하는 부위
