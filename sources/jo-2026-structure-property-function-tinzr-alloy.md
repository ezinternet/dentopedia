## Why Ingested

First clinical RCT evidence for a β-type Ti-Nb-Zr (TNZ) implant alloy — the aluminum/vanadium-free alternative that the zirconia-vs-titanium material literature has been missing a clinical arm for, and a direct test of the stress-shielding argument for lower-modulus implant materials → [[implants/bosshardt-2017-osseointegration-titanium-zirconia-review]] and [[implants/khaohoen-2023-biomaterials-dental-implants-bone-density-narrative-review]].

## Three-line Summary

Bench + randomized single-blind controlled trial (J Funct Biomater 2026;17:96; Hallym University and Seoul National University Bundang Hospital; 80 enrolled, 77 analyzed at 12 months) evaluating a β-type Ti-Nb-Zr (TNZ) alloy implant against cold-worked modified Grade 4 cpTi (MG4T).

TNZ showed higher tensile strength (1,139 vs 900 MPa) and ~4-fold fatigue life (1.06M vs 0.25M cycles) with a *lower* elastic modulus (75 vs 110 GPa), while clinically both groups reached 100% success and survival, no marginal bone loss >1 mm, and no difference in soft-tissue parameters apart from a lower 12-month gingival index in TNZ.

Lower modulus did not produce the extra trabecular complexity predicted by orthopedic stress-shielding theory (fractal dimension rose less around TNZ), supporting the view that implant design and the bone–implant interface, not bulk stiffness, dominate dental bone adaptation.

## 세줄요약

벤치 + 무작위 단일맹검 대조 임상시험 (J Funct Biomater 2026;17:96, 한림대·분당서울대; 80명 등록, 77명 12개월 분석): β형 티타늄-니오븀-지르코늄 (Ti-Nb-Zr, TNZ) 합금 임플란트 vs 냉간가공 개량 4등급 상용순티타늄 (Modified Grade 4 Titanium, MG4T).

TNZ는 인장강도 (1,139 vs 900 MPa)와 피로수명 (약 106만 vs 25만 사이클)이 더 높으면서 탄성계수 (Elastic Modulus)는 오히려 낮았고 (75 vs 110 GPa), 임상적으로는 양군 모두 성공률·생존율 100%, 1 mm 초과 변연골소실 없음, 12개월 치은지수 (Gingival Index, GI)만 TNZ가 낮음.

낮은 탄성계수가 정형외과 응력차단 (Stress Shielding) 이론이 예측하는 골소주 복잡성 증가를 낳지 않았으며 (TNZ 주변 프랙탈 차원 (Fractal Dimension, FD) 증가가 오히려 작음), 치과 임플란트의 골 적응은 재료 강성보다 임플란트 디자인과 골-임플란트 계면이 지배적임을 시사.

## Key Contributions
- Characterizes a β-phase Ti-Nb-Zr alloy (Nb 40.12-41.11 wt%, Zr 7.10-7.49 wt%) with a fine, homogeneous microstructure versus the coarse cold-worked α-phase of MG4T.
- Demonstrates the two-property combination that orthopedic rationale predicts is desirable but rarely shown together in dentistry: higher tensile strength *and* higher fatigue resistance at a *lower* elastic modulus.
- Provides the first 12-month randomized clinical data for TNZ implants, with 100% success/survival and no implant-related adverse events.
- Directly tests the stress-shielding hypothesis in the mouth via fractal-dimension analysis of peri-implant trabecular bone — and finds lower, not higher, FD increase around the lower-modulus alloy.
- Argues that bulk material stiffness is secondary to implant design, surface topography and interface mechanics under cyclic non-continuous oral loading.

## Methodology
**Bench arm.** Grade 4 cpTi-based MG4T and TNZ specimens (n = 5 each) assessed by SEM/EDS for microstructure and composition, tensile testing for strength/modulus/elongation, and cyclic fatigue testing to failure. No statistical comparison was run because of the small sample.

**Clinical arm.** Randomized, single-blind, controlled, two-center trial; 80 participants randomly allocated 1:1 to TNZ (test) or MG4T (control) single implants; 77 completed 12 months per protocol (TNZ 38, MG4T 39). Exclusions: one bone graft at placement per group (protocol deviation), one salivary gland tumour diagnosis (test). Outcomes: implant success and survival, probing depth, plaque index, gingival index, bleeding on probing at 6 and 12 months, marginal bone level on periapical radiographs read by two blinded observers, fractal dimension (FD) of peri-implant trabecular bone at second-stage surgery, prosthesis delivery, 6 and 12 months, and adverse events. Non-inferiority framed with a one-sided 97.5% lower confidence interval against a predefined margin.

## Key Results

**Mechanical / surface (n = 5 per group, no statistical testing)**

| Property | MG4T | TNZ |
|---|---|---|
| Tensile strength (MPa) | 900.0 ± 95.0 | 1,139.0 ± 102.9 |
| Elastic modulus (GPa) | 110.0 ± 11.2 | 75.0 ± 9.7 |
| Elongation (%) | 25.0 ± 2.0 | 10.0 ± 1.6 |
| Fatigue life (cycles) | 254,587 ± 56,060 | 1,062,566 ± 110,015 |

EDS confirmed homogeneous Nb/Zr distribution across TNZ surfaces; SEM showed fine β-phase grains versus coarse α-phase grains in MG4T, with no surface defects or segregation in either.

**Clinical (12 months)**

| Outcome | TNZ (n=38) | MG4T (n=39) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Success / survival | 100% / 100% | 100% / 100% | non-inferior |
| Marginal bone loss >1 mm | none | none | — |
| Bleeding on probing | 0% positive | 0% positive | — |
| Pocket depth 12 mo (mm) | 2.62 ± 0.71 | 2.72 ± 0.86 | 0.58 |
| Plaque index 12 mo | 0.08 ± 0.27 | 0.21 ± 0.47 | 0.19 |
| Gingival index 12 mo | 0.00 ± 0.00 | 0.10 ± 0.31 | 0.045 |
| ISQ at placement | 71.39 ± 10.56 | 77.17 ± 6.59 | 0.012 |
| ISQ change (placement → 2nd stage) | +4.43 ± 8.93 | 0.00 ± 7.53 | 0.021 |

Baseline imbalance: sex distribution differed (TNZ 47% female vs MG4T 23%, p = 0.026); insertion torque did not differ (41.3 vs 43.7 Ncm). Both groups gained ISQ between placement and second-stage surgery, TNZ significantly more.

**Fractal dimension.** FD increased over time in both groups (physiologic remodeling, no pathological pattern), but the increase around TNZ was smaller — i.e., the lower-modulus alloy did not drive the greater trabecular complexity that a stress-shielding-driven adaptation would predict.

## Limitations
Small cohort and only two centres; 12-month follow-up only. FD was computed from 2D periapical radiographs (projection error, anatomical overlap) with no CBCT-based volumetric assessment. Bench fatigue testing used 5 specimens per group with no statistical comparison — a screening comparison, not a fatigue-limit determination. Baseline sex imbalance between groups. The implant systems differ in more than alloy composition (design/surface), so the clinical comparison cannot isolate the material effect. EDS composition table shows an inconsistent titanium fraction in one TNZ specimen (21.99%), unexplained in the text.

## Glossary
- **TNZ:** Ti-Nb-Zr — β-type titanium-niobium-zirconium alloy, Al- and V-free.
- **MG4T:** Modified Grade 4 Titanium — cold-worked cpTi used in most current implant systems.
- **β-type alloy:** Body-centred-cubic titanium phase with lower elastic modulus than α-phase cpTi.
- **Stress shielding:** Bone resorption attributed to a stiff implant carrying load that would otherwise stimulate bone.
- **FD:** Fractal Dimension — 2D radiographic index of trabecular bone complexity.
- **ISQ:** Implant Stability Quotient.
- **EDS/SEM:** Energy-Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy / Scanning Electron Microscopy.
