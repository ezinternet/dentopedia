## Why Ingested

The wiki's shaping category has no direct ex vivo test of the widely repeated clinical claim that minimally invasive endodontic (MIE) shaping (smaller apical sizes/tapers) protects against vertical root fracture (VRF) — most existing shaping pages address cyclic fatigue of NiTi files or unprepared canal surface area, not post-obturation fracture resistance. This paper directly tests that assumption with a well-powered (n=18/group, power=0.9) chewing-simulation model and finds it does NOT hold, which is a counterintuitive negative result worth flagging against the intuitive "shape less to preserve strength" heuristic. It complements [[endodontics/shaping/siqueira-2018-unprepared-root-canal-surface-areas]], which argues from the opposite direction (under-instrumented canals leave biofilm untouched) — together the two papers frame the MIE shaping tradeoff as one where minimal shaping buys neither a fracture-resistance benefit (this paper) nor a guaranteed disinfection benefit (Siqueira 2018), undercutting a purely mechanical rationale for minimal shaping.

## Three-line Summary

Ex vivo study (n=18/group, 6 groups) on extracted human maxillary central incisors found that minimally invasive endodontic (MIE) canal shaping (#40/.04) did not significantly reduce vertical root fracture (VRF) incidence or crack formation compared to conventional enlargement (ISO #80) after chewing simulation, regardless of sealer type (cement-based vs.

adhesive resin-based);

VRF resistance was in fact significantly lower in the +MIE/cement-sealer group than in untreated controls (p=0.020).

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Ex vivo investigation on the effect of minimally invasive endodontic treatment on vertical root fracture resistance and crack formation
- **Authors**: Rathke A, Frehse H, Bechtold M
- **Journal**: Scientific Reports, 2024 Jun 8;14(1):13205
- **DOI**: 10.1038/s41598-024-63396-y
- **PMID**: 38851745 / **PMCID**: PMC11162497
- **Study type**: Ex vivo laboratory study (extracted human teeth), 6 groups, n=18/group (power=0.9), prospective sample-size calculation
- **Institution context**: University of Ulm, Germany (ethics committee waiver applicable per German regulations for anonymized extracted teeth)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Directly tests, with adequate statistical power, the common clinical assumption that MIE shaping (smaller apical file size/taper) reduces VRF risk versus conventional/more invasive shaping — a question prior systematic reviews of in vitro data found inconclusive.
- Uses a clinically realistic fatigue protocol (chewing simulation, staircase loading 25–150 N over 120,000 cycles with thermocycling) rather than single-load-to-failure testing alone, which prior authors note gives better clinical insight than static compressive loading.
- Factorially varies both shaping extent (+MIE #40/.04 vs. −MIE ISO #80) and obturation material (non-adhesive calcium hydroxide-based Sealapex vs. adhesive resin-based RealSeal SE), plus unfilled/untreated controls, isolating the radicular (root, not coronal/access-cavity) contribution to fracture resistance.
- Reports a counterintuitive negative/harmful finding: minimal shaping did not protect against VRF, and one minimally-shaped group (+MIE with cement sealer) had significantly lower fracture resistance than untreated teeth.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

**Sample**: Extracted human permanent maxillary central incisors with single, straight canals and complete root formation, decoronated to a standardized 13 mm root length; excluded for caries, restorations, prior root fillings, resorption, or pre-existing dentin defects (12x stereomicroscope screening). Root cross-sectional area measured and used for stratified randomization into 6 groups of n=18 (no significant baseline cross-sectional area differences, p>0.05 one-way ANOVA). Roots wrapped in ~250 µm latex (periodontal ligament simulation) and embedded in acrylic resin with cervical third exposed.

**Groups** (2 control + 4 experimental):
- Negative control: untreated (no endodontic treatment)
- Positive control: shaped to −MIE (ISO #80) but left unfilled
- +MIE/C: shaped to #40/.04 taper (NiTi rotary, Twisted File, single-length technique #25→#30→#35 @0.06 taper, then to #40 @0.04), filled with calcium hydroxide-based Sealapex sealer, single-cone technique
- +MIE/A: same shaping, filled with adhesive resin-based RealSeal SE (RS) sealer, single-cone
- −MIE/C: shaped to +MIE first then enlarged to Twisted File #50/.04 + hand K-files to ISO #80, filled with Sealapex
- −MIE/A: same enlarged shaping, filled with RealSeal SE

Irrigation: 5 mL 3% NaOCl + 15% EDTA smear-layer removal, 5 mL distilled water flush, paper-point drying in all instrumented groups. Canal orifices sealed with temporary Cavit; 1 mm of canal space left unfilled at the coronal end so mechanical loading transmitted force to root dentin rather than the filling material.

**Aging/fatigue protocol**: 24 h water storage (37°C) → 1500 thermocycles (5–55°C) → chewing simulation via staircase method: starting load 25 N at 10° to root axis, increased by 25 N every 20,000 cycles (2 Hz) up to 120,000 cycles (i.e., load escalates toward 150 N over the full cycle count).

**Outcome assessment**: Pre-testing failures (PTFs) recorded and excluded from further analysis (7 of 108 total teeth). Surviving roots examined under stereomicroscope (12–100x) for crack/defect classification per root third (cervical/middle/apical): (a) no defect, (b) craze line, (c) vertical crack, (d) horizontal crack — up to 9 possible defect codings per root. Roots then loaded to fracture (1 mm/min crosshead speed) on a universal testing machine using the same conical antagonist as the chewing simulator; fracture load (N) recorded at 30% force drop.

**Statistics**: Non-normal data (Shapiro–Wilk p=0.002 for VRF resistance, p=0.0001 for crack formation) → Kruskal–Wallis omnibus test, followed by Mann–Whitney U post-hoc with Bonferroni correction for 15 pairwise comparisons; alpha=0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Pre-test failures**: 7/108 teeth fractured during chewing simulation before formal testing (2 negative control, 2 +MIE/A, 2 −MIE/A, 1 +MIE/C) — excluded from analysis.
- **Crack formation (dentin defects)**: Kruskal–Wallis found a significant omnibus difference across groups (p=0.006), but post-hoc pairwise comparisons showed NO statistically significant between-group differences (all p≥0.077). +MIE/A had the fewest defects (median 1, IQR 0–2); the positive control (shaped but unfilled) had the most (median 3, IQR 2–4).
- **Defect location**: 31.7% of defects in the apical third, 63.4% in the cervical third, 73.3% in the middle third (defects could be recorded in multiple thirds per root).
- **Defect type**: 56.3% vertical root cracks, 23.6% horizontal root cracks, 20.1% craze lines.
- **VRF resistance**: Significant omnibus difference (p=0.008). Post-hoc: untreated (negative control) teeth had significantly HIGHER fracture resistance than the +MIE/C group (p=0.020). No other pairwise comparison reached significance (all p≥0.068). +MIE/C had the lowest median resistance (972 N, IQR 868–1185); −MIE/A had the highest (1397 N, IQR 1057–1540) among experimental groups.
- **Overall conclusion**: The null hypothesis (no difference in VRF resistance/crack formation between minimally and more invasively shaped, or between obturation materials) could NOT be rejected as a general matter — but where a significant difference did emerge, it went against MIE (lower resistance vs. untreated controls), not in its favor. Minimal shaping did not confer a fracture-resistance benefit.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single tooth type (maxillary central incisors only, straight single canals) — results may not generalize to other tooth types/canal morphologies (e.g., oval canals, multi-rooted teeth).
- Roots tested decoronated, without access cavity or coronal restoration — isolates radicular effects but excludes cuspal deflection and coronal (adhesive) reinforcement effects known to matter clinically.
- Sealers chosen (Sealapex, non-adhesive/high-solubility calcium hydroxide-based; RealSeal SE, an older self-adhesive resin sealer with known intracanal bonding limitations) are not fully representative of contemporary sealer chemistries (e.g., bioceramic sealers) — authors explicitly call for future studies using more modern sealer materials.
- Staircase fatigue loading escalates over a limited number of cycles (120,000) versus much higher cycle counts under real functional chewing loads over years; in vivo biological/loading complexity not fully replicable ex vivo.
- Extracted-tooth fatigue is influenced by uncontrolled donor factors (age, dentin microstructure, storage history) beyond the study's control.
- 7 pre-test failures were excluded rather than assigned a zero fracture-strength value, a methodological choice that differs from some other groups' conventions and could affect between-study comparability.

## 6. Related Work

- Contrasts with the frequently cited claim (attributed to several prior authors in the Introduction) that MIE treatment — small access cavity, minimal canal shaping, adhesive resin-based approaches — increases fracture resistance of root-filled teeth; this study's ex vivo radicular-only data do not support that claim for canal shaping specifically.
- Consistent with prior findings that access-cavity size (as opposed to canal shaping) makes too small a difference in fracture resistance to be clinically relevant when adhesive coronal restoration is used.
- Cites conflicting finite-element (FEM) literature: some FEM studies show higher radicular stress with larger canal preparation (supporting MIE), while others (combined experimental+FEM) show fracture load of root dentin sections increasing with larger canal diameter (opposing MIE) — the field's mechanistic literature is itself unsettled.
- Related in the wiki's endodontics/shaping category (conceptual counterpoint, not a direct prior study on the same question): [[endodontics/shaping/siqueira-2018-unprepared-root-canal-surface-areas]] on residual unprepared canal wall/biofilm risk with conservative shaping.

## 7. Glossary

- **MIE (Minimally Invasive Endodontic treatment)**: Conservative canal shaping approach using smaller apical file sizes and tapers (here #40/.04) intended to preserve more root dentin.
- **VRF (Vertical Root Fracture)**: A longitudinally oriented fracture of the root, a major cause of extraction in root-filled teeth (reported prevalence 3–32%).
- **PTF (Pre-Testing Failure)**: A sample that fractured during the fatigue/chewing-simulation phase, before the deliberate load-to-failure test.
- **Staircase method**: A fatigue-loading protocol where applied force is incrementally increased at fixed cycle intervals until failure or a target cycle count is reached.
- **Craze line**: A superficial incomplete crack in dentin, distinguished from a full vertical or horizontal crack.
- **Single-cone technique**: An obturation method filling the canal with one master cone (GP or resin) and sealer, without additional compaction (e.g., lateral/vertical condensation).
