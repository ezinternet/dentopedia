## One-line Summary

Comprehensive review (Theranostics 2024) of salivary diagnostics covering salivary biomarker classes (proteins/peptides, nucleic acids, extracellular vesicles, metabolites, hormones, electrolytes), state-of-the-art detection systems (biosensors, microfluidics, wearable intraoral devices), and the collection/standardization challenges that limit clinical translation.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Organizes salivary biomarkers into classes and maps each to diseases: **proteins/peptides** (MMPs, PSA, CRP, TNF-α, enzymes), **nucleic acids** (viral RNA, ctDNA, miRNA, methylation, BRCA), **extracellular vesicles**, **metabolites**, **hormones**, **electrolytes**.
- Surveys **detection systems** from lab methods to **biosensors, microfluidics, and wearable intraoral devices** — trend toward higher sensitivity, faster response, smaller/portable, automated point-of-care.
- Frames the field's central obstacle: **pre-treatment stability and standardization** — many salivary biomarkers lack reference ranges and individual variability confounds comparison across labs.
- Positions saliva as a safer alternative to blood/urine that blocks needlestick/pathogen (HIV, hepatitis) transmission risk and improves compliance in pain-/blood-sensitive patients.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative comprehensive review structured around three axes: (1) saliva components and biomarkers, (2) cutting-edge detection techniques, (3) sample pre/post-processing and standardization. Saliva is ~99% water, 0.5% organic/inorganic material; salivary K⁺ concentration exceeds plasma while Na⁺ is lower; its top 20 proteins comprise only ~40% of total protein (vs plasma where 22 proteins dominate 99%), giving a more diverse, complex proteome.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **MMP-1** cutoff ~199 pg/mL as an oral-cancer biomarker; MMP-1/-2/-10/-12 elevated in oral cancer.
- **Salivary MMP-9** higher in OSCC/OPMD than tobacco users/controls; decreased **588.15 → 131.80 ng/mL** by 9 months after tumor surgery (p<0.05) — diagnostic + prognostic.
- Electrochemical biosensor detected **salivary PSA at 40 fg/mL** sensitivity.
- **Salivary RNase** activity significantly elevated in breast cancer vs benign/controls; **lactate dehydrogenase** elevated in oral cancer.
- **Salivary ctDNA / HPV ctDNA** detects and monitors HPV-related HNSCC; salivary miRNA/exosomal miRNA distinguish OSCC.
- **BRCA1/BRCA2** mutations and BRCA1 methylation detectable in saliva (breast/ovarian cancer).
- Alzheimer's: elevated salivary **S100A8, S100A9, cystatin B** variants.
- Multiple **FDA EUA** saliva/oral-fluid SARS-CoV-2 tests authorized.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Most salivary biomarkers are at **lower concentration than blood**, requiring highly sensitive assays and normalization to stable salivary components.
- **No standardized reference ranges** for many biomarkers; collection variability (time of day, swab position, flow rate, stimulation) undermines reproducibility.
- EV extraction hampered by high salivary protein/viscosity.
- Solutions proposed: multiplexed assays, standardized collection (e.g. Salimetrics protocols — avoid oral stimulants), reference intervals, round-robin inter-lab trials.

## 7. Glossary

- **Point-of-care testing (POCT)** — diagnostic testing at or near the patient rather than a central lab.
- **MMP** — matrix metalloproteinase; degrades extracellular matrix; MMP-9 a diagnostic/prognostic OSCC marker.
- **PSA** — prostate-specific antigen; detectable in saliva, correlates with serum in prostate adenocarcinoma.
- **Extracellular vesicle (EV)** — nano-sized cell-derived vesicle carrying RNA/DNA/protein cargo.
- **Wearable intraoral device** — sensor mounted in the oral cavity for continuous salivary analyte monitoring.
- **Standardization** — unified collection/processing/QC to make results reproducible and comparable across labs.
