## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42022327645; PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane, Embase, 2013 to June 2023): 765 records screened, 10 observational studies (6 case-control, 3 cohort, 1 retrospective) with 272 MRONJ cases, mean age about 66 years, asking whether medication-induced salivary changes relate to MRONJ.

Reduced salivary flow was significantly associated with MRONJ in 3 of the 4 studies that measured flow (Liao p=0.017, Kuo p=0.0024, Stockmann p=0.039; Margaix-Muñoz resting and stimulated saliva p>0.05), and single studies reported higher salivary IL-6, IL-1 family cytokines, MMP9, hypotaurine and RBMS3 in MRONJ; meta-analysis was not possible because of heterogeneity.

Evidence is hypothesis-generating only: small heterogeneous case-control and cohort studies, no standardized saliva sampling, chemotherapy imbalance between groups, and no validated salivary biomarker; the authors recommend saliva sampling before and after MRONJ onset.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic approach (per the authors) to the link between medication-related salivary changes and MRONJ.
- Concludes that reduced salivary flow and altered salivary protein concentrations were associated with MRONJ, with an explicit caution that evidence is limited.
- Proposes that polypharmacy, Sjögren's syndrome and diabetes may act through hyposalivation as modifiers of MRONJ risk (discussion-level, not tested).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- PICO: cancer and osteoporosis patients; medications inducing salivary changes; no comparator; outcome MRONJ.
- Databases: PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane, Embase (January 2013 to June 2023), no language restriction; grey literature (ProQuest, OpenGrey, Google Scholar) and cross-reference search.
- Exclusions: animal studies, case reports, systematic reviews, abstracts, letters, editorials, surveys; prior radiotherapy, jaw metastasis, or head and neck cancer surgery affecting salivary flow.
- Two independent reviewers, third expert for disagreement, Cohen's kappa for agreement; risk of bias with modified Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (max 9 stars).
- Qualitative synthesis only; meta-analysis not possible owing to heterogeneous methods, agents and outcomes.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 765 records; 10 studies included; 272 MRONJ cases (35% women, 32% men, 32% gender not reported); mean age 65.6 years (range 33-81).
- Primary disease: breast cancer 57 (20.95%), multiple myeloma 55 (20.22%), prostate cancer 19 (6.98%), renal cancer 8 (2.94%), other cancer 58 (21.32%), osteoporosis 19 (6.98%). Comorbidity: hypertension 107 (37.5%), diabetes mellitus 30 (11.02%).
- Medication counts in Table 1: bisphosphonate 530 (85.76%), denosumab 4 (0.65%), corticosteroids 11, chemotherapy 11, others 14. Most common salivary change: xerostomia due to Sjögren's syndrome (n=35).
- Designs: case-control 6, cohort 3, retrospective 1.

- Discussion-level statements (not pooled results): polypharmacy of five or more drugs had the highest prevalence of xerostomia and MRONJ (71%); in case-control studies xerostomia and MRONJ occurrence were nearly three times higher than with no medication; Sjögren's syndrome doubles caries and early tooth loss prevalence; Bagan et al. reported an odds ratio of 1.01 for MRONJ with high salivary IL-6.
- Risk of bias: modified NOS, overall good (78%) and fair (22%) quality; the text also states one study was rated poor (internally inconsistent in the paper); outcome assessment showed significant shortcomings.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Few studies, mostly small samples; heterogeneity in design, drugs, primary disease and outcome methods; no standardized saliva collection or biomarker panels, so no direct comparison.
- MRONJ patients more often received chemotherapy at saliva collection than non-MRONJ patients, a confounder that may partly explain differences.
- Case-control designs cannot establish whether salivary change precedes MRONJ; the authors recommend sampling before and after onset, and longitudinal large-sample studies to define biomarker sensitivity and specificity.
- The paper itself reports bisphosphonate medication counts (530) exceeding the 272 MRONJ cases in the same table, and a Margaix-Muñoz flow result of p>0.05 that is included in the "reduced flow" narrative; readers should treat pooled-sounding statements cautiously.

## 7. Glossary

- MRONJ: exposed necrotic bone or bone probeable through a fistula in the maxillofacial region persisting more than 8 weeks, without radiotherapy or jaw metastasis history.
- Hyposalivation / xerostomia: reduced salivary flow / subjective dry mouth.
- Sjögren's syndrome (SS): autoimmune exocrine gland disease causing dry mouth.
- Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS): star-based risk-of-bias tool for observational studies.
- RBMS3: RNA binding motif single-stranded interacting protein 3, gene associated with MRONJ in one study.
