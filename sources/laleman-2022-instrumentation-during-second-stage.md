## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional survey of 2008 European Federation of Periodontology (EFP) members (general dental practitioners, periodontists, dental hygienists) on instrumentation practices during the second stage (subgingival instrumentation) of periodontal therapy.

94.4% of respondents combined hand and mechanical instruments; Gracey curettes were the most popular hand instrument and ultrasonic scalers the preferred mechanical device (standard+micro/slim inserts combined in 42.4%); instrument wear was systematically checked by only 46.1% (hand instruments) and 41.3% (inserts) of respondents.

More experienced clinicians checked instrument/insert wear more frequently; abstract-only ingest limits methodological detail (sampling, response rate, statistical analysis not available).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Documents that combined hand + mechanical instrumentation (94.4%), not either modality alone, is the dominant real-world practice pattern during subgingival instrumentation among European periodontal professionals.
- Identifies Gracey curettes and ultrasonic scalers as the most popular hand and mechanical devices respectively, with mixed standard/micro-slim ultrasonic insert use (42.4%) more common than either insert type alone.
- Surfaces a modifiable quality-control gap: systematic instrument/insert wear-checking is done by fewer than half of respondents, and improves with clinician experience.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: cross-sectional questionnaire survey.
- Population: 2008 respondents actively involved in periodontal therapy — general dental practitioners, periodontists, and dental hygienists — members of the European Federation of Periodontology (EFP).
- Scope: frequency of use, instrument type/choice, and maintenance (wear-checking) practices during the second stage of periodontal therapy.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- 94.4% of respondents combined hand and mechanical instruments during second-stage periodontal therapy; frequency of use of hand vs mechanical instruments was similar overall.
- Most popular hand instrument: Gracey curettes.
- Preferred mechanical device: ultrasonic scalers.
- Ultrasonic insert choice: combination of standard + micro/slim inserts 42.4%; standard inserts only 32.1%; micro/slim inserts only 25.5%.
- Wear of hand instruments systematically checked by 46.1% of respondents; wear of ultrasonic inserts checked by 41.3%.
- More experienced dental professionals checked instrument/insert wear more frequently.

## 7. Glossary

- Second stage of periodontal therapy: per the EFP S3-level stepwise framework, Step 2 = subgingival instrumentation (root surface debridement/SRP) of periodontitis-affected sites, following Step 1 (behavioural change/supragingival biofilm control).
- Gracey curette: area-specific hand instrument with an offset blade angle, used for subgingival calculus/biofilm removal.
- Ultrasonic scaler: powered instrument using high-frequency tip oscillation for subgingival debridement; inserts range from standard to micro/slim tips for deeper, narrower pocket access.
- Standard vs micro/slim inserts: ultrasonic tip sizes — standard for supragingival/moderate pocket depth, micro/slim for deep, narrow periodontal pockets.
