## Why Ingested

Extends the material-science axis initiated by [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/delgado-2025-thermoplastics-clear-aligners-review]] (narrative review of thermoplastic properties) with the first SR+MA directly comparing physical, mechanical, and dimensional accuracy between thermoformed (TM) and direct-3D-printed (DTP) aligner technologies. Provides pooled quantitative estimates where available and contextualizes bench-level differences for clinical selection — bridging property descriptions to decision-relevant comparisons.

## One-line Summary

SR+MA (23 studies; 4 in meta-analysis) comparing thermoformed vs direct 3D-printed aligners: TM shows higher flexural strength/modulus/hardness; DTP shows higher shape-recovery potential, traction forces in vertical tests, geometric accuracy, and lower glass-transition temperature (Tg = 42.3 °C); no statistically significant difference in thickness variation (SMD 1.48; 95% CI −7.26 to 4.30; p = .62; I² = 97%).

## 2. Key Contributions

- First SR+MA to pool quantitative bench data comparing TM and DTP clear aligners on physical/mechanical properties and dimensional accuracy
- Establishes that TM aligners have higher initial rigidity (flexural strength, storage modulus, surface microhardness), whereas DTP aligners have higher shape-recovery potential
- Documents DTP superiority in geometric accuracy under laboratory conditions — with clinical implications for precision-demanding movements
- Quantifies the dimensional behaviour difference: TM loses ~39.76% thickness after thermoforming fabrication, while DTP gains ~11.02%; the thickness variation meta-analysis (SMD 1.48; I² = 97%) is non-significant but heavily heterogeneous
- Reports DTP's lower glass-transition temperature (Tg = 42.3 °C) as a thermomechanical risk factor for intraoral performance

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design:** Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA)
- **Eligibility:** In vitro studies comparing mechanical/physical properties or dimensional accuracy of TM vs DTP aligners
- **Total included:** 23 studies (4 contributed to meta-analysis)
- **Interventions:** Thermoformed aligners (TM, various thermoplastic sheets) vs direct 3D-printed (DTP) resin aligners
- **Outcomes:** Flexural strength, storage modulus, surface microhardness, shape recovery, traction forces (vertical loading), geometric accuracy, thickness variation, surface roughness (Sa, Sv, Sz), Tg
- **Risk of bias:** QUIN tool — 18 studies medium RoB, 4 high RoB; no low RoB studies
- **Meta-analysis statistic:** SMD with 95% CI; heterogeneity I²

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Mechanical properties (qualitative synthesis, no MA):**
- TM: higher flexural strength, higher storage modulus (stability), higher surface microhardness, greater initial rigidity
- DTP: higher shape-recovery potential (spring-back), greater traction forces in vertical test configurations

**Dimensional accuracy:**
- DTP superior geometric accuracy under laboratory conditions

**Thickness variation (meta-analysis, 4 studies):**
- TM fabrication: average thickness decrease of −39.76%
- DTP fabrication: average thickness increase of +11.02%
- Meta-analysis: SMD 1.48 (95% CI −7.26 to 4.30); p = .62; I² = 97% — **non-significant, extreme heterogeneity**

**Surface roughness:**
- DTP higher Sa (mean surface amplitude)
- TM higher Sv (valley depth)
- After clinical use: DTP maintained higher Sz (total amplitude)

**Thermal:**
- DTP lower glass-transition temperature: Tg = 42.3 °C (below intraoral temperature ranges during hot beverage consumption)

**Overall verdict:** No universally superior material established; all evidence in vitro.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- All included evidence is in vitro — clinical force delivery, wear, and aging not captured
- Only 4/23 studies contributed to meta-analysis; extreme heterogeneity (I² = 97%) precludes firm pooled conclusions
- No low-RoB studies (18 medium, 4 high by QUIN) — systematic bias possible
- Highly variable testing conditions across studies (specimen geometry, loading protocols, storage conditions)
- No long-term aging or intraoral simulation data included
- Future work: standardized in vitro testing protocols; in vivo or ex vivo comparison of force delivery and tooth movement accuracy between TM and DTP

## 7. Glossary

- **TM (thermoformed):** aligners produced by pressure/vacuum-thermoforming a heated thermoplastic sheet over a model
- **DTP (direct 3D-printed):** aligners produced directly by resin-based additive manufacturing (e.g., MSLA/DLP) without a separate thermoforming step
- **Flexural strength:** maximum stress at rupture under bending load (MPa)
- **Storage modulus:** elastic component of viscoelastic modulus (stiffness under cyclic load)
- **Shape-recovery potential:** ability of a deformed polymer to return to its programmed geometry (related to shape-memory effect)
- **Tg (glass-transition temperature):** temperature below which amorphous polymers behave rigidly; above Tg, viscoelastic softening occurs — DTP Tg = 42.3 °C
- **Sa:** arithmetic mean surface amplitude (ISO 25178 areal roughness parameter)
- **Sv:** maximum surface valley depth
- **Sz:** maximum height of the surface (Sp + Sv)
- **SMD:** standardized mean difference (meta-analysis effect-size statistic)
- **QUIN:** Quality assessment tool for In vitro studies in Orthodontics and related Non-clinical disciplines
