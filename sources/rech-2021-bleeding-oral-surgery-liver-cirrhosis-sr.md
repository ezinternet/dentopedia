## Why Ingested

The sweep on alcohol and oral surgery kept returning to cirrhosis, because cirrhosis is the clinical state where heavy drinking becomes a surgical bleeding question. This review is the wiki's highest-level answer to that question, and its answer is counter-intuitive: despite thrombocytopenia and raised INR, extraction bleeding is rare and locally controllable. It is the safety-side anchor for [[oral-surgery/medina-2018-bleeding-dental-extractions-liver-cirrhosis]], which gives the primary data.

## Three-line Summary

PRISMA systematic review (PubMed, LILACS, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane) of 7 studies on bleeding during dental surgery in patients with liver cirrhosis; samples 23–318 participants, 62–1,183 teeth extracted.

Platelet counts ranged from 16,000 to 216,000/mm³ and INR was below 4, yet the prevalence of haemorrhagic events ranged only from 0% to 8.9%, and nearly all were controlled with local haemostatic measures.

Cirrhosis with moderated coagulation indices does not by itself justify pre-operative transfusion or factor replacement before extraction — local measures sufficed across the reviewed literature.

- **Included studies**: 7

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Quantifies the paradox.** The liver synthesises nearly all coagulation factors, yet the measured haemorrhagic event rate in cirrhotic patients undergoing oral surgery was 0–8.9%.
- **Brackets the laboratory envelope.** The reviewed studies sit in the platelet 16,000–216,000/mm³ and INR < 4 range — the practical envelope in which the "local measures are enough" conclusion applies.
- **Positions local haemostasis as the primary tool**, with transfusion and factor replacement not established as necessary in this population.
- **Sets the upper bound of the evidence.** Seven studies, no meta-analysis, heterogeneous designs — this is a low-certainty systematic review, and it should be cited as such.

## 3. Methodology

- **Design**: systematic review following the PRISMA checklist.
- **Search**: PubMed, LILACS, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane.
- **Inclusion**: studies reporting bleeding outcomes of dental/oral surgical procedures in patients with liver cirrhosis.
- **Included**: 7 studies; sample sizes 23 to 318 participants; 62 to 1,183 teeth extracted per study.
- **Laboratory range across studies**: platelet count 16,000–216,000/mm³; INR < 4.
- **Outcome**: prevalence of haemorrhagic events and how they were managed.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Parameter | Range across included studies |
|---|---|
| Participants per study | 23–318 |
| Teeth extracted per study | 62–1,183 |
| Platelet count | 16,000–216,000/mm³ |
| INR | < 4 |
| Haemorrhagic event prevalence | 0%–8.9% |

- Almost all haemorrhagic events were controlled with **local haemostatic measures**.
- Conclusion stated by the authors: patients with cirrhosis carry a **low bleeding risk** during dental treatment despite decreased platelets and increased INR.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Seven studies, no pooling.** No meta-analysis; the 0–8.9% range is a narrative span, not an estimate with a confidence interval.
- **Cirrhosis is not alcohol.** The review does not stratify by aetiology, so it cannot tell you whether alcohol-related cirrhosis bleeds differently from hepatitis- or autoimmune-related cirrhosis.
- **Heterogeneous surgery.** "Oral surgery" spans single extractions to multi-tooth clearance; the review does not model bleeding risk by invasiveness.
- **Laboratory thresholds are descriptive.** INR < 4 and platelets ≥ 16,000/mm³ are the range observed in the included studies, not validated cut-offs for proceeding.
- **Publication and reporting bias** are not formally assessed at this level of detail; small series reporting successful local control may be over-represented.

## 7. Glossary

- **INR (international normalized ratio)**: standardised prothrombin-time ratio; higher means slower clotting.
- **Thrombocytopenia**: low platelet count; a common feature of cirrhosis from splenic sequestration.
- **Local haemostatic measures**: suturing, pressure, haemostatic agents, splints — as opposed to systemic transfusion or factor replacement.
- **PRISMA**: reporting standard for systematic reviews.
- **Acquired coagulopathy**: abnormal clotting arising from disease (here cirrhosis) rather than an inherited disorder.
