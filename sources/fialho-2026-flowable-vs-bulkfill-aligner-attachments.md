## One-line Summary
Split-mouth RCT (n=50, 140 days, 800 attachments): flowable vs. bulk-fill flowable composites show equivalent attachment survival (HR 0.84, p=0.293), with an overall failure rate of 18.75%.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First study specifically comparing flowable and bulk-fill flowable composites for aligner attachment fabrication
- Confirms equivalent clinical performance across 140 days (10 aligner sets, 14-day change interval)
- Split-mouth design minimizes confounding from patient-level variables (age, gender, compliance)
- Clinically relevant finding: regular flowable (more cost-effective, widely available) can substitute for bulk-fill without performance compromise
- Identifies molars as higher-risk sites for attachment failure regardless of material

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Single-center split-mouth RCT, 1:1 allocation
- **Population**: n=50 (21M/29F), mean age 37.86±6.90y, Class I mild crowding (LII ≤2mm), complete permanent dentition
- **Exclusions**: agenesis, missing teeth (except 3rd molars), bisphosphonates, premolar extractions
- **Materials**: 3M Filtek Supreme Flowable (SF, conventional) vs. 3M Filtek Bulk Fill Flowable (BF)
- **Bonding**: 37% phosphoric acid + 3M Adper Single Bond 2 + Emitter D curing light
- **Aligners**: Invisalign SmartTrack; 10 aligners × 14-day changes = 140 days
- **Split**: Group 1 (n=25): right=SF, left=BF; Group 2 (n=25): right=BF, left=SF
- **4 posterior attachments per quadrant** (14-17, 24-27, 34-37, 44-47)
- **Primary outcome**: attachment survival rate at T2 (after aligner #10)
- **Secondary**: time to failure (Cox regression, Kaplan-Meier at 30/60/90/120/140 days)
- **Blinding**: measurement evaluator blinded to group assignment

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Overall failure rate**: 18.75% (150/800 attachments lost and rebonded)
- **Cox regression**: HR=0.84 (95%CI 0.60–1.16), p=0.293 — no significant group difference
- **Survival at 140 days**: BF 80.5% (76.7–84.5%), CF 83.5% (79.9–87.2%)
- **McNemar test per tooth**: no significant differences for any individual tooth
- **Molar vs. premolar failure**: molars consistently higher failure rate in both groups (tooth 17, 27, 37, 47 had n=8–9 failures vs. n=2–3 for premolars)
- **Notable exception**: mandibular right second molar — SF 8 vs. BF 4 failures (p=n.s., trend only)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- All Class I mild crowding — may not generalize to complex malocclusions (crossbite, deep bite, extraction cases)
- No objective wear-time monitoring
- No IPR or intermaxillary elastics in treatment setup — restricted to proclination movements
- Relatively short follow-up (140 days / 10 aligners); longer treatment phases unknown
- Future: complex malocclusion populations, longer follow-up, standardized attachment geometries, objective compliance monitoring

## 7. Glossary
- **SF (Filtek Supreme Flowable)**: conventional (non-bulk-fill) flowable composite; standard filler content
- **BF (Filtek Bulk Fill Flowable)**: bulk-fill flowable composite; optimized for polymerization depth without layering
- **LII (Little Irregularity Index)**: contact point displacement sum for crowding severity (≤2mm = mild)
- **Cox regression / HR**: survival analysis method; HR<1 = reduced hazard (fewer failures) for test group
- **Split-mouth design**: each patient serves as their own control; reduces inter-patient variability
