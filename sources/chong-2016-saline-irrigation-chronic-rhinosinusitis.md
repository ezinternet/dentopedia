## Why Ingested

The dental wiki's existing evidence on sinus irrigation comes from a single retrospective case series ([[wiki/oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/jaiswal-2024-sinus-irrigation-as-an-adjunctive]]) with no control group. This Cochrane SR+MA provides the highest-level evidence for saline irrigation in chronic rhinosinusitis generally, allowing dental clinicians to contextualise irrigation's role when managing odontogenic maxillary sinusitis.

## Three-line Summary

Cochrane systematic review with meta-analysis (2 RCTs, 116 adults) evaluating nasal saline irrigation (large-volume hypertonic vs usual care; low-volume nebulised vs intranasal corticosteroids) for chronic rhinosinusitis with or without polyps.

Large-volume (150 ml) hypertonic (2%) saline improved disease-specific HRQL vs usual care at 3 months (MD 6.3, 95% CI 0.89–11.71) and 6 months (MD 13.5, 95% CI 9.63–17.37), but evidence quality was low/very low; low-volume (5 ml) nebulised saline was inferior to intranasal corticosteroids (MD -13.5, 95% CI -14.44 to -12.56), very low quality.

Delivery method and volume matter: daily large-volume hypertonic irrigation shows some benefit over no treatment, while low-volume nebulised saline offers no advantage over intranasal steroids; both findings are limited by high risk of bias and small samples.

## 세줄요약

코크란 체계적 문헌고찰·메타분석 (RCT 2편, 성인 116명): 비강 생리식염수 세척(대용량 고장성 vs 통상치료; 소용량 네뷸라이저 vs 비강 스테로이드)을 만성 부비동염(폴립 유무 무관)에 비교.

대용량(150 mL) 고장성(2%) 생리식염수는 통상치료 대비 3개월(평균차이 6.3, 95% CI 0.89–11.71)과 6개월(평균차이 13.5, 95% CI 9.63–17.37)에 질병특이적 HRQL 개선 효과 보였으나 근거 질 낮음/매우 낮음; 소용량(5 mL) 네뷸라이저 식염수는 비강 스테로이드 대비 열세(평균차이 -13.5, 95% CI -14.44 ~ -12.56), 매우 낮은 근거 질.

전달 방식·용량이 핵심: 매일 대용량 고장성 세척은 무치료 대비 일정한 이점, 소용량 네뷸라이저 식염수는 비강 스테로이드에 비해 이점 없음 — 둘 다 비뚤림 위험 높고 표본 작아 해석 제한적.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First Cochrane review to apply a strict EPOS 2012 definition of chronic rhinosinusitis and separate evidence by delivery method/volume/concentration (large-volume hypertonic vs low-volume nebulised).
- Quantifies the effect of large-volume (150 ml) hypertonic (2%) saline vs usual care on disease-specific HRQL (RSDI): MD +6.3 at 3 months, +13.5 at 6 months (both favouring saline), though GRADE quality is low/very low.
- Demonstrates that low-volume (5 ml) nebulised saline is inferior to intranasal corticosteroids for disease severity (MD -13.5, 95% CI -14.44 to -12.56), very low quality.
- Reports adverse event rate: 23% of participants in the large-volume saline group experienced side effects (epistaxis, irritation, burning); no control-group AE data collected.
- Highlights that the two included RCTs differ substantially in population (with/without polyps), intervention (large-volume hypertonic vs low-volume nebulised), and comparator (usual care vs intranasal steroids), preventing pooled conclusions.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cochrane Intervention Review (PRISMA-compliant). Part of a suite of 6 reviews on primary medical management of chronic rhinosinusitis.
- **Search**: Cochrane ENT Trials Register, CENTRAL 2015 Issue 9, MEDLINE, EMBASE, ClinicalTrials.gov, ICTRP, Google Scholar; search date 30 Oct 2015; no language/year/status restrictions.
- **Inclusion criteria**: RCTs (including cluster/quasi-RCTs) with ≥2-week follow-up; adults with chronic rhinosinusitis (EPOS 2012 criteria, with or without polyps); saline delivered by any means (douche, irrigation, drops, spray, nebuliser) vs placebo/no treatment/other pharmacological interventions.
- **Exclusion criteria**: Majority cystic fibrosis, fungal/eosinophilic/allergic rhinosinusitis, aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease, sinus surgery within 6 weeks; within-patient (side-of-nose) randomisation; peri-surgical studies.
- **Primary outcomes**: Disease-specific HRQL (SNOT-22, RSOM-31, SNOT-20, RSDI), patient-reported disease severity (CSS, VAS), epistaxis.
- **Secondary outcomes**: Generic HRQL (SF-36, EQ-5D, SF-12), local irritation, discomfort, endoscopic score (Lund-Mackay/Lund-Kennedy), CT scan score (Lund-Mackay).
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB tool (random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting, other bias).
- **GRADE**: Used to assess quality of evidence for each outcome.
- **Analysis**: Random-effects meta-analysis where appropriate; MD for continuous outcomes, RR for dichotomous; subgroup by follow-up period (3 to <6 months, 6 to 12 months, >12 months).
- **Included studies**: 2 RCTs (116 participants total).
  - Study 1 (Large-volume hypertonic vs usual care): 76 adults (52 intervention, 24 control), with or without polyps, 6-month follow-up, open-label, 2:1 randomisation.
  - Study 2 (Low-volume nebulised vs intranasal corticosteroids): 40 adults with polyps, 3-month treatment + 3-month follow-up.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 2 small RCTs included (total n=116); both at high risk of bias (open-label, unclear allocation concealment, small samples, 2:1 randomisation in Study 1, baseline results shown to participants at 6-month follow-up).
- Study 1: no adverse event data in control group; disease severity score range not reported, limiting interpretability.
- Study 2: primary outcome (disease-specific HRQL) not reported; only 40 participants with polyps; very low quality for all outcomes.
- The two comparisons address fundamentally different clinical questions (saline vs usual care vs saline vs active pharmacological treatment) and cannot be pooled.
- GRADE quality low/very low for all outcomes due to risk of bias, imprecision, and indirectness.
- No data on isotonic vs hypertonic, different volumes, or saline as adjunct to intranasal corticosteroids.
- No long-term follow-up beyond 6 months.
- Future research needs adequately powered RCTs with standardised delivery methods, validated outcome measures, proper blinding, and longer follow-up.

## 7. Glossary

- CRS: Chronic rhinosinusitis — inflammation of nose and paranasal sinuses ≥12 weeks with ≥2 symptoms including nasal blockage/discharge.
- CRSwNP / CRSsNP: Chronic rhinosinusitis with/without nasal polyps.
- RSDI: Rhinosinusitis Disability Index (0–100, higher = better HRQL).
- SNOT-22: Sino-Nasal Outcome Test-22 (disease-specific HRQL).
- SF-12: Short Form-12 Health Survey (generic HRQL, 0–100).
- EPOS: European Position Paper on Rhinosinusitis and Nasal Polyps.
- GRADE: Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation.
- HRQL: Health-related quality of life.
- MD: Mean difference.
- RR: Risk ratio.
- Neti pot: Gravity-based nasal irrigation vessel.
