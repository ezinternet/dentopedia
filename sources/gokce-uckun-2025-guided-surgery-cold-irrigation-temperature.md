## Summary

Ex vivo study (Sakarya/Istanbul Universities) on 40 sheep iliac crest bone specimens (D3, CBCT-confirmed mean 696 HU), 192 implant sites total. Stereolithographic surgical guide fabricated with Real Guide 5.0; K-type thermocouple 1mm from osteotomy site, 100ms sampling.

Three study groups:
- **Group A** (n=80): Guided + conventional, high speed 900 rpm, 24°C irrigation (100 mL/min)
- **Group B** (n=80): Guided + conventional, low speed 300–600 rpm, NO irrigation
- **Group C** (n=36): Guided + conventional, high speed 900 rpm, 10°C irrigation

Four guided implant sizes: 2.9×8.5mm, 2.9×13mm, 3.8×8.5mm, 3.8×13mm; control: 3.5×10mm conventional

Key results:
- Mean temperatures: Group A 30.5 ± 4°C, Group B 31 ± 5°C, Group C 28 ± 3°C (from 24°C baseline)
- Group C significantly lower than A and B (p=0.001 both)
- A vs B: NOT significantly different — low-speed drilling without irrigation offers no thermal advantage
- Group B: 8 specimens exceeded +10°C threshold at 600rpm without irrigation, 5 at 300rpm
- Larger bur diameter → significantly higher temperature in all groups (p=0.001)
- Deeper drilling (13mm > 8.5mm) → higher temperature even in Group C
- Guided surgery consistently higher temperature than conventional technique
- Under 10°C irrigation: wider diameter did not significantly increase temperature at 8.5mm depth

Conclusions: (1) guided surgery is thermally safe under effective external irrigation; (2) 10°C irrigation is the most effective cooling strategy; (3) low-speed drilling without irrigation does NOT reduce thermal risk and may exceed the +10°C critical threshold; (4) wider and deeper = hotter regardless of technique.

Limitations: ex vivo lacks vascularity/body temperature; no standardized cortical/cancellous thickness; single guide design.
