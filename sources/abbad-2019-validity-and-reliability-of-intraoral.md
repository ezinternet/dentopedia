## Three-line Summary

In-vivo method-comparison study (n=24 completely edentulous patients) comparing a MATLAB-linked intraoral digital gothic-arch tracer with a conventional intraoral tracer, and comparing upright vs supine recording of horizontal jaw relation (centric relation).

Supine recording was significantly more reproducible than upright (mean 3.80 upright vs 5.12 supine; median 4 vs 5; SD 0.81650 vs 1.33292; P=0.0001; Fischer's exact P=0.002), while upright–supine agreement was very weak and non-significant (Pearson r=−0.130, P=0.535); the digital tracer was reported as more valid than the conventional tracer.

Record horizontal jaw relation supine for higher reproducibility and prefer the digital tracer for visualization/data storage; transfer of the record to a virtual articulator is difficult, and the paper contains internal reporting inconsistencies (n=24 enrolled vs n=25 in the correlation table; r reported as r²).

## 세줄요약

생체 내 방법 비교 연구 (완전무치악 환자 n=24명) — MATLAB 연동 구내 디지털 고딕아치 트레이서와 기존 구내 트레이서를 비교하고, 직립·앙와위 체위에서 수평 악간관계(중심관계, Centric Relation, CR) 기록을 평가.

앙와위 기록이 직립보다 유의하게 재현성 높음 (평균 직립 3.80 vs 앙와위 5.12; 중앙값 4 vs 5; SD 0.81650 vs 1.33292; P=0.0001; Fischer 정확검정 P=0.002), 직립–앙와위 일치도는 매우 약하고 유의하지 않음 (Pearson r=−0.130, P=0.535); 디지털 트레이서가 기존 트레이서보다 유효하다고 보고.

임상 시사점: 수평 악간관계는 앙와위에서 기록하고 시각화·데이터 저장 측면에서 디지털 트레이서를 선호; 가상 관절기(Virtual Articulator)로 기록 이전이 어렵고, 논문에 내부 보고 불일치(등록 n=24 vs 상관표 n=25; r을 r²로 표기) 존재.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. Introduced a **MATLAB-linked intraoral digital gothic-arch tracer** that visualizes the arrowhead tracing on a laptop and records X/Y mandibular coordinates, addressing the conventional tracer's drawback that readings cannot be visualized or stored.
2. Directly compared **validity of the intraoral digital tracer against the conventional intraoral tracer** in the same edentulous patients.
3. Tested **body posture (upright vs supine)** as a variable in the reproducibility of horizontal mandibular (centric-relation) position recording.
4. Reported supine as significantly more reproducible than upright (P=0.0001), with the digital tracer judged more valid than the conventional one.
5. Described as the first study to record horizontal mandibular position using MATLAB software (Maharashtra, as stated by the authors).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Participants**: 24 edentulous patients with well-formed maxillary and mandibular ridges and inter-ridge distance ≥22 mm. Exclusions: macroglossia, limited mouth opening, TMJ disorder signs/symptoms, poor neuromuscular control, extraction socket. Institutional Ethics Committee approval and patient consent obtained.
- **Prosthetic set-up**: Conventional impressions → dental-stone master cast → heat-cure acrylic record bases (Acrayln-H) → working cast (Kalrock, Kalabhai, India) → occlusal rims. Orientation recorded with facebow (Hanau Springbow); maxillary cast mounted on semi-adjustable articulator (Hanau Wide Vue II); tentative centric relation registered at the established vertical dimension.
- **Conventional tracer**: central bearing plate + custom-modified central bearing screw/stylus (CNC-fabricated, DX200); graph paper on the mandibular occlusal rim; stylus above rim level; attached with modeling wax (Prodent).
- **Digital tracer system**: three parts — (1) resistance intraoral digital tracer (2.5 cm × 3.5 cm; 400–500 mg), (2) extra-oral digitizer control circuit with A/D converter, (3) laptop running MATLAB analysis software, interfaced via MAX 232C. Contact of stylus on the tracer yields X/Y coordinates on screen; arrowhead apex defines the CR point.
- **Recording protocol**: Light closure until stylus–tracer contact, closure in the most retruded position, guided left and right lateral movements, then retruded followed by forward/backward movement and return to origin. Readings taken upright, then repeated supine; the same procedure performed with the conventional tracer.
- **Statistics**: Student's paired t-test (upright vs supine), Fischer's exact test (association of supine with upright), Pearson's correlation (agreement between postures).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Reproducibility by posture** (intraoral digital tracing; Table 1):

| Measure | Upright | Supine |
|---|---|---|
| Mean | 3.8000 | 5.1200 |
| Median (range) | 4 (0–5) | 5 (3–7) |
| SD | 0.81650 | 1.33292 |

- **Student's paired t-test**: values significantly higher in supine than upright, **P=0.0001**.
- **Fischer's exact test**: significant difference in frequency distribution for supine vs upright association, **P=0.002**.
- **Pearson's correlation** (Table 2): **r = −0.130** (abstract states "r² = −0.130"), two-tailed **P = 0.535** — very weak, downhill, non-significant agreement between upright and supine; table reports n=25 per variable despite n=24 enrolled.
- **Mean reproducibility** (narrative/Graph 1): upright 3.8, supine 5.12 — supine generates maximum reproducibility.
- **Conclusion as reported**: intraoral digital tracing is more valid than the conventional intraoral tracer (visualization and accuracy), and horizontal mandibular position recorded supine is more accurate/reproducible than upright.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Difficult to transfer the digital tracing record to a **virtual articulator**.
- Very limited published data on intraoral digital tracing; authors call for more research in digital tracing methods.
- Small sample (n=24), single center; custom (non-commercial) devices — transferability to other systems uncertain.
- **Internal reporting inconsistencies reproduced verbatim**: abstract reports "r²= −0.130" while Table 2 labels the same value as Pearson correlation −0.130 (a squared value cannot be negative); Table 2 lists n=25 per variable while Methods/Abstract state n=24; Table 1 caption orders columns "supine and upright" but the Discussion maps 3.8 to upright and 5.12 to supine (column order follows the Discussion's mapping here).
- No long-term prosthesis outcome was correlated with the tracing reproducibility (surrogate endpoint only).

## 6. Related Work

- Gysi (1910) needlepoint tracer; conventional tracers and modifications (Nandini et al. efficiency order: intraoral tracer > functiograph > Chandra tracer > checkbite > hight tracer).
- Rubel (1866) graphic recording method; historical comparison of tracing (~5° error) vs wax/compound bites (~25° error).
- Watanabe — intraoral gothic arch tracing more reproducible supine than upright; bimanual manipulation supine closer than upright; light chin guidance more variable upright; digital gothic arch tracing with diagnostic dentures at baseline and 1/3 months.
- Lund, Moller, Tripodakis — supine better than upright with conventional methods (attributed to reduced lateral pterygoid activity supine).
- Jones, El-Aramany, Smith, Langer & Michman, Myer, Linsen, Sabarigirinathan — intraoral gothic arch tracing among the most precise/repeatable centric jaw relation methods.

## 7. Glossary

- **Centric relation (CR)**: reproducible maxillomandibular reference position used to coordinate occlusion in complete-denture construction.
- **Horizontal jaw relation**: horizontal component of maxillomandibular relation recorded here; the CR point at the gothic-arch apex.
- **Gothic-arch (arrow-point) tracing**: border-movement tracing whose arrowhead apex marks the retruded/centric mandibular position; X/Y coordinates read digitally here.
- **Intraoral digital tracer**: custom resistance sensor pad (2.5×3.5 cm, 400–500 mg) connected via MAX232C/A-D converter to MATLAB for on-screen coordinate readout.
- **Conventional intraoral tracer**: central bearing screw/stylus scribing on graph paper on the opposing occlusal rim; readings not directly visualizable or storable.
- **Reproducibility**: consistency of repeated horizontal mandibular position recordings within a posture; compared upright vs supine.
- **Virtual articulator**: digital articulator; the authors note the digital tracing record is difficult to transfer into it.
