## Why Ingested

Serves the **post-adjustment polishing/glazing protocol** axis of the 2026-11-12 `zirconia-occlusal-adjustment` topic — after occlusal grinding, which chairside polishing kit returns a translucent zirconia surface close enough to glaze to limit *Streptococcus mutans* colonization — and it is the batch's only paper that pairs roughness with a biological outcome instead of wear or strength. It also gives the concrete roughness ladder (grind 0.97 µm → EVE 0.68 → Komet 0.39 / OptraGloss 0.40 → glaze 0.02 µm for zirconia) that the 0.2 µm plaque threshold of [[dental-materials/bollen-1997-comparison-surface-roughness-oral-hard]] and the "polishing never restores glaze" conclusion of [[dental-materials/zirconia/mohammadi-bassir-2017-grinding-overglazing-polishing-zirconia]] can be read against; its two-material design (lithium disilicate vs ultra-translucent zirconia) files it in the parent `dental-materials` folder rather than either ceramic subcategory.

## Three-line Summary

In vitro bench study (BMC Oral Health 2026; Chulalongkorn University, Bangkok; 100 specimens of 10 × 10 × 3 mm³, 5 groups per material, n = 10) comparing glaze, fine-grain diamond grinding, and three chairside polishing systems (Komet ZR kit, OptraGloss, EVE Diapro/Diacera) on lithium disilicate (IPS e.max CAD) and ultra-translucent zirconia (KATANA UTML) by contact profilometry (Ra/Rz), SEM, EDS and 48-h *S. mutans* biofilm area coverage by CLSM.

All polishing systems reduced roughness after grinding but none reached glaze level (zirconia Ra: glaze 0.02, grind 0.97, EVE 0.68, Komet 0.39, OptraGloss 0.40 µm; lithium disilicate Ra: glaze 0.03, grind 1.29, EVE 0.86, Komet 0.51, OptraGloss 0.44 µm; P < 0.001), and the three systems differed in roughness but not in *S. mutans* adhesion (P = 1.000 for lithium disilicate, P ≥ 0.903 for zirconia).

On zirconia the polished surfaces cut biofilm coverage from 2.59% (ground) to 0.33–0.74% with no significant difference between kits, whereas on lithium disilicate polishing did not lower biofilm at all (6.38–8.11% vs 8.51% ground) — clinically, chairside polishing is a defensible endpoint for adjusted zirconia but not for adjusted lithium disilicate, where re-glazing remains the recommendation (single-species in vitro model, CLSM area coverage only).

## 세줄요약

인비트로 실험실 연구 (BMC Oral Health 2026; 출라롱콘 대학, 방콕; 10 × 10 × 3 mm³ 시편 100개, 재료당 5군·군당 n = 10) — 리튬 디실리케이트 (Lithium Disilicate, LDS; IPS e.max CAD)와 초투명 지르코니아 (Ultra-Translucent Zirconia; KATANA UTML)에서 유약(대조), 미세 다이아몬드 버 연삭, 그리고 세 가지 체어사이드 연마 시스템 (Komet ZR kit·OptraGloss·EVE Diapro/Diacera)을 접촉식 조도계(Ra·Rz)·주사전자현미경(Scanning Electron Microscope, SEM)·에너지분산분광법(Energy-Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy, EDS)·48시간 공초점레이저주사현미경(Confocal Laser Scanning Microscopy, CLSM) 생물막 면적률로 비교.

모든 연마 시스템이 연삭 후 표면거칠기 (surface roughness, Ra)를 낮췄지만 유약 수준에는 도달하지 못했고 (지르코니아 Ra: 유약 0.02, 연삭 0.97, EVE 0.68, Komet 0.39, OptraGloss 0.40 µm; LDS Ra: 유약 0.03, 연삭 1.29, EVE 0.86, Komet 0.51, OptraGloss 0.44 µm; P < 0.001), 세 시스템은 조도에서 유의차가 있었으나 스트렙토코커스 뮤탄스 (*Streptococcus mutans*) 부착에서는 차이가 없었다 (LDS P = 1.000, 지르코니아 P ≥ 0.903).

지르코니아에서는 연마 후 생물막 면적률이 2.59%(연삭)에서 0.33–0.74%로 감소했고 키트 간 유의차가 없었으나, LDS에서는 연마가 생물막을 전혀 줄이지 못했으므로 (6.38–8.11% vs 연삭 8.51%) — 임상적으로 조정된 지르코니아는 체어사이드 연마로 마무리해도 되지만 조정된 LDS는 재유약이 권장된다 (단일 균종 인비트로 모델, CLSM 면적률만 평가).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Direct head-to-head of **three commercially distinct chairside polishing protocols** on **two ceramic classes** — the material-specific versus universal-kit question (EVE material-specific vs OptraGloss universal) is the study's own framing.
- Pairs **physical** (contact profilometry Ra/Rz, SEM topography, EDS of the abrasive itself) with **biological** (*S. mutans* 48-h biofilm area coverage by CLSM) outcomes on the same specimens.
- Quantifies the **residual gap after adjustment**: even the best kit leaves zirconia at 0.39–0.40 µm and lithium disilicate at 0.44–0.51 µm, i.e. 13–20× the 0.02–0.03 µm glaze baseline and above the 0.2 µm plaque threshold.
- Documents that **Ra is not a sufficient predictor of biofilm**: Komet and OptraGloss gave indistinguishable zirconia Ra (0.39 vs 0.40 µm) but different biofilm coverage (0.74% vs 0.33%) and different SEM scratch patterns.
- Reports **material-specific clinical advice**: zirconia's intrinsically lower biofilm retention justifies chairside polishing after adjustment, while lithium disilicate should be re-glazed when feasible.
- Characterizes the **abrasive particles themselves** by SEM/EDS (EVE Diapro ~8.69 µm, EVE Diacera ~4.58 µm, Komet ~4.38 µm, OptraGloss ~4.22 µm diamond; OptraGloss stage-2 additionally contains Al and Si, i.e. likely Al₂O₃/SiC).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: 2 materials × 5 surface treatments, n = 10 per group for roughness (100 specimens total), n = 5 per group for the bacterial assay.
- **Sample size**: a priori G*Power 3.1.9.6 (α = 0.05, power = 0.80, f = 0.40) → fewer than 10 per group required; post hoc check confirmed f > 0.40.
- **Specimen preparation**: sectioned with a low-speed saw under water cooling; flattened with 300/400/600/1200-grit SiC papers; ultrasonically cleaned in 70% ethanol 3 min; lithium disilicate crystallized in a Programat P300, zirconia sintered in an inFire HTC speed furnace.
- **Groups**: (1) glazed control (AKZENT Plus GLAZE LT, VITA; fired per manufacturer), (2) grinding only, (3) grind + Komet ZR kit, (4) grind + OptraGloss, (5) grind + EVE (Diapro for lithium disilicate, Diacera for zirconia).
- **Adjustment simulation**: one surface ground with a fine-grain diamond bur (ZR 8881, Komet) in a high-speed handpiece under water cooling **until a permanent marker line was fully removed**; bur replaced every 5 specimens.
- **Polishing protocol**: slow-speed handpiece at **10,000 rpm** for all kits, standardized **~2 N** finger pressure, **linear motions, 60 s** per specimen, one operator. (Manufacturer-recommended speeds differ; a single speed was used deliberately.)
- **Roughness**: contact profilometer (Form Talysurf Series 2), cutoff 0.8 mm, 1 mN, 0.5 mm/s over 5 mm; 3 parallel traces per specimen, mean used.
- **Topography / chemistry**: SEM at 100× (specimens gold-coated) and SEM-EDS of polisher tips at 3,000×, 15 kV.
- **Bacterial assay**: *S. mutans* ATCC 25175, BHI agar, 37 °C/5% CO₂; suspension standardized to 3 × 10⁸ cells/mL (1.0 McFarland); specimens autoclaved (121 °C, 30 min) and pre-coated with artificial saliva for 2 h to form a pellicle; 48-h biofilm in 5 mL suspension; rinsing, LIVE/DEAD BacLight (SYTO 9 / propidium iodide) staining; CLSM at 63×, z-stacks by maximum-intensity projection; 5 random fields per specimen (25 areas per group); area fraction quantified in ImageJ.
- **Statistics**: Shapiro–Wilk then **Welch's ANOVA + Games–Howell** for Ra/Rz; Shapiro–Wilk then **Kruskal–Wallis with Bonferroni** for biofilm coverage; α = 0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Surface roughness, lithium disilicate (Table 3), mean ± SD, µm (n = 10):**

| Treatment | Ra | Rz |
|---|---|---|
| Glaze | 0.03 ± 0.00 a | 0.11 ± 0.03 a |
| Grind | 1.29 ± 0.07 b | 8.45 ± 0.50 b |
| EVE Diapro | 0.86 ± 0.09 c | 5.66 ± 0.62 d |
| Komet | 0.51 ± 0.10 d | 3.33 ± 0.64 d |
| OptraGloss | 0.44 ± 0.09 d | 3.02 ± 0.77 d |

**Surface roughness, zirconia (Table 4), mean ± SD, µm (n = 10):**

| Treatment | Ra | Rz |
|---|---|---|
| Glaze | 0.02 ± 0.00 a | 0.09 ± 0.00 a |
| Grind | 0.97 ± 0.22 b | 6.41 ± 1.35 b |
| EVE Diacera | 0.68 ± 0.22 b | 4.50 ± 1.43 c |
| Komet | 0.39 ± 0.06 c | 2.34 ± 0.27 d |
| OptraGloss | 0.40 ± 0.12 c | 2.60 ± 0.57 d |

- Same-letter superscripts = not significantly different. On lithium disilicate OptraGloss and Komet tie (0.44 vs 0.51 µm), while on zirconia Komet and OptraGloss tie (0.39 vs 0.40 µm) — the ranking is **material-dependent**, not kit-dependent.
- EVE was the roughest kit on both materials (0.86 µm lithium disilicate, 0.68 µm zirconia); SEM showed EVE's high-gloss polisher carries the largest diamond particles (~8.69 µm Diapro, ~4.58 µm Diacera) versus Komet ~4.38 µm and OptraGloss ~4.22 µm.
- Relative to the **0.2 µm plaque threshold** (Bollen) nothing polished qualifies; relative to the **0.5 µm tactile-detection threshold**, OptraGloss clears both materials, Komet is at the limit for lithium disilicate (0.51 µm) and clears zirconia (0.39 µm), EVE fails both.
- SEM confirmed deep grooves and scratches after diamond grinding; all polished surfaces were smoother but retained scratches; OptraGloss looked smoothest.

**Biofilm area coverage, lithium disilicate (Table 5), median % (IQR):**

| Treatment | Median | IQR |
|---|---|---|
| Glaze | 0.14 b | 0.06–0.34 |
| Grind | 8.51 a | 5.83–13.35 |
| EVE Diapro | 7.30 a | 3.00–13.98 |
| Komet | 6.38 a | 3.20–12.53 |
| OptraGloss | 8.11 a | 4.56–8.03 |

→ Glaze is significantly lower than all four; the three polished groups are **not** different from the merely ground group (P = 1.000), so polishing bought roughness but no measurable biofilm benefit on this material.

**Biofilm area coverage, zirconia (Table 6), median % (IQR):**

| Treatment | Median | IQR |
|---|---|---|
| Glaze | 0.15 c | 0.05–0.38 |
| Grind | 2.59 a | 2.07–3.30 |
| EVE Diacera | 0.60 b | 0.31–0.95 |
| Komet | 0.74 b | 0.33–1.64 |
| OptraGloss | 0.33 bc | 0.20–0.69 |

→ Here polishing did reduce biofilm versus ground, the three kits did not differ (P ≥ 0.903), and OptraGloss was statistically indistinguishable from glaze (0.33% vs 0.15%) although the absolute coverage was still ~2.2× higher.

**Cross-material**: lithium disilicate retained 3–13× more *S. mutans* than zirconia at every treatment level (e.g. ground 8.51% vs 2.59%; OptraGloss 8.11% vs 0.33%), which the authors attribute to material surface free energy/hydrophilicity rather than roughness.

**Hypotheses**: H1 and H2 (roughness) rejected; H3 and H4 (adhesion) not rejected.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

**Author-stated:** limited number and brands of ceramics and polishing kits; polishing only partly standardized (hand pressure, linear motion, but not instrumented); one speed applied to all kits despite different manufacturer recommendations; no manufacturer guidance for pressure or time; in vitro design cannot reproduce salivary flow, pH, temperature or masticatory load; monospecies *S. mutans* model does not represent multi-species biofilm; CLSM area coverage is semiquantitative with no CFU or metabolic-activity confirmation; future work should add CFU/viability assays.

**Critical appraisal (added at ingest):**
- **No strength or phase-transformation outcome.** Grinding damage in zirconia is normally read through flexural strength and monoclinic-phase fraction ([[dental-materials/zirconia/mohammadi-bassir-2017-grinding-overglazing-polishing-zirconia]], [[dental-materials/zirconia/sarabi-2024-grinding-ltd-flexural-strength-zirconia]]); this study reports only topography and biofilm, so "polishing is adequate for zirconia" is a surface/biological statement, not a mechanical one.
- **Grinding endpoint is a marker line**, not a controlled depth of cut, so the amount of adjustment removed — the very variable that matters clinically — is not quantified and probably varied between specimens.
- **Single operator, no blinding** of the measurer for SEM/CLSM fields; profilometry traces were averaged from 3 runs but no operator-blinding or repeatability (ICC) is reported.
- **Biofilm n = 5 per group** with wide IQRs (e.g. lithium disilicate EVE 3.00–13.98%) — the "no difference between kits" conclusion rests on a Kruskal–Wallis with Bonferroni on 5 specimens and is therefore an underpowered negative, not evidence of equivalence.
- **Biofilm magnitude is model-specific**: 48 h static monospecies culture in artificial saliva with a pellicle, no salivary flow or shear; absolute percentages (0.14% on glaze) will not transfer to the mouth, only the ordering may.
- **Only S. mutans**, a hydrophobic cariogenic species; plaque on ceramic margins in vivo is a multi-species, largely streptococcal/actinomyces community, and the paper's own hydrophobicity argument would predict a different result for hydrophilic species.
- **Glaze control is a laboratory glaze fired on a flat plate** — not the anatomy-bearing, possibly thin, clinically adjusted glaze layer, so the "glaze is unbeatable" baseline is best-case.
- **Reported statistics are borderline inconsistent**: the abstract gives P ≥ 0.903 for zirconia adhesion while Table 6 marks OptraGloss as "bc" (overlapping glaze and polished letters), and the text simultaneously says there is "no significant difference between OptraGloss and glaze" — the exact post hoc P values are not tabulated.
- **Text/data artifacts**: Table 4 lists the zirconia grind Rz SD as "6.41 ± 0.1.35" (read here as 1.35), and Table 3/4 Rz superscripts are not monotonic with the means (grind and EVE share "d" on lithium disilicate Rz despite 8.45 vs 5.66 µm).
- **No XRD/EDS of the specimen surfaces after grinding**, so the EDS work characterizes the polisher tips rather than the treated ceramic; the claim that OptraGloss's Al/Si content explains its finish is mechanistic inference, not demonstrated.
- **No aging/thermocycling**, so nothing is known about how polished-versus-glazed surfaces drift (glaze chipping, zirconia LTD) over clinical time.

## 7. Glossary

- **Ra (arithmetic mean roughness, µm)**: mean absolute deviation of the profile from its centre line over the evaluation length — the standard "how rough" number, blind to whether roughness is scratches or plateaus.
- **Rz (mean roughness depth, µm)**: mean of the maximum peak-to-valley heights of successive sampling lengths; more sensitive to deep grooves than Ra.
- **Surface roughness threshold 0.2 µm**: Bollen's proposed Ra below which further smoothing does not reduce bacterial accumulation.
- **Tactile detection threshold 0.25–0.50 µm**: roughness range at which patients can feel a surface with the tongue; 0.50 µm is used as the clinical acceptability ceiling, with natural enamel at ~0.28 µm.
- **Chairside polishing system**: two-step diamond-in-polyurethane kit (pre-polisher + high-gloss polisher) used at slow speed after intraoral adjustment when re-glazing is impractical.
- **Glaze**: thin fired glass layer (here AKZENT Plus GLAZE LT) that gives the smoothest achievable surface and a hydrophilic, low-adhesion character.
- **CLSM-derived biofilm area coverage (%)**: fraction of the imaged field covered by fluorescent bacteria after LIVE/DEAD staining — a semiquantitative, viable-plus-nonviable area measure, not a cell count.
- **Pellicle**: salivary protein film adsorbed onto a surface in the first ~2 h, which conditions subsequent bacterial attachment; modelled here with artificial saliva.
- **EDS (energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy)**: elemental analysis of a surface; used here to identify the abrasive fillers in each polisher tip (C/O = diamond-dominant matrix; Al/Si/Ti/Fe/Cr/Ni as trace fillers).
