## Why Ingested
This letter-to-the-editor describes an inert teaching model for practicing newer mandible reduction techniques for acute temporomandibular joint dislocation; it extends the practical training discussion found in [[wiki/tmj/prechel-2018-the-treatment-of-temporomandibular-joint.md]] by documenting how to construct a low-cost biomechanical trainer to teach and practice those techniques.

## Three-line Summary
The paper describes an educational innovation: construction of an inexpensive skull-based biomechanical model to teach/practice mandible reduction techniques for temporomandibular joint dislocation.
The model uses a plastic skull, elastic string, resistance bands, and simple hardware to simulate the joint and muscle resistance; the total parts cost reported is $67 (USD) with a linked video demonstration.
The clinical implication is that hands-on practice with a trainer can improve understanding and execution of techniques (including wrist pivot and extraoral approaches) that are difficult to learn from static images, though the model itself has no patient data or outcome validation.

## 세줄요약
이 논문은 급성 악관절 탈구(temporomandibular joint dislocation, TMJ dislocation)의 수동복원(manual reduction) 기법 교육을 위한 저비용 생역학 모델(골격 모델)에 대한 기술문(letter)이다.
사용한 재료는 플라스틱 두개골 모델, 탄성줄, 저항밴드 등의 간단한 부품이며, 부품비 총액은 67달러(미화)로 보고되었다.
손으로 조작해 보는 실습(trainer)이 정적 이미지만으로 학습하기 어려운 수술적/수동 기법(손목축법 wrist pivot method, 외측법 extraoral method 등)의 이해와 숙련에 도움이 될 수 있다는 교육적 함의를 제시하지만, 모델 자체의 임상 성과 검증은 포함되지 않았다.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Describes construction of an inexpensive biomechanical trainer for practicing mandible reduction techniques (conventional/sequential, wrist pivot, extraoral).
- Provides explicit materials (plastic skull, elastic string, resistance bands, toggle stoppers, hardware) and notes a demonstration video (youtu.be/OLPu0vwmTpo).
- Emphasizes that hands-on practice helps clarify hand positioning and force application for techniques that are difficult to learn from static images.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Educational/technical letter describing an inert physical model (bench setup with no living subject).
- **Model construction**: Plastic human skull; removed temporomandibular joint (TMJ) capsules; drilled holes at center of TMJ/articular fossa and through condyles; looped elastic string through TMJ and condyles secured with toggle stoppers; resistance bands looped around zygomatic arches, tied at base of mandible, held by drywall screws; cranial cap secured with nut/fender washer.
- **Techniques demonstrated**: Conventional (and sequential reduction of one side at a time), wrist pivot method, extraoral technique.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Reported total parts cost: $67 USD (specific item Amazon URLs provided in the original letter).
- Demonstrated feasibility of practicing the three approaches on the model (including sequential reduction when simultaneous fails, and the directional force application in the extraoral technique).
- Authors note that the trainer allowed direct visualization of condyle disengagement with the wrist pivot motion.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- No patient data, no clinical outcomes (success rates, complication rates) are reported for the model.
- Model is an inert bench representation; it does not replicate all anatomical/clinical variability (muscle spasm, trauma, patient tolerance).
- Validation is anecdotal (authors’ practice session); not tested in controlled educational outcomes study.

## 7. Glossary
- **Biomechanical model (trainer)**: Physical apparatus designed to simulate mechanical behavior of anatomy to practice techniques.
- **Wrist pivot method (손목축법)**: Manual reduction technique that uses wrist motion/hand positioning to disengage the mandibular condyle.
- **Extraoral method (외측법)**: Technique applying force from outside the mouth (avoids placing fingers intraorally).
- **Sequential reduction**: Reducing one side at a time when simultaneous bilateral reduction is not feasible/tolerable.
