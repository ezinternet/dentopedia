## Why Ingested

기존 위키의 TMD 페이지들은 대부분 진단·치료(스플린트, arthrocentesis, exercise 등)에 집중되어 있고, 저작곤란(chewing difficulty)의 "원인 감별" — TMD성 vs 결손치성(tooth loss) — 을 직접 비교한 논문은 없었다. 본 cross-sectional 연구(n=392, 50세 이상)는 두 원인군(보철군 193명 vs TMD군 199명)을 NRS·SRI·ISI로 직접 비교해, 노년 저작곤란 환자를 진료실에서 "치아 문제인지 관절 문제인지" 감별하고 치료 우선순위를 정하는 데 실용적 근거를 제공한다. [[wiki/tmj/pigozzi-2021-quality-of-life-tmd-sr-ma]]가 보여준 TMD의 심리사회적 부담(QoL 저하)을 본 논문이 SRI(스트레스 반응)·ISI(불면) 수치로 더 세분화하여 재확인하며, [[wiki/tmj/valesan-2021-tmd-prevalence-sr-ma]]가 밝힌 TMD의 여성 우세(1.5–2배)를 본 연구(TMD군 여성 77.4%)가 재확인한다. `geriatric-dentistry` 범주와도 접점(노년 저작기능·구강노쇠)이 있어 향후 synthesis에 활용 가능.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional comparative study, n=392 patients aged ≥50 with chewing difficulty, split into a prosthetic/tooth-loss group (n=193) and a TMD group (n=199) at a Korean university hospital dental clinic.

The prosthetic group had significantly higher chewing discomfort (NRS) and more missing posterior teeth/worse Eichner class; the TMD group had significantly higher Stress Response Inventory scores (tension, anger, depression, fatigue, frustration, total) and a higher rate of clinically significant insomnia (13.6% vs 9.9%, ISI ≥15).

Tooth loss (occlusal support) drives chewing discomfort severity more than TMD pain does, but TMD patients need concurrent stress/sleep assessment and a multidisciplinary approach, especially women (77.4% of the TMD group).

## 세줄요약

단면 비교연구, 50세 이상 저작곤란 환자 392명을 보철(결손치)군(n=193)과 측두하악장애(TMD)군(n=199)으로 나눠 한국 대학병원 치과에서 분석.

보철군은 저작불편감(NRS)과 상실 구치 수·Eichner 분류가 유의하게 더 나빴고, TMD군은 스트레스반응척도(SRI, 긴장·분노·우울·피로·좌절·총점)와 임상적 불면증 비율(ISI≥15, 13.6% vs 9.9%)이 유의하게 더 높았다.

저작불편감의 중증도에는 결손치(교합지지 상실)가 TMD 통증보다 더 크게 기여하지만, TMD 환자는 스트레스·수면 평가와 다학제 접근이 필요하며 특히 여성(TMD군의 77.4%)에서 그러하다.

- **PMID**: 39873035 / **PMCID**: PMC11763206
- **Study design**: Cross-sectional comparative study
- **Setting**: Dental Clinic Center, Pusan National University Hospital (PNUH), Korea; ethics approval PNUH 2009-002-094
- **Enrollment period**: June 2018 – September 2020
- **Sample**: 392 patients aged ≥50 years with chewing difficulty; Prosthetic group n=193 (seeking prosthetic treatment for tooth loss, free of TMD signs/symptoms), TMD group n=199 (referred for TMD-related joint/muscle pain, diagnosed via RDC/TMD axis I)
- **Exclusions**: history of facial/jaw fracture or orthognathic surgery; psychiatric conditions; neurologic impairment/disease (stroke, tumor, trauma, epilepsy); other systemic muscular disorders (fibromyalgia, rheumatoid arthritis)

## 2. Key Contributions

- First comparative (head-to-head) study of TMD-caused vs tooth-loss-caused chewing difficulty in the same cohort, rather than studying each cause in isolation.
- Quantifies that occlusal-support loss (posterior tooth loss, worse Eichner class) is the dominant driver of subjective chewing-discomfort severity (NRS), more so than TMD pain.
- Quantifies that TMD (not tooth loss) is the dominant driver of psychological stress response (SRI) and clinical insomnia (ISI) among older chewing-difficulty patients.
- Identifies sex as an effect modifier: TMD group skews strongly female (77.4%), and interaction analysis shows female prosthetic patients are more susceptible to severe chewing discomfort than TMD patients.
- Provides clinically actionable triage logic: prosthodontic restoration of occlusion for tooth-loss-driven discomfort vs psychological stress/sleep screening + multidisciplinary approach for TMD-driven discomfort.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Cross-sectional, two-group comparison (Prosthetic vs TMD), single center.
- **Group assignment**: Prosthetic group — patients seeking prosthetic treatment due to tooth loss, free of TMD signs/symptoms. TMD group — patients referred for TMJ-pain-related discomfort, diagnosed per RDC/TMD axis I.
- **Chewing discomfort**: Numeric Rating Scale (NRS), 0–10, self-reported; severe discomfort defined as NRS ≥7.
- **Tooth/occlusion assessment**: number of missing posterior teeth (pontics counted as present teeth; tooth stumps/copings excluded); occlusal support classified by Eichner's index (Class A = all 4 support zones intact; Class B = partial loss, B1–B4; Class C = no posterior contact zones).
- **Psychological stress**: Stress Response Inventory (SRI, Ko et al., Korean-validated), 39 items across 7 subscales (tension, aggression, somatization, anger, depression, fatigue, frustration), 5-point Likert (0–4), global score 0–156.
- **Sleep/insomnia**: Insomnia Severity Index (ISI), Korean-validated (Cho et al. 2014), 5-point Likert (0–4) across items, total score 0–28; grade 0 (0–7) to grade 3 (22–28); clinically significant insomnia = ISI ≥15; remission threshold <8.
- **Statistics**: Kolmogorov–Smirnov normality test; independent t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum for continuous variables; chi-square/Fisher exact for categorical variables; logistic regression (adjusted for age, sex, Eichner's class) for group contributions to NRS/ISI/SRI outcomes, plus interaction-effect testing; significance at p<0.05; R v4.0.5.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Age: TMD group significantly younger than Prosthetic group.
- NRS: Prosthetic group significantly higher chewing discomfort than TMD group (p<0.001); severe discomfort (NRS≥7) in 36.3% of Prosthetic group vs 12.1% of TMD group.
- Sex distribution: TMD group 77.4% women / 22.6% men; Prosthetic group 60.6% women / 39.4% men (p<0.001).
- Missing posterior teeth: Prosthetic group mean 6.5±5.2 vs TMD group 1.5±3.1 (p<0.001).
- Eichner's classification: TMD group — A 85.9%, B 12.1%, C 2.0%; Prosthetic group — A 31.6%, B 47.2%, C 21.2% (p<0.001).
- ISI: TMD group significantly higher average ISI than Prosthetic group (p<0.001). Mild-to-severe insomnia (ISI≥8): Prosthetic 32.7% vs TMD 47.8%; clinically significant insomnia (ISI≥15): Prosthetic 9.9% vs TMD 13.6% (p=0.015).
- SRI: TMD group significantly higher than Prosthetic group in tension, anger, depression, fatigue, frustration subscales, and total score.
- Sex subgroup (TMD group): women higher than men on tension, somatization, fatigue, frustration, total SRI; no sex difference in severe chewing discomfort or clinical insomnia prevalence.
- Sex subgroup (Prosthetic group): women had higher clinical insomnia prevalence (13.7% vs 3.9% men) and higher frustration subscale (p=0.044); no sex difference in severe chewing discomfort.
- Logistic regression: Prosthetic group more susceptible to severe chewing discomfort (NRS≥7); TMD group more susceptible to clinically significant insomnia. Interaction effect: sex significantly affects severe chewing discomfort (p=0.041) — female prosthetic patients more susceptible than TMD patients.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Single study site (Pusan National University Hospital); limited generalizability across wider populations, economic/social conditions.
- Only one psychological tool (SRI) and one sleep tool (ISI) used; authors call for more multifactorial psychological/sleep assessment in future studies.
- Cross-sectional design — cannot establish causal direction between TMD/tooth loss and stress/insomnia.
- TMD group diagnosed via RDC/TMD axis I only (no DC/TMD axis II psychosocial sub-diagnosis), so TMD subtype (muscle vs joint) effects on stress/sleep are not separately analyzed.
- No control group without chewing difficulty — cannot quantify effect size vs an asymptomatic reference population (contrast with [[wiki/tmj/pigozzi-2021-quality-of-life-tmd-sr-ma]], which does have asymptomatic controls).

## 6. Related Work

- [[wiki/tmj/pigozzi-2021-quality-of-life-tmd-sr-ma]] — SR+MA showing TMD patients (vs asymptomatic controls) have significantly worse QoL (SMD 0.59–1.06); this paper's SRI findings add specific stress-subscale/insomnia granularity to that QoL burden, but without an asymptomatic control arm.
- [[wiki/tmj/valesan-2021-tmd-prevalence-sr-ma]] — SR+MA establishing TMD's 1.5–2× female predominance in painful TMD; this study's TMD group (77.4% women) is consistent with that pattern.
- Kurita et al. (cited in-text, not in wiki) — prior study on chewing-ability score (SCA) decline in TMD patients over age 50, cited as background for the age-related chewing decline this paper investigates further.
- Studies on gender/stress-reactivity differences in chronic pain and insomnia (Wilson et al., Dragioti et al., cited in-text) — background for the sex-stratified insomnia/stress findings.

## 7. Glossary

- **NRS (Numeric Rating Scale)**: 0–10 self-reported severity scale, here used for chewing discomfort.
- **SRI (Stress Response Inventory)**: Korean-validated 39-item, 7-subscale (tension, aggression, somatization, anger, depression, fatigue, frustration) psychological stress questionnaire, global score 0–156.
- **ISI (Insomnia Severity Index)**: validated insomnia questionnaire, total score 0–28; ISI ≥15 = clinically significant insomnia.
- **Eichner's index/classification**: occlusal-support classification (Class A/B/B1–B4/C) based on posterior tooth contact zones between antagonist arches.
- **RDC/TMD (Research Diagnostic Criteria for Temporomandibular Disorders)**: standardized axis I clinical diagnostic protocol used to diagnose the TMD group in this study.
- **Prosthetic group**: study cohort seeking prosthodontic treatment for tooth loss, free of TMD signs/symptoms (n=193).
- **TMD group**: study cohort referred for TMJ/masticatory-muscle pain-related discomfort, diagnosed via RDC/TMD (n=199).
