## Why Ingested

Foundational in-vitro investigation of the oxygen-inhibited layer (OIL) role in resin bonding — directly relevant to adhesive selection, especially self-etching all-in-one adhesives used with dual-cure or self-cure cements.

→ [[resin-bonding/suh-2004-oxygen-inhibited-layer-adhesion-dentistry]]

## Summary

Suh (2004) investigated whether the oxygen-inhibited layer (OIL) of cured resin composites is necessary for bonding with subsequent composite increments. Four experiments were conducted at Bisco Inc.: shear bond strength (BisCover/D/E Resin on Renew composite), microtensile bond strength (composite cured in air vs nitrogen), camphorquinone (CQ) decomposition by UV-Vis spectroscopy, and degree of conversion (DoC) by FTIR.

Key findings:
- Bond strength was NOT significantly different between samples with and without OIL
- CQ photoinitiator was depleted/decomposed within the OIL
- Degree of conversion of post-cured OIL was lower than control

Conclusion: OIL is not necessary for bonding with composite resin. However, with simplified self-etching all-in-one adhesives, the OIL is acidic, which interferes with self-curing reactions and renders these adhesives incompatible with self-cured or dual-cured composite cements without additional neutralization steps.

## Methods

1. Shear bond strength: BisCover and D/E Resin placed on Renew composite disks, cured with/without OIL. Light-Bond composite cured over resin in gelatin capsule; tested after 2h water soak at 37°C (Instron Model 4466).
2. Microtensile bond strength: Renew A2 substrates cured in air or nitrogen. Light-Core placed on each, sectioned into beams, stressed to failure.
3. CQ decomposition: Experimental CQ resin between glass plates irradiated at intervals, absorption spectrum measured (Cary 50 Bio UV-Vis Spectrometer).
4. Degree of conversion: Mylar-strip CQ resin pre-cured in air at different intervals, post-cured under nitrogen; FTIR (Spectrum 1000).

## Clinical Implication

Self-etching all-in-one adhesives leave an acidic OIL that inhibits the amine activator in self-cured composites → not compatible with dual-cure or self-cure resin cements without additional steps (separate bonding agent, oxygen-blocking layer, or dual-cure compatible adhesive).
