## Three-line Summary

Double-blind RCT (n=100, Kurdistan, 2024–2025) comparing conventional (pH ~4.2) vs NaHCO3-buffered (pH ~7.3–7.4) 2% lidocaine (1:100,000 epi) for supraperiosteal infiltration of maxillary posterior teeth with periapical lesions.

Buffered lidocaine showed significantly faster onset (2.3 vs 4.7 min, 51% faster), lower injection pain (VAS 2.8 vs 4.2), and longer anesthesia duration (45.8 vs 36.4 min, 26% longer); reinjection rates were not significantly different (4% vs 8%).

Bedside NaHCO3 buffering is a clinically simple intervention that significantly improves patient comfort and anesthetic efficiency for maxillary infiltration of infected teeth.

## 세줄요약

치근단 병소를 가진 상악 구치부 상악침윤마취에서 일반(pH ~4.2) vs NaHCO3 완충(pH ~7.3–7.4) 2% 리도카인(1:100,000 에피네프린)을 비교한 이중맹검 RCT (n=100, 쿠르디스탄, 2024–2025).

완충 리도카인은 발현 시간(2.3 vs 4.7분, 51% 단축)·주사 통증(VAS 2.8 vs 4.2)·지속 시간(45.8 vs 36.4분, 26% 연장) 모두 유의하게 우수했으며, 재주사율은 유의한 차이 없었다(4% vs 8%).

베드사이드 NaHCO3 완충은 임상적으로 간단하며 상악 침윤마취의 환자 편의와 마취 효율을 유의하게 개선한다.

## Summary

Double-blind RCT, n=100 (50/group), Kurdistan region, Sep 2024 – Apr 2025.
Group A: conventional 2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 adrenaline (pH ~4.2)
Group B: buffered 2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epi + 8.4% NaHCO3 (1:10 ratio; pH ~7.3-7.4)
Target: maxillary posterior teeth extraction with periapical lesions
Technique: supraperiosteal infiltration, 1.8mL, 27G, 1mL/min, single blinded surgeon

Results:
- VAS injection pain: B 2.8±0.7 vs A 4.2±0.9 (p<0.01)
- Onset: B 2.3 min vs A 4.7 min (p<0.01) — 51% faster
- Duration: B 45.8±7.6 min vs A 36.4±8.2 min (p<0.01) — 26% longer
- Reinjection: B 4% vs A 8% (p=0.678, NS)

Mechanism:
- Conventional lidocaine pH 4.2 → tissue irritation → nociceptor activation → injection pain
- Buffering to pH 7.4 → more uncharged base form → faster membrane penetration → faster onset
- Reduced acidic vasodilation → epinephrine maintains vasoconstriction longer → extended duration

## Clinical Implications

- Buffering protocol (0.18mL 8.4% NaHCO3 per 1.8mL carpule) is simple, low-cost, immediate
- Particularly beneficial for anxious patients and complex extraction cases (periapical pathology)
- ~2.4 minute faster onset is clinically meaningful when patient is anxious
- No difference in reinjection rate — buffering doesn't eliminate failure
- Study limited to maxillary infiltration; mandibular IANB data mixed in literature

## Limitations

- Maxillary posterior only; mandible and implants not studied (authors acknowledge)
- Fixed NaHCO3 ratio; optimal ratio unknown
- VAS only (no objective biomarkers)
- Single center
