## Why Ingested

This RCT directly compares a suture-free wound closure strategy (healing abutment attachment, HAA) against conventional suturing at implant placement and quantifies the effect on peri-implant soft tissue dimensions at 6 months — a practically important question given the routine morbidity of suturing during second-stage or simultaneous implant surgery. It complements existing [[suture-wound-closure/kumar-2022-suture-versus-sutureless-third-molar-impactions|sutureless vs suture evidence in third molar surgery]] with the first RCT evidence specifically targeting implant soft-tissue dimensional outcomes.

## Three-line Summary

This parallel-group RCT (n=30 patients; HAA: 15 patients/20 implants, Suture: 15 patients/15 implants) compared suture-free wound closure using a healing abutment attachment (HAA) versus conventional suturing at posterior maxillary and mandibular implant sites over a 6-month follow-up.
The HAA group showed significantly less KTW decrease (−0.46 ± 0.71 mm vs −1.23 ± 0.95 mm at implant level, p<0.05) and significantly greater STH increase (0.95 ± 0.47 vs 0.59 ± 0.67 mm, p<0.05), alongside dramatically shorter flap stabilization time (43.73 s vs 198.27 s, p<0.001).
Suture-free HAA wound closure is more time-efficient and better preserves buccal keratinized tissue width compared to conventional suturing at implant surgery sites.

## Key Contributions

1. **First RCT in the implant context** to directly compare suture-free HAA wound closure against conventional suturing, with peri-implant soft tissue dimensional outcomes (KTW and STH) as primary endpoints.
2. **KTW preservation**: The HAA approach significantly reduced buccal keratinized tissue shrinkage over 6 months (−0.46 vs −1.23 mm, p<0.05), which may reduce the need for subsequent soft-tissue augmentation.
3. **STH gain**: HAA produced a greater increase in supracrestal tissue height (0.95 vs 0.59 mm, p<0.05), suggesting a more favorable peri-implant mucosal profile.
4. **Surgical efficiency**: Flap stabilization was 4.5× faster with HAA (43.73 s vs 198.27 s, p<0.001), reducing intraoperative time and patient burden.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Parallel-group randomized controlled clinical trial
- **Sample**: 30 patients — HAA group (15 patients, 20 implants), Suture group (15 patients, 15 implants)
- **Sites**: Posterior maxillary and mandibular implant sites
- **Intervention (test)**: Suture-free wound closure using a healing abutment attachment (HAA) seated at the time of surgery to mechanically stabilize the flap without sutures
- **Control**: Conventional primary closure with sutures
- **Follow-up**: 6 months
- **Outcome measures**:
  - Keratinized tissue width (KTW) — measured at implant level
  - Buccal supracrestal tissue height (STH)
  - Surgery time for flap stabilization (seconds)
- **Measurement timing**: At surgery and at 6-month follow-up

## Key Results

| Outcome | HAA group | Suture group | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| ΔKTW (implant level) | −0.46 ± 0.71 mm | −1.23 ± 0.95 mm | p<0.05 |
| ΔSTH over 6 months | +0.95 ± 0.47 mm | +0.59 ± 0.67 mm | p<0.05 |
| Flap stabilization time | 43.73 s | 198.27 s | p<0.001 |

- HAA significantly reduced KTW loss compared to conventional suturing.
- STH increase was significantly higher in the HAA group.
- Suture-free approach was ~4.5× faster for flap stabilization.

## Limitations

- Small sample size (n=30 patients total); imbalance in implant count between groups (20 vs 15) suggests a non-matched allocation or a within-patient multi-implant design that is not fully described in the abstract.
- Abstract-only ingestion — full methodology, randomization sequence, allocation concealment, blinding procedures, and statistical power calculation are not available.
- Posterior maxillary and mandibular sites only; generalizability to anterior esthetic zones is not established.
- 6-month follow-up may not capture long-term stability of observed KTW/STH changes.
- Baseline tissue characteristics (thickness phenotype, initial KTW) are not reported in the available text.

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| KTW (Keratinised Tissue Width) | The mesiodistal dimension of keratinized mucosa around an implant, measured at the implant level; loss of KTW may compromise long-term peri-implant health |
| STH (Supracrestal Tissue Height) | The vertical height of soft tissue coronal to the implant platform or bone crest on the buccal aspect; increase reflects mucosal thickening/volume gain |
| HAA (Healing Abutment Attachment) | A suture-free wound closure method in which a healing abutment is placed at surgery to mechanically adapt and stabilize the mucosal flap without sutures |
| Suture-free wound closure | Wound management strategy that achieves flap stabilization without sutures, using a device (HAA, tissue adhesive, or membrane) to hold the flap |
| Keratinized tissue | Oral mucosa with a keratinized epithelium overlying a firm connective tissue base; at implant sites, adequate KTW (≥2 mm) is associated with better peri-implant health and plaque control |
