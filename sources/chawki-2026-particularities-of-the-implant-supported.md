## Why Ingested

이갈이(Bruxism)–임플란트 축은 위키에 이미 [[implants/survival/barboza-2026-bruxism-implant-failure-umbrella-review]](우산리뷰, 실패 오즈비(Odds Ratio, OR) 4.68)와 [[implants/survival/song-2021-implant-complications-bruxism-patients]](편집자 논평, late-failure 프레임)가 있으나, 둘 다 원자료 코호트의 실제 생존율·합병증 수치를 직접 펼친 페이지가 아니다. 본 Chawki 2026 체계적 문헌고찰(Systematic Review, SR; 20편·9,313명·31,209개 임플란트)은 이갈이군 임플란트 생존율 57.5–100%·기계적 합병증 최대 60%·보철 실패 최대 29.3%·야간 스플린트로 재료 마모 1.8배 감소라는 1차 근거 수치를 제공해 barboza 페이지의 방향성 결론에 층위를 더하고, [[occlusion/mojaver-2025-occlusal-overload-peri-implant-health-sr]]의 교합 과부하(Occlusal Overload)–병리 축과 보철물 생존 축을 잇는다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA, no meta-analysis) of 20 clinical cohorts (6 prospective, 14 retrospective; 9,313 patients, 31,209 implants; follow-up 6 months–36 years) comparing implant-supported prosthesis outcomes in bruxers vs non-bruxers.

Implant survival in bruxers ranged 57.5%–100% and prosthetic survival 70.6%–100% over 12–291 months, consistently below non-bruxers; mechanical complications reached 60% and prosthetic failure up to 29.3%, while regular protective-splint wear was associated with a 1.8-fold reduction in prosthetic material wear.

Clinical implication: treat bruxism as a significant risk factor warranting occlusal precautions (single-point occlusion, metal-ceramic/screw-retained design, delayed loading, hard night splint, annual maintenance); key limitations are purely observational designs, heterogeneous bruxism diagnosis, and an internal search-date conflict (20 Oct 2025 in abstract vs 20/06/2025 in Methods).

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰(Systematic Review, SR; PRISMA, 메타분석 없음) — 이갈이(bruxism) 환자의 임플란트 지지 보철 결과를 비이갈이군과 비교한 임상 코호트 20편(전향 6/회향 14; 9,313명·31,209개 임플란트; 추적 6개월~36년).

이갈이군 임플란트 생존율 57.5–100%·보철 생존율 70.6–100%(12~291개월)로 비이갈이군보다 낮았고, 기계적 합병증(Mechanical Complication) 최대 60%·보철 실패 최대 29.3%; 정기 보호 스플린트 착용은 보철 재료 마모를 1.8배 감소시킴.

임상적 의미 — 이갈이는 유의한 위험 인자이므로 단일점 접촉 교합·금속-세라믹/나사 고정 보철·지연 부하(Delayed Loading)·경성 야간 스플린트·연간 유지관리가 필요; 한계는 전량 관찰연구·이갈이 진단기준 이질성·검색일 내부 불일치(초록 2025-10-20 vs 방법 2025-06-20).

## 2. Key Contributions
- Direct numerical extraction from 20 primary cohorts: bruxer implant survival 57.5–100% and prosthetic survival 70.6–100% (12–291 months) — the primary-evidence layer beneath the held umbrella review's pooled OR.
- Head-to-head bruxer vs non-bruxer comparison across study subgroups: implant failure 13% vs 4.6% (Chrcanovic 2016, p=0.012), 25.5% vs 11.2% (Chrcanovic 2017, p=0.001), 42.53% vs 11.93% at 36 years (Chrcanovic 2018, p=0.001).
- Risk-factor ranking: in Papi 2017 bruxism was the **only** mechanical-risk variable significantly associated with implant failure (15%, p<0.05); bruxism combined with risky loading (c/i <0.8, angulation >25°, cantilever) yielded the worst success (69.23%, De Angelis 2017).
- Immediate loading caveat: failure in bruxers reached 29.3% (Ji 2012) and 41% (Glauser 2001) but only 0.97% in total-edentulous immediate loading (Ibañez 2005, 99.03% in bruxers) — full-arch immediate loading is the stated exception.
- Quantifies the splint benefit (1.8-fold lower prosthetic material wear) and turns the review into an explicit chairside recommendation set (occlusal scheme, Ti grade 4 over zirconia implants, delayed loading, screw retention, annual maintenance).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review per PRISMA; qualitative/narrative synthesis — no meta-analysis (no pooled effect estimate produced).
- **Databases**: Medline-PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Google Scholar; Boolean queries on dental implant/prosthesis + bruxism/parafunction/clenching/grinding/occlusal overload.
- **Search date**: abstract states last computer search 20 October 2025; Methods section states 20/06/2025 — internal conflict, both recorded verbatim (see Limitations).
- **Eligibility**: human clinical studies, English, follow-up ≥6 months; excluded case series, expert opinions, animal/cadaver, in vitro/in silico, reviews & meta-analyses. Dual independent screening (readers A.L, M.A) with consensus on disagreement.
- **PICO**: P = patients with bruxism; I = implant placement; C = bruxer vs non-bruxer complication/success rates; O = impact of bruxism on the implant-supported prosthesis. Success per Albrektsson criteria; survival = implant in situ at latest follow-up without removal indication.
- **Quality appraisal**: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS, good/fair/poor via AHRQ thresholds) + Cochrane Handbook ROBINS-I risk-of-bias domains.
- **n**: 1330 records screened → 277 full-text → 20 studies included (6 prospective cohorts, 14 retrospective cohorts); 9313 patients, 31,209 implants; follow-up 6 months–36 years.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Survival ranges (bruxers)**: implant survival 57.5%–100%; prosthetic survival 70.6%–100%; follow-up 12–291 months — consistently lower than non-bruxers.
- **Headline complication rates**: mechanical complications up to 60% (Brägger 2001: 6/10 bruxers vs 13/75 non-bruxers, p=0.01); abstract reports prosthetic failure up to 29.3% (body tables show 29.4% Chrcanovic 2020 full-arch and 34.8% Mikeli 2016 in bruxers — see Limitations).
- **Implant failure, bruxers vs non-bruxers**: Chrcanovic 2016 13% vs 4.6% (p=0.012); Chrcanovic 2017 25.5% vs 11.2% (p=0.001); Chrcanovic 2018 (291-month mean, 36 y cumulative survival 87.8%) 42.53% vs 11.93% (p=0.001); Chrcanovic 2020 (108-month follow-up) 5.7% vs 4% (p=0.279, ns); Chatzopoulos 2020 overall 1.7% with no bruxism association (p=0.881).
- **Implant fracture**: 3.6% bruxers vs 0.3% non-bruxers (Chrcanovic 2018) — 18.19× more frequent in bruxers.
- **Prosthetic/ceramic failure**: Mikeli 2016 34.8% vs 13.3% (p=0.002); Kinsel 2009 patient-level 34.9% vs 17.2% (p=0.01); Chitumalla 2018 by prosthesis type single crown 29%, partial 37%, complete 37% (p=0.012), bruxer male survival 90% (1 y) → 72% (5 y).
- **Full-arch (Chrcanovic 2020, 709 patients, 10.7 y)**: implant failure 25.35% vs 5.6% (p<0.001); prosthetic failure 29.4% vs 6.3% (p=0.001); hazard ratios — screw fracture 4.956, ceramic crack/fracture 5.685, loosening 3.302, resin tooth fracture 2.125.
- **Immediate loading**: Ibañez 2005 failure 0.97% (cumulative success 99.42%; bruxers 99.03% vs 100% non-bruxers); Ji 2012 bruxers 17/58 (29.3%) vs 11/239 (4.6%); Glauser 2001 bruxers 41% vs 12% (p=0.002).
- **Subtotal edentulism**: total-edentulous implant survival 74.65–100%, prosthetic survival 70.6–100%; posterior single crowns — Koenig 2019 bruxers 92.3% survival / 79.6% success; Mangano 2013 bruxers 87.7% prosthetic survival (differences vs non-bruxers ns).
- **Splint effect**: 1.8-fold higher prosthetic material wear risk without a splint (Kinsel 2009; Papaspyridakos 2019).
- **Quality**: NOS across the 10 survival-comparison studies — good 7, fair 1, poor 2; most studies at "uncertain" risk of bias for outcome measurement (evaluator blinding unreported).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Observational designs only (no RCTs — assigning patients to harmful exposure is deemed unethical) and **no meta-analysis**: no pooled effect size, so its weight sits below the held umbrella review (barboza-2026, OR 4.68 from one MA).
- Heterogeneous bruxism diagnosis (self-report, clinical signs, BiteStrip, EMG; several studies noted "no clear criteria for bruxism diagnosis") and wide follow-up range (6 months–36 years) preclude pooling.
- **Source-data internal conflicts reproduced verbatim**: (a) search date 20 Oct 2025 (abstract) vs 20/06/2025 (Methods); (b) abstract "prosthetic failure rates up to 29.3%" vs body table values 29.4% (Chrcanovic 2020) and 34.8% (Mikeli 2016) — likely conflation with Ji 2012's 29.3% implant-level failure.
- Case series were excluded by protocol, and NOS rated 2 of the 10 comparison studies "poor"; outcome-measurement bias flagged uncertain in most studies.
- Recommendations (e.g., avoid zirconia implants, Ti grade 4 preference) partly extend beyond what the included data directly tested.

## 7. Glossary
- **Bruxism**: repetitive masticatory-muscle parafunction (clenching, grinding, tapping) in awake and/or sleep states; prevalence 8–31.4%.
- **Implant survival vs success**: survival = implant still in situ at latest follow-up without removal indication; success per Albrektsson criteria.
- **Mechanical (technical) complication**: damage to implant components/superstructure — screw loosening, screw/implant fracture, ceramic chipping/fracture, resin tooth fracture.
- **Occlusal overload**: non-physiological occlusal force beyond implant–bone tolerance (bruxers ≈ 50 kgf vs 26.7–30.2 kgf in chewing/swallowing).
- **Protective (night) splint**: hard occlusal appliance redistributing parafunctional load; 1.8-fold lower prosthetic wear with regular use.
- **Crown-to-implant ratio (C/I)**: prosthetic crown height vs implant length; <0.8 flagged as risky loading factor.
- **Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS)**: cohort-study quality tool (selection/comparability/outcome stars; AHRQ good/fair/poor thresholds).
- **ROBINS-I**: Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for non-randomized studies.
