## One-line Summary

Retrospective cohort study with 1:1 propensity score matching (312 primary molars with diagnostically equivocal pulp status, 156 matched pairs per group) found pulpotomy (iRoot BP Plus) had significantly higher 24-month survival than pulpectomy (ZOE-iodoform) — 86.4% vs 67.1%, log-rank p<0.001; benefit concentrated in asymptomatic teeth (HR=0.42, p=0.002) with no significant difference in symptomatic teeth (HR=0.88, p=0.41); pulpotomy showed more internal resorption, pulpectomy more external resorption/periapical radiolucency.

## 한줄요약

성향점수매칭(PSM) 후향적 코호트 연구(치수 상태가 애매한 유구치 312개, 매칭 후 156쌍)에서 24개월 생존율이 pulpotomy(iRoot BP Plus군) 86.4% vs pulpectomy(ZOE-요오드폼군) 67.1%로 유의하게 높음(log-rank p<0.001); 무증상 치아에서만 pulpotomy 유리(HR=0.42, p=0.002), 유증상 치아는 차이 없음(HR=0.88, p=0.41); pulpotomy군은 내흡수, pulpectomy군은 외흡수·치근단 투과상이 더 흔함.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Pulpotomy vs. pulpectomy for equivocal pulp status in primary molars: a retrospective cohort study with propensity score matching
- **Authors**: Xiao F, Ren L, Xie L, Tian W, Zhang L
- **Journal**: BMC Oral Health, published 2026-01-10
- **DOI**: 10.1186/s12903-025-07635-2
- **PMID**: 41514405 / **PMCID**: PMC12903533
- **Setting**: Hefei Stomatological Hospital, China; single pediatric dentist (20 years' experience) performed all procedures, April 2021–March 2023
- **Study type**: Retrospective cohort with 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM)

## 2. Key Contributions

- First comparative study specifically targeting the diagnostic "gray zone" — primary molars with *equivocal* pulp status (prolonged bleeding 5–10 min, or inconclusive radiographic signs) — rather than clearly reversible or clearly irreversible pulpitis.
- Demonstrates a clinically important interaction: preoperative symptom status (asymptomatic vs symptomatic) modifies the pulpotomy-vs-pulpectomy treatment effect — pulpotomy's advantage is concentrated in asymptomatic equivocal cases.
- Characterizes distinct radiographic failure signatures by treatment: internal resorption predominates after pulpotomy; external resorption and periapical radiolucency predominate after pulpectomy.
- Identifies restoration/material quality as independent failure predictors (incomplete stainless steel crown sealing HR=3.12; ZOE usage HR=1.68) alongside treatment modality.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Sample**: 337 primary molars screened; 312 included after exclusions (incomplete records, insufficient follow-up, severe root resorption, systemic conditions raising infection/healing risk). Age 3–8 years.
- **Inclusion criterion**: borderline/equivocal pulp status — prolonged bleeding (5–10 min) after pulp exposure or inconclusive radiographic signs. Teeth requiring >10 min for hemostasis were excluded from pulpotomy eligibility and treated with pulpectomy instead (per Wolters' classification of irreversible pulpitis).
- **Treatments**:
  - Pulpotomy: iRoot BP Plus (Innovative BioCeramix Inc., bioceramic/calcium silicate material), under rubber dam, 2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine, high-speed bur/water spray, sterile saline irrigation, hemostasis with sterile cotton pellets.
  - Pulpectomy: ZOE–iodoform (modified ZOE–iodoform–clove oil) paste obturation.
  - Both groups: glass ionomer cement base, then stainless steel crown (SSC) restoration cemented with glass ionomer cement.
- **Propensity score matching**: 1:1 nearest-neighbor without replacement, caliper = 0.2 SD of logit. Covariates: age, gender, tooth location, caries severity (ICDAS II/III), presence of preoperative pain. Child cooperation level and parental oral-health awareness were NOT included (no standardized retrospective data).
- **Outcome assessment**: Three calibrated pediatric dental specialists; disagreements resolved by majority. Inter-examiner reliability κ=1.0 (clinical), κ=0.80 (radiographic). Success = no clinical symptoms AND no progressive pathological radiographic change vs baseline. Annual periapical radiographs (panoramic occasionally).
- **Statistics**: Kaplan–Meier survival analysis with log-rank test; univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression; subgroup analysis stratified by preoperative pain; sensitivity analysis excluding censored observations (exfoliated teeth, lost to follow-up).
- **Follow-up**: up to 36 months; mean follow-up 24.6 months.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Baseline balance after PSM**: 156 pairs; all covariates SMD <0.1, p>0.05 (age, gender, tooth location, caries severity, preoperative pain) — successful matching.
- **Overall survival (Kaplan–Meier, 36-month follow-up)**: pulpotomy significantly higher than pulpectomy (log-rank p<0.001).
  - 24-month survival: pulpotomy 86.4% (95% CI 81.2–91.6%) vs pulpectomy 67.1% (95% CI 60.3–73.9%).
- **Subgroup by preoperative pain**:
  - Asymptomatic teeth: pulpotomy 91.2% vs pulpectomy 74.5% survival; HR=0.42 (95% CI 0.24–0.73), p=0.002 — pulpotomy significantly better.
  - Symptomatic teeth: HR=0.88 (95% CI 0.49–1.58), p=0.41 — no significant difference.
- **Multivariate Cox regression**: treatment modality independently predicted survival — pulpectomy vs pulpotomy HR=0.783 (95% CI 0.615–0.997), p=0.047. Age, gender, tooth location NOT significant (all p>0.05).
- **Failure pattern analysis** (34 pulpotomy failures, 51 pulpectomy failures):
  - Clinical failure: similar between groups (44.1% vs 37.3%, p=0.49).
  - Internal resorption: pulpotomy 32.4% (11/34) vs pulpectomy 5.9% (3/51), p<0.01 — more common after pulpotomy.
  - External resorption: pulpotomy 8.8% (3/34) vs pulpectomy 33.3% (17/51), p<0.01 — more common after pulpectomy (text also cites 47.1% vs 13.6% elsewhere in Discussion).
  - Periapical radiolucency: 14.7% vs 23.5%, p=0.21 — not significant.
- **Restoration/material risk factors** (multivariate): incomplete SSC sealing HR=3.12; ZOE usage HR=1.68 — independent failure predictors.
- **Reliability**: inter-examiner agreement excellent for clinical evaluation (κ=1.00), good for radiographic (κ=0.80). Sensitivity analysis (excluding censored cases) confirmed robustness of survival estimates.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Retrospective design limits causal inference; single-center, single-operator (though this controls for technique variability, it limits generalizability).
- PSM reduces confounding but shrinks sample size and cannot adjust for unmeasured confounders (child cooperation, parental oral-health awareness were explicitly excluded from the model).
- Operational bleeding-time criterion (5–10 min) may not accurately reflect true histological pulp status.
- Mean follow-up (24.6 months) may be insufficient to capture long-term sequelae, including effects on permanent successor tooth development.
- Failure events may be underestimated due to loss to follow-up.
- Results are specific to iRoot BP Plus and ZOE-iodoform; authors note other calcium silicate cements (MTA, Biodentine) might perform comparably but this requires direct study.
- Authors call for prospective, multicenter RCTs with objective diagnostic tools (e.g., laser Doppler flowmetry) and longer follow-up to validate and refine clinical decision-making protocols.

## 6. Related Work

- AAPD guidance: pulpotomy recommended when hemostasis achieved within 5 minutes with no spontaneous pain or radiographic pathology.
- Histological literature cited: up to 30% of teeth clinically diagnosed with reversible pulpitis may have subclinical irreversible pulp changes — a rationale for why symptom/bleeding-time criteria under-discriminate near the equivocal boundary.
- Wolters' classification: bleeding requiring >10 minutes for hemostasis is treated as indicating irreversible pulpitis (used here as the pulpotomy exclusion threshold).
- Related wiki page: `[[wiki/endodontics/vpt/chawla-2026-pulpotomy-vs-pulpectomy-primary-teeth-sr-ma]]` — SR+MA (2 studies only, very low certainty) found no significant difference between pulpotomy and pulpectomy for *confirmed* irreversible pulpitis without swelling/periapical signs; this Xiao 2026 cohort instead targets the *diagnostically equivocal* zone and, with a much larger matched sample, finds pulpotomy superior overall and especially in asymptomatic equivocal cases — a complementary, arguably extending, finding for a related-but-distinct clinical scenario (uncertain diagnosis rather than confirmed irreversible pulpitis).

## 7. Glossary

- **Pulpotomy**: removal of the coronal (crown) pulp only, retaining vital radicular (root) pulp, followed by a therapeutic dressing/capping material.
- **Pulpectomy**: complete removal of coronal and radicular pulp tissue, followed by canal obturation (here, ZOE–iodoform paste).
- **iRoot BP Plus**: premixed bioceramic (calcium silicate) pulpotomy medicament; promotes odontoblastic differentiation, has antimicrobial activity and sealing ability.
- **ZOE–iodoform paste**: zinc oxide–eugenol combined with iodoform (and clove oil in this study's modified formulation), used as a resorbable pulpectomy filling material for primary teeth.
- **Propensity score matching (PSM)**: statistical technique that pairs treatment and comparison subjects with similar covariate profiles (via a summary "propensity score") to reduce confounding in non-randomized/retrospective data.
- **Standardized mean difference (SMD)**: covariate-balance metric after matching; <0.1 conventionally indicates adequate balance.
- **ICDAS II/III**: International Caries Detection and Assessment System severity codes; II/III denote advancing lesions extending into dentin with visible cavitation.
- **Internal vs external root resorption**: pathological resorption originating from the pulp/canal wall (internal) vs from the periodontal ligament/root surface (external); distinct radiographic failure signatures tracked in this study.
- **Stainless steel crown (SSC)**: full-coverage preformed metal crown used to restore primary molars after pulp therapy; sealing quality was an independent predictor of failure.
