## Why Ingested

The wiki's odontogenic sinusitis cluster is dominated by source-control-plus-FESS algorithms ([[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/albu-2025-treatment-landscape-odontogenic-sinusitis]], [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/lin-2024-odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-expert-consensus]]) and has no paper on a minimally invasive intraoral drainage-and-lavage option for acute/subacute cases. This small Korean case series fills that gap and pairs with the intraoral intentional-drainage approach of [[sinus-lift/lateral/park-2024-surgical-drainage-simultaneous-sinus-floor]].

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center case series (Seoul National University Bundang Hospital, Feb 2019-Nov 2023; n=7 adults, mean age 52.6±16.6 years) of acute/subacute odontogenic maxillary sinusitis treated by removing the causative tooth or implant, perforating the sinus through the socket with a 19-gauge needle, and irrigating with saline plus culture-guided antibiotics.

Mean 2.7±1.3 irrigation sessions (median 3, IQR 2-4) were needed to resolve symptoms; no oroantral fistula formed, and the authors report that additional surgery (FESS) was avoided, including cancellation of a planned otolaryngology operation in the illustrated case.

Suggests socket-based lavage as a less invasive step before endonasal surgery in non-chronic cases with a patent natural ostium, but n=7, no comparison group and no follow-up duration given in the text limit it to hypothesis-level support.

## 세줄요약

후향적 단일기관 증례군 (서울대학교 분당병원, 2019년 2월~2023년 11월; 성인 7명, 평균 연령 52.6±16.6세): 급성·아급성 치성 상악동염에서 원인 치아 또는 임플란트를 제거하고, 발치와를 통해 19-gauge 바늘로 상악동을 천공한 뒤 생리식염수로 세척하고 배양 기반 항생제를 병용.

세척 평균 2.7±1.3회 (중앙값 3회, 사분위 2-4회)로 증상이 해소되었고, 구강상악동루 (Oroantral Fistula, OAF)는 발생하지 않았으며, 저자들은 추가 수술(기능적 내시경 부비동수술, FESS)을 피할 수 있었다고 보고 (예시 증례는 계획된 이비인후과 수술 취소).

자연공 (natural ostium)이 개존된 비만성 증례에서 내시경 수술 전 단계로 덜 침습적인 선택지가 될 수 있으나, n=7·대조군 없음·추적기간 미기재로 가설 수준의 근거.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Describes a simple chairside protocol for acute/subacute odontogenic maxillary sinusitis: remove the causative tooth/implant, intentionally perforate the sinus through the socket with a 19-gauge needle, irrigate with saline under local anesthesia, repeat as needed, add culture-guided antibiotics.
- Reports a mean of 2.7±1.3 irrigation sessions to symptom resolution in 7 patients, with no oroantral fistula.
- Positions socket lavage as a possible step before endonasal surgery (FESS) when natural ostium function is presumed intact (saline was seen to flow through the nose in all patients).
- Proposes a practical stop rule: if saline injection does not irrigate or drainage does not improve after 2-3 irrigations, look for non-odontogenic causes and involve otolaryngology.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: retrospective case series, Helsinki-compliant, IRB-approved; patients treated Feb 2019-Nov 2023.
- **Inclusion**: (1) maxillary sinus lavage through the extraction socket, (2) age >19 years, (3) no previous maxillary sinusitis-related treatment or pathology, (4) dental treatment within 3 months or a dental lesion communicating with the sinus, (5) extraction of teeth judged non-restorable after endodontic/periodontal therapy.
- **Exclusion**: prior sinus-related treatment in otolaryngology, bilateral sinusitis on CBCT, prior FESS or lateral-wall sinus opening, history of sinus tumor or trauma.
- **Intervention**: surgical removal of the suspected tooth or implant (guided by CBCT); intentional sinus perforation through the surgical site with a 19-gauge needle to drain into the mouth; saline irrigation through that route with the patient saying "Ah" to reduce aspiration risk; empiric antibiotics immediately after extraction.
- **Follow-up irrigations**: every 3 days to 2 weeks depending on patient cooperation and discharge volume; culture and antibiotic sensitivity testing, then tailored antibiotics. Treatment ended when pus discharge, symptoms and radiographic sinusitis signs resolved; recurrence prompted otolaryngology consultation.
- **n**: 7 (5 males, 2 females; mean age 52.6±16.6 years).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Irrigation sessions: mean 2.7±1.3 (abstract/discussion); median 3 (IQR 2-4) (results). All treatments concluded after reduced sinus radiolucency on CT and symptom relief.
- No postoperative oroantral fistula in any patient. The authors chose repeated syringe irrigation over an indwelling oral drainage tube because of fistula risk.
- Saline injected through the socket was confirmed to emerge from the nose in all patients, taken as evidence of ostial patency.
- Illustrated case: 36-year-old male referred from otolaryngology before sinus surgery; pain and gingival swelling on percussion of tooth #26 with a periapical lesion and left maxillary sinus radiolucency. After extraction and needle perforation a large amount of pus drained. Cephalexin 1 g bid for 5 days was given first; culture grew Viridans streptococcus, Candida albicans and Staphylococcus aureus, with penicillin resistance, so ciprofloxacin 500 mg bid for 7 days followed. Irrigations every 2 weeks reduced pus markedly; CBCT showed complete resolution and the planned otolaryngology surgery was cancelled.
- Authors' comparison with the literature (cited from other studies, not generated here): in a review of 43 patients, 52% improved with medication plus dental treatment and 48% needed FESS (14% both dental treatment and FESS); Kim et al. found 79% of 19 post-implant-surgery odontogenic sinusitis patients required FESS; Wang et al. reported 33% FESS only and another 33% FESS plus dental surgery.
- Discussion statements on antibiotics (cited from other work): susceptibility highest for piperacillin (93.9%) and ampicillin (80%) with beta-lactamase inhibitors, then cefotaxime (78%), cefuroxime (69%), clindamycin (50%); suggested treatment duration at least 14 days or at least 7 days beyond symptom resolution.
- In the authors' Case 2, lavage alone without specific antibiotics led to resolution, which they interpret as mechanical lavage possibly outperforming systemic antibiotics alone (single observation).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Only 7 patients, single center, retrospective, no comparison group; follow-up duration after treatment end is not stated in the retrieved text.
- Pre-morbid ostium status and sinus function could not be assessed; patency is inferred from saline flowing through the nose.
- Limited to non-chronic cases without prior sinus history; not applicable to chronic ostial obstruction, where FESS may still be required.
- Cultures were performed "when needed" rather than universally; antibiotic regimens are described in detail for only one patient.
- The authors suggest the approach may reduce repeat imaging and patient burden, but this was not measured.

## 6. Related Work
- Lin et al. 2024 expert consensus: the authors state that it did not recommend dental surgical interventions for maxillary sinusitis and argue socket irrigation before endonasal surgery as a less invasive alternative.

## 7. Glossary
- **Odontogenic maxillary sinusitis**: sinusitis arising from dental infection or dental treatment (extraction, implant, endodontic material).
- **Acute / subacute**: symptoms lasting up to 4 weeks / 4-12 weeks (definition used in the paper).
- **FESS**: functional endoscopic sinus surgery; restores drainage via the natural ostium.
- **Extraction-socket lavage**: intentional sinus perforation through the socket with a 19-gauge needle followed by repeated saline irrigation.
- **Natural ostium**: the sinus's native drainage opening into the nose; its patency is the key premise of this approach.
