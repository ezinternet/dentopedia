## Why Ingested

→ [[resin/lima-2023-polywave-light-emitting-diodes-more]]

First systematic review directly comparing polywave vs monowave LED photoactivation for resin-based materials containing alternative photoinitiators (primarily TPO). Clinically relevant because alternative photoinitiators require broader-spectrum light activation; determines whether polywave LEDs are worth recommending when using TPO-containing materials.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 18 studies comparing polywave vs monowave LED curing of resin composites, adhesives, and cements containing alternative photoinitiators (mainly TPO). Polywave LED improved degree of conversion (DC) in 9/18 studies and microhardness in 7/18 studies; no significant differences in flexural strength between lamp types. Evidence quality is LOW due to high heterogeneity and risk of bias (11/18 studies high risk).

## Key Contributions

1. First SR to quantitatively summarize polywave vs monowave LED comparison specifically for alternative photoinitiator-containing resins
2. Demonstrates that polywave LED advantage is material-dependent and limited to DC and hardness—not mechanical strength
3. Identifies TPO (diphenyl-2,4,6-trimethylbenzoyl phosphine oxide) as the primary alternative photoinitiator studied
4. Provides evidence-based guidance that polywave LEDs provide partial—not universal—benefit for alternative photoinitiator resins

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review
- **Databases searched**: Not specified in abstract
- **Studies included**: 18 studies
- **Materials evaluated**: Resin composites, adhesives, and cements containing alternative photoinitiators
- **Primary alternative photoinitiator**: TPO (diphenyl-2,4,6-trimethylbenzoyl phosphine oxide)
- **Outcomes assessed**: Degree of conversion (DC), microhardness, flexural strength
- **Risk of bias**: 11/18 studies rated high risk of bias

## Key Results

| Outcome | Polywave improved vs Monowave | No significant difference |
|---|---|---|
| Degree of Conversion (DC) | 9/18 studies (50%) | 9/18 studies |
| Microhardness | 7/18 studies (39%) | 11/18 studies |
| Flexural strength | 0/18 studies | All studies |

- Overall DC improvement (polywave superior): 11 studies showed improvement
- Overall microhardness improvement (polywave superior): 7 studies
- Flexural strength: No differences between polywave and monowave across all studies
- Evidence quality: **LOW** (11/18 studies high risk of bias; high heterogeneity)

## Limitations

- High heterogeneity among included studies
- 11 out of 18 studies rated as high risk of bias
- Only abstract available; detailed study selection criteria not verifiable
- TPO was the primary photoinitiator studied; other alternative photoinitiators (e.g., Ivocerin, PPD) less represented
- Most included studies were in vitro; clinical relevance requires further investigation

## Glossary

- **Polywave LED**: Broad-spectrum LED curing light (typically 380–500 nm) activating multiple photoinitiators
- **Monowave LED**: Narrow-spectrum LED curing light (typically 440–480 nm), optimized for camphorquinone (CQ)
- **TPO (diphenyl-2,4,6-trimethylbenzoyl phosphine oxide)**: Alternative photoinitiator with absorption peak ~380–400 nm, requires UV/violet light for activation
- **Degree of Conversion (DC, 중합전환도)**: Percentage of C=C double bonds converted to C-C bonds during polymerization; higher = better cure
- **Microhardness (미세경도)**: Surface hardness measurement reflecting degree of polymerization
- **Flexural strength (굴곡강도)**: Resistance to bending deformation; mechanical property less affected by photoinitiator type
