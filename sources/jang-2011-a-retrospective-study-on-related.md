## Why Ingested

Adds a Korean public-hospital (Seoul Veterans Hospital) 10-year implant-level cohort with Kaplan-Meier survival by age, implant type/surface, length, location and prosthesis type, extending the general risk-factor literature already held, e.g. the classic cohort risk analysis → [[implants/survival/moy-2005-dental-implant-failure-rates-risk]] and the recent retrospective failure-factor study [[implants/survival/alrowis-2025-factors-affecting-dental-implant-failure-retrospective]].

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-institution cohort (Seoul Veterans Hospital dental center, Jan 2000-Dec 2009): 6,385 implants in 3,755 patients (3,120 men, 635 women; mean age 65 ± 10.58 years), mean observation 45.73 ± 12.48 months.

108 implants failed (cumulative survival 96.33% as reported); survival differed significantly by age, implant type/surface, length, location and prosthesis type (p < .05) but not by gender, diameter or bone quality; lowest survival in patients older than 79 years (66.67%), narrow (<3.75 mm, 92.39%) and short (<10 mm, 95.11%) implants.

Single-centre retrospective data with no multivariable adjustment, censoring-sensitive follow-up and operator learning-curve confounding (e.g. one early-era system with many failures) limit causal interpretation; smoking, grafting and loading protocol were not analysed.

## 2. Key Contributions
1. 10-year hospital cohort of 6,385 implants / 3,755 patients with Kaplan-Meier survival and chi-square (Bonferroni-corrected) comparisons across eight factors.
2. Early vs late failure split: 22 failures before prosthesis delivery vs 86 after loading (of 108).
3. Reports significant effects of age (p = .048), implant length, location and prosthesis type (p = .012), and non-significant gender (p = .059) and bone quality (p = .26).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Design: retrospective study; recall every 6 months; several surgeons and prosthodontists.
- Population: 3,755 patients; exclusions included immunosuppressive treatment, radio/chemotherapy, hormonal imbalance, osteoporosis, pregnancy, alcohol/drug addiction, psychiatric disease, intraoral chronic infection, immune disease and untreated periodontal disease. Systemic-disease patients had medical consultation first.
- Implants: eight implant types/surfaces; insertion torque at least 10 Ncm (measured with a surgical engine); GBR, ridge augmentation (staged), osteotome sinus floor elevation, bone-added OSFE or lateral-window sinus graft used as needed; immediate placement when feasible; immediate loading limited to the anterior region.
- Follow-up: prosthesis after at least 3 months of healing; recalls at 1 week, 6 months, 1 year after prosthesis, then annually with radiographs and plaque control. Periotest and Osstell used for stability evaluation.
- Factors analysed: age, gender, implant type/surface, diameter, length, location, bone quality (Lekholm and Zarb), prosthesis type. Not analysed: mucosal coverage, additional surgery, timing of placement, 3D position, prosthesis maintenance, occlusal scheme, operator.
- Failure definition: implant loss or removal, after Buser/Weber-type criteria (pain, suppuration, mobility, continuous radiolucency, non-restorability); survival time from placement to failure or last survey.
- Statistics: Kaplan-Meier, chi-square, Bonferroni-corrected significance (.05/n), odds ratios, SAS 9.2.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- 6,385 implants, 3,755 patients; 3,120 men (83.09%), 635 women (16.91%); age 18-88, mean 65 ± 10.58 years.
- Cumulative survival rate 96.33% as reported; 108 implants failed (99 in men, 9 in women). Mean observation 45.73 ± 12.48 months (men 42.69 ± 30.68, women 48 ± 28.47).
- Failure stage: 22 early (before prosthesis delivery) vs 86 late (after loading); the Discussion gives 20.37% vs 79.63%.
- Age: survival decreased with age (significant; p = .048); lowest in patients older than 79 years (66.67%).
- Gender: failure 4.59% in men vs 6.39% in women (p = .059, not significant); female survival 93.61%.
- Implant type: significant differences; Biohorizon RBT 99.55%, GS 99.53%, Camlog 97.34%, Astra 97.0%; Biohorizon RBM 93.74%, Paragon MTX 93.1%, Replace 94.81%; authors attribute differences partly to operator learning curve and period of use.
- Diameter (not significant): <3.75 mm 92.39%, 3.75-4.5 mm 96.90%, >4.5 mm 94.74%.
- Length: significant (p reported as 4.84×10 with exponent lost in extraction); short (<10 mm) 95.11%.
- Location: significant; best in mandibular posterior; failure rates maxillary posterior 6.27%, mandibular anterior 6.06%, maxillary anterior 5.63%, mandibular posterior 3.65%; mandible survival 95.15% vs maxilla 94.04%; maxillary posterior survival 93.73%.
- Prosthesis type: significant (p = .012); splinted highest, single crown lower (single implant 93.81%); overdenture (122 implants) failures occurred only in the mandible.
- Bone quality (not significant, p = .26): tendency to lower survival from type I to III (type III 94.45%); type IV only 112 implants.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Retrospective, single-centre, mostly male veterans (83%) and older patients; generalisability limited.
- No multivariable adjustment; factors analysed one at a time with chi-square, so confounding (e.g. implant system by era, operator experience, implants clustered within patients) is unaddressed.
- Internal inconsistency: 108 failures among 6,385 implants gives an implant-level failure of about 1.7%, which does not match the reported 96.33% cumulative survival (the authors do not explain the calculation); the Conclusion names "Paragon TSV" while the Discussion names "Paragon MTX". Numbers here are reproduced as printed.
- Survival-rate p-value for length is garbled in the text (exponent lost).
- Initial stability (RFA) not recorded; smoking, grafting, immediate placement/loading, systemic disease, operator and occlusion were excluded or not analysed. Small groups (type IV bone 112 implants; maxillary overdentures) limit power.
- Authors call for long-term, well-controlled prospective multicentre studies.

## 7. Glossary
- Cumulative survival rate: Kaplan-Meier proportion of implants not lost or removed over time.
- Early failure: loss between placement and prosthesis delivery; late failure: loss after functional loading.
- Lekholm and Zarb bone quality: types I-IV (I densest cortical, IV thin cortex with low-density trabecular bone).
- Bonferroni correction: significance threshold divided by the number of comparisons.
