## Why Ingested

No pooled SR+MA directly comparing All-on-4 vs All-on-6 survival, marginal bone loss (MBL), and complications across short-, mid-, and long-term follow-up horizons was held. Prior pages [[implants/full-arch/pandey-2023-comparison-all-on-four-all-on-six]] and [[implants/full-arch/la-monaca-2022-immediate-flapless-full-arch-rehabilitation]] provided cohort-level or pre-meta-analytic evidence; this 55-study SR+MA provides the first pooled quantitative comparison, grounding the full-arch decision heuristic in the wiki with meta-analysis-level survival numbers.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (55 studies, PubMed/WoS/Embase/Cochrane) comparing All-on-4 versus All-on-6 implant-supported fixed prostheses in edentulous patients across short-, mid-, and long-term follow-up.

Short-term (1 yr) survival: All-on-4 99.20%, All-on-6 100%; mid-term (1–5 yr): 99.66% vs 98.55%; long-term (≥5 yr): 98.14% vs 97.50%; MBL long-term: All-on-4 1.28 mm vs All-on-6 0.94 mm; mechanical complications 5.91% vs 6.64%; biological complications 6.54% vs 7.41%.

Both techniques show similar overall clinical outcomes; zirconia prostheses appear to reduce mechanical complications; high between-study heterogeneity limits the strength of all conclusions.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First large-scale SR+MA (55 studies) directly pooling All-on-4 vs All-on-6 survival, MBL, and complication rates across three follow-up horizons
- Quantifies the long-term MBL divergence: All-on-4 accumulates more bone loss by ≥5 years (1.28 mm) than All-on-6 (0.94 mm), despite similar implant survival
- Identifies zirconia prostheses as a mechanical-complication mitigation strategy
- Flags the guided vs freehand surgery comparison as underpowered — requires larger trials

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Study design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA-compliant)
- **Databases**: PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane Library
- **Included studies**: 55
- **Population**: Edentulous patients (maxilla/mandible, not reported separately in abstract)
- **Outcomes**: Implant cumulative survival rate (CSR), marginal bone loss (MBL), mechanical complications, biological complications
- **Follow-up strata**:
  - Short-term: ~1 year
  - Mid-term: 1–5 years
  - Long-term: ≥5 years
- **Heterogeneity**: Described as high — a primary caveat on all pooled estimates

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Implant Survival Rate (Cumulative)

| Follow-up | All-on-4 | All-on-6 |
|---|---|---|
| Short-term (~1 yr) | 99.20% | 100% |
| Mid-term (1–5 yr) | 99.66% | 98.55% |
| Long-term (≥5 yr) | 98.14% | 97.50% |

### Marginal Bone Loss (MBL)

| Follow-up | All-on-4 | All-on-6 |
|---|---|---|
| Short-term | 0.77 mm | 0.85 mm |
| Long-term (5 yr) | 1.28 mm | 0.94 mm |

### Complications (Pooled Rates)

| Complication type | All-on-4 | All-on-6 |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanical | 5.91% | 6.64% |
| Biological | 6.54% | 7.41% |

- Zirconia framework prostheses associated with reduced mechanical complications
- No statistically significant difference between techniques reported overall

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **High heterogeneity** across included studies — all pooled estimates must be interpreted with caution; this is the dominant limitation
- Guided vs freehand surgery subgroup insufficiently powered for valid comparison
- Abstract-level extraction; full subgroup analyses, funnel plots, GRADE appraisal not retrievable
- Publication bias not assessed from abstract alone

## 7. Glossary

- **All-on-4**: Full-arch fixed prosthesis concept using 4 implants (2 axial anterior, 2 posterior tilted up to 45°) per jaw
- **All-on-6**: Full-arch fixed prosthesis concept using 6 implants per jaw
- **CSR (Cumulative Survival Rate)**: Proportion of implants surviving at a given follow-up time point
- **MBL (Marginal Bone Loss)**: Radiographic peri-implant bone loss measured from implant platform
- **Mechanical complications**: Prosthesis fracture, screw loosening, implant fracture, veneer chipping
- **Biological complications**: Peri-implantitis, soft tissue inflammation, fistula
