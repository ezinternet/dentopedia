## Three-line Summary


Retrospective cross-sectional study (n=445 teeth, Hong Kong postgraduate endodontic clinic, 2019-2024) finding 5.6% prevalence of cemental tear and four independent risk factors (incisor tooth type, tooth mobility, severe crestal bone loss, adequate root filling quality) on multivariate logistic regression, with PR sensitivity only 0.6 vs CBCT.

## 1. Document Information

International Endodontic Journal, 2025;59(1):57-79. Retracted status: none — active publication. Funding acquisition: C.F. Zhang. No conflicts of interest declared. IRB approval: University of Hong Kong (UW 20-447), Declaration of Helsinki compliant, PROBE reporting guideline.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study to combine both periapical radiograph (PR) AND high-resolution small-FOV CBCT in the same cohort to estimate cemental tear prevalence, finding 5.6% — nearly 3x higher than prior PR-only or larger-FOV CBCT studies, because PR alone missed 40% of confirmed cases.
- First multivariate (not just univariate) risk-factor model for cemental tear: tooth type, mobility, crestal bone loss severity, and root filling quality remain independently significant after adjustment.
- Applies the Lee et al. (2021) Class(1-6)/Stage(A-D) classification prospectively to a consecutive clinical cohort, showing real-world distribution (76% Class 2/4, 76% Stage C/D) rather than case-report-derived estimates.
- Reports PR diagnostic accuracy against CBCT as reference standard (sensitivity 0.60, specificity 1.00, PPV 1.00, NPV 0.98) — the first such quantification for cemental tear detection.
- Surfaces a novel, previously unreported association: adequate (vs inadequate) root canal filling quality is itself a significant risk factor (OR 8.38), hypothesized to relate to obturation-technique-induced thermal/mechanical stress on the root.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Design: retrospective, observational, cross-sectional. Setting: University Postgraduate Endodontic Clinic, Hong Kong, 1 Sept 2019 – 31 March 2024. Population: 575 consecutively referred patients screened → 445 patients/teeth met inclusion (one tooth per patient, to avoid clustering). Sample size powered for anticipated 5% prevalence (pilot data) at 95% CI / 2% precision. Data source: clinical notes + PR + reconstructed CBCT (Veraview X800, 40x40mm FOV, ENDO mode, 0.08mm voxel, 1.5mm reformatted slices). Single assessor (endodontist, >25y experience), intra-observer reliability re-check on 10% of cases at 3 months (Cohen's Kappa 1.0). 30 candidate factors (patient-, jaw-, tooth-, endodontic-, periodontal-related) tested by Pearson chi-squared/exact univariate tests, then significant factors entered into backward-stepwise multivariate logistic regression (α=0.05). PR-vs-CBCT diagnostic accuracy computed with CBCT as reference standard.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Prevalence: 25/445 (5.6%).
- Multivariate risk factors (all p<0.05): tooth type (molars OR 0.08 vs incisors, 95% CI 0.02–0.34, p<0.001); mobility (Grade 1 OR 26.87 [5.93–121.81]; Grade 2 OR 18.69 [1.23–283.50]; Grade 3 OR 48.27 [2.33–1000.44], all vs no mobility); crestal bone loss (severe ≥70% OR 10.83 [2.85–41.21] vs mild ≤30%); root filling quality (adequate OR 8.38 [1.73–40.69] vs inadequate).
- Classification: 76% Class 2 or 4; 76% Stage C or D (i.e. most cases involve extensive apico-marginal bone loss across 3+ root surfaces).
- Demographics: mean age 58.7y, no significant age/gender association (concordant with prior prevalence studies).
- 72% of cases had RCT initiated/completed at referral.
- PR diagnostic accuracy vs CBCT: sensitivity 0.60, specificity 1.00, PPV 1.00, NPV 0.98, reliability 0.98 — 10/25 cases (40%) detectable only on CBCT.
- Direct inspection/histopathologic gold-standard confirmation achieved in only 60% (15/25) of cases.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Retrospective design with documentation-dependent case ascertainment (likely underestimates true prevalence); occlusal interference — a mechanistically plausible factor — could not be analyzed due to sparse charting; substantial missing data for trauma history (72% unspecified, borderline-significant p=0.073, raising Type II error concern), bruxism, hard diet, and mobility fields; single assessor despite excellent intra-observer reliability; only 60% of cases confirmed by direct inspection/histopathology rather than all; CBCT's limited FOV may have missed cemental tears in neighbouring teeth outside the imaged field. Authors flag two follow-on studies in progress: PR/CBCT diagnostic accuracy against histopathology as reference standard, and multi-observer diagnostic-accuracy comparison.

## 6. Related Work

Builds directly on Lee et al. 2021 (Int Endod J, cemental tear classification proposal) and contrasts with the two prior prevalence studies — Keskin & Güler (PR-only, 0.89%) and Özkan & Özkan (larger-FOV/lower-resolution CBCT, 1.9%) — attributing its higher 5.6% figure to combined-modality, higher-resolution imaging. Cross-references Lin et al. (2011, 2012, 2014) case-series work on clinical/histopathologic characteristics and treatment outcome by apicocoronal location that underlies the Liang et al. 2025 consensus statement already in this wiki.

## 7. Glossary

- **CDJ**: cemento-dentinal junction, the histologic weak point where most cemental tears separate.
- **FOV**: field of view (CBCT imaging parameter).
- **PR**: periapical radiograph.
- **Class (1-6) / Stage (A-D)**: Lee et al. (2021) classification of cemental tear by apico-marginal bone-loss pattern (Class) and number of root surfaces affected (Stage).
- **PPV/NPV**: positive/negative predictive value.
