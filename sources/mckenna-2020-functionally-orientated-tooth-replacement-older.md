## Three-line Summary

Primary Dental Journal clinical review arguing that the growing population of older adults who retain natural teeth into old age requires treatment planning that manages chronic caries and periodontal disease alongside tooth replacement, rather than defaulting to conventional prosthetic replacement.

Large numbers of these patients currently receive removable partial dentures, but functionally orientated tooth replacement following shortened dental arch principles can, in correctly chosen cases, deliver an acceptable, functional and esthetic reduced dentition.

The stated advantage is not primarily clinical outcome but **maintenance burden**: the approach significantly reduces ongoing demands on both patient and clinician and is therefore described as ultimately more cost-effective than removable alternatives.

## 세줄요약

Primary Dental Journal 임상 리뷰: 자연치를 노년까지 보유하는 고령 인구가 늘면서 임상가는 **만성 우식·치주질환 관리와 결손부 수복을 동시에** 다뤄야 하며, 통상적 보철 대체로 기본값을 두어서는 안 된다고 주장한다.

이 환자군의 상당수가 현재 가철성 국소의치 (Removable Partial Denture, RPD)를 받고 있으나, 단축치열 (Shortened Dental Arch, SDA) 원칙에 따른 **기능지향 수복 (functionally orientated tooth replacement)** 은 적절히 선택된 케이스에서 수용 가능하고 기능적이며 심미적인 축소 치열을 제공할 수 있다.

내세우는 장점은 임상 결과가 아니라 **유지관리 부담**이다 — 환자와 임상가 양쪽의 지속적 부담을 유의하게 줄이며, 따라서 가철성 대안보다 궁극적으로 더 비용효과적이라고 서술한다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Frames the RPD decision **demographically**: an ageing, increasingly dentate population changes what "replacing missing teeth" should mean.
- Names the default it is arguing against — large numbers of older patients currently receiving RPDs.
- Shifts the argument for SDA from clinical equivalence to **maintenance burden**, a dimension the trial literature does not measure.
- Frames cost-effectiveness as arising from reduced ongoing maintenance rather than lower initial fee.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative clinical review in a primary-care journal. No search strategy, inclusion criteria or risk-of-bias appraisal reported. Structured around the demographic shift, the challenges of managing chronic dental disease alongside replacement in older adults, and functionally orientated tooth replacement as an alternative approach.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Narrative review with no quantitative content.** Every claim above is qualitative; nothing here can be cited as a number.
- The load-bearing qualifier is **"in correctly chosen cases"**, and the abstract does not state what those criteria are — for an operational ladder use Witter's classification in [[wiki/removable-partial-denture/nassani-2013-fesrpd-shortened-dental-arch-reappraisal]] instead.
- The maintenance-burden and cost-effectiveness claims are attributed to unnamed evidence not recoverable from the abstract.
- UK primary-care framing; maintenance burden and cost-effectiveness are health-system dependent and do not transfer directly to Korean practice.
- Guidance article, not primary research — appropriate as framing and as a reminder of the demographic shift, not as an evidence citation.

## 7. Glossary

- **Functionally orientated tooth replacement (기능지향 수복)**: replacing only the units needed for acceptable function rather than restoring the complete arch.
- **SDA (단축치열, Shortened Dental Arch)**: deliberately ending the arch at the premolars.
- **Maintenance burden (유지관리 부담)**: cumulative demand a restoration places on patient and clinician over its lifetime — repairs, relines, recalls, hygiene.
- **Partially dentate (부분 유치악)**: retaining some natural teeth; the growing older-adult category this paper addresses.
