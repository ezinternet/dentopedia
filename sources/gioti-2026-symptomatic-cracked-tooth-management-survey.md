## One-line Summary
International cross-sectional survey (n=427 dentists; Greece, UK, Europe); periodontal burden is the dominant extraction trigger; CBCT use did not independently affect diagnosis or treatment planning.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Largest international survey on cracked tooth clinical decision-making practice
- Periodontal status (probing depth, bone loss) is the single most important extraction trigger (p<0.001)
- CBCT use was NOT associated with changed diagnosis or treatment planning — diagnostic value questioned
- Significant management heterogeneity observed — no evidence-based guidelines exist yet
- Most dentists (79.5%) use magnifying devices; history taking equally prevalent
- Bruxism/occlusion (95.2%) and metal restorations (92.4%) are universally recognized risk factors

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Study design: international cross-sectional electronic survey
- Sample: 427 fully completed questionnaires (dentists in Greece, UK, other European countries)
- Content validity: 6-expert panel; test-retest reliability (n=20 dentists)
- Content: demographics, diagnostic methods, treatment approaches, 4 clinical scenarios
- Statistical analysis: descriptive + multivariate; p<0.05

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Diagnostic methods used most frequently:
  - History taking: 79.5%
  - Magnifying devices: 79.5%
  - Clinical examination: 75.9%
  - Tooth slooth bite test: 69.6%
- Risk factor associations by dentists:
  - Bruxism / occlusal disorders: 95.2%
  - Presence of metal restorations: 92.4%
- Periodontal burden → most important extraction predictor (multivariate, p<0.001)
- CBCT use: NOT associated with changed diagnosis or treatment plan (p<0.05 [not significant])
- Case frequency: majority reported 1–5 cracked tooth cases per month
- Treatment variability: highest in uncertain prognosis scenarios

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Self-reported survey — actual clinical behavior may differ
- Convenience sample skewed toward European dentists (Greece, UK); generalizability limited
- Scenarios were hypothetical; real-time clinical decisions may vary
- No validated cracked tooth classification system was tested
- Highlights need for evidence-based international clinical guidelines

## 6. Related Work
- Echoes existing literature linking periodontal involvement to poor cracked tooth prognosis
- CBCT utility finding is provocative — prior studies suggested diagnostic benefit but this survey questions clinical translation

- Tooth slooth: bite test device to localize pain to individual cusps/cracks
- CBCT: Cone Beam Computed Tomography
