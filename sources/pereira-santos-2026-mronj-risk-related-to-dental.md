## Why Ingested

[[drug/mronj/li-2024-antiresorptive-implants-mronj-sr]] covers antiresorptive drugs broadly (BPs + denosumab) for osseointegration outcomes, and [[drug/mronj/dahiya-2024-antiresorptive-drugs-osteonecrosis-dental-implants]] critically appraises the antiresorptive–implant literature, but neither isolates denosumab monotherapy in osteoporosis nor applies the implant surgery-triggered (ISTO) vs implant presence-triggered (IPTO) osteonecrosis distinction. This PROSPERO-registered systematic review (CRD42017067170) fills that gap: 10 studies, 8220 patients, with the headline count that among 366 denosumab-treated patients undergoing implant procedures, 18 developed MRONJ involving 24 implants — the first denosumab-specific quantification of the implant–MRONJ interface.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42017067170, PRISMA 2020) of 10 studies (1 RCT, 2 retrospective cohorts, 1 case-control, 6 case series; 8220 patients, mean age 71.45 ± 6.30 yr, mean follow-up 91.3 mo) assessing MRONJ risk around dental implants in osteoporosis patients treated with denosumab (DEN).

Heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis (narrative synthesis only); in the comparative pool of 5428 examined patients (5043 osteoporotic, 366 on DEN with implants, 1817 implants analyzed), 18 osteoporotic DEN patients developed implant-related MRONJ involving 24 implants; reported implant failure rates under DEN ranged 6%–100% across series (Seki case-control: 11.2% antiresorptive vs 2.9% control).

Observed cases fit an IPTO (implant-presence-triggered) pattern more than ISTO (surgery-triggered) — Seki 2025 and Massaad & Magremanne 2022 reported all cases as IPTO, while the one ISTO-oriented series (Otto 2023) reported zero MRONJ; BP→DEN transition patients showed higher incidence and post-surgical recurrence, supporting the conclusion that implant placement under osteoporosis-dose DEN is not contraindicated but failed/peri-implantitis-inflamed implants can act as local necrosis triggers.

이질성으로 메타분석 불가(서술적 종합만); 비교 풀 5428명(골다공증 5043·데노수맙+임플란트 366명·임플란트 1817개) 중 **골다공증 데노수맙 환자 18명이 임플란트 관련 MRONJ 발생(24개 임플란트)**; 데노수맙 하 임플란트 실패율은 증례군 간 6%~100% 편차(Seki 환자대조군: 항흡수제군 11.2% vs 대조군 2.9%).

## 2. Key Contributions

- First denosumab-specific systematic review of the implant–MRONJ interface in osteoporosis: 10 studies, 8220 patients, 1823 implants reported in study characteristics (1817 in the comparative pool).
- Headline quantitative anchor: **18 of 366 DEN-treated implant patients developed MRONJ, involving 24 implants** — low absolute incidence but high enough to warrant caution.
- Applies the **IPTO vs ISTO trigger taxonomy** systematically: observed peri-implant MRONJ cases consistently fit an implant-presence-triggered pattern (pre-existing osseointegrated implants with peri-implant inflammation), while the sole surgery-focused series reported no MRONJ — reframing the implant question from "is placement safe?" to "is implant survival/maintenance safe?"
- Compiles implant failure rates under DEN (6% Cheng to 100% Tempesta; 42.4% Escobedo; <5% Smith; 0% Otto; Seki 11.2% vs 2.9% control) and peri-implantitis prevalence (Seki: 18.2% of implants, 13.1% of patients).
- Confirms the BP→DEN sequential-therapy risk signal in the implant context: transition patients had higher MRONJ incidence and higher post-surgical recurrence (Voss 2018), consistent with oncology-cohort data (Loyson 2018; Srivastava 2021).
- Positions results against population benchmarks: Watts 2019 RCT 5.2 per 10,000 patient-years overall MRONJ incidence on DEN; duration-dependent risk 0.04%/3 yr, 0.06%/5 yr, 0.44%/10 yr (Nicolatou-Galitis 2019).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Registration/design**: PROSPERO CRD42017067170; PRISMA 2020-guided systematic review with narrative (descriptive) synthesis — meta-analysis explicitly precluded by methodological heterogeneity.
- **Quality appraisal by design**: ROBINS-I referenced; Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for retrospective cohorts (Cheng 8/9 stars, Voss 7/9 — good quality); JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for case series and case-control; RoB 1 for the RCT (moderate risk/moderate quality). Two independent assessors (RS, KR) with third-party adjudication (MB).
- **Included studies (n=10)**: Watts 2019 (RCT, n=4550); Cheng 2022 (retrospective cohort, n=3486); Voss 2018 (retrospective, n=52); Seki 2025 (case-control, n=61); six case series — Bagan 2016 (n=10), Escobedo 2020 (n=7), Massaad & Magremanne 2022 (n=6), Otto 2023 (n=16), Smith 2022 (n=13), Tempesta 2022 (n=19).
- **Only one study rated low quality/high risk of bias** (Bagan 2016, JBI) — retained for clinical relevance given scarce literature.
- **Extraction framework**: study type, patient/implant counts, IPTO vs ISTO classification, implant timing relative to DEN start, BP→DEN transition status, exposure duration/doses (doses back-calculated from 6-monthly injection schedule where unreported), antibiotic prophylaxis, staging, site, treatment, resolution.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **PRISMA outcome**: 10 studies included; significant heterogeneity → no meta-analysis.
- **Cohort descriptors**: 8220 patients total, majority female, mean age 71.45 ± 6.30 yr, mean follow-up 91.3 months (range up to 250 months, Cheng).
- **Comparative pool (Table 6)**: 5428 patients examined; 5043 osteoporotic; 366 on DEN undergoing implant procedures; 1817 implants analyzed (Cheng contributing 1472; Bagan a single implant); 140 total MRONJ cases across conditions; **18 MRONJ cases in osteoporotic DEN patients with implants, involving 24 implants**.
- **Trigger pattern**: 7 of 10 studies reported implant-associated MRONJ; 1 reported none (Otto 2023); 1 NR (Watts 2019). All cases in Seki and Massaad & Magremanne were IPTO; observed PI-MRONJ across studies fits an IPTO pattern; ISTO risk could not be estimated (no events).
- **Implant failure under DEN**: 6% (Cheng) – 100% (Tempesta), incl. 42.4% (Escobedo), <5% (Smith), 0% (Otto); Seki: 11.2% (ART group) vs 2.9% (control). Massaad: 14 explantations, none in DEN users.
- **Peri-implantitis**: Seki 18.2% (35/192 implants), 13.1% (8/61 patients); Smith reclassified initial peri-implantitis diagnoses as MRONJ.
- **Transition (BP→DEN)**: higher incidence (Smith: BP 48–72 mo then DEN; Escobedo: BP 24–60 mo, DEN 18–24 mo) and higher recurrence after surgery (Voss 2018 — the only study reporting higher recurrence under DEN in osteoporotic patients); DEN exposure in cases spanned 12–192 months, so short exposures were not protective.
- **Benchmarks cited**: Watts 2019 — 5.2 per 10,000 patient-years (DEN, up to 10 yr); Jung 2022 — 4.1%; Nicolatou-Galitis 2019 — 0.04% (3 yr), 0.06% (5 yr), 0.44% (10 yr); Stavropoulos 2018 — implant presence triggered ~30% of MRONJ, installation/explantation ~16%.
- **Treatment**: surgical management (debridement/sequestrectomy/partial maxillectomy) yielded better outcomes than conservative (Cheng, Tempesta all healed; Bagan conservative case not cured).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No meta-analysis possible** — heterogeneity in methodology, populations, and reporting; all pooled counts are descriptive, not pooled estimates.
- **Dominance of low-level designs**: 6 of 10 studies are case series; only 1 RCT (which did not report implant counts) and 2 cohorts; one included series was low quality/high risk of bias (Bagan 2016).
- **Trigger ambiguity**: most studies did not differentiate implants placed before, during, or after DEN initiation; variable implant numbers and follow-up preclude true risk estimation.
- **Unreported confounders**: smoking, corticosteroids, anticoagulants inconsistently reported; comorbidity/MRONJ links not formally tested; antibiotic prophylaxis rarely documented (only Otto 2023 detailed: amoxicillin/clavulanate 875/125 mg or clindamycin 600 mg 1 d preop + 5–7 d postop, 0.2% chlorhexidine).
- **Gaps flagged for research**: anatomical location, pre-existing radiographic osteosclerosis/marginal bone loss, and bone volume as predisposing factors; standardized IPTO/ISTO reporting; minimum dose/exposure thresholds before MRONJ onset.

## 7. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| DEN | Denosumab — anti-RANKL monoclonal antibody; osteoporosis dose 60 mg SC q6mo; not deposited in bone, effects wane after discontinuation (half-life ~1 month) |
| MRONJ | Medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw — exposed/probeable jaw bone >8 wk in antiresorptive-exposed patients without radiation/metastasis |
| ISTO | Implant surgery-triggered osteonecrosis — MRONJ directly related to the implant placement procedure |
| IPTO | Implant presence-triggered osteonecrosis — MRONJ arising around already-osseointegrated implants, likely via pre-existing peri-implant inflammation |
| PI-MRONJ | Peri-implant MRONJ — necrosis presenting as peri-implantitis-like lesions; may persist with the implant remaining osseointegrated |
| Transition therapy | Sequential treatment: bisphosphonate course followed by denosumab; associated with higher MRONJ incidence and post-surgical recurrence |
| NOS / JBI / RoB 1 | Quality appraisal tools: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (cohorts), JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist (case series/case-control), Cochrane RoB 1 (RCTs) |
