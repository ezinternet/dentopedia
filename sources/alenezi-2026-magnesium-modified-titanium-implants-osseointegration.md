## Why Ingested

The wiki already holds an umbrella review of coating chemistries ([[implants/surface/beckwith-2024-implant-coatings-affect-healing]] — calcium phosphate/HA, bisphosphonate, bioactive glass) that found only weak, heterogeneous evidence for coating benefit; this paper adds a quantitative meta-analysis for a coating chemistry (magnesium) not covered there, with a much sharper statistical signal (BIC Z=4.38, p<0.001) than the "weak evidence" verdict on HA/CaP — worth holding both to see whether Mg is a genuine outlier or whether the stronger signal reflects a smaller, more homogeneous preclinical (animal-only) literature versus beckwith's mixed clinical SR base.

## Three-line Summary
Systematic review and meta-analysis (INPLASY202610101) of 11 preclinical animal studies (rabbit/rat, tibial/femoral sites, healing 2–24 weeks) comparing magnesium-modified/Mg-coated titanium implants against uncoated titanium controls, across four Mg-delivery strategies (ion implantation, Mg-substituted HA coatings, Mg-loaded mesoporous titania, Mg-incorporated nanotubes).
Pooled bone-to-implant contact (BIC) was significantly higher for Mg-modified implants (Z=4.38, p<0.001); pooled bone area (BA) showed no significant difference (Z=0.93, p=0.35), though a trend toward higher BA appeared in studies with ≥8-week healing.
Mg incorporation appears to accelerate early-stage contact osteogenesis (BIC) more reliably than it changes overall peri-implant bone volume (BA); all evidence is small-animal, non-loaded, and preclinical — no human/clinical data yet exist for Mg-modified implants.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First quantitative meta-analysis (not just narrative synthesis) of magnesium-modified titanium implant osseointegration across preclinical animal studies.
- Separately pools two distinct histomorphometric outcomes (BIC vs. BA), showing they respond differently to Mg modification — BIC significant, BA not.
- Surveys four distinct Mg-delivery/coating strategies (ion implantation, Mg-substituted HA coatings, Mg-loaded mesoporous titania, Mg-incorporated nanotubes) and discusses how differing Mg release kinetics may explain outcome heterogeneity.
- Applies dual quality/bias appraisal (ARRIVE 2.0 for reporting quality, SYRCLE for risk of bias) specific to animal-research methodology, rather than a generic clinical-SR tool.
- Frames Mg's proposed mechanism via Wnt/β-catenin and VEGF/HIF-1α pro-osteogenic pathways and M2 macrophage immunomodulation, tying preclinical histomorphometric results to plausible molecular mechanisms.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis of preclinical (animal) studies, PRISMA-guided, prospectively registered (INPLASY202610101).
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science; search conducted May 2025; animal studies published from 2005 onward; English-language only.
- **n**: 11 included studies (rabbit/rat models; tibial/femoral implant sites; healing durations 2–24 weeks).
- **Inclusion criteria**: Mg applied locally (coating or releasable surface agent) to endosseous Ti implants, with an uncoated/Mg-free control group of otherwise similar surface characteristics; quantitative bone outcomes reported (BIC, BV/TV, or BA).
- **Quality/bias tools**: ARRIVE 2.0 (21-item reporting-quality checklist, scored 0/1/2 per item, categorized excellent 0.8–1 / average 0.5–0.8 / poor <0.5) and SYRCLE risk-of-bias tool for animal studies.
- **Statistical analysis**: Weighted mean differences with 95% CI, implants as unit of analysis; I² statistic for heterogeneity; random-effects (inverse-variance) model used when heterogeneity was significant, otherwise fixed-effects; performed in Review Manager (RevMan) v5.3.3, Nordic Cochrane Centre.
- **Outcomes**: Primary — bone-to-implant contact (BIC, %); secondary — bone area (BA, %) / bone volume fraction.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Quality assessment**: 8/11 studies rated "excellent," 3/11 rated "average" on ARRIVE 2.0; no studies excluded for quality.
- **Risk of bias**: Overall low-to-moderate risk (SYRCLE) — low risk for baseline characteristics, outcome assessment, and selective outcome reporting domains; outcome data generally complete.
- **BIC meta-analysis**: Significant increase for Mg-modified implants vs. uncoated Ti controls — Z = 4.38, p < 0.001. No formal subgroup analysis by coating strategy was performed.
- **BA meta-analysis**: No significant pooled difference — Z = 0.93, p = 0.35. A trend toward higher peri-implant bone formation was observed specifically in studies with extended healing (≥8 weeks), and in studies using controlled-release systems (Mg-loaded mesoporous titania, Mg-incorporated nanotubes), which reported enhanced bone volume.
- Improved osseointegration was also reported in compromised-bone (osteoporotic) animal models specifically, per several of the included primary studies discussed in the Discussion section.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Methodological heterogeneity across studies: differing coating techniques (ion implantation, micro-arc oxidation, mesoporous carriers, nanotubular structures) produce differing Mg release kinetics and degradation behavior; no subgroup analysis performed to isolate technique-specific effects.
- Inadequate reporting of biomechanical outcomes across the included primary studies.
- Ideal Mg concentration/release profile remains undefined; high Mg concentrations may increase corrosion risk and cytotoxicity, but no included study directly compared release levels or dose-response.
- All evidence is from small-animal (rabbit/rat), long-bone (tibial/femoral) models without functional occlusal loading — no jawbone-specific or clinically loaded models, and no human clinical data.
- Authors call for large-animal studies and well-designed human clinical trials before translating these findings to routine dental implant practice.

## 6. Related Work
- beckwith-2024: Umbrella review of implant coatings (calcium phosphate/HA, bisphosphonate, bioactive glass) found only weak evidence for osseointegration/MBL benefit and flagged CaP-coating complication signals — this paper's much stronger, quantitative Mg-BIC signal sits in the same coating-evidence space but is preclinical-only, so direct comparison to beckwith's clinical-SR conclusions should be cautious.
- bonfante-2012: Plasma-sprayed calcium phosphate coating study — a specific coating-chemistry precedent for how additive coatings alter BIC/removal-torque outcomes.
- yamada-2012: Nanopolymorphic hydroxyapatite coating study — another additive-coating precedent in the same category, useful for comparing HA-based vs. Mg-based coating strategies on BIC.

## 7. Glossary
- **BIC (Bone-to-Implant Contact)**: The percentage of implant surface in direct histological contact with mineralized bone, measured on undecalcified histomorphometric sections; used as a marker of osseointegration quality/early contact osteogenesis.
- **BA (Bone Area)**: The percentage of bone tissue occupying a defined region of interest around the implant (regional bone volume/density), distinct from surface-contact BIC.
- **ARRIVE 2.0**: Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments guidelines (21-item checklist) used to assess the completeness/quality of animal-study reporting.
- **SYRCLE**: SYstematic Review Centre for Laboratory animal Experimentation risk-of-bias tool, adapted from Cochrane's tool for animal intervention studies.
- **Mesoporous titania**: A nanoporous titanium-dioxide surface layer engineered to load and sustain-release therapeutic ions (here, Mg²⁺) at the implant-bone interface.
- **Wnt/β-catenin and VEGF/HIF-1α pathways**: Molecular signaling pathways implicated in osteoblast differentiation/mineralization (Wnt/β-catenin) and angiogenesis (VEGF/HIF-1α), proposed as mechanisms by which released Mg ions promote bone formation.
