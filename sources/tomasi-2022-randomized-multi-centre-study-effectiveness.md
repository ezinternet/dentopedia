## Why Ingested

Ingested as part of a 2026-07-20 sweep on dental hygienist scaling technique (치위생사 스케일링 술기, requested by the user); this multi-centre field trial supplies effectiveness-level (not just efficacy-level) evidence that dental-hygienist-delivered non-surgical periodontal therapy in ordinary general practice achieves outcomes matching controlled trials. It **extends** [[periodontics/unknown-2009-non-surgical-and-surgical-periodontal]] — that 2009 narrative review established the non-surgical-first treatment hierarchy (PPD reduction, re-evaluation-driven decision to add surgery) from earlier landmark studies; this paper provides a large-sample, real-world randomized validation of that same non-surgical phase, specifically as delivered by dental hygienists rather than in an efficacy-trial setting.

## Three-line Summary

Multi-centre, quasi-randomized (clinician-level cluster) two-armed field effectiveness trial in Swedish general dental practice: 95 dental hygienists (59 clinics) treated 615 periodontitis patients (GPIC n=280, CNST n=335) under a non-inferiority design, comparing a guided patient-education-first single-session protocol against conventional multi-visit scaling and root planing.

At 6 months, pocket closure (PPD ≤4 mm) was ~69–72% overall with no significant between-group difference (shallow sites 72% GPIC vs 75% CNST; deep sites 30% vs 33%), while GPIC required significantly less total treatment time (134±40 vs 161±61 min, p<.001) and fewer instrumentation sessions.

Efficacy-trial-level non-surgical periodontal outcomes reproduce in real-world dental-hygienist-delivered general practice; smoking, older age, and molar tooth type independently lowered the odds of pocket closure, supporting smoking-cessation counselling and avoidance of over-instrumentation.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First large-scale **effectiveness** (as opposed to efficacy) evaluation of guided periodontal infection control (GPIC) versus conventional non-surgical therapy (CNST), conducted entirely in general dental practice by practising dental hygienists rather than by trial-calibrated specialists.
- Demonstrates that a single-session, patient-education-first protocol (GPIC) achieves clinical outcomes statistically indistinguishable from multi-visit conventional SRP (CNST), while requiring ~17% less total chairtime and markedly less local anaesthesia.
- Quantifies time-efficiency directly: 9.5 ± 10.5 min of instrumentation per closed pocket (GPIC) versus 14.5 ± 20.8 min (CNST), p=.001.
- Multilevel modelling isolates independent predictors of pocket closure at the tooth-site level: baseline PPD (OR 0.33 per mm), current smoking (OR 0.56), age (OR 0.97 per year) and molar versus anterior tooth type (OR 0.41) — treatment group and BMI were not significant predictors.
- Confirms, at effectiveness scale, that pocket closure rates achieved in prior efficacy RCTs (~70%) are reproducible under real-world general-practice conditions with a heterogeneous patient sample and 95 clinicians of varying experience.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Multi-centre, quasi-randomized (clinician-level, stratified by geography and clinic sociodemographics, computer-generated random numbers table), two-armed field study; non-inferiority hypothesis (GPIC not inferior to CNST at 6 months). Approved by the Regional Ethical Review Board, Gothenburg (Dnr: 288-13); registered at ClinicalTrials.gov.
- **Setting/therapists**: Public Dental Service, Region Västra Götaland, Sweden. Of 120 invited dental hygienists (DHs), 95 (59 clinics) participated; each DH randomized to GPIC (n=47) or CNST (n=48) after a 1-day training/calibration session on Good Clinical Practice, protocol review, and clinical-assessment calibration. Study monitor performed repeated site visits.
- **Participants**: Adults with periodontitis, ≥18 teeth, ≥5 teeth with proximal PPD ≥5 mm + bleeding on probing (BoP); general health compatible with general-practice care; no subgingival instrumentation within the prior 6 months. Recruited June 2014–December 2017. Of 825 invited patients, 689 agreed to participate; modified intention-to-treat analysis comprised 615 patients (GPIC 280, CNST 335) after 74 did not complete the baseline phase.
- **Power calculation**: Designed as non-inferiority; primary outcome pocket closure (PPD ≤4 mm). n=506 (253/arm) gives 80% power at α<.05 to detect a 5-percentage-point between-group difference; target enrolment inflated to 700 to allow for dropout.
- **GPIC protocol**: Dedicated patient-education/motivation visits until full-mouth plaque score <30%, then a single session of full-mouth ultrasonic debridement (EMS Piezon, P/PS tips).
- **CNST protocol**: Integrated patient education, motivation and SRP across consecutive appointments (typically 1–2 weeks apart); number of sessions determined by the treating DH ("business as usual").
- **Common pathway**: Oral-hygiene check at 2–4 weeks in both groups; re-evaluation and re-instrumentation (hand and/or ultrasonic) of sites with residual PPD ≥5 mm + inflammation at 3 months; final evaluation at 6 months.
- **Outcome measurement**: PPD and BoP at 4 sites/tooth (mesial, buccal, distal, lingual) at baseline, 3 and 6 months, standardized probe (LM Dental 52B XSI, 2 mm markings, 0.5 mm tip). Plaque scored present/absent at 4 sites/tooth in quadrants 1 and 3. Treatment time (minutes) and local-anaesthesia use (carpules) logged per session. Patient-reported oral-health change captured on a 5-point scale at 6 months.
- **Statistics**: Modified intention-to-treat (last-observation-carried-forward for dropouts); independent-samples t-test / chi-square for group comparisons; three-level (clinician/patient/tooth-site) logistic multilevel model for probability of pocket closure; two-level (clinician/patient) linear multilevel model for change in proportion/number of teeth with residual pockets; partial correlation (adjusted for treatment allocation and baseline severity) between clinical and patient-reported outcomes.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Baseline sample**: GPIC 280 vs CNST 335 patients; mean age 53.3 in both arms; current smokers 22.5% (GPIC) vs 25.3% (CNST); Stage III–IV with ≥4 teeth PPD≥6mm in 45.7% (GPIC) vs 58.6% (CNST) — groups were not perfectly balanced on disease severity, so analyses were adjusted for baseline severity.
- **Treatment time (baseline phase)**: 96.1±33.8 min (GPIC) vs 119.7±50.3 min (CNST), p<.001. **Total time (baseline+3mo)**: 134.0±40.1 vs 160.9±61.3 min, p<.001. Mechanical-instrumentation time (baseline+3mo): 64.7±21.3 vs 107.5±49.2 min, p<.001.
- **Sessions/instruments**: GPIC = 100% single instrumentation session, 100% ultrasonic only. CNST = only 29.0% single session (37.7% two, 17.7% three, 15.6% four-or-more); instrumentation modality mixed (72.6% ultrasonic+hand, 14.0% hand only, 13.4% ultrasonic only).
- **Anaesthesia**: No anaesthesia used in 70.0% (GPIC) vs 59.9% (CNST) of initial sessions (p<.001); injected carpules 0.2±0.7 (GPIC) vs 1.2±2.3 (CNST), p<.001.
- **Pocket closure at 6 months (primary outcome)**: ~69–72% of initial pockets overall; shallow sites (5–6 mm) 72% (GPIC) vs 75% (CNST); deep sites (≥7 mm) 30% vs 33% — no significant between-group difference at any severity stratum.
- **Disease-severity gradient**: ~75% pocket closure in Stage II, ~66% in localized Stage III–IV, ~50% in generalized Stage III–IV (≥4 teeth PPD≥6mm); 6% of the most-affected subjects were pocket-free post-treatment, 62% still had >4 teeth with residual pockets.
- **Time efficiency**: 9.5±10.5 min instrumentation per closed pocket (GPIC) vs 14.5±20.8 min (CNST), p=.001.
- **Patient-reported outcomes**: >75% of patients rated oral health "much" or "very much" improved after treatment; weak but significant positive correlation (r=0.16, p<.001) between patient-reported benefit and % pocket closure.
- **Multilevel logistic model (pocket closure, site level)**: PPD at baseline OR 0.33 (95% CI 0.31–0.36) per mm; current smoker OR 0.56 (95% CI 0.37–0.85); age OR 0.97 (95% CI 0.96–0.98) per year; molar vs anterior OR 0.41 (95% CI 0.35–0.48); premolar vs anterior OR ~0.72 (β −0.33); treatment group (GPIC vs CNST) NOT significant (β −0.35, 95% CI −0.85 to 0.09, p=.117); BMI NOT significant. Model explained 27% of variance.
- **Linear multilevel model (residual pocket count/proportion)**: baseline pocket burden, age and smoking status were significant predictors (40% variance explained); current smokers showed less pocket reduction than non-/former smokers; treatment group and BMI again not significant.
- No adverse events reported in either arm.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No comprehensive plaque-score tracking or assessment of local anatomical features (angular bony defects, furcation involvement) that could confound treatment-response prediction.
- Potential selection bias in patient enrolment; disease-severity distribution was unbalanced between arms at baseline (partially mitigated by statistical adjustment, not randomization).
- Calibrating clinical assessment across 95 different clinicians is inherently harder than in small efficacy trials — contributes to the larger outcome variance observed versus efficacy studies.
- Quasi-randomization was performed at the clinician (DH) level, not the patient level — a cluster design with the statistical dependencies that implies, addressed via multilevel modelling but still a departure from patient-level RCTs.
- Follow-up limited to 6 months; longer-term stability of pocket closure and the comparative durability of GPIC vs CNST were not assessed.
- Authors note total treatment time was unrelated to baseline disease severity, suggesting practice may default to a standardized time allotment rather than individualizing to need — flagged as a target for future practice-improvement work.

## 6. Related Work

- Builds directly on the original GPIC protocol described by **Wennström et al.**, replicating its shallow (86% in the original) vs deep (50%) pocket-closure gradient, with this study finding a somewhat lower deep-site success rate (30%) attributed to greater real-world heterogeneity.
- Consistent with the systematic review of non-surgical periodontal therapy efficacy by **Suvan et al.**, whose ~70% pocket-closure benchmark and disease-severity gradient this effectiveness study reproduces.
- Time-efficiency advantage of guided/single-session protocols corroborates **Koshy et al.** and Wennström et al.
- Smoking as an independent negative predictor of pocket closure is consistent with **D'Aiuto et al., Tomasi et al. (prior work), and Wan et al.**
- Reduced treatment response in older patients parallels a retrospective finding by **Trombelli et al.**
- Companion patient-reported-outcome data (treatment discomfort/pain, no significant between-group difference) from the same cohort reported by **Liss et al.**, who also describe the DH workforce characteristics (average 13 years' experience).
- In this wiki, extends the treatment-hierarchy framework of [[periodontics/unknown-2009-non-surgical-and-surgical-periodontal]] with contemporary, hygienist-delivered, effectiveness-scale evidence for the non-surgical therapy phase.

## 7. Glossary

- **GPIC** — Guided Periodontal Infection Control: patient-education-first protocol requiring full-mouth plaque score <30% before a single session of full-mouth ultrasonic debridement.
- **CNST** — Conventional Non-Surgical Therapy: integrated patient education + scaling and root planing (SRP) across consecutive appointments, session count at clinician discretion.
- **DH** — Dental Hygienist; in this study, the sole treatment provider (no dentists performed instrumentation).
- **PPD** — Probing Pocket Depth.
- **BoP** — Bleeding on Probing.
- **SRP** — Scaling and Root Planing.
- **Pocket closure** — Primary outcome: PPD ≤4 mm at a site that had PPD ≥5 mm at baseline.
- **ITT (modified)** — Modified Intention-To-Treat: all patients completing the baseline treatment phase were analyzed; last-observation-carried-forward used for dropouts before 6 months.
- **OR / CI** — Odds Ratio / Confidence Interval, from the multilevel logistic regression.
