## Why Ingested

[[wiki/cracked-tooth/patel-2025-position-statement-longitudinal-cracks-fractures]] (ESE consensus, same senior author) condenses the VRF-in-root-filled-teeth entity into a few paragraphs; this 2022 narrative review is the long-form primary-literature backbone behind it (aetiology, three-stage pathogenesis, CBCT limits, prevention) and was requested as the detailed reference. It also frames the pericervical-dentine and minimally-invasive-access debate that [[wiki/endodontics/shaping/lee-2026-residual-pericervical-apical-dentine-vertical]] later tests clinically.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (PubMed search to March 2022, English full texts; Int Endod J "Present status and future directions" series) of the prevalence, aetiology, pathogenesis, diagnosis, management and prevention of vertical root fracture (VRF) in root filled teeth.

In the cited retrospective series 86-97% of extracted VRF teeth were root filled, and reported VRF prevalence in root filled teeth is 4-32%; CBCT cannot reliably detect the fracture line itself but does reveal the periradicular bone-loss pattern, and extraction remains the most predictable treatment (root resection in selected multi-rooted teeth).

Prevention (preserving residual tooth structure, prompt cuspal coverage within about 4 months, passive posts, occlusal/parafunction control) is the main lever, but the review itself notes high-level evidence is lacking and most data are retrospective or ex vivo.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Proposes a working classification: incomplete VRF (one side of root) vs complete VRF (extends from one proximal aspect to the opposite side); "split tooth" = VRF with visible separation of components.
- Separates risk factors into predisposing (structural integrity, pre-existing cracks, dentine biomechanics, root anatomy, tooth location, parafunction/occlusal arrangement, diet) and contributory (excessive dentine removal, prolonged exposure to disinfectants/medicaments, inappropriate post-endodontic restoration) (Table 1).
- Proposes a three-stage pathogenesis: crack initiation, fracture propagation (fatigue; fracture toughness regarded as probably the most important factor), separation.
- Critically appraises the dentinal-microcrack literature: sectioning studies are methodologically invalid; micro-CT and cadaver data show no causal link between canal preparation and new microcracks.
- States that CBCT should be used for the secondary periradicular bone-loss pattern of VRF rather than to find the fracture line (consistent with ESE 2019 and AAE/AAOMR guidance when clinical and PR assessment are inconclusive).
- Summarizes prevention and the limited management options, and lists research priorities.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Narrative review, not a systematic review. Unrestricted PubMed search by two evaluators, MeSH keywords (prevalence, aetiology, diagnosis, management of VRF in root filled teeth); English, full text available, cut-off March 2022; plus manual reference search. Studies on "cracks" not specific to VRF were included only to discuss aetiology.
- **Databases**: PubMed only.
- **n**: No pooled n; no risk-of-bias appraisal or quantitative synthesis.
- **Outcomes**: Prevalence, risk factors, clinical/radiographic presentation, diagnostic accuracy of PR/CBCT, treatment options, prevention.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Prevalence**
- Yoshino 2015 (736 extracted teeth): 31.7% extracted for VRF, 93.6% of those root filled. Sugaya 2015 (304 VRF teeth): 97% root filled, 2.3% vital, 0.7% non-vital not root filled. Liao 2017 (Taiwan): 86% of extracted VRF teeth previously root filled.
- VRF prevalence in root filled teeth reported 4-32% (heterogeneous diagnosis); most affected: maxillary (pre)molars and mandibular molars; incidence rises after age 40.
- Surgical exploration cannot see mesial/distal root surfaces (underestimation); extracted-tooth examination may over-estimate (extraction-induced fracture).

**Aetiology**
- Pre-existing cracks: PradeepKumar 2017: 7.1% of 633 non-endodontically treated extracted teeth had cracks; Kang 2016: VRF the most common reason for extraction of endodontically treated cracked teeth; Leong 2020 SR: 5-year survival of endodontically treated cracked teeth 84.1%; multiple cracks, radicular cracks and probing >3 mm carried an 8-9% higher extraction risk.
- Al-Nuaimi 2017 (137 posterior teeth): <30% residual coronal tooth structure had significantly higher 1-year retreatment failure.
- De-Deus 2019: 178 cadaver teeth, 65,530 micro-CT images, no dentinal microcracks; micro-CT studies show canal preparation does not induce new microcracks.
- Dentine ageing: roughly 25 MPa strength loss per decade (Yan 2017); dentine from patients >55 y had up to 50% lower fracture strength than <30 y (Arola & Reprogel 2005). Dehydration effect on dentine is contested (Papa 1994: microhardness only 3.5% higher in vital teeth; 80-85% of free water lost within 2 h of extraction, so storage conditions confound lab studies).
- Anatomy: isthmus-associated in 56.3% of VRFs in a 30-tooth histological study (von Arx & Bosshardt 2017); flattened roots (mesial of mandibular molars, MB of maxillary molars) are "danger zones".
- Occlusion: Ng 2011 (4-year prospective): 68% of terminal teeth requiring extraction were fractured; 58% of teeth without proximal contacts were extracted due to fracture vs 38% with proximal contacts.
- Access cavity: ex vivo results conflicting; micro-CT-standardized studies consistently show no fracture-resistance difference for minimally invasive access; loss of marginal ridges (Reeh 1989) matters more than the access cavity itself. Clark & Khademi pericervical-dentine proposals are opinion/case-series based, not scientific evidence per the authors.
- Restorative: Salehrabi & Rotstein 2004 (1,462,936 root filled teeth): 97% survived 8 years, but 85% of extracted teeth lacked cuspal coverage; Dammaschke 2013 (676 teeth): mean survival 15.3 y with crowns vs 13.4 y composite, 11.8 y amalgam, 6.6 y GIC; Boren 2015 (420 teeth): 10-year survival 91.3% crowned vs 76% not crowned, VRF 36% of failures; Pratt 2016: crown >4 months after RCT ~3x extraction risk, with a >9-fold rise in failure after 18 months; Toure 2011 (119 extracted): 94% lacked cuspal coverage, VRF 13.4% of extractions.

**Clinical and radiographic presentation**
- Delayed presentation, typically 2-5 years after root canal treatment. Sinus tract in 18.2-67% (when present, 77.8% at mid-root level and 22.2% at the gingival margin). Isolated deep narrow probing defect is pathognomonic when present on both sides of the root.
- Radiographic: J-shaped or halo radiolucency, root separation; early VRF may show no change or subtle crestal bone loss.

**Diagnosis by CBCT**
- Salineiro 2017 meta-analysis favoured CBCT but heterogeneity was high and included widely displaced fragments (0.2-0.4 mm). Patel 2013: CBCT not accurate for simulated VRF in root filled teeth; beam hardening from gutta-percha/metal posts raises false positives; fibre posts interfere less; MAR algorithms did not improve VRF diagnosis. Byakova 2019 (in vivo): CBCT could not accurately assess the VRF within the root but was significantly more sensitive for the secondary periradicular bone loss.
- MRI and OCT: promising ex vivo, not yet clinically ready; AI-based CBCT analysis anticipated.

**Management and prevention**
- Prognosis poor; extraction is most predictable. Root resection/hemisection: >90% tooth survival over 10 years in general root-resection literature (Carnevale 1998; Derks 2018), not VRF-specific. Intentional replantation/repair with adhesive resin, GIC or Biodentine: only case (series) evidence, no recommended protocol.
- Davis & Shariff 2019 (prospective, 2-4 years): nanoglass fluoride-releasing resin barrier in the canal beyond a radicular crack extension plus immediate cuspal coverage gave 96.6% survival at 4 years.
- Endocrowns: ~90% survival at 7 and 10 years comparable to full crowns (Fages 2017; Otto & Mormann 2015). Posts only for core retention when minimal sound coronal tissue; passive cementation, avoid screw posts and post-space over-preparation.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative design: single database (PubMed), English full texts only, no formal quality appraisal; selection bias possible.
- Most prevalence/survival data are retrospective with operator/selection bias (teeth with better prognosis more likely to be crowned); no randomized trials on cuspal coverage and VRF-specific failure.
- Cause-of-extraction data frequently misclassify VRF (e.g., isolated pocket read as periodontal disease).
- Authors call for well-designed RCTs of contemporary cuspal coverage, long-term studies of access/instrumentation protocols, and clinical outcome studies of VRF repair techniques.

## 7. Glossary

- **VRF (vertical root fracture)**: longitudinal fracture involving cementum, dentine and canal system of a root; incomplete (one side) vs complete (to opposite side).
- **Split tooth**: VRF with visible separation of the two components.
- **Pericervical dentine (PCD)**: region ~4 mm coronal and apical to the crestal bone level (Clark & Khademi).
- **Fracture toughness**: ability of residual tooth tissue, with or without a pre-existing crack, to absorb energy without fracturing; regarded as probably the most important propagation factor.
- **Beam hardening**: CBCT artefact from radiodense materials (gutta-percha, metal posts) that degrades VRF diagnosis.
- **Cuspal coverage restoration**: crown/onlay covering cusps to limit cuspal flexure in root filled teeth.
