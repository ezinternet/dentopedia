## Why Ingested

This is the primary-study anchor for a figure the wiki currently holds only second-hand: [[wiki/implants/mbl/mortazavi-2021-bone-loss-tissue-bone-level-implants]] tabulates "Wu 2018 | BL, 114 implants | 1.2mm (yr 1) + 0.78mm (yr 1–5) | 5 yr" as one of its 38 trials, yet no page in this wiki owns those numbers or reports the MBL-by-protocol breakdown the paper's title promises. More importantly, it is the **within-system** test of the exact axis on which the wiki's three reviews disagree: [[wiki/implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]] pools 6 RCTs for a *significant* MD 0.12 mm more MBL with submerged placement, [[wiki/implants/survival/troiano-2018-early-late-failure-submerged]] assigns a +2% early-failure penalty to the *non*-submerged arm with a 0.13 mm MBL trend favouring it, and [[wiki/implants/mbl/al-amri-2016-crestal-bone-loss-submerged]] reports 5 of 6 human studies null. Wu 2018 holds implant system, connection design, bone level and placement timing constant and varies only the flap/loading protocol — the one design that controls the surface-and-system confound left open by [[wiki/implants/pirc-2026-one-piece-two-piece-implants-15year-rct]] (one-piece vs two-piece MBL 0.08 vs 1.53 mm, but different brands and surfaces in each arm). The Moustafa Ali page explicitly records this paper as a still-pending reconciliation target.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective 5-year cohort (114 bone-level XiVE S plus implants with internal hexagonal connections in 72 patients, Sun Yat-sen University / Xiamen Stomatological Hospital, China), all immediately implanted, comparing submerged vs nonsubmerged healing and testing guided bone regeneration (GBR) application, implant site, length and diameter as potential influencing factors.

5-year cumulative survival rate (CSR) was 94% (submerged) vs 96% (nonsubmerged), with no statistically significant difference in marginal bone loss (MBL) between the two protocols, and no significant difference in CSR by healing protocol, GBR application, implant site or implant length.

Both protocols are defensible for immediately placed bone-level internal-hexagonal implants and neither implant length, site nor GBR use altered long-term success — but as a non-randomised, single-system, abstract-only record with no MBL values, no p-values and no per-protocol group sizes, its null cannot be power-checked and must not be read as outranking the pooled 0.12 mm MBL effect reported for submerged placement by [[wiki/implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]].

두 프로토콜 모두 즉시 식립 골수준 내부 육각형 임플란트에서 타당하고 임플란트 길이·부위·GBR 적용은 장기 성공에 영향을 주지 않음 — 다만 비무작위·단일 시스템(XiVE)·초록만 확보한 기록으로 MBL 수치·p값·그룹별 표본수가 없어 음성(null) 결과의 검정력을 확인할 수 없고, 침습식에서 유의한 MBL 차이(평균차 MD 0.12 mm)를 보고한 [[wiki/implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]]의 풀링 결과를 대체하지도 않음.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Isolates the healing protocol as the only varying exposure within a single implant system** — same bone-level design, same internal hexagonal connection, same immediate-placement timing in both arms; the submerged vs nonsubmerged decision is the contrast. This removes the brand/surface confounding that limits [[wiki/implants/pirc-2026-one-piece-two-piece-implants-15year-rct]] and the design-factor reviews.
- **Adds a 5-year life-table / Kaplan–Meier survival estimate for immediately placed internal-hexagonal bone-level implants**, a design generation (pre-platform-switching) now largely displaced clinically.
- **Tests candidate modifiers in the same cohort** — guided bone regeneration application, implant site, implant length and implant diameter — and finds none of them altered long-term implant success.
- **Ranks the surgical protocol against those modifiers**: with high CSRs and "good marginal bone levels" under both protocols, the flap/loading decision is reported as a non-determinant of long-term success in this setting, which is a decision-support framing rather than a bone-loss claim.

## 3. Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort (chart/record review), 5-year follow-up. Not randomised; protocol assignment was a clinical decision.
- **Sample**: 114 bone-level implants (XiVE S plus, internal hexagonal connections) inserted into **72 patients**, all by immediate implantation.
- **Primary exposure**: submerged vs nonsubmerged healing protocol.
- **Potential influencing factors tested**: guided bone regeneration (GBR) application, implant site, implant length, implant diameter.
- **Outcomes**: (a) marginal bone loss (MBL), compared between submerged and nonsubmerged groups by **t-test**; (b) cumulative survival rate (CSR) by **life table method**, illustrated with **Kaplan–Meier** survival curves; (c) between-group CSR comparisons by **log-rank test**.
- **Not recoverable from the abstract** (do not assume): inclusion/exclusion criteria, socket or site-eligibility criteria, patient demographics and age distribution, time interval between extraction and implant placement, primary-stability thresholds, loading and prosthetic protocol, restoration type, radiographic technique and the timepoints at which MBL was measured, the **MBL values themselves**, any p-value, confidence interval or effect size, graft material, follow-up completeness and attrition, ethics approval, funding, and conflict-of-interest statements.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Submerged | Nonsubmerged |
|---|---|---|
| 5-year cumulative survival rate (CSR, life table) | **94%** | **96%** |
| Marginal bone loss (t-test between protocols) | No statistically significant difference | No statistically significant difference |

| Factor tested for CSR | Result as reported in the abstract |
|---|---|
| Healing protocol (submerged vs nonsubmerged) | No statistically significant difference |
| Guided bone regeneration (GBR) application | No statistically significant difference |
| Implant site | No statistically significant difference |
| Implant length | No statistically significant difference |
| Implant diameter | Tested by log-rank per the methods, **but not listed among the non-significant results** — see caveat below |

- **Study scale**: 114 implants, 72 patients, 5-year follow-up, immediate implantation throughout.
- **Authors' conclusion**: high CSRs and good marginal bone levels were achieved at 5 years with **both** submerged and nonsubmerged techniques; implant length, site and GBR application did not affect long-term success.

**How to read these numbers honestly:**

- **The 94% vs 96% split is a 2-percentage-point difference reported as non-significant.** No p-value, CI, log-rank statistic, or per-protocol group size appears in the abstract, so the test's power cannot be assessed and the result must not be restated as *equivalence* — no equivalence or non-inferiority margin was designed.
- **"No statistically significant difference in MBL" carries no magnitude.** The abstract reports no MBL value for either arm, in mm, at any timepoint — so this null cannot be placed on the same axis as the pooled MD 0.12 mm (95% CI 0.00–0.24) in [[wiki/implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]] or the 0.13 mm effect in [[wiki/implants/survival/troiano-2018-early-late-failure-submerged]]. A retrospective null and a pooled borderline-significant effect are statistically compatible.
- **"Good marginal bone levels" is qualitative.** No threshold (e.g. the 2 mm success criterion), mean, SD or range is given.
- **Abstract-level internal inconsistency on diameter**: the methods state that log-rank comparisons covered "implants with different sites, lengths, **and diameters**", but the results enumerate only marginal bone loss, healing protocol, GBR application, implant site and implant length. Whether the diameter comparison was null, underpowered or simply omitted from the abstract cannot be determined — reproduced as-found rather than silently resolved.
- **The MBL figures circulating in this wiki (1.2 mm at year 1 + 0.78 mm over years 1–5) are not in this abstract.** They appear only as a row in the data table of [[wiki/implants/mbl/mortazavi-2021-bone-loss-tissue-bone-level-implants]], which cites this paper secondarily. They are recorded here as a **secondary citation pending full-text confirmation**, not as this paper's own reported values.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Stated or structural (readable from the abstract):

- **Non-randomised.** Healing protocol was a clinical decision, not an allocation, so patient selection, site morphology, operator preference and grafting decisions are uncontrolled confounders; the null is the best available reading, not a demonstration of no effect.
- **Single implant system.** All 114 implants are XiVE S plus with an internal hexagonal connection. Nothing here transfers to external-hex, Morse-taper or platform-switched designs — the design classes the MBL literature in [[wiki/implants/mbl/mortazavi-2021-bone-loss-tissue-bone-level-implants]] and [[wiki/implants/mbl/yu-2020-internal-tapered-vs-nontapered-connection]] actually concerns.
- **Immediate-placement sites only.** The cohort cannot speak to delayed or early placement, and its 5-year CSR mixes the healing window where [[wiki/implants/survival/troiano-2018-early-late-failure-submerged]] locates the protocol signal (early failure, <6 months from placement).
- **No early-vs-late failure split.** Troiano's +2% early-failure penalty cannot be tested against a single 5-year cumulative figure, so this paper is silent on the outcome where the pooled signal is largest.
- **Retrospective with a 5-year horizon and no reported attrition** — loss to follow-up, if any, is not described in the abstract.
- **Older design generation.** Internal hexagonal connections without platform switching are no longer the dominant design; the finding is of mechanistic and historical interest more than of direct prescriptive value today.

*(inferred)* Additional gaps from abstract-only retrieval:

- No per-protocol n, no MBL values, no p-values, no CIs, no Kaplan–Meier table and no log-rank statistics — the null on MBL cannot be power-checked and cannot be numerically reconciled against the pooled reviews.
- No radiographic method, reference plane, or measurement timepoints, so MBL is not comparable like-for-like with the other pages in this wiki.
- No subgroup analysis by smoking, bone quality, or anterior/posterior site is reported, although [[wiki/implants/survival/giok-2026-factors-implant-failure-umbrella-review]] grades the healing protocol as a high-certainty failure-risk factor — the interaction with the best-supported modifiable risk factor is therefore untested here.
- **Full-text retrieval is the single highest-value follow-up**, specifically to recover the per-protocol MBL means and the subgroup structure that would allow this cohort to be placed on the same effect-size axis as Moustafa Ali 2018 and Troiano 2018.

## 6. Related Work

- [[wiki/implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]] — Moustafa Ali 2018 · sr+ma · **same question, opposite statistical direction; the tension is stated, not resolved.** That SR+MA of 6 RCTs reports **significantly more MBL with submerged placement** (MD 0.12 mm; 95% CI 0.00–0.24; I² = 0%, P = .04) — judged by its own authors *not clinically relevant*, with 4 of 6 RCTs at high risk of bias. This retrospective cohort reports **no significant MBL difference**. Reconciling honestly: the SR+MA outranks this cohort on both design and evidence weight, so the correct reading is **not** "the null refutes the pooled effect"; it is that a 114-implant non-randomised cohort is simply underpowered to resolve a difference of the pooled magnitude, whose CI lower bound is 0.00 mm. The direction of the 94% vs 96% survival split is also numerically consistent with submerged being the weaker arm. Its page already records this paper as a pending reconciliation target.
- [[wiki/implants/survival/troiano-2018-early-late-failure-submerged]] — Troiano 2018 · sr+ma + TSA · the same axis from the pooled side, and the **opposite** arm assignment. Troiano puts a +2% early-failure penalty on **non-submerged** healing and a 0.13 mm MBL trend favouring non-submerged; this cohort finds no significant difference on either outcome, and reports no early/late split that could test the +2% directly. Consistent with a small true effect in either direction.
- [[wiki/implants/mbl/al-amri-2016-crestal-bone-loss-submerged]] — Al Amri 2016 · sr (13 studies; 5 of 6 human studies null on submerged vs nonsubmerged crestal bone loss) · the closest published consensus to this cohort's own null, reached 2 years earlier by a much weaker method (a vote count with no pooled estimate) and with platform-switching studies excluded.
- [[wiki/implants/pirc-2026-one-piece-two-piece-implants-15year-rct]] — Pirc 2026 · rct (15–17 y) · the **clinical mirror image** of this comparison: one-piece implants are non-submerged by design, two-piece are submerged by design, and Pirc's two-piece arm lost 1.53 mm of marginal bone vs 0.08 mm for one-piece. Because Pirc's arms differ in brand and surface (Straumann SLA vs Brånemark TiUnite) as well as in architecture, **this cohort is the missing within-system control**: holding system and surface constant and varying only the protocol produced no MBL difference, which argues that Pirc's 1.45 mm gap is driven by the system/surface pair rather than by the submerged-vs-non-submerged architecture itself. Pirc's own page warns against extrapolating the older two-piece system.
- [[wiki/implants/mbl/mortazavi-2021-bone-loss-tissue-bone-level-implants]] — Mortazavi 2021 · sr (38 trials) · **already tabulates this paper's MBL figures (1.2 mm at year 1 + 0.78 mm over years 1–5) as a secondary citation**, and treats design levers — platform switching, Morse taper, SLActive — rather than the surgical healing protocol as the dominant MBL determinants. This cohort is the primary source behind one of its rows and the procedural-axis counterpoint to its design-axis conclusion.
- [[wiki/implants/mbl/kim-2022-abutment-connection-mbl-survival]] — Kim 2022 · retrospective (374 implants, 5 y) · attributes 1-year MBL to the abutment **connection** (external hex 1.23 vs internal 11° conical hex 0.72 mm, p<0.001, gap gone by year 5) with no survival difference (93.3% vs 90.7%). Same connection family, same null-on-survival pattern, and a more plausible MBL lever than flap design — but it excludes immediate placement, so the cohorts do not overlap.
- [[wiki/implants/liu-2021-clinical-radiographic-performance-one-piece]] — Liu 2021 · sr+ma · the pooled one-piece vs two-piece contrast (the same mirror axis as Pirc), useful as the design-level counterpart to this cohort's within-system protocol test.
- [[wiki/implants/survival/giok-2026-factors-implant-failure-umbrella-review]] — Giok 2026 · sr+ma (umbrella) · grades "submerged vs nonsubmerged healing" as a **high-certainty** RCT-derived failure-risk association but, being abstract-only, never names the penalised arm. This cohort cannot adjudicate that claim: its 2-point survival gap is non-significant and it does not split early from late failure.

**Supersession check: no.** This cohort's clinical bottom line (both healing protocols give high 5-year survival; protocol is not a survival determinant) is *weaker* evidence than the pooled SR+MAs already held on this axis, so nothing in this wiki is overturned and no `superseded_by` is set. The direction conflict with Moustafa Ali 2018 is recorded here and on that page's `## Related Papers`; a typed edge is written on the new page only, since existing pages are not edited at ingest.

## 7. Glossary

- **Submerged healing (two-stage surgery)**: Protocol in which the implant is fully covered by the mucoperiosteal flap at placement and exposed only at a second operation after healing, so the implant–abutment interface sits away from the crestal bone during the healing period.
- **Nonsubmerged healing (one-stage surgery)**: Protocol in which the implant is exposed to the oral cavity, or fitted with a healing abutment, at placement, leaving the implant–abutment interface adjacent to the crestal bone from the outset.
- **Internal hexagonal connection**: Implant–abutment interface in which the abutment seats inside a hexagonal cavity within the implant shoulder; in this paper applied to the bone-level XiVE S plus system.
- **Bone-level implant**: Implant whose platform is positioned at the crestal bone margin, so the implant–abutment microgap lies at or near the bone crest.
- **Immediate implantation**: Insertion of an implant into the extraction socket at the time of tooth removal, the placement condition for all 114 implants here.
- **Marginal bone loss (MBL)**: Apical change in peri-implant crestal bone level measured on radiographs; the outcome compared between the submerged and nonsubmerged groups here by t-test.
- **Cumulative survival rate (CSR)**: Proportion of implants still in function at a given follow-up time, computed by the **life table method** and illustrated with **Kaplan–Meier** curves; between-group comparisons by the **log-rank test**. Reported here as 94% (submerged) and 96% (nonsubmerged) at 5 years.
- **Guided bone regeneration (GBR)**: Barrier-membrane technique for augmenting deficient peri-implant bone, tested here as a potential influencing factor (no effect on long-term success).
- **XiVE S plus**: The bone-level implant system with internal hexagonal connection used throughout this cohort (Frialit-2 lineage); surface treatment and platform geometry are not specified in the abstract.
