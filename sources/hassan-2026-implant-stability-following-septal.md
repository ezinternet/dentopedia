## Why Ingested

Adds the first randomized (pilot) head-to-head of piezoelectric septal expansion (Intralift Kit) vs osseodensification for immediate mandibular molar placement, extending the retrospective OD-only feasibility data held in [[wiki/immediate-implant/molar-septum/bleyan-2021-molar-septum-expansion-osseodensification-immediate-implant]] with a controlled comparator arm.

## Three-line Summary

Pilot feasibility RCT (20 patients, 1:1, single center, Alexandria University) comparing piezoelectric implant site preparation (PISP, Intralift Kit) vs osseodensification (OD, Densah burs) for interradicular-septum expansion in immediate mandibular molar implant placement.

Primary and secondary implant stability quotients (ISQ) were comparable (primary 66.75±2.86 vs 69.25±2.86, p=0.082; secondary at 3 months 76.75±2.05 vs 78.50±2.50, p=0.124); PISP produced higher insertion torque (40.33±3.24 vs 35.00±3.53 Ncm, p=0.004) and lower VAS pain (p<0.05) at the cost of longer osteotomy preparation time (9.00±0.94 vs 6.00±0.82 min, p<0.001).

Both techniques achieved clinically acceptable stability with no intraoperative or postoperative complications — PISP is clinically feasible as an alternative to OD for molar septal expansion, though pilot design and differing implant diameters (3.7 vs 4.2 mm) limit the strength of inference.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study to apply the Intralift Kit (Acteon) for controlled septal expansion in mandibular molars (authors' claim).
- First pilot RCT comparing PISP vs OD for septal expansion in immediate mandibular molar placement — both reached clinically acceptable stability (primary and secondary ISQ not significantly different, p>0.05).
- PISP achieved significantly higher insertion torque (40.33 Ncm) but significantly longer osteotomy time and significantly lower first-week VAS pain than OD.
- All predefined feasibility criteria met: recruitment, protocol adherence, retention (0 dropouts), safety (no serious adverse events), procedural and outcome feasibility.
- Quantifies that both groups exceeded the generally accepted insertion-torque threshold (>30–35 Ncm) regarded as sufficient for predictable primary stability and immediate placement.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Single-center, parallel-group pilot feasibility randomized clinical trial (November 2023–August 2024).
- **Randomization**: 1:1 computer-generated sequence with block allocation (Random Allocation software 1.0); allocation concealment with sequentially numbered sealed opaque envelopes; surgeon unblinded, outcome assessors (Osstell operator, torque measurer) blinded.
- **n**: 20 patients (18–40 y), 10 per group; one immediate implant (Vitronex VPLUS) per patient.
- **Groups**: PISP — Intralift Kit diamond-coated tapered tips TKW1–TKW4 (1.35→2.80 mm) under continuous irrigation; OD — Densah burs VT1525→VT3238 (2.0→3.5 mm) in counterclockwise densifying mode at 800–1500 rpm. Both after pilot drill +1 mm beyond planned length.
- **Inclusion**: septum width ≥2.5 mm, residual bone height ≥5 mm apical to socket, intact buccal/lingual walls (CBCT), ASA I–II, no acute periapical infection.
- **Outcomes**: Primary — ISQ (Osstell) immediately (primary stability) and at 3 months (secondary stability), mean of bucco-lingual and mesio-distal readings. Secondary — insertion torque (Ncm), osteotomy preparation time, VAS pain during first postoperative week.
- **Statistics**: Shapiro–Wilk + Q–Q plots; independent/paired t-tests for between/within-group continuous outcomes; Mann–Whitney U and Friedman for VAS; α=0.05, two-tailed (SPSS 23).
- **Sample size**: based on Elgrany et al. (ISQ 62.80±2.25 PISP vs 68.30±3.83 OD; effect size 1.43; α=0.05; 80% power) → 9/group, inflated to 10/group (N=20).
- **Post-extraction protocol**: gap grafting with MinerOss cancellous allograft (300–1000 μm) + customized healing abutments; implants placed subcrestal with manual torque ratchet.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Baseline bone density (CBCT gray values)** — no between-group difference (OD 523.00±35.92 vs PISP 515.00±33.75; p=0.614).

**Implant stability (ISQ)** — comparable between groups at both time points; significant within-group rise from primary to secondary stability in both (p<0.001).

| Outcome | OD (n=10) | PISP (n=10) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary ISQ | 69.25±2.86 (65.00–73.00) | 66.75±2.86 (63.00–70.00) | 0.082 |
| Secondary ISQ (3 mo) | 78.50±2.50 (75.00–82.00) | 76.75±2.05 (75.00–80.00) | 0.124 |
| % ISQ increase | 13.40±1.17 (12.33–15.38) | 15.13±4.81 (8.70–22.22) | 0.310 |
| Within-group primary→secondary | p<0.001 | p<0.001 | — |

**Insertion torque** — significantly higher in PISP:

| Outcome | OD (n=10) | PISP (n=10) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insertion torque (Ncm) | 35.00±3.53 (30.00–40.00) | 40.33±3.24 (35.00–45.00) | 0.004 |

**Osteotomy preparation time** — significantly shorter in OD (6.00±0.82 min) than PISP (9.00±0.94 min), p<0.001.

**Postoperative pain (VAS, first week)** — significantly lower in the PISP group at all time points (p<0.05); pain decreased progressively in both groups.

**Safety** — no intraoperative exclusions; no early implant failure, infection, wound dehiscence, or neurosensory disturbance; all 20 patients completed follow-up.

**Demographics/implant dimensions** — age 30.3±1.5 vs 29.4±1.1 (p=0.138); sex p=0.682; implant dimensions differed by design (PISP 3.7 mm diameter, OD 4.2 mm; overall p=0.049) — an acknowledged confound (final tip diameter forces different implant sizes to avoid over-torque in PISP).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Pilot feasibility design: small sample (n=10/group), single center, strict age range (18–40 y), short follow-up (3 months) — no survival or marginal-bone-loss data (≥12 months needed).
- Different absolute implant diameters (3.7 vs 4.2 mm) may partially confound insertion torque and ISQ comparisons; final osteotomy diameters differ by system (2.8 mm PISP vs 3.5 mm OD).
- Surgeon blinding impossible due to inherent technique differences (assessors blinded).
- Retrospective trial registration, on-device torque ratchet measurement, single implant system, and no standardized implant length/intraosseous depth.
- Future directions requested by authors: larger multi-center samples, longer follow-up with survival/MBL, validated pain/swelling/soft-tissue indices, broader implant sizes/systems, standardized implant length and depth.

## 7. Glossary

- **PISP**: Piezoelectric implant site preparation — osteotomy with high-frequency ultrasonic (24–29 kHz) instrument tips; here the Intralift Kit with diamond-coated tapered tips (1.35–2.80 mm).
- **Osseodensification (OD)**: Bone-compacting site preparation using counterclockwise-rotating Densah burs (reverse flutes), condensing trabeculae instead of excavating them.
- **Implant Stability Quotient (ISQ)**: Resonance frequency analysis (RFA, Osstell) value of implant stability; scale ~1–100, higher = more stable.
- **Interradicular septum**: The bone column between the roots of a multi-rooted tooth, denser/intact relative to the surrounding socket walls and used as an anchorage site in molar immediate placement.
- **Insertion torque**: Peak torque (Ncm) applied during final implant seating — surrogate for mechanical primary stability.
- **Septal expansion**: Widening the interradicular septum (Smith-Tarnow Type C → Type A/B socket) to enable implant placement without sacrificing septal bone.
