## Why Ingested

[[drug/anticoagulants/al-suliman-2025-surgicel-gelfoam-post-extraction-bleeding-anticoagulant]]와 [[oral-surgery/dinkova-2025-local-hemostasis-oral-surgery-review]]가 정리한 국소 지혈제 목록(콜라겐·산화셀룰로오스·젤라틴 스폰지)에 없던 **이상성 황산칼슘 시멘트(Biphasic Calcium Sulfate, BCS)** 라는 새로운 후보 재료를 다루기 때문에 재료 스펙트럼 보완 목적으로 인제스트했다. 다만 설계가 교란(confounded)되어 있어 — 실험군은 항응고제 유지 + 3D Bond, 대조군은 항응고제 중단 + 콜라겐 — [[drug/anticoagulants/kim-2024-post-extraction-bleeding-direct-oral-anticoagulants]] 같은 유지-vs-중단 근거를 보강하는 자료로 쓸 수 없고, "이런 재료가 제안되었다"는 기록 이상으로 인용해서는 안 된다.

## Three-line Summary

Polish/US four-author study in Cureus comparing 20 patients on uninterrupted vitamin K antagonist (VKA) therapy who received 3D Bond biphasic calcium sulfate cement plus a single suture and compression (24 extractions), against a historical control group of 20 patients from 2005–2006 whose anticoagulation was discontinued with low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) bridging and who received standard collagen sponge compression (24 extractions).

No bleeding occurred in 19/20 (95%) of the cement group versus 16/20 (80%) of the collagen control group; the single cement-group bleed was grade I (tranexamic acid rinse for one day) and the four control-group bleeds were grade II (resuturing, dressing change, three-day tranexamic acid), with mean visual analog scale (VAS) pain 34 mm versus 46 mm.

This 95%-versus-80% gap is **not attributable to the cement**: the two arms differ simultaneously in hemostatic agent, anticoagulant status (mean international normalized ratio, INR 2.54 vs 1.25), treatment era, antibiotic protocol, and suture material, and the between-group bleeding difference was itself statistically non-significant (p = 0.342) — the paper documents that a biphasic calcium sulfate protocol was feasible in 20 uninterrupted-VKA patients, nothing more.

## 세줄요약

폴란드·미국 4인 저자의 Cureus 연구로, 비타민 K 길항제(Vitamin K Antagonist, VKA)를 **중단하지 않은** 환자 20명에게 3D Bond 이상성 황산칼슘(Biphasic Calcium Sulfate, BCS) 시멘트 + 단일 봉합 + 압박을 적용한 군(발치 24개)과, 2005–2006년에 항응고제를 **중단**하고 저분자량 헤파린(Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin, LMWH) 가교요법을 받은 뒤 표준 콜라겐 스폰지 압박을 받은 과거 대조군 20명(발치 24개)을 비교했다.

시멘트군은 19/20(95%)에서 출혈이 없었고 대조군은 16/20(80%)이었으며, 시멘트군의 1건은 I도 출혈(트라넥삼산 양치 1일)·대조군 4건은 II도 출혈(재봉합·드레싱 교체·트라넥삼산 3일)이었고, 시각통증척도(Visual Analog Scale, VAS) 평균 통증은 34 mm 대 46 mm였다.

이 95% 대 80% 차이는 **시멘트의 효과로 볼 수 없다**: 두 군은 지혈재료뿐 아니라 항응고 상태(국제표준화비율 INR 평균 2.54 대 1.25), 치료 시기, 항생제 프로토콜, 봉합사까지 동시에 다르고 군간 출혈 차이 자체가 통계적으로 유의하지 않았다(p = 0.342) — 이 논문이 남기는 것은 "VKA 비중단 환자 20명에서 BCS 프로토콜이 시행 가능했다"는 기록뿐이다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Introduces biphasic calcium sulfate (BCS) regenerative cement (3D Bond, Augma Biomaterials, Katzir, Israel) as a candidate **dual-purpose** socket material — hemostatic plug plus socket-preservation graft — in anticoagulated oral surgery, a role not covered by the conventional collagen/oxidized-cellulose/gelatin-sponge menu.
- Proposes a two-part mechanism: in-situ hardening mechanically occludes cancellous-bone microvasculature within the socket, while released calcium ions may act as a coagulation-cascade cofactor (prothrombin-to-thrombin conversion). The authors explicitly state the calcium-ion mechanism is a thesis requiring in vitro testing, not a demonstrated finding.
- Documents feasibility: 20 consecutive patients underwent 24 extractions on uninterrupted VKA therapy (mean INR 2.54, range 1.80–3.34, including mechanical-valve patients) with a single grade I bleed managed on an outpatient basis.
- Adds to the author group's own series of anticoagulated-extraction hemostasis studies (a 293-patient/560-extraction multicenter series with 6.8% bleeding; a 2021 PARASORB Cone collagen study).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: two-group comparison. Study arm enrolled prospectively; control arm is a **historical cohort treated in 2005–2006** — the arms were not contemporaneous, not randomized, and not blinded. No power calculation is reported.
- **Study group (n = 20; 9 F / 11 M)**: chronic VKA therapy **continued** (acenocoumarol 9 patients, 4.9 ± 1.76 mg/day; warfarin 11 patients, 6.8 ± 2.52 mg/day). Indications: atrial fibrillation 9, mechanical aortic valve 3, mechanical mitral valve 2, mechanical mitral+aortic 2, CABG 2. Mean age 65.5 ± 12.65 y; INR 2.54 ± 0.43.
- **Control group (n = 20; 11 F / 9 M)**: VKA **discontinued** with LMWH bridging (enoxaparin 40–80 mg/day, mean 62.00 ± 15.76) until INR ≤ 1.5. Mean age 64.25 ± 9.11 y; INR after discontinuation 1.25 ± 0.17.
- **Surgical protocol (both arms)**: local anesthesia with 4% articaine; atraumatic removal of erupted teeth, impacted teeth, and roots; one or two teeth/roots per session; socket curettage of inflamed tissue. 24 extractions per arm.
- **Study-arm socket management**: 3D Bond BCS cement placed and compacted with dry sterile gauze plus crestal compression (median 1.0 cm³, range 0.5–1.0; 17 cc total), covered by a single compressed collagen sponge, then single **polydioxanone** resorbable monofilament sutures (4-0 or 5-0) across the crest. Graft exposure permitted. Antibiotics withheld except for mechanical-valve and impacted-tooth patients.
- **Control-arm socket management**: standard collagen dressing only (Cutanplast Dental, Mascia Brunelli SpA, Milano, Italy; 82 sponges total, median 4 per patient) with **polyglycolic acid** sutures. **All** control patients received amoxicillin/clavulanic acid two-shot prophylaxis (2.0 g pre-op, 1.0 g post-op).
- **Bleeding scale (3-point)**: 0 = none; I = seepage requiring drops of tranexamic acid mouthwash for 3–5 days; II = bleeding requiring larger tranexamic acid volume, local re-intervention, and optionally systemic vitamin K 5–10 mg IM or per os.
- **Assessment schedule**: 60 minutes post-op and at days 2, 3, 5, 7, and 10. Pain by 100 mm VAS; wound healing on a 2-point visual scale (0 = correct healing, 1 = inflammatory features). Sutures removed at 10 days.
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk normality check; Student's t-test (Cochran-Cox adjustment when variances unequal); Mann-Whitney U otherwise; chi-squared with Fisher's adjustment for categorical data; test for differences between structural indices; alpha = 0.05. One- or two-tailed critical regions were used "depending on the details of the hypotheses" — the choice per test is not specified.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Primary bleeding outcome**: study arm 19/20 (95%) no bleeding vs control arm 16/20 (80%) no bleeding. The between-group difference was **not statistically significant (Fisher's exact, p = 0.342)** — the paper's own Table 12 reports this, so the headline 95%-vs-80% contrast has no inferential support.
- **Bleeding severity**: the single study-arm event (patient 14, male, tooth 37, INR 2.69) was grade I on day 3, resolved with intraoral tranexamic acid three times daily for one day. All four control-arm events were grade II on day 3, requiring resuturing, collagen dressing change, and three days of tranexamic acid rinsing.
- **Overall cohort bleeding**: 5/40 (12.5%).
- **Coagulation status differed profoundly by design**: INR 2.54 ± 0.43 vs 1.25 ± 0.17 (t = 12.48, p < 0.001, eta² = 0.86); PT median 34.15 s (30.70–50.95) vs 99.26 s (88.65–120.95) (Z = -5.50, p < 0.001, r = 0.87); APTT 54.08 ± 9.79 vs 38.64 ± 5.13 s (t = 6.25, p < 0.001, eta² = 0.58).
- **Baseline comparability**: no significant group differences in age (p = 0.722), body weight (p = 0.518), sex (p = 0.527), cardiological conditions (all p > 0.25), or anticoagulant dose (p = 0.787). Type of dental intervention per patient did not differ (p = 0.508), but **per-tooth type distribution did** (chi² = 8.13, p = 0.043, Cramér's V = 0.41) — incisors 29% vs 4%, bicuspids 17% vs 46%.
- **Sex and bleeding**: all 5 bleeding events occurred in males (Fisher's exact p = 0.047; OR 14.55, 95% CI 0.75–283.39). The confidence interval spans 1, so the odds ratio is uninformative despite the p-value.
- **Non-significant associations with bleeding**: age (p = 0.447), body weight (p = 0.066), cardiological treatment (p = 0.443), anticoagulant type (p = 0.658), anticoagulant dose (p = 0.499), intervention type (p = 0.469), jaw location (p = 0.813), number of teeth removed (p > 0.999), tooth type (p = 0.358).
- **Within the control arm**: bleeding was unrelated to INR class (p > 0.999), PT class (p = 0.549), or APTT class (p > 0.999); bleeders trended toward higher enoxaparin doses (median 80.0 vs 60.0 mg/day, p = 0.080).
- **Pain (VAS)**: study arm 20–45 mm (mean 34) vs control arm 35–65 mm (mean 46). Reported as ranges and means only — no dispersion statistic, no significance test.
- **Wound healing**: impaired healing at day 7 in the four control-arm bleeders (20%), managed with chlorhexidine rinse and Alveogyl dressing. No healing complications reported in the study arm.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Confounded two-group design — the central defect.** The arms differ in at least five variables simultaneously: (1) hemostatic material (3D Bond vs collagen sponge), (2) anticoagulant status (continued, INR 2.54 vs discontinued with LMWH bridging, INR 1.25), (3) treatment era (contemporary vs 2005–2006), (4) antibiotic protocol (withheld in most study patients vs universal amoxicillin/clavulanate in controls), and (5) suture material (polydioxanone vs polyglycolic acid). No between-group difference can be assigned to the cement. The finding must never be stated as "3D Bond reduced bleeding versus collagen."
- **The direction of the confound is adverse to the authors' own interpretation.** The arm reported as bleeding less is the arm that remained fully anticoagulated — the higher-bleeding-risk condition. This makes the comparison uninterpretable rather than conservative: whatever produced the 95%-vs-80% gap, it worked against a hemostatic gradient the design cannot separate from the material.
- **The headline contrast is not statistically significant.** p = 0.342 for the group-by-bleeding comparison. The abstract and conclusions foreground the 95%/80% percentages without carrying this p-value forward.
- **No randomization, no blinding, no power calculation, no sample-size justification.** Bleeding assessment was visual and unblinded, performed by the treating clinicians. With 5 events across 40 patients, the study is powered only to detect very large effects.
- **Historical control arm** (authors' own acknowledgment): retrospective, treated in a different period, introducing selection and procedural variability. Standards of care, materials, and operator experience all shifted over the ~20-year gap.
- **Single-brand, product-specific evaluation.** Only 3D Bond (Augma Biomaterials) was tested; results cannot be generalized to biphasic calcium sulfate as a material class. One co-author is in private practice; no funding or conflict-of-interest statement is captured in the retrieved text.
- **Cureus is a low-barrier, author-pays journal** with limited peer-review depth relative to specialty surgical journals — an additional reason to treat the effect claim as unverified.
- **No objective blood-loss quantification** (authors' acknowledgment); the 3-point ordinal scale is clinician-judged.
- **Secondary outcomes weakly reported.** VAS pain is given as range and mean with no standard deviation and no hypothesis test; the wound-healing difference is fully collinear with the bleeding events (the same four control patients).
- **Statistical reporting concerns**: mixed one- and two-tailed testing without per-test specification; a "significant" sex effect whose 95% CI (0.75–283.39) crosses 1; a significant baseline imbalance in per-tooth type distribution (p = 0.043) dismissed without adjustment.
- **The regenerative claim is unsupported by this study.** Socket preservation is asserted in the abstract and conclusions, but no radiographic or histomorphometric analysis was performed — the authors concede the study was not designed for regenerative outcomes.
- **The calcium-ion coagulation mechanism is a hypothesis**, explicitly requiring in vitro validation the authors have not performed.
- **Authors' stated future work**: prospective, randomized, multicenter trials with larger cohorts and standardized bleeding assessment protocols.

## 6. Related Work

- Bacci et al. — 451 warfarin patients (INR 1.8–4.0) vs 449 healthy controls undergoing identical procedures; no significant difference in post-extraction bleeding (7 vs 4 events), with topical hemostatics, collagen sponges, sutures, fibrin, and tranexamic acid-soaked gauze; no hospitalizations or transfusions.
- Keiani Motlagh et al. — recommends 5% tranexamic acid oral rinse during and after dental surgery in anticoagulated patients; no local bleeding reported.
- Dudek et al. (authors' own multicenter series) — 560 extractions in 293 patients on chronic acenocoumarol/warfarin using collagen, a bovine bone formulation (Tablet, Tecnoss-Dental, Torino, Italy), and tranexamic-acid compressive gauze; overall bleeding rate 6.8%, with significantly higher risk when more than two teeth were extracted.
- Dudek et al. 2021 (authors' own) — PARASORB Cone collagen (iRES SAGL, Switzerland) in 30 patients / 66 extractions on continued anticoagulation vs an LMWH-bridged control arm (50 extractions); three local bleeds in each arm on days 2–3.
- Background literature cited for the continue-versus-interrupt question: reported post-extraction bleeding in anticoagulated patients of ~2–13% depending on INR, extraction count, and local measures; evidence that temporary VKA discontinuation carries thromboembolic risk without substantially reducing bleeding.

## 7. Glossary

- **BCS (Biphasic Calcium Sulfate)**: resorbable graft material combining calcium sulfate hemihydrate and dihydrate phases; sets hard in situ, giving both a mechanical seal and a resorbable scaffold. Marketed here as 3D Bond (Augma Biomaterials).
- **VKA (Vitamin K Antagonist)**: warfarin, acenocoumarol; inhibit hepatic synthesis of clotting factors II, VII, IX, X; monitored by INR.
- **Dihydroxycoumarin derivatives (DD)**: the paper's term for the coumarin-class VKAs used (warfarin, acenocoumarol).
- **INR (International Normalized Ratio)**: standardized prothrombin time ratio; therapeutic range typically 2.0–3.5 for the indications in this cohort.
- **PT (Prothrombin Time)** / **APTT (Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time)**: extrinsic and intrinsic pathway coagulation screening tests.
- **LMWH (Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin)**: enoxaparin here; used as bridging therapy when VKA is interrupted — the control-arm strategy.
- **Bridging therapy**: substituting short-acting parenteral anticoagulation for an interrupted oral anticoagulant across the perioperative window.
- **VAS (Visual Analog Scale)**: 100 mm pain scale, 0 = no pain, 100 = severest possible pain.
- **Socket preservation**: grafting an extraction socket to limit post-extraction alveolar ridge resorption.
- **Confounding**: when two or more variables differ together between compared groups, so an observed outcome difference cannot be attributed to any single one — the defining problem of this study's design.
