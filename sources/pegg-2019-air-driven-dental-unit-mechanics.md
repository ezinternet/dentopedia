## One-line Summary
Narrative review (J Vet Dent 2019) describing the mechanical structure and function of the air-driven dental unit — low-speed handpiece with air micromotor, high-speed handpiece with turbine, plus compressor, rheostat, air-water syringe, suction, fiber-optic light, and handpiece couplings — aimed at equipment troubleshooting and maintenance.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Provides a structured mechanical-level description of both major air-driven handpiece classes: **low-speed (air micromotor)** and **high-speed (air turbine)** — filling the engine-mechanics gap explicitly flagged in the wiki overview.
- Covers the full compressed-air delivery chain: compressor → rheostat (speed control) → handpiece coupling → turbine/micromotor — enabling understanding of torque, speed, and maintenance dependencies.
- Discusses fiber-optic illumination integration, air-water syringe function, and suction mechanics — contextualising the handpiece within the complete dental unit.
- Goal stated: troubleshooting problems and understanding maintenance requirements for optimal equipment function.

## 7. Glossary
- **Rotary vane motor**: Air micromotor mechanism — vanes in a rotor spin as compressed air deflects them, generating rotation; lower RPM (~25,000) but higher torque than turbine
- **Turbine**: Ball-bearing-mounted rotor spun directly by compressed air jets; very high RPM (~300,000–400,000) but lower and more variable torque
- **Rheostat**: Foot-controlled valve regulating air pressure to the handpiece, governing speed
- **Handpiece coupling**: Interface connecting handpiece to dental unit air/water/suction lines; standard types include 2-hole, 4-hole (ISO), Midwest (4-hole variant), and Luer-lock
- **Fiber-optic light**: Glass fibre bundle transmitting light from the unit to illuminate the operative field — integrated into the handpiece head
