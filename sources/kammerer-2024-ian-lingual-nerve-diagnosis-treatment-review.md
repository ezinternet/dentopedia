## Three-line Summary

Scoping review synthesizing international literature on traumatic inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) and lingual nerve injuries from mandibular dental procedures, covering diagnosis through therapeutic interventions.

IAN injury rates 0.4–5.5% for wisdom tooth removal; lingual nerve 0.06–10%; implantology >30% transient injury; neuropathic pain managed with TCAs, SNRIs, antipsychotics; surgical options include decompression, readaptation, reconstruction.

No universal diagnostic gold standard exists; treatment must be tailored per case; immediate intervention for reversible causes yields best outcomes.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Epidemiology / Risk
| Procedure | IAN injury rate | Lingual nerve injury |
|---|---|---|
| Wisdom tooth removal | 0.4–5.5% (permanent); 8–12% transient | 0.06–10% |
| Dental implantology | >30% transient | — |
| Orthognathic surgery | up to 60% transient | — |
| Cystectomy / IANB | ~0.15% | — |

- Neurovascular bundle visualization during surgery → ~20% postoperative paresthesia risk
- Persistent (>3–6 months) impairment: up to 1% of all oral-maxillofacial surgical procedures
- Risk increases with: severe nerve damage evidence, older patient age, injury near main nerve trunk

### Classification (Seddon)
1. **Neuropraxia** — temporary conduction interruption, axonal continuity intact (stretching/pressure)
2. **Axonotmesis** — axonal + myelin loss, connective tissue preserved
3. **Neurotmesis** — complete nerve fiber disruption

### Diagnosis
- No gold standard; comprehensive history + clinical-neurological evaluation + radiographic imaging
- Panoramic radiograph: screening tool
- MRI: recommended for detailed nerve anatomy (conventional radiograph shows only osseous boundaries)
- Neuropathic pain criteria (Schlereth): (1) history aligns with lesion; (2) pain in neuroanatomically plausible area; (3) pathologic sensory findings in same area; (4) examination identifies peripheral somatosensory lesion
- Distinguish: certain / probable / possible / unlikely nerve injury at first presentation
- Comorbidities (anxiety, depression, sleep disorders) must be assessed → interdisciplinary care

### Sensory symptoms
- Primary deficits: anesthesia, hypoesthesia, paresthesia
- Neuropathic pain: dysesthesia, allodynia, hyperalgesia
- IAN injury functional effects: uncontrolled salivation, lip biting, speech difficulties
- Lingual nerve injury additional effects: taste loss, tongue biting, articulation difficulties, eating dysfunction
- Quality-of-life impairment: psychological and social limitations documented

### Treatment
**Surgical (reversible/acute causes)**
- Immediate action recommended when reversible cause identified
- Procedures: decompression, readaptation (end-to-end), reconstruction (nerve graft)
- Favorable outcomes reported for timely surgical intervention

**Conservative (neuropathic pain)**
- Topical: local anesthetics (topical), capsaicin
- Systemic: tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs), antipsychotics, serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs)
- Goal: reduce dysesthesia/allodynia, improve QoL

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No systematic guideline currently exists for diagnosing/treating IAN or lingual nerve injuries in dental practice
- Under-reporting of cases limits true incidence estimates
- Scoping/narrative design: no meta-analysis, no pooled effect sizes
- Treatment recommendations based on heterogeneous literature; evidence quality not formally graded
- Calls for development of standardized diagnostic and treatment protocols

## 7. Glossary

- **IAN**: Inferior Alveolar Nerve (하치조신경)
- **Lingual nerve** (설신경): branch of V3 at risk during mandibular procedures
- **Neuropraxia** (신경압박손상): reversible conduction block, no structural damage
- **Axonotmesis** (축삭손상): axon disrupted, endoneurium intact
- **Neurotmesis** (신경절단): complete nerve severance
- **Hypoesthesia** (감각저하): reduced sensory perception
- **Paresthesia** (이상감각): abnormal sensation (tingling, burning)
- **Dysesthesia** (불쾌이상감각): unpleasant abnormal sensation
- **Allodynia** (이질통증): pain from normally non-painful stimulus
- **Hyperalgesia** (통각과민): exaggerated pain response
- **TCA**: Tricyclic antidepressant (삼환계 항우울제)
- **SNRI**: Serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor
