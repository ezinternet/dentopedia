## Why Ingested

Part of the "healing abutment (HA) reuse" controversy batch. [[infection-control/bidra-2019-should-healing-abutments-cover-screws]] is the anchor SR framing whether reused HAs can be adequately decontaminated; the in vitro papers in the batch (burioni-2024, abreu-2024, cetinsoy-2026, naghsh-2024) argue over how much residual protein/biofilm survives cleaning and whether the biologic response tolerates reuse. This Kyaw 2023 study is the **highest study design in the set (RCT on patient-retrieved HAs)** and it adds a device-integrity axis the others omit — it does not just ask "is the surface clean?" but "does repeated reuse + cleaning damage the implant–HA interface (contact surface area, micro-gap, microleakage)?" It stakes out the pro-reuse-with-rigorous-protocol pole: Ti HAs can be reused multiple times **if** a combined chemical + electrochemical protocol is used, in tension with abreu-2024's biologic "do not reuse."

## Three-line Summary


RCT on 90 implants + 90 HAs (80 patient-retrieved): repeated NaOCl-only cleaning progressively enlarged micro-gap/microleakage and shrank implant–HA contact surface, whereas combined chemical + electrochemical decontamination removed debris up to triple use without altering the surface, near the new-HA control — so Ti HA reuse is acceptable multiple times only with the combined protocol.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Effect of chemical and electrochemical decontamination protocols on single and multiple-used healing abutments: A comparative analysis of contact surface area, micro-gap, micro-leakage, and surface topography
- **Authors**: Kyaw TT, Abdou A, Arunjaroensuk S, Nakata H, Kanazawa M, Pimkhaokham A
- **Journal**: Clinical Implant Dentistry and Related Research 2023;25(6):1207–1215 (e-pub 2023-09-01)
- **DOI**: 10.1111/cid.13269 | **PMID**: 37654160
- **Study type**: Randomized controlled trial (bench/RCT on patient-retrieved healing abutments) — highest study design in the reuse batch
- **Source**: PubMed abstract; full text not retrieved (no PMC; paywalled) — **abstract-only**

## 2. Key Contributions

- Adds a **device-integrity axis** to the HA-reuse debate that the in vitro cleaning studies omit: instead of only asking "is the surface decontaminated?", it measures whether repeated reuse + cleaning damages the **implant–HA interface** (contact surface area, micro-gap volume, microleakage).
- Directly contrasts **NaOCl-only** cleaning against **NaOCl + electrochemical** cleaning across escalating reuse frequency (single → double → triple → >triple use).
- Finds NaOCl-only cleaning is not integrity-neutral: repeated cycles **changed the implant–HA contact surface area** and progressively worsened micro-gap/microleakage.
- Establishes a **pro-reuse-with-rigorous-protocol** conclusion: Ti HAs can be reused multiple times **provided** a combined chemical + electrochemical decontamination protocol is used.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Specimens**: 90 bone-level titanium implants assembled with 90 bone-level titanium HAs; **80 of the HAs were contaminated HAs collected from human participants** (patient-retrieved), plus new HAs.
- **Cleaning groups**:
  - **Group I**: ultrasonication with 5.25% NaOCl (15 min) + steam autoclave.
  - **Group II**: ultrasonication with 5.25% NaOCl (15 min) + **electrochemical cleaning** + steam autoclave.
  - **Group III (control)**: 10 new, unused HAs.
- **Reuse tiers** (cleaning applied after each insertion): (a) single-use + 1 cycle; (b) double-use + 2 cycles; (c) triple-use + 3 cycles; (d) >triple-use + >3 cycles. Groups are labeled by protocol + tier (e.g., Ia, Ib, Ic, Id; IIa, IIb, IIc, IId).
- **Assessments**:
  - Contact surface area + **micro-gap volume** by **micro-CT** (X-ray microtomography).
  - **Microleakage** by 2% methylene blue penetration.
  - Surface morphology by **SEM**; elemental composition by **EDX**.
- Further per-group numerical detail (means, dimensions) is not available — **abstract-only**.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Group Id** (NaOCl only, >triple use) had the **smallest implant–HA contact surface area**.
- **Micro-gap volume and microleakage differed significantly (p < 0.001)**, in descending order:
  **Id > Ic > Ib > IId > Ia, IIa, and III.**
  - Interpretation: repeated **NaOCl-only** reuse progressively worsened micro-gap/microleakage (Id worst), while the **combined chemical + electrochemical** groups stayed near the new-HA control (III).
- **Groups IIa, IIb, IIc** (combined protocol, up to triple use): residual biological debris was **optimally removed without altering surface properties**.
- Effect sizes / exact micro-gap volumes not reported here — **abstract-only**.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only**: full-text methods, per-group numbers, and statistical detail were not retrieved.
- Bench-level device-integrity outcomes (contact surface, micro-gap, microleakage, surface topography) — **does not measure the biologic/tissue response** to reused HAs (the axis where [[infection-control/abreu-2024-assessment-detoxification-strategies-used-dental]] concludes inflammation persists → do not reuse). Clean interface ≠ tolerated interface.
- **Electrochemical cleaning is not a routine chairside/CSSD capability** — the pro-reuse conclusion is conditional on equipment most clinics lack; generalizability to typical NaOCl/autoclave-only reuse (which this very study shows degrades the interface) is limited.
- Single implant/HA system; no in vivo peri-implant tissue or clinical follow-up.

## 7. Glossary

- **Healing abutment (HA)**: transmucosal component placed on an implant during soft-tissue healing; frequently reused across patients, the practice this batch scrutinizes.
- **Micro-gap**: the microscopic gap at the implant–abutment interface; a larger micro-gap promotes bacterial leakage and microleakage.
- **Microleakage**: penetration of fluid/bacteria across the implant–abutment interface, here quantified by 2% methylene blue dye penetration.
- **Contact surface area**: the area of intimate contact between implant and abutment; reduction indicates a compromised, less-sealed interface.
- **Electrochemical cleaning**: decontamination using an electric current in an electrolyte to remove adherent contaminants from a metal surface; the added step distinguishing Group II.
- **Micro-CT (X-ray microtomography)**: high-resolution 3D imaging used here to measure contact surface area and micro-gap volume.
- **SEM / EDX**: scanning electron microscopy (surface morphology) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (elemental composition).
