## Why Ingested

Largest pharmacological NMA specifically targeting painful intra-articular TMDs (disc displacement + OA) with 23 RCTs and 1264 patients — updates the TMD-joint pharmacological component of [[tmj/haggman-henrikson-2017-pharmacological-orofacial-pain-sr-ma]] (which combined TMD-joint, TMD-muscle, and BMS in a single SBU health technology assessment). Provides 2026-current rankings for MESNA, hyaluronate, palmitoylethanolamide, glucosamine, and avocado-soybean extract across disc displacement and OA subgroups.

## Three-line Summary

SR + NMA (7 databases; 23 RCTs, 1264 patients; Cochrane RoB 2.0; CINeMA certainty of evidence) comparing pharmacological treatments — used alone or adjunctively — for painful intra-articular TMD (disc displacement and osteoarthritis).

For TMJ disc displacement: short-term pain reductions with multiple interventions; MESNA and HA arthrocentesis showed sustained effects at >3 months; for TMJ OA: palmitoylethanolamide and avocado-soybean extract reduced short-term pain; glucosamine + HA showed long-term pain and mouth-opening benefit — all low to very low certainty.

No pharmacological treatment for intra-articular TMD can be recommended with confidence; estimates were generally below the minimal clinically important difference, and no clear superior intervention emerged — current management should be guided by clinical judgment until higher-quality RCTs are available.

## 세줄요약

SR+NMA (7 DB; 23 RCT, 1264명; Cochrane RoB 2.0; CINeMA 근거 확실성) — 통증성 관절내 악관절증(원판 변위+골관절염) 약물치료(단독 또는 보조) 비교 네트워크 메타분석.

TMJ 원판 변위(Disc Displacement, DD): 여러 중재가 단기 통증 유의 감소; MESNA·히알루론산(HA) 관절강세척은 장기(>3개월) 효과도 유의. TMJ 골관절염(Osteoarthritis, OA): 팔미토일에탄올아미드(Palmitoylethanolamide, PEA)·아보카도-콩 추출물 단기 통증 감소; 글루코사민+HA 장기 통증·개구량 유의 개선 — 전반적 근거 확실성 매우 낮음~낮음.

어떤 약물치료도 자신감 있게 권고할 수 없음 — 대부분 효과 추정치가 최소 임상 중요차(MCID) 이하이며, 이질성과 매우 낮은 근거 수준이 임상 권고를 제한.

## Key Contributions

- Largest NMA (23 RCTs, 1264 patients) focused exclusively on intra-articular TMDs (DD + OA)
- Separates disc displacement and OA subgroups — allows more targeted pharmacological comparison than earlier generic TMD SRs
- Identifies MESNA + HA arthrocentesis for long-term disc displacement pain as potentially effective
- Identifies glucosamine + HA as the only intervention with long-term OA evidence for both pain and mouth opening
- Uniformly low to very low certainty of evidence — highlights urgent need for high-quality RCTs

## Methodology

- **Design**: Frequentist network meta-analysis
- **Databases**: 7 databases searched (inception to publication date)
- **Population**: Adults with painful intra-articular TMD (disc displacement and osteoarthritis)
- **Interventions**: Pharmacological agents alone or adjunctive (MESNA, HA, PEA, avocado-soybean extract, glucosamine HCl/sulfate, others)
- **Outcomes**: Pain intensity (VAS/NRS), maximum mouth opening (mm)
- **Time points**: Short-term (≤3 months), long-term (>3 months)
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB 2.0
- **Certainty**: CINeMA approach
- **Sample**: 23 RCTs, 1264 patients

## Key Results

**TMJ Disc Displacement (DD):**
- Short-term: Multiple interventions showed statistically significant pain reductions
- Long-term: MESNA (sodium 2-mercaptoethanesulfonate) and HA arthrocentesis maintained significant effects
- Mouth opening: Multiple interventions showed significant improvement
- Certainty: Low to very low across outcomes

**TMJ Osteoarthritis (OA):**
- Short-term pain: Palmitoylethanolamide and avocado-soybean unsaponifiables showed significant pain reduction
- Long-term: Glucosamine HCl or sulfate combined with HA showed significant improvement in both pain and mouth opening
- Short-term mouth opening: No intervention showed significant effect
- Certainty: Low to very low across outcomes

**Overall**: No intervention above MCID threshold with confidence; results should be interpreted cautiously due to heterogeneity and low certainty

## Limitations

- Low to very low certainty of evidence throughout
- Substantial heterogeneity in most analyses
- Most effect estimates below the minimal clinically important difference
- No dominant treatment identified for either DD or OA subgroup
- Urgent need for standardized, high-quality RCTs with clear comparators and longer follow-up
