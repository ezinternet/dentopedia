## Three-line Summary

Narrative review synthesizing anatomical, physiological, and clinical evidence on somatosensory (somatic) tinnitus — tinnitus modulated by temporomandibular joint (TMJ), head/neck, or eye-movement maneuvers.

Somatic modulation of tinnitus loudness/pitch occurs in roughly two-thirds of tested patients (range 33%–87% across studies), TMJ maneuvers are the most frequent modulating region (up to 94.3% loudness increase with clenching/opening/protrusion), and TMJ disorder treatment resolves or improves tinnitus in an average of 69% of patients across 16 pooled studies (1964–2014).

Clinically, somatic screening (history of TMJ/head-neck dysfunction plus a standardized somatic maneuver battery) is essential to identify tinnitus patients — especially those with normal hearing — who would benefit from a multidisciplinary somatic (dental/gnathologic/osteopathic/physiotherapy) approach rather than purely otologic management.

## 세줄요약

체성감각 이명 (Somatosensory Tinnitus, ST)의 해부-생리-임상적 근거를 종합한 서술적 리뷰로, 턱관절(Temporomandibular Joint, TMJ)·두경부·안구운동 조작으로 조절되는 이명을 다룬다.

체성 조작에 의한 이명 크기/음고 변조는 검사 대상자의 약 2/3(연구별 33~87%)에서 관찰되며, TMJ가 가장 흔한 변조 부위(악물기·최대개구·하악 전방 이동 시 최대 94.3%에서 이명 증가)이고, TMD 치료 후 16개 연구(1964~2014) 종합 평균 69%에서 이명이 호전 또는 소실되었다.

임상적으로는 정상 청력을 가진 이명 환자를 중심으로 TMJ/두경부 기능장애 병력 확인과 표준화된 체성 조작 검사를 통한 체성 선별검사가 필수적이며, 이를 통해 치과(교합)·구강안면통증·정형외과·물리치료 등 다학제적 체성 접근이 필요한 환자군을 식별할 수 있다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Synthesizes the neuroanatomical basis for auditory–somatosensory convergence (cochlear nucleus/CN) as the substrate for tinnitus modulation by non-auditory somatic stimuli.
- Reviews clinical prevalence data on tinnitus modulation by TMJ, head/neck, and eye-movement maneuvers across multiple published cohorts (Levine, Sanchez et al., Rubinstein et al., Won et al., Kapoula et al., Simmons et al., and the authors' own 310-patient cohort).
- Summarizes the epidemiological association between TMJ disorders and tinnitus (prevalence, risk ratios, incidence) from cross-sectional and cohort studies (Bernhardt et al., Buergers et al., Lee et al., Manfredini et al., SHIP-0 cohort).
- Pools 16 studies (1964–2014) on tinnitus outcome after TMJ disorder treatment, reporting an average 69% improvement/resolution rate.
- Proposes a clinical framework: somatic history-taking + a standardized somatic maneuver battery (19 maneuvers in the authors' prior study) to identify candidates for multidisciplinary somatic tinnitus management.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

This is a narrative (non-systematic) review; no PRISMA flow, no formal quality appraisal, no meta-analytic pooling described (the "average 69%" figure is a simple summary across 16 cited studies, not a weighted meta-analysis). The review draws on:

- Animal-model electrophysiology studies (dorsal cochlear nucleus/DCN, ventral cochlear nucleus/VCN neuron firing changes after cochlear deafferentation and trigeminal/somatosensory stimulation).
- Human imaging/tractography studies (auditory-tactile synesthesia after thalamic infarct; A1–SII connections).
- Multiple independent clinical cohorts assessing tinnitus modulation by somatic maneuvers (TMJ clenching/opening/protrusion, head/neck pressure/movement, eye gaze, limb stimulation).
- The authors' own prior cohort (310 patients with somatosensory tinnitus, normal hearing, no psychiatric comorbidity, positive TMJ/head-neck history and/or positive somatic modulation), using a 19-maneuver somatic examination protocol (TMJ maneuvers + head/neck resistance maneuvers).
- Epidemiological studies linking TMJ disorder diagnosis to tinnitus incidence/prevalence (cross-sectional and prospective cohort designs).
- Treatment-outcome studies of TMJ therapy (stabilization splints, physiotherapy, cognitive therapy, cervical manipulation) and their effect on tinnitus symptoms.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Prevalence of somatic modulation of tinnitus** (varies by study/maneuver set):
- Levine (1999, n=70): 68% could modulate tinnitus with head/neck maneuvers; 80% when TMJ maneuvers added.
- Sanchez et al. (n=121): 65.3% modulation.
- Rubinstein et al.: ~33% (one-third) modulation via TMJ pressure/jaw movement.
- Other cited studies: 85%, 83.3%, 79%, 57.9%, 78%, 57.1%.
- Ward et al. (large UK cohort): self-reported modulation prevalence 16%; predicted by age, pulsatility, loudness, and TMJ complaints.

**TMJ as dominant modulating region** (authors' 310-patient cohort):
- 94.31% of TMJ maneuvers (clenching, maximal mouth opening, jaw protrusion) increased tinnitus loudness; only 5.69% decreased it.
- Head/neck maneuvers: 59.1% increased loudness, 40.9% decreased loudness — a markedly different pattern from TMJ.
- Strongest modulation from occiput, temple, and sub-mandibular pressure resistance.

**TMJ disorder–tinnitus association**:
- Bernhardt et al.: TMJ palpation pain → 12% developed tinnitus at 5 years, risk ratio 2.4 (95% CI 1.6–3.7).
- Buergers et al.: tinnitus incidence 36.6% (with TMD) vs 4.4% (without TMD), >8-fold higher.
- Lee et al. (retrospective cohort): 3.22-fold higher tinnitus risk over 3-year follow-up (95% CI 2.67–3.89).
- Manfredini et al.: ~30% tinnitus prevalence in TMD patients.
- Vielsmeier et al. (n=1204): concurrent tinnitus+TMJ complaints in 22%; these patients were significantly younger, earlier onset, more often female.

**Treatment effect on tinnitus**:
- Tullberg and Ernberg: 2 years post-TMJ treatment, 43% treatment group vs 12% control had decreased tinnitus.
- Buergers et al.: stomatognathic therapy (occlusal stabilization + physiotherapy) improved tinnitus in 44% (11/25).
- Pooled across 16 studies (1964–2014): average 69% improvement/resolution, 32% no change.
- Authors' cohort: within-subject correlation between positive somatic history and positive somatic modulation in the same anatomical region — 76.2% for TMJ in unilateral tinnitus, 56.0% for TMJ+head/neck in bilateral tinnitus (p<0.001).

**Demographic predictors of modulation**: inconsistent across studies — Pinchoff et al. found male predominance, Won et al. found female association, the authors' own cohort found no strong demographic predictor but noted high-pitched tinnitus was 2.4× more frequent and male sex conferred 1.4–2.0× greater modulation likelihood.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Narrative (not systematic) review — no formal search strategy, quality appraisal, or risk-of-bias assessment; the "69% improvement" figure is a simple across-study average, not a weighted or adjusted meta-analytic estimate.
- Underlying pathophysiology of somatosensory tinnitus remains partly unclear; the dorsal-cochlear-nucleus convergence model is derived mainly from animal (rodent) deafferentation studies and extrapolated to humans.
- Demographic predictors of who is able to modulate tinnitus are inconsistent across studies (sex, age, pulsatility findings conflict).
- The authors explicitly call for further animal studies to clarify pathophysiology and further human studies to determine how much correct diagnosis/treatment of the underlying somatic disorder contributes to tinnitus management.
- No RCT-level evidence is synthesized for treatment efficacy; treatment-outcome data come from heterogeneous, mostly uncontrolled or weakly controlled studies (stabilization splints, physiotherapy, cervical manipulation, myofascial trigger-point injection, laser therapy, TENS, magnet therapy) without comparative pooling.

## 7. Glossary

- **Somatosensory (somatic) tinnitus**: Tinnitus that can be evoked or modulated by inputs from the somatosensory, somatomotor, or visual-motor systems (e.g., TMJ, head/neck, eye movements) rather than solely by the auditory system.
- **Otic tinnitus**: Tinnitus attributable primarily to hearing loss / auditory pathway pathology, without somatic modulation.
- **Dorsal cochlear nucleus (DCN) / ventral cochlear nucleus (VCN)**: Brainstem auditory nuclei that also receive somatosensory (trigeminal, dorsal column) afferents; proposed site of auditory-somatosensory integration underlying somatic tinnitus.
- **VGLUT1/VGLUT2 (vesicular glutamate transporter 1/2)**: Markers distinguishing auditory (VGLUT1) vs somatosensory (VGLUT2) glutamatergic terminals in the CN; VGLUT2+ terminals increase after cochlear deafferentation.
- **Somatic maneuver battery**: A standardized set of TMJ/head-neck provocation maneuvers (e.g., clenching, maximal opening, jaw protrusion, occipital/temporal/sub-mandibular pressure) used to test whether a patient's tinnitus can be modulated.
- **Gaze-evoked tinnitus**: Tinnitus modulated by eye position, classically reported after acoustic neuroma surgery.
