## Why Ingested

SR quantifying IAN neuropathy prevalence across implant modalities — fills a critical evidence-base gap for mandibular implant risk counseling. Directly extends [[oral-surgery/lee-2015-inferior-alveolar-nerve-injury-management]] with pooled prevalence figures and a management evidence summary.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (38 studies, meta-analysis) of neuropathy prevalence and management after mandibular dental implant placement.

Without nerve lateralization: 12% neuropathy at 1 week, resolving to 5% after ≥3 months; with lateralization: 90% at 1 week, 42% after ≥3 months — an ~8-fold higher burden.

Pharmacological management was most common; laser therapy and implant removal were also reported, but only 5 management studies existed, limiting treatment conclusions.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First SR to separately pool prevalence for non-lateralization vs nerve-lateralization implant procedures
- Establishes that ≥3-month neuropathy (irreversible threshold per ICOP) is 5% (non-lateralization) vs 42% (lateralization)
- Identifies the paucity of management RCTs (only 1 RCT, n = 5 total management studies)
- Provides JBI methodological quality ratings: 18 high / 16 moderate / 4 low quality studies

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Databases searched**: Cochrane, Embase, LILACS, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science + grey literature (Google Scholar, OpenGrey, ProQuest Dissertations & Theses); search date: 01 December 2023
- **Study designs included**: observational (cross-sectional, case series, case-control) for prevalence; interventional (RCT, non-RCT, before-and-after) for management
- **Population**: Adults (≥18 y) undergoing mandibular dental implant surgery; any implant brand; any neuropathy diagnostic criteria accepted
- **Outcome**: (1) prevalence of neuropathy/nerve injury; (2) treatment outcomes
- **Quality tool**: Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal tools by study design
- **Meta-analysis**: Weighted average prevalence via MetaXL 5.3 (EpiGear), 95% CI; two time frames: ≤1 week and ≥3 months
- **Included studies**: 38 qualitative; 30 for meta-analysis (1 thesis excluded from quantitative as also published as article)

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Prevalence — non-lateralization (13 studies)

| Time frame | Prevalence | 95% CI | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 week post-op | **12%** | 4–22% | 364 |
| ≥3 months post-op | **5%** | 1–11% | 662 |

- Most studies used questionnaires; numbness was most common symptom followed by paresthesia
- Two studies showed neuropathy even when implant had direct IAN contact, but all resolved

### Prevalence — nerve lateralization (25 studies)

| Time frame | Prevalence | 95% CI | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 week post-op | **90%** | 72–100% | 503 |
| ≥3 months post-op | **42%** | 24–60% | 493 |

- 9 studies showed 100% neuropathy at 1 week post-lateralization
- Lateralization carries ~8× higher neuropathy burden at both time frames

### Management (5 studies: 1 RCT, 2 non-RCT/before-after, 2 other)

- Pharmacological: most common (analgesics, anti-inflammatory, steroids, membrane stabilizers)
- Laser therapy: low-level laser therapy reported in some studies
- Implant removal: reserved for persistent/severe cases
- Sample sizes ranged 16–64 patients; follow-up 7 days to 7 years
- IAN was the most affected nerve; lip, chin, and tongue the most affected sites

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Heterogeneity in diagnostic methods (self-report vs clinical examination vs QST)
- Pooling of retrospective and prospective studies in the same meta-analysis
- Only 1 RCT for management outcomes — very low management evidence base
- Long-term data (>1 year) limited for the non-lateralization group
- Publication bias not formally assessed

## 6. Related Work

- Prior SRs identified by authors showed early/correct diagnosis improves recovery (93% vs 78% late diagnosis)
- One prior SR reported 13% altered sensation at 10 days → 3% at 1 year (consistent with current 12% → 5% non-lateralization finding)

## 7. Glossary

- **IAN**: Inferior Alveolar Nerve — the main sensory branch of the mandibular division of trigeminal nerve running through the mandibular canal
- **Nerve lateralization**: Surgical technique repositioning the IAN laterally to place implants in severely atrophic posterior mandible
- **Paresthesia**: Abnormal sensation (tingling, numbness) without obvious external stimulus
- **Hypoesthesia**: Reduced sensitivity to sensory stimuli
- **QST**: Quantitative Sensory Testing — standardized psychophysical tests of sensory nerve function
- **JBI**: Joanna Briggs Institute critical appraisal tools
- **ICOP**: International Classification of Orofacial Pain — defines irreversible neuropathy as symptoms >3 months
