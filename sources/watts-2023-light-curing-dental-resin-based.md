## Why Ingested

This is the foundational mechanistic science review explaining HOW light-curing works at the photon/photoinitiator/polymer level. Fills the critical understanding gap in the resin category: how photons penetrate composites, how photoinitiators (Type I vs Type II) absorb energy and generate free radicals, why reciprocity is not reliably assumed, and how clinicians should select and use LED-LCUs for effective bulk-fill curing.

## Three-line Summary

Watts (2023) provides a comprehensive narrative review of the photophysics and chemistry underlying light-curing of dental resin-based composites, including bulk-fill materials. The review covers photon wave-particle duality, Beer-Lambert attenuation in composites, Norrish Type I vs Type II photoinitiator mechanisms, LED-LCU technology, and the reciprocity hypothesis. Clinical implications include LCU selection criteria, depth-of-cure verification, and adverse consequences of under-curing.

## Key Contributions

1. **Photon physics framework** — Particle-wave duality; energy E = hν; why blue (470 nm) penetrates deeper than violet (410 nm) due to less scattering by filler particles
2. **Beer-Lambert law in composites** — Exponential irradiance decay with depth; attenuation = absorption coefficient + scattering coefficient
3. **Photoinitiator classification** — Norrish Type II (CQ + amine co-initiator, ~470 nm absorption) vs Type I (acylphosphine oxides: TPO, BAPO, bond-cleavage, ~410 nm); both generate free radicals
4. **LED-LCU technology** — Monowave (~470 nm only) vs polywave/multi-chip (470 nm + 410 nm LED arrays); beam profile uniformity critical for even cure
5. **Reciprocity hypothesis critique** — Dose = irradiance × time, but shortening time while raising irradiance does NOT always give equivalent curing outcomes; over-optimistic assumption for many formulations
6. **Depth of Cure standard** — Bulk fill defined as DoC ≥ 4 mm; bottom/top hardness ratio should be >95%
7. **Under-curing adverse consequences** — Resin matrix breakdown, increased monomer elution, reduced bond strength, increased bacterial colonization, secondary caries

## Key Results

- **DC (degree of conversion)** typically 60–70% for well-polymerized dimethacrylate composites; 100% unreachable due to vitrification
- **Filler particle size effect** — particles >470 nm scatter blue light; nanoparticles (100 nm) do not scatter
- **Violet vs blue penetration** — violet (410 nm) scatters more, penetrates less; relevant limitation for bulk-fill at 4 mm depths
- **Reciprocity** — applies to Type I PI systems or composites with certain viscosity ranges, but exceptions and anomalies exist; ultra-rapid 3–5 s cure requires specially formulated RBCs
- **Beam profile** — many commercial LCUs have non-uniform "hot" and "cold" spots; top-hat profile ideal

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Photoinitiator (PI) | Molecule that absorbs photons and generates free radicals to initiate polymerization |
| Norrish Type I | PI mechanism: direct photolytic bond cleavage (e.g., TPO, BAPO, acylphosphine oxides) |
| Norrish Type II | PI mechanism: bimolecular; excited PI reacts with amine co-initiator (e.g., CQ/amine) |
| Camphorquinone (CQ) | Yellow Type II photoinitiator; peak absorption ~470 nm blue light |
| TPO / BAPO | Acylphosphine oxide Type I photoinitiators; absorb ~410 nm violet |
| Irradiance | Power per unit area incident on target (mW/cm²) |
| Radiant exposure (dose) | Energy per unit area = irradiance × time (mJ/cm²) |
| Degree of Conversion (DC) | % of C=C bonds converted to C-C bonds; typically 60–70% for dimethacrylates |
| Depth of Cure (DoC) | Depth at which bottom/top hardness ratio ≥ 95%; bulk fill ≥ 4 mm |
| Reciprocity hypothesis | Assumption that equal dose gives equal cure regardless of irradiance/time trade-off |
| Monowave LCU | LED-LCU with single ~470 nm blue peak |
| Polywave / multi-chip LCU | LED-LCU with both ~470 nm blue and ~410 nm violet chips |
| Beer-Lambert law | Exponential decay of irradiance with depth in composite |
| Vitrification | Transition to glassy state during polymerization that self-limits further conversion |
