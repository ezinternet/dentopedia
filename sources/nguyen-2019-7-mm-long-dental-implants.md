## Why Ingested
Adds Korean single-centre retrospective data on 7-mm implants (short dental implants, SDIs) placed without bone augmentation in medically compromised patients (systemic disorders, oral cancer reconstruction, osteomyelitis/BRONJ/ORN). Complements the reduced-dimension implant evidence → [[implants/short-narrow/pohl-2017-short-dental-implants-long-dental]] and the short-vs-standard survival evidence → [[implants/short-narrow/kim-2026-long-term-outcomes-short-standard-implants]].

## Three-line Summary
Retrospective single-surgeon study at Seoul National University Dental Hospital: 33 medically compromised patients (11 male, 22 female; mean age 62 years) received 47 internal-connection tapered 7-mm implants (4.0 mm n=38, 4.5 mm n=8, 5.0 mm n=1) without bone augmentation, followed for 7 to 36 months (mean 15 months). Implant survival from stage I surgery to last follow-up was 95.74% (2 of 47 failed; both 4.0-mm implants in one oral-cancer reconstruction patient), and mean marginal bone loss (MBL) at 2 years was 0.58 +/- 0.60 mm mesial and 0.71 +/- 0.60 mm distal, with most change occurring in the first year. Authors conclude 7-mm implants are a reliable option when sinus lift or vertical grafting should be avoided, but the small sample, short follow-up, heterogeneous medical groups, and absence of a control group limit generalisation.

## One-line Summary
Retrospective (33 patients, 47 7-mm implants, mean 15 months): 95.74% survival in medically compromised patients with 2-year MBL of 0.58-0.71 mm.

## 한줄요약
후향적(환자 33명, 7mm 임플란트 47개, 평균 15개월): 전신 위험 환자에서 생존율 95.74%, 2년 변연골 소실 0.58~0.71mm.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Reports survival of 7-mm implants specifically in medically compromised patients, a population with limited short-implant data
- Documents MBL on panoramic radiographs over 2 years (installation, 3 months, 1 year, 2 years)
- Presents three illustrative cases, one per medical-condition group
- Frames 7-mm implants as an alternative to sinus lifting or vertical bone grafting in these patients

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: retrospective data analysis, single maxillofacial implant surgeon
- **Population**: 33 patients, 47 implants; 11 male, 22 female; age 30 to 82 years (mean 62)
- **Inclusion**: (1) at least one of three groups: systemic disorders (controlled or uncontrolled diabetes mellitus, mental disability, uncontrolled hypertension); oral cancer ablation with reconstruction at the implant site, with or without radiotherapy; diverse osteomyelitis (osteoradionecrosis, BRONJ); (2) internal submerged tapered Luna or internal non-submerged Stella sand-blasted and acid-etched implants (Shinhung, Seoul); (3) no bone augmentation at the site. All had insufficient residual bone due to mandibular atrophy or sinus pneumatization.
- **Implants**: all 7 mm long; diameter 4.0 mm (n=38), 4.5 mm (n=8), 5.0 mm (n=1); 6 Stella and 41 Luna; 19 maxilla, 28 mandible
- **Surgery**: 1- or 2-stage with a 3- to 6-month interval; good primary stability initially in all
- **Failure definition**: lost or mobile implant, or severe peri-implantitis requiring prompt removal
- **MBL**: panoramic radiographs; distance from implant shoulder to most coronal bone-to-implant contact, mesial and distal; each aspect measured 3 times and averaged; at installation, 3 months, 1 year, 2 years
- **Statistics**: SPSS 25.0, descriptive statistics, mean +/- SD, Shapiro-Wilk normality test; single analyst

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Follow-up**: 7 to 36 months, mean 15 months
- **Prostheses on 45 surviving implants**: 19 single crowns, 22 multiple fixed prostheses, 4 removable overdentures
- **Survival**: 2 of 47 failed; survival 95.74% (stage I surgery to last follow-up). By diameter: 4.0 mm 94.74%, 4.5 mm 100%, 5.0 mm 100%; differences not significant (p=0.069 reported, with ">0.05")
- **Failures**: both 4.0-mm implants, in one patient with oral cancer treated by mandibular resection and reconstruction; discussion attributes failure to insufficient volume and unfavourable quality of postoperative bone; most failures occurred in the healing phase at abutment connection
- **MBL (mesial / distal)**: installation to 1 year 0.53 +/- 0.58 / 0.67 +/- 0.56 mm; installation to 2 years 0.58 +/- 0.60 / 0.71 +/- 0.60 mm; 3 months to 1 year increase 0.19 +/- 0.24 / 0.14 +/- 0.02 mm; 1 to 2 years increase 0.05 +/- 0.12 / 0.04 +/- 0.05 mm
- **MBL tests**: MBL at 3 months, 1 year, 2 years significantly higher than at installation; 1 year significantly higher than 3 months; 1 and 2 years not significantly different
- **Benchmark**: authors state MBL is within the classic success threshold (mean MBL <=1.5 mm in year 1 and <=0.2 mm per year thereafter)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Retrospective, single-centre, single-surgeon design; no control group of standard-length implants or of healthy patients
- Small sample (33 patients, 47 implants) with heterogeneous medical groups; survival not analysed per group beyond noting both failures were in the oral cancer reconstruction patient
- Short follow-up (mean 15 months; range 7 to 36), so 2-year MBL data come from a subset; censoring not described
- Panoramic radiograph MBL measurement; single analyst
- Failed implants placed "adventurously" in compromised bone per the authors, a case-selection confounder
- Authors call for long-term follow-up

## 6. Related Work
- Short dental implants are defined as <8 mm; authors cite no distinct linear relation between length and survival
- Concurs with literature on diabetes and uncontrolled hypertension as potential risks to osseointegration; negative bisphosphonate effects cited
- See Why Ingested for linked wiki pages

## 7. Glossary
- **SDI**: short dental implant, <8 mm
- **MBL**: marginal bone loss, shoulder to most coronal bone-to-implant contact
- **BRONJ**: bisphosphonate-related osteonecrosis of the jaw
- **ORN**: osteoradionecrosis
- **Luna / Stella**: internal submerged tapered / internal non-submerged S&E implants (Shinhung)
