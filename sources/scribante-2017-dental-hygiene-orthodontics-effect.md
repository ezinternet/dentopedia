## Why Ingested
Ingested as part of the 2026-07-20 user-requested sweep on dental hygienist scaling technique (치위생사 스케일링 술기), specifically its iatrogenic-risk angle: what a routine ultrasonic scaling pass can do to a patient's existing orthodontic appliance. Extends the shear-bond-strength (SBS) / adhesive-remnant-index (ARI) evaluation paradigm used in [[dental-materials/zirconia/lee-2018-zirconia-primers-bracket-bonding]] (which optimizes *pre*-bonding primer choice for ceramic-bracket SBS) to a *post*-bonding perturbation — ultrasonic instrumentation during routine hygiene maintenance — showing that bracket base/mesh area, not primer chemistry, is the dominant variable determining whether ultrasonic instrumentation can debond a lingual bracket.

## Three-line Summary
In vitro study (BioMed Research International, 2017) using 200 bovine mandibular incisors bonded with 4 different lingual orthodontic brackets (STB, TTR, Idea, 2D) plus a vestibular control bracket (Victory), testing the effect of ultrasonic instrumentation (UI, Mini Piezon G1 tip, 8 W, 15 s/side) on shear bond strength (SBS) and adhesive remnant index (ARI).
UI significantly reduced SBS in the three lingual brackets with smaller mesh/base area (TTR, Idea, 2D; p<0.05) — some post-UI values fell below the clinically accepted minimum bond strength of 6–8 MPa — while the vestibular control (Victory) and the large-base lingual bracket (STB) showed no significant SBS change; bracket base area correlated linearly with the magnitude of SBS reduction, and UI also raised ARI scores (more adhesive left on the bracket, less on tooth) for the same vulnerable brackets.
Clinical implication: dental hygienists should minimize ultrasonic instrumentation time/pressure directly on bracket bases of small-mesh-area lingual brackets to avoid unintended bond failure; key limitation is bovine (not human) enamel and a single scaler-tip/power protocol.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Tests UI effect on **four different lingual bracket systems** (STB, TTR, Idea, 2D) plus a vestibular control, rather than a single bracket type as in prior single-device studies.
- Demonstrates a **linear relationship** between bracket base/mesh area and the magnitude of SBS reduction after UI: smaller-base brackets (TTR, Idea, 2D) lost significant bond strength; larger-base brackets (Victory, STB) did not.
- Shows some post-UI SBS values fall **below the clinically accepted minimum bond strength (6–8 MPa)**, i.e., a real risk of unwanted intraoral bracket failure from routine hygiene procedures, not just a statistical effect.
- ARI analysis: UI **increased ARI scores (2–3)** for the same vulnerable brackets, meaning more adhesive remained on the bracket base rather than the tooth — a secondary clinically relevant finding since ARI=3 failure mode lowers enamel-fracture risk at eventual debonding.
- Uses SEM imaging of bracket bases to explain baseline (pre-UI) SBS differences via mesh design (retention-wire angulation 0° vs 45° to bracket sides, sandblasted vs non-sandblasted surfaces).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Specimens**: 200 bovine permanent mandibular incisors, thymol-stored; inclusion criteria: no cracks/caries, smooth buccal surface, intact enamel. Embedded in acrylic resin in metal rings.
- **Design**: 10 groups (n=20), random assignment — 5 bracket types × {UI, no-UI control}.
- **Brackets tested** (all designed for maxillary central incisors, bonded to bovine incisor buccal surface for test repeatability): STB (Ormco), TTR (Rocky Mountain Orthodontics), Idea (Leone), 2D (Forestadent) — lingual; Victory (3M) — vestibular control.
- **Bonding protocol**: pumice cleaning (10 s) → 37% orthophosphoric acid etch (30 s) → Transbond XT primer + Transbond XT resin → light cure 20 s (5 s/side).
- **UI protocol**: Mini Piezon (EMS) ultrasonic scaler, G1 tip (manufacturer-recommended for supragingival scaling), 8 W power, 0° tip-to-enamel angulation, 15 s per side (1 min total per bracket), water coolant, single trained operator.
- **Testing**: Instron 3343 universal testing machine, shear mode, 1 mm/min head speed; maximum debonding load recorded in N, converted to MPa (force/bracket surface area).
- **ARI scoring**: stereomicroscope at ×20; scale 0 (no resin on tooth) to 3 (100% resin remaining on tooth).
- **Statistics**: two-way ANOVA + Tukey post hoc for SBS; linear regression (bracket base area vs. SBS reduction); chi-square test for ARI score distributions; α=0.05; R v3.1.3.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
| Bracket (type) | Base/mesh area | Control SBS (no UI) | Effect of UI on SBS | Effect of UI on ARI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Victory (vestibular) | Large, sandblasted | Highest tier | No significant change (p>0.05) | No significant change |
| STB (lingual) | Large, irregular mesh | Lowest tier | No significant change (p>0.05) | No significant change |
| TTR (lingual) | Small, narrow/irregular grooves | Highest tier | Significant decrease (p<0.05) | Significant increase to 2–3 |
| Idea (lingual) | Small, conventional 80-gauge mesh | Lowest tier | Significant decrease (p<0.05) | Significant increase to 2–3 |
| 2D (lingual) | Small, 45° retention-wire angle | Highest tier | Significant decrease (p<0.05) | Significant increase to 2–3 |

- Overall SBS range across all groups: ~3–30 MPa.
- Literature-cited clinically adequate minimum SBS: 6–8 MPa; theoretical safe ceiling to avoid enamel damage on debond: ~40–50 MPa.
- Linear regression confirmed bracket base area significantly predicts SBS reduction magnitude after UI (p<0.05).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **Bovine, not human, enamel** — SBS values may differ from human teeth; used specifically because bovine buccal surfaces are flatter/more reproducible than bovine lingual surfaces, which was itself a study-design compromise (both lingual and vestibular brackets were bonded to the buccal aspect of bovine incisors, not their true anatomical target surface).
- **Single UI protocol**: one tip (G1, metal), one power setting (8 W), one duration (15 s/side). Authors explicitly flag that other scaler tips (Teflon-coated, plastic, nonmetallic), power outputs, and adhesive systems need separate testing.
- **Single operator** — inter-operator technique variability not assessed.
- **In vitro shear loading** (single force vector, one loading rate) may not fully replicate the cyclic, multidirectional intraoral forces a bracket experiences over a course of orthodontic treatment.
- Authors note prior literature reported conflicting ARI-score effects of bracket base design (some studies found no significant effect, others did) and attribute this to differing materials/protocols across studies — this paper's own ARI conclusions should likewise be read as protocol-specific.

## 6. Related Work
- Cites a prior report of significant SBS reduction after UI, but on a **single vestibular bracket only**; this study's contribution is extending that finding across five different bracket geometries (four lingual + one vestibular).
- Cites prior work establishing that **UI does not significantly alter shear bond strength of resin cements** used for composite restorations, contrasted here with the metallic-orthodontic-bracket case, where UI-transmitted vibration and cavitation/acoustic microstreaming from the coolant water are hypothesized to have detectable mechanical effects at the bracket-adhesive interface.
- References the general dental-materials literature on bracket mesh/retention design and SBS (both vestibular and lingual bracket contexts), and on the theoretical clinically acceptable SBS window (5–50 MPa) balancing adequate retention against enamel-fracture risk on debonding.
- Related wiki page: [[dental-materials/zirconia/lee-2018-zirconia-primers-bracket-bonding]] — same SBS + ARI evaluation paradigm applied to orthodontic bracket bonding, but investigating a pre-bonding factor (zirconia surface primer chemistry) rather than a post-bonding perturbation (hygiene-procedure exposure).

## 7. Glossary
- **UI (Ultrasonic Instrumentation)**: use of a piezoelectric or magnetostrictive ultrasonic scaler to remove plaque/calculus via tip vibration.
- **SBS (Shear Bond Strength)**: maximum shear force (converted to MPa) required to debond a bracket from the tooth surface.
- **ARI (Adhesive Remnant Index)**: 0–3 scale scoring how much resin adhesive remains on the tooth surface after bracket debonding (0 = none, 3 = 100%).
- **Lingual orthodontics**: fixed orthodontic appliance bonded to the lingual (tongue-side) tooth surface for esthetic ("invisible") treatment.
- **Mini Piezon**: piezoelectric ultrasonic scaler unit (EMS) used with a G1 tip for supragingival scaling.
- **MPa (megapascal)**: unit of SBS, force per unit bonding surface area.
