## Why Ingested

This is the **commissioned systematic review behind ADA 2024 CPG Recommendation 1.5** ("suggests AGAINST adding oral, submucosal, or intramuscular corticosteroids to standard analgesic therapy") — the wiki held [[wiki/drug/analgesics/carrasco-labra-2024-pharmacologic-management-acute-dental-pain-guideline]] and its chairside guides, but not the evidence base for that specific recommendation. It also directly conflicts with the wiki's existing pro-corticosteroid cluster: [[wiki/drug/analgesics/almeida-2019-efficacy-corticosteroids-versus-placebo-impacted]] (SR+MA, 17 studies, pain MD −17.38) and [[wiki/drug/analgesics/tamgadge-2025-preoperative-dexamethasone-third-molar-pain-swelling-trismus]] (RCT, n=60, significant pain benefit), while agreeing with [[wiki/drug/analgesics/markiewicz-2008-corticosteroids-reduce-postoperative-morbidity-after-third-molar-surgery]] (pain not significant). Ingested to make the "why does ADA recommend against?" answer traceable rather than an assertion.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis of 40 placebo-controlled RCTs (Epistemonikos/L·OVE search of MEDLINE, Embase, CENTRAL, ClinicalTrials.gov from inception to April 2023; Cochrane RoB 2; GRADE) on oral, submucosal, or intramuscular corticosteroids for acute pain after surgical extraction of mandibular third molars — the commissioned evidence review for the ADA 2024 acute dental pain CPG.

Corticosteroids reduced pain intensity by 8.79 points on a 0–100 VAS at 6 hours (95% CI 14.8 to 2.77 lower; 7 RCTs, n=396; low certainty) and 8.89 points at 24 hours (95% CI 10.71 to 7.06 lower; 23 RCTs, n=1,555; very low certainty) — both **below the 10-point triviality threshold** (10% of scale range) the authors pre-specified, hence "trivial benefit"; no difference in postoperative infection (RD 0%, 95% CI −1% to 1%; 21 RCTs, n=1,630; low) or alveolar osteitis (RD 0%, 95% CI −3% to 4%; 8 RCTs, n=410; very low).

The finding is not "corticosteroids do nothing" but "the analgesic benefit falls below the smallest difference patients would notice" — and because no included study measured total pain relief, global efficacy rating, or mood alteration, the patient-centred outcomes that could justify the addition were never tested; this is the reasoning behind the ADA's conditional recommendation against corticosteroids as an analgesic add-on.

## 세줄요약

40편 위약대조 무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT)의 체계적 문헌고찰·메타분석 (Epistemonikos/L·OVE 검색, MEDLINE·Embase·CENTRAL·ClinicalTrials.gov, 개시~2023년 4월; 코크란 비뚤림위험도구 2판 (Risk of Bias 2, RoB 2); 근거수준평가체계 (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation, GRADE)): 하악 제3대구치 외과적 발치 후 급성통증에 대한 경구·점막하·근육내 코르티코스테로이드 (Corticosteroids).

통증강도는 시각아날로그척도 (Visual Analog Scale, VAS) 0–100에서 6시간 8.79점 감소 (95% 신뢰구간 14.8~2.77 감소; RCT 7편, n=396; 낮은 확실성), 24시간 8.89점 감소 (95% 신뢰구간 10.71~7.06 감소; RCT 23편, n=1,555; 매우 낮은 확실성) — **둘 다 저자가 사전 규정한 미미함(trivial) 문턱 10점(척도 범위의 10%)에 못 미친다**. 술후 감염(위험차 0%, 95% 신뢰구간 −1%~1%; RCT 21편, n=1,630; 낮은 확실성)·건성발치와 (Alveolar Osteitis)(위험차 0%, 95% 신뢰구간 −3%~4%; RCT 8편, n=410; 매우 낮은 확실성) 모두 차이 없음.

결론은 "코르티코스테로이드가 효과 없다"가 아니라 **"진통 효과가 환자가 체감하는 최소 차이에 못 미친다"** 이며, 총진통량 (Total Pain Relief)·전반효능평가 (Global Efficacy Rating)·기분변화를 측정한 연구가 **단 한 편도 없어** 추가 투여를 정당화할 환자중심 결과가 애초에 검증된 적이 없다 — 이것이 미국치과의사협회 (American Dental Association, ADA) 2024 지침이 진통 목적 코르티코스테로이드 추가를 조건부로 반대하는 근거다.

## Key Contributions

- First SR on this question to apply **GRADE certainty assessment** with a pre-specified triviality threshold — the authors note almost all prior SRs were published before 2018, none used GRADE, and they "applied suboptimal methods to synthesize pain-related outcomes"
- Reframes the corticosteroid question from *statistical significance* to **minimal important difference**: the pooled effect is directionally real but sits under the 10-point threshold on a 100-point scale
- Documents a hard evidence vacuum: **no included RCT measured total pain relief, global efficacy rating, or mood alteration** — the three outcomes that would speak to patient-perceived benefit
- Separates the *pain* question from the *inflammatory-complication* question by design (trismus, facial swelling, and infection-as-primary-outcome studies were excluded), which is why it does not contradict corticosteroid use for swelling/trismus
- Finds no clinically significant difference between oral, submucosal, and intramuscular routes

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review + random-effects meta-analysis; PRISMA-compliant
- **Search**: Two-step — Epistemonikos database (meta-search over Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, PubMed, MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, PsycINFO, LILACS), then the L·OVE (Living Overview of Evidence) platform for third-molar literature; inception to April 2023
- **Selection**: 240 records screened → 103 full texts assessed → **40 RCTs included** (reported in 64 references). Exclusions at full text: wrong outcomes (n=52), wrong intervention (n=8), wrong population (n=3)
- **Population**: Adolescents, adults, older adults undergoing *surgical* third-molar extraction (flap + osteotomy required)
- **Intervention**: Corticosteroids (glucocorticoids) oral, submucosal, or intramuscular, any dose, vs placebo/no treatment. **Intravenous administration excluded.**
- **Outcomes**: Continuous — pain intensity at 6 h and 24 h, total pain relief at 6 h, global efficacy rating at 6 h. Dichotomous — adverse effects (postoperative infection, alveolar osteitis, mood alteration, GI events) at any time
- **Excluded**: studies reporting only inflammatory-complication outcomes (trismus, facial swelling, infection)
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB 2
- **Certainty**: GRADE, **minimally contextualized approach with a null-effect threshold**. Triviality defined as 10% of the scale range for continuous outcomes (= 10 points on a 0–100 VAS) and 10% of baseline risk for dichotomous outcomes
- **Agents represented**: dexamethasone (4–10 mg), methylprednisolone (40 mg), prednisolone (10–30 mg), across parallel-group and split-mouth designs from England, Denmark, Germany, Turkey, Italy, Spain, Republic of Korea, Estonia, Brazil, India, Iraq and others

## Key Results

| Outcome | Studies | n | Effect (corticosteroid vs placebo) | GRADE certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pain intensity at 6 h (VAS 0–100) | 7 RCTs | 396 | **MD 8.79 points lower** (95% CI 14.8 to 2.77 lower) | Low |
| Pain intensity at 24 h (VAS 0–100) | 23 RCTs | 1,555 | **MD 8.89 points lower** (95% CI 10.71 to 7.06 lower) | Very low |
| Postoperative infection | 21 RCTs | 1,630 | RD 0% (95% CI −1% to 1%) | Low |
| Alveolar osteitis | 8 RCTs | 410 | RD 0% (95% CI −3% to 4%) | Very low |
| GI adverse effects (nausea/vomiting) | 3 RCTs | 120 | RD −19% (95% CI −54% to +16%) | Very low |
| Total pain relief at 6 h | 0 | — | **Not measured by any included study** | — |
| Global efficacy rating at 6 h | 0 | — | **Not measured by any included study** | — |
| Mood alteration | 0 | — | **Not measured by any included study** | — |

- **The threshold is the whole story.** Both pain estimates exclude zero (statistically significant) yet fall under the pre-specified 10-point minimal important difference — the authors therefore label the benefit *trivial*, not *absent*.
- **Route of administration**: subgroup analyses by oral / submucosal / intramuscular found "any minor quantitative differences observed across analyses were not clinically significant."
- A statistically significant route-dependent difference was detected for GI adverse effects, but the pooled estimate is very low certainty with a CI spanning both benefit and harm.

## Limitations

- Certainty is **low to very low across every outcome** — driven by risk of bias, inconsistency, and imprecision. The 24-hour pain estimate, despite pooling 23 RCTs and 1,555 participants, is rated very low.
- The 6-hour pain estimate rests on only 7 RCTs (n=396) with a wide CI (14.8 to 2.77 points).
- GI adverse-effect evidence is 3 RCTs / 120 participants with a CI from 54% fewer to 16% more — effectively uninformative.
- **Patient-important outcomes were never measured** by the underlying trials, so the review can only report a vacuum, not a null result, for total pain relief and global efficacy.
- Scope is deliberately narrow: intravenous corticosteroids excluded, and inflammatory complications (trismus, swelling) excluded by design — this review must **not** be read as evidence against corticosteroids for swelling/trismus control.
- Restricted to *surgical* mandibular third-molar extraction; does not speak to simple extractions or other acute dental pain contexts.

## Glossary

- **Minimally contextualized approach (GRADE)** — a certainty-rating strategy that judges effects against a single pre-specified threshold (here, the null effect) rather than weighing the full range of alternatives; it is what allows a statistically significant effect to be graded as "trivial."
- **Triviality threshold** — the smallest effect considered important; set here at 10% of the measurement scale range (10 points on a 0–100 VAS) and 10% of baseline risk for binary outcomes.
- **Alveolar osteitis (건성발치와)** — dry socket; loss of the blood clot from the extraction socket with exposed bone and severe pain, typically 2–4 days postoperatively.
- **L·OVE (Living Overview of Evidence)** — Epistemonikos' continuously updated evidence-mapping platform used here as the second-stage search.
- **Risk difference (RD)** — absolute difference in event proportions between arms; 0% means the two arms had the same event rate.
- **Anti-inflammatory potency** — glucocorticoid strength relative to hydrocortisone (=1): prednisone/prednisolone 4, methylprednisolone/triamcinolone 5, dexamethasone 25.
