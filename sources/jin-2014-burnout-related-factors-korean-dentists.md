## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional survey of 444 Korean dentists (45.9% adjusted response rate from a random sample of 1,000 dentists registered with the Korean Dental Association), measuring burnout with the Maslach Burnout Inventory–Human Services Survey (MBI-HSS).

High emotional exhaustion (EE) in 41.2%, high depersonalization (DP) in 55.9%, and low personal accomplishment (PA) in 41.4%; higher burnout was associated with younger age, male gender, absence of occupational calling, and unwillingness to reselect dentistry as a career.

The only Korea-specific primary burnout dataset in this wiki — subscale-cutoff prevalence runs markedly higher than pooled global SR+MA figures, though the two scoring conventions are not directly comparable; occupational calling and career satisfaction are proposed as the actionable management levers.

## 2. Key Contributions
- The only Korea-specific primary burnout dataset in this wiki — every other prevalence figure held is a pooled multi-country SR+MA estimate
- Reports subscale-level prevalence using MBI-HSS high/low cutoffs (EE 41.2%, DP 55.9%, reduced PA 41.4%) — a different scoring convention from the "full syndrome, all 3 subscales positive" (~13%) definition used in [[professional-wellbeing/moro-2022-burnout-syndrome-among-dentists-systematic]]
- Identifies specific, arguably modifiable determinants: absence of occupational calling and unwillingness to reselect dentistry as a career, alongside fixed demographic factors (younger age, male gender)
- Sampling frame (random sample from all 13,207 KDA-registered dentists) gives it national representativeness for Korea, unlike convenience-sample single-clinic surveys

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Cross-sectional survey design
- Sampling: random sample of 1,000 dentists selected from 13,207 dentists registered with the Korean Dental Association (KDA)
- Response: adjusted response rate 45.9% (444/967 analyzed)
- Instrument: custom-designed and validated questionnaire incorporating the MBI-HSS (three subscales: emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, personal sense of accomplishment) plus sociodemographic and job-related characteristics
- Analysis: bivariate analysis followed by multivariate analysis to identify factors independently associated with each burnout subscale

| Subscale / Metric | Result |

| High Depersonalization (DP) | 55.9% |
| Low Personal Accomplishment (PA, poor) | 41.4% |

| Factors associated with higher burnout | younger age, male gender, absence of occupational calling, unwilling to reselect dentistry as a job |

Conclusion stated by the authors: burnout is a common problem among Korean dentists, and occupational calling plus willingness to reselect dentistry are important factors for managing burnout.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Abstract-only ingest (PMC full-text body empty) — detailed regression tables (adjusted odds ratios, confidence intervals per factor) are not available from this record
- Cross-sectional design — no causal inference; burnout and "unwillingness to reselect dentistry" could be mutually reinforcing rather than one driving the other
- Single-country (Korea), single-timepoint (2014) — predates COVID-19; cannot speak to pandemic-era shifts covered by [[professional-wellbeing/negucioiu-2024-prevalence-management-burnout-dental-covid]]
- 45.9% adjusted response rate raises nonresponse-bias risk (burned-out dentists may be over- or under-represented among responders)
- MBI-HSS subscale-cutoff scoring (used here) is not directly poolable with the "3-subscale-positive full syndrome" definition used in the wiki's SR+MA references — numeric comparison across pages should be read as contextual, not a shared statistic

## 7. Glossary
- **MBI-HSS (Maslach Burnout Inventory – Human Services Survey)**: the human-services-tailored variant of the MBI used for healthcare and other service occupations
- **EE (Emotional Exhaustion)**: feeling emotionally drained/depleted by one's work
- **DP (Depersonalization)**: cynicism, detached or impersonal response toward patients/colleagues
- **PA (Personal Accomplishment)**: sense of competence and achievement in one's work; "reduced/low PA" indicates burnout risk
- **KDA**: Korean Dental Association
- **Occupational calling**: a sense of vocational purpose/meaning in one's work, identified here as protective against burnout
