## Why Ingested

Comparative study of ultraconservative vs traditional access cavity design on two leading NiTi instrumentation systems (XP-endo Shaper vs Reciproc). Directly extends [[endodontics/anatomy/dioguardi-2024-access-cavity-designs-canal-orifice-scoping]] by quantifying geometric outcomes (unprepared areas, dentin removal, canal transportation) under thermomechanical cycling, providing practical efficacy data for access-cavity selection in mandibular molars.

## Three-line Summary

In vitro study of 40 mandibular molars (n=10/group) comparing ultraconservative vs traditional access cavities paired with XP-endo Shaper or Reciproc instrumentation, followed by thermomechanical cycling and load-capacity testing.

Ultraconservative access cavities resulted in significantly greater unprepared canal areas, more voids/remnant filling material, and greater canal transportation with Reciproc (but not XP-endo Shaper), yet no difference in fracture load capacity between groups.

Access-cavity conservation trades shaping efficiency (particularly with reciprocating systems) against dentin preservation and instrumentation accessibility—load capacity preserved despite geometric compromises suggests pulp-chamber remnant voids may not immediately compromise fracture resistance.

## 세줄요약

만곡근관 있는 하악 제2대구치 40개 발치치(n=10/그룹)에서 초보존적 접근과 전통적 접근 교정+XP-endo Shaper/Reciproc 기구와의 조합을 비교했고, 열기계적 순환·하중 검사 시행.

초보존 접근에서 유의미하게 더 높은 미성형근관부위·충전재료 남음·Reciproc 사용시 근관편이(canal transportation) 증가했으나 XP-endo Shaper에서는 차이 없었고, 그룹 간 하중용량(load capacity) 차이 없음.

접근 보존성은 성형효율(특히 왕복운동 기구)과 상아질 보존 간 trade-off를 나타내며, 하중용량 보존은 근관실 충전재료 남음이 즉각적 파절저항성을 손상하지 않음을 시사.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantified the geometric consequences of ultraconservative access-cavity preparation on root canal shaping outcomes—including unprepared canal walls, dentin removal volume, accumulated hard tissue debris (AHTD), and canal transportation
- Direct comparison of XP-endo Shaper (continuous rotation) vs Reciproc (reciprocating) within ultraconservative vs traditional access cavity groups under thermomechanical cycling
- Demonstrated that ultraconservative access cavities resulted in significantly more voids and remnant filling material within the pulp chamber, despite equivalent load-capacity outcomes
- Showed that ultraconservative + XP-endo Shaper combination preserved the least dentin removal while incurring higher AHTD; traditional + XP-endo Shaper had the lowest overall canal transportation

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: In vitro experimental study with matched-pair micro-CT analysis
- **Sample**: 40 extracted, caries-free mandibular molars (no endodontic treatment history)
- **Groups**: 4 groups, n=10/group based on access-cavity type × instrumentation protocol:
  - TradAC/RC: Traditional access + Reciproc
  - TradAC/XP: Traditional access + XP-endo Shaper
  - UltraAC/RC: Ultraconservative access + Reciproc
  - UltraAC/XP: Ultraconservative access + XP-endo Shaper
- **Teeth Preparation**: Dental mannequin; all root-canal procedures standardized by one operator
- **Scanning**: High-resolution micro-computed tomography (microCT) before instrumentation, after instrumentation, and after filling
- **Measured Outcomes**:
  - Unprepared canal area (%)
  - Dentin removed (volume in mm³)
  - Accumulated hard tissue debris (AHTD, %)
  - Canal transportation in MB, ML, distal canals (µm)
  - Presence/volume of voids within root-canal filling
  - Remaining filling-material volume in pulp chamber
  - Load-capacity test (thermomechanical cycling before fracture-load testing)
- **Statistical Analysis**: Two-way ANOVA with randomized-blocks design; α = 0.05

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | TradAC/RC | TradAC/XP | UltraAC/RC | UltraAC/XP | P-value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unprepared canal area (%) | Lower* | Lower* | Higher | Higher | <0.05 |
| Dentin removed (%) | Intermediate | Intermediate | Intermediate | **Lowest** | <0.05 |
| AHTD (%) | Higher* | Lower | Higher* | Lower | <0.05 |
| Canal transportation—MB (µm) | Intermediate | **Lowest** | Highest | Intermediate | <0.05 |
| Canal transportation—ML/distal (µm) | Intermediate | **Lowest** | Intermediate | **Lowest** | <0.05 |
| Voids & remnant filling (%) | Lower | Lower | **Higher** | **Higher** | <0.05 |
| Load capacity after thermomechanical cycling | No difference among all groups | | | | NS |

**Key Findings**:
- UltraAC/XP had the lowest dentin removal (dentin conservation advantage) but highest AHTD
- TradAC/XP had the overall lowest canal transportation across all root-canal systems
- UltraAC groups (both Reciproc and XP-endo Shaper) had significantly more voids and remnant filling material within the pulp chamber after cleaning procedures
- Fracture load capacity was equivalent across all four groups—no statistical difference despite geometric variations

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Single-operator study**: Operator experience and technique standardization may not generalize to multi-operator clinical settings
- **Extracted teeth only**: No periodontal ligament, no bone support; extracted teeth may not represent in vivo biomechanical response
- **Thermomechanical cycling parameters**: Laboratory protocol may not replicate full in vivo loading and thermal cycling stress
- **No direct clinical outcome**: In vitro model does not address endodontic outcome (healing, apical healing, retreatment rate) or long-term clinical follow-up
- **Limited to mandibular molars**: Findings specific to mandibular molar anatomy; maxillary molar and anterior tooth behavior unknown
- **No blinding**: Operator performed both access-cavity preparation and instrumentation; potential for technique bias
- **Cross-sectional rather than longitudinal**: Single post-cycling timepoint does not address progressive wear or repeated loading effects

## 7. Glossary

- **Ultraconservative Access Cavity (UltraAC)**: Minimal access opening preserving occlusal tooth structure; reduces pulp-chamber visibility and instrumentation space
- **Traditional Access Cavity (TradAC)**: Conventional trapezoidal or triangular occlusal access providing full pulp-chamber exposure and instrument visibility
- **XP-endo Shaper**: Continuous-rotation NiTi instrument (single file, rotary); variable taper design
- **Reciproc**: Reciprocating NiTi instrument (single file, alternating rotation); ISO 40 .06 taper
- **Accumulated Hard Tissue Debris (AHTD)**: Dentin powder and calcified-tissue fragments remaining within the root canal after instrumentation
- **Canal Transportation**: Deviation of the canal's original axis during instrumentation; measured in micrometers from original centerline
- **Thermomechanical Cycling**: Laboratory simulation combining thermal cycling (temperature fluctuation) and mechanical loading (mastication simulation) on restored/filled teeth
- **Micro-computed Tomography (microCT)**: High-resolution 3D imaging allowing precise volumetric measurement of dentin removal, voids, and debris
- **Load-Capacity Test**: Testing to fracture under axial/non-axial loading to measure tooth structural strength after endodontic and restorative procedures
