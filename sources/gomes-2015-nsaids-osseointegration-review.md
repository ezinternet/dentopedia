This is the key review establishing the COX-1-safe / COX-2-culprit distinction that [[wiki/drug/analgesics/etikala-2019-nsaids-periodontal-implant-therapy-review]] later echoes; it grounds the overview's "COX-2 dilemma" by mapping the evidence tiers (clinical, animal, cellular) onto which COX isoform impairs osseointegration.

## Three-line Summary

Structured review (Cochrane/PubMed/Medline, Jan 1982–Dec 2012; 360 references screened, 31 included: 2 randomized double-blind placebo-controlled clinical trials, 20 in vivo animal osseointegration studies, 9 in vitro osteoprogenitor-cell studies) of how NSAIDs affect osseointegration and dental implant survival.

Clinical trials showed COX-1 inhibitors did not impair osseointegration; animal studies showed any drug capable of inhibiting COX-2 may impair it; in vitro studies identified COX-2 inhibitors as the most potent depressors of osseointegration at the cellular level.

Caution is warranted when selecting COX-2-active NSAIDs in the postoperative period around implant placement.

## 세줄요약

NSAID가 골유착 (Osseointegration)과 임플란트 실패에 미치는 영향을 다룬 구조화 리뷰 (Cochrane/PubMed/Medline, 1982.1–2012.12; 360편 스크리닝 후 31편 포함: 무작위 이중맹검 위약대조 임상시험 2편, 동물 골유착 연구 20편, 시험관 골전구세포 (Osteoprogenitor cell) 연구 9편).

임상시험에서 사이클로옥시게나제-1 (Cyclooxygenase-1, COX-1) 억제제는 골유착을 저해하지 않았으나, 동물 연구에서는 COX-2를 억제할 수 있는 약물이 골유착을 저해할 수 있었고, 시험관 연구에서 COX-2 억제제가 세포 수준에서 가장 강력한 골유착 억제 (depressor)로 나타났다.

임플란트 식립 후 술후 기간에 COX-2 활성 NSAID 선택에는 주의가 필요하다.

## 1. Document Information

- Type: Review with explicit inclusion criteria and electronic database search (evidence level: sr).
- Databases: Cochrane Library, PubMed, Medline.
- Search window: January 1982 – December 2012.
- Keywords: nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, dental implants, bone healing, osteoprogenitor cells.
- Yield: 360 references identified; 31 met inclusion (2 clinical trials, 20 animal studies, 9 osteoprogenitor-cell studies).
- Source: PubMed structured abstract (abstract-only; full text not retrieved).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Frames the NSAID-osseointegration question around COX isoform selectivity rather than NSAIDs as a monolithic class.
- Aligns three evidence tiers (clinical, animal, cellular) on a single conclusion: COX-1 inhibition appears safe for osseointegration, COX-2 inhibition is the concern.
- Identifies COX-2 inhibitors as the most potent depressors of osseointegration at the cellular level, providing a mechanistic anchor for the clinical caution.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Bibliographic electronic search across Cochrane Library, PubMed, and Medline for articles published January 1982–December 2012.
- Inclusion criteria restricted to three study designs: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled clinical studies, in vivo animal osseointegration studies, and in vitro osteoprogenitor-cell studies.
- Of 360 references found, 31 met inclusion: 2 clinical trials, 20 animal studies, and 9 osteoprogenitor-cell studies.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Clinical trials (n=2): COX-1 inhibitors did NOT impair osseointegration.
- Animal studies (n=20): any drug capable of inhibiting COX-2 may impair the osseointegration process.
- In vitro osteoprogenitor-cell studies (n=9): COX-2 inhibitors are the most potent depressors of osseointegration at the cellular level.
- Practice implication: caution must be taken when selecting COX-2 NSAIDs during the postoperative period.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 2 clinical trials met inclusion, so the human-level conclusion (COX-1 safe) rests on a thin clinical base relative to the 20 animal and 9 cellular studies.
- Search ends December 2012; more recent NSAID/implant evidence is outside scope.
- Ingested abstract-only — quantitative effect sizes, per-drug detail, and risk of bias for the included studies were not available from the full text.

## 7. Glossary

- COX-1 (Cyclooxygenase-1): constitutively expressed cyclooxygenase isoform; its inhibition did not impair osseointegration in the included clinical trials.
- COX-2 (Cyclooxygenase-2): inducible cyclooxygenase isoform central to inflammation and bone healing; drugs inhibiting it may impair osseointegration and were the most potent cellular depressors.
- Osteoprogenitor cell: precursor cell that differentiates into bone-forming osteoblasts; the in vitro model used to test NSAID effects at the cellular level.
- Osseointegration: direct structural and functional connection between living bone and the surface of a load-bearing implant.
