## Why Ingested

지르코니아 교합 조정 배치에서 "연삭 → 연마" 후 표면조도가 어디까지 회복되는지를 Rmax로 정량한 기준 논문이다. 전장관 지르코니아 (Full-contour Zirconia, FZ)와 도재비니어 지르코니아 (Veneered Full-contour Zirconia, VFZ)를 같은 3단 연삭·3단 연마 프로토콜로 직접 비교해, 조정 후 연마가 유약 수준 조도를 되찾는 조건을 제시한다 → [[dental-materials/zirconia/mohammadi-bassir-2017-grinding-overglazing-polishing-zirconia]]

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study (Int J Oral Sci 2014; 30 bar-shaped 3% Y-TZP specimens, 2 groups of n=15 — glazed full-contour vs feldspathic-veneered+glazed) measuring maximum roughness depth (Rmax) by profilometer after sequential diamond grinding (20/10/7.5 µm, 15 s, 2 N, water coolant) and 3-step polishing.

Rmax rose significantly after coarse grinding in both groups; fine grinding restored group 1 to glazed level (P=0.19) but not group 2 (P=0.009), and fine polishing of FZ gave Rmax 2.41±0.51 µm versus 4.31±1.93 µm for glazed FZ (P=0.49) while polished VFZ stayed significantly rougher than its glazed state (P=0.32 as reported).

For full-contour zirconia the NTI 3-step grinding sequence alone reached glazed-level smoothness and polishing lowered roughness below glaze, whereas veneered zirconia retained deep grinding grooves and condensation voids that polishing could only round off, not remove.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Surface roughness of zirconia for full-contour crowns after clinically simulated grinding and polishing
- **Authors**: Hmaidouch Rim, Müller Wolf-Dieter, Lauer Hans-Christoph, Weigl Paul
- **Journal**: International Journal of Oral Science (Int J Oral Sci), 2014
- **DOI**: 10.1038/ijos.2014.34 — **PMID**: 25059249 — **PMCID**: PMC5153586
- **Study type**: In-vitro bench study (profilometry + SEM), no phase-transformation or strength testing
- **Materials**: 3% (n/n) Y2O3-stabilized ZrO2 (Zirluna ACF, Amberger Central Fraescenter, Amberg, Germany)

## 2. Key Contributions

- Provides Rmax data for **full-contour zirconia (FZ)** versus **veneered zirconia (VFZ)** across the whole clinical adjustment sequence — as-glazed → coarse → medium → fine grinding → coarse → medium → fine polishing — rather than at a single endpoint.
- Fixes the two variables that most bench polishing studies leave uncontrolled: **constant 2 N press-on force and 15 s per step**, applied to every specimen by a dedicated apparatus.
- Reports that the **glazed surface is not the smoothest surface**: glazed FZ measured 4.31±1.93 µm Rmax, attributed to small bubbles within the glaze layer, while fine-polished FZ reached 2.41±0.51 µm.
- Suggests a clinical shortcut worth testing: after the NTI 3-step grinding sequence, FZ roughness was statistically indistinguishable from coarse-polished FZ, i.e. the 3-step grind may already be sufficient without a separate polishing pass.
- Documents the material-dependence of polishability: VFZ retains deep grinding grooves and condensation porosity that polishing only smooths and rounds, never removes.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

**Specimen fabrication.** 30 bar-shaped specimens were cut from pre-sintered blocks with a water-cooled diamond saw (Isomet; Buehler) at 100 r·min⁻¹, planarized with 1200 and 4000 grit SiC paper on a microgrinding system (400CS; Exakt), and sintered at 1530 °C for 2 h (VITA Zyrcomat; Vita Zahnfabrik). Final dimensions 30 mm × 6 mm × 2 mm.

**Groups (n=15 each).** Group 1 (experimental, GFZ): glazed at 900 °C with Vita Glaze, no veneering. Group 2 (control, VFZ): veneered with an approximately 2 mm layer of feldspathic ceramic (Vita VM9) and then glazed with Vita Glaze at 900 °C (Vita InCeramat oven).

**Outcome.** Maximum roughness depth (Rmax — perpendicular distance between the highest peak and lowest valley of the roughness profile within the measurement line) on a Perthometer SP5 profilometer, **5 measurements per specimen** after every step, mean used for analysis. 525 measurements per group in total.

**Simulated clinical adjustment.** Specimens were clamped in a stand to keep orientation constant and treated under water coolant with NTI K881 (141)-016 F-FG round-end cylinder diamonds on a turbine at **200 000 r·min⁻¹, 15 s, 2 N**, sweeping along the specimen length. Grinding sequence: red 20 µm → yellow 10 µm → white 7.5 µm, new bur per specimen, acetone cleaning and air drying between steps.

**Polishing.** NTI kit HP 802104 (P341, P3401, P34001): coarse 15 000 r·min⁻¹, medium 10 000 r·min⁻¹, fine 5 000 r·min⁻¹, each 15 s at 2 N, same sweep direction, new instrument per specimen.

**Statistics and imaging.** One-way ANOVA with Tukey HSD, α=0.05 (OriginLab v7.5). Representative specimens per group per step imaged by SEM (FEI Quanta 600 FEG) at ×500.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Coarse grinding (20 µm)** significantly increased Rmax in both groups — the expected roughening step.
- **Medium and fine grinding** significantly reduced roughness **only in group 1 (FZ)**. In group 2 (VFZ) roughness dropped significantly after medium grinding but **not** after fine grinding (P=0.51).
- **Fine-ground versus as-glazed**: FZ showed no significant difference (P=0.19); VFZ fine-ground surfaces were **significantly rougher** than glazed VFZ (P=0.009).
- **Coarse polishing versus fine grinding**: no significant difference in either group (FZ P=0.54; VFZ P=0.99) — coarse polishing added nothing over the fine grinding step.
- **Fine polishing versus glaze (FZ)**: no significant difference (P=0.45). Discussion states fine-ground FZ 2.41±0.51 µm versus glazed FZ 4.31±1.93 µm, P=0.49.
- **Fine polishing of VFZ** left roughness higher than its as-glazed state; the paper prints P=0.32 for this comparison while calling it significant (see §5).
- **Between groups**: group 1 (FZ) had significantly lower Rmax than group 2 (VFZ) after glazing, after grinding and after polishing (P<0.05).
- **SEM**: FZ coarse-ground grooves were progressively reduced by the following two burs and the coarse polisher, and markedly reduced after full polishing. VFZ showed ridges, grooves and **voids from incomplete condensation**; after polishing the voids were shallower and rounded but the ridge/groove traces and pitted areas persisted.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Internal numerical inconsistency (reported as printed).** The Results section gives P=0.19 for fine-ground FZ versus glazed FZ, while the Discussion gives P=0.49 for the same comparison; and the VFZ fine-polishing versus as-glazed comparison is called "significantly higher" with P=0.32. Both readings point the same clinical direction (no recovery / residual roughness) but the statistics should not be quoted as-is.
- **Only two absolute Rmax values appear in the retrieved full text** (2.41±0.51 µm and 4.31±1.93 µm). Table 1, the figures (Figures 1–8) and the reference list are not present in the PMC text artifact, so the full per-step means/SDs could not be transcribed. All other results are reported as the paper's own P-values.
- **Rmax only.** No Ra/Rz/Rq, no monoclinic fraction (XRD), no flexural strength or Weibull data — so this paper cannot say whether the polish-restored surface is mechanically equivalent to the glazed one. The authors note only that wet grinding has a better influence on the m→t transformation of 3% Y-TZP than dry grinding, citing prior work rather than measuring it here.
- **Single material system, single instrument system.** One 3% Y-TZP brand, one veneering ceramic, one diamond series and one polishing kit; the authors state explicitly that results predict only what happens with this exact polishing protocol.
- **Bar specimens, not crowns.** Flat bars in a fixed stand with a machine-controlled 2 N load cannot reproduce crown curvature, occlusal anatomy, operator variability or intraoral access.
- **Open question the authors pose**: whether the 3-step grinding sequence alone is adequate, making a separate polishing pass unnecessary — flagged as needing further investigation, and not settled by roughness data alone.

## 6. Related Work

- Prior in-vitro Y-TZP surface-treatment studies (references 24–27 in the paper) established that grinding/sandblasting alter surface properties; the authors position roughness after *clinically simulated* adjustment as the unstudied gap.
- Curtis et al. (ref 33) reported increased 3% Y-TZP roughness after both wet and dry grinding — consistent with the coarse-grinding increase seen here.
- A study using a commercial Soflex polishing system (3M-ESPE, ref 28) confirmed significant roughness reduction of zirconia after polishing versus grinding — consistent with the polishing effect reported here.
- The paper's clinical rationale chain (rough surface → plaque retention, aesthetics, antagonist wear) draws on refs 15–21 and 30–31; within this wiki the wear endpoint is covered by [[dental-materials/zirconia/janyavula-2013-wear-polished-glazed-zirconia-enamel]] and [[dental-materials/zirconia/alfrisany-2019-occlusal-adjustments-zirconia-enamel-wear]].
- The authors state no prior study measured Rmax after clinically simulated grinding and polishing of these materials, so no direct numerical comparison with earlier work was possible.

## 7. Glossary

- **Rmax (maximum roughness depth)** — perpendicular distance between the highest peak and lowest valley of the roughness profile within one measurement line; a peak-to-valley extreme, more sensitive to single deep grooves than Ra.
- **FZ / GFZ (full-contour zirconia / glazed full-contour zirconia)** — monolithic zirconia restoration that is glazed only, without a veneering ceramic layer.
- **VFZ (veneered full-contour zirconia)** — zirconia framework with a feldspathic veneering ceramic, then glazed; the "classical" bilayer design.
- **Y-TZP (yttria-stabilized tetragonal zirconia polycrystal)** — tetragonal-phase zirconia toughened by 3% (n/n) Y2O3.
- **m→t transformation** — tetragonal-to-monoclinic phase transformation; here mentioned only as something wet grinding influences less adversely than dry grinding.
- **Profilometer (Perthometer SP5)** — stylus instrument tracing the surface to compute roughness parameters.
- **Tukey HSD** — post-hoc test controlling family-wise error after a significant one-way ANOVA (α=0.05).
