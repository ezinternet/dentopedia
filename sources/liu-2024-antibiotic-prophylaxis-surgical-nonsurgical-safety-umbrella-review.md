## Why Ingested

치과 항생제 예방투여(AP) 결정은 늘 "이 처치가 정말 감염 위험을 낮추는가, 아니면 내성·부작용만 키우는가"라는 질문과 마주한다. 본 umbrella review는 78개 임상 시나리오(수술 37종·비수술 침습술 11종·의학적 예방 29종)를 GRADE 등급까지 매겨 evidence map으로 제시해, 치과 밖 수술/시술의 AP 근거 지형을 한눈에 보여주는 참고 좌표축 역할을 한다. 기존 [[drug/antibiotics/lodi-2021-antibiotics-tooth-extraction-cochrane-sr]]가 "건강한 환자의 발치 후 루틴 항생제는 불필요"라는 결론을 냈는데, 본 논문의 핵심 발견(AP 효능은 창상 청결도와 대체로 무관하지만 안전성 근거는 부족하며 상당수 시나리오에서 routine 사용이 정당화되지 않음)이 이와 같은 방향으로 수렴한다는 점에서 발치 externally-validating reference로 ingest.

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review synthesizing 75 systematic reviews (725 RCTs, 119 outcomes, 163,832 participants) across 78 clinical scenarios (37 surgery types, 11 nonsurgical invasive procedures, 29 medical prophylaxis conditions) evaluating antibiotic prophylaxis (AP) efficacy and safety.

Of 119 outcomes, 67 (56.3%) showed statistically significant benefit (34 convincing/highly suggestive evidence); AP had little/no benefit in Mohs surgery, simple hand surgery, herniorrhaphy, hepatectomy, thyroid surgery, rhinoplasty, stented distal hypospadias repair, midurethral sling placement, endoscopic sinus surgery, and TURBT, and AP significantly increased antibiotic resistance/adverse events in cystoscopy and afebrile neutropenia post-chemotherapy.

AP efficacy appeared largely independent of surgical wound cleanliness or baseline infection risk, but low-to-very-low certainty evidence (70% of outcomes) and scarce safety reporting (only 21/78 scenarios) limit external validity, so clinicians must weigh case-by-case rather than apply AP routinely.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Largest and most comprehensive umbrella review to date of antibiotic prophylaxis (AP) efficacy and safety, spanning both surgical and nonsurgical/medical scenarios in a single evidence map.
- Reanalyzed 28 outcomes by removing nonrandomized studies of intervention (NRSIs), which flipped two conclusions from significant to nonsignificant (all-cause mortality in acute necrotizing pancreatitis; ventriculostomy-related infection in external ventricular drain patients).
- Applied AMSTAR2 (methodological quality of included meta-analyses) and GRADE (certainty of evidence per outcome) systematically, plus five-tier evidence classification (convincing / highly suggestive / suggestive / weak / nonsignificant).
- Quantified absolute effect via number needed to treat (NNT) for all statistically significant outcomes and assessed publication bias (Begg's, Egger's, Harbord's) with trim-and-fill sensitivity analysis.
- First systematic synthesis of AP-related adverse events (antibiotic resistance, drug reactions) across scenarios, though only available for 21/78 scenarios.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Umbrella review (PRISMA 2020) of pairwise meta-analyses comparing antibiotic prophylaxis vs placebo/no treatment across any clinical scenario, RCTs and NRSIs eligible but NRSIs excluded on reanalysis when included in original meta-analysis.
- **Search**: PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science Core Collection, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, inception–30 December 2022. English language only. Duplicate-topic meta-analyses resolved by keeping the one with the largest number of included RCTs.
- **Reanalysis**: Random-effects DerSimonian-Laird estimator for pooled RRs and 95% CIs; excess significance bias test (χ² comparing observed vs expected significant studies); 95% prediction intervals for outcomes with >3 trials.
- **Quality assessment**: AMSTAR2 for included systematic reviews; GRADE (very low/low/moderate/high) for outcome-level certainty.
- **Classification**: Outcomes grouped into 6 classes (mortality, overall infection, surgical site/wound infection, localized infection, bloodstream infection, symptoms/complications); scenarios split into surgical (surgery + nonsurgery invasive procedures, further stratified by wound class: clean/clean-contaminated/contaminated/dirty-infected per National Academy of Science classification) and medical prophylaxis (nonemergency vs emergency).
- **Adverse events**: Antibiotic resistance and other drug-related adverse events extracted and pooled where reported (21/78 scenarios).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **Scope**: 75 systematic reviews, 725 RCTs, 119 outcomes, 163,832 participants. AMSTAR2: 47 (39%) high methodological quality, 24 (20%) low. GRADE: 36 (30%) high-to-moderate certainty, 83 (70%) low-to-very-low certainty.
- **Surgical prophylaxis** (37 surgery types, 55 outcomes): 13/18 clean, 26/33 clean-contaminated, 3/3 contaminated incision outcomes showed significant benefit; only 6/18 clean, 18/33 clean-contaminated, 1/3 contaminated outcomes reached convincing/highly suggestive evidence. AP showed little/no effect: simple hand surgery, herniorrhaphy (wound infection); Mohs surgery, thyroid surgery (SSI); ureteroscopic lithotripsy (fever/UTI); midurethral sling placement, kidney transplant asymptomatic bacteriuria (UTI); endoscopic sinus surgery, rhinoplasty, stented distal hypospadias repair (overall infection/complications). Convincing evidence for benefit: total joint arthroplasty (NNT=24), breast cancer surgery (NNT=29), cesarean section (NNT=18), elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy (NNT=62), abdominal hysterectomy (NNT=31).
- **Nonsurgical invasive procedures** (11 types, 16 outcomes): possible reduction in UTI after cystoscopy, postoperative urinary catheterization, urodynamic studies — but only suggestive/weak, low-to-very-low certainty evidence.
- **Medical prophylaxis** (29 scenarios, 47 outcomes): 22/47 significant benefit, 10 convincing/highly suggestive — tick bite (NNT=69), anti-EGFR skin rash, spontaneous bacterial peritonitis in cirrhosis+ascites (NNT=9), neonatal infection in maternal GBS colonization (NNT=14), COPD quality of life (high certainty). Mortality benefit in cirrhotic GI bleeding and chemo-induced afebrile neutropenia (high certainty), but no mortality benefit in acute necrotizing pancreatitis, post-cardiac-arrest, or ICU stay.
- **Adverse events** (21 scenarios): antibiotic resistance significantly increased in cystoscopy (RR 1.73, 95% CI 1.04–2.87) and afebrile neutropenia post-chemo (RR 1.47, 1.08–2.01); other adverse events significantly increased in HSCT (RR 3.32, 1.45–7.63) and afebrile neutropenia post-chemo (RR 1.58, 1.19–2.12).
- **Overall**: 67/119 (56.3%) outcomes significant, 34 convincing/highly suggestive. AP efficacy appeared independent of wound cleanliness and baseline disease infection risk.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- 70% of outcomes rated low-to-very-low GRADE certainty, substantially limiting external validity and generalizability of recommendations.
- Safety/adverse-event data available in only 21 of 78 scenarios — authors explicitly state AP safety "cannot be currently determined" for most scenarios due to sparse reporting.
- Only primary outcomes extracted per meta-analysis to avoid multiple-comparison inflation, which may have missed secondary safety signals.
- Heterogeneity in quality among included systematic reviews and potential publication bias (21 outcomes across 18 scenarios flagged by Egger's/Begg's/Harbord's tests); trim-and-fill sensitivity analysis lost significance in 5 scenarios (afebrile neutropenia, urodynamic studies, elective abdominal hysterectomy, breast reduction surgery, history of cellulitis).
- English-language restriction and reliance on published meta-analyses (rather than primary RCT-level umbrella synthesis for every scenario) are additional constraints.
- Authors call for future reviews focused specifically on AP-related safety outcomes and well-designed RCTs to confirm low-certainty findings.

## 6. Related Work

- Cited as building on prior single-topic meta-analyses (e.g., ESGE EUS-FNA pancreatic cyst antibiotic guideline vs contradicting evidence synthesis) that left "fragmented and controversial conclusions."
- References US CDC prophylactic antibiotic recommendations (high-infection-risk surgery, labor/delivery, immunosuppression, chronic infection) and AUA Best Practice Policy Statement (percutaneous nephrolithotomy) as guideline context the umbrella review's findings are compared against.

## 7. Glossary

- **AP (Antibiotic Prophylaxis)**: prophylactic antibiotic use to prevent infection before evidence of present infection.
- **NNT (Number Needed to Treat)**: number of patients needed to treat with AP to prevent one additional infection event.
- **GRADE**: Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation — 4-tier evidence certainty framework (very low/low/moderate/high).
- **AMSTAR2**: A MeaSurement Tool to Assess systematic Reviews 2 — 16-item methodological quality tool for systematic reviews.
- **NRSI**: Nonrandomized Study of Intervention.
- **TURBT**: Transurethral Resection of Bladder Tumor.
- **HSCT**: Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation.
