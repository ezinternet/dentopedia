## Why Ingested

치과 영상의 기본기(pano·구내)와 CBCT를 한 권에서 훈련하는 최초 진입 리뷰 — CBCT-only 최신 리뷰들이 전제하는 "2D가 기본, CBCT는 demanding case" 적응증 원칙·선량 사다리의 기초를 세운다. [[radiology/kaasalainen-2021-dental-cone-beam-ct-updated-review]]는 같은 헬싱키 계열의 후속 의학물리 업데이트이고, 이 페이지는 그 전에 나온 교육 중심 원본이다.

## Three-line Summary

Teaching-oriented narrative review (Insights Imaging 2015, 16 pp) covering panoramic radiography (PTG) image formation, normal anatomy, and positioning errors, plus cone beam CT (CBCT) technology, radiation dose, artifacts, and specialty-specific indications.

Panoramic + intraoral radiographs remain the basic 2D imaging modalities; CBCT provides accurate 3D hard-tissue imaging at lower dose than MSCT (dentoalveolar CBCT 11–674 µSv, median 61; craniofacial CBCT 30–1,073 µSv, median 87; MSCT maxillo-mandibular 280–1,410 µSv).

CBCT's disadvantages are poor soft-tissue contrast and artifacts; it should be used only for justified demanding cases under ALARA, with the smallest field of view compatible with the task.

파노라마+구내방사선이 기본 2D 기법이고, CBCT는 다중슬라이스 CT(MSCT)보다 낮은 선량으로 정확한 3D 경조직 영상을 제공(치조 CBCT 유효선량 11–674 µSv, 중앙값 61; 두개안면 CBCT 30–1,073 µSv, 중앙값 87; MSCT 280–1,410 µSv).

CBCT의 단점은 연조직 대조도 저하·인공물 — ALARA 원칙하에 과제에 맞는 최소 FOV(Field of View, 시야)로, 정당화되는 demanding case에서만 사용.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Teaching-oriented exposition of panoramic tomography: narrow-beam rotational technique (X-ray angled upward ~8°), horseshoe-shaped image layer (IL), central plane (CP), and the positioning errors (posteroanterior, horizontal, vertical, ghost-shadow, air-shadow) with their image consequences.
- Systematic normal PTG anatomy catalog (36 labeled structures incl. ghost shadows) and FDI two-digit tooth numbering for interpretation.
- Consolidated radiation dose ladder (per EC 2012 RP 172 / Sedentexct) for conventional dental radiography, CBCT, and MSCT.
- CBCT indications per Sedentexct evidence-based guidelines across developing dentition, restoring dentition (caries, periodontics, periapical, endodontics, trauma) and surgical applications (exodontia, implants, bony pathosis, facial trauma, orthognathic surgery, TMJ), with per-specialty nuance (implant, third-molar, endodontics, fracture, TMJ, cleft, orthodontics, sinus, airway).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Narrative review with clinical case examples and teaching figures; grounded in the Sedentexct/European Commission Radiation Protection No. 172 (2012) evidence-based CBCT guidelines.
- **Scope**: Panoramic tomography (technique, quality/errors, anatomy, interpretation) and CBCT (devices, technical basis, dose/image quality, artifacts, clinical use per specialty).
- **n**: Not applicable (review); primary data sources cited include Kaeppler et al. 2014 (mandibular fractures) and Mota de Almeida et al. 2014 (endodontics).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Effective dose ladder (Table 1): intraoral <1.5 µSv; panoramic 2.7–24.3 µSv; cephalometric <6 µSv; dentoalveolar CBCT (FOV height <10 cm) 11–674 µSv (median 61); craniofacial CBCT (FOV height >10 cm) 30–1,073 µSv (median 87); MSCT maxillo-mandibular 280–1,410 µSv. (Finland annual background ~3,200 µSv ≈ 10 µSv/day.)
- Only one diagnostic reference level (DRL) currently available: 250 mGy·cm² for upper first molar implant placement in adults.
- CBCT acquisition 6–20 s, single gantry rotation 180–360°; isotropic voxels down to 0.076 mm; sub-millimetre high-contrast resolution often exceeding MSCT.
- Implant planning (EAO/ICOI): if clinical assessment + conventional radiography show sufficient bone width/height and relevant boundaries, no additional imaging required; CBCT supports linear measurements, 3D ridge topography, proximity to vital structures, surgical guides.
- Third molar / tooth localisation: where 2D suggests direct mandibular-canal relationship and surgical removal is decided, CBCT may be indicated (only few high-evidence efficacy studies exist).
- Endodontics: limited-volume high-resolution CBCT when conventional imaging inadequate; Mota de Almeida 2014 — significant impact on therapeutic decision efficacy per Sedentexct guidelines; ESE position statement supports use.
- Fractures: Kaeppler et al. 2014 — CBCT of suspected mandibular fractures changed treatment plan in 9.5 %; CBCT alternative to MSCT when soft-tissue detail not required.
- TMJ: good evidence for osseous-abnormality detection but no routine use recommended (no evidence of treatment-decision impact).
- Orthodontics: systematic review found no high-quality evidence of benefit; large-FOV CBCT not routine; justified in complex skeletal cases where MSCT would be the method of choice.
- Cleft palate (SABG): CBCT determines graft volume needed and adequacy of fill; smallest FOV selected for lower dose.
- Sinus/airway: CBCT when soft-tissue contrast not mandatory; 3D upper-airway segmentation (sleep apnoea) of limited validity.
- Artifacts: physics-, patient-, scanner- and cone-beam-related; Feldkamp reconstruction degrades away from central plane; patient motion → double contours (affects entire volume, unlike MSCT); metal/windmill artifacts generally reduced vs MSCT; HU/grey-value quantification unreliable (declines toward edges).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Narrative review with a 2015 evidence cutoff — predates later recommendations and updated DRL literature (partially superseded in that role by the newer held reviews).
- The authors concede sparse high-quality evidence on third-molar CBCT efficacy, orthodontic benefit, and airway-model validity; call for research on whether CBCT changes treatment outcome versus conventional imaging.

## 7. Glossary
- **PTG (Panoramic Tomography)**: extraoral 2D curved-surface tomography producing a single image of maxilla, mandible, teeth, TMJs and maxillary sinuses.
- **IL (Image Layer)**: the tomographic plane of a PTG; horseshoe shaped; only objects within it are well delineated.
- **CP (Central Plane)**: central region of the IL where objects are depicted sharply and undistorted; outside it, horizontal vs vertical magnification discrepancy causes distortion.
- **CBCT (Cone Beam Computed Tomography)**: 3D hard-tissue imaging using a cone-shaped X-ray beam and flat panel detector in a single 180–360° gantry rotation.
- **FOV (Field of View)**: height/diameter of the CBCT scanned volume; small FOV preferred to reduce dose.
- **MSCT (Multislice Computed Tomography)**: conventional spiral CT; higher dose than CBCT but with soft-tissue capability.
- **DRL (Diagnostic Reference Level)**: benchmark dose value per examination for optimisation; only one exists for dental CBCT (250 mGy·cm², upper first molar implant).
- **ALARA**: "as low as reasonably achievable" — the radiation-justification/optimisation principle.
- **HU (Hounsfield Unit)**: CT density scale; CBCT grey-value→HU conversion is unreliable quantitatively.
- **KCOT (Keratocystic Odontogenic Tumour)**: odontogenic cyst/tumour commonly imaged/followed up with CBCT.
- **SABG (Secondary Alveolar Bone Grafting)**: cleft-palate grafting for which CBCT quantifies defect/graft volume.
