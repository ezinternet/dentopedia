## Why Ingested

Chairside gutta-percha (GP) cone decontamination is a small but recurring endodontic aseptic-chain step, and this in vitro study extends the standard NaOCl protocol by benchmarking two nanoparticle disinfectants (silver, chitosan) against it and adding a quantitative AFM surface-damage read-out. It reinforces the microbiology-of-persistent-infection framing in [[endodontics/neelakantan-2018-endodontic-microbiology-editorial]] and pairs with the SR anchor [[endodontics/aucinaite-2025-naocl-chx-gutta-percha-cone-decontamination-sr]] as one arm of a 4-paper GP-cone-decontamination cluster.

## Three-line Summary

In vitro comparison (335 F1 ProTaper GP cones, *B. subtilis* + *C.

albicans*): 2.62% and 5.25% NaOCl eliminated both organisms within 1 min with minimal AFM surface change, whereas 250 µg/mL AgNPs and 625 µg/mL ChNPs needed 5 min against *B. subtilis* (both cleared *C.

albicans* in 1 min); all agents caused progressive, time-dependent topographical damage.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. Head-to-head comparison of NaOCl (2.62% and 5.25%) versus two nanoparticle disinfectants — silver nanoparticles (AgNPs, 250 µg/mL, ~40 nm) and chitosan nanoparticles (ChNPs, 625 µg/mL, 50–500 nm) — for chairside GP-cone disinfection.
2. Uses atomic force microscopy (AFM, tapping mode) to give a *quantitative* surface-topography read-out (root-mean-square, RMS roughness), rather than SEM's qualitative 2-D image, at three immersion times (1, 3, 5 min).
3. Establishes MIC/MBC of all three agents against both a Gram-positive bacterium and a yeast, situating antimicrobial potency alongside surface-damage cost.
4. Notes that while AgNPs/ChNPs have been studied as intracanal medicaments, none had previously been evaluated specifically for GP-cone disinfection.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Solutions**: 5.25% NaOCl and 2.62% NaOCl (PPH CERKAMED); AgNP stock 1000 ppm, ~40 nm; ChNP stock 1.25 mg/mL (2.5 mg powder in 2 mL 1% acetic acid), 50–500 nm.
- **Cones**: 335 total F1 ProTaper (Dentsply Maillefer) cones from freshly opened boxes. Antimicrobial arm used 270 cones (130 per organism); AFM arm used 65 cones (60 in four groups of 15, plus 5 fresh controls).
- **MIC/MBC**: broth microdilution (MIC, 0.5 McFarland, 24 h/37 °C) and CFU assay on Mueller–Hinton agar (MBC = CFU < 30).
- **Contamination**: cones immersed in BHI broth with ~10⁸ CFU/mL suspension, 30 min at 37 °C.
- **Disinfection groups** (n = 30 each per organism, subdivided by time): Group A 5.25% NaOCl, Group B 2.62% NaOCl, Group C 250 µg/mL AgNPs, Group D 625 µg/mL ChNPs; 1, 3, 5 min immersion (n = 10 per time). Untreated positive controls; uncontaminated negative controls.
- **CFU recovery**: post-disinfection cones cultured in thioglycollate broth (24 h/37 °C), spread-plated on BHI (bacteria) / SDA (fungus), CFU counted.
- **AFM**: Bruker Dimension Icon, tapping mode; each cone sectioned 3 mm from tip, glued to slide; images processed in Nanoscope v8.15; RMS roughness reported.
- **Statistics**: two-way ANOVA + Bonferroni (GraphPad Prism v5.0).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**MIC / MBC (µg/mL unless noted):**

| Agent | *B. subtilis* MIC | *B. subtilis* MBC | *C. albicans* MIC | *C. albicans* MBC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.25% NaOCl | 4653 | 19,513 | 4653 | 4653 |
| AgNPs | 31.25 | 62.5 | 250 | 250 |
| ChNPs | 312.25 | 625 | 312.25 | 625 |

**GP-cone disinfection (mean CFU):**
- *C. albicans*: all four agents (both NaOCl, AgNPs, ChNPs) → 0 CFU at 1 min.
- *B. subtilis*: NaOCl (both 2.62% and 5.25%) → 0 CFU at 1 min. AgNPs still 4121.2 CFU at 1 min, 341 at 3 min, 0 at 5 min. ChNPs 3.7 CFU at 1 min, 1.4 at 3 min, 0 at 5 min.
- ChNPs significantly more effective than AgNPs against *B. subtilis* at 1 min and 3 min (P < 0.05).

**AFM topography (RMS; lower = more surface deterioration):**
- Fresh control RMS 124.6.
- At 1 min, only AgNPs (76.2) differed significantly from control; NaOCl (103.08 at 5.25%, 108 at 2.62%) and ChNPs (121.2) did not.
- At 3 min, all disinfectants significantly reduced RMS vs control (5.25% NaOCl 45.36; 2.62% NaOCl 60; AgNPs 52.6; ChNPs 79.6).
- At 5 min, all remained significantly reduced (RMS ~40–44).
- Topographical damage increases progressively with immersion time.

**Bottom line**: NaOCl at both concentrations was the most effective disinfectant and caused minimal surface alteration at 1 min — supporting the classic 1-min NaOCl chairside protocol over slower nanoparticle alternatives.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- In vitro only; artificial contamination with a single Gram-positive bacterium and a single yeast — not clinical polymicrobial biofilm, no anaerobes or *E. faecalis*.
- Small per-cell n (10 for CFU, 5 for AFM).
- Nanoparticle concentrations/sizes tested were fixed; potency is size-dependent, so results may not generalize to other AgNP/ChNP formulations.
- AFM RMS is a surface-roughness proxy for "deterioration"; clinical sealing/adaptation consequences not directly assessed.
- No functional outcome (obturation quality, leakage) measured.

## 6. Related Work

- Senia et al. — classic recommendation of 5.25% NaOCl 1-min immersion for GP-cone disinfection (cited as protocol basis).
- Karunakar et al.; Mishra & Tyagi — prior AFM/topography comparisons of NaOCl vs AgNPs on GP cones, reporting NaOCl's greater surface deterioration at 1 min (contrast with this study's minimal-1-min finding).
- Prado et al. — large baseline RMS variability of fresh GP cones.

## 7. Glossary

- **GP cone / gutta-percha cone**: thermoplastic root-canal obturation point; cannot be autoclaved, so requires chairside chemical disinfection before use.
- **NaOCl**: sodium hypochlorite; oxidizing disinfectant, the standard chairside GP-cone agent.
- **AgNPs**: silver nanoparticles; antimicrobial via disruption of the respiratory chain and cell division.
- **ChNPs**: chitosan nanoparticles; polycationic, disrupt the negatively charged bacterial surface and alter membrane permeability.
- **MIC / MBC**: minimum inhibitory / minimum bactericidal concentration.
- **CFU**: colony-forming unit; count of viable microbes recovered.
- **AFM**: atomic force microscopy; quantitative 3-D surface-topography imaging (tapping mode used here).
- **RMS (root mean square)**: statistical measure of surface-height deviation; lower RMS here indicates greater surface deterioration.
- **MTCC**: Microbial Type Culture Collection (strain source).
