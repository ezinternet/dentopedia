## Why Ingested

The wiki has deep NSAID *prescribing* coverage in `drug/analgesics` (60+ pages on efficacy, dosing, perioperative use, and long-term GI/CV/renal safety) but no page defining the **classification of NSAID hypersensitivity** — the framework a clinician needs to turn a vague "aspirin/NSAID allergic" label into a specific phenotype and a safe analgesic plan. This 2025 World Allergy Organization Statement is the current authoritative scheme and extends the AERD / alternative-agent material already sketched in [[wiki/drug/analgesics/kotowska-rodziewicz-2023-nsaids-role-dentistry-review]]. It also qualifies the generic "aspirin-allergic → acetaminophen or a COX-2 selective" advice in [[wiki/overviews/drug-analgesics-postop-pain-overview]] and [[wiki/drug/analgesics/carrasco-labra-2024-pharmacologic-management-acute-dental-pain-guideline]] by making the choice phenotype-dependent and, for cross-reactive phenotypes, challenge-verified.

## Three-line Summary

WAO consensus Statement (World Allergy Organ J 2025) updating the classification and routine diagnosis of hypersensitivity reactions to non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs, NSAIDs).

It defines cross-reactive COX-1–driven phenotypes (N-ERD, NECD, NIUAA — the new umbrella term replacing NIUA — plus mixed variants), immunologic single-drug phenotypes (SNIUAA, SNIDHR), and newly includes NSAIDs as food-allergy cofactor/aggravator (NEFA/NIFA); oral aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid, ASA) challenge / drug provocation testing is the diagnostic gold standard.

For cross-reactive patients, selective COX-2 inhibitors (celecoxib, etoricoxib) are the safest alternative (~3.3% challenge positivity overall, 0.13% in respiratory reactors) and low-dose paracetamol is usually tolerated, but a verifying drug challenge is still recommended before prescribing for NECD or NIUAA.

## 세줄요약

세계알레르기기구(World Allergy Organization, WAO) 합의 성명서 (World Allergy Organ J 2025): 비스테로이드성 소염진통제 (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs, NSAIDs) 과민반응의 분류와 일상 진단을 갱신.

COX-1 억제 기반 교차반응형 표현형 (N-ERD, NECD, NIUAA — 기존 NIUA를 대체하는 새 포괄 용어 — 및 혼합형), 면역학적 단일약물형 (SNIUAA, SNIDHR)을 정의하고, 음식알레르기 공동인자·악화인자로서의 NSAIDs (NEFA/NIFA)를 새로 포함; 경구 아스피린 (Acetylsalicylic Acid, ASA) 유발검사·약물유발검사 (Drug Provocation Test)가 진단의 표준.

교차반응형 환자에게는 선택적 COX-2 억제제 (셀레콕시브, 에토리콕시브)가 가장 안전한 대체약 (전체 유발검사 양성률 ~3.3%, 호흡기 반응군 0.13%)이고 저용량 파라세타몰도 대개 내약 가능하나, NECD·NIUAA에서는 처방 전 확인용 약물유발검사가 여전히 권고됨.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Replaces the chronological-only frame** (immediate vs delayed) with a phenotype scheme that pairs timing with mechanism (cross-reactive COX-1 vs drug-specific immunologic).
- **Introduces NIUAA** (NSAID-Induced Urticaria/Angioedema/Anaphylaxis) as an umbrella term replacing NIUA: reactions to ≥2 chemically unrelated NSAIDs with urticaria/angioedema and/or involvement of a second organ system, in a patient with **no underlying chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU)**. Absorbs the old "blended reactions" category.
- **Formalizes "mixed" variants**: mixed N-ERD (N-ERD + cutaneous symptoms) and mixed NECD (NECD + respiratory symptoms); these may also be called anaphylactic reactions.
- **Newly includes NEFA/NIFA**: NSAIDs as an *aggravating factor* (NEFA — worsens an otherwise mild food reaction) or *cofactor* (NIFA — patient tolerates the food alone, reacts only with NSAID co-exposure) in food-allergic subjects. ~16–18% of patients with immediate NSAID reactions in two European prospective cohorts (n=328, n=414).
- **Consolidates the diagnostic algorithm** around oral ASA challenge / drug provocation testing as gold standard, with skin testing limited to pyrazolones (SNIUAA) and delayed reactions (patch tests, delayed-reading intradermal tests).
- **Pools alternative-agent challenge data** relevant to any clinician needing a non-culprit analgesic (see §4).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Expert task force commissioned by the World Allergy Organization; topic teams (N-ERD, NECD, NIUAA, SNIUAA, NEFA/NIFA, SNIDHR) each performed literature research and drafted their section; Steering Committee and Review Panel revised.
- Not a systematic review — narrative synthesis + expert consensus; recommendations are graded implicitly ("according to most of the authors of this paper…", "we suggest…", "we recommend…").
- **Phenotype scheme**:

| Phenotype | Timing | Drugs implicated | Underlying disease | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **N-ERD** (aspirin/NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease; = AERD) | Immediate (30 min–3 h) | ≥2 COX-1 inhibitors (cross-reactive) | Asthma + chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps | COX-1 inhibition → cysteinyl-leukotriene overproduction (non-immunologic) |
| **Mixed N-ERD** | Immediate | COX-1 inhibitors | N-ERD + cutaneous/extra-respiratory signs | as N-ERD |
| **NECD** (NSAID-exacerbated cutaneous disease) | Minutes–hours | ≥2 chemically unrelated COX-1 inhibitors | **Chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU)** | COX-1 inhibition (non-immunologic); reaction intensity tracks CSU activity |
| **Mixed NECD** | Minutes–hours | COX-1 inhibitors | NECD + respiratory symptoms (≈ anaphylaxis) | as NECD |
| **NIUAA** (NSAID-induced urticaria/angioedema/anaphylaxis) — *replaces NIUA* | Within hours | ≥2 chemically unrelated NSAIDs | **No CSU** (often atopic, dust-mite sensitized) | COX-1 inhibition (non-immunologic); aeroallergen sensitization may contribute |
| **SNIUAA** (single-NSAID-induced urticaria/angioedema/anaphylaxis) | <1 h (up to 6 h) | Single NSAID or same chemical group; other NSAIDs tolerated | No CSU | **IgE-mediated / immunologic** (best proven for pyrazolones); independent of COX-1 potency |
| **SNIDHR** (single-NSAID-induced delayed hypersensitivity reactions) | Delayed (>24 h) | Single NSAID / same chemical group | — | **T-cell mediated** (CD4/CD8); FDE, MDE, contact/photoallergic dermatitis, rarely SJS/TEN, DRESS, AGEP |
| **NEFA / NIFA** (NSAID-exacerbated / NSAID-induced food allergy) — *newly included* | Immediate | NSAID + food co-exposure (within 4 h) | Food sensitization (Pru p 3 LTP, ω-5 gliadin) | NSAID ↑ gut allergen absorption (impaired COX-1–dependent tight junction) + direct mast-cell effect |

- **Cross-reactive triad (N-ERD/NECD/NIUAA)** share one mechanism — COX-1 inhibition shunts arachidonic acid down the 5-lipoxygenase pathway → cysteinyl-leukotriene (CysLT) surge, loss of the PGE2/EP2 "brake" on mast cells and 5-LO. Baseline and post-ASA urinary LTE4 / 9α,11β-PGF2 profiles are shared across the three. **These are not IgE-mediated.**
- **SNIUAA / SNIDHR** are drug-specific immunologic reactions (IgE and T-cell respectively).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Prevalence / distribution**
- N-ERD: 0.1–0.6% general population; 7.2% of adult asthmatics, up to 14.9% in severe asthma.
- NIUAA: the most common NSAID hypersensitivity phenotype at all ages — >40% of adult NSAID hypersensitivity, 45–60% in children. Adult female predominance (60–65%); up to 60% atopic (dust mite).
- NECD: affects up to 30% of CSU patients (acute exacerbation on chemically unrelated NSAIDs).
- SNIUAA: 15.9–47.2% of immediate NSAID hypersensitivity in adult series; commonest culprits pyrazolones (metamizole/dipyrone, propyphenazone) and propionic acid derivatives (ibuprofen, ketoprofen).
- SNIDHR: least common type diagnosed by allergy testing.
- NEFA/NIFA: ~16% (FDNIH, 52/328) and ~18% (NEFA/NIFA, 75/414) of immediate NSAID reactions in two prospective cohorts; Pru p 3 involved in ~85–89%.

**Diagnosis**
- **Oral ASA challenge / drug provocation test = gold standard.** Oral ASA challenge sensitivity for N-ERD ~80–90%; tolerating 325 mg graded ASA generally excludes COX-1 hypersensitivity (a negative challenge is less conclusive if the patient is on leukotriene-receptor antagonists / high-dose steroids, or recently had polyp surgery).
- Inhaled (bronchial/nasal) lysine-ASA or ketorolac challenge: less sensitive (77–90%) but safer; negative inhaled → proceed to oral.
- β-blockers contraindicated before challenge; FEV1 must be ≥70% predicted.
- Up to 15% of N-ERD patients give no clear COX-1-inhibitor history → challenge needed.
- **NIUAA** may be diagnosed without challenge if recent (<5 y) well-documented reactions to ≥2 chemically unrelated NSAIDs and no CSU — but ASA challenge was positive in only 63% of those who had reacted to 2 NSAIDs vs 92% of those reacting to ≥3, so this risks overdiagnosis; equivocal or >5 y old histories → challenge.
- **Skin tests**: useful only for **pyrazolones** (SNIUAA; metamizole BAT sensitivity 42–65%, specificity 83–100%) and **delayed reactions** (patch tests + delayed-reading intradermal tests — high specificity, low sensitivity; dIDT with metamizole > patch test). Not recommended for common NSAIDs (not standardized, NPV not established). Basophil activation test **not recommended** for cross-reactive phenotypes.
- **NECD**: do not perform graded ASA challenge during active CSU; biomarkers (LTE4, LTE4/9α,11β-PGF2 ratio, tryptase) proposed but data limited.
- **NEFA/NIFA**: question the food–NSAID time interval (≤4 h), skin-prick/specific-IgE to regional food allergens, then NSAID challenge avoiding the positive food for 4 h either side.

**Management — alternative agents (dental-relevant)**
- **Selective COX-2 inhibitors (coxibs) are the preferred alternative** in N-ERD / NECD / NIUAA. Systematic review of 62 studies / 3218 blinded placebo-controlled challenges with selective COX-2 inhibitors: **106 positive (3.3%)** overall. Celecoxib 3.5% (40/1140), etoricoxib 5.4% (32/595), parecoxib 0% (0/116). 93% of reactions were urticaria/angioedema only.
- In patients with **NSAID-induced respiratory reactions** specifically, only **1 of 753 coxib challenges (0.13%)** was positive.
- A patient reacting to one coxib often tolerates another (etoricoxib is more COX-2 selective than celecoxib).
- **Meloxicam** (preferential COX-2): higher — **4.7%** of 847 challenges; nimesulide and meloxicam ~8–10% in one retrospective series; up to 14–16% in dedicated N-ERD cohorts.
- **Paracetamol (acetaminophen)**: a weak COX-1 inhibitor — low doses generally tolerated, but **high dose (≥1 g) triggers reactions in ~20% of cross-reactive patients** (22.4%, 11/49, of N-ERD patients; ~9% of NIUAA). Dose matters: children tolerated 15 mg/kg but some reacted at higher cumulative doses.
- Reactions to alternative-agent challenges were **milder than the index reaction** and easily controlled — but a **verifying drug challenge is still recommended before prescribing an alternative for NECD or NIUAA**, and may be considered for N-ERD with prior anaphylaxis. Suggested protocol: 1/10–1/4 of therapeutic dose, then remainder, 1–2 h observation after each step.
- **SNIUAA / SNIDHR management**: avoid the culprit drug **and its chemical group** (cross-reactivity within propionic acids, acetic acids, pyrazolones, oxicams); alternative chemically unrelated NSAIDs are otherwise fine. Ketoprofen photoallergy → also avoid benzophenone-containing drugs and chemical sunscreens; piroxicam photoallergy → other oxicams tolerated.
- **NEFA/NIFA management**: avoid NSAIDs (incl. antiplatelet-dose ASA) for 4 h before/after meals containing the culprit food — this alone lets all NEFA/NIFA patients keep using NSAIDs.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Consensus/narrative document, not a systematic review; recommendations rest on expert judgment and heterogeneous primary studies.
- Tables and diagnostic-algorithm figures were not captured in the text extraction used for this page.
- NIUAA diagnosis without challenge is acknowledged to risk overdiagnosis (63% ASA-challenge positivity in 2-NSAID reactors).
- NMSIR ("NSAID-multiple selective immediate reaction" — reacts to several chemically unrelated NSAIDs but tolerates high-dose ASA) is recognized as an area of uncertainty; no biomarker; whether truly IgE-mediated is unresolved.
- Pediatric practice diverges: most European pediatric allergists avoid ASA challenges (Reye syndrome concern).
- Genetic studies remain concentrated on N-ERD.

## 7. Glossary

- **N-ERD / AERD**: NSAID-Exacerbated Respiratory Disease (= Aspirin-Exacerbated Respiratory Disease) — asthma + nasal polyps + respiratory reactions to COX-1 inhibitors.
- **NECD**: NSAID-Exacerbated Cutaneous Disease — NSAID-triggered urticaria/angioedema flare in a patient with chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU).
- **NIUA → NIUAA**: NSAID-Induced Urticaria/Angioedema (± Anaphylaxis) — cross-reactive cutaneous ± second-organ reactions to ≥2 chemically unrelated NSAIDs, **no** underlying CSU. The WAO Statement renames NIUA to NIUAA.
- **SNIUAA**: Single-NSAID-Induced Urticaria/Angioedema/Anaphylaxis — immediate, immunologic (IgE), one drug or one chemical group; other NSAIDs tolerated.
- **SNIDHR**: Single-NSAID-Induced Delayed Hypersensitivity Reaction — T-cell mediated, delayed (fixed drug eruption, maculopapular exanthem, photoallergic contact dermatitis, rarely SJS/TEN, DRESS, AGEP).
- **NEFA / NIFA**: NSAID-Exacerbated / NSAID-Induced Food Allergy — NSAID as aggravating factor (NEFA) or obligatory cofactor (NIFA) of a food-allergic reaction.
- **FDNIH**: Food-Dependent NSAID-Induced Hypersensitivity — the collective term from earlier European studies, now split into NEFA/NIFA.
- **Cross-reactive (vs selective)**: cross-reactive phenotypes react to multiple chemically unrelated NSAIDs via COX-1 inhibition; selective (single-NSAID) phenotypes react to one drug/group via an immune mechanism.
- **COX-1 inhibition mechanism**: blocking constitutive cyclooxygenase-1 shunts arachidonic acid to 5-lipoxygenase → cysteinyl-leukotriene (LTC4/LTD4/LTE4) surge → bronchoconstriction, vascular permeability, mast-cell activation.
- **Drug Provocation Test (DC / DPT)**: supervised graded re-administration of the suspected or an alternative drug — the diagnostic gold standard here.
- **BAT**: Basophil Activation Test — flow-cytometry functional assay; useful mainly for pyrazolone SNIUAA, not for cross-reactive phenotypes.
- **Coxib**: selective COX-2 inhibitor (celecoxib, etoricoxib, parecoxib; historically rofecoxib, valdecoxib).
