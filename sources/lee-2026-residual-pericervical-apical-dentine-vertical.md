## Why Ingested

The wiki's existing position statement on longitudinal fractures ([[cracked-tooth/patel-2025-position-statement-longitudinal-cracks-fractures]]) recommends "conservative endodontics preserving pericervical dentine, avoid over-preparation" as VRF prevention, but that guidance is stated qualitatively without a quantitative dose-response link. This case-control study directly quantifies that mechanistic risk factor — using a radiographic RCF/root-width ratio to categorize preparation extent (intact/minimum/traditional/excessive) at both pericervical and apical levels — and isolates residual **apical** dentine loss (not pericervical) as the significant independent predictor of VRF in endodontically treated molars, giving the existing qualitative recommendation a measurable clinical threshold.

## Three-line Summary

Case-control study (44 VRF cases vs.

92 controls) in endodontically treated molars found excessive residual apical dentine loss (RCF/root-width ratio > 0.21), tooth type (mandibular molars), history of root canal re-treatment, and time since primary RCT ≥ 15 years were independent predictors of vertical root fracture, with a combined model AUC of 0.940.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First study to focus exclusively on molars when examining the association between residual root dentine and VRF.
- Introduces a radiograph-based (periapical film, PA) categorization system for residual pericervical and apical dentine using RCF(or canal)-to-root-width ratio cut-offs derived from 48 reference roots (intact canals + known-instrument-size canals): intact canal, minimum, traditional, excessive.
- Demonstrates that residual **apical** dentine, not pericervical dentine, is the significant independent radiographic predictor of VRF in molars — contrasting with the pericervical-dentine-preservation emphasis common in prior literature (e.g., Clark and Khademi).
- Confirms and quantifies cumulative/fatigue-related risk factors: history of re-treatment (reRCT) and long elapsed time since primary root canal treatment (pRCT).
- Produces a combined multivariate diagnostic/predictive model (tooth type + residual apical dentine + reRCT history + time from pRCT ≥15y) with AUC 0.940, useful for discriminating VRF from non-VRF in molars presenting with an isolated periodontal probing depth (PD) ≥5 mm.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: case-control study drawn from a prior observational cohort of 228 teeth with confirmed isolated PD ≥5 mm (221 patients, specialist endodontic clinic in Tokyo, 2012–2020).
- **Sample**: 136 endodontically treated molars (ETMs) — 44 VRF cases and 92 non-VRF controls (matched only on tooth type [molars] and root-filled status), from 132 patients (37 male, 95 female; mean age 46.7 ± 9.9 years). Anterior teeth, premolars, non-root-filled teeth, and teeth without follow-up (n=5) were excluded.
- **Power analysis**: based on prior post-rate effect size (Silva et al., OR 2.4), required 136 teeth for 80% power / 95% CI via logistic regression — matched by final sample size.
- **VRF diagnosis**: visual confirmation under dental operating microscope (OPMI PROergo, Zeiss) during treatment, matched against PD location, CBCT osseous defects, and direct observation after extraction; controls' outcome assessed clinically/radiographically (blinded second examiner, 15+ years' experience).
- **Variables assessed**: age, sex, tooth type, proximal contacts, restoration type, presence/type of post, apical terminus of RCF (radiographic categories: short/adequate/exact/over), residual pericervical dentine (measured at furcation level), residual apical dentine (measured at apical terminus of RCF, or −1 mm from radiographic apex if RCF reached/exceeded it), history of reRCT (and number of reRCTs), time elapsed since pRCT.
- **Dentine measurement**: ratio of mesiodistal RCF-(or canal-)width to root width on periapical radiographs (PAs), measured perpendicular to the root at coronal (furcation-level, pericervical) and apical reference points. Categorized into 4 groups using cut-offs derived from 48 reference roots (24 with known instrument sizes, 24 from intact molars), matched for tooth-type distribution.
  - Cut-offs (mean ± SD RC-or-RCF/root ratio): Pericervical — intact 0.13(±0.03), minimum 0.23(±0.03), traditional 0.27(±0.01), excessive >0.27. Apical — intact 0.11(±0.04), minimum 0.16(±0.04), traditional 0.21(±0.04), excessive >0.21.
- **Reliability**: calibration on 10 non-study ETMs; intra- and inter-examiner agreement re-assessed at 2 months (Cohen's kappa reported as "very good," >0.8) for both apical terminus and dentine measurements.
- **Statistics**: SPSS v15.0. Simple binary logistic regression on 12 variables (threshold p<0.01 for entry into multivariate model); multiple binary logistic regression (stepwise); ROC curve/AUC, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV for the final model. Significance set at α=0.05.
- **Sub-analysis sample sizes**: reRCT frequency analysis, n=120 (39 cases/81 controls); time-from-pRCT analysis, n=111 (35 cases/76 controls) — smaller due to missing clinical-record data.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Mandibular first molars were the most frequent tooth type in both cases (52.3%, 23/44) and controls (35.9%, 33/92); within case mandibular-first-molars, VRF occurred predominantly in mesial roots (69.6%, 16/23). Overall, VRF was prevalent in mesial roots (61.4%, 27/44 across all molar types).
- Most teeth had no post (72.7% cases, 76.1% controls); post presence was **not** a significant risk factor when analysis was restricted to molars (OR 1.19, 95% CI 0.53–2.71, p=0.672) — contrasting with prior studies including mixed tooth types.
- **Residual pericervical dentine**: "excessive" preparation seen in 81.8% (36/44) of cases vs. 65.2% (60/92) of controls; mean RCF/root ratio 0.37 (cases) vs. 0.29 (controls). NOT significant in the final multivariate model.
- **Residual apical dentine**: "excessive" preparation seen in 61.4% (27/44) of cases vs. only 10.9% (10/92) of controls; mean ratio 0.25 (cases) vs. 0.14 (controls). Significant in both univariate (OR 13.0, 95% CI 5.33–31.8, p<0.001) and multivariate models (adjusted OR 12.9, 95% CI 2.92–57.5, p=0.001).
- Apical terminus of RCF rated "short" reduced VRF probability (OR 0.23, 95% CI 0.08–0.64, p=0.005) in univariate analysis — consistent with the apical-dentine-preservation finding.
- History of reRCT: 89.7% (35/39) of VRF cases vs. 46.9% (38/81) of controls had ≥1 reRCT; 46.1% (18/39) vs. 7.4% (6/81) had ≥2 reRCTs. Strong multivariate predictor — 1 reRCT: adjusted OR 9.89 (95% CI 1.68–58.2, p=0.011); ≥2 reRCTs: adjusted OR 87.9 (95% CI 10.7–722.1, p<0.001).
- Time since pRCT ≥15 years: adjusted OR 20.7 (95% CI 4.20–102.0, p<0.001).
- Tooth type: mandibular first molar adjusted OR 19.2 (95% CI 2.44–150.5, p=0.005) vs. maxillary first molar reference; mandibular second molar adjusted OR 18.9 (95% CI 1.85–193.7, p=0.013).
- **Final multivariate model** (tooth type + residual apical dentine + reRCT history + time from pRCT ≥15y): sensitivity 85.7%, specificity 89.5%, PPV 78.9%, NPV 93.2%; AUC 0.940 (95% CI 0.899–0.981, p<0.001) for discriminating VRF vs. non-VRF in molars with isolated PD ≥5 mm.
- Intra- and inter-observer reliability for apical terminus and dentine measurements: "very good" (kappa >0.8).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

Author-stated limitations:
1. Retrospective case-control design — cases/controls matched only on tooth type and root-filled status (not other variables) due to sample constraints.
2. Unaccounted contributory factors: parafunctional habits, preoperative dentine volume/canal size, coronal dentine loss above orifices, axial root morphology, instrumentation technique/file system/obturation technique (all unavailable retrospectively; hand files + cold lateral condensation assumed as standard practice in the source general dental practices in Japan).
3. Two-dimensional (periapical radiograph) assessment risks image distortion and cannot capture bucco-lingual dentine thickness.
4. Potential uncontrolled confounding — authors recommend well-designed prospective studies to isolate the true effect of residual dentine thickness.
5. Sample restricted to Japanese patients treated in a system with low-cost, accessible endodontic retreatment (contributing to unusually high reRCT rates — 46.1% of cases had ≥2 reRCTs) — limits generalizability to other populations/healthcare systems.

Additional identified limitation: VRF diagnosis, while microscope-confirmed, and dentine measurement rely on 2D PA reference points (furcation level, RCF apical terminus) that assume consistent projection geometry: angulation variance across serial radiographs is not explicitly quantified as a source of measurement error.

Future work suggested: prospective, more tightly controlled study (including instrumentation protocol, preoperative canal size, 3D imaging) to isolate the causal weight of apical dentine loss versus other cumulative/mechanical stress factors.

## 6. Related Work

- Silva et al. — prior CBCT-based study of dentine thickness and VRF (informed this study's power calculation); the present study's PA-based method addresses methodological gaps in that study (no calibration/intra-examiner agreement reported, mixed single/multi-rooted teeth, arithmetic mean thickness across only 3 points).
- Lee et al. (authors' own prior cohort) — the source 228-tooth isolated-PD-≥5mm dataset from which this study's 136-tooth molar subsample was drawn; also established that isolated periodontal pockets in molars can indicate either VRF or apical periodontitis from endodontic failure (vs. premolars, where isolated pockets are exclusively VRF-associated).
- Chan et al.; Guldener et al. — prior reports that mesial roots of mandibular first molars are most VRF-susceptible regardless of root canal treatment, corroborated by this study's finding (69.6% mesial-root VRF among case mandibular first molars).
- Clark and Khademi — originators of the "pericervical dentine" preservation concept, which this study's apical-dominant (not pericervical-dominant) finding partially contradicts/refines for the molar subgroup.
- Versiani et al. — review concluding dentinal microcracks after canal preparation are largely a laboratory phenomenon, cited to contextualize why this study focused on measurable dentine volume loss rather than microcrack counts.

## 7. Glossary

- **VRF (Vertical Root Fracture)**: complete or incomplete fracture initiated from the root at any level, usually directed buccolingually.
- **ETM (Endodontically Treated Molar)**: a molar that has undergone root canal treatment.
- **pRCT / reRCT**: primary root canal treatment / re-treatment (retreatment) of root canal.
- **RCF (Root Canal Filling)**: the obturation material filling the prepared canal.
- **RCF/root ratio**: mesiodistal width of RCF (or canal, or post) divided by mesiodistal root width at a given radiographic reference level — used as a proxy for residual dentine thickness.
- **Pericervical dentine**: dentine in the region roughly 4 mm coronal and apical to the crestal bone/furcation level; concept popularized by Clark and Khademi as critical for fracture resistance.
- **Apical terminus of RCF**: radiographic classification of how far the canal filling extends relative to the radiographic apex (short/adequate/exact/over).
- **PD (Probing Depth)**: periodontal pocket depth; "isolated PD ≥5 mm" (localized defect with normal sulcus depth on either side) was the inclusion criterion for the source cohort.
- **AUC (Area Under the Curve)**: ROC curve metric of discriminative accuracy for the multivariate predictive model.
- **PPV / NPV**: positive/negative predictive value of the diagnostic model.
