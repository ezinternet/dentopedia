## Three-line Summary

Scoping review of 37 studies (5 databases, adults ≥18y) mapping the patient experience evidence base for clear aligner treatment across five domains: pain/discomfort, OHRQOL, aesthetics/psychosocial, speech, and compliance/satisfaction. Pain and OHRQOL were the most studied outcomes; only 36% of patients achieved full compliance (≥22h/day). High aesthetics satisfaction coexisted with compliance struggles, suggesting motivation ≠ adherence.

## 2. Key Findings

- Most studied outcomes: pain/discomfort and OHRQOL; aesthetics/psychosocial third
- Full compliance (≥22h/day): only 36% of patients across studies
- Aesthetic satisfaction: consistently high — patients motivated by appearance
- Speech: transient impact; adapts within days to weeks
- Compliance predictors: younger age, higher education, shorter treatment, higher initial motivation
- Gap identified: long-term PRO data (>18 months) and compliance with attachments/elastics understudied

## 3. Methodology

Scoping review methodology (Arksey and O'Malley framework). 5 databases. Adults ≥18y, clear aligner treatment, PRO outcomes. Narrative synthesis; no formal risk-of-bias tool applied (scoping design). 37 included studies (number after dedup and eligibility screening).

## 4. Clinical Implications

The 36% full-compliance rate is a critical benchmark — well below the assumed ≥22h/day threshold. Clinicians should implement compliance monitoring (e.g., Smart Force, temperature-sensitive aligner trackers) and address compliance predictors at treatment planning.

## 5. Limitations

Scoping design — no quality assessment. Heterogeneous compliance definitions across studies (some use ≥20h/day). Predominantly short-term studies. English-language publications may introduce language bias.
