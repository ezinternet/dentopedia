## Three-line Summary

Prospective pre-clinical training study at the University of Zurich (3 student cohorts, n=41/34/48) comparing hand-scaling performance before and immediately after an 8.5-hour manual instrumentation course, against experienced-hygienist and layperson controls on a typodont model.

Baseline student scores (29.8-42% tooth surface cleaned) resembled laypeople (27.9%), but post-training scores (61.7-79.5%) improved significantly (P<.001) and were statistically indistinguishable from experienced hygienists (67.4%).

A shortened 8.5-hour pre-clinical course was sufficient to bring novice students to expert-level hand-scaling proficiency, though the outcome is simulation-based (typodont, not live patients) and only the abstract was retrievable.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Quantifies dental-student hand-scaling skill acquisition after a shortened (8.5-hour) pre-clinical training course, benchmarked against experienced-hygienist (positive control) and layperson (negative control) performance.
- Shows post-training student performance (percentage of tooth surface cleaned, by planimetric analysis) is statistically indistinguishable from experienced dental hygienists.
- Supports the adequacy of reduced pre-clinical training time following Bologna-reform curriculum restructuring, a live curricular-design question for dental schools.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Prospective, controlled, pre/post skill-acquisition study (educational cohort design; no randomization stated).
- **Participants**: 3 consecutive classes of undergraduate dental students (n=41, 34, 48); 8 experienced dental hygienists (positive control); 8 laypeople (negative control).
- **Task**: Timed 5-minute scaling/root-planing session removing lacquer concrement from the maxillary left canine on a typodont, using Gracey and universal (Deppeler M23A) curettes.
- **Timing**: Baseline session prior to any instruction; second session immediately following 8.5 hours of manual pre-clinical training.
- **Outcome measurement**: Instrumented teeth collected, scanned, and planimetrically analyzed for the percentage of tooth surface cleaned.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Baseline (pre-training) student scores: 29.8%, 31.0%, 42% (3 cohorts) vs 27.9% laypeople — not significantly different.
- Post-training student scores: 61.7%, 79.5%, 76% vs 67.4% experienced hygienists (P<.001, students' improvement over baseline).
- No statistically significant difference between post-training students and experienced hygienists.

## 7. Glossary

- **Scaling/root planing**: mechanical removal of calculus/biofilm and smoothing of root surfaces using hand instruments.
- **Gracey curette**: area-specific hand curette with an offset blade, used for subgingival scaling.
- **Universal curette**: hand curette with a symmetrical blade usable on multiple tooth surfaces (Deppeler M23A used here).
- **Typodont**: artificial dentition model used for pre-clinical skills training.
- **Planimetric analysis**: image-based measurement of the proportion of a surface area (here, cleaned tooth surface) from scanned images.
