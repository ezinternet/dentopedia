Dahiya 2024는 Li and Leung 2024를 포함한 10편의 SR/MA(2014-2024)를 AMSTAR 2로 재평가·통합하여, "ARD 사용 임플란트 환자의 MRONJ 발생"이라는 동일 주제에 대한 상위 레벨 근거(umbrella review, 실패 임플란트 524개 중 MRONJ 2-17%)를 제공한다. AMSTAR 2 평가 결과 10편 중 모더레이트 2편·낮음 6편·매우낮음 2편으로, 개별 SR의 방법론적 한계를 드러내면서 [[drug/mronj/li-2024-antiresorptive-implants-mronj-sr]]의 임상적 결론(첫 3년 집중 모니터링)에 근거 강도 맥락을 더한다.

## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (10 systematic reviews, 7 without and 3 with meta-analysis, published 2014-2024, PROSPERO CRD42024583017) synthesizing evidence on antiresorptive drug (ARD: bisphosphonates, denosumab) therapy and medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ) in dental implant patients.

Across the 10 included reviews, total failed implants = 524, with MRONJ accounting for 2-17% of failures; IV-route bisphosphonate for ≥45-60 months carried the highest MRONJ risk, and 3 meta-analyses on "implant failure/loss" found no statistically significant pooled effect (heterogeneous, I² up to 96.87%).

AMSTAR 2 quality appraisal rated only 20% of included reviews as moderate quality (60% low, 20% critically low) — clinical decisions should rely on individualized risk assessment (route, duration, comorbidities) rather than the pooled meta-analytic estimates, which are underpowered by heterogeneity.

## 세줄요약

10편의 체계적 고찰(SR 7편, MA 포함 3편, 2014-2024년 출판, PROSPERO CRD42024583017)을 종합한 umbrella review로, 임플란트 환자에서 항흡수제(ARD: 비스포스포네이트, 데노수맙) 투여와 약제 관련 악골괴사(MRONJ)의 관계를 평가.

10편 전체에서 실패 임플란트 524개 중 MRONJ가 실패 원인의 2-17%를 차지했으며, IV 경로 비스포스포네이트를 45-60개월 이상 투여한 경우 MRONJ 위험이 가장 높았고, "임플란트 실패/상실"에 대한 3건의 메타분석은 통계적으로 유의한 통합 효과를 보이지 않음(이질성 I² 최대 96.87%).

AMSTAR 2 방법론적 질 평가에서 포함된 SR의 20%만 중등도 품질(60% 낮음, 20% 매우 낮음)로 평가되어, 통합 메타분석 수치보다 경로·투여기간·동반질환을 고려한 개별 환자 위험평가가 임상적으로 더 중요함을 시사.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First umbrella review specifically synthesizing SR/MA evidence on the relationship between ARD (antiresorptive drug) therapy and MRONJ in dental implant patients.
- Consolidates 10 systematic reviews (2014-2024) spanning 171 primary studies and an implant range of 38.5-3583 (mean 1830) implants across reviews.
- Quantifies overall MRONJ burden: of 524 total failed implants across the 10 reviews, MRONJ accounted for 2-17% of failures depending on the review.
- Applies AMSTAR 2 quality appraisal across all 10 included reviews (a cross-review methodological-quality layer absent from any single included SR).
- Identifies IV bisphosphonate route and long treatment duration (≥45-60 months) as the dominant MRONJ risk driver, with posterior mandible as the predominant MRONJ site across reviews.
- Identifies comorbidities (smoking, diabetes, hypertension, corticosteroid therapy, anti-cancer therapy) and ill-fitting dentures as MRONJ trigger factors reported across included reviews.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Umbrella review (overview of systematic reviews) per PRISMA, registered PROSPERO CRD42024583017.
- **Focused question**: Does ARD treatment significantly manifest MRONJ in individuals receiving dental implants?
- **PICOS**: P = patients undergoing dental implant therapy; I = history of ARD intake before/during/after implant placement; C = patients without ARD history; O = MRONJ; S = systematic review with or without meta-analysis.
- **Databases**: Google Scholar, Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed/MEDLINE; MeSH-term Boolean search strings (Table 1 of paper) for January 2014-May 2024 (English, human studies).
- **Screening**: Two independent reviewers (MD, SR) for title/abstract and full-text screening; conflicts resolved by a third reviewer (PD), further conflicts by a fourth (VS).
- **Eligibility**: SR with/without MA, patients with ARD history before/during/after implant placement, benign or malignant indication, English language, Jan 2014-Jul 2024. Excluded: non-English, no MRONJ outcome specified, in vitro/animal studies, primary research, narrative/scoping reviews, expert opinion, short communications, editorials. Nine studies were explicitly excluded with stated reasons (Table 2 of paper), e.g., Kellesarian 2017 (implant coating with ARD, not clinical outcome), Fiorillo 2022 (no MRONJ outcome), Limones 2020 (denosumab vs zoledronic acid comparison only), He 2020 (narrative review).
- **Quality appraisal**: AMSTAR 2 (16-item checklist, yes/partial yes/no/not relevant), independently applied by three examiners (MD, SR, PD), consensus meeting, fourth reviewer (VS) for unresolved disagreement. Reviews graded high/moderate/low/critically low quality.
- **Search yield**: 950 records identified → duplicates and 10 ineligible records removed → 10 systematic reviews included (7 without meta-analysis, 3 with meta-analysis).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

### Included reviews overview
- 10 SR/MA included (Sher 2021, Stavropoulos 2018, Rebelo 2023, Li and Leung 2024, Gelazius 2018, Anitua 2024, Papadakis 2021, de-Freitas 2016, Ata-Ali 2016, Chappuis 2018).
- Implants per review ranged 38.5-3583 (mean 1830); primary studies per review ranged 7-36.
- 6 reviews assessed ARD taken prior to implant placement; 1 review assessed ARD taken after placement; none assessed ARD taken during placement.
- Failed implants per review ranged 1-58; MRONJ-attributable failure ranged 0.4-17% of failed implants per review.
- Time from implant placement to review follow-up ranged 24-111.5 months (reported review mean stated as 511.1 months in the text, an apparent internal inconsistency in the source paper).

### Pooled/meta-analytic findings (3 reviews performed MA)
| Review | Event | p-value | RR | 95% CI | Heterogeneity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stavropoulos 2018 | Implant loss | <0.01 | 2.7 | 0.00-0.007 | I²=36.9% |
| Ata-Ali 2016 | Implant failure rate | 0.156 | 2.18 | 0.01-51.21 | OR=1.43 |
| Chappuis 2018 | Implant failure rate | <0.01 | 2.02 | 2.67-6.39 | I²=96.87% |

All three meta-analyses were judged statistically insignificant/uninterpretable for the "implant failure or peri-implant marginal bone loss" outcome given high heterogeneity.

### AMSTAR 2 quality grading (10 reviews)
- Moderate quality: 20% (Ata-Ali 2016, Chappuis 2018)
- Low quality: 60% (Sher 2021, Rebelo 2023, Gelazius 2018, Anitua 2024, Papadakis 2021, de-Freitas 2016)
- Critically low quality: 20% (Stavropoulos 2018, Li and Leung 2024)
- No review achieved high quality.
- Scores ranged 4-11.5 out of 16 (mean ≈ 6).

### MRONJ incidence per review (% of failed implants attributable to MRONJ)
Sher 2021 = 3%; Stavropoulos 2018 = 2%; Rebelo 2023 = 4%; Li and Leung 2024 = 17%; Papadakis 2021 = 0.4%; Anitua 2024 = 4%; Gelazius 2018 = 4%; de-Freitas 2016 = 8%; Ata-Ali 2016 = 0.5%; Chappuis 2018 = 4%.

### Cumulative burden
Total failed implants across all 10 reviews = 524, MRONJ incidence range 2-17%.

### Risk modifiers identified across reviews
- **Route**: IV bisphosphonate carries substantially higher MRONJ risk than oral/SC route (e.g., de-Freitas 2016: 100% implant loss with IV cancer-dose ARD; Gelazius 2018: 100% failure with 72-month IV bisphosphonate; Papadakis 2021: 100% survival with 20.5-month oral bisphosphonate).
- **Duration**: cessation of ARD ≥12-132 months prior to implant placement associated with lower MRONJ onset (4-14%) in 6 reviews; ARD initiated 8-72 months after implant placement associated with 100% implant success in 2 reviews.
- **Indication**: cancer patients more prone to MRONJ than osteoporosis/benign-disease patients.
- **Site**: posterior mandible most common, followed by posterior maxilla.
- **Latency**: time from implant placement to MRONJ onset ranged 3-19 months in one analysis (average stated as 99.5 months elsewhere in the text — internal inconsistency in the source).
- **Comorbidities/triggers**: hypertension, ischemic heart disease, anemia, dementia, renal failure, diabetes, smoking, chemotherapy, corticosteroid use, ill-fitting dentures, and peri-implantitis were cited as MRONJ risk/trigger factors across included reviews.
- **Prevention**: antibiotic prophylaxis (one meta-analysis reported 66.9% reduction in implant failure with antibiotics), strict oral hygiene, chlorhexidine use, and drug holiday were recommended mitigation strategies.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Author-stated limitations**:
  - Most included primary evidence within the SRs was cohort studies, case reports, and case series (high risk of bias inherent to lower-tier study designs).
  - Substantial heterogeneity in data presentation across reviews; several reviews did not report ARD type/route consistently.
  - No consistent reporting of the time window between implant placement and MRONJ onset in some reviews; some reviews omitted MRONJ site.
  - Only 2/10 reviews achieved AMSTAR 2 moderate quality; none achieved high quality — signals inadequate methodology (protocol registration, literature search comprehensiveness, risk-of-bias handling, meta-analytic methods) across the evidence base.
  - Meta-analytic pooling (3 reviews) was undermined by high heterogeneity (I² up to 96.87%), limiting the interpretability of "implant failure" pooled estimates.
- **Future directions proposed by authors**: more controlled trials and individualized parameter assessment; high-quality RCTs and long-term follow-up studies to corroborate SR findings and to define ARD regimens that minimize MRONJ incidence in implant patients.
- **Appraisal note for this wiki**: as an umbrella review of SRs rather than a re-analysis of primary patient-level data, its conclusions are only as strong as the (mostly low/critically-low-quality) underlying reviews; it should be read as a risk-factor synthesis and quality map rather than as a definitive quantitative pooled-risk estimate.

## 7. Glossary

- **ARD (Antiresorptive Drug)**: Medication class that reduces bone resorption by inhibiting osteoclast activity — includes bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate, ibandronate, pamidronate, zoledronate, clodronate, etidronate, tiludronate) and denosumab (RANKL inhibitor).
- **MRONJ (Medication-Related Osteonecrosis of the Jaw)**: Exposed bone (or bone probeable through an intra-/extraoral fistula) in the maxillofacial region persisting >8 weeks in a patient with a history of antiresorptive or antiangiogenic drug treatment, without prior radiation to the jaws.
- **BRONJ (Bisphosphonate-Related Osteonecrosis of the Jaw)**: Subtype/precursor terminology for MRONJ specific to bisphosphonate exposure, used in older included reviews.
- **AMSTAR 2**: A MeaSurement Tool to Assess systematic Reviews (version 2) — 16-item critical appraisal instrument for systematic reviews with or without meta-analysis, yielding an overall confidence rating (high/moderate/low/critically low).
- **Umbrella review**: A review that systematically synthesizes and appraises multiple existing systematic reviews/meta-analyses addressing the same or related question, sitting one evidence level above a single SR.
- **PROSPERO**: International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews.
- **Drug holiday**: Temporary discontinuation of ARD therapy intended to allow partial recovery of osteoclast function/bone turnover before invasive dental procedures.
