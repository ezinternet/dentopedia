## Why Ingested
Animal split-mouth dog study (2025) directly comparing histomorphometry and CBCT bone density between OD (Densah) and conventional (NucleOSS) drilling with and without implant placement — adds quantitative dog-maxilla data to support [[wiki/implants/osseodensification/gaikwad-2022-osseodensification-animal-biomechanics-bic-sr-ma]] and provides a novel comparison of immediate drilling effects (thermal osteonecrosis vs bone chip condensation) absent from earlier animal models.

## Three-line Summary
Animal study (8 mongrel dogs, split-mouth) comparing OD Densah drills vs conventional NucleOSS drills in dog maxilla, with and without NucleOSS T6 implant placement (3.5×10 mm), evaluated at 0 days (drilling only) and 2 months post-implant via H&E/Masson's Trichrome histomorphometry and CBCT.

OD produced significantly higher bone area percentage (22.26±2.58% vs 11.11±0.82%, p<0.001 by H&E) and markedly higher CBCT bone density around implants (buccal: 1678.75±73.37 vs 919.5±220.84, p<0.001; palatal: 1785±213.68 vs 1186.5±71.32, p<0.001); conventional drilling caused immediate thermal osteonecrosis while OD compressed viable bone chips into osteotomy walls.

Supports OD as the preferred technique for implant placement in low-density maxillary bone; limited by small sample (n=8 dogs) and 2-month follow-up insufficient to assess long-term remodeling.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Direct histomorphometric demonstration that conventional NucleOSS drilling causes immediate thermal osteonecrosis (empty lacunae, microcracks) at day 0, while OD Densah drilling preserves viable bone chips compressed into osteotomy walls
- Quantitative bone area % superiority of OD vs conventional at 2 months in dog maxilla implant sites: H&E 22.26% vs 11.11% (p<0.001), Masson's Trichrome 14.55% vs 10.70% (p<0.001)
- CBCT radiographic bone density around implants substantially higher with OD: buccal 1678 vs 920 HU equivalent (p<0.001), palatal 1785 vs 1187 (p<0.001)
- Collagen maturity confirmed: OD subgroup IIB showed mature collagen type I (red Masson's staining) vs immature collagen type III (blue) in conventional IIA at same 2-month timepoint
- No statistically significant CBCT density difference between OD and conventional drilling WITHOUT implants at day 0 (p=0.066 buccal, p=0.258 palatal), indicating implant-specific compaction effect

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Animal experimental, split-mouth, controlled
- **Animals**: 8 adult male mongrel dogs, 15–16 kg, ≥1.5 years old; from Cairo University Faculty of Veterinary Medicine
- **Groups**:
  - Control: distal socket of extracted maxillary 2nd premolar (no drilling)
  - Group I (0 days, no implant): IA = NucleOSS conventional CW drills, IB = Densah CCW OD drills
  - Group II (2 months, with implant): IIA = NucleOSS CW + NucleOSS T6 implant, IIB = Densah CCW + NucleOSS T6 implant
- **Implants**: NucleOSS T6, 3.5 mm × 10 mm cylindrical threaded titanium endosseous
- **Drill sequences**: OD = 2.0, 2.3, 2.5, 3.0 mm CCW; Conventional = 2.0, 2.8, 3.3, 3.5 mm CW
- **Histology**: H&E stain + Masson's Trichrome stain; histomorphometric analysis
- **Imaging**: CBCT radiographic bone density measurement (buccal and palatal)
- **Stats**: IBM SPSS v20; Kolmogorov-Smirnov + Shapiro-Wilk normality; independent t-test (2 groups), ANOVA + Bonferroni post-hoc (3 groups); p<0.05 significance
- **Ethics**: IACUC Cairo University approval CU/III/F/69/19; ARRIVE guidelines

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Small sample size: n=8 dogs total (4 per time point per split-mouth side)
- 2-month follow-up may be insufficient for complete bone remodeling assessment (dogs require 2–3 months for full healing)
- Different implant diameters created by OD vs conventional drill sequences (3.0 mm final OD vs 3.5 mm conventional) — different osteotomy sizes may partially confound results
- No ISQ / insertion torque data; only histomorphometry and CBCT
- Authors recommend longer follow-up, micro-CT, and osteogenic gene marker evaluation
- No data on implant survival or clinical failure rates

## 6. Related Work
- Trisi 2016 (sheep iliac crest, OD vs conventional): foundational animal OD study; present study confirms OD superiority in a canine maxillary model
- Gaikwad 2022 (SR+MA animal biomechanics/BIC): this study adds histomorphometric + CBCT data to the animal evidence base
- Lima Monteiro 2024 (SR+MA histomorphometry): this dog study contributes new histomorphometric evidence
- Mello-Machado 2021 (RCT, OD vs conventional, neovascularization): contrasted — IIB in present study showed extensive vascularization while Mello-Machado found NS difference in neovascularization

## 7. Glossary
- **Osseodensification (OD)**: Biomechanical non-subtractive osteotomy using CCW-rotating Densah burs at 800–1500 rpm; compacts bone apically and laterally against osteotomy walls
- **Spring-back effect**: Viscoelastic recovery of compacted cancellous bone post-OD, narrowing osteotomy diameter and creating bone-implant compressive interlock
- **NucleOSS T6**: Cylindrical threaded titanium implant system used in this study (3.5×10 mm)
- **Bone area percentage (BA%)**: Histomorphometric measure of newly formed bone area relative to total area of interest in histological section
- **BAFO%**: Bone Area Fraction Occupancy — proportion of implant surface covered by bone (referenced from discussion; not directly measured in this study)
- **Thermal osteonecrosis**: Heat-induced bone cell death from conventional drilling friction, evidenced by empty osteocyte lacunae
