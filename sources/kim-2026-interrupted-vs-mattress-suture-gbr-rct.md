## Why Ingested

This preliminary RCT directly tests whether adding a tension-relieving horizontal mattress suture to interrupted sutures reduces wound dehiscence after horizontal GBR — a clinically relevant question given the high rate of membrane exposure following GBR. The result (no significant difference, dehiscence 60% vs 55%) refines the [[suture-wound-closure/suture-wound-closure|suture-wound-closure]] evidence base and contextualizes the high absolute dehiscence rate with important patient-demographic caveats.

## Three-line Summary

A preliminary RCT (n=40) at a Veterans hospital compared simple interrupted sutures alone versus interrupted plus horizontal mattress sutures for primary closure after horizontal GBR; no statistically significant differences were found in wound dehiscence width (2.92 vs 2.17 mm), dehiscence length (5.32 vs 4.37 mm), or dehiscence length/incision ratio (0.42 vs 0.34) at 2 weeks (all p>0.05). The absolute dehiscence occurrence was high in both groups (60% vs 55%), partially attributable to the predominantly elderly male Veterans cohort (mean age ~72), non-fixed ADM membrane, and a very broad dehiscence definition (≥1 mm). Both suture approaches may be considered clinically acceptable for horizontal GBR primary closure.

## Key Contributions

1. First RCT to directly compare interrupted vs interrupted+horizontal mattress sutures specifically in horizontal GBR; prior literature on mattress sutures and primary closure came predominantly from third molar extraction contexts.
2. Demonstrates that adding a horizontal mattress suture beyond the mucogingival junction does NOT significantly reduce GBR wound dehiscence width, length, or proportion.
3. Reports high absolute dehiscence rates (60%/55%) with clinical context: older Veterans cohort, non-fixed membrane, and a 1-mm minimum dehiscence definition explain much of the apparent severity.
4. Provides dehiscence metrics (DW, DL, DL/i) as quantitative benchmarks for future GBR suture trials.
5. Counter-intuitive subgroup finding: primary healing was numerically more frequent in 2-tooth-span defects than 1-tooth-span defects, warranting investigation in larger trials.

## Methodology

**Design**: Prospective, single-center, parallel-group preliminary RCT; allocation ratio 1:1; single surgeon.

**Patients**: 40 patients requiring horizontal GBR (buccal defect width 3–5 mm) either staged (prior to implant placement) or simultaneous with implant placement. Setting: Veterans Health Service Medical Center, Seoul, Korea; June 2022–August 2023. Predominantly older male Veterans (mean age 70.7±7.6 years, Group S; 74.65±6.2 years, Group S+H).

**Surgical protocol**: All patients received standardized periosteal releasing incision, xenogenic particulate bone substitute (THE Graft, Purgo Biologics), and resorbable acellular dermal matrix (ADM) membrane (Megaderm, L&C Bio) placed without fixation. Flap advancement and primary closure attempted in all cases.

**Suture groups**:
- **Group S** (n=20): Simple interrupted sutures only, 5-0 nylon.
- **Group S+H** (n=20): Horizontal mattress suture placed at/beyond the mucogingival junction first (to relieve flap tension), then simple interrupted sutures over the entire incision line, 5-0 nylon.

**Stitch removal**: 2 weeks postoperatively.

**Outcome measures** at 2 weeks:
- Primary: Wound dehiscence width (DW, mm) — greatest width of any wound opening.
- Secondary: Wound dehiscence length (DL, mm); dehiscence length/incision-line ratio (DL/i); occurrence rate of any dehiscence (defined as any gap ≥1 mm).

## Key Results

### Primary and Secondary Outcomes (all patients)

| Measure | Group S (n=20) | Group S+H (n=20) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| DW (mm) | 2.92 ± 2.73 | 2.17 ± 2.51 | 0.478 |
| DL (mm) | 5.32 ± 5.49 | 4.37 ± 4.65 | 0.718 |
| DL/i ratio | 0.42 ± 0.41 | 0.34 ± 0.38 | 0.583 |
| Dehiscence occurrence | 12/20 (60%) | 11/20 (55%) | >0.05 |

**None** of the comparisons reached statistical significance.

### Dehiscence-Positive Subgroup Only

| Measure | Group S (n=12) | Group S+H (n=11) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| DW (mm) | 4.87 | 3.95 | 0.316 |

Still no significant difference when restricting to cases where dehiscence occurred.

### Other Findings

- Primary closure (no dehiscence at 2 weeks) was achieved in 40% (Group S) and 45% (Group S+H).
- In a subgroup analysis by defect span, primary healing was numerically more common in 2-tooth-span defects than 1-tooth-span defects — an unexpected finding that may reflect greater flap advancement reserve over larger spans or patient-selection effects.
- No major postoperative complications reported in either group.

### Clinical Contextualization of High Dehiscence Rates

The 60%/55% dehiscence occurrence rates are substantially higher than some GBR literature benchmarks. Three factors largely explain this:
1. **Patient demographics**: Predominantly elderly male Veterans (mean age ~72), a population with higher comorbidities, reduced tissue vascularity, and altered healing.
2. **Dehiscence definition**: Any opening ≥1 mm was counted — far more sensitive than definitions requiring membrane exposure or clinically significant openings.
3. **Non-fixed membrane**: Megaderm ADM placed without tacks/pins; membrane micromotion may contribute to early wound disruption.

## Limitations

- **Small sample size**: n=40 (20 per group); the study is explicitly labeled "preliminary." Low power to detect moderate effect sizes.
- **Single surgeon**: No inter-surgeon variability; limits generalizability.
- **Veteran/elderly population**: Mean age ~72 years, high comorbidity burden, predominantly male; findings may not apply to general implant patients.
- **No smoking data**: Tobacco use (a major GBR healing confounder) was not controlled or reported.
- **Short follow-up**: 2-week wound healing only; no bone gain, implant survival, or longer-term outcomes reported.
- **Broad dehiscence definition**: ≥1 mm threshold inflates incidence compared to studies using membrane-exposure as the criterion.
- **No membrane fixation**: Megaderm not fixed, which may independently elevate dehiscence rates regardless of suture technique.

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| GBR (Guided Bone Regeneration) | Barrier membrane technique to exclude soft tissue from a bony defect, allowing bone regeneration |
| DW (Wound Dehiscence Width) | Maximum transverse gap of wound opening, in mm; primary outcome of this study |
| DL (Wound Dehiscence Length) | Longitudinal extent of wound opening along the incision line, in mm |
| DL/i | DL divided by total incision line length; dimensionless ratio allowing size-normalized comparison |
| Horizontal Mattress Suture (HMS) | Suture pattern placed perpendicular to the incision, spanning beyond the mucogingival junction; intended to relieve flap tension before definitive interrupted closure |
| Interrupted Suture (IS) | Single-loop suture closing one point on the incision independently of adjacent sutures; standard GBR closure stitch |
| Periosteal releasing incision | Incision through the periosteum at the base of the flap to allow coronal advancement without tension |
| ADM (Acellular Dermal Matrix) | Resorbable soft-tissue substitute derived from processed dermis; used here as GBR barrier membrane |
| Mucogingival junction (MGJ) | Boundary between attached keratinized gingiva and mobile alveolar mucosa |
