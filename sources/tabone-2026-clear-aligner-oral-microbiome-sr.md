## Why Ingested

기존 [[wiki/orthodontics/clear-aligner/alhuwaizi-2026-clear-aligner-fixed-oral-hygiene-periodontal-grade-sr]]는 CA(clear aligner) vs FA(fixed appliance)의 구강위생·치주 지표(PI, GI, BOP 등) 임상 결과를 다루지만, 그 표면적 지표를 만들어내는 **구강 미생물총(oral microbiome) 조성 변화** 자체는 다루지 않는 gap이었다. 본 SR(Tabone 2026, PRISMA, 12편 관찰연구 정성종합, PROSPERO 628072)은 CA 착용 중 alpha/beta diversity, 특정 taxa(균종) 변화를 시간대별(4시간~12개월)로 정리해 그 gap을 직접 메운다. 특히 마지막에 인용된 Wang et al. 연구(구강 미생물 기능 대사경로 분석)는 "Invisalign이 FA보다 미생물총 측면에서 우월하지 않다"는 상반된 결론을 제시해, 대다수 다른 연구의 "CA가 더 건강한 구강환경을 조성한다"는 주된 결론과 내부적으로 긴장 관계에 있다.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA, PROSPERO 628072) of 12 observational studies (4 hours–12 months follow-up) found that clear aligners (CA) generally promote more stable oral microbiota and better periodontal parameters than fixed appliances (FA), though early (4–24h) CA use induces transient alpha-diversity decreases and increased acidity, and one included study found Invisalign did not outperform FA in microbiome composition/function.

## 1. Document Information

- **Title**: Impact of Clear Aligners on the Oral Microbiome: A Systematic Review of Current Evidence
- **Authors**: Mariangela Tabone, Niccoló Cenzato, Rocio Gonzalez Soltero, Carmen Martin Carreras-Presas
- **Journal**: Clinical and Experimental Dental Research (Wiley)
- **DOI**: 10.1002/cre2.70265
- **PMID**: 41849703
- **Registration**: PROSPERO 628072
- **Reporting standard**: PRISMA

## 2. Key Contributions

- First SR to unify **microbial composition data** (16S rRNA sequencing, qPCR, CRT bacteria system) with **clinical periodontal/caries parameters** (PI, GI, BOP, PPD) across the full time spectrum of CA treatment, from 4 hours to 12 months.
- Organizes evidence around two PICO-derived research questions: (Q1) associations between specific oral microbial composition and CA treatment; (Q2) impact of CA on oral health parameters that in turn influence the microbiome.
- Synthesizes findings across short-term (4–24h), medium-term (up to 6 months), and long-term (12 months) time horizons, showing a consistent pattern of early transient dysbiosis followed by relative microbial stabilization under CA compared to FA.
- Flags one dissenting study (Wang et al., inferred metagenome analysis) that found both FA and Invisalign caused oral microbiome dysbiosis, with Invisalign not outperforming FA and showing higher predisposition to periodontal disease-associated metabolic pathways — directly countering the review's dominant narrative.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: PRISMA-guided systematic review (no meta-analysis), PROSPERO-registered (628072).
- **PICO question**: In patients undergoing CA orthodontic treatment, how does CA use affect oropharyngeal microbiota (salivary, lingual, gingival, pharyngeal composition), clinical parameters (PI, GI, BOP, PPD), and functional diversity (alpha/beta diversity, metabolic pathways) vs baseline or other appliances (FA)?
- **Databases**: Medline, Cochrane Library, Embase, Scopus (via PubMed + OvidSP at Universidad Europea de Madrid); no date restriction (inception to December 2024); search terms: ("Bacteria" OR "Microbio*") AND ("Orthodontic clear aligners" OR "invisalign").
- **Eligibility**: interventional or observational (cross-sectional, case-control, prospective/retrospective cohort) studies in English, peer-reviewed, reporting oropharyngeal microbiota composition (taxa at phylum/family/genus/species level) or functional diversity in CA patients. Excluded: books, editorials, letters, corrigenda, reviews, meta-analyses, case reports, gray literature, conference abstracts.
- **Screening**: 34 records identified (16 PubMed + 18 other databases) → 5 duplicates removed via Covidence → 29 screened → 12 included after full-text review (2 independent reviewers N.C./M.T.; conflicts resolved by third reviewer R.G.S., disagreements also by C.M.-P.).
- **Bias assessment**: Jadad scale initially considered (inapplicable — no RCTs), then AXIS (Appraisal Tool for Cross-Sectional Studies) applied to all 12 included non-randomized studies.
- **Analytic methods across included studies**: qPCR (1 study), CRT bacteria system/Ivoclar Vivadent (2 studies), 16S rRNA gene sequencing metagenomics targeting V3–V4 region (8 studies) or V1–V3/HOMING protocol (1 study).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**12 included studies**, all observational (cross-sectional/longitudinal), follow-up ranging 4 hours to 12 months, sample sizes 8–205 participants (mostly adults, one adolescent cohort n=205, one adolescent cohort n=30).

**Short-term (4–24h):**
- Zheng et al.: significant alpha/beta diversity differences between CA and FA within 4h; FA linked to increased anaerobic/periodontal pathogen abundance.
- Yan/Yang et al.: time-dependent microbial shifts on CA inner surface over 24h — alpha diversity decreased, beta diversity slightly increased; Firmicutes abundance increased; aligner liquid pH decreased (more acidic) especially after 12h of continuous wear, favoring a demineralization-prone environment.

**Medium-term (1–6 months):**
- Sifakakis et al. (1 month, adolescents): no difference in S. mutans/S. sanguinis between CA and FA; lower S. mutans levels with CA vs FA; significantly better plaque (s-PlI) and gingivitis (s-GI) scores favoring CA.
- Rouzi et al. (3 months): alpha diversity in aligner tray plaque decreased over time; PI decreased, periodontal health improved; no significant subgingival microbiota changes.
- Mummolo, Nota et al. (6 months, two studies): only ~8–10% of CA users reached high-risk S. mutans/Lactobacilli levels vs ~40% of multi-bracket (MB/FA) users (odds ratio 5.05 for S. mutans, 4.33 for Lactobacilli in MB vs CA/removable positioner); CA users maintained PI = 0 throughout, MB users had significant PI increase.
- Zhao et al. (6 months): no significant alpha-diversity change pre/post CA; PI decreased significantly; BOP/PPD unchanged; brushing frequency increased.
- Wang et al. (6 months, salivary microbiome vs FA): alpha diversity higher in FA, beta diversity higher in CA; 4 genera and 15 species significantly more enriched in FA; PI increased over 6 months in FA but remained stable in CA; PI/GI/PD associations significant in FA group only.

**Long-term (12 months):**
- Song et al. (adolescents, WSL vs non-WSL under CA): alpha diversity lower in white-spot-lesion (WSL) group; beta diversity showed separation; 14 taxa (including Actinobacteria) enriched in WSL group, 58 taxa (including Firmicutes) enriched in controls; salivary microbiome-metabolome correlation found.
- Shokeen et al. (8–56 years, longitudinal FA vs CA): beta but not alpha diversity differed between groups; PI and GI increased significantly in FA but not CA.
- Duong et al.: no alpha-diversity change between groups; beta diversity differed for both FA/CA; FA group showed increased disease-associated genus and decreased health-associated Haemophilus; CA group showed no consistent directional trend; clinical parameters worsened in FA but remained stable in CA.
- **Dissenting result** — Wang et al. (inferred metagenome/functional pathway analysis): both FA and Invisalign caused oral microbiome dysbiosis; Invisalign group showed higher predisposition to periodontal disease and distinct metabolic pathway enrichment (energy, amino acid, carbohydrate, terpenoid metabolism) vs FA (membrane transport, nucleotide metabolism) — explicitly stating Invisalign "did not perform better than fixed appliances in terms of microbiome composition and function, contradicting previous claims of its periodontal health benefits."

**Risk of bias (AXIS)**: all studies had clear aims/appropriate design; common weaknesses were inadequate sample-size justification (Sifakakis, Mummolo-Nota, Zhao) and inconsistent handling of non-responders/missing data; most lacked control groups (Duong, Mummolo-Nota #2, Wang, Yan, Shokeen, Rouzi); Zheng et al. flagged for potential conflict-of-interest bias; several studies showed sex imbalance (Zhao: 22F/3M; Zheng: female-only).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- All 12 included studies were non-randomized observational designs (cross-sectional/longitudinal) — Jadad scale (for RCTs) was not applicable, underscoring the low methodological rigor ceiling of current evidence.
- Lack of standardized methodology across studies (different sequencing regions, different bacterial panels, different time points) limits direct comparability and precluded meta-analysis.
- Most studies lacked control groups, and several had small samples (n=8–30) or sex imbalance.
- Long-term (>12 months) and intraday interval microbiome dynamics remain unexplored — the authors explicitly call for longitudinal, targeted investigations at both long-term and intraday time scales.
- Clinical implications of specific microbiome shifts (which taxa drive disease progression) remain incompletely characterized; findings on cariogenic bacteria/caries risk are described as "mixed."

## 6. Related Work

- Lucchese et al. — cited systematic reviews comparing removable and fixed orthodontic appliances' impact on the oral microbiome; reported microbial changes were mostly transient and predominantly studied in removable devices; a more recent Lucchese review directly compared FA vs CA and found CA generally associated with better microbiological/periodontal outcomes (aligning with the present review's dominant conclusion, though the present SR adds the Wang et al. dissenting data point).
- The review situates its findings within a broader oral-dysbiosis literature linking dysbiosis to systemic conditions (cardiometabolic syndrome, autoimmune disease, head/neck and GI cancer, autism spectrum behavioral associations via gut-brain axis).

## 7. Glossary

- **CA**: Clear aligner(s) — removable thermoplastic/3D-printed orthodontic appliance.
- **FA / MB**: Fixed appliance / multi-bracket appliance — conventional bonded orthodontic braces.
- **Alpha diversity**: within-sample measure of microbial richness/evenness; a proxy for ecosystem stability.
- **Beta diversity**: between-sample measure of compositional dissimilarity (e.g., Weighted-UniFrac, Bray-Curtis).
- **Dysbiosis**: imbalance in microbial community composition, typically an overgrowth of disease-associated taxa relative to health-associated taxa.
- **PI**: Plaque Index.
- **GI**: Gingival Index.
- **BOP**: Bleeding on Probing.
- **PPD/PD**: Pocket/Probing Depth.
- **WSL**: White Spot Lesion — early carious demineralization, often a caries precursor.
- **16S rRNA sequencing**: metagenomic sequencing of a conserved bacterial gene region used to profile microbial community composition.
- **AXIS**: Appraisal Tool for Cross-Sectional Studies, used for risk-of-bias assessment.
