## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (BMJ Open Quality 2024) synthesising patient safety literature specific to dentistry: defines dental-specific terms (dental adverse event, near miss, safety culture, sentinel event) and identifies reasons for dentistry's delayed recognition of patient safety (technical-proficiency focus, ambulatory/dispersed practice, private-practice profit concerns, lower perceived risk).

Seven-component institutional safety framework: strategic priority, care-delivery integration, annual training, patient safety plan, incident management system, blame-free culture, performance indicators, plus ethical framework and patient representatives — applicable to dental practices, dental schools, and hospitals.

Dentistry must be proactive given: pharmaceutical use, aggressive surgical techniques (implantology), ionising radiation and laser use, and infection-control risks; safety culture must be ingrained from dental school through clinical career.

## Key Contributions

- **Dental-specific definitions**: dental adverse event, dental medical error, dental near miss, dental safety culture, dental sentinel event — distinct from general healthcare equivalents
- **7 reasons for delayed patient safety recognition in dentistry**: (1) historical technical-proficiency focus; (2) lower perceived risk vs. medicine; (3) lack of standardised reporting; (4) professional autonomy; (5) ambulatory dispersed practices; (6) private profit concerns; (7) absence of explicit safety culture
- **7-component institutional safety framework**: strategic priority, care-delivery integration, annual training, patient safety plan, incident management system, blame-free culture, performance indicators
- **Role of dental schools**: safety culture instilled during education → dental graduates equipped from career start
- **Patient representatives in decision-making**: patient-centred perspective improves safety protocol design

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative literature review
- **Databases/Sources**: Review of research articles, guidelines, and reports on patient safety in dentistry
- **Scope**: Dental practices, dental schools, dental hospitals

## Key Results

- Patient safety gaining recognition in dentistry but challenges persist: lack of standardised reporting systems, procedural complexity
- Dental education now incorporating patient safety training; guidelines covering infection control, medication safety, anaesthesia management, patient communication
- Primary objective: promote collaboration among dental professionals, researchers, and policymakers for effective interventions

## Limitations

- Narrative review (no systematic search protocol documented)
- No quantitative data
- May reflect selective literature coverage

## Glossary

- **Dental adverse event**: unintended/harmful incident during dental care delivery resulting in patient harm
- **Dental near miss**: event that could have harmed but didn't — due to timely intervention or luck; learning opportunity
- **Dental safety culture**: collective values/attitudes/behaviours within a dental setting prioritising patient safety, open communication, continuous learning
- **Dental sentinel event**: serious, unexpected, avoidable adverse event in dental care resulting in significant harm or death
- **Blame-free culture**: environment where reporting is encouraged without fear of punishment; blame shifts focus to individual rather than system
