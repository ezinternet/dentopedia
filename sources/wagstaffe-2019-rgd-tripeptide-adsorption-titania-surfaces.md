## Three-line Summary

In vitro surface-science study (Mater Sci Eng C 2019, Univ Manchester + Diamond Light Source): XPS + STM investigation of RGD (Arg-Gly-Asp) tripeptide adsorption on (a) atomically clean single-crystal rutile TiO₂(110), pyrocatechol-capped TiO₂(110), and (b) model SLA/SLActive dental implant surfaces at pH 4/7/9.2.

RGD binds via deprotonated carboxylate groups to surface Ti atoms; SLActive showed higher RGD uptake than SLA at every pH tested; capping the clean crystal with pyrocatechol counterintuitively *increased* RGD uptake (95% higher N 1s signal) despite being more hydrophobic, because pyrocatechol is more weakly bound and more easily displaced than the airborne adventitious hydrocarbon layer that forms spontaneously on uncapped/real implant surfaces.

Cites Balajka et al. (Science 2018) that atmospheric adventitious hydrocarbon contamination on titania is mainly composed of small organic acids (e.g., formic, acetic acid) chemisorbed via carboxylate-cation bridging — NOT oil/grease — and concludes that RGD/peptide uptake is governed by how easily this adventitious carbon layer can be displaced (its solubility/binding strength), not by surface hydrophilicity per se.

## 세줄요약

In vitro 표면과학 연구(Mater Sci Eng C 2019, 맨체스터대 + Diamond Light Source): X선광전자분광법 (X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy, XPS) + 주사터널링현미경 (Scanning Tunneling Microscopy, STM)으로 RGD (Arg-Gly-Asp) 삼중펩타이드의 (a) 원자수준 청정 단결정 루타일 TiO₂(110)·피로카테콜 캡핑 TiO₂(110), (b) SLA/SLActive 모형 임플란트 표면(pH 4/7/9.2) 흡착 분석.

RGD는 탈양성자화된 카르복실기로 표면 Ti 원자에 결합; SLActive가 모든 pH에서 SLA보다 RGD 흡수량 높음; 청정 결정에 피로카테콜을 캡핑하면 더 소수성임에도 오히려 RGD 흡수가 증가(N 1s 신호 95%↑) — 피로카테콜이 실제 임플란트 표면에 자연 형성되는 부착탄화수소층보다 약하게 결합해 더 쉽게 치환되기 때문.

Balajka et al(Science 2018) 인용: 대기 중 티타니아 표면의 부착탄소오염 (Adventitious Carbon) 주성분은 기름·유분이 아니라 폼산·아세트산 같은 **소형 유기산**이며 카르복실기-양이온 브리징 결합으로 화학흡착됨 — RGD/펩타이드 흡수량은 표면 친수성 자체가 아니라 이 부착탄소층이 얼마나 쉽게 치환되는지(용해도/결합강도)로 결정된다는 결론.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **First specific chemical identification, in this wiki cluster, of what "hydrocarbon contamination" actually is**: not a generic oil/grease film, but predominantly small organic acids (formic, acetic acid) that chemisorb onto titania via deprotonated carboxylate groups bridging to under-coordinated surface cations — citing Balajka et al. Science 2018.
- Shows this adventitious carbon layer forms spontaneously from **both** air exposure and from water that has itself been exposed to air (dissolved trace organic acids) — though the air-exposed route deposits substantially more.
- Demonstrates hydrophilicity is not a reliable proxy for how "clean" (decontaminated) a surface is: a hydrophobic pyrocatechol-capped surface adsorbed *more* RGD than the more hydrophilic uncapped crystal, because the determining factor is how weakly/strongly the adventitious layer is bound (its displaceability), not the contact angle.
- SLActive (stored/etched under inert N₂ + saline) shows less adventitious hydrocarbon and correspondingly higher RGD peptide uptake than SLA (etched/stored in air) — direct surface-chemistry evidence for why SLActive accelerates early osseointegration.

## 3. Methodology

Model system: rutile TiO₂(110) single crystal, UHV-cleaned (Ar⁺ bombardment + annealing to ~740°C), studied by XPS (Al Kα, Kratos Axis Ultra) and STM (Omicron AFM-STM). Surface optionally capped with a pyrocatechol monolayer to limit adventitious hydrocarbon adsorption, then exposed to RGD solution (0.1–1 mg/mL) and analyzed via Ti 2p / O 1s / C 1s / N 1s core-level spectra (CasaXPS fitting). Model dental-implant surfaces: SLA (sandblasted + acid-etched, cleaned/stored in air) vs SLActive (same but cleaned/stored under inert N₂ in medical saline) grade-2 Ti disks, soaked 24 h in 0.1 mg/mL RGD solution at pH 4, 7, and 9.2 (phthalate/phosphate/borate buffers), then XPS-analyzed.

## 4. Key Results

- N 1s-based RGD uptake on TiO₂ vs pyrocatechol-capped TiO₂: 8.8% vs 17.2% (95% relative increase) — capping *increases* uptake despite being more hydrophobic.
- RGD binds via deprotonated carboxylate groups (O 1s stoichiometry COO⁻:O=C–N:CO(OH) = 2.0:1.0:0.2, close to the fully-deprotonated theoretical 2.0:1.0:0.0).
- RGD overlayer thickness: ~18 Å as-deposited (multilayer), ~6 Å after rinsing (chemisorbed monolayer + some retained precipitate).
- SLActive showed consistently higher RGD uptake than SLA at pH 4, 7, and 9.2 (lowest uptake for both at pH 7).
- Mechanistic explanation (citing Balajka 2018): atmospheric adventitious hydrocarbon on titania is mainly small organic acids (e.g., formic/acetic acid), strongly and orderly chemisorbed via carboxylate-cation bridging; the previously reported ~1 nm thicker adventitious layer on SLA vs SLActive (Murphy 2016) is disproportionately consequential because it is this layer's *ease of displacement*, not merely its thickness, that governs peptide/protein adsorption and (by extension) early biological response.

## 5. Limitations

Model/bench system (single-crystal TiO₂ and lab-fabricated SLA/SLActive analogs, not clinical implants); pH-dependent uptake mechanism not fully resolved (buffer cation confound — K⁺ at pH 4/9 vs Na⁺ at pH 7 — acknowledged by authors as a possible confound distinct from pH itself); C 1s/O 1s spectra on the real SLA/SLActive surfaces were too complex to deconvolute reliably (semi-quantitative N 1s/Ti 2p proxy used instead); no direct cell/osteoblast/animal readout — this is a surface-chemistry mechanism paper, not a biological-outcome study.

## 7. Glossary

Adventitious carbon: a thin organic layer that spontaneously forms on any surface exposed to ambient air/water — on titania, shown here to be mainly small organic acids (formic/acetic acid), not oil or grease; RGD: Arg-Gly-Asp tripeptide, a cell-adhesion motif studied as a proxy for protein/biological adsorption; XPS: X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy — surface-sensitive elemental/chemical-state analysis; STM: Scanning Tunneling Microscopy; SLA/SLActive: Straumann's sandblasted-acid-etched titanium surface and its hydrophilic (inert-storage) variant; pyrocatechol capping: a sacrificial monolayer used experimentally to limit adventitious hydrocarbon adsorption on a model surface.
