## Why Ingested

Extends the wiki's digital-imaging plaque-monitoring work ([[periodontics/oral-hygiene-instruction/giese-kraft-2022-digital-imaging-plaque-monitoring]]) from measurement validation to using intra-oral-camera images as a patient-education tool during oral hygiene instruction, and adds a trial-level counterpart to the digital-technology SR/MA ([[periodontics/oral-hygiene-instruction/guo-2026-digital-technologies-plaque-assessment-sr-ma]]).

## Three-line Summary

Pilot parallel-group, randomised, examiner-blind trial (n=22 adults with gingivitis, UK): standard oral hygiene advice (OHA) plus personalised intra-oral-camera (IOC) images (test) versus standard OHA alone (control), re-assessed at 4 weeks.

Plaque scores improved more in the test group than control (39.4% vs 20.6%, p<0.05); gingival and bleeding scores approached significance; baseline plaque was 0.63 (control) vs 0.61 (test) and bleeding 1.17 vs 0.96; the test group reported significantly greater confidence in adhering to their oral health plan, and IOC-derived and clinical plaque scores agreed well.

IOC-based pictorial reports may add engagement and plaque reduction to standard OHA, but this is a 22-person, 4-week pilot with only an abstract available, so the size of any gingival benefit and durability remain unestablished.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Tests adding personalised IOC images to standard chairside OHA to show patients their own plaque and areas to improve.
- Compares IOC-derived plaque scores with clinical scores (good agreement reported).
- Reports higher self-reported confidence in following the bespoke oral health plan in the IOC group.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Pilot parallel-design, randomised, examiner-blind, two-treatment study; 22 healthy adults with gingivitis.
- Baseline: questionnaires on home-care habits and attitudes; IOC image taken; plaque, gingival and bleeding scores recorded.
- Test group: standard OHA plus IOC images indicating areas for improvement; control group: standard OHA.
- Repeat questionnaires and plaque/gingival/bleeding scores at 4 weeks; plaque also scored from IOC images and compared with clinical plaque scores.
- Group sizes, indices used, randomization details and dropouts: not reported in the abstract.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Baseline plaque 0.63 (control) vs 0.61 (test); bleeding 1.17 (control) vs 0.96 (test); lifestyle habits and attitudes similar.
- At 4 weeks plaque improvement 39.4% (test) vs 20.6% (control) as reported, p<0.05 (abstract order reads 'test as compared to control').
- Gingival and bleeding scores approached significance (no values in abstract).
- No between-group difference in lifestyle habits; test group significantly more confident in adhering to the oral health plan.
- Agreement between clinical and IOC plaque scores: good (no statistic in abstract).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Pilot with 22 participants and 4-week follow-up.
- Abstract-only: gingival/bleeding effect sizes, indices, and agreement statistics not retrievable.
- Gingival and bleeding differences did not reach significance in the abstract, so the abstract's 'improved gingival health' conclusion is stronger than the reported gingival data.
- Behavioural advantage was self-reported confidence, not observed brushing behaviour.

## 7. Glossary
- **IOC**: intra-oral camera: chairside camera that photographs the patient's own teeth and plaque.
- **OHA**: oral hygiene advice, standard chairside instruction in general dental practice.
- **Pictorial report**: patient-specific image report used to explain gingival health and areas for improvement.
