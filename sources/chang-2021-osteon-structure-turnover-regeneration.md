## One-line Summary
Tissue Engineering Part B narrative review (2021) summarizing osteon hierarchical structure, Haversian canal function, lacunocanalicular network mechanotransduction, BMU-level turnover, aging effects, and bioengineering strategies for cortical bone regeneration.

## 한줄요약

## 2. Key Contributions
- Cortical bone = ~80% of skeletal mass yet disproportionately understudied vs trabecular bone in regeneration literature; review fills this gap
- Hierarchical structure of osteons mapped across six levels: collagen fibril → lamellar unit → osteon → cortical bone → organ
- Lacunocanalicular network (LCN) identified as primary mechanosensing network: osteocyte dendrites transmit fluid shear signals to BMU activation
- Cement lines (reversal lines between secondary osteons) act as crack-deflection barriers — key fracture-toughness mechanism
- Aging reduces osteon diameter, increases osteon density (secondary), and impairs LCN connectivity → decreased toughness

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
**Structural parameters:**
- Osteon (secondary): diameter 100–300 μm; Haversian canal diameter 20–100 μm
- Lamellae per osteon: 10–30; lamellar thickness 3–7 μm
- LCN: osteocyte density ~25,000/mm³; canalicular density ~100/mm²
- Cortical bone porosity: 5–10% (dominated by Haversian + Volkmann canals)

**Turnover:**
- Basic Multicellular Unit (BMU) remodeling rate: 3–4 mm/day osteoclast tunnel advance
- Each BMU cycle (activation → resorption → reversal → formation → mineralization): ~4–6 months
- Annual cortical bone turnover rate: ~2–5% in young adults; rises to ~10–15% in aged bone
- Woven bone (disorganized collagen) → parallel-fibered → lamellar = maturation sequence
- Osteon secondary formation: osteoclast cuts cylindrical tunnel → osteoblast deposits concentric lamellae → final mineralization takes 6–12 months

**Aging effects:**
- Decreasing lamellar organization and increasing microcrack density
- Decreased LCN connectivity reduces mechanosensing → reduced adaptive remodeling
- Higher proportion of hypermineralized "old osteons" with poor crack-propagation resistance

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- No new primary data; mechanistic details derived from animal and in vitro studies
- LCN connectivity data largely from murine models (limited human histology)
- Bioengineering scaffold section: most approaches achieve cortical-like density but not organized lamellar/osteon architecture
- Future: 3D bioprinting + organoid approaches to recreate Haversian canal orientation and LCN

## 7. Glossary
- **Osteon (Secondary osteon)**: cylindrical structural unit of cortical bone formed by BMU remodeling; ~150–250 μm diameter; central Haversian canal
- **Primary osteon**: thin-walled vascular canal surrounded by concentric lamellae; formed during initial cortical bone modeling (not remodeling)
- **Cement line**: highly mineralized, collagen-poor boundary demarcating secondary osteon periphery; deflects microcracks
- **Lacunocanalicular network (LCN)**: interconnected system of osteocyte lacunae + canaliculi; mechanosensing organ of bone
- **BMU (Basic Multicellular Unit)**: coupled osteoclast-osteoblast unit executing one remodeling cycle
- **Volkmann's canal**: transverse canal connecting Haversian canals and periosteum; perpendicular to long axis of bone
- **Interstitial lamellae**: remnants of old osteons between secondary osteons; oldest, most mineralized bone
