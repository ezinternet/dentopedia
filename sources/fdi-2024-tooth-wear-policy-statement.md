## Why Ingested

Ingested to give the dental-erosion category a formal guideline-level frame for non-carious tooth surface loss. It positions erosion within the wider tooth-wear umbrella and extends the aetiology framework of [[dental-erosion/kanzow-2016-etiology-pathogenesis-dental-erosion]], while its cautious stance on abfraction connects to [[nccl/dioguardi-2024-abfraction-theory-controversy-scoping-review]].

## Three-line Summary

FDI World Dental Federation policy statement (Int Dent J 2024;74(1):163-164; PubMed type: Practice Guideline) on tooth wear, defined as cumulative non-carious loss of mineralized tooth substance from erosion, attrition, abrasion and possibly abfraction.

It states that wear progression can be controlled if the aetiology is recognized (acid exposure, brushing, malocclusion/parafunction, low saliva, GERD, bulimia), and that prevalence has been reported as high as 80%.

Management is sequenced: address behaviours and underlying medical causes first, restore afterwards; the retrieved PMC text lacks the formal recommendation list and grades.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Umbrella definition of tooth wear: cumulative surface loss of mineralized tooth substance from physical or chemo-physical processes not related to caries.
- Separates mechanisms: erosion (non-bacterial acid), abrasion (objects other than teeth), attrition (tooth-to-tooth), abfraction (questioned as a separate process).
- Extrinsic (dietary, environmental, drug) vs intrinsic (gastric) acid distinction.
- Principle: aetiology recognition enables control; interdisciplinary care for GERD and eating disorders; restoration after aetiology is addressed.

## 7. Glossary

- Tooth wear (치아 마모): cumulative non-carious loss of mineralized tooth substance.
- Erosive tooth wear (dental erosion): chemical-mechanical loss of hard tissue not caused by bacteria.
- Abrasion: loss caused by objects other than teeth.
- Attrition: loss from tooth-to-tooth contact.
- Abfraction: cervical notching attributed to occlusal forces; separate existence questioned.
- Extrinsic/intrinsic acid: dietary/environmental/drug vs gastric.
