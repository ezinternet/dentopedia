## Why Ingested

기존 [[wiki/oral-medicine/khan-2023-trigeminal-neuralgia-therapeutic-approach-sr]]는 삼차신경통 (Trigeminal Neuralgia, TN)의 약물 사다리(카르바마제핀 1차)를 정리했지만 침술 (Acupuncture) 같은 비약물 대안은 다루지 않았다. 본 narrative review는 TN·구강작열감증후군 (Burning Mouth Syndrome, BMS)·측두하악장애 (Temporomandibular Disorders, TMD)·대상포진후신경통 (Postherpetic Neuralgia, PHN) 전반에 걸친 침술의 기전·임상근거를 정리해 신경병증성 구강안면통증 (Neuropathic Orofacial Pain, NOP) 관리 옵션을 확장하고, 특히 TN에서 침술이 카르바마제핀보다 우월했다는 NMA 인용은 [[wiki/oral-medicine/khan-2023-trigeminal-neuralgia-therapeutic-approach-sr]]의 약물 우선 사다리에 대한 상충 신호로 검토할 가치가 있다.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative literature review of acupuncture for neuropathic orofacial pain (NOP), covering four subtypes — trigeminal neuralgia (TN), burning mouth syndrome (BMS), temporomandibular disorders (TMD), and postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) — summarizing proposed neurochemical/anti-inflammatory mechanisms and citing primary studies (mostly Chinese-language RCTs and one large network meta-analysis for TN) per subtype.

Evidence is presented as generally favorable (reduced pain intensity, improved QoL, good safety) across all four subtypes, but the review itself flags that most primary studies are small, methodologically heterogeneous, non-standardized in acupoint/protocol selection, and have short follow-up; for TMD it explicitly notes conflicting results (some trials showing no advantage over sham).

As a narrative (non-systematic, non-meta-analytic) review with no explicit search strategy, PRISMA flow, or risk-of-bias appraisal described, its efficacy claims — especially the TN "acupuncture outperforms carbamazepine" claim sourced from a single cited network meta-analysis — should be treated as hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing until independently verified.

## 세줄요약

신경병증성 구강안면통증 (Neuropathic Orofacial Pain, NOP)에 대한 침술 (Acupuncture) narrative review — 삼차신경통 (TN)·구강작열감증후군 (BMS)·측두하악장애 (TMD)·대상포진후신경통 (PHN) 4개 아형을 다루며, 아형별 신경화학적/항염 기전과 1차 연구(주로 중국어권 RCT, TN은 대규모 네트워크 메타분석 1건 인용)를 정리.

전반적으로 통증감소·삶의 질 개선·양호한 안전성 등 우호적 근거로 서술되지만, 저자 스스로도 대부분 1차 연구가 소규모·방법론적 이질성·경혈/프로토콜 비표준화·단기 추적임을 인정하며, TMD 항목에서는 sham 대비 우월성이 없었다는 상반된 결과도 명시.

체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review)이 아닌 narrative review로 검색전략·PRISMA·비뚤림위험 평가가 기술되지 않아, 특히 TN에서 "침술이 카르바마제핀보다 우수"하다는 단일 네트워크 메타분석 인용 근거의 주장은 확정적 임상 지침이 아니라 가설 생성 수준으로 취급해야 함.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Synthesizes proposed mechanisms of acupuncture analgesia relevant to NOP: endogenous opioid release (μ/δ/κ receptors in PAG, thalamus, dorsal horn), 5-HT/norepinephrine/GABA modulation, TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB-mediated neuroinflammation suppression (↓IL-1β/TNF-α/IL-6, ↑IL-10, M2 microglial polarization), fMRI-documented normalization of ACC/insula/somatosensory cortex activity, and peripheral adenosine-A1-mediated analgesia.
- Maps acupoint selection by NOP subtype: local points (TE21/Erheliao, ST7/Xiaguan, SI18/Quanliao, ST6/Jiache, ST2/Sibai, CV24/Chengjiang) target orofacial circulation/muscle/nerve activity; distal points (LI4/Hegu, PC6/Neiguan, ST36/Zusanli, GB20/Fengchi) provide systemic neuromodulation and anxiolysis.
- Cites a network meta-analysis (Yin et al., 58 RCTs, >4000 participants) reporting combined electroacupuncture (EA) + manual acupuncture outperformed carbamazepine monotherapy for TN on both pain-intensity reduction and ≥50%-response rate, with a favorable safety profile (no serious adverse events reported).
- Documents mixed/conflicting evidence for TMD specifically (Wu et al., Laurence et al.: limited evidence of superiority over sham), which the review attributes to methodological heterogeneity across trials.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Narrative literature review — no stated systematic search strategy, database list, inclusion/exclusion criteria, PRISMA flow diagram, or formal risk-of-bias/quality appraisal (e.g., GRADE, Cochrane RoB) for the primary studies cited.
- **Scope**: Four NOP subtypes — BMS, TN, TMD, PHN — each covered with (a) pathogenesis/symptom summary (consolidated in the paper's Table 1), (b) proposed acupuncture mechanism specific to the subtype, (c) subtype-specific acupoints, and (d) a narrative summary of representative primary studies (mostly individual RCTs/comparative trials, one large NMA for TN).
- **Primary evidence base**: A mix of individual RCTs, double-blind trials (e.g., Heckmann et al. on systemic clonazepam for BMS; Gremeau-Richard et al. on topical clonazepam for BMS), multicenter studies (Edwards et al., Hu et al. for TN), and one network meta-analysis (Yin et al., TN) — sourced largely from the acupuncture/TCM and Chinese-language rehabilitation-medicine literature; original studies not independently re-appraised by the current authors.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **TN**: Yin et al. NMA (58 RCTs, n>4000) — combined EA + manual acupuncture > carbamazepine monotherapy on VAS pain reduction and ≥50%-response rate, advantage most evident vs. sham-acupuncture controls; Gao et al. reported concurrent cognitive/QoL improvement; Edwards et al. and Hu et al. (multicenter) confirmed consistency across EA, manual needling, fire acupuncture; few adverse events reported, none serious (Yin et al.).
- **BMS**: Acupuncture at SI1/TE1/LI4/TW21/ST5/ST6/CV24 proposed to improve oral microcirculation and vascular homeostasis (Scardina et al.); Franco et al. — acupuncture + auricular therapy addresses physical and emotional (anxiety/depression) domains. Comparator drug evidence (clonazepam, topical and systemic) also cited as effective in separate RCTs, not head-to-head against acupuncture.
- **TMD**: Liu et al. and Sung et al. (EA + ultrashort-wave) reported significant pain and jaw-mobility improvement persisting weeks post-treatment; mechanisms include β-endorphin release, ↓TNF-α/IL-6, HPA-axis modulation (↓cortisol; Jung et al.). However, Wu et al. and Laurence et al. found limited/mixed evidence of superiority over sham — explicitly flagged by the review as a methodological-heterogeneity problem, not a settled result.
- **PHN**: Pei et al. — low-frequency EA improved pain and sleep quality; Wang et al. — fire needling superior to other acupuncture techniques; Ashi-point bloodletting-cupping gave substantial relief with slightly increased mild adverse events (dizziness, bleeding).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **This is a narrative review, not a systematic review or meta-analysis** — no described search strategy, no PRISMA flow, no formal quality/risk-of-bias appraisal of the underlying primary studies. Evidence-ladder position is low; findings should be read as a mechanism-and-hypothesis map, not a pooled effect estimate.
- The authors themselves state the underlying evidence is "limited due to inadequate methodology, small sample sizes and lack of standardization in acupuncture protocols," with inconsistent acupoint selection, treatment frequency, session duration and study design across primary studies, short follow-up periods, and inadequately controlled placebo/sham effects.
- No standardized acupuncture protocols or clinical guidelines currently exist for NOP; the TN "outperforms carbamazepine" claim rests on a single cited NMA (Yin et al.) that this review does not independently critique for its own methodological quality.
- Long-term efficacy/safety data are lacking across all four subtypes; research combining acupuncture with other non-invasive/pharmacological therapies is minimal.
- Future work priorities named by the authors: standardized acupoint/protocol guidelines, longer follow-up, mechanistic imaging studies (fMRI) of central sensitization/neuroinflammation/plasticity, and comparative trials against established pharmacological and non-pharmacological treatments.

## 7. Glossary

- **NOP (Neuropathic Orofacial Pain)**: chronic pain syndromes in the oral/facial/cranial/cervical region arising from peripheral or central nervous system dysfunction or injury.
- **TN (Trigeminal Neuralgia)**: sudden, severe, electric-shock-like unilateral facial pain along one or more trigeminal nerve branches, typically triggered by light stimuli.
- **BMS (Burning Mouth Syndrome)**: chronic burning/dysesthetic oral mucosal sensation without detectable mucosal lesions, more common in postmenopausal women.
- **TMD (Temporomandibular Disorders)**: conditions affecting the temporomandibular joint (TMJ), masticatory muscles, and associated structures.
- **PHN (Postherpetic Neuralgia)**: chronic neuropathic pain persisting after resolution of a herpes zoster (shingles) infection, along the affected dermatome.
- **EA (Electroacupuncture)**: acupuncture technique applying a mild electric current through inserted needles for enhanced stimulation.
- **Acupoint**: a designated anatomical point (e.g., LI4/Hegu, ST7/Xiaguan) targeted by acupuncture needling per Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) meridian theory.
- **NMA (Network Meta-Analysis)**: a meta-analytic method comparing multiple interventions simultaneously (here used to compare acupuncture modalities against carbamazepine for TN).
