## Why Ingested

동적 하악운동 추적 (Dynamic Jaw Motion Tracking, DJMT) 방법으로 제작된 지르코니아 크라운이 기존 방법 대비 편심위 교합 간섭 조정량을 유의하게 감소시킨다는 임상 RCT 근거를 제공한다. IOS와 CAD/CAM 디지털 워크플로우에서 하악운동 기록이 보철 적합도에 미치는 임상적 가치를 정량화한 최초 수준의 임상 연구다.

## Three-line Summary

Dynamic jaw motion tracking (DJMT) integrated into CAD software significantly reduces intraoral occlusal adjustment for zirconia crowns compared to conventional fabrication. In 15 patients (30 crowns), mean RMS adjustment was 67.5 µm (DJMT) vs 91.8 µm (conventional), with P = .028. The finding supports wider clinical adoption of DJMT for single-crown restorations to improve prosthetic efficiency.

## 세줄요약

동적 하악운동 추적 (Dynamic Jaw Motion Tracking, DJMT)을 CAD 소프트웨어에 통합하면 기존 방법 대비 지르코니아 크라운의 구내 교합 조정량이 유의하게 감소한다. 15명 환자 (30개 크라운)에서 평균 RMS 조정값은 DJMT 67.5 µm 대 기존 방법 91.8 µm (P = .028)였다. 이 결과는 단일 크라운 수복에서 DJMT의 임상 활용 확대를 지지한다.

## Key Contributions

1. First quantitative clinical study comparing DJMT-designed vs conventional single-crown restorations using RMS 3D inspection analysis.
2. Demonstrates statistically significant reduction in eccentric interference adjustment: 67.5 µm vs 91.8 µm (26.5% reduction, P = .028).
3. No significant difference between anterior and posterior crown adjustment amounts (P = .450), suggesting DJMT is effective across locations.
4. Double-blind design with blind-coded crowns eliminates clinician bias in adjustment decisions.
5. Provides color deviation map methodology (Geomagic Control X) as a replicable outcome measure for future studies.

## Methodology

**Design**: Double-blind, within-subject randomized clinical trial. Each patient received two crowns: one designed with DJMT (Zebris for Ceramill, Amann Girrbach) and one conventionally designed, both fabricated from the same zirconia blocks (CeramillZolid HT).

**DJMT workflow**:
- Optic facebow (Zebris) with LED lower jaw sensor captures maxillary position and mandibular movements (left lateral, right lateral, protrusive, opening — 3× each)
- Data exported and imported to exocad CAD software
- Digital facebow transfer performed; dynamic occlusal simulation modifies crown occlusal surface

**Conventional workflow**:
- Rubber impression → plaster cast → desktop scanner
- Crown design from CAD library with technician manual adjustment for occlusion

**Outcome measurement**:
- Intraoral scanner (MEDIT i700) used pre- and post-adjustment
- 3D inspection (Geomagic Control X): RMS deviation of adjusted area vs pre-adjustment scan
- Posterior teeth: segmentation 1 mm below occlusal-axial line angle
- Anterior teeth: entire palatal side + incisal line angle
- Statistical: Shapiro-Wilk normality, paired t-test (α = 0.05, SPSS 27.0)

## Key Results

| Group | Mean RMS adjustment (µm) | SD / range |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional | 91.8 | (reported; exact SD not extracted) |
| DJMT | 67.5 | (reported; exact SD not extracted) |
| P-value | .028 | Statistically significant |

- Anterior vs posterior region: P = .450 (NS) — no location effect
- Color deviation maps consistently showed greater deviations for conventional crowns
- Null hypothesis rejected: DJMT significantly reduces occlusal adjustment

## Limitations

1. Small sample (n = 15); single-center, single-technician — limits generalizability
2. Para-occlusal attachment secured with pattern resin may allow minor movement during recording
3. Some patients may not have fully relaxed during tracking, introducing recording errors
4. Study measures total pre-to-post-adjustment RMS, not specifically post-MIP adjustment isolated from post-eccentric adjustment — cannot definitively separate lateral interference from MIP adjustments
5. High cost and time requirement of DJMT may limit adoption for single crowns
6. No assessment of masticatory efficiency, patient satisfaction, or long-term outcomes
7. No true control group recording actual mandibular motion trajectories for three-way comparison

## Related Work

- Peng et al. found no significant difference in occlusal adjustment for digital articulators and DJMT for occlusal devices — contrast with this study's single-crown finding
- Digital facebow alternatives: CBCT-based, facial scan, cephalometric; studies show discrepancies vs conventional facebow transfer
- In vitro studies on DJMT-fabricated prostheses documented (refs 20, 25, 26 in original paper)
- Posterior restoration studies using optical mandibular motion tracking documented (ref 24)

## Glossary

- **DJMT**: Dynamic Jaw Motion Tracking — optical recording of mandibular movement using LED sensor and optic facebow
- **RMS**: Root Mean Square deviation — scalar measure of 3D surface difference between pre- and post-adjustment scans
- **Bennett angle**: Lateral condylar path angle during lateral mandibular movement
- **Sagittal condylar angle**: Condylar guidance angle in sagittal plane
- **MIP (MICP)**: Maximum Intercuspal Position — tooth-to-tooth occlusion at maximal intercuspation
- **Para-occlusal attachment**: Resin attachment fixed to mandibular anterior teeth to hold lower jaw sensor
- **Geomagic Control X**: 3D inspection software for computing surface deviations (3D Systems)
- **exocad**: CAD software platform for dental prosthesis design
- **Zebris for Ceramill**: DJMT system (Amann Girrbach) using optic facebow and LED sensors
