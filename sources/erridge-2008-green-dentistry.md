## Why Ingested

The earliest sustainability-focused item in the practice-management collection: a 2008 British Dental Journal readers' letter questioning whether dentistry has ever been audited for environmental impact. Documents the profession's environmental-responsibility debate at a time when dental amalgam was already declining — a useful historical contrast point for any modern green-dentistry/amalgam-phase-down discussion. Included with the editor's note that the BDJ itself was printed on certified sustainable paper.

## Three-line Summary

Letters-to-the-Editor piece (Br Dent J 2008, DOI 10.1038/sj.bdj.2008.703) by P. Erridge (East Grinstead) prompted by press criticism of dentistry's mercury use, asking whether environmental audits of dentistry have been carried out.

The letter argues dental mercury is a minor pollution source relative to volcanic emissions and that amalgam use had already declined over the previous two decades; it calls for wide-ranging audits (manufacturing → clinic waste containers, clinic front door → extracted air), and asks whether treatment plans, training, and journal publishing (recycled paper, online-only) should be guided by environmental concerns, and whether "being green" is cost-effective.

Editor's note responds that the BDJ is printed on paper from well-managed sustainable sources with internationally recognised certification, referencing the first carbon-neutral dental practice featured in Vital.

## 세줄요약

영국치과저널(BDJ) 독자편지 (2008, DOI 10.1038/sj.bdj.2008.703): 언론이 치과의 수은 사용을 비판한 것에 반박하며 치과의 환경 감사(environmental audit)가 이루어진 적이 있는지 질문.

치과 수은은 화산 배출 대비 세계 오염의 작은 원천이고 지난 20년간 아말감 사용은 더 줄었다고 주장; 제조 공정부터 진료실 폐기물 용기·추출 공기까지 폭넓은 감사, 환경 고려한 치료 계획·교육과정 포함 여부, '그린'이 비용효과적인지, BDJ의 재생지 인쇄·온라인 전환 여부를 제안.

편집자 주: BDJ는 국제 인증을 받은 지속가능 경영 산림지(pulp) 종이에 인쇄되며, Vital 여름호에서 최초의 탄소중립 치과 진료소를 다뤘음.
