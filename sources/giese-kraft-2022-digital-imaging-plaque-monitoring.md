## Why Ingested

Wen et al. 2026 (RCT on dental floss sequence and plaque removal efficacy) quantified plaque with a manual index (RMNPI) applied chairside — a scoring approach whose only record is the number assigned at the visit. This paper offers the digital-imaging alternative to that same index: it validates whether RMNPI-style plaque scoring can instead be captured on permanent 2D intraoral-camera and 3D intraoral-scanner images, which matters for any future re-analysis or reproducibility check of manual-index RCTs like Wen 2026. It also directly extends the methodology used in [[interdental-cleaning/jung-2025-flossing-performance-plaque-removal]] (same Giessen group, overlapping authors Jung K / Ganss C), which scores plaque on aligned intraoral scans (PSPI) — Giese-Kraft 2022 is the foundational validation study showing that intraoral-scan-based plaque scoring agrees substantially-to-almost-perfectly with chairside clinical examination.

## Three-line Summary

Prospective in vivo study (n=20 healthy adults, Germany) comparing clinical RMNPI plaque scoring against plaque scores derived from 2D intraoral-camera images and 3D intraoral-scanner images, at habitual plaque, after 72h no oral hygiene, and after habitual brushing.

Both 2D and 3D image-based plaque scores tracked the same significant within-subject changes as the clinical reference (each p<0.0001), with substantial-to-almost-perfect Kappa agreement overall (2D agreed slightly better than 3D, especially on oral/lingual surfaces where scanner-head geometry limited visualization).

Intraoral 2D images give more precise plaque scoring but are slow to acquire per tooth; intraoral scanners image the whole arch quickly with easy multi-angle review at some cost to image sharpness — both are reliable enough for research and clinical plaque documentation, but the study is limited to young subjects with minimal restorations/recession.

## 세줄요약

전향적 생체내 연구(Prospective in vivo study, n=20명 건강 성인, 독일): 임상 러스토기 변형 네이비 치태지수(Rustogi Modification of Navy Plaque Index, RMNPI)와 2D 구강내 카메라 영상·3D 구강스캐너(Intraoral Scanner) 영상 기반 치태 점수를 습관적 치태, 72시간 구강위생 중단 후, 습관적 칫솔질 후 세 시점에서 비교.

2D·3D 영상 기반 치태 점수 모두 임상 기준과 동일한 유의한 시점 간 변화를 보였고(각 p<0.0001), 전체적으로 substantial~almost perfect 수준의 카파(Kappa) 일치도를 보였다(2D가 3D보다 약간 우수, 특히 스캐너 헤드 형태 제약이 있는 구개/설측면에서 3D 일치도 저하).

2D 구강내 영상은 더 정밀한 치태 판정이 가능하지만 치아당 촬영 시간이 오래 걸리고, 구강스캐너는 전체 치열을 빠르게 스캔하고 다각도 검토가 쉬운 대신 영상 선명도가 다소 떨어진다 — 두 방법 모두 연구·임상 치태 문서화에 신뢰할 만하나, 젊고 수복물/치은퇴축이 적은 대상자로 제한된 연구.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First systematic comparison of a complex 9-area plaque index (RMNPI) scored simultaneously by clinical examination, 2D intraoral-camera images, and 3D intraoral-scanner images, across three plaque-level conditions (habitual, 72h accumulation, post-brushing).
- Demonstrates that both 2D and 3D digital images can substitute for chairside-only clinical index scoring while additionally providing a permanent, re-analyzable record.
- Identifies a specific failure mode of 3D scans: lingual/oral surfaces (limited by scanner-head size and anatomy) show lower agreement with the clinical reference than vestibular surfaces, and 3D images are more pixelated/blurred than 2D in proximal areas.
- Quantifies systematic bias via Bland-Altman analysis: image-based scores run slightly higher than clinical scores (attributable to magnification, uniform illumination, absence of patient-related confounders).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Prospective in vivo (non-randomized, single-arm, repeated-measures) study, GCP/Declaration-of-Helsinki compliant.
- **Subjects**: n=20 (16F/4M), mean age 27.5±1.2 y, convenience sample; inclusion required full dentition to 2nd molar, no extensive fillings/prosthetics on facial/lingual surfaces of study teeth, good general health; exclusion for caries, periodontitis treatment, fixed orthodontic appliances, recessions >1/3 root length, etc.
- **Time points**: T1 baseline (habitual plaque) → T2 after 72h no oral hygiene → T3 after habitual toothbrushing (subject's own manual or powered brush/paste).
- **Plaque disclosure**: Mira-2-Ton solution, applied twice via saturated foam pellet, water rinse.
- **Index**: RMNPI (Rustogi Modification of the Navy Plaque Index) on Ramfjord teeth (16, 21, 24, 36, 41, 44), vestibular + oral surfaces, 9 areas/surface scored dichotomously (0/1, any plaque = positive); reported as % of positive areas.
- **Imaging devices**: 2D — Carestream CS 1500 intraoral camera (4 images/tooth: mesial-lateral and distal-lateral of both facial and oral surfaces, lip retractor + air blower). 3D — Carestream CS 3600 intraoral scanner (whole-dentition scan, rescanned until arch fully captured, dried with air/suction).
- **Image scoring**: CS Mesh Viewer, standardized Full-HD 15.6" monitor (8-bit, RGB SDR, 120Hz, 50% brightness, 125% scaling); 2 examiners (K.G-K, K.J), scored clinical exam / 2D / 3D independently and sequentially (not randomized/blinded to modality order, due to workflow constraints), with pre- and post-study calibration.
- **Reliability**: Cohen's Kappa for inter- and intra-examiner agreement, computed per index area, per time point (ranges: inter-examiner CE 0.853–0.957; 2D 0.742–0.912; 3D 0.720–0.854).
- **Statistics**: Stata 16.1 / SPSS 25; linear mixed models for within- and between-method comparisons (Scheffé correction); Bland-Altman plots + regression for proportional bias + one-sample t-tests for systematic bias vs. clinical reference; Kappa per RMNPI area (Landis & Koch strength-of-agreement scale).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **RMNPI % positive areas (mean±SD), all surfaces**: T1 CE 62.1(10.6) / 2D 65.1(10.0) / 3D 64.4(10.6); T2 CE 76.9(8.0) / 2D 77.9(8.6) / 3D 77.5(9.4); T3 CE 56.3(11.1) / 2D 58.2(12.1) / 3D 61.2(10.8).
- All three methods showed significant increase T1→T2 and decrease T2→T3 (each p<0.0001); post-brushing (T3) was slightly below baseline on clinical exam (p<0.01) and 2D (p=0.001), but not significantly different from baseline on 3D (p=0.125).
- Vestibular surfaces showed larger plaque-level swings than oral surfaces across all three methods; the numerically largest increase after 72h abstention was on the upper first molar vestibular surface.
- **Bland-Altman bias vs. clinical exam**: oral surfaces −0.5±2.1 (2D, n.s.) / −2.6±4.9 (3D, p<0.001); vestibular surfaces −1.5±2.2 (2D, p<0.001) / −1.5±2.4 (3D, p<0.001). A small but significant proportional bias existed only for 3D vestibular scores (overestimate at low plaque, underestimate at high plaque; r=0.069, p<0.05).
- **Kappa agreement (overall, all time points/areas pooled)**: 2D vs clinical 0.681–0.909 depending on area (best 0.909, area E); 3D vs clinical lowest 0.269 (oral area D, "fair") up to 0.864 (area E); 2D vs 3D 0.634–0.919. 2D agreed with clinical exam slightly better than 3D overall, especially on oral/lingual surfaces where scanner-head geometry limits visualization.
- Inter- and intra-examiner Kappa throughout the study remained in the 0.72–0.97 range (substantial to almost perfect), supporting the calibration protocol.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Young, healthy, minimally restored convenience sample (mean age 27.5y) — generalizability to older patients, crowns/metallic restorations, and periodontal patients with recessions/wide embrasures (which the authors flag as a likely 3D-scanning limitation) is untested.
- RMNPI is binary per area (any plaque = positive) and does not grade plaque quantity within an area, limiting discriminative sensitivity for both clinical and image-based scoring — a ceiling/floor effect likely compresses observed differences.
- Modality-scoring order was not randomized/blinded between 2D and 3D due to workflow/time constraints, a potential source of residual examiner bias.
- Single-index study (RMNPI only); authors call for replication with other plaque indices.
- 3D scan quality (pixelation, blurring, inter-tooth demarcation) is a hardware/software-generation-specific limitation the authors expect to improve as intraoral scanners advance.

## 6. Related Work

- Builds on prior 2D fluorescence/planimetric extraoral-camera plaque-imaging literature and earlier digital-camera-vs-intraoral-camera plaque-imaging comparisons, extending them to modern intraoral-scanner (3D) imaging.
- Directly antecedent, methodologically, to [[interdental-cleaning/jung-2025-flossing-performance-plaque-removal]], which uses aligned intraoral-scan plaque scoring (PSPI, Carestream CS 3800) as a core outcome measure — the same Giessen group (Jung K, Ganss C) later relies on the digital-scan plaque-scoring validity this paper establishes.

## 7. Glossary

- **RMNPI (Rustogi Modification of the Navy Plaque Index)**: A 9-area-per-tooth-surface dichotomous (0/1) plaque index; scored on Ramfjord teeth here.
- **Ramfjord teeth**: A standardized partial-mouth set of six index teeth (16, 21, 24, 36, 41, 44) used to represent whole-dentition plaque/periodontal status.
- **Bland-Altman analysis**: A statistical method for assessing agreement between two measurement methods by plotting the difference against the mean, used here to compare 2D/3D image scores against the clinical reference.
- **Kappa (κ) statistic**: Chance-corrected agreement measure; interpreted via the Landis & Koch scale (e.g., 0.61–0.80 "substantial", 0.81–1.00 "almost perfect").
- **Intraoral scanner (3D)**: A digital device (here, Carestream CS 3600) that captures a color 3D surface mesh of the dentition, enabling multi-angle post-hoc review.
- **Intraoral camera (2D)**: A handheld digital camera (here, Carestream CS 1500) for close-up, well-illuminated 2D images of individual tooth surfaces.
