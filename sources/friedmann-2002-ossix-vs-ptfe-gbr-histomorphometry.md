## Three-line Summary

Randomized controlled trial (Charité/Humboldt University Berlin, Clin Oral Implants Res 13(6):587-594, 2002) comparing **Ossix** (original ribose-crosslinked collagen membrane, test) vs **e-PTFE** (Gore-Tex, control) with deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM) for jaw bone augmentation in 28 patients, with histomorphometric biopsy analysis at 7 months.

**Total mineralized bone area was 42 ± 18% (Ossix) vs 39 ± 15% (e-PTFE)**, unmineralized tissue 44% vs 46%, DBBM remnants 14% vs 15% — all differences **non-significant** (Mann-Whitney test), establishing Ossix as histomorphometrically equivalent to the gold-standard non-resorbable membrane.

Barrier exposure events occurred in both groups but did **not** interfere with histological outcomes in either — confirming that Ossix can withstand exposure without losing regenerative efficacy, a key advantage over conventional resorbable membranes.

## 세줄요약

RCT (베를린 샤리테, COIR 2002) — Ossix (초기 ribose 가교, 현 Ossix Plus 전신) + DBBM vs e-PTFE + DBBM, 28명, 7개월 생검 조직형태계측.

총 석회화골 42% vs 39% (**유의차 없음**), DBBM 잔존 14% vs 15% (NS) — 흡수성 콜라겐 멤브레인이 비흡수성 골드스탠다드(e-PTFE)와 동등한 골재생 달성.

노출 발생해도 조직학적 결과 무영향 (양군 공통) — 노출에 강한 GLYMATRIX 가교의 임상적 이점.

## 3. Clinical Interpretation

- **Ossix = e-PTFE in bone quality**: Ossix achieved histomorphometrically identical regeneration to the gold standard non-resorbable membrane at 7 months
- **DBBM remnants ~14-15%** in both groups: Deproteinized bovine bone mineral persists at this timepoint regardless of membrane type — not a GLYMATRIX-specific finding
- **Landmark significance**: This 2002 RCT was the first peer-reviewed evidence that a resorbable collagen membrane (cross-linked) could match e-PTFE outcomes — paving the way for clinical adoption of Ossix Plus, Ossix Volumax, and OSSIX Bone
- **Bernimoulin/Pitaru collaboration**: Pitaru (Hebrew University, Datum Dental co-founder) co-authored — explains the platform development trajectory from 2002 to current GLYMATRIX products

## 4. Limitations

- Small sample (n=28; biopsies: 14 vs 13)
- 7-month endpoint only; survival rates not reported
- Ossix = original formulation (thinner, different handling vs Ossix Plus)
- Abstract-based ingest (COIR not OA in 2002)
