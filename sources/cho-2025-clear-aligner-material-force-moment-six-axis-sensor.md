## Why Ingested

기존 [[wiki/orthodontics/clear-aligner/cao-2025-clear-aligner-biomechanics-finite-element-analysis-sr]]는 29편의 FEA(유한요소해석) 시뮬레이션을 종합해 CA(clear aligner) biomechanics의 응력분포 이론을 제시하지만 전부 시뮬레이션 기반이다. 본 논문(Cho 2025)은 6축 기계 센서(six-axis mechanical sensor)와 치근·치주인대(PDL) 공간을 갖춘 3D프린팅 typodont를 이용해 실제로 힘·모멘트를 물리적으로 측정한 벤치 실험으로, FEA 예측을 실측 데이터로 검증·대비할 수 있는 근거를 제공한다. 또한 [[wiki/orthodontics/clear-aligner/delgado-2025-thermoplastics-clear-aligners-review]]가 PETG 계열 열가소성 재료(Zendura, Trioclear 상당)와 direct-3D-print 레진(Graphy TA-28 상당)의 재료과학적 특성을 서술적으로 정리한 반면, 본 논문은 이 두 재료군을 동일 실험 조건에서 직접 비교한 정량적 force/moment 수치를 제공해 재료선택 리뷰에 임상 수치를 보강한다.

## Three-line Summary

In-vitro six-axis mechanical sensor bench study (6 groups, n=6/group; 3D-printed typodont with PDL simulation, 37°C water bath) comparing three clear-aligner materials — Zendura and Trioclear thermoplastics vs Graphy TA-28 direct-3D-printed resin — at 0.3 mm and 0.6 mm labial tipping of a maxillary lateral incisor.

At 0.3 mm no significant inter-material differences were found; at 0.6 mm Zendura produced greater labial force than Trioclear (p<0.01), while Graphy paradoxically produced lingual (reverse-direction) force on the labially-tipped tooth; Zendura and Trioclear generated significantly greater labial-inclination moment than Graphy (p<0.01), with Graphy showing the lowest absolute force/moment values overall.

Clinicians should select clear-aligner material based on the specific biomechanical requirement, as Graphy TA-28's shape-memory/thermal effects can produce unexpected reverse-force directions; absence of attachments and short 2-minute observation window limit extrapolation to clinical CAT.

## 세줄요약

6축 기계 센서를 이용한 in-vitro 벤치 실험(6그룹, n=6/그룹; PDL 공간 포함 3D 프린팅 typodont, 37°C 수조): Zendura·Trioclear 열가소성 vs Graphy TA-28 직접출력형 투명교정장치 재료를 상악 측절치 0.3 mm 및 0.6 mm 순측경사(labial tipping) 조건에서 비교.

0.3 mm에서는 재료 간 유의차 없었으나, 0.6 mm에서 Zendura가 Trioclear보다 큰 순측 힘(p<0.01), Graphy는 역방향(설측) 힘 발생; Zendura·Trioclear가 Graphy보다 순측경사 모멘트 유의하게 큼(p<0.01); Graphy가 전체적으로 가장 낮은 force/moment 값.

임상적으로 투명교정 재료는 적용 biomechanical 목적에 따라 선택해야 하며, Graphy TA-28의 형상기억/열 효과로 예상치 못한 역방향 힘이 발생할 수 있음; 부착물 없음·2분 관찰 창 한계로 임상 적용 시 외삽 주의.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First (per authors) six-axis mechanical sensor setup that simultaneously addresses six prior methodological gaps: sensor too close to teeth (no water-bath immersion), sensor long-axis misaligned with tooth long-axis, no proximal contact with adjacent teeth, no root/PDL simulation, no wet/37°C intraoral simulation, and single-movement-magnitude-only measurement.
- Directly compares two conventional thermoformed thermoplastics (Zendura — single-layer sheet; Trioclear — three-layer sheet) against a direct-3D-printed resin (Graphy TA-28) under identical labial-tipping protocols.
- Measures both the target tooth (#12, maxillary right lateral incisor) and the adjacent anchor tooth (#11, central incisor), capturing reciprocal/reactive force-moment patterns, not just the moved tooth.
- Documents an unexpected, clinically relevant reverse-direction (lingual) force generated by the 3D-printed material despite a labially-tipped target position, attributed to shape-memory/spring-back and thermal-softening artifacts of the fabrication/insertion process.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- A 3D virtual maxillary typodont was built from CT data with crown, root, and a 0.5 mm PDL space; the PDL space was filled with a custom soft resin (two polymers + trimethylolpropane triacrylate) to emulate physiologic tooth mobility. The model was 3D-printed at 100 µm layer thickness.
- Labial tipping of the maxillary right lateral incisor (#12) was digitally simulated (Meshmixer) at two magnitudes: 0.3 mm and 0.6 mm. No attachments were used, to isolate the aligner-material effect.
- Six experimental groups (2 tipping magnitudes × 3 materials: Zendura, Trioclear, Graphy TA-28), n=6 aligners per group; sample size determined by power analysis referencing McKay et al. Baseline (no-movement) aligners were also fabricated for each material.
- A metal rod (1.96 mm) was embedded in the roots of #11 and #12, connecting to a six-axis mechanical sensor; forces (Fx, Fy, Fz) were calculated at incisal-edge midpoints and moments (Mx, My, Mz) at the estimated center of resistance (root apex).
- Testing simulated the intraoral environment: thermoplastic aligners were seated and the typodont immersed in a 37°C water bath; the 3D-printed aligner was pre-softened in a 60°C bath for 5 seconds before seating, then also placed in the 37°C bath. Data were acquired for 2 minutes, with the mean of the final 10 seconds used for analysis.
- Reliability: same operator (KCC) repeated measurements twice, 2 weeks apart; Dahlberg error was 88.79 mN (force, range 0.01–673.10 mN) and 0.40 N·mm (moment, range 0.00007–2.94 N·mm).
- Statistics: descriptive statistics, Mann-Whitney U test, and Kruskal-Wallis test with DSCF (Dwass-Steel-Critchlow-Fligner) pairwise comparison, SPSS v12.0; α = 0.05.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- **0.3 mm vs 0.6 mm tipping, within-material comparisons:**
  - Zendura: #12 showed a significant increase in labial-inclination moment at 0.6 mm vs 0.3 mm (p<0.05); labial/mesial/occlusal forces on #12 were stable. #11 showed significant increases in lingual and occlusal forces (p<0.01) and reduced distal-in rotation moment (p<0.01), with an angulation-moment shift from mesial to distal.
  - Trioclear: labial force on #12 significantly decreased with more tipping (p<0.05) while occlusal force increased (p<0.01); labial-inclination moment increased (p<0.01) with decreased distal-in rotation moment. #11 showed significant increases in lingual, distal, and occlusal forces and in distal-angulation/mesial-in-rotation moments (all p<0.01).
  - Graphy: no significant change in labial-inclination moment or distal-direction forces on #12, but a significant shift from occlusal to gingival force at 0.6 mm (p<0.01). #11 forces were largely unchanged.
- **Cross-material comparison at 0.3 mm tipping (#12):** no significant labio-lingual/mesio-distal force differences among the three materials; Zendura produced the greatest occlusal force (p<0.001); Trioclear showed higher distal-in rotation moment than Graphy (p<0.01); Graphy generated mesial angulation moment while Zendura/Trioclear produced distal angulation moment (p<0.01).
- **Cross-material comparison at 0.6 mm tipping (#12) — the key clinically distinguishing result:** Zendura produced the greatest occlusal force (p<0.001) and significantly higher labial force than the other two materials (p<0.01); Graphy showed particularly low labial/lingual force values. Zendura and Trioclear produced significantly stronger labial-inclination moment than Graphy (p<0.01); Graphy showed almost no angulation moment while Zendura and Trioclear produced distal angulation moment (p<0.001).
- Only Trioclear produced the theoretically "expected" lingual-inclination reactive moment on the anchor tooth (#11); Zendura and Graphy instead produced labial-inclination moments on #11 — an unpredictable/counter-intuitive biomechanical pattern.
- Graphy TA-28 generated paradoxical lingual (reverse-direction) forces on the labially-tipped target tooth at both 0.3 mm and 0.6 mm, hypothesized to result from (1) thermal softening (60°C bath) plus shape-memory recovery toward a distorted intermediate shape, (2) incomplete spring-back expression within the 2-minute measurement window, and (3) residual insertion stress.
- Absolute force/moment magnitudes in this study were smaller than in prior aligner biomechanics studies, attributed to root/PDL force absorption, adjacent-tooth (#11/#13) contact constraining independent movement, absence of attachments, and Z-axis sensor-rod effects at the root apex (no PDL cushioning there).
- Conclusion: clinicians should select aligner material based on the specific biomechanical requirement of the case, since materials can produce unexpected force/moment directions and magnitudes (notably Graphy's reverse-direction force and generally lower absolute values).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- No simulated saliva, chewing forces, composite attachments, or interproximal reduction — all common in actual clinical CAT (clear aligner therapy) and likely to modify force transmission.
- Short (2-minute) observation window does not capture long-term material behavior, including fatigue and the full expression of Graphy TA-28's shape-memory effect.
- Only labial tipping was tested; rotations and translations, and other tooth positions, remain unexamined.
- Authors note between-tooth variability (#12 lateral incisor vs #11 central incisor fit/size differences) may partly explain non-significant #12 force findings at some comparisons — a fit-related confound rather than a pure material effect.

## 6. Related Work

- Cites McKay et al. for baseline force/moment means/SDs used in the power analysis.
- Frames its methodological advance against prior six-axis-sensor aligner biomechanics studies criticized for lacking water-bath immersion, tooth-axis sensor alignment, proximal contacts, root/PDL simulation, intraoral thermal/wet conditions, or multi-magnitude movement testing.
- Discusses structural material differences (Zendura single-layer sheet vs Trioclear three-layer sheet) as a plausible mechanistic explanation for observed force differences, consistent with the material-science framing in thermoplastic reviews.

## 7. Glossary

- **CAT** — Clear Aligner Therapy
- **PDL** — Periodontal Ligament
- **Typodont** — Artificial dental model/arch used for laboratory simulation
- **Six-axis mechanical sensor** — Sensor apparatus measuring force (Fx, Fy, Fz) and moment (Mx, My, Mz) simultaneously along three orthogonal axes
- **Center of resistance** — The point in a tooth-root system about which applied force produces pure translation (used here at the root apex to compute moments)
- **DSCF test** — Dwass-Steel-Critchlow-Fligner pairwise comparison test (nonparametric post-hoc test following Kruskal-Wallis)
- **Dahlberg error** — Formula for quantifying method/measurement error from repeated-measures data
- **Distal-in rotation moment** — A moment component describing rotational (torque-like) tendency of the tooth about its long axis toward the distal
- **Shape-memory effect** — Property of certain polymers (here, the 3D-printed Graphy TA-28 resin) to recover a pre-set shape after thermal/mechanical deformation, implicated in the paradoxical force finding
