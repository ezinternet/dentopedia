## Why Ingested

[[suture-wound-closure/plonka-2017-flap-designs-flap-advancement-implant-therapy]]'s SR taxonomizes periosteal releasing incision (PRI) as the single largest contributor to flap advancement (+5.5 mm) but has no dedicated outcome series for a protocol that drops the vertical incision entirely and relies on a *wide* PRI alone. Kim 2015 supplies exactly that: a 40-site/34-patient retrospective series reporting concrete complication and delayed-exposure numbers for vertical-incision-free flap advancement, quantifying what the review's taxonomy only classifies.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective clinical study (Int J Oral Maxillofac Implants 2015; 34 patients, 40 GBR sites, April 2012–April 2013) evaluating a flap advancement technique that omits the vertical releasing incision entirely, substituting a wide periosteal releasing incision plus a selective facial-muscle (orbicularis oris/buccinator) releasing incision.

All 40 sites achieved >7 mm flap advancement and clinically passive primary closure with only slight/mild swelling and no long-lasting pain, paresthesia, or infection over 6 months; 2/40 sites (both wearing a provisional removable prosthesis) had delayed titanium-mesh exposure at 4 months, closing spontaneously within 2 weeks after prosthesis discontinuation and dressing.

The technique enables reliable passive primary closure without any vertical incision, but the study is a single-arm retrospective series (no comparator, no vertical-incision control group) reported here from the abstract only.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Introduces a flap-advancement protocol that eliminates the vertical releasing incision entirely, substituting a **wide** periosteal releasing incision plus a selective releasing incision through part of the facial expression musculature (orbicularis oris or buccinator).
- Reports concrete outcome numbers (40 sites, 34 patients, 6-month follow-up) for this specific technique variant — advancement magnitude, complication rates, and a titanium-mesh delayed-exposure signal tied to removable-prosthesis wear.
- Demonstrates that >7 mm of flap advancement (Plonka 2017's "major" stratum) is achievable without any vertical incision, using periosteal and muscle release alone.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective clinical study, single technique arm (no comparator group).
- **Population**: 34 patients, 40 GBR sites (implant-related), treated April 2012–April 2013.
- **Incision protocol**: midcrestal incision on the edentulous alveolar ridge + sulcular incision extended to the adjacent two teeth; **no vertical incision**. Instead: (1) a wide periosteal releasing incision, and (2) an additional releasing incision selectively cutting part of the facial expression muscles (orbicularis oris or buccinator).
- **Outcomes assessed**: postoperative swelling, pain, paresthesia, signs of infection, and membrane exposure — evaluated at 1, 2, 4, 12, and 24 weeks postoperatively.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Flap advancement | >7 mm at all 40 sites |
| Primary closure | Clinically passive at all sites; no obvious primary-closure failure |
| Postoperative swelling | Slight or mild in all patients; no functional limitation (mouth opening, eye opening, swallowing) |
| Long-lasting pain (>1 week) | None |
| Paresthesia | None |
| Signs of infection | None during 6-month follow-up |
| Titanium mesh exposure | Delayed exposure at 4 months in 2/40 sites, both provisional-removable-prosthesis wearers; closed spontaneously within 2 weeks after halting prosthesis use + daily dressing |

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Retrospective, single-arm design** — no comparator group (e.g., conventional vertical-incision technique) to establish relative superiority; advancement/complication figures cannot be causally attributed to the incision design alone.
- **Single center**, moderate sample (40 sites/34 patients); generalizability across operators/institutions untested.
- **Abstract-only ingestion** — exact defect dimensions, graft/membrane materials, statistical analysis, and patient demographics are unavailable and not reconstructed.
- The titanium-mesh delayed-exposure signal (2/40, both removable-prosthesis wearers) suggests a prosthesis-pressure interaction worth controlled follow-up, not established causally here.

## 7. Glossary

- **PRI (periosteal releasing incision)**: an incision scoring/releasing the periosteum (distinct from the overlying mucosa) to gain flap length/elasticity for tension-free coronal advancement.
- **Vertical releasing incision**: a releasing incision perpendicular to the crestal incision, extending apically, conventionally used to mobilize a flap for advancement — omitted entirely in this technique.
- **Facial expression muscle releasing incision**: a releasing incision that selectively divides part of a mimetic muscle (here, orbicularis oris or buccinator) to further mobilize the flap without a vertical cut.
- **Passive primary closure**: flap closure achieved without residual tension across the wound margins, considered a prerequisite for uneventful GBR membrane/graft healing.
- **Delayed membrane/mesh exposure**: exposure of the underlying titanium mesh/barrier occurring after the early healing period (here, at 4 months) rather than in the immediate postoperative weeks.
