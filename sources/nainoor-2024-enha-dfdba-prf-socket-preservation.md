## Three-line Summary

Double-blinded RCT (n=40 mandibular molar extraction sites) comparing eggshell-derived nanohydroxyapatite (EnHA)+PRF vs demineralized freeze-dried bone allograft (DFDBA)+PRF as a barrier membrane in socket preservation.

Both modalities improved pain, wound healing, bone height, bone density, and lamina dura over time within each group, but no statistically significant between-group difference was found at 1 or 4 months; EnHA+PRF trended numerically better on wound healing, bone height, and bone density.

Clinical implication: EnHA is a viable, likely lower-cost/more available alternative to DFDBA for socket preservation when paired with PRF as a barrier membrane; abstract-only source (full text unavailable via PMC) limits methodological scrutiny (randomization/blinding detail, exact p-values).

## 2. Key Contributions

- First (among wiki holdings) to test eggshell-derived nanohydroxyapatite (EnHA) — a biowaste-derived, potentially low-cost graft material — head-to-head against a standard allograft (DFDBA) in extraction socket preservation.
- Uses PRF specifically as a **barrier membrane** covering the graft (rather than as a graft-mixing adjunct or "sticky bone" carrier), distinguishing it from most PRF-socket-preservation papers already in the wiki (e.g., Kollati 2019 Cerabone+PRF, Alrayyes 2022 A-PRF vs collagen plug).
- Double-blinded design (rare for graft-material comparison RCTs, which are often open-label given visibly different graft materials) — increases confidence in the clinical outcome assessments (pain, wound healing).
- Tracks both clinical (pain, wound healing) and radiographic (lamina dura, bone height, bone density) endpoints longitudinally out to 4 months.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

**Participants**: 40 mandibular molar extraction sites, randomized 1:1 into two groups.

- **Group I (test)**: Eggshell-derived nanohydroxyapatite (EnHA) graft + PRF placed as a barrier membrane over the graft.
- **Group II (comparator)**: Demineralized freeze-dried bone allograft (DFDBA) + PRF placed as a barrier membrane over the graft.

**Design**: Double-blinded RCT — abstract does not specify blinding method in detail (full text unavailable).

**Outcome measurements**:
- *Clinical*: Pain (assessed post-operative day 1 and day 2); wound healing (assessed day 7 and day 14).
- *Radiographic*: Intraoral periapical (IOPA) radiographs taken before grafting, immediately post-grafting, and at 1 month and 4 months, assessing changes in lamina dura, bone height, and bone density.

**Statistical comparison**: Within-group changes over time, and between-group (EnHA+PRF vs DFDBA+PRF) comparisons at each time point.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Statistically significant **within-group** improvement over time for pain, wound healing, bone density, bone height, and lamina-dura change in both groups (abstract states "> 0.05" for these within-group comparisons, which — given the surrounding text describing these as "statistically significant" — appears to be a typographic/rendering error for "< 0.05"; full text was unavailable to confirm the exact figures).
- **No statistically significant between-group difference** at any time point, clinically or radiographically.
- EnHA+PRF trended numerically better than DFDBA+PRF for wound healing, increase in bone height, and bone density, though this did not reach significance.
- Conclusion (authors'): EnHA+PRF "seems to offer significantly better results both clinically and radiographically when compared to DFDBA+PRF" — note this conclusion sentence is in some tension with the results paragraph's statement of "no statistically significant difference…between the two groups"; likely reflects the authors summarizing a favorable numerical/descriptive trend as "better" rather than a formally significant between-group difference. ## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only source** — full text not retrieved via PMC; exact p-values, randomization method, allocation concealment, sample-size justification, and full statistical tables could not be verified.
- Apparent internal inconsistency in the abstract between "no statistically significant difference…between the two groups" (Results) and "EnHA with PRF seems to offer significantly better results…compared to DFDBA with PRF" (Conclusion) — likely an overstatement of a non-significant numerical trend; treat the "EnHA is better" framing cautiously until full text can be checked.
- Small sample (n=20 per arm), single-tooth-type socket type (mandibular molars only) — generalizability to anterior/maxillary sites or single-rooted teeth is unknown.
- No comparison arm without PRF barrier membrane, so PRF's independent contribution (vs. graft material choice alone) cannot be isolated.
- Follow-up limited to 4 months radiographically; no implant-placement or long-term ridge-dimension outcomes reported.
- EnHA is a novel/biowaste-derived material — biocompatibility, sterilization/processing standardization, and long-term safety data are comparatively immature relative to DFDBA, which has decades of clinical use.

## 6. Related Work

- Extends the PRF-in-socket-preservation literature already in the wiki (Kollati 2019 Cerabone+PRF vs extraction alone; Alrayyes 2022 A-PRF vs collagen plug in smokers) by testing PRF specifically as a barrier membrane over two different graft materials rather than PRF as the primary intervention.
- Complements Scheyer 2016's multicentre RCT, which used DFDBA + cross-linked collagen membrane as the allograft comparator arm against a DBBM-collagen system — this paper instead pairs DFDBA with a PRF (autologous) membrane, and adds a lower-cost eggshell-HA test arm.
- Eggshell-derived nanohydroxyapatite is part of a broader push toward sustainable/biowaste-derived bone-graft substitutes; distinct from the more established bovine-derived xenografts (DBBM/BCHA) already well-represented in the wiki.

## 7. Glossary

- **EnHA (Eggshell-derived Nanohydroxyapatite)**: Nano-scale hydroxyapatite synthesized from eggshell (calcium carbonate) waste, used as an osteoconductive bone graft substitute.
- **DFDBA (Demineralized Freeze-Dried Bone Allograft)**: Human-donor bone processed by demineralization and freeze-drying; provides both osteoconductive scaffold and residual osteoinductive growth factors (BMPs) exposed by demineralization.
- **PRF (Platelet-Rich Fibrin)**: Autologous fibrin matrix concentrate derived from centrifuged venous blood, used here as a barrier membrane over the graft rather than mixed into it.
- **Barrier membrane**: A physical layer placed over a bone graft to exclude soft-tissue ingrowth and stabilize the graft during early healing (conventionally collagen or PTFE; here substituted with autologous PRF).
- **Lamina dura**: The thin radiopaque bone lining the tooth socket, visible on periapical radiographs; used here as a radiographic healing marker.
