## Why Ingested

2026-09-29 흡연과 조기 임플란트 실패 서베일런스에서, 위키는 흡연-실패 연관의 역학 근거 ([[implants/survival/fan-2024-smoking-early-implant-failure-sr-ma]], OR 2.59)와 기전 리뷰 ([[periodontics/apatzidou-2022-cigarette-smoking-periodontal-implant-review]])는 있었으나 사람 조직학(human histology)으로 골-임플란트 접촉률 (Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC)을 직접 측정한 근거가 없었다. 본 리뷰는 인체 미니임플란트 조직계측 연구를 모아 그 공백을 메운다 (단일 연구그룹 서술적 리뷰라는 한계 있음). 종합: [[overviews/smoking-tobacco-periodontal-implant-overview]].

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of a single research group's (Guarulhos University) human histologic micro-implant studies: 90 edentulous individuals, 123 micro-implants, 7 surfaces, 8-week unloaded healing; only two of the studies enrolled smokers.

Treated surfaces gave higher BIC than machined cpTi (mean 21% cpTi vs 38.8% airborne-abraded/acid-etched; anodized 38.84%); in one smoker/non-smoker comparison BIC was about 50% lower in smokers (p<0.05), and on treated vs cpTi surfaces in smokers BIC was more than twice as high (p<0.001).

Smoking reduces early bone-implant contact and rougher/treated surfaces partly buffer it, but the review has no pooled estimate, is limited to one group and one method, and its mechanism text is largely extrapolated from other literature.

## 세줄요약

한 연구그룹(과룰류스대)의 사람 조직학 미니임플란트 연구를 모은 서술적 리뷰: 무치악 90명, 미니임플란트 123개, 표면 7종, 비부하 8주 치유; 흡연자를 포함한 연구는 2편뿐.

처리 표면이 기계가공 cpTi보다 BIC가 높았고 (평균 cpTi 21% vs 공기입자분사+산부식 38.8%, 양극산화 38.84%); 한 흡연/비흡연 비교에서 흡연자 BIC는 약 50% 낮았고 (p<0.05), 흡연자에서 처리 표면은 cpTi의 2배 넘는 BIC를 보였다 (p<0.001).

흡연은 초기 BIC를 낮추고 처리 표면이 일부 완충하나, 합산 추정치가 없고 단일 그룹·단일 방법이며 기전 서술은 대부분 다른 문헌에서 외삽한 것이다.

## 1. Document Information

- Journal: Braz Dent J 2024;35:e246115 (published 2024-10-25). Departments: Periodontology, Guarulhos University; Tufts; UNESP Araçatuba; Chieti-Pescara.
- Type: narrative review of the authors' own controlled human histologic studies from the last 20 years. Case reports, SRs and narrative reviews were excluded.
- Full text retrieved from PMC; Box 1 (per-study data) and figure images not in retrieved text (captions only).

## 2. Key Contributions

- Pools the narrative of human (not animal) histology on implant surface and smoking, unloaded, 8-week healing.
- Reports mean BIC by surface and a smoker vs non-smoker contrast.
- Frames why surface topography matters most in type IV bone and in smokers.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- 90 partially/completely edentulous individuals (54 women, 36 men; mean age 50.43 +/- 14.5 y); 123 micro-implants, 7 surfaces.
- Exclusions: rheumatoid arthritis, diabetes, chronic alcoholism, glucocorticoid/immunosuppressant use, moderate-advanced periodontitis, mucosal disease, head-neck radiotherapy; ex-smokers excluded. Smokers were defined as >10 filter cigarettes/day for >5 years, included in only two of the studies.
- Micro-implants 2.5 mm x 6 mm or 8 mm, and 2.0 mm x 4 mm (last study); placed distal to the last conventional implant; two per patient (test/control) in most studies, one per patient in others. Sites prepared with 1.8 mm burs in type IV bone, 2.2 mm in types I-II.
- Surfaces: machined cpTi (grade 4); TiO2 airborne-particle abrasion + HNO3/HF; Al2O3 abrasion + HNO3; anodized (calcium glycerol phosphate/calcium acetate electrolyte); CaP nanometric (Ossean); double acid etch; laser-sintered Ti-6Al-4V + acids.
- Retrieval at 8 weeks with 3.25 mm trephine; ground sections stained with basic fuchsin/toluidine blue; blinded calibrated examiners measured BIC%, bone area within threads (BA%), bone density (BD) 200-500 um from implant, osteocyte index; SEM/XRD in some. Mann-Whitney, Wilcoxon, Kruskal-Wallis, p<0.05.
- Sample-size power >80% with >=10 individuals (based on Ivanoff studies).
- Antibiotics: clindamycin 300 mg 3x/day for 7 days; chlorhexidine 0.12% for 14 days.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Losses: 15 of 123 micro-implants mobile at removal; 7 (almost 50% of losses) were cpTi in posterior maxilla; 5 lost in smokers, all posterior maxilla. Failed implants excluded from histometry except one study (counted as 0%). Only micro-implants in smokers showed marginal bone resorption.
- Mean BIC: cpTi 21%; airborne-abraded + acid 38.8%; anodized 38.84%; CaP-impregnated 43%; double acid attack 21.82%.
- Type IV bone (posterior maxilla near tuberosity) study: cpTi 13.06% and abraded+acid 21.81% (vs 23.08% and 38.383% in other studies); sintered 23.29% (p>0.05 vs abraded+acid), both higher than cpTi.
- Smokers: non-smokers showed normal-looking early bone; smokers showed drastic reduction in new bone, inflammatory infiltrate and some osteoclasts in the coronal region; BIC about 50% lower than non-smokers (p<0.05); treated-surface BIC more than twice cpTi (p<0.001). The text does not state the surface for the 50% figure explicitly; the linked figure caption cites Bezerra et al. 2015 with a sandblasted acid-etched surface. Smoker-by-surface BIC values are in Box 1, not retrieved.
- Conclusions: treated surfaces > cpTi; smoking reduced BIC around anodized implants; in smokers abraded+acid surfaces had higher mean BIC than cpTi. (Abstract additionally states anodized surfaces exceeded machined in smokers; body conclusions do not.)
- Histology: inflammatory cells (lymphocytes, macrophages, giant cells) near soft tissue on some treated (abraded+acid, sintered) surfaces.
- Background human studies recapped: Trisi (60 d, posterior maxilla, 11 patients) BIC 19.00 +/- 14.68% machined vs 47.81 +/- 14.01% abraded+acid; Orsini CaP 32.2% vs 19% at 8 weeks; Lang retromolar hydrophilic vs hydrophobic, both 62% at 42 days.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Authors: limited to studies from a single research group using a single methodology; suggest proteomic histology and long-term clinical studies.
- No pooled estimate; no risk-of-bias appraisal; micro-implants (not clinical implants), unloaded, 8 weeks only, so no survival outcome.
- Per-study data box and figures absent from retrieved text.
- Abstract vs body wording differ on anodized surfaces in smokers.
- Mechanistic text (nicotine vasoconstriction, CO hypoxia, angiogenesis, osteoprotegerin, collagen I/ALP/osteocalcin) is drawn from other literature; authors state exact mechanism in peri-implant bone is not fully elucidated.

## 7. Glossary

- BIC: bone-to-implant contact (%). BA: bone area within threads. BD: bone density. cpTi: commercially pure (machined) titanium. Micro-implant: reduced-size research implant retrieved with surrounding bone.
