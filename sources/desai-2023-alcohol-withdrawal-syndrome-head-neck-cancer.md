## Why Ingested

Every other alcohol page in this wiki concerns a dental outcome — implant failure, peri-implantitis, bleeding, osteomyelitis. None addresses the patient who is already physically dependent, where the operative risk is not the surgery but the withdrawal. This commentary is the wiki's only page on that axis, and it belongs to the same pre-operative conversation: a patient who drinks heavily enough to impair osseointegration is also the patient at risk of postoperative delirium and arrhythmia.

→ [[drug/systemic-disease/desai-2023-alcohol-withdrawal-syndrome-head-neck-cancer]]

## Three-line Summary

Narrative commentary synthesising addiction-medicine principles for otolaryngology–head and neck surgeons managing alcohol withdrawal syndrome in the perioperative period.

Postoperative alcohol withdrawal in head and neck cancer patients is associated with increased medical and surgical complications, longer hospitalisation, and higher hospital costs, and harm-reduction/addiction-medicine approaches can reduce morbidity and mortality though they are not fully validated in this surgical population.

The actionable message is to screen and plan for withdrawal *before* operating — the same history that flags a heavy drinker for implant risk also flags a withdrawal risk, and it needs to be handed to someone trained to manage it.

## 세줄요약

서술형 논평 (narrative commentary) — 이비인후과·두경부 외과 의사를 대상으로 수술 전후 알코올 금단 증후군 (Alcohol Withdrawal Syndrome, AWS) 관리 원칙을 정리.

두경부암 수술 환자의 술후 AWS는 의료·수술 합병증, 재원 기간, 병원 비용을 증가시키며, 위해 감소 (harm reduction)·중독의학 접근이 이환율과 사망률을 낮출 수 있으나 이 수술군에서 완전히 검증되지는 않음.

핵심은 수술 전에 선별하고 계획하라는 것 — 과량 음주력을 임플란트 위험 신호로만 쓰지 말고 금단 위험 신호로도 읽어 중독의학 관리로 넘겨야 함.

## 1. Document Information

- **Journal**: Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery 2023;168(5):1258–1260
- **DOI**: 10.1002/ohn.164
- **Design**: commentary / narrative synthesis (not a systematic review, not primary data)
- **Scope**: perioperative alcohol withdrawal syndrome in head and neck cancer surgery
- **Disclosure**: no primary cohort or trial data reported

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Names the outcome that dental pages miss**: postoperative withdrawal, not postoperative infection or non-integration.
- **States the harm quantitatively at the level of direction**: increased medical and surgical complications, length of stay, and hospital-related costs.
- **Imports addiction medicine into surgical planning** — harm reduction as a philosophy distinct from abstinence-only advice.
- **Flags its own evidence status**: these approaches "have not been fully validated in perioperative surgical management for head and neck cancer".

## 3. Methodology

- **Design**: commentary synthesising addiction-medicine principles and current perioperative strategies.
- **Audience**: otolaryngology–head and neck surgery surgeons performing major ablative/reconstructive surgery.
- **Content**: risk identification, perioperative assessment, and management strategies for alcohol withdrawal syndrome.
- **Evidence base**: narrative — no systematic search, no pooled estimates, no primary cohort.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Head and neck cancer surgical patients are described as at **increased risk** for alcohol withdrawal syndrome.
- Postoperative withdrawal is associated with:
  - increased medical and surgical complications,
  - increased length of hospitalisation,
  - increased hospital-related costs.
- Harm-reduction and addiction-medicine approaches are presented as able to **reduce morbidity and mortality**, with the explicit caveat that validation in this surgical population is incomplete.
- No numerical effect sizes, incidence figures, or protocol doses are reported in the abstract-level record available.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **Not primary evidence.** A commentary cannot supply incidence, effect size, or a validated protocol; it supplies framing and a care pathway.
- **No reported search strategy**, so coverage of the addiction-medicine literature is the authors' selection.
- **Head and neck oncology population**, not routine dental surgery: the absolute risk of withdrawal in an implant or extraction patient is far lower, and the commentary does not address that lower-risk stratum.
- **No benzodiazepine dosing, CIWA-Ar thresholds, or protocol specifics** in the available text — the practical algorithm must come from an addiction-medicine source.
- **Validation gap is acknowledged by the authors** and should be repeated whenever this page is cited.

## 7. Glossary

- **AWS (alcohol withdrawal syndrome)**: the constellation of autonomic, neurological, and psychiatric symptoms after cessation or reduction of heavy alcohol intake; ranges from tremor and diaphoresis to seizures and delirium tremens.
- **Harm reduction**: policies and practices aimed at reducing the harms of substance use without requiring abstinence as a precondition.
- **CIWA-Ar**: a standardised withdrawal severity scale (not detailed in this commentary's available text).
- **Perioperative**: the period spanning before, during, and after surgery.
- **Narrative review/commentary**: expert synthesis without systematic search — the lowest tier of evidence in this wiki's vocabulary.
