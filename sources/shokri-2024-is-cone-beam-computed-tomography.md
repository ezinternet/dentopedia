## Why Ingested

The cracked-tooth cluster holds only narrative/consensus statements on whether CBCT can show a vertical root fracture ([[cracked-tooth/patel-2025-position-statement-longitudinal-cracks-fractures]] says CBCT cannot reliably detect the intraradicular fracture line; [[cracked-tooth/patel-2022-present-status-future-directions-vertical]] says the same), plus one retrospective CBCT-vs-PA bone-defect study ([[cracked-tooth/alaugaily-2022-cbct-patterns-bone-loss-clinical]]). This SR+MA supplies the first pooled sensitivity/specificity for CBCT vs periapical radiography, so the "CBCT cannot detect VRF" statement can be read against numbers (and against the fact that most of the pooled data are ex vivo).

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science to 22 Nov 2023; PROSPERO CRD42023477634; QUADAS-2) of studies comparing CBCT with periapical (PA) radiography for vertical root fracture (VRF) detection: 23 studies in the review (18 in vitro/ex vivo, 5 in vivo per Table 1), 16 in the meta-analysis.

Pooled random-effects sensitivity was 0.708 (95% CI 0.608–0.792) for CBCT vs 0.518 (0.408–0.626) for PA (P = 0.011), while specificity did not differ (CBCT 0.841 vs PA 0.876, P = 0.46); CBCT sensitivity fell from 0.911 in non-root-filled to 0.758 in root-filled teeth, and heterogeneity was high (I² 70–87%).

CBCT detects more VRFs than PA but still misses roughly 3 in 10, with a false-positive rate similar to PA; the authors advise CBCT only after careful clinical examination, not as the initial radiograph. Evidence is mostly in vitro with induced fractures, heterogeneous, and shows publication bias (Egger P < 0.05 for CBCT sensitivity, CBCT specificity and PA specificity).

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (PubMed·Scopus·Web of Science, 2023년 11월 22일까지; PROSPERO CRD42023477634; QUADAS-2) — 수직치근파절 (Vertical Root Fracture, VRF) 검출에서 콘빔 CT (Cone-Beam Computed Tomography, CBCT)와 치근단방사선사진 (Periapical radiography, PA)을 비교한 연구 23편(Table 1 기준 체외·발치치 18편, 생체 5편), 메타분석 16편.

랜덤효과 통합 민감도는 CBCT 0.708 (95% CI 0.608–0.792) vs PA 0.518 (0.408–0.626) (P = 0.011), 특이도는 차이 없음 (CBCT 0.841 vs PA 0.876, P = 0.46); CBCT 민감도는 근관충전 없는 치아 0.911에서 근관충전치 0.758로 감소, 이질성 높음 (I² 70–87%).

CBCT가 PA보다 VRF를 더 찾지만 여전히 약 10개 중 3개를 놓치고 위양성률은 PA와 비슷함; 저자들은 CBCT를 첫 영상이 아니라 정밀한 임상검사 이후에 권고. 근거는 대부분 인공 파절을 만든 체외 연구이며 이질성이 크고 출판 편향 있음 (Egger P < 0.05: CBCT 민감도·CBCT 특이도·PA 특이도).

## 2. Key Contributions

- First pooled head-to-head diagnostic-accuracy estimate of CBCT vs PA radiography restricted to VRF detection.
- Quantifies the sensitivity gap (0.708 vs 0.518) with no significant specificity difference (0.841 vs 0.876).
- Subgroup analysis by root-canal filling status: CBCT sensitivity and specificity are significantly higher in non-root-filled than root-filled teeth (both P < 0.001); PA shows the root-filled penalty only for specificity.
- Documents high heterogeneity and publication bias, and flags the in vitro dominance of the evidence base.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis, PRISMA and QUADAS-2; PROSPERO CRD42023477634. PICO: human teeth with VRF; CBCT vs PA; detection accuracy.
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science (to 22 Nov 2023) plus reference-list hand search; English original research only.
- **Inclusion**: studies using both CBCT and PA in intact, root-filled or post-and-core human teeth; at least 10 teeth; CBCT scanner, exposure settings and voxel size reported; reference standard stated (in vivo: exploratory surgery/extraction or direct observation; in vitro: methylene blue staining or microscopic assessment); sensitivity/specificity (or TP/TN/FP/FN) reported. Animal, horizontal-fracture, non-comparative and positive-cases-only studies excluded.
- **n**: 1,251 records + 8 from bibliographies; 33 full texts; 23 studies qualitative synthesis; 16 meta-analysis.
- **Statistics**: Comprehensive Meta-Analysis v3; inverse-variance random-effects model on log-odds of sensitivity and of specificity separately; I² for heterogeneity; funnel plots + Egger test for publication bias; subgroup by root filling (yes/no).
- **Outcomes**: pooled sensitivity and specificity per modality; subgroup estimates.

- Study mix: 19 in vitro and 4 in vivo per Results text; Table 1 lists 5 in vivo (Bernardes 2009, Wang 2011, Chavda 2014, Mizuhashi 2019, Sha 2021) and 2 ex vivo (Patel 2013, Brady 2014); the Discussion says 5 in vivo.
- PA pooled sensitivity 0.518 is not distinguishable from 0.5 (Z = 0.314, P = 0.753 in Table 2).
- Risk of bias: 69.6% of studies rated low risk on QUADAS-2 (Fig. 2).
- Authors' reading: VRFs were "not detected in 27% of the teeth" (stated in the CBCT context; 1 − 0.708 = 29%) and 49% by PA; artifacts from gutta-percha and metal posts (beam hardening, stellate lines) and PA beam angulation (fracture line detectable only if beam within about 4 degrees of the crack plane) are the proposed mechanisms; flat-panel detector CBCT units reportedly perform better.
- Publication bias: asymmetric funnels and Egger P < 0.05 for CBCT sensitivity, CBCT specificity and PA specificity; authors note pooled values may be overestimates.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Most included studies are in vitro/ex vivo with fractures induced by hammer/chisel or universal testing machine; the authors note clinical conditions (soft-tissue volume, motion blur) degrade image quality, so in vitro accuracy likely overstates clinical performance.
- High heterogeneity (I² 70–87%): sample size (21 to over 110 teeth), scanner/detector, voxel size, FOV, exposure, reference standard (in vivo surgery vs in vitro staining/microscopy), examiner expertise, mixing intact and root-filled teeth.
- Sensitivity and specificity pooled separately (random-effects on log-odds); no bivariate/HSROC model reported (inferred from methods text).
- Publication bias present for three of four pooled estimates.
- Internal inconsistencies in the source, reproduced as written: study design counts (19 in vitro + 4 in vivo in Results vs 5 in vivo in Table 1/Discussion); the Discussion says all in vivo studies favoured CBCT while the Discussion's own citation of Chavda 2014 (ref 15, in vivo) places it among studies finding no significant difference; the pooled CBCT sensitivity (0.708) is lower than both root-filling subgroup estimates (0.758 and 0.911); PRISMA counts (1,251 + 8 − 1,193) do not equal the 33 full texts as reported.
- Authors call for further in vivo studies and subgroup analyses by sample size, scanner type, test conditions and examiner expertise.

## 7. Glossary

- **VRF (vertical root fracture)**: longitudinal root fracture originating in the canal and extending toward the periapical/periodontal tissue.
- **PA (periapical radiography)**: 2D intraoral radiograph; fracture line visible only when the beam is nearly parallel to it.
- **CBCT (cone-beam computed tomography)**: 3D imaging with no superimposition but vulnerable to beam-hardening artifacts from dense materials.
- **QUADAS-2**: tool for risk of bias in diagnostic-accuracy studies (patient selection, index test, reference standard, flow/timing).
- **Egger test**: regression test for funnel-plot asymmetry (publication bias); P < 0.05 suggests bias.
- **Beam-hardening artifact**: dark bands/streaks near gutta-percha or metal posts that can mimic or obscure a fracture line.
