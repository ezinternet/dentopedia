## Why Ingested

측방 골창 (Lateral Window) 상악동거상술 (Sinus Elevation) 의 천공 (Perforation) 근거는 "어떤 술식·기구가 천공률을 낮추는가"를 무작위·네트워크 비교로 순위 매기는 쪽([[sinus-lift/lateral/lee-2023-impact-sinus-floor-elevation-techniques]])에 집중돼 있었고, **수술 현미경 (Operating Microscope) 이라는 광학 축**이 골창 크기·막 조작·술자 인체공학을 어떻게 바꾸는지는 위키에 없었다. 본 논문은 5 mm급 골창·피에조 서징 (Piezoelectric Surgery) 천공률 7–8% vs 회전 버 (Rotary Bur) 20–30%, HPISE (Hydrodynamic Piezoelectric Internal Sinus Elevation) 2.83% 같은 수치를 묶어 미세수술 (Microsurgery) 축의 첫 페이지를 만든다.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (PubMed, Web of Science and Google Scholar, 1986–2025, 45 landmark and contemporary studies; two authors screened independently, no meta-analysis) of microscope-assisted maxillary sinus augmentation in both lateral-window and transcrestal approaches, covering instrumentation, piezoelectric/osteotome/osseodensification techniques and surgical ergonomics.

Magnification allows antrostomies of roughly 5 mm (Shakibaie, 20 sinuses) to 5 × 8 mm / 31.4 mm² (Pariente, 15 procedures) and 5–6 mm (Merli, 147 sinuses, perforation 6%, infection 2–5.6%, mean vertical bone gain 7.44 mm), while piezoelectric lateral windows perforated at 7–8% versus 20–30% for rotary burs and hydrodynamic PISE at 2.83% (353 implants/250 sinuses, 97.2% success).

The technical case for the microscope is strong (smaller windows, earlier tear detection, neutral posture, stepwise 4–7× → 10–15× magnification) but the clinical superiority claim rests on case series, pilot studies and small comparative trials with short follow-up, so RCTs with long-term outcomes are still required.

작은 골창·조기 천공 발견·중립 자세 (4–7× → 10–15× 단계적 배율) 라는 기술적 논거는 탄탄하나, 근거는 대부분 후향 증례군·파일럿·소규모 비교연구이고 추적이 짧아 장기 RCT 없이는 "현미경이 우월하다"는 결론을 확정할 수 없다.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Frames the microscope as an optical axis that changes **three things at once** in sinus augmentation: window size (smaller antrostomy), membrane handling (earlier detection of small tears), and operator posture — rather than as a pure "better vision" upgrade.
- Collects the concrete dimensions actually used in microsurgical practice: ~5 mm circular antrostomy (MGES, 20 sinuses), 5 × 8 mm ellipse = 31.4 mm² (EBI Sinus Kit, 15 procedures), 5–6 mm circular piezo opening (147 sinuses), and ~5 mm access window shown under the microscope.
- Links window size to *histologic* graft maturation via Avila-Ortiz's 24-procedure biopsy series (vital bone 21.7% ± 16.3%; window area negatively correlated with vital bone, positively with residual allograft) — the biologic argument for small windows.
- Provides an explicit **stepwise magnification protocol**: 4–7× with low illumination for incision and flap reflection, 10–15× with higher light for membrane elevation and integrity checks in small transcrestal osteotomies.
- Specifies microsurgical instrument design requirements (lightweight, rigid, long handles resting on the thumb–index saddle, round cross-section, smaller working tips, non-magnetic) and a six-handed ergonomic setup (surgeon 9–12 o'clock, patient supine with chair reclined 5°–10°, primary assistant 12–15 o'clock, secondary assistant 2–4 o'clock).
- Separates what the evidence actually supports per technique: piezo 7–8% vs rotary 20–30% perforation; cortical-wall-erosion entry 0% vs direct outlining 11.1% (p < 0.05); HPISE 2.83% perforation with 97.2% success; osseodensification promising but *not* proven to reduce complications (the 100% no-perforation claim rests on CBCT only).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: narrative review — no meta-analysis, no risk-of-bias instrument, thematic synthesis.
- **Databases**: PubMed, Web of Science, Google Scholar (1986–2025) plus manual screening of reference lists of key reviews and textbooks.
- **Search terms**: combinations of "microscope-assisted" OR "microsurgery" OR "magnification"; "sinus augmentation" OR "sinus floor elevation"; "lateral window" OR "transcrestal" OR "osteotome"; "piezoelectric surgery" OR "osseodensification".
- **Inclusion**: studies describing techniques, outcomes or complications of lateral or transcrestal sinus augmentation, and studies of microsurgical/magnification approaches.
- **Exclusion**: non-English publications without accessible translation; studies unrelated to maxillary sinus augmentation.
- **Screening**: two authors independently screened titles and abstracts, then full texts; study quality "considered" by design, sample size and follow-up duration (no formal appraisal).
- **Evidence base**: 45 landmark and contemporary studies.
- **Sections**: fundamental principles → microsurgical lateral → microsurgical transcrestal → instrumentation (piezo, osteotome, osseodensification, PISE) → ergonomics → conclusions.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Baseline numbers quoted for conventional practice**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Lateral-window vertical augmentation capacity | up to 9 mm or more |
| Osteotome transcrestal vertical bone gain | 3–9 mm |
| Osseodensification vertical gain (no perforation) | 7.0 ± 2.49 mm |
| Implant survival, both approaches | 92%–98% |
| Membrane perforation range, lateral | 0%–58.2% |
| Membrane perforation range, transcrestal | 0%–21.4% |
| Hsu et al. SR (74 studies, 4411 procedures) | 17.8% lateral, ~5% transcrestal |

**Microsurgical lateral window**

| Study | n | Window | Outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shakibaie (MGES) | 20 sinuses | ~5 mm diameter, alveolar bone preserved | improved implant primary stability from preserved vestibular bone |
| Pariente et al. (EBI Sinus Kit) | 15 procedures | elliptic 5 mm × 8 mm = 31.4 mm² | less intraoperative bleeding, improved predictability |
| Merli et al. (piezo, Piezosurgery/Mectron) | 147 sinuses | circular 5–6 mm | perforation 6%, postoperative infection 2%–5.6%, mean vertical bone gain 7.44 mm, marginal bone maintained |

**Window size vs graft maturation (Avila-Ortiz et al., 24 lateral procedures, allograft, 6-month core biopsy)**

- Vital bone (VB): 21.7% ± 16.3%
- Remaining allograft (RA): 23.5% ± 16.3%
- Non-mineralized tissue (NMT): 55.1% ± 8.5%
- Window area (mm² = height × width): significant **negative** correlation with VB, significant **positive** correlation with RA — i.e. larger windows delay consolidation.

**Piezoelectric lateral window**

- Meta-analysis: perforation 7%–8% (piezo) vs 20%–30% (conventional rotary burs).
- Prospective case series (21 lateral augmentations): perforation 4.7%; the single incident occurred with a sinus septum.
- RCT, direct window outlining vs cortical wall erosion: erosion 0 perforations vs 11.1% in the outlining group (p < 0.05), no vascular injuries; operating time 13.3 vs 9.2 min (difference not clinically significant).
- Practical trick: thick lateral wall → outline/thin with rotary burs or sharp osteotomy/bone-chip piezo inserts; once a bluish hue appears, switch to diamond-coated smoothing inserts for the final window.

**Transcrestal, osteotome vs osseodensification vs piezo**

- 12 surgeries, osteotome vs osseodensification: similar mild-to-moderate pain and edema, no significant difference; osseodensification significantly shorter operative time and higher primary stability.
- 36 surgeries, osteotome vs piezosurgery: no significant difference in bone augmentation level; piezosurgery better surgical control, less postoperative discomfort.
- Osteotome drawbacks: benign positional vertigo, membrane perforation risk, consistent elevation beyond 5–6 mm technically difficult.
- Osseodensification (Huwais & Meyer 2016): predictable vertical gain 6.65 ± 1.06 mm, 100% implant survival in one pilot trial, but membrane integrity assessed only by CBCT — "absence of perforation" should be read cautiously.
- PISE: piezo microvibrations at 25–30 kHz; membrane elevated by hydrodynamic cavitation pressure.
- HPISE multicentre (250 sinuses, 353 implants): perforation 2.83%, implant success 97.2%, average loading > 1 year.

**Magnification and ergonomics**

- Perrin et al.: unaided vision insufficient for intraradicular structures; Galilean loupes 2.59× gave limited orifice visualization (especially in younger practitioners); only the operating microscope (6.9×) enabled measurable intracanal visualization regardless of examiner age.
- Eichenberger et al.: microscope outperformed loupes and unaided vision in both direct and indirect tooth preparation.
- Transcrestal microsurgical magnification reported as 6–20×.
- Protocol: 4–7× low light (incision, flap) → 10–15× high light (membrane elevation, integrity assessment).
- Positioning: operator 9–12 o'clock (12 o'clock directly behind the head is favourable for both approaches); patient supine, chair reclined 5°–10°; lateral approach — rotate head fully to one side for direct membrane view; transcrestal — indirect view with front-surface mirror plus defogging.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Evidence is dominated by case series, pilot studies and small comparative trials with heterogeneous methodology and short follow-up; no RCT of microscope vs conventional sinus augmentation exists, so "clinical superiority" is a technical inference, not a demonstrated outcome.
- Narrative method: single-pass thematic synthesis of 45 studies, no systematic search string reporting per database, no risk-of-bias assessment, no meta-analysis — publication and selection bias cannot be excluded.
- Several benchmark numbers are borrowed from other reviews (Hsu et al.'s 74 studies/4411 procedures) rather than recomputed, and osseodensification complication data were absent from that source.
- The osseodensification "no perforation" claim rests on CBCT alone, which cannot confirm membrane integrity.
- No cost, learning-curve, or microscope-acquisition analysis — a real barrier to the "integrate gradually, start with simple cases" recommendation.
- Future work called for: RCTs with long-term outcomes comparing microscope-assisted and conventional lateral/transcrestal augmentation.

## 6. Related Work

- Tatum (1986): original lateral-window technique — the procedure this review seeks to make less invasive.
- Summers (1994): osteotome transcrestal approach; the 5–6 mm elevation ceiling this review cites as the technique's limit.
- Shanelec & Tibbets (1992): first microscope-assisted periodontal therapy; Sirinirund et al. later extended the microscope to sinus augmentation.
- Avila-Ortiz et al.: window-size vs histologic maturation — the biologic rationale for microsurgical small windows.
- Shakibaie (MGES), Pariente et al. (EBI Sinus Kit), Merli et al. (piezo 147 sinuses): the three microsurgical lateral series that supply the window dimensions and complication rates.
- Gargallo-Albiol et al., Sirinirund et al.: microscope use in the transcrestal approach and early tear detection.
- Hsu et al. (SR, 74 studies, 4411 procedures): the complication denominator this review uses to keep minimally invasive claims honest.
- Huwais & Meyer (2016): osseodensification, the newest transcrestal competitor discussed.

## 7. Glossary

- **Microsurgery (미세수술)**: surgery performed with an operating microscope, micro-instruments and deliberate ergonomic setup to gain precision beyond loupes.
- **Operating Microscope (수술 현미경)**: high-magnification coaxial-illumination optical system; 6.9× in Perrin's comparison, 6–20× for transcrestal sinus work here.
- **Lateral Window / Antrostomy (측방 골창)**: bony access opening in the lateral maxillary wall through which the Schneiderian membrane is elevated.
- **Schneiderian Membrane (슈나이더막)**: the sinus mucosal lining that must be elevated intact; its perforation is the most common intraoperative complication.
- **MGES (Microscope-Guided External Sinus elevation)**: Shakibaie's technique for a ~5 mm window while preserving alveolar bone.
- **PISE / HPISE (Piezoelectric Internal Sinus Elevation / Hydrodynamic PISE)**: transcrestal piezo elevation, with membrane lift driven by cavitation-generated hydraulic pressure in the HPISE variant.
- **Osseodensification (골밀도화, OD)**: counter-clockwise densifying burs that compact bone laterally/apically while elevating the membrane.
- **Residual Bone Height (잔존 골고)**: bone height between crest and sinus floor; the main determinant of simultaneous vs staged implant placement (Wang & Katranji ABC classification: simultaneous placement feasible at ≤5 mm if primary stability is achieved).
- **Six-handed dentistry (식스핸디드)**: surgeon + two assistants with divided roles (field maintenance vs biomaterial/instrument delivery).
- **Vital Bone / Remaining Allograft / Non-mineralized tissue (VB / RA / NMT)**: histomorphometric fractions from 6-month core biopsies.
