## Why Ingested

This systematic review directly evaluates whether conservative endodontic access cavities (ConsAC) preserve fracture resistance better than traditional access cavities (TradAC) in molars — a core question for minimally invasive endodontic practice. Extends the scope of [[endodontics/anatomy/rover-2020-influence-minimally-invasive-endodontic-access]] (in vitro in mandibular incisors) to molars and updates evidence synthesis prior to the later network meta-analysis by [[endodontics/anatomy/motiwala-2022-effect-of-different-access]].

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of in vitro studies (2010–January 2020) comparing conservative vs traditional endodontic access cavity designs on fracture resistance of extracted human molar teeth; screened 105 studies, included 10 with low-to-moderate bias.

Only 4 of 10 included studies showed significantly higher fracture resistance in conservative cavities; remaining studies found no significant differences or mixed results (mandibular molars showed higher resistance in one study, but maxillary molars did not).

Conclusion: Insufficient evidence to definitively state that conservative access cavities are superior to traditional designs for fracture resistance; heterogeneous outcomes across included studies indicate need for larger, well-controlled trials.

10개 포함 논문 중 4개만 ConsAC에서 유의미하게 높은 파절저항성 확인; 나머지는 유의미한 차이 없음 또는 상충 결과 (하악 대구치에서만 차이, 상악 대구치에서는 아님).

결론: ConsAC가 TradAC보다 파절저항성 측면에서 우월한지에 대한 결정적 근거 부족, 연구 간 이질성이 높으므로 대규모·잘 통제된 시험 필요.

## 2. Key Contributions

- **Systematic review methodology**: Comprehensive search of 7 electronic databases (PubMed, ScienceDirect, Scopus, ResearchGate, EBSCOhost, Wiley, Google Scholar) using MeSH and text-word keywords; structured PRISMA flowchart; quality assessment with bias categorization (low/moderate/high).

- **Heterogeneous outcomes across 10 studies**: 4 studies showed ConsAC superiority; 6 showed no significant difference or conflicting results by tooth position (mandibular vs maxillary), challenging the hypothesis that conservative design uniformly improves fracture resistance.

- **Candidate methodological confounds**: Studies varied in sample size (30–60 teeth), canal filling/restoration protocols (some tested without obturation, risk of bias), load-test conditions (compression vs shear), and thermmechanical cycling presence/absence.

- **Clinical implication identified**: Peri-cervical dentin preservation is biomechanically important (reduces cuspal flexure), but this structural benefit does not consistently translate to higher fracture resistance under laboratory loading conditions.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Systematic review of in vitro comparative studies (no meta-analysis)
- **Databases Searched**: PubMed, ScienceDirect, Scopus, ResearchGate, EBSCOhost, Wiley Online Library, Google Scholar; date range January 2010 – January 2020
- **Search Terms**: Boolean combinations of "endodontic access design," "endodontically treated tooth," "molars," "conservative access cavity," "traditional access cavity," "fracture resistance," "fracture strength," "minimally invasive endodontics"
- **Screening Process**: 
  - Electronic search yielded 108 publications; after de-duplication, 59 articles
  - First pass (title/abstract): 33 articles selected for full-text review
  - Second pass (full text): 10 articles met inclusion criteria
- **Inclusion Criteria** (PICO scheme):
  - **Population**: Extracted human permanent molar teeth with fully formed (mature) apex
  - **Intervention**: Conservative endodontic cavities (ConsAC) / minimally invasive access
  - **Comparison**: Traditional endodontic cavities (TradAC)
  - **Outcome**: Fracture resistance / fracture strength (load-to-failure in Newtons or MPa)
  - **Study Type**: In vitro experimental studies only
  - **Sample Size**: 30–60 teeth (justified for population inference)
  - **Publication**: English language; post-2010
- **Exclusion Criteria**: Literature reviews, opinion articles, letters, case reports, conference abstracts, clinical/observational studies, studies with immature teeth or artificial teeth, finite-element analysis (FEA) only, studies not directly comparing access designs or not measuring fracture resistance
- **Risk of Bias Assessment**: Modified quality assessment tool for in vitro studies; categorized each paper as low, moderate, or high risk
- **Statistical Analysis**: Qualitative synthesis only (no meta-analysis); descriptive summary of findings per study with tabulated load-to-failure means, standard deviations, and statistical significance reporting

### Narrative Synthesis Conclusions

- **Studies Supporting ConsAC Advantage**: 4/10 studies (Krishan 2014, Makati 2018, Osman & Ahmed 2018 for mandibular teeth, Plotino 2017 — not detailed in extraction but mentioned in Discussion)
  - Rationale: ConsAC preserves peri-cervical dentin and marginal ridges, reducing cuspal flexure, thereby maintaining load-bearing capacity
  - Mandibular molars showed larger effect than maxillary (anatomical differences in ridge morphology and root divergence)

- **Studies Showing No Significant Difference**: 6/10 studies (Corsentino 2018, Sabeti 2018, Maske 2020, Barbosa 2020, and 2 others)
  - Possible explanations: (a) restoration material and technique may override access-cavity benefit, (b) in-vitro loading conditions (static compression) may not reflect clinical stress patterns, (c) sample size/variability insufficient for small true effects

- **Confounders Noted**:
  - Several studies (Krishan, Osman) tested untreated/un-restored teeth (higher risk of bias; restoration restores ~72% of fracture strength to intact level)
  - Heterogeneous testing protocols (dry vs hydrated, thermomechanical cycling vs static)
  - No standardized access cavity dimensions across studies

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

### Limitations of Included Studies (as reported in review)

1. **Limited sample sizes**: Studies used n=30–60 per group; underpowered for small effect detection
2. **Lack of standardization**: 
   - Access cavity dimensions not uniform across studies
   - Load-testing protocols varied (compression force, speed, angle)
   - Restoration materials and techniques differed
3. **Moisture/environmental conditions**: Studies tested in dry, hydrated, or thermomechanically cycled conditions — outcome-dependent variation
4. **Absence of canal treatment in some studies**: Krishan et al and Osman et al tested without obturation/restoration, creating confounding regarding the true effect of access design vs restoration
5. **Mandibular/maxillary confounding**: Single studies did not directly compare tooth position; Osman & Ahmed found position-dependent effects
6. **Limited generalizability**: All studies ex-vivo extracted teeth; PDL, vascular dynamics, and proprioception absent

### Limitations of the Review Itself

1. **No meta-analysis**: Heterogeneity precluded pooling (different outcome measures, test conditions, populations)
2. **Publication bias risk**: Hand-search recovered few unpublished/negative studies
3. **Inclusion scope**: Only English-language, in-vitro, peer-reviewed studies (excludes grey literature, clinical trials, non-English publications)

### Future Research Directions (per conclusion)

- **Randomized clinical trials (RCT)**: Long-term fracture incidence and survival rates comparing ConsAC vs TradAC in vivo
- **Standardized testing protocols**: ISO 6874 or similar harmonization for access cavity dimensions and loading conditions
- **Subgroup analysis**: Separate evaluation by tooth type (premolar vs molar) and restoration material (composite, crown, etc.)
- **Mechanism studies**: Finite-element analysis (FEA) + in-vitro correlation to understand stress redistribution under various access designs
- **Clinical adoption**: Learning curve, operator dependence, and real-world compliance with ConsAC technique

## 7. Glossary

- **Conservative/Contracted Endodontic Cavity (ConsAC / CEC)**: Access cavity design that minimizes dentin removal while maintaining canal access; preserves marginal ridges, occlusal contours, and pulp chamber roof. Also termed "minimally invasive endodontic access" or "orifice-directed access."
- **Traditional Endodontic Cavity (TradAC / TEC)**: Standard access cavity following the concept of "extension for prevention" — broad triangular or trapezoidal outline to ensure straight-line access and visibility, removing more coronal dentin.
- **Fracture Resistance / Fracture Strength**: Maximum load (in Newtons, N) or stress (MPa) applied to a tooth before structural failure in-vitro; proxy for clinical fracture risk (validation ongoing).
- **Peri-cervical Dentin**: Dentin in the cervical third of the tooth root (approximately 4 mm apical to and 6 mm occlusal to the alveolar bone crest); acts as a mechanical "shock absorber" transmitting occlusal forces to the root.
- **Cuspal Flexure**: Deflection of the cusps during mastication; increased when coronal dentin (especially peri-cervical) is removed, potentially increasing stress concentration and fracture risk.
- **Root Canal Obturation**: Filling of the root canal system after instrumentation, typically with gutta-percha and sealer; restores ~72% of tooth fracture resistance compared to untreated teeth.
- **Risk of Bias Assessment**: Quality evaluation using study-design criteria (e.g., sample size, blinding, standardization, moisture conditions) to categorize methodological reliability (low/moderate/high risk).
- **PICO Scheme**: Structured clinical question framework: Population (study subjects), Intervention (treatment/exposure), Comparison (control), Outcome (measured result).
- **MeSH (Medical Subject Headings)**: Controlled vocabulary used in PubMed for indexing and searching literature.
