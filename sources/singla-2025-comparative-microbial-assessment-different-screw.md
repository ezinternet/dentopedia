## Why Ingested
이 RCT는 "스크류 접근홀 (Screw Access Hole, SAH)을 무엇으로 막는 것이 세균 오염·미세누출을 가장 잘 막는가"라는 임상 질문(면·PTFE·1%CHX 면)에 직접 답한다. [[wiki/prosthetic-materials/abutment-screw/packaeser-2025-effect-resin-composite-filling-thickness]]가 같은 PTFE 테이프+복합레진 SAH 밀봉 기법을 기계적/피로 관점에서 다룬다면, 이 논문은 미생물학적 관점을 더해 "얼마나 두껍게"뿐 아니라 "무엇으로 막을지"까지 함께 다룰 근거를 제공한다.

## Three-line Summary
(Line 1: Prospective randomized clinical trial, 35 patients/45 single-tooth screw-retained implants; cotton pellet used as baseline SAH plug, then randomized via lottery method to PTFE tape or cotton soaked in 1% chlorhexidine (Co1%CHX) subgroups, each retrieved and cultured after 1 month.)
(Line 2: Both PTFE and Co1%CHX showed significantly less aerobic/anaerobic bacterial contamination than cotton (e.g., anaerobic positive samples: cotton 95% vs PTFE 50%, p=0.001; cotton 70% vs Co1%CHX 20%, p=0.001); Co1%CHX outperformed PTFE for anaerobic bacteria (20% vs 50% positive, p=0.04), though aerobic difference between PTFE and Co1%CHX was not significant; IL-6 in peri-implant sulcular fluid did not rise significantly with either material over 3 months.)
(Line 3: Clinically, PTFE or cotton dipped in 1% chlorhexidine should replace dry cotton pellets as the SAH plugging material to reduce oral flora colonization and likely halitosis/cacogeusia, though the study did not identify bacterial species and follow-up was limited to 5 months post-restoration.)

## 세줄요약
(줄1: 전향적 무작위 임상연구, 환자 35명/단일치아 나사고정형 임플란트 45개 — 면(cotton)을 기저 SAH 충전재로 사용 후 뽑기(lottery) 방식으로 PTFE 테이프군과 1% 클로르헥시딘(1% Chlorhexidine, CHX) 침지 면(Co1%CHX)군으로 무작위 배정, 1개월 후 회수해 배양)
(줄2: PTFE·Co1%CHX 모두 면(cotton)보다 호기성·혐기성 세균 오염이 유의하게 낮았고(예: 혐기성 양성률 — 면 95% vs PTFE 50%, p=0.001; 면 70% vs Co1%CHX 20%, p=0.001), 혐기성균에서는 Co1%CHX가 PTFE보다 우수(20% vs 50%, p=0.04)했으나 호기성균 차이는 유의하지 않았음; 임플란트주위 열구액(Peri-implant Sulcular Fluid, PISF)의 인터루킨-6(Interleukin-6, IL-6)는 3개월간 양 군 모두 유의한 증가 없음)
(줄3: 임상적으로는 건조 면 대신 폴리테트라플루오로에틸렌(Polytetrafluoroethylene, PTFE) 또는 1% 클로르헥시딘(CHX) 침지 면을 SAH 충전재로 사용해 구강 상주균 집락화·구취/이상미각을 줄일 것을 권고하나, 균종 동정 없이 5개월로 추적기간이 제한된 한계가 있음)

- **Journal**: International Journal of Applied & Basic Medical Research 2025;15(4):265-270

## 2. Key Contributions
- Direct in vivo (patient-level) comparison of three SAH plugging materials (cotton, PTFE, cotton+1% CHX) using a common cotton-baseline-then-randomized crossover-like design within the same patients.
- Quantifies both aerobic and anaerobic bacterial colony counts and positive-sample rates for each material, not just a binary leakage outcome.
- Adds a biochemical endpoint (IL-6 in PISF) to test whether microbiologic contamination translates into measurable peri-implant inflammation at 3 months.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: Prospective, single-center, randomized (lottery method) parallel-group clinical trial (rct)
- **Databases**: N/A (primary clinical study, not a review)
- **n**: 35 patients / 45 implants enrolled; 4 patients lost to follow-up; 40 sites with cotton baseline data, 20 each randomized to PTFE and Co1%CHX
- **Outcomes**: Primary — presence/colony count of aerobic and anaerobic bacteria cultured from retrieved SAH plugging material at 1 month; Secondary — IL-6 concentration in PISF at baseline and 3 months

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
Cotton vs PTFE (n=20 each): aerobic positive samples 90% vs 40% (p=0.01), aerobic colony count 16.10±13.69 vs 0.45±0.60 (p=0.000); anaerobic positive samples 95% vs 50% (p=0.001), anaerobic colony count 13.10±23.60 vs 0.8±1.20 (p=0.03).

Cotton vs Co1%CHX (n=20 each): aerobic positive samples 65% vs 15% (p=0.001), aerobic colony count 10.45±18.88 vs 0.20±0.52 (p=0.02); anaerobic positive samples 70% vs 20% (p=0.001), anaerobic colony count 9.05±20.13 vs 0.2±0.41 (p=0.05).

Co1%CHX vs PTFE (n=20 each): aerobic positive samples 15% vs 40% (p=0.07, NS), aerobic colony count 0.20±0.52 vs 0.45±0.60 (p=0.17, NS); anaerobic positive samples 20% vs 50% (p=0.04), anaerobic colony count 0.2±0.41 vs 0.8±1.20 (p=0.04) — Co1%CHX significantly better than PTFE for anaerobic bacteria only.

IL-6 in PISF: PTFE group 256.30±60.94 → 261.30±69.50 pg/ml (p=0.79); Co1%CHX group 227.06±49.63 → 243.99±71.16 pg/ml (p=0.17) — neither change statistically significant. No implant failures observed.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- No qualitative/species-level identification of the bacteria cultured (Gram stain and colony morphology only, no molecular/species-level ID)
- Follow-up limited to 5 months from final restoration placement — long-term contamination/inflammation trajectory unknown
- Cotton phase was not randomized (used as a universal first-visit baseline for all patients before randomization to PTFE/Co1%CHX), so cotton comparisons are within-patient before/after rather than fully parallel-randomized

## 7. Glossary
- **SAH (Screw Access Hole)**: the channel through the prosthetic crown that provides access to the abutment/prosthetic screw, typically sealed coronally with a plugging material and composite restoration
- **IAJ (Implant-Abutment Junction)**: the micro-gap interface between the implant fixture and abutment, a recognized site of bacterial microleakage
- **PTFE (Polytetrafluoroethylene)**: an inert, pliable synthetic tape material used to plug the SAH prior to composite sealing
- **PISF (Peri-implant Sulcular Fluid)**: the crevicular fluid at the peri-implant sulcus, sampled here to measure local inflammatory biomarkers
- **IL-6 (Interleukin-6)**: a pro-inflammatory cytokine biomarker measured in PISF as a proxy for peri-implant inflammatory response
