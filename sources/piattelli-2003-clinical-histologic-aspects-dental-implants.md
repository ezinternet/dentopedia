## Why Ingested

Ingested as the largest published human histological series of retrieved failed implants (n=51), providing ground-section evidence for the fibrous connective tissue interface that characterizes late osseointegration failure. The hallmark finding — compact, highly mineralized surrounding bone with **few areas of remodeling** — is mechanistically consistent with the overcompression/overload pathway (mechanical trauma prevents remodeling from proceeding), contrasting with the osteolytic/inflammatory remodeling seen in peri-implantitis. Supports [[wiki/overviews/implant-failure-mechanism-xray-differential-overview]] Mechanism 2 differential and the late-failure histology axis.

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective histological examination of 51 root-form implants retrieved for mobility over 5 years; ground sections showed a uniform 600–1,100 µm thick fibrous connective tissue layer between every implant and surrounding bone, with compact, highly mineralized bone and few remodeling areas — and no bacteria at the coronal implant surface.

The histological pattern is consistent with mechanical overload/trauma to bone as the primary late-failure pathway: compact bone with minimal remodeling suggests osseointegration occurred but was disrupted by stress, producing a fibrous encapsulation rather than the osteolytic/pocket pattern of peri-implantitis.

Authors conclude late osseointegrated implant failures are multifactorial (poor bone quality + mechanical trauma + overloading forces), with the fibrous encapsulation serving as the histological endpoint of mechanical failure.

## 세줄요약

5년간 동요도(Mobility)로 제거된 근관형 임플란트 51개의 후향적 조직학 검사: 전 증례에서 임플란트와 주위골 사이 두께 600–1,100 µm의 섬유성 결합조직(fibrous connective tissue) 확인, 주위골은 치밀·고석회화로 골개조(remodeling) 흔적 희박.

임플란트 상방부에서 세균 미검출, 일부 표본에서 결합조직 내 희박한 염증세포; 전 증례에서 임플란트 주위를 에워싸는 섬유성 피막이 기계적 과부하 기전의 조직학적 종착점을 시사.

저자들은 골유착된 임플란트의 지연 실패가 불량 골질·기계적 외상·과부하의 복합요인으로 발생한다고 결론 — 이 조직 패턴은 골흡수+포켓 형성의 임플란트주위염 조직상과 대비됨.

## Key Contributions

- Largest (n=51) human histological ground-section series of retrieved mobility-failed implants.
- Establishes the **fibrous connective tissue layer** (600–1,100 µm) as the universal histological finding in late osseointegration failure — a distinct pattern from early thermal necrosis (bone sequestra + no regeneration) or peri-implantitis (osteolysis + deep pocket).
- **No bacteria at coronal implant portion** distinguishes this late mechanical failure from infection-driven failure.
- Compact, minimally remodeled surrounding bone confirms that osseointegration had been established before failure — consistent with mechanical overload/overcompression disrupting a previously osseointegrated interface.
- Validates "mobility" as a reliable marker of late osseointegration loss rather than of peri-implantitis per se.

## Methodology

Retrospective case series over 5 years. 51 root-form implants retrieved for clinical mobility. Ground histological sections of each retrieved specimen evaluated for: connective tissue layer thickness and characteristics, bone structure (Haversian canals, remodeling activity), bacterial presence, inflammatory infiltrate, epithelial migration.

## Key Results

| Histological Finding | Result |
|---|---|
| Fibrous connective tissue layer | All specimens; 600–1,100 µm thick |
| Surrounding bone type | Compact, highly mineralized |
| Haversian canal structure | Well-structured in all |
| Areas of bone remodeling | Few |
| Bacteria at coronal implant surface | None |
| Inflammatory infiltrate in connective tissue | Scarce (some specimens) |
| Epithelial migration | ~10% of specimens tended to surround implant perimeter |

## Limitations

- Abstract-only ingest; Haversian canal measurements, connective tissue composition details, and per-case clinical data not recoverable.
- No comparison group; cannot attribute to a specific pathway without clinical context for each case.
- Retrospective; no standardized data on insertion torque, bone density class, or loading protocol.
- n=51 is the largest series to date but still a single-center convenience sample.

## Glossary

- **Fibrous encapsulation**: formation of a fibrous connective tissue layer between implant and bone, replacing osseointegration — the histological endpoint of mechanical failure
- **Haversian canal system**: the vascular channel network of cortical bone; well-structured canals indicate established mature cortical bone, not inflamed/remodeling tissue
- **Ground section**: a histological preparation technique for mineralized tissue (implant + bone) that does not require decalcification — the standard method for implant histomorphometry
