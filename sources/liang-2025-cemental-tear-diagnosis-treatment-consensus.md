## Three-line Summary

Multi-institution Chinese expert consensus (Int J Oral Sci 2025) synthesizing epidemiology, pathogenesis, predisposing factors, diagnosis, differential diagnosis (vs VRF / endodontic / periodontal disease), and a location-based treatment decision tree for cemental tear, whose central principle is complete removal of torn cementum fragments.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First standardized expert-consensus clinical guideline dedicated to cemental tear, an under-recognized entity often misdiagnosed as VRF, endodontic, or periodontal disease.
- Consolidates the histologic basis (cemento-dentinal junction weakness; acellular vs cellular cementum; CDJ proteoglycan-rich hygroscopic layer 10–50 µm) into an internal-vs-external pathogenesis model.
- Tabulates seven possible predisposing factors (age >60, male predominance, systemic conditions e.g. aplastic anemia/malnutrition, tooth type, occlusal trauma, periodontitis history, dental trauma history) with explicit Oxford CEBM Level 4 caveat.
- Proposes a location-based treatment decision tree (cervical vs middle vs apical third) tying fragment removal to periodontal/apical surgery and intrabony-defect–specific regenerative choices (EMD/GTR/bone graft by depth, width, wall count).
- Quantifies prognosis by fragment location: healing 60% (coronal third), 66.7% (middle third), 11.1% (apical third); 94% functional retention at 1 year after complete removal.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Design: narrative expert-consensus review; not a systematic review or meta-analysis. Evidence base is dominated by case reports and small retrospective series.
- Scope: epidemiology, cementum histology, pathogenesis/predisposing factors, clinical + radiographic + histopathologic manifestations, diagnosis, differential diagnosis, treatment modalities, and outcomes.
- Diagnostic hierarchy defined: history → clinical exam (occlusal, periodontal probing for ledge-like root projections, pulp vitality) → auxiliary (radiograph/CBCT, methylene blue staining) → histopathology (gold standard).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Prevalence: 0.89% (periapical radiographs, 4,629 teeth, Keskin et al.) to 1.9% (813 CBCT images, Özkan et al.), Turkey; radiographic-only diagnosis, no clinical confirmation — likely imprecise.
- Cementum thickness: cervical ~20–50 µm, apex ~150–200 µm; elderly cementum 3–5× thicker than youth.
- Fragment detachment site: CDJ 77.6% vs within cementum 22.4%. Surrounding soft tissue: granulation 92.3%, cystic 7.7%.
- Associated lesions: periodontal ~86%, periapical ~65% of cases.
- Gender: male 77.5% vs female 22.5% (Lin multicenter, 71 teeth); 63% male in Lee's 41-case review; NS in Keskin/Özkan.
- Outcomes: non-surgical cure only 28.6%; surgical healing 57.7%; 94% 1-year functional retention after complete fragment removal; location-based healing 60% / 66.7% / 11.1% (coronal/middle/apical third).
- Regenerative decision thresholds: intrabony depth <3 mm → resective; ≥3 mm → EMD/GTR. Apical regeneration favored if periapical defect ≥10 mm diameter or residual buccal plate ≤3 mm.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Consensus rests on Level 4 evidence (case reports/retrospective series); no RCTs or high-quality cohorts exist for cemental tear diagnosis or treatment.
- Prevalence data from a single country (Turkey) via radiography alone; true incidence unknown.
- Predisposing factors are theoretically plausible but clinically unvalidated; gender/tooth-type findings conflict across studies.
- Treatment decision tree and regenerative thresholds are extrapolated from general periodontal-regeneration evidence, not cemental-tear–specific trials.
- Calls for larger multi-region studies with combined clinical + CBCT + histopathologic confirmation.

## 6. Related Work

- Schroeder's cementum classification (AAC/AEFC/CMSC/CIFC) underpins the histology section.
- Lee et al. classification of cemental tear and corresponding treatment strategies (recent, cited as the closest prior framework).
- Lin et al. multicenter retrospective series (Taiwan, 71 teeth); Keskin et al. and Özkan et al. radiographic prevalence studies.
- General periodontal intrabony-defect regeneration literature (EMD, GTR, bone grafts) informing the surgical decision tree.

## 7. Glossary

- **Cemental tear**: partial/complete detachment of cementum along the cemento-dentinal junction (CDJ) or along incremental lines within the cementum body; a root-surface fracture involving cementum.
- **Cemento-dentinal tear**: cemental tear that additionally involves dentin — more severe, may render the tooth non-retainable.
- **CDJ (cemento-dentinal junction)**: 100–200 µm interface attaching cementum to dentin, containing a 10–50 µm proteoglycan-rich hygroscopic layer; a structural weak point.
- **AAC / AEFC / CMSC / CIFC**: acellular afibrillar / acellular extrinsic-fiber / cellular mixed stratified / cellular intrinsic-fiber cementum (Schroeder).
- **VRF (vertical root fracture)**: key differential; vertical separation of root structure, usually root-filled teeth, poor prognosis.
- **EMD / GTR**: enamel matrix derivative / guided tissue regeneration — regenerative options for intrabony defects.
- **Intentional replantation**: extraction, extraoral fragment removal, and replantation — option when fragment access is difficult or near critical anatomy.
