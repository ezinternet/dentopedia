## Three-line Summary

ADA expert panel systematic review of 95 publications (2010–2022) synthesized evidence-based recommendations on dental radiation safety, imaging optimization, and regulatory compliance for all dental imaging modalities including CBCT.
Priority recommendations: use digital sensors (eliminates need for film), rectangular collimation (>40% dose reduction), minimum necessary FOV for CBCT, and strict ALARA/ALADA principles; CBCT delivers 10–15× the dose of conventional radiography.
Clinicians must adhere to applicable federal/state regulations; CBCT should never replace lower-dose modalities when adequate diagnostic information can be obtained otherwise.

## 세줄요약

ADA 전문가 패널이 95편(2010–2022) 체계적 문헌고찰로 치과 방사선 안전·영상 최적화·규정 준수 권고안을 제시한 2024년 JADA 가이드라인.
최우선 권고: 디지털 센서 사용(필름 대비 선량 대폭 감소), 직사각형 콜리메이션(40% 이상 선량 감소), 콘빔 전산화단층촬영 (CBCT, Cone-beam Computed Tomography) 최소 시야각(FOV), ALARA/ALADA 원칙 준수; CBCT는 기존 방사선 촬영의 10–15배 선량.
CBCT는 저선량 촬영으로 동등한 진단 정보를 얻을 수 없을 때만 사용; 모든 술자는 연방·주 규정 및 OSHA/FDA 지침을 준수해야 한다.

## Key Contributions

- Comprehensive effective dose table: FMS adult digital/rect collimation = 34.9 μSv; bitewing digital single = 0.3–1.4 μSv; panoramic CCD = 14.2–30.0 μSv; CBCT small FOV adult = 19–652 μSv; CBCT large FOV adult = 68–1,073 μSv
- Priority recommendations explicitly flagged: (1) ALARA compliance, (2) digital sensors, (3) rectangular collimation, (4) CBCT only when necessary, (5) smallest FOV for CBCT
- Distinguishes regulations (OSHA, FDA, state) from professional recommendations (ADA, NCRP, ICRP)
- Handheld FDA-cleared devices: must follow specific handling guidance; backscatter shield essential
- Rectangular collimation reduces dose >40% consistently; F-speed film gives 60% reduction vs D-speed
- CBCT: annual dosimetry evaluation recommended (mandatory every 2 years); QA/QC protocols detailed
- Pediatric patients: higher organ radiosensitivity + longer lifespan = greater cumulative risk; size/age must guide prescription

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review of systematic reviews, organizational guidelines, and regulatory literature; expert panel consensus
- **Databases**: Ovid MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews (August 2020 + April 2022 update)
- **Inclusion**: Publications from 2010 onward relevant to primary oral health care
- **Gray literature**: Technical reports, white papers, position statements, federal regulations (CFR)
- **N included**: 95 articles/guidance documents/regulations (74 available for qualitative synthesis after dedup)
- **Panel composition**: General, public health, and pediatric dentists; oral and maxillofacial radiologists; health physicist; patient representative; ADA staff

**Key recommendations (Box 1 – priority items):**
1. Adhere to all applicable federal, state, and local regulations (1.0.1)
2. Use digital sensors over film for intraoral imaging (3.1.1.0)
3. Use rectangular collimation whenever possible (3.1.2) — >40% dose reduction
4. Never use CBCT routinely when lower-dose modality sufficient (3.2.1)
5. Use smallest FOV for CBCT (3.2.2)
6. CBCT dosimetry performance evaluated by qualified expert ≥every 2 years, preferably annually (4.1.2)

**CBCT vs conventional:** CBCT delivers 10–15× the dose of traditional radiography.

**Regulatory framework:** OSHA (occupational), FDA (device performance), state radiation control programs, NCRP, ICRP 103.

## Limitations

- Systematic search limited to 2010 onward; earlier foundational studies not included
- Gray literature search may miss some national/international regulations
- Dose values represent averages/ranges; individual clinical variation substantial
- No original data; synthesis of existing SRs and guidance documents

## Glossary

- **ALARA** (As Low As Reasonably Achievable): Principle minimizing radiation exposure while maintaining diagnostic utility
- **ALADA** (As Low As Diagnostically Acceptable): CBCT-specific evolution of ALARA, emphasizing diagnostic necessity
- **Effective dose (mSv/μSv)**: Radiation dose weighted for tissue radiosensitivity; allows comparison across modalities
- **Rectangular collimation**: Beam restriction to the receptor size, reducing scatter and patient dose by >40%
- **FOV** (Field of View): Volume of tissue captured in CBCT; smallest FOV = lowest dose
- **NCRP** (National Council on Radiation Protection and Measurements): US advisory body issuing radiation safety standards
