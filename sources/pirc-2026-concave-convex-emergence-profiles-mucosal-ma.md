## Three-line Summary

SR+MA, 4 RCTs (144 implants), MEDLINE/Embase through May 2026, PROSPERO CRD420251139042 — comparing concave vs convex implant emergence profiles for midfacial mucosal stability at 12 months.

Concave emergence profiles showed a trend toward less midfacial mucosal recession vs convex (MD −0.31 mm, p=0.064, I²=28%); sensitivity analysis excluding one study achieved statistical significance (p=0.002) with consistent direction across all iterations.

Only 4 RCTs available; all rated "some concerns" for risk of bias; primary analysis is borderline non-significant; result robust on sensitivity analysis but replication with larger, standardized trials is needed.

## 세줄요약

SR+MA, 4편 RCT (144개 임플란트), MEDLINE/Embase 2026년 5월까지 — 오목형 vs 볼록형 임플란트 이행윤곽의 12개월 midfacial 점막 안정성 비교.

오목형 이행윤곽이 볼록형 대비 midfacial 점막 퇴축 적은 경향 (MD −0.31 mm, p=0.064, I²=28%); 민감도 분석에서 통계적 유의성 달성 (p=0.002).

4편 RCT만 가용; 전부 "일부 우려" 비뚤림위험; 1차 분석 경계 비유의 — 표준화된 대규모 시험으로 검증 필요.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First meta-analysis specifically addressing midfacial mucosal stability as primary outcome for emergence profile design comparisons
- Pooled 4 RCTs to show consistent directional trend (concave > convex) despite borderline significance in primary analysis
- Sensitivity analysis (leave-one-out) confirms robustness: direction consistent across all iterations; significance achieved when Barwacz 2025 (minimal morphological contrast study) excluded
- Low heterogeneity (I²=28%) confirms biological consistency across study settings
- Biological mechanism proposed: concave profiles provide "running room" for soft tissue accommodation

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- Study type: Systematic review + meta-analysis of RCTs
- Databases: MEDLINE (PubMed), Embase (search to May 7, 2026)
- PICO: partially edentulous patients / implant-supported single crowns; concave EP (I) vs convex EP (C); midfacial mucosal level changes in mm (O); RCTs only
- Risk of bias: RoB 2 tool — all 4 studies rated "some concerns" (none low, none high)
- Statistics: random-effects MA; weighted mean differences; REML for heterogeneity; leave-one-out sensitivity analysis

**Included studies:**

|---|---|---|---|

| Siegenthaler et al. 2022 | 44 | Anterior mandible/maxilla | 12 mo |
| Wang et al. 2022 | 20 | Mandibular molar | 12 mo |

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Primary meta-analysis:**
- MD = -0.31 mm (95% CI -0.63 to 0.02; p = 0.064)
- I² = 28%; not significant test for heterogeneity (p=0.27)
- Interpretation: trend toward greater midfacial recession with convex profiles; borderline significance

**Sensitivity analysis (leave-one-out):**
- Range: -0.12 to -0.43 mm; direction consistently favors concave
- Excluding Barwacz 2025: MD = -0.43 mm (95% CI -0.70 to -0.16; p = 0.002) — **statistically significant**
- Excluding Wang 2022 (largest weight): MD = -0.12 mm (p=0.548) — effect attenuated

**Clinical conclusion:** Concave emergence profiles preferred for midfacial mucosal stability; convex profiles associated with approximately 0.3 mm greater recession

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Only 4 RCTs; small sample sizes; 12-month follow-up only
- EP designs not standardized across studies; Barwacz compared concave vs linearly divergent (minimal contrast)
- Heterogeneity in abutment design, height, implant systems, outcome assessment methods
- All studies rated "some concerns" for risk of bias — allocation concealment and missing data issues
- Longer-term RCTs with standardized EP definitions needed

## 7. Glossary

- **Emergence profile (EP)**: Contour of restoration transitioning from implant platform through peri-implant mucosa to crown cervix
- **Midfacial mucosal level**: Buccal soft tissue margin position relative to reference point; regression = recession
- **Concave EP**: Inwardly curved transmucosal contour providing space for soft tissue
- **Convex EP**: Outwardly curved contour that may compress soft tissue and reduce vascularized connective tissue space
- **REML**: Restricted Maximum Likelihood — heterogeneity estimation method preferred over DerSimonian-Laird for small study sets
