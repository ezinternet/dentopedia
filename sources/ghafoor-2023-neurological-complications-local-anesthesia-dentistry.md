## Why Ingested

기존 위키는 국소마취(LA) 효능·기법·약리학은 두텁게 다루지만, 안면신경 (Facial Nerve, CN VII) 마비와 안구 합병증(복시·안검하수·실명) 경로는 다루는 페이지가 없다. 이 Cureus 리뷰는 [[wiki/local-anesthesia/becker-2012-local-anesthetics-pharmacological-considerations]]의 약리학적 기반에 신경학적 합병증 축을 추가하고, [[wiki/local-anesthesia/hwang-2023-proposal-clinical-guidelines-local-anesthesia-dentistry]]가 지적한 표준화 공백 중 신경 합병증 인지·대응 영역을 구체화한다.

## Three-line Summary

Narrative (non-systematic) review of neurological complications following dental local anesthesia, organized by nerve — ophthalmic (CN V1), maxillary (CN V2), mandibular (CN V3), and facial (CN VII).

Documents ocular complications (diplopia, ptosis, transient/permanent blindness) via inadvertent intravascular spread through the middle meningeal → ophthalmic artery pathway, mandibular-block paresthesia (7.3x risk with 4% articaine per Garisto et al.), and facial nerve palsy (Bell's palsy) as the most frequent neurological complication after IANB, with immediate (parotid-gland anatomic) vs delayed (sympathetic vasospasm/viral reactivation) mechanisms.

Cureus narrative review with no systematic search strategy or PRISMA methodology — findings are mechanism/case-report-level awareness points, not incidence estimates suitable for informed-consent quantification.

## 세줄요약

치과 국소마취(LA) 후 신경학적 합병증을 안신경(CN V1)·상악신경(CN V2)·하악신경(CN V3)·안면신경(CN VII)별로 정리한 서술적(비체계적) 리뷰.

안구 합병증(복시·안검하수·일과성/영구 실명 — 중뇌막동맥→안동맥 경로의 우발적 혈관내 확산), 하악블록 감각이상(4% 아티카인 시 위험 7.3배, Garisto 등), IANB 후 가장 흔한 신경학적 합병증인 안면신경마비(벨마비, 즉시형=이하선 해부학적 변이·지연형=교감신경혈관연축/바이러스재활성화)를 다룸.

체계적 검색전략·PRISMA 방법론이 없는 Cureus 서술적 리뷰 — 발생률 추정치가 아닌 기전·사례 수준의 인지 포인트로 취급해야 함.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Organizes dental-LA neurological complications by the affected nerve (ophthalmic, maxillary, mandibular, facial) rather than by symptom, giving a clean differential-diagnosis map for post-injection neurological signs.
- Proposes a vascular anatomic mechanism for ocular complications: inadvertent intravascular injection into the inferior alveolar artery → retrograde flow into the maxillary artery → middle meningeal artery → anastomosis with the ophthalmic artery's territory (lacrimal artery / central retinal artery), explaining diplopia, ophthalmoplegia, and blindness after intraoral injection (typically IANB or PSAN block).
- Distinguishes immediate-onset vs delayed-onset facial nerve palsy (FNP) after IANB, with separate proposed mechanisms for each (anatomic/direct LA spread into the parotid gland vs sympathetic vasospasm/latent viral reactivation days later).
- Summarizes paresthesia risk stratification by anesthetic concentration (4% articaine/prilocaine vs lower-concentration agents), consistent with the pharmacology literature already in the wiki.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Narrative literature review; no PICO framework, no database search strategy, no PRISMA flow, no risk-of-bias assessment reported. Synthesizes case reports, case series, and older narrative sources (e.g., Hillerup, Garisto, Rishiraj, Penarrocha-Diago, Ngeow) organized anatomically by cranial nerve and its branches. Includes anatomic figures (trigeminal/facial nerve distribution) and two summary tables (direct ocular effects; periorbital effects).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- Overall ocular unfavorable-outcome prevalence after dental LA: **0.07%-0.09%**.
- Peripheral nerve block neurological complication incidence generally **<3%**, mostly transient; persistent injury **0.02%-0.5%**.
- Permanent nerve injury from mandibular LA: **0.000007%-0.003%**.
- Hillerup et al.: 54 cases of CN V injury after IANB — lingual nerve **77.8%**, IAN **22.2%**.
- Garisto et al.: lingual/IAN paresthesia distribution **89%/11%**; elevated protracted-paresthesia risk with **4% articaine and 4% prilocaine** vs lower-concentration agents.
- Needle fracture prevalence: **0.000007%** (Pogrel).
- Bell's palsy (FNP) identified as the **most frequent neurological complication after IANB**; immediate-onset cases typically resolve within **~3 hours** (some sources cite up to 7 hours); delayed-onset cases can take days and longer to recover.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Non-systematic: no defined search strategy, inclusion/exclusion criteria, or quality appraisal — publication-bias-prone case-report aggregation.
- Most cited incidence figures derive from small case series or single institutional studies, not population-level surveillance; true population incidence remains poorly characterized (the review itself notes multiple studies give divergent numbers depending on measurement technique).
- Mechanisms for ocular and delayed facial-palsy complications are proposed/inferred anatomic hypotheses, not experimentally confirmed causal pathways.
- No discussion of prevention protocol effectiveness (e.g., aspiration technique adherence rates) beyond general recommendation.

## 7. Glossary

- **IANB**: Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block.
- **PSAN**: Posterior Superior Alveolar Nerve.
- **MMA**: Middle Meningeal Artery.
- **CRA**: Central Retinal Artery.
- **FNP**: Facial Nerve Palsy (Bell's palsy).
- **TNS**: Transient Neurological Symptoms.
- **EMLA**: Eutectic Mixture of Local Anesthetics (2.5% lidocaine + 2.5% prilocaine).
