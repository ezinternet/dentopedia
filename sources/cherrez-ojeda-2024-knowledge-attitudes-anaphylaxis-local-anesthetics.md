## Why Ingested

기존 위키는 국소마취제 (Local Anesthetic, LA) 알레르기·아나필락시스를 [[wiki/local-anesthesia/bina-2018-true-allergy-amide-local-anesthetics-case]] 한 편(진성 아마이드 알레르기 증례·대체 프로토콜)으로만 다루고, "치과의사가 실제로 이 응급을 다룰 준비가 되어 있는가"라는 축은 비어 있었다. 이 횡단연구는 라틴아메리카 치과의사 480명에서 준비도 격차를 정량화한다 — 아나필락시스 관리 자신감 9.6%, 응급키트 에피네프린 보유 43.5%, 올바른 근육주사 (Intramuscular, IM) 경로 인지 50.1%. [[wiki/local-anesthesia/hwang-2023-proposal-clinical-guidelines-local-anesthesia-dentistry]]가 지적한 국소마취 표준진료지침 공백의 임상적 결과(응급 대비 미비)를 실증하고, [[wiki/local-anesthesia/bina-2018-true-allergy-amide-local-anesthetics-case]]가 언급한 "치과의사가 충분한 평가 없이 성급하게 마취제 알레르기를 진단한다"는 문제를 설문 수치로 뒷받침한다.

## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional web survey (QuestionPro, snowball sampling, Nov 2021–Feb 2022) of board-certified dentists across 10 Latin American countries; 704 reached, 507 completed (72.0% response), 480 analyzed after excluding 15 students and 12 practicing outside the region; 81.9% from Ecuador, mean age 35 (SD 10), 59.3% female, ~10 y mean experience.

Lidocaine was the most-used LA (88.1%); 85.2% identified dyspnea as an anaphylaxis symptom but only 56.7% named epinephrine as the drug of choice, 50.1% knew the correct intramuscular route, 43.5% stocked epinephrine in their emergency kit (22.1% had none of the listed emergency drugs), and 79.0% did not use a test dose; confidence in managing anaphylaxis was 9.6% and in administering epinephrine 9.4%, despite 63.3% rating anaphylaxis as extremely important.

Older age and more years of experience were inversely associated with both anaphylaxis-management knowledge (age OR 0.97, 95% CI 0.96–0.99, p=0.003; experience OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.94–0.98, p=0.0001) and office epinephrine possession (age OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.94–0.98, p=0.0001); the perceived-importance vs actual-readiness gap points to a need for hands-on emergency training, though the Ecuador-heavy sample and an unvalidated instrument limit generalizability.

## 세줄요약

횡단 웹 설문(QuestionPro, 눈덩이 표집, 2021년 11월–2022년 2월): 라틴아메리카 10개국 전문의 치과의사 대상, 704명 도달·507명 완료(응답률 72.0%)·학생 15명 및 역외 근무 12명 제외 후 480명 분석; 81.9%가 에콰도르, 평균 연령 35세(SD 10), 여성 59.3%, 평균 경력 약 10년.

리도카인 (Lidocaine)이 최다 사용 LA(88.1%); 호흡곤란 (Dyspnea)을 아나필락시스 증상으로 인지 85.2%지만 에피네프린 (Epinephrine)을 1차 약제로 지목 56.7%·올바른 근육주사 (IM) 경로 인지 50.1%·응급키트 에피네프린 보유 43.5%(응급약 전무 22.1%)·시험용량 (Test Dose) 미시행 79.0%; 아나필락시스가 매우 중요하다는 응답 63.3%임에도 관리 자신감 9.6%·에피네프린 투여 자신감 9.4%.

고령·긴 경력은 아나필락시스 관리 지식(연령 OR 0.97, 95% CI 0.96–0.99, p=0.003; 경력 OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.94–0.98, p=0.0001)과 진료실 에피네프린 보유(연령 OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.94–0.98, p=0.0001) 모두와 역상관; 인식과 실제 대비도의 격차는 실습 중심 응급교육 필요를 시사하나 에콰도르 편중 표본·미검증 설문도구가 일반화를 제한한다.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: BDJ Open 2024;10(1):28
- **DOI**: 10.1038/s41405-024-00210-x
- **PMID / PMCID**: 38575579 / PMC10994913
- **Lead institution**: Universidad Espíritu Santo, Samborondón, Ecuador (multi-country collaboration)
- **Design**: cross-sectional web survey, 26 structured multiple-choice items, snowball sampling via WhatsApp/email; data Nov 8 2021 – Feb 28 2022
- **Setting**: Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Costa Rica, Ecuador, Honduras, Mexico, Peru, Venezuela, and other Latin American countries
- **Analysis**: Stata 17.0; chi-square, univariate + multivariate logistic regression; 2-tailed p<0.05

## 2. Key Contributions
- Quantifies an emergency-preparedness gap for LA anaphylaxis among practising dentists: only 9.6% feel confident managing it and 43.5% stock epinephrine, against 63.3% who rate it extremely important.
- Documents dangerous route misconception: only 50.1% know epinephrine is intramuscular, and ~30% of oral/maxillofacial surgeons would give it intravenously (higher overdose / cardiovascular risk).
- Identifies an inverse age/experience gradient — older, more experienced dentists scored lower on management knowledge and epinephrine possession, inverting the usual assumption that experience improves readiness.
- Shows prior exposure to a systemic adverse reaction (21.3% of respondents) did not raise management confidence, suggesting experience alone does not substitute for structured training.
- Notes the surveyed countries have no federal minimum standard for dental-office emergency equipment/training.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Design**: cross-sectional, web-based (QuestionPro), 26 structured multiple-choice questions, ~8 min completion; three sections (LA practice; anaphylaxis knowledge/awareness; attitudes).
- **Sampling**: snowball via social media (WhatsApp) and email; introductory message + survey URL.
- **Inclusion**: board-certified dentist practising in Latin America. **Exclusion**: dental students, practitioners outside Latin America, incomplete responses.
- **Respondents**: 704 reached → 507 completed (72.0%) → 27 excluded (15 students, 12 non-regional) → 480 analyzed.
- **Demographics**: mean age 35 (SD 10); 59.3% female; general dentists 49.2%; 45.0% specialists (prosthodontics and endodontics 19.2% each of that subgroup); mean experience ~10 y (SD 9); private practice 66.3%; urban 78.2%; Ecuador 81.9%, Colombia 5.4%, Honduras 4.2%, Mexico 2.1%.
- **Analysis**: chi-square; univariate then multivariate logistic regression (predictors of drug-of-choice knowledge, office epinephrine possession, confidence in identifying at-risk patients).
- **Ethics**: approved by CEISH (Comité de Ética e Investigación en Seres Humanos); informed consent; Declaration of Helsinki.

|---|---|
| Lidocaine first choice | 88.1% |
| Mepivacaine | 40.8% |
| Articaine | 29.0% |
| Prefer LA + epinephrine adjuvant | 60.2% |

**Anaphylaxis knowledge**

|---|---|

| Know epinephrine is the drug of choice | 56.7% |
| Know correct route is intramuscular | 50.1% |
| Epinephrine in emergency kit | 43.5% |
| Antihistamines in emergency kit | 53.1% |

**Attitudes**
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Anaphylaxis "extremely important" | 63.3% |

| Strong confidence identifying anaphylaxis | 28.7% |
| Confident managing an anaphylactic reaction | 9.6% |

**Regression (statistically significant associations)**
| Predictor / outcome | OR (95% CI), p |
|---|---|
| Dyspnea recognition → better drug-of-choice knowledge (univariate) | OR 1.73 (1.04–2.87), p=0.034 |
| Age → drug-of-choice knowledge (uni & multivariate) | OR 0.97 (0.96–0.99), p=0.003 (inverse) |
| Years of experience → drug-of-choice knowledge | OR 0.96 (0.94–0.98), p=0.0001 (inverse) |
| Age → office epinephrine possession (uni & multivariate) | OR 0.96 (0.94–0.98), p=0.0001 (inverse) |
| Confidence using epinephrine → office epinephrine possession (multivariate) | OR 2.84 (1.68–4.80), p=0.0001 |
| Male sex → confidence identifying at-risk patients (multivariate) | OR 1.75 (1.15–2.67), p=0.009 |

**Regional**: no significant Ecuador-vs-rest difference in knowledge/attitudes overall (p>0.05), but Ecuadorian dentists more confident managing anaphylaxis (49.1% vs 31.1%, p=0.002) and using epinephrine (45.8% vs 27.6%, p=0.002). ~30% of OMF surgeons would choose the intravenous route for epinephrine.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Sample dominated by Ecuador (81.9%) — limited generalizability across Latin America.
- Respondents were told the study aim → potential response bias.
- Survey instrument not formally validated → replicability concern.
- Snowball sampling → non-probability sample, unknown selection bias.
- Cross-sectional self-report; no objective competency (e.g., simulation) assessment.
- Authors call for educational interventions and for state-level minimum standards on dental-office emergency equipment/training.

## 6. Related Work
- Bina 2018 (amide LA true allergy, case + review): true amide allergy is rare and most "LA allergy" is misattributed — this survey shows the corresponding management-side deficit.
- Hwang 2023 (Korean LA clinical-guideline proposal): argues standardized LA guidance is missing; this survey supplies empirical readiness-gap data.
- Ghafoor 2023 (neurological complications of dental LA): companion knowledge-gap review on a different LA complication class.

## 7. Glossary
- **Anaphylaxis**: acute, potentially fatal type I (IgE-mediated) systemic hypersensitivity reaction; features bronchospasm, angioedema, hypotension.
- **Local anesthetic (LA)**: sodium-channel-blocking agent; amides (lidocaine, mepivacaine, articaine, bupivacaine, prilocaine) predominate in dentistry.
- **Epinephrine**: first-line treatment for anaphylaxis; correct emergency route is intramuscular (anterolateral thigh), not intravenous.
- **Test dose**: small pre-treatment aliquot of LA intended to detect hypersensitivity before the full dose (of limited predictive value for IgE-mediated reactions).
- **Metabisulfite / methylparaben**: cartridge additives (antioxidant / preservative) that account for many reactions misattributed to the anesthetic molecule itself.
- **Snowball sampling**: non-probability recruitment where participants recruit further participants from their contacts.
