## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 19 studies (18 cohorts + 1 RCT, n=2,878; searched to August 2015) on 4-factor prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC) for vitamin K antagonist (VKA)-associated bleeding, mostly intracranial haemorrhage.
INR 1 h after PCC was 1.4–1.9 vs 2.2–12 after fresh frozen plasma (FFP); mortality OR 0.64 (0.27–1.5) vs FFP and 0.41 (0.13–1.3) vs no treatment (both NS); thromboembolic events mean 2.5% with PCC vs 6.4% with FFP.
4F-PCC reverses VKA rapidly and with low thrombotic risk, but the evidence is observational (one RCT) and drawn from life-threatening medical bleeding — not dental bleeding, which held cohorts control locally.

## 2. Key Contributions

- First review restricted to **4-factor** PCC (3-factor and activated PCC excluded) in VKA-associated **bleeding** (emergent-procedure reversal excluded).
- Quantifies INR correction speed: PCC brings INR to 1.4–1.9 within 1 h; FFP rarely normalizes INR completely.
- Pools mortality and thromboembolic (TE) harms against FFP and no-treatment comparators.
- Introduction states vitamin K kinetics: IV vitamin K starts lowering INR within **4 h** and normalizes it after **12–16 h**; oral vitamin K takes up to **24 h**. Vitamin K is the "most straightforward" reversal; PCC/FFP are for immediate reversal.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis, PRISMA
- **Databases**: PubMed (1945–Aug 2015), EMBASE, CENTRAL; manual reference search of four reviews
- **n**: 3,962 records → 49 full texts → 19 studies, 2,878 participants; 13 prospective cohorts, 5 retrospective cohorts, 1 RCT
- **Population**: VKA-associated bleeding; intracranial haemorrhage in 15 studies; mean age 67–78
- **Products**: Octaplex (most used), Beriplex/Kcentra, Cofact, Kanokad, Proplex T, PPSB-HT, Kaskadil, Prothromplex; dosing fixed, INR-based, weight-based or combined (typical 25–50 IU/kg; range 5.3–80)
- **Outcomes**: INR normalization, mortality, TE events within 30 days, functional outcome, ICU stay
- **Quality**: 6 good, 9 moderate, 4 poor; Mantel–Haenszel random-effects pooling for mortality

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

| Outcome | PCC | Comparator |
|---|---|---|
| INR 1 h after administration | 1.4–1.9 | FFP 2.2–12 |
| Baseline INR across studies | 2.2 to >20 | — |
| Mortality (observed range, mean) | 0–43% (17%) | FFP 4.8–54% (16%); no treatment 23–69% (51%) |
| Mortality OR, PCC vs FFP | 0.64 (95% CI 0.27–1.5) | NS |
| Mortality OR, PCC vs no treatment | 0.41 (95% CI 0.13–1.3) | NS |
| TE complications | 0–18% (mean 2.5%) | FFP 6.4% |

- PCC shortened time to INR correction versus FFP or no treatment.
- Most included cohorts also gave vitamin K alongside PCC (e.g. 68–100% of PCC recipients in several studies).

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- One RCT; 18 cohorts — no causal inference; more than half had no control group.
- Selection bias: patients with very poor prognosis may have received less aggressive treatment.
- Heterogeneous dosing, INR targets (<1.3–1.6) and outcome reporting.
- High mortality reflects intracranial haemorrhage case-mix; not transferable to minor or dental bleeding.
- Unclear whether TE events stem from PCC or from stopping anticoagulation.

## 7. Glossary

- **4F-PCC (4-factor prothrombin complex concentrate)**: plasma-derived concentrate of factors II, VII, IX and X plus proteins C and S.
- **FFP (fresh frozen plasma)**: plasma containing all coagulation factors at low concentration; large volumes needed, volume-overload risk.
- **INR normalization**: correction of the international normalized ratio to a target (here ≤1.3–1.6).
- **TE complication**: thromboembolic event (stroke, MI, VTE) after reversal.
