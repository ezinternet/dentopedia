## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort of dental records (179 subjects, 374 implants) comparing patients reporting penicillin allergy (exposed) with unexposed patients at a 1:2 ratio, for surgical complications, early implant failure and preloading crestal bone loss (PLCBL).

Early failure occurred in 6.1% and surgical complications in 8.0% of implants (PLCBL 32.5%); crude prevalence of both was higher with reported allergy, but regression showed no independent association of allergy with any outcome (p > 0.05), while azithromycin and other alternatives had higher odds of surgical complications versus penicillin in exploratory models (p < 0.05).

Only the abstract was retrieved, so group sizes, effect sizes, the early-failure time window and covariate sets are unverified; the authors call for larger studies.

## 세줄요약

치과 기록 후향적 코호트 (환자 179명, 임플란트 374개): 페니실린 알레르기 자가보고군(노출)과 비보고군을 1:2 비율로 비교해 수술 합병증, 조기 임플란트 실패, 보철 전 치조골 소실 (preloading crestal bone loss, PLCBL)을 평가.

조기 실패 6.1%, 수술 합병증 8.0%, PLCBL 32.5%; 조잡(crude) 유병률은 알레르기 보고군에서 더 높았으나 회귀분석에서는 알레르기가 어떤 결과와도 독립적 연관이 없었고 (p > 0.05), 탐색적 다변량 분석에서 azithromycin 및 기타 대체 항생제는 penicillin 대비 수술 합병증 odds가 높았다 (p < 0.05).

초록만 확보되어 군별 인원, 효과크기, 조기 실패 시간 기준, 보정 변수는 확인되지 않았으며, 저자들은 더 큰 연구가 필요하다고 결론.

## 2. Key Contributions

- Evaluates self-reported penicillin allergy and the alternative antibiotic prescribed as separate exposures against three outcomes: postsurgical complications, early implant failure and PLCBL.
- Early failure 6.1% and surgical complications 8.0% of implants; PLCBL 32.5% of implants.
- Bivariate comparisons favored penicillin (higher prevalence of complications and failure with reported allergy; azithromycin higher complications; azithromycin or clindamycin higher early failure), but adjusted models did not show allergy to be independent.
- Exploratory multivariable models: azithromycin and other alternatives had higher odds of surgical complications vs penicillin (p < 0.05); no significant antibiotic-type association with PLCBL or early failure (p > 0.05).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort from dental records of patients who received at least one dental implant.
- **Exposure**: Self-reported penicillin allergy (exposed) vs no reported allergy (unexposed), selected 1:2 exposed-to-unexposed; antibiotic regimen (penicillin vs alternatives) as a second exposure.
- **Sample**: 179 subjects, 374 implants (split by group not stated in the abstract).
- **Outcomes**: postsurgical complications, PLCBL, early implant failure (time-window definition not stated in the abstract).
- **Analysis**: bivariate analyses and mixed-effects regression models (implant clustering within patient implied by the model type; covariate list not given in the abstract).
- **Setting/country**: not stated in the retrieved text.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Abstract-only: group sizes, effect sizes, CIs, early-failure window, covariates and setting could not be verified.
- Retrospective record-based design; allergy was self-reported and not confirmed by testing (as described in the title/abstract).
- Small sample with few failure events (6.1% of 374 implants); exploratory multivariable models, per the authors.
- Crude and adjusted results diverge, suggesting confounding by patient or implant factors in the crude comparisons; the antibiotic type is also tied to allergy status, so the two exposures are hard to separate.
- Authors call for larger studies.
