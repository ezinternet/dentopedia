## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA 2020, OSF-registered protocol, 21 in-vitro studies, all rated low risk of bias by QUIN) synthesizing how final irrigation protocols and activation methods affect calcium silicate-based sealer (CSS) dentinal tubular penetration in extracted teeth.

NaOCl- and EDTA-based final irrigation protocols were the most frequently investigated and generally associated with more favorable penetration, and activation methods (PUI, laser, sonic, XP-Endo Finisher) generally outperformed conventional irrigation, but no single irrigant, chelating agent, or activation technique could be identified as superior due to substantial methodological heterogeneity across the 21 studies; penetration was consistently greater in coronal/middle thirds than apical third.

Dentinal tubular penetration is a laboratory surrogate outcome not shown to correlate with clinical sealability/success, so these findings should guide protocol emphasis on smear-layer removal and dentin conditioning rather than be extrapolated as proof of superiority of any one irrigation/activation regimen; meta-analysis was not possible.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰(PRISMA 2020, OSF 사전등록, in-vitro 연구 21편, QUIN 기준 전부 low risk of bias)로, 최종 세정 프로토콜과 활성화 방법이 발거치에서 칼슘실리케이트 실러 (Calcium Silicate-based Sealer, CSS)의 상아세관 침투 (Dentinal Tubular Penetration)에 미치는 영향을 종합했다.

NaOCl과 17% EDTA 기반 프로토콜이 가장 많이 연구됐고 대체로 더 나은 침투와 연관됐으며, 활성화 방법(PUI·레이저·소닉·XP-Endo Finisher)이 일반적으로 통상 세정보다 우수했지만, 21편 간 방법론적 이질성이 커서 어떤 세정제·킬레이팅제·활성화 기법도 우월하다고 단정할 수 없었다; 침투는 치관/중간부가 근단부보다 일관되게 우세했다.

상아세관 침투는 임상적 봉쇄성·성공과의 상관관계가 입증되지 않은 실험실 대리 지표(laboratory surrogate outcome)이므로, 이번 소견은 특정 세정·활성화 프로토콜의 우월성 근거가 아니라 도말층 제거와 상아질 표면 컨디셔닝 강조라는 방향성 정도로만 활용해야 한다; 메타분석은 수행하지 못했다.

## Summary

This systematic review (PRISMA 2020, OSF-registered protocol) asked how final irrigation protocols influence calcium silicate-based sealer (CSS) dentinal tubular penetration in extracted teeth. From 82 records screened, 21 in-vitro studies were included, all rated low risk of bias by QUIN. Final irrigation protocols evaluated spanned saline, 17% EDTA, NaOCl combined with HEBP, maleic acid, glycolic acid, apple cider vinegar, chitosan nanoparticles, QMix, citric acid, and chlorhexidine, crossed with activation methods (conventional, PUI, laser-assisted, sonic, XP-Endo Finisher, heat). NaOCl and EDTA were the most frequently investigated and generally associated with favorable penetration, and activation methods generally outperformed conventional irrigation — but which activation approach was best varied by study, and substantial methodological heterogeneity (tooth type, irrigation sequence, sealer formulation, obturation technique, fluorescent dye, microscopy method, outcome parameter) prevented identification of a single superior protocol and precluded meta-analysis. A consistent regional trend emerged: penetration was greater in the coronal/middle thirds than the apical third. The authors caution that dentinal tubular penetration is a laboratory surrogate outcome not established to correlate with clinical sealability or success.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic synthesis (21 in-vitro studies) isolating the effect of **final irrigation protocol** — as distinct from sealer selection — on CSS dentinal tubular penetration.
- Maps the irrigant landscape (NaOCl, 17% EDTA, CHX, citric/maleic/glycolic acid, HEBP, QMix, apple cider vinegar, chitosan nanoparticles) and activation-method landscape (PUI, sonic, laser, XP-Endo Finisher, heat, lentulo, apical negative pressure) tested against CSS penetration.
- Confirms a recurrent coronal/middle > apical third penetration gradient across studies.
- Flags, as a field-wide caution, that dentinal tubular penetration is a laboratory surrogate not shown to correlate with clinical sealability/interfacial adaptation.

## Methodology

- **Protocol**: PRISMA 2020; OSF-registered (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/5HTVN).
- **PICOS**: (P) extracted teeth; (I) root canal treatment with a specific final irrigation method + CSS filling; (C) alternative irrigation method; (O) CSS dentinal tubular penetration/adaptation; (S) in vitro study.
- **Search**: MEDLINE, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, SciELO (October 2025); free-text (no MeSH/Emtree) for cross-database consistency.
- **Screening**: Mendeley dedup → title/abstract → full-text by 2 reviewers, third-reviewer arbitration.
- **Quality**: QUIN 12-item checklist (2/1/0 per item); global score classified low (>70%), moderate (50–70%), high risk (<50%) of bias.

## Results

- **Study flow**: 82 identified → 12 duplicates removed → 52 excluded at first screening → 18 eligible on full-text → +3 via manual reference search → **21 studies included**; all rated low risk of bias by QUIN (though individual domains still showed reporting gaps).
- **Irrigants**: NaOCl and 17% EDTA most studied and generally linked to more favorable penetration; other agents (CHX, citric acid, HEBP, QMix, maleic/glycolic acid, apple cider vinegar, chitosan nanoparticles) too sparsely studied for firm conclusions.
- **Activation**: PUI, laser, sonic, and XP-Endo Finisher generally improved penetration versus conventional irrigation, but the best-performing method was inconsistent across studies (device, activation time, irrigant volume/sequence, sealer formulation, and assessment method all varied).
- **Regional gradient**: penetration was consistently greater in the coronal/middle thirds than the apical third, plausibly related to tubule density, canal diameter, and irrigant-exchange differences apically.
- **No meta-analysis**: outcome heterogeneity (max/mean depth, penetration area/percentage, push-out bond strength, etc.) precluded quantitative pooling — findings are laboratory trends, not high-certainty evidence.
- **Bottom line**: final irrigation protocols that promote smear-layer removal and dentin conditioning (chiefly NaOCl/EDTA-based, optionally activated) are associated with more favorable CSS penetration, but no single irrigant, chelating agent, or activation technique can currently be recommended as superior, and penetration itself is not established as a clinical-success surrogate.
