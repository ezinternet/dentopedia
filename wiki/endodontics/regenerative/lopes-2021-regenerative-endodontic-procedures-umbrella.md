## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PRISMA, 5 databases searched to December 2020) of 29 systematic reviews (20 without meta-analysis, 9 with meta-analysis) on regenerative endodontic procedures (REP) for necrotic immature permanent teeth, appraised with AMSTAR2.

Methodological quality of the 29 SRs was unfavorable — only 2 rated high quality, 14 moderate, 3 low, and 10 critically low; clinical-outcome and stem-cell-research evidence was low quality on average, and REP vs apexification success rates were broadly comparable (e.g., 87.9–100% REP survival vs 90.6–97.1% apexification) but protocols for irrigation, intracanal medication, scaffold, and coronal barrier remain unstandardized.

Clinicians should treat REP protocol specifics (irrigant concentration, TAP/DAP choice, scaffold type, coronal barrier material) as still evidence-immature; histological evidence in the included SRs shows REP produces repair-like (cementum-/bone-like) tissue rather than a true regenerated pulp-dentin complex.

## 세줄요약

우산리뷰(umbrella review, PRISMA, 2020년 12월까지 5개 데이터베이스 검색)로 괴사된 미성숙 영구치 재생근관치료(Regenerative Endodontic Procedure, REP)에 관한 체계적 문헌고찰(Systematic Review, SR) 29편(메타분석 없는 SR 20편 + 메타분석 SR 9편)을 AMSTAR2로 평가.

29편 SR의 방법론적 질은 우호적이지 않아 고품질 2편, 중등도 14편, 저품질 3편, 매우 저품질 10편에 불과했으며, 임상결과 및 줄기세포 연구 근거 수준은 평균적으로 낮았고, REP 대 근단폐쇄술(apexification) 성공률은 대체로 유사했음(예: REP 생존율 87.9–100% vs apexification 90.6–97.1%)에도 세정·근관 내 투약·스캐폴드·치관측 배리어 프로토콜은 여전히 표준화되지 않음.

임상의는 REP 세부 프로토콜(세정액 농도, TAP/DAP 선택, 스캐폴드 종류, 치관측 배리어 재료)을 아직 근거가 미성숙한 것으로 다뤄야 하며, 포함된 SR들의 조직학적 근거는 REP가 진성 재생 치수-상아질 복합체가 아닌 복구양(백악질·골 유사) 조직을 만든다는 점을 보여줌.

## Summary

This umbrella review critically appraised 29 systematic reviews (SRs) on regenerative endodontic procedures (REP) — the biologically based approach that replaces a damaged pulp-dentin complex with new vital tissue in necrotic immature permanent teeth. Five databases (PubMed-Medline, Cochrane CENTRAL, Scielo, Web of Science, LILACS) were searched to December 2020, yielding 403 records that narrowed to 29 eligible SRs (20 without meta-analysis, 9 with meta-analysis). Each SR was graded with AMSTAR2, and findings were organized into three domains: clinical protocols (irrigation, intracanal medication, scaffolds, coronal barrier), clinical outcomes (survival, apical closure, root development), and stem cell research. The central finding is that the REP evidence base, despite growing volume, remains methodologically weak — only 2 of 29 SRs achieved "high quality" on AMSTAR2, and no protocol domain (irrigant, medicament, scaffold, or barrier) had a meta-analytically established superior choice.

## Key Contributions

- First AMSTAR2-graded umbrella synthesis of the REP systematic-review literature (29 SRs from 403 initial hits), giving the field a comparative quality baseline it previously lacked.
- Structures the fragmented REP evidence into protocol domains (irrigation, intracanal medication, scaffold, coronal barrier) and outcome domains (survival/success, root development, stem cell/histology), clarifying where meta-analytic consensus does and does not exist.
- Documents that REP success is broadly comparable to apexification across multiple SRs (survival ranges 87.9–100% REP vs 90.6–97.1% apexification), with REP showing an advantage in lateral dentinal wall thickening and continued root development in several included SRs.
- Flags systemic methodological weaknesses across the underlying literature: heavy reliance on case reports within SRs, sparse a priori protocol registration (3/29), inconsistent risk-of-bias tool application, and small pooled-study counts undermining meta-analytic confidence.
- States plainly that no REP protocol has produced a true regenerated pulp-dentin complex histologically — outcomes are predominantly cementum-/bone-like repair tissue, a conceptually important distinction from "regeneration" as commonly marketed.

## Methodology

Umbrella review per PRISMA guidelines with an a priori protocol. Search: 5 databases (PubMed-Medline, Cochrane CENTRAL, Scielo, Web of Science, LILACS) to December 2020, plus OpenGrey and manual reference-list screening; no year/language restriction. Two independent reviewers screened and extracted data (disagreements resolved by a third reviewer) into a predefined table (author, year, databases, study counts/types, interventions, quality tool, results, conclusion). Quality appraisal used AMSTAR2, rating each SR High / Moderate / Low / Critically Low; inter-rater reliability was excellent for both full-text screening (kappa = 1.00) and RoB assessment (kappa = 0.91, 95% CI 0.89–0.92).

## Results

**Study flow**: 403 records → 174 duplicates removed → 35 full-text screened → 6 excluded → 29 SRs included (20 without meta-analysis, 9 with meta-analysis).

**AMSTAR2 quality**: 2 high, 14 moderate, 3 low, 10 critically low; no SR fully satisfied all AMSTAR2 items. Recurrent weaknesses: unclear reporting of included-study types, weak literature search strategy, no list of excluded studies with justification, undisclosed funding sources.

**Protocols**: Irrigation predominantly NaOCl 0.5–8.0% ± EDTA 5.0–17.0% ± CHX 0.12–2.0% (most evidence moderate quality, no meta-analytic irrigant comparison). Intracanal medication predominantly triple antibiotic paste (TAP: metronidazole/ciprofloxacin/minocycline, or modified variants), calcium hydroxide ± CHX, or formocresol. Scaffolds predominantly blood clot; PRP/PRF/PLGA/hydroxyapatite variants reported but no scaffold shown meta-analytically superior. Coronal barrier predominantly MTA; MTA caused more discoloration than Biodentine in a direct comparative SR; other barrier materials reported include glass ionomer cement, Portland cement, bioceramic, calcium-enriched mixture.

**Clinical outcomes**: REP successful in 94.5% overall across etiologies — trauma (64.94%), dens evaginatus (22.92%), caries (5.61%), unknown/broken cusp (6.51%). REP vs apexification success/survival was broadly comparable across different SRs/meta-analyses (87.9% vs 90.6%; 100% vs 95%; 97.8% vs 97.1%), with REP showing superior lateral dentinal wall thickening and continued root development versus MTA apical-plug apexification in several SRs. Reinfection/persistent infection was the leading REP failure mode; crown discoloration was the leading complication in the REP-survival meta-analysis.

**Stem cell research**: 8/29 SRs addressed stem cells/histology (2 with meta-analysis), covering five dental stem cell populations (DPSC, SHED, SCAP, PDLSC, DFPC). Cell homing is currently the most clinically available regenerative pathway per one review. No protocol produced a predictable true pulp-dentin complex histologically — regenerated tissue was predominantly cementum-/bone-like repair tissue rather than dentin/pulp/periodontal-like tissue.
