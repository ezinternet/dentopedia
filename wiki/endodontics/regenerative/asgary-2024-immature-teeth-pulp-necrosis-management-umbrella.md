## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (Iranian Endodontic Journal 2024) pooling 31 systematic reviews/meta-analyses (12 SR, 19 SR/MA) comparing calcium hydroxide (CH) apexification, MTA apexification, and regenerative endodontic treatment (RET) for immature permanent teeth with pulp necrosis, quality-appraised with AMSTAR-2.

MTA apexification achieved faster apical barrier formation than CH with similar overall success rates; RET generally outperformed apexification in root development (root length, dentinal wall thickness) while showing comparable survival/success rates; methodological quality of included reviews was critically low to low except one rated high.

Clinically, MTA and RET are both preferable to CH apexification for reduced treatment time, but RET's structural superiority does not translate into higher survival, and the field still lacks standardized RET protocols, limiting definitive guideline-setting.

## Summary

This umbrella review synthesizes 31 systematic reviews and meta-analyses (12 SR, 19 SR/MA; search through July 2024, MEDLINE/Embase/Cochrane/Scopus + grey literature) evaluating three management strategies for immature permanent teeth with pulp necrosis: calcium hydroxide (CH) apexification, MTA apexification, and regenerative endodontic treatment (RET). Rather than pooling raw data into a new meta-analysis, the authors qualitatively synthesize the direction and consistency of findings across the 31 reviews and formally grade each review's methodological quality using AMSTAR-2.

The central finding is a two-axis comparison. On the apexification axis, MTA (and other calcium silicate cements) consistently outperforms CH for speed of apical barrier formation, while overall clinical success rates are statistically similar between the two materials — meaning MTA's main advantage is efficiency/time, not superior long-term success. On the RET axis, RET is compared both internally (which scaffold — blood clot vs platelet concentrates such as PRP/PRF — performs best) and externally (RET vs apexification). Platelet concentrates trend toward better periapical healing than blood clot alone, but effects on pulp vitality, apical closure, and dentinal wall thickness are inconsistent across studies. When RET is compared head-to-head with apexification, survival and overall success rates are statistically comparable, but RET shows a fairly consistent advantage in promoting structural root development (root length increase, dentinal wall thickening) — a finding echoed across multiple included SR/MAs such as Panda 2022 and Stefanidou 2024.

The review's most sobering contribution is the AMSTAR-2 quality layer: despite the consistent directional signal favoring MTA (for speed) and RET (for root development), the underlying evidence base is almost entirely "critically low" to "low" confidence by AMSTAR-2 criteria, with only a single review rated "high." This tempers how strongly the umbrella review's conclusions should be treated as practice-changing versus hypothesis-generating, and the authors explicitly call for standardized RET protocols and long-term outcome studies.

## Key Contributions

- Largest synthesis to date (31 SR/MA) directly comparing CH apexification, MTA apexification, and RET for immature necrotic permanent teeth in a single umbrella framework.
- Formal AMSTAR-2 appraisal of all 31 included reviews — exposes that the evidence base underlying common clinical preferences (MTA > CH for speed; RET > apexification for root development) is largely low-quality, informing how much confidence clinicians should place in these preferences.
- Structured stratification into three evidence tables: apexification-material comparisons, RET-scaffold comparisons, and RET-vs-apexification head-to-head comparisons — useful as a single reference map of where the RET/apexification literature agrees and diverges.
- Highlights platelet concentrates (PRP/PRF) as generally favorable RET scaffolds for periapical healing, while flagging inconsistency in their effect on pulp vitality, apical closure, and dentinal wall thickness — nuance often lost in single-SR summaries.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Umbrella review of systematic reviews (SR/MA), conducted per Cochrane Handbook principles.
- **Search**: MEDLINE (PubMed), Embase, Cochrane Library, Scopus, plus grey literature and manual reference screening; inception to July 2024; no language restriction.
- **Eligibility**: SR/MA assessing apexification and/or RET clinical/radiographic outcomes in immature permanent teeth with pulp necrosis; excluded case reports/series, primary observational/experimental studies, opinion articles, and SR/MA lacking sufficient outcome data.
- **Selection & extraction**: Two independent reviewers, discrepancies resolved by discussion; standard PRISMA-style flow (1100 records → 250 full-text reviewed → 31 included).
- **Extracted outcomes**: Survival/success rate, periapical healing, apical barrier formation time, apical foramen width, apical closure, root development, dentinal wall thickness, pulp vitality, adverse effects.
- **Quality tool**: AMSTAR-2 (16 domains), overall confidence graded critically low / low / moderate / high.
- **Synthesis approach**: Narrative/qualitative synthesis only — heterogeneity in study designs and outcome definitions across the 31 reviews precluded a formal re-meta-analysis.

## Results

- **Yield**: 1100 records screened → 250 full-text reviewed → 31 SR/MA included (12 SR, 19 SR/MA); publication years 2011-2024; reported patient ages spanned roughly 6-58 years across reviews.
- **Apexification materials (5 reviews)**: MTA/CSC achieved faster apical barrier formation than CH in 2 of these SR/MAs; overall success rates statistically similar in 3 SR/MAs; apical closure comparable in 2 SR/MAs.
- **RET scaffolds (19 reviews)**: Platelet concentrates generally trended toward better periapical healing vs blood clot; overall success rates similar between PC and BC in most comparisons; pulp vitality, apical closure, and dentinal wall thickness results were inconsistent (roughly evenly split between "no difference" and "PC superior" across studies).
- **RET vs apexification (11 reviews)**: No significant difference in survival/overall success rate in most comparisons; RET showed superior root development (root length, dentinal wall thickness) in the majority of reviews; periapical healing generally comparable; one outlier review (Kahler, no meta-analysis) favored apexification for clinical success/healing — low-confidence finding.
- **AMSTAR-2 grading**: Methodological quality "critically low to low" for nearly all 31 reviews; only one review (a 998-patient, 10-RCT RET-vs-apexification comparison) rated "high."
- **Adverse effects**: CH — cervical root fracture risk, prolonged treatment (9-24 months). MTA — tooth discoloration (aesthetic concern in anterior teeth). RET — outcome variability, insufficient long-term complication data.
