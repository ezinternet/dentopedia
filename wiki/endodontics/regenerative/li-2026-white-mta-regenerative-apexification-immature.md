## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD420261337704; 15 studies including 4 RCTs) isolating White MTA as a coronal barrier or apical plug in REP and apexification for necrotic immature permanent teeth.

Pooled radiographic success was very high (0.99, 95% CI 0.96–1.00; very low GRADE certainty); platelet concentrate vs blood clot scaffold showed no significant difference (OR 3.56), but WMTA carried significantly higher tooth discolouration than alternative bioceramics (58.33% vs 7.69%, P=.01).

WMTA remains effective but the discolouration disadvantage is clinically actionable in anterior/esthetic zones, where Biodentine or Totalfill should be preferred as the coronal barrier.

## Summary

This SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD420261337704, PRISMA-compliant) quantifies the efficacy of White MTA (WMTA) specifically in its role as a coronal barrier (REP) or apical plug (apexification) in immature, necrotic permanent teeth, a role previous broader syntheses (e.g., Abdellatif et al., 91.20% overall success across 30 studies) did not isolate. Fifteen studies (4 RCTs, 2 cohorts, 9 case reports/series, mostly from India, Egypt, Turkey) were pooled. Radiographic success was very high (pooled proportion 0.99) but of very low GRADE certainty due to risk of bias and imprecision. Platelet concentrate scaffolds (PRP/PRF/PP) showed a non-significant trend toward better radiographic outcomes than blood clot scaffolds, while root-maturation patterns diverged: PC favoured root lengthening, BC favoured dentinal wall/canal-area thickening. The clinically actionable finding is the discolouration risk: WMTA carries a significantly higher risk of tooth discolouration than alternative bioceramics (Biodentine, Totalfill) in head-to-head studies, informing material selection especially in anterior/esthetic zones.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA to isolate WMTA's specific contribution as coronal barrier/apical plug (rather than pooling it with all bioactive materials), separating radiographic success, PC-vs-BC scaffold comparison, and WMTA-vs-alternative-bioceramic comparison as distinct quantitative outcomes.
- Provides the first meta-analytic quantification of the WMTA discolouration disadvantage relative to alternative bioceramics (58.33% vs 7.69%, P=.01), a clinically important esthetic consideration not previously pooled.
- Demonstrates a scaffold-dependent divergence in root maturation pattern: PC scaffolds favour root lengthening, BC scaffolds favour dentinal wall/canal-area thickening — a nuance obscured in prior broad overall-success syntheses.
- Applies GRADE per-outcome, revealing that near-ceiling success rates (95-100%) coexist with very-low-to-moderate certainty, tempering over-interpretation of point estimates.

## Methodology

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA, Cochrane Handbook, PROSPERO CRD420261337704). Search of PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Library from inception to September 1, 2025, no language restriction. From 229 records (32 duplicates removed, 197 screened), 15 studies were included after excluding 18 of 33 full-text-assessed articles (13 unsuitable designs, 5 reviews/meta-analyses): 4 RCTs, 2 cohort studies, 9 case reports/series. RoB2 (RCTs) and ROBINS-I (non-RCTs) assessed risk of bias. Random-effects models pooled proportions (Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation, generalised linear mixed models for boundary values) and odds ratios; subgroups stratified by scaffold combination (PRP+WMTA, PRF+WMTA, PP+WMTA, apexification+WMTA); heterogeneity via τ², I², Cochran's Q; certainty via GRADE.

## Results

- Radiographic success (6 studies, 112 obs, 110 events): pooled 0.99 (95% CI 0.96-1.00); GLMM estimate 0.96 (95% CI 0.90-0.99). No heterogeneity (I²=0.00%). GRADE: very low.
- PC vs BC scaffold, radiographic success (4 studies, 145 obs): OR 3.56 (95% CI 0.87-14.52), not significant; PRP+MTA subgroup significant (OR 7.49, 95% CI 1.16-48.22). GRADE: very low (indirectness, imprecision).
- WMTA vs alternative bioceramics, clinical success (2 studies, 67 obs, 63 events): alternative bioceramics 100.00% vs WMTA, OR 0.18 (95% CI 0.02-1.68), not significant. GRADE: moderate (downgraded only for imprecision).
- Tooth discolouration: 58.33% (WMTA) vs 7.69% (Biodentine), P=.01 (Aly et al., significant); 14.30% vs 0.00% (Bartaw et al., not significant).
- Pulp sensibility regained: 60%-86% across studies; no significant between-group differences where compared.
- Root development: PC favoured root length increase (P=.004 vs BC in one study); BC favoured dentinal wall/canal-area thickening (P<.05 vs most PC groups in another study).
- No heterogeneity across any analysis (all I²=0.0%); symmetrical funnel plots (no evident publication bias).
