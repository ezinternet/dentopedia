## One-line Summary

In vitro study (25 human third molars, 5 groups) quantifying native coronal dentin composition and how four clinical endodontic irrigation protocols (NaOCl; NaOCl/EDTA; NaOCl/EDTA/CHX; NaOCl/HEDP) alter its organic (collagen/amide), inorganic (phosphate/carbonate apatite, Ca/P), elemental, and ultrastructural (tubule/smear-layer) makeup via Raman, ATR-FTIR, EDS, and SEM.

## 한줄요약

사람 관상 상아질의 표준 성분 구성(유기 20wt%·무기 70wt%·수분, 유기의 90%가 제1형 콜라겐)을 정량하고, 4가지 임상 근관세정 프로토콜이 콜라겐·인산/탄산 아파타이트·Ca/P·상아세관/smear layer 성분·구조를 어떻게 바꾸는지 Raman·FTIR·EDS·SEM으로 특성화한 in vitro 연구.

## Summary

This in vitro study is a useful compositional reference for dentin: it restates the standard makeup of human dentin — approximately **20% organic / 70% inorganic by weight (33% / 45% by volume), with the balance water**, where the organic matrix is **90 wt% type I collagen** embedded in a hydroxyapatite matrix — and then measures how clinically recommended endodontic irrigation protocols reshape that composition at the coronal-dentin surface (the primary substrate for adhesive restorations). Using confocal Raman mapping, ATR-FTIR, EDS elemental analysis, and SEM on 25 human third molars, the authors show that **3% NaOCl alone deproteinizes dentin into a collagen-depleted, "ghost mineral" hypermineralized surface**, while **chelators (EDTA strong, HEDP weak) demineralize and re-expose collagen, open tubules, and cause peritubular erosion**. Critically, the native **Ca/P ratio (~1.87)** is preserved by NaOCl and by continuous NaOCl/HEDP chelation but is disturbed (↑1.99) by EDTA-containing sequences. It extends the wiki's FTIR/structure irrigation work with the first Raman-imaging characterization of post-irrigation coronal dentin.

## Key Contributions

- Supplies a quantified baseline for **native dentin composition** (organic/inorganic/water fractions; collagen fraction) plus control-group EDS atomic % and FTIR band areas — a compositional anchor for the wiki.
- Characterizes the **organic vs inorganic rebalancing** each protocol produces (NaOCl → mineral-rich/collagen-poor; EDTA → collagen re-exposed/mineral removed).
- First to apply **confocal Raman imaging** to map collagen, phosphate, and carbonate distribution on post-endodontic-irrigation coronal dentin.
- Head-to-head, same-tooth comparison of **sequential EDTA chelation vs continuous NaOCl/HEDP soft chelation**.
- Shows the **Ca/P ratio is preserved** by NaOCl and NaOCl/HEDP but disturbed by EDTA sequences — a mineral-proportionality marker with adhesive implications.

## Methodology

In vitro (PRILE 2021). 25 intact human third molars (16–40 yr); deep coronal dentin discs finished to 0.5 mm with a standardized 600-grit smear layer; each tooth contributed 5 fragments randomized across 5 groups (teeth as random effect in a mixed linear model, α=0.05). Groups: **DW control**; **3% NaOCl** (37°C, 30 min); **NaOCl + 17% EDTA (1 min)**; **NaOCl + EDTA + 2% CHX (2 min)**; **3% NaOCl / 9% HEDP mixture** (37°C, 30 min, refreshed q20min). Outcomes: confocal Raman (n=1/group; phosphate 960, carbonate 1070, collagen 2875 cm⁻¹), ATR-FTIR (n=22; amide II, phosphate, carbonate; phosphate/amide II & carbonate/phosphate ratios), EDS (n=4; C, N, O, P, Ca; Ca/P), SEM (2500×/10,000×).

## Results

**Native dentin (DW control):** EDS atomic % — C 19.41±1.40, N 5.36±0.58, O 47.63±0.97, P 9.23±0.55, Ca 17.20±0.36, **Ca/P 1.87±0.08**; FTIR — amide II 20.39, phosphate 1205.87, carbonate 31.00, phosphate/amide II 60.17, carbonate/phosphate 0.026.

| Protocol | Amide II (collagen) | Phosphate (mineral) | Ca/P | Ultrastructure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DW control | 20.39 | 1205.87 | 1.87 | dense smear layer, occluded tubules |
| NaOCl 3% | **1.67** (lowest) | **8980.99** (highest) | 1.90 | dense smear layer, smear plugs, tubules obliterated |
| NaOCl/EDTA | 15.25 | 1557.42 (≈control) | 1.99 | open tubules, peritubular erosion |
| NaOCl/EDTA/CHX | 10.47 | 2538.16 | 1.99 | like NaOCl/EDTA (CHX no tissue dissolution) |
| NaOCl/HEDP | 11.56 | 2491.60 | 1.87 | partially open tubules, highest surface roughness |

- **NaOCl** deproteinizes with no mineral removal → "ghost mineral layer" (collagen-sparse, mineral-rich); lowest C/N, highest Ca/P mineral peaks.
- **EDTA** demineralizes and re-exposes previously embedded collagen (amide II, C, N rise); strong chelator → more demineralization than HEDP **despite 1 min vs 30 min**; disturbs Ca/P (1.99).
- **CHX** binds exposed collagen (glutamic/aspartic residues), lowering amide II vs EDTA; no ultrastructural change.
- **HEDP** (continuous chelation) preserves native Ca/P (1.87) but yields the roughest surface (simultaneous deproteinization + demineralization).
- All variables differed significantly across groups (FTIR p<0.001; EDS elements p<0.001; Ca/P p=0.005).

Clinical significance: because coronal dentin is the primary adhesive substrate, these compositional shifts — altered collagen network, mineral content, Ca/P, smear-layer pattern, and an oxygen-rich surface — may compromise bonding of the final restoration, even though the protocols remain necessary for disinfection.
