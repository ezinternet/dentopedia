## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (Lasers Med Sci 2026): 16 RCTs, 1479 participants; intracanal cryotherapy (cold saline final irrigation) vs room-temperature irrigation for postoperative endodontic pain.

Cryotherapy significantly reduces pain incidence (RR=0.72, 95% CI 0.62–0.84) and intensity at 6h (SMD=-0.75), 24h (SMD=-0.58), and 48h (SMD=-0.53); effect attenuates over time but remains significant.

Intracanal cryotherapy with cold saline is a simple, evidence-supported adjunct for postoperative pain management in root canal treatment.

## Summary

Huang et al. (Lasers Med Sci 2026) pooled 16 RCTs (1479 participants) evaluating intracanal cryotherapy — defined as a final irrigation with cold saline (typically ≤2.5°C) before obturation — for postoperative endodontic pain. Cryotherapy significantly reduced pain incidence (RR=0.72) and pain intensity at 6h (SMD=-0.75), 24h (SMD=-0.58), and 48h (SMD=-0.53) post-treatment. The effect diminished over time but remained statistically significant through 48 hours. Protocol heterogeneity (temperature, volume, duration) across studies limits direct cross-trial comparison, but the overall evidence supports cryotherapy as a clinically effective and easily implemented adjunct.

## Key Contributions

- Largest SR+MA on intracanal cryotherapy: 16 RCTs, 1479 participants
- Quantifies pain reduction across multiple post-treatment timepoints with effect sizes
- RR=0.72 provides actionable risk reduction estimate for clinical counseling
- Establishes that cryotherapy benefit is greatest at 6h and diminishes toward 48h
- Effect is achievable with minimal cost/complexity (cold saline syringe)

## Methodology

SR+MA; databases: Cochrane Library, EMBASE, PubMed. Inclusion: RCTs comparing intracanal cryotherapy (cold saline ≤2.5°C or similar) vs control irrigation at point of final irrigation before obturation. Outcomes: pain incidence (RR), pain intensity SMD at 6h, 24h, 48h. 16 RCTs, 1479 participants.
