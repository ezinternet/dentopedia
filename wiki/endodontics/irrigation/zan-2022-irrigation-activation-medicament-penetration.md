## Three-line Summary

Controlled in vitro study (n=180 extracted mandibular premolars, 5 activation groups × 3 medicament subgroups) using CLSM fluorescence to measure dentinal tubule penetration area at 2, 5, and 8 mm from apex.
Triple antibiotic paste (TAP) achieved highest penetration at every level and every activation method (P<0.05); ultrasonic activation (UI) significantly outperformed sonic, NaviTip FX, KTP laser, and conventional needle for all medicaments (P<0.05); penetration gradient was coronal > middle > apical.
Ultrasonic activation combined with TAP is recommended for deep tubule disinfection (e.g., regenerative endodontics for immature necrotic teeth); calcium hydroxide (CH) remains appropriate for routine inter-appointment antimicrobial use.

## 세줄요약

대조 실험실 연구 (180개 발치 하악 소구치, 5개 세정 활성화 방식 × 3개 약제 하위군), 근단부에서 2·5·8 mm 위치에서 공초점 레이저 주사 현미경 (Confocal Laser Scanning Microscopy, CLSM)으로 형광 침투 면적 측정.
삼중 항생제 페이스트 (Triple Antibiotic Paste, TAP)가 모든 활성화 방식에서 가장 높은 상아세관 (Dentinal Tubule) 침투율 기록 (P<0.05); 초음파 활성화 (Ultrasonic Irrigation, UI)가 음파·NaviTip FX·KTP 레이저·기존 주사바늘보다 유의하게 우수 (P<0.05); 치관부 > 중간부 > 근단부 순.
초음파 활성화 후 TAP 적용이 심부 상아질 소독이 필요한 케이스(미성숙 괴사치 재생 근관치료 등)에 권장; 수산화칼슘 (Calcium Hydroxide, CH)은 일상적 내원 간 항균 약제로 여전히 적합.

## Summary

This controlled in vitro study quantifies how five irrigation activation techniques affect dentinal tubule penetration depth of three intracanal medicaments — calcium hydroxide (CH), double antibiotic paste (DAP), and triple antibiotic paste (TAP) — using confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM) with Rhodamine B fluorescent tracer. The study addresses a methodological gap: prior work examined irrigant (not medicament) penetration and rarely compared laser activation against conventional methods head-to-head.

The key finding is additive: the combination of UI + TAP produced the deepest dentinal tubule penetration at every root level, supporting its use before blood clot scaffold placement in regenerative endodontic procedures (REP) where residual tubular infection is a recognized failure mechanism.

## Key Contributions

- First direct CLSM comparison of KTP laser vs ultrasonic/sonic/NaviTip FX/conventional needle specifically for **medicament** (not irrigant) penetration depth
- TAP penetration reached up to 200 μm into dentinal tubules — superior to DAP and CH at all root levels across all activation techniques
- Quantified the additive benefit: ultrasonic activation yielded highest penetration values regardless of medicament choice
- KTP laser (532 nm) performed unexpectedly poorly — attributed to poor water absorption at 532 nm wavelength compared to Er:YAG; acoustic streaming/cavitation minimized
- Confirmed coronal-to-apical gradient: tubular sclerosis and reduced tubule diameter at the apical third limit all methods

## Methodology

- **Design**: Controlled in vitro study
- **Sample**: 180 extracted single-rooted, single-canal mandibular premolars; standardized to 19 mm length; no calcification, fractures, or caries
- **Preparation**: ProTaper Next (X1–X4); 5.25% NaOCl irrigation; 17% EDTA + NaOCl + distilled water for smear layer removal; ethylene oxide sterilization
- **Activation groups (n=36 each)**:
  - Conventional needle (2 mm short of working length, up-down motion, 3 min)
  - NaviTip FX (Ultradent; same insertion/motion, 3 min)
  - KTP laser (SMARTLITE D, DEKA; 532 nm, 2.0 W, 100 mJ, 20 Hz, 200 μm fiber, 3 min)
  - Sonic activation (EndoActivator, Dentsply, 3 min)
  - Ultrasonic activation (IrriSafe #20/.00, Satelec Acteon piezo unit, 3 min)
- **Medicament subgroups (n=12 each)**:
  - CH: calcium hydroxide powder + distilled water (2:1)
  - DAP: metronidazole + ciprofloxacin (equal parts) + distilled water
  - TAP: metronidazole + ciprofloxacin + minocycline (equal parts, 1 g/mL) + distilled water 0.50 mL
- **Fluorescent tracer**: 0.1% Rhodamine B mixed into medicaments
- **Sections**: Precision saw (IsoMet 1000) at 2 mm (apical), 5 mm (middle), 8 mm (coronal); ~1 mm thick
- **Imaging**: Leica TCS-SPE CLSM; penetration area measured with ImageJ (μm → mm²)
- **Statistics**: Three-way ANOVA + Tukey post hoc (P=0.05); SPSS v22

## Results

Penetration area (mm²) by activation method and medicament:

| Activation | CH (A/M/C) | DAP (A/M/C) | TAP (A/M/C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conventional | 0.04 / 0.18 / 0.54 | 0.11 / 0.36 / 0.58 | 0.24 / 0.61 / 0.78 |
| NaviTip FX | 0.10 / 0.32 / 0.68 | 0.19 / 0.52 / 0.74 | 0.32 / 0.70 / 0.92 |
| KTP Laser | 0.18 / 0.48 / 0.76 | 0.26 / 0.65 / 0.83 | 0.44 / 0.81 / 1.08 |
| Sonic | 0.26 / 0.62 / 0.96 | 0.38 / 0.86 / 0.91 | 0.46 / 0.98 / 1.24 |
| Ultrasonic | 0.38 / 0.82 / 1.06 | 0.48 / 0.92 / 1.12 | 0.72 / 1.18 / 1.46 |

A = apical (2 mm), M = middle (5 mm), C = coronal (8 mm). All values in mm².

- TAP > DAP > CH at every level and every activation method (P<0.05)
- UI > Sonic > KTP Laser > NaviTip FX > Conventional for all medicaments (P<0.05 for UI vs all others)
- Coronal > Middle > Apical at all conditions (P<0.05)
- Null hypothesis rejected: activation technique significantly affects medicament penetration

**Limitations**: In vitro only (no biofilm model); single tooth type (mandibular premolar, straight canals); 24-hour incubation; Rhodamine B may slightly alter medicament viscosity/surface tension; penetration depth ≠ antibacterial efficacy demonstrated.
