## One-line Summary
SR of 37 studies: solution-based endodontic irrigants outperform gel-based formulations in antimicrobial efficacy, smear layer removal, and instrument safety during shaping; gel-NaOCl sludge increases cyclic fatigue and file separation risk; gels should always be followed by thorough solution irrigation.

## 한줄요약
SR (37편): 용액형 세정제가 항균·스미어층 제거·기구 안전성 모든 측면에서 젤형 우위 — 젤-NaOCl 슬러지가 파일 피로파절 위험 증가; 임상에서 젤 사용 후 반드시 용액으로 충분히 후속 세정 필요.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 37 studies (in vitro, ex vivo and clinical) comparing gel-based with solution-based endodontic irrigants, synthesised narratively without pooling.

Solutions gave better smear layer removal, cleaning and antimicrobial activity, whereas gels mixed with dentinal debris into sludge that increased torsional and cyclic fatigue and file separation risk; clinical trials showed no gel advantage in bacterial load or postoperative pain.

Use solution irrigants as the primary irrigant and, if a gel is used for lubrication, follow it with thorough solution irrigation; the evidence is heterogeneous with few high-quality clinical RCTs.

## 세줄요약

젤형과 용액형 근관 세정제를 비교한 37편 (in vitro, ex vivo, 임상)의 체계적 문헌고찰로, 풀링 없이 서술적으로 종합했다.

용액형이 도말층 제거·세정력·항균 효과에서 우수했고, 젤은 상아질 잔사와 섞여 슬러지를 만들어 비틀림·반복 피로와 파일 파절 위험을 높였으며, 임상시험에서 젤은 세균 감소나 술 후 통증에서 이점이 없었다.

용액형을 기본 세정제로 쓰고 윤활 목적으로 젤을 쓰면 반드시 용액 세정을 충분히 이어서 한다; 연구 간 이질성이 크고 고품질 임상 RCT는 적다.

## Summary
This systematic review evaluated 37 studies comparing gel-based versus solution-based formulations of endodontic disinfectants (NaOCl, EDTA, CHX) during root canal shaping. In vitro and ex vivo evidence consistently showed gel formulations produce dense dentine-gel sludge that traps debris, increases torsional and cyclic fatigue, and raises file separation risk. Solution-based irrigants demonstrated superior smear layer removal, cleaning efficiency, and antimicrobial activity. Clinical trials found no advantage for gels in bacterial load reduction or postoperative pain. The review concluded that while gels may offer glide-path lubrication in narrow canals, their overall clinical performance is inferior to solutions, and gel use should always be followed by thorough solution irrigation.

## Key Contributions
- Definitive class-level comparison: solution-based > gel-based for antimicrobial action, smear layer removal, and canal cleanliness
- Key failure mechanism identified: gel + dentinal debris → "dentin-gel sludge" → instrument clogging → torsional overload → file separation
- Gels have one legitimate advantage: glide-path lubrication in narrow/curved canals — but this is offset by sludge accumulation
- Clinical trials (RCTs) showed no bacterial load or pain reduction benefit from gel formulations
- Practical recommendation: if gels are used, they must be followed by extensive solution irrigation to eliminate sludge

## Methodology
PRISMA 2020 systematic review; narrative synthesis (heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis). Databases: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, LILACS, Google Scholar + grey literature (up to February 2025). Inclusion: RCTs, in vitro, ex vivo, clinical studies comparing gel vs solution formulations of the same disinfectant during root canal shaping. Outcomes: smear layer/debris removal, instrument separation, torsional/cyclic fatigue, bacterial reduction, debris extrusion, postoperative pain. Studies included: 37. European Endodontic Journal (EEJ-2024-11-169).

## Results
**In vitro / ex vivo (dominant evidence base):**
- Gel formulations: ↑ dentinal debris retention, ↑ torsional/cyclic fatigue (consistent), ↑ file separation risk; some models showed ↓ apical extrusion
- Solution formulations: superior smear layer removal, better cleaning efficiency, stronger antimicrobial activity
- Sludge mechanism: gel viscosity binds debris → prevents flushing → instrument clogging

**Clinical trials:**
- No significant bacterial load reduction advantage for gels
- Postoperative pain: comparable between groups (no clinical difference)
- NaOCl solution: consistently better antimicrobial outcomes
