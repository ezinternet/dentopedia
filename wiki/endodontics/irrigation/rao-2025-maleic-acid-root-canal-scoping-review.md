## Three-line Summary
PRISMA-ScR scoping review (Arksey & O'Malley methodology) mapping the evidence for maleic acid (MA) — a mild low-pH organic chelator — as a root-canal conditioner across efficacy, antimicrobial action, dentin effects, adhesion, and regenerative contexts.

7% maleic acid consistently outperformed 17% EDTA for apical-third smear-layer removal (the region where EDTA is weakest), while also offering advantages in adhesive and regenerative contexts including calcium-hydroxide removal; maleic acid's demineralizing activity necessitates controlled short conditioning times of 1–3 minutes to optimize smear removal while minimizing erosive risk to dentin.

As a focused synthesis on an agent covered incompletely in broader chelator SRs (Fortea 2024), this scoping review deepens the evidence specifically supporting maleic acid as a targeted EDTA alternative when apical-third conditioning is the clinical priority.

7% 말레산은 EDTA가 가장 약한 치근단 1/3 스미어층 제거에서 17% EDTA보다 일관되게 우월하고, Ca(OH)₂ 제거·접착·재생 맥락에서도 이점; 탈회 활성으로 인한 상아질 침식을 제한하기 위해 1–3분 단시간 적용 권장.

광범위 킬레이트제 SR(Fortea 2024)에서 불완전하게 다뤄진 말레산에 초점을 맞춰 치근단 조건화가 임상 우선순위일 때 EDTA의 표적 대안으로서 말레산의 근거를 심화.

## Summary
This scoping review mapped the evidence for maleic acid (MA), a mild low-pH organic chelator, as a root-canal conditioner. It concludes that 7% MA is a potent irrigant with consistent superiority over EDTA for removing the apical-third smear layer — the region where EDTA is weakest — and offers advantages in adhesion and regenerative contexts (including calcium-hydroxide removal). Because MA also demineralizes dentin, the authors recommend controlled, short conditioning times (1–3 min) to optimize smear-layer removal while minimizing erosive risk. It deepens the maleic-acid strand of the broader chelating-agent literature.

## Key Contributions
- Focused synthesis of maleic-acid efficacy, antimicrobial action, dentin effects, and interactions.
- Practical 1–3 min conditioning recommendation to balance efficacy vs erosion.

## Methodology
Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR 2018; Arksey & O'Malley) mapping MA's role across endodontic outcomes.

## Results
7% MA superior to EDTA for apical smear-layer removal, with adhesion/regeneration advantages; short application advised to limit demineralization.
