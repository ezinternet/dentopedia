## One-line Summary

In-vitro comparative study (60 human single-rooted teeth, 21-day E. faecalis root canal infection model, n=10/subgroup): both 2% CHX gel (mean reduction 4.94 logCFU) and RG-Dent (metronidazole/iodoform/dexamethasone paste, 4.77 logCFU) were significantly more effective than Ca(OH)2 (3.69 logCFU) against mature E. faecalis (P<0.001, Cohen's d>4); CHX vs RG-Dent P=.036, d=0.69 (medium); application duration (2 vs 7 days) had no significant effect on efficacy (P=.78, no interaction).

## Summary

This in-vitro study established a 21-day mature E. faecalis intracanal infection model in 60 human single-rooted premolars and compared three intracanal medicaments — 2% chlorhexidine (CHX) gel, RG-Dent (metronidazole/iodoform/dexamethasone paste), and calcium hydroxide [Ca(OH)2] — at two application durations (2 vs 7 days). Both CHX and RG-Dent produced substantially and statistically superior bacterial reductions compared with Ca(OH)2 (very large effect sizes, Cohen's d>4), while CHX showed only a marginal (medium-effect) advantage over RG-Dent. Critically, extending medicament dwell time from 2 to 7 days provided no additional antimicrobial benefit for any of the three agents, suggesting that maximal bactericidal activity against E. faecalis is reached within the first 48 hours.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the first rigorous peer-reviewed antimicrobial evaluation of RG-Dent against E. faecalis, positioning it as statistically comparable to CHX (P=.036, d=0.69) and a viable non-antibiotic-paste-discoloration alternative (RG-Dent lacks minocycline).
- Quantifies Ca(OH)2's clear inferiority against mature E. faecalis biofilm using a validated 21-day infection model, reinforcing that Ca(OH)2 should not be relied upon as a sole disinfecting agent in persistent E. faecalis cases.
- Directly tests the traditional 7-day (or longer) intracanal medicament dwell-time convention against a 2-day protocol and finds no efficacy difference — supporting shorter inter-appointment intervals.

## Methodology

60 human mandibular premolars (single root, Vertucci Type I) were instrumented (ProTaper Universal to size 30/.09) with standard NaOCl/EDTA irrigation, sterilized, then inoculated with E. faecalis (ATCC 29212) and incubated anaerobically for 21 days to create a mature intracanal infection. Specimens were randomized to Ca(OH)2 (Metapaste), 2% CHX gel (Clopexid), or RG-Dent (YG-Dent), n=20 each, further split into 2-day and 7-day application subgroups (n=10 each). Bacterial load (logCFU/mL) was sampled pre- and post-treatment via paper-point sampling and blinded plate counting. Analysis used two-way ANOVA (medicament × duration), one-way ANOVA/Tukey HSD, paired t-tests, and Cohen's d effect sizes; sample size was powered a priori (f=0.40, power=0.80).

## Results

- Baseline bacterial load was uniform across all six subgroups (~5.11±0.21 logCFU/mL, P=.54).
- Mean logCFU reduction: CHX 4.94±0.23, RG-Dent 4.77±0.26, Ca(OH)2 3.69±0.27 (pooled across durations).
- Medicament type was the dominant factor: two-way ANOVA F(2,54)=178.9, P<0.001, η²=0.869 (~87% of variance explained).
- Tukey HSD: CHX vs Ca(OH)2 mean diff 1.25 (95% CI 1.10–1.40), P<0.001, d=5.08 (very large); RG-Dent vs Ca(OH)2 mean diff 1.09 (95% CI 0.92–1.25), P<0.001, d=4.13 (very large); CHX vs RG-Dent mean diff 0.16 (95% CI 0.02–0.32), P=.036, d=0.69 (medium, but only 56% post-hoc power).
- Application duration had no significant main effect (F(1,54)=0.078, P=.781, η²=0.001) and no interaction with medicament (F(2,54)=0.026, P=.974, η²=0.001) — i.e., neither medicament benefited from extending 2-day dwell time to 7 days.
