## Three-line Summary

Ex vivo push-out bond strength (POBS) and ATR-FTIR study using 60 extracted single-rooted human mandibular premolars, comparing two all-in-one root canal irrigants — Dual Rinse HEDP (etidronate + NaOCl) and Triton (PBTC/citric-acid-based) — against a saline control, on dentin bonded with AH Plus (epoxy resin) or CeraSeal (bioceramic) sealer.

Dual Rinse HEDP produced the highest POBS for both AH Plus (8.98±0.2 MPa) and CeraSeal (11.36±0.5 MPa), versus Triton (7.20±0.4 / 5.85±0.5 MPa) and lowest with saline (3.56±0.3 / 2.39±0.3 MPa) (all P<0.001); FTIR showed Dual Rinse HEDP preserved higher posttreatment amide III:phosphate and carbonate:phosphate ratios than Triton, indicating less organic/inorganic dentin degradation.

Dual Rinse HEDP is the preferable root-conditioning irrigant before either epoxy-resin or bioceramic sealer placement, improving dislodgement resistance versus Triton or saline; findings are ex vivo and need clinical/longitudinal validation.

## Summary

This ex vivo laboratory study compared two commercially available "all-in-one" continuous-chelation root canal irrigants — Dual Rinse HEDP (etidronate capsule dissolved in NaOCl) and Triton (a two-component PBTC/citric-acid/NaOCl automix system) — against a saline control, for their effect on push-out bond strength (POBS) of two chemically distinct sealers: AH Plus (epoxy resin) and CeraSeal (premixed hydraulic calcium-silicate/bioceramic). Sixty extracted human mandibular premolars were instrumented, irrigated per group (n=20), then subdivided by sealer (n=10) for POBS testing via universal testing machine; a parallel ATR-FTIR arm on separate dentin blocks quantified organic (amide III:phosphate) and inorganic (carbonate:phosphate) content before/after irrigant exposure. Dual Rinse HEDP produced significantly higher POBS than Triton for both sealers, with saline lowest for both; FTIR data showed Dual Rinse HEDP best preserved dentin's organic and inorganic content, mechanistically explaining its bonding advantage. The paper concludes that root conditioning with Dual Rinse HEDP is preferable to Triton or saline before placement of either an epoxy-resin or a bioceramic sealer.

## Key Contributions

- First reported head-to-head comparison of Dual Rinse HEDP vs Triton for sealer push-out bond strength, tested against **both** an epoxy-resin sealer (AH Plus) and a hydraulic calcium-silicate/bioceramic sealer (CeraSeal) in the same design.
- Adds ATR-FTIR mechanistic evidence (amide III:phosphate, carbonate:phosphate ratios) linking irrigant chemistry to dentin organic/inorganic preservation and downstream bond strength — explains *why* Dual Rinse HEDP outperforms Triton, not just *that* it does.
- Shows Triton, despite established smear-layer-removal and antibacterial efficacy, underperforms on the previously untested outcome of sealer dislodgement resistance — attributed to its stronger citric-acid decalcification and higher NaOCl concentration (<10%) aggressively demineralizing/deproteinizing dentin.
- Fractographic analysis corroborates the POBS ranking: Dual Rinse HEDP produced predominantly cohesive failures (strong bonding) for both sealers, while saline produced predominantly adhesive failures (weak bonding).

## Methodology

- **Sample**: 60 extracted human mandibular single-rooted premolars, straight roots (<30° curvature), single canal/mature apex verified radiographically + microscopically; apices sealed, roots embedded in polyvinylsiloxane (closed-end system).
- **Instrumentation**: ProTaper Universal to F3; taper removed with Peeso reamers #1–2.
- **Groups (n=20 each)**: (1) Dual Rinse HEDP (0.9 g etidronate capsule + 10 mL 3% NaOCl); (2) Triton (automixed A+B: PBTC <15%, citric acid <2%, NaOCl <10%, surfactants); (3) saline control. Standardized delivery (30-gauge side-vented needle, 1 mm short of WL; 2 mL between instrument changes + 5 mL final flush, 1 min each), followed by 5 mL distilled-water rinse.
- **POBS**: each irrigant group split n=10 by sealer (AH Plus vs CeraSeal); 2.0±0.1 mm middle-third dentin slices; sealers set 48 h/100% humidity; push-out via Instron (1 mm/min, 0.7 mm plungers); POBS (MPa) = Force / (2πrh).
- **Fractography**: 40× stereomicroscopy, adhesive/cohesive/mixed failure classification.
- **ATR-FTIR**: separate 15 dentin blocks (8 extracted maxillary incisors), n=5/group; pre/post 30-min irrigant exposure (ultrasonic agitation); amide III:phosphate and carbonate:phosphate band-area ratios via diamond ATR, 23°C standardized.
- **Stats**: one-way ANOVA + Tukey HSD, α=0.05, SPSS v25.

## Results

- **POBS — AH Plus**: Dual Rinse HEDP 8.98±0.2 MPa > Triton 7.20±0.4 MPa > saline 3.56±0.3 MPa (P<0.001).
- **POBS — CeraSeal**: Dual Rinse HEDP 11.36±0.5 MPa > Triton 5.85±0.5 MPa > saline 2.39±0.3 MPa (P<0.001).
- **Fractography — AH Plus**: Dual Rinse HEDP 70% cohesive/30% mixed; Triton 50% cohesive/30% adhesive/20% mixed; saline 60% adhesive/20% cohesive/20% mixed.
- **Fractography — CeraSeal**: Dual Rinse HEDP 60% cohesive/40% mixed; Triton 60% mixed/40% cohesive; saline 60% mixed/40% adhesive.
- **FTIR amide III:phosphate (organic)**: posttreatment saline (28.4±4.0) far higher (less protected) than Dual Rinse HEDP (12.7±3.4, P<0.001) and Triton (8.6±0.6, P<0.001); Dual Rinse HEDP vs Triton NS (P=0.131).
- **FTIR carbonate:phosphate (inorganic)**: posttreatment Dual Rinse HEDP (39.6±3.4) significantly higher than Triton (28.1±3.5, P<0.001) and saline (32.7±2.8, P=0.016) — Dual Rinse HEDP best preserved mineral content.
- **Bottom line**: Dual Rinse HEDP > Triton > saline for POBS with both sealer chemistries; FTIR mechanistically supports Dual Rinse HEDP's milder, better-preserving effect on dentin organic and inorganic structure versus Triton's more aggressive citric-acid/higher-NaOCl formulation.
