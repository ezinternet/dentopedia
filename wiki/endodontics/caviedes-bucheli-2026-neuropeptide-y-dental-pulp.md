## Three-line Summary
PRISMA-guided narrative synthesis of 92 studies (in vitro, in vivo animal, ex vivo human tissue) on NPY biology in dental pulp from inception to February 2026.
NPY released from sympathetic perivascular fibres acts via Gi/Go-coupled Y1/Y2 receptors to suppress SP/CGRP-driven neurogenic inflammation, inhibit TRPV1 nociceptors, and drive tertiary dentinogenesis via BMP-2; NPY/Y1 expression peaks in mild-moderate caries then collapses in advanced lesions as sympathetic fibres are destroyed.
The SP/CGRP (excitatory) ↔ NPY (inhibitory) balance determines pulp survival; no clinical RCTs exist and mechanistic data remain largely immunohistochemical without human in vivo functional validation.

## Summary

Dental pulp operates within a rigid, low-compliance dentinal chamber where minimal inflammatory stimuli produce disproportionate biological responses. The classical excitatory axis — SP (NK1/Gq → vasodilation, plasma extravasation) and CGRP (CALCRL/RAMP1/Gs → potent vasodilation, TRPV1 sensitisation) — is released antidromically from C fibres during noxious stimulation and drives neurogenic inflammation. Elevated intrapulpal pressure then activates HIF-1α and the NLRP3 inflammasome, accelerating transition from reversible inflammation to irreversible pulp necrosis.

**NPY as the counter-regulatory axis**: NPY, released from sympathetic perivascular fibres alongside noradrenaline, activates Y1 and Y2 receptors (Gi/Go proteins → ↓cAMP, presynaptic VGCC closure) to oppose this excitatory cascade. Y1 receptors (postsynaptic, on vascular smooth muscle, sensory neurons, fibroblasts, and odontoblasts) mediate vasoconstriction and direct antinociception. Y2 receptors (presynaptic) act as a brake on SP/CGRP release from sensory terminals — though Y2 protein has not yet been confirmed in human dental pulp tissue specifically.

**Stage-dependent NPY in caries**:
- Healthy pulp: NPY at baseline picomolar levels in perivascular fibres; SP > NPY
- Mild–moderate caries: NPY and Y1 expression peak; fibres extend into odontoblastic layer and predentine; NPY/Y1 on fibroblasts upregulates BMP-2 → odontoblast differentiation + tertiary dentinogenesis + angiogenesis
- Advanced caries: Sympathetic fibre destruction → NPY collapses → SP/CGRP unchecked → irreversible pulpitis/necrosis

This stage-dependent pattern explains the clinical observation that early pulpitis may be self-limiting while advanced caries progresses rapidly to necrosis.

**Dentinal hypersensitivity mechanism**: Restorative procedures activate TRPV1/TRPA1/TRPM8 channels on odontoblasts and sensory terminals, releasing SP/CGRP and initiating neurogenic inflammation. Concurrent sympathetic activation releases NPY which limits this cascade — proposing NPY as an endogenous analgesic response to procedural pain.

**VIP counterpoint**: Parasympathetic VIP (vasodilatory) increases modestly in mild-moderate caries as a compensatory fine-tuner against NPY vasoconstriction. NPY is ~2× more abundant than VIP in healthy pulp, reflecting greater sympathetic than parasympathetic fibre density.

## Key Contributions

1. **Receptor-resolved NPY pharmacology in pulp**: First comprehensive synthesis mapping Y1 (postsynaptic vasoconstriction/antinociception/dentinogenesis) vs Y2 (presynaptic SP/CGRP brake) function in dental pulp context
2. **Stage-dependent caries model**: NPY/Y1 protective programme that actively fails in advanced caries — mechanistic explanation for irreversible pulpitis progression
3. **Reparative NPY arm**: Y1 on pulpal fibroblasts → BMP-2 → odontoblast differentiation + tertiary dentinogenesis + angiogenesis — links sympathetic neuropeptide to regenerative biology
4. **Intrinsic procedural analgesia hypothesis**: NPY released during restorative procedures may constitute an endogenous pain-limiting mechanism
5. **Unified inhibitory model**: All NPY receptor subtypes (Y1, Y2, Y5) signal via Gi/Go — establishing a coherent anti-excitatory axis contrasting with Gq (SP/NK1) and Gs (CGRP/CALCRL) excitatory pathways

## Methodology

- Design: PRISMA-guided narrative synthesis
- 92 studies from 140 retrieved (PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus; inception → February 2026)
- Included: in vitro, in vivo (rat/cat), ex vivo human tissue, mechanistic reviews
- Two independent reviewers; discrepancies resolved by third reviewer
- Narrative synthesis used (full SR not feasible due to study heterogeneity and diverse experimental models)

## Limitations

- Predominantly immunohistochemical evidence: spatial expression ≠ functional activation or downstream signalling efficacy
- Animal model dominance (rat continuous eruption, cat anatomy) with uncertain human translation
- No RCTs on NPY-targeted dental interventions
- Y2 receptor role in dental pulp is inferred from other tissues (no confirmed human pulp Y2 protein)
- NPY quantification challenging at picomolar concentrations; real-time release dynamics during procedures unstudied
