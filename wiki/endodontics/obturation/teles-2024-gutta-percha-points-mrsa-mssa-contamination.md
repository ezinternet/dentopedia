## Three-line Summary

In-vitro contamination screen of 315 gutta-percha points from packages in clinical use for 1 month (2 brands, 6 ISO gauges; BHI enrichment culture + chromogenic selective medium + PCR with mecA/MSSA-specific genes) sampling the realistic cross-infection scenario at a university dental clinic.

Only 6/315 points (1.9%) were contaminated, PCR-confirmed as 1 MRSA and 5 MSSA; a 3% NaOCl 60-second chairside disinfection protocol cleared all six (100% efficacy with no re-growth).

GP-cone contamination is low but non-negligible given MRSA's nosocomial burden and national high-prevalence context; the 1-minute NaOCl protocol is sufficient and extended immersion (>1 min) risks GP elasticity loss and chloride-crystal formation.

## Summary

Gutta-percha (GP) points are the obturation gold standard but, because of their thermoplastic nature, cannot be sterilized by autoclave or hot-air oven — chairside chemical disinfection is the only sterility safeguard once a package is opened and points are exposed to handling, aerosols, and repeated instrument access. This in-vitro study from a Porto university dental clinic asked how contaminated *in-use* GP points really are, targeting the clinically important pathogens Methicillin-resistant (MRSA) and Methicillin-sensitive (MSSA) Staphylococcus aureus. Using packages already in clinical use for one month, it combined classical culture (BHI enrichment → chromogenic MRSA/MSSA selective medium → Gram/catalase/coagulase) with PCR confirmation, then applied a 3% NaOCl 60-s disinfection protocol to any positive cone. The take-home: contamination is low (<2%) but non-negligible, and a simple chairside protocol reliably clears it.

## Key Contributions

- Quantifies MRSA/MSSA contamination on GP points sampled from packages **in clinical use for 1 month**, the realistic cross-infection scenario rather than freshly opened cones.
- Uses **PCR confirmation** (mecA-type MRSA gene, MSSA-specific gene) on top of chromogenic phenotyping, avoiding phenotype-only misidentification.
- Confirms a feasible **3% NaOCl / 60-s chairside disinfection** clears 100% of contaminated cones.
- Positions the finding for infection control: low prevalence, but MRSA's nosocomial burden and Portugal's high European MRSA rate mean the risk "cannot be considered negligible."

## Methodology

- **Samples**: 315 GP points, 2 brands / 6 ISO gauges — main cones K20/K25/K30/K35 (R&S) and accessory A/B (Dentsply) — from boxes used ≥4 weeks (~8 appointments/week), stored per manufacturer specs.
- **Contamination screen**: each cone in 5 mL BHI, 37 °C / 72 h, turbidity read at OD 600 nm; controls C− (BHI) and C+ (S. aureus ATCC 25923).
- **Phenotypic ID**: positives subcultured on chromID MRSA / chromID S. aureus chromogenic medium (green = MRSA, beige = MSSA); Gram-positive cocci, catalase and coagulase positive.
- **Molecular ID**: PCR — MRSA via mecA-type gene (527 bp); mecA-negatives screened for MSSA-specific gene (255 bp); agarose-gel electrophoresis with ATCC and no-DNA controls.
- **Disinfection (CPD)**: contaminated cones in 3% NaOCl for 60 s, rinsed with 10 mL sterile distilled water, dried on sterile gauze, re-incubated in BHI 37 °C / 72 h.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 25.0, binomial test, α = 0.05.

## Results

- **315 GP points** collected from 2 brands, packages open/in-use for 1 month.
- Only **6/315 = 1.9%** positive for microbial growth (98.1% uncontaminated).
- **PCR confirmed 1 MRSA (16.6% of positives) + 5 MSSA (83.3%)**; the single MRSA cone showed the green chromogenic phenotype, the five MSSA cones beige.
- All positives were Gram-positive cocci, catalase- and coagulase-positive.
- **3% NaOCl / 60-s disinfection protocol was effective in all 6 contaminated cones (100%)** — no turbidity on re-incubation.
- Both the <2% contamination level and the 100% disinfection efficacy align with Bracciale et al. (2020).
- Caveat (from discussion, not tested): NaOCl immersion >1 min risks loss of GP elasticity and chloride-crystal formation unless rinsed — immersion time must be controlled.
