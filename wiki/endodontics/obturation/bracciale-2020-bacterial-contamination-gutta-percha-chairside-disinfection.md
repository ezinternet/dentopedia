## Three-line Summary

In vitro survey of 240 gutta-percha (GP) points sampled from commercially open, in-use clinical boxes (4 brands, 4–8 weeks open, ~8 appointments/week) cultured individually in fluid thioglycolate medium for up to 21 days — a realistic cross-infection model rather than sealed-box testing.

22.9% of GP points harbored bacteria with no significant difference between brands; contamination was concentrated in larger, less-used cones (K30, Dentsply D-size) that remain in open boxes longest; a chairside disinfection protocol (5.25% NaOCl 1 min + detergent 5 min) disinfected 76.4% of contaminated points (P<0.001).

Handling and box-residence time — not manufacturer — drive GP contamination, supporting routine chairside disinfection of all GP cones before obturation using separate tweezers for picking and placing.

## Summary

Gutta-percha (GP) is thermolabile and cannot be heat-sterilized, so it must be chairside-disinfected before use to keep the root-canal aseptic chain intact. This study sampled GP points not from sealed unused boxes but from **commercial boxes already in clinical use** (open 4–8 weeks, ~8 appointments/week) to better model real cross-infection risk. Of 240 points across 4 brands and 13 ISO sizes, 22.9% grew bacteria in fluid thioglycolate culture. Contamination was **not** related to brand, but concentrated in **less-used, oversized cones** (K30, Dentsply® D-size) that linger longer in open boxes and undergo more tweezer handling. A simple chairside disinfection protocol (5.25% NaOCl 1 min → detergent → rinse) disinfected 76.4% of contaminated cones. The authors conclude handling — not box exposure per se — drives contamination, and endorse routine chairside disinfection plus separate tweezers for picking vs placing cones.

## Key Contributions

- Documents real-world GP contamination from **in-use** (not sealed) boxes — a more clinically realistic cross-infection scenario.
- First contamination data reported for the **R&S®** brand (n=104).
- Shows contamination is driven by **cone size/usage pattern**, not manufacturer, implicating handling and box residence time.
- Validates an easy chairside disinfection protocol with routine clinic reagents.

## Methodology

- **240 GP points** across ISO sizes A, B, C, D, K15–K40, F1–F3 from Dentsply® (104), R&S® (104), Proclinic® (8), ProTaper®.
- Collected by one operator with sterile gloves from **already-open in-use boxes** during filling; treating operators blinded to study aim.
- Each point cultured individually (1 point/tube, sterile tweezers) in sterile **fluid thioglycolate medium** at 37°C, turbidity checked every 72 h up to 21 days.
- **Chairside disinfection protocol** on contaminated points: 10 mL 5.25% NaOCl (1 min) → 10 mL detergent (3% Tween 80 + 5% sodium thiosulfate, 5 min, to strip NaOCl crystals) → 10 mL sterile-water rinse → dry → re-culture.
- Statistics: SPSS v25; chi-square + binomial test; P<0.05.

## Results

- **Overall**: 22.9% contaminated vs 77.1% clean (P<0.001).
- **By brand**: Dentsply® and R&S® highest at **47.3% each**; no significant brand–contamination relation (chi-square P=0.273; P=0.188 excluding small-n Proclinic®).
- **Most contaminated size**: **K30 at 16.4% (9/55)** — 8/9 R&S®, 1/9 Proclinic®.
- All six **Dentsply® D-size** points contaminated (10.9% of collected points) — only size with a significantly higher positive rate; attributed to ~6% taper / low use → longer open-box residence.
- **Chairside disinfection protocol effective in 76.4% (42/55)** of contaminated cones (P<0.001).
