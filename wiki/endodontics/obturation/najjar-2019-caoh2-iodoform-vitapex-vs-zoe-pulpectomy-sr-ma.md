## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42016037563; 15 studies, 1,669 primary teeth, 337 anterior/1,332 molars; 10 pooled in meta-analysis) comparing Ca(OH)2/iodoform paste (Vitapex/Metapex) versus ZOE-based materials for pulpectomy success in healthy primary teeth.

Overall pooled ORs showed no significant difference at 6 or 12 months; however, quality-stratified subgroup analysis revealed that in the 2 high-quality studies, ZOE and ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 significantly outperformed Ca(OH)2/iodoform at 12 months and ≥18 months (clinical OR 0.21, p=0.010; radiographical OR 0.10, p=0.003).

The clinical recommendation is exfoliation-timing-based: Ca(OH)2/iodoform (Vitapex) for primary teeth nearing physiologic exfoliation given its resorbability, and ZOE or ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 when longer functional retention is expected.

## 세줄요약

유치 1,669개(전치 337·구치 1,332; 15편 SR, 10편 메타분석) SR+MA로, Ca(OH)2/iodoform(Vitapex/Metapex)과 ZOE 계열의 발수 성공률을 6·12·18개월 이상 추적 비교했다.

전체 풀링에서는 어느 시점에서도 유의차 없었으나, 고품질 연구 2편 분층 분석에서는 12개월·18개월 이상에서 ZOE·ZOE+iodoform+Ca(OH)2가 Ca(OH)2/iodoform보다 유의하게 높은 성공률을 보였다(임상 OR 0.21, p=0.010).

임상 권고는 탈락 임박 치아에는 Ca(OH)2/iodoform(재흡수성), 장기 잔존 예상 치아에는 ZOE 계열을 사용하는 방식으로 탈락 시기 기반의 재료 선택이다.

## Summary

This SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42016037563) compares Ca(OH)2/iodoform paste (commercially Vitapex or Metapex) against ZOE as root canal filling material for pulpectomy in primary teeth. From 5,000 initially identified titles, 15 studies (1,669 teeth: 337 anterior, 1,332 molars; follow-up 1 week to 30 months) met inclusion criteria, with 10 pooled in meta-analysis. Overall pooled odds ratios showed no statistically significant difference in clinical or radiographical success between Ca(OH)2/iodoform and ZOE (or ZOE/iodoform, or ZOE/iodoform combined with Ca(OH)2) at 6, 12, or ≥18 months. However, when stratified by study quality (modified CONSORT 2010, 27-point scale), the 2 high-quality studies showed ZOE and ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 significantly outperforming Ca(OH)2/iodoform at 12 and ≥18 months, while the 11 moderate-quality studies showed no difference — indicating that the true effect, once bias is minimized, favors ZOE-based materials for longer-retained teeth. Clinically, the authors recommend Ca(OH)2/iodoform (Vitapex) for teeth nearing physiologic exfoliation (given its resorbability) and ZOE or ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 when a longer functional lifespan is expected.

## Key Contributions

- First reported meta-analysis directly comparing Ca(OH)2/iodoform (Vitapex/Metapex) against ZOE-based materials for primary tooth pulpectomy success.
- Quality-stratified subgroup analysis reveals that pooling all studies together masks a real quality-dependent signal: high-quality studies show ZOE-based materials significantly outperforming Ca(OH)2/iodoform beyond 12 months, while low/moderate-quality studies dilute this to non-significance.
- Confirms via subgroup analysis that intracanal irrigation type (NaOCl vs. other) and tooth type (mandibular-only vs. combined maxillary/mandibular molars) do not significantly influence success rates.
- Translates evidence into a practical, exfoliation-timing-based material selection recommendation for pediatric dentists.

## Methodology

PRISMA-guided SR+MA; PubMed/MEDLINE, Google Scholar, Scopus searched without language/date restriction (search updated 2016 and 2018). Included RCTs and non-randomized clinical trials comparing Ca(OH)2/iodoform vs. ZOE (or ZOE/iodoform, or ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2) pulpectomy outcomes in healthy children's primary teeth; excluded cross-sectional/retrospective/lab/animal studies, permanent or traumatized teeth, and special-needs populations. Two independent reviewers screened studies (Cohen's kappa 0.92 for selection, 0.82 for extraction) with a third reviewer resolving disagreements. Success defined clinically (no pain/swelling/abscess/percussion pain, and/or decreased mobility) and radiographically (decreased/absent radiolucency vs. baseline). Quality appraised via modified CONSORT 2010 (27-point scale: ≥19 high quality, 10–18 moderate, ≤9 low). Meta-analysis used RevMan 5.3, Mantel–Haenszel pooled odds ratios with 95% CI, I² heterogeneity statistic, fixed- or random-effects models as appropriate, funnel plots and Egger's test for publication bias, and subgroup analyses by irrigation type, tooth type, and study quality tier.

## Results

- 15 studies included in SR (1,669 primary teeth); 10 studies pooled in meta-analysis; 2 rated high-quality, 11 moderate-quality, 2 low-quality (modified CONSORT).
- **Overall pooled OR (Ca(OH)2/iodoform vs. ZOE)**: 6 months — clinical OR 1.84 (95% CI 0.51–6.61, p=0.35), radiographical OR 0.71 (95% CI 0.34–1.51, p=0.38); 12 months — clinical OR 0.78 (95% CI 0.21–2.88, p=0.07), radiographical OR 0.39 (95% CI 0.07–2.35, p=0.31); ≥18 months — clinical OR 0.52 (95% CI 0.17–1.59, p=0.25), radiographical OR 0.13 (95% CI 0.06–1.61, p=0.16). None statistically significant.
- **Quality-stratified**: In high-quality studies only, ZOE and ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2 showed statistically significantly higher success than Ca(OH)2/iodoform at 12 months (p<0.05) and ≥18 months (clinical OR 0.21, 95% CI 0.07–0.69, p=0.010 vs. ZOE; OR 0.10, 95% CI 0.02–0.45, p=0.003 vs. ZOE/iodoform+Ca(OH)2). Moderate-quality studies showed no significant difference at any timepoint.
- Heterogeneity was strong at 12 months (I²=45% clinical, 62% radiographical) and ≥18 months (I²=58% clinical, 42% radiographical); low at 6 months.
- Egger's test detected significant small-study/publication-bias effect only for radiographical success at ≥18 months (p=0.02).
- Subgroup analyses (irrigation type, tooth type) showed no significant effect on ZOE-group outcomes.
- Bottom-line clinical recommendation: Ca(OH)2/iodoform (Vitapex) for primary teeth nearing exfoliation; ZOE or ZOE/iodoform combined with Ca(OH)2 for teeth expected to remain in function longer.
