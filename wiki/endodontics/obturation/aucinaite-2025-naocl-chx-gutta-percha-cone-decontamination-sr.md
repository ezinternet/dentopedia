## Three-line Summary

First systematic review (PRISMA, INPLASY-registered, 309 records screened → 7 in vitro studies included) comparing sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) versus chlorhexidine digluconate (CHX) for chairside gutta-percha cone decontamination against Enterococcus faecalis, Staphylococcus aureus, and Candida albicans at 0–10 min exposure.

Higher-concentration NaOCl (5.25%/6%) eliminated E. faecalis and S. aureus within 1–5 minutes and is the most time-efficient agent; CHX (especially combined with cetrimide) shows promise against Candida and preserves GP elasticity; all 7 included studies were at moderate or high risk of bias.

No meta-analysis was possible due to methodological heterogeneity; the absence of a standardized decontamination protocol prevents evidence-based guideline formulation, but high-concentration NaOCl represents the current practical gold standard for chairside cone disinfection.

## Summary

Gutta-percha (GP) cones are the standard root-canal filling material but cannot be heat-sterilised and become contaminated during storage, glove handling, and aerosol exposure. Chairside chemical decontamination before obturation is therefore mandatory to prevent cross-infection and secondary endodontic infection, yet no standardised protocol exists. This first-of-kind systematic review compares the two most common irrigants — sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) and chlorhexidine digluconate (CHX) — for GP cone decontamination against *Enterococcus faecalis*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, and *Candida albicans* across 0–10 min exposure. The qualitative synthesis (no meta-analysis was possible) concludes that higher-concentration NaOCl is the most effective and time-efficient agent, eliminating the bacterial pathogens within 1–5 min, while CHX — especially combined with additives such as cetrimide — shows promise particularly against fungal contamination and avoids NaOCl's deteriorative effect on GP elasticity.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic review comparing NaOCl vs CHX for GP cone decontamination against *E. faecalis*, *S. aureus*, and *C. albicans* at 0–10 min intervals.
- Establishes higher-concentration NaOCl (5.25%/6%) as the practical gold standard for chairside GP cone disinfection within a clinically realistic 1–5 min window.
- Identifies CHX (± cetrimide) as a promising alternative where fungal species are prevalent or GP-elasticity preservation matters.
- Documents the methodological heterogeneity (species, concentrations, exposure times, CFU-counting accuracy) that currently blocks evidence-based protocol formulation.

## Methodology

- Design: systematic review following PRISMA; protocol per Cochrane Handbook v6.5; registered INPLASY202530050.
- Databases: PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, SciELO, Scopus, LILACS + manual hand-search; last search 30 Sep 2024; English-only, no year restriction.
- PICO: GP cones (P) disinfected with NaOCl (I) vs CHX (C) — decontamination efficacy at specific time intervals (O).
- Two independent reviewers for selection, extraction, and risk of bias; third reviewer resolved disagreements.
- Risk of bias: modified Cochrane tool, 6 parameters (sample-size calculation, randomisation, blinding of examiner/outcome, control group, standardisation, CFU-based efficacy measurement).
- All included studies in vitro; antimicrobial efficacy quantified by colony-forming units (CFU).

## Results

- **Screening funnel**: 309 records (308 database + 1 hand-searched) → 93 duplicates removed → **216 title/abstract screened** → 32 full-text assessed → **7 included** (179 irrelevant, 5 no full text, 25 methodological exclusions).
- **Risk of bias**: 5/7 high, 2/7 moderate; worst parameters — sample-size calculation (7/7), outcome-assessment blinding (7/7), randomisation (5/7). No study at low risk of bias.
- **7 included in vitro studies**: Pauletto 2024 (*C. albicans*), Al-Jobory 2021 (*E. faecalis*), Asnaashari 2020 (*S. aureus*), Chandrappa 2014 (*E. faecalis* + *S. aureus*), Brito-Júnior 2012 (*E. faecalis*), Rai 2019 (*E. faecalis* + *S. aureus*), Banka 2024 (*E. faecalis*).
- **NaOCl (bacterial)**: 5.25% NaOCl fully inhibited *E. faecalis* (CFU = 0) immediately and at 7 days; 5.25% NaOCl reduced *E. faecalis* CFU ~8.5× more than 2% CHX at 1 min (p = 0.003); 6% NaOCl effective within 3 min. NaOCl activity is concentration- and time-dependent.
- **Conflicting findings**: neither 5.25% NaOCl nor 2% CHX eliminated *E. faecalis* after 5 min in one study; another found no significant 2.5% NaOCl vs 2% CHX difference at 5 min — attributed to methodological/assessment differences.
- **CHX**: limited *E. faecalis* data; 2% CHX effective at 5 min in some studies; CHX + cetrimide eliminated *E. faecalis* within 1–5 min. Against **Candida**, 2% CHX was the only agent effective **without agitation** at 1 and 5 min.
- **Bottom line**: higher-concentration NaOCl eliminates *E. faecalis* and *S. aureus* within 1–5 min (gold standard); CHX (± additives) promising, notably against fungal species.
