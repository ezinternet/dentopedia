## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort with 1:1 propensity score matching (312 primary molars aged 3–8y with diagnostically equivocal pulp status [prolonged bleeding 5–10 min or inconclusive radiographs]; 156 matched pairs; pulpotomy with iRoot BP Plus vs pulpectomy with ZOE–iodoform, both with stainless steel crown; Kaplan–Meier/Cox up to 36 months).

24-month survival was significantly higher for pulpotomy (86.4%) than pulpectomy (67.1%; log-rank p<0.001); the advantage was present in asymptomatic teeth (HR=0.42, p=0.002; 91.2% vs 74.5%) but absent in symptomatic teeth (HR=0.88, p=0.41); pulpotomy failures favored internal resorption (32.4% vs 5.9%), pulpectomy failures favored external resorption (33.3% vs 8.8%).

Preoperative pain status is an important effect modifier: in asymptomatic equivocal teeth, pulpotomy is significantly superior, but pain may signal irreversible pulp change that negates the advantage; incomplete SSC sealing (HR=3.12) and ZOE use (HR=1.68) were independent modifiable failure risk factors.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort study (Hefei Stomatological Hospital, China, April 2021–March 2023) addresses primary molars whose pulp status could not be clearly classified as reversible or irreversible pulpitis — the diagnostic "gray zone" defined by prolonged bleeding (5–10 min) after pulp exposure or inconclusive radiographic signs. 312 such molars (after screening 337) were treated with either pulpotomy (iRoot BP Plus bioceramic) or pulpectomy (ZOE–iodoform paste), both restored with stainless steel crowns. Propensity score matching (1:1, caliper 0.2 SD logit; covariates: age, gender, tooth location, ICDAS caries severity, preoperative pain) balanced the two groups into 156 matched pairs (all SMD <0.1).

Kaplan–Meier analysis over 36 months showed pulpotomy had significantly better survival than pulpectomy (log-rank p<0.001), with 24-month survival of 86.4% vs 67.1%. Multivariate Cox regression confirmed treatment modality as an independent predictor (pulpectomy vs pulpotomy HR=0.783, p=0.047), while age, gender, and tooth location were not significant. Critically, the benefit was not uniform: in asymptomatic teeth, pulpotomy showed a large, significant advantage (HR=0.42, p=0.002; 91.2% vs 74.5% survival), while in symptomatic teeth there was no significant difference (HR=0.88, p=0.41) — suggesting preoperative pain may itself be a signal of irreversible pulp change that erases pulpotomy's advantage.

Failure modes also differed qualitatively: pulpotomy failures were more often internal resorption (32.4% vs 5.9%, p<0.01), while pulpectomy failures were more often external resorption (33.3% vs 8.8%, p<00.01) and (non-significantly) periapical radiolucency. Incomplete stainless steel crown sealing (HR=3.12) and ZOE usage (HR=1.68) were independent risk factors for failure, underscoring that restoration quality matters alongside the choice of pulp therapy.

## Key Contributions

- Targets a specific, clinically common but understudied population: primary molars with genuinely equivocal (not clearly reversible or irreversible) pulp diagnosis.
- Provides a much larger (312 teeth / 156 matched pairs), higher-certainty comparison than existing SR+MA evidence on this treatment choice.
- Identifies preoperative pain/symptom status as an effect modifier: pulpotomy's survival advantage holds in asymptomatic equivocal teeth but disappears in symptomatic ones.
- Characterizes distinct radiographic failure signatures (internal resorption after pulpotomy vs external resorption/periapical radiolucency after pulpectomy) useful for follow-up monitoring.
- Flags restoration quality (SSC sealing) and material choice (ZOE) as independent, modifiable failure risk factors.

## Methodology

Single-center, single-operator (20-year-experienced pediatric dentist) retrospective cohort; 312 primary molars (age 3–8y) with equivocal pulp status (prolonged 5–10 min bleeding or inconclusive radiographs); pulpotomy (iRoot BP Plus) vs pulpectomy (ZOE–iodoform), both with stainless steel crown restoration. 1:1 propensity score matching (caliper 0.2 SD logit) on age, gender, tooth location, ICDAS severity, and preoperative pain yielded 156 matched pairs (all covariate SMD <0.1). Outcomes (clinical + radiographic success, evaluated by 3 calibrated examiners, κ=1.0/0.80) analyzed via Kaplan–Meier/log-rank and univariate + multivariate Cox regression; subgroup analysis by preoperative pain; sensitivity analysis excluding censored cases. Follow-up to 36 months (mean 24.6 months).

## Results

- 24-month survival: pulpotomy 86.4% (95% CI 81.2–91.6%) vs pulpectomy 67.1% (95% CI 60.3–73.9%); log-rank p<0.001.
- Asymptomatic subgroup: HR=0.42 (95% CI 0.24–0.73), p=0.002, favoring pulpotomy (91.2% vs 74.5% survival).
- Symptomatic subgroup: HR=0.88 (95% CI 0.49–1.58), p=0.41, no significant difference.
- Multivariate Cox: pulpectomy vs pulpotomy HR=0.783 (95% CI 0.615–0.997), p=0.047, independent of age/gender/tooth location.
- Failure patterns: internal resorption more common after pulpotomy (32.4% vs 5.9%, p<0.01); external resorption more common after pulpectomy (33.3% vs 8.8%, p<0.01); periapical radiolucency NS (14.7% vs 23.5%, p=0.21); clinical failure rate similar (44.1% vs 37.3%, p=0.49).
- Independent failure risk factors (multivariate): incomplete SSC sealing HR=3.12; ZOE usage HR=1.68.
