## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study (14 extracted mandibular second premolars, single-cone technique) comparing polydopamine (PDA)-coated versus uncoated gutta-percha with a bioceramic sealer (CeraSeal) for sealing ability (apical dye penetration, n=7/group), interfacial adaptation (ESEM, n=3/group), and wettability (contact angle, n=14/group).

PDA-coated gutta-percha showed significantly lower apical dye penetration than uncoated gutta-percha (3.04 +/- 0.59 mm vs 4.30 +/- 1.11 mm, p=0.041), no detectable ESEM interfacial gaps in any root region after 28 days in simulated body fluid versus persistent and enlarging gaps (up to 2.8 micrometers) in the uncoated group, and a markedly lower contact angle indicating much-improved wettability (28.75 +/- 9.42 degrees vs 77.42 +/- 27.10 degrees, p<0.001).

Bioinspired PDA coating is a low-cost, simple, retreatment-compatible surface modification that may improve long-term obturation quality with bioceramic sealers, but conclusions are limited to a small in-vitro dye-penetration/SEM design pending clinical, micro-CT, and long-term durability validation.

## Summary

This in-vitro study evaluated whether coating gutta-percha cones with polydopamine (PDA) — a mussel-inspired, catechol/amine-rich bioadhesive polymer formed by dopamine self-polymerization — improves the gutta-percha-sealer interface when used with a bioceramic sealer (CeraSeal, calcium-silicate-based) under the single-cone obturation technique. Gutta-percha cones were dip-coated in dopamine solution for 24 h or 72 h; ATR-FTIR, ESEM, and EDX confirmed successful, uniform PDA deposition, with 72 h chosen for downstream testing. Fourteen extracted mandibular second premolars were obturated with either uncoated or PDA-coated gutta-percha plus CeraSeal (single-cone) and tested for apical dye penetration (n=7/group) after 14 days in simulated body fluid (SBF). A separate 6-root cohort was sectioned at coronal/middle/apical levels and examined by ESEM for interfacial gaps at baseline, 14, and 28 days in SBF (n=3/group). A third cohort of 28 gutta-percha discs was tested for wettability via contact angle with CeraSeal droplets (n=14/group). PDA-coated gutta-percha significantly outperformed uncoated gutta-percha on all three outcomes: lower dye penetration, no detectable SEM interfacial gaps by day 28, and a markedly lower (better) contact angle. PDA-coated gutta-percha also showed SBF bioactivity (calcium/phosphorus deposits by EDX) that uncoated gutta-percha lacked. The authors conclude PDA is a promising, inexpensive, retreatment-compatible surface-modification strategy for improving bioceramic-sealer sealing performance, pending clinical and micro-CT/long-term validation.

## Key Contributions

- First wiki paper testing **polydopamine (PDA)** as a gutta-percha surface coating to enhance interfacial behavior with a **bioceramic sealer** under the **single-cone** technique.
- Confirms PDA deposition via three converging characterization methods (ATR-FTIR functional groups, ESEM uniform layer, EDX elemental signature) and identifies 72 h dip-coating as superior to 24 h.
- Shows PDA-coated gutta-percha is bioactive in simulated body fluid (calcium/phosphorus deposition by EDX after 14 days), unlike uncoated gutta-percha.
- Demonstrates convergent improvement across three independent test methods (dye penetration, ESEM interfacial gap, contact angle) — the most striking finding being **zero detectable interfacial gaps** in the PDA-coated group after 28 days in SBF, versus progressively enlarging gaps (up to 2.8 micrometers) in the uncoated group.

## Methodology

- **Coating**: gutta-percha single cones (Conform Fit, ProTaper Next X3-matched) abraded and cleaned, then dip-coated in 2 mg/mL dopamine hydrochloride/TRIS buffer (pH 8.5) for 24 h or 72 h at 37 degrees C; 72 h selected for all subsequent tests based on ATR-FTIR/ESEM/EDX characterization.
- **Sealing ability** (n=7/group): 14 extracted mandibular second premolars (single straight canal, closed apex), shaped with ProTaper Next X1-X3, obturated single-cone with CeraSeal + uncoated or PDA-coated gutta-percha, stored 1 week for sealer setting, then immersed 14 days in SBF; apical dye penetration (1% methylene blue, 48 h) measured blinded via stereomicroscope.
- **Adaptability** (n=3 roots/group, 3 time points): ESEM interfacial-gap measurements at coronal/middle/apical transverse sections at baseline, day 14, and day 28 of SBF immersion; descriptive only (no inferential statistics given the small sample).
- **Wetting ability** (n=14 discs/group): thermoplasticized gutta-percha discs (uncoated vs PDA-coated), sessile-drop contact angle with CeraSeal measured on an optical tensiometer (spherical cap model), triplicate readings averaged.
- **Stats**: independent Student's t-test (dye penetration, contact angle) after normality checks; power-calculated sample sizes (7 and 14 per group) inflated from minimums of 3 and 5 respectively; significance at p <= 0.05.

## Results

- **Sealing ability (apical dye penetration, mm)**: uncoated 4.30 +/- 1.11 vs PDA-coated 3.04 +/- 0.59; **p=0.041**.
- **Adaptability (interfacial gap, micrometers, descriptive)**: at day 28, uncoated gutta-percha showed gaps of 1.5 (coronal), 2.3 (middle), 2.8 (apical, worsening over time); PDA-coated gutta-percha showed **no detectable gaps in any root region** at day 28 — the study's most striking adaptability finding. At baseline and day 14, PDA-coated gaps were consistently smaller than uncoated at every level, though the apical third remained the largest gap location in both groups until day 28.
- **Wetting ability (contact angle, degrees)**: uncoated 77.42 +/- 27.10 vs PDA-coated 28.75 +/- 9.42; p<0.001 (reported as 0.000001 in text, 0.0001 in the results table).
- **Bioactivity**: PDA-coated gutta-percha developed calcium/phosphorus (calcium-phosphate-like) surface deposits after 14 days in SBF by EDX; uncoated gutta-percha showed no comparable mineral change.
- **Bottom line**: PDA coating significantly improved sealing ability, interfacial adaptation, and wettability of gutta-percha with a bioceramic sealer under the single-cone technique — the null hypothesis was rejected on all three outcomes. Authors frame PDA as inexpensive, scalable, and expected to remain retrievable with conventional retreatment (solvents/mechanical instrumentation) since it forms only a thin organic surface layer without altering gutta-percha's core.
