## Three-line Summary

RCT (BMC Oral Health 2026): 125 patients; 4 EAL devices compared against periapical radiography for working length determination; primary outcome: patient pain/discomfort.

No significant pain differences observed between any of the 4 EALs and periapical radiography; all methods appear equivalent from a patient experience perspective.

EAL-based working length determination causes no more discomfort than conventional radiographic methods, reinforcing EAL as a patient-equivalent (and radiation-free) alternative.

## Summary

Nalci Calik et al. (BMC Oral Health 2026) conducted a multi-arm RCT in 125 patients comparing four different electronic apex locators against periapical radiography as the reference for working length determination. The primary outcome was patient pain/discomfort during the procedure. None of the four EAL devices produced significantly different pain levels compared with radiographic working length determination. This patient-centered RCT complements existing accuracy data for EALs (see kaur-2024, pisano-2024) by demonstrating that EAL use does not impose greater discomfort on patients.

## Key Contributions

- First multi-arm RCT comparing 4 EAL devices vs radiography with patient pain as primary outcome
- Demonstrates patient experience equivalence — EALs are no more uncomfortable than radiography
- Adds patient-centered dimension to EAL evidence base (previously focused on accuracy)
- Head-to-head comparison of 4 EAL devices in same trial (device-specific comparisons possible)

## Methodology

Multi-arm RCT; 125 patients; 5 groups (4 EAL devices + periapical radiography). Primary outcome: patient pain/discomfort (likely VAS or NRS). Statistics: comparative analysis across groups. Journal: BMC Oral Health.
