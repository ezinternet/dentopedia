## Three-line Summary
Prospective 12-month cohort study in a university endodontics clinic; 571 ProTaper Next (M-Wire) files discarded after clinical use by 6 specialist endodontists using X-Smart motors (300 rpm, 4-5.2 Ncm).
Overall defect rate 13.83% (fracture 7.53%, unwinding/curving remainder); X1 17.04 files had 19.87% fracture rate (32/161) and 31.67% total defects; cyclic fatigue accounted for 79.07% of fractures.
Smaller ProTaper Next files (especially X1 17.04) show high clinical fracture/deformation rates supporting single-use policy; all files should be inspected at ≥10× magnification before each use.

## 세줄요약

전체 결함률 13.83% (파절 7.53%, 언와인딩/굴곡 등 나머지); X1 17.04 파일이 파절률 19.87% (32/161), 총 결함률 31.67%로 가장 높았고, 파절의 79.07%가 순환 피로(cyclic fatigue) 기전.
작은 ProTaper Next 파일(특히 X1 17.04)은 임상 파절/변형률이 높아 일회용 사용 원칙 권장; 모든 파일은 사용 전 ≥10배 확대경 검사 필수.

## Summary
This prospective clinical study evaluated fracture and deformation defects in 571 ProTaper Next rotary instruments (M-Wire NiTi alloy) discarded after normal clinical use over a 12-month period in a university endodontics clinic. Six specialist endodontists used the files with X-Smart motors at 300 rpm and 4-5.2 Ncm torque, following a standardized protocol including glide path preparation with K-files and full working length instrumentation. Files were inspected under stereomicroscope before each use and after discard. The overall defect rate was 13.83%, with fracture being the most common defect (7.53%). The X1 17.04 file showed dramatically higher fracture (19.87%) and total defect (31.67%) rates compared to larger files (X2 25.06: 4.40% fracture; X3 30.07: 2.58% fracture; X4/X5: 0% fracture). Stereomicroscopic analysis revealed cyclic fatigue (flexural fatigue) as the dominant fracture mechanism (79.07%), with fragments ranging 1.29–4.27 mm from the original 25 mm length. The authors conclude that smaller ProTaper Next files should be considered single-use instruments and all files require magnification inspection before each use to detect subclinical defects.

## Key Contributions
- First prospective clinical study quantifying size-specific fracture/deformation rates of ProTaper Next files in real-world specialist practice
- X1 17.04 fracture rate of 19.87% provides concrete clinical evidence for single-use recommendation
- Cyclic fatigue identified as primary fracture mechanism (79.07%) in clinical conditions
- Fragment size data (1.29–4.27 mm) highlights risk of undetected fragment retention
- Structured usage protocol with defined discard criteria (max 2 wide-canal molars; single-use for curved/narrow/calcified canals)

## Methodology
- **Design**: Prospective observational cohort study (12 months)
- **Setting**: Exclusive endodontics clinic, University of Santiago de Compostela, Spain
- **Participants**: 6 endodontists trained in ProTaper Next technique
- **Sample**: 571 ProTaper Next files discarded after clinical use
- **Instrumentation**: X-Smart motor (300 rpm, 4-5.2 Ncm); glide path with K-files #08, 10, 15, 20; preparation initiated with X1 17.04; final file matched to apical gauging
- **Usage limits**: ≤2 molars with wide canals per file; curved/narrow/calcified canals — discard after each case
- **Inspection protocol**: Digital caliper length measurement; stereomicroscope examination pre-use and post-discard
- **Defect classification**: Fracture, unwinding, curving/bending
- **Fracture analysis**: Stereomicroscopic typing (cyclic fatigue vs torsional); fragment measurement
- **Statistics**: Chi-square and z-test for defect rate comparisons

**Fracture mechanism (n=43 fractured files):**
- Flexural fatigue (cyclic fatigue): 34 files (79.07%)
- Torsional: 9 files (20.93%)
- Both mechanisms more frequent in X1 files

**Fragment characteristics:**
- Size range: 1.29–4.27 mm (original length 25 mm)
- Cyclic fatigue fragments generally larger than torsional fragments
- Most fractures located in apical third
