## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42024582245) of 8 systematic reviews (7 with meta-analysis, published 2018-2022) on the effect of root canal instrumentation kinematics — rotary vs reciprocating vs manual — on post-endodontic pain (POP), AMSTAR-2 quality-appraised (all "high," 12.5-15/16) and ROBIS bias-assessed.

Rotary instrumentation was generally associated with lower overall POP and better moderate-to-severe pain reduction (Hou XM 2017 OR 2.24-11.71; Sun C 2018 RR 0.32-0.73 vs manual/reciprocating), attributed to reduced apical debris extrusion, while the abstract additionally reports a reciprocating advantage for mild pain within the immediate ~24h phase — a claim that sits awkwardly against the paper's own 24h-SMD figure (0.25, favoring rotary) reported in the Discussion.

No single system was universally superior (multifactorial outcome); major limitations are unreported/uncontrolled operator confounders across all 8 included SRs, heterogeneous VAS-based pain assessment, ≤72h assessment windows, and an unreconciled PRISMA-flow record count in the paper's own Methods/Results.

## Summary

This umbrella review asks a narrow, focused question — does instrumentation *kinematics* (rotary continuous rotation vs reciprocating alternating motion vs manual) affect post-endodontic pain? — by synthesizing 8 systematic reviews (7 with meta-analytic pooling) published 2018-2022. All 8 SRs were rated "high quality" on AMSTAR-2 (12.5-15/16), and ROBIS was used for risk-of-bias/confounder assessment. The synthesis leans toward rotary systems producing lower overall and less severe POP, mechanistically linked to reduced apical debris extrusion, but flags genuine time-dependence and inconsistency across the underlying SRs, plus an apparent internal inconsistency between the abstract's summary claim and its own discussion-level statistics.

## Key Contributions

- Narrowly scoped (kinematics-only) umbrella review complementing broader postop-pain umbrella reviews that span irrigation and obturation as well.
- Formal evidence-stratification model (significance × heterogeneity × quality) plus sensitivity analyses (subgroup by pain severity/time interval, meta-regression) layered on top of the 8 SRs' pooled data.
- Explicit 4-domain confounder scoring (patient/procedure/operator/methodological, 0-2 scale) — operator-related confounders were the weakest-documented domain across all 8 SRs (none reported operator count, experience, magnification use, or torque/speed protocol adherence).

## Methodology

- **Registration**: PROSPERO CRD42024582245; PRISMA-guided; PRIRES checklist used as umbrella-review reporting checklist.
- **Search**: PubMed, Scopus, EBSCOhost, LILACS, Epistemonikos, Google Scholar; 2000 to March 2025; English-language SRs (± MA) plus RCTs grading pain mild/moderate/severe.
- **Screening/quality**: 2 reviewers (title/abstract + full text) + 3rd for disagreements; AMSTAR-2 (16-item, 3 raters + arbiter); ROBIS (3 phases, 4 domains in Phase 2).
- **Evidence grading**: stratified by statistical significance, heterogeneity, and SR quality; sensitivity analyses (exclusion of pre-op-pain studies, subgroup by pain nature/time, meta-regression) where data allowed.
- Included-SR pain-assessment windows varied widely: 6-72h (Cunha TC 2020), first 24h (da Silveira MT 2021; one Oliveira 2019 study within Antony DP 2022), 12/24/48h (Nobar BR 2021; Martins CM 2019), 2/4/6/12/24/36/48h (Krithikadatta 2016), no explicit window (Sun C 2018, Hou XM 2017).
- Data-quality note: the paper's own PRISMA-flow numbers are internally inconsistent — 400,255 records screened → 400,003 duplicates + 50 + 100 excluded leaves 102 for full-text review, but the next sentence in the paper states "50 records were assessed for eligibility." Reproduced here as-is rather than silently corrected.

## Results

- **8 SRs included** (7 with meta-analysis), 2018-2022, in BMC Oral Health (n=2), Iranian Endodontic Journal (n=2), European Endodontic Journal (n=1), Oral Diseases (n=1), Journal of International Oral Health (n=1), Journal of Conservative Dentistry and Endodontics (n=1). AMSTAR-2: all 12.5-15/16 → "high quality."
- **Rotary-favoring SRs**: da Silveira MT 2021 (SMD 0.27-0.37, P≤0.001 vs reciprocating, 21 studies/17 quantitative); Sun C 2018 (RR 0.32 vs manual, P=0.0005; RR 0.73 vs reciprocating, P<0.0001); Hou XM 2017 (OR 2.24-11.71 for moderate/severe pain reduction, attributed to less apical debris extrusion).
- **Reciprocating/mixed-time-dependent SRs**: Neto JCL (reciprocating favored at 48h, RR 1.04; rotary favored at 24h, SMD 0.25, P=0.01 — "no definitive kinematics-pain correlation" per the authors' own reading).
- **No-difference SRs**: Nobar BR 2021 (no significant difference 12-48h); Antony DP 2022 (MD 0.13, P=0.48, I²=26%); Martins CM 2019 (MD 0.13, P=0.48).
- **Glide-path-specific**: Cunha TC 2020 — continuous-rotation glide path (CR-GP) superior to manual (M-GP)/reciprocating glide path (WGP) for intra-/postoperative pain; CR-GP ≈ R-GP.
- **Pooled discussion-level statistics**: 24h — SMD 0.25 (95% CI 0.06-0.44) favoring rotary; 48h — RR 1.04 (95% CI 1.01-1.06) favoring reciprocating.
- **Bottom-line framing (Abstract)**: rotary generally produces lower overall POP; reciprocating "may offer advantages in the immediate post-operative phase by reducing mild pain within 24h" — this claim is not clearly reconciled with the 24h-SMD figure above, an internal inconsistency we flag rather than resolve.
- **Secondary outcomes** (cleaning/shaping effectiveness, debris amount, additional-medication need, complications, radiographic healing): favor rotary systems on debris management and analgesic requirement per the authors' narrative synthesis, though radiographic-healing evidence remained limited across the 8 SRs.
- **Confounder analysis**: patient-related confounders generally well-controlled via inclusion criteria; procedure-related confounders (canal morphology, curvature, irrigation protocol) showed substantial outcome influence with only 2/8 SRs standardizing procedure/equipment; operator-related confounders were essentially uncontrolled/unreported across all 8 SRs (only 3/8 attempted any control; none documented operator count, experience level, magnification, or torque/speed protocol adherence).
