## One-line Summary

Ex vivo study (n=18/group, 6 groups) on extracted human maxillary central incisors found that minimally invasive endodontic (MIE) canal shaping (#40/.04) did not significantly reduce vertical root fracture (VRF) incidence or crack formation compared to conventional enlargement (ISO #80) after chewing simulation, regardless of sealer type; VRF resistance was in fact significantly lower in the +MIE/cement-sealer group than in untreated controls (p=0.020).

## Summary

This ex vivo study tested whether minimally invasive endodontic (MIE) canal shaping — using smaller apical file sizes/tapers (#40/.04) to preserve root dentin — actually protects root-filled teeth against vertical root fracture (VRF), a common clinical assumption. Using 108 extracted human maxillary central incisors randomized into 6 groups (n=18 each: untreated negative control, unfilled shaped-to-#80 positive control, and 4 experimental groups crossing +MIE #40/.04 vs. −MIE ISO #80 shaping with calcium-hydroxide-based vs. adhesive resin-based sealer), roots were fatigued via chewing simulation (staircase loading 25→150 N over 120,000 cycles with thermocycling) and then loaded to fracture. Neither crack/defect incidence nor VRF resistance differed significantly between MIE and conventional shaping groups. The only significant pairwise difference was that untreated teeth had significantly higher fracture resistance than the +MIE/cement-sealer group (p=0.020) — i.e., minimal shaping did not protect, and in one comparison was associated with lower resistance than no treatment at all. The authors conclude minimal canal shaping does not guarantee higher fracture resistance and should not be relied upon as a fracture-protective strategy independent of disinfection needs.

## Key Contributions

- Well-powered (power=0.9), factorial ex vivo test isolating canal-shaping extent and sealer type as independent variables affecting radicular (root-only, decoronated) fracture resistance and crack formation.
- Uses fatigue (chewing-simulation) loading rather than single-cycle load-to-failure, giving more clinically relevant data than most prior in vitro fracture studies.
- Produces a negative/counterintuitive result against the common clinical heuristic that "less shaping = stronger root," directly relevant to how aggressively canals should be enlarged when balancing disinfection needs against fracture-resistance goals.

## Methodology

- Human maxillary central incisors (single straight canal, complete root formation), decoronated to 13 mm standardized root length, screened microscopically to exclude pre-existing defects/caries/prior treatment.
- 6 groups (n=18, stratified-randomized by cross-sectional area): negative control (untreated), positive control (−MIE shaped, unfilled), +MIE/C (Sealapex cement sealer), +MIE/A (RealSeal SE adhesive resin sealer), −MIE/C, −MIE/A. All obturation via single-cone technique.
- +MIE: NiTi rotary shaping to size #40, 0.04 taper. −MIE: further enlarged via rotary + hand K-files to ISO #80 (roughly double the apical diameter of +MIE).
- Roots embedded with simulated periodontal ligament (latex wrap) in acrylic resin, then: 24h water storage → 1500 thermocycles (5–55°C) → chewing simulation (staircase method, 25 N start, +25 N every 20,000 cycles, up to 120,000 cycles at 2 Hz, 10° loading angle).
- Post-fatigue: stereomicroscopic crack/defect grading by root third (cervical/middle/apical; no defect / craze line / vertical crack / horizontal crack), then load-to-fracture testing (universal testing machine, 1 mm/min) with fracture load recorded at 30% force drop.
- Non-parametric statistics: Kruskal–Wallis omnibus + Mann–Whitney U post-hoc with Bonferroni correction (15 comparisons), alpha=0.05.

## Results

- 7/108 teeth failed during chewing simulation (pre-testing failures) and were excluded from analysis.
- Crack/defect incidence: significant omnibus difference (Kruskal–Wallis p=0.006) but NO significant pairwise differences after Bonferroni correction (all p≥0.077). +MIE/A had fewest defects (median 1); unfilled positive control had most (median 3).
- Defect distribution: most defects in middle (73.4%) and cervical (63.4%) thirds vs. apical (31.7%); 56.3% of all defects were vertical root cracks.
- VRF resistance: significant omnibus difference (p=0.008); only significant pairwise finding was untreated teeth > +MIE/C group (p=0.020) — i.e., minimally shaped teeth with cement sealer fractured at LOWER load than untreated teeth. All other pairwise comparisons non-significant (p≥0.068). −MIE/A had the numerically highest resistance among experimental groups (median 1397 N); +MIE/C had the lowest (median 972 N).
- Bottom line: minimal canal shaping did NOT reduce VRF risk or crack incidence relative to more invasive shaping, regardless of sealer (adhesive vs. non-adhesive). The null hypothesis of no difference could not be rejected as a general matter, and where a difference existed it disfavored MIE.
