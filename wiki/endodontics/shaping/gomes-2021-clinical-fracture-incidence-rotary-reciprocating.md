## Three-line Summary
Systematic review and meta-regression (PRISMA) of clinical studies reporting NiTi file fracture incidence, comparing rotary vs reciprocating kinematics. Pooled fracture incidence was 2.27% (rotary 2.43%, reciprocating 1.0%), with no statistically significant difference between kinematics. Meta-regression identified number of teeth treated, operator inexperience, and molar teeth as significant predictors of higher fracture rates.

## 세줄요약

# Clinical fracture incidence of rotary vs reciprocating NiTi files — Gomes 2021 (SR + Meta-regression)

## 📌 Key Findings at a Glance
| Metric | Value | Evidence |
|--------|-------|----------|
| **Overall file fracture incidence** | **2.27%** (95% CI) | 🟢 SR+MA |
| **Rotary kinematics** | 2.43% | 🟢 SR+MA |
| **Reciprocating kinematics** | 1.0% | 🟢 SR+MA |
| **Rotary vs Reciprocating difference** | **Not statistically significant** | 🟢 SR+MA |
| **Significant predictors (meta-regression)** | Teeth treated ↑, Operator inexperience ↑, Molar teeth ↑ | 🟡 Meta-regression |

> **Bottom line:** Clinical NiTi file fracture is low (~2%). Kinematics (rotary vs reciprocating) does **not** significantly predict fracture. Operator experience and case complexity (molars) are stronger drivers.

---

## 🔬 Study Design
- **Type:** Systematic review + meta-regression (PRISMA)
- **Databases:** PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane (search to 2020)
- **Inclusion:** Clinical studies (prospective/retrospective) reporting NiTi file fracture incidence
- **Exclusion:** In vitro, case reports, reviews
- **Quality:** Newcastle-Ottawa Scale
- **Analysis:** Random-effects meta-analysis; meta-regression on study-level covariates (year, sample size, operator experience, tooth type, kinematics)

## 📊 Results Summary

### Overall Fracture Incidence
| Kinematics | Fracture Rate | Note |
|------------|---------------|------|
| **Overall** | **2.27%** (95% CI) | Pooled estimate |
| **Rotary** | 2.43% | Trend higher, NS |
| **Reciprocating** | 1.0% | Trend lower, NS |

> **Statistical note:** The difference between rotary and reciprocating did **not** reach statistical significance.

### Meta-Regression: Significant Predictors of Fracture Rate
| Predictor | Direction | Significance | Evidence Grade |
|-----------|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Number of teeth treated** | Positive association (more teeth → higher fracture) | Significant | 🟡 |
| **Operator proficiency** | Less experience → higher fracture rates | Significant | 🟡 |
| **Tooth type (molars)** | Molars → higher fracture rates | Significant | 🟡 |
| **Kinematics (rotary vs reciprocating)** | No significant difference | **Not significant** | 🟢 |
| Year, sample size, file system | Not significant predictors | NS | 🟡 |

## 💡 Clinical Implications

### 🟢 Strong Recommendations (SR+MA evidence)
1. **Reassure patients:** File fracture is rare (~2% of files/cases).
2. **Prioritize training over kinematics:** Operator proficiency outweighs rotary vs reciprocating choice for fracture prevention.
3. **Exercise extra caution in molars:** Higher fracture incidence in posterior teeth — consider pre-flaring, glide path verification, and conservative file use.

### 🟡 Conditional Recommendations (Meta-regression evidence)
4. **Track file usage per case:** Fracture risk accumulates with number of teeth/canals treated in a session.
5. **Standardize reporting:** Future studies should report fracture per file, per canal, per tooth, and per case for better pooling.

---

## ⚠️ Limitations & Caveats
- **Heterogeneity:** I² substantial across included studies (different file systems, alloys, protocols, operator calibrations)
- **Confounding:** Kinematics confounded with file design, heat treatment, cross-section, and clinical protocol
- **Study design mix:** Retrospective studies prone to recall bias
- **Meta-regression power:** Limited by number of included studies — risk of Type II error for kinematics
- **Publication bias:** Possible (small-study effects)

---

## 🧠 Conceptual Takeaways for Shaping Protocols
- **Kinematics ≠ Fracture destiny:** Reciprocating files show better cyclic fatigue resistance *in vitro*, but this does not translate to clinically significant fracture reduction.
- **Human factor > Material factor:** Training, case selection, and glide path management are higher-yield interventions than switching kinematics.
- **Molar-specific protocols:** Consider dedicated file sequences, stricter reuse limits, and enhanced irrigation/glide path for posterior teeth.

---

## 📚 Related Evidence
- **Cyclic fatigue lab data:** Reciprocating > Rotary (consistent across heat-treated alloys) — *in vitro only*
- **File reuse studies:** Fracture risk increases with reuse cycles, especially in curved canals
- **Glide path literature:** Mechanical glide path reduces torsional stress → lower fracture incidence
