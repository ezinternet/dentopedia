## Three-line Summary

Comprehensive review (narrative, not systematic) evaluating the clinical significance of apical patency in root canal therapy, drawing on recent meta-analyses and clinical studies to address the longstanding debate over whether patency maneuvers improve or worsen postoperative outcomes.

Recent meta-analyses suggest patency may alleviate postoperative pain intensity rather than exacerbate it, though individual study evidence remains conflicting; preoperative CBCT is essential for identifying complex anatomy, and specialized NiTi files with reciprocating kinematics enhance glide path predictability.

Clinicians should prioritize "Anatomical Patency" (respecting original morphology) over forceful "Operative Patency" — failure to achieve patency does not always dictate a negative outcome, but maintaining it is associated with improved long-term healing.

## Summary

Apical patency — the practice of maintaining a small file at or near the apical foramen during root canal preparation — has been a debated clinical concept for decades. This comprehensive review by Ishizaki & Matsuura (2026) synthesizes the available evidence on its clinical significance, its relationship with postoperative pain, the role of preoperative CBCT imaging in navigating complex anatomy, and the technical strategies (specialized files, motor kinematics) that enhance the predictability of glide path establishment.

The paper's central contribution is the conceptual distinction between "Anatomical Patency" (respecting the original root canal morphology during negotiation) and "Operative Patency" (forceful negotiation that extends beyond the apical foramen). The authors argue that clinicians should prioritize the former to ensure procedural integrity and clinical success.

Regarding the contentious relationship between patency and postoperative pain, the review notes that recent meta-analyses suggest patency may actually alleviate discomfort intensity — a finding that contradicts longstanding clinical anxiety about patency maneuvers causing flare-ups. However, the evidence across individual studies remains conflicting, and the authors call for further RCTs with standardized protocols.

Preoperative CBCT is identified as essential for identifying complex anatomy such as the MB2 canal, which significantly impedes patency negotiation when undetected. The use of specialized NiTi files and reciprocating motor modes is shown to enhance the predictability of glide path establishment.

While failure to achieve patency does not always dictate a negative outcome, the review concludes that maintaining patency is associated with improved long-term healing outcomes.

## Key Contributions

1. **First dedicated comprehensive review of apical patency**: A concept debated for decades but never synthesized in a focused review — this fills that gap.
2. **Anatomical vs Operative Patency framework**: The distinction between respecting original morphology (Anatomical Patency) and forceful over-instrumentation (Operative Patency) provides a practical clinical decision-making framework.
3. **Pain–patency relationship**: Synthesizes conflicting evidence from recent meta-analyses, showing the relationship is more nuanced than the traditional fear that patency increases postoperative pain.
4. **CBCT and negotiation technology**: Highlights the role of preoperative 3D imaging and specialized instruments in improving patency outcomes.

## Methodology

- Literature review of clinical studies and recent meta-analyses on apical patency.
- Three focus areas: (1) correlation between patency maneuvers and postoperative pain, (2) role of preoperative CBCT imaging, (3) efficacy of specialized negotiation instruments and motor kinematics.
- Narrative synthesis — not a systematic review with formal inclusion/exclusion criteria or meta-analytic pooling.

## Results

| Domain | Finding |
|---|---|
| Patency → postop pain | Recent meta-analyses suggest patency may alleviate pain intensity (conflicting across individual studies) |
| CBCT | Preoperative CBCT essential for detecting complex anatomy (e.g., MB2 canal) that impedes patency |
| Specialized files | NiTi files + reciprocating motor modes enhance glide path predictability |
| Long-term outcomes | Patency maintenance associated with improved long-term healing; non-patency does not always predict failure |
