## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 5 clinical studies (4370 roots) evaluating whether maintaining apical patency (AP) during endodontic treatment influences healing outcome. Maintaining AP was associated with a 2-fold increase in healing outcome on long-term follow-up, though the evidence base is limited (only 1 RCT among the 5 studies). The review concludes AP likely enhances endodontic outcome but calls for more long-term RCTs.

## Summary

This systematic review addresses whether maintaining apical patency (AP) — keeping a file at or beyond the apical constriction during instrumentation — improves endodontic treatment outcome. The authors conducted a PRISMA-compliant review (PROSPERO CRD42022374966) searching PubMed, Cochrane, LIVIVO, LILACS, and EBSCO host from inception to July 2024. Of 314 screened articles, 5 met inclusion criteria (all rated low risk of bias). The included studies comprised 1 RCT (Arslan et al.) and 4 prospective clinical studies, published between 2011 and 2021, from the UK, Turkey, and US, with follow-up of 1–4 years. Total roots analyzed: 4370.

The key finding was that maintaining AP was associated with a **2-fold increase in healing outcome** on long-term follow-up. Three of 5 studies showed a statistically significant positive influence of AP. Due to heterogeneity in study designs, follow-up durations, and outcome measures, a quantitative meta-analysis was not performed — only qualitative synthesis was possible. The authors conclude that maintaining AP is likely to enhance endodontic treatment outcome, but emphasize the need for more long-term, well-designed RCTs.

**Key clinical takeaway**: Apical patency maintenance appears beneficial for periapical healing, but the evidence remains Level 2b at best (SR of predominantly non-randomized studies). Clinicians should maintain AP as a standard practice when feasible, while acknowledging the limited certainty.

## Key Contributions

- **First systematic review** specifically synthesizing clinical evidence on the influence of AP on endodontic treatment outcome.
- **2-fold healing advantage**: Quantifies the benefit of AP maintenance on long-term outcome.
- **Evidence gap identified**: Only 1 RCT among included studies; highlights the need for more rigorous trial designs.
- **PROSPERO-registered protocol**: Enhances transparency and reproducibility of the review methodology.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review with qualitative synthesis (no meta-analysis).
- **Protocol registration**: PROSPERO CRD42022374966.
- **Search strategy**: PRISMA-compliant; 5 electronic databases (PubMed, Cochrane Library, LIVIVO, LILACS, EBSCO host); searched from inception to July 2024.
- **Screening**: 314 articles → 5 included after full-text assessment.
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane Collaboration tool (for RCT) and ROBINS-I tool (for non-randomized studies); all 5 rated low risk of bias.
- **Inclusion criteria**: Clinical studies (RCTs or prospective clinical studies) evaluating AP influence on endodontic treatment outcome.
