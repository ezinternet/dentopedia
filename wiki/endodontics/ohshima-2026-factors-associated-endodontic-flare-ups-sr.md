## Three-line Summary

A systematic review and meta-analysis of 15 studies (24,320 cases) found an overall severe endodontic flare-up incidence of 2.83%. Preoperative spontaneous pain (RR = 5.83) and percussion pain (RR = 3.45) were the strongest independent risk predictors, followed by non-vital pulp status, periapical lesion, multiple-visit treatment, re-treatment, mandibular location, and female sex. Age, tooth type, canal count, instrumentation technique, and preoperative analgesics showed no significant association with flare-up risk.

## Summary

Endodontic flare-ups — defined as severe postoperative pain and/or swelling requiring unscheduled intervention — remain a clinically significant complication of root canal treatment. This SR/MA synthesized evidence from 15 eligible studies (24,320 cases) identified through a comprehensive search of PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus through September 2025.

The pooled severe flare-up incidence was **2.83%**. The two strongest risk factors were preoperative spontaneous pain (RR = 5.83) and preoperative percussion pain (RR = 3.45), highlighting that pre-existing symptomatic pulp/periapical pathology substantially elevates flare-up probability. Other significant factors included non-vital pulp status, periapical lesion presence, multiple-visit treatment, re-treatment, mandibular tooth location, and female sex.

Importantly, several clinically assumed risk factors — age, tooth type, number of root canals, instrumentation technique, and preoperative analgesic use — were found not significantly associated with flare-up incidence.

The overall GRADE certainty of evidence was rated **low**, owing to study design heterogeneity and predominance of non-randomized designs. The authors call for well-designed prospective studies.

---

## Key Contributions

1. **Baseline incidence**: 2.83% severe flare-up rate across 24,320 cases — actionable figure for informed consent discussions.
2. **Risk hierarchy quantified**: Spontaneous pain (RR 5.83) and percussion pain (RR 3.45) are the dominant modifiable predictors at time of examination.
3. **Null findings**: Instrumentation technique and preoperative analgesics showed no significant effect — challenges the assumption that technique choice or prophylactic analgesia reduces flare-up risk.
4. **GRADE assessment**: Low overall certainty — findings guide clinical reasoning but are not definitive.
