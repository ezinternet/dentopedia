## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of in vitro studies (2010–January 2020) comparing conservative (ConsAC) vs traditional (TradAC) endodontic access cavity designs on fracture resistance of extracted human molars; 105 studies screened, 10 included with low-to-moderate bias.

Only 4 of 10 included studies showed significantly higher fracture resistance with conservative cavities; the rest found no significant difference or mixed results (mandibular molars favored ConsAC in one study, maxillary did not).

Conclusion: insufficient evidence to state that conservative access cavities are superior to traditional designs for fracture resistance; heterogeneous outcomes indicate a need for larger, standardized trials.

## Summary

This systematic review (no meta-analysis) synthesized 10 in vitro comparative studies published 2010–2020 that tested fracture resistance of extracted human permanent molars prepared with conservative (contracted/minimally invasive) versus traditional endodontic access cavities. From an initial 108 hits (59 after de-duplication, 33 full-text screened), 10 studies met PICO inclusion and were graded low-to-moderate risk of bias. Findings were heterogeneous: only 4/10 studies reported a statistically significant fracture-resistance advantage for conservative cavities, while 6/10 showed no significant difference or tooth-position-dependent effects. The authors concluded there is insufficient evidence to declare conservative access uniformly superior, attributing the mixed picture to unstandardized cavity dimensions, varied load-testing protocols, and confounding from obturation/restoration status. The later network meta-analysis by Motiwala et al. (2022) provides a higher-tier quantitative ranking on this same question and partially supersedes this page.

## Key Contributions

- First dedicated systematic review focused specifically on **molars** (versus single-rooted teeth) for the conservative-vs-traditional access fracture-resistance question, extending in vitro incisor evidence such as [[endodontics/anatomy/rover-2020-influence-minimally-invasive-endodontic-access]].
- Documented that the conservative-access fracture-resistance benefit is **not uniform**: 4/10 studies positive, 6/10 null or mixed, with a notable **mandibular > maxillary** effect (Osman & Ahmed 2018) that the pooled NMA does not resolve.
- Catalogued the main **methodological confounders** driving heterogeneity — unstandardized access dimensions, static vs thermomechanical loading, and testing teeth without obturation/restoration (restoration alone restores ~72% of intact fracture strength), which inflate or mask the true access-design effect.
- Reinforced the biomechanical rationale (peri-cervical dentin and marginal-ridge preservation reduce cuspal flexure) while showing this does **not** consistently translate to higher measured fracture resistance under laboratory loading.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review of in vitro comparative studies; qualitative narrative synthesis (no meta-analysis, heterogeneity precluded pooling).
- **Databases**: PubMed, ScienceDirect, Scopus, ResearchGate, EBSCOhost, Wiley Online Library, Google Scholar; January 2010 – January 2020; English only.
- **Screening**: 108 records → 59 de-duplicated → 33 full-text → 10 included.
- **PICO**: Population = extracted mature human permanent molars; Intervention = conservative/minimally invasive access; Comparison = traditional access; Outcome = fracture resistance (load-to-failure, N or MPa).
- **Risk of bias**: Modified in vitro quality tool; included studies graded low-to-moderate.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Studies included / screened | 10 / 105 (108 raw hits) |
| Studies favoring conservative access | 4 / 10 (statistically significant) |
| Studies with no significant difference / mixed | 6 / 10 |
| Tooth-position effect (Osman & Ahmed 2018) | Mandibular: CEC > TEC (P<0.05); Maxillary: no difference |
| Largest positive effect | Makati 2018 (CEC > TEC, ~1662 N mean difference) |
| Restoration effect (Maske 2020) | MI access alone did not improve crown strength; CEC+restoration > TEC+restoration (borderline) |
| Overall risk of bias | Low-to-moderate |
| Pooled quantitative estimate | None (no meta-analysis) — see Motiwala 2022 NMA |
| Bottom line | Insufficient evidence for uniform superiority of conservative access |
