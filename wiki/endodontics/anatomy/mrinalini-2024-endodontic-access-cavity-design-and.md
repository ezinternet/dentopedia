## Three-line Summary
Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, 14 in vitro studies) comparing fracture resistance of mandibular molars prepared with conventional endodontic access (CEA) versus conservative (contracted) and truss access cavity designs.
Conservative access showed significantly higher fracture resistance than conventional access (SMD = 2.61, 95% CI 1.47–3.74, p < 0.001; I² = 92%); truss access also outperformed conventional access (SMD = −1.26, 95% CI −1.81 to −0.71, p < 0.001; I² = 65.6%).
Newer minimally invasive access designs offer a measurable biomechanical advantage over conventional wide access, though high heterogeneity and restriction to in vitro mandibular molars limit direct clinical translation.

## 세줄요약
PRISMA 2020 지침에 따른 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (14개 생체외 연구): 종래 근관치료 접근 (Conventional Endodontic Access, CEA) 대 보존적 (축소형) 및 트러스 (Truss) 접근 설계 간 하악구치 파절저항강도 (Fracture Resistance) 비교.
보존적 접근이 종래 접근 대비 유의하게 높은 파절저항강도 (표준화 평균차 [Standardized Mean Difference, SMD] = 2.61, 95% CI 1.47–3.74, p < 0.001; 이질성 [Heterogeneity, I²] = 92%); 트러스 접근도 종래 접근 대비 우월 (SMD = −1.26, p < 0.001; I² = 65.6%).
보존적 최소침습 접근 설계가 생역학적 이점을 제공하나, 높은 이질성 및 하악구치 생체외 연구로의 제한으로 직접적 임상 전환에 주의 필요.

## Summary
This PRISMA 2020-registered systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized 14 in vitro studies to compare fracture resistance of endodontically treated mandibular molars across three access cavity design categories: conventional, conservative (contracted), and truss. Standardized mean differences were pooled using STATA software. Conservative access cavities yielded significantly greater fracture resistance than conventional access (SMD = 2.61, p < 0.001), and truss designs similarly outperformed conventional access (SMD = −1.26, p < 0.001), despite high heterogeneity in the conservative comparison (I² = 92%). These findings provide level-1 in vitro evidence that preserving pericervical dentin through smaller access openings meaningfully increases load-bearing capacity. However, all included studies used extracted, non-vital teeth with no periodontal ligament, and no clinical trial data existed, limiting direct generalizability to in vivo fracture behavior.

## Key Contributions
- **Quantified effect size for conservative access benefit**: SMD of 2.61 for conservative vs. conventional access is the first pooled estimate at meta-analytic level, giving clinicians a concrete magnitude of biomechanical benefit from tooth structure preservation.
- **Truss design evidence**: Confirms truss (intervening dentin bridge) approach also provides statistically significant fracture resistance advantage (SMD = −1.26), broadening the evidence base beyond contracted-access alone.
- **Risk-of-bias transparency**: 78.6% of included studies rated low risk via modified QUIN tool; no high-risk studies included, supporting reliability of pooled estimates despite high heterogeneity.
- **Heterogeneity characterization**: I² = 92% for conservative vs. conventional comparison signals substantial study-level variability driven by instrumentation systems, tooth anatomy pre-selection, and loading protocols — identifies the primary methodological gap requiring standardization.

## Methodology
- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis; PRISMA 2020; pre-registered at Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/zmh6p).
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, EBSCOHost, BVS, Wiley, Google Scholar; January 2000 – July 2024; English-language only.
- **Inclusion**: In vitro/ex vivo studies; extracted mandibular molars; ≥3 access cavity designs compared; fracture resistance as primary outcome (load-to-fracture in N or MPa); mean ± SD reported or calculable.
- **Exclusion**: Narrative reviews, case reports, clinical studies, non-English, missing data.
- **Quality assessment**: Modified QUIN tool (low / moderate / high risk of bias).
- **Statistics**: STATA; standardized mean difference (SMD) with 95% CI; forest plots; I² heterogeneity; α = 0.05.
- **Selection**: 243 articles identified → 14 met strict inclusion criteria; all 14 included in quantitative synthesis.
