## Three-line Summary
In vitro study (n=30 extracted mandibular molars) comparing traditional (TEC), conservative (CEC), and truss (TAC) access cavity designs across instrumentation efficacy, microbial reduction, filling quality, and fracture resistance using micro-CT and Reciproc Blue files.
Conservative and truss access cavities left significantly larger unprepared canal surface areas and greater residual filling material in the pulp chamber (P < 0.05) versus TEC, with no compensating advantage in microbial reduction, canal transportation, centering ability, filling voids, or fracture resistance (all P > 0.05).
Traditional straight-line access (TEC) remains the evidence-based standard for mandibular molar endodontics; minimally invasive designs sacrifice instrumentation completeness without measurable clinical benefit.

## Summary
This in vitro study used micro-CT and standardized microbiological techniques to compare three endodontic access cavity designs—Traditional Endodontic Cavity (TEC), Conservative Endodontic Cavity (CEC), and Truss Access Cavity (TAC)—across 30 extracted mandibular molars divided into matched groups of ten. Reciproc Blue reciprocating files (R25 then R40) with 0.5% NaOCl and 17% EDTA irrigation were used for instrumentation, and *Enterococcus faecalis* biofilm provided a standardized microbial challenge. Conservative and truss designs consistently underperformed TEC in canal surface preparation, leaving larger uninstrumented areas and more residual obturation material in the pulp chamber. No compensating benefit was detected in antimicrobial efficacy, canal geometry preservation, filling quality, or post-restoration fracture resistance. These findings challenge the rationale for adopting minimally invasive access designs in mandibular molar endodontics and support the primacy of traditional straight-line access.

## Key Contributions
- Three-way in vitro comparison of TEC, CEC, and TAC on mandibular molars across instrumentation, microbiological, obturation, and biomechanical endpoints in a single study
- Quantitative micro-CT evidence that conservative/truss cavities produce larger unprepared canal surface areas than TEC (P < 0.05) without reducing dentin removal overall
- Demonstration that remaining root filling material volume in the pulp chamber is significantly greater in CEC and TAC groups (P < 0.05), indicating a cleaning disadvantage
- Refutation of the fracture-resistance argument for conservative access: post-restoration fracture resistance was equivalent across all three designs (P > 0.05)

## Methodology
- **Design**: In vitro laboratory study
- **Sample**: 30 extracted intact mandibular molars, matched by micro-CT anatomical features; n=10 per group
- **Groups**: TEC (straight-line reference), CEC (minimally invasive), TAC (dentin-bridge-preserving conservative)
- **Microbial model**: *Enterococcus faecalis* suspension with 21-day biofilm incubation
- **Instrumentation**: Reciproc Blue R25 → R40; 0.5% NaOCl (8 mL/instrument); final flush: 2 mL 0.5% NaOCl + 2 mL 17% EDTA + 2 mL 0.5% NaOCl
- **Imaging**: Micro-CT pre-/post-instrumentation and post-filling
- **Outcomes**: CFU/mL bacterial counts at four sampling points; unprepared canal surface area (%); dentin volume removed (%); canal transportation and centering ability; root filling voids (%); remaining filling material in pulp chamber (volume); fracture resistance (N) after temporary restoration
- **Statistics**: Negative binomial and beta 0-1 inflation regression for microbial data; ANOVA + Tukey HSD for remaining outcomes (α = 0.05)
