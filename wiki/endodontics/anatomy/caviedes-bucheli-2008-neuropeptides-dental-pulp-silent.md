## Three-line Summary

The five principal neuropeptides of dental pulp — SP, CGRP, NKA, NPY, and VIP — govern neurogenic inflammation, vascular regulation, and immune-cell crosstalk via specific receptors. Their release during dental procedures and extensive pulp/dentin injury drives plasticity changes, and their cascading action can lead to pulp necrosis and periapical lesion growth. Receptor antagonists (NK1, NK2, CGRP-R) represent pharmacologic targets for modulating pulpal and periapical inflammation.

## Summary

Caviedes-Bucheli et al. (2008) provide a comprehensive narrative review of the five principal neuropeptides found in dental pulp: Substance P (SP, 물질 P), Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide (CGRP, 칼시토닌 유전자 관련 펩타이드), Neurokinin A (NKA, 뉴로키닌 A), Neuropeptide Y (NPY, 뉴로펩타이드 Y), and Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide (VIP, 혈관작용장폴리펩타이드).

The paper synthesizes evidence on:
- **Pulp physiology**: Each neuropeptide's normal homeostatic role — SP/CGRP as pro-inflammatory vasodilators from C/Aδ sensory fibers; NPY as a sympathetic vasoconstrictor/anti-inflammatory co-transmitter; VIP as a parasympathetic vasodilator with immunomodulatory effects.
- **Release during dental procedures**: Operative stimuli, caries, and pulpal injury trigger neuropeptide liberation from nerve terminals, initiating neurogenic inflammation (신경성 염증).
- **Neuroplasticity in extensive injury**: Pulp and dentin injury induces altered neuropeptide expression (upregulation of SP/CGRP, shifts in NPY/VIP distribution) — a plasticity response that amplifies or modulates the inflammatory cascade.
- **Receptor-mediated interactions**: Neuropeptides bind specific receptors on pulp fibroblasts, odontoblasts, and immune cells (macrophages, mast cells, dendritic cells), integrating the nervous and immune systems in pulpal pathology.
- **Neurogenic inflammation → pulp necrosis**: Unresolved neuropeptide-driven neurogenic inflammation results in pulp necrosis through sustained vasodilation, plasma extravasation, and immune amplification.
- **Periapical lesion growth**: Both peripherally and centrally released neuropeptides contribute to periapical lesion expansion — SP and CGRP stimulate osteoclastogenesis and bone resorption in periapical tissues.
- **Central sensitization**: Centrally released neuropeptides in the trigeminal nucleus amplify dental pain perception beyond the peripheral stimulus.
- **Therapeutic pharmacology**: NK1 receptor antagonists (block SP), NK2 antagonists (block NKA), and CGRP receptor antagonists are proposed as targets for controlling pulpal neurogenic inflammation and periapical pathology.

This 2008 review represents the foundational reference for the same group's subsequent focused reviews: the 2025 IEJ paper on the endogenous opioid system in orthodontic pain, and the 2026 IEJ paper on NPY specifically in dental pulp. The neuropeptide framework established here — receptor-mediated pulp-immune crosstalk, plasticity, and pharmacologic antagonism — recurs as the conceptual backbone across the series.

## Clinical Relevance

- Neurogenic inflammation may explain post-operative pulpal pain and progression to necrosis independent of bacterial contamination.
- NPY's sympathetic anti-inflammatory role offers a counterweight to the pro-inflammatory SP/CGRP axis — relevant to understanding pain variability between patients.
- Receptor antagonist pharmacology (NK1, CGRP-R) foreshadows modern dental pain management research.
- CGRP density provides a proxy for pulp nerve fiber load — relevant to pulp vitality assessment concepts.
