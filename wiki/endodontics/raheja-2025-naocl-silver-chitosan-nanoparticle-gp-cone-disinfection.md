## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study (335 F1 ProTaper GP cones; 270 for antimicrobial arm, 65 for AFM; organisms: B. subtilis MTCC 441 and C. albicans MTCC 227) comparing 5.25%/2.62% NaOCl, AgNPs (250 µg/mL), and ChNPs (625 µg/mL) on disinfection efficacy and GP-cone surface topography by tapping-mode AFM.

Both NaOCl concentrations eliminated both organisms in 1 min (0 CFU) with minimal AFM surface change (RMS 103–108 vs control 124.6); AgNPs and ChNPs cleared C. albicans in 1 min but required 5 min for B. subtilis; all agents produced progressively worse surface damage with extended immersion (RMS ~40–44 at 5 min).

NaOCl (either concentration) is the superior chairside GP-cone disinfectant for speed and surface safety; nanoparticle agents confer no efficacy advantage and impose comparable surface damage, supporting the established 1-minute NaOCl protocol.

## Summary

Gutta-percha (GP) cones cannot be autoclaved (thermoplastic), so contaminated cones — even from freshly opened boxes or after chairside exposure — must be chemically disinfected before obturation. This in vitro study compared the established agent, sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl, at 2.62% and 5.25%), against two nanoparticle disinfectants — silver nanoparticles (AgNPs, 250 µg/mL) and chitosan nanoparticles (ChNPs, 625 µg/mL) — for both disinfection efficacy and the surface damage they inflict, the latter quantified by atomic force microscopy (AFM). NaOCl at both concentrations killed both a Gram-positive bacterium (*Bacillus subtilis*) and a yeast (*Candida albicans*) within 1 min and caused only minimal surface alteration at that time, whereas the nanoparticle agents needed 5 min to clear *B. subtilis*. All agents produced progressively worse surface topography with longer immersion, reinforcing the classic short (1-min) NaOCl protocol.

## Key Contributions

- Direct head-to-head of NaOCl (2.62%, 5.25%) vs AgNPs and ChNPs specifically for GP-cone disinfection — a use for which the nanoparticles had not previously been tested (only as intracanal medicaments).
- Adds a *quantitative* AFM (tapping-mode, RMS roughness) surface-damage read-out across 1, 3, and 5 min immersion, improving on SEM's qualitative 2-D imaging.
- Reports MIC/MBC of all three agents against both a bacterium and a yeast, letting efficacy be weighed against surface-damage cost.

## Methodology

- **Agents**: 5.25% and 2.62% NaOCl (PPH CERKAMED); AgNPs ~40 nm (250 µg/mL working); ChNPs 50–500 nm (625 µg/mL working).
- **Cones**: 335 F1 ProTaper (Dentsply Maillefer) from fresh boxes — 270 for the antimicrobial arm (130 per organism), 65 for AFM.
- **Organisms**: *Bacillus subtilis* MTCC 441 and *Candida albicans* MTCC 227.
- **MIC/MBC**: broth microdilution (0.5 McFarland, 24 h/37 °C) and CFU assay (MBC = CFU < 30).
- **Contamination**: 30 min in ~10⁸ CFU/mL BHI suspension at 37 °C.
- **Disinfection**: immersion 1, 3, or 5 min (n = 10 per cell); post-treatment culture in thioglycollate broth then spread-plated (BHI for bacteria, SDA for fungus); CFU counted.
- **AFM**: Bruker Dimension Icon, tapping mode; cones sectioned 3 mm from tip; RMS roughness compared to fresh controls (n = 5).
- **Statistics**: two-way ANOVA + Bonferroni (GraphPad Prism v5.0).

## Results

**Disinfection efficacy (mean CFU):**
- *C. albicans*: all four treatments (2.62% NaOCl, 5.25% NaOCl, AgNPs, ChNPs) → 0 CFU at 1 min.
- *B. subtilis*: both NaOCl concentrations → 0 CFU at 1 min. AgNPs = 4121.2 CFU (1 min) → 341 (3 min) → 0 (5 min). ChNPs = 3.7 CFU (1 min) → 1.4 (3 min) → 0 (5 min).
- ChNPs significantly outperformed AgNPs against *B. subtilis* at 1 and 3 min (P < 0.05).

**MIC/MBC (µg/mL):** AgNPs — *B. subtilis* 31.25/62.5, *C. albicans* 250/250. ChNPs — 312.25/625 for both organisms. 5.25% NaOCl — 4653 (MIC) with MBC 19,513 (*B. subtilis*) / 4653 (*C. albicans*).

**AFM surface topography (RMS; lower = more deterioration):**
- Fresh control RMS = 124.6.
- At **1 min**, only AgNPs (76.2) differed significantly from control; NaOCl (5.25% = 103.08, 2.62% = 108) and ChNPs (121.2) did not — i.e. NaOCl caused minimal alteration at 1 min.
- At **3 min**, all agents significantly reduced RMS vs control (5.25% NaOCl 45.36; 2.62% NaOCl 60; AgNPs 52.6; ChNPs 79.6).
- At **5 min**, all remained significantly reduced (RMS ~40–44).
- Surface damage increases progressively with immersion time for every agent.

**Bottom line**: NaOCl (either concentration) is the most effective chairside GP-cone disinfectant — clearing both organisms in 1 min with minimal surface change — and the nanoparticle alternatives offer no efficiency advantage, needing 5 min against *B. subtilis* and inflicting comparable time-dependent surface damage.
