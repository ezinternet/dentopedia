## Three-line Summary

Retrospective clinicopathological series (Taiwan, J Dent Sci 2021): 7 histopathology-confirmed periapical scars from 445 periapical lesion biopsies (prevalence 1.6%); all 7 presented as persistent well-defined radiolucency, 86% asymptomatic, lesion size 3–10 mm (mean 5.6 mm); 0/7 suspected as PS clinically before surgical removal.

Histopathology: dense fibrous collagenous tissue with absent or minimal chronic inflammation; hyalinization in long-standing cases; 1/7 contained amalgam particles from prior retrograde filling, confirmed by polarized microscopy.

Five clinical criteria proposed for PS diagnosis without histopathology — when all 5 present, conservative monitoring is appropriate and unnecessary periapical surgery can be avoided.

## Summary

### Background

Periapical scar (PS) is an alternative healing response to periapical inflammatory lesions, producing dense fibrous collagenous tissue instead of alveolar bone after endodontic therapy. Unlike periapical granuloma and radicular cyst — the two most studied periapical lesions — PS is rare, benign, and requires no treatment. Its clinical and radiographic features closely mimic treatment failure, making it a frequent source of unnecessary retreatment or surgery.

The central clinical problem: PS presents as a persistent periapical radiolucency indistinguishable on routine radiographs from pathological entities. Histopathological examination of surgically removed tissue is currently the only definitive diagnostic method. This study aimed to characterize the clinical and radiographic features sufficiently to allow PS diagnosis without surgery in appropriate cases.

### Study Design

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | Retrospective clinicopathological series |
| Setting | Private pathological center, Taipei, Taiwan |
| Study period | August 2020 – February 2021 |
| Total biopsies reviewed | 922 tissue specimens (445 periapical lesions) |
| PS cases included | 7 (1 excluded: questionable endodontic origin) |
| PS prevalence | 1.6% of 445 periapical lesions |
| Gold standard | H&E histopathology (2 independent oral pathologists) |

Inclusion criteria: (1) histopathological PS diagnosis with available records; (2) periapical/panoramic radiograph showing radiolucency at root apex of endodontically treated tooth; (3) microscopy showing fibrous collagenous tissue with no or minimal chronic inflammation.

Periapical lesion composition in the biopsy pool: 197 granuloma (44.3%), 239 radicular cyst (53.7%), 8 PS (1.8%), 1 abscess (0.2%).

### Results

**Demographic and clinical features of 7 PS cases:**

| Feature | Finding |
|---|---|
| Location | Maxilla 3, Mandible 4 |
| Sex | 3M : 4F (ratio 1:1.3) |
| Age | Mean 51±15 years (range 30–78) |
| Teeth involved | Incisors 4, 1st/2nd molars 3 |
| Symptomatic | 1/7 (mild pain, case 2) |
| Clinical signs | 2/7 (swelling/redness case 2; labial cortical fenestration case 7) |
| Prior treatment | NSRCT/retreatment only: 5; NSRCT + periapical surgery: 2 |
| Pre-histopathology diagnosis | Radicular cyst: 5/7; Granuloma: 2/7; PS: 0/7 |
| Lesion size (radiograph) | 3–10 mm (mean 5.6±2.6 mm) |
| Radiographic character | Persistent, well-defined radiolucency in all 7 |

**Histopathological findings:**
- All 7: dense fibrous collagenous tissue, no or focal minimal chronic inflammation
- Long-standing cases: hyalinization of fibrous connective tissue
- Case 7 (prior retrograde amalgam filling): amalgam particles in fibrous tissue — large fragments surrounded by minimal inflammation; fine granules by mild chronic inflammation (confirmed polarized microscopy)

### Clinical Diagnostic Criteria

The authors propose 5 clinical criteria for PS diagnosis without histopathology. All 5 should be present:

1. **Adequate root canal filling** — previous NSRCT/retreatment shows technically satisfactory obturation
2. **No symptoms or signs** — tooth is asymptomatic; no soft tissue changes at the periapical area
3. **No root fracture; healthy periodontium** — except for the periapical radiolucency itself
4. **Stable well-defined radiolucency** — persistent without significant size change over long-term follow-up
5. **Well-performed periapical surgery** *(if applicable)* — retrograde filling is adequate

**When all 5 criteria are met:** long-term follow-up is a conservative and appropriate strategy. Periapical surgery for histopathological confirmation remains an option if clinical suspicion persists for other diagnoses (malignancy, cyst not amenable to observation).

**Surveillance caveat:** A quiescent PS should remain asymptomatic. New symptoms or signs suggest bacterial reinfection from intraradicular or extraradicular sources and should trigger reassessment, not automatic assumption that the lesion remains a scar.

### Proposed Mechanisms

Why some periapical lesions heal with bone regeneration and others with fibrous scar remains incompletely understood. This paper synthesizes proposed mechanisms:

1. **Cortical plate loss** — PS occurs most commonly when both facial and lingual cortical plates are absent, removing the periosteum/endosteum reservoir of osteoblasts; connective tissue-forming cells colonize before bone-forming cells
2. **Cellular competition (Nair)** — connective tissue-forming cells reach the periapical area before cells responsible for generating the structural components of the apical periodontium
3. **Collagen remodeling failure** — excess type III collagen production not replaced by type I collagen; deficiency of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) unable to degrade over-produced extracellular matrix
4. **TGF-β and myofibroblast overactivation** — macrophage over-production of TGF-β; myofibroblast over-production of collagenous proteins during wound healing remodeling phase

### Limitations

- Very small n (7 cases): no statistical power; estimates unreliable
- Single-center, single 7-month period: selection bias from referral pathology center
- No control group (cases without PS undergoing similar follow-up)
- Radiographic size measured on 2D periapical/panoramic radiograph — not volumetric
- No long-term follow-up data post-diagnosis; natural history of untreated PS not characterized
- 5-criterion clinical diagnosis framework is expert consensus based on 7 cases only; not validated prospectively

## Key Contributions

- Provides the only systematic PS clinicopathological series with explicit criteria-based histopathological inclusion
- PS prevalence 1.6% in biopsy series (cf. Grung 1990: 9% of surgical follow-up cases — different denominators)
- Demonstrates 100% misdiagnosis rate pre-histopathology: 5/7 called radicular cyst, 2/7 granuloma, 0/7 PS
- Formalizes 5-criterion clinical diagnosis framework for PS identification without surgery
- Mechanistic synthesis: cortical plate loss + cellular competition + collagen remodeling failure
- Foreign body findings: amalgam particle size determines inflammatory response within fibrous scar
