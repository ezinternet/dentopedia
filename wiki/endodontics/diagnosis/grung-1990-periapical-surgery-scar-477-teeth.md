## Three-line Summary

Retrospective follow-up of 477 teeth treated by periapical surgery (single surgeon, Norwegian county hospital): complete healing 78%, scar tissue healing 9%, combined success 87%, failure 13%; outcomes confirmed by independent endodontist and oral surgeon with radiologist adjudication.

Periapical fibrous scar incidence (Group 2) was strongly linked to large preoperative lesion size; retrograde fill technique carried 28% failure vs 4% with orthograde fills.

Established the canonical ~9% post-surgical periapical scar rate and validated scar tissue as a clinically acceptable "success" outcome, formally separating it from endodontic failure in the radiographic classification system.

## Summary

### Background

Before Grung 1990, there was no large-scale radiographic follow-up study specifically classifying periapical scar tissue healing as a distinct outcome category after periapical surgery. Clinicians lacked a data foundation for distinguishing scar tissue from genuine treatment failure. This study provided the first robust epidemiological estimate of post-surgical scar tissue incidence.

### Study Design

- Design: Retrospective radiographic follow-up until stable radiographic situation
- Setting: Norwegian county hospital, single surgeon
- n = 477 teeth treated by periapical surgery
- Outcome classification: 4-group radiographic system
- Adjudication: independent endodontist + oral surgeon; disagreements → joint evaluation; borderline → oral radiologist

### Radiographic Outcome Classification

| Group | Designation | Frequency | Clinical Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Complete healing | 78% | Full periapical bone regeneration |
| 2 | Incomplete healing (scar tissue) | 9% | Persistent radiolucency = fibrous scar; classified as SUCCESS |
| 3 | Uncertain healing | ~4% | Equivocal — extended observation needed |
| 4 | Unsatisfactory healing | ~9% | True failure |
| **Success** | Groups 1+2 | **87%** | — |
| **Failure** | Groups 3+4 | **13%** | — |

### Scar Tissue Risk Factors

1. **Preoperative lesion size**: Large periapical lesions → significantly higher scar tissue rate. When both facial and lingual cortical plates are lost, osteoblasts cannot migrate from adjacent periosteum/endosteum → fibrous connective tissue colonizes before bone-forming cells arrive (Nair 2006 mechanism).
2. **Retrograde fill technique**: 28% failure rate vs 4% for orthograde group — independent predictor of poor outcome regardless of scar tissue classification.

### Limitations

- Abstract-only: specific n for each group, follow-up duration, and statistical analysis details not available from abstract
- Single surgeon, single institution: skill-dependent outcomes may not generalize
- 1990 methodology: pre-CBCT, 2D radiographic classification; scar tissue defined radiographically, not histopathologically
- No patient-level risk factor analysis in abstract (age, sex, tooth type)

## Key Contributions

- Established the **canonical ~9% post-surgical periapical scar tissue incidence** — still cited as the primary epidemiological reference
- Formally classified scar tissue as **"incomplete healing" (Group 2) = SUCCESS**, not failure — conceptual foundation for the entire scar tissue surveillance literature
- Identified large preoperative lesion size as a primary risk factor for scar tissue healing
- Demonstrated retrograde fill inferiority (28% vs 4% failure) independent of scar tissue outcome
