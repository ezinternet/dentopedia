## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional diagnostic accuracy study (n=57, biopsy gold standard, King Saud University) testing CBCT adjusted grey density values to distinguish periapical cystic from solid lesions: AUC 0.44 (P=0.45) — chance level, not diagnostically useful.

Measurement reliability was excellent (intra- and inter-examiner ICC); the failure is intrinsic — grey density does not encode the structural difference between cyst fluid and granuloma tissue at current CBCT resolution.

Clinicians should not use CBCT grey density to infer cyst vs granuloma; this negative result, alongside the volume-based decision tree (Pitcher 2017), defines CBCT's operative diagnostic limits pre-surgically.

## Summary

This study directly tests whether CBCT grey (Hounsfield-equivalent) density values can non-invasively distinguish periapical cysts from solid granulomas before surgery. The motivation is clinically important: if CBCT could reliably predict lesion type, it could guide the decision between non-surgical retreatment (granuloma likely heals) and direct surgical management (true cyst unlikely to resolve non-surgically).

The result is a clear negative: AUC 0.44 is statistically indistinguishable from random classification. Crucially, measurement reliability was excellent — the examiners could measure grey density consistently, but the values themselves carried no diagnostic information about lesion type.

This establishes that at current CBCT resolution and grey-value calibration, the fluid/necrotic contents of a cyst and the cellular/vascular contents of a granuloma produce overlapping grey density signals. The structural difference is real histologically but is not resolved by CBCT grey values as a discriminating feature.

## Key Contributions

- **Definitive negative result** for CBCT grey density as a cyst/granuloma discriminator (AUC 0.44, P=0.45)
- Separates measurement reliability (excellent) from diagnostic utility (absent) — important methodological distinction
- Directly contradicts any clinical assumption that "darker" or "lighter" lesions on CBCT indicate cyst vs granuloma
- Narrows the diagnostic role of CBCT to **morphometric features** (volume, shape, cortical displacement) rather than density

## Results

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| n | 57 patients |
| Lesion types | Cystic vs solid (biopsy confirmed) |
| AUC | **0.44** (P = 0.45) |
| Best-cutoff accuracy | 0.54 |
| Best-cutoff sensitivity | 1.00 |
| Best-cutoff specificity | **0.075** |
| Measurement reliability | Excellent (intra- and inter-examiner) |

The optimal cutoff achieves 100% sensitivity but only 7.5% specificity — in practice, it would classify nearly every lesion as cystic, making the test clinically useless.

## Clinical Implications

1. **Do not use grey density** to pre-operatively classify periapical lesions as cyst vs granuloma.
2. **Volume is the better CBCT predictor** (Pitcher 2017: lesion volume >247 mm³ → 80% cyst probability).
3. For pre-surgical planning, volumetric analysis and lesion morphology (cortical expansion, root displacement) are more informative than density.
4. Histopathology after biopsy/surgery remains the definitive diagnostic standard (Nair 2006).
