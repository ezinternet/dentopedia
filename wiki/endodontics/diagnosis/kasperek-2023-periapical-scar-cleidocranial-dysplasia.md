## Three-line Summary

Case report (University of Liverpool, J Endod 2023): 18-year-old female with cleidocranial dysplasia (CCD) presented with persistent periapical radiolucency at tooth #9 after prior endodontic treatment; asymptomatic apical periodontitis and endodontic failure were initially suspected.

Extensive childhood periapical surgery for CCD-related supernumerary teeth had produced fibrous scar tissue — correctly re-diagnosed as periapical scar after clinical/radiographic evaluation, averting unnecessary retreatment.

Periapical fibrous scar prevalence is 2.5–12%; risk factors include prior periapical surgery, large preoperative lesions, and cortical plate loss; long-term monitoring without intervention is the appropriate management once scar is diagnosed.

## Summary

### Background

Periapical fibrous scars are a benign but diagnostically challenging sequela of endodontic treatment, occurring when healing produces dense collagenous fibrous tissue rather than bone regeneration. Their prevalence is 2.5–12% post-treatment. Because they persist as radiolucent lesions on periapical radiographs, they are frequently misdiagnosed as endodontic failure, triggering unnecessary retreatment or surgery.

Cleidocranial dysplasia (CCD) is a rare autosomal dominant skeletal disorder caused by RUNX2 gene mutations, characterized by hypoplastic or aplastic clavicles, delayed fontanelle closure, and — critically — multiple supernumerary teeth. Management of CCD in childhood typically requires extensive surgical removal of supernumerary teeth, creating significant periapical surgical trauma that predisposes to fibrous scar formation.

### Case Presentation

- Patient: 18-year-old female with known CCD
- Tooth: #9 (maxillary left central incisor), previously root canal treated
- Presentation: Persistent periapical radiolucency; no symptoms
- Initial clinical impression: Asymptomatic apical periodontitis / endodontic failure
- Relevant history: Extensive periapical surgery in childhood to remove CCD-associated supernumerary teeth in the maxillary anterior region

### Diagnosis

After thorough clinical and radiographic re-evaluation accounting for the surgical history:

- Radiographic features: Well-defined periapical radiolucency, stable size over time, no expansion
- Clinical features: Asymptomatic, adequate root canal filling, no periodontal pathology, no root fracture
- Final diagnosis: Periapical scar — correct identification avoided unnecessary retreatment

### Discussion

**Why CCD predisposes to periapical scar:**

CCD-related supernumerary teeth are managed by repeated surgical extraction, often involving the periapical region of permanent teeth. This surgical trauma damages or removes the periosteum and endosteum, eliminating the osteoblast reservoir needed for bone regeneration. When connective tissue-forming cells (fibroblasts) repopulate the wound before bone-forming cells (osteoblasts), the result is fibrous scar rather than new bone — a pathophysiological sequence described by Nair 2006.

**Risk factors for periapical scar:**

| Risk factor | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Prior periapical surgery | Destroys periosteal/endosteal osteoblast supply |
| Large preoperative lesion | Greater osseous defect → higher scar probability (Grung 1990: 9% overall, higher with large lesions) |
| Bilateral cortical plate loss | Removes both osteoblast sources |
| Systemic conditions affecting bone healing (e.g., CCD) | Impaired bone morphogenetic protein signaling via RUNX2 mutations |

**Prevalence:** 2.5–12% across studies (nonsurgical + surgical combined); Grung 1990 found 9% scar tissue in a 477-tooth surgical series.

**Long-term monitoring approach:**

Once periapical scar is diagnosed clinically and radiographically:
1. No active treatment — the scar is biologically inert (benign fibrous tissue)
2. Scheduled periapical radiographic surveillance to confirm size stability
3. Any increase in size, development of symptoms, or cortical plate involvement → reconsider diagnosis; biopsy if doubt persists
4. The scar should never enlarge if truly PS; enlargement indicates active pathology

**Diagnostic criteria checklist (from this case and Lee 2021):**
- Adequate root canal filling confirmed
- Tooth asymptomatic, no swelling or sinus tract
- No root fracture, healthy periodontium
- Radiolucency well-defined, stable size on serial imaging
- Relevant surgical history explaining scar formation

## Key Contributions

- Documents CCD as a systemic risk factor for periapical scar formation via its surgical management sequelae
- Provides clinically actionable 2.5–12% prevalence figure for periapical fibrous scar
- Describes pathophysiological link: CCD (RUNX2 mutation) → impaired osteoblast differentiation + surgical periosteal damage → fibrous repair
- Demonstrates misdiagnosis risk and correct diagnostic pathway
- Outlines long-term monitoring protocol as definitive management strategy
