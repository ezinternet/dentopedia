## Three-line Summary

In-vitro materials study (flattened gutta-percha specimens, n=9/group for contact angle, n=4/group for XPS/XRD; n=3/group single-rooted-tooth root canal specimens) testing atmospheric air plasma, 100 mM cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC) immersion, and combined plasma+CPC surface treatment of gutta-percha to enable intact, unmodified MTA to function as a root canal sealer without added setting-modifier/handling additives.

Combined plasma+CPC treatment reduced GP water contact angle from 113.27°±5.64 (untreated, hydrophobic) to ~22° (hydrophilic) immediately post-treatment and showed the most stable hydrophilic effect at 8 weeks (versus hydrophobic recovery of plasma-only within 2 weeks); surface-treated GP + intact MTA showed significantly less dye penetration/interfacial voids at the sealer-GP interface than untreated GP (P<0.05), performing comparably to a conventional ZnO-based sealer (P>0.05), while CPC significantly prolonged MTA setting time without significantly altering compressive strength or flowability.

This in-vitro proof-of-concept supports hydrophilized GP paired with additive-free intact MTA as a biocompatibility-motivated alternative to modified MTA-based sealers, pending clinical validation, micro-CT confirmation of 3D interfacial gaps, and resolution of MTA's sub-ISO flowability.

## Summary

This in-vitro materials-science study addresses a longstanding tension in MTA-based root canal sealer design: additional components are commonly mixed into MTA (methylcellulose, calcium chloride, propylene glycol) to shorten setting time and improve handling, but these additives can compromise biocompatibility. Instead of reformulating MTA, the authors modified the **gutta-percha (GP)** core-material surface — via atmospheric air plasma, 100 mM cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC) surfactant immersion, or both combined — to make hydrophobic GP wettable enough to bond well with hydrophilic, unmodified ("intact") MTA used as sealer. Combined plasma+CPC treatment gave the largest and most durable reduction in GP contact angle (113° → ~22°, stable through 8 weeks), while plasma alone reverted toward baseline hydrophobicity within 2 weeks (a known "hydrophobic recovery" phenomenon). XPS confirmed CPC molecular attachment as the mechanism (N1s/Cl2p bands matching pure CPC spectra, r=0.99 correlation between surface free energy and peak intensity). In a single-rooted-tooth model, surface-treated GP obturated with intact MTA showed significantly less coronal dye penetration and fewer sealer-GP interfacial voids than untreated GP, performing on par with a conventional ZnO-based sealer (Canals-N) used as reference. CPC as MTA's mixing liquid did not significantly alter MTA's compressive strength or flowability, though it did significantly prolong setting time. The findings support treating the GP surface, not the MTA formulation, as the lever for improving intact-MTA sealer performance.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes the MTA-sealer affinity problem: instead of adding setting-modifier/handling components to MTA (which risks cytotoxicity), hydrophilize the **gutta-percha surface** and keep MTA intact.
- Shows **combined plasma + CPC (PC)** treatment gives the most durable hydrophilic GP surface (stable to 8 weeks) versus plasma alone (reverts by 2 weeks) — plasma likely "primes" the surface for more stable CPC surfactant adsorption.
- XPS mechanistically links CPC surface attachment (N1s/Cl2p bands) to the wettability improvement, distinguishing it from plasma's more transient -NH2-mediated effect.
- Demonstrates surface-treated GP + intact MTA achieves sealer-GP interfacial sealing (dye penetration) statistically indistinguishable from a conventional ZnO-based sealer, while avoiding sealer-reformulation additives.
- Confirms CPC as MTA's mixing liquid does not compromise MTA's compressive strength, though it significantly prolongs setting time — a practical handling trade-off to weigh.

## Methodology

- **GP specimens**: flattened GC Gutta-percha Bar Plus, 4 groups (untreated control, plasma PT, CPC CT, plasma+CPC PC); n=9 (contact angle), n=4 (XPS), n=4 (XRD).
- **Treatments**: atmospheric air plasma (PIB-10, 6 mA, 1 min); 100 mM CPC solution, 5-min dip.
- **Wettability tracking**: contact angle + derived surface free energy (SFE) measured immediately and at 2/4/8 weeks.
- **Surface chemistry**: XPS (C1s/O1s/Cl2p/N1s) vs pure-CPC reference spectra.
- **MTA physicochemistry with CPC as mixing liquid**: XRD (day 1/7), ISO 6876:2012 flowability, Vicat setting time, compressive strength (day 1/7).
- **Interfacial sealing**: single-rooted extracted teeth, single-cone GPP #80 obturation; Groups A (untreated GP+MTA) to D (surface-treated GP+MTA) vs Group E (ZnO-based Canals-N reference), n=3/group; 7-day methylene blue coronal dye penetration, cross-sectional grinding + light microscopy/ImageJ quantification of dye depth and sealer-GP/sealer-dentin interfacial voids.
- **Stats**: SPSS v26, one-way ANOVA + Tukey/Games-Howell, Mann-Whitney U for non-normal comparisons, P<0.05.

## Results

- **Contact angle**: control 113.27°±5.64 (hydrophobic) → PT 22.21°±4.42, CT 19.54°±5.24, PC 21.99°±6.08 immediately post-treatment (all P<0.05 vs control).
- **Durability**: PT reverted toward control by 2 weeks (hydrophobic recovery); **PC remained significantly lower than control even at 8 weeks** — the most stable of the three treatments.
- **XPS**: CT/PC N1s/Cl2p bands matched pure-CPC spectra, confirming CPC surface coating; SFE correlated with N1s/Cl2p peak intensity at r=0.99 in CT/PC groups.
- **MTA physicochemistry (CPC as mixing liquid)**: only setting time significantly prolonged (P<0.05); compressive strength and flowability not significantly different from water-mixed MTA (P>0.05); neither mixing liquid met the ISO 6876 flowability threshold (>20 mm diameter).
- **Dye penetration**: untreated-GP group (A) had significantly higher dye penetration/void formation than all surface-treated groups and the ZnO reference (P<0.05); surface-treated groups (B–D) were statistically indistinguishable from the ZnO reference (P>0.05); dentin-wall/sealer interface showed no group differences (intact MTA already bonds dentin well via hydration/expansion).
- **Bottom line**: hydrophilizing GP — especially via combined plasma+CPC — lets intact, additive-free MTA achieve sealer-GP interfacial sealing on par with a conventional sealer, without needing to modify MTA's own formulation.
