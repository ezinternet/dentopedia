## Three-line Summary

PRISMA-NMA network meta-analysis of 21 RCTs comparing calcium-silicate and calcium-hydroxide materials for vital pulp treatment of mature permanent teeth, with stratified follow-up at 6, 12, and 24 months.

Calcium hydroxide showed significantly higher failure rates than MTA at all time points (OR 2.26 at 6 mo, 2.53 at 12 mo, 2.46 at 24 mo), while Biodentine and Totalfill were statistically equivalent to MTA.

For VPT in mature permanent teeth, calcium hydroxide is not recommended; MTA, Biodentine, and Totalfill are the evidence-based material choices with equivalent performance.

## Summary

This PRISMA-NMA stratified network meta-analysis compares calcium-silicate and calcium-hydroxide bioactive materials against MTA for vital pulp treatment of mature permanent teeth. Across 21 RCTs and three follow-up windows, calcium hydroxide consistently failed at roughly 2.3–2.5× the odds of MTA, while Biodentine and Totalfill were statistically indistinguishable from MTA. The authors conclude MTA, Biodentine, and Totalfill are the efficient materials and that calcium hydroxide is not recommended for VPT.

## Key Contributions

- Ranks individual contemporary calcium-silicate cements (not pooled) against MTA.
- Stratifies by follow-up (6/12/24 mo) and modality (DPC, partial/full pulpotomy).
- Delivers a clear chairside material hierarchy.

## Methodology

- PRISMA-NMA, RCTs only; PROSPERO CRD42022375573; search to Jan 20, 2024.
- PICOS: mature permanent teeth, calcium-silicate/Ca(OH)2 vs MTA, failure rate, RCTs; follow-up ≥6 mo.
- RoB2; effect measure OR (>1 = higher failure than MTA).

## Results

- Ca(OH)2 vs MTA: OR 2.26 (1.52–3.36) 6 mo; 2.53 (1.76–3.62) 12 mo; 2.46 (1.60–3.79) 24 mo — significant.
- Totalfill vs MTA: OR 1.19 (0.55–2.58) 6 mo; 1.43 (0.71–2.92) 12 mo — NS.
- Biodentine vs MTA: OR 1.09 (0.66–1.78) 6 mo; 1.21 (0.74–1.96) 12 mo; 1.47 (0.81–2.68) 24 mo — NS.
- DPC subgroup mirrored overall; pulpotomy subgroups underpowered.
