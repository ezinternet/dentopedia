## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 33 systematic reviews (from 272 screened records, AMSTAR2-rated) on vital pulp therapy (VPT: indirect pulp treatment, direct pulp capping, pulpotomy) and non-vital pulp therapy (NPT: pulpectomy) in primary teeth.

Methodological quality of included SRs was mostly moderate (17/33) to low (9/33), with 6 high and 3 critically low; MTA emerged as the best-supported pulpotomy medicament (superior to formocresol, ferric sulfate, calcium hydroxide in multiple moderate-to-high quality SRs), while direct pulp capping remained controversial due to complication rates.

Overall quality of evidence for both VPT and NPT was judged moderate — future high-standard SRs and well-designed RCTs with longer follow-up are needed to firm up clinical protocols and material choices.

## Summary

This umbrella review (SR-of-SRs) appraises the entire spectrum of pulp therapy for primary teeth with deep caries: the three vital pulp therapy (VPT) approaches — indirect pulp treatment (IPT), direct pulp capping (DPC), and pulpotomy — plus non-vital pulp therapy (NPT, i.e., pulpectomy). From 272 initially screened records across seven databases (PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane, Scielo, Embase, LILACS, TRIP) plus grey literature up to June 2021, 33 systematic reviews met inclusion criteria and were graded with AMSTAR2.

Methodological quality was mixed: 3 critically low, 9 low, 17 moderate, and 6 high; no SR fully satisfied the AMSTAR2 checklist. The most common systemic weaknesses were failure to report funding sources of primary studies (93.9%), inadequate discussion of heterogeneity (27.3%), unreported conflicts of interest (27.3%), and unclear search strategy (20.0%).

For VPT, IPT (leaving some affected dentin covered by a biocompatible liner) showed no benefit from any particular liner material (CH vs. GIC/adhesive) and outperformed pulpotomy on success rate, with the Hall technique variant showing 78% success vs. 76% for pulpectomy. DPC achieved an 88.8% success rate largely independent of capping material in a high-quality SR, but remained controversial due to complication risk and underperformed pulpotomy overall. For pulpotomy, MTA was consistently the best-supported medicament across multiple moderate-to-high-quality SRs, outperforming formocresol, ferric sulfate, and calcium hydroxide, while showing no significant difference from Biodentine; laser pulpotomy results were inconsistent across 7 SRs of generally lower quality. Formocresol's formaldehyde content was repeatedly flagged as a carcinogenicity/mutagenicity/cytotoxicity concern.

For NPT/pulpectomy, no single obturation medicament (ZOE, Vitapex, Sealapex, Metapex, Endoflas) was conclusively superior per a high-quality Cochrane review; rotary instrumentation generally matched manual instrumentation on clinical/radiographic success while reducing chair time. The authors conclude both VPT and NPT achieve high clinical efficacy overall, but the underlying evidence quality is only moderate, warranting further high-standard SRs and well-designed RCTs with longer follow-up.

## Key Contributions

- First umbrella review combining AMSTAR2-graded appraisal of both VPT (IPT/DPC/pulpotomy) and NPT (pulpectomy) evidence for primary teeth in a single synthesis.
- Cross-material ranking of pulpotomy medicaments (MTA > FC/FS/CH, MTA ≈ Biodentine) drawn from multiple independent SRs rather than a single primary-study comparison.
- Quantified, field-wide transparency gaps in pediatric pulp-therapy SR literature (funding disclosure, heterogeneity discussion, COI reporting, search-strategy clarity).

## Methodology

- PRISMA 2020-compliant umbrella review (systematic review of systematic reviews) with a priori protocol.
- PECO: Population = primary-dentition patients with deep caries; Exposure = clinical pulp management; Comparison = VPT (IPT/DPC/pulpotomy) vs. NPT (pulpectomy); Outcome = diagnosis and treatment-type success.
- Seven-database search (PubMed/Medline, Scopus, Cochrane DSR, Scielo, Embase, LILACS, TRIP) + grey literature, through June 2021; no language/year restriction.
- Dual independent screening and data extraction with third-reviewer arbitration; full-text inter-examiner kappa = 1.00.
- AMSTAR2 (16-item) used for methodological quality rating: High / Moderate / Low / Critically low.

## Results

- Study flow: 272 records → 60 full-texts → 33 included SRs (13 excluded at full-text).
- AMSTAR2 distribution: 3 critically low, 9 low, 17 moderate, 6 high quality; zero SRs fully compliant with AMSTAR2.
- IPT: liner material (CH vs. GIC/adhesive) irrelevant to success; IPT > pulpotomy (low confidence); Hall technique 78% vs. pulpectomy 76% (moderate confidence).
- DPC: 88.8% success regardless of material (high-quality SR, corroborated by moderate-quality and Cochrane SRs); DPC < pulpotomy (moderate confidence).
- Pulpotomy medicaments: MTA best-supported (> FC, FS, CH across multiple moderate/high-quality SRs); MTA ≈ Biodentine (moderate/high quality); FC ≈ FS (one moderate-quality meta-analysis); herbal medicaments comparable but underpowered evidence.
- Laser pulpotomy: inconsistent across 7 SRs — no difference, inferior, or superior to conventional pulpotomy depending on the SR, mostly lower methodological quality.
- Formocresol flagged repeatedly for formaldehyde-related toxicity concern.
- NPT: ZOE ≈ Vitapex ≈ Sealapex (moderate confidence); no medicament/technique conclusively superior per high-quality Cochrane SR; Ca(OH)2/iodoform best near exfoliation; LSTR ≈ pulpectomy (moderate/high-quality meta-analyses); rotary ≈ manual instrumentation with faster treatment time in most SRs.
- Overall conclusion: both VPT and NPT show high clinical efficacy, but evidence quality is only moderate overall.
