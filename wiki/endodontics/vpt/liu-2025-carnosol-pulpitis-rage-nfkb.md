## Three-line Summary

Carnosol (CA), a natural diterpenoid from rosemary and sage, dose-dependently suppresses IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α in LPS-stimulated human dental pulp cells (hDPCs) and reduces histological inflammation in an SD rat pulpitis model. The mechanism operates through the RAGE/NF-κB signalling pathway: CA downregulates RAGE at the transcriptional level, reduces NF-κB p65 phosphorylation and nuclear translocation, and RAGE siRNA knockdown abolishes CA's anti-inflammatory benefit — confirming RAGE as the obligate upstream mediator. This is the first study to demonstrate CA-mediated anti-inflammatory effects in pulpitis, identifying the RAGE/NF-κB axis as a pharmacologically accessible target for vital pulp preservation.

## Background

Pulpitis is characterised by severe persistent tooth pain and, if untreated, progresses to pulpal necrosis, periapical lesions, abscess formation, and potentially systemic spread. Standard treatment (root canal therapy) sacrifices pulp vitality, which is essential for normal tooth metabolism and physiological function. Vital pulp therapy (VPT) — preserving live pulp by controlling inflammation — depends on identifying agents that can modulate the inflammatory cascade without compromising pulp repair capacity.

Carnosol (CA) is a phenolic diterpenoid derived from rosemary (*Rosmarinus officinalis*) and sage (*Salvia officinalis*). It has established antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, immunomodulatory, and anticancer properties, with clinical applications in dermatitis, rheumatoid arthritis, and autoimmune encephalomyelitis. Sage and rosemary have a history in European herbal medicine for oral inflammation; a randomised double-blind RCT confirmed rosemary toothpaste efficacy for gingivitis.

The receptor for advanced glycation end products (RAGE) — a pattern recognition receptor of the Ig superfamily — is upregulated during chronic inflammation and activates NF-κB, driving pro-inflammatory cytokine production. Prior studies (refs 31–32 in this paper) found elevated RAGE expression in inflamed human pulpal tissues correlating with pulpitis progression, making RAGE/NF-κB a candidate therapeutic axis.

## Methods

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Study type | In vitro + in vivo controlled laboratory study |
| Cell source | hDPCs from third molars / orthodontically extracted healthy teeth |
| In vitro stimulation | LPS 1 µg/mL, 6 h |
| CA doses tested | 2.5 / 5 / 10 µM (in vitro); 50 µM 3 µL (in vivo) |
| In vivo model | SD rat drilled pulp exposure (4 groups: drilled, CA, DMSO, intact control) |
| Cytokine readouts | IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α (qRT-PCR, ELISA, WB) |
| Pathway readouts | RAGE (qRT-PCR, WB, IHC), p-p65 (WB, IF, IHC), p65 nuclear translocation (immunofluorescence) |
| Mechanistic validation | si-RAGE knockdown; CHX protein stability assay |
| Transcriptomics | RNA-seq with GO and KEGG enrichment |
| Histology | HE staining; IHC for RAGE, p-p65, IL-1β in rat pulp tissue |

## Key Findings

### Cytotoxicity
CA at 2.5–10 µM was non-cytotoxic to hDPCs (CCK-8, with or without LPS, P > 0.05). Safety threshold established for all three doses.

### Anti-inflammatory Efficacy
CA dose-dependently reduced IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α mRNA and protein levels in LPS-stimulated hDPCs. At 10 µM, cytokine levels returned to near-baseline (control) values by both WB and ELISA. RNA-seq confirmed transcriptome-wide downregulation of inflammatory mediators in LPS+CA vs LPS group.

### RAGE Suppression
CA pre-treatment dose-dependently reduced RAGE mRNA (qRT-PCR) and protein (WB) in LPS-treated hDPCs. At 10 µM, RAGE returned to near-control levels. GO/KEGG enrichment of RNA-seq data identified AGE-RAGE and NF-κB pathways as the primary CA targets.

### NF-κB Pathway Inhibition
- CA reduced NF-κB p65 phosphorylation in LPS-stimulated hDPCs; at 5 µM, phosphorylation was nearly completely inhibited
- Immunofluorescence confirmed CA blocked LPS-induced p65 nuclear translocation

### RAGE as Obligate Mediator
RAGE siRNA knockdown (si-RAGE) alone reduced cytokine levels toward control. Critically, in si-RAGE + LPS + CA conditions, CA's additional anti-inflammatory effect was abolished — confirming RAGE is the required upstream target for CA's mechanism. CA did not further suppress p65 nuclear translocation in RAGE-silenced cells.

### Transcriptional Mechanism
CHX protein stability assay showed no significant difference in RAGE protein degradation between CHX alone and CHX + CA (P > 0.05). CA reduces RAGE expression at the transcriptional level, not through modulation of post-translational protein stability.

### In Vivo Validation
- HE staining: CA-treated drilled pulps showed reduced neutrophil infiltration, more organised odontoblast arrangement, minimal vasodilation vs drilled control
- IHC: RAGE, p-p65, and IL-1β staining intensity markedly reduced in drilled + CA vs drilled + DMSO groups; quantitative IHC confirmed significant reductions

## Mechanistic Summary

```
LPS stimulation
     ↓
RAGE upregulation (transcriptional)
     ↓
NF-κB phosphorylation (p-p65)
     ↓
p65 nuclear translocation
     ↓
IL-1β / IL-6 / TNF-α production
     ↓
Pulpal inflammation

CA (Carnosol) ─── inhibits RAGE transcription ──→ ↓ RAGE
                                                    ↓ p-NF-κB
                                                    ↓ p65 nuclear entry
                                                    ↓ cytokines
```

RAGE silencing (si-RAGE) achieves a similar end-point to CA alone but by protein knockdown rather than transcriptional suppression. CA + si-RAGE: no additive benefit — they converge on the same obligate node (RAGE → NF-κB).

## Limitations

- LPS model represents gram-negative bacterial challenge only; does not replicate polymicrobial caries-associated pulpitis
- SD rat mechanical pulp exposure ≠ caries-induced pulpitis environment
- In vitro concentrations (2.5–10 µM) and in vivo dose (50 µM) require pharmacokinetic validation for clinical delivery
- No assessment of pulp repair potential, dentin bridge formation, or mineralisation
- No head-to-head comparison with MTA, Biodentine, or corticosteroid medicaments
- Single early time-point analysis; no long-term pulp fate data

## Clinical Significance

This is the first study establishing CA as an anti-inflammatory agent for pulpitis via RAGE/NF-κB pathway inhibition. Key translational implications:

1. **New pharmacological target**: RAGE/NF-κB axis is mechanistically validated as a druggable pathway in pulpitis — not only for CA but potentially for other RAGE antagonists
2. **Natural origin**: CA's known safety profile in other inflammatory conditions (dermatitis, arthritis) supports further development with potentially fewer adverse effects than synthetic compounds
3. **VPT context**: Effective inflammation modulation without pulp-killing is a prerequisite for successful VPT; CA's profile (anti-inflammatory without cytotoxicity at therapeutic doses) is consistent with VPT adjunct candidacy
4. **Research gap**: Delivery vehicle development (incorporation into pulp-capping materials, intracanal formulations) and comparison vs established VPT agents are required before clinical translation
