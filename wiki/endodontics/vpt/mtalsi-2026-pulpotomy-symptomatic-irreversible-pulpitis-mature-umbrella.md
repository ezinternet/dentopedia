## Three-line Summary
Umbrella review (PRISMA/PROSPERO CRD42024500772, Cureus 2026) of 11 systematic reviews (2018–2022, from 332 initial hits) on pulpotomy for symptomatic irreversible pulpitis in mature permanent teeth, appraised with AMSTAR 2.

Pulpotomy success reached up to 100% overall (short-term 80–95%, often exceeding root canal treatment); calcium hydroxide had the lowest success (as low as 37%) while calcium silicate-based cements (Biodentine 80–100%, MTA 82–100% clinical, CEM 86–100%) performed best; long-term (>36 mo) success may decline (93.97%→71.3% at 60 mo in one included SR).

Methodological quality of the underlying SRs was mostly "critically low" to "low" (only 1/11 "moderate" per AMSTAR 2), so despite favorable numbers the evidence base calls for cautious interpretation and high-quality long-term RCTs.

## 세줄요약
Umbrella review(PRISMA/PROSPERO CRD42024500772, Cureus 2026): 332편 중 선별된 2018–2022년 체계적 문헌고찰(Systematic Review, SR) 11편을 AMSTAR 2로 메타평가, 성숙 영구치 증상성 비가역 치수염(symptomatic irreversible pulpitis)의 치수절단술(pulpotomy) 효과 분석.

전체 치수절단술 성공률은 최대 100%(단기 80–95%, 근관치료보다 우수한 경우 다수); 수산화칼슘(Calcium Hydroxide, CH)이 최저(최저 37%), 칼슘실리케이트계 시멘트(Biodentine 80–100%, MTA 임상 82–100%, CEM 86–100%)가 최고 성적; 장기(36개월 초과) 성공률은 감소 가능(포함 SR 1편에서 93.97%→60개월 71.3%).

기저 SR들의 방법론적 질은 대부분 "critically low"~"low"(AMSTAR 2 기준 moderate는 11편 중 1편뿐)로, 우수한 수치에도 불구하고 신중한 해석과 장기 고품질 RCT가 필요하다.

## Summary
This umbrella review pools the conclusions of 11 systematic reviews (published 2018–2022) addressing pulpotomy — as coronal pulp removal preserving vital radicular tissue — for symptomatic irreversible pulpitis in mature permanent teeth. Rather than re-pooling raw effect sizes, it performs a qualitative synthesis of each SR's reported success rates, stratified by pulp-capping material, and formally grades the SRs' methodological quality with AMSTAR 2. The headline finding is that pulpotomy achieves clinically favorable short-term outcomes (80–95%, sometimes up to 100%) broadly comparable to or better than conventional root canal treatment (RCT), with material choice (calcium silicate-based cements over calcium hydroxide) and coronal seal quality as the dominant modifiers of success — but the evidence base itself is weak, with 10 of 11 included SRs rated "critically low" or "low" quality.

## Key Contributions
- Synthesizes 11 SRs (2018–2022) specifically restricted to **mature** permanent teeth with **irreversible** pulpitis treated by pulpotomy — a narrower and more clinically decision-relevant population than prior broader VPT reviews.
- Ranks pulp-capping materials by reported success across the SR literature: Biodentine (80–100%, highest, lowest discoloration risk) ≈ MTA (clinical 82–100%, more variable radiographic 53.3–95%) ≈ CEM (86–100%) all clearly superior to calcium hydroxide (as low as 37%); PRF remains experimental (35.5–100%, heterogeneous).
- Confirms short-term pulpotomy success (80–95%) frequently exceeds RCT, with comparable long-term outcomes in most SRs, though one SR (Cushley 2019) shows possible decline over time (93.97% at 36 mo → 71.3% at 60 mo).
- Identifies non-material success determinants: perioperative technique, hemostasis (duration itself not predictive but diagnostically informative), periapical involvement, and — emphasized as the most decisive factor — a tight, well-sealed coronal restoration (prosthetic crown/amalgam preferred over composite resin).
- Applies formal AMSTAR 2 appraisal across all 11 SRs and reports the distribution transparently (6 critically low, 4 low, 1 moderate), explicitly cautioning that the favorable success percentages rest on a weak evidence base.

## Methodology
PRISMA-guided umbrella review, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42024500772). Five-database search (PubMed, Google Scholar, Science Direct, CINAHL, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews) conducted October–December 2022, English-language only, PICO-framed (Population: children/general population, mature permanent teeth with caries-induced irreversible pulpitis; Intervention: pulpotomy; Comparison: pulpectomy/RCT; Outcome: healing — asymptomatic tooth, absence of pulp/periodontal complications). Dual independent title/abstract and full-text screening with third-reviewer arbitration for disagreements. Methodological quality of each included SR assessed with the 16-item AMSTAR 2 tool by two independent raters (inter-rater kappa = 1.000). Screening yielded 332 records → 15 duplicates removed → 304 excluded at title/abstract → 13 full-text assessed → 2 excluded (primary or immature permanent teeth) → **11 SRs included** (spanning 2018–2022, some overlapping in primary-study composition).

## Results
- **Overall success**: up to 100%, strongly dependent on capping material and follow-up duration.
- **By material** (ranges as reported across the 11 SRs): calcium hydroxide (CH) 37–100% but generally the worst performer; MTA clinical 82–100% / radiographic 53.3–95%; CEM clinical 86–100% / radiographic 86–98%; Biodentine 80–100% (best overall, lowest discoloration risk); PRF 35.5–100% (experimental, heterogeneous).
- **Time course**: short-term (≤12 months) success 80–95%, often exceeding conventional RCT and with better postoperative pain control; long-term outcomes generally comparable to RCT, but one included SR (Cushley et al. 2019, n=1,447 patients) reported a decline from 93.97% clinical success at 36 months to 71.3% at 60 months.
- **Pulpotomy vs. RCT**: 4 of the 11 SRs directly compared the two modalities and concluded they are comparable in efficacy, with some favoring pulpotomy on postoperative pain.
- **AMSTAR 2 quality**: 6 SRs critically low, 4 low, 1 moderate (Cushley et al. 2019 was the highest-rated).
- **Success determinants beyond material**: perioperative decontamination/anesthetic/isolation technique; hemostasis achieved promptly (duration itself not statistically predictive of success, though informative diagnostically about inflammation extent); periapical radiographic involvement (associated with lower success in 4 SRs, though pulp vitality can persist despite periapical inflammation); patient age (no demonstrated effect); and coronal restoration quality — identified as the single most emphasized factor, with prosthetic crowns/amalgam outperforming composite resin.
- **Pediatric subgroup**: only 3 of 11 SRs explicitly included pediatric cohorts, so no independent pediatric-specific conclusion could be drawn — the age range across all included studies spans roughly 6–79 years.
