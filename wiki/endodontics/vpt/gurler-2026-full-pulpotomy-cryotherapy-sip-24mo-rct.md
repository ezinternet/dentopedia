## Three-line Summary

RCT (COIR 2026): 62 patients, symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP) in permanent molars; full pulpotomy ± adjunctive cryotherapy; 24-month follow-up per-protocol analysis.

Both groups achieved ~92% success (92.9% vs 92.3%); no statistically significant difference; cryotherapy adds no long-term benefit to full pulpotomy outcomes in SIP.

High 24-month success (~92%) confirms full pulpotomy as a predictable VPT strategy for SIP in permanent molars; cryotherapy adjunct does not improve these outcomes.

## Summary

Gürler et al. (COIR 2026) conducted a 24-month RCT in 62 SIP patients to determine whether adjunctive cryotherapy improves outcomes when added to full pulpotomy in permanent molars. Both groups maintained excellent success (~92%) with no significant between-group difference. This confirms full pulpotomy as a highly predictable approach for SIP at 24 months, while also showing that cryotherapy — despite its pain-reduction benefits in early post-operative settings (see huang-2026) — does not translate to improved long-term VPT success.

## Key Contributions

- 24-month follow-up extends the evidence base for full pulpotomy in SIP beyond most short-term studies
- High success rate (~92%) adds to the growing body of evidence supporting VPT over root canal treatment in SIP
- First RCT testing cryotherapy as adjunct to full pulpotomy specifically for VPT success outcomes
- Clarifies that cryotherapy's early pain-reduction benefit does not extend to long-term pulp healing

## Methodology

Parallel-arm RCT; 62 patients; SIP permanent molars; intervention: full pulpotomy + adjunctive intracanal cryotherapy; control: full pulpotomy alone. Follow-up: 24 months. Primary outcome: clinical and radiographic success (per-protocol). Journal: Clinical Oral Investigations.
