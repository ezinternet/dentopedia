## Three-line Summary

Literature review (Journal of Dental Research, 2014; Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile) examining the cellular and molecular basis of aging-induced defects in gingival wound healing, using the aging model to reveal essential requirements for normal healing across three sequential phases: epithelial migration, granulation tissue formation, and tissue remodeling.

Aging disrupts all phases: exaggerated prolonged inflammation (phases 1–2); decreased integrin α5β1/αvβ6 signaling impairing keratinocyte migration across fibrin, reduced EGF/TGF-β activity, diminished cell proliferation and angiogenesis (phase 3); excess MMP-1/MMP-8 collagen breakdown, reduced synthesis, and deteriorated stem cell proliferative and differentiation potential (phase 4).

By mapping deficits at every healing phase, this review establishes the molecular machinery of normal gingival wound closure; integrin-dependent keratinocyte migration and stem cell renewal are the most clinically relevant therapeutic targets, and these same pathways are disrupted in diabetic and other medically compromised patients.

## Summary
Review from Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile examining the cellular and molecular basis of aging-induced defects in gingival wound healing. Using an aging model, the paper reveals the essential requirements for normal gingival healing: epithelial migration across the fibrin scaffold, granulation tissue formation, and tissue remodeling. Each phase is specifically disrupted by aging, providing a roadmap of the molecular machinery underpinning oral epithelial wound closure. The clinical implication is that elderly patients have impaired gingival healing capacity at every phase, not just slower closure.

## Key Contributions
- Mapped aging-induced defects across all 3 wound healing phases (epithelial migration, granulation tissue, remodeling)
- Identified integrin signaling as a critical mechanism for keratinocyte migration (re-epithelialization under fibrin)
- Quantified that reduced growth factor activity (EGF, TGF-β) impairs granulation tissue formation
- Demonstrated that aging increases MMP activity → excess collagen breakdown, paradoxically reducing tissue quality despite continued synthesis
- Established deterioration of stem cell proliferative and differentiation potential as a late-phase determinant (consistent with Walton 2026 Wnt+ stem cell depletion)

## Methodology
Literature review (no systematic search). Focus: cellular and molecular mechanisms of aging in gingival wound healing. Experimental sources: animal models, in vitro studies, clinical observations from multiple groups. PMC4814024.

## Results
**Phase 1-2 (Haemostasis/Inflammation)**: fibrin clot forms normally but inflammatory resolution is impaired; exaggerated and prolonged inflammatory response in aged tissue; increased TNF-α and IL-1β.  
**Phase 3 (Proliferation/Re-epithelialization)**: 
- ↓ integrin α5β1/αvβ6 signaling → impaired keratinocyte migration across fibrin scaffold
- ↓ EGF, TGF-β, FGF activity → reduced fibroblast activation and granulation tissue
- ↓ angiogenesis (VEGF pathway deficiency)
- ↓ cell proliferation rate  
**Phase 4 (Remodelling)**: 
- ↑ MMP-1, MMP-8 activity → net collagen breakdown
- ↓ collagen synthesis (TGF-β1 pathway deficiency)
- Stem cell pool: ↓ proliferative capacity and ↓ differentiation into fibroblasts/keratinocytes
