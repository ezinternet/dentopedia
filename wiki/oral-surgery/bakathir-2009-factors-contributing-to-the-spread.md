## Three-line Summary

Prospective multicentre pilot study (West of Scotland, n=25 patients with severe spreading odontogenic infection) of social, systemic and microbial factors in the pathogenesis of spreading infection.

Most infections were tooth- or root-related; 80% were tobacco smokers, 72% came from deprived areas, 5 were intravenous drug users and 4 chronic alcohol users; raised CRP at admission indicated severity; inappropriate prior antibiotics without surgical drainage were common; cultures were dominated by strict anaerobes (anaerobic streptococci, Prevotella, Fusobacterium).

Small pilot with no comparison group, so the factors are associations only, and the abstract leaves open whether bacterial factors turn a localized abscess into a spreading infection.

## Summary

Spreading odontogenic infections are the commonest serious orofacial infections. This pilot looked at who gets them and what grows from them, in 25 severe cases.

## Key Contributions

- Social and lifestyle associations (smoking, deprivation) with spreading infection.
- Anaerobe-dominant microbiology with Prevotella and Fusobacterium.
- Notes that prior antibiotics without drainage were common before admission.

## Methodology

- Prospective multicentre pilot, six-month recruitment, 25 patients; admission clinical, hematological and biochemical assessment, histories, microbiology, management recorded.

## Results

- Smokers 80%; deprived areas 72%; IV drug use 5; chronic alcohol abuse 4; systemic disorders 6; malnutrition risk 2.
- CRP raised at admission was a useful severity indicator.
- Strict anaerobes predominated.

## Limitations (reader's appraisal)

- n=25 with no comparison group: percentages describe this series and cannot show that smoking or deprivation causes spread.
