## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Neth J Med, 2017) of the pathophysiology and clinical management of cellulitis, written around Dutch guidance for lower-extremity cellulitis.

Cellulitis, a bacterial skin and soft-tissue infection, occurs when the physical skin barrier, the immune system and/or the circulatory system are impaired; diabetes, obesity and old age affect all three and are major predisposing factors; relapses are frequent because of persistent risk factors and post-inflammatory lymphatic damage.

Abstract only and lower-limb focused: the mechanism is general, not specific to odontogenic facial cellulitis, and no numbers are available.

## Summary

The review explains cellulitis as a consequence of weakened defenses (skin barrier, immunity, circulation), lists conditions often mistaken for it, and places Dutch management guidance in context.

## Key Contributions

- Three-defense model of cellulitis onset; predisposing factors diabetes, obesity, old age.
- Mimickers named: venous insufficiency, eczema, deep vein thrombosis, gout.

## Methodology

- Narrative review; method not given in the abstract.

## Results

- Relapse is frequent because risk factors persist and lymphatic damage remains after inflammation.

## Limitations (reader's appraisal)

- Abstract only, lower-limb context; the odontogenic route (infection spreading from a tooth into soft-tissue spaces) is a different entry point and is not covered.
