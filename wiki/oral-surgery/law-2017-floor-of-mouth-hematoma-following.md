## Three-line Summary

Structured review (1990–2016) of 25 reported floor-of-mouth hematoma cases after dental implant placement plus one case presentation. Lingual cortical perforation caused hemorrhage in 84% of cases; 68% required emergency airway intervention; 72% required surgical management in hospital. Only 1 of 17 cases used preoperative 3D imaging, supporting its role in reducing risk.

## Summary

Law et al. reviewed 25 reported cases of floor-of-mouth (FOM) hematoma during or after dental implantation (1990–2016) and presented an additional serious case. Hemorrhage resulted from lingual cortical perforation in 84% (n=21). Airway obstruction led to emergency intubation or tracheostomy in 68% (n=17), and 72% (n=18) required surgical management in hospital. Management of the offending implant was inconsistently reported: among 17 cases, 5 implants removed, 9 retained in situ, 3 abandoned. Only 1 case used preoperative 3D imaging. The authors advocate preoperative 3D imaging to visualize individual mandibular shape and reduce serious complications, with further assessment before discharge if vessel injury cannot be excluded.

## Key Contributions

- Quantified etiology and morbidity of FOM hematoma after implant placement.
- Strong association with lingual cortical perforation (84%).
- High airway compromise rate (68%) and hospital surgical management (72%).
- Advocacy for preoperative 3D imaging (CBCT) given only 1/17 cases used it.

## Methodology

- Online search 1990–2016; terms: floor of mouth hematoma, sublingual hematoma, dental implant hematoma, implant in mandible, complication of dental implant.
- English-language reports; abstracts screened for relevance.
- Data extracted on demographics, implant location, coagulopathy, imaging, airway management, hematoma treatment, offending implant management.
