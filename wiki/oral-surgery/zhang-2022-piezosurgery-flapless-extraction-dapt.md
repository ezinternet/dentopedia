## Summary
Patients on dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) — standard of care after acute coronary syndrome or drug-eluting stent — face heightened hemorrhagic risk during tooth extraction. This RCT at Shanghai Stomatological Hospital randomized 80 DAPT patients (confirmed low platelet aggregation) to flapless extraction using piezosurgery (PI, n=40) or conventional turbine handpiece (TH, n=40). The primary outcome was immediate intra-alveolar clot formation. Piezosurgery achieved normal clotting within 30 min in 70% vs only 40% with TH (p=0.007). Fewer intraoperative complications occurred with PI (25% vs 47.5%, p=0.036). Logistic regression confirmed instrument type as an independent risk factor for prolonged bleeding (OR 3.10 for TH). Surgical time was longer with piezosurgery but postoperative complications at 7 days were not significantly different. Piezosurgery is recommended for DAPT patients when flapless extraction is planned.

## Key Contributions
- Piezosurgery reduces prolonged bleeding OR by 3.10-fold vs turbine handpiece in DAPT patients
- 30 percentage-point improvement in normal immediate clotting (70% vs 40%)
- Instrument type is an independent predictor of hemostatic outcome — not platelet count or coagulation values alone
- First RCT directly addressing instrument selection for minimally invasive extraction in high-hemorrhagic-risk patients

## Methodology
- Design: RCT; 80 DAPT patients equally divided (PI n=40, TH n=40)
- All patients confirmed: low platelet aggregation, similar coagulation function (no DAPT discontinuation)
- PI technique: Piezosurgery for sulcular incision, PDL severance, flapless extraction
- TH technique: High-speed turbine with bur, conventional coronal reduction and forceps extraction without flap
- Primary outcome: Immediate intra-alveolar clot status — normal (≤30 min) vs prolonged (>30 min)
- Secondary outcomes: Intraoperative complications, surgical duration, postoperative complications (7-day)
- Statistics: Logistic regression; Chi-square; α=0.05

## Results
- Normal immediate clotting: PI 70% vs TH 40% (p=0.007)
- Intraoperative complications: PI 25% vs TH 47.5% (p=0.036)
- OR for prolonged bleeding (TH vs PI): 3.10 (95% CI: 1.20–8.00, p=0.019) by logistic regression
- Surgical duration: PI longer (exact values not reported in abstract)
- 7-day postoperative complications: NS between groups
