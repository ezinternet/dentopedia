## Three-line Summary

Narrative review and expert protocol from the Misch International Implant Institute (survey: 73% of dentists encountered postoperative neurosensory impairment) covering IAN injury classification, testing, and time-based management after dental implant surgery.

Reported incidence 0–44%; management divides into 4 periods — intraoperative (topical dexamethasone 4 mg/mL ± implant removal), 1-week post-op (oral dexamethasone + NSAIDs), 12-week (palliative or referral), with neurosurgery referral criteria at each stage.

Key thresholds: immediate referral for observed nerve transection; referral at 1 week for dysesthesia or complete anesthesia; referral at 12 weeks if no improvement — providing the first organized protocol for general dentists.

## Summary

Misch and Resnik (2010) present the first organized management protocol for inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) neurosensory impairment following dental implant surgery, targeted at general dentists who—unlike neurosurgeons—lack prior standardized guidance. The protocol is grounded in peripheral nerve anatomy, Seddon/Sunderland injury classifications, and pharmacologic/surgical treatment literature, combined with institutional survey data (73% of dentists reporting patient encounters with postoperative paresthesia, dysesthesia, or anesthesia).

**Nerve anatomy context**: The IAN is polyfascicular (7,000–12,000 axons; 18–21 fascicles at the third-molar region, tapering to ~12 at the mental foramen), giving it superior recovery potential over monofascicular nerves through compensatory innervation from uninjured fascicles.

**Terminology**: Adopts IASP-standardized nomenclature — paresthesia (not unpleasant), dysesthesia (unpleasant), anesthesia (total loss) — as the diagnostic and triage language for the protocol.

**Neurosensory testing battery**:
- Nociceptive: pin-prick (27-gauge needle + cosmetic pencil mapping), temperature (ice/ethyl chloride; 43°C heated mirror handle)
- Mechanoreceptive: static touch (cotton-tip applicator), directional movement (soft brush), two-point discrimination (caliper; normal threshold = 6 mm)

**Management protocol (Table 3)**:
1. **Intraoperative — injury suspected**: Topical dexamethasone 4 mg/mL (1 mL × 1–2 min) + 6-day oral dexamethasone (4 mg × 2 tabs AM × 3 days, then 1 tab × 3 days) + cryotherapy 1 week + radiographic confirmation of implant position; implant removal/repositioning if canal encroachment.
2. **Intraoperative — known transection**: Same pharmacology + implant removal + **immediate microneurosurgery referral**.
3. **1-week post-op**: Comprehensive neurosensory examination; high-dose NSAIDs (ibuprofen 600–800 mg TID × 3 weeks, extendable 3 more weeks); **refer if dysesthesia or complete anesthesia**; repeat exam every 2 weeks.
4. **12-week post-op**: Neurosensory examination; palliative management PRN; **refer if no sign of improvement**.

The article emphasizes that neurosensory recovery most often occurs by 2–3 months; beyond 3–4 months without improvement the microneurosurgery window narrows significantly.

## Key Contributions

- First organized, time-based management protocol for IAN neurosensory impairment specifically designed for general dentists performing implant surgery.
- Clear referral criteria at each time point prevent delayed specialist referral — the most common management error.
- Standardized neurosensory testing battery (nociceptive + mechanoreceptive) with reproducible mapping methodology.
- Topical intraoperative dexamethasone protocol (no morbidity; observational improvement data) as an immediately applicable intervention.
- Unification of IASP nomenclature with Seddon/Sunderland classification into a clinically actionable decision framework.

## Methodology

Expert narrative review integrating:
- Peripheral nerve histology and anatomy literature (Sunderland references for axon count and fascicle number)
- Seddon (1943) and Sunderland (1951) nerve injury classifications
- Pharmacologic evidence: corticosteroids, NSAIDs, antidepressants, anticonvulsants, topical agents
- Institutional survey (Misch International Implant Institute) — 73% of dentists reporting neurosensory impairment encounters
- No formal systematic search, PRISMA, or GRADE methodology

## Results

- Incidence of nerve injury after implant surgery: 0–44% (highly variable across literature)
- IAN axon count: 7,000–12,000; fascicles: 18–21 (third molar region) → ~12 (mental foramen)
- Normal two-point discrimination threshold: 6 mm (mandibular nerve territory)
- Topical dexamethasone (4 mg/mL): no reported morbidity; improved recovery observed
- Oral dexamethasone + ibuprofen 600–800 mg TID: recommended adjunct regimen
- Most neurosensory improvement occurs within 2–3 months; beyond 3–4 months → microneurosurgery indication
