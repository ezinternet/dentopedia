## Three-line Summary
Cochrane systematic review (2020 update) comparing surgical removal vs retention of asymptomatic disease-free impacted wisdom teeth — only 2 studies included (1 RCT with 77 adolescents, 1 prospective cohort with 416 men followed 3–25 years).
Very low to low certainty evidence (GRADE ⊕⊕⊝⊝ to ⊕⊝⊝⊝): retention of bony-impacted teeth may increase long-term periodontitis risk at adjacent second molar (RR 0.32, 95% CI 0.19–0.54 for bone loss; RR 0.11 for soft-tissue impaction), but no difference in caries risk. RCT found no clinically significant effect on dental arch crowding (Little's index 0.30 mm lower in extraction group, not clinically meaningful).
Insufficient evidence to determine whether asymptomatic impacted wisdom teeth should be removed or retained; shared decision-making guided by patient values and clinical expertise recommended.

## Summary
This Cochrane Intervention Review (4th update, originally published 2005) evaluates whether asymptomatic, disease-free impacted wisdom teeth (third molars) should be surgically removed or retained. The review identified only two eligible studies: one parallel-group RCT conducted in a UK dental hospital (77 adolescents who had completed orthodontic treatment with "crowded" wisdom teeth, 5-year follow-up) and one prospective cohort study from a US private dental clinic (416 healthy men aged 24–84, followed 3 to over 25 years). The primary outcome (health-related quality of life) was not assessed in either study. Secondary outcomes showed very low-certainty evidence that retention of bony-impacted wisdom teeth may be associated with increased risk of periodontitis affecting the adjacent second molar (RR 0.32 for distal bone loss, 95% CI 0.19–0.54; RR 0.11 for soft-tissue impaction), but insufficient evidence for caries risk (RR 0.69, 95% CI 0.27–1.82). The RCT found no clinically significant effect of extraction on dental arch dimensional changes (Little's index of irregularity mean difference 0.30 mm, 95% CI −1.30 to 0.70; intercanine width and arch length changes also not clinically meaningful). No study measured adverse events of retention (pericoronitis, root resorption, cysts, tumours) or removal (alveolar osteitis, nerve injury, ONJ, bleeding), nor costs. Both studies were at high/serious risk of bias. The authors conclude there is **insufficient evidence to support or refute prophylactic removal**, and recommend shared decision-making incorporating patient preferences, with regular monitoring if retention is chosen.

## Methodology
- **Design**: Cochrane systematic review (Intervention Review) — standard Cochrane methodology
- **Databases**: Cochrane Oral Health Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE Ovid (1946–2019), Embase Ovid (1980–2019), ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP
- **Selection**: RCTs comparing removal vs retention; quasi-RCTs & prospective cohorts with ≥5-year follow-up also eligible
- **n**: 2 studies, 493 participants (RCT: 77 adolescents post-orthodontics; Cohort: 416 men, 24–84 yrs)
- **Outcomes**: Primary — health-related QoL; Secondary — periodontitis (clinical/radiographic), caries (2nd molar), dimensional changes (Little's index, intercanine width, arch length), adverse events (retention & removal), costs
- **Analysis**: GRADE assessment; risk of bias (RCT: high — allocation concealment, incomplete outcome data; Cohort: serious — confounding, missing data, male only)
