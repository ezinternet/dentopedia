## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 7 RCTs testing clindamycin for infection prevention after oral surgery; only 4 trials of oral clindamycin in third molar (M3) extraction were poolable: RR 0.66 (95% CI 0.38–1.16), p=0.15, NNT=29 — not statistically significant, with zero heterogeneity (I²=0).

Clindamycin — the standard antibiotic alternative for penicillin-allergic patients in oral surgery — cannot be shown to prevent infection after M3 extraction; insufficient RCTs exist for other oral surgery procedures.

Routine clindamycin prescription for M3 surgery lacks supporting evidence despite its widespread use in penicillin-allergic patients.

## Summary

Arteagoitia et al. (2022) conducted a SR+MA of 7 RCTs to evaluate whether clindamycin (any route, regimen, or dosage) prevents infectious complications after oral surgery. Only 4 trials of oral clindamycin in third molar extraction provided poolable data. The pooled analysis of 486 extractions (245 clindamycin, 241 control) showed a non-statistically significant RR of 0.66 (95% CI 0.38–1.16, p=0.15), with no heterogeneity (I²=0). NNT=29 with a wide CI (12 to −57) crossing infinity indicates no reliable benefit. Notably, topical clindamycin (Hamiti-Krasniqi) showed fewer dry sockets (2/60 vs 19/60) and IV clindamycin (Halpern, n=15) showed 0 vs 5 infections — but these were too small or heterogeneous to pool. The only endodontic surgery trial (Lindeboom) also showed minimal differences. The authors conclude that routine oral clindamycin prescription for M3 surgery is not evidence-based, even for penicillin-allergic patients.

## Key Contributions
- Demonstrates oral clindamycin is ineffective for M3 extraction infection prevention (RR 0.66, NS)
- NNT=29 with CI crossing infinity — prescribing benefit unreliable
- Zero heterogeneity (I²=0) — consistent null result across 4 trials
- Highlights critical prescribing gap: penicillin-allergic patients have no evidence-based alternative here
- Topical clindamycin may be effective (dry socket endpoint) — different mechanism from oral systemic

## Methodology
- PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD4202122624; search to January 2021
- Databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane CENTRAL, Web of Science, Embase Ovid, Scopus
- 540 records → 7 included; 4 pooled for MA (oral clindamycin, M3 extraction)
- Effect measure: RR, Mantel–Haenszel; NNT calculated
- I²=0, Q-statistic supports homogeneity
