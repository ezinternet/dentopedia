## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 16 RCTs (14 pooled, 2 narrative) evaluating any antibiotic for third molar (M3) extraction; antibiotics reduced postoperative infection risk by 69% (p<0.001), but had no effect on dry socket, wound dehiscence, haematoma, lymphadenopathy, or intraoral inflammation; adverse events were comparable between antibiotic and placebo groups (RR 0.96, NS).

Routine antibiotic prophylaxis is not supported for uncomplicated M3 extraction in healthy patients; evidence for comorbid or immunosuppressed patients remains controversial; surgical difficulty (ostectomy, >10 min), age, and female sex increased infection risk.

This SR definitively separates antibiotic-preventable infection from dry socket — antibiotics prevent the former but not the latter.

## Summary

Torof et al. (2023) conducted a SR+MA of 16 RCTs examining antibiotic use around M3 extraction procedures. Among 12 RCTs providing infection data (n=2,069), antibiotics reduced infection risk by 69% compared to placebo (34/1,186 vs 78/883, p<0.001). However, in 7 trials examining dry socket incidence, antibiotics showed no significant benefit. Adverse event rates were comparable between antibiotic and control groups across 8 studies (RR 0.96, 95% CI 0.58–1.59). Specific analyses (Lacasa et al.) showed that surgical difficulty (ostectomy, duration >10 min) significantly increased infection risk, suggesting that antibiotic benefit may be concentrated in difficult cases. Age and female sex were additional risk factors for infection. The reviewers conclude that routine prophylaxis is not supported for healthy patients undergoing uncomplicated M3 extraction, while evidence for high-risk patients (comorbidities, immunosuppression) remains insufficient.

## Key Contributions
- 69% infection reduction with antibiotics (p<0.001) — statistically and clinically significant
- Dry socket: antibiotics provide NO benefit — definitively separated from infection prevention
- Adverse events: RR 0.96 (NS) — safety profile acceptable when antibiotics used
- Risk stratification: ostectomy, >10 min surgery, age ≥40, female sex → higher infection risk
- Routine prophylaxis not justified for healthy patients in uncomplicated procedures
- Adverse event profile: antibiotics safe but not protective against dry socket

## Methodology
- PRISMA-P; PROSPERO CRD42021269522; University of Wolverhampton ethics approval
- Databases: PubMed, Science Direct, Cochrane; search to November 30, 2021
- Population: healthy adults, any M3 extraction
- Intervention: any oral/systemic antibiotic vs placebo/control
- 494 records → 16 RCTs (14 MA, 2 narrative)
- Quality: EPHPP tool; moderate–strong global rating
