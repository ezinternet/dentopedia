## Three-line Summary

PRISMA systematic review of 7 studies (23–318 participants each, 62–1,183 teeth extracted) on bleeding during oral surgery in patients with liver cirrhosis.

Despite platelet counts of 16,000–216,000/mm³ and INR below 4, haemorrhagic events occurred in only 0–8.9% of cases and were almost all controlled with local haemostatic measures.

Moderately deranged coagulation indices in cirrhosis are not by themselves a reason to transfuse before extraction — but the review carries no aetiology stratification, so it cannot separate alcohol-related from other cirrhosis.

## Summary

Seven studies met PRISMA criteria, together covering 23 to 318 participants and 62 to 1,183 extracted teeth per study, with platelet counts spanning 16,000–216,000/mm³ and INR kept below 4. Across that literature the prevalence of haemorrhagic events was only 0–8.9%, and almost every event was managed with local haemostatic measures. The authors' conclusion is deliberately blunt: cirrhotic patients have a low bleeding risk during dental treatment despite thrombocytopenia and raised INR. For practice, that reframes the pre-operative question from "what is the INR?" to "is there a local measure plan and a way to reach the patient if bleeding continues" — the laboratory threshold does not decide the case by itself.

## Key Contributions

- **Names the paradox precisely.** The organ that makes nearly all coagulation factors can be severely diseased while extraction bleeding stays rare and locally controllable.
- **Provides the working laboratory envelope.** Platelets 16,000–216,000/mm³ with INR < 4 is the range in which the reviewed evidence supports proceeding without systemic replacement.
- **Demotes transfusion from default to exception** for this population in the oral surgery setting.
- **Marks the limit of the evidence.** Seven heterogeneous studies with no pooling — a low-certainty review whose value is direction, not effect size.

## Methodology

- **Design**: PRISMA-compliant systematic review; no meta-analysis.
- **Databases**: PubMed, LILACS, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane.
- **Population**: patients with liver cirrhosis undergoing dental/oral surgical procedures.
- **Included**: 7 studies.
- **Outcome**: prevalence of haemorrhagic events and the measures used to control them.

| Participants | 23–318 per study |
| Teeth extracted | 62–1,183 per study |
| Platelet count | 16,000–216,000/mm³ |
| INR | < 4 |
| Haemorrhagic events | 0%–8.9% |

- Almost all events were controlled with **local haemostatic measures**.
- Authors' conclusion: low bleeding risk during dental treatment despite decreased platelets and increased INR.

## Clinical Reading

- **Plan local haemostasis, not a transfusion.** Across seven studies the events that occurred were handled locally; prophylaxis with blood products was not the pattern that made surgery safe.
- **Do not treat the INR in isolation.** INR < 4 with platelets ≥ 16,000/mm³ is the envelope in which this evidence sits — a patient outside it is outside the reviewed data, not automatically a transfusion case either.
- **Cirrhosis ≠ alcohol, and the review cannot bridge that gap.** Aetiology is not stratified, so citing this page as "alcohol causes bleeding" is a misread: it is about the cirrhotic state, however caused.
- **The extraction itself is the low-risk procedure.** More invasive oral surgery is not represented proportionally, so extrapolate upward cautiously.
