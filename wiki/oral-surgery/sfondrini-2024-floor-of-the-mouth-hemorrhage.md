## Three-line Summary

Two cases of life-threatening floor-of-mouth hemorrhage after interforaminal mandibular implant surgery (one with GBR); both patients developed airway compromise requiring ICU hospitalization and nasotracheal intubation, with spontaneous hemostasis and complete hematoma resorption by ~2 weeks. The classic mechanism is lingual cortical plate perforation during drilling injuring the sublingual artery; a novel reported mechanism is ascending mental artery injury during periosteal release incision in GBR, with hematoma spreading to the floor of the mouth through the lingual flap incision. Management prioritizes bimanual compression, immediate hospital transfer, and airway security (intubation/tracheostomy), with surgical evacuation/ligation or embolization reserved for large nonrestrictive hematomas; prevention centers on preoperative CT, buccolingual drilling angulation, wide flap reflection, meticulous hemostasis, short implants, and a 2 h postoperative observation period.

## 세줄요약

하악 전치간부(interforaminal) 임플란트 수술(1건은 GBR 동반) 후 발생한 구강저 출혈 2예; 두 환자 모두 혈종이 구강저 연조직으로 빠르게 퍼져 혀를 후방·상방으로 밀어 기도 폐쇄가 발생해 ICU 입원 및 비강기관삽관이 필요했고, 출혈은 자연 지혈되어 약 2주 내 혈종이 완전 흡수되었다. 고전적 기전은 drilling 중 설측 피질골 천공으로 설하동맥(sublingual artery)이 손상되는 것이며, 이 보고에서는 GBR의 골막 방출 절개(periosteal release incision) 중 상승성 정신동맥(ascending mental artery) 손상으로 구강저로 혈종이 전파되는 새로운 기전도 제시한다. 관리는 이중수지압박(bimanual compression)·즉시 병원 이송·기도 확보(삽관/기관절개)를 우선하고, 큰 비제한성 혈종에만 수술적 배액·결紮/색전술을 사용하며, 예방으로는 수술 전 CT, 협설측 drilling 각도 조정, 넓은 피판 반사, 정밀 지혈, 단축 임플란트, 2시간 수술 후 관찰을 강조한다.

## Summary

This case report describes two life-threatening floor-of-mouth hemorrhages after interforaminal mandibular implant placement (one with GBR). In both, hematoma spread in loose floor-of-mouth tissue, displacing the tongue posteriorly/cranially and compromising the airway, requiring ICU admission and nasotracheal intubation; bleeding stopped spontaneously and hematomas resorbed by ~2 weeks. Mechanisms: (1) lingual cortical plate perforation → sublingual artery injury (classic); (2) first reported case of ascending mental artery injury during GBR periosteal release incision, with hematoma crossing to the floor of the mouth via the lingual flap incision. Anatomically, the sublingual artery (~2 mm) runs between genioglossus/mylohyoid and reaches the lingual cortex via MLC/LLC, anastomosing with the submental artery. Management prioritizes bimanual compression, urgent hospital transfer, and airway security; surgical evacuation/ligation or embolization for large nonrestrictive hematomas. Prevention: preoperative CT, buccolingual drilling angulation, wide flap reflection, meticulous hemostasis (especially after periosteal incision), short implants (≤10 mm), ≥2 h observation, patient education.

## Key Contributions

- Two cases of floor-of-mouth hemorrhage with airway compromise after interforaminal implant surgery (one with GBR).
- Classic mechanism confirmed: lingual cortical perforation → sublingual artery injury.
- Novel mechanism first reported: ascending mental artery injury during GBR periosteal release incision → hematoma spread to floor of mouth.
- Management hierarchy and prevention strategies provided.

## Methodology

Descriptive case report (n=2) with imaging and literature review; no controlled design; incidence not quantifiable.
