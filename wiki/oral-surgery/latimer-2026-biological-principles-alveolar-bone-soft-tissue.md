## Summary
This 2026 review from Harvard Dental School (with Cha Jae-Kook of Yonsei as co-author) synthesizes biological principles underlying oral tissue regeneration across three tissues: alveolar bone, gingiva, and periodontium. The 4 wound healing stages (hemostasis — fibrin clot formation; inflammation — immune regulation; proliferation — matrix and cellular activity; remodeling — collagen architecture) apply to all three tissues but are modulated by the uniquely dynamic oral environment. Defect classification systems aid treatment selection. Biologic adjuncts (growth factors, membranes, cell-based therapies) complement the endogenous healing capacity. Emerging technologies — customized bioprinted scaffolds, immunoengineering to shift macrophage phenotype (M1→M2), and organ-on-chip disease modeling — represent the next frontier for personalized periodontal therapy.

## Key Contributions
- 2026 state-of-the-art synthesis confirming 4-phase oral wound healing biology
- Explicitly frames hemostasis (fibrin clot) as the cell-migration substrate for subsequent regeneration
- Identifies oral-specific challenges: masticatory mechanical forces, polymicrobial biofilm, complex immune landscape
- Introduces immunoengineering (M1→M2 macrophage shift) as a modifiable lever for improved healing outcomes
- Organ-on-chip platforms for periodontal disease modeling and therapeutic development

## Methodology
Narrative review. Covers alveolar bone regeneration, gingival soft tissue augmentation, and periodontal regeneration. Literature synthesis encompassing wound healing biology + regenerative biomaterials + emerging technologies. No systematic search protocol reported.

## Results
**Hemostasis**: platelet aggregation → fibrin clot formation. The fibrin matrix is the initial provisional scaffold; its integrity is the foundation of all downstream healing (eschar = fibrin clot).  
**Inflammation**: oral polymicrobial environment drives prolonged and complex immune activation; macrophage M1/M2 balance is a key determinant of regenerative vs. fibrotic outcome.  
**Proliferation**: fibroblast-driven matrix synthesis; keratinocyte re-epithelialization; angiogenesis (VEGF-dependent); growth factors (PDGF, TGF-β, BMP) as regulators.  
**Remodeling**: mechanically sensitive in the oral cavity; collagen crosslinking; tissue phenotype determined by connective tissue type.  
Defect classification guides material selection and clinical decision-making.  
Bioprinted scaffolds: customized pore architecture + growth factor loading → predictable bone/soft tissue fill.
