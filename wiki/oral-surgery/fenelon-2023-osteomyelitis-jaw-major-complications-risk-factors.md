## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort of 54 histologically confirmed jaw osteomyelitis cases over 20 years at a single university hospital, testing which factors predict major complications or a refractory course.

Alcohol habit (41.2% vs 10.8%, p = 0.025), smoking (p = 0.018), malnutrition (p = 0.027), and implant-induced osteomyelitis (p = 0.003) were associated with major complications (17/54, 31.5%), while clinical bone exposure predicted treatment failure (p = 0.030).

Alcohol is one of four pre-treatment flags for a complicated course of jaw osteomyelitis — with the important caveat that the study is small, single-centre, and never separates alcohol from the smoking and malnutrition it travels with.

## Summary

Fifty-four patients with histologically confirmed osteomyelitis of the jaw, treated between 2002 and 2022, were analysed for factors associated with major complications (pathological fracture, deep neck abscess, cutaneous fistula) and with a refractory treatment course. Seventeen patients (31.5%) suffered a major complication. On bivariate testing, an alcohol habit was present in 41.2% of complicated cases versus 10.8% of uncomplicated ones (p = 0.025), alongside smoking (p = 0.018), malnutrition (p = 0.027), and implant-induced osteomyelitis (p = 0.003); 41.2% of complicated cases were implant-related. For treatment outcome, clinical bone exposure predicted failure (p = 0.030) while a dental cause and periosteal reaction predicted success. The value of the page is that it converts "heavy drinkers do worse" from a general impression into a specific, countable outcome in a defined jaw-infection population — with the caveat that the alcohol effect is unadjusted and small.

## Key Contributions

- **Moves alcohol from aetiology to prognosis.** Earlier literature listed alcohol as a predisposing factor for *developing* jaw osteomyelitis; this study associates it with *complications once the disease exists*.
- **Gives absolute contrasts, not just p-values**: 41.2% vs 10.8% for major complications.
- **Defines implant-induced osteomyelitis as an aggressive subtype** — 41.2% of complicated cases followed recent implant placement (p = 0.003), consistent with the wider literature on implant-associated OMJ.
- **Separates two different prognostic questions** — who gets a complication, and who fails treatment — and finds different predictors for each.

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective cohort, STROBE-reported, single university hospital (Geneva), 2002–2022.
- **Inclusion**: histologically confirmed OMJ with complete records, no age limit.
- **Exclusions**: prior head-and-neck irradiation, antiresorptive/anti-angiogenic medication, oral malignancy or jaw metastasis — i.e. MRONJ and malignancy are excluded by design.
- **Outcomes**: major complications (pathological fracture, deep neck abscess, cutaneous fistula); treatment success versus partial/no resolution.
- **Statistics**: Fisher's exact test for categorical variables, Kruskal–Wallis for non-normal continuous variables, α = 0.05.

## Results

| Variable | No complication (n = 37) | Complication (n = 17) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alcohol habit | 4 (10.8%) | 7 (41.2%) | 0.025 |
| Smoking habit | 14 (37.8%) | 13 (76.5%) | 0.018 |
| Malnutrition | 0 (0.0%) | 3 (17.6%) | 0.027 |
| Recent implant placement | 2 (5.4%) | 7 (41.2%) | 0.003 |

- Cohort: mean age 47.5 ± 18.9; 29 female (53.7%); acute 13.0%, secondary chronic 64.8%, primary chronic 22.2%.
- Alcohol habit overall 20.4%; smoking 50.0%.
- Treatment success 88.9% (48/54); decortication first-line in 60.0%.
- **Refractory course**: bone exposure → unfavourable (p = 0.030); dental history (p = 0.015) and periosteal reaction (p = 0.036) → success.

## Clinical Reading

- **Ask about drinking before jaw surgery, and document it.** In this series a documented alcohol habit more than tripled the proportion of patients who went on to a fracture, deep neck abscess, or fistula.
- **Do not separate it from smoking and nutrition.** All three were significant, all three cluster in the same patients, and the study reports no multivariable model — treat them as one "host reserve" cluster rather than three independent levers.
- **Implant-related osteomyelitis deserves a lower threshold for escalation.** 41.2% of complicated cases followed recent implant placement; a non-healing implant site in a drinker is not a wait-and-see situation.
- **Bone exposure is the visible failure flag.** If the bone is exposed, the course tends to be refractory regardless of the habit history.
- **This is not a bleeding page.** It concerns infective complications; haemostasis in cirrhosis is covered by [[oral-surgery/rech-2021-bleeding-oral-surgery-liver-cirrhosis-sr]].
