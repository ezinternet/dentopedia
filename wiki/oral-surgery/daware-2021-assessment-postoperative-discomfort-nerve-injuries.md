## Three-line Summary

Prospective cohort study of 163 patients undergoing surgical extraction of impacted mandibular third molars (M3) to assess postoperative discomfort, complications, and neurosensory deficits.

Transient lingual nerve injury (LNI) occurred in 4 patients (2.45% of cases; reported as 2%), resolving completely within 12 weeks, while no cases of inferior alveolar nerve paresthesia (IANP), dry socket, or postoperative infection were reported.

Although surgical extraction is a safe procedure with low long-term morbidity, adequate preoperative risk assessment and patient education regarding potential neurosensory deficits are critical.

## Summary

This prospective clinical study by Daware et al. evaluates the incidence of postoperative complications, discomfort (pain, swelling, trismus), and neurosensory disturbances following the surgical removal of impacted mandibular third molars (M3). Conducting research on a cohort of 163 patients, the study provides valuable empirical benchmarks for temporary versus permanent nerve injuries, highlighting that while temporary lingual nerve injury (LNI) occurs in a small percentage of cases, permanent deficits and inferior alveolar nerve paresthesia (IANP) are rare under standard maxillofacial surgical protocols.

## Key Contributions

* **Neurological Injury Quantitation**: Identifies a 2.45% (reported as 2%) incidence of temporary lingual nerve injury (4 out of 163 patients) associated with surgical third molar extractions. Crucially, all affected patients fully recovered tactile and nociceptive function within 12 weeks, confirming a neuropraxia or low-grade axonotmesis injury mechanism.
* **Absence of IANI**: Documents 0% incidence of inferior alveolar nerve injury (IANI) in this sample, supporting the safety of standard bone removal and tooth sectioning techniques.
* **Patient-Reported Pain Profiles**: Demonstrates that by postoperative day 7, 98% of patients experience either no pain (32%) or only mild pain (66%) on a Visual Analogue Scale (VAS).
* **Mouth Opening & Swelling Benchmarks**: Establishes that surgical removal results in a statistically significant reduction in mouth opening (mean interincisal reduction of 8.067 mm) and a significant increase in facial swelling at day 7 (p < 0.001).

## Methodology

* **Cohort details**: 163 patients (108 males, 55 females) undergoing impacted mandibular M3 surgical extraction. 48% of patients were aged 25–30 years; 33% were under 25 years.
* **Surgical approach**: Elevation of a mucoperineal flap, buccal bone guttering, and tooth sectioning (odontectomy) under local anesthesia.
* **Postoperative Assessment (Day 7)**:
  * Pain intensity measured via a 10 cm Visual Analogue Scale (VAS).
  * Swelling measured via facial landmark tape measurements (tragus-corner of mouth, tragus-pogonion, lateral eye-angle of mandible).
  * Trismus measured via maximum interincisal distance (mm).
  * Neurosensory function evaluated through Two-Point Discrimination (TPD), Pinprick (PP) test, and Light Touch (LT) mapping.
* **Follow-up**: Patients diagnosed with neurosensory deficits were followed for up to 6 months postoperatively to monitor recovery.

## Results

* **Postoperative Pain (VAS)**:
  * *No pain (VAS 0)*: 32%
  * *Mild pain (VAS 1–3)*: 66%
  * *Moderate pain (VAS 4–6)*: 1%
  * *Severe pain (VAS 7–9)*: 1%
* **Trismus & Swelling**: A statistically significant postoperative increase in facial swelling and a mean reduction in maximum interincisal opening of 8.067 mm (p < 0.001) were documented.
* **Complication Rates**:
  * *Lingual Nerve Injury (LNI)*: 2.45% (4 patients: 2 males, 2 females). All recovered fully within 12 weeks.
  * *Inferior Alveolar Nerve Injury (IANI)*: 0%
  * *Dry Socket (Alveolar Osteitis)*: 0%
  * *Postoperative Infection*: 0%
  * *Postoperative Bleeding*: 0%
  * *Intraoperative Syncope*: 2 cases (resolved immediately).
