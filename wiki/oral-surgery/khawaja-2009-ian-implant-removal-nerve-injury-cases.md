## Three-line Summary

Retrospective 4-case series of implant-related IAN injury at King's College Hospital (2007–2009), with early removal at 18 h to 4 days post-injury.

Cases removed within 36 h (18 h and 36 h) achieved near-complete sensory recovery; cases removed at 2 and 4 days suffered permanent complete numbness with functional deficits.

Early implant removal within 36 hours of suspected IAN compromise is the critical intervention window; adjunctive corticosteroids and high-dose NSAIDs may enhance recovery.

## Summary

Khawaja and Renton (2009) present four cases of implant-related inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) injury referred to King's College Hospital oral surgery department. All patients underwent early implant removal (18 hours to 4 days). The outcomes correlate strongly with removal timing:

- **Case 1** (55F, removal at 18 h) + adjunctive NSAID and corticosteroid: near-complete sensory recovery
- **Case 2** (removal at 36 h): near-complete sensory recovery
- **Case 3** (removal at 2 days): persistent complete IAN numbness, functional problems
- **Case 4** (removal at 4 days): persistent complete IAN numbness, functional problems

Three of four patients also experienced neuropathic pain (allodynia, dysesthesia) in addition to numbness. The authors conclude that the critical intervention window for implant removal to prevent permanent neuropathy is **less than 36 hours** from injury.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes the **36-hour threshold** for early implant removal as the key determinant of IAN recovery
- Proposes a **practical clinical management algorithm** for suspected/actual implant-related nerve injuries for general practitioners
- Highlights that neuropathic pain (not just numbness) is the predominant complaint — a common misconception corrected
- Emphasizes prevention: pre-surgical CBCT, 2 mm safety zone from IAN canal, drill length awareness (drills up to 1.5 mm longer than placed implant)
- Notes medico-legal severity: IAN injury incidence 0–40% across literature; permanent effects in ~25% of iatrogenic paraesthesias

## Methodology

Retrospective review of health records for 4 patients referred to specialist oral surgery over approximately 2 years. Clinical sensory assessment (IAN dermatome mapping). All implants removed by reverse-torque (re-attaching driver, running handpiece in reverse).

## Results

| Case | Removal timing | Adjunctive Rx | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 18 h | NSAID + steroid | Near-complete recovery |
| 2 | 36 h | Not stated | Near-complete recovery |
| 3 | 2 days | Not stated | Permanent complete numbness |
| 4 | 4 days | Not stated | Permanent complete numbness |

**Key threshold**: removal ≤36 h → favorable; removal ≥2 days → permanent deficit.

After 3 months from any nerve injury, permanent central and peripheral neurological changes occur, closing the window for meaningful surgical or conservative intervention.
