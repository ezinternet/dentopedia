## Summary

Yang et al. 2022 is a narrative review synthesizing the pathogenesis of delayed tooth extraction socket (TES) healing in diabetic patients and available clinical interventions. Healing delay is most evident at day 7 post-extraction vs. non-diabetic patients, driven by five interacting mechanisms: (1) reduced expression of TGFB1-3, VEGF, BMP-4, and IGF-1; (2) elevated MMP-9 and AGE-mediated collagen crosslinking; (3) dysregulated M1/M2 macrophage polarization toward pro-inflammatory M1; (4) impaired neutrophil migration and phagocytosis; (5) deficient neuropeptide (Substance P) signaling. Osteogenic proliferation and bone remodeling occurs 4–8 weeks post-extraction and is particularly vulnerable to hyperglycemia. Well-controlled DM patients (Goss et al.) show no statistically significant difference in healing vs. non-diabetic — implying glycemic control is the primary determinant of outcome. Clinically, topical PRGF (Anitua), L-PRF, A-PRF, and HA gel are the best-supported approaches for improving early TES closure. Most interventions beyond platelet concentrates remain in animal/in vitro stages.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes that day 7 is the clearest timepoint showing healing difference between DM and non-DM patients.
- Documents that BMP-4 is the most under-expressed bone-regulating gene in T2DM blood (11 differentially expressed genes), linking to impaired MSC osteogenic differentiation.
- Summarizes that well-controlled DM patients heal comparably to non-DM, making glycemic control the primary risk stratification tool for extraction management.
- Identifies PRGF, L-PRF, A-PRF, and HA as having clinical RCT evidence vs. other interventions (still animal data).
- First review to synthesize the miRNA angle (miR-191, miR-497, miR-15b, miR-20b, miR-21) in diabetic TES healing — mechanistic expansion relevant to future therapeutic targets.

## Methodology

Narrative review with systematic literature search (PubMed, unspecified databases). Both human clinical trials and animal/in vitro studies included. A clinical trials summary table provided with intervention, year, study design, and results. No PRISMA, no systematic quality appraisal (AMSTAR-2 not applied). Non-systematic methodology limits evidence hierarchy.

## Results

**Healing timeline:**
- Day 7: Most evident difference between poorly controlled DM and non-DM
- Weeks 4–8: Osteogenic proliferation and bone remodeling particularly impaired by hyperglycemia
- Well-controlled DM: No statistically significant difference from non-DM (Goss et al.)

**Key mechanisms:**
- TGFB1-3/VEGF/BMP-4/IGF-1 deficiency → impaired osteogenesis and angiogenesis
- MMP-9 elevation + AGE collagen crosslinking → structural collagen degradation
- M1 macrophage dominance → TNF-α/IL-6 excess, inhibited MSC and endothelial function
- Neuropeptide (Substance P) deficiency → impaired wound re-epithelialization

**Clinical interventions with human RCT evidence:**
- PRGF: 34 T1DM patients — improved socket closure and healing index days 3–14
- L-PRF: Enhanced bone density (p=0.007); L-PRF + HA reduced alveolar osteitis
- HA (0.8%): 30 T2DM patients — improved wound healing especially first days post-extraction
- LLLT 808 nm: Less inflammatory infiltration and more angiogenesis (rat model; 808 nm clinically applied)
