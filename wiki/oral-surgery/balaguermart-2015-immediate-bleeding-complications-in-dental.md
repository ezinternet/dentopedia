## Summary
This systematic review screened 486 articles and included 15 on severe immediate bleeding complications after dental implant placement (mean age 60 years). The mandibular canine region was the most common site, explained by the close proximity of the sublingual and submental arteries to the lingual cortical plate. The dominant mechanism was lingual cortical plate perforation damaging the sublingual artery, always with implants ≥15 mm when length was specified. Bleeding could be immediate (70.6%) or delayed up to 7 hours. Management prioritized securing the airway (41% intubation, 47% tracheotomy), followed by hematoma drainage and artery ligation; no fatalities were reported. One upper-jaw case involved the posterior superior alveolar artery (PSAA) after indirect sinus lift.

## Key Contributions
- Mandibular canine region identified as highest bleeding-risk zone in implant surgery.
- Lingual cortical plate perforation → sublingual artery damage as dominant mechanism.
- Bleeding associated with implants ≥15 mm in length.
- Airway-first emergency management protocol documented.

## Methodology
Systematic review (PubMed + Embase, English, up to May 2014). 486 articles screened, 28 after dedup, 15 included. Excluded anticoagulated patients. Outcomes: time to hemorrhage, implant length, cause, treatment.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Most common site | Mandibular canine (6 cases), incisor (2), premolar (1) |
| Cause (when specified) | Lingual plate perforation → sublingual artery damage |
| Implant length | Always ≥15 mm when specified |
| Bleeding timing | Immediate 70.6%; up to 7 h post-op |
| Intubation | 41% |
| Tracheotomy | 47% |
| Gauze pressure efficacy | 1 case |
| Fatalities | None |
| Upper jaw | 1 case (PSAA, post sinus lift, Caldwell-Luc) |
| Pterygoid region | No heavy bleeding reported |
