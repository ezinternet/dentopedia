## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of the authors' own Wistar-rat facial nerve transection/anastomosis experimental program (multiple series, video-based vibrissal whisking analysis + immunohistochemistry) testing whether trigeminal (V) sensory input governs facial (VII) motor recovery.

Excising the ipsilateral infraorbital sensory nerve worsened whisking recovery and abolished the benefit of manual stimulation, while manual/vibrissal stimulation of intact trigeminal afferents restored synaptic density on facial motoneurons, cut pathological polyinnervation roughly in half (53%→22%), and produced near-normal whisking-amplitude recovery.

Trigeminal sensory input is a necessary co-factor — not merely an adjunct — for accurate and functional facial motor reinnervation, but the evidence is entirely rat-based; translation to human post-surgical/traumatic facial palsy rehabilitation is a stated hypothesis, not clinical evidence.

## 세줄요약

저자들 자신의 위스타 랫트 안면신경(Facial Nerve, CN VII) 절단-봉합 실험 프로그램(여러 시리즈, 진동모(vibrissae) 휘스킹 동작분석 + 면역조직화학)을 정리한 내러티브 리뷰로, 삼차신경(Trigeminal Nerve, CN V) 감각 입력이 안면 운동회복을 좌우하는지 검증한다.

동측 안와하신경(Infraorbital Nerve, IOn) 절제는 휘스킹 회복을 악화시키고 수기자극(Manual Stimulation, Mstim)의 이득을 무효화한 반면, 온전한 삼차신경 구심로에 대한 수기·진동모 자극(Vstim/Mstim)은 안면신경핵 운동신경세포(Motoneuron)의 시냅스 밀도를 회복시키고 병적 다중신경지배(Polyinnervation, 53%→22%)를 절반으로 줄이며 거의 정상에 가까운 휘스킹 진폭 회복을 이끌었다.

삼차신경 감각 입력은 정확하고 기능적인 안면 운동 재신경분포에 필요한 보조인자이나, 근거가 전적으로 랫트 실험에 국한되어 있어 수술 후·외상성 안면마비 재활로의 인간 임상 적용은 아직 가설이며 실증 근거는 아니다.

## Summary

This 2022 IJMS review by the Cologne group (Angelov, Rink-Notzon, Gordon and colleagues) consolidates a series of their own Wistar-rat experiments testing one central hypothesis: recovery of mimic (facial-expression) function after facial nerve injury is poor not only because of intrinsic motor axon regrowth problems, but because axotomized facial motoneurons are deprived of adequate sensory (trigeminal) input, leaving them "hyperexcitable but unable to discharge" — electrically silent despite being primed to fire.

The rat facial-nerve/vibrissae model allows two things simultaneously: (1) behavioral quantification of recovery via video-based analysis of rhythmic whisker (vibrissal) whisking amplitude, and (2) detailed morphological analysis of muscle-level polyinnervation and brainstem-level synaptic coverage on facial motoneurons. The infraorbital nerve (IOn), a sensory branch of the trigeminal nerve (V2, maxillary division) supplying the vibrissal whisker pad, is the experimental lever — it can be excised (removing sensory input) or its territory stimulated (manual massage = Mstim, or forced ipsilateral use via contralateral vibrissal-hair removal = Vstim) to test causal necessity and sufficiency of trigeminal sensory input for motor recovery.

Across the reviewed experiments, the pattern is consistent: intact or stimulated trigeminal sensory input improves whisking recovery and reduces pathological (polyneuronal) muscle reinnervation, whereas ablating trigeminal sensory input worsens recovery and can even convert a normally beneficial intervention (manual stimulation) into a harmful one. A separate finding — that excising the *contralateral* infraorbital nerve improves the accuracy (myotopic specificity) of motoneuron reinnervation on the injured side — has no direct clinical application but supports a mechanistic model in which forced reliance on the sensory-intact, motor-injured side strengthens appropriate axonal regrowth.

## Key Contributions

- Synthesizes multiple linked rat experimental series into a unified causal framework: trigeminal sensory afferents are necessary, not just adjunctive, for accurate and functional facial motor (VII) reinnervation.
- Shows that infraorbital nerve excision abolishes or reverses the benefit of manual stimulation on whisking recovery — a causal demonstration, not merely correlation.
- Links three levels of analysis — whisking behavior, muscle endplate polyinnervation histology, and facial-nucleus synaptophysin (perisomatic synapse) histology — into one explanatory model of post-axotomy motoneuron dysfunction.
- Rules out three candidate muscle neurotrophic factors (FGF2, IGF1, NGF) as the mechanism behind manual stimulation's benefit, pointing instead toward a central/synaptic (rather than peripheral/trophic) mechanism.
- Proposes a directly clinically-motivated hypothesis: massage-based sensory stimulation of trigeminal-innervated facial skin/mucosa could substitute for more elaborate physiotherapy sensory-stimulation techniques after combined facial-trigeminal nerve injury in humans.

## Methodology

Multiple linked Wistar rat experimental series sharing a common architecture: unilateral facial nerve (Fn) transection-and-suture (Fn-n) — sometimes limited to the buccal branch (Bn-n) — with or without concurrent infraorbital nerve (IOn) transection/excision (ipsilateral or contralateral), and with or without a post-injury sensory-stimulation intervention (Mstim = daily 5–10 min manual whisker-pad massage; Vstim = forced ipsilateral whisker use via contralateral vibrissal-hair removal).

Outcome measures:
- **Functional**: video-based motion analysis of vibrissal whisking amplitude and angular velocity.
- **Muscle histology**: class III-tubulin/α-bungarotoxin staining of the levator labii superioris (LLS) muscle to quantify polyneuronal (pathological) vs mono-neuronal (normal) innervation of motor endplates.
- **Reinnervation specificity**: retrograde tracer double-labeling (Fluoro-Gold, DiI, Fast Blue applied to distal nerve branches or the target whisker-pad muscle) to map myotopic accuracy of facial-nucleus motoneuron regeneration.
- **Synaptic input**: synaptophysin immunofluorescence with grayscale densitometric quantification of perisomatic synaptic coverage on retrogradely-labeled facial motoneurons (measuring "synaptic stripping" and its reversal).
- **Trophic-factor assay**: sandwich ELISA for FGF2, IGF1, and NGF protein in LLS muscle 8 weeks post-injury.

## Results

**Ablating trigeminal sensory input worsens recovery and negates Mstim benefit** — After Fn-n alone, whisking amplitude fell from ~62±13° (intact) to 19±6°; adding ipsilateral IOn excision did not change amplitude further on its own but eliminated the previously-beneficial effect of manual stimulation (Fn-n + IOn-ipsi-ex + Mstim = 14±6°, *worse* than untreated). Reinnervated-muscle polyinnervation likewise stayed pathologically high (51±10%) when Mstim was combined with IOn excision, versus 22±3% with Mstim and intact sensory supply.

**Stimulating trigeminal afferents improves recovery after combined facial+infraorbital nerve injury** — In rats with both facial and infraorbital nerves transected and repaired, postoperative Vstim, Mstim, or both significantly improved whisking amplitude (28–32°) versus no treatment (11±4°), roughly doubling amplitude though still ~50% below intact. Endplate polyinnervation fell from 58±8% (untreated) to 33–40% with stimulation. Facial-nucleus synaptophysin coverage and synaptic-profile counts, however, were *not* restored by any stimulation regimen in this double-lesion model — implying the functional gain reflects improved synaptic efficacy rather than synapse number.

**Contralateral (not ipsilateral) infraorbital lesion improves reinnervation accuracy** — After buccal-nerve transection-repair alone, myotopic (correct-target) reinnervation accuracy was ~48–56% by retrograde tracer mapping; excising the *contralateral* infraorbital nerve — forcing the rat to rely on the sensory-intact, motor-injured side — raised correct targeting to 69% and reduced double-labeled (misdirected) motoneurons to ~8%. A parallel whisker-pad-specific tracing experiment showed the fraction of motoneurons reinnervating their original target rising from 27% (Bn-n alone) to 41% (contralateral IOn excision, p=0.01 vs ipsilateral excision).

**Manual stimulation with fully intact sensory supply restores synaptic density and near-normalizes whisking** — In the single-lesion (Fn-n only, sensory intact) model, daily Mstim restored facial-nucleus synaptophysin signal to near-intact levels (33×10^n vs 34.3×10^n intact, vs 29.2×10^n in handling-only controls, p=0.007), cut polyinnervation from 53±10% to 22±5%, and produced near-complete restoration of whisking amplitude — the strongest and most clinically suggestive result in the review.

**Neurotrophic factors ruled out as the mechanism** — FGF2, IGF1, and NGF protein levels in the reinnervated muscle did not differ between Mstim and handling-only groups at 2 months, arguing against a peripheral trophic-factor explanation for Mstim's benefit and favoring a central/synaptic mechanism (e.g., preservation of glutamatergic synaptic input, analogous to exercise-induced synapse retention on axotomized spinal motoneurons).

**Three named causes of poor mimic-function recovery** (the paper's explicit framing, stated in the abstract and Section 1):
1. Poor axonal navigation and excessive collateral branching of regenerating motor axons.
2. Axonal crosstalk — abnormal exchange of nerve impulses between adjacent regrowing axons (possible ephaptic transmission).
3. Insufficient synaptic input to the axotomized facial motoneurons, rendering them hyperexcitable (from increased resting membrane potential and residual axo-dendritic synapses) yet unable to discharge — "electrical silence."

Infraorbital-nerve/vibrissal-hair/manual whisker-pad stimulation targets primarily cause (3): it supplies the missing sensory drive that keeps facial motoneurons capable of discharging and organizing accurate target reinnervation, secondarily reducing pathological polyinnervation (a downstream marker of cause 1/2-type misdirected regrowth) and improving whisking motor performance.

## Limitations

All data are from Wistar rat facial/trigeminal nerve transection models; there is no human clinical trial data in this paper. The authors themselves note that the combined bilateral facial+trigeminal surgical paradigm (contralateral IOn excision) has "no application in human patients" — it is a mechanistic probe, not a translatable protocol. Even the more clinically suggestive finding (manual stimulation benefit with intact sensory supply) is extrapolated from rat massage protocols; the paper proposes but does not test the clinical extension (temporary trigeminal anesthetic blockade, or massage-based sensory stimulation in facial-palsy patients). **Direct clinical translation to post-dental-procedure or postsurgical facial palsy is a stated hypothesis, not evidence** — no human facial-palsy trial data appear anywhere in this review. Mechanistically, synaptic counts on motoneuron somata were not restored by stimulation in the double-lesion experiments despite functional improvement, so the precise synaptic mechanism (efficacy vs number) remains unresolved. The neurotrophic-factor assay was a single 2-month endpoint; earlier time-course changes cannot be excluded. Because this is a synthesis across several of the authors' own prior studies (potentially different rat sub-cohorts/paradigms), individual effect sizes should be cross-checked against primary sources if used for precise quantitative claims.
