## Three-line Summary

Rat sciatic nerve transection-and-repair model (n=120 Sprague-Dawley, 4 groups) testing Ginkgo biloba extract (EGb) at 0, 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg/day over multiple postoperative intervals.

EGb significantly improved sensory regeneration distance, motor nerve conduction velocity, CMAP, axon regeneration index, and muscle mass vs saline; all parameters except SFI showed clear dose-response (200 > 100 > 50 > saline).

EGb promotes peripheral nerve regeneration dose-dependently in rats, supporting its adjunct potential for IAN paresthesia management, though rat-to-human dose extrapolation requires clinical validation.

## Summary

Lin et al. (2007) investigated the dose-effect relationship of Ginkgo biloba extract (EGb) on peripheral nerve regeneration using a standardized rat sciatic nerve transection-and-repair model. 120 Sprague-Dawley rats were allocated to four groups receiving normal saline, low-dose EGb (50 mg/kg/d), moderate-dose EGb (100 mg/kg/d), or high-dose EGb (200 mg/kg/d), with multi-timepoint assessments of functional, electrophysiological, and histological outcomes.

All EGb-treated groups outperformed saline controls across virtually every outcome measure. Crucially, a clear dose-response gradient was demonstrated: the 200 mg/kg/d group achieved superior results compared to the 100 and 50 mg/kg/d groups in MNCV, CMAP amplitude, axon regeneration index, sensory regeneration distance, and gastrocnemius muscle mass. The sciatic functional index (SFI) was the single exception — all EGb doses improved SFI over saline, but no statistically significant inter-dose difference was detected, possibly reflecting a ceiling effect in the walking-track assay.

The proposed mechanism centers on EGb's multimodal neuroprotective/neuroregenerative activity: anti-apoptotic effects (bilobalide, flavonoids), anti-oxidant activity, Schwann cell promotion, anti-glutamate neurotoxicity, and macrophage nitric oxide inhibition. The study provides the first dose-characterized evidence for EGb's neuroregenerative efficacy in a controlled in vivo peripheral nerve model.

## Key Contributions

- Established a dose-response relationship for EGb in peripheral nerve regeneration (50 → 100 → 200 mg/kg/d gradient confirmed for most parameters)
- Multi-parameter outcome battery (functional + electrophysiological + histological) strengthens internal validity
- Identified SFI as a potentially ceiling-limited measure when comparing EGb doses in this model

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Model | Rat sciatic nerve transection + epineural repair |
| Animals | 120 Sprague-Dawley rats (n=30/group) |
| Groups | Saline / EGb 50 / EGb 100 / EGb 200 mg/kg/d |
| EGb formulation | Standardized Ginkgo biloba extract (EGb 761-type: 24% flavonoid glycosides, 6% terpene lactones) |
| Outcomes | Sensory regeneration distance, SFI (walking track), MNCV, CMAP, axon regeneration index, gastrocnemius muscle mass |
| Assessment | Multiple postoperative intervals |

## Results

- **Sensory regeneration distance**: EGb > saline; dose-dependent (200 > 100 > 50)
- **Motor nerve conduction velocity (MNCV)**: EGb > saline; dose-dependent
- **CMAP amplitude**: EGb > saline; dose-dependent
- **Axon regeneration index (histology)**: EGb > saline; dose-dependent
- **Muscle mass**: EGb > saline; dose-dependent
- **Sciatic functional index (SFI)**: EGb > saline; NO dose-response gradient detected (exception)
- **Conclusion**: "The higher the dose, the better the result" — with SFI caveat
