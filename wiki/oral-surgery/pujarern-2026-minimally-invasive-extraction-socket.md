## Summary

Tooth extraction triggers irreversible alveolar remodeling, with buccal bone undergoing preferential resorption owing to loss of the periodontal ligament (PDL)-dependent bundle bone. Pujarern et al. synthesize the four-phase healing model (hemostasis → inflammation → proliferation → remodeling) and cite Amler's (1960) foundational time sequence and Araújo & Lindhe's (2005) beagle dog histology to quantify the buccal-lingual crest divergence (~45 μm/day, net 2.2 mm over 8 weeks). The review then catalogs 10 minimally invasive extraction techniques — ranging from hand periotomes and piezotomes to magnetic mallets, vertical extraction systems, vestibular root extraction (VRE), and enzymatic collagenase application — evaluating each on mechanism, evidence quality, and procedural constraints. Socket preservation strategies (ARP, SCTG) are positioned as complements to, not substitutes for, atraumatic technique.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates the four-stage socket healing framework with cellular actors (fibroblasts, osteoblasts) and growth factor axes per phase
- Quantifies Araújo & Lindhe buccal crest resorption: +0.3 mm (1w) → −1.9 mm (8w) vs lingual; net 2.2 ± 0.2 mm (~45 μm/day)
- Maps 10 minimally invasive techniques by mechanism (syndesmotomy vs force redistribution vs axial pull vs enzymatic)
- Identifies piezotome as producing least buccal bone loss but most heat (+18°C) and longest operating time
- Establishes enzymatic extraction (ColG, Clostridium histolyticum) as reducing PDL disruption force by 50% in ex vivo porcine model

## Methodology

Narrative review; no systematic search or GRADE. Searches across minimally invasive extraction, socket healing, and socket preservation literature. Includes RCTs, SRs, case series, and histological studies. No date restriction stated; recent literature emphasized (2020s).

## Results

### Socket Healing Phases

| Phase | Timing | Key cells / events |
|---|---|---|
| Hemostasis & coagulation | 0–24 h | Platelet aggregation, fibrin clot |
| Inflammation | 24–72 h | Neutrophils, macrophages, fibroblast activation |
| Proliferation | 2–3 weeks | Granulation tissue, angiogenesis, provisional matrix; woven bone begins |
| Remodeling | ≥ 4 weeks | Woven → lamellar bone + marrow; osteoclast-osteoblast coupling |

**Amler (1960) human sequence**: epithelial proliferation day 4 → bone formation day 7 → clot organization days 7–21 → epithelial fusion day 22 → bone fill day 35.

**Fibroblasts** orchestrate healing through growth factor secretion, MMP expression, ECM remodeling, and myofibroblast transdifferentiation for wound contraction.

### Dimensional Changes

- Buccal > lingual resorption (bundle bone PDL-vascular dependency)
- Molar > premolar ≥ incisor (larger alveoli, complex morphology, greater occlusal forces)
- Araújo & Lindhe buccal crest: 1w +0.3 mm → 2w −0.3 mm → 4w −0.9 mm → 8w −1.9 mm vs lingual; buccal wall thinning at all levels
- ~50% ridge volume loss within 12 weeks; two-thirds within first 3 months
- Soft tissue closure: 10–20 weeks; radiographic bone fill: 3–6 months

### Minimally Invasive Technique Summary

| Technique | Mechanism | Notable data |
|---|---|---|
| Periotome | PDL fiber cutting (syndesmotomy) | Op time 5.8 vs 12.8 min; VAS↓; gingival laceration↓ |
| Flexible periotome | Syndesmotomy (flexible blade) | Op time 4.43 vs 7.2 min; superior Landry healing index |
| Piezotome | Ultrasonic 29–32 kHz | Least buccal bone loss; heat +18°C; longest op time |
| Magnetic mallet | Axial magnetic pulse | 427 ankylosed teeth: 0 cortical plate loss |
| SIBS | High-freq air vibration | Heat <3°C (vs piezotome +18°C) |
| Physics forceps | Single lever, 8× mechanical advantage | Crown/root fracture↓; VAS↓ day 1, 3 |
| Vertical extraction (Benex) | Axial screw pull | Overall 83–85%; single-root 87.2%, multi-root 69.7% |
| VRE | Vestibular access + coronal push | Pink esthetic score +1.27 (95% CI 0.15–2.39) |
| Enzymatic (ColG) | PDL collagen degradation | Force ↓50% vs PBS control (ex vivo porcine) |
| SST / Root sectioning | Buccal root retention | Preserves buccal bundle bone; favorable histology |

### Socket Preservation

ARP graft stabilizes blood clot + osteoconductive scaffold → gradual resorption as new bone replaces. Buccal SCTG = gold standard for ridge contour. Adjuvant factors: cigarette smoking, thin buccal bone wall, thin gingival phenotype all worsen outcomes.
