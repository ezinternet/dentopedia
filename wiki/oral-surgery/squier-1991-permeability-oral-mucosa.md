## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Critical Reviews in Oral Biology and Medicine, 1991; Dows Institute, University of Iowa) synthesizing animal and in vitro permeability studies available through 1991 on site-specific variation in oral mucosal permeability — no original experimental data; 20-page review with extensive literature citations.

Oral mucosal permeability is markedly region-dependent: keratinized sites (gingiva, hard palate) are least permeable due to a lipid-type intercellular barrier in superficial epithelial layers; non-keratinized lining mucosa (buccal, floor of mouth, soft palate) is most permeable, correlating with faster turnover and greater susceptibility to mucosal diseases at those sites.

Clinically, high-permeability non-keratinized sites are most suitable for transmucosal drug delivery; the molecular cascade (PITX1, IRX1) driving these permeability differences was unknown in 1991, but the lipid-barrier architecture established here remains the foundational framework for modern oral mucosal biology.

## One-line Summary

Classic 1991 narrative review establishing that oral mucosal permeability varies regionally — keratinized areas (gingiva, hard palate) are least permeable, non-keratinized lining mucosa is most permeable — due to differences in intercellular lipid composition.

## Summary

This foundational review by Squier (University of Iowa) establishes that oral mucosa is not uniformly permeable and shows marked regional variation. Keratinized masticatory mucosa (attached gingiva, hard palate) has the lowest permeability, while non-keratinized lining mucosa (buccal, floor of mouth, soft palate) is most permeable. The permeability gradient is attributed to differences in the types of lipids forming the intercellular permeability barrier in the superficial epithelial layers — analogous to the lipid barrier of skin stratum corneum but distinct in composition from non-keratinized sites. Clinically, this regional variation explains patterns of mucosal disease susceptibility (higher in less-protected non-keratinized sites) and can be exploited for transmucosal drug delivery via buccal or sublingual routes.

## Key Contributions

- **Regional permeability hierarchy**: keratinized (lowest) < non-keratinized lining (highest) — directly parallels the turnover rate hierarchy
- Barrier mechanism is **lipid intercellular** (not simply keratinization per se) — aligns with Squier 1981's argument that keratinization ≠ impermeability
- Non-keratinized mucosa is useful for **transmucosal systemic drug delivery** (buccal, sublingual)
- Regional permeability differences relate to **mucosal disease susceptibility** patterns
