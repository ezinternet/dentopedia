## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study of 190 visits / 333 teeth extracted in cirrhotic patients on a transplant waiting list (platelets 16,000–216,000/mm³, INR < 3).

Unusual intra-operative bleeding occurred in 12 visits (6.31%) and postoperative haemorrhagic complications in 12, all controlled locally; low platelet count was associated with intra-operative bleeding (P = 0.026) but explained only 16% of cases (adjusted R² = 0.16).

Platelets above 16,000/mm³ with INR below 3 required no pre-operative transfusion — and the unexplained variance is what points at platelet function rather than platelet number.

## Summary

This retrospective series followed cirrhotic patients awaiting liver transplant through 190 visits in which 333 teeth were extracted. Two bleeding outcomes were separated: unusual intra-operative bleeding (persisting beyond 10 minutes after routine haemostatic measures) and postoperative haemorrhagic complications. Each occurred 12 times — 6.31% of visits for the intra-operative outcome — and every event was controlled with local measures. Low platelet count was statistically associated with intra-operative bleeding (P = 0.026), but the model explained only 16% of the variance (adjusted R² = 0.16), which the authors read as evidence that platelet *function* is the more relevant defect. The practical conclusion is a threshold statement: above 16,000 platelets/mm³ and below INR 3, no pre-operative transfusion is required, provided a local haemostatic plan is in place.

## Key Contributions

- **Gives event denominators** that the systematic review cannot: 12/190 intra-operative, 12/190 post-operative, zero uncontrolled events.
- **Splits the outcome** into intra-operative versus post-operative bleeding, which behave differently and are managed differently.
- **Reports a negative result honestly.** Platelet count predicts bleeding weakly (16% of variance), and the authors say so instead of presenting it as a screening rule.
- **Produces a usable threshold** — platelets >16,000/mm³, INR <3 — with the explicit caveat that local measures do the work.

## Methodology

- **Design**: analytic retrospective observational study of records.
- **Population**: cirrhotic patients on the liver transplant waiting list.
- **Volume**: 190 visits, 333 teeth (1–9 per visit).
- **Laboratory range**: platelets 16,000–216,000/mm³; INR < 3.
- **Outcomes**: unusual intra-operative bleeding (>10 min after routine haemostasis); postoperative haemorrhagic complications.
- **Analysis**: bivariate association of outcomes with clinical/laboratory variables, plus regression with adjusted R².

## Results

| Outcome | Events | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Unusual intra-operative bleeding | 12 | 6.31% of visits |
| Postoperative haemorrhagic complications | 12 | — |
| Events requiring more than local measures | 0 | 0% |

- Low platelet count ↔ intra-operative bleeding: **P = 0.026**.
- Variance explained by platelet count: **16%** (adjusted R² = 0.16; P = 0.44).
- Threshold statement: platelets >16,000/mm³ and INR < 3 → no pre-operative transfusion needed.

## Clinical Reading

- **Make a local haemostasis plan, not a haematology referral.** Zero events escaped local control in this series, and the same was true across the seven studies in the review.
- **Use the numbers as an envelope, not a guarantee.** 16,000/mm³ and INR 3 are the observed limits of a single-centre series, not a validated cut-off — a patient below them is outside the evidence, not automatically a transfusion case.
- **Platelet function is the open question.** Only 16% of bleeding was explained by platelet number; if a cirrhotic patient bleeds despite an acceptable count, the number is not the whole story.
- **Do not cite this as an alcohol finding.** Alcohol-related cirrhosis is included but not isolated — the exposure studied is cirrhosis, not drinking.
