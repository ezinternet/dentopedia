## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (Am J Emerg Med, 2016) of antibiotic treatment failure in randomized trials of cellulitis: 19 articles, 20 studies, 3,935 patients, trials from developed countries only.

Treatment failure ranged 6–37% in the 9 trials reporting intent-to-treat results, pooled 18% (95% CI 15–21%); among clinically evaluable participants 3–42%, pooled 12% (95% CI 10–14%).

The authors attribute the wide variation to confusion of cellulitis with mimics and to doubtful construct validity of the diagnosis, and call for objective diagnostic tools; abstract only, so trial-level data are not available.

## Summary

Cellulitis trials report very different antibiotic failure rates. This meta-analysis pooled them and suggests the spread reflects who was enrolled (including mimics) rather than drug differences.

## Key Contributions

- Pooled antibiotic failure estimates: 18% (ITT), 12% (clinically evaluable).
- Argues for objective diagnostic tools for cellulitis.

## Methodology

- PubMed to August 2015, English, developed-country studies only; two-reviewer extraction; random-effects (Freeman-Tukey).

## Results

- 20 studies, 3,935 patients; failure 6–37% (ITT trials), 3–42% (evaluable); pooled 18% and 12%.

## Limitations (reader's appraisal)

- Abstract only; the mimics explanation is interpretation. Skin cellulitis trials, not odontogenic infection; failure in dental cellulitis often means an undrained collection or unremoved source, which this review does not address.
