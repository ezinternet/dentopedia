## Three-line Summary

Exploratory non-randomized clinical study (n=27; 18 inferior alveolar nerve [IAN], 9 lingual nerve [LN] injuries after mandibular third molar surgery) comparing low-level laser therapy (LLLT), TENS, and placebo for neurosensory recovery, evaluated with VAS symptom scores plus fiber-type-specific electromyographic cutaneous silent period (CSP) testing — painful stimuli activating Aδ and C fibers, painless stimuli activating Aβ fibers.

LLLT produced statistically significant improvement across a broad symptom set (numbness, pain, burning, speech difficulty, accidental biting, taste disturbance), significantly outperforming TENS and placebo on several symptoms, while CSP electrophysiological recovery remained largely non-significant and dissociated from the clinical improvement.

Clinically useful as a non-invasive option for post-third-molar IAN/LN neurosensory symptoms, but the small, non-randomized, unblinded design (with TENS→LLLT crossover in 5 patients) limits causal certainty; larger randomized trials with standardized protocols are needed.

## 세줄요약

하악 제3대구치 발치 후 하치조신경(IAN, 18명)·설신경(LN, 9명) 손상 환자 27명을 대상으로 저출력 레이저치료(LLLT)·경피신경전기자극(TENS)·위약을 비교한 비무작위 탐색 임상연구로, 통증성 자극(Aδ·C 섬유)과 무통성 자극(Aβ 섬유)을 구분한 근전도 피부 무반응기(CSP) 검사를 함께 시행했다.

LLLT는 저림·통증·화끈거림·발음장애·물림·미각장애 등 광범위한 증상에서 통계적으로 유의한 개선을 보여 TENS·위약보다 우수했으나, 전기생리학적(CSP) 회복은 대부분 유의하지 않아 임상적 개선과 분리되는 양상을 보였다.

발치 후 IAN/LN 감각이상에 대한 비침습적 옵션으로 유용할 수 있으나, 소규모·비무작위·비맹검 설계(TENS→LLLT 교차 5례 포함)로 인과성 확정에는 한계가 있어 표준화된 대규모 무작위 연구가 필요하다.

## Summary

This exploratory, IRB-approved, non-randomized clinical study evaluated LLLT and TENS for neurosensory recovery in 27 patients with persistent IAN (n=18) or LN (n=9) paresthesia after impacted mandibular third molar surgery. Treatment allocation was pragmatic (not randomized): 6 IAN and 6 LN patients received LLLT (830 nm GaAlAs diode, 100 mW, 3 points along the nerve, 20 sessions over 4 weeks), 10 IAN patients received TENS (50–100 Hz, 2 extraoral points, 20 sessions), and 5 patients (2 IAN, 3 LN) received placebo. Outcomes combined a composite Visual Analog Scale (VAS) symptom score and electromyographic cutaneous silent period (CSP) testing that explicitly separated painful stimuli (activating Aδ and C fibers) from painless stimuli (activating Aβ fibers) to probe trigeminal inhibitory reflex pathways.

LLLT achieved significant pre-post improvement in 12 of the assessed symptoms (including pain, burning, speech difficulty, accidental biting, and taste disturbance), versus only 8 symptoms for TENS, and none for placebo. Direct LLLT-vs-TENS comparison showed significantly greater benefit from LLLT in tension, stiffness, crawling sensation, cold sensation, speech difficulty, salivation, and accidental biting. A nerve-type subgroup signal emerged: LN-injury patients had more residual symptoms after LLLT than IAN-injury patients, despite significant improvement in both groups.

Electrophysiologically, CSP durations were consistently longer on the healthy side than the pathological side at baseline (confirming the test's sensitivity to injury), but post-treatment CSP prolongation on the pathological side reached significance only for the mylohyoid muscle under specific tasks — the electrophysiological signal did not track the clinical improvement closely, a dissociation the authors flag as important for interpreting future IAN/LN recovery studies.

## Key Contributions

- Explicit fiber-type mapping for trigeminal CSP testing: painful stimulus → Aδ/C fiber activation; painless stimulus → Aβ fiber activation, giving a concrete methodological anchor for how Aβ/Aδ/C classification is operationalized in IAN/LN electrophysiological assessment.
- Head-to-head (non-randomized) comparison of two non-invasive modalities — LLLT and TENS — in real post-third-molar nerve-injury patients, rather than isolated single-arm reports.
- Quantifies a nerve-type interaction (LN vs IAN) in LLLT response that had not been captured elsewhere in the wiki's IAN literature.
- Demonstrates dissociation between clinical (VAS) and electrophysiological (CSP) recovery — clinical improvement can occur without commensurate objective nerve-conduction/reflex normalization.

## Methodology

Prospective, non-randomized, pragmatic-allocation study (IRB: Ege University Faculty of Dentistry, code 193372). 27 patients with post-third-molar-surgery IAN (n=18) or LN (n=9) paresthesia, no systemic disease, no spontaneous recovery. LLLT: 830 nm GaAlAs continuous-wave diode, 100 mW, 40 s/point (4 J/point), 3 points along the nerve course, 20 sessions (5x/week, 4 weeks), started after edema/trismus resolution. TENS: 50–100 Hz, 40–75 μs pulse, 10–30 mA, 2 extraoral points x 5 min, 20 sessions. CSP measured via 4-channel EMG (orbicularis oculi, bilateral) under painful (Aδ/C) and painless (Aβ) stimulus conditions for masseter and mylohyoid muscles, pre/post treatment. Primary outcome: composite total VAS score. Statistics: Wilcoxon signed-rank (within-group) and Mann-Whitney U (between-group/subgroup), with Holm-Bonferroni correction on secondary individual-symptom analyses.

## Results

LLLT: significant improvement (p<0.05) in numbness, pain, stabbing, electric shock, tension, stiffness, crawling, tingling, burning, speech difficulty, accidental biting, taste disturbance. TENS: significant improvement only in numbness, tension, crawling, cold sensation, tingling, burning, speech difficulty, taste disturbance. Placebo: no significant change in any parameter. LLLT vs TENS: LLLT significantly better for tension, stiffness, crawling, cold sensation, speech difficulty, salivation, accidental biting. Nerve-type subgroup: LN patients showed higher residual symptom severity post-LLLT than IAN patients (biting, crawling, speech difficulty, taste disturbance), though both groups improved significantly on their respective symptom subsets. CSP: healthy side significantly longer than pathological side at baseline for masseter and mylohyoid across nearly all tasks (p<0.05, one exception p=0.066); post-treatment, only mylohyoid CSP under specific painful/painless maximal-force tasks showed significant prolongation (p<0.05) — masseter CSP showed no significant post-treatment change.
