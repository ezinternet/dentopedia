## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Dent Clin North Am, 2017) of odontogenic infections: pathogenesis, microbiota, spread and treatment principles.

The pathogenesis is polymicrobial (facultative and strict anaerobes; dominant isolates strictly anaerobic gram-negative rods and gram-positive cocci); periapical infection is the most common form; infection is usually confined near the alveolar ridge but can spread into deep fascial spaces, with cavernous sinus thrombosis, brain abscess, airway obstruction and mediastinitis as possible complications.

The most important element of treatment is eliminating the primary source, with antibiotics as an adjunct; abstract only, so no numbers or regimens are available.

## Summary

Odontogenic infection starts from a tooth, is a mixed anaerobe-dominated infection, most often periapical, and usually stays near the alveolar ridge; when it spreads into deep fascial spaces it can reach the airway, mediastinum or intracranial structures.

## Key Contributions

- Concise statement of the polymicrobial, anaerobe-dominated nature of dental infection.
- List of serious complications: cavernous sinus thrombosis, brain abscess, airway obstruction, mediastinitis.
