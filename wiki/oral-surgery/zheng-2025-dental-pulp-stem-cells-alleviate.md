## Three-line Summary

Preclinical mechanistic study — crab-eating macaque facial-nerve-transection model (n=12, 1-year follow-up), rat facial nerve injury (FNI) crush model (n=16), and in-vitro RSC96 Schwann-cell pyroptosis co-culture — evaluating dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs) for nerve regeneration.

DPSC-loaded nerve conduits improved long-term myelin/axon regeneration and CMAP recovery vs bare conduit in macaques (approaching autograft); in the rat FNI model and in vitro, DPSCs reduced Schwann-cell pyroptosis and mitochondrial ROS by transferring functional mitochondria via tunneling nanotubes (TNTs), a transfer amplified by Schwann-cell TNFα → NF-κB signaling in DPSCs, and TNFα-preconditioned DPSCs showed superior efficacy.

Purely preclinical (macaque/rat/in-vitro only, no human data) — DPSC cell therapy for facial nerve injury is a mechanistic proof-of-concept, not a clinical option today; dental pulp (extracted third molars) is highlighted as a practical, low-immunogenicity autologous cell source for future translation.

## 세줄요약

전임상 기전연구 — 게잡이원숭이 안면신경절단 모델(n=12, 1년 추적), 랫드 안면신경손상(FNI) 압박모델(n=16), RSC96 슈반세포 파이롭토시스 공동배양(in vitro) 3단 구조로 치수줄기세포(DPSC)의 신경재생 효과를 평가했다.

원숭이에서 DPSC 탑재 신경도관이 대조(Neurolac® 단독) 대비 장기 수초·축삭 재생과 CMAP 회복을 유의하게 개선(자가이식에 근접); 랫드 FNI 및 in vitro에서는 DPSC가 터널링 나노튜브(TNT)를 통해 기능성 미토콘드리아를 슈반세포로 전달함으로써 파이롭토시스와 미토콘드리아 ROS를 감소시켰고, 이 전달은 슈반세포 유래 TNFα→DPSC NF-κB 신호로 증폭되며 TNFα 전처리 DPSC가 더 우수한 효과를 보였다.

완전한 전임상 단계(원숭이/랫드/in vitro만, 인체 데이터 없음) — 안면신경손상에 대한 DPSC 세포치료는 기전적 개념증명이지 현재 임상 옵션이 아니며, 발치한 사랑니에서 얻는 치수는 향후 임상전환을 위한 실용적·저면역원성 자가세포원으로 제시된다.

## Summary

Zheng et al. (2025, *Bioactive Materials*) investigate why dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs, 치수줄기세포) enhance peripheral nerve regeneration — a benefit long observed but mechanistically unexplained. Using a three-tier design (12-month non-human-primate facial-nerve-transection model → rat facial nerve injury (FNI, 안면신경손상) crush model → in-vitro Schwann-cell (슈반세포) pyroptosis co-culture), they show DPSCs do not simply differentiate into Schwann cells or act only through diffusible paracrine factors. Instead, DPSCs physically transfer functional mitochondria into pyroptosis (파이롭토시스)-stressed Schwann cells through tunneling nanotubes (터널링 나노튜브, TNT), rescuing mitochondrial ROS (활성산소종) overload and membrane-potential collapse that would otherwise drive inflammasome-mediated Schwann-cell death. The transfer is amplified by a feed-forward loop: pyroptotic Schwann cells secrete TNFα, which activates NF-κB signaling in DPSCs, boosting mitochondrial biogenesis and TNT formation — and pre-treating DPSCs with TNFα before transplantation further improved in-vivo nerve repair.

For dentistry, the clinical relevance is the cell source: DPSCs come from a tissue dentists routinely discard (extracted third molars), making this an autologous, low-immunogenicity, chairside-accessible cell platform. The paper does not test this in humans — it is bench/animal mechanism work — but it strengthens the case that dental pulp is a viable stem-cell reservoir for future peripheral-nerve (including facial and possibly inferior alveolar) regenerative strategies, complementing the existing conduit + pharmacologic-adjunct literature in this wiki.

## Key Contributions

1. Identifies intercellular mitochondrial transfer via tunneling nanotubes — not differentiation or soluble paracrine signaling alone — as the operative mechanism by which DPSCs rescue pyroptotic Schwann cells.
2. Proves functional-mitochondria dependence: mitochondrial-DNA-depleted DPSCs (ρ⁰-DPSCs) still transfer mitochondria at a normal rate but fail to rescue Schwann-cell pyroptosis, showing that the mitochondria themselves — not merely the transfer event — must be functional.
3. Describes a feed-forward paracrine amplification loop: pyroptotic Schwann cell TNFα → NF-κB activation in DPSCs → increased mitochondrial biogenesis/TNT formation → more transfer → less pyroptosis.
4. Demonstrates that TNFα-preconditioning DPSCs before transplantation improves therapeutic efficacy in vivo, suggesting a practical "priming" strategy for future cell-therapy protocols.
5. Provides one of the longest-duration (12-month) non-human-primate validations of DPSC-loaded nerve conduits, with outcomes approaching (but not equaling) autologous nerve graft.

## Methodology

- **Macaque facial-nerve-transection model** (n=12): 5 mm facial-nerve defect repaired with (a) Neurolac® conduit alone, (b) autologous nerve graft, or (c) Neurolac® conduit + DPSC injection (7.5×10⁷ cells/mL). 12-month endpoints: H&E, Ki67 proliferation IHC, TEM (myelin/axon thickness, G-ratio), and electrophysiology (CMAP amplitude/conduction velocity).
- **Rat FNI crush model** (n=16 SD rats): forceps-crush injury; DPSC (5×10⁵ cells) or TNFα-preconditioned DPSC (20 ng/mL × 12 h) injected at the injury site; functional scoring (blink reflex, whisker movement, facial symmetry) to day 42, double-blind; TEM at 2 weeks; immunofluorescence/Western blot for pyroptosis markers (GSDMD, cleaved-Caspase1, IL1β) and Schwann-cell markers (S100, MPZ) at day 4; MitoSox for mitochondrial ROS.
- **In-vitro mechanism**: RSC96 Schwann cells pyroptosis-induced with LPS + ATP (LA-RSC96). Direct-contact vs transwell co-culture with DPSCs isolated the requirement for cell-cell contact. Mito-mCherry-labeled DPSCs + CFSE-labeled RSC96 cells visualized/quantified mitochondrial transfer by confocal microscopy and flow cytometry. Pharmacologic tools: Mito-TEMPO (mitochondrial ROS scavenger), Cytochalasin B (TNT blocker), Bay 11-7082 (NF-κB inhibitor), TNFα-neutralizing antibody. ρ⁰-DPSCs (mtDNA-depleted) tested dependence on functional mitochondria. RNA-seq/GO/KEGG identified TNFα signaling as the key pathway activated in pyroptotic Schwann cells.

## Results

| Model | Key finding |
|---|---|
| Macaque, 12 mo | DPSCs@Neurolac® > Neurolac® alone: ↑ proliferation (Ki67), ↑ myelin/axon thickness, ↓ G-ratio, ↑ CMAP amplitude/conduction velocity (bare Neurolac® showed NO detectable CMAP); approached but did not match autograft |
| Rat FNI | DPSC treatment accelerated functional recovery; ↓ GSDMD, cleaved-Caspase1, IL1β; ↓ mitochondrial ROS; attenuated pyroptosis (most), ferroptosis, and necroptosis marker upregulation |
| In vitro | Mito-TEMPO alone alleviated pyroptosis (mitochondrial ROS is causal); DPSC protection required direct contact (transwell was insufficient); mitochondrial transfer via TNTs confirmed, higher into pyroptotic vs healthy cells, sharply increasing after 8 h, coincident with caspase-1 suppression by 16 h |
| ρ⁰-DPSC | Transferred mitochondria normally but FAILED to rescue pyroptosis — functional mitochondria required, not just the transfer event |
| Cytochalasin B (TNT block) | Abolished mitochondrial transfer and abolished the protective effect |
| TNFα/NF-κB axis | Pyroptotic Schwann cells secrete TNFα → activates NF-κB in DPSCs; blocking TNFα (neutralizing Ab) or NF-κB (Bay 11-7082) reduced TNT formation/transfer/protection; exogenous TNFα enhanced transfer |
| TNFα-preconditioned DPSC | ↑ mitochondrial biogenesis (PGC1α, NRF1, TFAM) and antioxidant/mitophagy markers (CAT, SOD1, Parkin, PINK1); improved in-vivo functional recovery and myelin thickness vs untreated DPSC |

**Limitation**: entirely preclinical — no human trial. Cross-species design (human DPSCs into rat/macaque hosts); in-vivo mitochondrial transfer was inferred rather than directly visualized in vivo (authors call for future fluorescent-labeling/EM/isotope-tracing confirmation in vivo). DPSC-based facial-nerve cell therapy is not a clinical option at this time.
