## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort study (n=287 orthognathic surgery patients, Tel-Aviv Sourasky Medical Center, 2010–2024) analyzing incidence, anatomical distribution, and postoperative treatment outcomes of trigeminal nerve injuries.

Neurosensory deficits occurred in 5.9% of cases (17/287, 88.2% mandibular/CN V3); low-level laser therapy (LLLT, 8/17 patients) produced subjective improvement in 75% vs 33% with no treatment, while corticosteroid (preoperative dexamethasone) and vitamin B complex were used adjunctively but complete sensory restoration was rarely achieved with any modality.

No standardized pharmacologic/physical-therapy protocol exists for post-orthognathic trigeminal neurosensory deficit; small event count (n=17) and non-standardized regimens limit causal inference — LLLT emerges as the most promising, but hypothesis-generating, adjunct.

## 세줄요약

후향적 코호트 연구(n=287, 정악교정수술 환자, 2010–2024) — 삼차신경 (Trigeminal Nerve) 손상의 발생률·해부학적 분포·치료 결과 분석.

감각이상은 5.9%(17/287, 88.2% 하악/CN V3)에서 발생; 저출력레이저치료 (Low-Level Laser Therapy, LLLT, 8/17명)는 무치료군 대비 주관적 호전율 75% vs 33%로 우세했으나, 코르티코스테로이드(수술 전 덱사메타손)·비타민B 복합제를 포함한 어떤 치료로도 완전한 감각 회복은 드물었음.

## Summary

This single-center retrospective cohort study reviewed 287 orthognathic surgery patients (Le Fort I, BSSO/SSO, IVRO, genioplasty combinations) treated at Tel-Aviv Sourasky Medical Center between 2010 and 2024 to characterize the incidence, anatomical distribution, and adjunctive treatment outcomes of postoperative trigeminal nerve injury. Neurosensory deficits occurred in 5.9% of cases (17/287), overwhelmingly in the mandibular (inferior alveolar nerve/CN V3) distribution. Of the 17 affected patients, most received preoperative dexamethasone prophylaxis (82.2%); postoperative adjunctive therapy included low-level laser therapy (LLLT, 47%), a single case of added vitamin B complex, or no treatment (53%). LLLT was associated with the highest rate of subjective sensory improvement (75%) compared to no treatment (33%), though no patient in any treatment arm achieved complete sensory restoration. The authors note the absence of any published, validated, step-by-step management protocol for post-orthognathic (or post-third-molar-extraction) trigeminal nerve injury, and call for prospective studies to establish one.

## Key Contributions

- Real-world incidence (5.9%) and anatomical distribution of trigeminal nerve injury specifically after orthognathic surgery, a less-studied context than third-molar extraction or implant placement.
- Head-to-head (though non-randomized, retrospective) comparison of LLLT, corticosteroid, and vitamin B adjuncts within the same cohort.
- Operative time (230 vs 198 min median) flagged as a candidate surrogate marker for neural-traction injury risk.
- Explicit confirmation that no standardized pharmacologic/physical-therapy protocol exists for this indication — the same gap identified in the single-case [[oral-surgery/lee-2015-inferior-alveolar-nerve-injury-management]], now demonstrated at cohort scale.

## Methodology

Complete retrospective census of orthognathic surgery patients (≥18 years) at one tertiary center, 2010–2024. Primary outcome: clinician-documented CN V2/V3 neurosensory deficit, with objective bedside testing (light touch, two-point discrimination) recorded when available. Treatment exposures: perioperative dexamethasone 20 mg, postoperative LLLT (970 nm diode, 2 W continuous mode, 10 s/point, 20 Hz, ~10–20 J/cm² per point, 6–8 sessions over 3–4 weeks), vitamin B complex (B1/B2/B3/B6/B12, oral, once daily × 30 days), or no treatment. Statistics via SPSS 28.0 (Shapiro-Wilk, Kruskal-Wallis, Mann-Whitney, Fisher's exact, Chi-square), with a sensitivity analysis excluding incomplete-follow-up cases.

## Results

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Overall neurosensory deficit incidence | 5.9% (17/287, 95% CI 3.7–9.3%) |
| Mandibular (CN V3) vs maxillary (CN V2) | 88.2% vs 11.8% |
| Preoperative dexamethasone (of affected) | 82.2% (14/17) |
| LLLT → subjective improvement | 75% (6/8) |
| No treatment → subjective improvement | 33% (3/9) |
| No treatment → persistent deficit | 33% (3/9) |
| Median operative time, injured vs cohort | 230 min vs 198 min |
| Mean mandibular movement, injured vs non-injured | 4.3±1.3mm vs 4.5±1.7mm (NS) |

Higher deficit incidence with BSSO or IVRO+LeFort-I combinations than isolated LeFort-I. No patient in any treatment arm achieved full sensory restoration.
