## Three-line Summary
Crossover RCT (n=56, single-tooth closed extractions, Medical University Plovdiv 2022–23) comparing Benex vertical extraction system vs conventional forceps.
Benex preserved intact buccal cortical plate in 95% vs 71.8% with forceps (p<0.05); extraction success equivalent (4.61 vs 4.55); Landry wound healing at day 10 significantly favored Benex (100% vs 78.6% Very Good/Excellent).

## Summary
The Benex extraction system extracts teeth by vertical traction via a screw-post anchored in the root canal, avoiding the lateral leverage forces of conventional forceps. In this crossover RCT at Medical University Plovdiv (Jan 2022–Jan 2023), 56 patients were randomized to Benex (n=28) or conventional forceps (n=28) for any closed-extraction-indicated tooth. The primary finding was a statistically significant 23-percentage-point advantage in intact buccal cortical plate preservation for Benex (95% vs 71.8%). Extraction success scores were equivalent (4.61 vs 4.55). Wound healing by Landry's index at day 10 was 100% Very Good/Excellent for Benex vs 78.6% for forceps. VAS pain differences were not significant. Endodontically treated and multi-rooted teeth carried higher Benex failure risk due to root canal weakening and bulk of the device.

## Key Contributions
- Benex preserves buccal cortical plate in 95% vs 71.8% for forceps — first RCT-level quantification
- Equivalent extraction success rate (4.61 vs 4.55 on 5-point scale)
- Superior early wound healing by day 10 (100% vs 78.6% Very Good/Excellent)
- Endodontically treated teeth and molars are relative Benex contraindications
- Vertical extraction direction eliminates lateral bone compression/expansion forces

## Methodology
- Design: Crossover RCT; block randomization; single clinician (operator-blinded design)
- Population: n=56, mean age 37.2 ± 8.1 yr; any closed-extraction-indicated tooth
- Benex technique: Diamond bur drill along root axis → screw-post insertion → support disk placement → graduated clockwise screw traction (vertical)
- Control: Conventional forceps; multi-rooted teeth sectioned in both groups to standardize difficulty
- Outcomes: Success (Choi/Patel 1–5 scale), buccal cortical plate (probing depth difference: preserved/partial/<4 mm/missing/>4 mm), VAS pain days 0,1,3,7, Landry's Wound Healing Index days 3,7,10
- Analytic: Chi-square, independent samples t-test, repeated-measures ANOVA, Mann-Whitney U

## Results
- Buccal plate preservation: Benex 95% intact vs forceps 71.8% (p<0.05); ~25% forceps had partial fracture (<4 mm)
- Equal complete buccal plate loss (>4 mm) in both groups
- Extraction success: NS (4.61 Benex vs 4.55 forceps)
- VAS pain day 7: NS
- Wound healing day 10: 100% Benex vs 78.6% forceps (significant)
- Literature: Hong et al. — surgical extraction rate 6% (Benex) vs 22% (forceps)
