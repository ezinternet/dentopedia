## One-line Summary
Consensus review (10th EWP 2014, n=literature): oral soft tissue wound healing follows 4 sequential phases (hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, maturation), with surgical wound re-epithelialization completing within 7–14 days at teeth; implant soft tissue maturation requires 6–8 weeks.

## Summary
Consensus statement from the 10th European Workshop on Periodontology systematically reviewed the biological processes of oral soft tissue wound healing at teeth, implants, and the edentulous ridge. Oral mucosal and skin wound healing share the same 4-phase sequence: haemostasis (fibrin clot formation = the initial eschar substrate), inflammation (provisional matrix and cellular recruitment), proliferation (keratinocyte migration, fibroblast activity, angiogenesis), and maturation/remodelling (collagen crosslinking). Epithelial healing of surgical wounds at teeth completes within 7–14 days. Soft tissue healing at implants requires 6–8 weeks for full maturation, yielding scar-like tissue. The soft connective tissue determines the phenotype of the overlying oral epithelium. Human histological evidence shows long junctional epithelial attachment rather than true new attachment after most regenerative procedures.

## Key Contributions
- Canonical 4-phase framework for oral soft tissue healing: haemostasis → inflammation → proliferation → maturation
- Fibrin clot in Phase 1 acts as scaffold for subsequent cellular events (= 가피/eschar precursor)
- 7–14 day epithelial healing at teeth (consensus benchmark for SOP timing)
- 6–8 week implant soft tissue maturation (scar tissue phenotype)
- Soft connective tissue governs epithelial phenotype (basis for connective tissue graft superiority over free gingival graft in certain indications)

## Methodology
Consensus review; Group 1 of the 10th EWP (Zurich, 2014). Literature search on wound healing at teeth, implants, edentulous ridge; adjunct therapies (barrier membranes, growth factors, soft tissue substitutes). Histological outcomes classified as pre-clinical or human.

## Results
**Phase 1 — Haemostasis**: platelet aggregation → fibrin clot. This fibrin matrix is the structural foundation for all subsequent healing (analogous to the visible 가피/eschar on the wound surface).  
**Phase 2 — Inflammation**: neutrophils → macrophages; cytokines; provisional matrix degradation by MMPs.  
**Phase 3 — Proliferation**: keratinocyte migration under fibrin scaffold → re-epithelialization (7–14 days at teeth); fibroblast activation; angiogenesis.  
**Phase 4 — Maturation**: collagen crosslinking, matrix remodelling; ongoing well beyond visible closure.  
Human histological outcome at teeth: consistently long junctional epithelial attachment; no regenerative treatment reliably produces new attachment in humans.  
Implant site: 6–8 weeks for soft tissue maturation; resulting tissue resembles scar.
