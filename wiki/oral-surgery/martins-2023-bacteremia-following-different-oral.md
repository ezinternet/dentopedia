## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 64 non-randomized controlled trials (nRCTs) + 25 RCTs quantifying bacteremia incidence, timing, and duration following dental procedures and activities of daily living (ADL) in healthy individuals; primary outcomes: timing and duration of bacteremia.

Dental extraction had the highest bacteremia incidence (62–66%), followed by scaling and root planing (SRP, 44–36%) and oral hygiene procedures (OHP, 27–28%); peak bacteremia occurred within 5 minutes post-procedure; ADL (toothbrushing 8–26%, flossing 16%) also produced transient bacteremia.

These quantified risk estimates provide the evidence base for antibiotic prophylaxis guidelines for infective endocarditis and prosthetic joint infection.

## Summary

Martins et al. (2023) performed a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis encompassing 64 nRCTs and 25 RCTs to quantify bacteremia rates following invasive dental procedures and routine activities of daily living in healthy individuals. The primary outcomes were the timing and duration of bacteremia, with the secondary outcome being incidence (proportion with bacteremia ≤5 min after procedure vs baseline). Dental extractions showed the highest incidence (62–66%), followed by SRP (44–36%) and oral hygiene procedures (27–28%). Peak bacteremia occurred within 5 minutes of procedure completion and decreased rapidly thereafter. Importantly, routine ADLs including flossing (16%) and toothbrushing (8–26%) also caused bacteremia, underscoring that dental bacteremia is not exclusively iatrogenic — cumulative ADL-related bacteremia may exceed that from occasional dental procedures.

## Key Contributions
- Largest comprehensive quantification of dental bacteremia across procedure types in healthy individuals
- Establishes hierarchy: extraction > SRP > OHP > ADL
- Peak within 5 min supports brief window for antibiotic prophylaxis effectiveness
- ADL-related bacteremia data challenges the rationale for restricting routine dental care in at-risk patients
- Foundational evidence for IE and prosthetic joint infection prophylaxis guidelines

## Methodology
- 8 databases searched
- Healthy individuals only
- Risk of bias: RoB 2.0 (RCTs), ROBINS-I (nRCTs)
- Primary outcome: timing and duration of bacteremia
- Secondary: incidence proportion ≤5 min post-procedure vs baseline
- 64 nRCTs + 25 RCTs included

## Results
| Procedure | Bacteremia Incidence |
|---|---|
| Dental extraction | 62–66% |
| Scaling and root planing (SRP) | 44–36% |
| Oral hygiene procedures (OHP) | 27–28% |
| Flossing (ADL) | ~16% |
| Toothbrushing (ADL) | 8–26% |

- Peak bacteremia: within 5 minutes post-procedure
- Rapid decline thereafter
- Most studies: some concerns (RCTs) or moderate risk of bias (nRCTs)
