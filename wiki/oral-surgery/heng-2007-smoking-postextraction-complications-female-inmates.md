## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (n=219 female inmates, Federal Correctional Institution) comparing dental extraction complications 8 months before vs after a smoke-free policy change (September 2004); outcomes: overall complications and alveolar osteitis incidence across smoking status, tooth type, and surgical trauma.

Overall complication incidence 19.6%; alveolar osteitis 5.0%; smoking significantly associated with overall complications (P=0.02) and multiple-extraction complications (P=0.03); mandibular third molars independently raised alveolar osteitis risk (P=0.02) regardless of smoking status; surgical trauma independently predicted both outcomes.

Smoking is a significant modifiable risk factor for postextraction complications in a general dental extraction population; mandibular third molars and surgical trauma are independent predictors; all-female prison population limits broad generalizability.

## Summary
This retrospective cohort study evaluated the relationship between cigarette smoking and postoperative complications following dental extractions in a female prison population (n=219). The institution's smoke-free policy implemented in September 2004 created a natural experiment comparing 8-month pre-ban and post-ban periods. Overall complication incidence was 19.6% and alveolar osteitis 5.0%. Smoking was significantly associated with overall complications (P=0.02) and contributed to increased complications in multiple extractions (P=0.03). Mandibular third molars independently increased alveolar osteitis risk regardless of smoking status. Surgical trauma independently contributed to both outcomes. The study extends alveolar osteitis risk-factor evidence from impacted third molar literature to general extraction practice.

## Key Contributions
- Smoking increases overall postextraction complication risk (P=0.02) in a general extraction population
- Mandibular third molars carry elevated alveolar osteitis risk independent of smoking (P=0.02)
- Surgical trauma is an independent risk factor (complications P=0.05; dry socket P=0.01)
- Natural experiment via smoke-free policy change enables quasi-causal inference
- Baseline overall complication rate: 19.6%; alveolar osteitis: 5.0% in a general extraction cohort

## Methodology
- Design: retrospective cohort
- n=219 female inmates; smokers 61.1%, nonsmokers 37.9%; mean age 37.7 years (SD 10.4)
- Setting: Federal Correctional Institution, Danbury CT, January 2004–April 2005
- Outcome: overall postextraction complications (pain, swelling, bleeding, revisits) and alveolar osteitis
- Statistics: chi-square; P<0.05 threshold

## Results
- Overall complications: 19.6%; alveolar osteitis: 5.0%
- Smokers vs nonsmokers overall complications: P=0.02 (significant)
- Mandibular third molars vs other extractions for alveolar osteitis: P=0.02 (regardless of smoking)
- Surgical trauma → total complications P=0.05; alveolar osteitis P=0.01
- Multiple extractions in smokers: P=0.03
