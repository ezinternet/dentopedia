## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 7 RCTs examining therapeutic modalities for late (≥1 week post-procedural) IAN/LN paresthesia following dental procedures including implant placement, third molar extraction, orthognathic surgery, and anesthetic injection.

Photobiomodulation (PBM), vitamin B12, and corticosteroids (prednisolone 3–6 weeks post-op) each accelerated sensory recovery; PBM was superior to PBM acupuncture; at 4 weeks, PBM and vitamin B12 showed comparable overall outcomes.

No gold standard treatment exists; all 7 included RCTs had small sample sizes and high methodological risk of bias, precluding meta-analysis and definitive recommendations.

## Summary

Iatrogenic paresthesia of the inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) and lingual nerve (LN) occurs in 0.26–8.4% and 0.1–22% of relevant dental procedures, respectively. Approximately 18.67% of affected patients develop persistent neurosensory disturbance (NSD) annually. This SR addressed a specific gap: treatments for *late* paresthesia (≥1 week post-procedure), deliberately excluding preemptive or adjunctive-during-procedure interventions.

From 3122 database records, 7 RCTs were included. Treatments studied: PBM (4 studies), electroacupuncture (1), vitamin B12/pyrimidine ribonucleotides (1 arm within a 3-arm RCT), prednisolone (1). Microsurgery/nerve grafting did not appear in any qualifying RCT.

**Key findings**:
- **PBM**: 4 of the PBM studies showed significant benefit in light touch, 2-point discrimination, and subjective VAS; one RCT (Miloro 2018, >90-day delay, IAN+LN) found no difference vs sham — suggesting delay-to-treatment and nerve type may moderate PBM efficacy
- **Vitamin B12 + pyrimidine ribonucleotides**: improved neurosensory recovery; no significant overall difference from PBM at 4 weeks; cost-effective alternative with fewer visits
- **Prednisolone**: administration 3–6 weeks post-op accelerated sensation recovery; optimal timing window important; long-term corticosteroid risks (adrenal insufficiency, osteoporosis, GI) must be weighed
- **Electroacupuncture**: minimal benefit — only tactile sensation improved; no 2-point discrimination or EPT benefit
- **Injury classification** (Seddon 1942) matters: neuropraxia (demyelination only) recovers best; neurotmesis (complete severance) has worst prognosis; injury severity should guide treatment selection

## Key Contributions

- First SR restricted to *late* paresthesia, separating preemptive prophylaxis evidence from therapeutic evidence
- Establishes PBM, vit. B12, and corticosteroids as the three evidence-supported modalities (albeit low-quality evidence)
- Highlights optimal corticosteroid timing: 3–6 weeks post-injury, not immediately
- Risk-of-bias assessment (JBI checklist) identifies randomization and allocation concealment as major bias sources across all included studies

## Methodology

- **Design**: SR following Cochrane Handbook + PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD42023447657
- **Search**: PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science (to December 4, 2023); gray literature (Google Scholar, ProQuest)
- **Inclusion**: RCTs; human subjects; iatrogenic paresthesia ≥1 week post-procedure; IAN or LN; controlled design
- **Exclusion**: preemptive/adjunctive treatments; no control group; retrospective/cohort/case reports
- **Outcome measures**: light touch, 2-point discrimination, thermal discrimination, vibratory threshold, VAS, EPT, OHIP-14
- **ROB tool**: JBI RCT checklist; visualized with robvis

## Results

| Treatment | Studies (n) | Main Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| PBM (laser photobiomodulation) | 4 RCTs | Significant benefit in 3/4 studies; non-significant in 1 (>90-day delay) |
| Vitamin B12 + pyrimidine ribonucleotides | 1 arm (Oliveira 2021) | Comparable to PBM at 4 weeks; superior to PBM acupuncture |
| Prednisolone (corticosteroid) | 1 RCT (Seo) | Accelerates recovery when initiated 3–6 weeks post-op |
| Electroacupuncture | 1 RCT (Oliveira 2017) | Only tactile sense improved; no EPT/2-pt discrimination benefit |
| PBM acupuncture | 1 arm (Oliveira 2021) | Inferior to standard PBM and vitamin B12 |

**Persistent NSD annual incidence**: 18.67% (95% CI 14.54–22.79) — substantial burden justifying active treatment
