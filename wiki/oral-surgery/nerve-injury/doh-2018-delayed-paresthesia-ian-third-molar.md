## Three-line Summary

This case report describes a 53-year-old woman who developed delayed paresthesia of the inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) 2 weeks after surgical extraction of the left lower third molar, with symptoms lasting 5 weeks before complete resolution.

Neurosensory testing at onset showed decreased sensation on the affected side (brush direction test 7/10, pin prick 5/10 vs. control 10/10) and two-point discrimination of 15 mm (control 12 mm); postoperative CBCT revealed discontinuity of the upper cortical layer of the inferior alveolar canal.

The patient was diagnosed with neuropraxia of the IAN based on the temporary nature of symptoms and complete recovery within 6–8 weeks, with pathophysiology attributed to hemoglobin release and local inflammatory processes following partial loss of the blood clot, similar to alveolar osteitis.

## Summary

This case report presents a 53-year-old woman who developed delayed paresthesia of the inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) two weeks following surgical extraction of the left lower third molar. The patient presented with numbness of the lower lip, chin, and lower teeth on the left side, accompanied by increased discomfort at the extraction site with partial loss of blood clot but no swelling or redness. Neurosensory testing performed at onset demonstrated decreased sensation on the affected side across all three tests (brush direction test 7/10, pin prick 5/10 vs. control 10/10, two-point discrimination 15 mm vs. control 12 mm). Postoperative cone beam computed tomography revealed discontinuity of the upper cortical layer of the inferior alveolar canal. Treatment with anti-inflammatory drugs and steroids was initiated, with resolution of pain at one week and complete resolution of paresthesia by patient report and subjective testing at six weeks post-onset. The injury was classified as neuropraxia based on Seddon's classification, characterized by temporary demyelination and conduction block with full recovery within 6–8 weeks. The authors discuss potential pathophysiologic mechanisms, proposing that hemoglobin release from clot breakdown and local inflammatory processes, rather than mechanical compression from organized clot or bone fragments, may underlie the delayed presentation in this case.

## Key Contributions

- Documents a rare case of delayed IAN paresthesia with complete resolution at 6 weeks post-onset.
- Provides quantitative neurosensory testing data (brush direction, pin prick, two-point discrimination) at both onset and recovery.
- Identifies partial loss of blood clot (alveolar osteitis-like) without hematoma organization or bone fragments, differentiating from previously proposed mechanisms.
- Proposes hemoglobin release and local inflammation as the pathophysiologic mechanism for delayed paresthesia.
- Demonstrates radiologic evidence of upper cortical layer discontinuity of the inferior alveolar canal on postoperative CBCT.
- Classifies the injury as neuropraxia based on Seddon's classification, confirming expected 6–8 week recovery timeline.

## Methodology

This is a single case report of a 53-year-old female patient presenting with chronic pericoronitis of the left lower third molar, with no significant medical history. The patient underwent surgical extraction under inferior alveolar nerve block anesthesia using 2% lidocaine with 1:100,000 epinephrine. No intraoperative complications were encountered, and the IAN was not visualized during surgery. At two weeks postoperatively, the patient returned with complaints of numbness of the lower lip, chin, and lower teeth on the left side, and increased discomfort at the extraction site. Clinical examination revealed partial loss of blood clot at the extraction site without swelling, redness, or submandibular lymphadenopathy. Neurosensory testing was performed following Poort et al. (2009) recommendations, including brush direction test, pin prick test based on visual analog scale, and two-point discrimination test, using the unaffected right side as control. Postoperative cone beam computed tomography was obtained to evaluate cortical bone integrity. The patient was diagnosed with delayed paresthesia of the IAN and prescribed anti-inflammatory drugs and steroids. Follow-up assessments were conducted at 1 week and 1 month post-diagnosis. The injury was classified using Seddon's (1943) classification of nerve injuries.

Neurosensory testing was performed 14 days after extraction (first day of paresthesia). All tests showed decreased sensation on the affected left side compared to the unaffected right side. The two-point discrimination test measured 15 mm on the affected side versus 12 mm on the control side. Postoperative CBCT revealed partial destruction of the continuity of the upper cortical layer of the inferior alveolar canal. The patient reported complete resolution of paresthesia at 6 weeks post-onset, confirmed by subjective testing.
