## Three-line Summary

This case report describes a 65-year-old male patient with a two-year history of right lower lip paresthesia and occasional drooling following implant placement in the right mandibular first molar region (tooth 46). Clinical and radiological examination with cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) confirmed the implant apex resting on the inferior alveolar nerve canal. Surgical retrieval of the well-osseointegrated implant resulted in complete neurosensory recovery within six weeks, after which the edentulous site was restored with a provisional three-unit fixed partial denture.

## Summary

This case report presents a 65-year-old male patient who developed right lower lip paresthesia, occasional drooling, intermittent pain, and burning sensation following implant placement at tooth 46 two years prior to presentation. Clinical examination revealed gingival inflammation with probing depth of 5 mm around the implant-supported crown, and complete loss of tactile and thermal sensation in the right lower lip and mental region. Orthopantomogram and cone beam computed tomography confirmed the implant apex resting on the inferior alveolar nerve canal. The well-osseointegrated implant was surgically retrieved under local anesthesia after obtaining patient and physician consent. Postoperative neurosensory evaluation at weeks 3, 4, and 6 demonstrated progressive recovery, with complete resolution of all symptoms including paresthesia, burning sensation, pain, and salivary drooling by the sixth week. The edentulous site was subsequently restored with a provisional three-unit fixed partial denture at week 4. The authors conclude that even delayed implant retrieval can lead to complete neurosensory recovery when the implant merely contacts the mandibular canal surface without intraneural impingement, and that peri-implantitis with circumferential bone loss may facilitate uncomplicated implant removal despite prolonged placement duration.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrated complete neurosensory recovery following delayed implant retrieval (two years after placement) in a case of inferior alveolar nerve paresthesia.
- Confirmed the diagnostic value of cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) for evaluating implant-neural canal relationships.
- Identified peri-implantitis with circumferential bone loss as a factor facilitating uncomplicated implant retrieval despite extended placement duration.
- Showed resolution of multiple symptoms including paresthesia, burning sensation, pain, and salivary drooling following surgical intervention.
- Highlighted that surgical exploration remains the optimal method for visualizing inferior alveolar nerve injury when imaging suggests neural involvement.

## Methodology

This is a single case report of a 65-year-old male patient who presented with right lower lip and mental region numbness, occasional drooling from the right mouth corner, intermittent dull throbbing pain, and episodic burning sensation in the right inner cheek, all persisting for two years after implant placement for a missing right mandibular first molar (tooth 46). The patient had a history of myocardial infarction 20 years prior and was on regular medication. Clinical examination revealed an implant-supported prosthetic crown at tooth 46 with edematous gingiva, bleeding on probing, and probing depth of 5 mm. Tooth 45 showed signs of inflammation. Soft tissue sensitivity was evaluated using a periodontal probe and cotton pellet soaked in ethyl chloride, demonstrating complete loss of tactile and thermal sensation in the right lower lip, mental region, and chin skin. Radiological examination using orthopantomogram and cone beam computed tomography confirmed the implant apex resting on the inferior alveolar nerve canal. The patient underwent surgical implant retrieval under local anesthesia after obtaining informed consent and physician clearance. The procedure involved removal of the prosthetic component, elevation of buccal and lingual flaps, implant extraction with forceps, debridement of granulation tissue, osseous surgery for positive bony architecture, and flap approximation with sutures. The edentulous site was restored with a three-unit fixed partial denture at week 4. Neurosensory function was objectively and subjectively assessed using tactile discrimination tests and a 10-point scale preoperatively and at weeks 3, 4, and 6 post-surgery. The patient was monitored for three months to confirm complete recovery.

Complete resolution of neurosensory deficit was achieved by six weeks post-surgery. The patient reported gradual improvement in salivary drooling, which resolved completely by week 6. Burning sensation reduced from a Visual Analogue Scale score of 6/10 preoperatively to 0/10 approximately two weeks after surgery. Intermittent dull pain resolved completely within two days following the surgical procedure.
