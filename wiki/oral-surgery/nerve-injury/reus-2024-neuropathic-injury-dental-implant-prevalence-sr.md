## Three-line Summary

SR of 38 studies (meta-analysis, n up to 503–662) examining neuropathy prevalence and management after mandibular dental implant placement.

Without nerve lateralization: 12% neuropathy at 1 week → 5% at ≥3 months; with lateralization: 90% → 42% — approximately 8× higher burden at both timepoints.

Pharmacological treatment is most used; only 1 RCT addresses management, revealing a critical evidence gap for treating implant-related IAN injury.

## Summary

This SR answers two questions: (1) prevalence of IAN neuropathy after mandibular implant placement, and (2) available treatments. Searches of 6 databases (Cochrane, Embase, LILACS, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science) and grey literature through December 2023 yielded 38 included studies. Meta-analysis used MetaXL 5.3 with weighted average prevalence at two timepoints: ≤1 week and ≥3 months (the ICOP threshold for irreversible neuropathy).

**Non-lateralization group** (13 studies): neuropathy prevalence 12% (95% CI 4–22%) at 1 week, falling to 5% (95% CI 1–11%) at ≥3 months. Most symptoms were transient; numbness and paresthesia were most common.

**Lateralization group** (25 studies): neuropathy prevalence 90% (95% CI 72–100%) at 1 week — 9 studies showed 100% — and 42% (95% CI 24–60%) at ≥3 months. The ~8× amplification of both early and persistent neuropathy reflects the inherent nerve mobilization trauma.

**Management** (5 studies): pharmacological management (analgesics, anti-inflammatories, steroids, membrane stabilizers) was most common. Laser therapy and implant removal were also reported. Only 1 RCT (n not specified per study), samples of 16–64 patients, follow-up 7 days to 7 years. Insufficient evidence for definitive management recommendations.

JBI quality: 18 high, 16 moderate, 4 low of 38 studies.

## Key Contributions

- Separate pooled estimates for non-lateralization vs lateralization — clinically essential distinction absent from prior SRs
- Quantifies that ≥3-month (irreversible) neuropathy affects 5% of non-lateralization and 42% of lateralization patients
- Reveals the management evidence gap: only 1 RCT exists
- Consistent with the prior SR finding of 13% altered sensation at 10 days → 3% at 1 year (non-lateralization trajectory)

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review with meta-analysis
- **PICO (management)**: Adults with IAN neuropathy post-implant / any treatment / placebo or no treatment / neuropathy remission
- **PEO (prevalence)**: Adults / neuropathy after mandibular implant placement / prevalence
- **Meta-analysis**: Weighted average prevalence, 95% CI, MetaXL 5.3; two timeframes analyzed separately
- **Quality**: JBI critical appraisal by study design
- **Search date**: 01 December 2023

## Results

| Group | 1-week prevalence | ≥3-month prevalence |
|---|---|---|
| Non-lateralization | 12% (95% CI 4–22%, n=364) | 5% (95% CI 1–11%, n=662) |
| Lateralization | 90% (95% CI 72–100%, n=503) | 42% (95% CI 24–60%, n=493) |

Management evidence: pharmacological (first-line), low-level laser therapy, implant removal for persistent/severe cases. Single RCT limits conclusions.
