## Three-line Summary

Randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled trial (n=60) comparing laser acupuncture (LA), direct photobiomodulation (PBM) along the IAN trajectory, and sham in adults with post-extraction IAN hypoesthesia lasting 7 days to 12 weeks; all arms received mecobalamin 0.5 mg TID × 30 days.

Both active treatments significantly outperformed sham: two-point discrimination (TPD) improved −3.2 mm (LA) and −2.6 mm (direct PBM) vs −0.8 mm (sham); SWME improved ≥1 grade in 85%, 80%, and 25% respectively; VAS numbness decreased 60%, 48%, and 12%.

Under matched 808 nm dosimetry (9 J/site, every other day × 15 sessions), LA and direct PBM yield comparable neurosensory recovery; LA produces greater patient-reported numbness reduction, possibly reflecting additive central neuromodulation via acupoint targeting.

## Summary

IAN hypoesthesia following mandibular third molar extraction is common and can persist for weeks to months, affecting speech, mastication, and quality of life. Photobiomodulation (PBM) has demonstrated efficacy for peripheral nerve regeneration in prior studies, but the optimal irradiation strategy — direct anatomical delivery vs acupoint-targeted laser acupuncture (LA) — was unknown.

This RCT (n=60 completers, 1:1:1 allocation) directly compares these two strategies against sham under matched PBM dosimetry. All participants received background mecobalamin (0.5 mg TID × 30 days). PBM was delivered with an 808 nm diode laser at 100 mW, 90 s/site (9 J; 9 J/cm²), every other day for 15 sessions.

Both active strategies produced statistically and clinically meaningful improvements over sham in TPD, SWME, and VAS numbness, sustained at 60-day follow-up, with no adverse events.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes both LA and direct PBM as effective adjuncts to mecobalamin for post-extraction IAN hypoesthesia
- Under matched dosimetry, LA and direct PBM are comparably effective on objective neurosensory measures (TPD, SWME)
- LA achieves greater reduction in patient-reported numbness (VAS −60% vs −48%), suggesting central neuromodulatory effects from acupoint targeting beyond local PBM
- Provides specific, clinically replicable PBM parameters: 808 nm, 100 mW, 9 J/site, every other day × 15 sessions
- Day-60 follow-up confirms sustained recovery in both active arms

## Methodology

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind (patient + assessor), sham-controlled, parallel-group RCT

**Population:** Adults 18–60 years with unilateral post-extraction IAN hypoesthesia lasting ≥7 days and ≤12 weeks

**Sample size:** 60 completers (20 per arm)

**Laser parameters:** 808 nm diode, continuous wave, 100 mW, beam area 1.0 cm², 90 s/site = 9 J/site (9 J/cm²), every other day, 15 sessions total

**LA acupoints:** ST4, ST6, ST7, CV24, Jiachengjiang (EX-HN19) [local face/mandible points] + LI4, PC6 [distal systemic points]; 5+2 fixed prescription per STRICTA/WHO standards

**Direct PBM:** Irradiation along the external projection of the IAN anatomical course

**Background treatment (all arms):** Mecobalamin 0.5 mg TID × 30 days

**Reporting:** CONSORT 2010 + STRICTA (acupuncture extension)

**Primary outcomes:** TPD (two-point discrimination) and SWME (Semmes–Weinstein monofilament) at day 30

**Secondary outcomes:** VAS numbness, additional sensory tests, day-60 follow-up

## Results

| Outcome | LA | Direct PBM | Sham |
|---|---|---|---|
| TPD change at day 30 (mm) | −3.2 ± 1.8 | −2.6 ± 1.7 | −0.8 ± 1.1 |
| SWME ≥1 grade improvement | 85% | 80% | 25% |
| VAS numbness reduction | 60% | 48% | 12% |
| Day-60 maintenance | Yes | Yes | Minimal |
| Adverse events | None | None | None |

- Group × time interaction: p < 0.01 for both TPD and SWME
- LA vs direct PBM difference in objective measures: non-significant (comparable efficacy)
- LA superior to direct PBM on VAS numbness (patient-reported) — clinically meaningful difference
