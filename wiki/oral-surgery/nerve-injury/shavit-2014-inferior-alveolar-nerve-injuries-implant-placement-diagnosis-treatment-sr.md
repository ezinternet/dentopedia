## Three-line Summary

This systematic review analyzed 6 studies (n=157 patients) to assess diagnostic timing and treatment outcomes for inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) injuries following dental implant placement, searching MEDLINE (PubMed) from December 2004 to March 2014.

Subjective neurosensory testing was the most commonly used diagnostic method (64% of cases); early diagnosis within 36 hours was achieved in 47% of cases, while 43% were diagnosed very late (>6 months), with full recovery observed in cases treated before 48 hours post-injury.

Early diagnosis and treatment (implant removal within 36 hours, corticosteroid therapy, canal decompression) are critical for favorable outcomes; however, severe injuries (neurotmesis) may not recover regardless of timing, and the review is limited by missing exact diagnosis times and small case numbers.

## Summary

This systematic review, registered with PROSPERO (CRD42014014724), examined diagnostic procedures, risk factors, and treatment outcomes associated with inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) injury following dental implant placement. The review included 6 studies (n=157 patients) published between December 2004 and March 2014, identified through MEDLINE (PubMed) database searches. The studies encompassed diagnostic methods, treatment outcomes, clinical presentations, and risk factor management. Subjective neurosensory testing (light touch, sharp/blunt discrimination, two-point discrimination) was the predominant diagnostic approach (64% of cases), while objective methods such as masseter inhibitory reflex (MIR) were not utilized in any of the reviewed studies despite reported high specificity (99%). Data analysis revealed that 47% of cases were diagnosed within the critical 36-hour window, whereas 43% were diagnosed very late (>6 months). Treatment outcomes demonstrated that cases managed before 48 hours post-injury consistently achieved full recovery, except in severe injury cases (neurotmesis). The authors concluded that early diagnosis and intervention are essential for favorable outcomes, with implant removal within 36 hours recommended when radiographic evidence of mandibular canal contact exists. However, the review acknowledges limitations including limited sample size, missing exact diagnosis times in several cases, and referral bias in studies where authors did not perform the initial surgery. The findings support the importance of establishing diagnosis within the first 36 hours and implementing appropriate treatment (corticosteroids, implant removal, canal decompression) to maximize recovery potential.

## Key Contributions

- Systematic review of 6 studies (157 patients) on IAN injury after implant placement, identifying early diagnosis (<36 hours) in 47% of cases
- Subjective neurosensory testing was the dominant diagnostic method (64%); objective tests (MIR, SEPs) were not used in any reviewed study
- Full recovery observed with treatment before 48 hours post-injury; late diagnosis (>6 months) showed no recovery in all reported cases
- Phentolamine mesylate proposed as novel approach to enable immediate postoperative sensory assessment
- Early implant removal (<36 hours) recommended if radiographic evidence of mandibular canal contact is present
- Risk factor classification: general (anatomical, sex, age), intraoperative (canal perforation, mechanical injury), postoperative (compression, ischemia, timing)

## Methodology

This systematic review was registered in PROSPERO (CRD42014014724) and conducted according to PRISMA guidelines. The authors performed an electronic search of MEDLINE (PubMed) using keywords including "INFERIOR ALVEOLAR NERVE INJURY ASSOCIATED WITH IMPLANT SURGERY", "INFERIOR ALVEOLAR NERVE INJURY IMPLANT", and "INFERIOR ALVEOLAR NERVE INJURY DIAGNOSIS" covering December 2004 to March 2014. The search was restricted to English-language publications on humans. Of 33 initially identified articles, 27 were excluded due to incompatibility with inclusion criteria (literature reviews, animal studies, no diagnostic method mentioned, non-implant-related injury, no IAN injury, or no diagnostic procedures described). Six studies were included: one on diagnostic methods, two on treatment outcomes, two on clinical presentation, and one on risk factors and management guidelines. The included studies comprised observational studies and case series with 157 total patients. Risk of bias was assessed using the Cochrane Collaboration's tool, with identified biases including operation not performed by authors, missing exact diagnosis time, single examiner for neurosensory examination, low patient numbers, and sex scission. Subjective neurosensory tests (light touch, sharp/blunt discrimination, two-point discrimination, moving point discrimination, pin prick, brush stroke direction, pain threshold) were the primary diagnostic evidence across all reviewed studies. Descriptive statistics were used for data synthesis; some studies reported using SPSS or Excel for analysis.

**Khawaja et al. 2009 (4 cases):**
- 17.5 hours + medicament + implant removal → markedly improved sensation
- 24 hours + implant removal → markedly improved sensation
- 48 hours + implant removal → no improvement
- 96 hours + implant removal → no improvement

- Mild injury (38 patients): 48-hour treatment, full recovery within 1-4 weeks
