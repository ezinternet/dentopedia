## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (emergency medicine, 2021) of background, pathophysiology, diagnosis and management of cellulitis and abscess for emergency clinicians.

Cellulitis is mostly Staphylococcus aureus and β-hemolytic streptococci, abscess mostly MRSA; history and exam often separate the two, with POCUS for unclear cases; cellulitis gets a penicillin or cephalosporin, abscess gets incision and drainage (loop drainage preferred over traditional I&D), adjunctive antibiotics optional, most patients outpatient.

Non-dental skin/soft-tissue review from an abstract only; organism and drug statements come from the abstract and do not replace odontogenic-infection data.

## Summary

Emergency clinicians see cellulitis and abscess constantly, and the two call for different treatment: antibiotics for cellulitis, drainage for abscess. The review summarizes how to tell them apart and how to treat each.

## Key Contributions

- Concise statement of the cellulitis-vs-abscess treatment split in the emergency setting.
- Loop drainage preferred over traditional incision and drainage for abscess.

## Methodology

- Narrative review; method not described in the abstract.

## Results

- Cellulitis organisms: S. aureus, S. pyogenes, other β-hemolytic streptococci. Abscess organism: MRSA most common.
- History and exam help in many cases; POCUS for unclear ones. Cellulitis: penicillin or cephalosporin. Abscess: I&D (loop drainage preferred), adjunctive antibiotics can be considered. Most are outpatient.

## Limitations (reader's appraisal)

- Abstract only; skin and soft-tissue infection in general. Odontogenic infections are mixed oral flora with a dental source to remove; the abstract does not address them, so the antibiotic choices here are not a dental recommendation.
