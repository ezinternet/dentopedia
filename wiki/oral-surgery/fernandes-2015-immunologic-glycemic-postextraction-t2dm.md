## Three-line Summary

Prospective case-control study (n=53 T2DM + 29 controls, University of São Paulo) assessing association between glycemic control (HbA1c), immunologic profile (neutrophil function), and postextraction complications after extraction of one erupted tooth; assessments at 3, 7, 21, and 60 days post-surgery.

Despite impaired neutrophil function and variable glycemic control in T2DM patients, no increase in postoperative complications was observed; no association between delayed wound epithelialization at day 21 and HbA1c level; all sockets in both groups fully epithelialized at day 60 with no infection.

Type 2 diabetes per se and glycemic control level are not independent risk factors for postextraction complications in erupted tooth extraction — antibiotic prophylaxis should not be prescribed solely based on diabetic status or HbA1c, countering common practice.

## Summary

This prospective case-control study from the University of São Paulo School of Dentistry enrolled 53 patients with type 2 diabetes and 29 controls without diabetes, all undergoing extraction of one erupted tooth. Exclusion criteria included active smokers and periodontal pockets >4 mm. Glycemic control (HbA1c) and immunologic profile (neutrophil function) were measured at extraction time. Patients were evaluated at 3, 7, 21, and 60 days for healing pattern and postextraction complications.

**Key findings:**
- No significant difference in postoperative complication rates between T2DM and control groups
- No association between HbA1c level and delayed epithelialization at day 21
- No association between neutrophil dysfunction and complication rate
- At day 60: complete epithelialization in all sockets of both groups; no infection in either group

This challenges the common assumption that poor glycemic control automatically translates to increased infection risk after simple tooth extraction, and suggests that the physiological reserve in T2DM patients undergoing routine erupted tooth extraction is sufficient to prevent clinically significant healing problems.

## Key Contributions

- First prospective case-control from Brazil measuring immunologic parameters + glycemic control simultaneously against postextraction outcomes
- Directly counters the extrapolation from medical literature suggesting diabetic patients need antibiotic prophylaxis for tooth extractions
- Day 21 outcome: the timepoint thought most vulnerable for impaired healing showed no association with HbA1c
- Practical clinical guideline: Fernandes et al. conclude that T2DM patients undergoing simple erupted tooth extraction (no acute infection) should not receive prophylactic antibiotics based solely on their diabetic status or glycemic control level

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective case-control
- **n**: 53 T2DM + 29 controls; single center (University of São Paulo)
- **Procedure**: Extraction of one erupted tooth per participant; no teeth with acute infection or >4mm pockets
- **Exclusion**: Smokers; periodontal pockets >4mm
- **Assessment**: Signs and symptoms at days 3, 7, 21, 60 post-extraction
- **Measurements**: HbA1c, neutrophil function at extraction time; healing pattern and complication incidence
- **Outcome**: Healing pattern comparison; incidence of postextraction complications

## Results

- Postoperative complications: no significant difference T2DM vs control
- Delayed epithelialization at day 21: no association with HbA1c
- Neutrophil dysfunction: no association with complication incidence
- Day 60: complete epithelialization in both groups; no infection
