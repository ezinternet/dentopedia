## Three-line Summary

Prospective pilot case-control study (University of São Paulo; 48 CKF/hemodialysis patients [study group, SG] + 29 controls [CG]; 87 SG teeth and 76 CG teeth extracted) evaluating postextraction complications without antibiotic prophylaxis, with assessments at 3, 7, 21, and 60 days.

No postoperative infectious complications in either group; prolonged bleeding >30 minutes in 18.5% of SG interventions (12 of 65), controlled with local hemostatic agents; delayed epithelialization at day 21 in 29.9% (26/87) of SG vs 3.9% (3/37) of CG (p<0.001); all sockets fully epithelialized at day 60 in both groups with radiographic bone neoformation.

Chronic kidney failure on hemodialysis increases bleeding risk and delays early mucosal healing but does not cause postoperative infection — antibiotic prophylaxis is not evidently necessary for simple extraction in CKF/HD patients.

## 세줄요약

양군 모두 술후 감염 없음; SG 시술의 18.5% (65건 중 12건)에서 출혈 >30분 (국소 지혈 처치로 조절); 21일째 지연 상피화 SG 29.9% (87치 중 26) vs CG 3.9% (37치 중 3) (p<0.001); 60일째 양군 전례 상피화 완료, 방사선상 신생골 확인.

## Summary

This prospective pilot case-control study from the University of São Paulo School of Dentistry enrolled 48 patients with chronic kidney failure on hemodialysis (study group, SG) and 29 patients without CKF (control group, CG). All underwent simple tooth extractions. No prophylactic antibiotics were administered to any participant — this design explicitly tested whether antibiotic prophylaxis is necessary in CKF/HD patients.

SG: 65 interventions (87 teeth extracted); CG: 36 interventions (76 teeth extracted). Patients were evaluated at days 3, 7, 21, and 60 for hemostasis, infection signs, epithelialization, and radiographic bone formation.

**Key findings:**

| Parameter | CKF/HD (SG) | Control (CG) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interventions / teeth | 65 / 87 | 36 / 76 | — |
| Bleeding >30 min | 18.5% (12/65) | 0% | — |
| Postop infection (local/distant) | 0% | 0% | NS |
| Delayed epithelialization (day 21) | 29.9% (26/87) | 3.9% (3/37) | <0.001 |
| Day 60 outcome | 100% healed | 100% healed | — |

Bleeding was managed with local hemostatic agents in all 12 cases — no systemic intervention required. The delayed mucosal closure in SG at day 21 likely reflects uremia-related impaired cellular proliferation and platelet dysfunction; however, by day 60, the healing deficit was completely resolved in all sockets with radiographic alveolar bone neoformation confirmed in both groups.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that CKF/HD patients do not show increased postoperative infection rate after simple extraction even without antibiotic prophylaxis
- Quantifies bleeding risk: 18.5% of interventions in CKF/HD require hemostatic intervention — clinical preparation for local hemostasis is more important than antibiotic cover
- Day 21 delayed epithelialization (29.9% vs 3.9%) is the critical monitoring point for CKF/HD patients; eventual outcome at day 60 is equivalent
- Challenges routine prescribing of prophylactic antibiotics for CKF/HD patients undergoing simple extractions

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective pilot case-control
- **n**: 48 CKF/HD + 29 controls; single center (University of São Paulo)
- **Procedure**: Simple tooth extractions; no antibiotic prophylaxis
- **Assessment**: Days 3, 7, 21, 60 post-extraction by a calibrated single dentist
- **Outcomes**: Hemostasis time, local/distant infection signs, socket epithelialization, radiographic bone deposition
- **Bleeding management**: Local hemostatic agents when bleeding exceeded 30 minutes

## Results

- Prolonged bleeding >30 min: 18.5% CKF/HD vs 0% control
- Postoperative infection: 0% in both groups
- Delayed epithelialization at day 21: 29.9% CKF/HD vs 3.9% control (p<0.001)
- Day 60: 100% epithelialization; bone neoformation in both groups
