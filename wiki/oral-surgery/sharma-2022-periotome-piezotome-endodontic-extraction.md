## Summary
This prospective double-blind RCT enrolled 100 patients requiring extraction of nonrestorable endodontically treated single-rooted teeth, randomized to periotome (n=50) or piezotome (n=50). Primary outcomes were operative time, postoperative pain (VAS 0–7 days), immediate marginal bone loss (Hu-Friedy probe), and 6-month bone loss (CBCT). Periotome was significantly faster (5.78 vs 12.8 min) and achieved better pain control by day 7. Piezotome produced markedly less immediate marginal bone loss (0.083 vs 0.54 mm, p=0.007), likely because it severs PDL without mechanical leverage against alveolar bone. However, at 6 months, both groups showed comparable bone resorption in height and width, suggesting that the immediate mechanical advantage of piezotome does not translate to long-term ridge preservation superiority. Authors recommend periotome as first-line for routine cases (faster, cheaper) and piezotome near neurovascular structures.

## Key Contributions
- First RCT comparing periotome vs piezotome specifically for endodontically failed teeth
- Piezotome: immediate marginal bone loss 6.5× less than periotome (0.083 vs 0.543 mm)
- 6-month CBCT bone loss: not significantly different — mechanical advantage does not persist
- Operative time: piezotome requires 2.2× longer (12.8 vs 5.78 min)
- Both techniques: no significant dry socket, buccal plate fracture, or major complications

## Methodology
- Design: Prospective, double-blind RCT; computer-generated randomization; single observer blinded to technique for assessments
- Population: n=100 (96 analyzed — 4 lost to follow-up), 58F/42M, age 20–55 yr (mean 32.47); all single-rooted teeth with failed root canal therapy
- Periotome technique: Hu-Friedy periotome at 20° to tooth axis, sever cervical fibers then PDL to 2/3 root depth all 4 surfaces; complete with forceps
- Piezotome technique: SATLEC ACTEON piezotome, LC 2 tips, ultrasonic blade parallel to tooth axis, 2–4 mm apical increments all 4 surfaces; complete with forceps
- Outcomes: Operation duration, VAS pain (0–7 days), marginal bone loss (probing, immediate), CBCT bone loss (6 months), gingival laceration, complications
- Statistics: SPSS 22.0, independent samples t-test, p<0.05 threshold

## Results
- Operation time: Piezotome 12.8 ± 7.4 min vs periotome 5.78 ± 4.0 min (p<0.01)
- Pain reduction day 7 intergroup: periotome significantly better (p=0.002)
- Immediate marginal bone loss: piezotome 0.083 ± 0.24 mm vs periotome 0.543 ± 0.25 mm (p=0.007)
- 6-month bone loss — vertical: piezotome 4.21 ± 0.26 mm vs periotome 3.71 ± 1.24 mm (p=NS)
- 6-month bone loss — buccolingual: piezotome 2.85 ± 1.28 mm vs periotome 2.67 ± 2.24 mm (p=NS)
- Gingival lacerations: Grade 1 in 2 periotome + 1 piezotome (NS)
- No dry socket or buccal plate fracture in either group
