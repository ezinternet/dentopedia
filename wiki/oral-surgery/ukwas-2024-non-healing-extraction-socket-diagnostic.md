## Three-line Summary

Narrative review / case-series synthesis of 50 non-healing extraction socket cases (28F, 22M; mean age 55.2 years; 71 teeth) from five major databases.

Malignancy (primary and metastatic) was the most frequent definitive diagnosis, followed by medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ).

Clinicians must maintain a low biopsy threshold for persistently non-healing sockets, as the socket may be the first presentation of an undiagnosed serious systemic disease or malignancy.

## Summary

Non-healing extraction sockets — sockets that fail to show proper healing signs over weeks to months — represent a rare but diagnostically significant clinical challenge. This narrative review synthesized 50 reported cases to characterize the underlying etiologies. Unlike alveolar 골염 (Alveolar Osteitis, AO) / dry socket, which is painful and self-limiting, true non-healing sockets may be painless and harbor serious pathology requiring urgent investigation.

The review found that malignancy (both primary oral cancers and metastatic lesions) accounts for the largest diagnostic category, followed by 약물 관련 턱뼈 괴사증 (Medication-Related Osteonecrosis of the Jaw, MRONJ). The mean patient age of 55.2 years aligns with peak periods for both malignancy and polypharmacy (antiresorptives, antiangiogenics), making the index of suspicion particularly important in middle-aged to older adults. In a country performing over 18 million extractions annually, even a rare condition at this scale represents meaningful absolute numbers.

## Key Contributions

- First focused case-series synthesis mapping specific systemic diagnoses behind non-healing extraction sockets
- Diagnostic hierarchy: malignancy (primary + metastatic) > 약물 관련 턱뼈 괴사증 (MRONJ) > other serious systemic disorders
- Establishes non-healing socket as a potential herald sign of undiagnosed systemic disease or malignancy
- Provides a clinical decision framework applicable to general dental practice
- Reinforces that patient age ≥55 years warrants heightened vigilance given overlap with malignancy incidence

## Methodology

- **Study type**: Narrative review / case-series review
- **Databases**: PubMed, Embase, Scopus, ProQuest, Google Scholar
- **Search term**: "non-healing extraction socket"
- **Filters**: humans, English language
- **Sample**: 50 cases, 71 extracted teeth; 28 women, 22 men; mean age 55.2 years
- **Limitation**: case-series synthesis without control group; incidence and prevalence cannot be derived; publication bias likely inflates serious diagnoses

## Results

- **Most common definitive diagnosis**: Malignancy (primary oral and metastatic)
- **Second most common**: 약물 관련 턱뼈 괴사증 (Medication-Related Osteonecrosis of the Jaw, MRONJ)
- Sockets may remain non-healing for weeks to months with no obvious initiating cause at follow-up
- Both leading diagnoses require escalation (oncology referral / medication review + specialist referral) rather than local socket treatment
- Clinical conclusion: persistent non-healing should trigger biopsy and systemic investigation, not repeated local interventions
