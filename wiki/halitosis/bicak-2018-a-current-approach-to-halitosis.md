## Three-line Summary

Narrative mini-review of 54 articles providing a structured overview of halitosis classification (genuine vs delusional), aetiology centred on volatile sulfur compounds (VSC, Volatile Sulfur Compound), and a catalogue of nine detection techniques from organoleptic scoring to PCR. Oral origin accounts for 85% of cases, with tongue biofilm and periodontal disease as primary drivers; extraoral causes span respiratory, gastrointestinal, metabolic, and drug-related origins. Conventional management (oral hygiene instruction, tongue cleaning, chlorhexidine rinse, scaling and root planing) remains more effective and widely adopted than novel agents such as Melaleuca oil, champignon extract, or probiotics.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/1874210601812010322)), Bicak (2018) conducted a narrative mini-review in *The Open Dentistry Journal* (12:322–330) synthesising 54 full-text articles on halitosis aetiology, detection, and treatment. The review serves as a structured reference for dental practitioners as first-line managers of oral malodor.

**Classification framework** — Halitosis divides into two top-level categories:

1. **Delusional halitosis**
   - *Pseudohalitosis*: patient complaint without objective detection; managed with oral hygiene instruction and reassurance.
   - *Halitophobia*: persistent belief of malodor after treatment; requires referral to clinical psychologist or psychiatrist.

2. **Genuine halitosis**
   - *Physiologic*: transient morning breath from nocturnal hyposalivation; resolves with oral hygiene.
   - *Pathologic–oral* (85% of cases): tongue biofilm, periodontal diseases (gingivitis, periodontitis, periimplantitis), dental abscess, candidiasis, hyposalivation, orthodontic appliances.
   - *Pathologic–extraoral*: respiratory (sinusitis, tonsillitis, bronchiectasis), gastrointestinal (GERD, H. pylori), metabolic (diabetes, renal/hepatic failure, trimethylaminuria), drug-induced.

**Aetiology — VSC production pathway**

The principal malodorous gases are hydrogen sulfide (H₂S), methyl mercaptan (CH₃SH), and dimethyl sulfide ((CH₃)₂S). Non-VSC contributors include indole, skatole, cadaverine, putrescine, and volatile fatty acids. Gram-negative anaerobes are the chief VSC producers; reduced salivary flow increases bacterial counts and VSC output. The tongue dorsum — with its irregular papillary–fissure morphology — provides an anaerobic niche for biofilm accumulation and is the primary malodor source even in periodontally healthy individuals.

**Detection techniques**

| Technique | Category | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Organoleptic measurement | Direct | 0–5 scale via tube; widely used; subjective |
| Gas chromatography | Direct | Quantifies individual VSCs; OralChroma portable version available; expensive |
| Halimeter (portable sulfide monitor) | Direct | Electrochemical; easy field use; total VSC only |
| BANA test | Indirect | Detects T. denticola, P. gingivalis, T. forsythia; 5–10 min |
| Chemical sensor / electronic nose | Indirect | 6-sensor array; developmental stage |
| Beta-galactosidase paper disc | Indirect | Colorimetric; first deglycosylation step proxy |
| Salivary incubation test | Indirect | Less affected by exogenous odors than organoleptic |
| Ammonia monitoring | Indirect | Urea-rinse then ammonia gas detection |
| Ninhydrin colorimetry | Indirect | Detects polyamines not captured by sulfide monitor |
| PCR | Indirect | Quantitative microbiome; most specific |

**Treatment recommendations**

- *Physiologic / pseudohalitosis*: oral hygiene instruction, tongue brushing/scraping, interdental flossing.
- *Pathologic oral*: chlorhexidine or stannous fluoride mouthwash, toothpaste with stannous, professional prophylaxis, SRP for periodontal cases.
- *Novel agents*: Melaleuca alternifolia (tea tree) oil, champignon extract, probiotics, green tea (Camellia sinensis), homeopathy, aromatherapy — reviewed positively in limited studies but judged less effective overall.
- *Extraoral*: referral to appropriate medical specialist.

Adjunct SRP + glycine powder air-polishing (GPAP) showed no additional VSC reduction benefit over SRP alone (Caygur et al., n=60, 30-day follow-up).

## Key Contributions

- Unified classification table covering delusional and genuine halitosis subtypes with distinct management pathways for each.
- Comprehensive detection technique catalogue (9 methods) with practical notes — broadens clinical toolkit beyond the Halimeter most reviews focus on.
- Summary of emerging/alternative treatments (Melaleuca oil, champignon, probiotics, green tea) with cautious interpretation.
- Clear delineation of dentist's role as primary referral gatekeeper for extraoral causes.

## Methodology

Narrative literature review; expanded database search (PubMed/Medline, Scopus, Google Scholar, TÜBİTAK ULAKBİM); hand-search of references. Included: peer-reviewed full texts in Turkish or English. Excluded: abstracts, editorials, non-dental, duplicates, other languages. Final corpus: 54/978 full-text articles. No PRISMA, no quality appraisal tool, no meta-analysis.

## Results

Prevalence up to 50% (methodology-dependent). Oral origin confirmed in 85% of malodor cases. Tongue biofilm identified as the primary site in periodontally healthy patients. VSC-accelerated periodontal tissue destruction creates a bidirectional relationship between periodontal disease and halitosis. H. pylori evidence mixed — oral colonisation may elevate VSC-producing periodontopathogen load; eradication may reduce halitosis in some patients. Conventional management outperforms novel agents in both effectiveness and clinical uptake. Dentists are positioned as first-line professionals for diagnosis, oral treatment, and medical referral when needed.
