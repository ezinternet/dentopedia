## Three-line Summary

This 2014 periodontics-focused narrative review covers the etiology, assessment methods, systemic disease associations, and management strategies for halitosis, emphasizing that local (oral) factors dominate but extra-oral and systemic contributions must be considered in a multidisciplinary framework.

Assessment modalities reviewed include organoleptic measurements, sulfide monitoring (Halimeter), gas chromatography, microbial testing, and chemical test strips — the most comprehensive diagnostic toolkit discussion in the halitosis literature to that date.

Management is categorized as masking, source reduction (lowering VOC/VSC levels), and mechanical/chemical treatment, with multidisciplinary collaboration highlighted as essential for cases with systemic involvement.

## Summary

Mokeem reviews halitosis from a periodontics and community dentistry perspective, defining it as an unpleasant odor emanating from oral or non-oral sources. Local intra-oral factors play the dominant role in the majority of cases, but extra-oral contributions — including ENT conditions, gastrointestinal disease, respiratory disease, and systemic metabolic conditions — require differential diagnosis.

A notable feature of this review is its systematic treatment of **assessment tools**: organoleptic scoring (trained evaluator scoring exhaled air), sulfide monitoring (portable electrochemical sensors measuring H₂S and CH₃SH), gas chromatography (GC; laboratory standard for individual VSC quantification), microbial testing, and chemical test strips. This provides a practical reference for clinicians selecting measurement approaches appropriate to their setting.

Management is organized into three categories: (1) masking — temporary reduction of perceived malodor; (2) source reduction — mechanically and chemically lowering VOC/VSC production at the microbial level; and (3) multidisciplinary treatment of underlying systemic conditions when indicated.

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnostic toolkit overview**: Most comprehensive single-article comparison of halitosis assessment methods available at that date.
- **Etiology taxonomy**: Clear intra-oral vs extra-oral categorization with specific systemic disease examples (ENT, GI, respiratory, metabolic).
- **Management framework**: Three-tier treatment taxonomy (masking, source reduction, systemic treatment) applicable in clinical practice.
- **Multidisciplinary model**: Explicit call for team-based care integrating dentistry, internal medicine, and relevant subspecialties.

## Methodology

Narrative review by a single periodontics author. Covers etiology, differential diagnosis, assessment, and management. Published in a clinical dental practice journal targeting practicing clinicians. Abstract-only; full search strategy and inclusion criteria not available.

## Results

- Halitosis is multifactorial; intra-oral (local) factors are the most common cause.
- Extra-oral factors (ENT, gastrointestinal, respiratory, systemic/metabolic disease) contribute in a significant minority.
- Assessment methods span bedside organoleptic scoring to laboratory gas chromatography.
- VOC/VSC reduction through mechanical debridement and chemical agents is the primary treatment target.
- A multidisciplinary approach is essential for cases involving systemic disease.
