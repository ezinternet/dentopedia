## Three-line Summary

This 2026 narrative review frames the oral cavity as a bioreactor in which microbial consortia, enzymatic kinetics, and mass-transport processes within biofilms govern the production and flux of volatile sulfur compounds (VSC) and volatile organic compounds (VOC) responsible for halitosis.

The review draws on systems engineering and chemical engineering concepts — including structured biofilm heterogeneity, substrate degradation kinetics, and biofilm-bulk fluid interfacial transport — to explain intra-oral malodor arising from tongue dorsum, periodontal pockets, and dental plaque.

Management strategies covered include mechanical debridement, antimicrobial agents, and probiotics, all interpreted through the bioreactor lens.

## Summary

Halitosis is a prevalent condition predominantly arising from intra-oral sources, including periodontal disease, tongue coating, and xerostomia. The pathogenesis centers on microbial degradation of sulfur-containing substrates by proteolytic anaerobes inhabiting the tongue dorsum, periodontal pockets, and dental plaque — predominantly organized as structured biofilms.

Marinis et al. propose a systems-based analytical framework that conceptualizes the oral cavity as a dynamic bioreactor. Within this model, the oral environment is governed by microbial consortia and their metabolic pathways, mass transport phenomena, enzymatic kinetics, and microenvironmental factors (pH, oxygen tension, substrate availability). Modeling approaches are advocated to elucidate intricate biofilm dynamics, including heterogeneous structural organization and fundamental transport/metabolic processes occurring at the biofilm-bulk fluid interface and within the biofilm matrix — these interfacial processes regulate the flux of volatile compounds into oral air.

The review also explores associations between halitosis and dental pathologies — gingivitis, periodontitis, and periodontal abscesses — through the lens of microbial dysbiosis, connecting systemic inflammatory burden to volatile compound production.

## Key Contributions

- **Bioreactor framework**: First explicit application of chemical engineering bioreactor concepts to oral halitosis pathogenesis.
- **Biofilm interface modeling**: Detailed treatment of transport and metabolic processes at the biofilm-bulk fluid boundary as the proximal regulator of VSC flux.
- **Dysbiosis-halitosis link**: Mechanistic connection between periodontal dysbiosis and halitosis via altered microbial consortia and metabolic output.
- **Integrated management overview**: Mechanical debridement, antimicrobial agents, and probiotics reviewed within the bioreactor model.

## Methodology

Narrative review; multidisciplinary synthesis integrating oral microbiology, chemical engineering, and clinical dentistry. Systems-based analytical approach applied to existing halitosis literature. Abstract-only; full search strategy and inclusion criteria not available.

## Results

- Intra-oral sources (tongue dorsum, periodontal pockets, dental plaque biofilms) are the dominant origin of halitosis.
- Proteolytic anaerobes degrade sulfur-containing substrates; VSC and VOC are the odorant products.
- Biofilm heterogeneity and interfacial transport processes are identified as key determinants of volatile compound flux into oral air.
- Periodontal dysbiosis (gingivitis, periodontitis, periodontal abscess) amplifies malodor production.
- Contemporary management includes mechanical debridement, antimicrobial agents, and probiotics.
