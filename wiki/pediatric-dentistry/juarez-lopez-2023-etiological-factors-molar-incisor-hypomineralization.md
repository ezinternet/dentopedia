## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 40 studies (25 for meta-analysis; 44,037 participants; 2004–2022); random-effects model due to high heterogeneity (I²=54–94%); PROSPERO-registered (CRD42022348603).
Maternal illness during last trimester (OR 4.03, 95% CI 1.33–12.16), low birth weight (OR 1.23, 95% CI 1.10–1.38), childhood illness (OR 4.06, 95% CI 2.03–8.11), antibiotic use (OR 1.76, 95% CI 1.31–2.37), and high fever (OR 1.48, 95% CI 1.18–1.84) are significant MIH risk factors; prematurity, cesarean delivery, and respiratory disorders are not.
MIH is multifactorial — likely requiring ≥2 simultaneous risk factors with genetic susceptibility; early dental surveillance for at-risk children is the key preventive implication.

## Summary

This PROSPERO-registered SR+MA searched six international databases (2004–2022) to identify etiological factors for molar incisor hypomineralization (MIH). Forty studies qualified for qualitative synthesis (28 cross-sectional, 5 cohort, 7 case-control; n=44,037), and 25 contributed quantitative data. Applying the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, 75% of studies were rated high quality. Using random-effects models in RevMan v5.4 (due to I²=54–94%), three postnatal factors — childhood general illness, antibiotic use, high fever — and two early-life factors — maternal illness during the last trimester and low birth weight — showed statistically significant associations with MIH. Importantly, prematurity, cesarean delivery, and respiratory disorders were not significant after pooling. The authors conclude that MIH is multifactorial: a combination of ≥2 risk factors plus genetic susceptibility is likely required for its expression.

## Key Contributions

- First quantified pooling of pre/peri/postnatal MIH risk factors with 25-study meta-analysis
- OR hierarchy: childhood illness (4.06) > maternal illness (4.03) > antibiotic use (1.76) > high fever (1.48) > low birth weight (1.23)
- Definitively refutes prematurity and cesarean delivery as significant independent risk factors for MIH
- Proposes that ≥2 risk factors must co-occur (umbrella-study-supported finding)
- Identifies amoxicillin and high fever as potentially disrupting amelogenin gene expression

### Prevalence Context
MIH prevalence in included studies ranged 2.5–54% (global estimate ~13.5%; moderate-to-severe ~36.3% of MIH cases). Wide variation reflects geographic differences and diagnostic criteria heterogeneity.
