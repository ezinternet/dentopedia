## One-line Summary

Examiner-blind split-mouth RCT (30 patients, 60 NCCL restorations, 18-month follow-up) found no significant difference between rubber dam and cotton roll/retraction cord isolation for retention rate (73% vs 73% at 18 months), gingival damage, patient preference, or chairside time.

## Summary

This examiner-blind, split-mouth RCT tested whether isolation method (rubber dam vs cotton rolls/retraction cord) affects the clinical performance of adhesive restorations placed in noncarious cervical lesions (NCCL). Thirty patients each received one restoration per method (60 total), randomized by coin toss, using the same self-etch adhesive and composite resin. Across 18 months of FDI-criteria follow-up, retention, marginal staining, marginal adaptation, gingival damage (bleeding, laceration, insertion level, sensitivity), patient preference, and chairside time were statistically equivalent between isolation methods, supporting cotton roll/retraction cord as a valid alternative to rubber dam for this specific clinical scenario when performed by experienced operators.

## Key Contributions

- First prospective, split-mouth, examiner-blind comparison of RD vs CR/RC isolation specifically for NCCL restorations (prior evidence was mostly observational).
- Coordinated outcome set: retention + gingival damage + patient preference + chairside time, all evaluated with FDI criteria to 18 months.
- Demonstrates gingival damage from either isolation method is transient/reversible (1-week insertion level ≈ baseline).
- Incidentally reveals a low 18-month overall retention rate (73%) for the one-step self-etch adhesive tested (GO!, SDI), attributed in discussion to the adhesive's chemistry rather than isolation method.

## Methodology

Examiner-blind split-mouth RCT at State University of Ponta Grossa (Brazil). 30 subjects with matched-pair Class V NCCLs (no undercuts, occlusal contacts present, ≤50% margin in enamel); isolation method randomized per lesion by coin toss; both restorations placed same day by calibrated experienced residents (>10y) under an experienced supervisor, no cavity prep/bevel. Outcomes: patient preference; gingival laceration/bleeding/insertion level (Löe & Silness Index)/sensitivity; chairside time; FDI criteria (marginal staining, fractures/retention, marginal adaptation, postoperative sensitivity, recurrent caries) at baseline, 6, 12, 18 months by two blinded calibrated examiners. Statistics: McNemar, Wilcoxon signed-rank, paired t-test, Fisher exact test (α=0.05).

## Results

- Retention at 18 months: RD 73% (55–86%) vs CR/RC 73% (55–86%), no significant difference at any recall (P>0.05); both groups declined significantly from baseline by 12–18 months.
- Patient preference: RD 54% vs CR/RC 46% (P=0.86).
- Chairside time: RD 20.8±5.2 min vs CR/RC 21.2±5.2 min (P=0.77).
- Gingival laceration 63% vs 37% (P=0.64); 1-week bleeding 60% vs 47% (P=0.57); 1-week sensitivity 43% vs 27% (P=0.52) — none significant.
- Gingival insertion level: median 1mm at baseline both groups, no significant baseline (P=0.82) or 1-week (P=0.56) between-group difference; damage reversible by 1 week.
- No postoperative sensitivity or recurrent caries in either group at any timepoint; FDI marginal staining/adaptation/fracture ratings not significantly different between groups.
- Null hypothesis accepted: CR/RC performs similarly to RD across all measured NCCL restoration outcomes.
