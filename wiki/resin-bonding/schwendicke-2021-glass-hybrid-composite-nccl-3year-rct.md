## Three-line Summary

Cluster-randomized trial (NCT02631161; 88 patients aged 50-70, 175 sclerotic NCCLs; 83 glass hybrid vs 92 resin composite restorations placed without mechanical cavity preparation), mean follow-up 36 months (range 31-55) — abstract-only ingest.

Total retention loss in 17 GH vs 19 RC restorations (5 additional partial GH losses); survival not significantly different (p = 0.396, multi-level Cox); FDI quality did not differ except surface luster (RC better, p < 0.001); total cost per restoration 41.72 EUR (GH) vs 51.60 EUR (RC), p < 0.001.

Survival was equivalent but GH was cheaper both initially and long-term, so RC is cost-effective only for payers willing to pay a large premium for minimal survival gains; only the abstract was available, so lesion-level and failure-mode detail is not captured.

## Summary

abstract-only — full text not retrieved. This is the 3-year report of a cluster-randomized trial (NCT02631161) from Charité Berlin comparing a glass hybrid restorative (EQUIA Forte Fil/Coat) with a resin composite (OptiBond FL/Filtek Supreme XTE) in sclerotic non-carious cervical lesions, placed without mechanical cavity preparation in 88 patients (175 lesions). Over a mean 36 months, total retention loss occurred in 17 GH and 19 RC restorations (5 GH were partially lost) with no significant survival difference (p = 0.396). Restoration quality by FDI criteria differed only in surface luster, favoring RC. GH was significantly cheaper from a German payer perspective (41.72 vs 51.60 EUR), so RC is cost-effective only if payers accept a high premium for minimal survival gains.

## Key Contributions

- Longest follow-up (mean 36 months) of the sNCCL GH-vs-RC trial; extends the 18-month interim report.
- Shows equivalent survival for a self-adhesive-type glass hybrid and an etch-and-rinse composite in non-prepared sclerotic lesions.
- Adds payer-perspective cost data (initial and follow-up), which most NCCL material-comparison RCTs lack.

## Methodology

Cluster-randomized (as described in the abstract) trial, NCT02631161. 88 patients aged 50-70 with 175 sclerotic NCCLs: 43 patients/83 restorations received GH (EQUIA Forte Fil + EQUIA Forte Coat), 45 patients/92 restorations received RC (OptiBond FL + Filtek Supreme XTE). No mechanical cavity preparation. Mean follow-up 36 months (31-55), variable because of the COVID-19 lockdown. FDI-criteria re-evaluation at 1, 18 and 36 months; survival by multi-level Cox regression; costs from a German payer perspective (micro-costing for initial treatment, statutory-insurance fee catalogues for follow-up; GLM comparison).

## Clinical Insights

- For retention loss in sclerotic NCCLs without cavity preparation, the trial shows no survival difference between a glass hybrid and an etch-and-rinse composite at about 3 years; loss of the whole restoration occurred in roughly one in five restorations in both arms (computed from the abstract counts).
- A glass hybrid is a reasonable lower-cost option when the lesion is restored without preparation; the composite's only quality advantage reported in the abstract was surface luster.
- Limits: abstract-only data, sclerotic lesions in patients aged 50-70, one payer system; failure mode (adhesive vs cohesive) and lesion-level predictors are not available from this record.
