## Three-line Summary

5-year follow-up of a split-mouth RCT restoring 200 non-carious cervical lesions (NCCLs) with Scotchbond Universal without (SBU) or with selective enamel etching (E-SBU), or Vitremer RMGIC without (RMGIC) or with EDTA pretreatment (E-RMGIC); 119 restorations re-evaluated.

All four strategies showed statistically similar 5-year survival; E-SBU had more marginal-integrity defects than the ionomer groups and more marginal discoloration than RMGIC, whereas ionomer restorations had poorer surface texture and RMGIC wore more than E-SBU.

Retention loss was driven by lesion/patient factors — premolars, greater dentin sclerosis, and greater gingival recession — rather than by adhesion strategy.

## Summary
This is the 5-year report of the UNESP randomized split-mouth trial previously held at its 4-year horizon ([[resin-bonding/omoto-2025-nccl-adhesion-strategies-4year-rct]]). Two hundred NCCL restorations were placed with four adhesion strategies — a universal adhesive (Scotchbond Universal + Filtek Z350XT) applied in self-etch mode (SBU) or with selective enamel etching (E-SBU), and a resin-modified glass-ionomer (Vitremer) applied without (RMGIC) or with EDTA dentin pretreatment (E-RMGIC). At 5 years, 119 restorations were re-evaluated. Survival did not differ among the four groups. Differences emerged in secondary criteria: E-SBU accumulated more Bravo scores for marginal integrity (vs both ionomer groups) and marginal discoloration (vs RMGIC); the ionomer groups had more Bravo scores for surface texture, and RMGIC showed greater wear than E-SBU. Color and secondary caries did not differ. Multiple logistic regression showed retention was significantly influenced by tooth type (premolars), degree of dentin sclerosis, and degree of marginal tissue recession.

## Key Findings
- **Survival**: SBU ≈ E-SBU ≈ RMGIC ≈ E-RMGIC at 5 years (no significant difference).
- **Composite (E-SBU) trade-off**: more marginal-integrity Bravo vs ionomer groups; more marginal discoloration vs RMGIC.
- **Ionomer trade-off**: poorer surface texture (both ionomer groups); more wear (RMGIC vs E-SBU).
- **No difference**: color match, secondary caries.
- **Retention risk factors**: premolars, higher dentin sclerosis, greater gingival recession.

## Change vs 4-year report
- The 4-year report found survival curves equivalent (p=0.315) but noted SBU alone lost retention vs baseline while E-SBU did not. At 5 years the abstract reports equivalent survival across all four strategies and no longer frames selective enamel etching as a retention advantage; instead E-SBU shows more marginal defects/discoloration.
- EDTA pretreatment of RMGIC still shows no distinguishing clinical benefit.
- New at 5 years: lesion/patient-level retention predictors (premolar, sclerosis, recession).

## Clinical Insights
- For NCCLs, a universal adhesive (with or without selective enamel etching) and RMGIC performed equivalently for survival at 5 years — material choice can follow esthetic and wear priorities rather than retention.
- Composite gives better surface texture/wear resistance; RMGIC gives fewer marginal-integrity defects — a trade-off, not a winner.
- Flag premolar lesions, heavily sclerotic dentin, and deep gingival recession as higher retention-risk situations regardless of adhesion strategy (see [[resin-bonding/hamdi-2025-surface-pretreatments-sclerotic-dentin-bond-sr]] for sclerotic-dentin pretreatment evidence).

## Limitations
- Abstract only: exact survival rates, effect sizes, and odds ratios not retrieved.
- 119/200 restorations re-evaluated — substantial attrition by 5 years.
- Material-specific results (Scotchbond Universal, Vitremer); single research group.
