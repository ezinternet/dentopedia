## Three-line Summary
Randomized clinical trial (Universidade Federal de Santa Catarina, Brazil): 105 cervical non-carious defects (buccal surfaces of canines and premolars) split into three groups of 35 - no enamel bevel + microfilled composite (Durafill VS), enamel bevel + microfilled composite, enamel bevel + flowable composite (Natural Flow) - all with a total-etch one-bottle adhesive (One-Step), followed for 3 years.
At 6 months beveled margins gave 100% retention regardless of composite versus 66% for non-beveled margins; at 2 and 3 years retention did not differ significantly among the three groups, and sensitivity, marginal discoloration and secondary caries were unaffected by beveling or composite viscosity.
Enamel beveling improved retention only transiently (first 6 months) and neither bevel nor flowable-vs-microfilled viscosity changed 3-year outcomes in these non-retentive Class V defects; abstract-only record (no group-level data beyond the abstract, no bias assessment), so the early-retention signal cannot be appraised further.

## Summary
This randomized clinical trial from Santa Catarina, Brazil, tested whether enamel beveling and resin viscosity affect Class V composite restorations. One hundred five non-carious cervical defects on canines and premolars were randomized to no bevel + microfilled composite, bevel + microfilled composite, or bevel + flowable composite (35 each), all bonded with a total-etch one-bottle adhesive. At 6 months the beveled groups showed 100% retention against 66% in the non-beveled group, but at 2 and 3 years retention no longer differed significantly among groups, and sensitivity, marginal discoloration and secondary caries were unaffected. The authors conclude that beveling helps retention only in the first 6 months and that neither bevel nor composite viscosity matters at 3 years in non-retentive Class V lesions. Only the abstract was available.

## Key Contributions
- Early randomized evidence that the enamel-bevel advantage in cervical composites is transient (6 months) rather than durable.
- No difference between flowable and microfilled composite with a bevel at 3 years.
- Secondary caries, discoloration and sensitivity unaffected by either variable.

## Methodology
Parallel-group randomized trial; 105 buccal non-carious defects (canines, premolars), 35 per group; One-Step total-etch adhesive for all; outcomes retention, sensitivity, marginal discoloration, secondary caries over 3 years. Test names, dropout and blinding are not in the abstract.

## Results
