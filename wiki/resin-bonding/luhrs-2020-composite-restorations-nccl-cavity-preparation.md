## Three-line Summary

Prospective randomized clinical trial of 85 Class V composite restorations in non-carious cervical lesions (NCCLs; 24 patients; enamel coronal margin, dentin cervical margin) comparing four dentin pretreatments: cleaning only (CLEAN), bur roughening + flowable (PREP_FLOW), roughening + fine groove (GROOVE), roughening + groove + flowable (GROOVE_FLOW); recall at 7.7 (± 0.35) years in 64 restorations (75.3%).

Overall retention was 82.8%; the no-preparation CLEAN group had the highest loss rate (27.8%), significantly higher than the three pooled prepared groups (p = 0.041), with no difference among the prepared groups (p = 0.328). Modified USPHS esthetics, marginal adaptation, anatomic form and marginal discoloration did not differ between groups; no secondary caries and one case of hypersensitivity.

Mechanically roughening sclerotic cervical dentin with a round bur (with or without a 0.5 mm groove or a flowable liner) appears to improve long-term retention versus cleaning alone; the study is small (per-group n = 11-18 at recall), single-center, used a three-step adhesive (Syntac) now rarely used, and rated each restoration as a whole without separating coronal and cervical margins.

## 세줄요약

비우식성 치경부 병소 (Non-Carious Cervical Lesion, NCCL) 85개 (24명)의 5급 복합레진 수복물을 4가지 상아질 전처치 — 세정만 (CLEAN), 라운드 버 거칠기 + 플로어블 (PREP_FLOW), 거칠기 + 미세 그루브 (GROOVE), 거칠기 + 그루브 + 플로어블 (GROOVE_FLOW) — 로 무작위 배정한 전향적 무작위 임상시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT)이며, 7.7 (± 0.35)년 시점에 64개 (75.3%)를 재평가했다.

전체 유지율은 82.8%였고, 전처치 없는 CLEAN군의 탈락률이 27.8%로 가장 높아 전처치 3군 합산 대비 유의하게 높았으며 (p = 0.041), 전처치 3군 사이에는 차이가 없었다 (p = 0.328). 수정 USPHS 기준 심미성·변연적합성·해부학적 형태·변연변색은 군 간 차이가 없었고 이차우식은 없었다.

경화상아질 (sclerotic dentin)을 라운드 버로 거칠게 하면 세정만 한 경우보다 장기 유지율이 높아 보이나, 군당 n = 11-18로 작은 단일기관 연구이며 3단계 접착제 (Syntac)를 썼고 치은측·교합측 변연을 구분해 평가하지 않은 한계가 있다.

## Summary

In a prospective randomized clinical study from Hannover Medical School, 85 non-carious cervical lesions (coronal margin in enamel, cervical margin in dentin, depth >= 1 mm) in 24 patients were restored with Syntac (selective enamel etch) and Tetric EvoCeram after one of four dentin pretreatments. At 7.7 years 64 restorations (75.3%) were re-examined; overall retention was 82.8%. Restorations placed after dentin cleaning only had the highest loss rate (27.8%), significantly worse than the pooled roughened/grooved groups (p = 0.041), while roughening, roughening + groove, and the flowable liner did not differ from each other (p = 0.328) or on any USPHS criterion. The authors conclude that roughening of cervical dentin and/or a fine groove can be added to the clinical NCCL protocol.

## Key Contributions

- Randomized, blinded-examiner evidence over 6.8-8.2 years on how cervical cavity preparation affects retention of NCCL composite restorations (most NCCL RCTs are <= 3 years).
- Cleaning only (no dentin preparation) lost the most restorations (27.8%), consistent with poor bonding to hypermineralized sclerotic dentin.
- A 0.5 mm cervical groove and a 0.5 mm flowable liner gave no measurable clinical benefit over roughening alone, but eased handling (clearer margin, less slippery surface than unfilled adhesive).

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective randomized clinical study (randomization list), 4 parallel groups; two calibrated, blinded examiners; Cohen's kappa = 1; chi-square tests (p < 0.05).
- **Patients/teeth**: 24 patients, 85 NCCLs (recruited Sept 2007-May 2008; last recall June 2015); lesion depth >= 1 mm (Smith and Knight Tooth Wear Index); exclusions included bruxism, carious lesions, non-vital pulp, severe periodontitis, orthodontic treatment.
- **Groups**: (1) CLEAN: fluoride-free prophylaxis paste only; (2) PREP_FLOW: round carbide bur (14/16) roughening of dentin + thin (<= 0.5 mm) flowable (Tetric EvoFlow); (3) GROOVE: roughening + fine cervical groove with a 010 round bur (depth max 0.5 mm); (4) GROOVE_FLOW: groove + flowable. Preparation at 2,000 rpm, no water cooling, light pressure.
- **Common protocol**: enamel bevel 1-1.5 mm; retraction cord; selective enamel etch (36% phosphoric acid, 30 s); Syntac (maleic-acid self-etching primer, adhesive, Heliobond); Tetric EvoCeram in increments <= 2 mm.
- **Outcomes**: retention; modified USPHS (esthetics, marginal adaptation, anatomic form, marginal discoloration, secondary caries, gingival response, sensitivity); bleeding on probing at 6 sites.

## Clinical Insights

- For a lost-cervical-restoration complaint, the preparation step matters: cleaning only and bonding to the sclerotic dentin surface was the worst-retaining protocol here (27.8% loss at 7.7 y), consistent with the paper's explanation of hypermineralized, tubule-obliterated dentin that etching cannot fully remove.
- Whether roughening is done with a bur alone, with a fine groove, or under a flowable liner made no retention difference in this trial; groove and flowable are handling choices, not retention levers.
- Context cited by the authors: a meta-analysis of cervical restoration retention reported 82.6% at 5 years and 67.7% at 8 years, so 82.8% at 7.7 years is on the favorable side; this trial used a three-step adhesive, so the result should not be transferred directly to one-step universal adhesives.

## Limitations

- Small groups at recall (11-18 restorations), 24.7% attrition, and wide patient age (older cohort with gingival recession by recall).
- Single center; one adhesive (Syntac Classic, three-step) and one composite; results may not transfer to current universal adhesives.
- Each restoration was rated as a whole; coronal (enamel) and cervical (dentin) margins were not scored separately, which matters when a flowable lines only the cervical margin.
- Loss rates per group are reported as percentages without a clearly tabulated denominator, and no survival analysis was performed (chi-square only).
- Authors report no conflict of interest; funding source is not stated in the text provided.
