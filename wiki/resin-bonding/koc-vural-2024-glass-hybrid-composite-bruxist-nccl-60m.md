> - 브럭시즘 환자만 대상으로 한 최초의 60개월 분구개내 이중맹검 RCT
> - 유리 혼합형 수복재 (Glass Hybrid Restorative, GHR, Equia Forte Fil) vs 나노도자기 복합레진 (Composite Resin, CR, Ceram.X One Universal)

> - 전 기간 치아 과민증·이차 우식 없음 — 양 재료 모두 안전

## Three-line Summary

Split-mouth RCT (25 bruxist patients, 148 NCCLs, 60 months) found no significant difference in retention between glass hybrid (GH 66.7%) and nano-ceramic composite resin (CR 73.5%) at 60 months (p=0.464).
TWI significantly correlated with CR retention loss but not GH; both materials showed significant marginal changes over time with no between-group difference at 36–60m; zero sensitivity or secondary caries throughout.
In bruxist NCCL patients, GH and CR are clinically equivalent at 60 months; GH's simplified placement and bioactive properties make it a cost-effective choice, while TWI may help guide material selection.

## Study Overview

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | Single-center, split-mouth, double-blind RCT |
| Population | 25 bruxist patients (clinical diagnosis; no polysomnography) |
| Lesions | 148 NCCLs |
| Follow-up | 60 months (6/12/24/36/48/60m) |
| Dropout | 10 patients; 60% recall at 36–60m (COVID-19, etc.) |
| Materials | GH: Equia Forte Fil (GC); CR: Ceram.X One Universal (Dentsply) |

## Key Findings

### Retention (Primary Outcome)

- **CR**: 73.5% retention (13/49 restorations lost)
- **GH**: 66.7% retention (16/48 restorations lost)
- **Log-rank test**: p=0.464 (not significant)
- **Kaplan-Meier**: p=0.487 (not significant)

No material demonstrated superiority in bruxist patients over 60 months.

### Tooth Wear Index as Predictor

TWI (parafunctional load severity) significantly predicted CR retention failure (p<0.05) but did **not** predict GH failure. This suggests GH may tolerate increasing wear severity more consistently — possibly due to its self-adhesive, fluoride-releasing properties and lack of a separate adhesive layer that could degrade under cyclic stress.

### Marginal Outcomes

- **Marginal adaptation**: Both materials showed significant deterioration over time. At 24m, GH was significantly worse (p=0.002). At 36m and 60m, no significant difference.
- **Marginal discoloration**: Significant increase over time in both; no between-material difference.

### Adverse Events

- **Tooth sensitivity**: 0 cases (all timepoints)
- **Secondary caries**: 0 cases (all timepoints)

## Clinical Implications

1. **Both materials are viable in bruxist patients** — bruxism should not be an absolute contraindication for either GH or CR in NCCLs.
2. **GH advantages**: Self-adhesive (no separate bonding), fluoride release, bioactivity, lower cost, faster placement — all without compromising long-term retention.
3. **TWI-guided selection**: In patients with high TWI (severe wear), GH may be preferable since CR retention correlates with wear severity.
4. **No sensitivity or caries**: Both materials maintained pulp protection and prevented caries progression over 5 years.

## Limitations

- Bruxism confirmed by clinical signs only (no polysomnography); severity heterogeneity
- No rubber dam — real-world conditions but potential contamination
- 40% dropout at 60m (COVID-related); reduced statistical power
- Single center
