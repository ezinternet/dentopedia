## Three-line Summary

A 48-month single-operator RCT (30 patients, 134 NCCLs) in systemically compromised patients found no significant difference between Fuji Bulk GIC (82.1% survival) and G-ænial Posterior RC (89.3% survival) at four years using modified USPHS criteria. Both groups showed significant marginal changes over time but no between-group differences at any evaluation period, and neither developed secondary caries. GIC is a clinically acceptable, potentially more practical alternative to resin composite for NCCL restoration in patients with systemic diseases.

## Background

Most NCCL RCTs—including major multi-year trials (Peumans 2023, Omoto 2025)—explicitly **exclude patients with complex medical histories** as a confound. This creates a practical gap: clinicians treating medically compromised patients lack direct evidence for material selection. Fuji Bulk, a glass ionomer cement (GIC), is fluoride-releasing, moisture-tolerant, and less technique-sensitive than resin composites, making it potentially advantageous when patient cooperation, salivary flow, or compliance are concerns.

## Study Details

- **Design**: Parallel-arm RCT, single operator
- **n**: 30 patients with systemic diseases; 134 restorations (FB: 38, GP: 30 at recall)
- **GIC arm**: Fuji Bulk (FB) — glass ionomer cement (글래스아이오노머 시멘트, GIC)
- **RC arm**: G-ænial Posterior (GP) + universal adhesive, etch-and-rinse (에치앤린스) mode
- **Follow-up**: 48 months; recall rate 60%
- **Outcome**: Modified USPHS criteria — retention, marginal discoloration (변연 변색), marginal adaptation (변연 적합성), secondary caries (이차 우식), surface texture (표면 질감), post-operative sensitivity (술후 민감증)

## Results

| Outcome | Fuji Bulk (GIC) | G-ænial Posterior (RC) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retention loss | 7/38 (18.4%) | 4/30 (13.3%) | 0.57 (ns) |
| 48-month survival | 82.1% | 89.3% | 0.305 log-rank (ns) |
| Marginal adaptation Δ from baseline | Significant (p<0.05) | Significant (p<0.05) | — |
| Between-group difference (all periods) | — | — | p>0.05 (ns) |
| Secondary caries | 0 | 0 | — |

Marginal discoloration and surface texture also changed significantly over 48 months in both groups, but without between-group differences at any evaluation period.

## Clinical Implications

1. **GIC is a valid NCCL option for systemic disease patients**: comparable 4-year survival to resin composite in this population.
2. **Practical advantages of GIC**: lower technique sensitivity (moisture tolerance, no bonding step), fluoride release, and lower cost — may matter more for patients with polypharmacy, xerostomia, or frequent dental visits.
3. **RC still numerically superior** (89.3% vs 82.1%) but difference is not statistically significant; small sample limits power.
4. **Both materials show expected marginal changes** over 4 years — consistent with broader NCCL literature.

## Limitations

- Small sample (30 patients; 134 restorations); only ~18–30 restorations per arm at recall
- 60% recall rate at 48 months — survival analysis subject to dropout bias
- Single clinician — operator-specific results
- Systemic disease types not specified in abstract (heterogeneous population likely)
- Abstract-only extraction; full methodology (randomization, blinding, disease breakdown) not confirmed
