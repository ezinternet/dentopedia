> - **5종류**: 알데히드(Aldehyde)계, 키토산(Chitosan)계, 리보플라빈(Riboflavin)계, 폴리페놀(Polyphenol)계, 카르보디이미드(Carbodiimide)계

> - **임상 적용 시기 아직 아님**: 이질성 높고 근거 질 낮아 신중한 해석 필요
> - 핵심 임상 시사점: E&R 시스템 + 알데히드/리보플라빈/폴리페놀 전처리가 현재로선 가장 근거 있는 조합

## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (63 in vitro studies, 2015–2025): aldehyde-, riboflavin-, polyphenol-, and carbodiimide-based cross-linkers enhance resin-dentin bond strength under specific conditions; effects are most consistent when used as pretreatment with etch-and-rinse adhesive systems.

Chitosan and siloxane cross-linkers show limited and unconfirmed reinforcing evidence; efficacy depends on cross-linker type, application mode, and adhesive strategy — no single agent performs universally.

GRADE evidence is low to very low; publication bias exists in aged subgroups of aldehyde and polyphenol treatments, warranting cautious interpretation and delaying clinical translation.

## Background

The hybrid layer (HL) is susceptible to collagen degradation by endogenous MMPs and cysteine cathepsins. Collagen cross-linkers offer a structural approach: by forming covalent bonds between collagen fibrils, they stabilize the organic matrix and resist enzymatic attack. Compared to CHX (which inhibits enzymes functionally), cross-linkers act mechanically — making the collagen substrate less digestible. The five main types differ in mechanism, source, and clinical feasibility:

| Type | Examples | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Aldehyde** | Glutaraldehyde, proanthocyanidin (from grape seed) | Forms Schiff bases with collagen amino groups |
| **Polyphenol** | Grape seed extract (GSE), tannic acid | Hydrogen bonding + phenol-protein interactions |
| **Riboflavin** | Riboflavin (B2) ± UVA | Photo-cross-linking; induces collagen fiber stiffening |
| **Carbodiimide** | EDC (1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide) | Zero-length cross-linking; bridges carboxyl–amine groups |
| **Chitosan** | Chitosan oligomers | Electrostatic + partial cross-linking; antimicrobial |

## Methods

- **Search**: PubMed, WoS, Embase, Cochrane; January 2015–September 2025
- **63 in vitro studies** included
- **Outcome**: microtensile bond strength (µTBS) — immediate and after artificial ageing
- **Subgroups**: by cross-linker type, application method, adhesive strategy
- **Analysis**: random-effects model; standardized mean difference (SMD) + 95% CI
- **Bias**: most studies medium risk; GRADE evidence low to very low

## Results by Cross-Linker Type

| Cross-linker | Immediate effect | Long-term (aged) | Best context | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aldehyde** | Enhanced | Enhanced (specific conditions) | E&R pretreatment | Publication bias in aged subgroups |
| **Polyphenol** | Enhanced | Enhanced (specific conditions) | E&R pretreatment | Publication bias in aged subgroups |
| **Riboflavin** | Enhanced | Enhanced under specific conditions | E&R pretreatment | UVA activation required in some protocols |
| **Carbodiimide (EDC)** | Enhanced | Enhanced | E&R pretreatment | Application time/concentration sensitivity |
| **Chitosan** | Limited evidence | Unconfirmed | — | Small number of studies |
| **Siloxane** | Limited evidence | Unconfirmed | — | Minimal data |

**Most consistent finding**: E&R adhesive system + cross-linker pretreatment outperforms self-etch context.

## Clinical Significance

1. **Cross-linkers are not yet clinically validated**: all evidence from in vitro — clinical RCTs absent
2. **E&R adhesive + cross-linker pretreatment**: currently the most evidence-supported strategy for enhancing long-term bond stability via collagen modification
3. **Proanthocyanidin/polyphenol (grape seed extract)**: natural origin, commercially feasible; evidence-supported but publication bias caution
4. **Riboflavin**: interesting as it can be UVA-activated without separate chemical agent; limited clinical data
5. **Chitosan**: antimicrobial bonus but insufficient bonding evidence to recommend
6. **Application strategy matters more than type alone**: even the best cross-linker underperforms if adhesive strategy mismatched
7. **Complement to CHX**: cross-linkers and CHX target same collagen degradation pathway via different mechanisms — synergy possible but not yet studied clinically

## Limitations

- All in vitro — no clinical trials
- Heterogeneity: different ageing protocols, cross-linker concentrations, application times, adhesive systems
- GRADE low to very low — limited confidence in effect size estimates
- Publication bias in aldehyde and polyphenol aged subgroups
- Limited studies on chitosan/siloxane types
