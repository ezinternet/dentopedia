## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 9 systematic reviews (7 meta-analyses, 175 primary studies, PROSPERO CRD42024588804) synthesizing 20 dentin biomodification agents (natural/physical/chemical) across 51 experimental setups for their effect on resin-dentin bond durability of composite restorations.

Non-thermal atmospheric plasma (NTAP) was the most consistently beneficial physical agent long-term (SMD up to 3.28); grape seed extract and chitosan improved long-term bond strength among natural agents; sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) and hypochlorous acid (HOCl) both significantly reduced bond strength as chemical agents. Overlap among included SRs was slight (CCA = 1.52%).

Only scrubbing and air abrasion are currently affordable/chairside-ready physical biomodification techniques; NTAP is promising but limited by equipment cost and workflow integration, and chemical deproteinizers (NaOCl especially) should be avoided as routine dentin pretreatment before bonding.

## Summary

This umbrella review (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD42024588804) synthesizes 9 systematic reviews (7 with meta-analysis, including 1 network meta-analysis; 175 primary in vitro studies) evaluating dentin biomodification agents used to preserve resin-composite bond durability. Twenty biomodifiers were identified across 51 experimental setups and grouped into three mechanistic categories: natural (7 agents — baicalein, chitosan, cocoa seed extract, grape seed extract, green tea extract, EGCG, riboflavin), physical (7 agents — NTAP, scrubbing, air abrasion, LASER, warm air blow, electric current, ozone), and chemical (6 agents — dentin desensitizers, DMSO, ethanol wet bonding, HOCl, NaOCl, glycolic acid). Bond strength was assessed predominantly by micro-tensile bond strength (µTBS, 68.62% of setups) at time points ranging from immediate to 36 months, most commonly immediate and 12 months. Overlap among the 9 SRs was slight overall (CCA = 1.52%), though a very-high pairwise overlap (57.9%) was found between the two NTAP-focused SRs, a caveat for NTAP conclusions specifically. ROBIS risk-of-bias assessment showed generally low risk in study-eligibility criteria but increasing unclear/high risk in later stages (synthesis and overall appraisal), warranting cautious interpretation. Clinically, natural agents were uniformly beneficial-or-neutral (never harmful) but remain unapproved for routine dental use; among physical agents only scrubbing and air abrasion are both affordable and chairside-ready today; chemical deproteinizing agents (NaOCl, HOCl) consistently reduced bond strength and should not be used as dentin pretreatment before composite bonding.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic taxonomy unifying dentin biomodifiers by mechanism (natural / physical / chemical) specifically for resin-composite bond durability, rather than by individual agent or single SR.
- Quantifies both overall SR overlap (CCA = 1.52%, slight) and flags agent-specific overlap risk (57.9% pairwise CCA between the two NTAP SRs) — a methodological signal rarely surfaced in single-topic SRs.
- Cross-maps biomodifier agent × bond-strength test type (µTBS/SBS/µSBS) × adhesive strategy (self-etch vs etch-and-rinse), exposing where evidence is testing-method-heterogeneous vs converged.
- Time-stratifies pooled effects into short- (<3mo), medium- (3-12mo), long-term (≥12mo) windows, aligning in vitro aging with clinically interpretable durability language.
- Explicit clinical-translation gate: separates "efficacious in vitro" from "chairside-ready today" (only scrubbing and air abrasion clear both bars among physical agents).

## Methodology

**Design**: Umbrella review (systematic review of systematic reviews) per PRISMA 2020; PROSPERO CRD42024588804.

**PICOS**: Population = resin-composite restorations; Intervention = biomodifier application to dentin surface prior to restoration; Comparator = resin-composite restorations with no prior dentin pretreatment; Outcome = bond durability; Study design = SRs of in vitro studies (with or without meta-analysis).

**Search**: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, searched October 2024, three core concepts (dentin, biomodification, bond strength), no date/language restriction. Screening (title/abstract then full-text) duplicated independently by 2 reviewers, disagreements resolved by a 3rd author. Missing data requested from corresponding authors (1-week follow-up).

**Data harmonization**: Adhesive strategies collapsed into self-etch (1-step/2-step) vs etch-and-rinse (2-step/3-step) categories, with universal adhesives allocated by mode of application. Bond-strength time points grouped into short-/medium-/long-term bins.

**Overlap quantification**: GROOVE citation matrix + Corrected Covered Area (CCA), interpreted per Pieper et al thresholds (slight 0-5%, moderate 6-10%, high 11-15%, very high >15%).

**Risk of bias**: ROBIS tool — (1) optional relevance assessment, (2) 4-domain concern identification (eligibility criteria; identification/selection of studies; data collection/study appraisal; synthesis and findings), (3) overall judgement — applied independently in duplicate, disagreements resolved by 3rd author.

## Results

- **Flow**: 486 records → 64 duplicates removed → 383 excluded at title/abstract → 34 assessed full-text (5 unretrievable) → 9 SRs included (7 meta-analyses incl. 1 network meta-analysis) → 175 primary studies (19 duplicated across the 9 SRs).
- **Overlap**: overall CCA = 0.0152 (1.52%, slight); pairwise CCA = 57.9% (very high) between the two SRs both focused on NTAP.
- **Quality (ROBIS)**: 3/9 SRs low risk across all domains; 2/9 unclear risk in 1 domain; 3/9 at least 2 domains unclear; 1/9 high risk in 3 domains. By domain: 100% low risk (study eligibility criteria); 75% low / 25% unclear (identification and selection of studies); 70% low / 20% unclear / 10% high (data collection and study appraisal); 60% low / 25% unclear / 15% high (synthesis and findings); overall risk of bias: 50% low / 30% unclear / 20% high.
- **Testing methods**: µTBS used in 35/51 setups (68.62%), SBS in 8/51 (15.68%), µSBS in 6/51 (11.76%), unspecified in 2/51 (3.92%). Adhesive strategy split near-even: etch-and-rinse 25 setups (49.01%), self-etch 26 setups (50.98%).

**Natural agents** (best performers): grape seed extract — medium-term MD = 8.29 (I²=96%), long-term MD = 13.88 (I²=46%), both favoring GSE; chitosan — immediate SMD = -0.75 (I²=35%, self-etch), medium/long-term SMD = -1.24 (I²=50%, etch-and-rinse only, no self-etch benefit over time); riboflavin — short-term MD = -8.49 (I²=89%), medium-term MD = -10.23 (I²=96%), photoactivated > non-photoactivated. Green tea extract improved long-term only. Cocoa seed extract and EGCG showed no significant effect (p=0.23, p=0.95 respectively).

**Physical agents** (best performers): NTAP — 3 SRs consistent direction; strongest network-meta-analysis effect sizes SMD = 1.92 (short-term) and 3.28 (long-term), I²=97% both, with He-NTAP effective short- and long-term vs Ar-NTAP short-term only. Scrubbing improved uTBS immediate and medium-term. Air abrasion SMD = -0.75 (95% CI -1.79 to 0.29, I²=92%), short-term only, wide CI. LASER conflicting: one SR found immediate-term reduction (I²=56%), another (network meta-analysis, Erbium lasers only) found Er:YAG and Er,Cr:YSGG both improved SBS, Er:YAG + etch-and-rinse best overall.

**Chemical agents** (mostly negative except two): ethanol wet bonding — immediate SMD = -1.41 (low heterogeneity), favoring ethanol. DMSO — improves long-term etch-and-rinse bond stability (not dose-dependent), controversial for self-etch; useful as a solvent for incorporating natural extracts. NaOCl and HOCl both significantly reduced µTBS in self-etch mode (NaOCl more damaging than HOCl); when deproteinization is needed, 2-step self-etch adhesives outperformed 1-step on HOCl-deproteinized dentin. Dentin desensitizers reduced bond strength (SMD = 0.96, I²=80%).
