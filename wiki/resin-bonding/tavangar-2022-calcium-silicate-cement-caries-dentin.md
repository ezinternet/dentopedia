## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (Clin Exp Dent Res 2022) comparing calcium silicate-based self-adhesive resin cement (e.g., TheraCem) vs conventional self-adhesive resin cement for shear bond strength (SBS) to caries-affected dentin (CAD) and sound dentin, with 6-month remineralization assessment.

The calcium silicate cement had lower immediate SBS than the conventional cement for both dentin types; at 6 months it induced remineralization of caries-affected dentin whereas the conventional cement showed no remineralization; both cements produced lower SBS on CAD than sound dentin initially.

A clinical trade-off exists: calcium silicate cement offers biological remineralization of retained carious dentin at the cost of lower immediate bond strength, making it relevant for conservative preparations where caries-affected tissue is intentionally preserved.

## 세줄요약

In vitro 연구 (Clin Exp Dent Res 2022): 칼슘실리케이트 자가접착형 레진 시멘트(TheraCem 등) vs 전통 자가접착형 레진 시멘트의 우식이환상아질(caries-affected dentin, CAD)·건전상아질 전단결합강도(SBS)와 6개월 재광화 평가.

칼슘실리케이트 시멘트 즉시 SBS < 전통 시멘트(모든 상아질 유형); 6개월 시점에서 칼슘실리케이트는 CAD 재광화 유도, 전통 시멘트는 재광화 없음; 초기에 두 시멘트 모두 CAD에서 건전상아질 대비 낮은 SBS.

임상적 트레이드오프: 칼슘실리케이트 시멘트는 즉시 결합강도가 낮은 대신 잔존 우식상아질의 생물학적 재광화 효과 — 최소침습 접근으로 CAD를 의도적으로 보존하는 상황에서 선택 근거 제공.

## Summary
In vitro study comparing calcium silicate-based self-adhesive resin cement vs conventional self-adhesive resin cement for SBS to caries-affected dentin (CAD), with 6-month follow-up for remineralization assessment. Trade-off identified: calcium silicate offers biological benefit (remineralization) at the cost of lower immediate bond strength.

## Key Contributions
- Caries-affected dentin: lower SBS than healthy dentin for both cements initially
- Calcium silicate cement: induces CAD remineralization at 6 months — bonds improve over time
- Conventional self-adhesive cement: higher immediate SBS but no remineralization benefit
- Clinical scenario: conservative preparations preserving CAD may benefit from calcium silicate cement
- TheraCem as a calcium silicate cement: gaining interest as caries-protective luting agent
