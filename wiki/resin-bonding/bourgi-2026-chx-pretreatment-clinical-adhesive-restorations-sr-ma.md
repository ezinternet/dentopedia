## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (11 RCTs/CCTs, 1 week–4 years): CHX dentin pretreatment (0.2–2%) showed no significant effect on restoration retention (RD 0.01), postoperative sensitivity (RD 0.01), or secondary caries (RD 0.00), with zero statistical heterogeneity across all outcomes.

Lab evidence for MMP inhibition and hybrid layer preservation does not translate to measurable clinical benefit, likely because modern etch-and-rinse adhesives with functional monomers (10-MDP) form hybrid layers robust enough to render CHX redundant.

Clinical conclusion: CHX pretreatment is safe but not required in routine adhesive protocols; reserve for antimicrobial/disinfection purposes in high-caries-risk cases.

## 세줄요약

SR+MA (11편, 최대 4년): CHX 전처리는 유지율·술후 과민·2차 우식에서 대조군과 유의차 없음 (I²=0%).

실험실 MMP 억제 효과가 임상으로 전이 안 되는 이유 — 현대 접착제의 화학적·미세기계적 하이브리드층이 CHX 혜택을 상쇄.

임상 결론: 일상적 접착 프로토콜에서 CHX 불필요; 고위험 우식·항균 목적 시 여전히 선택 가능.

## Background

The hybrid layer (HL) is vulnerable to enzymatic degradation by matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and cysteine cathepsins activated during acid etching. CHX digluconate, with its MMP-inhibitory and substantive properties, has been proposed to preserve HL integrity and extend bond durability. Lab studies show CHX maintains bond strength for up to 10 years of accelerated ageing (Breschi et al.). However, clinical evidence has remained inconsistent, motivating this SR+MA.

**PICOS framework:**
- P: Patients receiving adhesive restorations in vital teeth
- I: Dentin pretreatment with CHX (solution or incorporated in adhesive)
- C: No CHX (conventional adhesive protocol)
- O: Restoration retention, postoperative sensitivity, secondary caries (≥6 months)
- S: RCTs and controlled clinical trials

## Methods

- **Search**: PubMed, Scopus, WoS, Cochrane; September 2025; PROSPERO CRD420251165239
- **11 studies** from 900 records; published 2005–2025
- **CHX concentrations**: 0.2% and 2% most common; application 15–60 s
- **Adhesives**: etch-and-rinse majority; some self-etch and universal
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB 2.0; most studies low to moderate risk
- **Meta-analysis**: random-effects; risk differences (RD) + 95% CI
- **Certainty**: GRADE approach

## Results

### Pooled Meta-analyses

| Outcome | Studies | RD (95% CI) | p | I² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Restoration retention | 9 (~833) | 0.01 (−0.02 to 0.04) | .53 | 0% |
| Postoperative sensitivity | 10 (~880) | 0.01 (−0.04 to 0.06) | .67 | 0% |
| Secondary caries | 4 (~302) | 0.00 (−0.03 to 0.03) | .80 | 0% |

Zero heterogeneity (I²=0%) across all outcomes — remarkable consistency.

### GRADE Evidence Summary

| Outcome | Certainty | Reason for downgrade |
|---|---|---|
| Retention (≥6 months) | ⊕⊕⊕⊝ Moderate | Imprecision (modest samples, few events) |
| Postoperative sensitivity | ⊕⊕⊕⊝ Moderate | Imprecision |
| Secondary caries | ⊕⊕⊝⊝ Low | Very serious imprecision (rare events, short follow-up) |

### Study Characteristics

- Follow-up range: 1 week to 4 years
- Most studies: ≥6 months
- Cavity types: Class II (posterior), Class V/NCCLs (mostly), Class I (children)
- One pediatric study (primary molars) — consistent with adult outcomes
- CHX application styles: post-etch cavity disinfectant (most common); incorporated into adhesive (Altıntop 2025)

## Clinical Significance

1. **CHX not required for routine adhesive protocols**: zero clinical benefit in retention, sensitivity, or caries prevention with modern adhesives
2. **Lab-clinic gap explained**: CHX applied to sound mineralized dentin has limited penetration → reduced MMP binding. Modern 10-MDP–containing adhesives form stable chemical bonds with HAp independently of CHX
3. **CHX can still be used for disinfection**: antimicrobial properties remain valid, especially in caries-risk patients or after caries removal
4. **Self-etch / universal adhesive evidence is thin**: most studies used etch-and-rinse systems. SE/universal adhesives are more susceptible to hydrolytic degradation, and CHX may be more relevant there — but evidence is insufficient
5. **Safety**: CHX pretreatment does not harm bond strength or compromise restoration performance

## Limitations

- Embase and grey literature not searched (limited access)
- 11 studies only — sample size modest
- CHX concentration/application time heterogeneity despite I²=0%
- 1 pediatric study (cannot generalize for primary teeth exclusively)
- Follow-up ≤4 years — uncertain long-term (>5 year) picture
- No direct comparison of CHX vs other MMP inhibitors (BAC, QAMs, proanthocyanidins)
