## Three-line Summary

Suh (2004) demonstrated in vitro that the oxygen-inhibited layer (OIL) is **not** necessary for adhesive bonding with composite resin — bond strengths were equivalent whether OIL was present or absent. Within the OIL, camphorquinone photoinitiator is depleted and degree of conversion is lower than control. Clinically, self-etching all-in-one adhesives produce an acidic OIL that is incompatible with self-cured or dual-cured resin cements without additional steps.

## 세줄요약

산소억제층 (Oxygen-Inhibited Layer, OIL)의 존재 여부가 레진 접착강도에 유의한 차이를 만들지 않음을 시험관 내 실험으로 입증한 선구적 연구. OIL 내에서는 광개시제 캄포퀴논 (Camphorquinone, CQ)이 분해·고갈되며 중합전환율 (Degree of Conversion, DoC)도 낮다. 임상적으로, 자가부식 올인원 접착제의 OIL은 산성이어서 자가중합 또는 이중중합 레진시멘트와 비호환성(incompatibility)을 유발한다.

## Background

The oxygen-inhibited layer is a soft, incompletely polymerized surface zone that forms on light-cured composites when atmospheric oxygen quenches free radicals. It had been assumed that this layer facilitates chemical bonding with subsequently placed composite increments. This study tested that assumption directly.

## Methods

Performed at Bisco Inc. (Schaumburg, IL):

1. **Shear bond strength**: BisCover and D/E Resin placed on Renew composite disks cured with or without OIL. Light-Bond composite cured on top in gelatin capsule; specimens soaked 2h at 37°C then sheared to failure (Instron Model 4466).
2. **Microtensile bond strength**: Renew A2 substrates cured in air (OIL present) or under nitrogen (OIL absent). Light-Core placed on each substrate, cured, sectioned into beams, stressed to failure.
3. **CQ decomposition**: Experimental camphorquinone resin irradiated at progressive intervals between glass plates; UV-Vis absorption spectra (Cary 50 Bio Spectrometer).
4. **Degree of conversion**: Mylar film strips coated with CQ resin pre-cured in air at varying intervals, post-cured 5 min at low intensity under nitrogen; FTIR (Spectrum 1000).

## Results

| Outcome | Finding |
|---|---|
| Shear bond strength (with vs without OIL) | No significant difference |
| Microtensile bond strength (air vs N₂ cure) | No significant difference |
| CQ in OIL after irradiation | Depleted / decomposed |
| Degree of conversion of OIL post-cured | **Lower** than control |

## Conclusions

- OIL is **not** necessary for bonding with composite resin.
- Composites bond equally well in the absence of OIL.
- OIL from self-etching all-in-one adhesives is **acidic** → inhibits the tertiary amine activator in self-cured composites → **incompatible** with self-cured or dual-cured resin cements without additional steps.

## Clinical Implications

| Adhesive type | OIL characteristic | Compatibility with self/dual-cure cement |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional (etch-and-rinse) | Near-neutral | Compatible |
| Self-etching all-in-one (simplest) | **Acidic** | **Incompatible** without extra step |

Options to restore compatibility: use a separate hydrophobic bonding resin over the primer, apply an oxygen-blocking layer, or choose a dual-cure–compatible adhesive.

## Limitations

- Single-laboratory study (Bisco Inc., manufacturer of tested products) — potential conflict of interest.
- Abstract-only source; full methods detail may be more nuanced in original article.
- In-vitro only; clinical correlation not established.
