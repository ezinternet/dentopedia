## Three-line Summary

In vitro experimental study — 14 extracted human premolars sectioned into 42 root-disc samples (21 per group), comparing push-out bond strength of prefabricated fiberglass posts cemented with TheraCem self-adhesive resin cement after intracanal NaOCl irrigant activation via mechanical rotary brush vs ultrasonic scaler.

Ultrasonic activation produced numerically higher mean push-out bond strength than mechanical activation (13.60±5.10 MPa vs 11.62±4.13 MPa), but the difference was not statistically significant (p=0.1745); both groups showed highest resistance in the apical third (ultrasonic apical 15.00±3.11 MPa, the highest value overall).

Clinically, the two NaOCl activation methods appear comparable for fiber-post bond strength within the limits of this small in-vitro study (n=14 teeth), though ultrasonic activation may be preferable when thorough apical-third canal cleaning is a clinical priority.

## Summary

This in vitro study from Universidad del Valle (Cali, Colombia) compared push-out bond strength of prefabricated glass fiber posts (DT Light-Post IllusionX-RO) cemented with TheraCem self-adhesive resin cement in root canals cleaned with 5% NaOCl activated either mechanically (rotary brush) or ultrasonically (piezoelectric scaler). Fourteen extracted premolars were endodontically prepared, randomly assigned to the two activation groups (7 teeth each), post-cemented, aged 30 days in 36°C water, and sectioned into cervical/middle/apical discs (42 total samples) for push-out testing. Ultrasonic activation gave a numerically higher mean bond strength than mechanical activation (13.60±5.10 vs 11.62±4.13 MPa), but two-factor ANOVA found the difference not statistically significant (p=0.1745). Both groups showed the greatest resistance in the apical third, with the single highest value of the study (15.00±3.11 MPa) in the ultrasonic-activated apical discs. Failure analysis showed adhesive failure predominating in both groups. The authors conclude the two activation methods are comparable for fiber-post bond strength within this study's limits, with ultrasonic activation potentially favored when apical-third canal cleaning is a clinical priority — an honest, non-overclaiming conclusion given the lack of statistical significance.

## Key Contributions
- First direct in-wiki comparison of NaOCl intracanal-cleaning activation method (mechanical vs ultrasonic) as a variable affecting fiber-post cementation bond strength, complementing prior work on adhesive-system class and post surface conditioning.
- Reinforces the apical-third bond-strength advantage seen elsewhere in the fiber-post literature, attributable to thinner cement film and greater post-to-dentin wall contact apically.
- Demonstrates TheraCem produces clinically adequate bond strength (>10 MPa in most subgroups) regardless of which NaOCl activation method precedes cementation, consistent with TheraCem's substrate-independent bonding profile reported by Mahrous 2020.

## Methodology
In vitro push-out bond strength study. 14 extracted human premolars, endodontically prepared (step-back technique, 5% NaOCl, gutta-percha/MTA Fillapex obturation), randomized to mechanical (Versa Brush, 10 s) or ultrasonic (Biosonic S1, 28±3 kHz, 20 s) NaOCl activation before TheraCem cementation of a DT Light-Post IllusionX-RO fiberglass post. Samples aged 30 days (36°C water), sectioned into cervical/middle/apical discs (21 samples/group, 42 total), tested on a universal testing machine (0.5 mm/min, 1 mm plunger); failure mode assessed by stereomicroscopy. Two-factor ANOVA (zone × activation) at alpha 0.05.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Overall push-out bond strength, mechanical | 11.62 ± 4.13 MPa |
| Overall push-out bond strength, ultrasonic | 13.60 ± 5.10 MPa |
| Statistical significance (mechanical vs ultrasonic) | **not significant**, p = 0.1745 |
| Cervical third, mechanical vs ultrasonic | 11.71 ± 1.92 vs 12.28 ± 4.33 MPa |
| Middle third, mechanical vs ultrasonic | 11.03 ± 4.98 vs 13.51 ± 7.35 MPa |
| Apical third, mechanical vs ultrasonic | 12.10 ± 5.25 vs 15.00 ± 3.11 MPa (highest overall) |
| Predominant failure mode | adhesive (dentin/post), both groups |
