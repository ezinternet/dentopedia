## Three-line Summary

Retrospective recall study of 460 composite restorations in noncarious cervical lesions (NCCLs) in 96 patients (mean age 60.9 y, mean restoration age 38.3 months), placed by undergraduate or postgraduate students and scored with modified USPHS criteria; factors tested by binary logistic regression.
Adhesive strategy and occlusal wear facets were the most influential factors: restoration loss was associated with wear facets (p=0.041) and with 2-step etch-and-rinse (2-E&R, p=0.043) or 2-step self-etch (2-SE, p=0.050); marginal integrity was the most frequent failure (gingival 50.7%, occlusal 42.4%); age, arch, tooth position/type and composite were not associated.
Selective enamel etching before a 2-SE adhesive had the lowest failure across parameters and wear-facet teeth failed more, but the retrospective design, operator-adhesive confounding (students chose different strategies) and absent odds ratios in the extracted text limit causal and quantitative conclusions.

## Summary

This retrospective recall study evaluated 460 composite restorations in noncarious cervical lesions (NCCLs) in 96 patients (mean age 60.9 years, mean restoration age 38.3 months) placed by undergraduate and postgraduate students. Two calibrated examiners scored retention, caries, marginal discoloration and marginal integrity with modified USPHS criteria, and binary logistic regression related failure to ten patient, tooth, operator and material factors. Occlusal wear facets and adhesive strategy were the most influential factors; restoration loss was associated with wear facets and with 2-step etch-and-rinse or 2-step self-etch adhesives, whereas selective enamel etching before Clearfil SE Bond had the lowest failure rates. Marginal integrity was the most frequent failure (gingival 50.7%, occlusal 42.4%). Clinically, wear-facet teeth and unetched enamel margins deserve attention when restoring cervical lesions, but operator-adhesive confounding and the retrospective design limit causal interpretation.

## Key Contributions

- Real-world, multi-operator NCCL failure-factor analysis (460 restorations), complementing single-operator RCTs.
- Occlusal wear facets linked to restoration loss (p=0.041), gingival partial retention loss (p=0.004), gingival marginal discoloration (p=0.002) and gingival marginal integrity failure (p<0.001).
- Adhesive strategy was the dominant extrinsic factor; selective enamel etching + 2-SE adhesive outperformed both 2-E&R and 2-SE alone on most parameters (per the authors' Discussion).
- Age, arch, tooth position, tooth type and composite type (96.5% nanofilled vs 3.5% microhybrid) were not associated with any failure parameter.

## Methodology

- Retrospective recall study (May-October 2018); modified USPHS criteria by two calibrated examiners (kappa 0.95 inter-, 0.96 intra-examiner); Bravo/Charlie = failure (restoration loss and caries: Charlie only).
- Teeth: 71.9% posterior, 28.1% anterior; lesions with occlusal enamel and gingival dentin margins, <=50% of tooth height, no proximal involvement; no liner/base.
- Adhesives: 2-E&R 40.2% (Adper Single Bond 2, Optibond Solo Plus, ExciTE F), 2-SE 20.2% (Clearfil SE Bond), selective enamel etching 39.6% (37% phosphoric acid on enamel + Clearfil SE Bond). Isolation by retraction cord, cotton rolls and saliva ejector.
- Factors: gender, age, arch, tooth position, tooth type, occlusal wear facet, caries risk (ADA), operator (undergraduate vs postgraduate), adhesive strategy, composite. Binary logistic regression, alpha 0.05; minimum n=371.

## Results

| Parameter | Associated factors (p) |
|---|---|
| Restoration loss (C) | wear facet 0.041; 2-E&R 0.043; 2-SE 0.050 |
| Partial retention loss, occlusal margin | 2-E&R 0.013 |
| Partial retention loss, gingival margin | gender 0.014; wear facet 0.004 |
| Marginal discoloration, occlusal | wear facet 0.047; 2-SE <0.001 |
| Marginal discoloration, gingival | gender 0.047; wear facet 0.002; 2-SE 0.008 |
| Marginal integrity, occlusal (failure 42.4%) | 2-E&R 0.028; 2-SE <0.001 |
| Marginal integrity, gingival (failure 50.7%) | gender 0.010; wear facet <0.001; high caries risk 0.035; moderate caries risk 0.001; operator 0.001 |
| Caries | no associated factor |

- Operator-strategy distribution: undergraduates 2-E&R 46.9% / selective etch 30.8% / 2-SE 22.3%; postgraduates selective etch 77% / 2-E&R 11.5% / 2-SE 11.5% - strategy and operator are confounded.
- Authors' reading: selective etching failed less than 2-E&R for restoration loss, partial retention loss and marginal integrity, and less than 2-SE for restoration loss, marginal discoloration and marginal integrity (mainly at the occlusal enamel margin); they attribute the 2-E&R gap partly to phosphoric acid on dentin versus the milder 10-MDP primer, and note 2-E&R is more prone to salivary contamination at the gingival margin.
- Odds ratios, confidence intervals, reference categories and the overall restoration-loss rate are not in the extracted full text (Tables 2-3 absent); directions come from the Discussion.

## Limitations

- Retrospective, no baseline lesion depth/sensitivity/sclerosis data; no survival (time-to-failure) analysis; restoration ages 1-10 years.
- Operator-adhesive confounding; product-level pooling (three E&R products vs one SE product); unbalanced composite groups.
- No multiplicity adjustment across many factors; wear facets recorded present/absent only; gender explanation speculative.
