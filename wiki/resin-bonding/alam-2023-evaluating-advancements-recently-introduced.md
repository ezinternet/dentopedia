## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (n=24 molars, 4 groups of 6) comparing Scotchbond Universal Plus (SP) to Scotchbond Universal (SB) in self-etch and etch-and-rinse modes for dentin bonding, oxygen inhibition layer (OIL) thickness, viscosity, hardness, elastic modulus, and degree of conversion (DC).
SP had significantly thinner OIL (12.2 ± 1.4 µm vs 35.4 ± 4.3 µm, p<0.001), lower viscosity (50.2 vs 115.5 mPa·s), higher hardness (329.6 vs 292.8 MPa), higher elastic modulus (7.7 vs 6.7 GPa), and higher DC (92.6% vs 81.4%); microtensile bond strength (µTBS) and survival probability were statistically equivalent between adhesives across both application modes.
Improved physicochemistry of the upgraded adhesive does not translate into superior dentin bond strength, suggesting that OIL thickness alone does not drive µTBS in this generation of universal adhesives.

## Summary

Alam et al. (2023) conducted an in vitro comparison of Scotchbond Universal Plus (SP, 3M) versus its predecessor Scotchbond Universal (SB, 3M) across two application modes (self-etch, SE; etch-and-rinse, ER) on mid-coronal human molar dentin (n=6/group, 24 molars total). The upgraded SP was engineered with a modified monomer system and photoinitiators intended to reduce the oxygen-inhibited layer (OIL) and improve polymerization efficiency.

**Primary finding:** Despite dramatically thinner OIL and superior physicochemical properties, SP produced equivalent µTBS and survival probability to SB under all tested conditions.

## Key Contributions

### OIL Thickness Reduction
SP achieved a 65% reduction in OIL thickness (12.2 ± 1.4 µm vs 35.4 ± 4.3 µm, p<0.001). The proposed mechanism: atmospheric oxygen has greater affinity for free radicals than methacrylate monomers → reacts to form a gel-like OIL that impedes monomer conversion. SP's modified photoinitiator and monomer system reduces this competition. However, the thinner adhesive layer in SP (ER: 2.9 µm vs SB 6.1 µm) may be more susceptible to OIL-driven inhibition per unit thickness, partially offsetting the absolute thickness benefit.

### Physicochemical Profile

| Outcome | SB | SP | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| OIL thickness (µm) | 35.4 ± 4.3 | 12.2 ± 1.4 | <0.001 |
| Viscosity (mPa·s) | 115.5 ± 0.6 | 50.2 ± 0.3 | <0.001 |
| Hardness (MPa) | 292.8 ± 31.5 | 329.6 ± 50.3 | 0.015 |
| Elastic modulus (GPa) | 6.7 ± 0.7 | 7.7 ± 1.1 | 0.001 |
| DC — Raman (%) | 81.4 ± 2.4 | 92.6 ± 0.4 | <0.001 |
| In-situ DC — NIR (%) | 86.4 ± 2.1 | 90.1 ± 1.7 | <0.001 |

### Bond Strength Equivalence
µTBS and Kaplan-Meier survival probability were statistically equivalent between SP and SB in both SE and ER modes (all p>0.05). Failure patterns were predominantly non-adhesive (mixed/cohesive) in both adhesives and both modes — no significant difference.

### Adhesive Layer Thickness (SEM)
SP produced a thinner adhesive layer: ER mode 2.9 ± 0.2 µm vs SB 6.1 ± 0.4 µm; SE mode 6.1 ± 0.8 µm vs SB 10.6 ± 2.7 µm. Lower viscosity of SP facilitates more uniform spreading and thinner film.

## Methodology

**Design:** In vitro, 4-group, blinded evaluator
**Substrate:** Mid-coronal dentin of 24 non-carious human molars (SiC grit-polished, standardized smear layer)
**Application modes:** SE (no acid etch) and ER (37% phosphoric acid, 15 s enamel / 15 s dentin)
**µTBS:** Stick specimens ≈1 mm² cross-section, tested at 0.5 mm/min crosshead speed after 24 h water storage
**OIL measurement:** SEM cross-section after air-curing without matrix strip (simulates clinical open-air curing)
**Viscosity:** Rotary rheometer
**Hardness / Elastic modulus:** Nanoindentation
**DC:** Raman spectroscopy (ex situ, fully cured)
**In-situ DC:** Near-infrared (NIR) spectroscopy during curing
**Statistical analysis:** ANOVA / Kruskal-Wallis; Breslow test for survival; α=0.05

## Limitations

- n=6 per group: limited statistical power for detecting small µTBS differences
- No aging (water storage, thermocycling) — short-term in vitro data only
- Only dentin substrate tested; no enamel bonding data
- No comparison with other universal adhesive brands
- Both adhesives from the same manufacturer (3M) — potential generalizability limits
