## Three-line Summary

Monocenter RCT (lesion-level randomization) (KU Leuven; 52 patients, 267 NCCLs, patient recall 63% and restoration recall 71% at 14 years) comparing the HEMA-free 1-step self-etch adhesive G-Bond (GB) with the 3-step etch-and-rinse adhesive Optibond FL (OFL) under the microhybrid composite Gradia Direct; evaluated by two blinded examiners at 6 months and 1, 2, 3, 5, 9 and 14 years with Vanherle criteria and a 2-way GEE model.

At 14 years the overall clinical success rate was 58.9% (GB) vs 57.9% (OFL) and retention 80.6% vs 80.4%, with no significant difference (p > 0.05); 79 restorations failed (39 GB, 40 OFL), mainly from unacceptable marginal deterioration, then retention loss. GB showed more enamel-side marginal discoloration (OR 0.356, p = 0.006) and more increased sensitivity (18.9% vs 6.8%, p = 0.01), which the authors attribute to wear and recession rather than the adhesive.

Failure and retention loss rose sharply in the last 5 years (success 80.3%/79.5% at 9 years), driven by patient medical decline and recurring abrasion/erosion/abfraction, and multiple restorations per patient gave worse results than two per patient; limitations are single center, two operators, a 37% patient dropout, older non-FDI criteria and a single adhesive pair.

## Summary
14-year RCT (52 patients, 267 NCCLs, 71% restoration recall) of the HEMA-free 1-step self-etch adhesive G-Bond versus the 3-step etch-and-rinse Optibond FL under a microhybrid composite. Success (58.9% vs 57.9%) and retention (80.6% vs 80.4%) did not differ. Marginal deterioration was the main failure cause, and late failures were driven by patient health decline and recurrent abrasion/erosion/abfraction.

## Key Contributions
- Longest published NCCL composite follow-up (authors' claim): 14 years, blinded examiners, GEE clustering.
- A mild 1-step self-etch matched the 3-step E&R gold standard clinically despite in-vitro predictions favoring E&R.
- Patient-level factors (medical decline, xerostomia, wear recurrence) and the number of restorations per patient strongly affect long-term NCCL success.

## Methodology
- RCT, 133 GB + 134 OFL Class-V restorations; two operators; recalls 6 months to 14 years; Vanherle criteria; 2-way GEE with Bonferroni correction.
- Patient recall 63%; earlier failures in non-recalled patients counted as failures.

## Results
- Success 58.9% (GB) vs 57.9% (OFL); failure OR 1.008 (0.536-1.896). Retention 80.6% vs 80.4%, OR 1.0025 (0.519-1.936).
- Severe marginal defect 14.5% vs 19.2%; deep marginal discoloration 18.2% vs 13.2%; margin caries 4 teeth (2 vs 2).
- GB: more enamel-side marginal discoloration (OR 0.356, p = 0.006) and increased sensitivity (18.9% vs 6.8%, p = 0.01), attributed to wear/recession.
- More failures in the mandible (p = 0.0058); OFL better on sclerotic dentin (p = 0.022). With two restorations per patient: retention 86% vs 88%.
- Limitations: 63% patient recall, single center, older criteria, dated materials; do not extrapolate to modern universal adhesives.
