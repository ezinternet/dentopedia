## Three-line Summary
3-year randomized, controlled, double-blind split-mouth trial (NCT03086720): 30 patients with at least 2 NCCLs; composite restorations placed after 37% phosphoric acid etching with either a placebo solution (control) or 10% NaOCl deproteinization (experimental), Adper Single Bond 2 + Filtek Z350; 64 restorations in 23 patients evaluated at 3 years (FDI criteria; Kaplan-Meier, log-rank).
Annual failure rate was 9% (control) vs 17.8% (10% NaOCl), a non-significant difference (p=0.077); restorations in the mandible (p=0.039) and in premolars (p=0.013) had significantly lower clinical survival, while number of teeth in the mouth was not associated (p=0.320).
10% NaOCl deproteinization after acid etching gave no additional retention benefit (the point estimate trended worse), so the extra step is not supported; lesion location and tooth type, not the adhesive pretreatment, tracked retention loss. Abstract-only record - full text not retrieved.

## Summary
This split-mouth, double-blind randomized trial (Pelotas, Brazil; NCT03086720) asked whether deproteinizing etched cervical dentin with 10% NaOCl improves the retention of composite restorations in noncarious cervical lesions. Thirty patients with at least two NCCLs were randomized by lesion to control (37% phosphoric acid, placebo solution, Adper Single Bond 2, Filtek Z350) or NaOCl (same, with 10% NaOCl before the adhesive); 64 restorations in 23 patients were evaluated at 3 years by FDI criteria. The annual failure rate was 9% in the control and 17.8% in the NaOCl group, which was not a statistically significant difference (p=0.077), so deproteinization offered no additional retention. Mandibular restorations (p=0.039) and premolars (p=0.013) showed significantly lower survival. Only the abstract was available, so details beyond it are not verified.

## Key Contributions
- Clinical evidence that the NaOCl deproteinization step does not improve NCCL composite retention after phosphoric acid etching, consistent with bench data.
- Numerically higher failure with NaOCl (AFR 17.8% vs 9%) though not significant, so no signal of benefit to offset the extra step.
- Retention risk stratification by tooth location: mandible and premolars fared worse regardless of treatment arm.

## Methodology
Randomized, controlled, split-mouth, double-blinded trial; 30 patients with at least 2 NCCLs; evaluation at baseline (1 week), 6, 12, 24 and 36 months by a calibrated examiner using FDI criteria; primary outcome retention; Kaplan-Meier and log-rank (alpha=0.05).

## Clinical insights
- Skipping NaOCl deproteinization after etching is supported by this trial; it adds a step and chair time without a retention benefit.
- When counselling on expected cervical restoration retention, tooth location (mandibular, premolar) matters more than this pretreatment variable in this cohort.
- Caveat: one etch-and-rinse adhesive was tested and the record is abstract-only; do not generalize to universal adhesives without further evidence.
