## Three-line Summary

A 6-year randomized double-blind RCT (140 NCCLs, 77 participants) found bulk-fill composite (Filtek Bulk Fill Posterior) achieved equivalent retention to nanofill (Filtek Z350 XT) at 85.7–91.4% across all groups (Log-rank p=0.961). Occlusogingival distance (OGD) of 1.5mm vs 3mm did not significantly influence clinical performance of either material. Clearfil SE Bond (2-step self-etch) provided acceptable long-term adhesion with both composites.

## Background

Non-carious cervical lesions (NCCLs) present challenges for resin composite restoration due to difficult moisture control, stress concentration at the gingival margin, and polymerization shrinkage effects on thin cervical walls. Bulk-fill composites were introduced to simplify the incremental technique, but long-term data in NCCLs — particularly stratified by lesion extension — were lacking prior to this study.

## Study Design

- **Type**: Randomized, parallel, double-blind clinical trial
- **n**: 77 participants, 140 NCCLs (35 per group)
- **Follow-up**: 6 years (66–94 months; extended due to COVID-19)
- **Recall rate**: 75.3% (19 dropouts)
- **Adhesive**: Clearfil SE Bond (2-step self-etch, all groups)
- **Outcome criteria**: FDI (World Dental Federation) clinical criteria

## Groups

| Group | OGD | Material | 6yr Retention |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5mm-C | 1.5 mm | Nanofill (Filtek Z350 XT) | 85.7% |
| 1.5mm-B | 1.5 mm | Bulk-fill (Filtek Bulk Fill Posterior) | 91.4% |
| 3mm-C | 3.0 mm | Nanofill (Filtek Z350 XT) | 91.4% |
| 3mm-B | 3.0 mm | Bulk-fill (Filtek Bulk Fill Posterior) | 91.4% |

Kaplan-Meier Log-rank: **p = 0.961** (no significant differences among groups)

## Key Findings

### Primary — Retention
- All groups achieved clinically acceptable retention at 6 years (85.7–91.4%)
- No statistically significant difference between bulk-fill and nanofill
- No statistically significant difference between OGD 1.5mm and OGD 3mm

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Marginal adaptation**: 1.5mm-B, 3mm-C, and 3mm-B showed increased marginal discrepancy at 6yr (p<0.01); 1.5mm-C (nanofill, shallow) remained stable — the lone exception
- **Marginal staining**: 41.42% minor staining at 6yr; no significant group differences
- **Surface texture**: 72 restorations rated Bravo at 6yr; no significant group differences
- **No caries recurrence, postoperative sensitivity, or anatomic form alteration** in any group at 6 years

## Clinical Implications

1. **Bulk-fill simplifies NCCL restoration without longevity penalty**: single-increment placement achieves equivalent 6-year outcomes, potentially reducing chair time and technique sensitivity.
2. **OGD (lesion extension) does not drive material choice**: both 1.5mm and 3mm lesions showed equivalent performance with both materials — clinicians need not preferentially select incremental composite for deeper NCCLs.
3. **Marginal adaptation decline is material- and depth-agnostic**: marginal discrepancy increases were present in 3 of 4 groups, suggesting this is an expected long-term finding in NCCL restorations rather than a bulk-fill-specific concern.
4. **2-step self-etch adhesive (Clearfil SE Bond) remains a reliable choice** for NCCL bonding with both conventional and bulk-fill composites at 6 years.

## Limitations

- Dropout rate of 24.7% (19/77) — potentially introduces attrition bias; authors used intention-to-treat analysis where possible
- Variable follow-up duration (66–94 months) due to COVID-19 disruptions
- Single adhesive system tested — generalizability to other adhesives not established
- Operator and setting not described in detail available

## Evidence Grade

**RCT** — randomized, parallel, double-blind; Kaplan-Meier survival analysis; FDI criteria; 6-year follow-up. High internal validity; limited by dropout rate.
