## Three-line Summary

A split-mouth RCT (n=63, 126 restorations, 18 months) found that airborne-particle abrasion (APA) with 50 μm Al₂O₃ as dentin pretreatment did not improve retention of universal adhesive + bulk-fill composite restorations in NCCLs. Retention was 96.7% (control) vs 90.3% (APA) at 18 months; HR=3.03 for failure was not significant (p=0.122). No benefit was seen for any secondary outcome.

## Background

Sclerotic dentin underlying NCCLs has reduced tubule patency and hypermineralized peritubular dentin, which may limit adhesive infiltration. Airborne-particle abrasion (APA) was proposed as a pretreatment to remove the sclerotic surface layer and improve adhesive bonding. This RCT from the Loguercio/Reis group directly tested this hypothesis in a clinical setting.

## Study Design

- **Design**: Prospective randomized split-mouth double-blind RCT
- **n**: 63 participants, 126 restorations (≥2 matched NCCLs per patient)
- **Follow-up**: Baseline, 6, 18 months
- **Criteria**: FDI + modified USPHS; primary outcome = retention/fracture
- **Isolation**: Rubber dam

## Interventions

| Group | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Control | Universal adhesive (selective enamel etching) + bulk-fill composite |
| APA | Same + APA with 50 μm Al₂O₃, 10 s, 5 bar |

## Key Results

| Outcome | Control | APA | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retention at 18m | 96.7% (92.4–100.0) | 90.3% (83.3–98.0) | — |
| Restorations lost | 1/63 | 5/63 | — |
| HR for failure | ref | 3.03 (0.74–12.37) | 0.122 |
| Marginal staining | ns | ns | ns |
| Marginal adaptation | ns | ns | ns |
| Postoperative sensitivity | ns | ns | ns |
| Secondary caries | ns | ns | ns |

## Clinical Implication

Air abrasion of sclerotic dentin in NCCLs does **not** improve restoration longevity at 18 months; the pretreatment step can be omitted from routine NCCL management. The APA group showed numerically lower retention and a 3-fold hazard ratio for failure (non-significant), suggesting no benefit and a possible (underpowered) trend toward harm.

## Limitations

- Short follow-up (18 months); APA differences may emerge at longer intervals
- Likely underpowered to confirm or exclude the observed HR=3.03 as clinically meaningful
- Single APA protocol tested; other particle sizes/pressures/durations not evaluated
- Loguercio/Reis group has strong prior literature on universal adhesives in NCCLs — results are consistent with their other publications
