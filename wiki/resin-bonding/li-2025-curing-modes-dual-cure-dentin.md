## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study (Front Bioeng Biotechnol 2025) comparing six dual-cure resin cements under light-curing versus self-curing at three aging time points (24 h, 3 months, 6 months) for µTBS to dentin; curing mode (F=1237.5), cement type (F=59.5), and storage time (F=97.9) were all significant factors.

Light-curing was the dominant variable — it significantly increased both immediate µTBS and long-term durability compared with self-curing across all six cements; Nexus 3 (N3) achieved the highest light-cured µTBS and the best durability overall; all cements degraded significantly over 6 months.

All dual-cure resin cements should be light-activated when access permits; self-curing alone is an inferior option that accelerates bond degradation, regardless of cement brand.

## Summary
In vitro study comparing six dual-cure resin cements under two curing modes (light-curing vs self-curing alone) at three time points (24h, 3 months, 6 months) for µTBS to dentin. Key finding: curing mode is the most significant variable — all dual-cure cements should be light-activated for optimal bond strength and durability.

## Key Contributions
- Curing mode: dominant factor (explains most variance in µTBS, F=1237.5)
- Light-curing: significantly higher µTBS AND slower degradation at 6 months
- N3 (Nexus 3 Universal): best overall — highest µTBS light-cured and best durability
- TheraCem (calcium silicate): benefits from light-curing like other cements
- Clinical mandate: always light-cure dual-cure cements when access allows
