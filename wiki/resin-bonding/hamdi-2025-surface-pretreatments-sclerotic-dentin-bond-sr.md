## Three-line Summary

SR (8 in vitro studies): surface pretreatments for sclerotic dentin in NCCLs — EDTA and NaOCl showed no benefit or inferior outcomes; prolonged phosphoric acid (37%, 15–90 s) alone or combined with sandblasting showed potential improvement.

Evidence quality is low with high risk of bias in most studies; no reliable guidelines can be established from current data.

Best current recommendation: pretreat sclerotic NCCL dentin with prolonged 37% phosphoric acid etching (±mechanical roughening) before composite bonding — while acknowledging the weak evidence base.

## Background

Sclerotic dentin in non-carious cervical lesions (NCCLs) is a clinically challenging bonding substrate. Unlike normal dentin, sclerotic dentin is:
- Hypermineralized (intratubular mineral deposits fill and occlude tubules)
- Surface layer dense and poorly permeable
- Collagen structure altered — reduced exposure for micromechanical bonding
- Less responsive to standard adhesive protocols → lower bond strength, higher failure rates

NCCLs are common (abrasion, erosion, abfraction) and restorations in this area have high clinical failure rates. Optimizing the surface pretreatment before adhesive application is critical.

## Methods

- **Design**: Systematic review; PRISMA-compliant; PROSPERO CRD420251102471
- **Databases**: PubMed/Medline, WoS, Scopus, Cochrane, Google Scholar
- **Population**: In vitro studies, cervical non-carious sclerotic dentin
- **Intervention**: Surface pretreatment before resin composite bonding
- **Outcome**: Bond strength (typically µTBS or SBS)
- **8 studies** met inclusion criteria

## Results by Pretreatment Agent

| Pretreatment | Effect vs Control | Evidence Quality | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EDTA** | No improvement or inferior | Low | Demineralization insufficient for sclerotic intratubular mineral |
| **NaOCl** | No improvement or inferior | Low | Protein-dissolving effect limited in hypermineralized substrate |
| **37% Phosphoric acid (prolonged, 15–90 s)** | Potential improvement | Low | Removes hypermineralized surface layer; reopens occluded tubules |
| **Sandblasting + phosphoric acid** | Potential improvement | Low | Mechanical roughening + chemical etching synergy |

**Inconsistency**: studies reported conflicting results even within the same pretreatment category.

## Clinical Significance

1. **Prolonged phosphoric acid etching is the best current evidence-based approach**: 15–90 s (vs standard 15 s) removes the hypermineralized sclerotic surface layer more effectively, re-exposing collagen fibers and opening occluded tubules
2. **EDTA and NaOCl not recommended for sclerotic NCCL**: failed to improve and may worsen bond outcomes
3. **Sandblasting + acid etching**: more preparation required but potentially superior — consider for cases where longer-lasting bond is priority (parafunctional patients, high stress areas)
4. **Standard protocols underperform on sclerotic dentin**: clinicians should recognize that standard 15-s etch time is often insufficient for NCCLs
5. **Laser pretreatments**: not covered by sufficient studies in this review — promising but evidence gap exists

## Limitations

- Only 8 studies — very small evidence base
- All in vitro — no clinical validation
- High risk of bias: lack of blinding, inadequate sample size calculations, no clinical condition simulation (thermocycling, loading, oral fluid)
- Inconsistent results even within same pretreatment type
- Laser pretreatment data absent
