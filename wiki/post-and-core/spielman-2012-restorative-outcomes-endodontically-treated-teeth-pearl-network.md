## Three-line Summary

This retrospective cohort study from the PEARL Network enrolled 1,298 patients across 64 general dental practices to evaluate the three- to five-year outcomes of definitive restorations placed after primary endodontic therapy, assessing factors associated with restorative success and failure.

Overall restorative failure rate was 13.9% (181/1,298; 95% CI, 12.1-15.8%), with 6.2% needing replacement, 4.4% replaced, and 3.4% presenting cracks or fractures. Multivariate analysis identified that molars, full-coverage restorations, presence of proximal contacts, good periodontal health, non-Hispanic ethnicity, specialist-performed endodontics, and older age were associated with restorative success.

These findings from private practice settings provide an evidence base to guide clinical decisions when planning restorations for endodontically treated teeth, highlighting the importance of full-coverage crowns and maintaining proximal contacts.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort study, conducted within the Practitioners Engaged in Applied Research and Learning (PEARL) Network, evaluated the three- to five-year outcomes of definitive restorations placed after primary endodontic therapy in general dental practices. A total of 1,298 eligible patients were enrolled from 64 practices across the northeastern United States. The overall restorative failure rate was 13.9% (181/1,298; 95% CI, 12.1-15.8%), with failure defined as tooth fracture, restoration replacement, or the clinical judgment that restoration needed replacement. Multivariate logistic regression identified several factors significantly associated with restorative success: full-coverage crowns (OR for failure 0.25; P < .01 compared to intracoronal restorations), presence of proximal contacts, molar tooth type, good periodontal health, non-Hispanic ethnicity, specialist-performed endodontic therapy, and older patient age. The authors concluded that these findings from private practice settings contribute to the evidence base for clinical decision-making in restoring endodontically treated teeth, with particular emphasis on the protective role of full-coverage restorations and the importance of maintaining proximal contacts.

## Key Contributions

- Established a 13.9% restorative failure rate at 3-5 years in general practice.
- Demonstrated that full-coverage crowns reduce failure risk by 75% compared to intracoronal restorations.
- Showed that teeth without proximal contacts have significantly higher failure risk.
- Identified specialist-performed endodontics as protective against restorative failure.
- Found that periodontal attachment loss increases restorative failure risk.
- Reported that Hispanic/Latino ethnicity was associated with higher failure rate (site effect possible).
- Provided practice-based evidence from 64 general dental practices.

## Methodology

This was a retrospective cohort study conducted within the PEARL Network, a dental practice-based research network. Practitioner-investigators (P-Is) from 64 general practices enrolled patients who had undergone primary endodontic therapy and definitive restoration in a permanent tooth three to five years previously. Patients were ≤70 years at the time of endodontic treatment. The index tooth was the earliest-treated tooth in the specified period. Exclusion criteria included incomplete apices, abutments for removable partial dentures, third molars, and active orthodontic treatment. Data collection included demographic information, clinical examination (percussion, restoration status, proximal contacts, cracks/fractures, caries, clinical attachment level), periapical radiographs, and patient-reported outcomes (tooth sensitivity, Oral Health Impact Profile). Radiographs were evaluated by masked reviewers (periodontist and endodontist) using the periapical index (PAI score ≥3). Restorative failure was defined as tooth cracked/fractured, restoration replaced, or restoration judged to need replacement. Multivariate logistic regression with backward elimination (P = .05) was used to identify factors associated with restorative failure.
