## Three-line Summary
In-vitro nondestructive occlusal-loading study on 42 extracted intact maxillary second bicuspids, comparing cuspal stiffness loss across sequential endodontic and restorative procedures (access, instrumentation, obturation, occlusal/two-surface/MOD cavity preparation).
Endodontic procedures alone reduced relative cuspal stiffness by only ~5%, versus ~20% for an occlusal cavity preparation and ~63% for an MOD cavity preparation — the largest losses tracked with loss of marginal ridge integrity, not with the endodontic steps.
Clinically foundational finding: intact endodontic treatment does not meaningfully weaken a tooth that retains its marginal ridges — the restorative cavity design, not root canal therapy, is the dominant driver of cuspal weakening; note this page is built from the abstract only (full text not retrieved).

발치된 상악 제2소구치 42개를 대상으로 비파괴 교합하중 (nondestructive occlusal loading) 시험을 시행하여, 근관치료 각 단계 (접근와동 형성, 근관성형, 충전)와 수복 와동 (교합면·2면·MOD 와동) 형성이 교두 강성 (cuspal stiffness)에 미치는 영향을 순차적으로 비교한 시험관내 (in-vitro) 연구.

## Summary
This is the landmark Reeh, Messer & Douglas (1989) in-vitro biomechanical study establishing that restorative cavity design, not endodontic treatment itself, is the primary driver of cuspal stiffness loss in posterior teeth. Using bonded strain gauges on 42 extracted intact maxillary second bicuspids under nondestructive occlusal loading, the authors measured relative cuspal stiffness before and after each of a sequence of endodontic and restorative procedures. Endodontic access, instrumentation, and obturation together reduced stiffness by only ~5%, while an occlusal cavity preparation alone caused ~20% loss and an MOD cavity preparation caused ~63% loss — by far the largest single reduction observed, and one clearly tied to loss of marginal ridge integrity rather than to the endodontic steps. The clinical implication, still widely cited today, is that "endodontically treated teeth are weak" is more precisely "MOD-prepared teeth are weak" — treatment planning for post-endodontic restoration should weigh remaining marginal ridge structure at least as heavily as the fact of root canal treatment. ## Key Contributions
- Isolated the strength contribution of each individual endodontic and restorative step on the same tooth via sequential nondestructive strain-gauge measurement, rather than pooling destructive fracture-test results across different tooth groups.
- Quantified the now-canonical benchmark figures (~5% endodontic-only loss, ~20% occlusal-cavity loss, ~63% MOD-cavity loss) that later post-and-core and restorative literature routinely cites as the rationale for conservative cavity design and marginal-ridge preservation.
- Reframed the causal narrative for post-endodontic tooth fracture from "loss of pulp vitality/moisture weakens the tooth" toward "loss of coronal structure (marginal ridges) weakens the tooth."

## Methodology
In-vitro nondestructive biomechanical experiment; n=42 extracted intact maxillary second bicuspids. Encapsulated strain gauges were bonded on enamel just above the cementoenamel junction (buccal and lingual), teeth were mounted in nylon rings with 2 mm of root exposed, and each tooth was loaded at 37 N/s for 3 s and unloaded at the same rate in a closed-loop servo-hydraulic system. A stress-strain curve was generated before and after each procedure. Teeth were assigned to one of two sequential procedure series: (1) unaltered → access → instrumentation → obturation → MOD prep; or (2) unaltered → occlusal prep → two-surface prep → MOD prep → access → instrumentation → obturation.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Endodontic procedures alone (access + instrumentation + obturation) | ~5% reduction in relative cuspal stiffness |
| Occlusal cavity preparation alone | ~20% reduction in relative cuspal stiffness |
| MOD cavity preparation | ~63% average reduction in relative cuspal stiffness (largest single loss) |
| Driver of largest stiffness losses | Loss of marginal ridge integrity, not the endodontic steps |
