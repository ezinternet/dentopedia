## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 25 in vitro studies evaluating fiber-reinforced composite (FRC) as core material for post-endodontic restoration, comparing fracture resistance against other core materials and intact teeth.

FRC core materials increase fracture resistance of endodontically treated teeth; polyethylene and short fiber-reinforced composites outperform glass FRC; retention slots and occlusal-third placement further amplify resistance.

FRC does not match intact tooth fracture resistance; fractures are predominantly favorable (enamel-dentin level) and repairable, reducing catastrophic failure risk.

## 세줄요약

근관충전 후 코어 재료로서의 섬유보강 복합레진 (Fiber-Reinforced Composite, FRC)의 파절저항성을 평가한 25개 체외 연구의 체계적 문헌고찰.

FRC 코어는 근관치료 치아의 파절저항성을 향상시키며, 폴리에틸렌 섬유 (Polyethylene Fiber) 및 단섬유 복합레진 (Short Fiber-Reinforced Composite)이 유리섬유 FRC보다 우수; 유지홈 (Retention Slot) 및 교합면 상부 배치로 파절저항성 추가 증가.

FRC는 건전 치아 수준의 파절저항성에 도달하지 못하나, 대부분의 파절이 호의적(수복 가능)이어서 재앙적 파절 위험을 줄임.

## Summary

This systematic review synthesized evidence from 25 in vitro studies on fiber-reinforced composite (FRC) as a core build-up material for endodontically treated teeth. Searching five databases (PubMed, EBSCO Host, Scopus, Google Scholar, Hinari) for studies published 2000–2019, the authors found that FRC cores consistently increased fracture resistance compared to unreinforced composite and amalgam cores, but none of the material configurations restored teeth to the fracture resistance of intact controls. Among fiber subtypes, polyethylene-fiber and short fiber-reinforced composites (e.g., Ever X Posterior) provided superior mechanical performance relative to glass fiber variants, attributed to the monoblock stress-distribution effect of polyethylene fibers. Design factors — specifically retention slots and placement within the occlusal third — further boosted fracture resistance. The predominance of favorable, repairable fracture modes (at or coronal to the enamel-dentin junction) over catastrophic root fractures supports the clinical viability of FRC as a conservative core strategy.

## Key Contributions

- Established FRC as a superior core material over unreinforced composites and amalgam for fracture resistance of endodontically treated teeth, based on pooled in vitro evidence
- Identified polyethylene and short fiber-reinforced composites as mechanically superior to glass FRC, attributed to the monoblock effect and stress redistribution along the tooth's long axis
- Quantified design optimization factors: retention slots and occlusal-third placement significantly increase fracture resistance independent of fiber type
- Characterized fracture modes — predominantly favorable (adhesive and enamel-dentin fractures) rather than catastrophic — supporting clinical repairability after FRC core failure
- Highlighted the ceiling effect: even optimized FRC configurations do not restore fracture resistance to intact-tooth levels, setting realistic expectations for core material selection

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review without meta-analysis
- **Databases**: PubMed, EBSCO Host, Scopus, Google Scholar, Hinari, plus manual library search
- **Search Period**: 1 January 2000 – 30 November 2019
- **Inclusion**: In vitro studies on extracted human teeth evaluating FRC as core material for fracture resistance via universal testing machine
- **Exclusion**: Reviews, abstracts, case reports, letters to editor, in vivo studies, pre-2000 studies
- **Selection**: 157 identified → 55 after title screening → 27 after duplicate removal → 26 full texts assessed → 25 included
