## Three-line Summary
This systematic review (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42022287217; 11 studies from 1707 screened) compared survival and success rates of endodontically treated posterior teeth restored with full veneer crowns versus full cuspal coverage onlays.
Onlay survival was 95–100% (2yr) / 90.7–100% (3yr) and success 86.6–96.6% (2yr) / 86.6–96% (3yr); full veneer crown survival was 87.8% (>2yr) / 95.1% (3yr) / 84–97.73% (5–10yr) and success 91.11–92.64% (5yr) / 60% (6yr) — overlapping ranges, no meta-analysis possible (narrative synthesis only), 3/11 studies at serious risk of bias.
The authors conclude onlays are favourable over full veneer crowns in the short-to-midterm for restoring endodontically treated posterior teeth, given comparable survival with substantially less tooth-structure removal.

## Summary
This PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered systematic review (CRD42022287217) from the University of Edinburgh compares full veneer crowns against full cuspal coverage onlays for restoring endodontically treated posterior teeth. Searching MEDLINE/EMBASE (Ovid), PubMed, and Cochrane CENTRAL from 1946 to April 2022, supplemented by Google Scholar citation chaining, the authors screened 1707 records and included 11 in-vivo studies (RCTs, prospective and retrospective cohorts). Because the included studies used heterogeneous preparation protocols and restorative materials, meta-analysis was not possible and results were synthesized narratively. Onlay survival (95–100% at 2yr, 90.7–100% at 3yr) and full veneer crown survival (87.8% at >2yr, 95.1% at 3yr) overlapped substantially at comparable timepoints, and no included study reported onlay outcomes beyond 3 years, leaving long-term comparison unresolved. The authors concluded that, given comparable short-to-midterm survival combined with substantially less tooth-structure removal, onlays are a favourable alternative to full veneer crowns for restoring endodontically treated posterior teeth — while cautioning that 9/11 studies carried moderate and 3/11 serious risk of bias, and that most included studies pooled post and non-post restorations, confounding the crown-vs-onlay comparison.

## Key Contributions
- First SR to frame the endodontically-treated-posterior-tooth final-restoration question specifically as full veneer crown vs full cuspal coverage onlay (as opposed to crown vs direct composite, or a broader all-posterior-teeth onlay-vs-crown scope).
- Tabulates survival/success at 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, and 12-year timepoints across 11 primary studies, exposing that no study follows onlays beyond 3 years — a clear long-term evidence gap.
- Identifies retrievability as a secondary consideration beyond raw survival/success: onlay failures were predominantly material fracture and often repairable in place, while crown failures included root fracture, post/core fracture, and crown dislodgement.
- Names post/non-post pooling within most included studies as a confound limiting a clean crown-vs-onlay comparison.

## Methodology
PRISMA-guided systematic review, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42022287217), PICOS-framed (Population: endodontically treated posterior teeth, ages 13–99; Intervention: full cuspal coverage onlay; Comparison: full veneer crown; Outcome: survival/success; Studies: RCTs, prospective and retrospective cohorts). Search of MEDLINE/EMBASE via Ovid, PubMed, and Cochrane CENTRAL (8 April 2022) plus Google Scholar forward-citation chaining, screened by two independent reviewers (κ=1 via Cohen's kappa). 1707 records → 19 sought for retrieval → 11 included; 8 excluded (listed with reasons) and 3 additional non-English studies discussed narratively but excluded from quantitative synthesis (translation unavailable). Risk of bias assessed with ROBINS-I (cohort studies) and RoB-2 (RCTs), visualized via ROBVIS; quality cross-checked with CASP checklists for cohort studies and RCTs. Meta-analysis was not attempted due to protocol/material heterogeneity — narrative synthesis only.
