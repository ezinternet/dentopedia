## Three-line Summary

This critical review surveys current evidence on adhesive approaches for restoring endodontically treated teeth (ETT) with and without extensive coronal tissue loss, examining partial versus full coverage, ferrule effect, post type influence on catastrophic failures, and postless alternatives including endocrowns and postless build-ups.

Preservation of remaining tooth structure is paramount; the presence of a 1.5-2 mm ferrule improves fracture resistance, while partial restorations show comparable survival to full crowns (94-100% vs 91.9-100%), and randomized trials with PFM crowns demonstrate post placement improves retention but adhesive approaches with bonded ceramics may render posts unnecessary.

Clinical evidence remains contradictory: in vitro studies favor postless approaches with bonded ceramic crowns to reduce catastrophic failures, but RCTs using PFM crowns show higher success with posts (84% cast vs 95% fiber post over 4 years), highlighting that restoration type (cemented vs bonded) critically influences outcomes and more clinical studies on modern adhesive postless techniques are needed.

## 세줄요약

본 종설은 근관치료된 치아 (Endodontically Treated Teeth, ETT)의 접착 수복에 관한 현재 지식을 조사하였으며, 부분 피개 대 완전 피개, 페룰 효과, 포스트 종류가 치명적 파절에 미치는 영향, 엔도크라운 및 포스트 없는 코어 축성 (postless build-up)과 같은 무포스트 대안을 검토하였다.

잔존 치아 구조의 보존이 가장 중요하며, 1.5-2 mm 페룰의 존재는 파절 저항성을 향상시키고, 부분 수복물은 완전관과 유사한 생존율을 보였으며 (94-100% 대 91.9-100%), PFM 크라운을 사용한 무작위 임상시험에서는 포스트 식립이 유지력을 향상시켰으나 접착성 세라믹을 사용한 접근법에서는 포스트가 불필요할 수 있음을 시사하였다.

임상 근거는 여전히 상반된데, 시험관 연구는 접착성 세라믹 크라운과 무포스트 접근법이 치명적 파절을 감소시킨다고 보는 반면, PFM 크라운을 사용한 RCT는 포스트 식립이 더 높은 성공률을 보였으며 (4년간 주조포스트 84% 대 섬유포스트 95%), 이는 수복물 유형 (시멘트형 대 접착형)이 결과에 결정적 영향을 미침을 강조하며, 현대 접착 무포스트 기법에 관한 추가 임상 연구가 필요함을 시사한다.

## Summary

This critical review examines current adhesive approaches for restoring endodontically treated teeth (ETT), focusing on the tension between traditional post-and-core methods and emerging postless alternatives. The authors synthesize evidence from in vitro studies, clinical trials, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses to evaluate partial versus full coverage, the ferrule effect, post type influences on catastrophic failures, and techniques such as endocrowns and postless build-ups. Key findings indicate that preservation of remaining tooth structure is paramount, with 1.5-2 mm ferrule significantly improving fracture resistance and partial restorations showing comparable survival to full crowns (94-100% vs 91.9-100%). However, clinical evidence remains contradictory: in vitro studies favor postless approaches with bonded ceramic crowns to reduce catastrophic root fractures, while randomized clinical trials using cemented PFM crowns demonstrate that posts improve retention (up to 50% crown dislodgment without posts) and survival (84% cast vs 95% fiber post at 4 years). The authors conclude that restoration type critically influences outcomes—cemented versus bonded—and that modern adhesive postless techniques require more clinical studies to resolve the current evidence gap, though preliminary data on endocrowns (90-99% survival) are promising.

## Key Contributions

- Preservation of coronal tooth structure is paramount for ETT survival; 1.5-2 mm ferrule significantly improves fracture resistance.
- Partial adhesive restorations show comparable survival to full crowns (94-100% vs 91.9-100%) for ETT with minimal to moderate tissue loss.
- Post placement does not improve fatigue resistance of posterior teeth with ferrule and is associated with more catastrophic root fractures in vitro.
- Endocrowns represent a promising conservative alternative for posterior ETT (90-99% survival in short-to-medium term).
- RCTs with PFM crowns show posts improve survival (84% cast vs 95% fiber post at 4 years), but studies with bonded ceramic crowns favor postless approaches.
- Restoration type (cemented PFM vs bonded all-ceramic) critically influences stress distribution and outcomes.
- Immediate dentin sealing (IDS) is essential for optimizing bond strength in postless approaches.

## Methodology

This is a critical narrative review rather than a systematic review. The authors surveyed the current literature on adhesive approaches for restoring endodontically treated teeth, drawing from in vitro studies, finite element analyses, prospective and retrospective clinical studies, randomized clinical trials, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses published primarily between 1989 and 2018. The review examines four key areas: partial versus full coverage restorations, the ferrule effect and its impact on survival, post type and its association with catastrophic versus repairable failures, and postless alternatives including endocrowns and postless build-ups. No formal systematic search strategy, PRISMA protocol, or quantitative synthesis was performed; instead, the authors critically evaluate and synthesize findings from the included studies, highlighting inconsistencies between in vitro and clinical evidence, particularly regarding the necessity of posts and the influence of restoration type (cemented PFM versus bonded ceramic) on outcomes.
