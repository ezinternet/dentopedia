## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (AMSTAR2-appraised; 14 SR/MA pooling 118 studies, >10,971 samples) of prosthetic restoration success/survival/failure on endodontically treated (ET) teeth.

Single crowns and endocrowns showed the most reliable outcomes (endocrown 5-yr success ~94% in some analyses vs conventional crown 5-yr survival ~98.3%); fiber posts had a higher success rate than metal posts in low-risk-of-bias reviews, while root-fracture rates between fiber and metal posts were similar; fixed/removable prostheses success (78%/66%) trailed single crowns.

Only 9 of 14 included S/M-R were low risk of bias (41.5%), and marked heterogeneity across pooled studies means the umbrella review's conclusions (favoring single crowns/endocrowns and fiber posts) should be treated as moderate-confidence, hypothesis-supporting evidence rather than definitive practice guidance.

## 세줄요약

우산리뷰(umbrella review, AMSTAR2 평가; SR/MA 14편, 원저 118편·표본 10,971명 초과 통합) — 근관치료치아(Endodontically Treated Teeth, ET teeth) 보철 수복의 성공률·생존률·실패율 종합.

단일크라운(single crown)과 엔도크라운(endocrown)이 가장 안정적 결과를 보였고(엔도크라운 5년 성공률 약 94% vs 통상 크라운 5년 생존율 약 98.3%), 저위험편향(low risk of bias) 리뷰에서는 섬유포스트(fiber post)가 금속포스트(metal post)보다 성공률이 높았으며 치근 파절률(root fracture rate)은 두 재료 간 유사, 고정성/가철성 보철물의 성공률(78%/66%)은 단일크라운에 못 미쳤다.

포함된 SR/MA 14편 중 저위험편향은 9편(41.5%)에 불과하고 연구 간 이질성(heterogeneity)이 커, 단일크라운·엔도크라운·섬유포스트를 선호하는 결론은 확정적 임상지침이 아니라 중등도 신뢰도의 가설 지지 근거로 받아들여야 한다.

## Summary

This umbrella review addresses the persistent lack of consensus on how best to restore endodontically treated (ET) teeth by systematically collecting and appraising the SR/MA layer itself. From 43 candidate systematic/meta-analytic reviews (S/M-R) identified through PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane, and Google Scholar (search through November 2020), 14 SR/MA covering 118 underlying primary studies and more than 10,971 pooled samples were included. Rather than re-pooling effect sizes, the authors organized the constituent evidence into three narrative buckets — general prosthetic-restoration success, single-crown success with vs without posts (and by post type), and failure-mode analysis — and separately graded each constituent SR/MA's own risk of bias using AMSTAR2 (16 items; low risk 8–11 positive, moderate 4–7, high ≤3). Nine of the 14 SR/MA (41.5% of the broader survey pool) were rated low risk of bias and treated as the strongest clinical evidence.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the field's highest-tier synthesis (umbrella review of SR/MA) specifically on prosthetic restoration outcomes for ET teeth, distinct from prior SR/MA that each addressed only one variable (post material, ferrule, endocrown vs crown, etc.).
- Applies formal AMSTAR2 bias grading to the *included reviews themselves*, identifying which of the pre-existing SR/MA in this space are trustworthy (9/14 low risk) vs only moderately reliable (5/14).
- Cross-validates several individually-studied clinical questions by pooling narrative conclusions across independently conducted SR/MA: ferrule effect on survival, fiber-vs-metal post success/failure, endocrown vs conventional crown, and single crown vs fixed/removable prostheses.
- Flags a systemic reporting gap (funding-source disclosure) across the field's SR/MA layer, a methodological signal for future review authors.

## Methodology

PRISMA-guided umbrella review. PICO: Population = root-canal-treated teeth; Intervention = prosthetic restoration; no comparator specified; Outcomes = success rate, survival rate, post-endodontic failure rate. Search of PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane, and Google Scholar through November 2020 without language restriction yielded 43 candidate S/M-R (36 PubMed/MEDLINE, 5 Cochrane, 2 manual search); title/abstract/eligibility screening narrowed this to 17, and full-text review to the final 14 included SR/MA. Three independent trained reviewers extracted prosthesis type, success rate, and survival rate (inter-rater kappa = 1.0), with disagreements resolved by discussion or a fourth reviewer. Risk of bias in the 14 included SR/MA was assessed with AMSTAR2 (16 questions) by two reviewers (kappa = 0.9), third reviewer as tiebreaker.

## Results

- **Bias grading**: 9/14 SR/MA low risk of bias, 5/14 moderate risk, none high risk after full-text screening; 41.5% of the initial survey pool were low-risk.
- **General success rate** (3 SR/MA, 22 studies): endocrown 5-yr success ~77% vs conventional crown ~94% in one study; single crown 92%, fixed prosthesis 79%, removable prosthesis 66% (Sedrez-Porto/Ploumaki).
- **Single crown with/without post** (3 SR/MA, 34 studies): no-post 94%, with-post 92%, cast post-and-core 93%, prefabricated post 94% success (Ploumaki); fiber/elastic post success 71.8–100% (Sarkis-Onofre); prefabricated posts favored over metal posts clinically (45.8% vs 16.7%, Girotto).
- **Survival, single crowns** (8 SR/MA, 73 studies): endocrown 91.4% vs conventional crown 98.3% 5-yr survival (Al-Dabbagh); crown 94% vs composite resin 91% survival (Suksaphar); crown 81% vs direct restoration 63% 10-yr survival (Stavropoulou & Koidis); fiber posts significantly outsurvive metal posts, especially with >2 missing coronal walls (Wang); metal post 90% vs fiber post 83.9% survival (Figueiredo); cast post-and-core survival 87.2–88.1% (Heydecke & Peters); ferrule preserves cavity walls and raises survival (Naumann); no-ferrule teeth show wide survival variance 0–97% vs with-ferrule teeth (Sarkis-Onofre).
- **Failure rate** (6 SR/MA, 48 studies): fiber vs metal post root-fracture and post-debonding rates similar (Wang, Figueiredo); prefabricated metal + carbon-fiber posts show double the root-fracture rate of metal + fiberglass posts (Figueiredo); post-debonding is the leading failure mode for single crowns and fixed prostheses (Ploumaki, Sorrentino); endocrown failure rate higher than conventional methods (Sedrez-Porto).
- **Bottom line**: single crowns (and, in the endocrown-specific subset, endocrowns) show the most consistently favorable outcomes; among posts, fiber posts edge out metal posts on success rate with comparable fracture risk; heterogeneity across the pooled evidence is substantial.
