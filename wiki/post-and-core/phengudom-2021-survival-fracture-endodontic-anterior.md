## Three-line Summary

This retrospective cohort study evaluated 263 endodontically treated anterior teeth (157 restored with direct resin composite, 106 with full-coverage crowns) from dental records of patients treated between 2007-2019 at Mahidol University, Thailand, with a mean recall period of 38 months, comparing survival rates from unrestorable fracture and identifying predisposing factors using Kaplan-Meier and Cox proportional-hazard models.

The survival rate of teeth restored with full-coverage crowns was significantly higher (99.1%) than those with direct resin composite (90.4%) (P<0.01). Significant predisposing factors for unrestorable fracture in the resin composite group included less than three walls of remaining cervical tooth structure (HR: 8.95; 95% CI: 1.43-56.01), root dentin thickness-to-canal width ratio <1:1:1 (HR: 12.98; 95% CI: 1.55-108.73), loss of posterior-teeth support (HR: 9.05; 95% CI: 1.68-48.77), and parafunctional habits (HR: 19.37; 95% CI: 2.25-166.71).

For anterior teeth without these risk factors, direct resin composite restorations showed survival rates comparable to crowns. The presence of palatal cervical tooth structure in maxillary anterior teeth significantly improved survival, suggesting that resin composite can be a successful restoration in selected cases, though long-term adhesive stability requires further investigation.

## 세줄요약

이 후향적 코호트 연구는 태국 마히돈 대학에서 2007~2019년 사이에 치료받은 263개의 근관치료된 전치(직접 레진 수복 157개, 완전피개관 106개)를 평균 38개월간 추적 관찰하여, 회복불가능한 파절의 생존율을 비교하고 위험인자를 Kaplan-Meier 및 Cox 비례위험모형으로 분석하였다.

완전피개관의 생존율(99.1%)이 직접 레진 수복(90.4%)보다 유의하게 높았다(P<0.01). 직접 레진 수복군에서 회복불가능한 파절의 유의한 위험인자는 경부 치질 구조물이 3개 미만인 경우(위험비: 8.95; 95% CI: 1.43-56.01), 치근 상아질 두께 대 근관 폭 비율이 1:1:1 미만(위험비: 12.98; 95% CI: 1.55-108.73), 후치 지지 상실(위험비: 9.05; 95% CI: 1.68-48.77), 그리고 이갈이 습관(위험비: 19.37; 95% CI: 2.25-166.71)이었다.

이러한 위험인자가 없는 치아에서는 직접 레진 수복이 완전피개관과 비슷한 생존율을 보였다. 상악 전치에서 구개측 경부 치질 구조물의 존재가 생존율을 유의하게 향상시켰으며, 이는 선택된 증례에서 직접 레진 수복이 성공적일 수 있음을 시사하나, 장기적 접착 안정성에 대한 추가 연구가 필요하다.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort study from Mahidol University, Thailand, evaluated survival rates from unrestorable fracture in endodontically treated anterior teeth restored with either direct resin composite (DRC) or full-coverage crowns (FCC). A total of 263 teeth (157 DRC, 106 FCC) from patients treated between 2007-2019 were analyzed, with a mean follow-up of 38 months (range 12-144 months). Overall survival was 93.9%, with FCC showing significantly higher survival (99.1%) compared to DRC (90.4%) (P<0.01). Multivariate Cox regression identified four significant risk factors for unrestorable fracture in DRC-restored teeth: fewer than three remaining cervical tooth structure walls (HR 8.95), cervical root dentin thickness-to-canal width ratio <1:1:1 (HR 12.98), loss of posterior teeth support (HR 9.05), and parafunctional habits (HR 19.37). Importantly, DRC-restored teeth without these risk factors demonstrated survival rates comparable to crowns. Subgroup analysis of maxillary anterior teeth revealed that preservation of palatal cervical tooth structure was associated with significantly higher survival (95.8%) compared to teeth without palatal structure (78.6%), comparable to teeth with complete cervical structure (94.5%). The authors conclude that while crowns provide superior overall survival, resin composites can be successfully used in selected cases without identified risk factors, and recommend randomized controlled trials to confirm these retrospective findings.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the first clinical evidence comparing survival rates of anterior endodontically treated teeth restored with DRC versus FCC, showing significantly better survival with crowns (99.1% vs 90.4%).
- Identifies four key clinical risk factors for unrestorable fracture in DRC-restored anterior teeth: insufficient cervical tooth structure (<3 walls), inadequate cervical root dentin thickness (ratio <1:1:1), loss of posterior support, and parafunctional habits.
- Demonstrates that DRC restorations can achieve crown-comparable survival rates (94.3% vs 99.1%) when none of these risk factors are present, offering a conservative treatment option.
- Establishes the clinical importance of palatal cervical tooth structure preservation in maxillary anterior teeth, with palatal wall retention providing similar protection to complete cervical structure.
- Provides practical clinical guidelines for restoration selection based on objective risk factors rather than restoration type alone.
- Highlights the need for long-term studies on adhesive degradation in resin composite restorations.

## Methodology

This retrospective cohort study analyzed dental records and radiographs of patients who received endodontic treatment at the Endodontic Postgraduate Clinic, Faculty of Dentistry, Mahidol University, Thailand, between 2007 and 2019. Eligible teeth were mature anterior teeth restored with single-unit direct resin composite or full-coverage crown, with an occluding pair and at least 12-month recall. Exclusion criteria included preoperative root resorption, crack, or fracture; veneer restorations; bone loss >50% of root length; history of post removal before retreatment; procedural errors; and ongoing orthodontic treatment. A total of 263 teeth from 263 patients (155 female, 108 male; mean age 56±15.5 years) were included, with 157 DRC and 106 FCC restorations. Data collection included age, gender, tooth type and location, restoration surfaces, remaining cervical tooth structure walls, posterior support, adjacent teeth, post type and length, cervical root dentin thickness-to-canal width ratio, and parafunctional habits. Outcomes were survival from unrestorable fracture (tooth extraction due to extensive destruction). Statistical analysis used Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, log-rank test for comparisons, and Cox proportional-hazard multivariate model for identifying risk factors, with significance set at P<0.05. Subgroup analysis examined the effect of remaining cervical tooth structure sites on survival of maxillary anterior teeth using Cox regression.
