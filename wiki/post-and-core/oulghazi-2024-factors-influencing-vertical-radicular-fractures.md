## Three-line Summary
Scoping review (PubMed/Medline + Scopus 2013–2023, English/French; 1,464 records screened, 18 studies included: 8 labeled FEA, 4 laboratory experimental, 3 in vitro, 2 retrospective, 1 RCT) of factors behind sub-prosthetic vertical root fracture (VRF) in teeth restored with metallic post-and-core.
Key findings: maxillary premolars and mandibular molars are most predisposed; a 2 mm ferrule significantly raises fracture resistance (FEA); high-modulus alloys transmit nearly all stress to dentin; resin-modified glass-ionomer luting gave the highest fracture resistance in one bench study; the single RCT found cast-metal 1.2%/yr vs fiber 1.7%/yr annual failure with no significant difference in root fracture risk.
Evidence is mostly FEA/in vitro with no quality appraisal, so the recommendations (preserve ferrule, post length, cement choice) are hypothesis-grade rather than clinical-outcome evidence.

## 세줄요약
범위 문헌고찰 (PubMed/Medline + Scopus 2013–2023, 영어·프랑스어; 1,464편 검토 후 18편 포함: 유한요소분석 8·실험실 4·시험관 3·후향 2·RCT 1): 금속 포스트-코어 수복치의 보철 하방 수직 치근파절 (Vertical Root Fracture, VRF) 관련 인자를 정리.
주요 결과: 상악 소구치·하악 대구치가 호발; 2 mm 페룰 (ferrule)이 파절저항을 유의하게 증가 (유한요소분석); 고탄성계수 합금은 응력을 거의 전부 상아질로 전달; 한 실험에서 레진강화형 글라스아이오노머 합착이 파절저항 최고; 유일한 RCT에서 연간 실패율 금속 1.2% vs 파이버 1.7%, 치근파절 위험 유의차 없음.
근거 대부분이 유한요소분석·시험관 연구이고 질 평가가 없어, 제시된 권고(페룰 보존·포스트 길이·시멘트 선택)는 임상 결과 근거가 아닌 가설 수준이다.

## Summary
This scoping review from Mohammed V University (Rabat) maps the parameters implicated in vertical root fracture beneath metallic post-and-core restorations. Eighteen studies (2013–2023) were included, predominantly finite element and bench studies, with one RCT and two retrospective series. The synthesis points to tooth predisposition (maxillary premolars, mandibular molars; thin, flat roots), intracanal preparation-induced cracks, ferrule height (2 mm protective), high post stiffness transmitting stress to dentin, luting cement, and occlusal fatigue as the main contributors. The only RCT (Sarkis-Onofre 2020; 119 patients, 183 posts) found no significant difference in root fracture between cast-metal and fiber posts. Because the evidence base is largely non-clinical and unappraised, the conclusions are best read as a biomechanical checklist rather than outcome-proven recommendations.

## Key Contributions
- Metal/cast-post-specific synthesis of VRF risk factors (predisposition, preparation, ferrule, length, diameter, modulus, cement, occlusion).
- Documents conflicting bench/FEA data on ferrule location (Samran 2015: NS), post length (Dejak 2013: irrelevant with 2 mm ferrule; Sugaya 2015: longer posts fewer apical fractures), and post material (cast highest fracture load in thin-walled, ferrule-less teeth).
- Highlights failure-mode contrast: cast posts irreparable apical fracture under high load vs repairable coronal fracture with fiber (Rippe 2014).
- Flags "danger zones" (mesial root of mandibular molars, buccal root of maxillary premolars) for dentin over-removal.

## Methodology
Scoping review; PubMed/Medline and Scopus (June 2013–June 2023) plus hand-searching; English/French; four-step selection (title, abstract, full text, analysis); data extracted on author, date, design, n, objective, intervention. No formal risk-of-bias appraisal. Selection criteria are internally contradictory (grey literature and non-English/French listed under inclusion).

## Results
| Factor | Finding (study) |
|---|---|
| Predisposition | Maxillary premolars, mandibular molars most affected; mandibular first molar > maxillary second premolar > maxillary central incisor (Liao 2017) |
| Intracanal preparation | Manual > rotary cervical dentin irregularities/cracks; not removed by post-space preparation (Rippe 2014) |
| Ferrule | 2 mm ferrule significantly raises fracture resistance (Mosharaf 2023 FEA); eliminates post length/material effects (Kainose 2014); location NS in mandibular premolars (Samran 2015) |
| Post length | Longer = fewer apical fractures, no cervical association (Sugaya 2015); shorter metal post raises apical stress/debonding (Kainose 2014); shorter feasible with high coronal height (Mosharaf 2023) |
| Post material | Stainless steel > zirconia > titanium > glass fiber > carbon fiber in stress (Madfa 2014); cast posts highest fracture load in thin-walled teeth without ferrule (Iemsaengchairat 2021) |
| Luting cement | RMGIC highest fracture resistance (Torres-Sanchez 2013); zinc phosphate higher resistance but composite more restorable (Dabbagh 2016) |
| RCT | Cast metal 1.2%/yr vs fiber 1.7%/yr; 10 root fractures among 23 failures; no significant root fracture difference (Sarkis-Onofre 2020) |
| Occlusion | Repetitive loading propagates surface cracks (fatigue fracture); bruxism a risk factor |
