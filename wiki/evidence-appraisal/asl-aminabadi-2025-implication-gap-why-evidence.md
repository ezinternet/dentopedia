## Three-line Summary

Two-page editorial (JODDD 2025;19(4):213-214) arguing that poor adherence to evidence-based dental guidelines stems not from clinician negligence or lack of awareness but from systemic, structural, cognitive, and patient-related barriers.

It identifies four barrier classes — economic mismatch (unreimbursed time-intensive preventive care), guideline complexity/usability (dense technical documents without decision trees), real-world patient variability (comorbidities, socioeconomic constraints vs "ideal" trial patients), and cognitive overload/guideline fatigue (decision fatigue).

Proposed fixes are system-level: simplified summaries and digital decision-support embedded in electronic dental records, reimbursement models aligned with evidence-based practices, flexible implementation pathways, and clinician feedback loops into guideline revision.

## 세줄요약

2페이지 사설(JODDD 2025;19(4):213-214): 근거기반 치의학 (Evidence-Based Dentistry, EBD) 지침 준수율이 낮은 원인이 임상의 무관심·인식 부족이 아니라 경제적 미스매치·지침 복잡성·환자 변이성·인지과부하 (Cognitive Overload) 같은 체계적·구조적 장벽에 있다고 재프레임.

네 가지 장벽: (1) 예방 처치·교육·진단이 수가로 보전되지 않는 경제 구조, (2) 의사결정 나무·체크리스트 없는 밀도 높은 기술적 문서, (3) 통제된 시험의 "이상적 환자"와 전신질환·사회경제적 제약을 가진 실제 환자의 괴리, (4) 끝없이 누적되는 지침이 유발하는 의사결정 피로 (Decision Fatigue).

해법은 개인 규율이 아닌 시스템 차원: 전자치과기록 (Electronic Dental Record) 내 의사결정지원 삽입, 근거기반 진료에 정렬한 수가 모델, 유연한 실행 경로, 임상의 피드백을 지침 개정으로 되먹임.

## Summary
This editorial reframes the guideline-adherence problem in dentistry: the gap between what guidelines recommend and what clinics do is a feasibility failure of the system, not a motivation failure of clinicians. Four barrier classes are dissected with dental examples — economic mismatch (preventive sealants and caries risk assessment dropped under volume-and-reimbursement pressure), usability failure (dense academic documents lacking chairside decision trees), patient variability (protocols assuming ideal trial patients colliding with comorbidity, transport, and financial reality), and cognitive overload from accumulating guideline bodies (decision fatigue). The remedy menu is correspondingly systemic: workflow-embedded digital decision support, reimbursement aligned with evidence-based practice, flexible implementation pathways, and clinician feedback into guideline revision. Its core inversion — "shift the focus from individual compliance to system-level support" — makes it the implementation-side complement to this wiki's appraisal-methodology pages.

## Key Contributions
- Names and frames the "implication gap": non-adherence reflects structural, cognitive, and patient-centered constraints on feasibility rather than negligence or ignorance.
- Taxonomizes four barrier classes, each anchored to a concrete dental scenario (sealants omitted in high-volume practice; infection-control protocols without streamlined tools; periodontal follow-up lost to access barriers; selective attention under guideline accumulation).
- Delivers an intervention checklist for guideline developers and system designers: structured summaries/flowcharts, electronic dental record prompts at point of care, payment-model alignment, culturally sensitive patient engagement, and feedback systems feeding clinician friction back into revisions.

## Methodology
- Narrative editorial commentary; no new empirical data.
- Synthesizes implementation-science literature: guideline-development strategy for dental practice (Frantsve-Hawley 2022), overview-of-systematic-reviews on implementation strategies (Pereira 2022; Wang 2023), empirical barrier study of sealant guideline rollout (Polk 2018), and a 2025 systematic review of decision fatigue in healthcare professionals (Maier).

## Results
Position piece — no benchmarks. Central claim: adherence failures are "economic necessity", not "clinical negligence", and only system-level redesign (not more dissemination/education) converts evidence into consistent care.
