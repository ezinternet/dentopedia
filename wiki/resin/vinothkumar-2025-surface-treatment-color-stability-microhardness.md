## Three-line Summary

In vitro, n=240 discs of two nanohybrid enamel-shade composites (G-aenial A'CHORD, Beautifil II Enamel), comparing four OIL-control treatments: glycerin (G), Mylar strip (M), finishing+polishing (FP), and no treatment (NT) on ΔE (spectrophotometer) and Vickers microhardness (VHN). FP achieved the highest hardness; M showed the least color change (ΔE); glycerin occupied an intermediate position on both outcomes; G-aenial A'CHORD outperformed Beautifil II on both metrics. Finishing and polishing is superior for surface hardness, while Mylar strip or glycerin better preserve color.

## Summary

Vinothkumar et al. (2025) conducted a 4-way head-to-head comparison of OIL-management strategies applied to two nanohybrid composites, assessing both surface microhardness (VHN) and color stability (ΔE). The core finding is that the optimal strategy differs by outcome: finishing and polishing (FP) maximized surface hardness, while Mylar strip application best preserved color. Glycerin, often the default clinical recommendation, performed intermediately on both measures — better than no treatment for hardness, but not significantly superior to FP for hardness or to Mylar for color. This suggests that blanket glycerin promotion simplifies a nuanced trade-off. Material brand also mattered independently of treatment: G-aenial A'CHORD consistently outperformed Beautifil II Enamel on both VHN and ΔE across all treatments, indicating formulation-level differences in OIL susceptibility.

## Key Contributions

- 4-way OIL strategy comparison on dual outcomes (hardness + color) — neither glycerin nor any single strategy dominates both axes
- FP = highest VHN; M = lowest ΔE; glycerin is not the best for either endpoint
- Brand effect independent of OIL treatment: G-aenial A'CHORD > Beautifil II Enamel on both outcomes
- Provides clinically applicable decision guidance: prioritize FP for stress-bearing surfaces; consider Mylar for esthetic-critical anterior composites

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | In vitro controlled experiment |
| Composites tested | G-aenial A'CHORD (GC, AC group); Beautifil II Enamel (Shofu, BT group) |
| n per subgroup | 15 (× 2 outcomes × 4 treatments × 2 composites = 240 total) |
| OIL treatments | Glycerin (G), Mylar strip (M), Finishing+polishing (FP), No treatment (NT) |
| Hardness measurement | Vickers microhardness tester (VHN) |
| Color measurement | Spectrophotometer (ΔE) |
| Statistics | One-way ANOVA; Tukey post-hoc; p=0.001 |

## Results

### Hardness (VHN) — FP wins

| Treatment | Rank | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| FP | **1st (highest)** | Both AC and BT, p=0.001 |
| G or M | 2nd–3rd (intermediate) | — |
| NT | Lowest | Both groups |

### Color Stability (ΔE) — M wins

| Treatment | Rank | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| M | **Lowest ΔE (best)** | AC: p=0.001; BT: M ≈ G |
| G | BT: similar to M | AC: higher ΔE than M |
| FP | High ΔE | Polishing surface increased color change |
| NT | Baseline comparison | — |

G-aenial A'CHORD showed significantly lower ΔE and higher mean HV than Beautifil II Enamel across all treatment subgroups.
