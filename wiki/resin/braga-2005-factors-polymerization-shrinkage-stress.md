> - Ferracane(Jack Ferracane)이 공저 — 중합수축 분야 기준 문헌

## Three-line Summary

Polymerization shrinkage stress in resin-composites arises from the interplay of volumetric shrinkage, viscoelastic relaxation during the pre-gel phase, and cavity confinement (C-factor). No single restorative technique universally reduces stress because material-specific properties dominate each factor. This 2005 SR by Braga, Ballester & Ferracane remains the foundational reference for the multi-factorial framework.

## Background

Polymerization shrinkage stress (PSS) has long been implicated in marginal gap formation, cusp deflection, enamel crack propagation, and post-operative sensitivity in bonded resin-composite restorations. Despite decades of research, the mechanisms remained incompletely systematized until this review synthesized the evidence into a coherent multi-factorial model.

## Three Primary Factors

### 1. Volumetric Shrinkage (부피수축)

Shrinkage is determined by monomer type and content:
- Dimethacrylate monomers (Bis-GMA, TEGDMA, UDMA) differ in double-bond density and molecular weight, yielding different shrinkage magnitudes.
- Higher monomer-to-filler ratio → greater shrinkage.
- The absolute magnitude of shrinkage (typically 1.5–5 vol%) sets the ceiling for potential stress.

### 2. Viscoelastic Behavior (점탄성 거동)

The resin transitions through two critical phases during polymerization:
- **Pre-gel (flowable) phase**: the material can flow and relax stresses before the polymer network rigidifies. Stress relaxation here reduces final residual stress.
- **Post-gel (vitrification) phase**: once the glass transition temperature approaches ambient temperature, the material becomes rigid and stresses are locked in.
- A material that remains flowable longer (slower reaction kinetics, lower crosslink density) accumulates less net stress — but this trades off with final mechanical properties.

### 3. Restrictions on Polymerization Shrinkage (수축 구속 — C-factor)

- The cavity configuration factor (C-factor = bonded surfaces / free surfaces) quantifies geometric confinement.
- High C-factor (e.g., class I occlusal box: 5 bonded walls) dramatically amplifies residual stress.
- Low C-factor restorations (class V, anterior facings) allow more free shrinkage and develop less stress.
- Incremental layering reduces effective C-factor per increment.

## Reaction Kinetics and Degree of Conversion

- Degree of conversion (DC): the proportion of C=C double bonds that react. Higher DC → greater volumetric shrinkage, but also greater mechanical properties.
- Faster polymerization (high-irradiance curing) accelerates the pre-gel-to-post-gel transition, reducing the window for stress relaxation → net higher stress.
- "Soft-start" curing protocols (ramped or stepped irradiance) aim to extend the pre-gel window — efficacy is material-dependent.

## Clinical Implications

| Stress-reduction strategy | Mechanism targeted | Material-dependence |
|---|---|---|
| Incremental layering | Reduce C-factor per layer | Low (generally beneficial) |
| Soft-start / ramped curing | Extend pre-gel relaxation window | High (varies by resin) |
| Low-modulus liner (stress-absorbing) | Buffer confinement at cavity floor | Material- and geometry-dependent |
| Bulk-fill materials | Reduced shrinkage monomers / stress relaxers | Formulation-specific |
| Flowable base layer | Pre-gel relaxation, low C-factor at gingival floor | Limited by depth of cure |

**Key conclusion**: because PSS is multi-factorial and each factor is governed by material-specific chemistry, no single technique is universally superior. Evidence for technique efficacy must be interpreted in the context of the specific material tested.

## Limitations

- 2005: bulk-fill composites, stress-relaxing monomers (e.g., silorane, ormocer), and ring-opening monomers had not yet been extensively studied.
- Abstract-only ingest; specific cited studies and meta-analytic statistics are not available in this wiki entry.
- The framework remains valid, but newer materials partly circumvent the volumetric shrinkage axis by design.
