## Three-line Summary

In-vitro preliminary study (81 specimens, 27 groups: 3 composites × 3 LED exposure modes × 3 curing-tip distances, 0–4 mm) measuring degree of conversion (DC) by Raman spectroscopy.

Top-surface DC was governed by material and distance (p<0.001, p=0.049); bottom-surface DC by material and exposure mode (p<0.001, p=0.032); flowable composite achieved the highest DC (top 74%, bottom >60%) while bulk-fill fell below the ~55% clinical minimum at the bottom surface (<45%).

Bulk-fill composites must not be cured from a distance without ensuring adequate bottom-surface DC; soft-start mode performed best at the top surface (69%) and may help reduce pulp heat in deep cavities.

## Summary
This in vitro preliminary study evaluated how curing-light-tip distance (0/2/4 mm) and LED exposure mode (standard, soft-start, pulse) affect the degree of conversion (DC) of three resin-based composites — a nanohybrid (Evetric), a bulk-fill (Filtek One Bulk Fill), and a flowable (G-aenial Universal Flo) — using a Woodpecker LED.H curing light. DC was measured by Raman spectroscopy at both top and bottom surfaces of 81 specimens across 27 experimental groups (n=3 each). Material type dominated DC at both surfaces (p<0.001), but the second significant factor diverged by depth: distance mattered at the top surface (p=0.049) while exposure mode mattered at the bottom surface (p=0.032). The flowable composite achieved the best conversion overall; the bulk-fill composite fell below the ~55% clinically suggested minimum DC at its bottom surface, flagging a polymerization-adequacy risk for thick bulk-fill increments cured from a distance.

## Key Contributions
- Combines curing distance, LED exposure mode, and composite material type in a single factorial design, rather than isolating one variable as most prior work has done.
- Follow-up to the authors' own prior photometer study (irradiance measured at a sensor) — this study measures actual DC within the composite, adding a validated in-material outcome to the earlier light-output data.
- Shows the significant DC-driving factor differs by specimen depth: distance governs top-surface DC, exposure mode governs bottom-surface DC — a previously uncharacterized depth-dependent dissociation.
- Identifies bulk-fill composite bottom-surface DC (<45%) as falling below the ~55% clinically suggested minimum, a concrete polymerization-adequacy warning for thick posterior increments cured at increased tip distance.
- Soft-start mode is the best performer at the top surface (69%) and nearly matches standard mode at the bottom (56% vs 57%); pulse mode gives the lowest overall DC but may reduce pulp-overheating risk in deep, vital-pulp cavities.

## Methodology
In vitro factorial study: 3 RBCs (Evetric nanohybrid, Filtek One Bulk Fill, G-aenial Universal Flo flowable) × 3 LED exposure modes (standard 1000 mW/cm² full power; soft-start ramping to peak over 5 s; pulse oscillating 850–1000 mW/cm² every 2 s) × 3 tip-to-surface distances (0, 2, 4 mm, set with spacer rings) = 27 groups, n=3 specimens/group (81 total, 4 mm diameter × 2 mm height, single operator, 20 s cure with a Woodpecker LED.H light; irradiance verified with a Bluephase Meter II photometer). Specimens were stored in light-proof, 37 ± 1 °C distilled/deionized water for 24 h before analysis. DC was measured at top and bottom surfaces by Raman spectroscopy (Labura HR 800, He-Ne 632.8 nm), using the ratio of aliphatic C=C (1639 cm⁻¹, reactive) to aromatic C=C (1609 cm⁻¹, internal standard) bands, deconvoluted with Fityk 1.3.1 (Lorentzian fit). Statistics: separate three-way ANOVAs (material × exposure mode × distance) with interaction terms for top and bottom surfaces, Tukey post hoc, alpha = 0.05 (Statistica 13.3).

## Results
- **Top surface**: material p<0.001, distance p=0.049 (both significant), exposure mode p=0.391 (not significant); no significant interactions.
- **Bottom surface**: material p<0.001, exposure mode p=0.032 (both significant), distance p=0.068 (not significant); no significant interactions.
- DC was consistently higher at top than bottom across all conditions.
- Flowable composite: top DC 74%, bottom DC >60% (best overall).
- Bulk-fill composite: top DC >55%, bottom DC <45% (worst overall, below the ~55% suggested clinical minimum at the bottom).
- Nanohybrid composite: intermediate DC values.
- Soft-start mode: 69% top / 56% bottom; standard mode: 67% top / 57% bottom (soft-start best at top, essentially tied at bottom).
- Increasing distance 0→4 mm still permitted adequate top-surface DC (~70% at 2 mm, ~60% at 4 mm).
- Pulse mode: acceptable top-surface polymerization even at increased distance but the lowest overall DC among the three modes.
- Companion prior photometer data (same authors): distance reduced irradiance most in soft-start mode (985→884 mW/cm² at 2→4 mm) vs standard (996→906) and pulse (993→893) — that study measured sensor-level irradiance only, motivating the present in-material DC analysis.

This is a preliminary/pilot in vitro study (n=3/group, no a priori power analysis); results should be read as exploratory pending larger confirmatory studies.
