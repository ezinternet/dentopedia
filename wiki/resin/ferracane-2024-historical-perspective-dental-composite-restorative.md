## One-line Summary

Single-author historical-perspective review (Ferracane, J Funct Biomater 2024) tracing dental resin-based composite (RBC) evolution — from 1950s silicate/PMMA acrylics through Bowen's 1962 Bis-GMA and Buonocore's acid-etch, to chemical→UV→visible/CQ→LED light-curing, low-shrinkage monomer and nano/hybrid filler refinements, bulk-fill composites, and the emerging "bioactive"/self-healing frontier — with amalgam framed as the posterior material composites gradually displaced.

## Summary

Ferracane — a leading dental-materials scientist — organizes the history of tooth-colored direct restoratives along three evolutionary axes: **how polymerization is initiated, the resin monomers, and the reinforcing fillers**. Before composites, esthetic dentistry was anterior-only (silicate cements, then 1950s PMMA acrylics that shrank badly, did not bond, and were toxic/weak). The founding leap was **Rafael Bowen's Bis-GMA (US patent, 1962)** — a methacrylate–epoxide hybrid that was stronger, less shrinking, and tooth-like in thermal expansion — paired with **Michael Buonocore's phosphoric-acid enamel etch** for adhesion. For decades composites were anterior-only while **amalgam remained the posterior workhorse**; the review is the story of composites steadily earning the posterior indication as light-curing, monomers, and fillers improved. Key transitions: chemical two-paste → UV (~1970) → visible-light camphorquinone (late 1970s–80s); QTH bulbs → LED (dominant by mid-2000s). Polymerization shrinkage is reframed as a **stress** problem (contraction resisted by bonding), driving low-shrinkage chemistries (silorane, ormocer, large monomers) and bulk-fill (4–5 mm) stress-relief networks. Fillers evolved large-quartz → 40–50 nm microfill → hybrid/microhybrid/nanohybrid → true nanofill and single-shade supra-nanofills. The closing agenda is **"bioactivity"** (antimicrobial, anti-biofilm, ion-releasing/remineralizing) and self-healing — promising in vitro but, Ferracane cautions, not yet clinically proven superior.

## Key Contributions

- A **chronological backbone** for the wiki's many single-property composite pages (shrinkage, bulk-fill, bioactive, longevity), organizing them by initiator / monomer / filler evolution.
- Anchors founding **milestones and dates**: PMMA (early 1950s) → **Bis-GMA / Bowen (1962, patent 3,066,112; literature 1963)** → Buonocore acid-etch.
- Documents the **light-curing lineage** (chemical → UV → CQ visible → QTH → LED → laser/PAC/poly-wave) and debunks universal **exposure reciprocity**.
- Reframes shrinkage as **shrinkage stress**, explaining why low-shrinkage monomers (silorane/ormocer) delivered only *equivalent* clinical results.
- Frames **amalgam** as the posterior standard that composites progressively displaced, tying the material-science history to the clinical amalgam-vs-composite debate.

## Methodology

- **Design**: narrative / historical-perspective review, single author; not a systematic search or meta-analysis.
- Author-selected literature to trace the *commercially realized* changes to polymerization initiators, resin monomers, and fillers; each milestone illustrated with named products.
- Structure: four evolutionary sections (intro/founding, initiation, monomers, fillers) + a future-directions section (bioactivity, self-healing).

## Results

Evolutionary timeline (as synthesized):

| Era / axis | Milestone |
|---|---|
| Pre-composite | Silicate cements; PMMA acrylics (1950s) — high shrinkage, no bonding, MMA toxicity, weak |
| **1962** | **Bowen's Bis-GMA** (methacrylate+epoxide); + TEGDMA diluent |
| Bonding | Buonocore phosphoric-acid enamel etch → anterior use; posterior stays amalgam |
| Initiation | Self-cure → UV (~1970) → visible CQ+amine (~468 nm) → less-yellow PPD/BAPO/TPO/Ivocerin |
| Curing lights | QTH → **LED** (420–500 nm, 800–2000 mW/cm², dominant mid-2000s) → laser/PAC/poly-wave |
| Low-shrinkage | Large monomers (TCD-urethane, DX511); silorane ring-opening (Filtek LS); ormocer (Admira/Ceram-X) — *equivalent, not superior* |
| Bulk-fill (early 2010s) | 4–5 mm single cure; refractive-index match + covalent adaptable networks (Filtek One, Tetric PowerFill) |
| Fillers | Large quartz → 40–50 nm microfill → hybrid → microhybrid → nanohybrid → true nanofill (Filtek Supreme) → supra-nanofill / single-shade (Omnichroma); radiolucent→radiopaque Ba/Sr/Zr glass |
| Future | Bioactivity (MDPB, Nobio Infinix, ion-releasing Cention N/Activa/Beautifil), self-healing — in-vitro promise, clinical superiority unproven |

Two conceptual take-homes the review stresses: (1) it is **polymerization stress, not shrinkage magnitude**, that opens marginal gaps; (2) most "advances" (low-shrinkage, self-adhesive, bioactive) have delivered **equivalence rather than demonstrated clinical superiority** — improvements were largely handling/placement and esthetics driven by practitioner demand.
