## One-line Summary

In-vitro material-science study (Clin Oral Investig 2026, Turku/SCUT) synthesizing a polymerizable radical inhibitor PBHT (a methacrylate-functional BHT analog) and adding it at 1–3 wt% to a UDMA/Bis-EMA/TEGDMA composite: PBHT significantly lowered polymerization shrinkage stress (max stress-rate falling from 47.5 to 13.1 ×10⁻³ MPa·s at 3 wt%) where non-polymerizable BHT did not, while leaching far less inhibitor (1%PBHT 132 vs 1%BHT 549 µg/g); 1 wt% PBHT gave the best overall balance.

## 한줄요약

In-vitro 재료연구(Clin Oral Investig 2026): 중합성 라디칼 억제제 **PBHT**(메타크릴레이트 결합형 BHT 유도체)를 합성해 UDMA/Bis-EMA/TEGDMA 레진에 1–3 wt% 첨가 — 비중합형 BHT(Butylated Hydroxytoluene)는 수축응력(Shrinkage Stress, SS)을 못 낮췄으나 PBHT는 유의 저감(최대 수축응력속도 47.5→13.1 ×10⁻³ MPa·s, 3 wt%), 용출 억제제도 훨씬 적음(1%PBHT 132 vs 1%BHT 549 µg/g); 최적 농도 1 wt%.

## Summary

This bench study tackles polymerization **shrinkage stress** — the interfacial stress (not the raw volumetric contraction) that causes microleakage, cusp deflection, and post-operative sensitivity — through the underused lever of **delaying the gel point**. Before a composite gels, contraction stress relaxes by molecular rearrangement; slowing polymerization therefore lowers final stress. The commercial way to slow polymerization is the radical inhibitor **BHT**, but BHT does not copolymerize, so it leaches out and is cytotoxic in excess. The authors synthesized **PBHT**, a methacrylate-functional BHT analog that copolymerizes into the network, and compared 1–3 wt% PBHT against matched BHT in a UDMA/Bis-EMA/TEGDMA composite. PBHT **significantly reduced shrinkage stress** (max stress-rate down to 13.1 ×10⁻³ MPa·s at 3 wt%, vs 47.5 for the control) while — notably — plain BHT did **not** reduce stress in this UDMA-rich matrix (rapid gelation traps it). Because PBHT is bound into the polymer, it **leached ~4× less** than BHT at every concentration. Flexural strength, modulus, and fracture toughness were preserved; only water solubility rose modestly (still within ISO 4049). The recommended sweet spot is **1 wt% PBHT**. For the wiki, this extends the shrinkage-stress toolkit (Meereis 2018 taxonomy) with a gel-point-delay + low-leaching strategy.

## Key Contributions

- A **polymerizable inhibitor (PBHT)** that lowers shrinkage stress *and* stays in the network (low leaching) — solving BHT's elution/cytotoxicity problem.
- Confirms **gel-point delay > volumetric-shrinkage reduction** as the effective stress-control mechanism (SS drops; VS barely moves).
- Documents a **matrix-dependent failure of BHT**: in a high-UDMA matrix, non-polymerizable BHT loses its stress-reducing effect, while PBHT persists.
- Preserves mechanical properties (FS/FM/fracture toughness); identifies **1 wt%** as the optimal dose.

## Methodology

- **PBHT synthesis**: HBHT + 2-(methacryloyloxy)ethyl isocyanate (MEI), DBTL catalyst; 90% yield; FT-IR + 1H-NMR confirmed.
- **Composites**: UDMA 69.3 / Bis-EMA(04) 19.8 / TEGDMA 9.9 / CQ 0.5 / EDB 0.5 (wt%), silanated glass filler (resin:filler 1:3); BHT or PBHT at 1/2/3 wt%; no-inhibitor control.
- **Outcomes**: DC (ATR-FTIR, Elipar DeepCure-L 1600 mW/cm², 40 s); volumetric shrinkage (Archimedes density); shrinkage stress (glass-FRC-rod cantilever, 1.5 mm, 3 min); FS/FM (ISO 4049); fracture toughness (SENB, ISO 20795-2); WS/SL (ISO 4049:2019, 7 d); leachable inhibitor (GC-MS SIM).
- **Stats**: two-way ANOVA + Tukey/Sidak, α=0.05.

## Results

Max shrinkage-stress rate and key properties (from Table 2):

| DRC | Max SS rate (×10⁻³ MPa·s) | FS (MPa) | Leachable inhibitor (µg/g) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control | 47.5 ± 2.8 | 133 ± 20 | — |
| 1% BHT | 34.8 | 128 | 549 |
| 3% BHT | 21.8 | 111 | 2105 |
| **1% PBHT** | **30.8** | 136 | **132** |
| 2% PBHT | 19.8 | 125 | 290 |
| **3% PBHT** | **13.1 ± 1.5** | 134 | 717 |

- **Shrinkage stress**: PBHT ↓ significantly (p<0.05); BHT no significant effect (p>0.05).
- **Volumetric shrinkage / DC**: only 3 wt% PBHT lowered VS and DC (66.4% vs 68.7%); all DC >60%.
- **Mechanical**: FS, FM, fracture toughness comparable to control (except 3%BHT higher FM).
- **Leaching**: PBHT ~4× lower than BHT at matched concentration.
- **WS/SL**: WS up only at 3 wt%; SL raised by both but PBHT < BHT; all within ISO 4049.
- **Mechanism**: PBHT's mono-methacrylate copolymerizes → persists longer quenching radicals + lowers cross-link density → delays gel point → dissipates stress; being bound, it leaches less.
