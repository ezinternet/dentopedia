## One-line Summary
In-vitro study (J Oral Biol Craniofac Res 2025) curing Filtek Z350 XT nanocomposite disks at light-curing tip distances of 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8 mm (n=4/group) found degree of conversion (DC%, ATR-FTIR) and human gingival fibroblast viability (MTT) both declined significantly with increasing distance, with viability dropping below the ISO 70% biocompatibility threshold at ≥6 mm and correlating positively with DC% (top surface R=0.597, bottom R=0.490).

## Summary
This in vitro study examined whether the distance between a light-curing unit tip and a composite resin surface — a variable that increases in deep proximal or Class II cavities — affects both the degree of conversion (DC%) of the composite and its biocompatibility as measured by gingival fibroblast cell viability. Disk samples of a nanocomposite (Filtek Z350 XT) were light-cured at five tip distances (0–8 mm) and assessed via ATR-FTIR for DC% and MTT assay for cell viability. Both DC% and cell viability declined as tip distance increased, and cell viability fell below the ISO 10993 70% biocompatibility threshold at 6 mm and 8 mm. DC% and cell viability were positively and significantly correlated, supporting a mechanistic chain from insufficient light exposure to under-polymerization to residual monomer release to cytotoxicity. Clinically, this quantifies a safety-relevant reason to minimize curing-tip-to-composite distance, particularly in deep or interproximal restorations near the gingiva.

## Key Contributions
- Directly links a modifiable clinical/device parameter (curing tip distance) to a cell-viability biocompatibility outcome, not just a mechanical/conversion outcome
- Identifies a distance threshold (~6 mm) at which gingival fibroblast viability drops below the ISO 10993 70% biocompatibility cutoff for this composite/curing-unit combination
- Quantifies the DC% decline curve (44.57% at 0 mm to 32.99% at 8 mm) alongside light-intensity falloff (to ~313 mW/cm² at 8 mm, near the ~300 mW/cm² minimum cited for adequate cure)
- Demonstrates a statistically significant positive correlation between DC% and cell viability, stronger at the directly-irradiated top surface (R=0.597) than the bottom surface (R=0.490)

## Methodology
In vitro, five groups (G0, G2, G4, G6, G8; n=4/group) of disk-shaped (2×6 mm) Filtek Z350 XT nanocomposite (shade A2) samples were light-cured for 20 s at 1200 mW/cm² using an LED unit (O-Star Cordless, Woodpecker) positioned at 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8 mm from the sample surface (spacers used for distance control; radiometer-verified intensity at each distance). After 24 h incubation at 37°C, DC% was measured via ATR-FTIR (aliphatic C=C at 1638 cm⁻¹ vs carbonyl reference at 1720 cm⁻¹) on both top and bottom surfaces. Biocompatibility was assessed per ISO 10993 using an MTT assay on human gingival fibroblasts exposed to 24-h composite eluates. Group comparisons used one-way ANOVA (after Shapiro-Wilk normality testing); associations among DC%, cell viability, and distance were tested via Pearson correlation.

## Results
- DC% was significantly higher on the top than bottom surface at every distance (p < 0.001) and trended downward with increasing distance on both surfaces (44.57% at 0 mm → 32.99% at 8 mm), reaching pairwise significance only for G0 vs G6 and G0 vs G8 at the bottom surface (p < 0.05)
- Cell viability decreased significantly with distance for every pairwise group comparison except G0 vs G2 (p < 0.001)
- Cell viability stayed above the ISO 70% threshold through 4 mm, then fell to 65.5% (6 mm) and 58% (8 mm) — both below threshold
- DC% and cell viability were positively correlated at both surfaces (p < 0.05): top R = 0.597, bottom R = 0.490
- Tip distance was negatively correlated with both DC% and cell viability at both surfaces (p < 0.05)
