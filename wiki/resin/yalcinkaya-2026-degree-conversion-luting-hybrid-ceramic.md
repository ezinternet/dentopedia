## Three-line Summary

In-vitro ATR-FTIR study (n=7/group, 63 specimens) comparing degree of conversion (DC) of dual-cure resin cement, flowable composite, and bulk-fill composite polymerized beneath Cerasmart 270 hybrid ceramic CAD/CAM substrates of 1.5, 2.0, and 2.5 mm thickness.

Significant material × thickness interaction (p<0.001): flowable composite had the highest DC at ≤2.0 mm (36.57% at 1.5 mm) but collapsed to the lowest at 2.5 mm (11.69%); dual-cure resin cement was the most thickness-stable and highest DC at 2.5 mm (30.88%); bulk-fill composite showed stable but intermediate DC across all thicknesses.

Luting material selection should be thickness-driven: use flowable composite for thin restorations (≤2.0 mm) and dual-cure resin cement for restorations >2.0 mm to ensure adequate polymerization beneath hybrid ceramic CAD/CAM substrates.

## 세줄요약

In-vitro ATR-FTIR 연구 (그룹당 n=7, 총 63개 시편) — Cerasmart 270 하이브리드 세라믹 (Hybrid Ceramic) CAD/CAM 기판 두께(1.5/2.0/2.5 mm)에 따른 이중경화 레진시멘트 (Dual-Cure Resin Cement)·플로어블 복합레진 (Flowable Composite)·벌크필 복합레진 (Bulk-Fill Composite)의 중합도 (Degree of Conversion, DC) 비교.

재료 × 두께 유의한 상호작용(p<0.001): 플로어블 복합레진은 ≤2.0 mm에서 DC 최고(1.5 mm에서 36.57%)이나 2.5 mm에서 최저(11.69%)로 급락; 이중경화 레진시멘트는 전 두께에서 가장 안정적이고 2.5 mm에서 최고(30.88%); 벌크필 복합레진은 전 두께에서 안정적이나 중간 수준.

합착재 선택은 두께 기반으로: ≤2.0 mm 수복물에는 플로어블 복합레진, >2.0 mm에는 이중경화 레진시멘트 사용이 하이브리드 세라믹 CAD/CAM 하부의 충분한 중합 확보에 유리.

## Summary
This in-vitro study evaluated how hybrid ceramic CAD/CAM substrate thickness (1.5, 2.0, 2.5 mm; Cerasmart 270, GC) affects the degree of conversion (DC, via ATR-FTIR) of three light-curable luting materials from the same manufacturer: a dual-cure resin cement (Estecem II Plus), a flowable composite (Estelite Universal Flow), and a bulk-fill composite (Estelite Bulk-Fill Flow). It also characterized how substrate thickness changes the optical properties (translucency, opalescence, contrast ratio) of the hybrid ceramic itself, mechanistically linking reduced light transmission to reduced DC. The core finding is a significant material × thickness interaction: no single luting material is universally superior — flowable composite wins at ≤2.0 mm but fails badly at 2.5 mm, while dual-cure resin cement is comparatively thickness-independent and becomes the best performer at 2.5 mm. Bulk-fill composite sits in between, statistically stable across thicknesses but never the top performer. Clinically, this supports thickness-based luting material selection rather than a one-size-fits-all default.

## Key Contributions
- First head-to-head DC comparison of dual-cure cement vs flowable vs bulk-fill composite as CAD/CAM luting agents across three restoration thicknesses using the same hybrid ceramic and manufacturer product line (controls for confounds from mixing brands).
- Demonstrates that "best luting material for DC" is thickness-dependent, not a fixed ranking — flowable composite's DC advantage at thin restorations inverts to a disadvantage at 2.5 mm.
- Quantifies the optical (translucency/opalescence/contrast ratio) mechanism underlying the DC decline, and identifies a clinically detectable esthetic threshold at the 1.5→2.0 mm step (exceeds translucency perceptibility/acceptability limits) that plateaus by 2.0→2.5 mm.
- Positions bulk-fill composite as a viable alternative for thin-to-moderate restorations (≤2.0 mm) given its relatively thickness-stable DC, though it never outperforms dual-cure cement at 2.5 mm.

## Methodology
In-vitro factorial design. Hybrid ceramic CAD/CAM substrates (CeraSmart 270, shade A2 HT) sectioned to 16×12 mm at 1.5/2.0/2.5 mm thickness. Optical properties (RTP_ab, RTP_00 translucency parameters, opalescence, contrast ratio) measured spectrophotometrically (VITA Easyshade Advance 4.0, CIE Lab* D65, black/white backing) on 6 uncemented substrates per thickness group. Three luting materials (Estecem II Plus dual-cure cement, Estelite Universal Flow flowable composite, Estelite Bulk-Fill Flow bulk-fill composite; all Tokuyama) polymerized through each substrate thickness in a standardized jig at 80 μm final thickness using a polywave LED unit (Valo X, 1000 mW/cm², 385–515 nm, 0 mm distance); n=7 specimens per material per thickness (63 total). Dual-cure cement received 20 s light-cure plus standardized seating pressure (finger pressure then 25 N load for 2.5 min); flowable composite 20 s; bulk-fill composite 10 s (manufacturer protocols). DC measured by ATR-FTIR (1634 cm⁻¹ methacrylate C=C vs 1608/1592 cm⁻¹ aromatic reference peak ratio, cured vs uncured). Statistics: robust ANOVA with Bonferroni pairwise comparisons for non-normal DC data; one-way ANOVA + Tukey for optical parameters; α=0.05; power ≈1.000 (post-hoc, f=2.75).

## Results
**Optical properties** (all p<0.001 by thickness): translucency (RTP_ab) fell from 11.21±1.4 (1.5mm) → 7.9±1.5 (2.0mm) → 6.06±1.1 (2.5mm); RTP_00 fell from 16.51±1.5 → 11.52±1.5 → 8.46±1.2; opalescence fell from 7.35±0.6 → 6.73±0.2 → 5.87±0.5; contrast ratio rose from 0.63±0.04 → 0.74±0.05 → 0.81±0.04 (more opaque with thickness). The 1.5→2.0 mm and 1.5→2.5 mm translucency differences exceeded published perceptibility/acceptability thresholds; the 2.0→2.5 mm difference stayed within the acceptability limit (non-linear, plateauing relationship).

**Degree of conversion** (significant material × thickness interaction, p<0.001):
- **1.5 mm**: flowable composite 36.57±1.8% (highest, p<0.05 vs both others) > dual-cure cement 28.01±5.2% ≈ bulk-fill 23.34±3.6% (NS between these two).
- **2.0 mm**: flowable composite still highest (30.18±1.9%), statistically similar to dual-cure cement (27.16±2.2%); bulk-fill lowest (22.69±2.9%), NS vs dual-cure.
- **2.5 mm**: dual-cure cement highest (30.88±2.5%) > bulk-fill (19.50±5.9%) > flowable composite lowest (11.69±2.2%) — flowable DC collapsed significantly from its 1.5/2.0 mm values.
- Dual-cure cement and bulk-fill composite each showed no significant DC difference across the three thicknesses (thickness-stable, p>0.05); flowable composite DC was highly thickness-dependent.

**Clinical bottom line (per authors)**: flowable composite is suitable for thin restorations (≤2.0 mm) where light transmission is adequate; dual-cure resin cement gives the most stable DC and is preferable for thicker restorations (>2.0 mm, up to 2.5 mm tested); bulk-fill composite is a reasonable light-cure alternative for thin-to-moderate thickness (≤2.0 mm) but should be used cautiously beyond that, as it does not surpass dual-cure cement's DC at 2.5 mm.
