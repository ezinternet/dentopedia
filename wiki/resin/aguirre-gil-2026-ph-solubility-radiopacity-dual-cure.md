## Three-line Summary
In-vitro study (BMC Oral Health 2026, n=30 discs) comparing physicochemical properties of two dual-cure resin cements (NX3 Nexus vs Variolink N) by measuring radiopacity, pH, and solubility per ISO 4049 protocols.

Variolink N had significantly higher radiopacity (2.00 vs 1.59 mmAl, p=0.026) due to ytterbium trifluoride fillers (Yb, Z=70 vs Ba, Z=56); NX3 Nexus had significantly higher (more neutral) pH (5.84 vs 5.32, p=0.008); solubility did not differ significantly (p=0.841), and both cements met ISO 4049:2019 thresholds.

Variolink N is preferred when radiographic marginal-excess detectability is critical (subgingival margins, implant restorations); both cements remain above the pH=3 pulpal-safety threshold.

## Summary
This in-vitro study compared three physicochemical properties — pH, solubility, and radiopacity — of two commonly used dual-cure resin cements, NX3 Nexus (Kerr) and Variolink N (Ivoclar Vivadent), using 8 mm × 1 mm discs (n=15 per cement, n=5 per subgroup/test) prepared and light-cured per manufacturer instructions. Radiopacity was measured against a 10-step aluminum wedge (ISO 4049); pH was measured directly on mixed cement paste; solubility was measured via mass change after 7-day water immersion. Variolink N exhibited significantly higher radiopacity, attributed to its ytterbium trifluoride (Yb, Z=70) filler versus NX3 Nexus's barium-glass fillers (Ba, Z=56). NX3 Nexus exhibited significantly higher (more neutral) pH. Solubility did not differ significantly between the two cements, and both remained within ISO 4049:2019 thresholds.

## Key Contributions
- Direct three-property (pH, solubility, radiopacity) in-vitro comparison of two widely used dual-cure resin cements in a single standardized protocol.
- Mechanistic explanation linking radiopacity differences to radiopacifier chemistry and atomic number (Yb vs Ba).
- Clinical framing: recommends Variolink N when radiographic detectability of marginal excess cement/integrity is critical (e.g., subgingival margins, implant-supported restorations); otherwise either cement is appropriate.
- Confirms both cements meet ISO 4049:2019 solubility (≤7.5 µg/mm³) and sorption (≤40 µg/mm³) thresholds.
- Both cements' pH remained above the pH=3 threshold associated with pulpal inflammation/postoperative sensitivity risk.

## Methodology
Experimental in-vitro study, Lima, Peru (Feb–Sept 2024), CRIS-guideline reporting. G*Power-calculated sample size: n=30 discs total (15 per cement: NX3 Nexus, Variolink N), subdivided n=5 per test (pH, solubility, radiopacity) per cement. Discs (8 mm × 1 mm) light-cured with Bluephase N LED (1200 mW/cm², 20 s). Radiopacity per ISO 4049 using PSPIX imaging plate/scanner + 10-step aluminum wedge reference, greyscale converted to mmAl via Adobe Photoshop CC (5 measurements/sample). pH measured with digital pH meter (PH55) on freshly mixed cement paste. Solubility via mass loss (analytical balance, 0.001 g precision) after 7-day 37°C water immersion, calculated as (M1−M2)/V in µg/mm³. Statistics: Student's t-test (radiopacity, normal distribution) and Mann-Whitney U test (pH, solubility, non-normal), SPSS v28.0, significance p<0.05.

## Results
| Test | NX3 Nexus (mean ± SD) | Variolink N (mean ± SD) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radiopacity (mmAl) | 1.59 ± 0.30 | 2.00 ± 0.15 | 0.026* |
| Solubility (µg/mm³) | 0.46 ± 1.66 | -0.46 ± 0.84 | 0.841 |
| pH | 5.84 ± 0.09 | 5.32 ± 0.04 | 0.008* |

(*significant, p<0.05; n=5 per cement per test)

- Variolink N: significantly higher radiopacity than NX3 Nexus — attributed to ytterbium trifluoride (YbF₃) filler vs NX3 Nexus's barium-glass fillers; higher atomic number (Yb Z=70 vs Ba Z=56) correlates with greater X-ray attenuation.
- No significant difference in solubility between cements; both remained below ISO 4049:2019 thresholds. Negative solubility values (Variolink N) consistent with net water sorption exceeding leachable mass loss.
- NX3 Nexus: significantly higher (more neutral) pH than Variolink N; both cements' pH remained above the pH=3 pulpal-safety threshold.
- Null hypothesis (no differences) partially rejected — significant differences found for pH and radiopacity, not solubility.
- Limitations: no simulation of dynamic in-vivo pH/salivary conditions; only initial post-cure pH measured (no longitudinal tracking); degree of conversion and mechanical properties (bond/compressive strength) not assessed.
