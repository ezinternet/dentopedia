## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of 64 articles (1985–2020) comparing all major dental photoinitiator systems. Camphorquinone (CQ, type-II) dominates commercial composites but causes yellowing and has cytotoxic/genotoxic concerns; type-I acylphosphines (TPO, BAPO) offer better DC and color stability but require polywave LEDs. Germanium compounds—especially Ivocerin—are the most promising alternatives: CQ-compatible absorption range, ~2× DC, superior hardness, and low toxicity profile.

## Background

Light-cured resin composites have been the standard dental restorative material since the 1970s. The photoinitiator system—typically 0.1–1 wt% of the composite—determines degree of conversion (전환율, Degree of Conversion, DC), mechanical properties, color stability, and biocompatibility. Camphorquinone (CQ) has been the gold standard since 1971 (Dart and Nemcek patent), but its yellow tint and cytotoxicity drive ongoing research into alternatives.

## Photoinitiator Classification

**Type-1 (Norrish I, alpha-cleavage)**
- Mechanism: homolytic C–P bond cleavage → 2 radicals per molecule (TPO), 4 for BAPO
- Absorption: shorter wavelength violet/near-UV (380–425 nm for TPO/BAPO)
- Advantage: no co-initiator needed; higher molar absorptivity; less yellow
- Disadvantage: requires polywave LED (standard 450–470 nm single-peak insufficient)
- Examples: TPO, BAPO, MAPO, novel TMBOPF, TOPF

**Type-2 (Norrish II, H-abstraction, bimolecular)**
- Mechanism: excited state + co-initiator (amine) → ketyl radical + aminoalkyl radical
- Absorption: 400–490 nm (visible blue range); matches standard dental LEDs
- Rate: slower (bimolecular); depends on amine concentration and diffusion
- Examples: CQ, PQ, benzophenone (BP)

**Hybrid (both mechanisms):** PPD (1-phenyl-1,2-propanedione)

## Individual Photoinitiators

### Camphorquinone (CQ) — Commercial Standard
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Norrish II alpha-diketone |
| Absorption range | 360–510 nm |
| Absorption max | ~468 nm |
| Typical concentration | 0.17–1.03 wt% resin (actual: 0.03–0.09 wt% composite) |
| Color | Intense yellow; poor photobleaching post-cure |
| Optimal CQ:EDMAB for max hardness | 1.44:0.42 or 1.05:1.65 mol% |
| Optimal CQ:EDMAB for max DC | 2.40:0.83 mol% |
| Cytotoxicity | Yes (concentration-dependent pulp cell toxicity) |
| Genotoxicity | ROS/RNS generation; genetic toxicity potential |

Co-initiator comparison (DMAEMA vs EDMAB at 0.5 wt% CQ):
- DMAEMA: DC 45% (10 s) / 62% (120 s) — most biocompatible, lower activity
- EDMAB: DC 65% (10 s) / 68% (120 s) — cytotoxic, more effective

### TPO (2,4,6-Trimethylbenzoyl-diphenylphosphine oxide) — Lucirin TPO
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Norrish I acylphosphine oxide |
| Absorption | 380–425 nm, max ~381–400 nm |
| Co-initiator needed | No |
| DC vs CQ | ~10% higher (0.86 wt% → 74%; 0.22 wt% → 68%) |
| Knoop hardness (top) | 33.5 ± 2.4 vs CQ 26.3 ± 0.7 |
| Depth of cure | 3.2 ± 0.1 mm vs CQ 3.7 ± 0.1 mm |
| Color stability | Highest (no amine group → no long-term darkening) |
| Genotoxicity | None |
| Pulp toxicity | None (Van Der Lann et al.) |
| Light requirement | **Polywave LED required** |
| Commercial examples | Tetric EvoCeram Bleach, Vit-1-escence |
| Disadvantages | Higher polymerization shrinkage stress; lower depth of cure |

### BAPO (Bisacylphosphine oxide) / Irgacure 819
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Norrish I, symmetric structure |
| Absorption | 365–416 nm, max ~371–400 nm |
| Radicals per molecule | 4 (vs 2 for TPO) |
| Molar extinction coefficient | 870 L/mol·cm (CQ: 33 L/mol·cm) |
| Quantum yield | 5× CQ |
| Knoop hardness (top) | 32.9 ± 1.7 (similar to TPO, both > CQ) |
| Flexural strength | Highest among CQ/TPO/BAPO |
| Cytotoxicity | High (primary cell growth inhibition — Wang et al.) |
| Genotoxicity | Weak genotoxic effect (less than CQ) |
| Solubility | Poor in many monomers (solid, symmetric structure) |
| Shelf life | Problems with UDMA matrix |

### PPD (1-Phenyl-1,2-propanedione)
- Absorption: 300–400 nm, max 393–410 nm; pale yellow viscous liquid
- Hybrid mechanism (both photocleavage + H-abstraction)
- Synergistic with CQ at 1:1 and 1:4 PPD:CQ ratios → maximum DC
- Less yellow than CQ; suitable for esthetic restorations
- Controversy: Brandt et al. found combined CQ/PPD did NOT consistently increase DC; result depends on light-curing unit type
- Lower polymerization rate → potentially inferior crosslink density

### Phenanthrenequinone (PQ)
- Aromatic diketone, absorption max ~420 nm; orange solid; requires co-initiators
- Lower color stability vs CQ; lower depth of cure vs CQ (absorption nearer UV)
- DPI co-initiator with PQ increases DC and reduces yellow values

### Germanium Compounds (Novel)

**BTMGe / DBDEGe**
- Absorption: BTMGe max 411 nm; DBDEGe max 418 nm (compatible with standard LEDs)
- No amine co-initiator needed; setting time 3–5 s vs CQ 8 s
- Low cytotoxicity; no gene mutations; slight yellowing (reducible with UV stabilizer)

**Ivocerin (dibenzoyldiethylgermanium)**
- Absorption: 390–445 nm, max 418 nm — overlaps CQ range → **no special light unit required**
- Luting cement DC: ~87% vs CQ ~44%
- Vickers hardness (luting cement): 47 VHN vs CQ 33 VHN
- Flexural strength comparable to CQ (119.93 vs 120.41 MPa)
- Low cytotoxicity; no mutagenic effect
- Currently patented by Ivoclar Vivadent (Variolink E product line)

## Measurement Methods

- **Degree of Conversion (DC)**: FTIR spectroscopy — primary outcome metric
- **Surface hardness**: Knoop (KHN) or Vickers (VHN) microhardness
- **Flexural strength and elastic (Young's) modulus**: mechanical bending test
- **Depth of cure**: ISO 4049 guideline (guillotine method)
- **Color measurement**: CIE L\*a\*b\* system; yellowing measured by b\* parameter
- **Cytotoxicity**: cell culture assays (MTT, cell growth inhibition)
- **Genotoxicity**: micronucleus test, comet assay (ROS/RNS production)

## Clinical Implications

1. **Polywave LED units** are required for TPO- and BAPO-containing composites (standard single-peak 450–470 nm insufficient)
2. **Ivocerin** (Variolink E luting cement) provides the best DC and hardness with CQ-compatible light units — most clinically accessible alternative
3. **CQ yellowing**: partly addressable by synergistic PPD or TPO use with polywave LEDs
4. **Co-initiator selection**: DMAEMA preferred for biocompatibility despite lower efficiency; EDMAB more effective but cytotoxic — leaches from uncured resin
5. **Thick-layer placement** possible with TPO (cures >2 mm vs standard 2 mm CQ layers)
6. **Material labeling**: manufacturers should declare photoinitiator type to guide LED selection

## Limitations

- Narrative review (not systematic/meta-analysis); no formal quality assessment of included studies
- Predominantly in vitro; no clinical survival or patient outcome data
- Literature range 1985–2020; more recent formulations (post-2020 Ivocerin-in-composites) not covered
- Proprietary formulations limit direct comparison of commercial products

## Future Perspectives

- Novel acylphosphine oxides (TMBOPF, TOPF): BAPO-like reactivity with improved color
- CQ/R3GeH/iodonium salt combinations for blue-LED-compatible amine-free systems
- Coumarin-based iodonium photoinitiators (P3C-Sb): DC improvement when combined with CQ
- Liquid BAPO (LMBAPO): improved solubility — promising but not yet applied in dentistry (as of 2020)
