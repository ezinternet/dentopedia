## Three-line Summary
SANRA-guided narrative review (PubMed, n=181 refs, 2000–2026) synthesising LCU technology evolution, selection criteria, and adjunctive optical features across polywave/monowave LED, QTH, PAC, and laser systems.
Polywave 3rd-generation LED LCUs show modest but consistent SR/MA-supported advantage over monowave for mixed Norrish type I/II photoinitiator composites; delivered radiant exposure (J/cm²), spectral compatibility, beam homogeneity, and effective tip diameter — NOT manufacturer-stated irradiance (mW/cm²) — determine curing adequacy; clinical variables (distance, angulation, maintenance, battery, training) often override device-generation effects; intrapulpal ΔT ≤5.5 °C not exceeded by ≤2–3 standard curing cycles.
Clinicians should adopt polywave LED LCUs as the conservative default, extend curing time rather than use "boost" modes, and note that integrated fluorescence/NIR diagnostic features await independent validation.

## Summary
This 2026 SANRA-compliant narrative review by Ceinos et al. synthesises 181 references (PubMed 2000–2026) on light-curing unit (LCU) performance in restorative dentistry. The central thesis is that light-curing must be treated as a controlled energy-delivery process, not a timed procedural step: the clinically meaningful parameter is radiant exposure (J/cm²) — irradiance integrated over time — not the manufacturer-reported irradiance (mW/cm²) alone. The review traces LCU evolution from QTH through plasma arc, lasers, and three LED generations, establishing 3rd-generation polywave LED LCUs (dual violet ~395–415 nm + blue ~450–470 nm) as the most versatile option for routine practice because they can activate both Norrish type II (camphorquinone, CQ, peak ~468 nm) and type I (TPO ~380 nm, PPD ~410 nm, Ivocerin 400–420 nm) photoinitiators. For CQ-dominant adhesives, well-maintained 2nd-generation monowave LEDs perform comparably when radiant exposure is adequate. Beam inhomogeneity — caused by asymmetric LED chip placement — is invisible to standard dental radiometers and leads to localised undercuring. Clinical factors (distance, angulation, maintenance, battery state, training) are frequently more decisive than device generation. Intrapulpal temperature rise of ≤5.5 °C is not exceeded by ≤2–3 standard polywave curing cycles; air-syringe cooling is effective. Emerging adjunctive features (fluorescence-aided identification, transillumination, beam-shaping tips) are conceptually sound but lack independent comparative evidence.

## Key Contributions
- Reframes LCU selection around **radiant exposure** (J/cm²) rather than irradiance (mW/cm²)
- "Boost" modes often deliver *less* total energy than standard modes within manufacturer-recommended exposure times
- Polywave LCUs have SR/MA-supported modest advantage for mixed Norrish type I/II composites; advantage less clear for adhesives (mostly CQ-dominant)
- Beam spatial inhomogeneity from asymmetric LED chip placement undetectable by conventional radiometers → hot spots and undercuring zones
- Effective tip diameter critical for wide posterior restorations and indirect luting; larger tip requires proportionally more total radiant power
- Intrapulpal ΔT ≤5.5 °C not exceeded with ≤2–3 standard polywave cycles; air syringe reduces pulpal heat effectively
- Adequate conversion under indirect restorations achievable at 2–3 mm thickness with prolonged irradiation; predictability decreases beyond 3 mm
- Fluorescence-aided removal of adhesive remnants and excess cement: useful but evidence limited, highly operator/material dependent
- TPO regulation: EU CLP CMR category 1B classification may drive substitution with TPO-L in future formulations

## Methodology
- **Design**: Narrative review; SANRA quality checklist
- **Database**: PubMed/MEDLINE only; January 2000–February 9, 2026
- **References**: n=181 (128 structured searches + 53 citation tracking/expert)
- **Three structured search equations**: technology evolution | device selection & clinical use | adjunctive optical features
- **Screening**: Single author; no formal inter-rater reliability; no risk-of-bias tool
- **Limitations**: Single database, English only, single screener, predominantly in vitro evidence, no long-term clinical outcomes for multifunctional LCUs
