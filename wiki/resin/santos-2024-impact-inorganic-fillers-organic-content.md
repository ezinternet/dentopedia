## Three-line Summary

Scoping review of 15 studies (PubMed 2011–2024) on degree of conversion (DC) of resin-matrix cements: dual-cure cements achieve the highest DC (70–90%) with superior flexural strength and hardness. Ceramic thickness critically modulates DC — 0.4 mm veneer yields ~83% DC vs. ~77% through 1.5 mm. Filler composition, monomer type (Bis-GMA/UDMA/TEGDMA ratio), and refractive index matching all influence polymerization adequacy.

## 세줄요약

레진 시멘트 전환율(Degree of Conversion, DC) 스코핑 리뷰(15편, PubMed 2011–2024): 듀얼큐어 시멘트가 DC 70–90%로 최고, 굴곡강도·경도도 우수. 세라믹 두께가 DC에 직접 영향 — 0.4 mm 비니어 시 ~83% vs 1.5 mm 시 ~77%. 충전재 조성, 단량체 종류(비스-GMA/UDMA/TEGDMA 비율), 굴절률 매칭이 중합 적절성을 결정.

## Study Design

- **Type**: Scoping review (not systematic review with meta-analysis)
- **Database**: PubMed (January 2011 – January 2024)
- **Initial hits**: 140 studies; 38 duplicates removed; 102 screened by title/abstract; 15 included
- **PICO**: "How different is the degree of conversion of monomers comparing several types of resin-matrix cements?"
- **Measurement methods**: FTIR spectroscopy (10 studies, 66%), Raman spectroscopy (3), NIRS (1), hardness/flexural strength proxy (3)

## Key Findings

### Degree of Conversion by Polymerization Mode

| Mode | DC Range | Mechanical Properties |
|---|---|---|
| Dual-cured | Highest (70–90% in high-DC studies) | Best flexural strength, hardness |
| Light-cured | Intermediate | Moderate |
| Self-cured (chemical only) | Lowest | Weakest |

- 8/15 studies (53%) reported high DC (70–90%)
- 7/15 studies (46%) reported lower DC values
- Dual-cure superiority: both chemical initiator (peroxide) + light-induced (camphorquinone/amine) pathways active simultaneously

### Effect of Ceramic Veneer Thickness on DC

| Veneer Thickness | Measured DC |
|---|---|
| 0.4 mm | ~83% |
| 1.5 mm | ~77% |

- Light attenuation through ceramic is the primary mechanism
- Translucency of prosthetic material (not just thickness) modulates light transmission
- Glass-ceramic (e.g., lithium disilicate) more translucent than zirconia; partially stabilized zirconia less translucent than fully stabilized

### Filler Composition Effects

- Filler weight content in commercial cements: 36–87%
- High filler content → increased opacity → reduced light transmittance → lower DC
- Filler refractive index matching to organic matrix → better light transmission → higher DC
- Particle sizes: micro-scale (0.1–10 µm) and nano-scale (20–60 nm)
- High nano/micro particle content increases strength but can reduce polymerization depth

### Monomer Composition Effects

- **Bis-GMA**: high viscosity → low mobility → decreases DC; but good overall mechanical properties
- **TEGDMA**: low viscosity diluent → improves DC by increasing monomer mobility and reactivity
- **UDMA**: flexibility + crosslinking capacity; can substitute or complement Bis-GMA
- High molecular mass monomers: lower mobility → lower shrinkage but lower DC

### DC Threshold for Clinical Adequacy

- DC >55% recommended for occlusal restorations (reported by multiple studies)
- Below this threshold: water sorption increases, strength/hardness decrease, biocompatibility issues (unreacted monomers, inflammatory reactions to dentin-pulp complex)

## Inorganic Fillers in Commercial Cements

Types identified across reviewed products:
- Amorphous silica, strontium glass, zirconium silicate
- Barium borosilicate glass, fluoro-aluminosilicate glass
- Sodium persulfate cupric acetate, ytterbium fluoride

Cements reviewed: Panavia™ (Kuraray Noritake), Multilink™ (Ivoclar Vivadent), RelyX™ (3M ESPE), Choice 2™ (Bisco), Nexus 3™ (Kerr), G-CEM LinkAce™ (GC Corp), Enamel HFO™ (Micerium)

## Clinical Implications

1. **Thick indirect restorations** (crowns, thick veneers >1.5 mm): use dual-cure cement — chemical component ensures adequate DC regardless of light attenuation
2. **Thin ceramic veneers** (≤0.5 mm): light-cure cements acceptable; translucency allows sufficient light transmission
3. **LCU parameters**: distance <8 mm, adequate irradiance, sufficient exposure time; energy density 20 J/cm² associated with higher DC
4. **Filler-monomer matching**: high refractive index fillers (e.g., zirconia) allow better light transmission; critical for dual-cure cements with light-activation component
5. **Opacity/shade**: higher opacity cements → lower DC; extend curing time to compensate

## Limitations

- Only 15 studies; heterogeneous LCU irradiance across studies makes comparison difficult
- Predominantly in vitro data; limited in vivo / clinical evidence
- Scoping (not systematic) review — no formal bias/quality assessment
