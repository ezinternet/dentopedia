## Three-line Summary

In vitro FTIR + VHN study on BisGMA-TEGDMA model resin (n=6/group) comparing oxygen-exposed vs oxygen-protected surfaces during the delayed post-curing stage (DPCS) up to 360 min post light-cure. Degree of conversion (DC%) rose 50%→65% in oxygen-protected specimens but stagnated in air-exposed ones; surface Vickers hardness diverged to 9.10 vs 4.80 VHN (p<0.005) at 6 h. Oxygen inhibition of free-radical polymerization persists well beyond light cessation, reducing both conversion and mechanical surface quality.

## Summary

Aromaa and Vallittu (2018) investigated the interplay between oxygen inhibition and the delayed post-curing stage (DPCS) — the window of continued free-radical polymerization that follows light-curing. Using ATR-FTIR to track conversion and Vickers hardness as a surface mechanical proxy, they monitored BisGMA-TEGDMA specimens for 360 minutes post-cure under two conditions: open to air (O-exposed) or shielded from oxygen (O-protected). The pivotal finding was that oxygen effectively halted further conversion after light cessation in exposed specimens, while protected specimens continued to polymerize from ~50% to ~65% DC. This conversion gap translated to a near-doubling of surface hardness (9.10 vs 4.80 VHN at 6 h) and was corroborated by strong DPCS–hardness correlations. The study provides in vitro mechanistic grounding for clinical interventions — glycerin application, Mylar strips — that exclude oxygen from restoration margins during and after curing.

## Key Contributions

- Oxygen inhibition operates **beyond the curing phase**: DPCS conversion gain is completely abolished by air exposure
- Surface hardness penalty at 6 h: O-exposed achieves only ~53% of O-protected hardness (4.80 vs 9.10 VHN)
- Strong mechanistic correlation: DPCS duration → DC% → surface VHN, both O-groups (p<0.001)
- First study to isolate DPCS-specific oxygen inhibition as a distinct, quantifiable surface degradation mechanism

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | In vitro controlled experiment |
| Material | BisGMA-TEGDMA (60:40 wt%), CQ-amine initiator |
| Curing | 430–480 nm, 1200 mW/mm², 20 s |
| Groups | O-exposed (air) vs O-protected (n=6 each) |
| Outcomes | DC% (ATR-FTIR), VHN at 0, ~60, ~180, 360 min |
| Statistics | Paired comparison p<0.005; Pearson r |

## Results

| Metric | O-protected | O-exposed |
|---|---|---|
| DC% at light-cure end | ~50% | ~50% |
| DC% at 360 min | ~65% | ~50% (plateau) |
| VHN at 360 min | **9.10** | **4.80** (p<0.005) |
| VHN–DPCS r | 0.950 | 0.940 |
| DC–time r | 0.941 | 0.780 |

The DC% plateau in the O-exposed group demonstrates that reactive radicals generated during DPCS are efficiently quenched by atmospheric oxygen rather than consumed by monomer conversion.
