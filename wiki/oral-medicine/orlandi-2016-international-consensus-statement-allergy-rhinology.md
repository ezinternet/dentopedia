## Three-line Summary

International Consensus Statement (ICAR:RS) by 75 global rhinology experts synthesizing evidence-based reviews with recommendations (EBRRs) for 140+ topics across all rhinosinusitis subtypes (ARS, RARS, CRSsNP, CRSwNP, AECRS, pediatric RS).
Evidence grades assigned per topic using Oxford LOE system; aggregate grades predominantly C (low-level evidence), highlighting significant gaps in high-quality RCTs and meta-analyses for most RS management decisions.
Provides the first comprehensive, critically appraised evidence compendium for RS, establishing baseline recommendations while explicitly identifying research priorities to upgrade the evidence base.

## Summary

The **International Consensus Statement on Allergy and Rhinology: Rhinosinusitis (ICAR:RS)** is a landmark 2016 multinational consensus document produced by 75 primary authors (and 46 contributing authors) from 19 countries across six continents. Published as a 188-page supplement in *International Forum of Allergy & Rhinology*, it represents the first comprehensive, evidence-graded compendium covering the full spectrum of rhinosinusitis (RS): acute RS (ARS), recurrent acute RS (RARS), chronic RS without nasal polyps (CRSsNP), chronic RS with nasal polyps (CRSwNP), acute exacerbation of CRS (AECRS), pediatric RS, surgical management, and special populations (immunodeficiency, cystic fibrosis, AERD, fungal RS, pregnancy).

Unlike traditional narrative reviews or consensus panels driven by expert opinion, ICAR:RS employed a structured **evidence-based review with recommendations (EBRR)** methodology for over 140 clinical questions. Each topic underwent systematic literature search, evidence grading via the Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine Level of Evidence (LOE) system (1a–5), and assignment of an aggregate Grade of Evidence (A–D). The entire manuscript underwent stepwise anonymous writing and iterative review by all authors to achieve consensus.

**Critical finding**: The vast majority of RS recommendations rest on **Grade C evidence** (level 3–4 studies: non-randomized cohorts, case series, expert opinion). Few topics achieved Grade A (consistent level 1 evidence) or Grade B (consistent level 2–3). This evidence gap is explicitly mapped in Section XIII (Knowledge Gaps), identifying 30+ specific research priorities.

**Dental relevance**: Section V.B.1.e addresses **odontogenic infections as a contributing factor for ARS** (aggregate Grade C), citing periapical abscess–maxillary sinus microbiological concordance, oroantral fistula as the only independent predictor of acute maxillary RS (Bomeli 2009), and implant penetration data (Abi Najm 2013: <3 mm penetration not associated with RS). This makes ICAR:RS a key primary source for [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/psillas-2020-odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-comprehensive-review]] and [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/lin-2024-odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-expert-consensus]].

## Key Contributions

1. **First global allergy-rhinology consensus on RS** — 75 authors, 19 countries, 6 continents; bridges allergy/immunology and rhinology perspectives.
2. **Structured EBRR methodology** — 140+ clinical questions addressed via systematic evidence review, anonymous iterative writing, and full-author consensus; minimizes expert-opinion bias.
3. **Transparent evidence grading** — Every recommendation tagged with Oxford LOE + aggregate Grade (A–D); reveals that most RS management rests on Grade C evidence.
4. **Comprehensive subtype coverage** — ARS, RARS, CRSsNP, CRSwNP, AECRS, pediatric RS, surgery, special populations — all in one document.
5. **Explicit knowledge-gap map** (Section XIII) — 30+ specific research priorities (e.g., ARS antibiotic RCTs, CRS endotype-driven biologics trials, prospective odontogenic RS studies).
6. **Odontogenic sinusitis recognition** — Section V.B.1.e grades odontogenic ARS evidence as Grade C, documents anaerobic microbiology, periapical–sinus concordance, and implant penetration thresholds.
7. **Clinical algorithms with evidence grades** — Diagnostic criteria, medical therapy (INCS Grade A for CRS, antibiotics Grade B for ABRS, saline Grade B), surgical indications (ESS Grade B), postoperative care.

## Methodology

- **Panel**: 75 primary authors (rhinology/otolaryngology/allergy leaders) + 46 contributing authors; international multidisciplinary representation.
- **Topic formulation**: 140+ clinical questions across 13 sections (Definitions, Burden, ARS, RARS, CRSsNP, CRSwNP, AECRS, Surgery, Pediatric, Special Considerations, Knowledge Gaps, References, Appendix).
- **Evidence review per topic**:
  - Systematic search (PubMed, Cochrane, Embase) per clinical question
  - Evidence-Based Review (EBR) where evidence insufficient for recommendations
  - Evidence-Based Review with Recommendations (EBRR) where sufficient evidence existed
  - Oxford CEBM LOE (2011) applied to each study: 1a (SR of RCTs) → 5 (expert opinion)
  - Aggregate Grade of Evidence: **A** (consistent level 1), **B** (consistent level 2–3), **C** (level 4–5), **D** (expert opinion)
- **Consensus process**: Stepwise anonymous drafting → iterative full-author review → final consensus on recommendations and evidence grades.
- **Declared limitations**: Not a meta-analysis; not a clinical practice guideline (CPG); does not define standard of care or medical necessity; recommendations context-dependent on evidence robustness; static snapshot (2015 search cutoff).

## Results

### Evidence Grade Distribution (Representative)

| Domain | Key Recommendations | Aggregate Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **RS Definitions** (Section III) | Clinical criteria for ARS, ABRS, AVRS, CRSsNP, CRSwNP, RARS, AECRS, pediatric RS | D (consensus) |
| **ARS Diagnosis** (V.C) | Clinical diagnosis; duration >10d or "double sickening" for ABRS; imaging not routine | B (diagnosis), C (differentiation) |
| **ARS Antibiotics** (V.D) | ABRS: amoxicillin-clavulanate 5–10d; watchful waiting option for mild cases | B |
| **ARS Odontogenic Factor** (V.B.1.e) | ODS considered in unilateral/uncontrolled ARS; anaerobes prevalent; periapical–sinus microbiological concordance | C |
| **CRSsNP Medical Therapy** (VII) | INCS first-line (Grade A); saline irrigation (Grade B); antibiotics (Grade C); systemic steroids (Grade C) | A (INCS), B–C (others) |
| **CRSwNP Medical Therapy** (VIII) | INCS (Grade A); systemic steroids short-course (Grade B); leukotriene antagonists (Grade C); biologics emerging | A–B |
| **ESS for CRS** (X) | Indicated after failed appropriate medical therapy; SNOT-22 improvement ~30–40 points; complication rate low | B |
| **Pediatric RS** (XI) | Adenoidectomy first-line (Grade B); INCS (Grade B); antibiotics (Grade C); ESS reserved (Grade C) | B–C |
| **Special Populations** (XII) | AERD: aspirin desensitization (Grade B); CF: aggressive medical/surgical (Grade C); immunodeficiency: IgG replacement (Grade C) | B–C |

### Dental-Relevant Evidence Summary

| Topic | Finding | Grade | Key References |
|---|---|---|---|
| Odontogenic ARS prevalence | Historically 10–15%; oroantral fistula only independent predictor (Bomeli 2009) | C | Bomeli 2009 (level 4) |
| Periapical abscess → sinus concordance | Microbiological match between periapical aspirate and sinus flora | C | Regev 1995, Jung 2007 (level 4) |
| Implant penetration <3 mm | Not associated with clinical/radiological RS (20-yr retrospective, n=70) | C | Abi Najm 2013 (level 4) |
| Implant penetration 4 mm | Associated with mucosal thickening | C | Jung 2007 (level 4) |
| Periodontal disease + root projection/abscess | Interaction effect predictive of maxillary opacification | C | Bomeli 2009 (level 4) |
| Dislodged bone grafts/implants blocking ostium | Case reports only; ARS not defined in reports | C | Level 4 case reports |
