## Three-line Summary

Narrative pharmacology review (Frontiers in Pharmacology 2025) updating approximately 60 years of anti-HSV-1 drug development across nucleoside/nucleotide analogs, helicase-primase inhibitors (HPIs), and immunomodulators/natural products; global HSV-1 seroprevalence ~64% in individuals under 50.

Nucleoside analogs (aciclovir, valaciclovir, famciclovir) remain first-line but face resistance via thymidine-kinase and DNA-polymerase mutations, especially in immunocompromised hosts; non-nucleoside HPIs (pritelivir, amenamevir) retain activity against resistant strains; no approved HSV vaccine exists.

No single next-generation agent has reached clinical approval for HSV-1, and most novel candidates (latency-targeting, gene-editing) remain preclinical — the absence of a vaccine sustains the drug-development imperative.

## 세줄요약

서술 약리 리뷰(Frontiers in Pharmacology, 2025) — 약 60년간의 항-HSV-1 약물 개발(뉴클레오시드/뉴클레오티드 유사체·헬리카제-프라이메이스 억제제(Helicase-Primase Inhibitor, HPI)·면역조절제/천연물) 개관; 전 세계 50세 미만 HSV-1 혈청양성률 ~64%.

뉴클레오시드 유사체(아시클로버, 발라시클로버, 팜시클로버)가 1차 치료제이나 면역저하 환자에서 티미딘 키나아제(thymidine kinase)·DNA 중합효소 돌연변이로 내성 발생; 비뉴클레오시드 HPI(프리텔리버, 아메나메비르)는 내성 균주에 활성 유지; 승인된 HSV 백신 없음.

차세대 약물은 대부분 임상 전 단계이며, 잠재성(latency) 표적·유전자편집 전략도 연구 중이나 아직 미승인 — 백신 공백이 항바이러스제 개발의 지속적 동기.

## Summary

This pharmacology review updates the anti-HSV-1 therapeutic landscape. Nucleoside/nucleotide analogs (aciclovir, valaciclovir, famciclovir) remain first-line but face resistance via thymidine-kinase and DNA-polymerase mutations, especially in immunocompromised hosts. Non-nucleoside helicase-primase inhibitors (pritelivir, amenamevir) offer activity against resistant strains. The review also surveys immunomodulatory and natural-product candidates and emerging latency-targeting/gene-editing strategies, motivated by the continued lack of an approved HSV vaccine.

## Key Contributions

- Updates mechanisms of approved and investigational anti-HSV-1 agents.
- Centers nucleoside-analog resistance as the core clinical problem.
- Reviews helicase-primase inhibitors as a resistance-active class.
- Surveys immunomodulatory, natural-product, and latency-targeting directions.

## Methodology

Narrative review of HSV-1 replication (entry via gB/gC/gD and heparan sulfate; sensory-ganglion latency) and the pharmacology of antiviral drug classes.

## Results

Global HSV-1 seroprevalence ~64% (<50 y). Nucleoside analogs remain standard despite resistance; helicase-primase inhibitors provide an alternative mechanism. No approved vaccine exists, sustaining the drug-development imperative. Most novel agents are preclinical/early-phase.
