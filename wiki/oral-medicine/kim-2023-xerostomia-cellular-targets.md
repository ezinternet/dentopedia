## One-line Summary

Narrative mechanistic review (Int J Mol Sci 2023) mapping saliva secretion at the cellular level — parasympathetic neurotransmitter → GPCR (M3 mAChR) → PLC/IP3 → ER Ca²⁺ release + store-operated Ca²⁺ entry (SOCE) → AQP5 apical trafficking → water secretion — and proposing GPCR, IP3R, SOCE, and AQP5 as druggable cellular targets in xerostomia.

## 한줄요약

침 분비의 세포 기전 — 부교감신경 전달물질 → G단백연결수용체(GPCR, M3 무스카린수용체) → PLC/IP3 → 소포체 Ca²⁺ 방출 + 저장고작동 Ca²⁺ 유입(SOCE) → 아쿠아포린5(AQP5) 정단막 이동 → 물 분비 — 을 정리하고, GPCR·IP3R·SOCE·AQP5를 구강건조(xerostomia)의 치료 표적으로 제시한 기전 리뷰 (IJMS 2023, 서울대 치대). 구강건조 유병률 10~50%(여성 30%·고령 50%↑), FDA 승인 완치약은 없음.

## Summary

This single-author review from Seoul National University School of Dentistry explains the cellular and molecular basis of salivation that underlies clinical xerostomia. A healthy adult secretes 0.5–1.5 L of saliva daily (90% from parotid, submandibular, sublingual glands), driven by **polarized acinar cells** whose tight junctions enforce unidirectional secretion. Salivation is triggered when parasympathetic acetylcholine binds **M3 muscarinic GPCRs**, activating PLC → IP3 → **ER Ca²⁺ release via IP3R2/IP3R3**, sustained by **store-operated Ca²⁺ entry (SOCE: STIM1 + Orai1/TRPC1)**. The rising intracellular Ca²⁺ opens K⁺/Cl⁻ channels and drives **AQP5** trafficking to the apical membrane so water follows the osmotic gradient. Knockout evidence anchors each node (M1/M3, IP3R2/R3, AQP5 KO all abolish or halve salivation). Xerostomia — prevalence 10–50%, rising with aging and polypharmacy — is caused mainly by xerogenic drugs (>400), head-and-neck radiation, and Sjögren's syndrome; current sialagogues (pilocarpine, cevimeline) act on these receptors but cause systemic effects, and no permanent FDA-approved cure exists. The author frames **GPCR, IP3R, SOCE, and AQP5 as therapeutic targets** and argues for acinar-cell-specific GPCR agonists.

## Key Contributions

- A **cell-level secretory pathway map** (GPCR → IP3R → SOCE → AQP5) that mechanistically underlies clinical dry mouth.
- Explains **why sialagogues work and why they have off-target effects**, and why radiation hyposalivation is refractory.
- Consolidates **KO-mouse evidence** for each signaling node and structural **aging** data.
- Identifies **acinar-specific GPCR agonists** (mAChR, P2Y2R) as a route to fewer systemic side effects.

## Methodology

Narrative mechanistic review integrating salivary gland anatomy/histology, knockout-mouse physiology, human labial-gland receptor expression, and RNA-seq cellular heterogeneity. Ductal system converts isotonic acinar fluid into hypotonic saliva (secretes HCO3⁻/K⁺, reabsorbs Na⁺/Cl⁻). Resting acinar [Ca²⁺]i ≈ 50–100 nM.

## Results

| Node / cause | Finding |
|---|---|
| M1/M3 mAChR double-KO | Salivation completely lost regardless of pilocarpine dose |
| IP3R2/IP3R3 double-KO | Pilocarpine salivation severely impaired; die <4 wk without wet food |
| AQP5-KO | ~60% reduction in pilocarpine-induced saliva |
| Resting [Ca²⁺]i | ≈ 50–100 nM |
| Persistent xerostomia prevalence | 10–50% (≈20% conservative); women ≤30%, elderly ≤50% |
| Aging acinar volume loss | SM ~30%, SL ~25%, PAR ~12% |
| Disease burden | ~550,000 H&N radiation/yr; >4M Sjögren's; >400 xerogenic drugs |
| GPR39 (zinc) | Zinc gargle ↑ salivation incl. Sjögren's/hyposalivation |
| P2Y2R agonist diquafosol | Approved for dry eye (Korea/Japan, Diquas) |
