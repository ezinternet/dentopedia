## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PubMed, CINAHL, Embase to August 2022; PRISMA/CRD) of anticholinergic use or burden versus xerostomia and/or xerophthalmia in people aged 65 or older; 1211 records, 10 studies included (mostly observational, one RCT).

Anticholinergic use was significantly associated with xerostomia in 4 studies; high anticholinergic burden was more closely associated with xerostomia than low/intermediate burden; xerostomia prevalence 10.6-59.2% and xerophthalmia 5.1-34%; the single RCT (ADS reduced by 2 units) showed no salivary flow change at 4 weeks.

Evidence is limited by heterogeneous, mostly non-validated measurement tools and incomplete confounder control; xerophthalmia evidence is too sparse to conclude.

## 세줄요약

65세 이상 노인에서 항콜린 약물 사용·항콜린 부담 (Anticholinergic burden)과 구강건조증 (Xerostomia)·안구건조증 (Xerophthalmia)의 연관성을 본 체계적 문헌고찰 (PubMed, CINAHL, Embase, 2022년 8월까지); 1211편 검색, 10편 포함 (대부분 관찰연구, 무작위대조시험 1편).

항콜린 약물 사용은 4편에서 구강건조증과 유의한 연관; 높은 항콜린 부담에서 연관이 더 뚜렷하고 낮음~중간 부담에서는 불명확; 구강건조증 유병률 10.6-59.2%, 안구건조증 5.1-34%; 유일한 무작위대조시험 (ADS 2점 감소)은 4주 후 침 분비 변화 없음.

검증되지 않은 다양한 측정 도구와 교란변수 통제 미흡이 주요 한계이며, 안구건조증은 연구가 너무 적어 결론 불가.

## Summary

This systematic review (Prado-Mel et al., BMC Pharmacol Toxicol 2022; source: PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40360-022-00637-8)) searched PubMed, CINAHL and Embase to August 2022 for studies in people aged 65 or older linking anticholinergic use or burden to xerostomia or xerophthalmia. Ten studies were included. The authors conclude there is a closer connection between high anticholinergic burden or anticholinergic use and xerostomia, while xerophthalmia data are too few to conclude. Quality was limited by non-validated tools and inadequate confounder control. No meta-analysis was performed.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates primary-study evidence on anticholinergic exposure and xerostomia in older adults.
- Highlights that the association is clearer at high burden and less conclusive at low/intermediate burden.
- Identifies measurement heterogeneity (different scales and xerostomia questionnaires) as the main barrier to comparison.

## Methodology

- PRISMA/CRD-style review; 1211 records, 11 duplicates, 1136 excluded on title/abstract, 64 pre-selected, 10 included.
- Appraisal: JBI (cross-sectional), NOS (cohort/case-control), RoB 2 (RCT).
- Design and patient counts differ between abstract (six cross-sectional, two cohort, one case-control, one RCT; 3535 patients) and full text (six descriptive, three analytical observational, one trial; 3622 patients, mean age over 75).

## Results

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Xerostomia prevalence | 10.6-59.2% (seven studies) |
| Xerophthalmia prevalence | 5.1-34% (three studies) |
| Anticholinergic exposure | 3.9-100% |
| Use vs xerostomia | Significant association in four studies |
| Use vs xerophthalmia | One study; no association |
| Burden vs xerostomia | Positive association with high vs lower/no burden; no association when any burden vs none (one study) |
| RCT | Median ADS reduced by 2 units; no salivary difference at 4 weeks |
| Quality | Two cross-sectional studies adequate (at least 7/8 JBI); cohorts NOS 6 and 5; case-control NOS 6; RCT low risk of bias |

Confounders: polypharmacy considered in all but one study; none addressed head and neck radiotherapy, eye surgery or Sjögren syndrome. Scales used: ARS, ADS, DBI, ACB.
