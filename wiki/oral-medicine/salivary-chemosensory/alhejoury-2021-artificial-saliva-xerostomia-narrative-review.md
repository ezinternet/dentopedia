## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (J Pharm Bioallied Sci 2021, no stated systematic search strategy) surveying artificial saliva and sialogogue options for therapeutic management of xerostomia and salivary gland hypofunction (SGH).

Catalogues topical stimulants (flavored gums, citric acid), systemic sialogogues (pilocarpine 5–10mg 3–4x/day, cevimeline 30mg/day, anetholetrithione, yohimbine, interferon-α lozenges), saliva substitutes (mucin/carboxymethylcellulose-based, Recaldent, xylitol/Biotene/BioXtra products), and emerging approaches (electrostimulation devices, aquaporin-1 gene therapy, salivary pacemakers).

As a narrative (not systematic) review with no stated search methodology or risk-of-bias assessment, treat its claims as a practical treatment-options catalogue rather than graded evidence; pilocarpine/cevimeline dosing and contraindications are the most actionable, clinically vetted details.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.4103/jpbs.jpbs_236_21](https://doi.org/10.4103/jpbs.jpbs_236_21)), this narrative review surveys the current therapeutic landscape for managing xerostomia and salivary gland hypofunction (SGH) using artificial saliva and sialogogues. Normal saliva provides lubrication, digestive, taste, mucosal-protective, and antimicrobial functions; its loss drives caries risk, speech/chewing difficulty, and reduced oral-health-related quality of life. The review organizes management into general principles (patient education, oral hygiene reinforcement with fluoride/chlorhexidine, monitoring for candidiasis), topical stimulants (flavored/sugar-free chewing gums, citric acid — the latter risky for caries via calcium chelation), systemic sialogogues used mainly in Sjögren's syndrome (pilocarpine 5–10mg 3–4x/day, cevimeline 30mg/day, anetholetrithione 25mg 3x/day, yohimbine, interferon-α lozenges 150 IU 3x/day), and saliva substitutes (mucin-based formulations tracking natural saliva's pseudoplastic viscosity better than carboxymethylcellulose/glycerol-based alternatives; commercial products such as Recaldent, Spry, Biotene, and BioXtra). It also surveys emerging approaches — intraoral electro-stimulation devices, aquaporin-1 (AQP1) gene therapy for postradiation SGH, implantable salivary pacemakers (Salitron, GenNarino, SaliPen, Saliwell Crown), and intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) as a preventive strategy in head-and-neck cancer patients.

Clinically, this is a practical armamentarium reference rather than graded evidence: no systematic search strategy or risk-of-bias appraisal is reported, so the review should be used to identify *what options exist* (and pilocarpine/cevimeline dosing specifics) rather than to rank their comparative effectiveness. It pairs well with mechanistic pages (why these drugs work) and outcome-focused SRs (how much xerostomia actually degrades dental treatment success) already in the wiki.

## Key Contributions

- Clinician-oriented catalogue of xerostomia/SGH management options across four tiers: topical stimulants, systemic sialogogues, saliva substitutes, and emerging technologies.
- Concrete dosing regimens for the main systemic sialogogues: pilocarpine (5–10mg, 3–4x/day), cevimeline (30mg/day), anetholetrithione (25mg, 3x/day), interferon-α lozenges (150 IU, 3x/day).
- Explains why mucin-based artificial saliva formulations more closely match natural saliva's pseudoplastic viscosity than carboxymethylcellulose/glycerol-based (Newtonian) alternatives.
- Surveys emerging device/gene-therapy approaches (electrostimulation, AQP1 gene therapy, salivary pacemakers) not typically covered in mainstream restorative-dentistry literature.
- Frames management within the "Minimally Invasive Dentistry" preventive philosophy.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative review; no PRISMA flow diagram, no stated database search strategy or date range, no explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria, and no formal risk-of-bias/quality appraisal reported.
- **Scope**: Pharmacological (topical + systemic) and non-pharmacological (substitutes, devices, gene therapy) interventions for xerostomia/SGH, discussed largely across mixed etiologies (Sjögren's syndrome, radiotherapy, medication-induced, age-related) without stratified comparative data.
- **Evidence weight**: Lowest tier in the confidence vocabulary (`narrative-review`) — treat as a practical options catalogue, not a critically appraised synthesis.

## Therapeutic Options Reviewed

- **General principles**: patient education on etiology/prognosis; reinforced oral hygiene (fluoride toothpaste + rinse, minimized refined-carbohydrate intake, chlorhexidine, denture-cleaning technique); regular monitoring including microbiological screening for candidiasis; individualized choice between artificial saliva and combination sialogogue therapy.
- **Topical stimulants**: flavored/sugar-free chewing gums and lozenges (sorbitol, xylitol) stimulate salivation via taste + mastication; citric acid is an effective stimulant but risks caries in dentate patients via calcium chelation.
- **Systemic sialogogues** (mainly Sjögren's syndrome): pilocarpine — adverse effects diaphoresis/flushing/polyuria, contraindicated in uncontrolled asthma, narrow-angle glaucoma, acute iritis; cevimeline — more M3-selective, favored in postradiation xerostomia; anetholetrithione — useful in minor/senile salivary dysfunction; yohimbine — weaker evidence base, reported more effective than anetholetrithione in patients on psychotropics; interferon-α lozenges — cited meta-analysis reports efficacy with minimal side effects plus immunomodulatory benefit in Sjögren's syndrome.
- **Saliva substitutes**: mucin-with-xanthan-guar-gum (or mucin-alone) formulations approximate natural saliva's viscoelastic/pseudoplastic behavior better than carboxymethylcellulose- or glycerol-based Newtonian-fluid alternatives (assessed via rheometer creep/recovery and dynamic testing). Commercial products: Recaldent Gum (casein phosphopeptide–amorphous calcium phosphate, remineralization), Spry Dental Defense System (xylitol, reduces S. mutans with extended use), Biotene and BioXtra (protein enzyme systems for lubrication/hygiene). Chewing gums increase flow and provide cariostatic/remineralizing benefit but have limited effect on saliva's biochemical composition.
- **Emerging technologies**: acupuncture (improved parotid blood circulation, benefit reported in Sjögren's/radiotherapy/idiopathic xerostomia); intraoral electro-stimulators (reported 3–4-fold salivary flow increase; mouthguard-like or implant-fixed devices); AQP1 gene therapy via viral vector for postradiation SGH (Baum et al.); implantable salivary pacemakers (Salitron, GenNarino, SaliPen, Saliwell Crown); IMRT as a gland-sparing radiotherapy technique reducing postradiation oral dryness versus conventional radiotherapy.
