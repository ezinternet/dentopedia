## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA, no meta-analysis) of 7 studies (n=1,029; 6 cross-sectional/case-control designs plus 1 randomized crossover trial, 2009-2022) on medication-induced xerostomia and orofacial pain.

The authors report a positive association between medication-induced xerostomia and orofacial pain (e.g., xerostomia inventory 7.14 vs 5.04 in craniofacial pain vs controls, p<0.001; OR 1.04 in dementia residents), but no association between salivary flow measurements and medication use.

Evidence is low-level (one RCT, no xerostomia-free controls, recall-based self-report, no drug dose/duration data), so the conclusions should be used with prudence.

## 세줄요약

7편(n=1,029; 횡단·환자-대조 연구와 무작위 교차시험 1편, 2009-2022)을 다룬 체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review, SR)로, 약물 유발 구강건조증 (Xerostomia)과 구강안면통증 (Orofacial Pain)의 연관성을 봤다 (메타분석 없음).

약물 유발 구강건조증과 구강안면통증 사이에 양의 연관성이 보고됐다 (구강건조 설문 점수 7.14 vs 5.04, p<0.001; 치매 거주자 OR 1.04). 반면 타액 분비량 (Salivary Flow) 측정과 약물 사용 사이에는 연관성이 없었다.

근거 수준이 낮다: RCT 1편, 구강건조 없는 대조군 부재, 환자 회상 기반 자가보고, 약물 용량·기간 미보고. 결론은 신중히 해석해야 한다.

## Summary

Source: PubMed / PMC full text, DOI [10.3290/j.qi.b4154345](https://doi.org/10.3290/j.qi.b4154345) (Quintessence Int 2023;54(8):658-670). The review asked whether patients taking xerostomia-causing medications who have xerostomia also report orofacial pain. Seven studies (1,029 participants, mean ages 43 to 100 years) were included; the authors conclude that a positive association exists, while salivary flow measurements showed no association with medication use. The authors state that none of the studies had a control group without xerostomia and that only one was a randomized trial, so risk of bias could not be assessed and no meta-analysis was possible. Medications were analyzed as covariates rather than exposures, with antidepressants listed in all four studies that reported drugs.

## Key Contributions

- Self-described first review of the xerostomia-orofacial pain association in medicated patients.
- Positive association reported; no association between salivary flow and medication use.
- Lists research gaps: validated/standardized xerostomia tools, salivary flow measurement, documented pain diagnosis, drug dose and duration.

## Methodology

PRISMA-reported SR searching WoS, PubMed, SCOPUS and MEDLINE (English only). PICO: medicated patients; xerostomia-causing drugs; xerostomia vs no xerostomia; orofacial pain. Sjogren's, cancer and studies exclusively on burning mouth syndrome were excluded. Two-phase dual screening, dual extraction, Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for quality, narrative synthesis.

## Results

| Study | Design/population | Key finding |
|---|---|---|
| Michalak 2022 | Institutionalized seniors | Mirror-sliding dryness with higher oral pain VAS (p=0.018); more medications (p=0.014) |
| Van de Rijt 2020 | Dementia residents | Orofacial pain 48.8% (OPS-NVI); xerostomia predictor OR 1.04 (p<0.05) |
| Yuen 2009 | Spinal cord injury | Oral pain 42%; xerostomia ~60%; positively associated |
| da Silva 2011 | Orofacial pain vs controls | Flow 0.125 vs 0.175 g/min (p=0.008); amitriptyline 35%, antihypertensives 16% |
| de Siqueira 2021 | Craniofacial pain vs controls | Xerostomia inventory 7.14 vs 5.04 (p<0.001); 62.6% reported xerostomia |
| Barbe 2018 | Medication-induced xerostomia | Stimulated flow 0.3 +/- 0.2 and 0.4 +/- 0.2 ml/min |
| Beker 2019 | Centenarians | Xerostomia 18%, oral pain 12%; xerostomia linked to medication use (p=0.001) |

Limitations noted by authors: non-comparable scales, recall-based reporting, only two studies with objective flow, no dose/duration data, xerogenic and non-xerogenic drugs combined.
