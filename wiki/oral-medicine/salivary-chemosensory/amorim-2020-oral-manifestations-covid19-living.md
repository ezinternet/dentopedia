## Three-line Summary

This original living systematic review (40 studies, 10,228 patients, 19 countries) was the first to establish gustatory impairment as the most prevalent COVID-19 oral manifestation at 45% (95% CI 34%–55%; I²=99%), with subtype rates of dysgeusia 38%, hypogeusia 35%, and ageusia 24%. Taste disorders showed strong association with COVID-19 diagnosis (OR 12.68), mild/moderate severity (OR 2.09), and female sex (OR 1.64). Oral mucosal lesion timing was characterized: preceding or concurrent with respiratory onset in mild cases, but 7–24 days after onset in hospitalized patients.

## Summary

This is the founding paper of the COVID-19 oral manifestations living systematic review series (PROSPERO CRD42020184468), published in J Dent Res in September 2020 — early in the pandemic. It synthesized 40 studies (33 cross-sectional, 7 case reports) encompassing 10,228 patients from 19 countries.

**Primary finding — gustatory impairment**

Gustatory impairment was the dominant oral manifestation at 45% (95% CI 34%–55%; I²=99%). Subtype breakdown: dysgeusia 38%, hypogeusia 35%, ageusia 24%. The very high heterogeneity reflects early-pandemic variability in case definitions and testing access.

Taste disorders demonstrated strong diagnostic association with COVID-19 (OR 12.68, 95% CI 6.41–25.10), more than double the effect size later reported in the 6-month update (OR 7.54), likely due to selection bias in early case series. Associations were also identified for mild/moderate disease severity (OR 2.09) and female sex (OR 1.64).

**Oral mucosal lesions — morphology and timing**

Lesion types: white and erythematous plaques, irregular ulcers, small blisters, petechiae, desquamative gingivitis. Sites: tongue > palate > lips > gingiva > buccal mucosa.

A key clinical observation — not superseded in the 2021 update — is the temporal pattern: in mild cases, oral lesions tend to appear before or simultaneously with respiratory symptoms. In severe/hospitalized patients, lesions appear 7–24 days after symptom onset. The authors interpret oral mucosal lesions primarily as co-infections or secondary manifestations, not direct SARS-CoV-2 mucosal injury.

**Living SR design**

The paper was designed as a living SR, anticipating pandemic-era evidence growth. The first update (6-month update, same group, J Dent Res 2021) expanded to 183 studies and 64,876 patients, adding xerostomia as a new major outcome.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic, quantitative synthesis of COVID-19 oral manifestations
- Established gustatory impairment as the dominant COVID-19 oral finding (45%)
- Provided pooled prevalence estimates for dysgeusia, hypogeusia, and ageusia separately
- Demonstrated strong COVID-19 diagnostic association for taste disorders (OR 12.68)
- Characterized oral mucosal lesion morphology and timing (mild vs. severe disease)
- Established living SR infrastructure for iterative updates (PROSPERO CRD42020184468)

## Methodology

Living systematic review; PRISMA-compliant; 6 databases + gray literature; all languages; 2-step study selection; Joanna Briggs Institute risk of bias; GRADE evidence certainty; meta-analysis with pooled prevalence and OR. Sample: 10,228 patients (4,288 male, 5,770 female, 170 unknown) from 19 countries. Abstract-only; full text not retrieved.
