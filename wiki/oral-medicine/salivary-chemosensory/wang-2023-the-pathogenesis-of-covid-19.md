## Three-line Summary

SARS-CoV-2 causes taste dysfunction through direct cytocidal effect via high ACE2/TMPRSS2 expression in taste buds, indirect immune-mediated damage (cytokine storm → apoptosis of taste bud stem cells), and neurotropic invasion via the olfactory bulb. Angiotensin II accumulation from local RAS imbalance amplifies immune damage, while zinc deficiency, genetic susceptibility, and altered neurotransmitters further contribute. Treatment options remain limited; stem cell-based regeneration and tongue organoids represent the most promising future directions for persistent post-COVID dysgeusia.

## 세줄요약

SARS-CoV-2는 미뢰 세포의 ACE2/TMPRSS2 고발현을 통한 직접 세포파괴, 사이토카인 폭풍에 의한 미뢰 줄기세포 손상(간접 면역 손상), 후각구를 통한 신경침범이라는 세 경로로 미각장애를 유발한다. 국소 레닌-안지오텐신계 불균형에 의한 Ang II 축적이 면역 손상을 증폭시키며, 아연 결핍·유전적 감수성·신경전달물질 이상도 병인에 기여한다. 치료법은 제한적이나 줄기세포 기반 미뢰 재생과 혀 오가노이드가 장기 미각장애의 유망한 미래 방향으로 제시된다.

## Summary

This narrative review synthesizes evidence on the pathogenesis of COVID-19-related taste disorders and surveys currently available treatments. Taste dysfunction is an early and sometimes permanent symptom of COVID-19, yet its mechanism has been difficult to study due to its subjective nature.

The review identifies five overlapping pathomechanisms:

1. **Direct viral cytocidal effect**: ACE2 and TMPRSS2, the main entry receptors for SARS-CoV-2, are highly expressed in taste bud cells, making them a direct target for viral invasion.
2. **Cytokine storm-mediated damage**: The systemic inflammatory response triggers cell apoptosis and disrupts the renewal cycle of taste bud stem cells. Interferon and multiple proinflammatory cytokines contribute to this secondary damage.
3. **RAS dysregulation**: Unbalanced local renin-angiotensin system activity leads to Angiotensin II (Ang II) accumulation, which amplifies the immune response and further damages taste receptor cells.
4. **Neurotropism**: SARS-CoV-2 can invade the central nervous system via the olfactory bulb, affecting taste-related neural pathways.
5. **Host factors**: Zinc deficiency, genetic susceptibility, altered sialic acid expression, and neurotransmitter changes also contribute to the pathogenesis.

For treatment, the review notes that only limited strategies exist for post-infectious dysgeusia. Stem cell-based approaches — using exogenous epithelial- and neural-derived factors to stimulate taste bud stem cell differentiation — and tongue organoids (3D functional taste bud models) are highlighted as the most promising future directions. Clinical evidence for stem cells in long COVID immune regulation is cited.

## Key Contributions

- Unified mechanistic framework for COVID-19-related taste disorder pathogenesis
- Identification of ACE2/TMPRSS2 expression in taste buds as the primary direct entry route
- Linking of cytokine storm to taste bud stem cell renewal impairment
- Proposal of RAS imbalance (Ang II accumulation) as an amplifying pathway
- Summary of current treatment options and limitations
- Introduction of tongue organoids as a novel research and therapeutic model

## Methodology

Narrative review design; non-systematic literature synthesis. Full text not retrieved (abstract-only). Published September 20, 2023, J Dent Res 102(11):1191–1198.

## Results

- Taste dysfunction occurs early in COVID-19 and may be permanent in some cases
- Five mechanistic pathways identified (see Summary above)
- Several medical interventions show effectiveness but evidence base remains limited
- Stem cell-based regeneration and tongue organoids represent the most promising future therapeutic directions for long-term taste disorders
