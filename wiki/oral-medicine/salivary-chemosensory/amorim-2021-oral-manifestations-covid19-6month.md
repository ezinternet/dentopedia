## Three-line Summary

This 6-month update of the COVID-19 oral manifestations living SR expanded the evidence base to 183 studies (64,876 patients worldwide) and introduced xerostomia (43%) as a new major finding alongside taste disorders (38% overall prevalence). Taste disorders showed high-certainty association with positive COVID-19 test (OR 7.54) and low-certainty association with mild/moderate severity and female sex. The update proposes xerostomia + taste dysfunction + oral mucosal lesions as the triad of common COVID-19 oral manifestations.

## Summary

This paper is the first 6-month update of the living systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42020184468) originally published in 2020 by the same group. It expands the data from 40 studies and 10,228 patients (original) to 183 studies and 64,876 patients worldwide.

**Taste disorder findings (updated)**

Overall taste disorder prevalence: 38% (95% CI 22%–56%; I²=98%). Subtype pooled prevalence: hypogeusia 34%, dysgeusia 33%, ageusia 26% — slightly lower than the original SR (45% overall), reflecting higher evidence base and regression to pooled estimates.

Taste disorders remain strongly associated with positive COVID-19 test (OR 7.54, 95% CI 5.24–10.86; high certainty), confirming taste screening utility in clinical practice. Associations with mild/moderate severity (OR 1.63) and female sex (OR 1.77) are confirmed but rated low certainty due to heterogeneity.

**Xerostomia — new finding**

Xerostomia (dry mouth) emerged as a prominent new feature with pooled prevalence of 43% (95% CI 36%–50%; I²=71%), making it quantitatively the most prevalent single oral finding in this update. This suggests direct salivary gland involvement or neurogenic/autonomic dysfunction from SARS-CoV-2 infection.

**Oral mucosal lesions (updated)**

Rank order of patterns: aphthous-like > herpes-like > candidiasis > glossitis/depapillation/geographic tongue > parotitis > angular cheilitis. Lesions predominantly affect tongue, lips, and palate. The authors maintain the interpretation that these represent co-infections rather than direct SARS-CoV-2 mucosal infection.

**Triad proposal**

The update proposes xerostomia + taste dysfunction + oral mucosal lesions as the triad of common oral COVID-19 manifestations, warranting routine oral assessment in COVID-19 patients.

## Key Contributions

- Largest SR on COVID-19 oral manifestations at time of publication (183 studies, 64,876 patients)
- Introduction of xerostomia as a major outcome (43%)
- Confirmation of high-certainty taste disorder association with COVID-19 (OR 7.54)
- Hierarchical catalogue of oral mucosal lesion patterns
- Proposal of the xerostomia–taste dysfunction–mucosal lesions triad
- Living SR methodology enabling rapid evidence synthesis during a pandemic

## Methodology

Living systematic review (first 6-month update); PRISMA-compliant; searched 6 databases + gray literature; all languages; Joanna Briggs Institute risk-of-bias tools; GRADE evidence certainty assessment; meta-analysis with pooled prevalence and OR. Abstract-only; full text not retrieved.
