## Three-line Summary

A comprehensive narrative review of 208 case reports (1980–2022) cataloging drug-induced tongue disorders. Antineoplastic/immunomodulating agents and anti-infective drugs are the most frequently implicated classes; tongue discoloration and black hairy tongue (BHT) are the most common presentations. Drug discontinuation combined with oral hygiene resolves most cases, underscoring the importance of recognizing these benign but alarming adverse reactions.

## 세줄요약

1980–2022년 208건 증례보고를 종합한 서사 고찰로 약물 유발 혀 이상(Drug-Induced Tongue Disorders, DITD)을 망라한다. 항종양·면역조절제와 전신 항감염제가 가장 많고, 혀 변색과 흑모설(Black Hairy Tongue, BHT)이 가장 흔한 증상이다. 원인약 중단과 구강위생 유지만으로 대부분 완전 호전되므로, 이상 소견을 초기에 인식해 환자 불안과 복약 불순응을 예방하는 것이 핵심이다.

## Summary

Drug-induced tongue disorders encompass a spectrum of adverse reactions including discoloration, BHT, dysgeusia (미각이상, Dysgeusia), glossitis (설염, Glossitis), glossodynia (설통, Glossodynia), swelling, and ulceration. These reactions are not rare yet are frequently underrecognized in clinical practice. The review synthesized 208 case reports and case series published over four decades, providing a clinical reference for diagnosis and management.

Most disorders are benign and self-limiting upon drug withdrawal. Identification of the offending agent and counseling about expected resolution is the cornerstone of management. Clinicians prescribing high-risk drug classes — antineoplastics, immunomodulators, broad-spectrum anti-infectives — should proactively monitor for tongue changes and counsel patients accordingly to prevent unnecessary therapy discontinuation driven by cosmetic alarm.

## Key Contributions

- First comprehensive catalog covering the full breadth of drug-induced tongue disorders across 208 case reports spanning 42 years
- Hierarchy of implicated drug classes: antineoplastic/immunomodulating agents ranked highest, followed by anti-infectives for systemic use
- Most common presentations: tongue discoloration (pigment deposition, drug staining) and black hairy tongue (BHT)
- Establishes management algorithm: identify and discontinue offending drug + reinforce oral hygiene → resolution
- Clinical relevance: medication adherence is threatened when patients are alarmed by visible tongue changes; early reassurance and explanation prevents discontinuation of necessary therapy

## Methodology

- **Design:** Narrative review (comprehensive literature search)
- **Databases:** Google Scholar and PubMed
- **Search terms:** 16 MeSH/keyword terms covering taste dysfunction, tongue morphology changes, oral burning, and tongue structural disorders
- **Inclusion:** Published 1980–2022; English-language full text or non-English with sufficient English abstract
- **N:** 208 case reports and case series included
- **Exclusion:** Non-English reports without adequate English abstract data

## Results

- **N included:** 208 case reports/case series
- **Top drug classes:** Antineoplastic and immunomodulating agents; anti-infectives for systemic use
- **Top presentations:** Tongue discoloration; black hairy tongue (BHT)
- **Outcome:** Drug discontinuation ± good oral hygiene → significant improvement or complete resolution in the majority
- **Conclusion:** Drug-induced tongue disorders are not rare adverse reactions; nearly all are benign with withdrawal of the causative agent
