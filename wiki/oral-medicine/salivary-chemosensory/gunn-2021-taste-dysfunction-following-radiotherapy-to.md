## Three-line Summary

A systematic review of 31 studies meta-analysing radiation-induced taste dysfunction (미각장애, Taste Dysfunction) in head and neck cancer (두경부암, Head and Neck Cancer, HNC) radiotherapy (방사선요법, Radiotherapy, RT). Acute dysfunction affects ~96% of patients objectively; ~25% experience long-term impairment with associated weight loss and quality-of-life decline. Dose to the anterior tongue and oral cavity drives dysfunction, and proton therapy or bite blocks offer feasible taste-preserving mitigation.

## 세줄요약

두경부 방사선치료 후 미각장애 유병률을 31편으로 메타분석한 체계적 고찰로, 급성기 객관적 유병률 약 96%·주관적 약 79%를 보고하며 대다수는 부분 회복하지만 약 25%는 장기 지속됨을 확인한다. 전방 2/3 혀와 구강의 선량 노출이 핵심 예측인자이며, 양성자치료(Proton Beam Therapy)와 맞춤형 바이트블록이 미각기관 선량을 줄여 보존 가능성을 시사한다. 무작위 대조 연구 부족이 주요 한계로, 혼란변수를 충분히 보정한 전향적 대규모 연구가 필요하다.

## Summary

Radiation-induced taste dysfunction is a near-universal acute complication of head and neck radiotherapy, affecting approximately 96% of patients by objective measures. While most patients show at least partial recovery, ~25% experience persistent long-term impairment that negatively impacts nutritional status and quality-of-life (삶의 질, Quality of Life, QoL).

The anterior two-thirds of the tongue harbors the highest density of taste buds (미뢰, Taste Buds) and is highly sensitive to radiation dose. When this region and the broader oral cavity fall within the high-dose RT field, taste dysfunction is more prevalent and severe, supporting the concept of dose constraint for the gustatory organs-at-risk.

Advanced RT modalities — particularly proton beam therapy (양성자치료, Proton Beam Therapy) with its Bragg-peak dose distribution — and customized bite blocks can physically displace or reduce dose to the gustatory field. This systematic review provides the epidemiological foundation for integrating taste preservation into RT treatment planning for HNC.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic review with meta-analysis specifically quantifying radiation-induced taste dysfunction prevalence: ~96% acute (objective), ~79% acute (subjective), ~25% long-term
- Established taste dysfunction sequelae: weight loss and QoL reduction documented across included studies
- Anatomic dose–response relationship: anterior two-thirds of tongue and oral cavity irradiation predicts worse outcomes
- Provides clinical rationale for proton beam therapy and bite blocks as taste-preserving interventions
- Calls for prospective randomized evidence to establish formal dose constraints for gustatory organs-at-risk

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review with meta-analysis
- **Databases:** PubMed, Medline, EMBASE
- **Inclusion criteria:** Randomized trials, cohort studies, cross-sectional studies reporting taste outcomes in HNC patients receiving RT
- **N studies:** 31
- **Outcome measures:** Acute and long-term taste dysfunction prevalence (objective and subjective), recovery patterns, association with nutrition/QoL, dose–effect data

## Results

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Acute dysfunction (objective) | ~96% (95% CI 64–100%) |
| Acute dysfunction (subjective) | ~79% (95% CI 65–88%) |
| Long-term dysfunction | ~25% |
| Recovery | Majority: at least partial recovery |
| Sequelae | Weight loss; reduced QoL |
| Anatomic predictor | Oral cavity + anterior 2/3 tongue irradiation |
| Dose-sparing interventions | Proton beam therapy; customized bite blocks |

The wide CI for objective measures (64–100%) reflects high inter-study heterogeneity in RT technique, dose, and assessment method. Despite this, the direction is unambiguous: acute taste loss is nearly universal in this population.
