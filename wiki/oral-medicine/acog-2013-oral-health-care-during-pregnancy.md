## Three-line Summary
ACOG Committee Opinion No. 569 (2013), a multidisciplinary consensus statement by ACOG, ADA, HRSA, and the National Maternal and Child Oral Health Resource Center, synthesizes published literature and meta-analyses on oral health throughout pregnancy; at publication, 56% of US women had not visited a dentist during pregnancy (2007–2009 data).

Dental care—including radiographs, local anesthesia, and most medications—is endorsed as safe across all trimesters; periodontal treatment during pregnancy does NOT reduce preterm birth or low birth weight based on multiple meta-analyses; endocarditis prophylaxis is limited to AHA highest-risk cardiac patients only.

Untreated oral disease poses greater risk to mother and fetus than dental treatment itself; maternal-to-infant Streptococcus mutans transmission via utensil sharing is a modifiable caries risk factor warranting routine counseling during prenatal dental visits.

## Summary
The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), in collaboration with the American Dental Association, HRSA, and National Maternal and Child Oral Health Resource Center, published this Committee Opinion affirming that oral health is integral to general health during pregnancy. Key clinical conclusions: (1) dental care including X-rays, local anesthesia, and most dental medications is safe in all trimesters; (2) periodontal disease is associated with systemic conditions but recent meta-analyses show no benefit of periodontal treatment for reducing preterm birth or low birth weight; (3) untreated oral disease poses greater risk to mother and fetus than the dental treatment itself; (4) endocarditis prophylaxis only warranted for patients meeting AHA highest-risk criteria. The document also addresses maternal transmission of S. mutans to infants via utensil sharing and recommends counseling on oral hygiene throughout pregnancy.

## Key Contributions
- Authoritative multidisciplinary ACOG consensus (OB/GYN + Dentistry + federal health agencies)
- Explicitly states dental treatment safety across all trimesters — medicolegal anchor for clinical practice
- Updates periodontal–preterm birth relationship: no intervention benefit despite association
- Addresses practical concerns: antibiotic prophylaxis criteria, fluoride use, oral cancer screening, infant transmission prevention
- Establishes that access barriers (income-related) are the dominant oral health challenge in pregnancy

## Methodology
Committee Opinion based on narrative review of published literature, meta-analyses, and guidelines from AHA, ADA, USPSTF. Reviewed by multi-stakeholder advisory panel. Level of evidence: expert consensus synthesizing existing literature.

## Results
Key evidence statements:
- Dental care (X-rays, LA, medications) is **safe throughout pregnancy** — all trimesters
- 35% of US women had no dental visit in past year; 56% did not visit dentist during pregnancy (2007–2009)
- Periodontal disease + preterm birth: **association exists but periodontal treatment does NOT reduce preterm birth or low birth weight** (multiple meta-analyses)
- Periodontal disease + preeclampsia: conflicting evidence
- Endocarditis prophylaxis: AHA criteria only (dental procedures involving gingival manipulation/periapical region in highest-risk cardiac patients)
- Fluoride: recommended for caries prevention in pregnancy
- S. mutans transmission: maternal oral hygiene directly affects infant caries risk
