## Three-line Summary

Narrative literature review of acupuncture for neuropathic orofacial pain (NOP), covering four subtypes — trigeminal neuralgia (TN), burning mouth syndrome (BMS), temporomandibular disorders (TMD), and postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) — summarizing proposed neurochemical/anti-inflammatory mechanisms and citing primary studies (mostly individual RCTs plus one large network meta-analysis for TN) per subtype.

Evidence is presented as generally favorable (reduced pain intensity, improved quality of life, good safety) across all four subtypes, but the review itself flags that most primary studies are small, methodologically heterogeneous, and non-standardized in acupoint/protocol selection with short follow-up; for TMD it explicitly notes conflicting results (some trials showing no advantage over sham).

As a narrative (non-systematic) review with no described search strategy, PRISMA flow, or risk-of-bias appraisal, its efficacy claims — especially the TN claim that acupuncture outperforms carbamazepine, sourced from a single cited network meta-analysis — should be treated as hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing until independently verified; it is best used to map non-pharmacological adjunct options and mechanisms, not to replace the existing pharmacological ladder.

## 세줄요약

신경병증성 구강안면통증 (Neuropathic Orofacial Pain, NOP)에 대한 침술 (Acupuncture) narrative review — 삼차신경통 (Trigeminal Neuralgia, TN)·구강작열감증후군 (Burning Mouth Syndrome, BMS)·측두하악장애 (Temporomandibular Disorders, TMD)·대상포진후신경통 (Postherpetic Neuralgia, PHN) 4개 아형의 신경화학적/항염 기전과 1차 연구(대부분 개별 RCT, TN은 대규모 네트워크 메타분석 1건)를 정리.

전반적으로 통증감소·삶의 질 개선·양호한 안전성 등 우호적으로 서술되지만, 저자 스스로도 대부분 1차 연구가 소규모·방법론적 이질성·경혈/프로토콜 비표준화·단기 추적임을 인정하며, TMD 항목에서는 sham 대비 우월성이 없었다는 상반된 결과도 명시.

체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review)이 아닌 narrative review로 검색전략·PRISMA·비뚤림위험 평가가 없어, 특히 TN에서 "침술이 카르바마제핀보다 우수"하다는 단일 네트워크 메타분석 인용 근거의 주장은 확정 지침이 아닌 가설 생성 수준으로 취급해야 하며, 기존 약물 사다리를 대체하기보다는 비약물 보조요법·기전 지도로 활용하는 것이 적절함.

## Summary

This narrative literature review synthesizes the mechanistic rationale and clinical evidence for acupuncture as an adjunct or alternative therapy across four neuropathic orofacial pain (NOP) subtypes: burning mouth syndrome (BMS), trigeminal neuralgia (TN), temporomandibular disorders (TMD), and postherpetic neuralgia (PHN). It proposes that acupuncture acts through endogenous opioid release, neurotransmitter modulation (5-HT, norepinephrine, GABA), suppression of neuroinflammation via the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB pathway, restoration of aberrant central pain-processing activity (fMRI-documented normalization of anterior cingulate cortex, insula, somatosensory cortex), and peripheral adenosine-mediated analgesia. For each subtype it maps a distinct set of local and distal acupoints and cites representative primary studies. The most notable clinical claim is for TN, where a cited network meta-analysis (Yin et al., 58 RCTs, >4000 participants) reported combined electroacupuncture (EA) plus manual acupuncture outperforming carbamazepine monotherapy on both pain reduction and response rate. However, the review is explicit that the overall evidence base for acupuncture in NOP is methodologically weak — small samples, inconsistent protocols, short follow-up, uncontrolled placebo effects — and for TMD specifically notes directly conflicting trial results. No standardized acupuncture protocol or clinical guideline for NOP currently exists.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates proposed acupuncture analgesia mechanisms relevant to NOP: endogenous opioid release (μ/δ/κ receptors, PAG/thalamus/dorsal horn), 5-HT/norepinephrine/GABA modulation, TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB-mediated anti-neuroinflammatory effects (↓IL-1β/TNF-α/IL-6, ↑IL-10, M2 microglial polarization), central neuroplasticity normalization (fMRI: ACC, insula, somatosensory cortex), and peripheral adenosine-A1-mediated analgesia.
- Maps subtype-specific acupoint protocols: TN (local ST2/ST4/ST6/ST7; distal LI4/GB20/DU26), BMS (SI1/TE1/LI4/TW21/ST5/ST6/CV24), TMD (SI18/TW21/LI4/ST6/ST7/GV20-24/ST36/LI3/LI11), PHN (Ashi points, Jiaji/EX-B2, GB34, LI4, ST36, GB21, LR3, SP6, BL15, PC6).
- Cites a network meta-analysis (Yin et al., 58 RCTs, n>4000) reporting combined EA + manual acupuncture outperformed carbamazepine monotherapy for TN on VAS pain reduction and ≥50%-response rate, with acupuncture reported as well-tolerated (no serious adverse events).
- Transparently reports conflicting TMD evidence (Wu et al., Laurence et al.: limited evidence of superiority over sham), attributed to acupoint-selection and protocol heterogeneity across trials — a useful honesty check against overselling the TN finding to other subtypes.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative literature review. No stated systematic search strategy, database list, inclusion/exclusion criteria, PRISMA flow diagram, or formal risk-of-bias/GRADE appraisal of the primary studies cited.
- **Scope**: Four NOP subtypes (BMS, TN, TMD, PHN), each structured as pathogenesis/symptom summary → subtype-specific proposed mechanism → subtype-specific acupoints → narrative summary of representative primary studies.
- **Evidence base**: A mix of individual RCTs and double-blind trials (e.g., Heckmann et al. systemic clonazepam and Gremeau-Richard et al. topical clonazepam for BMS, used as pharmacological comparators rather than head-to-head against acupuncture), multicenter studies (Edwards et al., Hu et al. for TN), and one network meta-analysis (Yin et al., TN) — sourced largely from acupuncture/Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) and Chinese-language rehabilitation-medicine literature. The current review authors do not independently re-appraise the quality of these underlying studies.

## Results

| NOP subtype | Key acupuncture finding cited | Comparator / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| TN | EA + manual acupuncture > carbamazepine on pain reduction and ≥50%-response rate (Yin et al. NMA, 58 RCTs, n>4000); cognitive/QoL gains (Gao et al.); consistent across EA/manual/fire acupuncture (Edwards et al., Hu et al.) | No serious adverse events reported; claim rests on a single NMA not independently critiqued by this review |
| BMS | Improved oral microcirculation/vascular homeostasis (Scardina et al.); acupuncture + auricular therapy addresses physical + emotional domains (Franco et al.) | Topical/systemic clonazepam also effective in separate RCTs (Gremeau-Richard, Heckmann) — not compared head-to-head with acupuncture |
| TMD | Significant pain/jaw-mobility improvement, EA + ultrashort-wave synergy (Liu et al., Sung et al.); ↓TNF-α/IL-6, ↓cortisol, improved emotional function (Jung et al.) | Wu et al. and Laurence et al.: limited/mixed evidence of superiority over sham — explicitly flagged as unresolved |
| PHN | Low-frequency EA improved pain and sleep (Pei et al.); fire needling superior to other techniques (Wang et al.) | Ashi-point bloodletting-cupping: relief but slightly more mild adverse events (dizziness, bleeding) |

**Evidence-quality caveat (this page's key limitation to carry forward):** all findings above are narrative-review-level citations of individual primary studies, not a pooled or quality-appraised estimate. The authors themselves cite small sample sizes, inconsistent acupoint/protocol selection, short follow-up, and inadequate placebo control as limiting the current evidence base; no standardized NOP-acupuncture protocol or clinical guideline exists.
