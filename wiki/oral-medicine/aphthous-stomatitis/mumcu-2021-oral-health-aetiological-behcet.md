## Three-line Summary

Narrative review synthesizing the aetiological role of oral health (oral microbiome, innate/adaptive immunity, periodontal status) in Behçet's disease (BD) pathogenesis and disease course.

Poor periodontal health and plaque accumulation are associated with more severe BD (especially in males); a structured 1-month dental/periodontal intervention reduced recurrent oral ulcer frequency at 6 months despite an early flare from mechanical trauma.

Clinical implication: dental treatment should be deferred during active BD flares (trauma-triggered exacerbation), but proactive periodontal treatment and oral hygiene during quiescence is an under-acknowledged adjunct to systemic BD management; evidence is narrative-review level, not a single RCT.

## Summary

Behçet's disease (BD) is a chronic multisystem inflammatory vasculitis with recurrent oro-genital ulceration as its most common and usually initial manifestation, most prevalent along the ancient Silk Route (Turkey highest, with a decreasing prevalence/milder course reported in Japanese and Korean populations of low ethnic diversity). Mumcu & Fortune (2021) argue that oral health is not merely a site of manifestation but an active **aetiological driver** of BD: oral dysbiosis (particularly *Streptococcus sanguinis* and *S. mutans* overgrowth), impaired innate immunity (low serum mannose-binding lectin, neutrophil extracellular trap formation, TLR2/4 activation), and dysregulated adaptive responses (γδ T cells, IL-1/TNF-α gene polymorphisms) interact with periodontal infective foci to trigger and sustain systemic disease activity. Clinically, poor periodontal status and plaque accumulation predict more severe disease (especially in males, confirmed by 5-year mediation analysis), while a structured dental/periodontal treatment intervention reduced recurrent oral ulcer frequency at 6 months — despite an initial trauma-triggered flare in the first days of treatment. The review closes with practical guidance: avoid elective dental treatment during active BD flares (mechanical trauma from treatment can itself activate exacerbation via epithelial breach), but treat periodontal disease and reinforce oral hygiene during quiescent periods as an adjunct to systemic management, individualising recall interval to risk.

## Key Contributions

- Positions oral dysbiosis/periodontal infection as a bidirectional (not merely secondary) driver of BD activity, mediated by specific microbial (*S. sanguinis*/*mutans*), innate (MBL deficiency, NETs, S100A12, TLR2/4), and adaptive (γδ T cells, IL-1α-889C, TNF-α-1031T/C polymorphisms) immune pathways.
- Reports the authors' own salivary/gut microbiome cohort data showing distinct dysbiosis signatures at BD ulcer sites vs. RAS ulcer sites, and normalisation of the salivary microbiome toward symbiosis after dental/periodontal treatment.
- Provides direct interventional evidence: 1-month dental/periodontal treatment → early flare (days 1–2, mechanical trauma) → significantly reduced recurrent oral ulcer frequency at 6 months vs. pre-treatment and vs. untreated controls.
- Synthesizes pharmacologic oral-ulcer options with a rough hierarchy: BD-specific mouthwash (Triorasol) > betamethasone mouthwash; colchicine + prophylactic benzathine penicillin > colchicine alone; azithromycin/minocycline (immunomodulatory adjuncts); apremilast (PDE4 inhibitor, ulcer reduction within 1 week).
- Translates mechanism into chairside guidance: defer elective dental treatment during active flares; use "reason for last dental visit" and hygiene-habit questions as screening red flags; individualise recall frequency.

## Methodology

Narrative (non-systematic) review integrating epidemiologic, immunologic, oral-microbiome and interventional literature on BD oral health, substantially anchored in the corresponding author's own Turkish/UK BD-clinic cohort and microbiome studies. No formal search protocol, pooled effect size, or risk-of-bias assessment reported (confidence: narrative-review).

## Results

**Periodontal status ↔ disease severity**: Regression and 5-year mediation analyses show elevated plaque accumulation and periodontal destruction predict more severe BD course, mediated through male gender; tooth extraction is sometimes needed as a last resort in severely affected male patients with advanced, non-treatable periodontal disease.

**Country-level oral health gap**: UK BD patients showed higher filled-teeth counts, brushing frequency, and dental-visit frequency than Turkish BD patients, whose lower dental-service utilisation and hygiene correlated with poorer oral health — framed as a National Oral Health Policy / access issue, not purely a disease effect.

**Dental/periodontal intervention outcome**: 1-month structured treatment (vs. untreated control) → transient flare in oral ulcer activity in the first 2 days (mechanical trauma) → significant reduction in recurrent oral ulcer frequency at 6 months, with the intervention group outperforming controls; systemic BD treatment was held constant throughout.

**Microbiome mechanism**: Serum MBL <100 ng/ml (innate defence deficit) correlates with *S. mutans* overgrowth and severe disease; site-specific taxa distinguish BD ulcer sites from RAS ulcer sites and from healthy controls; dental/periodontal treatment shifts the salivary microbiome toward a more symbiotic pattern in the short term.

**Pharmacologic ulcer control** (narrative synthesis, not head-to-head RCT unless noted): Triorasol mouthwash > betamethasone mouthwash for pain/healing/quality of life over 6 months; colchicine + prophylactic benzathine penicillin reduces ulcer frequency/duration vs. colchicine alone; minocycline and azithromycin show immunomodulatory benefit (azithromycin ↓ healing time and ulcer count via IL-10↑/IFN-γ↓ shift); apremilast reduces ulcers within the first treatment week.
