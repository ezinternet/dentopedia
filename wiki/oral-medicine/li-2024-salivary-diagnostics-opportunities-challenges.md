## Three-line Summary

Comprehensive narrative review (Theranostics 2024) mapping six salivary biomarker classes (proteins/peptides, nucleic acids, EVs, metabolites, hormones, electrolytes) to diseases including OSCC, COVID-19, Alzheimer's, and breast cancer, and surveying detection systems from biosensors and microfluidics to wearable intraoral devices.

Salivary MMP-9 decreased from 588 to 132 ng/mL (p<0.05) 9 months post-OSCC surgery; electrochemical biosensors achieve salivary PSA sensitivity as low as 40 fg/mL; standardization and reference-range gaps remain the dominant clinical-translation barrier.

Saliva offers a non-invasive liquid biopsy alternative to blood, but most biomarkers lie at lower concentration than in plasma and lack validated reference intervals — multiplexed standardized assays are needed before routine clinical use.

## 세줄요약

종합 서술 리뷰(Theranostics 2024) — 침 바이오마커 6종류(단백질·핵산·세포외소포(Extracellular Vesicle, EV)·대사체·호르몬·전해질)를 구강편평세포암(OSCC)·COVID-19·알츠하이머·유방암 등 질환과 연결하고, 바이오센서·마이크로플루이딕·웨어러블 구강기기를 포함한 검출 시스템 전반을 개관.

침 MMP-9는 OSCC 수술 9개월 후 588→132 ng/mL (p<0.05)로 감소해 예후 지표로 활용 가능하며, 전기화학 바이오센서는 침 PSA를 40 fg/mL 감도로 검출; 대부분 바이오마커가 혈장보다 낮은 농도로 존재하고 표준 참조 범위(reference interval)가 부재.

침은 비침습적 액체생검(liquid biopsy) 대안으로 유망하나, 채취 조건 변동성·표준화 부족이 임상 전환의 핵심 장벽으로, 다중화(multiplexed) 표준 프로토콜 및 기관 간 라운드로빈 검증이 선행되어야 함.

## Summary

Saliva mirrors both local oral and systemic health because salivary gland secretions incorporate elements from blood, making it a non-invasive, painless alternative to blood and urine. This Theranostics review organizes the field along three axes: salivary biomarkers, detection techniques, and sample processing/standardization. Biomarker classes include **proteins/peptides** (MMPs, PSA, CRP, TNF-α, enzymes such as RNase and lactate dehydrogenase), **nucleic acids** (viral RNA of SARS-CoV-2/HIV/HPV, ctDNA, miRNA, DNA methylation, BRCA mutations), **extracellular vesicles**, **metabolites**, **hormones**, and **electrolytes**. Detection is moving from lab methods toward **biosensors, microfluidics, and wearable intraoral devices** with higher sensitivity, faster response, and portability for point-of-care use. The persistent obstacle is that most salivary biomarkers sit at lower concentration than in blood and **lack standardized reference ranges**, while collection variability (time, swab position, flow rate, stimulation) undermines reproducibility — addressed through multiplexed assays, standardized protocols, reference intervals, and inter-lab round-robin trials.

## Key Contributions

- Disease-mapped **catalog of salivary biomarker classes** (protein, nucleic acid, EV, metabolite, hormone, electrolyte).
- Survey of **detection hardware** from biosensors/microfluidics to **wearable intraoral sensors** for continuous monitoring.
- Frames **standardization/pre-treatment** as the field's rate-limiting challenge and lists concrete solutions.
- Emphasizes **infection-control and compliance** advantages (blocks HIV/hepatitis needlestick risk; better for pain-/blood-sensitive patients).

## Methodology

Narrative comprehensive review. Saliva is ~99% water and 0.5% organic/inorganic material; salivary K⁺ exceeds plasma while Na⁺ is lower; the salivary proteome is more diverse than plasma (top 20 salivary proteins ≈ 40% of total vs 22 proteins ≈ 99% of plasma protein). Evidence is drawn across biomarker discovery studies, biosensor/microfluidic engineering reports, and commercial standardization practice (e.g. Salimetrics).
