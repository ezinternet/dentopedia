## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center case series (n=21, Pusan National University Dental Hospital, 2016–2022) of intraoral sinus irrigation through a small lateral window for odontogenic maxillary sinusitis not improved by antibiotics, with at least 2 months of follow-up.

Radiopacity on CBCT decreased in 16/21 patients (76.1%; 2 unchanged, 3 increased), mean reduction 40.15%, and symptoms improved in 17/21 (80.9%) with no worsening; extraction-related cases improved most (5/5) and implant-related cases least (5/7 radiographic).

Irrigation looks effective as a minimally invasive adjunct for iatrogenic OMS, but there was no control group, the sample was small, and follow-up was short, so no comparison with antibiotics alone or with Caldwell-Luc/FESS can be drawn.

## 세줄요약

후향적 단일기관 증례군 연구(n=21, 부산대학교치과병원, 2016–2022): 항생제에 호전되지 않은 치성 상악동염 (Odontogenic Maxillary Sinusitis, OMS) 환자에게 구강내 소형 측방창(small lateral window) 상악동 세척술을 시행하고 최소 2개월 추적.

CBCT 불투명도(radiopacity)는 21명 중 16명(76.1%)에서 감소(2명 불변, 3명 증가), 평균 감소율 40.15%; 증상은 17명(80.9%)에서 호전되었고 악화는 없었음; 발치 관련군은 5/5로 가장 좋았고 임플란트 관련군은 영상 5/7로 가장 낮았음.

의인성 OMS에서 최소침습 보조요법으로 효과가 있어 보이나, 대조군이 없고 표본이 작으며 추적이 짧아 항생제 단독이나 Caldwell-Luc·내시경 부비동 수술 (Functional Endoscopic Sinus Surgery, FESS)과의 우열은 판단할 수 없음.

## Summary

This retrospective series from Pusan National University Dental Hospital reviewed 21 patients with odontogenic maxillary sinusitis (OMS), mostly iatrogenic (implant-related 7, sinus graft 7, tooth extraction 5, other 2), who underwent sinus irrigation through a small lateral window and were followed for at least 2 months. Opacified sinus volume was measured on CBCT with ImageJ before and after surgery. Opacity decreased in 16 patients (76.1%) with a mean reduction of 40.15%, and symptoms improved in 17 (80.9%), with none worse. The authors conclude that irrigation via a small lateral window is a clinically and radiologically effective treatment for OMS. The design (no control, n=21, concurrent source removal, short follow-up) supports feasibility and a signal of benefit, not a comparative efficacy claim.

## Key Contributions

- Provides 3D CBCT volumetric quantification (ImageJ, 1-mm slices) of opacified sinus volume before and after irrigation, alongside symptom outcome.
- Stratifies response by iatrogenic etiology: extraction-related cases responded best, implant-related and sinus-graft cases less.
- Describes a window placed at least 10 mm above the alveolar crest (3-mm round bur) to preserve future implant or graft options, rather than the conventional site below the infraorbital foramen.
- Frames irrigation as a lower-morbidity option compared with Caldwell-Luc (authors cite long stay, bone-wall defects, postoperative maxillary cyst).

## Methodology

- **Design / setting**: retrospective electronic-database review, Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, PNUDH; patients treated 2016-01-02 to 2022-06-30; IRB 2022-07-006.
- **Population**: 21 OMS patients with at least 2 months of follow-up; MRONJ, cyst, polyp, tumor, and infection-related sinusitis excluded. Aim framed around patients not improving with antibiotics; all received antibiotics for 1 week (another week if improving) before surgery was chosen.
- **Intervention**: vestibular or intrasulcular incision, small lateral window at least 10 mm above the alveolar crest, removal of inflamed membrane and foreign bodies, irrigation with saline or iodine solution, packing for hemostasis, postoperative antibiotics. General anesthesia (nasotracheal intubation) in 16, monitored anesthesia care in 2, local anesthesia in 3.
- **Measurement**: CBCT (ProMax, Planmeca; 110 kVp, 4.0 mA, 1-mm slices); opaque volume V = sum(S x h); severity = opaque volume / total sinus volume (Vt0/Vs); symptoms assessed postoperatively.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 16.0; t-test for sex differences in age, chi-square for etiology associations; p < 0.05.

## Protocol Detail (full-text re-read)
1. **Selection**: antibiotic-refractory OMS. All patients had antibiotics for 1 week; if improving, another week; if no improvement, surgery to remove the odontogenic cause and irrigate the sinus. Where a dental cause was removed, primary closure was done and the sinus irrigated (Discussion).
2. **Access**: vestibular or intrasulcular incision with vertical releasing cuts, periosteum detached. The conventional window site is the thinnest lateral area below the infraorbital foramen on CBCT; here it was placed at least 10 mm above the alveolar crest with a 3-mm round bur, to avoid limiting later implant surgery or grafting.
3. **Debridement + irrigation**: inflamed membrane and foreign bodies removed, then the cavity irrigated with **saline or iodine solution** (not stated which patients got which), then **packed for hemostasis**; antibiotics prescribed afterwards.
4. **Anesthesia**: general with nasotracheal intubation in 16, monitored in 2, local in 3 (Table 1). Oroantral fistula present in 7.
5. **Irrigation timing**: the text describes irrigation at surgery, before packing. It does not describe serial postoperative irrigation sessions.

**Not specified in the text**: irrigant volume, iodine concentration, irrigation device or pressure, antibiotic agent/dose/duration after surgery, when and how the packing was removed, timing of the postoperative CBCT beyond the 2-month minimum follow-up. Because membrane and foreign-body removal accompanied irrigation, the effect of irrigation alone cannot be isolated.

Authors' explanation for cases that did not clear: in implant- and bone-graft-related sinusitis, opacity could rise because bone particles were not easily washed away and stayed in place.

| Symptoms by cause | Implant 6/7 (1 postoperative facial pain); extraction 5/5; sinus graft 5/7; other: endodontic case improved, fixation-plate case not |

| Post-irrigation ratios (as printed) | Extraction 11.2% (greatest drop); sinus graft 27.4%; implant 41.8%; other 32.2% (smallest reduction) — sentence ambiguous, verify against Fig. 4 |

**Caveats from the text itself**: the prose lists implant cases as 3 perforation + 3 fixture removal + 1 peri-implantitis, whereas Table 2 gives 4 + 2 + 1; the "other" group description is partly contradictory. The authors suggest opacity rose in some implant and graft cases because bone particles were not easily washed out. Limitations named by the authors: retrospective, small sample, short long-term follow-up.
