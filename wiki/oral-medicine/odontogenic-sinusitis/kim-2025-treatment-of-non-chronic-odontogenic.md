## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center case series (n=7 adults, mean age 52.6±16.6 years; Seoul National University Bundang Hospital, 2019-2023) of acute/subacute odontogenic maxillary sinusitis treated by removing the causative tooth or implant, perforating the sinus through the socket with a 19-gauge needle, and irrigating with saline plus culture-guided antibiotics.

Mean 2.7±1.3 irrigation sessions (median 3, IQR 2-4) resolved symptoms, no oroantral fistula occurred, and the authors report that additional surgery was avoided, including a planned otolaryngology operation in the illustrated case.

Socket lavage may be a less invasive step before endonasal surgery when the natural ostium is patent, but n=7, no control group and no stated follow-up duration make this hypothesis-level evidence.

## Summary

This retrospective case series from the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital, evaluated whether acute or subacute odontogenic maxillary sinusitis can be managed by removing the causative tooth or implant and irrigating the sinus through the extraction socket. Seven adults (5 males, 2 females) were treated under local anesthesia: the sinus was intentionally perforated through the socket with a 19-gauge needle, drained into the mouth, and irrigated with saline, with empiric antibiotics followed by culture-guided therapy. Symptoms resolved after a mean of 2.7±1.3 irrigation sessions, with no oroantral fistula. The authors suggest this approach may avoid or precede functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) in non-chronic cases whose natural ostium is presumed patent, while acknowledging the very small sample.

## Key Contributions
- Defines a chairside socket-lavage protocol (19-gauge needle perforation, saline irrigation every 3 days to 2 weeks, upright patient with a pus pan, "Ah" phonation to limit aspiration risk).
- Reports 2.7±1.3 sessions to symptom resolution (median 3, IQR 2-4) in 7 patients with no oroantral fistula.
- Uses saline emerging from the nose as a bedside check of ostial patency.
- Proposes a stop rule: if there is no irrigation flow or no drainage improvement after 2-3 sessions, look for non-odontogenic causes and consult otolaryngology.

## Methodology
- **Design**: retrospective case series, IRB approved (SNUBH B-2408-917-102), Feb 2019-Nov 2023.
- **Population**: adults over 19 years with acute (up to 4 weeks) or subacute (4-12 weeks) odontogenic maxillary sinusitis, no previous sinus treatment, dental treatment within 3 months or a dental lesion communicating with the sinus. Excluded: prior otolaryngology sinus treatment, bilateral disease on CBCT, prior FESS or lateral-wall sinus opening, sinus tumor or trauma history.
- **Intervention**: CBCT-guided removal of the causative tooth/implant; intentional perforation with a 19-gauge needle; saline irrigation; immediate empiric antibiotics, then culture-and-sensitivity-guided antibiotics.
- **Endpoint**: resolution of pus discharge, symptoms and radiographic sinusitis signs; recurrence prompted otolaryngology referral.

## Protocol Detail (as written in the full text)
1. **Work-up**: CBCT (plus initial radiographs, CT and Waters' view in some patients) to identify the causative tooth/implant; unilateral disease only. Teeth were removed when judged non-restorable after endodontic or periodontal therapy.
2. **Removal + perforation (same visit, local anesthesia)**: extract the tooth or remove the implant, then intentionally perforate the sinus through the socket with a 19-gauge needle so the sinus drains into the mouth. A large pus discharge followed in the illustrated case.
3. **Culture**: microbial culture and sensitivity were taken (in the illustrated case at the perforation visit).
4. **Irrigation**: saline through the drainage route. Patient **upright**, **pus pan under the chin**, and told to say **"Ah"** during irrigation to lower aspiration risk, because discharge can pass out through the nose into the throat.
5. **Antibiotics**: empiric antibiotic started immediately after extraction; changed to culture-guided therapy. Illustrated case: cephalexin 1 g bid x 5 days, then ciprofloxacin 500 mg bid x 7 days after penicillin resistance (Viridans streptococcus, Candida albicans, Staphylococcus aureus).
6. **Repeat irrigations**: every 3 days to 2 weeks, depending on patient cooperation and amount of discharge (illustrated case: every 2 weeks, with discharge falling markedly).
7. **Endpoint**: stop when pus discharge, symptoms and radiographic sinusitis signs have resolved. If symptoms recur, consult otolaryngology. Stop rule: no irrigation flow or no drainage improvement after 2-3 irrigations leads to a search for non-odontogenic causes and ENT collaboration.
8. **Patency check**: saline injected via the socket was seen emerging from the nose in all patients, taken as evidence the natural ostium was patent.

Design choice stated by the authors: a sutured indwelling drainage tube is possible but carries a significant oroantral-fistula risk, so repeated syringe irrigation was used instead (no fistula in this series).

**Not specified in the retrieved text** (do not infer): irrigation volume per session, irrigant temperature or additives, needle entry point and angle in the socket, syringe/cannula type, how the 19-gauge needle was connected for irrigation, and whether a needle was left in place. Table 1 and Figures 1-3 were not available.

Antibiotic background cited by the authors (from other literature, not their own data): at least 14 days or 7 days beyond symptom resolution, some advocating up to 28 days; amoxicillin first-line for most acute cases; broader anaerobic cover with a beta-lactamase inhibitor (amoxicillin/clavulanate) or clindamycin, metronidazole in combination; levofloxacin, tetracyclines or moxifloxacin in refractory cases. Their Case 2 resolved with lavage alone and no specific antibiotics, from which they speculate mechanical lavage may outperform antibiotics alone (single observation).

## Clinical reading (synthesis from held evidence)
- The case series is compatible with the wiki's source-control-first thesis (see [[overviews/odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-overview]]): the causative tooth or implant is removed first, and sinus drainage is added intraorally. It differs by substituting repeated socket lavage for FESS in non-chronic cases.
- The logic depends on a patent natural ostium; where the ostium is obstructed, [[sinus-lift/lateral/nam-2014-fess-intraoral-implant-related-sinusitis]] describes FESS plus intraoral surgery, and chronic or ostium-obstructed disease remains an otolaryngology indication.
- Evidence weight is low (n=7, uncontrolled), so it should not replace the ENT-dental algorithms of [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/lin-2024-odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-expert-consensus]].
