## Three-line Summary

A narrative review synthesizing recent data on odontogenic sinusitis (OS) epidemiology, etiology, microbiology, diagnosis, and treatment. OS accounts for 10–14% of maxillary sinusitis (up to 40%) and 75% of unilateral cases; iatrogenic causes (55.97%) now exceed infectious causes, with sinus lifts, implants, and extractions as leading triggers. CBCT is the diagnostic gold standard (detects dental pathology in 67% vs 14% on periapical radiographs); treatment requires dental source control ± antibiotics ± endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS), which offers 96.5% success with lower morbidity than Caldwell-Luc.

## Summary

Martu et al. (2022) provide a comprehensive narrative review of odontogenic sinusitis (OS) from an ENT perspective, covering epidemiology, etiology (with emphasis on the rising iatrogenic proportion), microbiology, clinical and radiological diagnosis, and medical/dental/surgical treatment options. The review synthesizes 108 references (1988–2022) and positions OS as a multidisciplinary condition at the dentistry-ENT interface. Key messages: (1) OS is under-recognized (10–40% of maxillary sinusitis, 75% of unilateral cases); (2) etiology has shifted — iatrogenic causes (sinus lifts, implants, endodontic overfills, extractions) now dominate at ~56%; (3) CBCT is the imaging modality of choice for dual confirmation (sinus pathology + dental source); (4) antibiotics alone fail without dental source control; (5) FESS is the preferred surgical approach (96.5% success, mucosa-preserving, ostium-patency-restoring) over Caldwell-Luc; (6) OAF closure follows a size-and-timing algorithm with buccal fat pad flap favored for larger defects.

## Key Contributions

1. **Epidemiology update**: OS = 10–14% of maxillary sinusitis (up to 40% in referral series); 75% of unilateral maxillary sinusitis; peak age 40–60; slight female predominance (57.7%).
2. **Etiology shift documented**: Iatrogenic 55.97% (sinus lift perforation, malpositioned implants, endodontic overfilling, root displacement during extraction, orthognathic/cleft surgery) vs infectious (periapical 25.1%, periodontal 8.3%, endo-perio) per Lechien 2014 (n=674).
3. **Anatomical risk quantification**: 2nd molar roots closest to sinus floor (1.97 mm); 1st molar most commonly implicated (35.6% of OS cases) due to higher endo/perio disease burden.
4. **Microbiology profile**: Acute OS — aerobic (Streptococcus, Haemophilus, Moraxella); Chronic OS — anaerobic (Prevotella, Porphyromonas, Fusobacterium); fungal/biofilm involvement in recalcitrant cases.
5. **Diagnostic hierarchy**: Clinical (unilateral symptoms + fetid odor) → nasal endoscopy (purulent middle meatus discharge) → imaging: CBCT > CT > panoramic > periapical. CBCT detects dental etiology in 67% vs 14% on periapical; 60% periapical pathology missed on 2D.
6. **Treatment algorithm**: Dental source control (RCT/extraction/periodontal/foreign body removal) is non-negotiable; antibiotics adjunctive (culture-guided ideal); FESS preferred (96.5% success, mucosa preservation, ostium patency, lower morbidity); Caldwell-Luc reserved for foreign bodies, cysts, tumors, fungal ball, spread beyond maxillary sinus.
7. **OAF management**: Size-based: <3 mm observation, <5 mm primary closure, ≥5 mm flap (buccal fat pad superior per NMA), >10 mm palatal rotation; timing critical — acute primary closure ~95% vs chronic secondary ~67%.

## Methodology

- **Study design**: Narrative review (non-systematic).
- **Literature scope**: 108 references spanning 1988–2022, covering anatomy, definition/classification, epidemiology, etiology, microbiology, clinical diagnosis, radiological diagnosis (comparative), medical treatment, dental treatment, surgical treatment (FESS vs Caldwell-Luc), and OAF management.
- **Search/selection**: Not explicitly described; authors state aim to "present the etiopathogenesis, bacteriology, clinical manifestations, as well as treatment of odontogenic sinusitis from an updated perspective through reviewing the literature."
- **No formal quality assessment**: No PRISMA flow, no risk-of-bias tool, no GRADE — inherent limitation of narrative reviews.
- **Synthesis method**: Qualitative narrative synthesis organized by thematic sections; key statistics extracted from individual studies (e.g., Lechien 2014 n=674 for etiology breakdown; De Corso 2022 n=262 for FESS success; Kim 2016 for implant protrusion threshold).

## Results

### Epidemiology & Etiology
- OS prevalence: 10–14% of maxillary sinusitis (range up to 40%); 75% of unilateral maxillary sinusitis.
- Age distribution: 40–60 years peak; F:M ratio ~1.3:1 (57.7% female).
- Etiology breakdown (Lechien 2014, n=674): iatrogenic 65.3%, apical periodontitis 25.1%, marginal periodontitis 8.3%.
- Iatrogenic sub-causes (55.97% overall): sinus lift complications, malpositioned implants, endodontic perforations/overfills, root displacement during extraction, orthognathic surgery, cleft surgery, Le Fort osteotomies.
- Tooth-level: 1st molar 35.6%, 2nd molar 17.4%, 2nd premolar 14.4% (Troeltzsch 2015); meta-analysis (Arias-Irimia 2010): molar region 47.68% (1st molar 22.51%, 3rd molar 17.21%, 2nd molar 3.97%).
- Right maxillary sinus slightly more involved (+2%); bilateral OS rare.

### Microbiology
- Acute OS: polymicrobial, aerobic predominance (Streptococcus spp., Haemophilus influenzae, Moraxella catarrhalis).
- Chronic OS: anaerobic predominance (Prevotella, Porphyromonas, Fusobacterium, Peptostreptococcus).
- Fungi (Aspergillus, Candida) and biofilm formation implicated in antibiotic resistance and chronicity.
- Dental origin confirmed by culture match between sinus aspirate and periodontal/periapical samples.

### Clinical Diagnosis
- Cardinal symptoms: unilateral nasal obstruction, purulent rhinorrhea (yellow-green, fetid), facial pain/pressure, hyposmia/anosmia.
- Anterior rhinoscopy: purulent middle meatus discharge.
- Nasal endoscopy: superior visualization, detects ostial obstruction, polyps, anatomic variants.
- Dental exam: percussion sensitivity, caries, periodontal probing, implant mobility, OAF detection, sinus lift history.

### Radiological Diagnosis
| Modality | Sensitivity for Dental Pathology | Key Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Periapical radiograph | 14% (vs CBCT) | 2D, superimposition, misses 60% periapical lesions |
| Panoramic | Low for periapical | Overlap, distortion, low resolution |
| CT | High | Higher dose, metal artifacts, less detail for periodontal/endodontic |
| **CBCT** | **67%** (detects 67% missed on 2D) | 10× dose of periapical; cost/access |

- Mucosal thickening >3 mm (or >2 mm with symptoms) = pathological; can increase 15× in acute sinusitis.
- CBCT advantages: 3D dentoalveolar-sinus relationship, sinus floor thickness, pre-implant sinus health assessment, septum/mucosal cyst detection.

### Treatment

#### Medical
- Antibiotics alone insufficient for treatable odontogenic lesions (source control required).
- Empiric: amoxicillin/clavulanate, clindamycin (penicillin allergy), doxycycline.
- Culture-guided preferred; duration 7–14 days (extend if chronic).
- Adjuncts: nasal saline irrigation, topical steroids, mucolytics, decongestants.

#### Dental (Source Control) — Mandatory
- Endodontic: RCT or retreatment for periapical lesions.
- Periodontal: scaling/root planing, pocket elimination for infrabony pockets.
- Foreign body removal: gutta-percha/sealer overfill, displaced root fragments, migrated implants.
- Extraction: of non-restorable causative teeth; socket management to prevent OAF.
- Implant removal: if implant protrudes >3–4 mm into sinus or is infected.

#### Surgical
- **FESS (preferred)**: uncinectomy, maxillary antrostomy, ostium enlargement, mucosa preservation, infected tissue debridement. Success 96.5% (De Corso 2022, n=262). Advantages: physiological drainage, lower morbidity, shorter hospitalization, better visualization vs Caldwell-Luc.
- **Caldwell-Luc (reserve)**: wide access for foreign bodies, cysts, tumors, fungal ball, spread to adjacent sinuses/pterygomaxillary fossa. Higher morbidity: infraorbital nerve injury, canine fossa numbness, mucosal stripping → ciliary dysfunction, higher OAF risk.
- **One-stage dental + FESS**: ~97% success, recurrence <1–6% (Albu 2025 citing multiple series).

#### Oroantral Fistula (OAF)
- Size algorithm: <3 mm spontaneous healing; <5 mm primary suture; 5–10 mm local flap (buccal advancement, buccal fat pad); >10 mm palatal rotation flap.
- Buccal fat pad (BFP) flap: NMA (Oliva 2024, 9 studies, n=519) shows OR 15 vs buccal advancement and OR 15.29 vs palatal flap for medium-large defects.
- Timing: acute primary closure ~95% success; chronic secondary closure ~67% (Lazow 1999) — early closure strongly favored.
