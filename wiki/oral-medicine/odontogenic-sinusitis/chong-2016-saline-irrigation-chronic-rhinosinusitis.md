## Three-line Summary

Cochrane systematic review with meta-analysis (2 RCTs, 116 adults) evaluated nasal saline irrigation for chronic rhinosinusitis: large-volume (150 ml) hypertonic (2%) saline vs usual care, and low-volume (5 ml) nebulised saline vs intranasal corticosteroids.

Large-volume hypertonic saline improved disease-specific HRQL (RSDI) vs usual care at 3 months (MD +6.3, 95% CI 0.89–11.71) and 6 months (MD +13.5, 95% CI 9.63–17.37), but evidence quality was low/very low; low-volume nebulised saline was inferior to intranasal corticosteroids for disease severity (MD -13.5, 95% CI -14.44 to -12.56), very low quality.

Delivery method and volume critically determine efficacy: daily large-volume hypertonic irrigation shows some benefit over no treatment, while low-volume nebulised saline offers no advantage over intranasal steroids; both conclusions are limited by high risk of bias and small samples.

## Summary

This Cochrane Intervention Review (part of a suite of six reviews on primary medical management of chronic rhinosinusitis) applied a strict EPOS 2012 diagnostic definition and searched up to October 2015. Only two RCTs met inclusion criteria (total n=116), and they addressed fundamentally different comparisons:

**Comparison 1 — Large-volume hypertonic saline vs usual care (n=76):** One open-label RCT randomised adults with CRS (with or without polyps) 2:1 to daily 150 ml hypertonic (2%) saline irrigation or usual care for 6 months. Disease-specific HRQL (RSDI, 0–100) favoured saline at 3 months (MD +6.3, 95% CI 0.89–11.71, low quality) and 6 months (MD +13.5, 95% CI 9.63–17.37, very low quality). Disease severity (single-item score, range unreported) also favoured saline at both timepoints (low/very low quality). Generic HRQL (SF-12) showed no clear difference at 3 months but a small uncertain benefit at 6 months (MD +10.5, 95% CI 0.66–20.34, very low quality). Adverse events were collected only in the saline group: 23% experienced side effects (epistaxis, irritation, burning, headache), with 3% rating them significant.

**Comparison 2 — Low-volume nebulised saline vs intranasal corticosteroids (n=40):** One RCT randomised adults with nasal polyps to 5 ml nebulised saline twice daily or intranasal corticosteroids for 3 months, with 3-month post-treatment follow-up. The primary outcome (disease-specific HRQL) was not reported. Disease severity scores favoured intranasal corticosteroids at end of treatment (MD -13.5, 95% CI -14.44 to -12.56) and at 6 months (MD -7.71, 95% CI -8.72 to -6.70), both very low quality. Adverse events (epistaxis, local irritation) were imprecise (very low quality) but numerically higher with corticosteroids.

The authors concluded that the two studies are too different in population, intervention, and comparator to draw unified practice conclusions. There is no benefit of low-volume nebulised saline over intranasal steroids. There may be some benefit of daily large-volume hypertonic saline vs placebo/usual care, but the evidence quality is low (3 months) to very low (6 months).

## Key Contributions

- First Cochrane review to separate evidence by saline delivery method (large-volume irrigation vs low-volume nebulisation), concentration (hypertonic vs isotonic), and comparator (usual care vs active pharmacological treatment).
- Quantifies the effect size of large-volume hypertonic saline on disease-specific HRQL: moderate effect at 3 months, large effect at 6 months, but GRADE quality low/very low due to risk of bias, imprecision, and indirectness.
- Demonstrates that low-volume (5 ml) nebulised saline is not a viable alternative to intranasal corticosteroids for CRS with polyps — corticosteroids show a large effect size advantage, albeit very low quality.
- Reports the only systematic adverse event data for saline irrigation in this context: 23% side-effect rate (mostly mild epistaxis/irritation) with large-volume hypertonic saline; no control-group AE data.
- Highlights critical methodological gaps: no studies on isotonic vs hypertonic head-to-head, no studies on saline as adjunct to intranasal corticosteroids, no long-term data >6 months, no adequately powered blinded trials.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Cochrane Intervention Review (PRISMA 2009/2020). Editorial group: Cochrane ENT Group.
- **Search**: Cochrane ENT Trials Register, CENTRAL 2015 Issue 9, Ovid MEDLINE (1946–Oct 2015), Ovid EMBASE (1974–Oct 2015), ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP, Google Scholar; date 30 Oct 2015; no language/year/status restrictions.
- **Inclusion**: RCTs (cluster/quasi-RCTs included) with ≥2-week follow-up; adults with CRS per EPOS 2012 (≥2 symptoms including blockage/discharge for ≥12 weeks + endoscopic/CT evidence), with or without polyps; saline by any means (douche, irrigation, drops, spray, nebuliser) vs placebo/no treatment/pharmacological interventions.
- **Exclusion**: Majority cystic fibrosis, fungal/eosinophilic/allergic rhinosinusitis, aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease, sinus surgery within 6 weeks; within-patient randomisation; peri-surgical studies.
- **Primary outcomes**: Disease-specific HRQL (SNOT-22, RSOM-31, SNOT-20, RSDI), patient-reported disease severity (CSS, VAS, single-item scores), epistaxis.
- **Secondary outcomes**: Generic HRQL (SF-36, EQ-5D, SF-12), local irritation, discomfort, endoscopic score (Lund-Mackay/Lund-Kennedy), CT score (Lund-Mackay).
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB tool (6 domains). Both included studies judged high risk (open-label, unclear allocation concealment, small samples, 2:1 randomisation, baseline results revealed at 6-month follow-up in Study 1).
- **GRADE**: Applied per outcome; all outcomes rated low or very low quality.
- **Analysis**: Random-effects meta-analysis (MD for continuous, RR for dichotomous). Subgroups by follow-up period (3 to <6 months, 6 to 12 months, >12 months). No pooling across the two comparisons due to clinical heterogeneity.
- **Included studies**:
  1. **Large-volume hypertonic vs usual care**: 76 adults (52/24), with/without polyps, 6 months, open-label, primary care recruitment. Intervention: 150 ml hypertonic (2%) saline daily via irrigation bottle. Comparator: usual care (no placebo).
  2. **Low-volume nebulised vs intranasal corticosteroids**: 40 adults with polyps, 3 months treatment + 3 months follow-up, secondary care. Intervention: 5 ml nebulised saline twice daily. Comparator: intranasal corticosteroids daily.

## Results

### Comparison 1: Large-volume (150 ml) hypertonic (2%) saline vs usual care (1 RCT, n=76)

| Outcome | Follow-up | Mean Difference (95% CI) | GRADE | Clinical Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Disease-specific HRQL (RSDI, 0–100, higher=better) | 3 months | +6.3 (0.89 to 11.71) | Low | Moderate effect; patients in saline group had better QoL |
| Disease-specific HRQL (RSDI) | 6 months | +13.5 (9.63 to 17.37) | Very low | Large effect; but patients saw baseline scores before 6-month questionnaire |
| Disease severity (single-item, range unknown, lower=better) | 3 months | -0.9 (-1.45 to -0.35) | Low | Moderate effect favouring saline; scale range not reported |
| Disease severity | 6 months | -1.59 (-2.15 to -1.04) | Very low | Large effect favouring saline; same scale limitation |
| Generic HRQL (SF-12, 0–100) | 3 months | +5.3 (-4.38 to 14.98) | Low | CI crosses zero — unclear if real difference |
| Generic HRQL (SF-12) | 6 months | +10.5 (0.66 to 20.34) | Very low | Small benefit possible but uncertain; CI barely excludes zero |
| Adverse events (epistaxis, irritation, burning) | 6 months | 23% in saline group (10/52); 0 events reported in control | — | No control data; 8 mild, 2 significant |

**Key caveats from the review**: Study 1 was open-label with subjective outcomes; 2:1 randomisation created imbalance; participants were shown their baseline scores before completing the 6-month questionnaire (potential recall bias); disease severity score range not reported, making effect sizes uninterpretable in absolute terms; no adverse event data from control group.

### Comparison 2: Intranasal corticosteroids vs low-volume (5 ml) nebulised saline (1 RCT, n=40)

| Outcome | Follow-up | Result | GRADE | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Disease severity overall score (range unknown, higher=worse) | 3 months (end of treatment) | MD -13.5 (-14.44 to -12.56) favouring INCS | Very low | Large effect; intranasal corticosteroids superior |
| Disease severity overall score | 6 months (3 months post-treatment) | MD -7.71 (-8.72 to -6.70) favouring INCS | Very low | Large effect persists after stopping treatment |
| Adverse events — epistaxis | End of study | RR 2.00 (0.20 to 20.33) | Very low | Imprecise; trend toward more epistaxis with INCS |
| Adverse events — local irritation | End of study | RR 3.00 (0.13 to 69.52) | Very low | Imprecise; trend toward more irritation with INCS |
| Disease-specific HRQL | Not reported | — | — | Primary outcome missing |

**Key caveats**: Study 2 was very small (20 per arm); open-label with subjective outcomes; unclear random sequence generation and allocation concealment; primary outcome not reported; all outcomes very low quality.
