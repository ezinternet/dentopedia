## Three-line Summary

Self-described prospective randomized controlled trial (n=40; 20 sinus irrigation vs 20 Caldwell–Luc, Fujian, China, 2012–2018) of large odontogenic cysts (median diameter about 44 mm) involving the maxillary sinus with unilateral maxillary sinusitis, all treated by cyst enucleation.

Irrigation (100 mL saline twice daily via an epidural catheter through an inferior meatal antrostomy, 2 weeks) vs Caldwell–Luc: mean VAS pain on day 1 was 2.50 (1.43) vs 4.15 (2.78), fever above 38.0 °C on day 2 in 1/20 (5%) vs 8/20 (40%), and severe swelling in 2/20 (10%) vs 6/20 (30%); all P<0.05 per the authors. All patients were cured, CT showed the sinus cleared at 8–12 weeks in both arms, and no cyst recurrence was seen at 36 months.

Irrigation appears to cause less postoperative morbidity than Caldwell–Luc with equivalent cure, but the trial is small and single-center, does not describe randomization, allocation concealment or blinding, and reports no objective mucosal or mucociliary measurement.

## Summary

This trial from Fujian Medical University compared two ways of managing the maxillary sinus after enucleation of a large odontogenic cyst (OC) that had grown into the sinus and was accompanied by unilateral maxillary sinusitis. In 40 patients, 20 received a mucosa-preserving approach (inferior meatal antrostomy with a catheter and 2 weeks of saline irrigation) and 20 received the traditional Caldwell–Luc (CDL) procedure with stripping of the sinus lining and packing. Early postoperative morbidity was lower after irrigation: mean VAS pain on day 1 was 2.50 vs 4.15, fever above 38.0 °C occurred in 1/20 vs 8/20, and severe facial swelling in 2/20 vs 6/20. Every patient in both groups was cured, CT showed the sinus clear at 8–12 weeks, and no cyst recurrence or paresthesia was seen to 36 months. The authors propose irrigation as an alternative to CDL; the evidence is limited by the small sample, a randomization process that is not described, and the absence of objective mucosal or mucociliary outcomes.

## Key Contributions

- Comparative, same-setting data on irrigation after enucleation of large OCs that involve the maxillary sinus, versus the traditional CDL procedure.
- Documents a concrete protocol: inferior meatal antrostomy (IMA) at the thinnest bony portion of the cyst wall per CT, epidural catheter fixed near the base of the nose, 100 mL normal saline twice daily via a 20-mL syringe, catheter removed at 2 weeks.
- Shows lower postoperative swelling, pain and temperature elevation with the mucosa-preserving approach, with equivalent cure at 8–12 weeks (CT) and no recurrence at 36 months.

## Methodology

- **Design**: prospective, randomized controlled design (as stated by the authors); IRB-approved; informed consent obtained. No description of the randomization method, allocation concealment or blinding.
- **Participants**: 40 patients, enrolled 2012-01-01 to 2018-01-31; 20 per group. Inclusion: clinically diagnosed maxillary OC; otolaryngologist-diagnosed unilateral maxillary sinusitis; CT showing the cyst extending into the sinus with complete obliteration of the sinus; cyst adherent to the sinus mucosa at surgery; histopathologic confirmation. Non-vital involved teeth were endodontically treated before surgery.
- **Both groups**: intravenous-inhalation anesthesia, cyst enucleation with its non-separable adherent sinus membrane, absorbable sutures, cefazolin 2000 mg IV twice daily for 3 days, sinus precautions for 2 weeks.
- **Group A (irrigation)**: sinus mucosa left as far as possible; IMA; epidural catheter; saline irrigation twice daily for 2 weeks.
- **Group B (CDL)**: diseased and inflamed sinus membranes stripped; sinus packed for hemostasis.
- **Outcomes and tests**: facial swelling day 2 (Wilcoxon rank sum), VAS pain day 1 (t-test), temperature elevation day 2 (chi-squared), CT clearance, recurrence, nasal obstruction and paresthesia to 36 months. Exact P values are not reported (P<0.05).

## Clinical Notes

- The comparison applies to a specific scenario: a large OC (median about 44 mm) that obliterates the sinus on CT, adheres to the sinus mucosa, and coexists with unilateral sinusitis. It does not address sinusitis without a cyst or cysts that do not enter the sinus.
- The authors argue that CDL stripping risks sclerosis of the sinus walls, osteitis and impaired mucociliary function, and that transnasal endoscopic cyst removal is limited by a narrow field and dead angles and cannot perform root-end resection with retrograde filling (their discussion, not tested here).
- They cite a report that the inferior meatal antrostomy was closed in 82% of 367 cases at 3 months, and a rabbit study in which the nasal antral window did not redirect mucociliary clearance; this trial itself does not report antrostomy patency.
- Reasoning only from our held pages: this trial's mucosa-preserving principle (preserve lining, add an inferior-meatus window) matches the single-case mucosa-preserving approach in [[sinus-lift/lateral/ohyama-2022-novel-management-severe-odontogenic-maxillary]] and addresses the CLP morbidity listed in [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/kim-2019-definition-management-odontogenic-maxillary]]. Because the trial's randomization is undocumented and it is small and single-center, treat it as supportive rather than definitive.
