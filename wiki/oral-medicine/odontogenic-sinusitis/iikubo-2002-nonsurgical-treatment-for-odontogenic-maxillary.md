## Three-line Summary

Single-patient preliminary case report (25-year-old woman, left maxillary second molar with apical periodontitis and acute odontogenic maxillary sinusitis, OMS, refractory to 6 weeks of root-canal treatment plus antibiotics; Tohoku University Dental Hospital).

One session of saline irrigation (15 ml total, 3 x 5 ml, 23 G needle) through the root canal of the causal tooth, followed by 15 ml air, abolished the cheek pain, nasal obstruction and retrorhinorrhoea by 2 days; CT at 1 week showed near-disappearance of mucosal thickening and marked improvement of ostiomeatal unit obstruction, complete resolution at 6 months, and no recurrence or side effect at 12 months.

Transdental irrigation may open the ostiomeatal unit without tooth extraction or surgery in antibiotic- and endodontic-refractory OMS, but this is n=1 with concurrent antibiotics/endodontic therapy and no control, so efficacy cannot be separated from natural resolution.

## Summary

This preliminary single-patient case report from Tohoku University describes a 25-year-old woman with acute odontogenic maxillary sinusitis (OMS) from apical periodontitis of the left maxillary second molar, in whom six weeks of root-canal treatment plus antibiotics left cheek pain, nasal obstruction and retrorhinorrhoea unchanged and CT showed an air-fluid level with total ostiomeatal unit (OMU) obstruction. A single irrigation of the sinus through the canal of the causal tooth (15 ml saline in three 5 ml aliquots via a 23 G needle, then 15 ml air) was followed by complete symptom relief within 2 days, near-disappearance of mucosal thickening and marked OMU improvement on CT at 1 week, complete resolution at 6 months, and no recurrence at 12 months. The authors propose transdental irrigation as a tooth-preserving, OMU-preserving nonsurgical alternative to extraction, antral washout, Caldwell-Luc or FESS for refractory OMS. As n=1 with concurrent antibiotics and endodontic therapy, it is a feasibility signal rather than evidence of efficacy.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the root canal of the causal tooth as the access portal for sinus irrigation, preserving the tooth (unlike extraction-socket lavage) and avoiding the OMU destruction attributed to FESS.
- Rationale: inflammatory bone and mucosal destruction at the apex leaves no obstructive structure, so saline passes without resistance; internal pressure may open a partially closed ostium; air injection after saline is proposed as an aid to OMU opening.
- Risk-mitigation sequence: thorough canal cleaning/shaping before irrigation, apical length by apex locator, no needle protrusion past the apex, air injected only after smooth saline passage, antibiotic cover afterwards.

## Methodology

Single patient, informed consent for the irrigation. Initial therapy: conventional root-canal treatment, aminopenicillin (antibiogram-guided), clarithromycin and pronase, analgesics, for 6 weeks. Irrigation: 23 G needle to canal apex, 5 ml physiological saline x 3 (15 ml total) once, then 15 ml air, canal sealed with zinc oxide eugenol cement, medication resumed; later canal obturation and 2 months of preventive macrolide plus pronase. Outcomes: symptoms, CT at 1 week and 6 months, recurrence over 12 months.

## Results

| Time point | Finding |
|---|---|
| After 6 weeks of root-canal treatment + antibiotics | Fever/malaise/spontaneous pain gone within 1 week; oppressive cheek pain, nasal obstruction, retrorhinorrhoea persist; CT unchanged (anterior ethmoid improved only) |
| During irrigation | Fluid felt leaking into nasal cavity and nasopharynx with fetid odor; no resistance, no pain |
| 2 days after irrigation | All symptoms completely disappeared (odorless serous retrorhinorrhoea only until next day) |
| CT 1 week | Maxillary mucosal thickening almost gone; OMU obstruction greatly improved; slight thickening on sinus floor |
| CT 6 months | Maxillary mucosal thickening completely disappeared |
| 12 months | No recurrence; no side effect of saline irrigation |
