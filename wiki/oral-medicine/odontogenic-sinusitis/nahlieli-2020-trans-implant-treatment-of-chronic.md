## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study of 31 patients with chronic recurrent maxillary sinusitis (CRS) treated by trans-implant lavage through implants with an internal removable sealing screw — 28 had simultaneous implant placement plus sinusitis treatment (referred for sinus elevation), 3 were treated via an existing implant.

28/31 (90%) had complete relief of most symptoms (nasal obstruction/discharge, anosmia/hyposmia) by day 30; follow-up nasal endoscopy showed no active sinus disease; 27 implants were well-osseointegrated and in prosthetic use, 1 implant failed from poor bone quality, and no CRS recurrence was reported during follow-up.

The proposed protocol (pus drainage via the punctured sinus floor, 3 lavage sessions, 4 CBCT scans, 4 transnasal endoscopies, with implant and sinus assessment at days 30 and 60) is promising, but this is an abstract-level, uncontrolled retrospective series.

## Summary

This retrospective study (Nahlieli et al., Int J Oral Maxillofac Surg 2020) tested a protocol for treating chronic recurrent maxillary sinusitis (CRS) through implants that carry an internal central port closed by a removable sealing screw. Of 31 patients, 28 were referred for sinus elevation and received simultaneous implantation and sinusitis treatment, while 3 were treated through an already existing implant. The protocol combines drainage of pus through the punctured sinus floor with antral irrigation/lavage, imaging and endoscopic surveillance, and a joint evaluation of implant status and sinus status at days 30 and 60. In the abstract, 90% of patients had complete relief of most symptoms by day 30 with no endoscopic sign of active sinus disease, and no CRS recurrence was reported during follow-up. ## Key Contributions

- Describes an implant-as-access-port approach: a dental implant with an internal central port and integral sealing screw that permits sinus drainage, irrigation, observation, and further treatment.
- Proposes a defined schedule — 3 lavage sessions, 4 CBCT scans, 4 transnasal endoscopic observations — with implant and sinusitis outcomes assessed together at days 30 and 60.
- Reports the series' outcomes: 90% symptom relief by day 30, 27 implants osseointegrated and in prosthetic use, 1 failure (poor bone quality), no CRS recurrence reported.

## Methodology

Retrospective study, n=31 CRS patients. 28 patients (referred for sinus elevation) had simultaneous implantation and trans-implant sinusitis treatment; 3 had treatment through an existing implant. Treatment: drainage of pus through the punctured sinus floor, then antral irrigation/lavage. Outcomes: symptoms (nasal obstruction/discharge, anosmia/hyposmia), nasal endoscopy, CBCT, and implant status. Inclusion criteria, irrigant, antibiotic co-therapy, follow-up length, and implant system are not in the abstract.
