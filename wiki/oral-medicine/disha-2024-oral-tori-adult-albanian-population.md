## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional pilot study, n=122, adult dental patients at a single center in Tirana, Albania (mean age 32.3±12.7y).

Overall oral tori prevalence 48.4% (torus mandibularis 39%, alveolar bone exostosis 16%, torus palatinus 14%), peaking in the 18–29 age group (54.2% of cases); no significant association with gender or bruxism.

Prevalence was markedly higher than most prior population reports (2.1–38%), highlighting large population-to-population variability; small single-center sample and incomplete vitamin D/calcium self-report data limit generalizability.

## Summary

This single-center pilot cross-sectional study examined 122 adult dental patients at Diamond Dental Hospital, Tirana, Albania, to determine the prevalence and morphologic pattern of oral tori (torus mandibularis, TM; torus palatinus, TP) and alveolar bone exostosis (ABE) — the first published prevalence data for Albania. Overall prevalence of any oral tori was high at 48.4%, with TM the most common subtype (39%), followed by ABE (16%) and TP (14%). Prevalence peaked in the youngest age group studied (18–29 years, 54.2% of tori cases), contrasting with several other population studies that report peaks in middle-to-older age. No statistically significant association was found between oral tori occurrence and gender, bruxism, or malocclusion, though the small sample size may limit power to detect true associations. TP was most commonly flat in shape (71.4%) and TM most commonly bilateral solitary (47.9%). The measured prevalence substantially exceeds most prior international reports (range 2.1–38% across cited studies), underscoring how widely oral tori prevalence varies by population/ethnicity/methodology.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes baseline prevalence and morphology data for oral tori/exostoses in an Albanian population, previously unreported.
- Separately characterizes TM, TP, and ABE prevalence and shape/pattern distributions, useful for cross-population comparison.
- Discusses (without directly testing) a range of proposed etiologic contributors — occlusal stress, genetics, vitamin D/calcium status, high-altitude hypoxia, and air pollution — situating this case within the broader mechanistic uncertainty around exostosis formation.
- Notes literature suggesting co-occurrence of different exostosis types (having one roughly doubles odds of another), consistent with a shared predisposition across TM/TP/ABE.

## Methodology

Single-center, single-examiner cross-sectional clinical study (Diamond Dental Hospital, Tirana, Feb–Apr 2024; ethics approval Council of Ethics UMT No. 375/1). Adults ≥18y of Albanian ethnicity, dentulous or edentulous, without orthodontic history, were examined by visual inspection and palpation for TP, TM, and ABE at standard anatomic sites (palate midline, sublingual mandible, buccal/lingual distal jaw regions). Self-reported vitamin D level, calcium level, and bruxism were collected via questionnaire. Chi-square testing (SPSS 26.0) compared groups by age, gender, and tori pattern.

## Results

- Overall oral tori prevalence: 48.4% (59/122 patients).
- By subtype: torus mandibularis 39.3%, alveolar bone exostosis 16%, torus palatinus 11.5% (overlap/combination patterns observed; no TP+ABE combination cases).
- Age: highest prevalence in 18–29y group (54.2% of tori cases); no statistically significant age-group difference overall.
- Gender: no significant difference in TM (p=0.359), TP (p=0.466), or ABE (p=0.968) pattern distribution.
- Bruxism/malocclusion: present more often in the tori group (53%/43%) than the non-tori group (20%/10%), but the association was not statistically significant (p=0.745/0.813) — plausibly due to sample size.
- Comparator prevalence across cited studies ranged 2.1–38%, well below this study's 48.3%.
