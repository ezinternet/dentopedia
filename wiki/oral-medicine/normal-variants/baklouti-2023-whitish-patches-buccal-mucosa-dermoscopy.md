## Three-line Summary

Case report (n=1; 46-year-old woman; Dermlite DL4 dermoscopy) of chronic habitual lip biting (morsicatio labiorum) misdiagnosed as oral lichen planus (OLP) for 2 years and managed unsuccessfully with topical steroids.

Dermoscopy showed structureless whitish/yellowish areas, dotted-vessel erosions, and loose easily-removable white scales — critically lacking Wickham striae (OLP's pathognomonic sign) — enabling correct reclassification and avoiding biopsy.

The case illustrates that patients are often unaware of their biting behavior; dermoscopy (mucoscopy) provides a low-cost, non-invasive pathway to distinguish morsicatio from OLP and prevent unnecessary treatment escalation.

## Summary

This single case report demonstrates a common diagnostic pitfall: chronic habitual lip/cheek biting (morsicatio labiorum/buccarum, 교흔) can closely mimic oral lichen planus (OLP) clinically, leading to inappropriate steroid treatment when the true cause — an often-subconscious biting habit — goes unrecognized. In this case, a 46-year-old woman with 2 years of unresponsive "OLP" treatment was found on dermoscopic exam to lack Wickham striae (the pathognomonic reticular pattern of OLP) and instead showed whitish/yellowish structureless areas, dotted-vessel erosions, and loose easily-removable white scales — a pattern consistent with morsicatio. The diagnosis was confirmed when the patient herself admitted to an anxious temperament and intermittent self-inflicted lip biting. Management was simple behavioral counseling rather than continued steroid therapy, with psychiatric referral reserved for refractory/compulsive cases. The case illustrates the practical value of dermoscopy (mucoscopy) as a non-invasive tool to avoid unnecessary biopsy and mistreatment of what is fundamentally a benign, behavior-driven mucosal alteration.

## Key Contributions

- First reported dermoscopic characterization specifically of morsicatio labiorum (per the authors), providing a concrete visual/diagnostic signature (absence of Wickham striae plus specific erosion/scale pattern).
- Highlights that patients are often unaware of their own biting behavior, which is a key driver of chronic misdiagnosis and unnecessary treatment escalation.
- Demonstrates a clear, low-cost, non-invasive diagnostic pathway (dermoscopy → patient history confirmation) that can replace biopsy in classic presentations.

## Methodology

Single case report. 46-year-old female, no relevant medical history, presented with a 2-year history of asymptomatic whitish lip plaques previously managed (without improvement) as presumed OLP with topical steroids. Dermoscopic examination performed using a Dermlite DL4 (3Gen) handheld dermatoscope. Diagnosis was clinicopathologically supported by dermoscopic pattern and confirmed by patient self-report of habitual lip biting linked to an anxious/nervous temperament.

## Results

- Clinical exam: scaly, jagged, irregular whitish patches with thin erosions, inner lip mucosa; no other skin lesions or systemic symptoms.
- Dermoscopy: whitish/yellowish structureless areas and lines; small superficial erosions with dotted vessels; loose, easily removable white scales.
- Critical negative finding: absence of Wickham striae and absence of other OLP-associated vascular signs (linear/hairpin vessels, pigmented structures, hyperkeratotic-bordered erosions) — this negative pattern was decisive for ruling out OLP.
- Outcome: patient counseled to stop lip-biting; no biopsy required; psychiatric referral considered only for refractory cases (not needed here).
