## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cross-sectional record review (n=16,572 outpatient dental records, Lalitpur Nepal tertiary hospital, 2016–2019; WHO-guide-based diagnosis) establishing the first population-level prevalence baseline for oral mucosal lesions (OMLs) and benign normal variants in this population.

21.08% had an OML; 13.96% had a benign normal variant — most commonly frictional keratosis, linea alba (교흔), and Fordyce's granules (포다이스 그래뉼, 8.84% overall); 80.10% of all normal variants were located on the buccal mucosa.

Cross-population Fordyce's granule prevalence varies enormously (1.2–82.8% across studies) — clinicians should recognize the lesion pattern as benign rather than calibrating against a single global prevalence figure; buccal mucosa dominance underscores its role in differentiating normal variants from potentially malignant buccal lesions.

## Summary

This large retrospective cross-sectional study from a tertiary dental hospital in Lalitpur, Nepal, screened 16,572 outpatient dental records (2016-2019) to establish the first population-level prevalence baseline for both pathological oral mucosal lesions (OMLs) and benign "normal variant" mucosal findings in this population. Normal variants — findings that should not be mistaken for disease — were present in 13.96% of patients, most commonly frictional keratosis and linea alba (교흔), with Fordyce's granules (포다이스 그래뉼) present in 8.84% overall and peaking at 31.76% of normal variants in the 11-20y age group. Normal variants were more common in males overall and were concentrated on the buccal mucosa (80.10% of all normal-variant findings). The study underscores that reported prevalence of these entities varies enormously across populations (e.g., Fordyce's granules 1.2%-82.8% across studies), meaning clinicians should not use a single global "expected" number but instead recognize the lesion pattern itself as benign.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes Nepal's first population-based (non-biopsy-restricted) prevalence data for both OMLs and normal variants.
- Quantifies age-, gender-, and site-specific distribution of Fordyce's granules, linea alba, and frictional keratosis as distinct, non-pathological entities.
- Documents that normal variants cluster overwhelmingly on the buccal mucosa (80.10%), directly relevant to differentiating them from potentially malignant buccal lesions.
- Highlights wide cross-population variability in normal-variant prevalence, cautioning against over-interpreting any single benchmark figure.

## Methodology

Cross-sectional retrospective record review at the Dept. of Oral Medicine and Radiology, KIST Medical College and Teaching Hospital (Lalitpur, Nepal). Census sampling of all oral lesion cases (clinically or histologically diagnosed) recorded January 2016-December 2019, with data entry/analysis conducted January-March 2021 following WHO's guide to epidemiology and diagnosis of oral mucosal diseases and conditions. Cases with incomplete demographic/clinical data were excluded; duplicate patients removed via unique OPD identifier. Descriptive frequency analysis performed in SPSS v27, stratified by age group, gender, and orofacial site.

## Results

- Overall: 3,495/16,572 (21.08%) had an OML; 2,314/16,572 (13.96%) had a normal mucosal variant; 64.94% had normal mucosa with neither.
- Normal variants were more common in males (56.08%) than females (43.91%). Frictional keratosis predominated among females (38.52% of their normal variants); linea alba predominated among males (34.77%).
- Fordyce's granules: overall prevalence 8.84%; most prominent normal variant in the 11-20y age group (31.76% of that group's normal variants). Cross-study comparison shows this figure sits mid-range versus other populations reported (1.2%-82.8%).
- Linea alba: most prevalent among the 41-50y age group (37.21% of that group's normal variants); overall the second most common normal variant site-wise, second only to frictional keratosis.
- Leukoedema: prevalence 0.72%, comparatively low versus several other reported populations (up to 15.72%).
- Location: 80.10% of all normal variants were on the buccal mucosa, followed distantly by labial mucosa (9.14%), tongue (4.35%), vestibule (2.28%), and gingiva (3.76%).
