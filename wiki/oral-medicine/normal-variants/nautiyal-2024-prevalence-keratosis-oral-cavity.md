## One-line Summary

Retrospective clinical study (144,056 South Indian dental patients screened, 2019-2023) found 5,613 (3.89%) had a benign keratotic white lesion — frictional keratosis (71.65%), tobacco pouch keratosis (27.38%), or morsicatio buccarum/cheek biting (0.9%) — all significantly more common in men, reinforcing that these are treatable-by-habit-cessation entities distinct from potentially malignant white lesions.

## 한줄요약

## Summary

This four-year single-center retrospective study screened 144,056 dental patients in Chennai, India, and identified 5,613 (3.89%) with one of three innocuous keratotic white lesions: frictional keratosis (from mechanical trauma by a sharp tooth or appliance), tobacco pouch keratosis (from chronic smokeless-tobacco/quid contact), or morsicatio buccarum (from chronic cheek biting, a lesion that the authors describe as a continuation of linea alba along the occlusal biting plane). Frictional keratosis was overwhelmingly the most common (71.65% of cases, mean age 26.3y), followed by tobacco pouch keratosis (27.38%, mean age 44.5y, reflecting cumulative habit duration), with morsicatio buccarum the rarest at only 0.9% (mean age 29.3y). All three lesions were significantly more common in men (chi-square p=0.01), a finding that contrasted with some prior smaller studies reporting female predominance for individual lesions. Diagnosis in this study relied on clinical exam plus favorable response to conservative management (irritant/habit removal) rather than routine biopsy — supporting the clinical practice of deferring biopsy for classic presentations and reserving it for lesions that fail to resolve.

## Key Contributions

- Directly compares three distinct benign keratotic entities in the same large cohort, clarifying their relative prevalence, age profile, and gender distribution.
- Frames morsicatio buccarum as mechanistically continuous with linea alba (chronic occlusal-plane friction from cheek biting) rather than a separate pathological process.
- Supports a "treat the cause first, biopsy only if unresponsive" clinical algorithm for these lesions.

## Methodology

Retrospective review of DIAS (Digital Information and Archiving Software) records, Saveetha Dental College, Chennai, June 2019-June 2023 (institutional ethics approval IHEC/SDC/UG-2157/23/OMED/303). Inclusion required an identifiable etiologic factor (sharp-tooth/appliance trauma for frictional keratosis; SLT/quid-use history for tobacco pouch keratosis; cheek-biting history for morsicatio buccarum) and diagnosis was confirmed by response to conservative management; presence of a co-existing oral potentially malignant disorder was an exclusion criterion for all three groups. Statistical analysis (SPSS v26.0) used chi-square testing with post hoc comparisons and Shapiro-Wilk normality testing.

## Results

- 5,613/144,056 screened patients (3.89%) had one of the three lesions; overall mean age 37.28y (range 18-78y), approximately normally distributed.
- Frictional keratosis: n=4,026 (71.65%), mean age 26.28y, 72.5% male.
- Tobacco pouch keratosis: n=1,537 (27.38%), mean age 44.53y, 93.55% male.
- Morsicatio buccarum: n=54 (0.9%), mean age 29.34y, 75.92% male.
- Gender association significant across all three lesion categories (chi-square p=0.01; linear-by-linear trend p=0.034).
- Discussion notes population-dependent discrepancies versus prior literature (e.g., Arora et al. reported frictional keratosis more common in women; Majorana et al.'s pediatric series found morsicatio buccarum slightly more common in girls) — cautioning against assuming a universal gender pattern.
