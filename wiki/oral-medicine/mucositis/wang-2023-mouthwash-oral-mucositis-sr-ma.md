## Three-line Summary

(1) This was a systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis of 13 randomized controlled trials (570 patients) comparing 10 mouthwash types for treating established oral mucositis in cancer patients receiving radiotherapy or chemotherapy.

(2) After one week, GM-CSF mouthwash was ranked most effective (54% probability), with povidone-iodine being superior to chlorhexidine (WMD, 2.63; 95% CI, 0.20-5.01). After three weeks, natural drugs (43% probability) and vitamin E (24%) were ranked highest; vitamin E (WMD, -0.94; 95% CI, -1.03 to -0.85), natural drugs (WMD, -0.93; 95% CI, -1.46 to -0.40), and phenytoin (WMD, -0.38; 95% CI, -0.59 to -0.17) all outperformed placebo.

(3) The authors conclude that GM-CSF is the most effective overall, but povidone-iodine and sodium bicarbonate offer better cost-effectiveness, while natural drugs provide a safe, well-tolerated alternative with delayed but sustained benefit, suitable for long-course chemoradiotherapy.

## Summary

This systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of 10 mouthwash types for treating established oral mucositis (OM) in cancer patients undergoing radiotherapy or chemotherapy. The study included 13 randomized controlled trials (n=570) and assessed OM severity improvement using the WHO grading system at 1, 2, and 3 weeks. Pairwise meta-analyses showed that vitamin E, natural drugs (e.g., Achillea, Althaea, curcumin, royal jelly), and phenytoin were significantly more effective than placebo at 3 weeks. Network meta-analysis revealed that povidone-iodine was superior to chlorhexidine at 1 week (WMD, 2.63; 95% CI, 0.20-5.01). Rank probability analysis identified GM-CSF as the most effective at 1 week (54% probability), sodium bicarbonate at 2 weeks (24%), and natural drugs at 3 weeks (43%). The authors concluded that GM-CSF provides the best overall efficacy, but povidone-iodine and sodium bicarbonate are more cost-effective alternatives. Natural drugs offer a safe, well-tolerated option with delayed but sustained benefits, making them particularly suitable for patients with mild OM or those undergoing long-course chemoradiotherapy.

## Key Contributions

- First network meta-analysis ranking 10 mouthwashes for treating established chemoradiotherapy-induced OM.
- GM-CSF ranked best at 1 week (54% probability); natural drugs ranked best at 3 weeks (43%).
- Povidone-iodine outperformed chlorhexidine at 1 week (WMD, 2.63; 95% CI, 0.20-5.01).
- Gabapentin was no better than placebo, and may be cytotoxic.
- Provides evidence-based hierarchy to guide clinical decision-making and future research.

## Methodology

This was a systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis following PRISMA guidelines. Four databases (PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science) were searched from inception to April 2022. Inclusion criteria were RCTs comparing ≥2 mouthwashes for treating OM (WHO grades) in cancer patients receiving radiotherapy/chemotherapy, with 7–28 days follow-up. Prophylactic studies and non-RCTs were excluded. Two independent investigators extracted data and assessed risk of bias using the Cochrane ROB tool. Pairwise meta-analyses used random-effects models (WMD, 95% CI, I²). Bayesian network meta-analysis used Markov chain Monte Carlo methods (ADDIS v1.15) with 100,000 iterations; ranking probabilities were calculated. Inconsistency was assessed and found to be non-significant.
