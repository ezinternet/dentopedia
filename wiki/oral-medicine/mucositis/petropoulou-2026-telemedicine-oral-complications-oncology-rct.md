## Three-line Summary

Prospective RCT (n=105 randomized, 90 completed; breast/lung/colon cancer patients starting chemotherapy or combined therapy; Oncology Hospital of Kifissia, Athens, Greece, March 2022–November 2023) comparing a structured telemedicine/e-health teledentistry monitoring protocol against standard oncology-unit oral care advice, with assessments at baseline (T0), mid-treatment (T1), and end of treatment (T2).

The telemedicine group had significantly lower rates of mucosal lesions (OR 7.59 at T2, p<0.001), gingival bleeding (OR 35.25, p<0.001), gingivitis (OR 38.83, p<0.001), dental caries (OR 26.11, p<0.001), and moderate/advanced periodontitis (OR 8.25, p<0.001) at end of treatment, plus higher toothbrushing frequency (OR 5.52, p<0.001); no group differed at baseline and TMJ disorders showed no significant difference at any timepoint.

Structured teledentistry monitoring (91.8% adherence, 100% referral follow-through) is a low-cost, high-effectiveness model for oncology oral-complication prevention/early detection, but the design confounds "more structured follow-up" with "digital monitoring" per se, blinding was infeasible, and differential mortality (14 control vs 1 intervention deaths) reduced the final sample without registry pre-registration.

## 세줄요약

전향적 무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) (등록 105명, 완료 90명; 유방암/폐암/대장암 항암화학요법 또는 병용요법 시작 환자; 그리스 아테네 Kifissia 종양병원, 2022년 3월–2023년 11월) — 원격의료/이헬스(e-health) 구조화 원격치과(teledentistry) 모니터링 프로토콜 vs 표준 종양내과 구강관리를 기준선(T0)·치료중기(T1)·치료종료(T2)에서 비교.

원격의료군은 치료종료 시점에서 점막병변(mucosal lesion, OR 7.59, p<0.001), 치은출혈(gingival bleeding, OR 35.25, p<0.001), 치은염(gingivitis, OR 38.83, p<0.001), 치아우식(dental caries, OR 26.11, p<0.001), 중등도-중증 치주염(periodontitis, OR 8.25, p<0.001) 발생률이 유의하게 낮았고 칫솔질 빈도(OR 5.52, p<0.001)는 유의하게 높았음; 기준선에서는 군간 차이 없었고 턱관절장애(TMJ disorder)는 전 시점에서 유의차 없음.

구조화 원격치과 모니터링(순응도 91.8%, 의뢰 후속조치 100%)은 종양환자 구강합병증 예방·조기발견에 저비용·고효과적 모델이나, 설계상 "더 구조화된 추적관찰" 효과와 "디지털 모니터링 자체" 효과가 혼재되어 있고 눈가림이 불가능했으며 대조군에서 사망(14명)이 개입군(1명)보다 많아 최종표본이 줄었고 임상시험 사전등록도 없었음.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.3389/fonc.2026.1774001](https://doi.org/10.3389/fonc.2026.1774001)), this prospective RCT (Petropoulou et al., 2026, Frontiers in Oncology) tested whether a structured telemedicine/e-health teledentistry monitoring protocol reduces oral complications in oncology patients undergoing antineoplastic therapy, compared with standard oncology-unit oral-care advice. Conducted at the Oncology Hospital of Kifissia (Athens, Greece) from March 2022 to November 2023, it enrolled 105 treatment-naive patients with solid tumors of the breast, lung, or colon starting chemotherapy or combination therapy, randomized to a control arm (standard advice to see a dentist, n=56) or an intervention arm (structured hygiene education + scheduled synchronous/asynchronous teleconsultations via a digital platform + MASCC/ISOO-guided management of reported symptoms + in-person referral when indicated, n=49). Ninety patients (48 intervention, 42 control) completed follow-up to T2 after 15 deaths (14 control, 1 intervention) during treatment.

The telemedicine arm showed significantly lower rates of mucosal lesions, gingival bleeding, gingivitis, dental caries, and periodontitis at mid-treatment and end-of-treatment relative to the control arm, alongside a significantly higher toothbrushing frequency — with no significant between-group differences at baseline (confirming randomization balance) and no significant effect on TMJ disorders at any timepoint. Adherence to the digital monitoring protocol was high (91.8% of scheduled remote communications completed), and all patients referred for in-person dental evaluation via remote symptom reporting attended (100%).

The scope of this trial is broader than xerostomia/hyposalivation specifically — it is a general oral-complication monitoring-model study covering mucositis-adjacent mucosal lesions, periodontal disease, and caries, with xerostomia named only among the background list of oncology-therapy oral complications the authors motivate the study with, not a dedicated outcome analyzed separately in the results tables presented here. Clinically, the paper supports embedding a dedicated dental specialist and a structured telehealth monitoring layer into oncology care as a low-cost, scalable supportive-care model — though the intervention bundles increased contact frequency with digital-platform monitoring, so the specific contribution of "telemedicine" versus "more attention/follow-up" cannot be isolated from this design, and blinding was not feasible given the nature of the intervention.

## Key Contributions

- First prospective RCT conducted in Greece to comprehensively evaluate oral health in oncology patients with solid tumors (breast, colorectal, lung) during active antineoplastic treatment, per the authors.
- Demonstrates that structured teledentistry monitoring (scheduled tele-consultations + digital symptom reporting + guideline-based triage/referral) significantly reduces incident mucosal lesions, gingival bleeding, gingivitis, dental caries, and periodontitis compared with usual oncology-unit oral-care advice.
- Reports strong real-world adherence to a digital monitoring protocol in an oncology population (91.8% of scheduled remote communications completed) and complete follow-through on triggered dental referrals (100%).
- Frames structured teledentistry as a low-cost, low-infrastructure-burden model for integrating oral care into oncology treatment pathways, relevant to national cancer-control policy (cited: Europe's Beating Cancer Plan, 2022).
- Provides longitudinal (T0/T1/T2) GEE-modeled odds ratios across five oral-health outcomes plus a behavioral outcome (toothbrushing frequency), giving one of the more granular effect-size pictures available for a supportive-care/monitoring-model (rather than a pharmacologic-prevention) intervention in this space.

## Methodology

- Prospective, randomized, interventional 2-arm trial; independent-statistician computer-generated randomization with concealed allocation until baseline data confirmed; not prospectively registered in a public trial registry.
- 105 patients (breast/lung/colon solid tumors, treatment-naive, ECOG 0–2) randomized to control (standard oncology-unit advice, n=56) vs intervention (structured telemedicine/e-health teledentistry protocol, n=49); assessed at T0 (baseline), T1 (mid-treatment), T2 (end of treatment), timing individualized to each patient's oncology regimen.
- Intervention: baseline oral-hygiene education + e-health platform training; scheduled synchronous/asynchronous teleconsultations between clinic visits; MASCC/ISOO-guideline-based management of reported symptoms; referral to hospital Dental Department or private dentist when clinically indicated.
- Outcomes: periodontal exam (PPD, CAL, BOP, Gingival Index, Plaque Index) by a single calibrated periodontist; Oral Mucosa Rating Scale (OMRS) + CTCAE v5.0 grading for mucosal alterations; patient-reported xerostomia; categorical toothbrushing frequency; caries by clinical/radiographic exam. QoL instruments (EORTC QLQ-C30, QLQ-OH15, HADS) were collected but analyzed under a separate doctoral protocol, not reported here.
- Statistics: GEE models (group × time interaction) reported as OR with 95% CI, SPSS v21.00, two-sided p<0.05; complete-case sensitivity analysis performed. Blinding of participants/examiner not feasible given the nature of the intervention.

## Results

- **Enrollment/attrition**: 105 randomized (49 intervention/56 control) → 90 analyzed at T2 (15 deaths during treatment: 14 control, 1 intervention).
- **Baseline homogeneity**: confirmed across demographic/clinical variables (all p>0.05, borderline p=0.058 for toothbrushing frequency).
- **Mucosal lesions** (control vs telemedicine): baseline OR=1.01 p=0.977 (NS); mid-therapy OR=2.20 p=0.053 (NS trend); end of therapy **OR=7.59 (95% CI 2.98–19.37), p<0.001**.
- **Gingival bleeding**: baseline OR=0.93 p=0.853 (NS); mid-therapy **OR=19.20, p<0.001**; end of therapy **OR=35.25 (95% CI 4.44–280.15), p<0.001**.
- **Gingivitis**: baseline OR=0.93 p=0.853 (NS); mid-therapy **OR=20.92, p<0.001**; end of therapy **OR=38.83, p<0.001**.
- **Dental caries**: baseline OR=0.93 p=0.853 (NS); mid-therapy **OR=3.12, p=0.030**; end of therapy **OR=26.11, p<0.001**.
- **Moderate/advanced periodontitis**: baseline OR=1.63 p=0.224 (NS); mid-therapy **OR=3.63, p<0.001**; end of therapy **OR=8.25 (95% CI 2.50–27.18), p<0.001**.
- **TMJ disorders**: no significant difference at any timepoint (all p>0.05, p=0.120 at end of therapy).
- **Toothbrushing (2–3×/day)**, telemedicine vs control: baseline OR=2.44 p=0.058 (borderline NS); mid-therapy **OR=3.60, p=0.006**; end of therapy **OR=5.52 (95% CI 1.96–15.51), p<0.001**.
- **Adherence**: intervention group 91.8% of scheduled remote communications completed; 28.6% reported symptoms remotely; 12.2% referred for dental evaluation, 100% of those attended. Control group only 21.4% self-reported visiting a dentist during the study.
- **Sensitivity**: complete-case analysis (n=90 with full follow-up) consistent with primary GEE results.
