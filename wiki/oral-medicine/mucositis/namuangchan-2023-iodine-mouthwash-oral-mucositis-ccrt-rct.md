## Three-line Summary

This was a single-center, prospective, double-blind, randomized controlled trial evaluating the efficacy of an in-house iodine solution (IS) mouthwash versus normal saline solution (NSS) for prophylaxis of concurrent chemoradiation (CCRT)-induced oral mucositis (OM) in 20 head and neck cancer patients (1:1 randomization) conducted at Khon Kaen University, Thailand, from January to December 2019. The primary outcome was weekly Oral Mucositis Assessment Scale (OMAS) score, assessed from baseline through 4 weeks post-CCRT.

No statistically significant differences were found between the IS and NSS groups in mean weekly OMAS (overall mean difference -0.40, 95% CI -3.95 to 3.15, p=0.825; p>0.999 for each week), pain score, or impact on swallowing score. The mean onset to OM (all grades) was 12.70 vs 11.80 days (p=0.704), and median duration of OM was 72.00 vs 88.00 days (p=0.365). Severe OM (WHO grade 3-4) occurred in 2 patients in the IS group and 1 in the NSS group.

The study failed to demonstrate statistically significant superiority of IS mouthwash over NSS for preventing CCRT-induced OM, likely due to small sample size and possibly insufficient iodine concentration. Larger-scale studies are warranted to confirm or refute any potential benefit.

## Summary

This randomized controlled trial investigated whether an in-house iodine solution (IS) mouthwash, containing 0.025% free iodine, could prevent oral mucositis (OM) in head and neck cancer patients undergoing concurrent chemoradiation (CCRT), compared to normal saline (NSS) placebo. Twenty patients were randomized 1:1 and instructed to gargle three times daily from the start to the end of CCRT. The primary outcome was weekly OMAS score, with secondary outcomes including pain, swallowing impact, OM onset/duration, severe OM incidence, treatment breaks, and secondary infections. Results showed no significant differences between groups across all measured endpoints. Weekly OMAS scores yielded p > 0.999 every week. Severe OM occurred in three patients total, with no clear pattern. The IS group had no secondary oral infections, whereas one NSS patient developed candidiasis. The authors concluded that this preliminary study did not demonstrate efficacy of IS mouthwash for OM prophylaxis, attributing the null finding primarily to small sample size and possibly the low iodine concentration, and called for larger studies with higher concentrations.

## Key Contributions

- First double-blind RCT of an in-house iodine solution for CCRT-induced OM prophylaxis in head and neck cancer.
- No significant benefit of IS over NSS in OMAS, pain, or swallowing scores (p > 0.999 for weekly comparisons).
- No significant differences in OM onset, duration, or severe OM incidence between groups.
- No secondary oral infections in the IS group, suggesting potential antiseptic effect despite lack of efficacy on OM.
- Highlights critical issues: small sample size (n=20) and low free iodine concentration (0.025%) may have contributed to negative results.

## Methodology

This single-center, prospective, double-blind, randomized placebo-controlled trial was conducted at Khon Kaen University, Thailand, from January to December 2019. Patients with head and neck cancer (aged 18-70, ECOG ≤2, receiving platinum-based CCRT with oral cavity irradiation >50 Gy) were eligible. Twenty patients were randomized 1:1 using stratified simple random sampling (by primary site and stage) with opaque envelope allocation concealment. The intervention group received 30 mL of in-house iodine solution (free iodine 0.025%, colored brown) and the control group received 30 mL of normal saline solution (colored light brown to mimic IS). Patients gargled for 30 seconds three times daily after meals from day 1 of CCRT until completion. All received standard oral hygiene care per MASCC/ISOO guidelines. Oral examinations were performed at baseline, weekly during CCRT, at completion, and 4 weeks post-CCRT using OMAS and WHO grading. The primary outcome was OMAS; secondary outcomes included pain score (0-10), swallowing impact (0-10), incidence/severity/onset/duration of OM, analgesic use, treatment breaks, and secondary infections. Statistical analyses used GEE models with Bonferroni correction for repeated measures, along with t-tests, Mann-Whitney U, and Fisher's exact tests. Intention-to-treat analysis was performed for all 20 enrolled patients, though 5 withdrew and 1 was lost to follow-up.
