## One-line Summary

Narrative review (JADA, Wong/UCLA group) organizing saliva-based diagnostics into three pillars — salivaomics, saliva exosomics, saliva liquid biopsy — and introducing the EFIRM electrochemical platform that detects lung-cancer EGFR ctDNA and SARS-CoV-2 in saliva without extraction or amplification.

## 한줄요약

## Summary

Blood-based biomarker testing is uncomfortable and not universally feasible; saliva offers a less invasive, more reproducible alternative, and presymptomatic saliva testing of at-risk patients could improve outcomes for cancers (e.g. head and neck) that are otherwise diagnosed late. The central bottleneck is mechanistic: how blood-borne biomarkers migrate to saliva is still poorly understood, though **20–30% of the salivary proteome overlaps with the plasma proteome**, and NIH consortia (RADx, ERCC) have accelerated the field. The review organizes the discipline into three pillars — **salivaomics** (high-throughput DNA/RNA/protein/metabolite/microbiome profiling), **saliva exosomics** (analysis of 30–100 nm exosome cargos whose lipid bilayer protects contents from degradation), and **saliva liquid biopsy** (detection of ctDNA/CTC/EV). It introduces **EFIRM (Electric Field–Induced Release and Measurement)**, an electrochemical sensor that detects <100 target molecules in ≈30 min from ~40–50 µL without extraction/amplification, now CLIA-certified for EGFR mutations (plasma and saliva) and SARS-CoV-2 IgG. A novel **ultrashort cell-free DNA (uscfDNA, 25–75 nt)** subtype is also introduced.

## Key Contributions

- **Three-pillar framework** for saliva diagnostics: salivaomics, exosomics, liquid biopsy.
- Identifies **systemic→saliva biomarker transport** as the key unsolved mechanism limiting clinical translation; reports **20–30% salivary/plasma proteome overlap**.
- Introduces **EFIRM** — reagentless, amplification-free electrochemical detection of ctDNA and viral targets in saliva.
- Documents **CLIA-certified** EFIRM assays: EGFR L858R / exon 19del / T790M (plasma + saliva); SARS-CoV-2 IgG S1 (saliva).
- Introduces **uscfDNA** (25–75 nt, modal 50 nt) as a distinct cfDNA subtype, higher in healthy than cancer patients.

## Methodology

Narrative expert synthesis (not a systematic review). Evidence spans omics reference catalogs (salivary proteome; exRNA Atlas — saliva = 2.5%, 256/9,892, of deposited RNA-seq), preclinical exosome-transport models, and EFIRM assay development. Sample handling requires protease + ribonuclease inhibitors and control for food, oral bacteria, and rest-vs-stimulated collection. EFIRM immobilizes a capture probe on a gold electrode via pyrrole electropolymerization; a cyclic square-wave field drives near-field mixing so only perfectly matched sequences hybridize, enabling single-nucleotide-variant detection in complex biofluids.

## Results

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Salivary/plasma proteome overlap | 20–30% |
| Saliva exRNA share of exRNA Atlas | 2.5% (256/9,892) |
| Exosome transport (preclinical) | Dominant-negative Rab11 abolished tumor mRNA in saliva (pancreatic xenograft); CD63-GFP vesicles + GAPDH recovered from lung-xenograft mouse saliva |
| Biological activity | Tumor-mouse saliva suppressed NK markers CD69/NKG2D in controls; reversed by Rab11 knockdown |
| Lung cancer (clinical) | Saliva EFIRM detected recurrence T790M ctDNA in **2/26** patients missed by ddPCR and NGS |
| SARS-CoV-2 EFIRM triplex | Antigen + neutralizing Ab + genomic RNA (50 µL); outperformed all EUA tests for all 3 biomarkers |
| uscfDNA | 25–75 nt (modal 50 nt); higher in healthy than cancer |
| EFIRM assay speed/volume | ≈30 min, 40–50 µL |
