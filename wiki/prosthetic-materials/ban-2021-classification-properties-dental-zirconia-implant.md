## Three-line Summary

Review (Materials 2021) presenting a 12-type classification of dental zirconia by yttria content, mono/polychromatic color, uniform/hybrid composition, and mono/multilayer structure — the most comprehensive taxonomy of dental zirconia available, covering both implant fixture and superstructure applications.

ATZ (alumina-toughened zirconia) and NanoZR are suitable for implant fixtures (high mechanical strength despite poor esthetics); yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ, 3Y→5Y) is used for superstructures; higher yttria = higher translucency + lower mechanical strength + lower LTD resistance; mirror-polished zirconia has least bacterial adhesion among dental ceramics.

Adequate zirconia selection requires matching yttria content (strength vs esthetics priority) to clinical region and restoration design; zirconia fixtures achieve osseointegration comparable to titanium after surface modification (sandblasting + acid etching).

## Summary

Ban 2021 provides the most comprehensive classification framework for dental zirconia, organizing the rapidly expanding landscape of zirconia types into a 12-category system. The four axes are: yttria content (3Y/4Y/5Y), monochromatic vs polychromatic color distribution, uniform vs hybrid composition, and monolayer vs multilayer architecture. Key clinical insights: ATZ composites (zirconia-alumina) are indicated for implant fixtures where maximum mechanical strength is required; yttria-stabilized zirconia provides sufficient esthetics for superstructures. The strength-translucency trade-off (more yttria = more translucent but weaker) has since been confirmed by multiple experimental studies (Kui 2023, Aboushelib 2026). The low-temperature degradation (aging/LTD) concern for zirconia is addressed: even 3Y zirconia has sufficient durability under oral conditions, and LTD risk decreases as yttria content increases. Mirror polishing of zirconia surfaces yields uniquely favorable biological properties: least bacterial adhesion and best soft tissue response among all dental ceramics.

## Key Contributions

- 12-type classification: systematic framework unifying diverse dental zirconia types
- ATZ/NanoZR identification as preferred fixture materials (strength > esthetics acceptable for subgingival use)
- Established that yttria content is the primary determinant of the strength-translucency trade-off
- Low-temperature degradation (LTD/aging) quantified by yttria content — concern is clinically manageable
- Mirror polishing: least bacterial adhesion + best soft tissue response — key argument for zirconia abutments
- Surface modification for fixtures: sandblasting + acid etching achieves osseointegration comparable to titanium

## Methodology

Literature review (narrative). Author: Aichi Gakuin University, Japan (Department of Dental Materials Science). Covered: phase transformation, fracture mechanics, low-temperature degradation, antagonist wear, bacterial adhesion, osseointegration, and surface treatment methods for both implant fixtures and superstructures.

## Results

**12-type classification framework:**
- Axis 1: Yttria content — 3Y / 4Y / 5Y
- Axis 2: Color — Monochromatic (uniform) / Polychromatic (gradual color layers)
- Axis 3: Composition — Uniform / Hybrid (gradient particle composition)
- Axis 4: Architecture — Monolayer / Multilayer (gradient translucency + strength profile)

**Material properties by yttria content:**
| Yttria | Flexural strength | Translucency | LTD resistance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3Y-TZP | Highest (~1,200 MPa) | Low | Good |
| 4Y-TZP | Medium (~800 MPa) | Medium | Moderate |
| 5Y-TZP | Lowest (~500 MPa) | High | Lower |

**Implant fixtures:**
- ATZ: highest mechanical strength, adequate for bite-force-bearing fixture applications
- Surface modification for osseointegration: roughening + surface activation + coating
- Clinical data: modified zirconia fixtures achieve osseointegration comparable to titanium

**Soft tissue/bacterial properties (mirror polished):**
- Zirconia: least bacterial adhesion among dental materials
- Better soft tissue adhesion vs other ceramics
