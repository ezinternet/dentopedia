## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (17 systematic reviews, 301 in vitro crown assessments) quantitatively synthesizing determinants of marginal gap in single crowns before cementation, using Welch ANOVA, Games-Howell post hoc, and robust-clustered multiple regression.

Mean marginal gap was 79.4 ± 22.8 µm (range 34.5–125.5 µm); crown type (F=0.37, P=.775), fabrication material (F=0.48, P=.784), and fabrication method (conventional 88.0 ± 35.0 µm vs CAD-CAM 80.3 ± 35.0 µm, F=1.00, P=.319) showed no significant effect, but the impression-replica measurement instrument significantly overvalued gaps versus microscopy (β=37.86, P<.001).

The contemporary acceptable in vitro marginal gap limit is confirmed at 120 µm; crown type/material/fabrication method can be treated as largely interchangeable for marginal fit, while impression-replica-derived gap values run systematically high compared with optical/microscopy methods.

## Summary

Dudley and Farook conducted an umbrella review of 17 systematic reviews (301 pooled in vitro crown assessments, from 82 screened records) evaluating what determines the marginal gap of single crowns before cementation. Using Welch ANOVA, Games-Howell post hoc testing, and multiple regression with robust clustered standard errors, they found that crown type, crown fabrication material, and fabrication method (conventional vs CAD-CAM) had no significant effect on marginal gap, while the measurement instrument used was the dominant source of reported variability — impression replica measurements significantly overvalued gaps relative to microscopy. The pooled mean marginal gap was 79.4 ± 22.8 µm, and the study reaffirms the historically accepted 120 µm clinical acceptability threshold as still contemporary. Clinically, this suggests that clinicians need not prioritize one crown material or fabrication workflow over another purely on marginal-fit grounds, but should interpret marginal gap values cautiously when they derive from impression-replica studies.

## Key Contributions

- First quantitative (not purely narrative) umbrella-level statistical synthesis of systematic reviews on in vitro single-crown marginal gap.
- Shows crown type, fabrication material, and conventional-vs-CAD-CAM fabrication method are statistically indistinguishable for marginal gap outcomes.
- Identifies measurement instrument (specifically impression replica vs microscopy) as a major confounder explaining cross-study variability, rather than material/technique differences.
- Reaffirms 120 µm as the contemporary evidence-based in vitro marginal gap acceptability threshold.

## Methodology

Umbrella review of systematic reviews of in vitro studies (PRISMA-guided), searched June 2025 across EBSCOHost, Scopus, PubMed, Web of Science, and Cochrane (no date restriction). 82 records screened → 38 assessed → 17 SRs included (301 in vitro crown assessments); 17 additional SRs excluded for inseparable in vivo/in vitro data. Quality appraised with the JBI 11-domain Critical Appraisal Checklist by 2 independent reviewers (mean score 82.7%, low risk). Statistical synthesis used Welch test/Games-Howell post hoc (crown type, material, tooth type, instrument) and multiple regression with robust clustered SE + Wald test (α=.05).

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Included SRs / assessments | 17 SRs, 301 in vitro crown assessments |
| Mean marginal gap | 79.4 ± 22.8 µm (range 34.5–125.5 µm, median 82.7 µm) |
| Crown type effect | Not significant (F=0.37, P=.775) |
