## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (7 cohort/prospective studies, PRISMA 2020, 694 patients / 663 screw-retained ceramic-veneered implant-supported fixed dental prostheses [ISFDPs] on 1246 implants, follow-up 3–12 years) evaluating survival, success, and biological complication rates.

Pooled survival rate 97% (ES 0.97, 95% CI 0.92–1.09, fixed-effects, I²=0.0%); pooled success rate 82% (ES 0.82, 95% CI 0.74–0.90, random-effects, I²=55.3%); pooled complication rate 13% (ES 0.13, 95% CI 0.08–0.18, fixed-effects, I²=5.7%); most common complications were ceramic chipping, screw loosening, and screw/prosthesis fracture.

Screw-retained ceramic-veneered ISFDPs are a predictable long-term treatment option with high survival and manageable complication burden, but only 7 studies (mostly retrospective) were poolable, success-rate heterogeneity (I²=55.3%) reflects inconsistent "success" definitions across studies, and no PROSPERO registration limits methodological transparency.

## 세줄요약

체계적 고찰 + 메타분석 (Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis, SR+MA) (코호트/전향 연구 7편, PRISMA 2020, 환자 694명 / 나사고정 세라믹-비니어드 임플란트 지지 고정성 보철물[ISFDP] 663개, 임플란트 1246개, 추적 3–12년) — 생존율·성공율·생물학적 합병증률 평가.

풀링 생존율(survival rate) 97% (ES 0.97, 95% CI 0.92–1.09, 고정효과모형, I²=0.0%); 성공율(success rate) 82% (ES 0.82, 95% CI 0.74–0.90, 랜덤효과모형, I²=55.3%); 합병증률(complication rate) 13% (ES 0.13, 95% CI 0.08–0.18, 고정효과모형, I²=5.7%); 가장 흔한 합병증은 세라믹 치핑(chipping), 나사 풀림(screw loosening), 나사·보철물 파절.

나사고정 세라믹-비니어드 ISFDP는 높은 생존율과 관리 가능한 합병증 부담을 지닌 예측 가능한 장기 치료옵션이나, 풀링 가능했던 연구는 7편(대부분 후향적)에 불과하고, 성공율 이질성(I²=55.3%)은 연구간 "성공" 정의 불일치를 반영하며, PROSPERO 사전등록 부재로 방법론적 투명성이 제한된다.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, 27-item checklist; not PROSPERO-registered) evaluated the survival rate, success rate, and biological complication rate of **screw-retained ceramic-veneered** implant-supported fixed dental prostheses (ISFDPs). Databases searched (to January 2025): Cochrane, Embase, MEDLINE (PubMed/Ovid), supplemented by reference checking and Google Scholar; English-language articles only. Two independent blinded reviewers performed screening and Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) risk-of-bias assessment, with a third reviewer resolving disagreements.

Of 474 records identified, 7 studies (694 patients, 663 ISFDPs on 1246 implants, follow-up 3–12 years) were included after PRISMA screening. Five studies were rated low risk of bias, two moderate, none high. Pooled outcomes: survival 97% (fixed-effects, no heterogeneity), success 82% (random-effects, moderate heterogeneity I²=55.3%), and complication 13% (fixed-effects, low heterogeneity I²=5.7%). Sensitivity analysis excluding the two moderate-risk studies did not meaningfully change the pooled estimates. The most frequently reported complications were ceramic chipping, screw loosening, screw fracture, prosthesis fracture, proximal contact loss, and loss of implant access hole sealing material — predominantly technical/mechanical rather than biological in nature, despite the paper's title emphasis on "biological complications."

The authors position this SR+MA as more focused than prior implant-FDP meta-analyses (e.g., Pozzi et al., Pjetursson et al.) that pooled heterogeneous retention types (screw + cement) and material combinations together; by isolating the screw-retained ceramic-veneered subgroup, this study aims to reduce clinical/methodological heterogeneity and provide a more precise estimate for this specific prosthetic design.

## Key Contributions

- Focused (retention-type-controlled) pooled survival/success/complication estimates specific to screw-retained ceramic-veneered ISFDPs, distinct from prior SR+MAs pooling mixed retention and material types.
- Explains the complication mechanism: veneering ceramic chipping in bilayered zirconia frameworks is attributed to CTE (coefficient of thermal expansion) mismatch between zirconia core and veneering ceramic plus residual cooling-phase tensile stress, worsened by bruxism/occlusal overload.
- Contrasts fabrication predictability: veneered zirconia depends on manual layering (technician-skill-dependent variability) vs. monolithic zirconia's standardized digital workflow.
- Sensitivity analysis (excluding 2 moderate-risk-of-bias studies) supports robustness of the survival/complication pooled estimates.

## Methodology

- PICO: Population = patients missing >2 teeth (upper/lower/both jaws); Intervention = screw-retained ceramic-veneered ISFDPs; Comparison = not applicable; Outcome = survival, success, complication rates.
- Inclusion limited to RCTs or cohort studies; exclusion of other designs.
- Risk of bias: Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (0–9; high 0–3, moderate 4–6, low 7–9). Result: 5 low-risk, 2 moderate-risk, 0 high-risk.
- Statistical model chosen by heterogeneity: fixed-effects inverse-variance for survival and complication rate (I²=0.0% and I²=5.7% respectively); random-effects DerSimonian–Laird for success rate (I²=55.3%, statistically significant heterogeneity, p=0.04). Stata 17; significance threshold p<0.05.
- PRISMA flow: 474 identified → 373 title/abstract screened (316 excluded) → 57 full-text assessed → 7 included (50 excluded: 8 incomplete data, 5 not matching intervention, 34 no extractable survival/complication data, 3 without separable data).

## Results

| Outcome | Pooled estimate (ES) | 95% CI | Model | Heterogeneity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Survival rate | 0.97 (97%) | 0.92–1.09 | Fixed-effects | I²=0.0%, Q=3.41, p=0.7565 |
| Success rate | 0.82 (82%) | 0.74–0.90 | Random-effects (DerSimonian–Laird) | I²=55.3%, τ²=0.0058, Q=13.42, p=0.04 |
| Complication rate | 0.13 (13%) | 0.08–0.18 | Fixed-effects | I²=5.7%, Q=6.36, p=0.3839 |

- Study pool: 694 patients (369F/325M), mean age 56.58y; 663 ISFDPs on 1246 implants; follow-up 3–12 years across 7 studies (Pontevedra 2024 & 2022, Zhang 2024, Solá-Ruiz 2022, De Angelis 2021, Diéguez-Pereira 2020, Wittneben 2014).
- Most frequent complications: ceramic chipping, screw loosening, screw fracture, prosthesis fracture, proximal contact loss, loss of access-hole sealing material.
- Sensitivity analysis excluding the 2 moderate-risk-of-bias studies: pooled survival and complication rates remained stable.
- No statistically significant survival difference is implied between screw- and cement-retained implant reconstructions per cited prior literature, but screw retention offers retrievability and reduced cement-related biological complication risk (author interpretation, not directly tested in this SR+MA's own pooled data).
