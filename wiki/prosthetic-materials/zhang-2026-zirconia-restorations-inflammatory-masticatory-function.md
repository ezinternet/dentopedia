## Three-line Summary

Single-center **retrospective, propensity-score-matched observational cohort** (196 patients treated 2023-01 to 2024-01, matched to 79 zirconia all-ceramic vs 79 porcelain-fused-to-metal full crowns; 3-month follow-up) — not an RCT — measuring serum IL-6, TNF-α, CRP, masticatory efficiency, chewing VAS, gingival status and 3-month "clinical success".

All three inflammatory markers fell further in the zirconia group at 3 months (IL-6 7.2 ± 2.0 vs 9.8 ± 2.5 pg/mL; TNF-α 11.5 ± 2.7 vs 14.3 ± 3.0 pg/mL; CRP 3.5 ± 1.0 vs 4.8 ± 1.3 mg/L; all P < .001), masticatory efficiency reached 78.6 ± 9.2% vs 70.2 ± 8.9% and chewing VAS 7.8 ± 1.0 vs 6.9 ± 1.1 (time×group P < .001), with an indirect effect of −0.37 (95% CI −0.58 to −0.19) on ΔCRP running through gingival improvement.

The effect sizes are far larger than a single crown can plausibly produce, material is confounded with digital-vs-conventional workflow and cement, and no multiplicity correction was applied — the material→gingival health→systemic inflammation pathway is a hypothesis, not an established biological advantage, and the 3-month "success" figures must not be read as survival data.

## Summary

A single-center observational study from Huanggang Central Hospital (Hubei, China) that asks whether zirconia all-ceramic crowns differ from porcelain-fused-to-metal (PFM) crowns not only in local gingival response but in **serum inflammatory markers and chewing function**. Of 196 patients treated between January 2023 and January 2024 (102 zirconia, 94 PFM), 79 per arm were retained after 1:1 propensity score matching; baseline age (~43 years), sex, comorbidity, smoking, bruxism, restored-tooth number/position, reason for restoration, pulp status and periodontal condition were reported as balanced (all P > .05). Serum IL-6, TNF-α and CRP were measured by ELISA at baseline, 6 weeks and 3 months, alongside objective masticatory efficiency (color-mixing/sieving method) and patient-reported chewing VAS (0–10). Every outcome moved in favour of zirconia: larger biomarker reductions, higher masticatory efficiency (78.6 ± 9.2% vs 70.2 ± 8.9%), higher chewing VAS (7.8 ± 1.0 vs 6.9 ± 1.1), fewer gingival bleeding events (7.6% vs 17.7%) and higher 3-month clinical success (93.7% vs 83.5%). A mediation analysis attributed roughly 30% of the CRP effect to gingival improvement (indirect β = −0.37).

**How much this should move practice: very little.** This is a retrospective, non-randomized comparison in which the two arms also differ in impression technique, fabrication route and cement, with outcomes measured at a single 3-month horizon and no multiplicity control. The size of the reported systemic effects is the main red flag: a single crown reducing serum IL-6 by 5.4 pg/mL and CRP by 3.3 mg/L, and lifting masticatory efficiency from ~52% to ~79% in three months, exceeds what the intervention can mechanistically account for. Its value to the wiki is as the **only held data point linking crown material to masticatory function and systemic inflammatory markers** — a hypothesis generator for a question no one else has measured, not evidence for choosing zirconia on biological grounds.

## Key Contributions

- **Masticatory function as an outcome of a crown-material comparison** — objective efficiency (sieving method) plus patient-reported VAS, at baseline, 6 weeks and 3 months. This is the axis the survival/chipping literature we hold does not cover.
- **Serum-level (not only local) inflammation readout**: IL-6, TNF-α, CRP at three timepoints, with responder thresholds (IL-6 ↓≥30%, CRP ↓≥1.0 mg/L) and effect estimates (RR/OR with 95% CI).
- **Explicit mechanistic hypothesis** — material → gingival health → systemic inflammation — tested by bootstrap mediation (5000 resamples): total effect −1.25, direct −0.88, indirect via Δ gingival index −0.37 (all P < .001).
- **Chewing VAS responder rate** of 73.4% vs 54.4% (OR 2.30, 95% CI 1.22–4.33, P = .010).
- Reports a lean adverse-event set (gingival bleeding, de-bonding, chipping) plus composite 3-month clinical success.

## Methodology

- **Design**: observational cohort, 1:1 propensity score matching, **no randomization** (the paper states "this retrospective study" in §2.3; group assignment at treatment and fixed blood-draw intervals are described as if prospective — design reporting is internally inconsistent).
- **Sample**: 196 patients (zirconia 102, PFM 94) → **79 vs 79** after matching; all treatment by 3 attending prosthodontists on one team.
- **Inclusion**: ≥18 y; full-crown indication (caries, fracture, structure loss, esthetics); preserved root, stable periodontium, basically normal occlusion; complete data, follow-up possible.
- **Exclusion**: uncontrolled diabetes, hematologic/severe cardiovascular/immune disease, radio- or chemotherapy; severe periodontitis, mobility III, severe bone loss, periapical lesions, severe occlusal disorder, TMD; long-term heavy smoking, alcohol abuse, severe bruxism; pregnancy/lactation; mucosal disease or maxillofacial tumor; material allergy; psychiatric impairment or lost follow-up.
- **Zirconia arm**: intraoral scan → CAD/CAM zirconia coping → **veneering and sintering** → try-in with marginal/occlusal adjustment → resin-modified glass ionomer cement.
- **PFM arm**: silicone rubber impression → cast metal framework + layered porcelain → try-in → cementation (cement not specified).
- **Outcomes**: serum IL-6/TNF-α/CRP by ELISA (baseline, 6 wk, 3 mo); masticatory efficiency by color-mixing/sieving (% passing sieves); chewing VAS 0–10; gingival index/symptoms; adverse events; composite clinical success.
- **Bias control**: two independent investigators blinded to crown material extracted masticatory/VAS data (ICC = 0.89). Clinicians and patients unblinded.
- **Statistics**: t test, χ²/Fisher, repeated-measures ANOVA (time × group, Greenhouse–Geisser) with LMM (random intercept) as robustness check, RR/OR with 95% CI, bootstrap mediation; **no multiple-comparison adjustment** (author-stated); post hoc power for CRP MD 1.3 mg/L, SD 1.2 → 1−β = 0.93 at n = 158.

## Limitations and Critical Appraisal

**This is an observational propensity-matched single-center study, not an RCT.** It cannot establish that zirconia causes lower inflammation or better chewing; the design only shows that patients who received zirconia did better on these measures in this one hospital.

- **No randomization / residual confounding.** Material was chosen per patient. PSM handles measured covariates only, and the matching algorithm, caliper, ratio and use of exact matching are unreported. Matching retained 79/102 zirconia and 79/94 PFM patients, so ~20% of each arm dropped out of the analysis — selection on the matched subset is itself a bias source.
- **Material is bundled with technique.** Zirconia crowns were digitally scanned, CAD/CAM milled and cemented with resin-modified glass ionomer; PFM crowns came from silicone impressions, conventional casting and an unspecified cement. The "material effect" therefore includes workflow, cement and possibly preparation design — it cannot be attributed to zirconia per se.
- **Effect sizes exceed plausibility.** A single full crown (with 28/79 patients in the zirconia arm receiving an anterior restoration, which contributes little to chewing) lowering serum IL-6 by 5.4 pg/mL and CRP by 3.3 mg/L, and raising masticatory efficiency from 52% to 79% within 12 weeks, is far beyond the intervention's mechanistic reach. Baseline CRP 6.8 mg/L and IL-6 12.6 pg/mL describe a systemically inflamed population in which regression to the mean, healing of the treated tooth and assay variation can produce large apparent changes; BMI, periodontal disease elsewhere, intercurrent infection and medication were not modelled.
- **Mediation is associational.** Mediator (Δ gingival index) and outcome (ΔCRP) are measured at the same visits; mediation requires correct temporal ordering and no unmeasured mediator–outcome confounding. The −0.37 indirect effect is a decomposition of covariance, not a demonstrated mechanism.
- **Multiplicity.** Three biomarkers × three timepoints, two masticatory indices, three responder analyses, four event comparisons and a mediation model were tested with α = .05 and no correction (author-acknowledged). The P = .039 (success) and P = .047 (gingival bleeding) findings are fragile; the ANOVA time×group effects are the sturdier results.
- **Post hoc power is circular.** Power was computed from the observed CRP difference, which cannot validate the study's sensitivity to a clinically meaningful difference; sample size was simply the available patient pool.
- **3-month horizon.** No survival, no late chipping/framework fracture, no marginal bone or periodontal follow-up. "Clinical success" here is a 3-month composite of fit, occlusion and satisfaction — it must not be compared with 5-year crown survival figures such as 97.1% metal-ceramic vs 97.3% veneered zirconia.
- **Partial blinding only.** Data extractors were blinded (ICC 0.89) but patients and clinicians were not; VAS is a patient-reported outcome under an unblinded, cost- and appearance-salient comparison.
- **Generalizability and reporting.** Single center, one province, ~43-year-old mostly posterior-restoration cohort; no registration, no STROBE checklist, no ethics approval number, no ELISA kit, no test food/sieve specification, no PFM cement, no gingival index scale.
- **Text defect.** Table 1's periodontal-condition cell for the zirconia arm is garbled in the PMC full text ("Nov-68"), so the one baseline variable most relevant to the mediation hypothesis is unverifiable from the available record.
- **Veneered, not monolithic.** The zirconia crowns were veneered, so chipping risk (and the 1.3% vs 6.3% nonsignificant difference) concerns a layered design that current practice increasingly replaces with monolithic zirconia.
