## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (13 RCTs, n=454 children/1172 crowns; 5 clinical studies, n=810 children/2667 crowns) comparing 6 pediatric primary-tooth crown materials using Poisson regression to estimate pooled retention rates.

SSC had the highest 5-year retention (97.88%), followed by zirconia (97.09%), composite resin (92.18%), RMGIC (90.30%), compomer (88.90%), and strip crowns (83.48%); SSC was the only material with zero retentive complications in RCT data.

Zirconia showed the highest gingival inflammation (21.8% in clinical studies), composite resin the broadest technical/esthetic complication profile; SSC remains the gold standard for badly broken-down primary teeth.

## Summary

This SR+MA pooled 13 RCTs and 5 prospective/retrospective clinical studies (total >3800 crowns) to quantify retention rates and failure reasons for six pediatric primary-tooth crown materials/systems: stainless steel crown (SSC), zirconia, composite resin, resin-modified glass ionomer cement (RMGIC), compomer, and strip crowns. Using a fixed-effects Poisson regression model on cumulative crown-exposure time, the authors estimated 3-year and 5-year retention rates per material. SSC was the standout performer — highest pooled 5-year retention (97.88%) and the only material with zero reported retentive complications (chipping, fracture, decementation, or material loss) among RCTs — reaffirming its role as the long-standing gold-standard restoration for badly broken-down primary teeth. Zirconia matched SSC closely on retention (97.09%) but carried a distinct biological-complication signature: minimal secondary caries but the highest gingival inflammation rate among clinical-study data (21.8%), plus a significant Egger-test publication-bias signal. Composite resin and RMGIC performed moderately (92.18% / 90.30%) but composite resin showed the widest spread of technical/esthetic complications (shade mismatch 29.2%, staining 15.6%). Strip crowns had the lowest retention (83.48%) and the highest incidence of partial/complete material loss.

## Key Contributions

- Quantifies pooled 5-year retention across five materials + strip crowns using a rigorous, PROSPERO-registered PICOS/MOOSE/PRISMA-P protocol and RoB2-assessed RCT evidence base.
- Separates retentive, biological, and technical complication categories per material, giving clinicians a complication *profile*, not just a single failure percentage — critical for material selection beyond retention alone.
- Updates and reaffirms Chisini et al 2018's finding (SSC highest success rate) with a larger, more recent (2014–2022 RCTs) evidence base, while adding zirconia and compomer data not as thoroughly quantified previously.
- Flags a specific safety signal: zirconia's high gingival-inflammation rate (21.8% in clinical-study data) despite excellent mechanical retention — a nuance obscured if only retention rate is considered.

## Methodology

PICOS-based search of Cochrane, Embase, PubMed/MEDLINE (July–August 2023) for RCTs and prospective/retrospective clinical studies of pediatric crowns (ages 1–10) in stainless steel, zirconia, composite resin, compomer, RMGIC. Two independent reviewers screened titles/abstracts and full texts, with a third reviewer as tiebreaker. Risk of bias assessed via Cochrane RoB2 (RCTs) and a Moga et al-adapted checklist (clinical studies); only moderate/low-risk studies retained. For meta-analysis, failure rate = failures / total exposure time (cumulative crown-days); a fixed-effects Poisson regression model (justified by non-significant heterogeneity, p>.05 in all groups) estimated 3-year and 5-year retention rates per material. Funnel plots and Egger tests assessed publication bias.

## Results

**Pooled 5-year retention (Poisson regression):**

| Material | 5-year retention |
|---|---|
| Stainless steel crown (SSC) | 97.88% |
| Zirconia | 97.09% |
| Composite resin | 92.18% |
| RMGIC | 90.30% |
| Compomer | 88.90% |
| Strip crowns | 83.48% |
| Overall (unstratified) | 92.20% (95% CI 92.14–92.26%) |

Descriptive RCT retention ranges: compomer 77.8–100%; composite resin 80.6–100%; SSC 92.3–100%; strip crowns 78–100%; zirconia 86.4–100%.

**Retentive complications (RCTs)**: SSC = zero across all categories (decementation, chipping, fracture, poor anatomic form, complete/partial loss). Compomer had 8 fractures and no other retentive complications. Composite resin had the most varied retentive complications (12 chipping, 3 fracture, 3 poor anatomic form, 1 complete + 2 partial loss). Strip crowns had the highest material-loss burden (7 complete + 19 partial loss). Zirconia had 5 decementation events plus 1 complete and 1 partial loss.

**Biological complications**: In clinical studies, gingival inflammation was 21.8% (80/368) for zirconia vs 3.3% (10/289) for SSC; secondary caries was 8.3% (compomer) and 8.8% (composite resin), 0% for zirconia in RCT data. In RCTs, gingival inflammation was highest for composite resin (24.24%) and lowest for compomer (0%).

**Technical complications**: Compomer's only technical issue was marginal adaptation (29%, 19/67). SSC had minimal technical complications aside from opposing-tooth wear (13.6%). Composite resin had the broadest spread — 29.2% shade mismatch, 15.6% staining, 13% plaque retention. Zirconia stayed low across most categories (opposing-tooth wear 8.5%, shade mismatch 4.8%).

**Publication bias**: significant Egger-test asymmetry for zirconia (p<.001); insufficient study counts to robustly test SSC, composite resin, or strip crowns.
