## Three-line Summary

RCT (n=47, 60 IR-FDPs, 60 months): zirconia vs FRC cantilever IRFDP — overall survival 91.7%, success 81.7%; no significant difference between designs or materials (P>0.05)

Zirconia failures: catastrophic connector fractures; FRC failures: repairable deformation — material choice influences failure mode, not survival rate

Inlay ring (most conservative) design performs comparably to lingual/occlusal coverage at 5 years

## Background

Cantilever inlay-retained fixed dental prostheses (IR-FDPs) offer a minimally invasive option for posterior tooth replacement, avoiding implant surgery and full crown preparation. The 36-month data from this RCT (kasem-2025) showed 97.5% survival with no material difference. This 5-year extension provides longer-term evidence and reveals emerging failure patterns.

## Methods

- **Design**: Prospective RCT
- **n**: 47 patients, 60 cantilever IR-FDPs replacing mandibular second premolars
- **Groups** (n=20 each):
  - IR: inlay ring retainer (most conservative)
  - LC: lingual coverage retainer
  - OC: occlusal coverage retainer
- **Materials per group**: Zirconia (n=10) vs FRC (n=10)
- **Randomization**: computer-generated, allocation concealment via sealed opaque envelopes
- **Bonding**: standardized adhesive protocol
- **Evaluation**: modified FDI criteria at 60 months
- **Statistics**: Monte Carlo tests, Cochran's Q test

## Results

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Overall survival | 91.7% |
| Overall success | 81.7% |
| Between-group difference | P>0.05 (NS) |

### Failures (n=5, requiring replacement)
| Failure type | Material |
|---|---|
| Connector fracture ×2 | Zirconia |
| Debonding ×2 | Zirconia |
| Irreversible framework deformation ×1 | FRC |

### Complications (n=6, managed without replacement)
- Debonding (re-bonded)
- Plastic deformation
- Veneering composite delamination
- Minor veneering chipping
- Secondary caries ×2

### Material-specific failure patterns
| Material | Failure mode | Reparability |
|---|---|---|
| **Zirconia** | Connector fractures, loss of retention | Non-repairable (catastrophic) |
| **FRC** | Progressive deformation, occasional repairable complications | Partially repairable |

No statistically significant differences between groups — failure behavior differences are exploratory due to small n per subgroup.

## Clinical Significance

1. **Survival 91.7% at 5yr** is acceptable for a minimally invasive posterior replacement — no implant, no full-crown preparation
2. **Material choice shapes failure mode more than survival rate**: zirconia fails abruptly at the connector; FRC degrades gradually and may be repaired
3. **Inlay ring (IR) design** — the most conservative option (least tooth preparation) — shows equivalent outcomes to more extensive retainer designs
4. **Connector integrity** is the critical zone for zirconia cantilever IR-FDPs; generous connector dimensions are important
5. **Supersedes 36-month data** from kasem-2025 as primary 5yr reference

## Comparison to 36-month Data (kasem-2025)

| Parameter | 36-month (kasem-2025) | 60-month (this paper) |
|---|---|---|
| Overall survival | 97.5% | 91.7% |
| Overall success | 96.6% | 81.7% |
| Material difference | NS | NS |

Incremental failures at 36-60mo period account for the survival drop.

## Limitations

- Small n=10 per subgroup — insufficient power for subgroup comparisons
- Failure mode differences should be treated as exploratory
- Single jaw (mandibular), single tooth type (second premolar)
- Single operator preparation — may not generalize
