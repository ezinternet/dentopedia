## Three-line Summary

Clinical patient-based study of 65 patients receiving 65 single metal-ceramic restorations on implants; after cementation the restoration-abutment unit was unscrewed and cement remnant area quantified in pixels from photographs of all specimen quadrants, testing implant location, diameter and restoration-tissue undercut.

Cement-covered soft tissue / restoration area: molars 4% / 7%, premolars 3.8% / 7.3%, anteriors 3% / 3.4%; diameter 3.5 mm 3.3% / 7.4%, 4.0 mm 7.7% / 7.7%, 5.0 mm 3.9% / 2.1%; undercut 1 mm 3.5% / 5.4%, 2 mm 4% / 8.1%, 3 mm 4.8% / 8.4%.

Implant location was insignificant (p > 0.05) for both soft tissue and specimen, while diameter (soft tissue p = 0.026, specimen p = 0.600) and undercut (soft tissue p = 0.004, specimen p = 0.046) were related to remnant amount — minimize undercuts when cement retention is chosen; abstract-only (full text not retrieved).

## 세줄요약

임상 환자 기반 연구, 단일 금속-세라믹 (metal-ceramic) 보철물을 식립한 환자 65명·임플란트 65개; 접착 후 보철물-지대주 일체를 풀어 모든 사분면 사진의 픽셀 (pixel) 면적비로 시멘트 잔여물 (cement remnants)을 정량하고 임플란트 위치·직경 (diameter)·보철물-연조직 언더컷 (undercut)을 평가.

시멘트로 피복된 연조직/보철물 면적: 대구치 (molars) 4%/7%, 소구치 (premolars) 3.8%/7.3%, 전치 (anteriors) 3%/3.4%; 직경 3.5 mm 3.3%/7.4%, 4.0 mm 7.7%/7.7%, 5.0 mm 3.9%/2.1%; 언더컷 1 mm 3.5%/5.4%, 2 mm 4%/8.1%, 3 mm 4.8%/8.4%.

임플란트 위치는 두 표적 모두 유의하지 않았고 (p > 0.05), 직경 (연조직 p = 0.026, 보철물 p = 0.600)과 언더컷 (연조직 p = 0.004, 보철물 p = 0.046)은 잔여물과 관련 — 시멘트유지 (cement-retained) 보철을 택한다면 언더컷을 최소화해야 함; 초록만 확보 (abstract-only, full text not retrieved).

## Summary

This clinical study tested whether three chairside factors — where the implant sits in the arch, how wide it is, and how deep the restoration–tissue undercut is — predict how much luting cement is left behind around cement-retained single crowns. Sixty-five patients received single metal-ceramic restorations on 65 implants (39 molars, 22 premolars, 4 anteriors; 21 / 34 / 10 implants of 3.5 / 4.0 / 5.0 mm). After cementation the restoration-abutment unit was unscrewed, all quadrants of each specimen were photographed, and cement remnant area was expressed as a ratio to total restoration and peri-implant tissue area in pixels. Implant location was not significant (p > 0.05 on both surfaces); diameter reached significance only for soft tissue (p = 0.026, but p = 0.600 for the specimen), whereas undercut was significant for both soft tissue (p = 0.004) and the specimen (p = 0.046). The practical conclusion is that when a cemented crown is desired, undercuts should be reduced to a minimum to improve cement-excess removal, regardless of implant diameter or location. Key caveat: the PubMed abstract only was retrieved, and remnant measurement was ex vivo on retrieved specimens, with no peri-implant clinical outcome reported.

## Key Contributions

- First patient-based stratification of undetected cement coverage by implant location, implant diameter and restoration–tissue undercut, using pixel planimetry of all specimen quadrants.
- Demonstrates that arch location does not predict retained cement (p > 0.05) while undercut does (p = 0.004 soft tissue, p = 0.046 specimen) — the actionable factor is undercut, not implant position.
- Shows the diameter signal is surface-specific: significant for peri-implant soft tissue (p = 0.026) but absent for the restoration specimen (p = 0.600).
- Gives a chairside rule for the cement-retained branch: minimize the undercut when cleanup is expected to be difficult.

## Methodology

- **Design**: clinical patient-based single-cohort study with ex-vivo specimen analysis; cementation in patients, then retrieval and photographic analysis of the restoration-abutment unit. No randomization or comparator arm described in the abstract (`prospective` is the study-design label; the abstract does not itself use the term).
- **n**: 65 patients / 65 implants / 65 single metal-ceramic restorations — 39 molars, 22 premolars, 4 anteriors; diameters 21 × 3.5 mm, 34 × 4.0 mm, 10 × 5.0 mm.
- **Undercut**: measured between restoration and tissue; 1 mm in 118 sites, 2 mm in 96 sites, 3 mm in 46 sites.
- **Outcome**: cement remnant area relative to total restoration area and peri-implant tissue area, calculated in pixels from photographs of all quadrants after unscrewing the restoration-abutment unit.
- **Significance**: α = 0.05.

## Results

Cement-covered area (soft tissue % / restoration %):

| Factor | Level | Soft tissue | Restoration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implant location | Molar (39) | 4% | 7% |
| Implant location | Premolar (22) | 3.8% | 7.3% |
| Implant location | Anterior (4) | 3% | 3.4% |
| Implant diameter | 3.5 mm (21) | 3.3% | 7.4% |
| Implant diameter | 4.0 mm (34) | 7.7% | 7.7% |
| Implant diameter | 5.0 mm (10) | 3.9% | 2.1% |
| Undercut | 1 mm (118 sites) | 3.5% | 5.4% |
| Undercut | 2 mm (96 sites) | 4% | 8.1% |
| Undercut | 3 mm (46 sites) | 4.8% | 8.4% |

| Factor | p (soft tissue) | p (specimen) |
|---|---|---|
| Implant location | > 0.05 (insignificant) | > 0.05 (insignificant) |
| Implant diameter | 0.026 (significant) | 0.600 (insignificant) |
| Undercut | 0.004 (significant) | 0.046 (significant) |

Larger undercuts tracked more cement on both surfaces (soft tissue 3.5% → 4.8%; restoration 5.4% → 8.4% from 1 mm to 3 mm), whereas diameter and location showed no consistent ordering. No peri-implantitis or marginal bone loss (MBL) outcome was reported in the abstract.
