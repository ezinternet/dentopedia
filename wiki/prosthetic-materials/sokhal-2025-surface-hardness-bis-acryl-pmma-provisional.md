## One-line Summary

In-vitro Vickers-hardness study (Cureus 2025, n=40/group, 160 total) of four provisional crown-and-bridge materials: three bis-acryl composites (3M Protemp 4, Dentsply Integrity, Vericom Care C&B) all had significantly higher surface hardness than the PMMA self-cure acrylic (Pyrax SC-10) — ranking Protemp 4 (20.54 VHN) > Integrity (19.80) > Vericom (18.55) > Pyrax (16.65), all pairwise p<0.01.

## Summary

This well-powered laboratory study (160 specimens, ADA spec. no. 27 dies) ranked the Vickers surface hardness of four provisional crown-and-bridge materials — three bis-acryl composites (3M Protemp 4, Dentsply Integrity, Vericom Care C&B) versus one traditional PMMA self-cure acrylic (Pyrax SC-10). Every bis-acryl outperformed the PMMA material, with a clear order: **Protemp 4 (20.54 VHN) > Integrity (19.80) > Vericom (18.55) > Pyrax PMMA (16.65)**, all pairwise differences significant (p<0.01). The authors attribute the bis-acryl advantage to a cross-linked polymer matrix plus inorganic filler, which resists wear and surface degradation. The clinical translation is a duration-based split: harder **bis-acryls suit long-term, high-stress provisionals** (though harder materials are trickier to adjust and polish), while **PMMA acrylics remain appropriate for short-term, adjustable, cost-sensitive** use. This is the mechanical-property counterpart to the SNAP marginal-fit/color study (Givens 2007): acrylic provisionals are competitive on fit and color but measurably softer.

## Key Contributions

- Standardized, well-powered **hardness ranking** across the two dominant provisional material classes.
- Reaffirms **bis-acryl > PMMA** surface hardness with tight statistics (all pairwise p<0.01).
- Frames a practical **long-term (bis-acryl) vs short-term (PMMA)** material-selection rule, noting the adjustability trade-off of harder materials.

## Methodology

- **Design**: in-vitro comparative Vickers hardness; 160 specimens (40/group) in stainless-steel ADA spec. no. 27 dies (30×10×2 mm).
- **Groups**: 3M Protemp 4 (bis-acryl, bifunctional MA); Dentsply Integrity (bis-acryl, multifunctional MA); Vericom Care C&B (dual-cure bis-acryl); Pyrax SC-10 (PMMA self-cure).
- **Fabrication** per manufacturer: bis-acryls auto-mixed (Garant 10:1, press 30 psi/5 min) or extruded + light-cured 40 s/post-cured 10 min (Vericom); PMMA hand-mixed 2:1, self-cure 15 min.
- **Testing**: HV-1000B, 25 g load, 15 s, 2 indentations/specimen; VHN = 1.8544·F/d².
- **Stats**: G*Power sizing (95% power, f=0.40); SPSS v25; one-way ANOVA + Tukey HSD, p<0.05.

## Results

| Rank | Material | Type | Mean hardness (VHN) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3M Protemp 4 | Bis-acryl | **20.54 ± 0.69** |
| 2 | Dentsply Integrity | Bis-acryl | 19.80 ± 0.47 |
| 3 | Vericom Care C&B | Dual-cure bis-acryl | 18.55 ± 1.36 |
| 4 | Pyrax SC-10 | PMMA (self-cure) | **16.65 ± 0.66** |

- One-way ANOVA p<0.01; all pairwise comparisons significant (Tukey HSD, p≤0.001).
- Largest difference: Protemp 4 vs Pyrax (MD 3.89, 95% CI 3.39–4.39). Smallest significant: Protemp 4 vs Integrity (MD 0.74).
- Consistent with prior work (Jo, Savabi, Kerby, Knobloch) and the Astudillo-Rubio SR/MA that hardness is decisive for provisional clinical performance.
