## Three-line Summary

Integrative overview (Biomedicines 2023) synthesizing evidence on translucent zirconia in fixed prosthodontics across light properties, mechanical performance, adhesion strategies, and implant-supported restorations, covering 3Y-TZP, 4Y-TZP, and 5Y-TZP types.

Higher yttria content increases translucency (more natural appearance) but decreases flexural strength (3Y: ~1,000–1,200 MPa → 5Y: ~400–600 MPa); cyclic loading reduces 4Y/5Y more than 3Y; MDP-containing resin cement after Al₂O₃ sandblasting remains the standard adhesive protocol.

Translucent zirconia is a clinically viable option for single crowns through complex bridgework and implant-supported restorations, but material selection must match regional mechanical demands — monolithic 3Y-TZP for posterior stress; 4–5Y-TZP for anterior esthetics with single-crown-only restriction.

## Summary

Kui et al. 2023 provides a clinician-oriented synthesis of translucent zirconia types and their applications in fixed prosthodontics. The key contribution is a clear characterization of the yttria content spectrum: as yttria increases (3Y → 5Y), translucency rises (enabling natural esthetic outcomes) while flexural strength falls sharply. The review confirms that all three types maintain clinically acceptable fracture resistance under cyclic loading, but 4Y and 5Y subtypes are more susceptible to fatigue-induced strength reduction. Adhesive bonding to translucent zirconia requires surface preparation (Al₂O₃ sandblasting) and MDP-containing cements for durable results. For implant-supported restorations, translucent zirconia achieves precision and reliability comparable to opaque 3Y-TZP with the added benefit of natural esthetics.

## Key Contributions

- Defined the 3Y/4Y/5Y-TZP strength-translucency spectrum with clinical implications
- Characterized cyclic loading effects: 4Y and 5Y more vulnerable to fatigue than 3Y
- Established that Al₂O₃ sandblasting + MDP-containing resin cement is standard adhesive protocol for translucent zirconia
- Documented marginal fit of CAD/CAM translucent zirconia as clinically acceptable
- Reviewed implant-supported restoration applications: suitability for comprehensive restoration spanning single crowns to complex bridgework

## Methodology

Narrative/integrative review. Iuliu Hatieganu University, Cluj-Napoca, Romania. Covered: optical properties (light transmission, fluorescence, opalescence), mechanical attributes (flexural strength, fracture toughness, fatigue), surface treatment protocols, adhesive strategies, and implant-supported restoration use.

## Results

**Strength-translucency trade-off:**
| Type | Flexural strength | Translucency | Clinical indication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3Y-TZP | ~1,000–1,200 MPa | Low | Posterior high-stress, multi-unit |
| 4Y-TZP | ~700–900 MPa | Medium | Anterior single crowns |
| 5Y-TZP | ~400–600 MPa | High | Anterior esthetics, single only |

**Adhesion:** Al₂O₃ sandblasting (50–110 µm) + MDP-containing resin cement (e.g., Panavia series) — standard protocol; silane alone insufficient for zirconia.

**Fatigue:** 3Y-TZP: stable under cyclic loading; 4Y and 5Y: progressive strength reduction after fatigue.

**Marginal fit:** CAD/CAM translucent zirconia achieves marginal gaps comparable to or better than metal-ceramic.
