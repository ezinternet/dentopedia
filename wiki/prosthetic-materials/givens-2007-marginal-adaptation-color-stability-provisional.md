## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study comparing marginal fit and 1-week tea-stain color stability of three bis-acryl provisionals against SNAP (PEMA control), n=10/group, J Prosthodont 2007.

The dual-cure Luxatemp Solar had the worst marginal discrepancy (319 µm); Protemp Garant was the only material with clinically noticeable shade change (ΔE=4.33).

SNAP matched auto-cure bis-acryls for both low marginal gap and color stability, showing acrylic provisionals are competitive on these two properties.

## Summary

This laboratory study asked whether the traditional acrylic provisional **SNAP (Parkell, polyethyl methacrylate)** still holds up against newer bis-acryl composites on two clinically important properties — marginal adaptation and color stability. Provisional crowns and discs were made from four materials (SNAP control; auto-cure bis-acryls Protemp Garant and Integrity; dual-cure bis-acryl Luxatemp Solar), and marginal gaps were measured at four engraved reference points while color change was assessed after 1 week of cyclic tea immersion (ΔE). The headline is that the **acrylic control was not the weak link**: SNAP, Protemp Garant, and Integrity all showed similarly low marginal discrepancy, and SNAP was among the materials with no clinically detectable shade change. The two flagged weaknesses belonged to bis-acryls — the **dual-cure Luxatemp Solar fit worst (319 µm)** and **Protemp Garant stained noticeably (ΔE=4.33)**. For the wiki, this is the "SNAP product" evidence anchor, paired with the PMC-fulltext hardness study (Sokhal 2025) that covers the mechanical dimension SNAP tends to lose on.

## Key Contributions

- Provides direct **SNAP-vs-bis-acryl** benchmarking on marginal fit and short-term color stability.
- Demonstrates the acrylic control (SNAP) is **competitive for fit and stain resistance**, countering the assumption that bis-acryls dominate on every axis.
- Localizes material-specific weaknesses: **dual-cure marginal fit** (Luxatemp Solar) and **staining** (Protemp Garant).

## Methodology

- **Design**: in-vitro comparative materials study; n=10 crowns and n=10 discs per material (4 materials).
- **Marginal fit**: maxillary central incisor ivorine tooth, full-coverage all-ceramic prep (1.5-mm chamfer), 40 replicas; crowns on polyethylene coping template; discrepancy measured at four points (1 mm below F/L/M/D margins) under 10×.
- **Color stability**: 10 mm × 2 mm discs, cyclic tea immersion 1 week (Tucillo/Nielson); ΔE baseline→post-stain.
- **Materials**: SNAP (Parkell, PEMA, self-cure control); Protemp Garant (3M ESPE, auto-cure bis-acryl); Integrity (Dentsply, auto-cure bis-acryl); Luxatemp Solar (DMG, dual-cure bis-acryl).
- **Stats**: 1-way ANOVA + Tukey.

## Results

| Material | Type | Marginal discrepancy | Color change (1 wk tea) |
|---|---|---|---|
| SNAP (Parkell) | PEMA acrylic (control) | Low (∼ auto-cure bis-acryls) | No clinically detectable change |
| Protemp Garant (3M) | Auto-cure bis-acryl | Low | **ΔE = 4.33 (noticeable, p<0.05)** |
| Integrity (Dentsply) | Auto-cure bis-acryl | Low | No clinically detectable change |
| Luxatemp Solar (DMG) | Dual-cure bis-acryl | **319 µm (worst, p<0.05)** | No clinically detectable change |

Take-homes: (1) the **dual-cure** material fit worst; (2) SNAP, Protemp Garant, and Integrity had similarly low marginal gaps; (3) only Protemp Garant stained clinically; SNAP, Luxatemp Solar, and Integrity were color-stable at 1 week.
