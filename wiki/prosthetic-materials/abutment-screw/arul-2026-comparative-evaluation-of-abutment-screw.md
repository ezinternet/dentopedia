## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (n=30 implant-abutment assemblies, 3 groups × 2 loading conditions) comparing detorque values after application of no threadlocker (control), industrial-grade dimethacrylate (Loctite® 243), or medical-grade methacrylate (Nobil Grip™) under static storage (1 month) and chewing simulation (240,000 cycles, 50 N, ≈1 year).

Methacrylate yielded the highest detorque values (static: 36.46 ± 0.95 Ncm; dynamic: 33.22 ± 0.34 Ncm), followed by dimethacrylate (32.84 / 29.98 Ncm) then control (28.80 / 25.84 Ncm); all pairwise differences P < 0.0005; cyclic loading reduced detorque in all groups.

Medical-grade methacrylate threadlocker outperforms industrial dimethacrylate and uncoated control for abutment screw retention, supporting its integration in high-risk clinical scenarios (bruxism, posterior zones, long-span prostheses), pending clinical validation.

## Summary

Abutment screw loosening (reported incidence 12.7–45% in single-implant restorations) is the most prevalent mechanical prosthetic complication. This in vitro study from SRM Kattankulathur Dental College (Tamil Nadu, India) is the first to directly compare a medical-grade anaerobic methacrylate adhesive (Nobil Grip™) against an industrial-grade dimethacrylate threadlocker (Loctite® 243) and an untreated control. Thirty Adin implant-abutment assemblies were torqued to 35 Ncm, subdivided into static (room temperature, 1 month) and dynamic (chewing simulator, 240,000 cycles, 50 N, 48 h moist) subgroups, and detorque values were recorded post-condition. Methacrylate outperformed dimethacrylate and control under both conditions (all pairwise P < 0.0005), and static detorque consistently exceeded dynamic detorque across groups. Both null hypotheses (no difference with/without threadlockers; no difference between threadlocker types) were rejected. The results support adding medical-grade methacrylate threadlockers to screw-retained implant protocols, especially in high-occlusal-load situations, though single-system, single-operator in vitro data require prospective clinical validation.

## Key Contributions

- First direct comparison of medical-grade anaerobic methacrylate (Nobil Grip™) vs industrial-grade dimethacrylate (Loctite® 243) vs uncoated control for abutment screw retention under both static and cyclic loading.
- Establishes a clear detorque hierarchy: methacrylate > dimethacrylate > control, statistically significant under both conditions.
- Quantifies the detorque penalty of cyclic loading: dynamic values were ~3–4 Ncm lower than static across all groups, confirming that functional loading accelerates screw-retention loss.
- Distinguishes medical-grade from industrial-grade adhesive performance and biocompatibility rationale — Loctite 243 is not FDA-approved for intraoral use; Nobil Grip is biocompatible and designed for dental prosthetic components.

## Methodology

- **Design**: In vitro, prospective, single-operator; ethics board approved (IEC SRMIEC-ST0323-421, 2023).
- **n**: 30 implant analogs (Adin, 12.6 mm × 3.7 mm); 30 straight abutments (4.5 mm diameter, 8.5 mm height); 3 groups (n=10 each), each split into static (n=5) and dynamic (n=5) subgroups.
- **Groups**: Control (35 Ncm torque + retorque at 10 min, no adhesive) | Dimethacrylate (Loctite® 243, 24 h anaerobic cure) | Methacrylate (Nobil Grip™, 24 h anaerobic cure).
- **Loading**: Static = dry room-temperature storage 1 month; Dynamic = chewing simulator 240,000 cycles, 50 N, 16 mm/s, 48 h moist (Steiner et al. 2009 protocol = ≈1 year masticatory simulation).
- **Primary outcome**: Detorque value (Ncm) measured with digital torque meter (hex driver, bench vice stabilization).
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk normality; one-way + two-way ANOVA; Tukey HSD post hoc; α = 0.05; G*Power-calculated sample size (power 80%, effect size 0.60).

## Results

| Group | Condition | Detorque mean ± SD (Ncm) | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control | Static (1 month) | 28.80 ± 0.96 | 27.10–29.50 |
| Dimethacrylate | Static (1 month) | 32.84 ± 1.12 | 31.10–33.70 |
| Methacrylate | Static (1 month) | 36.46 ± 0.95 | 35.40–37.50 |
| Control | Dynamic (240 k cycles) | 25.84 ± 0.63 | 24.90–26.50 |
| Dimethacrylate | Dynamic (240 k cycles) | 29.98 ± 1.05 | 28.30–30.90 |
| Methacrylate | Dynamic (240 k cycles) | 33.22 ± 0.34 | 32.80–33.50 |

- Static overall (all groups) = 32.70 ± 3.37 Ncm; dynamic = 29.68 ± 3.20 Ncm (P = 0.000)
- Methacrylate vs control: Δ static = 7.66 Ncm, Δ dynamic = 7.38 Ncm (both P < 0.0005)
- Methacrylate vs dimethacrylate: Δ static = 3.62 Ncm, Δ dynamic = 3.24 Ncm (both P < 0.0005)
- Dimethacrylate vs control: Δ static = 4.04 Ncm, Δ dynamic = 4.14 Ncm (both P < 0.0005)
