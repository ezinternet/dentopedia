## Three-line Summary

In-vitro factorial study (N = 80 CAD-CAM zirconia 3-unit FDPs on two Conelog implants, n = 10 per group; non-engaging/non-engaging, non-engaging/engaging and engaging/engaging Ti-bases; 0 µm control vs 100 µm horizontal or vertical misfit; 1.2 × 10^6 thermomechanical cycles at 49 N) measuring reverse torque and maximum failure load (Fmax).

Engagement configuration did not affect Fmax or reverse torque, and 100 µm misfit in either direction did not change reverse torque; horizontal misfit lowered Fmax versus control (P < .001), and distal implants showed lower reverse torque than mesial (13.5 ± 0.5 vs 18.6 ± 0.5 Ncm; P < .001).

Implant position (distal) and horizontal misfit were the relevant risk factors rather than Ti-base engagement, but the single zirconia/implant system, low 49 N load, and several internal inconsistencies in the reported tables limit generalizability.

## 세줄요약

시험관 내 요인설계 연구 (CAD-CAM 지르코니아 3본 고정성 보철 80개, 임플란트 2개 [Conelog], 군당 n = 10; 비결합형/비결합형·비결합형/결합형·결합형/결합형 Ti-base; 0 µm 대조 vs 100 µm 수평 또는 수직 부적합; 49 N로 1.2 × 10^6회 열기계 순환)로 역회전력 (reverse torque)과 최대 파절하중 (Fmax)을 측정.

Ti-base 결합 형태는 Fmax와 역회전력에 영향이 없었고, 어느 방향이든 100 µm 부적합은 역회전력을 바꾸지 않았다. 수평 부적합만 Fmax를 대조군보다 낮췄고 (P < .001), 원심 임플란트의 역회전력이 근심보다 낮았다 (13.5 ± 0.5 vs 18.6 ± 0.5 Ncm; P < .001).

관건은 Ti-base 결합 형태가 아니라 임플란트 위치 (원심)와 수평 부적합이었으나, 단일 지르코니아·임플란트 시스템, 낮은 49 N 하중, 보고된 표들의 내부 불일치가 일반화를 제한한다.

## Summary

This bench study tested whether Ti-base engagement (anti-rotation feature) and the orientation of a 100 µm framework misfit alter reverse torque and fracture strength of 3-unit zirconia FDPs on two internal-connection implants after 1.2 × 10^6 thermomechanical cycles at 49 N. Engagement type had no significant effect on Fmax or reverse torque, and neither misfit orientation changed reverse torque. Horizontal misfit reduced Fmax relative to the 0 µm control, while vertical misfit did not. Distal implants lost more torque than mesial implants, so implant position, rather than abutment engagement, was the clinically relevant variable for preload retention in this model. The engaging groups were cement-retained and the non-engaging groups screw-retained, so engagement and retention mode are confounded.

## Key Contributions
- Factorial design combining abutment engagement and misfit orientation with reverse-torque readout under fatigue, where the authors state earlier work examined these separately.
- Shows no reverse-torque penalty from 100 µm horizontal or vertical misfit in this model, consistent with the clinical observation that screw complications cluster at larger misfit (134–169 µm) than tested here.
- Reports lower reverse torque on the distal than the mesial implant of a two-implant bridge.
- Horizontal misfit, not vertical, lowered fracture load, with failures concentrated at the pontic-distal connector junction.

## Methodology
Two Conelog Screw-Line implants (Ø4.3 × 13 mm, 7.5° internal connection; 22 mm span, 10° divergence) in a Frasaco model simulating a mandibular second premolar to second molar bridge. Eighty CAD-CAM 3Y-TZP frameworks (full-contour design) with 1 mm buccal feldspathic veneer, assigned by random number generator to N-N, N-E (screw-retained) or E-E (cement-retained, Panavia V5 on zirconia abutments) Ti-base combinations; 0 µm control, 100 µm horizontal and 100 µm vertical misfit casts (E-E tested at control and vertical only). Screws tightened to 20 Ncm. Aging: 5–55 °C thermocycling with 1.2 × 10^6 cycles at 49 N, 1.67 Hz (estimated by the authors as 4–5 years of function). Reverse torque measured at T0 and T1 with new screws at T1; Fmax at 1 mm/min; failure modes by digital microscope. Sample size from a pilot (d = 0.848), n = 10 per group. Two-way ANOVA (Fmax) and mixed-model ANOVA (reverse torque, with implant position).

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Specimens surviving aging | All intact except one N-E vertical-misfit specimen (broken screw) |
| Fmax, control (N) | N-N 1828.06 ± 473.63; N-E 1758.70 ± 255.76; E-E 1284.54 ± 283.71 |
| Fmax, vertical misfit (N) | N-N 2119.74 ± 453.71; N-E 1816.18 ± 327.39 (n = 9); E-E 1563.26 ± 404.36 |
| Fmax, horizontal misfit (N) | N-N 1509.20 ± 179.64; N-E 1557.25 ± 283.67 |
| Engagement effect on Fmax | F = 1.12, P = .294 |
| Engagement × misfit interaction | F = 0.92, P = .421 |
| Misfit factor on Fmax | F = 6.37, P < .001; horizontal vs control P < .001; vertical vs control P > .05 |
| Reverse torque, implant position | Distal 13.5 ± 0.5 vs mesial 18.6 ± 0.5 Ncm (abstract); P < .001 |
| Reverse torque, engagement / misfit | P = .318 / P = .267; no significant interactions |
| Dominant fracture pattern | Cracks started at the pontic-distal connector junction; transversal distal-connector fractures only in N-N control/vertical groups; fossa-to-distal-connector common in N-E and E-E, especially under horizontal misfit |

Caveats on the reported data: the abstract's P = .421 is labeled as the engagement effect but is the interaction term in the Results; Table 2 lists n = 4–6 for N-N torque cells despite n = 10 per group; four Table 3 columns sum to 11 failures for n = 10; some T1 torques exceeded T0, attributed by the authors to friction and new screws.
