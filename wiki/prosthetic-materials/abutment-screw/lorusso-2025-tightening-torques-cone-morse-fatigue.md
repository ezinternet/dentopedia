## Three-line Summary

In-vitro fatigue study of 80 implants with a 6° cone morse internal-hex joint (10 per group; 4 torque levels x loaded/unloaded control), embedded at 30° and tightened to 25, 30, 35 or 40 Ncm, with implant-abutment microgap measured on longitudinal sections by SEM and digital microscopy.

Microgap differed significantly across torque levels in all four conditions (Kruskal-Wallis p=0.0121 loaded flexion, 0.0027 loaded extension, 0.0337 control flexion, 0.0021 control extension), and 30 Ncm gave the smallest microgap; no joint failed and all loaded groups showed more distortion than unloaded controls.

The authors recommend 30 Ncm for this particular joint, but the evidence is a single-design bench test with n=10 per cell, no raw microgap values reported in the text, and internal inconsistencies in the loading protocol, so it supports direction of effect rather than a transferable torque number.

## 세줄요약

6° 콘 모스(cone morse) 내부 육각 연결 임플란트 80개(그룹당 10개; 토크 4수준 x 하중군/비하중 대조군)를 30°로 매립해 25·30·35·40 Ncm로 체결한 뒤, 종단면에서 주사전자현미경(SEM)·디지털 현미경으로 임플란트-어버트먼트 미세간극(microgap)을 측정한 in vitro 피로시험.

네 조건 모두에서 토크군 간 미세간극 차이가 유의했고(Kruskal-Wallis p=0.0121 하중 굴곡, 0.0027 하중 신전, 0.0337 대조 굴곡, 0.0021 대조 신전), 30 Ncm에서 미세간극이 가장 작았다. 파손된 연결부는 없었고, 하중군은 모두 비하중 대조군보다 변형이 컸다.

저자들은 이 연결부에 30 Ncm을 제안하지만, 단일 디자인 벤치 시험·셀당 n=10·본문에 원자료 수치 없음·하중 프로토콜의 내적 불일치 때문에 효과의 방향을 보여줄 뿐 다른 시스템에 옮길 토크 수치로는 쓸 수 없다.

## Summary

This bench study tested how the abutment tightening torque affects a 6° cone morse internal-hex implant joint after fatigue loading. Eighty implants were split into four torque groups (25, 30, 35, 40 Ncm), each with 10 loaded and 10 unloaded specimens, and the implant-abutment microgap was measured on longitudinal sections at the flexion and extension points. The microgap differed significantly among torque levels with and without loading, and 30 Ncm produced the smallest gap; higher torques (35 and 40 Ncm) were accompanied by screw-head marks and frictional metal flaking in the deep cone. No specimen failed. Clinically, the result suggests that the torque applied to a cone morse abutment screw is not interchangeable across the 25-40 Ncm range, but the data are from one implant design in dry bench conditions and cannot be turned into a general torque rule.

## Key Contributions

- Torque-dose comparison (25/30/35/40 Ncm) on a single cone morse joint, with SEM-measured microgap as the outcome and a matched unloaded control for each torque.
- Shows that torque changes the interface even without loading (control groups also differed, p=0.0337 and 0.0021).
- 30 Ncm gave the smallest microgap; 35 and 40 Ncm showed screw-head marks and frictional flaking in the deep cone without loss of the marginal seal.
- Documents that loaded specimens had more microgap distortion than unloaded ones at every torque.

## Methodology

- In-vitro cyclic fatigue test, 80 implants (CLC, 6° cone morse, internal hex, platform switching), resin-embedded at 30 degrees +/- 2; 10 specimens per group (4 Test, 4 Control).
- Calibrated electronic torque meter; single-axis ZT-HIGH fatigue device, sinusoidal load control, Fmax 150 N / Fmin 9 N; the 25 Ncm group was run at 3 Hz and the other groups at 15 Hz.
- Longitudinal sections examined by SEM and transmitted-light digital microscopy; microgap measured at flexion and extension points.
- Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn's post-test across torques; Mann-Whitney versus control; Wilcoxon for flexion versus extension.

> [!note] Evidence note
> Absolute microgap values (micrometers) are only in figures and were not in the retrieved text. The paper has internal inconsistencies: implant diameter 3.5 vs 3.8 mm, 1 x 10^4 loads vs a 1,000,000-cycle limit, a different test frequency for the 25 Ncm group (3 Hz vs 15 Hz), a mislabelled 40 Ncm paragraph, and a Discussion sentence about a "significant reduction" in microgap that contradicts the Results. Preload, removal torque and screw loosening were not measured. Treat the 30 Ncm finding as hypothesis-level for this design only.
