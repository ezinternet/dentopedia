## Three-line Summary

In vitro bench study of two ISO 14801-certified titanium abutment screws (gold-plated hexalobular Gold-Tite/ZIMVIE vs anodized star-shaped Vega/Klockner), 6 groups of n = 5 screws, tightened 10 or 20 cycles at 20-40 N·cm into a new dummy implant per screw, with reverse torque value (RTV) recorded every cycle and micro-CT (n = 3 per group) of screw geometry.

RTV was below the applied torque in 30/30 screws (73-89%; median 87%); RTV fell significantly per cycle in the Vega/Klockner screws (-0.17 N·cm/cycle, p = 0.006) but not in the Gold-Tite screws (-0.11, p = 0.302); micro-CT showed head-localized area change with the mean cross-sectional area essentially unchanged, and in Gold-Tite only after 20 cycles (max head area about +8%).

Preload loss is intrinsic to screw tightening and RTV behaviour under cycling is system-dependent, so abutment screws should be treated as limited-use components; between-protocol contrasts were underpowered (n = 5) and micro-CT was descriptive only (n = 3), so no numeric replacement threshold is supported.

## 세줄요약

체외 실험(ISO 14801 인증 티타늄 지대주 나사 2종: 금도금 육각형 헤드 Gold-Tite/ZIMVIE vs 양극산화 별모양 헤드 Vega/Klockner; 6군 각 n = 5; 나사마다 새 더미 임플란트; 20-40 N·cm로 10 또는 20회 조임), 매 회 역회전 토크값 (Reverse Torque Value, RTV) 측정 및 마이크로 CT (micro-CT, n = 군당 3) 분석.

RTV는 30/30개 나사에서 적용 토크보다 낮았고(73-89%, 중앙값 87%), Vega/Klockner에서는 회당 -0.17 N·cm로 유의하게 감소(p = 0.006)했으나 Gold-Tite에서는 유의하지 않았다(-0.11, p = 0.302); micro-CT에서는 평균 단면적은 거의 불변이고 헤드 부위에 국한된 변화가 관찰되었으며, Gold-Tite는 20회 과사용 조건에서만 헤드 최대 단면적 약 +8% 증가했다.

프리로드 손실은 나사 조임에 본질적이고 반복 조임에 따른 RTV 거동은 시스템 의존적이므로 지대주 나사는 제한 사용 부품으로 다뤄야 하나, 군간 비교는 검정력 부족(n = 5)이고 micro-CT는 기술적 분석(n = 3)에 그쳐 구체적 교체 기준 수치는 제시할 수 없다.

## Summary

This in vitro study tested how torque magnitude (20, 30, 40 N·cm) and tightening cycle count (10 vs 20) affect reverse torque and screw geometry in two titanium abutment screw systems: the gold-plated hexalobular Gold-Tite (3i Zimmer Biomet/ZIMVIE) and the anodized star-shaped Vega (Klockner). Each group had five screws, each tightened into a new dummy implant with a calibrated electronic torque wrench. Every screw returned a reverse torque below its insertion torque (median 87%), and RTV declined progressively across cycles only in the Vega/Klockner system. Micro-CT showed deformation localized to the screw head with essentially unchanged mean cross-sectional area; in Gold-Tite this appeared only under the 20-cycle over-use condition, whereas Vega/Klockner screws showed comparable head change under all protocols. The authors conclude that abutment screws behave as limited-use components, but the small samples preclude protocol-specific thresholds.

## Key Contributions

- RTV analyzed as a trajectory across repeated cycles (linear mixed-effects model, screw as random effect) instead of a single endpoint.
- Factorial design separating torque magnitude from cycle count within each system, with a new implant per screw to remove the implant-thread wear confound.
- Micro-CT morphometry showing that tightening-related change is concentrated at the screw head, not distributed along the shaft.
- Evidence that preload loss is universal (30/30 screws) while its cycle-by-cycle evolution is surface- and geometry-dependent.

## Methodology

Bench study, single operator, calibrated electronic torque wrench (NSK iSD900), RTV by digital torque screwdriver at every loosening event; implants embedded in acrylic at 90 degrees. Groups (n = 5 screws): Gold-Tite 10 x 20 N·cm (recommended), 20 x 20 (over-use), 10 x 30 (over-torque); Vega 10 x 30 (recommended), 20 x 30 (over-use), 10 x 40 (over-torque). Screw was the experimental unit; mixed-effects model per system, Mann-Whitney U on per-screw mean RTV (final-cycle RTV as sensitivity), Wilcoxon signed-rank for RTV vs insertion torque. Micro-CT at 19.9 um voxel on 3 screws per group plus 2 unloaded references per system, with the two screwdriver-engagement sections excluded; results descriptive with Cohen's d.

## Clinical Reading (from this paper only)

- Treat the final tightening torque as an upper bound on retrievable preload, not the clamping force actually retained.
- Screws taken out repeatedly during provisional and try-in phases (about 10-20 cycles in this model) are plausible candidates for replacement; the authors explicitly decline to give a numeric threshold.
- Do not generalize a cycle-related RTV benefit across systems: here only the anodized Vega screw showed a significant per-cycle RTV decline, and the gold-plated screw showed head deformation only at 20 cycles.
- Findings are bench-only with an electronic torque wrench and no functional loading; they do not transfer directly to manual-driver clinical use.
