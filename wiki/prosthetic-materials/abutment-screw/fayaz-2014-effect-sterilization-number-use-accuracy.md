## Three-line Summary

In vitro bench study of 15 new friction-style mechanical torque-limiting devices (5 each from Astra Tech 25 Ncm, BioHorizons Dynatorq ITL 30 Ncm, Dr. Idhe 15–60 Ncm), measured with a Tohnichi digital torque gauge across 100 steam-sterilization cycles (134 °C, 0.9 bar, 18 min) with 10 torque applications per cycle.

All devices started within 10% of target, then exceeded it with systematic under-delivery: BioHorizons worst at 7.75 Ncm max absolute difference (25.83% of the 30 Ncm target, >10% deviation on 76% of all measurements), Astra Tech 3.35 Ncm (13.4%), Dr. Idhe 3.75 Ncm (12.5%); mean error deviated significantly through 100 cycles in BioHorizons (P<0.05) and Astra Tech (P<0.001 at cycle 100, P=0.04 through 75) but only through 75 cycles in Dr. Idhe (P=0.002).

Manufacturer dominates the degradation — absolute error was significantly lower for Astra Tech and Dr. Idhe than for BioHorizons (P<0.05) — implying a per-manufacturer maintenance/calibration interval rather than a universal one; single-site lubrication at the toggled position (the BioHorizons protocol) is the proposed driver of its failure.

## Summary

This in vitro bench study asked whether steam sterilization and accumulated use degrade the torque accuracy of friction-style mechanical torque-limiting devices (F-S MTLDs) — the hand instruments used to set abutment screw preload. Fifteen new devices from three manufacturers (Astra Tech 25 Ncm, BioHorizons Dynatorq ITL 30 Ncm, Dr. Idhe 15–60 Ncm; five each) were torqued with a Tohnichi digital torque gauge before and after 1, 5, 10, 20, 50, 75 and 100 autoclave cycles (134 °C, 0.9 bar, 18 min), with ten applications per cycle to simulate clinical use and artificial-saliva immersion plus disinfection before each cycle. Every device began within the accepted ±10% tolerance, but all three groups drifted systematically **under** the target as cycling progressed: BioHorizons was the outlier, reaching a 7.75 Ncm maximum absolute error (25.83% of target) with >10% deviation on 76% of measurements across all five devices, versus 3.35 Ncm (13.4%) for Astra Tech and 3.75 Ncm (12.5%) for Dr. Idhe. Between-manufacturer repeated-measures ANOVA confirmed that BioHorizons' absolute error was significantly larger (P<0.05) than both Astra Tech and Dr. Idhe, which did not differ from each other, and the authors attribute this to lubrication practice — multi-point lubrication after full disassembly for Astra Tech and Dr. Idhe versus single-site lubrication at the toggled position for BioHorizons. The clinically transferable point is that MTLD accuracy is manufacturer- and protocol-dependent rather than universal, so wrench verification and replacement intervals must be set per device instead of by a single blanket rule.

## Key Contributions

1. **Sterilization cycles × number of use isolated as one 2-factor design** — prior work examined autoclaving alone (Dellinges & Curtis 1996) or clinical aging alone (Gutierrez 1997; McCracken 2010); this study measured both together, so the combined maintenance burden is quantified rather than inferred.
2. **The ±10% clinical tolerance is a threshold, not a steady state** — all devices were inside 10% of target *before* 100 cycles, then exceeded it in varying degrees during cycling. This supplies an empirical basis for routine re-verification and retirement intervals rather than assuming a factory-calibrated wrench stays trustworthy.
3. **Manufacturer dominates the degradation rate** — between-group ANOVA found absolute error significantly lower in Astra Tech and Dr. Idhe than in BioHorizons (P<0.05), with no difference between the former two. BioHorizons' Dynatorq ITL, the same wrench family previously reported as sterilisation-tolerant in new condition, degraded the fastest here.
4. **Lubrication protocol proposed as the explanatory variable** — devices lubricated at multiple sites after dismantling behaved better than a single-site lubrication at the toggled position, suggesting maintenance technique is an actionable determinant of accuracy (hypothesis-level, not experimentally isolated).

## Methodology

- **Design**: Two-factor (sterilization cycles × number of applications per cycle) repeated-measures bench study; blinded operator split (one applied torque, one recorded peak values); randomized measurement sequence.
- **n**: 15 new F-S MTLDs, five per manufacturer — Astra Tech 25 Ncm (Hader SA, Switzerland), BioHorizons Dynatorq ITL 30 Ncm (Irvine, CA, USA), Dr. Idhe 15–60 Ncm (Eching/Munich, Germany). Target torque 25 Ncm (Astra Tech) and 30 Ncm (BioHorizons, Dr. Idhe). Sample size justified by effect size 0.37 Ncm, SD 0.13.
- **Measurement**: Tohnichi digital torque gauge (manufacturer-calibrated, ±2% of full scale); drivers clamped in a 3-jaw chuck, gauge zeroed, torque applied slowly over 4 s until release at the preset target; peak values recorded.
- **Sterilization**: artificial saliva (Bioxtra) immersion → 15 min disinfection with 2% aldehyde-free non-fixing disinfectant (Deconex 53 plus) → steam autoclave at 134 °C, 0.9 bar vacuum, 18 min. Pre-treatment followed each manufacturer's recommendation: Astra Tech and Dr. Idhe dismantled, cleaned, dried and lubricated at multiple sites then reassembled; BioHorizons lubricated with the bent handle at one site.
- **Outcomes**: signed and absolute difference between delivered and target peak torque (mean, SD, min, max) at 0/1/5/10/20/50/75/100 cycles; frequency of >10% deviation.
- **Statistics**: One-sample t-test of mean difference from target at each cycle; one-way repeated-measures ANOVA with manufacturer as the between-subject comparison (α=0.05).

- All three groups showed a bias toward **under-delivery** of peak torque — the failure mode that lowers preload and therefore compromises implant-abutment mechanical stability.
- Lowest pre-cycling absolute error: Astra Tech 0.44 Ncm at X10; BioHorizons 0.79 Ncm and Dr. Idhe 0.65 Ncm at X0.
- Conclusion as stated by the authors: before 100 sterilization cycles each individual device stayed within 10% of its target torque value, but >10% deviation appeared in varying degrees during those consequent cycles.
