## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of the literature on causes of implant abutment screw loosening, illustrated by one clinical case (45-year-old female, maxillary left central incisor implant crown, Zimmer internal-hex implant 3.7 × 12 mm).

Screw loosening is the second most common cause of implant-restoration failure after loss of osseointegration; inadequate preload was judged the most common upstream cause, compounded by the settling effect (2–10% preload loss within minutes of tightening), a coefficient of friction below ~0.12, external-hex connection geometry, and wide occlusal tables/cantilevers.

The case demonstrates a conservative, crown-preserving management protocol — crown/abutment/screw removed as one unit, cement disintegrated by furnace heating (250°C), screw retightened to 35 Ncm with a 10-minute re-torque to counter settling, same-day recementation — one of three described management routes (conservative removal, access-hole retightening, or destructive sectioning).

임플란트 지대주 나사 풀림의 원인에 대한 서술적 문헌고찰(45세 여성, 상악 좌측 중절치 임플란트 크라운, Zimmer 내부육각 임플란트 3.7×12mm 증례 1건 포함).

## Summary

Abutment screw loosening is the second most common complication of implant-supported restorations after loss of osseointegration, and is especially common in single-implant, external-hex, molar restorations. This review compiles the reported causes — inadequate preload, screw-joint bending resistance, the settling effect, coefficient of friction, screw material, connection geometry, implant number/diameter, and restoration design/occlusal table — and grounds them in a real case: a 45-year-old woman whose maxillary central-incisor implant crown loosened one week after cementation due to inadequate initial torque. The case is used to walk through a **conservative retrieval technique** that removes the crown, abutment, and screw as one unit, disintegrates the cement in a 250°C furnace, retightens the screw with a 10-minute re-torque to counter settling, and recements the same crown same-day — avoiding the need to sacrifice the restoration. The paper frames this alongside two alternative management routes (access-hole retightening, destructive sectioning) that trade off crown preservation against access difficulty.

## Key Contributions

- Assembles the full causal chain behind abutment screw loosening (preload → settling effect → joint-separating force exceeding clamping force) into one applied narrative, anchored by a worked clinical example.
- Provides a **3-method taxonomy for managing a loosened screw** (conservative removal / access-hole retightening / destructive sectioning), each with an explicit crown-preservation outcome.
- Documents a reusable step-by-step conservative retrieval protocol: proximal-contact reduction → intact crown+abutment+screw removal → 250°C furnace cement disintegration → 35 Ncm retightening + 10-min re-torque → same-day recementation.
- Reproduces a compact "factors that increase vs decrease screw-joint load" table useful as a chairside checklist.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative literature review (no stated systematic search strategy or inclusion/exclusion criteria) combined with a single clinical case report.
- **Case**: 45-year-old female; Zimmer (SWB) internal-hex implant, 3.7 mm D × 12 mm L, maxillary left central incisor; mobility from the day after cementation, presenting at 1 week; periapical radiograph showed a fixture–abutment gap, diagnosed as screw loosening.
- **Retrieval protocol**: proximal ceramic contact reduced (tapering fissure + round diamond points) → crown + abutment + connecting screw removed intact from the fixture → 250°C ceramic furnace for 15 min to disintegrate the luting cement → components separated → abutment/screw retightened to 35 Ncm with a torque wrench → retightened again after 10 min (counters the settling effect) → PVS impression → ceramic contact rebuilt on the same crown and glazed → recemented same day.

## Results

**Factors affecting screw-joint load (as tabulated in the paper):**

| Factors increasing load to screw | Factors decreasing load to screw |
|---|---|
| Micro-movement between implant and abutment | Friction-lock design, absence of flat mating surface |
| Inadequate tightening torque | Adequate tightening torque |
| Mismatch between implant and abutment | Reduced coefficient of friction at interface |
| Off-axis load on the prosthesis | Axial load on the prosthesis |
| Reduced size/number of implants | Adequate number and width of implants |
| Increased cantilever | Reduced cantilever |
| Sharp cusp, large occlusal table | Flattened cusp, narrow occlusal table |
| External hex connection | Internal hex connection |

**Cited benchmarks**: optimum preload ≈ 75% of screw yield strength (or 65% of fracture strength); settling/embedment relaxation loses 2–10% of initial preload within seconds to minutes (retighten ~10 min after initial torque); coefficient of friction ≈ 0.12 at the interface maximizes retained preload; external hexagon needs ≥1.2 mm height for optimal antirotational effect.

**3 management methods for a loosened screw:**

| Method | Description | Crown preserved? |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative removal (this case) | Crown + abutment + screw removed together, separated, screw retorqued, crown repaired and recemented | Yes |
| Access-hole retightening | Perforation through crown (labial/occlusal/lingual, ideally recorded at cementation) to reach the screw directly | Depends on access location |
| Destructive sectioning | Vertical cuts through the crown to force separation | No — crown discarded |
