## Three-line Summary

In vitro mechanical study (n=30 implant-retained mandibular molar metal-ceramic restorations via UCLA abutments, 3 groups of 10: SRS = screw-retained with SAH sealed, SRNS = screw-retained with SAH unsealed, CR = cement-retained) comparing static fracture resistance.

SAH sealing protocol: aluminum-oxide air-abrasion → 10% hydrofluoric acid etch → silane coupling agent → bonding agent → cotton pellet filling → P-60 resin composite restoration; CR specimens cemented with RelyX U100; load applied via a 6.0 mm spherical-tip rod on buccal/lingual cusps at 0.5 mm/min crosshead speed until fracture (one-way ANOVA + Dunnett test, p<0.05; failure mode by SEM).

No significant difference in fracture resistance was found between the two screw-retained groups (SRS vs SRNS); the cement-retained (CR) group showed the highest mean fracture resistance — sealing the SAH does not compromise the mechanical strength of screw-retained implant restorations.

## Summary

This in vitro bench study evaluated whether sealing the screw access hole (SAH) affects the fracture resistance of screw-retained implant-supported restorations. Thirty UCLA-abutment-based, implant-retained mandibular molar metal-ceramic restorations were divided into three groups of ten: screw-retained with SAH sealed (SRS), screw-retained with SAH unsealed (SRNS), and cement-retained (CR). The SAH sealing protocol used aluminum-oxide air-abrasion, 10% hydrofluoric acid etching, a silane coupling agent, a bonding agent, a cotton pellet filler, and P-60 resin composite as the final restorative material; CR specimens were cemented with RelyX U100. A vertical static load was applied through a 6.0 mm spherical-tip rod on the buccal and lingual incline cusps at 0.5 mm/min until fracture. No significant difference in fracture resistance was found between SRS and SRNS, while the CR group showed the highest mean fracture resistance. Clinically, this supports sealing the SAH as a safe chairside step that does not weaken the screw-retained restoration.

## Key Contributions

- SAH sealing (vs leaving it unsealed) does not significantly change the fracture resistance of screw-retained restorations
- Documents a specific, reproducible chairside SAH restoration protocol usable as a clinical reference
- Cement-retained restorations retained the highest mean fracture resistance of the three groups in this model

## Methodology

In vitro comparative mechanical study, 3 parallel groups (n=10 each) of implant-retained mandibular molar restorations on UCLA abutments: SRS (SAH sealed), SRNS (SAH unsealed), CR (cement-retained, RelyX U100). SAH restoration protocol: Al2O3 air-abrasion → 10% HF etch → silane → bonding agent → cotton pellet → P-60 composite. Static vertical load via 6.0 mm spherical-tip rod on buccal+lingual incline cusps, crosshead speed 0.5 mm/min, until fracture. Analysis: one-way ANOVA + Dunnett test (p<0.05); failure mode assessed by SEM.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| SRS vs SRNS fracture resistance | No significant difference |
| Highest mean fracture resistance | CR (cement-retained) group |
| SAH sealing effect on SR fracture resistance | No significant influence |
