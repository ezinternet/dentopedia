## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs (3 parallel-arm RCTs, 8 publications, 126 patients, 341 implants) comparing abutment-level (AL, intermediate abutment) versus implant-level (IL) connections in screw-retained implant-supported fixed partial prostheses.

Statistically significant but small reduction in marginal bone loss (MBL) favoring AL at 36 months (MD -0.13 mm; 95% CI -0.23 to -0.03; p=0.01; moderate CoE), and significant probing depth (PD) reduction at 36 months (MD -0.66 mm; p=0.01) and 60 months (MD -0.60 mm; p<0.001); no differences in MBL at 12/60 months, or in bleeding on probing, implant survival, mechanical complications, or patient satisfaction at any time point.

Intermediate abutments improve soft-tissue health (reduced PD) without added complications or failure risk, but the MBL benefit is not clinically relevant — both AL and IL remain viable protocols for multi-unit screw-retained FDPs; evidence base is limited to 3 trials from 2 countries with low-to-moderate certainty (GRADE).

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD42023475778) evaluated whether using an intermediate (abutment-level, AL) prosthetic abutment — versus connecting the screw-retained fixed partial denture (FPD) framework directly at implant level (IL) — affects clinical outcomes in partially edentulous patients. The evidence base consisted of only 3 independent parallel-arm RCTs (Göthberg et al., Sweden; Maceiras et al., Spain; Toia et al., Sweden), reported across 8 publications as longitudinal follow-up extensions, contributing 126 patients and 341 implants (195 AL, 146 IL). All implants were bone-level systems (external or internal conical hexagonal connection) restored with metal-ceramic screw-retained prostheses.

The primary outcome, marginal bone loss (MBL), showed a statistically significant but small reduction favoring AL at 36 months (MD -0.13 mm; 95% CI -0.23 to -0.03; p=0.01) with moderate certainty of evidence, but the difference was judged not clinically relevant; no significant MBL differences appeared at 12 or 60 months. Probing depth (PD), a secondary outcome, showed a clearer AL advantage at 36 months (MD -0.66 mm; p=0.01) and 60 months (MD -0.60 mm; p<0.001), both with low certainty of evidence. Bleeding on probing, implant survival (RD=0.00 at all time points), mechanical complications, and patient-reported satisfaction showed no significant between-group differences at any follow-up. Risk of bias (Cochrane RoB 2) was low across all 8 included reports.

## Key Contributions

1. First SR+MA to isolate the clinical impact of AL vs IL connection specifically in **multi-unit screw-retained FDPs** (as opposed to single-unit restorations), where passive-fit and biomechanical demands differ substantially.
2. Correctly consolidates 8 publications into 3 underlying RCTs (treating longitudinal follow-up extensions as single trials with multiple time points), avoiding unit-of-analysis error/double counting common in living-evidence syntheses.
3. Provides GRADE-rated Summary of Findings tables at 12, 36, and 60 months across all outcomes, giving clinicians an explicit certainty-of-evidence context rather than raw p-values alone.
4. Notes all included studies had low risk of bias (RoB 2, all 5 domains) — a clean evidence base despite its small size (3 trials).

## Methodology

- PRISMA 2020 + Cochrane Handbook methodology; PROSPERO-registered (CRD42023475778).
- Search across MEDLINE (PubMed), Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane CENTRAL, EMBASE (up to 2025-05-22), plus manual reference screening and Google Scholar; no date/language restriction.
- PICOS: partially edentulous adults; screw-retained FDPs with AL vs IL connection; ≥6 months follow-up RCTs; outcomes = MBL (primary), PD/BoP/survival/mechanical complications/PROMs (secondary).
- Two independent reviewers screened titles/abstracts and full texts (κ=0.89 then κ=1.00); two independent reviewers extracted data; third-reviewer arbitration for both stages.
- Random-effects meta-analysis (DerSimonian-Laird or inverse-variance) with weighted mean differences (continuous outcomes) or risk differences (dichotomous outcomes); heterogeneity via Cochran's Q / I²; analyses at both patient level and implant level.
- Risk of bias via Cochrane RoB 2; certainty of evidence via GRADE with SoF tables; leave-one-out sensitivity analysis on the primary outcome (MBL).

- Study selection: 757 → 416 screened → 13 full-text → 8 included reports from 3 RCTs (Sweden ×2, Spain ×1).
- 195 AL implants vs 146 IL implants; bone-level systems (Brånemark Mark III external; Ticare Inhex and OsseoSpeed EV internal conical hexagonal); all metal-ceramic screw-retained.
- Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis confirmed robustness of the primary MBL estimates.
- Proposed mechanism: AL abutments reposition the implant-abutment micro-gap coronally away from the alveolar crest, potentially reducing microbial-induced inflammatory bone resorption and preserving biological width via the "one abutment, one time" principle (minimizing abutment dis/reconnection).
