## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study, 20 titanium CAD/CAM molar implant abutments with convex emergence profile, margin positions simulated at 0/1/2/3 mm below a silicone gingival mask, zirconia crowns cemented with zinc oxide non-eugenol (Temp Bond NE) or methacrylate (Panavia V5) cement, cement remnant area quantified by digital-photograph planimetry.

Cement remnants were found at every margin depth and nearly every abutment surface, increasing as the margin was placed more submucosally; lingual surfaces were significantly more prone to residue with Panavia V5 at 3 mm depth (p=0.0291); basal (undercut) abutment surface residue was significantly lower with Panavia V5 (4.9%±3.7%) than Temp Bond NE (8.6%±5.5%, p=0.007), with no significant difference between cements on lateral surfaces.

Clinical implication: CAD/CAM molar abutment margins should be placed as coronally as possible; if subgingival placement is unavoidable for esthetics, margin depth should not exceed 1.5 mm in proximal/oral regions to limit undetected cement excess, a known driver of peri-implant inflammation and marginal bone loss (MBL) — key limitation is the in-vitro silicone-mask model cannot fully replicate the peri-implant sulcus or clinical cementation conditions.

## 세줄요약

시험관내 (in-vitro) 연구, 티타늄 CAD/CAM 구치부 임플란트 어버트먼트 20개, 실리콘 치은 마스크로 변연 (margin) 위치를 점막하 0/1/2/3mm로 재현, 지르코니아 크라운을 산화아연비유지놀 (Temp Bond NE) 또는 메타크릴레이트 (Panavia V5) 시멘트로 접착 후 잔류 시멘트 면적을 디지털 사진 평면측정법으로 정량.

변연이 점막하로 깊어질수록 모든 깊이·거의 모든 표면에서 잔류 시멘트가 검출됐고, 설측 (lingual) 면은 Panavia V5 3mm 깊이에서 유의하게 더 취약했으며 (p=0.0291), 기저부 (basal) 표면 잔류율은 Panavia V5 (4.9%±3.7%)가 Temp Bond NE (8.6%±5.5%)보다 유의하게 낮았음 (p=0.007), 측면 표면에서는 두 시멘트 간 유의차 없음.

임상적 의미: CAD/CAM 구치부 어버트먼트 변연은 최대한 치은연상 (coronal)에 위치시켜야 하며, 심미상 불가피하게 점막하로 위치시킬 경우 근원심·설측 (proximal/oral) 영역에서 1.5mm를 초과하지 않아야 미검출 잔류 시멘트 — 임플란트주위염·변연골소실 (marginal bone loss, MBL)의 알려진 위험인자 — 를 줄일 수 있음; 핵심 한계는 실리콘 마스크 모델이 실제 임플란트주위 열구·임상 접착 조건을 완전히 재현하지 못한다는 점.

## Summary

Undetected residual cement is a recognized driver of peri-implant biofilm accumulation, sulcular bleeding/suppuration, and downstream marginal bone loss (MBL) around cement-retained implant restorations. Most prior evidence on cement-excess risk vs margin depth was generated on prefabricated **stock** abutments; this study is among the first to isolate the effect specifically for **CAD/CAM custom molar abutments** with a convex emergence profile, where the individually designable submucosal contour was hypothesized (but not previously tested) to reduce cement-trapping risk.

Twenty titanium CAD/CAM abutments were manufactured at four controlled margin depths (0, 1, 2, 3 mm below a simulated mucosal margin), and monolithic zirconia crowns were cemented onto them using either a provisional zinc oxide non-eugenol cement (Temp Bond NE) or a definitive methacrylate resin cement (Panavia V5). After simulated clinical cleanup (scaler + super-floss), specimens were disassembled and cement-covered surface area was quantified by digital-photograph planimetry across all four abutment surfaces (mesial, buccal, distal, lingual) plus the abutment's basal (undercut) aspect.

Cement remnants were detected at essentially every margin depth and surface studied — confirming that CAD/CAM custom design flexibility does **not** eliminate the residual-cement problem, though depth still matters directionally (more remnant with deeper submucosal margins). The lingual surface was the single statistically significant location-specific finding (p=0.0291, Panavia V5 at 3 mm), consistent with the general clinical experience that oral/proximal surfaces are hardest to visually access and clean. Cement type mattered specifically at the basal (undercut) surface, where Panavia V5 left significantly less residue than Temp Bond NE — plausibly reflecting differences in viscosity/flow or resin polymerization behavior versus the softer, more washable provisional cement — but the two cements performed equivalently at the more clinically visible lateral surfaces.

The authors' bottom-line clinical recommendation: locate CAD/CAM molar abutment margins as coronally as possible, and if subgingival placement cannot be avoided for esthetic reasons, do not exceed 1.5 mm depth in the proximal and oral (lingual/palatal) regions — the surfaces shown here to be hardest to clean.

## Key Contributions

- First controlled quantification of undetected cement remnant area on **CAD/CAM custom molar abutments** (vs the stock-abutment literature that preceded it) across a systematic range of margin depths (0–3 mm).
- Direct within-specimen comparison of a provisional (zinc oxide non-eugenol) vs a definitive (methacrylate resin) luting cement for residue propensity, at both lateral and hard-to-access basal abutment surfaces.
- Demonstrates that cement collects not only at the crown-abutment margin but also underneath the convex abutment body itself — an area invisible and inaccessible to conventional cleaning instruments, and not addressed by prior margin-depth-only studies.
- Provides an actionable depth threshold (1.5 mm subgingival, proximal/oral regions) directly usable in restorative treatment planning.

## Methodology

- 20 titanium CAD/CAM implant abutments (BEGO Medical GmbH), internal taper connection/internal hex, convex emergence profile, virtually designed (Implant Studio, 3Shape) at margin depths of 0, 1, 2, and 3 mm below a simulated mucosal margin; based on a duplicated clinical master cast (maxillary first molar implant site).
- Peri-implant soft tissue simulated with polyvinyl siloxane (silicone) gingival masks, replicated separately for each of the 4 margin depths.
- 20 corresponding monolithic zirconia crowns (Zirlux, Cerec 3D) with occlusal access openings for abutment-screw protection/retrieval.
- Two sequential cementation trials on the same 20 abutments, with thorough interim cleaning/decontamination: Trial 1 = Temp Bond NE (zinc oxide non-eugenol, provisional); Trial 2 = Panavia V5 (methacrylate, definitive resin).
- Cementation by one experienced clinician; cement-excess cleanup by a second, blinded investigator (steel scaler + super-floss), simulating real clinical technique rather than an idealized removal protocol.
- Cement remnant quantification via computerized planimetry (Linkevicius et al. method): standardized macro digital photographs of all 4 quadrants per specimen, cement-covered vs total surface area measured in Adobe Photoshop; intra-examiner calibration agreement 0.96.
- Statistics: Mann-Whitney-U and Kruskal-Wallis H tests, α=0.05.

## Results

| Comparison | Finding |
|---|---|
| Temp Bond NE, margin depth vs lateral-surface residue | Trend up 0→2mm, slight decrease at 3mm; not significant overall (p=0.1520) or per-surface (all p>0.05) |
| Temp Bond NE, margin depth vs basal residue | Not significant (p=0.336) |
| Panavia V5, margin depth vs lateral-surface residue | Not significant at buccal/mesial/distal (p=0.086–0.968); **significant at lingual, 3mm depth (p=0.0291)** |
| Panavia V5, margin depth vs basal residue | Not significant (p=0.9846) |
| Cement type, basal-surface residue | Panavia V5 4.9%±3.7% vs Temp Bond NE 8.6%±5.5% — **significant, Panavia lower (p=0.007)** |
| Cement type, lateral-surface residue (sum) | No significant difference (p=0.398) |

Overall pattern: cement remnants were essentially ubiquitous across depths/surfaces for both cements; the two cements were clinically equivalent at visible lateral surfaces but Panavia V5 (definitive resin) left less residue at the inaccessible basal/undercut surface.
