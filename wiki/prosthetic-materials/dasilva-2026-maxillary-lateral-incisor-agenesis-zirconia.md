## Three-line Summary

Mixed-methods study combining 3D finite element analysis (FEA) of 10 simulated implant-abutment models (control vs atrophic anterior maxilla; titanium/titanium [TT], titanium/hybrid [TH], zirconia/zirconia [ZZ] configurations at 3.0/3.5 mm diameters) with a PRISMA systematic review of 25 clinical studies (2011–Dec 2024) on implant-supported crowns for maxillary lateral incisor agenesis (MLIA).

FEA showed all implant/abutment configurations had failure risk below 0.7 (low mechanical failure risk), but narrower diameter and atrophic bone increased peri-implant strain energy density (SED) beyond the pathological threshold in nearly all models; the SR found 19/25 clinical studies reported successful outcomes, yet none used zirconia implants clinically.

Regular-diameter (3.5 mm) titanium implants with hybrid zirconia-over-titanium-base abutments offered the most favorable combined biomechanical/esthetic profile; zirconia implants, despite numerically low FEA failure risk, remain clinically unvalidated for MLIA due to low fracture toughness and absence of long-term survival data.

## Summary

This study addresses a clinical decision point in maxillary lateral incisor agenesis (MLIA) rehabilitation: whether narrow-diameter and/or zirconia implants can safely substitute for regular-diameter titanium in the anatomically constrained lateral incisor position. Using matched 3D FEA models of control and atrophic (MLIA-representative) anterior maxilla, the authors simulated 10 implant-abutment configurations — titanium/titanium (TT), titanium/hybrid zirconia-over-Ti-base (TH), and zirconia/zirconia (ZZ) — at 3.0 and 3.5 mm diameters (ZZ tested only at 3.5 mm, the narrowest commercially available screw-retained zirconia implant). In parallel, a PRISMA/PICOS systematic review of 25 clinical studies (2011–Dec 2024) evaluated real-world outcomes of implant-supported crowns for MLIA, explicitly checking whether clinical evidence corroborates the in silico predictions.

The FEA found all configurations mechanically safe under a 100 N oblique load (failure risk < 0.7 of critical stress for both titanium yield strength and zirconia ultimate tensile strength), but peri-implant bone strain energy density (SED) frequently exceeded the pathological threshold (109.6 μJ/mm³, derived from Frost's Mechanostat), especially with narrower diameters and in atrophic bone — only the ZZ group in the non-atrophic control model stayed below threshold. The systematic review corroborated general implant reliability in MLIA (19/25 studies positive) but revealed that no clinical study to date has actually used a zirconia implant (only zirconia abutments on titanium implants), leaving the FEA's favorable ZZ mechanical numbers clinically unverified.

## Key Contributions

- Matched FEA + systematic review design directly testing consistency between in silico biomechanical predictions and clinical outcomes for MLIA implant rehabilitation.
- Three-way implant-abutment material comparison (TT/TH/ZZ) rather than the more common single-material FEA comparison.
- Introduces the Peri-Implant Bone Resorption Risk Index (PIBRri): mean of the 10 highest peri-implant SED values divided by a pathological SED threshold (109.6 μJ/mm³), classified as low (<0.8), medium (0.8–1.0), or high (>1.0) resorption risk.
- Confirms via systematic review (25 studies, 2011–Dec 2024) that zero clinical MLIA studies have used zirconia implants — establishing the evidence gap the FEA component is designed to probe.

## Methodology

**FEA**: Two 3D anterior-maxilla models — Control (C, IRB-approved CT-derived) and Atrophic (A, modified to MLIA-characteristic reduced buccal bone thickness). Modeled with Blue Sky Plan → MeshMixer → Rhinoceros 7 → MSC.Apex (mesh, ~730k–758k elements) → MSC.MarcMentat (loading/BCs). Materials: linear, elastic, homogeneous, isotropic. Groups: TT, TH, ZZ; diameters 3.0/3.5 mm (ZZ 3.5 mm only); 13 mm implant length; lithium disilicate crowns. 100 N oblique load (45°) split at two palatal contact points simulating a 2 mm overbite. Boundary conditions: proximal/superior surfaces fully constrained; all interfaces bonded (100% osseointegration assumed). Failure risk = max component stress ÷ material critical stress (von Mises/yield strength for titanium; max principal stress/ultimate tensile strength for zirconia).

**Systematic review**: Cochrane Oral Health Group Handbook methodology, PICOS framework, PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science search (2011–Dec 2024), dual independent screening, PRISMA flow (1096 → 575 after dedup → 94 full-text → 25 included).

## Results

- All FEA configurations: failure risk < 0.7 (low mechanical failure risk under the simulated load).
- Atrophic maxilla and narrower (3.0 mm) diameter increased SED; TT groups had higher SED than TH and ZZ groups.
- All models exceeded the pathological SED threshold (109.6 μJ/mm³) except the ZZ group in the control (non-atrophic) maxilla.
- Zirconia abutments (TH/ZZ) concentrated stress on the palatal side only; titanium abutments (TT) on both buccal and palatal sides.
- Zirconia's fracture toughness (~8 MPa·m^1/2) is far lower than titanium's (60.4–85.8 MPa·m^1/2), making zirconia components more prone to catastrophic subcritical crack propagation despite lower numeric FEA failure-risk values.
- SR: 25 studies included (16 case reports, plus cohorts/trials); implant diameters ranged 1.8–4.8 mm; 19/25 reported successful outcomes; 20/25 used prior orthodontic space opening; 8/25 used bone/soft-tissue augmentation; zero used zirconia implants clinically (only zirconia abutments on titanium implants were reported, e.g., in a documented abutment fracture case at 6 weeks post-placement due to excessive angulation).

**Clinical implication**: Regular-diameter (3.5 mm) titanium implants with hybrid (zirconia-over-titanium-base) abutments currently represent the best-supported choice for MLIA rehabilitation, balancing biomechanical reliability with esthetic benefit. Narrow-diameter and all-zirconia implant options may be biomechanically plausible per FEA but lack corroborating long-term clinical survival data — orthodontic space opening or bone augmentation should be prioritized over resorting to narrow implants where feasible.
