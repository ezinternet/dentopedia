## Three-line Summary

Multicentre RCT, 56 edentulous patients (280 implants) across three centres, randomized to 4-implant (4-I) or 6-implant (6-I) groups supported by a titanium CAD/CAM milled framework fixed complete denture (FCD) in the maxilla, followed for 3 years.

Implant survival was 100% (4-I) vs 99% (6-I); 3-year marginal bone level (MBL) change was not significantly different between groups despite significant absolute MBL differences at baseline and 3 years (4-I: 0.30±0.50→0.24±0.38 mm; 6-I: 0.14±0.32→0.12±0.26 mm); technical/biological complications were low (1.6%/6.0%) but prosthetic complications (mainly tooth chipping/fracture) affected 47.2% of FCDs.

Four implants are a feasible, cost-effective alternative to six for maxillary full-arch fixed rehabilitation avoiding bone augmentation, but prosthetic maintenance visits should be anticipated regardless of implant number.

## Summary

This multicentre (3-centre) RCT randomized 56 edentulous patients (280 implants total) to receive a maxillary implant-supported fixed complete denture (FCD) on a titanium CAD/CAM milled framework, supported by either 4 implants (4-I) or 6 implants (6-I). The primary outcome was change in marginal bone level (MBL) over 3 years of function. Implant survival was very high in both groups (100% 4-I, 99% 6-I). Considering clustering effects (implants nested within patients), the 3-year MBL *change* did not differ significantly between the two implant-number groups, even though absolute MBL values differed significantly between groups at baseline and at 3 years. Clinical parameters showed no significant between-group differences at any timepoint. Technical (1.6%) and biological (6.0%) complication rates were low; the dominant complication category was prosthetic — chipping/fracture of prosthetic teeth affected nearly half (47.2%) of the fixed complete dentures. The authors conclude that a 4-implant approach is a feasible, more cost-effective option when bone augmentation is to be avoided, while flagging that patients on either protocol should be counselled about the likelihood of prosthetic maintenance visits.

## Key Contributions

- Randomizes implant number (4 vs 6) within a single standardized prosthetic protocol (titanium CAD/CAM milled framework FCD) across 3 centres, isolating the implant-number variable from framework-material/design confounds common in retrospective full-arch comparisons.
- Reports 3-year MBL, survival, and complication benchmarks that serve as the early-timepoint companion to a subsequent 5-year follow-up of the same cohort.
- Highlights prosthetic tooth chipping/fracture (47.2% of prostheses) — not implant-number-related biological complications — as the dominant clinical burden in maxillary full-arch fixed rehabilitation, regardless of 4-I vs 6-I allocation.

## Methodology

- Prospective, multicentre (3-centre), randomized controlled trial.
- 56 edentulous patients, 280 implants, randomized to 4-implant or 6-implant groups supporting a maxillary FCD on a titanium CAD/CAM milled framework.
- Radiographic MBL and clinical periodontal/peri-implant parameters assessed at baseline, 1 year, and 3 years.
- Statistical analysis accounted for clustering (multiple implants per patient).

## Results

| Outcome | 4-Implant (4-I) | 6-Implant (6-I) |
|---|---|---|
| Implant survival | 100% | 99% |
| MBL at baseline | 0.30 ± 0.50 mm | 0.14 ± 0.32 mm |
| MBL at 1 year | 0.24 ± 0.31 mm | 0.16 ± 0.35 mm |
| MBL at 3 years | 0.24 ± 0.38 mm | 0.12 ± 0.26 mm |

- 3-year MBL change: not significantly different between 4-I and 6-I (clustering-adjusted); absolute MBL significantly different between groups at baseline and 3 years.
- Clinical parameters: no significant between-group differences at any timepoint.
- Technical complications: 1.6%. Biological complications: 6.0%.
- Prosthetic complications (mainly tooth chipping/fracture): 47.2% of FCDs (25/53 evaluable).
