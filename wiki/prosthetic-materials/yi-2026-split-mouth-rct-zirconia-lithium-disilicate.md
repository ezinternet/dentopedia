## Three-line Summary

Split-mouth RCT (n=40 patients, 80 implants) compared monolithic CAD-CAM ZrO₂ vs lithium disilicate (LS) crowns on customized Ti abutments at bilateral posterior implant sites over 3 years.
ΔMBL: ZrO₂ −0.62 mm vs LS −0.36 mm; paired difference 0.26 mm (95% CI −0.17 to 0.70) — non-inferiority demonstrated; no crown fractures, chipping, or debonding in either group.
Monolithic LS crowns are non-inferior to ZrO₂ for posterior single implant restorations at 3 years; biological complications were numerically higher with LS (15% vs 5%) but not significant.

## Summary

This prospective split-mouth RCT enrolled patients with bilateral single-tooth implants at homologous posterior sites, randomizing each implant to receive either a monolithic CAD-CAM zirconia (ZrO₂, control) or monolithic CAD-CAM lithium disilicate (LS, test) crown, both cemented onto customized titanium abutments. At 3-year follow-up (40 patients completing), marginal bone level change was the primary outcome, with a prespecified non-inferiority margin of −0.5 mm. The paired difference in ΔMBL (LS−ZrO₂) was 0.26 mm (95% CI −0.17 to 0.70), with the lower CI bound exceeding −0.5 mm, confirming non-inferiority of LS. Notably, neither material experienced crown fractures, chipping, or debonding. Biological complication rates were 15% (LS) vs 5% (ZrO₂), a clinically notable but statistically non-significant difference at this sample size. Both materials demonstrated comparable esthetic and functional scores (FIPS).

## Key Contributions

- Established non-inferiority of monolithic LS vs ZrO₂ for posterior implant crowns based on 3-year ΔMBL data
- Both materials: 0 crown fractures, chipping, or debonding — monolithic design eliminates veneering-related technical failures
- Biological complication rate difference (LS 15% vs ZrO₂ 5%) warrants longer follow-up; mechanism unclear
- Split-mouth design controls for patient-level factors, providing high internal validity
