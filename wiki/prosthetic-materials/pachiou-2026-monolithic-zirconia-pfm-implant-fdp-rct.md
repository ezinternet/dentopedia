## Three-line Summary

Prospective RCT (n=55 patients; 45 completing 3 years) at University of Zurich compared 3-unit posterior iFDPs in monolithic zirconia (MonoZR, n=22) vs porcelain-fused-to-metal (PFM, n=23); primary outcome was ceramic fracture.
Veneering ceramic chipping: PFM 13% (3/23) vs MonoZR 0% (0/22), p=0.11; 1 prosthesis failure per group (both implant loss); peri-implant parameters and MBL change did not differ significantly between groups.
Monolithic ZrO₂ iFDPs show zero chipping and comparable clinical outcomes to PFM at 3 years, supporting their use as an alternative for posterior multi-unit implant restorations.

## Summary

This single-center RCT from the University of Zurich randomized partially edentulous patients requiring a posterior 3-unit iFDP on two implants to receive either monolithic zirconia (MonoZR) or porcelain-fused-to-metal (PFM) prostheses. At 3-year follow-up (45 patients), the primary outcome of ceramic fracture was not significantly different between groups, but the numerical finding was clinically meaningful: 13% chipping in PFM vs 0% in MonoZR. Two prosthesis failures occurred (one per group), both due to implant loss, confirming equivalent prosthesis-level outcomes. Peri-implant parameters increased over time but did not differ between groups; probing depth and MBL remained stable. Both groups met USPHS acceptability criteria. The 3-year data support MonoZR as a promising, chipping-free alternative to PFM for posterior implant-supported multi-unit restorations.

## Key Contributions

- Randomized direct comparison of MonoZR vs PFM posterior 3-unit iFDPs with 3-year follow-up
- Zero chipping with monolithic zirconia eliminates the dominant PFM technical complication
- Comparable peri-implant biology (MBL, probing depth, bleeding) between materials
- Primary outcome (ceramic fracture) not statistically different — sample powered for biological outcomes, not complications
- Supports guideline recommendation of MonoZR for posterior implant FDPs
