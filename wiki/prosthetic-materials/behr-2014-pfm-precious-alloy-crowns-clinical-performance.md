## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort of 997 PFM precious alloy single crowns inserted 1984–2009 (median follow-up 4.33 years) using Kaplan-Meier analysis to quantify chipping, recurrent caries, periodontitis, and loss of retention rates over 10 years.

5-year survival: anterior 96.4%, posterior 97.5%; 10-year survival: anterior 92.3%, posterior 95.9%; chipping rare (1.7%, mainly early); periodontitis dominant long-term risk (10-year free-of-PE rate 72.2%), increasing continuously with service time.

PFM crowns demonstrate excellent long-term survival; chipping and loss of retention are predominantly early complications, while periodontitis risk accumulates continuously with age and service duration — supporting peri-implant hygiene focus regardless of crown type.

## Summary

Behr et al. 2014 provides one of the largest single-center PFM cohort data sets with up to 25 years of follow-up. Using Kaplan-Meier analysis on 997 crowns placed under a standardized protocol, the study demonstrated that PFM precious alloy crowns achieve excellent long-term survival. The key clinical finding was the differential timing of complications: technical failures (chipping, loss of retention) occur predominantly in the first few years and then plateau, while biological complications — especially periodontitis — accumulate continuously throughout the restoration's lifetime. The 10-year free-of-PE rate of 72.2% emphasizes that any fixed prosthesis demands sustained periodontal maintenance.

## Key Contributions

- Large single-center PFM data (n=997) with up to 25-year follow-up under standardized protocol
- Established PFM chipping rate (1.7%) and loss of retention pattern for precise comparison with all-ceramic data
- Demonstrated that periodontitis — not material failure — is the dominant long-term clinical risk
- Identified risk factors: bruxism, antagonist type, crown location influence chipping/LR timing
- 10-year free-of-PE rate 72.2% highlights long-term peri-prosthetic periodontal vulnerability

## Methodology

Retrospective cohort. 997 PFM precious alloy SCs, standardized treatment protocol, University Medical Center Regensburg. Period: January 1984 – May 2009. Median follow-up 4.33 years (inverse Kaplan-Meier). Outcomes: chipping/facing failure, recurrent caries (RC), periodontitis (PE), loss of retention (LR). Risk factors analyzed: bruxism, antagonist type (metal vs ceramic vs natural), crown location (anterior/posterior, maxilla/mandible). abstract-only — publisher landing/abstract page, full text not retrieved.

## Results

| Outcome | 5-year rate | 10-year rate |
|---|---|---|
| Survival (anterior) | 96.4% | 92.3% |
| Survival (posterior) | 97.5% | 95.9% |
| Free of chipping (anterior) | 98.9% | 98.9% |
| Free of chipping (posterior) | 98.2% | 97.3% |
| Free of recurrent caries | 98.7% | 97.2% |
| Free of periodontitis | 85.8% | 72.2% |
| Free of LR (anterior) | 92.2% | 92.2% |
| Free of LR (posterior) | 97.1% | 97.1% |

Chipping 1.7% total; RC 1.3%; PE 14.4%. Chipping/LR: predominantly early. PE: progressive accumulation with time.
