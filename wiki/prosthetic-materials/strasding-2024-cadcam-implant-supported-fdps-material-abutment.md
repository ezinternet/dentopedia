## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Clin Oral Implants Res 2024;35:984–999, Geneva/Tufts) synthesizing current evidence for CAD/CAM abutment and restorative material selection in implant-supported single crowns and multi-unit FDPs in partially edentulous patients.

Ti-base abutments recommended for single crowns (metallic connection stability + ceramic esthetic potential); posterior zone = monolithic zirconia (3–5Y mol%) or lithium disilicate; anterior = micro-veneered ceramic; LD limited to single crowns while zirconia (3–5Y mol%) extends to multi-unit and cantilever FDPs.

Clinical decision requires matching zirconia type to mechanical demand — high-translucency zirconia (4–5Y, more yttria) has significantly lower mechanical strength and is not indicated for all-span lengths and regions.

## 세줄요약

CAD/CAM 임플란트 지지 고정성 보철물의 지대주·수복 재료 선택 최신 서술 고찰(Geneva/Tufts, 2024): 단일 크라운은 Ti-base 지대주 권장(금속 연결부 강도 + 세라믹 심미성 결합), 구치부 = 모놀리식 지르코니아/LD, 전치부 = 마이크로베니어 세라믹.

LD는 단단관에만 제한, 지르코니아(3–5Y mol%)는 다단위 및 캔틸레버 FDP까지 확장 가능.

고투명 지르코니아(4–5Y, 이트리아↑)는 기계적 강도 유의하게 낮아 모든 부위·스팬에 적합하지 않음 — 지르코니아 선택 시 임상 적응증 엄격히 고려 필요.

## Summary

Strasding et al. 2024 provides a comprehensive, clinician-oriented framework for selecting CAD/CAM abutments and restorative materials for implant-supported FDPs. The review reflects the rapid evolution of digital workflows and new material generations. Ti-base abutments have emerged as the dominant paradigm for single implant restorations, offering the mechanical reliability of titanium at the implant connection while allowing full ceramic crowns for esthetics. For multi-unit restorations, conical Ti-base systems designed for bridges accommodate divergent implant axes. Material selection guidance emphasizes region-dependent decision-making: posterior stability favors monolithic designs (zirconia, LD), while anterior esthetics may justify micro-veneered ceramics. A critical practical warning: zirconia selection must account for yttria content — higher-translucency types (4Y, 5Y) have substantially lower mechanical strength and are contraindicated for high-stress multi-unit spans.

## Key Contributions

- Ti-base abutments as preferred for single crowns: combines metallic connection stability with esthetic ceramic crowns
- Conical Ti-base bridge designs for multi-unit: compensates for implant axis divergence
- Region-based material decision: posterior monolithic vs anterior micro-veneered
- LD vs zirconia: LD is single-crown only; zirconia spans all indication types (3–5Y mol%)
- Critical caution: 4Y and 5Y zirconia = more translucent but significantly lower strength — not for all spans/regions

## Methodology

Narrative review. Authors: University of Geneva Division of Fixed Prosthodontics + Tufts University Department of Prosthodontics. Covers provisional restorations (PMMA, bis-acrylic) and definitive CAD/CAM materials for partially edentulous patients receiving implant-supported single crowns and FDPs.

## Results

**Abutment selection — single crowns:**
- Ti-base abutments: preferred (various geometries and heights)
- Customized titanium: still indicated for specific clinical scenarios (deep margin, complex emergence profile)

**Abutment selection — multi-unit FDPs:**
- Conical Ti-base bridge systems: recommended for divergent implant axes
- One-piece customized titanium: complex multi-unit cases

**Restorative material — posterior:**
- Monolithic zirconia (3–5Y mol%) or LD: first choice; avoids veneering chipping risk

**Restorative material — anterior:**
- Micro-veneered ceramic: optimal esthetics
- LD: single crowns only; zirconia 3–5Y mol%: single + multi-unit + cantilever

**Key warning:** Increased yttria content (4–5Y) = higher translucency + significantly lower mechanical strength — not indicated for all regions or span lengths.
