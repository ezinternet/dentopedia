## Three-line Summary

Prospective pharmacokinetic study (n=14 subjects, 41 procedures; Anesth Analg 2016) establishing dose-safety thresholds for tumescent lidocaine anesthesia: 28 mg/kg without liposuction and 45 mg/kg with liposuction carry <1/2000 risk of exceeding the 6 μg/mL mild toxicity threshold.

Strong linear Cmax-dose relationship (r²=0.85 without liposuction); Tmax ≈13 h (slow absorption from subcutaneous depot + epinephrine-induced vasoconstriction = fundamentally different kinetics from dental carpule injection); liposuction removes ~28% of lidocaine before systemic absorption.

The FDA's 7 mg/kg limit was established for epidural anesthesia in 1948 without PK data; this study provides the first prospective dose-concentration evidence, showing doses up to 28 mg/kg without liposuction remain well below the 6 μg/mL toxicity threshold.

## 세줄요약

전향적 약동학 연구 (n=14, 41회 시술; Anesth Analg 2016) — 리포 없이 28 mg/kg, 리포 포함 45 mg/kg에서 경증 독성 역치(6 μg/mL) 초과 위험 <1/2000.

Cmax-용량 선형 관계 r²=0.85 (리포 없음); Tmax ≈13h — 피하 데포에서 지연 흡수 + 에피네프린 혈관수축 → 치과 카폴 주사와 완전히 다른 동역학; 지방흡입은 전신흡수 전 리도케인 28% 제거.

FDA 7 mg/kg 한계는 1948년 경막외마취 기준(PK 근거 없음); 이 연구가 최초의 전향적 용량-혈중농도 증거 — 리포 없이 28 mg/kg까지 역치 하회 확인.

## Toxicity Threshold and Definition

**6 μg/mL** = threshold for **mild lidocaine toxicity onset**:
- Symptoms: lightheadedness, perioral numbness, metallic taste, tinnitus, blurred vision, nystagmus, ataxia, slurred speech, confusion
- Progression beyond 6 μg/mL: seizures → cardiovascular collapse → cardiac arrest
- Karatassas 1993 uses 5 mg/L as "toxicity threshold" — both reflect the same continuum; 6 μg/mL is mild onset, 5 mg/L is the older conservative reference

## Dose-Concentration Formula

Linear regression (without liposuction, r²=0.85):

**Cmax (μg/mL) = k × dose (mg/kg)**

Where k ≈ 0.065–0.070 (estimated from study data; exact slope not published — tolerance interval tables provided instead)

**Tolerance interval results (99% confidence, without liposuction)**:

| Dose (mg/kg) | Risk of Cmax ≥6 μg/mL | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| 22.5 | Negligible | Safe |
| 28 | <1/2000 | Nonsignificant risk |
| 35 | Very low | Low risk |
| 45 | ~1/80 | Significant risk |
| 55 | Higher | Not recommended |

**With liposuction**: 45 mg/kg → <1/2000; 52 mg/kg → low risk

## Key Pharmacokinetic Parameters

| Parameter | Without Liposuction | With Liposuction |
|---|---|---|
| Dose range tested | 19.2–45 mg/kg | 19.4–52 mg/kg |
| Cmax range | 1.2–4.4 μg/mL | 0.97–3.8 μg/mL |
| Mean Cmax (at 45 mg/kg) | 2.9 μg/mL | 2.38 μg/mL |
| Tmax | ~13.1 h (range 8–24) | ~12.5 h (range 8–18) |
| AUC∞ (at 45 mg/kg) | 56.2 μg·h/mL | 40.7 μg·h/mL |
| Cmax-dose relationship | Linear r²=0.85 | r²=0.36 (weaker) |

## Why Tumescent Kinetics Differ from Dental Injection

| Factor | Tumescent (Klein 2016) | Dental carpule injection |
|---|---|---|
| Concentration | Dilute: ≤1 g/L (0.09%) | Concentrated: 2% (20 g/L) |
| Volume | 2–4 L over hours | 1.8 mL bolus |
| Absorption rate | Very slow (12–13h to peak) | Fast (10–30 min to peak) |
| Epinephrine | 1:1,000,000 (vasoconstricts for hours) | 1:100,000 (briefer vasoconstriction) |
| Dose per kg | Up to 45–55 mg/kg | ≤7 mg/kg (FDA limit) |

**Clinical lesson**: The safety of 28 mg/kg tumescent lidocaine does NOT mean dental doses can be increased beyond 7 mg/kg. Different route, different pharmacokinetics, different safety profile.

## Drug Interactions (Reduce Safe Dose)

CYP3A4 and CYP1A2 inhibitors slow lidocaine hepatic metabolism → higher Cmax / longer t½:
- Erythromycin, sertraline, fluconazole, ciprofloxacin
- Propofol (CYP3A4 inhibition)
- General anesthesia

**Clinical action**: reduce tumescent dose in patients on these medications. Same principle applies to dental LA: patients on CYP inhibitors may accumulate lidocaine faster.

## Clinical Insights

1. **The 6 μg/mL threshold is the universal reference** for lidocaine toxicity in the literature; use this as the working number for any lidocaine concentration calculation.

2. **Safety margin for dental injection**: Moore 2008 (dental carpule PK) shows Cmax of dental doses is far below 6 μg/mL. The concern in dentistry is inadvertent intravascular injection (bypasses absorption delay), not cumulative dose toxicity in non-compromised patients.

3. **Tolerance interval analysis** (not simple range) is the correct statistical approach for estimating maximum safe dose in pharmacokinetic studies — accounts for inter-individual variation at a specified confidence level.

4. **Historical FDA limit**: the 7 mg/kg limit for infiltration anesthesia has no supporting PK data for infiltration — it was extrapolated from epidural dosing. This study provides the empirical basis for a higher tumescent-specific limit, not a higher dental limit.
