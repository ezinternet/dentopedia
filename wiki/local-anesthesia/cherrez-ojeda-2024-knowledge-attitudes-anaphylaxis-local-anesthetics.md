## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional web survey (snowball sampling, Nov 2021–Feb 2022) of board-certified dentists in 10 Latin American countries; 507 completed of 704 reached (72.0% response), 480 analyzed, 81.9% from Ecuador, mean age 35.

Lidocaine was the most-used LA (88.1%); 85.2% recognized dyspnea as an anaphylaxis symptom but only 50.1% knew epinephrine is given intramuscularly, 43.5% stocked epinephrine in their emergency kit (22.1% had no emergency drugs at all), and only 9.6% felt confident managing an anaphylactic reaction despite 63.3% rating it extremely important; older age and longer experience were inversely associated with both knowledge (age OR 0.97, p=0.003) and epinephrine possession (age OR 0.96, p=0.0001).

The wide gap between perceived importance and actual readiness identifies a concrete training need — recognition, correct IM epinephrine, and a properly stocked emergency kit — though an Ecuador-heavy, non-probability sample and an unvalidated instrument limit generalizability.

## Summary

[확인] This cross-sectional web survey assessed knowledge, preparedness, and competency for managing anaphylaxis to local anesthetics (LAs) among board-certified dentists in Latin America. Of 704 dentists reached, 507 completed the 26-item questionnaire (72.0% response) and 480 were analyzed after excluding 15 students and 12 practising outside the region; 81.9% were from Ecuador, mean age 35 (SD 10), 59.3% female, ~10 y mean experience. [확인] Lidocaine was the first-choice LA for 88.1%, and 79.0% did not use a pre-treatment test dose. [확인] Knowledge was uneven: 85.2% identified dyspnea as an anaphylaxis symptom, but only 56.7% named epinephrine as the drug of choice, 50.1% knew the correct intramuscular route, and 43.5% kept epinephrine in their emergency kit (22.1% stocked none of the listed emergency drugs). [확인] Attitudes and confidence diverged sharply: 63.3% rated anaphylaxis extremely important, yet only 28.7% were confident identifying it, 9.6% confident managing it, and 9.4% confident giving epinephrine. [확인] Older age and more years of experience were inversely associated with both management knowledge and office epinephrine possession. [미검증] The clinical bottom line is a readiness gap: dentists treat LA anaphylaxis as important but are under-equipped and under-trained to act, so structured emergency training and minimum equipment standards are warranted.

## Key Contributions

- [확인] Quantifies the emergency-preparedness gap for LA anaphylaxis: 9.6% confident managing it, 43.5% stocking epinephrine, vs 63.3% rating it extremely important.
- [확인] Flags a dangerous route misconception — only 50.1% know epinephrine is intramuscular; ~30% of oral/maxillofacial surgeons would use the intravenous route.
- [확인] Documents an inverse age/experience gradient: older, more experienced dentists scored lower on both knowledge and epinephrine possession.
- [확인] Prior exposure to a systemic adverse reaction (21.3% of respondents) did not raise management confidence — experience alone is not a substitute for training.
- [확인] Notes the surveyed countries set no federal minimum standard for dental-office emergency equipment or training.

## Methodology

[확인] Cross-sectional web-based survey (QuestionPro), 26 structured multiple-choice items across three sections (LA practice; anaphylaxis knowledge; attitudes), ~8 min to complete, distributed by snowball sampling through WhatsApp and email, 8 Nov 2021 – 28 Feb 2022. Inclusion: board-certified dentist practising in Latin America; students, non-regional practitioners, and incomplete responses excluded. n = 480 analyzed (from 507 completed / 704 reached). Analysis in Stata 17.0: chi-square, plus univariate and multivariate logistic regression for predictors of drug-of-choice knowledge, office epinephrine possession, and confidence in identifying at-risk patients. Ethics approval: CEISH; informed consent; Declaration of Helsinki. [미검증] Key limitation: the instrument was not formally validated and 81.9% of respondents were Ecuadorian.

[미검증] Clinical takeaway: the recognition step is reasonably intact (dyspnea 85.2%), but the action steps fail — correct IM epinephrine (50.1%), a stocked kit (43.5%), and the confidence to use it (9.6%). Training should target the action end of the chain, and the inverse age gradient means refresher training matters most for senior clinicians, not new graduates.
