## Three-line Summary

SR (Niger J Clin Pract 2026): 5 RCTs; mental/incisive nerve block (MINB) vs inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) for mandibular permanent premolars with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP).

MINB achieved up to 93% anesthetic success vs IANB; not statistically significant overall, but MINB produced significantly faster onset and less injection pain; clinically relevant advantage.

MINB is a viable primary or rescue option for SIP mandibular premolars — particularly given IANB's well-documented failure rate in the SIP context.

## Summary

Alsharif and Alqadi (Niger J Clin Pract 2026) systematically reviewed 5 RCTs comparing MINB against conventional IANB for mandibular permanent premolars diagnosed with SIP requiring root canal treatment. MINB achieved up to 93% anesthetic success — directionally superior to IANB, though not reaching statistical significance in the pooled analysis. However, MINB demonstrated significantly faster onset of anesthesia and significantly less injection pain compared with IANB. This patient-centered advantage is clinically meaningful, particularly given IANB's known failure rates in SIP (due to central sensitization and inflammatory tissue pH alterations).

## Key Contributions

- First SR specifically comparing MINB vs IANB for mandibular premolars with SIP
- MINB up to 93% success rate — clinically relevant even without statistical significance vs IANB
- Establishes MINB advantages: faster onset + less injection pain (both statistically significant)
- Provides evidence basis for MINB as primary or supplemental technique in SIP premolars
- Fills evidence gap for an underexplored tooth group (premolars vs molars where most IANB SIP research focuses)

## Methodology

SR; databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, WoS, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar, ClinicalTrials.gov (up to Sept 5, 2025). Inclusion: RCTs, adults, mandibular permanent premolars, SIP, NSRCT, comparing MINB vs IANB. 5 RCTs met criteria. ROB assessed by two reviewers independently. No MA performed due to heterogeneity.
