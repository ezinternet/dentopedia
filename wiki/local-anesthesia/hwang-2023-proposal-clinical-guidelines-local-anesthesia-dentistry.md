## Three-line Summary

Short Korean position paper (narrative, no evidence grading) arguing for the development of formal clinical practice guidelines for dental local anesthesia — a procedure performed daily in every Korean dental practice yet lacking a structured national standard.

The paper enumerates the scope a future guideline must cover: anesthetic selection across teeth, periodontal tissue, oral mucosa, TMJ, and the maxillofacial region; no primary outcomes are reported.

The institutional contribution is documenting the standardization gap and articulating the guideline framework, paired with the contemporaneous Korean sedation guideline introduction in the same JKDA issue.

## Summary

A short position paper from a Korean OMS academic, arguing for the development of formal clinical practice guidelines for dental local anesthesia. The author observes that dental local anesthesia is the most frequently performed clinical procedure in dentistry and a foundational element of dental-school curricula, yet Korea lacks an explicit, structured guideline. The article enumerates topics that a future guideline would need to address — sensory innervation and anesthetic selection across teeth, periodontal tissue, oral mucosa, TMJ, and the maxillofacial area.

## Key Contributions

- Documents the standardization gap in Korean dental local anesthesia practice.
- Articulates the scope a future guideline must cover.
- Pairs with [[drug/karm-2023-clinical-practice-guidelines-diagnostic-procedural-sedation]] as a "sedation has a guideline; local anesthesia does not" message in the same JKDA issue.

## Methodology

Short narrative position paper. No literature search or evidence grading.

## Results

No primary outcomes. The contribution is institutional rather than empirical.
