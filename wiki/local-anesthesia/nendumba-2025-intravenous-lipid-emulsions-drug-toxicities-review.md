## Three-line Summary

Comprehensive narrative review (Ann Intensive Care 2025) of intravenous lipid emulsion (ILE) across multiple drug toxicities, presenting the current mechanistic understanding and evidence base for 20% ILE as a rescue agent; identifies three overlapping mechanisms — lipid sink, lipid shuttle, and direct cardiotonic effects — and evaluates the supporting evidence by drug class.

Animal meta-analysis (22 RCTs, n=242) showed OR 0.26 (95% CI: 0.16–0.44) for mortality reduction with ILE versus control across drug classes; RCT evidence in humans exists for tramadol, clozapine, and organophosphate toxicity; human LAST evidence remains limited to case reports and case series, with no adequately powered RCT.

ILE efficacy correlates with lipophilicity (log P): bupivacaine (3.41) > ropivacaine (2.9) > lidocaine (2.44) > mepivacaine (1.95); agents with high lipophilicity are sequestered more efficiently; monitoring for ILE complications (hypertriglyceridemia, fat embolism, delayed ILE syndrome with ECMO) is necessary.

## ILE Mechanisms

### 1. Lipid Sink
The most established mechanism. Free lipid droplets in blood create a compartment that sequesters lipophilic drugs away from their sites of toxicity.
- High log P drugs are drawn into the lipid compartment (Nernst partition)
- Reduces free drug concentration at myocardial sodium/potassium channels and mitochondria
- Does NOT work for hydrophilic agents

### 2. Lipid Shuttle
Lipid particles carry drug from target organs (heart, brain) to metabolizing organs (liver, muscle).
- Accelerates redistribution and metabolism
- Mechanistic evidence from perfused heart models
- Synergistic with lipid sink

### 3. Direct Cardiotonic Effects
ILE has positive inotropic effects independent of drug sequestration:
- Volume expansion: large volume of ILE increases circulating volume
- Nitric oxide inhibition → vasoconstriction (supports blood pressure)
- Mitochondrial ATP production support
- Rescue of sodium channel blockade (reversal of Na+-channel inhibition by bupivacaine)
- GSK-3β phosphorylation: inhibits apoptosis in cardiomyocytes

## Cardiotoxicity Spectrum

**CC/CNS ratio** (cardiac collapse dose / CNS toxicity dose):
- Bupivacaine: ~7 (cardiac toxicity occurs at doses only ~7× the CNS dose → narrow safety margin)
- Lidocaine: ~7 (similar ratio, but lower absolute cardiotoxicity due to lower protein binding and lipophilicity)
- Ropivacaine: similar to bupivacaine but less potent

Bupivacaine's cardiac toxicity is particularly resistant to standard resuscitation (CPR + epinephrine), making ILE the key rescue agent. Lidocaine cardiotoxicity is more reversible with standard ACLS.

## ILE Protocol (ASRA 2020)

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Preparation | 20% lipid emulsion (Intralipid) readily available |
| Bolus | 1.5 mL/kg IV over 1 min |
| Infusion | 0.25 mL/kg/min for 30–60 min |
| Re-bolus | If cardiovascular collapse persists: repeat bolus × 1–2 |
| Maximum total | 10–12 mL/kg |
| Monitoring | Triglycerides, renal panel, CBC; avoid propofol as ILE substitute |

| Drug class | Evidence level for ILE |
|---|---|
| LAST (bupivacaine) | Case reports/series; animal RCTs strong |
| Tramadol | Human RCT (n=100): ILE improved outcomes |
| Clozapine | Human RCT: ILE accelerated recovery |
| Organophosphates | Human RCT: ILE reduced mortality |
| TCAs (amitriptyline) | Animal studies; case reports |
| Beta-blockers | Case reports |
| Calcium channel blockers | Case reports (limited) |
| Cocaine | Animal data (conflicting) |

## ILE Complications

1. **Hypertriglyceridemia**: serum triglycerides >500 mg/dL risk pancreatitis; monitor after large volumes
2. **Fat embolism**: particularly with rapid large-volume infusion; rare at LAST doses
3. **Interference with ECMO**: lipid droplets can occlude ECMO circuit oxygenators (documented); use with caution if ECMO initiated
4. **Delayed ILE syndrome**: progressive lipemia 12–24h post-administration; rare
5. **Propofol**: propofol is 10% lipid emulsion — do NOT substitute for 20% ILE; inadequate concentration and cardiotoxic propofol component

## Clinical Insights

The mechanistic framework here directly explains why **bupivacaine LAST is both more dangerous and more ILE-responsive** than lidocaine LAST:
- Higher log P → larger lipid sink effect
- Greater protein binding (95% vs 65%) → ILE competition liberates cardiac effects faster
- More resistant to standard ACLS → ILE is the necessary rescue

For dental practice: lidocaine LAST is less likely to progress to cardiac arrest, but ILE availability matters because progression can occur, particularly with inadvertent intravascular injection of large doses.
