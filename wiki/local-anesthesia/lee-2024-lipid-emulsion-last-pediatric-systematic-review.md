## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (Medicine 2024; 21 pediatric LAST cases with ILE treatment, 2006–2022) found that 20/21 patients (95.2%) achieved full recovery; the single death involved bupivacaine LAST in an infant with undiagnosed valproic acid-induced carnitine deficiency, which amplified bupivacaine cardiotoxicity — a unique metabolic vulnerability, not a failure of ILE per se.

Bupivacaine accounted for 14/21 cases (66.7%) and cardiac arrest for 5/21 (23.8%); patients aged <1 year comprised 43% of cases — confirming infants as the highest-risk group due to reduced α1-acid glycoprotein, immature CYP450, and low body weight; all full-recovery cases (20/21) received ILE within the first 30 minutes of LAST onset.

ILE dosing: bolus 1.5 mL/kg + infusion 0.25 mL/kg/min (20% emulsion), consistent with adult ASRA protocol; no pediatric-specific modification required, though weight-based precision is more critical at low body weights.

## 세줄요약

소아 LAST 증례에서 ILE 치료 체계적 리뷰 (Medicine 2024; 21례, 2006–2022) — 완전회복 20/21 (95.2%); 유일한 사망은 발프로산 유발 카르니틴 결핍으로 뷰피바카인 (Bupivacaine) 심장독성 증폭 — ILE 실패가 아닌 독특한 대사 취약성.

뷰피바카인 14/21 (66.7%), 심정지 5/21 (23.8%), <1세 43% — 영아가 최고위험군 (α1-산성 당단백 감소, 미성숙 CYP450, 저체중); 완전회복 20례 전부 LAST 발현 30분 내 ILE 투여.

투여 프로토콜: 볼루스 1.5 mL/kg + 지속주입 0.25 mL/kg/min (20% 에멀전); 소아 특이적 프로토콜 변경 불필요, 다만 저체중에서 용량 정밀도가 더 중요.

## The Single Death — Clinical Analysis

**Patient**: Infant receiving bupivacaine for caudal block  
**Unusual factor**: Receiving valproic acid for epilepsy → valproate inhibits carnitine reabsorption → **carnitine deficiency**

**Mechanism of amplified toxicity**:
- Carnitine is essential for fatty acid transport into mitochondria for beta-oxidation
- Carnitine deficiency impairs mitochondrial fatty acid metabolism
- Bupivacaine's myocardial toxicity (mitochondrial Complex I inhibition) was compounded by the already-impaired mitochondrial energy production from carnitine deficiency
- Standard ACLS + ILE could not rescue the combined mitochondrial failure

**Clinical lesson**: Patients receiving valproic acid should be screened for carnitine deficiency before major regional anesthesia with bupivacaine. This is NOT a contraindication to ILE — ILE was appropriate; the pre-existing metabolic vulnerability was the key variable.

## Pediatric LAST Risk Factors

### Pharmacokinetic differences vs adults:
1. **Reduced α1-acid glycoprotein (AAG)**: AAG binds LA in plasma; neonates have ~50% adult AAG levels → higher free (unbound) LA fraction → greater CNS/cardiac availability
2. **Immature CYP450**: reduced hepatic metabolism of amide LAs → longer half-life → accumulation risk
3. **Low body weight**: same absolute dose = much higher mg/kg dose; small calculation errors have large consequences
4. **High cardiac output relative to body mass**: rapid systemic distribution

### Injection site risks in children:
- Caudal blocks (most pediatric regional technique) — high vascularity, easy intravascular placement
- IANB in dental practice: risk amplified by small body weight + lack of weight-based dosing by dentists (only 40.1% per Bakır 2026)

## ILE Protocol in Pediatrics

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Emulsion | 20% lipid emulsion (Intralipid or equivalent) |
| Bolus | **1.5 mL/kg** IV over 1 minute |
| Infusion | **0.25 mL/kg/min** for 30–60 minutes |
| Re-bolus if collapse persists | Repeat bolus × 1–2 |
| Maximum total | **10–12 mL/kg** (watch for lipid overload in infants) |
| Key difference from adults | Lower absolute volumes; precision of syringe weight-based dosing critical |

**Note**: No dose modification factor needed by age — the per-kg protocol scales appropriately. The critical issue is accurate weight-based calculation and prompt administration (<30 min from onset).

## Clinical Insights

1. **Speed matters**: All 20 full recoveries received ILE within 30 minutes of onset. This single data point is the strongest argument for pre-stocking ILE wherever regional anesthesia is performed on children — including dental offices performing IANB.

2. **Bupivacaine disproportionate**: 66.7% of pediatric LAST cases involve bupivacaine despite it being far less commonly used in dentistry than lidocaine. This reflects bupivacaine's extreme cardiotoxicity, not frequency of use. Dental LAST cases are more likely to involve lidocaine (common dental agent) — but lidocaine LAST is also more recoverable without ILE.

3. **Infants are disproportionately affected**: 42.9% under 1 year — strongly supports weight-based dosing and conservative approach to regional blocks in infants.

4. **Carnitine-valproate interaction**: a rare but fatal vulnerability to document on the pediatric medical history form.
