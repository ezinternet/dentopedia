## Three-line Summary

Clinical review (Gen Dent 2015;63(1):48–52) on preventing LAST in pediatric dental patients through proper maximum recommended dose (MRD) calculations, covering Clark's Rule, the Rule of 25, and mg/lb body-weight methodology; notes that LAST occurs more frequently in children than adults and that 3% mepivacaine (plain, no vasoconstrictor) is a particular risk agent in pediatrics.

Provides practical dosing calculation tools to translate adult MRD tables to pediatric doses based on body weight, emphasizing that the same volume of cartridge administers far more mg/kg in a 20 kg child versus an 80 kg adult; identifies calculation errors as a root cause of pediatric LAST, particularly with higher-concentration agents.

Abstract-only; foundational pediatric dental dosing reference by Moore PA and Hersh EV — the most-cited authors in dental anesthesiology — directly applicable to avoiding the exact scenarios documented in Lee 2024 pediatric LAST SR.

## Dose Calculation Methods

### Method 1: Clark's Rule (weight-based fraction of adult dose)
```
Pediatric dose = (Child's weight in lbs / 150 lbs) × Adult MRD
```
- Based on proportion of adult body weight
- Simple, widely used
- Limitation: does not account for maturity of metabolic enzymes

### Method 2: Rule of 25 (simplified mg/lb calculation)
- Each agent has a maximum mg/lb dose
- Quick mental arithmetic
- Example for lidocaine: ~2 mg/lb (adult MRD ~300 mg)

### Method 3: Direct mg/kg calculation (most accurate)
- Use established MRD in mg/kg for each agent
- Calculate total dose = weight (kg) × MRD (mg/kg)
- Convert to cartridges: 1 dental cartridge = 1.8 mL

## 3% Mepivacaine: The Special Pediatric Risk

Mepivacaine 3% (plain) is often chosen in pediatric dentistry because:
- No epinephrine → no adrenergic side effects
- Useful in patients with cardiovascular conditions

However, **"plain" means faster systemic absorption**: without epinephrine-induced local vasoconstriction, LA diffuses more rapidly into the vasculature, reaching toxic plasma levels faster. In a small child:
- 1 cartridge of 3% mepivacaine = 1.8 mL × 30 mg/mL = **54 mg**
- For a 15 kg child: MRD = 15 × 6.6 = 99 mg → only **1.8 cartridges** at MRD
- Most dental procedures may require >2 cartridges → **easy to exceed MRD**

## Carpule Volume Reality Check

A useful clinical calculation frame:
- 1 dental cartridge = 1.8 mL
- Lidocaine 2%: 36 mg/cartridge
- Mepivacaine 3%: 54 mg/cartridge  
- Articaine 4%: 72 mg/cartridge

For a **20 kg child** (MRD lidocaine = 20 × 7 = 140 mg):
- Maximum: 140 ÷ 36 = **3.9 cartridges** of lidocaine 2%
- For mepivacaine 3%: 132 mg MRD ÷ 54 = **2.4 cartridges**

This makes it clear why calculation by weight is essential: the difference between safe and toxic doses in small children is ≤1 cartridge.

## Clinical Insights

1. **Never administer by "number of carpules" for pediatric patients** — always calculate weight-based MRD first
2. **3% mepivacaine plain is the highest per-volume dose agent in the dental tray** — 50% higher concentration than 2% lidocaine; in small children, the no-vasoconstrictor feature that makes it attractive also makes it absorb faster
3. **Articaine 4% in children <4 years**: FDA approval limitation based on lack of pediatric trials, not known toxicity; some practitioners use it off-label with caution
4. **The calculation error is the route**: Bakır 2026 found only 40.1% of dentists calculate dose by weight — this is the prevention gap
