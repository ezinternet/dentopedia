## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional survey (n=369 Turkish dentists, June–September 2025; Healthcare Basel 2026) found that only 0.5% of dentists had detailed knowledge of lipid emulsion administration steps and dosing protocol for LAST management, 45.0% did not know whether lipid emulsion was available at their institution, and only 14.1% confirmed availability.

Despite 45.8% reporting basic LAST knowledge and 74.8% obtaining systemic disease histories, only 3.3% had prepared an emergency response plan and 2.2% were prepared to use lipid emulsion — indicating a gap between general awareness and actionable emergency preparedness in dental practice.

Professional status (specialist/academic > general dentist, p=0.001) was associated with lipid emulsion knowledge, but effect size was small (ε²=0.030); years of experience showed no significant linear association with knowledge level.

## Clinical Background

**LAST mechanism**: LA overdose, repeated administration, or inadvertent intravascular injection → blood concentration exceeds safe limit → central nervous and cardiovascular system toxicity.

**Risk factors**: dose, injection technique, vascularity of injection site, patient-specific factors (e.g., hepatic/renal impairment, extremes of age, low body weight).

**Early symptoms**: perioral/lingual numbness, metallic taste, dizziness, tinnitus  
**Progression**: agitation, altered consciousness, respiratory depression, seizures, cardiac arrhythmias, hypotension, cardiac arrest

**IANB positive aspiration rate**: up to 15.3% — underscores importance of aspiration technique.

**Lipid emulsion rescue**: 20% IV lipid emulsion (Intralipid) is recommended by ASRA 2020 LAST checklist and AHA 2020 CPR Guidelines for severe LAST, especially bupivacaine-induced cardiac arrest.

**ASRA protocol**: bolus + continuous infusion of 20% lipid emulsion.

## Clinical Insights

This study quantifies what many suspected: **awareness of LAST does not translate to preparedness**. The most actionable findings for practice settings:

1. **Emergency kit gap**: Only 14.1% of institutions had lipid emulsion available — this is the clearest actionable gap in Turkish dental practice, likely mirrored in many other settings.
2. **Label check behavior**: 47.7% check labels only "occasionally" and 19.0% never — basic medication safety practice failing.
3. **Dose calculation**: 40.1% calculated dose by weight — reflects a deficit that can be addressed by simple clinical reminders.
4. **Institutional responsibility**: The focus of improvement should be at the institutional level (emergency protocols, stockpiling lipid emulsion) as individual knowledge growth is slow and shows no correlation with clinical experience years.
