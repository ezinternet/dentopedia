## Three-line Summary

RCT (Anesth Prog 2013;60(3):99–108) comparing methemoglobin (MetHb) formation after prilocaine 4% versus lidocaine 2% with epinephrine in pediatric dental patients; prilocaine produced significantly higher MetHb levels (~2× lidocaine), confirming dose-dependent methemoglobin formation from prilocaine's metabolite o-toluidine.

Prilocaine's methemoglobinemia risk is clinically significant in pediatric patients because (1) children have relatively lower MetHb reductase activity, (2) fetal hemoglobin is more susceptible to oxidation than adult HbA, and (3) the risk compounds with topical EMLA cream co-use (prilocaine + lidocaine combination); MetHb levels remained below the symptomatic threshold (~25%) in healthy children at dental doses, but the risk margin is reduced in infants, anemic patients, or those on oxidizing drugs.

This is the primary direct RCT evidence supporting the preference for lidocaine over prilocaine in pediatric dental anesthesia, particularly in patients <6 months or with hemoglobin disorders.

## 세줄요약

RCT (Anesth Prog 2013;60(3):99–108) — 소아 치과 환자 프릴로카인 4% vs 리도케인 2%+에피네프린 MetHb 형성 비교; 프릴로카인이 ~2배 높은 MetHb 유발, 대사산물 o-톨루이딘 기전 확인.

소아에서 메트헤모글로빈혈증 (Methemoglobinemia) 위험 증폭 요인: 상대적으로 낮은 MetHb 환원효소 활성, 태아헤모글로빈(HbF)의 산화 취약성, EMLA 크림 병용 시 가중; 건강한 소아의 치과 용량에서는 증상 역치(25%) 미만이나, 6개월 미만·빈혈·산화성 약물 병용 환자에서 안전역 감소.

소아 치과에서 프릴로카인보다 리도케인 선호를 뒷받침하는 직접 RCT 근거.

## Methemoglobinemia Mechanism

**Prilocaine → o-toluidine** (hepatic metabolism) → oxidizes Fe²⁺ hemoglobin to Fe³⁺ methemoglobin (MetHb)

MetHb cannot carry oxygen → functional anemia:
- 0–25% MetHb: usually asymptomatic; may see slight cyanosis at >10%
- 25–50%: headache, dyspnea, fatigue, tachycardia
- 50–70%: confusion, lethargy, severe hypoxia
- >70%: cardiac arrhythmia, death risk

**Lidocaine**: metabolized to monoethylglycinexylidide (MEGX) and glycinexylidide — neither produces significant methemoglobin at dental doses.

## Pediatric Vulnerability

1. **Lower MetHb reductase (cytochrome b5 reductase)**: activity at ~20% of adult levels in neonates; matures by ~4–6 months. Infants cannot reduce MetHb as efficiently.
2. **Fetal hemoglobin (HbF)**: more susceptible to oxidation than adult HbA; present in large proportion until ~6 months of age.
3. **EMLA cream risk**: EMLA = 2.5% lidocaine + 2.5% prilocaine (topical) — widespread dental use as topical anesthetic; prilocaine component adds to any subsequent prilocaine injection, raising cumulative MetHb formation.
4. **Body weight**: lower weight means same absolute dose = higher mg/kg → higher per-kg MetHb formation.

## High-Risk Scenarios (Prilocaine Caution)

| Scenario | Reason | Preferred alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Age <6 months | Low MetHb reductase, high HbF | Lidocaine 2% |
| EMLA + prilocaine injection | Additive methemoglobin | Lidocaine only; no EMLA before prilocaine |
| G6PD deficiency | Impaired reduction pathway | Lidocaine 2% |
| Hemoglobin M disease | Constitutive MetHb formation | Lidocaine 2% |
| Anemia (Hb <10 g/dL) | Reduced oxygen-carrying reserve | Lidocaine 2% |
| Nitrite medications (dapsone, etc.) | Additional oxidizing drugs | Avoid prilocaine |

## Treatment: Methylene Blue

If MetHb >25% symptomatic:
- **Methylene blue** 1–2 mg/kg IV over 5 minutes
- Provides alternative electron pathway for MetHb reduction (NADPH-dependent)
- Contraindicated in G6PD deficiency (cannot produce NADPH)
- Works within 30–60 minutes

## Clinical Insights

For dental practice, this RCT provides the following direct guidance:
1. **Prilocaine should not be the default pediatric agent** — particularly in infants; lidocaine is safer
2. **EMLA + prilocaine = double hit**: topical EMLA before prilocaine injection creates additive methemoglobin load; consider lidocaine-only topical before lidocaine injection
3. **MetHb vs LAST trade-off**: prilocaine is sometimes chosen for lower vasoconstrictors (reduced epinephrine load); but in pediatrics, the methemoglobin risk outweighs this benefit
4. **Diagnosis clue**: cyanosis not responding to O₂ in a recently anesthetized child → consider MetHb; SpO₂ falsely reads ~85% regardless of true MetHb level (co-oximetry required for accurate diagnosis)
