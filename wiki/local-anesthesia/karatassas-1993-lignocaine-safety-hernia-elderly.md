## Three-line Summary

Prospective pharmacokinetic study (n=14 elderly patients; Aust NZ J Surg 1993) measuring plasma lignocaine concentrations after near-maximum dose (7 mg/kg) infiltration anesthesia for inguinal hernia repair, demonstrating a large safety margin even in a high-risk population.

Peak plasma concentration (normalized to 7 mg/kg): 0.23–0.90 mg/L (mean 0.54), against a toxicity threshold of 5 mg/L — maximum observed Cmax is less than one-fifth of the threshold; no toxic symptoms in any patient.

Provides the empirical validation for the FDA's 7 mg/kg infiltration limit in an elderly population (conservative model for assessing safety margin), and establishes 5 mg/L as the older toxicity threshold still cited alongside Klein 2016's 6 μg/mL.

## 세줄요약

전향적 약동학 연구 (n=14 고령 환자; Aust NZ J Surg 1993) — 7 mg/kg 침윤마취(서혜부 탈장 복원) 후 혈장 리도케인 농도 측정; 고위험군에서도 큰 안전 마진 확인.

최고 혈중농도(7 mg/kg 정규화): 0.23–0.90 mg/L (평균 0.54), 독성역치 5 mg/L의 1/5 미만; 전환자 독성 증상 없음.

FDA 7 mg/kg 침윤 한도를 고령 인구에서 경험적으로 검증; 구(舊) 독성역치 5 mg/L의 근거 문헌.

## Toxicity Threshold — Historical Context

| Source | Threshold | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **Karatassas 1993** | **5 mg/L (5 μg/mL)** | "Toxicity threshold for lignocaine" |
| **Klein 2016** | **6 μg/mL** | "Threshold for mild lidocaine toxicity" |
| Typical clinical teaching | 5–6 μg/mL | Mild symptoms (perioral numbness, tinnitus, lightheadedness) |

The difference (5 vs 6 μg/mL) reflects different eras and definitions. Both are consistent: mild toxicity symptoms begin in the 5–6 μg/mL range. Both confirm the same clinical conclusion — standard dental doses produce Cmax far below either threshold.

## Significance for Dental Practice

### Why the elderly population matters:
- Elderly patients have reduced hepatic function → slower lidocaine clearance → higher Cmax for same dose
- Lower albumin and α1-acid glycoprotein → higher free drug fraction → greater CNS/cardiac availability
- This study used elderly patients specifically to test a conservative (worst-case) scenario

### Safety margin calculation:
- Standard dental 2% lidocaine with 1:100,000 epi: 1–3 carpules = 36–108 mg
- For a 70 kg patient: 108 mg ÷ 70 kg = 1.54 mg/kg (far below 7 mg/kg)
- Even at 7 mg/kg, Cmax maximum = 0.90 mg/L → 5.5× below toxicity threshold
- At 1.54 mg/kg: expected Cmax ≈ 0.12 mg/L → ~40× below toxicity threshold

### Implication: why does dental LAST occur at all?
The huge safety margin from pharmacokinetic absorption means LAST from dental injection almost exclusively results from **inadvertent intravascular injection**, not dose accumulation.

## Clinical Insights

1. **5 mg/L vs 6 μg/mL**: both thresholds are clinically correct — use 6 μg/mL as the current standard (Klein 2016), recognize 5 mg/L when encountered in older literature.

2. **Elderly patients are the strictest test case** — this study's safety findings in elderly patients are more conservative than equivalent findings in younger adults. Dental practice routinely treats elderly patients; this data provides direct reassurance.

3. **Wide safety margin for mucosal absorption**: the gap between standard dental doses and the toxicity threshold is enormous — safety concern should focus on injection technique (intravascular), not cumulative dose arithmetic in typical patients.
