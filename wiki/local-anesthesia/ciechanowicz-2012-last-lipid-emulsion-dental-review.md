## Three-line Summary

Foundational dental-targeted narrative review (Br Dent J 2012;212(5):237–41) on LAST in dentistry and ILE rescue therapy, published when ILE awareness was just emerging among dental practitioners and predating the ASRA 2012 and 2020 LAST checklists.

Covers amide LA cardiac toxicity pharmacology, high-risk patient identification, prodromal signs/symptoms (perioral numbness → CNS excitation → seizures → cardiovascular collapse), preventive measures, and initial LAST management including the then-emerging ILE protocol; notes that LAST accounts for a disproportionately high fraction of dental office fatalities compared to other medical settings.

Abstract-only; serves as the foundational dental-specific LAST + ILE reference establishing the clinical framework that subsequent reviews (Nendumba 2025, Lee 2024) and surveys (Bakır 2026) have built upon.

## Historical Context

This 2012 review was published at a pivotal moment:
- **2006**: First case reports of ILE rescue for LAST published (Rosenblatt 2006 — bupivacaine cardiac arrest)
- **2008**: AAGBI (UK) first guidelines including ILE for LAST
- **2012**: This review — one of the first to reach dental practitioners explicitly; ASRA's first formal LAST checklist also 2012
- **2020**: ASRA comprehensive LAST management checklist published; AHA CPR guidelines incorporated ILE

The review's framing ("rescued at LAST" — the ILE acronym was already embedded in the title as wordplay on the landmark guideline development) reflects the early adoption phase.

## Clinical Framework from 2012

### LAST Predisposing Factors (per Ciechanowicz 2012):
- Inadvertent intravascular injection (particularly IANB — up to 15% positive aspiration rate)
- Repeated injections over large area (e.g., full-mouth rehabilitation)
- Failure to aspirate before injection
- High-risk patients: hepatic impairment, low body weight, elderly, extremes of age, heart block
- Highly vascular injection sites

### LAST Prodrome Sequence:
1. Perioral numbness, metallic taste, tinnitus
2. Light-headedness, dizziness
3. Agitation, confusion, dysarthria
4. Seizures (CNS excitation phase)
5. CNS depression (unconsciousness)
6. Cardiovascular: arrhythmias, hypotension, bradycardia
7. Cardiac arrest (LA-induced — particularly resistant to standard ACLS)

### Why Dental LAST Is Particularly Dangerous:
- Dental offices often lack defibrillators, emergency medications
- Bupivacaine-induced cardiac arrest is refractory to epinephrine + CPR alone (the standard protocol)
- Isolated practice setting (limited backup)
- ILE not historically stocked in dental offices

## Preventive Measures Highlighted

1. Careful aspiration before injection
2. Slow incremental injection
3. Maximum dose calculation by body weight
4. Preference for infiltration over regional blocks when clinically appropriate
5. Awareness of early prodromal symptoms
6. Reduce dose for high-risk patients (elderly, hepatic impairment, low body weight)

## ILE Protocol as of 2012

The 2012 review predates formal ASRA protocol but presented emerging evidence:
- **20% lipid emulsion (Intralipid)**: 1.5 mL/kg bolus
- Continuous infusion at 0.25 mL/kg/min
- Maximum total ~12 mL/kg
- [Now formalized in ASRA 2020 LAST checklist — see Nendumba 2025 for current protocol]

## Clinical Insights

This review established the vocabulary and framework for dental LAST management. Its core contribution to current practice:

1. **ILE should be in every dental emergency kit** — this was the paper's main call to action; as Bakır 2026 shows, 14 years later only 14.1% of institutions comply.
2. **Bupivacaine LAST ≠ standard resuscitation**: the review was early to articulate why bupivacaine cardiac arrest requires ILE specifically, not just CPR + epinephrine.
3. **The dental setting creates unique risk**: isolation, limited emergency resources, and bupivacaine use for post-op pain management combine to make LAST preparation especially important in dental practice.
