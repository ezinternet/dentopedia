## Three-line Summary

Retrospective clinical study (Int J Oral Maxillofac Implants 2015; 34 patients, 40 GBR sites, April 2012–April 2013) evaluating a flap advancement technique that omits the vertical releasing incision entirely, substituting a wide periosteal releasing incision plus a selective facial-muscle (orbicularis oris/buccinator) releasing incision.

All 40 sites achieved >7 mm flap advancement and clinically passive primary closure with only slight/mild swelling and no long-lasting pain, paresthesia, or infection over 6 months; 2/40 sites (both wearing a provisional removable prosthesis) had delayed titanium-mesh exposure at 4 months, closing spontaneously within 2 weeks after prosthesis discontinuation and dressing.

The technique enables reliable passive primary closure without any vertical incision, but the study is a single-arm retrospective series (no comparator, no vertical-incision control group) reported here from the abstract only.

## Summary

This retrospective series introduces a flap-advancement protocol for GBR that drops the vertical releasing incision entirely. Instead of the conventional midcrestal + vertical incision combination, the authors used a midcrestal incision with sulcular extension to adjacent teeth, then relied on a **wide** periosteal releasing incision plus an additional incision selectively dividing part of the facial expression musculature (orbicularis oris or buccinator) to gain flap mobility. Across 40 GBR sites in 34 patients (April 2012–April 2013), this achieved more than 7 mm of advancement — Plonka 2017's "major advancement" stratum — with clinically passive primary closure at every site and no obvious closure failure. Postoperative morbidity was limited to slight/mild swelling with no functional limitation; no long-lasting pain, paresthesia, or infection occurred over 6 months. The one notable complication was delayed titanium-mesh exposure at 4 months in 2 of 40 sites, both in patients wearing a provisional removable prosthesis — both resolved spontaneously within 2 weeks after prosthesis discontinuation and careful dressing.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that ≥7 mm flap advancement and passive primary closure are achievable **without any vertical incision**, using a wide periosteal releasing incision plus selective facial-muscle release alone.
- Provides concrete complication/exposure data (40 sites, 34 patients, 6-month follow-up) for this specific vertical-incision-free technique — a variant that [[suture-wound-closure/plonka-2017-flap-designs-flap-advancement-implant-therapy]]'s taxonomy classifies but does not quantify with a dedicated outcome series.
- Flags a prosthesis-pressure association: both delayed titanium-mesh exposures occurred in provisional-removable-prosthesis wearers, resolving after prosthesis discontinuation — a practical postoperative-management signal.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective clinical study, single technique arm, no comparator group.
- **Population**: 34 patients, 40 GBR sites treated April 2012–April 2013.
- **Incision protocol**: midcrestal incision on the edentulous alveolar ridge + sulcular incision extended to the adjacent two teeth; **no vertical incision**. Release achieved via (1) a wide periosteal releasing incision and (2) a selective releasing incision through part of the orbicularis oris or buccinator muscle.
- **Follow-up**: swelling, pain, paresthesia, infection signs, and membrane exposure assessed at 1, 2, 4, 12, and 24 weeks postoperatively.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Flap advancement | >7 mm at all 40/40 sites |
| Primary closure | Clinically passive at all sites; no obvious closure failure |
| Postoperative swelling | Slight or mild in all patients; no functional limitation |
| Long-lasting pain (>1 week) | None |
| Paresthesia | None |
| Infection | None over 6-month follow-up |
| Titanium mesh exposure | 2/40 sites, delayed to 4 months, both provisional-removable-prosthesis wearers; closed within 2 weeks after halting prosthesis use + dressing |
