## Three-line Summary

Canine experimental study (9 adult beagle dogs) tracking the angiogenic response of the periosteal vascular plexus after mucoperiosteal flap surgery at postoperative days 3, 5, and 7, using India-ink-injected histological specimens under light microscopy, transmission electron microscopy of ultrathin sections, and 3-D scanning electron microscopy of acryl plastic vascular casts.

By day 3, new vessels had formed through sprouting, bridging, and intussusception, plus blood island-like clusters of immature endothelial cells in the repaired tissue; by days 5–7, vascular casts showed the new vessels as sinus-like structures in the interstitium of the periosteal vascular plexus, gaining continuity and thinning their endothelium ultrastructurally as they matured.

The periosteal vasculature exhibits potent vessel-forming and repair activity after flap elevation — 3-D confirmation that the periosteal vascular plexus is a central revascularization engine of flap healing; limits: abstract-only ingestion, canine model, no quantitative blood-flow data.

## Summary

This canine experimental study (9 adult beagle dogs) monitored the periosteal vascular plexus's angiogenic response to mucoperiosteal flap surgery at postoperative days 3, 5, and 7 with three complementary modalities: histological specimens with India-ink-injected vessels under light microscopy, ultrathin sections under transmission electron microscopy, and acryl plastic vascular casts under scanning electron microscopy for 3-D observation. By day 3, new blood vessels had formed through sprouting, bridging, and intussusception, and blood island-like structures of clustered immature endothelial cells appeared in the repaired tissue. From days 5 to 7, 3-D observation of the vascular casts showed the new vessels assuming a sinus-like morphology within the interstitium of the periosteal vascular plexus — stereoscopic sinusoidal vessels gaining continuity as they matured, while the vascular endothelium thinned ultrastructurally. The authors conclude that after mucoperiosteal flap elevation the periosteal vasculature exhibits potent blood-vessel-forming activity through various angiogenic mechanisms and through repair activity, providing 3-D clarification that the periosteal vascular plexus has an important role in the healing process after flap surgery. For the periosteal-elevation vascular-compromise question, this page is the early-phase healing-response evidence: it characterizes what the periosteal plexus *does* after elevation (days 3–7 revascularization), complementing outcome-level pages on flap vs flapless vascularity.

## Key Contributions

- Provides **3-dimensional clarification** — scanning-electron-microscopic observation of acryl plastic vascular casts — that the periosteal vascular plexus has an important role in the healing process after mucoperiosteal flap surgery.
- Documents that post-elevation revascularization proceeds through **multiple concurrent angiogenic mechanisms** (sprouting, bridging, intussusception), with blood island-like structures of clustered immature endothelial cells appearing in the repaired tissue as early as day 3.
- Establishes the **early morphological time-course** of periosteal revascularization: day-3 sprouts/bridges/intussusceptive vessels and blood-island-like structures → days 5–7 sinus-like sinusoidal vessels with stereoscopic structure, increased continuity, and ultrastructurally thinned endothelium as vessels mature.

## Methodology

Animal experimental study. Mucoperiosteal flap surgery was performed on 9 adult beagle dogs; the periosteal vascular plexus was examined at postoperative days 3, 5, and 7 in three modalities: (1) histological specimens with blood vessels injected with India ink under a light microscope, (2) ultrathin sections under a transmission electron microscope, and (3) acryl plastic vascular cast specimens under a scanning electron microscope (3-D observation). Endpoints were the morphology and timing of new blood vessel formation in the periosteal vascular plexus — qualitative/ultrastructural; no quantitative vascular or perfusion metrics are reported in the abstract.

## Results

| Observation window | Finding |
|---|---|
| Day 3 (ultrathin sections + vascular casts) | New blood vessels formed through sprouting, bridging, and intussusception |
| Day 3 (repaired tissue) | Blood island-like structures consisting of clustered immature endothelial cells |
| Days 5–7 (3-D vascular casts) | New blood vessels assume a sinus-like morphology in the interstitium of the periosteal vascular plexus |
| Days 5–7 (maturation) | Sinusoidal vessels exhibit stereoscopic structure with increased continuity; vascular endothelium thinned ultrastructurally |
| Conclusion | Periosteal vasculature shows potent vessel-forming activity via various angiogenic mechanisms and repair activity after flap elevation |

No quantitative results (vessel counts, perfusion volumes, blood-flow rates) are reported in the abstract — this page holds morphological/time-course evidence only, and no numbers beyond the timepoints (days 3/5/7) and sample size (9 dogs) should be cited from it.
