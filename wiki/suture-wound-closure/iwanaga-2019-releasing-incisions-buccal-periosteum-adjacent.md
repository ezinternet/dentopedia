## Three-line Summary

Cadaveric anatomical dissection study (14 sides, 7 fresh-frozen Caucasian cadaveric heads, mean age 75.9 ± 10.8 years) mapping the course of the facial artery/inferior labial artery (ILA) relative to a standardized periosteal releasing incision (PRI) on the mandibular buccal periosteum.

The ILA was identified in the fat tissue lateral to and close to the periosteum on all 14 sides; it was closest to the periosteum at the PRI midpoint — approximately between the first and second molar teeth or 10 mm mesial to the retromolar-pad apex — with a mean diameter of 2.72 ± 0.26 mm.

Clinical implication: PRI should be made without invading tissue deep to (underneath) the periosteum, particularly in the first/second-molar danger zone, to avoid injuring the ILA/facial artery; abstract-only ingestion, no living-tissue hemodynamics captured.

## Summary

Periosteal releasing incision (PRI) is the standard maneuver dentists use to achieve tension-free primary wound closure during GBR and related flap-advancement procedures — [[suture-wound-closure/plonka-2017-flap-designs-flap-advancement-implant-therapy]] quantifies it as the single largest contributor to advancement (+5.5 mm) among the reviewed techniques. But PRI is performed close to major vascular structures, and intra- and postoperative bleeding is a recognized complication. This cadaveric study directly maps that risk on the mandibular buccal periosteum: across 14 sides from 7 fresh-frozen Caucasian cadaveric heads, the authors made a standardized PRI with a no. 15c blade under a surgical microscope, then dissected the fat tissue lateral to the periosteum to locate and measure the facial artery/its branch.

On every side examined, the inferior labial artery (ILA) — a branch of the facial artery — was found in the fat tissue immediately lateral to the periosteum. Critically, the ILA's closest approach to the periosteum occurred **at the midpoint of the PRI**, which the authors localize two ways for clinical use: **between the first and second molar teeth**, or **10 mm mesial to the apex of the retromolar pad** (a landmark usable even when molars are missing). The vessel's mean diameter — 2.72 ± 0.26 mm — is large enough that inadvertent transection would produce more than trivial bleeding. The authors' recommendation is specific and actionable: make the PRI without invading tissue deep to (underneath) the periosteum, especially within this first/second-molar danger zone.

## Key Contributions

- First cadaveric anatomical mapping (that this wiki holds) of the facial artery/ILA course specifically relative to the PRI maneuver on the mandibular buccal periosteum.
- Localizes a precise **danger zone**: ILA closest to periosteum at the PRI midpoint — between the first and second molar, or 10 mm mesial to the retromolar-pad apex.
- Reports vessel caliber (mean ILA diameter 2.72 ± 0.26 mm) — large enough to explain clinically meaningful bleeding, not just capillary oozing, if transected.
- Converts vague "release carefully" technique guidance into a concrete instruction: keep the incision within the periosteum; do not invade the tissue underneath it.

## Methodology

- **Specimens**: 14 sides from 7 fresh-frozen Caucasian cadaveric heads (2 female, 5 male); mean age at death 75.9 ± 10.8 years.
- **Procedure**: standardized PRI on the mandibular buccal periosteum adjacent to the lower molars, made with a no. 15c blade under a surgical microscope.
- **Dissection**: fat tissue lateral to the periosteum slightly dissected to expose the facial artery/its branch; diameter and closest tooth-to-artery relationship recorded; artery traced proximally to confirm origin.
- **Evidence level**: `in-vitro` — this wiki classifies human fresh-frozen cadaver dissection studies as ex-vivo bench-type anatomical studies rather than `animal`/`in-vivo`, per the established precedent at [[implants/versah-protocols/rittipakorn-2025-clockwise-osseodensification-primary-stability-cadaveric]].
- Abstract-only ingestion (no PMCID); no additional body-text statistics beyond mean ± SD are available.

## Results

| Finding | Value |
|---|---|
| ILA identified lateral to periosteum | 14/14 sides (100%) |
| Closest ILA–periosteum approach | PRI midpoint — between 1st/2nd molar, or 10 mm mesial to retromolar-pad apex |
| Mean ILA diameter | 2.72 ± 0.26 mm |
| Sample | 14 sides, 7 fresh-frozen Caucasian cadavers (2F/5M), mean age 75.9 ± 10.8 y |

**Bottom line**: the mandibular buccal PRI danger zone for the inferior labial artery/facial artery is the first–second molar region (or 10 mm mesial to the retromolar pad apex); staying superficial to (within) the periosteum at this location avoids the vessel.
