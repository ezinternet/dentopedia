## Three-line Summary

Randomized, prospective clinical trial (n=23 Seibert Class III vertical/horizontal ridge defects; Tufts University School of Dental Medicine) comparing double-flap incision (DFI) vs conventional periosteal releasing incision (PRI) for flap advancement in vertical ridge augmentation. **Abstract-only page — full text was not retrieved (no PMC/PMCID; paywalled journal); all detail below is drawn from the structured PubMed abstract.**

DFI achieved significantly more flap advancement than PRI (9.64 ± 0.92 mm vs 7.13 ± 1.45 mm, P < .001) with lower pain, swelling, and bleeding VAS scores (P = .019 / .010 / .061) and fewer complications (DFI 1 site vs PRI 5 sites, P < .082, not significant).

DFI facilitated advancement and decreased morbidity relative to PRI, suggesting it as a potential alternative technique to overcome some of PRI's limitations; small single-center trial (n=23), abstract-only ingestion limits methodological detail.

## Summary

This is one of the few papers in the wiki's flap-advancement collection with actual **randomized, comparative trial** data rather than a single-arm case series or a narrative/systematic-review synthesis. Twenty-three Seibert Class III (combined horizontal + vertical) ridge defects were randomized to receive either a double-flap incision (DFI) or the conventional periosteal releasing incision (PRI) as the flap-advancement maneuver for tension-free primary closure during vertical ridge augmentation. Flap advancement was directly measured with a UNC-15 probe; postsurgical complications (premature membrane exposure, infection, paresthesia, continuous discomfort) were tracked at follow-up, and patient discomfort (pain, swelling, bleeding) was quantified with a visual analog scale (VAS). DFI outperformed PRI on advancement magnitude and on all three morbidity metrics, with a directionally lower (though not statistically significant) complication count. This page is abstract-only: no PMC full text was retrieved, so the exact DFI surgical steps, statistical methods, and per-defect anatomic distribution are unavailable.

## Key Contributions

- Delivers **RCT-level head-to-head evidence** comparing two named flap-advancement techniques on the same defect population and outcome set — the taxonomy that [[suture-wound-closure/plonka-2017-flap-designs-flap-advancement-implant-therapy]] later organizes narratively (PRI as the technique contributing the largest single advancement gain, +5.5 mm) is here tested directly against an alternative technique in a randomized trial.
- Quantifies a **~2.5 mm advancement advantage** for DFI over conventional PRI (9.64 mm vs 7.13 mm, P < .001) in matched Seibert Class III defects.
- Shows a **consistent morbidity advantage for DFI** across pain, swelling, and bleeding VAS scores, plus a directionally lower complication count — positioning DFI as a candidate alternative to PRI specifically to reduce morbidity, not just to increase advancement.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective, randomized clinical trial; technique (DFI vs PRI) randomly assigned per defect site.
- **Population**: 23 vertical and horizontal ridge defects (Seibert Class III) requiring flap advancement for vertical ridge augmentation.
- **Primary outcome**: flap advancement (mm), measured with a UNC-15 periodontal probe as the difference between initial elevated-flap position and final advanced-flap position.
- **Secondary outcomes**: incidence of postsurgical complications (premature membrane exposure, infection, paresthesia, continuous discomfort) at follow-up; patient-reported pain, swelling, and bleeding via VAS.
- **Abstract-only caveat**: exact DFI incision/dissection steps, statistical test names, follow-up visit schedule, and maxilla/mandible distribution are not stated in the abstract and are not reconstructed.

## Results

| Outcome | DFI | PRI | P value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flap advancement (mm) | 9.64 ± 0.92 | 7.13 ± 1.45 | < .001 |
| Pain VAS | 1.55 ± 1.21 | 3.75 ± 2.63 | .019 |
| Swelling VAS | 1.91 ± 0.94 | 3.25 ± 1.29 | .010 |
| Bleeding VAS | 0.40 ± 0.12 | 1.16 ± 0.34 | .061 |
| Postoperative complications (sites) | 1 | 5 | < .082 (n.s.) |

Premature membrane exposure occurred at 2 PRI sites and 1 DFI site; paresthesia, infection, and continuous discomfort each occurred at 1 PRI site (none in DFI). Bottom line: DFI facilitated greater flap advancement and lower morbidity than conventional PRI in this small randomized trial.

## Glossary

- **DFI (double-flap incision)**: the flap-advancement technique tested here as an alternative to conventional PRI, associated with greater advancement and lower morbidity in this trial; exact surgical steps not detailed in the available abstract.
- **PRI (periosteal releasing incision)**: the conventional/reference flap-advancement technique — a horizontal incision through the periosteum at the base of a reflected mucoperiosteal flap that releases its inelastic attachment to bone, allowing coronal advancement for tension-free primary closure.
- **Seibert Class III defect**: a combined horizontal AND vertical alveolar ridge deficiency per Seibert's ridge-defect classification — the most complex of the three Seibert classes.
- **UNC-15 probe**: a periodontal probe with 1 mm graduated markings to 15 mm, used here to directly measure linear flap-advancement distance.
- **VAS (visual analog scale)**: patient-reported outcome instrument used to quantify subjective symptom intensity (here: pain, swelling, bleeding).
