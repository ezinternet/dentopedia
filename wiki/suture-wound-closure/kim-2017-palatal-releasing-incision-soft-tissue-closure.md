## Three-line Summary

Case series (n=4) describing a simple palatal releasing incision technique for tension-free primary soft tissue closure following ridge splitting and small GBR procedures.

A horizontal incision placed 5-7mm palatal to the crestal incision mobilizes the flap without buccal periosteal release, achieving tension-free primary closure with minimal postoperative swelling, hematoma, and pain in all 4 cases.

This minimally invasive alternative to buccal periosteal releasing incision is applicable to maxillary ridge augmentation and ridge splitting cases; not applicable in mandible or patients with thin palatal mucosa.

## Summary

Kim (2017) presents a simple palatal releasing incision (PRI) as an alternative to conventional buccal periosteal releasing incision for achieving tension-free primary soft tissue closure after ridge splitting and small guided bone regeneration (GBR) procedures. The technique involves a single horizontal incision placed 5–7mm palatal to the crestal incision, mobilizing the palatal flap to advance the buccal flap without periosteal scoring. Four clinical cases demonstrated successful primary closure with minimal postoperative complications. A rotated palatal flap modification is described for areas adjacent to teeth.

## Key Contributions

- **Palatal approach to tension-free closure:** Places the releasing incision on the palatal aspect (5-7mm from crestal), completely avoiding buccal periosteal release
- **Reduced postoperative morbidity:** Elimination of buccal periosteal scoring results in less swelling, hematoma, and pain compared to conventional periosteal releasing
- **Rotated palatal flap modification:** For cases adjacent to teeth where a straight horizontal palatal incision is not feasible
- **Application scope:** Ridge splitting with simultaneous implant placement, ridge expansion, and small GBR procedures

## Methodology

Case series; 4 clinical cases in the maxillary arch; no control group. Surgical technique: crestal incision → buccal full-thickness flap elevation → horizontal palatal incision 5-7mm palatal to crest → palatal flap mobilization → ridge splitting or GBR membrane placement → tension-free primary closure without buccal periosteal release. Clinical photographs documented healing outcomes at 3–4 months. Outcome assessment was qualitative.

## Results

- Tension-free primary closure achieved in all 4 cases
- No wound dehiscence reported
- Minimal postoperative swelling, hematoma, and pain in all cases
- Uneventful healing at 3–4 month follow-up
- Rotated palatal flap modification successfully applied in tooth-adjacent cases
