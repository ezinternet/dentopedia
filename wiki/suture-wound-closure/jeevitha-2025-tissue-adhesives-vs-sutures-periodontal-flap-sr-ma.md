## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42023444615, PRISMA 2020) of 10 split-mouth/parallel RCTs (255 patients, 2013–2023) comparing tissue adhesives (mostly cyanoacrylate, 2 fibrin sealant/glue) vs sutures (mostly 3-0 silk) for wound closure in periodontal flap surgery, using validated wound-healing indices (Wachtel EWHI, Huang WHI, Landry HI) at 7/14/21 days.

Pooled forest-plot analysis found no statistically significant difference in wound-healing index between adhesives and sutures at any timepoint (overall SMD −0.50, 95% CI −1.42 to 0.42, p=0.29, I²=94%; dichotomous healing RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.19–3.00), though 7/10 individual studies numerically favored adhesives at 1 week, and adhesives showed consistently lower postoperative pain (7/7 studies) and better esthetics (3/3 assessed) with faster hemostasis and fewer inflammatory cells on histology.

GRADE certainty was moderate for both outcomes, downgraded for strongly suspected publication bias (Egger's intercept p=0.003; funnel-plot asymmetry); RoB-2 found low risk of bias in 80% of trials; authors conclude tissue adhesives are "better or comparable" to sutures but caution the clinical-superiority framing is not statistically supported by the primary pooled estimates.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 + 메타분석(SR+MA, PROSPERO CRD42023444615, PRISMA 2020) — 치주판막수술(periodontal flap surgery) 창상폐쇄에서 조직접착제(주로 시아노아크릴레이트(Cyanoacrylate), 2편은 피브린 실란트/글루(Fibrin Sealant/Glue)) vs 봉합(주로 3-0 실크)을 비교한 스플릿마우스/평행군 RCT 10편(환자 255명, 2013–2023), 표준화된 창상치유지수(Wachtel EWHI·Huang WHI·Landry HI)를 7/14/21일에 측정.

통합 포레스트플롯 분석은 모든 시점에서 접착제-봉합 간 창상치유지수 통계적 유의차 없음(전체 표준화평균차(Standardized Mean Difference, SMD) −0.50, 95% CI −1.42~0.42, p=0.29, I²=94%; 이분형 치유 위험비(Risk Ratio, RR) 0.75, 95% CI 0.19–3.00)을 보였으나, 개별 연구 10편 중 7편은 1주차에 접착제가 수치상 우세했고, 술후 통증(7/7편)·심미성(평가한 3/3편)에서 접착제가 일관되게 우수, 조직학적으로 지혈이 빠르고 염증세포가 적음.

근거등급평가(Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation, GRADE) 확실성은 두 결과 모두 중등도(moderate) — 강한 출판편향 의심(Egger 절편 p=0.003, 깔때기그림 비대칭)으로 하향; 비뚤림위험도구2판(Risk of Bias 2, RoB-2)상 시험 80%가 낮은 비뚤림위험; 저자들은 접착제가 봉합과 "동등하거나 우수"하다고 결론짓지만 이 임상적 우위 서술이 1차 통합추정치의 통계적 유의성으로는 뒷받침되지 않는다는 점에 주의.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42023444615) compares tissue adhesives against sutures for wound closure specifically in periodontal flap surgery — a narrower and previously unreviewed slice of the broader tissue-adhesive-vs-suture literature. From 252 initial records, 10 RCTs (255 patients) met inclusion, using cyanoacrylate esters (isoamyl-, N-butyl-, 2-octyl-) or fibrin sealant/glue against 3-0/4-0 silk sutures, assessed via one of three validated wound-healing indices (Wachtel EWHI, Huang WHI, Landry HI). The headline pooled result is a null finding: SMD and RR for wound-healing index showed no statistically significant difference between adhesives and sutures at 7, 14, or 21 days, with high heterogeneity (I²=85–96%) reflecting differing flap techniques, adhesive chemistries, and outcome instruments across trials. Where the evidence is more consistent is in secondary, patient-centered outcomes not captured by the primary meta-analysis: every trial that measured postoperative pain (7/7) and esthetics (3/3) favored adhesives, alongside faster hemostasis and reduced inflammatory infiltrate on histology in the trials that assessed those endpoints. GRADE certainty was moderate for the primary outcomes, downgraded specifically for a strongly suspected publication bias (Egger's intercept p=0.003) rather than for risk of bias, inconsistency, or imprecision. The authors' stated conclusion — that adhesives are "better or comparable" to sutures — is more strongly supported by the secondary patient-comfort outcomes than by the (non-significant) primary wound-healing-index pooled estimate, a distinction clinicians should keep in mind when reading the abstract's framing.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA (per the authors) isolating periodontal flap surgery specifically, distinct from prior reviews on third-molar cyanoacrylate use, general intraoral incisions, or gingival-recession/FGG surgery.
- Formal publication-bias quantification (Egger's regression, Begg-Mazumdar, Rosenthal fail-safe N=848, trim-and-fill) rather than a funnel-plot impression alone.
- Surfaces a split between statistically null primary wound-healing-index pooling and consistently positive secondary patient-centered outcomes (pain, esthetics, hemostasis, histology) — a clinically useful distinction the abstract's own conclusion partially blurs.
- GRADE-rated certainty (moderate) for both continuous and dichotomous wound-healing outcomes, with the downgrade reason made explicit (publication bias, not methodological risk of bias — RoB-2 was low in 80% of trials).

## Methodology

PRISMA 2020-compliant search across PubMed, Cochrane Library, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, Embase, and trial registries plus hand search, restricted to 2013–2023, no language limits. 252 records → 196 duplicates removed → 56 title/abstract screened (35 excluded) → 21 sought full text (2 unobtainable) → 19 assessed for eligibility → 9 excluded (no wound-healing outcome, systematic review, wrong surgical site, or wrong comparison) → 10 included qualitatively / 8 quantitatively. Population: adults 18–60 undergoing periodontal flap surgery (conventional non-displaced mucoperiosteal flap in 7 studies, modified Widmann flap in 2, full-thickness mucoperiosteal flap in 1). Two independent reviewers with Rayyan blinding and third-reviewer arbitration for both screening and data extraction. RoB assessed via Cochrane RoB-2 (reviewers not blinded to authorship). Random-effects inverse-variance SMD for continuous wound-healing-index outcomes; Mantel-Haenszel random-effects RR for dichotomous outcomes; I²>40% flagged as substantial heterogeneity; GRADEpro used for certainty rating.

## Results

| Timepoint | SMD (95% CI) | p | I² |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 days | −0.25 (−1.80 to 1.30) | 0.75 | 96% |
| 14 days | −0.77 (−1.96 to 0.43) | 0.21 | 85% |
| 21 days | −0.96 (−2.54 to 0.63) | 0.24 | 93% |
| Overall pooled | −0.50 (−1.42 to 0.42) | 0.29 | 94% |

Dichotomous wound-healing outcome (2 studies): RR 0.75 (95% CI 0.19–3.00, p=0.68, I²=0%); pooled healing rate ~88% (adhesive) vs ~86% (suture) at day 7 — no significant difference either way.

RoB-2: 8/10 trials low risk overall; 2/10 (Chandra 2021, Soundarajan 2021) rated "some concerns" in the randomization domain, none rated high risk.

Publication bias: Egger's regression intercept −14.10 (p=0.003, indicating small-study effects); Begg-Mazumdar Kendall's tau −0.29 (p=0.069, non-significant); Rosenthal fail-safe N=848 (Fisher's method 3,169, i.e. a large number of unpublished null studies would be needed to overturn the pooled effect); trim-and-fill adjustment minimal.

GRADE: moderate certainty for both the mean-difference wound-healing outcome (6 RCTs, 205 vs 205 patients, MD −0.50, −1.42 to 0.42) and the risk-ratio wound-healing outcome (2 RCTs, RR 0.75, 0.19–3.00) — both downgraded from high solely for "publication bias strongly suspected," with risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, and imprecision all rated not serious.

Secondary/narrative findings across the 10 trials (not meta-analyzed): 7/10 studies found adhesives numerically better at 1-week wound-healing scores, 3/10 found comparable scores; 7/7 studies assessing pain found lower pain/discomfort with adhesives; 3/3 studies assessing esthetics favored adhesives; 2 histology studies found reduced inflammatory cells and increased connective-tissue fibers with adhesives; 2 studies found faster hemostasis with adhesives; 1 study (Pulikkotil 2014) found reduced gingival crevicular fluid IL-1β/IL-8 with fibrin sealant. All included trials excluded medically compromised patients, so no trial reports how systemic disease modifies the adhesive-vs-suture comparison.

The Discussion section additionally reports a differently-framed "pooled effect size" (g=0.42, 95% CI 0.18–0.66, p<0.001, favoring adhesives, with larger-trial subgroup g=0.55 and smaller-trial subgroup g=0.28) that sits in tension with the non-significant primary SMD/RR forest-plot results reported above — noted here as an apparent internal inconsistency in the source paper rather than something this wiki page resolves by inference.
