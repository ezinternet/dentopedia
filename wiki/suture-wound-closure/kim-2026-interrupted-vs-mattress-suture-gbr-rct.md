## Three-line Summary

A preliminary RCT (n=40) comparing interrupted sutures alone (Group S) versus interrupted plus horizontal mattress sutures (Group S+H) in horizontal GBR found no statistically significant differences in wound dehiscence width (2.92 vs 2.17 mm), length (5.32 vs 4.37 mm), or incision-ratio (0.42 vs 0.34) at 2 weeks (all p>0.05). Absolute dehiscence rates were high in both groups (60% vs 55%), attributable largely to an elderly Veterans cohort (mean age ~72), non-fixed ADM membrane, and a sensitive ≥1 mm dehiscence threshold. Both suture approaches are clinically acceptable for horizontal GBR primary closure.

## Summary

This preliminary parallel-group RCT enrolled 40 patients requiring horizontal GBR at a Veterans hospital in Seoul (June 2022–August 2023). All procedures used xenogenic particulate bone graft and non-fixed resorbable ADM membrane (Megaderm); a standardized periosteal releasing incision was performed in every case. Group S (n=20) received 5-0 nylon interrupted sutures only; Group S+H (n=20) received a horizontal mattress suture placed beyond the mucogingival junction first, followed by interrupted sutures. At 2 weeks, wound dehiscence width (primary outcome), length, and length/incision ratio were measured. No statistically significant differences emerged between groups on any metric, and dehiscence occurrence was 60% (S) vs 55% (S+H, p>0.05). The high absolute dehiscence rates require clinical contextualization: the study population was predominantly elderly male Veterans (mean age ~72 years) with elevated comorbidity burden, the membrane was not fixed (potentially increasing micromotion), and even a ≥1 mm gap was counted as dehiscence — a very broad threshold by GBR standards. The subgroup finding that 2-tooth-span defects showed numerically more primary healing than 1-tooth-span defects was counter-intuitive and unexplained, warranting replication. Overall, adding a horizontal mattress suture does not confer measurable benefit for GBR wound closure over interrupted sutures alone.

## Key Contributions

- First RCT in the GBR context to test horizontal mattress suture addition; previous evidence was from third molar and periodontal surgery where mattress sutures do show benefit.
- Demonstrates that a technique extrapolated from third molar surgery (mattress suture for tension relief) does NOT translate into significantly reduced GBR dehiscence.
- Provides quantitative dehiscence benchmarks (DW ~2–3 mm; DL ~4–5 mm; DL/i ~0.34–0.42) for horizontal GBR with ADM membrane in high-comorbidity patients.
- Explicitly labels the trial as preliminary, inviting larger, better-controlled follow-up with smoking control and longer outcome follow-up.

## Results

### Wound Dehiscence Outcomes at 2 Weeks

| Measure | Group S (n=20) | Group S+H (n=20) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| DW — mean ± SD (mm) | 2.92 ± 2.73 | 2.17 ± 2.51 | 0.478 |
| DL — mean ± SD (mm) | 5.32 ± 5.49 | 4.37 ± 4.65 | 0.718 |
| DL/i ratio | 0.42 ± 0.41 | 0.34 ± 0.38 | 0.583 |
| Dehiscence occurrence | 12/20 (60%) | 11/20 (55%) | >0.05 |

No outcome reached statistical significance. Among dehiscence-positive cases only, DW was 4.87 mm (S) vs 3.95 mm (S+H), p=0.316.

### Context for High Dehiscence Rates

The 60%/55% occurrence figures exceed many published GBR benchmarks. Three compounding factors account for most of this:
1. **Elderly Veterans cohort** — mean age ~72, high comorbidities, reduced tissue vascularity.
2. **Broad definition** — ≥1 mm constitutes dehiscence; clinically significant membrane exposures were a subset.
3. **Non-fixed ADM** — membrane micromotion under a non-anchored ADM likely contributes to early wound disruption.

These factors also mean the null result does not establish equivalence; the study was underpowered to detect moderate effect sizes.
