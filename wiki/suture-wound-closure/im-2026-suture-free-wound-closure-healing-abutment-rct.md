## Three-line Summary

This parallel-group RCT (n=30 patients; HAA: 15 patients/20 implants, Suture: 15 patients/15 implants) compared suture-free wound closure using a healing abutment attachment (HAA) versus conventional suturing at posterior maxillary and mandibular implant sites over a 6-month follow-up.
The HAA group showed significantly less KTW decrease (−0.46 ± 0.71 mm vs −1.23 ± 0.95 mm at implant level, p<0.05) and significantly greater STH increase (0.95 ± 0.47 vs 0.59 ± 0.67 mm, p<0.05), alongside dramatically shorter flap stabilization time (43.73 s vs 198.27 s, p<0.001).
Suture-free HAA wound closure is more time-efficient and better preserves buccal keratinized tissue width compared to conventional suturing at implant surgery sites.

## Summary

This RCT compared suture-free wound closure using a healing abutment attachment (HAA) against conventional suturing at posterior implant sites (maxillary and mandibular) in 30 patients randomized equally to each technique. Clinical measurements of keratinized tissue width (KTW) and buccal supracrestal tissue height (STH) were recorded at the time of surgery and at 6-month follow-up; flap stabilization time was recorded intraoperatively. HAA significantly reduced KTW loss (−0.46 vs −1.23 mm), produced greater STH increase (0.95 vs 0.59 mm), and reduced flap stabilization time by approximately 4.5-fold (44 vs 198 seconds). These findings suggest that avoiding sutures during implant surgery — using the healing abutment as a mechanical flap-adaptation device — may help preserve the peri-implant keratinized mucosa that is often reduced by tension and ischemia associated with conventional suture closure.

## Key Contributions

1. **KTW preservation**: HAA reduced buccal keratinized tissue loss by approximately 63% compared to suturing (−0.46 vs −1.23 mm, p<0.05); clinically meaningful because KTW <2 mm is associated with peri-implant inflammation risk.
2. **STH augmentation**: HAA produced significantly greater supracrestal tissue height gain (0.95 vs 0.59 mm, p<0.05), indicating a more voluminous peri-implant mucosal cuff.
3. **Surgical time reduction**: Flap stabilization was ~4.5× faster (44 vs 198 s, p<0.001), reducing procedure complexity and patient time in chair.
4. **Novel closure paradigm**: First RCT evidence that HAA as a wound-closure device — rather than a prosthetic component — may confer soft-tissue dimensional advantages over suturing in the implant context.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Randomized controlled clinical trial, parallel group
- **Participants**: 30 patients (HAA: n=15, 20 implants; Suture: n=15, 15 implants)
- **Sites**: Posterior maxillary and mandibular implant sites
- **Intervention**: Suture-free closure with healing abutment attachment (HAA) placed at surgery to stabilize the mucosal flap
- **Control**: Conventional primary wound closure with sutures
- **Follow-up**: 6 months
- **Outcomes**: KTW (implant level), buccal STH, flap stabilization time (seconds)

## Results

| Outcome | HAA group | Suture group | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| ΔKTW (implant level) | −0.46 ± 0.71 mm | −1.23 ± 0.95 mm | p<0.05 |
| ΔSTH (6-month gain) | +0.95 ± 0.47 mm | +0.59 ± 0.67 mm | p<0.05 |
| Flap stabilization time | 43.73 s | 198.27 s | p<0.001 |

HAA produced less KTW loss, greater STH gain, and dramatically faster flap stabilization versus conventional suturing across both maxillary and mandibular posterior sites.
