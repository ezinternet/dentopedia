## Three-line Summary

Preliminary RCT (n=18 implant patients undergoing simultaneous bone grafting; Tehran University of Medical Sciences) comparing diode laser (810 nm) vs scalpel periosteal releasing incision (PRI) for flap advancement.

VAS pain score (days 1-4), NSAID consumption (days 1-3), and swelling intensity (days 1-5) were all significantly lower in the laser group vs scalpel control (all P<0.05); one scalpel-group patient had ecchymosis; all implants survived in function at 6 months in both groups.

Laser PRI reduces early postoperative morbidity relative to scalpel PRI without harming implant survival, but the trial is small, single-center, and abstract-only.

## Summary

This preliminary randomized clinical trial from Tehran University of Medical Sciences compared diode laser (810 nm, 2 W, 400-μm fiber tip) versus scalpel periosteal releasing incision (PRI) for flap advancement in 18 patients undergoing dental implant placement with simultaneous bone grafting. Postoperative morbidity — VAS pain, NSAID consumption, and swelling — was tracked over 6 days, and implant survival was assessed 6 months after loading. The laser group showed significantly lower pain (days 1-4), NSAID use (days 1-3), and swelling (days 1-5) than the scalpel control (all P<0.05), with one ecchymosis case in the control group and no laser-related detriment to implant survival (100% success both groups). The trial is the randomized, controlled counterpart to the same group's earlier uncontrolled diode-laser PRI case series [[suture-wound-closure/moslemi-2016-periosteal-releasing-incision-diode-laser-gbr]] — same senior authors (Moslemi, Khorsand, Fekrazad), with Aysan Shahnaz (a 2016 co-author) now leading as first author, this time adding the scalpel comparator arm that isolates the laser's contribution to reduced morbidity.

## Key Contributions

- Adds the randomized scalpel-control arm missing from the same group's [[suture-wound-closure/moslemi-2016-periosteal-releasing-incision-diode-laser-gbr]] case series, upgrading the evidence from uncontrolled observation to comparative RCT.
- Extends laser-PRI evidence from a GBR ridge-augmentation context (Moslemi 2016) to implant placement with simultaneous bone grafting, using an 810-nm diode laser (vs 940-nm in the 2016 series).
- Shows a consistent multi-domain morbidity benefit for laser over scalpel (pain, NSAID use, swelling), not a single isolated endpoint.
- Confirms no detrimental effect of laser incision on implant survival at 6 months of loading, addressing the safety question the earlier case series alone could not settle without a comparator.

## Methodology

Preliminary two-arm RCT: 18 patients scheduled for dental implant placement with simultaneous bone grafting were randomized to test (810 nm diode laser, 2 W, pulse interval 200 μs, pulse length 100 μs, 400-μm initiated fiber tip) or control (scalpel) periosteal incision to create a tension-free flap. VAS pain score, NSAID consumption rate, swelling intensity, and ecchymosis were recorded over the first 6 postsurgical days; implant survival was assessed 6 months after loading. Randomization method, allocation concealment, and blinding are not reported in the available abstract.
