## Three-line Summary
Animal experimental study (12 adult beagle dogs) tracking the periosteal vascular plexus after mucoperiosteal flap surgery at days 7, 14, 21, and 28, using light microscopy of India ink-injected specimens, transmission electron microscopy, and scanning electron microscopy of vascular corrosion casts.
Days 7–14: the elevated plexus interstitium filled with sinusoidal new blood vessels coupled to bone remodeling — osteoclastic resorption at day 7, glomerulus-like vessels in resorption cavities and activated osteoblasts at day 14 — with highly permeable endothelial fenestrations, VVOs, and caveolae; days 21–28: the plexus regressed via endothelial/fibroblast cell death into a rough mesh structure and the bone surface became smooth.
Periosteal elevation triggers a coupled angiogenesis–bone-remodeling sequence rather than simple permanent devascularization, with the vascular changes supporting substance transport for periodontal repair; abstract-only ingestion and the canine model limit clinical extrapolation.

## Summary
This animal experimental study performed mucoperiosteal flap surgery on 12 adult beagle dogs and observed the periosteal vascular plexus at days 7, 14, 21, and 28 after surgery using three complementary techniques: light microscopy of histological specimens with intravascular India ink injection, transmission electron microscopy of ultrathin sections, and scanning electron microscopy of acryl-plastic vascular corrosion casts. In the early phase (days 7–14), the interstitium of the elevated mucoperiosteal vascular plexus filled with sinusoidal new blood vessels, and bone remodeling was spatially coupled to these vessels — osteoclastic resorption around them at day 7, and activated osteoblasts around more markedly developed, glomerulus-like vessels consistent with bone resorption cavities at day 14. The vascular endothelium showed highly permeable structures (fenestrations, putative VVOs, and caveolae) during this phase. In the late phase (days 21–28), the plexus was dissected through regression of endothelial cells and fibroblasts and reconstructed into a rough mesh structure while the bone surface became smooth. The authors conclude that the morphology of the mucoperiosteal vascular plexus changes with bone metabolism and that these changes contribute to transport of substances involved in periodontal repair — evidence that periosteal elevation induces a dynamic angiogenesis–remodeling sequence rather than simple permanent devascularization.

## Key Contributions
- First-paper-in-this-wiki direct visualization of the periosteal microvascular response to flap elevation across a 4-week healing time course (days 7–28).
- Demonstrates spatial and temporal coupling of angiogenesis with bone resorption (osteoclasts around day-7 new vessels) and formation (activated osteoblasts around day-14 vessels).
- Identifies the permeability machinery of the newly formed endothelium (fenestrations, putative VVOs, caveolae) that plausibly supports substance transport for periodontal repair.
- Shows the late-phase fate of the elevated plexus — regression and reconstruction into a rough mesh structure — defining the duration of the vascular remodeling window.

## Methodology
- **Design**: experimental animal study (no control group described in the abstract)
- **n**: 12 adult beagle dogs, mucoperiosteal flap surgery
- **Time points**: days 7, 14, 21, 28 post-surgery
- **Techniques**: (1) India ink intravascular injection → histology → light microscopy; (2) ultrathin sections → transmission electron microscopy; (3) acryl-plastic vascular corrosion casts → scanning electron microscopy
- **Outcomes**: qualitative morphology of the periosteal vascular plexus, new-vessel and endothelial ultrastructure, and coupled bone resorption/formation (no quantitative metrics in the abstract)
