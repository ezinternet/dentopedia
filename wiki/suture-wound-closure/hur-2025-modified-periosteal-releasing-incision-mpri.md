## Three-line Summary

Technique-description/update paper (abstract-only; no reported patient cohort, n, or outcome data) on the "modified periosteal releasing incision" (MPRI) — a trauma-reduced variant of the standard periosteal releasing incision (PRI) used to achieve tension-free primary closure during flap advancement in periodontal/GBR surgery.

The paper updates the MPRI technique to emphasize visualizing the mental nerve during significant flap advancement in the posterior mandible, positioning MPRI as a safer alternative to traditional PRI (which "may necessitate deeper or more incisions" and risks edema, bleeding, paresthesia, and discomfort).

## Summary

This technique-description/update paper (abstract-only ingestion) addresses the "modified periosteal releasing incision" (MPRI) — a trauma-reduced modification of the standard periosteal releasing incision (PRI), the maneuver most responsible for achieving major flap advancement in guided bone regeneration and periodontal surgery. The authors position MPRI as minimizing submucosal trauma relative to conventional PRI, which "may necessitate deeper or more incisions" and can cause edema, bleeding, paresthesia, and patient discomfort. The paper's stated update is to emphasize visualizing the mental nerve during significant flap advancement in the posterior mandible, framing nerve-damage avoidance as MPRI's central safety rationale. No patient cohort, n, comparator, or outcome data are reported in the abstract — this page should be read as a technique/safety-rationale source, not as evidence of MPRI's clinical superiority or complication rate.

## Key Contributions

- Names and updates **MPRI**, distinguished from standard PRI by minimizing trauma into the submucosa (implying shallower and/or fewer incisions).
- States the clinical problem MPRI addresses: standard PRI risks edema, bleeding, paresthesia, and discomfort when deeper/more incisions are needed for adequate advancement.
- Extends MPRI's application to **visualizing the mental nerve** during major flap advancement in the **posterior mandible**, making nerve preservation the technique's explicit safety focus.
