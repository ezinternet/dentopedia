## Three-line Summary

Cochrane systematic review + meta-analysis of 35 RCTs (n=3929 adults) comparing home-use interdental cleaning devices (floss, interdental brushes, wooden/rubber cleaning sticks, oral irrigators) plus toothbrushing versus toothbrushing alone, and devices against each other.

Low- to very-low-certainty evidence suggests floss and interdental brushes may reduce gingivitis and/or plaque more than toothbrushing alone, with interdental brushes possibly outperforming floss (low certainty); evidence for cleaning sticks and oral irrigators was limited and inconsistent, and no included trial assessed interproximal caries.

Overall evidence certainty is low to very low and effect sizes may not be clinically important — this SR functions as the foundational "floor" reference against which later, more targeted device-vs-device RCTs in this category should be read.

## Summary

This Cochrane review is the most comprehensive pooled RCT synthesis of home-use interdental cleaning devices added to toothbrushing. It covers five device classes — dental floss, interdental brushes, wooden cleaning sticks, rubber/elastomeric cleaning sticks, and oral irrigators (water flossers) — against two comparators: toothbrushing alone, and floss (or, in one comparison, interdental brushes). Across 35 RCTs (3929 adult participants), the review found consistent methodological weaknesses (unblindable interventions → high risk of performance bias; only 2 trials otherwise low risk of bias) that pull almost every GRADE certainty rating down to low or very low. The headline pattern: floss and interdental brushes show the most (if still weak) supportive evidence for reducing gingivitis and/or plaque versus toothbrushing alone, with interdental brushes edging out floss in head-to-head gingivitis comparisons. Wooden/rubber sticks and oral irrigators have thinner, more inconsistent evidence, and none of the 35 trials measured interproximal caries — a striking gap given caries is named in the review's title as an outcome of interest.

## Key Contributions

- First (at time of publication) Cochrane-level pooled synthesis across five interdental device classes with formal GRADE certainty ratings for each comparison.
- Quantifies just how weak the underlying evidence base is: nearly all pooled estimates are low or very low certainty, explicitly flagging that observed effect sizes "may not be clinically important."
- Names an evidence gap that persists in the field: zero trials assessed interproximal caries, and few assessed periodontitis (vs gingivitis/plaque surrogates).
- Provides device-vs-device secondary comparisons (interdental brush vs floss; oral irrigator vs floss; sticks vs brushes/floss) beyond the simpler device-vs-toothbrushing-alone question.

## Methodology

- Cochrane systematic review, random-effects meta-analyses (MD/SMD) via Cochrane Oral Health.
- Searches: Cochrane Oral Health's Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL (to 16 Jan 2019); trial registries for ongoing studies; no language/date restriction.
- Eligibility: RCTs, toothbrushing + home-use interdental device vs toothbrushing alone or vs another device, ≥4 weeks duration.
- ≥2 authors independently screened/extracted/assessed risk of bias and GRADE certainty; interproximal-surface indices preferred where reported.
- 35 RCTs, 3929 adults included; high risk of performance bias throughout (device use cannot be blinded); only 2 trials otherwise low overall risk of bias; many participants had low baseline gingival inflammation.

## Results

**Vs toothbrushing alone:**
- Floss: gingivitis (GI) reduced at 1 month, SMD -0.58 (95% CI -1.12 to -0.04; 8 trials, 585 participants), low certainty; bleeding/plaque results inconsistent (very low certainty).
- Interdental brush: gingivitis (GI) reduced at 1 month, MD -0.53 (95% CI -0.83 to -0.23; 1 trial, 62 participants), very low certainty; plaque reduced, SMD -1.07 (95% CI -1.51 to -0.63; 2 trials, 93 participants), low certainty.
- Wooden sticks: bleeding reduced at 3 months (MD -0.25; 1 trial, 24 participants), plaque unchanged; very low certainty.
- Rubber/elastomeric sticks: plaque reduced at 1 month (MD -0.22), gingivitis unchanged; very low certainty, n=12-30.
- Oral irrigators: gingivitis (GI) reduced at 1 month (SMD -0.48; 4 trials, 380 participants) but not sustained at 3-6 months; no plaque or bleeding-site benefit shown at any timepoint (low certainty, no difference).

**Device vs device:**
- Interdental brushes may reduce gingivitis more than floss at 1 and 3 months (low certainty); no difference for periodontitis (probing pocket depth); plaque evidence inconsistent.
- Oral irrigation may reduce gingivitis more than flossing at 1 month (low-to-very-low certainty); no plaque difference.
- Interdental brushes/floss vs cleaning sticks: no clear superiority either direction (very low certainty).

**Safety:** no severe adverse events attributable to any device; no group differences in minor effects (e.g. gingival irritation).
