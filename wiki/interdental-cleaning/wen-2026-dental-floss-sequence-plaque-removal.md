## Three-line Summary

Single-blind RCT, n=54 Chinese university students, three parallel groups comparing flossing before brushing (FB), after brushing (BF), or during brushing (BFB, mid-brushing) for 21 days, measuring RMNPI plaque index, bleeding index (BI), and probing depth (PD).

Mid-brushing flossing (BFB) produced the largest RMNPI reduction at day 7 (p=0.039, driven by a significant BF-vs-BFB difference) and significantly greater BI reduction at both day 7 (p=0.028) and day 21 (p=0.015) versus the other sequences; by day 21 the RMNPI advantage disappeared (p=0.933) and PD showed no group differences at either time point (p=0.742, p=0.422).

Flossing midway through brushing may give an early gingival-bleeding benefit and is a safe, promotable self-care variant, but the plaque-index advantage is not durable to 21 days and likely reflects a Hawthorne/novelty effect rather than a lasting biofilm-control difference — sequence appears to matter far less than the act of flossing itself.

## Summary

This single-blind, three-arm parallel RCT (n=54, university students in Guangzhou, China) asked a narrower question than most prior flossing-vs-brushing trials: given that a patient will both brush and floss, does the *sequence* — floss-then-brush (FB), brush-then-floss (BF), or floss-*during*-brushing (BFB, i.e., brush 1 min → floss 1 min → brush 1 min) — change plaque removal and periodontal outcomes over 21 days? Plaque was scored with the 9-zone Rustogi Modified Navy Plaque Index (RMNPI, scanned intraorally and quantified digitally), alongside a bleeding index (BI) and probing depth (PD).

The headline result is a **split verdict by outcome and by time**. At day 7, BFB produced the greatest RMNPI reduction (overall p=0.039, with the BF-vs-BFB pairwise comparison significant), but by day 21 this signal vanished entirely (p=0.933) — the authors attribute the early-only effect to a Hawthorne effect (novelty/observation-driven compliance that fades). BI, in contrast, showed a durable BFB advantage at **both** day 7 (p=0.028) and day 21 (p=0.015), suggesting mid-brushing flossing may have a more robust anti-inflammatory benefit than a purely mechanical plaque-removal one. PD did not differ between groups at any timepoint, consistent with a short observation window and a largely periodontally healthy cohort.

Clinically, the trial does not overturn the basic recommendation to floss daily — all three sequences are safe (zero adverse events) and all reduced plaque/bleeding versus baseline. What it adds is a caution against over-interpreting short (7-day) trials of flossing *timing* variables, since the plaque benefit that looked sequence-dependent at day 7 was gone by day 21. It also contradicts two earlier crossover trials (Mazhari et al.; Torkzaban et al., both in dental-student cohorts) that found floss-first superior to brush-first — this trial found no significant FB-vs-BF difference in a broader, non-dental-student population, a discrepancy the authors attribute to population (oral-health literacy) rather than a true reversal of effect.

## Key Contributions

- Adds a third arm (floss-*during*-brushing) not tested by prior floss-sequence RCTs, which only compared floss-first vs. brush-first.
- Separates a *short-lived* plaque-index effect (day 7 only, likely Hawthorne-driven) from a *more durable* bleeding-index effect (day 7 and day 21) for the same intervention — a useful methodological caution for interpreting short dental-hygiene RCTs.
- Extends floss-sequence research beyond dental/dentistry-student cohorts (used in prior Mazhari/Torkzaban studies) to a general university population, improving external validity, and reports a discrepant floss-first-vs-brush-first result relative to those studies.
- Uses a zonal (9-region) RMNPI with digital intraoral scanning, enabling separate interdental vs. marginal plaque reporting (though neither reached significance individually).

## Methodology

Single-blind (sealed-envelope A/B/C allocation, examiners blinded), 3-arm parallel RCT, CONSORT 2025-compliant, ChiCTR2500108845. 54 volunteers (of 60 recruited; 29 M/25 F) completed 21 days of supervised protocol: FB = 1 min floss then 2 min brushing; BF = 1 min brush → 1 min floss → 1 min brush was labeled BFB in-text (mid-brushing group); the paper's group-letter table maps Group A=FB, Group B=BF (floss after brushing), Group C=BFB (floss during brushing) — all protocols capped at 3 minutes, Bass brushing technique, standardized supplies (Sensodyne toothpaste, Kissimee toothbrush, Bmbo floss). RMNPI (baseline/day 7/day 21), BI (0–5 scale), and PD (6 sites/tooth) were assessed by two calibrated, group-blinded examiners. Repeated-measures ANOVA with Tukey HSD post-hoc (α=0.05) in R v4.4.0; sample size (54) was powered from a 6-person pilot at 95% CI / 80% power.

## Results

- **RMNPI**: significant reduction at day 7 across groups (p=0.039), largest in BFB (BF-vs-BFB post-hoc: mean diff 0.108, 95% CI [0.004, 0.210], p=0.039); no significant group difference at day 21 (p=0.933). Interdental and marginal sub-scores never reached significance.
- **BI**: significant BFB advantage at day 7 (p=0.028; FB-vs-BFB post-hoc diff 0.233, 95% CI [0.005, 0.462], p=0.045) and day 21 (p=0.015; BF-vs-BFB post-hoc diff 0.360, 95% CI [0.070, 0.651], p=0.012).
- **PD**: no significant between-group, within-group, or interaction effects at day 7 (p=0.742) or day 21 (p=0.422); PD numerically increased slightly in all arms.
- **Safety**: zero adverse events (sensitivity, allergic reaction, mucosal injury) across all 54 participants over 21 days.
- Baseline RMNPI/BI/PD/age/gender did not differ between groups (all p>0.3), supporting valid randomization.
