## Three-line Summary

Bayesian network meta-analysis (BNMA) of 22 RCTs covering 10 interdental oral hygiene (IOH) aids used as toothbrushing adjuncts, comparing plaque, gingival inflammation (GI/BOP), and probing depth reduction.

Interdental brushes (IB) ranked best for gingival index reduction (0.23 [95% CI 0.09-0.37], 64.7% probability of being best) and plaque index, followed by water-jet (WJ, 0.19 [95% CI 0.14-0.24]); unsupervised flossing and toothpicks ranked lowest, with near-zero probability of being the "best" aid.

Clinical implication: no single IOH aid is a universal gold standard — interdental brushes and water-jets are supported as effective alternatives/first-line options over unsupervised flossing, but ranking varied by outcome and most included trials had unclear risk of bias and heterogeneous populations (periodontal status often unreported).

## Summary

This is a Bayesian network meta-analysis (BNMA) — a systematic review that pools direct and indirect trial comparisons — of 22 RCTs testing 10 interdental oral hygiene (IOH) aid categories (flossing, powered flossing, toothpicks ± intensive instruction, water-jet, interdental brushes, gum-massaging devices, powered/manual toothbrush controls) as adjuncts to toothbrushing. The primary outcome was gingival inflammation (Gingival Index and Bleeding-on-Probing); plaque index and probing depth were secondary outcomes. Because most head-to-head comparisons in the literature involved only 1-2 studies per pair, the authors used an arm-based Bayesian NMA model (JAGS/rjags, MCMC) to borrow strength across the whole network and rank all 10 aids simultaneously using posterior "probability of being best" and SUCRA values.

Interdental brushes (IB) emerged as the top-ranked aid for gingival index and plaque index reduction, with water-jet (WJ) a consistent second. For bleeding-on-probing, toothpick with intensive instruction (TO) ranked highest, followed by WJ — though this result rested on a single trial and should be interpreted cautiously. Unsupervised flossing and unaided toothpick use ranked at or near the bottom across outcomes, with near-zero probability of being the single best aid. This finding is presented as corroborating (not contradicting) the 11th European Workshop in Periodontology consensus that flossing cannot be generally recommended over interdental brushes except in interdental spaces too narrow for a brush.

Importantly, the authors are careful to frame this as a technique/adherence problem rather than a mechanistic refutation of floss: correctly and consistently performed flossing likely remains effective, but its technical difficulty and poor real-world execution likely explain its low ranking against easier-to-use alternatives.

## Key Contributions

- First BNMA-based comparative efficacy ranking across the full landscape of IOH aids (10 categories, 22 RCTs), rather than a pairwise floss-vs-brush comparison.
- Quantifies floss's underperformance relative to interdental brushes and water-jets specifically for gingival inflammation reduction, providing numeric posterior estimates (GI 0.23 vs control for IB, SUCRA 95.5% for IB on plaque).
- Explicitly reframes the clinical message from "which aid is objectively superior" to "aid selection should be individualized by anatomy, ability, and instruction," a framing that recurs throughout later interdental-cleaning literature in this wiki.

## Methodology

Systematic search (Ovid Medline, EMBASE, Web of Science, 1980-2015) + manual search, PRISMA-reported, dual independent screening/extraction with third-reviewer adjudication and Cohen's kappa agreement tracking. Cochrane risk-of-bias assessment across 5 domains. Random-effects arm-based Bayesian NMA per outcome (JAGS/rjags MCMC, 4 chains x 100,000 samples after 50,000 burn-in), ranking via posterior "probability of being best" and SUCRA.

## Results

- 22 RCTs / 10 IOH categories included after screening 615 records.
- GI reduction: interdental brushes best (0.23 [95% CI 0.09-0.37], 64.7% probability best), water-jet second (0.19 [95% CI 0.14-0.24], 27.4%).
- PI reduction: interdental brushes best (0.34 [95% CI 0.12-0.56], SUCRA 95.5%).
- BOP reduction: toothpick + intensive instruction best (26.4% [95% CI 7.50-45.4], single-trial result, interpret cautiously), water-jet second (19.3% [95% CI 16.2-22.4]); floss/powered floss significant vs control but small effect size.
- PD reduction: interdental brushes ranked #1 (very sparse data — only 3 pairwise comparisons).
- Only 6/21 active-vs-active comparisons reached statistical significance for BOP — most active aids were not clearly distinguishable from one another head-to-head.
