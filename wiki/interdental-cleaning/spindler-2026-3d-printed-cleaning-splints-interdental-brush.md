## Three-line Summary

Randomized clinical crossover pilot RCT (n=30 periodontitis patients, age 21–82) comparing customized 3D-printed cleaning splints plus interdental brushes (CS+IDBs) versus IDBs alone, each participant serving as own control across 2-week intervention/washout phases.

CS+IDBs produced a markedly greater plaque reduction than IDBs alone (median ∆Quigley-Hein index −1.38 vs −0.23, p<0.001; random-effects ANCOVA CS estimate −0.62, p<0.001), and enhanced cleaning specifically in interdental spaces with missing adjacent teeth (significant CS×gap interaction).

Cleaning splints are a promising handling adjunct to raise IDB effectiveness, but gingival-inflammation/BOP benefits reached significance mainly in younger patients, and this exploratory, unblinded, 2-week pilot with a ~360 € per-device cost leaves long-term adherence and periodontal impact unproven.

## Summary

Interdental brushes (IDBs) are the most effective interdental cleaning aid, but many patients struggle with the *handling* — insertion, positioning, and reaching approximal cervical areas — especially where guide surfaces are absent (missing adjacent teeth, partial edentulism, dentures/implants). This is the first clinical trial of a **customized 3D-printed cleaning splint (CS)**: an intraoral appliance with funnel-shaped guide openings that direct the IDB into each interdental space. In a randomized crossover of 30 periodontitis patients (age-stratified ≤40 vs ≥65 y), adding the splint to IDB use significantly outperformed IDBs alone for plaque removal (∆QHI −1.38 vs −0.23, p<0.001), with the benefit confirmed by a random-effects ANCOVA and independent of the patient's manual dexterity. The advantage was largest exactly where IDBs normally fail — spaces adjacent to missing teeth. Inflammation outcomes (GI, BOP) improved overall but were only reliably significant in younger participants. The device is promising but constrained by a ~360 € per-splint cost, unblinded 2-week design, and no long-term data.

## Key Contributions

- First **in-vivo (clinical)** evidence for cleaning splints, building on an earlier in-vitro typodont study (Rues et al.).
- A **customized, universally-adaptable** 3D-printed guide splint (triangular funnel openings, incircle 0.5 mm larger than passage hole diameter) that improves IDB plaque removal without obstructing brush insertion.
- Benefit is **independent of manual dexterity** (apraxia score no influence; no CS×apraxia interaction) — the guidance mechanism, not user skill, drives the effect.
- **Greatest gain in anatomically challenging sites** — interdental spaces with missing adjacent teeth (significant CS×gap interaction), the exact cases motivating the design.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Randomized parallel crossover, exploratory pilot; each participant both experimental (CS+IDBs) and control (IDBs alone). Unblinded, block randomization stratified by age (sealed envelopes). CONSORT 2025; registered DRKS00034674.
- **Population**: 30 periodontitis patients completing (31 enrolled, 1 dropout). AG1 ≤40 y (n=15), AG2 ≥65 y (n=15). Mean age 52.5 ± 21.7 y (range 21–82). 86.7% had prior active periodontal therapy.
- **Splint**: Intraoral scan (Primescan) → 3D-printed (Freeprint Splint 2.0, Detax). Funnel-shaped guide openings, triangular cross-section; guide surfaces along terminal teeth / edentulous-adjacent surfaces; guide-to-tooth distance 1.0 or 2.0 mm.
- **Protocol**: 2-week washout → baseline → 2-week intervention → reassess → 2-week washout → crossover 2-week intervention → final. Cylindrical IDBs (Curaprox) individually sized by passage hole diameter (max 3 sizes/patient); straight in-and-out motion; standardized Elmex toothpaste during washouts.
- **Outcomes**: Primary — Quigley-Hein plaque index (QHI, Turesky). Secondary — GI (Löe & Silness), BOP, PPD, CAL, 4 sites/tooth mesial+distal. Modifiers: Goldenberg apraxia (motor), MEDAS (diet), PSS-10 (stress).
- **Statistics**: Approximal space as primary unit, two-step aggregation. No carry-over → pooled. Wilcoxon signed-rank (within), Mann–Whitney (between), random-effects ANCOVA (Patient ID random; fixed: intervention, baseline, apraxia, MEDAS, PSS; gap and region interactions). SAS JMP v18.2.1, α=0.05; p-values descriptive.

## Results

- **QHI (primary)**: median ∆QHI −1.38 (CS+IDBs) vs −0.23 (IDBs alone), p<0.001. 2-week QHI: CS+ 1.48 vs CS− 2.87 (intergroup p<0.001). Consistent in AG1 (2W p=0.006) and AG2 (2W p<0.001). Within AG1, significant QHI reduction occurred ONLY with the splint.
- **ANCOVA**: CS estimate −0.620 (95% CI −0.731 to −0.510), p<0.001; higher baseline QHI → greater reduction (−0.434, p<0.001); apraxia no effect (p=0.854).
- **GI**: CS+ 0.26→0.13 (p<0.001) vs CS− 0.31→0.24 (p=0.089); overall intergroup p=0.002; significant within AG1 (p=0.010), AG2 intergroup p=0.080. Diet quality (MEDAS) associated with reduced GI (p=0.033).
- **BOP**: CS+ 12.77%→6.27% (p<0.001) vs CS− 11.25%→9.83% (p=0.078); overall intergroup p=0.034; significant in AG1 (p=0.020) not AG2 (p=0.213). PSS×CS interaction (p=0.001) — stress modulates CS-supported BOP reduction.
- **Missing teeth / region**: gaps reduced plaque removal (+0.107, p=0.002) but significant CS×gap interaction (−0.126, p<0.001) → CS *more* effective in gap areas. Front teeth slightly better regardless of treatment; no CS×region interaction.
- **Safety**: no harms or adverse events.
