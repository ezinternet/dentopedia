## Three-line Summary
5-year follow-up of a two-arm parallel RCT (24 patients analyzed at 6 months, 22 at 5 years) of simultaneous GBR for buccal peri-implant dehiscence defects (vertical height >= 3 mm) in single-tooth implant sites, comparing an individually shaped deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM) block versus particulate DBBM, both under a native collagen membrane with pin fixation.

At 5 years mean buccal horizontal hard-tissue thickness at the implant shoulder was 1.36 +/- 1.16 mm (block) vs 0.07 +/- 0.16 mm (particulate) (adjusted mean difference -1.2 mm, 95% CI -1.9 to -0.6, p < 0.001); 45-degree thickness 0.34 vs 0.00 mm (difference -0.3 mm, p = 0.002); marginal bone level, bleeding on probing, plaque and implant survival were comparable, with no implant loss or peri-implantitis.

The block preserved more buccal contour long-term but still lost thickness (the particulate arm lost nearly all of it during the first 6 months), so the benefit is relative, not absolute; the trial was small (below the planned 34 patients), CBCT-based, and had no soft-tissue thickness or esthetic data.

## Summary
This is the 5-year follow-up of a single-center, two-arm parallel RCT (University of Zurich) on simultaneous guided bone regeneration (GBR) of buccal peri-implant dehiscence defects (vertical height >= 3 mm) at single-tooth implant placement. An individually shaped deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM) block was compared with particulate DBBM, both covered by a native collagen membrane fixed with resorbable pins. Twenty-four patients were analyzed at 6 months and 22 at 5 years. Buccal hard-tissue thickness on CBCT fell in both arms, but was significantly better preserved with the block (5-year horizontal thickness 1.36 +/- 1.16 mm vs 0.07 +/- 0.16 mm; adjusted difference -1.2 mm, 95% CI -1.9 to -0.6, p < 0.001). Marginal bone levels, bleeding on probing, plaque and implant survival were comparable, no peri-implantitis or implant loss occurred, and two block cases showed late graft separation that was removed without consequence. The authors attribute the difference to mechanical space maintenance during early healing, especially in non-contained one-wall defects at healed sites.

## Key Contributions
- 5-year clinical data (the authors state none existed) comparing DBBM block with particulate DBBM for peri-implant dehiscence GBR (follow-up of a 6-month RCT).
- The block advantage in buccal hard-tissue thickness persists at the implant shoulder (horizontal) and at the 45-degree corono-buccal position (0.34 vs 0.00 mm, p = 0.002).
- Different time courses: particulate lost most thickness in the first 6 months (2.72 to 0.39 mm) then plateaued; block lost more gradually (2.69 to 1.36 mm between 6 months and 5 years).
- Peri-implant health and implant survival did not differ, even where the buccal plate had essentially disappeared (particulate arm).

## Methodology
Two-arm parallel RCT, 5-year follow-up; adults needing a single-tooth implant with a buccal dehiscence >= 3 mm; good periodontal health, exclusions for systemic conditions affecting healing and smoking > 15 cigarettes/day. Astra Tech OsseoSpeed EV implant; particulate Bio-Oss spongiosa (0.25-1 mm) or Bio-Oss block, Bio-Gide collagen membrane with polylactide pins; re-entry at 6 months; zirconia crowns; structured maintenance program. Primary outcome: CBCT buccal horizontal thickness (HT) and 45-degree thickness (45T) at post-op, 6 months and 5 years. Secondary: marginal bone level, PD, BOP, plaque, keratinized mucosa, survival, peri-implant diagnoses (2017 World Workshop definitions). Linear mixed-effects models. Planned 34 patients (17 per group; 0.5 mm difference, SD 0.5, 80% power), considered exploratory. Registered DRKS00005803; funded by the Osteology Foundation.

Caveats: small sample (22 completers vs 34 planned), CBCT artefacts, no mucosal-thickness or esthetic data, and the block arm started thicker (3.37 vs 2.72 mm, p = 0.063). The paper's text equates the -2.01 vs -2.65 mm change difference with the -1.2 mm 5-year adjusted difference, and the mucositis denominators (22, 21) exceed the 22 completers - treat these as reporting inconsistencies (see source page section 5).

## Clinical Insights
- For a non-contained one-wall dehiscence at a healed site (the type most of these defects were), the choice of graft *form* matters over 5 years: particulate DBBM under a collagen membrane lost nearly all measurable buccal thickness at the implant shoulder, whereas the block kept a mean of 1.36 mm. The relevant clinical question is contour, not implant survival or peri-implantitis, which did not differ.
- The block is not a permanent fix: it still lost thickness (about 1.33 mm between 6 months and 5 years), and two cases developed late fragment separation. A block-based plan should include counselling about possible late fragment removal.
- Because soft tissue may have compensated for the missing buccal bone in posterior sites (the authors' hypothesis; mucosal thickness was not measured), the buccal-bone result should not be read as a prediction of peri-implant disease risk.
- Paper-supported reasoning only; the wiki does not hold the 6-month predecessor RCT (Benic et al. 2019) or any head-to-head block-versus-particulate meta-analysis, so these conclusions rest on this single small trial.
