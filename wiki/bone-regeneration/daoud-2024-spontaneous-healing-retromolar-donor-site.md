## Three-line Summary

This prospective cohort study of 20 patients used computer-assisted 3D 콘빔 전산화 단층촬영 (Cone Beam Computed Tomography, CBCT) analysis — segmentation, superimposition, and Boolean subtraction — to quantify spontaneous bone healing at the mandibular retromolar donor site 6 and 12 months after bone block harvesting.

The residual bone defect showed 64.5 ± 4.24% volumetric healing at 6 months and 89.2 ± 2.6% healing at 12 months (p<0.05). Bone density (하운스필드 단위, Hounsfield Unit, HU) decreased to 102.5 ± 27.8 HU at 6 months but recovered to 453.9 ± 91.4 HU at 12 months, approaching the original harvest density of 690.3 ± 81 HU.

Spontaneous healing of the retromolar donor site is nearly complete (89.2%) at 12 months, supporting feasibility of re-harvesting from the same site and eliminating the need for routine donor site grafting.

## Summary

Bone block grafts harvested from the mandibular retromolar (external oblique ridge) region are a standard intraoral autograft source for alveolar ridge augmentation. A question that has received limited objective study is what happens to the donor site itself: does it heal spontaneously, and if so, how completely and with what bone quality?

Daoud et al. (2024) address this with a prospective cohort of 20 systemically healthy patients who underwent ridge augmentation using retromolar bone blocks, with the donor site closed in layers and left to heal without any additional grafting material. CBCT was acquired at three time points — intraoperatively, at 6 months, and at 12 months — and analyzed with Mimics Innovation Suite (Materialise) using a reproducible 3D pipeline: thresholding segmentation, 3D superimposition, and Boolean subtraction to isolate and measure the residual defect volume and mean bone density.

Results show near-complete volumetric healing (89.2%) and substantial density recovery (453.9 HU, ~66% of harvest-site baseline) by 12 months, with no significant predictors of healing variation by sex, age, block size, or jaw side. These findings support the clinical practice of leaving the donor site ungrafted and raise the possibility of sequential re-harvesting from the same region.

## Key Contributions

- First prospective cohort using fully computer-assisted 3D CBCT analysis to quantify retromolar **donor-site** spontaneous healing (prior studies focused on recipient-site graft outcomes).
- Establishes that 89.2% volumetric fill is achievable at 12 months with zero additional grafting material.
- Characterizes the biphasic density pattern: woven bone at 6 months (102.5 HU) → mineralizing bone at 12 months (453.9 HU).
- Provides mean harvested block volume (606.5 ± 77.7 mm³) as a planning benchmark.
- No prognostic variable (age, sex, block size, jaw side) predicted healing rate — simplifies patient selection.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective cohort study
- **n**: 20 patients (8 M, 12 F; mean age 48.3 ± 11.1 years); systemically healthy
- **Harvest technique**: External oblique ridge bone block (MicroSaw technique); donor site closed in layers, no added graft
- **Imaging**: CBCT at intraoperative, 6 months, 12 months postoperative
- **3D analysis**: Mimics Innovation Suite (Materialise) — segmentation by thresholding, 3D superimposition, Boolean subtraction for residual defect volume; mean HU for density
- **Statistics**: Paired t-test, two-tailed, α=0.05

## Results

| Time Point | Volumetric Healing of Defect | Bone Density (HU) |
|---|---|---|
| Intraoperative (harvest baseline) | — | 690.3 ± 81 |
| 6 months | 64.5 ± 4.24% | 102.5 ± 27.8 |
| 12 months | 89.2 ± 2.6% | 453.9 ± 91.4 |

- Mean harvested block volume: 606.5 ± 77.7 mm³ (range 485.7–740 mm³)
- 6-month → 12-month improvement significant for both volume (p<0.05) and density (p<0.05)
- No significant differences by sex, age, block size, or jaw side

**Interpretation of density curve**: The 6-month HU (~102 HU) is consistent with immature/woven bone or organizing clot with early mineralization. By 12 months (453.9 HU), the tissue has matured toward cancellous/trabecular bone density. Full cortical restoration (690 HU equivalent) likely requires beyond 12 months.

## Limitations

- Small sample (n=20); wide confidence intervals on density values; limited generalizability
- Three CBCT time points only (radiation safety); healing trajectory between intervals unknown
- No histologic analysis (ethical constraints); HU correlates with mineralization but does not confirm bone maturity histologically
- No control group (e.g., donor site grafted with synthetic bone or collagen plug)
- 12-month endpoint insufficient to characterize complete cortical remodeling or safe re-harvest timing
