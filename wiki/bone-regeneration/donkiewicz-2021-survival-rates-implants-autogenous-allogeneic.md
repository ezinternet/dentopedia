## Three-line Summary

This systematic review analyzed 100 studies to compare implant survival rates (ISRs) of implants placed in autogenous bone blocks (AUBB, n=6861) versus allogeneic bone blocks (ALBB, n=3434 implants).

No significant difference was found between overall ISR of AUBB (96.23 ± 5.27%) and ALBB (97.66 ± 2.68%) (p=0.54). ISRs were negatively affected by extraoral donor sites and total edentulism for AUBB, and by fresh-frozen allografts and vertical augmentations for ALBB.

ALBB is a feasible alternative to AUBB for pre-implant bone grafting, but the low evidence level, absence of comparative RCTs, and short ALBB follow-up limit conclusions.

## Summary

Donkiewicz et al. (2021) conducted a PRISMA-compliant systematic review of 100 studies (3397 patients) comparing implant survival rates in sites augmented with autogenous bone blocks (AUBB) versus allogeneic bone blocks (ALBB). The review covered studies published between January 1995 and November 2021 across MEDLINE, EMBASE, and Cochrane CENTRAL databases.

The principal finding is equivalence: overall ISR of 96.23 ± 5.27% (AUBB, n=6861 implants) versus 97.66 ± 2.68% (ALBB, n=3434 implants), with no statistically significant difference (p=0.54). However, important subgroup differences within each graft category inform clinical decision-making regarding donor site selection, allograft processing, augmentation direction, and implant timing.

---

## Key Contributions

- No significant ISR difference between AUBB and ALBB graft sites (p=0.54), supporting ALBB as a viable alternative when autograft harvest is undesirable.
- AUBB ISR is significantly lower with extraoral donor sites (vs intraoral, p=0.0003) and in totally edentulous patients (93.92% vs partial 98.08%, p=0.0002).
- Immediate implant placement in AUBB grafts yields lower ISR (91.56%) than early/delayed placement (97.60%, p=0.018).
- ALBB processed allografts (FDBA/DFDBA) achieve higher ISR (98.81%) than fresh-frozen allografts (95.94%, p=0.004).
- Vertical augmentations with ALBB carry lower ISR (97.11%) than horizontal augmentations (100%, p=0.009).

## Methodology

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Systematic review, PRISMA |
| Databases | MEDLINE (PubMed), EMBASE, Cochrane CENTRAL |
| Search period | January 1995 – November 2021 |
| Studies included | 100 (81 AUBB, 13 ALBB, 6 direct comparisons) |
| Quality tools | Modified NIH tool, Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, Jadad scale |
| Statistics | Shapiro-Wilk, Mann-Whitney U, Kruskal-Wallis + Dunn's post-hoc |
| Total implants | 10,295 (6861 AUBB + 3434 ALBB) |
| Mean follow-up | 38.3 months (AUBB) / 25.1 months (ALBB) |

| Factor | Better ISR | Worse ISR | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
