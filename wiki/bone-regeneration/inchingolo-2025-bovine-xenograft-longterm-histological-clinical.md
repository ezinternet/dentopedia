## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251111685; 11 included studies from 217 screened) evaluating long-term histological and clinical outcomes of bovine-derived xenografts (Bio-Oss/DBBM) across sinus augmentation, ridge augmentation, and alveolar ridge preservation.

GRADE certainty was moderate for short/mid-term implant survival and graft integration but low for long-term complications (migration, chronic inflammation, sinus pathology); ROBINS-I found most of the 11 studies at moderate risk of bias, with only Mordenfeld (2017) rated low risk across all seven domains.

Bovine xenografts remain effective for short-to-mid-term ridge volume preservation and implant survival, but their limited resorbability creates real long-term risk (chronic inflammation, particle migration, foreign body reaction, occasional surgical removal) — autogenous bone stays the gold standard, and clinicians should individualize xenograft use with transparent risk communication and informed consent.

## Summary

This PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review (PROSPERO CRD420251111685) searched PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus (2005–2025) and screened 217 records down to 11 included studies examining long-term histological and clinical outcomes of bovine-derived xenografts (mainly Bio-Oss/DBBM) in oral surgery. Unlike prior reviews that focus on short-term volumetric stability and early implant survival, this review specifically targets the underrepresented question of long-term safety — late-onset complications such as graft migration, chronic inflammation, foreign body reaction, and sinus pathology.

Two independent quality-appraisal layers were applied: GRADE certainty-of-evidence grading (across implant survival, histological remodeling/graft integration, long-term complications, and graft-material comparisons) and ROBINS-I risk-of-bias assessment for the 11 non-randomized included studies. GRADE certainty was moderate for implant survival and graft integration (RCTs generally reported high survival, though follow-up duration was heterogeneous), but low for long-term complications, which were derived mainly from case series and observational data with likely underreporting. ROBINS-I found most studies at moderate risk of bias (chiefly confounding and missing-data domains); only Mordenfeld (2017) achieved low risk across all seven domains, while Rodriguez (2019) and Nowzari (2022) — the two case series specifically focused on long-term complications — showed moderate risk consistent with their observational design.

The discussion synthesizes 11 heterogeneous studies spanning sinus floor elevation (Barone 2005, Schmitt 2013, Mordenfeld 2017, Huang 2025, Correia 2024), alveolar ridge preservation (Couso-Queiruga 2023, Lai 2020, Kim 2025), horizontal ridge augmentation (Hashemipoor 2020), and long-term complication case series (Rodriguez 2019, Nowzari 2022). The overarching narrative: bovine xenografts (DBBM) are volumetrically stable and support good short-to-mid-term implant outcomes, but their limited biodegradability in humans means residual particles can persist for a decade or more, occasionally migrating or triggering chronic inflammatory/foreign-body reactions that surface years after apparently successful treatment. Autogenous bone consistently outperforms xenografts/allografts in osteoinductive potential and true biological integration (Schmitt 2013), while porcine-derived xenografts and platelet-rich fibrin derivatives (A-PRF) are highlighted as promising lower-risk alternatives, pending more long-term comparative data.

## Key Contributions

- First SR (per the authors) to specifically synthesize **long-term safety/late-onset complications** of bovine xenografts, rather than short-term volumetric or survival endpoints.
- Applies both **GRADE** (outcome-level certainty) and **ROBINS-I** (study-level risk of bias) frameworks to the same 11-study evidence base, producing a two-axis appraisal rarely seen in prior bovine-xenograft reviews.
- Integrates evidence across three distinct clinical contexts (sinus augmentation, ARP, ridge augmentation) under one risk-benefit narrative for the same biomaterial class (DBBM), rather than siloing by procedure.
- Names the "Proteus Phenomenon" (selective publication of early positive findings) as a structural bias explaining why complication data remains sparse relative to efficacy data in the bovine-xenograft literature.

## Methodology

- PRISMA 2020 systematic review; PubMed + Web of Science + Scopus; English-language studies 2005–2025; PROSPERO-registered (CRD420251111685).
- PICO: dental-implant patients requiring bone regeneration; bovine-derived xenografts (e.g., Bio-Oss) vs autograft/porcine xenograft/synthetic substitutes/platelet concentrates; long-term clinical/histological outcomes (graft integration, complication rates, implant survival).
- Screening: 217 records (52 PubMed, 55 WOS, 110 Scopus) → 46 duplicates removed → 171 screened → 33 excluded + 9 not retrieved → **11 included studies**; two independent reviewers (I.T., G.D.V.) with discussion-based disagreement resolution.
- Exclusions: meta-analyses, animal studies, in-vitro studies, review articles, off-topic studies, no-free-full-text studies — i.e., this SR deliberately restricts itself to primary clinical/human studies.
- Quality appraisal: GRADE (5 domains: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias) per outcome; ROBINS-I (7 domains) per included non-randomized study (RCTs excluded from the ROBINS-I visual comparison for consistency).

## Results

- 11 included studies: Barone (2005), Schmitt (2013), Mordenfeld (2017), Rodriguez (2019), Lai (2020), Hashemipoor (2020), Nowzari (2022), Couso-Queiruga (2023), Correia (2024), Huang (2025), Kim (2025).
- **GRADE**: moderate certainty for implant survival and (low-to-moderate for) histological/graft integration outcomes; **low** certainty for long-term complications (migration, chronic inflammation, sinus pathology); moderate for xenograft-vs-comparator (autograft/porcine) short/mid-term equivalence, with insufficient long-term data.
- **ROBINS-I**: most studies moderate risk of bias; Mordenfeld (2017) alone low risk across all 7 domains; Huang (2025) critical risk in "deviations from intended interventions" (novel hydraulic technique, hard to standardize); Rodriguez (2019) and Nowzari (2022) moderate risk across multiple domains, an inherent limitation of case-series/observational designs.
- Selected primary findings synthesized: Huang (2025) hydraulic sinus lift + A-PRF gave 4.05 mm mean bone gain at 6 months with zero membrane perforations; Mordenfeld (2017) RCT (90:10 vs 60:40 DBBM:autograft) found DBBM proportion did not directly correlate with clinical outcome; Schmitt (2013) found autologous bone superior in osteoinductive potential to Bio-Oss/Puros/BoneCeramic; Couso-Queiruga (2023) ARP mineralized tissue rose from 13.5% (3 months) to 37% (9 months) with DBBM-C; Rodriguez (2019)/Nowzari (2022) documented migration, chronic inflammation, oroantral communication, and cyst formation emerging 2–13 years post-grafting.
- Overall conclusion: bovine xenografts are effective for short-to-mid-term ridge preservation and implant survival, but limited resorbability is a genuine long-term concern; autogenous bone remains the gold standard; porcine xenografts and A-PRF are promising, better-resorbing alternatives pending robust long-term RCT confirmation.
