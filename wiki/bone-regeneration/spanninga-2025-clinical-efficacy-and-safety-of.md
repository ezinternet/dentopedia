## Three-line Summary

Systematic literature review (SR without meta-analysis; PROSPERO CRD42022306683, PRISMA; PubMed/Embase/Web of Science/Cochrane searched 13 May 2023) of 28 human clinical studies on ABM/P-15 — a P-15 cell-binding peptide adsorbed onto anorganic hydroxyapatite — used as a bone graft substitute: maxillofacial (18 manuscripts, 298 participants, 575 periodontal defects), spine (9 manuscripts = 7 studies, 710 participants) and trauma (1 study, 22 participants), against autologous bone graft (ABG), allograft, DBM, rhBMP-2, Bio-Oss and debridement controls.

Published results show equivalent safety and efficacy versus ABG on moderate-to-high quality evidence (RoB 2 low 46%, some concern 50%): cervical fusion 88.97% (129/145) vs ABG 85.82% (121/141) at 12 months (non-inferiority p = 0.0004) and 99.00% vs 98.20% at 72 months (p < 0.0001); periodontal defect fill 72.3% vs DFDBA 51.4% and flap debridement 40.3% (p < 0.05); lumbar fusion 50% (63/126) vs allograft 20% (23/114) at 12 months (p < 0.001); earlier fusion than ABG (97.73% vs 59.09% at 6 months) and than rhBMP-2 (mean 4.05 vs 10.0 months to fusion); and no P-15-related adverse events or allergic reactions in any study.

ABM/P-15 is a defensible autograft alternative where donor morbidity, harvest volume or availability is limiting, and it is the only peptide-enhanced bone graft substitute held in this wiki with systematic clinical outcome data — but 8/20 RCTs carried some risk of bias and one high (randomisation by birthdate), the cervical evidence comes almost entirely from a single IDE trial, most included studies were partly funded by the product manufacturer, and heterogeneity of indications and outcome definitions, plus the absence of any trauma RCT, limit generalisability.

## Summary

Spanninga and colleagues (Bone & Joint Research 2025;14(2):77–92) provide the first systematic literature review of the clinical efficacy and safety of P-15 peptide-enhanced bone graft substitute (ABM/P-15) across the whole skeleton, not only in dentistry. P-15 is a synthetic 15-amino-acid replicate of the cell-binding domain of the alpha-I chain of type I collagen (766GTPGPQGIAGQRGVV780); adsorbed onto anorganic hydroxyapatite bone minerals it promotes osteogenic cell attachment, proliferation and differentiation and improves the scaffold's osteoconductivity through receptor-mediated binding (integrins, discoidin receptor 2, fibronectin) — biological activity without the growth-factor risks that constrain BMPs. Twenty-eight human clinical studies were included (1,767 records screened; maxillofacial 18 manuscripts, spine 9, trauma 1). The review reports moderate-to-high quality evidence (RoB 2: low 46%, some concern 50%) that ABM/P-15 is equivalent in safety and efficacy to autologous bone graft, with the most distinctive signal being **speed**: cervical fusion is non-inferior to ABG (88.97% vs 85.82% at 12 months, p = 0.0004; 99.00% vs 98.20% at 72 months, p < 0.0001) while lumbar fusion is significantly earlier than with ABG (97.73% vs 59.09% at 6 months) and than with rhBMP-2 or DBM (mean 4.05 vs 10.0 vs 9.44 months). In the dental subset, ABM/P-15 improved periodontal defect fill over debridement, DFDBA, ABM alone and PRP, reduced alveolar ridge resorption after extraction, and matched Bio-Oss and ABG in sinus augmentation, but showed no added benefit in gingival recession or furcation defects. No complication attributable to ABM/P-15 and no allergic reaction was reported in any of the 28 studies (the abstract phrases this more cautiously, as "very few clinically relevant adverse events related to P-15 peptide"). Because the review pools no effect estimates, the evidence is graded narratively rather than statistically.

## Key Contributions

- **The wiki's first systematic clinical dataset for a peptide-enhanced bone graft substitute** — before this review the wiki held P-15 only as a materials-class mention (peptide-functionalised matrices, SmartBonePep) or as a product name in a commercial catalogue (SmartBone ORTHO), with no clinical effect sizes.
- **Head-to-head non-inferiority against the gold standard** — the Arnold Investigational Device Exemption (IDE) trial (n = 319) is the largest ABM/P-15 trial anywhere and delivers non-inferior cervical fusion vs ABG at 12, 24 and 72 months, plus a higher composite success rate at 1 year (69% vs 57%, p = 0.04) and 2 years (70% vs 56%, p = 0.03).
- **A quantified "faster bone formation" claim** — the one thing this material is claimed to add over autograft, expressed as fusion rate at 6–12 months and as mean time to fusion (4.05 months vs 10.0 for rhBMP-2 and 9.44 for DBM).
- **Cross-specialty evidence** — maxillofacial (298 participants, 575 defects), spine (710 participants) and trauma (22 participants) in one search, which is why a dental reader can place a bone graft substitute against surgical practice outside the mouth.
- **An explicit safety statement** — no allergic reaction or intolerability to ABM/P-15 in any assessed study, and no rhBMP-2-type serious complications (ectopic bone formation, osteolysis, retrograde ejaculation); reported problems were procedure-attributed (swelling, membrane exposure, tissue damage) or radiological (graft migration 48% vs 14% for ABG in PLIF, without clinical consequence; grade-1 cage subsidence 21.7%).
- **Longest follow-up in the material's literature** — 72 months (cervical), 60 months (lumbar), 36 months (sinus) and 3 years (periodontal), with participants from 12 countries in four continents.
- **A named evidence gap** — sinus augmentation and implant fixation need large RCTs; traumatology needs any RCT at all; studies in systemic bone disease (osteoporosis) are entirely absent from the field.

## Methodology

- **Design**: systematic literature review without meta-analysis; reported per PRISMA, registered prospectively on PROSPERO (CRD42022306683).
- **Databases and search date**: PubMed (Medline), Embase (OVID), Web of Science and the Cochrane Library, all searched on 13 May 2023.
- **Search concept**: surgery terms ("maxillofacial surgery", "orthopedic procedures", "guided tissue regeneration", "bone regeneration", "spinal fusion", …) AND ("cell-binding peptide P-15", "P-15 peptide enhanced bone graft", "synthetic peptide P-15"); filters for clinical, comparative, randomised, observational and multicentre designs, English language, full text, 1990–present.
- **Eligibility**: human clinical studies of spinal, maxillofacial or traumatological surgical interventions using P-15 peptide-enhanced bone graft, with **≥ 3 months follow-up**; malignancy- and infection-focused studies and pre-1990 publications excluded.
- **Screening and extraction**: two reviewers independently (BJS, TAH) screened and extracted, with a third assessor (JJCA) arbitrating; Rayyan.ai used for screening; reference lists hand-searched.
- **Clustering**: studies grouped by implant location (maxillofacial, spine, trauma) and tabulated per cluster and study design with narrative descriptions.
- **Primary outcome**: percentage of fusion or percentage of defect fill where available; otherwise change in clinical attachment level (CAL) or time until consolidation.
- **Quality assessment**: RoB 2 for the 20 RCTs (11 low risk, 8 some concerns, 1 high — randomisation by birthdate); MINORS for the 8 non-randomised studies (2 high quality, 6 moderate; comparative max 24 points, non-comparative max 16).
- **Synthesis**: narrative and tabular only — **no pooled estimates, no meta-analysis**, hence `evidence_level: sr`.

- **Composite success** (fusion + neurological success + improved function + no reoperation or device-related serious adverse event) favoured ABM/P-15 over ABG: 69% (99/144) vs 57% (82/144) at 1 year (p = 0.04) and 70% (81/116) vs 56% (71/126) at 2 years (p = 0.03).
- **Patient-reported outcomes at 60 months** (ABM/P-15 vs allograft): back-pain VAS 22.5 vs 39.0 (p = 0.013); Oswestry Disability Index 18.5 vs 27.4 (p = 0.029).
- **Safety**: adverse events similar between arms at 12, 24 and 72 months; **new radiculopathy was significantly more common in the ABG group** at 12 and 24 months, a difference that was no longer significant at 72 months. No allergic reaction to i-FACTOR. One ABM/P-15 patient developed a lumbar bone haemangioma and another renal cancer; two ABG patients developed chronic lymphocytic leukaemia — none indicated as intervention-related. Grade-1 cage subsidence 21.7% (ABM/P-15) vs 30.2% (rhBMP-2) vs 14% (DBM); graft migration in PLIF 48% vs 14% (ABG), radiological only, and lower than the reported rhBMP-2 migration of 75%.

### Trauma subset (1 study; 22 participants)

- Gomar 2007 pilot case series — two Spanish hospitals, June 2000 – October 2003, two patient series (9 and 13 participants; mean age 50 and 52; mean 14 months since initial diagnosis), long-bone delayed union and nonunion fractures fixed with screws-on-plates, nail plates or plaster after fracture-site treatment with particulate ABM/P-15; no control arm.
- **Consolidation in 20/22 (90%)**; bone bridging at a mean of **1.7 months** and full consolidation at **4 months**; histology on 5 patients confirmed the clinical and radiological result; one poor-healing classification, one plate failure requiring a new plate with new P-15 material (consolidation then achieved) and one loss to follow-up at 12 months.

### Methodological quality

- **RoB 2 (20 RCTs)**: 11 low risk of bias; 8 some concerns (unclear randomisation process, protocol deviations, selection of the reported outcome, measurement of the outcome); **1 high risk** — Lauweryns & Raskin 2015, because randomisation was by birthdate, which RoB 2 does not accept as true randomisation.
- **MINORS (8 non-randomised studies)**: 2 high quality (Maharaj 2016 = 13; Sathe 2022 = 19), 6 moderate (Thompson 16, Scarano 16, Degidi 15, Mobbs 12, Gomar 11, Yukna 11); quality losses from absent power calculations, missing control groups, unreported baseline measurements, no blinded endpoint assessment and no published protocol.
- **Overall evidence grade stated by the authors**: moderate (some concern 50%) to high (low risk 46%) clinical evidence of equivalence in safety and efficacy versus ABG.

## Limitations

- **No meta-analysis** — no pooled effect estimate, no heterogeneity statistic, no funnel plot; all comparison is trial-by-trial in a narrative synthesis, so cross-study comparability rests on the authors' judgement.
- **Heterogeneity** — indications, outcome measures, study designs and even the definitions of fusion differ across the 28 studies, which the authors name as the main reason for reduced generalisability; imaging modality and fusion criteria were applied inconsistently across the underlying trials.
- **Single-trial dependence in the cervical stratum** — the cervical population comes almost exclusively from the Arnold IDE trial and its two follow-ups; 33 of 322 cervical participants were lost to follow-up in year one (289 analysed: 137 ABM/P-15, 139 ABG), and the trial enrolled patients with diabetes, so the reported fusion rates may be conservative.
- **Bias profile** — 8 of 20 RCTs with some concerns, 1 with high risk of bias, and non-randomised studies lacking power calculations, protocols and blinded endpoint assessment.
- **Uncontrolled arms** — three studies (Scarano 2006, Maharaj 2016, Gomar 2007) had no control group, so their results could not be statistically verified; the trauma stratum rests entirely on one of them.
- **Industry funding** — most included studies were partly funded by the manufacturer of ABM/P-15, so unfavourable results may simply be unpublished.
- **Incomplete search** — the May 2023 four-database search missed a Korean-database PLIF series (Park et al.; ABM/P-15 95.6% vs ABG 86.9%) cited in the discussion but not includable.
- **Evidence gaps named by the authors** — sinus augmentation and implant fixation need large RCTs; traumatology needs any RCT; studies in systemic bone disease such as osteoporosis do not exist, so performance in suboptimal bone cannot be inferred.
- **Scope caution for the dental reader** — two thirds of the included manuscripts are dental, but the pooled impression ("safe and effective across the skeleton") rests substantially on spine evidence generated under an IDE exemption; the material's long-term behaviour in the jaw is documented only to 3 years (periodontal) and 36 months (sinus augmentation).
