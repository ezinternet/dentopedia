## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA-guided PubMed search, January 1970–December 2023) of 24 articles covering animal and human in vivo studies plus in vitro studies on local delivery of melatonin together with bone grafts; abstract-only ingest.

91.7% of the included studies reported better outcomes than their control groups, and the proposed mechanism is activation of osteoblastic-differentiation signaling — Wnt, Nrf2/HO-1, PI3K/AKT, MAPK, NFkB, SIRT/SOD, BMP/Smad, PERK/ATF4.

The authors conclude that locally administered melatonin may stimulate bone healing in surgical bone defects and enhance peri-implant bone response, but the abstract reports no pooled effect size, no risk-of-bias appraisal and no per-study outcome data — hypothesis-supporting rather than practice-changing.

## Summary

This systematic review asks a narrow question that sits between two fields the wiki already holds separately: does melatonin, delivered locally alongside a bone graft, improve osteogenesis? The authors searched PubMed from January 1970 to December 2023 following PRISMA instructions, using a structured search designed to capture both in vivo and in vitro studies, and screened records by title and abstract; 24 articles were included, spanning animal in vivo, human in vivo, and in vitro designs.

The synthesis is a vote count rather than a quantitative pooling: **91.7% of the included studies reported better outcomes than their control groups**. Mechanistically, melatonin is described as acting on several signaling molecules and pathways that promote osteoblastic differentiation — Wnt, Nrf2/HO-1, PI3K/AKT, MAPK, NFkB, SIRT/SOD, BMP/Smad and PERK/ATF4 — which is the osteoinductive axis that processed graft materials (xenograft, allograft) are generally said to lack. The authors' conclusion is deliberately hedged: locally administered melatonin *may* stimulate bone healing in surgical bone defects and enhance peri-implant bone response.

What this page contributes to the wiki is the **indication**, not the molecule. Melatonin in this wiki so far appears only on the periodontal axis (as a systemic NSPT adjunct scored on PPD, CAL and HbA1c); here the same molecule appears as a local osteogenic agent delivered with graft material, scored on bone formation. The two are orthogonal outcome domains, and this page must not be read as transferring the periodontal efficacy estimates to bone outcomes, or vice versa.

## Key Contributions

- **First systematic scoping held here of melatonin as a local agent delivered with a bone graft**, evaluated on osteogenesis — distinct from melatonin's periodontal/host-modulation role already held as two SR+MA pages.
- **Mechanism stated, not implied**: the eight-pathway list (Wnt, Nrf2/HO-1, PI3K/AKT, MAPK, NFkB, SIRT/SOD, BMP/Smad, PERK/ATF4) is the paper's own mechanistic claim, and it maps onto the osteoinductive gap attributed to processed grafts in the bone-graft literature.
- **Deliberate evidence-tier mix** (animal in vivo + human in vivo + in vitro) that links clinical-adjacent bone-healing findings to the cell-signaling level.
- **Vote-counting synthesis (91.7%)** gives a directional signal readable at abstract level, but leaves effect magnitude, heterogeneity and certainty unquantified — the trade-off this page's Limitations section records.

## Methodology

- **Design**: systematic literature search conducted "following PRISMA instructions".
- **Source / window**: PubMed, January 1970 – December 2023.
- **Search strategy**: structured search targeting both in vivo and in vitro studies.
- **Screening**: title and abstract screening; **24 articles** included.
- **Included designs**: animal in vivo studies, human in vivo studies, in vitro studies.
- **Not reported in the abstract** (full text unavailable): additional databases or grey literature, language restrictions, protocol registration, number of reviewers, risk-of-bias tool, and any plan for quantitative pooling.

## Limitations and Future Work

- **Abstract-only ingest** — the page is bounded by the published abstract; search string, study tables and appraisal could not be checked against the full text.
- **No quantitative synthesis** — 91.7% cannot be converted into an effect magnitude, and with no confidence intervals or heterogeneity metric the clinical weight of the signal cannot be graded from this source alone.
- **Single database** — PubMed only; non-indexed animal work may be missing.
- **Unknown human/animal/in vitro split** — the abstract names all three tiers without giving their proportions, so the share of clinically applicable human evidence is unknown.
- **Intervention not reproducible from the abstract** — melatonin dose, delivery carrier and graft type are unspecified.
- **Safety not addressed** — no adverse-event data for local melatonin in bone defects or peri-implant sites.
- **Implied next step**: standardized, adequately powered human trials of melatonin-plus-graft protocols with quantitative pooling and formal risk-of-bias/GRADE appraisal.
