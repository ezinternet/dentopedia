## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (neonatal rat osteoblasts) examining adsorption of PDGF-BB and IGF-I to anorganic bovine bone mineral (ABB) matrix and their effect on osteoblastic cell proliferation.
Both growth factors adsorbed concentration-dependently; IGF-I affinity was 10-fold higher than PDGF-BB; ~50% PDGF-BB and ~10% IGF-I released after 10 days; PDGF-BB significantly enhanced osteoblast proliferation (p<0.05), IGF-I did not.
PDGF-BB retains biological activity after ABB adsorption, providing in vitro proof-of-concept for growth factor-modified bone grafts — limited by rat cells and absence of in vivo validation.

## 세줄요약

신생 쥐 조골세포를 이용한 시험관 연구로, 무기질 우골 (Anorganic Bovine Bone, ABB) 기질에 혈소판 유래 성장인자-BB (Platelet-Derived Growth Factor-BB, PDGF-BB)와 인슐린 유사 성장인자-I (Insulin-like Growth Factor-I, IGF-I)의 흡착 및 조골세포 증식 효과를 평가함.
두 성장인자 모두 농도 의존적으로 흡착; IGF-I 친화도가 PDGF-BB보다 10배 높음; 10일 후 PDGF-BB ~50%, IGF-I ~10% 방출; PDGF-BB만 조골세포 증식을 유의하게 증가(p<0.05), IGF-I는 유의하지 않음.
PDGF-BB는 ABB 흡착 후에도 생물학적 활성을 유지 — 성장인자 변형 골이식재의 개념 증명을 제공하지만, 동물 세포 사용 및 생체 내 검증 부재로 직접 임상 적용에 한계.

## Summary

This in vitro study from SUNY Buffalo investigated whether growth factors (혈소판 유래 성장인자-BB (PDGF-BB) and 인슐린 유사 성장인자-I (IGF-I)) could be adsorbed to anorganic bovine bone mineral (ABB; equivalent to DBBM/Bio-Oss) and whether the adsorbed factors would retain biological activity to stimulate osteoblast proliferation.

Using ¹²⁵I-radiolabeled growth factors, the study found that both PDGF-BB and IGF-I adsorbed to ABB in a concentration-dependent manner. IGF-I demonstrated approximately 10-fold higher affinity for ABB compared to PDGF-BB. Release kinetics at 10 days showed that ~50% of adsorbed PDGF-BB was released from ABB, while only ~10% of IGF-I was released, consistent with its higher binding affinity.

Cell proliferation assays (³H-thymidine incorporation) using primary neonatal rat osteoblasts cultured on ABB showed that PDGF-BB-loaded ABB significantly enhanced osteoblastic cell proliferation compared to control (p<0.05). IGF-I-loaded ABB did not produce a statistically significant proliferative effect despite its higher adsorption.

The findings establish that (1) ABB can serve as a carrier for growth factors, (2) PDGF-BB adsorbed to ABB retains mitogenic activity, and (3) the biological effect is growth-factor-specific. This represented early in vitro evidence that bone graft materials could be biologically enhanced by combining them with growth factors — a concept that subsequently evolved into products such as GEM 21S (PDGF-BB + β-TCP).

## Key Contributions

- First demonstration that PDGF-BB and IGF-I can be adsorbed to ABB with retained biological activity
- Quantification of relative binding affinities: IGF-I affinity ~10× higher than PDGF-BB for ABB matrix
- Differential release kinetics: PDGF-BB (~50% released/10 days) vs. IGF-I (~10% released/10 days)
- PDGF-BB-ABB combination stimulates osteoblast proliferation — IGF-I-ABB does not, despite higher binding
- Establishes ABB as a viable growth factor carrier — foundational for growth factor-enhanced graft development

## Methodology

In vitro experimental study. Anorganic bovine bone mineral (ABB) matrix loaded with ¹²⁵I-labeled PDGF-BB or IGF-I at varying concentrations. Adsorption measured by radioactivity remaining in ABB after incubation; release kinetics tracked over 10 days. Primary neonatal rat osteoblastic cells seeded on ABB-growth factor complexes; cell proliferation assessed by ³H-thymidine incorporation at defined time points. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) used for morphological assessment of cell attachment.

## Results

- **Adsorption**: Both PDGF-BB and IGF-I adsorb to ABB concentration-dependently
- **Relative affinity**: IGF-I ~10-fold higher binding affinity than PDGF-BB
- **Release at Day 10**: ~50% of PDGF-BB released; ~10% of IGF-I released
- **Cell proliferation (PDGF-BB)**: Significantly enhanced osteoblastic proliferation vs. control (p<0.05)
- **Cell proliferation (IGF-I)**: No significant proliferative effect
- **SEM**: Normal osteoblastic morphology and attachment observed on ABB surface
