## Three-line Summary

Within-animal comparative histomorphometric study in 6 Beagle dogs (23 analyzable sockets) comparing autogenous tooth root graft (chairside-processed dentin particulate) vs. collagenated equine xenograft for alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) after bilateral mandibular premolar extraction, evaluated at 3 months.

Tooth root graft sites showed near-complete replacement by vital bone (65.6 ± 9.1% total bone, residual graft only 0.5 ± 1.1%) versus xenograft sites (57.8 ± 17.2% total bone, 19.7 ± 16.0% residual graft remnants, p = 0.032); xenograft sites retained a slightly taller buccal crest (12.3 ± 1.8 mm vs 11.8 ± 1.7 mm, p = 0.031) and greater peripheral bone extension (0.52 ± 0.23 mm vs 0.20 ± 0.12 mm, p < 0.0001).

Tooth-derived grafts favor near-complete "vital bone" regeneration with minimal residual biomaterial, while xenografts favor slightly better buccal contour stability but leave persistent hybrid-bone particles — a biologically plausible but clinically unconfirmed trade-off, since no implants were placed in this small (n=6 dogs), single-time-point (3-month) animal study.

## 세줄요약

비글견 6마리(분석 가능 23개 발치와)를 이용한 within-animal 비교 조직형태계측 연구로, 자가 치근유래 그라프트(chairside 가공 상아질 입자)와 콜라겐 코팅 이종골을 하악 소구치 양측 발치 후 치조제보존술(Alveolar Ridge Preservation, ARP)에 적용해 3개월 시점에 비교.

치근유래 그라프트 부위는 거의 완전한 생활골(vital bone) 대체를 보인 반면(총 골량 65.6 ± 9.1%, 잔존 그라프트 0.5 ± 1.1%), 이종골 부위는 총 골량 57.8 ± 17.2%에 잔존 그라프트 입자가 19.7 ± 16.0% 남았고(p = 0.032), 협측 치조정 높이는 이종골군이 근소하게 더 높았다(12.3 ± 1.8mm vs 11.8 ± 1.7mm, p = 0.031).

치아유래 그라프트는 잔존 생체재료가 거의 없는 생활골 재생에 유리하고 이종골은 협측 윤곽(buccal contour) 안정성에 근소하게 유리한 trade-off를 보이나, 임플란트가 실제 식립되지 않아 골유착(osseointegration)에 대한 함의는 생물학적으로 그럴듯하나 임상적으로 미확인이며, 표본이 작고(개 6마리) 단일 3개월 시점만 평가한 한계가 있음.

## Summary

This within-animal (split-mouth-style) study in 6 Beagle dogs directly compared two ARP graft materials at the histological level for the first time: an autogenous tooth root graft produced chairside by an automated device (Tooth Transformer — fragmentation, demineralization, detoxification of the patient's own extracted tooth root in ~25 minutes) versus a collagenated equine xenograft (OsteOXenon + Heart collagen membrane). Twenty-four post-extraction sockets (12 per material) were created by bilateral extraction of mandibular 3rd/4th premolars; one site was excluded for a peri-extraction buccal bone fracture, leaving 23 sites for analysis. Baseline socket dimensions (depth, width, buccal wall thickness) were equivalent between groups, so the 3-month endpoint differences reflect graft biology rather than anatomical confounds.

The central finding is a clear divergence in regeneration "quality" versus contour "quantity." Tooth root graft sites were almost completely replaced by vital bone (total bone 65.6%, residual graft material essentially zero at 0.5%), with frequent coronal corticalization sealing the socket entrance. Xenograft sites retained substantially more residual graft material (19.7% overall, 20.3% in the coronal region specifically — the zone most relevant to future implant placement), consistent with the known slow resorption kinetics of xenogeneic bone substitutes. In exchange, the xenograft group maintained a slightly (but statistically significantly) taller buccal crest and greater peripheral bone extension, suggesting somewhat better volumetric/contour stability during the same healing window.

The authors frame this as "vital bone" vs. "hybrid bone" regeneration — a meaningful distinction because residual, non-resorbed graft particles at the coronal/implant-site level have been associated elsewhere in the literature with impaired osseointegration when implants are placed into grafted sites. However, this study did not place implants, so that clinical translation remains inferential. No inflammatory infiltrates were observed in any specimen from either group, and the tooth-processing device's chairside decontamination protocol appeared effective (biocompatible outcome) even though the initial handling of the extracted tooth was non-sterile.

## Key Contributions

- First histological head-to-head between a purely tooth-root-derived (dentin) particulate graft and a collagenated xenograft for ARP, using a rigorous within-animal design that controls for individual biological variability.
- Quantifies the vital-bone vs. residual-graft trade-off precisely: tooth graft ≈0.5% residual material / 65.6% total bone vs. xenograft ≈19.7% residual material / 57.8% total bone (p = 0.032 for residual graft).
- Coronal-region-specific histomorphometry (the zone most clinically relevant to subsequent implant placement) shows xenograft sites retain ~20% residual graft there versus 0% in tooth-graft sites (p = 0.044).
- Demonstrates chairside automated tooth-processing device safety/biocompatibility (Tooth Transformer) — no inflammatory response despite non-sterile preliminary tooth handling, decontamination effectively re-established sterility.
- Identifies a buccal-contour-stability advantage for the (slower-resorbing) xenograft, framing a potential combination strategy (e.g., cortical lamina + faster-turnover graft) for future protocol design.

## Methodology

- 6 adult female Beagle dogs; bilateral extraction of mandibular 3rd/4th premolars; 2 sockets per animal per graft type (24 sites total, 12/group), 23 analyzed after 1 exclusion (buccal bone fracture).
- Digital randomization with allocation concealment (sealed envelopes) and blinded histological analysis.
- Materials: OsteOXenon equine cortical-cancellous granules (0.25–1.0 mm) + Heart equine-pericardium collagen membrane (xenograft group) vs. Tooth Transformer-processed autogenous root particulate + same collagen membrane (tooth-graft group). No fixation, semi-submerged 4-0 Vicryl closure.
- 3-month healing period, then euthanasia and histomorphometric analysis (Technovit 7200 VLC embedding, Levai–Laczkó staining, ~50 µm sections, Nikon Eclipse/NIS-Elements imaging).
- Statistics: Shapiro–Wilk normality test, paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test as appropriate, α = 0.05, animal-level averaging for the primary analysis (a priori G*Power sample size: 6 dogs for 80% power, Cohen's dz ≈ 1.0).

## Results

- Lingual crest height (L–X): identical between groups, 14.8 ± 1.8 mm.
- Buccal crest height (B–X): 11.8 ± 1.7 mm (tooth graft) vs 12.3 ± 1.8 mm (xenograft), p = 0.031.
- L–B vertical discrepancy: 3.0 ± 1.2 mm (tooth graft) vs 2.5 ± 0.5 mm (xenograft), not significant.
- Total bone: 65.6 ± 9.1% (tooth graft) vs 57.8 ± 17.2% (xenograft).
- Residual graft material: 0.5 ± 1.1% (tooth graft) vs 19.7 ± 16.0% (xenograft), p = 0.032.
- Peripheral bone extension beyond L–B line: 0.20 ± 0.12 mm (tooth graft) vs 0.52 ± 0.23 mm (xenograft), p < 0.0001.
- Coronal-region total bone: 59.8 ± 14.8% (tooth graft) vs 42.3 ± 16.2% (xenograft), p = 0.239 (not significant, high variability).
- Coronal-region residual graft: 0% (tooth graft) vs 20.3 ± 16.5% (xenograft), p = 0.044.
- No inflammatory infiltrates, multinucleated giant cells, or adverse events in either group.
