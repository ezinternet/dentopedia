## Three-line Summary
Multicenter noninferiority RCT (n=49) comparing simultaneous guided bone regeneration at peri-implant dehiscences using synthetic bone substitute (biphasic calcium phosphate, SBS) versus deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM), both combined with a bioabsorbable collagen membrane, across two centers in Seoul and Madrid. Defect height reduction was 74.5% (SBS) versus 63.4% (DBBM) at 6-month re-entry (p=0.216, noninferiority confirmed); complete defect resolution reached 62.5% versus 44%, and marginal bone levels remained stable through 1 year in both groups. Biphasic calcium phosphate is a clinically valid xenograft-free alternative for GBR at dehiscences, relevant for patients with religious, ethical, or disease-transmission concerns, though the small sample (n=49) and retrospective trial registration limit confidence.

## One-line Summary
Multicenter noninferiority RCT (n=49): synthetic bone substitute (biphasic calcium phosphate) is noninferior to DBBM for GBR at peri-implant dehiscences; defect resolution 74.5% (SBS) vs. 63.4% (DBBM), p=0.216; marginal bone stable at 1-year in both groups.

## Summary
This multicenter noninferiority RCT (Yonsei + Universidad Complutense Madrid) randomized 49 patients with expected peri-implant dehiscence defects to GBR using synthetic bone substitute (SBS, biphasic calcium phosphate) versus deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM), both combined with bioabsorbable collagen membrane. At 6-month re-entry, defect height reduction was 74.5% for SBS and 63.4% for DBBM (p=0.216), confirming noninferiority. Complete defect resolution: 62.5% (SBS) vs. 44% (DBBM), not significantly different. Marginal bone levels remained stable through 1-year follow-up in both groups. SBS (biphasic calcium phosphate) is an effective alternative to xenogenic DBBM for patients with religious, ethical, or safety concerns.

## Key Contributions
- First multicenter RCT directly comparing SBS (biphasic calcium phosphate) vs. DBBM for simultaneous GBR at dehiscences
- Noninferiority design with appropriate power — rigorous evidence standard
- Higher complete defect resolution rate trend for SBS (62.5% vs. 44%) — though not statistically significant (possibly underpowered)
- Provides a xenograft-free option that avoids disease transmission, immunogenicity, and religious objections
- Both groups show stable marginal bone up to 19 months post-surgery

## Methodology
Multicenter noninferiority RCT, two centers (Seoul + Madrid). n=49 (SBS=24, DBBM=25). Patients with expected dehiscence defects after implant placement randomized to SBS or DBBM + bioabsorbable collagen membrane. Primary outcome: defect height (DH) change from implant insertion to 6-month re-entry. Secondary: peri-implant health, survival, bone levels, PROMs. Registration: KCT0008393 (retrospective — not pre-registered, limitation). Follow-up: 1 year post-implant placement.

## Results
- Defect height at insertion: SBS 5.1 ± 2.6 mm; DBBM 4.1 ± 1.7 mm
- Defect height at re-entry (6 months): SBS 1.3 ± 2.0 mm; DBBM 1.5 ± 1.9 mm
- Defect height reduction: SBS 74.5% vs. DBBM 63.4% (p=0.216, noninferiority confirmed)
- Complete defect resolution: SBS 62.5% (15/24) vs. DBBM 44% (11/25), p=NS
- Marginal bone levels: stable through 1 year (both groups)
- Implant survival: high in both groups
