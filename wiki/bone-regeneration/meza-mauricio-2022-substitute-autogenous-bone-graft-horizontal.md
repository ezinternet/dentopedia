## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (n=109; 55 test, 54 control) comparing allogeneic bone graft + autogenous bone graft versus allogeneic bone graft alone for horizontal guided bone regeneration (GBR) prior to implant placement.

No statistically significant difference in horizontal bone gain was found between groups, either at 0 mm from the crest (MD: -0.46 mm; 95%CI: -1.03 to 0.11) or at 4-5 mm from the crest (MD: 0.17 mm; 95%CI: -1.08 to 1.42); graft resorption, histology, and complication rates were also similar between groups.

GRADE certainty was very low for both outcomes (risk-of-bias concerns in all included studies, small samples); adding autogenous bone to allograft did not demonstrate additional benefit for horizontal bone gain, while autograft harvesting is known to add donor-site morbidity.

## Summary

This SR+MA (PRISMA-compliant, INPLASY-registered) asked whether combining a substitute (allogeneic) bone graft with autogenous bone graft improves horizontal bone gain compared with substitute graft alone, in patients undergoing horizontal GBR before implant placement. Four RCTs (2010-2020; USA, Israel, Iran; n=109 total, ages 21-73) met inclusion criteria, all using freeze-dried allogeneic bone graft as the "substitute" material combined with or without particulate autogenous bone (autograft:allograft ratios 50:50 or 30:70). Two meta-analyses were performed at different measurement distances from the crestal alveolar ridge (0 mm and 4-5 mm), each with membrane-type subgroup analysis (collagen membrane vs acellular dermal matrix). Neither meta-analysis found a statistically significant between-group difference in horizontal bone gain, and secondary outcomes (graft resorption, histology, complications, PROMs) also showed no significant between-group differences. GRADE certainty was rated very low for both primary outcomes due to risk-of-bias concerns across all included trials and small sample sizes.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA to specifically isolate the "add autograft or not" question for horizontal GBR bone gain, using two clinically meaningful measurement landmarks (0 mm and 4-5 mm from crest).
- Demonstrates that the theoretical osteogenic/osteoinductive advantage of autogenous bone does not translate into a measurable horizontal bone-gain advantage when mixed with allograft in this pooled dataset.
- Cross-references an independent RCT showing that autogenous block grafting (a more invasive autograft technique) carries significantly higher morbidity (sensory disturbance p=0.02, hematoma p=0.002) without a corresponding bone-gain benefit versus GBR alone — reinforcing a morbidity-without-benefit pattern across autograft-inclusion strategies.
- Applies GRADE explicitly, giving clinicians an honest "very low certainty" flag rather than overstating the null result.

## Methodology

- PICOS: horizontal-ridge-deficiency patients undergoing GBR; comparison = allograft+autograft vs allograft alone; primary outcome = horizontal bone gain; RCTs only.
- 4 databases searched to June 2021 + grey literature + hand searches; dual independent screening/extraction/RoB assessment with third-reviewer arbitration (Kappa ≥0.85).
- Cochrane RoB 2 tool for bias assessment; RevMan 5.3 random-effects meta-analysis (MD, 95%CI); heterogeneity via Cochran's Q and I²; GRADE for certainty of evidence.
- Two separate meta-analyses by crest-distance landmark (0 mm; 4-5 mm), each with membrane-type subgroup analysis.

## Results

| Outcome | Studies | MD (95% CI) | Heterogeneity (I²) | Significant? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Horizontal bone gain @ 0 mm from crest | 4 | -0.46 mm (-1.03 to 0.11) | 22% (low) | No |
| Horizontal bone gain @ 4-5 mm from crest | 2 | 0.17 mm (-1.08 to 1.42) | 57% (moderate/high) | No |

- Graft resorption (2 studies): numerically higher in the autograft+allograft combination group in both, but not statistically significant (e.g., 16.91% vs 11.12%; 30% vs 25%).
- Histology (2 studies): no significant between-group differences in vital/new bone percentage.
- No PROMs reported in any included trial.
- GRADE certainty: very low for both meta-analysis outcomes, due to risk-of-bias concerns in all 4 studies and small sample sizes.
- Limitations: only 4 RCTs, small defect sizes (2-4 missing teeth only — not generalizable to larger ridge defects), high risk of bias in most included studies, inherent lack of blinding.
