## Three-line Summary
This case series (4 cases) describes clinical applications of maxillary tuberosity (MT) block autografts for treating small-to-moderate localized alveolar defects, including immediate implant placement into type II/III sockets, horizontal/vertical defects, and circumferential defects. In all four cases, MT block autograft (alone or as a "triple graft" with connective tissue) successfully reconstructed defects, with stable bone and soft tissue outcomes up to 6 years post-treatment and no donor site complications reported. MT block autograft offers advantages over other intraoral donor sites (easier harvest, less morbidity, no donor-site restoration needed), but is limited by small tuberosity size and variable anatomy requiring careful preoperative CBCT assessment.

## Summary
Zufia & Abella Sans (2022) present four cases in which the maxillary tuberosity (MT) served as an intraoral autograft donor site for localized alveolar defect reconstruction. The cases span a range of clinical scenarios: a type II socket at immediate implant placement, a type III socket with a circumferential defect, a horizontal ridge defect, and a combined vertical/horizontal defect. A key contribution is the description of the "triple graft" technique, in which a single tuberosity harvest yields a corticocancellous bone block, particulate bone, and a connective tissue graft — enabling simultaneous hard and soft tissue augmentation in a single surgical session (Immediate Dentoalveolar Restoration, IDR protocol). All four cases showed stable bone and soft tissue outcomes at follow-up periods of up to six years, with no donor-site complications.

## Key Contributions

### Maxillary Tuberosity as Intraoral Donor Site
The MT provides corticocancellous bone of suitable density for small-to-moderate defects. Compared with the chin (symphysis) and retromolar pad/ramus:
- **No donor-site restoration required** — the tuberosity is not load-bearing and heals by secondary intention.
- **Lower neurovascular risk** — distal to the inferior alveolar nerve; risk of oroantral communication is low with careful technique.
- **Suitability post-third-molar extraction** — post-extraction remodeling can increase available volume.
- **Limitation**: Total harvestable volume is small and highly variable; not suitable for large defects.

### Triple Graft Technique
A single full-thickness flap distal to the maxillary second molar exposes the tuberosity, allowing sequential harvest of:
1. Connective tissue from the palatal aspect (soft tissue augmentation)
2. Corticocancellous bone block using a flat chisel (hard tissue reconstruction)
3. Residual cancellous particulate bone

This single-stage approach is particularly suited to IDR (Immediate Dentoalveolar Restoration, 즉시 치아-치조골 재건) in cases with type III sockets or thin biotype where buccal bone deficiency and keratinized tissue deficit coexist.

### Preoperative Planning
CBCT (Cone Beam Computed Tomography, 콘빔 전산화 단층촬영) assessment of tuberosity dimensions is mandatory before committing to this donor site. Variable anatomy — ranging from 2 mm to >10 mm of cancellous bone — directly determines graft volume and case selection.

## Methodology
- **Study design**: Prospective case series; n=4 patients (ages 38, 55, 50, 46).
- **Surgical technique**: Full-thickness crestal incision distal to maxillary second molar; mucoperiosteal flap; connective tissue harvested from palatal tuberosity; corticocancellous block harvested with 1-cm flat chisel and surgical hammer.
- **Fixation**: Cortical screws, titanium pins, or implant primary stability; primary tension-free closure in all cases.
- **Adjuncts**: Collagen membrane and/or particulate bone substitute used as needed.
- **Evaluation**: Clinical (probing depth, recession, keratinized tissue width) + radiographic (CBCT + periapical) at multiple intervals up to 6 years.

## Results
| Case | Defect Type | Technique | Follow-up | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Type II socket | Triple graft + immediate implant | 6 years | Stable bone + soft tissue |
| 2 | Type III socket (circumferential) | Triple graft + palatal anchorage implant | 4 years | Stable buccal plate, healthy mucosa |
| 3 | Horizontal ridge defect | Block + membrane (staged) | 3 years | Adequate width for implant |
| 4 | Combined vertical/horizontal | Block + membrane (staged) | 2 years | Volume restored; implant placed |

No oroantral communication, hematoma, or nerve injury at donor site in any case.
