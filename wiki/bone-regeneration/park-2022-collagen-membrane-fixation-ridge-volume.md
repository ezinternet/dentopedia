## Three-line Summary

Animal study (8 beagle dogs, split-mouth) comparing fixed vs. unfixed placement of two non-cross-linked collagen membranes in contained GBR defects with particulate bone substitute, assessed by micro-CT and histomorphometry at 8 weeks.
Membrane fixation did not improve total augmented volume for either membrane type (p > .05); CM2-F showed significantly greater ridge-crest augmented tissue width than CM2-UF (2.3 ± 0.1 mm vs. 1.57 ± 0.27 mm, p < .05), but no such difference was found for CM1.
Fixation is not universally beneficial in contained GBR defects; its coronal-width effect is membrane-type dependent, suggesting surgeons should not assume fixation always improves outcomes.

## Summary

This preclinical split-mouth experiment in 8 beagle dogs tested whether securing a collagen barrier membrane with fixation (pins/tacks) improves ridge volume outcomes in contained GBR defects. Two commercially distinct non-cross-linked collagen membranes (CM1, CM2) were tested with or without fixation over a particulate bone substitute in box-shaped defects created after maxillary pre-molar extraction. After 8 weeks, micro-CT and histomorphometric analyses showed that total augmented volume was not significantly different between fixed and unfixed conditions for either membrane. However, CM2 showed a fixation benefit specifically at the coronal portion of the ridge (2.3 ± 0.1 mm vs. 1.57 ± 0.27 mm, p < .05), while CM1 did not. These findings suggest that membrane fixation in contained defects is not universally advantageous, and that the structural properties of the membrane itself determine whether fixation provides additional mechanical support.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that membrane fixation does not improve total ridge volume stability in contained GBR defects regardless of membrane type
- Identifies a membrane-type-dependent fixation effect at the ridge crest (coronal portion) — effect present for CM2 but absent for CM1
- Implies that the biomechanical properties of the membrane (stiffness, rigidity) interact with fixation to determine whether fixation adds value
- Provides animal-level evidence that routine fixation in contained defects may not be necessary, challenging the assumption that fixation always improves GBR predictability

## Methodology

- **Design**: In vivo animal experiment, bilateral split-mouth controlled model
- **Subjects**: 8 beagle dogs; bilateral maxillary pre-molar extraction, box-shaped defects created
- **Intervention**: All defects augmented with particulate bone substitute, covered with either CM1 or CM2 (non-cross-linked collagen membranes), each with fixation (-F) or without fixation (-UF)
- **Follow-up**: 8 weeks
- **Outcome measures**: Total augmented volume (micro-CT); augmented tissue width at ridge crest (histomorphometry)

## Results

| Group | Ridge-crest augmented tissue width | Statistical significance |
|---|---|---|
| CM1-F | 2.4 ± 0.4 mm | No sig. diff. vs. CM1-UF |
| CM1-UF | 2.4 ± 0.5 mm | — |
| CM2-F | 2.3 ± 0.1 mm | **p < .05 vs. CM2-UF** |
| CM2-UF | 1.57 ± 0.27 mm | — |

- Total augmented volume: no significant difference between fixed and unfixed for both CM1 and CM2 (p > .05)
- Fixation improved coronal ridge width for CM2 but not CM1, indicating a membrane-specific effect
