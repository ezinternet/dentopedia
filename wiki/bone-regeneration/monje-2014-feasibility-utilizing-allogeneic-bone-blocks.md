## Three-line Summary

Systematic review with weighted-mean meta-analysis (random effects) of 15 prospective human studies (14 case series, 1 RCT; NOS 6.06 ± 1.04) on allogeneic bone block grafts for atrophic maxillary augmentation; 361 blocks followed 4–9 months.
9 blocks failed (2.4%; cumulative block survival 98%), all within 1–2 months, mainly from early membrane exposure; weighted horizontal gain was 4.79 mm (95% CI 4.51–5.08, 119 sites) and implant survival 96.9% (95% CI 92.8–98.7%, 228 implants, mean 23.9 months).
Allogeneic blocks look like a reliable alternative to autogenous blocks in the maxilla, but heterogeneous histology (necrotic bone persisting in some series), little vertical data and small samples mean the results need cautious interpretation.

블록 9개(2.4%)가 수술 후 1–2개월 내에 실패했고(누적 블록 생존율 98%) 주원인은 조기 차폐막 노출이었으며, 가중 수평 골증대량은 4.79 mm(95% 신뢰구간 4.51–5.08, 119부위), 임플란트 생존율은 96.9%(95% 신뢰구간 92.8–98.7%, 임플란트 228개, 평균 23.9개월)였다.

## Summary

Monje et al. (2014) systematically reviewed prospective human studies of allogeneic bone block grafts used to reconstruct the atrophic maxilla, searching MEDLINE, EMBASE and Cochrane sources for English articles from 2000 to 2013. Of 239 records screened, 15 studies (14 prospective case series, 1 RCT) were included, with moderate methodological quality (Newcastle-Ottawa scale 6.06 ± 1.04). Among 361 blocks followed 4–9 months, 9 failed (2.4%), all within the first 1–2 months, with early membrane exposure the main cause and fixation-screw loosening second. Pooled horizontal gain was 4.79 mm and implant survival in allograft-augmented maxillae was 96.9% over a mean 23.9 months. The authors conclude that allogeneic blocks are a reliable alternative to autogenous blocks for the atrophic maxilla, while stressing heterogeneity, small samples and unresolved late-stage graft fate.

## Key Contributions

- Pools prospective human data on allogeneic block grafts restricted to the atrophic maxilla (block survival, bone gain, implant survival, histology in one review).
- Shows block failures are early (≤2 months) and mostly attributable to early membrane exposure, then screw loosening; odds of success rise from month 3.
- Provides benchmark values: block survival 98%, horizontal gain 4.79 mm, implant survival 96.9% at ~24 months.
- Documents divergent histology (viable new bone with persisting nonvital allograft in some series; large necrotic segments with no direct host-graft contact in another), i.e. allogeneic blocks behave differently from autogenous blocks early in healing.

## Methodology

Systematic review (one reviewer searched; one blinded investigator analyzed). Eligible: prospective human trials of allograft bone blocks reporting survival; excluded case reports/series with <10 subjects, SRs, animal, retrospective and unclear-data studies. Weighted means with 95% CI by random-effects model (block site as unit for horizontal gain, implant as unit for survival); Kaplan-Meier for block survival; quality by Newcastle-Ottawa scale. Selection flow 239 -> 109 -> 26 -> 15 studies.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Studies included | 15 (14 prospective case series, 1 RCT); NOS 6.06 ± 1.04 |
| Block failure | 9/361 (2.4%) within 1–2 months; cumulative block survival 98% |
| Failed block type / membrane | 5 corticocancellous, 4 cancellous; 7 with membrane, 2 without (too few to analyze) |
| Main failure causes | Early membrane exposure; fixation-screw loosening second |
| Horizontal gain | 4.79 mm (95% CI 4.51–5.08), 119 sites, 5 studies |
| Vertical gain | 2 ± 0.5 mm (limited data, two studies) |
| Resorption at 6 months | 10 ± 10% to 52 ± 25.97%; mean 21.70 ± 30.55%; high heterogeneity |
| Implant survival | 96.9% (95% CI 92.8–98.7%), 228 implants, mean 23.9 months |
| Histology | 6 studies; Acocella 9 mo: new bone 61.96 ± 11.77% around nonvital bone with empty lacunae; Lumetti (6 mo) minimal difference from autograft; Spin-Neto large necrotic segments, no direct contact with remodeled bone |

Limitations stated by the authors: heterogeneity and small samples, almost entirely non-randomized evidence, limited vertical data, short block follow-up, and undetermined ideal implant timing.
