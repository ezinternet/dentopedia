## Three-line Summary
This systematic review and meta-analysis (23 studies, 14 for meta-analysis) compared implant survival rates and donor site complications between iliac crest bone grafts (ICG) and intraoral bone grafts (IOG) for dental implant placement, following PRISMA guidelines with PROSPERO registration.
Intraoral grafts consistently outperformed iliac crest grafts at all timepoints: 6-month (98.4% vs. 95.8%), 12-month (98.4% vs. 97.0%), 24-month (98.2% vs. 85.9%), 60-month (91.5% vs. 90.0%), and 120-month (95.2% vs. 88.8%), all p<0.001; iliac crest grafts also carried higher rates of donor site complications including pain, gait disturbance, and sensory disturbance.
Intraoral autografts are superior to iliac crest grafts for implant survival and morbidity, though evidence robustness is limited by a small number of high-quality studies and heterogeneous complication reporting.

## Summary
McKenna et al. (2022) conducted a PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42021283738) of 23 prospective studies comparing implant survival rates and donor site complications for intraoral bone grafts (IOG) versus iliac crest bone grafts (ICG) in implant dentistry. Fourteen studies contributed to the meta-analysis using a random effects model. Intraoral grafts demonstrated consistently higher implant survival across all follow-up periods from 6 to 120 months, with the most striking divergence at 24 months (IOG 98.2% vs. ICG 85.9%, p<0.001). Donor site morbidity was substantially greater with iliac crest harvest, including pain, gait disturbance, and sensory disturbance; mental region intraoral harvest was associated with anterior mandibular paresthesia. The findings support intraoral sites as the preferred autograft source when sufficient bone volume is available, reserving extraoral iliac crest harvest for cases requiring very large bone volumes.

## Key Contributions
- Highest-level evidence (sr+ma) comparing IOG vs. ICG implant survival across five follow-up timepoints (6, 12, 24, 60, 120 months)
- Consistently superior survival for IOG at all timepoints, largest gap at 24 months (12.3 percentage points)
- Iliac crest harvest associated with clinically significant donor site morbidity: pain/discomfort, gait disturbance, sensory disturbance
- Mental region IOG associated with paresthesia of lower lip and anterior mandibular teeth
- Donor site complication data too heterogeneous for meta-analysis — underscores need for standardized reporting

## Methodology
- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA); PROSPERO CRD42021283738
- **Databases**: MEDLINE (PubMed), EMBASE, Cochrane CENTRAL (1980–March 2022)
- **Eligibility**: Prospective human studies, 2-piece micro-rough implants, ≥6 months follow-up
- **n**: 23 studies total; 14 in meta-analysis
- **Statistics**: Random effects model; I² heterogeneity; funnel plots + trim-and-fill (publication bias)
- **Quality**: Cochrane tool (RCTs); Newcastle-Ottawa scale (non-randomized studies)

## Results

| Timepoint | ICG Survival | IOG Survival | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 months | 95.8% (CI: 93.1–97.4) | 98.4% (CI: 96.0–99.4) | <0.001 |
| 12 months | 97.0% (CI: 90.1–99.2) | 98.4% (CI: 95.8–99.4) | <0.001 |
| 24 months | 85.9% (CI: 80.7–89.9) | 98.2% (CI: 94.6–99.4) | <0.001 |
| 60 months | 90.0% (CI: 81.3–94.9) | 91.5% (CI: 31.8–99.6) | <0.001 |
| 120 months | 88.8% (CI: 79.8–94.0) | 95.2% (CI: 84.6–98.6) | <0.001 |

Note: Wide CI at IOG 60-month reflects limited number of studies at that timepoint.
