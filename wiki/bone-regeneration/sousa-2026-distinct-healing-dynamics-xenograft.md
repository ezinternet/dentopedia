## Three-line Summary

Animal study (n=54 Wistar rats; blood clot control vs Bio-Oss Collagen® vs Extra Graft XG13® in 5-mm critical-size calvarial defects under resorbable collagen membrane; sacrifice at 7, 14, 28 days) comparing two collagen-containing bovine xenografts with different HA/collagen ratios (~90%/~10% vs ~75%/~25%).

Despite equivalent early inflammatory and angiogenic responses, Extra Graft XG13® showed significantly greater new bone formation at 14 and 28 days (histomorphometry, p<0.05), higher mineralized tissue volume/fraction (micro-CT: 14d p<0.0001; 28d p<0.05), lower porosity, and higher mature collagen proportion at 28 days (p<0.01) compared to Bio-Oss Collagen®.

Both materials support GBR osteoconduction, but exhibit distinct time-dependent healing patterns; micro-CT cannot differentiate new bone from residual graft and the 28-day model does not capture long-term remodeling — clinical translation requires further study.

## 세줄요약

동물실험(위스터 쥐 54마리): 혈병 대조군·Bio-Oss Collagen®(~90% 수산화인회석 (Hydroxyapatite, HA)/~10% 콜라겐)·Extra Graft XG13®(~75% HA/~25% 콜라겐) 세 군에 5mm 두개관 임계크기 결손부를 흡수성 콜라겐막 차폐 하에 유도골재생 (Guided Bone Regeneration, GBR) 처치; 7일·14일·28일 희생.

두 이종골의 초기 염증·혈관신생 (Angiogenesis) 반응은 동등했으나, Extra Graft XG13®은 14일·28일 조직형태계측 신생골(p<0.05), 마이크로-전산화단층촬영 (micro-CT) 광화 조직 부피분율 (Bone Volume/Total Volume, BV/TV; 14일 p<0.0001), 공극률, 28일 성숙 콜라겐 비율(p<0.01)에서 Bio-Oss Collagen®보다 유의하게 우월.

두 재료 모두 골전도성 지지체로 작용하지만 서로 다른 시간 의존적 치유 패턴을 보임; 마이크로-CT는 신생골과 잔류 이식재를 구별할 수 없고 28일 모델은 장기 재형성을 반영하지 않아 임상 적용을 위한 추가 연구가 필요.

## Summary

This in vivo rat calvarial study compared two collagen-containing bovine xenografts — Bio-Oss Collagen® (~90% HA / ~10% collagen; Geistlich, Switzerland) and Extra Graft XG13® (~75% HA / ~25% collagen; Implacil, Brazil) — against a blood clot control in a standardized 5-mm critical-size defect under resorbable collagen membrane GBR conditions, with histological, histomorphometric, micro-CT, and collagen maturation assessments at 7, 14, and 28 days. Both biomaterials elicited comparable early inflammatory and angiogenic responses; however, Extra Graft XG13® demonstrated significantly greater mineralized tissue formation and more favorable microarchitectural parameters at intermediate and later time points, while Bio-Oss Collagen® exhibited a more gradual pattern of tissue formation. Collagen maturation analysis revealed a dynamic shift: at 14 days, Extra Graft XG13® showed more immature collagen, while at 28 days it demonstrated a higher proportion of mature fibers — a pattern interpreted cautiously as a possible transition toward remodeling given the absence of direct remodeling markers. The authors hypothesize that compositional differences (particularly the higher collagen fraction in XG13®) may influence early biological events, though independent physicochemical characterization was not performed and clinical translation remains speculative.

## Key Contributions

- First direct animal-model comparison of Bio-Oss Collagen® versus Extra Graft XG13® with sequential time points capturing early healing dynamics (7/14/28 days), not just final bone volume
- Demonstrates that equivalent early biological environments (inflammation, angiogenesis) do not predict equivalent bone formation outcomes — composition may modulate later healing phases
- Documents a BV reduction from 14→28 days in the XG13® group alongside increased collagen organization, raising (but not confirming) a remodeling-transition hypothesis
- Shows two bovine-collagen xenografts with identical clinical indications and graft category exhibit statistically significant differences across multiple healing parameters
- Reinforces the principle that commercial category ("collagenated bovine xenograft") does not equate to equivalent biological performance

## Methodology

- **Design**: In vivo animal experiment (ARRIVE guidelines; CEUA protocol 707/2024, UNESP); blinded histological analysis
- **Model**: 5-mm diameter bicortical calvarial defect, Wistar rat; all defects covered with resorbable collagen membrane (Green Membrane®, Type I collagen, Regener, Brazil)
- **Groups**: Blood clot (control), Bio-Oss Collagen® (Geistlich), Extra Graft XG13® (Implacil); n=6/group/time point
- **Time points**: Sacrifice at 7, 14, 28 days
- **Outcomes**: Inflammatory cells and blood vessel counts (point-counting, 100×, 130-point grid); new bone formation (histomorphometry, H&E, % area); micro-CT (Skyscan 1172; 11.87 μm voxel; BV, BV/TV, Tb.N, Tb.Th, Tb.Sp, porosity; 14d and 28d only); collagen maturation (Picrosirius Red, polarized light)
- **Statistics**: Two-way ANOVA (group × time) + Tukey post hoc; partial eta-squared (η²p); p<0.05

## Results

| Outcome | Extra Graft XG13® vs Bio-Oss Collagen® | Key effect |
|---|---|---|
| Inflammatory cells | No significant difference (any time point) | Both > control at 7d (p<0.0001); group×time η²p=0.67 |
| Angiogenesis | No significant difference | Both > control at 7d/14d (p≤0.002); no group×time interaction (η²p=0.17) |
| New bone (histomorphometry) | XG13® > Bio-Oss at 14d and 28d (p<0.05) | Group×time interaction η²p=0.81 |
| BV (micro-CT) | XG13® > Bio-Oss at 14d (p<0.01); 28d (p<0.05) | Group×time p=0.0007, η²p=0.38 |
| BV/TV (micro-CT) | XG13® > Bio-Oss at 14d (p<0.0001); 28d (p<0.05) | Group×time p=0.0013, η²p=0.36 |
| Porosity | XG13® lower at 14d (p<0.0001); 28d (p<0.05) | More compact structure in XG13® group |
| Trabecular density (Tb.N) | XG13® > Bio-Oss at 14d (p<0.01) | Difference reduced at 28d |
| Mature collagen (28d) | XG13® > Bio-Oss (p<0.01) | Group×time η²p=0.34 |
| Immature collagen (14d) | XG13® > Bio-Oss (p<0.05) | Reversed by 28d |

**Biomaterial composition (manufacturer data)**:

| Material | HA | Collagen | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bio-Oss Collagen® | ~90% | ~10% | Bovine (Geistlich, Switzerland) |
| Extra Graft XG13® | ~75% | ~25% | Bovine (Implacil, Brazil) |

**Key caveat**: Micro-CT cannot differentiate newly formed bone from residual xenograft particles; all reported mineralized tissue parameters reflect total mineralized tissue in the defect.
