## Three-line Summary

Single-center retrospective cohort (28 patients, 49 implants, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital) of simultaneous horizontal ridge augmentation with a porcine bone-derived xenograft (The Graft; Purgo Biologics) and implant placement, followed a mean 67.5 months after prosthesis loading.

Marginal bone loss was 0.23 mm at 1 year and 0.40 mm at final follow-up, with 95.9% survival and 89.8% success overall, while the 24 porcine-only sites reached 100% survival and success (0.23 mm at 1 year, 0.41 mm at 65.8 months); membrane use made no difference and every failure occurred in an infected ridge-split, mixed-graft site.

Porcine xenograft is durable enough over 5+ years in horizontal augmentation to be a legitimate alternative to bovine particulate and autogenous block, but the paper never measured horizontal width and its membrane/mixed-graft comparisons are confounded by defect severity.

## 세줄요약

단일기관 후향적 코호트 (28명, 임플란트 49개): 돼지뼈 유래 이종골 (Porcine Bone-Derived Xenograft, The Graft; Purgo Biologics)을 이용한 수평 치조골 증대 (Horizontal Ridge Augmentation, HRA)와 임플란트 동시 식립 후 보철 부하 후 평균 67.5개월 추적.

변연골소실 (Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)은 1년 0.23 mm·최종 0.40 mm, 생존율 95.9%·성공률 89.8%였고 돼지뼈 단독 24부위는 생존·성공 100%(1년 0.23 mm, 65.8개월 0.41 mm); 차폐막 사용 유무는 차이 없었고 실패는 전부 감염된 치조제 분리술 (Ridge Splitting) + 혼합 이식 부위였다.

돼지뼈 이종골은 수평증대에서 5년 이상 쓸 만한 소뼈 과립·자가골 블록 대안이나, 이 논문은 수평 골폭을 측정하지 않았고 막·혼합이식 비교는 결손 중증도에 교란되어 있다.

## Summary

This is the first cohort to report ≥5-year implant-level outcomes for horizontal ridge augmentation performed with a porcine-derived xenograft, and it establishes that the material itself does not degrade long-term stability: 0.23 mm of marginal bone loss at 1 year and only 0.40 mm at a mean 67.5 months is squarely inside the range reported for autogenous+bovine and allograft horizontal augmentation at much shorter follow-up. The most valuable internal contrast is not porcine-versus-bovine (there is no comparator arm) but porcine-only (24 sites, 100% survival and success) versus porcine-plus-something (25 sites, 92.0% survival / 80.0% success, log-rank P=0.022) — a difference the authors correctly attribute to defect severity and to ridge splitting rather than to material, since every one of the 5 failing sites was an infected ridge-split case in 2 patients. What the paper **claims** but cannot show is that the graft preserved horizontal bone volume: no CBCT, no width measurement, no histology, so the augmentation itself is documented only by the fact that implants could be placed and stayed stable. Clinically it supports porcine particulate as a defensible graft in moderate horizontal defects, and it supports omitting the membrane when the defect is small — but it does not license extrapolation to large defects or to ridge-split sites, where the failure mechanism was infection, not resorption.

## Key Contributions

- **First ≥5-year implant-level evidence for porcine xenograft in horizontal ridge augmentation** — fills the time horizon left open by 6-month histology and CBCT studies.
- **Clean separation of porcine-only versus mixed-graft sites**, converting a material question into a case-complexity question: 100% vs 80.0% success.
- **Membrane-null finding reproduced in two independent subsets** (whole cohort P>0.05; porcine-only 12 vs 12 split, P=0.443 and P=0.843), supporting the argument that a barrier is not mandatory for small contained horizontal defects.
- **A single failure mechanism identified**: perioperative infection in ridge-split + mixed-graft sites caused 2 losses and 3 success failures; no nerve injury, sinus perforation, graft exposure or fracture occurred elsewhere.
- **ISQ rise from 72.1 to 76.9** with simultaneous grafting, i.e. no stability dip, relevant when considering loading timing at grafted sites.

## Methodology

Retrospective single-operator cohort at Seoul National University Bundang Hospital; patients operated 2014–2017 with horizontal bone deficiency, simultaneous porcine graft (The Graft) plus implant placement, and >5 years of follow-up. Exclusions: heavy smoking (>10/day), alcohol addiction, untreated severe periodontitis, uncontrolled diabetes/hypertension, severe hepatic/renal disease, head-and-neck radiotherapy.

Surgery: full-thickness flap via crestal and releasing incisions, selective ridge splitting for very narrow ridges, bone-level implants per CBCT plan, buccal decortication with a 2-mm burr, porcine particulate on the buccal aspect, Auto BT and/or allograft added in severe defects, resorbable collagen membrane at operator discretion for large graft volumes. Prosthesis (crown or bridge) at a mean 8.9 months by two prosthodontists.

Outcomes: ISQ (Osstell Mentor) at placement and at second stage/impression; marginal bone level on periapical radiographs (parallel technique, INFINITT PACS 3.0) at prosthesis delivery, 1 year, and final, averaged mesial/distal; survival and success by Albrektsson 1986 criteria; complications. Statistics: Mann–Whitney for MBL, log-rank for survival/success, α=0.05.

## Results

Cohort: 28 patients (13 male, 15 female), mean age 61.1 ± 14.2 years (26–85), 49 implants (1.8 ± 1.1 per patient), 67.5 ± 10.3 months after loading (n=47). Ridge splitting in 6 sites; membrane in 32 (65.3%); mixed graft in 25 (51.0%). Implant diameter 4.2 ± 0.6 mm, length 9.3 ± 1.8 mm. Primary ISQ 72.1 ± 8.9 → secondary 76.9 ± 10.8.

| Group | MBL 1 yr (mm) | MBL final (mm) | Survival (%) | Success (%) | Follow-up (mo) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All (n=49) | 0.23 ± 0.36 | 0.40 ± 0.45 | 95.9 ± 2.8 | 89.8 ± 4.3 | 67.5 ± 10.3 |
| Membrane (n=32) | 0.24 ± 0.40 | 0.43 ± 0.47 | 93.8 ± 4.3 | 84.4 ± 6.4 | 67.2 ± 10.5 |
| No membrane (n=17) | 0.23 ± 0.30 | 0.36 ± 0.42 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 68.0 ± 10.3 |
| Mixed graft (n=25) | 0.24 ± 0.45 | 0.39 ± 0.54 | 92.0 ± 5.4 | 80.0 ± 8.0 (P=0.022) | 69.2 ± 10.5 |
| Porcine only (n=24) | 0.23 ± 0.26 | 0.41 ± 0.35 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 65.8 ± 10.0 |

Porcine-only subset split by membrane (n=12 vs 12): MBL 1 year 0.29 ± 0.32 vs 0.18 ± 0.18 (P=0.443); final 0.44 ± 0.47 vs 0.38 ± 0.18 (P=0.843); 100% success in both.

Complications: 5 sites / 2 patients, all ridge-split + mixed graft with infection — site #32 and #42 (both lost), #13, #21 and #23 (survived, 2 with fixture exposure, all failed success criteria). MBL differences by membrane or mixed-graft use were not significant (P>0.05); only mixed-graft use and success rate were associated (log-rank P=0.022).

## Limitations

- **No horizontal width outcome at all** — no CBCT, no superimposition, no histology; horizontal change was "substituted with clinical assessment" of soft-tissue contour. The central claim (that the ridge stayed augmented) rests on implant survival, not on measurement.
- **Confounding by indication**: membrane and mixed graft were used in the bigger, more invasive defects, so those comparisons reflect severity; conversely the porcine-only 100% result reflects small defects.
- No concurrent bovine, allograft or autogenous control arm — all comparators are historical/cross-study with shorter follow-up (2–3 years).
- "More than 5 years" is overstated: minimum follow-up after loading is 53.7 months (4.5 years).
- The 3 success-failure implants' actual MBL values are not reported, so how far they exceeded the 0.2 mm/year criterion is unknown.
- ISQ secondary timing is heterogeneous (second-stage surgery *or* impression); 3 light smokers retained; only unadjusted pairwise statistics, no multivariable model for arch, site, implant dimensions or defect severity.
- Graft resorption of the porcine material is never quantified, so "long-term stability of the graft" is inferred rather than shown.
