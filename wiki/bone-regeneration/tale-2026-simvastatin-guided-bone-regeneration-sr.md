## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (no meta-analysis; 5 studies — 3 human clinical, 2 animal) evaluating local simvastatin as an adjunct to guided bone regeneration (GBR) or related implant regenerative procedures during immediate/simultaneous implant placement.

Simvastatin reduced peri-implant crestal bone loss (0.975±0.044mm vs 1.356±0.038mm, P<0.0001), increased bone width gain (5.55±0.80mm vs 3.36±0.19mm) and bone mineral density (640.02±262.22 vs 297.38±82.94), and raised bone-to-implant contact in animal models (84.3% vs 59.5% at 4 weeks, grafted) vs controls.

Simvastatin is a low-cost, biologically plausible hard-tissue adjunct, but the evidence base is limited to 5 small/heterogeneous studies with no possible meta-analysis, and implant stability (ISQ) showed no consistent benefit.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰(메타분석 없음; 인체 임상 3편, 동물실험 2편)로, 즉시/동시 임플란트 식립 시 국소 심바스타틴을 유도골재생(Guided Bone Regeneration, GBR) 또는 관련 재생술식의 보조제로 사용한 근거를 종합.

심바스타틴군은 치조정골소실 감소(0.975±0.044mm vs 1.356±0.038mm, P<0.0001), 골폭 증가(5.55±0.80mm vs 3.36±0.19mm), 골밀도 증가(640.02±262.22 vs 297.38±82.94)를 보였고, 동물실험에서는 골-임플란트 접촉률(Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC)이 대조군 대비 유의하게 높았다(이식 시 4주째 84.3% vs 59.5%).

심바스타틴은 저비용·생물학적으로 타당한 경조직 재생 보조제이나, 근거는 5편의 소규모·이질적 연구에 국한되어 메타분석이 불가능했고 임플란트 안정성 지수(Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ) 개선은 일관되지 않았다.

## Summary

This systematic review (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251037455) asked whether localized simvastatin — an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor repurposed for its osteoinductive, anti-inflammatory, and angiogenic properties — improves outcomes when added to guided bone regeneration (GBR) or related peri-implant regenerative procedures. After searching five databases plus grey literature to April 2025, only 5 studies met eligibility (3 human clinical: 2 RCTs + 1 non-randomized split-mouth trial; 2 animal: dog and rabbit models). Because of heterogeneity in simvastatin concentration (1%, 1.2%, 0.5mM), delivery vehicle (gel, PRF, granules, implant coating), comparator protocol, and follow-up duration (2 weeks–12 months), the authors synthesized findings narratively rather than pooling them in a meta-analysis.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates the first evidence synthesis specific to simvastatin **in implant-associated regenerative contexts** (immediate/simultaneous implant placement, peri-implant defect regeneration) — distinct from prior, broader reviews of simvastatin in generic bone regeneration or periodontal defects.
- Separates human clinical evidence from preclinical/animal mechanistic evidence, clarifying which outcome domains (hard tissue vs implant stability) each supports.
- Applies domain-matched risk-of-bias tools: Cochrane RoB-2 (RCTs), ROBINS-I (non-randomized), SYRCLE (animal studies).

## Methodology

- PICOS: population = human/animal subjects undergoing implant-associated regenerative procedures; intervention = local simvastatin (any delivery form) added to GBR/regenerative protocol; comparator = same protocol without simvastatin; outcomes = quantitative bone-fill/bone-gain/stability/histologic measures; designs = RCT, controlled clinical trial, prospective study, animal study (in vitro, case reports, non-comparative studies excluded).
- Searches: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane CENTRAL, Google Scholar, ClinicalTrials.gov to 2025-04-30, no language restriction; two independent reviewers with third-reviewer arbitration.
- No meta-analysis performed (too few studies, too heterogeneous) — narrative synthesis grouped by clinical vs animal evidence.

## Results

**Human clinical studies (n=3):**
- **El Shafei 2022** (non-RCT split-mouth, 8pt/16 implants): 1.2mg simvastatin + PRF vs PRF alone during immediate implant placement (IIP). Bone loss at 12mo: 0.975±0.044mm (SMV) vs 1.356±0.038mm (control), P<0.0001; ISQ not significantly different.
- **Betha 2024** (RCT, n=50): 1% simvastatin gel at extraction socket during IIP, no graft/membrane. Probing depth 12wk: 5.7→3.2mm (SMV) vs 5.8→4.1mm (control); greater reduction in bleeding-on-probing and better crestal bone preservation with simvastatin.
- **Issa 2024** (RCT, 22pt/49 implants, horizontally atrophic ridges): 1.2% simvastatin gel + Tutobone xenograft + Tutopatch membrane during ridge splitting + simultaneous implant placement. At 9mo: bone width gain 5.55±0.80mm (SMV) vs 3.36±0.19mm (control); BMD 640.02±262.22 vs 297.38±82.94; Pink Esthetic Score improved; marginal bone loss comparable between groups.

**Animal studies (n=2):**
- **Mansour 2014** (dog, n=10): 2.2mg simvastatin granules in HPMC matrix in socket during IIP, no conventional membrane/graft. Simvastatin sites showed more new bone, higher osteocyte count, greater vascularity vs untreated control.
- **Jun 2021** (rabbit, n=12/48 implants): implants immersed in 0.5mM simvastatin ± UV treatment, with Bio-Oss xenograft + resorbable collagen membrane in peri-implant defects. Grafted BIC at 4wk: 84.3% (SMV) vs 59.5% (control); grafted bone area 75.9% vs 51.4%. No added benefit from combining simvastatin with UV.

**Overall trend**: consistent benefit in hard-tissue outcomes (crestal bone preservation, bone width, BMD, BIC); implant stability (ISQ) not consistently improved where measured — suggesting simvastatin's contribution is more biological/regenerative than mechanical. Risk of bias: both RCTs low risk across all domains; the non-RCT moderate (confounding); animal studies moderate (Mansour, multiple unclear domains) to low (Jun).
