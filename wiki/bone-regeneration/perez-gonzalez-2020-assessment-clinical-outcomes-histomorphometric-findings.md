## Three-line Summary
Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA; 7 non-randomized clinical studies, searched to April 2019; 234 patients, 323 allogeneic bone blocks, 501 implants, >=12 months follow-up, Newcastle-Ottawa scale scores 2-4 of 9) of allogeneic bone block grafts for atrophic-ridge augmentation before implant placement.

Pooled block survival was 94.52% (random effects; I2 = 56.3%), pooled implant survival 97.36% (fixed effects; I2 = 0%; range 95.2-100%); block exposure was the commonest complication, newly formed bone was 31-42% and residual graft 14-40% in the three studies with histomorphometry.

Allogeneic blocks look like a feasible alternative to autogenous blocks (no donor-site morbidity), but there was no usable allogeneic-versus-autogenous comparison, protocols were unstandardized and there were no randomized trials, so the conclusion rests on low-grade, short-to-medium-term evidence.

## 세줄요약
체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (PRISMA 준수; 2019년 4월까지 검색, 비무작위 임상연구 7편; 환자 234명, 동종골 블록 (Allogeneic Bone Block) 323개, 임플란트 501개, 추적 12개월 이상, Newcastle-Ottawa 척도 2~4점)으로, 위축된 치조제의 임플란트 전 증강에 쓰인 동종골 블록의 성적을 평가했다.

통합 블록 생존율 (Block Survival Rate)은 94.52% (변량효과 모형, I2 = 56.3%), 통합 임플란트 생존율은 97.36% (고정효과 모형, I2 = 0%, 범위 95.2~100%)였고, 블록 노출 (Block Exposure)이 가장 흔한 합병증이었다. 조직형태계측 (Histomorphometry)을 보고한 3편에서 신생골은 31~42%, 잔존 이식재는 14~40%였다.

동종골 블록은 공여부 이환이 없는 자가골 블록 (Autogenous Bone Block)의 대안으로 보이나, 동종골 대 자가골의 직접 비교 자료가 없고 프로토콜이 표준화되지 않았으며 무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT)이 없어 근거 수준은 낮고 추적 기간은 짧거나 중간 정도에 그친다.

## Summary
Pérez-González et al. (2020) performed a PRISMA-guided systematic review and meta-analysis of seven non-randomized clinical studies (234 patients, 323 allogeneic bone blocks, 501 implants; searches to April 2019; follow-up 12 to 96 months) of allogeneic bone block grafting for atrophic ridges. Pooled block survival was 94.52% and pooled implant survival 97.36%, with block exposure the most frequent complication and no neurosensory disturbance reported, unlike autogenous intraoral harvesting. Histomorphometry (three to four studies) showed 31-42% new bone and 14-40% residual graft, with a more clearly demarcated necrotic interface than autogenous blocks. The authors conclude allogeneic blocks are an adequate alternative to autogenous blocks, while stressing the absence of comparative trials and standardized protocols.

## Key Contributions
- Pooled block survival (94.52%) and implant survival (97.36%) for allogeneic blocks, at both block and implant level.
- Summary of secondary outcomes: horizontal gain 1.65-4.69 mm, resorption 0.4-0.5 mm or 5.39%, marginal bone loss 0.5 mm, complications.
- Histomorphometric ranges for allogeneic blocks and a statement of the standardization gap (healing time 3-6 months, fixation, membrane use).

## Methodology
PRISMA-guided SR/MA; MEDLINE, Cochrane CENTRAL, Scopus, Web of Science plus manual search to April 2019 (English/Spanish); 372 records, 52 full texts, 7 studies; Newcastle-Ottawa scale 2-4; survival pooled in Comprehensive Meta-Analysis v3 with fixed or random effects by Q and I2. Population: systemically healthy partially or fully edentulous patients; intervention: allogeneic block grafts before implants; no comparator.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Block survival | 94.52% pooled (random effects, I2 = 56.3%); 323 blocks |
| Implant survival | 97.36% pooled (fixed effects, I2 = 0%); 501 implants; range 95.2-100% |
| Follow-up | 12-96 months; implant placement after 3-6 months |
| Horizontal gain | 1.65 +/- 0.14 mm to 4.69 mm |
| Block resorption | 0.4-0.5 mm or 5.39 +/- 2.18% (no standard method) |
| Marginal bone loss | 0.5 +/- 0.5 mm at 37 +/- 17 months (1 study) |
| Complications | 28 blocks exposed, 9 osseointegration failures; 11 implants failed, 3 early losses |
| Histomorphometry | new bone 31-42%; residual graft 14-40%; marrow/connective tissue 19.3-53.7% |

Caveats: the abstract reports 13 failed blocks (4.02%), which does not match the pooled 94.52% survival; the discussion cites 347 blocks, versus 323 in the results. Treat the pooled figures as approximate. Comparisons with autogenous blocks in the discussion (e.g., fresh-frozen allograft resorption 46% vs 28%; residual graft 28.9% vs 19.5%) come from individual studies and were not pooled.
