## Three-line Summary

Narrative literature review (no formal systematic search strategy or meta-analysis) surveying dental bone graft and substitute materials — natural (autograft, allograft/DBM, xenograft, phytogenic), synthetic (HA, β-TCP, biphasic CP ceramics, bioactive glass, calcium phosphate cements, calcium sulfates, polymers, metals), composite, growth-factor-based, and living-cell-infused categories — for craniofacial/dental bone defect repair including implant site preparation.

Autografts remain the only material possessing all four fundamental biological properties (osseointegration, osteogenesis, osteoconduction, osteoinduction) and the gold standard for complex augmentation, but donor-site morbidity and limited supply drive use of alternatives; deproteinized bovine xenograft achieved 39% new bone formation at 6 months (vs 40% for autograft) in maxillary sinus grafting, with 95% implant survival reported at 14 years in one longitudinal cohort.

All currently available synthetic/allogeneic/xenogeneic substitutes satisfy only the osteoconductivity criterion (none reproduces autograft's full four-property profile), and evidence for newer materials (growth-factor-based, MSC-infused, nano-HA) is drawn largely from case reports/animal studies rather than standardized clinical trials — a key limitation for extrapolating to routine practice.

## 세줄요약

정식 체계적 검색전략이나 메타분석 없는 narrative review로, 치과용 골이식재/골대체재를 천연(자가골·동종골/DBM·이종골·식물유래)·합성(HA·β-TCP·이상성 CP세라믹·생체활성유리·인산칼슘시멘트·황산칼슘·고분자·금속)·복합재·성장인자기반·생세포주입형 범주로 나누어 임플란트 부위 준비를 포함한 두개안면/치과 골결손 수복 전반을 조망.

자가골(autograft)만이 4대 생물학적 특성(골유착·골형성·골전도·골유도)을 모두 갖춘 유일한 재료로 복잡 골증대의 gold standard이지만 공여부 이환율·제한된 공급 때문에 대체재 사용이 증가하며, 탈단백우골 이종골(deproteinized bovine xenograft)은 상악동거상술에서 6개월 시점 39% 신생골형성(자가골 40%와 비교)을 보였고 한 장기추적 코호트에서 14년 95% 임플란트 생존율이 보고됨.

현재 시판되는 합성/동종/이종 대체재는 모두 골전도성(osteoconductivity) 조건만 충족하며(자가골의 4대 특성 전체를 재현하는 재료 없음), 성장인자기반·MSC 주입형·나노 HA 등 신소재의 근거는 대부분 증례보고나 동물실험 수준이라 표준화된 임상시험 부재가 실제 진료 외삽의 핵심 한계.

## Summary

This 2021 narrative review (Zhao et al., *Molecules*) surveys the full landscape of dental bone graft and substitute materials used in implantology, periodontal regeneration, and craniofacial bone repair. It classifies materials into five categories — natural (autograft, allograft/DBM, xenograft, phytogenic), synthetic (HA, β-TCP, biphasic calcium phosphate ceramics, bioactive glass, calcium phosphate cements, calcium sulfates, polymers, metals), composite, growth-factor-based, and living-cell-infused — and evaluates each against the four fundamental biological properties a bone graft should ideally possess: osseointegration, osteogenesis, osteoconduction, and osteoinduction. Autografts remain the only material meeting all four criteria and thus the gold standard, though limited by donor-site morbidity and supply. All synthetic and most allogeneic/xenogeneic substitutes satisfy only osteoconductivity, motivating ongoing research into growth-factor-based and cell-infused hybrid constructs as the field's future direction.

## Key Contributions

- Dentistry-wide (not defect-type-restricted) taxonomy spanning implant site preparation, periodontal defects, sinus lift, and craniofacial repair — broader scope than ridge-augmentation-focused reviews.
- Quantifies market/utilization context: ~2.2M bone graft procedures/year globally, ~50% of dental implant procedures involve grafting, dental bone substitute market ~US$493M (2018) → ~US$931M projected by 2025.
- USFDA device classification of bone grafts (Class II vs Class III) is explicitly stated.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action and reported outcome data for specific commercial/experimental materials across all five categories, including phytogenic materials (Gusuibu, coral-HA, marine-algae-HA) and metal-based substitutes (nickel-titanium membranes, Mg-Sr alloys) not always covered in ridge-augmentation-focused reviews.
- Explicit flag that GFBS (growth-factor-based bone substitute) safety is a live concern: one major USFDA-approved BMP-based product has been discontinued and another linked to "a large number of life-threatening complications."

## Methodology

Narrative literature review without a stated PRISMA-style search protocol, inclusion/exclusion criteria, or quantitative synthesis. Organized topically: (1) ideal bone-grafting-material characteristics, (2) five-way material classification with subcategories, (3) future directions, (4) conclusions. Evidence synthesized narrativley from primary clinical studies, animal models, and commercial product literature, categorized by material class rather than by defect type or study design.

## Results

- Bovine xenograft in maxillary sinus grafting: 39% new bone formation at 6 months (vs 40% autograft); 31% xenograft material retained at site vs 18% autograft.
- 14-year longitudinal cohort: 95% implant survival in atrophic maxillae after sinus grafting with marine-algae-derived HA.
- Gusuibu (phytogenic) + collagen carrier: +24% new bone formation vs Gusuibu alone; +90% vs collagen sponge alone.
- MSC cell-sheet/injection (pig model): 52.7mm and 32.4mm increase in alveolar bone height regeneration respectively (dental-pulp-derived MSCs); periodontal-ligament-derived MSCs outperformed dental-pulp-derived MSCs in a dog model.
- Silk fibroin GBR membrane: ~4mm new bone gain at 6 months post-extraction (third molar sites).
- Allograft-associated unknown-disease prevalence: ~8% in osteoarthritic femoral heads sourced for allografting.
- Core conclusion: no current non-autograft material reproduces all four fundamental biological properties; all synthetic/most allogeneic-xenogeneic substitutes remain osteoconductive-only, restricting new bone formation largely to the graft's outer surface layer.
