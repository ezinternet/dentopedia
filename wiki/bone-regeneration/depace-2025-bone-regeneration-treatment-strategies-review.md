## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (PubMed/MEDLINE search through 31 Dec 2024, English, human studies, 2019–2024, orthopedic/oncologic surgery literature explicitly excluding dental/oral-surgery topics) synthesizing current bone-substitute strategies across six categories: autografts, allografts, xenografts, naturally derived biomaterials, synthetic scaffolds, and composite materials.

Autologous bone remains the clinical gold standard (osteogenic + osteoinductive + osteoconductive, >10% failure/complication rate from donor-site morbidity); allografts (esp. demineralized bone matrix) and xenografts (bovine/equine, decellularized) are common alternatives, while synthetic bioceramics/bioactive glass/metals/polymers and composite materials (e.g., xenograft + resorbable polymer) are reviewed with dozens of named commercial products and clinical outcome data (e.g., 90% consolidation in joint fusions, 84% spinal fusion success with bioactive glass autograft extenders).

As a narrative (non-systematic, non-meta-analytic) review focused on orthopedic/oncologic applications rather than dental/oral surgery, findings should be read as broad materials-science context rather than dentistry-specific clinical guidance; scaffold vascularization, degradation-rate control, and manufacturing standardization remain unresolved challenges across all material classes.

## Summary

This 2025 narrative review from an Italian biomaterials/regenerative-medicine group synthesizes current strategies for bone regeneration across orthopedic and oncologic surgery, deliberately excluding dental/oral-surgery literature from its PubMed search. It organizes the field into six bone-substitute categories — autografts, allografts, xenografts, naturally derived biomaterials (collagen, gelatin, alginate, silk fibroin, hyaluronic acid, chitosan, PHA), synthetic scaffolds (bioceramics, bioactive glass, metals, organic polymers), and composite materials — describing mechanism, evidence base, clinical outcomes, and an extensive catalog of ~25 named commercial products (xenografts, bioceramics, calcium sulfate, bioactive glass, resorbable polymers, and composite xenograft-polymer hybrids). A dedicated applications section covers reconstructive/regenerative surgery (foot/ankle, spine, pediatric, sarcoma), drug-delivery scaffolds, and PRP/BMAC delivery. Though not dentistry-specific, the material-category framework and comparative outcome data directly parallel dental bone-regeneration reviews and provide independent, cross-specialty corroboration of graft-material trade-offs already documented in the wiki.

## Key Contributions

- Six-category bone-substitute taxonomy with mechanism/evidence/outcome summary for each: autograft, allograft, xenograft, naturally derived biomaterials, synthetic scaffolds (bioceramics/bioactive glass/metals/polymers), composite materials.
- Extensive commercial-product catalog (~25 products) spanning xenografts (CANCELLO-PURE, Kiel-Surgibone, BIO-GEN), bioceramics (Apaceram, Straumann BoneCeramic, Cerament, Nanostim, KeraOs, Vitoss, SynthoGraft, Maxresorb, MBCP+, Ceraform), calcium sulfate (Bondbone, EthOss), bioactive glass (NovaBone, BonAlive, Glassbone, Cortoss, StronBone, OssiMend, Signafuse), resorbable polymers (LactoSorb, Neoveil, Ethisorb, RapidSorb), and xenograft-polymer composites (SmartBone ORTHO, Hypro-Oss ORTHO, Tutobone).
- Specific-applications section (§8) covering reconstructive/regenerative surgery, drug-delivery scaffolds (osteomyelitis antibiotics, osteoporosis bisphosphonates/PTH, osteosarcoma chemotherapeutics), and PRP/BMAC delivery scaffolds — a biologics-adjunct framing that parallels dental PRF/PRP literature.
- Quantifies allograft immunogenicity risk: average 48% of patients remain immunologically sensitized after allograft (citing Moraschini systematic review) — a figure not previously captured elsewhere in the wiki's bone-regeneration category.
- 3D-printing/bioprinting coverage across metals and polymers with concrete case outcomes (95.2% fusion rate for 3D-printed PCL/TCP lumbar cages, n=22; 90%+ stability in 3D-printed titanium acetabular/knee reconstructions).

## Methodology

Narrative literature review. Database: PubMed (MEDLINE) only, searched through 31 December 2024. Keywords: "bone substitute", "biomaterial", "bone graft", "orthopedic surgery", "oncologic surgery", "bone regeneration" (title/abstract/MeSH/keywords), with explicit exclusion of oral surgery/dentistry/dental-surgery terms. Filters: 2019–2024 publication window, English language, human subjects. Included article types: meta-analyses, systematic reviews, reviews, clinical trials; excluded in vitro-only and animal-only studies and those lacking sufficient statistical data. Population: patients undergoing orthopedic or oncologic surgery requiring bone regeneration. Synthesis is narrative/qualitative — no PRISMA flow diagram, no quantitative meta-analytic pooling.

## Results

| Category | Key property | Representative outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Autograft | Osteogenic+inductive+conductive, gold standard | >10% failure/complication from donor-site morbidity; rhBMP-2+autograft non-union fusion 96%@6mo, 100%@1yr (n=24) |
| Allograft (incl. DBM) | Osteoinductive (DBM)/conductive | 48% average immune sensitization (SR); freeze-dried allograft nasal augmentation 85% success (n=62) |
| Xenograft (bovine/equine) | Osteoconductive, decellularized | Fusion rates comparable to autograft in select spinal-fusion series (Kiel-Surgibone) |
| Bioceramics (HAp/TCP/BCP) | Osteoconductive; brittle | HAp pore fill 50–60% with cortical bone → bending strength 40–60 MPa |
| Bioactive glass | Osteoinductive + antibacterial | S53P4 for osteomyelitis: 70.3%@6mo, 83.3%@12mo healed without antibiotics (n=50); spinal fusion 84% success with autograft extender (n=396) |
| Synthetic polymers (PLA/PGA/PLGA/PCL) | Tunable degradation, bioinert | 3D-printed PCL/TCP lumbar cages: 95.2% fusion (n=22) |
| Composite (xenograft+polymer, e.g. SmartBone ORTHO) | Combines biological matrix + mechanical/resorption control | Complete replacement with natural bone within 1–2 years (manufacturer-reported) |
| BMAC/PRP adjuncts | Biologic augmentation, non-structural | BMAC+ACL reconstruction: higher graft metabolic activity @3mo, excellent knee function @9mo (n=80) |

- Foot/ankle and general joint-fusion bone grafting: consolidation rates up to 90%.
- Spinal fusion with autograft+synthetic-substitute combination: 90% clinical success, reduced healing time.
- Calcium sulfate: fastest-dissolving material (4–12 weeks), limited osteoconduction, unsuitable for load-bearing sites.
- Persistent cross-material challenges identified in Discussion: scaffold vascularization, degradation-rate/bone-healing-speed mismatch, and lack of manufacturing standardization.
