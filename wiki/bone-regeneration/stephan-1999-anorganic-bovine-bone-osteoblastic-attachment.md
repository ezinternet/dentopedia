## Three-line Summary

Line 1: In vitro study examining whether anorganic bovine bone matrix (ABB) supports attachment, spreading, and proliferation of primary neonatal rat calvarial osteoblastic cells.
Line 2: Cell attachment was time-dependent with significantly more cells at 180 min vs 15 min (p<0.05); proliferation significantly higher on 100 mg vs 10 mg ABB (p<0.05).
Line 3: ABB supports osteoblastic cell attachment and proliferation, providing in vitro mechanistic basis for its clinical use as a bone graft substitute.

## Summary

Stephan et al. (1999) conducted an in vitro investigation to determine whether anorganic bovine bone (ABB) — the deproteinized, sintered bovine bone mineral commercially available as Bio-Oss/DBBM — could support osteoblastic cell attachment and proliferation. Primary osteoblasts were isolated from neonatal rat calvaria by sequential collagenase digestion.

**Attachment assay:** ³H-leucine labeled cells were incubated with ABB for 15, 90, or 180 minutes. After washing to remove unattached cells, radioactivity was measured by liquid scintillation spectrometry. Attachment was significantly higher at 180 min than at earlier time points (p<0.05). SEM at 180 min showed cells progressing from initially round morphology to a flattened, spread conformation with cytoplasmic processes extending into ABB surface pores.

**Proliferation assay:** ³H-thymidine incorporation was measured over 24 h for cells on 10 mg vs 100 mg ABB. The 100 mg group showed significantly higher proliferation (p<0.05), suggesting a dose-response relationship between scaffold mass and cellular proliferative activity.

These results provided the first quantitative in vitro evidence that ABB supports osteoblastic attachment and proliferation, establishing cellular-level mechanistic rationale for the osteoconductive properties of DBBM observed in subsequent in vivo and clinical studies.

## Key Contributions

- First quantitative in vitro demonstration that ABB supports osteoblastic cell attachment (time-dependent) and proliferation (dose-dependent)
- SEM evidence of cytoplasmic process extension into ABB surface pores — morphological indicator of cell-material integration
- Established in vitro biological plausibility for DBBM's clinical osteoconductive function

## Methodology

- **Design:** In vitro experimental
- **Cells:** Primary osteoblasts from neonatal rat calvaria (sequential collagenase digestion)
- **Attachment:** ³H-leucine label; 15 / 90 / 180 min timepoints; liquid scintillation spectrometry
- **Proliferation:** ³H-thymidine incorporation; 10 mg vs 100 mg ABB; 24 h culture
- **Morphology:** Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)
