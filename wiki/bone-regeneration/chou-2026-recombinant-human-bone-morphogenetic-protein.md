## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (21 included studies — 6 RCTs, 2 prospective, 10 retrospective, 3 case reports; PubMed, Ovid and Web of Science searched 2008–2023) comparing rhBMP-2-infused allograft against iliac crest bone graft (ICBG) in pediatric craniofacial reconstruction.

For alveolar cleft repair, 4 of 12 studies favored rhBMP-2 for bone formation, 1 favored ICBG and 7 found no difference; operative time was shorter with rhBMP-2 in all 5 comparative studies and hospitalization was shorter in 7 of 8, while 5 of 12 complication studies favored ICBG and cost was mixed across 4 studies.

rhBMP-2 appears comparable, if not favorable, to ICBG for pediatric alveolar cleft repair while shortening operative time and hospital stay, but 18 of 21 studies address this single procedure, the search ends in 2023, and long-term pediatric efficacy and safety remain unconfirmed.

## Summary

Pediatric craniofacial reconstruction still defaults to autologous bone — most often an iliac crest bone graft (ICBG) — which adds a second surgical site, a second anesthetic and a donor wound to an already long operation in a child. This systematic review asks whether rhBMP-2-infused allograft can carry the graft instead, so the iliac crest is not opened at all.

The answer, at the level of direction of effect, is yes for the procedure that dominates the literature. Across 21 included studies, alveolar cleft repair was the indication in 18, and in that subgroup rhBMP-2 matched ICBG on bone formation in 7 of 12 studies and beat it in 4 (ICBG won 1), while every one of the 5 operative-time studies and 7 of the 8 hospitalization studies favored rhBMP-2. The trade-off is in complications: 5 of 12 studies reported more adverse effects with ICBG (driven by donor-site morbidity) against 2 favoring rhBMP-2 (driven by facial edema) — i.e. the morbidity is not eliminated, it is relocated from the iliac crest to the face. Economics are unresolved, with mixed results across 4 cost studies.

The authors' own conclusion is appropriately hedged: rhBMP-2 "appears comparable, if not favorable" to ICBG for alveolar cleft repair, but long-term follow-up is required to confirm efficacy and safety, particularly for craniofacial procedures other than cleft repair — where the evidence base in this review is three studies in total.

## Key Contributions

1. **The only harvest-avoidance evidence we hold for pediatric craniofacial surgery.** The wiki's donor-site synthesis asks which harvest site to use; this review is the counter-question — whether to harvest at all.
2. **Head-to-head framing against the pediatric standard of care** (ICBG) across five outcome domains rather than a single surrogate: bone formation efficacy, complications, operative time, length of hospitalization, financial burden.
3. **Quantifies the morbidity trade-off in both directions**: donor-site morbidity for ICBG, facial edema for rhBMP-2.
4. **Maps the procedure concentration explicitly** — 18/21 alveolar cleft repair, 2 calvarial defect, 1 mandibular continuity defect — which is the reason the conclusion cannot be generalized beyond cleft repair.
5. **Practice-level efficiency signal**: shorter operative time in 5/5 studies and shorter hospital stay in 7/8 studies, relevant to pediatric throughput and anesthetic exposure.

## Methodology

- **Design**: systematic review without meta-analysis; synthesis is direction-of-effect vote counting per outcome domain (no pooled estimate, no heterogeneity statistic).
- **Search**: PubMed, Ovid, Web of Science, 2008–2023.
- **Eligibility**: RCTs, prospective and retrospective case series, and case reports on rhBMP-2 in pediatric craniofacial reconstruction. Reviews, animal studies and basic science excluded.
- **Flow**: 211 articles screened → 21 included (6 RCTs, 2 prospective, 10 retrospective, 3 case reports).
- **Comparators**: rhBMP-2-infused allograft vs ICBG, evaluated for bone formation efficacy, complications, operative time, length of hospitalization and financial burden.
- **Not visible in the abstract**: full search string, duplicate handling, risk-of-bias appraisal, GRADE certainty, per-study data extraction, and patient-level denominators.

## Results

Procedure distribution (21 studies):

| Procedure | Studies |
|---|---|
| Alveolar cleft repair | 18 |
| Calvarial defect reconstruction | 2 |
| Mandibular continuity defect reconstruction | 1 |

Outcome comparison, rhBMP-2 vs ICBG (alveolar cleft repair studies):

| Outcome domain | Studies | Direction of effect |
|---|---|---|
| Bone formation efficacy | 12 | rhBMP-2 favored 4 · ICBG favored 1 · no difference 7 |
| Complications | 12 | more adverse effects with rhBMP-2 in 2 · with ICBG in 5 · no difference 4 |
| Operative time | 5 | shorter with rhBMP-2 in 5 of 5 |
| Length of hospitalization | 8 | shorter with rhBMP-2 in 7 of 8 |
| Financial burden | 4 | mixed |

- Most common rhBMP-2 complication: **facial edema**. Most common ICBG complication: **donor-site morbidity**.
- All figures above are counts of studies, not patients; no effect sizes, confidence intervals or p-values are reported in the abstract.
- Because the synthesis counts votes, a small RCT and a case report carry equal weight in each tally.
