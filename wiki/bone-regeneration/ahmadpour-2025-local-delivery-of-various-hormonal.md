## Why Ingested

The "locally delivered drug **plus** bone graft to drive osteogenesis" strategy had no page here — the closest held pages each cover a single agent: a statin adjunct to guided bone regeneration (GBR) in [[bone-regeneration/tale-2026-simvastatin-guided-bone-regeneration-sr]], and parathyroid hormone (PTH) supplementation for implant osseointegration in [[implants/survival/kindaro-2026-parathyroid-hormone-implant-osseointegration-osteoporosis-sr]]. This SR is the only cross-hormone synthesis the wiki holds for that frame (PTH · growth hormone (GH) · calcitonin), and its answer — 22 preclinical studies, reported as vote counts — is the single place where the strength of the "local hormonal drug + bone graft" concept can be calibrated. It also provides the preclinical evidence behind the intelligent-drug-delivery direction that [[bone-regeneration/haugen-2026-bone-grafts-everything-you-need]] lists only as a future for graft materials.

## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA-guided PubMed search, 1970 – December 2023) of 22 studies — **all** of them animal in vivo or in vitro experiments — on local delivery of parathyroid hormone (PTH), growth hormone (GH) and calcitonin together with bone grafts to improve osteogenesis; no human study was included.

Vote-count synthesis (proportion of studies with better outcomes than controls): **87.5%** of GH studies, **66.7%** of PTH studies and **60%** of calcitonin studies reported superior outcomes versus their control groups.

The authors conclude that locally administered hormonal drugs may positively affect osteogenesis in vivo and in vitro — most clearly PTH in surgical bone defect healing and peri-implant response, and GH in peri-implant bone response — but every included study is preclinical and the result is a study-level vote count rather than a pooled effect estimate, so no clinical protocol is supported.

## Summary

Ahmadpour et al. (J Craniofac Surg, epub 2025-04-14; 36(4):1189-1195) systematically reviewed the effect of **locally delivered hormonal drugs combined with bone grafts** on osteogenesis. The search covered PubMed from 1970 to December 2023 following PRISMA guidelines, using a structured search designed to pick up both in vivo and in vitro work; after title-and-abstract screening, **22 studies** were included. Every one of them was an animal in vivo study or an in vitro study, and every one used **parathyroid hormone (PTH)**, **growth hormone (GH)** or **calcitonin** delivered locally alongside a bone graft, against a control group.

The quantitative result is a vote count rather than a pooled estimate: **87.5%** of GH studies, **66.7%** of PTH studies and **60%** of calcitonin studies reported better or superior outcomes than their controls. The authors then attach the indication: the signal is "especially true for PTH in the healing of surgical bone defects and peri-implant response and for GH in peri-implant bone response", and locally administered hormonal drugs "may stimulate bone healing in surgical bone defects and enhance peri-implant bone response".

What this page adds to the wiki is a **drug-class axis for the graft-plus-adjunct strategy** that was previously covered one agent at a time. [[bone-regeneration/tale-2026-simvastatin-guided-bone-regeneration-sr]] established the local-pharmacologic-adjunct-to-GBR frame (statin; 3 human + 2 animal studies) and [[implants/survival/kindaro-2026-parathyroid-hormone-implant-osseointegration-osteoporosis-sr]] established the PTH-specific peri-implant line (12 preclinical studies, SYRCLE risk-of-bias). This review is the first page that compares three hormones against one another under one protocol definition and states, in one sentence, where the field stands: the concept is biologically plausible and repeatedly positive in animals, and completely untested in humans.

Because the retrieval here is abstract-only (closed access; PMC, Unpaywall and OpenAlex checked, no open copy), no per-study detail, dose, carrier, model, effect size or risk-of-bias judgement can be reproduced. The percentages above are carried forward exactly as reported; the per-hormone denominators are not stated in the abstract.

## Key Contributions

- **First cross-hormone synthesis of "local hormonal drug + bone graft" in this wiki.** PTH, GH and calcitonin are placed side by side under a single protocol definition, which no held page does.
- **A rare instance of a whole drug class being reported at the vote-count level** — the three figures (87.5% / 66.7% / 60%) are the only quantitative anchor the wiki has for the hormonal local-delivery concept, and they come with the methodological caveats spelled out on this page.
- **Indicative guidance that the authors themselves rank**: PTH for surgical bone defect healing and peri-implant response; GH for peri-implant bone response; calcitonin without an indication-level claim.
- **Documents an evidence vacuum rather than a therapy.** With 22 preclinical studies and zero human participants, the review's hedged wording ("may positively affect", "may stimulate") is the most that the current literature supports, and the page states that boundary explicitly.
- **Bridges the biomaterials and pharmacology sides of the bone-regeneration folder** — the drug-delivery axis that [[bone-regeneration/haugen-2026-bone-grafts-everything-you-need]] names as the next frontier for graft materials.

## Methodology

- **Design:** systematic review; the authors state they followed PRISMA guidelines.
- **Database / window:** PubMed, 1970 – December 2023.
- **Search:** a structured search targeting both **in vivo** and **in vitro** studies.
- **Screening:** title-and-abstract screening; **22 studies** included.
- **Included evidence:** all included studies were **animal in vivo studies or in vitro studies**; the three hormones examined were PTH, GH and calcitonin, administered locally with bone grafts.
- **Synthesis:** proportion-of-studies reporting better outcomes than controls, per hormone. **No meta-analysis is reported** — no pooled effect estimate, no confidence interval, no heterogeneity statistic, no funnel plot.
- **Institutions:** Tabriz Azad University of Medical Sciences and Tabriz University of Medical Sciences (Iran); Al-Zahra Hospital Women's Reproductive Health Research Center.
- **Not reported in the abstract:** full search string; other databases or grey literature; language restrictions; PRISMA flow counts (records retrieved, duplicates, full texts assessed, exclusion reasons); protocol registration; risk-of-bias / quality appraisal tool; per-hormone denominators; animal species and models; drug doses, carriers and delivery vehicles; graft materials; outcome definitions; follow-up durations; adverse events.

## Results

| Hormone delivered locally with bone graft | Studies reporting better/superior outcomes vs control (vote count) | Where the authors locate the signal |
|---|---|---|
| Growth hormone (GH) | **87.5%** | peri-implant bone response |
| Parathyroid hormone (PTH) | **66.7%** | surgical bone defect healing and peri-implant response |
| Calcitonin | **60%** | not further specified in the abstract |

- Total included: **22 studies**, all animal in vivo or in vitro; **no human study**.
- Authors' conclusion: locally administered hormonal drugs "may positively affect osteogenesis in vivo and in vitro"; this is "especially true for PTH in the healing of surgical bone defects and peri-implant response and for GH in peri-implant bone response"; they "may stimulate bone healing in surgical bone defects and enhance peri-implant bone response".
- **Reading the numbers:** these are study-level vote counts, not effect sizes — a study with a marginal and a study with a large benefit each count once, and no magnitude, variance or pooling justification is offered. The abstract gives **no per-hormone denominators**; the 87.5% figure is arithmetically consistent with 7 of 8 studies (66.7% with 2 of 3 or 4 of 6; 60% with 3 of 5), but that is inference from the arithmetic, not a reported count.
- **No clinical endpoints anywhere in the review as reported:** no human participants, no implant survival, no ISQ (Implant Stability Quotient), no histomorphometric or radiographic values, no safety data.

## Limitations

- **Abstract only.** Full text not retrieved (closed access; no OA copy via PMC, Unpaywall or OpenAlex). Search string, study list, per-study outcomes, risk-of-bias appraisal, doses, carriers, models and follow-up are unavailable.
- **Vote counting, not pooling.** 87.5% / 66.7% / 60% carry no effect magnitude, no variance and no heterogeneity assessment; the ordering GH > PTH > calcitonin is a frequency ordering, not a comparative efficacy ranking.
- **Undisclosed denominators.** Per-hormone study counts are absent, so the precision of each percentage — and how many studies were null — cannot be judged.
- **No risk-of-bias weighting.** PRISMA is mentioned for the review process, but no quality appraisal of the included animal/in vitro work is reported, so the vote counts are unweighted by study quality.
- **Preclinical-only.** No human participant, no clinical endpoint, no safety data — hypothesis-generating only.
- **Single database.** Only PubMed is reported for a 1970–2023 window; recall is unverifiable against Embase/Cochrane/Scopus.
- **Indication-level vagueness.** "Surgical bone defects" and "peri-implant response" are named without model, defect size, graft type or dosing, so the review cannot yet say whether the signal survives in a given protocol.
