## Three-line Summary

Expert consensus from closed meeting of 14 experienced GBR clinicians (Bologna, October 2016) reviewing systematic/narrative reviews on GBR clinical management. Formulated statements and clinical recommendations across 13 domains: patient selection, blood supply, antibiotic treatment, flap passivation, timing of implant placement, implant surface, barrier membranes, bone grafts, membrane fixation, timing of membrane removal, healing complications, soft-tissue management, GBR in scarred/esthetic sites. Concluded GBR is predictable for vertical/horizontal defects when strict protocols followed; healing >6 months (9 months for severe defects); soft-tissue management critical for long-term success.

GBR은 엄격한 프로토콜 준수 시 수직/수평 결손에 예측 가능한 기법; 치유 기간 6개월 이상(중증 결손 9개월) 권고; 연조직 관리가 장기 성공의 또 다른 핵심 요소. 증거 수준은 주로 전문가 의견과 체계적 고찰 기반이며, 향후 RCT 필요성 강조.

## Summary

The 2016 Bologna GBR Symposium consensus report represents the first published expert statements and clinical recommendations specifically for guided bone regeneration procedures. Fourteen experienced clinicians reviewed eight systematic/narrative reviews covering GBR clinical management and formulated recommendations across 13 domains. The consensus defines GBR as any reconstructive technique using a barrier membrane (resorbable or nonresorbable) with particulate bone graft, excluding onlay/inlay grafts and sinus lift. The Wang & Al-Shammari defect classification (Class H horizontal, V vertical, C combined; small <3mm, medium 4–6mm, large >7mm) was adopted as the clinical decision framework. The PASS principles (Primary closure, Angiogenesis, Space maintenance, Stability) are mandated as the foundational success criteria. Key clinical recommendations include: resorbable membranes for horizontal/small vertical defects; nonresorbable PTFE or Ti-reinforced membranes for large vertical defects; allograft/xenograft alone with cell-occlusive membrane for horizontal augmentation; xenograft/allograft mixed with autogenous bone for vertical augmentation with space-maintaining device; mandatory membrane fixation with osteosynthesis screws/titanium pins; nonresorbable membrane removal at 4–6 months (simultaneous implants), ≥6 months (lateral GBR), 6–12 months (vertical GBR); Fontana classification for nonresorbable membrane complications (Class I–IV); keratinized tissue width >2 mm around augmented-site implants; and staged approaches with soft-tissue augmentation for esthetic/scarred sites. The consensus identifies critical research gaps in compromised patient management, antibiotic regimens, cortical perforation parameters, fixation methods, and long-term soft-tissue outcomes.

## Key Contributions

- First published GBR-specific consensus statements and clinical recommendations (13 domains)
- Adoption of Wang & Al-Shammari defect classification as clinical decision framework
- PASS principles mandated as GBR success prerequisites
- Defect-type-driven membrane selection algorithm (resorbable horizontal/small vertical; nonresorbable/Ti-reinforced large vertical)
- Bone grafting protocol: horizontal → allograft/xenograft alone + cell-occlusive membrane; vertical → xenograft/allograft + AB + space-maintaining device
- Membrane fixation: osteosynthesis screws/titanium pins bilaterally recommended
- Nonresorbable membrane removal timing guidelines by defect type and implant timing
- Fontana complication classification for nonresorbable membranes (Class I–IV) with management protocols
- Soft-tissue management: KT >2 mm recommended for augmented sites
- Scarred site management: Urban classification (vestibule depth × periosteum integrity) with specific flap techniques
- Esthetic site protocol: staged, submerged healing, soft-tissue augmentation, peri-implant plastic surgery

## Methodology

- **Design**: Expert consensus meeting (closed meeting of 14 clinicians)
- **Setting**: Second GBR Symposium, Bologna, Italy, October 14–15, 2016
- **Evidence base**: 8 systematic/narrative reviews prepared pre-meeting (Retzepi & Donos 2010; Rakhmatia et al. 2013; Clementini et al. 2013; Carbonell et al. 2014; Al-Nawas & Schiegnitz 2014; Millinkovic & Cordaro 2014; Lutz et al. 2015; Keestra et al. 2016)
- **Process**: Evidence review → structured interactive discussion → statement formulation → clinical recommendations → future research implications
- **GBR definition**: Barrier membrane (resorbable/nonresorbable) + particulate bone graft; excludes onlay/inlay grafts, sinus lift

## Results

### Patient Selection & Risk Factors
| Factor | Recommendation | Evidence Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Smoking | Light smokers only (<10 cig/day); 80–90% survival but ↑ implant loss/peri-implantitis | Heitz-Mayfield & Huynh-Ba 2009; Lindfords et al. 2010 |
| Diabetes | HbA1c <7% (53 mmol/mol) mandatory pre-GBR; controlled DM similar bone matrix but ↓ BIC | Dubey et al. 2013; experimental studies |
| MRONJ risk | AAOMS position papers: drug holiday ≥2mo pre-op/1mo post-op for oral BP >4yr or +corticosteroids | Ruggiero et al. 2009/2014; Walter et al. 2016 |

### Antibiotic Prophylaxis
- **Universal recommendation**: Loading dose 1hr pre-op + 7-day postoperative course
- **Suggested regimen**: 2g amoxicillin+clavulanic acid + 500mg metronidazole 1hr pre-op → 1g amoxicillin+clavulanic acid q8h ×7d + 250mg metronidazole q8h ×4d
- **Evidence gap**: Optimal choice/timing/dose poorly documented; pathogenic bacteria adherence to membranes unstudied

### Flap Passivation (PASS "P")
- Tension-free primary closure = single most critical factor
- **Mandible**: Lingual + buccal flap management easier
- **Maxilla**: Buccal rotational, coronally positioned palatal sliding, split palatal rotated, palatal advanced flaps
- **Anterior maxilla**: Urban classification (4 scenarios: vestibule depth × periosteum integrity)

### Blood Supply (PASS "A")
- Cortical perforation/decortication for marrow communication and bleeding into defect
- Controversy: perforation may accelerate or reduce regeneration; non-perforated cortex can also regenerate
- **Research gap**: No data on optimal hole diameter, spatial configuration, decortication extent

### Implant Surface
- Rough surfaces (Sa 1–2 μm) → stronger bone response vs smooth (Sa <0.5) or very rough (Sa >2)
- Simultaneous GBR → rough-surfaced implants recommended for osseointegration
- **Caution**: Possible long-term peri-implantitis risk with rough surfaces in augmented areas; more data needed

### Timing of Implant Placement
| Approach | Success Rate | Survival | Advantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simultaneous | 87.7% (61.5–100%) | 98.9% | Native bone assessment, implant-guided augmentation, membrane/graft/clot stabilization |
| Staged | 86.4% (75–98.3%) | 100% | Less operative time, no primary stability needed, better vascularization, predictable marginal bone, prosthetic guidance |

- Short follow-up, no meta-analysis possible; clinician preference guides choice

### Barrier Membranes
| Type | Characteristics | Indication |
|---|---|---|
| e-PTFE | 5–30 μm pores, bacterial infiltration risk | Legacy gold standard |
| d-PTFE | 0.2–0.3 μm pores, eliminates bacterial infiltration, easier removal | Preferred nonresorbable for vertical ridges |
| Ti + PTFE | Ti frame for space maintenance/stability in large defects | Large vertical/non-space-maintaining defects |
| Native collagen | Enzymatic degradation, unpredictable resorption | Horizontal/small vertical defects |
| Cross-linked collagen | Longer barrier function, reduced biocompatibility | Horizontal/moderate defects |

- **Recommendation**: Resorbable for horizontal/potentially small vertical; nonresorbable for large vertical defects

### Bone Grafting
- **Autogenous bone (AB)**: Gold standard (osteogenic/osteoinductive/osteoconductive)
- **DBBM**: Most used xenograft (osteoconductive scaffold)
- **Al-Nawas & Schiegnitz review**: Implant survival 97.4% (BSM), 98.6% (AB), 100% (BSM+AB) — independent of biomaterial
- **Recommendations**:
  - Horizontal: allograft/xenograft alone + cell-occlusive membrane
  - Vertical: xenograft/allograft + AB + cell-occlusive membrane + space-maintaining device
  - Large/progressive defects: autogenous particulate bone

### Membrane Fixation
- Rigid fixation → faster, more organized bone formation (in vivo evidence)
- RCT: significant complications when membranes lacked adequate fixation
- **Recommendation**: Osteosynthesis screws/titanium pins on buccal AND lingual/palatal sides
- Optimal fixation method for human bone regeneration needs research

### Timing of Membrane Removal (Nonresorbable)
| Scenario | Healing Period |
|---|---|
| Lateral GBR (BSM±AB) | ≥6 months |
| Simultaneous implants | 4–6 months |
| Vertical GBR (simultaneous/delayed) | 6–12 months |

- High interstudy heterogeneity; no clear surgical conclusions

### Healing Complications (Fontana Classification for e-PTFE)
| Class | Description | Management |
|---|---|---|
| I | Early exposure <3mm, no pus | Leave ≤1 month, 0.2% CHX gel BID, close follow-up |
| II | Early exposure >3mm, no pus | Immediate removal (bone usually intact) |
| III | Early exposure with pus | Immediate removal, curette infected particles/tissue |
| IV | Abscess without exposure | Immediate removal, curette graft, local antibiotic wash |
| Late (>3mo) | Any exposure | Immediate removal regardless |

- d-PTFE/Ti mesh/resorbable may require different management

### Soft-Tissue Management
- **KT width >2 mm** recommended around augmented-site implants: prevents early marginal bone loss, facilitates hygiene, improves plaque/gingival scores
- **Methods**: Apically positioned flap + FGG/CTG; coronally advanced flap + CTG; collagen matrices with apical/coronal flaps
- **Research gap**: 5–10yr studies needed on soft-tissue augmentation effects on stability/esthetics/PROMs

### GBR in Scarred Sites (Urban Classification)
| Scenario | Techniques |
|---|---|
| Shallow vestibule + native periosteum | Periosteal incision, safety flap |
| Deep vestibule + native periosteum | Coronal advancement, papillary shift |
| Shallow vestibule + scarred periosteum | Periosteoplasty, muscle-releasing incision, double-layer suture |
| Deep vestibule + scarred periosteum | Remote flap, periosteoplasty, muscle release |

### GBR in Esthetic Sites
- Staged implant placement, submerged healing with soft-tissue augmentation
- Resorbable membranes for horizontal small defects; nonresorbable/Ti mesh for horizontal large + vertical defects
- Re-entry surgery with/without soft-tissue augmentation + esthetic peri-implant plastic surgery
