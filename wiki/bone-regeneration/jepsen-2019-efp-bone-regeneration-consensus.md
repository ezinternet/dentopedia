## Three-line Summary

Consensus statement from 20 European experts (EFP 15th Workshop, 2019) synthesizing four systematic reviews on alveolar ridge defect regeneration — lateral/vertical bone augmentation, peri-implantitis bone defects, and lateral window sinus augmentation.
Lateral bone augmentation before implant placement is predictable for ridge width gain; vertical ridge augmentation is effective but carries high complication rates; peri-implantitis defect reconstruction shows radiographic but not clinical benefit; lateral window sinus augmentation is reliable long-term.
All four procedures are effective for ridge reconstruction, but demanding techniques and complication risk must be weighed against patient needs.

## Summary

This EFP consensus report synthesizes evidence from four systematic reviews presented at the 15th European Workshop on Periodontology (2019). The expert group of 20 leading European clinicians and researchers evaluated bone augmentation procedures designed to enable implant placement in compromised ridges. Lateral bone augmentation prior to implant placement is supported by substantial evidence as a predictable procedure for gaining ridge width. Vertical ridge augmentation was found effective in many studies but consistently associated with high complication rates — the primary clinical caveat. For peri-implantitis-related bone defects, meta-analyses confirmed radiographic bone gain from reconstructive procedures, but clinical outcome improvements (probing depth, clinical attachment level) were not demonstrated in the limited RCT evidence available. Lateral window sinus floor augmentation was validated as a reliable long-term procedure for the partially and fully edentulous maxilla.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes lateral bone augmentation (staged) as a predictable, guideline-supported procedure for horizontal deficiency
- Flags high complication rates as the dominant caveat for vertical ridge augmentation
- Distinguishes radiographic from clinical benefit in peri-implantitis GBR — clinically important nuance
- Validates lateral window sinus augmentation for long-term edentulous maxilla rehabilitation
- Provides a single-document evidence synthesis across four bone augmentation domains from EFP authority
