## Three-line Summary

Animal (rabbit calvaria) histologic/histomorphometric study, n=13 rabbits with 52 identical 6.5-mm calvarial defects, comparing large- and small-particle Straumann Bone Ceramic (biphasic HA/beta-TCP) against Bio-Oss (bovine xenograft) and an unfilled control at 4 and 8 weeks.

No statistically significant difference in bone fill between any of the four groups (Bio-Oss numerically highest, then control, L-SBC, S-SBC); large-particle bone ceramic (L-SBC) produced significantly more inflammation and foreign-body giant-cell reaction than Bio-Oss and control.

The hypothesized advantage of bone ceramic's higher resorption rate translating into more new bone was not confirmed; clinical implication is that Bio-Oss and Straumann Bone Ceramic can be considered comparably osteoconductive at short/medium healing intervals, but small sample size and short 4-/8-week follow-up limit generalization to human alveolar grafting.

## 세줄요약

동물실험(토끼 두개골) 조직학·조직계측학 연구, n=13마리 토끼에 동일 규격 6.5mm 두개골 결손 52개를 형성해 Straumann Bone Ceramic(대·소입자, HA/beta-TCP 이상재료)과 Bio-Oss(우골 이종이식재), 무처치 대조군을 4주·8주 시점에 비교.

네 군 간 골충전량에 통계적으로 유의한 차이 없음(Bio-Oss가 수치상 최고, 이어서 대조군·L-SBC·S-SBC 순); 대입자 골세라믹(L-SBC)이 Bio-Oss·대조군보다 유의하게 더 많은 염증반응과 이물반응(foreign-body reaction)을 보임.

골세라믹의 높은 흡수율이 더 많은 신생골 형성으로 이어질 것이라는 가설은 확인되지 않았음; Bio-Oss와 Straumann Bone Ceramic은 단기·중기 치유 시점에서 비교적 유사한 골전도성(osteoconductive)을 가진다고 볼 수 있으나, 작은 표본 크기와 짧은 4주/8주 추적 관찰이 인체 치조골 이식 상황으로의 일반화를 제한하는 핵심 한계.

## Summary

This rabbit calvarial defect study directly pitted two grafting materials against each other in the same animal to remove inter-subject variability: a fully synthetic biphasic calcium phosphate (Straumann Bone Ceramic, 60% hydroxyapatite / 40% beta-TCP, tested at large and small particle sizes) versus Bio-Oss (deproteinized bovine bone mineral xenograft), plus an unfilled control. After 4 and 8 weeks of healing, histologic and pixel-count histomorphometric analysis found **no statistically significant difference in new bone formation** between any of the four groups — Bio-Oss was numerically highest, followed by control, large-particle ceramic, and small-particle ceramic, in that order, but none of the differences reached significance. The authors' starting hypothesis — that the synthetic ceramic's faster resorption would translate into more new bone — was not borne out. What *was* significant was the inflammatory profile: large-particle Straumann Bone Ceramic produced meaningfully more lymphocytic inflammation and foreign-body giant-cell reaction than Bio-Oss and the control, implicating particle size (and possibly incomplete sintering releasing microparticles) rather than material chemistry alone as a driver of local tissue reactivity.

## Key Contributions

- Within-animal, 4-arm design (Bio-Oss / large-particle synthetic ceramic / small-particle synthetic ceramic / unfilled control) in the same rabbit calvarium, controlling for individual biological variability across materials.
- Directly tested and refuted the hypothesis that higher material resorption rate predicts more new bone formation within a short (4-8 week) window.
- Isolated particle size as a driver of inflammatory/foreign-body response independent of overall bone-fill outcome — large-particle ceramic was most inflammatory despite not differing significantly in bone formation from other arms.
- Quantitative digital histomorphometry (pixel-count image analysis) applied blindly by two calibrated oral pathologists to both new bone and remaining graft material.

## Methodology

Thirteen New Zealand rabbits underwent creation of four standardized 6.5-mm circular calvarial defects each (52 total), with material assignment rotated across animals to control for anatomic-site bias. Sites received Bio-Oss (250-1000 µm bovine xenograft), large-particle Straumann Bone Ceramic (L-SBC, 500-1000 µm, 60% HA/40% beta-TCP), small-particle Straumann Bone Ceramic (S-SBC, 400-700 µm, same composition), or were left unfilled as controls. Animals were sacrificed at 4 weeks (n=6) or 8 weeks (n=7). H&E-stained sections were scored blindly by two calibrated oral pathologists for inflammation intensity (0-3 grade by lymphocyte density) and foreign-body reaction (0-3 grade by giant-cell foci), and quantified digitally (SigmaScan Pro + Adobe Photoshop) for new bone and residual graft material as pixel counts in a standardized central 1-mm region of interest. Friedman tests with Bonferroni-corrected post hoc comparisons assessed between-material differences; Mann-Whitney U tests assessed 4-week vs 8-week change within each material.

## Results

- **Bone formation**: no statistically significant difference among Bio-Oss, control, L-SBC, and S-SBC (p>.05), though Bio-Oss was numerically highest.
- **Inflammation**: control < Bio-Oss < S-SBC ≈ L-SBC, with L-SBC significantly higher than both control and Bio-Oss (p<.05); L-SBC vs S-SBC not significant.
- **Foreign-body reaction**: all grafted sites > control (p<.05); L-SBC highest and Bio-Oss lowest among grafted materials.
- **Time course**: only the Bio-Oss group showed a significant increase in new bone from 4 to 8 weeks (p<.05) — the authors attribute this to Bio-Oss's comparatively lower inflammatory burden permitting less cell damage and thus faster bone maturation.
- **Residual graft material**: not significantly different between materials; L-SBC retained the most (largest particle size, slowest resorption), followed by Bio-Oss, then S-SBC — the latter finding was unexpected given S-SBC's smaller nominal particle size, attributed by the authors to HA content and particle-shape (round SBC vs irregular Bio-Oss) effects on inter-particle space and angiogenesis.

## Key Contributions (Clinical framing)

Both materials behaved as classically **osteoconductive** (scaffold-only) rather than osteoinductive in this model, consistent with prior literature on synthetic HA/TCP ceramics and bovine xenografts. The practical takeaway for material selection is that, at short-to-medium healing intervals, bone-fill outcomes between Bio-Oss and biphasic calcium phosphate ceramic may not differ meaningfully — but particle size selection (favor smaller, better-sintered particles) may matter more for minimizing local inflammatory/foreign-body response than material chemistry per se.
