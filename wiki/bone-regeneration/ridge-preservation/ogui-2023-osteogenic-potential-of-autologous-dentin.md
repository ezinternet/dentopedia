## Three-line Summary

Prospective randomized controlled clinical trial (37 patients: 20 ADG vs 17 bovine xenograft + autologous bone; 37 CBCT scans, 22 bone biopsies) comparing autologous dentin graft (ADG) with bovine xenograft mixed with autologous bone (BX+AB) for alveolar ridge preservation in the esthetic zone of the maxilla.

CBCT dimensions and histomorphometry were comparable between groups (test-group new bone 72.55 ± 12.14%; no significant differences in new bone, residual biomaterial or soft tissue), while immunohistochemistry showed significantly higher TNF-α intensity in the new-bone area (p = 0.0003) and around residual biomaterial (p = 0.0027) and higher BMP-4 around biomaterial particles (p = 0.0001) in the BX+AB group.

ADG is biocompatible and achieved osteoconductive/osteoinductive performance comparable to the gold-standard mixture while removing the autologous-bone harvest (shorter chair time, lower cost, less morbidity); the small single-center sample (37 patients, 22 biopsies, one surgeon), 4-month horizon and unblinded CBCT measurement limit certainty.

## 세줄요약

전향적 무작위 대조 임상시험(Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT, 37명: 자가골 치아기질(Autologous Dentin Graft, ADG) 20명 vs 소 이종골+자가골(Bovine Xenograft with Autologous Bone, BX+AB) 17명, CBCT 37건·골생검 22개): 심미영역(esthetic zone) 상악 발치 후 치조제 보존술(Alveolar Ridge Preservation, ARP)에서 두 이식재를 직접 비교했다.

방사선(CBCT)·조직형태계측(histomorphometry) 결과는 두 군 간 유의한 차이가 없었고 시험군 신생골(new bone)은 72.55 ± 12.14%였으나, 면역화학(immunohistochemistry)에서는 BX+AB 군의 종양괴사인자-α(Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha, TNF-α) 강도가 신생골 영역(p = 0.0003)과 잔존 이식재(residual graft) 주위(p = 0.0027), 골이형화유도인자-4(Bone Morphogenetic Protein-4, BMP-4) 강도가 잔존 이식재 주위(p = 0.0001)에서 유의하게 높았다.

ADG는 생체적합성을 보이며 자가골 채취 없이(체어타임 단축·비용 저하·morbidity 감소) BX+AB와 비교 가능한 골전도성(osteoconductive)·골유도성(osteoinductive) 성능을 달성했지만, 37명·생검 22개·단일 술자의 표본 크기와 4개월 단기 추적, 비맹검(masking 없는) CBCT 측정이 근거를 제한한다.

## Summary

This prospective randomized controlled trial asked a question the wiki could previously not answer: does an autogenous dentin graft (ADG) behave like the graft combination clinicians actually use, and does it retain osteoinductive signaling in humans? Thirty-seven patients (screened from 42) referred for extraction of a hopeless tooth in the anterior maxilla (esthetic zone) followed by implant treatment were randomized after flap elevation to ADG (n = 20) or to bovine xenograft (cerabone®) mixed with locally harvested autologous bone (BX+AB, n = 17). Both arms used a resorbable collagen membrane (Biogide®) and a palatal connective tissue graft over a full-thickness flap, and sockets with three intact bone walls only.

At 4 months the arms were indistinguishable on the endpoints that matter clinically: CBCT ridge-width change showed no statistical difference between groups (37 scans), and histomorphometry of 22 trephine biopsies (12 test / 10 control) found no significant differences in new bone, residual biomaterial or soft tissue, with test-group new bone of **72.55 ± 12.14%**. Neither arm showed any inflammatory reaction.

The divergence appeared only at the marker level. Immunohistochemistry for TNF-α and BMP-4 was positive in both arms (in osteocytes embedded in new bone and in osteoblasts on the bone surface), but intensity was significantly higher in the BX+AB arm — TNF-α in the new-bone area (p = 0.0003) and around residual particles (p = 0.0027), and BMP-4 around residual particles (p = 0.0001). The authors read this as: the added autograft supplies osteoinductive BMP-4, and the xenogeneic component elicits a stronger inflammatory reaction, which signals active remodeling rather than failure — new-bone percentages were equivalent regardless.

Clinically the ADG arm buys three things the comparator cannot: no second donor site (less morbidity and fewer donor-site complications), shorter chair time (the assistant grinds the tooth during the operation), and lower cost. The price is certainty: 37 patients, 22 biopsies, one surgeon, a single esthetic-zone population, a 4-month horizon ending at implant placement, no allocation concealment reported, and CBCT measurements taken by the operating surgeon without masking.

## Key Contributions

- **Human immunohistochemistry on ADG for the first time** — per the authors, among the first reports of TNF-α and BMP-4 expression in human alveolar bone after ADG, and the first comparison of ADG with BX+AB on clinical, radiographic and histologic axes simultaneously.
- **Quantity equals, signaling differs** — the trial's central finding: osteoconduction and osteoinduction were equivalent between an autogenous dentin graft and a xenograft-plus-autograft mixture, with the *only* statistically significant differences being marker intensities.
- **Biopsy-grade evidence at a 4-month re-entry** — new bone 72.55 ± 12.14% in the ADG arm, residual biomaterial in close contact with new bone (no gap, no fibrous interzone), and no inflammatory reaction in any of the 22 specimens.
- **A comparison arm clinicians recognize** — BX+AB is the practical benchmark (mixing autograft with xenograft and covering it with a GBR membrane is reported to reduce autograft resorption by 50%), which makes the "same outcome without the autograft" message clinically actionable rather than academic.
- **Explains the mechanism behind the null result** — DDM's irregular surface and exposed collagen fibres favour osteoblast adhesion, and the retained osteoinductive potential after EDTA demineralization is demonstrated by BMP-4 positivity.

## Methodology

- **Design**: prospective RCT; 42 screened → 37 consented and randomized (test ADG n = 20, control BX+AB n = 17); randomization with a free online randomizer (randomizer.org) performed after flap elevation; no allocation concealment, registration or intention-to-treat analysis reported.
- **Population**: adults > 18, healthy, with at least one hopeless anterior maxillary tooth (incisors, canines, premolars) planned for extraction and implant therapy; one site per patient; excluded smokers (> 10/day), uncontrolled systemic disease, head/neck radiotherapy, pregnancy/lactation. Single center (Rijeka, Croatia), single surgeon (M.O.), June 2020 – March 2022; ethics approved by the University of Rijeka Faculty of Medicine (Class 003-08/21-01/20, No. 2170-24-04-3-21-6) and Clinical Hospital Centre Rijeka (Class 003-05/20-1/151, No. 2170-29-02/1-20-2).
- **Surgery (both arms)**: atraumatic extraction and curettage; only sockets with three intact bone walls included; full-thickness flap; resorbable collagen membrane (Biogide®) under the buccal flap; palatal connective tissue graft; primary closure with resorbable 6.0 monofilament; antibiotic prophylaxis and chlorhexidine rinse; checks at 10, 30, 60 days; all patients reached implant placement at 4 months (BEGO Semados RSXPro).
- **ADG preparation**: extracted teeth cleaned with a high-speed carbide bur (fillings, calculus, caries, periodontal ligament, discoloured dentin, part of enamel removed) → Smart Dentin Grinder (SDG, KometaBio) at 300–1200 µm → 5 min alcohol-based basic cleaner → drying → two sterile PBS rinses; the manufacturer protocol yields a demineralized dentin matrix (DDM).
- **BX+AB**: cerabone® bovine trabecular bone xenograft (> 1200 °C heat treatment) mixed with autologous bone harvested locally around the surgical site.
- **Radiography**: CBCT (Planmeca Promax 3D, 0.3 mm, 120 kV, 5 mA) pre-extraction and at 4 months with identical configuration; ridge width = distance between the most prominent buccal and oral points; 37 scans (20/17) measured by the operating surgeon (**unblinded**).
- **Biopsy & histology**: 22 trephine biopsies (2.3–2.8 mm) at 4 months during implant bed prep, fixed in 4% buffered paraformaldehyde, decalcified (Osteofast 2), paraffin, 5 µm sections, H&E; 10× photomicrographs in ImageJ v1.53 → new bone (NB) / residual biomaterial (BM) / soft tissue (ST) percentages.
- **Immunohistochemistry**: anti-TNF-α (ab6671) and anti-BMP-4 (ab124715), 0.3% H₂O₂, citrate retrieval, DAB (ab64238); 200× photomicrographs → cellSense v3.2 (intensity separation, black/white conversion, background subtraction, mean grey value) in two regions (new bone; around residual particles); read blind by one investigator (O.C.P.).
- **Statistics**: Kolmogorov–Smirnov normality test (data normal), mean ± SD, Student's t-test two-tailed, SPSS 25, p < 0.05.

## Results

- **Clinical**: uneventful healing in all 37 patients; moderate discomfort, pain and swelling reported by 5 test and 3 control patients; no exposed wounds, no biomaterial leakage; by 4 months mucosa and bone healed completely in every patient and all transitioned to implant therapy.
- **CBCT (37 scans)**: **no statistically significant difference** between arms in alveolar ridge width change from pre-extraction to 4 months (per-group values sit in Table 5 of the paper, which is not part of the retrieved text artifact and is therefore not reproduced here).
- **Histology (22 biopsies)**: new bone extended apico-coronally in every specimen of both arms, lamellar, with osteocytes in lacunae; residual biomaterial in close contact with new bone; fibroblasts in soft tissue; **no inflammatory reaction in any group**; no significant between-group differences in NB, BM or ST percentages (Table 3).
- **Histomorphometric benchmark**: test-group new bone **72.55 ± 12.14%** at 4 months (the authors compare this with a clinical ADG study under GBR reporting 63% new bone and 25% residual biomaterial at 7 months).
- **Immunohistochemistry**: TNF-α- and BMP-4-positive cells in the new-bone area of both arms — in osteocytes within new bone and in surface osteoblasts; intensity quantified in the new-bone area and around residual biomaterial particles.
- **Significant between-group differences (all in the BX+AB arm, marker intensity only)**:
  - TNF-α, new-bone area — **p = 0.0003**
  - TNF-α, around residual biomaterial — **p = 0.0027**
  - BMP-4, around residual biomaterial — **p = 0.0001**
- **Interpretation by the authors**: both materials were osteoconductive and osteoinductive with no inflammatory reaction; the higher BMP-4 in BX+AB reflects the added autologous bone, and the higher TNF-α reflects xenograft-driven inflammation and active remodeling — which did not translate into more bone, since new-bone percentages were equivalent.
- **Clinical advantages of ADG over BX+AB**: shorter chair time (graft prepared by the assistant during surgery), no additional autologous bone harvest (procedure length and patient morbidity/complications reduced), lower cost (no extra material).
- **Background figures cited**: horizontal ridge loss 29–63% and vertical loss 11–22% over the first 6 months after extraction; buccal wall ≤ 1 mm → 7.5 mm height loss at 2 months vs 1 mm when > 1 mm; autograft complications 0.5–10.5%, rapid resorption 5–28%; mixing autograft with bovine xenograft under a GBR membrane reduces autograft resorption by 50%.

## Limitations

- **Small, single-center, single-surgeon sample** (37 randomized; 22 biopsied; 12 test / 10 control) restricted to the anterior maxilla — the authors justify the esthetic-zone-only design as the reason the sample could be smaller.
- **No allocation concealment, no registration, no intention-to-treat analysis**; randomization was executed with a free web randomizer after flap elevation.
- **Unblinded radiographic assessment** — the CBCT measurer was the operating surgeon; only histology and immunohistochemistry were masked.
- **No graft-free arm and no pure-xenograft arm** — the comparator contains autograft, so the trial cannot isolate the contribution of the xenograft versus the autograft, nor of ADG versus a standard xenograft alone.
- **4-month horizon only**, ending at implant placement — no implant survival, marginal bone level or esthetic outcome.
- **Data-table values not in the retrieved artifact** — per-group CBCT widths (Table 5) and per-group histomorphometric percentages (Tables 3–4) are quoted only in narrative form, so they cannot be re-derived here.
- **The significant marker differences remain unexplained** — the authors explicitly state that the TNF-α/BMP-4 differences require further investigation and should be the focus of future clinical studies.

## Clinical Implications

- In the esthetic zone, an autogenous dentin graft is a defensible alternative to a xenograft-plus-autograft mixture: same 4-month radiographic and histologic outcome, without a second donor site.
- The decisive argument is logistical, not biological — chair time, morbidity and cost all favour ADG, and the biology was equivalent rather than superior.
- Marker intensity (TNF-α, BMP-4) at 4 months did **not** predict bone quantity in this trial; treating immunostaining intensity as a surrogate for regeneration quality is not supported by these data.
- Do not read the trial as evidence that ADG equals pure xenograft — the comparator contained autograft, and no graft-free control was run.
