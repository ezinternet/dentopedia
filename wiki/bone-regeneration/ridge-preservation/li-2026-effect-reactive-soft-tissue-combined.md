## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (40 sites, type IV severe-defect molar sockets) comparing socket sealing with reactive soft tissue (RST) + injectable platelet-rich fibrin (iPRF) + DBBM against guided bone regeneration (GBR) with collagen membrane + collagen sponge, assessed on CBCT at T0 (pre-op), T1 (immediately post-op) and T2 (6 months) — abstract-only ingest.

Horizontal width increased significantly in both arms at 1, 3 and 5 mm below the crest with no between-group difference, but RST was superior at the crest centre for vertical change from T2 to T1 (+0.34 mm gain vs −0.88 mm loss, p=0.023) and for graft material retention (92.84% vs 68.04%, p=0.003); secondary grafting rate did not differ and short-term implant survival was 100% in both arms — abstract-only ingest.

RST + DBBM reduced ridge resorption in severe defect molar sites and showed a better tendency for vertical height preservation and graft retention than GBR, which the authors frame as comparable bone preservation with a simpler, cheaper procedure — abstract-only ingest.

## Summary

Type IV (severe defect) molar sockets are the hardest ARP case the wiki holds: large multi-rooted defects where the incumbent is a full GBR stack — particulate graft, collagen membrane, collagen sponge. This retrospective cohort of 40 sites tests a leaner seal: **reactive soft tissue (RST)**, a soft tissue seal over the socket orifice, combined with **injectable platelet-rich fibrin (iPRF)** and DBBM. The comparator is GBR with collagen membrane + collagen sponge. Outcomes were read on CBCT at three timepoints (T0 pre-op, T1 immediately post-op, T2 at 6 months) and covered horizontal width (1, 3, 5 mm below crest), vertical height, graft material retention, post-operative complications, secondary grafting at implant placement, and short-term implant survival.

The result is not a horizontal win. Both arms gained width significantly at all three depths, with no difference between them — so the seal choice did not change how much width was recovered. The separation appears on the vertical axis and in the fate of the graft: from T2 to T1 the RST arm **gained 0.34 mm at the crest centre** while controls **lost 0.88 mm** (p=0.023), and graft retention was **92.84% vs 68.04%** (p=0.003). Secondary grafting rate did not differ, and short-term implant survival was 100% in both arms. The authors read this as comparable bone preservation achieved with a simpler and cheaper procedure than membrane-plus-sponge GBR.

Clinically, the interesting claim is that the membrane/sponge stack is buying less than assumed in this socket class: what it buys is horizontal width, which the RST seal matched anyway. What it appears *not* to buy is vertical crest stability or graft retention — both of which went the other way. The caveat is the design: retrospective, 40 sites, single centre, 6-month horizon, and abstract-level reporting that withholds per-arm n and complication counts.

## Key Contributions

1. Tests socket sealing in **type IV severe-defect molar sockets** — a socket class outside the esthetic-zone/anterior evidence the wiki previously held for socket seal vs GBR.
2. Introduces **iPRF (injectable, liquid-phase)** as an ARP adjunct to DBBM, distinct from the L-PRF membrane cover already documented for socket sealing.
3. Splits the dimensional outcome: horizontal width is a tie, the advantage is **vertical crest centre + graft retention** — a distinction pooled dimensional-change reporting hides.
4. Reports **graft material retention rate** (92.84% vs 68.04%) as a quantified endpoint, which most ARP trials do not report.
5. Makes a **procedure-simplification and cost** argument against collagen membrane + sponge GBR without conceding bone preservation.

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective cohort, single report; group-allocation rationale not stated in the abstract.
- **Sites**: 40 molar sites, all classified as type IV (severe defect).
- **Test arm**: RST socket sealing + iPRF + DBBM.
- **Control arm**: GBR — collagen membrane + collagen sponge with particulate graft.
- **Imaging**: CBCT at T0 (preoperative), T1 (immediately postoperative), T2 (6-month follow-up). Vertical change is expressed as **T2 − T1**.
- **Outcomes**: horizontal ridge width change at 1/3/5 mm below the crest; vertical ridge height change; graft material retention rate; post-operative surgical complications; incidence of secondary bone grafting at implant placement; short-term implant survival rate.
- **Follow-up**: 6 months to the T2 CBCT; implant outcomes limited to "short-term survival".

**Reading the table**: the two significant rows are on different axes from the non-significant one. Horizontal width — the outcome most ARP trials headline — is equivalent.
