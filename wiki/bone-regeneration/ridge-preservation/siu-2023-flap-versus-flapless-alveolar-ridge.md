## Three-line Summary
Single-blinded RCT (n=24, randomized to flapless vs flap alveolar ridge preservation) testing whether preserving the periosteal blood supply limits crestal ridge width/height loss; sockets in both groups grafted with demineralized bone matrix + mineralized particulate allograft under a barrier, re-entry at 4 months with histology.
Both groups lost significant ridge width (flapless 1.3±0.9 mm from 8.3±1.3 to 7.0±1.9 mm; flap 1.0±1.1 mm from 8.5±1.5 to 7.5±1.5 mm; each p<0.05) and midbuccal height (flapless 0.5±0.9 mm vs flap 0.9±1.3 mm; each p<0.05) with NO statistically significant between-group difference; flapless showed more vital mineralized tissue (44±10%, between-group p>0.05) and significantly thicker occlusal soft tissue at re-entry (p<0.05).
Periosteal blood-supply preservation does not translate into measurably better ridge dimensional outcomes in ARP — the flapless benefit appears in soft-tissue thickness and (non-significantly) histological tissue quality; abstract-only evidence, small n split between two groups, single-blinded design.

## 세줄요약
단일눈가림 무작위대조시험 (RCT, n=24): 치조제보존술 (Alveolar Ridge Preservation, ARP)에서 무절개 (Flapless) vs 절개 (Flap) 기법을 무작위 배정해, 골막 혈행 (Periosteal Blood Supply) 보존이 치조정 (crestal) 폭·높이 소실을 줄이는지 검증 — 양군 모두 탈회골기질 (Demineralized Bone Matrix, DBM) + 무기질 입자 동종이식골로 이식 후 차단막으로 피개, 4개월 재입술(re-entry) 시 조직학 검체 채취.
양군 모두 유의한 치조정 폭 소실 (무절개 1.3±0.9 mm, 8.3±1.3→7.0±1.9 mm; 절개 1.0±1.1 mm, 8.5±1.5→7.5±1.5 mm; 각 p<0.05)과 협측 수직 소실 (무절개 0.5±0.9 mm vs 절개 0.9±1.3 mm, 각 p<0.05)을 보였으나 군 간 차이는 통계적으로 유의하지 않았고, 무절개군은 생존 무기질화 조직 (Vital Mineralized Tissue)이 44±10%로 더 많았으나(군 간 p>0.05) 교합면 연조직 두께만 유의하게 두꺼웠다 (p<0.05).
골막 혈행 보존은 ARP의 치조골 크기(dimension) 유지로 이어지지 않음 — 무절개 ARP의 이점은 크기가 아닌 연조직 두께·조직학적 질에서 나타남; 초록(abstract)만 확보된 근거라는 점, n=24 소표본·단일눈가림 설계가 한계.

## Summary
Siu et al. (2023, Journal of Periodontology) ran a single-blinded randomized controlled trial asking whether preserving the periosteal blood supply — i.e., avoiding mucoperiosteal flap elevation — limits crestal ridge loss after alveolar ridge preservation (ARP). Twenty-four patients were randomized to flapless or flap ARP; every socket was grafted with demineralized bone matrix plus mineralized particulate allograft and covered with a barrier, so flap management was the sole between-group variable. At 4-month re-entry, both groups had lost significant crestal width and midbuccal vertical height (each p<0.05 within group), but there was no statistically significant between-group difference on any dimensional outcome; flapless ARP showed more vital mineralized tissue (44±10%, NS) and significantly thicker occlusal soft tissue (p<0.05). The clinical implication is that the theoretical vascular penalty of periosteal elevation does not translate into measurably worse ridge dimensions in ARP — where flaplessness helps, it helps through soft tissue (thickness) and possibly tissue quality, not bone quantity. The main caveats are the abstract-only evidence base, the small sample split across two arms, and the single-blinded design.

## Key Contributions
- Randomized, graft-controlled clinical test of the periosteal blood-supply hypothesis in ARP — flap vs flapless as the only surgical variable, both arms receiving identical grafting (DBM + mineralized particulate allograft + barrier).
- Combined clinical dimensional, histological (vital mineralized tissue), and soft-tissue (occlusal thickness) endpoints at a single 4-month re-entry.
- Demonstrates that flapless ARP does not significantly outperform flap ARP on ridge width, ridge height, or vital mineralized tissue — the vascular-preservation rationale does not deliver dimensional benefit, while the soft-tissue benefit (thicker occlusal tissue) is real and significant.

## Methodology
- **Design**: single-blinded RCT, parallel groups; n=24 patients randomly assigned to flapless or flap ARP (per-arm n not stated in the abstract).
- **Protocol**: socket grafting with demineralized bone matrix + mineralized particulate allograft, barrier coverage in both groups; re-entry at 4 months for histological sampling and implant placement.
- **Outcomes**: crestal ridge width change (mm), midbuccal vertical change (mm), % vital mineralized tissue on histology, occlusal soft-tissue thickness at re-entry.

## Results

| Outcome | Flapless ARP | Flap ARP | Between-group |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crestal ridge width, baseline | 8.3±1.3 mm | 8.5±1.5 mm | — |
| Crestal ridge width, 4 months | 7.0±1.9 mm | 7.5±1.5 mm | — |
| Crestal ridge width loss | 1.3±0.9 mm (p<0.05 within group) | 1.0±1.1 mm (p<0.05 within group) | NS |
| Midbuccal vertical change | 0.5±0.9 mm loss (p<0.05 within group) | 0.9±1.3 mm loss (p<0.05 within group) | NS |
| Vital mineralized tissue (histology) | 44±10% | value not stated in abstract | NS (p>0.05) |
| Occlusal soft-tissue thickness at 4 months | thicker | thinner | p<0.05 (flapless greater) |

- Both arms lost significant ridge width and midbuccal height over 4 months (each p<0.05 within group); no statistically significant between-group difference on any dimensional outcome.
- The (non-significant) directional pattern was mixed: flapless lost slightly more crestal width (1.3 vs 1.0 mm) but slightly less midbuccal vertical height (0.5 vs 0.9 mm).
- Flapless ARP showed more vital mineralized tissue (44±10%) but the histological between-group difference was not significant (p>0.05); the only significant between-group finding was thicker occlusal soft tissue in the flapless group at 4-month re-entry (p<0.05).
