## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 9 animal studies (403 animals: 8 rat models, 1 mongrel dog) assessing local statin administration (simvastatin × 5, fluvastatin × 4; single application in all studies) in tooth extraction sockets; PROSPERO-registered (CRD42022299247); SYRCLE bias assessment; meta-analysis precluded by heterogeneity.
Local statin application preserved residual ridge height (simvastatin-PLGA: 0.960 vs 0.922 at 12 weeks), upregulated TGF-β1 (골형성 성장인자, TGF-β1) mRNA (P<0.05) and 골형성단백질-2 (BMP-2) mRNA (P<0.01) at weeks 1–4, increased bone density and BV/TV across delivery systems, and reduced MRONJ-like necrotic bone exposure in two fluvastatin studies.
All evidence is preclinical; allocation concealment was HIGH risk in all 9 studies and detection blinding HIGH in 8/9 — translational validity is limited pending human RCTs.

## Summary

This PROSPERO-registered systematic review (Kommuri et al., *Annals of Translational Medicine* 2025) is the first to comprehensively aggregate preclinical evidence on local statin application for tooth extraction socket healing. Nine studies met eligibility from 412 screened (8 rat models + 1 mongrel dog split-mouth study; 403 animals total). Statins used were simvastatin (5 studies) and fluvastatin (4 studies), always applied as a single local administration at the time of extraction via PLGA scaffolds, hydrogel microspheres, calcium-phosphate carriers, or perisocket injection.

All nine studies reported positive effects on extraction socket healing relative to controls. The mechanistic basis was confirmed by Liu 2009 (in situ hybridization): simvastatin upregulates TGF-β1 (골형성 성장인자, TGF-β1) mRNA (P<0.05) and BMP-2 (골형성단백질-2, BMP-2) mRNA (P<0.01) in the socket at weeks 1–4, with VEGF (혈관내피세포 성장인자, VEGF) significantly elevated at weeks 1–2 — temporally aligned with bone formation. Wu 2008 (PLGA-simvastatin) demonstrated a significantly higher residual alveolar ridge at all time points through 12 weeks (0.960±0.026 vs 0.922±0.018). Willett 2017 (BMM + simvastatin 0.2 mg) showed reduced bone loss, lower inflammation density, higher osteoblast-to-osteoclast ratio, and greater ridge width. Fluvastatin studies (Yasunami 2015, Rakhmatia 2018) confirmed increased bone density and volume with various carrier systems. Two MRONJ-focused studies (Adachi 2020, Sanda 2021) demonstrated that fluvastatin injection after extraction reduced necrotic bone exposure and achieved epithelial closure in at-risk models.

SYRCLE risk-of-bias assessment revealed high risk of allocation concealment in all 9 studies and detection blinding in 8/9, substantially limiting internal validity. The use of young growing rats (4–8 weeks) in most studies further limits direct extrapolation to adult human patients. No human RCT data exist.

## Key Contributions

1. **First SR of statins in extraction socket healing**: 9 studies, 403 animals, PROSPERO registration, SYRCLE bias tool — establishes preclinical evidence landscape.
2. **Three therapeutic domains**: (a) alveolar ridge dimensional preservation; (b) osseointegration support around immediate implants; (c) MRONJ prevention/treatment.
3. **Growth-factor mechanism quantified**: TGF-β1 P<0.05, BMP-2 P<0.01 at weeks 1–4; VEGF upregulation weeks 1–2 by in situ hybridization.
4. **Delivery system inventory**: PLGA scaffold (1 mg simvastatin), hydrogel microspheres (0.01 mg simvastatin), PLGA injectable microspheres (fluvastatin 20–40 µg·kg⁻¹), HA granules + fluvastatin, perisocket injection — single application sufficient in all.
5. **MRONJ signal**: fluvastatin reduced necrotic bone ratio and enabled epithelial closure in bisphosphonate/MRONJ models — potential prophylactic application at high-risk extractions.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review; no meta-analysis (heterogeneity)
- **PROSPERO**: CRD42022299247
- **Search**: PubMed, Cochrane, EMBASE, Ovid Medline, Scopus, Web of Science, EBSCOhost, JSTOR (up to December 2023)
- **412 manuscripts** screened → 9 included
- **PICO**: P = animal models with tooth extraction; I = local statin; C = no statin; O = tissue healing
- **Risk of bias**: SYRCLE tool — allocation concealment HIGH in 9/9; detection blinding HIGH in 8/9; selective reporting and incomplete data LOW in all 9
- **Animal models**: Wistar rats (most), Sprague-Dawley rats (Willett), mongrel dogs (Mansour); age 4 weeks – 24 months; duration 14–84 days
- **Statin dosing**: simvastatin 0.01–2.2 mg; fluvastatin 0.1–10 mg/kg (or PLGA 20–40 µg·kg⁻¹); single local application

|---|---|---|---|
| Wu 2008 | Wistar rats | SIM 1 mg/PLGA | Ridge height 0.960 vs 0.922 at 12 wks; ↑BMD wks 4–12 |

| Yasunami 2015 | Wistar rats | FLU 20/40 µg·kg⁻¹ PLGA | ↑Bone density/volume on day 28; connective tissue ↑ |
| Willett 2017 | S-D rats | SIM 0.2 mg (BMM) | ↑Ridge width; ↓bone loss (P<0.01); ↑osteoblast/↓osteoclast ratio |
| Rakhmatia 2018 | Rats | FLU 0.5 mg (HA carrier) | ↑Bone volume; ↑BMD COFS group |
| Li 2019 | Wistar rats | SIM 0.01 mg (hydrogel MS) | Highest socket BD wk 8; consistent new bone wks 1–8 |

| Sanda 2021 | Wistar rats | FLU 1.0/10 mg/kg | Epithelial continuity; ↓necrotic bone ratio; complete closure FS-H+FS-L |

**Limitations**: All preclinical animal evidence; high bias risk (allocation concealment + detection blinding); growing rat models ≠ adult patients; no meta-analysis; optimal dose/delivery unknown.
