## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Int J Oral Implantol 2023, PMID 37158179, Wang group) summarising current knowledge of intrasocket reactive tissue (IRT) — its histopathology and biology, and the mechanisms by which residual IRT can affect extraction socket healing either favourably or unfavourably — and reframing IRT as a **removal-versus-preservation decision**, not an automatic debridement target — abstract-only ingest.

It also overviews the hand and rotary instruments used for IRT debridement, argues that IRT can be preserved as a socket sealing material, and presents clinical cases in which either strategy was chosen after extraction and before alveolar ridge preservation (ARP); the abstract gives no numbers — no sample sizes, no effect estimates, no follow-up durations.

Clinical bottom line: the decision should be driven by socket/tissue conditions and the reconstructive goal, since the beneficial effect of preserved IRT remains a hypothesis — the authors themselves call for future studies to test it.

## Summary

This 2023 International Journal of Oral Implantology review by Shehabeldin, Saleh, Tseng, Sirinirund, Zalucha, Chan and Wang treats **intrasocket reactive tissue (IRT)** — the reactive tissue that forms or persists inside an extraction socket — as a clinical decision variable rather than a contaminant to be cleared by default. The authors summarise the histopathological and biological background of IRT and set out the mechanisms through which *residual* IRT can influence socket healing in either direction. From that basis they overview the hand and rotary instruments currently used for IRT debridement, then argue the opposite case: that IRT can be deliberately preserved and used as a **socket sealing material**, with benefits for the healing site. Clinical cases illustrating both routes — removal and preservation, each performed after extraction and before ARP — close the argument. The abstract reports no quantitative data whatsoever, and the authors state explicitly that future studies are needed to test the suggested beneficial effects of IRT on socket healing outcomes.

## Key Contributions

- Turns IRT into an **explicit decision node** in the post-extraction workflow: the question posed is not "how thoroughly do we debride?" but "should this tissue stay or go?"
- Provides the **histopathological/biological account** of IRT's dual potential (positive or negative effect on healing), which the cohort-level RST pages in this wiki do not supply.
- Consolidates the **instrument families** used for IRT debridement — hand instruments and rotary instruments — as the practical arm of the removal option.
- Proposes **IRT as an autologous socket seal**, extending the socket-seal question beyond graft/matrix substitutes to the patient's own tissue.
- Situates the decision **inside ARP**: both strategies are illustrated as steps performed between extraction and ridge preservation, which is where the choice actually has consequences.

Narrative review — the abstract describes no protocol, so none is reported here:
- **Design**: expert narrative synthesis of "current knowledge," explicitly structured as (1) histopathology/biology of IRT, (2) mechanisms of positive vs negative effect of residual IRT, (3) overview of debridement instruments, (4) IRT as socket sealing material plus clinical cases of removal or preservation before ARP.
- **Search**: no databases, date range, or search string stated in the abstract.
- **Study selection**: no eligibility criteria and no included-study count stated.
- **Appraisal**: no risk-of-bias, certainty, or quality instrument stated.
- **Synthesis**: qualitative/narrative; no pooling, no meta-analysis.
- **Case material**: cases are referenced but the abstract gives no patient count, site, follow-up, or outcome metric.
