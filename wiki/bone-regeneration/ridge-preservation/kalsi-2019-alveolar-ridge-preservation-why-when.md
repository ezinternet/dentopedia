## Three-line Summary
Narrative CPD review (British Dental Journal 2019;227(4):264–274) by UK restorative-dentistry consultants, synthesizing ten systematic reviews published since 2009 on alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) case selection, materials, and technique.
ARP with xenograft or allograft preserves roughly 2 mm more alveolar bone height and width than unassisted extraction healing (Cochrane: +2.6 mm/+1.97 mm xenograft, +2.2 mm/+1.97 mm allograft), but 15–36% residual graft particles remain at a mean of 5.6 months and complete ridge preservation is never achieved.
No consensus exists on case selection, technique, or material choice; the authors propose a socket-type-based decision tree and a specific surgical protocol (no saline irrigation, atraumatic extraction, no routine antibiotics, sutures out at 7–10 days).

## Summary
This British Dental Journal CPD paper synthesizes the alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) evidence base — ten systematic reviews published since 2009 — into practical clinical guidance for general and restorative dentists. It reproduces Salama & Salama's socket-type classification and Jung et al.'s (2018) treatment-modality decision framework to structure case selection, and anchors its quantitative claims on the Atieh et al. (2015) Cochrane review (ARP with xenograft/allograft preserves ~2 mm more bone height and width than extraction alone). The paper is explicit that no consensus exists on case selection, surgical technique, or material choice among the ten reviews it draws on, and that even successful ARP leaves 15–36% residual graft particles and cannot achieve complete ridge preservation. Its main original contribution is a concrete, citable surgical protocol (atraumatic extraction, no saline irrigation — 3.5× alveolar osteitis risk — no routine antibiotics, suture removal at 7–10 days) and a decision-tree flowchart integrating immediate/early implant feasibility with the ARP-or-not decision.

## Key Contributions
- Consolidates ten systematic reviews (2009–2018) into a single clinical reference, explicit that "the literature does not support the use of one technique, or material, as superior"
- Reproduces Jung et al.'s (2018) treatment-modality framework (soft tissue / hard+soft tissue / hard tissue preservation) and Salama & Salama's socket-type classification (Type I–III) as a unified indications table
- Publishes a decision-tree flowchart (Fig. 3) combining immediate/early implant feasibility, aesthetic pontic-site status, and long-term implant planning into the ARP-or-not decision
- States a citable, evidence-backed technical warning against socket saline irrigation (3.5× increased alveolar osteitis risk) and against routine antibiotic prescription for ARP
- Tabulates graft-material trade-offs (autograft/allograft/xenograft/alloplast, resorbable/non-resorbable membranes, growth factors) including a four-material comparative summary (Jambhekar et al. 2015)

## Methodology
Narrative review, not a systematic review — no stated search strategy, PRISMA flow, or formal quality appraisal of included studies. Synthesizes ten prior systematic reviews (references 1, 3, 7–14) plus supporting primary literature (90 references total). Its central quantitative anchor is the Atieh et al. (2015) Cochrane review, pooling 233 extraction sites in 184 healthy adults across 8 RCTs (7 rated high risk of bias, 1 unclear). Outcomes discussed: alveolar bone height/width change, residual graft particle %, vital bone %, implant survival/success/marginal bone levels, and need for further augmentation at implant placement.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| ARP with xenograft vs extraction alone | +2.6 mm height, +1.97 mm width |
| ARP with allograft vs extraction alone | +2.2 mm height, +1.97 mm width |
| Xenograft vs allograft | No difference (height or width) |
| Untreated bucco-lingual width loss (12 mo) | ~50% (two-thirds within first 3 months) |
| Buccal wall ≤1 mm at extraction | 7.5 mm median vertical loss at 8 weeks |
| Buccal wall >1 mm at extraction | 1.1 mm vertical loss at 8 weeks |
| Residual graft particles (mean 5.6 mo) | 15–36%, material-dependent |
| Saline socket irrigation | 3.5× increased alveolar osteitis incidence |
| Ideal graft particle size | 100–380 μm |
| DBBM resorption rate | ~10%/year |
