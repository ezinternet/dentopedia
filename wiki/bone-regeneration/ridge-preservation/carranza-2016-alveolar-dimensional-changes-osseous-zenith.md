## Three-line Summary

Prospective CT study of 20 patients after minimally traumatic single-rooted extraction with preserved walls and primary closure, tracking the "osseous zenith" (bone crest apical to the gingival zenith) from <24 h to 6 months with a radiopaque-marker guide.

The osseous zenith displaced significantly (p<0.001): vertical 2.1 ± 1.7 mm (median 1.9, range 0.2–7.5), horizontal 1.8 ± 0.8 mm (median 1.7, range 0.6–4.4); crestal facial width did not predict displacement, but mid-level facial bone width <0.7 mm predicted >3 mm displacement.

Mid-root-level facial bone width may be the more useful CT predictor of zenith-supporting bone loss before implant planning — small n, abstract-only.

## Summary

The gingival zenith is held up by bone, and this study followed that bone — the "osseous zenith" — for six months after careful extraction. On average it dropped about 2 mm and moved about 2 mm horizontally, but the spread was large (up to 7.5 mm vertically). Facial bone width at the crest did not predict who would lose the most; facial width at the mid-level of the socket did, with <0.7 mm flagging displacement beyond 3 mm. For implant planning this means the CT slice to read for zenith risk is the mid-socket facial wall, not the crest.

## Key Contributions

- "Osseous zenith" landmark beneath the gingival zenith.
- 6-month zenith-bone displacement quantified after atraumatic extraction + primary closure.
- Mid-level facial width <0.7 mm → >3 mm displacement.

## Methodology

- 20 single-rooted extractions, intact walls, primary closure.
- CT at <24 h and 6 months with a marker-bearing radiographic guide.
- Correlation of displacement with anatomical variables.

## Results

| 6-month displacement | Mean ± SD | Median | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vertical | 2.1 ± 1.7 mm | 1.9 mm | 0.2–7.5 mm |
| Horizontal | 1.8 ± 0.8 mm | 1.7 mm | 0.6–4.4 mm |

- p<0.001 for both; crestal facial width not correlated; mid-level facial width <0.7 mm → >3 mm displacement.
