## 한줄요약

## One-line Summary
SR+MA of 22 RCTs (n=816) finds no significant overall benefit of ARP on histological new bone formation vs spontaneous healing (MD −5.86%, p=0.15), but PRF/PRP confers +16.28% (p<0.0001), xenograft+membrane yields −22.47% (p=0.004), and long-term sensitivity analysis reverses the trend in favor of ARP.

## Summary
This 2025 SR+MA from Aristotle University of Thessaloniki synthesizes 22 RCTs (816 patients; 505 ARP / 311 spontaneous healing) on biopsy-confirmed histomorphometric outcomes after socket preservation. The primary analysis found no statistically significant difference in new bone formation between ARP and spontaneous healing (MD −5.86%, 95% CI −13.84 to 2.11%, p=0.15, I²=98%), with extreme heterogeneity limiting interpretation. Subgroup analysis revealed that autologous platelet concentrates (PRF/PRP) significantly improved new bone formation (+16.28%, p<0.0001), while xenograft+collagen membrane reduced it (−22.47%, p=0.004). Mean residual graft content across studies was 20.49%, highlighting the dimensional stability vs bone turnover trade-off inherent to slow-resorbing materials. A sensitivity analysis restricted to studies with >3-month biopsy timing yielded a significant pro-ARP result (MD −16.30%, p<0.00001, I²=51%), suggesting early biopsy timing inflates apparent equivalence. GRADE certainty: moderate (downgraded for risk of bias and inconsistency; no publication bias detected, Egger's p=0.576).

## Key Contributions
- Largest histomorphometric RCT-only SR+MA to date: 22 RCTs, 816 patients
- Resolves paradox: material selection, not ARP per se, determines histological benefit — PRF/PRP superior, xenograft+membrane inferior to spontaneous healing
- Quantifies residual graft burden: 20.49% mean content occupies socket at biopsy, limiting vital bone fraction
- Sensitivity analysis on biopsy timing (>3 mo) substantially improves pro-ARP signal, arguing for delayed implant placement protocols
- GRADE moderate certainty supports cautious clinical guidance — ARP is not universally histologically superior to extraction alone

## Methodology
- Design: SR+MA, PRISMA 2020; RoB 2 for risk of bias; GRADE for certainty
- Databases: MEDLINE, Scopus, Cochrane CENTRAL, grey literature
- Eligibility: RCTs; ARP vs spontaneous healing; biopsy at ≥8 weeks post-extraction; histomorphometry (NBF%, CT%, residual graft%)
- n=22 RCTs; 816 patients (505 ARP / 311 control)
- Statistical model: random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird); heterogeneity via I², τ², Q-statistic
- Subgroups: graft type (xenograft, allograft, alloplast, PRF/PRP); membrane use (yes/no)
- Outlier detection: DFFITS, Cook's Distance; influence analysis
- Meta-regression: graft material category + membrane as moderators; pseudo-R²=5.4%
- Publication bias: Egger's test p=0.576 (no significant bias)
- PROSPERO: CRD42025101953

## Results

**New Bone Formation (primary)**
| Analysis | MD (%) | 95% CI | p | I² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overall | −5.86 | −13.84 to 2.11 | 0.15 (NS) | 98% |
| PRF/PRP subgroup | +16.28 | 7.53–25.03 | <0.0001 | 97% |
| Xenograft + collagen membrane | −22.47 | −33.59 to −11.34 | 0.004 | 57% |
| >3 mo sensitivity | −16.30 | −21.61 to −10.98 | <0.00001 | 51% |

Negative MD = spontaneous healing has more new bone; positive = ARP has more.

**Connective Tissue (secondary)**
- MD +1.30% (95% CI −3.55 to 6.14%, p=0.61, I²=90%) — not significant

**Residual Graft Material (secondary)**
- Pooled mean 20.49% (95% CI 16.78–24.20%, I²=88.99%)
- Alloplast+membrane: lowest residual (β=−16.34%, p=0.066 trend)
- Xenograft without membrane: highest persistence (β=+9.31%, p=0.177 trend)
