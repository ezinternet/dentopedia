## Summary
This narrative review from Gangneung-Wonju National University (Korea) traces the evolution of alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) across four material families — allograft/xenograft/synthetic bone substitutes, platelet concentrates (PRP/PRF/A-PRF/C-PRF/CGF), collagen membranes, and bioactive or injectable-hydrogel systems (e.g., an MSC/endothelial-cell-loaded photocurable hydrogel) — and across techniques from conventional grafting to minimally invasive injectable delivery. It aggregates clinical and patient-centered outcomes rarely gathered in one place: implant survival ranging 90.5–100% across cited long-term cohorts, a 1-year aesthetic VAS of 94/100, suture-removal pain VAS of 17/100, and a cost-effectiveness analysis showing barrier-membrane investment ($12–$189) — but not graft-material spend ($46.2–$140) — correlates with reduced ridge resorption. As a non-systematic narrative review with no stated selection criteria, it cannot weigh conflicting evidence it cites; notably, its own body text concedes ARP shows no significant difference vs. unassisted healing on implant loss/success in one cited study, a caveat not carried into its uniformly positive abstract/conclusion.

## Key Contributions
- Four-family taxonomy of ARP materials (bone substitutes / platelet concentrates / collagen membranes / bioactive & injectable materials) with representative primary studies for each.
- Consolidated patient-reported outcome benchmarks: VAS pain at suture removal 17 (SD 11, range 5–40); VAS aesthetic satisfaction at 1 year 94 (SD 6, range 85–100).
- Consolidated cost-effectiveness picture: graft substitutes $46.2–$140, sealing materials $12–$189; membrane (not graft) spend correlates with reduced resorption; DFDB-based ARP costs only 22.4% of iliac-crest-autograft-based ARP.
- Long-term implant survival synthesis across independent cohorts (90.5%, 93.7%, 100%) spanning 3–84 months of follow-up.

## Methodology
Narrative review — no PRISMA workflow, no stated database list, no explicit inclusion/exclusion or quality-appraisal criteria. Organized into five sections: (I) materials, (II) techniques, (III) clinical/histomorphometric outcomes, (IV) patient-centered outcomes including cost-effectiveness, (V) limitations. Synthesizes roughly 70 cited primary studies and prior systematic reviews rather than performing original search/selection/pooling.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Implant survival, 5–7 yr cohort | 100% |
| Implant survival, 108-patient/308-implant cohort | 93.7% (patient-level); MBL ≈2.2 mm at 5 yr |
| Implant survival, 288-patient ARP+immediate-implant cohort | 90.5% uncomplicated (9.5% complication rate) |
| Additional augmentation needed | 11.5% of ARP sites vs. 48.1% of non-grafted sites |
| Bone % at 3 mo (allograft) vs. 5 mo (xenograft), one cited SR | 54.4% vs. 23.6% |
| Pain VAS at suture removal | 17 (SD 11, range 5–40) |
| Aesthetic VAS at 1 year | 94 (SD 6, range 85–100) |
| Bone graft substitute cost | US$46.2–$140 |
| Socket sealing material cost | US$12–$189 |
| DFDB vs. iliac-crest autograft total treatment cost | 22.4% |
