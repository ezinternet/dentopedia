## Three-line Summary
Three-arm RCT (Naples, n=40 subjects/posterior teeth or roots candidate for extraction, sockets with intact alveolar bone walls) comparing bovine-derived xenograft with 10% collagen + collagen membrane (test) vs. bovine-derived xenograft particles + collagen membrane (control 1) vs. spontaneous healing / no grafting (control 2), 6-month follow-up.

All three groups showed statistically significant vertical and horizontal bone resorption from baseline to 6 months (p<0.001), except vertical resorption in the spontaneous-healing group (control 2), which was not significant; at 6 months, intergroup comparison showed no statistically significant difference among the three groups (p>0.05).

In posterior sites with intact bone walls and adequate buccal bone thickness, ridge preservation with either xenograft protocol failed to show a clinically relevant dimensional advantage over spontaneous healing — suggesting ARP's benefit may be conditional on wall/socket compromise rather than a universal posterior-site default.

## Summary
This 3-arm RCT from the University of Naples Federico II (40 subjects, 40 posterior teeth/roots) tested whether alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) still confers a dimensional benefit when the usual indication — a compromised or dehisced socket wall — is absent. Sites were restricted to posterior sockets with **intact alveolar bone walls** and randomized to: bovine-derived xenograft with 10% collagen + collagen membrane (test), bovine-derived xenograft particles + collagen membrane (control 1), or no grafting/spontaneous healing (control 2). All groups lost significant vertical and horizontal bone dimension from baseline to 6 months (p<0.001) except vertical loss in the no-graft group, but none of the three arms differed significantly from another at 6 months (p>0.05). The authors conclude that in posterior sites with adequate wall integrity and buccal bone thickness, ARP does not add a clinically relevant dimensional benefit over spontaneous healing — an indication-narrowing finding that stands in tension with other ARP-vs-spontaneous-healing evidence obtained in less wall-restricted populations.

## Key Contributions
- Isolates the ARP-vs-spontaneous-healing question specifically in posterior sockets with intact bone walls, rather than pooling with the more commonly studied compromised/dehisced-wall population.
- Head-to-head test of two xenograft formulations (10%-collagen-enriched vs. plain particulate) against a true no-graft control.
- Clinical-relevance conclusion explicitly cautions against routine posterior-site ARP when wall integrity and buccal bone thickness are already adequate.

## Methodology
3-arm parallel RCT, Naples, Italy; n=40 subjects/40 posterior teeth or roots candidate for extraction, all with intact alveolar bone walls at baseline. Test: bovine-derived xenograft + 10% collagen + collagen membrane. Control 1: bovine-derived xenograft particles + collagen membrane. Control 2: no grafting (spontaneous healing). Outcome: vertical and horizontal bone dimensional change, baseline vs. 6 months. (Abstract-only retrieval — per-arm n, exact mm values, and table data were not accessible; paywalled full text.)

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Vertical resorption, baseline→6mo (test, control 1) | Significant, p<0.001 |
| Vertical resorption, baseline→6mo (control 2 / spontaneous healing) | Not significant (sole exception) |
| Horizontal resorption, baseline→6mo (all 3 groups) | Significant, p<0.001 |

## Related Papers
- [[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/de-angelis-2022-ridge-preservation-spontaneous-healing-volumetric]] — retrospective ARP-vs-spontaneous-healing comparison found ARP significantly reduced horizontal/volumetric resorption (0.86 vs 2.03 mm HW-1, p<0.001); this RCT's intact-wall-restricted population narrows/qualifies when that benefit does and doesn't appear (refines — flagged tension, see note below).
- [[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/kim-2024-collagenated-xenograft-arp-rct]] — RCT of a similarly collagen-enriched xenograft (DBBM-C) vs. spontaneous healing found a late-phase volume-maintenance benefit; same evidence tier (RCT) as this paper but a less wall-restrictive inclusion criterion (>2-wall bone only), and reaches the opposite conclusion (refines — flagged tension).
- [[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/scheyer-2016-alveolar-ridge-preservation-multicentre-rct]] — RCT in posterior sockets with substantial buccal **dehiscence** comparing graft-vs-graft (not vs. spontaneous healing); complementary population to this paper's intact-wall cohort — together the two studies bracket when posterior-site grafting does and doesn't add value (extends).

> **Note on flagged tension.** This paper's null result (ARP = spontaneous healing when walls are intact) sits in direct tension with de-angelis-2022 and kim-2024, both of which found ARP superior to spontaneous healing. The most likely explanation is population selection — this trial's intact-wall inclusion criterion may exclude exactly the sockets (dehisced/thin walls) where ARP's benefit is concentrated — which is why the edges above are typed `refines` rather than `contradicts`. This is a judgment call flagged for a second look rather than a settled classification.
