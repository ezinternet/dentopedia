## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 10 human studies (MEDLINE/EMBASE, 2009–2019) comparing 6 extraction socket classification systems and their indications for socket preservation/augmentation.
No single existing classification captures all relevant morphological parameters; most studies showed moderate/unclear risk of bias.
A novel 2-branch clinical decision tree (aesthetic vs. non-aesthetic zone) was proposed, extending indications beyond socket morphology to tissue anatomy and implant placement timing.

## 세줄요약

MEDLINE/EMBASE 검색(2009–2019) 10편 체계적 고찰 — 6가지 발치와 분류체계(Extraction Socket Classification)와 소켓 보존술(Socket Preservation) 적응증 비교.
어떤 단일 분류체계도 치조제(Alveolar Ridge)의 경조직·연조직·방사선 변수를 모두 포괄하지 못함; 대부분 편향 위험 중간~불명확.
심미 영역·비심미 영역을 구분한 신규 의사결정 트리(Clinical Decision Tree) 제안 — 소켓 형태 외 치은 생형(Gingival Biotype), 각화치은(Keratinized Gingiva, KG) 폭, 임플란트 시기(Implant Placement Timing)를 반영.

## Summary

This systematic review (PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD42019136314) searched MEDLINE and EMBASE for human studies from January 2009 to May 2019, identifying 10 eligible studies. Six extraction socket classification systems were reviewed: Caplanis 2005 (hard + soft tissue walls), Elian 2007 (buccal bone + facial soft tissue), Juodzbalys 2008 (8 hard + 6 soft tissue parameters), Smith & Tarnow 2013 (septal bone for immediate implants), Chu 2015 (buccal plate + defect subclassification), and El Chaar 2016 (buccal plate loss + interproximal + apical topography). No single system incorporates all morphological parameters simultaneously. The authors propose a 2-branch decision tree: the aesthetic zone branch weighs gingival biotype and buccal bone plate integrity to recommend augmentation or connective tissue grafting; the non-aesthetic zone branch routes by residual bone volume. The review emphasizes that post-extraction bone loss is rapid (29–63% horizontal, 11–22% vertical within 6 months) and that indications extend beyond morphology to include systemic risk and parafunctional habits.

## Key Contributions

- Comprehensive head-to-head comparison of 6 socket classification systems (2004–2016)
- Demonstrates gap: no existing system captures hard tissue + soft tissue + radiographic parameters together
- Proposes first decision-tree framework differentiating aesthetic from non-aesthetic zones for preservation/augmentation decisions
- Quantifies post-extraction dimensional loss: horizontal 29–63%, vertical 11–22% within 6 months; ~50% width reduction in first year
- Clarifies that indications for socket preservation extend beyond socket morphology to gingival biotype, keratinized tissue width, implant timing, and patient risk factors

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review (PRISMA)
- **Databases**: MEDLINE (PubMed) + EMBASE
- **Search period**: Jan 2009 – May 2019; 553 initial hits → 10 included
- **Inclusion**: Human prospective/cohort/RCT/SR studies evaluating socket classification or preservation/augmentation indications in English
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane 7-domain tool; most studies moderate/unclear; meta-analysis not possible (heterogeneity)
- **PROSPERO**: CRD42019136314

## Results

### Extraction Socket Classification Systems

| System | Year | Key Parameters | Zone Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caplanis | 2005 | Socket walls, bone loss, DEJ distance; biotype | General |
| Elian | 2007 | Buccal bone level; facial STL | Aesthetic |
| Juodzbalys | 2008 | 8 hard + 6 soft tissue parameters | Comprehensive |
| Smith & Tarnow | 2013 | Septal bone coverage | Molar/IIP |
| Chu | 2015 | Buccal plate level + defect | Aesthetic |
| El Chaar | 2016 | Buccal plate loss, apical topography, interproximal | General |

### Decision Tree — Key Branches

**Aesthetic Zone:** thin biotype or buccal plate loss → socket augmentation ± CTG; intact plate + adequate soft tissue → preservation only

**Non-Aesthetic Zone:** compromised socket walls → augmentation; adequate bone volume → observation or preservation graft

### Post-Extraction Bone Changes

- Width: ~50% reduction in first year
- Horizontal: 29–63% loss at 6 months
- Vertical: 11–22% loss at 6 months
- Most height loss within first 90 days
