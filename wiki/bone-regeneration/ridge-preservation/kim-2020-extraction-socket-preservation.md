## Three-line Summary

Editor's-opinion narrative review (J Korean Assoc Oral Maxillofac Surg 2020;46:435-439, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital OMFS) covering extraction socket preservation (ESP) indications, graft materials, socket sealing, and timing of implant placement.

Post-extraction ridge width drops about 50% within a year (5-7 mm, two-thirds in the first 3 months); ESP reduces but does not prevent that resorption, does not change implant success or marginal bone loss, and implants are recommended at least 4 months after ESP.

ESP is a selective procedure — indicated for esthetic sites, severe residual wall destruction and delayed implant placement — with buccal wall thickness ≤1 mm as the key trigger, no consensus standard protocol, and primary closure not required.

## 세줄요약

종설 (Editor's Opinion, 대한구강악안면외과학회지 2020;46:435-439, 분당서울대 구강악안면외과): 발치와 보존술 (Extraction Socket Preservation, ESP)의 적응증·이식재·소켓 밀봉·임플란트 식립 시기를 정리한 종설.

발치 후 1년 내 치조제 폭 약 50% 감소 (평균 5-7 mm, 2/3가 첫 3개월 내); ESP는 흡수를 줄이지만 막지 못하고 임플란트 성공률·변연골소실 (Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)을 바꾸지 않으며, 식립은 ESP 후 최소 4개월 뒤 권장.

ESP는 심미 부위·잔존 골벽 심한 결손·지연 식립 예정 시 선택적으로 시행 — 협측 골벽 두께 (Buccal Bone Wall Thickness, BBWT) ≤1 mm가 핵심 트리거이고, 표준 프로토콜 합의는 없으며 1차 봉쇄 (primary closure)는 필수가 아님.

## Summary

This is an editor's-opinion narrative review, not a systematic review, and it reads as a decision primer rather than an evidence appraisal. It opens with the magnitude of post-extraction disuse atrophy, then sets out the positive and negative camps on ESP side by side before walking through technique, graft materials by origin, socket-sealing materials, and implant-placement timing. The practical payload is a four-class socket defect classification that drives the "should I graft this socket" decision, and a seven-point summary whose most quoted lines are that ESP does not affect implant success rate or marginal bone loss and that implants should be placed about 4 months after grafting. Diagnosis of the underlying problem — an unresorbed, non-standardized procedure landscape — is stated rather than resolved: the authors close by noting that no consensus exists on a standard ESP protocol.

## Key Contributions

- Quantifies the problem: ~50% ridge width reduction within 1 year, 5-7 mm average loss, two-thirds inside the first 3 months; maxillary buccal resorption is worse in molars, mandibular buccal loss exceeds lingual.
- Supplies an operational socket classification (Class I-IV) that maps defect morphology to the need for ESP.
- States the conservative clinical bottom line for Korean OMFS practice: ESP is not for every socket; it reduces but cannot prevent resorption; it does not change implant success or MBL.
- Names buccal bone wall thickness as the dominant prognostic factor, with ESP recommended at ≤1 mm using slowly resorbing substitutes (DBBM, alloplast).
- Gives a material-selection menu across autogenous, xenogeneic, synthetic, allogeneic and growth-factor options, plus five socket-sealing strategies.
- Fixes a timing rule: ~4 months after ESP, with primary stability taken from basal bone rather than the graft.

## Methodology

Narrative literature review organized in six parts: (1) the ESP controversy — positive view versus negative view; (2) surgical technique; (3) bone graft materials by origin; (4) socket sealing; (5) timing of implant placement after ESP; (6) seven-point summary. No search strategy, no eligibility criteria, no risk-of-bias assessment and no meta-analysis are reported; quantitative claims are taken from previously published SRs and RCTs (Van der Weijden 2009; Hämmerle 2012; Avila-Ortiz 2014; Artzi 2000-2001; Lee 2018).

## Results

**Dimensional change after extraction**

| Source cited | Finding |
|---|---|
| Schropp 2003 (12-month prospective) | ~50% ridge width reduction within 1 year; 5-7 mm average; two-thirds in first 3 months |
| Van der Weijden 2009 (SR) | ~3.87 mm horizontal / 1.67 mm vertical bone loss in the first 3 months |
| Hämmerle 2012 (multicentre RCT) | >60% of total resorption within the first 6 months |

**ESP effect size (Avila-Ortiz et al., cited)**

| Outcome vs simple extraction | Difference favouring ESP |
|---|---|
| Horizontal resorption | 1.89 mm less |
| Labial vertical resorption | 2.07 mm less |
| Lingual vertical resorption | 1.18 mm less |

Effect was most pronounced in the maxilla, where ESP in the posterior region can minimize or avoid sinus floor elevation and permit flapless implant surgery.

**Socket classification (Table 1)**

| Class | Description |
|---|---|
| I | 4-wall defect, intact bony housing |
| II | 3-wall defect, 1 wall with dehiscence/fenestration |
| III | Type 1: adequate height, inadequate width; Type 2: 2 intact walls |
| IV | 1-wall defect, inadequate vertical height and horizontal width |

**Indications for ESP:** esthetic concern; severe destruction of residual bone walls after extraction; planned delayed implant treatment. Absolute necessity is denied — "ESP is not required in all extraction cases."

**Graft materials**

| Class | Content |
|---|---|
| Autogenous | Reported (buccal bone of maxillary canine, rotated palatal flap) but limited by resorption risk |
| Xenogeneic | PBBM/DBBM — 82.3% socket fill with new bone at 9 months (Artzi); DPBM comparable to DBBM (Lee 2018) |
| Synthetic | HA, BCP, bioactive glass, calcium sulfate, Bioplant HTR |
| Allogeneic | FDBA, putty/gel forms miscible with particulate xeno/synthetic graft |
| Growth factors | rhBMP-2, PRP, PRF, P-15, VEGF |

**Socket sealing:** autogenous free gingival graft/connective tissue (since 1997); acellular dermal matrix over resorbable HA; resorbable collagen membrane (BCP + cross-linked collagen tolerated early exposure); non-resorbable dPTFE; collagen sponge (Bio-Col, packing graft into 1/2-2/3 of socket).

**Timing:** histology shows new bone growing from the recipient bed only up to the lower third of the defect; upper portions need a long healing period, and larger defects need longer. Recommendation: implant placement after approximately 4 months, with primary stability from basal bone.

## Limitations

Editor's opinion, not a systematic review: no reproducible search, no risk-of-bias appraisal, no quantitative synthesis, and no explicit evidence grading for the cited figures. The dimensional-change numbers are borrowed wholesale from earlier reviews and RCTs. No new clinical data are presented. Single-institution authorship with no stated funding or conflict of interest beyond the declaration.
