## Summary
This experimental study in dogs evaluated Bio-Oss collagen for ridge preservation using a novel extraction socket model with a very thin buccal bone wall. At 12 weeks post-extraction, the Bio-Oss collagen group demonstrated greater alveolar ridge width preservation and bone volume compared to untreated sockets on CBCT, though the original buccal bone wall was lost in both groups. Histological analysis showed differences in tissue composition between groups.

## Key Contributions
- Novel socket model mimicking clinically challenging thin buccal wall scenario
- Demonstrated efficacy of Bio-Oss collagen in preserving ridge dimensions under compromised buccal bone conditions
- Combined CBCT and histomorphometric evaluation

## Methodology
Experimental dog study comparing Bio-Oss collagen-filled sockets vs untreated controls in a novel thin-buccal-wall extraction socket model. Outcomes assessed at 12 weeks via CBCT (ridge width/volume) and histology (mineralized bone, bone marrow, other tissues).

## Results

|---|---|
