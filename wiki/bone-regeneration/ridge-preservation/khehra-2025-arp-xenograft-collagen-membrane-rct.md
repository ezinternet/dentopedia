## Three-line Summary

6-month parallel-arm RCT (n=20, post-extraction ARP sites with thick buccal bone, Harvard HSDM) randomizing patients to collagenated bovine xenograft (BG) alone vs BG plus non-crosslinked collagen membrane (MEM), both healed by secondary intention, assessed by CBCT, ultrasound, and biopsy.
All measured endpoints — keratinized tissue width (4.80 vs 4.80 mm, p=0.999), horizontal bone width at 3 mm (9.02 vs 8.41 mm, p=0.464), vertical bone change (Δ-0.78 vs Δ-1.31 mm, p=0.244), and histomorphometric new bone formation (25.8% vs 27.9%, p=0.803) — were statistically equivalent.
In ARP with thick buccal bone, the non-crosslinked collagen membrane adds no measurable benefit to the collagenated graft, supporting a simplified single-material protocol.

## Summary

This Harvard RCT tested a clinically common but under-studied question: when a collagenated bovine bone xenograft plug (which inherently contains a collagen matrix) is used for ARP, does adding a separate non-crosslinked collagen membrane on top provide any additional benefit? Twenty patients underwent post-extraction ARP and were randomized to graft alone (G1) or graft + membrane (G2), with secondary-intention wound closure in both groups. At 6 months, there were no statistically significant differences between groups on any outcome — KTW, gingival thickness, horizontal bone width, vertical bone change, or histomorphometric new bone percentage. Ultrasound findings corroborated the CBCT and clinical data. The authors conclude that the collagen component inherent to the collagenated graft may be sufficient to serve as a biologic barrier in sites where the buccal bone wall is intact and reasonably thick (~1 mm), making the additional membrane redundant.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that **non-crosslinked collagen membrane is dispensable** when DBBM-C with inherent collagen is used for ARP in thick-buccal-bone sites
- Employs **ultrasound as objective supplemental measurement** alongside CBCT — validates concordance between imaging modalities in ARP assessment
- Provides histomorphometric data (~25-28% new bone) confirming equivalent biological regeneration between protocols
- Proposes that collagenated graft's own collagen matrix may function as an adequate biologic barrier — reducing treatment steps and costs

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Study type | Parallel-arm RCT, single center |
| n | 20 participants (17 premolars, 1 canine, 2 incisors) |
| Groups | G1: BG alone (n=10); G2: BG + MEM (n=10) |
| Mean age | 54.25 ± 17.08 years |
| Baseline buccal bone | G1: 1.02 ± 0.44 mm; G2: 0.93 ± 0.45 mm (NS) |
| Wound closure | Secondary intention (both groups) |
| Follow-up | 6 months |
| Assessments | CBCT, ultrasonography, clinical measurements, histomorphometry |
| Institution | Harvard School of Dental Medicine |
| Trial registration | ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05838651 |

## Results

| Endpoint | BG alone | BG + MEM | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| KTW (mm) | 4.80 ± 1.48 | 4.80 ± 1.14 | 0.999 |
| Gingival thickness (mm) | 1.22 ± 0.25 | 1.20 ± 0.35 | 0.650 |
| Horizontal bone width at 3 mm (mm) | 9.02 ± 2.09 | 8.41 ± 1.45 | 0.464 |
| Vertical bone change — buccal (mm) | Δ-0.78 ± 0.59 | Δ-1.31 ± 1.20 | 0.244 |
| New bone formation (%) | 25.80 ± 15.48 | 27.89 ± 18.21 | 0.803 |

All results NS. Ultrasound evaluations consistent with clinical and radiographic measurements throughout.

## Limitations

- Small sample (n=10/group) — underpowered for detecting small effect sizes
- Sites selected had **thick buccal bone** (~1 mm) — conclusions limited to this morphology; thin-wall or fenestrated sites may behave differently
- Abstract-only ingest — specific graft product, membrane brand (likely Bio-Gide or equivalent), and detailed exclusion criteria not confirmed
- 6-month follow-up only — long-term stability beyond remodeling phase not assessed
- Secondary-intention healing protocol differs from primary closure ARP standard — may limit generalizability
