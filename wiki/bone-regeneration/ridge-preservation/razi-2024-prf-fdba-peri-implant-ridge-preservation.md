## Three-line Summary

RCT (n=60, 30 PRF vs 30 FDBA), single-tooth extraction sockets requiring alveolar ridge preservation, 6-month follow-up with clinical and CBCT measurements.

PRF group gained more keratinized tissue width at 6 months (4.0±0.7mm vs 3.5±0.6mm, P=0.036); FDBA group better preserved ridge height (−1.2±0.3mm vs −1.6±0.4mm, P=0.025) and ridge width (−1.8±0.4mm vs −2.3±0.5mm, P=0.018), and showed a 30% bone-density increase vs baseline (860 vs 700 HU).

Split-decision outcome — PRF favors soft-tissue quality/keratinized mucosa, FDBA favors hard-tissue dimensional stability and bone density; short-report-style article with sparse methodological detail (no randomization/blinding method reported).

## Summary

This RCT randomized 60 patients (35 M / 25 F, 30–65 y) undergoing single-tooth extraction to either PRF (Group A, n=30, Choukroun-protocol membranes placed in the socket) or FDBA (Group B, n=30, 0.25–1 mm particles + resorbable collagen membrane) for alveolar ridge preservation, with 6-month clinical and CBCT follow-up. The result is a genuine split-decision rather than a clean win for either material: PRF produced significantly greater keratinized tissue width gain by 6 months (a soft-tissue/peri-implant-health-relevant outcome), while FDBA significantly better preserved alveolar ridge height and width on CBCT and increased radiographic bone density by 30% versus baseline. The authors frame this as complementary biology — PRF's angiogenic/growth-factor profile supports vascularized keratinized mucosa formation, while FDBA's osteoconductive scaffold structurally resembles native bone and better resists post-extraction ridge collapse. Note this is a short, supplement-issue report (~3 pages); the soft-tissue-thickness figure highlighted in the abstract (2.5 mm PRF gain) is not shown as a table in the retrieved full text, and methodological detail on randomization/blinding/allocation concealment is minimal.

## Key Contributions

- Direct head-to-head RCT of PRF vs FDBA (not PRF-as-graft-adjunct) for single-tooth ARP, with paired soft-tissue and hard-tissue endpoints in the same cohort.
- Quantifies the trade-off: PRF wins on keratinized tissue width (+4.0 vs +3.5 mm at 6mo, P=0.036); FDBA wins on ridge height (−1.2 vs −1.6 mm loss, P=0.025), ridge width (−1.8 vs −2.3 mm loss, P=0.018), and bone density (+30% vs baseline, 860 vs 700 HU).
- Supports a material-selection framework based on the clinical priority at a given site — soft-tissue quality (esthetic zone, thin biotype) vs hard-tissue volume preservation (posterior sites needing implant bed volume) — rather than a single "best" ARP material.

## Methodology

- Design: 2-arm parallel RCT, single-tooth extraction sockets (maxillary or mandibular), n=60 (30/30).
- Inclusion: single-tooth extraction requiring ARP, good general health, sufficient bone for future implant.
- Exclusion: smoking, diabetes/osteoporosis or other bone-metabolism-impairing systemic disease, drugs altering bone repair.
- Group A (PRF): Choukroun-protocol PRF membranes placed in the socket.
- Group B (FDBA): 0.25–1 mm FDBA particles to bone level, covered with a resorbable collagen membrane.
- Both groups: atraumatic extraction, flap adjusted for primary closure, 4-0 resorbable sutures.
- Outcomes: keratinized tissue width (clinical, baseline/1/3/6 mo); ridge height/width and bone density (CBCT at mid-buccal/mid-lingual/interproximal, baseline/6 mo). SPSS v23 for analysis; randomization/blinding method not detailed in the available text.

## Results

- Keratinized tissue width at 6 months: PRF 4.0±0.7 mm vs FDBA 3.5±0.6 mm (P=0.036); no significant difference at baseline (P=0.687) or 3 months (P=0.084).
- Ridge height loss at 6 months: PRF −1.6±0.4 mm vs FDBA −1.2±0.3 mm (P=0.025).
- Ridge width loss at 6 months: PRF −2.3±0.5 mm vs FDBA −1.8±0.4 mm (P=0.018).
- Bone density: FDBA group +30% vs baseline (P<0.05); FDBA 860 HU vs PRF 700 HU.
- No baseline demographic differences between groups (P>0.05).
