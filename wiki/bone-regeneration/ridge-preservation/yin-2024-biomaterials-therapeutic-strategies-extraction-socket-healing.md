## Summary

This narrative review from Yin et al. (West China Hospital of Stomatology, Sichuan University) addresses a critical gap between socket healing biology and the selection of biomaterials for clinical management. The oral cavity presents a uniquely challenging microenvironment — salivary flow, oral movements, and bacterial biofilms all impede extraction socket healing and contribute to post-extraction complications such as dry socket, delayed ossification, and ridge resorption that compromise subsequent implant placement or prosthetic rehabilitation.

The review grounds biomaterial design in the sequential healing phases (hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, and remodeling) and the cellular-molecular interactions that govern each phase. Building on this biological foundation, it surveys the current landscape of biomaterials — including growth-factor delivery carriers, immunomodulatory scaffolds, and anti-infective materials — and highlights emerging strategies targeting specific molecular pathways. The authors also outline clinical translation challenges (controlled release, biocompatibility, infection risk, regulatory pathways) and future research directions.

## Key Contributions

- Frames extraction socket healing biology (hemostasis → inflammation → proliferation → remodeling) as the rational basis for phase-specific biomaterial design
- Catalogs current biomaterial classes used for socket healing: scaffolds, growth-factor carriers, immunomodulatory agents, antimicrobial materials
- Identifies emerging therapeutic targets and molecular pathways relevant to post-extraction complications and delayed healing
- Highlights innovations in drug-delivery systems and bioinspired scaffolds applicable to alveolar ridge preservation
- Outlines challenges in clinical translation and future directions for biomaterial-enhanced socket management

## Methodology

Narrative review; Journal Article classified as Review (PubMed). No PRISMA protocol or systematic search strategy reported. Breadth-oriented synthesis integrating materials science, cell biology, and oral surgery literature. Institution: State Key Laboratory of Oral Diseases, National Clinical Research Center for Oral Diseases, West China Hospital of Stomatology, Sichuan University, China.

## Results

- The oral microenvironment (salivary flow, bacterial biofilms, oral movements) is identified as a primary driver of delayed socket healing and post-extraction complications
- Biomaterials for socket healing span multiple mechanistic classes: structural scaffolds, growth-factor delivery systems, immunomodulatory materials, and anti-infective agents
- Emerging strategies include targeting the inflammatory phase to modulate macrophage polarization and promote angiogenesis and osteogenesis through biomaterial-mediated signaling
- Clinical application challenges include achieving controlled and localized release kinetics within the complex oral environment, ensuring long-term biocompatibility, and managing infection risk
- The review positions biomaterial selection as integral to both immediate socket healing and longer-term implant site preparation
