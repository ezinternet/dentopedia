## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 14 studies (8 RCTs, 2 CCTs, 5 cohort; 571 molar sites) comparing alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) vs spontaneous healing (SH) for subsequent implant placement at molar sites.

ARP reduced horizontal ridge width loss (ARP: −1.02 to −2.73 mm vs SH: −2.36 to −4.44 mm) and halved the need for advanced augmentation procedures (20.8% ARP vs 47.7% SH), including GBR and sinus floor augmentation.

ARP is clinically justified at molar sites — it limits post-extraction bone resorption and substantially reduces secondary augmentation requirements, with particular benefit in maxillary posterior sites by avoiding lateral-window sinus approaches.

## Summary

This systematic review specifically investigates whether alveolar ridge preservation (ARP) at molar extraction sites — compared to spontaneous healing (SH) — adequately preserves ridge dimensions to facilitate implant placement and reduces the need for advanced bone augmentation procedures. Prior ARP evidence was predominantly derived from non-molar or mixed-site studies, leaving a molar-specific evidence gap.

Fourteen studies met inclusion criteria (8 RCTs, 2 CCTs, 3 prospective cohort, 1 retrospective cohort), totaling 571 molar sites with per-study sample sizes ranging from 20 to 80 sites. All studies required a minimum 3-month follow-up and involved adult patients with molar extractions planned for implant placement.

## Key Contributions

1. **Molar-specific focus**: First SR restricted to molar sites, addressing the gap in ARP literature dominated by non-molar data
2. **Dimensional change quantification**: Provides ranged pooled data for both horizontal and vertical ridge changes at ARP vs SH molar sites
3. **Augmentation burden reduction**: Demonstrates ARP halves the rate of secondary grafting procedures (20.8% vs 47.7%)
4. **Sinus augmentation implications**: ARP at maxillary molar sites reduces likelihood of requiring SFA via lateral window approach — a more morbid procedure
5. **Study heterogeneity acknowledged**: Narrative synthesis without meta-analysis; limits generalizability of specific numerical thresholds

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review (no meta-analysis; narrative synthesis due to heterogeneity)
- **Literature search**: Four electronic databases
- **Inclusion**: RCTs, CCTs, cohort studies; adults; molar extraction + implant plan; ≥3-month follow-up
- **Total studies**: 14 | **Total sites**: 571
- **Primary outcomes**: Horizontal and vertical ridge dimension changes (mm)
- **Secondary outcomes**: Need for GBR, SFA, SFA-lateral window approach; implant-related outcomes
- **Comparator**: ARP vs spontaneous healing (SH)

## Results

### Ridge Dimension Changes

| Dimension | ARP range | Spontaneous Healing range |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal width reduction | −1.02 ± 0.88 to −2.73 ± 1.68 mm | −2.36 ± 0.91 to −4.44 ± 3.71 mm |
| Vertical height reduction | Reduced (trend) | Greater (trend) |

- Horizontal benefit most consistent in **periodontally sound** sockets
- Vertical benefit trend observed irrespective of socket condition

### Advanced Augmentation Procedures

| Procedure type | ARP | SH |
|---|---|---|
| Any advanced grafting (GBR/SFA) | 20.8% | 47.7% |
| SFA via lateral window approach | Reduced | Higher |

### Clinical Conclusion

Within study limits: ARP at molar sites limits post-extraction alveolar bone resorption and reduces the need for additional bone augmentation, including the more invasive lateral-window SFA in maxillary posterior regions.
