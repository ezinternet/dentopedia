## Three-line Summary

Technical note and single case (Vienna, 2021) that provides the structural anatomical explanation for why the labial socket wall loses more bone than the palatal wall after extraction — at the anterior maxilla, the labial bundle bone is **fused with the external cortical plate** with no trabecular bone behind it ("retroalveolar bone" absent), while the palatal side always has a trabecular compartment between its bundle bone and the cortical plate, giving it vascular and cellular resources the labial wall simply lacks.

The paper also introduces a practical intervention: 6–10 palatal/lingual perforations with a <1 mm bur (extending to trabecular bone, 2–3 mm spacing) to open marrow access and trigger Frost's Regional Acceleratory Phenomenon (RAP), analogous to decortication in GBR recipient sites.

The buccal wall is explicitly excluded from perforation: it is ≤1 mm, fused cortex only, with nothing to perforate through.

## 세줄요약

빈 의대 기술보고·단일증례(2021) — 전치부 발치 후 협측 치조제가 구개측보다 더 많이 흡수되는 이유를 구조 해부학으로 최초 설명. 핵심: **협측 다발골 (bundle bone, 치조골 고유층)은 치조돌기 외측 피질판과 융합** — 그 사이 교소골 공간 없음. 구개측/설측은 다발골과 피질판 사이에 후방 치조 교소골("retroalveolar bone")이 항상 존재하여 골수 혈관·줄기세포 자원 보유.

임상 술식 제안: 구개측/설측 다발골에 <1 mm 버로 6-10개 천공(간격 2-3 mm, 교소골까지) → 골수 혈관 접근 개선 + 성장인자 방출 + Frost 지역가속현상 (Regional Acceleratory Phenomenon, RAP) 유도. GBR 수혜부 피질 천공(decortication)과 동일한 원리.

**협측 천공 금지** — 피질판과 융합된 ≤1 mm 구조체라 천공 시 외측 피질 천공 위험.

## Core Anatomical Finding

At the anterior maxilla (incisors, canines), the labial alveolar wall has a **fundamentally different structure** than the palatal wall:

| Feature | Labial | Palatal/Lingual |
|---|---|---|
| Bundle bone | Present, 0.22–0.54 mm | Present |
| External cortical plate | Fused to bundle bone | Separated from bundle bone |
| Retroalveolar trabecular bone | **Absent** | Present |
| Marrow vascularization | None (all cortical) | Available from trabecular compartment |
| After Phase 1 resorption | Entire buccal wall gone | Trabecular bone remains |

This explains why the buccal crest collapses entirely in Araújo's two-phase model: Phase 1 (PDL-loss → bundle bone resorption) removes the **only** bone on the buccal side. There is no trabecular backstop.

## Perforation Technique

**Rationale**: Bundle bone at extraction = cortical barrier analogous to GBR recipient site cortex. As GBR decortication opens marrow access to improve vascularization and growth factor release, perforating the bundle bone should do the same for socket healing.

**Technique** (palatal/lingual only):

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Bur | Round, <1 mm diameter |
| Locations | Palatal + mesial/distal (if no adjacent teeth) |
| Number | 6–10 per socket |
| Spacing | ~2–3 mm |
| Depth | Into trabecular bone (bleeding provocation) |
| Buccal | EXCLUDED — fused with cortex, ≤1 mm |

**Proposed mechanisms**:
1. Improved vascularization from bone marrow
2. Growth factor and cytokine release
3. Frost's Regional Acceleratory Phenomenon (RAP) — local trauma accelerates bone remodeling 2–10× for 3–6 months

The technique can be applied before unassisted socket healing **or** before any ridge preservation procedure.

## Limitations

- Single case report — no histological data, no controls
- Structural asymmetry claim based on older anatomical texts (Sicher & Du Brul) and Tsigarida et al., not measured de novo
- No controlled data showing perforation reduces ridge resorption versus ridge preservation alone
- Preclinical micro-CT + histological trials were in preparation at publication — no follow-up published yet
