## Three-line Summary

Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR) of 23 clinical studies (2015–2024; 9 RCTs, 1 prospective, 2 pilot, 5 case series, 5 retrospective, 1 clinical trial) mapping customized digital (CAD/CAM, 3D-printed) workflows for alveolar bone regeneration/ridge augmentation.

Workflow is CBCT→DICOM→STL→CAD design→3D print; titanium mesh dominates (16/23 studies, mostly grade IV/V, fabricated via DMLS in 9, SLM in 7, or five-axis milling in 5 studies, average thickness ~0.5 mm), with PEEK mesh and 3D-printed alloplastic/hydroxyapatite bone grafts as emerging alternatives; one RCT (Kim et al., n=60) showed higher % bone volume with 3D-printed customized graft vs conventional block graft at 5 months, and another study (Wang et al., n=24) showed customized allogeneic blocks gave greater horizontal bone gain, less resorption, and shorter operative time vs autogenous blocks.

Clinical implication: digital customization improves surgical precision/efficiency and shows promising radiographic/operative-time benefits over conventional membranes and grafts, but the RCT base is still small and protocols heterogeneous — no meta-analysis was possible, so treat specific numeric benefits as preliminary pending protocol standardization.

## Summary

This scoping review systematically maps the digital planning-and-printing landscape for customized bone-regeneration scaffolds (titanium mesh, PEEK mesh, 3D-printed bone graft substitutes) used in alveolar ridge augmentation. Rather than pooling outcome data, its core contribution is a structured process map: CBCT scan → DICOM-to-STL conversion (commonly via Mimics or Btk-3D software) → CAD-based mesh/graft design → 3D printing (direct metal laser sintering, selective laser melting, or five-axis milling) → sterilization (autoclave or glutaraldehyde soak) → surgical fixation (typically 2–3 screws). Across 23 included studies (2015–2024), titanium alloy remained the dominant material (16/23 studies), but PEEK meshes and 3D-printed alloplastic/hydroxyapatite grafts are emerging alternatives. Two individual studies within the review reported outcome comparisons favoring the customized/digital approach: higher bone volume with 3D-printed alloplastic graft vs conventional block graft (Kim et al., n=60), and greater horizontal bone gain with less resorption and shorter operative time using customized allogeneic blocks vs autogenous blocks (Wang et al., n=24). A head-to-head RCT of custom PEEK vs titanium mesh (Mounir et al., n=16) found no statistically significant difference between the two materials.

## Key Contributions

- Documents the concrete, reproducible digital workflow (CBCT/DICOM/STL/CAD/3D-print/sterilize) used across 23 studies, including specific software names, printer technologies, and sterilization protocols — useful as a practical implementation reference beyond outcome-only reviews.
- Quantifies material/technique prevalence: titanium (16/23 studies) >> PEEK or 3D-printed bone substitutes; DMLS (9 studies) and SLM (7 studies) are the dominant additive-manufacturing routes vs five-axis milling (5 studies, subtractive).
- Surfaces specific comparative findings (Kim et al. 3D-printed alloplastic vs block graft; Wang et al. customized allogeneic vs autogenous block; Mounir et al. PEEK vs titanium mesh) that were previously scattered across individual trials.

## Methodology

PRISMA-ScR-guided scoping review with a formal PICO framework (adult BR patients; digital CAD/CAM 3D workflow intervention; conventional preformed/manually-shaped membrane/graft comparison; clinical + radiographic outcomes). Systematic search of MEDLINE/PubMed, Cochrane Library, Scopus, and Web of Science through 27 February 2025, English-language studies 2015–2025, RCTs through retrospective case studies included, in vitro/animal/narrative-review/case-report designs excluded. Two independent reviewers screened via Covidence with third-reviewer arbitration; data independently extracted and verified.

## Results

- 23 studies, 2015–2024 (9 RCT, 1 prospective, 2 pilot, 5 case series, 5 retrospective, 1 clinical trial).
- Common workflow: CBCT → DICOM → STL → 3D visualization/CAD software (Mimics most common, 6 studies; Btk-3D, 5 studies) → mesh/graft design → 3D printing.
- Materials: titanium alloy in 16/23 studies (grade IV or Ti64/grade V); PEEK mesh in 2 studies; 3D-printed bone graft substitutes (alloplastic hydroxyapatite/β-TCP, nanohydroxyapatite, milled allogenic bone) in 3 studies.
- Mesh design: perforated titanium mesh in 16 studies; occlusive titanium mesh in 3; occlusive PEEK mesh in 1; pore size specified (1 mm) in only 2 studies.
- Mesh thickness: 0.1–2 mm range, average ~0.5 mm (unspecified in 7 studies).
- Printing method: DMLS (9 studies), SLM (7 studies), five-axis milling (5 studies, subtractive).
- Sterilization: autoclave 135°C/15–20 min (3 studies), 2.4% glutaraldehyde/12 h (3 studies), remainder unspecified/pre-sterilized.
- Kim et al. RCT (n=60): 3D-printed customized alloplastic bone graft vs conventional block graft for post-extraction GBR — significantly higher % bone volume and smaller tissue surface area at 5 months.
- Wang et al. (n=24): customized allogeneic bone blocks vs autogenous blocks — greater horizontal bone gain, less resorption at 1 mm below crest, shorter operative time at 6 months.
- Mounir et al. RCT (n=16): custom PEEK mesh vs prebent titanium mesh — no statistically significant difference in 3D bone gain % or linear dimensional change.
- Titanium/SLM membrane fabrication was the dominant and best-performing approach overall per the authors' synthesis, though no meta-analysis was performed.
