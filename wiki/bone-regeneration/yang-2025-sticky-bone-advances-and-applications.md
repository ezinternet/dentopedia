## Three-line Summary

A narrative review of "sticky bone" — a composite biomaterial combining autologous fibrin glue (AFG) or injectable-PRF (i-PRF) with particulate bone grafts (xenograft, allograft, alloplast). The fibrin network confers gel-like handling, defect adhesion, and sustained growth-factor release (PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF, IGF), enhancing graft stability and osteogenic cell recruitment. Clinical applications span socket preservation, sinus floor elevation, peri-implant defects, and periodontal intrabony defects, with evidence largely from case series and animal studies; high-level human RCTs remain limited.

## 세줄요약

스티키 본(Sticky Bone) — 자가 피브린 접착제(AFG) 또는 주사형 PRF(i-PRF)와 골 이식재(이종골, 동종골, 합성골)를 결합한 복합 생체재료 — 에 대한 종설 논문. 피브린 네트워크가 겔 같은 조작성, 결손부 부착력, 성장인자(PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF, IGF) 지속 방출을 제공해 이식재 안정성과 골형성 세포 모집을 향상. 치조제 보존, 상악동 거상술, 임플란트 주위 결손, 치주 골내 결손 등 다양한 적용례가 보고되었으나, 고수준 인체 RCT 증거는 아직 제한적임.

## Summary

Sticky bone emerged in 2010 (Sohn et al.) as a fibrin-encapsulated, bio-cured adhesive bone graft network. By mixing autologous fibrin glue (AFG) or injectable platelet-rich fibrin (i-PRF) with bone graft particles, a viscoelastic scaffold forms where individual particles are interconnected through a fibrin matrix. This gel-like material resists disintegration under external forces, maintains the osteogenic space, and provides sustained release of platelet-derived growth factors. The review categorizes preparations into AFG-based (two-component commercial kits), i-PRF-based (chairside, no anticoagulant, "steak" technique), and self-folding i-PRF membrane techniques (Csongé et al. 2023). Biologically, sticky bone exhibits biocompatibility, biodegradability, growth factor delivery (PDGF, TGF-β1, VEGF, IGF-1, BMP-2 over 7–14 days), scaffold mimicry of provisional ECM, and enhanced MSC affinity. Clinically, applications include post-extraction socket preservation, sinus floor elevation (lateral and transcrestal), peri-implant dehiscence GBR, periodontal intrabony and furcation defects, and large alveolar cleft reconstruction. Current evidence is predominantly from animal models, case reports, and small case series; comparative human RCTs against standard GBR or particulate grafts alone are scarce.

## Key Contributions

- First comprehensive narrative review of sticky bone since its 2010 introduction
- Systematic classification of three preparation modalities with procedural nuances
- Mechanistic integration of fibrin biology, growth factor kinetics, and osteoimmunology (M2 macrophage polarization)
- Clinical indication mapping with representative cases across oral surgery
- Identification of evidence gaps: protocol standardization, long-term outcomes, cost-effectiveness, RCT need

## Methodology

- **Study design**: Narrative review (non-systematic)
- **Literature scope**: Sticky bone, platelet concentrates (PRF, i-PRF, CGF, PRP), bone substitutes (xenograft, allograft, alloplast), guided bone regeneration
- **Timeframe**: 2010–2025
- **Evidence sources**: In vitro cell studies, animal models (rabbit calvaria, goat sinus, rat femur), human case reports/series; limited comparative clinical studies
- **No formal search strategy, inclusion criteria, or risk-of-bias assessment reported**

## Results

### Preparation Modalities

| Modality | Fibrin Source | Preparation Notes | Key Reference |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|---------------|
| AFG-based | Commercial autologous fibrin glue kit (fibrinogen + thrombin) | Two-syringe mixing; polymerization in 30–60 s; requires AFG kit | Sohn et al. 2010, 2015 |
| i-PRF-based | Injectable-PRF (low-speed centrifugation, no anticoagulant) | Liquid i-PRF mixed with particulate graft chairside; "steak" concept | Mourão et al. 2015 |
| Self-folding i-PRF | i-PRF membrane folded around graft | Homogeneous cellularity; extended residence; sustained cytokine release | Csongé et al. 2023 |

### Biological Properties

| Property | Mechanism / Evidence |
|----------|---------------------|
| Biocompatibility | Natural fibrin + bone matrix components; minimal foreign body reaction |
| Biodegradability | Fibrinolysis + graft resorption synchronized with new bone formation |
| Growth factor delivery | PDGF, TGF-β1, VEGF, IGF-1, BMP-2 released 7–14 days (platelet α-granules) |
| Scaffold mimicry | 3D fibrin network porosity ~50–200 μm; mimics provisional ECM |
| Cell affinity | MSC attachment ↑, proliferation ↑, Runx2/Osterix/OCN expression ↑ |
| Immunomodulation | Macrophage M2 polarization (IL-10 ↑, TNF-α ↓); anti-inflammatory milieu |

### Clinical Applications (Evidence Level Indicated)

| Indication | Evidence | Notes |
|------------|----------|-------|
| Socket / ridge preservation | Case series, animal histomorphometry | Dimensional preservation comparable to conventional GBR |
| Sinus floor elevation (lateral) | Case reports, small series | Gel handling aids membrane elevation & graft containment |
| Sinus floor elevation (transcrestal) | Case reports | Injectable delivery through osteotome site |
| Peri-implant dehiscence / GBR | Case reports | Defect adhesion reduces graft migration |
| Periodontal intrabony / furcation | Limited case series | Combined with enamel matrix derivative in some reports |
| Large defects / alveolar cleft | Case reports | Volume stability from fibrin cohesion |
