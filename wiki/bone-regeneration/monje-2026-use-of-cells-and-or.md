## Three-line Summary

Systematic review with meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024605421; MEDLINE/EMBASE/Cochrane CENTRAL to 1 Nov 2024, manual screening update 21 Sep 2025) of adjunctive biologics or cell therapy combined with scaffolds in alveolar bone augmentation for implant site development — 56 studies in qualitative synthesis, and 23 RCTs (n = 1078 patients) meta-analyzed: ridge augmentation 6 RCTs (n = 172), ridge preservation 9 RCTs (n = 455), sinus floor elevation 8 RCTs (n = 451).

Biologics gave a statistically significant but small dimensional advantage over scaffold-only controls in ridge augmentation (ridge width WMD = 0.30 mm, p < 0.001, I² = 0%; PRP subset 0.52 mm, p = 0.015, I² = 11%; ridge height not significant, WMD = 0.39 mm, p = 0.155) and in ridge preservation (width WMD = 0.48 mm, p = 0.035, I² = 74%; height WMD = 0.40 mm, p < 0.001, I² = 5%; rhBMP-2 width WMD = 1.08 mm, p = 0.02, I² = 84.6%), with less non-mineralized tissue (p = 0.009), but no dimensional benefit in sinus floor elevation (height WMD = 0.32 mm, p = 0.878, I² = 87%) despite significantly less residual graft (WMD = −5.54, p < 0.001); no major adverse events were reported for biologics or cell therapy.

Effect sizes are sub-millimetre and procedure-specific, heterogeneity reaches I² = 74–98% in several pools, publication bias was detected for ridge-preservation height (Egger p = 0.010) and sinus mineralized tissue (p = 0.022), all participants were systemically healthy, and cell-therapy superiority could not be validated because meta-analysis was infeasible — so the adjunctive biologic is best read as a modest add-on for ridge augmentation and ridge preservation, and as unsupported for sinus floor elevation.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis, SR+MA; PROSPERO CRD42024605421, MEDLINE·EMBASE·Cochrane CENTRAL 2024-11-01까지 + 2025-09-21 수동 갱신): 스캐폴드(scaffold)와 병용한 생체치료제(biologic) 및 세포치료(cell therapy)의 치조골 증강(alveolar bone augmentation) 유효성·안전성 평가 — 정성 종합 56편, 정량 분석 대상 무작위대조시험(Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) 23편(환자 n = 1078; 골증대 6편 n = 172, 치조제 보존 9편 n = 455, 상악동거상 8편 n = 451).

생체치료제 병용군이 스캐폴드 단독 대조군 대비 골증대에서 치조골 폭(ridge width, RW) 유의한 소폭 우위(WMD = 0.30 mm, p < 0.001, I² = 0%; 혈소판풍부혈장(platelet-rich plasma, PRP) 부분집단 0.52 mm, p = 0.015; 치조골 높이(ridge height, RH)는 유의하지 않음 WMD = 0.39 mm, p = 0.155), 치조제 보존술(alveolar ridge preservation, ARP)에서 RW WMD = 0.48 mm(p = 0.035, I² = 74%)·RH WMD = 0.40 mm(p < 0.001, I² = 5%)·재조합인간골형성단백질-2(recombinant human bone morphogenetic protein-2, rhBMP-2) RW WMD = 1.08 mm(p = 0.02)로 우위를 보였고 비광화 조직(non-mineralized tissue)이 감소(p = 0.009)했으나, 상악동거상술(maxillary sinus floor elevation, MSFA)은 RH WMD = 0.32 mm(p = 0.878)로 치수상 이득이 없었고 잔존 이식재(residual graft)만 감소(WMD = −5.54, p < 0.001)했다 — 생체치료제·세포치료 모두 중대 이상반응(major adverse event)은 보고되지 않았다.

효과량은 1 mm 미만의 소폭·시술 특이적이며 조직형태계측(histomorphometric)·치조제 보존 메타분석에서는 이질성이 I² = 74–98%까지 높고 교란편향(publication bias)이 검출되어(RH Egger p = 0.010, 상악동거상 광화조직 p = 0.022) 해석에 주의가 필요하다. 전 포함 환자가 전신건강(systemically healthy)이어서 당뇨병(diabetes mellitus)·자가면역(autoimmune) 질환 환자에 대한 적용 근거는 별도 문헌이 필요하며, 세포치료는 자료 수와 이질성 때문에 메타분석이 불가해 우월성이 입증되지 않았다 — 결론적으로 생체치료제는 골증대·치조제 보존에서의 소폭 보조제(adjunct)로, 상악동거상술에서는 근거가 없다.

## Summary

This 2026 Journal of Periodontology systematic review with meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024605421, PRISMA-reported) evaluates whether adjunctive biologics or cell therapy combined with scaffolds improve the outcomes of alveolar bone augmentation procedures performed for implant site development (ISD). Two PICO questions were run in parallel: biologics (enamel matrix derivatives, bone morphogenetic proteins, autologous blood-derived products, platelet-derived growth factor) plus scaffold ± guided bone regeneration (GBR) membrane, versus the same procedure without biologics; and cell therapy (bone-derived mesenchymal stem cells) plus scaffold/GBR, versus scaffold/GBR without cells. Screening of 3,541 records yielded 56 studies in qualitative synthesis and 23 RCTs (n = 1078) in quantitative analysis, stratified into ridge augmentation (6 RCTs, n = 172), ridge preservation (9 RCTs, n = 455) and sinus floor elevation (8 RCTs, n = 451). The review was registered and protocol-driven, risk of bias was appraised with RoB 2 and ROBINS-I v2, publication bias was assessed with funnel plots and Egger's tests, and evidence strength was graded against adapted ADA Clinical Practice Guidelines criteria. Bottom line: biologics are safe and confer a small, procedure-specific dimensional and histologic advantage in ridge augmentation and ridge preservation, show no dimensional benefit in sinus floor elevation, and cell therapy is safe but not shown to be superior.

## Key Contributions

- **Supplies the pooled numbers the 2022 AAP BEC could not.** 23 RCTs (n = 1078) with random-effects WMDs, I² and Egger's tests, where the BEC stopped at qualitative synthesis because of heterogeneity. The author team explicitly frames this as a quantitative extension of that consensus.
- **Splits acellular biologics from cell therapy.** Meta-analysis was feasible for biologics and judged infeasible for cell therapies (limited sample size, data heterogeneity) — the first review in this corpus to treat mesenchymal-cell therapy as a distinct evidence axis in alveolar augmentation rather than folding it into "biologics".
- **Procedure-stratified rather than pooled-across-the-board**, with agent-level subsets: PRP for ridge augmentation (RW +0.52 mm, p = 0.015) and rhBMP-2 for ridge preservation (RW +1.08 mm, p = 0.02).
- **Meta-analyzes histology, not just dimensions**: % mineralized tissue, % non-mineralized tissue and % residual graft pooled separately for ridge preservation and sinus floor elevation. Fewer residual graft particles with biologics is the most consistent histological finding (MSFA WMD = −5.54, p < 0.001).
- **Safety carried through every subgroup**: no major adverse events for either modality, with one reproducible toxicity signal — increased facial/intra-/extra-oral edema and prolonged swelling with rhBMP-2, alongside less post-operative pain and rash.
- **Implant-level outcomes reported but explicitly not pooled**: marginal bone loss up to 60 months and ISQ at placement and 18 months were comparable with and without adjunctive biologics.
- **Methodological recommendation**: adherence to the ID-COSM reporting standard for future studies of biologics or cell therapy in bone augmentation for ISD.

## Methodology

PROSPERO-registered (CRD42024605421, approved 4 Nov 2024), PRISMA 27-item-reported SR+MA. Eligibility: randomized or non-randomized trials (parallel-arm or split-mouth) and prospective single-arm case series with ≥10 subjects per arm, systemically healthy participants, any re-assessment modality, no minimum follow-up; excluded: retrospective/cross-sectional cohorts, case series, case reports, heavy smokers (≥10 cig/day), and systemic disease affecting bone healing (or lack of assessment for it). Searches of MEDLINE (OVID), EMBASE (OVID), Cochrane CENTRAL and the Cochrane Oral Health Group Trials Register to 1 Nov 2024, plus NYAM grey literature, ClinicalTrials.gov, reference-list harvesting, and an updated manual screening on 21 Sep 2025; two independent reviewers searched and extracted. RoB 2 (2019) for RCTs, ROBINS-I v2 for non-randomized studies. Random-effects WMD with Z, p and 95% CI; Cochran's Q and I²; funnel plots and Egger's tests; subset analyses per biologic where data allowed; Metafor in R 4.3.1; α = 0.05. No network meta-analysis (control-group composition not amenable) and no meta-analysis for cell therapy.

## Results

**Screening**: 3,532 records after duplicate removal + 9 from reference lists = 3,541 evaluated; 80 full texts assessed; 66 included in qualitative analysis per the Results text (the abstract reports "68 eligible … 56 selected"); 31 articles (n = 1,369) met the initial quantitative criteria and 8 were dropped for using biologics without a scaffold; the September 2025 update brought qualitative synthesis to 56 studies.

### Ridge augmentation — biologics

| Outcome | Studies | WMD | p | I² | Publication bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ridge width | 5 | +0.30 mm | < 0.001 | 0% | Egger p = 0.916 (symmetric) |
| Ridge width, PRP subset | 2 | +0.52 mm | 0.015 | 11% | — |
| Ridge height | 2 | +0.39 mm | 0.155 (NS) | 0% | — |

Histology could not be pooled. No major complications in either arm, but more facial swelling with rhBMP-2. PROs (2 studies): less post-operative discomfort with PRF; more intra-/extra-oral sensitivity at suture removal and 4 months with rhBMP-2; control group less willing to repeat surgery.

### Ridge augmentation — cell therapy

Four studies, meta-analysis not feasible. Stem cells seeded on a synthetic bioabsorbable bone graft outperformed autogenous blocks for volume gain (p = 0.03); no superiority in the other included studies. No histologic outcomes reported. More dehiscences with stem-cell GBR than control, not statistically significant. High satisfaction and willingness to recommend.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ridge width | 7 | +0.48 mm | 0.035 | 74% | Egger p = 0.054 (asymmetry) |
| Ridge width, rhBMP-2 subset | 3 | +1.08 mm | 0.02 | 84.6% | — |
| Ridge height | 6 | +0.40 mm | < 0.001 | 5% | **Egger p = 0.010 (bias)** |
| Ridge height, rhBMP-2 subset | 3 | +0.42 mm | 0.09 (marginal) | — | — |

| % residual graft | 5 | −5.01% | 0.172 (NS) | 97% | Egger p = 0.948 |

Cell therapy (1 study): significant linear ridge height gain at 6 weeks (not 12 weeks) vs conventional GBR, with a tendency toward less simultaneous bone grafting at early re-entry; micro-CT and histomorphometry trends toward higher bone volume fraction and bone area/tissue area at 6 weeks, absent at 12 weeks. Minor complications did not differ (erythema most frequent), no major adverse events. PROs: lower post-operative VAS discomfort with biologics, high willingness to repeat.

### Maxillary sinus floor elevation — biologics

| Outcome | Studies | WMD | p | I² | Publication bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ridge height | 2 | +0.32 mm | 0.878 (NS) | 87% | Egger p = 0.672 (symmetric) |
| % mineralized tissue | 6 | +3.08% | 0.334 (NS) | 87% | **Egger p = 0.022 (bias)** |
| % non-mineralized tissue | 5 | — | 0.19 (NS) | 70% | — |
| % residual graft | 5 | −5.54 | < 0.001 | 21% | — |

Cell therapy: no dimensional difference across 4 studies; no histologic difference in mineralized tissue or residual particles across 3 studies, one showing a higher percentage of mature bone. rhBMP-2 increased extra- and intra-oral edema and prolonged post-operative swelling, while post-operative pain and rash were substantially less common; no major adverse events. PRP associated with increased willingness to undergo the procedure again.

### Implant-related outcomes (narrative, not pooled)

Marginal bone loss on periapical radiographs similar with and without adjunctive biologics up to 60 months; ISQ comparable at placement and at 18 months; post-operative VAS discomfort comparable, with only minor differences in swelling. No cell-therapy study met the inclusion criteria here.

## Clinical Insights

- **The biologic is an adjunct to the scaffold, never a substitute for it.** Every pooled comparison in the review is biologic + scaffold versus the same scaffold without the biologic, so the effect sizes are additive increments on top of whatever graft the surgeon has already chosen.
- **Where to spend the biologic: ridge augmentation and ridge preservation.** These are the two indications with statistically significant dimensional gains (+0.30 to +0.48 mm on width; +0.40 mm on height in ridge preservation). Where to save it: sinus floor elevation, where the dimensional effect is null (p = 0.878) and only the histological turnover signal remains.
- **rhBMP-2 carries the dimensional signal among the agents tested** in ridge preservation (width +1.08 mm, p = 0.02; height +0.42 mm, p = 0.09 marginal) — but it is also the agent with the reproducible soft-tissue cost (more facial/intra-/extra-oral edema, prolonged swelling). The trade is dimension against swelling, and it is the one biologic whose swelling signal should be consented for.
- **Read the effect sizes as sub-millimetre.** Even the significant pooled advantages are ≤ 0.5 mm except for the rhBMP-2 ridge-preservation width subset (1.08 mm). A 0.3–0.5 mm increment is a real but modest gain; it should not be sold as a change in treatment category.
- **The sinus-lift residual-graft signal is a timing signal, not a volume signal.** Significantly less residual graft (p < 0.001) with faster turnover may support earlier re-entry and implant placement, which the authors propose as a protocol implication — but no dimensional advantage and no published re-entry-timing trial in this review.
- **Cell therapy: safe, promising, unproven.** No meta-analysis was possible, and the individual signals are heterogeneous (6-week ridge height advantage in one study, no difference in four others). The authors' own caveat — toxicity, neoplasm formation and undesired immune reactions reported in the wider medical literature — argues for treating this as investigational rather than practice-ready.
- **Population limit is clinically load-bearing.** Every included patient was systemically healthy, so the review cannot be extrapolated to diabetes, autoimmune dysfunction, heavy smokers or patients on relevant medication; the authors direct clinicians to other literature and clinical judgement for those groups.
- **Patient-reported outcomes are the least developed part of this evidence base.** PROs were reported in only 2–3 studies per indication, and the consistent signal is reduced post-operative discomfort and greater willingness to repeat the procedure with autologous blood-derived products — an argument for patient comfort and preference, not for biological superiority.
