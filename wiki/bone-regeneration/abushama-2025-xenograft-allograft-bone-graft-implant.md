## Three-line Summary

PROSPERO-registered systematic review (PRISMA; RoB2/ROBINS-I) of 12 comparative studies (n=395 patients) evaluating xenograft (bovine/porcine, e.g. Bio-Oss) versus allograft (e.g. FFB, DFDBA) bone grafts across immediate implant (6 studies), delayed implant (2), alveolar ridge preservation (3), and sinus augmentation (1) contexts.

No consistent statistically significant differences emerged between xenograft and allograft on marginal bone level, implant survival/stability, gingival/plaque indices, or bone dimensional change; one study found allograft had higher newly formed bone (65% vs 45%, p<0.05) while another found the opposite pattern for residual graft particles, and meta-analysis was not feasible due to study heterogeneity.

Narrative-only synthesis (no pooled meta-analysis) across only 12 studies with substantial clinical/methodological heterogeneity and moderate-to-serious risk of bias (especially confounding in non-randomized studies) — findings support that both graft classes are broadly interchangeable but do not establish superiority in any specific clinical scenario.

## Summary

This PROSPERO-registered, PRISMA-guided systematic review addresses a comparison rarely isolated in the literature — xenograft versus allograft (not versus autograft) bone graft materials for pre-implant oral and maxillofacial site preparation. Twelve comparative studies (395 patients total) spanning immediate implant placement, delayed implant placement, alveolar ridge preservation (ARP), and sinus augmentation were synthesized narratively (meta-analysis was not attempted due to heterogeneity across indications and outcome metrics).

Across clinical (gingival/plaque index, probing depth), radiographic (marginal bone level, CBCT/CT bone volume and resorption), and histological (newly formed bone %, residual graft particle %) outcome domains, no consistent statistically significant differences emerged between xenograft (predominantly bovine bone mineral, e.g. Bio-Oss; 58.1% of grafts) and allograft (e.g. fresh-frozen bone, DFDBA; 41.9% of grafts) materials. Individual studies occasionally reported significant differences (e.g. one favoring allograft on newly formed bone 65% vs 45%; another showing the opposite direction; one showing FFB and BBM implant survival statistically equal at ~98–100% despite differing resorption rates), but the overall pattern across studies was of clinical equivalence. The authors conclude both materials are reliable, broadly interchangeable GBR biomaterials for implant site preparation, shifting the practical decision to non-efficacy considerations (cost, availability, patient acceptance of animal-derived material).

## Key Contributions

- Isolates the specific PICO question of xenograft vs allograft (as opposed to the far more common vs-autograft comparison) across the full spectrum of pre-implant procedures — immediate implant, delayed implant, ARP, and sinus augmentation — in one review.
- Synthesizes clinical, radiographic, and histomorphometric outcome domains side by side across the 12 included comparative studies.
- Applies dual risk-of-bias assessment (RoB 2 for RCTs, ROBINS-I for non-randomized studies), flagging confounding as the dominant threat to validity in the observational subset.
- Explicit clinical conclusion that xenografts (Bio-Oss-class) and allografts (FFB/DFDBA/PerioGlas-class) can be used interchangeably as GBR biomaterials regardless of the specific pre-implant augmentation procedure.

## Methodology

- PRISMA-guided systematic review, PROSPERO-registered; PICO — P: adults ≥18y undergoing oral/maxillofacial implant site preparation; I: xenograft; C: allograft; O: clinical/radiographic/histologic success.
- Search of Google Scholar, MEDLINE/PubMed, and Cochrane Database to January 2024, English-language human studies published within roughly the prior 8 years (~2016 onward).
- Included CCTs, cohort, case-control, and comparative studies with complete datasets; excluded animal/tissue-culture studies, oncology/radiotherapy patients, heavy smokers, and autograft-focused reviews.
- Dual independent screening (title/abstract, then full text) with disagreements resolved by discussion.
- Risk of bias: revised Cochrane RoB 2 for RCTs; ROBINS-I (7 domains) for non-randomized studies.
- Narrative synthesis only — meta-analysis was judged not feasible given heterogeneity of indications and outcome measures across the 12 studies.

## Results

- 12 studies included (from 330 electronic + 20 manual hits, 165 full-text reviewed) — 6 immediate implant, 2 delayed implant, 3 ARP, 1 sinus augmentation; 395 total patients, mean age 40.46y; graft mix 58.1% bovine bone blocks (xenograft) vs 41.9% allograft bone blocks.
- Clinical indices (gingival, plaque, probing depth) declined similarly in both arms over 3–12 months with no significant intergroup differences (P>0.05) in the majority of studies.
- Marginal bone level and bone resorption were generally comparable between groups; one study reported significantly different bone-volume resorption rates between FFB (31.2%) and BBM (12.22%) (P=0.009) despite near-identical implant survival (97.8% vs 100%, P=0.352).
- Histomorphometric findings were the least consistent domain: one study favored allograft for newly formed bone (65% vs 45%, P<0.05); another showed the reverse pattern — underscoring why pooling was not attempted.
- Implant stability values were statistically equivalent between xenograft and allograft groups in the one study reporting this outcome.
- Risk of bias was low-to-moderate for most RCT domains (one high-risk domain flagged) and more concerning for non-randomized studies (ROBINS-I), with confounding as the most consistent issue.
- Bottom line: across immediate/delayed implant, ARP, and sinus augmentation contexts, xenograft and allograft materials perform comparably on the outcomes measured, supporting interchangeable use based on non-efficacy factors.
