## Three-line Summary

Ex vivo preclinical study, n=60 horizontal bone augmentation (HBA) procedures on 30 fresh pig hemimandibles, comparing partially contained vs. contained defect morphology and collagen-membrane fixation with vs. without four pins.

Graft material displacement (GMD) at the implant platform was substantial regardless of defect morphology (PCD −28.3% vs. CD −28.6%, p≥0.068), but membrane pin-fixation significantly reduced GMD (−15.8% with pins vs. −38.1% without pins, p<0.001), with the greatest apico-lateral loss in the central-crestal zone.

Clinicians should over-contour graft material to compensate for an expected ~15–40% crestal collapse during primary wound closure, and four-pin membrane fixation is an effective, low-cost method to reduce that collapse and improve procedural predictability; limitation: ex vivo pig-mandible model without dynamic postoperative loading/healing.

## Summary

This ex vivo preclinical study (30 fresh pig hemimandibles, 60 horizontal bone augmentation (HBA) procedures) quantified 3D graft material displacement (GMD) — a particulate deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM, BioOss) covered by a resorbable collagen membrane (BioGide) — during primary wound closure in guided bone regeneration (GBR). Two variables were tested in a 2×2 factorial design: defect morphology (partially contained defect, PCD, vs. contained defect, CD) and membrane fixation (four fixation pins vs. no pins). GMD was measured both by CBCT (cross-sectional, at the implant platform and 8 further levels apically) and by intraoral-scan (IOS) 3D surface superimposition (nine 4×4mm subsections of the grafted footprint).

Defect morphology had no measurable effect on GMD — PCD and CD lost graft material to a similar degree at the implant platform (~28%) and across the grafted site. In contrast, four-pin membrane fixation cut GMD roughly in half at the crestal/implant-platform level (−15.8% with pins vs. −38.1% without, p<0.001) and significantly reduced displacement in nearly every measured surface section. Local soft-tissue phenotype (keratinized mucosa width, flap thickness, flap advancement) had no significant association with GMD, echoing a prior study by the same group on flap-advancement technique.

A key mechanistic finding: graft compression appears essentially unavoidable — pinned membranes compress the graft earlier (during pin placement/membrane tensioning) while non-pinned sites compress equivalently during flap closure itself, converging on a similar final graft thickness (~1.7–1.9mm from a 3mm starting overcontour) regardless of fixation. The clinical value of pinning is therefore less about the final graft volume and more about *predictability* — pins let the surgeon visually control and reshape the graft before closure, and substantially reduce the unpredictable apico-lateral collapse that otherwise concentrates at the crestal region critical for esthetics and peri-implant buccal bone maintenance.

## Key Contributions

- First 3D volumetric (IOS-superimposition) study to characterize the *directionality* of graft displacement during primary wound closure — showing it is consistently apico-lateral, concentrated in the central-crestal zone.
- Head-to-head comparison of two defect morphologies (PCD vs CD) that are meant to simulate late vs. early implant placement scenarios — finding defect containment does NOT protect against GMD as might be assumed.
- Quantifies the magnitude of benefit from four-pin membrane fixation (~2.5x reduction in crestal displacement vs. no fixation), providing a numeric benchmark absent from most narrative GBR literature.
- Rules out local soft-tissue phenotype as a confounder, isolating membrane fixation as the dominant modifiable variable.

## Methodology

2×2 factorial, ex vivo, pig hemimandible model (n=30 mandibles, 60 procedures, 15/group: PCD−Pins, PCD+Pins, CD−Pins, CD+Pins). Standardized 144mm³ bone defects at 2nd premolar sites; SPI Element implant placed 1mm within the bony envelope; BioOss graft over-contoured 2mm beyond envelope (3mm total thickness); BioGide membrane applied ± 4 Frios fixation pins. Standardized tension-controlled primary closure (≤0.1N via dynamometer). GMD assessed via CBCT (implant-platform-centered cross-sections, L0–L8 at 1mm increments) and via intraoral-scan 3D superimposition (9-section 4×4mm grid over a 12×12mm ROI). Sample size powered for Cohen's f=0.75 with pairwise-comparison correction (60 procedures exceeded the 42 required). Mann-Whitney/ANOVA/Kruskal-Wallis with appropriate post hoc corrections; ICC=0.91 for measurement reliability.

## Results

- **Defect morphology (PCD vs CD)**: no significant GMD difference at any CBCT level (p≥0.068) or IOS section (p≥0.086).
- **Membrane fixation**: pins significantly reduced GMD at the crestal levels — L0 (implant platform): −15.8%±25.6% (pins) vs −38.1%±27.4% (no pins), p<0.001; L1 p<0.001; L2 p=0.007. No significant difference beyond L3.
- **Volumetric/IOS**: apico-lateral displacement pattern, worst in central-crestal section; pins significantly reduced GMD in 8 of 9 sections (all except apical-distal), p≤0.040.
- **Soft tissue**: KMW, flap thickness (3/6/9mm), flap advancement — none significantly associated with GMD (p≥0.240).
- **Graft compression mechanism**: GMT after membrane placement but before closure was 2.9mm (no-pin) vs 2.2mm (pin) — pinning compresses the graft earlier; final post-closure thickness converged to ~1.7–1.9mm in all groups.
