## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Biomed Eng Online 2025) synthesizing 3D-printed scaffold technology for general orthopedic bone-defect repair — material selection (metals, polymers, bioceramics), hierarchical porosity/biomechanical design, biological functionalization, and bioprinting methods.

Ideal scaffolds must balance competing design constraints (large pores/high porosity favor cell ingrowth and angiogenesis but compromise mechanical strength; target Young's modulus 7-30 GPa, compressive strength 50-200 MPa, tensile strength ~150 MPa to approximate cortical bone), and composite/coated designs (e.g., titanium+SiHA+VEGF, PCL/beta-TCP+BMP-2, magnesium-crosslinked hydrogels) consistently outperform single-material scaffolds in preclinical models.

No original clinical trial data — this is a narrative synthesis of preclinical (mostly animal) studies and general orthopedic case examples; translational barriers (vascularization, load-bearing mechanical stability, manufacturing scalability) remain unresolved, and dental-specific applications are only briefly mentioned.

## Summary

This narrative review surveys 3D-printed scaffold technology for general orthopedic bone-defect repair, framing scaffold design around three pillars: material selection (metallic, polymeric, and bioceramic, plus composite combinations), structural optimization (hierarchical macro/micro/nano porosity tuned for mechanical/biological performance), and biological functionalization (growth-factor delivery, cell seeding, surface coatings). It does not report original clinical trial data — it synthesizes preclinical (predominantly animal) studies and a handful of general-orthopedic clinical case examples (spinal, lower-extremity, tibial). The review's core message is that no single material or design parameter suffices: composite and coated scaffolds (e.g., titanium alloy + hydroxyapatite coating + VEGF, PCL/beta-TCP + BMP-2-loaded microspheres, magnesium-crosslinked hydrogels) consistently outperform single-material scaffolds by compensating for individual material deficiencies (mechanical weakness of natural polymers, low bioactivity of synthetic polymers, brittleness/poor degradability of bioceramics, non-biodegradability/stress-shielding of metals).

Dental relevance for this wiki is indirect: the paper is a general bone-tissue-engineering materials-science reference, not a dental or alveolar-ridge-specific study. It is held here as background depth for [[bone-regeneration/bubalo-2026-bone-substitutes-alveolar-ridge-augmentation]], whose narrative review of alveolar ridge augmentation bone substitutes treats "3D-printed scaffold" as one of seven graft categories but only characterizes it briefly (osteoconductive ± osteoinductive, variable resorption, indicated for complex/irregular defects). This paper supplies the underlying materials-engineering rationale (why titanium/bioceramic/polymer composites behave as they do, what porosity/mechanical trade-offs drive scaffold design) without itself constituting dental-implant or ridge-augmentation clinical evidence.

## Key Contributions

- Comprehensive material taxonomy across metals (titanium alloys, magnesium, tantalum), polymers (natural: collagen; synthetic: PCL/PLA/PLGA), and bioceramics (hydroxyapatite, TCP, bioactive glass), each with worked preclinical examples.
- Articulates the central scaffold design tension: porosity/pore-size (favoring cell ingrowth, nutrient exchange, angiogenesis) directly trades off against mechanical strength (favoring load-bearing stability) — no pore-size/porosity consensus exists in the field.
- Quantifies target mechanical benchmarks for cortical-bone-mimicking scaffolds: Young's modulus 7-30 GPa, compressive strength 50-200 MPa, tensile strength ~150 MPa.
- Surveys bioprinting methods (inkjet/binder-jetting, selective laser melting, extrusion/FDM) with precision/cost/speed trade-offs relevant to manufacturing scalability.
- Forward-looking section on 4D dynamic (stimuli-responsive) scaffolds and AI-driven patient-specific design as next-generation directions.

## Results

- Compact bone porosity 5-30%; cancellous bone porosity 50-90%. Pore size ~100 μm favors osseointegration; >300 μm better supports vascular growth/angiogenesis; fibrocartilage requires 200-300 μm — the field lacks a single consensus threshold.
- Target mechanical properties for load-bearing scaffolds: Young's modulus 7-30 GPa, compressive strength 50-200 MPa, tensile strength ~150 MPa (approximating cortical bone).
- Titanium fiber plate (Takizawa et al.): 0.2 mm thickness achieving Young's modulus ~30 GPa (near cortical-bone range 10-30 GPa), avoiding stress-shielding while retaining osteogenic effect with appropriate pore size/porosity.
- SiHA-coated + VEGF-adsorbed macroporous Ti6Al4V scaffold: synergistic effect in a sheep model — SiHA alone or VEGF alone was unsatisfactory; the combination significantly boosted bone tissue regeneration.
- Porous tantalum elastic modulus ~2-8 GPa; self-passivating oxide surface promotes direct bone-to-bone interface formation; selective-laser-melted micro-gradient nanostructure surface enhanced early bone integration in a rabbit femur model.
- MAO-modified 3D-printed porous Ti6Al4V + vancomycin hydrogel (25-day degradation tuned to infectious bone-defect healing timeline) achieved effective antibacterial control and osseointegration in an infected rabbit bone-defect model.
- PCL/beta-TCP scaffold with polydopamine + alginate-microsphere BMP-2 delivery improved cortical bone connectivity/induction in a rabbit femoral segmental defect model.
- General-orthopedic (non-dental) clinical case examples: 3D-printed artificial vertebral body for multi-segment spinal resection (Hu et al.); porous Ti6Al4V scaffold repairing lower-extremity bone defects without additional bone grafting (Liu et al.); porous tantalum bone plate improving outcomes in tibial-fracture nonunion (Zhao et al.).
- Persistent unresolved translational barriers explicitly flagged: vascular network establishment, mechanical stability under real load-bearing conditions, manufacturing scalability.
