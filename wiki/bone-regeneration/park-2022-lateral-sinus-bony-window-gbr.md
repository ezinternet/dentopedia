## Three-line Summary

This case series (4 patients) describes a novel technique using the lateral sinus bony window, obtained during maxillary sinus augmentation (MSA), as an autogenous block bone graft for GBR in wide post-extraction defects adjacent to the sinus.
In all four cases, the lateral sinus bony window was successfully adapted to adjacent post-extraction defects (alone or with particulate graft) and covered with resorbable collagen membrane; at 6-month re-entry, grafted bone was well integrated and complete bone filling of defects was observed.
Within the limitations of this case series, the lateral sinus bony window appears to be a safe and effective intraoral donor site for GBR in wide defects adjacent to sinus-augmented sites, eliminating the need for a second surgical site.

## Summary

Park et al. (2022) describe a technique in which the lateral sinus bony window — the cortical bone flap typically discarded or repositioned at the end of lateral approach maxillary sinus augmentation — is instead repurposed as an autogenous block graft to reconstruct adjacent wide post-extraction defects. Four patients who simultaneously required lateral approach 상악동 거상술 (Maxillary Sinus Augmentation, MSA) and implant site development in adjacent areas with wide 발치 후 결손부 (post-extraction defects) underwent the combined procedure. In each case, after sinus grafting was completed, the window bone was adapted to the neighboring defect — either alone or combined with particulate 탈단백 소골 무기질 (Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral, DBBM) — and covered with a resorbable collagen membrane. At the 6-month re-entry for implant placement, all four sites demonstrated complete bone filling with good integration of the grafted window bone into the native bone. Implants were placed successfully in all four cases, with stable outcomes maintained at 12–24-month follow-up. The authors position this technique as a practical way to exploit material that would otherwise be wasted, avoiding additional donor site morbidity entirely.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces repurposing the lateral sinus bony window as an autogenous cortical block graft for GBR in wide post-extraction defects, converting surgical waste into a usable graft material.
- Complete bone filling at 6-month re-entry in all 4 cases; implants placed successfully in all cases.
- Demonstrates feasibility of applying the window bone alone or augmented with DBBM particulate, depending on defect size.
- Eliminates need for a separate intraoral or extraoral donor site, reducing surgical morbidity and treatment complexity.
- Stable clinical and radiographic outcomes at 12–24-month follow-up.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Case series (n=4)
- **Indication**: All patients simultaneously required lateral approach MSA and implant site preparation in adjacent areas with wide post-extraction defects
- **GBR procedure**: Lateral bony window preserved during MSA; after sinus grafting, window adapted to adjacent defect as block autograft (± DBBM particulate), covered with resorbable collagen membrane
- **Re-entry**: 6 months post-GBR for implant placement; clinical bone integration assessed
- **Follow-up**: 12–24 months, clinical and radiographic

## Limitations

- Low evidence level (case series, n=4); findings not generalizable
- No comparison group vs conventional GBR approaches or separate intraoral donor sites
- Technique is applicable only when the patient simultaneously undergoes lateral MSA — the window would not otherwise be available
- Short follow-up (12–24 months) insufficient to assess long-term implant survival
- Limited quantitative data on bone volume changes
