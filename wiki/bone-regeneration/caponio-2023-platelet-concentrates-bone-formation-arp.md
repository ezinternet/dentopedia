## Three-line Summary

PROSPERO-registered SR+MA with trial sequential analysis (8 RCTs, 255 sockets; 10–24 weeks histomorphometric follow-up) evaluating platelet concentrate (L-PRF or P-PRP) vs spontaneous healing on new vital bone formation in alveolar ridge preservation, with PICO restricted to adults undergoing atraumatic extraction and bone biopsy at implant placement.

Overall SMD was 1.77 (95% CI 1.47–2.06, p<0.001), indicating a large effect of platelet concentrates over spontaneous healing; no significant difference was found between L-PRF (SMD=1.75) and P-PRP (SMD=1.58; ANOVA p=0.71); trial sequential analysis confirmed the result is definitive after 6 studies with the required information size reached.

Any platelet concentrate significantly increases new vital bone formation in alveolar ridge preservation compared to spontaneous healing; the type of concentrate used is clinically less important than the decision to use one, and the simplest preparation technique should be favored.

## Summary

Caponio et al. 2023 is a PROSPERO-registered SR+MA with TSA evaluating the histomorphometric effect of platelet concentrates (PCs) on new vital bone formation in ARP. Unlike dimensional-change SRs, this study uniquely focuses on bone biopsy data (% vital bone at implant placement time). Eight RCTs (6 parallel, 2 split-mouth) from Europe, Brazil, and South Africa were included; L-PRF was used in 120 sockets, P-PRP in 31 sockets, 104 controls. Overall, PCs significantly increased new vital bone formation vs. spontaneous healing (SMD=1.77, p<0.001). No significant difference between L-PRF and P-PRP was found. TSA analysis confirmed the result is robust after 6 studies with RIS achieved. Meta-regression suggests longer follow-up yields greater bone formation benefit. The review recommends using any PC (choosing the simplest technique) when planning ARP prior to implant surgery.

## Key Contributions

- Only SR+MA focused exclusively on histomorphometric vital bone formation in ARP (not ridge dimension or pain).
- TSA confirms sufficient evidence — no additional RCTs needed to establish PC superiority over spontaneous healing for bone formation.
- No clinically meaningful difference between L-PRF and P-PRP — type of PC is less important than using one.
- Meta-regression finding: bone formation advantage may increase at longer follow-up — supports longer healing intervals before implant placement.

## Methodology

PROSPERO CRD42022340941; databases: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane (June 2022, updated December 2022); PICO: adults ≥18 undergoing atraumatic extraction → ARP with PC alone vs. blood clot → histomorphometric vital bone % at ≥10 weeks. Exclusion: third molars, head/neck RT, uncontrolled DM, PC + other materials, follow-up <10 weeks. 8 RCTs selected (from 3809 results). Risk of bias: Cochrane RoB 2; 1 low, 3 some concerns, 2 high. Fixed effect meta-analysis (I²=44%); SMD by Hedges' g. TSA with α/β=5%/10%, MID=7% new bone.

## Results

- **Overall SMD**: 1.77 (95% CI 1.47–2.06), p<0.001 — large effect
- **L-PRF subgroup**: SMD=1.75 (1.21–2.28), I²=58%
- **P-PRP subgroup**: SMD=1.58 (0.87–2.29), I²=0%
- **L-PRF vs. P-PRP**: ANOVA p=0.71 — no significant difference
- **Publication bias**: Trim-and-fill: 0 trimmed; Egger p=0.44; safe N=239
- **TSA**: RIS reached after 6 studies — result is definitive
- **Meta-regression**: Follow-up duration trending toward greater benefit at longer intervals (p=0.27, NS but directional)
