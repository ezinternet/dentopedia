## Three-line Summary

Beagle controlled study (n=13 dogs, standardized 10×3×4 mm mandibular defects) directly compared OCS-B Collagen® (NIBEC, Korea) vs Bio-Oss® Collagen (Geistlich) by micro-CT, histomorphometry, and resonance frequency analysis at 8, 16, and 48 weeks (24 weeks post-implant).
Both collagenated xenografts achieved significantly greater bone volume than no-graft control at 8 and 16 weeks (p<0.0001), with no statistically significant difference between the two graft materials on any endpoint — BMD, BV/TV, BIC, or ISQ.
OCS-B Collagen® is non-inferior to Bio-Oss® Collagen for GBR bone regeneration and peri-implant osseointegration, providing preclinical validation for the Korean DBBM alternative.

## Summary

This in vivo study from Seoul National University and NIBEC (Korea) is the first controlled head-to-head comparison of OCS-B Collagen® (NIBEC, Jincheon, Korea) and Bio-Oss® Collagen (Geistlich, Switzerland) using a standardized beagle mandibular defect model with implant placement. Thirteen male beagles underwent bilateral premolar extraction, followed 4 weeks later by creation of box-shaped defects (10×3×4 mm) filled with OCS-B.C, Bio-Oss.C, or left unfilled (NT control), all covered with Bio-Gide® membrane. Micro-CT analysis confirmed both graft materials significantly maintained bone volume versus the NT group at 8 and 16 weeks (p<0.0001), with no significant inter-material difference. Histological and histomorphometric analysis showed similar new bone formation patterns, graft integration, and defect wall preservation in both groups. At 24 weeks post-implant (48 weeks from grafting), BIC and ISQ were comparable between the two groups. Key material difference: OCS-B.C uses gelation-crosslinked porcine collagen while Bio-Oss.C uses non-crosslinked porcine collagen, which may influence degradation kinetics, but this did not translate into measurable differences in bone regeneration outcomes at the time points assessed. The authors conclude OCS-B.C is a viable and cost-effective alternative to Bio-Oss.C for GBR procedures.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes preclinical non-inferiority of OCS-B Collagen® vs Bio-Oss® Collagen across all measured endpoints
- Documents collagen crosslinking difference: OCS-B.C (gelation/crosslinked) vs Bio-Oss.C (non-crosslinked) — no outcome impact detected
- Confirms both products support effective osseointegration following GBR (BIC and ISQ comparable at 24 wk post-implant)
- Both DBBM-C products significantly outperform spontaneous healing for BV maintenance at 8 and 16 weeks

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Study type | Controlled in vivo animal study (beagle) |
| Animals | 13 male beagles, 10–15 kg, 7–9 months |
| Defect | Box-shaped mandibular; 10×3×4 mm; bilateral, post-extraction |
| Groups | OCS-B.C (NIBEC, Korea) / Bio-Oss.C (Geistlich, Switzerland) / NT (control) |
| Membrane | Bio-Gide® (Geistlich) on all defect groups |
| Implants | TS III SA (Osstem, ø3.0×8.5 mm) at 24 wk post-graft |
| Timepoints | 8 wk, 16 wk post-graft; 24 wk post-implant |
| Assessment | Micro-CT (SkyScan-1273), histomorphometry (Technovit 7200 VLC), RFA (Osstell ISQ) |
| Ethics | Seoul National University IACUC No. SNU-220914-6 |

| BMD/BV at 24 wk post-implant | sustained | sustained | NS |
| BIC at 24 wk post-implant | — | — | NS |

### Histomorphometry (8–16 wk)

- Both graft groups: significantly greater BV than NT (p<0.05) at 8 and 16 weeks
- Buccal and lingual wall height maintained significantly better than NT (p<0.001)
- Wall angle change minimal in both graft groups (p<0.05)
- Graft resorption nearly complete by 16 weeks in both groups, replaced by new bone

### ALP Activity (in vitro)

- Both OCS-B.C and Bio-Oss.C significantly higher ALP activity than NT at 3 days (p<0.05)
- No significant difference between OCS-B.C and Bio-Oss.C

### Implant Stability (24 wk post-implant)

- ISQ values: similar between OCS-B.C and Bio-Oss.C — both indicate successful osseointegration
- BIC: comparable; no significant inter-group difference
