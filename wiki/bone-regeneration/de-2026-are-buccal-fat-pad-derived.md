## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA; Oxford CEBM grading, ROBINS-I) of in vivo human studies of buccal fat pad–derived stem cells (BFPSCs) for oro-maxillofacial bone defect regeneration in adults — PubMed/MEDLINE, EMBASE and Cochrane, January 2015 to September 2025.

Of 375 records screened, 6 clinical studies involving 49 patients (mean age 42.8 y, range 18–65; 29 male / 20 female) met the criteria — 30/49 (61.2%) BFPSC-treated versus 19/49 (38.8%) conventional graft controls; all 6 reported favorable clinical and radiologic outcomes at a mean 15-month follow-up (5–48 months), 4/6 added histology confirming new bone, and ROBINS-I judged 5/6 at serious risk of bias.

Clinical implication: BFPSC harvesting appears to cut postoperative pain, hospital stay and donor-site morbidity versus autologous bone harvest, but the underlying evidence is weak (Oxford CEBM 2b ×1, 3b ×3, 4 ×2) and the authors themselves call for standardized randomized trials before effectiveness can be confirmed.

375건 중 최종 6편의 임상 연구, 총 **49명**(평균 나이 42.8세, 범위 18~65세, 남성 29명·여성 20명)이 포함됐고 — BFPSC군 30/49명(61.2%) 대 대조군 19/49명(38.8%); 6편 전부에서 임상적·방사학적 양호한 결과를 보고했고 평균 추적기간은 15개월(5~48개월)였으며, 4편은 조직학적 소견으로 신생골 (new bone) 형성을 확인함. ROBINS-I 평가에서는 6편 중 5편이 중대한 위험편향 (serious risk of bias)으로 판정됨.

임상적 함의 — BFPSC 채취는 자가골 (autologous bone) 채취(측방 ramus 피질판 (Lateral Ramus Cortical Plate, LRCP)·전장골능 (Anterior Iliac Crest, AIC)) 대비 수술 후 통증·입원 기간·공여부 이환율을 낮추는 것으로 보고됨. 그러나 근거등급이 낮고(옥스포드 CEBM 2b 1편, 3b 3편, 4 2편) 편향이 심해, 저자들은 효과 확정을 위해 표준화된 무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT)이 필요하다고 명시함.

## Summary

This systematic review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/scs.0000000000012186); PMID 41289609; PMCID PMC13200894) asks a narrow, clinically sharp question: are buccal fat pad–derived stem cells effective in adults for oro-maxillofacial bone defect regeneration? The answer it returns is not "yes" — it is "the evidence base is six small studies totalling 49 patients, and 5 of those 6 carry a serious risk of bias." A PRISMA-guided search of PubMed/MEDLINE, EMBASE and Cochrane (January 2015 – September 2025) yielded 375 records, 360 after deduplication, 8 full texts, and finally **6 included in vivo human studies**. Oxford CEBM grading put them at 2b (1 study), 3b (3 studies) and 4 (2 studies) — nothing above a small randomized trial, and two at case-series level — while ROBINS-I judged 5/6 at serious risk of bias, a finding the review itself says means its results "should be interpreted with caution."

Within that ceiling, the signal is consistently favourable and never contradicted. All 6 studies reported clinical and radiologic success (soft-tissue healing, graft integration, absence of pain/infection/dehiscence; CBCT-confirmed 3-dimensional bone formation) at a mean 15-month follow-up (range 5–48 months), and 4 added histology showing well-structured newly formed bone with good vascularization. One study characterised the cells themselves by flow cytometry on both cultured adipose-derived stem cells and a 3-month post-surgery bone biopsy, showing an MSC-compatible surface profile (HLA-ABC, CD29, CD49e, CD51, CD90 positive; HLA-DR, CD40, CD40L, CD80, CD86 negative) and inhibition of lymphocyte proliferation. The one comparative advantage reported consistently is morbidity: in the 2 studies that benchmarked BFPSC harvesting against autologous bone harvest from the lateral ramus cortical plate (LRCP) or anterior iliac crest (AIC), BFPSC patients had less postoperative pain, shorter hospital stays and lower morbidity.

Read this page accordingly. It documents that a cell-based route to maxillofacial bone regeneration is *feasible and clinically used*, and that it may reduce the donor-site burden that dominates the autogenous-graft literature. It does **not** document that BFPSCs are effective: no pooled effect estimate, no confidence interval and no p-value is reported anywhere in the review, the treatment split (30/49 BFPSC vs 19/49 control) is descriptive only, and the authors' own conclusion is that "high-quality studies are needed to confirm effectiveness and define standardized protocols," with donor variability flagged as a critical determinant of cell quality. Note also that the title says "maxillary" while the review's own PICOS, focused question and abstract all specify **oro-maxillofacial** defects, and the included studies use mandibular donor sites — the findings should not be read as maxilla-specific.

## Key Contributions

- **First dedicated systematic review of BFPSC/BFP-DFAT cell therapy for in vivo human oro-maxillofacial bone defect regeneration**, with the question pre-formulated as a PICOS ("are buccal fat pad–derived stem cells effective in adults for oro-maxillofacial bone defect regeneration?").
- **Exhaustively maps a thin evidence base**: 375 → 360 → 8 → 6 studies, 49 patients, 1 randomized trial — the yield is itself the headline finding.
- **Grades every included study on the Oxford CEBM hierarchy** — 2b (1), 3b (3), 4 (2) — and applies ROBINS-I, reporting 5/6 studies at serious risk of bias and 1 at moderate.
- **Isolates the morbidity advantage of the cell route**: BFPSC harvesting produced less postoperative pain, shorter hospital stay and lower morbidity than autologous bone harvesting (LRCP, AIC) in the 2 studies that compared them.
- **Provides direct cell-characterization evidence** (flow cytometry on cultured ASCs and on a 3-month post-surgery bone biopsy in one study) supporting MSC identity, immunomodulation (lymphocyte-proliferation inhibition) and phenotype stability after differentiation.
- **States the unresolved agenda precisely**: donor variability as a driver of regenerative potential, absent standardized protocols, and the need for randomized controlled trials with long-term follow-up.

## Methodology

Systematic review with narrative synthesis and **no meta-analysis**. PRISMA 2020 reporting (using the PRISMA Extension Statement for SRs incorporating network meta-analyses) was applied. Databases: PubMed/MEDLINE (MeSH + free-text), EMBASE and Cochrane Library (equivalent free-text keywords), searched January 2015 – September 2025; no automatic human/language filters at search stage — human-only and English-only restrictions were applied at manual screening. PICOS: P = in vivo studies using human-source BFPSCs/BFP-DFAT cells for oro-maxillofacial bone defect; I = maxillofacial bone defect treatment with BFPSCs; C = any treatment or no treatment; O = effectiveness; S = any study design. Excluded: in vitro, non-human source, cell-free BFP flap/mass applications, non-original articles, non-English, no full text. Screening by two independent reviewers (P.D.F., I.G.) at title/abstract and full-text stages, with discrepancies resolved by a senior reviewer (P.V.); abstracts with poorly defined results were deliberately carried forward to avoid losing potential evidence. Primary outcomes: clinical, radiologic (CBCT/orthopantomogram) and histologic; secondary outcomes: adverse effects, patient morbidity, hospital stay duration, patient discomfort. Appraisal: Oxford CEBM levels of evidence + ROBINS-I risk of bias.

**Background from cited literature (not this review's own data)** — first clinical ADSC use (Lendeckel 2004, calvarial defect in a 7-year-old); mandibular reconstruction with β-TCP + BMP-2 + autologous ADSCs (Sándor GK et al.); donor age inversely affects osteogenic potential (younger donors: higher OPN/OCL/BMP-2, greater calcium deposition); and the anatomical rationale for BFP as a cell source — specialized richly vascularized adipose tissue, minimally invasive access, size reportedly independent of fat distribution/body weight/age, and abundant neural-crest-derived cells with faster proliferation and higher osteogenic/angiogenic marker expression than other adipose sources.

**Reporting discrepancies (reproduced, not smoothed over)** — (1) the title says "maxillary" while the PICOS, focused question and abstract specify oro-maxillofacial, and the included studies use mandibular donor sites; (2) the risk-of-bias section describes "included diagnostic accuracy studies," a leftover from another template (ROBINS-I is the right tool for non-randomized interventions but is not designed for the one included RCT); (3) CD34 is listed among the variable-expression markers and then reappears among markers reported as exclusively expressed.
