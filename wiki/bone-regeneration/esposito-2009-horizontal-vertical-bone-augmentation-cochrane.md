## Three-line Summary

Cochrane SR+MA of 13 RCTs comparing horizontal and vertical bone augmentation techniques for dental implant treatment, covering GBR, osteodistraction, onlay/inlay grafts, and bone substitutes vs autogenous bone.

Vertical augmentation had more implant failures (OR=5.74, borderline p=0.06) and significantly more complications (OR=4.97, p<0.05) than short implants; osteodistraction gained 3.25 mm more bone than inlay grafts; patients strongly preferred bone substitutes over iliac autografts (OR=0.03).

Short implants appear preferable to vertical augmentation in resorbed mandibles due to lower complication risk; bone substitutes are a valid, lower-morbidity alternative to autogenous bone in GBR.

## Summary

This Cochrane systematic review (CD003607, pub4 2009) evaluated the evidence from RCTs on horizontal and vertical alveolar bone augmentation techniques used to allow dental implant placement in patients with insufficient bone volume. Thirteen RCTs were included: 3 on horizontal augmentation (106 patients) and 10 on vertical augmentation (218 patients), with follow-up ranging from 4 months to 3 years.

**Key clinical conclusions:**

1. **Short implants vs vertical augmentation**: In resorbed mandibles, vertical augmentation carried borderline-significantly more implant failures (OR=5.74, 95% CI 0.94–35.0, p=0.06) and significantly more complications (OR=4.97, 95% CI 1.31–18.8, p<0.05) compared to using short implants. This finding supports short implants as the preferred strategy when feasible.

2. **Osteodistraction vs inlay grafting**: Osteodistraction (distraction osteogenesis) produced 3.25 mm more vertical bone gain than inlay/interpositional grafting (MD=3.25 mm), though both techniques were associated with high complication rates (up to 60%).

3. **Bone substitutes vs autogenous bone in GBR**: Bone substitute materials (e.g., DBBM/deproteinized bovine bone) yielded marginally more bone gain (MD=+0.60 mm) compared to autogenous bone in GBR procedures. Patients strongly preferred bone substitute block grafts over iliac crest autografts (OR=0.03, p=0.02), reflecting lower morbidity.

4. **Overall evidence quality**: Small trial numbers, small sample sizes, short follow-up periods, and high risk of bias in many included RCTs precluded definitive conclusions. The authors called for larger, well-designed trials with longer follow-up.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes Cochrane-level evidence (highest tier in EBM hierarchy) that **short implants should be preferred over vertical augmentation in resorbed mandibles** where both options are feasible
- Quantifies the complication burden: vertical augmentation complication rates up to 60% vs substantially lower rates with short implants
- Confirms that **xenograft/alloplastic bone substitutes are valid alternatives to autogenous bone** in GBR horizontal augmentation, with equivalent or marginally superior bone gain and reduced donor-site morbidity
- Provides the foundational reference point against which subsequent augmentation technique comparisons are measured

## Methodology

- **Design**: Cochrane Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
- **Databases searched**: CENTRAL (Cochrane Central Register), MEDLINE, EMBASE; no language restrictions
- **Included studies**: RCTs only; both parallel and split-mouth designs
- **Comparisons evaluated**:
  - Vertical augmentation techniques vs short implants
  - Osteodistraction vs inlay/interpositional grafting
  - Different vertical augmentation materials (autogenous bone vs substitutes)
  - Horizontal GBR with bone substitute vs autogenous bone
- **Outcomes**: prosthesis failure, implant failure, peri-implant bone loss, complications (biological/prosthetic), bone gain (mm), patient preference/satisfaction
- **Analysis**: random-effects meta-analysis; pooled OR (dichotomous) and MD (continuous) with 95% CI; Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for quality assessment

## Results

| Comparison | Outcome | Statistic | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vertical augmentation vs short implants | Implant failures | OR = 5.74 | p = 0.06 (borderline) |
| Vertical augmentation vs short implants | Complications | OR = 4.97 | p < 0.05 |
| Osteodistraction vs inlay graft | Bone gain | MD = +3.25 mm | Favors osteodistraction |
| Bone substitute vs autogenous bone (GBR) | Bone gain | MD = +0.60 mm | Marginal favor substitute |
| Bone substitute vs iliac autograft (block) | Patient preference | OR = 0.03 | p = 0.02 (strong) |

Complication rates in vertical augmentation trials: up to 60%, including membrane exposure, infection, graft failure, wound dehiscence, and donor-site complications (iliac harvest).
