## Three-line Summary

Preliminary case series (5 consecutive edentulous patients, 20 sites, severe anterior mandibular horizontal ridge atrophy with adequate ridge height; 1-4 year follow-up) testing a bone shell technique that uses the coronal segment of the knife-edge ridge itself, relocated laterally, as an autologous shell instead of a remote-donor-site bone plate.

Average horizontal bone gain was 3.6 ± 0.76 mm at 1 mm below the newly formed ridge crest and 3.4 ± 0.92 mm at 5 mm below crest; all 20 sites regained sufficient ridge volume for staged implant placement, and only 2/20 sites needed additional hard-tissue grafting at implant placement.

Because donor and recipient sites coincide, the authors report primary closure required no periosteal releasing incision (PRI) or flap advancement — abstract-only evidence, n=5/20, no control group.

## Summary

This case series describes a variant of the bone shell technique for horizontal ridge augmentation in which the shell is not harvested from a remote donor site (external oblique ridge, symphysis, lateral sinus wall, or palate — the conventional sources the authors cite) but is instead the coronal segment of the atrophic knife-edge ridge itself, sectioned and relocated laterally over particulate graft at the same site. In 5 consecutive edentulous patients (20 sites) with severe horizontal atrophy but adequate ridge height, this achieved 3.6 mm and 3.4 mm of horizontal bone gain at 1 mm and 5 mm below the new crest, restoring enough volume for staged implant placement at all 20 sites (2 needed a small additional graft at placement). The paper frames this as a donor-site-free alternative to conventional bone-shell/block grafting, and — the detail most relevant to the periosteal-releasing-incision (PRI) literature — reports that because donor and recipient site are identical, no PRI and no flap advancement were needed to achieve tension-free primary closure, reducing muscle tension and wound dehiscence risk. This is a design-level solution to the same clinical problem PRI exists to solve, achieved through a different mechanism than PRI-avoidance-by-open-healing (see [[implants/vertical-ridge-augmentation/wang-2025-mucoperiosteal-flap-healing-vertical-bone-titanium-mesh]]).

## Key Contributions

- Relocates the ridge's own coronal knife-edge segment as the bone shell, eliminating the second surgical (donor) site required by conventional bone-shell/block techniques.
- Reports primary wound closure achieved without periosteal releasing incisions or flap advancement, attributed to the absence of a distinct donor-to-recipient tension vector when donor and recipient sites coincide.
- Quantifies horizontal bone gain (3.6 mm / 3.4 mm at 1 mm / 5 mm below crest) sufficient to enable staged implant placement in all 20 treated sites, with a low (2/20) additional-grafting rate at implant placement.

## Methodology

Preliminary, uncontrolled, single-arm case series. 5 consecutive edentulous patients, 20 sites, severe anterior mandibular horizontal ridge atrophy with adequate ridge *height* (the deficiency treated is horizontal only). Technique: the coronal segment of the knife-edge ridge is horizontally sectioned and relocated laterally as an autologous bone shell over particulate graft at the same donor/recipient site; staged implant placement follows once ridge volume is restored. Follow-up 1-4 years. Outcomes: horizontal bone gain at 1 mm and 5 mm below the newly formed ridge crest; need for additional hard-tissue grafting at implant placement.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Horizontal bone gain, 1 mm below new crest | 3.6 ± 0.76 mm |
| Horizontal bone gain, 5 mm below new crest | 3.4 ± 0.92 mm |
| Sites with ridge volume adequate for staged implant placement | 20/20 |
| Sites requiring additional hard-tissue graft at implant placement | 2/20 |
| PRI / flap advancement required for primary closure | None reported |

## Limitations

- Very small, single-arm, uncontrolled case series (n=5 patients, 20 sites) — no comparison arm against conventional remote-donor bone-shell technique, ridge-split, or GBR, and no dehiscence-rate or complication data beyond the 2/20 additional-graft figure.
- Technique specifically requires adequate residual ridge *height* to have a coronal segment available to relocate; not generalizable to ridges with combined horizontal + vertical deficiency or insufficient height.
- Abstract-only ingestion: no histology, no statistical testing, no implant-level survival/loading outcome (endpoint is ridge restoration adequate for staged placement, not post-loading implant data). The "no PRI needed" claim is an authors' procedural observation, not measured against a PRI comparator within this study.
