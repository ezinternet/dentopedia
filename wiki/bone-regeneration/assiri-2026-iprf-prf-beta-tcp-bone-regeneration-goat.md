## Three-line Summary

Animal experimental study (18 male Najdi goats, 72 critical-size metacarpal bone defects, 4 groups/goat: spontaneous healing, β-TCP alone, β-TCP+PRF, β-TCP+i-PRF; micro-CT at 2/5/8 weeks).

By week 8, the β-TCP+i-PRF group had significantly higher new bone volume (BV-NFB 80.083 mm3) and mineral density (BMD-NFB 0.822 g/mm3) than β-TCP+PRF (62.665 mm3 / 0.802 g/mm3), β-TCP alone (58.995 mm3 / 0.799 g/mm3), and spontaneous healing (39.455 mm3 / 0.425 g/mm3); β-TCP+i-PRF also showed the fastest residual graft resorption (TCPV 15.319 mm3) and best trabecular microarchitecture.

i-PRF's fibrin network appears to synergize with β-TCP's osteoconductive scaffold to accelerate graft turnover and bone quality in a large-animal critical-size defect model, but this is animal (goat) micro-CT evidence only — no histology was performed, and clinical extrapolation to human GBR/socket defects is not yet established.

## Summary

Assiri et al. (2026, King Saud University) directly compared i-PRF and PRF, both mixed with β-TCP, against β-TCP alone and spontaneous healing, in a controlled critical-size defect model created in the metacarpal bone of 18 goats (4 standardized defects per goat, 6 goats sacrificed at each of 2/5/8 weeks). Using micro-CT quantification of new bone volume/density, residual graft volume/density, and trabecular microarchitecture, the study found that β-TCP+i-PRF consistently outperformed β-TCP+PRF and β-TCP alone in both the quantity (BV-NFB) and quality (BMD-NFB, Tb-Th, Tb-Sp, Tb-No) of newly formed bone by 8 weeks, while also showing the fastest resorption of the residual β-TCP carrier. **This is an animal (goat) micro-CT study only — no histology was performed and no human clinical outcomes are reported; the findings should be read as preclinical mechanistic support, not as a basis for direct clinical protocol change.**

## Key Contributions

- First reported direct 4-arm comparison (spontaneous healing / β-TCP / β-TCP+PRF / β-TCP+i-PRF) within the same critical-size defect model in a large-animal (goat) long bone, isolating i-PRF's incremental effect over PRF when both are combined with the same alloplastic carrier.
- Full micro-CT parameter panel (BV-NFB, BMD-NFB, TCPV, TCPMD, Tb-Th, Tb-Sp, Tb-No) tracked across 3 time points (2/5/8 weeks) spanning inflammatory, matrix-maturation, and remodeling phases of bone healing.
- Shows that i-PRF's advantage is not confined to new bone formation — it also accelerates resorption/turnover of the β-TCP carrier itself, consistent with a mechanistic model where i-PRF's fibrin network creates a more bioactive local milieu.

## Methodology

- **Animals**: 18 male Najdi goats (mean 30 ± 2 kg, 8 ± 1.5 months).
- **Defects**: 4 critical-size defects per left metacarpal bone (6.1 mm outer trephine, 10 mm spacing), standardized proximal→distal order:
  1. Negative control — spontaneous healing
  2. β-TCP alone (IngeniOs β-TCP Bioactive)
  3. β-TCP + PRF (Choukroun/Dohan protocol)
  4. β-TCP + i-PRF (Mourão protocol, 700 rpm × 3 min)
- All defects covered with resorbable collagen membrane (Bio-Gide).
- **Sacrifice**: 6 goats each at 2, 5, and 8 weeks; micro-CT (SkyScan 1173) with blinded ROI-based quantification.
- **Statistics**: Two-way then one-way ANOVA, Tukey/Games-Howell post-hoc, α=0.05; normality and variance-homogeneity assumptions confirmed.

## Results

| Metric (8 wk) | Negative control | β-TCP | β-TCP+PRF | β-TCP+i-PRF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BV-NFB (mm3) | 39.455 | 58.995 | 62.665 | **80.083** (highest, p=0.000) |
| BMD-NFB (g/mm3) | 0.425 | 0.799 | 0.802 | 0.822 (NS vs other β-TCP groups at 8wk) |
| Residual TCPV (mm3) | — | 24.972 | 22.013 | **15.319** (fastest resorption) |
| TCPMD (g/mm3) | — | 0.734 | 0.725 | 0.720 (NS across groups) |
| Trabecular microarchitecture (Tb-Th↑/Tb-Sp↓/Tb-No↑) | worst | mid | mid-good | **best**, closest to normal bone |

**Bottom line**: β-TCP+i-PRF > β-TCP+PRF ≈ β-TCP alone > spontaneous healing for new bone quantity and quality at 8 weeks; β-TCP and β-TCP+PRF were not significantly different from each other on most endpoints, suggesting the incremental benefit came specifically from the injectable (i-PRF) formulation rather than platelet concentrate addition per se.

**Limitations**: No histology/histomorphometry performed (micro-CT only — authors flag this explicitly); goat metacarpal long-bone critical-size defect is anatomically and mechanically distinct from human alveolar/jaw defects, so clinical extrapolation to dental GBR, sinus lift, or socket preservation protocols is not established; defect position was anatomically standardized rather than randomized (though consistent across all animals); modest per-arm sample size at each time point (n=6 defects/group/time point).
