## Three-line Summary

Updated PROSPERO-registered systematic review (140 studies: 42 RCTs + 98 CCTs/cohorts from 11,427 screened records; 10,456 implants; search through Jan 2024) of implant-supported fixed dental prostheses (IFDPs) in partially edentulous patients, computing follow-up- and implant-count-weighted cumulative survival across all 9 reported implant placement (Type 1 immediate / Type 2-3 early / Type 4 late) × loading (A immediate / B early / C conventional) protocol combinations.

Weighted survival ranged 91.6–100% (Type 1A 98.0%, 1B 91.6%, 1C 95.0%, 2-3A 97.8%, 2-3B 100%, 2-3C 94.0%, 4A 97.2%, 4B 97.9%, 4C 97.5%); a 4-tier validation framework (study design + sample size + ≤10% outcome-homogeneity threshold) classified 1A/1C/2-3A/2-3C/4A/4B/4C as scientifically-and-clinically validated (SCV), while 1B and 2-3B remained only "clinically documented" (CD) despite 91.6% and 100% survival respectively.

No meta-analysis was feasible (substantial clinical/methodological/reporting heterogeneity; moderate-to-high risk of bias in most included studies); the paper itself cautions that "SCV" labeling can mask clinically meaningful differences — Type 1A (98.0%) vs. Type 1B (91.6%) is roughly a four-fold difference in implant loss despite both carrying enough data to be formally classified.

## Summary

This systematic review updates a PROSPERO-registered evidence base (CRD42017062420) on implant placement and loading protocols for implant-supported fixed dental prostheses (IFDPs) in partially edentulous patients, expanding from an earlier (2018) review to 140 studies (42 RCTs, 98 CCTs/cohorts) and 10,456 implants. Because heterogeneity in study design and reporting precluded meta-analysis, the authors instead computed a follow-up- and implant-count-weighted cumulative survival rate for each of 9 placement (Type 1 immediate / Type 2-3 early / Type 4 late) × loading (A immediate / B early / C conventional) protocol combinations, ranging from 91.6% (Type 1B) to 100% (Type 2-3B, based on only 82 implants across 3 studies). A 4-tier validation framework combining study design, sample size, and outcome homogeneity classified seven of the nine protocols (1A, 1C, 2-3A, 2-3C, 4A, 4B, 4C) as scientifically-and-clinically validated (SCV), while early-loading protocols on immediate/early-placed implants (1B, 2-3B) remained only clinically documented despite adequate-to-high numeric survival. Clinically, the review reinforces that conventional and immediate loading of properly selected implants are both well-supported across placement timings, while early loading (1 week–2 months) — a biomechanically transitional window between primary and secondary stability — remains the least validated loading choice and warrants careful case selection, most clearly for Type 1B.

## Key Contributions
- Updates a PROSPERO-registered SR (CRD42017062420) with 140 studies (vs. the smaller 2018 predecessor) and follow-up extending to 120–180 months, enabling a formal 4-tier scientific/clinical validation classification (SCV/CWD/CD/CID) instead of raw survival percentages alone.
- Computes a follow-up-duration- and implant-count-weighted cumulative survival rate for all 9 reported placement×loading protocol combinations, with a priori boxplot/±3-SD outlier exclusion applied only to the quantitative weighting.
- Newly validates Type 2-3A (early placement + immediate loading), previously underreported, and reclassifies Type 1A from "clinically documented" (2018) to "scientifically and clinically validated" (this update).
- Explicitly cautions that SCV/CD labeling can mask clinically meaningful gaps: Type 1A (98.0%) vs. Type 1B (91.6%) represents roughly a four-fold difference in implant loss despite both carrying enough data to be formally classified.

## Methodology

Systematic review (PRISMA + IOM + Cochrane Handbook standards; PICO: partially edentulous patients, immediate/early vs. late/conventional placement-loading protocols, implant-prosthodontic survival/success) searching PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL through January 1, 2024. From 11,427 screened records, 140 studies (42 RCTs assessed with RoB2; 98 CCTs/cohorts assessed with the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale) met inclusion, contributing 10,456 implants. Because clinical/methodological/reporting heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis, survival was analyzed descriptively as a per-protocol weighted cumulative rate (weighted by follow-up duration × implant count), and protocols were assigned one of four validation tiers (SCV/CWD/CD/CID) based on study design, sample size, and outcome homogeneity (≤10% between-study survival variation = OH+).
