## Three-line Summary

In vivo rabbit study (n=48 osteotomies, 4 groups) testing cylindrical vs conical drills × continuous vs intermittent movement on osteotomy temperature and 30-day bone healing.

Intermittent movement (0→3 mm → 0→5 mm → 0→8 mm, three passes) reduced temperature by 2.6°C for cylindrical drills (p=0.001, −37%) but showed no benefit for conical drills (p=0.977, Δ−0.008°C); conical drills outperformed cylindrical on all metrics regardless of technique.

Drill geometry determines whether peck drilling helps: for conical drills, the drill itself controls heat sufficiently; for cylindrical drills, intermittent technique is a meaningful complement.

## 세줄요약

In vivo 토끼 연구(n=48 골절제, 4군): 원통형 vs 원추형 드릴 × 연속 vs 간헐 동작(Peck drilling)이 골절제 온도·30일 골 치유에 미치는 영향.

간헐 동작(0→3mm / 0→5mm / 0→8mm 3단계)은 원통형 드릴에서 2.6°C 감소(p=0.001, −37%)했으나 원추형에서는 효과 없음(p=0.977); 원추형은 기법 무관 모든 지표 우수.

드릴 형태가 Peck drilling 효익을 결정: 원추형은 드릴 자체가 발열을 통제하므로 기법 의존성 낮음; 원통형에서는 간헐 동작이 의미 있는 보완책.

## Summary

This in vivo animal study compared the thermal and histological effects of cylindrical versus conical drill sequences, each performed with either continuous or intermittent (peck) movement, during implant osteotomy preparation in rabbit tibiae. The intermittent protocol used three standardized depth passes: 0→3 mm, 0→5 mm, and 0→8 mm (final depth), with drills physically marked at the transition points.

For cylindrical drills, intermittent movement significantly reduced the peak temperature rise (ΔT 6.91→4.30°C, p=0.001) and improved new bone formation at 30 days (1.00→1.48 mm², p=0.003). For conical drills, however, the technique made no significant difference on temperature (ΔT 2.78 vs 2.77°C, p=0.977) or histology. Conical drills outperformed cylindrical drills across all thermal and histological metrics, regardless of movement technique. All four groups remained below the 47°C necrosis threshold. Irrigation was room-temperature saline throughout; no chilled irrigation arm was included.

## Key Contributions

- **Peck interval defined in vivo**: 0→3 mm / 0→5 mm / 0→8 mm — the only in vivo study to specify peck depth increments for dental implant osteotomy.
- **Technique × geometry interaction**: Peck drilling significantly mitigates heat for cylindrical drills but adds negligible benefit for conical drills — refining blanket "use peck drilling" guidance to be drill-type-conditional.
- **Histological confirmation**: Intermittent technique improves new bone formation and reduces PMN cells even when temperature differences are modest — suggests mechanical benefits (chip clearance, reduced wall friction) beyond thermal effects.
- **Conical drills outperform across all metrics**: Geometry contributes more to thermal management than movement technique in this study.

## Methodology

- **Design**: In vivo, New Zealand rabbits (n=12, bilateral tibiae)
- **N**: 48 osteotomies (12 per group)
- **Groups**: G1 cylindrical+continuous / G2 cylindrical+intermittent / G3 conical+continuous / G4 conical+intermittent
- **Drill sequences**: Cylindrical 2.2/2.8/3.5 mm @ 800/600/500 rpm; Conical same diameters @ 1500 rpm
- **Peck protocol (G2, G4)**: Pass 1 → 3 mm, Pass 2 → 5 mm, Pass 3 → 8 mm (drill marked at 3 mm and 5 mm)
- **Irrigation**: External saline 23 ± 1°C, continuous
- **Temperature**: Type K thermocouple 1 mm from osteotomy wall, 3 mm depth; max ΔT from baseline (33.5°C)
- **Histology at 30 days**: Picrosirius hematoxylin; new bone formation (mm²) bilateral cortical sections; PMN count (six 500×500 µm quadrants, ImageJ)
- **Statistics**: One-way ANOVA + Bonferroni; p<0.05

## Results

**Temperature variation (ΔT):**

| Group | ΔT °C | SD | vs G1 p |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 cylindrical + continuous | 6.91 | ±1.4 | — |
| G2 cylindrical + intermittent | 4.30 | ±1.3 | 0.001 |
| G3 conical + continuous | 2.78 | ±0.6 | <0.0001 |
| G4 conical + intermittent | 2.77 | ±0.7 | <0.0001 |

G3 vs G4: p=0.977 (NS). Recovery to baseline: G1–G2 ~20 s; G3–G4 ~10 s.

**New bone formation at 30 days (mm²):**
G1: 1.00 ± 0.3 → G2: 1.48 ± 0.3 (p=0.003) → G3: 2.20 ± 0.4 → G4: 2.43 ± 0.4 (G1 vs G4 p<0.0001; G3 vs G4 p=0.163 NS)

**PMN cell count:**
G1: 62.4 ± 5.9 → G2: 50.7 ± 4.2 (p<0.0001) → G3: 44.4 ± 3.7 → G4: 42.4 ± 3.7 (G3 vs G4 p=0.174 NS)
