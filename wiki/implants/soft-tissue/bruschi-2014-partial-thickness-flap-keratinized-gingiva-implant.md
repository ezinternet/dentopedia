## Three-line Summary

1. A partial thickness flap (PTF) raised from residual keratinized mucosa and apically repositioned at implant placement increased peri-implant keratinized mucosa (KM) width and thickness in all 131 implants (85 patients) without connective tissue or free gingival grafts.
2. Mean KM was 7.26 ± 2.01 mm at 1 year and 7.37 ± 2.12 mm at 4 years — gains were stable over the entire observation period.
3. Implant survival was 87.79% at 4 years; this single-stage technique allows simultaneous tissue augmentation and implant placement with no separate grafting procedure.

## Summary

Bruschi et al. (2014) report a 4-year prospective clinical study evaluating a graft-free partial thickness flap technique for augmenting peri-implant keratinized mucosa at the time of implant placement. Eighty-five patients received 131 implants in edentulous ridges (103 mandibular, 28 maxillary). Prior to implant placement, residual keratinized mucosa width was measured buccal-to-palatal/lingual with a periodontal probe. A palatal or lingual incision was made to raise a partial thickness flap carrying all available keratinized tissue; after implant placement the flap was repositioned apically and secured with loose periosteal sutures, allowing secondary intention healing of the palatal/lingual donor area.

Keratinized tissue levels were assessed at baseline, 6 months, 1 year, and annually through 4 years. Clinical gain was confirmed in all cases, with mean KM width of 7.26 ± 2.01 mm at 1 year and 7.37 ± 2.12 mm at 4 years — a near-plateau indicating durable stability. The implant survival rate at 4 years was 87.79%.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes proof-of-concept for graft-free peri-implant KM augmentation using apically repositioned PTF at implant placement
- Demonstrates 4-year stability of KM gains (mean change <0.2 mm from year 1 to year 4)
- Confirms KM gain in 100% of treated cases — no regression observed
- Provides diameter-stratified outcome data (detail extractable from full text only)
- Positions this technique as a time-efficient single-stage alternative to separate soft tissue grafting

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Study design | Prospective clinical study with 4-year follow-up |
| Sample size | 85 patients, 131 implants |
| Location | Mandible: 103 (78.6%), Maxilla: 28 (21.4%) |
| Setting | Private practice, Rome, Italy |
| Technique | PTF raised lingual/palatal → implant placed → flap apically repositioned → periosteal sutures |
| Primary outcome | KM width measured with periodontal probe |
| Timepoints | Baseline, 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, 3 years, 4 years |
| Control | None (single-arm descriptive study) |
