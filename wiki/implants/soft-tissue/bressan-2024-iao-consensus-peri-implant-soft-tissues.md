## Three-line Summary

This is a 2024 IAO (Italian Academy of Osseointegration) consensus conference synthesizing 4 pre-registered PRISMA/PROSPERO systematic reviews on peri-implant soft tissue: keratinized mucosa (KM), collagen matrix vs. autogenous graft, abutment material, and soft-tissue thickness vs. marginal bone loss (MBL).

The assembly agreed supra-periosteal KM favorably affects peri-implant health/esthetics/cleanability/pain but not infection-unrelated crestal bone loss; free gingival graft (FGG) is best for KM gain, connective tissue graft (CTG) beats volume-stable collagen matrix (VCMX) for thickness gain though matrices cut morbidity/surgical time; titanium vs. zirconia abutment showed no consistent soft-tissue-health difference; and soft-tissue thickness vs. MBL correlation remained unresolved (review found none, but the assembly could not reach consensus).

Clinically this is a vote-based expert consensus (not new primary data) built on 4 underlying systematic reviews of variable quality (heterogeneity, few RCTs, some CCTs at high risk of bias), so it should be read as a synthesis snapshot rather than definitive proof.

## Summary

The Italian Academy of Osseointegration (IAO) convened a Consensus Conference (meeting held 15–16 October 2021) to evaluate the importance of peri-implant soft tissue across four dimensions: keratinized mucosa (KM), soft-tissue augmentation matrix vs. autogenous graft, abutment material, and soft-tissue thickness vs. marginal bone loss (MBL). Rather than presenting new primary data, the paper reports the results of four independently pre-registered (PROSPERO) systematic reviews and the formal votes the assembly reached (or failed to reach) on each topic. The consensus introduces a clinically useful terminological refinement — splitting KM into supra-periosteal (bone-attached, immobile) and crestal (mobile) subtypes — and provides a practical augmentation-technique hierarchy (FGG for width, CTG for thickness, matrices for lower morbidity/better esthetics/shorter surgery). On abutment material, no evidence-based superiority of titanium or zirconia was found for soft-tissue outcomes. Notably, on the soft-tissue-thickness-vs-MBL question, the assembly's expert judgment diverged from the underlying review's null finding, and trial sequential analysis showed the pooled evidence was markedly underpowered.

## Key Contributions

1. **Keratinized mucosa (KM) refinement and consensus**: KM ≥2 mm associated with less marginal inflammation, plaque, recession, and biological complications, and less patient discomfort during oral hygiene — but no difference in implant failure, PPD, or bone levels. The assembly formally distinguished **supra-periosteal KM** (bone-attached, immobile) from **crestal KM** (mobile), concluding supra-periosteal KM favorably affects peri-implant health/esthetics but not infection-unrelated bone crest resorption; both subtypes improve cleanability and reduce pain.
2. **Augmentation technique hierarchy (matrix vs. autograft, network meta-analysis)**: FGG is best for KM width gain; CTG outperforms volume-stable collagen matrix (VCMX) for thickness gain; but collagen matrices (XCM/VCMX) reduce surgical time, patient morbidity, and can give better esthetic camouflage than autografts.
3. **Abutment material consensus**: Titanium vs. zirconia showed no consistent difference in peri-implant soft-tissue health or recession; effect on crestal bone loss remains controversial/unresolved. Material choice should be esthetics/gingival-phenotype-driven, not evidence-driven.
4. **Soft-tissue thickness vs. MBL — unresolved**: The underlying systematic review found no correlation between initial soft-tissue thickness and MBL, but trial sequential analysis showed the pooled sample was <1/3 of the required information size (RIS), and the expert assembly explicitly failed to reach consensus on this point based on clinical experience — a rare case of a consensus document recording panel–literature disagreement rather than forcing false concordance.

## Methodology

Four PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered systematic reviews were independently conducted (search cutoffs: KM review May 2021; matrix review April 2021, updated April 2023; abutment-material review February 2022; thickness/MBL review May 2022), each addressing a distinct PICO-T question. Databases searched varied by review but generally included MEDLINE/PubMed, CENTRAL, EMBASE, Scopus, and Web of Science. Results were presented and discussed at the IAO Consensus Conference (15–16 October 2021), where active IAO members voted on consensus statements for each topic; where votes and review findings diverged, this was recorded explicitly rather than reconciled. The matrix-vs-autograft review used network meta-analysis; the thickness/MBL review applied trial sequential analysis (TSA) to assess whether accumulated evidence met the required information size (RIS).

## Results

| Topic | Systematic review finding | IAO consensus statement |
|---|---|---|
| Keratinized mucosa (KM) | KM ≥2 mm → less inflammation/plaque/recession/complications/discomfort; no difference in implant failure, PPD, bone levels. Evidence limited (1 RCT, 2 low-risk-of-bias studies) | Supra-periosteal KM favors peri-implant health/esthetics/cleanability/pain relief, but not infection-unrelated bone crest resorption; crestal KM also aids cleaning/pain |
| Matrix vs. autograft | FGG best for KM width; CTG best for thickness; matrices reduce morbidity/surgical time, XCM improves esthetic camouflage | Consensus aligned with review: FGG first choice for KM width, CTG for thickness, matrices favored when morbidity/time/esthetics prioritized |
| Abutment material (Ti vs. Zr) | Zirconia numerically better in most outcomes but high heterogeneity; no reliable superiority established | No conclusion on MBL effect; soft-tissue health/recession not influenced by material; choice driven by esthetics/phenotype |
| Soft-tissue thickness vs. MBL | No correlation found; TSA: RIS >3× actual pooled N (markedly underpowered) | Assembly could NOT reach consensus despite the review's null finding; more confounder-controlled RCTs recommended |
