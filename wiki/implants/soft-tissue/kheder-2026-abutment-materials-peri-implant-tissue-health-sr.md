## Three-line Summary

PRISMA-compliant systematic review (PubMed + Scopus 2019–2026; 5,362→13 clinical studies) comparing titanium, zirconia, and PEEK abutments on peri-implant tissue health outcomes (MBL, soft tissue response, inflammation, survival rate); qualitative synthesis due to heterogeneity; ROBINS-I bias assessment.
Titanium: consistent minimal MBL, low inflammation, highest long-term predictability; Zirconia: comparable biology with superior soft tissue response and aesthetics (anterior); PEEK: promising reduced inflammation and biocompatibility in short-term, insufficient for routine use.
Clinical recommendation: titanium for posterior/mechanical-demand zones; zirconia preferred for esthetic zones; PEEK requires long-term trial data before routine adoption.

## 세줄요약

PRISMA 체계적 문헌고찰(5,362→13편 임상 연구, 2019–2026), Ti·ZrO₂·PEEK 어버트먼트 임플란트 주위 조직 건강(MBL, 연조직, 염증, 생존율) 비교; 이질성으로 정성 합성; ROBINS-I 비뚤림 평가.

## Summary

This PRISMA-compliant systematic review included 13 clinical human studies (from 5,362 initially identified) comparing titanium, zirconia, and PEEK abutments on peri-implant tissue health. All studies were published between 2019 and 2026, and risk of bias was assessed with ROBINS-I (most low-moderate). A meta-analysis was not feasible due to substantial heterogeneity. Titanium abutments showed the most robust long-term evidence: consistent minimal marginal bone loss, stable peri-implant tissue, and high survival across follow-ups up to 12 years. Zirconia demonstrated comparable biological outcomes — in several studies, significantly lower probing depth and BOP than titanium — with clear aesthetic superiority in esthetic zones. PEEK showed promising early biological data (reduced inflammation vs titanium) but only short-term evidence in a single pilot study, insufficient to support routine use.

## Key Contributions

- Clinical-only PRISMA SR: excludes in-vitro/animal studies; 13 human studies, ROBINS-I bias
- Savitha 2024 (RCT, n=90, 1yr): ZrO₂ significantly lowest PD, BOP, plaque index vs Ti and gold alloy
- ZrO₂ and Ti comparable for MBL; ZrO₂ advantageous for soft tissue and esthetics
- PEEK evidence limited to 1 short-term pilot — cannot yet be used clinically for zirconia-comparable expectations
- Clear clinical routing: posterior/load-bearing → Ti; esthetic zones → ZrO₂; PEEK → experimental
