## Three-line Summary

Case report of a 19-year-old male with a severely deficient maxillary left central incisor site (failed prior implant, thin scalloped biotype, scarring, low labial frenum) managed through a staged four-phase tissue reconstruction.
Soft tissue was optimized first (frenectomy + APF + CTG), then GBR (Tutoplast allograft + titanium-reinforced Gore-tex membrane), then delayed implant placement (Astra 3.5×13 mm tapered), then 10 months of provisional contouring at initially 30%-reduced emergence profile, then final screw-retained restoration using a customized impression coping.
At 2.5-year follow-up, peri-implant tissue and papillae were stable; limitations included gold abutment–related gingival discoloration and minor #11 recession from cumulative surgeries, both non-progressive.

## Summary

This Korean case report describes the staged management of an esthetically demanding maxillary left central incisor (#21) site in a 19-year-old male with multiple complicating factors: a previously failed implant, thin scalloped gingival biotype, low labial frenum with scarring, and buccal recession of #22. The staged protocol prioritized soft tissue quality first — frenectomy, apically positioned flap (APF), and connective tissue graft (CTG) via envelope technique — before any hard tissue augmentation, recognizing that GBR flap closure over unprepared, scar-laden tissue would compromise keratinized tissue width and healing. After 4 months of soft tissue maturation, GBR was performed with Tutoplast allograft and a titanium-reinforced non-resorbable Gore-tex membrane (fixed with 4 mm screws) to rebuild the ~1.5 mm–wide residual ridge. Eight months post-GBR, a tapered Astra Tech 3.5×13 mm implant was placed 3 mm below the CEJ with Bio-Oss + Ossixplus for buccal volume maintenance. The provisional phase used a 30%-under-contoured screw-retained crown expanded biweekly over 10 months to sculpt peri-implant mucosa without papilla collapse. The final impression used a customized impression coping (VPS + pattern resin transferred from provisional abutment contour) to preserve the established soft tissue topography for the final screw-retained UCLA abutment restoration. At 2.5-year follow-up, peri-implant tissue and papillae remained stable.

## Key Contributions

- **Soft-tissue-first rationale**: Performing APF + CTG before GBR prevents keratinized tissue loss when the GBR flap is advanced coronally and establishes a healthy tissue bed for subsequent hard tissue work
- **Thin scalloped biotype protocol**: Early soft tissue augmentation reduces recession risk in thin biotype patients who are particularly vulnerable after multiple surgeries
- **Staged GBR with non-resorbable membrane**: Titanium-reinforced Gore-tex fixed with screws provides stable space for allograft consolidation over 8 months in a severely deficient ridge (~1.5 mm width)
- **Provisional contouring protocol**: Starting provisional at 30% under-contoured emergence profile and expanding biweekly over 10 months allows gradual mucosal adaptation without papilla regression
- **Customized impression coping**: Transferring the provisional's subgingival contour to the final impression (via VPS + pattern resin over fixture analog) ensures the laboratory reproduces the exact soft tissue profile

## Methodology

- **Design**: Single-patient case report
- **Patient**: 19-year-old male, #21 site, prior block graft + implant removed at 4 months due to mobility; thin scalloped biotype, low labial frenum, scar tissue, #22 buccal recession
- **Sequence**: Soft tissue surgery → 4 months → GBR → 8 months → implant placement → 5 months → 2nd stage + provisional → 10 months → final impression → restoration → 2.5-year follow-up
- **Outcomes assessed**: Peri-implant tissue health, papilla fill, gingival margin position, occlusal stability
