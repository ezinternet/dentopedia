## Three-line Summary

PRISMA 2020 SR+MA of RCTs only (10 RCTs, 3–60 months; 6 pooled) comparing narrowed (concave/curved), straight (linear/convex/cylindrical), and widened abutment profiles; RoB 2.0 and GRADE (overall moderate certainty).

Narrowed vs straight: no significant difference in MBL (SMD 0.08, p=0.66), probing depth (SMD −0.05, p=0.80), or PES (SMD −0.12, p=0.51); no differences in secondary biological or esthetic outcomes including mid-facial mucosal zenith; technical complications rare and mostly in concave abutments.

Abutment shape did not significantly change peri-implant tissue or esthetic outcomes in current RCTs — but lumping convex into "straight" may dilute a convex-vs-concave effect.

## Summary

This is the most direct challenge in the wiki to the thesis that emergence-profile **shape** is the dominant abutment lever. Pooling only RCTs, the authors found that narrowed (concave) abutments did not differ from straight ones on bone loss, probing depth, or pink esthetics, and secondary outcomes — including the mid-facial mucosal zenith — were also similar. Concave abutments carried most of the (rare) technical complications.

How it fits with the rest of the evidence. Two other SR+MAs we hold reach different conclusions on related contrasts: Liu 2025 found a significant MBL increase when EA >30° and more crestal bone remodeling with convex/wide abutments; Atieh 2023 found convex contours associated with more peri-implantitis (RR 2.32). In my reading the conflict is partly definitional. This review's "straight" group mixes convex with linear and cylindrical designs, so it compares concave against a heterogeneous pool rather than against convex. Its comparison axis is also abutment profile, not crown emergence angle. Until the full text shows the per-RCT categories, treat this as "concave vs linear makes little difference over 3–60 months", not "shape does not matter".

## Key Contributions

- RCT-only meta-analysis of abutment profile.
- Null pooled effects for MBL, PD, PES.
- Technical-complication signal in concave abutments.

## Methodology

- 8 databases plus grey literature; 10 RCTs; 6 pooled; RoB 2.0 (high 2, some concerns 5, low 3); GRADE.
