## Three-line Summary

Prospective case series of 40 patients (42 implants) with thin vertical soft tissue (≤2 mm), augmented with acellular dermal matrix (AlloDerm) membranes during submerged implant placement and re-evaluated at 3-month second-stage surgery.
Mean vertical soft tissue thickness increased from 1.54 ± 0.51 mm to 3.75 ± 0.54 mm — a mean gain of 2.21 ± 0.85 mm (P < 0.001); all 40 allografts healed successfully.
ADM appears effective for vertical soft-tissue thickening in the submerged-placement setting, but the uncontrolled single-arm design and abstract-only record limit the strength of inference.

## Summary

This prospective case series (Puisys et al., Clinical Oral Implants Research 2015;26(4):465-470) tested whether an acellular dermal matrix (ADM) membrane can augment **vertical** peri-implant soft-tissue thickness when placed at the time of submerged (two-stage) implant placement. Forty patients with a thin soft-tissue phenotype (≤2 mm) received 40 AlloDerm membranes and 42 laser-modified internal-hex implants; healing abutments were connected after 3 months and the augmented thickness was measured with a periodontal probe. Mean thickness rose from 1.54 ± 0.51 mm to 3.75 ± 0.54 mm, a mean gain of 2.21 ± 0.85 mm (P < 0.001), with all 40 allografts healing successfully. The study therefore supports ADM as a viable vertical-thickening option in the submerged-placement workflow, while its single-arm design, 3-month horizon, and abstract-only availability keep it at hypothesis-generating rather than comparative strength.

## Key Contributions

- Tests ADM for the **vertical thickness** goal at the **submerged-placement / second-stage** time point, rather than the more commonly studied keratinized-mucosa-width goal.
- Enrolls a defined thin-phenotype population (vertical soft tissue ≤2 mm) with a homogeneous implant platform (BioHorizons Tapered Laser Lok).
- Reports both absolute pre/post thickness and derived gain, with all 40 allografts healing successfully at abutment connection.
- Supplies primary, procedure-specific data that contextualizes later syntheses of ADM among soft-tissue substitutes (Tommasato 2024 NMA: ADM competitive for buccal thickness in the first ~3 months).

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective, single-arm case series (no control group).
- **Population**: 40 patients (15 male, 25 female; mean age 42.5 ± 1.7 years); inclusion required vertical soft tissue thickness ≤2 mm.
- **Intervention**: 40 acellular dermal matrix-derived allogenic membranes (AlloDerm, BioHorizons) placed at submerged implant placement; 42 laser-modified-surface internal-hex implants (BioHorizons Tapered Laser Lok).
- **Outcome assessment**: At 3 months, healing abutments connected and augmented soft-tissue thickness measured with a periodontal probe; vertical soft-tissue gain calculated.
- **Statistics**: Mann-Whitney U-test, significance set at 0.05.
- **Evidence level note**: `prospective` — a 40-patient prospective case series with scheduled follow-up; `case-report` is reserved here for n < 10.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Allograft healing | 40/40 healed successfully |
| Pre-augmentation thickness | 1.54 ± 0.51 mm SD (range 0.5–2.0 mm; median 1.75 mm) |
| Thickness at 3 months post-placement | 3.75 ± 0.54 mm SD (range 3.0–5.0 mm; median 4.0 mm) |
| Difference between medians | Statistically significant, P < 0.001 |
| Mean thickness gain | 2.21 ± 0.85 mm SD (range 1.0–4.5 mm; median 2.0 mm) |

**Limitations (abstract-only record)**: no control group; only 3-month follow-up; single time-point probing rather than profilometry/3D imaging; 42 implants in 40 patients with per-patient clustering not addressed; Mann-Whitney U-test reported for a within-subject pre/post comparison without justification in the abstract.
