## Three-line Summary

Narrative review with a clinical decision flowchart on the subgingival (critical, subcritical) and supragingival contours of the implant restorative emergence profile (IREP) for single-tooth implants in periodontally healthy patients.

Subgingival contours should mimic root/cervical crown anatomy where possible but depend on 3D position: a convex facial critical contour is often needed for a palatally or incisally placed implant to support the margin, a flat or concave contour for a facially placed implant, and the subcritical zone may be undercontoured to thicken and stabilize the soft-tissue cuff.

Converts "concave is better" into a position-conditional rule; expert synthesis without new data.

## Summary

Chu, Kan, Wang and co-authors argue the peri-implant seal is mechanically weaker than the periodontium (no Sharpey's fibers), so restorative contour matters more around implants. Their key move is to make contour depend on where the implant sits. If the implant is palatal or incisal, the facial critical contour often has to be convex to hold the gingival margin up at the right level. If the implant is too facial, a flat or concave critical contour is recommended. Where support is not needed, the subcritical zone is undercontoured to give the soft tissue room to thicken.

Read against the RCT evidence: Siegenthaler 2022 found convex profiles caused far more recession (OR 12.69). This paper does not contradict that — it narrows it. Convex critical contour is a tool for palatally placed implants with soft tissue to support, not a default.

## Key Contributions

- IREP split into critical / subcritical / supragingival.
- Position-dependent contour rules (palatal/incisal → convex critical; facial → flat/concave).
- Subcritical undercontouring for soft-tissue volume.
- Decision flowchart (in full text).
