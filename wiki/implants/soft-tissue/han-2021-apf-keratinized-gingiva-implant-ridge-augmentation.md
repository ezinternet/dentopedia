## Three-line Summary

Han 2021 prospectively followed 79 patients (203 implants) receiving a full-thickness apically positioned flap (fAPF) at implant placement, divided by whether concurrent ridge augmentation (RA) was performed. Despite the RA group starting with significantly less keratinized gingiva at baseline (0.82 mm vs 1.68 mm), multivariable GEE showed no significant difference in KG gain, shrinkage amount, or shrinkage ratio between groups. The total KG shrinkage ratio was 42.43%, and fAPF is concluded to be reliable for KG augmentation regardless of RA status.

## 세줄요약

Han 2021은 임플란트 식립 시 전층 치조정접근 판막 (Full-thickness Apically Positioned Flap, fAPF)을 적용한 79명(임플란트 203개)을 각화치은 (Keratinized Gingiva, KG) 및 골증대술 (Ridge Augmentation, RA) 유무에 따라 전향적으로 비교했다. 기저 KG폭은 RA군에서 유의하게 낮았으나(0.82 mm vs 1.68 mm, p<0.001), 다변량 일반화 추정 방정식 (Generalized Estimating Equation, GEE) 결과 KG 증가량·수축량·수축률 모두 군 간 유의차 없음(p>0.05). KG 전체 수축률 42.43%; RA 여부와 무관하게 fAPF가 신뢰할 수 있는 KG 증대 술식임을 확인했다.

## Summary

Single-center prospective study (April 2017–April 2019) evaluating whether ridge augmentation affects keratinized gingiva gain when using a full-thickness apically positioned flap at the time of implant placement.

- **Group A** (n = subset of 79 patients): One-stage implant surgery without ridge augmentation
- **Group B** (n = subset of 79 patients): Two-stage implant surgery with ridge augmentation
- KG width measured with paper ruler at baseline, 1 week, 3 weeks, 3 months, and 6 months
- Multivariable GEE model used to account for multiple implants per patient

The primary finding is that the fAPF reliably gains KG regardless of RA status, with Group B demonstrating meaningful KG gain despite starting with less than half the baseline tissue width of Group A.

## Key Contributions

1. **RA does not impair fAPF KG gain**: Despite a large and significant baseline difference in KG width (1.68 mm vs 0.82 mm), KG gain was statistically equivalent between groups. This provides direct clinical guidance for combined RA + fAPF procedures.

2. **Quantified KG shrinkage rate**: Total shrinkage ratio of 42.43% across the cohort provides a reference figure for surgical planning — clinicians should anticipate roughly 40% tissue contraction when planning fAPF outcomes.

3. **KG gain figures**:
   - Group A (no RA): 1.92 ± 1.67 mm
   - Group B (RA): 1.48 ± 1.36 mm
   - Difference: not significant (p > 0.05)

4. **GEE approach**: Proper handling of the clustered nature of the data (multiple implants per patient) strengthens the statistical validity.

## Results

| Outcome | Group A (no RA) | Group B (RA) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline KG | 1.68 mm | 0.82 mm | < 0.001 |
| KG gain | 1.92 ± 1.67 mm | 1.48 ± 1.36 mm | > 0.05 (NS) |
| Shrinkage amount | — | — | > 0.05 (NS) |
| Shrinkage ratio | — | — | > 0.05 (NS) |
| **Total shrinkage ratio** | **42.43%** | | |

Key interpretation: despite significantly lower baseline KG in the RA group, both groups achieved comparable KG gain — demonstrating that RA does not compromise the effectiveness of fAPF for KG augmentation.
