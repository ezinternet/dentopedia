## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 9 KM augmentation studies and 11 soft tissue volume studies around implants and partially edentulous sites (EAO 2014 Consensus Supplement) found APF/vestibuloplasty + FGG/SCTG to be the best-documented approach for KM width gain (1.4–3.3 mm; p < 0.05 vs controls). Collagen matrix yielded less KM gain but had statistically better surgery time and patient morbidity than SCTG (2 RCTs). SCTG was the best-documented method for soft tissue volume gain and aesthetic improvement at immediate implant sites.

## 세줄요약

2014 EAO 컨센서스 보충판에 수록된 체계적 문헌고찰로, 각화점막(Keratinized Mucosa, KM) 관련 9편 및 연조직 두께(volume) 관련 11편을 포함(메타분석 불가, 이질성 과다). 치조정접근 판막/전정성형술(Apically Positioned Flap/Vestibuloplasty, APF/V) + 유리치은이식(Free Gingival Graft, FGG)/상피하 결합조직이식(Subepithelial Connective Tissue Graft, SCTG)이 KM 폭 1.4–3.3 mm 증가로 최우수(대조군 대비 p < 0.05). 콜라겐 매트릭스(Collagen Matrix, CM)는 KM 증가량 열등하지만 수술시간·이환율에서 통계적 우위(2건 RCT); SCTG는 연조직 두께 및 심미(유두 충전, 변연점막 수위) 최선.

## Summary

This systematic review, published as part of the EAO 2014 Consensus Conference Supplement in the Journal of Clinical Periodontology (Suppl 15), searched MEDLINE for human studies evaluating soft tissue augmentation at implant sites and partially edentulous areas. Due to heterogeneity between studies, no meta-analysis was performed.

Nine studies examining keratinized mucosa (KM) augmentation and 11 studies examining soft tissue volume were included. The review synthesized outcomes across two main clinical goals: (1) increasing the width of KM, and (2) increasing soft tissue thickness/volume and aesthetics.

For KM augmentation, apically positioned flap/vestibuloplasty (APF/V) combined with a graft material — either FGG, SCTG, or collagen matrix (CM) — produced KM gains of 1.4–3.3 mm. APF/V + FGG or SCTG was statistically superior to controls (APF/V alone; no treatment; p < 0.05). Two RCTs specifically compared CM to SCTG and found CM significantly less demanding on surgery time and patient morbidity, though producing less KM gain.

For soft tissue volume and aesthetics, SCTG was the best-documented technique, showing better papilla fill and higher marginal mucosal levels at immediate implant sites compared to non-grafted sites. This remains the foundational reference supporting SCTG use for aesthetic outcomes at implant sites.

## Key Contributions

1. **Gold standard established**: APF/V + FGG/SCTG confirmed as best-documented and most effective method for KM width augmentation at implant sites (1.4–3.3 mm gain; p < 0.05 vs controls)
2. **CM trade-off quantified**: Collagen matrix demonstrated statistically significantly less morbidity and shorter surgery time than SCTG (2 RCTs), at the cost of reduced KM gain — establishing the patient-comfort case for CM
3. **SCTG for volume and aesthetics**: SCTG confirmed as best-documented method for soft tissue volume gain; superior papilla fill and marginal mucosal levels at immediate implant sites vs non-grafted controls
4. **Methodological gap identified**: High heterogeneity prevented meta-analysis, setting the stage for subsequent network meta-analytic approaches (Tavelli 2021, Oh 2024)
5. **EAO consensus anchor**: Published in the 2014 EAO Consensus Supplement, giving these findings formal consensus-level weight in European implantology guidelines

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review; MEDLINE search
- **Inclusion criteria**: Human studies evaluating augmentation of KM width or soft tissue volume at implant sites or partially edentulous areas
- **Studies included**: 9 (KM augmentation) + 11 (soft tissue volume)
- **Meta-analysis**: Not performed — heterogeneity between studies was prohibitive
- **Outcome measures**: KM width (mm), soft tissue volume change, surgery time, patient morbidity, aesthetics (papilla fill, marginal mucosal levels)
- **Comparators evaluated**: APF/V alone, FGG, SCTG, collagen matrix (CM), allogenic dermal matrix, no treatment
- **RCT evidence**: Two RCTs specifically addressed CM vs. SCTG for KM augmentation; primary source for morbidity/surgery time comparisons

## Results

### KM Augmentation (9 studies)

| Intervention | KM Gain | Statistical Result |
|---|---|---|
| APF/V + FGG or SCTG | 1.4–3.3 mm | p < 0.05 vs controls (APF/V alone; no treatment) |
| APF/V + CM | Less than FGG/SCTG | Lower morbidity; less surgery time (2 RCTs, p < 0.05) |
| APF/V alone | Minimal | Reference/control |

### Soft Tissue Volume (11 studies)

| Intervention | Outcome |
|---|---|
| SCTG | Best documented; superior volume gain |
| SCTG vs non-grafted | Better papilla fill + higher marginal mucosal levels at immediate implant sites |

### Summary Trade-offs

- **Efficacy (KM)**: FGG/SCTG > CM
- **Morbidity/time**: CM < SCTG (favors CM)
- **Volume and aesthetics**: SCTG best documented
- **Evidence quality**: RCT-based for CM vs. SCTG morbidity comparison; observational/mixed for most volume data
