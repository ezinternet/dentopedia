## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 9 studies (11 papers: 4 randomized clinical trials and 5 prospective controlled clinical trials) comparing zirconia (Zr) vs titanium (Ti) abutments directly within the same patient, identified by electronic and hand searches of English-language journals through December 1, 2014.

Meta-analysis was feasible only for soft tissue color, which was significantly superior for Zr abutments; no statistically significant Zr-vs-Ti differences were found for soft tissue recession, probing depths, bleeding on probing, marginal bone level, or patient-reported outcome, and one study reported significantly higher pink esthetic score (PES) at Zr implants with Zr abutments.

The authors conclude that the research does not support any obvious advantage of Ti or Zr abutments over each other, while noting a significant tendency for Zr abutments to evoke a better color response of peri-implant mucosa and superior PES esthetics — the retrieved abstract reports no numeric effect sizes or p-values.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰·메타분석(Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis, SR/MA) — 2014년 12월 1일까지 영문 학술지에 대한 전자·수기 검색으로 찾아낸 9개 연구(11편 논문: 무작위 대조 시험(Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) 4편 + 전향적 대조 임상시험(Controlled Clinical Trial, CCT) 5편)에서 같은 환자 내 지르코니아(Zirconia, Zr)·티타늄(Titanium, Ti) 지대주(abutment)를 직접 비교.

메타분석은 연조직 색상에만 가능했으며 Zr 지대주가 유의하게 우수; 연조직 퇴축·탐침깊이(Probing Depth, PD)·탐침 시 출혈(Bleeding on Probing, BOP)·변연골수준(Marginal Bone Level, MBL)·환자 보고결과(Patient-Reported Outcome, PRO)에서는 유의한 차이 없음, 1편의 연구에서는 Zr 임플란트에 Zr 지대주가 금속 임플란트·Ti 지대주보다 분홍심미점수(Pink Esthetic Score, PES)가 유의하게 높음.

임상 함의: 어느 소재가 명백히 우위라고 볼 근거는 없으나 Zr 지대주가 임플란트 주위 점막 색상 반응과 PES 심미 결과에서 더 좋을 경향이 유의하다는 결론 — 인용된 초록에는 수치적 효과크기·p값이 제시되지 않음.

## Summary

Linkevicius and Vaitelis (2015) systematically reviewed clinical studies comparing zirconia (Zr) and titanium (Ti) implant abutments with respect to soft peri-implant tissues. Electronic and hand searches of English-language journals through December 1, 2014 returned 9 relevant studies reported in 11 papers — 4 randomized clinical trials and 5 prospective controlled clinical trials — all restricted to direct within-patient Zr-vs-Ti abutment comparisons. Eight outcome measures were pre-specified: soft tissue color, recession, peri-implant probing, bleeding on probing, esthetic indexes, patient-reported outcome, marginal bone level, and biological complications. Heterogeneity in study design, statistical methods, and reported results limited meta-analysis to soft tissue color alone, which was significantly superior for Zr abutments; all other outcomes were synthesized qualitatively and showed no statistically significant material difference. One study reported significantly higher PES at Zr implants with Zr abutments than at metal implants with Ti abutments. The authors' bottom line is deliberately restrained: no obvious overall advantage of either abutment material, with a significant Zr tendency toward better peri-implant mucosal color and PES esthetics.

## Key Contributions

- Restricts the comparison to same-patient head-to-head Zr vs Ti abutments, avoiding between-patient confounding in the soft-tissue readouts.
- Establishes soft tissue color as the only outcome poolable across the controlled clinical evidence — and the only one significantly favoring Zr.
- Delivers an explicit equivalence verdict for recession, probing depth, bleeding on probing, marginal bone level, and patient-reported outcome, separating objective tissue measures from the color/esthetic axis.
- Captures the esthetic signal beyond color via the single study's significantly higher PES for Zr abutments on Zr implants.
- Provides the early SR/MA anchor for the later, broader abutment-material syntheses held in the wiki.

## Methodology

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Systematic review with meta-analysis where feasible; qualitative synthesis for the remaining outcomes |
| Search | Electronic and hand searches, English-language journals, through December 1, 2014 |
| Eligibility | RCTs and prospective CCTs with direct comparison of Zr vs Ti abutments in the same patient |
| n | 9 studies / 11 papers — 4 RCTs, 5 CCTs |
| Outcomes | 8: soft tissue color; soft tissue recession; peri-implant probing; bleeding on probing; esthetic indexes; patient-reported outcome; marginal bone level; biological complications |
| Analysis | Meta-analysis for soft tissue color only; qualitative analysis for all other outcomes owing to heterogeneity in design, statistical methods, and reported results |

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Soft tissue color | Meta-analyzable; significantly superior for Zr abutments (no effect size or p-value in the abstract) |
| Soft tissue recession | No statistically significant Zr-vs-Ti difference (qualitative) |
| Peri-implant probing / probing depths | No statistically significant Zr-vs-Ti difference (qualitative) |
| Bleeding on probing | No statistically significant Zr-vs-Ti difference (qualitative) |
| Marginal bone level | No statistically significant Zr-vs-Ti difference (qualitative) |
| Patient-reported outcome | No statistically significant Zr-vs-Ti difference (qualitative) |
| Esthetic indexes (PES) | One study: significantly higher PES at Zr implants with Zr abutments vs metal implants with Ti abutments |
| Biological complications | Pre-specified outcome; no comparative numeric result reported in the abstract |

Overall: the research "does not support any obvious advantage of Ti or Zr abutments over each other," with a "significant tendency in Zr abutments evoking better color response of peri-implant mucosa and superior esthetic outcome measured by PES score." Because the artifact is an abstract only, none of these findings can be quantified beyond the study counts above.
