## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 136 articles (MEDLINE, Google Scholar, Scopus) comparing peri-implant soft-tissue responses to tooth-colored abutment materials — primarily zirconia and PEEK — versus titanium, incorporating in-vitro and in-vivo evidence.
Zirconia: superior biocompatibility, enhanced cell attachment, reduced inflammation and biofilm vs titanium; PEEK: mechanically sound but limited cell attachment due to hydrophobicity — surface modification required; titanium remains clinical integration standard but shows higher inflammation and biofilm than tooth-colored materials.
Zirconia is the optimal tooth-colored abutment for peri-implant soft-tissue biology; PEEK requires surface treatment; both support the shift away from titanium in esthetic zones.

## Summary

This systematic review synthesized 136 articles to compare how tooth-colored abutment materials — primarily zirconia and PEEK — affect peri-implant soft tissues relative to titanium. Zirconia emerged as the superior tooth-colored option: it demonstrated excellent biocompatibility with enhanced epithelial and fibroblast cell attachment, reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression, lower bacterial adhesion, and better soft-tissue sealing than titanium. PEEK showed competitive mechanical properties and acceptable biocompatibility, but its intrinsic hydrophobicity impairs cell adhesion and matrix production, requiring surface modification (such as roughening, sulfonation, or coating) to achieve comparable biologic performance. Titanium, while the osseointegration standard, generates greater inflammatory responses and supports more biofilm formation at the soft-tissue interface than tooth-colored alternatives, suggesting an advantage of non-metallic abutments in esthetic and periodontally susceptible patients.

## Key Contributions

- Largest SR (136 papers) quantifying tooth-colored vs Ti abutment peri-implant soft-tissue evidence
- Zirconia: lowest inflammation + least biofilm + best cell attachment among abutment materials studied
- PEEK: mechanically viable but requires surface treatment to close the biological gap vs zirconia
- Provides direct clinical guidance: zirconia preferred for esthetic-zone and peri-implant-tissue-sensitive cases
- Establishes biological rationale for S3 guideline recommendations favoring ZrO₂ abutments
