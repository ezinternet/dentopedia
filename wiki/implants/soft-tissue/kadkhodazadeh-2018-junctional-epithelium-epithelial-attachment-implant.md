## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (JAMMR 2018;26(12):1–13; PubMed/ScienceDirect/Google Scholar, May 1980–May 2017; 49 screened → 45 reviewed) on the histology and terminology of the epithelium attached to dental implants vs natural teeth.

Ultrastructural consensus: peri-implant epithelium (PIE) attaches to the implant surface via hemidesmosomes and basal lamina-like structures (500–1000 Å) with laminin-5/integrin α6β4 — origin similar to junctional epithelium (JE) around teeth; however thickness, length and adhesion strength differ (implant-attached epithelium generally shorter and thinner, weaker probing resistance than JE-enamel).

Conclusion: because of controversial similarities/differences, the term "epithelial attachment" is recommended around implants instead of "junctional epithelium"; implant surface characteristics influence epithelial attachment dimensions.

## Summary

This narrative review (Kadkhodazadeh et al., JAMMR 2018;26(12):1–13, Shahid Beheshti University, Tehran) addressed whether the soft tissue attached to dental implants should be called "junctional epithelium" or "epithelial attachment". An electronic search of PubMed, ScienceDirect and Google Scholar (May 1980–May 2017) identified 49 articles after deduplication; 45 were reviewed in full text (histological and clinical studies, human and animal). The review consolidates ultrastructural, histological and clinical evidence on the epithelial–implant interface: the innermost cells of the peri-implant epithelium (PIE) attach to the implant surface via hemidesmosomes and basal lamina-like structures (500–1000 Å thickness); laminin-5 and integrin α6β4 mediate this adhesion (Atsuta et al., rat model: laminin-5 contributes to PIE attachment at the apical portion of the implant–PIE interface; internal basal lamina containing laminin-5 forms within ~4 weeks of epithelial regeneration). Around natural teeth, the junctional epithelium (JE) length was ≈2.1–2.6 mm in dog studies (flapless 2.54 mm buccal / 2.11 mm lingual vs flap 2.59 / 2.07 mm — no significant difference). Notable divergent findings: some studies report weaker resistance to probing of PIE vs JE-enamel (Ericsson), others postulate a strong bond between keratinized epithelium and circular collagen fibers around implants without cellular attachment; Hashimoto et al. describe implant-attached epithelium as shorter and thinner than JE and derived from oral epithelium, whereas primary JE around teeth originates from the reduced enamel epithelium (REE). Implant characteristics (surface, design) also affect epithelial attachment dimensions. Conclusion: the origin of implant-attached epithelium is histologically similar to JE, but thickness/length/adhesion-strength differences remain controversial — hence the recommendation to use "epithelial attachment" rather than "junctional epithelium" around implants.

## Key Contributions

- Terminology decision framework: recommends "epithelial attachment" over "junctional epithelium" for implant sites, explicitly because the two structures differ in measurable dimensions despite similar embryologic/structural origin.
- Consolidated ultrastructural inventory: hemidesmosomes + basal lamina-like structures (500–1000 Å), laminin-5/integrin α6β4 adhesion axis, ~4-week internal basal lamina maturation — the molecular basis of soft-tissue seal around implants.
- Quantified JE length reference values around teeth (≈2.1–2.6 mm, dog model) for comparison with implant sites.
- Highlights controversy: probing-resistance and adhesion-strength claims conflict across studies (weaker than JE-enamel vs strong collagen-fiber bond without cellular attachment) — caution against dogmatic statements.
- Notes the origin difference: implant-attached epithelium derives from oral epithelium, primary JE from reduced enamel epithelium (REE) — an argument that anatomically favors a distinct term.

## Methodology

Narrative review. Search: PubMed, Science Direct, Google Scholar; May 1980–May 2017. Inclusion: full-text, histological and clinical evaluation, animal and human studies. Selection: 49 articles after deduplication, 45 reviewed fully. Topic synthesis: epithelial attachment around teeth vs implants — origin, ultrastructure (basal lamina, hemidesmosomes, laminin-5/integrin α6β4), dimensions (thickness/length), adhesion/probing resistance, effect of implant surface characteristics, terminology recommendation.

## Key Histological Facts (as reviewed)

- PIE–implant interface: innermost epithelial cells attach via basal lamina-like structures (500–1000 Å) + hemidesmosomes — ultrastructurally almost identical to JE on natural teeth.
- Molecular adhesion: laminin-5 (in the internal basal lamina) and integrin α6β4; PIE attaches to titanium at the apical portion of the interface (Atsuta et al.); internal basal lamina with laminin-5 forms ~4 weeks post-epithelialization.
- JE length around teeth (dog study): flapless 2.54 mm (buccal) / 2.11 mm (lingual); flap 2.59 / 2.07 mm — no significant difference.
- Divergent claims on adhesion strength: PIE probing resistance weaker than JE-enamel (Ericsson et al.) vs strong bond between keratinized epithelium and circular collagen fibers without cellular attachment (others).
- Implant-attached epithelium generally shorter and thinner than JE (Hashimoto et al.); origin from oral epithelium vs primary JE from reduced enamel epithelium (REE).

## Clinical Takeaways

- Use "epithelial attachment" (상피부착) in implant-site terminology; reserve "junctional epithelium" for natural teeth — aligns with histologic reality and avoids confusion in education/communication.
- The soft-tissue seal around implants is molecularly real (laminin-5/integrin α6β4, hemidesmosomes) but biologically distinct and generally less robust than JE-enamel — reinforce patient-peri-implant hygiene and be gentler with probing in PIE zones.
- Implant surface characteristics influence epithelial attachment dimensions — surface selection may affect the mucosal seal, alongside the soft-tissue management covered in this category (e.g., KM width augmentation pages).

## Limitations

- Narrative review without systematic protocol (no PRISMA, no quality appraisal, no meta-analysis).
- Heterogeneous primary studies across species (rat/dog/human) and implant systems — conflicting adhesion-strength results unresolved.
- Terminology recommendation is expert opinion; no consensus body endorsement.
- No quantitative synthesis of implant-site epithelial thickness/length values comparable to tooth JE reference data.
