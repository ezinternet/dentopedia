## Three-line Summary

A mesh free gingival graft (mesh-FGG) combined with an apically positioned flap (APF) was applied in two mandibular implant cases to increase keratinized mucosa (KM) width, vestibule depth, and soft-tissue thickness. Scoring the conventional FGG in a lattice/mesh pattern physically expands its coverage area, allowing a smaller palatal harvest to cover a large recipient site — thereby reducing donor morbidity. At 4-month follow-up both sites healed uneventfully with good esthetic integration and minimal graft demarcation.

## Summary

Peri-implant keratinized tissue adequacy has become an increasingly recognized determinant of implant health and maintenance outcomes. This case report series (n=2) introduces the mesh-FGG — a modification of the conventional free gingival graft in which the harvested palatal tissue is scored in a lattice pattern, analogous to the split-thickness skin mesh grafts used in burn surgery. This scoring allows the graft to be physically stretched and expanded before suturing to the recipient bed, enabling coverage of significantly larger edentulous areas from a single, smaller palatal harvest.

Both cases involved large edentulous ridges with shallow vestibules and minimal KM following implant placement in the mandible. The mesh-FGG was combined with an APF to reposition the mucogingival junction apically and expose a periosteal recipient bed, simultaneously deepening the vestibule. At approximately 4 months, both patients showed increased KM width, vestibule depth, and soft-tissue thickness, with good overall esthetic outcome characterized by minimal color disparity and graft demarcation — a known disadvantage of conventional FGG compared to connective tissue grafts.

## Key Contributions

1. **Novel technique description**: Mesh-FGG — conventional FGG scored in a lattice pattern to expand its surface area prior to placement at the recipient site.
2. **Reduced donor morbidity**: A smaller palatal harvest covers a larger area, reducing the extent of the palatal wound.
3. **APF combination**: Simultaneous vestibuloplasty via APF deepens the vestibule while the mesh-FGG provides KM width gain.
4. **Esthetic advantage**: Good color integration and minimal demarcation at 4 months — contrasting with the often-cited esthetic limitation of conventional FGG.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Case series (n=2), healthy adults
- **Sites**: Posterior mandible (Subject 1); anterior mandible (Subject 2)
- **Indication**: Post-implant placement; large edentulous ridge with shallow vestibule and minimal KM
- **Technique sequence**:
  1. APF — mucosal flap displaced apically to expose periosteal bed and deepen vestibule
  2. FGG harvested from the palate
  3. Graft scored in a lattice/mesh pattern to allow expansion
  4. Expanded mesh-FGG secured to the periosteal recipient bed
- **Outcome measures**: KM width, vestibule depth, soft-tissue thickness (clinical), color/esthetic integration, patient-reported outcomes
- **Follow-up**: ~4 months

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Healing | Uneventful in both cases |
| KM width | Increased in both cases |
| Vestibule depth | Increased |
| Soft-tissue thickness | Increased |
| Color integration | Good; minimal color disparity and graft demarcation |
| Patient-reported discomfort | No accessory discomfort reported |

Authors' principal finding: mesh-FGG allowed expansion of harvested graft length, resulting in large-area recipient coverage, KM height increase, and good esthetic integration.

## Limitations

- **n=2 only** — strictly preliminary evidence; no comparative control, no statistics
- **4-month follow-up** — long-term KM stability, crestal bone level changes, and esthetic durability unknown
- **Expansion ratio not quantified** — the degree of surface area gain from mesh scoring is not reported
- **Single-center, case series** — high risk of selection and reporting bias
- **Authors' call**: RCTs comparing mesh-FGG vs. conventional FGG and other augmentation techniques are required
