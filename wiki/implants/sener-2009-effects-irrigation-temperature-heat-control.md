## Three-line Summary

In vitro bovine-mandible osteotomy study (Clin Oral Implants Res 2009) measuring bone temperature at 3, 7, and 12 mm drilling depths via thermoresistors, comparing no irrigation vs external 25°C and 10°C saline irrigation.

Without irrigation, peak temperatures at 3/7/12 mm were 50.9/47.4/38.1°C (superficial depths exceeded/approached the 47°C/1 min necrosis threshold); with irrigation all measurements stayed below body temperature except at 12 mm depth (37.4°C @ 25°C saline, 36.3°C @ 10°C saline), with colder saline consistently better.

Room-temperature external irrigation is generally sufficient at shallow depths where most heat is generated, but colder saline improves cooling further, and irrigation should continue between drilling steps — abstract-only source, full statistics/methodology not retrieved.

## Summary
An in vitro study using fresh frozen bovine mandible segments measured bone temperature at three depths (3, 7, 12 mm) during implant osteotomy drilling, using thermoresistors placed 0.5 mm from the cavity wall. Three conditions were compared: no irrigation, external irrigation with 25°C saline, and external irrigation with 10°C saline. Without irrigation, peak temperatures reached 50.9°C at 3 mm and 47.4°C at 7 mm — at or above the classic 47°C/1 min thermal-necrosis threshold — while the deepest point (12 mm) stayed cooler (38.1°C). With either irrigation temperature, all shallow/mid-depth measurements dropped below body temperature; only the deepest point remained slightly above it (37.4°C at 25°C saline, 36.3°C at 10°C saline). The authors conclude that more heat is generated superficially than at the bottom of the osteotomy, that room-temperature external irrigation is generally adequate, and that colder saline further improves cooling — irrigation should be maintained between drilling steps, not just during active drilling. The retrieved text is abstract-only (publisher landing page); full methodology, statistical testing, and sample-size details were not available.

## Key Contributions
- One of the earliest depth-stratified (3/7/12 mm) quantifications of implant osteotomy bone temperature.
- Direct comparison of two irrigation saline temperatures against a no-irrigation control in the same ex vivo model.
- Established the superficial-generates-more-heat-than-deep pattern that later studies (Bernabeu-Mira 2020, Aquilanti 2023) independently reproduce.

## Methodology
Ex vivo bovine mandible segments (12 × 6 cm), drilled with thermoresistors 0.5 mm from the osteotomy wall at 3, 7, and 12 mm depths; signals analyzed in ORIGIN 5.0. Three irrigation conditions: none, 25°C external saline, 10°C external saline. Outcome: maximum recorded bone temperature per depth per condition.
