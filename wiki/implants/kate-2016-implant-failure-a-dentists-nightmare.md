## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (concise review article, no formal systematic search protocol) proposing a consolidated "early vs. late" working classification of dental implant failure, synthesizing six prior classification schemes (Nallaswamy, Rosenberg et al., Esposito et al., Truhlar/Tonetti-Schmid, Matukas, El Askary et al.).

Early failures are attributed to implant selection (e.g., 9.5% failure for 7 mm implants vs. 3.8% for other lengths), bone type (35% of failures in porous trabecular bone per Jaffin & Berman 1991), surgical/prosthetic errors, and overheating (5.8% loss, Salonen 1993); late failures are attributed to host factors (diabetes: 85% success rate, Fiorellini 2000), smoking (11.3% vs. 4.8% failure in nonsmokers), bruxism (41% vs. 12% loss in parafunction, Glauser et al.), radiotherapy (~70% success), and peri-implantitis/retrograde infection.

Clinical implication: implant failure is multifactorial and the proposed early/late × etiology/personnel/failure-mode/biological-cause matrix is a useful chairside triage framework, but this is a low-evidence-weight narrative synthesis of secondary citations — cross-check numbers against [[implants/giok-2026-factors-implant-failure-umbrella-review]] or primary studies.

## Summary

This concise/narrative review (Kate, Palaskar, Kapoor — Sinhgad Dental College and Hospital, Pune, India; *Journal of Dental Implants* 2016;6(2):51-56) compiles six pre-existing implant-failure classification schemes and proposes a new consolidated **working classification** dividing failures into early (intraoperative to 3 months) and late (after 3 months), each cross-cut by etiology, personnel responsible, failure mode, and biological cause. Early-failure drivers discussed include implant selection (length/diameter — e.g., 9.5% failure for 7 mm implants vs. 3.8% for other lengths), bone type (35% of failures occur in porous trabecular bone), surgical/sterility errors, bone overheating (5.8% loss), and prosthetic misfit. Late-failure drivers include host factors (diabetes, osteoporosis, obesity, age 60–70), tissue-abuse/parafunctional habits (smoking, bruxism), radiotherapy, and biological causes (peri-implantitis, retrograde/apical peri-implant infection). The paper is explicitly a synthesis of secondary citations rather than a systematic review — no search protocol, database list, or quality appraisal is reported — but it is a frequently cited pedagogical reference for organizing implant-failure differential diagnosis.

## Key Contributions
- Side-by-side compilation of six historical implant-failure classification systems (Nallaswamy; Rosenberg et al.; Esposito et al.; Truhlar/Tonetti-Schmid; Matukas; El Askary et al.).
- Proposed early (≤3 months) vs. late (>3 months) working classification, cross-tabulated by etiology / personnel responsible / failure mode / biological cause (Table 1).
- Consolidates a wide range of secondary-cited numeric findings (implant dimensions, bone type, systemic disease, smoking, bruxism, radiotherapy) into one narrative reference table.

## Methodology
Narrative/concise review article with no PRISMA flow, no stated systematic search strategy, and no risk-of-bias appraisal of underlying primary studies. Synthesizes ~34 references (prior classification papers plus individually cited primary clinical studies) into a qualitative classification; no pooled effect estimates are calculated.

## Results

| Factor | Reported finding (secondary citation) |
|---|---|
| Implant length | 9.5% failure for 7 mm implants vs. 3.8% for other lengths (Bahat 1993, n=732) |
| Implant diameter/number | 5 mm-diameter implants 2%–3% failure; double implants 1%–2% (Bahat & Handelsman 1996) |
| Bone type | 35% of all failures occur in porous trabecular bone (Jaffin & Berman 1991) |
| Bone overheating | 5.8% osseointegration failure at >47°C for >1 min (Salonen 1993) |
| Diabetes | 85% implant success rate (Fiorellini 2000); longer diabetes duration → more failures (Olson 2000) |
| Age | 60–70 years higher failure risk vs. <40 years (Moy 2005) |
| Smoking | 11.3% failure in smokers vs. 4.8% in nonsmokers; 4.2% (321/7680) implants failed due to smoking incl. passive smoking (Twito & Sade, Israel 1999–2008) |
| Bruxism/parafunction | 41% loss in bruxers vs. 12% in non-bruxers among 127 immediately loaded implants (Glauser et al.) |
| Radiotherapy | ~70% implant success rate; loss increases over time post-irradiation (Jacobsson 1988) |
| Abutment screw loosening | 7% complication rate; overall failure rate 4.6% (Simon 2003) |
