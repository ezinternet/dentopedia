## Three-line Summary

Single-centre RCT of 104 patients / 188 bone-level, platform-switched implants in healed ridges, randomized per implant to a 5° conical connection (AnyRidge, MegaGen; n=125) or a 45° internal hex (Core, Kristal; n=63); buccal-zenith mucosal height (MH) and thickness (MT) measured digitally on casts after 12 months of provisionalization.

5° vs 45°: MH 3.32 ± 0.12 vs 2.70 ± 0.16 mm; MT 4.37 ± 0.16 vs 3.93 ± 0.18 mm; MT/MH 1.50 ± 0.88 vs 1.81 ± 1.20; ratio associated with group (p=0.047) and sector (p=0.015).

The 5° conical connection supported taller, thicker buccal-zenith mucosa, but implant-level Pearson analysis without clustering adjustment, 2:1 imbalance, posterior-dominant sample, and retrospective registration make it hypothesis-generating.

## 세줄요약

단일기관 RCT: 환자 104명·골수준 플랫폼 스위칭 임플란트 188개(치유 치조제), 5° 원뿔 연결 (AnyRidge, MegaGen; 125) vs 45° 내부육각 (Core, Kristal; 63) 임플란트 단위 무작위; 12개월 임시수복 후 협측 정점 (Buccal Zenith) 점막 높이 (MH)·두께 (MT) 디지털 계측.

5° vs 45°: MH 3.32 vs 2.70 mm, MT 4.37 vs 3.93 mm, MT/MH 1.50 vs 1.81; 비율은 군(p=0.047)·부위(p=0.015)와 연관.

5° 원뿔 연결에서 협측 정점 점막이 더 높고 두꺼웠으나, 군집 미보정 상관분석·2:1 불균형·구치부 편중·사후 등록으로 가설 생성 수준.

## Summary

The buccal zenith is where implant recession shows first, so the authors measured how tall and how thick the mucosa stood there one year after provisionalization, comparing two implant systems that differ mainly in connection angle. Implants with the narrow 5° conical connection carried about 0.6 mm more mucosal height and 0.4 mm more thickness than the 45° internal-hex system. Following Nozawa's idea that thickness "holds up" height, they read the lower thickness-to-height ratio in the 5° group as more stable tissue.

The direction is plausible and consistent with platform-switch/conical-connection bone data, but the trial is weak on method: the "between-group" test is a Pearson correlation on implant-level data from patients with multiple implants; the reported ± values look like standard errors; randomization by coin per implant left a 125:63 split; only 29 implants were in the upper esthetic zone; and the two arms are whole different implant systems, not just different connection angles. It belongs in the wiki as the only RCT measuring tissue dimensions *at the zenith*, not as proof that connection angle drives zenith stability.

## Key Contributions

- Only held RCT measuring peri-implant mucosal height and thickness specifically at the buccal zenith.
- 5° conical connection associated with greater MH and MT than 45° internal hex.
- Digital cast-superimposition measurement workflow.

## Methodology

- Healed ridges, staged placement at bone level, both arms platform-switched; heavy smokers and augmentation cases excluded.
- 12-month screw-retained provisional; individualized impression transfer of the provisional emergence profile.
- MH: analogue vestibular shoulder → gingival margin along implant axis; MT: shoulder → outer mucosa perpendicular to the axis.
- Pearson correlation (SPSS 17), α=0.05.

## Results

| Outcome | 5° conical (n=125) | 45° hex (n=63) |
|---|---|---|
| MH (mm) | 3.32 ± 0.12 | 2.70 ± 0.16 |
| MT (mm) | 4.37 ± 0.16 | 3.93 ± 0.18 |
| MT/MH | 1.50 ± 0.88 | 1.81 ± 1.20 |

- MT/MH associated with group (p=0.047) and sector (p=0.015).
- Sample 84% posterior (S3 34.0%, S4 49.5%); upper anterior 15.4%.

**Clinical reading**: connection design is a candidate lever for buccal-zenith soft-tissue volume, but this trial alone cannot separate connection angle from the rest of the implant system; emergence-profile shape and abutment height remain the better-supported levers.
