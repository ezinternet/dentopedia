## Three-line Summary
Single-center RCT 8-year follow-up (64 patients, 98 implants; University of Zurich, Switzerland) comparing two two-piece implant systems with non-matching (platform-switched) implant-abutment junctions supporting fixed restorations — S1 OsseoSpeed TX (Dentsply Sirona) vs S2 Straumann Bone Level SLActive (Straumann AG); 49 patients (42 S1 / 36 S2 implants) re-examined at mean 7.9 ± 0.4 years.
Survival was equivalent (S1 97.6% vs S2 97.2%, p=.930); the primary endpoint favored S1 (marginal bone level change from loading to 8 years: gain 0.21 mm vs loss 0.24 mm, intergroup p<.001, median difference 95% CI [0.27, 0.88] mm) while technical complications favored S2 (35.7% vs 16.7% of implants, p=.1413) and peri-implant mucositis favored S1 (21.4% vs 55.6%, p=.038) with peri-implantitis 0% vs 8.3%.
Both systems held bone within 0.3 mm of the implant shoulder over 8 years and all inter-system differences — including the statistically significant bone difference — were judged clinically negligible, so two-piece system choice should rest on handling and prosthetic preference rather than on survival or bone outcomes; the main weakness is the absence of a sample-size calculation (convenience sample) and a 23% dropout rate.

## Summary

This 8-year follow-up of a single-center randomized controlled trial (University of Zurich; 64 patients, 98 implants placed 2009) compares two contemporary **two-piece systems sharing a non-matching, internally conical (platform-switched) implant-abutment junction**: OsseoSpeed TX (Dentsply Sirona) and Straumann Bone Level SLActive (Straumann AG), both supporting fixed restorations. At a mean of 7.9 years, 49 patients with 42 S1 and 36 S2 implants were re-examined, and both systems performed well on the primary endpoint of survival (97.6% vs 97.2%, p=.930) and bone (marginal bone level change from loading to 8 years: gain 0.21 mm in S1 vs loss 0.24 mm in S2, intergroup p<.001). The clinically interesting result is not the bone difference — which the authors themselves call negligible at < 0.3 mm from the shoulder — but the **dissociation of outcome classes**: technical complications ran 2× higher with the better-bone system (35.7% vs 16.7%), while peri-implant mucositis ran 2.6× higher with the worse-bone system (21.4% vs 55.6%, p=.038), and the authors attribute the technical excess to >90% screw-retained retention rather than to the implant system. For the clinician, the paper's message is that brand choice within the platform-switched two-piece category is a handling-and-prosthetics decision, not a bone or survival decision — with the important caveat that the trial was never powered for complications (no sample-size calculation, 23% dropout), so the direction of those differences is more trustworthy than their magnitude.

## Key Contributions

- **8-year RCT head-to-head on two modern platform-switched two-piece systems** — the horizon the PS literature previously lacked, and the trial type that [[implants/mbl/schwarz-2013-implant-abutment-connection-platform-switching]] explicitly called for.
- **A statistically significant primary endpoint with no clinical consequence**: S1 gained 0.21 mm of bone while S2 lost 0.24 mm (intergroup p<.001, median difference 95% CI [0.27, 0.88] mm), yet both stayed within 0.3 mm of the implant shoulder. A clean demonstration that inter-brand variance inside one connection design is far smaller than the PS-vs-matching effect itself.
- **Bone-vs-complication trade-off quantified between contemporary brands** — better bone (S1) came with ~2× technical complications (35.7% vs 16.7% of implants), the same trade-off sign this group documented at 15–17 years for one-piece vs two-piece designs.
- **Outcome classes dissociate rather than move together** — peri-implant mucositis ran opposite to technical complications (S1 21.4% vs S2 55.6%, p=.038), so no single "winning" system label is defensible across endpoints.
- **Technical complications attributed to retention, not to the implant**: screw loosening predominated, then screw/abutment chipping and fracture, together 75% of all technical events in S1; the excess was independent of restoration material or type and tracked with >90% screw-retained restorations (cf. Sailer et al. pooled 24.4% screw-retained vs 11.9% cemented).
- **Very low peri-implantitis at 8 years (0% S1, 8.3% S2)** under an individually designed maintenance program with PCR maintained ≤ 0.3 — evidence that supportive care keeps peri-implantitis well below the 1–47% range reported from observational cohorts.
- **Quantifies the remodeling that hides a deep initial sink depth**: mean MBL at placement was −1.30 ± 1.00 (S1) and −1.26 ± 1.22 mm (S2), resolving to 0.04 ± 0.67 and −0.58 ± 0.78 mm at 8 years.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Single-center randomized controlled clinical trial, computer-generated allocation, 8-year follow-up report. Surgery, prosthetics and radiographic assessment were unblinded.
- **Setting**: Clinic of Reconstructive Dentistry, University of Zurich, Switzerland; implants placed Feb–Dec 2009. Ethics KEK-ZH-Nr. 2013-0121.
- **Sample**: 64 patients, 98 implants (67 maxillary, 31 mandibular). At T8 (mean 7.9 ± 0.4 years; mean age 64.3 ± 12.0) 49 patients were re-examined (dropout 23%: death, relocation, refusal) — implant level 42 S1 / 36 S2; patient level 25 S1 / 24 S2 after one implant per patient was randomly preselected for patient-level analysis.
- **Arms**: S1 = OsseoSpeed TX 3.0–5.0 S / TX 4.5 (Astra Tech Implant System, Dentsply Sirona); S2 = Straumann Bone Level Implants 3.3 / 4.1 / 4.8 mm, SLActive (Straumann AG). Both non-matching internally conical junctions.
- **Protocol**: Shoulder generally at crest but apico-coronal position explicitly **not** standardized; GBR (xenogeneic/synthetic graft + resorbable membrane) permitted for dehiscence/fenestration; restorations screw-retained or cemented per clinical preference; individualized maintenance program from baseline. Follow-up at T_i, T_L (1–3 weeks after loading = baseline), T1, T3, T5, T8.
- **Outcomes**: Primary = patient-level MBL change T_L → T8. Secondary = implant/restoration survival, technical and biological complications, PD/BOP/PCR (6 sites per implant, UNC-15 probe), plus adjacent and contralateral tooth/implant sites.
- **Radiography**: Standardized paralleling technique (Rinn holders, 90° tube alignment) at every timepoint; MBL via ImageJ at 10–15×, calibrated by inter-thread distance; DIB measured mesially/distally in 0.1-mm steps from the flat top of the implant shoulder to the bone crest; positive values = shoulder apical to crest.
- **Statistics**: Wilcoxon signed-rank within group and Wilcoxon rank-sum between groups with Bonferroni correction (3 tests) for the primary endpoint; nonparametric 95% CI and Hodges–Lehmann difference estimate; secondary endpoints by Wilcoxon rank-sum or chi-square at patient level (clustering eliminated by the one-implant-per-patient design); implant-level supporting analyses by parametric mixed models (time × group). Multiplicity controlled **only** for the primary endpoint.

Technical profile: **screw loosening predominated**, followed by screw/abutment chipping and fracture — these three = **75% of all technical complications in S1**. Not linked to restoration material (porcelain-fused-to-metal) or restoration type (single crown, cantilever, multi-unit, splinted), but to **retention** (>90% screw-retained; cf. Sailer et al. pooled 24.4% screw-retained vs 11.9% cemented). Rate exceeds literature benchmarks (5–15% over 5 y for single crowns; 4.6% at 5 y and 19.9% at 10 y for FDPs). Mucositis/peri-implantitis rates sit inside systematic-review ranges (19–65% and 1–47%), with the low peri-implantitis figure attributed to the maintenance program — PCR 0.1 ± 0.1 at baseline, 0.3 ± 0.3 (S1) vs 0.1 ± 0.2 (S2) at FU-8.

### Reading the trade-off

The four-part author summary is: high survival for both, stable bone, **more technical complications in S1**, **more mucositis in S2**. No outcome class makes one system the winner, and the authors' own verdict is that the differences "appear to be clinically negligible" — they explicitly flag that although the bone difference is statistically significant, its clinical relevance "remains questionable".

### Limitations

No sample size calculation (convenience sample; S2 had no prior data at trial start) → underpowered for complication endpoints, so treat the *direction* of the technical and mucositis differences as more reliable than their magnitude. Multiplicity controlled only for the primary endpoint, so the several sub-0.05 secondary p-values are unadjusted. 23% dropout, unanalyzed against baseline. Apico-coronal position not standardized, so change-from-T_L and absolute T8 values must not be conflated. Very broad inclusion criteria (site, GBR, healing type, loading time, retention, restoration type/material all unrestricted) — claimed as general-practice representativeness, conceded as the main drawback. Brand confound: arms differ in manufacturer, surface and geometry, so the junction variable cannot be isolated. No blinding. COI: senior authors held research support/lecture fees from both Straumann AG and Dentsply Sirona.

Note the trade-off is expressed along a different axis: design (one- vs two-piece) there, brand (within two-piece) here.
