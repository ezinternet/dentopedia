## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review with meta-analysis (8 pooled SR+MA/MA studies; abstract-only source — publisher paywall, full text not accessible) comparing marginal bone loss (MBL) and implant failure between tilted and axial implants supporting full-arch or fixed partial dentures.

MBL was non-significant short-term (MD=0.00mm; P=.75) but significantly greater around tilted implants at 3-year (MD=0.08mm; P<.00001) and long-term follow-up (MD=0.18mm; P<.00001); implant failure risk was equivalent between the two configurations (RR=1.02; 95% CI 0.85–1.23; P=.81).

Clinically, tilting an implant to avoid anatomical structures (All-on-4-style) does not raise failure risk but does carry a time-dependent marginal-bone-loss penalty that widens with follow-up; abstract-only sourcing means search strategy and quality-appraisal details are unverifiable.

## Summary

This umbrella review (Malak et al., *Int J Oral Maxillofac Implants* 2024) pooled 8 systematic reviews with meta-analysis or meta-analysis studies to synthesize the evidence on marginal bone loss (MBL) and implant failure between tilted and axial implants supporting full-arch or fixed partial dentures. Note: the source PDF captured for this ingest is a Quintessence Publishing paywall/abstract landing page ("Please login to purchase the article") — this page summarizes the structured abstract only; full-text methodology, database list, and study-level appraisal are not available.

**Key findings:**
- MBL did not differ significantly in the short term (MD=0.00mm; 95% CI −0.01–0.02; P=.75).
- MBL was significantly greater around tilted implants at 3-year follow-up (MD=0.08mm; 95% CI 0.05–0.11; P<.00001).
- MBL was significantly greater around tilted implants at long-term follow-up (MD=0.18mm; 95% CI 0.15–0.20; P<.00001).
- Implant failure risk was not significantly different between tilted and axial implants (RR=1.02; 95% CI 0.85–1.23; P=.81).

**Clinical implication**: Based on the high- and moderate-quality, low-risk-of-bias studies pooled, tilted implant placement does not increase implant failure risk relative to axial placement — but the MBL gap between tilted and axial widens over time, meaning axial placement should still be favored where anatomy permits, and tilted-implant patients may need closer long-term crestal-bone monitoring.

## Key Contributions

- Highest-evidence-tier synthesis (umbrella review of 8 SR+MA/MA studies) specifically isolating tilted-vs-axial MBL and failure outcomes.
- Demonstrates the tilted-implant MBL penalty is time-dependent — absent short-term, present and growing at 3-year and long-term follow-up — reconciling the apparent conflict between earlier short-follow-up SR+MAs (NS) and longer-follow-up SR+MAs (significant).
- Confirms implant failure risk equivalence (RR=1.02, NS) between tilted and axial configurations across the pooled evidence base.

## Methodology

Umbrella review with meta-analysis: electronic literature search without language restriction; only systematic reviews with meta-analysis or meta-analysis studies were eligible (8 included). Outcomes were MBL (mm, mean difference with 95% CI) stratified by short-term / 3-year / long-term follow-up, and implant failure (relative risk with 95% CI), comparing tilted vs axial implants supporting full-arch or fixed partial dentures. Full search-strategy and quality-appraisal details (e.g., AMSTAR-2/GRADE) are not extractable from the abstract-only source available for this ingest.
