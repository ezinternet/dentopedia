## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study, n=155 patients / MIS implants (single crown / splinted crown / bridge restorations), 5-year radiographic follow-up (3-month and 60-month intraoral X-rays).

Mean emergence angle 31.8°±10.4°; no significant emergence-angle-to-MBL relationship for single crowns (p=0.369) or splinted crowns (p=0.176), but a weak, statistically significant relationship for bridges (p=0.042, R²=7.9%); no significant marginal bone loss (MBL) or Corticalization Index (CI) differences among restoration types at 3 or 60 months.

Clinically, crown emergence angle alone is not a dominant driver of marginal bone loss for single or splinted restorations — other biological/biomechanical/systemic factors likely matter more — but the weak bridge-only signal and the 2D-radiograph limitation (no buccal/lingual assessment, no formal multivariable adjustment) mean the finding should not be over-generalized to very wide angles (e.g., 60° in preclinical models).

## Summary

This 5-year retrospective study analyzed 155 patients treated with MIS implants (single crowns, splinted crowns, or implant-supported bridges) to test whether crown emergence angle (EA) — measured on standardized 2D intraoral periapical radiographs — predicts marginal bone loss (MBL) and radiographic corticalization (Corticalization Index, CI). The mean EA across the whole cohort was 31.8°±10.4°, similar across all three restoration types (Kruskal-Wallis p=0.202). MBL increased gradually from 0.0 mm at baseline to 0.9±1.2 mm at 60 months, with no significant differences between restoration types at either 3 or 60 months. Simple regression of EA against 60-month MBL within each restoration group found no significant association for single crowns (p=0.369, R²=1.1%) or splinted crowns (p=0.176, R²=2.1%), and only a weak, statistically significant but low-explanatory-power association for bridges (p=0.042, R²=7.9%). CI rose over time in all groups but did not differ significantly by restoration type at 3 or 60 months. The authors interpret this as evidence that, within the relatively narrow EA range observed clinically (6.2–79.1°, median ~31°), emergence angle alone is not a dominant driver of peri-implant bone loss for single and splinted restorations, though bridges may be somewhat more sensitive to prosthetic-design factors — a finding consistent with several prior clinical retrospective studies (Lops 2022, Kou 2023) but which the authors are careful to reconcile with preclinical histological evidence (Lee 2025, beagle dogs) that very wide EA (60°) does impair connective-tissue attachment and increase bone remodeling.

## Key Contributions

- One of the few studies to combine the conventional MBL endpoint with a validated radiographic texture-analysis metric (Corticalization Index) to give a broader picture of peri-implant bone adaptation than linear bone-level change alone.
- Directly compares EA-to-MBL relationships across three prosthetic restoration types (single, splinted, bridge) within the same cohort and implant system, rather than pooling restoration types as most prior EA-MBL studies do.
- Confounder-controlled cohort (excludes diabetes, osteoporosis, prior radiotherapy, active bisphosphonate therapy, diagnosed bruxism) isolating the prosthetic-design signal from major systemic bone-metabolism disruptors.
- Provides a clinical-cohort counterpoint to the preclinical histological signal for wide EA, helping frame when the animal-model risk (very wide, 60° angles) does vs does not translate to the narrower angles seen in routine clinical practice.

## Methodology

Records from 155 patients with delayed-loading MIS implants (single implant system: tapered/cylindrical, double-thread, sandblasted rough surface, titanium alloy) were retrospectively reviewed and grouped as Group I (single crowns), Group II (splinted crowns), or Group III (implant-supported bridges). Intraoral periapical radiographs were taken with a standardized protocol (DIGORA OPTIME/DXR-50, 7 mA, 70 kV, 0.1 s exposure, perpendicular beam-positioning devices) at baseline, 3 months, and 60 months post-loading. Images were analyzed in ImageJ; EA was measured by one examiner on mesial and distal aspects, while MBL and CI were measured by a second examiner using the Kozakiewicz et al. texture-analysis framework for CI. Mesial/distal values were averaged to one value per implant/restoration unit for the principal analyses. Group comparisons used ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis as appropriate; EA-vs-60-month-MBL associations were tested with simple (not multivariable) regression, separately within each restoration group, using transformation-based models to accommodate the markedly right-skewed MBL distribution. The authors explicitly frame the statistical approach as exploratory: no a priori sample-size calculation, no mixed-effects/GEE adjustment for intra-patient clustering, and no formal multivariable adjustment for other covariates (implant geometry, insertion torque, platform-switching, soft-tissue phenotype) that were inconsistently available in the retrospective record.

## Results

- **Emergence angle**: overall mean 31.8°±10.4° (range 6.2–79.1°); by group — bridges 30±11° (n=218), splinted crowns 33±11° (n=208), single crowns 32±9° (n=110); no significant between-group difference (Kruskal-Wallis p=0.202).
- **MBL over time**: 0.0±0.0 mm (baseline) → 0.2±0.7 mm (3mo) → 0.9±1.2 mm (60mo, median 0.43 mm); markedly right-skewed with most patients near zero and rare high outliers (up to 8.0 mm); no significant between-group MBL differences at 3 months (ANOVA p=0.195) or 60 months (p=0.081).
- **EA-to-60-month-MBL regression**: single crowns — not significant (p=0.369, R²=1.1%); splinted crowns — not significant (p=0.176, R²=2.1%); bridges — statistically significant but weak (p=0.042, R²=7.9%), described by the authors as "clinically weak" given the low explained variance.
- **Corticalization Index**: rose over time in all groups (median 165 at 3mo → 234 at 60mo overall; bridges consistently highest, e.g., mean 329.6 at 60mo); no significant between-group differences at 3 months (p=0.121) or 60 months (p=0.072) — restoration type did not significantly affect CI.
- **Site/behavioral associations**: restoration type significantly associated with anterior/posterior location (χ² p=0.0004; single crowns more anterior, splinted crowns/bridges more posterior) but not with maxilla-vs-mandible (p=0.0767); a descriptive smoking-status association (p=0.0409) was noted but explicitly not interpreted as a causal/independent effect (no multivariable adjustment).
- **Contextualization in discussion**: findings for single/splinted crowns align with Lops et al. 2022 (n=312, no EA-MBL difference at EA>30° vs ≤30°) and Kou et al. 2023 (502 single-crown implants, no EA-MBL correlation, but molar sites intrinsically have larger EA than anterior sites); the bridge-only weak signal is discussed against Wach et al. 2024's larger corticalization cohort (>2000 implants) showing restoration-type-dependent CI/MBL relationships; the preclinical beagle-dog histology of Lee et al. 2025 (EA 30° vs 60° in splinted restorations) is cited as the mechanistic caution that very wide EA impairs connective-tissue attachment and junctional epithelium integrity even though this clinical cohort (narrower observed EA range) did not detect that risk.
