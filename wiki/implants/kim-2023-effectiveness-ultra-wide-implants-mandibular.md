## Three-line Summary

Five-year retrospective cohort (J Korean Assoc Oral Maxillofac Surg 2023;49:13-20, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital; 78 ultra-wide 6/7 mm SuperLine implants in 71 patients, placed 2008-2010, mean follow-up 97.8 months) evaluating which factors determine survival, success and marginal bone loss of ultra-wide posterior implants.

Survival was 92.3% and success 83.3% with 13 failures; MBL was 0.2 mm at 1 year and 0.54 mm at final observation, and diameter, length, arch, age, sex, adjunctive surgery and indication were all non-significant.

Only stability predicted outcomes — primary ISQ <60 gave 61% success versus 100% at 60-70, and secondary ISQ ≥71 gave 100% survival — so the ultra-wide implant is a viable posterior salvage option whose fate is decided by whether initial fixation is actually achieved.

## Summary

Ultra-wide implants sit at the practical end of the design spectrum: they exist to rescue a site where a standard fixture cannot gain stability, or where bone is too soft for one. This cohort of 78 SuperLine implants (6 and 7 mm, tapered, SLA, internal connection) placed between 2008 and 2010 and followed for a mean of 97.8 months gives that strategy a realistic report card — 92.3% survival but 83.3% success, with 13 failures spread across mobility, radiolucency, annual bone loss and inflammation. The interesting part is what did *not* matter: neither diameter, length, arch, patient age or sex, adjunctive sinus lift/GBR, nor even the reason for choosing an ultra-wide implant predicted anything. What mattered was measured stability. Primary ISQ below 60 collapsed success to 61%, the 60-70 band reached 100%, and secondary ISQ above 70 reached 100% survival. In other words, the ultra-wide implant does not rescue a bad site by being wide; it works when it does achieve engagement, and the 6-70 ISQ band shows that a shaky start can still consolidate.

## Key Contributions

- Supplies 5-year benchmarks for ≥6 mm posterior implants (survival 92.3%, success 83.3%, MBL 0.54 mm at ~8 years) in a literature where wide-diameter evidence is thin and mostly short-term.
- Negates design-parameter explanations: 6 vs 7 mm, 8/10/12 mm length, maxilla vs mandible and adjunctive surgery all failed to separate outcomes.
- Locates the prognostic signal in stability — primary ISQ for success, secondary ISQ for survival — and quantifies the penalty for a soft start (61% success at ISQ <60).
- Documents that the 60-70 ISQ band converted to 100% success and survival, i.e. borderline primary stability is not automatically a failure sentence.
- Itemizes failure modes by category (mobility, radiolucency, annual MBL ≥0.2 mm, first-year MBL ≥1.5 mm, inflammation/bleeding).

## Methodology

**Design.** Retrospective clinical study, single centre.

**Sample.** 78 ultra-wide implants (SuperLine, Dentium: SLA surface, internal connection, tapered, 6.0 or 7.0 mm diameter) placed in 71 patients (39 male / 32 female, mean age 54.2 years) between 2008 and 2010. Mean observation 97.8 months.

**Variables.** Sex, age group, site, diameter, length, adjunctive surgery (sinus lift only / GBR only / both / none), primary and secondary ISQ, and the reason an ultra-wide implant was selected (poor bone quality; failed initial fixation of a ≤5 mm implant; replacement after removal of a failed implant).

**Analysis.** One-way ANOVA and independent-samples t-tests for MBL; Kaplan-Meier for 5-year survival and success; success also tabulated against each variable. Defining success relative to the first year of prosthetic loading.

**Indications.** 59 implants for poor bone quality; 3 to replace a ≤5 mm implant that failed initial fixation; the remainder as replacements after failed implant removal.

## Results

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean observation | 97.8 months |
| Survival | 92.3% |
| Success | 83.3% |
| Failed implants | 13 |
| MBL at 1 year | 0.2 mm (0-2.3) |
| MBL at final observation | 0.54 mm (0-4.99) |

**Failures by mechanism.** Mobility 4; radiolucency 2; annual MBL ≥0.2 mm since year 1 → 2; ≥1.5 mm MBL within first year → 4; inflammation with bleeding → 1.

**Success by primary ISQ (P = 0.045)**

| Primary ISQ | Success |
|---|---|
| <60 | 61% |
| 60-70 | 100% |
| >70 | 91.2% |

**Survival by secondary ISQ (P = 0.036)**

| Secondary ISQ | Survival |
|---|---|
| 60-70 | 87.5% |
| >70 | 100% |

**Non-significant for success and survival:** age group (success 66.7-100%), sex (86.36% male / 79.41% female), site (maxilla 81.25% / mandible 84.78%), diameter (6 mm 82.26% / 7 mm 84.7%), length (8 mm 84.8%, 10 mm 89.47%, 12 mm 42.87%, P = 0.532), adjunctive surgery (none 78.6%, GBR 92%, sinus+GBR 94.4%, sinus only 100%), indication (poor bone quality 100%, failed ≤5 mm implant 88.6%, replacement 100%).

**MBL.** No variable — including sex, site, diameter and adjunctive surgery — showed a significant association.

## Limitations

Retrospective, single-centre, 2008-2010 cohort that predates current thread and surface generations; no internal matched control of standard-diameter implants, so the comparison with standard diameters is drawn from outside literature. The success definition (annual MBL ≥0.2 mm after year 1 = failure) is strict and study-specific, which widens the survival-success gap. Several subgroups are tiny — the 12 mm length group is 7 implants with 42.87% success, an estimate with very wide uncertainty. No multivariable modelling, so stability, bone quality and site confounding is unresolved. Immediate loading status and laterality are not analyzed.
