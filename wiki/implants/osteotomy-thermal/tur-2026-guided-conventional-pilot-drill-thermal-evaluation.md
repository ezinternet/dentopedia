## Three-line Summary

In a standardized bovine in vitro model with automated robotic drilling (160 preparations total), 2.0-mm sleeve-guided surgery produced significantly lower bone temperatures than conventional drilling under external irrigation (p=0.0107 at 10 mm, p=0.0005 at 12 mm), while 2.8-mm partially guided surgery produced significantly higher temperatures than conventional drilling with external cooling at 10 mm depth (p=0.0256). All externally irrigated preparations remained below the 44–47 °C thermal osteonecrosis threshold. The opposing thermal behaviors between 2.0 mm and 2.8 mm pilot drills implicate guide design and irrigation accessibility — not diameter alone — as the controlling variables for heat generation during guided osteotomy.

## Summary

This in vitro study by the Vienna group (Strbac lab) is a focused follow-up to their 2025 investigation of sequential drills 2.2–3.5 mm, targeting specifically the pilot drill phase of guided implant surgery (GIS). Using a bovine bone substitute platform (BoneSim™), automated robotic drilling, and real-time thermocouple measurement at 1 and 2 mm from the osteotomy, 160 preparations were executed across a 2×2×2×2 factorial design (technique × diameter × depth × irrigation).

The key clinical insight is that thermal behavior during guided surgery is not uniform across pilot drill diameters: sleeve-guided 2.0-mm preparations were cooler than conventional drilling with irrigation (the expected GS thermal penalty disappeared), whereas 2.8-mm partially guided preparations — using drill handle cylinders rather than sleeves — showed the opposite, generating more heat than conventional drilling. External irrigation eliminated all risk of thermal osteonecrosis across all groups, but the difference in irrigation access between sleeve-guided and partially guided configurations may explain the divergence.

## Key Contributions

- **Thermal reversal finding**: 2.0-mm GS (sleeve) = lower heat than CD under irrigation; 2.8-mm GS (drill handle) = higher heat than CD under irrigation. Same pilot drill sequence, opposite thermal outcomes.
- **Irrigation universally protective**: All externally irrigated preparations stayed below 44–47 °C regardless of technique or diameter (임플란트 골괴사 임계온도, Thermal Osteonecrosis Threshold).
- **Non-irrigated groups**: CD consistently generated more heat than GS without irrigation across all diameter/depth combinations (p = 0.0009–0.0162), confirming external cooling as the dominant thermal modifier.
- **Synergy with Tur 2025**: Combined interpretation across both studies reveals that guided surgery's thermal profile varies by drill diameter range — smaller pilots can be thermally equivalent or safer with sleeves; larger diameters and partially guided configurations need special irrigation attention.
- **Methodological benchmark**: 800 rpm, 50 mL/min saline, automated intermittent withdrawal (32 s for 10 mm, 39 s for 12 mm) provides a reproducible reference protocol.

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Model | BoneSim™ bovine substitute; 3 mm cortical + 15 mm cancellous |
| n | 160 preparations (16 groups × 10 each) |
| Drills | Stainless steel pilot drills, 2.0 mm and 2.8 mm (Komet/Brasseler) |
| Depths | 10 mm and 12 mm |
| Cooling | No irrigation vs. external (room-temp saline, 50 mL/min) |
| GS — 2.0 mm | Metallic guiding sleeve (titanium, D3.5/d2.0/L5.0) |
| GS — 2.8 mm | Sleeveless guide + drill handle cylinder (Straumann GIS protocol) |
| Speed | 800 rpm |
| Temperature sensors | Fine-wire Type K thermocouples, ±1.5 °C; 1 mm and 2 mm radial distances; depths 3/6/9/12/15 mm |
| Statistics | Linear mixed model (four-way interaction), Kenward-Roger adjustment, SAS 9.4 |

## Results

### ΔT (°C) under external irrigation — critical comparisons

| Depth | Diameter | GS ΔT | CD ΔT | p-value | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 mm | 2.0 mm | 9.44 (2.27) | 13.40 (3.65) | **0.0107** | GS < CD |
| 12 mm | 2.0 mm | 12.09 (4.52) | 22.10 (5.85) | **0.0005** | GS < CD |
| 10 mm | 2.8 mm | 8.16 (2.43) | 5.62 (2.23) | **0.0256** | GS > CD |
| 12 mm | 2.8 mm | 10.03 (2.83) | 7.42 (3.12) | 0.0655 | NS |

### ΔT without irrigation — all groups: CD > GS (p = 0.0016–0.0162)

### Four-way interaction (technique × diameter × depth × cooling): p = 0.0244

### Thermal osteonecrosis threshold: Not exceeded in any irrigated group

### Peak temperature location
- Irrigation shifts heat peak deeper: superficial (3–4.5 mm) without cooling → mid-depth (6–9 mm) with irrigation
- 2.8-mm irrigated preparations show peak heat at 9–12 mm depth under GS conditions
