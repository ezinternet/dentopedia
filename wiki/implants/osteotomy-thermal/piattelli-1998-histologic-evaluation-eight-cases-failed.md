## Three-line Summary

Case series of 8 human implant failures attributed to bone overheating during osteotomy: all specimens showed a consistent 6-feature histological pattern including bone sequestra, absence of peri-implant bone regeneration, and inflammatory infiltrate in the bone–implant gap.

Surrounding bone was compact and mature in all 8 cases, and bacteria were found around both the implant and necrotic bone — bacteria are secondary colonizers of pre-necrotic tissue, not the primary cause.

This 1998 Piattelli series established the **histological fingerprint of drill-induced thermal osteonecrosis** in human tissue and remains the foundational reference for distinguishing overheating failure from peri-implantitis and overcompression mechanisms.

## Summary

This 1998 paper from Piattelli et al. at the University of Chieti presented one of the earliest systematic human histological evaluations of implant failures attributed to surgical overheating. Eight retrieved failed implants, each examined by ground histological section, showed a strikingly uniform microscopical picture across all specimens. The six key features were: bone sequestra (fragments of dead devitalized bone), absence of peri-implant bone regeneration, an inflammatory infiltrate occupying the gap between the implant surface and the surrounding bone, failure of the peri-implant blood clot to organize, the presence of compact and mature surrounding bone, and bacteria colonizing both the implant surface and necrotic bone tissue. The uniform presence of these features across all 8 cases established them as a diagnostic pattern for thermal-osteonecrosis-mediated failure. The surrounding bone in all cases was described as compact and mature — consistent with placement into dense cortical bone where heat dissipation is reduced — and the bacterial presence was interpreted as secondary contamination of already-necrotic tissue rather than as the primary failure cause.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes the **6-feature human histological pattern of thermal osteonecrosis failure**: bone sequestra, absent regeneration, inflammatory gap, absent clot organization, compact surrounding bone, secondary bacterial colonization.
- Compact, mature surrounding bone as contextual finding: overheating is more likely in dense cortical bone (reduced vascular heat dissipation).
- Bacterial presence as secondary rather than primary: distinguishes overheating pattern from primary infection-driven failure.
- Foundational reference: cited by virtually all subsequent thermal-osteonecrosis and implant-failure histology papers.

## Methodology

Case series: 8 retrieved failed dental implants. Ground histological sections prepared from each specimen. Microscopical evaluation for presence/absence of the described features. No intraoperative temperature data; overheating attribution is inferential based on histological appearance.
