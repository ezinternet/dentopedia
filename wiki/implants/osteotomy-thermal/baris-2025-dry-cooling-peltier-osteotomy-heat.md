## Three-line Summary

A 4-group in vitro RCT (n=120, bovine rib bone) showed that a closed-loop Peltier-based dry cooling (DC) device outperformed external saline cooling (SC) on thermal control and bone particle preservation during dental implant osteotomy. DC at low pressure (DC-LP) produced the lowest temperature rise (ΔT 2.00 ± 0.84 °C; final 23.41 °C) versus SC at high pressure (SC-HP) which was highest (ΔT 5.45 ± 0.90 °C; final 26.38 °C); all groups remained far below the 47 °C necrosis threshold. DC groups yielded 2.75× more autogenous bone particles (28.53 vs 10.35 mg, p<0.001), an additional clinical advantage of eliminating irrigation fluid.

## Summary

Baris et al. (2025) conducted the first in vitro evaluation of a thermoelectric dry cooling (DC) system for dental implant osteotomy, comparing it against conventional external saline cooling (SC) under low (10 N) and high (20 N) hand pressure using standardized bovine rib specimens. The DC device operates via a closed-loop Peltier cooling module that maintains a cooler tip at approximately 9.5 °C (11.5 °C below ambient) adjacent to the drill entry point, without any saline flow. Key findings: DC-LP achieves the lowest heat generation; all four groups remain well below the 47 °C thermal necrosis threshold; and the absence of irrigation fluid in DC preserves significantly more autogenous bone particles. The study was randomized (Python-generated allocation), blinded, and followed CONSORT recommendations for in vitro reporting.

## Key Contributions

1. **First controlled evaluation of Peltier-based dry cooling** for implant osteotomy — establishes safety and efficacy vs. saline irrigation
2. **DC superior to SC at high pressure in cortical bone** — clinically relevant for mandibular D1/D2 sites where high-pressure drilling is unavoidable
3. **2.75× more autogenous bone particles** with DC (no irrigation fluid loss): DC 28.53 ± 2.46 mg vs SC 10.35 ± 2.64 mg (p<0.001)
4. **Improved operative visibility** — absence of irrigation fluid reduces visual obstruction
5. **Inverse time–temperature correlation** (r = −0.472): longer drilling under controlled conditions → less peak temperature, relevant for procedural technique optimization
6. All groups safely below 47 °C threshold even under high pressure with saline, confirming threshold robustness of modern cooling methods

## Methodology

- **Model**: Fresh bovine rib blocks (cortical bone 1.5–2 mm); n=30 per group; 4 osteotomies per rib specimen
- **Osteotomy**: Sequential protocol (pilot → 3.0 → 3.5 → 4.1 mm); depth 10 mm; 800 rpm; Nucleoss burs
- **Groups**: A = DC-LP (10 N), B = DC-HP (20 N), C = SC-LP (10 N), D = SC-HP (20 N)
- **DC system**: 5-component closed-loop — Peltier cooler, miniature pump, flexible pipes, custom cooler insert; tip ΔT: −11.5 ± 1 °C from ambient
- **SC system**: External irrigation 50 ml/min at 21 °C
- **Temperature**: Two K-type thermoprobes (0.4 mm diameter) placed at 5 mm depth, 0.9–1.1 mm from 3.5 mm osteotomy wall; positioned after 3.5 mm step, measuring during final 4.1 mm drill
- **Statistics**: ANOVA + Tukey; t-test; Pearson correlation; α = 0.05; G*Power n=120 (effect size 0.39, power 0.95)

## Results

### Temperature rise by group

| Group | Cooling | Pressure | ΔT (°C) | Final T (°C) |
|-------|---------|----------|---------|-------------|
| A (DC-LP) | Dry — Peltier | 10 N | **2.00 ± 0.84** | **23.41 ± 1.23** |
| B (DC-HP) | Dry — Peltier | 20 N | — | 24.00 ± 0.95 |
| C (SC-LP) | Saline external | 10 N | 3.45 ± 0.88 | 24.42 ± 1.19 |
| D (SC-HP) | Saline external | 20 N | **5.45 ± 0.90** | **26.38 ± 1.03** |

- Initial temperature: 20.95 ± 0.57 °C (homogeneous across groups, p=0.987)
- Group A < all other groups (Tukey, p<0.001); Group D > Groups B and C
- No statistically significant difference between Group B (DC-HP) and Group C (SC-LP) — DC at high pressure equivalent to saline at low pressure

### Bone particle yield

- DC groups: 28.53 ± 2.46 mg; SC groups: 10.35 ± 2.64 mg (p<0.001)

### Drilling time

- Range: Group B shortest (21.13 ± 0.78 s) to Group C longest (25.30 ± 0.89 s); overall mean 23.24 ± 2.14 s
- Increasing pressure → shorter drilling time (p<0.001)

### Key correlation

- Drilling time vs ΔT: r = −0.472 (p<0.001) — shorter drilling = higher peak temperature
