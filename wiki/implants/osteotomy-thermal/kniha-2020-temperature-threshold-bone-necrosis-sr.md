## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (Materials 2020; PROSPERO CRD42019141867; MEDLINE/Cochrane/Embase/WoS through Nov 2019): 25 in vivo animal studies, 455 animals (224 rats, 117 rabbits, 70 emus, 41 sheep, 10 dogs); no meta-analysis due to heterogeneity.

The thermal bone necrosis threshold ranges **47–55°C for 1 min** across species and bone sites; Eriksson 1983's 47°C/1min is the lower bound; cancellous bone significantly more susceptible than cortical bone (50°C/1min cancellous BIC decreased, cortical BIC unchanged); no single validated threshold.

Clinical practice's 47°C/1min safety limit is an evidence-supported lower bound, not a sharp demarcation — the actual risk zone is broader, and cortical bone mandibular drilling carries lower risk than cancellous maxillary implant sites.

## 세줄요약

체계적 고찰 (Systematic Review, SR) (Materials 2020; PROSPERO CRD42019141867; MEDLINE/Cochrane/Embase/WoS, 2019년 11월까지): 동물 대상 생체 내 연구 25편, 455마리(쥐 224, 토끼 117, 에뮤 70, 양 41, 개 10), 이질성 과다로 메타분석 미시행.

열 골괴사 (Thermal Osteonecrosis) 임계값은 연구마다 **47–55°C/1분** 범위 — 단일 명확한 임계값 없음; 47°C/1분은 하한선; 해면골 (Cancellous Bone)이 피질골 (Cortical Bone)보다 유의하게 더 취약(50°C/1분에서 해면골 골-임플란트 접촉률 (Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC) 유의하게 감소, 피질골 BIC 변화 없음).

47°C/1분 기준은 임상적으로 타당한 하한선이지만 뚜렷한 경계선이 아닌 확률적 위험 구간 — 실제 위험 범위는 더 넓으며, 피질골이 많은 하악 임플란트 부위가 해면골이 많은 상악보다 열 손상 위험이 낮음.

## Summary

This PROSPERO-registered systematic review (Aachen group) synthesized 25 preclinical in vivo animal studies through November 2019 to define temperature-time thresholds for thermal bone necrosis, motivated by thermo-explantation research. The central finding: **no single clear threshold value exists**. The literature range spans 47–55°C for 1 min of exposure. The well-known Eriksson & Albrektsson 47°C/1min value represents the lower limit of detectable damage (first tissue reactions: hyperemia) rather than a confirmed universal necrosis threshold. Bone quality emerges as a critical moderating variable — cancellous bone suffers significantly greater damage at the same temperature/time, while cortical bone shows substantial resilience even at 50°C/1min.

For the thermo-explantation application specifically, BIC values significantly decreased and infrabony pockets significantly increased around cancellous-sited implants at 50°C/1min, while cortical-sited implants showed no significant changes at the same parameters. This distinction is clinically important: mandibular implants (more cortical) tolerate more heat than maxillary implants (more cancellous) under equivalent thermal input.

The review could not synthesize laser data into a threshold due to extreme device heterogeneity. Cold stimulus data (cryoinsult) also lacks a defined threshold; temperatures around 1–3.5°C produce histologically proven bone necrosis in emu models. No meta-analysis was possible due to high clinical heterogeneity.

## Results — Warm Threshold

| Temperature | Duration | Bone Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 44°C | 1 min | No damage |
| 47°C | 1 min | First reactions (hyperemia) — Eriksson lower bound |
| 48°C | 1 min | Osteocyte apoptosis; 5 wks to regain formation (Yoshida) |
| 48–50°C | 1 min | Likely heat boundary (most studies, matches Lundskog) |
| 50°C | 30 s | Threshold value — Lundskog 1972 |
| 50°C | 1 min | Cancellous BIC ↓ significantly; cortical BIC unchanged |
| 55°C | 1 min | Threshold per Rouiller 1953 |
| 60°C | 1 min | Cancellous BIC 25.42% vs 38.05% control (p<0.01) |
| 180–190°C | seconds | No necrosis (Arnoldi 2012) — time overrides temperature |

## Results — Bone Quality Moderator

Cancellous bone (iliac crest) vs cortical bone (mandible) at **50°C/1 min**:
- Cancellous: significantly lower BIC, significantly larger infrabony pockets
- Cortical: no significant difference in either BIC or pockets

At **60°C/1 min**: both tissue types affected, but cancellous effect still larger.

## Clinical Implications

1. **47°C/1 min is a floor, not a ceiling**: clinical practice is correct to use this as the safety limit, but the true "safe" temperature in cancellous bone may be lower — 47°C is the onset of detectable tissue reactions, not guaranteed necrosis.
2. **Bone type matters more than the number**: mandibular (cortical-dominant) drilling has substantially more thermal tolerance than maxillary (cancellous-dominant) drilling under identical protocol conditions.
3. **Species and site non-transferability**: rabbit tibia, rat calvaria, sheep iliac crest, and emus give different thresholds — none directly validates human jaw response.
4. **Thermo-explantation requires narrower window than implant drilling avoidance**: the goal is controlled minimal necrosis in a 47–55°C range — a technically demanding target with no validated human data.
