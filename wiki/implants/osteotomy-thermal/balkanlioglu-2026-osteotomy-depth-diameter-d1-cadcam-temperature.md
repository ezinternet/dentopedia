## Three-line Summary

In a 3×3 factorial in vitro experiment using bovine D1-like dense cortical bone, osteotomy depth (η²=0.82) was the overwhelmingly dominant factor for intraosseous temperature rise, far exceeding drill diameter (η²=0.17). All 90 osteotomies stayed below the 47°C necrosis threshold with continuous irrigation; Tmax ranged 33.3–41.7°C. A linear rule of +0.63°C per 1-mm depth increase (R²=0.785) provides a practical clinical planning heuristic.

## Summary

Balkanlioğlu and Acikan (2026) conducted a standardized in vitro study to quantify the relative contributions of osteotomy depth and drill diameter to maximum intraosseous temperature (Tmax) during implant drilling in dense cortical bone. Using fresh bovine femoral cortical bone as a D1 analog and CAD/CAM-fabricated 3D-printed guides to precisely position K-type thermocouples at 1 mm lateral from osteotomy walls, they tested all combinations of three drill diameters (3.5, 4.0, 4.5 mm) and three depths (6.5, 11, 15 mm) with 10 replicates each (n=90).

Two-way ANOVA revealed that depth accounted for 82% of Tmax variance (η²=0.82, F=3883.56), while diameter accounted for 17% (η²=0.17, F=803.44). A significant depth×diameter interaction was also present (p<0.001). The depth–Tmax relationship was strongly linear: r=0.886, slope +0.63°C/mm, R²=0.785. Across all conditions with continuous irrigation, Tmax ranged 33.3–41.7°C — never exceeding the classical 47°C osteonecrosis threshold.

The methodological innovation — CAD/CAM-guided thermocouple standardization — reduces placement variability that has confounded prior thermocouple studies, making the effect-size estimates more reliable than most previous work in this category.

## Methodology

- **Model**: Fresh bovine femoral cortical bone — D1-like dense cortical; no cancellous component
- **Design**: 3×3 factorial — diameters (3.5/4.0/4.5 mm) × depths (6.5/11/15 mm); 10 per cell; n=90
- **Measurement**: Two K-type thermocouples per osteotomy, via 3D-printed guides at 1 mm lateral/apical
- **Irrigation**: Continuous external irrigation throughout
- **Outcome**: Tmax (peak temperature only; CEM43 not measured)
- **Statistics**: Two-way ANOVA, Tukey HSD post-hoc, Pearson r, linear regression

## Results

| Group | Tmax (°C) range | Key statistic |
|---|---|---|
| All combined | 33.3–41.7 | Below 47°C threshold |
| Depth effect | — | F(2,81)=3883.56; η²=0.82 |
| Diameter effect | — | F(2,81)=803.44; η²=0.17 |
| Interaction | — | F(4,81)=11.36; p<0.001 |
| Depth–Tmax regression | +0.63°C per mm | r=0.886; R²=0.785 |

**Interpretation**: At the deepest tested osteotomy (15mm), Tmax peaked at 41.7°C — a 5.3°C safety margin below 47°C under continuous irrigation. The linear regression implies that without irrigation, reaching depths >21mm from a 47°C ceiling (if baseline ≈33°C) could theoretically breach the threshold — though this extrapolation exceeds study conditions.
