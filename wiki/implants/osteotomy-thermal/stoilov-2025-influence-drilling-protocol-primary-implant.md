## Three-line Summary

In vitro bench study (390 groups × 6 sites = 2340 osteotomies per bone density, N = 2340) comparing six drilling protocols by peak insertion torque (IT) in rigid polyurethane foam at 35/25/15/10 PCF (D1–D4), across three SIC macro-designs × 4 diameters (3.4–5.2 mm) × 3 lengths (7.5–14.5 mm).

Drilling protocol shifted IT highly significantly at every density (D1 F₁,₃₉₈ = 115.5; D2 F₂,₄₅₃ = 850; D3 F₂,₆₇₁ = 924; D4 F₂,₄₈₅ = 241; all p < 0.001) with under-sizing > standard > over-sizing; target-window attainment rose from 3–4/10 to 7/10 for SICmax and SICtapered in D1, but was 0/10 for every implant and protocol at 10 PCF.

The recommendation is density-conditional, not universal — over-prepare D1 (5.2 mm implants exceeded the 80 Ncm ceiling), under-prepare small D2 diameters, prefer wide tapered implants in D3, and treat D4 as unreachable by drilling modification alone — and it rests on single-system PU-foam torque-only data with an internally inconsistent target window (25–50 Ncm in Methods vs 25–30 Ncm in all figure legends).

## 세줄요약

In vitro 벤치 연구 (390군 × 6사이트 = 골밀도별 2,340개 골절개, N = 2340) — 강경질 폴리우레탄 폼(Polyurethane Foam, PU) 35/25/15/10 PCF(치밀도, Pounds per Cubic Foot)에서 6가지 드릴링 프로토콜이 삽입토크(Insertion Torque, IT)에 미치는 영향을 3종 거시설계 × 4직경(3.4–5.2 mm) × 3길이(7.5–14.5 mm)로 평가.

모든 골밀도에서 드릴링 프로토콜이 IT에 매우 유의한 영향을 미쳤음(적정범위 25–50 Ncm 도달률 기준 D1·D2 SICmax/SICtapered가 3–4/10 → 7/10으로 향상, 그러나 10 PCF에서는 모든 임플란트·프로토콜이 0/10).

권고는 보편적이지 않고 골밀도별 조건부임 — D1은 과대직경 준비(Overpreparation)로 5.2 mm 임플란트의 80 Ncm 초과를 막고, D2는 소직경에 언더사이즈 준비, D3는 넓은 테이퍼형, D4는 드릴링 조정만으로 부족하다고 볼 것. 단일 임플란트 시스템·PU 폼·삽입토크만 측정한 벤치 데이터이며 목표 토크 범위가 본문(25–50 Ncm)과 도표(25–30 Ncm) 간 일관되지 않음.

## Summary

This bench study asks whether adapting the osteotomy drilling protocol to bone density can pull peak insertion torque into the immediate-loading window where the manufacturer's standard protocol misses it. Six protocols — standard, crestal-drill omission, two over-sized preparations and two under-sized preparations — were machine-drilled in rigid polyurethane foam at four densities spanning D1 to D4, then crossed with three SIC macro-designs across four diameters and three lengths. Protocol turned out to be a highly significant determinant of torque at every density (all p < 0.001), with a consistent direction: under-sized preparations raise torque, over-sized preparations lower it. The clinical payoff is narrow but real: in D1, adapting the protocol lifted target-window attainment from 3–4/10 to 7/10 for SICmax and SICtapered, and in D2 under-preparation was the only route above 25 Ncm at 3.7–4.2 mm. Two negative findings matter as much. First, at 10 PCF no implant–protocol combination reached the target range at all, so drilling modification is exhausted as a strategy in very soft bone. Second, macro-geometry mattered much less than the protocol dimension: parallel-walled implants equalled or beat the tapered design, and tapered-vs-parallel differences were non-significant in D3. As bench data with a single implant family, no biological endpoint, and an internally inconsistent optimum window (25–50 Ncm in the Methods versus 25–30 Ncm in every figure caption), this paper informs surgical plausibility rather than clinical outcomes.

## Key Contributions
- Systematic protocol × density × design × dimension crossing that isolates the drilling protocol as the manipulated variable, on CNC-machined osteotomies that remove manual drilling error.
- Protocol effect on IT is highly significant at all four densities (D1 F₁,₃₉₈ = 115.5; D2 F₂,₄₅₃ = 850; D3 F₂,₆₇₁ = 924; D4 F₂,₄₈₅ = 241; all p < 0.001) with under-sizing > standard > over-sizing.
- First density-resolved demonstration that drilling modification is ineffective in very soft bone — 0/10 target-window attainment at 10 PCF across all designs and protocols.
- Quantifies the D1 over-torque hazard: 5.2 mm SICmax and SICtapered exceeded the surgical motor's 80 Ncm ceiling and could not be seated flush.
- Corrects the direction of adaptation in hard bone: over-preparation, not under-preparation, is what D1 needs to avoid compression necrosis.
- Macro-geometry is demoted to a minor factor — parallel-walled equalled or exceeded tapered, and tapered's D3 advantage was not statistically significant.

## Methodology
In vitro bench study on ASTM F1839-08 rigid polyurethane foam in single-layer blocks, chosen so stability derives from apical engagement rather than crestal cortical support (mimicking the immediate-placement scenario). 390 groups × 6 implants = 2340 osteotomies per bone density (N = 2340 as reported). Three SIC invent macro-designs — SICace (parallel, 7.5 mm), SICmax (parallel, 9.5 mm), SICtapered (tapered, 13 mm), extending to 14.5 mm at larger diameters — all zirconia-blasted and acid-cleaned (Sa = 1.0 µm). Densities 35/25/15/10 PCF mapped to D1–D4. Protocols: S (standard drill, 100% depth, crestal drill, no tap), S2 (crestal drill omitted), O1 (next-larger drill), O2 (next-larger drill + tap), U1 (standard drill at 70% depth, no crestal drill), U2 (previous-smaller drill, 100% depth, no crestal drill). Osteotomies were CNC-milled with original SIC drills at 300 rpm; insertion by an experienced implantologist at 25 rpm on a torque-controlled motor (80 Ncm ceiling) with peak IT recorded. Linear mixed models per density with implant specification as random and design + protocol as fixed effects; log-transformed IT where needed; Bonferroni correction; Satterthwaite estimates; α = 0.05. Implants hitting the 80 Ncm ceiling before seating were measured for protrusion and excluded from statistics.

## Results

Protocol effect on insertion torque (linear mixed models, all p < 0.001):

| Bone density | Test statistic | p |
|---|---|---|
| 35 PCF (D1) | F₁,₃₉₈ = 115.5 | < 0.001 |
| 25 PCF (D2) | F₂,₄₅₃ = 850 | < 0.001 |
| 15 PCF (D3) | F₂,₆₇₁ = 924 | < 0.001 |
| 10 PCF (D4) | F₂,₄₈₅ = 241 | < 0.001 |

Direction, consistent at every density: **under-sized > standard > over-sized.**

| Density | Design | Standard (S) | Under-sized | Over-sized | Crestal drill omitted (S2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 35 PCF (D1) | SICace | 4/10 | — | 4/10 | — |
| 35 PCF (D1) | SICmax | 3/10 | — | 7/10 | — |
| 35 PCF (D1) | SICtapered | 4/10 | — | 7/10 | — |
| 25 PCF (D2) | SICace | 4/10 | 4/10 (U1) | 4/10 (O1) | — |
| 25 PCF (D2) | SICmax | 4/10 | 5/10 (U1) | 3/10 (O1) | — |
| 25 PCF (D2) | SICtapered | not reported | not reported | not reported | — |
| 15 PCF (D3) | SICace | 0/10 | 2/10 (U1), 2/10 (U2) | — | 1/10 |
| 15 PCF (D3) | SICmax | 2/10 | 3/10 (U1), 3/10 (U2) | — | 3/10 |
| 10 PCF (D4) | all designs | 0/10 | 0/10 | — | — |

Per-density operative findings:

| Density | Finding | Clinical reading |
|---|---|---|
| D1 (35 PCF) | Over-preparation reduced IT in every group; 5.2 mm SICmax/SICtapered exceeded 80 Ncm → motor stop, incomplete seating | Over-preparation protects bone from compression necrosis, but wide implants need a pre-planned anti-overtorque strategy |
| D2 (25 PCF) | Over-preparation reduced IT consistently; under-preparation was the only way above 25 Ncm at 3.7–4.2 mm; 5.2 × 14.5 mm reached acceptable torque only *with* over-preparation | Under-prepare small diameters, standard suffices for wide ones, over-prepare the widest |
| D3 (15 PCF) | Undersized osteotomies gave slight IT gains; crestal-drill omission helped only in 3 subgroups (SICace 4.5 × 9.5, SICmax 4.7 × 9.5, SICtapered 4.2 × 13); diameter↔IT positive but length↔IT inconsistent; tapered > parallel, not significant | No drilling tweak rescues D3 — choose wide tapered implants instead |
| D4 (10 PCF) | Diameter effect minimal; under-preparation raised IT only slightly for SICmax/SICtapered; nothing reached the window | Drilling modification exhausted — adjunctive strategies needed |

Macro-geometry and dimension: the three designs differed little at every density. Parallel-walled SICace/SICmax matched or exceeded tapered SICtapered in D4, and SICmax tended to exceed SICtapered under standard preparation; SICtapered ran slightly elevated in several D2 configurations and slightly higher in D3 (non-significant). Diameter was the dominant geometric driver; length was minor, with no consistent length↔torque relation even in D1/D2.
