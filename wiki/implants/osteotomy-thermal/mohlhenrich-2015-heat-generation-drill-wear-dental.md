## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (English-language MEDLINE/PubMed, Jan 2000–Feb 2014; 41 articles found, 27 on temperature increase) of heat generation and drill wear during dental implant osteotomy preparation.

Measured temperatures ranged 28.4–64.4°C across studies (thermocouples in 18, infrared thermography in 7, immunohistochemistry for cell viability in 3); drill wear after preparing 50 sites produced a significant temperature increase and a small shift in bone-cell protein balance — nearly all studies were in vitro, non-vital bovine/porcine bone, low evidence level.

Heterogeneous designs made meta-analysis inappropriate; authors recommend standardizing future work on axial load 2 kg, drilling speed 1500 rpm, irrigation, standard artificial bone blocks, and infrared thermography.

## Summary

This systematic review searched MEDLINE/PubMed for English-language studies published January 2000 to February 2014 on heat generation during dental implant osteotomy preparation, identifying 41 articles of which 27 addressed temperature increase directly. The included evidence was uniformly basic research at a low level of evidence — predominantly in vitro studies using non-vital bovine or porcine bone rather than living human tissue. Real-time temperature was measured by thermocouple in 18 studies and infrared thermography in 7; three studies used immunohistochemistry to assess immediate bone-cell viability after heat exposure. Measured temperatures spanned 28.4°C to 64.4°C across the included literature. Drill wear, evaluated after preparation of 50 sites, produced a significant temperature increase along with a small shift in the physiological protein balance of bone cells. Because study designs varied too widely, the authors judged meta-analysis inappropriate and instead recommend future studies standardize on an axial load of 2 kg, drilling speed of 1500 rpm, irrigation, standard artificial bone blocks, and infrared thermography for measurement.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes the empirical temperature range (28.4–64.4°C) that the wiki's broader osteotomy-thermal literature cluster is built around.
- Maps the field's measurement methodology early: thermocouple vs infrared thermography vs immunohistochemical viability assay.
- Quantifies a literature-level drill-wear effect (significant temperature increase + altered bone-cell protein balance after 50 sites) predating single-study confirmations.
- Names the heterogeneity problem — meta-analysis not appropriate — that every later SR in this folder (chauhan 2018, chakraborty 2024) independently re-confirms.
- Proposes a standardization protocol (2 kg axial load, 1500 rpm, irrigation, standard artificial bone blocks, infrared thermography) as a benchmark for comparable future studies.

## Methodology

Systematic review, MEDLINE/PubMed only, English-language studies Jan 2000–Feb 2014. 41 articles identified; 27 analyzed for temperature increase during implant site preparation (drill wear assessed separately across 50 prepared sites in the included literature). No meta-analysis — narrative synthesis only, due to study-design heterogeneity.

## Limitations

- Abstract-only ingest — no PMC full text; per-study citations and any risk-of-bias tool are unrecoverable.
- Low evidence level throughout — basic research, not clinical outcome data.
- Predominantly non-vital bovine/porcine bone, not living human tissue — perfusion and in vivo cooling not captured.
- No meta-analysis possible due to heterogeneous study designs.
- Pre-2014 search cutoff — osseodensification, modern piezosurgery, and guided-sleeve drilling systems covered by later SRs are not reflected.
