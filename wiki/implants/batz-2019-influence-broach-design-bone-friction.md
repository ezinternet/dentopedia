## Three-line Summary

Quasi-static bench experiment on ex vivo bovine trabecular bone cuboids comparing three total hip arthroplasty (THA) broach designs — compaction, blunt extraction, and sharp extraction — with broaching forces measured and micro-computed tomography (micro-CT) before and after testing.

Compaction and blunt extraction increased total bone densification by 121% and 117% over sharp extraction (P = .005) — arising from greater densification depth for compaction (P = .001) and greater maximum densification for blunt extraction (P = .008) — while sharp extraction friction coefficients exceeded the other two by 38% and 37% (P < .001), and blunt extraction produced fewer large debris particles than compaction (P = .005).

Blunt and compaction geometries densified similarly despite opposite working directions, implicating face geometry rather than direction of travel as the operative variable; but this is an orthopaedic, quasi-static, ex vivo bovine broaching model that tests no dental instrument, so it informs mechanism only — it transfers no clinical number to implant dentistry.

## 세줄요약

준정적(quasi-static) 벤치 실험 — 생체외 소 해면골 육면체(bovine trabecular bone cuboid)에서 인공 고관절 치환술(Total Hip Arthroplasty, THA)용 브로치(broach) 3종(압착형 compaction, 무딘 추출형 blunt extraction, 예리한 추출형 sharp extraction)의 broaching 힘을 계측하고 시험 전후 마이크로 CT(micro-computed tomography, micro-CT)로 촬영.

압착형·무딘 추출형은 예리한 추출형 대비 총 골밀도화(Osseodensification, OD)를 각각 121%·117% 증가시켰고(P = .005), 이는 압착형의 더 깊은 밀도화 깊이(P = .001)와 무딘 추출형의 더 높은 최대 밀도화(P = .008)에서 기인; 예리한 추출형의 마찰계수는 나머지 둘보다 38%·37% 높았으며(P < .001), 무딘 추출형은 압착형보다 큰 골편(debris) 입자를 적게 생성(P = .005).

작업 방향이 반대인데도 무딘 형상과 압착 형상이 비슷하게 밀도화했다는 점이 **방향이 아닌 날 형상(geometry)**을 작동 변수로 지목 — 다만 치과 기구를 전혀 시험하지 않은 정형외과·준정적·생체외 소뼈 broaching 모델이므로 기전 시사에 그치며, 임상 수치는 전이되지 않는다.

## Summary

This is an **orthopaedic** bench study — femoral cavity preparation for uncemented hip stems — held in this wiki for a mechanistic reason: it is the only paper we hold that **experimentally separates instrument geometry from working direction** in bone densification, the same process dentistry calls 골밀도화 (Osseodensification, OD).

The design is what matters. Three broach models — **compaction**, **blunt extraction**, **sharp extraction** — were driven quasi-statically into bovine trabecular bone cuboids, with forces measured and micro-CT before and after. The load-bearing comparison is **blunt extraction vs sharp extraction**: identical direction of travel, differing only in sharpness. Only the blunt one densified, and it did so **+117% over sharp extraction (P = .005)** — statistically alongside the purpose-built compaction broach (+121%). An instrument travelling in the direction that "should" cut still densified, because its geometry was blunt.

That result speaks directly to an open question in this wiki. [[implants/huwais-2017-novel-osseous-densification-osteotomy-primary-stability]] established OD by running the *same* bur clockwise (extraction) and counterclockwise (densify) and finding markedly different results — routinely read as *rotation direction causes densification*. Bätz suggests a different parse: rotation **selects which face engages the bone**, and it is the **face geometry** that densifies or cuts. This is exactly what the Densah patent [[implants/huwais-2017-autografting-tool-enhanced-flute-profile]] asserts with its negative-rake dual-face flute (cutting face + densifying face) — but that patent contains no measurements at all. Bätz is the first quantitative support this wiki holds for that geometric premise, and it arrives from a fully independent group with no stake in the dental OD market. It is likewise the first direct measurement behind the premise of [[implants/versah-protocols/rittipakorn-2025-clockwise-osseodensification-primary-stability-cadaveric]], which ran a densifying bur **clockwise** on the hypothesis that its non-sharp geometry compacts anyway — a study whose ISQ and insertion-torque outcomes were non-significant (p = 0.077, 0.052) and whose densification evidence was qualitative SR-µCT only.

The second contribution is that Bätz **decomposes densification into separable parameters**: total densification was similar for compaction and blunt extraction, but *distributed differently* — compaction reached **greater depth** (P = .001), blunt extraction reached **higher maximum density** (P = .008). Same total, different distribution. This is the closest proxy the wiki holds for the lateral-vs-apical distribution question raised by the HaeNaem condensing-screw patent [[implants/kim-2019-double-spiral-condensing-screw-implant]] — though Bätz tests no dental instrument and **validates nothing about that device**.

Two further findings stand on their own. **Friction coefficients** were 38%/37% higher for sharp extraction (P < .001), which the authors read as *lower* periprosthetic fracture risk for sharp broaches at equal implantation force — more applied force absorbed in friction, less driving cortex strain. Note this fracture conclusion is a **force-balance inference; no fractures were produced or counted**. And **blunt extraction produced fewer large debris particles than compaction** (P = .005), which the authors argue may favour osseointegration — a plausible but untested biological claim in a model with no biology.

**How far this does not travel.** A broach is impacted **axially**; a dental densifying bur **rotates at 800–1500 rpm**. "Extraction direction" in a broach and "clockwise rotation" in a bur are analogous only at the level of which face meets bone — not mechanically equivalent. The loading is **quasi-static**, so the strain-rate-dependent visco-elastic behaviour and strain-hardening window that the OD literature leans on are not reproduced. The specimens are **uniform bovine trabecular cuboids**, ex vivo — no cortex boundary conditions, no D1–D4 jaw variability, no sinus, no biology, no healing, no irrigation. And the broaches are simplified **geometric models**, not commercial instruments: strong internal validity, weak direct transfer. This page is evidence about a *mechanism*, and should never be cited as evidence about a dental bur.

## Key Contributions

- **Isolates geometry from working direction** — blunt vs sharp extraction holds direction constant and flips only sharpness; only the blunt geometry densifies (+117%, P = .005). The first held measurement bearing on "geometry, not direction, densifies."
- **Decomposes densification into depth vs maximum** — compaction reached greater depth (P = .001), blunt extraction greater maximum density (P = .008), at similar totals. Distribution and magnitude are separable parameters.
- **Quantifies broaching friction coefficients** (sharp extraction +38%/+37%, P < .001) and ties them to periprosthetic fracture risk by force balance.
- **Measures debris particle size as a design outcome** — blunt extraction produced fewer large particles than compaction (P = .005), argued to favour osseointegration.
- Supplies an **orthopaedic bench line of evidence on osseodensification independent of the dental OD literature** and its inventor-driven conflicts of interest.

## Methodology

- **Design**: quasi-static bench simulation of femoral cavity preparation (not rotary drilling; not surgical-rate impaction).
- **Specimen**: ex vivo bovine trabecular bone cuboids (MeSH: Cattle; Femur).
- **Groups**: three broach *models* representing **compaction**, **blunt extraction**, and **sharp extraction** designs — idealized geometries standing in for commercial broach families.
- **Measurements**: broaching forces → friction coefficients; micro-CT before and after → total densification, densification depth, maximum densification, and broached-bone-bed microstructure; size of debris particles pushed into bone.
- **n / statistics**: sample size per group, statistical tests, and effect estimates are **not recoverable from the abstract** (full text not retrieved). Only the P values below are available.
- **Outcomes**: friction coefficient; densification (total / depth / maximum); debris particle size; periprosthetic fracture risk (inferred, not measured).

## Results

| Outcome | Finding | P |
|---|---|---|
| Friction coefficient | Sharp extraction **> compaction by 38%**, **> blunt extraction by 37%** | < .001 |
| Total bone densification | Compaction **+121%**, blunt extraction **+117%** vs sharp extraction | .005 |
| Densification depth | Higher for **compaction** | .001 |
| Maximum densification | Higher for **blunt extraction** | .008 |
| Large debris particles | **Fewer** with blunt extraction than compaction | .005 |

**Authors' conclusions**
- Higher friction coefficients indicate **decreased periprosthetic fracture risk with sharp extraction broaches** at equal implantation forces.
- The blunt extraction and compaction designs investigated **densified bone to a similar extent**.
- Blunt extraction **may support better osseointegration** due to smaller bone debris particles.

**Caveats**: abstract-only (no means, SDs, or n); orthopaedic broaching, not dental drilling; quasi-static, not 800–1500 rpm; ex vivo bovine trabecular cuboids, not jaw bone; broach *models*, not commercial instruments; fracture risk inferred from force balance, never observed; no temperature measured.
