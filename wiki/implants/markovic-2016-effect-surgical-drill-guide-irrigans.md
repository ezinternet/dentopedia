## Three-line Summary

In vitro thermographic study, 48 bovine rib specimens, 2×2 factorial design (surgical drill guide present/absent × saline irrigant at 25°C vs 5°C cooled), measuring bone temperature change at 3 osteotomy depths (entrance/middle/bottom) during pilot-drill implant site preparation.

Drill guide use significantly raised bone temperature at the osteotomy entrance only (p<0.001; max increase 7.48°C with guide + 25°C saline); cooled 5°C saline significantly lowered bone temperature at all three depths (p<0.001) regardless of guide use, and also reduced drill-guide sleeve heating itself (median difference 2.785–7.185°C, p<0.001); guide temperature and entrance bone temperature correlated strongly (Spearman rho=0.868, p<0.001).

No experimental condition exceeded the 47°C/1-minute thermal-necrosis threshold, but guided drilling adds measurable heat at the cortical entrance where the metal sleeve blocks external irrigation — cooled irrigant is the most consistent mitigation regardless of surgical technique; limited by the bovine-rib (not human jaw) bone model.

## Summary

This in vitro thermographic study used 48 bovine rib specimens in a 2×2 factorial design to test how surgical drill guide use and saline irrigant temperature (25°C vs 5°C) affect bone temperature during pilot-drill implant osteotomy. Real-time infrared thermography measured temperature change at three depths (entrance, middle, bottom). The drill guide significantly increased bone heating, but only at the entrance/cortical level (p<0.001), where the metal sleeve most obstructs irrigation; cooled 5°C saline significantly reduced bone heating at all three depths regardless of guide use, and also reduced heating of the drill guide itself. Guide temperature correlated strongly with adjacent entrance bone temperature (Spearman rho=0.868), supporting a conductive-heat mechanism from the metal sleeve in addition to irrigation obstruction. No condition breached the 47°C/1-minute necrosis threshold. Clinically, cooled irrigant is recommended as a technique-independent heat-control strategy for guided implant surgery, particularly at the cortical entrance.

## Key Contributions
- Isolates the drill-guide thermal penalty to the cortical entrance depth specifically — not a uniform effect along the osteotomy, refining the blanket "guided drilling = hotter" claim.
- Quantifies the mitigating effect of cooled (5°C) saline across depths and shows it also cools the drill guide sleeve itself.
- Demonstrates a strong positive correlation (rho=0.868) between drill-guide temperature and adjacent bone temperature, evidencing a conductive-heat contribution alongside irrigation obstruction.

## Methodology
In vitro, randomized 2×2 factorial design (drill guide with/without × saline at 25°C/5°C), 48 bovine rib specimens (12/group), pilot drill (2.35 mm, SKYplanX/Bredent) at 600 rpm with 50 mL/min irrigation, W&H handpiece. Real-time infrared thermography (Varioscan R 3021, ±0.03°C) measured bone temperature at entrance/middle/bottom depths; Brunner-Langer nonparametric longitudinal analysis for the factorial effects, Exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test for drill-guide temperature comparison, Spearman correlation for guide-vs-bone temperature relationship.

## Clinical Implications
- Prioritize cooled (~5°C) saline irrigation in guided implant surgery, especially at the cortical entrance where guide-sleeve obstruction concentrates heat.
- The guide's thermal penalty is most relevant at the entrance level — deeper osteotomy heating is driven primarily by saline temperature, not guide presence.
- Drill-guide sleeve heat itself contributes to adjacent bone warming (conduction), reinforcing the case for guide designs that allow better irrigant access to the cortex-penetration zone.
