## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (AMSTAR-2 appraisal of systematic reviews; search to October 2020; 4 included SRs pooling 722 implants in 189 Sjögren's syndrome patients) evaluating the methodological quality of evidence on dental implants in Sjögren's syndrome.

Pooled implant success rate across the 4 SRs was 95.22% (follow-up 45.2 ± 23.8 to 125.5 months), but AMSTAR-2 rated 3 of the 4 SRs as critically low quality and 1 as low quality.

Dental implants appear clinically favorable in Sjögren's syndrome, but the underlying systematic-review evidence base is methodologically weak, so the reassuring success-rate figures should be interpreted with caution pending better-designed primary and secondary studies.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1111/scd.12591](https://doi.org/10.1111/scd.12591); abstract-only — full text not retrieved, Wiley journal with no PMC ID available), this umbrella review addresses whether Sjögren's syndrome (SS) should be considered a risk factor or contraindication for dental implants, given long-standing clinical concern that SS-driven xerostomia might elevate implant failure risk. Rather than pooling primary-study outcomes directly, the authors searched PubMed/Medline, LILACS, Science Direct, and DARE/Cochrane to October 2020, screened 833 initially identified articles, and retained 4 systematic reviews for detailed methodological appraisal using the AMSTAR-2 tool. Across those 4 SRs, the aggregate evidence comprised 722 implants placed in 189 SS patients, with a 95.22% pooled success rate and follow-up ranging from a minimum of 45.2 ± 23.8 months to a maximum of 125.5 months. Despite these reassuring outcome figures, AMSTAR-2 appraisal found 3 of the 4 SRs to be of critically low methodological quality and the remaining 1 to be of low quality — none reached moderate or high confidence.

Clinically, the paper's conclusion is two-layered: oral rehabilitation with dental implants in SS patients "seems to have a high success rate," but the low quality of the SRs underpinning that success rate means the reassurance is provisional. The umbrella review does not itself provide new outcome data — its contribution is a critical-appraisal layer flagging that the evidence base, while directionally favorable, has not yet been established with rigorous systematic-review methodology.

## Key Contributions

- First umbrella review (per the authors) to apply AMSTAR-2 methodological appraisal specifically to systematic reviews on dental implants in Sjögren's syndrome.
- Aggregates the total published SR-level evidence: 4 SRs, 722 implants, 189 patients, 95.22% success rate.
- Demonstrates a quality gap between favorable outcome numbers and weak underlying review methodology (3/4 critically low, 1/4 low by AMSTAR-2).
- Shifts the clinical framing from "do implants work in SS?" to "how confidently can we say implants work in SS, given current review quality?"

## Methodology

- **Design**: umbrella review (systematic review of systematic reviews); methodological quality appraisal of included SRs rather than a fresh outcome meta-analysis.
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, LILACS, Science Direct, DARE/Cochrane, searched to October 2020.
- **Screening**: 833 articles initially identified → inclusion/exclusion criteria applied → 4 SRs retained for detailed analysis.
- **Quality appraisal tool**: AMSTAR 2.
- Abstract-only source; further methodological detail (number of independent reviewers, specific AMSTAR-2 domain failures, which 4 SRs were included, PROSPERO registration) is not recoverable without the full Wiley-hosted text.

## Results

- **Included evidence**: 4 systematic reviews spanning 722 implants in 189 Sjögren's syndrome patients.
- **Follow-up**: minimum 45.2 ± 23.8 months; maximum 125.5 months.
- **Success rate**: 95.22% pooled across the 4 SRs.
- **AMSTAR-2 grading**: 3 of 4 SRs critically low quality; 1 of 4 low quality; none moderate or high.
