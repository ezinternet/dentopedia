## Three-line Summary

Bench + randomized single-blind controlled trial (J Funct Biomater 2026;17:96, Hallym University / Seoul National University Bundang Hospital; 80 enrolled, 77 analyzed) comparing a β-type Ti-Nb-Zr (TNZ) implant alloy against cold-worked modified Grade 4 cpTi (MG4T).

TNZ delivered higher tensile strength (1,139 vs 900 MPa) and roughly four times the fatigue life (1.06M vs 0.25M cycles) at a *lower* elastic modulus (75 vs 110 GPa); clinically both groups showed 100% success and survival over 12 months with no marginal bone loss >1 mm.

The lower modulus produced *less*, not more, peri-implant trabecular complexity (fractal dimension), so the orthopedic stress-shielding rationale does not transfer straightforwardly to dental implants — bulk stiffness appears secondary to design and interface mechanics.

## Summary

This paper couples a materials characterization arm with a 12-month randomized clinical trial to argue that a β-type Ti-Nb-Zr alloy is a mechanically superior alternative to the cold-worked Grade 4 cpTi that dominates current implant manufacturing. The bench data are internally consistent: Nb 40-41 wt% and Zr 7-7.5 wt% are homogeneously distributed, the β-phase microstructure is finer than MG4T's cold-worked α-phase, tensile strength is ~27% higher, fatigue life ~4-fold longer, and the elastic modulus is ~32% lower. The clinical arm then shows the material behaves like a contemporary implant — 100% success and survival, zero bleeding on probing, pocket depths ≤2.7 mm, no marginal bone loss over 1 mm — with a lower 12-month gingival index in TNZ as the only significant soft-tissue difference. The intellectually interesting result is the fractal-dimension analysis: the lower-modulus alloy produced a smaller rise in trabecular complexity, contradicting the orthopedic prediction that reduced stiffness shifts more load into bone and stimulates it. The authors read this as evidence that implant design, surface and interface mechanics dominate dental bone adaptation, and that the stress-shielding argument borrowed from long-bone orthopedics should not be applied to dental implants without clinical data.

## Key Contributions

- Bench demonstration, in one material, of the combination that matters for narrow or posterior implants under parafunction: higher tensile strength **and** longer fatigue life at a lower modulus.
- Compositional and microstructural characterization — homogeneous Nb/Zr, fine β-phase grains versus coarse cold-worked α-phase, no segregation or surface defects.
- First randomized 12-month clinical dataset for TNZ implants, with 100% success/survival and no implant-attributable adverse events.
- A direct in-vivo test of the stress-shielding hypothesis using fractal-dimension analysis, with a result that argues against its clinical relevance in the mouth.
- The ISQ trajectory finding: both materials gained stability between placement and second-stage surgery, TNZ significantly more (+4.43 vs 0.00).

## Methodology

**Design.** Parallel bench study + randomized, single-blind, controlled, two-centre clinical trial (non-inferiority framing; one-sided 97.5% lower confidence interval against a predefined margin).

**Bench.** n = 5 per material. SEM for microstructure, EDS for elemental composition, tensile testing (strength, elastic modulus, elongation), and cyclic fatigue to failure. No statistical comparison was performed (sample size).

**Clinical.** 80 participants requiring a single implant, randomized 1:1 to TNZ (WPM-TSIII SA) or MG4T. 77 analyzed per protocol (TNZ 38, MG4T 39); exclusions were one grafted site per group (protocol deviation) and one salivary gland tumour (test group withdrawal). Outcomes at 6 and 12 months post-loading: success/survival, probing depth, plaque index, gingival index, bleeding on probing; marginal bone level from periapical radiographs read by two blinded observers; fractal dimension of peri-implant trabecular bone at second-stage surgery, prosthesis delivery, 6 and 12 months; adverse events.

## Results

**Bench — tensile and fatigue (mean ± SD, n = 5)**

| Property | MG4T | TNZ | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tensile strength (MPa) | 900.0 ± 95.0 | 1,139.0 ± 102.9 | TNZ +27% |
| Elastic modulus (GPa) | 110.0 ± 11.2 | 75.0 ± 9.7 | TNZ −32% |
| Elongation (%) | 25.0 ± 2.0 | 10.0 ± 1.6 | MG4T more ductile |
| Fatigue life (cycles) | 254,587 ± 56,060 | 1,062,566 ± 110,015 | TNZ ~4.2× |

**Bench — surface.** EDS: Ti balance, Nb 40.12-41.11 wt%, Zr 7.10-7.49 wt%, homogeneous across the analysed region. SEM: MG4T coarse-grained cold-worked α-phase; TNZ finer, homogeneous β-phase; no defects or segregation in either.

**Clinical — 12-month outcomes**

| Outcome | TNZ (n=38) | MG4T (n=39) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Success / survival | 100% / 100% | 100% / 100% | non-inferior |
| MBL >1 mm | none | none | — |
| BOP positive | 0 (0%) | 0 (0%) | — |
| Pocket depth 6 mo (mm) | 2.39 ± 0.85 | 2.47 ± 0.75 | 0.67 |
| Pocket depth 12 mo (mm) | 2.62 ± 0.71 | 2.72 ± 0.86 | 0.58 |
| Plaque index 12 mo | 0.08 ± 0.27 | 0.21 ± 0.47 | 0.19 |
| Gingival index 12 mo | 0.00 ± 0.00 | 0.10 ± 0.31 | **0.045** |
| ISQ at placement | 71.39 ± 10.56 | 77.17 ± 6.59 | **0.012** |
| ISQ at 2nd stage | 75.83 ± 5.52 | 77.17 ± 6.89 | 0.35 |
| ISQ change | +4.43 ± 8.93 | 0.00 ± 7.53 | **0.021** |
| Max insertion torque (Ncm) | 41.32 ± 7.32 | 43.67 ± 6.50 | 0.17 |

**Baseline.** Age 51.2 vs 53.9 (p = 0.39); sex differed (47% vs 23% female, p = 0.026); arch distribution similar (mandible 71% vs 62%).

**Fractal dimension.** FD rose over the 12 months in both groups, consistent with time-dependent physiologic remodeling and no pathological adaptation. The increase around TNZ was smaller than around MG4T — the opposite of what a stress-shielding-driven adaptive response would predict. ICCs were acceptable at all time points except T0; Bland–Altman agreement was good.

**Adverse events.** None attributed to either implant system.

## Limitations

Two centres, modest cohort, 12 months only. Fractal dimension was computed on 2D periapical radiographs, which are prone to projection error and anatomical overlap; no CBCT-based volumetric assessment. Bench fatigue data come from 5 specimens per group with no statistical testing — explicitly a screening comparison, not a fatigue-limit determination. Baseline sex imbalance. The implants differ in design and surface as well as alloy, so the clinical comparison cannot isolate the material. One EDS titanium fraction (21.99%) is anomalous and unexplained. Non-inferiority conclusion at 12 months cannot speak to the long-term fatigue advantage that is the material's main selling point — the expensive failure mode (implant fracture) is rare and late.
