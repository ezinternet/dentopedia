## Three-line Summary

Split-mouth preliminary study, 60 implants (30 tapered / 30 parallel) in 17 patients, comparing primary implant stability via insertion torque (IT) and resonance frequency analysis (RFA, Osstell AND Penguin) between same-coronal-diameter conical and parallel implants.

Insertion torque was significantly higher with parallel implants (P<.05) despite similar bone density and alveolar ridge width (ARW) between groups; between-group ISQ differences were not significant (P>.05); in tapered implants IT was inversely correlated with ARW (P<.001); Osstell and Penguin ISQ readings differed significantly from each other (P<.001).

Final drill bit diameter differed by protocol (3.25 mm parallel vs 3.4 mm tapered), a likely confound of the IT comparison, and the two RFA devices are not interchangeable — clinicians should not assume tapered implants automatically deliver higher primary stability, nor treat Osstell and Penguin ISQ values as directly comparable; abstract-only source, full statistics not retrieved.

## Summary

This preliminary split-mouth study (17 patients, 60 implants — 30 tapered, 30 parallel) compared primary implant stability (PIS) between conical (tapered) and parallel implants of the **same coronal diameter (3.75 mm)**, using insertion torque (IT, rotational stability) and resonance frequency analysis (RFA, lateral stability) measured with **two different RFA systems (Osstell and Penguin)**. Bone density and alveolar ridge width (ARW) were estimated from CBCT with radiological-surgical templates and were similar between the two implant groups (P>.05), isolating the comparison to implant design and drilling protocol.

Contrary to the common assumption that tapered geometry produces higher primary stability through apical bone compression, IT was significantly **higher with the parallel implants** (P<.05) — likely explained by the smaller final drill bit used for parallel implants (3.25 mm vs 3.4 mm for tapered, per manufacturer recommendation) rather than by macrogeometry itself. Between-implant-type ISQ differences were not significant on either RFA device (P>.05). Within the tapered subgroup specifically, IT was inversely correlated with ARW (P<.001) — wider ridges produced lower insertion torque in tapered implants, a possible stability liability in thick, low-density bone. Critically, ISQ values from Osstell and Penguin differed significantly from each other (P<.001), meaning the two devices are not interchangeable for absolute-value comparison.

## Key Contributions

- Isolates implant macrogeometry effect on IT by matching coronal diameter (3.75 mm) across tapered and parallel groups in a split-mouth design
- First head-to-head **dual-RFA-device** (Osstell vs Penguin) measurement on the same implant cohort, quantifying inter-device disagreement (P<.001)
- Identifies a tapered-implant-specific vulnerability: IT falls as ARW increases (P<.001), flagging thick low-density ridges as a risk scenario for tapered designs
- Empirically demonstrates that final drill bit diameter (protocol-driven) can outweigh implant shape in determining IT — reinforcing that surgical technique, not macrogeometry alone, drives primary mechanical stability

## Methodology

- **Design**: Preliminary split-mouth study with randomized implant-type placement
- **n**: 17 patients, 60 implants (30 tapered, 30 parallel), same coronal diameter (3.75 mm)
- **Drilling protocol**: Final drill bit 3.25 mm (parallel) vs 3.4 mm (tapered), per manufacturer recommendation — NOT standardized between groups
- **Imaging**: CBCT with radiological-surgical templates → bone density + ARW estimation
- **Outcomes**: IT (implant motor), ISQ (Osstell RFA), ISQ (Penguin RFA), IT–ARW correlation

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Bone density, ARW (parallel vs tapered) | Similar, P>.05 |
| Insertion torque (parallel vs tapered) | Higher with parallel, P<.05 |
| ISQ (between implant types, either device) | Not significant, P>.05 |
| IT vs ARW, tapered implants only | Inverse correlation, P<.001 |
| Osstell vs Penguin ISQ (same implants) | Significantly different, P<.001 |
