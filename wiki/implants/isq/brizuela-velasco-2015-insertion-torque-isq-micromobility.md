## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (n=19 implants in fresh cow ribs) simultaneously measuring insertion torque (IT), Osstell ISQ, and directly observed implant micromotion under 100 N load via Questar microscope (2 µm resolution).

ISQ vs micromotion: inverse linear (perpendicular r=0.91, R²≈0.83; parallel r=0.86, R²=0.74; both p<0.0001); IT vs micromotion: inverse exponential (R²=0.78, p<0.0001) with a threshold at ~30–34 N·cm below which micromotion escalates steeply; IT vs ISQ: direct significant correlation (R²=0.73 perpendicular, 0.62 parallel).

ISQ is a stronger linear predictor of micromobility than IT alone; the ~34 N·cm IT threshold provides a biomechanical rationale for minimum torque guidelines in immediate-loading protocols.

## Summary

Brizuela-Velasco et al. (2015) conducted an in vitro study using 19 Klockner Essential Cone implants (10 mm × 4 mm) placed in fresh bovine rib bone (simulating Lekholm & Zarb type 1–2 density) to simultaneously quantify the relationships among insertion torque (IT), Osstell ISQ (resonance frequency analysis), and directly measured implant micromotion under a 100 N load applied at 6° to the occlusal plane. Micromotion was measured before and after loading with a Questar QM100 long-distance traveling microscope (2 µm resolution) and IMAGE-J image analysis. The study established an inverse linear relationship between ISQ and micromotion (perpendicular ISQ: r=0.91; parallel ISQ: r=0.86; both p<0.0001), and an inverse exponential relationship between IT and micromotion (R²=0.78, p<0.0001) with a clinically relevant threshold near 30–34 N·cm below which micromotion escalates sharply. A direct significant correlation between IT and ISQ was also confirmed (R²=0.73 perpendicular, 0.62 parallel). These findings suggest that ISQ and IT, while correlated, capture partially independent aspects of primary stability — ISQ predicts micromobility more linearly, while IT shows an exponential threshold effect.

## Key Contributions

- First simultaneous measurement of IT, ISQ, and directly observed micromotion in the same specimens — eliminates indirect inference across studies
- Identifies ~30–34 N·cm as an exponential inflection point: below this, micromotion escalates steeply; above this, gains diminish (~1 µm per N·cm increment)
- Demonstrates ISQ is a stronger linear predictor of functional micromobility than IT alone, supporting ISQ primacy in loading-decision protocols
- Confirms direct IT-ISQ correlation, contributing to the evidence later pooled in systematic reviews (Tisci 2026: r=0.44, p<0.001, 48 studies)
- Validates the 50–150 µm micromobility tolerance window referenced in the osseointegration literature

## Methodology

- **Design**: In vitro controlled bench study
- **Bone model**: Fresh bovine rib (12 fragments, periosteum removed, 50% ethanol/saline storage 5 days), Lekholm & Zarb type 1–2 equivalent
- **Implants**: Klockner Essential Cone, 10 mm × 4 mm, internal connection, 1.5 mm polished neck; 2 per rib, 20 mm apart; n=24 placed → 5 excluded (IT outside 10–90 N·cm meter range) → n=19 final
- **IT**: BTG90CN analog dynamometer (Tohnichi), N·cm
- **ISQ**: Osstell ISQ + SmartPeg (4–6 N·cm), parallel and perpendicular to bone long axis
- **Loading**: MTS BIONIX 358 servohydraulic machine, 100 N at 6°, 10 N/s rate
- **Micromotion**: Questar QM100 microscope (2 µm resolution), IMAGE-J v1.47, ≥5 points per specimen, 2D displacement at 0 and 100 N
- **Statistics**: Pearson correlation coefficient, p<0.05 threshold

## Results

| Correlation | r or R² | Shape | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perpendicular ISQ vs micromotion | r=0.91, R²≈0.83 | Inverse linear | <0.0001 |
| Parallel ISQ vs micromotion | r=0.86, R²=0.74 | Inverse linear | <0.0001 |
| IT vs micromotion | R²=0.78 | Inverse exponential | <0.0001 |
| IT vs perpendicular ISQ | R²=0.73 | Direct linear | <0.0001 |
| IT vs parallel ISQ | R²=0.62 | Direct linear | <0.0001 |

| Variable | Mean (SD) | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Peak IT (N·cm) | 32.7 (20.9) | 10–73 |
| Perpendicular ISQ | 68.2 (6.9) | 56–79 |
| Parallel ISQ | 72.0 (6.5) | 59–83 |
| Micromotion (µm) | 68.2 (44.3) | 14.6–164 |

Key threshold: IT < 30 N·cm → micromotion escalates steeply; IT > 34 N·cm → each +1 N·cm ≈ −1 µm micromotion (diminishing returns near-plateau).
