## Three-line Summary

RCT of 116 single implants placed in healed sites, comparing high-IT (삽입 토크, IT ≥50 Ncm) vs regular-IT (<50 Ncm) groups using implants with the same macro-geometry but different cutting-groove designs, followed for 3 years.

High-IT implants showed significantly greater marginal bone loss and facial soft-tissue recession at 3 years, with the harm most pronounced in mandibular sites; cumulative success rate was 91.3% (high IT) vs 98.2% (regular IT).

Buccal bone thickness ≥1 mm attenuated mucosal recession even in the high-IT group, suggesting cortical bone quality and thickness modify the clinical risk of excessive insertion torque.

## Summary

Marconcini et al. (2018) randomized patients requiring single implants in healed bone to receive one of two implant designs: a 90° self-tapping groove (tended higher IT) or the Blossom™ groove (tended lower IT), grouping outcomes post-allocation into high-IT (≥50 Ncm) and regular-IT (<50 Ncm). At 3-year follow-up, high-IT implants exhibited significantly greater marginal bone loss and facial soft-tissue level (FSTL) recession. The mandible was the most affected site, consistent with the hypothesis that high IT in dense cortical bone triggers osseocompression-induced ischemia, necrosis, and subsequent resorption. Sites with buccal bone thickness ≥1 mm were partially protected from mucosal recession. Overall survival was high (96.5%), but cumulative success rates diverged: 91.3% (high IT) vs 98.2% (regular IT). These findings challenge the clinical assumption that higher torque uniformly improves outcomes and are frequently cited as the primary clinical evidence against routine high-IT placement.

## Key Contributions

- First RCT with ≥3-year follow-up directly demonstrating that IT ≥50 Ncm causes **significantly greater MBL and mucosal recession** in healed-site single implants
- Identifies the **mandible** as the location where high-IT damage is most clinically significant — dense cortical bone amplifies osseocompression risk
- Establishes **BBT ≥1 mm as a protective modifier**: thick buccal plate attenuates recession even with high IT
- Provides biological mechanistic support: high IT → bone compression exceeding yield strength → cortical ischemia → necrosis → resorption cascade
- Paradoxically, overall survival (96.5%) remains high despite MBL/recession differences — raises the question of whether MBL at 3 years is clinically significant enough to affect long-term survival
- Frequently used to justify the guideline: "avoid IT >50 Ncm in dense mandibular bone"

## Methodology

**Design:** Randomized clinical study (RCT); 3-year longitudinal  
**n:** 116 implants, healed sites; allocation by implant type (cutting groove design), not by IT target  
**Groups (post-hoc IT classification):**
- High-IT: ≥50 Ncm
- Regular-IT: <50 Ncm

**Implants:** Two types — same macro-geometry:
1. 90° cutting groove (self-tapping)
2. Blossom™ cutting groove (Intra-Lock, Boca Raton, FL)

**Loading:** Delayed, 3 months post-placement  
**Outcomes:** MBL (radiographic), FSTL (facial soft tissue level = recession), survival, CSR  
**Baseline covariate:** BBT (buccal bone thickness) recorded at surgery  
**Setting:** University of Pisa, Italy; single center

## Results

**Survival:** 96.5% overall at 3 years

**Cumulative Success Rate:**
| Group | CSR (3 years) |
|---|---|
| High-IT (≥50 Ncm) | 91.3% |
| Regular-IT (<50 Ncm) | 98.2% |

**MBL:** Significantly greater in High-IT group at 3 years; effect size not reported in abstract

**FSTL (recession):**
- High-IT > Regular-IT, significantly (p not specified in abstract)
- Mandible > maxilla (cortical bone density drives the effect)
- BBT ≥1 mm: protective — smaller recession at 3 years compared to BBT <1 mm sites
