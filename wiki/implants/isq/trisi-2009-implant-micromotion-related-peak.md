## Three-line Summary
In vitro biomechanical study: 120 Ti-Bone implants (4 × 13 mm) placed in fresh bovine bone blocks of three densities (hard, normal, soft), tested at five peak insertion torque levels (20, 35, 45, 70, 100 N/cm) with lateral loads of 20, 25, and 30 N (1800 total measurements).
Spearman's rank correlation showed strong inverse dependence between peak insertion torque and micromotion (ρ = −0.54, P < 0.001); micromotion decreased significantly up to 45 N/cm in hard/normal bone, but in soft bone maximum achievable torque was only 35 N/cm before implant spinning occurred.
Increasing insertion torque reduces micromotion, but soft bone consistently exhibits high micromotion (>90 μm even at 35 N/cm), suggesting immediate functional loading in soft bone carries high failure risk.

## Summary
This controlled laboratory study directly measured implant micromotion — the definitive biomechanical determinant of primary stability — rather than relying on surrogate markers like ISQ (RFA) or Periotest (PTV). Using fresh bovine bone standardized into three density grades (hard/normal/soft) and a custom digital torque wrench sampling at 0.5 ms intervals, the authors quantified how peak insertion torque translates into actual interfacial micromovement under physiologically relevant lateral loads (20–30 N). The key finding is a strong inverse correlation (ρ = −0.54) between insertion torque and micromotion, but with critical nuances: (1) the benefit plateaus at ~45 N/cm in hard and normal bone, (2) soft bone cannot accept >35 N/cm without interfacial damage and implant spinning, and (3) even at the maximum achievable torque in soft bone, micromotion remains well above the 50–100 μm threshold associated with fibrous encapsulation. These data provide a biomechanical basis for cautious immediate loading protocols in low-density bone.

## Key Contributions
- First direct experimental correlation of peak insertion torque with measured implant micromotion (not surrogate ISQ/RFA) across controlled bone densities.
- Defined the practical torque ceiling in soft bone (35 N/cm) beyond which interfacial bone damage causes implant spinning.
- Demonstrated diminishing returns: micromotion reduction plateaus above 45 N/cm in hard/normal bone.
- Provided numeric micromotion thresholds (mean values at each torque/density/load combination) for benchmarking primary stability.

## Methodology
- **Design**: In vitro biomechanical experiment on fresh bovine bone blocks (2 × 2 cm).
- **Specimens**: 120 Ti-Bone implants (4 mm diameter × 13 mm length, one-piece 11 mm abutment).
- **Bone density groups**: Hard (dense compact), Normal (2–3 mm cortex + normal cancellous), Soft (1 mm cortex + low-density cancellous) — classified by drilling resistance and histology.
- **Torque groups**: 20, 35, 45, 70, 100 N/cm peak insertion torque (10 implants per torque × density cell).
- **Loading**: Custom device with digital force gauge (0–90 N, ±0.5%) and Mitutoyo digital micrometer; lateral forces 20, 25, 30 N applied at 10 mm above crest, 5 repetitions per implant per load.
- **Analysis**: Kruskal-Wallis across torque levels, Mann-Whitney (20 vs 35 N/cm), linear regression, Spearman's rank correlation (SPSS 11.0).

- **Spearman correlation**: ρ = −0.54, P < 0.001 (micromotion vs. insertion torque, all densities pooled).
- **Kruskal-Wallis**: Significant difference across torque levels (P < 0.05).
- **Mann-Whitney (20 vs 35 N/cm)**: Significant micromotion reduction at each lateral force (P < 0.05).
- **Soft bone ceiling**: Torque >35 N/cm not achievable — implants spun due to interfacial bone damage.
- **Clinical threshold context**: In hard/normal bone, micromotion at ≥45 N/cm falls near or below 50 μm (the lower bound of the fibrous-encapsulation threshold); in soft bone, all values exceed 70 μm even at maximum torque.
