## Three-line Summary
Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR; PubMed + Scopus, 2011–2024; 11 human clinical studies) on whether high insertion torque (IT >50 Ncm) harms marginal bone, facial soft tissue, and implant survival.
Narrative conclusion: high IT secures primary stability but is associated with more MBL (mandible 1.53 vs 1.03 mm; maxilla 1.16 vs 0.96 mm, 3 years) and more facial recession (mandible −1.40 vs −0.26 mm; maxilla −0.54 vs 0.03 mm, 36 months), modified by bone density and buccal bone thickness; failures cluster in D1 and D4 bone.
No meta-analysis, headline numbers trace to a single 3-year trial, and the post-hoc torque-classification confound is not addressed — it records a harm-leaning 2025 view but does not overturn the Lemos 2020 SR+MA null.

## Summary
Manfredini et al. (2025) ran a PRISMA-ScR scoping review (JBI three-stage search of PubMed and Scopus, human interventional and observational studies 2011–2024) and included 11 studies comparing implants placed with high IT (>50 Ncm) versus regular IT (<50 Ncm). Without pooling, they conclude that high IT is associated with greater marginal bone loss, greater facial soft-tissue recession, more peri-implantitis and failure, and "diminished osseointegration", attributing this to strain beyond bone's elastic limit causing microcracks, impaired microcirculation, ischemia, and necrosis. The quantitative claims — mandibular MBL 1.53 vs 1.03 mm and mandibular facial recession −1.40 vs −0.26 mm at 3 years, with smaller maxillary differences and buccal bone thickness <1 mm as a modifier — are each cited to a single study whose design matches Marconcini 2018, so they are one trial's findings restated rather than a convergent pattern. The review adds a useful density framing (failures concentrated in D1 and D4 bone, D2–D3 a "safer zone"), acknowledges dissenting no-harm studies, and has several reporting inconsistencies. Clinically it supports caution with very high torque in dense cortical crestal bone, but at lower evidence weight than the Lemos 2020 SR+MA it disagrees with.

**Supersession judgment (2026-09-28): none.** A narrative scoping review of 11 studies, whose key numbers come from one RCT already in the wiki and which excluded prior SRs from its evidence base, does not outweigh a pooled SR+MA (Lemos 2020) or the cross-paper reading in [[overviews/high-insertion-torque-primary-stability-crestal-bone-overview]]. That overview already explains why harm-leaning and null syntheses coexist (site-specific effect, post-hoc torque classification, pooling of sign-flipping populations); this review falls on the harm side of that explanation rather than resolving it. No `superseded_by` set anywhere.

## Key Contributions
- Most recent (2025) synthesis on IT >50 Ncm; harm-leaning bottom line in direct conflict with the Lemos 2020 null
- Carries the jaw-specific MBL and FSTL effect sizes (see Results) that our abstract-only Marconcini 2018 page lacks — secondhand, and attribution to Marconcini is inferred because PMC returned no reference list
- Bimodal failure-by-density framing: D1 and D4 bone account for most failures, D2–D3 safest — aligns with "IT reads the bone bed" rather than "IT is a dose"
- Names buccal bone thickness (<1 mm) and thin gingival biotype as modifiers of recession and MBL
- Restates the strain → ischemia → necrosis → MBL pathway (narrative, citing Campos et al. histology) — no new mechanistic data
- **Not new**: the soft-tissue recession finding is Marconcini's, already in the wiki

## Methodology
- **Design**: scoping review, PRISMA-ScR, JBI three-stage search, PCC framework; OSF-registered
- **Databases**: PubMed (MEDLINE), Scopus; English only; 2011–2024 (final search stated 16 Sept 2023)
- **Exclusions**: animal/ex vivo, no ethics approval, case reports/series, all narrative/systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- **Flow**: 823 records → 20 full texts → **11 included** (4 controlled intervention, 7 observational cohort)
- **Appraisal**: NHLBI tools (Methods) / Cochrane RoB + ROBINS-I (Results); overall "low risk of bias"
- **Synthesis**: narrative and tabular, no meta-analysis, no GRADE
- **Artifact**: PMC full text retrieved, but tables, figures and references were not returned — included studies cannot be listed from our copy

## Results
| Outcome | Regular IT (<50 Ncm) | High IT (>50 Ncm) | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| MBL, mandible | 1.03 ± 0.12 mm | 1.53 ± 0.29 mm (p<0.001) | 3 years |
| MBL, maxilla | 0.96 ± 0.46 mm | 1.16 ± 0.61 mm (p<0.0001, as reported) | 3 years |
| FSTL change, maxilla | +0.03 mm | −0.54 mm (p<0.001) | 36 months |
| FSTL change, mandible | −0.26 mm | −1.40 mm (p<0.001) | 36 months |
| Thin vs thick biotype (n=37) | MBL 0.9 → 1.21 mm (crown → 1 year); thin biotype significantly worse (p<0.05) | — | 1 year |
| BBT modifier | BBT <1 mm → more recession; >1 mm → significantly less | — | — |
| Failure by bone type | Mostly D1 and D4; D2–D3 "safer zone" | — | — |

- The mandibular penalty is ~2.5× the maxillary one for MBL (Δ 0.50 vs 0.20 mm) and ~2× for recession (Δ 1.14 vs 0.57 mm), which fits the overview's dense-crestal-cortex mechanism; the review's claim that harm holds "regardless of the jaw region" is technically true but hides that gradient
- Key limitations: single-trial provenance of the headline numbers; no pooled estimate; post-hoc torque classification not discussed; reporting inconsistencies (RoB tools, search dates vs window, "prospective" registration cited with a 2025 access date, template carry-over text); abstract says "better osseointegration" while conclusions say "diminished osseointegration"
