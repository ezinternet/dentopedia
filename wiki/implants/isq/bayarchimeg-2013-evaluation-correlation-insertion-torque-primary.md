## Three-line Summary
In-vitro study using polyurethane foam bone blocks (#10–#50 densities) with 5 experimental groups (n=20/group) testing IT and ISQ correlations across bone density, drill diameter, cortical thickness, and cancellous density variations.
Insertion torque and ISQ correlated strongly only in homogeneous bone (r=0.82, P<0.001); correlations weakened or diverged when cortical thickness, cancellous density, or drill diameter varied independently.
Clinical implication: primary stability is multifactorial — insertion torque alone cannot predict ISQ; bone quality (cortical thickness, cancellous density) and surgical protocol (drill size) independently modulate the IT–ISQ relationship.

## Summary
This in-vitro study systematically isolated the effects of bone quality parameters and surgical drilling protocols on the correlation between insertion torque (IT) and implant stability quotient (ISQ). Using Sawbones polyurethane foam blocks to simulate homogeneous bone densities (#10–#40), cortical bone (#50), and cancellous bone (#10–#30) with varying cortical thickness (0–2.0 mm), the authors tested five experimental groups with 20 repetitions each. In homogeneous bone (Group 1), IT and ISQ both increased with bone density and showed a strong positive correlation (r=0.82, P<0.001). However, when final drill diameter was varied in homogeneous bone (Group 2), IT decreased with larger drills while ISQ remained unchanged, breaking the correlation. Increasing cortical thickness (Group 3) raised both IT and ISQ. Increasing cancellous density alone with fixed cortical thickness (Group 4) increased IT but not ISQ. In combined cortical-cancellous blocks with varying drill diameters (Group 5), IT decreased with larger drills while ISQ showed weak correlation. The study concludes that primary stability cannot be inferred from insertion torque alone; bone quality compartments and surgical technique independently determine the IT–ISQ relationship.

## Key Contributions
- First in-vitro study to independently manipulate cortical thickness, cancellous density, and drill diameter while measuring IT and ISQ
- Quantified the boundary conditions where IT–ISQ correlation holds (homogeneous bone) vs. fails (heterogeneous bone compartments)
- Provided numerical benchmarks for IT and ISQ across clinically relevant bone quality scenarios
- Demonstrated that larger drill diameters reduce IT without necessarily reducing ISQ — challenging the assumption that lower IT always means lower primary stability

## Methodology
- **Design**: In-vitro experimental study (bench/laboratory)
- **Specimens**: Sawbones rigid polyurethane foam blocks (homogeneous #10, #20, #30, #40; cortical #50); short fiber-filled epoxy sheets for cortical layers (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 mm)
- **Implants**: Osstem SS II fixtures, 11.5 mm length × 4.1 mm diameter
- **Drilling**: 800 rpm, copious external irrigation, final drill diameters Ø2.7–Ø3.8 mm per protocol
- **Measurements**: Peak IT during placement (Ncm); ISQ via Osstell Mentor (magnetic peg, 1–100 scale, averaged per implant)
- **Groups**: 
  - Group 1: Homogeneous bone density variation (#10→#40), final drill Ø3.6 mm
  - Group 2: Final drill diameter variation (Ø2.7→Ø3.6 mm), homogeneous #20 bone
  - Group 3: Cortical thickness variation (0→2.0 mm), cortical #50, cancellous #20, drill Ø3.6 mm
  - Group 4: Cancellous density variation (#10→#30), cortical 1.5 mm #50 fixed, drill Ø3.6 mm
  - Group 5: Final drill diameter variation (Ø3.0→Ø3.8 mm), cortical 1.5 mm #50, cancellous #20
- **Statistics**: One-way ANOVA for group comparisons; Pearson correlation for IT–ISQ; P<0.05 significance
- **Sample size**: n=20 per subgroup

## Results

| Group | Variable | IT Range (Ncm) | ISQ Range | IT–ISQ Correlation |
|-------|----------|----------------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Bone density #10→#40 | ~15→50 | ~45→80 | r=0.82, P<0.001 |
| 2 | Drill Ø2.7→Ø3.6 mm (#20 bone) | ~50→20 | ~65–70 (flat) | Weak / NS |
| 3 | Cortical thickness 0→2.0 mm | ~15→50 | ~45→80 | Positive, significant |
| 4 | Cancellous #10→#30 (cortical 1.5 mm fixed) | ~25→45 | ~60–70 (flat) | Weak |
| 5 | Drill Ø3.0→Ø3.8 mm (cortical 1.5 mm, cancellous #20) | ~40→20 | ~60–68 (flat) | Weak |

Key findings:
- **Group 1**: Both IT and ISQ increased linearly with homogeneous bone density; strong correlation confirms the expected relationship in uniform bone.
- **Group 2**: Larger final drills reduced IT (less bone-implant contact) but ISQ did not decrease proportionally — IT underestimates stability when osteotomy is enlarged.
- **Group 3**: Cortical thickness is a dominant driver of both IT and ISQ; even thin cortical layers (0.5 mm) substantially increased both metrics.
- **Group 4**: Cancellous density primarily affects IT (thread engagement in trabeculae) with minimal ISQ impact when cortical thickness is controlled.
- **Group 5**: In realistic two-compartment bone, drill diameter modulates IT but ISQ remains relatively stable — confirming IT is not a reliable surrogate for ISQ in heterogeneous bone.
