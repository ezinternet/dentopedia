## Three-line Summary
- In vitro study (n=140 implants in 10 bovine ribs, bone quality type II) comparing ISQ (Osstell) and insertion torque with vs without thread initiator.
- Thread initiator reduced mean insertion torque (24.36±14.88 N vs 35.30±10.51 N) but did not significantly affect ISQ (69.14±8.39 vs 72.17±5.76); correlation IT-ISQ was moderate with thread initiator (r=0.587, p<0.05) and weak/non-significant without (r=0.193, p=0.11).
- Using a thread initiator lowers insertion torque without compromising primary stability (ISQ), suggesting IT and ISQ reflect different aspects of primary stability.

## Summary
This in vitro study evaluated the correlation between insertion torque (IT) and implant stability quotient (ISQ) measured by resonance frequency analysis (RFA) using the Osstell ISQ device, and assessed the influence of a thread initiator on both parameters. 140 rough-surfaced, screw-shaped implants (Klockner Essential Cone, 4mm×10mm) were placed in 10 bovine ribs (bone quality type II per Lekholm & Zarb) by an experienced clinician. Implants were randomly assigned to Group A (thread initiator used before insertion, n=70) or Group B (no thread initiator, n=70). Insertion torque was recorded using a manual torque wrench, and ISQ was measured perpendicular to the SmartPeg in two positions (buccal and lingual) after placement. The study found that using a thread initiator significantly reduced insertion torque (24.36±14.88 N vs 35.30±10.51 N) without significantly affecting ISQ values (69.14±8.39 vs 72.17±5.76). The Pearson correlation between IT and ISQ was moderate and significant in Group A (r=0.587, p<0.05) but weak and non-significant in Group B (r=0.193, p=0.11). Subgroup analyses showed that in Group A, implants with ISQ < 60 had significantly lower IT than those with ISQ ≥ 60, and implants with IT < 30 N had significantly lower ISQ than those with IT ≥ 30 N. The authors conclude that thread initiator use decreases insertion torque without influencing ISQ, and that IT and ISQ represent different biomechanical aspects of primary stability (shear vs bending resistance).

## Key Contributions
- Demonstrated that thread initiator significantly reduces insertion torque without compromising ISQ-measured primary stability
- Showed that IT-ISQ correlation is meaningful only when thread initiator is used (r=0.587 vs r=0.193)
- Provided threshold-based subgroup analysis (ISQ < 60 vs ≥ 60; IT < 30 N vs ≥ 30 N) revealing differential relationships
- Supports the concept that IT and ISQ measure distinct biomechanical properties of the implant-bone interface

## Methodology
- **Design**: In vitro experimental study
- **Sample**: 140 implants (Essential® Cone, Klockner Implant System, 4mm diameter × 10mm length, 1.5mm machined collar) in 10 bovine ribs
- **Bone quality**: Type II (dense cortical with coarse trabecular core) per Lekholm & Zarb
- **Groups**: A (thread initiator, n=70) vs B (no thread initiator, n=70)
- **Surgical protocol**: Full drilling sequence per manufacturer at 800 rpm with saline irrigation; implants placed with manual torque wrench
- **Measurements**: IT recorded at placement; ISQ measured via Osstell ISQ (SmartPeg, two perpendicular readings per implant)
- **Statistics**: SPSS 19.0; Kolmogorov-Smirnov for normality, Mann-Whitney for group comparisons, Kruskal-Wallis for multi-group, Pearson correlation

**Subgroup distributions:**
- Group A: 44% low ISQ (0–33), 26% medium (33–67), 30% high (67–100)
- Group B: 23% low, 49% medium, 29% high
- Group A: 51% low IT (<30 N), 31% medium, 17% high
- Group B: 21% low IT, 34% medium, 44% high
