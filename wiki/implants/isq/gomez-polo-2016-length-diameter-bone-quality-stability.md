## Three-line Summary

Prospective longitudinal clinical study of 88 self-tapping InHex implants in 63 partially edentulous patients, testing the effect of implant diameter (3.75 vs 4.25 mm), length (10 vs 11.5 mm), and Lekholm-Zarb bone quality on insertion torque (IT) and primary/secondary implant stability quotient (ISQ).

IT and primary ISQ were significantly affected by diameter (26.5 vs 33.8 N/cm; 75.0 vs 77.0 ISQ) and bone type (34.9 N/cm/77.4 ISQ in type 1+2 vs 20.6 N/cm/70.5 ISQ in type 4), not by length; IT correlated moderately with primary ISQ (Pearson r=0.56, P<.01) but not with secondary ISQ (r=0.14, P=.097).

Diameter and bone density are the dominant drivers of primary mechanical stability while length adds little in this 10–11.5 mm range; IT should not be used as a proxy for post-osseointegration (secondary) stability, and very high primary ISQ values tend to fall while low ones rise toward the mean by 8 weeks.

## 세줄요약

전향적 종적 임상연구(부분무치악 환자 63명, InHex self-tapping 임플란트 88본)로 임플란트 직경(3.75/4.25mm)·길이(10/11.5mm)·Lekholm-Zarb 골질이 삽입토크 (Insertion Torque, IT) 및 1차·2차 임플란트 안정성 지수 (Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ)에 미치는 영향을 분석.

직경과 골타입이 IT·1차 ISQ에 유의한 영향을 미쳤으나(직경별 IT 26.5 vs 33.8 N/cm) 길이는 유의한 영향 없음; IT는 1차 ISQ와 중등도 상관(Pearson r=0.56, P<.01)이나 2차 ISQ와는 유의한 상관 없음(r=0.14, P=.097).

직경·골밀도가 1차(기계적) 안정성의 주요 결정인자이며 이 범위에서 길이 영향은 미미; IT를 2차(생물학적) 안정성의 대리지표로 쓰면 안 되고, 초기 높은 1차 ISQ는 8주 후 감소, 낮은 값은 증가하는 평균회귀 경향이 관찰됨.

## Summary

This prospective longitudinal study from Complutense University of Madrid enrolled 63 partially edentulous patients receiving 88 self-tapping InHex implants (2 diameters, 2 lengths) to isolate the independent effects of implant diameter, length, and Lekholm-Zarb bone quality on insertion torque (IT) and implant stability quotient (ISQ) measured at placement (primary) and 8 weeks later (secondary). Diameter and bone quality significantly affected both IT and primary ISQ, while implant length in the narrow 10–11.5 mm range tested had no meaningful effect on either. IT correlated moderately with primary ISQ (r=0.56) but not with secondary ISQ (r=0.14, NS), confirming that IT and post-osseointegration RFA measure different biomechanical constructs. Notably, primary and secondary ISQ showed a regression-to-the-mean pattern: implants starting with very high primary ISQ tended to decrease by 8 weeks, while those starting low tended to increase — a clinically relevant nuance for interpreting single-timepoint ISQ readings.

## Key Contributions
- Directly compares IT, primary ISQ, and secondary ISQ across the same 3 predictors (diameter, length, bone type) in one cohort using multivariate linear regression (model R² 0.097–0.252), rather than univariate analysis alone.
- Confirms a moderate, statistically significant IT–primary ISQ correlation (r=0.56, P<.01) but explicitly shows this breaks down for secondary ISQ (r=0.14, P=.097), reinforcing that IT should not substitute for post-healing RFA monitoring.
- Documents a regression-to-the-mean dynamic in the primary→secondary ISQ transition (highest primary ISQ implants mean 78.6 tended to fall; lowest mean 73.8 tended to rise, P<.01) — a pattern relevant to interpreting "stability dip" phenomena.

## Methodology
Prospective longitudinal clinical study; single surgeon, single implant system (Mozo-Grau InHex, grade IV titanium, self-tapping); n=88 implants / 63 patients treated Jan–Dec 2014. Independent variables: diameter (3.75 vs 4.25 mm), length (10 vs 11.5 mm), Lekholm-Zarb bone type (1+2 merged, 3, 4). Outcomes: IT at seating; ISQ (Osstell RFA, mean of 2 axes) at placement and 8 weeks post-placement. Statistics: paired t test, 1-way ANOVA/Tukey, Pearson/Spearman correlation, multivariate linear regression; SPSS 22.0, α=.05.
