## Three-line Summary

In vitro polyurethane bone block study (56 implants, 7 types, D1–D4 bone) comparing four SmartPeg tightening methods: hand, plastic mount, SafeMount, and calibrated 6 Ncm torque wrench.

Hand tightening produced statistically significantly lower ISQ than the calibrated torque control (coef −2.05, p<.001); SafeMount and standard plastic mount showed no significant difference from the control.

Bone density accounted for 36% of ISQ variance — the dominant factor — while operator contributed only 6%; ISQ reliability was very poor in D4 bone regardless of tightening method.

## Summary

This 2023 in vitro study from Dublin Dental University Hospital is the first to evaluate Osstell's novel SafeMount torque-limiting device alongside the standard SmartPeg mount and a calibrated torque wrench across seven implant systems and four bone densities. The key finding that hand tightening yields significantly lower ISQ values (coef −2.05, p<.001) than calibrated 6 Ncm torque directly challenges Kästel et al. (2019), who found no effect across 2–11 Ncm. The SafeMount (click at 4–4.5 Ncm) and standard plastic mount performed equivalently to the calibrated control, suggesting that any guided method outperforms unguided hand tightening. Critically, bone density dominated ISQ variance (36%) over implant design (11%) and operator (6%), and D4 bone showed very poor inter-operator agreement (ICC <0.24) regardless of method — a clinical warning for low-density sites.

## Key Contributions

- First head-to-head comparison of SafeMount vs standard mount vs calibrated torque wrench across multiple implant systems
- Demonstrated hand tightening yields significantly lower ISQ than 6 Ncm calibrated torque (p<.001), contradicting Kästel 2019
- Quantified variance components: bone density 36%, implant 11%, operator 6% — establishing bone quality as the dominant ISQ determinant
- Revealed very poor inter-operator agreement in D4 bone (ICC <0.24) regardless of tightening method
- SafeMount offered marginal ICC improvement over standard mount but not statistically significant

## Methodology

- **Design**: In vitro, 8 polyurethane bone blocks (BoneModels™), D1–D4 densities (2 blocks each)
- **Implants**: 7 types — Straumann Standard/Standard Plus/Tapered Effect/Wide (SLA), Zimmer Biomet BNST (internal hex) and BOET (external hex), Dentsply Ankylos C/X
- **Total**: 56 implants, 1792 ISQ measurements
- **Tightening groups**: (1) hand with gauze, (2) standard plastic SmartPeg mount, (3) SafeMount (click at 4–4.5 Ncm), (4) calibrated Tohnichi torque wrench at 6 Ncm (NML-calibrated)
- **RFA**: Osstell IDx + Osstell ISQ, buccal + mesial, probe 2 mm / 90°
- **Operators**: 2 male operators, each performed all measurements independently
- **Statistics**: ICC (average measures, Koo & Li), linear mixed effects regression (R/lme4/lmerTest), Kruskal–Wallis + Dunn's post hoc

## Results

**Mean ISQ by tightening method:**

| Method | Mean ISQ ± SD |
|---|---|
| Hand tightening (gauze) | 64.17 ± 12.99 |
| Plastic SmartPeg mount | 65.89 ± 13.57 |
| SafeMount | 66.04 ± 14.33 |
| Calibrated torque (6 Ncm) | 66.22 ± 14.12 |

**Mixed model — tightening method vs control (6 Ncm):**

| Method | Coef | 95% CI | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hand tightening | −2.05 | (−2.89, −1.21) | <.001 |
| Plastic mount | −0.33 | (−1.17, 0.51) | .444 |
| SafeMount | −0.19 | (−1.03, 0.66) | .668 |

**ICC by bone density (operator agreement):**

| Bone | Hand | Plastic | SafeMount | 6 Ncm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | 0.830 | 0.811 | 0.977 | 0.944 |
| D2 | 0.934 | 0.972 | 0.972 | 0.983 |
| D3 | 0.791 | 0.767 | 0.762 | 0.803 |
| D4 | 0.112 | 0.192 | 0.238 | 0.039 |

**Variance decomposition:** Bone density 36.3%, implant 11.2%, operator 6.1%, residual 36.0%.

## Limitations

- Only 2 operators (both male) — broader operator pool including female operators would improve generalizability
- Statistical power insufficient for meaningful comparison between individual implant designs
- In vitro polyurethane model — may not fully replicate in vivo soft tissue conditions
- 6 Ncm control chosen to bridge Kästel (2–11 Ncm) and Geckili (5–8 Ncm) ranges

## Clinical Takeaway

Hand-tightening SmartPegs with gauze produces systematically lower ISQ values than calibrated torque. For research and long-term ISQ monitoring, use a guided method (SafeMount or plastic mount at minimum). In D4 bone, ISQ readings are inherently unreliable regardless of tightening method — interpret with caution.
