## Three-line Summary

Retrospective chart analysis at Ilsan Hospital (Korea) of 37 patients and 58 implants (secondary-stability check before occlusal loading, on average 91 days after placement) comparing two new damping capacity analysis (DCA) devices, AnyCheck (DCA-A) and The Trust (DCA-T), against Periotest (DCA-P) as the reference, with 3 readings per implant per device.

Accuracy versus Periotest: Spearman r = -0.870 for AnyCheck (R2 0.828) versus r = -0.473 for The Trust (R2 0.379; Z = 4.28, p < 0.05); The Trust improved to r = -0.70 (R2 0.6067) after excluding values above 90. Reliability was excellent for all three devices (ICC 0.98 Periotest, 0.96 AnyCheck, 0.94 The Trust) with no significant between-device difference.

AnyCheck accuracy was higher in the maxilla than the mandible (p = 0.0037) and lower at site #30 than #20 (p = 0.0010); The Trust showed no significant subgroup differences. The sample is small, Periotest is itself the reference rather than a histologic or outcome standard, and the paper contains several internal numerical inconsistencies, so the findings show agreement between devices, not diagnostic validity for predicting implant failure.

## Summary

A retrospective chart analysis from Ilsan Hospital (Korea) evaluated two newly developed damping capacity analysis (DCA) devices in the mouth, AnyCheck (Neobiotech; 6 strikes over about 2-3 s, 0.9 N, scale 0-99) and The Trust (Dentium; 7 strikes, 0.6-0.8 N per Table 5, scale 0-90), against Periotest (Medizintechnik Gulden; 16 strikes over about 4 s, 1.2 N per Table 5, PTV -8 to +50) as the reference. Data came from 37 patients with 58 implants whose secondary stability was checked in November 2023 before occlusal loading, on average 91 days (SD 41.91) after placement, with the healing abutment attached. Each device took 3 readings per implant, 10 seconds apart, striking from the buccal side with the device held parallel to the ground; manufacturer correction values for abutment height were applied.

Accuracy was defined as the Spearman correlation of each new device with the Periotest value (DCAV-P). AnyCheck correlated very strongly (r = -0.870; R2 0.828), The Trust only moderately (r = -0.473; R2 0.379), and the difference was significant (Z = 4.28 for r, Z = 4.24 for R2; p < 0.05). The Trust frequently hit its ceiling of 90, so values above 90 were excluded in a sensitivity analysis, which raised its correlation to r = -0.70 (R2 0.6067) but still left it below AnyCheck. Reliability by intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of the 3 readings was excellent for all devices: Periotest 0.98 (95% CI 0.97-0.99 in text), AnyCheck 0.96 (0.95-0.98), The Trust 0.94 (0.90-0.96), with no significant between-device difference.

Subgroup analyses showed AnyCheck accuracy differed by position: higher in the maxilla (r = -0.93) than the mandible (-0.69; p = 0.0037), lower at site #30 (left mandible; -0.54) than #20 (left maxilla; -0.96; p = 0.0010), and higher when DCAV-P was above -5.5 (-0.88) than below (-0.59; p = 0.0124). The Trust showed no significant accuracy differences by any variable. Periotest reliability was lower in the mandible than maxilla and at #40 than #10, whereas the two new devices showed no significant reliability differences by variable (judged by non-overlapping 95% CIs). The authors conclude that AnyCheck was more accurate than The Trust against Periotest in the intraoral setting and that all three were reliable.

## Key Contributions

- First clinical (intraoral, chairside) comparison reported here of The Trust, a new Korean DCA device whose reliability the authors say had not been documented, alongside AnyCheck and Periotest.
- Quantifies agreement with Periotest (AnyCheck r = -0.870 vs The Trust r = -0.473) and test-retest reliability (ICC 0.94-0.98) in the same 58 implants.
- Identifies device ceiling (The Trust maxes at 90) and intraoral position (mandible, left mandibular site) as factors affecting agreement, with the caveat that Periotest is both reference and itself position-sensitive.

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective analysis of chart data, single center (Ilsan Hospital, Republic of Korea); 37 patients, 58 implants; IRB No. NHIMC 2024-05-002, consent waived (anonymized data). The IRB paragraph calls the study "prospective" while also stating it was retrospective.
- **Timing**: secondary stability measured before occlusal loading, mean 91 days after placement (SD 41.91); measurement delayed when secondary surgery was done or initial readings were insufficient for loading (PTV < 0 criterion cited by the authors).
- **Measurement**: 3 readings per implant per device with healing abutment in place, buccal strike, device parallel to the ground, 10-second intervals; devices used sequentially.
- **Recorded variables**: arch, anterior vs posterior, quadrant (#10 right maxilla, #20 left maxilla, #30 left mandible, #40 right mandible), healing abutment height (>6 mm / 6 mm or less) and diameter (>4 mm / 4 mm or less), fixture diameter (>4 mm) and length (10 mm vs 8.5 mm or less), time since placement (90-day split), bone graft, and magnitude of DCAV-P (-5.5 split).
- **Sample size**: PASS 11, power 0.90, alpha 0.05, reference correlation 0.777; minimum 25 (data from more than 25).
- **Statistics**: Spearman correlation on the mean of 3 readings; Fisher z-transformation with Z-test for subgroup differences; ICC for reliability (poor < 0.4, fair-to-good 0.4-0.7, excellent > 0.7); reliability differences judged by non-overlapping 95% CIs of ICCs (acknowledged as conservative and approximate); SPSS 23.

## Limitations

- Small retrospective single-center sample (58 implants from 37 patients); subgroup cells are small (for example 11-17 per quadrant), and the authors state larger samples are needed. Implants within the same patient are not stated to be handled as clustered observations.
- Periotest is used as the reference, so the study measures agreement with another damping device, not accuracy against histology, removal torque, RFA/ISQ or clinical outcome; the authors themselves note apparent regional accuracy loss may reflect true DCA-P differences rather than DCA-A error.
- The Trust has a ceiling of 90; the exclusion of values above 90 (a post hoc sensitivity analysis) changes the result, and the methods also mention a truncation at 90 for AnyCheck without reporting that result.
- Reliability is "test-retest of 3 readings in the same session", and differences were judged by CI overlap, which the authors call conservative; measurements were sequential on the same abutment with manufacturer correction values, not randomized in order.
- No implant failures or loading outcomes were analyzed, so no sensitivity, specificity or cutoffs for early failure prediction are reported for the new devices.
- Source-data inconsistencies in the retrieved text: (1) the ethics paragraph calls the study "prospective" and also retrospective; (2) Table 4 lists the Periotest total ICC 95% CI as 0.78-0.99 while the text gives 0.97-0.99; (3) reported R2 values do not equal the squares of the Spearman r (0.870 squared is 0.757 vs 0.828; 0.473 squared is 0.224 vs 0.379; 0.70 squared is 0.49 vs 0.6067), which is possible only if R2 comes from a separate linear regression; (4) the p-value for r = -0.870 (0.0039) is larger than that for r = -0.473 (0.0002) at the same n = 58, which is not mathematically consistent; (5) Introduction describes The Trust force as under 3 N while Table 5 gives 0.6-0.8 N; (6) the text says AnyCheck accuracy at #30 was lower than at #10 and #20, but #10 vs #30 is only p = 0.0498. Values in the tables were used here.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

For chairside stability checks before loading, both AnyCheck and The Trust can be used with the healing abutment in place and show repeatable readings (ICC 0.94-0.96), which suits repeated visits and multiple implants without removing the abutment, an advantage the authors contrast with RFA. However, the evidence here supports only agreement with Periotest: AnyCheck tracked Periotest closely, The Trust less so (and saturates at 90), and no cutoff for deciding on loading or predicting early failure was established for either new device. Readings from the left mandible and in low-stability (more negative Periotest value) implants agreed less well with Periotest for AnyCheck, so unexpected readings there should be repeated or cross-checked with another method, such as ISQ, before changing the loading plan. For the early-failure question, the wiki's early-failure framework still relies on clinical and procedural risk factors rather than these devices.
