## Summary
Kim and Kahm (2026) compared the inter-examiner reliability and device agreement between the Anycheck IST (Implant Stability Test, percussion/damping-based) and the Osstell ISQ (RFA-based) across hard, normal, and soft bone quality standardized models. Seven dental hygienists with varying experience levels performed measurements. Both devices demonstrated ICC>0.90 across all bone quality types, with strong IST–ISQ correlation (r>0.85) regardless of bone density. No significant effect of examiner experience on measurement consistency was found for either device. The IST system offers clinical advantages by eliminating SmartPeg requirements and reducing abutment manipulation, making it a practical cost-effective alternative in routine settings.

## Key Contributions
- IST achieves ICC>0.90 comparable to ISQ across hard, normal, and soft bone quality models
- IST–ISQ cross-device correlation r>0.85 — clinically interchangeable under standardized conditions
- Examiner experience level (novice vs. experienced hygienist) does not significantly affect either device's reliability
- Both devices correctly differentiate between hard, normal, and soft bone quality models
- IST practical advantage: no SmartPeg, no abutment manipulation, simplified workflow

## Methodology
Controlled laboratory study. Seven dental hygienists with varying experience levels. Standardized implant models representing three bone quality types (hard, normal, soft). Measurement devices: Anycheck IST (Neobiotech, Seoul, Korea; percussion/energy dissipation principle) and Osstell ISQ (Osstell, Gothenburg, Sweden; RFA principle). Outcomes: ICC (inter-examiner reliability), Pearson r (IST–ISQ correlation), examiner experience effect analysis.

## Results
- Inter-examiner ICC: >0.90 for both IST and ISQ across all 3 bone quality types
- IST–ISQ correlation: r>0.85 (strong positive) across all bone densities
- Examiner experience effect: not statistically significant for either device
- Bone quality differentiation: both devices successfully distinguish hard vs. soft bone
