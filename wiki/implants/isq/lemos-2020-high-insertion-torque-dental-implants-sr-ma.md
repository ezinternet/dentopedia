## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (6 studies, 389 patients, 651 implants) comparing dental implants placed with high insertion torque (삽입 토크, IT ≥50 Ncm) versus regular or low IT (<50 Ncm), with outcome measures of survival rate and marginal bone loss.

Neither implant survival (RR 0.51, 95% CI 0.06–4.06, P=.52) nor MBL (MD 0.15mm, 95% CI −0.14 to 0.44, P=.30) differed significantly between groups; however, the high-IT group showed a numerically lower failure rate (1.1% vs 4.2%), most pronounced under immediate loading.

Evidence base is sparse (only 6 eligible studies), preventing definitive conclusions — further RCTs with standardized IT reporting are needed to establish a safe upper torque limit.

## Summary

Lemos et al. (2020) conducted the first SR/MA using a predefined threshold of ≥50 Ncm to define "high IT" and searched for its clinical effect on implant survival and marginal bone loss compared to lower IT placement. Six eligible studies were identified (389 patients, 651 implants: 437 high IT, 214 low/regular IT; mean age 55.28 years). The pooled meta-analysis found no statistically significant difference in either implant survival rate or MBL between groups. While the observed failure rate was numerically lower with high IT (1.1% vs 4.2%), this difference was not significant and was largely attributed to one immediate-loading study where low IT performed worse. The authors conclude that high IT (≥50 Ncm) does not negatively affect implant survival or bone levels but caution that evidence remains insufficient to draw definitive recommendations.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes **50 Ncm as the high IT threshold** in the first SR/MA framework — creates a quantitative reference point for clinical decision-making
- Provides pooled evidence (RR, MD with 95% CI) that high IT does not harm survival or MBL based on current literature
- Identifies immediate loading as the protocol most affected by IT level: high IT numerically protective when loading immediately
- Quantifies the evidence gap: only 6 studies met PICO criteria, underscoring the need for prospective RCTs
- Contrasts with Marconcini 2018 RCT findings (see Related Papers) — highlights the SR/MA vs individual-RCT tension in this literature

## Methodology

**Design:** Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA)  
**PICO question:** "Do patients receiving implants with IT ≥50 Ncm show similar survival rates and MBL as those receiving implants with IT <50 Ncm?"  
**Search:** Electronic databases, all studies up to April 2019; no language restriction  
**Included studies:** 6 (389 patients; 651 implants: 437 high-IT, 214 low/regular-IT)  
**Loading:** Mostly delayed; 1 study immediate (n=50 per group)  
**Statistics:** Mantel-Haenszel for survival (dichotomous); inverse variance for MBL (continuous); α=0.05

## Results

| Outcome | High IT (≥50 Ncm) | Low/Regular IT (<50 Ncm) | Effect size | P value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Failure rate | 1.1% | 4.2% | RR 0.51 (95% CI 0.06–4.06) | .52 (NS) |
| MBL | — | — | MD 0.15mm (95% CI −0.14–0.44) | .30 (NS) |

**Immediate loading subgroup:** Low IT showed notably higher failure rate vs high IT (driven by one study; n=100 total).

**Bottom line:** No statistically significant harm or benefit from high IT on survival or MBL based on 6 included studies.
