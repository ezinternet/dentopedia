## Summary
This animal experimental study asked how peri-implant bone responds to the strains and stresses created when implants are placed with low versus high insertion torque (IT). Titanium micro-implants were inserted into murine femurs with torque values scaled to approximate clinically sized implants, strains were calculated by finite element modeling, and stability and cellular/histomorphometric outcomes were assessed at multiple time points. Osteotomy preparation alone produced a narrow zone of dead and dying osteocytes; low IT did not significantly enlarge it, whereas high IT more than doubled it and was accompanied by micro-fractures, increased resorption and decreased bone formation. The authors conclude that high IT should be avoided to best preserve peri-implant bone viability. This summary is abstract-level only.

## Key Contributions
- Links high IT to a larger zone of dead and dying osteocytes, micro-fractures, increased resorption and decreased formation in peri-implant bone.
- Shows that the osteotomy itself leaves a narrow zone of osteocyte death that low IT does not significantly enlarge.

## Methodology
Murine femur model; titanium micro-implants with low vs high IT (torque scaled to clinically sized implants); finite element modeling for strain distribution; stiffness tests for primary and secondary stability; molecular, cellular and histomorphometric analyses of apoptosis, mineralization, resorption and collagen matrix deposition. Sample sizes not available (abstract only).

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Osteotomy alone | Narrow zone of dead and dying osteocytes |
| Low IT | Zone not significantly enlarged |
| High IT | Zone more than doubled |
| High IT, bone tissue | Micro-fractures, increased resorption, decreased bone formation |
| Interpretation | High interfacial stress/strain associated with peri-implant bone damage; avoid high IT |
