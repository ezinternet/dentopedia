## Three-line Summary

In vitro bovine bone study (n=30 implant sites, 9 specimens) measuring RFA and direct lateral displacement (μm) under a standardized 25 N lateral force, with per-site CBCT bone density quantification and continuous insertion torque recording.

Significant inverse correlation between RFA and lateral displacement in both perpendicular and parallel directions (P ≤ 0.001); flex constant (μm/N) also inversely correlated with RFA (P ≤ 0.001); both RFA and displacement correlated with CBCT bone density (P ≤ 0.001).

RFA reflects the actual micromobility of dental implants — a physical quantity determined by peri-implant bone density — empirically grounding the ISQ number in measurable implant mechanics.

## Summary

This in vitro bench study by Pagliani et al. (J Oral Rehab 2013;40:221-227) is a foundational reference for understanding what RFA physically measures. Thirty implant sites were prepared in nine fresh bovine bone specimens; bone density was quantified per site by CBCT. Implants were inserted with continuous torque recording, and RFA was then measured in both perpendicular and parallel orientations relative to the bone block's long axis. The blocks were embedded in plaster and fixed in a custom rig; a 25 N lateral force was applied via an abutment, and lateral displacement (μm) was recorded for both orientations. A flex constant (μm/N) was derived for each measurement. The study found significant inverse correlations between RFA and lateral displacement (P ≤ 0.001), between RFA and flex constant (P ≤ 0.001), and significant correlations of both RFA and displacement with bone density (P ≤ 0.001). The conclusion — that RFA reflects implant micromobility, which is determined by bone density — provides the empirical bridge between the abstract ISQ number and actual implant mechanics.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes, in the same specimens, that ISQ/RFA is inversely correlated with actual lateral displacement (μm) under a defined force — i.e., higher ISQ = less physical wobble.
- Introduces **flex constant (μm/N)** as a normalized, force-independent mechanical stability index alongside ISQ.
- Closes the triad empirically: bone density → micromobility (μm) → RFA/ISQ, showing all three are correlated in the same dataset (P ≤ 0.001).
- Validates measurement in two orthogonal orientations (perpendicular and parallel to bone long axis), showing the relationship holds in both.

## Methodology

- **Design**: In vitro bench study, fresh bovine bone blocks
- **n**: 30 implant sites, 9 bone specimens
- **Bone density**: CBCT + imaging software per site (Hounsfield-equivalent units)
- **Insertion**: Dental implants placed with continuous torque registration
- **RFA**: Measured perpendicular and parallel to long axis of specimens (Osstell device implied by co-authorship and era)
- **Loading rig**: Bone blocks embedded in plaster, fixated in a displacement-measurement rig; 25 N lateral force applied via abutment
- **Outcomes**: Lateral displacement (μm), flex constant (μm/N), RFA value, bone density
- **Statistics**: Correlation analysis, P ≤ 0.001
