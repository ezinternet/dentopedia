## Three-line Summary

Prospective 1-year clinical trial of 62 narrow implants (ø2.9mm) in 31 atrophic edentulous mandibles, grouped by insertion torque into four ranges: <10, 10–30, 30–45, and ≥45 Ncm.

G4 (IT ≥45 Ncm) achieved the highest survival rate (95%) and maintained the most stable ISQ; G3 (IT 30–45 Ncm) had the worst survival (64.3%), producing a paradox contradicting linear torque-stability assumptions.

IT did not significantly influence marginal bone loss at 12 months; instead, bone remodeling and cytokine behavior were primarily driven by loading and patient factors, not torque magnitude alone.

## Summary

Faot et al. (2019) conducted a prospective 1-year trial to determine whether insertion torque (IT) range affects peri-implant health, stability, cytokine profiles, and survival of narrow-diameter implants (NDI) retaining mandibular overdentures in atrophic edentulous patients. Sixty-two ø2.9×10mm implants were placed in 31 patients and grouped post-hoc by achieved IT: G1 (<10 Ncm), G2 (10–30 Ncm), G3 (30–45 Ncm), and G4 (≥45 Ncm). All implants received conventional loading at 3 months. The primary finding was a paradoxical survival pattern: G4 (≥45 Ncm) achieved the best outcomes (95% survival, highest ISQ, positive marginal bone remodeling), while G3 (30–45 Ncm) showed the worst outcomes (64.3% survival, 5 failures). IT did not significantly influence MBL, IL-1β, or peri-implant clinical health indices. These results challenge the widely cited "optimal IT of 32 Ncm" recommendation and suggest that in atrophic edentulous mandible patients, higher IT may be beneficial rather than harmful — at least for narrow implants with this specific design and surface.

## Key Contributions

- Provides first prospective clinical data categorizing IT into four discrete ranges and tracking 1-year survival — the **G3 paradox** (intermediate-high IT 30–45 Ncm = worst survival) is a novel finding
- Demonstrates that IT ≥45 Ncm is clinically safe and associated with higher ISQ and positive bone remodeling in this patient population, contrary to osseocompression-damage theory
- Shows TNF-α (but not IL-1β) is negatively correlated with IT — lower torque produces greater proinflammatory response
- Confirms IT is only a weak predictor of primary ISQ (Kendall τ = 0.252, p=0.01), consistent with the literature
- Highlights that bone type, atrophy severity, and time since edentulism influence probing depth outcomes more than IT

## Methodology

**Design:** Prospective longitudinal clinical trial, 1-year follow-up  
**n:** 31 patients (21F/10M, mean age 66.9 years), 62 NDI (ø2.9×10mm Facility® NeoPoros, Neodent)  
**Setting:** Federal University of Pelotas, Brazil; one surgeon, one-stage protocol  
**IT grouping (manual wrench, post-hoc):**  
- G1: <10 Ncm (12 implants)  
- G2: 10–30 Ncm (16 implants)  
- G3: 30–45 Ncm (14 implants)  
- G4: ≥45 Ncm (20 implants)  

**Loading:** Conventional at 3 months; Equator abutments + mandibular overdenture  
**Primary outcomes:** Implant survival/success (Misch/Papaspyridakos), ISQ (Osstell), MBL  
**Secondary outcomes:** PD, GI, PI, BOP, CP, IL-1β and TNF-α in PICF  
**Statistics:** Mixed-effects regression, Kruskal-Wallis, Kendall τ, Kaplan-Meier

## Results

**Survival/success (12 months, Kaplan-Meier):**

| Group | IT (Ncm) | n | Survival | Failures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | <10 | 12 | 75% | 3 (at 2 months) |
| G2 | 10–30 | 16 | 81.3% | 3 (pre-loading) |
| G3 | 30–45 | 14 | **64.3%** | 5 (2 pre-load, 2 post-load) |
| G4 | ≥45 | 20 | **95%** | 1 (at 4 months) |

**ISQ:** G2 and G4 maintained higher ISQ throughout the 12-month period. G4 showed the smallest ISQ dip from baseline. G1 and G3 had lower and more variable ISQ trajectories. Weak positive correlation between IT and primary ISQ (Kendall τ=0.252, p=0.01).

**MBL/MBC:** No significant between-group differences in MBL at baseline or 12 months. G4 was the only group with positive MBC at 12 months (+0.29mm; non-significant).

**Cytokines:**
- IL-1β rose progressively in all groups, peaking at 12 months (~140% above month-1 values); not influenced by IT group
- TNF-α negatively correlated with IT; G1 had highest TNF-α at month 12 (~40% higher than G2/G4), suggesting greater inflammatory response with low torque
