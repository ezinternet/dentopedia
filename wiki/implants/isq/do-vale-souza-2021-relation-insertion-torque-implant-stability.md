## Three-line Summary
Prospective single-arm clinical study (n=25 patients, 25 implants, one uniform 11.5×3.75 mm Hexagonal Morse cone system, DSP Biomedical) correlating insertion torque (IT, Ncm) with ISQ (Osstell Mentor RFA) at placement and at 6 months.

IT positively correlated with initial ISQ (r=0.457, p=0.022) but not with final 6-month ISQ (p=0.308); mean ISQ rose from 48.24±19.28 to 65.96±8.47 (p<0.001); only 1/25 implants (Type III bone) failed osseointegration.

The IT–ISQ correlation is confined to primary stability; low initial ISQ (<60) in Type III bone mostly still osseointegrated (IT was the more consistent predictor), supporting ISQ as a complement to IT rather than a standalone predictor of osseointegration.

## Summary
Prospective single-arm study of 25 patients (25 uniform 11.5×3.75 mm Hexagonal Morse cone implants, DSP Biomedical; single operator) correlating insertion torque (IT) with implant stability quotient (ISQ) at placement (primary stability) and at 6 months (secondary stability). IT showed a positive correlation with initial ISQ (r=0.457, p=0.022) but no correlation with 6-month ISQ (p=0.308); mean ISQ rose significantly from 48.24±19.28 to 65.96±8.47 (p<0.001). Bone type drove both metrics — Type I bone had higher IT (36.50 vs 31.20 Ncm, p=0.018) and higher ISQ at both time points (66.60→70.10 vs 36.00→63.20). Only 1/25 implants (Type III) failed osseointegration, and failure was predicted by neither IT nor ISQ. Clinical implication: the IT–ISQ correlation is a primary-stability phenomenon, and a low initial ISQ does not mandate osseointegration failure — ISQ complements IT rather than replacing it.

## Key Contributions
- IT correlates positively with initial (placement) ISQ in a uniform single-system cohort: r=0.457, p=0.022.
- The IT–ISQ correlation does NOT carry over to secondary stability: IT vs 6-month ISQ was not correlated (p=0.308).
- Initial ISQ<60 (Type III mean 36.00) did not predict osseointegration failure — 14/15 Type III implants osseointegrated; IT was the more consistent predictor at the Osstell ISQ<60 threshold.
- Bone type negatively correlated with IT (r=−0.433, p=0.017) and with initial ISQ (r=−0.704, p<0.001): denser bone → higher torque and stability.

## Methodology
Prospective single-arm clinical study; 25 patients (68% female; mean age 50±9 years), one immediately post-extraction implant each, all 11.5×3.75 mm Hexagonal Morse cone (DSP Biomedical), single experienced operator, Araçatuba Dental School (UNESP), Brazil. Bone type classified by Lekholm–Zarb (Type I n=10, Type III n=15). IT maximum recorded with a calibrated torque wrench at placement; ISQ from Osstell Mentor RFA (mean of four SmartPeg points: mesial/distal/buccal/lingual) at placement and at 6 months; osseointegration by Albrektsson–Zarb survival criteria. Statistics: Pearson correlation (IT–ISQ), dependent Student's t (initial vs final ISQ), two-way repeated-measures ANOVA + Tukey (ISQ by bone type/time), independent t (IT by bone type), Kendall's correlation (osseointegration); α=0.05.

- Survival at 6 months: 24/25 (96%); the single failure (Type III bone) was predicted by neither IT nor ISQ.
- Loading inference: Type I implants qualified for delayed/early loading (ISQ ≥65); Type III implants only delayed loading; mean IT (31–37 Ncm) stayed below the 45 Ncm immediate/early-loading threshold cited from Trist et al., so delayed loading applied regardless of bone type.
