## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (review of reviews) of 12 systematic reviews/meta-analyses (MEDLINE/EMBASE/JBI/Cochrane/PROSPERO, 1990–May 2018) on diabetes mellitus (DM) and dental implant complications, AMSTAR-2 appraised.

Implant survival ranged 83.5–100% and failure 0–14.3% in DM patients; all three meta-analyses on "implant failure" found no statistically significant DM vs non-DM difference, while peri-implantitis risk appeared increased in DM; 50% of included reviews were AMSTAR-2 "critically low" quality.

DM as a whole does not appear to significantly raise implant failure risk in the pooled SR evidence, but the true effect of hyperglycemia specifically remains uncertain, and the underlying review quality is largely weak — a caution for over-interpreting reassuring individual SR/MA results.

## Summary

This umbrella review pools 12 systematic reviews/meta-analyses (search through May 2018) examining diabetes mellitus (DM) and dental implant complications — failure, survival, marginal bone loss, peri-implant disease, and post-surgical infection. Implant survival across the 12 reviews ranged 83.5–100% and failure ranged 0–14.3% in DM subjects. All three meta-analyses that specifically pooled "implant failure" as an event found **no statistically significant difference** between diabetic and non-diabetic patients. In contrast, peri-implantitis risk appeared elevated in DM/hyperglycemia, though this conclusion rests on fewer reviews. Critically, AMSTAR 2 appraisal classified half of the 12 included reviews as "critically low" quality, a quarter as "low", and only a quarter as "moderate" — none as "high" — meaning the reassuring failure-rate findings should be read with substantial caution.

The abstract also flags a recurring methodological gap across the underlying evidence: most primary studies treated DM as a single undifferentiated category rather than stratifying by glycemic control, DM type, or duration, so this umbrella review cannot resolve whether hyperglycemia itself (as opposed to a DM diagnosis broadly) is the operative risk factor.

## Key Contributions

- Provides a review-of-reviews layer synthesizing 12 SRs on DM and dental implants, rather than adding another primary-study-level SR.
- Formally grades the quality of the underlying evidence base with AMSTAR 2 — exposing that "DM does not significantly increase implant failure" rests mostly on critically-low/low-quality reviews.
- Separates the "implant failure" question (no significant difference found) from the "peri-implant disease" question (apparently increased risk in DM) — two outcomes that are sometimes conflated in narrower SRs.
- Search cutoff (May 2018) predates the wiki's existing individual DM-implant SR/SR+MA pages, giving a useful earlier reference point against which later, larger studies can be compared.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Umbrella review (systematic review of systematic reviews), search of MEDLINE, EMBASE, JBI Database of Systematic Reviews and Implementation Reports, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, and PROSPERO, 1990 through May 2018.
- **Eligibility**: SRs/meta-analyses evaluating DM's association with implant failure, survival, bone loss, peri-implant disease, or post-surgical infection.
- **Quality appraisal**: AMSTAR 2, 16-item tool rating each review as high/moderate/low/critically low.
- Abstract-only ingest (no PMC full text available); the number of primary studies/patients pooled within the 12 SRs is not specified in the retrievable abstract.

## Results

| Outcome | Finding |
|---|---|
| Implant survival range (DM) | 83.5%–100% |
| Implant failure range (DM) | 0%–14.3% |
| Meta-analyses on "implant failure" | 3 — none statistically significant (DM vs non-DM) |
| Peri-implantitis risk in DM | Apparently increased (limited number of reviews) |
| AMSTAR-2 quality: critically low | 50% of the 12 reviews |
| AMSTAR-2 quality: low | 25% |
| AMSTAR-2 quality: moderate | 25% |
| AMSTAR-2 quality: high | 0% |

**Clinical takeaway**: The pooled SR evidence through 2018 does not show DM significantly raising implant failure risk overall, but this reassurance is built on largely weak-quality reviews and on a DM-as-a-whole framing that cannot isolate hyperglycemia's specific contribution. Peri-implantitis risk, not just implant failure, is the more consistently flagged concern in DM patients and warrants closer peri-implant maintenance regardless of the "no significant failure difference" headline.
