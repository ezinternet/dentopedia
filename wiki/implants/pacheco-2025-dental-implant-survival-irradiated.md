## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (11 systematic reviews, 73,674 dental implants / 16,193 head-and-neck-cancer patients, PROSPERO CRD42023406059) comparing implant survival in irradiated vs. non-irradiated bone.

Pooled meta-analytic data (48,563 implants) showed 81.52% survival in irradiated bone vs. 94.64% in non-irradiated bone; irradiated maxilla and irradiated grafted bone fared worse than irradiated mandible and irradiated native bone, respectively.

All 11 included systematic reviews were rated critically low methodological quality (AMSTAR 2), so despite a consistent direction of effect, the certainty of evidence for implant survival in irradiated bone remains weak.

## Summary

This umbrella review pooled 11 systematic reviews (2013–2023, 73,674 dental implants in 16,193 head-and-neck-cancer patients, screened from 1,811 records) to compare implant survival between irradiated and non-irradiated bone. Across 48,563 implants entering meta-analytic synthesis, survival was 81.52% in irradiated bone (2,674/14,471 failures) versus 94.64% in non-irradiated bone (1,825/34,092 failures), a difference supported by 11 separate meta-analyses. Subgroup data further showed irradiated maxilla implants faring worse than irradiated mandible implants (survival 79.04% vs 93.55%), and irradiated grafted bone faring worse than irradiated native bone (82.21% vs 87.06%); a trend toward lower survival at higher radiation doses did not reach significance, nor did the timing of implant placement relative to radiotherapy (before/after 12 months). Critically, AMSTAR 2 appraisal rated all 11 included systematic reviews as critically low methodological quality, meaning the review's authors themselves caution that these pooled numbers are not reliable enough to drive clinical decision-making on their own. The clinical bottom line offered is that implants remain placeable in irradiated patients, but each case warrants multidisciplinary planning, weighing added risk/cost/time, meticulous oral hygiene, and close follow-up.

## Key Contributions

- Largest umbrella-review-level synthesis to date directly comparing dental implant survival in irradiated vs. non-irradiated head-and-neck-cancer patients (11 SRs, 73,674 implants).
- Quantifies subgroup risk gradients within irradiated bone: implantation site (maxilla worse than mandible), bone type (grafted worse than native), and (non-significant) trends for radiation dose and RT-to-implant timing.
- Formally appraises the evidence base with AMSTAR 2, documenting that the entire body of included systematic reviews is critically low quality — an explicit evidence-certainty caveat often missing when these survival percentages are cited clinically.

## Methodology

Umbrella review per PRISMA 2020 (PROSPERO CRD42023406059). Search: PubMed, Cochrane Library, Embase, Web of Science + manual/grey literature (Google Scholar), inception–Feb 2024, no language/date restriction, two independent reviewers (Cohen's κ=0.90). Inclusion: systematic reviews (with or without meta-analysis) comparing implant survival in irradiated vs. non-irradiated head-and-neck-cancer patients. 1,811 records screened → 45 full texts → 11 systematic reviews included (all 11 had conducted meta-analysis; 48,563 implants entered quantitative synthesis). Methodological quality: AMSTAR 2 (16 items, 7 critical domains), independently rated by two reviewers with third-reviewer arbitration.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Overall survival, irradiated vs non-irradiated bone | 81.52% (2,674/14,471 failed) vs 94.64% (1,825/34,092 failed); significant, 11 MAs |
| Irradiated maxilla vs irradiated mandible | 79.04% vs 93.55% (RR 3.16, 95% CI 1.76–5.68; RR 5.96, 95% CI 2.71–13.12) |
| Irradiated grafted vs irradiated native bone | 82.21% vs 87.06% (OR 2.06, 95% CI 1.44–2.94, ≥36 mo; OR 2.26, 95% CI 1.50–3.40, ≥60 mo) |
| High vs low radiation dose | 82.05% vs 90.01% (RR 1.59, 95% CI 0.98–2.59, p=0.06, NS) |
| Implant ≤12 mo vs >12 mo post-RT | 84.26% vs 88.51% (RR 1.37, 95% CI 0.76–2.45, p=0.29, NS) |
| AMSTAR 2 rating of included SRs (n=11) | All critically low overall confidence |
