## Three-line Summary

Prospective RCT comparing crestal bone loss among submerged implants (cover screw), non-submerged anatomical healing abutments, and non-submerged esthetic healing abutments during osseointegration (3 months) in mandibular posterior sites (n=30 implants, 16 patients). Submerged group showed the least bone loss (0.18±0.06 mm CBCT at 3 months), significantly less than anatomical healing abutment group (0.34±0.03 mm, p<0.001); esthetic healing abutment group was intermediate (0.21±0.03 mm) and not significantly different from submerged at 3 months. Submerged protocol with cover screw minimizes early marginal bone loss compared to non-submerged healing abutments, especially anatomical designs.

## 세줄요약

하악구치부에 식립된 30개 임플란트(16명)를 submerged (커버스크류), non-submerged anatomical healing abutment, non-submerged esthetic healing abutment 3군으로 무작위 배정해 3개월 골유착 기간 동안 변연골소실(MBL)을 IOPA·CBCT·IMAGE J로 비교. **서브머지드 군(커버스크류)**이 3개월 CBCT 기준 **0.18±0.06 mm**로 가장 적었고, **anatomical 힐링어버트먼트 군(0.34±0.03 mm)**과는 유의한 차이(p<0.001); **esthetic 힐링어버트먼트 군(0.21±0.03 mm)**은 submerged와 유의차 없음. 초기 골유착기에 **커버스크류로 submerged 치유하는 것이 힐링어버트먼트(특히 anatomical 디자인)보다 변연골소실 억제에 유리**하다.

## Summary

This randomized controlled trial from King George's Medical University (Lucknow, India) directly compared three healing protocols during the osseointegration phase: (1) submerged with cover screw (two-stage), (2) non-submerged with anatomical healing abutment, and (3) non-submerged with esthetic healing abutment (narrow base). Thirty implants were placed in mandibular posterior sites of periodontally healthy patients (soft tissue ≥2 mm, no augmentation). Crestal bone loss was measured at baseline, 1 month, and 3 months using IOPA with IMAGE J software and CBCT.

At 3 months, the submerged group had the lowest mean crestal bone loss by both CBCT (0.18±0.06 mm) and IOPA (0.12±0.03 mm). The anatomical healing abutment group showed the highest loss (CBCT 0.34±0.03 mm; IOPA 0.42±0.06 mm), significantly worse than both other groups (p<0.001). The esthetic healing abutment group was intermediate (CBCT 0.21±0.03 mm; IOPA 0.20±0.03 mm) and **did not differ significantly from the submerged group at 3 months by CBCT** (p=0.334), though it was significantly higher at 1 month (p=0.037). Clinical parameters (GI, PI, PPD) showed no intergroup differences.

The study concludes that the submerged technique with cover screw is superior for minimizing early crestal bone loss. Among non-submerged options, esthetic healing abutments (narrow base design) significantly outperform anatomical healing abutments, likely due to better soft tissue adaptation and reduced mechanical stress on the crestal bone.

## Key Contributions

- **First 3-arm RCT** directly comparing submerged (cover screw) vs two non-submerged healing abutment designs (anatomical vs esthetic) during early osseointegration
- **Quantified healing abutment design effect**: esthetic (narrow base) reduced MBL by ~38% vs anatomical at 3 months (0.21 vs 0.34 mm, p<0.001)
- **Submerged protocol advantage confirmed**: cover screw with primary closure yielded the least MBL, supporting traditional two-stage approach for bone preservation
- **Multi-modal radiographic assessment**: IOPA+IMAGE J and CBCT showed consistent ranking (submerged < esthetic < anatomical)
- **Mandibular posterior focus**: clinically relevant for the most common implant site

## Methodology

### Study Design
- Prospective, parallel-group, randomized controlled trial
- Registered in Clinical Trials Registry–India
- Ethics approval: KGMU Institutional Ethics Committee (Ref: 102nd ECM II B-Thesis/P14)
- CONSORT guidelines followed

### Participants
- **Screened**: 17 subjects
- **Analyzed**: 16 subjects (7 male, 9 female), age 18–50 years
- **Dropout**: 1 subject (2 implants) lost to follow-up
- **Inclusion**: Periodontally healthy, single missing mandibular posterior tooth, soft tissue thickness ≥2 mm (Linkevicius 2015), no bone augmentation needed
- **Exclusion**: Systemic diseases affecting healing, smokers, pregnant/lactating, steroid therapy

### Interventions
All implants: Bioner Implant System (Barcelona, Spain), bone-level, placed per manufacturer protocol

| Group | n (implants) | Protocol | Component |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group 1 (Submerged) | 10 | Two-stage, primary closure | Cover screw + 3-0 silk suture |
| Group 2 (Non-submerged Anatomical) | 10 | One-stage, transmucosal | Anatomical healing abutment 4.5 mm (DM-PCR45) |
| Group 3 (Non-submerged Esthetic) | 10 | One-stage, transmucosal | Esthetic healing abutment 4.5 mm (DM-PC45, narrow base) |

**Key design difference**: Esthetic abutment has same height (4.5 mm) and taper (120°) as anatomical, but **narrower base than implant platform** → better emergence profile, healthier peri-implant mucosa, barrier against microbe access.

### Outcome Measures
- **Primary**: Crestal bone loss (mesial, distal, total) at 1 and 3 months
- **Imaging**: 
  - IOPA (60 kV, 7 mA) + IMAGE J software (NIH)
  - CBCT (Carestream CS 9300, Trophy Dicom 6.4.0.4)
- **Timepoints**: Baseline (day of surgery), 1 month, 3 months
- **Clinical**: Gingival Index, Plaque Index, Probing Pocket Depth at baseline and 3 months

### Statistics
- Sample size: n=10/group (α=0.05, power 90%, effect size 0.22 mm, SD 0.12–0.14 from Mariano et al. 2016)
- One-way ANOVA + post-hoc for intergroup comparisons
- Repeated-measures ANOVA for intragroup changes over time
