## Three-line Summary

Narrative literature review (self-labeled "systematic review" but no PRISMA flow, no formal risk-of-bias tool, no meta-analysis; PubMed + Google Scholar, 2005–2017) compiling human clinical, FEA, and case-report evidence on platform switching (PS) and crestal bone loss.

All 9 tabulated human comparative studies favored PS over platform-matching; Hürzeler et al. 2007 (cited primary study) reported PS MBL 0.22 mm vs PM 2.02 mm and concluded a 0.45 mm per-side abutment-diameter reduction is sufficient to prevent peri-implant bone loss, with compiled per-side mismatches across studies clustering mostly at 0.25–0.45 mm (one outlier at 1.0 mm).

No original data of its own — a narrative aggregation, most studies (6/9) using the Biomet 3i system, so generalizability across connection designs is limited, and no dose-response analysis of offset magnitude was performed by the review's own authors.

## Summary

This 2019 narrative review from MMCDSR (Mullana, Ambala, India) surveys human clinical, FEA, and case-report literature on platform switching (PS) published 2005–2017 via PubMed and Google Scholar. Despite its title, it lacks the formal apparatus of a systematic review (no PRISMA flow, no risk-of-bias tool, no meta-analysis). Its distinct contribution to this wiki is Table 1, which — unlike any other platform-switching page held here — tabulates the actual abutment/implant diameter pairs used across 9 primary comparative studies, allowing per-side horizontal mismatch to be computed directly rather than inferred. The review surfaces Hürzeler et al. 2007's specific numeric claim that a 0.45 mm per-side abutment reduction is "sufficient" to avoid peri-implant bone loss, and separately reports Rodríguez-Ciurana et al. 2009's inter-implant distance findings (a related but distinct "distance" question — spacing between adjacent PS implants, not abutment-to-implant offset).

## Key Contributions

- First page in this wiki's PS cluster to report actual offset dimensions (mm) rather than only PS-vs-PM bone-loss outcomes — fills the "how much mismatch is needed" gap left open by [[overviews/platform-switching-marginal-bone-preservation-synthesis]].
- Surfaces the closest available numeric threshold claim: Hürzeler et al. 2007's 0.45 mm/side "sufficient" conclusion (single-study, not independently re-tested by this review).
- Distinguishes two separate "distance" concepts that are easy to conflate: (1) horizontal abutment-implant mismatch (the PS offset itself) and (2) inter-implant distance between adjacent PS implants (Rodríguez-Ciurana 2009: <3 mm spacing, 41 pairs, bone peak 0.24 mm coronal to the IAJ-to-IAJ line).
- Compiles 5 FEA reports on butt-joint vs taper-joint abutment connection biomechanics under PS (butt-joint e.g. Replace Select/3i vs taper-joint e.g. Straumann, Astra, Ankylos; vertical/diagonal loading 10–250N at 15°–30°).
- Gives the fullest mechanistic account in this wiki's PS cluster: inward IAJ shift → inflammatory cell infiltrate (ICT) moves toward the implant's central axis and away from crestal bone → connective tissue thickens laterally → ICT stays confined above the implant platform → biologic width need not relocate apically → no compensatory crestal remodeling. Cites Ericsson et al. (~1mm connective tissue zone encircling bone) and Jansen et al. (ICT extends ~0.5–0.75mm coronal and apical to the IAJ, always separated from bone by ~1mm connective tissue).
- Proposes combining Morse taper connections (narrower microgap) with PS (greater microgap-to-bone distance) as complementary crestal-preservation strategies.

## Methodology

Narrative review, PubMed + Google Scholar, 2005–2017, with stated inclusion/exclusion criteria but no PRISMA flow diagram, no formal quality/risk-of-bias appraisal, and no meta-analysis. 9 human comparative studies tabulated; 5 FEA studies discussed narratively.

## Results

| Cited study | Per-side mismatch | Bone outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Hürzeler 2007 | 0.45 mm (test) vs 0 (control) | 0.22 mm (PS) vs 2.02 mm (PM); "0.45mm/side sufficient" conclusion |
| Cappiello 2008 | 0.35 mm vs 0 | 0.95±0.32mm (PS) vs 1.67±0.37mm (PM) |
| Canullo 2009 | 0.85 mm vs 0 | 0.3±0.157mm (PS) vs 1.19±0.138mm (PM) |
| Canullo & Rasperini 2007 | 1.0 mm (largest tested) | M 0.57mm, D 1.01mm bone resorption |
| Prosper 2009 | 0.25–0.35 mm | 98.3% (PS) vs 66.1% (PM) implants with no crestal bone loss, P<0.001 |
| Rodríguez-Ciurana 2009 | 0.35–0.45 mm (offset); separately, interimplant spacing <3mm | Offset: M 0.7±0.57mm; interimplant: vertical 0.62mm, horizontal 0.60mm resorption |

## Glossary

- **Platform Switching (PS/PLS)**: use of a smaller-diameter abutment on a larger-diameter implant platform, shifting the implant-abutment junction (IAJ) inward toward the implant's central axis.
- **Implant-Abutment Junction (IAJ)**: the interface/microgap between implant platform and abutment; its horizontal and vertical position relative to crestal bone is central to the PS mechanism.
- **Inflammatory Cell Infiltrate (ICT)**: the zone of inflammatory cells forming around the IAJ microgap from bacterial colonization; PS is hypothesized to relocate it away from crestal bone.
- **Biologic width**: the soft-tissue dimension (junctional epithelium + connective tissue) re-established around an implant; crestal bone remodels to accommodate it if the ICT encroaches too close to bone.
