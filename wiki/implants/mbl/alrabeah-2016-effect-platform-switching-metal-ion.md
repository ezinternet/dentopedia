## Three-line Summary

In vitro accelerated-corrosion study: 42 CPTi implant cylinders connected to titanium-alloy (Ti-6Al-4V) or cobalt-chrome (CoCr) abutments at three platform diameters (6mm matched, 5mm/4mm switched), plus unconnected controls, statically immersed in 1% lactic acid (pH 2.3, ISO 10271, 37°C, 7 days), with released Ti/V/Al/Co/Cr/Mo quantified by ICP-MS and interfacial surfaces examined by SEM/EDX.

Platform-matched groups released significantly more metal ions than platform-switched groups for V, Al (titanium-abutment groups) and Co, Cr, Mo (CoCr-abutment groups) (p<0.05, roughly twofold), with the decrease proportional to the amount of mismatch for Co, Cr, and Mo; titanium was the highest-released element overall. SEM showed active pitting corrosion at implant-abutment interfaces in all groups, most pronounced at the outer borders of the platform.

Provides the first indirect evidence that reduced corrosion/metal-ion release — not only mechanical stress redistribution or microgap-inflammation distancing — may partly explain platform switching's clinical crestal-bone-preservation effect, since wider mismatch moves the corroding interface further from peri-implant tissue.

## 세줄요약

In vitro 가속부식(Accelerated Corrosion) 연구 — 순티타늄(Commercially Pure Titanium, CPTi) 임플란트 42개를 티타늄합금(Ti-6Al-4V) 또는 코발트-크롬(CoCr) 지대주(abutment)에 3가지 플랫폼 직경(6mm 매칭, 5mm/4mm 스위칭)으로 연결, 1% 젖산(lactic acid, pH 2.3, ISO 10271)에 37°C 7일 침지 후 유도결합플라즈마 질량분석(Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry, ICP-MS)으로 Ti/V/Al/Co/Cr/Mo 이온 정량, 주사전자현미경(Scanning Electron Microscopy, SEM)·에너지분산형X선분광(Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy, EDX)으로 접촉면 관찰.

플랫폼 매칭(Platform-Matched) 그룹이 플랫폼 스위칭(Platform-Switched) 그룹보다 바나듐·알루미늄(티타늄 지대주군), 코발트·크롬·몰리브덴(CoCr 지대주군) 이온 유출이 유의하게(p<0.05, 약 2배) 많았고, Co·Cr·Mo는 부정합(mismatch) 정도에 비례해 감소; 티타늄은 전 그룹에서 유출량 최다 원소.

플랫폼 스위칭의 임상적 치조정골(crestal bone) 보존 효과가 기계적 응력 재분배·미세간극(microgap) 거리화뿐 아니라 부식 산물(corrosion product, 금속이온) 감소로도 일부 설명될 수 있음을 시사하는 첫 간접 증거를 제공.

## Summary

This in vitro corrosion study adds a third mechanistic explanation to the wiki's platform-switching (PS) evidence base. Existing PS pages explain the bone-preservation effect via stress redistribution (biomechanical theory — see [[implants/mbl/yadav-2025-finite-element-analysis-platform-switching]]) or via horizontal repositioning of the microgap away from crestal bone (bacterial/biologic-width theory). This paper tests a third, previously unexplored candidate borrowed from orthopaedic corrosion literature: that platform-switched configurations release less metal ion/corrosion product at the implant-abutment interface, and that this reduced ionic burden may itself contribute to less peri-implant bone disruption. Using accelerated static corrosion (1% lactic acid, ISO 10271) on titanium implant cylinders connected to either Ti-alloy or CoCr abutments at matched (6mm) vs. switched (5mm, 4mm) platform diameters, the authors found platform-matched groups released significantly more V, Al, Co, Cr, and Mo than switched groups, with a dose-response relationship (greater mismatch → less Co/Cr/Mo release). SEM confirmed active pitting corrosion concentrated at the outer edge of the interface — the region closest to peri-implant soft tissue — supporting the geometric logic that platform switching physically distances the corroding surface from the tissue that would otherwise absorb its by-products.

## Key Contributions

- First study to directly quantify (via ICP-MS) elemental metal-ion release differences between platform-matched and platform-switched implant-abutment couples, across two clinically relevant abutment materials (Ti-6Al-4V, CoCr) and two switching magnitudes.
- Demonstrates a dose-response pattern: for Co, Cr, and Mo, ion release decreased proportionally as platform mismatch increased (6mm matched > 5mm switched > 4mm switched) — directly paralleling the clinical dose-response between mismatch amount and marginal bone loss reported by Canullo et al.
- SEM evidence that corrosion (pitting) concentrates at the outer border of the implant-abutment interface, geometrically consistent with platform switching moving the corrosion source away from peri-implant tissue.
- Proposes corrosion/metal-ion release as a mechanistic pathway complementary to — not competing with — the established biomechanical-stress and bacterial/biologic-width theories of PS's crestal-bone-preservation effect.

## Methodology

- In vitro accelerated static-immersion corrosion test, ISO 10271-compliant (1% lactic acid, pH 2.3, 0.1 mol/L lactic acid + 0.1 mol/L NaCl; 37°C, 7 days).
- 42 CPTi (Grade II) implant cylinders; 36 connected to abutments (18 Ti-6Al-4V, 18 CoCr, each split into matched [6mm], switched [5mm], switched [4mm] subgroups, n=6), plus unconnected implant/abutment controls (n=6 each). 9 groups, 54 specimens total.
- Metal ion release (Ti, V, Al, Co, Cr, Mo) quantified by ICP-MS (ppb). Interfacial surfaces examined pre-/post-immersion by SEM and EDX.
- Statistics: ANOVA/Bonferroni or Kruskal-Wallis depending on homogeneity (Levene's test); Mann-Whitney U for connected-vs-unconnected comparisons; α=0.05.

## Results

- **Titanium**: highest-released element in every group regardless of abutment material/size/connection status (p<0.05); no significant difference between matched (TM) and switched (TSW/TS) Ti-abutment groups specifically for Ti.
- **Vanadium & Aluminium** (Ti-abutment groups): significantly higher release in matched (TM) vs. both switched groups (TSW, TS) (p<0.05).
- **Cobalt, Chromium, Molybdenum** (CoCr-abutment groups): all three significantly higher in matched (CM) than switched groups, with release decreasing significantly as abutment platform size decreased (dose-response with mismatch amount) (p<0.05).
- **Matched vs. switched, overall**: matched groups released approximately twofold higher V, Co, Cr, and Mo than switched groups within each abutment material; differences statistically significant for V, Al, Co, Mo, and Cr.
- **Unconnected vs. connected**: unconnected implants (UI) released significantly more Ti than connected matched (CM) and switched (CSW) groups (p<0.05); unconnected CoCr abutments (UC) released significantly more Co than the most-switched connected group (CS) (p<0.05) — attributed to larger surface area directly exposed to the electrolyte when unconnected.
- **SEM**: active pitting corrosion on implant and abutment interfacial surfaces in all groups post-immersion, most pronounced near the outer borders of the contacting platform — the zone closest to surrounding peri-implant tissue in vivo.
- **EDX**: no cross-deposition of abutment-derived elements onto implant surfaces or vice versa, indicating corrosion products are released into the surrounding environment rather than redepositing locally — consistent with these products reaching peri-implant tissue through the microgap in vivo.
- Authors' interpretation: the reduced metal-ion release in platform-switched groups may partly explain PS's clinically observed crestal-bone-preservation effect, since greater platform mismatch positions the active corrosion zone (interface outer border) further from the peri-implant tissue that would otherwise absorb the released ions.
