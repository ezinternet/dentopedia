## Three-line Summary

Comparative clinical study (abstract-only) of 30 laser-microtextured 4.6 mm implants versus 30 platform-switched 5/4 mm implants in 30 patients with thin mucosa (≤2 mm), radiographically followed to 1 year.

Laser-microtextured sites lost less crestal bone before loading — 0.71 ± 0.25 mm vs 1.02 ± 0.25 mm at 2 months (p = 0.001) and 1.10 ± 0.30 mm vs 1.37 ± 0.27 mm at restoration delivery (p = 0.001) — but the difference vanished at 1 year (1.41 ± 0.42 mm vs 1.43 ± 0.23 mm, p = 0.976).

With vertical soft-tissue thickness ≤2 mm at placement, neither a laser-microtextured collar nor platform switching eliminated crestal bone loss; the laser benefit is limited to the pre-loading period.

## Summary

This comparative clinical study (Clin Oral Implants Res 2015;26(5):599–605; PMID 25558894) tested whether a laser-microtextured collar or a platform-switched connection could maintain crestal bone in the most demanding setting — thin peri-implant mucosa (≤2 mm). Thirty laser-microtextured 4.6 mm implants (Tapered Internal Laser-Lok, BioHorizons; Group 1) and thirty platform-switched 5/4 mm implants (Certain Prevail, Biomet/3i; Group 2) were placed in the posterior mandible of 30 patients (12 men, 18 women; mean age 42.3 ± 2.4 years) in a one-stage approach and restored with screw-retained metal-ceramic restorations after integration. Crestal bone loss was measured radiographically at placement, 2 months, restoration delivery, and 1 year. Group 1 lost significantly less bone at 2 months (0.71 ± 0.25 mm vs 1.02 ± 0.25 mm, p = 0.001) and at delivery (1.10 ± 0.30 mm vs 1.37 ± 0.27 mm, p = 0.001), but the groups converged by 1 year (1.41 ± 0.42 mm vs 1.43 ± 0.23 mm, p = 0.976). The authors conclude that neither strategy eliminates crestal bone loss when vertical soft tissue is ≤2 mm at placement, though laser microtexturing may reduce proximal bone loss before loading. The artifact is a PubMed abstract only, so all numbers are abstract-level and no full-text methodological details are available.

## Key Contributions

- Head-to-head comparison of laser microtexturing versus platform switching **inside the same thin-mucosa (≤2 mm) constraint**, isolating design strategy from soft-tissue thickness.
- Demonstrates a **time-limited** laser benefit: significant crestal-bone sparing at 2 months and restoration delivery (both p = 0.001) that is fully lost by 1 year (p = 0.976).
- Confirms that ≤2 mm vertical soft tissue at placement overrides the bone-preserving intent of both surface microtexturing and connection offset — ~1.4 mm 1-year loss in both groups.
- Supplies four time points, making the early-healing versus loading-phase trajectory visible rather than reporting a single endpoint.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Comparative clinical study (randomization not stated in the abstract; evidence level `prospective`).
- **Population**: 30 patients (12 males, 18 females; mean age 42.3 ± 2.4 years) with thin mucosal tissues (≤2 mm).
- **Groups**: Group 1 = 30 laser-microtextured 4.6 mm implants (Tapered Internal Laser-Lok, BioHorizons); Group 2 = 30 platform-switched 5/4 mm implants (Certain Prevail, Biomet/3i).
- **Setting**: Posterior mandible, one-stage placement; screw-retained metal-ceramic restorations after integration.
- **Outcomes**: Mean crestal bone loss at four radiographic time points — after placement, 2 months after healing, at restoration delivery, and after 1-year follow-up.
- **Statistics**: Mann–Whitney U-test, significance set at 0.05.
- **Source limitation**: PubMed abstract only (no PMC record, no PDF obtained).

## Results

| Time point | Laser-microtextured 4.6 mm (Group 1, n = 30) | Platform-switched 5/4 mm (Group 2, n = 30) | P value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 months after healing | 0.71 ± 0.25 mm SD (range, 0.25–1.6 mm) | 1.02 ± 0.25 mm SD (range, 0.6–1.55 mm) | 0.001 |
| At restorations' delivery | 1.10 ± 0.30 mm SD (range, 0.65–1.85 mm) | 1.37 ± 0.27 mm SD (range, 0.90–1.80 mm) | 0.001 |
| After 1-year follow-up | 1.41 ± 0.42 mm SD (range, +0.1–2.30 mm) | 1.43 ± 0.23 mm SD (range, 1–1.80 mm) | 0.976 |

- The laser-microtextured group's advantage is 0.31 mm at 2 months and 0.27 mm at restoration delivery, both statistically significant.
- At 1 year the groups differ by only 0.02 mm (p = 0.976); the Group 1 range includes a +0.1 mm value (bone gain) but the group mean still converges with platform switching.
- No covariate adjustment, site-level clustering, survival, or peri-implant-disease outcomes are reported in the available abstract.
