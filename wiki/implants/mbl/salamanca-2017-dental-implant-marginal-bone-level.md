## Three-line Summary

Retrospective clinical study (51 patients, 60 Dentium implants — 30 platform-switched (PS) in 24 patients vs 30 platform-matched (PM) in 27 patients, Shuang-Ho Hospital, Taiwan, 12-month follow-up after final prosthetic restoration) measuring vertical and horizontal peri-implant marginal bone **gap** (implant shoulder to bone defect margin) rather than the more typical "bone loss from baseline" framing.

Both groups showed gap reduction ("bone gain") over 12 months, but PS showed significantly more vertical gap reduction (0.93±1mm gain, p<0.05) than PM (0.23±0.58mm); a second reported metric (average vertical gap reduction, baseline→12mo) confirmed this: PS 0.92±1.11mm vs PM 0.29±0.85mm (p<0.05); horizontal gap gain favored PS numerically (0.50±0.56mm vs 0.22±0.53mm) but was not statistically significant.

100% implant survival/success in both groups (Buser criteria + bone loss <1mm); authors conclude PS was more effective for peri-implant vertical marginal bone gap reduction at 1 year, consistent in direction with prior PS systematic reviews.

## Summary

This retrospective 1-year study from Shuang-Ho Hospital (Taiwan) compares platform-switched (PS) and platform-matched (PM) implants using the same implant system (Dentium) and the same surgeon, controlling for two common confounds in the PS literature. Its distinguishing methodological feature is the **outcome framing**: rather than tracking bone loss from a flush baseline, the study measures a vertical and horizontal "gap" between the implant shoulder and the most apical/lateral point of the peri-implant marginal bone defect at six timepoints (baseline through 12 months after loading), and reports how much that gap **shrinks** over time — termed "bone gain" or "gap reduction." Both PS and PM groups showed gap reduction, but the vertical reduction was significantly larger with PS (0.93±1mm vs 0.23±0.58mm bone gain, p<0.05; corroborated by a second reported figure of 0.92±1.11mm vs 0.29±0.85mm, p<0.05). The horizontal gap reduction favored PS numerically (0.50±0.56mm vs 0.22±0.53mm) but did not reach significance. Both groups achieved 100% implant success/survival (Buser criteria + bone loss <1mm) over the study period. The direction of the finding — PS better preserving/restoring marginal bone than PM — is consistent with prior systematic reviews the paper's discussion cites, though the "gap reduction" framing is not directly numerically comparable to "bone loss" figures reported by most other PS studies.

## Key Contributions

- Same implant system (Dentium, internal conical connection) and same surgeon for both PS and PM groups, removing two confounds common across the broader PS literature (system design, operator variability).
- Six radiographic timepoints (baseline, immediately after loading, 1/3/6/12 months) rather than the typical baseline-and-endpoint-only design, showing that most of the gap reduction in PS occurred by 6 months and plateaued by 12 months.
- Explicitly measures and reports **gap size** (implant shoulder to bone defect) rather than bone loss from baseline — the defect present at implant placement fills in over the follow-up period, which the paper frames as "bone gain."
- 100% success/survival in both groups over 12 months, situating the statistically significant vertical difference within an overall favorable and comparably safe outcome for both configurations.

## Methodology

- Retrospective clinical study, Taipei Medical University Joint IRB approval 201301034, Declaration of Helsinki (Edinburgh 2000) compliant.
- 51 patients (30 men, 21 women), 60 Dentium implants, treated March 2010–January 2015 at Shuang-Ho Hospital, New Taipei, Taiwan.
- 30 PM implants (27 patients; diameter 3.4–4.5mm, length 9.5–13mm); 30 PS implants (24 patients; diameter 3.3–4.8mm, length 8–12mm, 0.6mm horizontal circular step). Early placement, 6 weeks post-extraction; healing abutment surgery at 3 months; final crown ~8 weeks after that.
- Periapical radiographs (long-cone paralleling technique) at baseline, immediately after loading, and 1/3/6/12 months after loading; vertical and horizontal gap (implant shoulder to bone defect margin) measured mesially and distally with EZ-Dental software by two examiners blinded to the surgical operator.
- Success = Buser (1990) criteria + bone loss <1mm (mean mesial/distal). Statistics: paired/independent t-tests, Jarque-Bera normality test, significance at p<0.05.

## Results

| Metric | Platform Switching (PS) | Platform Matching (PM) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vertical gap, baseline → 12mo | 2.09±1.22mm → 1.17±1.01mm | 1.24±0.86mm → 1.01±0.58mm | — |
| Horizontal gap, baseline → 12mo | 1.55±0.97mm → 1.05±0.56mm | 1.33±0.82mm → 1.11±0.53mm | — |
| Vertical gap gain ("bone gain") | 0.93 ± 1 mm | 0.23 ± 0.58 mm | <0.05 |
| Horizontal gap gain ("bone gain") | 0.50 ± 0.56 mm | 0.22 ± 0.53 mm | NS |
| Avg. vertical gap reduction, baseline→12mo | 0.92 ± 1.11 mm | 0.29 ± 0.85 mm | <0.05 |
| Avg. horizontal gap reduction, baseline→12mo | 0.50 ± 0.87 mm | 0.22 ± 0.58 mm | NS |

- Within-PS: vertical and horizontal gap reduction both significant baseline vs 6 months and baseline vs 12 months.
- Within-PM: vertical gap reduction significant only for distal measurements at baseline vs 12 months; horizontal gap reduction not significant at any timepoint.
- Implant success/survival: 100% in both groups at 12 months (Buser criteria + bone loss <1mm).
- Only the vertical gap-reduction difference between PS and PM reached statistical significance; the horizontal difference did not.
