## Three-line Summary

One-year retrospective study of 4.7-mm-diameter implants in the maxilla (control, conventional non-platform-switched: 25 patients; test, platform-switched: 26 patients), most with transcrestal sinus augmentation using mineralized xenograft, relating prosthetic abutment height to marginal bone loss in cement-retained restorations.

Mean mesial and distal marginal bone loss in the control group was significantly more than twice that of the test group at similar abutment height; regression showed an inverse abutment height–bone loss relationship in both groups, with the intercept 50% lower for the test group, and bone loss annulled at 2.5 mm (platform-switched) versus 3.0 mm (conventional).

Shorter abutments meant greater bone loss, and abutment height mattered more in platform-switched implants; bone loss did not differ between native and grafted maxillary bone (abstract-only).

## 세줄요약

4.7 mm 직경 상악 임플란트에 대한 후향 연구 (Retrospective Study) — 대조군 (기존 비-플랫폼 스위칭, 25명) 대 시험군 (플랫폼 스위칭 (Platform Switching, PS), 26명), 다수는 광화 이종골 (Xenograft)을 이용한 치조정접근 상악동 거상 (Transcrestal Sinus Floor Elevation, TSFE) 병행; 시멘트 유지형 보철에서 지대주 높이 (Abutment Height)와 변연골소실 (Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)의 관계를 분석.

대조군의 근·원심 평균 MBL은 시험군의 2배 넘게 유의하게 컸고 (지대주 높이는 유사), 회귀분석에서 양 군 모두 지대주 높이와 MBL이 유의한 역상관이었으며 시험군 회귀선의 절편이 50% 낮았고, MBL은 시험군 2.5 mm, 대조군 3.0 mm 지대주에서 0이 되었다.

지대주가 짧을수록 MBL이 컸고 지대주 높이의 영향은 PS 임플란트에서 더 컸으며, 자연골과 이식골 식립 임플란트 간 MBL 차이는 없었다 (초록만 확보).

## Summary

This retrospective study by Spinato, Galindo-Moreno, Bernardello and Zaffe (Int J Oral Maxillofac Implants 2018;33(2):405-411) estimated the minimum prosthetic abutment height needed to eliminate marginal bone loss around 4.7-mm-diameter implants in the maxilla. Two implant types with a similar neck design were placed singly: platform-switched implants (test, 26 patients) and conventional non-platform-switched implants (control, 25 patients). Patients needing augmentation had unilateral transcrestal sinus augmentation with mineralized xenograft. Radiographs were taken after placement, after prosthesis delivery, and after 12 months of loading. Control-group mesial and distal marginal bone loss was significantly more than twice that of the test group at similar abutment height. Linear regression showed a significant inverse relation between abutment height and bone loss in both groups, with the intercept 50% lower for the test group; bone loss was annulled at 2.5 mm (platform-switched) and 3.0 mm (conventional). No significant difference was found between native and grafted maxillary bone. Only the PubMed abstract is available, so absolute values and p-values are not reproduced here.

## Key Contributions

- Gives numeric abutment-height thresholds at which regression-predicted bone loss reaches zero: 2.5 mm (platform-switched) and 3.0 mm (conventional).
- Shows platform switching both lowers bone loss (control more than twice test) and makes bone loss more sensitive to abutment height.
- Finds no significant bone-loss difference between native and grafted (transcrestal sinus augmentation with xenograft) maxillary sites.
- Extends the "short abutment = more bone loss" finding of [[implants/mbl/galindomoreno-2014-prosthetic-abutment-height-is-a]] from a categorical <2 vs ≥2 mm contrast to a continuous regression.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective study; radiographs at implant placement, prosthesis delivery and 12 months of loading.
- **Population**: Control group 25 patients (conventional implants), test group 26 patients (platform-switched implants); 4.7-mm-diameter implants in maxillary bone, singly placed.
- **Grafting**: Unilateral sinus augmentation via transcrestal technique with mineralized xenograft where needed.
- **Restoration**: Cement-retained prostheses.
- **Analysis**: Linear regression of mesial and distal marginal bone loss on abutment height.
- **Evidence note**: Abstract-only — implant brands, absolute bone-loss values, coefficients and p-values are not available.

## Results

| Finding | Control (conventional) | Test (platform-switched) |
|---|---|---|
| Patients | 25 | 26 |
| Mean mesial/distal marginal bone loss | significantly more than twice the test group | reference |
| Regression intercept (mesial and distal) | reference | 50% lower than control |
| Abutment height at which bone loss was annulled | 3.0 mm | 2.5 mm |

- Average abutment height was similar between groups.
- Inverse relationship between abutment height and marginal bone loss was statistically significant in both groups.
- No statistically significant difference in marginal bone loss between native maxillary bone and grafted areas.
- **Interpretation limits**: the thresholds are regression-derived; implant type (PS vs conventional) is confounded with implant system; follow-up is 12 months after loading; abstract-only.
