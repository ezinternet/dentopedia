## Three-line Summary

Randomized clinical trial comparing crestal bone loss at mesial/distal surfaces of 310 Astra Tech implants placed via one-stage (170 implants, 90 patients) or two-stage (140 implants, 50 patients) surgery, with 6-month post-loading follow-up using parallel periapical radiography.
Mean MBL was 0.760±0.04 mm (one-stage) vs 0.842±0.04 mm (two-stage); difference not statistically significant (p>0.05). Maxilla (0.860 mm) vs mandible (0.729 mm) also showed no significant difference.
One-stage surgery offers comparable bone preservation with reduced treatment time and patient discomfort, supporting its use as a viable alternative to two-stage approach.

## Summary

This randomized clinical trial from Shiraz University of Medical Sciences (Iran) compared marginal bone loss (MBL) between one-stage and two-stage surgical approaches for dental implant placement. A total of 310 Astra Tech cylindrical implants were placed in 140 systemically healthy (ASA I), non-smoking patients aged 18–65 years. The one-stage group (90 patients, 170 implants) received healing abutments at surgery with transmucosal healing, while the two-stage group (50 patients, 140 implants) underwent submerged healing with cover screws and primary closure. All surgeries were performed by a single operator using a standardized protocol.

Baseline parallel periapical radiographs were taken immediately post-surgery. After 3 months of healing, prosthetic loading was completed, and follow-up radiographs were taken at 6 months post-loading using the same technique and machine. MBL was measured on mesial and distal surfaces by an oral and maxillofacial radiologist using Adobe Photoshop CS5, with the known implant length as a magnification reference.

At 6 months post-loading, mean MBL was 0.760±0.04 mm (one-stage) versus 0.842±0.04 mm (two-stage). The difference was not statistically significant (p>0.05). Combined analysis showed MBL of 0.860 mm in the maxilla and 0.729 mm in the mandible, also non-significant (p>0.05). The authors conclude that one-stage surgery provides comparable crestal bone preservation while reducing treatment time, number of surgeries, and patient discomfort — particularly beneficial for patients with systemic conditions.

## Key Contributions

- **Large-sample RCT** (310 implants, 140 patients) directly comparing one-stage vs two-stage protocols with standardized radiographic assessment
- **6-month post-loading follow-up** extends beyond the typical 3-month osseointegration phase assessed in many submerged vs non-submerged studies
- **Single-operator, single-implant-system design** (Astra Tech cylindrical) minimizes confounding from surgical technique and implant macrogeometry
- **Standardized radiographic methodology** using implant length as a known reference for magnification correction on 2D periapical images

## Methodology

### Study Design
- Prospective, parallel-group, randomized clinical trial (unequal allocation: ~55% one-stage, ~45% two-stage)
- Ethics approval: Shiraz University of Medical Sciences IRB (#92-6692)
- Single center, single surgeon, single implant system

### Participants
- **140 patients** (100 female, 40 male), age 18–65 years
- **Inclusion**: ASA I, non-smokers, no addiction, cooperative, no periodontal disease, no need for hard/soft tissue augmentation, fixed prosthesis treatment plan
- **Exclusion**: Systemic disease, smoking, local pathology, need for regeneration/grafting

### Interventions
| Parameter | One-stage (Group A) | Two-stage (Group B) |
|---|---|---|
| Patients | 90 | 50 |
| Implants | 170 | 140 |
| Healing protocol | Healing abutment placed at surgery, flap closed around it (non-submerged) | Cover screw placed, primary flap closure (submerged) |
| 2nd surgery | None | Required for healing abutment connection |

All implants: Astra Tech system (Dentsply Implants, Sweden), cylindrical design. Preoperative antibiotics (2g amoxicillin + 400mg ibuprofen), 0.12% chlorhexidine mouthwash. Local anesthesia: 2% lidocaine with 1:100,000 epinephrine. Crestal mucoperiosteal incision with envelope flap.

### Outcome Measures
- **Primary**: Marginal bone loss (mesial + distal) at 6 months post-loading
- **Measurement**: Parallel periapical radiography (same machine, same technique) at baseline (post-op) and 6 months post-loading
- **Analysis**: Adobe Photoshop CS5; implant length as magnification reference
- **Statistics**: Independent t-test (SPSS PASW 18); significance p<0.05

### Follow-up Timeline
1. Surgery → baseline radiograph (immediate)
2. 3 months → prosthetic loading
3. 6 months post-loading → final radiograph
