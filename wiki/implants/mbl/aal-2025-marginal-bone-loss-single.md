## Three-line Summary

RCT (n=80 completely edentulous patients, mandibular midline Tapered Screw-Vent, SIRMO context, 4-arm: submerged/non-submerged × ball/CM-LOC, 2-year follow-up with 55 completers): no statistically significant difference in marginal bone loss between healing protocols (N 0.27±0.39 mm vs S 0.43±0.51 mm, p=0.6) or attachment types (ball 0.28 vs CM-LOC 0.38, p=0.3).

Non-submerged healing numerically favored across all time points; Ball-Non-submerged (BN) had the lowest 2-year MBL (total 0.27±0.39 mm), while Ball-Submerged (BS) had the highest (0.49±0.48 mm, significant as time effect p=0.021), though confounded by older age in the BS group.

Both protocols and both attachment types produced MBL within physiological limits (Albrektsson criteria: <1.5 mm year 1, <0.2 mm/yr thereafter); 6 implant failures during healing (2 N, 4 S).

두 치유 프로토콜 모두 생리적 범위 내 (Albrektsson 기준: 첫해 <1.5 mm, 이후 연 <0.2 mm); 치유 기간 중 임플란트 실패 6개 (비매몰형 2, 매몰형 4).

## Summary

This 4-arm RCT from Cairo University enrolled 80 completely edentulous patients and randomized them first to submerged (S, cover screw) or non-submerged (N, healing abutment) healing, then to ball or CM-LOC (PEKK-matrix) attachment for a mandibular midline single-implant overdenture. After 3 months of osseointegration, attachments were picked up in existing or new dentures; radiographic MBL was measured at baseline, 12, and 24 months. No significant difference was found between healing protocols or attachment types. Non-submerged favored in all comparisons but did not reach significance, and the BS group's higher MBL was partly confounded by significantly older patient age. The paper attributes the NS advantage to "form follows function" — early functional loading during the healing phase promotes denser lamellar bone formation.

## Key Contributions

- First RCT specifically powered to compare S vs N healing protocols within a SIRMO design
- 2-year MBL data for all 4 subgroup combinations (BS/CS/BN/CN)
- Shows both CM-LOC (novel PEKK-based attachment) and ball attachment produce acceptable MBL — adds evidence for CM-LOC as a viable SIRMO attachment
- Identifies age confound in BS group — older age may partly explain higher MBL in submerged-ball group
- 6 implant failures during healing: more in S group (n=4) vs N group (n=2), consistent with non-submerged protective trend

## Methodology

- RCT, 2-stage randomization (SNOSE); Cairo University; SPIRIT-compliant; PACTR20183003085193
- n=80 enrolled; 71 received attachment; 55 at 2 years
- Implant: Tapered Screw-Vent Zimmer Biomet 3.7×10 mm, mandibular midline; ISQ ≥60, torque ≥30 Ncm required
- Healing: S = cover screw 3 months; N = healing abutment 3 months; relieved soft-lined denture during healing
- Radiograph: long-cone paralleling, XCP Rinn bite block, digital periapical; DIGORA software; calibrated to implant length and shoulder-to-first-thread distance (2.5 mm in TSV)
- Statistics: Mann-Whitney U, Kruskal-Wallis, Wilcoxon signed rank; χ²; ICC

## Results

| Comparison | MBL at 2yr (mean ± SD mm) | p |
|---|---|---|
| Non-submerged | 0.27 ± 0.39 | p=0.6 (vs S) |
| Submerged | 0.43 ± 0.51 | — |
| Ball | 0.28 ± 0.44 | p=0.3 (vs CM-LOC) |
| CM-LOC | 0.38 ± 0.46 | — |
| BN (best) | 0.27 ± 0.39 total; 0.01 ± 0.45 yr-2 | — |

Year 1: Ball 0.21±0.38 mm (p=0.018), CM-LOC 0.23±0.28 mm (p<0.001) vs baseline — both significant time effects.
All groups within physiological limits. ICC 0.991–0.999 (excellent reliability).
