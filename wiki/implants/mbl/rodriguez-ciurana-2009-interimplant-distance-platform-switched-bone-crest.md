## Three-line Summary
Prospective radiographic study (n=37 patients, 41 pairs of adjacent implants placed <3 mm apart) evaluating whether platform switching changes the bone-resorption pattern between closely spaced adjacent implants, compared to the pre-platform-switching 3 mm rule established by Tarnow et al. 2000.

Mean vertical bone resorption was 0.62 mm and mean horizontal resorption was 0.60 mm; the interproximal bone peak extended coronally 0.24 mm beyond the imaginary line connecting the two implant-abutment interfaces (IAJ-to-IAJ line). Separately (per Gupta 2019's review of this paper), four abutment/implant diameter pairs — 4.1/4.8, 5.0/5.8, 4.1/5.0, 3.4/4.1 mm — gave a per-side offset of 0.35–0.45 mm, with bone resorption M 0.7±0.57 mm / D 0.55±0.52 mm.

Platform switching appears to preserve a measurable bone peak above the IAJ-to-IAJ line even when adjacent implants are placed <3 mm apart, closer than Tarnow's classic 3 mm guideline — but this single-arm prospective study (no internal non-PS control) does not by itself prove PS "rescues" close spacing; only the abstract was retrieved (no PMC full text, no verified DOI).

## Summary
This 2009 prospective radiographic study by Rodríguez-Ciurana, Tarnow, and colleagues follows directly from Tarnow et al.'s 2000 finding that adjacent implants placed ≤3 mm apart lose more interproximal crestal bone than those placed >3 mm apart. Here the authors ask a distance-specific question about platform-switched (PS) implants: across 41 pairs of adjacent PS implants placed less than 3 mm apart in 37 patients, mean vertical bone resorption was 0.62 mm, mean horizontal resorption was 0.60 mm, and the interproximal bone peak was preserved 0.24 mm coronal to an imaginary line connecting the two implants' abutment junctions (IAJ-to-IAJ line). This is a genuinely different research question from the *offset-magnitude* question (how much narrower a single PS abutment should be relative to its own implant platform) — Gupta et al. 2019's review separately tabulates this paper's diameter-pair offset data (four combinations, 0.35–0.45 mm per-side mismatch), which this page keeps clearly distinguished below. Only the PubMed abstract was retrievable for this paper (no PMC full text, no DOI returned by PubMed metadata for this Quintessence-published journal).

## Key Contributions
- Extends Tarnow et al. 2000's pre-platform-switching 3 mm inter-implant-distance (IID) rule to platform-switched implants specifically, in adjacent pairs placed closer than 3 mm.
- Introduces the IAJ-to-IAJ imaginary line as a distance-specific reference for how much interproximal bone peak survives between two closely spaced implants.
- Distinguishes (via its own data, and as surfaced by Gupta 2019) two separate "distance" questions in the platform-switching literature: inter-implant spacing between adjacent implants vs. abutment/implant diameter offset on a single implant.

## Methodology
Prospective radiographic study. 41 pairs of adjacent implants placed <3 mm apart, in 37 patients. Outcomes: vertical bone resorption, horizontal bone resorption, and interproximal bone-peak height relative to the IAJ-to-IAJ line. (Only the abstract was retrieved; implant system, connection type, and statistical methods are not confirmed beyond what the abstract states.)

## Results

**Interimplant distance (this paper's primary focus — 41 pairs, <3 mm apart, n=37 patients)**

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Mean vertical bone resorption | 0.62 mm |
| Mean horizontal bone resorption | 0.60 mm |
| Bone peak above IAJ-to-IAJ line | 0.24 mm preserved |
| Reference (Tarnow 2000, non-PS, IID ≤3 mm) | 1.04 mm crestal bone loss |
| Reference (Tarnow 2000, non-PS, IID >3 mm) | 0.45 mm crestal bone loss |

**Offset magnitude (a distinct question — abutment/implant diameter mismatch on a single implant, per Gupta 2019's Table 1 summary of this paper)**

| Diameter pairs (implant/abutment, mm) | Per-side offset | Bone resorption |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1/4.8, 5.0/5.8, 4.1/5.0, 3.4/4.1 | 0.35–0.45 mm | M 0.7±0.57 mm, D 0.55±0.52 mm |

Do not conflate the two tables above: the first answers "how close can adjacent PS implants be placed," the second answers "how much narrower should a single PS abutment be."
