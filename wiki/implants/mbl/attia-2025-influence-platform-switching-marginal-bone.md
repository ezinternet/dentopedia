## Three-line Summary

Retrospective clinical study (37 patients with immediate dental implants, 21 platform switching (PS) vs 16 platform matching (PM), follow-up 6 months to 23 years, University Hospital Gießen) comparing marginal bone loss (MBL) and implant success.

Mesial MBL was significantly lower with PS (0.26mm vs 0.75mm, p=0.044); regression confirmed PM associated with significantly greater mesial bone loss (β=2.11, p=0.039); distal MBL and overall implant success (Buser/Albrektsson/Attia scores) showed no significant group difference.

Both configurations remained functional long-term (up to 23 years); PS may offer a mesial-bone-preservation advantage in immediate implants, but small sample size and an uneven smoking-status distribution (PS group only) limit certainty.

## Summary

This retrospective study from University Hospital Gießen and Marburg examines a question the PS literature had largely left untouched: does the platform-switching bone-preservation advantage hold for **immediately** placed implants, not just delayed placement? Across 37 patients (21 PS, 16 PM) followed 6 months to 23 years, mesial bone levels were significantly better preserved with PS, and multiple regression confirmed platform matching as an independent predictor of greater mesial bone loss after adjusting for confounders. Distal-side MBL and composite success scores (Buser, Albrektsson, and the newly proposed Attia score) showed no significant between-group difference. The very long observation window — some implants followed for over two decades — is unusual in this literature and shows both configurations remain functional long-term. A notable confound is that all smokers in the cohort were in the PS group, yet PS still outperformed PM mesially, suggesting the effect is not merely a healthier-patient artifact, though the imbalance still limits certainty.

## Key Contributions

- Extends PS-vs-PM comparison specifically into the **immediate implant placement** context — most existing PS evidence (the SR+MAs and RCTs already in this wiki) is built on delayed placement.
- Exceptionally long follow-up range (6 months–23 years), demonstrating durability of both configurations well beyond the 1–5 year windows typical of PS RCTs.
- Applies the newly developed 18-point **Attia score**, a composite of clinical, radiographic, and patient-reported outcomes, alongside classic Buser/Albrektsson binary criteria.
- Regression-adjusted result (β=2.11, p=0.039 favoring PS on mesial TBL) is more robust than the raw unadjusted group comparison, which did not reach significance.

## Methodology

- Retrospective cohort, immediate implants placed 2000–2023 at UKGM, follow-up exams June 2023–February 2024. n=37 (21 PS / 16 PM), BEGO RI system in 83.8% of cases.
- MBL measured on orthopantomograms (postoperative vs current), mesial + distal.
- Success assessed via Buser (1990), Albrektsson (incl. <0.2mm/year bone loss after year 1), and the novel Attia score (0–18 points combining clinical, radiographic, and PROM measures).
- Multiple linear regression for total bone loss (TBL), adjusting for sex, age, chronic illness, medication, smoking, jaw, implant diameter/length, service time, and superstructure type.

## Results

- Mesial current bone level: PS 2.24 ± 0.74mm vs PM 3.00 ± 1.19mm (p=0.044).
- MLR for mesial TBL (R²=0.521): platform matching β=2.11 (95% CI 0.11–4.11, p=0.039) vs platform switching — PM independently predicts greater mesial bone loss.
- Distal MBL: no significant difference (TBL p=0.774).
- Success: Buser 97.3% overall (p=0.432 between groups); Albrektsson 45.9% overall (p=0.272); Attia score PS 16.24±1.04 vs PM 13.71±6.59 (p=0.794).
- Confound: 42.9% of PS-group patients were smokers vs 0% of PM-group patients (p=0.005); PM implants had longer mean service time (9.69 vs 3.18 years, p<0.001).
- Patient-reported chewing, speech, and aesthetic satisfaction were high and comparable between groups.
