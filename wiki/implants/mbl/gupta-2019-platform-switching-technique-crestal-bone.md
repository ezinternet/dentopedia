## Three-line Summary

Qualitative/narrative systematic review (PubMed + Google Scholar, 2005–2017, no meta-analysis) of platform switching (PLS) and its effect on crestal bone loss around dental implants, synthesizing human clinical studies, immediate-placement case series, PS-vs-non-PS comparisons, and finite element analysis (FEA) reports.

Across the reviewed literature, PLS implants consistently showed less marginal/crestal bone loss than platform-matched implants (e.g., Hürzeler et al.: 0.22mm PLS vs 2.02mm non-PLS; Cappiello et al.: 0.95±0.32mm vs 1.67±0.37mm), attributed mechanistically to inward relocation of the implant-abutment junction (IAJ) shifting the inflammatory cell infiltrate away from crestal bone and increasing the microgap-to-bone distance.

The review concludes PLS is a major contributing factor limiting crestal bone resorption and should be followed when clinical situations permit, but reports no pooled statistic of its own — its evidence base is individually-reported study numbers (mostly Biomet 3i implants, follow-up ≤2 years) rather than a computed summary effect.

## 세줄요약

정성적(qualitative) 체계적 문헌고찰(PubMed + Google Scholar, 2005–2017, 메타분석 없음) — 플랫폼 스위칭(Platform Switching, PLS)이 임플란트 주위 치조정골소실(Crestal Bone Loss, CBL)에 미치는 영향을 인체 임상연구·즉시식립 증례·PLS vs 비-PLS 비교·유한요소분석(Finite Element Analysis, FEA) 문헌으로 종합.

검토된 문헌에서 PLS 임플란트는 일반형(platform-matched) 임플란트보다 일관되게 적은 변연골/치조정골 소실을 보였음 (예: Hürzeler 등 — PLS 0.22mm vs 비-PLS 2.02mm; Cappiello 등 — 0.95±0.32mm vs 1.67±0.37mm); 기전은 임플란트-지대주 접합부(Implant-Abutment Junction, IAJ)의 내측 이동이 염증세포침윤(Inflammatory Cell Infiltrate, ICT)을 치조정골에서 멀어지게 하고 미세간극(microgap)-골 거리를 늘리는 것으로 설명.

리뷰는 PLS가 치조정골 흡수를 제한하는 주요 요인이며 임상 상황이 허락하는 한 따라야 한다고 결론짓지만, 자체 통합(pooled) 통계치는 제시하지 않음 — 근거는 개별 연구가 보고한 수치(대부분 Biomet 3i 임플란트, 추적기간 2년 이하)를 그대로 나열한 서술적 종합에 가까움.

## Summary

Gupta et al. (2019) is a narrative/qualitative systematic review — not a meta-analysis — that searched PubMed and Google Scholar for 2005–2017 literature on platform switching (PLS) and crestal bone preservation. Rather than pooling numbers, the paper's contribution is largely mechanistic and organizational: it explains *why* PLS is thought to preserve crestal bone (inward relocation of the implant-abutment junction shifts the inflammatory cell infiltrate away from bone and increases the biologic width laterally rather than apically), then walks through four clusters of supporting literature — a table of nine human clinical studies, immediate-implant-placement case series, direct PLS-vs-non-PLS comparisons, inter-implant-distance studies, and finite element analysis (FEA) reports on implant-abutment biomechanics. Every quantitative figure in the paper is quoted from an individual source study rather than computed by the review authors, and the review does not report a PRISMA flow diagram, formal quality appraisal, or pooled effect size. Its conclusion is a clinical recommendation ("PLS helps to prevent the bone loss around the implants... this concept can be used in clinical practice") rather than a numeric estimate.

## Key Contributions

- Clear mechanistic account of the PLS-bone-preservation hypothesis: inward IAJ shift → inflammatory cell infiltrate (ICT) moves toward the implant's central axis and away from crestal bone → connective tissue thickens laterally (with increased local blood flow) → ICT stays confined above the implant platform → biologic width does not need to relocate apically → no compensatory crestal bone remodeling/loss.
- Table 1 tabulates nine human studies (evidence base anchored to ~2009) comparing PLS vs control bone resorption, implant system, and abutment/implant diameter ratio (Canullo; Calvo-Guirado ×3; Prosper et al.; Rodríguez-Ciurana; Cappiello; Canullo & Rasperini; Hürzeler).
- Separately synthesizes three additional literature clusters: (1) immediate implant placement + PLS, (2) direct PLS-vs-non-PLS comparisons, and (3) PLS effect on inter-implant distance and interproximal bone-peak preservation.
- Reviews five finite element analysis (FEA) reports contrasting butt-joint (e.g., Replace Select, 3i) vs taper-joint (e.g., Straumann, Astra, Ankylos) implant-abutment designs under vertical/diagonal loading (10–250N, 15°–30°).
- Proposes combining Morse taper connections (narrower microgap) with PLS (greater microgap-to-bone distance) as a complementary strategy for crestal bone preservation.

## Methodology

- Narrative/qualitative systematic review: PubMed + Google Scholar search, English-language literature 2005–2017.
- Keywords: "crestal bone preservation," "crestal bone loss," "PLS," "small diameter abutments," "immediate implant placement and PLS," "loading in implants."
- Exclusion criteria: unpublished papers, letters to the editor, historical reviews, non-English case reports, studies with no results, abstract-only studies.
- No PRISMA flow diagram, no risk-of-bias/quality appraisal tool, and no formal meta-analysis reported — quantitative results are quoted directly from each source study rather than pooled by the review authors. A "statistical analysis" statement (SPSS 20, unpaired Student's t-test, ANOVA) in Methods appears to describe how the underlying *included* studies analyzed their own data, since no such pooled statistic appears in Results.

## Results

- **Mechanism**: cites Ericsson et al. for the ~1mm healthy connective tissue zone encircling bone, and Lazzara & Porter for the hypothesis that inward IAJ shift moves the inflammatory infiltrate away from crestal bone; cites Jansen et al. for the ICT extending ~0.5–0.75mm coronal and ~0.5–0.75mm apical to the IAJ, always separated from bone by ~1mm of connective tissue.
- **Human-study table**: 6 of 9 reports used Biomet 3i (PLS-originating) implants; longest follow-up ~2 years; most reports concluded PLS positively preserves crestal bone. E.g., Canullo — PLS 0.3±0.157mm vs control 1.19±0.138mm; Prosper et al. — 98.3% of PLS implants had no crestal bone loss at 12 months vs 66.1% control (p<0.001).
- **Immediate placement**: Canullo — PLS 0.3±0.16mm vs non-PLS 1.19±0.35mm (p<0.005) at ~25 months. Canullo & Rasperini — 0.78±0.36mm vs a 1.7mm reference value over 18–36 months. Baumgarten — PLS helps prevent anticipated bone loss given ≥~3mm soft-tissue depth.
- **PLS vs non-PLS**: Hürzeler et al. — 0.22mm (PLS) vs 2.02mm (non-PLS) crestal bone loss; ≥0.45mm abutment reduction per side deemed sufficient to avoid peri-implant bone loss. Cappiello et al. — 0.95±0.32mm (PLS) vs 1.67±0.37mm (non-PLS). Salamanca et al. — PLS showed greater vertical (0.93±1mm, p<0.05) and horizontal (0.50±0.56mm) bone gain vs platform-matching (0.23±0.58mm / 0.22±0.53mm).
- **Inter-implant distance**: Rodríguez-Ciurana — 41 pairs of PLS implants <3mm apart: mean vertical resorption 0.62mm, horizontal 0.60mm, interproximal bone peak extending 0.24mm coronal to the IAJ-to-IAJ line — PLS helped retain the interproximal bone peak vs conventional restorations.
- **FEA**: five reports contrasted butt-joint vs taper-joint designs under vertical/diagonal loading, framed as biomechanical support for the PLS stress-distribution rationale rather than new bone-loss quantification.
- **Conclusion (narrative, not a pooled estimate)**: PLS is confirmed as a major contributing factor limiting crestal bone resorption; the concept should be followed in clinical practice when clinical situations permit.

**Caveat**: because this is a qualitative SR, none of the figures above have been pooled or weighted by the review authors — they are individual-study numbers from heterogeneous implant systems, diameter ratios, and follow-up durations, quoted as reported.

## Glossary

- **Platform Switching (PLS)**: use of a smaller-diameter abutment on a larger-diameter implant platform/collar, shifting the implant-abutment junction (IAJ) inward toward the implant's central axis.
- **Implant-Abutment Junction (IAJ)**: the interface/microgap between the implant platform and the abutment; its horizontal and vertical position relative to crestal bone is central to the PLS mechanism.
- **Inflammatory Cell Infiltrate (ICT)**: the zone of inflammatory cells that forms around the IAJ microgap due to bacterial colonization; PLS is hypothesized to relocate it away from crestal bone.
- **Biologic width**: the dimension of soft tissue (junctional epithelium + connective tissue) that re-establishes itself around an implant/tooth; crestal bone remodels to accommodate it if the ICT encroaches too close to bone.
- **Finite Element Analysis (FEA)**: computational biomechanical modeling method used to study stress distribution in implant-abutment systems under loading.
