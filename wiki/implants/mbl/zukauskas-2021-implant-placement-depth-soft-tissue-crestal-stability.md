## Three-line Summary

Case-control clinical trial (66 patients, two-piece internal-hex implants) comparing early crestal bone changes around platform-switched implants placed subcrestally in thin soft tissue with matching-connection implants placed supracrestally in thick soft tissue.

At 2 months mean bone loss was 0.2 mm (SD 0.22 mm; range 0.1–1.2 mm) in controls versus −0.69 mm (SD 0.65 mm; range 0–2.6 mm) in the test group (P < .05); at 1 year the values were 0.28 mm (SD 0.36 mm) versus −0.6 mm (SD 0.55 mm).

Platform-switched subcrestal implants in thin tissue showed significantly more bone loss than non-switched supracrestal implants in thick tissue — but connection geometry, placement depth, and soft-tissue thickness all differed between groups, so the effect cannot be attributed to any single variable.

## Summary

This case-control clinical trial followed 66 patients receiving two-piece internal-hex implants to compare early crestal bone stability under two multi-factorial treatment packages. The control group (n=33) received implants with a horizontally matching implant–abutment connection placed approximately 0.5–1 mm supracrestally in thick soft tissue; the test group (n=33) received platform-switched implants placed approximately 1.5 mm subcrestally in vertically thin soft tissue. Clinical examination and intraoral radiography showed a mean bone loss at 2 months of 0.2 mm (SD 0.22 mm) in controls versus −0.69 mm (SD 0.65 mm) in the test group, a statistically significant difference (P < .05); at 1 year the corresponding means were 0.28 mm (SD 0.36 mm) and −0.6 mm (SD 0.55 mm). The authors conclude that platform-switched subcrestal implants in thin tissue lost significantly more bone than non-switched supracrestal implants in thick tissue. Because connection geometry, vertical position, and mucosal thickness all changed together, the study is best read as a boundary-condition warning rather than as evidence against platform switching in isolation.

## Key Contributions

- Reports 2-month and 1-year crestal bone values for the understudied combination of platform switching + subcrestal placement + thin soft tissue.
- Provides a clinical counterpoint to the wiki's generally positive platform-switching (PS) bone-preservation literature — in this trial the PS arm had the larger reported bone loss.
- Reinforces the emerging view that placement depth and soft-tissue thickness modify crestal remodeling outcomes, but does so with a design that confounds all three variables, so it cannot rank their individual contributions.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Case-control comparative clinical trial (per the abstract's own label).
- **Population**: 66 patients; control n=33, test n=33; two-piece internal-hex dental implants throughout.
- **Control arm**: horizontally matching implant–abutment connection; ~0.5–1 mm supracrestal placement; thick soft tissue.
- **Test arm**: platform-switched connection; ~1.5 mm subcrestal placement; vertically thin soft tissue.
- **Measurements**: clinical examinations and intraoral radiographs; crestal bone loss reported at 2 months and 1 year; statistical analysis with P < .05 as significance threshold.
