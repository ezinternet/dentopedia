## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (J Interdiscip Dent 2012;2(2):84–91, single author) applying the natural-tooth biologic width concept (Gargiulo 2.04 mm = supraalveolar connective tissue + junctional epithelium) to implant dentistry, covering structure, function, crestal bone-loss mechanisms, and the platform-switching rationale.

Peri-implant biologic width is larger than tooth biologic width (two-piece > one-piece > teeth): 4–4.5 mm around one-piece mini implants (Glauser); 6.17 mm mesial / 3.63 mm midfacial / 5.93 mm distal by bone sounding on anterior implants (Kan); epithelium around two-piece implants always lies apical to the microgap.

The implant–abutment microgap is the dominant driver of early crestal bone loss (bone resorbs ~2 mm below the microgap, Hermann); other factors — biologic seal formation (~6 weeks), abutment inflammatory cell infiltrate (1.5–2 mm horizontal+vertical resorption, Ericsson: crest consistently 1.0–1.5 mm apical to the implant–abutment junction), occlusal overload (progressive), and crest module design — are reviewed; platform switching repositions the biologic width horizontally (min 3 mm soft tissue for seal) confining the ICT within <90°.

## 세줄요약

치아 생물학적 폭경(Gargiulo 2.04 mm, 결합조직+접합상피) 개념을 임플란트로 확장한 내러티브 리뷰 — 구조·기능·변연골소실 기전·플랫폼 스위칭 원리를 한 권에 정리.

임플란트 주위 폭경은 치아보다 큼(2-piece > 1-piece > 치아): 1-piece mini 4–4.5 mm(Glauser), 전치부 bone sounding 6.17/3.63/5.93 mm(근심/협측/원심, Kan), 2-piece 상피는 항상 미세극 하방에 위치.

미세극(implant–abutment microgap)이 초기 변연골소실의 주동인(미세극 하방 약 2 mm 골흡수, Hermann); 기타 인자: 생물학적 봉쇄 형성(약 6주), 지대주 염증세포침윤(ICT, 1.5–2 mm 흡수·Ericsson: 골정은 IAJ 하방 1.0–1.5 mm), 교합과부하(진행성), crest module 설계(연마칼라+직선=전단 / 거친면+경사=압축); 플랫폼 스위칭은 폭경을 수평 재배치(연조직 최소 3 mm)해 ICT를 <90° 영역에 국한.

## Overview

The biologic width concept — historically derived from Gargiulo et al.'s cadaver measurements (2.04 mm average dimension comprising supraalveolar connective tissue + junctional epithelial attachment) — is hypothesized to apply around titanium implants, where its violation may explain early crestal bone loss. This review synthesizes the peri-implant soft-tissue biology, the factors driving crestal bone remodeling, and the mechanical rationale for platform switching.

## Key Findings

- **Peri-implant biologic width exists around all implant shapes** after the uncovery stage; its stability depends chiefly on implant type (one-piece vs two-piece) and crestal bone.
- **Dimensions**: Glauser et al. — one-piece mini implants 4–4.5 mm; Kan et al. — bone sounding on anterior implants 6.17 mm mesial, 3.63 mm midfacial, 5.93 mm distal. Biologic width order: **two-piece > one-piece > natural teeth**; epithelium around two-piece implants always located apical to the microgap.
- **Microgap (implant–abutment junction)**: for two-stage implants, crestal bone levels depend on microgap location ~2 mm below it; Hermann et al. first demonstrated a direct effect of the microgap on crestal bone loss independent of surgical approach (epithelial proliferation to establish biologic width → ~2 mm bone loss below the microgap).
- **Crestal bone-loss factors**: (1) biologic seal formation within ~6 weeks of implant–abutment junction exposure (bone loss is in part a process of establishing the seal); (2) surgical trauma — unlikely; (3) microgap — dominant; (4) occlusal overload — progressive, not limited to year one; (5) crest module design — polished collar + straight module transmits shear, rough surface + angled collar transmits compressive force.
- **ICT concept (Ericsson et al.)**: plaque-associated (PaICT) and implant-associated (IaICT) inflammatory cell infiltrates; peri-implant bone crest consistently 1.0–1.5 mm apical to the implant–abutment junction, ICT always separated from bone crest by ~1.0 mm of healthy connective tissue; IaICT identified as the etiological factor for crestal bone loss. Microgap microleakage (disaccharides, short peptides) feeds the abutment ICT → 1.5–2 mm horizontal+vertical resorption (saucerization).
- **Stability**: connective tissue is more stable than the epithelial dimension (collagen cross-linking); biologic width formation is a physiologic response independent of loading status or loading time.
- **Platform switching**: horizontal repositioning of the biologic width via an undersized prosthetic component; minimum 3 mm soft-tissue thickness required for biologic seal; Berglundh — 1-mm healthy connective tissue zone separates crestal bone from ICT; Wennström 5-year study — MBL 0.06 mm after first year of load; ICT confined to a <90° area away from crestal bone.
- **Neck macrodesign**: microthreads convert shear into compression, reducing resorption while biologic width forms.

## Clinical Implications

1. Predict ~2 mm crestal bone remodeling below the microgap for two-piece implants — plan vertical placement accordingly; countersinking below crest minimizes interface movement and exposure risk.
2. One-piece/non-submerged protocols produce less remodeling (no microgap, no second surgery) — preferable when biologic-width stability is the priority.
3. Keep soft-tissue thickness ≥3 mm for a stable peri-implant seal; platform switching reduces ICT effects on crestal bone and is most beneficial where biologic width space is limited.
4. Treat biologic-width formation as a physiologic, load-independent process — avoid attributing early bone loss solely to loading.
