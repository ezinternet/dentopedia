## Three-line Summary

Camlog Foundation International Expert Meeting (Rome, Jan 2013) consensus report synthesizing two systematic reviews — on implant-abutment connection type / machined-collar-microgap positioning, and on platform switching (PS) — into formal expert consensus statements and clinical recommendations for crestal bone level changes.

Supracrestal machined-collar positioning showed less bone loss than subcrestal; a subcrestal microgap position (≥1mm) caused more remodeling but better preserved bony coverage of the rough surface; connection type (internal/external, conical/butt-joint) impact "lacks documentation"; despite high study heterogeneity, evidence trends favor PS over platform matching (PM) to prevent/minimize peri-implant marginal bone loss, with no negative PS effects published to date.

Establishes the 2013 expert-consensus baseline for the PS literature that later quantitative systematic review + meta-analyses (SR+MA) and multi-year randomized controlled trials (RCT) go on to confirm with effect-size numbers.

## 세줄요약

Camlog Foundation 국제 전문가 회의(로마, 2013년 1월) 컨센서스 리포트 — 임플란트-지대주 연결부(Implant-Abutment Connection) 종류·기계가공 칼라/미세간극(Machined Collar/Microgap) 위치를 다룬 문헌고찰과, 플랫폼 스위칭(Platform Switching, PS)을 다룬 문헌고찰을 종합해 공식 합의문·임상권고안 도출.

매끈한 칼라의 치조정 상방(supracrestal) 위치가 하방(subcrestal)보다 골소실 적음; 미세간극 자체는 1mm 이상 치조정 하방 위치 시 골개조는 크지만 거친 표면부 골피개(bony coverage) 보존은 더 우수; 연결부 종류(내부형/외부형, 원추형/맞댐형) 영향은 "문서화 부족"; 연구 간 이질성(heterogeneity)이 높음에도 근거 경향은 PS가 일반형(Platform Matching, PM) 대비 임플란트 주위 변연골 소실 예방·최소화에 유리하며, 부정적 효과는 보고된 바 없음.

이후 정량적 체계적문헌고찰+메타분석(SR+MA)·다년 무작위대조시험(RCT)들이 수치로 재확인하게 될 2013년 시점 PS 문헌의 전문가 합의 기준선을 확립.

## Summary

This is the Camlog Foundation's 2013 International Expert Meeting consensus report (Rome, 18–19 January 2013), synthesizing two systematic reviews that were pre-circulated to and presented before an international panel of implant clinicians and researchers, who split into two working groups to produce formal consensus statements and clinical recommendations. Group 1 (chaired by Gil Alcoforado, rapporteur Frank Schwarz) addressed implant-abutment connection type and machined-collar/microgap positioning, based on the Schwarz, Hegewald & Becker systematic review. Group 2 (chaired by Thomas Taylor, rapporteur Frank Peter Strietzel) addressed platform switching, based on the Strietzel, Neumann & Hertel systematic review. As a formal practice-guideline-level consensus rather than a primary study, this report does not itself report pooled effect sizes, but its central conclusion — "a trend favoring the platform switching concept to prevent or minimize peri-implant marginal bone loss" — became a foundational reference point that later quantitative SR+MAs and RCTs went on to confirm and sharpen with numeric effect estimates.

## Key Contributions

- Formal, panel-approved consensus statements (not just review conclusions) spanning three related but distinct questions: implant-abutment connection design, machined-collar/microgap position, and platform switching.
- Establishes the frequently cited framing that evidence shows "a trend favoring the platform switching concept," explicitly hedged against high inter-study heterogeneity — a nuance often lost when later work cites PS benefit without heterogeneity caveats.
- Draws a non-obvious distinction between the *machined collar* (smooth neck) and the *microgap* (implant-abutment junction) as separate positioning variables with opposite-direction recommendations: supracrestal collar placement reduces bone loss, while subcrestal microgap placement — despite more remodeling — better preserves bony coverage of the rough surface.
- States plainly that implant-abutment connection type (internal vs external, conical vs butt-joint) "lacks documentation" for crestal bone impact — a null finding that sets a boundary on what the PS/microgap literature can and cannot yet claim.
- Flags still-open questions in 2013: long-term effect of PS on soft-tissue health and on implant-abutment biomechanical stability, plus the limits of two-dimensional radiographic bone measurement used across the underlying primary studies.

## Methodology

Structured consensus-development process: two independently authored systematic reviews were circulated in advance and presented in full (methodology, results, conclusions) to appointed international experts, who then divided into two topic-specific working groups, each led by a chairperson and secretary. Draft consensus statements and clinical recommendations from each group were presented in plenary session, revised per audience discussion, and formally approved. Each topic area's output follows a fixed structure: focused question → major findings/conclusions → consensus statements → clinical recommendations → implications for future research.

## Results

**Implant-abutment connection & machined collar/microgap:**
- Supracrestal machined-collar positioning (one- and two-piece implants) associated with less crestal bone change than subcrestal positioning.
- Subcrestal microgap positioning (≥1mm, two-piece implants) may be favored over epi-/supracrestal for retaining bony coverage of the rough surface, despite more pronounced remodeling.
- Clinical recommendation: avoid subcrestal positioning of smooth implant parts; smooth-rough border should ideally coincide with adjacent alveolar bone level; subcrestal shoulder placement should be limited to entirely roughened two-part implants.
- Implant-abutment connection type impact "lacks documentation" — no robust conclusions possible; bone remodeling observed with either internal/external or conical/butt-joint designs; microbial leakage can occur regardless of connection design.

**Platform switching:**
- High heterogeneity among included studies — results interpreted cautiously.
- Within available RCTs and prospective cohorts, tendency favors PS over PM to prevent/minimize peri-implant marginal bone loss.
- Human clinical outcomes have not as clearly shown the benefit seen in animal histological studies, though the trend is generally positive; no negative PS effects published to date.
- Clinical recommendation: evidence does not yet allow clear-cut indications for PS; PS restorations may be used wherever PM restorations would be, at clinician discretion.
- Open questions: long-term soft-tissue and biomechanical-stability effects of PS; reliance on two-dimensional radiographic measurement in underlying studies.

## Glossary

- **Platform Switching (PS)**: use of a prosthetic abutment narrower than the implant platform, shifting the implant-abutment microgap inward and away from crestal bone.
- **Machined Collar**: the smooth (non-roughened) portion of the implant neck, distinct from the roughened implant body surface.
- **Microgap**: the implant-abutment interface/junction, a potential site of micromovement and microbial leakage implicated in crestal bone remodeling.
- **Subcrestal / Epicrestal / Supracrestal positioning**: placement of the implant neck or microgap below, level with, or above the alveolar crest, respectively.
- **Crestal Bone Level Change**: radiographically measured vertical change in marginal/crestal bone height around an implant over time.
- **Implant-Abutment Connection**: the mechanical interface type joining implant and abutment (e.g., internal vs external, conical vs butt-joint).
