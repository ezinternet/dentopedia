## Three-line Summary

Comparative clinical study (abstract-only) of 80 patients receiving 80 platform-switched 4.1 mm bone-level Straumann implants, allocated by initial mucosal thickness to thin (≤2 mm, n=40) or thick (>2 mm, n=40), with radiographic follow-up to 1 year.

Thin-mucosa implants lost 0.79 mm of crestal bone at 2 months and 1.17 mm at 1 year, versus 0.17 mm and 0.21 mm in thick mucosa; between-group differences were significant (p<.001) at both time points.

Platform switching does not prevent crestal bone loss when mucosal tissue is thin at implant placement — its bone-preserving effect is conditional on thick (≥2 mm) soft tissue.

얇은 점막군은 2개월 0.79 mm, 1년 1.17 mm의 치조정골 소실을 보인 반면 두꺼운 점막군은 0.17 mm, 0.21 mm였고, 두 시점 모두 군 간 차이는 유의(p<0.001).

## Summary

This comparative clinical study (Clin Implant Dent Relat Res 2015;17(6):1228–1236; first published online 2014) tested whether the crestal-bone advantage of platform switching depends on the soft tissue present at placement — a modifier earlier PS studies had not addressed. Eighty patients each received one 4.1 mm bone-level platform-switched Straumann implant, allocated to thin (≤2 mm, n=40) or thick (>2 mm, n=40) mucosa, and were followed radiographically to 1 year. Thin-mucosa sites lost 0.79 mm at 2 months and 1.17 mm at 1 year, versus 0.17 mm and 0.21 mm in thick mucosa (p<.001 at both time points). The authors conclude that platform switching does not rescue crestal bone in thin mucosa; its bone-preserving effect requires thick soft tissue. The retrieved PDF is an abstract-only publisher landing page, so all numbers here are from the abstract and no subgroup or measurement details are available.

## Key Contributions

- Identifies initial vertical soft-tissue thickness as a **precondition** for platform switching's crestal-bone benefit — previously treated as an unconditional property of the implant–abutment offset ([[implants/mbl/gupta-2019-platform-switching-crestal-bone-loss-sr]], [[overviews/platform-switching-marginal-bone-preservation-synthesis]]).
- Quantifies the split at a 2 mm mucosal thickness cut-off: 1-year crestal bone loss 1.17 mm (thin) vs 0.21 mm (thick), a ~5.6-fold difference.
- Shows the divergence is already present at 2 months (0.79 vs 0.17 mm) — an early-healing/remodeling phenomenon, not a late-loading one.
- Supplies the primary evidence behind the "≥2 mm mucosa" inclusion criterion already used by later trials in this wiki ([[implants/mbl/verma-2024-comparison-bone-loss-submerged-nonsubmerged]]).

## Methodology

- **Design**: Comparative clinical study with prospective radiographic follow-up; allocation by measured mucosal thickness, not stated as randomized.
- **Population**: 80 patients (38 male, 42 female; mean age 44 ± 3.34 years), 80 implants (one per patient).
- **Groups**: Thin mucosa ≤2 mm (n=40) vs thick mucosa >2 mm (n=40).
- **Intervention**: One-stage placement of 4.1 mm diameter bone-level platform-switched implants (Institut Straumann AG, Basel, Switzerland); screw-retained restorations.
- **Radiographic time points**: After implant placement, 2 months after healing, after restoration, and 1-year post-reconstruction.
- **Statistics**: Mann–Whitney U-test, significance p ≤ .05.
- **Source limitation**: Abstract-only PDF — full Methods/Results body not retrieved.

## Results

| Time point | Thin mucosa (≤2 mm, n=40) | Thick mucosa (>2 mm, n=40) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 months after healing | 0.79 mm crestal bone loss | 0.17 mm crestal bone loss | < .001 |
| 1-year post-reconstruction | 1.17 mm crestal bone loss | 0.21 mm crestal bone loss | < .001 |

- The 0.96 mm absolute 1-year gap favors thick mucosa; thick-mucosa sites stayed near baseline ("minimal remodeling").
- The between-group difference is significant at both measured time points and does not attenuate over the first year.
- No covariate adjustment or continuous thickness–bone-loss model is reported in the available abstract.
