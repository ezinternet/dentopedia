## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of RCTs (7 RCTs; 283 implants) comparing convex/wide vs concave/flat abutments on early peri-implant MBL and the distinct outcome of crestal bone remodeling (CBR, bone above the implant platform).

Abutment shape alone did not significantly differ in overall early MBL (MD 0.083 mm; p = 0.082); emergence angle (EA) >30° subgroup showed a significant MBL increase (MD 0.190 mm; p < 0.001); convex/wide abutments produced significantly more CBR than concave/flat (MD 0.560 mm; p = 0.001).

EA >30° is the key MBL threshold; convex abutments independently drive more crestal bone remodeling even without exceeding that angle — both factors should be managed to minimize peri-implant bone changes.

## Summary

This SR+MA pooled 7 RCTs (283 implants) to assess whether abutment shape (convex/wide vs concave/flat) independently affects early peri-implant MBL and the novel outcome of crestal bone remodeling (CBR — bone change above the implant platform). The key result is a dissociation: overall MBL is not significantly affected by shape alone, but EA >30° (a subgroup) drives significant MBL, and convex abutments independently drive more CBR regardless of EA. This means the emergence angle threshold (30°) and the convex profile are two separate but additive risk factors for early peri-implant bone changes.

## Key Contributions

- RCT-only MA: highest-level evidence for abutment shape vs early MBL
- Introduces CBR (above-platform bone) as a distinct outcome from MBL
- EA >30° confirmed as significant MBL predictor (MD 0.190 mm; p < 0.001)
- Convex abutment → more CBR (MD 0.560 mm; p = 0.001), independent of MBL
- Two independent risk factors (EA and shape) must be managed separately

## Methodology

- Design: SR+MA of RCTs; PRISMA 2020
- Search: Electronic databases; up to April 2024
- Inclusion: RCTs; bone-level implants; convex/wide vs concave/flat abutment comparison; MBL/CBR outcomes
- n: 7 RCTs; 283 implants
- Outcomes: MBL (from implant platform to initial BIC), CBR (from implant platform upward)
- Statistics: Random-effects model; subgroup analysis by EA
