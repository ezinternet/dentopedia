## Three-line Summary

Multicenter crossover RCT (51 patients, 102 platform-switched conical-connection implants placed 1 mm vs 2 mm subcrestally; definitive abutments immediately connected; radiographic follow-up at placement, prosthetic delivery, and 1 year post-loading).

Mean peri-implant bone level (PBL) reduction: 0.49 ± 0.32 mm (2 mm subcrestal) vs 0.46 ± 0.35 mm (1 mm subcrestal; p = 0.66); history of periodontitis independently associated with greater bone loss; 2 mm subcrestal: 0% implant surface exposure at 1 year vs 11.8% for 1 mm group.

No significant MBL difference between depths; however, 2 mm subcrestal placement preserves implant surface bone coverage, potentially reducing peri-implant disease risk long-term.

## Summary

This multicenter crossover RCT assigned 51 patients to receive two implants each — one placed 1 mm subcrestally and one placed 2 mm subcrestally — using platform-switched conical-connection implants (bone-level design) with immediate definitive abutment connection in both arms. After a 4-month unsubmerged healing period and screw-retained metal-ceramic crown delivery, radiographic monitoring continued through 1 year post-loading. The primary outcome was peri-implant bone level (PBL) change; multivariate regression identified patient- and site-level predictors. No statistically significant difference in MBL was found between placement depths (p = 0.66), but the 2 mm group showed zero implant surface exposure at 1 year compared to 11.8% in the 1 mm group. History of periodontitis emerged as the only independent risk factor for PBL reduction.

## Key Contributions
- First multicenter crossover RCT comparing 1 mm vs 2 mm subcrestal depth in PS + conical-connection implants
- No significant MBL difference between depths over 1-year follow-up
- 2 mm subcrestal preserves full implant surface bone coverage at 1 year (0% exposure)
- 11.8% of 1 mm subcrestal implants showed exposure of implant surface below alveolar crest
- Periodontitis history: only significant independent predictor of greater PBL reduction
- Immediate definitive abutment connection (OAOT) applied in both groups

## Methodology
- Design: multicenter crossover RCT; 4 Italian centers; each patient serves as their own control
- Population: partially edentulous adults needing 2 implants in posterior maxilla or mandible
- Implants: bone-level PS conical-connection; both groups received definitive abutment at placement
- Loading: unsubmerged healing, 4 months; screw-retained metal-ceramic crowns
- Radiographic outcomes: PBL at T0 (surgery), T1 (prosthesis delivery), T2 (1 year post-loading)
- Analysis: paired t-test for PBL change; multivariate linear regression for predictors (periodontitis history, smoking, bone density, jaw location, implant site)

## Results

| Parameter | 2 mm Subcrestal | 1 mm Subcrestal | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| PBL reduction T0→T2 (mm) | 0.49 ± 0.32 | 0.46 ± 0.35 | 0.66 (NS) |
| Implant surface exposure at T2 | 0% (0/51) | 11.8% (6/51) | — |
| Implant survival | 100% | 100% | — |
| Periodontitis as predictor | Significant (β > 0) | Significant (β > 0) | <0.05 |

- No implant failures in either group
- Both groups showed PBL stability from T1 to T2 (most bone remodeling occurred during T0→T1 healing phase)
- Multivariate regression: smoking, bone density, jaw, and implant site not significant
