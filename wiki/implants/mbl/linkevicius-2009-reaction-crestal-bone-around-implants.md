## Three-line Summary

Prospective clinical study (26 patients, 64 implants) comparing 1-year crestal bone loss around implants placed about 2 mm supracrestally (test, n=32) or level with the bone (control, n=32), across three mucosal thickness groups.

Test implants lost 1.35 mm +/- 0.33 SD (thin, up to 2 mm), 0.32 mm +/- 0.44 SD (medium), and 0.12 mm +/- 0.16 SD (thick, 3.1 mm and more), with a significant thickness effect (F[2,29]=37.3; P=.000); control implants lost 1.8 mm +/- 0.52 SD, 1.62 mm +/- 0.63 SD, and 1.55 mm +/- 0.47 SD with no thickness effect (F[2,29]=0.73; P=.503).

Initial mucosal thickness changed crestal bone loss only for supracrestally placed implants, not for implants at bone level — but the abstract gives no exact medium-band boundaries or radiographic method.

## Summary

This 1-year prospective clinical study evaluated 64 implants in 26 patients to determine which mucosal tissue thickness measured at the crest qualifies as thin, medium, or thick, and how that thickness affects crestal bone loss. Thirty-two test implants were placed about 2 mm supracrestally and 32 control implants were positioned equal to the bone level; mucosa at placement was divided into thin (up to 2 mm), medium, and thick (3.1 mm and more) groups, and crestal bone changes were measured at placement and after 1 year. Around supracrestally placed test implants, mean bone loss fell steeply with thickness — 1.35 mm +/- 0.33 SD in thin, 0.32 mm +/- 0.44 SD in medium, and 0.12 mm +/- 0.16 SD in thick tissue — and ANOVA across the three groups was significant (F[2,29]=37.3; P=.000). Around bone-level control implants, bone loss was 1.8 mm +/- 0.52 SD, 1.62 mm +/- 0.63 SD, and 1.55 mm +/- 0.47 SD and did not vary by thickness (F[2,29]=0.73; P=.503). The authors conclude that initial tissue thickness can influence crestal bone changes around implants; the available PubMed abstract alone is the basis for every value reported here.

## Key Contributions

- Classifies mucosal tissue at implant placement into thin (up to 2 mm), medium, and thick (3.1 mm and more) bands and tests their effect on crestal bone loss.
- Crosses soft-tissue thickness with implant placement depth (about 2 mm supracrestal vs equal to bone level) in the same cohort — an early two-factor clinical test of the crestal remodeling question.
- Reports a ~11-fold gradient in 1-year bone loss across thickness bands for supracrestal implants (1.35 mm vs 0.12 mm), absent in bone-level controls.
- Supplies the early clinical anchor for the same group's later platform-switching thickness work and soft-tissue-thickening trials.

## Methodology

- Design: 1-year prospective clinical study; no randomization stated in the retrieved abstract.
- Population: 26 patients, 64 implants evaluated.
- Placement groups: test n=32 at about 2 mm supracrestal; control n=32 equal to the bone level.
- Tissue groups at placement: thin (up to 2 mm), medium (bounds not given), thick (3.1 mm and more).
- Measurements: crestal bone changes at implant placement and after 1-year follow-up.
- Statistics: ANOVA across the three thickness bands, computed separately for test and control implants.
