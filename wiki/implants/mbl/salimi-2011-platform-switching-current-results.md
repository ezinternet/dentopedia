## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (PubMed 1990–2011) synthesising clinical and finite-element evidence for platform switching (PLS), where a narrower abutment is placed on a wider implant platform to displace the implant-abutment junction inflammatory zone away from crestal bone.

PLS reduces marginal bone loss to 0.05–1.4 mm vs ~1.67 mm in platform-matched controls, attributed to coronal shift of biologic width and stress redistribution from the bone-implant interface to the implant screw centre.

PLS is recommended in esthetic zones, narrow ridges (<3 mm), limited bone height, and short implants; immediate-loading PLS evidence was inconclusive at the time of writing.

## 세줄요약

PLS는 변연골 소실(MBL)을 0.05–1.4 mm로 제한하며(대조군 ~1.67 mm), 이는 임플란트-지대주 접합부(IAJ) 염증 침윤의 치관 방향 이동 및 응력이 임플란트 나사 중심부로 재분산됨으로 설명된다.

## Summary

This 2011 narrative review from Isfahan University of Medical Sciences (Iran) compiles clinical, radiographic, and FEA evidence published between 1990 and 2011 supporting platform switching (PLS) as a strategy to preserve peri-implant crestal bone.

**Biologic mechanism:** The internal shift of the implant-abutment junction (IAJ) displaces the biologic width and its accompanying inflammatory connective tissue (ICT) zone coronally and horizontally away from the alveolar crest. This reduces the bone resorption stimulus at the crestal bone level.

**Biomechanical mechanism:** FEA studies demonstrate that PLS shifts peak stress concentration from the bone-implant interface toward the centre of the implant-abutment screw complex, reducing bending loads on crestal bone.

**Clinical outcomes compiled:**
- PLS crestal bone loss: 0.05–1.4 mm across multiple studies
- Platform-matched (PM) control bone loss: ~1.67 mm
- Follow-up range across cited studies: 4–169 months

**Indications identified:**
- Short implants in limited-height bone
- Narrow ridges (<3 mm buccal-lingual width)
- Esthetic zone implants requiring soft-tissue volume preservation
- Anatomically constrained sites where standard abutments risk bone exposure

**Controversial areas (2011):**
- Immediate loading combined with PLS: evidence insufficient to recommend routinely
- Animal studies: generally supportive but some variation in results

## Key Contributions

1. Early synthesis establishing the dual (biologic + biomechanical) rationale framework for PLS that subsequent RCTs and SR+MA would test quantitatively.
2. Provides the first compiled crestal bone loss range (0.05–1.4 mm) from the heterogeneous early clinical literature.
3. Identifies narrow ridges and esthetic zones as the highest-benefit PLS indications.
4. Notes the conceptual parallel between PLS and the biological width re-establishment seen after crestal bone remodelling in conventional implants.

## Methodology

- Study type: Narrative review
- Search: PubMed electronic database, 1990–2011
- Included study types: RCTs, prospective/retrospective clinical studies, case series, FEA computational studies
- No formal quality appraisal, no meta-analysis, no PRISMA flow

## Results

| Outcome | PLS | PM control |
|---|---|---|
| Crestal bone loss (range) | 0.05–1.4 mm | ~1.67 mm |
| Biologic width location | Coronally displaced | At crest |
| FEA peak stress | Screw centre | Bone-implant interface |
