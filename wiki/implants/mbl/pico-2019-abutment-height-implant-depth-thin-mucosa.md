## Three-line Summary

One-year randomized clinical trial (33 patients, 66 platform-switched implants in thin-mucosa sites) comparing 3-mm abutments placed 2 mm subcrestally with 1-mm abutments placed equicrestally.

Interproximal peri-implant bone loss at 3/6/12 months was 0.06 ± 0.21 / 0.07 ± 0.22 / 0.12 ± 0.33 mm in the 3-mm group versus 0.76 ± 0.79 / 0.92 ± 0.88 / 0.95 ± 0.88 mm in the 1-mm group, with significant differences at every time point.

Tall abutments combined with subcrestal placement preserved interproximal bone in thin mucosa, but height and insertion depth were changed together so the effect cannot be attributed to height alone (abstract-only).

## Summary

This 1-year randomized clinical trial from the University of Santiago de Compostela tested whether abutment height and implant insertion depth influence interproximal peri-implant bone loss (IPBL) in patients with thin peri-implant mucosa. Thirty-three patients received two platform-switched implants each (66 implants) supporting a prosthesis replacing at least two adjacent missing teeth; allocation paired a 3-mm abutment with 2-mm subcrestal placement against a 1-mm abutment with equicrestal placement. Radiographic and clinical measurements were taken at 3, 6 and 12 months and analysed with repeated-measures mixed ANOVA. The 1-mm group lost substantially more bone at every time point (0.76/0.92/0.95 mm vs 0.06/0.07/0.12 mm at 3/6/12 months), while patient characteristics and clinical variables showed no significant association with IPBL. The practical message is the *combination* of a tall abutment and subcrestal positioning in thin tissue — not height in isolation, because the two factors were deliberately varied together. Only the PubMed abstract was retrievable (no PMC or open-access full text), so no effect sizes, confidence intervals, exact p-values, or risk-of-bias details are available here.

## Key Contributions

- Randomized evidence on interproximal bone specifically in thin-mucosa sites, an outcome often buried in pooled mesial/distal marginal bone loss (MBL).
- Very large absolute group separation at 12 months: 0.95 ± 0.88 mm (1 mm) versus 0.12 ± 0.33 mm (3 mm).
- Early timing of the difference: most of the short-abutment loss was present by 3 months (0.76 mm) and increased only modestly to 12 months (0.95 mm).
- Confirms in a randomized design the direction first suggested retrospectively by [[implants/mbl/galindomoreno-2014-prosthetic-abutment-height-is-a]] (<2 vs ≥2 mm abutments).
- Adds the prosthetic-height axis to the thin-tissue placement-depth question studied by [[implants/mbl/linkevicius-2020-submerged-healing-abutment-subcrestal-soft-tissue]].

## Methodology

- **Design**: Randomized clinical trial, 1-year follow-up (Clinical Oral Implants Research 2019;30(7):595–602, DOI 10.1111/clr.13443, PMID 31021469).
- **Population**: 33 patients, 66 implants; each patient received one two-implant prosthesis replacing ≥2 adjacent missing teeth; thin peri-implant mucosa (no numeric threshold in the abstract).
- **Implants**: Platform-switched (PS) implants; system not named in the abstract.
- **Groups**: 3-mm abutment + implant placed 2 mm subcrestally; 1-mm abutment + implant placed equicrestally.
- **Outcome**: Interproximal peri-implant bone-level change assessed radiographically at 3, 6 and 12 months post-surgery; clinical measurements also recorded.
- **Statistics**: Repeated-measures mixed ANOVA for between-group bone-level changes; association of IPBL with categorical variables also examined.
- **Evidence note**: Abstract-only — allocation concealment, blinding, radiographic analysis method, exact p-values and confidence intervals are not available.

## Results

| Time point | 1-mm abutment (equicrestal) | 3-mm abutment (2 mm subcrestal) | Between groups |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 months | 0.76 ± 0.79 mm | 0.06 ± 0.21 mm | Significant (p value not in abstract) |
| 6 months | 0.92 ± 0.88 mm | 0.07 ± 0.22 mm | Significant (p value not in abstract) |
| 12 months | 0.95 ± 0.88 mm | 0.12 ± 0.33 mm | Significant (p value not in abstract) |

- No statistically significant differences were found when IPBL was analysed against patient characteristics and clinical variables.
- **Interpretation limits**: height and insertion depth were co-varied by design, so the result supports the combined protocol rather than a clean abutment-height effect; the abstract gives no numeric definition of "thin mucosa"; follow-up stops at 1 year. See [[implants/munoz-2025-1mm-3mm-abutments-7year-rct]] for a 7-year view of a separate 1-mm vs 3-mm cohort (all 1.5 mm subcrestal), where the height difference was ~0.20 mm at 1 year and narrowed over time.
