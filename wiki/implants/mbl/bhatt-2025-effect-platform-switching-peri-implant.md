## Three-line Summary

Prospective RCT (60 patients, single posterior implant, 30 PS vs 30 platform-matching (PM), 12-month follow-up) comparing platform switching to platform matching on crestal bone loss (CBL) and clinical parameters.

At 12 months, mean CBL was 0.32 ± 0.15 mm in the PS group vs 0.81 ± 0.22 mm in the PM group (p < 0.001); probing depth and bleeding on probing trended lower with PS but did not reach significance.

Confirms PS as a biomechanical strategy for peri-implant bone preservation over a 12-month window; short duration and single-center design limit generalizability.

## Summary

Bhatt et al. (2025) is a prospective RCT enrolling 60 patients with single posterior implants, randomized 1:1 to platform switching (3.5mm abutment on a 4.5mm implant platform) versus platform matching (matched 4.5mm abutment). Over 12 months, crestal bone loss was tracked radiographically at baseline, 6, and 12 months, alongside probing depth, plaque index, and bleeding on probing. The PS group showed significantly less bone loss at both time points, with the gap widening from 0.35mm at 6 months to 0.49mm at 12 months. Soft-tissue parameters (PD, BOP) trended in the same direction but did not reach significance, likely underpowered for those secondary endpoints. No implant failures occurred in either group. The trial adds a contemporary RCT data point to a body of PS-vs-PM literature that is otherwise dominated by older SR+MAs and 3–5 year RCTs.

## Key Contributions

- Direct prospective RCT comparison of PS vs PM with standardized methodology at 12 months — addresses the authors' cited gap of "few prospective clinical trials... with standardized methodologies."
- CBL gap widens over time within the trial itself (6mo: 0.35mm; 12mo: 0.49mm), consistent with the broader PS literature's finding that PS advantage accumulates rather than being a one-time effect.
- Reports secondary soft-tissue parameters (PD, PI, BOP) alongside CBL — all directionally favor PS though underpowered.

## Methodology

- Prospective RCT, 18-month study period, single Department of Periodontology and Implant Dentistry.
- n=60 (30 PS / 30 PM), powered for 0.3mm minimum detectable CBL difference (α=0.05, β=0.20).
- Inclusion: age 20–60, systemically healthy, single posterior missing tooth, adequate bone volume without grafting.
- Titanium screw-type implants, 4.5mm diameter × 10mm length; PS group used 3.5mm abutments, PM group used matched 4.5mm abutments. Single surgeon, flapless where feasible.
- Healing abutments at 3 months; final restoration at 4 months post-placement.
- Marginal bone level measured on standardized periapical radiographs (paralleling technique) via ImageJ, mesial + distal, at baseline/6mo/12mo. PD/PI/BOP recorded with UNC-15 probe at 6 and 12 months.

## Results

| Time point | PS group CBL (mm) | PM group CBL (mm) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 months | 0.19 ± 0.10 | 0.54 ± 0.16 | <0.001 |
| 12 months | 0.32 ± 0.15 | 0.81 ± 0.22 | <0.001 |

- 12-month PD: PS 2.5 ± 0.6mm vs PM 2.9 ± 0.7mm (p=0.08, NS); BOP: PS 10% vs PM 17% (p=0.21, NS); PI comparable.
- 2 mucositis cases in the PM group; 0 implant failures in either group.
- Discussion situates the 0.49mm 12-month difference as clinically relevant (annual CBL >0.2mm after year 1 threatens long-term prognosis) and cites consistency with Atieh and Strietzel systematic reviews (~0.3–0.5mm PS advantage).
