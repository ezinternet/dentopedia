## Three-line Summary

Prospective, non-randomized comparative trial: 14 wide-diameter implants with platform-switched (PS) abutments (0.45 mm per-side offset, 5 mm implant/4.1 mm abutment) vs 8 regular-diameter implants with traditional non-PS abutments, followed 1 year after final restoration in 15 patients.

Mean crestal bone height at 1 year: -0.22 mm ± 0.53 mm (PS) vs -2.02 mm ± 0.49 mm (non-PS), P ≤ .0001; bone-level change from baseline to 1 year -0.12 mm ± 0.40 mm (PS) vs -0.29 mm ± 0.34 mm (non-PS), ANCOVA P ≤ .0132.

Authors concluded a 0.45 mm per-side abutment reduction "seems sufficient" to avoid peri-implant bone loss — a specific, frequently-cited threshold, but drawn from a small (n=14 vs 8), non-randomized, single-offset, 1-year study.

## Summary

This 2007 prospective clinical trial by Hürzeler et al. compared 14 wide-diameter implants restored with platform-switched (PS) abutments against 8 regular-diameter implants restored with traditional, diameter-matched abutments, in 15 patients. Group assignment tracked implant diameter rather than randomization, so this is a controlled but non-randomized prospective comparison (`evidence_level: prospective`, not RCT). At 1-year follow-up after final restoration, mean crestal bone height was -0.22 mm for the PS group versus -2.02 mm for the control group (P ≤ .0001), and the additional bone-level change over that year (-0.12 mm PS vs -0.29 mm control) was also statistically significant by ANCOVA (P ≤ .0132). The paper's most frequently cited conclusion — that reducing abutment diameter by 0.45 mm per side (a 5 mm implant paired with a 4.1 mm abutment) "seems sufficient" to avoid peri-implant bone loss — is the specific offset-magnitude number that [[implants/mbl/gupta-2019-platform-switching-crestal-bone-loss-sr]] attributes to this study when compiling per-side mismatch values across the platform-switching literature. This page sources that claim directly from the primary study (via PubMed structured abstract; no PMC full text or PDF was obtainable) rather than only through Gupta 2019's secondhand citation.

## Key Contributions

- Earliest primary controlled clinical comparison in this wiki's platform-switching cluster to report a specific per-side offset magnitude (0.45 mm) alongside quantified 1-year bone-loss outcomes.
- Source of the "0.45 mm/side sufficient" claim that later narrative reviews (Gupta 2019) cite as evidence toward a minimum-effective-mismatch threshold — now independently sourced in this wiki rather than relying solely on a citing intermediary.
- Distinguishes baseline crestal bone height difference (already present at final restoration: -0.09 mm PS vs -1.73 mm control) from the smaller, but still significant, additional divergence accrued over the following year — most of the 1-year between-group gap reflects a baseline difference already established before the observation period began.

## Methodology

Prospective, non-randomized, controlled clinical trial; 15 patients, 14 PS implants (test) vs 8 non-PS implants (control). Standardized digital radiographs at final restoration and 1-year follow-up; mesial/distal crestal bone levels measured via digital image analysis; group comparison by statistical means and ANCOVA for bone-level change.

## Results
