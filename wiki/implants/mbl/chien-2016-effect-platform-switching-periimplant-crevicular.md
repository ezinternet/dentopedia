## Three-line Summary

Nonrandomized, controlled, split-mouth observational clinical trial (14 patients, 1 platform-switched (PS) + 1 platform-matched (PM) implant per patient, clinical parameters and periimplant crevicular fluid (PICF) cytokines sampled at 1/2/4/6 weeks post-abutment-connection) comparing early soft-tissue healing between PS and PM implants.

Probing depth was significantly deeper in PM than PS only at week 1 (3.5±0.4mm vs 2.9±0.2mm, p=0.03), converging by week 6; PICF volume decreased significantly over time only in PS (p=0.0005); IL-6 and MIP-1β decreased significantly in both groups, but TNF-α decreased significantly only in PS and was paradoxically ~3x higher in PS than PM at week 1 (17±7pg vs 6±1pg, p=0.005) — the only cytokine with a significant between-group difference.

Overall periimplant soft-tissue/cytokine response was mostly similar between PS and PM during early healing, with only time- and mediator-specific differences — the higher week-1 TNF-α in PS complicates a simple "PS reduces inflammation" narrative and is left mechanistically unexplained by the authors.

## Summary

Chien et al. (2016) is a nonrandomized, controlled, split-mouth clinical trial at Ohio State University's Graduate Periodontology Clinic, enrolling 17 patients (14 completing) who each received one PS and one PM implant in different quadrants. At second-stage (uncovering) surgery, healing abutments established the PS/PM comparison — coin-flip randomized for patients with matched 5.0mm implant diameters, forced by anatomy for patients with mismatched diameters. Clinical parameters (plaque index, sulcus bleeding index, probing depth) and PICF (both volume and a 20-cytokine multiplex panel) were tracked at 1, 2, 4, and 6 weeks post-surgery. This is presented as the first study reporting PICF proinflammatory cytokine profiles for PS versus PM implants during early soft-tissue healing — testing, at the biological level, the "microgap distancing reduces inflammatory infiltrate" mechanism that many PS-and-MBL papers invoke to explain why PS preserves crestal bone. The result is not a clean confirmation: most parameters converge by week 6 and show only transient or absent between-group differences, and the one cytokine with a clear, sustained group effect (TNF-α) runs in the *opposite* direction expected — higher in PS at week 1, not lower.

## Key Contributions

- First study reporting PICF proinflammatory cytokine expression around PS implants during early soft-tissue healing, directly compared against PM implants in the same patients (split-mouth).
- Screens 20 cytokines via Bio-Plex multiplex assay; narrows to 3 with meaningful time- or group-dependent signal (IL-6, MIP-1β, TNF-α) out of 10 detectable.
- Documents a genuinely counterintuitive finding: TNF-α is ~3x higher in PS than PM at week 1 (p=0.005), the only cytokine with a significant between-group difference — this does not support a simple "PS lowers local inflammation" story, and the authors flag their own explanation (rough seating-platform-surface exposure) as speculative and unproven.
- Shows clinical parameters (PD, mPI, mSBI) largely converge between PS and PM by week 6, with only a transient week-1 PD difference (PM deeper) attributable more plausibly to probing mechanics (inward-shifted abutment junction, soft-tissue folding) than to a true health difference.

## Methodology

- Nonrandomized, controlled, observational, split-mouth trial. Two Osseotite tapered Certain implants (BIOMET 3i) placed per patient (different quadrants), 2-stage submerged protocol, 2–4 months healing before second-stage (uncovering) surgery.
- At uncovering: standardized 4mm healing abutments assigned as PS (4.1mm) vs PM (5.0mm) test/control. For patients with two 5.0mm-diameter implants, PS/PM assignment was randomized by coin flip; for patients with mismatched implant diameters (5.0mm=PS, 4.0mm=PM), both received the same 4.1mm abutment (not randomized). Same surgeon, both implants uncovered same appointment, H-shaped flap incision.
- 17 patients recruited (Jan–Dec 2012), 14 completed (5 men, 9 women, age 25–73, mean 58.7); 3 excluded (1 noncompliant, 2 early implant failure). Non-smokers, systemically/periodontally healthy adults 18–75.
- Clinical parameters (mPI, mSBI, PD via UNC-12 probe, 6 sites/implant averaged) and PICF (PerioPaper strips, Periotron 8000 volume, 4 pooled strips/implant/timepoint) collected at 1, 2, 4, 6 weeks post-uncovering. PICF eluted and assayed for 20 cytokines via Bio-Plex multiplex bead assay.
- Statistics: linear mixed-model regression for repeated measures; repeated-measures ANOVA for within-group (week 1 vs 6) and between-group (PM vs PS) comparisons; Bonferroni correction, α=0.05. Sample size powered for a large (1 SD) effect; n=14 gave ≥93% power.
- Note on evidence level: despite a coin-flip randomization step for the abutment-diameter-matched subgroup, the study is explicitly described by the authors as a "nonrandomized, controlled, observational clinical trial" (not full randomization across all subjects), so this page classifies it as `prospective` rather than `rct`.

## Results

| Parameter | PM week 1 | PM week 6 | PS week 1 | PS week 6 | Within-PM p | Within-PS p | Between-group p (wk1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PD (mm) | 3.5 ± 0.4 | 2.3 ± 0.1 | 2.9 ± 0.2 | 2.0 ± 0.1 | 0.0005 | 0.0005 | 0.03 (PM deeper) |
| PICF volume (μL) | 1.8 ± 0.25 | 1.4 ± 0.2 | 2.0 ± 0.35 | 1.2 ± 0.24 | NS | 0.0005 | NS |
| IL-6 (pg total) | 33 ± 7 | 0.4 ± 0.1 | 47 ± 15 | 1 ± 0.4 | 0.0005 | 0.0005 | NS |
| MIP-1β (pg total) | 17 ± 3 | 5 ± 1 | 21 ± 6 | 6 ± 2 | 0.03 | 0.003 | NS |
| TNF-α (pg total) | 6 ± 1 | 3 ± 1 | 17 ± 7 | 4 ± 1 | NS | 0.001 | **0.005 (PS higher)** |

- mPI and mSBI decreased over time in both groups but neither within-group nor between-group differences reached significance (p>0.05); mSBI stayed below 0.2 throughout — minimal gingival inflammation in both arms.
- PD difference between groups was significant only at week 1 (PM deeper), not week 6 — interpreted as a transient probing-mechanics artifact (inward-shifted implant-abutment junction / soft-tissue folding in PS) rather than a lasting health difference.
- 10 of 20 screened cytokines were negligible with no differential expression (IL-1ra, IL-4, IL-10, IL-12, IL-17, MIP-1α, eotaxin, FGF, PDGF, RANTES); of the remaining 10, only IL-6, MIP-1β, and TNF-α showed meaningful differences. IP-10 (anti-inflammatory) showed a nonsignificant increase concomitant with the proinflammatory decline.
- TNF-α is the standout, nuanced finding: at week 1 it was ~3x higher in PS than PM (17±7 vs 6±1 pg, p=0.005) — the only cytokine with a significant between-group difference, and it points toward *more*, not less, early inflammation in PS. The authors speculate this could reflect soft-tissue exposure to the implant's (minimally rough) seating-platform surface activating inflammatory cells, but explicitly state this "remains to be proven." By later timepoints TNF-α differences between groups were no longer significant, and PS TNF-α dropped significantly overall (week 1→6, p=0.001) while PM's drop did not reach significance.
- Bottom line stated in the paper's own conclusion: periimplant soft-tissue response around PM and PS implants is "mostly similar with time- and mediator-specific differences" — this is not a broad demonstration that PS reduces early inflammation; if anything, the one robust group-level cytokine signal runs the other way.
