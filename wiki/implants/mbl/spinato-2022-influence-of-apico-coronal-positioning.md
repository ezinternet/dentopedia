## Three-line Summary

Multi-center prospective observational study (STROBE; NCT05363306), 49 patients / 49 tissue-level implants (3-mm machined transmucosal collar, 4.1 mm) placed in the posterior mandible at depth adapted to vertical mucosal thickness (thin <2.5 mm: collar 2 mm subcrestal, n=18; medium 2.5–3.5 mm: 1 mm subcrestal, n=16; thick >3.5 mm: equicrestal, n=15), radiographic follow-up to 5 months before prosthesis delivery.

Mean marginal bone resorption at 5 months (T2 ΔPBL) was 0.66 ± 0.49, 0.32 ± 0.41 and 0.22 ± 0.52 mm in the thin, medium and thick groups (ANOVA p = 0.025; at 3 months p = 0.184, not significant); bone loss exposing the treated implant surface occurred in 0/18, 1/16 and 8/15 implants (unexposed in 100%, 93.7% and 53.3%).

Thin mucosa still showed the most resorption despite deeper placement, but depth adaptation prevented early exposure of the rough implant surface; the 5-month, single-site (posterior mandible), 2D-radiograph design has no equicrestal thin-mucosa control and excludes the prosthetic phase.

## 세줄요약

다기관 전향적 관찰연구(STROBE, NCT05363306), 환자 49명·조직수준 임플란트 (Tissue-level implant) 49개(3 mm 기계가공 경점막부, 직경 4.1 mm), 하악 구치부, 수직 점막 두께에 맞춰 식립 깊이를 조절(얇음 <2.5 mm: 경점막부 2 mm 치조정하방 n=18; 중간 2.5–3.5 mm: 1 mm 하방 n=16; 두꺼움 >3.5 mm: 치조정 수준 n=15), 보철물 장착 전 5개월까지 방사선 추적.

5개월 평균 변연골흡수 (T2 ΔPBL)는 얇음·중간·두꺼움 군에서 각각 0.66 ± 0.49, 0.32 ± 0.41, 0.22 ± 0.52 mm (ANOVA p = 0.025; 3개월 시점은 p = 0.184로 비유의); 처리 표면을 노출시키는 골소실은 각각 0/18, 1/16, 8/15개(비노출 100%, 93.7%, 53.3%).

얇은 점막에서는 더 깊게 식립해도 골흡수가 가장 컸지만 깊이 조절이 거친 임플란트 표면의 조기 노출을 막았다; 5개월·하악 구치부 한정·2차원 방사선 측정이며 치조정 수준으로 식립한 얇은 점막 대조군이 없고 보철 단계는 제외되어 일반화에 한계가 있다.

## Summary

This multi-center prospective study followed 49 single tissue-level implants (3-mm machined transmucosal collar, moderately rough body, external hex, 4.1 mm diameter) in the posterior mandible from placement to 5 months, immediately before impression taking. Implant depth was set by vertical mucosal thickness measured after buccal flap reflection: 2 mm subcrestal for thin mucosa (<2.5 mm), 1 mm subcrestal for medium (2.5–3.5 mm) and equicrestal for thick (>3.5 mm). Marginal bone resorption at 5 months differed significantly between groups (0.66 ± 0.49, 0.32 ± 0.41, 0.22 ± 0.52 mm; ANOVA p = 0.025), with thin mucosa losing the most bone, and most change occurred within the first 3 months. However, the deeper placement in thin and medium mucosa kept the treated implant surface unexposed in 100% and 93.7% of implants, versus 53.3% at equicrestal placement in thick mucosa. The authors conclude that depth adaptation to mucosal thickness can prevent unwanted surface exposure even though thin mucosa still produces more resorption during supracrestal tissue height (STH) formation.

## Key Contributions

- Prospective three-depth design for tissue-level implants (2 mm subcrestal / 1 mm subcrestal / equicrestal) matched to measured mucosal thickness, evaluated before prosthesis delivery so abutment height, shape and loading do not confound the result.
- Separates bone **remodeling** (resorption around the 3-mm machined collar) from bone **loss** (resorption exposing the rough implant body): at 5 months group 1 had 0 BL (18/18 BR), group 2 had 1/16 BL, group 3 had 8/15 BL.
- Shows that thin-mucosa sites, even with the collar sunk 2 mm, resorbed the most (0.66 mm), while thick-mucosa sites placed equicrestal lost little bone (0.22 mm) yet exposed the treated surface in 8/15 — suggesting slight subcrestal placement may also help thick sites.
- Finds no significant PBL change from 3 to 5 months in groups 2 and 3 (group 1 p = 0.075), consistent with STH establishment occurring mainly within about 3 months.

## Methodology

- **Design**: multi-center (six centers) observational prospective study, STROBE; registered NCT05363306; ethics approval Regione Calabria, Nr. 370/2020; one implant per patient.
- **Participants**: 54 enrolled; 49 analyzed (25 men, 24 women; mean age 56.7 ± 11.8 years). Excluded: 4 group 2 patients lost to follow-up, 1 group 3 patient with peak insertion torque >60 Ncm. Inclusion: healed posterior mandibular crest, ≥6 mm width, ≥9 mm height above the canal, ≥3 mm keratinized mucosa, FMPS ≤25%, no augmentation.
- **Intervention**: i-Smart (i-Res) tissue-level implant, one-stage unsubmerged protocol, healing cap, 5-month healing before prosthetic referral.
- **Outcome**: PBL on customized-jig periapical radiographs at T0, T1 (3 months), T2 (5 months); ΔPBL = PBL(Tn) − PBL(T0); two blinded calibrated examiners (κ 87.2% intra, 81.6% inter).
- **Analysis**: ANOVA, simple linear regression and coincidence-of-lines analysis; patient as unit; p < 0.05.

## Results

| Group (mucosa; collar depth) | n | T1 ΔPBL (mm) | T2 ΔPBL (mm) | BR / BL at T2 | Treated surface unexposed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 thin <2.5 mm; 2 mm subcrestal | 18 | 0.41 ± 0.30 | 0.66 ± 0.49 | 18 / 0 | 100% |
| 2 medium 2.5–3.5 mm; 1 mm subcrestal | 16 | 0.26 ± 0.40 | 0.32 ± 0.41 | 15 / 1 | 93.7% |
| 3 thick >3.5 mm; equicrestal | 15 | 0.19 ± 0.35 | 0.22 ± 0.52 | 7 / 8 | 53.3% |

- Inter-group ANOVA: T1 p = 0.184 (NS); T2 p = 0.025.
- PBL decreased significantly T0→T1 in all groups; T1→T2 no significant change in groups 2 and 3, group 1 marginal (p = 0.075).
- Regression of ΔPBL vs time: significant for groups 1 and 2 (p < 0.05), marginal for group 3 (p = 0.0871); slope halved group 1→2 (p = 0.04), one third in group 3; groups 2 vs 3 not different (p = 0.55); groups 1 vs 2 lines differ (p = 0.007), groups 2 vs 3 similar (p = 0.594).
- All 49 implants osseointegrated; no complications or adverse events. The extracted full text has empty Tables 2 and 3, so regression values come from the prose.

**Limitations (as stated or evident from the design).** Placement depth was assigned by mucosal thickness, so thickness and depth are confounded (no thin-mucosa equicrestal group, no randomization); the follow-up is only 5 months and excludes the prosthetic phase; mucosal thickness was probed after flap reflection; 2D radiographs give no buccal/lingual information; posterior mandible and one implant system only. The authors also note that subcrestal placement may not suit all implant–abutment connections.
