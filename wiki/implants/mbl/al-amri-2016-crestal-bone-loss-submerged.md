## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (13 studies — 6 human + 7 animal, all university-based; searched 1986 through October 2015; King Saud University, Riyadh) assessing crestal bone loss (CBL) around submerged vs nonsubmerged dental implants, framed as the focused question "Does crestal and subcrestal placement of dental implants influence crestal bone levels?"

No significant difference in CBL was found between submerged and nonsubmerged implants: 5 of 6 human studies reported no significant difference (n = 8–84 individuals, follow-up 1–5 years; CBL 0.17–0.9 mm at test sites vs 0.02–1.4 mm at control sites), and 4 of 7 dog studies were likewise null (follow-up 2–6 months).

The evidence does not support choosing submerged vs nonsubmerged healing to protect crestal bone — but the null is a vote count with no pooled effect estimate, 7 of 13 studies are short-term animal work, and platform-switching studies were excluded, so the conclusion cannot be extended to modern platform-switched implants.

## Summary

This systematic review (Al Amri, King Saud University — *J Prosthet Dent* 2016;115(5):564-570.e1) asked a single focused question: "Does crestal and subcrestal placement of dental implants influence crestal bone levels?", searching 1986 through October 2015 across combinations of *crestal*, *sub-crestal*, *bone loss*, *dental implant*, *submerged*, and *nonsubmerged*, plus hand-searching reference lists of original and review articles. Thirteen studies were included — 6 human (8–84 individuals, 1–5 years of follow-up) and 7 animal (all dogs, mean age 1 to ~2 years, 2–6 months) — all performed at universities; letters, case reports, commentaries, platform-switching studies, and non-English publications were excluded. Reported CBL spanned 0.17–0.9 mm at test sites and 0.02–1.4 mm at control sites, with 5 of 6 human studies and 4 of 7 animal studies finding no significant difference between submerged and nonsubmerged implants. No meta-analysis was performed, so the synthesis is a directional vote count plus value ranges rather than a pooled effect estimate. **Clinical reading:** the protocol axis of closing the flap over the implant versus exposing it at placement carries no demonstrable crestal bone penalty, so healing protocol should be chosen for soft-tissue, esthetic, and patient factors — but the null is weak evidence (small samples, half the base animal and short-term, platform-switching studies carved out) and cannot exclude a small effect, which is exactly what the later pooled estimate of MD 0.12 mm in [[implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]] reports while itself calling clinically irrelevant.

## Key Contributions

- **Stated gap, not an update** — the author explicitly notes no prior systematic review of CBL around submerged vs nonsubmerged implants was known, making this the first SR on the axis in this wiki's holdings
- **Healing protocol treated as its own lever** — most MBL reviews in this wiki analyse implant *design*; this one isolates the surgical flap/closure decision, which is a decision the clinician makes per case
- **Placement depth folded into the question** — crestal vs subcrestal positioning is assessed alongside submerged vs nonsubmerged healing, the two variables most often conflated in the two-stage literature
- **Scope boundary set by exclusion** — platform-switching studies were excluded, which fixes exactly where this null applies (conventional non-platform-switched implants) and where it must not be transferred
- **Human and animal evidence reported separately** with species, age, and follow-up, making the 7/13 animal share visible rather than pooled away
- **Usable as a null anchor** — provides the pre-pooling baseline against which the later 0.12 mm pooled difference can be judged as clinically trivial

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review **without** meta-analysis (narrative synthesis of reported CBL values); risk-of-bias tool, certainty grading, and publication-bias assessment are not mentioned in the abstract
- **Focused question**: "Does crestal and subcrestal placement of dental implants influence crestal bone levels?"
- **Databases**: not named in the abstract — only the date window and keyword combinations are reported
- **Search window**: 1986 through October 2015
- **Keywords**: combinations of *crestal*, *sub-crestal*, *bone loss*, *dental implant*, *submerged*, *nonsubmerged*
- **Additional searching**: hand-search of reference lists of potentially relevant original and review articles
- **Exclusions**: letters to the editor; case reports; commentaries; **studies on platform-switched implants**; non-English-language studies
- **Included**: 13 studies (6 human, 7 animal), all university-based
- **Outcomes**: crestal bone loss (mm) at test vs control sites
- **Statistics**: none pooled — no effect size, confidence interval, p-value, or heterogeneity measure is reported in the abstract

**Reading the ranges correctly:** the 0.17–0.9 mm (test) and 0.02–1.4 mm (control) figures are **cross-study minima and maxima, not paired group means**. The control maximum exceeds the test maximum, but this cannot be read as submerged implants losing less bone — the values come from different studies and no within-study contrast is reported.

**The null is a vote count, not an effect estimate:** with no per-study effect sizes, "5 of 6 human studies null" cannot distinguish a genuine absence of protocol effect from an underpowered test that missed a small one. **4 of 7** animal studies were null, so 3 animal studies did not report a null result; the abstract does not state what they showed, so no direction or magnitude can be attributed to them.

## Limitations

- **No meta-analysis, no pooled estimate** — no effect size, CI, or heterogeneity assessment, so the null cannot be quantified or power-checked
- **Over half the evidence base is animal** — 7 of 13 studies, all canine, with 2–6 months of follow-up against 1–5 years in the human studies (20–60× shorter); canine remodeling is faster than human, so these cannot speak to long-term crestal stability
- **Platform-switching studies explicitly excluded** — the null is scoped to conventional implants and does not transfer to platform-switched designs, the dominant class in the newer MBL literature ([[implants/mbl/mortazavi-2021-bone-loss-tissue-bone-level-implants]])
- **English-language restriction** and **all-university setting** — language bias and practice generalizability unassessed
- **Small, short human samples** — as few as 8 individuals and as little as 1 year; since most crestal bone loss occurs in year 1, 1 year is the minimum informative window and later remodeling is unobserved
- **Two variables in one question** — crestal vs subcrestal placement *and* submerged vs nonsubmerged healing are not separated, so the pair's null cannot be assigned to either factor alone
- **Abstract-only retrieval** *(inferred)* — no per-study table, no risk-of-bias instrument, no certainty rating, and no publication-bias assessment are available; the review's own rigor cannot be appraised from this page

## Related Papers

- [[implants/moustafa-ali-2018-submerged-vs-nonsubmerged-implant]] — **Same question, opposite statistical direction.** This 2016 SR found **no significant CBL difference**; Moustafa Ali 2018 (SR+MA, 6 RCTs) reports **significantly more** MBL with submerged placement (MD 0.12 mm; 95% CI 0.00–0.24; I² = 0%, P = .04). Stated honestly, the two are not cleanly refuted: this review is 2 years earlier, includes 7 animal studies, excludes platform-switching, and pools nothing, while the later one is RCT-only but self-described as underpowered with 4 of 6 trials at high risk of bias — and **its own authors judge the 0.12 mm difference not clinically relevant**, which converges with this page's clinical reading. The overlap between the two reviews' included trials is not determinable from the abstracts. *No typed `relations:` edge is written because both pages were ingested in the same parallel batch; a later ingest can add `type: contradicts` here on the older page.*
- [[implants/pirc-2026-one-piece-two-piece-implants-15year-rct]] — **refines.** The 15–17-year RCT (60 patients, 151 implants) shows one-piece implants — which eliminate the implant-abutment interface entirely and are never submerged — at 0.08 mm MBL vs 1.53 mm for two-piece Brånemark. That gap is an order of magnitude larger than any healed-protocol effect measured in either review, narrowing what this page's null can be taken to mean: **removing the interface** matters, closing the flap over it does not. (Its two-piece arm is also self-caveated against extrapolation to modern platform-switched systems.)
- [[implants/mbl/mortazavi-2021-bone-loss-tissue-bone-level-implants]] — **Complementary axis, no typed edge.** That SR (38 trials) names the *design* levers on MBL — platform switching 0.7–2.5 → 0.12–0.29 mm, Morse taper connection, SLActive surface — and is silent on the healing protocol this page reviews. Read together: procedural choice looks like the smaller contributor, but this page's platform-switching exclusion means the two cannot be numerically reconciled.
- [[implants/mbl/kim-2022-abutment-connection-mbl-survival]] — **No typed edge; relevant contrast.** The 5-year retrospective cohort (374 implants) attributes 1-year MBL to abutment **connection** geometry (external hex 1.23 vs internal 0.72 mm, p<0.001) in loaded implants. Together with this page's null on flap design, it points to connection and interface handling — not submerged vs nonsubmerged healing — as the more plausible crestal bone lever.
- [[implants/mbl/dhir-2012-significance-clinical-relevance-biologic-width]] — **Mechanism.** Supplies the biologic-width account (microgap position relative to crestal bone driving ~2 mm of apical resorption) that explains why two-stage submerged surgery per se adds no bone loss beyond the interface effect.
