## Three-line Summary

Pilot RCT (23 patients, 21 platform-switching (PFS) + 18 standard (StE) titanium implants, 6-month follow-up) testing whether PFS improves crestal bone preservation and clinical outcome over standard platform-matching implants of the same base design.

No statistically significant difference in marginal bone level was found between PFS and StE implants at any timepoint (Wilcoxon two-sample test), nor in pocket depth or attachment height; bone stabilized approximately 1mm below the implant shoulder in both groups.

This is a **null-result RCT**, discordant with most PS literature: the authors conclude it seems unlikely that the already-optimal outcome of the standard (no-PFS) implant tested here can be meaningfully improved by introducing PFS.

## Summary

Rossi et al. (2015) is a small pilot RCT (23 patients, 21 platform-switched [PFS] vs 18 standard non-PFS [StE] titanium implants, ELEMENT RC system, Thommen Medical AG) with 6 months of radiographic and clinical follow-up from abutment connection. Surgeons were blinded to implant type until after patient screening. Marginal bone level was tracked at 5 timepoints (AC, 4-week, 6-week/loading, 3-month, 6-month) via standardized parallel-technique periapical radiographs, alongside pocket depth (PD) and attachment height (AH). At every timepoint, PFS implants showed a numerically smaller mean bone-to-implant distance than StE implants, but the difference never reached statistical significance (Wilcoxon two-sample test). Both groups stabilized at roughly 1mm below the implant shoulder by 6 months, matching this implant line's known long-term (5-year) behavior. No implants failed. The authors explicitly frame their finding against the broader PS literature — including the Atieh systematic review's own mixed results (5 of 10 pooled trials actually favored platform-matching) — and conclude that PFS is unlikely to further improve an already clinically excellent standard implant design, at least within 6 months.

## Key Contributions

- One of the few RCTs in this wiki's platform-switching collection reporting a **null result** — no significant PFS advantage in either bone level or soft-tissue parameters — directly at odds with the pooled effect reported by [[implants/mbl/juan-montesinos-2022-platform-switching-conventional-sr-ma]] (MD 0.255mm favoring PS, p<0.05).
- Surfaces, from within its own introduction, that the foundational Atieh SR+MA (10 controlled trials, 1,239 implants) itself was far from unanimous: only 1 trial trended toward PFS benefit, 4 found no difference, and 5 found platform-matched implants *significantly superior* — evidence that disagreement about PFS efficacy predates and is not unique to this trial.
- Reports a matched "split-mouth"-style case (same patient, PFS at position 15 vs StE at position 25) as a qualitative illustration of clinically indistinguishable outcomes between the two connection designs.
- Blinded surgeons to implant assignment until after screening — a design strength relative to some earlier, non-blinded PS comparisons.

## Methodology

- Prospective, randomized, controlled pilot trial; empirically chosen sample size (no prospective power calculation); 6-month follow-up from abutment connection (AC).
- 23 patients enrolled (Jan 2010–Mar 2012), randomized to 21 PFS / 18 StE implants (Thommen Medical AG ELEMENT RC, platform Ø5.0mm, lengths 8.0/9.5/11.0mm); 2 randomized patients could not undergo surgery (discontinued); 21 patients completed the 6-month follow-up.
- All implants in posterior premolar/molar sites (FDI x4–x7): 12 (31%) maxilla, 27 (69%) mandible. Moderate smokers (<10 cig/day) and patients with periodontitis history were eligible.
- Two-stage submerged placement, implants ~0.5mm subcrestal; AC after 4–12 weeks healing; functional loading 6 weeks post-AC.
- Marginal bone level: standardized parallel-technique periapical radiographs (customized jig bite blocks), digitized, mesial+distal distance from implant shoulder to first bone-to-implant contact measured with ImageJ, at AC/4wk/6wk/3mo/6mo.
- PD and AH via calibrated probe at AC, 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months.
- Statistics: parametric t-tests and non-parametric Wilcoxon two-sample tests (SAS 9.3.1).

## Results

Bone-to-implant distance (mm, mesial+distal averaged, mean ± SD):

| Time point | PFS | StE |
|---|---|---|
| Abutment connection (AC) | 0.63 ± 0.68 | 0.79 ± 0.48 |
| 4-week follow-up | 0.81 ± 0.50 | 1.18 ± 0.39 |
| 6-week (loading) | 1.06 ± 0.61 | 1.22 ± 0.62 |
| 3-month recall | 0.93 ± 0.69 | 1.14 ± 0.63 |
| 6-month (end of study) | 0.88 ± 0.76 | 1.06 ± 0.51 |

- **No statistically significant PFS-vs-StE difference at any timepoint** (Wilcoxon two-sample test), despite PFS trending numerically lower throughout.
- Bone in both groups stabilized ~1mm below the implant shoulder (at/above the first implant thread) by 6 months — consistent with this implant line's documented 5-year performance.
- PD remained <4mm (healthy) in both groups; AH loss trended slightly *lower* for StE than PFS (opposite direction to the bone-level trend), also not significant.
- No implant failures. Complications: 1 partially exposed cover screw (PFS, healed uneventfully), 1 pregnancy (PFS patient, discontinued further radiographs).
- Discussion notes the result does not confirm a separately cited meta-analysis (28 publications, 1,216 PFS + 1,157 platform-matched implants, not held in this wiki) that found PFS benefit growing with follow-up length and mismatch size — at 6 months, with this specific implant design, no such advantage was measurable.

## Related Papers

- [[implants/mbl/juan-montesinos-2022-platform-switching-conventional-sr-ma]] — **Contradicts.** This 2022 SR+MA (9 studies, 475 PS vs 462 conventional) found PS significantly reduces MBL (MD 0.255mm, p<0.05); this individual RCT found no significant PS advantage at 6 months, illustrating that pooled/meta-analytic significance does not guarantee replication in every underlying trial — important for a balanced reading of the PS evidence base rather than treating the SR+MA conclusion as universally confirmed.
- [[implants/mbl/strietzel-2015-platform-switching-mbl-sr-ma]] — same-year (2015) SR+MA of 22 studies also favoring PS (0.49mm vs 1.01mm MBL, p<0.0001); this trial was not part of that pooled dataset and stands as a contemporaneous null counterexample.
- [[implants/mbl/enkling-2013-platform-switching-bone-level-3year-rct]] — individual 3-year multi-center RCT in this wiki reporting significantly less bone loss with PFS; discordant with this trial's null 6-month result (different implant system and follow-up length, so not a head-to-head contradiction, but the two RCTs disagree on whether PFS confers a measurable clinical advantage).
- [[implants/mbl/messias-2019-platform-switching-mbl-5year-rct]] — 5-year equivalence RCT finding PS 0.23mm lower MBL (p=0.025); also discordant with this trial's null finding, over a much longer observation window than this study's 6 months.
- **Broader context**: this trial's own introduction cites the Atieh SR+MA (10 controlled trials, 1,239 implants) in which only 1 trial trended toward PFS benefit, 4 found no difference, and 5 found platform-matched implants *significantly superior* — a reminder that null and even PS-negative RCTs exist in the literature independent of this trial, and that the wiki's currently PS-favoring individual RCTs (Enkling 2013, Messias 2019, Rocha 2016, Bhatt 2025) should not be read as fully representative of the underlying evidence without weighing counterexamples like this one.
