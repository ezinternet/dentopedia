## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (8 systematic reviews, English-language, March 2017–July 2022) pooling narratively — not meta-analytically — the effect of systemic diseases/medications on dental implant osseointegration, success/survival rate, peri-implantitis, and implant loss; PROSPERO CRD42023397955, AMSTAR 2 quality appraisal.

Antiresorptive drugs (bisphosphonates) show no significant effect on osseointegration or survival (e.g., de Medeiros SR-MA: 4.70% vs 3.57% failure, osteoporotic vs healthy, n.s.); SSRIs and PPIs show a consistent negative signal across multiple included reviews (implant failure-rate differences of ~7.5% and ~4.5% respectively); diabetes results are conflicting and mostly short-term; neurologic disease, HIV, hypothyroidism, cardiovascular disease, and antihypertensives/beta-blockers/diuretics show no reduced osseointegration.

Clinical implication: no systemic condition reviewed is an absolute osseointegration contraindication except prolonged high-dose glucocorticoid use; SSRI/PPI users and poorly-controlled/long-term diabetics warrant closer monitoring, but overall evidence quality is low-to-moderate (most of the 8 included SRs AMSTAR 2 low/moderate, one critically low), so conclusions are provisional pending more/better reviews.

## 세줄요약

우산리뷰(umbrella review, SR 8편, 2017년 3월~2022년 7월 영어 논문)로, 전신질환·약물이 임플란트 골유착·성공/생존율·임플란트주위염·임플란트 상실에 미치는 영향을 메타분석 없이 서술적으로 종합; PROSPERO CRD42023397955 등록, AMSTAR 2 품질 평가.

항흡수제(비스포스포네이트)는 골유착·생존율에 유의한 영향 없음(de Medeiros SR-MA: 골다공증군 4.70% vs 건강군 3.57% 실패율, 비유의); SSRI·PPI는 여러 포함 리뷰에서 일관되게 부정적 신호(실패율 차이 각각 약 7.5%, 약 4.5%); 당뇨는 결과가 상충하며 대부분 단기 연구; 신경계질환·HIV·갑상선기능저하증·심혈관질환·항고혈압제/베타차단제/이뇨제는 골유착 저하를 보이지 않음.

임상적 의미: 검토된 전신질환 중 절대적 골유착 금기는 장기 고용량 글루코코르티코이드 사용뿐이며, SSRI/PPI 복용자 및 조절 불량·장기 당뇨 환자는 더 주의 깊은 모니터링이 필요하나, 전반적 근거 질이 낮음~중등도(포함 SR 8편 중 대다수 AMSTAR 2 low/moderate, 1편 critically low)라 결론은 잠정적이다.

## Summary

This umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42023397955) systematically searched PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, BioMed Central, Cochrane Library, and PROSPERO to 1 February 2023 for systematic reviews (with or without meta-analysis) evaluating whether systemic diseases and medications affect dental implant osseointegration, success rate, survival rate, peri-implantitis, and implant loss. From 5488 initial records, 8 systematic reviews (published March 2017–July 2022) met inclusion criteria. Each was appraised with AMSTAR 2; most were rated low or moderate quality, with one critically low. Rather than pooling numbers across the 8 SRs, the authors performed a narrative synthesis of their findings by disease/drug category.

## Key Contributions

- Broadens the evidence base beyond single-disease SRs by triangulating findings across 8 independently-conducted systematic reviews spanning osteoporosis/antiresorptives, diabetes, hypothyroidism, NSAIDs, glucocorticoids, SSRIs, PPIs, cardiovascular disease, neurological disorders, and HIV.
- Identifies SSRIs and PPIs as the two medication classes with the most consistent (though still preliminary) negative signal for implant osseointegration/failure across multiple reviews (not just one).
- Clarifies that antiresorptive (bisphosphonate) therapy at osteoporosis-treatment doses is not, by itself, a contraindication to implant placement, provided MRONJ risk counseling is given.
- Frames diabetes' conflicting evidence base as a short-term/well-controlled-disease sampling artifact rather than a true null result, aligning with the 2017 World Workshop classification of diabetes as a peri-implant risk factor in poorly-controlled/long-term disease.
- Applies AMSTAR 2 transparently, exposing that the entire evidence base in this domain is currently low-to-moderate quality — a useful caveat for any downstream clinical guideline.

## Methodology

PRISMA-compliant umbrella review using a PEO (Population–Exposure–Outcome) framework. Two-reviewer independent screening (title/abstract and full text) with a third reviewer for disagreements; reference-list hand-searching of included reviews. Data extraction by two reviewers (third for disputes) covering author/year/journal/review type/study quality/number and design of included studies/drug or disease/outcomes/results/conclusions. Narrative synthesis (Microsoft Excel 2019) — explicitly no quantitative pooling across the 8 SRs. AMSTAR 2 used for methodological quality/risk-of-bias appraisal of each included SR.

## Results

- 5488 records screened → 8 systematic reviews included (Aghaloo et al. among them).
- **Antiresorptive drugs**: no significant effect on osseointegration, success, survival, or loss vs. non-users in most reviews; de Medeiros SR-MA (10 studies): 4.70% failure (702 implants, osteoporotic) vs. 3.57% (4114 implants, healthy) — n.s.; Stavropoulos meta-analysis: no significant implant-loss difference (success rate 85.7–100% test vs. 84.6–100% control); individual studies conflict on implant-loss odds ratios (Yip 2012 OR 2.7 vs. Al-Sabbagh & Robinson 2015 OR 9.2, in opposite directions).
- **Diabetes**: no short-term osseointegration difference (Aghaloo, Javed, Naujokat); peri-implantitis risk conflicting (Ferreira OR 1.9; Tawil — HbA1c ≤7% → 0/141 peri-implant disease vs. HbA1c 7–9% → 6/141; Costa — no difference); long-term/poorly-controlled diabetes trends worse (increased MIBL, peri-implantitis).
- **SSRIs**: Chappuis +7.5% failure rate; Aghaloo survival 89.4–94.4% (SSRI) vs. 95.4–98.15% (non-SSRI); Cheng confirms 7.5%; Wu retrospective cohort (916 implants/490 patients) — increased failure risk.
- **PPIs**: Chappuis +4.5% failure rate; Aghaloo failure 12–6.8% (PPI) vs. 4.5–3.2% (non-PPI); Cheng confirms 4.5%; Vinnakota and Chawla — consistent negative signal.
- **Cardiovascular disease/antihypertensives**: no adverse early-failure effect (Aghaloo); antihypertensives/beta-blockers/ACE inhibitors show a protective signal (Wu: 0.6% vs. 4.1% failure).
- **Glucocorticoids**: prolonged high-dose use impairs osteoblast-mediated bone formation and is considered a main absolute contraindication to implant placement, though robust long-term human data are lacking.
- **NSAIDs, hypothyroidism, neurological disorders, HIV**: no clear/consistent evidence of altered osseointegration or implant failure in the included reviews.
- **Quality**: most of the 8 SRs AMSTAR 2 low/moderate; one critically low.
