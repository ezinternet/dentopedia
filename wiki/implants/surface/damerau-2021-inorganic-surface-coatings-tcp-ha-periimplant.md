## Three-line Summary
Systematic review (40 large-animal studies, dog/goat/sheep/pig, 2003–2016) plus random-effects meta-analysis (15 studies) comparing tricalcium phosphate (TCP)- and hydroxyapatite (HA)-coated titanium implants against uncoated rough titanium reference surfaces on bone-to-implant contact (BIC) at 14 days, 21–28 days, and 42–84 days of healing.

TCP-coated implants showed lower BIC than reference at 14 days (−3.48 pp, NS) and 21–28 days (−0.85 pp, NS), then higher BIC at 42–84 days (+13.79 pp, NS, high variability); HA-coated implants showed significantly lower BIC than reference at 14 days (−6.94 pp, p=.001) and small non-significant differences later (+1.53 pp at 21–28d, +1.57 pp at 42–84d).

Neither TCP nor HA coating produced a statistically robust BIC advantage over uncoated moderately-rough titanium in large-animal short-term healing — surface roughness of the reference itself likely overshadows any osteoconductive benefit from the calcium-phosphate coating chemistry.

## Summary
This systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 40 large-animal studies (2003–2016, dog/goat/sheep/pig) evaluating inorganic (calcium phosphate) implant surface coatings, then ran a random-effects meta-analysis on the 15 studies using the two most-studied coating types — tricalcium phosphate (TCP) and hydroxyapatite (HA) — each compared against an uncoated **rough** titanium reference surface (SLA/DAE-type, not smooth/machined) on bone-to-implant contact (BIC) at three healing timepoints (14 days, 21–28 days, 42–84 days). Neither coating produced a statistically significant BIC advantage over the rough reference at any timepoint; HA coating was in fact significantly **lower** than reference at 14 days (p=.001). The authors conclude there is little short-term BIC advantage of TCP- or HA-coated implants over uncoated rough titanium in large-animal models, attributing this to the reference surface's own roughness overshadowing the coating's chemical osteoconductivity. This nuances the wider CaP-coating literature, much of which historically compared coated implants against **smooth** machined controls rather than modern roughened references.

## Key Contributions
- Meta-analysis (not narrative synthesis) restricted to studies with a *rough* uncoated titanium comparator — a stricter, more clinically current benchmark than earlier reviews
- Three-timepoint trajectory (14d / 21–28d / 42–84d) tracking the coating-effect direction across early-to-mid healing
- Direct statistical evidence that TCP/HA coating chemistry adds no significant BIC benefit once the reference surface is already roughened — reframes decades of favorable CaP-coating literature that mostly used smooth/machined controls
- Species-level pattern flagged as a confound: dog models tended to favor the uncoated reference, goat/sheep/pig models tended to favor the coated surface

## Methodology
PRISMA-compliant systematic review of Biosis/Medline/Scopus (2003–2016) for large-animal (≥6 systemically healthy dog/goat/sheep/pig) studies comparing inorganic-coated vs uncoated rough-titanium implants. 4,174 titles → 40 studies in the SR; the 15 studies using TCP or HA coatings entered a random-effects meta-analysis (R `metafor`) of BIC percentage-point difference (test − reference) at T1 (14d), T2 (21–28d), T3 (42–84d).

## Results

| Coating | Timepoint | BIC diff. vs uncoated rough Ti | 95% CI | p | Significant |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TCP | 14 days | −3.48 pp | −7.62, 0.67 | .1 | No |
| TCP | 21–28 days | −0.85 pp | −6.46, 4.77 | .76 | No |
| TCP | 42–84 days | +13.79 pp | −1.83, 29.41 | .08 | No |
| HA | 14 days | **−6.94 pp** | −11.18, −2.70 | **.001** | **Yes, favors reference** |
| HA | 21–28 days | +1.53 pp | −4.28, 7.34 | .61 | No |
| HA | 42–84 days | +1.57 pp | −5.02, 8.17 | .64 | No |

TCP shows a trend toward the coated surface catching up and surpassing reference over time (but never significantly, and with growing between-study variability); HA shows only a small, consistently non-significant swing after an initial significant deficit. Dog-model studies tended to favor the reference surface across both coating groups; goat/sheep/pig studies tended to favor the coated surface — a species-level confound the authors note but cannot resolve with the available data.
