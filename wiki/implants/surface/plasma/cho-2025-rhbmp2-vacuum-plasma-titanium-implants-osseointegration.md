## Three-line Summary

In vivo beagle mandibular defect study (24 SLA implants, 4 beagles, 8-week healing): untreated (NS), rhBMP-2-only (BS), vacuum plasma-only (PS), and combined plasma+rhBMP-2 (PB) titanium implants compared on BIC, inter-thread bone density (ITBD), new bone area (NBA), micro-CT bone volume, and ISQ.

Combined PB group showed significantly higher BIC (83.91% vs 68.81%/70.98% for BS/PS), ITBD (81.32%, sig. > all others), and micro-CT new bone volume (75.00% vs 52.87% untreated); ISQ showed no significant between-group differences at any timepoint.

Vacuum plasma pretreatment appears to synergize with low-dose rhBMP-2 by increasing surface energy and improving protein retention, producing large histometric osseointegration gains that RFA/ISQ alone fails to detect.

## Summary

This beagle mandibular-defect study tests whether vacuum plasma pretreatment can serve as a coating-carrier enhancer for low-dose (0.05 mg/mL) rhBMP-2 on SLA titanium implants, rather than as a standalone surface reactivation tool. Across four groups (untreated, rhBMP-2 alone, plasma alone, combined), only the combined plasma+rhBMP-2 group achieved statistically significant improvements in bone-to-implant contact, inter-thread bone density, and micro-CT bone volume over untreated controls; plasma alone and rhBMP-2 alone were statistically indistinguishable from each other. Implant stability quotient (ISQ), measured by resonance frequency analysis, showed no significant differences among any group despite the large histometric differences — echoing prior reports that RFA correlates only weakly with bone-to-implant contact. Cumulative-release ELISA data suggest the mechanism is improved rhBMP-2 surface retention (lower burst release from plasma-treated surfaces) rather than a distinct osteoinductive effect of plasma itself.

## Key Contributions

- First test of vacuum plasma as a rhBMP-2 coating-carrier enhancer (not standalone treatment) on SLA titanium implants, in vivo.
- Demonstrates synergy: plasma-alone and BMP-alone groups are statistically similar; only the combination reaches significance on BIC/ITBD/NBV.
- Documents a clear dissociation between ISQ (no group differences) and histometric/micro-CT measures (large, significant differences) — a methodological caution for studies relying on RFA alone to detect coating/surface effects.

## Methodology

Animal in-vivo (beagle mandibular defect, 4-group design: NS/BS/PS/PB) plus companion in-vitro SLA-disk mechanistic work (ALP activity, qRT-PCR, XPS, SEM, cumulative-release ELISA). 24 SLA implants (3.5×8.5mm) in 4 male beagles, occlusal cylindrical defects with 3 exposed threads, 8-week healing; endpoints via Osstell ISQ, medical CT + micro-CT volumetric analysis, and Goldner's trichrome histomorphometry.
