## Three-line Summary

Within-patient RCT (BMC Oral Health 2026): 10 patients, 40 implants (4/patient: vacuum plasma, argon jet plasma, cold atmospheric plasma, untreated control); 60-second pre-insertion cold plasma applied to titanium implant surface; ISQ tracked at baseline, 14, 28, and 56 days.

All three cold plasma protocols showed significantly more favorable ISQ trajectories than untreated control at day 56 — vacuum +10.0, argon +8.9, cold atmospheric +8.3 ISQ units (all p<0.001); control implants showed a transient ISQ dip at day 28 while plasma-treated implants rose continuously.

Pilot study (n=10) — plasma surface activation promotes faster secondary stability transition, but replication in adequately powered, prospectively registered, site-standardized RCTs is mandatory before clinical adoption.

## Summary

Balkanlıoğlu and Acikan (BMC Oral Health 2026) conducted a within-patient RCT in 10 patients who each received 4 implants assigned to one of: untreated control, vacuum plasma (60s pre-insertion), argon jet plasma, or cold atmospheric plasma. Non-mirrored placement sites were used. ISQ was measured by Osstell at baseline, 14, 28, and 56 days. All plasma-treated implants showed a consistently rising ISQ trajectory, while controls showed a transient dip at day 28 (classical mechanical-to-biological stability transition). By day 56, plasma-treated implants were significantly more stable than controls (all p<0.001). No differences between the three plasma types were observed. No adverse events occurred. The within-patient design is methodologically strong but the small n and retrospective registration limit generalizability.

## Key Contributions

- First within-patient RCT comparing 3 cold plasma protocols vs untreated control for ISQ
- All 3 plasma types: significant ISQ advantage at 56 days — day 28 dip avoided
- Within-patient design eliminates inter-patient bone density confounders
- Mechanism: cold plasma → increased surface wettability and energy → faster protein adsorption and cell adhesion → earlier osseointegration
- No adverse events — cold plasma appears safe for chairside use
- Pilot study: effect direction and magnitude are promising, replication required

## Methodology

Prospective within-patient RCT; single center; n=10 patients, 40 implants (10/group); 60s plasma before insertion; Osstell ISQ at day 0/14/28/56; nested linear mixed-effects model; NCT07348770 (retrospective).
