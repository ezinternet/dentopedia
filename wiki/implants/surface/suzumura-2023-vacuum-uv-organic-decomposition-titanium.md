## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (grade 4 cp-Ti + grade 5 Ti-6Al-4V + PMMA; methylene blue as model organic molecule): four UV sources compared — UVC (254 nm, 1.2 mW/cm²), HUVC (high-energy UVC commercial device), PUV (proprietary UV commercial device), and VUV (172 nm xenon excimer, ~60 mW/cm²) — for 1-minute organic decomposition in quartz ampoules.

VUV decomposed 57% of coated methylene blue in 1 minute vs. 2% (UVC), 36% (HUVC), 42% (PUV); dose-dependency plateau at 60 s (R² = 0.93); quartz ampoules did not compromise VUV efficacy.

TiO₂ photocatalysis accounted for only 20–25% of total decomposition (PMMA control), confirming that direct photolysis by UV photons — which scales with photon energy and therefore wavelength — dominates the mechanism; VUV's superiority at thin-coating loads makes it uniquely robust where other sources fail.

## 세줄요약

In vitro 연구 (상업용 티타늄, 메틸렌블루 모델 유기분자): 4종 자외선원 — UVC(254 nm)·고에너지UVC(HUVC)·전용UV장비(PUV)·진공자외선 (Vacuum Ultraviolet, VUV, 172 nm 크세논 엑시머, ~60 mW/cm²) — 1분 처리 비교.

VUV 1분 처리로 57% 유기물 분해 (UVC 2%·HUVC 36%·PUV 42% 대비); 60초에 최대 효능 도달 (R²=0.93 음의 지수 곡선); 석영 앰풀 내 처리 시 VUV 효능 유의 감소 없음.

이산화티타늄 (TiO₂) 광촉매 기여도 ~20–25%에 불과(PMMA 대조) → 직접 광분해가 주기전임을 확인; 얇은 오염층에서 UVC 계열은 거의 효능 없으나 VUV는 모든 오염 밀도에서 일관 40–52% 분해 — 임상 불규칙 오염도에 가장 강인.

## Summary

The major practical bottleneck of UV photofunctionalization has been treatment time: original protocols required 48 h, later improved to 12–30 min, but still too long for chairside use after preparing the implant site. This study tested whether a 172 nm vacuum UV (VUV) xenon excimer lamp could achieve sufficient organic decomposition in clinically acceptable time. Using methylene blue as a model hydrocarbon surrogate, VUV decomposed 57% in 1 minute — far exceeding UVC (2%), HUVC (36%), and PUV (42%). A dose-dependency test confirmed maximal effect at 60 s. The finding that quartz ampoules do not reduce VUV efficacy is particularly important: it means implants can remain sealed in packaging during VUV treatment, eliminating the sterility window that conventional UV creates. The 20–25% difference in decomposition between titanium and PMMA specimens shows TiO₂ photocatalysis is a minor contributor; the dominant mechanism is direct photolysis driven by photon energy (∝ 1/λ). Load-testing reveals VUV's key clinical advantage: unlike other sources, it maintains 40–52% decomposition regardless of contamination density, while UVC is near-ineffective at thin loads.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes VUV (172 nm) as markedly superior to all tested conventional UV sources for 1-minute organic decomposition on titanium.
- Separates photocatalytic (TiO₂-dependent) and photolytic (photon energy-dependent) mechanisms: photolysis dominates (~75–80%).
- Demonstrates quartz containment compatibility — solves the sterility problem of conventional UV (which requires implant unpacking).
- Identifies the load-efficacy curve difference: VUV is robust to contamination density; other sources are not.
- Sets upper bound on the photon-energy ladder: VUV (172 nm) > UVC (210–254 nm) > UVB (310 nm) for carbon removal.

## Methodology

Specimens: grade 4 cp-Ti and grade 5 Ti-6Al-4V (14 mm × 6 mm × 2 mm, machine-milled) and PMMA. Model organic: methylene blue (5 µL of 0.07%, air-dried 12 h). UV sources: UVC (low-pressure mercury, 254 nm, 1.2 mW/cm²), HUVC (TheraBeam Affiny, Ushio), PUV (SuperOsseo, Ushio), VUV (xenon excimer, 172 nm, ~60 mW/cm²; DIO, Busan). Containers: quartz ampoules vs. plastic tubes (open/closed). Measurement: residual MB dissolved in 800 µL ddH₂O, absorbance at 650 nm. Load test: 0.5–4× standard MB density. Statistics: n=3/group; one-way ANOVA + Bonferroni post hoc; regression analysis.

## Results

| UV source | Residual methylene blue @ 1 min | Decomposed |
|---|---|---|
| VUV (172 nm) | ~43% | **57%** |
| PUV (proprietary) | ~58% | 42% |
| HUVC (high-energy UVC) | ~64% | 36% |
| UVC (254 nm) | ~98% | 2% |

- VUV dose plateau at 60 s (negative exponential, R² = 0.93)
- Quartz vs. open: no significant difference in VUV efficacy; closed plastic: near-zero decomposition for all sources
- TiO₂ contribution: PMMA showed 20–25% less decomposition than Ti for all sources (p<0.05)
- Load test: VUV 40–52% stable across all densities; UVC near-zero at 0.5× density; PUV/HUVC plateau at high density
