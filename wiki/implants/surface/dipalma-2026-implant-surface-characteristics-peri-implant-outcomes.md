## Three-line Summary
Systematic review (PRISMA/PROSPERO #1301140, ROBINS-I) of 13 comparative human clinical studies (668 records screened → 13 included; 6 RCT, 3 prospective/controlled, 4 retrospective/pooled cohort; 18–600 patients, 35–630 implants) on implant surface characteristics and peri-implant clinical/radiographic/microbiological outcomes, PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science 2015–2025.
Short/medium-term RCTs found minimal marginal bone loss (MBL) differences across surface types; long-term retrospective cohorts found rough surfaces associated with higher peri-implantitis odds (Ferrantino: OR 4.877, 95% CI 1.701–13.980; Raes: 12/42 TiUnite vs 3/41 turned at 5 years, p<0.01), but patient/site factors (smoking, periodontitis history, maintenance) outweighed surface effects; microbiological/biomolecular data were scarce.
Clinical implication: surface modifications should be treated as adjunctive, not determinant, factors in peri-implant health — long-term risk assessment and supportive care matter more than surface choice.

## Summary
This systematic review (PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered #1301140, ROBINS-I risk-of-bias appraisal) pooled 13 comparative human clinical studies — 6 RCTs, 3 prospective/controlled studies, and 4 retrospective/pooled cohorts — screened from 668 records across PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science (2015–2025) to evaluate whether implant surface characteristics (topography, chemistry, wettability, coatings, ionic modifications, antibacterial strategies) predict peri-implant clinical, radiographic, and microbiological outcomes. Short- and medium-term RCTs found essentially comparable marginal bone loss (MBL) across surface types (SLA vs hydrophilic/chemically-activated variants, anodized/laser-microgrooved collars), while surface-related advantages were most evident in compromised scenarios such as low-density bone or early/immediate loading. In contrast, long-term retrospective and pooled cohorts found rough surfaces associated with higher odds of peri-implantitis, but patient- and site-related factors (smoking, history of periodontitis, maintenance compliance) exerted a stronger influence than surface type alone. Microbiological/biomolecular evidence remained sparse and heterogeneous. Clinically, the review concludes implant surface modifications should be regarded as adjunctive rather than determinant factors in long-term peri-implant health.

## Key Contributions
- First wiki-held systematic review of comparative clinical (not narrative) evidence testing whether implant surface type predicts peri-implant disease occurrence and MBL, spanning RCT, prospective, and retrospective designs.
- Reconciles the short-term "surfaces are clinically equivalent" RCT literature with the long-term "rough surfaces raise peri-implantitis odds" retrospective literature by framing the surface effect as real but secondary to patient/site risk factors.
- Identifies microbiological/biomolecular outcome data as a distinct, under-studied evidence gap separate from clinical/radiographic outcomes.
- Proposes concrete methodological standards for future trials: CONSORT adherence, prospective registration, ≥3–5 year follow-up when peri-implantitis incidence is the outcome, stratified randomization by smoking/periodontitis history, clustering-aware analysis, and ID-COSM core outcome sets.

## Methodology
Systematic review (no meta-analysis — heterogeneity precluded pooling) of comparative human clinical studies from PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science (1 Jan 2015 – 1 Dec 2025). 668 records → 129 duplicates removed → 539 screened → exclusions (15 animal, 72 in vitro/ex vivo, 22 SR/MA, 417 not aligned with objectives) → 13 studies included (18–600 patients, 35–630 implants). Quality appraised with ROBINS-I by two reviewers, third-reviewer consensus for disagreements. Outcomes: MBL, probing depth, bleeding on probing, peri-implantitis/mucositis incidence (primary); bacterial load, inflammatory mediators, implant stability quotient (ISQ) (secondary).

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Short/medium-term MBL (RCTs) | Comparable across surface types at 1 year (Ko 2019, Vílchez 2025) and up to 3 years (Guarnieri 2019); anodized/laser-microgrooved collars showed no consistent advantage (Longhi 2025) |
| Compromised-scenario benefit | Nano-superhydrophilic bioactive surfaces aided primary-to-secondary stability transition in low-density bone (Canullo 2024); thermo-chemically treated surfaces performed well under early/immediate loading (Albertini 2021) |
| Long-term peri-implantitis, split-mouth RCT | Raes 2018: 12/42 moderately rough TiUnite vs 3/41 minimally rough turned implants at 5 years (p<0.01); cumulative survival 100% vs 97.6% |
| Long-term peri-implantitis, pooled retrospective cohort | Ferrantino 2022 (630 implants, ~13-yr mean follow-up): rough surfaces (Sa > 1 µm) OR 4.877 (95% CI 1.701–13.980) vs machined, adjusted for smoking/location |
| Patient/site vs surface effect | Hussain 2024, Gnanajothi 2024: patient-related factors outweighed surface topography in predicting peri-implant outcomes |
| Microbiological evidence | Carinci 2019: antibacterial internal implant-abutment coating reduced early bacterial load; overall microbiological/biomolecular data scarce and heterogeneous across the 13 studies |
