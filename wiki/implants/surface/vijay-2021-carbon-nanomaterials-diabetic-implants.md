## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (QUT, Nanomaterials 2021) of carbon-nanomaterial-modified biomimetic dental implant surfaces as a strategy to counter implant failure in diabetic patients — bioinspired nanopatterned surfaces, polydopamine coatings, and graphene/GO, carbon nanotubes, nanodiamonds, carbon dots.

CNM coatings offer three mechanisms: sustained local insulin/drug delivery via π-π stacking (10 U topical insulin safe without altering blood glucose), contact-mediated antibacterial action (e.g., dragonfly-wing nanopillars lysing E. coli), and anti-inflammatory/immunoregulatory effects (GO-coated titanium raised osteogenic marker gene expression).

Preclinical/in-vitro evidence only — no clinical trials; carbon nanomaterial safety, coating durability, and translation to diabetic patients remain unresolved, so clinical decisions cannot rest on this tier alone.

## Summary

This narrative review proposes carbon-nanomaterial (CNM) coatings on biomimetic dental implant surfaces as a materials-side remedy for the elevated implant-failure risk of diabetic patients, in whom hyperglycemia impairs osseointegration and predisposes to infection/peri-implantitis. It surveys three mechanism classes: sustained local insulin and drug delivery (loaded onto carbon nanomaterial carriers via non-covalent π-π stacking), contact-mediated antibacterial surfaces (including bioinspired nanopillar topography that mechanically ruptures bacteria), and anti-inflammatory/osteoimmunomodulatory surfaces (e.g., graphene-oxide-coated titanium upregulating osteogenic markers). Supporting preclinical evidence — a diabetic-model reduction in bone-implant contact restored by insulin, pro-osteogenic TNT surfaces under high glucose, and orthotopic nanoparticle drug delivery in diabetic rats — is summarized in a table of surface coatings. The review is explicitly a strategy proposal: all cited data are in-vitro or animal, no clinical trials of CNM-coated implants in diabetics are available, and nanomaterial safety, coating durability, and regulatory pathways are left open.

## Key Contributions

- Frames dental implant failure in diabetes as a material-surface problem addressable by biomimetic, carbon-nanomaterial-functionalized coatings.
- Triple-function platform proposal: local insulin/drug delivery (π-π stacking) + antibacterial + anti-inflammatory/immunoregulatory.
- Polydopamine (mussel-inspired) as a universal anchoring layer binding graphene oxide, growth factors (bFGF, BMP-2), and drug carriers to titanium.
- Consolidates bioinspired antibacterial topography (cicada/dragonfly-wing nanopillars) as an implant-coating rationale.

## Methodology

Narrative (non-systematic) review by a QUT materials-engineering group. Evidence surveyed is predominantly in-vitro and animal: diabetic-rat osseointegration models, titanium-disc insulin-release studies, cell-culture osteogenic/immune assays on coated titanium, and bacterial-killing studies on nanopatterned surfaces. No pooled meta-analysis; outcomes reported as mechanism-level synthesis (drug delivery, antibacterial action, anti-inflammatory effect). No risk-of-bias or systematic search methodology.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Topical insulin safety | 10 U topical insulin safe without changing blood glucose; AgNP-sustained 50 µL insulin balanced wound inflammatory cytokines |
| Bone-implant contact (BIC) in diabetes | Reduced in diabetic experimental model; restored to ~control (osteoid/bone matrix production) with insulin therapy |
| Insulin-coated titanium discs | Controlled insulin release beneficial for mineralization (Malekzadeh et al.) |
| TNT (TiO2 nanotube) surface in DM | Good biocompatibility + pro-osteogenic activity; lower ROS, higher total SOD — less high-glucose osteogenesis inhibition (Yang et al.) |
| Graphene-oxide (GO)–coated titanium | Increased osteogenic-marker gene expression; immunoregulatory effect on osteogenesis (Su et al.) |
| AntagomiR204-AuNP/PLGA in diabetic rats | Restored miR204 misexpression; promoted osseointegration of titanium implant |
| Bioinspired nanopattern (dragonfly wing) | Mechanical rupture/lysis of E. coli (SEM-confirmed) |
| Clinical trials of CNM-coated implants | None in diabetics (review tier: preclinical only) |
