## Three-line Summary
Systematic review (PRISMA 2020, no meta-analysis; 25 studies: 4 human clinical [2 RCT, 2 non-randomized], 10 in vivo animal, 1 in vitro, 10 combined in vitro/in vivo) comparing nanocoated/nanostructured titanium implant surfaces (TiO2 nanotubes, HA/Ca-P, Ag, Sr, Zn/Mg, graphene/nanospikes) against conventional microrough (chiefly SLA/machined) titanium controls.
21/25 studies favored the nanosurface on ≥1 osseointegration metric (e.g., Ko et al. 2024 RCT: ISQ ~76.5 vs 71.3 at 2 months, ~79.1 vs 73.4 at 4 months, both p<0.01/p<0.0001; Li et al. 2023 dog model: BIC 76.6% vs 44.1% at 12 weeks for Sr-loaded vs plain TiO2-nanotube); gains are largest at 2–4 weeks and commonly converge with microrough controls by 8–12 weeks; GRADE certainty for patient-important clinical outcomes (MBL, survival) is low to very low.
Nanofeatures accelerate early osseointegration and can add antibacterial activity without cytotoxicity, but no consistent long-term (≥12-month) survival/marginal-bone-loss advantage over contemporary microrough titanium is demonstrated — the authors call for adequately powered, preregistered ≥3–5-year head-to-head RCTs.

## Summary
This PRISMA 2020 systematic review evaluated whether nanocoated or nanostructured titanium dental implant surfaces improve osseointegration compared with conventional microrough titanium controls, drawing on 25 studies (4 human clinical, 10 in vivo animal, 1 in vitro, 10 combined) spanning TiO2 nanotube, hydroxyapatite/calcium-phosphate, silver, strontium, zinc/magnesium, and graphene/nanospike surface chemistries. No meta-analysis was performed due to methodologic heterogeneity; evidence was synthesized narratively and appraised with a risk-of-bias battery (RoB 2, ROBINS-I, RoBDEMAT, OHAT, SYRCLE) plus reporting-quality checklists (CAMARADES, CRIME-Q, MIRIBEL, ARRIVE 2.0) and GRADE for human comparative outcomes. The dominant pattern is time-dependent: nanofeatured surfaces reproducibly raise early bone-to-implant contact (BIC), implant stability quotient (ISQ), and removal/push-out torque at 2–4 weeks, but differences commonly narrow or vanish by 8–12 weeks as microrough controls catch up, and GRADE certainty for patient-important outcomes (marginal bone loss, survival) remains low to very low. Antimicrobial nanocoatings (silver, nanospikes, Zn/Mg) reduced bacterial colonization without compromising cytocompatibility or osseointegration in most studies. The authors conclude nanofeatured surfaces are most clinically relevant when early stability is decisive (immediate/early loading, low-density D3–D4 bone, short implants) rather than as a general replacement for contemporary microrough titanium.

## Key Contributions
- First systematic evaluation unifying clinical, in vivo animal, and in vitro nanofeatured-titanium-vs-microrough-control osseointegration evidence under one risk-of-bias and reporting-quality framework
- GRADE appraisal of human clinical outcomes: low certainty for MBL/soft-tissue indices, very low for infection/survival — insufficient to claim clinical superiority of nanocoated over uncoated implants
- Narrative synthesis by coating class shows the consistent signal is early-phase acceleration, not durable superiority, across TiO2 nanotubes, HA/Ca-P, Ag, Sr, Zn/Mg, and graphene/nanospike chemistries
- Establishes a time-course pattern (peak difference at 2–4 weeks, convergence by 8–12 weeks in most models) that reframes how early surrogate measurements (ISQ, BIC) should be interpreted clinically
- Documents that antimicrobial nanocoatings (Ag, nanospikes, Zn/Mg) reduced bacterial colonization (up to 70–90% bactericidal efficiency) without cytotoxicity or loss of osseointegration in the included studies

## Methodology
PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review; PubMed-Medline, Scopus, and Web of Science searched Jan 2016–Aug 2025, supplemented by an Elicit/Semantic Scholar search. 1171 records → 924 screened at title/abstract → 357 full texts assessed → 25 studies included (4 human clinical [2 RCT, 2 non-randomized], 10 in vivo animal, 1 in vitro, 10 combined in vitro/in vivo). Outcomes: BIC (17/25 studies), bone density/volume (~10 studies), removal/push-out torque (4 studies), ISQ (clinical subset), osteogenic gene expression/ALP, antimicrobial/biofilm assays, cytotoxicity. Risk of bias: RoB 2 (RCTs), ROBINS-I (non-randomized), RoBDEMAT (in vitro), OHAT Tier 1–3 + SYRCLE (animal; 0/4/13 studies Tier 1/2/3). GRADE applied only to human comparative outcomes; no meta-analysis performed.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Studies favoring nanosurface on ≥1 metric | 21/25 (2 null: Scarano 2020 Ag-ion, Hall 2019 anodized abutment; 2 mixed: Chappuis 2018, Ko 2024) |
| ISQ, HA-nanocoated SLA vs SLA (Ko et al. 2024, RCT) | ~76.5 vs 71.3 at 2 months (p<0.01); ~79.1 vs 73.4 at 4 months (p<0.0001); similar bone level |
| BIC, Sr-loaded nanotextured vs TiO2-nanotube (Li et al. 2023, dog) | 76.6% vs 44.1% at 12 weeks; bone volume fraction 54.9%±9.97 vs 28.8%±5.9 |
| BIC, micro/nanofeatured vs control (Rousseau et al. 2021, sheep) | 79.3%/86.4% (4/13 wk) vs 68.3%/74.8% — one of the few non-converging models |
| Reverse torque, HA nanoparticle vs blasted-only (Toscano et al. 2024, osteoporotic rat) | 4.75±2.22 vs 1.5±0.58 N·cm (p=0.03) |
| BIC/bone density, AgNP-coated (Memon et al. 2025, clinical) | p=0.013 / p=0.012, 0.006; IL-6/CRP lower (p=0.015); P. aeruginosa adhesion reduced (p=0.009) |
