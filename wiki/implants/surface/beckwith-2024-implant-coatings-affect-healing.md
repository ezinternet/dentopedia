## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (11 systematic reviews included) using PICOS/PROSPERO-registered protocol, AMSTAR 2 and ROBIS quality/bias assessment, evaluating whether dental implant surface coatings (calcium phosphate/hydroxyapatite, bisphosphonate, bioactive glass) affect healing outcomes.

Weak evidence that coatings (chiefly HA/calcium phosphate) improve osseointegration and reduce marginal bone loss (MBL); weak evidence for progressive complications with calcium phosphate coatings; included reviews showed considerable heterogeneity, small sample sizes, low confidence, and high risk of bias.

Abstract-only — full text not retrieved; current evidence is too weak/heterogeneous to guide coating selection, and long-term multicenter controlled trials with standardized protocols are needed.

## Summary

This umbrella review (a systematic review of systematic reviews) asked whether dental implant surface coatings affect healing outcomes. Using a PICOS-structured question and a PROSPERO-registered protocol (CRD42022321926), the authors searched MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, and ScienceDirect, screened studies in duplicate, and appraised the 11 included systematic reviews with AMSTAR 2 (methodological quality) and ROBIS (risk of bias). The most commonly evaluated coatings were calcium phosphate-based — hydroxyapatite (HA), brushite, and bioabsorbable nano-HA — followed by bisphosphonate and then bioactive glass coatings. Outcomes most frequently assessed across the included reviews were marginal bone loss (MBL), bone-to-implant contact (BIC), and implant survival/success rates. The overall evidence base showed considerable heterogeneity and small sample sizes, and the quality appraisal found low confidence in the reviews with high risk of bias. The authors' bottom line: weak evidence that coatings improve osseointegration and reduce MBL, and weak evidence of progressive complications specifically with calcium phosphate coatings — with a call for long-term, multicenter, standardized RCTs before firmer conclusions can be drawn. This ingestion is abstract-only (no PMC full text available for JOMI); numeric effect estimates per outcome/coating type are not available beyond what the abstract reports.

## Key Contributions

- Synthesizes 11 systematic reviews on implant coatings at the umbrella-review level, rather than re-analyzing primary studies directly.
- Applies AMSTAR 2 + ROBIS jointly to grade the quality and bias of the underlying SR evidence base itself — a meta-level appraisal above what individual SRs typically self-report.
- Identifies calcium phosphate/HA coatings as the dominant, most-studied coating category, with bisphosphonate and bioactive glass coatings as secondary categories.
- Flags a specific safety signal (weak evidence of progressive complications with calcium phosphate coatings) alongside the efficacy signal, rather than reporting benefit alone.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Umbrella review (SR of SRs), PROSPERO-registered (CRD42022321926), PICOS-structured question.
- **Search**: MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, ScienceDirect.
- **Screening**: Independent, duplicate, two-stage (title/abstract then full text).
- **Quality/bias appraisal**: AMSTAR 2 (SR methodological quality) and ROBIS (risk of bias), both independent and duplicate.
- **Data extraction**: Independent, duplicate, predefined extraction form.
- **Included evidence**: 11 systematic reviews, evaluating calcium phosphate/HA (incl. brushite, bioabsorbable nano-HA), bisphosphonate, and bioactive glass coatings.

## Results

- 11 systematic reviews included; most-studied coating category = calcium phosphate-based (HA, brushite, bioabsorbable nano-HA), followed by bisphosphonate, then bioactive glass.
- Most-assessed outcomes across included reviews: marginal bone loss (MBL), bone-to-implant contact (BIC), survival/success rates.
- Considerable heterogeneity and small sample sizes in the underlying primary-study evidence.
- Quality/bias appraisal: low confidence in the included reviews, high risk of bias overall.
- Net conclusion: weak evidence coatings improve osseointegration and reduce MBL; weak evidence of progressive complications for calcium phosphate coatings specifically; long-term multicenter controlled trials with better standardization/bias control are needed.
