## Three-line Summary
Narrative review of Ti dental-implant surface nanotopography at the transmucosal region — fabrication techniques (photolithography, electron-beam lithography, anodization, phase separation, nanoimprinting), topography-driven soft-tissue integration, antibacterial mechanisms, and topography-mediated immunomodulation (M1→M2 macrophage polarization).
Nanotopography at 10–100 nm prevents bacterial adhesion (mechano-bactericidal stretching/rupture + spatial confinement); ~30 nm TiO2 nanotubes drive M2 (anti-inflammatory) macrophage polarization while 80–100 nm drive M1 (pro-inflammatory); silver-containing nanotubular surfaces are antibacterial and significantly decrease fibrous encapsulation in vivo; Ra <0.2 µm (Bollen) = plaque no-promotion threshold.
Evidence is preclinical (in vitro + rat/rabbit/dog/goat models) with few human trials — authors call for long-term in vivo/human studies including compromised patients (diabetes, osteoporosis); no clinical superiority over microrough controls is established.

## 세줄요약
내러티브 문헌고찰: 티타늄 (Titanium, Ti) 임플란트 경점막부 (Transmucosal) 나노형태 (Nanotopography) 공정(포토리소그래피·전자빔리소그래피·양극산화·상분리·나노임프린팅), 연조직 통합, 항균 기전, 표면 매개 면역조절 (M1→M2 대식세포 편극).
나노스케일(10–100 nm) 구조는 기계적 살균(막 신장·파열)과 공간구속으로 세균 부착을 억제; 30 nm 이산화티타늄 (Titanium dioxide, TiO2) 나노튜브는 항염증형 M2, 80–100 nm는 염증형 M1 유도; 은(Ag) 함유 나노튜브 표면은 항균과 함께 생체내 섬유성 피막 (Fibrous Encapsulation) 형성을 유의 감소; 플라크 무형성 거칠기 임계치는 Ra <0.2 µm (Bollen).
근거 대부분 시험관내·실험동물(쥐·토끼·개·염소) 수준이고 인체 임상은 소수 — 장기 생체내·인체 연구, 특히 당뇨·골다공증 환자를 포함한 검증이 필요하다고 결론.

## Summary
This narrative review argues that engineering Ti implant surface nanotopography is a single lever on both soft-tissue integration and antibacterial defense at the transmucosal region, and that topography-mediated immunomodulation (macrophage M1→M2 shift) is the connecting mechanism. After summarizing tissue response (peri-implant epithelium hemidesmosome attachment, gingival-fibroblast contact guidance, perpendicular collagen orientation) and fabrication methods (photolithography, electron-beam lithography, anodization, phase separation, nanoimprinting), it consolidates the nanotube-diameter evidence: ~30 nm TiO2 nanotubes shift macrophages toward the anti-inflammatory M2/M2c phenotype whereas 80–100 nm drive pro-inflammatory M1 and favor osteoclasia. Antibacterial effects are attributed to mechano-bactericidal nanopillars/nanospears that rupture bacterial membranes, spatial confinement at the 10–100 nm scale, and drug/ion-loaded nanotubular reservoirs (rifampicin/amoxicillin-PLGA, silver/zinc nanoparticles), with silver-containing nanotubular topography reported to significantly decrease fibrous encapsulation in vivo. The clinical implication is a future "immune-informed" implant that resists peri-implant infection and integrates soft tissue, but the review itself is non-systematic and preclinical-dominated: human evidence is scarce, translation is blocked by sterilization and particle-release questions, and long-term in vivo/human studies in compromised populations are explicitly called for.

## Key Contributions
- Consolidates the dual-axis framework — one nanotopographic surface steering both soft-tissue integration (epithelial/fibroblast/collagen) and antibacterial function (mechano-bactericidal + local drug delivery) at the transmucosal region.
- Condenses the nanotube-diameter → macrophage-polarization literature: ~30 nm → M2/M2c (anti-inflammatory), 80–100 nm → M1 (pro-inflammatory) with mechanistic pathways (RhoA/ROCK, MAPK, NF-κB, integrin-FAK-PI3Kγ) and an osteogenesis/osteoclasia balance consequence.
- Supplies a specific citable claim the wiki lacked: silver-containing nanotubular topography was antibacterial against S. aureus (membrane damage) and significantly decreased fibrous encapsulation in peri-implant soft tissue in vivo.
- Catalogs the commercialization blockers for nanotopographic surfaces: sterilization-induced topography changes (autoclave compromises anodized TiO2 nanotubes), long-term Ti-particle release, mechanical stability/delamination, and regulatory (FDA/CE) timelines.

## Methodology
Non-systematic narrative review with an expert-opinion conclusion; ~187 cited in vitro + animal (Sprague-Dawley/Lewis rats, rabbits, beagle dogs, goats, mice) and in vitro human-tissue studies; 9 completed clinical-trial identifiers (clinicaltrials.gov) tabulated. No search protocol, eligibility criteria, or risk-of-bias appraisal reported. Outcomes summarized across soft-tissue integration (epithelial attachment, fibroblast alignment, collagen orientation, connective-tissue length), antibacterial/biofilm metrics, macrophage phenotype, and fibrous encapsulation.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Nanofabrication features | Photolithography 50 nm (600 nm gratings); EBL nanopits 120 nm / nanodots 12 nm; anodization 15–100 nm; nanoimprinting pits 50 nm |
| Soft-tissue sealing (Chen et al., goat tibia) | Anodized > non-anodized Ti peri-implant soft-tissue sealing at 8 weeks |
| Collagen orientation (Kato et al., rabbit) | Alkali-heat nano spikes/pores → perpendicular collagen attachment to implant, 8 weeks |
| Connective tissue (Wang et al., beagle, 4 wk) | Anodized nanotubular abutment ↑ connective-tissue length + perpendicular collagen vs machined |
| Antibacterial scale | Nanostructures 10–100 nm impede bacterial adhesion; higher micro-roughness ↑ adhesion (TiUnite highest roughness → highest in vivo adhesion) |
| Roughness thresholds | Ra <0.4 µm recommended (Li); Ra <0.2 µm = plaque no-promotion (Bollen) |
| Macrophage phenotype by nanotube diameter | ~30 nm → M2/M2c (anti-inflammatory); 80–100 nm → M1 (pro-inflammatory, osteoclasia bias, intense inflammation) |
| Drug-loaded surfaces | Rifampicin-PLGA/TiO2-nanotube inhibited S. epidermidis; amoxicillin-PLGA on anodized Ti vs S. aureus & S. epidermidis; Ag-nanotube vs S. aureus; ZnO/Ag-nanotube antifungal C. albicans/C. parapsilosis + anti-S. mutans |
| Fibrous encapsulation | Ag-containing nanotubular topography → significant decrease in fibrous encapsulation in peri-implant soft tissue in vivo |
| Soft-tissue integration timing | 6–8 weeks — vulnerable window for post-surgical pathogen-mediated inflammation |
