## Three-line Summary
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 preclinical animal studies (rabbit/rat, tibial/femoral sites, healing 2–24 weeks) comparing magnesium-modified/Mg-coated titanium implants against uncoated titanium controls, across four Mg-delivery strategies (ion implantation, Mg-substituted HA coatings, Mg-loaded mesoporous titania, Mg-incorporated nanotubes).
Pooled bone-to-implant contact (BIC) was significantly higher for Mg-modified implants (Z=4.38, p<0.001); pooled bone area (BA) showed no significant difference (Z=0.93, p=0.35), though a trend toward higher BA appeared in studies with ≥8-week healing.
Mg incorporation appears to accelerate early-stage contact osteogenesis (BIC) more reliably than it changes overall peri-implant bone volume (BA); all evidence is small-animal, non-loaded, and preclinical — no human/clinical data yet exist for Mg-modified implants.

## Summary
This systematic review and meta-analysis (INPLASY202610101, PRISMA-guided) pooled 11 preclinical animal studies evaluating magnesium (Mg)-modified or Mg-coated titanium implants against uncoated titanium controls in rabbit and rat models (tibial/femoral sites, 2–24 week healing periods). Four Mg-delivery strategies were represented: Mg-ion implantation, Mg-substituted hydroxyapatite (HA) coatings, Mg-loaded mesoporous titania layers, and Mg-incorporated nanotubular structures. Study quality was generally high (ARRIVE 2.0: 8 excellent, 3 average) with low-to-moderate risk of bias (SYRCLE). The meta-analysis found a statistically significant improvement in bone-to-implant contact (BIC) for Mg-modified implants (Z=4.38, p<0.001), but no significant pooled difference in bone area (BA) (Z=0.93, p=0.35) — though a trend toward greater BA appeared with longer healing (≥8 weeks) and with controlled-release coating designs (mesoporous titania, nanotubes). The authors interpret this BIC/BA divergence as reflecting different biological timescales: BIC captures early, localized contact osteogenesis near the Mg-releasing surface, while BA reflects broader regional bone remodeling that may take longer to shift. Proposed mechanisms include Mg-driven activation of Wnt/β-catenin and VEGF/HIF-1α pathways and M2 macrophage polarization. All evidence remains preclinical, small-animal, and non-functionally-loaded, so clinical translation requires large-animal and human trials.

## Key Contributions
- First quantitative meta-analysis (not merely narrative synthesis) of Mg-modified titanium implant osseointegration in preclinical animal studies.
- Separately pools BIC and BA outcomes, showing they respond differently to Mg modification — a distinction (early contact osteogenesis vs. regional bone volume) with direct relevance for interpreting other implant-coating meta-analyses.
- Surveys and contrasts four distinct Mg-delivery/coating architectures, discussing how release kinetics likely drive outcome heterogeneity.
- Uses animal-research-specific quality/bias tools (ARRIVE 2.0, SYRCLE) rather than generic clinical-SR appraisal tools.

## Methodology
Systematic review + meta-analysis of 11 preclinical animal studies (rabbit/rat, tibial/femoral implant sites, 2–24 week healing), PRISMA-guided and prospectively registered (INPLASY202610101). Searched PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science (May 2025) for English-language animal studies from 2005 onward comparing Mg-modified/Mg-coated Ti implants against uncoated Ti controls with matched surface characteristics. Quality assessed with ARRIVE 2.0 (21-item checklist); risk of bias assessed with SYRCLE. Meta-analysis used weighted mean differences (implants as unit of analysis), I² heterogeneity statistic, random-effects (inverse-variance) or fixed-effects models as appropriate, computed in RevMan v5.3.3.
