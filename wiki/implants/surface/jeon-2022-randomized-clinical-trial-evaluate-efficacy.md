## Three-line Summary

Randomized clinical trial analyzed by record review (J Korean Assoc Oral Maxillofac Surg 2022;48:225-231, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital; 55 patients, 80 implants registered, 75 analyzed) comparing two tapered internal-hex SLA implants that differ in surface roughness: Ra 3.09 µm (Sewon Medics IH2) vs Ra 2.50 µm (Osstem TSIII SA).

Primary ISQ 79.7 vs 82.9, secondary 82.1 vs 84.4; success and survival 100% in both groups, MBL 0.07 vs 0.00 mm (P = 0.0778), and the only soft-tissue difference was a lower plaque index in the rougher group (0.00 vs 0.29, P = 0.0004).

A 0.6 µm difference in Ra within the SLA family changed nothing clinically — stability, bone loss, success and safety were equivalent, so roughness above the established SLA benchmark is not a defensible selection criterion at 1 year.

## Summary

This trial takes the roughness-optimization argument seriously enough to test it in patients: two tapered, internal-hex, 11° Morse-taper, submerged SLA implants — one at Ra 3.09 µm, one at the widely used Ra 2.50 µm — placed in 55 patients and followed for a year. Nothing separated them except plaque. ISQ differed by about 3 points in favour of the smoother surface at both time points but by a margin the authors themselves report as non-significant (P > 0.999, reflecting the categorical <60 cut-off rather than a continuous test); MBL was 0.07 mm versus 0.00 mm (P = 0.0778), success and survival were 100% in both groups, and PPD, GI and BOP were indistinguishable. The single significant finding — plaque index 0.00 versus 0.29 in favour of the rougher surface — runs against the usual expectation that rougher surfaces retain more biofilm, which the paper does not explain. The practical reading is that within the established SLA range, roughness is not a lever a clinician can pull for better outcomes; the accompanying caveat is that the design is a record review of randomized treatment, so it tests allocation rather than prospectively blinded follow-up.

## Key Contributions

- Provides 1-year clinical evidence that a 0.6 µm Ra increment inside the SLA family does not change stability, marginal bone loss, success, survival or safety.
- Contradicts the bench-derived notion that a particular optimum Ra (Knabe's 3.43 µm) should drive implant selection.
- Reports one case of primary ISQ <60 that recovered to stable secondary stability, a small but useful datum for the "unstable start" decision.
- Isolates roughness with two otherwise matched designs (tapered, internal hex, 11° Morse, submerged, SLA) — as close to a controlled roughness experiment as clinical research gets, though the two come from different manufacturers.

## Methodology

**Design.** Randomized clinical trial, analyzed as a clinical record review; single centre (Seoul National University Bundang Hospital).

**Sample.** 55 patients (mean age 53.0 years), 80 SLA implants registered: 38 implants / 29 subjects at Ra 3.09 µm (test, Sewon Medics IH2) and 42 implants / 31 subjects at Ra 2.50 µm (control, Osstem TSIII SA). Five patients received both types. Analyzed after dropout: 37 test / 38 control implants (2 controls excluded for >3 missing teeth 8 weeks before implantation).

**Baseline.** No significant differences in sex, age, severe clenching/bruxism (none in either group), drinking, or smoking.

**Outcomes at 1 year.** Primary and secondary ISQ; success and survival; MBL; soft tissue (PPD, PI, GI, BOP).

## Results

| Parameter | Test (Ra 3.09 µm, n=37) | Control (Ra 2.50 µm, n=38) | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary ISQ | 79.7 ± 10.22 | 82.9 ± 10.00 | >0.999 |
| Primary ISQ <60 (n) | 1 | 0 | >0.999 |
| Secondary ISQ | 82.1 ± 11.12 | 84.4 ± 10.31 | >0.999 |
| Success / survival | 100% / 100% | 100% / 100% | — |
| MBL (mm) | 0.07 | 0.00 | 0.0778 |
| Plaque index | 0.00 | 0.29 | **0.0004** |
| PPD / GI / BOP | NS | NS | NS |

**Site distribution.** Test: 9 maxillary (4 premolar, 5 molar), 28 mandibular (5 premolar, 23 molar). Control: 10 maxillary (2 incisor, 3 premolar, 5 molar), 28 mandibular (5 premolar, 23 molar). Both groups dominated by mandibular molars.

**Unstable-start case.** One test implant had primary ISQ <60; no test or control implant had secondary ISQ <60, so the case recovered without intervention.

## Limitations

Outcome data were collected by clinical record review of a randomized allocation — not a prospectively blinded follow-up, so measurement and ascertainment bias are uncontrolled. One-year horizon: surface roughness effects on MBL and peri-implantitis are late-emerging. Small sample, skewed to mandibular molars, no reported power calculation. The two implants come from different manufacturers, so thread geometry, heat treatment and cleaning are confounded with Ra; "only roughness differs" is the authors' characterization, not a demonstrated equivalence. The paradoxical plaque result is reported without mechanistic explanation.
