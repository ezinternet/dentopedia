## Three-line Summary
Systematic review and meta-analysis (INPLASY-registered, PRISMA) of 26 preclinical animal studies (1,109 titanium implants; peptide/BMP/ECM-collagen biomolecule coatings vs uncoated etched controls) pooling bone-to-implant contact (BIC) at 4 weeks post-placement.
Random-effects meta-analysis (RevMan 5.4.1) found only one result favorable to coating (in the BMP subgroup); across grouped studies there was no significant difference between coated and uncoated implants, with very high heterogeneity (I²=99%) and funnel-plot evidence of publication bias.
Biomolecule surface coatings may favor osseointegration in early healing stages qualitatively, but the meta-analytic evidence is inconclusive and heterogeneous — long-term, better-standardized studies (and eventually clinical trials) are needed before any clinical recommendation.

## Summary
This 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated whether biomolecule-based bioactive surface modifications of titanium dental implants improve osseointegration, pooling 26 preclinical animal studies (1,109 implants) across three coating categories: peptides (5 studies), bone morphogenetic proteins/BMPs (14 studies), and extracellular-matrix/collagen products (7 studies). Using a random-effects meta-analysis of bone-to-implant contact (BIC) measured at 4 weeks post-placement, the authors found essentially no significant pooled advantage of coated over uncoated (SLA-type etched) surfaces — only a single BMP-subgroup comparison favored coating — against a backdrop of very high statistical heterogeneity (I²=99%) and funnel-plot evidence of publication bias. Applying SYRCLE risk-of-bias and ARRIVE reporting-quality tools, the review also exposed systematic underreporting in the underlying animal literature (0/26 studies reported 3Rs compliance or adverse events). The authors conclude that organic bioactive-molecule surface modification may favor early-stage osseointegration in principle, but that current preclinical evidence is too heterogeneous to support firm clinical conclusions, and call for longer, more standardized studies and eventual human trials.

## Key Contributions
- First meta-analytic (not purely narrative) synthesis of BIC outcomes for biomolecule-based (peptide/BMP/ECM-collagen) implant surface coatings, using RevMan random-effects modeling across 26 preclinical studies.
- Rigorous methodological appraisal (SYRCLE RoB + ARRIVE 23-item checklist, mean 16.5±1.5/23) documenting systematic quality gaps in the animal-study evidence base underlying bioactive-coating claims.
- Quantifies the gap between qualitative optimism about biomolecule coatings (seen in prior narrative reviews) and the actual pooled statistical evidence: near-total absence of significant benefit, extreme heterogeneity (I²=99%), and publication bias.

## Methodology
INPLASY-registered, PRISMA-guided systematic review with PICOS framework (animal models receiving biomolecule-coated titanium implants vs conventional etched/SLA-type controls, outcome = BIC). Searched PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science, and EMBASE to May 2022 (10,697 initial hits → 84 title/abstract screened → 26 full-text included, two independent reviewers, Cohen's κ=1). Meta-analysis performed in RevMan 5.4.1 using a random-effects model (mean difference/SD) due to expected methodological heterogeneity, with I²>50% deemed significant heterogeneity and a funnel plot to assess publication bias. Risk of bias assessed with the animal-study-adapted SYRCLE Cochrane RoB tool; reporting quality assessed with the 23-item ARRIVE checklist.

|---|---|
| Studies / implants | 26 studies, 1,109 implants |
| Coating categories | Peptides (5 studies), BMPs (14 studies), ECM/collagen (7 studies) |
