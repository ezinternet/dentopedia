## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (J Maxillofac Oral Surg 2025;24:886–898, 65 citations) consolidating single-tooth implant treatment planning into four anatomic regions — maxillary anterior/posterior, mandibular anterior/posterior — with region-specific bone density, dimensional minimums, and occlusal priorities.

Universal dimensional minimums are tabulated: mesiodistal space 6–7 mm, buccolingual ridge width ≥6 mm (≥1.5 mm bone on every side), interocclusal clearance 5–7 mm, ≥1.5 mm to the adjacent tooth root, ≥3 mm interimplant distance, and ≥3 mm implant shoulder to free gingival margin; the "triangle of bone" concept places the fixture to bisect buccolingual bone volume rather than follow the socket trajectory.

Region priorities diverge sharply: maxillary anterior is D3 with ~2 mm facial bone and platform 3–4 mm apical to the gingival margin (esthetic-driven); maxillary posterior is D4 requiring under-drilling by ~0.5 mm, ≥10 mm vertical bone or sinus elevation, and >4–6 months healing; mandibular anterior is D1–D2 needing 3–4 months only but risking thermal osteonecrosis; mandibular posterior is D2 with a 2 mm IAN safety margin, 5 mm from the mental foramen, a 1:1 crown-to-implant ratio, and reported success >95%.

Clinical value is a single-page regional decision aid; the principal limitation is that it is a narrative synthesis with no pooled effect sizes, no systematic search strategy, and no patient-level outcome data — its papilla (crest-to-contact ≤5 mm) and success (>95%) figures are inherited from cited primary studies rather than generated.

## 세줄요약

서술적 고찰(Narrative Review, J Maxillofac Oral Surg 2025;24:886–898, 인용 65편): 단일치 임플란트 치료계획을 상악 전치·후치, 하악 전치·후치 4개 부위로 분해해 부위별 골밀도(Misch D 등급), 치수 최소 기준, 교합 우선순위를 정리한 임상 가이드.

보편 기준 — 근원심 간격 6–7 mm, 협순골 치조 폭 ≥6 mm(모든 면에 ≥1.5 mm 잔존골), 교합 간 여유 5–7 mm, 인접치 치근까지 ≥1.5 mm, 임플란트 간 ≥3 mm, 플랫폼에서 자유치연까지 ≥3 mm. "뼈의 삼각형(triangle of bone)" 개념으로 소켓 궤적 대신 협순골 부피를 이등분하도록 배치.

부위별 우선순위 상이 — 상악 전치는 D3, 안면골 ~2 mm, 치은연 3–4 mm 아피칼 플랫폼(심미 우선); 상악 후치는 D4로 최종 드릴을 임플란트보다 ~0.5 mm 언더사이즈, 수직골 ≥10 mm 또는 상악동 거상술 필요, 치유 >4–6개월; 하악 전치는 D1–D2로 3–4개월이면 충분하나 열괴사 위험; 하악 후치는 D2, 하악관 2 mm·mental foramen 5 mm 안전거리, 크라운:임플란트 1:1, 보고 성적률 >95%.

임상 가치는 한 장짜리 부위별 의사결정 표이며, 핵심 한계는 효과크기 풀링·체계적 검색 전략·환자 수준 결과가 없는 **서술적 종합**이라는 점이다. 치대(<5 mm)·성공률(>95%) 수치는 인용된 1차 연구에서 차용한 것이지 본 논문에서 산출한 값이 아니다.

## Summary

This narrative review structures single-tooth implant treatment planning as a prosthetically driven workflow in which the diagnostic wax-up and final crown position dictate implant placement within the alveolar bone, then decomposes the resulting criteria by anatomic region. It assigns a typical Misch bone density to each of the four single-tooth sites (maxillary anterior D3, maxillary posterior D4, mandibular anterior D1–D2, mandibular posterior D2) and links each to a drilling, dimension, and loading strategy — under-preparation by ~0.5 mm and prolonged healing in D4 maxillary posterior bone versus stepped drilling, tapping, and only 3–4 months of healing in D1–D2 anterior mandible. The operative content sits in nine tables: universal dimensional minimums (mesiodistal 6–7 mm, buccolingual ≥6 mm, interocclusal 5–7 mm, ≥1.5 mm to adjacent root, ≥3 mm interimplant, ≥3 mm shoulder-to-gingival-margin), four region summaries, protocol comparisons, and an occlusal scheme table. The unifying clinical message is that esthetics dominates planning in the maxillary anterior region while occlusal load and biomechanics dominate posteriorly, so a single protocol applied uniformly across sites is wrong. Its usefulness is as a chairside checklist; its weakness is that every number is transcribed from a cited primary study with no systematic search, no pooled estimates, and no patient-level outcome data of its own.

## Key Contributions

- **4-region decision framework** — the explicit "universal principles vs. site-specific priorities" split, with maxillary anterior identified as esthetics-led (high esthetic demand, thin buccal plate, nasopalatine canal, papilla-critical) and posterior sites as biomechanics-led (sinus proximity in the maxilla, IAN canal / mental foramen / submandibular fossa in the mandible).
- **"Triangle of bone" placement concept** — on the CBCT cross-section the fixture should bisect the triangle formed by the buccolingual crestal width (base) and the ridge center (apex), aligning the implant with the denser, more voluminous bone trajectory rather than the natural socket trajectory, which risks malalignment or buccal plate perforation.
- **Quantitative dimensional standard** — one consolidated table set fixing 6–7 mm mesiodistal space, ≥6 mm buccolingual width with ≥1.5 mm bone all around, 5–7 mm interocclusal space, ≥1.5 mm to the adjacent root, ≥3 mm between implants, and ≥3 mm from implant shoulder to free gingival margin.
- **Bone-density-driven surgical adaptation** — site-specific drilling logic: under-drilling ~0.5 mm undersize in maxillary posterior D4 bone (or tapered/deep-thread implants) for primary stability; stepped drilling with copious irrigation plus bone tapping in D1/D2 bone to avoid heat-induced osteonecrosis and high insertion torque microfractures.
- **Anatomical safety margins** — ≥10 mm vertical bone or sinus elevation in the posterior maxilla with a 1–2 mm sinus-floor buffer; 2 mm between implant apex and the inferior alveolar canal; implant posterior edge ≥5 mm anterior to the mental foramen; awareness of the anterior mental nerve loop and the sublingual/submandibular lingual concavity.
- **Esthetic-zone thresholds** — bone crest to restoration contact point ≤5 mm for papilla fill (≥6 mm yields papilla in only ~50% of cases); implant platform 3–4 mm apical to the planned gingival margin in the maxillary anterior; ≥2 mm facial bone to hold the soft-tissue profile; narrower implants (~3.0–3.5 mm) for the maxillary lateral incisor with screw access emerging at the cingulum rather than labially.
- **Occlusal scheme for the single implant** — centralized light centric contact, minimized or eliminated excursive contacts, narrow occlusal table and flat cusp inclines, canine guidance or mutually protected occlusion with posterior disclusion, axial loading without cantilevers, a ~1:1 crown-to-implant ratio, and a night guard for bruxers.

## Methodology

Narrative review / clinical guide with no systematic-search component. Databases, search strings, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and study counts are not reported; 65 references are cited, dominated by classic implant texts (Misch, Contemporary Implant Dentistry) and pre-2010 reviews plus a few 2022–2025 primary and review papers. No risk-of-bias assessment, no quantitative synthesis, no meta-analysis, and no patient-level outcomes. The architecture is pre-treatment evaluation (systemic factors, clinical examination, radiographic assessment with CBCT and Hounsfield units, diagnostic aids including vacuum-formed stents and digital planning software) → surgical considerations for each of the four regions → surgical protocols (one-stage vs. two-stage, flap vs. flapless, immediate placement, immediate loading) → prosthetic considerations (space requirements, abutment selection, material choice, occlusal management, parafunction) → conclusion. Nine tables and five figures carry essentially all of the operative content, which is what makes the paper usable as a chairside reference despite its low formal evidence weight.

### Regional prerequisites (Tables 2–6)

| Region | Bone density | Esthetic concern | Ideal buccolingual width | Min mesiodistal space | Surgical note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maxillary anterior | D3 / Type III | High | ≥6 mm | 6–7 mm | Palatal placement, platform 3–4 mm apical to gingival margin, ~2 mm facial bone |
| Maxillary posterior | D4 / Type III–IV | Low | ≥6–7 mm | 6–7 mm | Under-drill osteotomy; sinus elevation if vertical bone <10 mm |
| Mandibular anterior | D1–D2 / Type I–II | Moderate | ≥5.5–6 mm | 5.5–6 mm | Careful spacing, avoid root proximity and lingual perforation, consider bone tapping |
| Mandibular posterior | D2 / Type I–II | Low | ≥6–7 mm | 6–7 mm | CBCT-guided angulation, 2 mm IAN safety, gradual drilling |

### Protocol selection (Table 7)

| Protocol | Advantages | Disadvantages | Indications |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-stage surgery | Fewer surgeries, less morbidity, shorter treatment | Higher soft-tissue complication risk in esthetic areas | Non-esthetic sites with good bone and primary stability |
| Two-stage surgery | Implant protection during healing, ideal for grafted sites | Second surgery, longer treatment | Esthetic or grafted sites, soft bone, augmentation cases |
| Flap | Direct bone visualization, enables GBR/ridge correction | More trauma, longer recovery, more discomfort | Sites needing grafting or ridge modification |
| Flapless | Preserves blood supply, less post-op discomfort, faster healing | Limited visibility; misplacement risk without guidance | Ideal bone and soft tissue, guided surgery available |
| Immediate placement | Preserves soft/hard tissue architecture, shorter treatment | Requires primary stability, difficult soft-tissue handling | Fresh sockets with intact walls |
| Immediate loading | Faster to final restoration, patient convenience | Requires high insertion torque; failure if overloaded | Selected cases with sufficient primary stability |

### Occlusal scheme (Table 9)

| Aspect | Recommended approach | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Centric contacts | Centralized, light, ideally in the central fossa | Minimizes overload at the bone–implant interface |
| Eccentric contacts | Minimized or eliminated, especially posteriorly | Reduces screw loosening and bone loss |
| Cuspal anatomy | Flat cusp inclines | Lowers shear stress on implant components |
| Occlusal table | Narrower | Decreases lateral load magnitude and direction |
| Guidance scheme | Canine guidance or mutually protected occlusion | Protects posterior restorations during movement |
| Posterior disclusion | Posterior implants disclude during excursions | Prevents occlusal trauma and overload |
| Force direction | Axial loading; avoid cantilevers and off-axis forces | Long-term biomechanical stability |

### Prosthetic material guidance

| Region | Preferred material | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior and premolar | Zirconia | Biocompatibility, translucency, strength; thin-biotype sites less gray-discolored |
| Posterior load-bearing | Metal–ceramic (or monolithic zirconia in high-risk cases) | Proven durability and cost-effectiveness; high fracture resistance |
| High parafunction | Metal–ceramic or monolithic zirconia + night guard | Resnick & Misch — bruxism drives fracture and screw loosening |
