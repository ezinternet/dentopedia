## Three-line Summary

In vitro comparative histology study (J Clin Med 2025; 54 implant bed preparations in 4 swine ribs, 3 implant drill systems [Hiossen ET, Paltop, AnyRidge] × 3 speeds [800/1200/1500 rpm] × 3 cooling conditions [saline RT, saline 4°C, no cooling], DAPI/rhodamine fluorescence microscopy, 4-parameter 1-16 scoring, Kruskal-Wallis/Dunn's post hoc).

4°C saline cooling gave the highest bone-bed-quality score across all systems (best combos: Hiossen 1200 rpm/4°C = 15.3, Paltop 800 rpm/4°C = 15.2, AnyRidge 800 rpm/4°C = 13.7, out of 16); no cooling produced the lowest scores in every system, worsening further as speed increased (worst: Hiossen 1500 rpm/no cooling = 5.5), and each drill system had its own optimal speed (Hiossen best at 1200-1500 rpm, Paltop and AnyRidge best at 800 rpm).

Cooling temperature (4°C saline) was the single most influential variable on bone-bed quality across all three drill systems; the study used swine rib (not human) bone and did not control for operator technique or intrinsic drill-design differences, so in vivo human validation is needed before the specific speed/system recommendations are applied clinically.

## Summary

This in vitro study compared 3 commercial implant drill systems (Hiossen ET, Paltop, AnyRidge) across 3 rotational speeds (800/1200/1500 rpm) and 3 cooling conditions (room-temperature saline, 4°C saline, no cooling) in 54 osteotomies prepared in swine ribs. Bone bed quality was graded via fluorescence microscopy (DAPI/rhodamine) on a 4-parameter, 16-point scale covering surface smoothness, compressed tissue, carbonization, and cracking/adjacent tissue damage. 4°C saline cooling produced the best scores across all three systems, while the absence of cooling produced the worst scores in every system — an effect that worsened sharply with higher rotational speed. Each drill system had a distinct optimal speed (Hiossen best at 1200-1500 rpm; Paltop and AnyRidge best at 800 rpm), suggesting that optimal drilling protocols are system-specific rather than universal. The authors note their own paper contains an internal inconsistency between the Abstract/Table 2 numeric ranking and the prose in the Conclusions section regarding which system performed "best" overall — this page follows the Abstract/Table 2 data as primary.

## Key Contributions
- Direct multi-system, multi-speed, multi-cooling comparative design on identical bone source, isolating cooling and speed as the primary drilling-protocol variables.
- Standardized 4-parameter, 16-point quantitative histological scoring system for bone bed quality, applicable to future comparative drilling studies.
- Shows cooling temperature (4°C) matters more than drill system choice — a protocol-first rather than device-first message.

## Methodology
In vitro comparative experimental study; 4 swine ribs, 54 implant bed preparations (3 systems × 3 speeds × 3 cooling conditions, n=6 per system-speed cell); NeoSurge implant engine, 32:1 contra-angle handpiece; fluorescence microscopy (Nikon Eclipse 80i, DAPI + rhodamine, 40×) scored by one calibrated histologist on 4 parameters (1-4 each, total 0-16: Poor 0-7 / Medium 8-11 / Good 12-16); Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn's post hoc (non-parametric).

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Best combination overall | Hiossen 1200 rpm + 4°C saline, score 15.3/16 (p=0.003) |
| Paltop best combination | 800 rpm + 4°C saline, score 15.2/16 (p=0.012) |
| AnyRidge best combination | 800 rpm + 4°C saline, score 13.7/16 (p=0.021 for AnyRidge optimum reported in abstract) |
| Worst combination | Hiossen 1500 rpm + no cooling, score 5.5/16 |
| No-cooling effect | Lowest scores in every system; worsens significantly with higher speed, especially 1500 rpm (p<0.001) |
| Speed effect (unconfounded) | 800 rpm > 1500 rpm on all 4 parameters; 800 rpm > 1200 rpm on Parameters 1 (smoothness) and 4 (cracking) |
