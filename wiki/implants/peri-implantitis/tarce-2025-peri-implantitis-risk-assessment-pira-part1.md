## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 49 systematic reviews (30 patient-related [15 with meta-analysis], 19 treatment-related [9 with meta-analysis]), screened from 4,724 records down to 103 SR candidates, identifying preoperative peri-implantitis risk factors as the evidence base for a clinical risk-assessment tool (PiRA).

Ten patient-related risk factors reached meta-analysis-level significance in ≥1 review — history of periodontitis, implant location, smoking, number of teeth lost, recall compliance, and systemic disease (notably diabetes) — while treatment-related factors reaching significance included implant surface roughness and prosthetic suprastructure characteristics (crown-to-implant ratio, restoration type).

Nearly all included SRs analyzed risk factors in isolation without multifactorial adjustment, so the relative weight of each factor is not quantifiable from this review alone; the authors explicitly built this synthesis as groundwork for the multifactorial PiRA predictive model developed in the companion Part 2 paper.

## Summary

This umbrella review (Tarce & Quirynen, University of Hong Kong / KU Leuven, *JOMI* 2025;40(5):555–561) searched PubMed and Embase through August 2021, screening 4,724 records down to 103 systematic-review candidates and ultimately including 49 SRs (47 via database search + 2 via manual/hand search): 30 addressing patient-related risk factors (15 with meta-analysis, tabulated in Appendix Table 1) and 19 addressing treatment-related confounders (9 with meta-analysis, Appendix Table 2). Strict inclusion criteria required controlled comparisons, reported OR/RR/prevalence/z-score with 95% CI, ≥10 participants per group, and ≥6 months follow-up; the review deliberately excluded zirconia/PEEK/non-screw implants, short/narrow implants, major bone augmentation cases, medically compromised patients, and antiresorptive-medication histories to reduce heterogeneity.

Ten patient-related risk factors reached significance in at least one meta-analysis: history of periodontitis, implant location, smoking, number of teeth lost, recall compliance, and presence of systemic disease (diabetes). A further set of factors (bleeding on probing, poor oral hygiene, number of PPD ≥5mm sites, occlusal overload) showed increased incidence but lacked meta-analysis data, while age, sex, edentulism, and most other systemic diseases showed non-significant or unclear evidence. On the treatment side, implant surface roughness and prosthetic suprastructure characteristics (crown-to-implant ratio, restoration type — splinted/full-arch designs trending higher risk) reached meta-analysis-level significance, while cemented-vs-screw retention, abutment material/connection, cement remnants, GBR at placement, and immediate-vs-delayed placement showed only non-significant trends.

The review's central limitation — and its stated rationale for existing — is that almost no included SR performed multifactorial modeling: risk factors were analyzed one or a few at a time, so their true relative importance when adjusted for each other remains unknown. The authors position this umbrella review explicitly as Part 1 of a two-part project: it identifies and grades the individual risk factors, while the companion Part 2 paper (Quirynen et al., same issue) uses a 460-patient retrospective cohort to build and validate a multifactorial predictive model (PiRA) that clinicians can use preoperatively.

## Key Contributions

- First umbrella review focused specifically on peri-implantitis risk factors as a distinct outcome (versus broader implant-failure or general peri-implant-disease umbrella reviews).
- Separates 10 patient-related risk factors into modifiable (smoking, BoP, plaque control, PPD ≥5mm sites, recall frequency, occlusal overload) vs non-modifiable (history of periodontitis, implant location, number of teeth lost, systemic disease) — directly actionable for patient counseling/screening.
- Catalogs treatment-related confounders (surface roughness, cemented vs screw-retained, abutment material/connection, cement remnants, GBR at placement, immediate vs delayed placement, flapless approach, keratinized tissue) separately, flagging which reached meta-analysis-level significance vs only trend-level evidence.
- Explicitly frames itself as the risk-factor identification phase feeding a subsequent multifactorial predictive tool (PiRA), operationalized in the companion Part 2 retrospective study.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Umbrella review (SR of SRs), Cochrane Collaboration methodology + PRISMA 2020.
- **Search**: PubMed (via MEDLINE) + Embase (via Ovid), English language, before August 2021; manual search of 9 journals' last 10 years; reference-list screening of included SRs and studies.
- **Screening**: 4,724 records (2,161 MEDLINE + 2,563 Embase) → 2,865 after dedup → 103 SRs assessed for eligibility → 49 SRs included (47 database + 2 hand search).
- **Inclusion criteria**: human subjects >18y; controlled design (≥2 cohorts/treatments); reported prevalence/OR/RR/z-score with 95% CI for peri-implantitis; ≥10 participants/group; ≥6 months follow-up.
- **Exclusion criteria**: zirconia/PEEK/custom/non-endosseous/non-screw implants; short (<8mm)/narrow (<3mm) implants; tooth-implant supported restorations; retrograde peri-implantitis; socket-shield technique; major bone augmentation (fibular/iliac/skin flap, Le Fort I); medically compromised patients; antiresorptive-medication history.
- **Outcomes**: primary — patient-related risk factors (prevalence/OR/RR/z-score); secondary — treatment-related confounders.
