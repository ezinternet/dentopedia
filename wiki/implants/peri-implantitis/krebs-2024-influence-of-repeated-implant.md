## Three-line Summary

Retrospective analysis of 27 edentulous-mandible patients (108 immediately loaded Morse-taper implants, 3–15 years; mean 10.2 ± 2.8) comparing one-abutment-one-time (OAOT, n=72 implants) with abutment replacement (AR, n=36 implants) under complete-arch double-crown prostheses.

Peri-implantitis prevalence at patient/implant level was 5.6%/5.6% in OAOT versus 88.9%/55.6% in AR; each additional abutment replacement raised the odds of mucositis/peri-implantitis (OR 6.13; 95% CI 2.61–14.39; p<0.001), and estimated mean radiographic bone loss was 1.38 mm larger in AR (p=0.0190).

First direct prevalence evidence favouring OAOT, but a retrospective, small, single-system cohort in which the case definition post-dated ethical approval — the magnitude of the effect should not be read as a causal estimate.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort is the first analysis to apply established peri-implant disease case definitions to the one-abutment-one-time (OAOT) question. Twenty-seven edentulous-mandible patients rehabilitated with four immediately loaded Ankylos Morse-taper implants each (108 implants; mean follow-up 10.2 years) were stratified by whether the definitive conical abutment was left in place (OAOT, 18 patients/72 implants) or replaced during prosthetic treatment (AR, 9 patients/36 implants). Peri-implantitis was far less prevalent in OAOT at both patient and implant level, and each additional abutment replacement independently raised the odds of mucositis or peri-implantitis (OR 6.13). Radiographic bone loss was 1.38 mm greater in the replacement group. Because the design is retrospective, the groups differed in prosthetic workflow, and the 2018 case definition was applied to records collected from 1998, the striking magnitude is best treated as an association rather than a causal effect size.

## Key Contributions
- First disease-prevalence (not just MBL) comparison of OAOT vs repeated abutment replacement using defined case criteria.
- Dose–response relationship: number of replacements, not only presence, predicts diagnosis (OR 6.13; 95% CI 2.61–14.39; p<0.001).
- Mean 10.2-year follow-up — the longest observation for a direct OAOT vs replacement prevalence comparison.
- Keratinized mucosa width was not a significant confounder in the mixed model.

## Methodology

Retrospective, STROBE-reported analysis of patients treated 1998–2015 at Goethe University Frankfurt. Inclusion required an edentulous mandible, four interforaminal subcrestal Morse-taper implants, immediate restoration with a double-crown-retained removable prosthesis, ≥3-year radiographic follow-up and yearly maintenance attendance. OAOT used prefabricated SynCone conical abutments retained for the life of the prosthesis; AR used lab-made zirconia primary crowns with electroformed gold secondary crowns requiring implant-level impressions and multiple abutment replacements. Peri-implantitis = BOP + progressive radiographic bone loss >1.50 mm; mucositis = BOP without bone loss. Mixed-effects regression modelled diagnosis against replacement status/count, keratinized mucosa category and radiographic bone loss.
