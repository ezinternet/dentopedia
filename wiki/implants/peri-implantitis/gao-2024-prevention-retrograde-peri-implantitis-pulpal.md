## Three-line Summary
Literature review (73 cases from 36 articles) finds untreated pulpal/periapical lesions in adjacent teeth as the primary and under-recognized cause of retrograde peri-implantitis (RPI).
RPI is defined as inflammation originating at the implant apex (crown direction), distinct from conventional peri-implantitis which progresses from the marginal bone apically.
No standardized clinical guidelines for RPI prevention or diagnosis exist; molecular/immunological and laser-based approaches proposed as future directions.

## Summary
Gao and Ma conducted a PubMed literature review to comprehensively map the epidemiology, etiology, clinical manifestations, classification, treatment, and prevention of RPI. Analyzing 73 cases from 36 articles, they identified untreated or undertreated pulpal/periapical lesions in teeth adjacent to implant sites as the dominant etiologic factor — one that clinicians frequently fail to evaluate systematically before implant placement. The review defines RPI directionally: inflammation propagates from the implant apex coronally, the reverse of conventional peri-implantitis (marginal → apical). Despite the clinical significance of RPI for implant success, the review concludes that no standardized treatment or prevention guidelines exist, and calls for prospective study designs to develop evidence-based protocols.

## Key Contributions
- Establishes adjacent-tooth pulpal/periapical disease as the primary RPI etiology — documented across 73 cases
- Provides updated (2024) comprehensive mapping of RPI: epidemiology, classification, treatment options, prevention strategies
- Formally distinguishes RPI direction (apex → coronal) from conventional peri-implantitis (marginal → apical)
- Highlights critical gap: no standardized clinical guidelines for RPI prevention or management
- Proposes future research directions: molecular/immunological diagnostics, laser treatment, novel materials

## Results

### Primary Etiology
- **Untreated/undertreated pulpal or periapical lesions in adjacent teeth** — dominant cause across 73 cases
- Clinicians routinely underassess adjacent tooth periapical status before implant placement

### Directional Definition of RPI
- RPI: inflammation originates at **implant apex**, propagates **coronally** (apex → crown direction)
- Conventional peri-implantitis: marginal bone → apical progression
- This directional difference is the key radiographic and clinical distinguishing feature

### Clinical Implications
- Implant success impacted by RPI — systematic preoperative adjacent-tooth evaluation is essential
- No uniform treatment protocols currently exist across the literature

### Future Directions
- Molecular biology and immunology-based precision diagnostics
- Laser treatment and novel biomaterial applications
