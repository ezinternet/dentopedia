## Three-line Summary

Four-center, three-arm, 12-week Korean RCT (118 randomized, 114 patients / 114 implants analyzed) of nonsurgical peri-implantitis treatment: debridement + metronidazole-minocycline ointment (MM) vs debridement + minocycline ointment (MC) vs debridement only (NST).

At 12 weeks the composite success rate was 31.6% (MM) and 20.5% (MC) vs 2.7% (NST) (p = 0.011 and 0.040); in implants with deepest PPD ≥8 mm, MM reduced PPD more than MC at week 4 (p = 0.025) and week 12 (p = 0.047), and T. forsythia detection was lower with MM (p = 0.038).

Local antibiotic ointment adds a real but small resolution gain over debridement alone, yet about two-thirds of implants stayed unresolved even in the best arm; 12-week follow-up, abstract-only, and one author is industry-affiliated (MXBIO).

## Summary

This multicenter Korean RCT (four centers, led by Yonsei University) randomized 118 patients with peri-implantitis to mechanical debridement plus a metronidazole-minocycline ointment (MM), debridement plus a minocycline ointment (MC), or debridement alone (NST); 114 patients with one implant each completed the 12-week trial. Composite treatment success (no bleeding or suppuration on probing and no sites with PPD ≥5 mm) was 31.6% with MM and 20.5% with MC versus 2.7% with debridement alone, both significantly higher. Adding metronidazole to minocycline gave a greater PPD reduction than minocycline alone only in the deepest pockets (deepest PPD ≥8 mm) and lowered Tannerella forsythia detection. The trial supports local antibiotic ointment as an adjunct to non-surgical therapy, but absolute resolution stayed low and follow-up was 12 weeks.

## Key Contributions

- Three-arm design isolates the effect of any local antibiotic (MC vs NST) and the incremental effect of adding metronidazole (MM vs MC).
- Both ointments significantly raised the 12-week composite success rate over debridement alone.
- The MM advantage over MC appears only in deep pockets (deepest PPD ≥8 mm) and microbiologically (T. forsythia).
- Primary trial behind the local-antibiotic success-rate estimate in the Grusovin 2022 meta-analysis (RR 9.89, from this trial only, per the Grusovin PDF).

## Methodology

Multicenter (4 centers), three-arm, parallel RCT with 12-week follow-up; 118 randomized, 4 excluded during the trial, 114 patients / 114 implants analyzed. Primary outcome: treatment success at 12 weeks (absence of bleeding or suppuration on probing and of sites with PPD ≥5 mm), analyzed with multivariate logistic regression. Secondary: PPD reduction (deepest-PPD ≥8 mm subgroup at weeks 4 and 12) and microbiological detection. Registered at CRIS (KCT0004557). Dose, schedule, blinding, allocation concealment and adverse events are not reported in the abstract.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Treatment success, 12 wk — MM | 31.6% (vs NST p = 0.011) |
| Treatment success, 12 wk — MC | 20.5% (vs NST p = 0.040) |
| Treatment success, 12 wk — NST | 2.7% |
| PPD reduction, deepest PPD ≥8 mm, MM vs MC | MM greater at wk 4 (p = 0.025) and wk 12 (p = 0.047) |
| T. forsythia detection ratio, MM vs MC | lower with MM (p = 0.038) |
| MM vs MC success rate | not reported in the abstract |

**Bottom line**: local minocycline (with or without metronidazole) added to debridement improves short-term resolution of peri-implantitis, but resolution remains a minority outcome at 12 weeks; the combination's extra value is limited to deep pockets.

**Product applicability note**: the abstract does not name the commercial minocycline ointment used in the MC arm, so mapping this arm to a specific 2% minocycline HCl product (e.g., Minoclin) is an inference from formulation, not a reported fact.
