## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Periodontology 2000, 2026; three-database search, 2010 to November 2025) on factors that shape the outcome of peri-implantitis surgical therapy across surgical technique, implant-site characteristics, and patient conditions.

Reconstructive adjuncts improve radiographic defect fill, with consistent benefit confined to contained 3- and 4-wall intrabony defects and slowly resorbing substitutes favored over autogenous bone; no clear benefit in resolving clinical inflammation, and flap design, healing mode, membranes, systemic antibiotics, and biologics remain undetermined.

Outcomes depend on surgeon experience, baseline severity (extensive bone loss, suppuration, deep probing depth, rough surfaces, post-operative bleeding), keratinized mucosa, and patient adherence to supportive care; as a narrative review it gives no pooled estimates.

## Summary

A narrative review in Periodontology 2000 that synthesizes the factors determining the outcome of surgical therapy for peri-implantitis, searched across three databases for 2010 to November 2025. It organizes factors into three domains. Surgical factors: reconstructive adjuncts improve radiographic defect fill and may help maintain soft-tissue levels, with a consistent advantage for contained 3- and 4-wall defects and for slowly resorbing substitutes over autogenous bone. Implant-site factors: severe initial disease, rough surfaces, and persistent post-operative bleeding compromise success, while keratinized mucosa favors reconstructive outcomes. Patient factors: adherence to supportive care is essential to keep surgical gains.

## Key Contributions

- Frames peri-implantitis surgical outcome as a joint function of defect morphology, disease severity, implant surface, surgeon experience, and patient adherence.
- Identifies contained (3- and 4-wall) intrabony defects as the most consistent indication for reconstructive therapy.
- Reports that reconstruction does not clearly resolve clinical inflammation even when it improves radiographic fill.
- Flags macrophage polarization as an emerging, not yet established, outcome modifier.

## Methodology

Narrative review. Three electronic databases, studies published January 2010 to November 2025. No pooled analysis or risk-of-bias table is reported in the abstract. Study count and database names are not stated in the abstract.

## Results

| Factor | Reported direction (narrative, no pooled effect sizes) |
|---|---|
| Reconstructive adjunct | Better radiographic defect fill; possible soft-tissue level maintenance; no clear benefit on clinical inflammation |
| Contained 3- and 4-wall defects | More favorable reconstructive outcome than 1- and 2-wall |
| Graft type | Slowly resorbing substitutes may outperform autogenous bone |
| Flap design, submerged vs non-submerged healing, membranes, systemic antibiotics, biologics | No definitive conclusion |
| Surgeon experience | Associated with effectiveness |
| Extensive bone loss, suppuration, deep probing depth, rough surface, post-op bleeding | Associated with poorer outcome |
| Keratinized mucosa | Associated with better reconstructive outcome |
| Macrophage polarization | Emerging association |
| Supportive care adherence | Essential to maintain results |
| Periodontitis history, smoking, gender | Inconsistent |
