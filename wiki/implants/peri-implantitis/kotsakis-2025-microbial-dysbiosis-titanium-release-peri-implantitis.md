## Three-line Summary

Invited critical review (Kotsakis & Ganesan, JDR 2025) synthesizing evidence that implant-derived titanium particles (i-TiPs) act as an "abiotic exposome" co-etiologic factor in peri-implantitis, alongside microbial biofilm, via a host-microbiome-biomaterial (H-M-B) triangle framework.

i-TiPs are detected in 75–100% of peri-implantitis cases; titanium concentration inversely correlates with peri-implant microbiome alpha-diversity and drives a *Veillonella parvula*-enriched dysbiotic shift, while independently activating inflammasome, TLR, and C3-complement inflammatory pathways.

Because titanium particles are an abiotic danger signal unaffected by antimicrobial therapy — and abrasive mechanical cleaning paradoxically increases their release — the review argues peri-implantitis needs host-modulatory treatment strategies, not biofilm control alone.

## 세줄요약

Kotsakis & Ganesan (JDR 2025)의 초청 Critical Review로, 임플란트 티타늄 부식산물(implant-derived titanium particles, i-TiPs)이 숙주-미생물-생체재료(host-microbiome-biomaterial, H-M-B) 삼각 모델 안에서 미생물 생체막과 함께 peri-implantitis의 공동 병인("abiotic exposome")으로 작용한다는 근거를 정리했다.

i-TiPs는 peri-implantitis 병소의 75~100%에서 검출되며, 티타늄 농도는 peri-implant 미생물총의 알파다양성과 역상관하고 Veillonella parvula 우세의 dysbiosis 전환을 유도하며, 세균 LPS와 독립적으로 inflammasome·TLR·C3 보체 경로를 활성화한다.

티타늄 입자는 항균 치료로 제거되지 않는 abiotic danger signal이고 연마성 기계적 세척이 오히려 입자 방출을 늘리므로, 저자들은 생체막 관리만으로는 부족하며 host-modulatory 치료 전략이 필요하다고 결론짓는다.

## Summary

This Critical Review reframes peri-implantitis pathogenesis away from a purely plaque-centric ("periodontitis of the implant") model toward a triangular interplay of host immunity, the peri-implant microbiome, and the implant biomaterial itself (H-M-B triangle). The central argument is that titanium implant degradation products — implant-derived titanium particles (i-TiPs) — constitute a co-etiologic abiotic exposome that explains why peri-implantitis responds so much worse to antimicrobial-only therapy (<50% disease resolution) than periodontitis around single-rooted teeth does to scaling/root planing ± antibiotics.

Titanium particles arise through biocorrosion (biological-fluid breakdown), tribocorrosion (corrosion + mechanical wear at the implant-abutment microgap), or iatrogenic corrosion (clinician-caused, e.g., abrasive titanium-brush cleaning). Once released, particles as small as submicron scale up to ~10 µm are internalized by peri-implant immune cells, triggering endosomal-lysosomal activation, oxidative stress (ROS release), and — critically — inflammasome, toll-like receptor, and C3 complement signaling independent of bacterial lipopolysaccharide. This gives peri-implantitis a second, non-microbial inflammatory driver that periodontitis around teeth lacks.

On the microbiome side, the review traces four generations of sequencing technology (culture-based → 16S pyrosequencing → 16S Illumina MiSeq → whole-genome/strain-level metagenomics → meta-transcriptomics), converging on a consistent finding: the peri-implant microbiome in disease is distinct from periodontitis, lower in alpha-diversity, and — per Daubert et al. 2018 — its diversity loss and taxonomic shift (toward *Veillonella parvula* dominance) tracks quantitatively with local titanium concentration. This is presented as direct evidence that titanium acts as a local abiotic driver shaping microbial community structure, not merely a bystander marker of inflammation.

A clinically important corollary: mechanical abrasive implant-cleaning methods (titanium brushes, some ultrasonic protocols) that are meant to treat peri-implantitis by removing biofilm can paradoxically worsen the disease by increasing titanium particle release — validated in a randomized clinical trial (Daubert et al. 2023) showing abrasive cleaning increased plaque titanium at 8 weeks post-treatment while non-abrasive nylon-brush cleaning did not. The review closes by arguing that, because antimicrobial monotherapy cannot address an abiotic danger signal, the future of peri-implantitis therapy lies in targeted host-modulatory interventions (e.g., the C3 complement inhibitor AMY-101) against titanium-mediated inflammatory pathways, used alongside — not instead of — biofilm control.

## Key Contributions

- Proposes and synthesizes evidence for the host-microbiome-biomaterial (H-M-B) triangle as the correct pathogenetic framework for peri-implantitis, replacing the plaque-only model inherited from periodontology.
- Consolidates cross-sectional human evidence that i-TiPs are detected in 75–100% of peri-implantitis cases and virtually absent in peri-implant health.
- Identifies three distinct mechanisms of titanium particle release (biocorrosion, tribocorrosion, iatrogenic corrosion) and their differing clinical relevance.
- Explains the mechanistic basis (inflammasome, TLR, C3 complement activation independent of bacterial LPS) for why peri-implantitis resists antimicrobial-only treatment.
- Traces the technological evolution of peri-implant microbiome sequencing studies and shows a convergent, implant-specific dysbiotic signature correlated with local titanium concentration.
- Flags the clinical paradox that abrasive mechanical implant-cleaning devices increase titanium release and may perpetuate the very inflammation they aim to treat.
- Proposes host-modulatory therapy (e.g., AMY-101 complement inhibitor) as the logical future direction for peri-implantitis management.

## Methodology

Narrative/critical review (invited "Critical Reviews in Oral Biology & Medicine" article in Journal of Dental Research), not a systematic review or meta-analysis — no PRISMA protocol, formal search strategy, or risk-of-bias assessment. Evidence synthesized from: cross-sectional human studies quantifying i-TiPs in peri-implant tissue/plaque (exfoliative cytology, immunohistochemistry, ICP-MS/dissolved-titanium assays); in vitro corrosion/tribocorrosion studies; orthopedic literature on titanium-wear-particle osteolysis as mechanistic precedent; four generations of peri-implant microbiome sequencing studies; and one RCT (Daubert et al. 2023) on abrasive vs. non-abrasive implant cleaning and titanium release.

## Results

- i-TiPs detected in 75–100% of peri-implantitis tissue/plaque samples depending on assay; rare in health.
- Titanium concentration inversely correlates with peri-implant microbiome alpha-diversity; disease samples show titanium-dose-dependent enrichment of *Veillonella parvula* (Daubert et al. 2018).
- Meta-transcriptomic correlation (Ganesan et al. 2022): 4- to 200-fold upregulation of microbial genes for biofilm thickness, heme transport/utilization, and gram-negative membrane synthesis in peri-implantitis, correlating with host genes for apoptosis, membrane transport, and inflammation.
- RCT evidence (Daubert et al. 2023): abrasive mechanical implant cleaning significantly increased titanium release in peri-implant plaque at 8 weeks; non-abrasive nylon-brush cleaning did not.
- Standard antimicrobial/mechanical peri-implantitis treatment achieves <50% disease resolution, versus high success rates for equivalent periodontitis treatment around single-rooted teeth.
- Shared upregulated transcriptomic pathways in human peri-implantitis: endosomal-lysosomal, inflammasome, and C3 complement signaling.
- Titanium microparticles range from submicron scale to ~10 µm — within the size range for cellular internalization and endosomal-lysosomal/inflammasome activation.
