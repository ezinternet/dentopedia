## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Periodontology 2000, 2019; no patient sample) from the University of Washington linking surgical and restorative design — emergence profile, implant design/position, implant–abutment interface manipulation — to peri-implant inflammatory disease.

Peri-mucositis and peri-implantitis prevalence "may be as high as" 40%–65% and 20%–47%, respectively; the authors frame etiology as multifactorial with prosthetic/positional factors modifying the host response.

Restorative and surgical design is a modifiable target for reducing disease prevalence; abstract-only ingest means no design-specific thresholds or effect sizes are available here.

## Summary

Dixon and London's 2019 Periodontology 2000 review addresses the inflammatory conditions around dental implants, whose prevalence they describe as substantial (peri-mucositis up to 40%–65%, peri-implantitis 20%–47%). Rather than treating these as purely patient- or biofilm-driven, the authors assemble a multifactorial picture in which initial surgical implant placement position, disruption of the biologic interface through implant–abutment interface manipulation, and prosthetic design may influence the host response and the diagnosis of inflammatory states. The review discusses natural and prosthetic emergence-profile concepts together with implant design and position, and other restorative concepts connected to complications and disease. Because disease progression around implants is temporal, the authors argue that accounting for these design parameters can reduce the variables facing the surgeon–restorative team and help lower peri-implant disease prevalence.

This page is **abstract-only** — no PMC record and no open-access full text were available, so the search strategy, number of included studies, and any design-specific thresholds are not retrievable here.

## Key Contributions

- Frames peri-implant mucositis/peri-implantitis as highly prevalent and **multifactorial**, not attributable to a single risk factor.
- Names three design-domain variables beyond classic host/biofilm factors: **initial implant placement position**, **implant–abutment interface manipulation** (biologic-interface disruption), and **prosthetic design**.
- Reviews natural and prosthetic **emergence profile** concepts with implant design/position as a unified restorative-design problem.
- Highlights the **temporal** character of peri-implant disease progression as a reason to plan surgically and restoratively together.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Narrative review (PubMed publication type: Review); no cohort or sample size reported in the abstract.
- **Institution:** University of Washington, Seattle, WA, USA.
- **Scope:** Surgical and restorative design factors associated with peri-implant mucositis/peri-implantitis.
- **Not verifiable from the abstract:** search strategy, study-selection count, risk-of-bias assessment, pooled estimates.

## Results

- Reported prevalence: peri-mucositis "may be as high as 40%–65%", peri-implantitis "20%–47%" — author-level summary ranges, not this review's own pooled data.
- Qualitative conclusions only: implant position, abutment-interface manipulation, and prosthetic design "may also influence" host response or inflammatory diagnosis.
- No effect sizes, CIs, p-values, or emergence-angle thresholds appear in the retrieved abstract.
