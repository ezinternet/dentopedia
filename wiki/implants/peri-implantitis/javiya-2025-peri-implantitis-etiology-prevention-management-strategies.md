## Three-line Summary

PRISMA 2020 systematic review (no meta-analysis) of human clinical studies on peri-implantitis etiology, prevention, and management — PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Cochrane Library, English-language studies from January 2010 to March 2025, two independent reviewers.

Peri-implantitis prevalence ranges from 10% to over 40%; biofilm (chiefly P. gingivalis, T. denticola, T. forsythia) with periodontitis history, smoking, poor glycemic control and poor plaque control drive risk, structured supportive maintenance significantly reduces incidence, and guided bone regeneration yields better bone fill than resective surgery.

Qualitative synthesis only — no pooled effect sizes, no included-study count reported and no prospective registration, and heterogeneous diagnostic criteria across the literature limit cross-study comparison.

## 세줄요약

PRISMA 2020 기반 체계적문헌고찰(Systematic Review, 메타분석 없음) — PubMed·Scopus·Embase·Cochrane Library 4개 데이터베이스에서 2010년 1월~2025년 3월 영문 인간 임상연구를 두 명의 독립 평가자가 선정.

주위염(Peri-implantitis) 유병률(Prevalence)은 10%~40% 초과, 생물막(Biofilm)의 그람음성 혐기균(P. gingivalis·T. denticola·T. forsythia)과 치주염 과거력(History of Periodontitis)·흡연(Smoking)·혈당조절 불량·치면세균막 관리 불량이 핵심 위험인자이며, 체계적 유지관리(Supportive Care)로 발생·중증도가 유의하게 감소, 골재생유도술(GBR)이 제거술(Resective)보다 골 충전량이 우세.

정성 종합(qualitative synthesis) 한정으로 메타분석 효과크기·포함 연구 수 미보고, 전향적 등록(Prospective Registration) 미실시, 진단기준 비일관성과 영문 제한·블러리터처 배제가 핵심 한계.

## Summary

This PRISMA 2020 systematic review synthesized human clinical studies (searched PubMed, Scopus, Embase, and Cochrane Library, January 2010–March 2025) on the etiology, prevention, and management of peri-implantitis, without performing a meta-analysis. It identifies biofilm accumulation — dominated by Gram-negative anaerobes such as Porphyromonas gingivalis, Treponema denticola, and Tannerella forsythia — as the chief precipitant, with host susceptibility (periodontitis history, smoking, diabetes with poor glycemic control), mechanical overload, excess cement, plaque-retentive prosthetic design, insufficient keratinized mucosa, and absent professional maintenance modifying risk. Prevention centers on early risk assessment, individualized oral hygiene, and structured supportive care, which significantly reduced disease incidence and severity, while implant surface modifications remain supported mainly by laboratory/short-term data. Treatment is stage-dependent: non-surgical modalities (debridement, antiseptics, local antibiotics, laser) give limited benefit confined to early disease, surgical intervention is typically needed for extensive bone loss, and guided bone regeneration produced better bone fill than resective procedures. The synthesis is qualitative — diagnostic criteria heterogeneity, no prospective registration, no reported included-study count, English-only scope, and scarcity of long-term RCTs constrain its authority relative to umbrella reviews and guidelines already held in this wiki.

## Key Contributions
- Single-document PRISMA 2020 synthesis spanning peri-implantitis etiology, prevention, and stage-based management with a search window through March 2025.
- Multifactorial etiology model: biofilm as chief precipitant plus mechanical factors (occlusal overload, implant–abutment junction micromovement, foreign-body reaction to surface particles) and host susceptibility.
- Past periodontitis confirmed as one of the strongest predictors, alongside smoking, poor glycemic control, excess cement, plaque-retentive prostheses, insufficient keratinized mucosa, rough/subcrestal surfaces, and absent maintenance.
- Stage-based treatment algorithm: non-surgical benefit limited to early disease; GBR favored over resective surgery for bone fill in suitable defects; adjuncts (probiotics, photodynamic therapy, host modulation) remain supportive, not standard of care.
- Names lack of standardized diagnostic criteria as the field's major limitation blocking guideline development.

## Methodology

Systematic review per PRISMA 2020 (not prospectively registered; qualitative synthesis, no meta-analysis). Four databases (PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Cochrane Library) searched with MeSH + free-text Boolean strategies, January 2010–March 2025, English only, plus hand-searched reference lists. Included human clinical studies (RCT, controlled clinical trial, prospective/retrospective cohort, case-control) with defined diagnostic criteria, measurable outcomes, and ≥6-month follow-up for interventional trials; excluded animal/in vitro studies, case reports, case series <10, narrative reviews, mucositis-only studies, non-English publications. Two independent reviewers screened with third-reviewer arbitration and a standardized data-extraction sheet. Included-study count not reported; Table 1 summarizes 8 key studies.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Peri-implantitis prevalence | 10% to >40% (varies by diagnostic criteria, population, follow-up) |
| Key pathogens | Gram-negative anaerobes: P. gingivalis, T. denticola, T. forsythia |
| Strongest risk predictor | History of periodontitis (plus smoking, poor glycemic control, poor plaque control, excess cement, insufficient keratinized mucosa, rough/subcrestal surfaces, no maintenance) |
| Prevention | Early risk assessment + individualized oral hygiene + regular maintenance → significantly reduced incidence and severity in long-term supportive-care patients |
| Non-surgical treatment | Debridement/antiseptics/local antibiotics/laser: limited benefit, early disease only |
| Surgical treatment | Required for extensive bone loss; GBR > resective surgery for bone fill in suitable defects |
| Adjuncts (probiotics, PDT, host modulation) | Supportive but limited additional outcome; need larger, longer RCTs |
| Pooled effect sizes | None — no meta-analysis performed |
