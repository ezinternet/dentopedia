## Three-line Summary

7-year follow-up of an RCT (original 57 patients/78 implants; 34 patients/49 implants completed) of reconstructive peri-implantitis surgery using xenograft covered by concentrated growth factor (CGF) vs collagen membrane (CM), all under supportive periodontal/peri-implant care (SPIC).

Treatment success fell from 46.2%/50.0% (CGF/CM, 1 year) to 23.1%/30.8% at 7 years; cumulative survival 81.25% vs 88.8%; CM held significantly better deepest PPD (p = 0.040) and radiographic vertical defect depth (p = 0.043) at implant level, while composite outcomes did not differ.

Reconstructive surgery's short-term gains relapse progressively despite regular SPIC, CGF offers no long-term advantage over standard collagen membrane, and baseline suppuration on probing is the dominant failure predictor (OR = 15.45; 95% CI 1.42–168.5).

## 세줄요약

임플란트주위염(Peri-implantitis) 재건수술(이종골 이식 + 익은성장인자(CGF)막 vs 콜라겐막(CM))의 7년 장기 추적 RCT — 원 기록 57명/78개 임플란트 중 34명/49개 임플란트 완료, 전원 임플란트 주위 지지치료(SPIC) 유지 하에서 분석.

치료 성공률은 1년 46.2%/50.0%(CGF/CM군)에서 7년 23.1%/30.8%로 하락, 누적 생존율 81.25% vs 88.8%; 심명 탐침깊이(Deepest PPD, p = 0.040)와 방사선학적 수직결손심도(Vertical Defect Depth, p = 0.043)는 임플란트 수준에서 CM군이 유의하게 우세했으나 복합 성공 지표는 차이 없음.

재건수술의 단기 개선은 SPIC 유지에도 점진적으로 재발하며, CGF는 콜라겐막 대비 장기 이점이 없고 기저 탐침화농(Suppuration on Probing, SOP) 존재가 유일한 유의한 장기 실패 예측인자(OR = 15.45; 95% CI 1.42–168.5)였다.

## Summary

This 7-year follow-up of the Isler et al. RCT reports the longest randomized comparison of barrier membranes in reconstructive peri-implantitis surgery to date: xenogenic bone substitute (Bio-Oss) covered by either autologous CGF membranes or a Bio-Gide collagen membrane, with submerged healing and risk-profiled SPIC. Clinical and radiographic improvements achieved at 1 year were largely maintained relative to baseline but showed a steady relapse trend from year 1 to year 7 in both groups; treatment success halved (CGF 46.2%→23.1%, CM 50.0%→30.8% at patient level) and disease recurrence climbed to ~31–35%. At implant level, the collagen membrane group held significantly better deepest PPD and radiographic vertical defect depth, though no composite endpoint differed between groups. Baseline suppuration on probing was the only significant patient-level predictor of treatment failure, and two-wall (vs four-wall) defects predicted failure at implant level. The authors conclude that long-term success of reconstructive therapy is limited, CGF adds nothing durable over standard collagen membrane, and outcomes hinge on disease baseline severity plus lifelong SPIC adherence.

## Key Contributions
- First RCT-grade 7-year survival/success data for reconstructive peri-implantitis surgery with a barrier membrane — addresses the long-term evidence gap flagged in the 2018 World Workshop follow-up literature.
- Directly refutes a durable benefit of CGF over collagen membrane: numerically lower success and significantly worse deepest PPD/defect depth with CGF at implant level.
- Quantifies the relapse trajectory under regular SPIC (success halving from 1 to 7 years), matching cohort declines reported by Roccuzzo (7→10 years) and La Monaca (1→5 years).
- Baseline SOP as a strong (imprecise-CI) failure predictor (OR = 15.45) supports using suppuration as a severity triage marker before committing to reconstructive surgery.
- Extends the "partial resolution" outcome category (15.4–23.5% at 7 years) as clinically meaningful between success and recurrence.

## Methodology

7-year follow-up report of an RCT (original 2018 protocol, NCT04769609, CONSORT 2010). Original 57 patients (78 implants) with 2-/3-/4-wall intrabony peri-implantitis defects ≥3 mm; all received non-surgical pre-treatment, then open-flap surgery with Bio-Oss xenograft covered by CGF or collagen membrane, submerged healing 6 months, and risk-based SPIC (3-month intervals initially, then 3–6 months). Outcomes: CAL (primary, 1-year report), treatment success/partial resolution/disease recurrence, PPD, BOP, PI, GI, PSTD, radiographic vertical defect depth at 1/5/7 years, PROMs (VAS) at 7 years. Statistics: GLMM, repeated-measures ANOVA, logistic regression, Kaplan–Meier.
