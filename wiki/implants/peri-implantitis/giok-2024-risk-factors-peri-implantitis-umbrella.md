## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of meta-analyses of observational studies (PROSPERO CRD42024512408) — 12 articles describing 41 unique meta-analyses on peri-implantitis risk factors, searched PubMed/Scopus/Cochrane/Epistemonikos from inception to October 2023.

Of 41 associations (24 significant), presence of periodontitis (OR 3.84, 95% CI 2.58-5.72) and cigarette smoking (RR 2.07, 95% CI 1.41-3.04) graded "highly suggestive"; 8 further associations (diabetes mellitus, hyperglycaemia, lack of prophylaxis, chronic periodontal disease history, anterior-jaw implant location, OPG gene polymorphisms, lack of keratinized mucosa) graded "suggestive"; none reached "convincing."

Periodontitis history and smoking are the most robustly evidenced peri-implantitis risk factors and should inform preoperative consent and monitoring; abstract-only page (paywalled Elsevier full text not retrieved), so per-study data and full bias assessment are not captured here.

## Summary

This umbrella review (Giok, Veettil, Menon — International Medical University Malaysia / Ajman University UAE, *J Dent* 2024;146:105065) synthesizes meta-analyses of observational studies examining risk factors for peri-implantitis specifically (as opposed to overall implant failure). Searching PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, and Epistemonikos from inception to October 2023 (PROSPERO CRD42024512408), the authors screened 5002 publications down to 51 full-text articles and included 12 articles describing 41 unique meta-analytic associations. Applying a standard umbrella-review credibility-grading framework with bias assessment, 24 of 41 associations were statistically significant, but none reached "convincing" credibility. Two associations reached "highly suggestive" — the second-highest tier: presence/history of periodontitis (OR 3.84, 95% CI 2.58-5.72) and cigarette smoking (RR 2.07, 95% CI 1.41-3.04). Eight further associations reached "suggestive": diabetes mellitus, hyperglycaemia, lack of prophylaxis/maintenance, history of chronic periodontal disease, ongoing/history of periodontal disease, implants located in the anterior maxilla/mandible, osteoprotegerin (OPG) gene polymorphisms, and lack of keratinized mucosal width. The authors conclude that periodontitis history and smoking are the most robustly evidenced modifiable/history-based risk factors for peri-implantitis and should be explicitly assessed, monitored, and incorporated into the preoperative informed-consent process, while the remaining suggestive-tier factors await higher-quality primary studies to upgrade their evidence grade.

## Key Contributions

- Peri-implantitis-specific umbrella review of 41 meta-analytic associations (12 source articles), applying formal credibility grading with bias assessment — a narrower, more recent (search-to-October-2023) complement to the wiki's existing larger 49-SR peri-implantitis umbrella review.
- Identifies periodontitis history and cigarette smoking as the only two "highly suggestive" risk factors among 41 examined associations, independently corroborating the same top-two ranking found in [[implants/peri-implantitis/tarce-2025-peri-implantitis-risk-assessment-pira-part1]].
- Adds osteoprotegerin (OPG) gene polymorphisms and lack of keratinized mucosal width to the "suggestive" evidence tier — genetic and soft-tissue factors not headlined in the Tarce umbrella review.
- Explicitly recommends integrating highly-suggestive/suggestive risk factors into the preoperative informed-consent conversation, not just clinical risk stratification.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Umbrella review of meta-analyses of observational studies, registered PROSPERO CRD42024512408.
- **Search**: PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Epistemonikos, from inception to October 2023.
- **Screening**: 5002 publications → 51 full-text articles assessed → 12 included articles describing 41 unique meta-analyses.
- **Grading**: Credibility tiers (convincing / highly suggestive / suggestive / weak / nonsignificant) with formal bias assessment across included meta-analyses.
