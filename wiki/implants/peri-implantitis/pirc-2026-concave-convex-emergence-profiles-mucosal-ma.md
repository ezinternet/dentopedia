## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (JERD 2026): 4 RCTs, 144 implants, 12-month follow-up — first quantitative synthesis specifically addressing midfacial mucosal stability as a function of emergence profile design.

Concave emergence profiles trend toward less midfacial mucosal recession vs convex (MD -0.31 mm; 95% CI -0.63 to 0.02; p=0.064; I²=28%); borderline significance; sensitivity analysis excluding one low-contrast study yields statistical significance (MD -0.43 mm; p=0.002).

Concave emergence profiles preferred for peri-implant soft tissue stability; convex profiles associated with approximately 0.3 mm greater midfacial recession.

## Summary

Pirc et al. (University of Zurich, JERD 2026) conducted the first systematic review and meta-analysis specifically examining how emergence profile concavity or convexity affects midfacial mucosal stability in single-crown implant restorations. Four RCTs (Barwacz 2025, Koutouzis 2019, Siegenthaler 2022, Wang 2022) with 144 implants and 12-month follow-up were pooled. The primary analysis yielded MD=-0.31mm (95% CI -0.63 to 0.02; p=0.064) favoring concave profiles, with low heterogeneity (I²=28%). Borderline significance in the primary analysis was attributed partly to one study (Barwacz 2025) comparing concave vs linearly divergent profiles with minimal morphological contrast. Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis confirmed consistent directionality across all iterations; excluding Barwacz 2025 produced a statistically significant result (MD=-0.43mm; p=0.002). The biological rationale proposes that concave profiles provide additional "running room" for soft tissue adaptation, favoring coronal tissue creep and epithelial barrier integrity.

## Key Contributions

- First meta-analysis with midfacial mucosal stability as primary endpoint for EP design comparison
- Consistent directional effect (concave > convex) across all leave-one-out iterations
- Low heterogeneity (I²=28%) — biological consistency across settings
- Provides quantitative estimate: ~0.3 mm greater recession with convex profiles at 12 months
- Supports prosthetically driven implant treatment concept: EP design = integral to surgical-prosthetic planning

## Methodology

PRISMA 2020; PROSPERO CRD420251139042. Search: MEDLINE + Embase to May 2026. Only RCTs; midfacial mucosal level changes (mm) as primary outcome. 4 RCTs, 144 implants. Risk of bias: RoB 2 — all "some concerns." Statistics: random-effects MA; weighted MD; REML; leave-one-out sensitivity analysis.

## Results

**Primary analysis (4 RCTs, 128 implants in synthesis):**
- MD = -0.31 mm (95% CI -0.63 to 0.02; p = 0.064)
- I² = 28%; test for heterogeneity p = 0.27

**Sensitivity analysis (leave-one-out range):**
- All iterations: -0.12 to -0.43 mm; direction consistently favors concave
- Excluding Barwacz 2025: MD = -0.43 mm (95% CI -0.70 to -0.16; **p = 0.002**)

**Included RCTs:**

| Study | n (implants) | EP comparison | Site |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barwacz 2025 | 54 | Concave vs linearly divergent | Anterior maxilla |
| Koutouzis 2019 | 26 | Convex vs concave | Maxillary premolar |
| Siegenthaler 2022 | 44 | Convex vs concave | Ant. mandible/maxilla |
| Wang 2022 | 20 | Buccal convex vs original EP | Mandibular molar |
