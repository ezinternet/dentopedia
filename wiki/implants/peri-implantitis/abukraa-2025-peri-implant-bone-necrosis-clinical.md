## Three-line Summary

Case series (5 patients, 10 implants) of peri-implant bone necrosis (PIBN): 3 early cases (overcompression at placement) and 2 late cases (peri-implantitis sequela), all managed by implant + necrotic bone removal with no recurrence.

Two cases biopsy-confirmed histologically — both showed the same pattern: multiple nonvital bone fragments, empty osteocyte lacunae, acute and chronic inflammatory cell infiltrate — regardless of the precipitating mechanism.

PIBN is rare but clinically critical; etiology bifurcates into mechanical (overcompression at insertion) and inflammatory (peri-implantitis), but both produce a convergent histological picture.

## Summary

Peri-implant bone necrosis (PIBN) is an uncommon but serious complication that contributes to implant failure. This 2025 case series from the University of Michigan presents 5 patients (10 total failed implants) to characterize two distinct PIBN pathways. Three cases occurred early — immediately after implant placement — with bone overcompression during placement as the likely cause. Two cases presented late (1–5 years post-placement) as a sequela of peri-implantitis. In two cases, bone biopsies were obtained and histopathological examination confirmed the diagnosis, revealing the classic pattern of bone necrosis: multiple nonvital bone fragments surrounded by acute and chronic inflammatory cells, and empty osteocyte lacunae. All five patients were successfully treated by removing the implants and associated necrotic bone, with no signs of recurrence at follow-up. The study concludes that PIBN results either from mechanical overcompression at insertion or from peri-implantitis-driven inflammation, and that the histological endpoint of both pathways is convergent — making etiology-specific diagnosis dependent on clinical timing and context rather than histology alone.

## Key Contributions

- First human case series to histologically document **overcompression as a cause of PIBN** — empty osteocyte lacunae confirmed in early-presenting cases.
- Two-pathway PIBN framework: (1) mechanical early-onset (overcompression → ischemia → necrosis); (2) inflammatory late-onset (peri-implantitis → osteoclastic + inflammatory → necrosis).
- Both pathways share an identical histological endpoint: empty lacunae + nonvital bone + inflammatory infiltrate — explaining why X-ray cannot differentiate.
- Management validation: surgical implant removal + necrotic bone debridement is curative; no recurrence in any of the 5 cases.
- Temporal staging: early PIBN (<1 month) → favor overcompression; late PIBN (>1 year) → favor peri-implantitis.

## Methodology

Case series. 5 patients, 10 failed implants total. Early group (n=3 cases): PIBN manifesting immediately after implant placement. Late group (n=2 cases): PIBN manifesting 1–5 years after placement. Diagnostic workup: clinical presentation, radiographic evaluation, histopathological biopsy in 2 of 5 cases. Treatment: implant removal + surgical excision of necrotic bone. Outcome measure: clinical resolution without recurrence.
