## Three-line Summary

Randomized clinical trial comparing nonsurgical peri-implantitis management with erythritol air polishing versus ultrasonic instrumentation, each combined with systemic metronidazole, integrating clinical and patient-centered outcomes over 12 months (sample size not reported in the abstract).

Ultrasonic instrumentation reduced probing depth and bleeding on probing throughout the 12-month period (abstract does not report effect sizes).

Ultrasonic instrumentation appeared favorable for clinical outcomes, but this rests on an abstract-only reading — group sizes, effect magnitudes, and patient-reported results must be checked in the full text before use.

## Summary

This randomized clinical trial from the University of Porto compared two mechanical approaches to nonsurgical peri-implantitis management, erythritol air polishing and ultrasonic instrumentation, each combined with systemic metronidazole, and assessed both clinical and patient-centered outcomes over 12 months. According to the abstract, ultrasonic instrumentation reduced probing depth and bleeding on probing throughout the 12-month period. The abstract does not report sample size, effect sizes, or the patient-centered results, so the finding should be treated as provisional until the full text is read.

## Key Contributions

- Direct comparison of air polishing versus ultrasonic instrumentation, both with systemic metronidazole, in peri-implantitis.
- Integrates clinical and patient-centered outcomes.
- Ultrasonic instrumentation reduced probing depth and bleeding on probing at every point through 12 months.

## Methodology

Randomized clinical trial; arms were erythritol air polishing plus systemic metronidazole versus ultrasonic instrumentation plus systemic metronidazole; 12-month follow-up. Sample size and exact outcome instruments are not stated in the abstract.
