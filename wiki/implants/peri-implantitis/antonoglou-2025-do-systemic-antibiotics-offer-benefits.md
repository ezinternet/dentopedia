## Three-line Summary

Systematic review with random-effects meta-analyses (PROSPERO CRD42019135753) of 7 studies (3 RCTs + 4 non-RCTs; 595 patients, 1388 implants) comparing surgical peri-implantitis treatment with vs. without adjunct systemic antibiotics, six databases searched to December 2024.

Short-term (1–2 years) treatment success favoured antibiotics (OR 2.33, 95% CI 1.29–4.21), with more radiographic bone gain (MD 0.37 mm), better bone-level stability (OR 2.73), less BoP (OR 0.49) and less SoP (OR 0.33), but no long-term (≥3 years) benefit (OR 1.84, p=0.43) and benefit confined to modified-surface implants (OR 4.10 vs 0.79 for turned).

Certainty is mostly low and harms data come from one trial (diarrhoea 21.2% vs 0%), so this supports at most case-by-case short-term prescribing for modified-surface implants rather than routine adjunctive use.

## 세줄요약

외과적 임플란트주위염 (Surgical Peri-Implantitis) 치료에 전신 항생제 (Systemic Antibiotics)를 보조하는 것의 이득을 평가한 체계적 문헌고찰+메타분석 (Systematic Review with Meta-Analyses, SR+MA) — 7편 (무작위 3 + 비무작위 4), 환자 595명, 임플란트 1,388개, 6개 데이터베이스 검색 (~2024년 12월).

단기 (1–2년) 치료 성공 (Treatment Success) 은 항생제군 우위 (Odds Ratio, OR 2.33; 95% CI 1.29–4.21)였고 방사선학적 골수준 (Radiographic Bone Level, RBL) +0.37 mm, 골소실 <0.5 mm 유지 OR 2.73, 탐침출혈 (Bleeding on Probing, BoP) OR 0.49, 화농 (Suppuration on Probing, SoP) OR 0.33 모두 유의 — 그러나 장기 (≥3년) 성공 이득은 없었고 (OR 1.84, p=0.43), 이득은 표면처리 (Modified) 임플란트에만 국한됐다 (OR 4.10 vs 무처리 0.79).

근거 확실성 (GRADE) 은 대부분 '낮음 (Low)'이고 이상반응 자료는 단일 시험뿐이어서 (설사 21.2% vs 0%), 상시 처방이 아니라 표면처리 임플란트에서 위험도 기반 사례별 단기 처방만 정당화된다.

## Summary

This systematic review with meta-analyses pooled 7 comparative studies (3 RCTs, 4 non-randomised; 595 patients, 1388 implants) to test whether adjunct systemic antibiotics improve surgical peri-implantitis treatment. Antibiotics were associated with greater short-term (1–2 year) treatment success (OR 2.33, 95% CI 1.29–4.21; 455 → 660 successes per 1000), more radiographic bone gain (MD 0.37 mm), greater likelihood of keeping bone loss ≤0.5 mm (OR 2.73), and less bleeding (OR 0.49) and suppuration (OR 0.33) on probing, while PPD change did not differ and recession increased slightly (0.18 mm). The effect disappeared at ≥3 years (OR 1.84, p=0.43; RCT-only OR 0.69) and was confined to modified-surface implants (OR 4.10 vs 0.79 for turned), plausibly because modified surfaces are harder to decontaminate. GRADE certainty was low for most pooled effects and moderate for the harms signal (diarrhoea 21.2% vs 0%), so the authors conclude that antibiotics may help selected modified-surface cases in the short term but are not justified routinely, and that shorter regimens plus supportive care are preferable.

## Key Contributions

- Separates the **time horizon** of the antibiotic effect: a real short-term (1–2 year) success gain that vanishes at ≥3 years — the cleanest statement yet that a 3–7 day antibiotic course cannot substitute for decontamination and supportive care.
- Establishes an **implant-surface interaction**: benefit in modified-surface implants (OR 4.10) but none in turned implants (OR 0.79), with control-group success of 76.2% (turned) vs 36.9% (modified), p=0.001.
- Provides **GRADE-rated** estimates rather than bare p-values: short-term success low, bone-level stability moderate, residual PPD ≥5 mm and SoP high, diarrhoea moderate — useful for guideline work.
- Adds the **harms** dimension (diarrhoea 21.2% vs 0%; phenoxymethylpenicillin+metronidazole worse than amoxicillin+metronidazole, 34.6% vs 7.7%, p=0.03) that most prior antibiotic reviews in implant dentistry omitted.
- Offers a practical prescribing frame: case-by-case for modified surfaces, likely unnecessary when implantoplasty has been performed, shorter courses preferred.

## Methodology

PROSPERO-registered SR (CRD42019135753) following the Cochrane Handbook and PRISMA. Six electronic databases were searched from inception to December 2024, supplemented by grey literature and reference/citation lists. RCTs and non-randomised comparative studies in adults with peri-implantitis treated surgically ± adjunct systemic antibiotics, with ≥12 months follow-up, were eligible. Two authors independently selected studies, extracted data and assessed risk of bias (RoB 2.0 for RCTs, ROBINS-I V2 for non-RCTs). Random-effects meta-analyses (restricted maximum likelihood) pooled ORs or MDs with 95% CIs, τ²/I² and 95% prediction intervals; subgroups compared RCTs vs non-RCTs and turned vs modified implant surfaces; certainty was rated with GRADE. Primary outcome was treatment success (disease resolution); secondary outcomes were PPD, RBL, BoP, SoP and recession, plus adverse effects. Individual patient data were requested, but only one author group supplied a complete dataset.
