## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42021254350) of 12 systematic reviews (2015-2020) covering 17 primary clinical trials (2014-2020) on adjunctive treatments (antiseptics, probiotics, air-polishing, laser/photodynamic therapy, antibiotics) added to non-surgical mechanical debridement (NSMD) for peri-implant mucositis (PIM).

Six SRs performing meta-analysis found no significant difference in probing depth, bleeding on probing, clinical attachment level, or plaque index between NSMD alone and NSMD + adjunctive treatment; vote-counting confirmed no additional benefit for antiseptics or probiotics specifically.

Overall AMSTAR 2 confidence of included SRs ranged from critically low to low, and about two-thirds of primary studies had high risk of bias — no adjunctive therapy has emerged as clearly superior to NSMD alone for managing PIM.

## Summary

This umbrella review (an overview of systematic reviews, PROSPERO CRD42021254350) synthesized 12 systematic reviews (2015-2020, all outside the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews) covering 17 primary clinical trials (16 RCTs + 1 CCT, 2014-2020) that tested adjunctive treatments — antiseptics (chlorhexidine 0.12-0.5%), probiotics, air-polishing (glycine powder), laser/photodynamic therapy (diode laser), and antibiotics (systemic azithromycin or local tetracycline fibers) — added to non-surgical mechanical debridement (NSMD) for peri-implant mucositis (PIM). All 12 SRs were graded using AMSTAR 2, and none reached "high" or "moderate" overall confidence: 7 were "low" and 5 "critically low." Six SRs that performed meta-analysis found no significant difference between NSMD alone and NSMD + adjunct across probing depth, bleeding on probing, clinical attachment level, or plaque index. A vote-counting synthesis (requiring ≥3 primary studies per adjunct, benefit concluded only if >2/3 of studies were significantly positive) likewise found no additional benefit for antiseptics or probiotics; other adjuncts had too few primary studies to vote-count. The authors conclude that, within the current low-quality evidence base, no adjunctive therapy has emerged as clearly superior to NSMD alone for managing PIM.

## Key Contributions

- First umbrella review focused specifically on adjunctive treatments for PIM (rather than peri-implantitis or mixed PIM/peri-implantitis populations).
- AMSTAR 2 quality grading of all 12 included SRs — all rated low or critically low, none moderate/high.
- Vote-counting synthesis method applied where meta-analysis was infeasible or not performed by the original SR (≥3 studies required; >2/3 significant-positive threshold for "benefit").
- Citation-network mapping revealing that the 17 primary trials are heavily reused/overlapping across the 12 SRs — a caution against treating "5 concordant SRs" as 5 independent lines of evidence.
- Flags adjuncts with zero existing SR-level evidence (ozone therapy, desiccant application, electrolytic cleaning, herbal medications).

## Methodology

- Design: umbrella review (overview of systematic reviews) per pre-registered PROSPERO protocol (CRD42021254350).
- Search: 7 databases (Cochrane Library, Embase, MEDLINE, ProQuest, PROSPERO, Scopus, Web of Science) to 15 Sep 2021, English only; dual search-strategy approach for sensitivity.
- Selection: 2 independent reviewers (title/abstract kappa 0.97, full-text kappa 0.89) + 3rd reviewer for consensus; 701 records → 22 full-text screened → 12 SRs included / 10 excluded.
- Eligibility: SRs of adult PIM patients comparing NSMD ± adjunctive treatment; peri-implantitis-only SRs excluded; ≥1 eligible primary study per included SR.
- Quality appraisal: AMSTAR 2 (7 critical + 9 non-critical domains), independently rated by 2 reviewers.
- Synthesis: meta-analysis outcomes reported where the original SR performed one; vote-counting applied elsewhere (minimum 3 primary studies, >2/3 significant-positive threshold for concluding benefit).

## Results

- 12 SRs (2015-2020) / 17 primary clinical trials (16 RCT + 1 CCT, 2014-2020) included.
- AMSTAR 2 overall confidence: 7 "low," 5 "critically low" — no SR rated moderate or high. Most common critical flaw: no interpretation of risk of bias when discussing results (9/12 SRs); second: no publication-bias assessment (5/12 SRs).
- Adjunctive modalities: antiseptics (5 RCTs, CHX 0.12-0.5%, 10 days-12 weeks), probiotics (6 RCTs, lozenges ± sulcus application, 3 weeks-3 months), air-polishing (1 RCT + 1 CCT, glycine powder, submucosal, 5 sec/site), laser/photodynamic therapy (2 RCTs, diode laser, 30 sec/surface), antibiotics (2 RCTs — systemic azithromycin 5 days; local tetracycline HCl fibers 10 days).
- 6 SRs performed meta-analysis: no significant difference vs. NSMD alone in probing depth, bleeding on probing, clinical attachment level, or plaque index.
- Vote-counting: no additional benefit for antiseptics (probing depth, BOP) or probiotics (probing depth, BOP, plaque index); other adjuncts had <3 primary studies, precluding vote-counting.
- Only 2 primary studies (antiseptic adjuncts) showed a significant short-term (≤3 month) advantage over control, without extended follow-up to confirm persistence.
- ~2/3 of the 17 primary studies were rated high risk of bias.
