## Three-line Summary

Prospective single-arm cohort (30 patients, one peri-implantitis implant each, 29 completed 10 years; single specialist practice, Sydney) treated with surgical reconstruction (24% EDTA decontamination + deproteinised bovine bone mineral with 10% collagen (DBBMC) + enamel matrix derivative (EMD) + doxycycline, coronally advanced flap) and individualised 4-6-monthly supportive peri-implant therapy (SPIT).

Implant survival was 29/30 (96.6%); probing depth fell from 8.90 ± 1.90 mm to 3.06 ± 0.98 mm and radiographic bone loss from 57% to 12.86% (stable from year 2); 12 implants needed azithromycin for recurrent suppuration; only 58.6% (17/29) met the composite successful-treatment-outcome criterion (STOC) at 10 years.

Survival is high but "success" is about 60% even in healthy non-smokers with crater-like defects and strict maintenance; no control arm, one operator, and a small cohort mean the contribution of each component (EMD, doxycycline, DBBMC, SPIT) cannot be separated.

## Summary

This single-arm prospective cohort followed 30 systemically healthy, non-smoking, periodontally stable patients (one peri-implantitis implant each, crater-like or circumferential defects) for 10 years after surgical reconstruction with 24% EDTA decontamination and a mixture of deproteinised bovine bone mineral with 10% collagen (DBBMC), enamel matrix derivative (EMD) and doxycycline powder, closed with a coronally advanced flap. All patients received individualised supportive peri-implant therapy (mostly every 4-6 months). Twenty-nine of 30 implants survived (96.6%), and probing depth and radiographic bone loss improved by year 2 and then stayed stable. However, only 17 of 29 implants (58.6%) met the composite successful-treatment-outcome criterion (STOC) at 10 years, and 12 implants needed azithromycin for recurrent suppuration. The data come from one operator in one practice with no control arm.

## Key Contributions
- A 10-year dataset for a uniform EDTA + DBBMC/EMD/doxycycline reconstructive protocol, extending the group's earlier 3-year report.
- Separates survival (96.6%) from composite success (58.6%): the gap between the two endpoints is the main message.
- No re-surgery was needed in 10 years; recurrent suppuration (12 implants) was handled with debridement plus a 3-day azithromycin course.
- A small non-randomised CTG subgroup (8 implants) showed more keratinised tissue (1.93 vs 1.34 mm) and less recession (0.00 vs 0.56 mm).

## Methodology
Prospective single-arm cohort, 30 consecutive referrals (2009-2012, Western Sydney Specialist Periodontal Practice); inclusion required PD ≥ 6 mm with BOP/suppuration, ≥ 20% radiographic bone loss and ≥ 2 years in function; full-mouth plaque/bleeding < 20%. Clinical/radiographic data at baseline, 2, 3, 5 and 10 years by a calibrated examiner not involved in surgery. STOC at 10 years: PD < 5 mm, no further bone loss > 10% vs baseline, no BOP/suppuration, recession < 0.5 mm (anterior) or < 1.5 mm (posterior). Paired t-tests, p < 0.01.

## Results
| Outcome | Baseline | 2 y | 5 y | 10 y |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Probing depth (mm) | 8.90 ± 1.90 | 3.50 ± 0.50 | 3.20 ± 1.12 | 3.06 ± 0.98 |
| Radiographic bone loss (mm) | 6.92 ± 1.26 | 2.62 ± 0.80 | 2.73 ± 0.67 | 2.78 ± 0.56 |
| Radiographic bone loss (%) | 57 ± 16.5 | 14.8 ± 8.2 | 12.3 ± 2.3 | 12.86 ± 2.47 |
| Mid-buccal recession (mm) | 0.16 ± 0.14 | 0.15 ± 0.12 | 0.20 ± 0.42 | 0.48 ± 0.96 (NS) |
| Keratinised tissue (mm) | 1.79 | 1.90 | 1.50 | 1.48 |

- Survival 29/30 (96.66%); one mandibular posterior implant lost by year 8.
- STOC success 58.62% (17/29); failures 12: persistent BOP 5, further bone loss 4, anterior recession 2, posterior recession 1, PD > 5 mm 0. Counting the removed implant (derived calculation, not in the paper): 17/30 = 56.7%.
- Suppuration recurred in 12 implants; FMBS 70% at baseline to 15-18% during follow-up.
- Values at 2, 3, 5 and 10 years did not differ significantly from each other; all differed from baseline for PD and bone loss (p < 0.01).

## Limitations
No control arm, so EMD, doxycycline, DBBMC and SPIT effects are inseparable; one operator who is also author/analyst; small single-institution cohort of healthy non-smokers with crater-like defects (limits generalisation); paired t-tests only, no adjustment for implant surface or defect type; the STOC denominator excludes the lost implant; the CTG comparison is confounded by indication. The text contains minor inconsistencies (for example the 5-year recession comparison quotes the baseline value 0.16 mm; 40% vs 41.37% STOC failure) that do not change the conclusions.
