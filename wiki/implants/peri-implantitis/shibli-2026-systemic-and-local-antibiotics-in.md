## Three-line Summary
Systematic review with pairwise and network meta-analysis (to March 2026; 21 studies, 1,718 implants; random-effects) of systemic and local antibiotics added to mechanical debridement in surgical and non-surgical peri-implantitis treatment, on PPD, CAL, BoP, PLI and MBL.

Systemic metronidazole + amoxicillin was most effective for PPD and MBL, especially with surgery; metronidazole alone was most effective with non-surgical treatment (notably CAL and MBL); among local agents, minocycline microspheres and local amoxicillin + metronidazole ranked best, and antibiotics overall improved clinical parameters versus debridement alone.

Local antibiotics helped but appeared less effective than systemic regimens; the abstract gives no effect sizes, certainty grading, harms or resistance data, and the systemic-first conclusion conflicts with Liu 2026 (no bone benefit) and with guideline caution against routine systemic antibiotics.

## Summary
This Periodontology 2000 review pooled 21 studies (1,718 implants) on adjunctive antibiotics with mechanical debridement for peri-implantitis and used a network meta-analysis to rank individual agents and regimens separately for surgical and non-surgical treatment. Systemic metronidazole plus amoxicillin ranked best for PPD and MBL reduction, particularly as an adjunct to open flap debridement, while systemic metronidazole alone ranked best in non-surgical therapy. Among local agents, minocycline microspheres and local amoxicillin plus metronidazole performed best, but local delivery as a whole appeared less effective than systemic regimens. Because only the abstract was retrieved, the size of these advantages, their certainty and the harms side of the balance cannot be judged, and the ranking should be read against Liu 2026 and the EFP S3 guideline before it shapes prescribing.

## Key Contributions
- Ranks antibiotic agents and regimens individually (metronidazole, amoxicillin + metronidazole, local minocycline microspheres, local amoxicillin + metronidazole) instead of pooling "antibiotics" as one class.
- Separates surgical from non-surgical treatment, a stratification Liu 2026 lacked.
- Finds systemic regimens rank above local ones, and a bone-level (MBL) benefit for systemic amoxicillin + metronidazole, siding with Bai 2024 against Liu 2026 on the bone outcome.
- Places local minocycline (microspheres) among the best local agents, which is the closest held SR-level signal for local minocycline products.

## Methodology
Systematic review of five databases plus gray literature through March 2026; 21 studies with 1,718 implants; pairwise and network meta-analyses with random-effects models. Comparisons: systemic or local antibiotics plus mechanical debridement versus debridement alone or other regimens, in surgical and non-surgical treatment. Outcomes: PPD, CAL, BoP, PLI and MBL, with microbiological findings described qualitatively. Study-design mix, risk-of-bias tool, certainty grading and network-consistency methods are not stated in the abstract.

## Critical Appraisal
- Rankings without effect sizes: a network meta-analysis can rank a regimen first on a small or uncertain margin; without SUCRA values, CIs or certainty grading the clinical size of "most effective" is unknown.
- The conclusion recommends systemic metronidazole (non-surgical) and amoxicillin + metronidazole (surgical) as most effective, but no harms or resistance data appear in the abstract; the EFP S3 guideline ([[implants/peri-implantitis/herrera-2023-prevention-treatment-peri-implant-efp]]) advises against routine systemic antibiotics, and a surgical RCT ([[implants/peri-implantitis/hakkers-2026-adjunctive-systemic-amoxicillin-and-metronidazole]]) found amoxicillin + metronidazole lowered only BoP, with no PPD or MBL benefit.
- Not a supersession of Liu 2026: both are SR+MA of similar size (21 studies vs 22 RCTs), Liu is full-text and RCT-only while this NMA is abstract-only with an unstated design mix, so neither clearly outweighs the other; the bone-level and route questions remain undetermined.
- Formulation caveat: minocycline here is microspheres; a 2% minocycline HCl ointment (as in [[implants/peri-implantitis/lee-2026-minocycline-local-delivery-peri-implantitis-multicenter-rct]]) is a different vehicle, so the local ranking supports the drug class more than any specific ointment product.
