## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional radiographic analysis (University of Washington; 96 patients/225 implants in the source data set, 83 patients/168 implants analysed; mean follow-up 10.9 years) of restoration emergence angle and convex/concave contour as risk indicators for peri-implantitis.

In bone-level implants, peri-implantitis prevalence was 31.3% at emergence angle >30° vs 15.1% at ≤30° (p = .04); convex contour combined with >30° gave 37.8% with a significant angle × profile interaction (p = .003), while tissue-level implants showed no such association.

Supports keeping the restoration emergence angle ≤30° and avoiding convex contours specifically in bone-level implants; observational, abstract-only evidence — an association, not causation.

## Summary

This cross-sectional radiographic study asked whether the contour of the implant restoration — specifically its emergence angle and whether its transmucosal profile is convex or concave — is associated with peri-implantitis. From a data set of 96 patients and 225 implants (mean follow-up 10.9 years), 83 patients with 168 implants met inclusion criteria; radiographs supplied the angle and profile measurements, and peri-implantitis was defined by probing depth plus radiographic bone loss. In bone-level implants, prevalence was 31.3% above 30° versus 15.1% at or below 30° (p = .04), and a convex contour combined with >30° reached 37.8% with a significant angle-by-profile interaction (p = .003). No such relationship was seen for tissue-level implants. The result is the primary observational anchor for the now-common 30°/convex-contour rule, but it is cross-sectional and, in this abstract-only ingest, verifiable only at abstract level.

## Key Contributions

- Restoration contour (emergence angle + profile shape) framed as a measurable risk indicator for peri-implantitis rather than an esthetic-only concern.
- 30° threshold with approximately doubled peri-implantitis prevalence in bone-level implants (>30°: 31.3% vs ≤30°: 15.1%, p = .04).
- Significant interaction between angle and profile: convex + >30° → 37.8% prevalence (p = .003).
- Implant-design boundary: the association did not hold for tissue-level implants.
- GEE used to account for multiple implants per patient.

## Methodology

- **Design**: cross-sectional radiographic analysis of an existing patient/implant data set.
- **Source data set**: 96 patients, 225 implants, mean follow-up 10.9 years; analysed sample 83 patients, 168 implants.
- **Exposure**: restoration emergence angle (threshold 30°) and restoration profile (convex vs concave), measured on radiographs.
- **Outcome**: peri-implantitis diagnosed from probing depth and radiographic bone loss.
- **Stratification**: bone-level vs tissue-level implants.
- **Analysis**: generalized estimating equations (GEE).
- **Abstract-only limitation**: measurement protocol, examiner calibration, exact case definition and reasons for exclusions are not available in the retrieved text.

## Results

| Implant group | Exposure | Peri-implantitis prevalence | Comparison / p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bone-level | Emergence angle >30° | 31.3% | vs ≤30°, p = .04 |
| Bone-level | Emergence angle ≤30° | 15.1% | reference |
| Bone-level | Convex profile + angle >30° | 37.8% | angle × profile interaction p = .003 |
| Tissue-level | Emergence angle >30° vs ≤30° | not reported in abstract | no correlation found |

Authors' conclusion: >30° is a significant risk indicator and convex profile adds further risk **for bone-level implants, but not for tissue-level implants**.
