## Three-line Summary
Compiled 54 intraoral periapical radiographs from 36 case reports to propose the first quantitative radiographic classification of retrograde peri-implantitis: mild (<25%), moderate (25–50%), and advanced (>50%) of implant length measured from the apex.
Radiographic hallmark: radiolucency confined to implant apex with coronal osseointegration maintained — distinguishing RPI from conventional marginal bone loss peri-implantitis.
Class-specific treatment recommendations: antibiotics ± endodontic treatment for mild; surgical debridement + GBR for moderate; implant removal for advanced.

## Summary
Shah et al. reviewed 36 published case reports, collecting 54 intraoral periapical radiographs (IOPAs) showing apical radiolucency around dental implants. They quantified radiolucency extent as the percentage of implant length involved from the apex, establishing three severity classes with clinical implications. The key radiographic distinguishing feature is localization of radiolucency at the implant apex with intact coronal osseointegration — the opposite pattern from conventional peri-implantitis where bone loss begins at the implant crest. This classification is widely cited as the standard radiographic staging tool for IPL/RPI and is integrated into the Gong 2022 histopathological treatment decision tree.

## Key Contributions
- First quantitative radiographic severity classification for RPI using standardized IOPA measurements
- Establishes the radiographic hallmark: apical radiolucency + preserved coronal osseointegration
- Provides class-specific treatment and prognosis guidelines enabling risk-stratified management
- Defines measurement methodology: (distance from apex to most coronal extent of radiolucency) / (total implant length) × 100%

## Methodology
**Study type**: Literature review / retrospective radiographic analysis  
**Source**: 36 case reports (54 IOPAs compiled)  
**Search**: PubMed ("retrograde peri-implantitis," "implant periapical lesion")  
**Measurement**: Radiolucency extent from implant apex as percentage of total implant length

## Results

### Radiographic Classification

| Class | Radiolucency Extent | Clinical State | Recommended Treatment | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | < 25% from apex | Early lesion, osseointegration largely intact | Antibiotics ± endodontic treatment of adjacent tooth | Good |
| **Moderate** | 25–50% from apex | Moderate bone loss, implant stability possible | Surgical debridement + GBR ± apicoectomy | Guarded |
| **Advanced** | > 50% from apex | Extensive bone loss, mobility risk | Implant removal + re-implantation | Poor |

### Defining Radiographic Features of RPI
- Radiolucency **confined to the implant apex** (periapical region)
- **Coronal osseointegration preserved** — at least in early presentations
- No marginal bone loss — distinguishes RPI from conventional crestal-pattern peri-implantitis

### Clinical Significance
- Apical localization = diagnostic key: radiolucency at the tip in the context of a new-onset lesion is RPI until proven otherwise
- Adjacent tooth periapical status must be evaluated: untreated root canal / periapical lesion in adjacent tooth is the primary etiology (see Gao 2024)
