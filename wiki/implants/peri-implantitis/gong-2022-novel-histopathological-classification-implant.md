## Three-line Summary
Systematic review of 28 studies (509 searched) proposes the first histopathology-based 5-class IPL classification: inflammation, abscess, cyst, foreign body reaction, poor bone healing.
Periapical cyst (Class III) is most common — 21/37 implants (57%) — a previously unrecognized category in prior classifications.
Apicoectomy achieves 97% success (38/39); cyst removal alone yields only 33%, supporting combined surgical approach.

## Summary
Gong et al. performed a PRISMA systematic review to synthesize histopathological evidence on implant periapical lesions (IPL) and propose a novel 5-class classification. From 509 identified records, 28 studies (27 case reports + 1 retrospective) were included, covering 37 implants with histopathological confirmation. The study addressed a key gap: prior IPL classifications (Penarrocha-Diago active/inactive; Sussman etiology-based) lacked histopathological grounding and failed to recognize periapical cyst as a distinct entity. The new classification enables treatment selection based on tissue pathology rather than symptoms or etiology alone. All included cases showed radiolucency at the implant apex, confirming that apical radiolucency is the defining radiographic feature of IPL — while coronal osseointegration was typically maintained in early stages.

## Key Contributions
- First histopathology-based IPL classification system — 5 classes covering all known tissue presentations
- Establishes periapical cyst (Class III) as the most prevalent IPL type (57%), previously absent from classification systems
- Provides treatment decision tree integrating histopathology, radiolucency extent, and clinical symptoms
- Demonstrates antibiotic-only therapy is insufficient for most IPL presentations
- Shows apicoectomy combined with cyst enucleation achieves near-complete implant salvage (97%)

## Methodology
**Design**: PRISMA systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42022378001)  
**Databases**: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science; period 1992–2021  
**Keywords**: "retrograde peri-implantitis," "apical peri-implantitis," "implant periapical lesion"  
**Inclusion**: Human studies with IPL confirmed by radiography AND histopathology  
**Exclusion**: Reviews, conference papers, non-English, insufficient data  
**N**: 509 identified → 28 included (27 case reports + 1 retrospective study, 37 implants total)  
**Quality**: JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist (9 low / 13 moderate / 6 high risk of bias)

## Results

### 5-Class Histopathological Classification

| Class | Histopathology | n (%) | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| I-a | Chronic periapical granuloma | 5 (14%) | Debridement ± GBR |
| I-b | Chronic suppurated + fistulized | 6 (16%) | Debridement + drainage |
| I-c | Acute non-suppurative inflammation | 1 (3%) | Antibiotics + monitor |
| II | Acute suppurated abscess | 2 (5%) | Urgent drainage; high failure risk |
| III | Periapical cyst | 21 (57%) | Cyst enucleation + apicoectomy |
| IV | Foreign body reaction | 1 (3%) | Debridement + source removal |
| V | Poor bone healing (aseptic) | 2 (5%) | GBR ± re-implantation |

### Treatment Outcomes by Approach
| Treatment | n | Success |
|---|---|---|
| Apicoectomy (retrospective series) | 39 | 38/39 (97%) |
| Cyst removal alone | 9 | 3/9 (33%) |
| Cyst removal + apicoectomy | all remaining | 100% |
| Antibiotics alone | — | Effective in 2 cases only |
| Class II acute suppurated | 2 | Both failed |

**Radiographic feature**: All 37 cases showed apical radiolucency; coronal osseointegration maintained in early-stage cases.
