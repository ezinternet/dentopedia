## Three-line Summary

The largest published human ground-section histology series of retrieved mobility-failed implants (n=51): all specimens showed a 600–1,100 µm thick fibrous connective tissue layer between the implant and surrounding bone, which was compact and highly mineralized with few remodeling areas and no bacteria at the coronal surface.

The histological pattern — fibrous encapsulation on compact, mature bone with minimal remodeling — is mechanistically consistent with osseointegration that was established but then disrupted by mechanical overload or trauma, not by infection-driven bone loss.

Authors conclude late osseointegration failure is multifactorial (poor bone quality + mechanical trauma + overloading), and the fibrous encapsulation endpoint is histologically distinct from early thermal necrosis or the osteolytic pocket pattern of peri-implantitis.

## Summary

This 2003 Piattelli paper is the largest histological series of retrieved failed implants in the literature at the time of publication: 51 root-form implants removed for clinical mobility over 5 years, each evaluated by ground-section histology. The defining finding was a universal 600–1,100 µm thick fibrous connective tissue layer between the implant surface and the surrounding bone in all 51 specimens. The surrounding bone was consistently described as compact and highly mineralized, with well-structured Haversian canals and few areas of active remodeling. Crucially, no bacteria were found at the most coronal portion of the implants, and inflammatory cell infiltrate was scarce. Approximately 10% of specimens showed epithelial extension around the implant perimeter. The authors interpreted these findings as consistent with mechanical overload, mechanical trauma to bone, and/or poor bone quality as the primary failure pathway — not infection. The fibrous tissue layer represents an organized, avascular barrier replacing bone contact: osseointegration was most likely achieved initially but disrupted, with fibrous encapsulation as the histological end-state.

## Key Contributions

- Largest (n=51) human ground-section histological series of late mobility-failed implants.
- Universal fibrous encapsulation (600–1,100 µm) as the histological signature of late mechanical osseointegration failure.
- No bacterial presence at coronal surface distinguishes mechanical late-failure from infection-driven peri-implantitis.
- Compact, minimally remodeling surrounding bone: supports that osseointegration was present before failure, ruling out a de-novo failure of osseointegration.
- Differentiates three failure patterns histologically: early thermal necrosis (Piattelli 1998 — bone sequestra), late fibrous encapsulation (this paper — compact bone + fibrous layer), and peri-implantitis (osteolytic pocket + bacterial plaque).

## Methodology

Retrospective case series over 5 years. 51 root-form implants from multiple cases retrieved for clinical mobility. Ground histological sections prepared for each retrieved implant. Evaluated for connective tissue thickness, bone structure (Haversian canals, remodeling activity, mineralization), bacterial presence, inflammatory infiltrate, and epithelial migration.
