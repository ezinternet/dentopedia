## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 5 systematic/meta-analysis studies (from 501 initial records, search through December 2022) comparing electronic cigarettes vs traditional cigarettes vs nonsmokers on periodontal and peri-implant clinical outcomes.

All 5 included SRs found that, compared with traditional tobacco smoke, e-cigarettes may reduce or not change clinical inflammatory symptoms of periodontitis/peri-implantitis (bleeding on probing, probing depth, peri-implant bone loss), but e-cigarettes still contain nicotine and are linked to a wide range of adverse oral-health effects vs nonsmokers.

Evidence is qualitative/narrative (umbrella of heterogeneous SRs, mostly cross-sectional primary studies) — e-cigarettes should not be presented as a "safe" alternative for implant health, and long-term chronic peri-implantitis risk from vaping remains insufficiently quantified.

## 세줄요약

우산 리뷰(Umbrella Review) — 초기 501편에서 선별된 체계적 문헌고찰/메타분석 5편(2022년 12월까지 검색)을 종합해 전자담배(e-cigarette) 대 전통 담배 대 비흡연자의 치주·임플란트주위염(peri-implantitis) 임상결과를 비교.

포함된 5개 SR 모두에서 전자담배는 전통 담배 대비 치주염·임플란트주위염의 임상적 염증 증상(탐침시출혈, 탐침깊이, 임플란트주위 골소실)을 감소시키거나 변화 없음을 보였으나, 전자담배도 니코틴을 함유하며 비흡연자 대비 광범위한 구강건강 악영향과 연관됨.

근거는 정성적(narrative)이고 기저 원발연구 대부분 단면연구이므로 전자담배를 임플란트 건강에 "안전한" 대안으로 제시해서는 안 되며, 장기 만성 임플란트주위염 위험에 대한 정량적 근거는 여전히 불충분함.

## Summary
This umbrella review (PRISMA-based) pooled 5 systematic reviews/meta-analyses identified from 501 initial records searched across PubMed, MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar through December 2022, evaluating the effect of e-cigarettes on periodontal and peri-implant health relative to traditional cigarettes and nonsmokers. ROBIS risk-of-bias appraisal rated 4 of 5 included SRs as low risk of bias and 1 as moderate. Across the included reviews, e-cigarette users generally fell between nonsmokers and traditional smokers on inflammatory clinical parameters (probing depth, plaque index, peri-implant bone loss), but still showed worse outcomes than nonsmokers and were linked to broader oral-health harms (mucosal lesions, caries, gingivitis, oral cancer risk) attributable to nicotine and other e-liquid/aerosol constituents. The authors conclude e-cigarettes are a potential — though comparatively milder than traditional smoking — risk factor for peri-implant healing and treatment outcomes.

## Key Contributions
- First umbrella review specifically synthesizing SR/MA-level evidence on e-cigarettes and peri-implantitis, rather than general periodontal smoking literature.
- Applies structured ROBIS risk-of-bias appraisal across the 5 included systematic reviews.
- Documents a consistent nonsmoker < e-cigarette user < traditional smoker gradient across probing depth, plaque index, and peri-implant bone loss (though bleeding-on-probing direction was inconsistent between source SRs).

## Methodology
Umbrella review of 5 systematic reviews/meta-analyses (from 501 records → 119 full-text assessed → 15 eligible → 5 selected), searched to December 2022. ROBIS-based risk-of-bias appraisal by 2 independent reviewers. Outcomes: bleeding on probing, probing depth, plaque index, peri-implant/radiographic crestal bone loss, clinical attachment loss, proinflammatory cytokines, oral mucosal lesions, and broader systemic/oral effects.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Overall gradient (probing depth, plaque index, bone loss) | Nonsmoker < e-cigarette user < traditional smoker (better to worse) |
| Bleeding on probing | Inconsistent between source SRs — reduced in e-cigarette users in some SRs, worse than nonsmokers in others |
| Included SRs | 5 (98, 18, 93, 8, and 5 primary studies respectively) |
| Risk of bias (ROBIS) | 4/5 low, 1/5 moderate |
| Long-term chronic peri-implantitis risk from e-cigarette use | Insufficient evidence to quantify |

## Limitations
- Umbrella-of-umbrella design pools SR-level narrative conclusions rather than re-analyzing primary data; inherits substantial heterogeneity (device types, e-liquid composition, follow-up duration, outcome definitions).
- Search cutoff December 2022 predates larger, more recent quantitative SR+MAs (e.g., [[implants/calciolari-2026-tobacco-smoking-smoke-free-products]]) that directly assess implant survival/CBL and still find e-cigarette data insufficient to pool.
- Most underlying primary studies are periodontal- rather than peri-implant-specific; peri-implantitis conclusions are partly extrapolated/hypothetical in the source SRs.
- Self-reported vaping status and predominantly cross-sectional primary-study designs limit causal inference.
