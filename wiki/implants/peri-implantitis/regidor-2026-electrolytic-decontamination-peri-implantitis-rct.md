## Three-line Summary

RCT (Clin Oral Implants Res 2026): 40 patients, 44 implants with peri-implantitis intra-bony defects; electrolytic cleaning (ElectroMed) added to titanium brush + GBR vs titanium brush + GBR alone; 12-month follow-up.

No significant difference in primary composite outcome (40.0% test vs 27.3% control; p=0.41); PPD reduction, MBL gain, and patient-reported outcomes also not significantly different; post-surgical complications only in electrolytic group.

Adjunctive electrolytic cleaning provides no additional benefit over titanium brush alone in reconstructive peri-implantitis surgery.

## Summary

Regidor et al. (Arrow Development / Sahlgrenska Academy, Clin Oral Implants Res 2026) conducted a single-center RCT to isolate the adjunctive effect of electrolytic implant surface cleaning (ElectroMed device) during reconstructive peri-implantitis surgery. Forty patients with 44 implants harboring intra-bony peri-implantitis defects were treated with a standardized protocol: rotating titanium brush decontamination followed by xenogeneic bone substitute + resorbable collagen membrane (GBR) in both groups, with the test group additionally receiving electrolytic cleaning before GBR. At 12 months, the composite success rate (implant survival + BoP/SoP absent + PPD ≤5 mm + REC ≤1 mm) was 40.0% in the electrolytic group vs 27.3% in controls (p=0.41). No significant intergroup differences were found for any clinical, radiographic, or patient-reported outcome. Post-surgical complications occurred exclusively in the electrolytic cleaning group. This negative result indicates that the ElectroMed adjunct does not improve outcomes beyond the titanium brush alone and may introduce unnecessary complications.

## Key Contributions

- Negative RCT: electrolytic cleaning adds no benefit to Ti brush + GBR protocol
- Titanium brush alone + GBR achieves favorable clinical/radiographic outcomes at 12 months
- Post-surgical complications exclusive to electrolytic group — possible safety concern
- Numerically higher composite success in test group (40% vs 27%) did not reach significance — reflects underpowering for secondary outcomes
- Fills evidence gap on adjunctive electrolytic decontamination in the reconstructive (vs resective) peri-implantitis approach

## Methodology

Single-center parallel-arm RCT; 40 patients (44 implants); peri-implantitis intra-bony defects. Both groups: rotating Ti brush decontamination → xenogeneic bone substitute + resorbable collagen membrane. Test group additionally: ElectroMed electrolytic cleaning between brush and GBR. Primary outcome: composite at 12 months (implant survival + BoP/SoP absence + PPD ≤5mm + REC ≤1mm). Secondary: PPD, BoP/SoP, REC, keratinized mucosa, radiographic MBL, PROs. Assessed at 6 and 12 months (MBL and PROs at 12 months only).
