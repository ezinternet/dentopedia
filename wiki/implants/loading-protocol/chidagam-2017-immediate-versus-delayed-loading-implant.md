## Three-line Summary
Randomized prospective trial (n=20 patients, 20 implants) comparing immediate loading within 2 days vs delayed loading at 3 months for single-tooth mandibular first molar replacements, with 72-month follow-up.
Both groups showed 100% implant survival, no peri-implant radiolucency, and minimal marginal bone loss (0.1–0.15 mm at 6 months); probing depth decreased significantly in the immediate group (1.8→1.2 mm, p=0.0277) but not in the delayed group.
Immediate loading is equally effective to delayed loading for mandibular first molar single-tooth implants, offering reduced treatment time and early function with equivalent long-term clinical and radiographic outcomes.

## Summary
This randomized prospective clinical trial evaluated and compared the clinical outcomes of immediately loaded (within 2 days) versus delayed loaded (after 3 months) single-tooth implants replacing missing mandibular first molars. Twenty patients (14 male, 6 female, age 19–31 years) were randomly assigned to two groups of 10 implants each. Implants were placed using flapless (immediate group) or flapped (delayed group) techniques, with internal-hex implants (3.3–4.5 mm diameter, 10–13 mm length). Clinical and radiographic parameters — bleeding on probing, probing depth, implant mobility (Periotest), marginal bone level, and peri-implant radiolucency — were assessed at 1, 6, 12, 36, and 72 months post-loading by a blinded periodontist. At 72 months, both groups demonstrated 100% implant survival with no peri-implant radiolucency. Marginal bone loss was minimal in both groups (0.1–0.15 mm at 6 months). Probing depth significantly decreased in the immediate loading group from 1.8±0.63 mm at 1 month to 1.2±0.42 mm at 6 months (p=0.0277, Wilcoxon), while remaining stable at 1.8 mm in the delayed group. Periotest values were comparable (-1.8 vs -1.6, p=0.76). The authors conclude that immediate loading is a successful treatment modality for mandibular first molar single-tooth replacement, providing equivalent long-term outcomes with the advantages of reduced treatment time and early function.

## Key Contributions
- Six-year follow-up for immediate vs delayed loading in single-tooth mandibular first molar implants — longer than most loading-time trials (typically 1–4 years)
- Randomized design with blinded outcome assessment by a periodontist
- Comprehensive evaluation covering all major peri-implant health parameters (BoP, PD, mobility, MBL, radiolucency)
- Demonstrates probing depth improvement over time in immediately loaded single-tooth posterior implants

## Methodology
**Design**: Randomized prospective parallel-group clinical trial
**Setting**: Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Sibar Institute of Dental Sciences, Andhra Pradesh, India; Advanced Dental Care Centre, Vijayawada
**Period**: December 2009 – November 2016 (patient recruitment Dec 2009 over 1 year)
**Sample**: 20 patients (14 male, 6 female), age 19–31 years (mean 23.1), 20 implants
**Randomization**: Computer-generated random table numbers
**Interventions**: Group I — immediate loading within 48 hours (flapless, single-piece implant with abutment, acrylic provisional crown); Group II — delayed loading at 3 months (flapped, two-piece implant with cover screw, healing abutment at 3 months)
**Implants**: Internal hex, diameter 3.3–4.5 mm (Group I mean 4.34 mm, Group II mean 3.98 mm), length 10–13 mm (mean 11.65 mm)
**Outcomes**: Implant survival, bleeding on probing (BoP), probing depth (PD), implant mobility (Periotest, clinical mobility scale), marginal bone level (mesial/distal on standardized IOPA), peri-implant radiolucency
**Follow-up**: 1, 6, 12, 36, 72 months post-loading
**Statistics**: Wilcoxon matched pairs test (within-group), Mann-Whitney U test (between-group), kappa for inter-observer agreement; p<0.05 significant
**Ethics**: IEC/2009-8, Declaration of Helsinki informed consent

## Results
| Outcome | Immediate Loading (Group I, n=10) | Delayed Loading (Group II, n=10) | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Implant survival (72 mo)** | 100% (10/10) | 100% (10/10) | — |
| **Bleeding on probing (72 mo)** | 0% | 0% | p=1.00 (Mann-Whitney) |
| **Probing depth 1 mo (mm)** | 1.80±0.63 | 1.80±0.42 | p=1.00 (between groups) |
| **Probing depth 6 mo (mm)** | 1.20±0.42 | 1.80±0.42 | p=0.0277* (within Group I, Wilcoxon) |
| **Probing depth 12–72 mo** | 1.8 mm (stable) | 1.8 mm (stable) | p=1.00 (between groups) |
| **Periotest value 6 mo** | -1.80±1.23 | -1.60±1.08 | p=0.7624 (Mann-Whitney) |
| **Clinical mobility (all timepoints)** | 0% | 0% | — |
| **MBL mesial 1 mo (mm)** | 1.10±0.57 | 0.95±0.49 | p=0.7337 |
| **MBL mesial 6 mo (mm)** | 0.10±0.21 | 0.15±0.24 | p=0.7055 |
| **MBL distal 1 mo (mm)** | 1.15±0.41 | 0.825±0.33 | p=0.0821 |
| **MBL distal 6 mo (mm)** | 0.15±0.24 | 0.15±0.33 | p=0.7913 |
| **MBL 12–72 mo (mesial/distal)** | 0.10–0.15 mm | 0.15 mm | p=0.7055–1.000 |
| **Peri-implant radiolucency (72 mo)** | 0% | 0% | — |

*Wilcoxon matched pairs test for within-group change from 1 to 6 months.

Key observations:
- Probing depth in Group I decreased significantly from 1 month to 6 months (p=0.0277), suggesting favorable soft tissue adaptation with immediate loading
- Marginal bone loss was highest at 1 month in both groups (likely remodeling), then stabilized at minimal levels (≤0.15 mm) from 6 months onward
- No statistically significant between-group differences in MBL at any timepoint
- Periotest values in the negative range (-1.8, -1.6) indicate excellent osseointegration in both groups
