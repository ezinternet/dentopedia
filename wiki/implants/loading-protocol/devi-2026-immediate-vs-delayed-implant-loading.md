## Three-line Summary
Narrative review prioritizing post-2015 randomized clinical trials and high-quality systematic reviews/meta-analyses comparing immediate loading (IL) vs delayed loading (DL) across single-tooth, posterior partial edentulism, mandibular overdenture, and full-arch maxillary rehabilitation indications.
Across indications, IL shows comparable implant survival to DL when primary stability is high, occlusal control is appropriate, and patient risk is low; mandibular overdenture RCTs show similar survival with less radiographic bone loss for IL at 1–5 years; single-tooth esthetic zone shows comparable outcomes at 5 years; short implants in posterior maxilla show higher bone loss with IL; full-arch fixed prostheses show comparable survival with some protocols reporting lower bone loss for IL.
IL is predictable in selected cases but more technique- and case-sensitive; DL remains preferable when primary stability is uncertain, parafunction exists, bone quality is poor, systemic/local risk is elevated, or occlusal control cannot be ensured.

## Summary
This 2026 narrative review from Narayana Dental College synthesizes contemporary randomized controlled trial and systematic review evidence comparing immediate loading (IL) versus delayed loading (DL) protocols across four major clinical indications: single-tooth implants in the esthetic zone, posterior partial edentulism, mandibular overdentures, and maxillary full-arch fixed prostheses. The authors emphasize that modern implant macrogeometry, surface microtopography, guided surgery workflows, and patient demand have collectively enabled IL to evolve from an experimental approach to a clinically accepted option. Across indications, IL demonstrates comparable implant survival to DL when primary stability is high (≥30–35 Ncm), bone quality is favorable (D1–D2, selected D3), occlusal forces can be controlled, and no major systemic or local risk factors exist. The review provides a structured clinical decision framework (Table 2) mapping patient and site factors to protocol selection. Key nuances include the biomechanically critical distinction between immediate functional loading (IFL) and immediate nonfunctional loading (INFL), and the finding that short implants in the posterior maxilla may exhibit higher bone loss and bleeding indices with IL. The review acknowledges its pragmatic methodology — not a formal PRISMA systematic review — and highlights the persistent heterogeneity in loading definitions across the literature.

## Key Contributions
- Structured clinical decision-making framework (Table 2) translating evidence into actionable IL vs DL selection criteria
- Indication-specific evidence synthesis across four clinical scenarios with outcome tables (Tables 1, 3)
- Clear delineation of functional vs nonfunctional immediate loading and their biomechanical implications
- Updated loading timing classification aligned with current ITI/EAO consensus terminology (Table 1)
- Emphasis on primary stability thresholds (IT ≥30–35 Ncm, ISQ ≥60–70) as the pivotal gatekeeper for IL success

## Methodology
Structured narrative review employing a pragmatic, publication-focused methodology. The authors intentionally did not adopt a formal PRISMA-compliant systematic review due to heterogeneity in loading protocol definitions and implant systems. Search prioritized randomized clinical trials (parallel-group or split-mouth) reporting IL vs DL outcomes with minimum 12-month follow-up, plus high-quality systematic reviews/meta-analyses (Level I evidence) from 2015 onwards. Pre-specified outcome domains included indication-specific survival rates, marginal bone level (MBL) change, peri-implant soft tissue indices (BOP, PD, plaque), esthetic outcomes (PES/WES, papilla indices), prosthetic complications, and patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs). Clinical heterogeneity was assessed qualitatively across loading definitions, implant systems/surfaces, prosthetic workflows, operator experience, and patient selection criteria.

## Results

| Indication | Study Design / Follow-up | Key Outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| Single-tooth, maxillary esthetic zone (healed sites) | RCT, 5 years (Gjelvold et al.) | Survival: 100% (IL) vs 95.8% (DL); MBL: −0.50±0.73 vs −0.54±0.65 mm (NS); PES/WES & papilla indices comparable; non-smokers had less MBL |
| Single-tooth (general) | RCT (Gjelvold et al.) | Comparable clinical and radiographic outcomes |
| Posterior mandible | Split-mouth RCT, >10 years | IL showed no significant disadvantage vs DL in crestal bone loss |
| Mandibular Locator-retained overdenture | RCTs, 1–5 years | Similar survival; some trials show less radiographic bone level change for IL vs DL |
| Short implants (posterior maxilla) | Various RCTs | IL associated with higher bone loss and bleeding indices than DL, despite generally acceptable outcomes |
| Maxillary full-arch fixed prostheses | Long-term data | Comparable survival between IL and DL; some protocols report lower mean bone loss in IL groups |

Clinical decision framework (Table 2 summary):
- **IL recommended when**: Primary stability ≥30–35 Ncm; Bone quality D1–D2/selected D3; Occlusal forces controllable; Good patient compliance; No major systemic/local risk factors
- **DL preferable when**: Poor bone quality (D4); Need for bone augmentation; Parafunction (bruxism); Systemic compromise (uncontrolled diabetes, heavy smoking); Inadequate primary stability (<20 Ncm or ISQ <60)
