## Three-line Summary

Umbrella systematic review of 21 systematic reviews (2004–2019, search to Dec 2019) on loading-time protocols (immediate vs conventional) for multiple dental implants in totally/partially edentulous patients, assessed with AMSTAR (methodological quality) and ROBIS (risk of bias).

13 SRs reported similarity between loading techniques, 2 were inconclusive, 6 favored conventional loading; 8/21 SRs were high risk of bias, 12 low risk, 1 uncertain — restricting to only the 12 low-risk-of-bias SRs still shows no significant difference in loading time outcomes.

Nearly 40% of the underlying SR evidence base was high risk of bias (poor study-selection restrictions, inadequate meta-analytic synthesis, only 1/21 published an excluded-studies list), so clinicians should weight loading-time conclusions by the source SR's risk-of-bias tier, not just its existence.

## 세줄요약

다수 임플란트(multiple dental implants)의 완전/부분 무치악 환자 로딩 시기(즉시 vs 재래식)를 다룬 체계적 문헌고찰 21편(2004–2019년 출판, 2019년 12월까지 검색)에 대해 AMSTAR(방법론적 질)와 ROBIS(비뚤림 위험)를 적용한 우산 체계적 문헌고찰.

13편은 로딩 방식 간 유사성을 보고, 2편은 결론 미확정, 6편은 재래식 로딩이 더 낫다고 보고; 21편 중 8편이 high risk of bias, 12편 low risk, 1편 uncertain risk — low risk of bias 12편만으로 한정해도 로딩 시기 간 유의한 차이는 없었다.

기저 SR 근거의 거의 40%가 high risk of bias(부적절한 연구선정 제한, 부실한 메타분석적 종합, 21편 중 1편만 제외논문 목록 공개)였으므로, 임상의는 로딩 시기 결론을 SR의 존재 여부가 아니라 그 SR의 비뚤림 위험 등급으로 가중해서 받아들여야 한다.

## Summary

This umbrella (overview-of-reviews) systematic review evaluated the methodological quality (AMSTAR) and risk of bias (ROBIS) of 21 systematic reviews addressing loading-time protocols — immediate vs conventional (delayed) — for multiple dental implants in totally or partially edentulous patients. From 757 initially identified records (609 after dedup, 50 at title/abstract screening), 21 SRs published between 2004 and 2019 met eligibility. Across these 21 SRs, 13 concluded loading techniques performed similarly, 2 could not determine which was superior, and 6 favored conventional loading. Applying ROBIS, 8 SRs were classified high risk of bias, 12 low risk, and 1 uncertain risk. Critically, when the authors restricted their read to only the low-risk-of-bias subset, the "no significant difference between loading times" conclusion held even more strongly (10/12 low-risk SRs showed similarity), while the high-risk subset disproportionately contained SRs favoring conventional loading. The paper's central message is methodological, not clinical: naive vote-counting across SRs on implant loading time is unreliable unless stratified by each SR's own risk of bias, because roughly 40% of the evidence base carried high risk of bias driven by inadequate search comprehensiveness, missing meta-analytic synthesis, and near-universal failure to report excluded-study lists.

## Key Contributions

- First (per authors) umbrella review to appraise AMSTAR methodological quality and ROBIS risk of bias specifically for SRs on multiple-implant loading-time protocols, rather than simply pooling their clinical conclusions.
- Shows the vote-count across all 21 SRs (13 similar / 2 inconclusive / 6 favor-conventional) shifts meaningfully once stratified by risk-of-bias tier — the low-risk subset (n=12) is even more consistently "no difference" (10 similar, 2 favor-conventional, 1 [reported as] inconclusive within that subset per the paper's breakdown), while high-risk SRs (n=8) disproportionately favored conventional loading (3/8) or were mixed.
- Pinpoints concrete methodological failure modes: single-database/restricted-language searches, absent/inadequate meta-analysis and heterogeneity handling, and only 1/21 SRs reporting an excluded-studies list, only 3/21 reporting a conflict-of-interest statement.
- Justifies using original AMSTAR (not AMSTAR-2) because several included SRs incorporated retrospective/prospective observational studies, and AMSTAR-2 is designed only for SRs of RCTs — a useful tool-selection precedent for future dental umbrella reviews.

## Methodology

Umbrella systematic review (overview of systematic reviews), PRIO-guided reporting, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42018093633). MEDLINE (PubMed) and Scopus searched through December 31, 2019, plus OpenGrey for grey literature and manual reference checking (no language/date restriction applied to the search itself). Eligibility: about multiple dental implants, mentions loading timing, is a systematic review. Immediate loading = prosthesis placed immediately after implant placement; conventional loading = prosthesis placement delayed ~4 months (mandible) to ~6 months (maxilla). Two independent reviewers screened (kappa 0.8) and extracted data, with a third reviewer resolving discordances; when SR updates shared a dataset, only the most complete version was retained. AMSTAR (methodological quality) and ROBIS (risk of bias) were each applied independently by a separate pair of reviewers. Synthesis was qualitative/narrative only — no new quantitative meta-analysis was performed across the 21 included SRs, a deliberate choice given SR heterogeneity and the consistency of the low-risk-of-bias subset's findings.

## Results

| Metric | Finding |
|---|---|
| Screening | 757 records → 609 unique → 50 at title/abstract → 21 SRs eligible |
| SR publication years | 2004–2019; 6–120 primary studies per SR |
| Clinical conclusion across all 21 SRs | 13 similarity, 2 inconclusive, 6 favor conventional loading |
| ROBIS risk of bias | 8 high risk, 12 low risk, 1 uncertain risk |
| High-risk subset conclusions | 3 favor conventional, 4 similarity, 1 inconclusive |
| Low-risk subset conclusions | 10 similarity, 2 favor conventional, 1 inconclusive |
| AMSTAR quality gaps | ~half did not assess included-study quality; 12/21 no meta-analysis/publication-bias check; only 1/21 listed excluded studies; only 3/21 reported conflict of interest |
| Bottom line | Restricting to low-risk-of-bias SRs only, no significant difference in implant loading time outcomes |
