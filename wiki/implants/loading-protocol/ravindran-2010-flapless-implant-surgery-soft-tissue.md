## Three-line Summary

Prospective comparative clinical study (n=20, 10/group) in partially edentulous anterior maxilla using flapless technique; immediate loading (single-piece, provisional → metal-ceramic day 14) vs delayed loading (two-piece, permanent crowns at 4 months); soft-tissue parameters assessed at Day 0, 60, 120, 180.
IL success 80% vs DL 90% (NS); mPI, mBI, ML, WKM showed no intergroup difference; PPI at Day 60 significantly higher in IL (mean 2.00 vs 1.00, p<0.05), with DL catching up by Day 120–180.
Flapless surgery supports esthetics in both loading protocols, but design confound (single-piece IL vs two-piece DL), small n, and 6-month limit restrict conclusions.

## Summary

This 2010 comparative clinical study from Chennai, India evaluated soft-tissue outcomes after flapless implant surgery across two loading protocols in the anterior maxilla. Twenty patients with partial edentulism were allocated to either immediate loading (IL, single-piece implants, metal-ceramic crown by day 14) or delayed loading (DL, two-piece implants, permanent crowns at 4 months). Both groups underwent flapless surgery with the same operator. At 180 days, success rates (80% IL vs 90% DL) and all soft-tissue metrics except PPI trajectory were equivalent. The key finding — higher PPI at Day 60 in the IL group with DL catching up by 120–180 days — suggests IL provisionalization may support earlier papilla fill, but the two-piece vs single-piece implant difference is a confound that cannot be separated from the loading protocol effect.

## Key Contributions

- First comparative report on flapless surgery + IL vs DL specifically measuring PPI, ML, and WKM in the Indian academic literature (JISP 2010)
- PPI trajectory finding: IL achieves faster initial papilla recovery; DL shows greater gain from Day 60 to 180
- No significant difference in plaque/bleeding control (mPI, mBI) between protocols — infection-risk equivalence with flapless approach
- 80% vs 90% success gap (NS, small n) aligns with contemporary reports that IL carries modestly higher early-failure risk in flapless anterior cases

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective comparative clinical study; described as "randomly allocated" but no allocation concealment or blinding protocol described
- **n**: 20 (10/group), anterior maxilla, natural adjacent teeth required mesial and distal
- **Implant system**: LifeCare Devices (single-piece for IL, two-piece for DL), 2.8–5.0 mm diameter, 10–16 mm length
- **Presurgical**: Full-mouth scaling/root planing, OPG + IOPA, surgical stents, casts
- **Follow-up**: 180 days (6 months)
- **Statistical tests**: Mann-Whitney U (intergroup), Wilcoxon signed rank (intragroup)

## Results

| Parameter | IL Group (Day 180) | DL Group (Day 180) | Intergroup p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Success rate | 80% (2 failures) | 90% (1 failure) | NS |
| mPI | No difference | — | NS |
| mBI | No difference | — | NS |
| ML | No difference | — | NS |
| WKM | No difference | — | NS |
| PPI Day 60 | 2.00 (higher) | 1.00 | p<0.05 |
| PPI Δ Day 60→180 | Smaller gain | Larger gain | Significant |

Both groups showed significant intragroup improvement in WKM and PPI over time. IL failures attributed to bone quality limitations of flapless approach (no direct bone assessment) and probable occlusal overload.
