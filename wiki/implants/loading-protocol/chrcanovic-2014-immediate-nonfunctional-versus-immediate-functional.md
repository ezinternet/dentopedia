## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis of 11 clinical human studies (1,059 identified; search March 2014, no time/language restrictions) comparing immediate nonfunctional loading (INFL) versus immediate functional loading (IFL) on implant failure rates, postoperative infection, and marginal bone loss.

Procedure type did not significantly affect failure risk (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.44–1.75; P=0.70) or marginal bone loss (MD 0.01 mm, 95% CI −0.04–0.06; P=0.74); postoperative infection could not be pooled for lack of data.

With 7 of 11 studies at high risk of bias and a wide confidence interval, the pooled null result is imprecise — no proven benefit from withholding occlusal contact during immediate loading.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (electronic search March 2014, without time or language restrictions) pooled 11 clinical human studies — randomized or not — comparing immediate nonfunctional loading (INFL) with immediate functional loading (IFL) of dental implants. Across 1,059 identified studies, the loading procedure did not significantly affect implant failure rates (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.44–1.75; P=0.70), and marginal bone loss showed no apparent difference between groups (MD 0.01 mm, 95% CI −0.04–0.06; P=0.74); postoperative infection could not be analyzed because of missing data. Seven of the 11 included studies were at high risk of bias (four at low risk), and the authors explicitly flag the wide confidence interval as demonstrating uncertainty about the effect size. They conclude that differences in occlusal loading between INFL and IFL might not affect implant survival, and that periodic review of loading concepts is needed as treatment philosophies evolve (abstract-only source; full text not retrieved).

## Key Contributions
- Meta-analytic quantification of the functional vs nonfunctional immediate-loading contrast: failure RR 0.87 (95% CI 0.44–1.75, P=0.70) — no significant difference, with the wide CI acknowledged as real uncertainty rather than equivalence.
- Marginal bone loss is likewise unchanged between loading modes (MD 0.01 mm, 95% CI −0.04–0.06, P=0.74).
- Exposes an evidence gap: postoperative infection, a prespecified outcome, could not be pooled at all because included studies lacked the data.
- Quality appraisal of the loading-protocol trial literature itself: 7 of 11 included studies at high risk of bias.

## Methodology

Systematic review + meta-analysis with an electronic search run in March 2014, unrestricted by time or language (specific databases not named in the abstract). Clinical human studies, randomized or not, were eligible; 1,059 studies were identified and 11 included (7 high, 4 low risk of bias). Relative effect estimates were pooled as risk ratio (RR) for implant failure and postoperative infection, and as mean difference (MD, in millimeters) for marginal bone loss.
