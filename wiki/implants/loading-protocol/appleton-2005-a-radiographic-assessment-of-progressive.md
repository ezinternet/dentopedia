## Three-line Summary

Randomized clinical study, 23 HA-coated implants in 20 subjects (single premolar crowns, posterior maxilla), progressive loading (staged infraocclusion → light → full occlusion over 6 months with acrylic crowns) vs conventional metal-ceramic crown after a 5-month healing period.

At 12 months crestal bone height loss was 0.2 ± 0.27 mm (progressive) vs 0.59 ± 0.27 mm (conventional), a statistically significant difference (P ≤ 0.05, repeated-measures ANOVA); the progressive group trended toward more crestal density gain, the conventional group toward more apical density gain.

Progressive loading was associated with less crestal bone loss around single maxillary implants, but this is a small, single-site, abstract-level result with no survival data.

## Summary

Abstract-level record of the first human randomized comparison of progressive vs conventional loading for single maxillary premolar implants. Progressive loading used an acrylic crown that moved from infraocclusion to full occlusion over six months; at 12 months crestal bone loss was 0.2 mm vs 0.59 mm (P ≤ 0.05). Full text was not retrieved, so design details (randomization, blinding, per-arm n) are unverified.

## Key Contributions

- First human randomized comparison of a staged (progressive) loading protocol vs conventional full-function loading for single maxillary premolar implants, using standardized radiographs plus digital subtraction radiography.
- Defines a concrete protocol: infraocclusion for months 1–2, light occlusion months 3–4, full occlusion months 5–6, then definitive metal-ceramic crown.

## Methodology

- 23 HA-coated endosseous implants in 20 subjects; 5-month healing before uncovering.
- Random assignment to experimental (progressive) or control (conventional metal-ceramic crown).
- Progressive group: heat-processed acrylic crown, occlusal table height increased in increments by adding acrylic resin.
- Standardized radiographs at restoration, then 2, 4, 6, 9 and 12 months; digital image analysis and digital subtraction radiography for crestal bone height and peri-implant density.

## Results

| Outcome (12 months) | Progressive | Conventional |
|---|---|---|
| Crestal bone height loss (mean ± SD) | 0.2 ± 0.27 mm | 0.59 ± 0.27 mm |
| Test | repeated-measures ANOVA across time, P ≤ 0.05 | |
| Peri-implant density | continuous increase over time; trend to higher crestal gain | trend to higher gain at implant apex |

Density differences were described as trends, not significant effects (abstract).

## Limitations

- Abstract-only: randomization method, allocation concealment, blinding, dropouts and per-group n are not available. [미검증 beyond abstract]
- 23 implants in 20 subjects — some subjects may have contributed one implant to each arm or the groups may be unpaired; the abstract does not say.
- 12-month radiographic endpoints only; no implant survival, prosthetic or patient-reported outcomes.
- The Ghoveizi 2013 follow-up RCT states that this study placed progressive and conventional implants in different patients, so diet, occlusal force and habits were not equalized (per Ghoveizi's reading, not verified here).
