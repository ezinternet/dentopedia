## Three-line Summary

Pilot comparative study, 26 implants in 8 patients: 15 implants were progressively loaded before the final restoration and 11 were left unloaded under healing abutments until the final restoration; Periotest values measured at uncovering and at final restoration.

Mean Periotest change was −0.27 (unloaded) vs −1.51 (progressively loaded), a significantly larger gain in implant rigidity with progressive loading (P < 0.001, abstract).

The comparator was no loading, not conventional loading, and the endpoint is implant mobility, so the result shows that loading adds rigidity but not that progressive is better than conventional loading.

## Summary

Abstract-level record of a pilot study (8 patients, 26 implants) in which progressively loaded implants showed a larger Periotest rigidity gain than implants left unloaded until the final restoration (−1.51 vs −0.27, P < 0.001). The comparator was no loading rather than conventional loading, so the study shows that loading increases rigidity but cannot show superiority over conventional loading. Full text was not retrieved.

## Key Contributions

- First Periotest-based test of whether progressive loading changes implant rigidity.
- Shows loaded-before-final-restoration implants gain rigidity compared with implants not loaded until the final crown.

## Methodology

- 8 patients, 26 implants; 15 progressively loaded before final restorations; 11 healed with healing abutments only and not loaded until final restoration.
- Three Periotest readings per implant at uncovering and at the final restoration appointment; mean change in Periotest value compared between groups.
- Progressive protocol details and allocation method are not in the abstract.

## Results

| Group | Mean change in Periotest value |
|---|---|
| Not loaded until final restoration (11 implants) | −0.27 |
| Progressively loaded (15 implants) | −1.51 |

- More negative values mean stiffer, more stable implants; difference between groups P < 0.001 (abstract).

## Limitations

- Pilot, 8 patients; abstract gives no allocation method, per-patient clustering, or loading protocol details. [미검증 beyond abstract]
- Comparator was unloaded implants rather than conventionally loaded ones, so "progressive vs conventional" cannot be read from it (Turner 2014 makes the same point).
- Periotest measures mobility/damping, not bone density or bone-to-implant contact, and has no survival or marginal bone outcome.
