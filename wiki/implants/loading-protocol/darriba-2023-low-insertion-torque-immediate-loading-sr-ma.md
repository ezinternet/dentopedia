## Three-line Summary
SR+MA of 7 intervention trials (4 RCTs, 1 CCT, 2 prospective; 326 implants, 129 patients, mean follow-up 24 months) comparing low (≤35 Ncm) vs medium/high (>35 Ncm) insertion torque in immediately loaded implants.
Low IT implants had 96% survival vs 92% for higher IT (IRR 1.05, 95% CI 0.79–1.39, p=.175, I²=0.0%); no significant difference. Subgroup: splinted implants >20 Ncm had 5% higher survival than ≤20 Ncm (NS); single implants 20–35 Ncm had 8% less failure risk than >35 Ncm (NS).
Low IT (≤35 Ncm) is compatible with immediate loading; implants ≤20 Ncm can be loaded with splinted prostheses, >20 Ncm with single crowns — though evidence base remains sparse.

## Summary
This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesised data from 7 intervention trials (4 RCTs, 1 controlled clinical trial, 2 prospective studies) encompassing 326 immediately loaded implants placed in 129 patients, with a mean follow-up of 24 months. The primary aim was to determine whether low insertion torque (≤35 Ncm) compromised implant survival compared to medium or high insertion torque (>35 Ncm). Pooled survival rates were 96% for the low-IT group and 92% for the higher-IT group, with no statistically significant difference between groups (IRR 1.05, 95% CI 0.79–1.39, p=.175, I²=0.0%). Subgroup analyses were also non-significant: splinted implants with IT >20 Ncm had 5% higher survival than those with IT ≤20 Ncm (p=.956), and single implants with IT 20–35 Ncm had 8% less failure risk than single implants with IT >35 Ncm (p=.799). All RCTs were judged at low risk of bias; non-RCTs ranged from moderate to serious. No meta-analysis of secondary outcomes (marginal bone loss, complications) was possible due to insufficient reporting. The authors concluded that low insertion torque values have no significant effect on survival rates of immediately loaded implants at 24 months, supporting immediate loading of splinted prostheses with IT as low as 20 Ncm and single crowns with IT >20 Ncm.

## Key Contributions
- First SR+MA to quantify pooled survival rates stratified by IT threshold for immediately loaded implants, addressing a gap identified by Chen 2019
- Demonstrated that low IT (≤35 Ncm) does not significantly compromise survival versus higher IT, with absolute homogeneity (I²=0.0%) — both groups achieved ≥92% survival
- Subgroup evidence: single implants with IT 20–35 Ncm show a numerically lower (8%) failure risk than IT >35 Ncm, though underpowered (3 studies only)
- ≤20 Ncm implants loaded with splinted prostheses achieved equivalent outcomes to higher-torque implants, providing clinical guidance for marginal cases

## Methodology
- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis, PRISMA 2020-compliant, PROSPERO registration (CRD42020189499)
- **Databases**: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Central Register (through June 2022)
- **Included studies**: 7 intervention trials (4 RCTs, 1 CCT, 2 prospective case series); 326 implants, 129 participants
- **Primary outcome**: Implant survival rate
- **Statistical method**: Random-effects meta-analysis (Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel); I² heterogeneity; RoB 2.0 + ROBINS-I for bias assessment
- **PECO framework**: Population - immediately loaded implants; Exposure - low IT ≤35 Ncm; Comparison - IT >35 Ncm; Outcome - survival

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Pooled survival (≤35 Ncm) | 96% (SR 0.96, 95% CI 0.91–0.98, p<.001) |
| Pooled survival (>35 Ncm) | 92% (SR 0.92, 95% CI 0.86–0.96, p<.001) |
| IRR (≤35 vs >35 Ncm) | 1.05 (95% CI 0.79–1.39, p=.175, I²=0.0%) |
| Splinted >20 vs ≤20 Ncm | IRR 1.05 (95% CI 0.78–1.43, p=.956, I²=0.0%) |
| Single 20–35 vs >35 Ncm | IRR 0.92 (95% CI 0.48–1.75, p=.799, I²=0.0%) |
| Mean follow-up | 24 months (range 12–36) |
| Total implants analysed | 326 (108 ≤20 Ncm, 80 >20–≤35 Ncm, 138 >35 Ncm) |
