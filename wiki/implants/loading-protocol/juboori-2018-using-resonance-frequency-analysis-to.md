## Three-line Summary

Split-mouth pilot, 6 patients (34–62 years), 12 SLA tapered implants in the posterior maxilla: one side immediate progressive loading (PL; composite provisional crown out of occlusion at placement, then occlusal table widened at 1 month, axial contact at 2 months, provisional cemented at 4 months, PFM crown at 6 months) vs the other submerged delayed loading (DL, exposed at 3 months); ISQ measured at 0, 1, 2, 3 and 6 months.

Mean ISQ at placement was 63 (PL) vs 57 (DL) (reported as significantly different); at 3 months 76 vs 69; at 6 months 79 vs 76; ISQ rose 13 (PL) vs 12 (DL) over the first 3 months, and all 12 implants succeeded (100%).

The authors conclude PL enhances implant stability and allows earlier function, but allocation was driven by insertion torque (> 30 Ncm to PL), so PL implants started with higher ISQ, and n = 6 with no inferential comparison of the later time points is reported — the pilot cannot show an advantage over delayed loading.

## Summary

A six-patient split-mouth pilot compared immediate progressive loading (composite provisional crown out of occlusion at placement, staged to axial contact at 2 months and a PFM crown at 6 months) with submerged delayed loading in the posterior maxilla, tracking ISQ to 6 months. Progressive implants reached ISQ 79 vs 76 at 6 months, but allocation depended on insertion torque (> 30 Ncm to progressive loading), so the groups started at different stability (63 vs 57) and the pilot cannot show an advantage over delayed loading. The paper calls itself randomized, which the torque-based allocation contradicts.

## Key Contributions

- Describes a step-by-step immediate progressive loading sequence for single posterior maxillary crowns, with no excursive contact at any stage.
- Reports longitudinal ISQ for progressive vs delayed loading to 6 months, a follow-up the authors note most loading comparisons lack.
- Proposes surgical and prosthetic suggestions (Tables 3 and 4 of the paper) for obtaining primary stability in soft bone.

## Methodology

- Inclusion: bilateral posterior maxillary edentulism, adequate keratinized tissue and height, normal occlusion with anterior/canine guidance. Exclusion: narrow ridge (< 2 mm bone around implant), sockets healing < 4 months, bone graft or sinus lift, parafunction, debilitated patients.
- Implants: tapered, SLA, threaded to the top (Superline, Dentium), placed subcrestally about 1 mm, manual insertion torque 20–35 Ncm. Implants reaching > 30 Ncm were assigned to PL; those below 30 Ncm were left submerged as DL.
- PL sequence: immediate temporary plastic abutment with light-cured composite crown, out of occlusion, narrow occlusal table, no adjacent contact; 1 month — occlusal table widened with mesial contact, still no occlusion; 2 months — height increased for axial contact only (centric, no excursions); 3 months — RFA, healing abutments for 1 month; 4 months — impression, provisional acrylic crowns cemented at 30 Ncm abutments; 6 months — RFA, PFM permanent crowns.
- DL: cover screw, primary closure, exposure at 3 months.
- RFA at placement and at 1, 2, 3 and 6 months (DL not measured at 1–2 months as it was submerged).

## Results

| Time after placement | ISQ, PL | ISQ, DL |
|---|---|---|
| Placement | 63 | 57 |
| 1 month | 73 | not measured |
| 2 months | 75 | not measured |
| 3 months | 76 | 69 |
| 6 months | 79 | 76 |

- ISQ gain over first 3 months: PL +13, DL +12; between 3 and 6 months PL +3, DL +8 (derived from the table values).
- All implants showed no rotation at cover-screw removal/tightening; no pain, radiolucency or infection; cumulative success 100%.
- Baseline ISQ difference reported as significant; no between-group P values for later time points appear in the text read.

## Limitations

- n = 6 patients, 12 implants, 6-month follow-up; "pilot" by the authors.
- Allocation by insertion torque (> 30 Ncm → PL) rather than by random assignment contradicts the randomized description and builds in a higher starting ISQ for PL; DL implants included ISQ values described as low as 20.
- PL and DL differ in timing, exposure (submerged vs transmucosal), provisional material and crown stage, so loading regimen is confounded.
- Crown materials in PL changed across stages (composite, acrylic, PFM), which the authors note can influence force transmission.
- RFA requires abutment disconnection, which can disturb the soft-tissue seal.
- No bone density or crestal bone measurement despite the conclusion that PL "enhances bone density"; ISQ only.
