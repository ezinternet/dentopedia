## Three-line Summary

Cochrane SR+MA (5 RCTs, n=239 patients, 761 implants) comparing 1-stage (non-submerged) vs 2-stage (submerged) implant placement with ≥6-month follow-up after loading.

No statistically significant differences in prosthesis failure (RR 1.87, 95% CI 0.33–10.40), implant failure (RR 1.39, 95% CI 0.59–3.27), marginal bone loss, complications, or soft-tissue recession between the two surgical protocols; evidence quality moderate (GRADE).

1-stage approach may be preferable in partially edentulous patients (avoids second surgery, shortens treatment); 2-stage submerged approach still indicated when primary stability is suboptimal, barriers used for GBR, or removable prostheses risk excessive forces on healing abutments in fully edentulous jaws.

## Summary

This 2009 Cochrane systematic review (pub2) addresses a fundamental surgical protocol question: whether dental implants should be placed using a 1-stage (non-submerged, transmucosal healing) or 2-stage (submerged, completely buried) approach. The review included 5 RCTs (239 patients, 761 implants) with follow-up ranging from 6 months to 1 year after loading. The meta-analyses found no statistically significant differences between the two protocols for any primary outcome — prosthesis failure, implant failure, marginal bone level change, complications, or aesthetic outcomes. However, the evidence was rated moderate quality (GRADE) due to high risk of bias in 4 of 5 trials and imprecision from small sample sizes. A clinically important trend emerged in fully edentulous mandibles (Becktor 2007), where 17 implants failed in 6 patients with 1-stage vs 5 implants in 4 patients with 2-stage, and complications (soft tissue reactions, pain) were substantially more frequent with 1-stage. The authors conclude that 1-stage placement may be preferred in partially edentulous patients (avoiding second surgery, shortening treatment time), while 2-stage remains indicated when primary stability is suboptimal, GBR barrier membranes are used, or removable temporary prostheses could transmit excessive forces to healing abutments — especially in fully edentulous patients.

## Key Contributions

- First Cochrane systematic review specifically comparing 1-stage vs 2-stage surgical protocols for implant placement
- Standardized definitions: 1-stage = implant penetrates oral mucosa during healing; 2-stage = implant completely buried under mucosa requiring second surgery for abutment connection
- Meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (239 patients, 761 implants) across diverse clinical settings (partial/full edentulism, various implant systems)
- Moderate-quality GRADE evidence showing no statistically significant difference in any primary outcome
- Clinically important trend: more implant failures with 1-stage in fully edentulous mandibles (Becktor 2007: 17 vs 5 failed implants)
- Identified clinical decision framework: primary stability, GBR barriers, prosthetic loading risk as key factors for protocol selection

## Methodology

- **Design**: Cochrane systematic review with meta-analysis (pub2 — 2nd version of the review)
- **Search**: Cochrane Oral Health Group's Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE (1966–21 Jan 2009), EMBASE (1980–12 Jan 2009); hand-searching 18 implant journals; contact with 55 manufacturers; no language restrictions
- **Inclusion**: RCTs comparing same implant system placed via 1-stage vs 2-stage; minimum 6-month follow-up after loading; parallel-group and split-mouth designs
- **Outcomes**: 
  - Prosthesis failure (if secondary to implant failure)
  - Implant failure (mobility + removal dictated by progressive marginal bone loss or infection)
  - Radiographic marginal bone level change (parallel technique from surgery to 1 year)
  - Patient preference including aesthetics (split-mouth only)
  - Aesthetics evaluated by dentists
  - Biological/mechanical complications up to prosthesis delivery
- **Analysis**: Random-effects models; risk ratios for dichotomous outcomes, mean differences for continuous; patient as statistical unit (split-mouth data combined using Elbourne 2002 method); Cochrane's test for heterogeneity, I² statistic; GRADE quality assessment
- **Bias assessment**: Allocation concealment, blinding of outcome assessors, completeness of follow-up; 4/5 trials high risk of bias, 1 low risk (Cordaro 2009)

## Results

| Comparison | Outcome | Effect | Significance | Trials (Patients) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Prosthesis failure | RR 1.87 (0.33–10.40) | NS | 3 (153) |
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Implant failure | RR 1.39 (0.59–3.27) | NS | 4 (210) |
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Marginal bone level change | No significant difference | NS | 3 |
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Complications | More frequent in 1-stage (Becktor 2007) | Descriptive | 3 |
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Soft tissue recession | No difference (Cordaro 2009) | NS | 1 |
| 1-stage vs 2-stage | Keratinized tissue height | 1.1 mm less with 2-stage at 1 yr (Cordaro 2009) | P<0.05 | 1 |

**Detailed findings by trial:**

- **Barber 1996** (split-mouth, 5 patients, IMZ implants, partial edentulism, 6 mo): No failures, no complications in either group
- **Heijdenrijk 2002** (parallel, 40 patients, IMZ implants, edentulous mandible overdentures, 1 yr): 1 implant + prosthesis failed in each group; no significant difference
- **Ceccinato 2004** (parallel, 84 patients, Astra implants, posterior partial edentulism, 5 yr): 3 implants failed in 3 pts (1-stage) vs 4 in 3 pts (2-stage); no significant difference
- **Becktor 2007** (parallel, 80 patients, Brånemark turned implants, edentulous mandible fixed prostheses, >1 yr): **17 implants failed in 6 pts (1-stage) vs 5 in 4 pts (2-stage)**; complications markedly more frequent in 1-stage (soft tissue reactions 13 vs 2, pain 18 in 6 pts vs 0, other 13 vs 3); no prosthesis lost
- **Cordaro 2009** (parallel, 30 patients, Straumann SLA, post-extractive single implants, 1 yr): 1 implant failed (1-stage); 3 minor cover-screw exposures in 2-stage group; no significant difference in bone loss, recession, or keratinized tissue width (though 1.1 mm less keratinized tissue with 2-stage, P<0.05)

**GRADE evidence quality**: Moderate for prosthesis failure and implant failure (downgraded for high risk of bias in most trials, imprecision from wide CIs and small sample sizes)
