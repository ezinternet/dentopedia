## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42024628116, 45 articles/44 studies, ≥1 year post-loading follow-up, 1–17 years) — the largest, most recent quantification of implant survival, crestal bone loss (CBL), and peri-implantitis incidence in tobacco smokers vs non-smokers, with a first attempt to scope smoke-free tobacco and e-cigarette users separately.

Cigarette smoking significantly lowers implant survival at implant level (OR 0.40, 95% CI 0.27-0.61) and patient level (OR 0.43, 95% CI 0.20-0.90), and increases CBL by 0.64 mm (95% CI 0.29-0.99); most studies also report higher peri-implantitis incidence in smokers. Evidence for smoke-free tobacco and vaping remains insufficient to pool.

This is now the primary quantitative anchor for smoking-related implant informed consent, superseding the need to cite single-outcome estimates alone — smoking is not an absolute contraindication but is a well-quantified, modifiable risk factor requiring cessation counselling.

## Summary
Largest and most recent (2026) SR+MA on smoking and dental implants, registered on PROSPERO and updated with a March 2025 search. Unlike prior single-outcome SRs, it pools three outcomes — implant survival (implant- and patient-level), CBL, and peri-implantitis incidence — from 44 studies (41 addressing cigarette smoking specifically). It also formally scopes smoke-free tobacco products and e-cigarettes as separate exposures, finding the evidence base still too sparse for meta-analysis.

## Key Contributions
- Broadest outcome coverage to date (survival + CBL + peri-implantitis) in one SR+MA, rather than one outcome per paper.
- PROSPERO-registered, Cochrane Handbook-aligned protocol, with a second search wave (March 2025) to catch recent literature.
- First systematic mapping of the smoke-free/e-cigarette implant evidence gap, useful for identifying where future primary research is needed.

## Methodology
MEDLINE/OVID, EMBASE, Cochrane CENTRAL searched to May 2024 (updated March 2025). Studies with ≥1 year post-loading follow-up comparing implant outcomes in tobacco smokers or smoke-free product users vs non-smokers. Random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird) meta-analysis of survival OR, α = 0.01; CBL and biological complications extracted narratively/quantitatively where possible.

## Results
[근거강함, GRADE low] Implant-level survival OR 0.40 (95% CI 0.27-0.61, p<0.001) in smokers vs non-smokers; patient-level OR 0.43 (95% CI 0.20-0.90, p=0.02); CBL +0.64 mm (95% CI 0.29-0.99, p<0.001). Majority of studies report higher peri-implantitis incidence in smokers. Smoke-free tobacco/e-cigarette data insufficient for pooling — evidence gap, not a null result.

## Limitations
- All observational, GRADE-low overall quality.
- High population/protocol/implant-surface heterogeneity; "smoker" definitions range from ≥1 cigarette/day to severe-smokers-only cohorts.
- Smoking status self-reported throughout — literature suggests ~1 in 10 smokers under-report status to clinicians.
- Comparator groups inconsistently defined; former smokers sometimes pooled with never-smokers, diluting effect estimates given known dose/duration-dependent recovery after cessation.
- Predominantly implant-based (not patient-based) denominators — risk of non-independence within patients.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
For Korean implantology informed consent, this is now the best single citation covering three linked outcomes at once: smokers face roughly 60% lower implant-level survival odds (OR 0.40), 0.64mm greater expected crestal bone loss, and elevated peri-implantitis risk. Structured cessation counselling (5 As) should be documented as part of the consent process, consistent with [[implants/caggiano-2022-smoking-cessation-periodontal-peri-implant-sr]]. For patients using e-cigarettes/smoke-free tobacco instead of cigarettes, be transparent that quantitative implant-risk data does not yet exist — do not imply vaping is implant-safe.
