## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 24 systematic reviews/meta-analyses (~8,063 patients, 18,373 implants) comparing immediate loading (IL) vs nonimmediate/delayed loading (NIL) of single-tooth implants, follow-up ≥6 months, search through August 2024.

Both protocols showed high long-term survival (IL 92–97.8% vs NIL 95–99%, not statistically different); IL showed slightly greater marginal bone loss (0.2–1 mm vs 0.1–0.8 mm, NS), especially in year 1; peri-implant soft-tissue health and prosthetic-complication rates were comparable.

IL is a viable choice when high primary stability is achieved, but NIL remains preferred for compromised bone quality or high-risk patients; synthesis is qualitative (narrative), not a new pooled quantitative estimate, limiting precision.

## Summary

This umbrella review pools 24 prior systematic reviews and meta-analyses (cumulative ~8,063 patients, 18,373 implants) that directly compared immediate loading (IL, prosthesis within 48 h) versus nonimmediate/delayed loading (NIL, 3–6 month osseointegration period) of single-tooth implants, restricted to studies with ≥6-month follow-up. Across the included evidence, both protocols achieved comparable long-term survival (IL 92–97.8%, NIL 95–99%; not statistically significant in the large majority of pooled comparisons), while IL showed a slightly higher — but not statistically significant — marginal bone loss, particularly during the first year. Peri-implant soft-tissue health, patient satisfaction, and prosthetic-complication rates were similar between groups. The authors conclude IL can be confidently offered when high initial implant stability is achievable and case selection is careful, while NIL remains the safer default for patients with compromised bone quality or high-risk conditions. A key methodological caveat: this is a qualitative (narrative) synthesis across heterogeneous SRs rather than a new quantitative meta-analytic pooling, so the precision of the "no significant difference" conclusion is bounded by the consistency of the underlying reviews rather than a formal combined effect size.

## Key Contributions

- Restricts the umbrella-review lens specifically to single-implant (not multi-implant/full-arch) IL vs NIL comparisons — narrower and more clinically actionable than prior broader loading-protocol umbrella reviews.
- Aggregates survival, MBL, soft-tissue, and prosthetic-complication data from 24 SRs/MAs spanning roughly two decades of literature, giving a "field-level" read of where consensus lies.
- Flags outlier findings (e.g., one MA reporting RR 4.76 higher IL failure risk even with nonocclusal provisionals) alongside the dominant non-significant-difference pattern, useful for identifying which subgroup/context claims deserve closer scrutiny.
- Reinforces that the classic IL prerequisite — adequate primary stability — remains the operative variable determining whether IL performs on par with NIL.

## Methodology

Umbrella review (PRISMA-guided) of SRs/MAs from PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, Science Direct, ProQuest, Cochrane Library, Magiran, MOH Articles, plus grey literature, searched through August 2024. Inclusion: SRs/MAs directly comparing IL vs NIL in single-tooth implants, English/Farsi, ≥6-month follow-up. Exclusion: multi-implant/full-arch studies, case reports, observational studies without SR/MA design. Three independent reviewers extracted data and scored included SRs on randomization, recency, follow-up length, geographic diversity, and meta-analytic rigor (risk-of-bias tiers: 1–3 criteria = high risk, 4–5 = moderate, 6–7 = low); disagreements resolved by a fourth reviewer. Analysis was qualitative/narrative — no new pooled effect size was computed across the 24 SRs. (Note: the paper's own PRISMA flow diagram shows 48 included studies while the Results narrative frames "24 systematic reviews" as the analytic unit; this internal inconsistency is not resolved in the text.)

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Implant survival | IL 92–97.8% vs NIL 95–99%; not statistically different in most pooled comparisons (e.g., 1-yr OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.32–1.76; RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.97–1.03, P = 1.00) |
| Outlier survival findings | One MA: IL failure RR 4.76 (95% CI 1.74–13.02, P = 0.002) even with nonocclusal provisionals; another: IL survival RR 0.974 (95% CI 0.954–0.994, P = 0.012), both lower than the dominant "no difference" pattern |
| Marginal bone loss | IL 0.2–1 mm vs NIL 0.1–0.8 mm, most pronounced in year 1; not statistically significant (P > 0.05) |
| Prosthetic complications | Similar rates (crown loosening, fracture), not statistically significant (P > 0.05) |
| Soft tissue / satisfaction | Reported as similar between IL and NIL across included SRs |
| Illustrative primary RCT (Gjelvold et al., cited) | IL 100% vs NIL 95.8% survival at 5 years, no significant MBL/esthetic difference |
