## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis of 6 RCTs (from 8 identified; MEDLINE/PubMed and Cochrane) comparing submerged vs nonsubmerged dental implant placement protocols, with risk of bias graded by the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool.

No difference in implant or prosthesis failure between protocols, but submerged implants showed significantly more MBL (MD 0.12 mm; 95% CI 0.00–0.24; I² = 0%, P = .04) — judged statistically significant yet *not clinically relevant*.

Authors themselves state the review is underpowered and 4 of 6 RCTs were at high risk of bias (1 unclear, 1 low), so the pooled MBL difference should not by itself drive protocol selection.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (Moustafa Ali, Alqutaibi, El-Din Gomaa, Abdallah — *Int J Prosthodont* 2018;31(1):15-22) pooled randomized controlled trials comparing submerged two-stage implant placement with nonsubmerged placement, searching MEDLINE (PubMed) and Cochrane electronically and manually, with two-reviewer independent extraction and Cochrane Risk of Bias assessment. Eight RCTs were identified and six included; four were judged at high risk of bias, one unclear, one low. For marginal bone loss the meta-analysis found statistically significantly more bone loss around submerged implants (MD 0.12 mm; 95% CI 0.00–0.24; I² = 0%, P = .04), whereas implant and prosthesis failure did not differ between protocols. The authors explicitly judge the MBL difference **not clinically relevant** and warn that the review is underpowered with mostly high-risk-of-bias trials. Clinically, this supports treating submerged vs nonsubmerged healing as a protocol decision driven by soft-tissue/esthetic and patient factors rather than by an expected survival or bone-preservation advantage.

**Conflict note (not resolved by this page):** the MBL direction reported here (submerged = more bone loss) runs opposite to the later meta-analysis commonly cited on this question (Troiano 2018), which is reported to favour nonsubmerged for bone preservation while accepting ~2% higher early failure — and [[implants/survival/giok-2026-factors-implant-failure-umbrella-review]] grades "submerged vs nonsubmerged healing" as a high-certainty failure-risk factor without stating direction or source. The Troiano and Wu 2018 papers on this exact axis are being ingested in parallel in the same batch; until their pages exist, the discrepancy is recorded here verbatim rather than resolved.

## Key Contributions

- First pooled RCT-only comparison of submerged vs nonsubmerged placement with **failure and MBL as separate outcomes** (implant failure, prosthesis failure, MBL) — a procedural (surgical-protocol) factor, complementing the design-factor MBL literature
- Reports the only statistically significant pooled result as **more MBL with submerged placement** (MD 0.12 mm; 95% CI 0.00–0.24; I² = 0%, P = .04), with zero statistical heterogeneity
- Separates **statistical significance from clinical relevance**, concluding the 0.12 mm difference is not clinically relevant — one of the few implant SRs to state that distinction explicitly
- Failure outcomes null in both directions, and the authors name the cause: **underpowering plus high risk of bias** (4/6 high, 1 unclear, 1 low)
- Transparent about its own weakness, making it usable as a *negative/null* anchor for a debated protocol axis rather than a positive recommendation

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review + meta-analysis, RCT-only inclusion
- **Databases:** MEDLINE (PubMed) and Cochrane — electronic plus manual searching
- **Screening/extraction:** two reviewers independently extracted data
- **Included:** 8 RCTs identified, 6 included
- **Risk of bias:** Cochrane Risk of Bias tool — 4 high, 1 unclear, 1 low
- **Outcomes:** implant failure, prosthesis failure, marginal bone loss
- **Statistics:** meta-analysis of included RCTs; MBL analysis reported I² = 0%, MD 0.12 mm, 95% CI 0.00–0.24, P = .04. Random-effects vs fixed-effects model, included-study characteristics (era, platform, surface, loading, follow-up), and MBL measurement method/timepoint are **not stated in the abstract** and could not be captured.

Risk of bias across the 6 included RCTs: high 4 · unclear 1 · low 1.

**Evidence caveats (all from the authors, plus one inference):**
- Underpowered — the failure analyses are vulnerable to false negatives, not evidence of equivalence.
- 4 of 6 trials at high risk of bias; the single significant result (MBL) therefore rests mostly on weak evidence.
- The 95% CI lower bound of 0.00 mm means the pooled MBL effect is borderline against no difference.
- *(inferred)* Without the full text it is not possible to tell whether the pooled MBL signal reflects a real protocol effect or residual confounding by platform/surface/loading/measurement method.
