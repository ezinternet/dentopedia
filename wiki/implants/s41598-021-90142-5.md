## Three-line Summary
Systematic review of 25 in vivo studies (1 human, 24 animal) examining the relationship between micromotion magnitude and osseointegration outcome, registered on PROSPERO (CRD42020196686).
Mean micromotion for osseointegrated implants was 112±176 µm vs 349±231 µm for non-osseointegrated (p<0.001), but ranges overlapped extensively (15–750 µm vs 30–750 µm) with no universal threshold identifiable.
Clinical implication: the 150 µm limit is misleading; implant factors (HA coating, thread geometry, pore shape) and environmental factors (loading frequency, rest periods, study duration ≥9 weeks) jointly determine tolerable micromotion.

## Summary
This PRISMA-compliant systematic review compiled all quantitative in vivo evidence (25 studies: 1 human post-mortem, 24 animal) relating micromotion at the bone-implant interface to osseointegration outcomes. The search covered PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science through November 2020. While osseointegrated implants exhibited significantly lower mean micromotion (112±176 µm) than non-osseointegrated implants (349±231 µm, p<0.001), the data ranges overlapped broadly (15–750 µm for success vs 30–750 µm for failure), refuting the existence of a universal "safe limit" such as the often-cited 150 µm threshold. The review identified multiple modifying factors: hydroxyapatite coating, larger threads in low-density bone, and square pore cross-section (implant factors); infrequent loading, a rest period after initial loading, and longer observation duration (≥9 weeks) (environmental factors) all permitted successful osseointegration at higher micromotion levels. A positive correlation emerged between observation time and %BIC (ρ=0.40, p=0.01), and between micromotion and %BIC for osseointegrated implants (ρ=0.41, p=0.02). The authors conclude that interfacial stress–strain state—not micromotion alone—drives tissue differentiation, and provide summary tables to guide investigators in selecting context-appropriate micromotion benchmarks for new biomaterials and implant designs.

## Key Contributions
- First comprehensive synthesis of all quantitative in vivo micromotion-osseointegration data, demonstrating that the 150 µm "gold standard" is not universally applicable
- Quantified the extensive overlap in micromotion ranges between successful and failed osseointegration (15–750 µm vs 30–750 µm)
- Mapped implant-specific and environment-specific modifiers that shift the tolerable micromotion threshold
- Provided tabulated benchmark data (Tables 1, 2) linking experimental conditions to observed micromotion values for translational reference

## Methodology
- **Design**: Systematic review (PRISMA 2020), PROSPERO registration CRD42020196686
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science (search to 16 Nov 2020)
- **Search terms**: (micromotion OR "micro-motion" OR "micro motion") AND ("osseointegration" OR "osteointegration")
- **Eligibility**: In vivo animal or pre-mortem human implant studies; micromotion applied or measured with displacement values reported; original research; English language
- **Exclusion**: ISQ/RFA only, duration <3 weeks, FEA/computational, cemented implants, post-mortem insertion, synthetic bone
- **n**: 284 records → 72 full-text → 25 included (1 human, 24 animal; 51 study groups)
- **Analysis**: Mann-Whitney tests (OI vs Non-OI), subgrouped by applied vs measured micromotion; Spearman correlations for BIC vs micromotion and BIC vs observation time; α=0.05

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Micromotion (OI, n=28 samples) | 112±176 µm (range 15–750 µm) |
| Micromotion (Non-OI, n=23 samples) | 349±231 µm (range 30–750 µm) |
| Mann-Whitney OI vs Non-OI | p<0.001 |
| Applied micromotion: OI vs Non-OI | p=0.001 |
| Measured micromotion: OI vs Non-OI | p=0.01 |
| Spearman ρ (BIC vs micromotion, OI group) | 0.41 (p=0.02) |
| Spearman ρ (BIC vs micromotion, Non-OI group) | NS (p=0.39) |
| Spearman ρ (BIC vs observation time) | 0.40 (p=0.01) |
| Human post-mortem (Engh 1992) | <40 µm (OI) vs 150 µm (failed) |

**Modifying factors permitting higher micromotion with successful osseointegration:**
- Implant: HA coating (Overgaard 1996, Søballe 1992), larger threads in low-density bone (Trisi 2015), square pore cross-section (Goodman 1993)
- Environment: Infrequent loading (Goodman 1993), rest period after initial loading (Overgaard 1996, Goodman 1994), observation time ≥9 weeks (Vandamme 2007, 2008)
