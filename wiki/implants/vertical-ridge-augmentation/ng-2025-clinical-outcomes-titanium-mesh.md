## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (8 systematic reviews, incl. 2 with meta-analysis; 51 index studies, 20 RCTs) appraising titanium mesh (Ti mesh) for alveolar ridge augmentation using AMSTAR 2 and GRADE.

Weighted mean vertical bone gain 4.05 mm and horizontal bone gain 3.96 mm across reviews; weighted mean mesh exposure rate 24.7% (range 16%–35%); GRADE certainty moderate for bone gain, low–moderate for mesh exposure, low for implant survival/success; the single high-quality meta-analysis (Sabri 2024) reported lower bone gain than the cross-review weighted mean.

Most included SRs were AMSTAR 2 "critically low" or "low" quality and shared overlapping primary studies, which limits the precision of pooled estimates; TM remains clinically effective for 3D bone reconstruction, but the ~1-in-4 exposure risk demands advanced surgical skill, and patient-reported outcomes remain an evidence gap.

## Summary

This umbrella review synthesizes evidence from eight systematic reviews (two with meta-analysis, including [[implants/vertical-ridge-augmentation/sabri-2024-titanium-mesh-bone-augmentation-sr-ma]] and [[implants/vertical-ridge-augmentation/abu-mostafa-2022-titanium-mesh-vertical-augmentation-sr]]) covering 51 unique primary studies (20 RCTs) on titanium mesh (TM) for alveolar ridge augmentation. Using AMSTAR 2 to appraise review quality and GRADE to rate certainty of evidence — a first for this topic — the authors report a weighted mean vertical bone gain of 4.05 mm and horizontal bone gain of 3.96 mm across reviews, comparable to pooled estimates for other GBR barrier types (Urban 2019: 4.2 mm vertical; Naenni 2019: 3.5 mm horizontal). Mesh exposure occurred in a weighted mean of 24.7% of cases (range 16%–35%), yet implant placement remained feasible in most exposed sites, and implant survival (90%–100%) and success (85%–100%) rates were high across follow-up periods of 6–96 months. Critically, most of the eight constituent SRs scored AMSTAR 2 "critically low" or "low," and a citation-matrix/overlap analysis showed substantial sharing of primary studies between reviews, which limits the precision of the pooled weighted means. Digitally customized (CAD/CAM) TM did not clearly reduce exposure risk relative to conventional hand-shaped TM. No review reported patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs), peri-implant disease incidence, or aesthetic parameters — flagged as the key evidence gap for future primary research.

## Key Contributions

- First quality-appraised (AMSTAR 2) and certainty-graded (GRADE) synthesis across the entire TM-for-GBR systematic-review literature, exposing that most existing SRs are methodologically weak despite sitting at the top of the traditional evidence hierarchy.
- Quantifies a citation-matrix overlap between the 8 SRs (51 unique index studies), making explicit how much double-counting of primary data underlies commonly cited pooled TM bone-gain figures.
- Shows customized/CAD-CAM TM does not clearly outperform conventional TM on exposure risk — digital workflows may reduce surgical time and improve fit, but do not appear to mitigate the dominant complication.
- Benchmarks TM against other GBR barrier systems: TM's ~16%–35% exposure range is higher than reported complication rates for horizontal (16%–23%) and vertical (11%–17%) augmentation using other techniques, reinforcing that TM is comparatively technique-sensitive.

## Methodology

Umbrella review (overview of reviews) following the Cochrane structure, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42024604349). Five databases (PubMed/Medline, Embase, Cochrane Library, Scopus, Web of Science) searched Oct 2024–Jun 2025 for systematic reviews reporting absolute vertical and/or horizontal bone gain with TM in ASA I–II patients intended for implant placement. Eight SRs (2 with meta-analysis) met inclusion criteria, covering 51 index studies (20 RCTs). Review quality assessed with AMSTAR 2; evidence certainty rated with (adapted) GRADE; primary-study overlap between reviews quantified via a node-link/citation-matrix graph.
