## Three-line Summary

Randomized, double-blind, split-mouth clinical trial (6 patients, 12 sites) comparing a leukocyte-platelet-rich fibrin (L-PRF) bone block graft against a composite graft mixture of 50% autogenous bone (AB) with 50% deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM) for vertical guided bone regeneration (GBR), with a dense polytetrafluoroethylene (d-PTFE) membrane at both test and control sites before implant placement.

Vertical bone height (VBH) gain did not differ significantly between arms at any timepoint — 4.6 ± 3.0 mm (test) vs 5.2 ± 2.7 mm (control) at implant placement and 3.0 ± 2.8 mm vs 3.8 ± 2.6 mm one year after implant loading (P = .96) — and complications were minimal (one implant loss due to infection in a test site).

The authors read the L-PRF bone block as a potentially viable alternative to the composite graft that could reduce the need for harvesting bone from a second surgical site, but with 6 patients and no significant difference at any timepoint this is feasibility evidence, not demonstrated equivalence; larger trials are required.

## 세줄요약

무작위·이중눈가림(split-mouth) 임상시험(Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) (환자 6명, 부위 12개): 임플란트 식립 전 수직 유도골재생(Vertical Guided Bone Regeneration, GBR)에서 백혈구-혈소판풍부피브린(Leukocyte-Platelet-Rich Fibrin, L-PRF) 골블록 이식재를 대조군인 자가골(Autogenous Bone, AB) 50% + 탈단백소골(Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral, DBBM) 50% 복합이식재와 비교했고, 양군 모두 조밀 폴리테트라플루오로에틸렌(Dense Polytetrafluoroethylene, d-PTFE) 막을 사용했다.

수직골높이(Vertical Bone Height, VBH) 증가량은 **어느 시점에서도 군간 유의차가 없었다** — 임플란트 식립 시점 시험군 4.6 ± 3.0 mm vs 대조군 5.2 ± 2.7 mm, 임플란트 하중 1년 후 시험군 3.0 ± 2.8 mm vs 대조군 3.8 ± 2.6 mm(P = .96). 합병증은 최소였고 시험군 1예에서 감염에 의한 임플란트 상실이 발생했다.

저자들은 L-PRF 골블록을 **제2 수술부위(second surgical site) 골 채취를 줄일 수 있는 유력한 대안**으로 제시하나, 환자 6명이고 전 시점 유의차가 없으므로 이는 "동등성 증명"이 아니라 "실행가능성(feasibility)" 수준의 근거다 — 저자들도 후속 대규모 연구 필요성을 명시했다.

## Summary

This is a small but structurally clean randomized trial that asks a question the wiki's graft-material literature had only addressed indirectly: for **vertical** ridge augmentation under a non-resorbable barrier membrane, does a leukocyte-platelet-rich fibrin (L-PRF) bone block perform comparably to the conventional composite of autogenous bone (AB) and deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM) — and can it do so without opening a second surgical site?

Six patients with bilateral vertical defects were treated in a split-mouth design (each patient is their own control), with a dense polytetrafluoroethylene (d-PTFE) membrane at both test and control sites. The primary outcome was vertical bone height (VBH) gain measured by CBCT at 9 and 25 months postoperatively, with the trial followed past implant loading for up to one year.

The result is a clean null: **no significant difference in VBH gain at any timepoint.** At implant placement the test arm reached 4.6 ± 3.0 mm versus 5.2 ± 2.7 mm in controls; one year after implant loading the figures had remodeled down to 3.0 ± 2.8 mm and 3.8 ± 2.6 mm respectively (P = .96). Both arms lost roughly 1.4–1.8 mm of gained height between those two moments, so the numerical gap that existed at implant placement (0.6 mm) never translated into a clinical difference. Complications were described as minimal, comprising a single implant loss from infection in a test site.

**Clinical implication.** The trial does not show that an L-PRF bone block is *better* than the composite graft — it shows that the two produced indistinguishable radiographic height in a small cohort under one fixed membrane protocol, and the authors' own framing is accordingly conservative: L-PRF "could be a viable alternative to the composite graft, potentially reducing the need for harvesting bone from a second surgical site." The donor-site argument is the real stake of the paper, and it is asserted as a potential rather than measured: the abstract reports no donor-site morbidity or patient-reported outcome data.

**How much weight this can carry.** With 6 patients (12 sites), a single printed p-value (P = .96), no confidence intervals, no sample-size basis and no non-inferiority margin, this is a feasibility signal — a randomized, blinded, split-mouth comparison that justifies a larger trial, not evidence of equivalence. The endpoint is also radiographic height only; no histomorphometric or mineralized-tissue outcome is described, so "bone gain" here means millimetres of height rather than vital bone. And because both arms used a d-PTFE membrane, the finding is conditional on that particular barrier and cannot be carried over to collagen or titanium-mesh protocols.

## Key Contributions

1. **The first head-to-head RCT of an L-PRF bone block used as the sole vertical GBR graft** against the 50:50 AB + DBBM composite — this wiki held PRF evidence in socket preservation, sinus lift and periodontal regeneration, but not as a stand-alone vertical-GBR graft.
2. **Split-mouth with a double-blind design in a fixed membrane protocol**, isolating the graft material as the only variable between the two arms (d-PTFE at both sites).
3. **Follow-up extends past implant loading** — VBH gain is reported "one year after implant loading", making this the only vertical-GBR trial in the wiki that reports the augmented site under loaded conditions.
4. **Quantifies post-loading remodeling of grafted height in both arms** (4.6 → 3.0 mm test; 5.2 → 3.8 mm control), a parameter most vertical-GBR reports stop before.
5. **Directly targets the donor-site burden** — the stated rationale for the L-PRF block is avoiding harvest of autogenous bone from a second surgical site, the exact trade-off that dominates [[overviews/autogenous-bone-graft-donor-site-selection-overview]].

## Methodology

- **Design**: randomized, double-blind, split-mouth clinical trial
- **n**: 6 patients requiring bilateral vertical GBR before implant placement (12 sites)
- **Test site**: leukocyte-platelet-rich fibrin (L-PRF) bone block graft
- **Control site**: composite graft of 50% autogenous bone (AB) + 50% deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM)
- **Barrier**: dense polytetrafluoroethylene (d-PTFE) membrane at both test and control sites → the trial compares graft materials, not membrane strategies
- **Primary outcome**: vertical bone height (VBH) gain, assessed by CBCT at 9 and 25 months postoperative
- **Clinical landmarks**: VBH gain reported "at implant placement" and "one year after implant loading"; the abstract does not explicitly map the two CBCT timepoints onto these two moments
- **Not reported in the abstract**: sequence generation and allocation concealment, sample-size calculation, defect morphology and baseline defect height, L-PRF preparation protocol (spin speed/RCF, block dimensions), implant system and implants per site, donor-site morbidity measurement, histomorphometry, implant survival table, funding and conflicts of interest

## Results

| Timepoint | Test (L-PRF bone block) | Control (50% AB + 50% DBBM) | Reported significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| At implant placement | VBH gain 4.6 ± 3.0 mm | VBH gain 5.2 ± 2.7 mm | no significant difference at any timepoint |
| 1 year after implant loading | VBH gain 3.0 ± 2.8 mm | VBH gain 3.8 ± 2.6 mm | P = .96 |

- No significant difference in VBH gain between test and control sites at **any** timepoint; the only p-value printed in the abstract is P = .96, attached to the one-year post-loading figures.
- **Complications**: minimal — one implant loss due to infection in a test site.
- Height gained at implant placement remodeled downward in both arms between implant placement and one year after loading (test 4.6 → 3.0 mm; control 5.2 → 3.8 mm); the inter-arm difference never became significant.
- The abstract reports no donor-site morbidity outcome, no implant-survival table and no histomorphometric data.

## Limitations

- **Abstract-only source.** No full text was retrievable (closed access; PMC, Unpaywall and OpenAlex all checked), so the protocol detail above is the limit of what can be asserted.
- **n = 6.** A non-significant result in a trial this small cannot support equivalence or non-inferiority; no confidence intervals, power calculation or non-inferiority margin are reported.
- **Radiographic endpoint only.** VBH gain is a CBCT height measurement with no histomorphometric or mineralized-tissue counterpart.
- **Single membrane protocol.** Both arms used a non-resorbable d-PTFE membrane, so the finding cannot be transferred to collagen-membrane or titanium-mesh vertical GBR.
- **The headline advantage is asserted, not measured.** Donor-site morbidity and patient-reported burden — the reason to prefer the PRF block — are not reported in the abstract.
- **Author-stated next step**: larger samples are needed to confirm the findings and to explore the biologic benefits of integrating the L-PRF bone block into bone graft materials for oral implantology.
