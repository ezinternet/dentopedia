## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (76 qualitative, 16 quantitative studies) comparing narrow-diameter implants (NDI ≤3.5 mm) in three categories vs standard-diameter implants (SDI) for implant survival, covering literature 1995–2017.
Category 1 mini-implants (<3.0 mm) showed significantly worse survival than SDI (OR 4.54; 95% CI 1.51–13.65); Category 2 (3.0–3.25 mm) and Category 3 (3.3–3.5 mm) showed no significant difference (OR 1.06 and 1.19, respectively); mean survival 94.7%, 97.3%, 97.7%.
Category 2/3 NDI are viable alternatives to bone augmentation in limited-width ridges; mini-implants (Cat 1) should remain restricted to overdenture rehabilitation in severely atrophic jaws.

## Summary

This 2018 PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis by Schiegnitz and Al-Nawas from the University of Mainz provides the most comprehensive evidence base for narrow-diameter implants (NDI) across three distinct diameter categories. The authors applied the Klein et al. (2014) classification — Category 1 (<3.0 mm, "mini-implants"), Category 2 (3.0–3.25 mm), and Category 3 (3.3–3.5 mm) — to 76 studies (12,186 NDI total) for qualitative synthesis and 16 comparative studies for meta-analysis. The principal finding is that Category 2 and 3 NDI achieve survival rates statistically equivalent to SDI, supporting their use in bone-width-deficient sites as an alternative to augmentation. In contrast, Category 1 mini-implants carry a significantly higher failure risk (OR 4.54) and should be limited to overdenture retention in severely atrophic arches. A critical limitation is that 69% of quantitative studies carried high risk of bias, which constrains definitive clinical conclusions.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA to apply the Klein 3-category NDI classification with meta-analytic statistics
- Quantified significantly worse survival for Category 1 vs SDI (OR 4.54; p<0.05) — supports restricting mini-implants to overdenture indications
- Category 2 and 3 NDI statistically equivalent to SDI — provides the evidence base for augmentation-avoidance strategies
- Identified high risk of bias in 11/16 quantitative studies — appropriately tempers all recommendations
- Covered 12,186 NDI across 76 studies; largest qualitative synthesis on NDI at time of publication

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA)
- **Search**: PubMed/MEDLINE + Cochrane Library; 1995–2017
- **Studies**: 76 qualitative, 16 quantitative (≥10 patients/group, ≥12-month follow-up, NDI vs SDI comparison)
- **Exclusions**: animal studies, orthodontic mini-implants, <10 patients, <1-year follow-up
- **Quality assessment**: 6-criterion tool (randomization, blinding, focused question, identical treatment, in/exclusion criteria, n≥20)
- **Statistics**: RevMan 5.3; odds ratios with 95% CI; forest + funnel plots; significance p<0.05
- **Total sample**: 5,955 patients; 12,186 NDI (Cat 1: 5,441; Cat 2: 1,133; Cat 3: 5,612)

## Results

### Survival by Category

| Category | Diameter | Studies (qualitative) | Patients | NDI | Mean Survival | Follow-up (mean) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cat 1 (mini) | <3.0 mm | 22 | 1,280 | 5,441 | 94.7 ± 5% (80–100%) | 34 ± 20 mo |
| Cat 2 | 3.0–3.25 mm | 19 | 823 | 1,133 | 97.3 ± 5% (80.5–100%) | 29 ± 17 mo |
| Cat 3 | 3.3–3.5 mm | 35 | 3,842 | 5,612 | 97.7 ± 2.3% (91–100%) | 39 ± 24 mo |

### Meta-analysis vs SDI

| Category | OR | 95% CI | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cat 1 vs SDI | **4.54** | 1.51–13.65 | **p<0.05 (significant)** |
| Cat 2 vs SDI | 1.06 | 0.31–3.61 | NS |
| Cat 3 vs SDI | 1.19 | 0.83–1.70 | NS |

### Indications and Restorations

- **Cat 1**: Primarily edentulous arch overdentures; single non-load-bearing anterior teeth; one-piece implants 1.8–2.4 mm; immediate loading common
- **Cat 2**: Predominantly anterior single-tooth restorations; 3.0 mm diameter in 17/19 studies
- **Cat 3**: All regions including posterior load-bearing; two-piece design predominant (3.3 mm most common); mixed restoration types

### Marginal Bone Loss (MBL)

- Cat 1: 0.6–1.43 mm; Cat 2: 0.09–1.6 mm; Cat 3: 0.1–2.17 mm (wide ranges across studies)

### Risk of Bias

- Low: 3/16, Moderate: 2/16, **High: 11/16 (69%)** — significantly limits definitive conclusions
