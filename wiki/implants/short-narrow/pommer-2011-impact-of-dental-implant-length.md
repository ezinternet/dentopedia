## One-line Summary

Meta-analysis of 54 prospective observational publications (19,083 implants): short implants (≥7 mm) did not fail more often than ≥10 mm implants in the mandible within the first year of loading, but short machined implants failed more in the anterior (OR 5.4) and posterior (OR 3.4) maxilla (abstract only).

## 한줄요약

전향적 관찰연구 54편(임플란트 19,083개) 메타분석에서 짧은 임플란트(7 mm 이상)는 하악에서는 부하 첫 1년 실패가 10 mm 이상과 차이가 없었지만, 상악에서는 기계가공 표면의 짧은 임플란트가 전치부 OR 5.4, 구치부 OR 3.4로 더 많이 실패했다(초록만).

## Summary

This meta-analysis tested whether short implants (minimum 7 mm) fail more often than longer ones (≥10 mm) within the first year of prosthetic loading. 54 prospective observational publications (19,083 implants), several with author-supplied data, were pooled. In the mandible, no length effect was shown. In the maxilla, short machined implants had higher failure (anterior OR 5.4, posterior OR 3.4), and short rough-surfaced implants failed more in anterior maxillary sites. Diameter and denture type did not modify the effect. The authors conclude short implants can reduce the need for augmentation where bone height is reduced.

## Key Contributions

- Site- and surface-specific length effect: harm concentrated in the maxilla and in machined surfaces.
- Null length effect in the mandible.
- Frames short implants as an alternative to augmentation, which Krisam 2019 cites when reporting its own short-implant OR 5.8.

## Methodology

Systematic electronic and hand search; inclusion of prospective observational trials comparing short (≥7 mm) and longer (≥10 mm) implants; additional data from authors; 54 publications, 19,083 implants. Outcome: failure within the first year of prosthetic loading. Meta-analytic odds ratios by jaw, site and surface.

## Results

Mandible: no significant length effect. Maxilla, machined surface: anterior OR 5.4, posterior OR 3.4. Rough surface: increased failure in anterior maxilla only. No influence of diameter or denture type on short-implant failure (CIs not in abstract).
