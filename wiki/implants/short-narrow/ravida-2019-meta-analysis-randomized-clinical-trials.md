## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs comparing extra-short (≤6 mm) vs longer (≥10 mm) implants (implant survival, marginal bone loss, complications, patient-reported outcomes).  
Extra-short implants showed broadly comparable survival in selected indications, but marginal bone loss and outcomes varied by augmentation/protocol; patient-reported data were limited.  
Supports cautious equivalence of ≤6 mm implants vs ≥10 mm implants in carefully selected cases, emphasizing case-specific decision-making.

## 세줄요약

RCT를 대상으로 한 체계적문헌고찰·메타분석으로, 초단축(≤6 mm) 임플란트와 장축(≥10 mm) 임플란트의 생존율·변연골소실·합병증·환자보고결과를 비교했다.  
선택된 적응증에서 생존율은 대체로 유사했지만, 변연골소실과 결과는 골증대 여부·시술 프로토콜에 따라 달랐고 환자보고자료는 제한적이었다.  
신중하게 선별된 증례에서 ≤6 mm 임플란트는 ≥10 mm 임플란트와 동등할 수 있으나, 해부학적·보철적 요인을 고려한 개별적 판단이 필요하다.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials compared clinical and patient-reported outcomes between extra-short (≤6 mm) and longer (≥10 mm) dental implants. The synthesis indicates broadly comparable implant survival in selected scenarios, with variable results for marginal bone loss depending on protocols (including bone augmentation). Patient-reported outcomes were less extensively reported. The findings support cautious equivalence in carefully selected indications rather than universal interchangeability.

## Key Contributions

- Provides an RCT-based quantitative synthesis of ≤6 mm vs ≥10 mm implants across survival, marginal bone loss, complications, and PROs.
- Highlights context-dependence (augmentation/protocols) of outcomes.
- Informs reduced-dimension implant strategies by quantifying tradeoffs relative to longer implants with/without augmentation.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials.
- **Comparison:** Extra-short (≤6 mm) implants (test) vs longer (≥10 mm) implants (control), with/without bone augmentation as reported.
- **Outcomes:** Implant survival, marginal bone loss, complications, patient-reported outcomes.
- **Synthesis:** Meta-analysis where data were sufficiently homogeneous; heterogeneity considered.
