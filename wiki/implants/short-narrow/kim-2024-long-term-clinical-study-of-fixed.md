## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center study at Seoul National University Bundang Hospital (Korea; implants 2007-2022, at least 6 years of follow-up required) of one-piece 2.5 mm and 3.0 mm narrow-diameter implants (Osstem MS SA narrow ridge): 25 patients, 40 implants, mean 10.5 years (range 6.1-14.0) of function.

Survival was 95.1% at the implant level and 96.0% at the patient level; two 2.5 mm implants failed in one patient with uncontrolled diabetes and poor hygiene (one osseointegration failure 3 months after immediate loading, one peri-implantitis 118 months after loading), and all 3.0 mm implants survived.

Mean marginal bone loss was 0.44±0.57 mm and only guided bone regeneration (GBR) at placement was significantly associated with it (p=0.046); the sample is very small and patient ethnicity is not stated in the text (Korean only implied by the setting).

## 세줄요약

서울대학교 분당병원(한국)에서 2007-2022년 식립한 Osstem MS SA 일체형 2.5 mm/3.0 mm 협경 임플란트 (narrow-diameter implant, NDI)를 6년 이상 추적한 후향적 단일기관 연구: 환자 25명, 임플란트 40개, 보철 후 평균 10.5년(범위 6.1-14.0).

생존율은 임플란트 기준 95.1%, 환자 기준 96.0%이며, 실패 2개는 모두 조절되지 않은 당뇨와 불량한 구강위생을 가진 한 환자의 2.5 mm 임플란트로 즉시 부하 3개월 후 골유착 실패 1개와 118개월 후 치주염 1개였고 3.0 mm는 전부 생존했다.

평균 변연골 소실 (marginal bone loss, MBL)은 0.44±0.57 mm였고 식립 시 골유도재생술 (guided bone regeneration, GBR)만 유의한 관련을 보였다 (p=0.046); 표본이 매우 작고 환자 인종은 본문에 명시되지 않았다(한국 환자는 기관 위치로만 추정).

## Summary

This retrospective study from the Section of Dentistry, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital (Seongnam, Korea) evaluated one-piece, tapered, sandblasted and acid-etched Osstem MS SA narrow ridge implants of 2.5 mm and 3.0 mm diameter (lengths 10-15 mm) placed between 2007 and 2022. Patients with under 6 years of follow-up, loss to follow-up, or extraction of adjacent natural teeth during follow-up were excluded. The final sample was 25 patients (17 men, 8 women) with 40 implants (5 maxillary, 35 mandibular; 32 anterior, 8 posterior), followed for a mean of 10.5 years (range 6.1-14.0) after prosthesis placement. Patient nationality or ethnicity is not stated in the text; the Korean population is implied only by the institutional setting.

Survival by Kaplan-Meier was 95.1% at the implant level and 96.0% at the patient level (2.5 mm 93.8%, 3.0 mm 100.0%). Both failures were 2.5 mm implants in the mandibular incisor region of the same patient, who had uncontrolled diabetes and poor oral hygiene: the right implant was removed 3 months after immediate loading for osseointegration failure (early failure) and replaced with a 3.0 mm implant that survived over 10 years; the left implant was removed 118 months after loading for peri-implantitis (late failure).

Mean marginal bone loss (MBL) was 0.44±0.57 mm over the observation period. In univariate and multivariate logistic regression only GBR at implant placement was significantly associated with MBL (p=0.046); the authors infer that sites needing GBR have lower bone quantity or quality. Two prosthetic complications occurred (one decementation, one veneer chipping).

## Key Contributions

- Long-term Korean single-center data for 2.5 mm and 3.0 mm one-piece NDIs supporting fixed single crowns, cantilevers and bridges in anterior and posterior regions.
- Early (3 months after loading) versus late (118 months) failure are separated, though both fall in one patient with uncontrolled diabetes and poor hygiene.
- Low MBL (0.44 mm) with 6-month to 1-year recall; GBR was the only associated factor.

## Methodology

- Retrospective, single center, IRB approved, consent waived. Data from electronic medical records and periapical radiographs (recall every 6 months or 1 year).
- Variables: age, sex, jaw, site, immediate vs delayed placement, placement depth, GBR, diameter, loading timing (immediate/early/delayed at 1 week and 8 weeks), splinted vs non-splinted, opposing dentition.
- Kaplan-Meier survival; univariate and multivariate logistic regression for MBL (outcome dichotomization not described in the retrieved text); R 4.1.0.
- No explicit failure definition is given; both failures were implant removals.

## Limitations

- Very small sample (25 patients, 40 implants, 2 clustered failures in one patient); subgroup comparison of 2.5 vs 3.0 mm and risk factor analyses are underpowered; group sizes by diameter are not in the retrieved text (tables absent).
- Inclusion required more than 6 years of follow-up and no loss to follow-up, a survivorship selection that could omit earlier failures in lost patients.
- Retrospective; bone quality and quantity at placement were insufficient (authors). Most implants were mandibular (35 of 40) and anterior (32 of 40).
- Source-data issue: 2 failures in 40 implants is 95.0%, whereas 95.1% is reported (method for the Kaplan-Meier estimate not detailed); the replacement implant's inclusion in the 40 is not clarified.
- No effect sizes (odds ratios, CIs) for MBL factors in the retrieved text. No explicit failure definition.
- Nationality/ethnicity not stated; Korean setting only.
