## Three-line Summary
Retrospective single-surgeon study at Seoul National University Dental Hospital: 33 medically compromised patients (11 male, 22 female; mean age 62 years) received 47 internal-connection tapered 7-mm implants (4.0 mm n=38, 4.5 mm n=8, 5.0 mm n=1) without bone augmentation, followed for 7 to 36 months (mean 15 months). Implant survival from stage I surgery to last follow-up was 95.74% (2 of 47 failed; both 4.0-mm implants in one oral-cancer reconstruction patient), and mean marginal bone loss (MBL) at 2 years was 0.58 +/- 0.60 mm mesial and 0.71 +/- 0.60 mm distal, with most change occurring in the first year. Authors conclude 7-mm implants are a reliable option when sinus lift or vertical grafting should be avoided, but the small sample, short follow-up, heterogeneous medical groups, and absence of a control group limit generalisation.

## One-line Summary
Retrospective (33 patients, 47 7-mm implants, mean 15 months): 95.74% survival in medically compromised patients with 2-year MBL of 0.58-0.71 mm.

## 한줄요약
후향적(환자 33명, 7mm 임플란트 47개, 평균 15개월): 전신 위험 환자에서 생존율 95.74%, 2년 변연골 소실 0.58~0.71mm.

## Summary
Single-centre retrospective analysis from the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Seoul National University Dental Hospital (implants placed January 2015 to March 2018, one surgeon). Patients belonged to at least one of three medically compromised groups: systemic disorders (controlled or uncontrolled diabetes, mental disability, uncontrolled hypertension), oral cancer ablation with reconstruction with or without radiotherapy, and osteomyelitis (osteoradionecrosis, BRONJ). All had insufficient residual bone (mandibular atrophy or sinus pneumatization) and no augmentation. Forty-seven 7-mm Luna (internal submerged tapered, n=41) or Stella (internal non-submerged, n=6) sand-blasted and acid-etched implants were placed (19 maxilla, 28 mandible). Failure was defined as a lost or mobile implant or severe peri-implantitis requiring prompt removal.

## Key Contributions
- Survival data for 7-mm implants in a medically compromised population where short-implant evidence is limited
- Two-year panoramic MBL trajectory showing early change (to 1 year) and stabilisation (1 to 2 years)
- Supports 7-mm implants as an option when sinus lifting or vertical grafting should be avoided

## Methodology
Retrospective data analysis (IRB S-D20180022). 33 patients, 47 implants, follow-up 7 to 36 months (mean 15). MBL measured on panoramic radiographs (implant shoulder to most coronal bone-to-implant contact, mesial and distal, mean of 3 measurements) at installation, 3 months, 1 year and 2 years. SPSS 25.0; mean +/- SD; Shapiro-Wilk test.

## Results
- Survival: 95.74% (45/47); by diameter 4.0 mm 94.74%, 4.5 mm 100%, 5.0 mm 100% (not significantly different, p=0.069 as reported)
- Both failures: 4.0-mm implants in one patient with oral cancer after mandibular resection and reconstruction; most failures occurred during healing at abutment connection
- Prostheses on surviving implants: 19 single crowns, 22 multiple fixed, 4 overdentures
- MBL mesial/distal: 1 year 0.53 +/- 0.58 / 0.67 +/- 0.56 mm; 2 years 0.58 +/- 0.60 / 0.71 +/- 0.60 mm; 1 to 2 year increase 0.05 +/- 0.12 / 0.04 +/- 0.05 mm
- MBL at 3 months, 1 year, 2 years significantly greater than at installation; 1 vs 2 years not significantly different

## Risk Factors and Patient Context
- The only failures clustered in the oral cancer reconstruction patient with poor postoperative bone volume and quality; the authors note these were placed in alveolar bone of insufficient height involved in cancer treatment
- No per-group (diabetes, hypertension, osteomyelitis) survival comparison is reported, so the paper cannot rank systemic risk factors

## Limitations
- Retrospective, single-centre, single-surgeon; no standard-length or healthy-patient control group
- Small, heterogeneous sample (33 patients, 47 implants); only 2 failure events, both in one patient (clustering, non-independence)
- Short follow-up (mean 15 months, range 7 to 36); censoring and number reaching 2-year MBL not described
- Panoramic radiograph MBL with single analyst; implant systems limited to two designs from one manufacturer
- Case selection confounding (compromised bone sites); authors request long-term follow-up
