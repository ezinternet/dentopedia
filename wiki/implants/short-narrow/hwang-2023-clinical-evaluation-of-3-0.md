## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort at Seoul National University Dental Hospital (Korea; records March 2017 to April 2022) of a single 3.0-mm two-piece SLA implant system (TS III SA 3.0, Osstem): 64 implants in 49 patients, median follow-up 30.5 months (IQR 26.75-45).

Six of 64 implants (6 of 49 patients) failed (5 osseointegration failure, 1 severe buccal thread exposure), all removed before prosthetic treatment at 0-4 months after placement, giving a Kaplan-Meier survival of 90.6%; older age was the only significant risk factor (univariate HR 1.12, p=0.03; multivariate HR 1.17, 95% CI 1.04-1.31, p=0.01), while GBR showed a non-significant trend (multivariate HR 8.12, 95% CI 0.86-76.28, p=0.07).

Mean marginal bone loss (MBL) in the 58 implants that reached prosthesis was 0.44±0.75 mm with no significant associated factor; the paper itself notes the small sample, under 5 years of follow-up, unanalysed systemic disease and smoking, and unmeasured bone quality as limits.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort comes from the Department of Periodontology at Seoul National University Dental Hospital, a Korean tertiary university hospital. It reviewed records from March 2017 to April 2022 for patients who received one specific narrow-diameter implant (NDI): TS III SA 3.0 (Osstem), a two-piece internal tapered sandblasted and acid-etched implant 3.0 mm in diameter (Klein category 2) in lengths of 8.5 to 13 mm. The cohort comprised 64 implants in 49 patients (24 male, 25 female; median age 64 years, IQR 49.75-72), mostly at maxillary lateral incisor and mandibular incisor sites (55 of 64). The text does not state patient nationality or ethnicity; Korean patients are implied only by the hospital setting.

Median follow-up was 30.5 months (IQR 26.75-45). Six implants (in 6 of 49 patients) were removed: 5 for osseointegration failure and 1 for an aesthetic problem from severe buccal thread exposure. Table 2 lists removal 0 to 4 months after placement for all six, and the text states all failed implants were removed before prosthetic treatment, so every failure was early; no late failure occurred within the observation window, which the authors say reflects the short follow-up. Kaplan-Meier cumulative survival at implant level was 90.6%, lower than the 93.8-100% cohort range and the 97.3±5% category 2 meta-analytic mean the authors cite. Failure was defined as removal of the implant, independent of complications; the denominator for survival is implants, with patient-level counts also reported.

Older age was associated with failure (univariate HR 1.12, 95% CI 1.01-1.23, p=0.03; multivariate HR 1.17, 95% CI 1.04-1.31, p=0.01, adjusted for jaw, location, sex, length, stage and placement type). GBR showed a higher but non-significant hazard (univariate HR 4.75, 95% CI 0.55-40.65, p=0.16; multivariate HR 8.12, 95% CI 0.86-76.28, p=0.07). Sex, jaw, location, length, surgical stage, placement type and surgeon proficiency were not significant. Residents placed 48 of 64 implants and 5 of the 6 failures; the authors discuss inexperience as a possible reason for lower survival but the Cox test for proficiency was non-significant (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.07-5.10).

For the 58 implants that reached prosthesis, mean MBL from the fixture-abutment junction was 0.44±0.75 mm (55.2% showed no bone loss; 3.4% over 2 mm), and no tested factor (jaw, GBR, prosthesis type, placement type, location, age, loading time) was significantly associated.

## Key Contributions

- Korean single-institution cohort restricted to one 3.0-mm implant system, reducing heterogeneity in NDI literature.
- Documents that all observed failures were early (0-4 months, before prosthesis), mostly osseointegration failure, with age as the only significant risk factor.
- Low short-term MBL (0.44 mm) supports acceptable bone stability among implants that reach prosthesis.

## Methodology

- Retrospective cohort (STROBE), IRB approved, consent waived; SNUDH records March 2017 to April 2022; excluded patients without final prosthesis or post-prosthesis radiograph.
- Covariates: age, sex, jaw, location, length, stage, GBR, placement type, prosthesis type, surgeon proficiency. Not analysed: systemic disease, smoking, bone quality.
- Kaplan-Meier; univariate then backward-stepwise multivariate Cox; MBL by fixture-abutment junction to first bone-to-implant contact on panoramic or periapical films (58 implants); non-parametric tests and Spearman.

## Limitations

- Only 6 failures among 64 implants; many Cox intervals are extremely wide (e.g. location 0.04, 0.00-744.30; stage 47.07, 0.06-3.97E+4), so null findings are uninformative.
- Follow-up under 5 years; late failure from overload or peri-implantitis could not be assessed; failures were essentially early failures.
- Systemic disease, smoking and bone quality not analysed; no comparison with regular-diameter implants; single center, single system; 48 of 64 implants placed by residents.
- MBL referenced to the fixture-abutment junction can miss bone loss above it for subcrestal implants; MBL covers 58 implants only.
- Text inconsistencies: Table 1 prosthesis counts sum to 58 not 64 (matching the six pre-prosthesis failures but unstated), and the Discussion quotes a 25-month average follow-up for MBL versus a 30.5-month median in Results.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

In a Korean university hospital setting, 3.0-mm two-piece SLA implants can serve as an alternative where standard-diameter implants do not fit (narrow incisor sites), but expect lower survival (90.6% here) than the 97% range cited for category 2 NDIs, with failures clustering in the first four months before loading. Caution is warranted in older patients (about 17% higher hazard per additional year of age in the adjusted model) and probably where GBR is combined with NDI placement, although the GBR signal was not significant. Whether results apply beyond this resident-heavy, single-center cohort is unknown.
