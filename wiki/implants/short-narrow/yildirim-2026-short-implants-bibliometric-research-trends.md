## Three-line Summary

Bibliometric / science-mapping analysis (Bibliometrix R) of 626 Web of Science articles on short implants published 1994–2024, covering performance, keyword co-occurrence, co-citation, and thematic-map analyses.

Annual publication growth was 12.69% (peak 2019, 57 papers, then decline); Clinical Oral Implants Research and Int J Oral Maxillofac Implants were the leading journals, Esposito M and Felice P the most influential authors, and thematic mapping resolved four research areas — clinical outcomes/survival, atrophic-posterior-jaw rehabilitation, prosthetic-biomechanical, and comparisons with standard implants/augmentation.

This maps *research activity*, not clinical effectiveness — it shows where the field's attention and evidence-production have concentrated and where gaps remain (long-term outcomes in compromised bone, RCTs vs alternatives, digital/patient-specific designs), not which treatment performs better.

## 세줄요약

Web of Science에서 1994–2024년 짧은 임플란트 논문 626편을 Bibliometrix(R)로 분석한 계량서지·과학지도 연구로, 성과·키워드 동시출현·동시인용·주제지도 분석을 수행했다.

연간 발행 증가율 12.69%(2019년 정점 57편 후 감소), 최다 영향 저널은 Clinical Oral Implants Research·Int J Oral Maxillofac Implants, 최다 영향 저자는 Esposito M·Felice P, 주제지도는 4개 영역(임상결과/생존, 위축 후방 악골 재건, 보철-생역학, 표준 임플란트/증대술 비교)을 도출했다.

이 논문은 임상적 *효과성*이 아니라 *연구 활동*의 지도다 — 어디에 근거 생산이 집중됐고 어디에 공백(취약골/전신질환자 장기결과, 대안 대비 RCT, 디지털·환자맞춤 설계)이 남았는지를 보여줄 뿐, 어떤 치료가 더 우수한지는 말하지 않는다.

## Summary

Yildirim (2026) is the first bibliometric analysis dedicated to short implants. Using the Web of Science (searched 1 Jan 2025) and the Bibliometrix R package, the author screened 888 records down to **626 articles (1994–2024)** in the Dentistry/Oral Surgery/Medicine category (English; SCI-EXPANDED + ESCI). No implant-length threshold was imposed — because "short" is defined inconsistently across studies (≤8 mm in some, down to 4 mm in others), every article self-describing implants as "short" was retained.

The value of the page for this wiki is as a **coverage audit of research activity**, not a clinical-decision source. The wiki already settles the short-implant-vs-sinus-graft question on outcomes (see Related Papers); this map instead tells you *who is producing the evidence, in which journals, and which themes are rising or exhausted*.

## Methodology

- **Corpus**: 626 WoS articles, 72 source journals, 1994–2024; 2,099 authors; 10,030 references; average 5.09 authors/article; 31.31% international co-authorship. Document types: 507 articles (81%), 103 reviews, 1 retracted publication.
- **Tool**: Bibliometrix (R); Louvain clustering for co-citation and thematic networks; PRISMA flow diagram adapted (no bibliometric-specific reporting guideline exists).
- **Four analysis blocks**: performance (trends, source/author impact, most-cited docs), word analysis (word cloud, author-keyword co-occurrence), co-citation (paper/author/source networks), thematic analysis (thematic map + four-period thematic evolution, top 250 author keywords appearing ≥8 times).

## Results

**Growth and momentum**
- Annual publication growth rate **12.69%**; average citations per document **33.9**; document average age 8.31 years.
- Publication volume peaked in **2019 (57 papers)** then declined slightly after 2019–2020. Average-citation peak was **1999 (7.6 avg)**, falling through the 2010s. The author attributes the post-2019 slowdown to attention shifting toward patient-specific subperiosteal implants, zygomatic-implant protocols, and augmentation-material innovation.

**Leading journals** (by h-index)
| Journal | h-index | NP | Total citations | Cites/paper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clin Oral Implants Res | 37 | 69 | 4,450 | 64.5 |
| Int J Oral Maxillofac Implants | 31 | 89 | 3,707 | 41.7 |
| Eur J Oral Implantol | 30 | 39 | 2,659 | 68.2 |
| Clin Implant Dent Relat Res | 26 | 58 | 1,892 | 32.6 |

**Leading authors** (by h-index)
| Author | h-index | Total citations | NP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Esposito M | 31 | 2,597 | 35 |
| Felice P | 30 | 2,616 (highest) | 45 |
| Pistilli R | 20 | 1,524 | 29 |
| Barausse C | 19 | 1,116 | 28 |

Barausse C — first author of the wiki's 4-mm 8-year page — ranks among the top-20 producers, but with lower citation density than Esposito/Felice. High per-paper impact belongs to Cannizzaro G (68/paper) and Thoma DS / Hämmerle CHF (~65/paper).

**Most globally cited articles**
1. Chiapasco M 2009 — *Bone Augmentation Procedures in Implant Dentistry* — 557.
2. Goodacre CJ 1999 — *Clinical Complications of Osseointegrated Implants* — 435.
3. Renouard F 2006 — *Impact of Implant Length and Diameter on Survival Rates* — 411.
4. Chrcanovic BR 2014 — *Reasons for Failures of Oral Implants* — 311.
5. Szmukler-Moncler S 2000 — *Early and Immediate Loading Protocols* — 301.

Most-cited works are dominated by systematic reviews and RCTs — a high-level-evidence emphasis. Highest-frequency keywords: "short implants" (287), "dental implants" (221).

**Thematic map — four research areas**
1. Clinical outcomes and survival of short implants.
2. Rehabilitation of atrophic posterior jaws.
3. Prosthetic and biomechanical considerations.
4. Comparisons with standard implants / augmentation approaches.

**Thematic evolution**: early work centred on implant length, survival, and basic feasibility → later shifted to atrophic-posterior-jaw rehabilitation, augmentation comparison, and prosthetic/biomechanical questions — foundational feasibility maturing into specialized, practice-oriented research.

**Stated gaps / future directions**: long-term outcomes in diverse or compromised-bone/systemic-condition patients; RCTs comparing short implants with alternative posterior modalities; digital workflows and patient-specific implant designs; and how these newer technologies affect short-implant survival and success. Relatively few studies address long-term complications, special patient groups, and digital/patient-specific integration — the map's identified white space.
