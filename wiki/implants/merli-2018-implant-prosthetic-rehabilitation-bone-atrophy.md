## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 12 systematic reviews of RCTs (search May 2012–May 2017; ROBIS low risk of bias in 8/12) on implant prosthetic rehabilitation in partially edentulous patients with bone atrophy, covering bone augmentation, short/tilted/zygomatic implants, sinus lift, and IAN transposition.

Short implants (≤8 mm) showed a notable decrease in complications versus long implants placed with bone augmentation; no sinus-lift/augmentation technique, membrane, or bone-substitute type was superior to another for implant/prosthetic failure; zero RCT evidence existed for zygomatic implants, tilted implants, or IAN transposition.

No single best treatment for atrophic partially edentulous ridges could be identified — short implants are preferable when feasible for vertical defects (fewer complications) but long-term data are lacking, and no quantitative meta-analysis was performed (qualitative synthesis, abstract-only retrieval).

## Summary

This umbrella review by Merli et al. (2018, *European Journal of Oral Implantology*) synthesized 12 systematic reviews of RCTs (search window May 2012–May 2017 across MEDLINE, Cochrane, PROSPERO, plus manual search) addressing implant prosthetic rehabilitation in partially edentulous patients with bone atrophy. Unlike many umbrella reviews that mix SRs of varying primary-study designs, this review restricted inclusion to **systematic reviews of RCTs only**, and assessed the included SRs' risk of bias using **ROBIS** rather than AMSTAR — 8 of 12 were judged low risk of bias. The scope spans bone augmentation procedures, short implants, tilted implants, zygomatic implants, sinus lift, and inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) transposition, across outcomes of implant/prosthetic failure, complications, peri-implant bone level change, esthetic/functional satisfaction, and vestibular soft-tissue recession.

The headline finding is that **short implants (≤8 mm) show notably fewer complications than long implants combined with bone augmentation** in the vertical-defect scenario, making them the preferred option when clinically feasible — though long-term follow-up data were not available at the time of this review. For technique-vs-technique comparisons (different sinus-lift approaches, different bone augmentation techniques, different membranes or bone substitutes), no approach was shown superior to another for reducing implant or prosthetic failure. A membrane appeared to improve hard-tissue regeneration in horizontal augmentation, but this did not translate into fewer failures or complications. Critically, **no RCT-based comparative data existed at all** for zygomatic implants, tilted implants, or IAN transposition — these remain evidence gaps rather than validated equivalents.

## Key Contributions

- Restricts inclusion to systematic reviews of **RCTs only**, avoiding the confounding of mixed-design primary evidence common in broader umbrella reviews.
- Uses **ROBIS** (Risk Of Bias In Systematic reviews) rather than AMSTAR-2 to grade the included SRs — 8/12 rated low risk of bias.
- Covers the **full breadth** of atrophic-ridge implant strategies (augmentation, short/tilted/zygomatic implants, sinus lift, IAN transposition) in a single synthesis rather than one technique pairing.
- Explicitly identifies **zero-evidence gaps**: no RCT-derived comparative data for zygomatic implants, tilted implants, or IAN transposition as of the 2012–2017 search window.

## Methodology

- **Design**: umbrella review of systematic reviews of RCTs.
- **Search**: MEDLINE, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, PROSPERO register, restricted to the prior 5 years (May 2012–May 2017); supplemented by manual search of SRs.
- **Population**: partially edentulous adult patients presenting bone defects.
- **Interventions of interest**: bone augmentation procedures; short, tilted, or zygomatic implants; sinus lift; IAN transposition.
- **Outcomes**: prosthetic and implant failure; complications; radiographic and clinical peri-implant bone level change; esthetic and functional satisfaction; vestibular peri-implant soft-tissue recession.
- **Risk of bias**: ROBIS, independently applied by two authors alongside predefined data extraction fields.
- **Analysis**: qualitative/narrative synthesis across the 12 included SRs — **no quantitative meta-meta-analysis was performed**.

## Results

- 12 systematic reviews of RCTs included; 8/12 low risk of bias (ROBIS).
- Short implants (≤8 mm) → notable decrease in complications vs. long implants + bone augmentation.
- No sinus-lift or bone-augmentation technique shown superior to another for implant/prosthetic failure.
- Membrane use → improved hard-tissue regeneration in horizontal augmentation, but no difference in failure/complications; different membrane or bone-substitute materials did not differ substantially.
- No RCT-based data for zygomatic implants, tilted implants, or IAN transposition.
- No conclusions possible on vertical bone augmentation technique choice in the atrophic posterior mandible (underlying reviews had not performed quantitative meta-analyses).
- Overall conclusion: evidence is not sufficiently robust to determine a single best treatment for atrophic partially edentulous ridges; short implants are the preferred default for vertical defects where feasible, pending longer-term follow-up data.
