## Three-line Summary

- **Study type & context:** RCT (50 patients, 300 implants) comparing conventional pick-up (gypsum) vs digital (Carestream CS 3600 IOS, splinted scan bodies) impressions for immediately loaded full-arch screw-retained maxillary rehabilitations on 6 implants (All-on-6).
- **Key results:** 24-month marginal bone loss: CIG 1.07 ± 0.66 mm vs DIG 1.11 ± 0.54 mm (p > 0.05); 100% implant and prosthetic survival both groups; procedure time significantly shorter for digital: 8:59 ± 2:46 min vs 16:45 ± 4:49 min (p < 0.05); zero voids at bar-implant connection in either group.
- **Clinical implication:** Digital workflow yields equivalent 2-year clinical/radiographic outcomes with ~50% time savings for full-arch maxillary impressions; more rescans needed (7 vs 2) but each rescan faster; IOS is a reliable alternative for full-arch rehabilitation.

## Summary

This prospective randomized clinical trial compared conventional versus digital impression workflows for full-arch screw-retained maxillary rehabilitations supported by six immediately loaded implants. Fifty patients (mean age 64.4 years, range 48–72) were randomized: 25 to conventional impression group (CIG, gypsum pick-up with splinted transfers) and 25 to digital impression group (DIG, Carestream CS 3600 intraoral scanner with splinted scan bodies). All patients received 6 CSR implants (Sweden & Martina) with immediate loading of temporary prostheses within 24 hours. At 4 months, definitive impressions were taken per group assignment. Framework fit was verified by intraoral digital radiographs (voids at bar-implant connection, marginal fit). Follow-up at 3, 6, 12, 24 months assessed implant survival, prosthetic survival, marginal bone loss (calibrated panoramic radiographs), and prosthetic complications.

**Results:** 100% fixture survival and 100% prosthetic survival in both groups at 24 months. Marginal bone loss at 24 months: CIG 1.07 ± 0.66 mm vs DIG 1.11 ± 0.54 mm (p > 0.05). Procedure time: CIG 16 min 45 sec ± 4:49 vs DIG 8 min 59 sec ± 2:46 (p < 0.05). Digital group required more rescans (7 vs 2 retakes) but each rescan was faster (2:34 vs 6:02 additional time). No voids at bar-implant connection in either group. All frameworks fit accurately per Sheffield test.

## Key Contributions

1. **RCT evidence for digital vs conventional in full-arch maxillary context** — Few RCTs exist with 2-year follow-up specifically for full-arch (All-on-6) maxillary rehabilitations.
2. **Time efficiency quantified** — Digital impression procedure took ~50% less time (8:59 vs 16:45 min) despite more rescans.
3. **Long-term bone loss data** — 24-month marginal bone loss ~1.1 mm in both groups, well within success criteria (<1.5 mm at 1 year, <0.2 mm/year thereafter).
4. **Framework fit equivalence** — Zero voids at bar-implant connection in both groups; Sheffield test confirmed equivalent marginal fit.
5. **Operator-blinded radiographic assessment** — Blinded radiologist measured bone levels, reducing measurement bias.

## Methodology

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Design** | Prospective, randomized, parallel-group clinical trial |
| **Setting** | San Raffaele Hospital, Milan, Italy (Department of Dentistry) |
| **Population** | 50 edentulous/terminal dentition maxillary patients, ASA I–II, mean age 64.4 years (48–72), good oral hygiene, adequate bone for 6 implants |
| **Exclusion** | Smoking >15 cigarettes/day, bruxism, systemic disease, active infection in planned sites (11 excluded) |
| **Randomization** | Lots in closed envelopes by blinded operator |
| **Blinding** | Radiologist blinded; outcome assessors blinded where possible |
| **Implants** | CSR system (Sweden & Martina), 3.8/4.2 mm diameter, 13/15 mm length; conical connection, platform switching, 0.8 mm machined neck; insertion torque ≥40 Ncm |
| **Surgery** | Full-thickness flaps; 4 posterior (axial or tilted ≤30°), 2 anterior axial; crestal leveling as needed; pre-angled 17° abutments for non-parallelism |
| **Provisional** | Screw-retained acrylic resin, delivered within 24h, torqued 15 Ncm, intraoral reline with autopolymerizing polyurethane |
| **Intervention (DIG)** | Carestream CS 3600 (v3.1.0), structured light; splinted scan bodies (orthodontic wire + composite); stitching strategy: distal left → anterior right → distal left palatal → buccal cross → anterior right; soft tissue scan + temporary prosthesis scan with analogs; STL export; lab scanner (Neway) verification |
| **Control (CIG)** | Gypsum (Éclair Class II); splinted impression transfers (orthodontic wire + composite); implant analogs attached; stone model |
| **Definitive prosthesis** | Both: titanium framework (CAD/CAM milled, Exocad), composite suprastructure (NACERA HYBRID), dual-cure cement (RelyX Unicem); screw-retained |
| **Follow-up** | 3, 6, 12, 24 months (hygienist); panoramic radiographs at each visit |
| **Primary outcomes** | Marginal bone loss (mesial/distal, calibrated to implant height), implant survival, prosthetic survival, framework fit (voids, Sheffield test) |
| **Secondary outcomes** | Impression procedure time, retakes/rescans, peri-implant soft tissue health |
| **Bone loss measurement** | Mesial/distal per implant; implant-abutment junction reference; calibration via known implant dimension on radiograph; DIGORA 2.5 software |
| **Statistical analysis** | Student's t-test for continuous variables; Wilcoxon signed-rank for time/retakes; p<0.05 significant; SPSS 11.5 |
| **Funding** | No external funding; no conflicts declared |

## Clinical Takeaway

For immediately loaded full-arch maxillary screw-retained rehabilitations (All-on-6), **digital impressions with Carestream CS 3600 achieve equivalent 2-year marginal bone loss (~1.1 mm), 100% implant/prosthetic survival, and framework fit compared to conventional gypsum pick-up impressions**, while reducing chairside impression time by ~50% (9 vs 17 minutes). The digital workflow required more rescans (7 vs 2), but each rescan was substantially faster (~2.5 vs ~6 min). **Digital impressions are a reliable, time-efficient alternative for full-arch implant rehabilitation.** The conical implant-abutment connection (CSR system) likely contributed to the excellent bone maintenance in both groups by resisting bacterial microleakage.

## Limitations

1. **Single center, single operator for scanning** — Results may not generalize to less experienced operators; operator learning curve acknowledged
2. **Single IOS system** (Carestream CS 3600 v3.1.0) — Results may not extrapolate to other scanners/software versions
3. **Maxilla only** — Mandibular full-arch accuracy may differ due to anatomy, tongue, saliva
4. **All-on-6 protocol** — All-on-4 or other implant numbers not tested
5. **No patient-reported outcomes** — Comfort, preference, satisfaction not formally measured (though discussion cites literature on patient preference for digital)
6. **No cost analysis** — Time savings demonstrated but equipment/software costs not evaluated
7. **Blinding limited** — Patients and surgeons not blinded to impression method; only radiologist and some assessors blinded
