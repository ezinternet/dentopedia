## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis of 11 studies (RCTs, cohorts, case series; 268 patients, 369 implants) on modern customized CAD/CAM 3D-printed titanium subperiosteal implants (CSI) for severely atrophic jaws, searched to November 2025 (PRISMA, PROSPERO, random-effects pooling).

Short-term survival was high (97.8% at ≤3-year follow-up), but overall pooled survival was 92.4% with substantial heterogeneity driven by long-term data — the single 6-year study reported survival falling to 54.1%, and soft tissue–related complications (implant exposure, dehiscence) were the most common adverse event and the main cause of late failure.

CSI give predictable short-term rehabilitation but survival alone overestimates clinical success; treat them as a graftless salvage option for carefully selected severe-atrophy patients — abstract-only page (JOMS full text paywalled, not retrieved).

## 세줄요약

심하게 위축된 악골에 쓰는 최신 맞춤형 CAD/CAM 3D 프린팅 티타늄 골막하 임플란트(CSI)를 다룬 SR+MA로, 2025년 11월까지 검색해 11편(RCT·코호트·증례군; 환자 268명, 임플란트 369개)을 random-effects로 통합했다.

단기 생존율은 높았으나(≤3년 97.8%) 전체 통합 생존율은 92.4%로 장기 데이터가 주도하는 상당한 이질성을 보였고 — 유일한 6년 추적 연구에서 생존율이 54.1%까지 떨어졌으며, 연조직 관련 합병증(임플란트 노출·열개)이 가장 흔한 부작용이자 후기 실패의 주원인이었다.

CSI는 단기 재건에는 예측 가능하나 생존율만으로는 임상 성공을 과대평가하므로, 신중히 선별된 심한 위축 환자의 무이식(graftless) salvage 옵션으로 다뤄야 한다 — 초록 전용 페이지(JOMS 전문 페이월, 미확보).

## Summary

**⚠️ Abstract-only — full text not retrieved (paywalled).** This page is built from the structured JOMS abstract (the PDF artifact is a publisher web-page print carrying the abstract plus reference list). No methods, subgroup, confidence-interval, or risk-of-bias detail beyond the abstract is available; reported numbers are limited to those in the abstract.

Rehabilitating a severely atrophic jaw is a standing challenge, and **customized CAD/CAM 3D-printed titanium subperiosteal implants (CSI)** have re-emerged as a **graftless alternative** to extensive bone augmentation. Unlike endosseous implants embedded in bone, a subperiosteal implant is a patient-specific framework that rests on the residual ridge beneath the periosteum and carries the prosthesis on transmucosal posts, allowing immediate fixed rehabilitation where there is too little bone for conventional implants and where the patient wants to avoid grafting or re-grafting.

This SR+MA (11 studies, 268 patients, 369 implants) shows a **survival-versus-time collapse**: short-term (≤3-year) pooled survival is excellent at 97.8%, but overall pooled survival drops to 92.4% with heterogeneity driven by longer follow-up, and the single 6-year study reports survival falling to 54.1%. Critically, the failure mode is **soft tissue, not osseointegration** — implant exposure and dehiscence were the most common adverse events and the main cause of late failure. The authors' bottom line is deliberately cautious: survival alone overestimates clinical success, and CSI should be reserved as a **salvage option for carefully selected patients**.

For the wiki's atrophic-jaw decision ladder (graft → short implant → tilted/full-arch → vertical augmentation), CSI slot in as the graftless salvage rung when bone-based options are exhausted or declined — but with an explicit long-term caveat and a soft-tissue-management dependency.

## Key Contributions

- First meta-analytic pooling (to Nov 2025) of the **additively manufactured** subperiosteal-implant generation, distinct from the discredited cast-frame subperiosteal implants of the mid-20th century.
- Frames CSI as a **graftless rehabilitation** path for severe atrophy (Cawood–Howell V–VI territory) with immediate fixed loading.
- Documents the **97.8% (≤3 yr) → 92.4% (overall) → 54.1% (6 yr)** survival trajectory.
- Names **soft tissue exposure/dehiscence** as the dominant complication and principal late-failure driver — the same soft-tissue-primacy lesson taught by the peri-implant dehiscence literature.

## Methodology

- **Design**: PRISMA-compliant SR + random-effects meta-analysis; PROSPERO-registered.
- **Sources**: PubMed/Medline, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar; searched to **November 2025**.
- **Eligible studies**: RCTs, cohort studies, and case series of customized CAD/CAM or 3D-printed titanium subperiosteal implants.
- **Outcomes**: implant survival (pooled with 95% CI) and complications.

## Results

- **Included**: 11 studies; **268 patients**; **369 implants**.
- **≤3-year pooled survival**: **97.8%**.
- **Overall pooled survival**: **92.4%**, substantial heterogeneity driven by long-term data.
- **6-year study (longest follow-up)**: survival declined to **54.1%**.
- **Complications**: soft tissue–related (implant **exposure, dehiscence**) most common; main cause of **late failure**.
- No per-jaw / per-material / point-estimate CI breakdowns are recoverable from the abstract.
