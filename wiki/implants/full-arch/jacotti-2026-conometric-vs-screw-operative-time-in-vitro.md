## Three-line Summary

In-vitro bench study comparing screw-retained and conometric (Orbit, Bionica) retention for All-on-4 and All-on-6 full-arch prostheses; activation phase titrated conometric coping torque from 10–30Ncm; time phase measured delivery and check-up durations across operators of varying experience.

Optimal conometric activation torque is 15Ncm (no coping deformation); screw-retained faster at delivery (All-on-4: 6min 4s vs 13min 56s; All-on-6: 8min 0s vs 18min 37s); conometric faster at check-up (All-on-4: 0min 30s vs 9min 2s; All-on-6: 0min 43s vs 12min 41s); conometric less operator-experience dependent.

Screw-retained preferred for provisional restorations (fast initial delivery); conometric preferred for permanent restorations (dramatically faster recall appointments over years); 15Ncm is the standardized activation protocol.

## 세줄요약

인비트로 비교, Orbit/Bionica 코노메트릭 vs 나사, All-on-4/6 전악; 코노메트릭 활성화 15Ncm(코핑 무변형).

나사 전달 빠름(4지: 6분 vs 14분); 코노메트릭 체크업 빠름(4지: 30초 vs 9분); 코노메트릭 술자 의존성 낮음.

임시보철=나사 선호; 최종보철=코노메트릭 선호.

## Study Design

- **Type**: In-vitro experimental bench study
- **System**: Orbit implant-prosthetic connection (Bionica) — dual-platform allowing both techniques
- **Phase 1 (activation)**: Conometric coping torqued from 10–30Ncm; pull-off force measured per torque value
- **Phase 2 (time)**: Delivery time and check-up time measured; multiple operators, different experience levels
- **Configurations**: All-on-4, All-on-6

## Key Results

### Activation: Optimal Conometric Torque

| Torque | Outcome |
|---|---|
| < 15 Ncm | Insufficient retention |
| **15 Ncm** | **No coping deformation — optimal** |
| > 15 Ncm | Coping deformation observed |

### Time Comparison

| Configuration | Phase | Screw-retained | Conometric | Faster system |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-on-4 | **Delivery** | **6 min 4 s** | 13 min 56 s | Screw |
| All-on-6 | **Delivery** | **8 min 0 s** | 18 min 37 s | Screw |
| All-on-4 | **Check-up** | 9 min 2 s | **0 min 30 s** | Conometric |
| All-on-6 | **Check-up** | 12 min 41 s | **0 min 43 s** | Conometric |

### Operator Dependence

Conometric showed less inter-operator variability — friction-fit activation more standardized than torque-dependent screw placement.

## Clinical Implications

The inverse efficiency profile creates a practical two-stage protocol recommendation:

**Provisional phase** → screw-retained:
- Faster delivery (~6–8 min vs 14–19 min) reduces chair time when adjustments/remakes are expected
- Easier intraoral screw access during early healing and occlusal refinement

**Permanent phase** → conometric:
- Check-up time ~0.5 min vs 9–13 min per arch
- With 6-monthly hygiene recalls over 5 years: ~10 appointments × ~10 min saved = **~100 min per arch saved**
- Less operator-dependent: consistent maintenance across different clinical staff

**Activation torque: 15Ncm** — same range as typical screw torque values, enabling standardized protocol on the Orbit dual-platform.

## Limitations

- In-vitro — no masticatory loading, saliva, soft tissue, or patient anatomy variables
- Single system (Orbit/Bionica) — generalizability to other conometric platforms unknown
- Abstract only — statistical analysis details, sample size, and inter-operator variability metrics unavailable
- Long-term conometric retention under cyclic loading not assessed
