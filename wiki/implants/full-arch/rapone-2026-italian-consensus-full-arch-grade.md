## Three-line Summary

Italian Consensus Conference (29 experts, modified Delphi, 3 rounds, ≥90% threshold, GRADE framework, literature 2015–2026) developed seven evidence-based statements on full-arch implant rehabilitation covering periodontal risk assessment, guided bone regeneration, mandibular implant number, tilted vs. axial configuration, prosthetic material, maintenance protocols, and systemic risk stratification.

Key quantitative benchmarks: 4 vs 6 mandibular implants — equivalent 5-year survival (98.6% vs 99.4%, p=0.136); tilted vs axial MBL MD=0.00 mm (p=0.75); monolithic zirconia prosthesis survival 97.23%; structured maintenance reduces peri-implantitis risk by ~75%; bruxism implant failure OR=2.189; non-compliant patients implant loss OR=14.59.

All seven statements achieved ≥90% consensus; five at High certainty, two (GBR, systemic risk) at Moderate; all graded Strong recommendation — aligns with EFP S3, ITI 6th Consensus, and AO/AAP 2025 Consensus.

## Summary

This Italian Consensus Conference, organized by the Associazione "DOD—Digital Operative Dentistry," convened 29 multidisciplinary experts (20 implant surgeons/prosthodontists, 4 academic researchers, 1 dental hygienist, 5 dental technicians) in Falcade, Italy (February 2026). Using a modified Delphi process (3 rounds, 100% participation, ≥90% agreement threshold) and GRADE-based evidence appraisal from literature published January 2015 – January 2026, the panel produced seven evidence-based consensus statements for full-arch implant rehabilitation.

## Statement 1: Periodontal Risk Assessment

**Evidence: High | Recommendation: Strong | Consensus: 96%**

Full validated risk assessment is mandatory before full-arch implant rehabilitation. In periodontally compromised patients, the risk of peri-implantitis is approximately 4-fold higher (RR = 4.09; 95% CI 1.93–8.58). Non-compliant patients exhibit markedly higher implant loss risk (OR = 14.59; 95% CI 1.30–164.29; Roccuzzo et al., 20-year follow-up).

**Stability criteria (partially edentulous):** All four simultaneously for ≥6 months post-active therapy:
- FMBS < 15%
- FMPS < 20%
- No PD ≥ 5 mm with BoP
- No radiographic progression of alveolar bone loss

**Biological readiness (fully edentulous):**
- Smoking cessation or ≤10 cigarettes/day for ≥3 months
- HbA1c < 7.5% in diabetic patients
- Structured anamnesis of tooth loss etiology (documented advanced periodontitis = progression-prone host response)
- Residual mucosal phenotype assessment (keratinized tissue width)

**Risk tools (combined use recommended):**
1. Periodontal Risk Assessment (PRA) model
2. 2017/2018 Staging and Grading framework (Grade C = progression-prone phenotype)
3. Implant Disease Risk Assessment (IDRA) — 8 weighted vectors; validated at 5-year follow-up; available at perio-tools.com/idra

## Statement 2: Guided Bone Regeneration (GBR)

**Evidence: Moderate | Recommendation: Strong | Consensus: 90%**

GBR achieves implant survival outcomes comparable to native bone, but is technique-sensitive with clinically relevant complication risk (membrane exposure, infection). GBR should be indicated **only** when prosthetically driven implant placement cannot be achieved in pristine bone, and only after alternative strategies (tilted implants, basal bone engagement, short implants) have been excluded.

**Simultaneous GBR** (one-stage) is indicated when:
- Insertion torque ≥ 35 Ncm or ISQ ≥ 70
- Peri-implant defect ≤ 2 mm without dehiscence beyond apical third

**Staged GBR** (two-stage, 6-month healing) is indicated when:
- Horizontal defects exceed 2 mm
- Peri-implant dehiscence would compromise primary stability
- Anatomically complex sites
- High-risk wound healing profiles (smoking, uncontrolled diabetes, advanced periodontitis history)

## Statement 3: Number of Implants — Mandible

**Evidence: High | Recommendation: Strong | Consensus: 94%**

Four implants are sufficient in standard mandibular full-arch cases. The largest controlled dataset (Caramês et al.: 943 patients, 5989 implants, mean follow-up 5.0 ± 3.2 years) shows:
- Four-implant 5-year cumulative survival: **98.6%**
- Six-implant 5-year cumulative survival: **99.4%**
- p = 0.136 (no statistically significant difference)

**Six implants preferred when:** documented bruxism, severe mandibular ridge atrophy, uncontrolled systemic comorbidities, full opposing fixed dentition.

**Minimum geometry for four-implant design (terminal implants):**
- Minimum length: 10 mm
- Minimum diameter: 4 mm
- When minimum cannot be achieved: six-implant configuration is biomechanically preferable (cumulative bone-to-implant contact (BIC) and A-P spread are primary determinants, not implant count per se)

## Statement 4: Tilted vs. Axial Implants

**Evidence: High | Recommendation: Strong | Consensus: 94%**

Tilted posterior implants (30–45°) are an acceptable alternative to axial implants with equivalent clinical outcomes:
- Short-term MBL difference: MD = 0.00 mm (95% CI −0.01 to 0.02; p = 0.75)
- Small MBL differences may emerge at longer follow-up in some datasets
- No significant difference in implant failure rates

Tilting is preferred when it enables avoidance of anatomical structures, reduces cantilever forces, improves anteroposterior (A-P) spread, or avoids GBR.

**Computer-aided implant surgery (CAIS):** considered integral for full-arch protocols. Static CAIS accuracy: 1.11 mm coronal, 1.40 mm apical, 3.51° angular (current meta-analysis).

**Implant-abutment connection:** Conical connections (Morse-taper/platform-switched) preferred — MBL at 2–5 years:
- External hex: 1.03 mm (95% CI 0.72–1.34)
- Flat-to-flat: 0.73 mm (95% CI 0.58–0.88)
- Narrow-cone (<45°): 0.45 mm (95% CI 0.34–0.56)

**Cantilever design limits:**
- Maximum: 1.5× A-P implant spread
- Absolute maximum: 15 mm (mandible), 10 mm (maxilla)
- Absolute contraindications to cantilever: definite bruxism with natural antagonist; prosthetic vertical dimension <10 mm with zirconia; A-P spread <5 mm

## Statement 5: Prosthetic Materials

**Evidence: High | Recommendation: Strong | Consensus: 91%**

Monolithic zirconia is the preferred material for definitive full-arch implant-supported prostheses:
- Prosthesis survival: **97.23%** over mean follow-up of 49.7 months
- Average complication rate: **2.25%**
- Veneered zirconia chipping: **15–54%**

**Bruxism risk:** Probable bruxism implant failure risk: OR = 2.189 (95% CI 1.337–3.583; p = 0.002) from meta-analysis of 27 studies, 12,369 implants. Risk persists over time (not limited to early loading phase).

**Protocol for definite bruxism:**
- Monolithic zirconia (preferred material)
- Avoid or strictly limit distal cantilevers
- Prefer six-implant configuration
- Conical implant-abutment connections
- Hard occlusal splint for nocturnal protection
- 3-month maintenance recall

Diagnostic grading: three-tier system (possible = self-report only; probable = positive clinical inspection; definite = instrumental confirmation — EMG or PSG). STAB tool recommended for structured assessment.

## Statement 6: Maintenance Protocols

**Evidence: High | Recommendation: Strong | Consensus: 98%**

Structured supportive maintenance is mandatory for long-term success. Meta-analytic evidence shows approximately **75% risk reduction** in peri-implantitis with structured protocols.

**Risk-stratified recall intervals (IDRA classification):**
- Low-risk: every 6 months
- Moderate-risk: every 4 months
- High-risk: every 3 months

**Each visit includes:** 6-site probing per implant, BoP, FMPS, professional biofilm removal with implant-safe instruments, home care reinforcement, occlusal screening.

**Radiographic follow-up:** Annually (low-risk); every 12–18 months (moderate-risk); every 6–12 months (high-risk).

**Intervention thresholds:**
- PD ≥ 5 mm + BoP → non-surgical debridement + antiseptic adjunct → 2-month re-evaluation
- Persistent BoP at re-evaluation or PD ≥ 6 mm → adjunctive antimicrobials + surgical referral
- Radiographic MBL ≥ 2 mm → surgical re-evaluation → reconstructive or resective therapy

Non-compliance is the single most significant patient-related risk factor. Non-compliant patients reclassified to high-risk regardless of other indicators. Non-compliant vs. compliant: OR = 14.59 for implant loss (95% CI 1.30–164.29; Roccuzzo et al., 20 years).

## Statement 7: Systemic Risk Stratification, Contraindications, and Perioperative Management

**Evidence: Moderate | Recommendation: Strong | Consensus: 94%**

Structured systemic risk stratification is mandatory before full-arch implant rehabilitation for all candidates.

**Absolute contraindications:**
- Active and untreated malignancy of the oral or maxillofacial region
- High-dose head and neck radiotherapy (cumulative dose >50–60 Gy)
- Ongoing high-dose BMA (HD-BMA) therapy for oncological indications (R+/R++) without feasible drug holiday
- Acute severe immunosuppression (ANC < 1,000/mm³, active chemotherapy, solid organ transplantation within 6 months)
- Uncontrolled bleeding disorders
- Severe uncontrolled mental health conditions precluding informed consent

**Relative contraindications (multidisciplinary evaluation required):**
- Uncontrolled diabetes mellitus (HbA1c ≥ 7.5%)
- Heavy smoking (>10 cigarettes/day)
- Prior lower-dose HN radiotherapy (>12 months post-completion)
- Long-term low-dose BMA (LD-BMA) for osteoporosis
- Antiangiogenic therapy without concurrent BMA
- Active autoimmune disease under immunosuppressive treatment
- Chronic corticosteroids (prednisone-equivalent ≥10 mg/day for ≥3 months)
- Sjögren syndrome or severe xerostomia
- Advanced age alone is **NOT** a contraindication

**MRONJ risk (SIPMO-SICMF stratification, aligned with AAOMS 2022):**
- HD-BMA R0 (therapy planned, not started): complete all dental procedures before BMA initiation
- HD-BMA R+ and R++ (therapy ongoing): elective full-arch implant placement generally **contraindicated**
- LD-BMA Rx (osteoporosis): feasible with multidisciplinary consultation + elimination of local infectious risk factors + informed consent documenting non-quantifiable MRONJ risk + dedicated maintenance enrollment

**Antibiotic prophylaxis (IAO 2021 framework):**
- Pre-op: chlorhexidine 0.20% mouthwash 60 s within 2 hours of surgery
- Single pre-op dose: amoxicillin 2–3 g orally 1 hour before surgery (or clindamycin 600 mg in documented penicillin allergy)
- Post-op extension (up to 6 days): only when simultaneous GBR, sinus floor elevation, or extensive grafting
- Post-op: chlorhexidine 0.20% twice daily for 14 days in all cases
- Antibiotic stewardship: routine courses >6 days not supported by evidence

## Key Contributions

1. First Italian GRADE-graded Delphi consensus covering all major full-arch decision points in a single integrated framework.
2. Largest mandibular 4 vs 6 implant controlled comparison (n=943 patients, equivalent at 5 years).
3. Operationalized GBR indications with quantitative thresholds (ISQ/torque, defect size).
4. Explicit cantilever limits derived from biomechanical analyses.
5. Bruxism as quantified risk factor (OR=2.189) with structured diagnostic and management protocol.
6. MRONJ stratification operationalized for Italian clinical setting, consistent with AAOMS 2022.
7. Aligned with EFP S3 guideline, 2017 World Workshop, ITI 6th Consensus Conference, AO/AAP 2025 Consensus.
