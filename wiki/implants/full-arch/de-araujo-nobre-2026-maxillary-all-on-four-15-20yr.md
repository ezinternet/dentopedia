## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study (n = 740 patients, 2960 implants, single center) of immediate full-arch maxillary All-on-4 rehabilitation (two anterior axial + two posterior tilted implants) with up to 20 years of follow-up — the longest available dataset for this maxillary concept.

Implant cumulative survival rate (CSurR) 90.7%, success rate (CSucR) 84.6%, and prosthetic success 98.1% at 20 years; MBL 1.07±1.38 mm at 15yr and 1.46±1.56 mm at 20yr; mechanical complications in 78.5% of patients; biological complications in 35.1% of patients / 15.2% of implants; smoking (HR=1.93) and biological complications (HR=2.18) identified as risk indicators for implant failure.

38.8% loss to follow-up introduces wide uncertainty (best-case 93.1% vs worst-case 59.4%); results warrant cautious interpretation but support viability of the concept in the long term with structured maintenance.

## Summary

This study reports the longest published follow-up (up to 20 years) for immediate full-arch implant-supported maxillary rehabilitations using the All-on-4 concept. Conducted at Malo Clinic (Lisbon, Portugal) with patient treatment between November 2002 and December 2008, it enrolled 740 patients (440 women, 300 men; mean age 55.3 years) rehabilitated with 740 prostheses supported by 2960 implants (predominantly NobelSpeedy Groovy, n=2851). The study follows STROBE guidelines and used life tables, Cox proportional hazards models, and binary logistic regression for analysis.

Key findings: prosthetic success 98.1%; implant CSurR 94.1% at 15yr and 90.7% at 20yr; implant CSucR 89.4% at 15yr and 84.6% at 20yr. MBL showed a stable trajectory: 1.07±1.38 mm at 15yr and 1.46±1.56 mm at 20yr, extending the same team's earlier publications (0.9–1.2 mm at 1yr; 1.18 mm at 5yr; 1.67 mm at 10yr). Mechanical complications occurred in 78.5% of patients over the full follow-up period; biological complications in 35.1% of patients (15.2% of implants).

Multivariate analysis identified smoking and biological complications as risk indicators across three simultaneous models (implant failure, MBL > 3 mm, and biological complications). A notable finding was that mechanical complications conferred a protective effect on MBL (OR = 0.26), likely because patients experiencing mechanical complications attended more maintenance appointments (termed by the authors a "pseudo-maintenance protocol").

## Key Contributions

1. **Longest maxillary All-on-4 follow-up**: Up to 20 years — extends the same group's 13-year data (94.7% implant survival, 99.2% prosthetic success) by seven additional years.
2. **Dual risk indicator confirmation**: Smoking and biological complications identified simultaneously in three multivariate models — implant failure (Cox HR), MBL > 3mm (logistic OR), and biological complications incidence — providing the strongest quantitative case for smoking cessation counseling pre-treatment.
3. **MBL stability**: Despite >15 years of function, mean MBL remains <1.5 mm, consistent with the peri-implant bone equilibrium concept.
4. **Mechanical complication paradox**: 78.5% cumulative incidence is high, but these complications are manageable and may inadvertently enhance peri-implant maintenance frequency.
5. **Maxilla companion to mandibular paper**: Together with [[de-araujo-nobre-2025-mandibular-all-on-four-20-25yr]] (mandible, 20–25yr), provides the most complete very-long-term bilateral dataset for the All-on-4 concept.

## Methodology

- **Study type**: Retrospective cohort (single center, STROBE-compliant)
- **Sample**: 740 patients, 2960 implants; 222 smokers (30%), 197 with bruxism (26.6%), 286 with systemic conditions
- **Surgical technique**: 2 anterior axial implants + 2 posterior implants tilted 30–45° along anterior wall of maxillary sinus; insertion torque 30–50 Ncm; 30° multi-unit abutments (posterior), 0° or 17° (anterior)
- **Prosthetic protocol**: Provisional acrylic on day of surgery; definitive prosthesis ≥ 6 months post-op
- **Maintenance**: Every 6 months with prosthesis removal + prophylaxis
- **MBL assessment**: Periapical radiographs (parallel technique), calibrated blinded assessor, iRYS v13.0 software
- **Statistics**: Life tables; Cox proportional hazards (implant failure); binary logistic regression (MBL > 3mm, mechanical and biological complications); sensitivity analysis (best/worst case for 38.8% lost to follow-up)

## Results

### Survival and Success

| Endpoint | 15 years | 20 years |
|---|---|---|
| Implant CSurR | 94.1% | 90.7% |
| Implant CSucR | 89.4% | 84.6% |
| Prosthetic success | — | 98.1% |
| Sensitivity (best case) | — | 93.1% |
| Sensitivity (worst case) | — | 59.4% |

170 implants (5.7%) failed in 101 patients (13.6%): 140 in first 180 months, 30 in months 181–240. 50 failures in smokers, 31 in bruxers, 12 in both.

### Marginal Bone Loss

| Timepoint | MBL mean ± SD |
|---|---|
| 15 years | 1.07 ± 1.38 mm |
| 20 years | 1.46 ± 1.56 mm |

Historical trajectory from same cohort: 0.9–1.2 mm (1yr) → 1.18 mm (5yr) → 1.67 mm (10yr) → 1.07 mm (15yr) → 1.46 mm (20yr). Note the 10yr value (1.67 mm) is higher than 15yr (1.07 mm), likely reflecting different radiograph sample composition (74% readable at 15yr vs 93% at 20yr).
