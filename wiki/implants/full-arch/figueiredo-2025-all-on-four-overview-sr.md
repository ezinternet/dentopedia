## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (overview of 8 SRs, PROSPERO CRD42023471446) of All-on-Four versus multi-implant full-arch rehabilitation; 4,468 records screened across 10 databases; AMSTAR-2 and ROBIS used for quality and bias assessment.

All-on-Four CSR ranged 94.8%–99.3%; mean MBL ranged 1.1–1.5 mm; none of the 8 included SRs reported statistically significant inferiority of All-on-Four versus approaches using more implants.

Evidence quality is limited: most included SRs rated low methodological quality (AMSTAR-2) and high risk of bias (ROBIS), restricting the strength of the conclusion.

## Summary

This PROSPERO-registered umbrella review (CRD42023471446) synthesizes evidence from 8 systematic reviews (published 2013–2021) comparing the All-on-Four treatment concept against other implant-supported full-arch rehabilitation approaches. After screening 4,468 records across 10 databases plus grey literature, 8 SRs met inclusion criteria. Two independent reviewers conducted all stages. Methodological quality was appraised with AMSTAR-2; risk of bias was evaluated with ROBIS. A narrative synthesis was performed (no pooled meta-analysis).

Across all included SRs, the All-on-Four technique demonstrated cumulative survival rates (CSR) ranging from 94.8% to 99.3%, and mean marginal bone loss (MBL) from 1.1 mm to 1.5 mm. No included SR identified statistically significant differences indicating All-on-Four is inferior to approaches employing more implants. However, the majority of included SRs were rated as low methodological quality on AMSTAR-2 and as having high risk of bias on ROBIS, meaning the underlying evidence base is methodologically weak. The authors conclude that All-on-Four demonstrates comparable clinical outcomes to conventional full-arch rehabilitation, but that this conclusion is constrained by the quality of the supporting evidence.

## Key Contributions

- Represents the highest evidence tier (umbrella review) for the All-on-Four vs. multi-implant full-arch comparison currently in the wiki.
- Synthesizes 8 SRs spanning 2013–2021 with formal dual quality appraisal (AMSTAR-2 + ROBIS), a combination not present in any single included SR.
- Establishes CSR 94.8–99.3% and MBL 1.1–1.5 mm as the benchmark ranges from systematic evidence for All-on-Four.
- Makes explicit the evidence ceiling: even at the umbrella review level, the underlying SR pool is methodologically weak — this limits how much confidence can be placed in the comparability conclusion.

## Methodology

- **Study type**: Umbrella review (overview of systematic reviews)
- **Registration**: PROSPERO CRD42023471446 (prospective)
- **Databases**: 10 databases + grey literature
- **Reviewers**: 2 independent reviewers, all stages
- **Outcomes**: Implant number, loading protocol, marginal bone loss (MBL), cumulative survival rate (CSR)
- **Quality appraisal**: AMSTAR-2 (methodological quality), ROBIS (risk of bias)
- **Synthesis**: Narrative (heterogeneity precluded pooled meta-analysis)
- **Included SRs**: 8, published between 2013 and 2021
- **Abstract-only note**: Full text not retrieved; all data sourced from abstract.

## Results

| Outcome | All-on-Four |
|---|---|
| CSR range | 94.8% – 99.3% |
| Mean MBL range | 1.1 mm – 1.5 mm |
| SRs reporting All-on-4 inferior | 0 / 8 |
| Included SRs with high RoB (ROBIS) | Majority |
| Included SRs with low quality (AMSTAR-2) | Majority |

Key finding: across all 8 included SRs, no statistically significant evidence emerged that the All-on-Four protocol underperforms approaches using a greater number of implants (e.g., All-on-Six or conventional multi-implant protocols).
