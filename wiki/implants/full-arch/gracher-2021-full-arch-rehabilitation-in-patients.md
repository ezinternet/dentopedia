## Three-line Summary

- **Study type & context:** PRISMA systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42020144836) of 42 clinical studies (2003–2019) encompassing 1247 patients and 2919 zygomatic implants for full-arch rehabilitation of atrophic maxillae.
- **Key results:** Overall ZI survival 98.22% (52/2919 failures) over 1–228 months follow-up. Intrasinusal most used (23 studies, 98.3% survival); extrasinusal 99.1% survival (11 studies, 464 ZIs); sinus slot 97.7% (4 studies). Sinusitis most common complication (39 cases).
- **Clinical implication:** ZIs provide high survival for atrophic maxillary rehabilitation regardless of technique; technique choice should follow surgeon experience and anatomy. Evidence limited by heterogeneity, no RCTs, and industry funding (Neodent).

## Summary

This PRISMA-compliant systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42020144836) synthesized clinical evidence on zygomatic implants (ZIs) for full-arch rehabilitation of atrophic maxillae. PubMed search (December 2019) with terms "zygom* AND dental (Implant OR implants) AND edentulous NOT (biomechanic* OR finite element) NOT cadaver" yielded 42 eligible studies (20 prospective, 19 retrospective, 2 case series, 1 longitudinal cohort) published March 2003–April 2019. No RCTs were identified. Data extracted: study type, follow-up, patient/implant counts, surgical technique, failures, complications (paresthesia, sinusitis, infection, fistula). Oxford CEBM levels of evidence assigned.

**Aggregate results:** 1247 patients, 2919 ZIs placed, 52 failures → **98.22% survival** (follow-up 1–228 months). Surgical technique distribution: intrasinusal (classic Brånemark) — 23 studies, 945 ZIs, 16 failures (98.3%); extrasinusal — 11 studies, 464 ZIs, 4 failures (99.1%); combined intrasinusal+extrasinusal — 3 studies, 276 ZIs, 9 failures (96.7%); sinus slot — 4 studies (2 standalone), 44 ZIs, 1 failure (97.7%); modified sinus slot — 1 study, 43 ZIs, 4 failures; 9 studies did not report technique.

**Complications (Table 2):** Sinusitis 39 cases (most common); local infection 16; paresthesia 9; fistula at implant level 7. Several studies reported zero complications (Mozzati, Aparicio 2010, etc.).

**Limitations acknowledged:** High heterogeneity (designs, follow-up, techniques, outcome measures) precluded meta-analysis; no RCTs; industry funding (Neodent); variable success criteria across studies.

## Key Contributions

1. **Largest aggregate ZI dataset to date** — 2919 implants across 42 studies provides robust survival estimate (98.22%).
2. **Technique-specific survival breakdown** — Quantifies survival by surgical approach (intrasinusal, extrasinusal, sinus slot, combinations), guiding technique selection.
3. **Complication profile** — Sinusitis identified as predominant complication (39/52 adverse events reported), informing preoperative counseling.
4. **Historical scope** — Captures 16-year evolution (2003–2019) of ZI protocols including immediate loading, all-on-4 double zygoma, hybrid ZI+CI concepts.
5. **PROSPERO registration** — Protocol registered a priori (CRD42020144836), reducing selection bias.

## Clinical Takeaway

**Zygomatic implants achieve 98.2% aggregate survival across 2,919 implants in 1,247 atrophic maxillary patients** (follow-up 1–228 months), making them a highly predictable graftless solution for full-arch rehabilitation. **Survival is consistently high across techniques**: intrasinusal 98.3%, extrasinusal 99.1%, sinus slot 97.7%. The **extrasinusal approach shows the highest survival (99.1%)** and offers prosthetic advantages (improved emergence profile, reduced sinus complications), but requires specific implant designs and surgical expertise. **Sinusitis is the dominant complication (39/52 reported adverse events)** — patients should be counseled on this risk. Technique selection should be guided by **surgeon experience, local anatomy, and prosthetic planning** rather than survival differences alone. The evidence base remains limited by **absence of RCTs, high heterogeneity, and industry sponsorship** — future well-designed comparative trials are needed.

## Limitations

1. **No RCTs** — Highest evidence level is prospective cohort (CEBM 1b); causal inferences limited
2. **High heterogeneity** — Designs, follow-up (1–228 months), techniques, outcome definitions varied; meta-analysis impossible
3. **Industry funding** — Neodent (manufacturer) funded; authors affiliated with Neodent/ILAPEO
4. **Variable success criteria** — Implant survival definitions differed across studies
5. **Search limited to PubMed** — May miss studies in other databases (Embase, Cochrane, Scopus)
6. **Search cutoff April 2019** — Does not include 2019–2026 literature (e.g., Fernandez-Ruiz 2021 RCT)
7. **Complication reporting inconsistent** — Many studies reported "NR" for specific complications
8. **Patient-centered outcomes scarce** — Few studies reported QoL, esthetics, phonetics, masticatory function
9. **Bone loss data not systematically extracted** — Marginal bone resorption around ZIs not quantified in this SR
