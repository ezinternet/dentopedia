## Three-line Summary

Retrospective comparison of 203 immediately loaded complete-arch patients with 1,356 implants and 261 fixed prostheses (65 conometric retention, 196 screw-retained); mean follow-up 28.5mo (12–80mo); implant survival, prosthesis success, and complication comparison by chi-square.

27 implant failures (2%); global prosthesis success 89.3%; no significant differences in implant survival or prosthesis success between systems; screw loosening 4.2% (most frequent complication, screw-retained dominant); veneering chipping/fracture second; conometric detachment 9.2% (significantly higher than screw-retained).

Both systems achieve equivalent survival and success; conometric shows significantly higher prosthesis detachment (9.2%) while screw-retained has more frequent but clinically manageable screw loosening (4.2%); complication type rather than rate drives system choice.

## Study Design

- **Type**: Retrospective comparative cohort
- **N patients**: 203
- **N implants**: 1,356
- **N prostheses**: 261 (conometric 65, screw-retained 196)
- **Follow-up**: Mean 28.5mo, range 12–80mo
- **Loading protocol**: Immediate loading for all
- **Analysis**: Chi-square test for between-group differences

| Complication | Rate | Group |

## Clinical Decision Framework — Retention System

| Factor | Conometric | Screw-Retained |
|---|---|---|
| Implant survival | Equivalent | Equivalent |
| Prosthesis success | Equivalent | Equivalent |
| Risk of detachment | ⚠ **9.2%** | Lower |
| Screw loosening | None | ⚠ **4.2%** |
| Hygiene maintenance speed | Faster (snap-off/on) | Slower (screw access) |
| Patient sensation of detachment | Potential distress | N/A |
| Repair simplicity | Snap back or recalibrate | Retighten screw |
