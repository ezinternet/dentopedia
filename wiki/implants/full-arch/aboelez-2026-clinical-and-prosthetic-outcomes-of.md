## Three-line Summary

RCT (n=30) comparing PEEK composite (G1, n=15) vs titanium-zirconia (G2, n=15) maxillary All-on-Six fixed prostheses opposing mandibular distal extension removable partial dentures; 12-month follow-up with clinical indices at T0/T6/T12 and VAS satisfaction/prosthetic complications at 1 year.

PEEK showed significantly better peri-implant health: VBL at 12 months 0.42±0.18 mm vs 0.89±0.31 mm (p<0.001), probing depth 2.1±0.3 mm vs 3.4±0.5 mm (p<0.001), plaque score 0.8±0.4 vs 1.6±0.5 (p<0.001); Ti-Zr had higher prosthetic complications — veneer fracture/separation 26.7% vs 0%, screw loosening 20% vs 6.7%; but Ti-Zr scored higher on esthetics/natural-teeth resemblance VAS.

PEEK composite frameworks offer superior biological and hygiene outcomes in maxillary All-on-Six opposed by mandibular distal-extension RPDs; titanium-zirconia provides better esthetic satisfaction but carries higher veneer fracture and screw loosening risk; 1-year implant/prosthesis survival 100% in both groups.

## Summary

This randomized controlled trial directly compared two framework materials for maxillary implant-supported fixed complete-arch prostheses (All-on-Six concept) in patients with an edentulous maxilla opposing mandibular distal extension removable partial dentures (RPDs). Thirty patients were randomized to receive either PEEK composite frameworks (Group 1, n=15) or titanium-zirconia frameworks (Group 2, n=15). Six implants were placed in the maxillary positions 12, 14, 16, 22, 24, 26 (lengths 10–14 mm, diameters 3.6–4.5 mm). Clinical parameters — plaque index, gingival index, probing depth, implant mobility, and vertical bone loss (VBL) — were recorded at prosthesis delivery (T0), 6 months (T6), and 12 months (T12). Patient satisfaction (visual analog scale, VAS) regarding appearance, natural teeth resemblance, cleaning ease, and chewing comfort, along with prosthetic complications, were assessed at 12 months.

## Key Contributions

- **First RCT** directly comparing PEEK composite vs titanium-zirconia frameworks for maxillary All-on-Six fixed prostheses in a clinically relevant scenario (opposing mandibular distal-extension RPDs).
- **Quantified peri-implant health advantage of PEEK**: significantly lower plaque accumulation, probing depths, and vertical bone loss at 6 and 12 months.
- **Documented prosthetic complication profile**: titanium-zirconia had substantially higher veneer fracture/separation (26.7% vs 0%) and screw loosening (20% vs 6.7%) rates at 1 year.
- **Patient-centered outcomes**: PEEK favored for hygiene/cleaning and chewing ease; titanium-zirconia favored for esthetics/natural appearance — a clinically actionable trade-off.
- **100% implant and prosthesis survival** at 12 months in both groups, confirming both materials are viable for All-on-Six rehabilitation.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Parallel-group, randomized controlled clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06111391).
- **Setting**: Single center (Mansoura University, Egypt / Applied Science Private University, Jordan).
- **Participants**: 30 edentulous maxillary patients with mandibular Kennedy Class I/II distal extension ridges; inclusion: adequate bone volume for 6 implants without grafting; exclusion: systemic contraindications, bruxism, untreated periodontitis, smoking >10 cigarettes/day.
- **Randomization**: Computer-generated block randomization, allocation concealment via sealed opaque envelopes.
- **Intervention**: 
  - **G1 (n=15)**: PEEK composite framework (BioHPP/PEEK-OPTIMA) with composite veneering (VITA VM LC).
  - **G2 (n=15)**: Titanium-zirconia framework (Ti base + zirconia veneering) with layered ceramic (VITA VM 9).
  - Both: screw-retained, full-arch maxillary prostheses on 6 implants (12, 14, 16, 22, 24, 26).
  - Mandibular distal-extension RPDs fabricated conventionally for all patients.
- **Follow-up**: T0 (delivery), T6, T12 months.
- **Outcomes**:
  - **Primary**: Vertical bone loss (VBL) measured on standardized periapical radiographs (mesial/distal implant sites).
  - **Secondary**: Plaque index (Silness & Löe), gingival index (Löe & Silness), probing depth (6 sites/implant), implant mobility (Periotest), patient satisfaction (VAS 0–100 mm for appearance, natural resemblance, cleaning ease, chewing comfort), prosthetic complications (veneer fracture/separation, screw loosening, framework fracture, loss of retention).
- **Statistical analysis**: Shapiro-Wilk normality test; independent t-test or Mann-Whitney U for intergroup comparisons; paired t-test/Wilcoxon for intragroup changes; χ²/Fisher's exact for complication rates; α=0.05.
