## Three-line Summary

In-vitro bench study, 45 specimens (3 groups n=15: 2mm, 4mm, 6mm zirconia height over Ti framework) for cantilever full-arch prostheses; cyclic loading 1.2M cycles at 200N followed by static loading to fracture; fracture resistance (N) and failure mode recorded.

Fracture resistance 849N (2mm), 862N (4mm), 871N (6mm) — no significant differences (P>.05); prosthetic screw fracture was the dominant failure mode (86.7%), followed by zirconia fracture (13.3%); zero titanium framework fractures.

Zirconia height 2–6mm has no structural significance over Ti framework in cantilever prostheses; the predominant failure is prosthetic screw fracture, redirecting design attention from veneering thickness to screw engineering.

## Study Design

- **Type**: In-vitro bench study
- **N specimens**: 45 (3 groups × 15)
- **Groups**: 2mm, 4mm, 6mm zirconia height over Ti framework
- **Loading**: Cyclic 1.2M cycles at 200N → static loading to fracture
- **Outcomes**: Fracture resistance (N), failure mode

## Clinical Design Implications

**Null finding = space freedom:** Zirconia height 2mm is structurally equivalent to 6mm over Ti framework. In space-constrained situations (e.g., V-shaped mandible, reduced inter-arch distance), clinicians can use minimum 2mm zirconia without structural penalty, allocating saved vertical space to:
- Improved hygiene access (thinner profile)
- Additional Ti framework cross-section for fatigue resistance
- Reduced occlusal profile to lower cantilever moment

**Failure mode shift:** 86.7% screw fracture dominance means the structural bottleneck is the fixation hardware. Design priorities should focus on:
- Higher fatigue-resistance screw alloys
- Optimal torque protocols (neither under- nor over-torque)
- Screw access geometry that maintains integrity under cyclic load

**Ti framework resilience:** Zero Ti framework fractures across all 45 specimens — current Ti framework engineering for full-arch cantilever prostheses is robust under this loading protocol.

## Limitations

- In-vitro — no biological environment (saliva, temperature cycling, osseointegration compliance)
- Single Ti framework design — different geometries may produce different failure hierarchies
- 200N cyclic load may underestimate parafunctional loading (bruxism >700N)
- Abstract only — screw specifications, cantilever length, and inter-specimen variability details unavailable
