## Three-line Summary

1. **Study Type & Context**: Systematic review of 9 clinical studies (885 narrow-diameter implants, 2.5–3.5 mm) in completely edentulous patients with full-arch removable restorations; follow-up ≥6 months, search until March 2019.
2. **Key Numerical Results**: 5-year estimated implant survival 92.25% (95% CI 90.87–100%); 5-year mean marginal bone loss 1.40 mm (95% CI 0.55–2.03 mm). All studies reported removable overdentures; zero fixed full-arch data.
3. **Clinical Implication & Limitation**: NDIs are a viable option for mandibular overdentures in edentulous patients, but evidence for fixed full-arch or maxillary use is absent. High heterogeneity in implant systems/diameters and exclusive focus on removable prostheses limit generalizability to all-on-x fixed protocols.

## 세줄요약

1. **연구 유형·맥락**: 완전 무치악 환자에서 좁은 직경 임플란트(2.5–3.5 mm)로 전악 가철성 보철을 지지한 9개 연구(임플란트 885개) 대상 체계적 문헌고찰, 2019년 3월까지 검색.
2. **핵심 수치**: 5년 누적 임플란트 생존율 92.25%(95% CI 90.87–100%), 5년 평균 변연골소실 1.40 mm(95% CI 0.55–2.03 mm). 전 연구가 오버덴처만 다루며 고정성 전악 데이터는 없음.
3. **임상적 함의·한계**: 하악 오버덴처에는 NDI가 대안이 될 수 있으나, 고정성 전악(all-on-x)이나 상악 적용 근거는 부재. 이질성이 크고 가철성만 다뤄 all-on-x 고정 프로토콜로의 일반화는 제한적.

## Summary

This systematic review by Storelli et al. (2021) evaluates the clinical performance of narrow-diameter implants (NDIs, 2.5–3.5 mm) for full-arch rehabilitations in completely edentulous patients. Nine studies comprising 885 implants met inclusion criteria (≥10 patients, ≥6 months follow-up). All studies reported on removable overdentures; none assessed fixed full-arch prostheses. Eight studies were mandibular-only; one included both jaws.

Using random-effects Poisson regression and life-table analysis, the authors estimated a **5-year implant survival rate of 92.25%** (weighted mean 96.75%, median 98.35%, 95% CI 90.87–100%) and a **5-year marginal bone loss of 1.40 mm** (weighted mean 1.40 mm, median 1.24 mm, 95% CI 0.55–2.03 mm). Prosthesis survival was 100% in the three studies reporting it.

Included studies were heterogeneous: 5 prospective RCTs, 2 prospective cohorts, 1 retrospective cohort, 1 case series. Implant diameters ranged 2.5–3.5 mm (mostly 3.0–3.3 mm). Systems included Straumann, IMZ, Ankylos, BioHorizons. Risk of bias varied across studies.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic review specifically on NDIs for **full-arch** restorations in edentulous patients
- Quantified 5-year survival (92.25%) and MBL (1.40 mm) via meta-analytic modeling
- Highlighted critical evidence gap: **zero data on fixed full-arch prostheses** supported by NDIs
- Identified mandibular overdenture as the primary evidence-backed indication for NDIs in edentulism

## Results

### Implant Survival (5-year estimates)
- **Pooled estimate: 92.25%** (95% CI 90.87–100%)
- Weighted mean: 96.75%; Median: 98.35%
- Life-table at 120 months: 95.85%
- Range across studies: 81.7% – 100%

### Marginal Bone Loss (5-year estimates)
- **Pooled mean MBL: 1.40 mm** (95% CI 0.55–2.03 mm)
- Weighted mean: 1.40 mm; Median: 1.24 mm
- Range: 0 mm – 2.82 mm

## Clinical Takeaway

> **NDIs (2.5–3.5 mm) show 92% 5-year survival and 1.4 mm MBL for mandibular overdentures in edentulous patients.** This supports their use as a cost-effective, minimally invasive alternative for removable full-arch prostheses. **However, no evidence exists for fixed all-on-x type rehabilitations with NDIs.** Clinicians should not extrapolate these findings to fixed full-arch protocols without further research.

## Research Gaps

1. **Fixed full-arch prostheses on NDIs** — zero studies
2. **Maxillary applications** — only 1 study included maxilla
3. **Long-term (>10 yr) data** — limited
4. **Standardized NDI diameter/design** — high heterogeneity
5. **Patient-reported outcomes** — rarely measured
