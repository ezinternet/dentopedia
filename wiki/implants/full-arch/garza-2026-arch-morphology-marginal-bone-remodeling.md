## Three-line Summary

Exploratory prospective cohort of 40 edentulous patients (49 monolithic zirconia full-arch prostheses: 25 maxillary, 24 mandibular; 308 implants); early MBR measured at baseline and 12mo; mandibular morphology (CBCT U/V classification), inter-implant distance, cantilever length assessed; linear mixed-effects model primary analysis.

Implant/prosthesis survival 99.4%/97.9%; mandibular MBR 0.86mm vs maxillary 0.73mm (β=0.13mm, 95%CI 0.10–0.16, p<0.001); V-shaped mandible MBR 0.91 vs U-shaped 0.81mm (diff 0.10mm, p<0.001); inter-implant distance negatively correlated with mandibular MBR (r=−0.70, p<0.001) but not maxillary (r=0.01); 2 minor chipping events (both V-shaped mandibles).

Mandibular arch and V-shaped morphology are independently associated with higher early MBR under rigid monolithic zirconia; statistically robust but numerically modest differences; wider inter-implant spacing reduces mandibular bone stress; exploratory findings warrant confirmatory studies.

## Study Design

- **Type**: Exploratory prospective cohort
- **N patients**: 40 edentulous
- **N prostheses**: 49 (25 maxillary, 24 mandibular; 9 patients contributed both)
- **N implants**: 308
- **Follow-up**: 12 months
- **Morphology**: CBCT U- vs V-shaped mandible classification
- **CAD measurements**: Inter-implant distance, cantilever length
- **Primary analysis**: Linear mixed-effects model (accounts for within-patient correlation)
- **Secondary (exploratory)**: ANOVA, Pearson correlation

## Clinical Implications

V-shaped mandibles and mandibular placement in general show higher early MBR. The negative correlation between inter-implant distance and MBR (r=−0.70 mandibular) provides a CAD design lever: increasing inter-implant spacing may reduce bone remodeling stress in the mandible.

Both minor chipping events occurred in V-shaped mandibles, suggesting this morphology may concentrate stress in both prosthetic and peri-implant bone — a dual risk signal.

Absolute MBR values are small (0.73–0.91mm at 1yr) and all within acceptable clinical ranges; whether the arch/morphology differences compound over longer follow-up is unknown.
