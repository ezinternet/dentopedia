## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort of 217 patients (1222 implants, 271 full-arch fixed prostheses; Peking University) comparing All-on-4 (202 prostheses) vs All-on-6 (69 prostheses) immediate loading protocols over 3–13 years, with an accompanying clinician questionnaire.

No significant overall differences were found in implant survival, prosthesis survival, biological or technical complications, or MBL at 1, 5, or 10 years (HR=1.0 for implant survival); however, for specific high-risk subgroups (elderly, opposing natural/fixed dentition, smoking, bruxism, long cantilever, low bone density, all-acrylic provisionals), All-on-6 was more predictable in some measurements.

Medium-experienced clinicians and prosthodontists significantly preferred All-on-6 (P<0.05), highlighting that the implant-number decision is shaped by both evidence-based risk stratification and clinical experience — supporting a conditional rather than uniform protocol preference.

## 세줄요약

후향적 코호트(217명, 1222임플란트, Peking University) — All-on-4(202개) vs All-on-6(69개) 즉시 부하 3–13년 비교; 임상가 설문조사 포함.

임플란트 생존율(HR=1.0), 보철 생존율, 합병증, MBL @1/5/10yr 전반 유의차 없음; 단 고위험군(고령·반대측 자연치/고정 보철·흡연·교갈증·긴 캔틸레버·저골밀도·전악 아크릴 임시)에서 All-on-6이 일부 지표 우위.

중급 경험 임상가·보철과 전문의 All-on-6 유의하게 선호(P<0.05) — 근거 기반 위험 분층과 임상 경험 모두가 식립 개수 결정에 영향.

## Head-to-Head Outcomes (All-on-4 vs All-on-6)

| Outcome | All-on-4 vs All-on-6 | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Implant survival (implant level) | HR = 1.0 (95%CI 0.8–1.2) | NS (p=0.96) |
| Implant survival (prosthesis level) | HR = 0.8 (95%CI 0.3–1.8) | NS (p=0.54) |
| Prosthesis survival | OR = 0.8 (95%CI 0.3–2.8) | NS (p=0.56) |
| Biological complications | OR = 0.9 (95%CI 0.5–1.8) | NS (p=0.78) |
| Technical complications (provisional) | OR = 1.3 (95%CI 0.7–2.3) | NS (p=0.42) |
| Technical complications (definitive) | OR = 1.1 (95%CI 0.6–2.2) | NS (p=0.33) |
| MBL @1yr | — | NS (p=0.65) |
| MBL @5yr | — | NS (p=0.28) |
| MBL @10yr | — | NS (p=0.14) |

**Overall verdict: No significant difference.**

## High-Risk Subgroups: When All-on-6 Is More Predictable

The following patient/prosthesis characteristics were associated with better All-on-6 outcomes in specific clinical measurements (not all outcomes):

| Risk Factor | Clinical Rationale |
|---|---|
| Elderly patients | Greater bone loss, reduced healing capacity |
| Opposing natural or fixed dentition | Higher masticatory load |
| Smoking | Impaired osseointegration and healing |
| Bruxism | Excessive occlusal forces |
| Long cantilever length | Biomechanical stress concentration |
| Low bone density | Reduced primary stability in distal implants |
| All-acrylic provisional prosthesis | Less rigidity, more micromovement during healing |

**Clinical decision rule**: consider upgrading to All-on-6 when ≥1 high-risk factor is present, especially combinations.

## Clinician Preference Survey

| Clinician Group | Implant Number Preference |
|---|---|
| Medium-experienced clinicians | All-on-6 significantly preferred (P<0.05) |
| Implant prosthodontists | All-on-6 significantly preferred (P<0.05) |
| Experienced implant surgeons | No significant preference |

Interpretation: clinicians with specialized prosthetic training or moderate (rather than extensive) surgical experience show risk-averse preference for All-on-6 — suggesting the evidence gap (no overall difference) leaves room for clinician expertise and risk stratification to guide the decision.
