## Three-line Summary

- **Study type & context:** Single-blind RCT (15 patients, 144 implants) comparing immediate post-extraction vs 6-month delayed implant placement for immediately loaded full-arch fixed prostheses in edentulous mandibles.
- **Key results:** Marginal bone loss at 12 months: immediate 0.54 ± 0.39 mm vs delayed 0.66 ± 0.25 mm (p=0.201); 100% implant survival in both groups; no significant differences in any peri-implant health parameters (PI, GI, PD, keratinized mucosa, crevicular fluid, mucositis) at 12 months.
- **Clinical implication:** Immediate placement yields equivalent 1-year bone preservation and implant survival to delayed placement for full-arch mandibular rehabilitation, supporting reduced treatment time; limited by small sample and short follow-up.

## 세줄요약

- **연구 유형·맥락:** 15명(144개 임플란트) 대상 단일맹검 RCT로, 무치악 하악에서 즉시부하 고정성 전악 보철을 위한 발치즉시식립 vs 6개월 지연식립을 비교.
- **핵심 수치:** 12개월째 골소실 즉시군 0.54±0.39 mm vs 지연군 0.66±0.25 mm (p=0.201), 양군 모두 100% 생존율, 모든 연조직 파라미터(치태지수, 치은지수, 탐침깊이, 각화치은, 치주낭액, 점막염) 유의차 없음.
- **임상적 함의:** 전악 하악 재활에서 즉시식립이 지연식립과 동등한 1년 골보존·생존율 보이며 치료기간 단축 가능하나, 소규모·단기 추시가 한계.

## Summary

This prospective, randomized, single-blind clinical trial evaluated whether immediate post-extraction implant placement provides equivalent outcomes to delayed placement (6 months post-extraction) when both protocols use immediate loading of full-arch fixed prostheses in the edentulous mandible. Fifteen patients (mean age 63.7 years) were randomized: Group A received 68 implants placed immediately after extraction, Group B received 76 implants placed after 6 months of healing. All implants were immediately loaded with full-arch screw-retained prostheses. Follow-up assessments at 1 week, 6 months, and 12 months measured peri-implant crevicular fluid volume, plaque index, gingival retraction, keratinized mucosa width, probing depth, modified gingival index, mucositis presence, and marginal bone loss (radiographic).

At prosthetic loading, immediate implants showed greater keratinized mucosa width and probing depth (statistically significant). By 6 and 12 months, these differences resolved. Marginal bone loss at 12 months was 0.54 ± 0.39 mm (immediate) vs 0.66 ± 0.25 mm (delayed), not statistically different (p=0.201). Zero implant failures occurred in either group. All peri-implant health parameters showed no significant intergroup differences at 12 months.

The authors conclude that immediate and delayed implant placement yield comparable implant success and marginal bone loss at 1 year for immediately loaded full-arch mandibular prostheses, though the small sample and short follow-up warrant caution.

## Key Contributions

1. **RCT evidence for immediate vs delayed in full-arch mandibular context** — Few controlled trials exist comparing these protocols specifically for full-arch immediate loading.
2. **Comprehensive peri-implant parameter assessment** — Evaluated 7 clinical parameters plus radiographic bone loss, not just survival.
3. **Immediate loading in both arms** — Isolates the timing variable (immediate vs delayed placement) while keeping loading protocol constant.
4. **Quantitative bone loss data** — Provides precise marginal bone loss measurements (0.54 vs 0.66 mm) for benchmarking.

## Methodology

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Design** | Prospective, randomized, single-blind, parallel-group clinical trial |
| **Setting** | University of Valencia, Spain (Oral Surgery Unit) |
| **Population** | 15 edentulous mandibular patients (9F/6M), mean age 63.7 years, requiring full-arch rehabilitation |
| **Randomization** | Computer-generated sequence; allocation concealment not detailed |
| **Blinding** | Single-blind (patient blinded to group assignment) |
| **Intervention (Group A)** | 68 implants placed immediately post-extraction, immediately loaded |
| **Control (Group B)** | 76 implants placed 6 months post-extraction, immediately loaded |
| **Prosthesis** | Full-arch screw-retained fixed prosthesis, immediate loading in both groups |
| **Follow-up** | 1 week, 6 months, 12 months post-loading |
| **Primary outcomes** | Marginal bone loss (radiographic), implant survival |
| **Secondary outcomes** | Peri-implant crevicular fluid volume, plaque index (Silness & Löe), gingival retraction, keratinized mucosa width, probing depth, modified gingival index (Löe & Silness), mucositis presence |
| **Bone loss measurement** | Standardized periapical radiographs with parallel technique; measured from implant shoulder to first bone-implant contact |
| **Statistical analysis** | Student's t-test for continuous variables, chi-square for categorical; significance p<0.05 |
| **Funding** | Self-funded by authors/institution; no conflicts declared |

## Results

### Implant Survival
- **Immediate group (n=68): 100% survival at 12 months**
- **Delayed group (n=76): 100% survival at 12 months**
- Zero failures in either group

| Group | Mean ± SD (mm) | p-value |
|-------|----------------|---------|

### Peri-implant Health Parameters at 12 Months
All parameters showed **no statistically significant differences** between groups:
- Plaque index
- Modified gingival index
- Probing depth
- Keratinized mucosa width
- Gingival retraction
- Crevicular fluid volume
- Mucositis presence

### Early Time Points
- At loading (baseline): Immediate implants had significantly greater keratinized mucosa width and probing depth vs delayed
- At 6 months: Differences between groups had disappeared for all parameters

## Clinical Takeaway

For edentulous mandibular patients receiving immediately loaded full-arch fixed prostheses, **immediate post-extraction implant placement achieves equivalent 1-year marginal bone loss (~0.5–0.7 mm) and 100% implant survival compared to delayed placement at 6 months**. The immediate protocol reduces overall treatment time by ~6 months and avoids a second surgery. Clinicians can confidently offer immediate placement for full-arch mandibular cases, though longer-term (>1 year) data and larger samples would strengthen the evidence base.

## Limitations

1. **Small sample** (15 patients, though 144 implants provides reasonable implant-level n)
2. **Short follow-up** (12 months only; late failures and progressive bone loss not captured)
3. **Single-blind only** (operator not blinded; potential bias in clinical measurements)
4. **Single center** (University of Valencia; limited generalizability)
5. **Mandible only** (maxillary outcomes may differ due to bone quality)
6. **Allocation concealment** not explicitly described
