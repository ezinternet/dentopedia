## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 55 studies (PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane) comparing **All-on-4** vs **All-on-6** implant-supported fixed prostheses in edentulous patients across short-, mid-, and long-term follow-up.

**Survival rates** were high and similar: All-on-4 99.20/99.66/98.14% vs All-on-6 100/98.55/97.50% at 1 yr/1–5 yr/≥5 yr; **long-term MBL** favored All-on-6 (0.94 mm vs 1.28 mm); mechanical and biological **complications** were comparable (~6% each); **zirconia** prostheses associated with fewer mechanical complications.

Both techniques are clinically viable for full-arch rehabilitation; high heterogeneity across studies limits conclusion certainty; the guided vs freehand surgery question remains underpowered.

## Summary

This SR+MA synthesized 55 studies to compare All-on-4 (4 implants per jaw, posterior implants typically tilted) and All-on-6 (6 implants per jaw) techniques for full-arch fixed rehabilitation in edentulous patients. All outcomes — implant survival, marginal bone loss, and complication rates — were pooled across three follow-up horizons.

Both protocols demonstrated consistently high survival rates. The most notable divergence was in long-term marginal bone loss, where All-on-4 showed greater peri-implant bone loss at 5 years (1.28 mm) compared to All-on-6 (0.94 mm), despite short-term bone loss being nearly identical (0.77 mm vs 0.85 mm). This pattern suggests that the posterior implant angulation in All-on-4 may impose slightly greater long-term loading stress at the implant–bone interface.

## Key Contributions

- Largest SR+MA directly comparing All-on-4 vs All-on-6, pooling 55 studies across three follow-up windows
- Provides benchmark pooled survival rates for clinical decision-making and informed consent
- Highlights the long-term MBL divergence between techniques (All-on-4 accumulates more bone loss by ≥5 years)
- Identifies zirconia as a mechanical complication mitigation factor for full-arch prostheses
- Flags the guided vs freehand surgery subgroup as underpowered — conclusion withheld

## Methodology

- **Study design**: SR+MA (PRISMA-compliant assumed)
- **Databases**: PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane Library
- **N included studies**: 55
- **Population**: Edentulous patients (jaw not separately reported in abstract)
- **Primary outcomes**: Implant cumulative survival rate (CSR), marginal bone loss (MBL), mechanical and biological complication rates
- **Follow-up stratification**: Short-term (~1 yr) / Mid-term (1–5 yr) / Long-term (≥5 yr)
- **Heterogeneity**: High across included studies

## Results

### Implant Survival Rate

| Follow-up | All-on-4 | All-on-6 |
|---|---|---|
| Short-term (~1 yr) | 99.20% | 100% |
| Mid-term (1–5 yr) | 99.66% | 98.55% |
| Long-term (≥5 yr) | 98.14% | 97.50% |

### Marginal Bone Loss (MBL)

| Follow-up | All-on-4 | All-on-6 |
|---|---|---|
| Short-term | 0.77 mm | 0.85 mm |
| Long-term (~5 yr) | 1.28 mm | 0.94 mm |

Key observation: MBL trajectory diverges over time. At short-term, All-on-4 actually shows slightly *less* bone loss; by long-term, All-on-6 has the advantage (0.34 mm difference).

### Complications

| Type | All-on-4 | All-on-6 |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanical | 5.91% | 6.64% |
| Biological | 6.54% | 7.41% |

- No statistically significant difference between techniques overall
- Zirconia prosthesis frameworks associated with reduced mechanical complications
- Guided vs freehand surgery: insufficient data for valid comparison
