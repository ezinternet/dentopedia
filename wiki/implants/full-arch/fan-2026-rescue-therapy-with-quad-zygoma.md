## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort of 28 consecutive patients receiving 112 quad zygomatic implants (ZIs) as rescue therapy for failed maxillary full-arch rehabilitations (2006–2024); mean follow-up 8.2±4.1yr; 113 failed conventional implants removed, 48 ZIs placed immediately into explantation sockets; all immediate loading.

ZI survival 98.2% (2 late removals); implant success 93.5%; prosthetic success 96.4%; sinusitis 28.5% (8 patients, mean 5.92yr postop) — all medically managed without implant loss; soft tissue recession 9.8%; oroantral/facial fistula each 0.9%.

QZP provides a reliable and predictable salvage option for failed full-arch cases despite higher sinusitis rates; immediate loading enables immediate function; long-term outcomes support clinical viability of zygomatic rescue approach.

실패한 상악 전악 재활 28명에 Quad Zygoma 112개 식립, 평균 8.2년 추적; 113개 실패 임플란트 제거·48개 즉시 발거와 식립; 전례 즉시 부하.

QZP는 실패 전악 재활의 신뢰할 수 있는 구조 옵션; 상악동염 빈도 높으나 생존 영향 없음; 즉시 부하로 즉시 기능 회복.

## Study Design

- **Type**: Retrospective cohort study
- **Centers**: University of Barcelona (Spain), University Medical Center Mainz (Germany), Shanghai Jiao Tong University (China), Vithas Davó Hospital (Spain)
- **Period**: 2006–2024
- **N patients**: 28 consecutive patients
- **N ZIs**: 112 (4 per patient, Quad Zygoma Protocol)
- **Follow-up**: Mean 8.2 ± 4.1yr (range <5yr to >15yr)
- **Loading protocol**: Immediate loading 100% (insertion stability >35 Ncm in 94.7%)
- **Definitive prosthesis**: Fixed 96.4%, overdenture 3.6%; hybrid bridge metal/titanium framework 71.4%, acrylic with metal wire 28.6%

## Key Results

### Survival and Success

| Outcome | Rate |
|---|---|
| ZI survival | **98.2%** (2/112 removed) |
| Implant success (Albrektsson/Zarb criteria) | **93.5%** |
| Prosthetic success | **96.4%** |
| Mean follow-up | 8.2 ± 4.1yr |

| Complication | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sinusitis | 28.5% (8/28 patients) | Mean 5.92yr postop; all medically managed, no ZI loss |
| Soft tissue recession | 9.8% of cases | |
| Oroantral fistula | 0.9% of implants | |
| Facial fistula | 0.9% of implants | |
| Late ZI removal | 2 ZIs | Due to late complications |

### Failed Implant Management

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Failed conventional implants removed | 113 (in 23 patients) |
| ZIs placed in explantation sockets | 48 (42.8%) |
| ZIs placed in healed alveolar sites | 64 (57.2%) |
| Peri-implantitis defect severity (Grade A, >6mm/>50% bone loss) | 45 implants |
| Implants with clinical mobility | 58 |
| Remaining non-failed implants retained | 7 (in 5 patients) |

### Mandibular Findings

- 15 patients with peri-implantitis affecting 37 mandibular implants
- 14 mandibular implants failed in 6 patients (Grade A bone loss)
- 7 patients with advanced periodontitis → extraction → 19 patients received mandibular full-arch restorations

## Clinical Interpretation

The Quad Zygoma Protocol demonstrates excellent long-term survival (98.2%) and success (93.5%) as a rescue therapy for catastrophic maxillary full-arch failure due to advanced peri-implantitis. The 28.5% sinusitis rate is higher than typical zygomatic implant series (12–21%), likely reflecting the rescue context (explantation sockets, compromised sinus biology, microbial contamination from peri-implantitis). Critically, **all sinusitis cases resolved with medical management without implant loss**, consistent with ITI consensus that sinusitis does not compromise zygomatic implant survival when properly managed.

Immediate placement into fresh explantation sockets (42.8%) is a distinctive feature of this rescue protocol — socket remodeling and thorough debridement are critical. The high insertion stability (>35 Ncm in 94.7%) enabled 100% immediate loading, providing immediate functional and psychosocial rehabilitation.

Limitations: Retrospective design, heterogeneous ZI systems, no control group, "probable" bruxism not assessed, single-surgeon bias possible.
