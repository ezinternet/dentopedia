## Three-line Summary

1. **Study Type & Context**: Parallel-group RCT (20 edentulous patients, 10 per group) comparing zirconia vs PEEK substructure frameworks for full-arch implant-supported fixed prostheses; digital impressions with Medit i700 IOS; marginal gap and 3D trueness via micro-CT.
2. **Key Numerical Results**: Marginal gap: zirconia 42.3 ± 12.7 μm vs PEEK 58.9 ± 15.3 μm (p = 0.003). 3D trueness (RMS): zirconia 28.4 ± 8.2 μm vs PEEK 41.7 ± 10.5 μm (p = 0.001). Scanning time: zirconia 142 ± 18 s vs PEEK 168 ± 22 s (p = 0.012).
3. **Clinical Implication & Limitation**: Zirconia's opaque surface yields significantly better scan accuracy and marginal fit than translucent PEEK. Both materials produce clinically acceptable gaps (<100 μm), but zirconia is superior for precision-critical full-arch cases. Single IOS, single center, delivery-time only — long-term stability unknown.

## Summary

Emam et al. (2025) conducted a randomized controlled trial comparing the marginal gap and three-dimensional trueness of suprastructures fabricated from two different substructure framework materials — monolithic zirconia (3Y-TZP) and polyetheretherketone (PEEK) — for full-arch implant-supported fixed restorations. Twenty completely edentulous patients were randomly allocated (1:1) to receive either zirconia or PEEK substructures on 4–6 implants (All-on-4/6 protocol). Digital impressions were captured using a single intraoral scanner (Medit i700) with Medit scan bodies. Suprastructures were designed in exocad and milled via 5-axis CAD/CAM. Outcomes were assessed at prosthesis delivery using micro-CT (marginal gap) and 3D superimposition against an industrial optical scanner reference (trueness).

**Primary findings**: Zirconia substructures demonstrated statistically and clinically superior accuracy:
- **Marginal gap**: 42.3 ± 12.7 μm (zirconia) vs 58.9 ± 15.3 μm (PEEK), **p = 0.003** (28.2% reduction)
- **3D trueness (RMS)**: 28.4 ± 8.2 μm (zirconia) vs 41.7 ± 10.5 μm (PEEK), **p = 0.001** (31.9% improvement)
- **Scanning time**: 142 ± 18 s (zirconia) vs 168 ± 22 s (PEEK), **p = 0.012**

Both materials yielded marginal gaps within commonly cited clinical acceptability thresholds (50–100 μm), but zirconia consistently outperformed PEEK across maxilla and mandible subgroups.

## Key Contributions

- **First RCT** directly comparing zirconia vs PEEK substructure scanning accuracy in full-arch patients
- **In vivo clinical data** (not in vitro) — strengthens applicability to real-world practice
- **Mechanistic insight**: Links material optical properties (opacity vs translucency) to IOS structured-light accuracy
- **Comprehensive outcome set**: Marginal gap (micro-CT), 3D trueness (RMS vs industrial scanner), scanning efficiency
- **Clinically actionable**: Supports material selection for precision-critical full-arch workflows

## Methodology

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Design | Parallel-group RCT, 1:1 allocation |
| Setting | Alexandria University, Egypt (single center) |
| Ethics | Alexandria University IRB 0796_11/2023; Helsinki Declaration |
| Patients | 20 edentulous (12M/8F), mean age 58.4 ± 9.2 yr |
| Arches | 12 maxilla, 8 mandible (balanced across groups) |
| Implants | 4–6 per arch (All-on-4/6), same system both groups |
| Randomization | Computer-generated, allocation concealment not detailed |
| Blinding | Outcome assessors blinded; patients/operators not blinded (material visible) |
| IOS | Medit i700 (structured light, blue LED) |
| Scan bodies | Medit scan bodies (same for both groups) |
| CAD/CAM | exocad design, 5-axis milling |
| Primary outcome | Marginal gap (μm) via micro-CT, 4 sites/implant |
| Secondary outcome | 3D trueness (RMS μm) vs ATOS Core reference; scanning time |
| Statistics | Shapiro-Wilk → t-test or Mann-Whitney U; α = 0.05 |
| Sample size | A priori calculation (80% power, pilot effect size) |

## Clinical Takeaway

> **For full-arch implant-supported fixed prostheses where passive fit is paramount, zirconia substructures provide measurably better marginal adaptation (~42 vs ~59 μm) and scanning trueness (~28 vs ~42 μm RMS) than PEEK.** The difference is clinically meaningful for high-precision workflows. However, **both materials fall within accepted marginal gap thresholds (<100 μm)**. PEEK's advantages (radiolucency, shock absorption, lighter weight) may justify its use when marginal gap is less critical or when biological factors favor polymer frameworks.

## Research Gaps

1. **Long-term marginal gap stability** — only delivery-time assessment; loading cycles, thermal cycling, aging unknown
2. **Multiple IOS systems** — single scanner (Medit i700); confocal vs triangulation vs structured light may interact differently with PEEK translucency
3. **PEEK surface treatments** — opaquing, sandblasting, coating to improve scannability not tested
4. **Patient-centered outcomes** — comfort, phonetics, satisfaction not measured
5. **Biological outcomes** — peri-implant health, plaque accumulation on different frameworks
6. **Cost-effectiveness** — zirconia milling vs PEEK milling/printing workflow economics
7. **Larger multicenter trials** — single center, n=20 limits generalizability
