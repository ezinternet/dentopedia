## Three-line Summary

1. **Study Type & Context**: Systematic review and meta-analysis (13 studies: 3 in vivo, 10 in vitro) comparing stereophotogrammetry (SPG) vs intraoral scanning (IOS) for complete-arch digital implant impressions; search May 2024, 7 IOS devices, 2 SPG devices.
2. **Key Numerical Results**: SPG superior in 10/13 studies. Meta-analyses (4 in vitro studies each): Angular deviation trueness Cohen's D = 1.20 (p = 0.02); Surface deviation (RMS) trueness effect size = 3.426 (p < 0.05); Surface deviation precision effect size = 4.893 (p < 0.05) — all favoring SPG. High heterogeneity (I² > 94%).
3. **Clinical Implication & Limitation**: SPG shows superior accuracy for complete-arch impressions, supporting its use for passive-fit full-arch prostheses. However, high heterogeneity, only 3 clinical studies, and IOS with prefabricated aids showed comparable results in one trial warrant cautious clinical translation.

1. **연구 유형·맥락**: 전악 디지털 임플란트 인상에서 광학측량(SPG) vs 구강내 스캐너(IOS) 정확도 비교 체계적 문헌고찰·메타분석(13편: in vivo 3, in vitro 10), 2024년 5월 검색, IOS 7종·SPG 2종.

## Summary

Pozzi et al. (2025) conducted the first systematic review with meta-analysis comparing stereophotogrammetry (SPG) and intraoral scanning (IOS) for complete-arch digital implant impressions. Following PRISMA 2020 and registered on PROSPERO (CRD42024490844), they searched PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL (May 2024). Thirteen studies met inclusion criteria (3 in vivo, 10 in vitro), evaluating 7 IOS systems (Trios 3/4, iTero Element 2/5D, Primescan, Aoralscan 3, Carestream 3600) and 2 SPG devices (PIC Camera, ICam4D). Quality was assessed with QUADAS-2.

**Primary finding**: SPG demonstrated higher accuracy than IOS in 10 of 13 studies. Three random-effects meta-analyses were performed on in vitro data (minimum 3 studies each):

1. **Angular deviation trueness**: Cohen's D = 1.20, p = 0.02 (favoring SPG)
2. **Surface deviation (RMS) trueness**: Effect size = 3.426, p < 0.05 (favoring SPG)
3. **Surface deviation (RMS) precision**: Effect size = 4.893, p < 0.05 (favoring SPG)

All meta-analyses showed high heterogeneity (I² > 94%). Sensitivity analysis for angular trueness identified Tohme 2021 as an outlier; exclusion yielded robust significance.

## Key Contributions

- **First meta-analysis** quantitatively comparing SPG vs IOS for complete-arch impressions
- **Comprehensive device coverage**: 7 IOS + 2 SPG systems reflecting current market
- **Separated trueness and precision** analyses per ISO 5725-1
- **Identified critical evidence gap**: Only 3 in vivo studies; most data from in vitro models
- **Highlighted scan aid potential**: Fu 2023 showed IOS with prefabricated aids comparable to SPG clinically

## Clinical Takeaway

> **For complete-arch implant-supported fixed prostheses requiring passive fit, stereophotogrammetry (SPG) provides superior accuracy (trueness and precision) over intraoral scanning (IOS) based on current evidence.** However, this conclusion rests primarily on in vitro data with high heterogeneity. **Clinically, IOS with prefabricated scan aids may achieve comparable results** (Fu 2023). Device selection should consider clinical workflow, cost, and case complexity—not just accuracy metrics.

## Research Gaps

1. **More in vivo studies** — only 3 clinical trials; patient factors (saliva, limited opening, mobility) not captured in vitro
2. **Standardized reference scans** — CMM vs EOS vs industrial scanners introduce measurement bias
3. **Prefabricated scan aids for IOS** — promising but only 1 clinical study
4. **Long-term clinical outcomes** — accuracy → prosthetic survival/complications link not established
5. **Cost-effectiveness analysis** — SPG systems add equipment cost; ROI unclear
6. **Operator learning curve** — not assessed in included studies
