## Three-line Summary

Murine oral implant model: osteotomies in healed maxillary extraction sites made by drilling alone vs drilling followed by stepwise condensation with tapered osteotomes (group sizes not stated in the abstract).

Condensation raised interfacial bone density (significant change in bone volume/total volume and trabecular spacing) but produced microfractures and osteoclast activity on postimplant day 1, very high interfacial strains and marginal bone resorption, with no improvement in implant stability.

Condensation did not positively contribute to implant stability in this animal model; extrapolation to humans is not tested in the abstract.

## 세줄요약

생쥐 구강 임플란트 모델: 치유된 상악 발치와에서 드릴링만 vs 드릴링 후 테이퍼드 골압축기 (Osteotome)로 단계적 압축 (군별 n은 초록에 없음).

압축은 계면 골밀도를 높였으나 (골체적/전체체적, 골소주 간격 유의한 변화) 식립 1일째 미세골절과 파골세포 활성, 매우 높은 계면 변형률과 변연골 흡수를 유발했고 임플란트 안정성은 개선되지 않았다.

이 동물 모델에서 골 압축 (Condensation)은 임플란트 안정성에 기여하지 않았으며, 사람으로의 외삽은 초록만으로는 검증되지 않는다.

## Summary

The authors note that bone condensation is thought to densify interfacial bone and improve primary stability, but scant data support either claim. In a murine oral implant model, osteotomies in healed maxillary extraction sites were prepared by drilling or by drilling plus stepwise tapered-osteotome condensation. Condensation increased interfacial bone density but damaged the bone: microfractures and osteoclast activity appeared on postimplant day 1. Finite element modeling, mechanical testing and immunohistochemistry across the osseointegration period showed very high interfacial strains, marginal bone resorption and no improvement in implant stability.

## Key Contributions
- Separates the two assumed benefits of condensation: density gain was observed, stability gain was not.
- Links condensation to interfacial microdamage, high strain and marginal bone resorption.
- Multiscale design (finite element, mechanical, immunohistochemical).

## Methodology
Murine model; drilling vs drilling plus stepwise osteotome condensation in healed maxillary extraction sites; analyses at multiple time points (n not stated in abstract).

## Results
