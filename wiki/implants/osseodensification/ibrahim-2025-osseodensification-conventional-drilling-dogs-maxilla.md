## Three-line Summary

Animal study (8 mongrel dogs, split-mouth) comparing OD Densah drills vs conventional NucleOSS drills in dog maxilla with and without NucleOSS T6 implant placement (3.5×10 mm), evaluated at 0 days and 2 months via H&E/Masson's Trichrome histomorphometry and CBCT.

OD produced significantly higher bone area percentage (H&E: 22.26±2.58% vs 11.11±0.82%, p<0.001) and CBCT bone density around implants (buccal: 1678.75±73.37 vs 919.5±220.84, p<0.001; palatal: 1785±213.68 vs 1186.5±71.32, p<0.001); conventional drilling caused immediate thermal osteonecrosis while OD compressed viable bone chips into osteotomy walls.

Supports OD as preferred technique for implant placement in low-density maxillary bone; limited by small sample (n=8 dogs), short 2-month follow-up, and different drill sequence final diameters (3.0 mm OD vs 3.5 mm conventional).

## Summary

This split-mouth dog study (n=8 adult male mongrel dogs) compared osseodensification (OD) using Densah burs against conventional NucleOSS drilling for maxillary implant site preparation. Animals were divided into two timepoints: Group I (euthanized at day 0) assessed only drilling effects without implant placement, while Group II (euthanized at 2 months) evaluated osseointegration outcomes after NucleOSS T6 implant insertion (3.5×10 mm). Histological evaluation revealed that conventional drilling immediately caused thermal osteonecrosis and microcracks, while OD preserved viable bone chips compressed against osteotomy walls. At 2 months, the OD + implant subgroup (IIB) demonstrated significantly superior bone area percentage and CBCT radiographic bone density compared to the conventional + implant subgroup (IIA). The OD technique showed a 2-fold greater bone area percentage by H&E (22.26% vs 11.11%) and approximately 82% higher buccal bone density by CBCT (1679 vs 920 units). These findings reinforce the utility of OD in low-density maxillary bone, consistent with prior sheep and in vitro animal evidence.

## Key Contributions
- Demonstrated immediate thermal osteonecrosis with conventional drilling vs bone chip condensation with OD at day 0, supporting histological superiority of OD from the first moment of drilling
- Quantified bone area % advantage: OD doubled bone area at 2 months (H&E: 22.26% vs 11.11%, p<0.001); Masson's Trichrome confirmed mature collagen type I (red) in OD vs immature type III (blue) in conventional
- Showed CBCT bone density advantage only emerges with implant in place (Group II significant, Group I without implant NS), suggesting bone chip compaction-spring-back effect depends on implant confinement

## Methodology
- **Design**: Animal experimental, split-mouth controlled
- **Model**: 8 adult male mongrel dogs, maxillary 2nd premolar site (post-extraction socket)
- **Groups**: Control (no drilling) / IA (conventional, 0d) / IB (OD, 0d) / IIA (conventional+implant, 2mo) / IIB (OD+implant, 2mo)
- **Outcomes**: H&E bone area %, Masson's Trichrome bone area %, CBCT radiographic bone density (buccal + palatal), qualitative histology

## Results

**Histomorphometric bone area % at 2 months:**

| Measure | IIA (Conventional) | IIB (OD) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| H&E bone area % | 11.11 ± 0.82% | 22.26 ± 2.58% | <0.001 |
| Masson's Trichrome % | 10.70 ± 1.07% | 14.55 ± 1.13% | <0.001 |

**CBCT bone density at 2 months with implant:**

| Site | IIA (Conventional) | IIB (OD) | Diff | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buccal | 919.5 ± 220.84 | 1678.75 ± 73.37 | 759.25 | <0.001 |
| Palatal | 1186.5 ± 71.32 | 1785 ± 213.68 | 598.5 | <0.001 |

**CBCT bone density without implant (Group I, NS):**

| Site | IA (Conventional) | IB (OD) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
