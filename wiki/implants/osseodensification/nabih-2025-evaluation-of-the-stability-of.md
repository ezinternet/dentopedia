## Three-line Summary
Double-centred, double-blinded randomized controlled trial (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT; n=22, one maxillary single-tooth site each, D3/D4 bone, buccolingual width ≥4 mm) comparing the 골밀도화 (Osseodensification, OD) technique (Versah Densah burs, counterclockwise 800–1200 rpm) against the HaeNaem expander kit ((주)해냄, Pyeongtaek, Korea; 2.6/3.2/3.6 mm, 50 rpm + ratchet wrench).

Implant stability showed no significant intergroup difference at baseline (75.27 vs 78.64, P=0.2247) or 3 months (73.10 vs 73.00, P=0.9770), but a significant 6-month difference in favour of OD (ISQ 79.60 vs 72.10, P=0.0268); ridge width at 2, 4 and 8 mm levels favoured OD at 6 months (P=0.0002/0.0004/0.0096); pain was equivalent (P=0.1480 → 0 by 1 week); 3 implants failed (87% retention).

OD appears effective for expanding narrow low-quality ridges and improving implant stability with minimal postoperative pain, but the groups differed significantly in preoperative crestal ridge width and follow-up was short (6 months) — the stability advantage may reflect baseline ridge-width disparity; authors suggest expanders may suit moderate density (D2–D3) with adequate width.

## Summary
This double-centred, prospective, double-blinded RCT randomized 22 patients (one missing maxillary tooth each) with D3 (350–850 HU) or D4 (150–350 HU) low-quality bone and a residual buccolingual width ≥4 mm to either the 골밀도화 (Osseodensification, OD) technique (Versah LLC Densah burs, counterclockwise 800–1200 rpm) or the HaeNaem expander kit ((주)해냄, Pyeongtaek-si, Korea; 2.6/3.2/3.6 mm at 50 rpm, completed with a ratchet wrench). The primary outcome was implant stability (primary stability at placement; secondary stability at 3 and 6 months measured via Osstell 임플란트 안정성 지수 (Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ)); secondary outcomes were pain (VAS) and ridge width at 2, 4, 8 mm levels on CBCT (pre-op, baseline, 3 and 6 months).

Stability was equivalent between interventions at baseline (75.27 vs 78.64, P=0.2247) and 3 months (73.10 vs 73.00, P=0.9770) but significantly higher in the OD group at 6 months (79.60 vs 72.10, P=0.0268). Ridge width significantly favoured the OD group at all three vertical levels at 6 months (2 mm 7.73 vs 5.47, P=0.0002; 4 mm 8.41 vs 6.65, P=0.0004; 8 mm 8.85 vs 7.19, P=0.0096), and at 2 mm the groups already differed preoperatively (6.63 vs 5.60, P=0.0193). Pain was similar at baseline (3.73 vs 4.82, P=0.1480) and nil (0.00) in both groups at 1 week. Three implants failed within 6 months (2 expander-group at 3 months, 1 OD-group post-loading; 87% retention; 2 additional D4 cases excluded after buccal cortex fracture during expander use). The authors conclude OD is effective in low-quality bone — improving stability, enabling expansion of narrow ridges, and producing minimal postoperative pain — while noting the significant preoperative difference in crestal ridge width and short follow-up as caveats.

## Key Contributions
- First held RCT comparing OD directly against ridge expanders for implant placement in D3/D4 low-density bone, extending the wiki's OD evidence base (previously OD vs standard drilling, and retrospective OD-for-expansion).
- Documents the HaeNaem expander kit ((주)해냄, Pyeongtaek-si, Korea) in a prospective double-blinded control arm — one of the first non-retracted clinical uses of a HaeNaem product held in the wiki (OD arm: Versah LLC Densah burs; implants: Roott/TRATE AG).
- Reports OD superior to expanders for secondary stability at 6 months (ISQ 79.60±6.36 vs 72.10±7.50, P=0.0268) while primary and 3-month stability were equivalent.
- CBCT ridge-width tracking at 2, 4, 8 mm from the implant platform showing OD favoured at all levels at 6 months — authors state it is the first study to assess ridge width at multiple vertical levels; a prospective RCT counterpart to the retrospective OD-ridge-expansion evidence.
- Adds VAS pain outcomes (no intergroup difference; ≈0 at 1 week in both groups) and 87% retention (2 expander failures, 1 OD failure; 2 D4 buccal-cortex fractures during expander use excluded).

## Methodology
Double-centred prospective double-blinded (participants + outcome evaluator; PI unblinded) two-arm parallel RCT, ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05330546, ethics ID 12722 (Cairo University), CONSORT. n=22 (11/group), randomized 1:1 by computer (randomizer.org) with sequentially numbered sealed opaque envelopes. Inclusion: single maxillary missing tooth, D3 (350–850 HU)/D4 (150–350 HU), buccolingual width ≥4 mm, interocclusal space 8–10 mm; exclusions: systemic contraindications, smoking, bruxism/clenching, alcohol/drug abuse, pregnancy/lactation.

Test group prepared the osteotomy with sequential Densah burs (Versah, LLC, Jackson, Michigan, USA) in counterclockwise rotation at 800–1200 rpm. Control group used the HaeNaem expander kit ((주)해냄, Pyeongtaek-si, Gyeonggi-do, Korea) — bone expanders of increasing diameter (2.6, 3.2, 3.6 mm) on an electric handpiece at 50 rpm, continuing with a ratchet wrench upon resistance, inserting instruments in intervals to allow bone to expand. Implants: Roott (TRATE AG, Root, Switzerland). Both groups: delayed implant placement, stability measured with Osstell ISQ (primary at placement; secondary at 3 and 6 months), healing abutment + crown at 3-month exposure. Ridge width measured on CBCT (90 kVp, 12 mA, 5×8 cm FOV, 1 mm slices) at 2, 4, 8 mm from the implant platform at pre-op, baseline, 3 and 6 months. Pain on 10 cm VAS at surgery + 1 week. Statistics: normality (Shapiro–Wilk, Kolmogorov–Smirnov), independent t-tests between groups, repeated-measures ANOVA + Tukey/Bonferroni within groups, α=0.05, MedCalc v22.

**Retention & safety**: 87% retention (19/22) — 2 expander-group failures at 3 months (visible mobility) and 1 OD-group failure after loading before 6 months; 2 additional D4 cases were excluded after buccal cortex fracture during expander use. Authors attribute failures to high insertion torque and/or systemic factors (e.g. vitamin D deficiency).

*Note*: the 2 mm level showed a significant preoperative intergroup difference (P=0.0193) — a confounding factor acknowledged by the authors; the 4 and 8 mm levels had equivalent preoperative widths (P=0.2346/0.2942). Intragroup: OD maintained ridge expansion through 6 months at 2/4 mm; the expander group's ~1 mm initial gain receded to ~preoperative width by 3–6 months.
