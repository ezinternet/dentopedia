## Three-line Summary

Parallel-group double-blind RCT (n=20 patients/20 implants, OD n=10 vs CD n=10) in Misch D3/D4 posterior-maxilla sites; ISQ tracked from insertion through 12 months of loading, plus insertion torque, crestal bone loss and ICOI-based survival.

OD showed significantly higher ISQ post-insertion (72.7±3.1 vs 63.3±12, p=0.037) and through week 4 with no early stability dip (CD dipped during weeks 2–3), while survival (9/9 [100%] vs 8/10 [80%], p=0.474, OR 2.125), insertion torque (38.3±7.1 vs 30.6±9.4 Ncm, p=0.074) and 12-month crestal bone loss (median 1 [0.85–1.51] vs 1.018 [0.8–2.4] mm, p=0.962) showed no significant between-group difference.

OD produced earlier, more stable implant stability in the posterior maxilla without a crestal-bone penalty, which the authors interpret as suitable for early/immediate-loading candidates — but the small sample (n=20) and non-significant survival or torque differences cap certainty.

## Summary

This parallel-group, double-blind randomized clinical trial (NCT04442763) compared osseodensification (OD, Densah burs counterclockwise) against conventional drilling (CD, clockwise) for implant placement in the Misch D3/D4 posterior maxilla, a site where OD is most promoted. Twenty patients received 20 single implants (OD n=10, CD n=10); 17 completed 12 months of loading after 1 OD dropout and 2 early CD failures. ISQ, the primary outcome, was measured at 9 timepoints from insertion to 12 months of loading, with insertion torque, crestal bone loss and survival as secondary outcomes. OD showed significantly higher ISQ at insertion and through week 4 — with no early stability dip — while CD dropped at weeks 2–3 before recovering; survival trended higher with OD (100% vs 80%) but torque and crestal bone loss were equivalent. The authors conclude OD offers earlier implant stability in the posterior maxilla without a crestal-bone penalty, potentially enabling early or immediate loading in low-density bone.

## Key Contributions

- Randomized, double-blind, CONSORT-compliant head-to-head of OD vs CD in the posterior maxilla restricted to Misch D3/D4 bone, with ISQ monitored over 9 timepoints spanning osseointegration and 12 months of loading.
- Documents an early ISQ superiority for OD (post-insertion through week 4) plus absence of the classic stability dip, versus a pronounced weeks-2–3 dip in the CD group — longitudinal trajectory data prior SR+MA could not pool.
- Reports a survival point-estimate gap favoring OD (9/9 [100%] vs 8/10 [80%], OR 2.125; NS) alongside equivalence of crestal bone loss and insertion torque.
- Demonstrates that in this cohort 88.9% of OD sites reached IT ≥35 Ncm and mean ISQ stayed above 68 even at the lowest point, framing OD as compatible with early/immediate-loading thresholds.

## Methodology

Parallel-group RCT, 1:1 allocation, randomized (random.org) with concealed allocation in opaque sealed envelopes; double-blind for participants, outcome assessors, and statistician. Enrolled 20 healthy adults with maxillary premolar–molar single sites, healed ≥6-month ridges (width ≥6 mm, height ≥10 mm), D3/D4 bone (Misch classification by CBCT visual assessment); exclusions included smoking, active periodontitis, and head/neck radiation. Implants were Neobiotech IS-II Active (tapered, SLA, buttress threads, 3.5–4 × 10–11.5 mm). OD: Densah burs CCW per densifying reference guide after CW pilot; CD: standard CW drills; both 800–1200 rpm with pumping action and saline irrigation. Loading at 3 months required ISQ ≥70. Outcome assessment: ISQ (Osstell Mega ISQ) at insertion, weeks 1–4, 6, 8, 3 months, 12 months post-loading; insertion torque (Ncm, manual wrench); crestal bone loss (standardized periapical radiographs, Digora) at insertion, 3, 6, 12 months; survival (ICOI Pisa criteria). Statistics: repeated-measures ANOVA + Bonferroni (ISQ), Student's t (IT), Mann-Whitney U / Friedman-Dunn (CBL), Fisher's Exact (survival); P ≤ 0.05.

Key observations: OD ISQ did not change significantly from insertion through 3 months, then rose to 77.7 at 12 months post-loading; CD ISQ dipped significantly at weeks 2–3, then recovered through 12 months. Between-group ISQ differences were significant only from insertion through week 4; groups converged from week 6 onward. Both groups significantly increased crestal bone loss over time (intra-group p<0.001), but never differed from each other at any timepoint; all implants except one CD implant (2.4 mm) stayed under 2 mm. Two early CD osseointegration failures; no other complications.
