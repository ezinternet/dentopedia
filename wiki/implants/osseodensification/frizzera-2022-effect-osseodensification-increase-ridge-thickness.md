## Three-line Summary

Randomized split-mouth in-vitro pilot on 10 fresh pig mandibles with limited ridge thickness (20 implants, 4.5 × 10 mm): conventional cutting-bur drilling (CTL, n = 10) vs osseodensification with Densah® burs (OD, n = 10).

OD gave significantly higher insertion torque (49.9 ± 11.45 vs 40.4 ± 8.07 N·cm), drastically smaller buccal defects (height 0.37 ± 0.72 vs 2.5 ± 2.14 mm; only 1 buccal defect > 0.5 mm vs 8), and greater buccal ridge expansion at the crest and 1 mm level after preparation (0.66 ± 0.64 vs 0.18 ± 0.2 mm).

OD expands the ridge and prevents buccal peri-implant defects versus conventional drilling, but as an ex-vivo pilot with no healing phase and no sample-size calculation, clinical benefit needs confirmation in human studies.

## Summary

This in-vitro pilot tested whether osseodensification (OD) — Densah® burs run counterclockwise to compact bone rather than remove it — increases ridge thickness and prevents buccal peri-implant defects in limited-thickness bone, versus conventional cutting-bur drilling. Using a randomized split-mouth design on ten fresh pig mandibles, two cone-morse 4.5 × 10 mm implants per jaw were placed into sites prepared either conventionally (CTL, n = 10) or with OD (n = 10), with insertion torque, photo-based defect geometry (height, width, area; buccal and lingual), and 3D STL ridge-thickness change measured.

OD produced significantly higher insertion torque (49.9 ± 11.45 vs 40.4 ± 8.07 N·cm, p < 0.05), a drastic reduction in buccal defects (defect height 0.37 ± 0.72 vs 2.5 ± 2.14 mm; only one buccal defect > 0.5 mm vs eight in CTL), and significantly greater buccal ridge expansion at the crest and 1 mm levels, both after site preparation and after implant placement. Lingual defect width/area were also reduced, though lingual expansion itself was limited by the thicker, less plastic lingual cortex. The authors position OD as a way to expand the ridge and allow simultaneous implant placement with less need for grafting in anatomically compromised sites.

## Key Contributions

- First randomized split-mouth in-vitro comparison of OD vs conventional drilling with peri-implant **bone defect extent** (height, width, area) as the outcome, measured on both buccal and lingual walls.
- Quantifies the OD-mediated **increase in buccal ridge thickness** with digital 3D STL analysis (blinded, duplicated measurements) at the crest and 1, 2, 4, 6 mm apical, at two time points (post-preparation T1→T2 and post-placement T2→T3).
- Confirms significantly **higher insertion torque** with OD in limited-thickness bone.
- Proposes OD as a less-invasive alternative to onlay grafting for ridges with compromised thickness, enabling simultaneous implant placement in native bone.
- Documents the **asymmetry**: buccal expansion is substantial while lingual expansion is minimal (thicker, less plastic lingual cortex).

## Methodology

Randomized split-mouth in-vitro pilot on 10 fresh pig mandibles (~1 yr old, ~100 kg, edentulous span ≥ 10 mm between lower canine and first premolar). Randomization software assigned the first operated side and which side received each protocol. Each jaw contributed one CTL and one OD site (n = 10 per group), prepared by a single operator. CTL used guide drill + twist 2.0 + tapered burs 3.0/3.3/4.0 mm, clockwise 800 rpm, 20 N·cm; OD used Densah® burs (pilot + 2.0/2.3/3.0/3.3/4.3), counterclockwise 800 rpm, 20 N·cm, with copious irrigation. Cone-morse implants (4.5 × 10 mm, CM SW Plus, S.I.N.) were seated at 20 rpm to 35 N·cm or crestal level, finished with a manual torque wrench (perforation depth 11 mm, 1 mm subcrestal).

Outcomes: primary = buccal bone defect height after placement; secondary = buccal and lingual defect width/area (ImageJ on standardized tripod photographs, periodontal probe as scale reference), insertion torque, and linear buccal ridge increase from STL overlap (Trios3 scanner → ExoCAD; first lower premolars as reference; blinded evaluator, measurements repeated 7 days apart). Non-parametric Mann-Whitney test, α = 5% (GraphPad Prism 8). Ethics: FOAr–UNESP Animal Ethics Committee, protocol 20/2019.

## Results

| Outcome | CTL (n = 10) | OD (n = 10) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insertion torque (N·cm) | 40.4 ± 8.07 | 49.9 ± 11.45 | < 0.05 |
| Buccal defect height (mm) | 2.5 ± 2.14 | 0.37 ± 0.72 | ≤ 0.01 |
| Buccal defect width (mm) | 2.46 ± 1.38 | 0.73 ± 0.82 | ≤ 0.01 |
| Buccal defect area (mm²) | 5.97 ± 6.57 | 0.36 ± 0.47 | ≤ 0.01 |
| Buccal defects > 0.5 mm height | 8 | 1 | — |
| Lingual defect width (mm) | 3.17 ± 1.54 | 1.98 ± 1.10 | ≤ 0.05 |
| Lingual defect area (mm²) | 4.65 ± 3.24 | 1.72 ± 1.06 | ≤ 0.05 |
| Buccal ridge gain after prep, crest (mm) | 0.18 ± 0.2 | 0.66 ± 0.64 | ≤ 0.05 |
| Buccal ridge gain after prep, 1 mm (mm) | 0.09 ± 0.15 | 0.39 ± 0.37 | ≤ 0.05 |
| Buccal ridge gain after placement, crest (mm) | 0.03 ± 0.11 | 0.28 ± 0.35 | ≤ 0.05 |
| Buccal ridge gain after placement, 1 mm (mm) | 0.07 ± 0.13 | 0.25 ± 0.35 | ≤ 0.05 |

Baseline ridge thickness did not differ between groups (crest, 1, 2, 4, 6 mm), and analog caliper vs digital STL measurements agreed at baseline. Lingual defect height was not significantly different. Ridge-gain differences were significant at the crest and 1 mm levels only; 2/4/6 mm levels were not significant — expansion concentrates coronally. All 20 implants seated as planned; the paper prints the torque unit as "N/cm²", which is a typographical error for N·cm.
