## Three-line Summary
Sheep in vivo study (16 adult sheep, 64 titanium implants with bone chambers, mandibular cortical bone, harvest at 3/6/12/24 weeks) comparing osseodensification (OD, Densah burs counterclockwise) against conventional subtractive drilling (Reg), crossed with machined (Mach) vs acid-etched (AE) surfaces, in 3.8 mm osteotomies for 4 mm implants.
Insertion torque (AE: 87.00 vs 80.95 N·cm; Mach: 74.04 vs 74.00 N·cm; p = 0.689 and 0.997 for OD vs Reg), bone-to-implant contact (BIC) and bone area fractional occupancy (BAFO) did not differ between OD and Reg at any time point (all p > 0.05); surface treatment also made no difference, and no necrosis, inflammation or microfractures were seen with OD.
In dense cortical bone either technique is acceptable when the osteotomy is not undersized relative to the implant major diameter; OD benefits seen in trabecular bone should not be assumed here (modest group size; histomorphometry limited to one section per implant; undersizing not tested).

## Summary
This preclinical study placed 64 bone-chamber titanium implants in the submandibular cortical bone of 16 sheep, comparing osseodensification (counterclockwise Densah burs) with conventional subtractive drilling, each with machined or acid-etched surfaces, and harvested at 3, 6, 12 or 24 weeks. Insertion torque, bone-to-implant contact (BIC) and bone area fractional occupancy (BAFO) were statistically indistinguishable between drilling techniques at every time point, and surface treatment did not matter either. Osseodensification sites showed no necrosis, inflammation or microfractures, but the osteotomy (3.8 mm) was matched to the implant major diameter rather than undersized. The authors conclude either technique is suitable in dense bone, and warn against undersizing with OD.

## Key Contributions
- No microfracture, necrosis or inflammation after OD in dense cortical bone when the osteotomy was not considerably undersized (safety statement; undersizing was not tested).
- OD did not raise insertion torque or BIC/BAFO over conventional drilling in cortical bone, contrasting with trabecular-bone results.
- Acid-etched vs machined surface had no measurable effect in cortical bone.

## Methodology
16 adult sheep, 4 implants each (n = 16 per group), randomized within animal; 4 sheep per time point (3, 6, 12, 24 weeks). Ti-6Al-4V 4 x 10 mm implants with designated bone chambers in 3.8 mm osteotomies, prepared at 1100 rpm with saline irrigation. Non-decalcified histomorphometry (one section per implant); mixed models, p < 0.05.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Insertion torque, AE | 87.00 (OD) vs 80.95 N·cm (Reg), p = 0.689 |
| Insertion torque, Mach | 74.04 (OD) vs 74.00 N·cm (Reg), p = 0.997 |
| BIC OD vs Reg | No difference at 3/6/12/24 wk (all p > 0.135) |
| BAFO OD vs Reg | No difference at 3/6/12/24 wk (all p >= 0.060) |
| Early trend | Numerically higher BIC/BAFO with OD at 3-6 wk, not significant |
| Adverse histology | No necrosis, inflammation or microfractures with OD |
