## Three-line Summary

Comprehensive overview of systematic reviews (umbrella/AMSTAR-rated synthesis; 41 SRs/MAs selected from 116 screened, published up to November 2015; 11 on implant factors, 10 on patient factors, 19 on surgical protocol, 1 on all three) on surgical and patient factors affecting marginal bone loss (MBL) around osseointegrated dental implants.

Statistically significant factors: periodontitis (esp. generalized aggressive vs chronic), male sex, and smoking (incl. greater MBL in maxilla than mandible in smokers) all increased MBL; alveolar socket/ridge preservation with grafts reduced MBL, while ridge augmentation sites had more MBL than sites treated with shorter implants; implant length/type and immediate vs delayed placement showed conflicting results across included SRs, while implant surface, implant position (tilted vs axial), flapless vs flapped surgery, soft-tissue augmentation, insertion torque, and 1-stage vs 2-stage surgery showed no significant effect.

As a narrative overview of pre-existing SRs (no new pooled meta-analysis, AMSTAR quality-graded only), it is best used as a clinical risk-factor triage map rather than a source of pooled effect sizes; inconsistent MBL reference points and follow-up durations across the 41 underlying reviews limit precision.

## 세줄요약

체계적문헌고찰들의 종합적 개관(umbrella/AMSTAR 평가 종합, 2015년 11월까지 발표된 SR/MA 116편 중 41편 선정 — 임플란트 요인 11편, 환자 요인 10편, 수술 프로토콜 19편, 3요인 모두 1편), 골유착 임플란트 주위 변연골 소실(Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)에 영향을 미치는 수술적·환자적 요인 평가.

통계적으로 유의한 요인: 치주염(특히 전신형 공격성 치주염 > 만성 치주염), 남성, 흡연(흡연자는 하악보다 상악에서 MBL이 더 큼)이 MBL을 증가시켰고; 발치와 보존술(socket/ridge preservation with grafts)은 MBL을 감소, 반면 치조제 증대(ridge augmentation) 부위는 짧은 임플란트 대체 부위보다 MBL이 더 많았음; 임플란트 길이·종류·즉시 vs 지연 식립은 결과 상충, 임플란트 표면·경사(tilted vs axial) 식립·flapless vs flapped 수술·연조직 이식술·삽입 토크·1-stage vs 2-stage는 유의한 영향 없음.

기존 SR들을 AMSTAR 등급 평가와 함께 서술적으로 종합한 개관(자체 pooled meta-analysis 없음)이므로 pooled 수치보다는 임상 위험요인 분류 지도로 활용하는 것이 적합하며, 41편 개별 SR 간 MBL 측정 기준점·추적기간의 불일치가 정밀도를 제한한다.

## Summary

Ting et al. (Temple University, *Implant Dentistry* 2017;26(2):303–315) conducted an AMSTAR-graded comprehensive overview of 41 systematic reviews/meta-analyses (published up to November 2015) addressing surgical and patient (non-prosthodontic) factors affecting marginal bone loss (MBL) around osseointegrated dental implants. Of the included reviews, 11 addressed implant factors, 10 patient factors, 19 surgical-protocol factors, and 1 covered all three domains; 25 were AMSTAR-rated high quality and 16 moderate. The overview concluded that patient factors — periodontitis, smoking, and male sex — consistently and significantly increased MBL, while several surgical technique choices long assumed to matter (flapless vs flapped surgery, tilted vs axial implant position, 1-stage vs 2-stage protocols, insertion torque, soft-tissue augmentation) showed no significant MBL effect across the pooled evidence. Grafting strategy mattered directionally: alveolar ridge/socket preservation at extraction reduced subsequent MBL, whereas ridge augmentation to accommodate standard-length implants produced more MBL than simply using a shorter implant. Implant length, implant type, and immediate-vs-delayed placement returned conflicting conclusions across the underlying SRs and could not be resolved by this overview. Clinically, the paper functions as an early risk-stratification map: modifiable/screenable patient factors (smoking cessation counseling, periodontal control) carry more consistent MBL evidence than most surgical-technique decisions.

## Key Contributions
- AMSTAR-graded umbrella synthesis of 41 SRs/MAs spanning implant, patient, and surgical-protocol factors affecting MBL — one of the earliest comprehensive risk-factor maps for non-prosthodontic MBL determinants.
- Identifies periodontitis, smoking, and male sex as the three patient factors with the most consistent statistically significant MBL association across independently pooled SRs.
- Clarifies that several surgical technique variables with intuitive appeal (flapless vs flapped, tilted vs axial, 1- vs 2-stage, insertion torque, soft-tissue grafting) lack proven MBL effect, redirecting clinical risk focus toward patient selection and extraction-site grafting strategy.
- Flags implant length, implant type, and immediate-vs-delayed placement as domains with genuinely conflicting SR-level evidence rather than false consensus.

## Methodology
Narrative overview of systematic reviews (umbrella-style; no new pooled meta-analysis by the authors). Databases: EMBASE, PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar, plus reference-list hand-searching, through November 2015. From 116 full-text-screened reviews, 41 SRs/MAs of moderate-to-high AMSTAR quality (≥4/11, reviewing ≥5 primary studies) were retained and organized into implant factors (12 SRs), patient factors (11 SRs), and surgical-protocol factors (20 SRs; some overlap across categories).

## Results
| Factor | Direction of effect on MBL |
|---|---|
| Periodontitis (vs periodontally healthy) | Significantly more MBL (6/6 concordant SRs) |
| Generalized aggressive vs chronic periodontitis | Significantly more MBL, long-term (2 SRs; WMD 0.28–0.43 mm) |
| Smoking (vs nonsmoking) | Significantly more MBL (4/5 SRs; +0.164 mm/year per Clementini 2014) |
| Smokers: maxilla vs mandible | Significantly more MBL in maxilla |
| Male vs female sex | Significantly more MBL in men (2/2 SRs) |
| Alveolar ridge/socket preservation (grafted) | Significantly less MBL vs untreated extraction sites |
| Ridge augmentation vs shorter implants | Significantly more MBL in augmentation group |
| Tilted vs axial implant position | No significant difference (4/4 SRs) |
| Flapless vs flapped surgery | No significant difference (5/5 SRs) |
| 1-stage vs 2-stage surgery | No significant difference (except in short implants) |
| Insertion torque (high vs conventional) | No significant difference |
| Soft-tissue augmentation | No significant difference (no long-term evidence of effect) |
| Implant length, implant type, immediate vs delayed placement | Conflicting results across SRs |
