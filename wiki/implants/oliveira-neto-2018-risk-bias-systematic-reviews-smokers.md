## Three-line Summary

Umbrella systematic review (2018, J Prosthet Dent) applying the ROBIS tool to 6 systematic reviews with meta-analysis on dental implant placement in smokers, searched through April 2017.

Only 1/6 (16.7%) of included meta-analyses had low risk of bias; 3/6 (50.0%) unclear risk; 2/6 (33.3%) high risk — the most commonly unmet ROBIS domains were study eligibility criteria and identification/selection of studies.

Most published smoking-implant meta-analyses at the time carried methodological risk of bias, so their pooled conclusions/recommendations warranted careful, not automatic, clinical adoption; abstract-only — full text not retrieved.

## Summary

**Abstract-only page — full text was not retrieved** (Elsevier paywall, no open-access PMC copy available). All content below is derived from the PubMed abstract and structured metadata only.

This umbrella systematic review does not generate new implant-outcome data; instead it turns the critical-appraisal lens onto the *existing* systematic reviews with meta-analysis (SR+MA) on dental implant placement in smokers. Using the ROBIS (Risk of Bias in Systematic Reviews) tool, two independent reviewers (kappa = 0.90, very good agreement) screened 2,539 initial records down to 6 eligible SR+MAs published in the preceding 11 years (i.e., roughly 2006–2017, search cutoff April 29, 2017). The headline finding: only 1 of the 6 (16.7%) scored low risk of bias; 3 (50.0%) were unclear risk; 2 (33.3%) were high risk. The domains most often failed were **study eligibility criteria** and **identification and selection of studies** — i.e., many of the underlying meta-analyses did not pre-specify sufficiently rigorous inclusion criteria or conduct sufficiently systematic searches.

The clinical takeaway is a caution flag rather than a new effect estimate: readers relying on smoking-implant meta-analyses (of that era) should treat pooled risk estimates and recommendations with more scrutiny than face-value citation would suggest, and future SR+MA authors on this topic should specifically tighten eligibility-criteria definition and study-identification/selection processes.

## Key Contributions

- Applies ROBIS — a bias-of-conduct tool distinct from AMSTAR-2 (which grades overall SR quality/reporting) — specifically to the smoking-implant SR+MA literature.
- Quantifies the proportion of smoking-implant meta-analyses (as of April 2017) that are trustworthy by ROBIS criteria: only 1/6 low risk.
- Pinpoints two concrete, actionable ROBIS domains (study eligibility criteria; identification/selection of studies) as the recurring weak points for future SR authors to address.

## Methodology

- **Design**: umbrella systematic review / methodological critical appraisal of existing SR+MAs (not a new outcome pooling).
- **Eligibility**: SR+MA articles on dental implant placement in smokers where implant survival/failure rate was the primary outcome and was reported specifically in relation to smoking status; duplicates excluded.
- **Search**: MEDLINE (PubMed), Scopus, Web of Science, LILACS, DARE-Cochrane; grey literature via SIGLE/OpenGrey, no language restriction; reference lists of included articles hand-screened. Last search April 29, 2017.
- **Screening**: 2 independent reviewers (title/abstract, then full text); a third reviewer resolved disagreements.
- **Risk-of-bias tool**: ROBIS (Risk of Bias in Systematic Reviews).

## Results

- 2,539 initial records → 6 eligible SR+MAs (all published within the preceding 11 years).
- Inter-reviewer agreement: kappa = 0.90.
- ROBIS distribution: **1/6 (16.7%) low risk**; **3/6 (50.0%) unclear risk**; **2/6 (33.3%) high risk** of bias.
- Most commonly unmet ROBIS domains: study eligibility criteria; identification and selection of studies.
- Bottom line: 5 of 6 included meta-analyses (83%) carried some risk of bias (high or unclear), so their conclusions/recommendations "required careful review" per the authors.

**Limitation of this wiki page specifically**: because only the abstract was retrievable, the identity of the 6 individual SR+MAs assessed, their domain-by-domain ROBIS scores, and any sensitivity analyses could not be extracted. This is a summary-level appraisal, not a domain-level audit trail.
