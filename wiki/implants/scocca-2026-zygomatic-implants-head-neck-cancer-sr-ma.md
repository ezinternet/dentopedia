## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 17 studies (294 head-and-neck oncology patients, 679 zygomatic implants) evaluating survival, timing-dependent failure, radiotherapy effect, and QoL of zygomatic implants (ZIs) for maxillary rehabilitation after tumor resection.

Pooled overall survival was 97% (666/679; 95%CI 94–99; I²=42.4%); failure was 2% for ZIs placed at primary surgery vs 9% at secondary surgery (p=0.4073, NS), and radiotherapy raised failure odds non-significantly (OR 3.42; 95%CI 0.97–12.06; p=0.0546), while PROMs (UW-QOL, EORTC QLQ-H&N35) showed satisfactory function even in irradiated patients.

ZIs are a predictable, minimally invasive option for extensive maxillary defects — placement at primary resection may be modestly favorable and rehabilitation remains feasible after radiotherapy, but high survival does not eliminate a substantial prosthodontic/maintenance burden.

## Summary

This is the first systematic review and meta-analysis to specifically evaluate zygomatic implants (ZIs) for rehabilitation of extensive maxillary/midface defects in head-and-neck oncology patients — a scenario where conventional bone grafting and standard implant placement are frequently contraindicated or technically infeasible after tumor resection. The ZI, anchored in the zygomatic body (Brånemark, 1998), bypasses the atrophic or resected maxillary alveolus and supports fixed or removable prostheses.

Across 17 studies (294 patients, 679 ZIs, mean 2.36 ZIs/patient, mean follow-up 37.3 months), pooled overall survival was **97%** with low-moderate heterogeneity (I²=42.4%). Failure was numerically lower when ZIs were placed at the time of primary tumor resection (2%) than at a later secondary surgery (9%), but the difference was not statistically significant. Adjuvant radiotherapy roughly tripled the odds of failure (OR 3.42) with borderline non-significance (p=0.0546), yet ZIs still achieved high survival in irradiated patients. Patient-reported outcomes across UW-QOL, EORTC QLQ-H&N35, and other instruments consistently indicated satisfactory oral function, mastication, speech, and psychosocial well-being — a meaningful advantage over conventional obturators, which are often poorly tolerated because of mucosal irritation, xerostomia, or ulceration.

The authors caution that high implant survival should not be equated with uncomplicated rehabilitation: altered anatomy after maxillectomy, flap reconstruction, scar contracture, and radiotherapy amplify prosthodontic complexity, hygiene access difficulty, and long-term maintenance burden. Biological (mucositis, peri-implantitis, oroantral/oronasal communication, chronic infection) and prosthetic (screw loosening, fracture, chipping) complications were reported across studies.

## Key Contributions

- **First outcome-level SR+MA on zygomatic implants in oncology** — until now the wiki carried ZI only as a sinusitis-risk comparator ([[sinus-lift/lateral/rocha-2023-sinusitis-rate-sinus-lift-zygomatic-ma]]). This page anchors the irradiated/oncologic-maxilla branch of atrophic-maxilla rehabilitation.
- Quantifies **overall survival (97%)** for ZIs in the most compromised host bed the implant literature covers.
- Provides a **timing signal** (primary ≈ 2% vs secondary ≈ 9% failure) that, though non-significant, aligns with a Butterworth 2018 HR of 2.25 for secondary placement — a practical argument for inserting ZIs during the ablative surgery.
- Confirms **radiotherapy is a risk modifier, not a contraindication** (OR 3.42, p=0.0546; ZIs still survive at high rates in irradiated bone).

## Methodology

- PRISMA-compliant, PROSPERO ID 1,245,372. Six databases + Google Scholar, 1950–2025 (last search 1 Dec 2025), plus snowballing.
- Inclusion: cohort (retro/prospective), case–control, cross-sectional, RCT with ≥4 oncology patients receiving ZIs for maxillary/midface reconstruction. Excluded case reports, abstracts, letters, technical notes, non-full-text, non-oncology indications. Mixed cohorts included only if oncology-specific data extractable.
- 521 records → 17 included (13 retrospective, 4 prospective).
- Single-arm proportion meta-analyses, random-effects, Freeman–Tukey double arcsine transformation; ORs for the radiotherapy comparison; heterogeneity by Cochran's Q and I²; R v4.4.2 (meta/dmeta).
- Failure defined strictly as complete implant loss; complications = biological/technical issues on retained implants.
- Quality: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (mostly low risk, NOS 6–8); leave-one-out sensitivity (robust); publication bias by funnel plot + Egger's test.

## Results

| Outcome | Pooled estimate | 95% CI | Heterogeneity / p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall ZI survival | 97% (666/679) | 94–99 | I²=42.4% |
| Failure — primary surgery placement | 2% (20/413) | 0–11 | — |
| Failure — secondary surgery placement | 9% (17/172) | 3–19 | primary vs secondary p=0.4073 (NS) |
| Failure odds with radiotherapy | OR 3.42 | 0.97–12.06 | p=0.0546 (NS); I²=0.2% |

- **Sample**: 294 oncology patients, 679 ZIs; mean 2.36 ZIs/patient (95%CI 2.04–2.74); mean follow-up 37.3 months (95%CI 11.1–63.6).
- **Largest study** (Butterworth 2018, 131 ZIs, up to 10y): Kaplan–Meier 5-yr survival ≥90% (12-mo 94%, 60-mo 92%); secondary-placement HR 2.25 vs primary (NS). **Lowest** (Schmidt 2004): 77% survival, 2/3 failures irradiated.
- **PROMs/QoL**: UW-QOL and EORTC QLQ-H&N35 most used; also MDADI, SHI, FACE-Q, LORQv3, VAS, OFS. Consistently satisfactory oral function and psychosocial well-being; Butterworth 2021 — 72% rated overall QoL good-or-better despite more dysfunction in the RT group. Prosthetic design influenced outcomes: hybrid prostheses higher for chewing/stability/phonetics; removable preferred for cleaning ease.
- **Complications** (per study, qualitative): peri-implant mucositis/peri-implantitis, oroantral/oronasal communication, chronic zygomatic infection, flap failure/necrosis, orbital breach, trismus, contracture; prosthetic screw loosening, fracture, chipping.
- **Publication bias**: survival funnel asymmetric (Egger t=2.59, p=0.0213) and timing asymmetric (t=4.46, p=0.0006), suggesting small-study effects; RT outcome symmetric (t=0.0177, p=0.9859).

**Clinical bottom line**: For extensive post-oncologic maxillary defects where grafting + standard implants are not viable, ZIs are a predictable option with ~97% survival and strong QoL gains over obturators. Favor placement at the primary resection when feasible; radiotherapy modestly raises failure risk but does not preclude rehabilitation. Counsel patients that survival is high but prosthodontic and maintenance complexity remains substantial.
