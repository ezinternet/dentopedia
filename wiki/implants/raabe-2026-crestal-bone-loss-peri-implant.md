## Three-line Summary

Single-center observational study, 147 patients / 233 tissue-level implants restored with single crowns (SC), splinted crowns (SP), or bridges (BR), evaluated at 10 and 25 years post-placement.

Peri-implantitis prevalence rose from 10→25 years (SC 2.3%→7.8%, SP 1.6%→6.3%, BR 0%→4.9%) with no significant difference between prosthesis types at either timepoint (p≥0.95); 25-year ΔCBL (SC −0.37±0.91mm, SP −0.25±0.85mm, BR −0.70±1.0mm) also did not differ significantly by prosthesis type (p=0.37), nor did prosthetic retention, marginal misfit, or emergence geometry.

Multivariate analysis found only incisor-region location, increased probing depth, and suppuration on probing significantly associated with greater bone loss (all p<0.001) — over a 25-year horizon in this tissue-level cohort, patient/site biology outweighed prosthetic-design factors.

## Summary

This 25-year single-center observational study (University of Bern) followed 147 patients / 233 tissue-level (TL) titanium SLA implants restored with single crowns (SC, n=129), splinted crowns (SP, n=63), or bridges (BR, n=41), re-examined clinically and radiographically at 10 years and 25 years post-placement. The primary question was whether implant-supported fixed dental prosthesis (IFDP) type affects long-term crestal bone level change (ΔCBL) and the prevalence of peri-implant mucositis (PM) and peri-implantitis (PI).

The headline finding is a null result for prosthesis type: peri-implantitis prevalence increased over time in all groups (SC 2.3%→7.8%, SP 1.6%→6.3%, BR 0%→4.9%) but with no significant between-group difference at 10 or 25 years (p≥0.95). Mean 25-year ΔCBL was numerically largest for bridges (−0.70±1.0mm) vs splinted (−0.25±0.85mm) and single crowns (−0.37±0.91mm), but the difference was not statistically significant (p=0.37). Prosthetic retention type (screw vs cement), marginal misfit, and transmucosal emergence geometry (angle/profile) were similarly non-significant for ΔCBL in multivariate analysis.

Instead, the multivariate model identified three significant, non-prosthetic drivers of greater bone loss: implant placement in the incisor region (vs canines/premolars/molars, p<0.001), increased probing depth (−0.16mm per additional mm, p<0.001), and suppuration on probing (−0.97mm, p<0.001). The authors interpret the TL implant's 2.8mm platform height and machined supracrestal collar as potentially buffering against prosthetic-design imperfections that matter more in bone-level implant literature — displacing the implant-abutment microgap away from the crestal bone may limit apical spread of inflammation even when misfit is present.

## Key Contributions

- One of the longest follow-up datasets in implant prosthodontics (25 years), directly testing IFDP type (SC/SP/BR) against long-term ΔCBL and peri-implant disease at tissue-level implants.
- Null result for prosthesis type across the full 25-year window — contrasts with shorter-follow-up and bone-level-implant literature showing positive associations between splinting/bridges and bone loss or peri-implantitis.
- Reframes the long-term risk hierarchy: at TL implants, incisor-region location, probing depth, and suppuration on probing outweigh prosthetic design variables (retention, misfit, emergence geometry) as predictors of bone loss.
- Supports a mechanistic hypothesis that TL implant geometry (elevated platform, machined collar) may mitigate prosthetic-design compromises that are more consequential at bone-level implants.

## Methodology

STROBE-guided single-center observational cohort; ethics approval KEK-BE-No. 2023-02279 (Cantonal Ethics Committee of Bern, Switzerland). Adult patients rehabilitated with tissue-level SLA titanium implants (May 1997–January 2001) were re-examined at 10-year (2010–2011) and 25-year (2024–2025) follow-ups, with both clinical and radiographic assessment required at both timepoints for inclusion. Final analytic sample: 147 patients, 233 implants (maxilla n=95, mandible n=138). Clinical measures (KMW, FMT, PD, BOP, SOP) were taken by two calibrated periodontists; radiographic CBL and emergence geometry were measured on panoramic radiographs by two calibrated examiners (ICC 0.91–0.96) using ImageJ. Statistics used robust linear mixed regression and multinomial-Poisson GLMM with patient ID as a random effect; a multivariate ΔCBL model was built via univariate screening (p≤0.10) with location as a nuisance random effect.

## Results

- **Peri-implantitis (10y→25y)**: SC 2.3%→7.8%, SP 1.6%→6.3%, BR 0%→4.9% — no significant between-group difference at either timepoint (p≥0.95).
- **Peri-implant mucositis at 25y**: SC 74.4%, SP 87.3% (non-significant trend upward), BR 80.5%.
- **Mean ΔCBL**: SC −0.37±0.91mm, SP −0.25±0.85mm, BR −0.70±1.0mm (p=0.37).
- **Significant multivariate predictors of greater bone loss**: incisor-region location (p<0.001), probing depth (−0.16mm/mm, p<0.001), suppuration on probing (−0.97mm, p<0.001).
- **Non-significant for ΔCBL**: IFDP type (p=0.17), platform height (p=0.17), emergence angle/profile, marginal misfit, KMW, FMT, BOP.
- **Radiographic misfit prevalence**: SC 10.9%, SP 23.8%, BR 14.6% — not associated with peri-implant health outcomes.
- Peri-implant sheathing/corticalization (a radiographic finding independent of clinical inflammation) occurred in 10.1–14.6% of implants regardless of prosthesis type; mechanism unresolved (proposed aseptic titanium-particle response or occlusal-trauma-related bone densification).
