## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (33 studies, 29,519 implants, more than 18,301 patients; PubMed and Cochrane search of 2020-2024 literature, mostly retrospective) on smoking and dental implant failure.

25 of 33 studies found a significant association between smoking and implant failure; 7 studies assessed early failure (e.g., OR 2.14; OR 2.07 early vs 1.48 late), and dose-response findings included OR 18.3 for more than 10 cigarettes per day; no pooled estimate was calculated.

Smoking appears to raise both early and late failure risk and reduction of daily consumption may help, but heterogeneity, single-database searching, and lack of confounder control limit the strength of the conclusions.

## Summary

Stiller and colleagues (Mainz) conducted a PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42024572472) systematic review of 2020-2024 literature on smoking and implant failure. Of 564 records, 33 studies (29,519 implants; more than 18,301 patients) were included. Twenty-five studies found a significant association between smoking and higher failure; eight (11,864 implants, 5475 patients) did not. Findings covered both early and late failure and suggested a dose-response relationship. No meta-analysis was performed because of heterogeneity, so there is no pooled effect size.

## Key Contributions

- Recent-literature update (2020-2024) complementing older pooled analyses.
- Reports early and late failure signals and dose-response data from individual studies.
- Notes that no e-cigarette study met inclusion criteria.

## Methodology

- PubMed and Cochrane search on 03.03.2024, terms (dental implant) AND (smoking) AND (implant failure) and MeSH; 2020-2024 only. The Limitations section states only one database was searched (internal inconsistency).
- Human studies with smokers vs non-smokers and at least ten implants; failure = implant loss.
- Risk of bias by Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (max 9): 1 study high risk, 4 some concerns, 28 low risk (NOS 7-8).
- Meta-analysis deliberately not done (heterogeneous designs, comparison groups, outcome definitions, follow-up, confounder handling, missing CIs). No formal certainty-of-evidence grading reported.
- Retrieved PMC text lacks the study table and figures.

## Results

| Domain | Finding |
|---|---|
| Overall | 25/33 significant; 8/33 not significant |
| Early failure (7 studies; "early" = 3-4 months to 12 months or until loading) | one study OR 2.140 (P < 0.001); Nagao et al. OR 2.07 early vs OR 1.48 late; 1.049 odds per pack-year (P < 0.001); 2 studies no association |
| Dose-response | >10 cigarettes/day: risk of loss multiplied by 18.3 (survival time -4.2%, P = 0.017); >10 pack-years HR 1.81 (P = 0.039) |
| Long term | 94% survival at 15 years in one study; another HR 36.35 (P = 0.001); OR 5.35 (years 1-4), OR 4.66 (after 4 years) |
| Large studies | 1420 implants/826 patients: smoking highest failure share vs diabetes (P < 0.05); 1820 implants/771 patients: 13.5% vs 4.4% (P = 0.027), 5.2 times risk |
| Brand / prosthesis | brand: 8 studies no difference, one found 2 of 5 brands lower risk; prosthesis type: one of three studies significant |

Non-significant studies were partly explained by few smokers (2.2% in Abrishami 2023), underrepresentation at follow-up, and few losses (7/254 implants in Zuffetti); Rotim (224 smokers) was null despite many smokers. Limitations: publication bias, heterogeneity, variable quality, single database, smoking frequency rarely assessed, confounding uneven. Text inconsistencies: "four of the 32 included studies"; all included studies dated 2020-2023.
