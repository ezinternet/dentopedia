## Three-line Summary

Historical (retrospective) observational study at the Mashhad Faculty of Dentistry implant department, Iran (March 2018 to 2020): 983 implants (56.5% in women, mean age 49.34 ± 13.67 years, mean follow-up 13.95 ± 5.88 months); early failure was defined as osseointegration failure (mobility and/or radiographic findings) within about 3-4 months of surgery, before prosthetic loading.

Early failure occurred in 42 implants (4.3%). Surgical incidents (inferior alveolar nerve exposure, implant fixture fracture; p=0.005) and implant loading time (p<0.001) were significantly associated with early failure, whereas sex (p=0.238), age (p=0.263), systemic disease (p=0.388), smoking (p=0.316), manufacturer (p=0.066), surface (p=0.886), jaw (mandible 4.7% vs maxilla 3.8%, p=0.511), implant dimensions and grafting (p=0.351) were not.

Only univariate chi-square/Fisher/Mann-Whitney tests were reported (no effect sizes or multivariable model, number of patients not stated, tables absent from the retrieved text), only 2.2% of implants were in smokers, and the shorter follow-up in failed implants was itself significant (p<0.001), so null findings and the loading-time association cannot be read causally.

## 세줄요약

이란 마슈하드 치과대학 임플란트과에서 2018년 3월~2020년에 시행한 후향적(historical) 관찰 연구: 임플란트 983개(여성 56.5%, 평균 연령 49.34 ± 13.67세, 평균 추적 13.95 ± 5.88개월); 조기 실패 (early failure)는 수술 후 약 3~4개월 이내, 보철 하중 전에 동요도 및/또는 방사선 소견으로 확인된 골유착 실패로 정의했다.

조기 실패는 42개(4.3%)였다. 수술 중 사건(하치조신경 노출, 임플란트 고정체 파절; p=0.005)과 임플란트 하중 시기 (loading time; p<0.001)가 조기 실패와 유의하게 연관되었고, 성별(p=0.238), 연령(p=0.263), 전신질환(p=0.388), 흡연(p=0.316), 제조사(p=0.066), 표면(p=0.886), 악골(하악 4.7% 대 상악 3.8%, p=0.511), 임플란트 크기, 골이식(p=0.351)은 유의하지 않았다.

단변량 검정(카이제곱/Fisher/Mann-Whitney)만 보고되어 효과 크기나 다변량 모델이 없고, 환자 수가 기재되지 않았으며, 흡연자가 임플란트의 2.2%뿐이고, 실패군의 추적 기간이 유의하게 짧아(p<0.001) 비유의 결과와 하중 시기 연관성을 인과로 해석할 수 없다.

## Summary

This Iranian single-center study (implant department of the Mashhad University of Medical Sciences dental school, March 2018 to 2020, STROBE, ethics-approved) analysed the early failure rate of 983 implants and its association with demographic, implant-related and surgery-related variables. The sample size was set by census. Implants were 56.5% (555) in women and 43.5% (428) in men; 217 (22.1%) were placed in patients with systemic disease and 22 (2.2%) in smokers. Mean follow-up was 13.95 ± 5.88 months (range 1-38).

Early failure was defined as failure to establish osseointegration (biological causes, e.g. peri-implantitis) appearing within 3-4 months of surgery, diagnosed from recorded clinical mobility and radiographs. Early failure occurred in 42 of 983 implants (4.3%).

Surgical incidents (inferior alveolar nerve exposure and implant fixture fracture during the procedure) were significantly associated with early failure (p=0.005), as was implant loading time (immediate versus delayed; p<0.001). The direction of the loading-time association is not given in the retrieved text beyond the Discussion statement that loaded implants carry higher risk. Non-significant: sex (higher failure in men, p=0.238), age (p=0.263), systemic disease (p=0.388), smoking (p=0.316), manufacturer (p=0.066; highest rate Biodenta, then Medentis and ITI), surface (p=0.886; highest in SLA), bone- vs tissue-level (3.9% vs 6%, no significant association), jaw (mandible 4.7% vs maxilla 3.8%, p=0.511), implant length/diameter, and grafting (p=0.351). Follow-up was significantly longer in implants without early failure (p<0.001).

## Key Contributions

- Reports a 4.3% (42/983) early implant failure rate in an Iranian university clinic; the authors describe it as the first epidemiological implant-failure study in Iran.
- Flags intraoperative incidents (IAN exposure, fixture fracture) and loading time as the associated factors, while patient factors and implant design showed no significant association.

## Methodology

- Design: historical (retrospective) observational, single center, Mashhad, Iran; records and radiographs of patients referred to the implant department over 2 years; census sampling.
- Variables: age, sex, systemic disease, smoking, implant system/manufacturer, surface, site, secondary surgeries from surgical complications, loading time (immediate or delayed), length, diameter, level type, grafting.
- Analysis: SPSS 22; chi-square, Fisher exact, Mann-Whitney; p<=0.05. No multivariable model and no odds ratios or confidence intervals are reported.

## Limitations

- Retrospective archived records, uncontrolled variables; bone quality and quantity, sinus-floor proximity and hormonal status were not examined (authors).
- Number of patients not stated; the unit of analysis is the implant, so clustering within patients is ignored.
- Only univariate tests; no effect sizes, so confounding cannot be adjusted. Mean follow-up of 13.95 months with a range of 1-38 months; follow-up was significantly shorter in failed implants (p<0.001).
- Only 22 implants (2.2%) were in smokers, so the null smoking result is underpowered (the authors say the same).
- Early failure window is given as 3-4 months in Methods, while the Introduction cites 3-5 months before prosthesis placement. The causal wording about loading time is unclear, since the direction is not tabulated in the retrieved text (tables were not extracted).
- The retrieved text starts at the Introduction, with no title, abstract or author list, so the title and author fields here are provisional.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

Within this single-center series, the avoidable risk markers were surgical incidents, especially inferior alveolar nerve exposure and fixture fracture, so careful surgical planning near the nerve and handling of the fixture is the actionable message; patient factors like age, smoking and systemic disease showed no significant association here but the study was underpowered for them, so pooled evidence should still guide counselling. The loading-time finding needs the original table to interpret (direction and numbers are not in the retrieved text).
