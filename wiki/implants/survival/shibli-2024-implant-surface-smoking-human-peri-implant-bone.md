## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of a single research group's (Guarulhos University) human histologic micro-implant studies: 90 edentulous individuals, 123 micro-implants, 7 surfaces, 8-week unloaded healing; only two of the studies enrolled smokers.

Treated surfaces gave higher BIC than machined cpTi (mean 21% cpTi vs 38.8% airborne-abraded/acid-etched; anodized 38.84%); in one smoker/non-smoker comparison BIC was about 50% lower in smokers (p<0.05), and on treated vs cpTi surfaces in smokers BIC was more than twice as high (p<0.001).

Smoking reduces early bone-implant contact and rougher/treated surfaces partly buffer it, but the review has no pooled estimate, is limited to one group and one method, and its mechanism text is largely extrapolated from other literature.

## Summary

This narrative review (Braz Dent J 2024) collects the human histologic micro-implant studies from one department (Guarulhos University) over 20 years. Micro-implants (2.5 mm x 6 or 8 mm; 2.0 mm x 4 mm in the last study) were placed distal to conventional implants, unloaded, and retrieved with a trephine after 8 weeks for histometry (BIC%, bone area in threads, bone density, osteocyte index). It is the wiki's only human-histology source on smoking and early osseointegration. It is not a pooled analysis.

## Key Contributions

- Human (not animal) histology of smoking effect on BIC, in unloaded 8-week healing.
- Surface-by-smoking pattern: treated surfaces retain higher BIC than cpTi in smokers.
- Explicit list of which mechanism statements are inherited from other literature (see Mechanism section).

## Methodology

- 90 individuals (54 women, 36 men; mean age 50.43 +/- 14.5 y), 123 micro-implants, 7 surfaces. Smokers: >10 filter cigarettes/day for >5 years; ex-smokers excluded; only two studies enrolled smokers. Diabetes, RA, immunosuppression, alcoholism, moderate-advanced periodontitis, head-neck radiotherapy excluded.
- Surfaces: machined cpTi, TiO2- or Al2O3-abraded + acid, anodized, CaP nanometric (Ossean), double acid-etched, laser-sintered + acids.
- Blinded calibrated examiners; Mann-Whitney, Wilcoxon, Kruskal-Wallis; p<0.05. Retrieval at 8 weeks; no occlusal loading.

## Results

- Losses: 15/123 micro-implants mobile at removal; 7 were cpTi in the posterior maxilla; 5 (all posterior maxilla) were in smokers. Marginal bone resorption was seen only around micro-implants in smokers.
- Mean BIC: cpTi 21%; abraded + acid 38.8%; anodized 38.84%; CaP-impregnated 43%; double acid 21.82%.
- Type IV bone (posterior maxilla near tuberosity): cpTi 13.06%, abraded + acid 21.81%, sintered 23.29% (p>0.05 vs abraded + acid).
- Smokers vs non-smokers (one study, figure caption cites Bezerra 2015, sandblasted acid-etched surface): about 50% lower BIC in smokers (p<0.05); drastic reduction of neoformed bone, inflammatory infiltrate and some osteoclasts in the coronal region. Treated-surface BIC more than twice cpTi in smokers (p<0.001).
- Conclusions as stated: treated surfaces beat cpTi; smoking reduced BIC around anodized implants; in smokers abraded + acid surfaces exceeded cpTi. The abstract also says anodized surfaces exceeded machined in smokers; the body conclusions do not repeat this.
- Smoker-by-surface BIC values are in the per-study Box 1, which was not in the retrieved text.

## Mechanism: human histology vs extrapolation

- Human histology (measured here): lower BIC, less neoformed bone, coronal inflammatory infiltrate and osteoclasts, marginal bone resorption in smokers at 8 weeks.
- Extrapolated from other literature (not measured in these studies): nicotine vasoconstriction and reduced blood/nutrient flow, carbon monoxide reducing oxygen transport and tissue hypoxia, impaired angiogenesis and leukocyte function, reduced osteoprotegerin, nicotine effects on collagen type I, alkaline phosphatase and osteocalcin, and heat/smoke local effects. Cited animal and other studies underlie these; the authors say the exact mechanism in peri-implant bone is not completely elucidated.
- Surface buffering explanation (better blood-clot stabilization and contact osteogenesis on treated surfaces) is the authors' interpretation, not tested here.

## Limitations

- Authors: single research group, single methodology; suggest proteomics and long-term clinical studies.
- Narrative review: no pooled estimate, no risk-of-bias assessment; micro-implants, unloaded, 8 weeks: no survival outcome, so it cannot show that treated surfaces reduce clinical early failure (the authors' statement that they could is an extrapolation).
- Few smoker-containing studies (two); small n; per-study data table not retrieved.
