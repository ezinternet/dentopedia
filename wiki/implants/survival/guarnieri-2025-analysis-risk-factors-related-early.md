## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-private-practice cohort (Treviso, Italy; 392 patients, 930 implants, 2000-2020): GEE analysis of patient-, surgical- and implant-related factors for early implant failure (before or at abutment connection).

Implant-level early failure rate was 5.8%; seven factors were significant (male gender OR 1.54, smoking, radiotherapy/chemotherapy history, posterior maxilla OR 2.26 [1.32-3.86], non-submerged healing, implant design, implant brand), but the retrieved text gives ORs for only two of them; smoking and non-submerged ORs are in tables not retrieved.

No smoking x healing-protocol interaction or smoker stratification was tested, so the paper cannot say whether smoking risk differs by submerged vs non-submerged healing; non-submerged is also confounded with tissue-level implant design and brand.

## 세줄요약

후향적 단일 개인의원 코호트 (이탈리아 트레비소; 환자 392명, 임플란트 930개, 2000-2020): 일반화추정방정식 (Generalized Estimating Equation, GEE)으로 조기 임플란트 실패 (지대주 연결 전 또는 연결 시점)의 환자·수술·임플란트 요인을 분석.

임플란트 단위 조기 실패율 5.8%; 유의 요인 7개 (남성 OR 1.54, 흡연, 방사선·항암치료 이력, 상악 구치부 OR 2.26 [1.32-3.86], 비매몰형 치유 (non-submerged), 임플란트 디자인, 브랜드)이나 확보된 본문에는 2개의 OR만 있고 흡연·비매몰형 OR은 미확보 표에 있음.

흡연 x 치유 프로토콜 교호작용이나 흡연자 층화 분석은 시행되지 않아, 매몰형 vs 비매몰형에 따라 흡연 위험이 달라지는지는 이 논문으로 답할 수 없음; 비매몰형은 조직수준 임플란트 디자인·브랜드와도 교락.

## Summary

This retrospective study of one Italian private periodontal-implant practice analysed 930 implants in 392 patients treated 2000-2020, with early failure defined as loss before or at abutment connection (implant-level rate 5.8%). A GEE model found seven significant factors, including smoking and non-submerged healing. Only two ORs appear in the retrieved text (male gender 1.54; posterior maxilla 2.26, 95% CI 1.32-3.86); the smoking and non-submerged ORs sit in tables that were not retrieved. The paper reports no smoking x healing-protocol interaction and no smoker stratification.

## Key Contributions

- Unselected private-practice cohort over 20 years, including patients with systemic disease.
- Independent-looking signals for smoking and non-submerged (tissue-level) healing in a single GEE model, but reported without ORs in the accessible text.
- Observation that Laser-Lok collar implants had lower early failure among tissue-level and bone-level implants (hypothesis-generating; brand and design confounded).

## Methodology

- Retrospective chart review; 392 patients, 930 implants; two calibrated data collectors (agreement >90%).
- Early failure diagnosed by persistent complaints, recurrent suppurative infection, mobility, or continuous radiolucency.
- Binary GEE (SPSS 19) "adjusted for confounding factors"; covariate list not given in text; no interaction terms described.

## Results

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Early failure definition | Before or at abutment connection; any of pain/foreign-body/dysesthesia, recurrent suppuration, mobility, continuous radiolucency |
| n | 392 patients, 930 implants; failures not counted in text (5.8% implant-level, about 54 implants if applied to 930) |
| Two-stage vs one-stage | 605 (65.1%) two-stage vs 325 (34.9%) one-stage |
| Smokers | 53 patients (13.5%) |
| Male gender | OR 1.54 (CI not in text); significant |
| Smoking | Significant; "high odds ratios in the multivariate analysis" (value not in retrieved text); adjusted GEE |
| Radiotherapy/chemotherapy history | Significant (no OR in text) |
| Posterior maxilla | OR 2.26 (95% CI 1.32-3.86), p=0.003 |
| Non-submerged healing | Higher early-failure risk than submerged (tissue-level vs bone-level); OR not in text; adjusted GEE |
| Design / brand | Cylindrical worse than conical; brand significant; Laser-Lok lower risk |
| Not significant | Diabetes, other systemic diseases, periodontitis history, short, narrow, torque <35 Ncm |

**Smoking x protocol:** the paper does not test an interaction and does not stratify smokers by submerged vs non-submerged healing. The wiki therefore still has no evidence on whether smoking risk differs by healing protocol. The non-submerged effect is also confounded with tissue-level design and brand (the authors' own Laser-Lok comparison).
