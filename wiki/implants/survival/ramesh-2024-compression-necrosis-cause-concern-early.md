## Three-line Summary

Case report (n=1) of early implant failure six weeks after placement — 33-year-old non-smoking, non-diabetic female, mandibular left posterior, 3.5 × 11.5 mm implant — with the bone harvested at re-entry sent for histopathology.

An undersized osteotomy (final drill 3.2 mm for a 3.5 mm implant), no pre-tapping, and 35–50 Ncm insertion torque in presumed D2-D3 bone produced histology-confirmed **aseptic** bone necrosis — bony trabeculae with absent osteoblastic riming, no viable bone anywhere in the specimen, osteocyte-empty lacunae — after bone loss reached half the implant length at 6 weeks.

Heat was excluded on the surgical record (copious saline irrigation) and infection/systemic causes on clinical and laboratory grounds (no inflammatory signs; CBC, ESR and viral markers all normal), leaving compression necrosis as the presumptive cause; with n=1 and no measured drilling temperature, misfit magnitude, or recorded peak torque, the attribution is a reasoned exclusion rather than a demonstrated cause.

## Summary

A 33-year-old non-smoking, non-diabetic female was treated with a two-stage implant in the mandibular left posterior region (#19) after a CBCT confirmed adequate dimensions (mesiodistal 11.03 mm, buccolingual 4.2 mm, apicocoronal 12.81 mm). Bone quality was presumed D2-D3, so an undersized drilling protocol was chosen: sequential drilling at 800–1200 rpm from a 2 mm pilot to a final 3.2 mm drill, then manual insertion of a 3.5 × 11.5 mm implant at 35–50 Ncm with a ratchet wrench until primary stability was obtained, without pre-tapping, with copious saline irrigation. Healing was uneventful at one week; at three weeks she reported intermittent dull pain with no clinical inflammation or infection; at six weeks she returned with pain and an IOPAR showing severe bone loss to half the implant length. After a five-day course of amoxicillin and metronidazole the site was re-entered: the implant was mobile with 50% bone loss and was removed with a fixture removal kit, and a single 0.3 × 0.15 × 0.05 cm non-viable bony sequestrum was retrieved. H&E sections of decalcified hard tissue showed bony trabeculae with an absence of osteoblastic riming, no viable bone anywhere in the specimen, and lacunae devoid of osteocytes. Blood examination at four weeks was normal in every parameter, including negative retroviral, HBsAg and HCV rapid tests. The authors reasoned through the alternatives: bone overheating during osteotomy, "a common cause of early implant loss" with necrosis at 47 °C for ≥1 min, was excluded because the osteotomy was prepared with copious irrigation; latent infection, implant contamination and systemic illness were excluded on clinical and laboratory grounds. What remained was over-compression — dense crestal bone, an undersized osteotomy, no pre-tapping, and torque transmitted straight into cortical bone — producing localised pressure necrosis and non-inflammatory bone death. The paper's contribution is that it demonstrates, in a single patient with histology, the reasoning that the wiki's differential overview had asserted was the only way to separate heat from compression, and it closes a gap the overview flagged: human histology for overcompression necrosis now has a second independent case, this one with the complete surgical record attached.

## Key Contributions

- **Biopsy-confirmed human aseptic bone necrosis in the early-failure window, with the surgical record.** Osteoblastic riming absent, no viable bone in the specimen, osteocyte-empty lacunae — in a patient with no inflammatory or infectious sign at any point between placement and failure, and with a normal systemic workup.
- **The heat-vs-compression differential performed on the surgical record, not the film.** Copious saline irrigation is the stated reason heat is excluded; D2 crestal bone, absent pre-tapping and the 3.2 mm-final-drill osteotomy are the stated reason compression is favoured. No held paper had applied this discriminator to an actual patient.
- **A concrete prevention list** drawn from the source's narrative review: precise surgical technique; avoid torque beyond the manufacturer's recommendation; reverse the implant a quarter turn after insertion; pre-tapping into dense bone before placement.
- **A necrosis event at 35–50 Ncm** — at or below the ≥50 Ncm threshold usually discussed as the harm boundary — in dense crestal bone without pre-tapping, which shifts attention from the torque number to what resisted it.
- **Bone-quality failure-rate context**: type III/IV bone reportedly carries 4.27–8.06% failure versus type I/II.
- **An operational caveat on the 47 °C threshold**: per Chacon et al., twist drills built with a relief angle stayed below 47 °C even at 15 mm depth after 25 uses — drill design, not use count alone, decides whether the threshold is crossed.

## Methodology

Single case report with a narrative literature review (48 references, no stated search strategy, no risk-of-bias assessment).

- **Patient**: 33-year-old female, non-smoker, non-diabetic, no other relevant history, written informed consent. Presented 2023-04-10; a successful implant placed 1 year earlier in the contralateral posterior region served as an informal intra-patient control for the risk factors considered.
- **Site**: pre-operative CBCT — mesiodistal 11.03 mm, buccolingual 4.2 mm, apicocoronal 12.81 mm.
- **Preparation**: two-stage plan; bone presumed D2-D3 → undersized drilling; sequential drilling 800–1200 rpm, 35–50 Ncm, from a 2 mm pilot drill to a 3.2 mm final drill; 3.5 × 11.5 mm implant inserted manually with a ratchet torque wrench at 35–50 Ncm until primary stability; **no pre-tapping**; copious saline irrigation; passive primary closure with 3-0 nonabsorbable silk.
- **Monitoring**: suture removal at 1 week (uneventful); 3-week recall with intermittent dull pain and no clinical inflammation or infection (warm saline rinses advised); symptomatic return at 6 weeks with IOPAR.
- **Failure management**: amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily plus metronidazole 250 mg three times daily for 5 days, then re-entry on day 5 with consent; biopsy; implant mobile with 50% bone loss; removal with a fixture removal kit; specimen fixed in 10% formalin.
- **Histology**: serial sections of haematoxylin- and eosin-stained decalcified hard tissue, examined at ×40.
- **Systemic workup**: blood examination at 4 weeks post-placement — Hb 13.8 g/dL, TLC 10,000/cumm, differential (neutrophils 69 %, eosinophils 3 %, basophils 0 %, monocytes 2 %, lymphocytes 28 %), ESR 5 mm/h, RBC 4.8 million/µL, platelets 296,000/µL, bleeding time 1 min 30 s, clotting time 3 min 36 s, RBS 113 mg%, and negative retroviral, HBsAg and HCV rapid tests — all within reference intervals.
- **Differential reasoning**: the discussion systematically eliminates overheating (irrigation), latent infection and contamination (no clinical signs), and systemic illness (normal bloods), then nominates compression on the basis of bone quality, drilling protocol and pre-tapping.

## Limitations

- **n = 1, no comparator, no denominator.** Nothing here estimates incidence or separates the effect of pre-tapping, undersized drilling, and bone density, all of which co-occurred.
- **Neither competing mechanism was measured.** Drilling temperature was never recorded, so heat is excluded by reported practice rather than by data; misfit magnitude, insertion strain and achieved peak torque were not instrumented — the "35–50 Ncm" is an operator-applied range by hand, not a recorded peak.
- **Histology is descriptive and non-specific.** Empty lacunae and absent osteoblastic riming establish necrosis but do not distinguish compression from thermal necrosis; the sequestrum's bacterial colonisation status — part of the thermal-failure fingerprint — is not reported.
- **Bone quality was presumed, not measured**, and is reported inconsistently (D2-D3 in the case management, D2 in the discussion); no radiograph-based density index is given.
- **The imaging endpoint is generic.** The 6-week IOPAR shows a severe angular defect — the non-specific picture of early non-integration — and contributes nothing to mechanism assignment.
- **One small fragment.** A 0.3 cm sequestrum from a single site cannot characterise the lesion around the whole implant, and no second specimen or per-implant sampling is reported.
- **Antibiotics preceded removal.** A five-day amoxicillin/metronidazole course before re-entry is not the sequence the compression hypothesis predicts; the paper does not discuss this.
- **Narrative review, not systematic.** 48 references without a search strategy, inclusion criteria or risk-of-bias assessment; the 47 °C threshold, the Chacon drill finding and the type III/IV failure rates are all cited second-hand.
- **Internal inconsistencies in the source** (logged as `source-data-issue`): the discussion states compression necrosis "usually occurs … within one month after implant placement" while this implant failed at six weeks; and it names 35 Ncm as the safe ceiling in dense bone while the case was placed at 35–50 Ncm, i.e. at or above that ceiling. Read the timeline figure as a general claim, not as this case's timing.

This is the worked clinical instance of that overview's central claim, that heat and compression cannot be separated on the radiograph and that "the surgical record is the only discriminator": irrigation excludes heat, bone quality plus pre-tapping plus osteotomy width indict compression. It also reinforces the overview's *negative* claim — the failure presented as a plain angular defect with nothing mechanism-specific on the film. The overview's gap statement is unchanged: still no held paper compares all three mechanisms, and this remains n=1.
- [[overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]] — **extends (typed)**. The first surgical-axis source paper in that overview: all 16 prior sources were host, habit, site or loading-protocol variables, none of them drilling heat, compression, torque or pre-tapping. Supplies the prevention levers (pre-tapping, manufacturer torque ceiling, quarter-turn reversal) and one more early-failure data point. n=1, so it cannot shift that overview's effect estimates.
- [[overviews/high-insertion-torque-primary-stability-crestal-bone-overview]] — **refines (typed)**. Necrosis appeared at 35–50 Ncm, at or below the ≥50 Ncm harm threshold the overview inherits from Marconcini/Lemos, because the site was dense crestal bone drilled undersized and left untapped. This supports the overview's own "do not ask how many Ncm is too many — ask what resisted the torque" framing and its dense-cortical-bone rule, while undercutting any reading of 50 Ncm as a safe line. Bounded by n=1, by post-hoc torque grouping in every human study that overview holds, and by the fact that this case's torques were never recorded.
- [[implants/peri-implantitis/abukraa-2025-peri-implant-bone-necrosis-clinical]] — **reinforces (typed)**. Independent corroboration of human biopsy-proven overcompression-related peri-implant bone necrosis, from a different group, with the same histological descriptors (empty osteocyte lacunae, nonvital bone fragments) reached without overlap in patients or site. Ramesh adds what Abukraa's abstract-only ingest could not: the full surgical record, the drilling protocol, and the explicit exclusion of overheating by irrigation. Both are single-digit series; neither can quantify the risk.
- [[implants/osteotomy-thermal/piattelli-1998-histologic-evaluation-eight-cases-failed]] — **refines (typed)**. The overheating counterpart, with a 6-feature thermal fingerprint present in all 8 retrieved implants: bone sequestra, absent regeneration, inflammatory gap, unorganized clot, compact mature bone, and secondary bacterial colonization. This paper's sequestrum under a compression attribution shows that sequestra and devitalized trabeculae are *not* specific to heat — feature 1 alone cannot assign a mechanism, and the assignment here rests on the surgical record. Note that this paper does not report bacterial colonisation status, so the comparison is partial.
- [[implants/isq/trisi-2011-high-low-implant-torque-histology-sheep]] — the strongest counterpoint: 110 Ncm assigned by design in dense sheep mandibular cortex produced no osteocyte lacunar emptying and no avascular zone. Read together, torque alone is not the variable; the operative context (no pre-tapping into human D2 cortical bone, 3.2 mm osteotomy for a 3.5 mm implant) is what Trisi's design held constant and this case did not.
- [[implants/isq/khayat-2011-clinical-outcome-dental-implants-high]] — human counterpoint: up to 176 Ncm with all implants integrated at 1 year and no significant MBL difference. Bounded by control n = 9, post-hoc torque grouping and a 1-year horizon.
- [[implants/peri-implantitis/piattelli-2003-clinical-histologic-aspects-dental-implants]] — the late mechanical endpoint: universal 600–1,100 µm fibrous encapsulation with no bacteria across 51 retrieved implants. Histologically distinct from this early necrotic endpoint, which is why the timing axis keeps doing the discriminating work in this literature.
- [[implants/coyac-2019-preclinical-model-links-osseo-densification-misfit]] — the rat µCT model of the same pathway (microfracture → osteocyte death → crater-like early failure) that this human case completes; abstract-only and without n or magnitudes, so it supplies direction only.
- [[implants/osteotomy-thermal/mohlhenrich-2015-heat-generation-drill-wear-dental]] — the measured-temperature backdrop (28.4–64.4 °C across 27 studies, drill wear raising temperature) behind the 47 °C threshold this paper cites when excluding heat; all in vitro bovine/porcine bone, no clinical outcome.
- [[oral-surgery/bertollo-2011-drilling-bone-practicality-limitations-complications]] — the origin of the time–temperature thresholds (47 °C/1 min, 50 °C/30 s, 56 °C) used in this paper's heat exclusion.
- [[implants/coyac-2019-preclinical-model-links-osseo-densification-misfit]] — also the flag for the practice whose ceiling this case marks: compaction drilling buys primary stability in low-density bone, and its destructive regime is the same pathway this human failure completed.
- [[implants/osteotomy-thermal/kosior-2025-histological-histomorphometric-implant-bed-preparation]] — the bed-preparation histology survey that would decide between this case's two candidate mechanisms had temperature been recorded; rotary drilling produced the most osteocyte damage and the lowest %BIC among five bed-preparation families, so an undersized non-rotary-prepped protocol is a harder case to attribute.
