## Three-line Summary

Retrospective practice-based cohort (one surgeon, Netherlands; 2019-2023): 101 single hybrid-surface (machined coronal, MSc) implants, all one-stage with a healing abutment, placed by flap in healed bone (n=49), flapless (n=28) or immediately after extraction (n=24); 12-62 months follow-up.

Six implants (5.9%) failed before loading and none after; 3/10 implants in smokers failed (30%) versus 3/91 in non-smokers (3.3%), while by protocol 3/49, 1/28 and 2/24 failed (not significant); bone remodeling was 0.74 mm at loading and 0.93 mm at final recall, unaffected by protocol or smoking (p=0.27).

The smoker figure rests on 3 events in 10 smokers and no smoking-by-protocol breakdown or interaction test is reported, so it cannot show whether smoking risk differs by healing protocol; no submerged (two-stage) arm exists.

## Summary

This single-surgeon, practice-based retrospective cohort followed 101 single MSc hybrid-surface implants for 12-62 months (mean 24). Every implant was placed one-stage with a healing abutment; the three protocols were flap in healed bone, freehand flapless, and immediate placement after extraction. Six implants (5.9%) failed before loading and none afterwards. Smokers had 3/10 failures against 3/91 in non-smokers, but with three events the estimate is fragile, and the paper neither stratifies smoking by protocol nor tests an interaction. Crestal bone remodeling was small and did not differ by protocol, implant type, gender or smoking.

## Key Contributions

- Only a small, directionally consistent smoker-failure signal (3/10) in a protocol-comparison dataset; it cannot answer whether smoking risk differs by healing protocol.
- All three protocols are non-submerged (healing abutment at placement); the wiki still has no submerged-versus-non-submerged smoking evidence from this paper.
- Protocol survival differences are not significant (flapless 96.4%, flap 93.9%, immediate 91.7%) and are confounded by surgeon site selection.

## Methodology

- Retrospective register review, one periodontist, one private clinic, July 2019-June 2023; 625 MSc implants screened, 120 single implants eligible, 101 analyzed (11 dropouts, 8 immediately loaded excluded).
- Protocols: Flap Healed + HA (n=49; full-thickness flap in healed bone); Flapless + HA (n=28; freehand, non-guided, crest not exposed); Immediate Placement + HA (n=24; after extraction). Immediate loading excluded.
- Bone level measured on periapical radiographs at baseline, loading and last recall (95 implants); Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney tests; no power calculation, no multivariable model.

## Results

| Group | Implants | Failed before loading | Survival |
|---|---|---|---|
| All | 101 | 6 | 94.1% |
| Smokers | 10 | 3 | 70% |
| Non-smokers | 91 | 3 | 96.7% |
| Flap + HA | 49 | 3 (derived) | 93.9% |
| Flapless + HA | 28 | 1 (derived) | 96.4% |
| Immediate + HA | 24 | 2 (derived) | 91.7% |

- Per-protocol failure counts are derived from the stated survival percentages and add up to 6; the paper's tables were not in the retrieved text.
- Smoking-by-protocol cell counts (which protocol the 3 smoker failures fell in) are not reported. No interaction test was performed.
- A simple Fisher exact test on 3/10 vs 3/91 (computed by the ingester, not the authors) gives two-sided p about 0.012; the authors report no test of this comparison, and the wide uncertainty from 3 events should be kept in mind.
- Bone loss 0.74 mm at loading, 0.93 mm at final recall (p=0.068); no difference by protocol (p=0.603 after loading), implant type, gender (p=0.557) or smoking (p=0.273).
- 54% gained bone after loading; 31% lost up to 1 mm; 4% lost more than 2 mm.
- The abstract's "irrespective of smoking status" conflicts with the 30% versus 3.3% split; the Discussion instead says smokers were too few for firm conclusions.
