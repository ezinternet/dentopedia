## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-institution study (Erciyes University Faculty of Dentistry) of 1228 patients who received 4841 implants between March 11, 2020 and April 01, 2022, testing whether COVID-19 and patient- and implant-related factors were associated with early implant failure (univariate and multivariate GEE logistic regression at the implant level).

Early implant failure occurred in 3.1% of implants and 10.4% of patients; smokers had higher odds than nonsmokers (OR 2.140, 95% CI 1.438-3.184, p < 0.001) and short implants (8 mm or less) had higher odds than long implants (12 mm or more) (OR 2.089, 95% CI 1.290-3.382, p = 0.003).

COVID-19 had no significant effect on early implant failure; the evidence is retrospective and based on the abstract only, so the early-failure time window, other covariate estimates and COVID-19 effect size are not verifiable here.

## Summary

Retrospective analysis from Erciyes University Faculty of Dentistry of 4841 implants in 1228 patients placed between March 11, 2020 and April 01, 2022, aiming to determine whether COVID-19 affected early implant failure and to identify patient- and implant-related risk factors. Early failure was 3.1% at the implant level and 10.4% at the patient level. In implant-level GEE logistic regression, smoking (OR 2.140, 95% CI 1.438-3.184, p < 0.001) and short implants of 8 mm or less versus 12 mm or more (OR 2.089, 95% CI 1.290-3.382, p = 0.003) were associated with higher risk; COVID-19 had no significant effect.

## Key Contributions

- Large implant-level dataset (4841 implants) on early failure in the COVID-19 period.
- Early failure reported at implant (3.1%) and patient (10.4%) level.
- Confirms smoking and short implant length as early-failure risk factors; COVID-19 was not one.

## Methodology

Retrospective study; recorded COVID-19, age, gender, smoking, diabetes, irradiation, chemotherapy, osteoporosis, implant system, location and implant characteristics. Univariate and multivariate GEE logistic regression at the implant level. Abstract-only: the early-failure time window is not stated.

## Results

## Limitations

- Abstract-only: time window of early failure, event counts, COVID-19 estimate and other covariate estimates not verifiable.
- Retrospective single-institution design; confounding possible.
- COVID-19 exposure ascertainment and number of exposed patients not described in the abstract.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

When planning implants, current smokers and short implants (8 mm or less) deserve extra attention for early (pre-prosthetic) failure risk, for example smoking counselling and careful case selection for short fixtures. A history of COVID-19 did not appear to raise early failure risk in this cohort, but the effect size is unavailable, so this is reassurance of limited strength.
