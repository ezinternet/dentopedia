## Three-line Summary

1:4 matched case-control study at Ewha Womans University Mokdong Hospital (Seoul, Korea) comparing 23 late mechanical failures (fixture, screw or abutment fracture) with 92 successful controls, matched for gender, age (+/-10 years), anatomical location and maintenance period (+/-2 years); masseter volume and cross-sectional area (CSA) were measured on 3D reconstructions of CBCT data.

In a multivariable generalized estimating equation model, larger masseter bulk was associated with mechanical failure (p < 0.001): odds rose 1.24-fold per 1000 mm3 of volume and 1.11-fold per 10 mm2 of CSA; AUCs were 0.846 (volume) and 0.871 (CSA), with Youden cut-offs of 31,272.56 mm3 and 691.30 mm2.

The authors call the cut-offs preliminary and exploratory, requiring prospective validation; this record is abstract-only, and nationality or ethnicity of participants is not stated (Korean setting only implied by the institution).

## Summary
Matched case-control study (Korean university hospital) of late mechanical implant failure, defined as fixture, screw or abutment fracture. 23 cases and 92 controls (1:4) were matched on gender, age, anatomical location and maintenance period; masseter volume and CSA came from CBCT 3D reconstruction. Larger masseter bulk was associated with failure (p < 0.001; OR 1.24 per 1000 mm3, 1.11 per 10 mm2), with AUC 0.846 and 0.871. Bootstrap AUC 95% CIs were 0.751-0.931 and 0.767-0.951.

## Key Contributions
- Patient-level muscle morphology (not just bruxism history) as a predictor of late mechanical failure.
- Preliminary thresholds: 31,272.56 mm3 (sensitivity 86.96%, specificity 77.17%) and 691.30 mm2 (82.61%, 79.35%).
- Contrast to early failure: concerns fracture-type failure after function, not failure before or soon after osseointegration.

## Methodology
Single-center 1:4 matched case-control; GEE multivariable model with confounder adjustment; ROC with maximum-Youden cut-offs; internal bootstrap validation. Setting: Ewha Womans University Mokdong Hospital, Seoul, Korea. Nationality/ethnicity of participants is not stated in the text; the Korean setting is implied only by the institution. Abstract-only: the time window for "late", the unit of analysis (patients vs implants) and covariates are not stated.

## Results
[확인] Masseter bulk significantly associated with mechanical failure (p < 0.001); OR 1.24 per 1000 mm3 volume and 1.11 per 10 mm2 CSA; AUC 0.846 / 0.871; thresholds 31,272.56 mm3 and 691.30 mm2; sensitivity 86.96% / 82.61%; specificity 77.17% / 79.35%; bootstrap AUC CIs 0.751-0.931 / 0.767-0.951. No early-failure rate or overall failure rate is reported in the abstract.

## Limitations
- Abstract-only; definition of "late", covariates and confidence intervals for the odds ratios are not verifiable.
- Only 23 cases; thresholds validated internally only, so discrimination is likely optimistic.
- Authors state findings are exploratory and need prospective validation in independent cohorts before routine use.
- Single center; generalization is unknown.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
Within the limits of this exploratory abstract, a large masseter on CBCT may justify more caution about mechanical load (implant diameter and material choice, prosthetic design, occlusal protection) when planning, but the cut-offs should not be used as a decision rule yet. This is a late-fracture risk marker, distinct from the early-failure risk factors in the Korean early-failure literature.
