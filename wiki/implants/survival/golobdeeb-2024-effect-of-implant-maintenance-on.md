## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort of 501 implants in 185 patients (1-7 years of follow-up) testing whether periodontal implant maintenance therapy (PIMT) frequency relates to peri-implantitis and early implant failure.

Peri-implantitis occurred in 6% (29) and early failure in 5% (22) of implants; peri-implantitis was 9% (n = 20) without PIMT vs 4% (n = 10) with PIMT (p = .0169), PIMT was not associated with early failure (p = .4372), but 25% of implants with peri-implantitis failed early vs 4% without (p = .0062).

Absence of PIMT was associated with peri-implantitis, which was itself associated with early failure; the authors favour 6-month maintenance intervals, but this is abstract-only retrospective evidence with unstated failure-timing definitions.

## 세줄요약

임플란트 501개 (환자 185명)를 1-7년 추적한 후향적 코호트로, 치주 임플란트 유지관리 치료 (Periodontal Implant Maintenance Therapy, PIMT) 빈도가 임플란트 주위염과 조기 임플란트 실패와 관련되는지 분석.

임플란트 주위염 6% (29개), 조기 실패 5% (22개); 임플란트 주위염은 PIMT 없음 9% (n = 20) vs 있음 4% (n = 10) (p = .0169), PIMT와 조기 실패는 유의한 연관 없음 (p = .4372), 그러나 임플란트 주위염 임플란트의 25%가 조기 실패 vs 비임플란트주위염 4% (p = .0062).

PIMT 부재는 임플란트 주위염과, 임플란트 주위염은 조기 실패와 연관; 저자들은 6개월 간격 유지관리를 권고하나 초록 기반 후향적 근거이며 실패 시점 정의는 확인 불가.

## Summary
Retrospective analysis of implant patients with 1-7 years of follow-up, with peri-implantitis and early implant failure as outcomes and PIMT frequency, implant characteristics and demographics as predictors. Data covered 501 implants in 185 patients. Associations were tested with Fisher exact tests (significance level .05).

## Key Contributions
- Links maintenance (PIMT) frequency to peri-implantitis incidence in a clinical cohort.
- Reports that peri-implantitis, not PIMT, was associated with early implant failure.
- Supports 6-month maintenance intervals (authors' conclusion).

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort, 1-7 years follow-up, 501 implants / 185 patients; predictors were PIMT frequency, implant characteristics and demographics; Fisher exact tests, alpha = .05. Setting/country and the definition (time window) of "early implant failure" are not stated in the abstract.

## Results
- Peri-implantitis: 29 implants (6%). Early implant failure: 22 implants (5%).
- PIMT vs peri-implantitis: 9% (n = 20) without PIMT vs 4% (n = 10) with PIMT; p = .0169.
- PIMT vs early failure: not significant (p = .4372).
- Peri-implantitis vs early failure: 25% of implants with peri-implantitis failed early vs 4% without; p = .0062.

## Limitations
- Abstract-only: setting, failure-timing definition, PIMT categories, covariate effect sizes and any adjustment are not verifiable.
- Univariate Fisher exact tests only as reported; confounding (e.g. smoking, periodontal history) not addressed in the abstract.
- Retrospective design; the authors note limitations without detailing them in the abstract.
- "Early" failure here arises within 1-7 years of follow-up and may not mean pre-prosthetic failure, unlike most of the early-failure cluster; the temporal order of peri-implantitis and failure is not clear from the abstract.
- 6% of 501 = about 30 and 5% = about 25 vs the reported 29 and 22 counts; percentages are rounded (22/501 = 4.4%).

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
Regular maintenance (about every 6 months per the authors) is associated with less peri-implantitis, and peri-implantitis carries higher failure risk, so recall systems are reasonable; do not infer from this abstract that maintenance prevents failure directly (p = .4372).
