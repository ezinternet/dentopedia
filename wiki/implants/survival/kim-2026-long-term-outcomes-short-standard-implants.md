## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort study of 84 implants (39 short 6 mm, 45 standard 8.5 mm) in 73 patients with posterior partial edentulism, mean follow-up 6.52 ± 2.81 years (up to 12.5 years).

Marginal bone loss (MBL) did not differ between standard (0.12 ± 0.07 mm) and short (0.05 ± 0.08 mm) implants (p > 0.05); crown–implant ratio (CIR) was higher in short implants (1.96 vs. 1.41, p < 0.001) but uncorrelated with MBL, while insertion depth was significantly correlated with MBL (r = 0.372, p < 0.001; linear mixed model p = 0.017).

Short implants achieved long-term marginal bone stability comparable to standard implants (100% survival both groups) when surgical/prosthetic protocols were meticulous; insertion depth — not implant length — was the parameter most associated with MBL, though the retrospective, unmatched-baseline design limits causal claims.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort study from a single Korean prosthodontic practice compares 6 mm short and 8.5 mm standard bone-level implants (5.0 mm diameter, internal hex, Osstem TS III) placed in premolar-molar sites between 2012 and 2018, with an unusually long mean follow-up of 6.52 years (up to 12.5 years). Across 84 implants in 73 patients, both groups showed 100% survival, and marginal bone loss (MBL) was small and statistically equivalent between groups (short 0.05 mm vs. standard 0.12 mm). Crown-implant ratio (CIR) was substantially higher in the short group (1.96 vs. 1.41) — as expected given the shorter implant body — but this did not translate into greater bone loss, staying below the 2.5 CIR threshold flagged as concerning in prior literature. Instead, using Spearman correlation and a linear mixed-effects model (patient as random effect to account for multiple implants per patient), the study identifies insertion depth as the only variable significantly associated with MBL (r = 0.372, p < 0.001; β = 0.1929, p = 0.017). The authors interpret this as likely reflecting physiological biological-width remodeling after subcrestal placement rather than pathological bone loss, since the absolute magnitude of MBL remained minimal (well under Albrektsson's 0.2 mm/year threshold) in both groups. The paper reframes the short-vs-standard implant question: implant length itself may matter less than how precisely the implant is positioned and restored.

## Key Contributions
- Long-term (mean 6.52y, up to 12.5y) direct clinical comparison of 6 mm vs 8.5 mm bone-level implants, with 100% survival and statistically comparable MBL in both groups.
- Identifies insertion depth (not implant length or CIR) as the factor significantly correlated with MBL, reframing the short-implant literature's focus from length to precise subcrestal positioning.
- Uses linear mixed-effects modeling with patient-level random effects to properly handle within-patient clustering (multiple implants per patient) — a methodological strength over simple group-mean comparisons.
- High measurement reliability (ICC excellent for CIR, good for emergence angle/abutment height) supports confidence in the radiographic outcome data.

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort, STROBE-guided, single-center/single-operator (>30 years' experience), IRB-approved. 90 patients screened, 84 implants (39 short 6 mm / 45 standard 8.5 mm) in 73 patients analyzed after excluding incomplete follow-up. Outcomes: MBL, CIR, abutment height, insertion depth, emergence angle/profile, survival/success (Albrektsson criteria), complications. Statistics: t-tests/Mann–Whitney U for group comparisons, Spearman correlation, linear mixed-effects models (patient random effect), ICC for intra-examiner reliability (18 implants), α = 0.05.
