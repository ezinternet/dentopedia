## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-institution Cox-regression cohort (Mayo Clinic, 2000–2014; 8,540 implants) analyzing first-year (early) implant failure against patient factors, preplacement surgical manipulations, and systemic conditions/medications.

362 implants (4.2%) failed within the first year at a mean of 129 (SD 96) days; bone augmentation only (HR 1.45, 95% CI 1.02–2.05, p=0.04), socket preservation (HR 2.67, 95% CI 1.33–5.38, p=0.006), and xenogenic material (HR 2.12, 95% CI 1.11–4.04, p=0.02) raised risk, alveoloplasty only at placement lowered it (HR 0.33, 95% CI 0.17–0.65, p=0.001), and any surgical complication was overwhelmingly associated with failure (HR 15.84, 95% CI 11.10–22.61, p<0.001).

After adjustment for age, sex, and implant era, no single or multiple medical condition(s) or medication(s) increased first-year failure risk; the authors recommend targeting surgical complications to reduce early failure, but abstract-only ingest prevents verifying model details and complication definitions.

## Summary

This single-institution retrospective cohort from the Mayo Clinic Department of Dental Specialties identified 8,540 dental implants placed 2000–2014 and tracked time to first implant failure using Cox proportional hazards regression. Early failure (within the first year) occurred in 362 implants (4.2%) at a mean of 129 (SD 96) days. The distinguishing feature is that **surgical manipulations**, not systemic disease, carried the signal: preplacement bone augmentation only (HR 1.45), socket preservation (HR 2.67), and xenogenic material (HR 2.12) each raised early-failure risk, while alveoloplasty only at placement was protective (HR 0.33). The strongest association by far was a first-year surgical complication, present in 318 implants (3.7%), which multiplied the hazard of early failure (HR 15.84, 95% CI 11.10–22.61, p<0.001). Critically, after adjustment for age, sex, and implant era, **no medical condition(s) and no medication(s)** increased first-year failure risk — a broad null that stands against many smaller single-factor cohort claims. The authors' clinical conclusion is direct: reduce early failure by targeting the incidence of surgical complications.

## Key Contributions

- Largest held test of **preplacement surgical manipulations** as early-failure risk factors, with HR, 95% CI, and p for bone augmentation, socket preservation, xenogenic material, and alveoloplasty.
- Quantifies **any first-year surgical complication → early implant failure** as the dominant association (HR 15.84, 95% CI 11.10–22.61) and names it as the prevention target.
- Reports a **broad null for systemic conditions and medications** after age/sex/era adjustment — a high-n cohort counterweight to positive single-factor systemic signals.
- Era-controlled design spanning a 14-year placement window.

## Methodology

- Design: retrospective cohort with time-to-event (survival) analysis.
- Setting/inclusion: Department of Dental Specialties, Mayo Clinic; consecutive adult patients with dental implants seen 2000–2014.
- Data: institutional database + individual medical records (demographic, surgical, medical data).
- Analysis: Cox proportional hazards regression; associations as hazard ratio (HR) with 95% confidence interval (CI).
- Outcome: first implant failure within the first year post-implantation.
- n: 8,540 implants.

## Results

| Outcome / predictor | Result |
|---|---|
| Early failure (year 1) | 362 / 8,540 implants (4.2%); mean (SD) 129 (96) days |
| Bone augmentation only (preplacement) | HR 1.45 (95% CI 1.02–2.05), p=0.04 |
| Socket preservation | HR 2.67 (95% CI 1.33–5.38), p=0.006 |
| Xenogenic material | HR 2.12 (95% CI 1.11–4.04), p=0.02 |
| Alveoloplasty only at placement | HR 0.33 (95% CI 0.17–0.65), p=0.001 |
| Surgical complication within year 1 | 318 implants (3.7%); mean (SD) 110 (114) days |
| Any surgical complication → early failure | HR 15.84 (95% CI 11.10–22.61), p<0.001 |
| Single/multiple medical condition(s) | No increase after adjustment (age, sex, implant era) |
| Single/multiple medication(s) | No increase after adjustment (age, sex, implant era) |

Most candidate predictors were non-significant on univariate analysis. No systemic condition or medication increased risk after adjustment.
