## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-centre cohort (Seoul National University Dental Hospital, Dept. of Periodontology; 151 patients, 490 Oneplant micro-threaded-neck implants placed 2006-2010, followed >10 years to December 2020) estimating Kaplan-Meier cumulative survival rate (CSR) and Cox risk factors.

10 of 490 implants failed (2.0%); overall 10-year CSR 97.9%; multivariate Cox identified only 2-stage surgery as a significant risk factor (HR 4.769, p=0.039, 95% CI 1.081-21.033); sex, age, diameter, length, fixture type, location, timing, GBR, sinus graft and restoration type were not significant.

Micro-threaded implants showed predictable long-term survival, but with only 10 events the Cox model is unstable (many degenerate CIs), smoking/diabetes were not analysed, and no non-micro-thread comparator existed.

## Summary

A Korean university-hospital retrospective cohort (Department of Periodontology, Seoul National University Dental Hospital) reviewing 151 patients and 490 Oneplant (Warantec) bone-level implants with a micro-threaded neck, placed 2006-2010 and followed more than 10 years (to December 2020). Survival was counted for implants rated success, satisfactory survival or compromised survival under Misch's (ICOI 2008) criteria. Overall 10-year Kaplan-Meier CSR was 97.9% (10 failures, 2.0%).

## Key Contributions

- Long-term CSR of 97.9% for a micro-threaded neck implant across external hexagon, internal octagon and one-body designs.
- Explicit failure-cause table: 4 fixture fractures (all internal-type; 3 at 4 years, 1 at 9 years), 3 peri-implantitis (9 years), 2 fibrous encapsulation (6-7 months), 1 unknown.
- 2-stage surgery as the only significant Cox factor (HR 4.769, p=0.039).

## Methodology

- Records and radiographs reviewed by three periodontists; inclusion required one or more Oneplant implants, >10 years follow-up after prosthesis installation, and adequate records. IRB approval, consent waived, STROBE.
- 2-stage surgery when insertion torque <30 N·cm, otherwise 1-stage; GBR, sinus augmentation and immediate placement as indicated.
- Kaplan-Meier CSR; chi-square and multiple Cox proportional hazards (backward stepwise; SPSS 25.0) with age, sex, diameter, length, fixture type, location, surgical procedure, timing, GBR, sinus graft and restoration type; p<0.05.

## Results

- Cohort: mean age 53 years (21-80), 60.2% male; 290 external (59.2%), 166 internal (33.9%), 34 one-body (6.9%); 62.6% maxillary, 87.1% posterior; 306 one-stage vs 184 two-stage; 12 immediate placements; 75 with GBR; 38 lateral and 8 crestal sinus augmentations; 89 single, 338 splinted, 63 bridge restorations.
- Overall CSR 97.9% (10/490 failed).
- By fixture type (Table 1): external 97.6% (7/290), internal 98.2% (3/166), one-body 100% (0/34). The text reports external 98.2% and internal 97.6% (p=0.670), the reverse of Table 1; the discrepancy is in the source.
- 2-stage vs 1-stage: CSR 97.3% (5/184) vs 98.4% (5/306); HR 4.769 (95% CI 1.081-21.033), p=0.039.
- Not significant: splinted crown HR 0.265 (p=0.094; 95% CI 0.056-1.255); female vs male p=0.141; length 13 mm vs 8.5 mm p=0.092; GBR p=0.313; crestal sinus approach p=0.105. Many CIs are degenerate because categories had zero events.
- Several failures had hypertension (5 of 10 rows in Table 2 list it) and one listed diabetes.

## Limitations

- Retrospective single-centre record review; marginal bone loss and success criteria beyond survival were not analysed.
- Smoking and systemic disease/glycaemic control were not analysed (authors' stated limitation).
- No non-micro-thread comparison group, so the micro-thread benefit is not tested.
- Only 10 events: wide or degenerate Cox CIs; the 2-stage association (lower CI 1.081) is imprecise and plausibly confounded by indication (low insertion torque, poor bone, grafting). The authors attribute it to secondary surgery in poor-bone sites.
- Requirement for >10 years of records implies possible selection/censoring bias (inferred from the inclusion criteria).
- "Compromised survival" counted as survival, which may inflate CSR relative to stricter definitions (authors' comment).
- The conclusion says other factors "were significantly associated", contradicting the Results (p>0.05); likely a typo.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

In this Korean tertiary-hospital cohort, a micro-threaded bone-level system performed with about 98% 10-year survival, with late failures dominated by fixture fracture (internal-type implants; authors recommend sufficiently wide diameters) and peri-implantitis around year 9. The 2-stage signal should be read as a marker of compromised sites (torque <30 N·cm, grafting) rather than proof that submerged healing harms survival; this is a single-centre retrospective observation with few events.
