## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study of 35 patients (133 implants, mean 13.2 yr in function) at a private clinic in Brazil evaluating peri-implant disease prevalence and risk/protective factors after ≥10 years of prosthetic loading.

Implant survival was 95.5%; peri-implant mucositis and peri-implantitis each affected 31.4% of patients; bruxism was independently associated with both peri-implant disease and early mechanical complications; insufficient keratinized mucosa, smoking, and osteoporosis were additional biological risk factors.

Abstract-only data; single private clinic with complete-records selection bias; cross-sectional design precludes causal inference.

## Summary

This cross-sectional study enrolled 35 patients with 133 dental implants (mean functional time 13.2 years) treated at a single private clinic in Brazil. All patients had implant-supported prostheses functioning for at least 10 years with complete clinical and radiographic records.

Clinical parameters assessed: modified plaque index (mpI), bleeding on probing (BOP), suppuration (SUPP), probing depth (PD), keratinized mucosa (KM) width, peri-implant soft tissue dehiscence (PISTD), and radiographic marginal bone loss (MBL). Systemic health, implant characteristics (connection type, loading protocol), and patient habits (smoking, bruxism) were recorded as covariates.

**Key findings:**

- Implant survival: **95.5%**
- Peri-implant mucositis: 31.4% (patient level), 32.3% (implant level)
- Peri-implantitis: 31.4% (patient level), 12.1% (implant level)
- **Biological risk factors**: insufficient keratinized mucosa, high plaque index, smoking, bruxism, osteoporosis
- **Mechanical complication risk factors**: bruxism, external hexagon connection, history of periodontitis, immediate loading
- Prosthetic complications were mainly mild and did not compromise survival, but were associated with greater bone loss, particularly in patients with osteoporosis

The study concludes that long-term biological and mechanical complications are influenced by systemic conditions, implant and prosthetic design, and patient habits — all of which should be integrated into treatment planning.

## Key Contributions

1. **Bruxism as dual-axis risk**: uniquely associated with both peri-implant disease (biological axis) AND early mechanical complications in the same cohort
2. **Real-world 10+ year data**: long-term outcomes from a private clinic setting with mean 13.2-year follow-up
3. **Keratinized mucosa**: insufficient KM identified as a modifiable risk factor amenable to pre-prosthetic soft tissue management
4. **Osteoporosis**: systemic condition linked to greater marginal bone loss, especially when prosthetic complications co-occur
5. **Connection type**: external hexagon associated with mechanical complications vs internal connections

## Methodology

- **Design**: Cross-sectional, retrospective records review
- **Setting**: Single private dental clinic, Brazil (UFSC-affiliated)
- **n**: 35 patients, 133 implants
- **Inclusion criteria**: prostheses in function ≥10 years; complete clinical and radiographic records
- **Clinical assessment**: mpI, BOP, SUPP, PD, KM, PISTD, radiographic MBL
- **Covariates**: systemic disease (osteoporosis, diabetes, etc.), implant features (connection type, loading protocol), prosthetic features, smoking, bruxism
