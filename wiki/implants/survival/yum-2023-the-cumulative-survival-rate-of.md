## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cross-sectional/cohort study at Seoul National University Dental Hospital (implants placed 2005-2015, at least 5 years of follow-up after prosthesis delivery) of sandblasted, large-grit, acid-etched (SLA) Dentium implants: 782 implants in 400 patients for survival and 372 implants for marginal bone loss (MBL).

Overall cumulative survival was 96.8% (25 failures; 17 peri-implantitis, 5 mechanical, 3 osseointegration failure); only implant type was independently associated with loss (double-threaded tapered vs dual-thread: HR 2.507, 95% CI 1.058-5.937, p=0.037), while diabetes (HR 1.962, p=0.131) and smoking (HR 2.050, p=0.333) were not significant.

Mean MBL after about 5 years of loading was 1.85±2.31 mm (mesial) and 1.59±2.03 mm (distal), greater in the maxilla and in augmented sites; the paper itself notes the small smoker group and panoramic-radiograph measurement as limits, so the null smoking/diabetes survival findings should not be read as proof of no effect.

## Summary

A retrospective study from the Dental Implant Center of Seoul National University Dental Hospital (Korea) analysed sandblasted, large-grit, acid-etched (SLA) Dentium implants placed between January 2005 and December 2015 in patients with at least 5 years of follow-up after prosthesis installation. Survival was analysed in 782 implants from 400 patients (mean age 66.22 years), and marginal bone loss (MBL) in 372 implants. Two designs were included: Implantium (straight, dual-thread, 446 implants) and Superline (tapered double-threaded body, 336 implants).

The overall cumulative survival rate was 96.8%, with 25 failures (peri-implantitis 17, mechanical complications 5, osseointegration failure 3). Failure was defined as removal of the implant for any reason; implants with bone loss but no removal were counted as peri-implantitis rather than failure. Failures occurred 1-16 years after placement (mean 8.26±3.53 years).

In univariate Cox analysis no variable reached significance. At the patient level, diabetes (HR 1.962, p=0.131) and smoking (HR 2.050, p=0.333) pointed toward higher loss but with very wide intervals (smoking 95% CI 0.479-8.782), with only 41 smoker implants (2 failures) and 125 diabetic implants (7 failures). In the multivariate model, implant type was significant: the double-threaded tapered design had HR 2.507 (95% CI 1.058-5.937, p=0.037) versus the dual-thread design, which the authors attribute partly to its later introduction and shorter follow-up (7.51±2.85 vs 8.84±3.87 years).

After about 5 years of loading, mean MBL was 1.85±2.31 mm mesial and 1.59±2.03 mm distal. Stepwise regression linked greater MBL to the maxilla (p=0.000 both sides), bone augmentation (distal p=0.004), diabetes (distal p=0.013), and splinted prosthesis (mesial p=0.032); lower MBL was linked to age over 60 (mesial p=0.001) and wide diameter (mesial p=0.014).

## Key Contributions

- Korean single-system SLA implant dataset with at least 5 years of post-prosthesis follow-up: 96.8% survival, peri-implantitis the dominant cause of loss (17 of 25).
- Reports both survival and radiographic MBL, with an itemised list of the 25 failed implants.
- Shows that, with few events, diabetes and smoking did not significantly predict survival, while the tapered design had higher hazard in this cohort.

## Methodology

- Retrospective cross-sectional design, single center (SNUDH), IRB approved, consent waived; STROBE reporting.
- Variables: sex, age, diabetes (taking anti-diabetic drugs), smoking, location, jaw, implant type, diameter, length, staged surgery, immediate placement, bone augmentation, prosthesis unit (single vs splinted).
- Survival: Kaplan-Meier with log-rank; univariate Cox then multivariate backward-stepwise Cox. MBL: panoramic radiographs (implant-abutment junction to first bone-to-implant contact), two examiners; Mann-Whitney/Kruskal-Wallis and stepwise multiple regression. Peri-implantitis radiographic threshold: 3 mm or more bone loss versus post-delivery film.

## Limitations

- Retrospective, single-center, no operator analysis; not randomized, so confounding by indication and by implant era is likely (tapered design introduced later, shorter follow-up).
- Only 25 failures, 41 smoker implants; wide CIs; null results for smoking and diabetes are underpowered and the authors say uncontrolled risk factors may mask effects.
- Survival defined as implant removal, so ailing implants and bone loss without removal are not counted as failures; follow-up censored at most recent visit; patients needed at least 5 years of follow-up, a selection effect.
- MBL measured on panoramic radiographs (lower accuracy than intraoral digital images); only 372 implants assessable.
- Text inconsistencies: 728 vs 782 implants (Figure 1 caption vs Results), sex rows in the table are implant counts, and univariate results are described as significant despite p>0.05. Table values were used here.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

In Korean hospital practice with SLA implants, about 97% survival at 5 or more years after prosthesis delivery is a realistic anchor, with peri-implantitis as the main route to loss, so recall and maintenance matter more than the choice between these two SLA designs. Patients with diabetes or smoking should not be told that this paper shows safety: it was underpowered for both, and pooled evidence on those factors should be used for counselling. Expect more bone loss in the maxilla and in augmented sites and plan monitoring accordingly.
