## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Clin Exp Dent Res 2019, University of Zurich), 34 studies screened from 3,997, Q&A format on why/how failed dental implants are removed plus reinsertion outcomes.

Counter-torque ratchet technique (CTRT) is least invasive and should be first choice; trephine burs are most common but sacrifice bone; 4 mm remaining osseointegration is the threshold for CTRT-alone (removal torque >200 Ncm → add trephine); reinsertion in previously failed sites survives 71–100%.

Clinical implication: choose the least-traumatic technique to preserve bone for reimplantation and let expected defect morphology guide the choice — but evidence is largely case reports (low certainty) and zirconia removal data are scarce.

## Summary

A University of Zurich narrative review that reframes implant explantation as five clinical questions. It answers **when/why** implants are removed, **how** (technique catalog ranked by invasiveness), **how implant material** (titanium vs zirconia) changes the calculus, **what happens to the bone** afterward, and **what to consider when reinserting** a new implant. It is the first review to explicitly address failing/ailing zirconia implants. Because the underlying evidence is almost entirely case reports and small series, the recommendations are pragmatic rather than high-certainty — but the paper is the most usable chairside decision framework for explantation.

## Key Contributions

1. **Technique ranking by invasiveness** with quantitative torque and bone-loss data, placing **CTRT first** and trephine burs as the fallback.
2. A **CTRT-alone threshold** (≈4 mm remaining osseointegration; removal torque >200 Ncm → combine with trephine/piezo).
3. First synthesis of **zirconia** removal considerations — CTRT likely contraindicated due to low fracture toughness.
4. Consolidated **reinsertion survival** (71–100%) plus replacement-implant sizing/surface strategy.

## Methodology

Narrative Q&A review. Databases: PubMed, MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane (up to 2018). Two reviewers screened titles/abstracts (Cohen's κ = 0.65), senior author adjudicated; 3,997 screened → 34 included. Inclusion: RCTs, case series, retrospective studies, case reports, systematic and narrative reviews. Exclusion: non-osseointegrated implants, non-screw implants, orthodontic implants, animal studies. No meta-analysis or formal risk-of-bias appraisal.

## Results

**Removal techniques, least → most bone-destructive**

| Technique | Parameters | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Extraction set (elevator/forceps) | mobile / minimal residual BIC | simplest; rotating movement often unnecessary |
| **Counter-torque ratchet (CTRT)** | counterclockwise torque; internal > external connection (leverage) | **least traumatic → first choice**; square threads / parallel bodies / apical antirotation are harder to unwind |
| Reverse screw technique (RST) | screw grips fractured implant, then counter-torque | for fractured/damaged implants; saline-cool during first unscrew |
| Piezosurgery | 24,000–29,500 Hz, intermittent + saline | less traumatic than trephine; spares nerves/vessels; pacemaker caution |
| Laser (Er,Cr:YSGG) | hydrokinetic cut, no thermal damage | removed ~½ the bone vs trephine but >2× slower (44 vs 17 s); good hemostasis |
| Trephine bur | 1,200–1,500 rpm, max water cooling; diameter just > implant | most common but sacrifices bone; mandible-fracture/osteomyelitis reports; CAD/CAM guide → less invasive |
| Electrosurgery (thermo-explantation) | monopolar 15 s → CTRT at 30 N after 1 wk | experimental; osteonecrosis risk (bone necrosis ≥47 °C for >1 min) |

**Torque evidence (Anitua, BTI kit):** 78/91 implants removed by CTRT alone; 139/158 non-mobile implants removed at 146 Ncm without burs; trephine indicated when removal torque >200 Ncm, or for fractured implants/prosthetic components. Removal torque lowest in plasma-sprayed, highest in acid-etched + sandblasted surfaces.

**Failure epidemiology:** overall failure 6.36% (642/10,096), 49% within the first year; peri-implantitis leads late failure (81.9–82.9%); implant fracture 0.4–1%; prosthetically inadequate malpositioning ~10%; inexperienced-surgeon failure 12.2% vs 2.4% experienced (OR 2.18).

**Zirconia:** BIC (25–88%) and removal torque (9–78 N) similar to titanium, but fracture toughness far lower (4–18 vs 77 MPa·m^0.5) → CTRT likely fractures the implant; feasible only with extensive surrounding resorption. Thermal removal questionable (zirconia insulates); no direct data.

**Bone fate:** implants lack bundle bone, so post-explantation defects differ from extraction sockets; Schwarz peri-implantitis defect classes — circumferential Class Ie (55.3%) behaves as self-containing three-wall defect, favorable for GBR. Expected defect morphology should guide technique choice.

**Reinsertion:** survival 88.7% (previously failed sites), 85% (twice-failed); 2nd attempt 71–100%, 3rd attempt 50–100%; success 90–100% (few reported). Prefer larger-diameter, rough-surface replacement for primary stability; sinus-grafted sites 92–100%; immediate vs delayed reimplantation equivalent given adequate bone and no infection; extended defects need buccal-plate GBR first. "Cluster effect" (simultaneous multi-implant failure) hints at systemic/genetic factors to control before re-surgery.
