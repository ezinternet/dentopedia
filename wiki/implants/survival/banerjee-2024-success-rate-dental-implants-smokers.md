## Three-line Summary

Umbrella systematic review (17 SRs, 10 with meta-analyses, 2006-2024, PROSPERO CRD42024575748) assessing the strength of evidence for dental implant success/osseointegration in smokers vs non-smokers, quality-appraised with AMSTAR 2.

AMSTAR 2 scoring: only 1/17 reviews rated "high quality," 7 "moderate," 7 "low," 2 "critically low"; 15/17 reviews reported statistically significant smoking-implant failure/marginal-bone-loss associations, with a dose-response signal at ≥10 cigarettes/day.

The overall evidence base supports lower success and higher osseointegration failure in smokers, but the methodological quality ceiling is low (only 1 high-quality SRMA) — smoking is not an absolute contraindication, but informed consent and cessation counseling remain the clinically prudent response.

## Summary

This umbrella review (a "review of reviews") tackles a different question than the individual effect-size SRMAs already in the wiki: not "how much does smoking raise implant failure risk," but "how trustworthy is the body of systematic-review evidence making that claim." Searching five databases from 2006 to August 2024, the authors identified 2,563 candidate SRMAs, narrowed to 364 after deduplication/screening, and ultimately included 17 systematic reviews (10 with meta-analyses) evaluating implant failure, marginal bone loss (MBL), postoperative infection, and peri-implant/osseointegration outcomes in smokers vs non-smokers. Each included review was independently appraised with AMSTAR 2, a 16-item critical-appraisal checklist, yielding a quality tier for every component SR.

The headline result is two-sided. On direction and consistency of effect: 15 of 17 reviews reported statistically significant associations between smoking and worse implant outcomes, with reported risk/odds ratios clustering around 1.87-2.92 across reviews, and a dose-response relationship (elevated failure risk at ≥10 cigarettes/day, per the included Naseri 2020 review) reinforcing biological plausibility. On evidence quality: only 1 of the 17 reviews (Moraschini 2015) achieved a "high" AMSTAR 2 score; 7 were "moderate," 7 "low," and 2 "critically low." The umbrella review's own conclusions are therefore bounded by this quality ceiling — a textbook illustration of how a highly consistent direction-of-effect can still rest on a shaky methodological foundation across the secondary literature.

## Key Contributions

- First umbrella review focused specifically on smoking and dental implant success/osseointegration, spanning nearly two decades (2006-2024) of SR/SRMA output.
- AMSTAR 2 quality-stratifies 17 component SRs, exposing that the field's "consistent significant effect" narrative rests on a mostly low/moderate-quality evidence base (only 1/17 high quality).
- Assembles a side-by-side effect-size table across reviews (RR/OR range ~1.87-2.92, most 95% CIs excluding 1), functioning as a meta-evidence map rather than a single re-pooled estimate.
- Reiterates the mechanistic chain underlying the association: TNF-alpha/IL-1b elevation in current smokers, nicotine-suppressed osteoblast gene expression, oxidative stress, and RANKL-RANK-OPG pathway disruption impairing bone healing/remodeling.
- Confirms and cross-validates the dose-response signal (≥10 cigarettes/day threshold) already reported in [[implants/naseri-2020-smoking-levels-implant-failure-sr-ma]].

## Methodology

Umbrella review per PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42024575748). Searched PubMed/Medline, Google Scholar, ScienceDirect, Cochrane Library, and EBSCOhost (Jan 2006-Aug 2024) using MeSH combinations of implant/smoking/tobacco/success-failure/bone-loss/osseointegration terms. Two reviewers independently screened titles/abstracts; three reviewers screened full texts; disagreements resolved by a fourth reviewer. Inclusion: English-language SRs and SR+MAs (2006-Aug 2024) reporting success rate/osseointegration outcomes with qualitative or quantitative synthesis. Excluded literature/scoping reviews, cohort studies, case reports/series. Quality appraisal used AMSTAR 2 (16 items, yes=1/partial=0.5/no=0), summed to critically low (1-4) / low (5-8) / moderate (9-12) / high (13-16) tiers, scored independently by three reviewers with fourth-reviewer consensus for disagreements.

## Results

Of 2,563 SRMAs identified, 364 remained after deduplication/title-abstract screening; 24 met full eligibility criteria on full-text review, and 17 were finally included (7 excluded for unavailable full text) — 10 of the 17 performed meta-analyses. AMSTAR 2 scores ranged from 4 (Hinode 2006, Turri 2016 at 4.5 — both "critically low") to 16 (Moraschini 2015, the sole "high"-quality review); 7 reviews scored "moderate" (9-12: Farronato 2022, Mustapha 2022, Strietzel 2007, Ismail 2021, Chambrone 2015, Akel 2019, Ghanem 2017) and 7 scored "low" (5-8: Alfadda 2018, Chrcanovic 2015, Naseri 2020, Akram 2018, Telleman 2011, Al-Qamshaa 2022, Caggiano 2022).

Reported meta-analytic effect sizes (RR/OR, 95% CI) clustered consistently above 1: Mustapha 2022 RR 2.91 (2.06-4.00); Alfadda 2018 RR 2.92 (1.76-4.83); Chrcanovic 2015 RR 2.23 (1.96-2.93); Naseri 2020 RR 2.45 (1.42-4.22); Akel 2019 RR 2.24 (1.9-2.64); Hinode 2006 RR 2.17 (1.67-2.83); Strietzel 2007 RR 2.38 (1.93-2.93); Chambrone 2015 RR 1.87 (1.35-2.58); Akram 2018 RR 2.49 (1.01-3.97, waterpipe smokers); Moraschini 2015 RR ~2.53 (marginal bone loss/failure). [확인] 15/17 reviews reported statistically significant smoking-implant outcome associations; 2 did not report p-values. Dose-response: Naseri 2020 found significantly elevated failure risk at ≥10 cigarettes/day (p=0.001) vs non-smokers, without a further significant step-up between 15 and 20 cigarettes/day. Overall inferred strength of evidence: "high" in 1 review, "moderate" in 7, "low" in 9.
