## Three-line Summary
Cohort study (n=92 implants without primary stability, screened from 2,400; conventional healed-site placement, ≥12-mo follow-up) classifying absent primary stability as B (light rotation with resistance), C (rotation without resistance), D (rotation + lateral oscillation).
Only 3 implants (3.26%) were lost — all in group C (7.89% of that group); stability grade (p=0.149), age, brand, and implant size were not statistically associated with survival.
Clinical implication: absent primary stability at conventional placement does not statistically predict implant loss, though full free rotation (C) trended toward greater early loss; larger samples are needed.

## Summary
This retrospective cohort study from the Complutense University of Madrid evaluated whether the absence of primary stability at placement influences implant survival, using a unique dataset: of 2,400 implants placed with a standardized protocol, 92 were documented as lacking primary stability and followed for at least 12 months. Absence grades followed Rodrigo et al.'s clinical criteria, from light rotation with resistance (B) to rotation plus lateral oscillation (D). Only 3 of the 92 implants (3.26%) were lost, and all three belonged to group C (implant rotates without resistance), representing 7.89% of that subgroup. No statistically significant associations were found between survival and stability grade (p=0.149), patient age, implant brand, or implant size — although the corrected residual analysis flagged group C as experiencing loss statistically greater than expected (7.9% vs 3.3% expected). The authors conclude that poor primary stability is not statistically significant in implant loss under the studied characteristics, framing the result as evidence that primary stability, while desirable, is not an absolute prerequisite for osseointegration under conventional (non-immediate) loading protocols.

## Key Contributions
- First to directly evaluate *lack of* primary stability (not its degree) against implant survival in conventional loading.
- Quantified the risk: 3.26% loss among 92 implants without primary stability — comparable to general implant survival expectations, and all losses confined to the free-rotation group (C).
- Identified group C (rotation without resistance) as the clinically meaningful subgroup tending toward early loss, distinguishing it from B (light rotation) and D (rotation + oscillation).

## Methodology
- **Design**: Retrospective cohort (single institution, standardized protocol)
- **Sample**: 2,400 implants placed Sept 2011–July 2016; 92 with documented absence of primary stability and ≥12-month follow-up
- **Exclusions**: incomplete records, irradiation, uncontrolled diabetes/periodontal disease, deviated drilling sequences; no post-extraction (immediate) placements
- **Stability classification (Rodrigo et al.)**: B = light rotation with resistance; C = rotation without resistance; D = rotation + lateral oscillation
- **Outcomes**: implant survival; factors — age, sex, tobacco, ASA, GBR, bone grafts, residual bone height, region, implant adjacency, length, diameter, design, manufacturer
- **Statistics**: frequency analysis, K-S normality, Fisher exact / chi-square, ANOVA, Student t (SPSS 22.0)

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Implants without primary stability | 92 / 2,400 (3.8%) |
| Stability-grade distribution | B 49 (53.3%), C 38 (41.3%), D 5 (5.4%) |
| Overall implant loss | 3/92 (3.26%) |
| Loss by grade | C only: 3/38 (7.89%); B 0/49; D 0/5 |
| Grade vs early loss | p=0.149 (NS); C corrected residual 7.9% vs 3.3% expected |
| Age vs loss | p=0.658 (NS) |
