## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 4 prior systematic reviews/meta-analyses (156 records screened, PRISMA) on dental implant success in type 2 diabetes patients, stratifying results by glycemic control (HbA1c).

Well-controlled diabetes (HbA1c <8%) does not compromise implant survival — 96.1–97.3% at 1 year and 87.3–96.1% at 5 years, with no survival difference versus non-diabetics (Andrade RR = 1.00, 95% CI 0.96–1.04); poor control (HbA1c >8%) worsens marginal bone loss and probing depth in a dose-response manner (Lv 2022).

Diabetes is not a contraindication to implants when HbA1c <8% (ideally <7%); key limitation is qualitative synthesis of heterogeneous SR/MAs without de novo statistical pooling.

## 세줄요약

당뇨 임플란트 성공률을 당화혈색소(Hemoglobin A1c, HbA1c) 조절 수준으로 층화해 본 기존 시스템틱 고찰·메타분석 4편을 종합한 PRISMA 기반 고찰(스크리닝 156건, 포함 4편).

조절 양호 제2형 당뇨병(Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus, T2DM)(HbA1c <8%)은 임플란트 생존율을 떨어뜨리지 않음 — 1년 96.1–97.3%, 5년 87.3–96.1%, 비당뇨 대비 생존 차이 없음(상대위험도(Relative Risk, RR) 1.00, 95% CI 0.96–1.04); 조절 불량(HbA1c >8%)은 변연골소실(Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)·탐침깊이(Probing Depth, PD)를 용량-반응적으로 악화.

## Summary

This umbrella-style systematic review consolidates four prior systematic reviews and meta-analyses (Schimmel 2018, Andrade 2022, Lv 2022, de Oliveira-Neto 2019) on dental implant success in type 2 diabetes, using HbA1c strata (well-controlled ≤7%, poorly controlled >8%) as the organizing axis. Implant survival is consistently high in well-controlled diabetes (96.1–97.3% at 1 year, 87.3–96.1% at 5 years, comparable to non-diabetics; Andrade RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.96–1.04). The adverse effects of diabetes concentrate on peri-implant health rather than survival: marginal bone loss and probing depth worsen in a dose-response manner as HbA1c rises beyond ~8%, with parallel elevation of inflammatory/bone-resorption biomarkers (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-8, RANKL). Clinical implication: maintain HbA1c <8% (ideally <7%), adhere strictly to protocols, and treat diabetic implant patients interdisciplinary.

## Key Contributions

- Frames glycemic control (HbA1c), not diabetes status per se, as the operative variable in diabetic implant success — with explicit thresholds (well-controlled ≤7%, poorly controlled >8%).
- Consolidates a distinct dose-response evidence thread: peri-implant MBL and PD worsen progressively as HbA1c rises (worsening seen >8%, Lv 2022 DRMA).
- Adds the biomarker mechanism linking poor control to bone loss (higher IL-6, TNF-α, IL-8, RANKL in diabetic/hyperglycemic patients, p = 0.01).
- Reaffirms in an SR-of-SRs frame that diabetes is not a contraindication to implants when well managed — matching the independent wagner/al-ansari conclusions already held.

## Methodology

PRISMA systematic review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses, with qualitative narrative synthesis (no de novo statistical pooling). Databases: PubMed (41), Cochrane (31), Embase (31), Scopus (20), Web of Science (33) — 156 records, 35 duplicates removed, 121 screened, 52 excluded, 28 unavailable, 41 assessed, 37 excluded, 4 included. Eligibility required T2DM implant patients, SR/MA design, HbA1c-based control stratification, and peri-implant health outcomes. Quality scored with AMSTAR 2 (2 high, 1 moderate, 1 moderate-to-high).

## Results

| Source study | Design / sample | Implant survival | Peri-implant outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schimmel 2018 | SR+MA; geriatric (≥75 y) & systemic conditions, 60 studies (7 for MA) | 97.3% 1-y (95% CI 94.3–98.7); 96.1% 5-y (95% CI 87.3–98.9) | T2DM/Parkinson high survival; cancer lower (radiotherapy) |
| Andrade 2022 | SR+MA; T2DM immediately loaded (IL), 7 studies (5 for MA) | RR 1.00 vs non-diabetic (95% CI 0.96–1.04, p=0.91); no difference even with poor control | MBL MD −0.08 mm (95% CI −0.25–0.08, p=0.33) IL vs conventional |
| Lv 2022 | SR+MA + dose-response (DRMA); 634 participants, 10 studies, ≤6 y follow-up | Not compromised at HbA1c <10% | AGEs in PICF p<0.01; ISQ p=0.04; BOP p<0.01; PD p=0.01; MBL p<0.01; PD/MBL worsen with HbA1c >8% |
| de Oliveira-Neto 2019 | Overview of 8 SRs (6 moderate, 2 high AMSTAR) | 31.8–100% across reviews; 4 meta-analyses: no DM effect on survival | MBL statistically significant but clinically irrelevant |

Overall framing: well-managed diabetes (HbA1c <8%) → survival 96.1–97.3% (1 y) / 87.3–96.1% (5 y), comparable to non-diabetics; poorly controlled → worse MBL/PD (mean difference −0.08 mm overall, dose-response gradient).
