## Three-line Summary

Systematic review with meta-analysis of 20 observational studies (13 in MA) examining the relationship between bruxism and mechanical complications in implant-supported prostheses, searched from 1984 onward across 6 databases.
Overall unspecific mechanical failure OR=6.10; subgroup ORs: implant fracture 16.14, abutment fracture 13.88, screw fracture 7.07, prosthesis mobility 4.51, ceramic fracture 4.36, prosthesis failure 3.66, screw loosening 3.40.
Bruxism significantly increases risk across all 7 mechanical complication subtypes; this 7-subgroup breakdown is the most granular quantitative analysis to date on this clinical question.

## Summary

*abstract-only — full text not retrieved (no PMC full text available for PMID 41102936)*

Vidal et al. (2025) conducted a systematic review with meta-analysis to assess the prevalence and association between 이갈이 (Bruxism) and different types of mechanical complications in implant-supported prostheses. Six databases (PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane Library, LILACS, Scopus) were searched from 1984 onward; grey literature was excluded; manual search of specialized literature was performed.

After removing duplicates from 934 initial records, 320 underwent full-text review, yielding 20 studies for the systematic review and 13 for the final meta-analysis. All included studies were observational (cross-sectional, case-control, or cohort designs). Bias quality analysis was performed on all selected studies.

The primary finding is a substantial increase in mechanical complication risk across every subgroup analyzed. The overall odds of unspecific mechanical failure were 6.10 times higher in bruxing patients. The most extreme elevation was for implant fracture (OR=16.14), followed by prosthetic 어버트먼트 파절 (abutment fracture, OR=13.88), which together suggest that bruxism-generated forces can cause catastrophic failure of metal components — not merely surface-level prosthetic wear.

## Key Contributions

1. **7-subgroup OR analysis** — first SR+MA to quantify bruxism's contribution to each distinct mechanical complication type separately, rather than treating complications as a composite endpoint.
2. **High OR range** — ORs span from 3.40 (screw loosening) to 16.14 (implant fracture), indicating that metal-component fractures carry disproportionately higher risk than soft/ceramic failures.
3. **Comprehensive search** — 6 databases, 1984–present; 934 initial records; no grey literature.
4. **Consistent directionality** — no subgroup showed a null or protective association; bruxism universally elevated risk.

## Methodology

- **Study type**: Systematic review with meta-analysis
- **Population**: Patients with implant-supported prostheses, with and without bruxism
- **Outcome**: Mechanical complications (unspecific and 7 specific subtypes)
- **Study designs included**: Cross-sectional, case-control, cohort (observational only)
- **Search period**: 1984 onward
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane Library, LILACS, Scopus
- **Grey literature**: Excluded
- **Manual search**: Specialized literature included
- **Records**: 934 identified → 320 full-text → 20 SR → 13 MA
- **Statistical measure**: 교차비 (Odds Ratio, OR)
- **Quality assessment**: Bias quality analysis performed on all included studies

## Results

### Overall mechanical failure

| Endpoint | OR |
|---|---|
| Unspecific mechanical failure (overall) | **6.10** |

### Subgroup analysis — specific complication types

| Complication | OR |
|---|---|
| 임플란트 파절 (Implant fracture) | **16.14** |
| 어버트먼트 파절 (Prosthetic abutment fracture) | **13.88** |
| 스크류 파절 (Prosthetic screw fracture) | **7.07** |
| 보철 이동성 (Prosthesis mobility) | **4.51** |
| 세라믹 파절 (Ceramic fracture) | **4.36** |
| 보철물 파손 (Prosthesis acrylic/teeth failure) | **3.66** |
| 스크류 이완 (Prosthetic screw loosening) | **3.40** |

All subgroups showed increased risk with bruxism presence. Confidence intervals, heterogeneity (I²), and GRADE ratings are not available from the abstract.

### Clinical pattern

Metal-component fractures (implant body, abutment, screw) carry the highest OR elevations (7.07–16.14), while prosthetic surface failures (ceramic fracture, acrylic/teeth failure) and screw loosening show lower but still substantial ORs (3.40–4.36). This gradient suggests that bruxism's force magnitudes are sufficient to cause catastrophic structural failure, not only wear-type complications.
