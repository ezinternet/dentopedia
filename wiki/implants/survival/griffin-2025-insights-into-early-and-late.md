## Three-line Summary

Retrospective electronic-health-record cohort of US veterans (2000-2021): 132,675 veterans with 468,496 implants entered the multivariable models; early failure was defined as within 6 months of placement and late failure as after 6 months, and 13,492 failures occurred in 9,423 veterans (reported as a 7.1% failure rate over 21 years).

For early failure, age 70 years or older (+257% odds), reimplantation (+89%), Asian race and osteoporosis were significantly associated; for late failure, active periodontitis (+139%), alendronate use (+114%), age 40-60 years and African American race were associated.

The intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC) of 86% indicates strong patient-level clustering, i.e. prior implant failure or success history predicts the outcome; the evidence is retrospective and abstract-only (adjusted ORs and CIs not available).

## Summary

Retrospective cohort analysis of electronic health records of US veterans (18 years or older) who received dental implants between January 1, 2000 and December 1, 2021. Failures were split into early (within 6 months of implantation) and late (after 6 months). Univariable screening was followed by multivariable mixed-effect logistic regression; the ICC quantified patient-level clustering.

## Key Contributions
- Very large veteran cohort (132,675 veterans, 468,496 implants) analysed separately for early versus late failure, a distinction few studies make.
- Shows that the risk-factor profile differs by timing: early failure with age 70 years or older, Asian race, osteoporosis and reimplantation; late failure with age 40-60 years, African American race, active periodontitis and alendronate use.
- High ICC (86%) highlights patient-level clustering and the weight of prior implant failure/success history.

## Methodology
- **Design/setting**: retrospective cohort; US Veterans Affairs electronic health records, 2000-2021.
- **Population**: veterans aged 18 years or older with dental implants; 132,675 veterans and 468,496 implants in multivariable models.
- **Definition of early failure**: within 6 months of placement; late failure: after 6 months.
- **Analysis**: univariable screening, then multivariable mixed-effect logistic regression for odds ratios of early/late failure; ICC from a mixed-effects model.
- Abstract-only: covariate list, failure ascertainment and model specification are not available.

## Results
- 13,492 failures in 9,423 unique veterans; 7.1% failure rate over 21 years as reported.
- Early failure, significantly increased adjusted odds: age 70 years or older at placement (reported as approximately +257%), Asian race, osteoporosis, reimplantation (+89%).
- Late failure, significantly increased adjusted odds: age 40-60 years, African American race, active periodontitis (+139%), alendronate use (+114%).
- ICC 86%: high patient-level clustering. Separate early and late failure counts and numeric ORs/CIs are not given in the abstract.

## Limitations
- Abstract-only: adjusted ORs, 95% CIs, early/late failure counts and covariates could not be verified.
- The reported 7.1% matches 9,423/132,675 (veterans with a failure), not 13,492/468,496 (about 2.9% of implants); the abstract labels it a failure rate, so the denominator is ambiguous (source-data-issue).
- Retrospective EHR data: misclassification, confounding by indication, and unmeasured factors (smoking, bone quality, implant system, operator) are possible.
- Veteran population (predominantly male, specific comorbidity profile) limits generalisability; race findings may reflect unmeasured social or access factors.
- Alendronate and osteoporosis effects are associations; the abstract gives no dose or duration.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
Early-failure screening should flag very old age (70 years or older), osteoporosis and a prior failed implant at the same patient, whereas late-failure surveillance should focus on active periodontitis and alendronate users; control periodontitis before and after placement. These are associations from an abstract-only veteran cohort, not Korean data.
