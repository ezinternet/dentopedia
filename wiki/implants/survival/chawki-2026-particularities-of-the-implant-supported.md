## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA, no meta-analysis) of 20 clinical cohorts (6 prospective, 14 retrospective; 9,313 patients, 31,209 implants; follow-up 6 months–36 years) comparing implant-supported prosthesis outcomes in bruxers vs non-bruxers.

Implant survival in bruxers ranged 57.5%–100% and prosthetic survival 70.6%–100% over 12–291 months, consistently below non-bruxers; mechanical complications reached 60% and prosthetic failure up to 29.3%, while regular protective-splint wear was associated with a 1.8-fold reduction in prosthetic material wear.

Clinical implication: bruxism warrants occlusal precautions (single-point occlusion, metal-ceramic/screw-retained design, delayed loading, hard night splint, annual maintenance); key limitations are purely observational designs, heterogeneous bruxism diagnosis, and an internal search-date conflict (20 Oct 2025 in abstract vs 20/06/2025 in Methods).

## Summary

This systematic review (Pan African Medical Journal 2026, PRISMA-guided, narrative synthesis without meta-analysis) assessed the longevity and reliability of implant-supported prostheses in patients with bruxism, screening 1330 records down to 20 clinical cohorts — 6 prospective and 14 retrospective — covering 9,313 patients and 31,209 implants with follow-up from 6 months to 36 years. Across the included studies, implant survival in bruxers ranged 57.5%–100% and prosthetic survival 70.6%–100%, in every comparison running below non-bruxer figures; mechanical complications reached 60% of bruxers in one cohort and prosthetic failure rates up to 29.3%, with immediate loading showing the starkest gradient (failure 29.3% and 41% in two bruxer cohorts vs 0.97% in total-edentulous immediate loading). The review concludes bruxism is a significant risk factor for implant and prosthetic failure and issues an explicit precaution set — axial force application, one implant per tooth, no zirconia implants, large-diameter fixtures, single-point gently-sloped occlusion, delayed loading, metal-ceramic screw-retained prostheses, systematic hard night splints (1.8-fold wear reduction), and annual maintenance. Because all included studies are observational and bruxism diagnosis is heterogeneous, the direction is robust but no pooled effect size is offered — its weight sits as primary-cohort support beneath the held umbrella review [[implants/survival/barboza-2026-bruxism-implant-failure-umbrella-review]] (OR 4.68).

## Key Contributions
- Provides the direct primary-cohort number layer (n=20 studies, 31,209 implants) beneath the umbrella-level conclusion that bruxism drives implant-supported prosthesis failure.
- Systematizes bruxer vs non-bruxer failure gradients across study subgroups: implant failure 13% vs 4.6% (Chrcanovic 2016, p=0.012), 25.5% vs 11.2% (Chrcanovic 2017, p=0.001), 42.53% vs 11.93% at 36 years (Chrcanovic 2018, p=0.001); implant fracture 3.6% vs 0.3% (18.19×).
- Risk-factor ranking: bruxism was the only mechanically significant variable for implant failure in Papi 2017 (15%, p<0.05), and bruxism + risky loading (C/I <0.8, angulation >25°, cantilever) produced the worst combined outcome (69.23% success, De Angelis 2017).
- Isolates total-edentulous immediate loading as the exception where bruxism did not preclude immediate loading (Ibañez 2005: 99.03% success in bruxers), contrasting with partial/single immediate loading (Glauser 41%, Ji 29.3% failure in bruxers).
- Quantifies the protective night splint (1.8-fold lower prosthetic material wear) and converts the evidence into an itemized chairside recommendation checklist (planning, materials, surface, dimensions, prosthetic design, loading, abutments, retention, splinting, maintenance).

## Methodology
Systematic review per PRISMA, narrative synthesis (no meta-analysis). Databases: Medline-PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Google Scholar; last computer search stated as 20 October 2025 in the abstract but 20/06/2025 in Methods (internal conflict recorded verbatim). Inclusion: human clinical studies, English, follow-up ≥6 months; exclusion: case series, expert opinions, animal/cadaver, in vitro/in silico, reviews/meta-analyses; dual independent screening with consensus. PICO: bruxism (P) vs implant placement (I), bruxer vs non-bruxer complication rates (C), impact on the implant-supported prosthesis (O). Appraisal: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (AHRQ good/fair/poor thresholds) + Cochrane ROBINS-I. Yield: 1330 → 277 full-text → 20 studies (6 prospective, 14 retrospective cohorts), 9,313 patients, 31,209 implants, follow-up 6 months–36 years.
