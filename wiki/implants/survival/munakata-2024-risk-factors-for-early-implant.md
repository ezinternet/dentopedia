## Three-line Summary

Narrative review arguing that early implant failure (variously defined as loss before the superstructure, within 6 months, or up to 1 year after prosthesis) is a failure to achieve osseointegration with different risk factors from late failure, and that most reported early-failure risk factors are bone augmentation related (technique, graft material, surgeon skill, postoperative complications) rather than systemic or implant-design factors.

Cited studies report early failure associated with bone grafting or GBR (e.g., OR 9.45 in the maxilla; OR 1.50 in a meta-analysis), surgeon skill (HR 5.13), sinus augmentation (86.1% vs 96.4% survival), and crestal sinus lift with membrane perforation; all numbers are quoted from other studies, since no systematic search or pooling was performed here.

The authors conclude that graft material should be chosen by new bone formation and residual graft rates rather than CBCT appearance, and that non-resorbable xenograft (especially bovine bone) should not be used alone under loading, with large thread exposure, or in sinus augmentation, although they state they could not establish an appropriate graft material.

## 세줄요약

조기 임플란트 실패 (early implant failure; 보철물 장착 전, 식립 후 6개월, 또는 보철 후 1년 등 정의가 통일되지 않음)를 장기 실패와 다른 "골유착 획득 실패"로 보고, 보고된 위험인자 다수가 전신질환이나 임플란트 설계가 아니라 골이식 관련 요인(술식, 이식재, 술자 숙련도, 술후 합병증)이라고 논증하는 서술적 고찰이다.

인용된 연구들에서 조기 실패는 골이식·유도골재생술 (guided bone regeneration, GBR) (상악 OR 9.45, 메타분석 OR 1.50), 술자 숙련도 (HR 5.13), 상악동 거상 (생존율 86.1% 대 96.4%), 치조정 접근 상악동 거상의 막 천공과 연관되었으며, 모든 수치는 타 연구의 인용이고 자체적인 체계적 검색이나 통합 분석은 없다.

저자들은 CBCT 소견이 아니라 신생골 형성률과 잔존 이식재 비율로 이식재를 선택해야 하며, 비흡수성 이종골(특히 소 유래 골)을 단독으로 하중 부위, 큰 나사산 노출, 상악동 거상에 쓰지 말라고 결론짓지만, 적절한 이식재를 확립하지는 못했다고 스스로 밝힌다.

## Summary

This narrative review (Bioengineering, 2024) examines why implants fail early and how bone augmentation and graft choice relate to it. It contrasts late failure (peri-implantitis, overload, prosthetic design) with early failure, which the authors describe as failure to achieve osseointegration, often with postoperative infection. They note that no universal definition of early failure exists (placement to superstructure, to 6 months, or to 1 year after superstructure), that timing data show most early losses occur before the superstructure, and that reimplantation at a failed site has lower survival (88.8% first, 74.2% second in a cited systematic review).

The review compiles risk factors from cited cohorts. Patient factors such as diabetes, osteoporosis, periodontal history and implant length/design were often not significant in the cited work (for example Tattan), whereas bone grafting, ridge preservation, immediate placement, surgeon experience and surgical complications were repeatedly associated with early loss (Jemt surgeon skill HR 5.13; Carr surgical complications HR 15.84; Chang bone graft OR 9.45; Clauser meta-analysis OR 1.50). Procedure-specific sections cover alveolar ridge preservation, horizontal/vertical ridge augmentation, and lateral and crestal sinus augmentation, where cited studies link early failure to membrane perforation, chronic sinusitis, simultaneous implant placement, low residual bone height and operator experience.

On graft material, the authors synthesize cited systematic reviews showing an inverse relation between residual graft particles and new bone formation, and argue that slowly resorbing xenograft (especially bovine bone) used alone can delay bone formation and reduce bone contact. They advise against xenograft alone in loaded or large-exposure situations and sinus augmentation, and recommend adjusting healing time to the material, while admitting no ideal material could be established.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes early failure as a mechanism distinct from late failure and supports looking at augmentation-related variables first.
- Gathers early-failure effect sizes from many cohorts in one place, with procedure-specific breakdown (ARP, ridge augmentation, sinus lift).
- Links graft material resorption behaviour (new bone vs residual graft) to the risk of poor bone contact at implant placement.
- Offers practical suggestions: avoid augmentation when feasible, match healing period to graft, be ready to convert crestal to lateral sinus approach.

## Methodology

- Narrative review; no search strategy, databases, selection criteria, quality appraisal or data pooling are reported in the text.
- Early failure definition: not fixed; three period types listed by the authors; cited studies use their own windows (before prosthesis, up to 6 months, up to 1 year of loading).
- Country, setting, n of patients and implants: not applicable to the review itself; sample sizes appear only for cited studies (for example 3448 cases, 8540 implants in 362 cases, 1050 implants, 2053 implants, 6113 implants, 1045 sinus augmentation implants).

## Results

Selected effect sizes quoted from the cited studies (not original data):

| Factor | Quoted effect |
|---|---|
| Surgeon skill/experience | HR 5.13 (Jemt); HR 12.95 (Li, crestal sinus lift) |
| Surgical complications | HR 15.84 (Carr) |
| Bone grafting alone | HR 1.45 (Carr); OR 9.45 maxilla (Chang); OR 1.50 (Clauser SR+MA) |
| Ridge preservation / socket preservation | HR 2.67 (Carr); HR 7.5 (Tattan) |
| Immediate placement | OR 3.509 (Yang) |
| Type I bone quality | OR 3.689 (Yang) |
| Maxillary molar region | OR 2.73 (Wu) |
| Sinus augmentation survival | 86.1% vs 96.4% without (Barone) |
| Sinus augmentation risk factors (Zinser) | smoking HR 1.98; one-stage HR 2.56; allograft/xenograft HR 4.74; residual bone 0-2 mm HR 3.51 |
| Chronic sinusitis | OR 16.7 for postoperative infection (Kozuma) |
| Early failure after augmentation (Jensen) | 1.7% overall; 12% (2 implants) after staged vertical augmentation |

Graft biology: Al-Moraissi (as cited) found lower new bone formation for alloplast alone and xenograft alone than for autogenous bone (differences -8.04% and -4.49% overall) and higher residual graft rates, suggesting residual graft delays new bone formation.

## Limitations

- Narrative design with undisclosed search and selection methods; susceptible to selective citation. Most quoted numbers are from observational studies spanning implant eras from the 1980s to the 2010s, which the authors say prevents consensus on risk factors.
- No original data and no pooled estimates; numbers should be traced to the original papers before clinical use.
- The authors concede they could not establish an appropriate bone graft material.
- The retrieved text lacks the title, author list, publication date, tables and reference list, and bracketed citations are empty, so the title, authors ("Munakata M et al.") and date (month fallback 2024-02-01) were taken from the task listing rather than the text.
- Wording quirks in the source (graft "survival" meaning residual graft fraction; a repeated 7.30 figure for different measures) make some graft-material statements hard to interpret.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

Treat early failure as a surgical-biology problem rather than a patient-risk-label problem: before routine grafting at implant placement, ask whether the augmentation is needed at all (narrow implant, site change, angulation) and who is doing it, since surgeon experience and postoperative complications recur as the strongest quoted factors. For sinus lifts, plan for the sinus condition (chronic sinusitis), residual bone height, and lift amount, and be able to convert from crestal to lateral access. Choose slowly resorbing xenograft cautiously in loaded or large-exposure sites and allow a longer healing period for it. This is an expert narrative synthesis, so use it for framing and check the specific numbers in the primary or SR/MA pages before counselling.
