## Three-line Summary

Lipophilic statins (simvastatin, lovastatin, atorvastatin) enhance peri-implant bone formation in preclinical models via BMP-2/Smad signaling and OPG/RANKL-mediated osteoclast suppression.
Hyperlipidemia impairs 골-임플란트 접촉률 (Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC) through reduced osteoblastic function, elevated osteoclast activity, and compromised collagen turnover.
Human clinical evidence remains inconclusive — biological plausibility is established but large-scale stratified trials with controlled statin delivery are required before clinical translation.

## Summary

abstract-only — full text not retrieved

This 2025 narrative review examines the molecular mechanisms by which statins may enhance dental implant 골유착 (Osseointegration), and how 고지혈증 (Hyperlipidemia) undermines it, bridging preclinical mechanistic evidence with the current gap in clinical translation.

## Background

Statins are among the most widely prescribed medications globally, primarily for dyslipidemia. Beyond their lipid-lowering action, pleiotropic bone-modifying effects have attracted interest in implant dentistry, particularly given the aging implant patient population with high rates of comorbid metabolic disease.

## Molecular Mechanisms

### Pro-osteoblastic signaling (lipophilic statins)

Lipophilic statins — simvastatin, lovastatin, and atorvastatin — enhance 조골세포 (Osteoblast) differentiation and matrix synthesis in preclinical models via two principal pathways:

- **BMP-2/Smad axis**: Statins upregulate 골형성 단백질-2 (Bone Morphogenetic Protein-2, BMP-2) expression and activate downstream Smad transcription factors, driving osteogenic commitment.
- **Ras-PI3K-Akt-Erk cascade**: Promotes osteoblast survival and matrix synthesis.

### Anti-osteoclastic signaling

- **OPG/RANKL axis modulation**: Statins shift the OPG (Osteoprotegerin) / RANKL (Receptor Activator of NF-κB Ligand) ratio in favour of 파골세포 억제 (Osteoclastogenesis suppression).
- **NF-κB inhibition**: Blocks a key transcription factor required for osteoclast differentiation.
- **p38 MAPK blockade**: Further attenuates osteoclastogenesis downstream.

## Hyperlipidemia and Osseointegration

Hyperlipidemia links to impaired osseointegration through:

1. Reduced osteoblastic function — lipid accumulation in bone microenvironment
2. Increased osteoclast activity — lipid-driven inflammatory signalling
3. Compromised collagen turnover — altered matrix quality at the bone-implant interface

The authors note that obesity and lipid metabolism introduce additional confounding variables, emphasising the need for patient stratification in clinical research on this topic.

## Human Clinical Evidence

Despite mechanistic consistency in experimental systems, human data remain inconclusive:

- Modest increases in 골밀도 (Bone Mineral Density, BMD) have been reported with statin use
- No confirmed reduction in fracture risk has been demonstrated
- No definitive clinical trial has confirmed statin-mediated enhancement of implant 골유착 (Osseointegration) outcomes in humans

## Clinical Implications

Current evidence supports the **biological plausibility** of statin-mediated enhancement of peri-implant bone formation. However:

- Definitive clinical translation is not yet established
- Large-scale, stratified randomised controlled trials are required
- Controlled delivery approaches (local vs systemic) and extended follow-up are needed
- Patient stratification for hyperlipidemia, obesity, and comorbid metabolic disease is essential

> **Clinical note**: This review does not provide actionable clinical guidance on statin prescribing for implant patients. The biological rationale is present; evidence-based protocol is absent.

## Why Ingested

Statins are common in the implant patient population; hyperlipidemia is an established implant risk factor.
