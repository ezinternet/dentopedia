## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (1990–2024) synthesizing implant complication data and management strategies for bruxism patients across biological, mechanical, and treatment dimensions.

Bruxing patients have 2.2–4.7 fold higher implant failure risk (Chrcanovic: 13.0% vs 4.6%, HR 3.396); occlusal splints reduce implant stress by 33–73% depending on load magnitude (300–900 N); splinted prosthetic designs demonstrate superior biomechanical performance in multiple SRs.

Peri-implant bone loss association is limited and not causally established; primary complications are mechanical (screw loosening, component fractures); RCT evidence for most management strategies is absent.

## Summary

This narrative review (Shafiee & Nourizadeh, BMC Oral Health 2025) synthesizes literature from January 1990 to December 2024 on bruxism-related complications and management in implant-supported rehabilitations. The core challenge is biomechanical: unlike natural teeth buffered by the 치주인대 (Periodontal Ligament, PDL), osseointegrated implants transmit parafunctional forces directly to the implant-bone interface.

**Failure risk**: Meta-analyses show 2.2–4.7 fold increased implant failure in bruxing patients. Chrcanovic's large retrospective study (2,670 patients, 10,096 implants) found 13.0% failure in bruxing vs 4.6% in non-bruxing (HR 3.396; 95% CI 1.314–8.777; P=0.012). Häggman-Henrikson reported OR 2.2 for probable bruxing patients. Technical complications OR 2.71 (Chrcanovic case-control).

**Bone loss nuance**: Contrary to intuition, systematic reviews (Bertolini, Di Fiore) find insufficient evidence for a causal relationship between bruxism and 임플란트 주위 골 소실 (Peri-implant Bone Loss, PBL). Differences are limited. The primary failure mechanism is mechanical overload — implant body fractures and component failures — not biological peri-implantitis.

**Occlusal splint effectiveness**: Teixeira photoelastic study: stress reduction of 33.22%, 66.66%, 73.33% at 300, 600, 900 N respectively — protective effect scales with load magnitude. Multiple FEA studies confirm (Dos Santos Marsico, Silva, Henrique). However, occlusal devices alone are not sufficient to negate the biomechanical benefit of splinting prosthetic components (Silva et al.).

**Bruxism does not preclude implant treatment**: With comprehensive management, successful outcomes are achievable. The clinical question shifts from "contraindication?" to "how do we manage?"

## Key Contributions

- Quantitative synthesis of failure risk: 2.2–4.7 fold range from multiple SR+MAs
- Stress reduction benchmarks for occlusal splints at three clinically relevant force levels
- Evidence framework distinguishing mechanical complications (well-documented) from biological/bone-loss complications (limited evidence)
- Comprehensive management taxonomy: behavioral → pharmacological → dental → prosthetic design → strategic placement → follow-up protocols
- Clinical recommendation: splinted prosthetic designs + occlusal splint + strategic placement + mutually protected occlusion + regular monitoring

## Methodology

**Design**: Narrative review; no formal PRISMA protocol; no risk-of-bias assessment for individual studies.
**Search**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane Library, Science Direct + Google Scholar; January 1990 – December 2024.
**Inclusion**: Clinical studies, SRs, meta-analyses, case series on bruxism and implant outcomes, complications, and management strategies.
**Exclusion**: Single case reports, animal studies, in vitro studies without clinical relevance, studies with insufficient bruxism diagnosis data.
**Bruxism diagnosis**: International consensus grading — "possible," "probable," "definite" sleep or awake bruxism. Diagnostic standardization across included studies remains inconsistent.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Häggman-Henrikson et al. | SR | — | — | OR 2.2 (95% CI 1.337–3.583, p=0.002) |

| Chrcanovic (mechanical) | Case-control | Higher | — | OR 2.71 (95% CI 1.25–5.88) |
| Pooled SR range | Multiple SRs | — | — | 2.2–4.7 fold |

### Occlusal Splint Stress Reduction

| Load (N) | Stress Reduction | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 300 N | 33.22% | Teixeira photoelastic |
| 600 N | 66.66% | Teixeira photoelastic |
| 900 N | 73.33% | Teixeira photoelastic |

FEA: Henrique — oblique loading stresses increase 4–19% without splints; MT implants produce 4–19% lower stress than HE implants under oblique loading.

### Prosthetic Design Evidence

Splinted designs (vs non-splinted):
- Less marginal bone loss (Shah SR)
- Lower implant failure (Shah SR)
- Splinted crowns + occlusal devices = most effective for abutment screw stress and bone tissue strain (Silva FEA)
- Systematic reviews (De Souza Batista, Ravida, Shah) consistently support splinting for biomechanical advantage

### Peri-implant Bone Loss

Bertolini SR: insufficient evidence for causal relationship with traumatic occlusal forces. Bredberg matched cohort: limited differences. Di Fiore SR: inconclusive. Tabrizi: minimal associations.

**Clinical implication**: Do not assume bruxism → bone loss. The evidence chain from bruxism to biological failure is weak; mechanical failure is the documented concern.
