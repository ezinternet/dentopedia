## Three-line Summary

A narrative review of 19 case reports/series (1978–2022) across 8 hyperdense jaw lesion types found an 84.2% overall implant success rate. Osteoid osteoma, odontoma, cementoblastoma, condensing osteitis, and idiopathic osteosclerosis allow implantation without major complications, while cemento-osseous dysplasia carries meaningful failure risk (all 3 failure cases were FCOD) and osteoblastoma is effectively contraindicated. No unified protocol exists; avoidance of drilling overheating and thorough pre-operative diagnosis (CBCT + histopathology) are the most critical success factors.

## Summary

This narrative review by Taghsimi et al. (2022) addresses a critical clinical gap: there are no established guidelines for dental implantation in hyperdense (radiopaque) jaw lesions. A systematic search of PubMed, Google Scholar, and Science Direct (1978–January 2022) identified 323 articles; 19 met inclusion criteria — all case reports or case series.

The overall implant success rate across all lesion types was **84.2%** (16/19 cases). The 3 failures were all in the cemento-osseous dysplasia (FCOD) group. Osteoblastoma had zero implant cases in the literature — contraindicated due to aggressive growth, recurrence tendency, and en bloc resection as standard of care.

## Key Contributions

1. **First narrative synthesis** across all 8 major hyperdense jaw lesion types
2. **Risk stratification framework**: safe / conditional / contraindicated
3. Identification of **drilling overheating** as the primary preventable failure mechanism
4. Multi-stage protocol descriptions for FCOD (3-stage), CO (GBR-bridge), and IO (size-adjusted)
5. Establishes CBCT + histopathology as mandatory pre-operative workup

## Results by Lesion Type

| Lesion Type | Cases (n) | Outcome | Key Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Osteoid osteoma | 2 | Success | Benign, low recurrence; Gardner syndrome case stable 7 yr |
| Odontoma | 5 | Success | Immediate and delayed both effective; no complications |
| Osteoblastoma | 0 | Contraindicated | Aggressive; en bloc resection required; no implant cases |
| Cementoblastoma | 2 | Success (conditional) | Must remove tooth + lesion first; immediate or delayed feasible |
| Cemento-osseous dysplasia | 7 | Mixed — 3 failures | Vascular impairment + overheating = failure; 3-stage protocol recommended |
| Condensing osteitis | 1 | Success | Extraction → GBR → 6-month wait → implant |
| Idiopathic osteosclerosis | 2 | Success | Stable 1–2 yr; protocol depends on lesion size and position |

**Overall**: 84.2% success; 15.8% failure (3/19, all FCOD)

### FCOD Failure Mechanisms
- Avascular/hypovascular dysplastic tissue → impaired osseointegration
- Drilling heat → perilesional necrosis → infection and sequestrum formation
- Exposure of dysplastic tissue during surgery increases risk — some authors favor avoiding lesion exposure

### Surgical Protocol Recommendations
- **CO**: Tooth extraction → immediate GBR → 6-month healing → implant placement
- **IO (small/apical)**: Adjust implant length, position, and angulation; no lesion removal needed
- **IO (large/coronal)**: Direct placement feasible; extremely dense lesion → remove, then delay
- **FCOD (3-stage)**: Stage 1: drilling + betadine + hermetic closure + antibiotics → Stage 2: re-entry at 3 weeks (proliferative phase) → Stage 3: healing abutment after 3 months
- **All types**: Standard sterile protocol; minimize periosteal reflection; avoid overheating; respect manufacturer torque values
