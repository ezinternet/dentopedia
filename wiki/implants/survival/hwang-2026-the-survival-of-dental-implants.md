## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort of 1694 implants in 806 women aged 55 years or older (follow-up at least 12 months) comparing anti-resorptive drug (ARD: bisphosphonate or denosumab) users with non-ARD controls, using propensity score matching, Firth logistic regression with patient clustering and a linear mixed model for marginal bone loss.

Implant survival was 99.13% (bisphosphonates), 98.08% (denosumab) and 98.24% (control) with no significant differences in failure or marginal bone loss; diabetes mellitus, alcohol consumption and overdenture prosthesis significantly increased failure risk.

Implant rehabilitation is feasible in post-menopausal women on ARD, but caution is advised for high-dose or long-term therapy; effect sizes are unavailable from the abstract and the invasive-procedure risk needs further study.

## Summary

Hwang et al. (2026) evaluated the effect of anti-resorptive drugs, including denosumab, on dental implant survival in post-menopausal women with osteoporosis versus non-ARD users. The retrospective cohort comprised 1694 implants in 806 women aged 55 years or older with at least 12 months of follow-up. Implants were grouped by exposure (bisphosphonates, denosumab, or non-ARD control); propensity score matching balanced the groups. Survival was **99.13% (bisphosphonates)**, **98.08% (denosumab)** and **98.24% (control)**, with no significant differences in implant failure or marginal bone loss. Diabetes mellitus, alcohol consumption and overdenture prosthesis were associated with a significantly increased risk of failure. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/cid.70122)

## Key Contributions

- Direct three-arm comparison (bisphosphonate, denosumab, non-ARD) of implant survival in post-menopausal osteoporotic women.
- Identifies diabetes mellitus, alcohol consumption and overdenture prosthesis as failure risk factors in this population.
- Supports feasibility of implant rehabilitation under ARD therapy.

## Methodology

Retrospective cohort; variables included demographic, ARD-related, implant/prosthesis-related factors and marginal bone loss. Propensity score matching; Firth logistic regression with patient-level clustering; linear mixed model for longitudinal bone change; Kaplan-Meier curves.

## Results

| Group | Implant survival |
|---|---|
| Bisphosphonates | 99.13% |
| Denosumab | 98.08% |
| Control | 98.24% |

No significant differences in failure or marginal bone loss between groups. Diabetes mellitus, alcohol consumption and overdenture prosthesis significantly increased failure risk. Per-group counts, odds ratios and CIs are not in the abstract.

## Limitations

- Retrospective design.
- Abstract-only ingest: effect estimates, group sizes and dose/duration analyses not available.
- Authors caution that high-dose or long-term ARD use may compromise bone metabolism and that risk from invasive procedures needs further evaluation.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

Post-menopausal patients on denosumab or bisphosphonates for osteoporosis showed implant survival comparable to non-users in this cohort, so ARD use alone need not preclude implant treatment; screen for diabetes and alcohol use and weigh overdenture designs, and take extra care with high-dose or long-term ARD patients (see MRONJ pages).
