## Three-line Summary
Retrospective case-control study (King Saud University, Saudi Arabia; 132 failed implants removed 2017–2022) analyzing clinical, surgical, and radiographic records of implant removals to identify underlying causes of failure.
Lack of osseointegration (36.4%) and absence of primary stability (22.4%) were the predominant removal reasons; implant type (p=0.004) and restoration type (p=0.001) significantly predicted survival (overdenture 3.44×, crown 2.42× higher failure risk), while gender (p=0.001) and medical conditions/smoking (p=0.004) shaped specific failure mechanisms.
Clinical implication: patient-specific risk assessment, meticulous surgical technique for primary stability, and prosthetic planning are vital — and only 36.4% of failure cases attended regular maintenance.

## Summary
This single-center Saudi retrospective case-control analyzed 132 implants removed due to failure between 2017 and 2022 at King Saud University's University Dental Hospital. The two dominant recorded removal reasons were lack of osseointegration (36.4%) and absence of primary stability (22.4%), followed by peri-implantitis (14.0%), iatrogenic/non-functional causes (17.7%), infection (7.5%), and prosthetic trauma (1.9%). Cox regression found only implant type (p=0.004) and restoration type (p=0.001) significantly predicted survival, with overdentures (3.44×, p=0.027) and crowns (2.42×, p<0.001) carrying higher failure risk; gender, medical conditions, and smoking structured the *mechanisms* of failure (e.g., peri-implantitis in men 11.2% vs 2.8% in women), and prior surgical intervention plus re-implantation decisions drove removal frequency. Clinically, the paper argues for patient-specific risk assessment, meticulous surgical technique to secure primary stability, and careful prosthetic planning — noting that 63.6% of failure patients were not in regular maintenance.

## Key Contributions
- Distribution of removal reasons in a failed-removal cohort, with lack of osseointegration (36.4%) and absence of primary stability (22.4%) dominant.
- Cox model isolating implant type and restoration type as the only significant survival predictors, with quantified hazard ratios (OD 3.44×, crown 2.42×).
- Gender- and medical-status-stratified failure mechanisms (peri-implantitis in men more than women; iatrogenic failures in women more than men).
- Removal-frequency analysis: prior surgery and re-implantation decisions significantly associated with repeated removals (χ²=34.797, p=0.001; χ²=28.252, p=0.005).

## Methodology
- **Design**: Retrospective case-control, single-center chart review (STROBE-guided; IRB approval E-21-5835)
- **n**: 132 removed implants (G*Power: alpha 0.05, effect 0.15, power 0.9)
- **Inclusion/exclusion**: complete records + pre-removal radiographs/CT; incomplete or distorted records excluded
- **Data collection**: 3 calibrated operators, inter-examiner agreement >90%
- **Statistics**: descriptive, Shapiro–Wilk, Cox regression survival, chi-square; SPSS 27; alpha 0.05; pairwise deletion for missing data
