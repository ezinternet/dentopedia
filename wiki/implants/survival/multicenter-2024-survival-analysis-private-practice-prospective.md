## Three-line Summary

Prospective multicenter cohort of 80 patients / 166 Avinent implants placed in two private clinics (2015–2017), 24-month follow-up, STROBE-reported.

Cumulative survival at 24 months was 97.44% (95% CI 93.30–99.03); 4 implants failed (2.41%), with bruxism (HR 96.62, p<0.001) and absence of primary stability (HR 23.54, p<0.001) significantly associated with failure in univariate Cox analysis.

Implication: single-brand private-practice implant treatment is predictable (>95% survival), but the reported HRs are univariate wide-CI estimates from a small failure count — confounder adjustment and longer follow-up are needed.

## Summary

This STROBE-adherent prospective multicenter cohort followed 80 patients and 166 Avinent (Biomimetic) implants placed in two private dental clinics between September 2015 and November 2017. At 24 months the Kaplan–Meier cumulative survival rate was 97.44% (95% CI 93.30–99.03), with 4 implant failures (2.41%) — 2 before restoration within the first 3 months and 2 after loading. Univariate Cox regression identified absence of primary stability (HR 23.54; 95% CI 3.28–169.16; p<0.001) and bruxism (HR 96.62; 95% CI 9.88–945.08; p<0.001) as the only covariates significantly associated with implant failure. The clinic-level finding that implant-supported rehabilitation for various edentulism patterns exceeds a 95% 24-month survival threshold in private practice is the paper's main contribution, while the extremely wide confidence intervals warn that the hazard ratios should be read as signals, not stable effect estimates.

## Key Contributions

- Private-practice, multicenter prospective cohort (STROBE) reporting 97.44% cumulative implant survival at 24 months across two clinics using a single implant system (Avinent Biomimetic).
- Quantifies the distribution of insertion torque: 54.82% of implants achieved primary stability >35 Ncm, none exceeded 45 Ncm, and only 4.82% failed to attain primary stability.
- Reports hazard ratios — not just association tables — for absence of primary stability (HR 23.54) and bruxism (HR 96.62) as implant-failure risk factors, aligning with prior SR/MA-level evidence on both topics.
- Documents a 50/50 early (pre-restoration) vs late (post-loading) split among 4 failures, consistent with the first-6-months critical window for implant loss.
- Adds external validity to the private-practice implant literature by pooling two independent clinic settings on a single implant brand.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective multicenter cohort (2 private dental clinics in Vancouver, Canada and Split, Croatia), STROBE reporting
- **n**: 80 outpatients (41 female; mean age 58.17, SD 13.86) receiving 166 implants (Sep 2015–Nov 2017), 24-month follow-up
- **Implants**: Bone-level titanium grade-5 Avinent system, sandblasted + anodized Biomimetic surface; Coral (parallel-walled, symmetric thread) or Ocean IC (tapered, reverse coronal, asymmetric thread) macrodesigns
- **Exclusions**: ASA >3, immunosuppression, bleeding disorders, active malignancy treatment, drug abuse, psychiatric illness, IV bisphosphonates, >20 cigarettes/day smokers, age <18
- **Outcomes**: Cumulative survival (Kaplan–Meier; survival = implant present at examination), failure = implant mobility; univariate log-rank + univariate Cox proportional-hazards (HR with 95% CI), significance p<0.05, STATA 14
- **Power**: post-hoc — 96% to detect HR 4 at 166 implants, corrected to 84% assuming moderate intra-subject correlation (ρ=0.5)

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| 24-month cumulative survival | 97.44% (95% CI 93.30–99.03) |
| Failed implants | 4/166 (2.41%; 95% CI 0.94–6.03) |
| Bruxism → failure (univariate HR) | 96.62 (95% CI 9.88–945.08; p<0.001) |
| Absence of primary stability → failure (univariate HR) | 23.54 (95% CI 3.28–169.16; p<0.001) |
| Primary stability >35 Ncm | 54.82% (0% >45 Ncm; 4.82% <35 Ncm) |
| Failure timing | 2 pre-restoration (<3 months), 2 post-loading |
| Placement site | healed bone 78.31%, fresh socket 19.88%, regenerated 1.81% |
| Loading protocol | delayed 86.75%, immediate 13.25% |
| Peri-implantitis in-study prevalence | 1.8% implant / 3.75% patient level (short follow-up) |
