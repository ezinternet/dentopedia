## Three-line Summary

Retrospective teaching-clinic cohort (Section of Oral Surgery and Oral Medicine, University of Bergen, January 2011–December 2018; 1,005 dental implants) reporting prevalence of and risk indicators for early implant failure (implant lost before functional loading).

54 implants failed early (EFR 5.4%; functional loading obtained for 94.6%); increased hazard for smokers, men, and younger patients (each 10-year age increase reduced failure risk by 14%, HRR 0.86, P=.037), anterior maxilla failed more than posterior maxilla (7.79% vs 3.29%, HRR 0.47, P=.041), posterior mandible failed more than posterior maxilla (HRR 3.68, P=.005), a prior implant failure raised the next implant's risk (HRR 1.82), and system EFRs differed (Straumann 5.2%, Nobel Biocare 7.2%, Astra Tech 2.5%).

The authors conclude the overall early failure rate was low despite significant patient- and implant-related differences; abstract-only ingest means the implant-system difference cannot be separated from confounding by operator/site/era, and no confidence intervals are reported.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort from the University of Bergen's teaching clinic reviewed all 1,005 dental implants placed between January 2011 and December 2018, using operation logbooks and patient records, with early failure defined as implant loss before functional loading. Fifty-four implants failed early, an early failure rate (EFR) of 5.4% (94.6% reached functional loading). Analysis identified an increased hazard for **smokers, men, and younger patients** — an inverted age effect, since each 10-year increase in age lowered failure risk by 14% (HRR 0.86, P=.037). Site mattered in both directions: within the maxilla, anterior implants failed more than posterior (7.79% vs 3.29%, HRR 0.47, P=.041), and comparing jaws, the posterior mandible failed significantly more than the posterior maxilla (HRR 3.68, P=.005). A recurrence signal was also reported — failure of the first implant predicted failure of the consecutive implant (HRR 1.82). Implant-specific EFRs differed (Straumann 5.2%, Nobel Biocare 7.2%, Astra Tech 2.5%). The authors emphasize that despite these differences, the overall 5.4% EFR was low.

## Key Contributions

- Reports an **inverted age effect** for early failure: older patients at *lower* risk (each decade, HRR 0.86) — opposite to several late-failure cohorts.
- Quantifies **within-jaw and between-jaw site gradients**: anterior > posterior maxilla (7.79% vs 3.29%), and posterior mandible > posterior maxilla (HRR 3.68).
- Identifies a **carryover/recurrence effect** — a prior implant failure raises the consecutive implant's hazard (HRR 1.82).
- Provides **system-specific EFRs** in one teaching clinic (Straumann 5.2%, Nobel Biocare 7.2%, Astra Tech 2.5%).
- Confirms the male and smoker early-failure signals in an independent Nordic teaching-clinic population.

## Methodology

- Design: retrospective cohort, all patients with an implant placed at one university teaching clinic.
- Period/inclusion: January 2011–December 2018 (Section of Oral Surgery and Oral Medicine, Department of Clinical Dentistry, University of Bergen).
- Data sources: operation logbooks and patient records.
- Outcome definition: early implant failure = implant lost before functional loading.
- Analysis: survival/hazard modelling; results as hazard ratios (HRR) with P-values.
- n: 1,005 dental implants; 54 early failures.

## Results

| Outcome / predictor | Result |
|---|---|
| Early failure | 54 / 1,005 implants — EFR 5.4% (functional loading 94.6%) |
| Age (per +10 years) | HRR 0.86 (P=.037) — risk reduced 14% |
| Anterior maxilla vs posterior maxilla | 7.79% vs 3.29%; HRR 0.47 (P=.041) |
| Posterior mandible vs posterior maxilla | HRR 3.68 (P=.005) |
| Consecutive implant after a first failure | HRR 1.82 |
| Straumann (53.4% of implants) | EFR 5.2% |
| Nobel Biocare (30.3% of implants) | EFR 7.2% |
| Astra Tech (16.3% of implants) | EFR 2.5% |

No confidence intervals are reported in the abstract. Independent predictors named by direction: smokers and men (increased hazard), younger age (increased hazard).
