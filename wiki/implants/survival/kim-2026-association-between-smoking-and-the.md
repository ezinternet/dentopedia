## Three-line Summary

Retrospective nationwide cohort study of Korean adults in the National Health Insurance Service-Health Screening Cohort (NHIS-HEALS, implant placement 2016-2019): 23,573 individuals (16,405 non-smokers, 5,381 ex-smokers, 1,787 current smokers); failure was claim-based (reimplantation or removal-surgery procedure codes) and the denominator is persons, not implants.

605 failures occurred (344 non-smokers 2.1%, 182 ex-smokers 3.4%, 79 current smokers 4.4%; 11.56, 16.54, 22.33 per 1,000 person-years); adjusted HR was 1.59 (95% CI 1.20-2.09) for current smokers and 1.16 (0.94-1.45, not significant) for ex-smokers, with significant risk for >10 cigarettes/day (1.31), >10 years (1.27) and >10 pack-years (1.30) but not for lighter or shorter exposure.

Sex-stratified current-smoker HR was 1.48 (1.11-1.97) in males and 3.39 (1.25-9.17) in females; the data give no early-vs-late timing split, no implant-level data (system, bone, surgeon), self-reported smoking and only up to 4 years of follow-up, so this is overall claim-based failure; patient nationality is implied by the Korean national database but not stated as an ethnicity variable.

## Summary

Population-based retrospective cohort from the Korea National Health Insurance Service-Health Screening Cohort (NHIS-HEALS), 1 Jan 2016 to 31 Dec 2019. Adults who underwent implantation on or after 1 Jan 2016 (ICD-10 K081 plus prespecified procedure codes) and had a health examination within 12 months of the index date were included: 23,573 individuals who had completed the implant process. Patient nationality or ethnicity is not stated as a variable; the Korean setting is implied by the nationwide Korean database (the Discussion refers to "Korean adults").

Failure was claim-based: reimplantation (osseointegration failure, reimbursable recipients only) or removal surgery (simple removal for osseointegration failure; complex removal with trephine bur or removal kit, typically for fixture fracture or potential nerve injury). 605 failures: 344/16,405 non-smokers (2.1%), 182/5,381 ex-smokers (3.4%), 79/1,787 current smokers (4.4%) (p<0.001); crude rates 11.56, 16.54 and 22.33 per 1,000 person-years.

Adjusted Cox HR: current smokers 1.59 (95% CI 1.20-2.09); ex-smokers 1.16 (0.94-1.45). Dose-response: >10 cigarettes/day 1.31 (1.06-1.61) vs 10 or fewer 1.07 (0.74-1.53); >10 years 1.27 (1.04-1.57); >10 pack-years 1.30 (1.05-1.62); shorter duration and lower pack-years not significant. Current smokers by sex: males 1.48 (1.11-1.97), females 3.39 (1.25-9.17); ex-smoker HRs non-significant in both sexes.

## Key Contributions

- First nationwide Korean cohort (per the authors) to quantify smoking intensity, duration and pack-years against implant failure.
- Treats former smokers as a separate group: modest, non-significant excess (HR 1.16), supporting cessation counselling before placement.
- Shows a larger current-smoker HR in females (3.39, wide CI) than males (1.48), although most women were non-smokers.

## Methodology

- Retrospective cohort, NHIS-HEALS 2016-2019, STROBE; Youngsan University IRB, consent waived (anonymized dataset).
- Follow-up from implantation (index) to failure, death or 31 Dec 2019.
- Exposure: self-reported smoking status at health examination; thresholds >10 cigarettes/day, >10 years, >10 pack-years.
- Covariates: sex, age, income quartile, disability, residence, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, osteoporosis, Charlson comorbidity index, BMI, blood pressure, fasting glucose, alcohol, physical activity. Multivariable model adjusted for demographics, comorbidities, CCI, BMI, alcohol and physical activity.
- Kaplan-Meier with log-rank; Cox models; proportional hazards checked (Schoenfeld residuals), no violation.

## Limitations

- Self-reported smoking (recall bias, possible under-reporting; no cotinine).
- No implant-specific data (bone quality, implant system, surgical technique, operator experience).
- Claim-based failure definition (reimplantation or removal codes) misses failures not billed and mixes failure causes (osseointegration failure, fixture fracture, nerve-injury risk).
- Short, medium-term follow-up; late failures not captured; no early/late split.
- Observational; residual confounding. Sex-stratified female estimate rests on few events (CI 1.25-9.17).

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

In Korean claims data, current smoking carried about a 1.6-fold adjusted failure hazard, with excess concentrated in heavier or longer exposure; ex-smokers did not differ significantly, so cessation counselling before implant planning is supported. Because ex-smoker confidence intervals include 1.0 but sit above it, this is not proof of full recovery. The data cannot say whether the excess arises early or late.
