## Three-line Summary

Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR; PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane) of clinical studies in adults reporting implant loss within 6 months and before prosthetic loading: 1,838 records → 962 after dedup → 28 full-text assessed → 8 studies included.

The 8 included studies showed substantial heterogeneity in how "early implant failure" itself was defined and assessed, and the authors synthesized three interacting aetiological domains — local (impaired bone healing, infection), systemic (host susceptibility, genetic predisposition), and procedural (surgical trauma, inadequate primary stability).

The main clinical message is preventative and mechanical: optimize primary stability, minimize micromovements during healing, and improve wound closure/infection control — but the tiny evidence base (8 studies) and inconsistent outcome definitions mean this is a framework proposal, not a quantified risk profile.

## Summary

This 2026 scoping review mapped the reported aetiological factors behind early dental implant failure — implant loss within six months and before prosthetic loading — and proposed a structured classification for them. Following PRISMA-ScR, the authors searched PubMed, Scopus and the Cochrane Library and screened 1,838 records down to just 8 eligible clinical studies in adult humans. The most consequential finding is methodological: "early implant failure" is defined and assessed so inconsistently across studies that their results cannot be meaningfully compared or pooled. Conceptually, the review organizes aetiologies into three interacting domains — local (impaired bone healing, infection), systemic (host susceptibility, genetic predisposition) and procedural (surgical trauma, inadequate primary stability). The authors' clinical conclusion is prevention-oriented: secure primary stability, limit healing-phase micromovement, and control wound closure and infection. Because the evidence base is only 8 studies and this is a scoping (not systematic) review, the framework is best treated as a hypothesis-generating map rather than a validated risk model.

## Key Contributions

- Names and quantifies the definitional inconsistency that blocks pooling in the early-failure literature — a limit that constrains how every risk-factor list (including the wiki's own overview) should be read.
- Proposes a three-domain aetiological framework (local / systemic / procedural) with early failure as multifactorial interaction.
- Reduces prevention to four actionable levers: primary stability, micromovement control, wound closure, infection control.

## Methodology

Scoping review per PRISMA-ScR. Databases: PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library. Eligibility: clinical studies in adult humans reporting implant loss within 6 months and before prosthetic loading. Screening: 1,838 identified → 962 after deduplication → 28 assessed for eligibility → 8 included. Extracted: study design, population characteristics, failure criteria, reported aetiological factors. Synthesis: narrative aetiological grouping into local, systemic and procedural domains. No meta-analysis, no pooled effects, no risk-of-bias-weighted ranking of factors.
