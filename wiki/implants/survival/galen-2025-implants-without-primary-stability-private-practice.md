## Three-line Summary
Retrospective study of 97 implants placed without primary stability (IT <10 N·cm or intraoperative spinning) across three implant systems in 63 patients at a private practice in Qazvin, Iran (2021–2024).
Overall success 95.9% (93/97; 95% CI 89.9–98.4%); immediate placements 100% (23/23) vs delayed 94.6% (70/74); no significant difference among implant systems (p=0.91) or placement timing (p=0.58).
In carefully selected cases with conservative loading and modern implant surfaces, absent primary stability can be compensated, though authors caution this does not replace the need for primary stability in routine practice.

## Summary
This retrospective study evaluated 97 dental implants placed without primary stability — defined as insertion torque (IT) <10 N·cm and/or intraoperative spinning — across three contemporary implant systems (Neodent GM Helix, Biotem AR, MegaGen AnyOne) in a private periodontal practice in Qazvin, Iran (2021–2024). The overall success rate was 95.9% (93/97; 95% CI 89.9–98.4%) using modified Albrektsson/Buser criteria at ≥6 months post-loading. Immediate placements achieved 100% success (23/23), while delayed placements achieved 94.6% (70/74), with no significant difference (p=0.58). All four early failures (≤4 weeks) occurred in posterior delayed placements; no late failures were observed. The study suggests that strict case selection, under-preparation in low-density bone, socket grafting/sealing for immediate sites, and a conservative two-stage loading protocol can compensate for the absence of mechanical primary stability, though the authors do not advocate replacing primary stability as a routine practice.

## Key Contributions
- First multi-system private-practice study showing ≥95% success across three implant brands (Neodent, Biotem, MegaGen) when primary stability is absent
- Immediate placements with socket grafting/sealing achieved 100% success despite IT <10 N·cm — no late failures in the entire cohort
- All four failures occurred exclusively in posterior delayed placements — anterior and immediate sites were failure-free
- Systemic conditions (diabetes, hypertension, hypothyroidism) did not increase failure risk in this controlled population

## Methodology
Retrospective chart review of 63 patients (34 female, 29 male; mean age 49.2 ± 12.7 years) receiving 97 implants placed without primary stability by a single periodontist in a private practice in Qazvin, Iran (Jan 2021–Sep 2024). Primary stability was defined as absent when IT <10 N·cm and/or spinning occurred during cover-screw placement. All implants were two-stage (submerged), loaded at approximately 4 months. Success assessed using modified Albrektsson/Buser criteria at ≥6 months post-loading. Statistical analysis used Wilson 95% CI and Fisher's exact or χ² tests.
