## Three-line Summary

Retrospective clinical study of 101 patients with 218 fitted implants followed 3–10 years, analyzing causative factors of implant body fracture (bruxism, occlusion, cantilevers, prosthesis planning, bone volume, implant diameter/number/inclination).

Implant fracture incidence was 2.3%; improper treatment planning, bruxism, and time of complication onset were the main factors, with typical size effect established only for bruxism, occlusal errors, and their activity duration — fractures concentrated in single crown prostheses.

Implant body fracture is rare but the most severe mechanical complication: occlusal overload from bruxism or inadequate occlusion during the first years after functional load — especially in single crowns without implant-protected occlusion — is the dominant causal cluster.

## Summary

This retrospective clinical study followed 101 patients with 218 fitted implants for 3 to 10 years and analyzed causative factors of implant body fracture, tracking biomechanical/physiologic overload factors (parafunctional activity such as bruxism, occlusion, cantilevers) alongside prosthesis-planning factors (available bone volume, implant area, implant diameter, number of implants, implant inclination). The incidence of dental implant fracture was 2.3% of investigated cases. Improper treatment planning, bruxism, and the timing of complication onset were the main factors leading to this complication, with a typical size effect established only for available bruxism, occlusal errors, and the duration of their activity. Fractures were observed most often with single crown prostheses, in combination with parafunctional activities such as bruxism and lack of implant-protected occlusion — leading the authors to conclude that occlusal overload from bruxism or inappropriate or inadequate occlusion, alone or combined, during the first years after functional loading can cause implant fracture. Limitations: retrospective design, abstract-only ingest (per-factor effect sizes, p-values, and raw fracture counts unavailable), and low event count inherent to a 2.3% complication rate.

## Key Contributions
- One of the few implant-fracture etiology papers with a denominator: 2.3% incidence over 3–10 years in 218 fitted implants, rather than case reports without a base rate.
- Separates overload factors (bruxism, occlusion, cantilevers) from planning/design factors (bone volume, implant area/diameter, number, inclination) in a single tracked factor set, with Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc Mann-Whitney testing per group.
- Identifies single crown prostheses as the prosthesis type with the highest fracture concentration, and pinpoints the first years after functional loading as the critical time window.
- Supports implant-protected occlusion (ImpOC) practice: absence of ImpOC co-occurred with fractures in the bruxism group.

## Methodology

Retrospective clinical study (chart review) of 101 patients with 218 fitted implants, follow-up 3–10 years. Tracked factors: parafunctional activity (bruxism), occlusion, cantilevers, prosthesis planning, available bone volume, implant area, implant diameter, number of implants, implant inclination. Statistics: Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc Mann-Whitney test for each group. Outcome: implant body fracture incidence and associated factors.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Implant fracture incidence | 2.3% of investigated cases |
| Cohort | 101 patients, 218 fitted implants, 3–10 yr follow-up |
| Main causative factors | Improper treatment planning, bruxism, time of complication onset |
| Typical size effect (significant) | Bruxism, occlusal errors, and their activity duration only |
| Most affected prosthesis | Single crowns (vs other implant-supported fixed dental prostheses) |
| Aggravating context | Bruxism + lack of implant-protected occlusion |
| Critical time window | First years after functional load |
