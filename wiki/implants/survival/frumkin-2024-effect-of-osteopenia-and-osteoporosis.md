## Three-line Summary

Single-clinic retrospective study using two randomly sampled cohorts: 112 patients with at least one implant failure (1990-2020; 94 received a second implant) for second-failure risk, and 152 patients over 50 years (implants 1994-2018; 1229 implants, 134 failed, 89% survival) for first-failure risk.

Osteopenia or osteoporosis (pooled as one positive category) was associated with second implant failure (p = 0.044 vs healthy; p = 0.0353 vs no OPN/OP), first implant failure (chi-square p = 0.0202; multiple logistic regression p = 0.0065), and early failure (<1 year; p = 0.0044), while diabetes predicted first (regression p = 0.0297) but not second failure.

The text reports only p-values (no effect sizes or confidence intervals), pools OPN and OP, is heavily female (34 of 38 OPN/OP patients), and gives inconsistent cohort sizes (152 vs 146), so the association is supportive but cannot be translated into a magnitude.

## 세줄요약

단일 치과의원의 후향적 연구로, 무작위 추출한 두 코호트를 사용했다: 임플란트 실패 이력 환자 112명 (1990-2020, 94명이 재식립)은 두 번째 실패 위험, 50세 초과 152명 (1994-2018 식립, 임플란트 1229개 중 134개 실패, 생존율 89%)은 첫 번째 실패 위험 분석에 쓰였다.

골감소증 (osteopenia, OPN) 또는 골다공증 (osteoporosis, OP)은 하나의 양성 범주로 합쳐 분석되었으며 두 번째 실패 (건강군 대비 p = 0.044, OPN/OP 없는 군 대비 p = 0.0353), 첫 번째 실패 (카이제곱 p = 0.0202, 다중 로지스틱 회귀 p = 0.0065), 조기 실패 (식립 후 1년 미만, p = 0.0044)와 연관되었고, 당뇨는 첫 번째 실패 (회귀 p = 0.0297)만 예측하고 두 번째 실패는 예측하지 못했다.

본문에는 p값만 있고 효과크기·신뢰구간이 없으며 OPN과 OP를 합쳤고 OPN/OP 38명 중 34명이 여성이며 코호트 크기 기술이 불일치 (152명 대 146명)하므로, 연관성은 지지되나 크기를 임상 수치로 옮길 수는 없다.

## Summary

This is a retrospective, observational archival study from a single private dental clinic (one principal surgeon, with a minority of patients treated intermittently by other practitioners) addressing whether osteopenia (OPN) and osteoporosis (OP) raise the risk of dental implant failure and of repeated failure at a replacement attempt. Sample sizes were calculated for a finite population (6000 patients seen at the clinic, 5% margin of error), then inflated by 20% for missing data, and patients were drawn at random from electronic-record lists.

Two cohorts were analysed. The second-failure cohort comprised 112 random patients with at least one implant failure between 1990 and 2020, of whom 94 received a second implant. The first-failure cohort comprised randomly selected patients over 50 years of age who received implants between 1994 and 2018 (152 patients in the Methods; 146 in the Results text), with 1229 implants of which 134 failed (89% survival). OPN or OP was recorded from a clinic health questionnaire and confirmed with physician reports, and both conditions were pooled as one binary "positive" category.

For second failure, most factors (sex, diabetes, smoking, prior bone loss, augmentation, immediate placement) were not significant, but maxillary implants failed more than mandibular (p = 0.0299) and patients with OPN or OP had a higher probability of second failure than healthy patients (p = 0.044) and than patients without OPN/OP (p = 0.0353). For first failure, OPN/OP (chi-square p = 0.0202) and diabetes (p = 0.0251) were significant in univariate analysis; in multiple logistic regression OPN/OP remained the strongest (p = 0.0065), followed by diabetes (p = 0.0297). Among patients with failures, a higher share of early failures (under 1 year) occurred in OPN/OP patients (p = 0.0044), which the authors read as a possible osseointegration problem.

## Key Contributions

- Reports an association of OPN/OP with first, second (repeated), and early (<1 year) implant failure in one clinic's data, in a sample the authors argue is representative (OP 18.5% and OPN 7.5% prevalence; early failure 1.4% of implants; overall implant survival 89%).
- Reports diabetes as a predictor of first but not second failure at the same site; the authors speculate that the failed first implant alters the local microenvironment.
- Found no sex difference in failure and similar failure rates (10-12%) across the minority implant designs.

## Methodology

- Design: retrospective observational archival study, STROBE-compliant, single clinic, anonymised routine data; patients consented to anonymous research use of records.
- Second-failure cohort: 112 random patients with at least one failure (1990-2020), no age limit; 94 received a second implant. Location and operative variables analysed per implant, other variables per patient.
- First-failure cohort: random patients over 50 years who received implants 1994-2018; those without complete medical history questionnaire excluded; 1229 implants. Maximum follow-up 25 years (second-failure cohort follow-up 2-25 years, mean 12).
- Exposure: OPN or OP from health questionnaire, confirmed by physician reports; pooled as binary.
- Implants: endosteal titanium; 87% tapered-body internal hex; 5.5% spline external connection; 7.5% straight-body internal hex.
- Statistics: chi-square (Fisher exact when n < 6) for univariate; multiple logistic regression for variables significant in univariate (first failure); Prism software. Early failure defined as under 1 year after implantation.

## Limitations

- Retrospective single clinic, one principal operator; generalisability limited.
- OPN and OP pooled, so no dose-response by severity; no bone density measurements, treatment status, or anti-resorptive drug use reported in the text; Medication effects are not analysed.
- Only p-values are reported in the text; effect sizes and CIs are absent, and tables are not in the retrieved text.
- 34 of 38 OPN/OP patients were female; sex-stratified analysis was not done.
- Time-to-failure analysis found no relationship; the early-failure comparison counts patients with early versus late failures only among failures, so it does not estimate an early failure rate in OPN/OP.
- Source-data inconsistencies: the first-failure cohort is 152 patients in Methods and 146 in Results, and the planned sample was 156; the second-failure rate of 20% is higher than some modern reports (authors cite a 4-29% range).
- Multiple chi-square tests without stated multiplicity correction.
- Authors state the mechanism (e.g. RANKL/OPG dysregulation) is speculative.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

In patients over 50 with known OPN or OP, treat early failure risk as raised and review bone-quality and healing-related preparation before placement, while recognising that this evidence is association-only with no quantified magnitude. For a patient presenting for replacement of a failed implant, a history of OPN or OP and maxillary site should raise caution about a second failure in this dataset. Diabetes is a separate risk for the first implant in this dataset. Nothing here supports a specific protocol or drug adjustment; use pooled evidence in the related pages for counselling.
