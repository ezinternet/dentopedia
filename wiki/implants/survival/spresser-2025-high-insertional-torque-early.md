## Summary
This prospective observational study from the Veterans Affairs North Texas Health Care System in Dallas (the same institution and senior author as [[implants/survival/carr-2022-low-insertional-torque-early]]) investigated whether *high* insertional torque predicts early implant failure. Of 207 implants placed in 95 patients, those seated at IT ≥60 N·cm were compared against a control group at 30–59 N·cm, with early failure defined as exfoliation, mobility during stage-2 surgery, or explantation prior to prosthetic loading. The high-torque group showed a statistically significant 9-fold increased early-failure risk (odds ratio 9.00, 95% CI 1.06–76.18, P=0.043). The authors suggest that although higher torque may offer greater primary stability, it may also contribute to bone resorption, impaired osseointegration, and soft-tissue loss. This is the **high-torque half of a deliberate contrast pair**: read against Carr (IT <30 N·cm → ~14× early failure), the two papers argue that both ends of the torque range raise early-failure risk, so the relationship is biphasic rather than monotonic, with the control band 30–59 N·cm emerging as the implied safe middle. One caveat separates the pair: Carr's comparator is ≥30 N·cm with no upper bound while Spresser's control is that narrower 30–59 N·cm band, so the two thresholds are complementary, not identical. The effect estimate carries a wide CI (1.06–76.18, barely significant at the lower bound).

## Key Contributions
- Supplies the **high**-torque mirror of Carr 2022's low-torque finding, in the direction opposite to "higher torque = better stability".
- Uses an active control band (30–59 N·cm) rather than an arbitrary low comparator, isolating the effect of *excessive* torque above the stable window.
- Provides a **prospective early-failure outcome** where the pre-existing high-torque harm evidence (Marconcini, Aldahlawi) is post-hoc-classified marginal-bone-loss/crestal-bone data.

## Methodology
- **Design**: Prospective observational study
- **Setting**: Veterans Affairs North Texas Health Care System, Dallas, TX
- **n**: 207 implants in 95 patients
- **Exposure groups**: IT ≥60 N·cm (high torque) vs 30–59 N·cm (control)
- **Outcome**: Early implant failure — exfoliation, mobility during stage 2 surgery, or need for explantation prior to prosthetic loading
- **Statistics**: odds ratio with 95% CI
