## Summary

This PRISMA systematic review asked whether **early implant failure (EIF)** has an intrinsic, patient-level genetic component, given that failures occur even under appropriate conditions in patients without local or systemic contraindications. Searching PubMed, ScienceDirect, and Scopus ("Dental implant" AND "Polymorphism", cutoff August 2019), the authors screened 225 records, read 24 in full, and found 10 meeting inclusion criteria, of which **5 studies** reported a polymorphism–EIF association. The association concentrated on **promoter-region variants of three matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) genes**: G-1607GG of MMP 1, C-799T of MMP 8, and -77 A>G of MMP 13. These promoter variants are hypothesized to change cellular transcriptional activity, with downstream effects on inflammation and extracellular-matrix degradation. The authors frame the work as establishing a relationship needed for **precision medicine** in dentistry rather than as a validated predictive test, and they note that risk of bias, methodologic quality, and heterogeneity of the included studies were assessed (no pooled effect estimate is reported).

## Key Contributions

- Introduces a **genetic/host-susceptibility axis** into the EIF framework — the "intrinsic component of the patient" that local/environmental/systemic risk-factor studies do not capture.
- Identifies the specific variants the included studies linked to EIF: **MMP 1 G-1607GG, MMP 8 C-799T, MMP 13 -77 A>G** — all promoter-region, all altering transcriptional activity.
- Frames the mechanistic link: gene polymorphism → altered MMP transcription → inflammation & extracellular-matrix degradation → disturbed osseointegration.
- Positions the field for **precision medicine in implantology**, while conceding the evidence base is only five studies.

## Methodology

- **Design**: systematic review (PRISMA); no meta-analysis.
- **Databases**: PubMed, ScienceDirect, Scopus.
- **Search terms**: "Dental implant" AND "Polymorphism"; cutoff August 2019.
- **Screening**: 225 records → 24 articles for complete reading → 10 met inclusion → **5 studies** with the polymorphism–EIF association.
- **Appraisal**: risk of bias, methodologic quality, and heterogeneity of included studies analysed.
- **Outcome**: association between genetic polymorphisms and early implant failure.
