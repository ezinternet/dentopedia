## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (642 patients, 876 ISFPDs, 2,241 implants, 2–6 units, mean 108 months follow-up) at a single specialist clinic in Sweden evaluating implant-supported fixed partial denture (ISFPD) survival and technical complications.

88 prostheses and 112 implants failed (CSR at 30 years: 72.7%); 33.2% of ISFPDs had technical complications; bruxism was an independent risk factor for screw fracture, implant fracture, and ceramic chipping; cantilever design increased risk of prosthesis failure and screw loosening/fracture; prosthesis span did not affect failure rate.

Bruxism and cantilever are the two key modifiable design/patient risk factors for multi-unit implant FPD complications; the 30-year CSR of 72.7% provides a useful long-term prognostic benchmark for patient counseling.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort study (Clinical Implant Dentistry and Related Research, 2020) evaluated all patients treated with 2–6-unit implant-supported fixed partial dentures (ISFPDs) at a single specialist clinic in Malmö, Sweden. The dataset comprised 642 patients, 876 ISFPDs, and 2,241 implants followed for a mean of 108.0 ± 76.2 months (~9 years). A total of 88 prostheses and 112 implants failed (26 before and 86 after prosthesis installation), yielding an estimated cumulative survival rate (CSR) of 72.7% at 30 years. Technical complications occurred in 299 ISFPDs (33.2%). Bruxism independently increased the risk of screw fracture, implant fracture, and ceramic chipping. Cantilever design was associated with higher prosthesis failure risk and screw loosening/fracture. Internal abutment connection and prostheses with two pontics carried higher ceramic chipping risk. Notably, the number of prosthetic units did not significantly influence failure or complications. Implant failure was the primary cause of ISFPD failure. Smoking was associated with lower implant survival. The study supports bruxism and cantilever as the two primary modifiable risk determinants in ISFPD outcomes.

## Key Contributions
- Large patient-level retrospective cohort (n=642, 876 ISFPDs, 2,241 implants) quantifying ISFPD-specific survival and complication rates over ~9 years mean follow-up
- Cox regression evidence that bruxism drives screw fracture, implant fracture, and ceramic chipping — providing hazard-ratio level data for the bruxism–ISFPD complication link
- 30-year CSR of 72.7% as a long-term prognostic benchmark
- Null finding that prosthesis span (2–6 units) does not significantly influence failure/complications — clinically useful negative result
- Identifies internal abutment connection and two-pontic configurations as ceramic chipping risk factors beyond bruxism
- Smoking confirmed as independent implant survival predictor in the ISFPD context

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort, all-inclusive (single specialist clinic). n=642 patients; 876 ISFPDs (2,241 implants, 2–6 prosthetic units). Mean follow-up 108.0 ± 76.2 months. Outcomes: implant/prosthesis failure and technical complications (screw fracture, screw loosening, ceramic chipping, implant fracture). CSR computed by Kaplan-Meier/life table analysis (30-year projection). Risk factors analyzed by Cox regression: bruxism, smoking, cantilever, abutment connection type, number of pontics, prosthesis span. Full statistical details (hazard ratios, CIs, p-values) not available from abstract.
