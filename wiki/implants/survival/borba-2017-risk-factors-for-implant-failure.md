## One-line Summary

Retrospective review of a Brazilian postgraduate implant clinic (2002–2014; 774 implants in 202 patients): 25 failures (3.2%), of which 22 were early (2.84%); in GEE analyses maxillary placement was the significant risk factor and bone augmentation showed a trend (P=0.054).

## 한줄요약

브라질 대학원 임플란트 진료실(2002–2014년, 환자 202명의 임플란트 774개) 후향적 연구에서 실패 25개(3.2%) 중 22개가 조기 실패(2.84%)였고, GEE 분석에서 상악 식립이 유의한 위험인자, 골증대는 경향(P=0.054)이었다.

## Summary

This study evaluated implant outcomes and risk factors in an educational institution, where postgraduate students placed implants under faculty supervision. Dental records of 202 patients with 774 implants (2002–2014) were analysed with GEE to account for multiple implants per patient. Survival was 96.8% at implant level and 91.8% at patient level; 22 of the 25 failures were early (before provisional restoration). Maxillary implants carried higher failure risk in univariate and multivariate GEE, and bone augmentation showed a borderline association. Only 13 implants were in smokers, so smoking could not be analysed.

## Key Contributions

- Provides an early-failure rate (2.84% implant level) from a supervised postgraduate teaching setting.
- Uses GEE with patient as cluster, highlighting the bias of implant-level logistic models.
- Maxilla is the consistent risk factor (univariate P=0.006, multivariate P=0.014); for early failure alone the association held (P=0.019 / 0.015).
- Bone augmentation trends toward higher failure (univariate OR 2.74, 0.93–8.06; multivariate P=0.054).

## Methodology

Retrospective record review (the paper also calls it cross-sectional) at the Institute of Dentistry, PUC-Rio; ethics approval UVA 945.096. Inclusion: patients with ≥1 implant loaded immediately or later; incomplete records excluded. Postgraduate students placed all implants under faculty supervision. Variables: age (<65/≥65), sex, systemic disorders, smoking, jaw, anterior/posterior, diameter (3.3–5.0 mm), length (short 6–8.5 / long 9–18 mm), bone augmentation. Early failure: loss before provisional restoration (or before first disconnection of an immediate provisional); late failure: after that point. Statistics: implant- and patient-based cumulative survival; GEE (forced entry) with patient as unit; SPSS 22.

## Results

202 patients, 774 implants; mean age 54.2 years, 59.2% women; 92.6% healthy; 13 implants (1.6%) in smokers; mean functional period 8.44 months. Survival 96.8% (implant) and 91.8% (patient). Failures: 25 implants in 18 patients (22 early = 2.84%, 3 late = 0.38%); early failures occurred at 1–11 months, mostly 2–5 months. GEE (reported with mandible as reference coding): jaw univariate P=0.006 (OR 0.247, 0.091–0.667), multivariate P=0.014 (OR 0.259, 0.088–0.760), interpreted as higher risk in the maxilla; bone augmentation univariate OR 2.738 (0.930–8.061, P=0.067), multivariate P=0.054. Age, sex, anterior/posterior and length NS. No failures in wide implants or in patients with systemic disorders.
