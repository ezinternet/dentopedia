## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 7 RCTs (1,859 participants, 3,014 implants) testing whether antibiotic prophylaxis reduces early dental implant failure in systemically healthy patients; RR 0.66 (95% CI 0.30–1.47), NNT=143 — not statistically significant.

This result mirrors the same conclusion from the parallel momand-2024 SR+MA which used the same 7-RCT dataset, reinforcing that routine prophylaxis does not significantly reduce early failure in healthy implant patients.

Routine antibiotic prophylaxis should be avoided in medically fit patients given negligible benefit (NNT=143) and antimicrobial resistance concerns.

## Summary

Mehrabanian et al. (2024) performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of 7 RCTs involving 1,859 participants and 3,014 implants to determine whether antibiotic prophylaxis reduces early implant failure (EIF) in medically healthy patients. Using the Cochrane RoB-2 tool and GRADE framework, they found that meta-analysis of these studies showed no statistically significant reduction in EIF with prophylaxis (RR 0.66, 95% CI 0.30–1.47). The NNT of 143 indicates that 143 patients would need antibiotic prophylaxis to prevent one additional early implant failure — a clinically marginal benefit. The same 7-RCT dataset was independently analyzed by Momand et al. (2024, BMC Oral Health) yielding identical conclusions, providing convergent evidence against routine prophylaxis in healthy patients.

## Key Contributions
- NNT=143: clinically negligible effect in healthy implant patients
- RR 0.66 (95% CI 0.30–1.47): confidence interval crosses 1 — not statistically significant
- Identical conclusion to momand-2024 (same 7 RCTs) — convergent evidence
- Provides stewardship rationale: marginal benefit does not justify resistance risk
- Notable contrast with esposito-2026 Cochrane (RR 0.34, NNT=19): different inclusion criteria drive divergent conclusions

## Methodology
- Databases: PubMed (MEDLINE), Cochrane Library, Web of Science; search to October 1, 2023
- Inclusion: RCTs comparing antibiotic prophylaxis vs no antibiotics/placebo, systemically healthy patients, standard implants
- Exclusion: medically compromised patients, mini-implants, immediate placement in infected sites, high-ROB studies
- 1,267 initial records → 7 RCTs, 1,859 participants, 3,014 implants
- Quality: 4 high quality, 3 moderate by RoB-2; random-effects model
