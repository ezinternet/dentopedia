## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center cohort (Affiliated Stomatological Hospital of Chongqing Medical University, China; implants treated March 2019-March 2024) of 1621 generally healthy adults aged 20-80 years (ASA I-II) in four age bands, with CBCT bone density, resonance-frequency ISQ, and early implant failure (loss within 6 months of surgery) as outcomes.

Early failure was 14.56% in the 66-80 group versus 4.99% (20-35), 1.07% (36-50) and 0.64% (51-65), and postoperative infection was 22.78% versus 7.34%, 5.76% and 3.51%; in the multivariate model, age 66-80 (vs 36-50: OR 9.235, 95% CI 3.208-26.587), posterior maxilla (OR 8.085), posterior mandible (OR 5.409) and periodontal disease (OR 1.885) raised risk while postoperative antibiotic use lowered it (OR 0.481, 95% CI 0.269-0.860); AUC was 0.823.

Age and bone density were strongly correlated (r = -0.85) so their separate effects cannot be cleanly isolated, HU values from CBCT are relative only, the youngest band had a higher failure rate than the two middle bands (not explained), and the retrospective design, clustering of multiple implants per patient and the antibiotic association (observational) limit causal reading.

## Summary

A retrospective single-center cohort from the Affiliated Stomatological Hospital of Chongqing Medical University (China) analysed 1621 generally healthy adults (ASA I-II, aged 20-80 years) receiving two-stage implants between March 2019 and March 2024, grouped into 20-35, 36-50, 51-65 and 66-80 years. Peri-implant bone density (CBCT, Hounsfield units) and implant stability (Osstell ISQ) were recorded immediately after surgery and at 1, 2, 3 and 6 months; early implant failure was defined as implant loss within 6 months of surgery, confirmed clinically and radiographically.

The 66-80 group had the lowest bone density and ISQ at every interval, early failure of 14.56%, and postoperative infection of 22.78%. Younger bands had failure rates of 4.99% (20-35), 1.07% (36-50) and 0.64% (51-65) and infection rates of 7.34%, 5.76% and 3.51%. Kaplan-Meier survival differed across bands (log-rank p < .001).

In univariate logistic regression, age, smoking (OR 1.901), diabetes (OR 2.068), osteoporosis (OR 2.389), periodontal treatment history (OR 1.772), posterior sites, implant diameter and postoperative antibiotics were associated with early failure. In the multivariate model, 66-80 years (vs 36-50: OR 9.235, 95% CI 3.208-26.587), posterior maxilla (OR 8.085), posterior mandible (OR 5.409) and periodontal disease (OR 1.885) raised risk, whereas postoperative antibiotics lowered it (OR 0.481, 95% CI 0.269-0.860). Smoking, diabetes and osteoporosis did not remain in the multivariate model. AUC was 0.823 and Nagelkerke R2 0.21; age and bone density were strongly correlated (r = -0.85) and the age x bone density interaction was not significant. OHIP-14 improved significantly in younger patients but not in the 66-80 group.

## Key Contributions

- Age-stratified early-failure data (four bands) with repeated CBCT bone density and ISQ in one cohort.
- Multivariate early-failure risk factors: oldest age band, posterior sites, periodontal disease; protective postoperative antibiotics.
- Shows a non-monotonic age pattern (20-35 band 4.99% vs 0.64-1.07% in middle bands), which the authors do not explain.

## Methodology

- Retrospective cohort, single center, standardized two-stage surgery by one team, cement-retained restorations after 3-6 months.
- Exclusions: ASA III or higher, bisphosphonates, radiotherapy, heavy smoking (>10 per day), bone-affecting systemic disease, edentulous opposing jaw, severe bruxism, pregnancy.
- Early failure = loss within 6 months post-surgery; infection = clinical signs or radiographic bone loss within 6 months; follow-up at 1, 2, 3, 6 months.
- Repeated-measures ANOVA, Kaplan-Meier, logistic regression (OR, 95% CI), ROC, VIF, interaction tests.

## Limitations

- Retrospective single-center; selection bias; multiple implants per patient treated as independent; adherence not assessed; CBCT HU only relative (authors).
- Age and bone density collinear (r = -0.85), so their separate contributions are not identifiable; the antibiotic association is observational and may reflect confounding by indication, and it differs from the RCT-based pooled estimate in the wiki (RR 0.66, not significant).
- Failure denominators per band are not given in the full text; the implant count is stated only in the PubMed abstract (1,821); the 20-35 band excess failure is unexplained; the bone density figures (e.g. 1.50 vs 1.90 "HU") are implausible as HU; the abstract's OR attribution (0.481 to age) conflicts with the full text (0.481 for antibiotics), and the full text was followed.
