## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-institution cohort at Wonkwang University Dental Hospital (Korea; nationality/ethnicity of patients not stated in the text, Korean setting only implied by the institution): 604 implants in 326 patients placed between the mental foramina (June 2010 - June 2023), with a mean observation of 44.38 months (range 1 day to 163 months).

Overall cumulative survival was 97.68% (14 implants failed; failure = implant loss or removal, denominator implants); log-rank curves were lower for immediate loading (chi-square 12.71, df 2), screw-retained prostheses (26.44, df 2) and cantilevered prostheses (5.25, df 1), and in Cox analysis only conventional versus immediate loading was significant (HR 0.00, 95% CI 0.000-0.509), a degenerate estimate the authors attribute to few events.

Timing is partly given: 3 of the 5 failed immediate-loading implants and 4 of the 5 failed screw-retained implants failed during the temporary prosthesis phase, but the text gives no overall early-versus-late split, no other effect sizes, and sex, age, smoking, diabetes, osteoporosis, hypertension, diameter, length and grafting were not associated with failure.

## Summary

A retrospective study from the Department of Prosthodontics, College of Dentistry, Wonkwang University (Iksan, Korea) analyzed implants placed between the mental foramina from June 2010 to June 2023. Of 631 implants in 343 patients, 604 implants in 326 patients met the criteria (all restored, at least one follow-up visit; exclusions for insufficient records, splinting to posterior implants, uncontrolled diabetes, head and neck radiotherapy). The text does not state patient nationality or ethnicity; the Korean population is implied by the hospital setting only.

Failure was defined as implant loss or removal, with the denominator being implants. Fourteen implants failed (overall cumulative survival 97.68%; mean observation 44.38 months, range 1 day to 163 months). Kaplan-Meier log-rank comparisons showed lower survival for immediate loading (chi-square 12.71, df 2), screw-retained prostheses (26.44, df 2) and cantilevered prostheses (5.25, df 1); opposing dentition (2.04, df 2) and prosthesis type (4.25, df 4) were not significant.

In Cox regression, only loading protocol was significant: conventional versus immediate loading HR 0.00, 95% CI 0.000-0.509, an unstable estimate that the authors attribute to few events and failure clustering. Of the failed immediate-loading implants, 3 of 5 (60%) failed during the temporary prosthesis phase, and 4 of the 5 failed screw-retained implants failed during the temporary restoration phase, which the authors read as excess stress or impaired initial stability. No overall early-versus-late failure split is given.

## Key Contributions

- Korean single-center data focused on the mandibular anterior region, including partially edentulous patients and five prosthesis designs.
- Identifies immediate loading, screw retention and cantilever as associated with lower survival curves, and places many of the failures in the temporary prosthesis phase.
- Reports no association of failure with smoking, diabetes, osteoporosis, hypertension, grafting, diameter or length, with the caveat of few events.

## Methodology

- Retrospective single-center cohort, IRB approved; failure = implant loss or removal (after Cochran criteria); loading defined after Weber (conventional more than 2 months, early 1 week to 2 months, immediate within 1 week).
- Kaplan-Meier with log-rank by prosthetic parameter; Cox regression for patient, surgical, implant and prosthetic factors; SPSS 25, p<0.05.

## Limitations

- Only 14 events; Cox HRs are extreme or unstable, so no other factor effects can be quantified, and null findings (smoking, diabetes, diameter, length) are underpowered.
- Screw-retained group included a temporary implant restoration cylinder, mixing temporary-phase and definitive-phase failures; retention comparison is confounded.
- Periodontal status, oral hygiene and bone quality not recorded; retrospective, non-randomized, mean follow-up only 44.38 months.
- Text inconsistency: the Discussion states survival curves differed by prosthesis type, but Results and Conclusion report no significant difference by prosthesis type. Tables were not in the retrieved text, so per-failure details are unavailable.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

In a Korean university hospital series, mandibular anterior implants survived about 97.7% over a mean of under 4 years, so the region is a low-risk site overall. The failures that did occur clustered in immediate loading and in the temporary prosthesis phase, which supports careful primary stability assessment and load control (avoiding excessive provisional loading, limiting cantilevers) rather than any claim about systemic factors, since the study was too small to test them.
