## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-institution cohort (Seoul Veterans Hospital dental center, Jan 2000-Dec 2009): 6,385 implants in 3,755 patients (3,120 men, 635 women; mean age 65 ± 10.58 years), mean observation 45.73 ± 12.48 months.

108 implants failed (cumulative survival 96.33% as reported); survival differed significantly by age, implant type/surface, length, location and prosthesis type (p < .05) but not by gender, diameter or bone quality; lowest survival in patients older than 79 years (66.67%), narrow (<3.75 mm, 92.39%) and short (<10 mm, 95.11%) implants.

Single-centre retrospective data with no multivariable adjustment, censoring-sensitive follow-up and operator learning-curve confounding (e.g. one early-era system with many failures) limit causal interpretation; smoking, grafting and loading protocol were not analysed.

## Summary
Retrospective cohort from the Seoul Veterans Hospital dental center (Korea), implants placed Jan 2000-Dec 2009: 6,385 implants in 3,755 patients (83.09% men; mean age 65 ± 10.58 years; range 18-88). Mean observation 45.73 ± 12.48 months with recall every 6 months. Failure was defined as implant loss or removal; Kaplan-Meier survival and chi-square tests with Bonferroni correction were used. 108 implants failed (22 before prosthesis delivery, 86 after loading); reported cumulative survival was 96.33%.

## Key Contributions
- Korean public-hospital implant-level cohort with survival split by eight host, implant, site and prosthesis factors.
- Late failures (86 of 108) outnumbered early failures (22 of 108), which the authors link to occlusal stress and prosthetic/maintenance problems.
- Significant effects: age (p = .048), implant type/surface, length, location, prosthesis type (p = .012). Not significant: gender (p = .059), diameter, bone quality (p = .26).

## Methodology
Retrospective analysis of consecutive implants placed by several surgeons and restored by several prosthodontists; eight implant systems; insertion torque at least 10 Ncm; GBR, staged augmentation and sinus elevation as needed. Exclusions: immunosuppression, cancer therapy, hormonal imbalance, osteoporosis, pregnancy, alcohol/drug addiction, psychiatric or immune disease, chronic intraoral infection, untreated periodontal disease. Survival time ran from placement to failure or last survey. Smoking, grafting, immediate loading, operator and occlusal scheme were not analysed.

## Results
- Cumulative survival 96.33% (as reported); 108 failures (99 in men, 9 in women).
- Age: survival fell with age; lowest in those older than 79 years (66.67%).
- Gender: failure 4.59% in men vs 6.39% in women, not significant (p = .059).
- Diameter (not significant): <3.75 mm 92.39%, 3.75-4.5 mm 96.90%, >4.5 mm 94.74%.
- Length: significant; short implants (<10 mm) 95.11%.
- Location: failure highest in maxillary posterior (6.27%), then mandibular anterior (6.06%), maxillary anterior (5.63%), mandibular posterior (3.65%); mandible survival 95.15% vs maxilla 94.04%.
- Prosthesis: splinted best, single implant 93.81%; overdenture failures only in the mandible (122 implants).
- Bone quality: tendency to lower survival from type I to III (type III 94.45%), not significant; type IV only 112 implants.
- Implant systems: Biohorizon RBT 99.55% and GS 99.53% vs Biohorizon RBM 93.74%, Paragon MTX 93.1%, Replace 94.81%; the authors attribute differences largely to period of use and operator learning curve.

## Limitations
- Retrospective single-centre cohort, predominantly older male veterans.
- Univariate comparisons only; no multivariable adjustment, so implant system, era and operator experience confound results.
- Reported 96.33% survival does not reconcile with 108 failures among 6,385 implants (about 1.7% implant-level failure); the source also names "Paragon TSV" in the Conclusion vs "Paragon MTX" in the Discussion.
- Initial stability, smoking, systemic disease and loading protocol not analysed; small subgroups (type IV bone, maxillary overdenture).
