## Three-line Summary

Retrospective 7-year single-practice cohort (213 patients, 810 implants; 2016–2023, Târgu Mureș, Romania) analyzing early implant failure (non-osseointegration before prosthetic loading) against age, sex, insertion region, implant type, and general health.

Non-osseointegration rate 2.71% (22/810); 77.3% (17/22) of failed implants occurred in patients with affected general health, and failed implants were most common at 10 mm length (40.9%); sex difference in failed-implant diameters was significant (p < 0.001).

The study found *no* significant association with diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or hypertension, and equal maxilla/mandible loss with female predominance — both contradicting the mainstream literature (Manzano: maxilla predominance; male predominance), so it should be read as a dissenting single-operator dataset, not a risk-factor confirmation.

## 세줄요약

7년 단일 개인병원 후향 코호트(환자 213명·임플란트 810개, 2016–2023, 루마니아 트르구무레슈) — 나이·성별·식립부위·임플란트 종류·전신건강 상태에 따른 보철 전 초기 실패(골유착 실패) 분석.

골유착 실패율 2.71%(22/810), 실패 임플란트의 77.3%(17/22)가 전신질환 환자에서 발생, 실패 최다 길이는 10 mm(40.9%), 성별 간 실패 임플란트 직경 차이 유의(p < 0.001).

당뇨·심혈관질환·고혈압과 유의 연관 **없음**, 상·하악 실패 동일 및 여성 우세 — 주류 문헌(Manzano: 상악 우세, 남성 우세)과 배치되므로 **확증이 아닌 반증 단일 술자 데이터**로 읽어야 한다.

## Summary

This single-center retrospective observational study followed 213 patients receiving 810 screw-type implants over seven years (2016–2023) in a Romanian private practice, defining failure as non-osseointegration requiring implant removal before final prosthetic loading. The overall early non-osseointegration rate was 2.71% (22/810 implants), and failures clustered in medically compromised patients (77.3% of failures). Mean/structural analysis showed failed implants were most often 10 mm long (40.9%), and a statistically significant sex difference in failed-implant diameters was found (p < 0.001). Critically, the study reports no significant associations with diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or antihypertensive/PPI medication, an equal maxilla–mandible failure distribution, and female predominance — all contrary to widely cited risk-factor literature. The authors themselves frame the study as confirming rather than challenging existing data, which is in tension with the contradictions they report; the dataset is therefore best used as a discording data point in a contested risk-factor picture rather than as decisive evidence.

## Key Contributions

- Provides a real-world 2.71% early non-osseointegration benchmark over 810 implants with a standardized, explicitly stated failure definition and follow-up schedule (1/3/6 months).
- Quantifies the host-susceptibility signal: 77.3% of failures occurred in patients with compromised general health — despite the individual systemic diagnoses (diabetes, CVD, hypertension) not reaching significance on their own.
- Documents the implant-dimension failure profile (narrow platform and 10 mm length most frequent among failures) within a system that used no wide-platform implants (>4.5 mm), leaving that dimension stratum untested.
- Reports *negative* findings on systemic disease and augmentation that oppose the mainstream literature, adding a genuinely discordant primary dataset to the early-failure evidence base.

## Methodology

Retrospective observational cohort in a single private implant practice (Detal Dor, Târgu Mureș, Romania), IRB 972/18.06.2020, 2016–2023. Inclusion: adults 21–78 requiring ≥1 implant, good or compensated morbidity (diabetes, CVD, hypertension); exclusion: uncompensated systemic disease, contraindications (oral-maxillofacial cancer), chemo/radiotherapy within the past year. 213 patients (78 M / 135 F) → 810 implants. All implants screw-type with conical connections, platform-switching or non-switching, diameters narrow (<3.5 mm) or standard (3.6–4.5 mm), lengths 8/10/11.5/13/16 mm. Surgical protocol: 45 s 0.20% chlorhexidine rinse, articaine 4% + 1:100,000 adrenaline local anesthesia, amoxicillin-clavulanate 1000 mg BID starting the day before surgery (clindamycin 300 mg TID for penicillin allergy), 4/0–5/0 Dafilon sutures, Bio-Oss + Bio-Gide for lateral sinus augmentation, vestibular bone addition where support was insufficient. Follow-up at 1, 3, 6 months with retro-alveolar and OPG radiographs; integration judged by Buser criteria. Data analyzed in IBM SPSS 25.0 (descriptive statistics, T-test). Outcomes: non-osseointegration vs age, sex, insertion region, implant type/diameter/length, and systemic condition.
