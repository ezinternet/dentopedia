## Three-line Summary
Retrospective cohort (n=214 implants, 103 patients, VA North Texas 2015–2016; time-to-event analysis, follow-up censored at 6 months) comparing early implant failure for insertional torque (IT) <30 N·cm vs ≥30 N·cm.
Early failures occurred in 14 implants (6.5%); implants placed below 30 N·cm were nearly 14 times more likely to fail early (hazard ratio 13.909, 95% CI 1.835–105.416, P=0.0108).
Low insertional torque (<30 N·cm) is associated with early failure in this cohort — but the estimate is imprecise, and it stands opposite to Norton 2017's low-torque survival finding.

## Summary
This retrospective cohort from the Veterans Affairs North Texas Health Care System (Dallas) examined whether insertional torque below 30 N·cm predicts early dental implant failure, using implants placed in 2015–2016 and a time-to-event design censored at 6 months. Across 103 patients and 214 implants, 14 implants (6.5%) failed early — before prosthetic loading — and implants placed at less than 30 N·cm were nearly 14 times more likely to fail than those placed at 30 N·cm or greater (hazard ratio 13.909; 95% CI 1.835–105.416; P=0.0108). The authors conclude that low insertional torque is associated with early failure and call for prospective studies. This is the **low-torque half of a deliberate contrast pair**: read against its sibling [[implants/survival/spresser-2025-high-insertional-torque-early]] (same institution and senior author, IT ≥60 N·cm → ~9× early-failure odds vs a 30–59 N·cm control), the two papers argue that *both* ends of the torque range carry elevated early-failure risk, implying a biphasic curve whose safe middle sits roughly at 30–59 N·cm. The contrast is not fully harmonious, however: Carr's comparator is ≥30 N·cm with no upper bound, whereas Spresser's control is the narrower 30–59 N·cm band, so the two cut-points are not identical. The effect estimate here is also very imprecise (CI spanning 1.8 to 105) and stands opposite to Norton 2017, where 30 implants at IT ≤20 N·cm — including three spinners under 5 N·cm — achieved 96.7% survival.

## Key Contributions
- Isolates the **low**-torque end of the insertion-torque–failure relationship, which the torque literature mostly debates at the high end.
- Quantifies a ~14-fold early-failure hazard for IT <30 N·cm in a defined VA cohort (HR 13.909, 95% CI 1.835–105.416, P=0.0108).
- Establishes ≥30 N·cm as the low-side risk boundary for early failure in this population, using a time-to-event (Kaplan-Meier + frailty) design rather than a crude failure-rate comparison.

## Methodology
- **Design**: Retrospective cohort with time-to-event analysis
- **Setting**: Veterans Affairs North Texas Health Care System, Dallas — implants placed 2015–2016
- **n**: 214 implants in 103 patients
- **Predictor**: Insertional torque dichotomised as ≥30 N·cm vs <30 N·cm, measured at placement
- **Outcome**: Early implant failure — exfoliation noted by the patient, or failure due to implant movement or pain necessitating explantation before prosthesis loading
- **Statistics**: Kaplan-Meier curves and a frailty model for group difference in time to failure; time to follow-up censored at 6 months

- [[implants/norton-2017-low-insertion-torque-primary-stability]] — **contradicts (typed)**. Norton's prospective cohort of 30 implants at IT ≤20 N·cm (three spinners under 5 N·cm) achieved 96.7% survival and argued that IT is a poor predictor of success because it measures rotational friction, not axial stability. Carr finds the opposite sign at a similar low-torque end; the two are not strictly comparable (Carr dichotomises at 30 N·cm with a huge CI; Norton is a small self-selected single-arm cohort), but they disagree on whether low torque marks failure risk.
- [[implants/survival/krisam-2019-factors-affecting-the-early-failure]] — the sharpest **null at the same cut-point**: Krisam's single-practice cohort reported identical early-failure rates in the IT <30 N·cm and ≥30 N·cm groups (4.8% vs 4.8%; mixed-model OR 1.0, 95% CI 0.3–4.2). Read together, Carr's 14-fold hazard and Krisam's null at 30 N·cm cannot both be the whole story — Carr's CI is wide enough to accommodate a much smaller true effect.
- [[implants/survival/galen-2025-implants-without-primary-stability-private-practice]] — context on the extreme low-torque end (IT <10 N·cm or intraoperative spinning): 95.9% success across 97 implants, i.e. *absence* of primary stability did not predict failure. Related in direction to Norton rather than Carr.
- [[implants/survival/cobo-vazquez-2018-effect-lack-primary-stability-survival]] — the conventional-placement analogue of the same extreme (92 implants documented without primary stability, 3.26% loss). This literature frames "no stability at all" as manageable, which sits on the opposite side from Carr's low-torque hazard finding.
