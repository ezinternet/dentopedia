## Three-line Summary

Murine experiment (adult mice; 10–15% ethanol in drinking water for 3 months pre-surgery) with a load-bearing porous titanium implant in the proximal tibia, comparing continuous ethanol against ethanol stopped immediately after implantation.

Continuous ethanol reduced peri-implant bone formation on histology and micro-CT and weakened the interface on pull-out testing; postoperative abstinence restored bone formation and interface strength to control levels.

Ethanol-induced osseointegration failure was **reversible** when exposure ceased at surgery — the one piece of held evidence that directly supports a perioperative abstinence strategy, in an orthopaedic rather than dental model.

## 세줄요약

뮤린 실험 (성체 마우스, 수술 전 3개월간 10–15% 에탄올 식수): 근위 경골 하중 부하형 다공성 티타늄 임플란트에서 음주 지속군과 수술 직후 중단군을 비교.

음주 지속군은 조직학·미세 전산화단층촬영 (micro-CT)에서 임플란트 주위 골형성 감소, 인발시험에서 계면 강도 저하를 보였으나, 수술 후 금주군은 골형성·계면 강도 모두 대조군 수준으로 회복.

에탄올로 인한 골유착 장애가 **가역적**이었음을 보여준 유일한 보유 근거 — 정형외과 모델이라는 한계는 있으나 술전·술후 금주 상담의 실험적 토대가 됨.

## Summary

Mice received 10–15% ethanol in drinking water for three months before a porous titanium implant was placed in the proximal tibia, then either continued ethanol to euthanasia at four weeks or had it withdrawn immediately after implantation. Continuous ethanol significantly reduced peri-implant bone formation on both histology and micro-CT and produced a weaker bone-implant interface on pull-out testing. The abstinence arm — ethanol stopped at the moment of surgery — recovered both bone formation and interface strength to control levels. The paper's importance in this wiki is not the effect size (which is directional rather than quantitative) but the **reversibility**: it is the only held study testing whether the alcohol effect can be undone, and it says yes, at least when exposure stops at the surgical timepoint.

## Key Contributions

- **Introduces reversibility to a literature that is otherwise only about risk.** All clinical alcohol pages in this wiki answer "is drinking associated with worse outcomes?"; this answers "does stopping change the outcome?".
- **Two orthogonal outcome modalities agree.** Histology and micro-CT both show the bone-formation deficit, and biomechanical testing confirms it is functional, not just morphological.
- **Uses a loaded implant.** The tibial model transmits load, so interface strength is a meaningful endpoint rather than a purely histological one.
- **Sets the clinical research agenda.** The authors explicitly call for trials of pre- and postoperative abstinence — naming the gap this wiki still has on the human side.

## Methodology

- **Animals**: adult mice, established murine tibial implant model.
- **Exposure**: regular water (control) or 10–15% ethanol in drinking water for 3 months before surgery.
- **Implant**: load-bearing, porous titanium implant in the proximal tibia.
- **Arms**: control / continuous ethanol to 4 weeks / ethanol stopped immediately post-implantation (abstinence).
- **Outcomes**: micro-CT, biomechanical pull-out testing, histological quantification of peri-implant bone formation.

## Clinical Reading

- **This is the citation for "stopping helps", not for "how long to stop".** Abstinence began *at* surgery in the experiment; nothing here justifies a specific number of pre-operative alcohol-free days.
- **Keep the model boundary explicit.** Tibial implant in a mouse — the transferable claim is about bone healing, not about peri-implantitis or plaque control. Say so when quoting it chairside.
- **Reversibility reframes the consent conversation.** A heavy drinker can be told the biological deficit appears reversible if drinking stops around surgery, which is a more actionable message than a risk ratio alone.
- **It does not licence a short abstinence as a substitute for reduction.** The experiment removed ethanol completely; it says nothing about cutting down.
