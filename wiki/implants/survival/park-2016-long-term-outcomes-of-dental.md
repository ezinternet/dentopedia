## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study of 902 implants in 346 patients aged 65-89 years followed for 2-17 years, assessing survival, marginal bone level and Cox-regression risk of implant loss.

Survival was 95.39% (implant-based) and 99.98% (patient-based) with 29 failures; marginal bone loss was 0.17 ± 0.71 mm; failures peaked at age 65-69 years (significantly greater than 70-74 years, P < 0.05) and varied by implant system.

Implant therapy can be successfully provided to elderly patients and age alone does not seem to affect survival; evidence is retrospective and based on the abstract only.

## Summary
Retrospective clinical and radiographic analysis of 902 implants in 346 patients older than 65 years (range 65-89) followed 2-17 years. Survival was 95.39% at the implant level and 99.98% at the patient level (29 failures); marginal bone loss averaged 0.17 ± 0.71 mm. Cox regression with shared frailty showed greater implant loss at 65-69 years than 70-74 years (P < 0.05), and differences between implant systems.

## Key Contributions
- Elderly-only cohort with up to 17 years of follow-up.
- Implant- and patient-based survival reported separately.
- Frailty-adjusted Cox model for clustered implants.

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort; serial radiographs for marginal bone level; Cox regression with shared frailty for implant loss. Abstract-only: further detail not available.

## Results
[확인] 95.39% implant-based and 99.98% patient-based survival; 29 failures; MBL 0.17 ± 0.71 mm; 65-69 vs 70-74 years P < 0.05; variation by implant system.

## Limitations
- Retrospective design (acknowledged by the authors).
- Abstract-only: definitions, covariates and subgroup estimates not verifiable.
- Implant-system variation may confound age comparisons.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
Age above 65 years alone is not a reason to withhold implant therapy on this evidence; counsel on the 95% implant-level survival over up to 17 years and attend to implant system selection.
