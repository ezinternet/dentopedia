## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (9 observational studies, 54–747 participants each; databases searched to 12 Dec 2024) pooling the association between alcohol consumption and dental implant failure.

Overall alcohol exposure was not associated with failure (OR 0.87, 95% CI 0.50–1.51), but heavy consumption raised the odds (OR 2.78, 95% CI 2.08–3.91, p < 0.001) while low-to-moderate intake was inversely associated (OR 0.40, 95% CI 0.21–0.76).

Heavy drinking — not social drinking — is the actionable implant-failure signal; the low-dose "protection" is explicitly confounder-prone and must not become patient advice.

## Summary

This PRISMA-guided systematic review and meta-analysis pooled nine observational studies (54–747 participants each) to test whether alcohol consumption is associated with dental implant failure, searching PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science to 12 December 2024. Pooling all drinkers together produced a null result (OR 0.87, 95% CI 0.50–1.51, p = 0.61), but the subgroup split by consumption level reversed the picture: heavy drinking was associated with a near-threefold increase in failure odds (OR 2.78, 95% CI 2.08–3.91, p < 0.001), whereas low-to-moderate consumption was inversely associated (OR 0.40, 95% CI 0.21–0.76, p = 0.01). Sensitivity analyses were reported as robust and no significant publication bias was detected. The authors interpret the low-dose inverse association as likely confounding and caution against reading it as a protective effect — clinically, the usable message is that heavy alcohol intake is a modifiable implant-failure risk factor, and a binary "drinks / does not drink" history is the wrong way to ask the question.

## Key Contributions

- **Separates dose from exposure.** Previous pages in this wiki carried alcohol as a yes/no covariate; this SR+MA shows that the binary variable is what washes the association out (overall OR 0.87 vs heavy-drinking OR 2.78).
- **Puts an effect size next to smoking.** The heavy-drinking OR (2.78) sits in the same range as the pooled smoking effect on peri-implantitis (OR 2.27), which is useful when counselling a patient who smokes and drinks.
- **Makes the "healthy drinker" trap explicit.** The low-to-moderate inverse association is named as confounder-prone (selection, socioeconomics, dental attendance) rather than as a biological effect.
- **Establishes the evidence ceiling.** Nine small observational studies is a thin base — this page should be cited as "association in observational data", never as a dose threshold.

## Methodology

- **Design**: systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies, PRISMA-compliant.
- **Databases**: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science; search cut-off 12 December 2024.
- **Eligibility**: observational studies (cohort, case-control, cross-sectional) assessing alcohol consumption against dental implant failure.
- **Included**: 9 studies, 54 to 747 participants each.
- **Exposure handling**: overall alcohol consumption plus post-hoc subgroup analysis by consumption level (heavy vs low-to-moderate).
- **Outcome**: dental implant failure.
- **Analysis**: random-effects pooling of ORs, subgroup analyses, sensitivity analyses, publication-bias assessment.

## Results

| Exposure stratum | OR (95% CI) | p | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall alcohol consumption | 0.87 (0.50–1.51) | 0.61 | No association when all drinkers are pooled |
| Heavy alcohol consumption | 2.78 (2.08–3.91) | < 0.001 | Modifiable failure risk |
| Low-to-moderate consumption | 0.40 (0.21–0.76) | 0.01 | Confounder-prone inverse association |

- Sensitivity analyses confirmed robustness of the pooled estimates; no significant publication bias was detected.
- The review does not report a grams-per-day threshold separating "heavy" from "low-to-moderate" — the cut-off is inherited from each primary study's own definition.

## Clinical Reading

- **Ask about amount and pattern, not presence.** A patient who answers "yes, socially" and a patient drinking daily to excess are pooled into the same variable in most older cohorts — that is precisely the variable that produced OR 0.87.
- **Heavy drinking belongs in the same pre-surgical conversation as smoking.** The effect size is comparable, and unlike smoking it is rarely asked about in a dental implant consultation.
- **Do not tell patients that light drinking protects implants.** The OR 0.40 is observational and confounded; the authors say so themselves.
- **This page is about failure, not peri-implant disease.** The peri-implantitis-specific estimate (OR 2.07) lives in [[implants/peri-implantitis/galarraga-vinueza-2025-peri-implant-disease-risk-factors]].
