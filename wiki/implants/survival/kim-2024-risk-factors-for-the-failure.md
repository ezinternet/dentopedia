## Three-line Summary

Single-center Korean retrospective study (Gangneung-Wonju National University Dental Hospital, Periodontics; 1999-2021) in which 80 second-time-placed implants, drawn from 259 failed implants in 170 patients (10,666 implants in 4063 patients overall), were analyzed for refailure.

The 1-year survival of re-implanted implants was 88.1%; the abstract names smoking (HR 5.066, p = 0.081), implant surface (HR 18.776, p < 0.01) and reimplantation timing (HR 0.086, p < 0.01) as risk factors, although the smoking p-value is above 0.05.

Re-implanted implants fared worse than first-time implants, so the cause of the first failure should be analyzed and modifiable factors controlled before reimplantation; abstract-only evidence, and nationality is not stated in the text (Korean setting implied by the institution).

## Summary
Retrospective analysis from the Department of Periodontics, Gangneung-Wonju National University Dental Hospital (Korea). Among 10,666 implants in 4063 patients placed between December 1999 and March 2021, 259 failed implants in 170 patients were evaluated and 80 second-time-placed implants met the inclusion criteria. One-year survival of second-time-placed implants was 88.1%. Smoking, implant surface and timing of reimplantation were identified as risk factors for refailure.

## Key Contributions
- Focuses on the less-studied question of how re-implanted implants perform.
- Provides a Korean university-hospital estimate of 1-year survival after reimplantation (88.1%).
- Identifies implant surface and reimplantation timing as factors associated with refailure.

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort from clinical and radiographic records covering patient-, surgical-, implant- and prosthesis-related factors; survival analysis of the 80 qualifying implants. Setting: single Korean university dental hospital (nationality of patients not stated in the abstract; Korean setting implied by the institution). Failure definition, early-versus-late timing and follow-up length are not given in the abstract. Denominator is implants (n = 80).

## Results
[확인] 1-year survival of second-time-placed implants 88.1%. Hazard ratios: smoking 5.066 (p = 0.081), implant surface 18.776 (p < 0.01), timing of reimplantation 0.086 (p < 0.01). Overall, 259 of 10,666 implants failed in this period (about 2.4%, calculated from stated counts). Authors state survival of second-time-placed implants was lower than that of first-time-placed implants.

## Limitations
- Abstract-only: reference categories, confidence intervals, follow-up, inclusion criteria and failure definition unavailable.
- Smoking is called a risk factor at p = 0.081; treat as a trend, not a confirmed effect.
- Very large surface HR from only 80 implants suggests few events and imprecision (inference).
- Single-center retrospective design; no early/late split reported.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
Before reimplanting at a failed site, identify why the first implant failed and correct modifiable factors (smoking, in particular); counsel that 1-year survival after reimplantation was 88.1% in this Korean hospital series, lower than for first-time placement. Direction of the surface and timing effects cannot be applied without the full text.
