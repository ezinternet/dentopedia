## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center, single-system study at Dankook University Dental Hospital (Korea) of 1,031 conical SLA bone-level implants (Luna, Shinhung) in 409 patients placed 2015-2017; early failure was defined as clinical mobility before final prosthesis or perifixtural radiolucency at the first radiograph after final prosthesis delivery, and 35 implants (26 patients) failed early (Table 1 count; the paper's text states an early-phase survival of 95.6%, i.e. 4.4%).

In the multivariate Cox model, resident surgeons (adjusted HR 2.86, 95% CI 1.37-5.94, p=0.005) and mandibular placement (adjusted HR 2.31, 95% CI 1.12-4.76, p=0.023) carried higher early-failure hazard, while sex, age, implant diameter, sinus-floor elevation and guided bone regeneration (GBR) were not significant; three individual residents had hazard ratios of 4.01 to 12.76 versus the reference faculty surgeon.

Medical history and bone quality or quantity were not analysed, causes of failure were mostly unrecorded, and the reported survival percentages do not match the table counts, so the effect sizes are best read as signals of an operator-experience effect rather than precise risks.

## 세줄요약

단국대학교치과병원에서 2015-2017년 단일 시스템(Luna, Shinhung; 원추형 SLA 골수준 임플란트)으로 식립한 409명의 임플란트 1,031개를 분석한 후향적 단일기관 연구로, 조기 실패 (early failure)는 최종 보철 전 임상적 동요 또는 최종 보철 후 첫 방사선 사진의 임플란트 주위 방사선 투과상으로 정의되었으며 35개(26명)가 실패했다(논문 본문의 조기 생존율은 95.6%).

다변량 Cox 분석에서 수련의 식립 (조정 위험비 [adjusted hazard ratio, aHR] 2.86, 95% CI 1.37-5.94, p=0.005)과 하악 식립 (aHR 2.31, 95% CI 1.12-4.76, p=0.023)이 조기 실패 위험을 높였고, 성별·연령·직경·상악동 거상술 (sinus-floor elevation, SFE)·유도골재생술 (guided bone regeneration, GBR)은 유의하지 않았다; 수련의 3명은 기준 교수 대비 위험비가 4.01-12.76이었다.

병력과 골질·골량은 분석되지 않았고 실패 원인은 대부분 기록이 없으며 본문 생존율과 표의 개수가 일치하지 않아, 효과 크기는 정확한 위험도라기보다 술자 경험 효과의 신호로 해석해야 한다.

## Summary

This study from Dankook University Dental Hospital (Korea) asked which factors influence early implant failure when implant system and centre are held constant. It enrolled patients who received implant-supported prostheses with a single bone-level, conical, SLA-surface system (Luna, Shinhung) between January 2015 and December 2017. The cohort was 1,031 implants in 409 patients (5 implants in patients lost to contact were excluded), followed for a median of 7.2 months (IQR 5.6-9.9) after placement. Procedures were done by 5 faculty members and 10 residents; sinus-floor elevation (SFE) and/or guided bone regeneration (GBR) were done when ridge height or width was insufficient, using a 1-stage or 2-stage protocol chosen by primary stability and need for augmentation.

Early failure was defined as clinical mobility before placement of the final prosthesis, or perifixtural radiolucency on the first radiograph taken within several weeks after the final prosthesis. Table 1 lists 35 early failures (26 patients); a further 8 implants were removed after final prosthesis cementation and counted as late failures. The text reports an early-phase cumulative survival rate of 95.6% (4.4% failure) and describes it as within the previously reported range of 1.3%-6.36%.

Multivariate Cox regression identified resident surgeon (aHR 2.86, 95% CI 1.37-5.94, p=0.005) and mandible (aHR 2.31, 95% CI 1.12-4.76, p=0.023) as independent risk factors. Among individual operators, residents 11, 6 and 7 had crude hazard ratios of 12.76 (3.89-41.88), 8.46 (3.17-22.56) and 4.01 (1.13-14.21) relative to the faculty member who placed the most implants. Cluster behavior was seen: 6 patients (23% of early-failure patients) had more than one early failure, accounting for 43% of early failures.

## Key Contributions

- Early-failure risk factors in a single implant system at a single centre, which removes system and surface heterogeneity that confounds multi-brand studies.
- Quantifies a surgeon-seniority effect (resident vs faculty) and shows it varies sharply between individual residents.
- Documents patient-level clustering of early failures and links it to the need to identify the cause before re-placing at a failed site.

## Methodology

- Retrospective chart review, STROBE, Dankook University IRB approved; unit of analysis the implant.
- Data: age, sex, surgeon seniority and identity, implant diameter and length, jaw, position, 1-stage vs 2-stage protocol, SFE approach, preoperative and simultaneous GBR, dates of placement, removal, final prosthesis and loading.
- Stability checks by Periotest or resonance frequency analysis (Osstell ISQ) at 1-2 month visits; referral to prosthodontics when Periotest value was below 0 or ISQ above 70.
- Statistics: Kaplan-Meier survival; univariate Cox then backward stepwise multivariate Cox with Wald test; significance p<0.05; RStudio.

- Faculty 1 (reference) placed 406 implants (6 failures); the three significantly worse residents placed 26, 108 and 65 implants (5, 12 and 4 failures).

## Limitations

- Medical history was not scrutinised and bone quality or quantity was not analysed (the authors treat concurrent GBR/SFE as only a proxy for insufficient bone).
- Primary stability (insertion torque, ISQ at placement) was not recorded, so surgeon and diameter effects cannot be separated from stability.
- Short follow-up (median 7.2 months); 42 implants in 16 patients had not reached prosthesis delivery by the end of the study (June 2019).
- Source-data inconsistencies: 35 failures of 1,031 implants is 3.4%, not the stated 4.4% (95.6% CSR); faculty 11/640 (1.7%) and residents 24/391 (6.1%) do not match the stated 97.5% and 92.6%. The mandible effect is not significant in univariate analysis (p=0.117) and emerges only after adjustment, and the authors themselves say it contradicts a meta-analysis that found maxilla unfavourable. Table 3 shows the multivariate model with three retained variables (mandible, resident, length).
- The authors' article does not provide a randomised or prospective comparison of surgeons, so the resident effect may be confounded by case selection.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

In a university hospital where residents place implants, early failure clustered with less experienced operators and with a few individuals rather than with implant dimensions or augmentation, which supports supervised, competency-based training and consistent plans for primary stability (the authors suggest wider "rescue" diameters may reflect poor initial fixation). Patients with one early failure often had others, so identify the cause before re-placing at the same site. The mandibular finding should not be generalised, since it was non-significant before adjustment and conflicts with pooled data.
