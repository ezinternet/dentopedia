## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center study (Academy of Oral Implantology, Vienna, Austria; 2004-2022) of 371 patients (median age 63 years) who lost 2 or more of at least 2 placed implants: 3,141 implants, of which 1,090 failed, with a median retention time of 108.11 months (about 9 years).

Shorter retention was associated with nicotine use (P < 0.001), age < 50 and > 70 years (P < 0.001), maxillary location (P = 0.05), transgingival healing (P < 0.001), no provisional restoration (P = 0.035) and short implants (P < 0.001); patients with multiple failures showed cluster behaviour.

Abstract-only: effect sizes (HR/OR), the time window defining early failure and the multivariate model are not reported in the retrieved text, so the findings are hypothesis-generating for a highly selected multiple-failure population.

## Summary
Retrospective study of implant retention time and risk factors in patients with multiple implant failures at a Vienna referral center (2004-2022). Inclusion required placement of at least two implants and failure of two or more; 371 patients (178 men, 193 women, median age 63 years; 3.14% of all patients treated since 2004) with 3,141 implants and 1,090 failures (59.01% of all failed implants at the institution). Median retention time was 108.11 months. Patients with multiple failures showed cluster behaviour.

## Key Contributions
- Isolates a multiple-failure patient phenotype rather than the general implant population.
- Uses retention time as the outcome, with patient-, implant-, anatomical- and surgical-level covariates.
- Identifies nicotine, young (< 50) and old (> 70) age, maxillary location, transgingival healing, absent provisional restoration and short implants as correlates of shorter retention.

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort; descriptive plus univariate and multivariate analysis, alpha 0.05. Covariates: age, sex, medical history, medication, smoking, alcohol, implant properties, anatomical and surgical factors. Abstract-only: the early-failure time window, model specification and effect sizes are not available.

## Results
[확인] 371 patients, 3,141 implants, 1,090 failures, median retention 108.11 months. Shorter retention: nicotine P < 0.001; age < 50 and > 70 years P < 0.001; maxilla P = 0.05; transgingival healing P < 0.001; no provisional restoration P = 0.035; short length P < 0.001. Periodontitis-related tooth loss showed no tendency to early failure (reported as P > 0.001) and was linked to longer retention in the conclusion. The conclusion also lists single-stage surgery and immediate loading as higher-risk and age 50-70 years as protective.

## Limitations
- Abstract-only: no effect sizes, confidence intervals, group sizes or early-failure definition.
- Selected cohort of patients with at least two failures; no comparison group of non-failing patients; single referral center.
- Within-patient clustering may inflate implant-level significance unless modeled (not verifiable).
- Abstract inconsistencies: "P > 0.001" for periodontitis; single-stage surgery and immediate loading appear only in the conclusion while transgingival healing and no provisional restoration appear only in the results; 1,090 failures in 371 patients implies about 2.9 failed implants per patient.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
In patients who have already lost two or more implants, smoking, short implants, young or advanced age and unprovisionalized non-submerged protocols are the correlates to address first; the data are associative and from a selected referral cohort, so use them to flag high-risk patients for counselling rather than as predictive thresholds.
