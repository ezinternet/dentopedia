## One-line Summary

Retrospective single-practice cohort (Germany, 2012–2017; 186 bone-level implants in 106 patients): early failure before the final prosthesis was 4.8%, associated with augmentation (OR 5.5) and implants shorter than 10 mm (OR 5.8).

## 한줄요약

독일 단일 개원의 후향적 코호트(임플란트 186개, 환자 106명)에서 최종 보철 전 조기 실패는 4.8%였고, 골증대술 동반(OR 5.5)과 10 mm 미만 짧은 임플란트(OR 5.8)가 위험 요인이었다. 흔히 인용되는 "조기 실패 2–6%"는 이 논문 서론의 배경 서술(참고문헌 10–18)이다.

## Summary

This study asks how often implants fail to osseointegrate in an ordinary dental practice, outside academic or referral centres, and which factors predict it. Anonymized records of all 2012–2017 cases from one German practice were analysed. One bone-level system was used, placed by two dentists (one experienced, one supervised junior). Of 186 analysable implants, 9 (4.8%) were lost before the final prosthesis. All 9 were posterior, and 8 were in the maxilla. In mixed models accounting for multiple implants per patient, augmentation and short implants (<10 mm) carried roughly five- to six-fold higher odds of early failure. Smoking, diabetes, periodontitis history and insertion torque were not significant. The authors conclude that practice-based early failure rates match the 2–6% literature range, at its upper end.

## Key Contributions

- **Practice-based benchmark:** 4.8% implant-level early failure in a general practice with an implantology focus, at the upper margin of the 2–6% range that the introduction cites from refs 10–18 (Chrcanovic 2016, Antoun 2017, Olate 2010, and others).
- **Implant-related risk factors, accounting for patient clustering:**
  - augmentation: OR 5.5 (95% CI 1.4–21.7), p = 0.017
  - length <10 mm: OR 5.8 (95% CI 1.3–26.4), p = 0.023
- **Site concentration:** all failures were posterior and 8 of 9 maxillary. These were trends only (maxilla OR 5.6, p = 0.11).
- **Null host factors in a small sample:** smoking OR 1.7 (p = 0.49). The authors calculate that about 3,169 implants would be needed to detect a smoking effect.

## Methodology

- **Design:** retrospective cohort from a single German dental practice (Hirschhorn), with all implant cases 2012–2017. Ethics approval S-248/2017; consent waived for anonymized data.
- **Implants:** blueSky (Bredent) bone-level implants, minimum insertion torque 20 Ncm, abutments at 25 Ncm. The surgeon chose loading time according to stability and augmentation; some were immediately loaded.
- **Sample:** 206 implants in 116 patients placed. After excluding 7 one-piece 2.8 mm implants and 13 implants not yet restored or lost to follow-up, 186 implants in 106 patients were analysed (about 90% response).
- **Patients:** mean age 60.6 years, 58.5% female, about 20% smokers, about 10% diabetic, 26% with a periodontitis history.
- **Outcome:** osseointegration success (yes/no) at incorporation of the final prosthesis. Mean healing time was 147.8 days (maxilla 154.3, mandible 137.8).
- **Predictors:** all dichotomised. Age, gender, number of diseases and medications (median split), smoking, diabetes, periodontitis history, length <10 mm, diameter <4 mm, posterior site, maxilla, torque <30 Ncm, augmentation, open/closed healing.
- **Statistics:** chi-squared bivariate tests, then univariate mixed models with patient as a random effect. Firth-corrected logistic regression was used where models did not converge (posterior site, healing mode). No multivariable model. SAS 9.4.

## Results

- **Early failure:** 9/186 implants (4.8%).
- **Bivariate:**
  - Short <10 mm: 17.6% vs 3.6%, p = 0.010
  - Augmentation: 13.2% vs 2.7%, p = 0.007
  - Maxilla: 7.1% vs mandible 1.4%, p = 0.072
  - Posterior: 6.3% vs anterior 0%, p = 0.092
  - Closed healing: 6.1% vs open 0%, p = 0.113
  - Smokers: 6.8% vs 4.2%, p = 0.48
  - Diabetes: 5.3% vs 4.8%, p = 0.93
  - Torque <30 vs ≥30 Ncm: 4.8% vs 4.8%
- **Mixed model (OR, 95% CI):**
  - augmentation 5.5 (1.4–21.7)
  - short implant 5.8 (1.3–26.4)
  - maxilla 5.6 (0.7–47.6)
  - posterior 6.1 (0.3–111.3)
  - smoker 1.7 (0.4–7.1)
  - diabetes 1.1 (0.1–9.7)
  - lower torque 1.0 (0.3–4.2)
- Failed implants had insertion torques of 20–45 Ncm (mean 32). No immediately loaded implant failed.
- **Caveats:**
  - Only 9 events, and all models are univariate.
  - Augmentation, short length, posterior site and maxilla are correlated markers of deficient bone, so their effects cannot be separated.
  - Short implants are chosen where bone height is limited (indication bias).
