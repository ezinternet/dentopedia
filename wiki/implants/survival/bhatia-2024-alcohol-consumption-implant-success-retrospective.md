## Three-line Summary

Single-centre retrospective cohort (implants placed 2010–2022; mean age 59 ± 11.3 years; sample size not reported) relating self-reported alcohol intake within 3 months of placement to peri-implantitis.

Peri-implantitis incidence was 11.5% in light/moderate drinkers versus 46.2% in heavy drinkers, with moderate intake showing a 79.1% reduction (P = 0.0365), light intake 51.3% (P = 0.026), and heavy drinking a significant increase (P = 0.0001).

The heavy-drinking arm is the usable signal and the low-dose inverse association is the trap — with no reported sample size in the article, this cohort grades a hypothesis, not a risk estimate.

## 세줄요약

단일기관 후속 관찰 연구 (2010–2022년 식립, 평균 연령 59 ± 11.3세, **표본 수 미기재**): 식립 후 3개월 내 음주력과 주위염 발생의 연관 분석.

주위염 발생률은 저·중등도 음주 11.5% 대 과량 음주 46.2%였고, 비음주 대비 중등도 음주 79.1% 감소 (P = 0.0365), 저용량 51.3% 감소 (P = 0.026), 과량 음주는 유의하게 증가 (P = 0.0001).

임상적으로 쓸 수 있는 신호는 과량 음주군이며 저용량의 역방향 연관은 해석 함정 — 표본 수가 보고되지 않아 위험 추정치가 아닌 가설 수준 근거로만 다뤄야 함.

## Summary

A single-centre retrospective cohort assembled implants placed between 2010 and 2022 and classified patients by alcohol intake recorded within three months of placement, testing whether intake level relates to implant success and peri-implantitis. The dose gradient was steep: peri-implantitis affected 11.5% of light/moderate drinkers and 46.2% of heavy drinkers, with moderate intake associated with a 79.1% reduction (P = 0.0365) and light intake a 51.3% reduction (P = 0.026) relative to abstainers, while heavy drinking was associated with a significant increase (P = 0.0001). Most events occurred within the first two years (median time to peri-implantitis 31.3 ± 22.2 months). The report is thin by modern reporting standards — it never states its sample size and gives no adjustment table — so its value in this wiki is the concrete dose-gradient percentages that sit underneath the pooled estimates in the systematic reviews, not an independent risk estimate.

## Key Contributions

- **Gives the dose gradient in absolute terms.** The systematic reviews report odds ratios; this cohort reports incidence — 11.5% vs 46.2% — which is what a patient can actually picture.
- **Locates the event in time.** Median time to peri-implantitis was 31.3 ± 22.2 months with most events inside two years, reinforcing that the early post-loading window is where a drinking habit bites.
- **Reproduces the low-dose inverse association.** The seeming benefit of light/moderate drinking appears here as it does in the pooled literature — which makes the confounding story, not the biology, the leading explanation.
- **Documents why binary exposure is inadequate.** Splitting intake into three levels is the entire reason this small cohort shows a signal that earlier binary-exposure cohorts missed.

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective cohort, tertiary care centre.
- **Period**: implants inserted 2010–2022.
- **Exposure**: alcohol intake from health maintenance records, collected within 3 months of implant placement; classified light / moderate / heavy / none.
- **Outcomes**: implant success or failure, and peri-implantitis at follow-up.
- **Analysis**: logistic regression, Wald test per parameter, odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals, α = 0.05.
- **Sample**: **not reported in the article** (mean age at placement 59 ± 11.3 years is the only sample descriptor given).

## Results

| Stratum | Peri-implantitis incidence | Change vs abstainers | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | 11.5% (reported jointly with moderate) | −51.3% | 0.026 |
| Moderate | 11.5% (reported jointly with light) | −79.1% | 0.0365 |
| Heavy | 46.2% | Increased | 0.0001 |

- Mean age at placement: 59 ± 11.3 years.
- Median time to peri-implantitis: 31.3 ± 22.2 months; most events within 2 years of placement.
- Light and moderate strata are pooled in the reported incidence figure, so monotonicity cannot be confirmed from the available text.

## Clinical Reading

- **The heavy-drinking number is the one to quote.** Roughly one in two heavy drinkers developing peri-implantitis is a far stronger counselling hook than an odds ratio, and it matches the pooled heavy-drinking OR 2.78 for failure.
- **Do not quote the 79% reduction.** It is the classic healthy-drinker artefact (lighter drinkers tend to be wealthier, better-attended, less often smokers); the meta-analysis authors say the same about their own OR 0.40.
- **Timing matters for the pre-surgical conversation.** With most events inside two years, a heavy drinker's abstinence plan is most valuable across the first two years of function, not indefinitely postponed.
- **Treat the precision as unknown.** With no stated sample size, do not present any of these percentages as a rate with a confidence interval.
