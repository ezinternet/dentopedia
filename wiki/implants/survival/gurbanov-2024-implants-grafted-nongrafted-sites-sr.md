## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA) of controlled clinical studies with ≥10-year follow-up comparing ISR in grafted vs non-grafted sites; 3 studies (n = 34–96 patients each) included from 6 databases.
ISR ranged 96–100% in grafted sites and 91.6–94% in non-grafted sites; all studies rated low risk of bias (ROBINS-I); meta-analysis was not feasible due to high heterogeneity.
Implants in grafted and non-grafted sites demonstrate similar long-term survival, but the evidence base is critically thin — only 3 eligible studies exist, none with prior sample-size estimation.

## Summary

This PRISMA-compliant systematic review searched six major databases through May 2024 for controlled clinical studies with at least 10 years of implant-in-function follow-up, comparing sites that had undergone bone grafting (GBR or autogenous) versus non-grafted sites. From an initial pool of 900 records, only 3 studies met eligibility criteria: Daubert et al (cross-sectional case-control, n = 59 + 37), De Moraes et al (retrospective cohort, n = 22 + 20), and Roccuzzo et al (non-randomised case-control, n = 19 + 15). ISR in grafted sites ranged from 96% to 100% and in non-grafted sites from 91.6% to 94%. All three studies received low-risk-of-bias ratings on ROBINS-I. The authors concluded that grafted and non-grafted sites support comparable long-term implant survival, while emphasising that the evidence base is critically small and that high inter-study heterogeneity precluded pooled meta-analysis. Well-designed, power-adjusted prospective trials remain a substantial unmet need.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes that with a strict ≥10-year threshold, only 3 eligible controlled studies exist globally — demonstrating how sparse the long-term grafted-vs-non-grafted evidence base is
- Documents ISR of 96–100% for implants placed in GBR/autogenous-graft sites at 10 years, numerically higher than non-grafted controls (91.6–94%), though formal comparison is not statistically poolable
- All included studies achieved low risk of bias (ROBINS-I), providing moderate confidence in the direction of the finding despite small n
- No prior sample-size estimation was conducted in any included study — power adequacy unknown

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review without meta-analysis; PRISMA 2020
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, EMBASE, Scopus, ISI Web of Knowledge, OVID, Google Scholar — inception through May 2024, no language restriction
- **Focused question (PICO)**: P = patients with implants in function ≥10 yr; I = grafted sites (GBR or autogenous bone); C = non-grafted sites; O = ISR
- **Inclusion**: Longitudinal controlled clinical studies, adults ≥18 yr, ≥10-yr follow-up, comparison grafted vs non-grafted, ISR + clinicoradiographic evaluation
- **Exclusion**: Case reports/series, review articles, animal/in-vitro/in-vivo experimental
- **Risk of bias**: ROBINS-I tool; OCEBM classification for evidence level
- **Studies included**: 3 (Daubert et al, De Moraes et al, Roccuzzo et al); all non-randomised controlled designs (OCEBM Level 3–4)
