## Three-line Summary
Split-mouth RCT (28 patients, 56 implant sites) comparing ITI nonsubmerged (one-stage) vs Brånemark submerged (two-stage) implants supporting fixed partial bridges in the partially edentulous maxilla, with 1-year loading follow-up.
No significant difference in implant survival (2/28 Brånemark lost pre-loading, 1/28 ITI lost at 1 year) or marginal bone change (0.2 ± 0.08 mm vs 0.1 ± 0.11 mm at 1 year). Both systems showed bone loss before baseline (1.9 mm vs 1.5 mm from reference point).
Crater-form bone destruction suggesting peri-implantitis observed at 18% of ITI implants, though clinical signs present at only 2 implants — highlights importance of radiographic monitoring for nonsubmerged systems.

## Summary
This prospective randomized split-mouth trial directly compared the two dominant implant systems of the era — ITI (Straumann) nonsubmerged one-stage protocol versus Brånemark (Nobel Biocare) submerged two-stage protocol — in 28 partially edentulous maxilla patients. Each patient received both systems contralaterally, with fixed partial bridges delivered after a standardized 6-month healing period for both systems. At 1 year post-loading, no statistically significant differences were found in survival rates (ITI 96.4%, Brånemark 92.9%) or marginal bone level changes (ITI 0.1 mm, Brånemark 0.2 mm). However, both systems demonstrated baseline bone levels indicating pre-loading bone loss (likely from surgical remodeling), and a notable 18% of ITI implants exhibited crater-form radiographic bone destruction suggestive of peri-implantitis, though clinical signs were minimal. The study concludes that both systems are viable for partially edentulous maxillary reconstruction, but radiographic surveillance is especially important for nonsubmerged implants where subclinical bone loss patterns may develop.

## Key Contributions
- First prospective split-mouth RCT directly comparing one-stage vs two-stage implant protocols in the same patient, eliminating inter-subject variability
- Standardized 1-year radiographic MBL data with defined reference points for both systems
- Identification of a distinct crater-form bone loss pattern at nonsubmerged (ITI) implants (18% prevalence) not seen in the submerged group
- Demonstrated comparable short-term outcomes despite fundamentally different surgical protocols

## Methodology
**Design**: Prospective randomized split-mouth clinical trial. Block randomization (block size 4) assigned ITI or Brånemark to right/left side.

**Population**: 28 patients with residual anterior dentition in the maxilla requiring bilateral posterior fixed partial prostheses. Mean age not explicitly stated; all received 6-month healing for both systems.

**Interventions**:
- ITI Dental Implant System (Straumann): Nonsubmerged hollow-cylinder/screw implants, one-stage surgery, transmucosal healing abutments
- Brånemark System (Nobel Biocare): Submerged titanium screw implants, two-stage surgery with 6-month submerged healing

**Outcomes**: Implant survival (primary), marginal bone level change from baseline to 1-year post-loading (standardized long-cone paralleling technique), peri-implant clinical indices (plaque, bleeding, probing depth, suppuration), crater-form bone destruction incidence.

**Statistics**: Paired comparisons (Wilcoxon signed-rank for MBL, McNemar for survival) appropriate for split-mouth design; α=0.05.

## Results
| Outcome | ITI (Nonsubmerged) | Brånemark (Submerged) | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implants placed | 28 | 28 | — |
| Pre-loading failures | 0 | 2 (1 patient) | NS |
| 1-year post-loading failures | 1 | 0 | NS |
| Cumulative survival (1 yr) | 96.4% | 92.9% | NS |
| MBL at baseline (mm from ref) | 1.5 | 1.9 | — |
| MBL change at 1 yr (mm) | 0.1 ± 0.11 | 0.2 ± 0.08 | NS (Wilcoxon) |
| Crater-form bone loss | 5/28 (18%) | 0/26 | Descriptive |
| Clinical peri-implantitis signs | 2 implants | 0 | Descriptive |

No significant differences in plaque index, bleeding on probing, probing depth, or suppuration between groups at 1 year.
