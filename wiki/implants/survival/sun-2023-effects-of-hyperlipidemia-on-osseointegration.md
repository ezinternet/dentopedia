## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (MEDLINE/EMBASE, 1985–2023) examining how hyperlipidemia disrupts peri-implant bone biology and cataloging local statin-based modification strategies to restore osseointegration (골유착, OI).
Hyperlipidemia suppresses osteoblast (조골세포) differentiation via Wnt/β-catenin inhibition and PPARγ upregulation, promotes osteoclast (파골세포) activation via RANKL, elevates reactive oxygen species (ROS), and was clinically linked to peri-implantitis and implant failure (OR = 5.1) in one two-year study.
Simvastatin (심바스타틴) surface coating consistently promotes osseointegration in hyperlipidemic animal models, but drug loading efficiency is low and no dental-implant–specific clinical trial under hyperlipidemic conditions exists.

## 세줄요약

## Summary

Hyperlipidemia (고지혈증) — abnormal elevation of plasma cholesterol, triglycerides, and lipoproteins — affects approximately 40% of Chinese adults and is highly prevalent in tooth-loss populations. This review synthesizes how hyperlipidemia undermines dental implant osseointegration (골유착, OI) and proposes implant modification strategies to counteract these effects.

**Biological mechanisms of impairment:**
- **Wnt/β-catenin inhibition**: Elevated LDL and reduced HDL inhibit this pathway, shifting bone marrow mesenchymal stem cell (BMSC) differentiation toward adipocytes (지방세포) and suppressing osteoblast (조골세포) formation via BMP2-Wnt axis.
- **PPARγ upregulation**: Oxidized LDL and minimally oxidized LDL activate PPARγ, suppressing osteoblast transcription factors Runx2 and Osx; simultaneously promoting osteoclast (파골세포) differentiation and further inhibiting Wnt/β-catenin.
- **Adipocyte feedback**: Adipocytes themselves suppress osteoblasts and secrete TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, and RANKL, amplifying osteoclast-mediated bone resorption.
- **Titanium-specific risk**: TiO₂ surface oxidizes circulating LDL to oxLDL, enhancing osteoclast activity and local ROS — a hyperlipidemia-specific hazard not present in normolipidemic patients.
- **Inflammatory amplification**: Hyperlipidemia activates NF-κB (IL-6, IL-10 release), shifts macrophages to M1 pro-inflammatory phenotype, and impairs neutrophil function, increasing susceptibility to peri-implantitis.

**Clinical signal (controversial)**: A two-year follow-up study found hypercholesterolemia independently associated with peri-implantitis and implant failure (OR = 5.1, P = 0.046). A separate retrospective cohort found no influence. Obesity alone was not associated with implant failure in one study. More controlled prospective data are needed.

## Key Contributions

1. **Three-route local delivery framework**: Categorizes statin delivery into peri-implant injection, implant surface modification, and bone graft material modification.
2. **Simvastatin surface modification evidence**: Direct coating on micro-arc oxidized (MAO) titanium, mesoporous TiO₂, and biomimetic calcium phosphate surfaces all show improved osseointegration in hyperlipidemic animal models; alginate hydrogel and nanohydroxyapatite carriers extend drug release.
3. **Cerium oxide (세리아, CeO₂) as novel strategy**: Antioxidant nano-catalytic coating reduces ROS, protects osteoblast differentiation, upregulates BMP/TGF-β, and modulates macrophage M1/M2 balance — proposed as a hyperlipidemia-specific modification material.
4. **Bioavailability argument for local delivery**: Systemic statins require large doses to achieve bone-level concentrations, increasing side effects; local delivery (coating, injection, graft) concentrates drug at the implant site.
5. **Mechanical requirements for coatings**: Dental implant coatings must withstand chronic lateral loading forces; delivery systems (PLGA, PEG, PLA, hydrogels, nanoparticles) must balance mechanical integrity, degradation timing, and osteogenic efficacy.

## Methodology

- **Review type**: Narrative (non-systematic); no PRISMA, no quality scoring.
- **Search**: MEDLINE, EMBASE; 1985–2023; terms: hyperlipidemia, dyslipidemia, cholesterol, obesity, osseointegration.
- **Animal models**: New Zealand rabbits, SD rats, mice; high-fat diet hyperlipidemia; implants in femur/tibia/jaw.
- **Outcomes measured**: Bone-to-Implant Contact (BIC, 골-임플란트 접촉률), peri-implant bone volume, push-out strength, histomorphometry, micro-CT.

## Results

| Strategy | Model | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Simvastatin, MAO-roughened Ti | Hyperlipidemic rat | Improved BIC vs plain Ti |
| Mesoporous TiO₂ + simvastatin | Animal | Mesopores enhance drug load and OI |
| Fluvastatin + PGA gel, local injection | Rat tibia | Superior histomorphometry and push-in strength |
| PLGA microspheres + fluvastatin | Animal | Improved OI and bone mechanical properties |
| Vitamin D coating | Hyperlipidemic model | Reduced alveolar bone loss; effect "very limited" |
| Cerium oxide coating | Osteoporotic/oxidative-stress model | ROS↓, osteoblast differentiation protected, BMP/TGF-β↑ |
| Hypercholesterolemia (clinical) | 2-yr follow-up | OR 5.1 for peri-implantitis/failure (P = 0.046) |
| Hyperlipidemia (retrospective cohort) | Clinical | No influence on implants — contradicts above |

**Clinical guidance recommended by authors**: Pre-surgical lipid normalization; high-compression implant insertion (bicortical anchorage where feasible); post-surgical antibiotic prophylaxis; delayed second-stage surgery; extended healing before loading.
