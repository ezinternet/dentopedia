## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + sensitivity meta-analysis of 8 studies (4 RCTs, 4 prospective cohorts; 318 short implants ≤6 mm in 214 patients) with ≥10-year follow-up, comparing short (≤6 mm) vs long (≥10 mm) implants.

Pooled 10-year survival was 91.2% (patient-level, PL) / 93.7% (implant-level, IL) by complete-case analysis, falling to 89.7%/92.8% after loss-to-follow-up (LTFU) interpolation; short implants had significantly lower 10-year survival than long implants (RR 0.920 PL / 0.943 IL), but no significant difference in technical complications (23.5%), biological complications (mucositis 33.6%, peri-implantitis 0.4%), or marginal bone loss (MBL 0.277 mm).

Short (≤6 mm) implants remain a clinically acceptable long-term option (>89% 10-year survival) for severely atrophic posterior ridges, though survival is statistically lower than long implants — driven partly by screw retention (83.6–97.0% survival gap vs cement retention) — and evidence certainty is limited by moderate-to-high risk of bias and high attrition (LTFU 2.86–37.6%).

## Summary
This systematic review and sensitivity meta-analysis pooled 8 studies (4 RCTs + 4 prospective cohorts; 318 short implants ≤6 mm in 214 patients) reporting ≥10-year clinical outcomes — the first to isolate long-term (≥10-year) data for the strict ≤6 mm short-implant definition. Complete-case 10-year survival was 91.2% (PL) / 93.7% (IL); after applying an interpolation model to account for LTFU (assuming a 2× higher failure rate among dropouts, per Akl et al.), sensitivity estimates fell to 89.7%/92.8%. Direct pairwise comparison against ≥10 mm long implants (4 RCTs) confirmed significantly lower survival for short implants (RR 0.920 PL, 0.943 IL), with 85.7% of short-implant failures occurring late (post-loading) — consistent with an accelerating failure risk over time. However, technical complications (23.5%), biological complications (mucositis 33.6%, peri-implantitis 0.4%), and MBL (0.277 mm, dropping to 0.154–0.185 mm after excluding high-risk studies) showed no significant difference from long implants. Subgroup analysis found screw-retained short implants had markedly lower implant-level survival (83.6–80.7%) than cement-retained (97.0–96.8%), attributed to non-axial loading and stress concentration at the bone-implant interface given the inherently high crown-to-implant ratio (CIR) of short implants. Overall risk of bias was moderate-to-high (RoB 2 for RCTs, NOS for cohorts), and attrition was substantial (10-year LTFU 2.86–37.6%), so the authors urge cautious interpretation despite a clinically acceptable (>89%) 10-year survival for patients with severe posterior bone atrophy who are otherwise poor candidates for long implants.

## Key Contributions
- First meta-analysis to specifically quantify ≥10-year outcomes for the strict ≤6 mm short-implant definition (prior reviews were mostly ≤5-year follow-up).
- Applies a validated LTFU interpolation/sensitivity model (Akl et al., 2× failure-rate assumption) to correct for attrition bias in long-term survival estimates — addressing a known gap in earlier short-implant reviews.
- Pairwise RCT meta-analysis confirms short implants have significantly lower 10-year survival than ≥10 mm long implants at both patient- and implant-level, while complications and MBL remain statistically equivalent.
- Screw-retained short implants show a large, clinically notable survival deficit (83.6–80.7% vs 97.0–96.8% for cement-retained) — an actionable prosthetic-design signal.

## Methodology
PRISMA-guided systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42024522613); PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane Library searched to 20 March 2026 (8,778 records → 8 included studies, κ=0.97). Single-group meta-analyses (metaprop, Freeman-Tukey double arcsine) and paired-comparison meta-analyses (metabin/metacont, random-effects DerSimonian-Laird) in R v4.3.1. RoB 2 (RCTs) and NOS (prospective cohorts) for risk of bias; funnel plots + Egger's test for publication bias (none detected).
