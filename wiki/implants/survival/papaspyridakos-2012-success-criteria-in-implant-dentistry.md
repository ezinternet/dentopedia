## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (MEDLINE/PubMed) of RCTs and prospective studies on roughened-surface implants with ≥5-year follow-up, analyzing success at implant, peri-implant soft-tissue, prosthetic, and patient-satisfaction levels.

The most frequently reported implant-level success criteria were mobility, pain, radiolucency and peri-implant bone loss (>1.5 mm); soft-tissue criteria were suppuration and bleeding; prosthetic criteria were technical complications/prosthetic maintenance, adequate function and esthetics; patient criteria were discomfort/paresthesia, satisfaction with appearance, and ability to chew/taste.

Implant success should ideally be judged on a long-term primary outcome of the implant-prosthetic complex as a whole rather than on implant-level criteria alone.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (MEDLINE/PubMed) — 거친 표면(roughened surface) 임플란트, 최소 5년 추적 RCT·전향 연구를 대상으로 임플란트·주위 연조직·보철·환자만족 4수준에서 성공기준을 분석.

가장 빈번히 보고된 임플란트 수준 성공기준은 동요도(mobility)·통증·방사선 투과상·주위 골소실(>1.5 mm)이었고, 연조직은 화농(suppuration)·출혈, 보철은 기술적 합병증/보철 유지관리·기능·심미, 환자만족은 불편감/감각이상·외모 만족·저작/미각 능력이었다.

임플란트 성공은 임플란트 단독 기준이 아니라 임플란트-보철 복합체 전체의 장기적 일차 결과로 평가해야 한다.

## Summary

This systematic review set out to catalogue the criteria most frequently used to define "success" in implant dentistry, after screening MEDLINE/PubMed for randomized controlled trials and prospective studies of roughened-surface implants with at least five years of follow-up. Rather than reporting survival percentages, it classified the reported success criteria into four levels: implant, peri-implant soft tissue, prosthetic, and patient satisfaction. At the implant level the dominant criteria were mobility, pain, radiolucency, and peri-implant bone loss (>1.5 mm); at the soft-tissue level, suppuration and bleeding; at the prosthetic level, technical complications/prosthetic maintenance, adequate function, and esthetics; and at the patient level, discomfort/paresthesia, satisfaction with appearance, and ability to chew/taste. The authors conclude that success should ideally be evaluated as a long-term primary outcome of the implant-prosthetic complex as a whole. Clinically, it is the reference catalogue showing how inconsistently "success" has been defined and why outcome studies are hard to compare.

## Key Contributions

- Structured the heterogeneous implant-outcome literature into four success-evaluation levels (implant, peri-implant soft tissue, prosthetic, patient satisfaction).
- Identified the specific criteria most frequently applied at each level — notably peri-implant bone loss >1.5 mm as the leading implant-level threshold.
- Argued for judging success on the whole implant-prosthetic complex as the primary long-term outcome.

## Methodology

Systematic review without meta-analysis. Electronic MEDLINE/PubMed search for randomized controlled trials and prospective studies reporting implant-dentistry outcomes. Inclusion: roughened-surface implants and ≥5-year follow-up. Data were extracted and analyzed descriptively for success at the implant, peri-implant soft-tissue, prosthetic (5-year complications/function/esthetics), and patient-satisfaction levels. ## Results

| Level | Most frequently reported success criteria |
|---|---|
| Implant | Mobility, pain, radiolucency, peri-implant bone loss (>1.5 mm) |
| Peri-implant soft tissue | Suppuration, bleeding |
| Prosthetic | Technical complications / prosthetic maintenance, adequate function, esthetics |
| Patient satisfaction | Discomfort and paresthesia, satisfaction with appearance, ability to chew/taste |

Qualitative outcome: no pooled success/survival percentage reported in the abstract; the review's contribution is the criteria map, not an effect estimate.
