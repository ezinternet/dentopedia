## Three-line Summary

abstract-only — full text not retrieved

Retrospective chart review of 594 individuals / 2,537 implants with early implant failure (EIF) defined as loss before and up to the abutment connection, analysed by logistic regression at both the individual and implant level (α = 5%).

EIF occurred in 144 implants (5.68%) and 97 individuals (16.3%); significant individual-level factors were smoking (OR 2.54), absence of postoperative antibiotic therapy (OR 2.73) and bone augmentation (OR 1.83), while implant-level analysis added postoperative complications (OR 28.35), implant length ≤8.5 mm (OR 1.79) and diameter <3.75 mm (OR 1.65).

Age, sex, alcohol abuse, diabetes, hypertension and long-term medication use were unrelated to EIF (P ≥ .12) at both levels — a two-level signal set to be controlled where feasible, pending confirmation by cohort studies.

## Summary

This single-institution retrospective chart review evaluated **early implant failure (EIF)** — loss occurring before and up to the abutment connection — in 594 individuals receiving 2,537 implants, and deliberately modelled the association at two units of analysis. EIF occurred in 144 implants (5.68%) and 97 individuals (16.3%). At the **individual level**, smoking (OR 2.54), absence of postoperative antibiotic therapy (OR 2.73), and bone augmentation (OR 1.83) were significant; at the **implant level**, smoking (OR 2.90), absence of postoperative antibiotic therapy (OR 2.77), postoperative complications (OR 28.35), implant length ≤8.5 mm (OR 1.79), and implant diameter <3.75 mm (OR 1.65) were significant. Age, sex, alcohol abuse, diabetes, hypertension, and long-term medication use were **not** associated at either level (P ≥ .12). The authors advise clinicians to be aware of these associations and to control them where feasible, while stressing that future cohort studies are needed to confirm the risk factors.

## Key Contributions

- Estimates EIF risk factors **simultaneously at individual and implant levels**, showing how the same factor (smoking) yields comparable estimates (OR 2.54 individual vs 2.90 implant) while level-specific factors (implant dimension, postoperative complications) only appear at the implant level.
- Flags **absence of postoperative antibiotic therapy** as an EIF-associated exposure at both levels — a *postoperative* timing axis that sits next to the *preoperative* prophylaxis question settled (as not-warranted) by [[drug/antibiotics/momand-2024-antibiotic-prophylaxis-early-implant-failure]].
- Reports a **null systemic profile** — diabetes, hypertension, age, sex, alcohol and long-term medication all unrelated to EIF — sharpening the contrast with cohorts that do find systemic signals.
- Quantifies **implant dimension** as an EIF risk (length ≤8.5 mm OR 1.79; diameter <3.75 mm OR 1.65), overlapping the short-implant signal pooled in [[implants/survival/manzano-2016-risk-factors-in-early-implant]].

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective study (clinical-chart review).
- **Sample**: 594 individuals; 2,537 dental implants.
- **EIF definition**: implant loss before and up to the abutment connection.
- **Outcome**: early implant failure.
- **Analysis**: logistic regression of EIF against local, environmental, and systemic factors, run separately at individual and implant levels; α = 5%.
