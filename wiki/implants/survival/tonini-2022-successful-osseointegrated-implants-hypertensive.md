## One-line Summary
Retrospective cohort (1887 implants/602 patients, Brazil, 2000–2017): hypertension and antihypertensive medication use showed no statistically significant association with implant failure (p=.958 and p=.939).

## 한줄요약

## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (1887 implants/602 patients; 2000–2017, Brazil); normotensive (432 patients, 71.4%) vs hypertensive with medication vs hypertensive without medication; overall success rate 97.51% (47 implants lost); chi-square and Fisher exact tests without confounder adjustment.

Hypertension status showed no significant association with implant failure (92.99% vs 93.28% success; p=.958); antihypertensive medication use also non-significant (92.5% vs 94.1%; p=.939); no drug-class subgroup analysis performed.

Large Brazilian cohort providing a null result; absence of multivariate adjustment and drug-class analysis limits direct comparison with Wu 2016 (HR 0.12 protective) and Masri 2024 (significant EIF reduction) — population heterogeneity and lack of confounding control likely contribute to the discordance.

## Summary
This Brazilian retrospective cohort (602 patients, 1887 implants, 2000–2017) investigated whether hypertension or antihypertensive medication use was associated with dental implant failure. Overall success rate was 97.51% (47 implants lost). Neither hypertension status nor medication use reached statistical significance for implant failure. The null result aligns with Hamade 2024 SR+MA (OR 1.100, p=0.671) showing no overall hypertension effect on implant failure, and contrasts with Wu 2016 (HR 0.12) and Masri 2024 showing protective antihypertensive effects.

## Key Contributions
- Large cohort (1887 implants) showing no significant hypertension effect on implant survival
- Supports null hypothesis in SR+MA by Hamade 2024
- HTN group success rate comparable to normotensive: 92.99% vs 93.28%
- AHT users vs HTN non-users also equivalent: 92.5% vs 94.1%

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort, STROBE-compliant, 602 medical records from 2000–2017. Outcomes: implant loss. Variables: age, sex, HTN status, AHT medication use, number of implants, prosthetic type. Statistical: chi-square and Fisher exact test only (no multivariate adjustment).

## Results
Overall success: 97.51% (47/1887 implants lost). HTN vs NT: p=.958 (NS). AHT users vs HTN non-users: 92.5% vs 94.1%, p=.939 (NS). No subgroup analysis by drug class.
