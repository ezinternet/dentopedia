## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-centre cohort (Dankook University Dental Hospital, Korea; 2013–2023) of 137 patients with registered disabilities who received 465 implants, followed 0.5–10 years.

Ten-year cumulative survival was 98.3% overall (98.1% physical, 99.1% mental, 95.7% multiple disabilities); 7 implants failed, 5 of them biological losses before prosthesis delivery, and the 343 restored implants had 99.7% functional survival.

Early healing is the main risk window and mechanical problems (decementation 9.33% of implants) outnumbered biological ones; limits are retrospective single-centre design, no between-group testing and 25% of implants still unrestored.

## 세줄요약

후향적 단일 기관 코호트 (한국 단국대학교 치과병원, 2013–2023)로 등록 장애 환자 137명에게 식립한 임플란트 465개를 0.5–10년 추적했다.

10년 누적 생존율은 전체 98.3% (신체장애 98.1%, 정신장애 99.1%, 중복장애 95.7%)였고 실패 7개 중 5개는 보철 장착 전 생물학적 실패였으며 보철이 장착된 343개의 기능 생존율은 99.7%였다.

초기 치유기가 주된 위험 시기이며 기계적 합병증 (크라운 탈락 임플란트의 9.33%)이 생물학적 합병증보다 많았다; 한계는 후향적 단일 기관 설계, 군간 검정 부재, 임플란트의 25%가 미수복 상태라는 점이다.

## Summary
Retrospective single-centre cohort at Dankook University Dental Hospital (Cheonan, Korea) of 137 patients with officially registered physical, mental or multiple disabilities who received 465 implants between 2013 and 2023 (follow-up 0.5–10 years). Survival was judged by the clinical criteria of Misch et al., with the survival period running from placement to documented failure or last follow-up; Kaplan–Meier analysis gave a 10-year cumulative survival of 98.3%. Treatment followed a preparatory phase that screened patient and caregiver ability to maintain hygiene, and a tailored maintenance protocol (annual recall for cooperative patients; examinations under general anaesthesia or sedation every 1–3 years in severe cases).

## Key Contributions
- Long-term survival data for a special-care population, with "multiple disabilities" analysed as a separate category.
- Distinguishes overall survival (all placed implants, unloaded ones censored) from functional survival of 343 restored implants.
- Quantifies mechanical versus biological complications and the effect of temporary cementation in a retrievability-oriented protocol.

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort; inclusion required a government disability card, clinical/radiographic records and at least one follow-up visit after placement. Treatment used general anaesthesia, intravenous sedation or chairside behavioural management depending on cooperation. Kaplan–Meier curves (SPSS 29.0) overall and by disability type; p < 0.05 threshold. Mean age is reported as 55.51 years in the Methods but 46.3 years in the Results.

## Results
- 137 patients (85 men, 52 women), 465 implants (213 maxillary, 252 mandibular); physical 46, mental 69, multiple 22 patients. 35 patients (117 implants) had no definitive prosthesis.
- 7 failures: 5 biological before prosthesis delivery (failed osseointegration, infection, peri-implantitis; 1–5.5 months), 2 mechanical (fixture fracture after a fall at 85.5 months; abutment screw fracture).
- 10-year cumulative survival 98.3%; 98.1% physical, 99.1% mental, 95.7% multiple disabilities (no formal between-group test). Functional survival of the 343 restored implants 99.7%.
- Mechanical complications 17.20% (59/343) versus biological 5.54% (19/343). Crown decementation 32 implants (41 events; 9.33% of implants), porcelain chipping 12 implants (18 events), screw loosening 12, screw fracture 3; 5 decementations required prosthesis re-making.
- 189 implants (55.1%) were temporarily cemented; 33 of 41 decementation events followed temporary cement, and all recurrent decementation occurred in patients with intellectual disability. One patient with severe nocturnal bruxism had six mechanical failures; 10 patients with bruxism and 2 with clenching were recorded.

## Limitations
- Single-centre retrospective design; cooperation and caregiver involvement not standardised (authors).
- No marginal bone level data; no formal comparison between disability groups; only 7 failure events.
- 25% of implants still unrestored; patients lost to follow-up after prosthesis delivery were excluded; only patients judged compliant in the preparatory phase were treated.
- Crude Kaplan–Meier estimates without adjustment; parafunction probably under-recorded.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
In Korean special-care and university-hospital settings, this cohort suggests that implants can be predictable in patients with disabilities when candidacy is judged on patient and caregiver hygiene capacity, early healing is protected, and prostheses are kept simple and retrievable. Expect decementation, especially with temporary cement and in patients with intellectual disability, and plan recall (with sedation or general anaesthesia when needed) accordingly.
