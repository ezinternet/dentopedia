## Three-line Summary

An 18-year-old male with a 13.82 × 9.91 mm condensing osteitis (CO) lesion at mandibular right first molar (tooth 46) underwent same-visit atraumatic extraction and immediate KOC Plus basal implant placement, with the hyperdense sclerotic bone deliberately used as an osseofixation substrate rather than being removed. Conventional crestal implant was contraindicated due to buccal plate loss and low bone density; guided bone regeneration would have required a 5–6-month delay. At follow-up, no mobility was observed and the immediately loaded fixed prosthesis met functional requirements — claimed as the first published case of this implant type placed directly into CO.

## Summary

This case report (Cureus 2023) describes the first documented placement of a KOC Plus basal implant (King of Compression Plus; Simpladent, India) directly into a condensing osteitis lesion. An 18-year-old systemically healthy male presented with secondary caries and a history of failed root canal treatment on tooth 46. CBCT revealed a well-defined dense radiopaque mass (13.82 × 9.91 mm) at the root apices with loss of lamina dura and partial buccal plate loss exposing the mesial root. Differential diagnoses were condensing osteitis, osteosclerosis, and cementoblastoma; the final diagnosis was CO with asymptomatic persistent apical periodontitis.

Because the patient demanded immediate rehabilitation, and because conventional implant placement was not feasible (buccal plate defect, low crestal bone density, conventional implant would require GBR with 5–6-month delay), a basal implant was selected. The KOC Plus design uses bicortical apical screws for osseofixation in cortical bone — the CO lesion itself served as the dense cortical substrate. Insertion torque reached 80 N/cm. An immediately loaded fixed prosthesis was delivered the same day.

## Key Contributions

1. **First reported case** of KOC Plus basal implant placed into condensing osteitis, using the hyperdense sclerotic lesion as an osseofixation anchor.
2. **Single-visit rehabilitation**: extraction + implant + immediate loading in one appointment, avoiding 5–6-month GBR wait.
3. **Basal vs. conventional implant distinction**: basal implants rely on cortical bone osseofixation rather than cancellous crestal osseointegration; CO bone (dense, sclerotic) is biomechanically advantageous for this mechanism.
4. Surgical protocol detail: DOS1/DOS2/DOS3 sequential drilling, 80 N/cm insertion torque, angulation correction by abutment neck bending, immediate postoperative CBCT verification.

| Implant | KOC Plus (single-piece Ti alloy, bicortical screws; Simpladent, India) |

| Antibiotic prophylaxis | Amoxicillin 500 mg, 1 hr preoperative |

**Basal implant concept**: Unlike conventional crestal implants that require cancellous bone for osseointegration, basal (corticobasal) implants gain primary stability in cortical bone via mechanical osseofixation. Cortical bone resorbs minimally compared to crestal bone, making it suitable for compromised ridges. The KOC Plus design incorporates bicortical apical screws that engage a second cortical layer — in this case the sclerotic CO bone — providing high primary stability even without a functional buccal plate.

## Outcome

- No mobility at crown placement and at reported follow-up.
- Fixed prosthesis achieved proper functional requirements.
- No infection reported.
- Immediate postoperative CBCT confirmed implant position.

**Limitation**: Follow-up duration is not explicitly stated; no ISQ, marginal bone level, or radiographic bone loss data reported. Evidence level is case-report only.
