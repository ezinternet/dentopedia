## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional multicenter survey of 16 Korean institutions (May 2017 - April 2018) using the Korean Academy of Prosthodontics (KAP) criteria: 841 implant prostheses in 485 patients, with failure defined as KAP grade C or D; the unit is the prosthesis, not the implant.

Median prosthesis survival was 16 years; in the multivariate Cox model, treatment by a non-licensed practitioner (HR 5.5, 95% CI 1.216-24.630, only 6 of 841 cases), an implant prosthesis as the antagonist (HR 1.9, 95% CI 1.152-3.334) and plaque index 3 (HR 2.5, 95% CI 1.207-5.249) were significant.

Early versus late failure timing was not reported; the lifespan model (principal component analysis, biological age score) underestimated actual lifespan, with non-failed prostheses potentially lasting about 1.34 years longer than estimated, and patient nationality is not stated in the text (Korean only by setting).

## 세줄요약

한국 16개 기관에서 2017년 5월-2018년 4월 대한치과보철학회 (Korean Academy of Prosthodontics, KAP) 기준으로 평가한 다기관 횡단 설문 연구: 환자 485명, 임플란트 보철물 841개이며 KAP C·D 등급을 실패로 정의했다; 분석 단위는 임플란트가 아니라 보철물이다.

보철물 중앙 생존기간은 16년이었고, 다변량 콕스 모형에서 무면허 시술 (위험비 5.5, 95% CI 1.216-24.630, 841건 중 6건뿐), 대합치가 임플란트 보철물인 경우 (위험비 1.9, 95% CI 1.152-3.334), 플라그 지수 3 (위험비 2.5, 95% CI 1.207-5.249)가 유의했다.

조기 대 후기 실패 시점은 보고되지 않았고, 주성분 분석 기반 수명 모형 (biological age score)은 실제 수명을 과소추정했으며 실패하지 않은 보철물은 추정보다 약 1.34년 더 사용될 수 있다고 했다; 환자 국적은 본문에 명시되지 않고 한국 기관이라는 설정으로만 추정된다.

## Summary

This multicenter study, coordinated through Inha University Hospital (IRB approval, written consent) with 16 Korean institutions, surveyed patients who visited between May 1, 2017 and April 30, 2018. Prosthodontists trained in workshops assessed implant prostheses by patient interview and clinical and radiological examination, using the Korean Academy of Prosthodontics (KAP) criteria. Grades A and B were counted as success; grades C (defects allowing only temporary use) and D (harmful to tissue, immediate removal needed) as failure. A total of 841 prostheses (single crown, fixed full denture, fixed partial denture, removable implant overdenture) from 485 patients were analysed. Patient nationality or ethnicity is not stated in the text; the Korean population is implied only by the Korean institutions.

Important distinction: the outcome is prosthesis failure (a grade at the examination), not implant removal. Prosthesis survival is not implant survival, so the 16-year median should not be compared with implant cumulative survival rates such as those in Korean university hospital cohorts.

Median survival was 16 years. In multivariate Cox analysis, non-licensed practitioner treatment (HR 5.5, 1.216-24.630, p=.03; 6 of 841 cases), implant prosthesis as antagonist versus an FDP with natural-tooth abutment (HR 1.9, 1.152-3.334, p=.01) and plaque index 3 (HR 2.5, 1.207-5.249, p=.01) were significant. Kaplan-Meier median survival: dental hospital not evaluable, local clinic 16 years, NL 7 years, unknown 15 years; antagonist FDP 16, RDP 17, implant prosthesis 12 years; plaque index 0: 15, 1: 18, 2 and 3: 13 years. A nomogram ranked insurance coverage, clinic type, plaque index, antagonist type, unit-to-implant ratio, age and gender. A principal component analysis-based biological age score (BAS) underestimated actual lifespan; non-failed prostheses may exceed the estimate by about 1.34 years.

## Key Contributions

- Korean multicenter dataset on implant PROSTHESIS lifespan with standardized KAP grading and an online pooled form.
- Identifies oral hygiene (plaque index 3) and implant-to-implant occlusion as modifiable risk factors for prosthesis failure.
- Offers nomogram and PCA/BAS tools for individualized survival and lifespan estimation.

## Methodology

- Cross-sectional survey, 16 institutions, one-year recruitment window; chief-complaint prostheses excluded; prostheses with unrecalled fabrication month/year excluded; fabrication date from patient statement.
- Variables: age, gender, prosthesis type, clinic type (hospital, local clinic, non-licensed practitioner, unknown), usage duration, insurance coverage, antagonist, plaque index, prosthesis-unit to implant-body ratio.
- Multivariate Cox, Kaplan-Meier, nomogram, PCA (R 3.4, p<.05).

Early versus late failure timing is not given; only survival time in years. Denominator: prostheses (841) with 485 patients; the paper does not explain how multiple prostheses per patient were handled.

## Limitations

- Outcome is prosthesis grade (KAP C/D), not implant loss; the text does not report implant-level survival.
- Usage duration from patient recall; failure onset unknown and may precede the examination; success prostheses are censored at examination date, causing lifespan underestimation (authors).
- Only 6 NL cases of 841 with a very wide CI; authors urge cautious interpretation. Sample too small for big data (authors).
- Cross-sectional sampling of attending patients with chief-complaint prostheses excluded; selection effects remain.
- In-paper issues: median reported "with a 95% confidence interval" without interval values; reference categories for clinic type not stated; insurance coverage ranks first in the nomogram but is not listed among the multivariate significant factors.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

In Korean multicenter practice, implant-supported prostheses showed a median lifespan of about 16 years, shorter when opposed by another implant prosthesis (12 years) and with heavy plaque (plaque index 3). This supports hygiene maintenance and careful occlusal planning (especially implant-to-implant occlusion and parafunction control) as the practical levers. The NL finding rests on 6 cases and should not be quoted as a robust effect size. Do not equate this with implant survival: it measures prosthesis condition.
