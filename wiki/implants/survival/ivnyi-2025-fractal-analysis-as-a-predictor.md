## Three-line Summary

Retrospective case-control study at Semmelweis University (Hungary, patients treated 2015-2025): 24 patients with early implant failure (implant lost before loading) versus 24 age- and sex-matched controls with implants surviving at least 5 years; preoperative mandibular trabecular bone was analysed by fractal dimension (FD) and lacunarity on panoramic radiographs (24 per group, three regions) and by volumetric 3D FD on CBCT (15 per group).

No significant difference was found between failure and control groups in any region or method (p>0.05; exact p-values not reported in the text); post hoc power ranged only from 5.1% to 42.2%, and e.g. molar-region FD (Cohen's d = 0.52) would need about 120 patients, premolar FD (d = -0.16) up to 1230.

The authors conclude that FD and lacunarity alone are not reliable predictors of early implant failure; the negative result is underpowered and the text also contains promotional statements that sit awkwardly with it, so fractal analysis should be treated as an unvalidated adjunct, not a screening tool.

## Summary

A retrospective case-control study from the Department of Public Dental Health, Semmelweis University (Hungary) asked whether fractal dimension (FD) and lacunarity of preoperative mandibular trabecular bone can predict early implant failure. The test group comprised 24 patients whose implants failed before loading; 24 controls with implants surviving at least 5 years were chosen to match age and sex distribution. Panoramic radiographs were available for all 48, and mandibular CBCT for 15 per group.

FD and lacunarity were measured in three mandibular regions (front, premolar, molar) on panoramic ROIs (White and Rudolph protocol, FracLac box counting), and volumetric 3D FD was computed from CBCT using BoneJ with two preprocessing methods. No comparison reached significance (p>0.05), and post hoc power ranged from 5.1% to 42.2%.

The authors conclude that FD and lacunarity alone are not reliable predictors of early implant failure, while stating that they may become adjuncts after methodological refinement.

## Key Contributions

- Negative, openly shared dataset on general mandibular trabecular architecture versus early implant failure.
- One of few volumetric 3D FD analyses on CBCT in this context (authors' claim).
- Explicit power and sample-size calculations showing that hundreds to over a thousand patients would be needed to detect small FD differences.

## Methodology

- Retrospective case-control, single center, 2015-2025, STROBE; 24 vs 24 patients (panoramic), 15 vs 15 (CBCT).
- Early failure: loss or removal before functional loading (no time window given); failures from malposition, landmark damage or biomechanical causes excluded; controls had at least 5 years of survival without progressive peri-implantitis.
- Systemic bone disease, bone-active drugs, irradiation, uncontrolled periodontitis and heavy smoking were exclusion criteria, so systemic risk factors were not studied.
- Implants: Callus Pro and Nobel Replace Parallel Conical Connection; conventional loading after 2 months.
- ImageJ-based FD and lacunarity; t-test or Mann-Whitney U; G*Power for power.

## Limitations

- Small, retrospective, very low power; non-significance is not evidence of no effect (authors' own caveat).
- General mandibular bone rather than peri-implant bone; no ISQ, HU, or surgical variables; early failure window not defined; implant-level covariates not compared in the retrieved text.
- FD values depend on ROI, voxel size, preprocessing and sigma.
- Source-text issues: Conclusions contradict themselves (not reliable vs valuable tool); lacunarity group order is reversed relative to FD sentences; tables and exact p-values not in the retrieved text; the title was reconstructed from the file stem.

## Clinical Implication

On the evidence in this paper, panoramic or CBCT fractal dimension and lacunarity of the mandible cannot be used to predict which patients will have early implant failure, and should not replace clinical risk assessment (see the early failure overview). The study neither supports nor excludes a small effect, because it was underpowered; treat fractal analysis as an experimental adjunct only.
