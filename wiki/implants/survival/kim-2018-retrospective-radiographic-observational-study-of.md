## Three-line Summary

Single-institution retrospective radiographic study of 1,692 Straumann tissue-level implants placed in 881 patients (Yonsei University Dental Hospital, 2003–2009) to estimate 10-year cumulative survival and describe patterns of implant loss.

The 10-year cumulative survival rates were 98.23% at the implant level and 95.70% at the patient level; 13 implants (10 patients) were removed before prosthesis (early stage) and 8 implants (7 patients) after prosthesis (late stage), with early failures mostly from osseointegration failure and some late failures occurring without apparent marginal bone loss.

Survival was related to implant diameter, length, site and insertion torque, so tissue-level implants can be considered a reliable long-term option, but only the abstract was available and effect sizes for the risk factors are not reported there.

## 세줄요약

연세대학교 치과병원 치주과에서 2003–2009년 식립한 Straumann 티슈레벨 임플란트 1,692개(환자 881명)의 10년 누적 생존율과 임플란트 상실 양상을 후향적으로 분석한 단일 기관 연구이다.

10년 누적 생존율은 임플란트 단위 98.23%, 환자 단위 95.70%였고, 보철 전(초기) 13개(10명), 보철 후(후기) 8개(7명)가 제거되었으며, 초기 실패는 대부분 골유착 실패, 후기 실패 일부는 뚜렷한 변연골 소실 없이 발생했다.

생존율은 임플란트 직경·길이·부위·식립 토크 (insertion torque)와 관련되어 티슈레벨 임플란트는 장기적으로 유용한 치료 선택지이나, 초록만 확보되어 위험인자의 효과 크기는 확인되지 않는다.

Retrospective study of 1,692 Straumann tissue-level implants (881 patients) over 10 years: cumulative survival 98.23% (implant level) and 95.70% (patient level), related to diameter, length, site and insertion torque (abstract only).

## Summary

This is Part I of a retrospective radiographic observational study from the Department of Periodontology, Dental Hospital, Yonsei University, Seoul. Between January 2003 and December 2009, 1,692 Straumann tissue-level implants were installed in 881 patients. Implant failure was defined as complete removal of the implant, determined from electronic or paper charts and radiographs. Survival was analyzed with life tables and Kaplan-Meier estimates; risk factors and failure types were analyzed with the log-rank test and Cox regression with shared frailty.

The 10-year cumulative survival rates were 98.23% (implant level) and 95.70% (patient level). Thirteen implants in 10 patients were removed before prosthesis installation (early stage) and eight implants in seven patients after prosthesis completion (late stage). Cumulative survival was related to implant diameter, length, site and insertion torque. Most failures within 1 year were attributable to osseointegration failure, and several late-stage failures occurred without apparent marginal bone loss. The authors conclude that the length, placement site and insertion torque might affect implant survival.

## Key Contributions

- Large single-institution 10-year denominator (1,692 implants, 881 patients) for one tissue-level implant system.
- Reports survival at both implant level (98.23%) and patient level (95.70%).
- Separates early (pre-prosthesis, 13 implants) from late (post-prosthesis, 8 implants) failures.
- Notes late failures without apparent marginal bone loss, i.e., not all late losses follow a bone-loss pathway.

## Methodology

Retrospective chart and radiograph review, Department of Periodontology, Yonsei University Dental Hospital, Seoul; implants placed January 2003 to December 2009. Failure = complete implant removal. Life-table and Kaplan-Meier survival; log-rank test and Cox regression with shared frailty for risk factors and failure types.

## Results

10-year cumulative survival 98.23% (implant level) and 95.70% (patient level). Early-stage removals: 13 implants/10 patients; late-stage removals: 8 implants/7 patients. Survival related to implant diameter, length, site and insertion torque (hazard ratios and p-values not given in the abstract).

## Limitations

- Abstract only; effect sizes, p-values and per-factor survival rates are not available.
- Retrospective single-institution design with a single implant system.
- Failure defined as complete removal only, so compromised but retained implants are not counted as failures.
- Patient-level survival (95.70%) is lower than implant-level, reflecting clustering of failures within patients.
