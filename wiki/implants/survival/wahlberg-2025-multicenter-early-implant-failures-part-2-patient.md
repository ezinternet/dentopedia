## Three-line Summary

Retrospective multicenter cohort (3 Swedish specialist centers; 1,875 patients, 4,670 implants; 2007 and 2017 cohorts): patient-level multivariable logistic regression of early (first-year) implant failure and complications.

Early failure was independently associated with exposed implant threads (OR 3.47), number of implants per patient (OR 1.27 per implant) and food allergy (OR 2.64); smoking was not significant for failure (OR 1.61, 95% CI 0.88-2.94, p=0.12) but was for early complications (OR 2.30, 95% CI 1.61-3.29).

Diabetes, cardiovascular and autoimmune disease were not associated with failure; interpretation is limited by retrospective self-reported data, binary disease coding and substantial missing bone-status data.

## 세줄요약

후향적 다기관 코호트 (스웨덴 전문센터 3곳; 환자 1,875명, 임플란트 4,670개; 2007·2017 코호트): 환자 단위 다변량 로지스틱 회귀로 1년 이내 조기 실패·합병증 위험인자 분석.

조기 실패는 노출된 임플란트 나사선 (OR 3.47), 환자당 식립 임플란트 수 (임플란트 1개당 OR 1.27), 음식 알레르기 (OR 2.64)와 독립적으로 연관; 흡연은 조기 실패에서 비유의 (OR 1.61, 95% CI 0.88-2.94, p=0.12)였고 조기 합병증에서만 유의 (OR 2.30, 95% CI 1.61-3.29).

## Summary

Part 2 of a two-part Swedish multicenter study analyses patient-related factors for early implant failure and complications, defined as events within the first year. Data come from surgeries at three specialist centers in 2007 and 2017 (1,875 patients, 4,670 implants). Early failure occurred in 23 patients (2.9%) in 2007 and 40 (3.7%) in 2017 (p>0.30), while implant-level failure rose from 1.1% to 2.4% (p<0.001) and patient-level early complications from 7.0% to 13.5% (p<0.001). In multivariable models, exposed threads, more implants per patient and food allergy predicted failure; smoking predicted complications but not failure.

## Key Contributions

- Patient-level multivariable model including disease and allergy variables (cardiovascular, diabetes, autoimmune, food/antibiotic/metal allergy) at ~1,900-patient scale.
- Distinguishes failure from complications: smoking is a complication risk factor (OR 2.30) but its failure estimate (OR 1.61, 0.88-2.94) is non-significant.
- Food allergy emerges as a failure factor (OR 2.64, 95% CI 1.15-6.06), a novel and biologically speculative finding.
- Each additional implant placed per patient raises failure odds by 27%.

## Methodology

- Retrospective cohort, three specialist centers, Västra Götaland, Sweden; cohorts treated 2007 (799 patients, 2,473 implants) and 2017 (1,076 patients, 2,287 implants). 74 patients excluded (reports missing or wrong year); zygoma implants excluded.
- Early = within the first year. Analysis at patient level (Fisher tests between cohorts; univariate screen p<0.20; multivariable logistic regression p<0.05). Implant-level data descriptive only.
- Failure model covariates: diseases, smoking, bone quality, implants per patient, bone augmentation, exposed threads, sinus perforation, diabetes, food allergy, antibiotic allergy, cardiovascular disease.
- Companion "Part I" (implant material/design/surgical technique) is cited but not held.

## Results

Multivariable ORs for **early failure**:

| Variable | OR (95% CI) | p |
|---|---|---|
| Exposed threads | 3.47 (1.56-7.71) | 0.0023 |
| Number of implants per patient | 1.27 (1.15-1.41) | <0.001 |
| Food allergy | 2.64 (1.15-6.06) | 0.022 |
| Smoking | 1.61 (0.88-2.94) | 0.12 |
| Diseases (any) | 1.46 (0.84-2.52) | 0.18 |
| Bone quality (per unit) | 1.50 (0.91-2.47) | 0.12 |
| Sinus perforation | 3.41 (0.68-16.98) | 0.13 |
| Diabetes | 1.84 (0.81-4.19) | 0.15 |
| Bone augmentation | 1.25 (0.63-2.48) | >0.30 |
| Antibiotic allergy | 1.33 (0.64-2.77) | 0.45 |
| Cardiovascular disease | 1.31 (0.76-2.25) | 0.34 |

Multivariable ORs for **early complications** (all significant): smoking 2.30 (1.61-3.29); no preoperative antibiotics 1.92 (1.33-2.78); implants per patient 1.21 (1.13-1.30); exposed threads 4.46 (2.59-7.69); sinus perforation 8.19 (2.45-27.35, few events); food allergy 1.92 (1.08-3.40); metal allergy 2.45 (1.08-5.57).

Maxilla vs mandible and incisor/premolar/molar region were not significant for failure or complications (p>=0.25); implant-level failure was 2.0% (maxilla) vs 1.3% (mandible), descriptive only. Smoking prevalence was similar across cohorts (15.4% vs 14.6%). Bone quality, volume and primary stability were missing in 20.7-57.7% depending on variable and cohort.

Caveats: retrospective self-reported medical history without severity; only 63 patients with failure; the text prose labels the 3.47 OR "bone perforations and exposed implant threads" whereas Table 3 lists exposed threads alone.
