## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (21 studies, 78,230 implants): maxillary Early Implant Failure (EIF, 조기 임플란트 실패) is 3.14% vs mandibular 1.96% overall (RR 1.41; I²=58%); the gap is driven entirely by the **anterior maxilla (6.2%)** — posterior maxilla vs posterior mandible is not significantly different (1.9% vs 1.46%, p=0.68).

Anterior mandible has the lowest EIF rate of all four quadrants (2.5%); posterior mandible (4.2%) unexpectedly exceeds posterior maxilla (1.9%) in within-jaw comparisons, meaning "mandible is safe" oversimplifies the picture.

Limitations: single-author review without independent screening, I²=58% on the primary comparison, no multivariate confounder adjustment, grafted/sinus-lift sites excluded.

## 세줄요약

## Summary

This single-author SR+MA pooled data from 21 studies enrolling 78,230 implants (39,468 maxillary; 38,762 mandibular) to estimate absolute Early Implant Failure (EIF, 조기 임플란트 실패) rates by alveolar bone site. EIF was defined as implant loss before prosthetic loading (pre-prosthetic phase). Overall, maxillary EIF (3.14%) exceeded mandibular EIF (1.96%), with RR 1.41 (95% CI 1.19–1.67, p<0.0001), but substantial heterogeneity (I²=58%) prompted subgroup analyses by quadrant.

The four-quadrant breakdown revealed that the **anterior maxilla** (6.2%) — not the posterior maxilla (1.9%) — drives the overall maxilla–mandible gap. The anterior maxilla vs anterior mandible comparison showed RR 2.44 (2.04–2.91, I²=0%), while posterior maxilla vs posterior mandible was non-significant (RR 1.07, p=0.68). Within the mandible, **posterior mandible (4.2%) had worse EIF than anterior mandible (2.5%)** (RR 0.52 for ant vs post mandible, p<0.00001), a clinically important reversal.

## Key Contributions

- First pooled estimate of **absolute EIF rates by anatomical quadrant** in a large dataset (n=78,230).
- Demonstrates that the maxilla–mandible EIF gap is **driven by the anterior maxilla (6.2%)**, not by the posterior maxilla (1.9% vs mandibular 1.46%: NS).
- Shows **posterior mandible (4.2%) is the highest-risk mandibular site** — worse than posterior maxilla within the same comparison set.
- Confirms **anterior mandible (2.5%) as the lowest-risk site** across all four quadrants.
- Quantifies a **3% absolute risk difference** between anterior maxilla and anterior mandible — the largest site-to-site gap in the literature.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis; PRISMA guidelines; single author (Cairo University, Egypt)
- **Databases**: MEDLINE, EMBASE, Web of Science, Cochrane, Egyptian Knowledge Bank (1998 to search date)
- **Included**: 21 studies (retrospective, prospective, RCTs); all implant types and smoking statuses
- **Excluded**: cadaver/animal studies, systemic bone disease (diabetes, osteoporosis, bisphosphonates, corticosteroids), post-loading failures, grafted sites, sinus lift sites, immediate post-extraction sites
- **Outcome**: EIF = implant loss before prosthetic loading
- **n**: 78,230 implants (39,468 maxilla; 38,762 mandible)
- **Model**: Random effects (overall maxilla vs mandible); fixed effects for subgroups (I²=0%)
- **Effects**: Risk Ratio (RR) + Risk Difference (RD) with 95% CI

### Four-quadrant subgroup analysis

| Comparison | EIF rates | RR (95% CI) | p | I² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ant. maxilla vs Ant. mandible** | **6.2% vs 2.5%** | 2.44 (2.04–2.91) | <0.00001 | 0% |
| Post. maxilla vs Post. mandible | 1.9% vs 1.46% | 1.07 (0.92–1.25) | 0.68 → **NS** | 0% |
| Ant. maxilla vs Post. maxilla | 4.0% vs 2.19% | 1.06 (0.92–1.21) | 0.44 → **NS** | 0% |

**Key surprises**: (1) Posterior maxilla vs posterior mandible: NOT significant — classical "maxilla is riskier" holds only for the anterior jaw. (2) Posterior mandible EIF exceeds posterior maxilla within their respective intra-jaw comparisons. (3) Anterior maxilla vs posterior maxilla: NOT significant in the primary subgroup (conflicting with the larger absolute rate difference observed in other subsets).
