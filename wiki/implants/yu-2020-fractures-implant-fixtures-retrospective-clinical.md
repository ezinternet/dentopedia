## Three-line Summary

Retrospective case series (Maxillofac Plast Reconstr Surg 2020;42:13, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital; 13 fractured fixtures in 12 patients removed 2007-2015) describing crown/implant ratio, time to fracture, preceding symptoms, treatment and replacement outcomes.

Fractures were overwhelmingly posterior (10 molars, 3 premolars), 9 of 12 patients were male, mean crown/implant ratio was 0.83:1, the mean interval from prosthesis delivery to fracture was 61.7 months, and screw loosening, marginal bone loss or peri-implant disease preceded fracture in most implants.

All fractured fixtures were removed and 12 of 13 sites re-implanted with no replacement failure over a mean 54.6 months — the actionable content is the warning-sign triad (repetitive screw loosening, progressive marginal bone loss, posterior overload) as an indication to intervene before the fixture fractures.

## Summary

Fixture fracture is rare enough that most clinicians accumulate anecdotes rather than series, so this 13-implant retrospective has value mainly as a phenotype description. What it shows is a remarkably consistent picture: male, posterior, about five years after loading, with a crown taller than the implant is long (C/I 0.83:1 is close to 1:1, which is the opposite of the "keep the crown short" instinct). More useful than the demographics is the symptom timeline — repetitive screw loosening in three of the four affected in-house implants, marginal bone resorption or peri-implant disease in most of the rest, and in the illustrative bruxism case a documented cascade of loosening at 42 and 48 months, hypo-occlusion at 59 months, then bilateral fracture. Since all 13 fixtures still had to be trephined out and 12 sites rebuilt, the paper's contribution is not salvage but prevention: it argues the fracture is usually announced months in advance. The weakness is equally clear — without a denominator of intact implants carrying the same warning signs, 0.83:1 and "61.7 months" are descriptions, not thresholds.

## Key Contributions

- Describes the typical fractured-implant phenotype in a Korean hospital population: male (9/12), molar (10/13), mean age 59.3 years at fracture, roughly five years after prosthesis delivery (61.7 months).
- Supplies crown/implant ratio data for fractured cases (mean 0.83:1), countering the assumption that only long-crown/short-implant combinations fail.
- Documents the warning-sign sequence — repetitive screw loosening, marginal bone resorption, peri-implantitis/mucositis — reported in parallel in most fractured implants, consistent with overload progressing to fatigue at the third thread.
- Confirms that removal by trephine and re-implantation is a workable salvage path: 12 of 13 sites re-implanted, 100% survival over mean 54.6 months.
- Illustrates the parafunction failure mode in detail (unworn splint → repetitive loosening → bilateral fracture), which is directly usable in consent and maintenance conversations.

## Methodology

**Design.** Retrospective review, single institution, IRB B-1910-568-112.

**Inclusion.** All patients whose fractured implant fixture was removed at Seoul National University Bundang Hospital between 2007 and 2015.

**Variables.** Sex, age, implant type/diameter/length, bone graft, parafunction, mesiodistal cantilever (single crowns), crown/implant ratio, type of prosthesis, time of fracture, symptoms before fracture, treatment, complications.

**Measurement.** Crown/implant ratio from panoramic and periapical radiographs; 5 of 6 externally placed implants used an estimated value corrected for enlargement; 1 could not be measured (fractured segment lost). Replacement implants assessed by ISQ (Osstell).

## Results

**Cohort and site**

| Variable | Value |
|---|---|
| Patients / implants | 12 / 13 |
| Sex | 9 male, 3 female |
| Maxilla / mandible | 6 / 7 |
| Molar / premolar | 10 / 3 |
| Mean age at fracture | 59.3 years |
| Placed in-house / elsewhere | 7 / 6 |

**Dimensions (n = 12)**

| Diameter | n | Length | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.8 mm | 2 | 10 mm | 1 |
| 4.0 mm | 3 | 11.5 mm | 4 |
| 4.5 mm | 5 | 12 mm | 4 |
| 5.0 mm | 2 | 13 mm | 3 |

**Prosthesis and timing.** 6 single crowns, 7 splinted prostheses; 1 mesiodistal cantilever; mean C/I ratio 0.83:1 (n = 12); healing to prosthesis 5.9 months; prosthesis to fracture 61.7 months (approximately 5 years).

**Preceding symptoms.** Screw loosening 4 of 7 in-house implants (repetitive in 3); peri-implantitis 3 and mucositis 1; bone resorption in 3 of the 6 external implants plus loosening in 1; periodontal problems in 6 of 13; BOP in only 1.

**Parafunction.** 2 of 12 patients. The documented bruxism case: hard resin splint prescribed but not worn, repetitive screw loosening at 42 and 48 months, sensation of hypo-occlusion at 59 months, bilateral fixture fracture.

**Treatment and follow-up.** All 13 fixtures removed; 12 of 13 sites re-implanted; no replacement implant failed or was removed over a mean 54.6 months.

## Limitations

Only 13 implants from one centre, with no denominator of intact implants — fracture incidence cannot be computed, and the C/I ratio of 0.83:1 cannot be read as a risk threshold without a comparison group. Six implants were placed elsewhere with unknown systems, and 5 of 6 ratios were radiographic estimates. The recommendation to treat warning signs is plausible clinically but not tested: the paper reports no data on how many implants with identical symptoms survived. Symptom ascertainment relies on retrospective chart notes. No multivariable analysis of diameter, length, C/I ratio or parafunction against outcome is possible at this sample size.
