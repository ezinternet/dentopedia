## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 11 articles reporting 10 studies (MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane CENTRAL, OpenGrey, searched to May 2019) comparing one-piece (OPI) vs two-piece (TPI) implants, with failure and complication rates as primary clinical outcomes and peri-implant bone loss from baseline to last follow-up as the radiographic outcome.

No significant difference in failure risk (RR 0.49, 95% CI 0.16–1.53, P = 0.22) or in peri-implantitis at 1 year (RR 1.15, 95% CI 0.37–3.53, P = 0.81) or at 2–3 years (RR 1.95, 95% CI 0.23–16.63, P = 0.54); in the platform-switching subgroup two-piece implants lost significantly less marginal bone (WMD 0.21 mm, 95% CI 0.07–0.36, P = 0.004), while the platform-matching subgroup showed none (P = 0.67).

Implant configuration per se does not change short-term survival or complication incidence; crestal bone is protected by the platform-switching connection rather than by the one-piece/two-piece label — with only 10 studies, very wide peri-implantitis confidence intervals, and a May 2019 search cut-off that predates modern internal-connection/titanium-base systems.

SR+MA (11편 논문 = 10개 연구; MEDLINE·EMBACE·Cochrane CENTRAL·OpenGrey, 2019년 5월까지 검색) — 원-피스(One-piece Implant, OPI) vs 투-피스(Two-piece Implant, TPI) 임플란트의 임상·방사선학적 성적 비교, 일차 임상 결과는 실패·합병증률이며 방사선학적 결과는 기저점부터 최종 추적까지의 임플란트주위 골소실이다.

실패 위험 차이는 유의하지 않았고(RR 0.49, 95% CI 0.16–1.53, P=0.22) 임플란트주위염(Peri-implantitis)도 1년(RR 1.15, 95% CI 0.37–3.53, P=0.81)·2–3년(RR 1.95, 95% CI 0.23–16.63, P=0.54) 모두 차이 없었으며 실패·합병증의 대부분을 임플란트주위염이 차지했다. 변연골소실(Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)은 플랫폼 스위칭(Platform Switching) 아군에서 투-피스가 유의하게 적게 소실(WMD 0.21 mm, 95% CI 0.07–0.36, P=0.004)했고 플랫폼 매칭(Platform Matching) 아군에서는 차이가 없었다(P=0.67).

임플란트 설계 자체는 단기 생존·합병증을 바꾸지 않으며 치조정골 보존은 원-피스/투-피스 라벨이 아니라 플랫폼 스위칭 연결 구조가 좌우한다 — 10개 연구라는 작은 표본, 매우 넓은 임플란트주위염 신뢰구간, 그리고 현대적 내부연결·티타늄 베이스(Ti-base) 시스템 이전인 2019년 5월 검색 종료가 근거 확신을 제한한다.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (Liu, He & Wang, *J Prosthodont Res* 2021;65(1):56-66, West China Hospital of Stomatology, Sichuan University) pooled clinical and radiographic outcomes of **one-piece implants (OPI)** versus **two-piece implants (TPI)**. Searching MEDLINE, EMBASE, the Cochrane CENTRAL register and OpenGrey to May 2019 — with two authors independently screening — it included 11 articles reporting 10 studies.

The headline result is a **null**: no statistically significant difference in failure risk (RR 0.49, 95% CI 0.16–1.53, P = 0.22), nor in peri-implantitis at 1 year (RR 1.15, 95% CI 0.37–3.53, P = 0.81) or 2–3 years (RR 1.95, 95% CI 0.23–16.63, P = 0.54), with peri-implantitis accounting for most failures and complications in both arms. The one significant signal came from a **subgroup analysis by connection geometry**: in the platform-switching subgroup, two-piece implants lost significantly *less* marginal bone (WMD 0.21 mm, 95% CI 0.07–0.36, P = 0.004), whereas the platform-matching subgroup showed no difference (P = 0.67). That is, the bone-preservation question is answered by **platform switching, not by the one-piece/two-piece label**.

**Clinical bottom line**: choose implant configuration on handling, esthetics and connection availability rather than expecting a survival advantage from either design; where a two-piece system is used, a **platform-switched** connection is the design feature most consistently associated with reduced crestal bone loss. The null results should be read as *underpowered*, not *equivalent* — a 2–3-year peri-implantitis CI of 0.23–16.63 is compatible with large differences in either direction — and the search cut-off of May 2019 means the evidence predates modern internal-connection/titanium-base systems.

## Key Contributions

- First pooled SR+MA of **one-piece vs two-piece clinical *and* radiographic outcomes**, rather than treating the two designs as separate implant categories that each accumulate their own literature.
- **Implant configuration is not a survival or peri-implantitis decision**: failure risk (RR 0.49, P = 0.22) and peri-implantitis at 1 y and 2–3 y all statistically indistinguishable between OPI and TPI.
- Identifies **peri-implantitis as the dominant failure/complication mode** for both configurations — the biological endpoint, not the presence of an implant-abutment interface, is what drives outcome.
- **Relocates the bone-preservation benefit to the connection-geometry axis**: platform-switched TPI loses 0.21 mm less marginal bone (95% CI 0.07–0.36, P = 0.004), while platform-matching OPI-vs-TPI comparisons show no difference (P = 0.67).
- Supplies the **pooled short-to-mid-term evidence weight** that balances the 15–17-year single-trial result in [[implants/pirc-2026-one-piece-two-piece-implants-15year-rct]] — same comparison, one trial vs ten pooled studies, opposite bone-loss emphasis.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis (risk ratios for clinical outcomes, weighted mean difference for radiographic outcome)
- **Databases**: MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane CENTRAL Register of Controlled Trials, OpenGrey (grey literature) — to **May 2019**
- **Screening**: Two authors independently screened retrieved literature against inclusion/exclusion criteria
- **N**: 11 articles reporting **10 different studies**
- **Primary clinical outcomes**: failure rate and complication rate
- **Radiographic outcome**: peri-implant bone loss from baseline to last available follow-up
- **Subgroup analysis**: platform switching vs platform matching (for marginal bone loss)
- **Institution**: State Key Laboratory of Oral Diseases & National Clinical Research Center for Oral Diseases, Department of Prosthodontics, West China Hospital of Stomatology, Sichuan University, China
- **Not available in the abstract** (full text paywalled): PROSPERO registration, risk-of-bias tool and results, heterogeneity (I²), GRADE certainty, publication-bias assessment, the implant systems and follow-up durations of individual studies

## Results

| Outcome | OPI vs TPI | Effect | 95% CI | P |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Implant failure (risk) | no significant difference | RR 0.49 | 0.16–1.53 | 0.22 |
| Peri-implantitis @ 1 year | no significant difference | RR 1.15 | 0.37–3.53 | 0.81 |
| Peri-implantitis @ 2–3 years | no significant difference | RR 1.95 | 0.23–16.63 | 0.54 |
| MBL — platform-switching subgroup | **TPI less bone loss** | WMD 0.21 mm | 0.07–0.36 | **0.004** |
| MBL — platform-matching subgroup | no significant difference | — | — | 0.67 |

- **Studies**: 11 articles / 10 studies.
- **Failure and complication profile**: peri-implantitis accounted for most failures and most complications across the pooled studies.
- **Authors' conclusion**: OPI and TPI show similar short-term survival rates and incidences of complications; TPI with platform switching may be a better option to reduce peri-implant bone loss.
- **Interpretation caution**: all three clinical RRs are null, but their confidence intervals are wide (failure 0.16–1.53; peri-implantitis at 2–3 y 0.23–16.63). These are *underpowered nulls*, not demonstrated equivalence — the review establishes that a difference has not been shown, not that no difference exists.
