## Three-line Summary
Combined in vitro + in vivo preclinical study: 40 titanium prototype implants (3 × 6 mm, grade IV) split into Control (standard cylindrical macrogeometry) vs Test (macrogeometry with healing chambers), insertion torque measured in polyurethane blocks (n = 10/group), then 21-day healing in rabbit tibia with an intra-animal paired design (5 rabbits, 4 implants each = n = 10/group).

Test implants seated at significantly lower insertion torque (5.70 ± 0.85 vs 8.01 ± 0.82 Ncm, p < 0.0001) yet achieved higher bone-to-implant contact (Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC) at the two deeper cervical levels — C2 59.30% vs 40.30%, C3 42.10% vs 17.90% (p = 0.0002) — with more bone tissue and less collagen tissue and marrow space at C2/C3, and a higher radiographic blue-channel (high-density) contribution in both imaging directions.

The clinically interesting reading is that in this model it was the LOW-torque design that healed better, but this is a 21-day rabbit study in a 3 mm non-clinical-sized implant where the authors explicitly cannot separate reduced cortical compression from the healing chambers' own clot-stability / haptotaxis / contact-guidance effects, so it refines rather than overturns the torque-vs-stability picture.

## Summary
This is a combined in vitro + in vivo preclinical study: a bench arm measured peak insertion torque for 40 titanium prototype implants (3 × 6 mm) in ASTM-standard polyurethane blocks, and an in vivo arm then followed the same two macrogeometries through 21 days of healing in rabbit tibia using an intra-animal paired design. Its methodological strength is that implant macrogeometry is the only manipulated variable — the osteotomy was held constant at a 2.8 mm drill to 6 mm depth in both arms — so the drilling-protocol confounds that plague much of the osteometry literature are removed. The Test design with healing chambers seated at roughly 29% lower insertion torque (5.70 vs 8.01 Ncm, p < 0.0001) yet reached markedly higher BIC at the middle and apical cervical levels (59.30% vs 40.30% and 42.10% vs 17.90%, p = 0.0002), with more bone tissue and less collagen tissue and marrow space, and a higher radiographic blue-channel (high-density) contribution in both projections; equivalence at the most coronal level C1 is presented as evidence that crestal cortical integrity is not sacrificed. The clinical implication is that deliberately lowering insertion torque by macrogeometry, rather than maximising it against dense cortical bone, may be the preferable trade — this is the anatomical counterpart to the human split-mouth finding that healing-chamber geometry reaches equivalent RFA stability at lower torque. The key limitation is that the authors decline to attribute the healing gain to reduced compression alone, naming clot stability, cell selection, haptotaxis, contact guidance and strain distribution as competing explanations for the healing-chamber architecture, and the 21-day rabbit tibia with a 3 mm prototype implant provides no basis for magnitude-of-effect transfer to human clinical practice.

## Key Contributions
- Isolates implant macrogeometry as the sole variable by holding the osteotomy constant (2.8 mm drill, 6 mm depth, 800 rpm, 1 kg bench pressure) across arms — separating macrogeometry from drilling-technique effects.
- First in vivo histology for this specific healing-chamber cylindrical design, and the first to show the LOWER-torque design achieving higher BIC rather than merely equivalent stability.
- Shows the dissociation is depth-dependent: no difference at C1 (74.50% vs 76.00%), significant gains only at C2/C3 — localizing the benefit to trabecular bone rather than the crestal cortical zone.
- Adds a non-destructive radiographic density readout (thermal pseudocolour + RGB decomposition in a 2 × 3 mm peri-implant ROI) alongside histology, using the group's previously published method.
- Explicitly reports a null result the authors interpret positively: high insertion torque did not cause marginal bone loss, consistent with the same group's earlier sheep-mandible work.

## Methodology
- **Design**: Combined in vitro + in vivo experimental study; intra-animal paired in vivo design (each rabbit receives both Test and Control implants; proximal/distal position counterbalanced between left and right tibia).
- **n**: 40 implants (commercially pure titanium grade IV, 3 × 6 mm, TiO₂-microparticle + sandblasted surface). 20/group. In vitro IT arm n = 10/group; in vivo arm 5 adult New Zealand rabbits (3.5–4.0 kg) × 4 implants = 20, n = 10/group. Ethics protocol 04/20 (University of Rio Verde), ARRIVE + CONCEA Res. 15/2013, 3Rs.
- **Test vs Control**: Test = cylindrical with healing chambers (circular holes between threads, 0.6 mm diameter × 0.5 mm deep); Control = unaltered cylindrical. Thread depth 0.5 mm and pitch 1 mm in both.
- **Osteotomy (identical both arms)**: lance drill then 2.8 mm cylindrical drill to 6 mm depth, 800 rpm at 1 kg pressure.
- **In vitro**: 95 × 45 × 35 mm polyurethane blocks (ASTM F1839-08), 1 mm cortex at 40 PCF over 10 PCF medullary layer mimicking rabbit tibia; digital torque meter CME-30 (Filizola, 0.002° angular resolution); peak torque per implant.
- **In vivo endpoints (21 days)**: BIC% by ImageJ histomorphometry on transverse sections C1/C2/C3 within the first 3 mm (IsoMet 1000, Technovit 7200 VLC historesin, picrosirius hematoxylin); BT/CT/MS area fractions in a 4 × 6 mm ROI by examiner-verified colour segmentation; radiographic RGB channel percentages in a 2 × 3 mm lateral peri-implant ROI, lateromedial and proximodistal projections (IriX-ray DX 3000 + RVG First, Fiji/ImageJ 2.9.0).
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk → parametric; paired Student's t-tests throughout; Cohen's dz; post hoc power > 99% at α = 0.05 (GraphPad Prism 8).

## Results

**In vitro insertion torque (n = 10/group)**

| Group | Peak insertion torque (Ncm) | SD | Comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control — standard macrogeometry | 8.01 | 0.82 | — |
| Test — healing-chamber macrogeometry | 5.70 | 0.85 | p < 0.0001 vs Control |

Healing-chamber geometry seated ~29% lower under identical drilling.

**Bone-to-implant contact (n = 10/group, mean ± SD)**

| Level | Control BIC% | Test BIC% | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 — most coronal | 74.50 ± 2.799 | 76.00 ± 3.266 | NS |
| C2 — middle | 40.30 ± 2.214 | 59.30 ± 2.541 | p = 0.0002 |
| C3 — apical | 17.90 ± 2.132 | 42.10 ± 2.726 | p = 0.0002 |

**In vivo histomorphometry (area %, mean ± SD; a/b = significant between groups)**

| Tissue | C1 Control | C1 Test | C2 Control | C2 Test | C3 Control | C3 Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bone tissue (BT) | 94.73 ± 0.99 | 94.80 ± 1.09 | 36.48 ± 1.88 a | 54.24 ± 1.67 b | 15.40 ± 0.99 a | 35.48 ± 1.51 b |
| Collagen tissue (CT) | 2.77 ± 0.72 | 2.90 ± 0.77 | 43.97 ± 2.51 a | 38.96 ± 2.13 b | 45.03 ± 1.28 a | 36.67 ± 4.46 b |
| Marrow space (MS) | 2.40 ± 0.46 | 2.40 ± 0.66 | 19.55 ± 4.15 a | 6.80 ± 2.74 b | 39.57 ± 3.16 a | 27.85 ± 5.50 b |

**Radiographic RGB channel contribution (%, mean ± SD)**

| Projection | Control R / G / B | Test R / G / B | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lateromedial (L-M) | 41.96 ± 2.811 / 48.32 ± 1.836 / 9.730 ± 2.363 | 26.78 ± 2.226 / 45.86 ± 1.327 / 27.36 ± 1.901 | Test blue ~2.8× higher (high density) |
| Proximodistal (P-D) | 30.10 ± 2.862 / 40.51 ± 2.308 / 29.39 ± 2.317 | 24.32 ± 3.006 / 33.08 ± 2.075 / 42.59 ± 1.544 | Test blue higher, red/green lower |

LUT: red = lower density, green = intermediate, blue = higher mineralized density.

**Qualitative histology**: C1 indistinguishable in both arms (close, gap-free contact). At C2 the Control group showed interfacial voids and reduced contact; at C3 Control showed larger gaps, uneven tissue distribution and little contact, versus continuous homogeneous contact along the Test implant perimeter.
