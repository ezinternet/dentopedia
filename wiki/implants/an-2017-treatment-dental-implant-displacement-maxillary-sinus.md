## Three-line Summary

Narrative review + illustrative case (Maxillofac Plast Reconstr Surg 2017, Chonnam National University, Korea) on removing dental implants displaced into the maxillary sinus.

Immediate surgical removal is the principle (Caldwell-Luc → now minimally invasive FESS or lateral bony-window); the authors propose an extended vertical bony window that removes the implant and performs simultaneous sinus grafting + new implant placement in one stage.

Clinical implication: displaced implants should be retrieved even when asymptomatic (foreign-body chronic sinusitis risk); the single-stage extended-window approach shortens rehabilitation versus the conventional 2–3-stage protocol.

## 세줄요약

내러티브 리뷰 + 증례 (Maxillofac Plast Reconstr Surg 2017, 전남대): 상악동으로 변위된 치과 임플란트의 제거.

즉시 외과적 제거가 원칙(Caldwell-Luc → 현재는 최소침습 FESS 또는 측방 골창); 저자들은 세로로 확장한 골창으로 제거 + 상악동 골이식 + 신규 식립을 1회에 동시 시행하는 술식을 제안.

임상적 의미: 무증상이어도 변위 임플란트는 제거해야 함(이물반응성 만성 부비동염 위험); 확장 골창 단일 단계 술식이 기존 2–3단계 프로토콜보다 재활 기간을 단축.

## Summary

A Korean oral-and-maxillofacial-surgery review of what to do when a dental implant is displaced into the maxillary sinus — a rare but consequential complication. It maps the etiology, the sinus anatomy that makes it happen, the complications that follow, and the surgical options for **retrieving** the fixture. Its distinctive contribution is a **simplified single-stage protocol**: an extended vertical lateral bony window whose upper portion removes the displaced implant and whose lower portion lifts the Schneiderian membrane, allowing simultaneous sinus grafting and placement of a new implant in one operation rather than the conventional 2–3 staged procedures. It sits alongside the general explantation literature as the sinus-specific retrieval scenario.

## Key Contributions

1. Etiology synthesis for displacement (pneumatization, residual bone <4 mm with simultaneous graft, poor primary stability, over-drilling, excessive removal force, air-pressure/foreign-body factors).
2. Retrieval-approach review — Caldwell-Luc → FESS → conservative lateral bony-window (Tsodoulos direct-vision window; Nogami piezoelectric osteotomy + rigid endoscope).
3. A **single-stage extended-window** technique: remove implant + graft sinus + place new implant together.

## Methodology

Narrative review (maxillary-sinus anatomy, displacement complications, treatment modalities) illustrated with one case (63-year-old man, implant displaced 3 years, left maxillary sinusitis). No systematic search or pooled analysis.

## Results

**Anatomy / risk:** Schneiderian membrane ~1.0 mm; ostium sits high, far from the floor; posterior superior alveolar neurovascular bundle ~16.9 mm from the crest; premolar-region septa 31.7% (perforation risk). Post-extraction pneumatization thins basal bone.

**Complications:** inhibited mucociliary clearance, mucosal thickening, ostium obstruction → maxillary sinusitis; oroantral fistula; onward migration to paranasal sinuses, orbital floor, or cranial fossa. Controversy over asymptomatic cases — Galindo-Moreno reported a retained migrated implant with no complications at 4 years, but Regev and Raghoebar argue foreign-body reaction causes chronic sinusitis, so removal is indicated even when asymptomatic.

**Retrieval / management:**
- Principle = **immediate removal**. If delayed, control infection first (amoxicillin-clavulanate + NSAID + pseudoephedrine, 1 week).
- Access options: Caldwell-Luc (historical); FESS; conservative intraoral **lateral bony window** (repositioned and sutured after removal).
- **Extended-window single-stage technique (case):** vertically enlarged lateral window — upper portion for implant removal (horizontal incision + dental suction), lower portion for membrane elevation; perforation covered with absorbable collagen membrane; simultaneous graft (maxillary-tuberosity autograft + allograft) + new implant. CBCT clear at 4 months, 2nd-stage surgery at 6 months, restoration at 8 months, uneventful at 14-month follow-up.
- Delayed/staged placement remains indicated when primary stability is unattainable, membrane perforation is extensive, or infection is severe.
