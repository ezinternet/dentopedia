## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42025634487) of 25 meta-analyses (9 RCT meta-analyses, 26 observational meta-analyses, 35 total associations) examining factors linked to dental implant failure, searched through June 2024.

High-certainty evidence (RCTs): turned-vs-anodized implants, submerged-vs-nonsubmerged healing, and bone augmentation with long-vs-short implants raised failure risk; among observational associations only smoking reached "highly suggestive" credibility (9 others, e.g. periodontal compromise, PPI therapy, bone quality, immediate loading, reached "suggestive"), and none reached "convincing."

Smoking remains the best-supported modifiable observational risk factor and anodized surfaces are concordantly supported by both trial and observational evidence; this wiki page is built abstract-only (paywalled full text not accessed), so individual OR/CI values and the complete 35-association table are not captured here.

## Summary

This umbrella review (Giok, Veettil, Wei, Menon — IMU University Malaysia / University of Michigan / Ajman University, *J Prosthet Dent* 2026;135(3):518-527) synthesizes 25 meta-analyses (35 associations total: 9 from RCTs, 26 from cohort/case-control observational studies) addressing what drives dental implant failure. Searching Scopus, PubMed, Cochrane, and Epistemonikos through June 2024 (2922 records → 224 full-texts → 25 included articles), the authors recalculated effect sizes under a random-effects model and applied dual grading frameworks: GRADE for RCT meta-analyses, and a five-tier observational credibility scale (nonsignificant/weak/suggestive/highly suggestive/convincing) for cohort/case-control meta-analyses, incorporating heterogeneity, 95% prediction intervals, small-study effects, excess significance, and credibility ceilings. Among RCT associations, turned-vs-anodized implants, submerged-vs-nonsubmerged healing, and bone augmentation with long-vs-short implants reached high certainty; short implants and immediate-vs-delayed placement reached moderate certainty. Among observational associations, only smoking reached "highly suggestive" — the strongest tier below "convincing," which no association in the entire review achieved. Nine further factors (periodontal compromise, PPI therapy, Crohn's disease, bone quality types II/III vs IV, immediate-loading protocols, short implants, SSRI use, and turned-vs-anodized surfaces) reached "suggestive" credibility. The paper's headline clinical message is that smoking is the most robustly evidenced modifiable risk factor for implant failure, and that anodized implant surfaces are concordantly favored across both trial and observational evidence — while cautioning that the bulk of proposed risk factors in the literature remain at suggestive-or-lower confidence pending better primary studies.

## Key Contributions
- Broadest current umbrella-review synthesis in this wiki of implant-failure risk factors spanning both RCT and observational meta-analysis literatures under one unified credibility framework.
- Establishes smoking as the single strongest ("highly suggestive") observational risk factor for implant failure among 26 examined associations.
- Cross-design concordance: anodized-vs-turned implant surface favored by both RCT (high-certainty GRADE) and observational (suggestive) evidence.
- Transparently reports that zero of 35 examined associations reached "convincing" credibility — a caution against over-interpreting individual risk-factor claims in the implant-failure literature.

## Methodology
Umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42025634487) of 25 meta-analyses / 35 associations (9 RCT-based, 26 observational-based), searched in Scopus, PubMed, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, and Epistemonikos from inception to June 2024. Effect sizes recalculated with a random-effects model per meta-analysis; heterogeneity, 95% prediction interval, small-study effects, excess significance, and credibility ceilings assessed. RCT associations graded via GRADE; observational associations graded on a five-tier credibility scale (nonsignificant → weak → suggestive → highly suggestive → convincing).
