## Three-line Summary

Prospective 5-year controlled clinical study (23 primary Sjögren's syndrome (pSS) patients vs 24 matched controls, all women, mean age 57.1y) evaluating single implant-supported crown outcomes.

All implants survived in both groups; marginal bone loss, probing depth, prosthetic complications, and clinician-rated aesthetics did not differ between pSS and control, despite pSS having significantly higher DMFT (p=0.008), lower saliva flow (p<0.001), and worse OHIP scores (p<0.001) throughout.

Dental implants are a safe, reliable rehabilitation option in pSS despite severe hyposalivation; quality-of-life burden in pSS is likely driven by hyposalivation symptoms themselves rather than implant-treatment outcomes — small sample size (n=47) limits generalizability.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1111/clr.14356](https://doi.org/10.1111/clr.14356)), this prospective controlled clinical study followed 23 primary Sjögren's syndrome (pSS) patients and 24 age-/gender-/region-matched non-SS control subjects (all women, mean age 57.1y) for 5 years after receiving single implant-supported crowns. Unlike prior evidence — mostly retrospective SRs or short-term (≤18-month) prospective cohorts that pooled primary and secondary SS — this study restricted inclusion to pSS only, isolating the effect of hyposalivation itself from the immunosuppressant/biologic confounding common in secondary SS treatment.

All implants survived in both groups over 5 years, and marginal bone loss, probing pocket depth, technical complications, and clinician-rated aesthetic outcomes (Copenhagen Index Score) did not differ significantly between pSS and control. The only clinical difference was a significantly higher gingival index (more inflammation) at implant sites in pSS despite comparable plaque scores — plausibly from hyposalivation-impaired oral clearance rather than hygiene failure. In sharp contrast to the equivalent clinical outcomes, patient-reported quality of life (OHIP-49) was significantly worse in pSS throughout all 5 years (p<0.001), correlating strongly with dry-mouth severity (r=0.63) — indicating the QoL burden in pSS patients stems from hyposalivation symptoms themselves (taste change, discomfort, dry mouth), not from inferior implant performance.

Clinically: this strengthens (with a matched-control, 5-year prospective design) the provisional conclusion of the pre-existing [[implants/almeida-2017-dental-implants-sjogren-syndrome-sr]] (94.6±5.6% survival, ~4y follow-up, mostly retrospective, no RCTs) — dental implants remain a safe, effective tooth-replacement option in pSS patients, and clinicians should not withhold implant therapy on the basis of hyposalivation alone. However, elevated DMFT and dentition disharmony reflect a lifetime of caries/extractions driven by hyposalivation, and higher gingival inflammation at implant sites argues for closer peri-implant maintenance intervals in this population.

## Key Contributions

- First 5-year prospective, matched-control study of single-tooth implant prognosis specifically in primary Sjögren's syndrome (pSS), isolating pSS from the immunosuppressant-confounded secondary SS population studied in most prior work.
- 100% implant survival in both pSS and control groups at 5 years, with no significant difference in MBL, PPD, technical complications, or clinician-rated aesthetics.
- Demonstrates a clinically important dissociation: implant-level clinical outcomes are unaffected by pSS, but patient-reported quality of life remains significantly and persistently worse — driven by hyposalivation symptoms, not implant performance.
- Provides granular longitudinal benchmarks (DMFT, saliva flow rates, OHIP subscales) at baseline/1y/3y/5y absent from prior retrospective SR data.
- Reinforces [[implants/almeida-2017-dental-implants-sjogren-syndrome-sr]] with substantially stronger study design (prospective, matched control, 5-year) than the mostly-retrospective evidence base that SR pooled.

## Methodology

- **Design**: prospective clinical study, 5-year follow-up, matched (age, gender, edentulism region) non-SS control group. Ethics approval H-3-2014-032 (Capital Region of Denmark); STROBE-consistent.
- **Population**: 293 pSS patients screened → 50 eligible → final 23 pSS + 24 matched controls. pSS diagnosed per 2002 AECG criteria (also retrospectively met 2016 ACR-EULAR criteria).
- **Intervention**: single bone-level implants (Straumann Bone Level Roxolid), 3-month transmucosal healing, then screw-retained (n=42) or cemented (n=5) single crowns on customized titanium abutments.
- **Follow-up**: T0 (baseline, 2 months post-restoration), T1 (1y), T3 (3y), T5 (5y).
- **Outcomes**: whole saliva flow (unstimulated/stimulated); implant survival/success (Albrektsson criteria); marginal bone loss (periapical radiographs); plaque/gingival/bleeding indices; probing pocket depth; peri-implantitis/mucositis (Berglundh criteria); technical complications; Copenhagen Index Score (clinician-rated aesthetics); OHIP-49 (patient-reported QoL).
- **Statistics**: Brunner-Langer model for longitudinal main effects/interaction; Mann-Whitney with Bonferroni correction; α=0.05.

## Results

- **Cohort**: 47 women, mean age 57.1±12.6y. pSS had significantly more comorbidities (57% vs 16%, p=0.004), medication use (78% vs 33%, p=0.002), and allergies (57% vs 25%, p=0.028).
- **DMFT**: significantly higher in pSS throughout (p=0.008).
- **Saliva flow**: pSS consistently in hyposalivation range (UWS 0.04-0.05 mL/min; SWS 0.49-0.71 mL/min) vs control in/near normal range (UWS 0.22-0.40 mL/min; SWS 1.50-2.02 mL/min); pSS significantly lower at every timepoint (p<0.001).
- **Implant survival**: 100% in both groups at 5 years; **MBL** T0→T5: -0.04±0.36 mm (pSS) vs -0.23±0.60 mm (control), p=0.685.
- **PPD**: no significant between-group difference (T5: 2.93±0.62 mm pSS vs 2.62±0.49 mm control, p=0.446).
- **Gingival index**: significantly higher in pSS at implant sites (p=0.003) and overall (p<0.001) despite comparable plaque scores.
- **Complications**: only 1 biological complication total — a control-group implant peri-implantitis at T1 (herpes zoster-related), resolved by T3/T5. Technical complications comparable between groups (p=0.241).
- **Aesthetics (CIS)**: harmony/symmetry worse in pSS (p<0.001, attributed to pre-existing DMFT/extraction pattern); crown morphology, color match, mucosal discoloration comparable.
- **OHIP-49**: significantly worse overall score in pSS throughout (p<0.001), improving over time in both groups (p=0.026); correlated with dry-mouth VAS (r=0.63, p<0.001).
