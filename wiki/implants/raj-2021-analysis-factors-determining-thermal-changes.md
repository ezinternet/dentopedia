## Three-line Summary

In-vitro bovine femur study (72 drillings, 12 factorial groups of n=5–6) testing drilling speed (1500/2000/2500 rpm), operator hand pressure (1.2/2.4 kgf), and irrigant temperature (room temp vs 0°C) on osteotomy thermal rise via FLIR infrared thermography.

Highest temperature rise occurred at 2000 rpm + 1.2 kgf + room-temperature saline; lowest occurred at 2500 rpm + 2.4 kgf + chilled (0°C) saline — all 12 groups stayed below the 47°C bone-necrosis threshold.

Clinical implication: high drilling speed combined with high hand pressure and copious chilled-saline irrigation minimizes thermal insult; limitation is bovine (not human) bone under a fixed-feed-rate bench rig, not freehand clinical drilling.

## 세줄요약

인비트로 우골 (Bovine Femur) 연구 (72회 시추, 12개 요인설계 군, n=5–6): 시추 속도 (1500/2000/2500 rpm)·술자 수압 (1.2/2.4 kgf)·세정액 온도 (실온 vs 0°C)가 절제부 (Osteotomy) 발열에 미치는 영향을 FLIR 적외선 열화상으로 측정.

최고 온도상승은 2000 rpm + 1.2 kgf + 실온 saline 군, 최저 온도상승은 2500 rpm + 2.4 kgf + 냉각(0°C) saline 군에서 관찰 — 12개 군 모두 골괴사 임계온도 (47°C) 미만.

임상적 의미: 고속 회전 + 강한 수압 + 충분한 냉각 saline 세정이 발열 최소화에 유리하나, 우골·고정 이송속도 벤치 실험이라 실제 프리핸드 임상 시추와는 차이가 있을 수 있음.

## Summary
This in-vitro study used bovine femur blocks and an automated drill jig with load-cell-controlled hand pressure to test the combined effect of drilling speed (1500/2000/2500 rpm), operator hand pressure (1.2/2.4 kgf), and irrigant temperature (room temperature vs 0°C chilled) on osteotomy site heat generation, across 72 drillings organized into 12 factorial groups. Temperature was recorded via FLIR infrared thermography as the maximum change from baseline (Δt max). The highest thermal rise occurred at 2000 rpm with low hand pressure (1.2 kgf) and room-temperature irrigation, while the lowest occurred at the highest speed (2500 rpm) combined with higher hand pressure (2.4 kgf) and chilled irrigation — none of the 12 combinations exceeded the 47°C critical bone-necrosis threshold. The counterintuitive finding is that speed alone does not dictate heat: higher speed paired with higher pressure and cooled irrigation actually minimized heat, suggesting the three variables interact rather than act independently.

## Key Contributions
- Factorial in-vitro design directly comparing speed × hand-pressure × irrigant-temperature simultaneously, rather than varying one factor at a time as in most prior drilling-heat studies.
- Demonstrates that all tested clinical parameter combinations remain safely below the 47°C thermal-necrosis threshold.
- Shows speed and pressure interact with irrigation temperature — high speed is not inherently more thermogenic when paired with adequate pressure and chilled coolant.

## Methodology
In-vitro factorial design on bovine femur (12 cm blocks); 72 osteotomies across 12 groups (n=5–6) combining 2 drill diameters (2 mm/2.8 mm), 3 speeds (1500/2000/2500 rpm), 2 hand pressures (1.2/2.4 kgf, load-cell-calibrated), and 2 irrigant temperatures (room temp/0°C, 40 mL/min flow). Outcome: Δt max via FLIR TG165 thermography. Statistics: F-test, T-test, Kruskal-Wallis, Mann-Whitney U (p<0.05).

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| ΔT at 1500 rpm | 38.9 ± 4.5 °C |
| ΔT at 2000 rpm | 41.1 ± 5.7 °C |
| Highest-ΔT combination | 2000 rpm, 1.2 kgf pressure, room-temp saline |
| Lowest-ΔT combination | 2500 rpm, 2.4 kgf pressure, chilled (0°C) saline |
| Bone-necrosis threshold exceeded | None of the 12 groups (all < 47°C) |
