## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of ligaplants (PDL cells + implant biomaterial) searched PubMed/Google Scholar/CENTRAL from Oct 2022; 328 articles relevant to PDL tissue engineering for implants reviewed after screening 450 excluded.

Ligaplants promise shock absorption, proprioception, orthodontic tooth movement potential, and bone remodeling capacity absent in conventional osseointegrated implants; one human trial (Gault 2010) confirmed firm integration without direct bone contact and tooth-equivalent mobility scores.

Clinical translation remains distant — complex culture requirements, nonperiodontal cell contamination risk, unpredictable host acceptance, and high cost are the current barriers; most evidence is from animal models.

## Summary

Conventional osseointegrated implants achieve rigid bone bonding but lack the periodontal ligament (PDL) — losing shock absorption, proprioception, and the ability to undergo orthodontic movement. Ligaplants address this directly: PDL cells isolated from extracted teeth are cultured on hydroxyapatite (HA)-coated titanium pins in a bioreactor, then transplanted into the extraction socket. Unlike osseointegrated implants, ligaplants achieve firm integration **without direct bone contact**, maintaining a PDL-like fibrous interface that confers tooth-equivalent mobility. This 2023 narrative review catalogues the concept, tissue-engineering pipeline, advantages, limitations, and current human evidence. The single human trial (Gault 2010) confirmed core viability — firm integration, new bone induction, and natural-tooth-equivalent mobility scores — but used only a basic mobility endpoint and is the field's sole human data point. Animal models across rodents, canines, primates, rabbits, and swine consistently demonstrate PDL-like tissue, cementum-like layers, and Sharpey's fiber insertion, but clinical translation remains blocked by culture complexity, cost, and unpredictable host acceptance.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidated conceptual framework for ligaplants as an alternative attachment paradigm distinct from osseointegration
- Documented the tissue-engineering pipeline: mid-root PDL cell isolation → temperature-responsive culture dishes → bioreactor seeding on HAP-coated titanium → socket transplantation
- Tabulated advantages vs disadvantages of ligaplants vs conventional implants:
  - **Advantages**: shock absorption via micro-movements, proprioception, bone remodeling support, bone graft elimination potential, orthodontic movement capacity
  - **Disadvantages**: complex culture, temperature/timing sensitivity, nonperiodontal cell contamination risk, high cost, unpredictable host acceptance
- Documented Gault 2010 as the sole human trial: HAP-coated titanium ligaplant achieved firm integration without bone contact; mobility equivalent to natural teeth

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative review (literature review with PRISMA flow diagram)
- **Search databases**: PubMed, Google Scholar, CENTRAL; October 2022 cutoff
- **Keywords**: ligaplants, PDL, osseointegration, tissue-engineered ligament, implants, tissue engineering, biomaterial
- **Selection**: included methodology studies and case reports on ligaplant concept, success, advantages/disadvantages, properties; excluded general oral tissue engineering (n = 450)
- **Scope**: 328 articles reviewed post-screening

## Results

- **Ligaplant definition**: PDL cells combined with implant biomaterial; initial loose fit during insertion protects the PDL cell cushion; firm integration achieved without direct bone contact
- **Gault 2010** (sole human trial): Human PDL cells from extracted tooth cultured on HAP-coated titanium pin in bioreactor → ligaplant transplanted into human jaw; result: firm integration, induced new bone formation, mobility scores equivalent to natural teeth
- **Animal model evidence** (Choi 2000, Lin 2011, Oshima 2014, Nakajima 2016): confirmed PDL-like tissue, cementum-like layers, Sharpey's fiber insertion, and orthodontic movement response across multiple species
- **Current state**: proof-of-concept established in animals and a single human; no survival, marginal bone loss, or implant stability quotient (ISQ) data from humans exist
