## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 40 FEA in-vitro studies (PubMed/Embase/Scopus/WoS, 2003–2023; PROSPERO CRD42022382211) evaluating how implant diameter and length affect bone stress distribution.
Diameter primarily governs cortical bone stress and crestal overloading; length governs cancellous bone stress — overall, diameter is the dominant factor; short implants with larger diameter generated lower stresses than longer implants with smaller diameter.
Clinically, wider-diameter implants should be prioritized over longer implants for stress reduction; in low-density bone (D3/D4), adequate length gains increasing relevance.

## Summary

This PRISMA-compliant systematic review synthesized 40 FEA in-vitro studies published 2003–2023, examining how dental implant diameter and length influence bone stress distribution. Of 852 records, 40 met inclusion criteria (PROSPERO CRD42022382211). Results show that implant diameter primarily affects cortical bone stress and crestal overloading while length primarily influences cancellous bone stress. The key finding is that diameter is the more important parameter: short implants with a larger diameter generated lower stresses than longer implants with a smaller diameter under both static and immediate loading FEA models. In lower bone density (D3/D4), implant length becomes increasingly relevant for adequate stress distribution. The review is limited by the lack of experimental validation in included studies (only 1 of 40) and high heterogeneity precluding meta-analysis.

## Key Contributions

- Diameter governs cortical bone stress (crestal overloading) while length governs cancellous bone stress — dual-mechanism separation first comprehensively established by this SR
- Short implants with larger diameter generate lower stresses than longer implants with smaller diameter: diameter > length as a clinical priority for stress reduction
- Low bone density (D3/D4) is the key modifier elevating length relevance for stress distribution
- Only 1 of 40 included FEA studies performed experimental validation — identifies a systemic methodological gap in FEA implant research

## Methodology

PRISMA systematic review (no meta-analysis due to heterogeneity). Databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, SciELO, grey literature. Search: Oct 2003–Oct 2023. Eligibility: peer-reviewed English FEA in-vitro studies evaluating both implant diameter and length. 852 records → 40 included. Primary metric: von Mises stress (MPa) in cortical and cancellous bone. Models: cortical + trabecular two-layer bone, predominantly Type II, posterior mandible/maxilla, titanium implants. Loading: static (35/40); immediate loading (5/40); axial-only (17/40), multi-directional (23/40).
