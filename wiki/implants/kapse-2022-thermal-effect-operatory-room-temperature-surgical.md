## Three-line Summary

In vitro thermographic study (64 goat-mandible bone specimens, 2 room temperatures × 2 drill diameters × 2 depths × 2 irrigant temperatures) evaluating heat generation during implant osteotomy.

Only irrigant temperature significantly affected bone temperature (5°C: 24.5–24.8°C vs 25°C: 29.3–29.7°C, p=0.003); operating room temperature (25°C vs 30°C), drill diameter (4.2 vs 5.6 mm), and drilling depth (10 vs 13 mm) showed no statistically significant differences.

Cooled saline (5°C) is the dominant, clinically actionable lever for controlling osteotomy-site heat; the study is ex vivo on nonvital goat bone (no living bone-healing endpoint) and is limited to two discrete levels per factor.

## Summary
An in vitro thermographic study using 64 goat mandible bone specimens investigated whether ambient operating-room temperature (25°C vs 30°C), surgical drill diameter (4.2 vs 5.6 mm), drilling depth (10 vs 13 mm), and irrigant temperature (5°C vs 25°C) affect bone temperature at the osteotomy site during implant site preparation. Specimens were drilled at 800 rpm with external saline irrigation and measured with a graphite-spray-calibrated laser thermometer. Of the four factors, only irrigant temperature produced a statistically significant difference — cooled saline at 5°C consistently kept bone temperature ~5°C lower than 25°C saline (p=0.003 in both room-temperature groups). Room temperature, drill diameter, and drilling depth showed no significant effect on osteotomy-site heat within the ranges tested. The authors conclude that lowering irrigant temperature is the most reliable clinical lever for thermal control during implant drilling, and that a moderate (5°C) swing in ambient operatory temperature is not a clinically relevant heat-generation risk factor.

## Key Contributions
- Isolates operatory room temperature as an independent variable for the first time (per the authors) in implant-drilling thermal research — found no significant effect between 25°C and 30°C.
- Provides a within-study head-to-head comparison of four candidate heat factors (room temp, diameter, depth, irrigant temp) using the same specimens and protocol, strengthening the relative-importance ranking established by prior multifactorial reviews.
- Uses a goat mandible ex vivo model (bone density/thermal conductivity reported to approximate human jaw) with emissivity-corrected infrared thermometry.

## Methodology
- In vitro/ex vivo experimental study; 64 goat mandible bone blocks (15×15 mm), split into Group A (25°C room) and Group B (30°C room), 32 specimens each.
- Fully crossed design: drill diameter (4.2/5.6 mm) × drilling depth (10/13 mm) × irrigant temperature (5/25°C), n=4 per subgroup.
- 800 rpm drilling speed, external saline irrigation only, single blinded operator; temperature measured by laser/IR thermometer with graphite-spray emissivity correction (0.95).
- Student's t-test, significance at p<0.05.

## Clinical Implications
- Cooled saline (~5°C) is the single most actionable variable for thermal control during implant osteotomy — consistent with the broader heat-generation literature already in this wiki.
- A moderate ambient operatory-room swing (25°C vs 30°C) does not appear to be a clinically meaningful heat-generation risk factor, somewhat easing concern in warm-climate clinical settings.
- Drill diameter and drilling depth (within the 4.2–5.6 mm / 10–13 mm ranges tested) were not independently significant here, but this is a narrower factor space than the multifactorial reviews and should not be over-generalized (small n=4 per subgroup, ex vivo nonvital bone, no bone-healing endpoint).
