## Three-line Summary

In vitro study on fresh low-density bovine ribs comparing three osteotomy systems for a SIN Strong SW CM 3.5 × 8.5 mm implant — conventional drilling (SIN, control), osseodensification (VERSAH/Densah burs), and bone expander (MAXIMUS) — with micro-CT bone surface/volume ratio measured separately at cervical, body, and apical regions (n=5/group) and peak insertion torque (n=8/group).

Nothing reached significance: no differences between cervical/body/apical regions, none between systems on microarchitecture, and none on insertion torque (SIN 35 ± 21.5, VERSAH 43.2 ± 27.1, MAXIMUS 59.6 ± 28.5 N·cm) — though the means ordered MAXIMUS > VERSAH > SIN, a ~70% spread.

The authors read this as densification hitting a "threshold of utility" in extremely porous bone; but with n=5–8/group and SDs of 21–28 N·cm swamping a 24.6 N·cm mean gap, the study is very likely underpowered — a failure to detect a difference, not evidence that none exists.

## Summary

De Agostinho Neto et al. (2026) prepared osteotomies in **fresh bovine ribs** — chosen deliberately for their low density — using three systems for the same SIN Strong SW CM 3.5 × 8.5 mm implant: the manufacturer's conventional drilling sequence (SIN, control), 골밀도화 (Osseodensification, OD) with Densah/VERSAH burs, and a bone expander kit (MAXIMUS, Brazil). Bone microarchitecture was measured by micro-CT (Skyscan 1172, 8 μm voxel) as a bone **surface/volume ratio** at three separate levels — cervical, body, apical — and 1차 안정성 (primary stability) by peak insertion torque with a digital torque wrench.

**Nothing separated.** No significant differences between regions, between systems, or on insertion torque. The authors interpret this as densification approaching a **"threshold of utility"**: where trabecular bone is extremely porous, there is too little substance to compact, so bone biology — not drill choice — governs primary stability. Their clinical inference is that in such sites, implant macrogeometry, conservative loading, and grafting matter more than which bur you pick.

**Read this null carefully.** The means were not close: MAXIMUS (59.6) ran ~70% above SIN (35), and that gap straddles a clinically live line — 35 N·cm barely clears the 30 N·cm immediate-loading threshold this paper itself cites, while 59.6 N·cm clears it comfortably. With n=5–8 per group, SDs of 21.5–28.5 N·cm, and no a priori power calculation, the study lacked the resolution to call a difference of that size either way. This is a **failure to detect**, and the paper's Conclusion ("similarity observed across… approaches") narrates the un-rejected null as demonstrated equivalence — the standard absence-of-evidence conflation. It is honest evidence that OD's advantage is *not large and reliable* in extremely porous bone; it is not evidence that OD does nothing there.

## Key Contributions

- **The wiki's first regional (cervical / body / apical) micro-CT comparison** of OD vs conventional vs expander — the measurement axis the [[implants/kim-2019-double-spiral-condensing-screw-implant]] patent's apical-deficit premise has always lacked.
- **A published null in a positive-leaning literature.** The authors explicitly position their result against "optimistic studies" and name a **threshold of utility** for densification in extremely porous bone.
- **Three-arm head-to-head including a bone expander** alongside OD in the same experiment — expanders are usually benchmarked only against conventional drilling.
- **Shifts the clinical lever** in very-low-density sites from instrument choice toward macrogeometry, loading protocol, and augmentation.

## Methodology

- **Design**: in vitro bench, three-arm parallel, unpaired. Null hypothesis of no difference in primary stability — not rejected.
- **Substrate**: fresh bovine rib, selected for low-density characteristics.
- **Implant**: SIN Strong SW CM 3.5 × 8.5 mm (CM 11.5, 16°), placed **at bone level** in all groups.
- **Protocols**:
  - *SIN (control)* — 2.0 mm spear → 2.0 mm twist → 3.0 mm conical; 1200 rpm initial / 800 rpm subsequent; clockwise; irrigated. (Final 3.0 mm bed for a 3.5 mm implant — the control is itself undersized.)
  - *VERSAH (OD)* — 1.7 mm pilot clockwise (cutting) → VT1525 (2.0 mm) → VT2535 (3.0 mm) counterclockwise (densifying); 800 rpm. Only two densifying burs.
  - *MAXIMUS (expander)* — 1.3 mm pilot → progressive bone reamers to indicated size (ALO34.TI); 800 rpm; clockwise.
- **n**: **inconsistently reported** — abstract says "n = 5 per group"; Methods say n=5 per system for micro-CT and **n=8 per technique** for torque.
- **Micro-CT**: Skyscan 1172; 8 μm isotropic voxel; 92 kV / 120 mA; 180° rotation; 0.5 mm Al filter; masked to 500 μm from osteotomy centre; three axial levels per osteotomy. Outcome = **bone surface area / volume ratio** (microarchitecture proxy, *not* bone mineral density).
- **Torque**: INSTRUTHERM TQ-680 digital torque wrench, peak insertion torque.
- **Statistics**: Shapiro–Wilk → one-way ANOVA → Tukey, α = 0.05 (GraphPad Prism 10). No power analysis.

### ⚠️ Reporting error in the abstract — the inequality is backwards

The abstract states, twice, that there were "no statistically significant differences … **(p < 0.05)**". For a null result this must read **p > 0.05**. The Discussion has it right — "SIN: 35 ± 21.5 N cm; VERSAH: 43.2 ± 27.1 N cm; MAXIMUS: 59.6 ± 28.5 N cm; **p > 0.05**" — which confirms the abstract is a typographic error rather than a mis-analysis. Anyone quoting this paper from its abstract alone will quote a statement that says the opposite of what the study found.

*(Caveat on our source: the PMC JATS extraction strips italic statistical symbols, so the retrieved text renders "(< 0.05)" and "(= 0.05)" without the leading `p` / `α`. The reading above is inferred from context and from the Discussion's correctly-signed counterpart.)*

### ⚠️ Underpowered — "no difference found" ≠ "no difference exists"

n=5 (micro-CT) / n=8 (torque) per group, SDs of 21.5–28.5 N·cm, no a priori power calculation. The SIN→MAXIMUS gap of 24.6 N·cm — ~70% relative, and spanning the 30 N·cm immediate-loading threshold — still fails to separate, which is the signature of insufficient n rather than of an absent effect. For contrast, [[implants/versah-protocols/de-lima-2026-osseodensification-vs-conventional-drilling-exvivo]] used a **paired** design (all three techniques per bone block), n=16, power 0.80, and did separate its groups in the same substrate class. The unpaired design here also folds between-rib density variation into the group SDs, inflating exactly the denominator that had to be beaten.

## Limitations

- Underpowered; no power calculation; Conclusion narrates an un-rejected null as demonstrated similarity.
- Unpaired design in a substrate (bovine rib) whose density varies along and between specimens.
- **Outcome is a proxy**: bone surface/volume ratio is a microarchitecture descriptor, not bone mineral density or 골-임플란트 접촉률 (Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC). A densification effect could exist and not register in S/V — compaction might even *lower* it. The paper never argues the construct validity of this choice, which materially weakens the regional (apical vs cervical) reading.
- n reported inconsistently between abstract and Methods.
- Implants placed at bone level, not the manufacturer-recommended 1.5–2.0 mm subcrestal; the authors acknowledge this and defend it as internal-validity standardisation (avoiding crestal cortical friction inflating torque), at the cost of clinical realism.
- Short VERSAH sequence (two densifying burs), with no report of the pumping/pecking motion or irrigation regime the Densah protocol depends on — an under-executed OD arm is an unconsidered alternative explanation for the null.
- Bovine rib ≠ human maxilla; no cortical architecture analogous to the posterior maxilla, no healing, no remodelling, no secondary stability.
- Bench torque only — no ISQ/RFA, no BIC, no histomorphometry. Torque and ISQ are known to dissociate in this exact model (de-lima 2026), so a single-metric null is thinner than it appears.

## Clinical Bottom Line

In extremely low-density bone, do not expect a densification bur to rescue primary stability on its own — but do not read this paper as showing it fails to. What it establishes is that **any advantage is not large and reliable enough to surface with n=5–8**, and that its size is a live question (the 35 → 60 N·cm spread here is clinically enormous if real). The authors' constructive point stands independently of the statistics: where trabecular bone is extremely porous, implant macrogeometry, conservative loading, and augmentation are the levers with better evidence behind them than instrument choice.

For the wiki's HaeNaem vs Densah question specifically: this is the first held paper to measure cervical / body / apical separately, and it found no regional gradient for VERSAH. That is the correct measurement axis for the [[implants/kim-2019-double-spiral-condensing-screw-implant]] apical-deficit premise — but because the study detects no densification effect at all, and because S/V ratio is not a density measure, it cannot test the gradient claim on that claim's own terms. The premise remains **unmeasured**, not refuted. The measurement the wiki actually needs is regional bone mineral density (or BIC) after OD vs conventional, in an adequately powered design.

## Related Papers

- [[implants/versah-protocols/de-lima-2026-osseodensification-vs-conventional-drilling-exvivo]] — **contradicts**. Closest comparable design held (ex vivo bovine rib, three osteotomy techniques, insertion torque) and reached the opposite conclusion: OD significantly beat conventional drilling (IT 77.6 vs 61.3 N·cm, p=0.007). Same substrate class, conflicting bottom line — but note de-lima was paired and powered while this was neither, so the conflict is weak-vs-strong, not a standoff. De-lima's absolute torques also run ~2× higher across every arm (61–95 vs 35–60 N·cm), implicating differences in block preparation, implant (Biomorse 4×10 vs SIN 3.5×8.5), and rpm (1000 vs 800).
- [[implants/versah-protocols/barbera-millan-2021-primary-stability-low-density-osseodensification]] — **contradicts**. Cited in this paper's own Discussion. Low-density porcine tibia (D4 analog, n=55/group): OD significantly superior to conventional **under-drilling** — the harder comparator — on torque (21.7 vs 8.9 N·cm, p<0.001) and ISQ (69.8 vs 65.2, p=0.001). Directly conflicting finding in the same low-density-bench class.
- [[implants/versah-protocols/rittipakorn-2025-clockwise-osseodensification-primary-stability-cadaveric]] — **reinforces**. Independent replication of the *pattern*, in a different model (human cadaveric tibia, D3/D4, 40 implants) by a different team: OD numerically ahead but non-significant on both ISQ (67.5 vs 62.9, p=0.077) and torque (34.0 vs 29.5 N·cm, p=0.052), with the authors likewise attributing non-significance to sample size. Two underpowered low-density studies landing on the same "numerically ahead, statistically undetectable" result is the strongest thing either can say.
- [[implants/kim-2019-double-spiral-condensing-screw-implant]] — **refines**. - [[implants/huwais-2017-novel-osseous-densification-osteotomy-primary-stability]] — foundational OD bench study (porcine tibia): significant torque increase and ~3× BIC with OD, ISQ NS.
