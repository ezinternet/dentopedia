## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (9 systematic reviews of osseodensification [OD] vs conventional drilling; PROSPERO CRD420251116804; PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science/LILACS/Cochrane; AMSTAR-2 quality appraisal; abstract-only — full text not retrieved).

Most included SRs reported higher primary implant stability (insertion torque, ISQ) with OD, particularly in low-density bone; marginal bone level change and implant survival were generally comparable between techniques, with survival >95% in both groups.

OD's clinical benefit appears limited to primary-stability gain in low-density bone, with no demonstrable long-term advantage in marginal bone preservation or survival; higher-quality RCTs are needed.

## Summary

This umbrella review (SR-of-SRs, PROSPERO CRD420251116804) synthesizes and critically appraises 9 systematic reviews comparing osseodensification (OD) with conventional drilling for implant osteotomy preparation, using AMSTAR-2 for methodological-quality assessment across the included SRs. Descriptive synthesis found that most of the 9 SRs reported higher primary implant stability (insertion torque and ISQ) with OD, particularly in low-density bone, while marginal bone level (MBL) change and implant survival were generally comparable between OD and conventional drilling, with survival exceeding 95% in both groups. The authors conclude OD's demonstrated advantage is confined to primary-stability enhancement in low-density bone, with no clear long-term benefit for crestal bone preservation or survival, and call for higher-quality RCTs. This is an abstract-only ingest (full text paywalled at thejpd.org); the structured abstract and reference list (9 included SRs, several already individually held in this wiki) were extracted via pypdf and cross-checked against PubMed (PMID 42242999) for DOI/author/affiliation.

## Key Contributions

- Raises the OD-vs-conventional-drilling evidence question from individual-SR level to evidence-of-evidence (umbrella review) level, with formal AMSTAR-2 appraisal of the SR literature itself.
- Confirms a consistent three-domain pattern across the SR literature: primary stability (IT/ISQ) favors OD, especially in low-density bone; MBL change and survival do not differ meaningfully from conventional drilling.
- Explicitly frames OD's appropriate clinical niche as low-density bone rather than a universal osteotomy upgrade, and flags the persistent RCT-quality gap in the underlying evidence base.

## Methodology

Umbrella review of systematic reviews/meta-analyses; PROSPERO-registered (CRD420251116804); searches in PubMed (MEDLINE), Scopus, Web of Science, LILACS, and Cochrane Library; 9 included SRs; AMSTAR-2 methodological-quality assessment; descriptive (non-quantitative re-pooled) synthesis of primary stability, MBL, and survival outcomes. Full-text methodological detail (PRISMA flow, inclusion/exclusion criteria, per-SR AMSTAR-2 scores) not available from the abstract-only extraction.
