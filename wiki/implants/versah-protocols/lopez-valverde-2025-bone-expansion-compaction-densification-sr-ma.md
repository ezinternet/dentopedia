## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (10 studies out of 2,464 screened; PROSPERO CRD42025646738) comparing bone expansion (골확장, BE), bone compaction (골압축, BC), and osteodensification (골밀도화, OD) in narrow alveolar ridges, measuring bone density (BD), crest expansion (CE), and implant stability quotient (임플란트 안정성 지수, ISQ).

Osteodensification significantly outperformed bone expansion on all three outcomes: BD SMD −0.71 (P=0.002), CE SMD −1.12 (P=0.04), ISQ SMD −8.88 (P=0.0005); bone compaction had insufficient data for independent meta-analysis.

OD with Versah Densah Burs provides the strongest evidence-based option for narrow ridge implant site preparation, offering simultaneous gains in bone density, ridge width, and primary stability compared to traditional bone expansion.

## Summary

López-Valverde et al. (2025) conducted the first SR+MA to directly compare the three main ridge management techniques for narrow alveolar ridges: bone expansion (osteotome or split-crest), bone compaction (progressive osteotome condensation), and osteodensification (Versah Densah Bur counter-clockwise rotation). From 2,464 screened records, 10 studies met PICO criteria. The primary meta-analysis (OD vs BE) showed osteodensification significantly outperformed bone expansion on bone density (SMD −0.71, P=0.002), crest expansion (SMD −1.12, P=0.04), and ISQ (SMD −8.88, P=0.0005). The ISQ advantage of nearly 9 units is clinically relevant, exceeding the typical ISQ threshold gap between immediate and delayed loading protocols. Bone compaction had insufficient eligible studies for quantitative synthesis. The authors conclude that osteodensification represents the preferred technique for narrow ridge implant site preparation based on current evidence.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA comparing all three narrow-ridge preparation techniques head-to-head with meta-analytic pooling
- Establishes **OD superiority over BE across three simultaneous outcomes** (BD, CE, ISQ) — multi-dimensional advantage, not limited to a single metric
- ISQ improvement with OD (SMD −8.88) is large-effect and clinically actionable: a ~9 ISQ point advantage shifts cases from "delayed loading required" to "immediate loading possible"
- Identifies bone compaction as a **research gap**: prevalent in clinical practice but underrepresented in comparative trials
- PROSPERO pre-registration (CRD42025646738) strengthens confidence in outcome selection and reporting completeness

## Methodology

SR+MA; PRISMA 2020; PROSPERO CRD42025646738. 2,464 records screened → 10 studies included. Inclusion: human studies comparing ≥2 of {BE, BC, OD} in narrow alveolar ridges with BD, CE, or ISQ outcomes. Primary comparison: OD vs BE. Statistics: SMD with 95% CI; random-effects model; α=0.05. Authors: Salamanca University, Spain.

## Results

**Osteodensification vs Bone Expansion:**
| Outcome | SMD (95% CI) | P |
|---|---|---|
| Bone Density (BD) | −0.71 (−1.15 to −0.27) | 0.002 |
| Crest Expansion (CE) | −1.12 (−2.20 to −0.04) | 0.04 |
| ISQ | −8.88 (−13.85 to −3.91) | 0.0005 |

Negative SMD = OD achieves higher values on all outcomes. Bone compaction: narrative synthesis only (insufficient data for meta-analysis).
