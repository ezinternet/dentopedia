## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (Scientific Reports 2021;11:7913, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital; 32 implants with adjacent-root injury in 28 patients, mean follow-up 122.7 months) grading injury as direct root invasion (group I), root surface contact (group II) or <1 mm proximity (group III).

90.6% of injured teeth remained functional — 7 of 27 untreated teeth required root canal treatment (3 later extracted), 19 of 27 (70.4%) stayed vital and asymptomatic, all 5 previously root-treated teeth were unaffected, and implant survival was 96.9% with ISQ rising from 64.1 to 73.9 (P = 0.007).

Direct invasion alone is not an indication to extract either the tooth or the implant; a monitored strategy with intervention reserved for symptoms or radiographic deterioration preserves the great majority of involved teeth.

## 세줄요약

후향 코호트 (Scientific Reports 2021;11:7913, 분당서울대; 인접치 손상 임플란트 32개/환자 28명, 평균 추적 122.7개월): 손상 유형을 직접 치근 침범 (group I)·치근 접촉 (group II)·1 mm 미만 근접 (group III)으로 등급화.

손상 치아의 90.6%가 기능 유지 — 미치료 27개 중 7개만 근관치료 (Root Canal Treatment, RCT) 필요(3개 추후 발치), 19/27 (70.4%)은 생활력 유지·무증상, 기존 근관치료 5개는 변화 없음; 임플란트 생존율 96.9% (골유착 실패 1개), 안정성 지수 (Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ) 64.1 → 73.9 유의 증가 (P=0.007).

직접 침범만으로 치아나 임플란트를 즉시 발치할 근거는 없고, 증상·방사선 악화 시에만 개입하는 감시 전략이 대다수 치아를 살린다.

## Summary

Adjacent-root injury is one of the few implant complications that is decided in a single irreversible moment — the bur either passes through the periodontal ligament or it does not — and the conventional response is to remove the implant or extract the tooth. This paper is essentially the only long-term dataset that tests that reflex, and it argues against it. Over a mean of 122.7 months (minimum 86), 32 injured teeth were followed and 90.6% stayed functional. The distribution matters: every adverse event clustered in the invasion group, where 4 of 10 teeth needed root canal treatment and 2 were lost, while contact and <1 mm proximity groups produced only one RCT each. Equally informative are the negatives — 19 of 27 untreated injured teeth remained vital and asymptomatic, and all 5 teeth that already had root canal treatment before implant placement carried on unchanged regardless of how deep the invasion was. The implant side is uneventful too: one osseointegration failure out of 32, 96.9% survival, and ISQ that rose rather than fell. The practical translation is a monitoring protocol: document the injury, test vitality, take a baseline radiograph, and treat only when symptoms or a periapical lesion appear — extraction is a fallback, not a first response. The counter-caveat is that this is 32 teeth from one centre with retrospective grading, so it describes a well-monitored cohort, not natural history.

## Key Contributions

- Supplies the largest long-term series (32 teeth, mean 122.7 months, minimum 86) on implant-induced adjacent-root injury, a complication otherwise reported only as single case reports.
- Provides a three-tier radiographic classification (invasion / contact / <1 mm proximity) that predicts where complications concentrate — all tooth losses occurred in the invasion group.
- Quantifies conservative management: 90.6% of injured teeth functional, 70.4% of untreated injured teeth vital and asymptomatic, 7 RCTs of which 3 later extracted (39 months, 7 years, 14 years).
- Shows a completed endodontic seal is protective — 5 of 5 previously root-treated adjacent teeth were unaffected by invasion or contact.
- Confirms the implant itself tolerates the adjacent injury: 96.9% survival, single osseointegration failure, ISQ 64.1 → 73.9 (P = 0.007).
- Documents the anatomic risk sites in practice — maxillary canines and mandibular second premolars dominate, with root curvature in 4 teeth and unnatural coronal contours in 15 of 32.

## Methodology

**Design.** Retrospective cohort, single institution (Seoul National University Bundang Hospital), all patients who received implants and sustained adjacent-tooth injury.

**Classification.** Group I: direct invasion of the adjacent root. Group II: root surface contact. Group III: <1 mm distance without contact. Graded on radiographs by the treating clinicians.

**Follow-up protocol.** Periodic clinical examination (vitality testing, symptoms, soft tissue status) plus periapical/panoramic radiographs; RCT performed when symptoms or a new apical lesion appeared. ISQ measured with Osstell where records were available (14 of 32 implants; primary and secondary).

**Statistics.** Paired t-test for ISQ change; chi-square for tooth complications; α = 0.05.

## Results

**Cohort.** 32 implants and adjacent teeth in 28 patients; mean follow-up 122.7 ± 31.7 months (minimum 86). Peak injury sites: maxillary canines and mandibular second premolars. Root curvature present in 4 injured teeth; 15 of 32 had crowns or lost coronal structure at placement.

**Tooth outcomes by group**

| Group | Teeth | New RCT | Extractions | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I (invasion) | 10 | 4 (+1 intentional replantation) | 2 | All adverse events concentrated here |
| II (contact) | 10 | 1 | 0 | Largely uneventful |
| III (<1 mm) | 12 | 1 | 1 | One pre-existing apical radiolucency remained asymptomatic |

- **Previously root-treated adjacent teeth:** 5 of 5 remained functional with no unusual change regardless of invasion depth.
- **Untreated injured teeth:** 7 of 27 required RCT (symptoms or apical lesion); 3 of those later extracted at 39 months, 7 years and 14 years; 1 intentionally replanted and later showed external root resorption; 19 of 27 (70.4%) stayed vital with no abnormal response.
- **Overall:** 90.6% of injured teeth remained functional.

**Implant outcomes.** Survival 96.9% (1 osseointegration failure, group I). ISQ 64.1 ± 12.4 (primary) → 73.9 ± 7.6 (secondary), P = 0.007, paired t-test (n = 14).

## Limitations

Retrospective, single centre, 32 teeth split 10/10/12 across groups — descriptive at group level, not powered for comparisons. Injury grade was assigned radiographically after the event without CBCT, so the true 3D relationship at drilling is unknown. ISQ data existed for fewer than half the implants (14/32). Vitality assessment was not protocolized and the decision to perform RCT was clinician-triggered by symptoms, which means the 70.4% vitality figure reflects a monitored and actively managed cohort rather than untreated natural history. There is no comparison group of uninjured adjacent teeth. Implant systems and treatment eras vary across the 2000s-2010s.
