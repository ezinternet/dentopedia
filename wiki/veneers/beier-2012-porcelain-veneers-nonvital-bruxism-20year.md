## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study (84 patients, 318 silicate glass-ceramic PLVs, Innsbruck, 1987–2009) with 20-year observation: estimated survival 94.4%/93.5%/82.93% at 5/10/20 years; ceramic fracture was the dominant failure mode (44.83%).

Nonvital teeth (ETT) had significantly higher failure risk (p=0.0012); bruxism carried a 7.7× greater failure risk (p=0.0004); smokers showed significantly more marginal discoloration.

This study contradicts more recent data (Gresnigt 2019, Etienne 2025) in the ETT finding — the likely explanation is the use of older feldspathic/silicate materials and pre-IDS adhesive protocols from the 1987–2009 era; bruxism remains the strongest identifiable patient risk factor regardless of era.

## Summary

Beier et al. (2012) conducted a retrospective analysis of 318 porcelain laminate veneers placed between 1987 and 2009 at Innsbruck Medical University in 84 patients. With a mean observation time of 118 ± 63 months, survival rates were 94.4% at 5 years, 93.5% at 10 years, and 82.93% at 20 years. The dominant failure mode was ceramic fracture (44.83%). Critically, nonvital (ETT) teeth showed significantly higher failure risk (p=0.0012), and bruxism patients had a 7.7-fold greater risk (p=0.0004). Marginal discoloration was significantly greater in smokers. These findings are important historically but must be interpreted in the context of 1987–2009 materials (silicate glass-ceramic) and adhesive protocols that predated IDS and modern dentin management techniques.

## Key Contributions

- Longest available follow-up period for LV survival (20 years)
- Identified nonvital teeth as statistically significant failure predictor (p=0.0012) — unique finding vs. modern-era studies
- Quantified bruxism risk: 7.7× greater failure — strongest identifiable patient-level risk factor
- Established 20-year survival benchmark (82.93%) for silicate glass-ceramic era veneers
- Demonstrated smoking does not cause restoration failure, only marginal discoloration

## Methodology

Retrospective clinical study; 84 patients (38 men, 46 women); 318 PLV restorations (maxilla + mandible anterior); silicate glass-ceramic material (Empress-era); evaluation using modified California Dental Association/Ryge criteria for esthetic match, surface quality, marginal discoloration, and integrity. Special populations: bruxism (42/84, 50%), smokers (23/84, 27.38%). Mean observation: 118 ± 63 months. Statistics: Kaplan-Meier survival analysis; Cox proportional hazards regression.

## Results

- **Total failures**: 29 (failure rate 17.24%, survival 82.76%)
- **Main failure mode**: Ceramic fracture (44.83%)
- **Estimated survival**:
  - 5 years: 94.4%
  - 10 years: 93.5%
  - 20 years: 82.93%
- **Nonvital teeth**: Significantly higher failure risk (p=0.0012)
- **Bruxism**: 7.7× greater failure risk (p=0.0004)
- **Smokers**: Significantly more marginal discoloration (p<0.01); no structural failure attributed to smoking
- **Mean observation**: 118 ± 63 months
