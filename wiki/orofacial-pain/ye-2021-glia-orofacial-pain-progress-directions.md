## Three-line Summary

Narrative review covering how non-neuronal glial cells — Schwann cells and satellite glial cells (SGCs) in the PNS, and microglia and astrocytes in the CNS — drive orofacial pain in headache, TMD, dental pulp injury, and head and neck cancer. Peripheral SGCs in the trigeminal ganglion amplify neuronal excitability via connexin-mediated gap junctions, PGE2/Nav1.7 upregulation, and cytokine release (IL-1β); central glia maintain sensitization in the trigeminal nucleus through glutamine synthetase, p38MAPK, and TNF-α. Glial-targeted therapies (connexin 43 inhibition, fluorocitrate, tonabersat) show preclinical promise, though ibudilast failed to improve chronic migraine in clinical trial.

## Background

Chronic orofacial pain (구강안면통증, Orofacial Pain) affects approximately 22% of American adults, representing a major healthcare burden. Research has predominantly focused on neuronal mechanisms, but non-neuronal glial cells are now recognized as important mediators of both peripheral and central pain sensitization. Glial cells in the PNS include satellite glial cells (SGCs, 위성신경절세포) in the trigeminal ganglia and Schwann cells (슈반세포) in peripheral nerves; in the CNS they include microglia (미세아교세포) and astrocytes (성상교세포) in the trigeminal nucleus (TN).

**Key glial markers:**
- S100B, GFAP (글리아 섬유산성단백, Glial Fibrillary Acidic Protein): Schwann cells, SGCs, astrocytes
- Iba1, CD11b/OX42: microglia-specific

## Headache and Migraine

Migraine (편두통) affects >1 billion adults globally. The trigeminovascular system — dura mater, meningeal blood vessels innervated by the ophthalmic branch of CN V, afferents to the trigeminal nucleus caudalis (TNC) — is central to migraine pathophysiology.

**Glial mechanisms:**
- SGC–neuron communication via gap junctions in TG: neuronal CGRP (칼시토닌 유전자 관련 펩타이드, Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide) activates adjacent SGCs → cytokine/chemokine release; CGRP is the target of newly approved migraine therapeutics (anti-CGRP monoclonals)
- Astrocyte S100B: serum levels elevated in migraine patients (children and adults)
- FHM2 (familial hemiplegic migraine 2): Atp1a2 mutations → loss of astrocyte Na+/K+ ATPase function → impaired glutamate clearance → facilitated cortical spreading depression (CSD, 피질확산억압)
- FHM2-knockin mice: elevated glutamate, lower CSD threshold, increased dendritic excitability in cingulate cortex; rescued by re-expression of Atp1a2 in astrocytes
- Adenosine A2A receptor on astrocytes regulates glutamate transporter 1 → glutamate homeostasis relevant to migraine
- GWAS of migraine patients: risk genes predominantly in signal transduction and protein modification pathways in astrocytes and oligodendrocytes

**Therapeutic attempts:**
- Tonabersat (gap junction modulator, inhibits CSD and neuronal-SGC signaling): some efficacy in RCTs for migraine with aura prophylaxis
- Ibudilast (cAMP PDE inhibitor, reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine production and gliosis): NO improvement in chronic migraine RCT; NO reduction in opioid use in MOH (medication overuse headache) study

## Temporomandibular Dysfunction (TMD)

TMD (턱관절장애, Temporomandibular Dysfunction) afflicts ~33% of the population, peak age 20–40. CFA (Complete Freund's Adjuvant) injection into the TMJ is the predominant animal model.

### Peripheral: SGCs in the Trigeminal Ganglion

CFA injection → SGC activation within hours, persisting ≥1 week:
- ↑ GFAP expression (SGC activation marker)
- ↑ P2Y1, P2Y2 purinergic receptors on SGCs; CGRP potentiates Ca²⁺ response via P2Y2 → downstream neuron hyperactivity; **P2Y2 blockade reduces hyperalgesia** (P2Y1 blockade does not)
- ↑ Connexin 36, 40 (neurons), connexin 43 (SGCs), connexin 26 (both): increased gap junction permeability
- **Connexin 43 selective inhibition** → reduced GFAP, connexin 43, IL-1β, Nav1.7, and mechanical hypersensitivity
- SGC-derived COX-2 → PGE2 → EP2 receptor → ↑ Nav1.7 expression → neuronal hyperexcitability; COX-2 inhibition or fluorocitrate (SGC blocker) reduces Nav1.7 and hyperalgesia
- SGC-derived IL-1β also increases Nav1.7 in TG neurons

### Central: Microglia and Astrocytes in the TNC

- Microglia (Iba1): activated as early as 24h post-CFA → ↑ TNF-α → pain development
- Astrocytes (GFAP): inconsistent at early time points; consistently upregulated **>3 days** post-CFA → astrocytes more critical for **persistence** of TMJ inflammation
- Intracisternal glial inhibitors → ↓ connexin 43, IL-1β, pNR1 (neuronal sensitization marker), and hyperalgesia
- Chronic stress model: astrocyte activation (not microglia) persists and drives hyperalgesia; L-α-aminoadipate (astrocyte inhibitor) reduces pain; **minocycline (microglia inhibitor) ineffective** → astrocyte-specific contribution to stress-induced TMD

### Other TMD Models
Masseter ligation, tooth movement, sustained mouth opening, chronic stress: all show peripheral and central glial activation; glial inhibitors reduce temporomandibular hyperalgesia.

## Dental Pulp Injury

### Schwann Cells Within the Pulp

The dental pulp is densely innervated by both myelinating (root region) and non-myelinating (coronal periphery) Schwann cells, marked by S100B and GFAP:
- Myelinated afferents branch, narrow, and lose myelin as they approach the pulp crown — the "de-myelination paradox"
- **Caries**: Schwann cell network expands below carious dentin → acts as biological barrier; induces macrophages into M2 (anti-inflammatory) phenotype; breaks down as caries enters the pulp
- **Primary tooth exfoliation**: Wallerian-like axon die-back; Schwann cells proliferate and dedifferentiate to clear myelin debris
- **Aging**: reduced Schwann cell density and arborization → diminished healing and regeneration capacity
- **Stem cell reservoir**: pulpal Schwann cells → multipotent mesenchymal stem cells → can differentiate into odontoblasts (dentin-secreting cells). Conversely, dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs) can differentiate into Schwann cells → therapeutic potential for peripheral nerve regeneration

### Glial Changes in TG and TNC After Pulp Injury

- TG: SGC GFAP ↑ adjacent to neurons innervating the injured tooth **and adjacent non-injured teeth** → mechanism for **ectopic tooth pain** (pain from inflamed tooth difficult to localize, perceived in other orofacial structures)
- GFAP increase predominantly in small- and medium-sized TG neurons
- SGC-released IL-1β acts on neighboring neurons → further sensitization; **connexin 43** mediates spread to uninjured TG neurons
- TNC (central): ↑ GFAP (astrocytes) ipsilateral — nucleus caudalis and caudalis-interpolaris transition zone
- ↑ Astrocyte **glutamine synthetase** activity → central sensitization and nociceptive behavior in acute and chronic pulp injury; inhibiting glutamine supply reduces sensitization
- ↑ **Iba1** (microglia) ipsilateral → **p38MAPK inhibitors block central sensitization**
- Persistent post-RCT pain likely mediated by pathological TG/TN glial changes — mechanism for a clinically recognized phenomenon

## Head and Neck Cancer (HNC) Pain

HNC (두경부암): sixth most common cancer worldwide; >650,000 cases and 330,000 deaths annually. Up to 85% of patients report pain at diagnosis; most receive inadequate pain treatment. Cancer microenvironment mediators activate and sensitize primary afferents.

**Glial findings in animal models:**
- Walker 256B breast cancer cells in rat vibrissa pad: ↑ microglia and astrocyte markers in ipsilateral TNC, **no SGC activation** → CNS-predominant glial response
- Oral cancer cells in rat gingiva: ↑ SGC activation in ipsilateral TG

Role of glial cells in HNC pain remains poorly understood; most mechanistic data come from non-HNC pain models.

## Clinical Relevance

1. **Ectopic tooth pain** — SGC activation spreading to adjacent TG neurons via connexin 43 explains why inflamed pulp pain is poorly localized and perceived in non-injured teeth
2. **Post-treatment persistent pain** — glial pathological changes in TG/TNC after RCT or extraction are a plausible biological mechanism
3. **Migraine aura** — astrocyte Atp1a2 dysfunction in FHM2 provides a direct genetic link; astrocyte-targeted therapy is theoretically rational
4. **TMD treatment** — connexin 43 inhibitors and P2Y2 antagonists show preclinical efficacy; clinical trials are warranted
5. **HNC pain** — CNS-predominant glial activation suggests centrally-acting glial modulators may be more relevant than peripheral approaches

## Limitations

- Narrative (non-systematic) review; no formal bias assessment
- Most mechanistic data from rodent models; behavioral phenotyping in orofacial region more challenging than limb models
- Existing studies do not distinguish neuropathic vs. non-neuropathic glial activation
- Glial modulators (ibudilast) have failed in clinical trials for migraine despite preclinical rationale
- Schwann cell contributions to headache and HNC pain not yet studied (listed as "ND" in animal tables)
