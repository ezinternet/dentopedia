## Three-line Summary

Animal study (anesthetized male rats, single-unit recording in trigeminal ganglion) testing whether fremanezumab (TEV-48125, a humanized anti-CGRP monoclonal antibody) differentially affects Aδ versus C-fiber meningeal nociceptors activated by cortical spreading depression (CSD).

Fremanezumab pretreatment selectively decreased the percentage of Aδ neurons activated by CSD, but had no effect on C-fiber neuron activation — the first identification of an anti-migraine drug selective for Aδ-fibers peripherally.

This peripheral Aδ-selectivity is proposed to explain the drug's previously reported selective inhibition of central high-threshold (HT, predominant Aδ input) but not wide-dynamic-range trigeminovascular neurons, and may explain incomplete efficacy in some migraine patients; abstract-only source (PMC full text was empty for this PMCID).

## Summary

Fremanezumab is a humanized anti-CGRP monoclonal antibody used clinically for migraine prevention. The authors had previously shown that fremanezumab selectively inhibits central high-threshold (HT) but not wide-dynamic-range (WDR) trigeminovascular dorsal horn neurons responding to cortical spreading depression (CSD), a migraine-relevant cortical event. To find the peripheral basis for this central selectivity, they recorded single-unit activity from Aδ and C-fiber meningeal nociceptors in the trigeminal ganglion of anesthetized rats. Fremanezumab pretreatment selectively reduced the percentage of Aδ neurons activated by CSD, with no effect on C-fiber neurons. Because HT central neurons receive predominantly Aδ input, this peripheral Aδ-selective inhibition provides a plausible mechanistic explanation for the drug's central HT-selective effect, and offers the first pharmacological evidence of a fiber-type-selective (Aδ vs C) drug action in the trigeminal meningeal nociceptive system. The authors note this selectivity may also explain why CGRP-mAbs are not effective in all migraine patients — those whose headache is driven more by a C-fiber/WDR pathway may respond less.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates direct pharmacological dissociation between Aδ and C-fiber meningeal nociceptors using a clinically approved anti-migraine biologic.
- Links a peripheral (trigeminal ganglion) mechanism to a previously reported central (dorsal horn) selective effect via the Aδ→HT neuron input pathway.
- Identifies Aδ meningeal nociceptors as the likely peripheral site of action for CGRP-mAb headache prevention.
- Proposes a mechanistic hypothesis for heterogeneous clinical response to CGRP-mAbs across migraine patients.

## Methodology

Single-unit extracellular recording of mechanosensitive primary afferent meningeal nociceptors in the trigeminal ganglion of anesthetized male rats, classified as Aδ (thinly myelinated) or C-fiber (unmyelinated) by conduction velocity. Cortical spreading depression (CSD) was used as the migraine-relevant activating stimulus. Percentage of Aδ vs C neurons activated by CSD was compared between fremanezumab-pretreated and control animals. ## Results

Fremanezumab selectively decreased the percentage of Aδ neurons activated by CSD; C-fiber neuron activation was unaffected. This parallels the authors' earlier central finding of selective HT (not WDR) neuron inhibition, consistent with HT neurons' predominant Aδ input — together supporting a peripheral Aδ-selective site of action for CGRP-mAb migraine prevention.
