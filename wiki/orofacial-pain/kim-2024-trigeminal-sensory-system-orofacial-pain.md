## Three-line Summary

Narrative review synthesizing the anatomy, cellular composition, and synaptic physiology of the trigeminal sensory system (trigeminal ganglion, mesencephalic/principal/spinal trigeminal nuclei, paratrigeminal nucleus) and relating it to four orofacial pain conditions.

Central sensitization in the spinal trigeminal nucleus (particularly Sp5C↔Sp5O/Sp5I intersubnuclear pathways) — driven by NMDA-receptor- and mGluR-dependent LTP, microglial activation, and reduced GABAergic/glycinergic inhibition — underlies orofacial neuropathic pain, trigeminal neuralgia's continuous-pain phase, headache chronification, and burning mouth syndrome (Aδ-fiber cooling-inhibition loss unmasking C-fiber burning).

No original data (narrative review); clinical implication is that four seemingly distinct orofacial pain syndromes converge mechanistically on a shared trigeminal-nuclei central-sensitization pathway, suggesting shared therapeutic targets (NMDA/mGluR/microglia) rather than fully independent disease processes.

## 세줄요약

삼차신경절 (Trigeminal Ganglion, TG), 중뇌핵 (Mesencephalic Nucleus, Me5)·주감각핵 (Principal Sensory Nucleus, Pr5)·삼차신경척수핵 (Spinal Trigeminal Nucleus, Sp5)·부삼차신경핵 (Paratrigeminal Nucleus, Pa5)의 해부학·세포구성·시냅스전달을 정리하고 4가지 구강안면통증 (Orofacial Pain)과 연결한 내러티브 리뷰.

Sp5C↔Sp5O/Sp5I 간 핵간연결(intersubnuclear pathway)에서 NMDA 수용체·대사성 글루타메이트 수용체 (Metabotropic Glutamate Receptor, mGluR) 의존성 장기강화 (Long-Term Potentiation, LTP), 미세아교세포 (Microglia) 활성화, GABA/glycine 억제성 전달 감소로 유발되는 중추감작 (Central Sensitization)이 신경병증성통증·삼차신경통 지속통·두통 만성화·구강작열감증후군 (Burning Mouth Syndrome, BMS, Aδ섬유 냉각억제 소실→C섬유 작열통 노출)의 공통 기전.

원저 데이터 없는 내러티브 리뷰이므로 근거수준은 제한적이나, 서로 다른 4개 임상증후군이 삼차신경핵 중추감작이라는 공유 경로로 수렴한다는 시사점은 NMDA/mGluR/미세아교세포 표적 치료의 공통 근거를 제공.

## Summary

This narrative review (Kim et al., 2024, *Int J Mol Sci*) provides a system-level anatomical and physiological account of the trigeminal sensory system — the trigeminal ganglion plus four brainstem sensory nuclei (mesencephalic, principal, spinal trigeminal [oralis/interpolaris/caudalis], and paratrigeminal) — and relates its synaptic organization to four orofacial pain conditions: orofacial neuropathic pain, trigeminal neuralgia (TN), headache (migraine/tension-type/trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias), and burning mouth syndrome (BMS).

The trigeminal ganglion (TG) is a pseudounipolar sensory neuron population classified into type A (large, light — tooth-pulp afferents), type B (smaller, dark — corneal afferents), and type C cells, with ~50% CGRP+, ~15% substance P+, and 32–45% glutamatergic. Autonomic (sympathetic and parasympathetic) fibers travel alongside the three trigeminal branches (V1/V2/V3), supplying pupillary, lacrimal, parotid, and submandibular/sublingual gland innervation via the ciliary, pterygopalatine, otic, and submandibular ganglia respectively.

Within the brainstem, the mesencephalic nucleus (Me5) houses proprioceptive afferents from masticatory/extraocular muscle spindles; the principal sensory nucleus (Pr5) processes discriminative tactile sensation (analogous to dorsal column nuclei) and projects to thalamic VPM/Po; and the spinal trigeminal nucleus (Sp5) — divided into oralis (Sp5O, intraoral touch/pressure), interpolaris (Sp5I, mixed mechanoreceptive/nociceptive), and caudalis (Sp5C, the "medullary dorsal horn," primary nociceptive processor with laminae 1/2/3-4 organization) — forms the core nociceptive relay. The paratrigeminal nucleus (Pa5) integrates orofacial somatic and upper-GI visceral nociceptive convergence via calbindin D28k+ neurons.

Synaptic transmission within Sp5 is mediated by excitatory glutamatergic (AMPA, kainate, NMDA) and inhibitory GABAergic/glycinergic mechanisms. Long-term potentiation (LTP) at Sp5C→Sp5O and Sp5C→Sp5I intersubnuclear synapses — mediated variably by NMDA receptors, group I mGluRs (mGluR1/5), and voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels — is proposed as a cellular substrate for persistent pain amplification. Wind-up (homosynaptic, C-fiber-driven progressive response enhancement) and central sensitization (heterosynaptic, receptive-field expansion, spontaneous activity increase) are described as sequential phenomena within Sp5O and Sp5C.

The four clinical sections connect this circuitry to disease: **orofacial neuropathic pain** arises from peripheral sensitization (VGSC/VGCC/TRPV1 upregulation, pro-inflammatory cytokines) plus central sensitization (Sp5 hyperexcitability, LTP, microglial activation, reduced GABA/glycine tone). **Trigeminal neuralgia** is attributed primarily to vascular compression (superior cerebellar artery most commonly) causing focal demyelination, ectopic discharge, and ephaptic transmission between low-threshold Aβ touch fibers and pain fibers — with V2/V3 branches most commonly affected, ~25% of patients showing no vascular compression (often MS/tumor-related), and Sp5 neuroplastic changes implicated in the continuous-pain (background burning/throbbing) phase between paroxysms. **Headache** (migraine, TTH, TACs) involves trigeminovascular CGRP/substance P/PACAP release, Sp5C central sensitization (manifesting clinically as cutaneous allodynia), and hypothalamic/thalamic/cortical involvement (cortical spreading depression in migraine with aura); CGRP-targeted therapies and neuromodulation (sTMS, nVNS) are noted as emerging treatments. **Burning mouth syndrome** is framed as a trigeminal small-fiber neuropathy — reduced intraepithelial nerve fiber density, elevated TRPV1/NGF/P2X3 on tongue biopsy, and QST evidence that Aδ-fiber (cooling) hypofunction is more prevalent than C-fiber hypofunction, supporting a model where loss of Aδ-mediated cooling inhibition unmasks persistent C-fiber burning; taste (chorda tympani) nerve hypofunction is also implicated, linking taste disturbance to pain severity. No validated BMS animal model currently exists.

## Key Contributions

- System-level anatomical/physiological synthesis of the entire trigeminal sensory system (TG + Me5/Pr5/Sp5/Pa5), including the often-overlooked parasympathetic circuitry riding along V1–V3 branches.
- Detailed mapping of Sp5 intersubnuclear synaptic connections (Sp5O↔Sp5I↔Sp5C) and their receptor pharmacology (AMPA/kainate/NMDA excitatory; GABA/glycine inhibitory), framed as a "multilevel filtering system" gating nociceptive input.
- Mechanistic bridge between cellular/synaptic plasticity (LTP/LTD, wind-up, central sensitization) and four distinct orofacial pain phenotypes, proposing a shared trigeminal-nuclei central-sensitization substrate across neuropathic pain, TN, headache, and BMS.
- Detailed clinical-mechanistic profile of TN (vascular compression epidemiology, branch-involvement rates, sex ratio, ion channel changes — Nav1.3↑/Nav1.7↓/Kv7.2↑ — in animal models) and BMS (biopsy/QST/immunohistochemistry findings supporting a small-fiber neuropathy model).

## Methodology

Narrative (non-systematic) literature review; no PRISMA protocol or formal search/selection criteria reported. Synthesizes rodent, cat, monkey, and human anatomical, electrophysiological, immunohistochemical, and clinical (QST, biopsy, imaging) studies across the trigeminal anatomy and four disease sections.

## Results

Qualitative synthesis (no primary data). Key points: TG neuron subtypes correlate with afferent target (tooth pulp → type A; cornea → type B); Sp5C→Sp5O and Sp5C→Sp5I LTP is NMDA- and/or mGluR1/5-dependent; TN shows no vascular compression in ~25% of patients (V2/V3 most commonly affected, V1 rare ~4%, female:male ~2–3:1); rat TN models show Nav1.3↑/Nav1.7↓/Kv7.2↑ in trigeminal ganglion neurons and an SCN8A (Nav1.6) mutation reported in a human TN case; BMS prevalence 0.1–3.9% (higher in postmenopausal women 50–70) with reduced intraepithelial nerve fiber density, increased TRPV1/NGF/P2X3, and QST-confirmed Aδ-fiber hypofunction exceeding C-fiber hypofunction.
