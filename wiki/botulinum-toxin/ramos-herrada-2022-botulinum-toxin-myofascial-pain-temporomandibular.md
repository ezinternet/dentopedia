## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (searched to February 2021; 900 records, 8 RCTs, n=314, ages 18-75, mostly women) of botulinum toxin for refractory myofascial pain related to temporomandibular disorders; the abstract does not mention a meta-analysis.

The authors conclude low-dose botulinum toxin is effective for refractory TMD myofascial pain; RoB 2.0 rated 7 of 8 trials "some concerns" and 1 low risk, and GRADE certainty was medium to low.

Clinically this supports botulinum toxin only as a refractory-case option; no pooled estimate, dose or follow-up data are available at abstract level.

## Summary

Published in Dental and Medical Problems (2022), this systematic review asked whether botulinum toxin benefits patients with myofascial pain related to TMD. Five databases (PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane Library, LILACS) plus grey literature were searched without language or year limits until February 2021. Of 900 records, 8 RCTs (314 patients) were included. Only 1 trial had low overall risk of bias by RoB 2.0; 7 had some concerns. The authors report that low doses of botulinum toxin were effective for refractory myofascial pain, with medium-to-low certainty (GRADE). Source: PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.17219/dmp/145759).

## Key Contributions

- Myofascial-pain-specific SR limited to RCTs, with RoB 2.0 and GRADE.
- Explicit certainty statement: medium to low, with 7 of 8 trials at some-concerns risk.
- Frames the "low dose" message for refractory cases.

## Methodology

PRISMA-style search (PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane Library, LILACS; grey literature via Google Scholar, ClinicalTrials.gov, OpenGrey, reference lists) to February 2021. RCTs of botulinum toxin for myofascial pain were included. Appraisal: Cochrane RoB 2.0 and GRADE. Abstract-only; data extraction details are not available.

## Results

- 900 records retrieved; 8 RCTs included; 314 patients (18-75 y, predominantly women).
- RoB 2.0: 7 trials "some concerns", 1 low overall risk.
- Low-dose botulinum toxin effective in refractory TMD myofascial pain (authors' conclusion).
- GRADE certainty: medium to low.
- Effect sizes, doses, injected muscles and follow-up are not given in the abstract.

## What this paper adds versus held pages

- Versus [[tmj/zhu-2020-btx-painful-tmd-sr-ma]] and [[tmj/saini-2024-botulinum-toxin-tmd-sr-ma]]: those pool painful TMD broadly (SR+MA); this SR is narrower (myofascial only) and narrative at abstract level, so it adds no pooled estimate, only a certainty appraisal. Its verdict agrees in direction. The 8 included trials very probably overlap with those syntheses (not verified, abstract-only).
- Versus [[botulinum-toxin/de-la-torre-canales-2024-botulinum-toxin-a-myogenous]]: that umbrella review finds BoNT-A not superior to standard care and recommends last-line use; this SR's "effective" claim is placebo-level and consistent with only the refractory framing.
- Versus later single trials: [[botulinum-toxin/kim-sr-2023-effect-botulinum-toxin-masticatory-muscle]] (positive pilot), [[botulinum-toxin/minston-2025-effect-pain-following-botulinum-toxin]] (null primary outcome at 100 U single session) and [[botulinum-toxin/abdulrahman-2026-botulinum-toxin-myofascial-pain-masseter-temporalis]] (uncontrolled 2-year cohort) all post-date its February 2021 search, so the "low doses effective" message has since been contested by the Minston null result. This is a tension, not a formal contradiction, since the abstract gives no dose or outcome definitions.
