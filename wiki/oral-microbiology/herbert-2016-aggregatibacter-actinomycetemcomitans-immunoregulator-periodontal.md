## Three-line Summary

Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans (Aa) deploys leukotoxin (LtxA), cytolethal distending toxin (CDT), and LPS to evade host immunity across both non-hematopoietic (epithelium, fibroblasts) and hematopoietic (myeloid, lymphoid) cell compartments, driving pathologic inflammation in the periodontal microenvironment.

Aa activates MAPK/NF-κB, NLRP3 inflammasome, and RANKL/OPG signaling axes in multiple cell types, resulting in elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-17) and alveolar bone loss via uncoupled osteoclast-osteoblast remodeling.

Localized aggressive periodontitis caused by serotype b (JP2 clone) represents the most virulent form; alveolar bone loss reflects both enhanced osteoclastogenesis and suppressed osteoblastogenesis, rendering anti-resorptive therapy alone insufficient.

## 세줄요약

응집간균 (Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans, Aa)은 백혈구독소 (LtxA), 세포독성팽창독소 (CDT), LPS를 이용해 비조혈계(치은 상피·섬유아세포)와 조혈계(골수계·림프계) 세포 구획 전반에서 숙주 면역을 회피하고 치주 미세환경의 병적 염증을 유발한다.

Aa는 여러 세포 유형에서 MAPK/NF-κB, NLRP3 인플라마좀, RANKL/OPG 신호 축을 활성화해 TNF-α·IL-1β·IL-6·IL-17 등 전염증성 사이토카인을 급증시키고, 파골세포 (Osteoclast)-조골세포 (Osteoblast) 리모델링 비결합을 통한 치조골 소실 (Alveolar Bone Loss)을 초래한다.

혈청형 b (JP2 클론)에 의한 국소 공격성 치주염 (LAP)이 가장 심한 형태이며, 치조골 소실은 파골세포 (Osteoclast) 활성 증가와 조골세포 (Osteoblast) 억제가 동시에 작용하기 때문에 항골흡수 치료만으로는 불충분하다.

## Background

Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans (Aa) is a Gram-negative, capnophilic coccobacillus that is the principal etiologic agent of localized aggressive periodontitis (LAP), formerly called localized juvenile periodontitis. It preferentially affects the succedaneous first molars and incisors in adolescents and young adults. Aa has also been implicated in extra-oral infections including infective endocarditis, bacterial arthritis, pregnancy-associated septicemia, cerebral abscesses, and osteomyelitis. Six serotypes (a–g) exist; serotypes a, b, and c are most frequently isolated from periodontal disease sites.

The JP2 clone of serotype b harbors a 530-bp deletion in the ltxA operon promoter, driving excessive leukotoxin production and conferring the highest virulence. Scaling and root planing alone achieves poor Aa depletion; concomitant systemic antibiotic therapy or surgical intervention is required for favorable microbiological and clinical outcomes.

This review systematically evaluates the host immune response to Aa across three compartments: (I) non-hematopoietic barriers, (II) hematopoietic myeloid and lymphoid lineages, and (III) osteoimmunological effects on alveolar bone remodeling.

## Primary Virulence Factors

### Leukotoxin (LtxA)
LtxA is a surface-associated and secreted exotoxin that is the principal Aa virulence factor for immune cell killing. It binds LFA-1 (CD18/CD11b) on leukocytes, triggering cofilin dephosphorylation, actin depolymerization, and lysosomal-mediated cell death. LtxA also forms pores in monocyte membranes, causing ATP leakage that activates P2X7 receptors → extracellular K+ influx → NLRP3 inflammasome activation → caspase-1 cleavage → IL-1β and IL-18 secretion. Serotype b JP2 strains demonstrate enhanced cytotoxicity against neutrophils, though evidence on PMN phagocytosis evasion is conflicting.

### Cytolethal Distending Toxin (CDT)
CDT (CdtA/CdtB/CdtC holocomplex) was detectable in the majority of Aa isolates from Swedish and Brazilian cohorts. CdtB binds monocyte surfaces and induces G2/M cell cycle arrest. CDT upregulates RANKL on gingival fibroblasts and PDL cells, indirectly promoting osteoclastogenesis. CDT induces IL-1β and IL-18 via NLRP3/ASC/caspase-1/ROS/ATP/K+ signaling in monocyte cell lines.

### LPS (Lipopolysaccharide)
Aa LPS is recognized primarily by TLR4 (also TLR2 and TLR5), activating MyD88 → IκB-α phosphorylation → NF-κB, and all three MAPKs (p38, JNK, ERK) in macrophages. Key regulatory nodes:
- **MK2** (p38α/β substrate): promotes pro-inflammatory cytokine production; MK2 attenuation in rat model decreases bone loss
- **MKP-1** (anti-inflammatory phosphatase): dephosphorylates p-p38 and p-JNK → dampens IL-6, TNF-α, IL-10, CXCL1 and decreases osteoclast formation
- **TTP** (tristetraprolin): under normal conditions, binds AU-rich elements (ARE) in IL-6/TNF-α mRNA to promote degradation; p38/MK2 phosphorylates TTP, preventing ARE binding → increased cytokine translation

### Other Virulence Factors
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|

## Host Response Architecture

### I. Non-Hematopoietic Barriers

**Gingival Epithelium**: Aa migrates through three layers of gingival epithelium, upregulating TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8 (CXCR1/CXCR2 chemokine → recruits PMNs). Epithelial apoptosis occurs via caspase 3/7 pathway; Smad2 phosphorylation via TGF-βR1 signaling leads to cleaved caspase-3 activity.

**Gingival and PDL Fibroblasts**: Secrete IL-6 and IL-8 in response to Aa. Aa does not directly stimulate IL-1β or TNF-α from fibroblasts, but both cytokines stimulate RANKL via p38 MAPK on stromal/bone marrow cells. PDL fibroblasts: Aa activates p38 and JNK phosphorylation.

### II. Hematopoietic Response

**Chemokine cascade**: CCL3 (MIP-1α), CCR5, CXCR3, IP-10 (CXCL10) elevated in disease; CXCL1 is the first chemokine mRNA upregulated post-inoculation (recruits PMNs); elevated CCL3 is a prognostic marker for bone loss.

**Neutrophils (PMNs)**: First responders; Aa-specific IgG titers enhance PMN phagocytosis and killing. Cathepsin G shows greater bactericidal activity against Aa than elastase. ROS production induced by Aa; intracellular azithromycin accumulation in neutrophils decreases Aa survival.

**Macrophages**: TLR4 (primary) and TLR2 cooperate; double TLR2/4 deficiency produces greater cytokine blunting than single. MyD88 dependence confirmed for TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-12p40.

**Monocytes/Inflammasome**: LtxA and CDT are primary NLRP3 inflammasome activators → caspase-1 → IL-1β, IL-18. Serotype-dependent: serotype a upregulates NLRP3/attenuates NLRP6 in one study; conflicting serotype b findings implicate cathepsin B and ROS independently of NLRP3/caspase-1.

**Dendritic Cells (DCs)**: Monocytes from LAP patients spontaneously differentiate into CD11c+ DCs. Aa is the most potent IL-12p70 inducer among tested periodontal bacteria. Serotype b most preferentially elicits IL-12, IL-1β, IL-23, CCR5, CCR6 via TLR2.

**B and T Lymphocytes**: Plasma cells (mature B cells) secrete opsonic IgG2a against Aa. Th cell polarization: serotype b → strongest Th1 and Th17 (via IL-12 and IL-23); Treg subset (CCR4/IL-4/CCL22 pathway) suppresses inflammation — CCR4/IL-4/CCL22 deficiency → exacerbated bone loss.

### III. Osteoimmunological Effects

**RANKL/OPG Imbalance**: 91.7% of radiographic vertical bone loss subjects are Aa-positive. Aa indirectly induces RANKL via CDT, LPS, OMP29 on fibroblasts and PDL cells; T cell OMP29 priming also drives RANKL surface expression. RANKL-RANK → NFATc1 (master osteoclastogenesis transcription factor).

**Direct Osteoclastogenesis**: LPS + TNF-α → p38/JNK MAPK → direct osteoclast formation from defined osteoclast progenitors (dOCPs). IL-1R signaling: IL-1R antagonist decreases osteoclast formation and bone loss in mouse LPS challenge.

**MMP-mediated Resorption**: Aa induces MMP-1, -2, -9 and TIMP genes; LPS → MMP-1, -3, -9 and TIMP-1 from peripheral blood monocytes. Aa possesses direct bone-degrading collagenase activity; binds saliva-coated hydroxyapatite.

**Osteoblast Impairment (Dual Mechanism)**:
- LPS (1 µg/mL): inhibits DNA synthesis 30% and collagen synthesis 40% in calvaria explants
- Capsular material (LPS-free): inhibits DNA/collagen at 10 ng/mL
- LPS concentration-dependent: 0.1 µg/mL suppresses BSP (osteoblast differentiation marker); 0.01 µg/mL enhances it
- NLRP3 inflammasome: knockdown (siRNA) attenuates Aa-induced osteoblast apoptosis in MG63 cells
- Net result: alveolar bone loss is secondary to **both** increased osteoclastogenesis AND decreased osteoblastogenesis

## Therapeutic Implications

| Target | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Systemic antibiotics | SRP alone poorly eliminates Aa; azithromycin accumulates in PMNs enhancing killing | Clinical + experimental |
| MK2 inhibition | Decreased inflammatory infiltrate and TRAP+ osteoclasts in rat model | Pre-clinical |
| MKP-1 overexpression | Decreased cytokine production, osteoclast formation, and bone loss | Pre-clinical |
| IL-1R antagonist | Decreased osteoclast formation and bone loss in mouse LPS challenge | Pre-clinical |
| CCL22/CCR4/Treg axis | Deficiency exacerbates bone loss; target to enhance Treg suppression | Murine |
| RANKL/OPG | RANKL/OPG imbalance drives bone resorption; OPG analog potential | Mechanistic |

## Knowledge Gaps (Authors' Identified)
- Majority of mechanistic data are from in vitro and murine models; limited human mechanistic studies
- Mast cell role in Aa pathogenesis unstudied for aggressive periodontitis specifically
- T cell (Th1/Th17) mechanistic responses less well characterized than Th2/Treg
- Whether Aa alone can stimulate osteoclastogenesis and/or inhibit osteoblastogenesis (without other cells) unclear
- Host response mechanisms appear cell-type-, strain-, and serotype-specific → limited generalizability

## Study Limitations
- Narrative (not systematic) review; no formal evidence grading
- High heterogeneity in methodology, strain/serotype used, and in vitro vs. in vivo settings across cited studies
- Many in vitro findings have not been validated in human clinical models
