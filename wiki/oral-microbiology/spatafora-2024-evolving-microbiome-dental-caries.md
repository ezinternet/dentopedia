## Three-line Summary

Dental caries is a polymicrobial, ecologically driven disease in which dietary carbohydrates shift the tooth biofilm from a symbiotic pioneer community toward an acidogenic, acid-tolerant dysbiosis dominated by Streptococcus mutans, Lactobacillus species, and Prevotella species at different lesion stages.
The extended ecological plaque hypothesis describes progressive biofilm succession — dynamic stability → acidogenic → aciduric stages — with S. mutans acting as a potential keystone pathogen despite being a minority species in metatranscriptomic analyses, while lactobacilli comprise 42.3% of deepest dentin caries sequences.
Modern treatment has shifted from broad-spectrum antimicrobials toward precision approaches including specifically targeted antimicrobial peptides (STAMPs), pH-responsive nanoparticles, and silver diamine fluoride, with the STAMP C16G2 currently in phase II clinical trials.

## Summary

Dental caries is one of the most prevalent chronic diseases worldwide, affecting an estimated 43% of children's deciduous teeth and 2.3 billion adults with untreated permanent tooth caries. The disease costs >172 billion USD annually in treatment and generates 2.7 million disability-adjusted life years (DALYs).

This review by Spatafora, Li, He, Cowan, and Tanner — primarily Forsyth Institute-affiliated researchers with century-long institutional ties to caries microbiology — synthesizes over 100 years of research from Willoughby Miller's chemo-parasitic theory (1890) and Kilian Clarke's isolation of S. mutans (1924) through modern multi-omics. The core argument: caries is not a "one germ, one disease" process but a community-level ecological shift (dysbiosis) driven by dietary carbohydrates in a susceptible host.

### Ecological Plaque Hypothesis (Extended)

The tooth-associated microbiome develops through successive colonization stages:

1. **Pioneer species / Dynamic Stability Stage**: Within 2 h of cleaning, Streptococcus gordonii, S. sanguinis, S. oralis, and Actinomyces species colonize the dental pellicle via receptor-ligand (cryptitope) interactions. These species buffer acid production via arginine deaminase (ammonia) and urease. S. gordonii can comprise up to 70% of early biofilm community.

2. **Early Colonizer / Acidogenic Stage**: Non-mutans streptococci (S. mitis, S. intermedius, S. constellatus) and additional Actinomyces species produce acidic byproducts, lowering pH and creating conditions for acid-tolerant organisms. At this stage, enamel remineralization is still possible.

3. **Secondary Colonizer / Aciduric Stage**: S. mutans, Lactobacillus spp., Veillonella, and Prevotella spp. dominate. Surface roughening from enamel demineralization accelerates colonization by more aggressive pathogens.

4. **Mature Biofilm / Advanced Dentin**: Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium dominate deepest dentin layers. Proteolytic species (Prevotella, Propionibacterium) contribute through dentin organic matrix degradation at higher pH (~5.0) than purely acidogenic species.

Dietary carbohydrate frequency and duration are the primary drivers of community shift. The biofilm architecture — EPS (Extracellular Polysaccharide) matrix, fluid channels, microcolonies — is maintained by S. mutans glucosyltransferases and provides structural support and acid diffusion barriers.

## Key Contributions

### 1. Polymicrobial Causation Documented

No single microbiota characterizes all dental caries. Species composition varies with:
- Subject age, diet, systemic health
- Lesion site (coronal vs. root), depth (enamel vs. dentin), and progression rate
- Individual microbiome variation

S. mutans is frequently absent from carious lesions, particularly less aggressive ones. Non-mutans streptococci, Actinomyces, Bifidobacterium, Prevotella, and Candida all contribute to lesion-type-specific pathogenesis.

### 2. Keystone Pathogen Concept for S. mutans

Metatranscriptomic analysis (Simon-Soro et al.) found S. mutans present as a minority species in adult caries despite being a prime etiological candidate. The keystone pathogen concept reconciles low abundance with high ecological impact: S. mutans may remodel community pH and biofilm architecture beyond what its numerical proportion suggests. Localized "rotund" concentrations of S. mutans over white-spot lesions support this.

### 3. Lactobacillus as Dominant Deep Caries Species

| Lesion Type | Lactobacillus Proportion | Key Species |
|---|---|---|
| Initial (WSL) | Low; variable | L. acidophilus |
| Dentin (Rocas et al.) | 42.3% of sequences | L. paracasei, L. rhamnosus |
| Advanced dentin (half of lesions) | 63–96% of sequences | L. fermentum, L. plantarum |

Lactobacillus colonization is reduced after successful restoration — the carious cavity is its primary ecological niche.

### 4. Metagenomics Reveals Functional Caries Signatures

Caries-active vs. caries-free comparisons (Belda-Ferre comparative metagenomics):
- **Caries-active enriched**: mixed-acid fermentation genes, iron scavenging, oxidative/osmotic stress response
- **Healthy enriched**: bacteriocin biosynthesis, periplasmic stress response (spy, cpxP), capsular/extracellular polysaccharide genes, bacitracin stress response

Healthy plaque functional composition (Xie et al.): carbohydrate metabolism 11.88%, amino acid metabolism 7.89%, protein metabolism 9.34% of assigned reads.

### 5. Metatranscriptomics Confirms Tissue-Dependent Etiology

Each adult caries sample contains 70–400 metabolically active species, with composition varying among individuals and between lesions in the same individual. Enamel (coronal) and dentin lesions harbor different metabolically active communities — supporting the concept that caries is a tissue-dependent disease with different etiologies by site. Carbohydrate metabolism accounts for ~15% of the total dental biofilm transcriptome, with disaccharide metabolism transcripts exceeding monosaccharide by factor of 2.

## Results

### Epidemiology (NHANES 2015–2016; Global Burden of Disease)

- Deciduous teeth caries prevalence (2019): 43% globally (38% high-income, 46% low-income)
- Untreated permanent tooth caries: 2.3 billion adults
- US dental spend: >$110 billion/year (2016); >60% for caries treatment
- Global caries cost: >$172 billion; 2.7 million DALYs
- NHANES: caries prevalence increases from 9.5% to 96% across the lifespan
- Highest caries disparity: Hispanic and non-Hispanic Black populations; income inversely correlated

### Microbiology Findings by Lesion Type

**Initial caries (white-spot lesions, WSL)**:
- S. mutans in only a fraction of lesions (0.0001–10% by culture)
- Non-mutans streptococci (S. mitis, S. oralis) and Actinomyces are ubiquitous
- Microbiota consistent with acidogenic/acid-tolerant shift, borderline of dynamic stability and acidogenic stages

**Root caries (adults)**:
- Complex anaerobic culture microbiota: S. mutans, Lactobacillus, Veillonella, Prevotella, Actinomyces, yeast
- Yeast (C. albicans) detected in >60% of older Chinese individuals with root caries
- Proteolytic bacteria: Prevotella, Propionibacterium, Fusobacterium — associated with higher lesion pH (~5.0)

**Deep dentin caries**:
- Complexity: one complex dominated by Lactobacillus, another by Prevotella, a third by Fusobacterium + Porphyromonas
- Lower pH correlates with higher lactobacilli, lower diversity
- Metatranscriptomic analysis: dentin shows 70–400 active species; wider gene expression range than coronal caries

**Severe early childhood caries (S-ECC)**:
- S. mutans at significantly higher levels than in caries-free children
- Bifidobacterium longum, Veillonella parvula, non-mutans streptococci co-prominent
- S. sobrinus detected without S. mutans in some children — alternate caries pathogen

**Aggressive caries (high-caries Romanian adolescents vs. low-caries Swedish)**:
- S. mutans and S. sobrinus strongly associated with high-caries population
- Low-caries group: more diverse microbiota, non-mutans streptococci and Actinomyces dominant

### Treatment Outcomes

| Treatment | Efficacy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Community water fluoridation | 30% preventive fraction primary teeth (95% CI 11–48%); 12% permanent | US children/adolescents, 2018 estimate |
| Fluoride varnish (2–4×/year) | 18–43% reduction permanent caries; 37% reduction primary | 22-trial review, 12,455 children |
| SDF 38% | 66–75% arrest primary teeth; 41–65% first permanent molars; 18–71% root caries | Arrests progression; causes black staining |
| STAMP C16G2 | Significant S. mutans reduction in plaque and saliva; increased resting plaque pH | Phase II clinical trials (C3J Therapeutics) |
| Fluoride toothpaste | 23–36% caries reduction vs. placebo (71-student review) | Concentration-dependent |
| Fluoride mouthrinse | 27% reduction in permanent tooth surfaces (37 RCTs, >15,000 children) | School-based programs effective |

## Limitations

- Narrative review — no systematic search protocol, PRISMA, or meta-analysis; selection bias possible
- Species identification throughout history subject to reclassification (taxonomy updates, eHOMD version changes)
- Individual variability in caries microbiome is high; no universal microbiota defined
- Many cited treatment approaches (anti-caries vaccines, bacteriophages, probiotics) have not achieved approved clinical status
- Metatranscriptomic datasets for dentin caries have smaller sample sizes than coronal caries studies
