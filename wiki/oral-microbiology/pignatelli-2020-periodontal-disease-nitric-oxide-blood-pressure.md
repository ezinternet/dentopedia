## Three-line Summary

This narrative review explores the link between oral health, specifically the abundance of nitrate-reducing bacteria and periodontal disease, and systemic blood pressure (BP) via the nitric oxide (NO) pathway. Endogenous NO, a key vasodilator, is supplemented by the "nitrate-nitrite-NO pathway," where dietary nitrates (e.g., from vegetables) are reduced to nitrite by oral commensal bacteria on the tongue and then to NO in the stomach and circulation.

Disruption of this pathway by antiseptic mouthwashes (e.g., chlorhexidine) or tongue cleaning can reduce salivary nitrite production, leading to increased BP. Conversely, periodontal disease, characterized by pathogenic bacteria and inflammation, is associated with endothelial dysfunction and hypertension, although the specific role of nitrate-reducing bacteria in this relationship requires further study.

Restoring a healthy oral microbiome, potentially through probiotics or dietary nitrate supplementation, may offer a novel therapeutic strategy to enhance NO bioavailability and manage hypertension.

## Summary

This review synthesizes the current understanding of how oral bacteria, specifically nitrate-reducing commensals, influence systemic blood pressure through the nitrate-nitrite-NO pathway. It explains that dietary and endogenous nitrates are concentrated in saliva, where bacteria on the posterior tongue (e.g., Veillonella, Actinomyces, Haemophilus) reduce them to nitrite. This nitrite is then swallowed and further converted to NO in the acidic stomach, contributing to systemic NO bioavailability, which promotes vasodilation and lowers blood pressure. The paper highlights that antiseptic mouthwashes, especially those containing chlorhexidine, can disrupt this pathway, significantly reducing salivary nitrite and leading to increases in blood pressure (e.g., 2-3.5 mmHg). It also discusses the association between periodontal disease, characterized by dysbiosis and inflammation, and hypertension, mediated by endothelial dysfunction and reduced NO. The authors propose that restoring a healthy oral microbiome, for instance through dietary nitrate or probiotics, could be a promising therapeutic strategy for managing hypertension.

## Key Contributions

- Elucidates the critical role of the enterosalivary nitrate-nitrite-NO pathway in systemic blood pressure regulation.
- Identifies specific oral bacteria (Veillonella, Actinomyces, Haemophilus, Neisseria) as key mediators of this pathway.
- Highlights the negative impact of antiseptic mouthwashes (chlorhexidine) on NO production and blood pressure.
- Consolidates evidence linking periodontal disease and its associated pathogens to hypertension.
- Proposes the oral microbiome as a potential therapeutic target (e.g., via probiotics) for managing cardiovascular health.

## Methodology

This is a narrative review. The authors conducted a literature review to synthesize and discuss existing evidence on the interplay between dietary nitrate, the oral microbiome, the NO pathway, and its implications for blood pressure and cardiovascular health. The review integrates findings from in vitro studies, animal models, randomized controlled trials, and observational studies. It specifically focuses on the effects of antiseptic mouthwashes on the enterosalivary cycle and the association between periodontal pathogens and hypertension. The paper provides a comprehensive overview of the biochemistry of NO, the role of oral bacteria, and the clinical relevance to systemic disease.
