## Three-line Summary

*Treponema denticola* is an oral spirochete with a comprehensive suite of virulence factors — including the dentilisin protease complex, the major outer sheath protein MspA, flagella-driven motility, and immunomodulatory LPS — that enable tissue damage and immune evasion in subgingival biofilms. In animal co-infection models, *T. denticola* synergizes with *P. gingivalis* and *T. forsythia* (its red complex partners) to amplify alveolar bone resorption beyond what any single species causes alone. This 2011 narrative review consolidates molecular-level evidence and frames *T. denticola* virulence within the polymicrobial disease paradigm.

## Background

*Treponema denticola* is a gram-negative oral spirochete and one of three members of the red complex defined by [[oral-microbiology/socransky-1998-microbial-complexes-subgingival-plaque]], alongside *Porphyromonas gingivalis* and *Tannerella forsythia*. The red complex shows the strongest epidemiological association with severe chronic periodontitis (Chronic Periodontitis). Earlier work established the community-level association; Dashper 2011 moves the analysis to the molecular level, cataloguing the virulence determinants that explain *why* this spirochete is pathogenic.

The paper notes parallels between *T. denticola*-associated chronic periodontitis and other treponeme-caused chronic diseases (e.g., syphilis caused by *T. pallidum*), suggesting shared virulence strategies across the genus.

## Classic Virulence Determinants

### Dentilisin / CTLP (Chymotrypsin-Like Protease)

The major surface-associated protease complex of *T. denticola*, encoded by *prtP* (dentilisin) and associated proteolytic chaperones *prcA*-*prcB*. Key activities:
- Degrades host extracellular matrix proteins: fibronectin, fibrinogen, type IV collagen
- Cleaves and activates protease-activated receptors (PARs), triggering pro-inflammatory signaling
- Degrades insulin-like growth factor binding protein-3 (IGFBP-3), disrupting tissue repair
- Enables bacterial adhesion and invasion of the periodontal pocket epithelium

### MspA (Major Outer Sheath Protein)

An abundant outer membrane protein that:
- Forms voltage-dependent ion channels (porin-like activity)
- Is directly cytotoxic to human gingival fibroblasts and epithelial cells
- Facilitates nutrient uptake across the outer membrane

### Flagella and Motility (Movement — MeSH)

*T. denticola* possesses periplasmic flagella that confer characteristic corkscrew motility:
- Enables penetration of highly viscous gingival crevicular fluid (GCF) and dense biofilm matrices
- Motility is required for colonization of the subgingival pocket and tissue invasion

### Lipopolysaccharide (LPS)

*T. denticola* LPS has an atypical lipid A structure that differs from canonical *E. coli* LPS:
- Weaker pyrogenic and endotoxic activity compared to enteric LPS
- Retains immunomodulatory activity: induces pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α) at lower potency
- May contribute to chronic, dysregulated inflammation characteristic of periodontitis rather than acute responses

## Non-Classic Virulence Determinants

### Polymicrobial Synergy

A key theme of Dashper 2011 is that *T. denticola* virulence is not only about what it does alone, but how it interacts with co-infecting species:

- **Co-infection with *P. gingivalis***: animal models demonstrate synergistic enhancement of alveolar bone resorption beyond additive effects — directly supporting the community virulence concept that underpins the PSD (Polymicrobial Synergy and Dysbiosis) model described in [[oral-microbiology/hajishengallis-2012-psd-model-periodontal-disease]]
- **Red complex synergy**: interactions with *T. forsythia* further amplify periodontal tissue destruction

### Bacterial Secretion Systems

MeSH annotation indicates the review covers bacterial secretion systems as virulence delivery mechanisms — likely outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) and contact-dependent effector secretion.

### Immune Evasion (Immune Evasion — MeSH)

*T. denticola* subverts host immunity through multiple mechanisms:
- **Complement evasion**: resists complement-mediated killing
- **Neutrophil impairment**: inhibits oxidative burst and phagocytosis by neutrophils (PMNs)
- **Immunomodulation**: alters cytokine environment to favour chronic inflammation over bacterial clearance

## Key Results

1. *T. denticola* co-infection with *P. gingivalis* enhances alveolar bone resorption in animal models — quantified evidence cited, but values not available from abstract.
2. Dentilisin and MspA are identified as the primary classic virulence determinants with the best-documented molecular mechanisms.
3. Non-classic (community-level) virulence determinants — inter-species interactions and immune evasion — are framed as equally important to disease progression.
4. Molecular methodological advances (genome sequencing, transcriptomics) have substantially improved mechanistic understanding since earlier culture-based characterizations.

## Clinical Implications

- **Detection as disease marker**: high *T. denticola* abundance at a subgingival site correlates with active disease — microbiological monitoring has clinical utility
- **Treatment targeting**: *T. denticola* virulence is amplified in the red complex biofilm; disrupting the polymicrobial community (subgingival debridement, antiseptic adjuncts) is more effective than strategies targeting any single pathogen
- **Anti-virulence strategies**: dentilisin and MspA are rational targets for antibody-based or small-molecule inhibitors
- **Systemic disease parallels**: treponeme virulence parallels (noted in the review) may have implications for understanding *T. denticola* in systemic disease associations (cardiovascular disease, adverse pregnancy outcomes)
