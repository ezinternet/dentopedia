## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Nature Reviews Microbiology, 2024) synthesizing recent advances in human oral microbiome diversity (>700 bacterial species across 7 phyla, candidate phyla radiation/CPR bacteria, microeukaryotes, archaea, viruses) and species-level biogeography via CLASI-FISH microscopy.

Site-specific spatial organization (e.g., Streptococcus salivarius/mutans/sanguinis occupying distinct buccal/plaque/tongue niches; S. mutans "rotund" biofilm architecture driving localized demineralization) links microbial community structure directly to caries, periodontal disease, oral cancer, and systemic diseases (Alzheimer disease, cardiovascular disease, colorectal cancer, rheumatoid arthritis, IBD) via bacterial translocation and immunomodulation.

Clinical implication: oral disease pathogenesis (caries, periodontitis) is increasingly understood as ecological/network-level dysbiosis rather than single-pathogen infection, supporting prebiotic/probiotic strategies (e.g., nitrate-reducing bacteria) over single-target antimicrobial approaches; limitation is that most systemic-disease mechanistic links remain animal-model-based with high human inter-individual variability.

Nature Reviews Microbiology 2024 내러티브 리뷰로, 인체 구강 미생물군집 다양성(700종 이상, 7개 문, 후보문방사선군(Candidate Phyla Radiation, CPR) 세균 포함 진핵미생물·고세균·바이러스)과 CLASI-FISH 현미경 기반 종 수준 생물지리학(biogeography)을 종합.

부위별 미생물 공간구조(예: S. salivarius/mutans/sanguinis가 각각 협측점막/치태/설배부에 특이적으로 분포, S. mutans의 "rotund" 생물막 구조가 국소 탈회를 유발)가 우식·치주질환·구강암 및 전신질환(알츠하이머병, 심혈관질환, 대장암, 류마티스관절염, 염증성장질환)과 세균 전위(translocation)·면역조절 기전으로 직접 연결됨.

임상적 의미: 우식·치주염 발병기전은 단일 병원체 감염이 아닌 생태학적·네트워크 수준 이상증(dysbiosis)으로 이해되어 프리바이오틱/프로바이오틱 전략(예: 질산염 환원균)을 뒷받침하나, 전신질환 연관 기전의 상당수는 동물모델 근거이며 인체 개인차가 크다는 한계가 있음.

## Summary

This 2024 Nature Reviews Microbiology narrative review by Baker, Mark Welch, Kauffman, McLean and He surveys the state of oral microbiome research, organized around three themes: (1) microbial diversity beyond canonical bacteria, (2) species-level spatial organization (biogeography) of oral biofilms, and (3) the role of the oral microbiota in oral and systemic disease. It functions as an updated successor to earlier Nature Reviews Microbiology syntheses of the oral microbiome (e.g., Lamont, Koo & Hajishengallis 2018), incorporating advances from CLASI-FISH microscopy, long-read metagenomic sequencing, and single-cell RNA-seq of host tissue.

## Key Contributions

- **Beyond-bacteria diversity**: documents candidate phyla radiation (CPR) bacteria — an ultrasmall, often epiparasitic group (Saccharibacteria/TM7, Absconditabacteria/SR1, Gracilibacteria/GN02) representing >25% of global bacterial diversity — plus oral fungi (Candida, Malassezia), archaea (Methanobrevibacter oralis), and a surprisingly large phage population (>60,000 species-level groups).
- **Species-level biogeography**: CLASI-FISH imaging reveals that oral biofilms are not randomly assembled but tightly spatially organized — e.g., a Corynebacterium filamentous scaffold with Streptococcus "corncobs" at the tips — challenging the older Fusobacterium-nucleatum-bridge model of plaque succession. Metagenomic resolution at the species level shows Streptococcus salivarius, S. mutans, and S. sanguinis occupy distinct niches (buccal mucosa, dental plaque, tongue dorsum, respectively).
- **Mechanistic caries link**: S. mutans in a densely packed "rotund" biofilm architecture generates a locally acidic microenvironment causing markedly greater enamel demineralization than dispersed S. mutans — directly tying spatial organization to caries initiation.
- **Systemic disease mechanisms**: details specific molecular pathways for oral-systemic links, including F. nucleatum Fap2/FadA-driven colorectal tumour progression and TIGIT/CEACAM1 immune evasion, and P. gingivalis gingipain-mediated tau protein degradation implicated in Alzheimer disease.
- **Experimental gingivitis phenotypes**: identifies a third ("slow") host inflammatory responder phenotype in addition to previously described high/low responders, with distinct microbial/host signatures — suggesting a route to personalized periodontal risk stratification.

## Methodology

Narrative (non-systematic) review synthesizing culture-independent metagenomic/metatranscriptomic sequencing, single-cell sequencing, CLASI-FISH microscopy, metaproteomic and metabolomic literature. No formal search strategy, PRISMA flow, or quality appraisal is reported — it is an expert synthesis by researchers from the Forsyth Institute, Oregon Health & Science University, J. Craig Venter Institute, UC San Diego, University of Washington, and University at Buffalo.

## Results

- Core oral bacteriome: >700 species across 7 phyla (Actinomycetota, Bacteroidota, Bacillota, Fusobacteriota, Pseudomonadota, Saccharibacteria, Spirochaetota).
- TM7x (oral Saccharibacteria): 200-300 nm ultrasmall cells, reduced genome, epiparasitic on Actinomyces odontolyticus; presence increased host survival under acid stress (arginine deiminase system) and, in a separate study, decreased Actinomyces-induced bone loss in a mouse periodontitis model.
- Streptococcus niche partitioning confirmed via short-read metagenomics: S. salivarius (buccal mucosa) / S. mutans (dental plaque) / S. sanguinis (tongue dorsum).
- Oral cancer: ~90% of US OSCC cases HPV-driven; >90% of nasopharyngeal carcinomas EBV-associated; Candida-rich dysbiotic mycobiome prevalent in OSCC, while higher Malassezia correlates with better OSCC survival (candidate prognostic biomarker).
- Systemic disease: P. gingivalis and F. nucleatum translocation/immunomodulation linked to Alzheimer disease, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, colorectal cancer, IBD, rheumatoid arthritis, NAFLD, and obesity — largely via animal-model mechanistic studies.
