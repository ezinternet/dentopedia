## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (12 human studies, molecular-method comparisons across saliva/subgingival plaque/mucosal swabs) testing whether e-cigarette users' oral microbiota differs from never-smokers and combustible-cigarette smokers.

Beta diversity (community structure) consistently differed between e-cigarette users and never-smokers, with sample-specific enrichment of Veillonella, Leptotrichia, and Fusobacterium; alpha diversity (richness) findings were inconsistent, and the overall vaping microbial profile partially overlapped with — but did not fully resemble — either smokers or never-smokers.

Certainty of evidence is GRADE very low (mostly cross-sectional, heterogeneous methods), so this is a hypothesis-generating dysbiosis signal, not a settled comparative-harm verdict for vaping vs smoking; the review's funding disclosures (see Limitations) also warrant reading its framing with appropriate caution.

## Summary
First systematic synthesis isolating e-cigarette (vaping) exposure's specific effect on oral microbiota, separate from combustible smoking exposure. Twelve human molecular-method studies (mostly cross-sectional) were pooled narratively (GRADE certainty too low for formal meta-analysis of most outcomes). The central finding — distinct but intermediate microbial community structure in e-cigarette users — positions vaping as microbiologically non-neutral, though far short of establishing clinical harm equivalence with smoking.

## Key Contributions
- First SR to specifically isolate vaping's oral microbiome signature from combustible smoking.
- Identifies beta-diversity as the more robust/consistent metric than alpha-diversity for detecting tobacco/vaping-related oral dysbiosis across heterogeneous study designs.
- Documents specific methodological reporting gaps (DNA extraction protocol, 16S variable region, batch-effect and negative-control reporting) as a concrete research-quality agenda for the field.

## Methodology
PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science searched 27 August 2025. Human studies using molecular methods (predominantly 16S rRNA sequencing) comparing oral microbiota (saliva, subgingival plaque, oral mucosal swabs) among e-cigarette users, never-smokers, and current smokers. Risk of bias: JBI tools; evidence certainty: GRADE. 12 studies included.

## Results
[근거매우약함, GRADE very low] Beta diversity: consistently distinct communities in e-cigarette users vs never-smokers. Alpha diversity: inconsistent across studies/samples. Taxonomic shifts heterogeneous and sample-dependent; enrichment of Veillonella, Leptotrichia, Fusobacterium noted vs never-smokers in some studies. Overall vaping profile intermediate — overlapping with, but distinct from, both comparator groups.

## Limitations
- GRADE certainty very low for all outcomes; serious risk of bias (exposure measurement, confounder control, incomplete follow-up/attrition in cohort studies).
- Predominantly cross-sectional — cannot capture the oral microbiota's temporal dynamics relative to vaping duration/intensity.
- Small samples, incomplete group matching, uncontrolled device/nicotine-content heterogeneity across studies.
- Inconsistent reporting of DNA-extraction method, 16S variable region, sequencing platform, batch-effect control, and negative/blank controls — limits cross-study comparability.
- **Source-quality caveat**: one included primary study was funded by a research foundation affiliated with the state-owned China National Tobacco Corporation. The review's senior/corresponding author group discloses substantial funding and consulting relationships with tobacco-harm-reduction organizations (including one historically funded by Philip Morris International) and multiple industry consultancies — transparently disclosed in the paper, but a reason to cross-check this SR's framing against independent sources before using it to counsel patients on vaping vs smoking relative risk.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
Do not present this SR as evidence that vaping is "safer" for oral/periodontal health than smoking — the data only show a distinct, not necessarily benign, microbial signature, at very low certainty. For patients who vape, apply the same general oral-health monitoring rigor as for smokers pending better longitudinal evidence, and note that implant-specific outcome data for e-cigarette users remains essentially absent (see [[implants/survival/calciolari-2026-tobacco-smoking-smoke-free-products]]).
