## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 방추형 간균 (Fusobacterium nucleatum, F. nucleatum)은 5개 아종(nucleatum, polymorphum, vincentii, animalis, fusiforme)을 가지며, 공생균(commensal)과 병원체(pathobiont) 사이를 오가는 "공생-병원체 딜레마 (Commensal-Pathobiont Dilemma)"의 대표 사례다
> - **가교종 (Bridging Species)** 핵심 역할: 초기 정착 그람 양성균(Streptococcus 등)과 후기 치주 병원균(Porphyromonas gingivalis 등) 사이를 연결, 치주 생물막 성숙을 구조적으로 지원
> - **1차 독력인자**: 부착소 (Adhesin) FadA — E-cadherin 결합 → 숙주 세포 침투 → NF-κB 활성화 → 염증 유발; 스트레스 하에 아밀로이드 FadA 형성 → 생물막 강화 + 쥐 치주 골소실 유발
> - 표면 부착소 다양성: FomA, RadD, Fap2, Aid1, CmpA — 공격응집 (Coaggregation) 촉진, 생물막 형성 가속
> - **LPS → NLRP3 inflammasome → IL-1β → 골흡수** 경로; 65 kDa 세린 프로테아제 → 세포외기질(ECM) 분해 + IgA chain 절단(면역 회피)
> - **뷰티르산 (Butyric Acid) 이중 역할**: 저농도 = 항염 에너지원 + hBD-2/3 유도; 고농도 = 골아세포 내 ROS 생성 → 골소실
> - 상피-간엽 전환 (Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition, EMT): Snail-1 상향, E-cadherin 하향 → 상피 장벽 붕괴 → 심부 조직 침투
> - 면역 미세환경: 중성구 초과산화물 억제 + 괴사/세포자멸사 유도 → 감염부 중성구 감소; IL-1β·IL-6·TNF-α·HMGB1 증가 → 대식세포 침윤 가속
> - 전신 연관: 조산(hematogenous 경로), 대장암(암 조직 내 농도 증가), 전신 감염 파종
> - 임상 takeaway: F. nucleatum은 건강 상태에서도 상재하지만 치주 환경 악화 시 병원체로 전환 — 치주 건강 관리가 전신 질환 예방과 직결됨을 시사

## Three-line Summary

**Background:** *Fusobacterium nucleatum* is a gram-negative oral anaerobe that acts as the canonical bridging species in subgingival biofilm, linking early gram-positive colonizers to late-arriving gram-negative periodontal pathogens.

**Key findings:** The FadA adhesin (conserved in oral strains, variable extraorally) binds E-cadherin to invade host cells and activate NF-κB; under stress it forms amyloid FadA that promotes biofilm and induces bone loss. Butyric acid plays a dual role — anti-inflammatory energy source at low concentrations but ROS-mediated osteoblast damage at elevated levels. LPS triggers NLRP3 → IL-1β; a serine protease cleaves IgA for immune evasion; F. nucleatum also induces EMT and suppresses neutrophil oxidative function.

**Implication:** F. nucleatum exemplifies the commensal-pathobiont dilemma: it maintains mucosal homeostasis (beta-defensin induction) under healthy conditions yet orchestrates multi-pathway periodontal destruction and systemic dissemination (preterm birth, colorectal cancer) when dysbiosis permits.

## 세줄요약

## Background

*Fusobacterium nucleatum* is a gram-negative, obligate anaerobe that colonizes the human oral cavity and digestive tract. Historically implicated as a periodontal pathogen, it has emerged as a keystone bridging species that structurally organizes the subgingival biofilm community. Recent evidence has extended its pathogenic associations beyond the oral cavity to colorectal cancer, preterm birth, and other systemic infections.

**Taxonomy:** Five subspecies — *F. nucleatum* subsp. nucleatum, polymorphum, vincentii, animalis, and fusiforme — each with a unique rRNA gene sequence. The genus name derives from Latin "fusus" (spindle) and "bacterion" (small rod), reflecting the organism's pointed, spindle-shaped morphology. Cells are typically slender rods occurring in pairs end-to-end.

## Metabolism & Culture

F. nucleatum is a fastidious anaerobe requiring nutrient-rich media. Primary energy sources are amino acids and peptides, with preference for glutamate, histidine, and aspartate. It ferments glutamate and lysine, yielding acetate and butyrate. Key metabolic byproducts:

- **Butyrate, propionate, ammonium ions**: inhibit gingival fibroblast proliferation → contributes to periodontal pathogenesis
- **Volatile sulfur compounds** (H₂S, methyl mercaptan): major halitosis contributors

## Commensal / Beneficial Role

Under healthy conditions, F. nucleatum participates in mucosal defense:

1. **Butyric acid production**: serves as an energy source for anti-inflammatory cells; promotes transcription of protease inhibitors released by neutrophils → supports mucosal tissue health
2. **Beta-defensin induction**: stimulates mucosal epithelial cells to produce human beta-defensin-2 (hBD-2) and, to a lesser extent, hBD-3 — effective against gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria and certain viruses

This dual role defines the commensal-pathobiont dilemma: the same metabolite (butyric acid) confers protection at physiological concentrations yet causes damage at elevated pathogenic concentrations.

## Bridging Species Function

F. nucleatum is the canonical bridging species in subgingival biofilm:

- **Links early colonizers** (gram-positive: Streptococcus spp.) with **late colonizers** (gram-negative: *P. gingivalis*, *T. forsythia*, *T. denticola*)
- Multiple surface adhesins mediate coaggregation: **FomA**, **RadD**, **Fap2**, **Aid1**, **CmpA**
- Outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) carry bioactive compounds for inter-bacterial communication and host–bacteria interaction

## Virulence Factors in Periodontal Disease

### FadA (Fusobacterium Adhesin A) — Primary Virulence Factor

FadA is the dominant virulence determinant of oral F. nucleatum:

| State | Form | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Active complex | Pre-FadA + mature FadA | Attaches to and invades host cells; binds E-cadherin |
| Stress/disease | Amyloid FadA | Biofilm reinforcement; acid tolerance; bone loss in rat models |

- FadA is **highly conserved** in oral F. nucleatum but **variable** in extraoral strains → potential diagnostic marker for oral F. nucleatum
- E-cadherin binding → disruption of cell adhesion and intercellular communication
- Interacts with retinoic acid-inducible gene I (RIG-I) receptor → **NF-κB pathway** activation → pro-inflammatory cytokine cascade

### LPS (Lipopolysaccharide)

Released upon bacterial death; recognized by TLRs on gingival epithelial cells and fibroblasts → intracellular danger signal → **NLRP3 inflammasome** activation → **IL-1β** secretion → alveolar bone resorption.

### 65 kDa Serine Protease

- Degrades ECM → provides essential nutrients for bacterial survival
- Cleaves IgA heavy chain → immune evasion

### Butyric Acid (Dual Role)

| Concentration | Effect |
|---|---|
| Physiological (low) | Energy source for anti-inflammatory cells; protease inhibitor induction |
| Elevated | ROS generation in osteoblasts → impaired bone formation |

## Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT)

F. nucleatum, together with other gram-negative periodontal pathogens, induces EMT in gingival epithelial cells:

- **Snail-1** upregulation
- **E-cadherin** downregulation → loss of epithelial cell–cell adhesion
- Net effect: disruption of the epithelial barrier → deeper tissue invasion

## Immune Microenvironment

F. nucleatum employs multiple immune evasion strategies:

1. **Neutrophil superoxide suppression**: subspecies nucleatum and animalis inhibit superoxide production → prevents neutrophil oxidative death at infection site
2. **Neutrophil depletion**: induces both necrosis and apoptosis → reduces immune cell numbers locally
3. **Pro-inflammatory cytokine surge**: increases IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, HMGB1 → promotes macrophage infiltration → amplifies gingival inflammation

## Systemic Associations

| Condition | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Preterm birth | Hematogenous transmission from maternal oral cavity to intrauterine environment |
| Colorectal cancer | Higher F. nucleatum concentrations in carcinomas vs. adjacent normal tissue |
| Systemic infections | Bacteremia from periodontal sources → dissemination to oropharynx and distant sites |

> **Note on Systemic Links:** This paper reviews associations; for mechanistic F. nucleatum–cancer evidence, see [[oral-microbiology/mcilvanna-2021-fusobacterium-nucleatum-oral-cancer]].

## Clinical Implications

- F. nucleatum occupies a commensal niche in healthy individuals but transitions to pathobiont when microbial balance is disrupted
- FadA conservation in oral strains may allow strain-level diagnostic differentiation
- Therapeutic targeting of F. nucleatum may require addressing multiple virulence pathways simultaneously (FadA, LPS-NLRP3, butyric acid accumulation)
- Periodontal infection control has implications beyond oral health — preterm birth prevention and potentially colorectal cancer risk reduction
