## Summary

This PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42024625706) systematic review and meta-analysis compared prefabricated zirconia crowns (PZCs) against resin-bonded composite strip crowns (RCSCs) for restoring carious primary anterior teeth in children. Nine RCTs (1746 teeth, children aged 3–6 years, from Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Canada, India, Egypt, and UAE) were pooled using risk ratios across eight outcomes spanning treatment effectiveness and adverse effects. PZCs demonstrated significantly better performance on secondary caries prevention, crown retention, restoration failure, and plaque accumulation, consistent with the material's superior wear resistance and highly polished surface that resists biofilm accumulation. RCSCs, however, caused significantly less wear of opposing teeth — a clinically important trade-off given zirconia's much higher intrinsic hardness (1000–1400 HV) versus enamel (300–400 HV) and composite resin (50–80 HV). No significant differences were found for color mismatch, gingival bleeding, or marginal fit, though these were based on fewer studies. The authors frame the choice as depending on individual patient factors — caries risk, esthetic demand, longevity needs, cost, and operator skill — rather than a uniform preference for one material.

## Key Contributions

- First meta-analysis to directly quantify PZC vs RCSC (rather than PZC vs stainless steel crown) specifically in **primary anterior teeth**.
- Provides pooled RR estimates across 8 clinically relevant outcomes with heterogeneity assessment and sensitivity analysis.
- Surfaces a wear-resistance/hardness trade-off: PZC superior on nearly all durability/hygiene outcomes but significantly worse for opposing-tooth wear (RR=7.58), reframing "PZC is simply better" into a genuine risk-benefit decision.
- Flags publication bias specific to the crown-retention outcome (Egger's p=0.041) — the PZC retention advantage should be treated as an upper-bound estimate, not a precise point estimate.
- Identifies a field-wide reporting gap: no study stratified results by anterior tooth position (central vs lateral incisor), so pooled effects represent an averaged "anterior cohort" rather than position-specific guidance.

## Methodology

PRISMA 2020-guided systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024625706). Searches of PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science to 2025-06-21, English-language only, supplemented by manual reference and grey-literature search. Eligibility: RCTs comparing PZC vs RCSC in children <13 years with primary anterior teeth requiring crown restoration; outcomes required at least one of marginal fit, restoration failure, wear of opposing teeth, secondary caries, plaque index, crown retention, gingival bleeding, or color mismatch. Risk of bias assessed via Cochrane RoB 2.0 by two independent reviewers with third-reviewer arbitration. Statistical pooling (Stata 16) used risk ratios with 95% CI; fixed-effects (inverse-variance) model when I²≤50% and p≥0.10, otherwise random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird). Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis performed for outcomes with >3 studies; funnel plots and Egger's test for outcomes with ≥5 studies, with trim-and-fill correction where bias detected. From 3760 initial records (1630 duplicates removed, 2112 excluded at full-text), 9 RCTs (1746 teeth) were included; 8/9 rated low overall risk of bias, 1/9 high risk (unclear randomization).

## Results

- **Secondary caries**: 5 studies, 636 teeth, fixed-effects, RR=0.41 (95% CI 0.18–0.92), p=0.030 — favors PZC.
- **Crown retention**: 5 studies, 820 teeth, fixed-effects, RR=0.15 (95% CI 0.07–0.33), p<0.001 — favors PZC (but publication bias detected, Egger's p=0.041; treat as upper-bound).
- **Restoration failure**: 3 studies, 241 teeth, fixed-effects, RR=0.07 (95% CI 0.02–0.22), p<0.001 — favors PZC.
- **Wear of opposing teeth**: 4 studies, 652 teeth, fixed-effects, RR=7.58 (95% CI 2.73–21.07), p<0.001 — favors RCSC (PZC causes more antagonist wear).
- **Plaque occurrence**: 2 studies, 358 teeth, fixed-effects, RR=0.08 (95% CI 0.01–0.23), p<0.001 — favors PZC.
- **Color mismatch**: 4 studies, 734 teeth, random-effects, RR=0.24 (95% CI 0.05–1.21), p=0.084 — no significant difference.
- **Gingival bleeding**: 3 studies, 446 teeth, fixed-effects, RR=0.09 (95% CI 0.01–1.73), p=0.109 — no significant difference.
- **Marginal fit**: 2 studies, 286 teeth, random-effects, RR=0.50 (95% CI 0.05–8.70), p=0.672 — no significant difference.

Sensitivity analyses (leave-one-out) showed no material change to effect estimates. The wear-of-opposing-teeth finding is explained by zirconia's substantially higher hardness (1000–1400 HV) versus enamel (300–400 HV), though the authors note surface polish — not bulk hardness alone — appears to be the decisive factor, citing a separate 2-year trial where highly polished 5Y-TZP crowns showed no excess enamel wear versus natural antagonists.
