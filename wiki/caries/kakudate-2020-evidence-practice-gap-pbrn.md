## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional web survey of 297 JDPBRN-affiliated Japanese dentists (206 responded, 69%; Jan–Nov 2017) using 10 clinical questions adapted from the US National Dental PBRN's Practice Impact Questionnaire, each response coded concordant or not with published minimal intervention dentistry (MID) evidence.

Mean and median overall concordance were both 60% (SD 18, IQR 50–75%); area means were primary caries 68%, deep caries 53%, restoration 54%; lowest items were use of magnification 33%, repair of defective composite with cementum-dentinal margins 48%, and caries risk assessment 51%.

Multiple logistic regression (n=197): female gender OR 2.33 (95% CI 1.01–5.39), clinic in a government-ordinance-designated city OR 2.01 (1.02–3.96), and frequent use of English-language scientific journal articles OR 2.45 (1.08–5.59) were associated with higher-than-median concordance (predictive accuracy 72.6%).

## Summary

Japanese dentists demonstrated medium (60%) concordance with published MID evidence, confirming that an evidence-practice gap (EPG) exists in Japanese dental practice nearly two decades after the MID concept was defined. The gap is not uniform: non-invasive management of frank occlusal discoloration is near-universal (82–97%), while behaviors requiring individualized judgment or new equipment/workflow lag badly — caries risk assessment (51%), magnification use (33%), indirect pulp capping for deep caries (52–54%), repair rather than replacement of defective composites (48–60%), and waiting for proximal lesions to reach dentin before restoring (53%). Compared with US National Dental PBRN practitioners (62% mean), Japanese dentists were similar overall but markedly lower on caries risk assessment (51% vs 85%), possibly because individual CRA is not reimbursed by Japanese National Health Insurance. The only evidence-source variable associated with higher concordance was frequent reading of English-language scientific journals — used regularly by only 32% of respondents.

## Key Contributions

- First quantification of the EPG for MID as a whole concept (10 caries-related decisions) in Japan, using the US National Dental PBRN instrument for direct international comparability.
- Item-level benchmark table exposing exactly which MID behaviors have disseminated (non-invasive occlusal management) and which have not (CRA, magnification, selective deep-caries excavation, repair-vs-replace).
- Identifies dentist-level predictors of adherence; the actionable one — English-language journal use (OR 2.45) — motivates interventions that lower language/access barriers to primary literature.

## Methodology

- Cross-sectional web questionnaire, January–November 2017; 297 JDPBRN dentists invited across Japan's seven regions, 206 analyzed (69% response).
- 10 questions from the 12-item Practice Impact Questionnaire (third molar extraction and defective amalgam excluded — amalgam no longer a Japanese insurance service); responses coded consistent/inconsistent with published evidence.
- Overall concordance = % consistent; dichotomized at the median (60%) for multiple logistic regression (linear and ordinal-tercile sensitivity analyses gave similar conclusions); SPSS 23, α=0.05.

## Results

- Overall concordance: mean = median = 60% (SD 18, IQR 50–75%); areas: primary caries 68%, deep caries 53%, restoration 54%.
- Highest items: occlusal 1 non-invasive treatment 97%, occlusal 2 82%, air drying 77%. Lowest: magnification 33%, cementum-dentinal margin repair 48%, CRA 51%, proximal restoration threshold 53%, deep caries options 52–54%.
- Regression (n=197): female OR 2.33 (1.01–5.39, p=0.048); designated city OR 2.01 (1.02–3.96, p=0.043); English journals OR 2.45 (1.08–5.59, p=0.033); predictive accuracy 72.6%; all other variables non-significant.
- Benchmarks: US practitioners 62% (2009–2010); VCU faculty 75%/students 67%; Japan vs US gaps on CRA (51% vs 85%), magnification (33% vs 57%), cementum-dentinal margin repair (48% vs 67%).
