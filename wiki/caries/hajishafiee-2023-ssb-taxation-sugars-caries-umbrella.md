## Three-line Summary
Umbrella review (JBI methodology, PROSPERO-registered CRD42022293187) of 14 systematic reviews (SSB tax → consumption/sugars, PRISMA: 419 identified → 48 screened → 14 included) and 5 systematic reviews (sugars → dental caries dose-response, 100 → 21 → 5), commissioned for WHO's Global Oral Health Strategy, covering both HIC and LMIC.

A 20% volumetric SSB tax is modelled to cut free sugars intake by 20.0% (HIC) / 18.0% (LMIC) (≥4.0 g/day per capita, up to 4.4 g/day HIC), preventing 0.03 carious teeth per adult and reducing child caries-surface counts by 0.18 (HIC) / 0.16 (LMIC) and caries occurrence by 2.9% (HIC) / 2.7% (LMIC) over 10 years.

Individual-level caries benefit is modest and LMIC estimates likely lean conservative (dose-response coefficients came only from HIC cohorts), but population-level impact is judged meaningful enough to support including SSB taxation in WHO oral health action plans.

## Summary
Hajishafiee et al. conducted the first umbrella review jointly assessing how SSB taxation affects dental caries in both HIC and LMIC, commissioned to inform WHO's Global Strategy on Oral Health. Two separate searches were run: Q1/Q2 (SSB tax → consumption/sugars) identified 419 systematic reviews, of which 14 were included after screening (48 retained for full-text review); Q3 (sugars → caries dose-response) identified 100 systematic reviews, of which 5 were included (2 via screening, 3 via expert contact, from 21 retained for full-text review). The best available evidence (high-AMSTAR SR by Teng et al.) showed a 10% SSB tax reduces consumption by 10.0% (95% CI: −5.0, 14.7%) in HIC; the best LMIC synthesis (Nakhimovsky et al.) showed a 9.0% average reduction. A 20% volumetric tax was modelled to reduce free sugars intake by 20.0% in HIC / 18.0% in LMIC (≥4.0 g/day, up to 4.4 g/day HIC). Applying dose-response coefficients from three original cohort studies (Bernabé et al. for adults; Turck et al. and the Michigan Study for children's primary and permanent dentitions, respectively), this translates over 10 years to 0.03 fewer carious teeth per adult and, in children, a 0.16–0.18 reduction in caries-surface count and a 2.7–2.9% reduction in caries occurrence (LMIC/HIC). The conclusion is that SSB taxation alone produces a modest but real population-level caries benefit in both income settings, supporting its inclusion in the WHO oral health action plan — though LMIC estimates likely understate the true effect, since no LMIC dose-response data exists.

## Key Contributions
- First umbrella review quantifying SSB-tax impact on dental caries in both HIC and LMIC within a unified modelling framework.
- PED estimate (HIC, high-AMSTAR, Teng et al.): 10% tax → 10.0% SSB consumption reduction (95% CI: −5.0, 14.7%).
- PED estimate (LMIC, Nakhimovsky et al.): 10% tax → 9.0% average reduction (range 6–12%); a second pooled estimate spanning HIC+LMIC (Cabrera Escobar et al., USA/Mexico/Brazil/France) gives 10% tax → 13.0% average reduction.
- Tax-design comparison (one SR): a nutrient-based (sugars-threshold) tax was the most effective design (avg 14.0% reduction at a 10% tax level), ahead of a volumetric tax (10.2%) and an ad valorem-style tax (2.3%, not statistically significant).
- 20% volumetric tax → sugars intake reduction of 20.0% (HIC) / 18.0% (LMIC), equivalent to ≥4.0 g/day (4.4 g/day HIC, 4.0 g/day LMIC).
- Modelled 10-year caries impact: 0.03 fewer carious teeth per adult (HIC and LMIC); in children, caries-surface count ↓0.18 (HIC)/0.16 (LMIC) and caries occurrence ↓2.9% (HIC)/2.7% (LMIC).

## Methodology
Umbrella review of systematic reviews; JBI methodology; PROSPERO-registered (CRD42022293187). Databases: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, CINAHL, Dentistry and Oral Sciences Source, Cochrane Library, JBI SR Register, PROSPERO; 2000–end 2021, no language restriction. Screening/extraction/AMSTAR appraisal done independently in duplicate. Quality assessment: AMSTAR (11-point; 8–11 high, 4–7 moderate, 0–3 low). PRISMA flow — Q1/Q2: 419 identified → 48 title/abstract retained → 14 included (4 high-AMSTAR, 9 moderate, 1 low). Q3: 100 identified → 21 retained → 5 included (2 high-AMSTAR, 3 moderate). Modelling: PED-derived sugars reduction (from Q1/Q2) combined with dose-response coefficients from three original cohort studies identified within the Q3 SRs (no meta-analysis available for Q3) → estimated caries prevented over 10 years, computed separately for HIC and LMIC.

## Results
- Primary (SSB → consumption, HIC): 10% tax → 10.0% reduction (95% CI: −5.0, 14.7%; PED −1.0; high-AMSTAR Teng et al.). Other HIC estimates: Afshin et al. 7.0% (US only, high AMSTAR); Powell et al. 7.1–22.6% (avg 12.1%, moderate AMSTAR, US); Andreyeva et al. 8–10% (moderate AMSTAR, US).
- LMIC: 9.0% average reduction (range 6–12%; Nakhimovsky et al., moderate AMSTAR). Pooled HIC+LMIC estimate (Cabrera Escobar et al.): 13.0% average.
- By tax design (10% level, single SR): nutrient-based 14.0% > volumetric 10.2% > ad valorem-style 2.3% (NS) — consistent with separate WHO guidance favoring nutrient-based taxes.
- SES: taxes more regressive (larger effect) in lower-SES groups in 3 of 4 SES-stratified reviews (Maniadakis, Thow, Nakhimovsky); Teng et al. found no SES difference in HIC.
- Sugars reduction: 20% tax → 4.4 g/day (HIC) / 4.0 g/day (LMIC) average, range 1.8–11.0 g/day across 9 original studies; equivalent to a 20.0% (HIC) / 18.0% (LMIC) reduction in sugars intake.
- Dose-response inputs (Q3, no meta-analysis; best-available cohort data): adults — Bernabé et al., DMFT +0.09 per 10 g/day sugars over 11 years; children primary dentition — Turck et al., dmft +1.64 per 10 g/day sucrose (ages 3–6); children permanent dentition — the Michigan Study, +1% caries per 5 g sugar over 3 years.
- Caries prevented (10 years, 20% tax): 0.03 carious teeth/adult (HIC and LMIC); children caries-surface count ↓0.18 (HIC)/0.16 (LMIC); caries occurrence ↓2.9% (HIC)/2.7% (LMIC).
- Overall: modest individual-level effect; population-level benefit judged non-trivial given global caries prevalence, though LMIC figures are likely conservative since dose-response data are HIC-only.
