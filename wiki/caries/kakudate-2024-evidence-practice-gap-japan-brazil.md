## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional web survey of 136 Japanese vs 110 Brazilian dentists (Dec 2021–Jun 2022) scoring concordance with MID evidence on 3 questions: restorative threshold for proximal caries, deep-caries excavation strategy, and caries risk assessment (CRA).

Brazil beat Japan on overall concordance (56% vs 38%, p=0.007); the only individually significant divergence was CRA (91% vs 68%, p<0.001), while deep-caries management leaned the other way (75% vs 81%, NS).

Adjusted logistic regression: frequent English-language journal reading predicted higher concordance in Japan (OR 2.44, 95% CI 1.13–5.29); frequent Internet use predicted lower concordance in Brazil (OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.16–0.92).

## Summary

This is the first study to administer the identical National Dental PBRN-derived EPG measurement tool to dentists in two countries concomitantly. Each dentist's answers on three MID scenarios were coded as consistent or inconsistent with published evidence; overall concordance required consistency on all three. Brazil outperformed Japan overall despite Japan's dense PBRN infrastructure — and the country-level difference concentrated almost entirely in whether dentists perform caries risk assessment at all, not in restorative thresholds or deep-caries philosophy, where both countries now sit above 60%.

The most actionable finding is about *where dentists get their evidence*. Japanese practice skews toward textbooks, non-academic journals, and domestic seminars; Brazilian dentists read English-language scientific journals at nearly double the Japanese rate (82% vs 49%). The authors attribute Japan's lag to a "Language Gap" — the US Foreign Service Institute rates Japanese-to-English acquisition at ~2,200 class hours (Category IV) versus 600–750 for Portuguese (Category I). Conversely, Brazilian dentists leaning on Internet information had *worse* concordance, consistent with prior findings of poor-quality online dental information in Brazil.

The paper also documents secular improvement: CRA uptake rose from 26% → 68% in Japan (2011→2021) and 36% → 91% in Brazil (2014→2021), suggesting EPGs are measurable and movable over time with accumulating evidence and guideline activity.

## Key Contributions

- **Direction-setting result**: Japan has the larger MID evidence-practice gap (overall concordance 38% vs Brazil's 56%, p=0.007).
- **Localizes the gap**: the divergence is driven by caries risk assessment (23-point gap), not restoration thresholds (Q1, NS) or deep-caries excavation (Q2, NS).
- **Information-source epidemiology**: quantifies which sources dominate each country and which are significantly associated with better/worst concordance after adjustment.
- **Two named mechanisms**: Language Gap (Japan) and unreliable-Internet-information problem (Brazil) — both policy-actionable.
- **Longitudinal benchmark**: repeatable instrument showing +42pp (JP) / +55pp (BR) CRA growth over a decade.

## Methodology

- Cross-sectional web survey fielded Dec 2021 – Jun 2022; Japanese/Portuguese versions (English reference public on dentalpbrn.jp).
- Recruitment: JDPBRN sites/e-mail across 7 Japanese regions (response rate 74%; n=136) and e-mail/social networks across 5 Brazilian regions (n=110; denominator indeterminate).
- Instrument (modified from Norton et al. 2014): Q1 proximal lesion depth warranting restoration (consistent = into-dentin threshold); Q2 management when caries excavation approaches the mesio-buccal pulp horn (consistent = stop near pulp, remove elsewhere; inconsistent = remove all decay or start endodontics); Q3 any caries risk assessment performed.
- Coding 1/0 per question; primary outcome = all three consistent. Characteristics: graduation year, gender, practice type, busyness, 8 information-source items dichotomized higher/lower frequency.
- Analysis: chi-square (country comparisons); country-stratified multiple logistic regression with adjusted ORs (95% CI); alpha 0.05; SPSS 23; available-case handling; sample sized via 1:10 events-per-variable rule (~100).

## Results

**Concordance (Table 3)**:

| Question | Japan (n=136) | Brazil (n=110) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 restoration threshold | 63% | 73% | 0.149 |
| Q2 deep caries | 81% | 75% | 0.299 |
| Q3 caries risk assessment | 68% | 91% | <0.001 |
| **Overall (all 3)** | **38% (51/136)** | **56% (61/110)** | **0.007** |

**Information sources (higher-frequency use)**: textbooks 92% JP vs 72% BR; non-academic journals 67% vs 21%; seminars/workshops 86% vs 58%; English scientific journals 49% vs 82% — all p<0.001. Colleague use identical (82%/82%); Internet similar (62%/59%, NS).

**Adjusted predictors of overall concordance**:
- Japan: English-language scientific journal articles OR 2.44 (95% CI 1.13–5.29), p=0.024. Textbook OR 7.18 but CI crossing unity (0.85–60.4).
- Brazil: Internet information sources OR 0.39 (95% CI 0.16–0.92), p=0.032.
- Model accuracy 64.7% (JP) / 68.3% (BR); gender, busyness, practice type, seniority not significant.

**Historical trend**: CRA performance Japan 26% (2011–12) → 68% (this study); Brazil 36% (2014–15) → 91%.
