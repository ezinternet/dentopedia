## Three-line Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis compared topical fluoride-antibacterial agent combined therapy versus fluoride monotherapy for preventing dental caries in children (1-16 years), including 16 RCTs from 3475 screened records. Combined therapy showed superior caries-preventive effectiveness (SMD = -0.12, 95% CI: -0.2 to -0.04, p = 0.004, I² = 20%). No significant difference was found for post-intervention salivary S. mutans count (SMD = -0.11, 95% CI: -0.33 to 0.10, p = 0.30, I² = 0%).

Fluoride-antibacterial agent combined therapy offers added benefit over fluoride monotherapy for caries prevention in children, though evidence is low certainty.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis compared the caries-preventive effectiveness of topical fluoride-antibacterial agent combined therapy versus fluoride monotherapy in children aged 1-16 years. Sixteen randomized controlled trials were included in qualitative synthesis, with nine in quantitative synthesis. Combined therapy showed superior caries-preventive effectiveness (SMD = -0.12, 95% CI: -0.20 to -0.04, p = 0.004) with low heterogeneity (I² = 20%). No significant difference was found for post-intervention salivary S. mutans count (SMD = -0.11, 95% CI: -0.33 to 0.10, p = 0.30, I² = 0%). The result was driven primarily by xylitol studies. The authors concluded that combined therapy offers an added benefit, but the evidence is low certainty and should be interpreted with caution.

## Key Contributions

- Combined fluoride-antibacterial therapy superior to fluoride alone for caries prevention (SMD = -0.12)
- No difference in salivary S. mutans counts between groups (SMD = -0.11, p = 0.30)
- Xylitol studies primarily drove the significant result
- Antibacterial agents included: PVP-I, CHX, xylitol, triclosan, CPC
- Low certainty evidence; good quality trials needed
- Potential benefit for high-risk children

## Methodology

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed PRISMA guidelines. A comprehensive search was conducted in PubMed, EbscoHost, Scopus, Web of Science, and Cochrane Central. Eligible studies were RCTs comparing topical fluoride-antibacterial agent combined therapy versus fluoride monotherapy in children 1-16 years. Sixteen trials were included in qualitative synthesis; nine in quantitative synthesis (meta-analysis). Risk of bias was assessed using Cochrane RoB tool. Meta-analysis used Inverse Variance test in Review Manager 5.4 with SMD and 95% CI. Heterogeneity was assessed with I². GRADE was used for evidence certainty (low).
