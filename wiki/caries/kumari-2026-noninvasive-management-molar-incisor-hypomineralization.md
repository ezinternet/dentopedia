## Summary
> [!note] Protocol — No Results Yet
> This is a Cochrane Protocol (CD016369). The full review is currently in progress. This page will be updated when results are published.

This Cochrane protocol addresses the management of molar-incisor hypomineralization (MIH), a developmental enamel defect affecting first permanent molars and sometimes incisors, characterised by demarcated opacities and enamel breakdown. MIH affects approximately 14% of children worldwide and is a significant clinical challenge because the hypomineralised enamel is structurally weak, hypersensitive, and prone to rapid caries progression. This protocol focuses specifically on non-invasive approaches — fluoride products, CPP-ACP, SDF, sealants, resin infiltration, and desensitising agents — as opposed to invasive management (crowns, extractions). The 6–14 year age window represents the critical period when first permanent molars erupt and the clinical window for non-invasive intervention.

## Key Contributions
- First Cochrane protocol specifically targeting non-invasive MIH management
- Population: children 6–14 years — optimal clinical window for first permanent molar management
- Interventions: fluoride varnish/gel/foam/toothpaste; CPP-ACP (Tooth Mousse); SDF; fissure sealants; resin infiltration; desensitising agents
- Will provide the first high-level evidence synthesis to guide clinical decisions in a condition currently managed by expert opinion and consensus
- Complements the existing SDF review (Worthington 2024) for the specific MIH subpopulation

## Methodology
- Design: Cochrane SR of RCTs
- Population: children 6–14 years with MIH (≥1 first permanent molar affected)
- Interventions: non-invasive agents (fluoride, CPP-ACP, SDF, sealants, resin infiltration, desensitising agents)
- Outcomes: caries increment, sensitivity reduction, enamel breakdown prevention, patient-reported outcomes
- Status: awaiting RCT identification and data extraction
