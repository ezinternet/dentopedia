## Summary
This Cochrane SR evaluated whether topical fluoride products — toothpaste, gel, varnish, mouthrinse — cause dental fluorosis in children. Forty-three studies were included (3 RCTs, 4 cohort, 10 case-control, 26 cross-sectional) with evidence to July 2022. The review examined timing of first toothpaste use, toothpaste concentration, brushing frequency, fluoride gel/varnish applications, mouthrinse use, and swallowing patterns. All evidence was judged very low certainty due to the observational nature of most studies, inconsistency across studies, and risk of bias. The review cannot determine which specific topical fluoride practices increase fluorosis risk, nor what threshold (dose/timing) is clinically relevant. This leaves paediatric fluoride recommendations largely unsupported by high-quality evidence.

## Key Contributions
- Cannot determine which topical fluoride practices increase fluorosis risk — insufficient evidence
- Early introduction of toothpaste, high-concentration toothpaste, gel/varnish applications, and fluoride mouthrinse all studied but no conclusions possible
- Only 3 RCTs in the entire evidence base — overwhelmingly observational
- Critical evidence gap: dental fluorosis develops during tooth formation (ages 0–6 for incisors), requiring long-term follow-up that RCTs have not provided
- Publication covers evidence through July 2022

## Methodology
- 43 studies: 3 RCTs, 4 cohort, 10 case-control, 26 cross-sectional; evidence to July 2022
- Population: children under risk period for dental fluorosis development
- Exposures: age at first toothpaste use, toothpaste fluoride concentration, brushing frequency/supervision, fluoride gel, fluoride varnish, fluoride mouthrinse
- Outcome: dental fluorosis (various indices: Dean's, TF, TSIF)
- Risk of bias: Cochrane tool; mostly moderate-to-high risk
- Certainty: GRADE (all outcomes very low)
