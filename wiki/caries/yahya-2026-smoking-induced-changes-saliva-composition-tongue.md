## Three-line Summary
Cross-sectional study of 78 adults (38 smokers, 40 nonsmokers) comparing salivary flow, pH, buffering capacity, and tongue coating (Miyazaki Index) by smoking status.
Smokers showed significantly lower salivary buffering capacity (p = 0.002) and greater tongue-coating thickness (p = 0.008) and distribution area (p = 0.000); flow and pH did not differ significantly.
Authors propose adding tongue-coating and saliva analysis to caries risk assessment, framing smoking as a modifiable behavioural factor in salivary defence and oral ecology.

## Summary
This cross-sectional study examined whether cigarette smoking is associated with changes in salivary parameters and tongue coating among 78 adults (38 smokers, 40 nonsmokers) attending a Malaysian university dental polyclinic. Salivary flow (resting and stimulated), pH, and buffering capacity were measured with a saliva kit, and tongue coating was scored with the Miyazaki Index (thickness, colour, distribution area) at nine tongue-dorsum sections (inter-rater ICC = 0.889). Smokers had significantly lower buffering capacity and significantly thicker and more widespread tongue coating, while flow rate and pH were statistically indistinguishable between groups. The authors interpret these changes as a smoking-associated weakening of salivary defence and a shift toward an acidogenic, biofilm-supportive oral environment, and suggest that salivary buffering and tongue-coating assessment could be incorporated into caries risk assessment and preventive protocols.

## Key Contributions
- First concurrent evaluation of salivary flow, pH, buffering capacity, and tongue coating in smokers vs nonsmokers within this wiki.
- Quantified the smoking signal: buffering capacity p = 0.002; tongue-coating thickness p = 0.008; distribution area p = 0.000.
- Framed tongue coating and saliva parameters as adjunctive, non-invasive biomarkers for caries risk assessment, tied to smoking-cessation counselling.

## Methodology
Cross-sectional, purposive sampling of adult smokers (> 6 months cigarette use) and nonsmokers without systemic illness or saliva-affecting medication. Planned sample 88 (44/group; from Rad et al. 2010 xerostomia 39% vs 12%, α = 0.05, β = 0.2, 80% power) but 78 were analysed after attrition/incomplete data. Stimulated saliva collected after 5 min paraffin-wax chewing; flow measured volumetrically; pH and buffering read from colourimetric strips (buffering: very low 0–5, low 6–9, normal/high 10–12). Tongue coating scored by Miyazaki Index by three examiners; smoking status confirmed with FTND and carbon-monoxide analysis. Comparisons by chi-square and Mann–Whitney U tests (p < 0.05); bivariate only, no multivariate adjustment. [확인 — PDF 추출 텍스트]
