## Three-line Summary

Comprehensive narrative review of complications of open (lateral window) and closed (transcrestal) sinus lifting procedures, covering classification and management, published in Periodontology 2000.
Hemorrhage is identified as a specific intraoperative complication of open sinus lifting; Schneiderian membrane perforation (~20–25%) is the most common, with rhinosinusitis, ostium blockage, and hemorrhage as less frequent but graft/implant-threatening complications.
New technologies (piezoelectric, hydraulic sinus lift, reamer burs) are proposed to reduce complications but evidence remains limited; thorough preoperative assessment is the primary risk-reduction measure.

## Summary

This narrative review by the University Complutense Madrid group (Molina, Sanz-Sánchez, Sanz-Martín, Ortiz-Vigón, Sanz) provides a classification and management framework for complications of both open and closed sinus lifting procedures. The lateral window approach (open sinus lift) has a complication profile dominated by Schneiderian membrane perforation (~20–25% incidence); less frequent but clinically important complications include hemorrhage, chronic rhinosinusitis, and ostium blockage from overfilling. The transcrestal (closed) approach has its own specific complications: membrane perforation, BPPV, and implant displacement into the sinus. The paper reviews emerging technologies (piezoelectric devices, hydraulic sinus lift, reamer burs) intended to reduce these complications but notes that supporting evidence is still insufficient. The key preventive message is thorough medical history, radiographic evaluation, and matching technique to the case characteristics and surgeon's experience.

## Key Contributions

- Provides a systematic classification of open vs closed sinus lift complications
- Names hemorrhage as a specific intraoperative complication of the lateral window approach
- Reviews evidence for new technologies aiming to reduce sinus lift complications
- Covers the full spectrum: intraoperative and postoperative, open and closed approaches

## Results

**Open sinus lifting (lateral window) — complications:**

| Complication | Frequency | Key notes |
|---|---|---|
| Membrane perforation (SMP) | ~20–25% | Most common; management depends on size |
| Swelling / hematoma | General | Shared with other oral surgery |
| Hemorrhage | Less frequent | May compromise graft/implant viability |
| Chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) | Less frequent | May compromise graft/implant viability |
| Ostium blockage (overfilling) | Less frequent | May compromise graft/implant viability |

**Closed sinus lifting (transcrestal) — specific complications:**

| Complication | Key notes |
|---|---|
| Membrane perforation | Occurs via transcrestal route |
| BPPV | Specific to transcrestal approach |
| Implant displacement | Into sinus cavity |

**New technologies:**

| Technology | Claim | Evidence status (per review) |
|---|---|---|
| Piezoelectric device | Reduces membrane perforation + vascular injury | Still lacking |
| Hydraulic sinus lift | Reduces trauma | Still lacking |
| Reamer burs | Reduces complications | Still lacking |
