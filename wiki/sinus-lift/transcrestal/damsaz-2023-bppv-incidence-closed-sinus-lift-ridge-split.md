## Three-line Summary

Prospective cohort study (n=112 patients, mean age 48.4±9.5y) at Mashhad Dental School comparing benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) incidence after closed (transcrestal) sinus lift vs ridge splitting surgery, diagnosed via Dix-Hallpike maneuver before and after surgery.

Overall BPPV incidence was 1.8%; 2 patients (3.1%) in the closed sinus lift group developed BPPV vs 0 patients (0%) in the ridge split group, but the between-group difference was not statistically significant (p=0.509).

Closed sinus lift may carry a higher BPPV risk than ridge splitting, but the study is underpowered (only 2 BPPV events total) — abstract-only source (full text not retrieved), larger studies needed to confirm.

## 세줄요약

마쉬하드 치과대학(Mashhad Dental School)에서 시행된 전향적 코호트 연구(n=112명, 평균연령 48.4±9.5세)로, 폐쇄형(경치조골접근, transcrestal approach) 상악동거상술과 치조제 분할술(ridge splitting) 후 딕스-홀파이크 검사(Dix-Hallpike maneuver, 수술 전후 시행)로 진단한 양성돌발성두위현훈(Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo, BPPV) 발생률을 비교.

전체 BPPV 발생률 1.8%; 폐쇄형 상악동거상술군 2명(3.1%) BPPV 발생 vs 치조제 분할술군 0명(0%)이었으나 군간 차이는 통계적으로 유의하지 않음(p=0.509).

폐쇄형 상악동거상술이 치조제 분할술보다 BPPV 위험이 더 높을 가능성이 시사되나 전체 BPPV 사건이 2건뿐이라 검정력이 부족함 — 초록만 확보(abstract-only, 원문 미확보), 대규모 후속 연구로 확인 필요.

## Summary

This single-center prospective cohort study at Mashhad Dental School (Sept 2021–Sept 2022) enrolled 112 healthy patients (51 women, 61 men; mean age 48.4±9.5y) requiring posterior maxillary augmentation, split into a closed (transcrestal) sinus lift group and a ridge splitting group, all performed by a single surgeon. The primary outcome, BPPV, was assessed with the Dix-Hallpike maneuver both before and after surgery. Across the whole cohort, BPPV occurred in 1.8% of patients, with the closed sinus lift group showing a numerically higher rate (3.1%, 2/patients) than the ridge split group (0%), though this difference did not reach statistical significance (p=0.509). The authors also found a significant association between certain (unspecified in the available abstract) systemic health conditions and BPPV onset, and no significant between-group difference for other symptoms (hypertension, headache, dizziness, nausea). The conclusion is cautious: closed sinus lift may carry a higher BPPV risk than ridge splitting, but the small event count (2 total BPPV cases) means the study is underpowered and larger confirmatory studies are needed. This source is abstract-only — the PMC full-text body was empty at retrieval, so no methodological detail beyond the structured abstract is available.

## Key Contributions
- First cohort study to directly compare BPPV incidence between closed (transcrestal) sinus lift and ridge splitting surgery using a standardized pre/post-operative Dix-Hallpike diagnostic protocol.
- Provides a symptom-prevalence benchmark for a posterior-maxilla augmentation cohort (hypertension 10.7%, headache 36.6%, dizziness 27.7%, nausea 12.5%, BPPV 1.8%).
- Flags a significant link between certain systemic health conditions and BPPV onset, suggesting patient-level risk factors may matter as much as surgical technique.

## Methodology
Single-surgeon, single-center prospective cohort at Mashhad Dental School (Sept 2021–Sept 2022). n=112 healthy patients requiring posterior maxillary closed sinus lift or ridge split surgery. Primary outcome: BPPV, diagnosed via Dix-Hallpike maneuver before and after the operation. Secondary outcomes: hypertension, headache, dizziness, nausea. Statistical analysis via SPSS 23, significance set at p<0.05. (Abstract-only; exact group sizes for sinus lift vs ridge split subgroups and which specific health conditions were associated with BPPV are not stated in the available abstract.)
