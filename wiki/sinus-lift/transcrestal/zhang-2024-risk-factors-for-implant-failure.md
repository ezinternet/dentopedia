## Three-line Summary

Case report plus literature review on early implant failure after transcrestal sinus floor elevation (TSFE): a 4.8 mm x 10 mm standard implant placed after TSFE loosened suddenly 6 weeks after surgery.

The authors group candidate risk factors into patient-related, anatomical, and operation- and implant-related factors, and within the study's limits name graft particles lodged between the implant surface and the socket as a direct risk factor; fracture of the buccal or palatal cortical plate during site preparation or insertion is a further consideration.

They recommend careful socket cleaning during TSFE and discourage loose particulate graft material; the evidence is a single case plus a review, taken from the abstract only, with no effect sizes or failure rates available.

## 세줄요약

경치조 상악동거상술 (transcrestal sinus floor elevation, TSFE) 후 조기 임플란트 실패에 대한 증례보고와 문헌고찰: TSFE 후 직경 4.8 mm, 길이 10 mm 표준 임플란트를 식립하고 6주 뒤 갑자기 동요가 관찰됨.

위험요인을 환자 관련, 해부학적, 수술 및 임플란트 관련 요인으로 분류했으며, 연구 한계 내에서 임플란트 표면과 식립와 사이에 낀 입자형 골이식재를 직접적 위험요인으로 보았고 부위 형성 및 식립 중 협측·구개측 피질골 골절 가능성도 추가 고려사항으로 제시함.

TSFE 중 식립와 세척을 강조하고 느슨한 입자형 이식재 사용을 지양할 것을 권고; 단일 증례와 문헌고찰이며 초록만 확인하여 효과 크기나 실패율은 알 수 없음.

## Summary

The authors note that although transcrestal sinus floor elevation (TSFE) is widely used for insufficient residual bone height in the posterior maxilla, few studies address risk factors for early implant failure associated with it. They report a patient whose standard implant (4.8 mm diameter, 10 mm length), placed after TSFE, loosened suddenly 6 weeks after the initial surgery, and discuss associated risk factors in a literature review.

Factors that could cause early failure are grouped into patient-related factors, anatomical factors of the operative area, and operation- and implant-related factors. Within the study's limitations, graft material particles between the implant surface and the socket are considered a direct risk factor. Fracture of the buccal or palatal cortical plate during site preparation and implant insertion is raised as another significant consideration for implant loss.

## Key Contributions

- Targets early failure specifically after TSFE, a niche the authors say is under-studied.
- Offers a three-class risk-factor framework (patient, anatomical, operation/implant).
- Proposes graft particles trapped between implant and socket wall as a direct mechanism of failure.

## Methodology

Single case report with accompanying literature review (abstract-only; search methods, number of reviewed studies, setting and country not stated). Early failure is not formally defined; the reported event is loosening at 6 weeks.

## Results

[확인] Implant loosening was observed suddenly 6 weeks after the initial surgery (4.8 mm x 10 mm standard implant after TSFE). No early failure rate, odds ratios or confidence intervals are given in the abstract. Proposed direct risk factor: graft particles between implant surface and socket; additional consideration: buccal or palatal cortical plate fracture.

## Limitations

- One case plus review; no quantitative risk estimates in the abstract.
- Abstract-only: review methods, included studies, graft type, and TSFE technique are not verifiable.
- Causal attribution to graft particles is tentative (authors' own hedge "within the current study's limitations").

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

When performing TSFE with simultaneous implant placement, clean the osteotomy of loose particulate graft before inserting the implant and be alert to buccal or palatal cortical plate fracture during preparation and insertion; this is a cautionary single-case signal, not a quantified recommendation.
