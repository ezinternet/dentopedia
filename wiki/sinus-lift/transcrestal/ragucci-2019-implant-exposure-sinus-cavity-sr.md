## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (MEDLINE/EMBASE/Cochrane, up to January 2018) of 8 studies with 493 implants and 52.7 months mean follow-up evaluating survival and complication rates when dental implants penetrate the sinus cavity without graft material (lateral or transalveolar approach, with or without membrane perforation).

Weighted mean survival rate 95.6%; clinical complication rate 3.4% (epistaxis most frequent); radiographic complication rate 14.8% (Schneiderian membrane thickening most frequent); penetration depth ≤4 mm vs >4 mm showed no statistically significant difference in survival (p=0.403) or clinical complications.

Implant penetration into the sinus cavity without grafting yields acceptable survival rates comparable to grafted procedures, with the main complication being asymptomatic membrane thickening on imaging — deeper penetration (>4 mm) trends toward higher radiographic complications (29.3% vs 5.29%) but does not reach significance.

## Summary

International University of Catalonia team performed a systematic review assessing the impact of implant exposure into the sinus cavity on survival and complications. This covered both lateral window and transalveolar (crestal) procedures where implants extended into the sinus without graft material and with or without Schneiderian membrane perforation ("punch out"). Literature search included MEDLINE, EMBASE, and Cochrane up to January 2018; only studies with ≥6 months follow-up were included.

Eight studies (493 implants, mean follow-up 52.7 months) met inclusion criteria. Studies were heterogeneous in design, approach, and penetration depth reporting, so meta-analysis was not performed.

**Survival:** Weighted mean survival 95.6% (95% CI). No significant difference between ≤4 mm and >4 mm penetration groups (p=0.403).

**Clinical complications (3.4%):** Epistaxis was the most frequent complication. No significant difference by penetration level.

**Radiographic complications (14.8%):** Schneiderian membrane thickening was most common. Rate was 5.29% at ≤4 mm penetration vs 29.3% at >4 mm, without reaching significance (p=0.301) — but suggests deeper penetration may carry higher radiographic risk.

## Key Contributions

- First SR specifically addressing survival/complications of implants that intentionally or inadvertently penetrate sinus without grafting
- Establishes that penetration depth ≤4 vs >4 mm does not significantly alter survival — a clinically important finding for transcrestal graftless approaches
- Identifies radiographic membrane thickening as an under-recognized but frequent finding (~15%) even when clinically asymptomatic
- Provides the complication benchmark (3.4% clinical, 14.8% radiographic) for risk counseling in graftless SFE

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review
- **Search**: MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane Oral Health Group; up to January 2018; two independent reviewers
- **Inclusion**: Human studies, ≥6 months follow-up, implants penetrating sinus floor without regenerative procedure or graft (lateral or transalveolar)
- **Outcomes**: Primary = survival rate; secondary = clinical and radiographic complications
- **Analysis**: Weighted mean ± 95% CI; chi-square for penetration depth group comparison

## Results

- 8 studies included; 493 implants; mean follow-up 52.7 months
- Weighted survival: 95.6%
- Clinical complication rate: 3.4% (epistaxis #1)
- Radiographic complication rate: 14.8% (membrane thickening #1)
- ≤4 mm vs >4 mm survival: p=0.403 (NS)
- ≤4 mm vs >4 mm radiographic complications: 5.29% vs 29.3%; p=0.301 (NS, trend)
