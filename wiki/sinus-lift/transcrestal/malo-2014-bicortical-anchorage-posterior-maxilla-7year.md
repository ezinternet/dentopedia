## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study, 88 patients / 124 posterior maxillary implants, bicortical anchorage (sinus or nasal floor) + immediate function; median 7-year follow-up (range 6 months–13 years).
Cumulative implant survival 94.2% (4 lost in complete-edentulous patients); prosthetic survival 100%; marginal bone level 1.56 mm (SD 0.87) at 5 years; mechanical complications in 56% of patients, with bruxism as dominant driver (30/49).
Bicortical anchorage with immediate function is viable at median 7 years; occlusal control mandatory in bruxers; biological and mechanical complications occur but prosthetic outcomes are maintained.

## Summary

Single-center retrospective study from Malo Clinic (Lisbon) following 88 patients who received 124 posterior maxillary implants with bicortical anchorage between 1999–2008. Bicortical anchorage was achieved by engaging either the sinus floor cortex or the nasal floor cortex as a second anchor point for the implant. Implants were loaded immediately (provisional restoration within 24–72 hours). Over a median 7-year follow-up (range 6 months–13 years), 4 implants were lost in complete-edentulous patients (partial-edentulous patients maintained 100% survival), yielding a cumulative survival rate of 94.2%. Prosthetic survival was 100% despite implant losses — remaining implants supported prosthetic load. Marginal bone loss at 5 years was 1.56 mm (SD 0.87), within accepted ranges. Mechanical complications were common (56%) with bruxism as the dominant driver — 30/49 patients with mechanical complications were heavy bruxers. The study establishes that bicortical anchorage with immediate function is a viable long-term strategy but demands careful occlusal management, especially in parafunction patients.

## Key Contributions

- Largest available long-term dataset for bicortical anchorage in posterior maxilla (median 7 years, n=124)
- 94.2% survival validates the technique beyond the 1–5 year window of Ng 2018
- Includes nasal floor engagement — extends bicortical concept beyond sinus floor only
- Immediate function protocol validated: provisional restoration same-day without compromising survival
- Bruxism quantified as mechanical complication driver — actionable risk stratification
- Prosthetic survival 100%: implant losses did not translate to prosthetic failure
