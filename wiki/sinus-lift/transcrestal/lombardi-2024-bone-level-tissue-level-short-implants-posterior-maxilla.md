## Three-line Summary

Prospective multicenter case-control, 58 patients / 71 short implants (36 tissue-level 5–6.5 mm, 35 bone-level 7 mm), posterior maxilla, mean RBH 6.2 ± 2.3 mm; primary outcome = physiological bone remodeling (PBR) at T1 (5 months) and T2 (12 months of loading).
Tissue-level implants showed significantly less PBR than bone-level at both timepoints (T1: 0.11 vs 0.34 mm, p=0.004; T2: 0.30 vs 0.55 mm, p=0.003); bicortical engagement correlated significantly with IT but not with ISQ or PBR in multivariate models; thin mucosal thickness (≤2 mm) was the dominant PBR driver.
Bicortical engagement is a positive IT predictor (confirming in vitro evidence), but tissue-level short implant design and mucosal thickness are the clinically dominant determinants of marginal bone remodeling.

## Summary

Prospective multicenter study from Italy comparing tissue-level (5, 6.5 mm) and bone-level (7 mm) short implants in the posterior maxilla. The novel feature was including bicortical engagement as an explicit predictor in multivariate regression, alongside mucosal thickness, abutment height, and crown emergence angle. Bicortical engagement emerged as a significant positive predictor of insertion torque at placement — confirming in vitro findings (Han 2016, Ahn 2012) for the first time in a clinical cohort. However, bicortical engagement did not significantly predict ISQ or marginal bone remodeling at either timepoint, suggesting its primary benefit is mechanical purchase at placement rather than long-term bone maintenance. The dominant PBR driver at 5 months was thin mucosa (≤2 mm), and at 12 months was short abutment height (≤2 mm) and high emergence angle (>30°) in bone-level implants. Tissue-level short implants consistently showed less PBR than bone-level, an actionable design choice.

## Key Contributions

- Clinical confirmation that bicortical engagement positively predicts IT (insertion torque), consistent with in vitro Han 2016 and Ahn 2012
- Bicortical engagement does NOT significantly predict ISQ, T0–T1 PBR, or T1–T2 PBR — multivariate evidence against a direct bone remodeling benefit
- Mucosal thickness ≤2 mm is the dominant early PBR driver — stronger signal than bicortical fixation status
- Tissue-level implants (5–6.5 mm) show significantly less PBR than bone-level (7 mm subcrestal) at 5 and 12 months
- Short abutment height (≤2 mm) and emergence angle >30° drive late bone remodeling independently

## Results

| Outcome | Tissue-level | Bone-level | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISQ T0 | 66.8 ± 9.3 | 64.7 ± 10.3 | 0.42 |
| IT T0 (N·cm) | 41.9 ± 14.0 | 40.8 ± 11.3 | 0.56 |
| ISQ T1 | 69.5 ± 7.0 | 71.4 ± 6.5 | 0.10 |
| PBR T0–T1 (mm) | **0.11 ± 0.27** | 0.34 ± 0.35 | **0.004** |
| PBR T0–T2 (mm) | **0.30 ± 0.23** | 0.55 ± 0.42 | **0.003** |

Multivariate predictors: bicortical engagement → IT (positive, p<0.05); mucosal ≤2 mm → PBR T0–T1 (both groups, p<0.001); abutment ≤2 mm → PBR T1–T2 (both groups); emergence >30° → PBR T1–T2 (bone-level only).
