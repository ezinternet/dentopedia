## Three-line Summary

Case report (n=1, 43-year-old female) of benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) immediately following osteotome sinus floor elevation (OSFE) with simultaneous implant placement, in a patient with an undisclosed history of two prior vertigo episodes over 9 years.

Standard same-night treatment (nootropic medication + canalith repositioning procedure) failed to resolve symptoms; the patient required readmission after 1 month and recovered only after repeated repositioning maneuvers, nine doses of IV serous fluid, and piracetam over a 10-day course, with no recurrence thereafter.

Prior vertigo history is proposed as a risk factor for protracted/refractory post-OSFE BPPV, supporting pre-operative screening for vertigo history before osteotome-based transcrestal sinus lift procedures — abstract-only, full text not retrieved.

## Summary

This case report describes a 43-year-old female who developed intense vertigo and nausea immediately after osteotome sinus floor elevation (OSFE) with simultaneous implant placement. On post-operative questioning, she disclosed two prior vertigo episodes over the preceding 9 years — a history not elicited before surgery. Standard same-night management (nootropic drug therapy plus a canalith repositioning procedure performed by a specialist) failed to resolve the symptoms. The patient did not fully recover and was readmitted one month later; resolution was ultimately achieved after repeated repositioning maneuvers, nine doses of intravenous serous fluid, and piracetam administration over a 10-day treatment course, with no recurrence of dizziness afterward. The authors frame this as a case of protracted BPPV attributable to vibratory forces from the osteotome/mallet technique and head hyperextension during surgery, occurring in a patient predisposed by a prior vertigo history, and use it to argue for pre-operative screening of vertigo history before OSFE. Abstract-only — full text not retrieved.

## Key Contributions
- Illustrates that BPPV following OSFE can be protracted and refractory to first-line treatment (10-day course, readmission required) rather than the typically transient, same-day-resolving course reported in most prior case reports.
- Identifies undisclosed prior vertigo history as a plausible risk factor for a more severe/prolonged post-OSFE BPPV course.
- Recommends explicit pre-operative screening for vertigo history in patients scheduled for osteotome-based transcrestal sinus elevation.

## Methodology

Single case report (n=1). A 43-year-old female underwent OSFE with simultaneous implant placement; post-operative vertigo/nausea prompted retrospective history-taking revealing two prior vertigo episodes over 9 years. Treatment course and outcome were tracked over approximately 1 month with two treatment episodes (same-night and 1-month follow-up) totaling 10 days of active management.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Onset | Immediate vertigo + nausea after OSFE + simultaneous implant placement |

| Course | Incomplete recovery → readmitted after 1 month |
