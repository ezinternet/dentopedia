## Three-line Summary

Prospective split-mouth RCT, 15 patients / 42 implants, posterior maxilla; one side OSFE + bicortical anchorage (1 mm past sinus floor), opposite side conventional unicortical (0.5–1 mm short of floor).
Bicortical group: superior ISQ at all timepoints — baseline 63.6 vs 57.9, 3-month 71.1 vs 65.7 (all p<0.001); crestal bone loss equivalent at 3 months (~0.08–0.09 mm, p>0.05).
Bicortical sinus floor engagement via OSFE provides significantly higher primary and early secondary stability without increased bone loss in RBH ≥10 mm atrophic posterior maxilla.

## Summary

This prospective split-mouth study (India, 2018–2019) compared implants placed via OSFE with bicortical sinus floor engagement (Group I, n=22) against conventional unicortical implants (Group II, n=20) in 15 bilaterally edentulous maxillary patients. The bicortical technique used Summer's osteotome to elevate the sinus membrane and placed implants 1 mm longer than available bone height, deliberately engaging the sinus floor cortex. Mean RBH was ~11 mm in both groups. ISQ (Osstell Mentor) was measured at baseline, 1 month, and 3 months. Bicortical implants showed significantly higher ISQ at every timepoint, with 3-month ISQ of 71.1 vs 65.7 (p<0.001). Crestal bone loss measured on OPG with ImageJ at 3 months was identical (~0.08–0.09 mm mesial/distal) with no inter-group difference. No sinus membrane perforations or nasal complications occurred. The study supports bicortical anchorage as a stability-enhancing approach avoiding grafting in suitable posterior maxilla cases.

## Key Contributions

- First prospective split-mouth clinical trial comparing bicortical vs unicortical anchorage with RFA
- Demonstrates dual-cortex engagement increases ISQ by ~5–6 units at every timepoint vs unicortical
- Crestal bone loss equivalent through 3 months — bicortical does not increase marginal bone risk
- No sinus complications despite intentional 1 mm floor perforation
- Supports 6–13 mm implants in ~11 mm RBH when using OSFE to achieve bicortical fixation

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Split-mouth prospective study |
| n | 15 patients, 42 implants (22 bicortical, 20 unicortical) |
| RBH (both groups) | ~11.2 mm (p=0.306, no difference) |
| Implant system | HC2, Switzerland (roughened SLA-type) |
| Bicortical length | Mean 12.11 ± 1.10 mm |
| Unicortical length | Mean 10.78 ± 1.25 mm |
| Follow-up | 3 months post-implant |
| Primary outcome | ISQ (Osstell Mentor) |
| Secondary outcomes | Crestal bone loss (OPG + ImageJ), pain VAS, swelling |

## Results

| Timepoint | Bicortical ISQ | Unicortical ISQ | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 63.64 ± 2.57 | 57.90 ± 3.65 | **<0.001** |
| 1 month | 66.95 ± 2.48 | 61.95 ± 3.41 | **<0.001** |
| 3 months | 71.14 ± 3.37 | 65.70 ± 3.13 | **<0.001** |

- Mesial CBL at 3 months: 0.09 mm vs 0.08 mm, p=0.789 (NS)
- Distal CBL at 3 months: 0.07 mm vs 0.08 mm, p=0.903 (NS)
- Pain/swelling: identical patterns in both groups; no significant inter-group difference
