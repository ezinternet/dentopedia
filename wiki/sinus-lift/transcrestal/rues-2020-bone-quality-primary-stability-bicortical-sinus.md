## Three-line Summary

In vitro μCT + torsional micromotion study, n=17 pig scapula segments simulating bicortical posterior maxilla; Straumann SLA 4.1 RN 10 mm implants inserted with standardized bicortical protocol.
Primary stability (micromotion) was uncorrelated with total bone thickness (p=0.827) but improved with higher BMD (p=0.095) or greater cortical engagement length (p=0.071); minimum micromotion ~4 μm at cortical engagement ≥2 mm and BMD >1.0 g/cm³.
Cortical bone quality and achieved engagement depth — not total RBH — are the critical determinants of bicortical primary stability in atrophic posterior maxilla.

## Summary

This German in vitro study (Heidelberg) used pig scapula segments — a two-cortex bone sandwich resembling the atrophic posterior maxilla sinus situation — to quantify how bone quality and quantity affect primary stability in bicortical implant placement. Seventeen segments underwent μCT measurement of BMD, total thickness, and cortical thickness, followed by Straumann 4.1 RN SLA 10 mm implant insertion with standardized drilling (just breaching the coronal cortex). Torsional bone-implant micromotion was measured with a custom 3D optical marker tracking device under ±15 Ncm cyclic torsion. The key finding was that total bone thickness had no correlation with stability (p=0.827), while BMD and cortical engagement length were the critical predictors. Implants with >2 mm in cortical bone and BMD >1.0 g/cm³ achieved minimum micromotion (~4 μm). Five specimens loosened prematurely — all showed inadequate cortical engagement or low BMD.

## Key Contributions

- Mechanistically isolates cortical thickness and BMD from total RBH as the critical bicortical stability factors
- Establishes minimum targets: cortical engagement ≥2 mm, BMD >1.0 g/cm³ for adequate primary stability
- Validates novel torsional micromotion method (accuracy ±1 μm) for dental implants
- Explains why some non-augmented bicortical cases fail: inadequate cortical engagement, not insufficient total height

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Model | Pig scapula segments (n=17), two-cortex anatomy |
| Implant | Straumann SLA 4.1 RN 10 mm |
| Bone measurement | μCT (SkyScan 1076, 18 μm) → BMD, cortical/total thickness |
| Stability metric | Torsional bone-implant micromotion (3D markers, ±15 Ncm) |
| Statistics | Pearson correlation: micromotion vs BMD, thickness, cortical length |

## Results

| Variable | Correlation with micromotion | p |
|---|---|---|
| Total bone thickness | r ≈ 0 | 0.827 (NS) |
| Bone mineral density (BMD) | Inverse trend | 0.095 |
| Cortical engagement length | Inverse trend | 0.071 |

- Minimum micromotion ~4 μm: cortical engagement ≥2 mm AND BMD >1.0 g/cm³
- 5/17 specimens failed at <15 Ncm: all had insertion torque <25 Ncm and/or <2 mm cortical embedding
