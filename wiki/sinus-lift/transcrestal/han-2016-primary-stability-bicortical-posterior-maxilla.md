## Three-line Summary

In vitro 2-factor ANOVA (2 cortical conditions × 4 RBH levels: 3/5/8/12 mm), n=80 polyurethane blocks; Dentium Superline 4.3×10 mm implants; ISQ, PTV, IT, RT measured.
Bicortical significantly outperformed monocortical on ISQ and PTV at all RBH (p<0.001); maximum ISQ 81.03 in B8; IT did not reliably track ISQ (r=0.58) — monocortical M3 paradoxically had high IT due to tapered body.
ISQ/PTV are the reliable stability metrics; bicortical fixation provides consistent stability gains, but IT alone cannot predict ISQ and should not be the sole criterion for immediate loading.

## Summary

Korean polyurethane model study systematically quantifying how cortical condition (mono- vs bicortical) and residual bone height (3, 5, 8, 12 mm) affect four stability metrics. Bicortical groups showed significantly higher ISQ and PTV across all bone heights, with peak stability at B8 (8 mm bicortical). Key clinical finding: ISQ and PTV are highly inter-correlated (r=−0.97) and are the reliable stability indicators; IT showed only moderate correlation with ISQ (r=0.58) and RT showed none (r=0.12). The M3 group's paradoxically high IT — driven by the tapered implant body engaging the apical cortex — demonstrates that tactile "tight insertion" does not equal good ISQ stability, and early loading decisions should not be based on IT alone. For bicortical fixation to be achieved, implant length must exceed RBH (B12 group failed to cross both cortices with a 10 mm implant in 12 mm bone).

## Key Contributions

- Systematic demonstration: bicortical ISQ advantage holds at ALL bone heights (3–12 mm)
- IT paradox defined quantitatively (M3 high IT but low ISQ)
- Confirms ISQ–PTV high correlation; ISQ–RT near-zero correlation
- Defines minimum implant length requirement for bicortical: must exceed RBH
