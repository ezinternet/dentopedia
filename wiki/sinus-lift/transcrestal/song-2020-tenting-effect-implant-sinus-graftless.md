## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study (49 implants, 26 patients) assessed bone formation patterns after lateral-approach graftless sinus floor elevation using implant tenting over 6 months.

Bone formation above the implant apex occurred in 18/46 cases (39.1%); tent-type morphology dominated (80.4%); new bone height correlated significantly with implant protrusion length (p<0.001).

Graftless sinus lift relying on the implant tenting effect is feasible, but success depends on primary stability in residual bone; the 6-month retrospective design without histology limits conclusions on bone quality.

## Summary

Song et al. (2020) investigated whether the dental implant itself can serve as a "tent pole" to mechanically elevate the Schneiderian membrane during lateral-approach graftless sinus floor elevation, thereby promoting new bone formation without any grafting material.

A retrospective CBCT analysis of 49 implants (26 patients) at 6 months showed that bone above the implant apex formed in 39.1% of cases. The dominant morphology was tent-type (80.4%), where the membrane was draped over the implant apex, confining a blood clot space that subsequently ossified. Dehiscence-type defects occurred in 10.9%. A statistically significant positive correlation was found between the length of implant protrusion into the sinus and the height of new bone gain (Pearson's r, p<0.001).

Implant survival was 93.5% at 6 months. Three implants failed; all had inadequate primary stability in the residual bone at placement. The authors conclude that primary stability in the residual alveolar bone — not grafting material — is the critical determinant of success in this approach.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that graftless lateral-approach sinus lift using the tenting mechanism is clinically viable, contributing retrospective case-series data to the graftless TSFE evidence base
- Establishes a dose–response relationship: longer implant protrusion into sinus → greater new bone height, suggesting the mechanical tent space geometry drives ossification
- Classifies bone formation morphology (tent / dehiscence / flat) providing a reproducible CBCT-based framework for outcome reporting in graftless sinus lift studies

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective case series; single center (Dong-A University Medical Center, Busan, Korea)
- **Sample**: 26 patients, 49 implants; lateral window antrostomy without graft material; simultaneous implant placement
- **Imaging**: CBCT at pre-op baseline and 6 months post-op
- **Outcome measures**: Residual bone height, implant protrusion length into sinus, new bone height above apex, bone morphology classification
- **Statistics**: Pearson's correlation coefficient (implant protrusion length vs. new bone gain)

## Results

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Bone formation above apex (any height) | 18/46 cases (39.1%) |
| Tent-type formation | 37/46 (80.4%) |
| Dehiscence-type defect | 5/46 (10.9%) |
| Bone gain 0–2 mm above apex | 11 implants |
| Bone gain >2 mm above apex | 7 implants |
| Implant survival at 6 months | 93.5% (46/49) |
| Implant protrusion vs. bone gain correlation | p<0.001 (Pearson) |

The three implant failures all occurred in sites where primary stability in residual bone was inadequate at placement. No complications attributable to lack of grafting material (e.g. sinus infection, graft extrusion) were reported, consistent with the graftless approach.
