## Three-line Summary

PRISMA systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42023455968) of 6 studies (5 RCTs + 1 prospective clinical study; 89 patients, 100 implants) comparing osseodensification (OD) vs osteotome for transcrestal sinus floor elevation in the atrophic posterior maxilla (mean age 41.1 y, follow-up 4–6 months).

Every included study reported statistically significant higher implant stability (ISQ) with OD (primary ISQ 65.1–74.2 vs 52.8–68.5; secondary 75.9–87.1 vs 65.1–80.2), while bone-loss differences were insignificant and residual bone height / bone gain improved with both techniques (bone gain 1.8–5.8 mm OD vs 1.4–6.9 mm osteotome); no meta-analysis was performed due to heterogeneity.

OD shows a potential primary-stability advantage, but the evidence base is heterogeneous and largely high risk of bias (2 moderate, 4 high RoB), so standardized head-to-head research is needed before definitive clinical guidance.

## 세줄요약

PRISMA 체계적 고찰(SR, PROSPERO CRD42023455968) — 경치조골 상악동거상술(Transcrestal Sinus Floor Elevation, TSFE)에서 골밀도화(Osseodensification, OD) vs 오스테오톰 비교, 6편(5 RCT + 1 전향적; 환자 89명, 임플란트 100개).

모든 연구에서 OD의 임플란트 안정성 지수(Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ)가 유의하게 높았고(1차 65.1–74.2 vs 52.8–68.5), 변연골소실(Marginal Bone Loss, MBL)은 유의차 없음; 잔존골높이(RBH)·골획득량은 두 술식 모두 개선(골획득 1.8–5.8 mm vs 1.4–6.9 mm); 이질성으로 메타분석은 수행되지 않음.

OD의 1차 안정성 우위 가능성은 있으나 근거가 비뚤림 위험 높음(고위험 4편·중등도 2편)이고 이질적이라, 표준화된 직접 비교 연구가 필요하다.

## Summary

This PRISMA systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42023455968) from an Indian OMFS group compares osseodensification (Densah burs, Huwais 2012) with the classic Summers osteotome for transcrestal sinus floor elevation in the posterior atrophic maxilla. Six studies (5 RCTs + 1 prospective clinical study; 89 patients, 100 implants) met inclusion, but the authors declined meta-analysis citing heterogeneity and insufficient data for pooling. The consistent signal across all six studies is a statistically significant primary- and secondary-stability (ISQ) advantage for OD, while marginal bone loss showed no significant between-group difference and residual bone height / bone gain improved with both techniques. The review positions OD as a promising crestal technique but stresses that the heterogeneity and mostly moderate-to-high risk of bias of the current evidence preclude definitive clinical guidance.

## Key Contributions

- PROSPERO-registered (CRD42023455968) PRISMA systematic review of OD vs osteotome specifically via the transcrestal (crestal) approach.
- Six-study qualitative synthesis (5 RCTs + 1 prospective; 89 patients / 100 implants), drawing largely on Egyptian/Alexandria clinical trials.
- Documents a statistically significant ISQ advantage for OD in all six included studies — primary ISQ 65.1–74.2 (OD) vs 52.8–68.5 (osteotome).
- Marginal bone loss differences were statistically insignificant in the 3 studies reporting it — the only outcome without an OD signal.
- Explicitly frames heterogeneity — not just missing data — as the reason pooling is not yet defensible.

## Methodology

PRISMA-compliant systematic review, protocol registered in PROSPERO (CRD42023455968). Searched PubMed (incl. MEDLINE), Cochrane Central, Google Scholar and grey-literature sources (OpenGrey) over the last ten years; hand-search and reference-list screening performed. PICO: population = studies comparing OD and osteotome via transcrestal approach; intervention = sinus lift via transcrestal approach; comparison = OD vs osteotome; outcomes = implant stability (ISQ), marginal bone loss, residual bone height, bone gain. Screening in two phases by two reviewers with third-author adjudication; risk-of-bias appraisal with QATSDD and Cochrane RoB 2 (two studies moderate, four high risk of bias); RevMan 5.3 used for RoB figures. Selection: 1205 records → 1165 screened → 10 full-texts → 6 included (5 RCTs + 1 prospective).

## Results

| Outcome | Osseodensification (OD) | Osteotome | N studies | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary ISQ (N/cm) | 65.1 ± 4.3 to 74.2 ± 3.9 | 52.8 ± 6.2 to 68.5 ± 2.2 | 6 | Significant OD advantage in all studies |
| Secondary ISQ (N/cm) | 75.9 ± 2.9 to 87.1 ± 3.3 | 65.1 ± 3.06 to 80.2 ± 1.6 | 5–6 | Significant OD advantage |
| Marginal bone loss (mm) | 0.2 ± 0.73 to 0.98 ± 0.15 | 0.43 ± 0.97 to 0.93 ± 0.15 | 3 | No significant between-group difference |
| Immediate postop RBH (mm) | 4.66–8.3 | 4.68–7.14 | 3 | Both improved |
| 6-month RBH (mm) | 10.47 ± 0.59 to 10.8 ± 1.1 | 8.27 ± 0.48 to 11.61 ± 0.53 | 3 | Both improved |
| Bone gain (mm) | 1.8 ± 0.4 to 5.81 ± 0.51 | 1.4 ± 0.5 to 6.93 ± 0.45 | 3 | Both gained; magnitude varied |

Meta-analysis was **not** performed — insufficient data for statistical pooling and heterogeneity across studies. Risk of bias: 2 studies moderate, 4 high (RoB 2). Population: 89 patients, 100 implants; 49 OD / 50 osteotome (1 split-mouth study counted in both); age 23–65 y (mean 41.08 ± 15.36); follow-up 4–6 months.
