## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort, 53 patients, posterior maxilla; Group A bicortical engagement (n=31) vs Group B unicortical conventional (n=25); 1–5 years prosthetic loading.
Group A survival 100%; Group B overall 74.68% (6-mm unicortical subgroup only 51.85%, 10-mm unicortical 93.33%); significant intrasinus bone gain mesial and distal in Group A only.
Bicortical 6-mm implants in atrophic posterior maxilla achieve 100% survival with spontaneous intrasinus bone gain — dramatically superior to unicortical 6-mm implants and supporting bicortical engagement as graft-free alternative to internal sinus lift.

## Summary

This Chinese retrospective study compared long-term outcomes of bicortical dental implantation (Group A, 31 patients) vs conventional unicortical implantation (Group B, 25 patients) in atrophic posterior maxillae. Bicortical engagement was employed as an alternative to internal sinus lifting with bone graft. Over 1–5 years of prosthetic loading, Group A achieved 100% implant survival. Group B showed 74.68% overall survival, driven critically by the poor performance of 6-mm unicortical implants (51.85% survival), while 10-mm unicortical implants performed acceptably at 93.33%. Marginal bone height differed significantly only between the 6-mm subgroups. Importantly, Group A implants showed significant intrasinus bone gain on both mesial and distal aspects — a phenomenon attributed to the periosteal reaction from sinus floor cortex perforation and membrane elevation, similar to the "tent pole" graftless sinus lift effect documented elsewhere in the wiki.

## Key Contributions

- 100% vs 51.85% survival: most dramatic evidence that bicortical 6-mm outperforms unicortical 6-mm
- Spontaneous intrasinus bone gain only in bicortical group — suggests tenting mechanism
- Supports bicortical engagement as equivalent to internal sinus lift + graft in atrophic cases
- Longest available follow-up (1–5 years) for this technique comparison

## Results

| Subgroup | Survival Rate |
|---|---|
| Group A: bicortical (all lengths) | **100%** |
| Group B: unicortical overall | 74.68% |
| Group B: 6-mm unicortical | **51.85%** |
| Group B: 10-mm unicortical | 93.33% |

- Marginal bone height: significant between-group difference in 6-mm subgroups only (bicortical better)
- Intrasinus bone gain: significant mesial + distal in Group A; absent in Group B
