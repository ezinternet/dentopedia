## Three-line Summary

3D FEA, posterior maxillary molar region; bone quantity A (normal) and C (atrophic) models; implant length, diameter, bicortical anchorage, and sinus augmentation varied; maximum principal stress on peri-implant cortical bone as outcome.
Bone quantity A: 4 mm × 6 mm implant showed greatest (worst) stress distribution; bone quantity C: 5 mm × 6 mm + bicortical anchorage yielded substantially lower stress than 4 mm × 13 mm + sinus augmentation.
For atrophic posterior maxilla with sufficient ridge width (bone quantity C), a short wide implant (5 mm diameter, 6 mm length) with bicortical anchorage is biomechanically preferable to a long implant with sinus augmentation.

## Summary

Japanese FEA study from Osaka University examining how implant dimensions and surgical approach interact to affect peri-implant cortical bone stress in the posterior maxilla. The study modeled both normal (bone quantity A) and atrophic (bone quantity C) bone conditions, comparing 4 mm vs 5 mm diameter implants at 6 mm length with and without bicortical anchorage, against a 13 mm long implant with sinus augmentation. The key finding is that the bicortical advantage for short implants is diameter-dependent: 4 mm diameter alone is insufficient in normal bone (highest stress), but 5 mm diameter + bicortical anchorage in atrophic bone achieves lower stress than conventional sinus-augmented long implants. This positions the short bicortical implant as a sinus-augmentation-avoidance strategy conditional on ridge width supporting a 5 mm implant.

## Key Contributions

- Diameter is a critical co-variable: 5 mm + bicortical > 4 mm + bicortical
- Bone quantity C: short bicortical outperforms sinus-augmented long implant (directly answers a clinical dilemma)
- Bone quantity A: short implants paradoxically show highest stress — bicortical benefit context-dependent
- Framework: ridge width assessment determines whether bicortical short implant is viable
