## Three-line Summary

CT-derived 3D FEA of atrophic posterior maxilla; 3 FPD scenarios: (1) 6×5 mm short + bicortical, (2) sinus-grafted 11×4 mm long, (3) 45°-tilted 11×5 mm long; oblique chewing forces; von Mises and minimum principal stress on implants, cortical bone, trabecular bone, and graft.
Crestal cortical bone had the highest compressive stress (49.761 MPa) in all models; graft material absorbed negligible stress (0.049 MPa — 1,000× less than crestal cortex); short bicortical implant had the lowest von Mises (219.63 MPa); 45°-tilted had the highest (499.50 MPa).
Short wide bicortical fixation is the most biomechanically feasible FPD option for atrophied posterior maxilla; graft material has inadequate load-transfer; 45°-tilted implants should be avoided when alternatives exist due to dramatically higher implant stress.

## Summary

Turkish FEA study from Gazi University using a CT-derived model of an actual atrophic posterior maxilla to compare three FPD approaches. The CT-derived geometry is a methodological strength over CAD-only models. Three clinical scenarios were tested: short-wide bicortical fixation (the bicortical hypothesis), sinus-grafted long implant (current standard), and 45°-tilted long implant (a sinus-avoidance alternative). The result unambiguously favors the short bicortical strategy on implant stress. Crucially, the graft material finding is clinically important: despite being surgically placed specifically to support implants, graft material absorbs almost no load (0.049 MPa vs 49.761 MPa crestal cortex). This means sinus graft success depends not on graft load absorption but on providing platform area for a long implant — and once a bicortical short implant achieves platform stability through a different mechanism (dual cortex), the graft becomes biomechanically redundant. The 45°-tilted scenario (All-on-4 style) showed the worst implant stress — more than double the bicortical scenario.

## Key Contributions

- CT-derived model: higher anatomical fidelity than CAD-only FEA studies
- Graft material has essentially no load-absorption capacity (0.049 MPa) — sinus graft works by enabling a longer implant, not by absorbing force
- Short wide bicortical: best implant-level stress of all three strategies
- 45°-tilted long implant: worst implant stress (2.3× bicortical) — avoid if alternatives exist
- Sinus cortical bone (14.144 MPa) confirms value as secondary anchor in bicortical strategy
