## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort study (Tishreen University, Syria; 34 implants, 29 patients; January 2020–September 2022) using motorized threaded bone expanders for graftless transcrestal sinus floor elevation with simultaneous implant placement; pre-operative and 6-month CBCT assessment of initial bone height (IBH), implant protrusion length (IPL), and initial bone gain (IBG).

Mean IBH 6.37 ± 0.85 mm; mean IPL 2.01 ± 0.55 mm; mean IBG 1.69 ± 0.44 mm; membrane perforation rate 8.82% (3/34); 6-month implant survival 100%; strong positive IPL–IBG correlation (significant); no IBH–IBG correlation (not significant).

Implant protrusion length (IPL) is the critical determinant of initial bone gain in graftless TSFE — initial bone height does not independently influence bone gain; selecting the implant length to ensure adequate protrusion beyond the sinus floor is more important than the pre-operative residual bone height when planning graftless TSFE.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort study from Tishreen University (Lattakia, Syria) evaluated 34 implants in 29 patients who underwent graftless transcrestal sinus floor elevation using motorized threaded bone expanders between January 2020 and September 2022. All implants were placed simultaneously with sinus elevation; no grafting material was used (blood clot only). CBCT imaging was performed pre-operatively and at 6 months post-operatively.

**Measured parameters:**
- **IBH** (Initial Bone Height): residual alveolar bone height below sinus floor, measured on pre-op CBCT
- **IPL** (Implant Protrusion Length): length of implant apex protruding beyond sinus floor into sinus cavity, measured on post-op and 6-month CBCT
- **IBG** (Initial Bone Gain): new bone formed around the protruding apex at 6-month CBCT

**Quantitative results:**

| Parameter | Mean ± SD |
|---|---|
| IBH | 6.37 ± 0.85 mm |
| IPL | 2.01 ± 0.55 mm |
| IBG | 1.69 ± 0.44 mm |
| Membrane perforations | 3/34 (8.82%) |
| Survival rate | 100% (34/34) |

**Correlation analysis:**

| Correlation | Result |
|---|---|
| IPL → IBG | Strong positive, significant |
| IBH → IBG | No correlation, not significant |

The finding that IBH does not correlate with IBG is clinically significant: it suggests that patients with limited residual bone height can still achieve adequate bone formation as long as sufficient implant protrusion is achieved. The critical modifiable factor is choosing an implant length that ensures adequate penetration beyond the sinus floor.

Membrane perforations (8.82%) were managed without aborting the procedure; all 34 implants survived to 6 months, consistent with graftless transcrestal SFE literature.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates IPL is the primary determinant of IBG in graftless TSFE with motorized expanders
- Debunks IBH as an independent predictor: pre-operative bone height alone should not determine case selection or expected bone gain
- 100% survival rate at 6 months in graftless TSFE — consistent with transcrestal graftless literature
- 8.82% membrane perforation rate with motorized expanders — lower than some osteotome series
- Provides planning guidance: optimize IPL selection, not just residual bone height, when planning graftless TSFE

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort; single center
- **n**: 34 implants, 29 patients; January 2020–September 2022
- **Technique**: Motorized threaded bone expanders (graftless TSFE); simultaneous implant placement
- **Imaging**: CBCT pre-op and 6 months post-op
- **Variables**: IBH (pre-op), IPL (post-op), IBG (6-month change at apex)
- **Statistics**: Correlation analysis (IBH–IBG and IPL–IBG)
- **Complications**: Membrane perforation recorded intraoperatively

## Results

- Mean IBH: 6.37 ± 0.85 mm
- Mean IPL: 2.01 ± 0.55 mm
- Mean IBG: 1.69 ± 0.44 mm
- Membrane perforations: 3/34 (8.82%)
- Survival: 100% at 6 months
- IPL–IBG: strong positive correlation (significant)
- IBH–IBG: no correlation (not significant)
