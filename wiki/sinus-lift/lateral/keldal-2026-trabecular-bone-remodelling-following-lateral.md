## Three-line Summary

Retrospective comparative study (Tokat Gaziosmanpaşa University, Türkiye; 100 systemically healthy patients, 50 allograft vs 50 xenograft, both 0.5–1.0 mm particles, treated 2015–2023) measuring trabecular fractal dimension (FD) on panoramic radiographs by the White–Rudolph box-counting method immediately postoperatively and at 3 months, 6 months and 1 year after lateral window sinus augmentation.

FD changed significantly over time in both groups (allograft p=0.021; xenograft p<0.001), with allograft significantly higher immediately postoperatively (1.3005 vs 1.2505, p=0.010) and at 3 months (1.2705 vs 1.1949, p<0.001, Cohen's d=0.747), but no difference at 6 months (1.3061 vs 1.2900, p=0.107) or 1 year (1.3054 vs 1.3011, p=0.751).

Radiographic trabecular complexity converged by 6 months despite distinct early kinetics (xenograft dipped more steeply at 3 months and recovered faster), but FD is a texture surrogate only — with 2D panoramic imaging, JPEG storage, no histology and no implant or clinical outcomes, this is not evidence of biological or clinical equivalence.

## Summary

This single-centre retrospective study asks whether the two dominant sinus graft classes leave different radiographic signatures in the healing site. One hundred patients treated with lateral window maxillary sinus augmentation between 2015 and 2023 were allocated by the material actually used — allograft (Atident®, 0.5–1.0 mm, n=50) or xenograft (Cerabone®, 0.5–1.0 mm, n=50) — and their panoramic radiographs taken immediately postoperatively and at 3, 6 and 12 months were analysed with the White–Rudolph box-counting fractal dimension (FD) protocol in ImageJ, using an anatomically anchored 50×50-pixel ROI in the central grafted region.

Both groups remodeled significantly over the year, but with different shapes. The xenograft group fell sharply at 3 months (1.2505 → 1.1949, Friedman p<0.001, five of six pairwise comparisons significant) and then climbed back; the allograft group held its FD nearly flat, with only the 3-to-6-month step reaching significance after Bonferroni correction (p=0.001). Between groups, allograft was significantly more "complex" only early — at baseline (p=0.010) and 3 months (p<0.001, d=0.747) — and the difference vanished at 6 months (p=0.107) and 1 year (p=0.751), i.e. both materials converged on FD ≈ 1.30.

For a clinician the practical reading is narrow but useful: an early radiographic dip in a xenograft site (largest at ~3 months) followed by recovery to allograft-like complexity by 6 months looks like a normal remodelling trajectory rather than failure, and the classic staged protocol of placing implants at about 6 months lands in the converged window in this dataset. The authors are, however, explicit that FD is an indirect texture surrogate with no histological, ISQ or implant-outcome arm, so the result supports "radiographically indistinguishable late" and not "biologically or clinically equivalent."

## Key Contributions

- Delivers the first within-wiki head-to-head of **allograft vs xenograft** trabecular remodelling in lateral window sinus augmentation using FD on panoramic radiographs at four time points (postoperative, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year).
- Quantifies **distinct early kinetics** — xenograft shows a deep, strongly significant 3-month trough (Friedman p<0.001, all pairwise comparisons significant except 6 mo vs 1 yr), allograft a shallow one (Friedman p=0.021, only 3 mo vs 6 mo significant) — then demonstrates **convergence** by 6 months (p=0.107) and 1 year (p=0.751).
- Places FD explicitly as a cheap, low-dose, widely available **surrogate** for microarchitecture and cautions that convergence in a texture index does not establish biological or clinical equivalence between materials.
- Documents a fully specified FD pipeline (σ=35 px blur, threshold 128, erode/dilate, skeletonize, 50×50-px landmark-anchored ROI, blinded duplicate observers, ICC 0.744–0.856) that is reproducible in routine practice without CBCT or histology.

## Methodology

**Design** — retrospective comparative clinical investigation; records from 2015–2023 at the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Tokat Gaziosmanpaşa University (ethics 25-MOBAEK-298). 600 sinus lift sites screened, 100 patients included: allograft 50 (Atident®, Ankara, Türkiye) and xenograft 50 (Cerabone®, Botiss, Zossen, Germany), both 0.5–1.0 mm. All windows covered with a porcine-pericardium collagen membrane (Jason®); no autogenous bone, PRP/PRF, hyaluronic acid or other additives; staged approach with implants ~6 months later.

**Population** — age ≥18, residual bone height <5 mm, exclusive use of one graft type, complete radiographic series, no bone-metabolism disease/medication; exclusions included smoking, diabetes or unknown glycemic control, abnormal/unavailable vitamin D, alcohol, parafunction, membrane perforation and any additional biomaterial — a deliberately narrow, systemically healthy cohort.

**Imaging and FD** — one panoramic unit (Veraviewepocs 2-D, Morita; 67 kVp, 5 mA, 7.4 s), standardized positioning; ImageJ White–Rudolph protocol with ROI fixed at 50×50 px in the central augmented sinus, anchored to roots of bordering teeth (dentate) or the maxillary tuberosity (edentulous) and to the native sinus floor vertically, then repositioned by the same landmark relationship at each time point. Two blinded surgeons with ≥5 years experience measured independently.

**Statistics** — SPSS 27; Shapiro–Wilk; Friedman + Bonferroni-corrected Wilcoxon (threshold p<0.0083 for six comparisons); Mann–Whitney U or independent-samples t-test between groups with Cohen's d; ICC(2,1) for interobserver reliability; G*Power sample size (d=0.6, α=0.05, 80% power → 45/group, inflated to 50/group).
