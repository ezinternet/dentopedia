## Three-line Summary

Translational study: a preclinical sheep model of maxillary sinus floor augmentation (MSFA) comparing an osteoinductive calcium phosphate with submicron needle-shaped topography (BCPN, MagnetOs Granules) against submicron grain-shaped calcium phosphate (BCPG) and autologous bone graft (ABG) as controls, followed by a prospective randomized controlled trial (RCT) of 10 patients with two-stage implant placement.
Both materials were highly biocompatible with direct bone apposition and bone formation as early as 3 weeks; BCPN formed significantly more bone than BCPG at the 12-week endpoint, and only BCPN reached an equivalent amount of bone formation in the available space with a greater proportion of calcified material (bone + graft material) than ABG, while clinically implant stability, bone height, new bone in trephine core biopsies and overall clinical outcome were comparable to ABG.
Osteoinductive calcium phosphate is a promising graft substitute for MSFA, but its bone-forming potential depends on the design of its surface features (needle > grain); the clinical arm is small (n=10) and this page is built from the abstract only, so no effect sizes, p-values or implant-survival figures are reported here.

## Summary

This translational study evaluates an osteoinductive calcium phosphate with submicron surface topography (BCPN, MagnetOs Granules, Kuros Biosciences BV) as a bone graft substitute for maxillary sinus floor augmentation (MSFA). A preclinical sheep model compared BCPN with a submicron grain-shaped calcium phosphate (BCPG) and autologous bone graft (ABG) as controls, and a subsequent prospective randomized controlled trial in 10 patients with two-stage implant placement compared BCPN with ABG clinically. In the animal model both materials were highly biocompatible and supported bony ingrowth with direct bone apposition, with bone formation as early as 3 weeks post-implantation; BCPN formed significantly more bone than BCPG at the 12-week endpoint, and only BCPN reached an equivalent amount of bone formation in the available space together with a greater proportion of calcified material (bone + graft material) than the "gold standard" ABG. Clinically, BCPN was comparable to ABG in implant stability, bone height, new bone formation in trephine core biopsies and overall clinical outcome. The authors conclude that osteoinductive calcium phosphates are promising bone graft substitutes for MSFA and that their bone-forming potential depends on the design of their surface features.

## Key Contributions
- Isolates **surface design** as the experimental variable: two calcium phosphates of the same bulk type, one with submicron needle-shaped and one with submicron grain-shaped topography, implanted in the same species and site.
- Reports bone formation **as early as 3 weeks** with direct bone apposition against both materials, i.e. osteoinductive behaviour is claimed for a synthetic calcium phosphate, not only for autograft.
- Shows a **significant BCPN > BCPG difference at 12 weeks** — bone-forming potential is not a property of "calcium phosphate" in general but of the specific surface feature design.
- Provides the **only ABG-matched result in this record**: only BCPN reached an equivalent amount of bone formation in the available space, plus a greater proportion of calcified material, versus the "gold standard" ABG at 12 weeks.
- Adds a small **randomized clinical validation (n=10, two-stage implant placement)** in which BCPN was comparable to ABG on implant stability, bone height, trephine-biopsy new bone and overall clinical outcome.

## Methodology

| Part | Design | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Preclinical | Animal experiment (sheep) | MSFA model; three arms — BCPN (calcium phosphate, submicron needle-shaped topography, MagnetOs Granules, Kuros Biosciences BV), BCPG (calcium phosphate, submicron grain-shaped topography), ABG (autologous bone graft) as control; endpoint 12 weeks, bone formation already seen at 3 weeks; endpoints: biocompatibility, bony ingrowth and direct bone apposition, bone formed in the available space, proportion of calcified material (bone + graft material) |
| Clinical | Prospective randomized controlled trial | 10 patients; BCPN vs ABG in MSFA with two-stage implant placement; outcomes: implant stability, bone height, new bone formation in trephine core biopsies, overall clinical outcome; registered in the Netherlands Trial Register (NL6436) |
| Institutions | — | UMC Utrecht (Oral & Maxillofacial Surgery; Regenerative Medicine Center Utrecht), Kuros Biosciences BV (Bilthoven, NL — manufacturer), Queen Mary University London (Materials Science & Engineering) |

## Limitations
- **Abstract-only record** (`full_text: false`): no effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values, implant-survival figures or methodological detail are available; any numeric claim beyond what the abstract states is unsupported.
- **Small clinical sample (n=10)**: precision and generalizability are limited, and no power calculation is reported in the abstract.
- **Translation gap**: the "gold standard" ABG equivalence is animal data; the clinical arm is feasibility-sized, and the abstract does not state the surgical approach (lateral window vs transcrestal) or follow-up duration.
- **Industry affiliation**: two authors (de Groot FG, de Bruijn JD) are affiliated with Kuros Biosciences BV, the manufacturer of MagnetOs Granules.
- **Missing outcome domains**: no long-term graft volume stability/resorption data, no cost analysis, no patient-reported outcomes in the retrieved record.
