## Three-line Summary

Prospective three-arm RCT (27 patients, 30 lateral sinus lifts, 10 sinuses per arm; Ordu University, Turkey) comparing guided piezosurgery, a dedicated lateral-window osteotomy bur and conventional rotary burs on perioperative complications and patient/clinician comfort, with VAS pain, analgesic use, facial edema, a 3-point experience scale and OHIP-14 followed to day 7.

Only membrane elevation time (p = 0.015) and surgeon's field visibility (p < 0.001) differed significantly — the guided-piezo column showed the shortest total operating time (16.80 ± 4.76 vs 22.3 ± 8.90 and 24 ± 8.99 min) and 9/10 "perfect visibility" scores; window osteotomy time (p = 0.058), total time (p = 0.135), pain, analgesic intake, edema (pre-op vs day 2 p = 0.868; vs day 7 p = 0.179), patient experience (p = 0.387) and OHIP-14 change (p = 0.912) were all null.

Guided piezosurgery buys surgeon ergonomics and visibility, not patient comfort — and because the edema and quality-of-life differences are non-significant in a 10-per-arm sample, the authors' suggestion to prefer dedicated lateral-window burs when edema is expected should be treated as hypothesis-generating.

## 세줄요약

터키 오르두 대학 (Ordu University) 단일기관 전향적 3군 무작위 대조 시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) — 환자 27명·상악동거상술 30증례(군당 10증례)에서 유도 압전수술 (Guided Piezosurgery) vs 측방창 전용 버 (Dedicated Lateral-Window Bur) vs 통상 회전 버 (Conventional Rotary Bur)를 술중 합병증·환자/술자 안위로 비교, 통증 시각아날로그척도 (Visual Analogue Scale, VAS)·진통제·안면 부종 (Edema)·환자 경험·구강건강영향지수 (Oral Health Impact Profile-14, OHIP-14)를 술후 7일까지 추적.

막 박리 (Membrane Elevation) 시간 (p = 0.015)과 술자 시야 (p < 0.001)만 유의 — 유도 압전군 열이 총 수술시간 최단 (16.80 ± 4.76 vs 22.3 ± 8.90, 24 ± 8.99분), "완벽한 시야" 9/10; 창 골절단 (Window Osteotomy) 시간 (p = 0.058), 총 시간 (p = 0.135), 통증·진통제·부종 (술전 vs 술후 2일 p = 0.868; vs 7일 p = 0.179)·환자 경험 (p = 0.387)·OHIP-14 변화 (p = 0.912)는 군간 차이 없음.

유도 압전수술은 술자 조작성·시야를 얻지만 환자 안위를 개선하지는 못함; 군당 10증례 규모에서 부종·삶의 질 차이가 비유의하므로, 부종이 예상될 때 전용 측방창 버를 선호하라는 저자 제안은 가설 수준으로 읽어야 함.

## Summary

This single-centre prospective RCT randomized 30 sinuses in 27 patients (16 men, 11 women, age 26–70; 33 eligible patients, 6 dropped for intraoperative membrane perforation) to lateral-window osteotomy with conventional rotary burs, a dedicated lateral-sinus-lift bur kit (Dentium Advanced Sinus Kit, "reamer" arm) or piezosurgery performed through a 3D-printed surgical guide. All osteotomies were followed by membrane elevation with direct sinus elevators and grafting with the same synthetic bone substitute by one surgeon, and outcomes covered intraoperative times, a 4-point surgeon access score, a 7-day VAS pain/analgesic diary, five-landmark facial edema at day 2 and day 7, a 3-point expectation-versus-experience item, and OHIP-14. Groups were balanced for age and sex. The two significant findings were procedural: membrane elevation was fastest in the guided-piezo arm (8.50 ± 3.44 min vs 15.8 ± 8.69 and 14 ± 6.04; p = 0.015) and surgeon visibility was best there (9/10 "perfect", p < 0.001), which also produced the numerically shortest total operating time (16.80 min vs 22.3 and 24.0; p = 0.135). Every patient-centred endpoint was statistically null: pain fell below 1 on the VAS by day 7 in all arms, analgesic use fell below one tablet per day by day 6, edema did not change significantly at either postoperative time point, 21 of 30 patients said the experience was better than expected, and OHIP-14 change scores were indistinguishable (p = 0.912). Membrane perforations occurred 3 times with conventional burs, twice with the dedicated bur and once with guided piezosurgery — cases that were excluded from the analysis rather than compared.

## Key Contributions

- Provides a three-arm patient RCT that isolates the **lateral-window osteotomy instrument** (conventional bur vs dedicated sinus bur vs guided piezo) while holding surgeon, graft material and postoperative protocol constant.
- Splits operating time into osteotomy vs membrane elevation and locates the technique effect in **elevation** (p = 0.015), not cutting (p = 0.058) — the guide's value is a better-placed, better-visualized window, not a faster cut.
- Reports a **surgeon-benefit / patient-benefit dissociation**: better access and shorter total time did not translate into less pain, less analgesic use, less edema or better OHIP-14.
- Adds a full patient-reported endpoint set (7-day VAS diary, analgesic counts, five-landmark facial edema, expectation-versus-experience scale, Turkish OHIP-14) that most lateral-window technique trials omit.
- Documents a raw perforation gradient of 3 (conventional) → 2 (dedicated bur) → 1 (guided piezo) among 33 treated patients, consistent in direction with the piezo/guided-window safety literature, though untested statistically.

## Methodology

Prospective, randomized (computer-generated, sealed opaque envelopes), controlled clinical trial at Ordu University, Turkey; recruitment 12 June – 14 December 2023; CONSORT; ethics No. 2023/109; retrospectively registered (NCT06601816). Randomization unit = the sinus (n = 10 per arm), 30 sinuses in 27 patients; inclusion required Cawood & Howell class V–VI posterior maxilla with residual bone height < 6 mm; patients with intraoperative membrane perforation were excluded (6 of 33). Guided arm used CBCT DICOM → STL (3D Slicer) plus intraoral scan (TRIOS 4) and MeshMixer planning to design a resin guide printed on a NextDent 5100. Outcomes: window osteotomy time, membrane elevation time, total time (chronometer); surgeon access 0–3 scale; VAS pain 6 h–7 days; analgesic consumption; facial edema from five landmark distances pre-op/day 2/day 7; 3-point expectation-vs-experience at day 7; OHIP-14 pre-op vs day 7. Sample size by G*Power (≥8 pain, ≥7 edema, ≥9 time per group; ~81–82% power, 95% confidence). Statistics: Shapiro–Wilk, one-way ANOVA/Kruskal–Wallis, repeated-measures ANOVA/Friedman, Wilcoxon/paired t, chi-square; α = 0.05; SPSS 29.
