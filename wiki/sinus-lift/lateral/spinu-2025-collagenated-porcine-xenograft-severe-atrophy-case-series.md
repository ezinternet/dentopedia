## Three-line Summary

Retrospective case series (single private clinic, Oradea, Romania; 3 partially edentulous patients aged 46–56, surgery May–November 2021) of lateral-window sinus floor elevation with a small-particle (0.25–1.00 mm) collagenated porcine xenograft and staged implant placement, with trephine core biopsies at 3.7, 4.7 and 7.5 months and a mean 46.8-month follow-up.

Mean vertical bone gain was 12.0 mm (baseline residual bone height 1.33 mm → 13.33 mm final), all 4 implants were functional with no failures at 38.3–52.2 months and marginal bone loss stayed <1 mm in the first year, while histology showed 20–30% new bone, 30–40% residual biomaterial and 30–50% connective tissue with hybrid bone on particle surfaces and TRAP-positive multinucleated giant cells at 7.5 months.

Within a 3-patient sample the material looks biocompatible, osteoconductive and slowly resorbing with stable long-term implant function, but the series is uncontrolled, single-centre and biopsy-based, so it cannot establish efficacy relative to bovine xenografts or any comparator.

## Summary

Spînu and colleagues report a retrospective case series of three patients (2 men, 1 woman; 46–56 years; mean 50.3) treated at a single private clinic in Oradea, Romania, between May and November 2021 for severely atrophic posterior maxillae with residual bone height ≤2 mm. Out of 83 patients who underwent sinus augmentation at the clinic during the screening window, only these three met every criterion — RBH ≤2 mm, no active sinus pathology, lateral-window elevation with the same small-particle (0.25–1.00 mm) collagenated porcine xenograft (THE Graft™, Purgo Biologics), non-smoker, ASA I, return for staged implant placement permitting a clinically indicated trephine biopsy, and consent. Implants were placed after 4–8 months of healing, and porcelain fused-to-metal screw-retained crowns six months after implant placement. One case involved an asymptomatic sinus mucocele enucleated through an intentionally created Schneiderian membrane perforation (Crock-eye technique), repaired with a collagen membrane, and subsequently needed an additional crestal sinus lift at implant placement plus a palatal connective tissue graft for keratinized mucosa.

The reported outcomes are uniformly favourable: vertical height rose from a mean baseline RBH of 1.33 mm to 13.33 mm (12.0 mm gain), all four implants were functional without failure at a mean 46.8 months (range 38.3–52.2), marginal bone loss stayed below 1 mm in the first year and was stable thereafter, and no sinus complications occurred. Trephine cores taken at 3.7, 4.7 and 7.5 months all showed vascularised new trabecular bone incorporating graft particles (hybrid bone), with new bone 20–30%, residual biomaterial 30–40% and connective tissue 30–50%; TRAP-positive multinucleated giant cells appeared only in the 7.5-month biopsy, which the authors read as ongoing rather than stalled degradation, and no severe inflammation was found at any time point. The authors interpret this as predictable osteoconduction and biocompatibility comparable to collagenated bovine xenografts, and they are explicit that the sample size limits generalisation.

## Key Contributions

- Pairs **human histology at three healing time points (3.7 / 4.7 / 7.5 months)** with **mean 46.8-month implant follow-up** in the same three patients — a combination that is rare because biopsies at implant osteotomy are not routine practice.
- Places a porcine xenograft in the **most extreme atrophy tier (RBH ≤ 2 mm)**, where most sinus histology series enrol 4–6 mm cases, and still reports staged implants of standard 12–13 mm length.
- Provides **direct human evidence of ongoing porcine graft degradation** — TRAP-positive MNGCs at 7.5 months alongside hybrid bone at all three time points — which is the mechanistic claim usually supported only by animal or in vitro data.
- Documents a **clinically driven membrane perforation for mucocele enucleation** with uneventful healing and 100% implant survival, adding to the argument that well-managed perforations (even deliberate ones) need not compromise outcomes.
- Publishes a per-case table (biopsy timing, implant positions, baseline RBH, final height, follow-up length) that keeps the raw case-level data visible instead of reporting only means.

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective case series (n=3), CARE-compliant, no comparator arm, no randomisation, no blinding.
- **Screening frame**: 83 sinus augmentations performed 3 May–30 November 2021 → 3 included (2 M / 1 F; 46–56 y). Inclusion required RBH ≤2 mm, absence of active sinus pathology, lateral-window elevation with the same collagenated porcine bone mineral, staged implant placement with an ethically justified biopsy, non-smoker status, ASA I, complete documentation and written consent. Exclusion: smoking, systemic conditions affecting bone healing, incomplete records, other graft materials, refusal of consent.
- **Surgery**: crestal incision with vertical releasing incisions; lateral window ~8 × 6 mm (3 mm round diamond bur); membrane elevated without perforation except in the mucocele case (intentional perforation, Crock-eye enucleation, repair with BioCover™ membrane); subantral cavity filled with ~2 g of 0.25–1.00 mm collagenated porcine xenograft (THE Graft™, Purgo Biologics) mixed with autologous blood; 30 × 40 mm resorbable collagen membrane over the window; tension-free closure with 4-0 PTFE; immediate postoperative CBCT.
- **Staging**: two-stage protocol (implant placement at 4–8 months) chosen because of reduced residual cortical height; one case received an additional crestal (internal) sinus lift at implant placement because the mucocele site still lacked height for standard implants.
- **Perioperative**: amoxicillin/clavulanate 875/125 mg q12h from 24 h preoperatively for 7 days (acknowledged as institutional practice despite limited evidence); 0.2% chlorhexidine rinse; articaine 4% + epinephrine 1:100,000 via PSA and greater palatine blocks; nimesulide 100 mg q12h; no nose blowing or closed-mouth sneezing for 2–4 weeks.
- **Biopsy and histology**: 5 mm trephine from the buccal wall adjacent to the osteotomy (new bone + xenograft, no cortical bone), taken only where clinically indicated; 10% formalin 48 h; decalcified in 10% tris-buffered EDTA at 37 °C for 7 days; paraffin sections 2–4 μm; HE, Masson–Goldner trichrome and TRAP.
- **Histomorphometry**: whole-bed "total scans" at ×200 on a Nikon Eclipse 80i with automated stage, NIS-Elements; read by one experienced oral pathologist not involved in surgery, not formally coded/blinded but unaware of clinical outcomes.
- **Follow-up**: 24 h, 1 week, 1, 3 and 6 months, then annually; CBCT immediately after surgery and after implant placement; prostheses at 6 months post-implant; outcomes = surgical events, vertical bone gain, marginal bone level, implant survival.

**How the authors read it**: the histomorphometric proportions (NB 20–30%, BSM 30–40%, CT 30–50%) are described as comparable to collagenated bovine xenograft series, and the 12.0 mm gain as consistent with or surpassing bovine, allograft and synthetic results in the cited literature (meta-analytic vertical gains 3.4–4.62 mm). The comparison across papers is descriptive only — the gain here is measured from baseline RBH (1.33 mm) to final augmented height (13.33 mm), which is not the same endpoint as the pooled "vertical gain" figures cited.
