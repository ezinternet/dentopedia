## Three-line Summary

Randomized, parallel, double-blind clinical trial (single center, Rome; 16 patients / 23 lateral-window sinus augmentations — 12 deproteinized equine bone mineral (DEBM), 11 anorganic bovine bone (ABB); 6-month healing; 23 trephine core biopsies) comparing two osteoconductive xenografts of identical 0.5–1 mm particle size before implant placement.

Newly formed bone did not differ (DEBM 22.20 ± 7.18% vs ABB 22.84 ± 7.34%, p>0.05), with marrow spaces 50.77% vs 46.20% and residual graft particles 27% vs 39.94% reported descriptively without a between-group test; interface inflammation was absent in both groups (mild infiltrate in 1 and 2 biopsies respectively).

Equine and bovine mineral behave equivalently for new bone formation at 6 months in human sinus augmentation, so graft-source choice can rest on prion/BSE risk perception, availability and cost rather than on osteoconductive performance — but with 23 sinuses, no clustering adjustment for 7 bilateral cases, no implant-level endpoint and only a 6-month horizon, this is an equivalence signal, not a proof of interchangeability.

## Summary

Grasso and colleagues randomized 23 lateral-window sinus augmentations in 16 systemically healthy, partially edentulous patients (residual crest height ≤4 mm on CBCT) to a deproteinized equine bone mineral (DEBM, Bio-Gen mix) or an anorganic bovine bone (ABB, Endobon), both 0.5–1 mm granules and both mixed with the patient's blood. The patient and the examiner who performed the histological processing were blinded to the material; the single operating surgeon was not. After six months, one 10-mm trephine core per augmentation was harvested at implant placement, embedded in glycolmethacrylate, ground to ~30 µm sections and analysed by histomorphometry (new bone, marrow spaces, residual graft particles as percentages of total sample volume; Mann–Whitney test, α=0.05).

New bone formation was statistically indistinguishable between the two xenografts (22.20 ± 7.18% DEBM vs 22.84 ± 7.34% ABB, p>0.05), and neither group showed multinucleated giant cells or phlogistic cells at the bone–graft interface. Marrow spaces (50.77% vs 46.20%) and residual graft particles (27% vs 39.94%) were reported as descriptive figures only, so the numerically lower residual fraction in the equine group cannot be claimed as a demonstrated resorption difference — though it agrees with the indented, resorbing particle margins the authors describe for DEBM and with ABB's classically slow resorption. The authors conclude that both materials are suitable short-term osteoconductive scaffolds for sinus lift, and explicitly contrast their null result with the Di Stefano RCT, which had found significantly more new bone with the equine xenograft (46.86 ± 12.81% vs 25.12 ± 7.25%).

## Key Contributions

- Supplies human histological and histomorphometric data for a **non-bovine xenograft source** (equine) against the bovine reference material, at identical particle size (0.5–1 mm) — controlling the one obvious material variable other than species of origin.
- Randomizes at the material level with **patient and outcome-assessor blinding**, a design strength rare in graft histology studies where the surgeon necessarily knows the material.
- Reports a **negative new-bone result** with adequate methodological transparency to be pooled with the other source-comparison trials (porcine vs bovine), rather than a brand-promotional positive.
- Documents a **biocompatibility equivalence**: no giant cells or phlogistic cells at either interface, only mild infiltrate in 1/12 (DEBM) and 2/11 (ABB) biopsies, plus perforation management with a collagen membrane in 3 cases without apparent adverse histology.
- Separates qualitative biology from quantitative endpoints honestly: DEBM particles show resorption morphology and lower residual fraction, ABB particles show new-bone encirclement and marrow colonisation by biomaterial spots — differences in *behaviour* that did not translate into a difference in *new bone*.
- Places itself explicitly against a contradicting RCT (Di Stefano) and offers a mechanistic explanation (the collagenic component of the equine xenograft) for why the two trials diverge.

## Methodology

- **Design**: randomized, parallel, double-blind (patient + histology examiner), single-center clinical trial; 6-month healing; registered NCT02865590; ethics approval Jewish Hospital of Rome (4099/7DG/O.I.).
- **Population**: 16 consecutive patients (10 male / 6 female; mean age 54 ± 7 y) with atrophic posterior maxilla, **residual crest height ≤4 mm** on CBCT. Exclusions: impaired health status, >10 cigarettes/day, bisphosphonates <3 months, head/neck radiotherapy history, ENT-confirmed sinusitis, pregnancy, cancer <1 y, BMI >30, autoimmune disease, allergy to amoxicillin/clavulanic acid/ibuprofen.
- **Materials**: DEBM — Bio-Gen mix (Bioteck, Italy); ABB — Endobon (Zimmer Biomet, USA); both 0.5–1 mm granules, mixed with blood, placed without excessive compression.
- **Surgery** (single operator): buccal bony window with round diamond burr at 40,000 rpm under saline cooling; membrane elevated from the cranial aspect; perforations classified (Vlassis–Fugazzotto) and sealed with absorbable collagen membrane (3 cases); outer window covered with double-layer collagen membrane; tension-free 5/0 monofilament closure.
- **Randomization**: STATA/12.1 randomisation software. **Allocation concealment and the exact assignment unit (patient vs sinus) are not described**, and the Methods ("23 consecutive patients") conflict with the Results ("23 procedures in 16 patients", 7 bilateral).
- **Histology**: 6-month 10-mm core with 3-mm internal-diameter trephine; formalin fixation; Technovit 7200 VLC resin; ~30 µm ground sections (3 per specimen); acid fuchsin + toluidine blue; Image-Pro Plus histomorphometry by one blinded examiner (G.I.).
- **Statistics**: Mann–Whitney test for independent variables; mean ± SD; p<0.05. No power calculation, no correction for multiple outcomes, no clustering adjustment for bilateral sinuses.

| Newly formed bone | 22.20 ± 7.18% | 22.84 ± 7.34% | p>0.05 (Mann–Whitney) |
| Marrow spaces | 50.77% | 46.20% | not reported |
| Residual graft particles | 27% | 39.94% | not reported |

**Qualitative histology**

| Feature | DEBM | ABB |
|---|---|---|
| New bone architecture | trabecular, large marrow spaces; bone bridges between particles mid-sample | trabecular bone bridging abundant residual particles; crestal particles fully encircled |
| Bone–graft interface | strict contact; osteocyte lacunae adjacent to particles | no spaces; particle margins of lower density (early resorption) |
| Resorption morphology | indented particle margins; apical particles only partly surrounded | biomaterial spots with blurred margins in marrow spaces |
| Inflammation | no giant/phlogistic cells; mild infiltrate 1 case | no giant/phlogistic cells; mild infiltrate 2 cases |

**Comparative context the authors cite** (external values, not this trial's data): ABB 27.5 ± 8.9% new bone at 6 months (Nevins, 14 sinuses); DEBM 23.5% (Nevins case series); DEBM 39.84 ± 2.96% (Rivara); six-biomaterial study with equine 22.8%/30.1% vs ABB 16.1%/37.2% (no statistics); **Di Stefano RCT — equine 46.86 ± 12.81% vs bovine 25.12 ± 7.25%, p<0.05, the opposite conclusion**.

**How to read the result.** The only tested endpoint (new bone) is null and the two means differ by 0.64 percentage points — far inside the ±7% SDs of a 12-vs-11 sample. The residual-graft difference (27% vs 39.94%) is larger and biologically coherent with the described resorption morphology, but it was never tested, so it stands as a hypothesis about relative resorption rate, not as a finding. Against the source-comparison literature the trial is concordant with the porcine-vs-bovine split-mouth RCT (equal new bone, equal remnant material) and discordant with Di Stefano's equine-superiority RCT — meaning the honest state of the evidence is that *species of xenograft origin has not been shown to matter for new bone formation*, with at least one outlier trial unexplained.
