## Three-line Summary

Prospective randomized clinical and histological study (n=40, 20 per arm; Semmelweis University, Budapest; NCT04811768) comparing piezosurgical window preparation with repositioning of the autologous bony wall against rotary preparation with resorbable collagen membrane coverage, both with the same sintered bovine xenograft, and both biopsied by trephine at 6 months.

Histomorphometry of 29 evaluable biopsies found no significant between-group difference — connective tissue 39.2 ± 9.0% (BW) vs 37.9 ± 8.5% (CM), residual bone substitute 32.9 ± 6.3% vs 31.8 ± 8.8%, newly formed bone 27.8 ± 11.2% vs 30.3 ± 4.5% — and clinical outcomes (surgery time, 6/20 vs 7/20 perforations, VAS, hematoma, edema) were all non-significant (p > 0.05); only within the BW arm was connective tissue significantly higher than new bone (p < 0.01).

Bony wall repositioning is a membrane-free alternative that produced equivalent 6-month histology here, but the trial changes closure method and osteotomy instrument together, lost 11 of 40 biopsies, and reports no sample-size or equivalence margin — so this is "no detectable difference", not demonstrated equivalence.

## Summary

Molnár and colleagues randomized 40 patients needing lateral maxillary sinus augmentation (residual bone height ≤5 mm, crestal width ≥7 mm) into two arms that both received the same particulate sintered bovine xenograft (cerabone, 0.5–1.0 mm) through a 7 × 7 mm quadratic window: a **bony wall (BW)** arm in which the window was cut with piezosurgery and repositioned as an autologous barrier, and a **collagen membrane (CM)** arm in which the window was drilled out with rotary diamond burs at 400 RPM and the antrostomy covered with a resorbable porcine collagen membrane (collprotect). Six months later, crestal trephine core biopsies were taken at the planned implant sites before guided placement of 4.1 × 10 mm Straumann Tissue Level implants.

The trial found nothing to separate the two closure methods. Surgery was slightly but non-significantly shorter in BW (45.8 ± 11.3 min vs 49.2 ± 11.3 min), as were window and mucosa preparation times, and perforation rates (6/20 = 30% vs 7/20 = 35%) and postoperative VAS/hematoma/edema scores were statistically indistinguishable. Histomorphometry on the 29 evaluable biopsies (13 BW, 16 CM) produced almost identical tissue fractions in both arms, with the same rank order — connective tissue (39.2 ± 9.0% vs 37.9 ± 8.5%) > residual xenograft (32.9 ± 6.3% vs 31.8 ± 8.8%) > newly formed bone (27.8 ± 11.2% vs 30.3 ± 4.5%). The authors' only positive histological signal is internal to the BW arm, where connective tissue significantly exceeded new bone (p < 0.01), which they attribute to connective tissue carried into the graft bed within the interspaces of the reinserted bony plate. Clinically, all 40 sites healed uneventfully, the repositioned walls re-integrated completely, all perforations were ≤5 mm and repaired, no site needed re-grafting at re-entry, and all implants were placed and restored.

The authors conclude that closing the lateral window with the retrieved autologous bony wall or with a native collagen membrane yields comparable bone augmentation, and that piezosurgery plus bony wall repositioning is a reliable membrane-free option. They are explicit that the design confound — piezo in BW versus rotary in CM — prevents attributing the equivalence to either variable, and that the split-thickness flap and unmeasured lateral–medial sinus width limit external validity.

## Key Contributions

- Supplies the human **histomorphometric** comparison of the two window-closure strategies that the authors state had not previously been reported, holding graft material constant.
- Shows that the repositioned bony wall re-integrates completely in 20/20 cases with no dislocation or graft failure, and that BSM particles become embedded in native bone at the window in the membrane arm — i.e. both closures achieve a stable, contained graft.
- Disentangles a **within-arm** effect that the headline null hides: BW's connective tissue fraction significantly exceeded its new-bone fraction (p < 0.01), consistent with the hypothesis that a reinserted bony plate carries connective tissue into the augmentation area.
- Contradicts the common preconception (and Geminiani et al.) that piezosurgery is slower than rotary preparation — piezo trended faster on total surgery, window preparation and mucosa preparation time.
- Documents a 30–35% Schneiderian membrane perforation rate and attributes it to a short minimum post-extraction interval (6 weeks; most cases 6–12 weeks), with all perforations ≤5 mm and none affecting 6-month hard-tissue quality.
- Confirms the same xenograft's osteoconductive performance in a fourth clinical context (~28–30% new bone at 6 months), bracketed by Barbeck et al. (31.63 ± 5.69%) and Rothamel et al. (25.8–49.6%).

## Methodology

- **Design**: prospective randomized controlled clinical and histological study; computer-generated randomization; parallel arms (BW n=20, CM n=20); clinicaltrials.gov NCT04811768; ethics SE TUKEB 7/2017.
- **Setting/operators**: Department of Periodontology, Semmelweis University, Budapest; all surgeries by one operator [BM]; clinical scoring by [ZSP]; histology/histomorphometry by two independent blinded-role investigators [AJ, MB].
- **Population**: 40 patients aged 18–70; ≥1 missing maxillary premolar/molar; crestal bone width ≥7 mm; residual bone height ≤5 mm on CBCT; FMPS and FMBS <20%. Excluded: smoking, systemic disease, untreated periodontitis, steroids/bisphosphonates, prior sinus surgery, extraction within 6 weeks. Baseline age 51 (SD 9) vs 48 (SD 8); FMPS 15 (SD 4) vs 14 (SD 4); FMBS 17 (SD 2) vs 15 (SD 3).
- **Surgery**: split-thickness flap; 3D guided stents for implant position and window mapping; 7 × 7 mm quadratic window 5 mm apical to the crest; sinus membrane elevated with hand instruments. BW = piezosurgery (NSK VarioSurg3), window lifted outward and repositioned after grafting. CM = 3 mm rotary diamond burs at 400 RPM, bony wall eliminated, resorbable porcine collagen membrane over the window. Two-layer closure (Dafilon 4/0, 5/0), sutures out at 14 days; amoxicillin/clavulanate 3 × 625 mg × 7 days; diclofenac up to 3 × 50 mg; 0.2% chlorhexidine × 2 weeks.
- **Re-entry (6 months)**: crestal core biopsy with 3.2/2.6 mm trephine at each planned implant site, then 4.1 × 10 mm Straumann Tissue Level RN implants by guided surgery; uncovering and screw-retained FPD 3 months later.
- **Histology**: 4% formalin 24 h → PBS → graded alcohol → paraffin → 3–5 µm sections; HE, Masson trichrome, von Kossa staining.
- **Histomorphometry**: ×10 digitized total scans; total implant area (TIA) outlined; NFB, BSM and CT areas expressed as % of TIA.
- **Outcomes**: primary = total sinus augmentation surgery duration; secondary = window and mucosa preparation duration, VAS (day 0–3), hematoma/edema (0–3 scale), and NFB/BSM/CT percentages.
- **Statistics**: paired t-test (within-group durations), Levene's + independent-samples t-test (between-group), chi-squared (perforations), ANOVA with Brown-Forsythe (histomorphometry), GraphPad Prism 8.0.2; α=0.05; mean ± SD. No sample-size calculation, no equivalence margin, no multiplicity correction reported.

## Results

**Histomorphometry — % of total implant area (mean ± SD); 29 of 40 biopsies evaluable (BW 13, CM 16)**

| Fraction | BW (n=13) | CM (n=16) | Between-group difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Connective tissue | 39.2 ± 9.0% | 37.9 ± 8.5% | None meaningful |
| Residual BSM | 32.9 ± 6.3% | 31.8 ± 8.8% | None meaningful |
| Newly formed bone | 27.8 ± 11.2% | 30.3 ± 4.5% | None meaningful |

- Rank order identical in both arms: CT > BSM > NB.
- Within BW only: connective tissue significantly higher than newly formed bone (** p < 0.01, Fig. 6).
- 11 of 40 biopsies discarded for lack of measurable material or processing difficulty.

**Healing and implant course**

| Parameter | Finding |
|---|---|
| Augmentation success | 40/40; no significant arterial injury or intraoperative bleeding |
| Perforation size/management | All ≤5 mm, all covered with a collagen membrane, all resolved |
| 6-month CBCT | Sufficient hard tissue for implant placement in all cases |
| Ridge width at re-entry | ≥7 mm in all cases; no additional grafting required in any case |

**Histology (qualitative)** — comparable in both arms: new bone deposited on BSM granule surfaces throughout the grafted volume; osteoblast-lined new bone matrix in connective-tissue areas (healing still in progress); macrophages and single multinucleated giant cells at granule surfaces with osteoclasts nearby; no intergranular inflammation; no signs of BSM biodegradation or phagocytosis. No bone growth originating from the reimplanted bony wall was seen in these crestal biopsies (contrary to Tawil et al.), which the authors ascribe to biopsy location.

**Bottom line as stated by the authors**: closure of the access window with the piezosurgically harvested autologous bony wall or with a native collagen membrane produced comparable bone augmentation with the xenograft, without statistically significant clinical or histological differences.
