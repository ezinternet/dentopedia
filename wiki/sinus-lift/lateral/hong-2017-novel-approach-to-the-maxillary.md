## Three-line Summary

Case report (n=1, 47-year-old patient) of acute right maxillary sinusitis diagnosed 10 days after crestal (osteotome) sinus elevation, bone graft and implant placement at a local clinic.

Treatment was explantation of the infected implant, removal of infected graft material, and four irrigation sessions 4 days apart through a canine-fossa antrostomy with a tube drain plus 21 days of antibiotic/decongestant/mucolytic/NSAID therapy; headache resolved immediately after graft/implant removal, no pus remained after four sessions, and CT at 3.5 months was conclusive of recovery.

The authors present the technique as a patient-friendly, office-based alternative to Caldwell-Luc/ESS, but this is a single uncontrolled case with a blind technique, so the claimed advantages are not tested in the paper.

## 세줄요약

증례보고 (n=1, 47세): 지역 의원에서 치조정접근 (crestal, osteotome) 상악동 거상·골이식·임플란트 식립 10일 후 우측 급성 상악동염으로 의뢰됨.

감염된 임플란트 제거 및 감염 이식재 제거 후, 견치와 (canine fossa) 상악동 개창 (antrostomy)을 통한 관류를 4일 간격 4회 시행하고 21일간 약물 (항생제·충혈제거제·점액용해제·NSAIDs) 투여; 이식재·임플란트 제거 직후 두통 소실, 4회 관류 후 농 축적 없음, 3.5개월 CT에서 회복 확인.

저자들은 Caldwell-Luc/내시경 부비동 수술 (Endoscopic Sinus Surgery, ESS) 대비 환자 친화적인 외래 시술이라 주장하지만, 단일 증례·대조군 없음·맹시 (blind) 술식이라 주장한 장점은 논문 안에서 검증되지 않았다.

## Summary

This case report describes a 47-year-old patient referred with foul odor, right sinus tenderness and headache 10 days after crestal-approach sinus elevation (osteotome), bone grafting and implant placement at a local clinic. Examination showed gingival redness and swelling, pus discharge from the right nose and right lymphadenopathy; a Waters' view showed haziness of the right maxillary sinus consistent with acute sinusitis. The infected implant was explanted and the sinus was irrigated via a lateral antrostomy through the canine fossa, with a tube drain and a flexible silastic suction tip placed in the ipsilateral nostril to prevent saline from escaping through the nose and mouth. After four irrigation sessions and 21 days of medication, pus was no longer present and CT at 3.5 months indicated recovery; the authors propose the method as an office-based, patient-friendly irrigation technique usable in any odontogenic maxillary sinusitis.

## Key Contributions

- Describes an endoscope-free, office-based irrigation route for post-graft sinusitis: canine-fossa antrostomy, tube drain sutured to the gingiva, and an ipsilateral nasal silastic suction tip positioned toward the ostio-meatal complex.
- Reports that culture-guided medical therapy plus source removal (implant and infected graft) was sufficient in this single case, with alpha-hemolytic *Streptococcus viridans* isolated.
- Frames the technique as an alternative to Caldwell-Luc and ESS for odontogenic sinusitis, while conceding that it is a blind technique requiring accurate sinus anatomy knowledge.

## Methodology

- **Design**: single case report, no comparator.
- **Patient**: 47 years old (sex not stated in the supplied text); crestal osteotome sinus elevation + graft + implant 10 days earlier.
- **Diagnosis**: clinical findings plus Waters' view.
- **Surgery**: explantation of the infected implant; antrostomy via the canine fossa; syringe aspiration of pus for culture; suction/irrigation tip to remove infected graft; tube drain sutured to gingiva; silastic suction in the right nostril; copious saline irrigation.
- **Schedule**: four sessions 4 days apart; drain removed on the last day.
- **Drugs**: amoxicillin 500 mg, pseudoephedrine hydrochloride 60 mg, carbocisteine 750 mg and NSAIDs, three times daily for 21 days.
- **Follow-up**: CT at 3.5 months.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Headache | Relieved immediately after removal of infected graft material and implant |
| Buccal discomfort / pus | Discomfort diminished and no pus accumulation after four irrigation sessions |
| Culture (day 1) | Alpha-hemolytic *Streptococcus viridans* |
| CT at 3.5 months | Radiolucency in the infected right maxillary sinus (legend: both sinuses), read as conclusive of recovery |
| Patient comfort | Abstract states satisfaction with less discomfort during irrigation; no scale or number reported |
| Recurrence beyond 3.5 months | Not reported |

Literature context quoted by the authors (not this study's data): odontogenic etiology accounts for 10-12% of maxillary sinusitis and 30-40% of chronic maxillary rhinosinusitis; iatrogenic causes were 65.7% in the Lechien et al. review; Caldwell-Luc recurrence 9-15%. Claims of lower complication rate, less blood loss, shorter operating time and lower cost versus Caldwell-Luc/ESS are asserted in the Conclusions without supporting data from this case.
