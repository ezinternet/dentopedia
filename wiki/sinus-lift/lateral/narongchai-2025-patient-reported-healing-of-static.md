## Three-line Summary

Randomized controlled trial (n = 40 patients, 20 freehand vs 20 static computer-assisted, SCA-SFA) in the posterior maxilla with residual bone height 1–5 mm, testing whether a 3D-printed lateral-window guide improves patient-reported healing (VAS 0–10 daily days 1–7 and day 14), complications, wound healing and operative time.

PROMs and intra/postoperative complications were equivalent, except significantly more swelling with the guide on day 2 (VAS 6.85 ± 2.80 vs 4.95 ± 2.85, p = 0.04), while the guide shortened window osteotomy from 18.56 ± 12.17 to 11.43 ± 4.75 min (p = 0.022) and total surgery from 69.89 ± 20.08 to 56.24 ± 16.01 min (p = 0.023).

Guided lateral window osteotomy buys operative time, not patient comfort — it is worth it only when time is the priority and cost is not, and full-frame guides should be trimmed superiorly to avoid the extra flap reflection that drove the early swelling.

## Summary

This single-center randomized controlled trial from Chulalongkorn University (Bangkok) compared conventional freehand lateral window sinus floor augmentation with static computer-assisted surgery in 40 patients (20 per arm, no dropouts) whose residual bone height was 1–5 mm. Patients recorded bleeding, swelling, pain, bruising and sinus symptoms on a 0–10 VAS daily for a week and at day 14, while complications and wound healing were assessed at 2 and 4 weeks and every operation was video-timed. No patient-reported measure or complication rate differed between arms except swelling, which was higher in the guided group on day 2 (6.85 vs 4.95, p = 0.04) — the day swelling peaked overall — plausibly because seating a full-frame guide forced additional superior flap reflection and, non-significantly, longer releasing incisions. The guide did deliver the operative benefit the technology promises: lateral window osteotomy was about 40% faster (11.4 vs 18.6 min) and total surgery about 20% shorter (56.2 vs 69.9 min), robust to multivariate adjustment and to exclusion of a 17-min guide-adjustment outlier. Clinically, static guidance is a time-and-consistency tool rather than a comfort intervention, and its value concentrates in anatomically complex cases (intraosseous arterial branches, septa, adjacent roots) — provided the guide design is made less invasive.

## Key Contributions

- First randomized evidence on **patient-reported healing** after guided vs freehand lateral window osteotomy: equivalent PROMs and complications, with one negative signal (day-2 swelling).
- Quantifies a **time trade-off**: osteotomy 11.43 ± 4.75 vs 18.56 ± 12.17 min (p = 0.022); total surgery 56.24 ± 16.01 vs 69.89 ± 20.08 min (p = 0.023).
- Identifies a **fixable design flaw**: a guide spanning the full flap footprint demands extra superior reflection (and trend-level longer releasing incisions), driving early swelling — trim the superior outline.
- Shows **operator experience outranks the guide** for total surgical time (specialists ≈ 15 min faster vs ≈ 12 min saved by SCA-SFA), with only ~18% of variance explained.
- Documents real-world guide workflow risk: misfit needing extraoral adjustment in 5/20 cases, one consuming 17 min — enough to erase the expected gain.

## Methodology

Randomized, examiner-assessed, two-arm controlled trial (computerized block randomization of 4, sealed opaque envelopes; TCTR20230427005; CONSORT). Forty ASA I–II adults ≥ 21 years needing 1–2 posterior maxillary implants with RBH 1–5 mm were allocated to freehand SFA (n = 20) or SCA-SFA (n = 20); sample size derived from published day-2 pain scores (effect size 1.39, power 0.95) with 30% inflation for dropout.

The guided arm fused CBCT (Dentiiscan) with an intraoral scan (Trios 3) in coDiagnostiX, designing a resin guide (Straumann CARES P10+, autoclaved) with a frame for the lateral window, supported by ≥ 4 teeth and/or teeth + bone; the freehand arm located the window with a periodontal probe and premeasured CBCT dimensions. Both arms used a full-thickness flap, rotary or piezo osteotomy, Schneiderian membrane elevation, deproteinized bovine bone, a collagen membrane over the window and tension-free 4/0 absorbable closure; two specialists (> 100 sinus procedures) and two residents (< 20, supervised) were distributed across arms.

**Primary**: PROMs on 0–10 VAS (bleeding, swelling, pain, bruising; nosebleed, nasal congestion, rhinorrhea, hyposmia, sinus pressure) days 1–7 and 14, plus analgesic consumption. **Secondary**: intra/postoperative complications at weeks 2 and 4, blinded examiner wound-healing assessment (κ = 0.83), video-based phase durations (implant placement subtracted from total), and operator Likert ratings of guide fit, stability, window precision, time-efficiency and vision. Statistics: independent t-tests, chi-square exact tests, repeated-measures ANOVA, univariate/stepwise multivariate linear regression, Spearman correlation; α = 0.05 with no multiplicity correction.

Additional findings: all VAS scores declined over 2 weeks (all p < 0.05 except hyposmia, p = 0.058) with **no group × time interaction**; patients judged to heal uneventfully self-reported better scores on days 3, 5 and 6 (p = 0.029, 0.034, 0.029); multivariate models — osteotomy time R² = 0.228 (SCA-SFA β = 0.399, p = 0.009; first quadrant β = 0.305), total time driven by specialist status (β = 0.358, p = 0.017) and SCA-SFA (β = 0.321, p = 0.0031), adj R² ≈ 0.18; excluding the 17-min guide-adjustment outlier, guided osteotomy + adjustment still faster (p = 0.046).

Clinical read: the swelling difference is real but small in absolute VAS terms and confined to day 2 (the natural swelling peak); the 15% minor perforation rate matches published lateral-approach figures; and the time advantage is only worth paying for when the alternative cost of operatory time is high, or in complex anatomy (arterial branches, septa, adjacent roots) where guide precision matters more.
