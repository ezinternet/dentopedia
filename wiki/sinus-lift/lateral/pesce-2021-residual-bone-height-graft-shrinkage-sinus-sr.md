## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42020199261; PubMed/MEDLINE + Scopus + CENTRAL to January 2021) of 21 RCTs — 412 patients, 533 maxillary sinuses — testing whether residual bone height and vertical graft size, rather than graft material alone, drive new bone formation and graft shrinkage after lateral sinus augmentation.

At 6 months, xenograft shrank least (7.30 ± 15.49%) and autogenous bone most (41.71 ± 12.63%; AU vs XG p=0.009, AG 30.23 ± 1.61% vs XG p=0.048, AP 27.82 ± 15.58%, AU+AP 26.68 ± 11.03%); new bone formation was independent of residual bone height (slope +2.11%/mm, 95% CI −2.19 to 6.43, R²=0.04, p=0.32) but fell by 1.56% for every additional millimetre of postoperative vertical height (95% CI −2.50 to −0.61, R²=0.33, p=0.0023).

Graft height is the one variable the surgeon controls at the moment of augmentation — but with 3/21 trials at low risk of bias, no meta-analysis possible, and study-level (not patient-level) regression, this is a directional signal favouring "graft less", not a validated height threshold.

## Summary

Pesce and colleagues performed a PRISMA-guided systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42020199261) of 21 RCTs published up to January 2021 — 412 patients and 533 maxillary sinuses, 620 implants — to test whether the *dimensional* baseline variables of a lateral sinus augmentation, namely residual bone height (RBH) and the vertical size of the graft, determine how much new bone forms and how much the graft shrinks. A meta-analysis of graft shrinkage was pre-specified but abandoned because no material comparison was supported by more than two studies; the design therefore delivers two descriptive evidence streams instead of pooled effects — 13 histomorphometric studies for NBF and 11 CBCT studies for volumetric change.

The two regressions point in opposite directions with respect to what the surgeon can control. NBF was essentially independent of preoperative RBH (slope +2.11% per mm, 95% CI −2.19 to 6.43, R²=0.04, p=0.32) but decreased significantly as the graft got taller (slope −1.56% per mm of postoperative height, 95% CI −2.50 to −0.61, R²=0.33, p=0.0023), a trend that was even steeper within alloplast (R²=0.37, n=6) and xenograft (R²=0.48, n=11) subgroups. Volumetric contraction at 6 months separated the material classes cleanly: xenograft 7.30 ± 15.49%, AU+AP 26.68 ± 11.03%, AP 27.82 ± 15.58%, AG 30.23 ± 1.61%, autogenous bone 41.71 ± 12.63%, with significance only for AU vs XG (p=0.009) and AG vs XG (p=0.048). Only 10 of 620 implants failed, with no material-class pattern.

The authors read the pattern biologically: bone is deposited centripetally from the peripheral walls of the augmented cavity, so a taller graft increases the distance from the vascular source to the apical core, where residual particles become fibrous-encapsulated (consistent with Beck et al.). They therefore push back on the "over-augment to pre-compensate for shrinkage" habit, arguing the compensation is partly self-defeating. The clinical claim they make is deliberately narrow — minimizing augmentation volume may benefit graft healing and stability, especially for alloplast and xenograft — and they flag that no existing RCT, review or meta-analysis has tested vertical bone gain against histomorphometric outcome directly.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes the sinus graft decision from material selection to **graft quantity**: quantifies NBF as a function of graft height and shows the height effect (p=0.0023) where the material-only framework had no such variable.
- Provides a clean **shrinkage ladder by material class** from 11 CBCT RCTs, with xenograft (7.30%) an order of magnitude more stable than autogenous bone (41.71%) at the same 6-month horizon.
- Demonstrates the RBH null: preoperative bone height — a variable that dominates technique-selection algorithms — did not predict histomorphometric new bone formation.
- Reports its own precision limits explicitly (no meta-analysis possible; 3/21 low RoB), making the page usable as a "direction of effect" source rather than an effect-size source.
- Adds implant-failure and complication denominators at review level (10/620 failures; membrane perforation as the dominant intraoperative event).
- Supplies a mechanistic bridge to the graft-consolidation literature (distance-from-floor gradient; Schneiderian membrane osteogenic capacity) that explains *why* taller grafts underperform.

## Methodology

- **Design**: systematic review without meta-analysis (meta-analysis pre-specified with a ≥3-studies-per-comparison trigger, never met). PROSPERO CRD42020199261; PRISMA.
- **Databases and search**: PubMed/MEDLINE (2,641), Scopus (1,090), Cochrane CENTRAL (279) — searched April 2020 to January 2021; no year or language exclusion at search stage; hand search of 18 implant/OMFS journals plus reference lists, ClinicalTrials.gov, CenterWatch, ClinicalConnection, Greylit and OpenGrey.
- **Eligibility**: RCTs only (parallel or split-mouth) with a test and control group; ≥6-month postoperative follow-up; ≥5 patients treated; English. Excluded non-randomized designs, case series, retrospective/cohort studies, reviews, animal and in vitro work, duplicate data, trans-sinus implants, and short-vs-long implant studies.
- **Screening**: two independent reviewers (G.G., L.M.), disagreements adjudicated by a third (P.P.).
- **Sample**: 21 RCTs (references [39]–[59]); 8 parallel, 11 split-mouth, 2 mixed. 412 patients, 533 sinuses — 319 sinuses in the NBF analysis, 301 in the shrinkage analysis (results section reports 11 RCTs / 302 sinuses). 620 implants.
- **Extraction**: Excel sheet covering design, country, sponsorship, operators, smoking, patients/sinuses, implant number and timing, follow-up, graft class (AU, XG, AG, AP, ATG, BIO, SH), membrane use, failures, complications, RBH, volumetric gain, histomorphometry. Missing data sought by e-mail from 10 corresponding authors; 3 replied.
- **Outcomes**: histomorphometric NBF%; CBCT volumetric contraction at ≥6 months (% of immediate postoperative volume); implant failure; postoperative complications.
- **Statistics**: GraphPad Prism 5.1; simple linear regression of study-level mean NBF% on mean RBH, then on postoperative bone height (RBH + vertical graft dimension); material-specific regressions only for AP (n=6) and XG (n=11); unpaired t-test for shrinkage comparisons; α=0.05.
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane Handbook domains (random sequence, allocation concealment, performance, detection, attrition, reporting), low/unclear/high.

## Results

**PRISMA flow**: 4,010 records → 661 duplicates → 3,349 screened → 3,135 excluded at title/abstract → 214 full texts → 193 excluded → **21 RCTs included**.

**Graft volumetric contraction at 6 months (CBCT)**

| Material | Studies | Volume reduction (%) | vs XG |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xenograft (XG) | 5 | 7.30 ± 15.49 | — |
| Autogenous + alloplast (AU+AP) | 2 | 26.68 ± 11.03 | NS |
| Alloplast (AP) | 6 | 27.82 ± 15.58 | NS |
| Allograft (AG) | 3 | 30.23 ± 1.61 | p=0.048 |
| Autogenous (AU) | 4 | 41.71 ± 12.63 | p=0.009 |

Composite groups studied only once (AG+XG, XG+AP, XG+AU) were listed but not compared.

**NBF determinants (13 histomorphometric studies, study-level linear regression)**

| Predictor | Slope (% NBF per mm) | 95% CI | R² | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Residual bone height (RBH) | +2.11 | −2.19 to 6.43 | 0.04 | 0.32 (NS) |
| Postoperative vertical height (RBH + graft) | −1.56 | −2.50 to −0.61 | 0.33 | 0.0023 |

**Material-specific NBF-vs-height regression**

| Subgroup | Studies | R² |
|---|---|---|
| Alloplast (AP) | 6 | 0.37 |
| Xenograft (XG) | 11 | 0.48 |

Both regressions nearly overlap with each other and sit steeper than the overall model.

**Biopsy timing**: 6 months in 10 studies [39,43,44,45,47,48,50,53,56,59]; 9 months [41]; 8 months [52]; 6–9 months [40].

**Implant failures and complications**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Implants placed | 620 |
| Failures | 10 (3 AG, 3 AP, 3 XG; 1 unspecified in Flichy-Fernández excluded from attribution) |
| Dominant intraoperative event | Schneiderian membrane perforation (4 Da Silva; 5 β-TCP vs 8 HA/β-TCP Jelusic; 2 Bio-Oss sites Panagiotou; 4 AU vs 2 AP de Lange; 2 Kuhl) |
| Postoperative infection | Bilateral sinus infection resolved with antibiotics (Chackartchi; Pereira) |

**Risk of bias**: low overall in 3 studies [50,53,56]; allocation concealment unclear in 10 and high in 6 [41,46,48,55,57,59]; detection bias high in 2 [44,57]; attrition and reporting bias low in all 21.

**Reading the page's bottom line**: the shrinkage ranking is descriptive and the regression is ecological, but the direction is consistent with the histological gradient literature — more height means more apical graft far from the vascular floor, hence less vital bone per unit volume. The actionable claim is a constraint on over-augmentation, not a numeric height limit.
