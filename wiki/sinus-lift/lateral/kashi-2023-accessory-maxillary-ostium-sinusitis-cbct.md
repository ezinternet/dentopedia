## Summary

This cross-sectional CBCT study of 244 scans separated two ostium-level findings that are easy to conflate on radiographs: the **accessory maxillary ostium (AMO)** — an extra opening in the fontanelle — versus patency/obstruction of the **primary maxillary ostium (PMO)**, the natural sinus drainage pathway. Scans were split into a maxillary-sinusitis group (mucosal thickening > 2 mm) and a normal group (< 2 mm), then scored for AMO, PMO obstruction, and paranasal anatomical variations.

The clinically load-bearing result: **AMO carried no significant association with maxillary sinusitis (p = 0.104), whereas PMO obstruction was significantly more frequent in the sinusitis group (p < 0.001).** In other words, it is drainage-pathway obstruction — not the presence of an accessory opening — that tracks with sinus pathology. AMO also showed no significant correlation with any anatomical variation in either group.

Two turbinate variants (paradoxical concha, p < 0.001; bifid concha, p = 0.017) were paradoxically more frequent in the **normal** group, while Haller cell, nasal septal deviation, and concha bullosa did not differ between groups. AMO presence was gender-dependent (p = 0.013), being more frequent in females within the normal group and in males within the sinusitis group.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that **PMO obstruction, not AMO**, is the ostium-level correlate of maxillary sinusitis on CBCT — reframing which finding matters for pre-sinus-lift/pre-implant sinonasal screening.
- Reports AMO as a common variant unassociated with sinusitis (p = 0.104) or with any measured paranasal anatomical variation.
- Documents a gender effect on AMO presence (p = 0.013).
- Finds paradoxical concha and bifid concha more frequent in the normal group, cautioning against assuming every turbinate variant is pathogenic.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Cross-sectional retrospective CBCT evaluation.
- **Sample**: 244 CBCT scans — 134 F (54.9%) / 110 M (45.1%); mean age 34.16 ± 19.01 y.
- **Groups**: sinusitis (mucosal thickening > 2 mm) vs normal (≤ 2 mm).
- **CBCT scoring**: presence/absence of AMO; PMO patency vs obstruction; anatomical variations (Haller cell, nasal septal deviation, concha bullosa, paradoxical concha, bifid concha).
- **Statistics**: independent t-test, Pearson Chi-square, Fisher's exact test; alpha = 0.05.

## Results

| Finding | Result | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| AMO ↔ maxillary sinusitis | No significant correlation | 0.104 |
| PMO obstruction (sinusitis vs normal) | Significantly higher in sinusitis group | < 0.001 |
| Paradoxical concha | Significantly higher in normal group | < 0.001 |
| Bifid concha | Significantly higher in normal group | 0.017 |
| Haller cell / septal deviation / concha bullosa | No significant difference | > 0.05 |
| AMO ↔ any anatomical variation | No significant correlation | > 0.05 |
| Gender effect on AMO presence | Significant | 0.013 |

- Gender pattern: AMO more frequent in females (normal group) and males (sinusitis group).
- Clinical takeaway: on pre-sinus-lift CBCT, prioritize assessment of PMO/ostiomeatal-complex patency over cataloguing accessory ostia when judging sinus-drainage risk.
