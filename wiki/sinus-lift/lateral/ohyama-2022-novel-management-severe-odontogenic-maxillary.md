## One-line Summary

Single case report (54-year-old man) of severe odontogenic maxillary sinusitis (OMS) from underfilled teeth #25/#26 that spread to the frontal sinus; treated by conservative maxillary sinus management (CMSM) — Caldwell–Luc access with mucosa preservation, a large inferior-meatus nasoantral window, plus apicoectomy and retrograde filling — with complete recovery, an open window, and preserved teeth and sinus function at 2 years.

## Summary

Odontogenic maxillary sinusitis (OMS) is a more severe condition than ordinary nasal sinusitis, and its causative site lies distally, *below* the natural ostium, with pus pooling in the inferior nasal meatus by gravity. The two established surgical options each have a pathophysiologic weakness: classical maxillary sinus radical surgery (MSRS) strips the maxillary sinus mucosa (MSM), destroying mucociliary clearance and leading decades later to a postoperative maxillary cyst (POMC), sinus deformation, and wall sclerosis; endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) relies on the natural ostium, which gives an inadequate excretory/ventilation route and **recloses easily**, and cannot treat the dental source.

This report presents "conservative maxillary sinus management" (CMSM, developed by Yokoo et al.): a Caldwell–Luc approach that **preserves the thickened MSM**, creates a **large nasoantral window in the inferior nasal meatus** for gravity-appropriate drainage and ventilation, and simultaneously performs **apicoectomy + retrograde root-end filling** of the causative teeth. In a 54-year-old man whose OMS had spread to the frontal sinus and had failed endodontic re-treatment and long-term antibiotics, CMSM achieved complete recovery at 2 years with a patent window and preserved teeth and sinus function.

## Key Contributions

- Frames OMS management by **pathophysiology**: because the causative site sits below the natural ostium and pus gravitates to the inferior meatus, an ostium-based drainage route (ESS) is anatomically mismatched and prone to reclosure.
- Preserves the **maxillary sinus mucosa** (pseudostratified ciliated epithelium) rather than removing it (as MSRS does), so mucociliary function can recover and POMC is avoided.
- Places a **large nasoantral window in the inferior nasal meatus** — vertical position guided by the palatal wall, positioned to secure drainage/ventilation while avoiding nasolacrimal duct injury.
- Adds **apicoectomy and retrograde filling** of the odontogenic source, a step impossible under ESS, allowing cure of the cause and tooth preservation in one operation.

## Methodology

- **Design**: single-patient case report (n=1); no comparator.
- **Patient/diagnosis**: 54-year-old man referred from otolaryngology; pain radiating cheek→forehead; cheek tenderness without swelling. Teeth #25/#26 (FDI) previously root-canal-treated but **underfilled**, apices exposed to the sinus on panoramic; CT showed opacification from the maxillary sinus extending to the **frontal sinus**. Endodontic re-treatment + long-term antibiotics failed.
- **CMSM procedure**: general anesthesia; 5–10 mm gingivobuccal-fold incision; mucoperiosteal flap; canine-fossa cortical bone removed for sinus access. MSM over the protruding #25/#26 apices removed → **apicoectomy**; missing filling in the buccal-distal canal of #26 → **retrograde filling with 4-META/MMA-TBB resin**. Saline irrigation; the remaining **thickened MSM preserved**. **Large nasoantral window** cut in the inferior meatus (saw and hammer atraumatically, then enlarged with bone forceps). Acromycin (tetracycline) gauze packed via the window, removed at 1 week; **clarithromycin for 1 month** postoperatively.

## Results

- At **2 years**: recovery from OMS; maxillary sinus **anatomy and function completely restored**; nasoantral window **open**; causative teeth **preserved**.
- Only single-case narrative outcomes are reported (no scores or quantitative endpoints). Authors call for larger studies to establish CMSM as a treatment option.
