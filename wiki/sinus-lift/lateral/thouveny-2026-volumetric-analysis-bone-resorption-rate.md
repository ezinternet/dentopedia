## Three-line Summary

Retrospective monocentric CBCT volumetric cohort (Toulouse 2020–2025; 41 sinuses from 153 screened) quantifying 1-year graft resorption after lateral sinus lift via 3D Slicer semi-automatic segmentation of immediate post-op vs 1-year CBCT scans.

Bone loss distribution: <10% (n=4), 10–25% (n=12), 25–50% (n=18), ≥50% (n=7); final graft volume fell from 2047.5±994 mm³ (10–25% group) to 969.2±528 mm³ (≥50% group), overall p=0.06 (ns, power-limited).

Schneiderian membrane perforations (15/41, 36.6%) did not affect 1-year graft volume when adequately managed (PRF/suturing small, Loma Linda large); PCA identified sinus morphology (palato-vestibular angle) + initial graft volume as main drivers of ≥50% resorption — anatomy outweighs perforation occurrence.

## Summary

This retrospective monocentric cohort study (STROBE) from Toulouse University Hospital (2020–January 2025) quantified 1-year volumetric bone resorption after lateral sinus lift in 41 sinuses (mean age 62.8±11.4 y; 63.4% male) retained from 153 identified cases after exclusion (duplicates, surgical revision, simultaneous implant placement, incomplete imaging, absent immediate post-op CBCT, sinus infection). Two calibrated examiners performed CBCT assessment and 3D Slicer v4.11 semi-automatic active-contour segmentation, comparing immediate post-operative and 1-year scans in mm³.

Relative volumetric loss was classified into clinical thresholds: <10% (n=4), 10–25% (n=12), 25–50% (n=18), ≥50% (n=7). No significant differences were found across groups for age, sex, initial bone height (2.3±1.4 to 3.8±2.3 mm, p=0.70), initial graft volume (1809±1271 to 2529±1271 mm³, p=0.64), membrane thickness (1.6±1.5 to 3.8±7.2 mm, p=0.81), palato-vestibular angle (105.1±19.1 to 126.9±18.8°, p=0.09), septa presence, post-operative infection, or GBR (all p>0.05). Final graft volume decreased with loss category (2047.5±994 → 969.2±528 mm³) with an overall ANOVA at p=0.06 (ns); exploratory post-hoc comparison of 10–25% vs ≥50% groups reached p<0.05 (uncorrected), attributed partly to limited statistical power.

Membrane perforation occurred in ~one-third of cases (Table 1: 7 small + 8 large = 15/41, 36.6%) and its frequency was comparable across groups (p=0.14). Small perforations managed with PRF or simple suturing appeared in all categories except the ≥50% group; large perforations managed with the Loma Linda technique were slightly more frequent in the ≥50% group. PCA identified PC1 (23.8% variance) — initial graft volume + palato-vestibular angle — as the component driving ≥50% loss; PC2 (18.6%, membrane thickness + minimal residual bone height) and PC3 (15.7%, initial bone volume + palato-vestibular angle) showed no distinct clustering. Conclusion: 1-year resorption is multifactorial; adequately repaired perforations do not compromise graft stability, while sinus anatomical configuration and initial graft volume dominate advanced resorption.

## Key Contributions

- Standardized CBCT volumetric quantification (3D Slicer, mm³) of 1-year graft resorption specifically comparing perforation vs control groups — first at the volumetric level (prior evidence: implant survival in [[sinus-lift/lateral/diaz-olivares-2021-schneiderian-membrane-perforation-sinus-lift]], implant loss in [[sinus-lift/lateral/soares-2024-sinus-membrane-perforation-repair-review]]).
- Repaired perforations (36.6% of cases) showed no independent effect on final graft volume — repair technique (PRF/suture for small, Loma Linda for large) matters more than perforation occurrence.
- PCA evidence that sinus morphology (palato-vestibular angle) + initial graft volume are the primary drivers of ≥50% volumetric loss, independent of perforation.
- Clinically actionable resorption thresholds: ~44% of sinuses lost 25–50%, ~17% lost ≥50% within the first year — extending the anatomical risk literature (septa·wall thickness, [[sinus-lift/lateral/yang-2024-sinus-septa-wall-thickness-perforation-risk]]) to volumetric outcomes.

## Methodology

Retrospective monocentric cohort (STROBE), Toulouse University Hospital Oral Surgery Unit, 2020–Jan 2025. Identification via billing codes (GBBA002 unilateral, GBBA364 bilateral) cross-checked against surgical reports; 41/153 sinuses analyzed. Exclusion: duplicates, surgical revision (lateral/crestal approaches), simultaneous implant placement, incomplete/poor imaging, absent immediate post-op CBCT, pre-existing sinus infection. Preoperative CBCT variables: septa presence/orientation, septal angulation (<30°/≥30°), edentulism type (bounded/non-bounded), membrane thickness (<2/≥2 mm), lateral wall thickness (<5/≥5 mm). Perforations classified small (<5 mm) / large (≥5 mm). Grafts: allogeneic (Biobank®) or xenogeneic (Bio-Oss®). Volumetry: 3D Slicer v4.11, semi-automatic active-contour segmentation in axial/sagittal/coronal planes, ROI = grafted bone volume (mm³). Statistics: one-way ANOVA + Bonferroni post-hoc (when assumptions met), Kruskal–Wallis/Mann–Whitney U otherwise, Fisher's exact test for qualitative variables, principal component analysis (PCA); significance p<0.05 two-tailed.

## Clinical Takeaways

- Expect meaningful volumetric loss within the first year: 25–50% in ~44% (18/41) and ≥50% in ~17% (7/41) of lateral sinus lifts — plan graft volume accordingly (autogenous resorbs faster than allogeneic/xenogeneic per literature).
- A repaired perforation should not change prognosis for graft volume — the repair quality (PRF/suturing, Loma Linda for large) is what matters; aligns with survival-level evidence ([[sinus-lift/lateral/diaz-olivares-2021-schneiderian-membrane-perforation-sinus-lift]], [[sinus-lift/lateral/soares-2024-sinus-membrane-perforation-repair-review]]).
- Preoperative CBCT: pay attention to palato-vestibular angulation, not just septa/wall thickness — more open sinus angles together with larger initial graft volume trended toward advanced resorption.
- Interpret p=0.06 results with caution: monocentric, n=41, exploratory post-hoc only.

## Limitations

- Monocentric retrospective design; 41/153 sinuses retained (selection on imaging completeness).
- Limited statistical power (overall ANOVA p=0.06; post-hoc uncorrected, exploratory).
- Semi-automatic segmentation is operator-dependent (mitigated by two calibrated examiners).
- No implant-level outcomes (survival/MBL); graft types (allogeneic Biobank® vs xenogeneic Bio-Oss®) analyzed as categories without counts in the retrieved text.
- Perforation management method was clinician-dependent, not standardized by protocol.
