## Three-line Summary

Split-mouth randomized controlled trial (13 patients enrolled; 11 patients / 22 sinuses analyzed) comparing small (Bio-Oss-S, 0.25–1 mm) vs large (Bio-Oss-L, 1–2 mm) deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM) in bilateral lateral-window maxillary sinus augmentation, with 6-month histomorphometry, 4-year radiographic graft-height follow-up and 3-year post-loading implant outcomes.

New bone (S 25.5% ± 7.0% vs L 23.6% ± 11.9%, P = .640), residual graft (S 19.6% ± 9.2% vs L 17.5% ± 6.3%, P = .365) and connective tissue (S 54.9% ± 9.2% vs L 58.9% ± 12.5%, P = .283) did not differ between particle sizes, but bone-to-graft contact was significantly higher with small particles (S 27.9% ± 14.8% vs L 19.9% ± 12.9%, P = .021); both groups lost ~10% of vertical graft height over 4 years (P < .01) and 100% of implants and prostheses survived at 3 years post-loading (marginal bone loss S 0.52 ± 0.19 mm vs L 0.48 ± 0.15 mm).

Particle size changes neither bone formation, graft dimensional stability nor implant outcome, so it remains a procedural-convenience variable; the only measurable advantage of small granules is a higher bone-to-graft contact (greater osteoconductivity) — from a single split-mouth trial of 11 patients (22 sinuses) available here as an abstract only.

## Summary

Krennmair et al. (2024) tested a purely technical question — does the granule size of a deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM) xenograft change the outcome of lateral-window maxillary sinus augmentation? — using a **split-mouth design**: each patient received small-particle Bio-Oss-S (0.25–1 mm) on one side and large-particle Bio-Oss-L (1–2 mm) on the other, so inter-patient variability in sinus morphology and bone quality is controlled within the patient. Thirteen patients were enrolled and 11 (22 sinuses) provided analyzable split-mouth data; after 6 months of healing, axial core biopsies were taken in the molar region for histomorphometry, followed by staged implant placement, with radiographic vertical graft heights recorded from immediately after augmentation through the 2- and 4-year follow-ups and implant-prosthodontic outcomes assessed at 1 and 3 years post-loading.

The three conventional histomorphometric endpoints did **not** differ between particle sizes: new bone (S 25.5% ± 7.0% vs L 23.6% ± 11.9%, P = .640), residual graft (S 19.6% ± 9.2% vs L 17.5% ± 6.3%, P = .365) and connective tissue (S 54.9% ± 9.2% vs L 58.9% ± 12.5%, P = .283). The one statistically significant difference was **bone-to-graft contact (BGC)**, higher with small particles (S 27.9% ± 14.8% vs L 19.9% ± 12.9%, P = .021), which the authors interpret as higher osteoconductivity of the small granules. Clinically the two materials were indistinguishable: both lost roughly 10% of vertical graft height over 4 years, with the loss concentrated in the interval between augmentation and implant placement; all implants and prostheses survived at 3 years post-loading, with comparable marginal bone loss (S 0.52 ± 0.19 mm vs L 0.48 ± 0.15 mm) and peri-implant health (S 87.5% vs L 81.2%).

Clinically this means DBBM granule size is **not** a tissue-outcome lever: choose it for procedural convenience (packability, graft volume, operating time) rather than for bone biology, with the caveat that the only measurable biological advantage of small granules is a higher BGC — an endpoint that did not translate into better implant outcomes in this trial. Because this page rests on an abstract-only record, the methodological details that would normally qualify such a claim (randomization and allocation concealment, blinding, membrane use, statistical model, power calculation, and follow-up losses) could not be verified.

## Key Contributions

- **Within-patient comparison of DBBM particle size.** The split-mouth design removes inter-patient variation in sinus anatomy and bone quality, isolating granule size as the single variable — the same comparison that [[sinus-lift/lateral/li-2023-dbbm-particle-size-sinus-floor-elevation]] pooled across four RCTs, here tested prospectively in one cohort.
- **Independent reproduction of the "particle size does not change histology" null.** New bone, residual graft and connective tissue were statistically indistinguishable between 0.25–1 mm and 1–2 mm granules, matching the pooled meta-analytic result and removing the heterogeneity caveat (I² 65.7–75.3%) that limited the SR+MA.
- **Bone-to-graft contact as a separate, discriminating endpoint.** BGC was significantly higher with small particles (P = .021) while all three conventional endpoints were null — a plausible bone-biological signal (osteoconductivity) that the pooled literature on particle size had not isolated.
- **Longitudinal dimension and clinical outcomes in the same cohort.** ~10% vertical graft height reduction over 4 years (localized to the augmentation→implantation interval) and 100% implant/prosthesis survival at 3 years post-loading with equivalent marginal bone loss and peri-implant health — evidence that particle size does not affect graft stability or implant prognosis.
- **Provenance.** Same senior author group (Krennmair G/S, Malek M, Forstner T, Weinländer M) as the 2025 bimaxillary full-arch series in this wiki, indicating a research line on graft/implant material performance in the atrophic posterior maxilla.

## Results

**Histomorphometry (6 months, axial core biopsy)**

| Outcome | Small (S, 0.25–1 mm) | Large (L, 1–2 mm) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| New bone (NB) | 25.5% ± 7.0% | 23.6% ± 11.9% | .640 (NS) |
| Residual graft particles | 19.6% ± 9.2% | 17.5% ± 6.3% | .365 (NS) |
| Connective tissue | 54.9% ± 9.2% | 58.9% ± 12.5% | .283 (NS) |
| **Bone-to-graft contact (BGC)** | **27.9% ± 14.8%** | **19.9% ± 12.9%** | **.021 (significant)** |

**Radiographic graft stability (4 years)**

| Outcome | Finding |
|---|---|
| Vertical graft height reduction over 4 years | ~10% in both particle-size groups (P < .01) |
| Timing of the loss | Predominantly between sinus augmentation and implant placement; smaller in any post-implant-placement follow-up interval |

**Implant–prosthodontic outcome (3 years post-loading)**

| Outcome | Small (S) | Large (L) | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implant & prosthesis survival | 100% | 100% | No difference (all survived) |
| Peri-implant marginal bone loss | 0.52 ± 0.19 mm | 0.48 ± 0.15 mm | No difference |
| Peri-implant health condition | 87.5% | 81.2% | No difference |

**Authors' conclusion**: small and large bovine xenograft granules produce comparable bone formation with stable graft dimensions, high implant success and healthy peri-implant conditions; small particle size yields a higher BGC and therefore higher osteoconductivity.

## Limitations

- **Abstract-only ingest** — no full text (closed access; no OA copy via PMC/Unpaywall/OpenAlex). Randomization, allocation concealment, blinding, membrane use, implant system, power calculation, statistical model, the definition of implant "success" and follow-up losses could not be verified, and no numeric tables or figures were retrievable.
- **Small sample** — 13 enrolled, 11 analyzed (22 sinuses); standard deviations of 6–14% on the histomorphometric endpoints mean the "no difference" findings are null results of an underpowered trial, not demonstrated equivalence.
- **Single timepoint histology** — 6-month axial core biopsies in the molar region only; BGC is a property of that specimen, not a whole-graft-surface measure.
- **Secondary, single-study signal** — the BGC advantage (P = .021) comes from one small trial and did not translate into any difference in implant survival, marginal bone loss or peri-implant health.
- **Long-term extrapolation** — the ~10% vertical graft height reduction and the 3-year implant results are drawn from 11 patients; generalization to long-term graft stability and microscopic bone-to-implant contact requires larger series.
