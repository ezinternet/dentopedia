## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-centre cohort (Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital, 2015–2021; 176 patients / 190 sinuses treated, 168 patients / 182 sinuses analysed) of lateral-window sinus floor augmentation with DBBM in advanced posterior maxillary atrophy (RBH ≤ 4 mm), pairing a preoperative CBCT cortical-bone classification with histomorphometry of trephine biopsies taken at second-stage implant placement.

Native trabecular bone averaged 40.01% ± 14.85% and newly formed bone 18.77% ± 5.69%; native trabecular percentage correlated positively with new bone formation (r = 0.23, P = .04), whereas age, sex, healing time (P = .06) and radiographic alveolar type (P = .82) did not.

Denser residual trabecular bone may favour osteogenesis after sinus augmentation, but the association is weak (r = 0.23, ~5% of variance) and comes from a retrospective, single-surgeon, single-graft series with no implant-level outcomes.

## 세줄요약

후향적 단일기관 코호트 (중국 상하이 제9인민병원, 2015–2021년; 환자 176명·상악동 190개 치료, 최종 분석 168명·182동): 잔존골높이 (Residual Bone Height, RBH) ≤ 4 mm의 심한 후방 상악 위축에서 탈단백우골무기질 (Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral, DBBM)로 측방접근 상악동저거상술 (Maxillary Sinus Floor Augmentation, MSFA)을 시행하고, 술전 콘빔전산화단층촬영 (Cone-Beam Computed Tomography, CBCT) 피질골 유형 분류와 2차 수술 시 채취한 골핵 생검의 조직형태계측을 결합.

원래 골소주 (Native Bone Trabecula) 비율 40.01% ± 14.85%, 이식부 신생골 (New Bone, NB) 비율 18.77% ± 5.69%; 원래 골소주 비율은 신생골 비율과 유의한 양의 상관 (r = 0.23, P = .04)이었으나 나이·성별·치유기간 (P = .06)·방사선학적 치조형 (P = .82)은 유의하지 않았다.

잔존 골소주가 치밀할수록 골형성이 촉진될 가능성을 시사하지만 상관이 약하고(r = 0.23, 설명력 약 5%) 후향적·단일 술자·단일 이식재 연구로 임플란트 수준 결과가 없어, 수혜부 골질을 예후 예측 지표로 확정하기엔 근거가 부족하다.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort asks a question the graft-material literature leaves open: does the *recipient* bone of the resorbed posterior maxilla determine how much new bone forms after sinus floor augmentation? The authors screened consecutive patients with RBH ≤ 4 mm treated with lateral-window MSFA and DBBM (Bio-Oss) plus a native collagen membrane (Bio-Gide) between 2015 and 2021, all operated by one surgeon, and analysed trephine biopsies (Ø 4.0/3.0 mm, 10 mm) harvested at second-stage implant placement 4–12 months later. Preoperative CBCT was used to classify each ridge by cortical-bone configuration — bicortical (a), unicortical (b), no cortical bone (c), or sinus floor–crestal bone fusion (d) — and histomorphometry quantified native trabecular area % in the native region and newly formed bone % in the DBBM-grafted region.

Of 190 sinuses in 176 patients, 182 sinuses in 168 patients entered analysis; 154 had complete CBCT, 147 native-bone specimens and 95 grafted-area specimens were measurable, and 139 sinuses could be correlated. Bicortical ridges were the most common pattern (42.21%), yet native trabecular percentage did not differ across the four radiographic types (P = .65) — the radiographic classification was not a proxy for histological density. Native trabeculae made up 40.01% ± 14.85% and new bone 18.77% ± 5.69%; in multiple linear regression, native trabecular % was the only significant predictor of new bone (r = 0.23, P = .04), with no effect of age, sex, healing time or radiographic type. The authors read this as support for the "creeping substitution" model in which denser native bone supplies more osteoblasts and a more stable scaffold, and argue that preoperative assessment of recipient-site bone quality could inform surgical planning and prognosis — while acknowledging the retrospective design, the 2D nature of both assessments, and the absence of vascular, densitometric and implant-level data.

## Key Contributions

- Adds the **host-bone axis** to a literature dominated by graft-material comparisons: native trabecular density, measured histologically, is the variable associated with osteogenesis, not graft brand or particle choice.
- Provides a large biopsy-based reference range for the severely resorbed posterior maxilla (native trabeculae 40.01% ± 14.85%; new bone in DBBM grafts 18.77% ± 5.69% at 8.1 ± 4.8 months).
- Empirically tests the Choucroun-type CBCT cortical classification against histology and finds **no correspondence** (P = .65) — a caution against reading cortical pattern as a density surrogate, despite substantial inter-examiner agreement (κ = 0.69).
- Documents the radiographic distribution of ridge configurations in RBH ≤ 4 mm sites (bicortical 42.21%, unicortical 25.97%, no cortex 19.48%, floor–crestal fusion 12.34%) and shows that even "fused" type d ridges retain a thin continuous mineralised zone beneath the cortical plate.
- Reports high reproducibility of the histomorphometric protocol (ICC 0.99 native, 0.98 new bone across three sections per specimen).

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective cohort (STROBE), single centre, single surgeon; IRB 2015[78] / SH9H-2021-T63-1.
- **Population**: adults ≥18 y with molar/premolar loss ≥3 months, RBH ≤ 4 mm and adequate width. Excluded: active sinus infection, uncontrolled inflammatory disease (incl. uncontrolled periodontal disease/diabetes), heavy smoking >10 cigarettes/day, head/neck RT >60 Gy, osteoporosis or bisphosphonate/steroid use, incomplete records; 4 further patients excluded for simultaneous ridge augmentation.
- **Surgery**: piezoelectric lateral window, membrane elevation, DBBM (Bio-Oss) graft, Bio-Gide coverage, primary closure.
- **Biopsy**: trephine core (outer 4.0 mm / inner 3.0 mm / 10 mm) at the grafted site at second-stage placement, 4–12 months post-MSFA; one specimen per patient, from the lowest-RBH site; 10% NBF fixation; Straumann tissue-level implants 10–12 mm.
- **CBCT**: Planmeca Promax (5 mA, 96 kV, 0.2 mm voxel, 13 × 9 cm FOV), coDiagnostiX reconstruction, within 1 month pre-op; RBH and bone-quality type read on sagittal sections along the virtual implant axis.
- **Histology**: decalcified paraffin sections 6 µm, H&E, sections #10/#20/#30; ×4 native-area overview and ×10 random grafted-area field; Image Pro-Plus 6.0 colour-contrast segmentation with manual tuning; mean of 3 sections.
- **Reliability**: two blinded experienced examiners; κ for radiographic type, ICC for histomorphometric percentages.
- **Statistics**: one-way ANOVA (native % by type); multiple linear regression (native %, new bone %, healing time, type); GEE with age/sex fixed effects and exchangeable correlation for bilateral cases; α = .05; PASW 18.0.

| Predictor | Effect | P |
|---|---|---|
| Native bone trabecula % | r = 0.23 (95% CI 0.01–0.19) | **.04** |
| Sex | NS | .59 |
| Age | NS | .70 |
| Healing time | NS | .06 |

**Reading the result**: the direction supports the biological rationale (native bone as the source of osteoblasts and vascular supply for creeping substitution), and it agrees with Reich et al.'s earlier native-vs-grafted correlation. But r = 0.23 with 95 grafted-area specimens means most of the variance in new bone formation is unexplained by native density — graft volume, sinus width, membrane behaviour and vascularity are not in the model.
