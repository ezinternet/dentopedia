## Three-line Summary

Case series of three early implant failures (all within 1 month of placement with sinus lifting and allograft bone grafting in the posterior maxilla) treated at Seoul National University Dental Hospital, with retrieved fixtures and peri-implant tissue examined by SEM, EDS, TEM, and toluidine-blue histology.

Failed fixtures showed heterogeneous surfaces, a low degree of osseointegration, organic matter coverage, no detectable bacteria, and unusual elements on the surface (Au up to 40.11% in case 3, Zr 11.10% and Tl 2.42% in single cases), while case 1 soft tissue showed neutrophils, macrophages with metal particles, osteoclasts around bone particles, and multinucleated giant cells.

The authors attribute failure to implant surface contamination and graft-material-related factors on top of sinus complications (graft displacement suggesting membrane perforation); with only three cases and no controls, this is hypothesis-generating, not evidence of causation.

## 세줄요약

서울대학교치과병원에서 상악 구치부 상악동거상술 및 동종골 이식과 함께 식립 후 1개월 이내 조기 실패한 임플란트 3례를 대상으로, 회수한 고정체와 주위 조직을 주사전자현미경 (scanning electron microscopy, SEM), 에너지분산형 X선 분광 (energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometry, EDS), 투과전자현미경 (transmission electron microscopy, TEM), 조직학으로 분석한 증례 보고.

실패한 고정체는 불균일한 표면, 낮은 골유착 정도, 유기물 피복이 관찰되었고 세균은 검출되지 않았으며, 금 (Au, 증례 3에서 40.11%)·지르코늄 (Zr 11.10%)·탈륨 (Tl 2.42%) 등 이례적 원소가 단일 증례에서 검출되었고, 증례 1의 연조직에서는 호중구·대식세포 (금속 입자 포함)·파골세포·다핵거대세포가 관찰되었다.

저자들은 상악동 합병증(이식재 변위로 추정되는 막 천공)에 더해 임플란트 표면 오염과 이식재 관련 요인이 실패에 기여했다고 보지만, 3례·대조군 없음이라 인과 증거가 아닌 가설 생성 수준이다.

## Summary

Three patients treated at the Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery Department of Seoul National University Dental Hospital had implants placed in the posterior maxilla with sinus lifting and bone grafting and lost them within about 1 month (cases 1 and 2 with Oragraft allograft; case 3 placed at a local clinic). Early failure was framed as lack of osseointegration before loading; implant removal was indicated mainly by mobility, with sinus symptoms (pain, odor, exudate, pseudocyst, air-fluid changes) preceding it. The authors report that the introduction found no prior prevalence data on acute sinusitis-related implant failure.

Patients: case 1, a 68-year-old woman with hypertension (aspirin) and diabetes, two tissue-level Stella implants at #26/#27, symptoms at 1 month; case 2, a 54-year-old man with prior chronic sinusitis and endoscopic sinus surgery, two bone-level Luna implants at #15/#16, symptoms at about 10-14 days, graft displaced into the sinus; case 3, a 30-year-old woman, one bone-level implant at #25, sinusitis at 1 week, apex cutting with endoscopic sinus surgery failed to save the implant (mobile at 2 months). All sites were re-implanted 4-5 months after removal with uneventful healing in the reported follow-up.

Retrieved fixtures were fixed in glutaraldehyde and analysed by SEM-EDS at four regions; peri-implant tissue (case 1) was analysed by TEM and toluidine-blue histology. The discussion links graft displacement (cases 1 and 2, suggesting membrane perforation) to acute sinusitis, inflammatory damage to graft osteoconduction, and failed osseointegration, and proposes that organic coverage plus Ca/P dissolution from the allograft and surface contaminants interrupted initial integration.

## Key Contributions

- Mechanistic, specimen-level look at acute sinusitis-related early failure after sinus augmentation, a topic the authors state has no prevalence data.
- Shows no bacteria on any of the three failed fixtures, which the authors read as a low likelihood of a bacterial infection etiology for these cases.
- Flags surface contaminants (Au, Zr, Tl, Si, S) and graft-related Ca/P findings as candidate contributors to be tested in larger studies.

## Methodology

- Three-case report, single institution (SNUDH, Korea), IRB approval S-D20200007, written informed consent.
- SEM at 10 kV with EDS at the upper, middle, apical fixture regions and attached bone (x10,000); wt% classified as major (>10), minor (1-10), trace (<1).
- TEM on 70-80 nm sections (x3000-10,000) and toluidine-blue light microscopy of 1 um sections; immune cells and metal particles screened.

## Results

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Timing of failure | All three within 1 month of placement (case 3 mobile 2 months after later apex-cutting surgery) |
| SEM, all fixtures | Heterogeneous surface, low osseointegration, organic matter (C, O rich), no bacteria |
| Case 1 | Bone only on upper part; attached bone without cells or lacunae; Ca low and no P; graft Ca 23.03% with no P; Tl 2.42%, Si 1.90%, S 0.41% |
| Case 2 | "Distant" osseointegration; Au 26.73% (upper) and 7.99% (apical); Zr 11.10% (middle) |
| Case 3 | Apical surface fibers and organic matter; no Ca; Au 40.11%; Si 11.86% |
| TEM, case 1 | Neutrophils, macrophages (metal particles inside), eosinophils |
| Histology, case 1 | Graft particles in inflammatory tissue, osteoclasts, multinucleated giant cells, metal particles near bone particles |

Limitations: three cases without controls, heterogeneous implant systems and patient factors (prior chronic sinusitis in case 2, diabetes in case 1), semi-quantitative EDS with possible preparation-related adsorption signals, speculative contaminant sources, and the authors' own note that direct effects of sinusitis on implant survival are unproven. The authors conclude surface contamination and graft-material components "played an important role", which exceeds what three uncontrolled specimens can show.
