## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and random-effects network meta-analysis (PRISMA-NMA, PROSPERO CRD42024607526) of 11 RCTs (283 patients, 362 lateral sinus augmentations, healing 5-10 months) comparing biphasic calcium phosphate (BCP) with autograft (AB), freeze-dried allograft (AL), beta-TCP and deproteinized bovine bone xenograft (Xeno).
Versus BCP, new bone formation was +12.33% for AB (95% CI 10.74-13.93), +5.14% for AL (3.33-6.95) and -4.14% for Xeno (-6.38 to -1.90); SUCRA ranking AB > AL > BCP > TCP > Xeno, and Xeno left 6.21% more residual graft than BCP (2.81-9.61).
BCP gives sufficient bone formation and outperforms xenograft histomorphometrically, but the evidence is mostly 60:40 HA/TCP, residual height of 6 mm or less, 5-10 months follow-up, and almost no implant-survival data.

## Summary

This PRISMA-NMA systematic review pooled 11 RCTs (283 patients, 362 lateral sinus floor augmentations) that reported histomorphometric new bone formation for biphasic calcium phosphate (BCP) versus autograft, freeze-dried allograft, beta-TCP or deproteinized bovine bone (DBB) xenograft. In the network model, autograft produced 12.33% more new bone than BCP and allograft 5.14% more, whereas xenograft produced 4.14% less; beta-TCP did not differ significantly from BCP (single trial). SUCRA ranked autograft first, then allograft, BCP, TCP and xenograft, and xenograft retained the most residual graft material. The authors conclude that BCP yields sufficient new bone and outperforms xenograft, while autograft and allograft remain superior in bone regeneration. Generalization is limited to mainly 60:40 HA/TCP BCP, residual bone height of about 6 mm or less, and short follow-up.

## Key Contributions
- First network meta-analysis placing BCP in a ranked network with AB, AL, TCP and Xeno for sinus-augmentation histomorphometry.
- Quantifies BCP's position: below autograft (-12.33%) and FDBA (-5.14%), above DBB xenograft (+4.14%).
- Residual graft: xenograft highest (+6.21% vs BCP), allograft and autograft lower (-14.71%, -28.1%), TCP similar.
- Defines the evidence gaps: HA/TCP ratio, residual height, smoking, long-term survival, cost-effectiveness.

## Methodology
Four databases (MEDLINE, Cochrane, Scopus, Embase) to 25 Nov 2024; RCTs of lateral sinus augmentation with implants reporting new bone as a percentage; combined or additive-supplemented grafts excluded. Two reviewers screened independently (268 records, 32 full texts, 11 included); RoB 2.0 appraisal (7 some concerns, 1 high, 3 low). DerSimonian-Laird pairwise models, then random-effects NMA (Stata 14.1) with global inconsistency test, node-splitting, funnel plot, SUCRA and league table. NMA covered new bone only; residual graft was analyzed pairwise (9 studies).

## Results

| Comparison vs BCP (new bone formation) | Difference | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Autograft (AB) | +12.33% | 10.74 to 13.93 |
| Allograft (AL, FDBA) | +5.14% | 3.33 to 6.95 |
| Beta-TCP | -2.26% (n.s.) | -10.53 to 6.01 |
| Xenograft (DBB) | -4.14% | -6.38 to -1.90 |

| Residual graft vs BCP (pairwise) | Difference | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Xenograft | +6.21% | 2.81 to 9.61 |
| Allograft | -14.71% | -20.59 to -8.82 |
| Autograft | -28.1% | -33.34 to -22.86 |
| Beta-TCP | -2.4% (n.s.) | -8.54 to 3.74 |

- SUCRA: AB > AL > BCP > TCP > Xeno; global inconsistency p = 0.6153; I2 = 0% except TCP; 60:40-only sensitivity analysis consistent.
- Limitations: mostly 60:40 HA/TCP, residual height about 6 mm or less, few survival data, no long-term BCP follow-up, English-only, no cost-effectiveness analysis.
