## Three-line Summary

Triple-blind randomized split-mouth trial (12 consecutive patients, 24 maxillary sinuses; Porto, Portugal, 2013–2017) comparing mandibular autologous bone with a collagenated porcine xenograft (Osteobiol MP3) in two-stage lateral-window sinus lift, with 6-month CT and trephine-biopsy histomorphometry.

Tissue height gain was 7.81 ± 2.34 mm with autologous bone vs 8.71 ± 2.15 mm with xenograft (p = 0.26; material effect p = 0.240, time p < 0.001, interaction p = 0.261), and total hard tissue was 57.31 ± 2.91% vs 56.01 ± 2.86% (p = 0.376), with no inflammation or foreign-body reaction in either group.

Equivalence at 6 months is real but weak evidence: n = 12 with no non-inferiority margin, a significant patient effect (p = 0.029) and material × patient interaction (p < 0.001), residual graft never separated from new bone, and no implant-level outcomes reported.

조직높이 증가는 자가골 7.81 ± 2.34 mm vs 이종골 8.71 ± 2.15 mm (p = 0.26; 재료 효과 p = 0.240, 시간 효과 p < 0.001, 상호작용 p = 0.261), 총 경조직 부피 (Total Hard Tissue, THT)는 57.31 ± 2.91% vs 56.01 ± 2.86% (p = 0.376)이며 양군 모두 염증·이물반응 없음.

## Summary

This single-centre randomized split-mouth trial (Faculty of Dentistry, University of Porto; NCT01836744; CONSORT-reported) enrolled 12 consecutive patients (6 male/6 female, mean age 59.7 ± 8.7 y) between March 2013 and June 2017, each of whom received a bilateral two-stage lateral-window maxillary sinus lift: mandibular autologous bone on one side (control) and a collagenated cortico-medullary porcine xenograft, Osteobiol MP3 (600–1000 µm, 10% type I/III collagen; Tecnoss, Italy), on the other (test). Allocation came from a randomized opaque sealed envelope opened intra-operatively after Schneiderian membrane elevation. A second CT was taken at 6 months, then trephine biopsies (2 mm internal diameter) were harvested transalveolarly on both sides immediately before implant placement, and 10 representative H&E slides per sample (240 slides total) were subjected to histomorphometry.

Both materials produced comparable bone augmentation. Radiographic tissue height increased by 7.81 ± 2.34 mm (autologous) and 8.71 ± 2.15 mm (xenograft) from a similar baseline (3.20 ± 0.93 vs 3.06 ± 1.13 mm, p = 0.69), a non-significant difference (p = 0.26) in which the xenograft was numerically greater in 75% of cases. Histomorphometrically, total hard tissue was 57.31 ± 2.91% vs 56.01 ± 2.86% (p = 0.376) and total soft tissue was similarly alike; histology showed active remodelling with osteoclasts and osteoblasts, vascularised new bone, no inflammatory cells and no foreign-body reaction in either group, with the autologous graft almost completely resorbed at 6 months while xenograft particles remained well integrated. The authors' key statistical observation is that graft type did not matter (p = 0.376) but patient identity did (p = 0.029), with a significant material × patient interaction (p < 0.001) — graft performance is patient-dependent. They conclude that the porcine xenograft is a valid alternative to autologous bone, sparing the donor site. The conclusion should be read against the small sample, the absence of a pre-specified equivalence margin, the failure to separate residual graft from new bone, and the absence of any implant-level outcome.

## Key Contributions

- Supplies **within-patient** evidence (both grafts in the same mouth) for autograft-versus-porcine-xenograft equivalence in two-stage lateral sinus lift — a design that cancels patient-level confounders that plague between-patient graft comparisons.
- Measures four domains in one cohort: radiological height (CT, 1 mm sagittal cuts), qualitative histology (240 slides), histomorphometry (hard vs soft tissue %), and patient preference — few graft trials report all four.
- Separates the **material effect (p = 0.376, NS)** from the **patient effect (p = 0.029)** and reports a significant **material × patient interaction (p < 0.001)**, arguing explicitly that the difference between materials is smaller than the variability between patients.
- Documents **complete absence of inflammation or foreign-body reaction** at 6 months for a collagenated porcine xenograft, in contrast to inflammatory infiltrates reported for biphasic calcium phosphate, calcium sulphate, coral HA and bioactive glass.
- Reports a patient-reported outcome that is rarely captured: 91.6% judged both sides equally good and the single preference went to the xenograft side; no patient preferred the autograft side (i.e. the donor-site morbidity bought nothing perceptible).
- Quantifies a realistic complication profile for the technique: 5/24 sinuses (20.8%) had a Schneiderian membrane perforation < 2 mm, all repaired with a resorbable collagen membrane without sequelae.

## Methodology

- **Design**: triple-blind randomized split-mouth controlled clinical trial; registered NCT01836744; ethics approval 00977 (University of Porto); CONSORT + Helsinki.
- **Sample**: 12 consecutive patients → 24 sinuses, two groups of 12. Exclusions: head/neck irradiation, immunosuppression, bisphosphonates, untreated periodontitis, poor hygiene/motivation, uncontrolled diabetes, pregnancy/lactation, psychiatric disorders, sinusitis.
- **Randomization**: intra-operative opening of a randomized opaque sealed envelope after membrane elevation — allocation was concealed from the patient and (per the authors) from outcome assessment, but the surgeon knew the assignment at the moment of grafting; the Discussion calls the design "double-blind", inconsistent with the Methods' "triple-blind".
- **Surgery (identical both sides)**: full-thickness flap with two vertical releasing incisions; lateral oval osteotomy by piezoelectric device or diamond bur; membrane elevation with curettes; graft to the desired height; collagen membrane over the window; polyamide 4.0 sutures. One surgeon performed all cases.
  - Test: Osteobiol MP3 porcine xenograft, 600–1000 µm + 10% type I/III collagen.
  - Control: autologous bone from mandibular ramus/body or chin, particulated in a bone mill.
- **Peri/post-operative**: amoxicillin 2 g pre-op then 1 g × 1 wk; 0.2% chlorhexidine rinse and gel; ibuprofen PRN; soft diet 2 wk; no nose blowing/straws; sutures out at 10 d.
- **Second stage**: at 6 months, CT + preference interview + full-thickness flap; 2 mm trephine core from both grafted sites before the first drill; submerged implants (Osseo Speed TX, Astra Tech) placed; sutures out at 10 d.
- **Radiology**: hard-tissue level = distance between coronal and apical bone level in the grafted region, averaged over 10 consecutive sagittal cuts at 1 mm intervals (Blue Sky Plan).
- **Histomorphometry**: H&E; 10 most central slides per sample; 50× images analysed in ImageJ for total, hard tissue (bone + graft) and soft tissue (collagen, fibroblasts, vessels, adipocytes, voids) areas, converted to %; 200×/400× review by a blinded expert. **Residual graft particles were not quantified separately** — the interface with residual bone was indistinguishable and remnants were minimal.
- **Statistics**: paired t-test, one-way/two-way ANOVA (Shapiro-Wilk justified parametric testing), α = 0.05, independent blinded assessor, SPSS 25.0; mean ± SD.

## Results

**Radiological outcomes (CT at baseline and 6 months)**

| Parameter | Autologous (n = 12) | Xenograft (n = 12) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline tissue height | 3.20 ± 0.93 mm | 3.06 ± 1.13 mm | 0.69 |
| Tissue height gain | 7.81 ± 2.34 mm | 8.71 ± 2.15 mm | 0.26 |
| Range of gain | 3.7 – 12.5 mm | 5.4 – 12.5 mm | — |
| Baseline–6 mo correlation | r = 0.149 | r = 0.298 | > 0.05 (both) |

Two-way ANOVA: time p < 0.001; material p = 0.240; time × material p = 0.261. Greater gain on the xenograft side in **75%** of cases; achieved height permitted **9–11 mm** implants.

**Histomorphometry (6-month trephine cores, % of area)**

| Parameter | Autologous | Xenograft | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total hard tissue (THT) | 57.31 ± 2.91% | 56.01 ± 2.86% | 0.376 |
| THT min (same patient) | 49.67% | 44.19% | — |
| THT max (same patient) | 64.07% | 64.19% | — |
| Between-slide SD | 1.29–7.03% (mean 2.91%) | not reported | — |
| Total soft tissue (TST) | reported as similar | reported as similar | not reported in text (Table 2 absent) |

- THT correlation between sides: **r = 0.546, p = 0.066**.
- Two-way ANOVA: graft type p = 0.376; **patient p = 0.029**; **material × patient p < 0.001**.
- 95% CI overlap in **7/12** patients; autologous higher in **3/12**, xenograft higher in **2/12**.

**Histology (240 slides)**

| Feature | Autologous | Xenograft |
|---|---|---|
| Remodelling stages | present | present, more evident turnover |
| Osteoclast/osteoblast activity | present | present (irregular particle lacunae) |
| Osteocytes in new bone | present | present (maturation at 6 mo) |
| Residual particles | few, fully integrated | well integrated, partially surrounded by soft tissue |
| Inflammation / infection / foreign-body reaction | none | none |

**Clinical and patient-reported**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Harvest site | ramus 83.3%, chin 16.7% |
| Preference | 11/12 (91.6%) equally good; 1/12 (8.3%) xenograft side; 0 autograft |
| Membrane perforation < 2 mm | 5/24 sinuses (20.8%), all repaired with collagen membrane |
| Major intra-/post-operative complications | none reported |
| Implant survival, ISQ, insertion torque, MBL | **not reported** |

**Reading the bottom line**: the trial supports using a collagenated porcine xenograft instead of mandibular autologous bone for two-stage lateral sinus lift on the dimensions it measured — 6-month radiological height, histology, and hard/soft tissue proportions — and it removes the donor site. It does **not** establish equivalence in the statistical sense (no margin was pre-specified and n = 12 cannot exclude a clinically relevant difference), it does not report how much of the hard tissue is residual xenograft versus new bone, and it carries no implant-level or long-term outcome.

- [[sinus-lift/lateral/jue-2025-maxillary-sinus-floor-augmentation-autogenous-bone]] — multicenter split-mouth RCT (50 patients, 198 implants) showing autogenous ramus bone and bovine bone mineral + 25% local autogenous chips give equivalent 12-month survival (92.9% vs 93.9%) and clinical success; that is the clinical-outcome analogue of the histological/radiological equivalence seen here, and both argue for avoiding the donor site.
- [[sinus-lift/lateral/del-fabbro-2025-sinus-augmentation-materials-lateral-approach]] — SR of 49 RCTs reporting implant survival 84.6–100% with no significant difference between DBBM, autogenous ± DBBM and synthetic HA/β-TCP; the present RCT contributes the histomorphometric axis that survival-based reviews cannot see.
- [[sinus-lift/lateral/harlos-2022-histomorphometric-evaluation-of-different-graft]] — RCT in wide antral cavities where xenogenic + autogenous 70:30 outperformed xenogenic + L-PRF and xenogenic alone at 8 months, i.e. the opposite ordering of the autograft question; the difference in comparator structure (all arms contained xenograft), graft, healing time and metric (absolute area vs %) means the two do not directly conflict, but together they show how sensitive this comparison is to design.
- [[sinus-lift/lateral/motimath-2026-xenograft-prf-sinus-histomorphometric]] — split-mouth xenograft study (n = 30, 60 sinuses) that reports new bone % and residual graft % separately at 9 months — precisely the decomposition the present trial could not perform, which is why its "equivalence" is coarser than it first appears.
- [[sinus-lift/lateral/nguyen-2026-histological-and-histomorphometric-assessment-of]] — prospective histomorphometric study of a composite allograft + biphasic calcium phosphate + PRF in severe atrophy; useful contrast in reporting granularity (new bone, residual graft, soft tissue as separate percentages) and in the absence of an autogenous comparator arm.
