## Three-line Summary

Split-mouth randomized controlled trial (University of Granada, Spain; 10 patients / 20 sinuses; January 2019–March 2021) comparing autologous cortical bone (ACB) mixed 20:80 with anorganic bovine bone (ABB, Bio-Oss Spongiosa 250–1000 µm) versus porcine bone mineral (PBM, Symbios Xenograft granules 0.25–1.0 mm) in bilateral staged lateral-window maxillary sinus floor augmentation, evaluated by CBCT, trephine-biopsy histology/histomorphometry, immunohistochemistry, TEM and mRNA at 6 months.

No group difference in any radiographic, histomorphometric or most immunohistochemical outcome — grafted-area mineralized tissue 29.24% (ABB) vs 32.51% (PBM) (p=.570), remnant biomaterial 31.00% vs 31.21% (p>.999), similar vertical graft resorption; the only between-group signals were more osteoid lines in ACB+ABB (5.89 vs 2.50 per mm², p=.031), higher ALP mRNA in ACB+PBM (2.13 vs 1.00, p=.011), and more osteocytes/leukocytes/vessels in ACB+ABB (CD44 p=.031, CD45 p=.047, CD34 p=.031).

Clinical implication: a porcine xenograft behaves like DBBM in sinus augmentation, so it is a defensible alternative where bovine material is undesirable — but the trial enrolled 10 patients against its own calculated requirement of 18, reported no implant-survival endpoint, and lost the registered 12- and 18-month CBCT re-measurements to COVID-19, so long-term remodeling and resorption remain unproven.

## Summary

This split-mouth randomized controlled trial (CONSORT-guided; Ethics 480/CEIH/2018; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03797963) tested whether a porcine-derived xenograft behaves like the field's most-studied bovine xenograft when used for maxillary sinus floor augmentation (MSFA). Ten consecutive patients at the University of Granada (January 2019 – March 2021) needing bilateral staged sinus augmentation (residual bone height <5 mm, full maxillary edentulism or Kennedy class I) each received **both** materials: autologous cortical bone (ACB) scraped from the lateral sinus wall, mixed at ~20:80 volume ratio with either anorganic bovine bone (ABB; Bio-Oss® Spongiosa, 250–1000 µm) on one side or porcine bone mineral (PBM; Symbios® Xenograft granules, 0.25–1.0 mm) on the contralateral side, covered by a resorbable collagen membrane. Sequence and side were randomized per patient with allocation concealed by a third party; the surgeon was blinded until applying the material and all evaluators and patients were blinded.

After 6 months of healing, trephine biopsies taken at implant placement were analysed by histology/histomorphometry, immunohistochemistry, TEM and RT-qPCR, and CBCT gave the radiographic primary outcome (bone height gain). **Every radiographic measure, every histomorphometric compartment and most immunohistochemical markers were statistically indistinguishable between the two materials.** The only between-group signals were: more osteoid lines in ACB+ABB grafted areas, higher ALP mRNA in ACB+PBM, and more osteocytes, leukocytes and vessels in ACB+ABB — mutually offsetting directions that the authors read as a difference in remodelling stage rather than in outcome. The authors describe this as the first clinical study to publish a complete dataset on Symbios® Xenograft granules, and conclude that porcine bone mineral combined with autogenous cortical bone shows the same biologic, radiologic and clinical features as the bovine comparator.

## Key Contributions

- **First complete clinical dataset on a porcine xenograft in sinus augmentation** — porcine-derived bone had been studied in humans only through the older products tabulated in the Discussion (The Graft™, OsteoBiol/Gen-Os, Apatos®), never with this product.
- **Randomized split-mouth head-to-head under one surgical protocol** — the same surgeon, same 20:80 autogenous:xenograft ratio, same membrane and same graft volumes on both sides, so anatomy and operator are controlled by design.
- **Multi-level endpoint battery in the same grafts**: radiography (height, volume, resorption), histomorphometry, IHC (7 cell markers + osteopontin), TEM, and osteogenic-pathway mRNA (ALP, RUNX2, Osterix).
- **Head-to-head mRNA comparison in sinus augmentation** with the ABB side of each patient used as the internal control.
- **Quantified equivalence on the endpoint that matters for graft choice** — 6-month remnant biomaterial was identical (31.00% vs 31.21%, p>.999) and vertical resorption did not differ, i.e. the porcine material is not the faster-turnover option that marketing language sometimes implies.
- **Microvascular-density caution** — vessel counts in ACB+ABB were below the same group's previously published values for the same combination, and ACB+PBM was lower still; absolute cross-study histological benchmarks are unreliable.

## Methodology

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Split-mouth randomized controlled trial, CONSORT-guided; single centre (University of Granada), single surgeon (P.G.-M.) |
| Ethics / registry | Ethics Committee for Human Research 480/CEIH/2018; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03797963 |
| Participants | 10 consecutive patients (5 men, 5 women), mean age 56 (range 36–73); 7 partial edentulism, most with moderate/severe periodontitis; no major systemic disease; 6 non-drinkers/non-smokers |
| Inclusion | Residual bone height <5 mm + bilateral two-stage sinus floor augmentation + full maxillary edentulism or Kennedy class I |
| Exclusion | Refusal, conditions affecting bone metabolism (e.g. bisphosphonates), heavy smoking (>10 cigarettes/day); periodontitis as extraction cause was **not** excluded |
| Interventions | ACB (Safescraper®, Meta) mixed ~20:80 (vol) with ABB (Bio-Oss® Spongiosa 250–1000 µm, Geistlich) on one side and with PBM (Symbios® Xenograft 0.25–1.0 mm, Dentsply Sirona) on the other; Symbios® Collagen Membrane SR over the window; amoxicillin 1 g q8h × 7 days, ibuprofen 600 mg q8h × 4 days, metamizole PRN |
| Follow-up | 6 months (graft maturation) → trephine biopsy at implant placement (3.5 × 22 mm trephine, AstraTech Implant System EV) |
| Primary outcome | Radiographic bone height gain at 6 months (CBCT; RAC, FH, HG = FH − RAC, IHG, vertical resorption = IHG − HG; mesial/central/distal sites; manual VOI segmentation for volume; bucco-lingual width at 5/10/15 mm) |
| Secondary outcomes | Histomorphometry (H&E, Masson's trichrome, ImageJ), IHC (CD44, CD56, TRAP, Musashi-1, CD45, CD68, CD34, osteopontin), TEM, RT-qPCR mRNA (ALP, RUNX2, Osterix; 2^−ΔΔCt, GAPDH, ABB side as control) |
| Randomization / blinding | randomization.com sequence per patient, concealed by a non-participating clinic assistant; surgeon blind until application; sample, radiographic and histopathological evaluators and patients blinded |
| Statistics | Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank test for all between-side comparisons; p<0.05; GraphPad Prism 7.0a |
| Sample size | Power calculation (G*Power 3.1.9) on the primary outcome using Lee, Shin et al. 2017 (final height 15.02 (2.17) bovine vs 13.26 (1.89) porcine; effect size 0.637; α=.05; power .80) required 18 — only 10 patients / 10 sinuses per material were enrolled |
| Protocol deviation | Registered 12- and 18-month CBCT re-measurements lost to the COVID-19 pandemic and therefore not registered for the study |

## Results

**Clinical course.** Healing was uneventful in all cases with no adverse events. Access-window height and width did not differ between groups; ACB+PBM was used in six right sinuses.

**Radiographic (primary outcome).** No difference between graft types in the primary outcome or in any secondary radiographic measure. Residual alveolar crest was <5 mm on average (lower centrally); graft height reached ~15 mm centrally immediately after grafting, so final height and height gain were greatest centrally — where resorption was also maximal. Initial and final graft volumes and the level of resorption did not differ between materials, and bucco-lingual sinus width did not differ at 5, 10 or 15 mm from the sinus floor.

**Histomorphometry** (column order ACB+ABB / ACB+PBM as confirmed by the Results narrative; Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank):

| Compartment | ACB+ABB | ACB+PBM | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pristine bone — mineralized tissue (%) | 47.85 (18.41) | 48.56 (16.72) | .945 |
| Pristine bone — non-mineralized tissue (%) | 52.15 (18.40) | 51.44 (16.72) | .945 |
| Grafted area — mineralized tissue (%) | 29.24 (10.82) | 32.51 (14.76) | .570 |
| Grafted area — non-mineralized tissue (%) | 39.76 (16.10) | 36.28 (13.43) | .652 |
| Grafted area — remnant biomaterial (%) | 31.00 (17.29) | 31.21 (19.61) | >.999 |
| Osteoid lines, grafted area (per mm²) | 5.89 (4.54) | 2.50 (2.62) | .031 |
| Osteoid lines, whole bone core (per mm²) | 3.90 (2.89) | 1.75 (1.75) | .043 |

New trabecular bone was seen in apposition to particles of both biomaterials. Osteoid lines were higher in the ACB+ABB grafted area than in that group's own pristine bone (1.56 (2.01) vs 5.89 (4.54); p=.031).

Pristine-vs-grafted comparison inside the ACB+PBM side: Musashi-1-positive cells 56.92 (84.08) pristine vs 163.86 (140.32) grafted (p=.008) and CD68-positive cells 40.32 (44.23) vs 179.93 (99.67) (p=.031). Grafted areas of either material carried three- to four-fold more Musashi-1-positive cells than pristine bone, but that pristine-vs-grafted difference reached significance only on the ACB+PBM side. Osteopontin (osteoconduction marker) was detected in both groups with similar expression (no numeric values reported in the text).

**mRNA.** Expression was overall higher in ACB+PBM, but the only significant between-group difference was alkaline phosphatase (ALP): 1.00 (0.19) ACB+ABB vs 2.13 (1.60) ACB+PBM (p=.011). RUNX2 and Osterix were numerically increased in ACB+PBM without significance — the authors caution that these results "must be interpreted with caution".

**TEM.** Firm union between newly formed trabeculae and particles of both materials, with cell colonization inside material pores and neo-colonization of old osteons.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Material equivalence at 6 months is supported.** When the alternative to DBBM is a porcine xenograft mixed 20:80 with autogenous cortical bone, this RCT shows no clinical, radiographic, histologic, immunohistochemical or molecular penalty for using the porcine material — the porcine option is a legitimate choice, not a compromise.
- **It is not a faster-turnover option.** Remnant biomaterial was identical between materials (31.00% vs 31.21%), so the porcine material does not buy faster resorption; choosing it for that reason is not supported.
- **The signals point to remodelling stage, not outcome.** Fewer osteoid lines with more osteocytes/leukocytes/vessels in ABB versus higher ALP mRNA with more MSC/monocytes in PBM is consistent with the two materials sitting at different points of the same remodeling sequence at 6 months, rather than with a biological advantage of either.
- **What this trial cannot support**: implant survival, long-term (≥12 months) graft stability, or superiority claims. It also cannot be used to benchmark absolute vessel density against other studies.
- **Reader caution**: the authors list small sample size (10 per group) and the 6-month maturation window as the main reasons for the null results, and the trial enrolled 10 patients against a calculated requirement of 18 — the null results are compatible with a real difference of the size their own power calculation was designed to detect.

## Limitations

- Under-powered against its own calculation (required 18, enrolled 10 patients / 20 sinuses); single centre, single surgeon.
- No implant-survival or implant-success endpoint reported (biopsy was taken at implant placement).
- Registered 12- and 18-month CBCT re-measurements lost to COVID-19 → long-term remodeling and resorption unmeasured.
- Highly selected population (residual bone height <5 mm, largely periodontitis-driven edentulism), 6-month endpoint only, no sham/empty control.
- The directionally opposite but individually significant signals (osteoid lines, ALP, CD44/CD45/CD34) are each based on single small-sample comparisons and are not corrected for multiple testing.
- **Source inconsistency (reproduced verbatim, not silently corrected)**: the Results text gives the pristine Musashi-1 value for the ACB+PBM side as "56.92 (84.08)", while Table 2 lists "56.82 (84.08)" for the same cell.
- **Conflict-of-interest context**: the first author is a frequent speaker for Dentsply Sirona Implants, manufacturer of the test porcine material (Symbios® Xenograft granules), the membrane used in both arms (Symbios® Collagen Membrane SR) and the implant system used at placement (AstraTech Implant System EV); the authors declare no conflict of interest.
