## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 37 CBCT studies (34 in meta-analysis) characterising the PSAA in the lateral sinus wall across 11,402+ sinuses from multiple populations.
Mean diameter 1.06 mm (55% < 1 mm, only 2% > 2 mm); detection rate 79%; location 64% intraosseous; mean distance from sinus floor 8.85 mm, from alveolar crest 16.71 mm.
Severe hemorrhage from PSAA injury during sinus augmentation is not a substantial clinical problem; only the 2% of arteries exceeding 2 mm diameter pose a risk of excessive (non–life-threatening) bleeding.

## 세줄요약

후상치조동맥 (Posterior Superior Alveolar Artery, PSAA)의 측방 상악동 측벽 위치를 콘빔전산화단층영상 (Cone Beam Computed Tomography, CBCT)으로 평가한 최대 규모 체계적 고찰·메타분석 (37편, 11,402건+ 상악동).
평균 직경 1.06 mm (55%가 <1 mm, >2 mm는 2%); 검출률 79%; 골내형 (Intraosseous) 64%; 동저 거리 8.85 mm, 치조정 거리 16.71 mm; 남성에서 직경·거리 모두 유의하게 큼.
상악동거상술 중 PSAA 손상으로 인한 중증 출혈은 실질적 임상 문제가 아님 — 반응성 혈관수축이 출혈량을 제한; 직경 >2 mm 혈관 (2%)만이 과다출혈 (비생명위협적) 위험.

## Summary

This SR+MA updates the 2015 Varela-Centelles review by incorporating 8 years of CBCT studies. The PSAA is detectable in 79% of sinuses on CBCT (cadaveric studies confirm 100% true prevalence — small arteries < 0.5 mm are simply invisible). Most PSAAs are thin (55% < 1 mm, mean 1.06 mm), predominantly intraosseous (64%), and located at a mean of 8.85 mm above the sinus floor and 16.71 mm above the alveolar crest. These distances define safe osteotomy boundaries: window height < 8 mm from sinus floor, superior border < 15 mm from alveolar crest. The paper's most clinically actionable finding is that only 2% of PSAAs exceed 2 mm diameter, the threshold for potentially excessive (yet non–life-threatening) bleeding. Reactive vasoconstriction further limits blood loss from smaller arteries. Male patients warrant extra caution due to larger artery diameter.

## Key Contributions

- Largest CBCT-based SR+MA of PSAA to date (37 studies vs 10 in Varela-Centelles 2015).
- Quantifies that 97% of PSAAs will produce manageable bleeding if damaged.
- Establishes 2 mm as the critical diameter threshold for excessive bleeding risk.
- Documents significant sex differences: males have both higher detection rate and larger diameter.
- Derives empiric safe osteotomy boundaries from pooled distance data.
- Explicit clinical verdict: "Life-threatening hemorrhage from PSAA injury is not a genuine clinical concern."

## Methodology

- PRISMA SR+MA; searched to May 2022 (Web of Science, MEDLINE, Scopus, Embase + gray literature)
- 37 studies included; 34 in meta-analysis (3 excluded: no mean values reported)
- Quality: 13 high / 22 moderate / 2 low
- Statistical: random-effects model; meta-regression for sex contribution to heterogeneity
