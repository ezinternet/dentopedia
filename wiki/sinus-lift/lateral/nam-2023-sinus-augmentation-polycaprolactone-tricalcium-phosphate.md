## Three-line Summary

Animal study (5 minipigs, 10 maxillary sinuses, 20 implants placed simultaneously with lateral sinus augmentation; 12-week healing) comparing a tube-shaped 80:20 PCL-TCP honeycomb scaffold loaded with hUCMSCs + PRP (n=5), with PRP alone (n=5), or scaffold alone (n=6).

Grayscale density (102.48±68.38 vs 58.10±29.69, P=0.045) and bone-implant contact (0.34±0.21 vs 0.09±0.19, P=0.016) were significantly higher with hUCMSCs+PRP than scaffold alone, while micro-CT bone volume fraction (4.42% vs 6.73%, P=0.366) and new bone height (1.26 vs 0.73 mm, P=0.056) were not; 4 of 20 implants failed, all in the two cell/PRP arms.

The friction-fitted tube scaffold demonstrably tents the sinus membrane and allows single-stage placement, but the cell+PRP payload produced only interface-level (BIC) gains in an underpowered 12-week pilot with no negative control — read it as scaffold-feasibility evidence, not as proof that stem cells or PRP improve sinus bone.

## Summary

This minipig study tests whether a structure can substitute for particulate graft packing in one-stage sinus augmentation: a 3D-printed PCL-TCP tube (inner Ø 4.0 mm, outer Ø 8.0 mm, height 4.0 mm) is friction-fitted onto each 4.0 mm implant fixture so that the implant itself carries the scaffold into the elevated sinus, tenting the Schneiderian membrane. Twenty implants in 5 animals were divided into scaffold + hUCMSCs + PRP, scaffold + PRP, and scaffold only, and evaluated at 12 weeks by soft X-ray densitometry, micro-CT, fluorochrome labelling and non-decalcified histomorphometry.

The statistical pattern is narrow but consistent: only the two *interface/density* endpoints separated — grayscale (P=0.045) and BIC (P=0.016), both hUCMSC+PRP versus scaffold alone — while every volumetric micro-CT parameter and new-bone area/height failed to reach significance. At the same time 4/20 implants floated into the sinus, all in the two arms that received PRP (2 each) and none in the scaffold-only arm, so the more "biologically enhanced" protocols were also the mechanically least stable ones.

The defensible position is that the tube geometry works as a space-maintaining, fixture-borne scaffold and is worth developing (the authors propose a threaded inner lumen), but the study does not establish that hUCMSCs or PRP add anything: PRP alone sat between the other two groups on every endpoint without separating from either, and the comparison rests on 5–6 specimens per arm with no negative control and no multiplicity control. Its value to the wiki is as the animal-model counterweight to the clinical SR/MA evidence that platelet concentrates and cell therapy do not change sinus outcomes.

## Key Contributions

- Proposes and tests a **fixture-borne** scaffold concept for sinus augmentation: the tube's 4.0 mm inner diameter matches the implant, its 8.0 mm outer diameter × 4.0 mm height rests on the elevated floor, and the honeycomb wall (70% porosity, 0.515 mm pores) carries cells/growth factors — replacing "pack particles and hope they stay" with a friction-fit space maintainer.
- First in-vivo sinus histomorphometry for this PCL-TCP tube in a large-animal model, with three arms isolating scaffold, growth factors (PRP) and cells (hUCMSCs).
- Shows xenogeneic hUCMSCs can be placed in non-immunosuppressed porcine sinuses with no inflammatory reaction reported in that group, whereas the scaffold-only group showed central fibrosis with inflammatory changes.
- Documents a real safety/feasibility signal in the opposite direction: 20% implant loss, confined to the PRP-containing arms, attributed by the authors to the complexity and handling time of cell seeding and PRP loading.
- Demonstrates that the tenting effect occurs in all arms (new bone on the *outer* scaffold surface in scaffold-only too), which usefully separates the geometric contribution from the biological one.

## Methodology

- **Scaffold**: PCL-TCP 80:20 (Osteopore International), fused deposition modelling, lay-down 0°/60°/120°, 70% porosity, 0.515 mm mean pore size, inner Ø 4.0 mm / outer Ø 8.0 mm / height 4.0 mm / wall 2.0 mm, male-female screw outer profile, EO-sterilized at 36.5 °C; inner lumen non-threaded.
- **Cells**: hUCMSCs at passage 5, CD73+/CD90+/CD105+/CD166+, CD14−/CD31−/CD34−/CD45−, tri-lineage differentiation confirmed; seeded 4.0×10⁷ cells/scaffold intra-operatively by drop-down and soak.
- **PRP**: 200 mL jugular blood, EDTA, double centrifugation (3,200 rpm × 5 min; 5,200 rpm × 5 min), 1.0–1.5 mL PRP per 50 mL blood, activated with thrombin 1,000 U + calcium gluconate (1:1), mixed 1:6 with PRP; 0.2–0.5 mL loaded per scaffold.
- **Animals/surgery**: 5 male minipigs (12.5–17.5 months, 32.8–41.0 kg); lateral infraorbital access with inverted-L incision, membrane elevation, 2 submerged Osstem GSII implants (10.0 × 4.0 mm) per sinus at the zygomatico-maxillary buttress — 20 implants / 10 sinuses. Groups: hUCMSCs+PRP (n=5), PRP (n=5), PCL-TCP only (n=6).
- **Assessment at 12 weeks**: paranasal CT; soft X-ray densitometry (30 kVp, 2 mA, 90 s; 5 random points, grayscale + % new bone area); micro-CT (Skyscan 1072/1172, 80 kV, 0.9° step; ROI 4.0–5.0 mm high × 8.0–8.5 mm; 30–40 HU threshold to exclude bare-scaffold radiopacity of 0–15 HU and metal artifact); non-decalcified 30 μm ground sections (HE, Masson's trichrome) for NBH, NBA, BIC and RSA; fluorochrome sequence tetracycline (3, 11 wk), calcein (6 wk), alizarin red (9 wk).
- **Statistics**: one-way ANOVA + Fisher PLSD, mean ± SD, P<0.05 (Statview).

## Results

**Survival.** 4/20 implants floated into the sinus — 2 in hUCMSCs+PRP (analysed n=5) and 2 in PRP (analysed n=5); scaffold-only had no failures (n=6).

**Densitometry (soft X-ray).** Grayscale 102.48±68.38 (hUCMSCs+PRP) vs 88.09±66.62 (PRP) vs 58.10±29.69 (PCL-TCP), P=0.045 for hUCMSCs+PRP vs scaffold-only. New bone area 23.3±7.9% / 22.9±5.9% / 20.1±5.9% (P=0.170, NS).

**Histology/fluoromicroscopy.** hUCMSCs+PRP: bone and scaffold degradation from the sinus-floor interface inward, no inflammatory reaction, bone on the outer scaffold surface (tenting) and at the scaffold-implant interface, woven-bone-like tissue inside micropores. Scaffold-only: bone at the floor interface and outer surface, but central fibrosis with inflammatory change. All groups: BIC-area labelled bone present yet "rare chronological ingrowth patterns", i.e. no orderly advancing mineralisation front through the scaffold.

## Limitations

- **No negative control** — no empty-sinus, no particulate-graft arm, so the scaffold is never compared against the standard of care, only against itself with/without cells and PRP.
- **Underpowered and multiplicity-naive**: 5/5/6 specimens, no correction; the two "positive" endpoints (P=0.045, P=0.016) sit next to a borderline NBH (P=0.056) and a flat micro-CT panel, and the abstract's own failure count is internally inconsistent with the Results (abstract text says two failures in a "PCL-TCP+hUCMSC" arm; Results specify PCL-TCP+hUCMSCs+PRP and PCL-TCP+PRP).
- **20% implant loss in the enhanced arms** confounds efficacy: the surviving specimens in those arms are a selected subset with better primary stability.
- 12 weeks, unloaded, no long-term survival/MBL; scaffold mechanical contribution (tenting force) was not measured, only inferred from histology.
- Non-threaded inner lumen — the friction-fit concept the authors emphasise was not built as intended; cell distribution/viability after drop-down seeding was not quantified (no bioreactor, no homogeneity check).
- Xenogeneic human cells in a pig: immunomodulation argued from prior literature, not measured; no tracking of transplanted cell fate.
- Micro-CT 30–40 HU threshold chosen to exclude PCL-TCP radiopacity (0–15 HU) and metal artifact may also exclude low-density new bone, biasing volumetric comparisons against the very group being credited with early bone.
