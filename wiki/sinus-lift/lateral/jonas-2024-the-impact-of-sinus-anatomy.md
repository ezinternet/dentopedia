## Three-line Summary

Single-center retrospective cohort (Rabin Medical Center, Israel) of 159 lateral-window sinus floor augmentations in 143 patients (residual ridge height 3.8 ± 1.4 mm; xenograft Bio-Oss; simultaneous or delayed placement), analysed per sinus, asking whether preoperative CBCT/MDCT sinus anatomy predicts early implant failure (EIF, defined as implant removal before temporary or permanent abutment attachment).

At least one EIF occurred in 21 of 159 sinuses (13.2%); in the multivariable GEE model bony septa were associated with lower odds of EIF (OR 0.13, p = .033), smoking with higher odds (OR 6.09, p = .0019), and lateral wall thickness was borderline (OR 0.67 per mm, p = .054), while residual bone height, Schneiderian membrane perforation (18.2%), diabetes and sinus shape showed no significant association.

The authors argue pre-surgical planning should look beyond residual bone height to sinus anatomy, but the design is retrospective with multiple surgeons, only 21 events, and the lateral wall effect was not significant at the 5% level in the adjusted model despite being called protective in the conclusions.

## Summary

A single-center retrospective cohort at the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Rabin Medical Center (Israel) included consecutive adults who had lateral-window sinus floor augmentation (residual height under 5 mm, xenograft, simultaneous or delayed implant placement) and preoperative CBCT or MDCT. Early implant failure (EIF) was removal of an implant before temporary or permanent abutment attachment; a sinus counted as failed if any implant in it was removed.

The sample was 159 sinuses in 143 patients (mean age 60.6 ± 12.7 years). EIF occurred in 21 sinuses (13.2%). Univariately, EIF sinuses had fewer septa (4.8% vs 37.7%, p = .006), thinner lateral walls (1.2 ± 0.96 vs 1.8 ± 0.7 mm, p = .04) and more smokers (28.6% vs 5.8%, p = .002). In the multivariable GEE model, septa (OR 0.13, p = .033) and smoking (OR 6.09, p = .0019) remained associated with EIF, whereas lateral wall width was borderline (OR 0.67 per mm, p = .054).

## Key Contributions

- Ties pre-operative sinus anatomy, not only residual bone height, to early failure after lateral augmentation.
- Authors report this as among the first studies relating septa and lateral wall width to EIF.
- Residual bone height, membrane perforation (18.2%), diabetes, hypertension and sinus shape were not significantly associated with EIF.

## Methodology

- Retrospective single-center cohort; each sinus is the unit; ethics-approved with informed consent.
- Lateral window (Boyne and James), cross-linked collagen membrane for perforations, Bio-Oss; delayed placement at 4 months, loading at 9 months.
- Radiographic variables: septa, alveolar antral artery, membrane thickness, lateral wall width, residual height, shape; two surgeons measured twice, third for disagreement over 5%.
- Chi-square and Mann-Whitney univariate; multivariable GEE (variables with p under .1, VIF under 5).

## Limitations

- Retrospective, multiple surgeons of varying experience (authors); only 21 events, CIs not reported in the retrieved text, so effects are imprecise.
- Lateral wall effect is not significant at 5% in the adjusted model, yet the Conclusions call it a significant protective factor; treat as a hypothesis.
- Protective role of septa (extra bone source for graft) is the authors' assumption, not tested; the Results text also loosely says septa correlated with "procedural success".
- Single center, one graft material, residual height under 5 mm; residual height measured as a single minimum value, which authors suspect hid its effect.
- Tables and figures were not in the retrieved text.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

For lateral-window sinus lifts in patients with very low residual height, review CBCT for septa and lateral wall thickness in addition to residual height, and counsel smokers that early graft-site implant loss was substantially more frequent in this cohort (OR 6.09, with wide uncertainty given few events). Do not read the septa result as a reason to prefer septate sinuses or avoid planning for them: other held work treats septa mainly as a surgical-difficulty and perforation issue, and this single study is hypothesis-generating.
