## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + random-effects meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420261301066; PubMed/Cochrane/Google Scholar; 4 randomized clinical studies, healing 4–6 months) of PRF added to allograft or xenograft in lateral maxillary sinus augmentation.

PRF significantly increased newly formed bone (Hedges' g 0.61, 95% CI 0.15–1.08, p=0.01, I²=17%) and reduced residual graft material (g −0.65, 95% CI −1.15 to −0.14, p=0.01, I²=8%), with no significant pooled effect on connective tissue/marrow spaces (g −0.14, p=0.65, I²=55%) or radiographic bone height (g −0.39, p=0.15, I²=37%).

The histological benefit was not universal (Nizam showed equal new bone), absolute gains ranged only ~0.1–11.2%, and no included trial reported implant-level outcomes — PRF is a supportive biologic adjunct for early graft remodelling, not a replacement for the graft nor a proven clinical benefit.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰+메타분석(PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420261301066; PubMed·Cochrane Library·Google Scholar; 무작위 임상연구 4편, 치유 4–6개월): 측방 상악동거상술에서 동종골(Allograft)·이종골(Xenograft)에 혈소판풍부피브린(Platelet-Rich Fibrin, PRF)을 병용한 효과.

PRF 병용 시 신생골(New Bone, NB) 유의 증가(헤지스 g 0.61, 95% CI 0.15–1.08, p=0.01, I²=17%), 잔류이식재(Residual Graft) 유의 감소(g −0.65, 95% CI −1.15 to −0.14, p=0.01, I²=8%); 결합조직·골수강(g −0.14, p=0.65, I²=55%)과 방사선학적 골높이(g −0.39, p=0.15, I²=37%)는 유의차 없음.

조직학적 우위가 4편 중 3편에서만 확인되고(Nizam은 신생골 동등) 신생골 절대 차이는 약 0.1–11.2%에 그쳤으며 임플란트 수준 결과(삽입토크·임플란트 안정성 지수, Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ·생존율)는 보고되지 않음 — PRF는 초기 이식재 리모델링을 돕는 보조 제제로 해석해야 함.

## Summary

This PRISMA-guided systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis pooled four randomized clinical studies (Reis 2025 with H-PRF + DBBM; Shiezadeh with PRF + allograft; Nizam with L-PRF + DBBM split-mouth; Eken with T-PRF vs DBBM) to test whether adding PRF to an allograft or xenograft improves histomorphometric outcomes after lateral maxillary sinus augmentation, with healing periods of four to six months. PRF significantly increased the percentage of newly formed bone (Hedges' g 0.61, 95% CI 0.15–1.08, p=0.01, I²=17%) and significantly reduced residual graft material (g −0.65, 95% CI −1.15 to −0.14, p=0.01, I²=8%), while connective tissue/marrow spaces (g −0.14, p=0.65) and radiographic bone height (g −0.39, p=0.15) showed no significant difference. Three of the four trials were at low risk of bias and one (Nizam) had some concerns. The authors interpret the pattern as PRF accelerating early biological healing and graft remodelling rather than increasing augmented bone volume — micro-CT in Reis supported better microarchitecture, whereas Eken found significantly *worse* radiographic bone height, bone gain, graft volume and density in the T-PRF group. Because no trial reported insertion torque, ISQ or implant survival, and because graft type (allograft vs DBBM) is confounded with PRF within each trial, the histological advantage remains biologically plausible but clinically unproven.

## Key Contributions

- First synthesis of PRF-modified grafts in lateral sinus augmentation that pools **both** allograft and xenograft comparisons and multiple PRF subtypes (L-PRF, H-PRF, T-PRF) — explicitly the gap left by L-PRF-only and DBBM-only reviews such as Almutairi 2025.
- Provides quantified random-effects estimates: new bone g=0.61 and residual graft g=−0.65, both p=0.01, with low heterogeneity (I²=17% and 8%).
- Demonstrates a **histology–radiography dissociation**: significant cellular-level benefit without any pooled dimensional (bone height) benefit at 4–6 months.
- Names PRF-subtype pooling (different centrifugation, tube material, fibrin density) as an inherent limitation that plausibly drives the moderate I² in the connective-tissue and bone-height analyses.
- Makes the clinical translation gap explicit: no implant-level endpoints and no long-term data in any included trial; absolute new-bone differences ranged from ~0.1% (Nizam) to 11.2% (Eken).

## Methodology

- **Design**: SR + meta-analysis; PRISMA 2020 checklist (Appendix A); registered PROSPERO CRD420261301066.
- **Databases**: PubMed, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar — identical Boolean string, searched March 2026 in duplicate by two reviewers, plus manual reference-list screening.
- **Limits**: English only, last 10 years; no country or publication-status restriction.
- **PICO**: atrophic posterior maxilla undergoing lateral MSA before implant placement; allograft + PRF or xenograft + PRF vs the same graft alone; primary outcomes = newly formed bone %, residual graft %, qualitative bone characteristics; secondary = radiographic bone height gain, graft volume, bone density.
- **Inclusion**: RCTs, split-mouth randomized studies, controlled clinical trials, single-blinded and observational/retrospective studies directly comparing graft ± PRF with histologic/histomorphometric outcomes. **Excluded**: animal/in vitro, case reports, reviews/meta-analyses, crestal-only technique, no histology, PRF without graft, grafts other than allograft/xenograft, non-English/unavailable full text.
- **n**: 4 randomized clinical studies (Reis 2025 — 26 histological sections; Shiezadeh — 20 patients; Nizam — 21 of 26 sinuses; Eken — 10 patients). No pooled patient total is reported.
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB 2 (5 domains, low/some concerns/high; Robvis summary). 3 studies low risk, Nizam some concerns (randomisation reporting, outcome measurement/reporting uncertainty).
- **Statistics**: Hedges' g with 95% CI, random-effects model; Cochran's Q and I² (0–40% low, 30–60% moderate, 50–90% substantial, 75–100% considerable); fixed-effects only when heterogeneity low and non-significant; α=0.05; forest plots (Figures 3–6); publication bias not assessed because k=4.

## Results

**PRISMA flow**: 718 records → 138 duplicates → 580 title/abstract screened → 35 full texts → 29 excluded (wrong study type/secondary literature 8; not lateral window 2; intervention not PRF 3; no eligible comparator 7; no histologic outcome 4; outside 10-year window 4; wrong graft material 3) → **4 included**.

**Reading the review's bottom line**: the pooled histological benefit is real in direction but modest in size (small-to-moderate Hedges' g; absolute new-bone differences ~0.1% in Nizam to 11.2% in Eken), it coexists with unchanged radiographic dimensions, and one trial (Eken) found significantly *less* radiographic bone gain with T-PRF. The authors therefore frame PRF as a supportive biologic adjunct for early healing rather than a substitute for the grafting material.
