## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA/PROSPERO CRD42022379384) of 4 RCTs (n=94 patients, 44 large-particle vs 50 small-particle implant sites) comparing large-granule (1–2 mm) vs small-granule (0.25–1 mm) deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM, Bio-Oss) for histological outcomes after maxillary sinus floor elevation.

No significant differences were found between large- and small-particle DBBM in percentage of connective tissue (WMD −5.76%, 95% CI −12.96 to 1.45, p=0.117), percentage of newly formed bone (WMD +5.15%, 95% CI −1.56 to 11.86, p=0.132), or percentage of residual xenograft (WMD +1.89%, 95% CI −2.10 to 5.88, p=0.353).

DBBM particle size (large vs small granule) does not significantly affect histological healing outcomes in sinus floor elevation, but the evidence base is limited to only 4 RCTs with substantial heterogeneity in two of the three outcomes.

## Summary

Li et al. (2023) conducted a PRISMA/PROSPERO-registered (CRD42022379384) systematic review and meta-analysis isolating one specific variable — **DBBM granule/particle size** — in maxillary sinus floor elevation, pooling all RCTs that directly compared large-granule (1–2 mm) versus small-granule (0.25–1 mm) Bio-Oss for histological outcomes.

Only 4 RCTs (94 patients total; 44 implant sites large-particle, 50 small-particle) met the strict inclusion criteria after screening 149 records down through 72 de-duplicated studies. Pooling percentage of connective tissue (CT), percentage of newly formed bone (NFB), and percentage of residual xenograft (RX) — all three showed no statistically significant difference between the two particle sizes, despite individual included studies disagreeing in direction (some favored large particle for new bone formation).

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA to isolate **DBBM particle size alone** (large 1–2 mm vs small 0.25–1 mm Bio-Oss) as the comparison variable in maxillary sinus floor elevation histology, rather than comparing DBBM against a different graft material.
- Pooled evidence across all three standard histomorphometric endpoints (CT, NFB, RX) — none reached significance, supporting histological equivalence between particle sizes.
- Explicitly surfaces the clinical trade-offs that remain independent of histology: large particles reduce graft volume/surgical time but increase void space (theoretical infection risk); small particles allow finer control of fill volume but require more material and patient burden.
- Transparent about evidence weakness: only 4 RCTs, substantial heterogeneity in 2 of 3 outcomes, and explicitly states more clinical evidence is needed before treating particle size as a settled question.

## Results

| Outcome | Model | WMD (large vs small) | 95% CI | p | I² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| % Connective tissue | Random | −5.76 | −12.96 to 1.45 | 0.117 | 65.7% |
| % Newly formed bone | Random | +5.15 | −1.56 to 11.86 | 0.132 | 75.3% |
| % Residual xenograft | Fixed | +1.89 | −2.10 to 5.88 | 0.353 | 0.0% |

None of the three histomorphometric outcomes reached statistical significance between large- and small-particle DBBM. Individual included studies (Chackartchi 2011, Testori 2013, Molon 2018, Kamolratanakul 2022) disagreed in direction — some found more new bone with large particles by histomorphometry — but the pooled effect was non-significant in every outcome, and the authors conclude the two particle sizes are practically equivalent for maxillary sinus floor elevation histology at 6–9 months.
