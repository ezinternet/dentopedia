## Three-line Summary

Systematic narrative review (PubMed, 1997–2011) describing the evolution of the graftless lateral sinus membrane elevation technique and reviewing available clinical evidence; focused on studies using lateral approach with blood clot only (no bone graft or substitute; no osteotome technique); Uppsala University authors.

All included studies showed high implant survival rates for the graftless technique; the mechanism proposed is Schneiderian membrane osteogenic capacity (periosteum-like deep layers with microvascular cells) + blood clot providing the scaffold for osteoprogenitor migration; the technique is described as cost-effective and lower-morbidity than grafted lateral SFE.

As of 2011 only a limited number of long-term studies existed, but all pointed to reliable outcomes; subsequent meta-analyses (Dongo 2018: 97.5% survival; Hegde 2016 SR) have expanded and quantified this evidence base.

## Summary

Uppsala University oral and maxillofacial surgery team (Riben and Thor) performed a PubMed search (1997 to November 2011) to document the surgical evolution and available clinical evidence for the graftless lateral sinus membrane elevation (SME) technique — distinct from the osteotome-based transalveolar approach. Only studies using lateral approach without bone graft or substitute and with ≥1 year follow-up were included. Two reviewers (not blinded) assessed titles/abstracts then full texts.

**Evolution context:**
- Tatum first reported sinus lift in 1976 (oral presentation); published in 1980 by Boyne and James
- Autogenous bone → bone substitutes → graftless (blood clot only) starting 1997
- Key insight: grafts are exposed to substantial resorption — not a limitation with graftless approach

**Mechanism for bone formation without graft:**
1. Blood clot fills the elevated sub-membrane space — autologous osteogenic matrix
2. Schneiderian membrane deep layers: periosteum-like structure with microvascular cells → source of osteoprogenitor cells and osteogenic signaling
3. Osteoprogenitors migrate from membrane and adjacent bony walls → differentiate and mineralize

**Clinical findings across included studies:** All reported high implant survival rates. No quantitative pooling performed due to study heterogeneity and limited eligible studies.

**Cost-effectiveness:** No graft material cost; shorter procedure; no harvesting time; lower donor-site morbidity.

## Key Contributions

- Foundational English-language review consolidating evidence for graftless lateral SFE (blood-clot-only) as of 2011
- Articulates the dual osteogenic mechanism: membrane deep layers + blood clot scaffold
- Provides historical context from Tatum 1976 to contemporary (2011) graftless approach
- Notes that graft resorption — a major problem with conventional SFE — is avoided with graftless technique

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic narrative review with PubMed search
- **Search terms**: "sinus" AND "implant"; 1997–November 2011
- **Inclusion**: Human clinical studies; ≥1 year follow-up; lateral approach; graftless (no bone graft or substitute); simultaneous or delayed implant placement
- **Exclusion**: Osteotome technique; studies with bone graft/substitute
- **Process**: Two authors (CR, AT); not blinded; no PRISMA reported

## Results

All investigated studies showed high implant survival for graftless technique. No quantitative pooling. Technique described as reliable and cost-effective.
