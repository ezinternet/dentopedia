## Three-line Summary

This 2008 Italian position paper establishes a 3-step ENT management framework for sinus lift candidates: preventive-diagnostic (endoscopy + CT), preventive-therapeutic (FESS for reversible contraindications), and diagnostic-therapeutic (postoperative sinusitis management).

Reversible contraindications (OMC variants, rhinosinusitis, polyposis, oro-antral fistula) can be corrected via FESS to enable surgery; irreversible contraindications (cystic fibrosis, Kartagener's syndrome, immunodeficiency, sinonasal malignancy) absolutely preclude sinus lift.

The paper's clinical impact is the ENT risk-stratification algorithm and multidisciplinary collaboration mandate — though recommendations are expert-opinion based with no original clinical data.

## Summary

Pignataro et al. (2008) is the foundational position paper establishing ENT specialist involvement as a clinical standard in sinus lift pre-operative workup. Based at the University of Milan, the authors synthesize sinus anatomy, mucociliary physiology, and contemporary ENT evidence to propose a structured risk-stratification protocol.

**Core argument:** Sinus floor augmentation intrinsically disturbs maxillary sinus homeostasis by elevating the Schneiderian membrane (슈나이더막), disrupting mucociliary clearance, and creating a foreign-body environment. Without preoperative ENT evaluation, undiagnosed sinonasal pathology becomes a source of postoperative maxillary sinusitis — the most significant ENT sequela of the procedure.

**Three-step management algorithm:**
1. **Preventive-diagnostic phase:** ENT assessment including nasal endoscopy (비내시경) and CT imaging when sinus pathology is suspected on clinical or radiographic grounds.
2. **Preventive-therapeutic phase:** FESS to surgically correct reversible ENT contraindications, restoring ostium patency and mucociliary clearance *before* the implant/sinus procedure.
3. **Diagnostic-therapeutic phase:** Post-surgical ENT surveillance and management of any postoperative maxillary sinusitis (약물 또는 FESS).

**Contraindication classification (central clinical value of this paper):**

| Type | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Reversible (FESS-correctable) | OMC anatomical variants, chronic rhinosinusitis, nasal polyposis, oro-antral fistula |
| Irreversible (absolute contraindication) | Cystic fibrosis, Kartagener's syndrome (primary ciliary dyskinesia), primary immunodeficiency, sinonasal malignancy |

## Key Contributions

1. First systematic ENT framework for pre-sinus-lift risk stratification in the implantology literature
2. Binary contraindication taxonomy (reversible vs irreversible) — still the organizing framework for later consensus papers
3. Clinical mandate for nasal endoscopy + CT as standard preoperative workup when sinus disease is suspected
4. Establishment of FESS as the corrective bridge procedure enabling sinus lift in patients with reversible contraindications
5. Formal articulation of the implantologist–ENT specialist multidisciplinary model

## Methodology

Narrative review and position paper based on:
- Anatomical and physiological review of maxillary sinus (ostiomeatal complex, mucociliary transport, Schneiderian membrane)
- Summary of contemporary evidence (pre-2008) on ENT complications of sinus augmentation
- Expert opinion from Ear, Nose, and Throat specialists and implantologists at the University of Milan

No systematic search protocol, no PRISMA, no original patient data, no control group, no statistical analysis.

## Results

**Postoperative maxillary sinusitis** is identified as the primary ENT risk of sinus floor elevation. The mechanism is multifactorial:
- Membrane elevation → disruption of mucociliary clearance
- Graft material as foreign body within sinus cavity
- Pre-existing OMC obstruction amplifying drainage failure

The paper reports no original incidence data — complication rates are referenced qualitatively from the contemporary literature.

**FESS outcomes for pre-SFE correction:** Referenced (not meta-analyzed) as effective in restoring ostium patency and enabling safe subsequent sinus lift, consistent with the authors' clinical experience.
