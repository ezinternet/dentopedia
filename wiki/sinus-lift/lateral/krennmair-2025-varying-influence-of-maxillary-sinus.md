## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study of lateral-window sinus augmentation (23 sinuses) comparing porcine (DPBM, n = 10) versus bovine (DBBM, n = 13) xenograft, testing sinus width (SW), inner sinus contour length (IMSCL) and residual ridge height (RRH) as predictors of histomorphometric new bone formation (%NBF).
%NBF fell with increasing SW (DPBM r = −0.660, p = 0.038; DBBM r = −0.614, p = 0.026) and IMSCL (DPBM r = −0.737, p = 0.015; DBBM r = −0.573, p = 0.041), but not RRH; multivariate R² was 0.591 (DPBM) versus 0.314 (DBBM).
Sinus anatomy significantly drives new bone formation, and the effect is stronger with porcine than bovine xenograft — graft choice should be conditioned on sinus dimensions.

## Summary

Most sinus-augmentation evidence treats residual bone height as the single anatomical driver of new bone formation and asks which graft material wins on average. This retrospective study flips the question: holding patient and surgical factors constant, do sinus *geometry* variables predict histomorphometric new bone, and does that relationship depend on which xenograft is used? In 23 lateral-window sinuses (10 porcine DPBM, 13 bovine DBBM), %NBF correlated negatively and significantly with sinus width and inner contour length in both groups, while residual ridge height was not significant. The multivariate models explain 59.1% of %NBF variance with porcine graft but only 31.4% with bovine — the authors' central claim that anatomical influence is material-dependent. The practical reading: in a wide, large-contour sinus, the expected new-bone penalty differs by graft source. Abstract-only artifact limits this to the reported Associations.

## Key Contributions

- Adds sinus width and inner contour length as anatomical effect-modifiers of %NBF beyond residual ridge height.
- Quantifies a graft-source interaction: anatomy explains ~59% of %NBF variance with porcine vs ~31% with bovine xenograft.
- Reports that RRH was not a significant %NBF predictor in either group — a contrast with RBH-centred material-ranking syntheses.

## Methodology

- Retrospective design; lateral-window sinus augmentation with either DPBM (n = 10) or DBBM (n = 13).
- Anatomical variables measured radiographically (SW, IMSCL, RRH); %NBF measured histomorphometrically.
- Linear regression (correlations + R²) and multivariate regression, computed separately per graft material.
- Groups comparable on patient epidemiology, anatomical variables and surgical/patient risk factors.

## Results

| Predictor | DPBM (n = 10) | DBBM (n = 13) |
|---|---|---|
| SW → %NBF | r = −0.660, p = 0.038; R² 0.435 | r = −0.614, p = 0.026; R² 0.377 |
| IMSCL → %NBF | r = −0.737, p = 0.015; R² 0.543 | r = −0.573, p = 0.041; R² 0.328 |
| RRH → %NBF | not significant; R² 0.258 | not significant; R² 0.053 |
| Multivariate R² | 0.591 (59.1%) | 0.314 (31.4%) |

- All significant SW/IMSCL correlations were negative (larger sinus → less new bone).
- No between-group differences in epidemiology, anatomy or risk factors.

## Limitations

- **Abstract-only artifact** — paywalled; absolute %NBF values, healing times, regression coefficients beyond R² and follow-up could not be verified.
- Retrospective, small per-group samples (10 and 13).
- Erratum issued (doi 10.1111/cid.70054) — corrections not verifiable from the abstract alone [미검증].
- Unmeasured confounders (technique, membrane handling, healing time) may drive part of the material interaction.
