## Three-line Summary
Case report (n=2) of maxillary sinusitis that developed after dental implant placement, describing oral and radiographic clinical features and management.
Symptoms improved with curettage through the lateral wall of the maxillary sinus, antibiotics and sinus irrigation (abstract states improvement "after 2 weeks of the operation").
The authors conclude acute maxillary sinusitis should be treated by a direct lateral-wall opening, regular in-hospital irrigation and antibiotics; n=2 and abstract-only limit the evidence.

## Summary
abstract-only — full text not retrieved. This case report evaluated the prognosis and treatment of maxillary sinus infection associated with dental implants in two patients who developed maxillary sinusitis after implant placement. Oral and radiographic clinical features were described. Symptoms improved with curettage through the lateral wall of the maxillary sinus, antibiotics and sinus irrigation. The authors concluded that acute maxillary sinusitis should be treated by a direct approach via lateral-wall opening, with regular in-hospital irrigation and antibiotics.

## Key Contributions
- Documents two cases of implant-associated maxillary sinusitis and their management.
- Supports a direct lateral-wall (open) approach with curettage, antibiotics and regular irrigation for acute post-implant maxillary sinusitis, at case-report level only.

## Methodology
Case report, n=2 patients. Clinical (oral) and radiographic features were described; treatment was curettage through the lateral sinus wall, antibiotics and sinus irrigation. Implant site, timing, imaging details and follow-up length are not given in the abstract.

## Results
