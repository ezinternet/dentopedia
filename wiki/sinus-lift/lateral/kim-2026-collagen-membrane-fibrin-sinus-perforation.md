## Three-line Summary

Line 1: Technical note and single case report of a modified Schneiderian membrane perforation repair using a resorbable collagen membrane with a custom "handle" tab and fibrin glue, performed in a 71-year-old osteoporotic female on denosumab during lateral sinus augmentation with simultaneous implant placement.
Line 2: The handle design prevented inward membrane displacement during graft packing; fibrin glue mixed with graft + rhBMP-2 created a cohesive putty mass; the bony window was stabilized with fibrin glue (no tacks); graft height maintained 13.3 mm at 6 months (from 15.2 mm post-op) with no sinus complications.
Line 3: The technique simplifies intraoperative perforation management and avoids mechanical window fixation, but evidence is limited to a single case with no control group or long-term histologic data.

## Summary

Kim et al. (2026) present a technical note and case report describing a modified approach to intraoperative Schneiderian membrane (슈나이더막) perforation during lateral sinus floor augmentation (측방 상악동 거상). The key innovation is a **handle design**: a resorbable collagen membrane (Bio-Gide) is cut with a protruding tab that extends through the bony window, giving the surgeon a grip point for precise intrasinus positioning without distorting the membrane body. Once seated, the handle is trimmed flush.

**Fibrin glue (피브린 접착제)** serves dual roles: (1) mixed with the particulate graft (Bio-Oss + rhBMP-2), it creates a cohesive putty-like mass that packs without crumbling and limits migration through the perforation; (2) applied to the outer surface of the repositioned bony window, it replaces titanium tacks for cortical fixation — relevant in this patient given denosumab-associated bone quality concerns.

The single case: 71-year-old female, osteoporosis, denosumab history, lateral sinus augmentation + simultaneous implant at site #26. Perforation detected at 5–7 mm; additional membrane elevation reduced functional perforation to 3–4 mm before membrane placement. Six-month CBCT showed graft height maintained at 13.3 mm (15.2 mm immediately post-op; 12.5% resorption), no graft leakage, no sinusitis, implant clinically stable.

## Key Contributions

- **Handle-design membrane modification:** enables one-handed intrasinus membrane seating; prevents the membrane from folding or being pushed inward during incremental graft packing
- **Fibrin glue as cohesive graft binder:** reduces interparticulate space migration and improves handling; combined with rhBMP-2 may augment osteoinduction
- **Tack-free window fixation:** fibrin glue applied to the outer cortex substitutes for titanium fixation tacks, simplifying instrumentation and avoiding additional periosteal trauma
- **Medically compromised applicability:** technique avoids screw fixation risks in a patient with anti-resorptive drug history

## Methodology

- Setting: Asan Medical Center (Seoul, Korea), single operator
- Patient: 71F, osteoporosis (denosumab), edentulous #26 region
- Procedure: piezosurgical lateral osteotomy → membrane elevation → perforation (5–7 mm) detected → extended elevation → handle-design Bio-Gide placed over perforation → Bio-Oss + rhBMP-2 + fibrin glue packed → handle trimmed → bony window repositioned with fibrin glue → primary closure
- Implant placed simultaneously
- Follow-up: periapical radiograph + CBCT at 6 months
- No control arm; single operator; no blinded outcome assessment
