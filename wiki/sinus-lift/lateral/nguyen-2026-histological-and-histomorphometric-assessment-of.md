## Three-line Summary

Prospective single-arm descriptive histomorphometric study (42 patients / 60 lateral-window sinus augmentation sites, Vietnam, 2022–2024; residual bone height ≤4 mm, mean 2.31 ± 0.93 mm) using a 2:1 composite of corticocancellous allograft and 30% HA/70% β-TCP plus autologous PRF, assessed on 6-month trephine core biopsies.

At 6 months newly formed bone (NB) was 31.28 ± 17.71% (95% CI 26.70–35.85), residual graft (RG) 15.48 ± 12.33% (12.29–18.68) and connective tissue (CT) 53.23 ± 18.74% (48.38–58.07); histology showed mature lamellar bone with Haversian canals, active osteoblast/osteoclast remodeling and no inflammation or necrosis, while ISQ rose from 58.20 ± 6.47 to 74.85 ± 7.68 at six months, with ISQ1 tracking residual bone height (r = 0.626) and inversely CT (r = −0.627).

The composite is biocompatible and integrates as an osseoconductive scaffold in severely atrophic maxillae, but the single-arm design, one time point and 2-D area fractions from 3-mm cores make the result descriptive — the separate contributions of allograft, β-TCP/HA and PRF cannot be determined, and 15.48% residual graft at 6 months must not be read as long-term resorption.

## Summary

This prospective, single-arm, descriptive histomorphometric study from Can Tho University of Medicine and Pharmacy (Vietnam) enrolled 42 patients contributing 60 posterior maxillary sites with residual bone height ≤4 mm (mean 2.31 ± 0.93 mm) between January 2022 and February 2024. Lateral-window sinus floor augmentation was performed with a 2:1 volume mixture of corticocancellous allograft (HC Biologics, USA) and a biphasic synthetic substitute (30% HA/70% β-TCP, Osteon II, Dentium, Korea), combined with two autologous PRF membranes per site (Choukroun protocol, 2700 rpm × 12 min). At six months, 3-mm trephine cores were harvested immediately before implant placement and sectioned at coronal, middle and apical levels (three non-consecutive H&E sections per core, ImageJ segmentation, two blinded examiners).

Newly formed bone averaged 31.28 ± 17.71% of the biopsy area, residual graft material only 15.48 ± 12.33%, and connective tissue/marrow 53.23 ± 18.74%. Histology showed residual particles enveloped in vital bone with a seamless mineralized interface, mature lamellar architecture with Haversian canals and osteocyte lacunae, numerous osteoblast/osteoclast pairs at the graft periphery, and no inflammatory infiltrate, foreign-body reaction or necrosis. Clinically, all sites healed uneventfully, three membrane perforations (5.0%) were managed intraoperatively, ISQ increased from 58.20 ± 6.47 at placement to 74.85 ± 7.68 at six months, and all implants and restorations were functional at one year.

The clinical implication is narrow but useful: an allograft-dominant composite with a resorbable β-TCP/HA phase and PRF produces a vital-bone fraction comparable to published DBBM series while leaving a materially smaller residual graft fraction at six months — a plausible signature of the faster-resorbing β-TCP phase rather than proof of it. Because no comparator arm exists and PRF was used in every site, the study cannot attribute the outcome to any single component; the authors classify the findings as descriptive rather than causal.

## Key Contributions

- Largest single-protocol histomorphometric series held in this wiki for a **three-component composite graft** (allograft : 30% HA/70% β-TCP = 2:1, plus autologous PRF) — 60 biopsies in severely atrophic maxillae (RBH mean 2.31 mm), a specimen count several-fold larger than typical sinus histology studies.
- Reports the **complete tissue budget** (NB 31.28% + RG 15.48% + CT 53.23%) rather than new bone alone, making the residual-graft and connective-tissue trade-off explicit — and showing that over half the augmented volume at 6 months is non-mineralized tissue.
- Quantifies a **low 6-month residual graft fraction** in a β-TCP-containing composite, the natural contrast to DBBM literature where residual xenograft typically dominates the non-vital fraction.
- Links histology to **implant stability**: ISQ1 58.20 → ISQ2 74.85, and ISQ1 correlates more strongly with residual bone height (r = 0.626) and connective tissue fraction (r = −0.627) than with vital bone fraction (r = 0.405) — i.e. RFA reads mineralized continuity, not "bone %" as such.
- Documents a **Southeast Asian (Vietnamese) cohort**, where the authors argue nutritional and metabolic differences (calcium intake, vitamin D, metabolic bone disease) may shift graft remodeling relative to the European/North American literature.
- Reports a **sex difference in residual graft** (female 18.19% vs male 11.42%, p = 0.036) with identical new bone formation (p = 0.932) — a hypothesis-generating signal the authors themselves decline to over-interpret.

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Prospective, single-arm (no comparator), descriptive histomorphometric study; specimen-level analysis |
| Registration | ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07186166 (30 Aug 2025 — retrospectively registered) |
| Setting / period | Can Tho University of Medicine and Pharmacy Hospital, Vietnam; Jan 2022 – Feb 2024 |
| Patients / sites | 42 patients; 60 grafted sites (some patients contributed >1 site) |
| Inclusion | RBH ≤4 mm; prosthetic space ≥5 mm; consenting adults |
| Exclusion | Sinusitis, bleeding disorder, diabetes, bone metabolic disease, periodontal disease, malignancy, anticoagulants/NSAIDs/steroids, smokers, pregnancy |
| RBH assessment | CBCT cross-sections perpendicular to the ridge, two calibrated examiners |
| Surgery | Crestal incision, piezotome lateral window, Schneiderian membrane elevation, standardized single-institution protocol |
| Graft | 2:1 allograft (corticocancellous, HC Biologics, USA) : 30% HA/70% β-TCP (Osteon II, Dentium, Korea) + autologous PRF |
| PRF protocol | Choukroun; glass tubes without anticoagulant; 2700 rpm × 12 min (DLAB DM0412); 2 membranes per site mixed with graft |
| Biopsy | 3 mm trephine core at 6 months, before implant osteotomy |
| Processing | 10% formalin → EDTA decalcification → paraffin → ~5 μm → H&E |
| Histomorphometry | 3 non-consecutive sections (coronal/middle/apical) per core; ImageJ polygon segmentation; 2 blinded examiners with reproducibility checks |
| Outcomes | % NB, % RG, % CT; qualitative histology; ISQ1 (placement) and ISQ2 (6 months); complications and 1-year survival |
| Statistics | SPSS 26.0; Shapiro–Wilk, Levene; mean ± SD / 95% CI / range; independent t-test by sex; Pearson or Spearman correlation; exploratory one-way ANOVA; α = 0.05, two-tailed |
| Sample size | n = Z²·p(1−p)/d², Z = 1.96, p = 9.09%, d = 0.0231 |

New bone formation was highly variable across cores (2.87–72.19%) even under one standardized protocol, and connective tissue was the largest compartment in nearly every specimen — the distribution the authors themselves flag as wider than the pooled means suggest. The correlation matrix must be read with the compositional caveat the paper states explicitly: NB, RG and CT sum to 100%, so part of the NB–CT inverse relationship (r = −0.772) is mathematical rather than biological.
