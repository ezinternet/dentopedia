## Three-line Summary

Case series (8 patients, 24 implants) introducing the Sinus-Plate Augmentation Method (SPAM): in posterior maxillae with Class C horizontal resorption, the vestibular cortical wall of the lateral sinus window is harvested with piezosurgery and re-fixed with an 8- to 10-mm screw as an autologous lateral onlay plate over a particulate xenogeneic graft, together with external sinus lifting and simultaneous implant insertion.

CBCT horizontal alveolar width increased from 5.99 ± 1.71 mm pre-operatively to 9.28 ± 1.20 mm post-operatively and settled at 8.37 ± 1.50 mm at a mean 19.5 ± 6.98 months (0.91 ± 1.06 mm = 9.8% remodeling loss), a statistically significant net gain of 2.55 ± 1.47 mm, with 100% implant survival and no biological or prosthetic complications.

SPAM is a feasible one-site alternative to autogenous block grafting for moderate-to-severe posterior maxillary atrophy, but the evidence is case-series level (n = 8, non-consecutive, mixed materials, 12–36-month follow-up) and thin plates risk fracture if the fixation screw is over-tightened.

## 세줄요약

증례군 연구 (n = 8명, 임플란트 24개) — 상악 후방부 Class C 수평 흡수 증례에서 상악동 골판 골증대법 (Sinus-Plate Augmentation Method, SPAM)을 소개: 측방 상악동 골창의 전정측 피질골판을 압전기구로 채취해 8–10 mm 나사로 재고정하여 자가골 측방 차폐판으로 사용하고, 그 아래를 입자형 이종골로 채우면서 동시에 측방 상악동거상술 (Lateral Sinus Lift)과 임플란트 식립을 시행.

콘빔 전산화 단층촬영 (Cone-Beam CT, CBCT)상 수평골폭 (Horizontal Bone Width)은 술전 5.99 ± 1.71 mm → 술후 9.28 ± 1.20 mm → 평균 19.5 ± 6.98개월 추적 시 8.37 ± 1.50 mm (개조 (Remodeling) 소실 0.91 ± 1.06 mm, 9.8%)로, 순증가 2.55 ± 1.47 mm의 통계적 유의차; 임플란트 생존율 100%, 생물학적·보철적 합병증 없음.

SPAM은 제2 공여부 없이 중등도~중증 상악 후방부 위축에 적용 가능한 저이환 술식이나, n = 8·비연속 표본·이식재 및 차폐막 브랜드 혼재·추적 기간 불균일 (12–36개월)로 증례군 수준의 근거이며, 골판이 얇은 경우 고정 나사 과조임 시 골판 골절 위험이 있다.

## Summary

This single-operator case series (8 patients, 24 implants; 7 female, 1 male, age 52–71 years; 2 smokers under 10 cigarettes/day; all Allen Class C horizontal resorption, FMPS < 30%) introduces the Sinus-Plate Augmentation Method (SPAM) for posterior maxillary atrophy requiring simultaneous lateral sinus lift, lateral ridge augmentation and implant placement. The innovation is a re-purposing of the lateral window: after piezo-osteotomy and elevation, the vestibular cortical plate of the sinus window — normally discarded or merely repositioned over the antrostomy — is harvested, stored in saline, and later fixed to the alveolar bone with an 8- to 10-mm screw as an autologous onlay shield; the gap between plate and ridge is filled with particulate xenogeneic bone mineral (BioOss in 3 patients, porcine THE Graft in 5) and the whole area covered with a resorbable collagen membrane. On CBCT, horizontal alveolar width rose from 5.99 ± 1.71 mm pre-operatively to 9.28 ± 1.20 mm immediately post-operatively and was 8.37 ± 1.50 mm at a mean 19.5 ± 6.98 months — a 9.8% (0.91 ± 1.06 mm) remodeling loss but a still-significant net gain of 2.55 ± 1.47 mm. Healing was uneventful in every patient (tenderness and light-to-moderate swelling for 3–5 days only), all 24 implants survived, and no biological or prosthetic complication occurred. The authors frame the value of SPAM as avoidance of a second donor site (ramus or iliac crest) while still providing an autologous space-maintaining shield, and stress careful case selection because a very thin plate can fracture under screw torque and remodeling loss of about 1 mm must be planned for.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces SPAM, a variant of lateral-window sinus lifting in which the harvested cortical window plate itself becomes the autologous lateral onlay graft — no ramus or iliac donor site.
- Provides the first clinical outcome data for the technique: 8 patients, 24 implants, 12–36-month follow-up, 100% implant survival, no biological or prosthetic complications.
- Quantifies horizontal gain and its decay on CBCT: +3.29 mm immediate, 2.55 ± 1.47 mm net at mean 19.5 months, with only 0.91 ± 1.06 mm (9.8%) remodeling loss versus 2.3 ± 1 mm cited for membrane/autogenous-block GBR.
- Argues a mechanical advantage: the curved sinus plate acts as a rigid space-maintaining shield whose curvature also eases fixation at the recipient ridge.
- Names the technique's failure modes up front — plate fracture in thin bone or under over-tightened screws, and inability to fix the plate when primary implant stability is unattainable.

## Methodology

- **Design**: case series, single experienced oral surgeon (CD), private practice; no control group; no ethical approval required per the authors; written informed consent obtained for treatment and publication.
- **Inclusion**: 52–71 years (mean 57.37 ± 7.44), partially edentulous, moderate-to-severe horizontal resorption of the posterior maxilla (Allen 1985 Class C, mild-to-moderate), clinical indication for implant placement with simultaneous sinus lift and lateral ridge augmentation; non-heavy smokers only. Exclusions: head/neck radiation, cardiovascular disease requiring antibiotic prophylaxis, organ transplantation, infectious disease.
- **Surgical sequence**: crestal incision with papilla preservation + full-thickness flap with two vertical releasing incisions → piezo-osteotomy of the lateral sinus window (Piezotome CUBE + Piezo Kit, Acteon) → harvest the thin lateral bony window and store in sterile saline → elevate the Schneiderian membrane → graft the sinus with bovine (BioOss, 3 patients) or porcine (THE Graft, Purgo, 5 patients) natural bone mineral, 0.25–1 mm and 1–2 mm granulations → place 2–5 implants per site (24 total; MIS V3, MIS Seven, Dentium Superline, Dentium Bright, Straumann BLX; Ø 3.3–4.5 mm, 11–16 mm) → fix the harvested autologous plate to the alveolar bone with an 8–10-mm screw (Meisinger) → fill the plate-to-bone gap with the same particulate xenograft → cover the whole augmented area with a bioresorbable collagen membrane (BioGide 30/40 mm in 3 patients; THE Cover Flex 25/30 mm in 5), fixed with PTFE mattress sutures (Coreflon) → periosteal incision and sectioning of inserting muscle fibres for tension-free primary closure.
- **Post-operative**: 0.12% chlorhexidine twice daily for 2 weeks, no brushing in the area; amoxicillin 875 mg + clavulanic acid 125 mg plus ibuprofen 400 mg/8 h for 7 days; sutures removed at 2 weeks; final prosthetics by another dentist 6–12 months post-operatively. Graft, membrane and implant brands were chosen according to patients' financial means.
- **Measurements**: CBCT with the same unit and standardized parameters/positioning, pre-operative, immediate post-operative and 12–36 months; horizontal alveolar width in the vestibulo-oral direction at implant shoulder level (OnDemand3D, CyberMed), single examiner with previously established intra-examiner reliability. Clinical peri-implant assessment (PPD, bleeding on probing, oral hygiene) at 6/12/18 months (six patients), 6+12 months (one), 6+36 months (one).
