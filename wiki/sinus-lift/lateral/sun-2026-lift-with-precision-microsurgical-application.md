## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (PubMed, Web of Science, Google Scholar, 1986–2025; 45 landmark and contemporary studies; two independent screeners, no meta-analysis) of microscope-assisted maxillary sinus augmentation, covering lateral-window and transcrestal approaches, micro-instrumentation, piezoelectric/osteotome/osseodensification techniques and surgical ergonomics.

Magnification enables antrostomies of about 5 mm (MGES, 20 sinuses) to 5 × 8 mm = 31.4 mm² (EBI kit, 15 procedures) and 5–6 mm (147 sinuses; perforation 6%, infection 2–5.6%, mean vertical bone gain 7.44 mm); piezoelectric lateral windows perforated at 7–8% versus 20–30% for rotary burs, and hydrodynamic PISE gave 2.83% perforation with 97.2% implant success (353 implants / 250 sinuses).

The technical rationale is strong — smaller windows mature grafts better, micro-vision catches small tears early, and a stepwise 4–7× → 10–15× protocol plus six-handed setup protects the operator — but superiority over conventional techniques is still inferred from case series, pilot studies and small comparative trials with short follow-up, not demonstrated in an RCT.

## Summary

This narrative review synthesizes the use of the operating microscope (수술 현미경) in maxillary sinus augmentation across the lateral-window and transcrestal routes, drawing on 45 studies published between 1986 and 2025 and screened independently by two authors. The central claim is that magnification changes three variables at once: it permits smaller antrostomies (roughly 5 mm, versus conventional windows), it lets the surgeon see and seal small Schneiderian membrane (슈나이더막) tears before they become complications, and it enforces a neutral ergonomic posture with a defined team layout. The authors connect window size to biology through Avila-Ortiz's 24-procedure biopsy series, where larger window area correlated with less vital bone (21.7% ± 16.3% overall) and more residual allograft (23.5% ± 16.3%), and they aggregate technique-specific complication rates: piezo 7–8% versus rotary bur 20–30% perforation in lateral windows, 0% versus 11.1% for cortical-wall erosion versus direct outlining entry (p < 0.05), 2.83% perforation and 97.2% success for hydrodynamic PISE, and perforation ranges of 0–58.2% (lateral) and 0–21.4% (transcrestal) in the conventional literature. The clinical message is a staged adoption pathway — start with straightforward cases, use 4–7× for incision and flap, escalate to 10–15× for membrane elevation — with the explicit caveat that no randomized trial yet compares microscope-assisted against conventional sinus augmentation.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes the microscope as a three-way intervention (window size + membrane surveillance + operator posture) rather than an optical luxury, and supplies the actual antrostomy dimensions used: ~5 mm circular (MGES, 20 sinuses), 5 × 8 mm ellipse = 31.4 mm² (EBI Sinus Kit, 15 procedures), 5–6 mm circular piezo opening (147 sinuses).
- Supplies the histologic bridge from window size to graft quality: in 24 lateral procedures with allograft and 6-month core biopsies, window area correlated negatively with vital bone and positively with residual allograft (VB 21.7% ± 16.3%, RA 23.5% ± 16.3%, NMT 55.1% ± 8.5%).
- Gives an actionable magnification protocol by surgical phase — 4–7× with low illumination for incision/flap, 10–15× with higher light for membrane elevation and integrity checks — plus the transcrestal range of 6–20×.
- Specifies microsurgical instrument design criteria (lightweight, rigid, long handle seated on the thumb–index saddle, round cross-section for rotation, smaller working tips, non-magnetic, high-precision machining) and a six-handed ergonomic layout (surgeon 9–12 o'clock; patient supine, chair reclined 5°–10°; primary assistant 12–15 o'clock; secondary assistant 2–4 o'clock).
- Keeps the minimally invasive claims calibrated: osseodensification is promising but its "no perforation" claim rests on CBCT alone, and the cited SR of 74 studies / 4411 procedures (17.8% lateral, ~5% transcrestal perforation) provided no osseodensification-specific complication data.
- Adds a practical piezo workflow for thick lateral walls: outline/thin with rotary burs or osteotomy/bone-chip inserts, then switch to diamond-coated smoothing inserts once a bluish hue signals membrane proximity.

## Methodology

Narrative review (no meta-analysis, no formal risk-of-bias tool). Literature searched in PubMed, Web of Science and Google Scholar for 1986–2025, supplemented by manual reference-list and textbook checks; search strings combined "microscope-assisted / microsurgery / magnification" with "sinus augmentation / sinus floor elevation", "lateral window / transcrestal / osteotome" and "piezoelectric surgery / osseodensification". Two authors independently screened titles and abstracts, then full texts; study quality was "considered" by design, sample size and follow-up length. Thematic synthesis across 45 landmark and contemporary studies, organized as: fundamental principles, microsurgical lateral, microsurgical transcrestal, instrumentation (piezo, osteotome, osseodensification, PISE), ergonomics, conclusions. Illustrated with clinical/CBCT case material including a 3-year lateral-window follow-up and a 2-year transcrestal follow-up with an intraoperative micro-perforation sealed by collagen matrix.
