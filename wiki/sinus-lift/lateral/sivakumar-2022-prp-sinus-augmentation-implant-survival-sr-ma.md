## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis of 6 RCTs (188 patients, 781 implants) comparing platelet-rich plasma (PRP) plus bone graft versus bone graft alone in maxillary sinus augmentation before implant placement.

Pooled implant survival OR was 0.84 (95% CI 0.37–1.91, I2=0%) — no statistically significant difference between PRP and control groups; subgroup analysis by split-mouth vs. parallel-group design also showed no significant difference (p=0.45).

PRP's effect on dental implant survival in sinus augmentation is uncertain, not proven beneficial; clinicians should not expect PRP addition to meaningfully change implant survival outcomes, and evidence quality is limited by heterogeneous PRP protocols and some risk of selective-outcome-reporting bias.

## Summary

This PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42020184501) systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 6 RCTs (188 patients, 781 implants; publication years 2008–2017) comparing PRP added to bone graft versus bone graft alone in maxillary sinus augmentation prior to dental implant placement. Implant survival was 93.3–100% (weighted mean 98.05%) in the PRP arm versus 96.2–100% (weighted mean 97.25%) in the control arm — a numerically small, statistically non-significant difference. The pooled odds ratio for implant survival was 0.84 (95% CI 0.37–1.91, I2=0%), and subgroup analysis separating split-mouth from parallel-group trial designs (to avoid confidence-interval distortion from ignoring within-patient correlation) likewise showed no significant subgroup difference (p=0.45). The authors explicitly conclude the effect of PRP on implant survival is "uncertain" — a deliberately conservative reading that corrects an earlier systematic review which pooled implant failures by patient count (not implant count) and omitted 2 eligible RCTs. Risk of bias was rated "some concern" for 3 of the 6 included trials, chiefly due to lack of a pre-specified analysis plan enabling assessment of selective outcome reporting.

## Key Contributions

- Highest-evidence answer available (6-RCT SR+MA, implant-level pooling, 781 implants) to the narrow clinical question "does adding PRP to a sinus-lift bone graft improve implant survival?" — answer: no significant effect (OR 0.84, 95% CI 0.37–1.91).
- Methodologically corrects a prior review's patient-count pooling error and missed-RCT gap by pooling at implant-count level and including all 6 eligible trials.
- Properly separates split-mouth and parallel-group designs into subgroups (per Lesaffre et al. recommendation) rather than pooling them naively, which would distort the confidence interval.
- Reconciles the discordant primary literature: positive-signal individual studies (Lee 2008: 100% survival with PRP; Khairy: PRP improved bone density; Karaca: 100%/83% 1yr/5yr success with PRP) are statistically absorbed into a null overall estimate once pooled with the full RCT evidence base and adjusted for study design.
- Explicitly frames "uncertain" (not "no effect" and not "beneficial") as the honest conclusion given the wide CI (0.37–1.91) — the study is underpowered to detect anything but a large effect, and absence of significance is not equivalent to proof of no difference.

## Methodology

- PRISMA-guided SR+MA; PROSPERO CRD42020184501.
- RCTs only, ≥6-month mean follow-up, no language restriction; searched PubMed, CENTRAL, Cochrane registry, journal databases, Scopus to April 2021 + manual reference search.
- 2 independent reviewers for screening and data extraction; 3rd reviewer adjudicated disagreements (kappa=0.94).
- Cochrane RoB tool for RCTs (5 domains); R v4.1.1 (meta package); OR with 95% CI, Mantel-Haenszel fixed-effect model; split-mouth studies design-effect adjusted (assumed intra-patient r=0.07); Hartung-Knapp CI adjustment; heterogeneity via Cochran's Q / I2.

## Results

| Metric | Intervention (PRP) | Control (no PRP) |
|---|---|---|
| Patients | 188 total | — |
| Implants | 411 | 370 |
| Implant survival rate range | 93.3%–100% | 96.2%–100% |
| Weighted mean survival | 98.05% | 97.25% |
| Implant failures | 12 | 14 |

| Analysis | OR | 95% CI | I2 | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overall pooled (6 RCTs) | 0.84 | 0.37–1.91 | 0% | NS |
| Split-mouth subgroup | 1.40 | 0.52–3.76 | — | favors control |
| Parallel-group subgroup | 0.28 | 0.06–1.19 | — | favors intervention |
| Subgroup difference test | — | — | 0% | 0.45 (NS) |

Risk of bias: 3/6 studies "low risk," 3/6 "some concern" (selective outcome reporting, no pre-specified analysis plan). Most common complication: sinus membrane perforation/dehiscence, followed by sinusitis and peri-implant infection.

**Bottom line**: No statistically significant implant-survival benefit from adding PRP to bone graft in sinus augmentation. The point estimate (OR 0.84) trends toward benefit but the confidence interval (0.37–1.91) spans both meaningful benefit and meaningful harm — this is genuine uncertainty from a small evidence base (6 RCTs, 781 implants), not a precise null.
