## Three-line Summary

Line 1: Case-control CT volumetric study (n=19 patients, 20 sinuses) quantifying graft material requirements at 5, 10, and 15 mm augmentation heights in edentulous vs dentulous patients scheduled for lateral maxillary sinus floor elevation.
Line 2: Edentulous sinuses required significantly more graft volume at every height: 0.56 vs 0.41 cm³ (5 mm, p=0.027), 2.35 vs 1.76 cm³ (10 mm, p=0.018), 4.85 vs 3.80 cm³ (15 mm, p=0.044).
Line 3: Preoperative CT volumetric analysis enables rational graft quantity estimation and donor-site selection, with direct relevance to autograft harvest planning where volume is finite.

## Summary

This 2007 Korean case-control study addresses a practical gap in lateral sinus floor augmentation planning: how much graft material is actually needed? By using automated CT volume analysis (IDL 6.0), the authors measured maxillary sinus volumes at three clinically relevant augmentation heights (5, 10, 15 mm from the sinus floor) in 19 patients (20 sinuses) divided into edentulous (n=8) and dentulous (n=12) groups.

The key finding is that edentulous sinuses are larger — likely due to post-extraction sinus pneumatization — and therefore require substantially more graft material at every planned augmentation height. The difference is statistically significant across all three heights. This has direct practical implications: underestimating graft volume leads to underfilling; overestimating leads to unnecessary morbidity at donor sites.

The study also tabulates resorption rates for common graft materials: iliac crest autograft resorbs ~50%, mandibular autograft less, and deproteinized bovine bone (xenograft) ~20%. When combined with the volumetric benchmarks, these rates allow the surgeon to calculate the *actual* graft volume to harvest/prepare, accounting for expected resorption.

## Key Contributions

- Reference table of CT-measured sinus graft volumes at 5, 10, and 15 mm augmentation heights, stratified by edentulous vs dentulous status
- Quantitative evidence that edentulous sinuses require ~36% more graft volume than dentulous sinuses at 15 mm height (4.85 vs 3.80 cm³)
- Integration of graft resorption rates into volume planning protocol
- First volumetric CT analysis specifically targeting graft quantity estimation for lateral sinus lift in Korean literature (J Kor Oral Maxillofac Surg 2007)

## Methodology

- **Design**: Case-control (cross-sectional measurement)
- **Sample**: 19 patients (20 sinuses); edentulous n=8, dentulous n=12; all male, 60s
- **Imaging**: Preoperative CT scans; volume measured with IDL 6.0 automated CT volume analyzer
- **Measurement**: Sinus volume from floor to 5, 10, and 15 mm above floor
- **Statistics**: Mann-Whitney U test (non-parametric)
- **Limitation**: Assumed linear cross-sectional surface; did not model actual curved graft contour; no post-operative validation

## Results

| Augmentation Height | Edentulous (cm³) | Dentulous (cm³) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 mm | 0.56 | 0.41 | 0.027 |
| 10 mm | 2.35 | 1.76 | 0.018 |
| 15 mm | 4.85 | 3.80 | 0.044 |

**Graft resorption rates (reported from literature review within the paper):**
- Iliac crest autograft: ~50% resorption
- Mandibular symphysis/ramus autograft: lower than iliac
- Deproteinized bovine bone (xenograft): ~20% resorption
