## Three-line Summary

RCT (24 patients) compared 3D volumetric graft changes and implant stability after lateral sinus floor augmentation with dpDTM vs DBBM and simultaneous implant placement, with 6-month CBCT and resonance frequency analysis (RFA) follow-up.

Volumetric graft reduction was comparable (dpDTM 120.33 mm³ vs DBBM 108.51 mm³, p=0.690); ISQ values increased similarly to ~70 in both groups; mesial graft height reduction was significantly greater in dpDTM (−1.08 mm vs −0.58 mm, p=0.04).

dpDTM is a clinically viable alternative to DBBM for lateral sinus augmentation based on short-term volumetric and stability outcomes, though longer follow-up and histologic evaluation are needed.

## Summary

This single-center RCT from Prince of Songkla University (Thailand) evaluated 24 patients randomized to lateral sinus floor augmentation with either deproteinized human demineralized tooth matrix (dpDTM, n=12) or deproteinized bovine bone mineral (DBBM, n=12), each with simultaneous implant placement. Outcomes at 6 months included 3D volumetric graft reduction by CBCT and implant stability by resonance frequency analysis (ISQ).

The primary finding was no statistically significant difference in total volumetric graft reduction between groups (dpDTM 120.33 mm³ vs DBBM 108.51 mm³; p=0.690). ISQ values in both groups increased comparably from placement to ~70 at 6 months. The sole significant difference was mesial graft height reduction, which was greater in the dpDTM group (−1.08 mm vs −0.58 mm; p=0.04), though other height positions (mid, distal) showed no significant differences and the clinical relevance of this finding remains uncertain.

## Key Contributions

- First RCT using 3D CBCT volumetric analysis to compare dpDTM vs DBBM for lateral sinus augmentation
- Establishes dpDTM as producing equivalent graft volume stability and implant stability quotient (ISQ) to DBBM at 6 months
- Identifies mesial graft height as the only site where dpDTM shows greater reduction than DBBM
- Provides ISQ trajectory data for human tooth-derived vs bovine-derived grafts in sinus augmentation

## Methodology

- **Design**: Parallel-group RCT; block randomization; n=24 (12 per group); single center
- **Intervention A**: dpDTM — deproteinized human demineralized tooth matrix
- **Intervention B**: DBBM — deproteinized bovine bone mineral (xenograft reference)
- **Procedure**: Lateral window sinus floor augmentation with simultaneous implant placement
- **Imaging**: CBCT at immediate post-op and 6 months; 3D volumetric subtraction for graft volume change
- **Stability**: RFA/ISQ at implant placement and 6 months
- **Height measurements**: Sagittal and coronal reconstructions at mesial, mid, distal positions
- **Statistics**: Paired t-test, independent t-test, Mann-Whitney U test; α=0.05

## Results

| Outcome | dpDTM (n=12) | DBBM (n=12) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volumetric graft reduction (mm³) | 120.33 | 108.51 | 0.690 |
| ISQ at 6 months | ~70 | ~70 | NS |
| Mesial height reduction (mm) | −1.08 | −0.58 | 0.04* |
| Mid/distal height reduction | −0.59 to −0.93 | −0.55 to −0.82 | >0.05 |

*Statistically significant; clinical relevance uncertain (0.5 mm difference).

Both materials supported osseointegration with comparable stability trajectories. No implant failures reported in either group at 6 months.
