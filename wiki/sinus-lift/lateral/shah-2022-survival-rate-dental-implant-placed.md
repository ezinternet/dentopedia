## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (PRISMA, 17 studies, 1998–2020, 1,259 implants total) comparing implant survival between direct (lateral window) and indirect (osteotome/crestal, antral balloon, piezosurgery) maxillary sinus floor elevation techniques.

Pooled survival: direct 96.9% (95% CI 95.2–98.5%, 6 studies/337 implants/18 failures) vs indirect 97.0% (95% CI 95.9–98.1%, 13 studies/922 implants/35 failures) — no statistically significant difference.

Technique choice should follow clinical indication (residual bone height: <5 mm → lateral, ≥5 mm → crestal) rather than an expected survival advantage, since none was found; evidence base is limited to a single RCT among the 17 included studies.

## Summary

Shah, Chauhan, and Shah (2022) ran a PRISMA-guided systematic review and meta-analysis directly comparing implant survival between direct (lateral window) and indirect (osteotome/crestal, antral membrane balloon elevation, piezosurgery) maxillary sinus floor elevation techniques, pooling 17 studies (1998–2020, 1,259 implants total) via random-effects binary-proportion meta-analysis. Direct-approach implants (337 implants, 6 studies) pooled to 96.9% survival (95% CI 95.2–98.5%), and indirect-approach implants (922 implants, 13 studies) pooled to 97.0% survival (95% CI 95.9–98.1%) — heterogeneity was low in both arms (I² = 0%) and the two estimates showed no statistically significant difference. The authors conclude that technique should be selected on the basis of residual bone height and clinical indication (the paper reconfirms the standard <5 mm → lateral, ≥5 mm → crestal threshold) rather than an expected survival advantage, since the review found none. The evidence base is thin at the top of the design hierarchy — only one of the 17 included studies is an RCT — so this should be read as a technique-equivalence signal from mostly observational data, not a definitive head-to-head trial result.

## Key Contributions

- First page in this wiki to run a formal, symmetric random-effects proportion meta-analysis on *both* arms of the direct-vs-indirect sinus-lift split, rather than reporting survival for one technique family and citing the other narratively.
- Both pooled estimates are tight and low-heterogeneity (I² = 0% for both arms) despite the underlying studies spanning 1998–2020 and multiple technique variants within the indirect arm (osteotome, antral balloon elevation, piezosurgery).
- Reconfirms the residual-bone-height (RBH) decision threshold (<5 mm → lateral; ≥5 mm → crestal) that underlies technique-selection guidance elsewhere in this wiki.
- Reports an osteotome-only subset within the indirect arm (9 studies) pooling to 97.1% (95% CI 95.8–98.4%), consistent with the indirect-overall estimate.

## Methodology

SR+MA, PRISMA/PICOS framework; PubMed, Medline, Embase, Cochrane (1997–2020) + hand search; 895 records → 17 included studies (1 RCT, remainder prospective/retrospective/clinical-report). Risk of bias: Cochrane tool (RCT) + Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (non-RCTs), mostly rated "Good." Statistics: RevMan 5.4, binary random-effects proportion meta-analysis with forest plots for direct approach, indirect-osteotome subset, and indirect-overall.
