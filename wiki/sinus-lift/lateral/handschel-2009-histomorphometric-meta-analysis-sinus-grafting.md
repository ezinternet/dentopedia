## Three-line Summary

Histomorphometric meta-analysis of 30 human studies (53 material observations, English/German literature 1995–2006; external sinus floor elevation; weighted ANOVA, random-effects model, Tukey-Kramer post hoc) pooling Total Bone Volume (TBV) for autogenous bone, DFDBA, hydroxyapatite, β-TCP, anorganic deproteinized bovine bone (Bio-Oss®) and their combinations.

In the 4–9 month sampling cluster autogenous bone had the significantly highest TBV; from 9 months onward no material differed significantly, with Bio-Oss® + autogenous bone showing the highest albeit non-significant tendency, and pooled per-material TBV values were never reported as numbers (figures only).

The 2009 conclusion — autogenous bone if loading is expected within 9 months, otherwise Bio-Oss® + autogenous bone, with donor-site morbidity excluded — is the historical baseline against which later NMAs (autologous bone ranked 12th at 5–8 months) should be read.

## 세줄요약

2009년 조직형태계측 (Histomorphometry) 메타분석: 1995–2006년 영어·독일어 문헌에서 선별한 인체 연구 30편(이식재 관찰 53건)을 대상으로 측방 상악동거상술 (Lateral Sinus Floor Elevation) 후 총골부피 (Total Bone Volume, TBV)를 자가골 (Autogenous Bone)·탈회동결건조골 (Demineralized Freeze-Dried Bone Allograft, DFDBA)·수산화인회석 (Hydroxyapatite, HA)·베타-삼인산칼슘 (β-Tricalcium Phosphate, β-TCP)·탈단백우골무기질 (Anorganic Deproteinized Bovine Bone, DBBM; Bio-Oss®) 및 조합별로 가중 통합.

4–9개월 채취 구간에서는 자가골의 TBV가 유의하게 가장 높았고, 9개월 이후에는 이식재 간 유의차가 사라졌으며 DBBM+자가골 조합이 비유의하게 가장 높은 경향을 보였으나 이식재별 pooled TBV 값은 그림으로만 제시되고 수치로 보고되지 않았다.

"9개월 내 부하가 예상되면 자가골, 그 외에는 DBBM+자가골"이라는 이 결론이 이 분야의 역사적 기준선이며, 이후 베이지안 네트워크 메타분석 (Network Meta-Analysis, NMA)이 자가골 단독을 5–8개월 순위 12위로 평가한 것과 대비해 읽어야 한다.

## Summary

Handschel and colleagues pooled the published histomorphometric literature on external (lateral) maxillary sinus floor elevation to answer which grafting material produces the most bone at the histological level. Searching the National Library of Medicine for English and German literature from 1995 to 2006 with the keywords "sinus", "augmentation" and "bone substitute", they screened 120 records down to 30 studies with an evaluable Total Bone Volume (TBV, the percentage of a histological section consisting of bone tissue), yielding 53 material observations — 26 of the 30 studies prospective, one retrospective, three case reports. Because several materials appeared in only one or two reports, pooling was restricted to autogenous bone (9 usable observations), Bio-Oss® (13), Bio-Oss® + autogenous bone (8) and β-TCP (6); DFDBA, hydroxyapatite and single combinations could not be meta-analysed. TBV was weighted by observation count and inverse variance and compared with a random-effects weighted ANOVA using Tukey-Kramer adjustment.

The sampling times clustered into 4–9 months and ≥9 months. In the early cluster autogenous bone had the significantly highest TBV; in the later cluster no statistically significant difference remained between materials, although Bio-Oss® + autogenous bone showed the highest non-significant tendency and pure autogenous bone fell over time while Bio-Oss®, Bio-Oss® + autogenous bone and β-TCP rose (significantly so for Bio-Oss®). The authors explain this as two opposing processes — resorption of an unloaded autogenous graft (they cite up to 55% resorption of augmented autogenous bone within six months) versus partial resorption of the substitute particles with concurrent bone apposition — and conclude that autogenous bone is advantageous when functional loading is expected within nine months, otherwise a Bio-Oss® + autogenous bone combination is preferable, with donor-site morbidity ignored. They also concede that publication bias probably makes even these pooled figures optimistic, and position the work as "evidence based" only in the limited sense that the available literature is case reports and case series rather than RCTs.

**Historical baseline, not current ranking.** This is the field's classic 2009 reference point and should be read as such: it predates PRISMA, has no risk-of-bias assessment, no I², no funnel plot, and — importantly for citation hygiene — reports its material-level weighted means **only as bar charts (Figs 2–3)**, so no pooled TBV number per material exists in the text. Later syntheses answer the same question with far stronger designs and reach a different ordering: the Bayesian NMA by [[sinus-lift/lateral/trimmel-2021-relative-performance-various-biomaterials]] (34 RCTs, crestal core biopsies at 5–8 months) ranks autologous bone alone only twelfth by SUCRA, whereas Handschel found autogenous bone significantly superior in the 4–9 month window. The two are not irreconcilable — different time points, different biopsy protocols, and a TBV arithmetic that counts residual particles against the bone percentage — but neither should be quoted as a stand-alone verdict on graft choice.

## Key Contributions

- First quantitative, **time-stratified** synthesis of graft histology in external sinus elevation: two sampling clusters (4–9 months, ≥9 months) instead of a single pooled number.
- Establishes the classic **"convergence" claim** — autogenous bone leads early, all materials become statistically indistinguishable by 9 months — that every later NMA/SR on this question implicitly tests.
- Documents the **evidence-availability ceiling of the era**: only four material categories had ≥6 usable observations; DFDBA (1 observation), HA (3) and the 1:1 combinations were unpoolable.
- Uses TBV's dependence on residual particle volume to explain why xenograft and β-TCP start low and rise (particle resorption + bone apposition) while unloaded autogenous bone falls.
- Self-reports **publication bias** as a reason its own numbers are optimistic — an explicit ceiling on its own evidence grade.

## Methodology

- **Design**: meta-analysis of published human histomorphometric studies; no protocol registration, no PRISMA, no formal risk-of-bias tool (pre-PRISMA era).
- **Search**: National Library of Medicine; keywords "sinus" AND "augmentation" AND "bone substitute"; English and German; 1995–2006.
- **Inclusion**: external (lateral) sinus floor elevation in humans; materials used in several studies — autogenous bone, DFDBA, hydroxyapatite, β-TCP, anorganic deproteinized bovine bone (Bio-Oss®, Geistlich), and combinations. **Excluded**: animal studies, in vitro studies, reviews, interviews, editorials.
- **Pooling rules**: >80% Bio-Oss® mixtures pooled into the Bio-Oss® group; <80% (e.g. 50:50) assigned to Bio-Oss® + autogenous bone; β-TCP used without autogenous bone in almost all studies.
- **Outcome**: TBV (% of section that is bone tissue), taken from the paper or calculated; woven + lamellar summed; lateral and central biopsy values averaged.
- **Statistics**: weights from observation count and inverse variance; weighted ANOVA, random-effects model; Tukey-Kramer alpha adjustment; significance at p < 0.05.
- **n**: 120 records → 30 studies (26 prospective, 1 retrospective, 3 case reports) → 53 material observations → 41 remaining after mean/SD and single-case-report criteria → 36 usable observations across four material categories.

**Safety**: no allergic reaction to grafting material and no graft-related infection reported in any included study.

**Authors' mechanistic reading**: unloaded autogenous grafts resorb (up to 55% within six months, per the literature they cite), whereas Bio-Oss® and β-TCP particles are at least partly resorbed and replaced by bone guided from native bone surfaces — hence rising TBV in the substitute groups and falling TBV in the autogenous group. β-TCP is reported elsewhere to be fully resorbed within 12–18 months.
