## Three-line Summary

Case series (5 patients, private-practice referral cohort, 2 to 13 years after bovine-derived xenograft bone augmentation — mostly maxillary sinus lift and extraction-socket grafting) documenting late clinical complications of anorganic bovine bone (ABB) graft material.

Reported complications across the series: sinus and maxillary bone pathoses, displacement/scattering of graft particles, oroantral communication, implant failure, foreign body reaction with encapsulation, chronic inflammation, soft-tissue fenestration, and a bovine-bone-associated cyst — every case resolved or improved after surgical removal of the graft material.

Bovine-derived graft is not biodegradable in humans (persists 10-20+ years per cited histology), so late-onset symptoms years after an apparently successful augmentation should prompt evaluation for retained/migrated xenograft particles, and definitive management is surgical removal, sometimes technically demanding.

## Summary

This case series from a private periodontal/implant referral practice reports late (2-13 year) clinical complications in five patients who had received bovine-derived xenograft (anorganic bovine bone, ABB) for dental bone augmentation — most commonly in the maxillary sinus and posterior/anterior maxillary sites. The authors' central thesis is that ABB dental-literature is dominated by positive efficacy studies, while negative/complication data is scarce and likely underpublished, creating an incomplete picture of the material's long-term safety.

Across the five cases, complications included: encapsulated xenograft masses with chronic soft-tissue inflammation and gingival fenestration (Case 1, ~2 years); migrating bovine bone particles with peri-implantitis and facial edema requiring eventual implant removal in a full-arch rehabilitation (Case 2, 5 years); graft particle displacement causing sinus/maxillary bone pathology plus a histologically confirmed bovine-bone-associated cyst in a medically complex cardiac patient (Case 3, 7 years); bovine-bone-associated chronic maxillary sinusitis with 80% sinus opacification on CT in an elderly patient with multiple comorbidities (Case 4, 13 years); and particle migration causing soft-tissue fenestration with color/texture change over the graft site (Case 5, 3 years). In every case, surgical removal of the xenograft material resolved or substantially improved symptoms, and autogenous bone or connective-tissue grafting — never re-grafting with xenograft — was used for reconstruction.

The discussion situates these findings within prior histologic evidence that deproteinized bovine bone particles are not meaningfully resorbed in humans even at 10, 11, 14, and 20 years post-grafting (citing Mordenfeld, Ayna, and Traini), framing non-biodegradability as the mechanistic driver of the observed late complications: encapsulation, foreign body reaction (multinucleated giant cells, lymphocytes, plasma cells, histiocytes), and chronic inflammation. The authors also raise — without demonstrating an actual event — the theoretical long-latency risk of prion-mediated disease transmission (bovine spongiform encephalopathy / variant Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease), noting that standard manufacturing heat treatments (300 degC for Bio-Oss, 1100 degC for PepGen P-15) have not been proven to inactivate BSE prions, and that residual growth-factor/collagen proteins have been detected in commercially "anorganic" bovine bone products, raising questions about the consistency of the deproteinization process. Cited comparator data include a 4.3% acute sinusitis rate after xenograft-augmented sinus lift (Chirila) and an association between Bio-Oss and maxillary sinus fungus ball formation in all 13 reported cases (Scolozzi).

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates the sparse dental literature on **late (multi-year) clinical complications** of bovine-derived xenograft into a single consecutive case series spanning the full range: sinus/maxillary bone pathoses, graft particle displacement/scattering, oroantral communication, implant failure, foreign body reaction/encapsulation, chronic inflammation, soft-tissue fenestration, and (reported here for the first time in dental literature, per the authors) a bovine-bone-associated cyst.
- Frames non-biodegradability of anorganic bovine bone — supported by histologic data showing unresorbed particles at 10-20+ years post-grafting — as the mechanistic common thread linking these otherwise disparate complications.
- Demonstrates that surgical removal of the retained xenograft material is consistently curative across complication types, reinforcing removal (rather than antibiotics/anti-inflammatories alone) as the definitive management once a bovine-bone-related complication is identified.
- Raises (as a discussion point, not a demonstrated finding) the unresolved question of whether standard manufacturing heat treatments reliably inactivate BSE prions in bovine-derived graft material, and whether "anorganic" bovine bone products are consistently free of residual immunogenic protein.

## Methodology

Retrospective case series of five patients referred to a private practice specifically because of bone-augmentation complications (inherent referral bias — not a population-based incidence study). Complication onset ranged from 2 to 13 years after the original bovine-xenograft augmentation. Diagnostic workup included clinical examination/palpation, periapical radiography, CT imaging (Case 4), and histopathologic examination of excised tissue (Case 3: chronic mixed inflammatory infiltrate, hyalinized cyst wall, necrotic debris with cholesterol clefts, no malignancy). All patients underwent surgical exposure and removal of the bovine bone material; reconstruction, where performed, used autogenous bone blocks or autogenous connective-tissue grafts rather than re-grafting with xenograft. Written informed consent, HIPAA documentation, and local ethical approval were obtained for all cases.

## Results

- Complication latency ranged **2 to 13 years** post-augmentation across the five cases — these are late, not early-postoperative, events.
- Complication spectrum: encapsulated xenograft mass with chronic inflammation and gingival fenestration (Case 1); migrating particles with peri-implantitis leading to implant removal in a full-arch rehabilitation (Case 2); sinus/maxillary bone pathology with an associated cyst on histopathology (Case 3, 7 years); bovine-bone-associated chronic maxillary sinusitis with 80% CT sinus opacification (Case 4, 13 years); soft-tissue fenestration from particle migration (Case 5, 3 years).
- Surgical removal of the graft material resolved or substantially improved symptoms in **all five cases**, with no re-grafting with xenograft performed during reconstruction.
- Cited supporting literature: DPBB particles show no significant size change at 10-11 years (Mordenfeld), residual particles detected at 14 years (Ayna) and 20 years (Traini); acute sinusitis after xenograft sinus lift reported at 4.3% (Chirila); maxillary sinus fungus ball associated with Bio-Oss in all 13 reported cases in one series (Scolozzi).
