## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort at a tertiary referral center (2013-2021) of 425 implants in 152 patients undergoing sinus augmentation, testing whether hypertension (HTN) and antihypertensive drug classes are risk factors for early implant failure (EIF).

HTN itself was not significantly associated with EIF, but beta-blocker therapy was an independent risk factor (OR 4.05, p=0.018 patient level; OR 3.13, p=0.045 implant level), as were tobacco smoking (OR 8.52, p<0.001) and a higher number of implants per patient (OR 1.49, p=0.039); diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs and CCBs were not significant.

Evidence is abstract-only and retrospective; the EIF time window, EIF rate, CIs and sinus technique are not stated in the abstract, so the beta-blocker signal needs confirmation against the full text and prospective data.

## 세줄요약

상악동 거상술 (sinus augmentation) 후 조기 임플란트 실패 (Early Implant Failure, EIF)에 고혈압 (Hypertension, HTN)과 항고혈압제가 미치는 영향을 3차 의료기관에서 2013-2021년 후향적으로 분석 (환자 152명, 임플란트 425개).

HTN 자체는 EIF와 유의한 관련이 없었으나 베타차단제 (beta-blocker)는 독립 위험요인 (환자 수준 OR 4.05, p=0.018; 임플란트 수준 OR 3.13, p=0.045)이었고, 흡연 (OR 8.52, p<0.001)과 환자당 임플란트 수 증가 (OR 1.49, p=0.039)도 위험을 높였으며, 이뇨제·ACE 억제제 (ACEI)·ARB·칼슘채널차단제 (CCB)는 유의하지 않았다.

초록 기반의 후향적 근거이며 EIF 기간 정의, 실패율, 신뢰구간, 거상술 접근법은 초록에 없어 전문 확인과 전향적 자료가 필요하다.

## Summary
Retrospective cohort study conducted at a tertiary referral center between 2013 and 2021 on 425 implants in 152 patients who underwent sinus augmentation. The primary outcome was early implant failure (EIF). Univariate and multivariable analyses identified risk factors; p < 0.05 was significant. Hypertension did not significantly affect EIF. Beta-blocker therapy was an independent risk factor at both the patient level (OR 4.05; p = 0.018) and the implant level (OR 3.13; p = 0.045). A higher number of implants per patient (OR 1.49; p = 0.039) and tobacco smoking (OR 8.52; p < 0.001) were also associated with EIF. Other antihypertensive medications (diuretics, ACEIs, ARBs, CCBs) were not significantly associated with EIF.

## Key Contributions
- Sinus-augmentation-specific analysis of hypertension and antihypertensive drug classes for early implant failure, extending an earlier non-sinus antihypertensive cohort from the same research group.
- Reports patient-level and implant-level multivariable estimates side by side.
- Identifies beta-blocker therapy as the only antihypertensive class associated with EIF; smoking had the largest effect size (OR 8.52).

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort; single tertiary referral center; 2013-2021; 425 implants in 152 patients; primary outcome EIF; univariate then multivariable analyses at patient and implant levels; significance p < 0.05. Abstract-only: the country, EIF definition (time window), sinus technique (lateral vs transcrestal), simultaneous vs delayed placement, graft material and covariate list are not stated in the available text.

## Results

- Hypertension: no significant effect on EIF.
- Beta-blocker therapy: OR 4.05, p = 0.018 (patient level); OR 3.13, p = 0.045 (implant level).
- Tobacco smoking: OR 8.52, p < 0.001.
- Number of implants per patient: OR 1.49, p = 0.039.
- Diuretics, ACEIs, ARBs, CCBs: not significantly associated with EIF.
- The EIF count/rate and confidence intervals are not reported in the abstract.

## Limitations
- Abstract-only: EIF definition, event number/rate, CIs, model covariates and sinus technique cannot be verified.
- Retrospective single-center design; confounding by indication is possible (beta-blockers are prescribed for cardiac conditions beyond hypertension, which the abstract does not address).
- Few events are likely given the sample, so the beta-blocker estimate may be imprecise; this is an inference, not stated in the abstract.
- Implants are clustered within patients; the abstract does not say how clustering was handled at the implant level.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
In patients with hypertension scheduled for sinus augmentation with implants, hypertension per se was not a risk factor in this cohort, so it need not by itself change candidacy; flag beta-blocker use and smoking as higher-risk (smoking cessation counseling is the more modifiable factor) and consider limiting the number of implants placed per session. The beta-blocker finding conflicts with bone-protective mechanistic data cited in the wiki, so treat it as a hypothesis-generating signal and coordinate with the patient's physician rather than altering medication on this evidence alone.
