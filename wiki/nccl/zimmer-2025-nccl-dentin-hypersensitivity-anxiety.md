## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study (n=157, age 18–45 yr) found 상아질 과민증 (Dentin Hypersensitivity, DH) prevalence of 84.7% and 비우식성 치경부 병소 (Non-Carious Cervical Lesions, NCCL) prevalence of 49.7% in young adults. Anxiety measured by the Beck Anxiety Inventory was independently associated with higher DH prevalence, alongside NCCL presence, whitening toothpaste, and post-meal brushing. Soft toothbrush use and oral health knowledge were protective.

## Background

비우식성 치경부 병소 (NCCL) and 상아질 과민증 (DH) are closely linked conditions with multifactorial etiology including mechanical abrasion, erosion, and abfraction. While lifestyle and oral hygiene habits have been studied, the role of psychological factors such as anxiety in their prevalence had not been formally investigated prior to this study.

## Methods

- **Design**: Analytical cross-sectional study
- **n**: 157 participants; age 18–45 years
- **Assessment**: Clinical oral examination + structured questionnaire (lifestyle, daily habits)
- **Psychological measure**: Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI)
- **Statistics**: Poisson regression models with robust variance

## Results

### Prevalence

| Condition | Prevalence |
|-----------|-----------|
| Dentin Hypersensitivity (DH) | 84.7% |
| NCCL | 49.7% |

### Risk Factors for DH

| Factor | Direction |
|--------|-----------|
| NCCL (≥1 tooth) | Risk ↑ |
| NCCL (>4 teeth per individual) | Risk ↑ |
| Anxiety signs (BAI) | Risk ↑ |
| Whitening toothpaste use | Risk ↑ |
| Brushing immediately after meals | Risk ↑ |

### Protective Factors for DH

| Factor | Direction |
|--------|-----------|
| Soft/extra-soft toothbrush | Risk ↓ |
| Oral health knowledge | Risk ↓ |

## Clinical Significance

1. **Anxiety as a novel risk factor**: Psychological stress/anxiety may contribute to DH through bruxism, clenching, or altered salivary flow — adds a new dimension to NCCL/DH counseling.
2. **Behavioral risk factors confirmed**: Post-meal brushing (when enamel/dentin softened by acid) and whitening toothpaste (abrasive) are modifiable targets.
3. **Oral health education as intervention**: Knowledge-based protection suggests patient education directly reduces DH burden.
4. **High prevalence in young adults**: 84.7% DH rate in 18–45 yr suggests under-recognition in clinical practice; active screening warranted.

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional design: association, not causation
- Cannot determine whether anxiety causes DH or DH-related pain causes anxiety
- Abstract-only; full methodology (DH diagnostic criteria, anxiety cutoffs) not verifiable
- Likely single-center; generalizability uncertain
