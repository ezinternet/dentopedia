## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (PMC full text) of the evidence on extending a shortened dental arch (SDA) with a free-end saddle removable partial denture (FESRPD), covering the biomechanical problem, clinical outcome trials, patient attitudes, and case-selection factors.

The mixed tooth–mucosa support of a distal-extension denture rotates under load because mucosa is far more compressible than periodontal ligament — worse in the mandible, where the hard palate is unavailable to spread load — and the outcome literature is correspondingly negative: extending an SDA of three to five occlusal units produced no evident improvement in chewing, comfort, esthetics, dentition stability or TMJ function, no difference in tooth loss at 38 months or 5 years, and cantilevered resin-bonded bridges were more comfortable and acceptable at a lower biological price.

Case selection is where the paper earns its place: the proportion of SDA patients who actually seek restoration rises from 3% (second molars missing) to 58% (both molars) to 93% (premolars missing), and Witter's ladder recommends no prosthetic extension for slightly shortened arches, exceptional extension for moderately shortened ones, and extension of extremely shortened arches only back to the moderately-shortened level.

## 세줄요약

서술적 문헌고찰 (PMC 전문): 단축치열 (Shortened Dental Arch, SDA)을 원심 유리단 국소의치 (Free-End Saddle Removable Partial Denture, FESRPD)로 연장하는 것의 근거를 생역학·임상결과·환자태도·케이스 선택 인자 네 축으로 정리했다.

원심 유리단 의치는 치아–점막 **혼합 지지** 때문에 하중 하에서 회전한다 — 점막이 치주인대 (Periodontal Ligament, PDL)보다 훨씬 잘 압축되기 때문이며, 경구개로 하중을 분산할 수 없는 **하악에서 더 심하다**; 결과 문헌도 부정적이다: 교합단위 3~5개의 SDA를 연장해도 저작·편안함·심미·치열 안정성·턱관절 기능에 뚜렷한 개선이 없었고, 38개월·5년 치아상실 발생률에도 차이가 없었으며, 캔틸레버 접착성 브릿지가 **더 편하고 수용도가 높으면서 생물학적 대가는 더 낮았다**.

값어치는 **케이스 선택**에 있다 — 실제로 보철을 원하는 SDA 환자 비율은 제2대구치만 결손 시 3%, 제1·2대구치 결손 시 58%, 소구치까지 결손 시 93%로 오르며, Witter의 3단계는 경도 단축은 **연장 근거 없음**, 중등도는 예외적으로, 고도 단축은 **중등도 수준까지만** 연장하라고 권고한다.

## Summary

Every other paper in this category compares strategies at the group level and reports a tie. This one goes underneath the tie and asks which patients the tie does not apply to.

**The mechanism.** A distal-extension denture is supported by two tissues with different compressibility — periodontal ligament at the abutment end, mucosa over the saddle. Under occlusal load the mucosa yields far more, so the denture rotates about the axis joining the most distal rests. Those rotational forces are what reach the abutment teeth and the residual ridge. This is the mechanical account behind [[removable-partial-denture/drummond-2024-rpd-long-term-periodontal-health-sr-ma]]'s abutment extraction risk (OR 1.99), and it predicts the mandibular asymmetry directly: without a hard palate to spread load, the mandibular free-end saddle rotates more and resorbs more.

**The demand gradient.** Fueki's numbers reframe the consultation. Only 3% of patients missing second molars seek restoration; 58% when both molars are gone; 93% once premolars are involved. Most "molar gaps" are not a problem the patient has — they are a problem the clinician sees.

**The ceiling.** Witter's ladder is the most directly usable content here, and its third rung is the one clinicians skip: extremely shortened arches *should* be extended, but only back to the moderately-shortened level — at which point a fixed bridge may be sufficient. The recommendation is not "restore to full dentition."

**Where the RPD still wins.** The review is honest that FESRPDs persist for economic reasons — cheap, simple, non-invasive, within most dentists' skills — and that in *extremely* shortened arches they do improve masticatory performance. The case against them is strongest exactly where they are most often prescribed: routine molar replacement in a mildly shortened arch.

**Compliance predictor.** The single strongest determinant of whether a denture is worn is whether it replaces **anterior** teeth (Jepson, Armellini). A posterior-only RPD is the design least likely to be used — which connects directly to the ~40% discontinuation in [[removable-partial-denture/campbell-2017-rpd-clinical-need-for-innovation]].

## Key Contributions

1. **Mechanism**: differential PDL/mucosa compressibility → rotation about the distal rest axis → abutment stress and ridge resorption; worse in the mandible.
2. **Demand gradient** (3% / 58% / 93%) turning "need" into a measurable question.
3. **Witter's three-rung ladder** with an explicit restoration ceiling.
4. **Compliance predictor**: anterior replacement teeth, not posterior.
5. **Comparator**: cantilevered resin-bonded bridge — more comfortable, more acceptable, lower biological price.

## Methodology

Narrative review; no search strategy, inclusion criteria or risk-of-bias appraisal reported. Organised into the biomechanical problem, clinical outcome investigations, patient attitudes, and case-suitability factors, with findings attributed to named primary studies rather than pooled.

## Results

**Demand gradient (Fueki) — SDA subjects seeking prosthetic restoration**

| Extent of shortening | Seeking restoration |
|---|---|
| Second molar(s) missing | **3%** |
| First and second molars missing | **58%** |
| Premolar(s) missing | **93%** |

Predictors of seeking restoration: young age, more missing occlusal units, asymmetric arch, existing chewing complaint.

**Witter's case-selection ladder**

| Category | Definition | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Slightly shortened | Molars missing | **No evidence supports prosthetic extension** |
| Moderately shortened | Molars + first premolar missing | Exceptional cases, particularly esthetic |
| Extremely shortened (incl. asymmetric) | Molars + premolars missing | Extend — but only back to *moderately shortened*; a fixed bridge may suffice |

**Outcome findings**

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| Nijmegen group | Extending a 3–5 occlusal-unit SDA by FESRPD: no evident improvement in chewing, comfort, esthetics, dentition stability, TMJ function, functional habits |
| — | No difference in tooth-loss incidence at 38 months; confirmed at 5 years |
| Leak | Small socio-functional impact until fewer than three posterior functional units |
| Aras | No difference in masticatory performance with vs without FESRPD; *extremely* shortened arches did improve, though not to dentate levels |
| Creugers | Mandibular FESRPDs restore only a fraction of occlusal support; no TMJ effect |
| RCTs | Cantilevered resin-bonded bridges: effective alternative at **lower biological price**, more comfortable and acceptable than bilateral FESRPDs |
| Cited systematic review | Evidence insufficient to conclude relative effectiveness of FPD vs RPD for SDA |

**Satisfaction predictors (Knezović Zlatarić, n = 205).** Kennedy classification, base material, base shape and support type did **not** significantly affect general satisfaction or comfort. Higher education → less satisfied with esthetics and hygiene; males with mandibular RPDs → less satisfied with chewing; more missing mandibular teeth → less oral comfort.

**Limitations.** Narrative, not systematic — the selection of cited work is not auditable. Published 2013, predating current digital removable workflows and later SDA trial reports. **The paper's two summary tables did not survive PMC extraction** (problems of FESRPDs; factors affecting outcome) — open the PMC article for those. Two cited attitude studies used hypothetical scenarios in non-SDA populations, a caveat the review itself flags. The multicentre RCT's OHIP-49 improvement rests on 10 completers of 17. Reference numerals were stripped in extraction, so primary sources carry author names only.
