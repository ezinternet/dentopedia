## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (EFP workshop supplement; literature 1966–2020, PRISMA, RoB 2.0 + Newcastle-Ottawa) asking whether removable dental prostheses (RDPs) are more efficacious than no treatment, a shortened dental arch (SDA), or tooth-supported fixed dental prostheses (FDPs) in partially edentulous patients with periodontitis.

Two retrospective studies found RDPs raised tooth-loss risk relative to FDPs in patients with a periodontitis history, while prospective studies found RDPs could be maintained long-term without significant periodontal destruction; heterogeneity prevented meta-analysis, several studies showed increased plaque accumulation, and RDPs improved OHRQoL less than SDA or FDP treatment.

There is no strong evidence that RDPs *per se* cause periodontal destruction or tooth loss — the risk travels with plaque control and maintenance rather than with the appliance, making supportive periodontal therapy, not avoidance of the RPD, the operative decision.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (유럽치주학회 European Federation of Periodontology, EFP 워크숍 특집; 1966–2020 문헌, PRISMA·RoB 2.0·Newcastle-Ottawa): 치주염이 있는 부분무치악 환자에서 가철성 국소의치 (Removable Dental Prosthesis, RDP)가 무처치·단축치열 (Shortened Dental Arch, SDA)·치아지지 고정성 보철 (Fixed Dental Prosthesis, FDP)보다 유효한가를 물었다.

후향 연구 2편에서는 치주염 병력 환자에서 RDP가 FDP 대비 치아상실 위험을 높였으나 전향 연구들에서는 장기간 유지해도 유의한 치주 파괴가 없었다; 이질성 때문에 메타분석은 불가했고, 여러 연구에서 RDP가 **치태 축적을 증가**시켰으며, 구강건강관련 삶의 질 (Oral Health-Related Quality of Life, OHRQoL) 개선폭은 SDA·FDP보다 작았다.

**"RDP 자체가 치주 파괴나 치아상실을 일으킨다는 강한 근거는 없다"** — 위험은 장치가 아니라 **치태 조절과 유지관리**를 따라 움직이므로, 임상 결정은 "국소의치를 피한다"가 아니라 "치주 유지치료를 붙인다"가 된다.

## Summary

This review takes the most common reason clinicians refuse an RPD — *"the patient has periodontitis, the denture will finish off the remaining teeth"* — and tests it in exactly that population, against four comparators rather than the usual two.

The answer is genuinely mixed, and the mixture is informative. The two studies that found higher tooth loss with RDPs versus FDPs are **retrospective**; the studies finding no significant periodontal destruction are **prospective**. Heterogeneity was severe enough that nothing could be pooled. What is consistent across studies is not destruction but **plaque accumulation** — the appliance reliably makes the mouth harder to clean, and the periodontal consequence then depends on whether that is met with maintenance.

That distinction is the clinically usable part. It converts a categorical contraindication into a conditional one: the RPD is not the hazard, an unmaintained RPD is. It also sets expectations honestly on the benefit side — OHRQoL does improve, but less than with an SDA or a fixed prosthesis, and masticatory efficiency and nutritional status barely move.

Note the confounding this design cannot escape: patients who end up with an RDP rather than an FDP generally had a weaker starting dentition. Some of the observed tooth-loss difference is very likely indication, not causation.

## Key Contributions

1. **Tests the standard contraindication in the highest-risk subgroup** and finds no strong support for it.
2. **Four-comparator framing** (no treatment / SDA / FDP / RDP) rather than a two-arm question.
3. **Separates plaque risk from destruction risk** — the consistent finding is the former, which is modifiable.
4. **Calibrates the benefit**: OHRQoL gain is real but smaller than SDA or FDP; masticatory efficiency and nutrition change little.

## Methodology

PRISMA-guided systematic review for an EFP workshop supplement. Electronic search 1966–2020; two independent assessors. Risk of bias by RoB 2.0 and Newcastle-Ottawa. Heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis; synthesis is narrative by comparator and outcome domain.

## Results

| Domain | Finding | Evidence base |
|---|---|---|
| Tooth loss vs FDP | RDP increased risk (periodontitis history) | 2 retrospective studies |
| Periodontal destruction over time | Maintainable without significant destruction | Prospective studies |
| Plaque accumulation | Increased with RDP | Several studies |
| Masticatory efficiency / nutrition | Limited effect | Narrative |
| OHRQoL | Improved, but less than SDA and less than FDP (incl. resin-bonded) | Narrative |
| Quantitative pooling | **Not possible** (heterogeneity) | — |

**Limitations.** No meta-analysis, so no effect size attaches to the tooth-loss signal. That signal rests on the review's two weakest-design studies while the reassuring signal comes from prospective work — a discordance left unresolved. Abstract-only ingest: included-study count, follow-up ranges and per-study RoB judgements unavailable. Confounding by indication is intrinsic. Maintenance intensity, on which the conclusion implicitly rests, is not standardised across studies.
