## Summary
This systematic review by the Radboud/Wuhan group synthesised 8 studies (published 2003–2014) examining distal-extension removable dental prostheses (RDPs) in patients with shortened or extremely shortened dental arches. Masticatory performance was assessed by comminution (X50 particle size, 4 studies) and mixing ability (colour-change gum ratio, 3 studies). Patients with (E)SDA showed 28–39% lower masticatory performance versus complete-dentition controls. RDPs partially compensated this deficit: comminution improved 2–32% over the no-prosthesis condition, and mixing ability remained 28–83% lower on the RDP side versus the dentulous side. A dose-effect was demonstrated in 4 of 5 studies: a longer occlusal platform (more artificial teeth) produced better masticatory outcomes. Meta-analysis was not performed due to methodological heterogeneity.

## Key Contributions
- Establishes a quantitative functional baseline: (E)SDA patients experience a 28–39% masticatory performance deficit, not merely a subjective complaint
- Demonstrates RDP partial compensation of ~50% — useful for patient counselling when full recovery is not achievable
- Identifies a dose-effect relationship (4/5 studies significant): each additional artificial tooth improves masticatory performance, informing RPD design decisions
- Distinguishes ESDA (0–2 pairs) from SDA (3–5 pairs): greater deficit in ESDA provides functional justification for prosthetic extension in extreme cases
- Provides functional evidence complementary to OHRQoL and survival data in the SDA decision framework

## Methodology
- **Design:** Systematic review (no meta-analysis)
- **Databases:** PubMed, Medline, Cochrane Library, Embase, Science Direct
- **Search period:** 2003–2014
- **Included studies:** 8 (4 comminution, 3 mixing ability, 1 both)
- **Population:** Adults with SDA (3–5 posterior occluding pairs) or ESDA (0–2 pairs)
- **Intervention:** Distal-extension RDP vs no-RDP condition
- **Outcomes:** Comminution (X50), mixing ability (colour-change gum), chewing side comparison

## Results
- **(E)SDA deficit vs complete dentition:** 28–39% lower comminution or mixing ability
- **RDP comminution improvement:** 2–32% reduction in X50 vs no-RDP
- **RDP mixing ability (RDP side vs dentulous side):** 28–83% lower — residual deficit even with RDP
- **Dose-effect:** Longer occlusal platform → better comminution and mixing ability; significant in 4/5 studies
- **ESDA:** Greater masticatory deficit; RDP more beneficial relative to no-prosthesis condition
