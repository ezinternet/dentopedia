## One-line Summary
Randomized crossover trial (n=28) using occlusal splints to simulate unilateral SDA: PCS molar loss significantly impairs masticatory efficiency (ME) and subjective masticatory ability (SMA) (p<0.05) with compensatory loading on the second premolar, while non-PCS molar loss has no significant effect (J Prosthodont Res 2026).

## Summary
This experimental randomized crossover trial from the Institute of Science Tokyo enrolled 28 participants with intact dentition and used custom occlusal splints to simulate a unilateral shortened dental arch by eliminating molar contacts on one side. Participants underwent both PCS removal and non-PCS removal conditions in randomized order. PCS molar removal significantly decreased masticatory efficiency (comminution-based) and subjective masticatory ability (p<0.05). The second premolar on the removal side showed a compensatory increase in maximum occlusal force. In contrast, non-PCS molar removal produced no significant changes in overall masticatory function. A subgroup analysis revealed that individuals whose main occluding area was located at the second premolar (rather than the first molar) experienced a significantly smaller reduction in full-arch occlusal force after PCS removal, suggesting a naturally protective anatomical arrangement.

## Key Contributions
- Demonstrates that PCS-specific molar loss causes measurable functional impairment, whereas non-PCS loss does not — adds a side-specificity dimension missing from earlier SDA literature
- Identifies compensatory loading on the second premolar as a mechanical consequence of PCS molar loss, explaining why SDA extending to premolars can still be functional
- Defines a protective subgroup: individuals with MOA at the second premolar are less functionally affected by PCS molar removal
- Supports the clinical principle that PCS molar loss may warrant prosthetic intervention while non-PCS molar loss may not, refining the SDA decision threshold
- Provides experimental RCT design with controlled crossover, reducing confounding compared to retrospective SDA studies

## Methodology
- **Design:** Experimental randomized crossover trial
- **n:** 28 participants with intact dentition
- **Intervention:** Custom occlusal splints removing molar contacts on PCS or non-PCS
- **Randomization:** Group 1: PCS removal first; Group 2: non-PCS removal first
- **Outcomes:** Maximum Occlusal Force (MOF), Masticatory Efficiency (ME, comminution-based), Subjective Masticatory Ability (SMA)
- **Subgroup:** MOA location (second premolar vs first molar)
- **Analysis:** α=0.05; crossover comparison; subgroup analysis by MOA

## Results
- **Full-arch MOF:** NS between PCS removal and intact conditions
- **Second premolar MOF (removal side):** Significantly higher after PCS removal (compensatory loading)
- **ME:** Significantly decreased after PCS removal (p<0.05)
- **SMA:** Significantly decreased after PCS removal (p<0.05)
- **Non-PCS removal:** No significant effects on ME, SMA, or MOF
- **MOA subgroup:** Individuals with MOA at second premolar had significantly smaller full-arch MOF reduction after PCS removal vs MOA at first molar (p<0.05)
