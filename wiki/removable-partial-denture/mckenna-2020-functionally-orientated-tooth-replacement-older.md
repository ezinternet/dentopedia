## Three-line Summary

Primary Dental Journal clinical review arguing that the growing population of older adults who retain natural teeth into old age requires treatment planning that manages chronic caries and periodontal disease alongside tooth replacement, rather than defaulting to conventional prosthetic replacement.

Large numbers of these patients currently receive removable partial dentures, but functionally orientated tooth replacement following shortened dental arch principles can, in correctly chosen cases, deliver an acceptable, functional and esthetic reduced dentition.

The stated advantage is not primarily clinical outcome but **maintenance burden** — the approach significantly reduces ongoing demands on both patient and clinician and is therefore described as ultimately more cost-effective than removable alternatives.

## 세줄요약

Primary Dental Journal 임상 리뷰: 자연치를 노년까지 보유하는 고령 인구가 늘면서 임상가는 **만성 우식·치주질환 관리와 결손부 수복을 동시에** 다뤄야 하며, 통상적 보철 대체로 기본값을 두어서는 안 된다고 주장한다.

이 환자군의 상당수가 현재 가철성 국소의치 (Removable Partial Denture, RPD)를 받고 있으나, 단축치열 (Shortened Dental Arch, SDA) 원칙에 따른 **기능지향 수복 (functionally orientated tooth replacement)** 은 적절히 선택된 케이스에서 수용 가능하고 기능적이며 심미적인 축소 치열을 제공할 수 있다.

내세우는 장점은 임상 결과가 아니라 **유지관리 부담**이다 — 환자와 임상가 양쪽의 지속적 부담을 유의하게 줄이며, 따라서 가철성 대안보다 궁극적으로 더 비용효과적이라고 서술한다.

## Summary

This short review supplies the demographic frame the rest of the category assumes. Populations are ageing and, critically, ageing **with their teeth** — which changes the problem from replacing a lost dentition to managing chronic caries and periodontal disease *while* deciding what, if anything, to replace.

Its argument for shortened-dental-arch thinking is distinctive because it does not rest on clinical outcome. [[removable-partial-denture/walter-2021-shortened-dental-arch-15year-rct]] already established that the strategies tie on prosthetic success. This paper adds a dimension no trial measures: **maintenance burden**. Recalls, relines, repairs, hygiene demands, and the clinician time they consume — reduce those, and cost-effectiveness follows from a different direction than a lower initial fee.

That matters most in exactly the population it addresses. An older patient with reduced dexterity, competing medical appointments and limited transport does not experience a denture's maintenance schedule the way a fifty-year-old does.

**What this page cannot do.** The argument turns on the phrase *"in correctly chosen cases"*, and the abstract does not say what those criteria are. For an operational ladder, use Witter's classification in [[removable-partial-denture/nassani-2013-fesrpd-shortened-dental-arch-reappraisal]]. Nothing here is quantitative; the maintenance and cost claims are attributed to evidence not identifiable from the abstract, and the framing is UK primary care, where maintenance burden and cost-effectiveness are shaped by a health system unlike Korea's. Treat this as framing, not as an evidence citation.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demographic framing**: an ageing, increasingly dentate population changes what "replacing missing teeth" should mean.
2. Names the default being argued against — large numbers of older patients receiving RPDs.
3. **Shifts the SDA argument to maintenance burden**, a dimension the trial literature does not measure.
4. Derives cost-effectiveness from reduced ongoing maintenance rather than lower initial fee.

## Methodology

Narrative clinical review in a primary-care journal; no search strategy, inclusion criteria or risk-of-bias appraisal reported. Structured around the demographic shift, managing chronic dental disease alongside replacement in older adults, and functionally orientated tooth replacement as the alternative.

## Results

| Claim | Type |
|---|---|
| Population ageing; older patients retaining more natural teeth | Demographic observation |
| Large numbers currently receiving RPDs | Practice observation |
| SDA-principled replacement gives acceptable, functional, esthetic reduced dentition **in correctly chosen cases** | Conditional recommendation |
| Approach significantly reduces maintenance burden for patients and clinicians | Evidence-referenced (sources not in abstract) |
| Ultimately more cost-effective than removable alternatives | Derived claim |

No effect sizes, sample sizes or comparative statistics reported.

**Limitations.** Narrative review with no quantitative content — nothing here is citable as a number. The load-bearing qualifier "in correctly chosen cases" is unspecified in the abstract. Maintenance-burden and cost-effectiveness claims attributed to unnamed evidence. UK primary-care framing; both claims are health-system dependent. Guidance article, not primary research.
