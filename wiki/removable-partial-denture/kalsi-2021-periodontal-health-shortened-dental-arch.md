## Three-line Summary

Evidence-Based Dentistry commentary summarising the ten-year periodontal outcomes of the German multicentre randomised trial (14 centres, from October 2000) that allocated 215 adults aged ≥35 with bilateral missing molars to a removable partial denture (n = 109) or a shortened dental arch approach (n = 106).

By intention-to-treat, ten-year vertical clinical attachment loss differed significantly in favour of the SDA group in both the study jaw (0.79 mm) and across all teeth (0.69 mm), and the SDA group was also more favourable on bleeding on probing and plaque index.

The commentators conclude there is evidence of reduced periodontal impact from the SDA approach **while explicitly judging the difference unlikely to be clinically significant** — a sub-millimetre attachment difference over a decade is real but small next to the cost, invasiveness and maintenance differences between the strategies.

## 세줄요약

근거중심치의학 (Evidence-Based Dentistry) 논평: 독일 다기관 무작위 임상시험 (14개 기관, 2000년 10월 개시)의 10년 **치주** 결과를 요약한다. 35세 이상·양측 대구치 결손 성인 215명을 가철성 국소의치 (Removable Partial Denture, RPD, n = 109) 또는 단축치열 (Shortened Dental Arch, SDA, n = 106)로 무작위 배정했다.

배정치료분석 (Intention-To-Treat, ITT) 기준 10년 수직적 임상부착소실 (Clinical Attachment Loss, CAL)은 연구대상 악 (0.79 mm)과 전체 치아 (0.69 mm) 모두에서 **SDA 군이 유의하게 유리**했고, 탐침시 출혈 (Bleeding on Probing, BOP)과 치태지수에서도 SDA가 우수했다.

논평자들은 SDA의 치주 영향이 더 적다는 근거가 있다고 하면서도 **"그 차이가 임상적으로 유의할 가능성은 낮다"** 고 명시한다 — 10년에 걸친 1 mm 미만의 부착 차이는 실재하지만 두 전략 간 비용·침습도·유지관리 차이에 비하면 작다.

## Summary

This page exists because the same trial answers two questions differently, and the difference is where the clinical decision actually lives.

On **prosthetic success** over fifteen years, [[removable-partial-denture/walter-2021-shortened-dental-arch-15year-rct]] found the two strategies indistinguishable. On **periodontal health** at ten years, the SDA arm wins: 0.79 mm less vertical attachment loss in the study jaw, 0.69 mm across all teeth, plus less bleeding and less plaque. Randomised evidence, which is more than the observational literature on this question can offer.

Then the commentators do something worth preserving: they call the size of their own positive finding. A sub-millimetre attachment difference accumulated over a decade is statistically real and clinically marginal, and they say so. Reading this paper as "SDA protects the periodontium, therefore avoid RPDs" inverts its actual message.

The mechanism reading matters too. The BOP and plaque findings are what you would expect if the appliance simply makes the mouth harder to clean — which is the modifiable pathway [[removable-partial-denture/gotfredsen-2021-removable-partial-prosthesis-periodontitis-sr]] identifies. That points at maintenance intensity as the lever, not at strategy choice.

**Two cautions.** This is a commentary on a trial, not the trial report; numbers should ideally be traced to the primary publication. And the attrition is severe — 109/106 randomised became 79/71 analysed by ITT and just **25/22 per-protocol**, a per-protocol population under a quarter of those randomised, with no explanation available in the abstract.

## Key Contributions

1. **Randomised evidence** on RPD-versus-SDA periodontal impact, where the field otherwise relies on observational data.
2. **Supplies the periodontal axis** of the trial whose prosthetic axis Walter 2021 reports.
3. **Reports the full analysis ladder** (randomised → ITT → per-protocol), making attrition visible.
4. **Calls its own effect size** — significance reported together with the judgement that it is unlikely to matter clinically.

## Methodology

Primary design as summarised: multicentre RCT, 14 German dental schools/universities from October 2000. Adults ≥35 with bilateral missing molars, both canines and ≥1 premolar per side in one jaw. Central randomisation, permuted blocks stratified by age. Arms: posterior restoration with RPD, or fixed prosthesis following SDA principles. Follow-up at 6 months, annually to 5 years, then 8 and 10 years. ITT and modified per-protocol analysis; two-sided α = 0.05.

## Results

| Analysis set | RPD | SDA |
|---|---|---|
| Randomised | 109 | 106 |
| ITT / modified per-protocol | 79 | 71 |
| Per-protocol | 25 | 22 |

| Ten-year outcome | Direction | Magnitude |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical CAL, study jaw | Favours SDA | 0.79 mm (p ≤ 0.05) |
| Vertical CAL, all teeth | Favours SDA | 0.69 mm (p ≤ 0.05) |
| Bleeding on probing | Favours SDA | Significant |
| Plaque index | Favours SDA | Significant |

**Limitations.** Secondary source — a commentary, not the trial report. Severe attrition to a per-protocol population under a quarter of randomised, unexplained in the abstract. The commentators' own conclusion is that the difference is unlikely to be clinically significant. Plaque/BOP differences are consistent with a hygiene-access mechanism that maintenance can modify.
