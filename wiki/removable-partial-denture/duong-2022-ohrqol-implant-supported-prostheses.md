## Three-line Summary

Periodontology 2000 comprehensive review (PMC full text) of oral health-related quality of life in patients rehabilitated with fixed and removable implant-supported prostheses, analysed across functional, esthetic and cost-related dimensions, with a survey of the OHRQoL instruments themselves.

From the patient's perspective OHRQoL was **not significantly greater for dental implants than for conventional tooth-supported prostheses**, patients' and clinicians' assessments diverged (clinicians applying higher esthetic standards), soft-tissue-level versus bone-level implants and all-ceramic versus metal-ceramic restorations produced no significant differences in patient-perceived esthetics, and preoperative expectation strongly coloured perceived outcome while being a poor predictor of it.

For the removable axis, supporting a conventional removable partial denture with implants is cost-effective once payers will pay more than €80 per OHIP point gained, and a cited six-year comparison found OHRQoL improved only in the implant-supported fixed denture group, not in the fixed or removable partial denture groups.

## 세줄요약

Periodontology 2000 종합 리뷰 (PMC 전문): 고정성·가철성 임플란트 지지 보철 수복 환자의 구강건강관련 삶의 질 (Oral Health-Related Quality of Life, OHRQoL)을 기능·심미·비용 세 축으로 분석하고 측정도구 자체도 함께 정리했다.

환자 관점에서 OHRQoL은 **임플란트가 통상 치아지지 보철보다 유의하게 낫지 않았고**, 환자 평가와 술자 평가가 갈렸으며(술자가 더 높은 심미 기준), 연조직 수준 vs 골 수준 임플란트·전부도재 vs 도재용착금속관 간 환자 지각 심미에 유의차가 없었고, 수술 전 기대치는 인지 결과를 강하게 물들이면서도 결과의 예측인자로는 부적절했다.

가철성 축에서는 통상 국소의치에 임플란트를 붙이는 것이 **OHIP 1점당 €80 이상 지불 의향이 있을 때** 비용효과적이며, 인용된 6년 비교에서 OHRQoL이 개선된 것은 임플란트 지지 고정성 의치군뿐이었다.

## Summary

This review is in the RPD category for one reason: it is the strongest available answer to *"but wouldn't an implant be better?"* — and the answer, from the patient's side, is **not measurably**.

That is a narrow claim, and worth stating precisely. It does not say implants and conventional prostheses are clinically equivalent; it says that when you ask patients about their oral health-related quality of life, the difference does not reach significance. The authors' own methodological warning limits it further: there is no consensus on OHRQoL definitions or instruments, which makes cross-study comparison unreliable — a caveat that cuts against their headline as much as for it.

Two adjacent findings sharpen the consent conversation. Patients and clinicians do not agree on esthetic outcomes, with clinicians applying stricter standards — and the *degree of clinician specialisation* itself shifts esthetic index scores. And preoperative expectation strongly colours what patients report afterwards while being a poor predictor of it, which means expectation-setting matters more than expectation-measuring.

### The survival discrepancy — hold both numbers

The retrieved body cites Kurosaki's six-year comparison: implant-supported fixed denture 94.7%, fixed partial denture 77.4%, **removable partial denture 33.3%** cumulative survival, with OHRQoL improving only in the implant group.

**33.3% at six years cannot be reconciled with the 95.1% five-year survival pooled in [[removable-partial-denture/drummond-2024-rpd-long-term-periodontal-health-sr-ma]] as a like-for-like number.** Neither source is reproduced here with its survival definition; the gap is almost certainly definitional — whether a reline, repair or remake counts as failure, versus whether the prosthesis is still in service. Do not quote either as *the* RPD survival rate without opening the primary source and checking the endpoint. This page holds both precisely so the wiki cannot quietly adopt one and forget the other.

## Key Contributions

1. **No patient-perceived OHRQoL superiority for implants** over conventional tooth-supported prostheses.
2. **Clinician–patient perception gap** on esthetics, modulated by clinician specialisation.
3. **Expectation is influential but not predictive** of outcome.
4. **No patient-perceived difference** soft-tissue vs bone-level implants, or all-ceramic vs metal-ceramic.
5. **Willingness-to-pay threshold** for implant-assisted RPD: > €80 per OHIP point.
6. **Instrument non-standardisation** named as the field's central methodological problem.

## Methodology

Comprehensive electronic search of English-language publications to 2021. OHRQoL questionnaires and scales (GOHAI, OHIP, SOHSI, DIDL, OIDP, PPIDAQ, SOOQ) analysed and compared. Analysis structured into functional, esthetic and cost-related dimensions. Narrative synthesis — no pooling, no risk-of-bias appraisal.

## Results

| Comparison | OHRQoL finding |
|---|---|
| Implants vs conventional tooth-supported prostheses | **Not significantly greater** for implants |
| Soft-tissue-level vs bone-level implants | No significant esthetic difference |
| All-ceramic vs metal-ceramic | No significant difference in patient perception |
| Implant overdentures vs conventional dentures | Improved stability, function, satisfaction |
| Implant-assisted RPD with vs without magnetic attachments | Significantly increased with magnets |
| Implant-supported fixed vs FPD vs RPD (one cited study) | No significant OHRQoL difference |

**Cited six-year comparison (Kurosaki et al.)**

| Prosthesis | 6-yr cumulative survival | OHRQoL change |
|---|---|---|
| Implant-supported fixed denture | 94.7% | Significantly improved, sustained |
| Fixed partial denture | 77.4% | No improvement vs baseline |
| **Removable partial denture** | **33.3%** | No improvement vs baseline |

**Limitations.** Narrative review — no pooling or risk-of-bias appraisal. The authors' own central caveat is that OHRQoL instruments are not standardised, undermining cross-study comparison including their headline. **Extraction gap**: the concluding bullet list did not survive PMC retrieval (text jumps from "indicates that:" to CLINICAL CASES), so summary claims rest on the PubMed abstract; figures, tables and reference numerals were also stripped. The Kurosaki RPD survival figure conflicts sharply with Drummond 2024 and neither endpoint definition is available here. Implant-centred author group; the removable axis appears mainly as comparator. Appended clinical cases are illustrative, not evidence.
