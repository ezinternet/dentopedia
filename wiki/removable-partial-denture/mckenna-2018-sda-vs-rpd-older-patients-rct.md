## Three-line Summary

RCT in 89 partially dentate patients aged ≥65 (Cork, Ireland, 2 centres: university hospital CUDH and geriatric day hospital SFDH) comparing cobalt-chromium RPD vs shortened dental arch (SDA) restored with resin-bonded bridgework to premolar occlusion (10 occluding pairs), using OHIP-14 as primary outcome over 24 months.

SDA group maintained OHIP-14 improvements throughout 24 months (+5.9 pts from baseline, p<0.05); RPD improvements diminished after 6 months (+4.2 pts at 24mo, p<0.05); adjusted between-group differences significantly favoured SDA at both centres (CUDH: +2.9 pts, p<0.0001; SFDH: +7.9 pts, p<0.0001); RPD patients returned for maintenance >3× more frequently (p<0.001).

SDA was superior to RPD for OHRQoL in older partially dentate patients; benefit was greatest in older, more systemically unwell patients (SFDH geriatric cohort), supporting SDA as the preferred approach for frail elderly patients.

## 세줄요약

아일랜드 코르크 89명 (≥65세, 2개 센터: 대학치과병원 CUDH·노인주간병원 SFDH) — 코발트-크롬 국소의치 (Removable Partial Denture, RPD) vs 단축치열 (Shortened Dental Arch, SDA) 레진-접착 브리지워크로 소구치 교합(10쌍) 회복; 주요결과 구강건강관련 삶의 질 지수 (Oral Health Impact Profile-14, OHIP-14) 24개월 추적 무작위대조시험.

SDA군 OHIP-14 개선 24개월 지속(+5.9, p<0.05); RPD군 6개월 이후 효과 감소(+4.2, p<0.05); ANCOVA 조정 집단 간 차이: CUDH +2.9(p<0.0001), SFDH +7.9(p<0.0001) — SDA 유의하게 우수; RPD 유지방문 >3배 (p<0.001).

SDA가 고령 부분 무치악 환자에서 OHIP-14 우수; 효과는 연약(frail) 고령자(SFDH)일수록 더 컸으며, SDA가 이 집단의 우선 치료법으로 지지됨.

## Study Overview

| Variable | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | RCT, 2-centre, intention-to-treat |
| Setting | CUDH (Cork Univ. Dental Hospital) + SFDH (St Finbarr's Geriatric Day Hospital), Ireland |
| Patients | 89 completed (44 RPD, 45 SDA); age ≥65yr; mean age RPD 74.1yr / SDA 73.9yr |
| Recruitment | January 2011 – March 2013; follow-up completed May 2015 |
| Randomisation | Computer-generated, block, stratified by age and gender; allocation concealed |
| RPD intervention | Cobalt-chromium framework RPD replacing all missing teeth |
| SDA intervention | Resin-bonded bridgework (RBB) restoring premolar occlusion — 10 occluding pairs; teeth distal to SDA left unopposed |
| Primary outcome | OHIP-14 (Oral Health Impact Profile 14-item; lower = better) |
| Timepoints | Baseline, 1, 6, 12, 24 months |
| Statistics | Mixed model ANCOVA repeated measures; adjusted for age, gender, CIRS comorbidity score, social class, centre |

## Key Results

| Outcome | RPD @24mo | SDA @24mo | Between-group difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| OHIP-14 mean (SD) | 7.4 (3.3) | 4.0 (2.3) | — |
| Improvement from baseline @12mo | +5.7 (p<0.05) | +8.0 (p<0.001) | — |
| Improvement from baseline @24mo | +4.2 (p<0.05) | +5.9 (p<0.05) | — |
| **CUDH adjusted difference @24mo** | — | — | **SDA better 2.9 pts (p<0.0001)** |
| **SFDH adjusted difference @24mo** | — | — | **SDA better 7.9 pts (p<0.0001)** |

**OHIP-14 domains significantly favouring SDA at 24 months**: physical pain, psychological discomfort, physical disability, psychological disability, functional limitation.

**Temporal pattern**: Both groups improved initially. RPD improvements began declining after 6 months — particularly pronounced at SFDH. SDA improvements were maintained across all timepoints.

**Maintenance burden**: RPD patients returned for follow-up care >3× more frequently than SDA patients (p<0.001).

## Centre Comparison (CUDH vs SFDH)

| Centre | Patient profile | SDA benefit @24mo |
|---|---|---|
| CUDH | Standard university dental hospital patients | +2.9 OHIP-14 pts (p<0.0001) |
| SFDH | Older, more systemically unwell (geriatric day hospital); mobile dental unit | +7.9 OHIP-14 pts (p<0.0001) |

The significantly larger SFDH effect supports SDA as especially beneficial for frail, systemically unwell elderly patients. This extends Witter's original SDA rationale into a frailty dimension.

## Clinical Implications

- **Elderly patients (≥65yr)**: SDA with resin-bonded bridgework is the preferred restoration strategy over cobalt-chromium RPD for OHRQoL.
- **Frail elderly**: The benefit gap widens further with increasing frailty and systemic illness — strongest argument for SDA in geriatric settings.
- **Maintenance cost**: SDA substantially reduces recall burden — clinically meaningful for both patient quality of life and healthcare resource use.
- **Temporal advantage**: RPD provides early gains that diminish; SDA benefits accumulate and are sustained.

## Limitations

- 2-year follow-up (shorter than Walter 2021 15yr)
- Single operator per centre, single dental lab
- SFDH mobile dental unit environment may have disadvantaged RPD construction
- RBB as primary SDA restoration — not all practices use resin bonding
- No dietary/nutritional outcome in this paper (separate McKenna publications from same trial cohort)
