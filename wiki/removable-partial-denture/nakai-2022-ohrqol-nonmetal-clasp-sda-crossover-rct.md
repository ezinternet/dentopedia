## Three-line Summary

Three-period randomized crossover within-subject trial (n = 24, mean age 59.0 years; ISRCTN49105064) in patients with a fully dentate maxilla and unilateral distal-extension loss of two or three mandibular teeth, comparing a conventional metal-clasp RPD, a unilateral nonmetal clasp denture, and a shortened dental arch (no prosthesis), each used for two-week periods.

Both the unilateral nonmetal clasp denture (P = .002) and the shortened dental arch (P = .049) outperformed the conventional RPD on total OHIP, with medium and small effect sizes; the nonmetal clasp denture and SDA were equivalent to each other, both showed medium effects in the oral function dimension, and the nonmetal clasp denture was distinctly better on orofacial appearance than either conventional RPD (P = .001) or SDA (P = .006).

For a few unilaterally missing posterior teeth, the conventional clasp denture was the least-preferred of the three options and doing nothing was not inferior to it — while sex significantly modified the SDA result (P = .033), so patient-level preference should be elicited rather than assumed.

## 세줄요약

3기간 무작위 교차 (crossover) 동일피험자 내 대조 임상시험 (n = 24, 평균 59.0세; ISRCTN49105064): 상악은 완전 유치악이고 하악에 편측 원심 결손 2~3치가 있는 환자에서 통상 금속 클라스프 국소의치 · 편측 비금속 클라스프 의치 (Nonmetal Clasp Denture, NMCD) · 단축치열 (Shortened Dental Arch, SDA, 무보철)을 각 2주씩 사용시켜 비교했다.

NMCD (P = .002)와 SDA (P = .049) 모두 총 구강건강영향지수 (Oral Health Impact Profile, OHIP)에서 통상 국소의치보다 우수했고 (중간·작은 효과크기), NMCD와 SDA는 서로 동등했으며, 둘 다 **구강기능** 영역에서 중간 효과를 보였고, NMCD는 **구강안면 외모** 영역에서 통상 국소의치 (P = .001)·SDA (P = .006) 양쪽보다 뚜렷이 우수했다.

편측 소수 구치 결손에서는 **통상 클라스프 의치가 세 선택지 중 가장 선호되지 않았고, 아무것도 하지 않는 것이 그보다 못하지 않았다** — 다만 성별이 SDA 결과를 유의하게 조절했으므로 (P = .033) 환자별 선호를 가정하지 말고 물어야 한다.

## Summary

Every other comparison in this category is between patients. This one is **within** them: each of the 24 participants wore a conventional clasp denture, a nonmetal clasp denture, and nothing at all, two weeks each, in randomised sequence, and rated each. That design removes the between-patient confounding that weakens the observational literature here, and it answers a question the group-level trials cannot — not "which strategy performs better on average" but "which does this person prefer."

The result is uncomfortable for routine practice: the **conventional metal-clasp RPD came last**. The nonmetal clasp denture beat it with a medium effect, and *doing nothing* beat it with a small one. The two non-conventional options were statistically indistinguishable from each other on total score, differing only in appearance, where the nonmetal denture was clearly ahead.

This aligns with Witter's ladder in [[removable-partial-denture/nassani-2013-fesrpd-shortened-dental-arch-reappraisal]], which places small posterior losses in the band where there is no evidence supporting prosthetic extension — and it supplies the patient-side confirmation that ladder was missing.

**Two cautions before generalising.** Two weeks measures acceptance, not adaptation: a cast RPD is arguably judged before the patient has learned it, and none of the abutment or maintenance consequences that accrue over years can appear in this window. And nonmetal clasp dentures are widely regarded as interim rather than definitive; short-term preference is not an endorsement for long-term use.

**Scoring-direction note.** The abstract reports *higher* total OHIP scores in the arms it concludes were *better*, inverted relative to the standard convention where lower is better; the instrument variant is not recoverable from the abstract. This page carries forward the authors' stated conclusion, not the raw score direction.

## Key Contributions

1. **Within-subject crossover** — removes between-patient confounding that every other comparison here carries.
2. **"Doing nothing" as a randomised arm**, not an observational comparator.
3. **Dimension-level separation** — the nonmetal advantage is concentrated in appearance; function is shared with SDA.
4. **Sex effect on the SDA result** (P = .033), a patient-level modifier rarely reported.
5. Addresses the **commonest real-world indication**: a few unilaterally missing posterior teeth.

## Methodology

Three-period randomized crossover, within-subject controlled trial at a private practice. Twenty-four participants (7 men 62.6 [45–85] y, 17 women 57.5 [24–75] y), all fully dentate in the maxilla with unilateral distal-extension loss of two or three mandibular teeth. Six sequence groups; each option used for two-week periods; OHIP completed after each. Mixed-effects model (α = .05).

| NMCD vs SDA | Orofacial appearance | .006 | Medium |

| Men vs women, SDA arm | Total OHIP | .033 | — |

**Limitations.** n = 24 at a single private practice; the 7/17 sex split makes the sex-effect finding fragile. Two-week exposure captures acceptance, not adaptation, maintenance burden or abutment consequences — plausibly disadvantaging a cast RPD judged before adaptation. Scoring direction inverted relative to convention and instrument variant unrecoverable (see above). Washout, carry-over testing and dropout not reported. Nonmetal clasp dentures are generally considered interim prostheses. Applies specifically to 2–3 unilaterally missing mandibular teeth against a complete maxillary dentition.
