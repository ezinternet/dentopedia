## Three-line Summary

Fifteen-year German multicentre randomised trial (14 dental schools) in patients >35 years with missing molars in one jaw and at least the canine plus one premolar bilaterally, comparing a precision-attachment partial removable dental prosthesis (PRDP, N = 81) against ending the arch at the second premolar (shortened dental arch, SDA, N = 71).

Event-free success for moderate-or-worse failure fell below 50% at 14.2 years (PRDP) and 14.3 years (SDA), and in none of the analyses was a significant group difference found; about half of all patients kept their per-protocol prosthetic status entirely complication-free for more than ten years.

Over fifteen years, replacing the molars versus not replacing them did not measurably change prosthetic success, moving the decision away from prosthetic longevity and onto patient-side factors — cost, invasiveness, chewing demand, and maintenance capacity.

## 세줄요약

15년 독일 다기관 무작위 임상시험 (14개 치과대학): 35세 초과, 한쪽 악에 대구치가 없고 양측에 견치와 소구치 최소 1개가 남은 환자에서 정밀부착장치 유지 국소의치 (Partial Removable Dental Prosthesis, PRDP, N = 81) vs 제2소구치에서 치열을 끝내는 단축치열 (Shortened Dental Arch, SDA, N = 71)을 비교했다.

중등도 이상 실패에 대한 무사건 성공률이 50% 아래로 떨어진 시점은 PRDP 14.2년 · SDA 14.3년이었고 **어느 분석에서도 군간 유의차가 없었다**; 전체 환자의 약 절반은 10년 넘게 합병증 없이 최초 보철 상태를 유지했다.

15년에 걸쳐 "대구치를 채우느냐 안 채우느냐"가 보철 성공률을 바꾸지 못했다 — 결정의 축이 보철 수명에서 **환자 쪽 인자** (비용·침습도·저작 요구·유지관리 감당 능력) 로 옮겨간다.

## Summary

This trial is the reason "shortened dental arch" is a plan and not a compromise. Fifteen years of randomised follow-up across fourteen German centres found the two strategies indistinguishable on prosthetic success: 14.2 versus 14.3 years to the point where fewer than half of patients remained free of moderate-or-worse failure, with no analysis reaching significance.

Two secondary readings matter as much as the headline. First, the durability ceiling is lower than either camp usually admits — the proportion of patients whose prosthetic status has changed rises almost linearly from soon after delivery and covers the majority by fifteen years. Neither strategy is "definitive" in the sense of never needing intervention. Second, because the outcomes tie, the deciding variables are necessarily elsewhere: what the patient is willing to pay for, tolerate, and maintain.

Note the comparator's strength. The removable arm used **precision attachments**, not simple cast clasps. This is a comparison against good removable prosthodontics, which makes the null result harder to dismiss as a straw-man RPD — but also means it does not license extrapolating the null to a poorly designed clasp denture.

## Key Contributions

1. **The only 15-year randomised comparison** of replacing versus not replacing posterior teeth.
2. **Null result on prosthetic success** (14.2 vs 14.3 years; no analysis significant), which is itself the clinically actionable finding.
3. **Honest durability curve**: change in prosthetic status accumulates almost linearly and affects the majority by 15 years.
4. **Legitimises SDA** as an evidence-supported definitive plan in patients retaining canine + at least one premolar bilaterally.

## Methodology

Multicentre randomised trial, 14 German dental schools. Eligibility: age > 35, molars missing in one jaw, canine and at least one premolar present bilaterally in that jaw. PRDP arm: precision-attachment retained prosthesis replacing molars and missing second premolars. SDA arm: arch ended at the second premolar, present or replaced by a cantilever fixed dental prosthesis. Fifteen years of follow-up examinations. Primary outcome a composite of four failure categories, analysed by Kaplan–Meier.

## Results

| Outcome | PRDP (N = 81) | SDA (N = 71) | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time to <50% event-free success | 14.2 years | 14.3 years | NS |
| Per-protocol status complication-free >10 years | ≈ half | ≈ half | NS |
| Analyses reaching significance | — | — | **None** |

**Limitations.** Abstract-only ingest: the four failure-category definitions, per-arm event counts, attrition and subgroup analyses are unavailable, so the composite cannot be decomposed into repairs versus lost abutments. Eligibility required a relatively favourable residual dentition (canine + premolar bilaterally). The removable arm was precision-attachment retained, not cast-clasp. University-clinic maintenance intensity may exceed private practice. Patient-reported outcomes are not in the abstract.
