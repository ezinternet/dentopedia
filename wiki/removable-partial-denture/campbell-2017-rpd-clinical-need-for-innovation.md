## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (J Prosthet Dent) of the current state and future needs of removable partial denture treatment for the growing partially dentate adult population, covering designs, materials, fabrication technologies, and their impact on oral health and patient compliance.

Almost 40% of patients no longer use their RPD within five years — driven by sociodemographic factors, pain, and esthetics — and design, materials, ease of repair, patient education and follow-up all significantly affect treatment success, while research on both disease-oriented and patient-centred RPD outcomes is described as lacking.

Prosthesis survival and prosthesis *use* are different endpoints, so a high technical survival rate cannot be quoted to a patient as a success rate; the modifiable levers identified sit largely outside the laboratory.

## 세줄요약

서술적 문헌고찰 (Journal of Prosthetic Dentistry): 늘어나는 부분무치악 성인 인구를 대상으로 가철성 국소의치 (Removable Partial Denture, RPD) 치료의 현재 상태와 향후 과제를 설계·재료·제작기술·구강건강 영향·환자 순응도 측면에서 정리했다.

**5년 내 거의 40%의 환자가 RPD를 더 이상 사용하지 않으며** (사회인구학적 요인·통증·심미가 원인), 설계·재료·수리 용이성·환자교육·추적관찰이 모두 치료 성공에 유의한 영향을 미치는 반면, 질환 중심·환자 중심 결과 양쪽 모두에서 RPD 연구가 부족하다고 지적한다.

보철의 **생존**과 보철의 **사용**은 다른 종점이다 — 기술적 생존율을 환자에게 성공률로 말할 수 없다. 조절 가능한 지렛대(교육·추적·수리 가능성)는 대부분 기공실 바깥에 있다.

## Summary

Every other paper in this category follows the patients who kept their denture. This one counts the ones who stopped.

That reframing is the contribution. [[removable-partial-denture/drummond-2024-rpd-long-term-periodontal-health-sr-ma]] reports 95.1% five-year prosthesis survival — but survival measures whether the appliance still *exists and functions*, not whether it is in the patient's mouth. Set beside a ~40% five-year discontinuation rate, the two numbers describe the same treatment from opposite ends, and only one of them belongs in a consent conversation as a "success rate."

The stated drivers are telling: sociodemographics, pain, esthetics. None is a laboratory problem. Correspondingly, the levers the authors identify as significant — education, follow-up, ease of repair — are chairside and relational rather than technical. That is a different improvement agenda than "better framework alloys."

**How much weight to put on the 40%.** This is a narrative review, so the figure is a cited summary rather than a pooled estimate, and because the page was built abstract-only its primary source cannot be traced from here. Treat it as an order-of-magnitude signal that discontinuation is common — not as a rate to quote precisely, and not as a Korean rate, since abandonment tracks health-system coverage and cost closely.

## Key Contributions

1. **Names discontinuation as an endpoint** and attaches a magnitude (~40% within 5 years).
2. **Locates the causes patient-side** (sociodemographics, pain, esthetics) rather than technical.
3. **Identifies modifiable levers**: design, materials, repairability, patient education, follow-up.
4. **Flags the evidence gap** on both disease-oriented and patient-centred RPD outcomes.

## Methodology

Narrative review — no search strategy, inclusion criteria or risk-of-bias appraisal reported. Literature on partial edentulism treatment summarised with focus on current and emerging RPD designs, materials and production methods and their oral-health impact; patient satisfaction and compliance data reviewed separately.

## Results

| Finding | Value / direction |
|---|---|
| Patients no longer using their RPD within 5 years | **≈ 40%** |
| Drivers of discontinuation | Sociodemographics · pain · esthetics |
| Factors significantly affecting success | Design · materials · ease of repair · patient education · follow-up |
| State of the evidence base | Lacking, both disease-oriented and patient-centred |

**Limitations.** Narrative, not systematic; the 40% is a cited figure, not a pooled estimate, and its primary source is not recoverable from the abstract-only ingest — re-derive before quoting in a consent document. US/UK author group; discontinuation tracks health-system coverage, so transfer to Korean practice is unproven. Written 2017, predating mature CAD/CAM and 3D-printed removable workflows. No time-to-discontinuation curve, so early-versus-gradual abandonment is unknown.
