## Three-line Summary

EAO consensus-conference narrative review of English-language cost-effectiveness literature comparing dental implant therapy against root canal treatment, periodontal treatment, and fixed or removable partial dentures; heterogeneity across studies prevented meta-analysis.

Tooth-preserving care won where viable — root canal treatment was the most cost-effective initial option for a restorable incisor, orthograde retreatment the most cost-effective salvage, and non-surgical periodontal therapy both more effective and cheaper than an implant crown for molars with Class I furcation invasion — while after failed endodontic retreatment a single implant-supported crown became more cost-effective than fixed or removable partial dentures; the single-tooth implant-versus-bridge comparison split across evaluations, and mandibular implant overdentures improved OHRQoL at higher cost than complete dentures.

The cheapest durable option is usually the one that keeps the natural tooth, and prosthetic replacement should be costed against outcome (quality-adjusted tooth years) rather than fee alone — though the authors close by noting the underlying evaluations are too few and too varied to settle the question.

## 세줄요약

유럽골유착학회 (European Association for Osseointegration, EAO) 합의회의의 서술적 문헌고찰: 임플란트 치료를 근관치료·치주치료·고정성/가철성 국소의치와 비용효과 (cost-effectiveness) 관점에서 비교했으며, 연구 간 이질성이 커서 메타분석은 시행하지 못했다.

치아를 살릴 수 있는 곳에서는 보존이 이겼다 — 수복 가능한 절치는 근관치료가 1차로 가장 비용효과적, 실패 시 정방향 재치료가 가장 비용효과적, 1급 근분지부 병변 (Class I furcation invasion) 대구치는 비수술적 치주치료가 임플란트 단관보다 **더 효과적이면서 더 저렴**했다; 반면 근관 재치료까지 실패한 뒤에는 임플란트 단관이 고정성·가철성 국소의치보다 비용효과적이었고, 단일 결손의 임플란트 vs 브릿지는 평가마다 결론이 갈렸으며, 하악 임플란트 오버덴쳐는 총의치보다 삶의 질은 좋았으나 비용이 더 들었다.

**가장 싼 내구적 선택지는 대개 자연치를 지키는 쪽**이며, 보철 대체 선택은 수가가 아니라 **결과 보정 지표 (quality-adjusted tooth years)** 로 비용을 따져야 한다. 단 저자들 스스로 기반 평가 연구가 너무 적고 방법론이 제각각이라 확정할 수 없다고 닫는다.

## Summary

This is the wiki's cost axis for the restorative decision, and it exists because the clinical axis runs out. Once [[removable-partial-denture/walter-2021-shortened-dental-arch-15year-rct]] shows that replacing molars and not replacing them tie on prosthetic success over fifteen years, the decision has to be made on something else — and in practice that something is cost, invasiveness, and what the patient will maintain.

The review's ordering is more useful than any single number in it. Tooth-preserving treatment is repeatedly the economically dominant choice while the tooth is restorable: root canal treatment first, orthograde retreatment on failure, non-surgical periodontal therapy over an implant crown for a Class I furcation molar — that last one being **both more effective and cheaper**, which is a rare unambiguous result in health economics.

The removable partial denture appears here only as a comparator, and it loses to the single implant crown once endodontic retreatment has failed. That is a narrower claim than it sounds: it is about replacing one tooth in a mouth that otherwise has support, not about the multi-unit distal-extension situations where RPDs are actually chosen.

**Applicability caution.** Economic evaluations are bound to their fee schedules, insurance coverage and labour costs, and none of the included work is Korean. The transferable content is the ordering, not the amounts — and the paper is from 2015, before current digital removable workflows changed relative laboratory costs.

## Key Contributions

1. **Consensus-level ranking on cost-effectiveness rather than survival alone.**
2. **Establishes tooth preservation as economically dominant** while the tooth is restorable.
3. **Locates the crossover**: after failed endodontic retreatment, the implant single crown overtakes both fixed and removable partial dentures.
4. **Preserves genuine disagreement** in the single-tooth implant-versus-bridge comparison instead of smoothing it.

## Methodology

Narrative review for an EAO consensus conference. English-language cost-effectiveness literature on implant therapy versus complete dentures, fixed partial dentures, root canal treatment and periodontal treatment. No meta-analysis — high heterogeneity in economic methodology across evaluations.

## Results

| Clinical situation | Most cost-effective option |
|---|---|
| Restorable central incisor, irreversible pulpitis + coronal lesion | Root canal treatment (initial) |
| Initial root canal treatment failed | Orthograde retreatment |
| Root canal retreatment failed | Extraction + single implant crown > fixed or **removable** partial denture |
| Molar, Class I furcation invasion | Non-surgical periodontal therapy (more effective **and** cheaper) |
| Single missing tooth | Split: 2 evaluations favour implant crown (better outcome, lower cost); 1 finds implant costs more with greater survival |
| Edentulous mandible | Implant overdenture (2 or 4) improves OHRQoL, costs more than complete denture |

**Limitations.** No pooled estimates; each row is a narrative reading of few evaluations. Published 2015 — implant, laboratory and digital-removable costs have shifted. Jurisdiction-bound; no Korean data, so absolute conclusions do not transfer. The RPD is never the indexed intervention, only a comparator, so its own cost-effectiveness profile is uncharacterised.
