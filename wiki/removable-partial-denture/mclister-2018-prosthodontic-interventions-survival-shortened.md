## Summary
This PROSPERO-registered systematic review (CRD42017064851) assessed prosthodontic strategies—removable partial dentures (RPDs), fixed dental prostheses (FDPs), implants, and continued observation—in adult patients with shortened dental arches (SDAs). Ten studies were included: analyses from a multicentre German RCT (Wöstmann group), two analyses from McKenna's RCT in UK/Ireland, and prospective cohort data from Denmark and the Netherlands. Outcomes covered prosthesis survival, tooth loss risk, and oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL). Data heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis. The overall conclusion was that no single strategy can be recommended over another based on current evidence, highlighting a significant evidence gap and calling for CONSORT-compliant RCT reporting to enable future pooling.

## Key Contributions
- First PROSPERO-registered SR specifically examining prosthodontic effectiveness and tooth survival in SDA patients across all strategies
- Synthesises the three major European research programmes (Germany multicentre RCT, McKenna RCT, Danish/Dutch cohorts)
- Confirms that SDA concept maintenance is a clinically valid strategy — none of the compared interventions demonstrated superiority
- Identifies absence of CONSORT compliance as the main barrier to meta-analysis in this field
- Proposes standardised outcome reporting framework for future SDA trials

## Methodology
- **Design:** Systematic review of RCTs and non-randomised controlled trials (PRISMA)
- **Search:** Medline (OVID), EMBASE (OVID), Cochrane CENTRAL; 112 potentially relevant publications identified; 10 included
- **Population:** Adults with SDA (≥3–5 posterior occluding pairs per side)
- **Interventions compared:** RPD, FDP (including resin-bonded FDP), implant-supported prostheses, continued observation/SDA
- **Outcomes:** Prosthesis survival, residual tooth survival, tooth loss risk, OHRQoL
- **Included studies:** 5 publications from German multicentre RCT + 2 from McKenna UK/Ireland RCT + 3 prospective cohorts (Denmark, Netherlands)
- **Meta-analysis:** Not possible due to heterogeneity

## Results
- **Primary conclusion:** Insufficient evidence to recommend one tooth replacement strategy over another
- **OHRQoL:** SDA approach showed OHRQoL comparable to prosthetically restored groups in several studies; McKenna data suggested SDA/adhesive-FDP outperformed RPD at 24 months
- **Tooth loss:** Some retrospective data suggested higher tooth loss risk with RPDs vs FDPs in periodontally compromised patients, but not confirmed by RCT-level evidence
- **Prosthesis survival:** Not quantitatively pooled; limited by non-uniform reporting
- **Evidence quality:** Predominantly moderate; RCT heterogeneity prevented synthesis
