## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (33 studies from 2,322 records; five databases; GRADE) comparing oral health of individuals with eating disorders (EDs) against healthy controls across 12 outcomes, including dental erosion.

Caries and gingival inflammation severity, and prevalence of erosion, myofascial pain, malocclusion, oral lesions and dentine sensitivity were higher in EDs; salivary flow and pH were lower; bruxism did not differ; oral hygiene and periodontal status were inconclusive.

All estimates are of very low certainty; dentists may recognize EDs at routine visits and refer early (abstract-level findings only).

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰·메타분석 (Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis, SR+MA): 5개 데이터베이스 2,322건 중 33편을 포함하여 섭식장애 (Eating Disorders, ED) 환자와 건강 대조군의 구강건강 12개 지표(치아 침식 (Dental Erosion) 포함)를 비교.

ED군에서 우식·치은염증 중증도와 침식·근막통증·부정교합·구강병변·상아질 지각과민 유병률이 높았고 타액 분비량·pH는 낮았음; 이갈이 (Bruxism)는 차이 없음, 구강위생·치주 상태는 결론 불충분.

## Summary

Source: PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2024.105427). This SR/MA (J Dent 2024;151:105427) compares the oral health status of people diagnosed with eating disorders with healthy controls. Two reviewers independently screened, extracted data and assessed risk of bias; sensitivity and ED-type subgroup analyses were run and certainty was graded with GRADE. The prevalence of dental erosion was higher in EDs than in controls, alongside higher caries, gingival inflammation, myofascial pain, malocclusion, oral lesions and dentine sensitivity, and lower salivary flow and pH. Because the certainty of evidence is very low, the direction is consistent but the magnitude is uncertain.

## Key Contributions

- Systematic comparative evidence that erosion is more prevalent in EDs, placing the intrinsic-acid pathway named in [[dental-erosion/kanzow-2016-etiology-pathogenesis-dental-erosion]] on a graded evidence footing.
- Lower salivary flow and pH are reported as risk factors that may amplify erosive challenge.
- Clinical role: dental professionals may identify ED signs and refer early.

## Methodology

Five databases; 2,322 records; 33 included articles; dual independent screening, extraction and risk of bias; sensitivity analysis; subgroup analysis by ED type; GRADE. Outcomes: caries, erosion, oral hygiene, periodontal status, salivary flow/pH/composition, myofascial pain, bruxism, malocclusion, oral lesions, dentine hypersensitivity, OHRQoL.

## Results

Higher in EDs vs controls: caries and gingival inflammation severity; prevalence of erosion, myofascial pain, malocclusion, oral lesions, dentine sensitivity. Lower salivary flow and pH. No significant difference in bruxism; inconclusive for oral hygiene and periodontal status. Pooled effect sizes, heterogeneity and ED-type subgroup results were not available in the abstract and are not stated here.
