## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (Dig Dis Sci 2025; 4 databases searched July 1999 to June 2024, 1382 records screened, 22 studies included) of GERD/laryngopharyngeal reflux (LPR) and dental erosion in adults.

Dental erosion was significantly more prevalent in adults with GERD or LPR than in healthy controls, and subjects with erosion had a higher prevalence of LPR or GERD (abstract reports no pooled effect size).

The association supports screening for reflux in patients with erosion and vice versa, but heterogeneous outcome measures, diagnostic methods and reflux definitions limit precision; this record is abstract-level only.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review, SR; Dig Dis Sci 2025) — 4개 데이터베이스 (1999.7~2024.6) 1382편 중 22편 포함, 성인의 위식도역류질환 (Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease, GERD) 및 후두인두역류 (Laryngopharyngeal Reflux, LPR)와 치아침식 (Dental Erosion)의 관계.

GERD 또는 LPR 성인에서 치아침식 유병률이 건강 대조군보다 유의하게 높았고, 반대로 치아침식 환자에서도 LPR/GERD 유병률이 더 높았음 (초록에 통합 효과크기 없음).

침식 환자의 역류 선별과 역류 환자의 치과 검진 근거가 되나, 결과 지표·진단법·질환 정의의 이질성이 크고 본 기록은 초록 수준이다.

## Summary

Source: PubMed abstract ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10620-024-08824-9)). Wang, Zhou and Lei systematically reviewed adult studies on how gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) and laryngopharyngeal reflux (LPR) relate to the occurrence and severity of dental erosion. PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science, Cochrane Library and Scopus (English, July 1999 to June 2024), grey literature and reference lists yielded 1382 studies, of which 22 were included. Dental erosion was significantly more prevalent in adults with GERD or LPR than in healthy populations, and subjects with erosion showed a greater prevalence of LPR or GERD. The authors caution that outcome evaluation, diagnostic methods and disease definitions were heterogeneous and recommend a universal guideline definition of reflux in future studies.

## Key Contributions

- Systematic evidence, in adults, that reflux (GERD and LPR) is associated with dental erosion.
- Bidirectional association reported.
- Clinical message: detect and manage underlying factors of erosion in reflux patients.

## Methodology

- Systematic review; four databases plus OpenSIGLE, ICTRP and manual search; English, July 1999 to June 2024.
- 1382 records, 22 included; adult populations only.
- Risk-of-bias tool, study designs and any quantitative pooling are not stated in the abstract.

## Results

- Erosion prevalence significantly higher in GERD/LPR adults vs healthy populations.
- Reflux prevalence greater among subjects with erosion.
- No numeric effect sizes in the abstract; full-text data not retrieved.
