## Three-line Summary

Systematic review with random-effects meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42021270150; 9 databases to April 2024; 148 observational studies from 4403 retrieved) of erosive tooth wear (ETW) prevalence across seven risk groups.

Pooled prevalence ranged from 30% (legal drugs and medications) to 67% (drugs and alcohol); GERD 54.1%, eating disorders 65%, special diets 65.9%, acidic beverages 40%, occupational and sports 51%.

Every risk group showed elevated ETW, supporting targeted prevention and monitoring; subgroup pooling was limited by heterogeneity of wear indices (abstract-level only).

## Summary

This systematic review (Clin Oral Investig 2024; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00784-024-05963-1); source: PubMed, PMID 39387908) estimates the prevalence of erosive tooth wear in patients belonging to recognized risk groups: GERD, eating disorders, special diets, acidic beverages, drugs and alcohol, legal drugs and medications, and occupational or sports exposure. From 4403 retrieved records, 148 observational studies were included and pooled with random-effects models. All risk groups showed elevated ETW; pooled values ran from 30% to 67%. The authors conclude that these patients warrant greater preventive care and dental monitoring. Only the abstract was available, so confidence intervals, heterogeneity statistics, and risk-of-bias results are not recorded here.

## Key Contributions

- One review quantifying ETW prevalence across seven distinct risk groups.
- Large evidence base (148 observational studies) with pre-registered protocol (PROSPERO CRD42021270150).

## Methodology

Nine databases (PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, LILACS/BVS, SciELO, Scopus, Science Direct, Web of Science, Open Grey) to April 2024 plus grey literature; observational studies of children and adults with ETW prevalence data, no date or language limits; quality by Joanna Briggs Institute Prevalence Data Critical Appraisal Tool; random-effects meta-analysis, general and by subgroup. Subgroup analysis was not possible for all groups because of heterogeneous wear indices.

## Results

| Risk group | Overall prevalence of ETW |
|---|---|
| Legal drugs and medications | 30% |
| Acidic beverages | 40% |
| Occupational and sports | 51% |
| Gastroesophageal reflux disease | 54.1% |
| Eating disorders | 65% |

| Drugs and alcohol | 67% |

Selection: 4403 retrieved, 148 included. Values are as reported in the abstract.
