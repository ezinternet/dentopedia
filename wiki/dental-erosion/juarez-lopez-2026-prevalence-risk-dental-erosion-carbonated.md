## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42023398708) of 24 observational studies (21 cross-sectional, 2 case-control, 1 cohort; 21,541 adolescents, mean age 15.0 years) on dental erosion and carbonated drinks.

Pooled erosion prevalence was 37.6% (95%CI 26.3-49.7); carbonated drink intake above once daily was associated with erosion, OR 1.98 (95%CI 1.42-2.77; 7,785 participants; I2 = 80%).

Only 5 studies were poolable for the OR, exposure definitions differed, and the designs were mostly cross-sectional with self-report, so the association is an observational signal, not a causal estimate.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (Oral Health Prev Dent 2026; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3290/j.ohpd.c_2619); source: PubMed, PMID 42113589) pooled 24 observational studies of adolescents searched in five databases through December 2023. The random-effects prevalence of dental erosion was 37.6% (95%CI 26.3-49.7), with individual studies ranging from 1.4% to 80%. Five poolable studies defined high-frequency intake as more than once daily (or more than seven times weekly) and gave a pooled OR of 1.98 (95%CI 1.42-2.77) with substantial heterogeneity (I2 = 80%). Nineteen of 24 studies (74%) reported a significant association qualitatively. The authors caution that cross-sectional designs, self-reported intake and inconsistent exposure definitions limit interpretation.

## Key Contributions

- First adolescent-specific pooled prevalence of erosion in this wiki: 37.6% (95%CI 26.3-49.7).
- Pooled OR 1.98 for high-frequency carbonated drink intake, linking in-vitro erosive potential to clinical epidemiology.
- Documents drinking-behaviour modifiers reported in primary studies: bedtime intake (OR 7.8 in one Jordanian study), slow sipping, retaining the drink in the mouth, milk as protective.
- Transparent about weaknesses: 5 of 24 studies poolable, I2 = 80%, funnel asymmetry without significant Egger test.

## Methodology

- Registration and reporting: PROSPERO CRD42023398708, PRISMA.
- Databases: PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, LILACS, SciELO; 2000 to 17 Dec 2023; English and Spanish.
- Design: PEO framework; case-control, cross-sectional, cohort studies; in vitro studies, reviews and severity-stratified data excluded.
- Outcome: presence or absence of erosion, using BEWE (8 studies), TWI (6), O'Sullivan (4), Lussi (3), SEPRS (2), Carlsson (1).
- Exposure: sugar-containing effervescent drinks; OR analysis restricted to intake more than once daily or more than 7 per week.
- Appraisal and statistics: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (studies scoring 5 or more included); DerSimonian-Laird random effects; Egger test and funnel plot.

## Results

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Studies / participants | 24 / 21,541 (mean age 15.0 +/- 2.7 y) |
| Study designs | 21 cross-sectional, 2 case-control, 1 cohort |
| Pooled prevalence | 37.6% (95%CI 26.3-49.7) |
| Pooled OR, carbonated drinks | 1.98 (95%CI 1.42-2.77); 7,785 participants; I2 = 80%; p < 0.0001 |
| Quality (NOS) | 20 good, 4 fair; comparability weakest (11 studies) |
| Egger test | 7.38 (95%CI -14.05 to 28.81); p = 0.479 |

- Study-level ORs ranged from 1.04 (El Aidi 2011 cohort) to 7.8 (Al-Hadi 2013, drinking before bedtime); Arnadottir 2003 (OR 1.7, 95%CI 0.5-5.0) and Kirthiga 2015 found no association.
- The authors attribute risk to low pH (typically 2.3 to 3.5) against a 5.5 critical threshold and to titratable acidity; this is discussion, not a result of their pooled analysis.
- Sex-stratified analysis was not possible.
