## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA, PROSPERO-registered) of 23 cross-sectional studies (26 sources, 16,777 children aged 2-7 years) on erosive tooth wear in the primary dentition.

Pooled prevalence was 35.6% (95% CI 24.8-48.1); acidic drinks (OR 6.90), acidic food (OR 5.14), GERD (OR 1.98), holding drinks in the mouth (OR 1.82) and regular snacking (OR 1.58) were significant risk factors, while drinking with a straw (OR 0.58) and fruit consumption (OR 0.66) were protective.

GRADE certainty is very low to low (observational design, heterogeneity, few studies per factor; acidic drinks rest on a single study), so the ORs are hypothesis-grade and support history-taking for diet and reflux rather than precise risk quantification.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/ipd.13250)), Marschner, Kanzow and Wiegand (Int J Paediatr Dent 2024) pooled 23 cross-sectional studies (16,777 children aged 2-7 years) to estimate the prevalence of erosive tooth wear (ETW) in the primary dentition and to quantify anamnestic risk factors. The pooled prevalence was 35.6% (95% CI 24.8-48.1). After Bonferroni-Holm correction, GERD, acidic food, acidic drinks, holding drinks in the mouth and regular snacking were significantly associated with ETW; drinking with a straw and fruit consumption were inversely associated. Certainty of evidence was very low to low (GRADE), and several estimates rest on one or two studies.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA to pool a broad set of dietary and medical risk factors (not just a handful) for ETW in the primary dentition.
- Pooled prevalence 35.6%, independent of index type and examination mode on meta-regression.
- Mapped 52 risk factors and exposed the lack of standardized questionnaires.

## Methodology

PRISMA-compliant, PROSPERO CRD42023397767; PubMed/Embase/Scopus/Web of Science (Feb 2023) plus gray literature; cross-sectional studies in English or German; two independent reviewers; modified Newcastle-Ottawa scale; GRADE; random-effects models with fixed-effect sensitivity analysis, Egger test and meta-regression.

## Results

| Risk factor (studies) | OR (95% CI) | Holm-adjusted p |
|---|---|---|
| Acidic drinks (1) | 6.90 (4.64-10.25) | <.001 |
| Acidic food (2) | 5.14 (3.56-7.42) | <.001 |
| GERD (5) | 1.98 (1.37-2.87) | .008 |
| Holding drinks in mouth (2) | 1.82 (1.26-2.63) | .035 |
| Regular snacking (2) | 1.58 (1.18-2.10) | .041 |
| Drinking with straw (3) | 0.58 (0.42-0.80) | .019 |
| Fruit consumption (4) | 0.66 (0.54-0.81) | .002 |

- Not significant after adjustment: vomiting (OR 1.36, p=.139), fruit juice (OR 1.69 random-effects; fixed-effect OR 1.44, significant), carbonated drinks (OR 1.64), bottle feeding (OR 1.30), sweets (OR 0.92), fluoride toothpaste use (OR 1.09), gender (OR 1.12).
- Prevalence was lower in larger studies (p<.001); index, year and examination mode had no significant effect.
- GRADE: very low for prevalence and most domains; low for oral hygiene. Interpret acidic-drink OR cautiously (single study); fruit "protective" effect likely reflects unspecified fruit types.
