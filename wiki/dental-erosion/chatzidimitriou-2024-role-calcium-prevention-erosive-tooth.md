## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42021229819) of in situ randomized controlled trials; 869 records screened, 21 eligible, testing calcium formulations (juices, chewing gum, milk, CPP-ACP pastes) against erosive tooth wear.

Calcium added to juice drinks reduced enamel loss (blackcurrant vs orange juice: 2.6 times less enamel loss, p = 0.0001, I2 = 89%); chewing gum with vs without CPP-ACP showed no significant difference in surface microhardness (p = 0.31, I2 = 71%); results for milk and CPP-ACP pastes were contradictory.

Evidence is in situ (not clinical ETW progression), with 5 low, 12 unclear and 4 high risk-of-bias studies and high heterogeneity; calcium appears useful mainly as an additive to acidic drinks. Abstract-level only.

## 세줄요약

치아 침식마모 (Erosive Tooth Wear, ETW) 예방에 대한 칼슘 제제를 평가한 in situ 무작위대조시험 체계적 고찰 및 메타분석 (PROSPERO CRD42021229819)으로, 869편 중 21편이 선정되었다.

주스에 칼슘을 첨가하면 법랑질 손실이 감소했고(블랙커런트 주스가 오렌지 주스 대비 2.6배 적은 손실, p = 0.0001, I2 = 89%), 카제인 포스포펩타이드-비정질 인산칼슘 (Casein Phosphopeptide-Amorphous Calcium Phosphate, CPP-ACP) 함유 껌은 유무 간 표면 미세경도 차이가 없었으며(p = 0.31, I2 = 71%), 우유와 CPP-ACP 페이스트의 결과는 상반되었다.

근거는 in situ 모델이라 임상적 ETW 진행 감소의 증거로 볼 수 없고, 비뚤림 위험이 낮음 5편·불확실 12편·높음 4편에 이질성이 커서, 칼슘은 주로 산성 음료 첨가제로서 유용해 보인다(초록 수준 요약).

## Summary

This systematic review with meta-analysis (Evidence-Based Dentistry 2024, per PubMed) is presented as the first to evaluate calcium formulations for preventing erosive tooth wear. It included only in situ randomized controlled trials, so the evidence setting is controlled intraoral enamel models with surrogate outcomes (enamel loss, microhardness, roughness, softening depth, mineral change), not clinical ETW progression.

Of 869 retrieved studies, 21 were eligible. Calcium-fortified acidic drinks showed the clearest benefit, whereas CPP-ACP chewing gum did not differ from control gum on microhardness and milk and CPP-ACP pastes gave contradictory results. Clinical weight is therefore limited: the signal supports calcium as a dietary-acid modifier, not a proven restorative or clinical ETW-arresting treatment.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA on calcium for ETW prevention (as stated by the authors).
- Identifies calcium-enriched acidic drinks as the most consistent benefit; CPP-ACP gum and pastes are unsupported or inconsistent.
- Applies Cochrane RoB 2.0: 5 low, 12 unclear, 4 high risk of bias.

## Methodology

- PROSPERO CRD42021229819; electronic database search for in situ RCTs of calcium formulations.
- Outcomes: mean enamel loss, surface microhardness, surface roughness, mean erosion/softening depth, mineral loss/precipitation, remineralization.
- Random-effects meta-analysis; RoB 2.0 appraisal. Full methods not retrieved.

## Results

- 869 studies retrieved; 21 eligible.
- Juice drinks with added calcium: reduced enamel loss; blackcurrant juice had 2.6 times less enamel loss than orange juice (p = 0.0001, I2 = 89%).
- Chewing gum with vs without CPP-ACP: no significant difference in surface microhardness of eroded enamel (p = 0.31, I2 = 71%).
- Milk and CPP-ACP pastes: contradictory.
- Authors' conclusion: calcium formulations play an important role, mainly via addition to acidic drinks.
- Caveats: I2 of 71-89% (substantial heterogeneity); the juice contrast compares different juices; no per-study data or certainty grading in the abstract.
