## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis (PRISMA; searches to November 2024; 133 cross-sectional studies after STROBE screening) of tooth wear prevalence in the general population; tooth wear here spans attrition, erosion, abrasion and abfraction, and most included studies measured erosion.

Pooled global prevalence was 40.8% (95% CI 36.7-45.0) with I2 = 99.1%; Egger test showed no significant publication bias (p = 0.346); meta-regression found prevalence higher with larger sample size and lower with later publication year.

The headline number is useful as a burden estimate only: heterogeneity is near-total, no pooled estimates by wear type, age group or region are reported, and the authors' "increasing rate annually" conclusion conflicts with their own year meta-regression.

## 세줄요약

일반 인구의 치아 마모 (Tooth Wear) 유병률에 대한 체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review)과 메타분석 (Meta-analysis): 2024년 11월까지 검색, STROBE 선별 후 단면연구 133편; 치아 마모는 교모 (Attrition)·침식 (Erosion)·마모 (Abrasion)·치경부 쐐기결손 기전 (Abfraction)을 모두 포함하며 포함 연구 다수는 침식을 측정.

전 세계 통합 유병률은 40.8% (95% CI 36.7-45.0), I2 99.1%; Egger 검정상 출판 편향 유의하지 않음 (p = 0.346); 메타회귀에서 표본 수가 클수록 유병률은 높고 출판 연도가 늦을수록 낮았음.

이 수치는 질병 부담의 대략적 추정으로만 쓸 것: 이질성이 거의 100%이고, 마모 유형·연령·지역별 통합 추정치가 없으며, 저자들의 "해마다 증가" 결론은 자체 연도 메타회귀와 모순됨.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.puhip.2025.100708); PMID 41502587), Salari and colleagues (Public Health in Practice 2026) pooled 133 cross-sectional studies to estimate the global prevalence of tooth wear in the general population. Note the scope: this is all tooth wear (attrition, erosion, abrasion, abfraction), not erosive tooth wear alone, although most included studies are labelled as measuring erosion. The pooled prevalence was 40.8% (95% CI 36.7-45.0, I2 99.1%). The wiki files it under dental-erosion because no tooth-wear category exists.

## Key Contributions

- A general-population pooled figure to sit beside the risk-group (30-67%) and primary-dentition (35.6%) estimates already held.
- Meta-regression: larger studies reported higher prevalence; later-published studies reported lower prevalence.

## Methodology

Six databases to November 2024; cross-sectional studies only; Persian or English; STROBE score of at least 16 required (369 studies excluded as low quality); random-effects model in CMA v2; I2 and Egger tests; meta-regression on sample size and publication year. No protocol registration or GRADE rating is stated, and no pooled subgroup by wear type, age, region or diagnostic method is given.

## Results

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Records / included | 1900 / 133 |
| Pooled prevalence | 40.8% (95% CI 36.7-45.0) |
| Heterogeneity | I2 = 99.1% |
| Publication bias (Egger) | p = 0.346 |
| Meta-regression | prevalence rises with sample size, falls with publication year (p < 0.05) |

Individual study prevalences span roughly 1.4% to 97.9%. The abstract's "92,153 individuals with TW" is ambiguous (total vs affected). The factor table (GERD, acidic drinks, sex) lists p-values from a few single studies with conflicting results and is not a pooled association.

## Clinical insights

Use 40.8% as a rough statement that tooth wear in some form is common, not as an expected rate in any particular clinic population: with I2 of 99.1% and study prevalences from about 1% to 98%, age mix and diagnostic method drive the number. For erosive wear risk by patient group, the held risk-group review and the primary-dentition review are more specific. The authors' statement that prevalence is "increasing annually" is not supported by their own year meta-regression, which points the other way.
