## One-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (N=325 patients, mean 13.9yr, up to 24.8yr) of leucite ceramic in/-onlays vs. cast gold partial crowns by supervised undergraduates: both achieved ~91–92% survival at 23.5 years with distinct complication profiles — ceramic fracture dominates CIO failure, endodontic complications dominate CGPC failure.

## 한줄요약

## Summary

This retrospective cohort from the University of Würzburg tracked 325 patients with posterior partial-coverage restorations placed by supervised undergraduate students over up to 24.8 years (mean 13.9 ± 3.8 years). CIOs used leucite-reinforced glass ceramic (IPS Empress I, n=161) and CGPCs used a gold alloy (Degunorm, n=164). Of the 325 allocated restorations, 296 were clinically and radiographically assessed at final recall.

Kaplan-Meier survival curves showed both materials maintained high survival over two decades: CIO reached 93.9% at 15.2 years, declining modestly to 91.7% at 23.5 years; CGPC showed 92.6% at 14.9 years and 91.8% at 23.5 years. Annual failure rates were 0.5%/year (CIO) and 0.7%/year (CGPC). Success rates (accounting for complications not requiring replacement) were 92.1% (CIO) vs 84.2% (CGPC) over comparable mean service times, with CGPC showing higher rates of endodontic complications requiring treatment. Endodontically treated teeth performed significantly worse than vital teeth in both groups (p=.02). Overall clinical quality was rated good or excellent in ~70% of restorations in both groups with no significant inter-material differences.

## Key Contributions

- Longest published clinical follow-up for cast gold partial crowns (~24.8 years, n=163)
- Establishes CGPC annual failure rate benchmark: 0.7%/yr over 23.5 years
- Demonstrates that supervised student operators achieve long-term outcomes comparable to specialist literature (CIO 91.7%, CGPC 91.8% at 23.5yr)
- Identifies divergent complication profiles: ceramic fracture (CIO) vs endodontic complications (CGPC) — has direct implications for tooth pulp protection in CGPC preparation
- Confirms vital tooth status as a significant modifier of restoration success (p=.02) in both material types

## Methodology

**Study design:** Retrospective single-center cohort, University Hospital of Würzburg
**Sample:** 325 patients; posterior pre-molars and molars; 296 restorations assessed at final recall
**Groups:** CIO — IPS Empress I leucite-reinforced glass ceramic (n=161); CGPC — Degunorm gold alloy (n=164)
**Operators:** Supervised undergraduate students (standardized protocol)
**Follow-up:** Baseline to recall; up to 24.8 years; mean 13.9 ± 3.8 years
**Outcome measures:** USPHS criteria (modified) — clinical and radiographic assessment; Kaplan-Meier survival curves; biological and technical complication recording; caries risk and periodontitis assessment

## Results

| Outcome | CIO | CGPC |
|---|---|---|
| Survival at ~15yr | 93.9% (mean 15.2yr) | 92.6% (mean 14.9yr) |
| Survival at 23.5yr | 91.7% | 91.8% |
| Success at ~14–15yr | 92.1% (mean 14.5yr) | 84.2% (mean 14.9yr) |
| Annual failure rate | 0.5%/yr | 0.7%/yr |
| Ceramic fracture | 6.7% | — |
| Endodontic complication | 2.7% | **8.8%** |
| Secondary caries | 4.7% | 4.8% |
| Tooth fracture | 1.3% | — |
| Decementation | — | 2.0% |
| Good/excellent quality | 71.8% | 68.0% |

Endodontically treated teeth had significantly less success vs vital teeth (p=.02) in both material groups. No significant difference in quality ratings between material types.
