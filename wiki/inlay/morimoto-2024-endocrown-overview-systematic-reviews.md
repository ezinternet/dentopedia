## Three-line Summary

Overview of 9 SRs on one-piece endodontic crowns (엔도크라운, endocrown) in posterior teeth, synthesizing survival, success, and clinical outcomes through June 2024.
Ceramic and indirect resin endocrowns demonstrate high 5-year survival and success rates comparable to conventional crowns with posts; loss of retention is the dominant failure mode with similar rates for molars and premolars.
Firm conclusions are not possible due to low SR methodological quality, very high study overlap, and absent evidence for zirconia/metal materials and patient-reported outcomes (PROMs); primary RCTs are urgently needed.

## Summary

This 2024 overview by Morimoto et al. is the first systematic synthesis of SR-level evidence on **엔도크라운 (endocrown)** — a minimally invasive, single-piece indirect restoration that uses the pulp chamber for retention and eliminates the need for a separate post-and-core. The motivation is the growing trend toward minimally invasive dentistry (최소 침습 치과학, minimally invasive dentistry) and the clinical need for guidance on endocrown indications and longevity.

After searching MEDLINE/PubMed, Cochrane, Web of Science, and OpenGrey through June 2024, 468 records were screened and **9 SRs** were included. The best available evidence (from SRs providing meta-analyses with low ROBIS risk of bias) shows that:

- **Ceramic and indirect resin endocrowns** achieve **high 5-year survival and success rates**, comparable to conventional full crowns with posts and cores.
- The **dominant failure mode is loss of retention** (유지력 상실), not fracture.
- **Failure rates are similar between molars and premolars.**

However, the evidence base has critical structural weaknesses: most SRs rated **low or critically low on AMSTAR-2**, study overlap across SRs is **very high** (the same primary studies appear in multiple SRs), and there are no SRs on **zirconia or metal endocrowns** and no SRs addressing **환자 보고 결과 (PROMs)**. The authors conclude that endocrowns using ceramic and indirect resin can be a suitable, reliable option, but the literature does not yet support firm conclusions. More primary clinical trials — not further SRs — are the stated priority.

## Key Contributions

1. **Overview-level synthesis**: Aggregates 9 SRs rather than primary studies, exposing structural redundancy in the existing SR landscape.
2. **5-year benchmark**: Establishes that ceramic and indirect resin endocrowns match conventional post-crown survival at 5 years.
3. **Failure taxonomy**: Identifies retention loss (not fracture) as the dominant failure type, equal across tooth types.
4. **Evidence gap mapping**: No SR found for zirconia endocrowns, metal endocrowns, or PROMs — highlights where primary research is absent.
5. **Methodology critique**: AMSTAR-2 and ROBIS dual assessment exposes low quality and high overlap that undermine any strong recommendation.

## Results

### Survival and Success

| Material | 5-Year Outcome | Comparator |
|---|---|---|
| Ceramic endocrown | High survival/success | Comparable to conventional crown + post |
| Indirect resin endocrown | High survival/success | Comparable to conventional crown + post |
| Zirconia endocrown | Evidence limited | No SR found |
| Metal endocrown | Evidence limited | No SR found |

### Failure Analysis

- **Main failure cause**: Loss of retention (유지력 상실)
- **Molar vs. premolar**: Similar failure rates — no significant difference
- Fracture not identified as the primary failure mode

### Quality Assessment

| Tool | Finding |
|---|---|
| AMSTAR-2 | Low or critically low in most SRs |
| ROBIS | Low risk of bias in 2 of the 3 best-evidence SRs |
| Study overlap | Very high (same primary studies repeatedly included across SRs) |

### Evidence Gaps

- No SR on PROMs (patient satisfaction, pain, function)
- No SR on zirconia endocrowns
- No SR on metal endocrowns
- Heterogeneity in preparation design, operative steps, follow-up duration, and outcome definitions
