## Three-line Summary

In-vitro + numerical study (pig PDL specimens; μCT-based 3D FEM of mini-pig mandibular premolars) characterizing the non-linear, time-dependent viscoelasticity of the periodontal ligament (PDL) under continuous horizontal loading of 0.2 mm displacement over 5–600 s, fitted with a bilinear material model (two Young's moduli E1, E2 + ultimate strain ε12).

Stiffness increased with loading velocity: equivalent PDL stress reached 2.2 MPa at 5 s (E1=0.10, E2=0.45 MPa) vs 0.17–0.65 MPa at 600 s (E1=0.015–0.02, E2=0.072–0.12 MPa); E1 ranged 0.015–0.15 MPa and E2 0.04–1.00 MPa across loading times; equivalent total strain 1.3–3.5%.

PDL shows viscoelastic anisotropic behaviour (short-term responses overestimate stiffness via fluid pressurization); stress concentrates at furcation/narrow-root regions — a bilinear approximation adequately describes measured force/displacement curves and serves as start parameters for biphasic time-dependent PDL models.

## Summary

This combined experimental–numerical study (European Journal of Orthodontics 2013;35:811–818; Bonn/Zurich) quantified the non-linear, time-dependent viscoelasticity of the periodontal ligament (PDL) — the key regulator tissue for tooth movement. In the experimental arm, pig PDL specimens were loaded in vitro with continuous horizontal forces producing 0.2 mm displacement over loading durations spanning 5 to 600 s (nine time points, five specimens). In the numerical arm, three-dimensional finite element models of mini-pig mandibular premolars with surrounding tissues were built from micro-computed tomography (μCT) of the actual specimens, and tooth mobility was simulated under the same force systems. The PDL was modelled as a bilinear material (two Young's moduli E1 — low-strain stiffness, and E2 — higher-stiffness region after transition at ultimate strain ε12); tooth (E=20 000 MPa) and bone (E=2000 MPa) were isotropic/homogeneous (Spears 1993 parameters). Numerical force/displacement curves were iteratively fitted to experimental curves by varying loading velocities and non-linearity parameters.

PDL stiffness increased with loading velocity: highest equivalent stress 2.2 MPa at 5 s (E1=0.10, E2=0.45 MPa) versus 0.65 MPa at 600 s (E1=0.02, E2=0.12 MPa; lowest overall 0.17 MPa). E1 ranged 0.015–0.15 MPa, E2 0.04–1.00 MPa (in agreement with earlier linear-model literature), and E1/E2 varied statistically significantly across the 5–600 s loading spans (Friedman two-way ANOVA by ranks, p≤0.05). Equivalent total strain was 1.3–3.5%, converging to stable values regardless of velocity. Stress concentrated at furcation regions and PDL cervix near furcations (root-geometry-dependent) and scaled with E1/E2. The authors conclude that a bilinear approximation adequately describes measured force/displacement behaviour, that short-term loading overestimates stiffness via initial fluid pressurization (hydrodynamic cushioning), and that the derived parameters serve as start values for more complex biphasic, time-dependent and non-linear PDL models (e.g., Favino 2012).

## Key Contributions

- Combined experimental + μCT-based FEM pipeline within one specimen set — numerical curves fitted directly to measured force/displacement data at 0.2 mm displacement across 5–600 s.
- Quantified loading-velocity dependence of PDL stiffness: E1/E2 rise with velocity; equivalent stress ranges 2.2 MPa (5 s) → 0.17 MPa (600 s).
- Bilinear (E1/E2/ε12) parameter set for PDL suitable for FEA start parameters; values align with the linear-model literature range (Goel 1992; Pietrzak 2002).
- Mechanical explanation of fluid-phase (hydrodynamic) contribution in short-duration loading — links to mastication (impact-type) vs orthodontic (sustained) loading regimes.
- Stress/strain localization map: furcation and PDL cervix near furcations are high-stress zones — relevant to orthodontic force design and to the clinical mobility measurements of the same group ([[orthodontics/keilig-2016-in-vivo-measurements-pdl]], [[orthodontics/jepsen-2023-biomechanical-properties-periodontal-tissues-non-periodontitis]]).

## Methodology

In-vitro: pig PDL specimens; continuous horizontal force, 0.2 mm displacement, nine loading durations 5–600 s; force/displacement recorded. Numerical: μCT of specimens → 3D FEM of mini-pig mandibular premolar + PDL + bone; PDL as bilinear material (E1, E2, ultimate strain ε12), tooth E=20 000 MPa, bone E=2000 MPa, Poisson 0.30 (Spears 1993); iterative fitting of numerical to experimental curves for each specimen × velocity. Outcome: E1, E2, ε12 means/SD per loading time; equivalent stress & total strain distributions in PDL. Statistics: Friedman two-way ANOVA by ranks (SPSS v20), two-tailed p≤0.05.

## Results

| Loading duration | Equivalent stress | E1 (MPa) | E2 (MPa) | Total strain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 s | 2.2 MPa (highest) | 0.10 | 0.45 | — |
| 600 s | 0.65 MPa (specimen-dependent; min 0.17) | 0.015–0.02 | 0.072–0.12 | — |
| All spans | stress rises with E1/E2 | 0.015–0.15 | 0.04–1.00 | 1.3–3.5% |

- E1 and E2 vary significantly across the 5–600 s loading spans (Friedman, p≤0.05); ε12 relatively stable.
- Stiffness increases with increasing loading velocity; short-term responses overestimate stiffness (fluid pressurization).
- Stress concentration: furcation regions and PDL cervix near furcations; related to narrow root morphology.
- Strain converges to stable values per model across velocities (1.3% lowest, 3.5% highest).
- Bilinear fit: most numerical curves showed good agreement with experimental force/displacement curves.

## Clinical Takeaways

- PDL stiffness is load-rate–dependent over time spans 5–600 s — same displacement requires ~13× higher stress at 5 s than at 600 s (2.2 vs 0.17 MPa): short, impulsive loads (mastication, bruxism) engage the fluid phase; sustained loads (orthodontic forces) engage the compliant collagen network.
- Clinically measured mobility (e.g., [[orthodontics/jepsen-2023-biomechanical-properties-periodontal-tissues-non-periodontitis]] with the same Bonn ILD) is therefore loading-protocol–dependent — comparisons across studies require matched loading durations.
- Furcation/narrow-root zones carry peak PDL stress under load — a biomechanical rationale for furcation involvement in mobility/trauma sensitivity.
- E1/E2/ε12 parameter set can be reused directly as FEA start values for orthodontic force-system simulations.

## Limitations

- Animal tissue (pig PDL) — human PDL may differ in absolute values (the same group's later in-vivo human data: [[orthodontics/keilig-2016-in-vivo-measurements-pdl]]).
- Bilinear model cannot describe continuous modulus change with strain; a simplification of true viscoelastic behaviour.
- Small sample (5 specimens), non-parametric statistics only.
- Continuous (static) horizontal loading only — cyclic/frequency-dependent behaviour not directly tested.
