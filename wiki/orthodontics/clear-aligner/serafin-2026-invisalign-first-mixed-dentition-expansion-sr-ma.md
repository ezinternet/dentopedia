## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD420251083929, 9 studies included / 8 meta-analyzed) of Invisalign First for dental arch expansion in mixed dentition, pooling expansion predictability (achieved/planned ratio) separately for maxillary and mandibular arches using binomial logit-scale meta-analysis, with ROBINS-I risk-of-bias and GRADE certainty assessment.

Pooled predictability: maxilla 65% (GRADE low; tooth range permanent first molar 58% to deciduous canine 70%) and mandible 71% (GRADE moderate; deciduous canine 75% peak); correlation and regression analyses found no significant association between amount of planned expansion and achieved predictability in any subgroup.

Invisalign First achieves modest to moderate predictability for arch expansion in mixed dentition — the mandibular arch and anterior teeth are more favorable zones, the posterior maxilla is the weakest link, and planning decisions cannot rely on a dose-response assumption between target magnitude and achievability.

## Summary

This Angle Orthodontist systematic review and meta-analysis (Serafin, Boccalari, and Caprioglio 2026) is the first pooled analysis of Invisalign First's ability to achieve planned arch expansion in mixed dentition. Following PRISMA guidelines and PROSPERO registration (CRD420251083929), nine studies were identified (eight included in the meta-analysis), and predictability — defined as the ratio of achieved to planned expansion — was pooled separately for maxillary and mandibular arches using binomial modeling on the logit scale. The maxillary arch yielded 65% pooled predictability (GRADE low), with a tooth-level range from 58% at the permanent first molar to 70% at the deciduous canine. The mandibular arch performed better at 71% (GRADE moderate), peaking at 75% for deciduous canines. A key secondary finding was the absence of any significant dose-response: correlation and regression analyses showed no meaningful association between the amount of planned expansion and the achieved predictability in any subgroup or arch, meaning a clinician cannot expect larger planned corrections to achieve proportionally better or worse outcomes. Sensitivity analyses were robust and no publication bias was detected. The authors conclude that Invisalign First demonstrates modest to moderate predictability for mixed dentition arch expansion, with the posterior maxilla being the weakest zone and the mandibular arch showing greater reliability.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA to pool predictability data for Invisalign First expansion in mixed dentition across both arches — mandible (71%, GRADE moderate) outperforms maxilla (65%, GRADE low)
- Tooth-level resolution: deciduous canines most predictable (mandible 75%), permanent first molars least (maxilla 58%), consistent with the anterior-to-posterior gradient seen in prospective cohorts
- Null dose-response finding: no significant association between amount of planned expansion and achieved predictability in any subgroup — planners cannot assume that conservative targets are easier to achieve than aggressive ones
- GRADE certainty quantified separately by arch, providing a practical confidence level for clinical expectation setting
- No publication bias detected, and sensitivity analysis confirmed robust pooled estimates

## Methodology

- **Design**: systematic review and meta-analysis; PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD420251083929
- **Literature search**: electronic databases through July 2025
- **Eligibility**: studies evaluating Invisalign First for dental arch expansion in mixed dentition patients
- **Primary outcome**: predictability = ratio of achieved to planned expansion (%)
- **Meta-analytic method**: proportions meta-analysis, binomial model on logit scale, separate analyses for maxillary and mandibular arches
- **Secondary analyses**: correlation analyses and univariate linear regression for planned expansion amount vs achieved predictability, globally and by arch/tooth group
- **Risk of bias**: ROBINS-I tool (appropriate for non-randomized studies)
- **Publication bias**: funnel plot; Egger's test; Begg's test
- **Certainty of evidence**: GRADE framework
- **Included studies**: 9 (systematic review); 8 (meta-analysis)

## Results

**Maxillary arch:**
- Pooled predictability: **65%** (GRADE: low)
- Tooth-level range:
  - Permanent first molars: **58%** (lowest)
  - Deciduous canines: **70%** (highest)

**Mandibular arch:**
- Pooled predictability: **71%** (GRADE: moderate)
- Tooth-level peak:
  - Deciduous canines: **75%**

**Dose-response analyses:**
- Correlation analyses: no significant association between planned expansion amount and achieved predictability in any subgroup
- Univariate linear regression: no significant predictive relationship (global and by arch/tooth group)

**Robustness:**
- Sensitivity analysis: findings stable
- Publication bias: no evidence (Egger's / Begg's / funnel plot)
