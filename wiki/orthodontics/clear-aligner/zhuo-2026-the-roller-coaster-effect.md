## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study, 65 patients (34 mild crowding, 31 moderate-severe crowding) treated with clear aligner therapy (CAT) after four first-premolar extractions; posterior functional cusp distances (FCDs) measured pre/post-treatment on 3D digital models.

Post-treatment FCDs increased significantly in the mild-crowding group at all posterior sites (greatest at first molar: MB 1.64±1.39 mm, DB 1.48±1.55 mm, both p<0.001), while the moderate-severe crowding group increased only at the first molar MB cusp (p<0.001); smaller ANB angle and greater upper incisor proclination (U1-NA, U1-SN) were positively associated with FCD increase.

Posterior FCD increase (the "roller coaster effect," a posterior open-bite tendency) is most pronounced at the first molar and in mild-crowding cases, and can be used as a simple clinical indicator; case selection should weigh sagittal skeletal pattern (ANB) and incisor proclination before extraction-based CAT.

## Summary

This retrospective study quantifies the "roller coaster effect" (RCE) — a posterior open-bite tendency described in extraction-based clear aligner therapy (CAT) — using posterior functional cusp distance (FCD) as a simple, 3D-measurable clinical indicator. In 65 patients treated with CAT after four first-premolar extractions (34 mild crowding, 31 moderate-to-severe crowding), FCD increased significantly at all posterior sites in the mild-crowding group, with the largest changes at the mandibular first molar (mesiobuccal +1.64±1.39 mm, distobuccal +1.48±1.55 mm; both p<0.001). The moderate-severe crowding group showed a more limited effect, with significant FCD increase only at the first molar mesiobuccal cusp. Because mesial molar movement was minimal in this cohort, the authors attribute the FCD increase primarily to insufficient posterior vertical control (extrusion or failure to intrude) rather than horizontal drift. Correlation and regression analyses identified two cephalometric risk factors: greater upper incisor proclination (U1-NA, U1-SN) correlated with larger posterior FCD increases, and a smaller ANB angle was an independent predictor of RCE susceptibility. The authors conclude that posterior FCD is a practical clinical screening tool and that case selection — factoring in sagittal skeletal pattern and incisor inclination — is important before committing to extraction-based CAT.

## Key Contributions

- First study to operationalize the "roller coaster effect" (RCE) — previously a descriptive term from CAT-vs-fixed-appliance comparisons (Qian et al. 2024) — as a quantifiable, site-specific 3D metric (FCD) within a CAT-only extraction cohort.
- Shows that RCE severity is *crowding-dependent*: mild-crowding patients paradoxically showed a more widespread and larger-magnitude posterior FCD increase than moderate-to-severe crowding patients.
- Localizes the effect to the first molar (both MB and DB cusps) as the site of greatest change, implicating incomplete posterior vertical control rather than mesial molar drift as the operative mechanism.
- Identifies two independently actionable pre-treatment risk markers — ANB angle and upper incisor proclination (U1-NA/U1-SN) — that clinicians can screen for before starting extraction-based CAT.
- Provides a companion data point to [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/tang-2025-evaluating-the-effectiveness-of-clear]]: both papers come from a related author lineage studying extraction-CAT biomechanical shortfalls, one in the sagittal/vertical curve-of-Spee dimension (Tang 2025) and this one in the posterior vertical (open-bite/FCD) dimension.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort analysis, single or multi-clinician setting (not specified in retrieved abstract), non-registered (observational retrospective design).
- **Population (n=65)**: Patients treated with CAT following extraction of four first premolars, stratified into:
  - Mild crowding (Mi), n=34
  - Moderate-to-severe crowding (Mo-Se), n=31
- **Outcome measure — Posterior Functional Cusp Distance (FCD)**: Measured at four posterior sites per side/arch: second premolar, mandibular first molar (mesiobuccal and distobuccal cusps separately), and second molar.
- **Measurement technique**: Pre- and post-treatment digital models analyzed with a standardized 3D coordinate system (cusp-tip superimposition method), consistent with the digital-model measurement approach used in the related Tang 2025 curve-of-Spee study.
- **Analysis**: Paired pre/post FCD comparison within each crowding subgroup; between-group (Mi vs. Mo-Se) comparison of FCD increase magnitude; correlation analysis linking cephalometric variables (ANB, U1-NA, U1-SN) to posterior FCD increase; regression analysis to isolate independent risk factors for RCE.
- **Note on evidence granularity**: The PDF retrievable for this ingest is a Wiley abstract/reference landing page rather than the full-text article; exact statistical models, adjustment covariates, and full result tables beyond the abstract are not available in this artifact and should be revisited if full text becomes accessible.

## Results

| Group | Site | FCD change | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mild crowding (Mi), n=34 | First molar MB | +1.64 ± 1.39 mm | <0.001 |
| Mild crowding (Mi), n=34 | First molar DB | +1.48 ± 1.55 mm | <0.001 |
| Mild crowding (Mi), n=34 | All other posterior sites (2nd premolar, 2nd molar) | Significant increase | <0.001 (implied "at all posterior sites") |
| Moderate-severe (Mo-Se), n=31 | First molar MB | Significant increase | <0.001 |
| Moderate-severe (Mo-Se), n=31 | Other posterior sites | No significant increase reported | — |

- **Between-group comparison**: Mi group showed significantly greater FCD increases than Mo-Se group across compared sites (all p<0.05) — i.e., mild-crowding cases are at *higher* risk of RCE than more severely crowded cases, an outcome that runs counter to a naive assumption that "more crowding = more complex treatment = more risk."
- **Mechanism**: Minimal mesial molar movement in this cohort points to insufficient vertical control of posterior teeth (relative extrusion/incomplete intrusion) as the driver of FCD increase, rather than anteroposterior molar drift.
- **Cephalometric correlations**: Greater upper incisor proclination (larger U1-NA, U1-SN angles) positively correlated with greater posterior FCD increase.
- **Regression**: Smaller ANB angle was an independent risk factor for RCE — patients with a relatively reduced sagittal (Class II-to-Class III direction) discrepancy showed greater susceptibility.

## Clinical Implications

1. **Posterior FCD is a practical chairside/lab screening metric**: Clinicians (or lab technicians using digital model superimposition) can track FCD at the first molar as a simple indicator of emerging posterior open-bite tendency during extraction CAT.
2. **Mild-crowding extraction cases are not "low risk" by default**: This study found the opposite of what might be assumed — mild crowding cases showed *larger and more widespread* FCD increases than moderate-to-severe crowding cases, so case selection should not treat mild crowding as inherently safer.
3. **Pre-treatment cephalometric screening**: Patients with a smaller ANB angle and/or greater planned/existing upper incisor proclination (higher U1-NA/U1-SN) may warrant closer monitoring for posterior open bite during extraction-based CAT, or consideration of posterior vertical-control adjuncts (e.g., posterior bite ramps, careful attachment/staging design to limit molar extrusion).
4. **First molar is the sentinel site**: Since the largest and most consistent FCD change occurred at the first molar (both MB and DB cusps), this tooth is the most efficient single site to monitor for early signs of RCE during treatment.
5. **Complements Tang 2025's curve-of-Spee findings**: Both this paper and [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/tang-2025-evaluating-the-effectiveness-of-clear]] independently confirm that premolar-extraction CAT carries biomechanical penalties beyond simple space closure — one in the sagittal/vertical curve-of-Spee leveling dimension, the other in posterior vertical (open-bite) control — reinforcing the general clinical message that extraction-based CAT requires more conservative planning and closer monitoring of posterior occlusion than non-extraction CAT.

## Limitations

- Retrospective, non-registered design with unspecified control for confounders (aligner brand/attachment protocol, treating clinician, age/sex) beyond crowding severity.
- Only the abstract-level data were retrievable for this ingest; full regression coefficients, confidence intervals, and complete site-by-site tables were not available in the source PDF (a Wiley reference/abstract landing page, not the full article).
- FCD increase is a geometric/positional proxy for "posterior open bite tendency," not a direct functional occlusal outcome measure (e.g., occlusal contact area, bite force); the clinical significance threshold for a given FCD increase is not established by this study alone.
- No non-extraction comparator arm — unlike Tang 2025, this study cannot isolate how much of the observed FCD increase is specifically attributable to extraction versus general CAT posterior-control limitations.
