## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study (n=60: 30 extraction, 30 non-extraction) comparing mandibular curve of Spee leveling with clear aligner therapy (Invisalign); 3D model measurements (Geomagic Studio).

Extraction group showed significantly greater discrepancy between predicted and actual CoS change (−1.38±0.74 mm vs. −0.84±0.58 mm, p<0.001), indicating reduced effectiveness compared to non-extraction group.

Post-treatment CoS positively correlated with pre-treatment CoS and predicted CoS, negatively with initial crowding; suggests extraction patients face inherent biomechanical limitations in CoS leveling that current attachment designs cannot fully overcome.

## Summary

This retrospective study quantifies the biomechanical penalty of premolar extraction on curve of Spee (CoS) leveling during clear aligner therapy (CAT). While both extraction and non-extraction groups significantly undercorrected compared to ClinCheck software predictions, extraction patients undercorrected by 1.64× (−1.38±0.74 mm actual gap vs. −0.84±0.58 mm, p<0.001). The authors attribute this to three mechanisms: (1) insufficient aligner stiffness for bodily incisor movement under retraction forces (torque loss), (2) loss of anchorage support as the distance between support points (canine and second premolar) increases after first premolar extraction, and (3) inappropriate attachment design for extraction cases—current G5/G6 attachments cannot simultaneously address sagittal and vertical tooth movements. Regression analysis identified four significant predictors of post-treatment CoS: deeper pre-treatment CoS (β=0.265, harder to level), higher predicted CoS (β=0.749, stronger software accuracy), higher initial crowding (β=−0.091, reduces anterior retraction and torque loss), and use of Class II elastics (β=0.668, worsens CoS by increasing molar extrusion).

### Key Clinical Implications

1. **Extraction cases are inherently harder for CoS leveling**: Pre-counsel patients that CAT may not fully achieve predicted CoS improvement in extraction cases. Consider fixed appliances or phased treatment if maximal CoS leveling is essential.

2. **Attachment sequencing matters**: Use G5, G7, or horizontal beveled attachments initially for CoS leveling; once leveled, bond new G6 attachments for space closure. Current practice of applying G6 throughout extraction cases may compromise CoS outcomes.

3. **Class II elastics impair CoS leveling**: If intermaxillary elastics are needed for Class II correction, consider delaying their application until after CoS leveling or switching to mandibular second premolar attachment points to minimize molar extrusion.

4. **Moderate crowding may be beneficial**: Higher initial crowding was paradoxically protective (negatively correlated with CoS leveling failure), possibly because it limits anterior retraction force intensity and thus reduces torque loss. In severe crowding, CoS leveling may be secondarily improved.

5. **Pre-treatment CoS depth predicts difficulty**: Deep pre-treatment CoS (>2.5 mm) predicts greater leveling difficulty; plan for additional refinement aligner stages or consider hybrid CAT + fixed appliance approach.

## Key Findings

### Baseline Characteristics
- No significant differences in age, gender, initial crowding, overjet, or Angle classification between groups.
- Extraction group had significantly lower pre-treatment overbite (1.61±1.68 mm vs. 3.13±2.14 mm, p=0.004), more Class II skeletal patterns (13 Class II vs. 5 in non-extraction, p=0.042), and more hyperdivergent cases (6 vs. 0, p=0.037)—consistent with extraction case selection criteria.

### Pre-Treatment CoS Correlations
- **Pre-treatment CoS was significantly correlated with overbite** (r=0.510, p<0.001), overjet (r=0.524, p<0.001), and Angle classification (η=0.255, p=0.020).
- Pre-treatment CoS was **NOT correlated with** age, crowding, gender, or craniofacial patterns (sagittal/vertical skeletal classification)—contradicting some older literature (Trouten, Orthlieb) but supporting recent studies (Halimi, Rozzi, Farella, Krüsi).

### CoS Leveling Outcomes (Table: Key Results)

| Outcome | Non-extraction | Extraction | p-value | Interpretation |
|---------|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-treatment CoS (mm)** | 2.15±1.15 | 1.34±0.92 | <0.001 | Extraction group started with flatter CoS (selection bias toward milder vertical patterns). |
| Predicted CoS (mm) | 0.33±0.65 | −0.28±0.50 | <0.001 | ClinCheck predicted overleveling in extraction group (negative CoS). |
| **Actual post-treatment CoS (mm)** | 1.17±0.82 | 1.10±0.88 | 0.670 | No significant difference in final CoS between groups—extraction group ended at same level but achieved it via less leveling (higher starting point + less correction). |
| **Predicted CoS change (mm)** | −1.82±1.05 | −1.62±0.95 | 0.295 | Similar expected improvements; no difference in software prediction. |
| **Actual CoS change (mm)** | −0.98±1.02 | −0.24±0.99 | **<0.001** | **Extraction group changed 4× less** (only −0.24 mm actual vs. −1.62 mm predicted = 15% accuracy; non-extraction: −0.98 vs. −1.82 = 54% accuracy). |
| **Prediction gap (mm)** | −0.84±0.58 | −1.38±0.74 | **<0.001** | **Extraction group's shortfall was 1.64× larger**, the primary study finding. |

**Interpretation**: Both groups failed to achieve predicted leveling (expected from prior literature—aligner stiffness insufficient for anterior intrusion/posterior extrusion). However, extraction added a *secondary* biomechanical penalty: the gap between prediction and reality was 64% larger in extraction cases, suggesting factors specific to extraction (torque loss, anchor loss, attachment mismatch) further compromise CAT efficacy.

### Multivariate Linear Regression (Extraction Group Only)

Four significant predictors of post-treatment CoS in extraction cases:

1. **Pre-treatment CoS** (β=0.265, 95% CI 0.041–0.489, p=0.022)
   - For every 1 mm increase in baseline CoS depth, final CoS increases 0.27 mm (i.e., less leveling achieved).
   - Clinical interpretation: Deep baseline CoS increases CoS leveling difficulty; risk factor for undercorrection.

2. **Predicted CoS** (β=0.749, 95% CI 0.376–1.121, p<0.001) — *strongest predictor*
   - ClinCheck's prediction is reasonably accurate; higher software prediction → higher actual outcome.
   - Suggests aligner software captures biomechanical constraints fairly well; if software predicts poor leveling, trust it.

3. **Initial crowding** (β=−0.091, 95% CI −0.174 to −0.009, p=0.031)
   - Higher crowding → flatter post-treatment CoS (paradoxical protective effect).
   - Likely mechanism: Crowding reduces available space for anterior retraction, limiting incisor bodily movement and thus reducing torque loss during retraction.
   - Clinical implication: Patients with crowding may need sequential treatment (resolve crowding first with minimal anterior retraction, then focus on CoS leveling).

4. **Class II intermaxillary elastics** (β=0.668, 95% CI 0.146–1.190, p=0.013)
   - Use of Class II elastics → higher post-treatment CoS (less leveling).
   - Finite element analysis (Wang et al. 2022) showed Class II elastics increase molar extrusion and second premolar intrusion, both counteracting CoS leveling.
   - Clinical recommendation: When Class II correction is needed in extraction cases, consider delaying elastics until after CoS leveling, or attach elastics to second premolars rather than first molars to reduce extrusion.

**Non-significant predictors**:
- **Attachment type** (L3, L5, L6, L7): Rectangle vs. optimized made no difference (p range 0.303–0.627). Suggests current G5/G6 attachment optimization is insufficient for extraction cases, or that attachment design is less limiting than aligner material stiffness.
- **Bite ramps**: No effect (p=0.424). Differs from fixed appliances (where anterior bite plates promote posterior extrusion); likely because aligner trays provide occlusal coverage that passively limits posterior extrusion.
- **Age, gender**: Not significant.

## Clinical Recommendations from Discussion

1. **Sequenced attachment use**: Initially bond G5, G7, or horizontal beveled attachments to facilitate anterior intrusion and premolar extrusion for CoS leveling. Once CoS is acceptably leveled, rebond new G6 attachments on the entire arch for space closure.

2. **Delay first premolar extraction if high anterior intrusion needed**: Extracting premolars increases the "beam length" between canine and second premolar support points, exponentially reducing aligner stiffness (Proffit's beam model). Posterior teeth lose anchorage capability. Recommend delaying extraction until after some CoS leveling in severe cases.

3. **Intrude canines and incisors separately**: Using targeted attachments on canines vs. incisors allows mutual anchorage and distributes intrusive forces, reducing the span and improving biomechanics.

4. **Avoid Class II elastics during CoS leveling phase**: If Class II correction is needed, apply elastics only after CoS has been leveled, or switch to mandibular second premolar attachment points to minimize unwanted molar extrusion.

5. **Patient expectation management**: CoS leveling in extraction CAT has inherent accuracy limits (15% in extraction, 54% in non-extraction, vs. 40% cited for non-extraction in Goh 2022). Counsel patients pre-treatment that final CoS may not match predicted ClinCheck outcome.

## Methodological Strengths

- **High measurement precision**: ICC 0.995–0.999 intra-operator, 0.885–0.981 inter-operator (excellent).
- **3D quantitative CoS measurement**: Geomagic Studio methodology is objective and reproducible (avoids 2D cephalometric errors).
- **Adequate sample size**: n=60 (30 per group) exceeds the n=13 per group minimum calculated for power 0.8.
- **Clear extraction/non-extraction comparison**: Direct head-to-head design isolates extraction's effect on CoS leveling.
- **Multivariable regression**: Regression model identifies independent predictors beyond extraction status (pre-treatment CoS, predicted CoS, crowding, elastics).

## Methodological Limitations

- **Retrospective, single-practitioner bias**: All 60 patients treated by one specialist orthodontist; findings may not generalize to other CAT protocols or practitioners.
- **Missing treatment variables**: Sagittal/vertical skeletal patterns, duration of elastics use, and sequencing of tooth movements were not analyzed—all potentially confounding.
- **Attrition bias**: Patients with severe off-tracking (who did not complete the 1st aligner series) were excluded, potentially overestimating CAT effectiveness, especially in extraction group.
- **CoS-only outcome**: Study did not measure individual tooth movements (incisor intrusion depth, second molar extrusion, etc.), limiting mechanistic insight. A follow-up with 3D individual-tooth tracking would clarify which tooth's movement (or lack thereof) drives CoS leveling failure in extraction cases.
- **Small Class III elastics sample**: Only 1 patient with Class III elastics vs. 8 with Class II; conclusion about Class III elastics is speculative (β=−0.330, p=0.667, non-significant).
