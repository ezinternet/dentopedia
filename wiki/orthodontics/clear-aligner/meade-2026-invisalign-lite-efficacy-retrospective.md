## Three-line Summary

Retrospective analysis (n=122 adults, 79.5% female; Bland-Altman agreement analysis) of planned vs achieved outcomes for the Invisalign Lite product line — the first predictability dataset specific to this limited-movement, reduced-aligner-count variant.

Overjet (mean difference +0.183 mm, within ±0.5 mm threshold), arch depth (no significant difference), and mandibular incisor labiolingual inclination (−1.088°) tracked plan well; overbite showed a clinically significant discrepancy (+0.746 mm with proportional bias — larger initial overbite = larger error) and maxillary incisor inclination under-achieved plan by −2.905°.

The maxilla-mandible asymmetry in incisor torque accuracy is clinically relevant: maxillary incisors consistently ended up more lingual than planned, while overbite correction accuracy worsened with increasing initial depth — plan conservatively for both movements with Invisalign Lite (abstract-only; full text not retrieved).

## Summary

This retrospective study evaluated how closely achieved orthodontic outcomes matched the digitally planned outcomes for adult patients treated with the **Invisalign Lite** appliance — a limited-movement, reduced-aligner-count Invisalign variant. Using treatment-planning software data and metrology tools, the authors compared planned vs achieved values for overjet, overbite, arch depth, and maxillary/mandibular central incisor labiolingual inclination in 122 patients (79.5% female), applying Bland-Altman agreement analysis.

## Key Contributions

- First predictability data specific to the **Invisalign Lite** product line (as opposed to standard Invisalign) for overjet, overbite, arch depth, and incisor inclination.
- Demonstrates a **proportional bias** in overbite correction accuracy — the larger the initial overbite, the larger the planned-vs-achieved discrepancy — via Bland-Altman analysis.
- Identifies a **maxilla-mandible asymmetry** in incisor labiolingual inclination accuracy: mandibular incisors achieved close to plan, while maxillary incisors consistently under-achieved (ended up less proclined / more lingual than planned).

## Methodology

*Reconstructed from the abstract only.*

- Adult patients meeting inclusion criteria for overjet and overbite were identified from Align Technology's treatment-planning application data.
- Metrology software measured pretreatment, planned, and achieved arch depth and labiolingual inclination of maxillary and mandibular central incisors.
- Planned vs achieved values compared for overjet, overbite, arch-depth subgroups, and incisor inclination; Bland-Altman plots used to assess agreement and detect bias patterns.
- A ±0.5 mm interval was used as the clinically acceptable threshold for overjet.

## Results

- **n = 122** patients met selection criteria; 95 (79.5%) female.
- **Overjet**: mean planned-achieved difference **+0.183 mm** (SD 0.557) — within the ±0.5 mm clinically acceptable interval.
- **Overbite**: mean difference **+0.746 mm** (SD 0.857); Bland-Altman plot showed a proportional bias, with discrepancy increasing as initial overbite increased.
- **Arch depth**: differences remained within an acceptable range across all subgroups; no significant differences (p > .05) between planned and achieved.
- **Mandibular incisor labiolingual inclination**: achieved closely matched planned, mean difference **-1.088°** (SD 2.78°).
- **Maxillary incisor labiolingual inclination**: mean difference **-2.905°** (SD 2.671°) — achieved consistently lower (less proclined) than planned.
- **Bottom line**: overjet, arch depth, and mandibular incisor inclination tracked plan well; overbite and maxillary incisor inclination showed statistically and clinically significant under-achievement relative to plan.
