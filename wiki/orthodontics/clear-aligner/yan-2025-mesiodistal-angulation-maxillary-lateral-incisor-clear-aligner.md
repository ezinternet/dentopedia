## Three-line Summary

Retrospective CBCT study (27 patients, 54 maxillary lateral incisors; Angle class I/II, four-premolar extraction, clear aligner) measuring mesiodistal angulation change T0 (pretreatment) to T1 (refinement) under three factors: attachment presence, retraction sequence, and horizontal movement distance.

Lateral incisors showed significant distal inclination (p<0.001); attachment reduced distal tipping (88.4% vs 86.9% accuracy, p<0.001); canine-first retraction best preserved angulation vs en masse (p<0.001); horizontal movement >4mm and >7mm were dose-dependent risk thresholds (p<0.001); crown-to-root ratio had no effect (p>0.05).

Clear aligner extraction treatment plans should include attachments and canine-first retraction sequence as the two primary design choices to protect maxillary lateral incisor mesiodistal angulation.

## 세줄요약

후향적 CBCT 연구(27명, 상악 측절치 54개; Angle I·II급, 소구치 4개 발치, 투명교정) — 전처치(T0)→재치료(T1) 근원심 경사 (Mesiodistal Angulation, MDA) 변화를 첨부장치 유무·후퇴 순서·수평 이동 거리로 비교.

측절치에서 원심 경사가 유의하게 발생(p<0.001); 첨부장치가 원심 경사 감소(88.4% vs 86.9%, p<0.001); 견치 우선 후퇴 (Canine-First Retraction)가 일괄 후퇴 (En Masse Retraction)보다 각도 보존 우수(p<0.001); 수평 이동 >4mm·>7mm가 용량의존적 위험 임계값(p<0.001); 치관-치근 비율 영향 없음(p>0.05).

발치 투명교정 치료계획 시 첨부장치 추가와 견치 우선 후퇴 순서가 상악 측절치 근원심 경사 보호를 위한 두 핵심 설계 조치.

## Summary

This retrospective CBCT study by Yan et al. (2025) specifically investigates mesiodistal angulation changes of maxillary lateral incisors during four-premolar extraction treatment with clear aligners. Using CBCT-derived panoramic reconstruction with the midpalatal suture as a reference line, the study found that lateral incisors consistently undergo distal inclination — a clinically important finding given that the 미국 교정의 심사위원회 기준 (American Board of Orthodontics Objective Grading System, ABO OGS) requires post-treatment root parallelism within 1mm apex deviation, Brunzel's esthetic standard allows a maximum 9° mesial inclination, and Jain's calculation shows each 1° inclination increase consumes 0.021mm of arch circumference, compounding space-closure risk.

The lateral incisor is biomechanically vulnerable in clear aligner extraction treatment: its small, blunt crown provides poor aligner seating; it must move in both mesiodistal and labiopalatal directions simultaneously during retraction; its labial cortical bone is often thin; and clinicians historically pay more attention to canines and centrals. AlBalaa reported that clear aligner actual precision for lateral incisor movement is only 51.19%, with correction amount 48.81%.

## Key Contributions

- Identifies the maxillary lateral incisor as a biomechanically weak link in clear aligner extraction treatment
- Quantifies three modifiable risk factors with statistical significance: attachment presence, retraction sequence, horizontal movement distance
- Establishes dose-dependent movement distance thresholds (>4mm, >7mm) for distal inclination risk
- Provides mechanistic explanation for why 견치 우선 후퇴 (Canine-First Retraction) protects lateral incisor angulation: reaction force from canine displacement opposes distal tipping; improved aligner wrap after canine moves distally; reduced roller-coaster effect
- Challenges the prior SR+MA finding of no significant attachment effect on root angulation in extraction cases (muthuswamy-pandian 2025, based on only 1 study)

## Methodology

- **Study design**: Retrospective, single-center, CBCT-based
- **Sample**: 27 patients (18F/9M, mean age 21.1±4.9y); 54 maxillary lateral incisors; ANB 0–8°, Angle class I/II, four premolar extraction
- **Imaging**: I-CAT scanner (120 kVp, 5 mA, 7s, 0.25mm slices, 230×170mm FOV); Dolphin panoramic reconstruction; Mimics 21.0 3D superimposition
- **Measurement**: Angle between midpalatal suture reference line and long axis of lateral incisor (root canal extension) on reconstructed panoramic image; T0 = pretreatment, T1 = refinement stage
- **Groups**: Attachment vs nonattachment; 일괄 후퇴 (En Masse) vs 견치 우선 후퇴 (Canine-First) vs 개구리 패턴 후퇴 (Frog-Pattern) retraction; horizontal movement 1–3 mm vs 4–6 mm vs 7–9 mm; crown-to-root ratio groups
- **Statistics**: Paired T0 vs T1 comparison; LSD post-hoc tests for multi-group comparisons

## Results

| Factor | Finding | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| Overall T0→T1 angulation | Significant distal inclination | <0.001 |
| Bilateral symmetry | No significant difference (data merged) | >0.05 |
| Attachment vs nonattachment | Attachment: less distal tipping (88.4% vs 86.9% accuracy) | <0.001 |
| En masse vs canine-first | En masse: more distal inclination | <0.001 |
| Canine-first vs frog-pattern | Canine-first: less distal inclination | 0.004 |
| En masse vs frog-pattern | Not significant | >0.05 |
| Movement 1–3 vs 4–6 mm | 4–6 mm: more distal inclination | 0.008 |
| Movement 4–6 vs 7–9 mm | 7–9 mm: more distal inclination | 0.01 |
| Movement 1–3 vs 7–9 mm | 7–9 mm: significantly higher | <0.001 |
| Crown-to-root ratio | No significant effect | >0.05 |

**Clinical benchmarks cited:**
- Brunzel aesthetic standard: ≤9° mesial inclination for an attractive smile
- Pani aesthetic standard: ≤5° tilt from central incisor axis
- ABO OGS: apex deviation ≤1mm; roots parallel and perpendicular to occlusal plane
- Jain: 1° inclination increase = 0.021mm arch circumference consumed
