## Three-line Summary

PROSPERO-registered SR (CRD42022357639, PRISMA; ROBINS-I; GRADE; 919 articles screened, 6 included to September 2024) synthesizing evidence on mesiodistal predictability of upper and lower molars with clear aligners without auxiliary aids.

Upper molar (predominantly distalization) predictability: 61.1 ± 9.1% for movements of 0.45–3.2 mm; molar mesial movement predictability: 85.6 ± 1.1% (much higher than distalization); lower molar distalization: less predictable than upper; evidence quality moderate-to-serious risk of bias, very low GRADE certainty.

Upper molar distalization is clinically predictable for 1.5–3.2 mm; anchorage reinforcement or overcorrection is recommended for any mesiodistal molar movement; measurement standardization is necessary to improve cross-study comparisons.

## Background

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.2319/063024-507.1](https://doi.org/10.2319/063024-507.1)), molar mesiodistal movement is a critical component of orthodontic treatment — distalization to resolve Class II relationships and mesial movement for space closure. While clear aligners are increasingly used for these movements, their predictability had not been systematically reviewed in isolation from auxiliary aids (miniscrews, elastics). This PROSPERO-registered SR is the first to synthesize exclusively auxiliary-free mesiodistal molar predictability data.

## Methods

- **Design**: Systematic review without meta-analysis (PRISMA)
- **Registration**: PROSPERO CRD42022357639
- **Search**: Multiple databases to September 2024
- **Reviewers**: 2 independent + arbitration
- **Risk of bias**: ROBINS-I tool
- **Certainty**: Qualitative GRADE assessment
- **Inclusion**: Prospective and retrospective studies on clear aligner mesiodistal molar movement without auxiliary aid
- **N included**: 6 studies (from 919 initial); predominantly Invisalign system

## Results

| Movement type | Predictability | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Upper molar (distalization) | **61.1 ± 9.1%** | Range 0.45–3.2 mm studied |
| Molar mesial movement | **85.6 ± 1.1%** | High predictability |
| Lower molar distalization | Lower than upper | No specific % in abstract |

**Key findings:**
- Upper molar distalization is clinically predictable for planned movements of 1.5–3.2 mm
- Mesial molar movement (space closure) is substantially more predictable than distalization
- Lower molar distalization is less predictable than upper — different anchorage conditions and treatment mechanics
- Moderate-to-serious risk of bias; very low quality of evidence per GRADE
- Heterogeneity in measurement methods across studies limits quantitative pooling

**Clinical recommendation**: Apply anchorage reinforcement or plan overcorrection for all mesiodistal molar movements to compensate for the ~39% expression deficit in distalization.

## Clinical Takeaway

1. **61% upper molar distalization accuracy** should be the baseline expectation when planning with clear aligners without auxiliaries — plan for overcorrection.
2. **Mesial movement (85.6%)** is significantly more predictable than distalization — clinicians can rely on CA more confidently for space closure scenarios.
3. **Lower molar distalization** is less predictable — if lower molar distalization is needed, consider auxiliary aids or a different treatment approach.
4. **Mostly Invisalign data** — other brands' mesiodistal predictability remains unknown.
5. **GRADE: very low** — treat these figures as approximations; high-quality prospective RCTs with standardized measurement protocols are needed.
