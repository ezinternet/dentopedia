## Three-line Summary

In-vitro bench study using a modified force/moment transducer device (Grant et al. design) and digital image correlation (DIC) on 3D-printed clear aligners, testing 8 design combinations (4 margin designs × attachment presence/absence, n=15/group) for mandibular first premolar extrusion.

With attachment, extended margin (Group 2, median +0.96 N) and reduced buccolingual margin width (Group 4, +0.46 N) delivered adequate extrusion force at the first premolar, while thickened margin (Group 3) produced significantly lower extrusion force; without attachment, all groups paradoxically generated an intrusive force instead of the planned extrusion.

Attachment presence is a key requirement for extrusion — without it the aligner's force system is unpredictable and can act opposite to the planned direction; margin design (extension, buccolingual width reduction) independently modulates extrusion efficiency, but findings are bench-only (mechanical device, no PDL/root/biological load) and not yet clinically validated.

## 세줄요약

3D프린팅 투명교정장치(Clear Aligner, CA)에 대해 개조된 force/moment transducer 장치(Grant 등 디자인)와 디지털이미지상관법(Digital Image Correlation, DIC)을 이용, 하악 제1소구치 정출을 대상으로 8개 디자인 조합(margin 4종 × attachment 유무, 그룹당 n=15)을 비교한 in-vitro 벤치 연구.

Attachment 있는 경우 margin 연장(Group 2, 중앙값 +0.96N)·협설폭 1mm 축소(Group 4, +0.46N)가 적절한 정출력을 발현했고, margin 두께 증가(Group 3)는 유의하게 낮은 정출력을 보임; attachment 없는 경우 모든 그룹에서 계획과 반대로 함입력(intrusion force)이 발생.

Attachment 존재가 정출의 핵심 조건이며 없으면 힘 체계가 예측 불가능하고 계획과 반대 방향으로 작용할 수 있음; margin 디자인(연장·협설폭 축소)이 attachment와 독립적으로 정출 효율을 조절하나, 결과는 기계적 장치 기반 벤치 연구(치주인대·치근·생물학적 하중 미반영)로 임상 검증은 아직 없음.

## Summary

This in-vitro study addresses a specific, poorly-studied gap in clear aligner (CA) biomechanics: how does aligner design affect **extrusion** — a movement type consistently reported as one of the least predictable with CA. Using a mechanical force/moment transducer system (modified from Grant et al.) instrumented at the mandibular canine, first premolar, and second premolar, the authors quantified the forces and moments generated by 3D-printed aligners of 8 different design combinations (4 margin geometries × attachment presence/absence) when a 0.5 mm extrusion of the mandibular first premolar was planned. In parallel, digital image correlation (DIC) mapped the aligner's own surface displacement and strain to connect *why* a given design produces its measured force pattern.

The headline finding is that **attachment presence, not margin geometry, is the dominant determinant of whether extrusion is achieved at all** — without an attachment, every design tested produced an intrusive (opposite-direction) force instead of the planned extrusion. With attachment present, margin design becomes the second-order lever: extending the margin 1 mm subgingivally (Group 2) or reducing the buccolingual margin width by 1 mm (Group 4) produced adequate extrusion force, while simply thickening the margin (Group 3) did not — actually generating significantly lower extrusion force than the other designs.

## Key Contributions

- First direct force/moment quantification of clear-aligner-driven **premolar extrusion** across a systematic design matrix (margin geometry × attachment).
- Pairs force/moment transducer data with DIC surface-strain mapping — linking the aligner's physical deformation pattern to the force it delivers, rather than measuring force alone.
- Shows margin extension and buccolingual-width reduction (not thickening) are the design changes that meaningfully improve extrusion efficiency — thickening the margin does not help and can hurt.
- Demonstrates that without attachment, CA extrusion attempts can generate the *opposite* of the intended movement (intrusion), reinforcing the clinical guidance to always use attachments for extrusion.

## Methodology

- Nissin mandibular model, 3D-scanned/CAD-designed aligners, direct 3D printed (TC-85 resin, Graphy) at 0.5 mm thickness (not thermoformed).
- Simulated case: mandibular first premolar (#34) intruded 1 mm, with a 0.5 mm extrusion movement planned via the aligner.
- 2 attachment conditions (dome-shaped, 3×2×1 mm, vs none) × 4 margin designs = 8 groups:
  - Group 1 — equi-margin, gingival scalloped (reference)
  - Group 2 — extended margin, 1 mm subgingival scalloped
  - Group 3 — equi-margin, 1 mm thickened at gingival margin
  - Group 4 — equi-margin, 1 mm reduced buccolingual width (0.5 mm buccal + 0.5 mm lingual)
- Force/moment measured via multi-axis transducers (AFT20-D15, Aidin Robotics) at canine/1st premolar/2nd premolar (#33–35), 1 hour after seating, 37°C incubator, n=15/group.
- DIC (VIC-3D dual-camera system) mapped aligner surface displacement/strain via speckle-pattern imaging.
- Non-parametric statistics (Kruskal–Wallis + Bonferroni post-hoc, median/IQR), SPSS v29, α=0.05.

## Results

**With attachment:**
- Extrusion force (Fz) at the first premolar: significantly lower in Group 3 (thickened margin); highest in Groups 2, 4, 1, 3 in descending order.
- Group 2 (extended margin) achieved the largest median extrusion force (+0.96 N); Group 4 (reduced buccolingual width) achieved +0.46 N — both judged adequate for the planned movement.
- Group 4 uniquely produced a buccal force and near-zero intrusion force at the canine (median +0.05 N vs −0.68 to −0.12 N elsewhere), interpreted as increased lingual-side aligner support reducing unwanted intrusive drift.

**Without attachment:**
- **All 8 groups produced an intrusive force at the first premolar instead of the planned extrusion** — the opposite of the treatment plan. Groups 3 and 4 showed somewhat weaker intrusive force than Group 1, but none achieved extrusion.

**DIC:**
- With attachment, aligner displacement concentrated around the attachment site (most pronounced in Groups 1 and 4); strain consistently localized at the gingival margin across all groups.
- Without attachment, displacement was more diffuse across the buccal surface; Group 4 showed a distinctive proximal-area displacement peak.
