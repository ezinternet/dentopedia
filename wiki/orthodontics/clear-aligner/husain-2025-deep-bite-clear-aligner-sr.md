## One-line Summary

SR (18 studies): clear aligners open deep bites 0.4–3.8 mm with 33–48.88% accuracy; effective for dentoalveolar cases but low-quality evidence and uncertain versus fixed appliances for skeletal deep bites.

## Summary

This systematic review evaluated the effectiveness and accuracy of clear aligners for dental and skeletal deep bite correction. Eighteen studies were included from comprehensive database searches (inception to May 2024). Effectiveness (achieved bite opening) ranged from 0.4 to 3.8 mm; accuracy (achieved vs planned) ranged from 33% to 48.88% across all studies. GRADE evidence quality was rated low due to high risk of bias, indirectness, and imprecision. Clear aligners can effectively correct mild to moderate dentoalveolar deep bites, but their efficacy in skeletal cases compared to full-fixed appliances (FFA) remains uncertain. Overcorrection and refinements were identified as clinically necessary to achieve target outcomes.

## Key Contributions

- Distinguishes effectiveness (achieved correction) from accuracy (predicted vs achieved ratio)
- Documents accuracy range 33–48.88% — substantially below planned correction
- GRADE: low quality evidence for both effectiveness and accuracy
- CA recommended for dentoalveolar (not skeletal) deep bites
- Overcorrection and refinements necessary as standard clinical approach

## Methodology

Design: SR (ROBIN-I for risk of bias). Databases: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane, grey literature (inception to May 2024). Population: all age groups, dental and/or skeletal deep bite treated with clear aligners. 18 studies (8 effectiveness, 8 accuracy, 2 both). Outcomes: achieved bite opening (mm), accuracy (%), comparison to FFA. PROSPERO CRD42022334078.

## Results

- Effectiveness (bite opening): 0.4–3.8 mm
- Accuracy (deep bite correction): 33–48.88%
- GRADE evidence quality: low (both effectiveness and accuracy)
- Primary mechanism: anterior intrusion ± posterior extrusion
- vs FFA for skeletal deep bite: insufficient evidence to recommend CA as superior
- Clinical implication: systematic overcorrection or refinement plans required
