## Three-line Summary

This SR+MA analyzed 14 studies (8 in meta-analysis) comparing white spot lesions (WSLs), plaque accumulation, and salivary caries-associated bacteria (SCB) between clear aligners (CA) and fixed appliances (CF). CA showed significantly lower plaque accumulation (SMD −1.58; 95% CI: −2.57, −0.58; p = 0.002) and qualitatively lower WSL incidence, severity, and SCB levels. Moderate-quality evidence supports CA for oral health preservation, though high ROB and heterogeneity warrant caution.

## 세줄요약

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the oral health impacts of clear aligner therapy compared to fixed orthodontic appliances. Across 14 studies, clear aligners demonstrated significantly reduced plaque accumulation and qualitatively fewer white spot lesions and caries-associated bacteria. The findings suggest that for patients who can be treated with either appliance type, clear aligners may offer advantages in maintaining oral health during orthodontic treatment. However, moderate evidence quality and study heterogeneity mean these results should be interpreted with appropriate clinical caution.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA to simultaneously compare WSLs, plaque accumulation, and SCB between CA and CF
- Statistically significant reduction in plaque accumulation with CA (SMD −1.58)
- Moderate-quality GRADE evidence supporting CA for oral health
- Clinical relevance: CA may be preferable for patients concerned about oral hygiene during treatment

## Methodology

Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs and non-randomized studies comparing CA vs CF orthodontic appliances. Searches covered MEDLINE, Scopus, Embase, Google Scholar, and gray literature. Risk of bias assessed with Cochrane ROB tool; certainty of evidence with GRADEpro. Effect sizes calculated as standardized mean difference (SMD) using STATA 17.

## Results

- Plaque accumulation: SMD −1.58 (95% CI: −2.57 to −0.58; p = 0.002) favoring CA
- WSL incidence: qualitatively lower in CA group (descriptive synthesis)
- WSL severity: qualitatively lower in CA group
- SCB levels: qualitatively lower in CA group
- High heterogeneity across studies noted
