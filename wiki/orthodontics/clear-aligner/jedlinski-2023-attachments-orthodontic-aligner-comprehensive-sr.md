## Three-line Summary

This comprehensive SR analyzed 26 articles on composite attachments for aligner treatment, covering attachment bonding (4 studies) and movement efficacy (22 studies). Attachments significantly improve orthodontic movement expression and aligner retention, with specific tooth sites identified for optimal efficacy by movement type. The review provides evidence-based guidance on attachment placement, shape, and bonding protocols.

## Summary

This is the most comprehensive systematic review to date on composite attachments used in orthodontic aligner therapy. Across 26 studies, the evidence consistently demonstrates that attachments significantly improve both the expression of planned tooth movements and aligner retention. The review synthesizes findings on optimal attachment placement by tooth and movement type, attachment geometry (shape, size, bevel), and bonding protocols. While most evidence comes from in-vitro and FEA studies, the clinical implications are clear: strategic attachment design is essential for predictable aligner outcomes.

## Key Contributions

- Most comprehensive SR on aligner attachments (26 studies)
- Evidence-based guidance on attachment placement by movement type
- Distinguishes bonding protocol evidence from movement efficacy evidence
- Identifies gaps: limited clinical RCTs, manufacturer-specific protocols

## Methodology

Systematic review following PRISMA guidelines. Six databases searched (Dec 2022). PROSPERO registration: CRD42022383276. Quality assessment tools matched to study type. 26 articles included: 4 on bonding, 22 on movement efficacy. Narrative synthesis (heterogeneity prevented meta-analysis).

## Results

- Attachments significantly improve movement expression vs no attachments
- Specific sites identified for optimal efficacy per movement type
- Attachment design influences treatment predictability
- Bonding protocol affects retention during treatment
- Most evidence from in-vitro/FEA; limited clinical RCTs
