## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-center cohort (n=75 of an initial 630-patient pool) of permanent-dentition patients undergoing sequential bilateral maxillary molar distalization (2-4 mm/side) with Invisalign clear aligners, evaluated for post-treatment open gingival embrasures (OGE) of the maxillary central incisors via the Jemt papilla index.

OGE incidence was 17.3% (13/75); multivariate logistic regression identified age (OR 1.17, 95% CI 1.07-1.30), anteroposterior overlap (OR 2.41, 95% CI 1.05-5.54), ANB angle (OR 2.31, 95% CI 1.08-4.94), and U1-NA angle (OR 1.39, 95% CI 1.03-1.89) as independent pre-treatment predictors, with good model discrimination (ROC AUC 0.839, 95% CI 0.72-0.96).

Gingival biotype and amount of anterior tooth retraction were NOT significant predictors, contrary to hypothesis; small sample (only 13 OGE events) and single-center, single-ethnicity design limit generalizability pending external multicenter validation.

## Summary

This retrospective study addresses a gap the clear-aligner literature had not specifically filled: what pre-treatment factors predict open gingival embrasure (OGE, "black triangle") formation in patients undergoing sequential maxillary molar distalization with Invisalign aligners. Among 75 patients (from an initial screening pool of 630) who completed sequential bilateral distalization of 2-4 mm per side, 13 (17.3%) developed OGE of the maxillary central incisors, as judged by pre/post-treatment Jemt papilla-index scoring on standardized intraoral photographs. Multivariate logistic regression isolated four independent pre-treatment predictors — age, anteroposterior overlap, ANB angle, and U1-NA angle — with the combined model achieving good discriminatory power (ROC AUC 0.839). Notably, gingival biotype (thin vs. thick) and the planned amount of anterior tooth retraction were not significant, diverging from prior general-orthodontic OGE literature and from the authors' own pre-study hypothesis. The authors propose a distalization-specific biomechanical mechanism: during sequential (V-shaped) distalization, anterior teeth initially bear reactive anchorage loads and then undergo compensatory retraction as space closes, potentially compressing the interradicular alveolar bone crest and widening the distance between the interproximal contact point and the alveolar bone crest — a distance previously linked to papilla-fill failure (Mahasneh et al.). The study is explicitly framed as a first step toward a pre-treatment risk-screening tool for this specific clinical scenario, pending external validation.

## Key Contributions

- First study to isolate OGE risk factors specifically within the sequential maxillary molar distalization + clear aligner population, rather than mixed-appliance or general orthodontic cohorts.
- Reports a scenario-specific incidence (17.3%) that sits below general clear-aligner OGE rates reported elsewhere (25.7-38%, Wang et al./Yang et al.), suggesting distalization-specific mechanics may not uniformly elevate OGE risk beyond baseline CAT rates — though direct comparison is limited by different inclusion criteria.
- Produces a 4-variable pre-treatment prediction model (age, anteroposterior overlap, ANB, U1-NA) with AUC 0.839 — a candidate basis for a chairside pre-treatment risk-screening checklist.
- Strict exclusion criteria (no pre-existing diastema/OGE, no periodontitis, no anterior IPR, no anterior restorations) isolate distalization-attributable mechanical/biological risk from confounding pre-existing periodontal or restorative factors — a methodological strength relative to looser prior cohorts.
- Null findings for gingival biotype and anterior retraction amount are clinically informative: they suggest skeletal/dental-angular factors (ANB, U1-NA) may matter more than soft-tissue phenotype or the magnitude of retraction per se in this specific distalization-driven mechanism.

## Methodology

Retrospective single-center cohort at Tianjin Stomatological Hospital (patients treated June 2014-September 2024). From an initial pool of 630 patients, 75 met inclusion criteria: permanent dentition, sequential bilateral maxillary molar distalization (2-4 mm/side) with Invisalign, complete pre/post-treatment records (digital casts, lateral cephalograms, intraoral/extraoral photographs), single experienced orthodontist (>10 years). Exclusions: pre-treatment diastema/OGE, prior anterior extraction or congenital absence, anterior periodontitis/gingivitis, existing anterior restorations, prior anterior IPR, secondary orthodontic treatment, missed follow-up, systemic disease (e.g., diabetes).

Outcome: OGE status via Jemt papilla index (0-4) on standardized frontal intraoral photographs, blinded assessor, OGE = score 0-2 vs. non-OGE = score 3-4 (intra-observer ICC ≥0.885, Kappa ≥0.824). Seventeen candidate pre-treatment factors spanned sociodemographic, cephalometric (Dolphin Imaging), and digital-model (OrthoCAD) domains — including age, gender, gingival biotype (visual, thin/thick), overjet, overbite, crowding, anteroposterior overlap, transverse overlap, rotation, anterior teeth retraction, ANB, MP-SN, MP-FH, U1-SN, U1-NA (angle and mm), U1-L1.

Statistics: chi-square (categorical), t-test/Welch's t-test/Mann-Whitney U (continuous, per normality), univariate logistic regression to screen candidates at P<0.1, multivariate logistic regression for independent associations (OR, 95% CI), VIF/tolerance for multicollinearity, Hosmer-Lemeshow for model fit, ROC/AUC for discrimination. SPSS v21.0.

## Results

- OGE incidence: 13/75 (17.3%); non-OGE 62/75 (82.7%).
- No significant association with gender (P=0.974) or gingival biotype (P=0.242).
- Univariate group comparison: only age differed significantly between groups (median 21.0 vs. 34.0 years, P=0.005); overjet, overbite, crowding, anteroposterior/transverse overlap, rotation, anterior retraction, ANB, MP-SN, MP-FH, U1-SN, U1-NA, U1-L1 were not significant by direct group comparison.
- Univariate logistic regression flagged age, overjet, anteroposterior overlap, ANB, U1-NA angle, and U1-L1 angle as candidates (P<0.1).
- Multivariate logistic regression — independent predictors (all P<0.05):
  - Age: OR 1.17 (95% CI 1.07-1.30) per year
  - Anteroposterior overlap: OR 2.41 (95% CI 1.05-5.54) per mm
  - ANB angle: OR 2.31 (95% CI 1.08-4.94) per degree
  - U1-NA angle: OR 1.39 (95% CI 1.03-1.89) per degree
- Combined model ROC AUC: 0.839 (95% CI 0.72-0.96) — good discrimination.
- Mean age: 23.69 ± 8.30 years (non-OGE) vs. 33.08 ± 10.96 years (OGE), roughly a 9-year gap.

**Clinical implication**: patients who are older, have greater anteroposterior overlap (localized sagittal crowding), a larger ANB angle (more skeletal Class II tendency requiring greater anterior compensation), and a larger U1-NA angle (more incisor inclination correction needed) may warrant pre-treatment counseling about elevated OGE risk before starting sequential distalization with clear aligners — and may benefit from adjunctive anchorage reinforcement (attachments, TADs) to limit uncontrolled anterior retraction, per the mechanistic link to anchorage loss described in [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/bhate-2025-cat-maxillary-molar-distalization-class-ii-sr]].

**Key limitation**: small sample (13 events) relative to the number of candidate predictors screened (EPV concern), single-center/single-ethnicity/84%-female sample, visual-only gingival biotype assessment, 2D photographic OGE grading without periapical/3D confirmation, and no external validation cohort — the AUC 0.839 is an internal-sample estimate, not yet externally validated.
