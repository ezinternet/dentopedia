## Three-line Summary

In vitro time-course study comparing microbial adhesion and biofilm formation of S. mutans (ATCC 25175) and L. acidophilus (ATCC 4356) on 6 clear aligner brands (Invisalign, 3M Clarity, ClearCorrect, Smartee, Orthero, Graphy) at 0, 24, 48, 72, 96, 120, 168, and 240 hours; two-way repeated-measures ANOVA (Greenhouse-Geisser) + Bonferroni T2 post-hoc.

ClearCorrect showed significantly more bacterial formation than Smartee at 120, 168, and 240 h (p<0.05); Graphy showed more biofilm than Smartee at 168 h (p<0.05); combined S. mutans + L. acidophilus formed more biofilm on Graphy than on Invisalign at 120 and 168 h (p<0.05); Smartee was the consistently lowest performer across all brands.

All 6 aligner materials support increasing biofilm formation with extended wear; brand-level differences emerge at 120+ hours, suggesting aligner replacement timing and brand-specific oral hygiene counseling may be clinically relevant.

## Background

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1016/j.ajodo.2024.08.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajodo.2024.08.010)), elevated biofilm formation on clear aligners poses caries and periodontal risks. While prior studies have examined biofilm on individual aligner materials, no study had compared microbial adhesion simultaneously across six commercially distinct aligner systems. This study is the first to provide a time-series, multi-brand microbiological comparison, revealing that brand-level differences in biofilm accumulation emerge specifically after the 5-day (120 h) mark of continuous wear.

## Methods

- **Organisms**: Streptococcus mutans (ATCC 25175) and Lactobacillus acidophilus (ATCC 4356)
- **Timepoints**: 0, 24, 48, 72, 96, 120, 168, 240 hours
- **Brands**: Invisalign (Align Technology, USA), Clarity (3M ESPE, USA), ClearCorrect (Straumann, Switzerland), Smartee (Smartee Denti-Technology, China), Orthero (Turkey), Graphy (South Korea)
- **Statistical test**: Two-way repeated-measures ANOVA (Greenhouse-Geisser correction) + Bonferroni T2 post-hoc; p<0.05 significance
- **Institution**: Istanbul Aydin University (Orthodontics) + University of Sydney
- **Note**: Abstract-only source; detailed CFU methodology, sample size, and surface characteristics not available

## Results

**Significant pairwise differences:**

| Comparison | Timepoints (p<0.05) |
|---|---|
| ClearCorrect > Smartee | 120h, 168h, 240h |
| Graphy > Smartee | 168h |
| Graphy > Invisalign (combined organisms) | 120h, 168h |

**Smartee**: consistently lowest biofilm across all timepoints and both organisms — no other brand exceeded Smartee at any timepoint.

**No significant differences**: reported between most other brand pairs (Invisalign vs Clarity, Invisalign vs ClearCorrect, Orthero vs others — not specifically noted as significant in abstract).

All materials showed progressive biofilm accumulation over 240 h. The clinical implication is that extended aligner wear beyond standard wear cycles increases microbial colonization risk regardless of brand.

## Clinical Takeaway

1. **Smartee (Chinese brand)** had the lowest biofilm in this study — suggesting its surface properties may be microbiologically favorable; however, clinical outcome data for Smartee remains limited.
2. **ClearCorrect showed higher biofilm than Smartee** at late timepoints — consistent with its inner surface characteristics (see [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/kobkiatkawin-2025-clear-aligner-color-stability-brand]] for surface roughness data).
3. **Graphy (Korean brand)** had the worst combined biofilm at peak timepoints — clinicians prescribing Graphy should emphasize more frequent aligner cleaning and reinforce oral hygiene.
4. **Invisalign is not the worst biofilm-forming brand** — Graphy exceeded Invisalign for combined S. mutans + L. acidophilus; however, Invisalign's surface roughness characteristics (Sa=1.97 μm, highest of all tested materials) may still predispose it to clinical biofilm accumulation.
5. Brand-specific oral hygiene counseling and aligner replacement timing guidance may be appropriate given these inter-brand differences.
