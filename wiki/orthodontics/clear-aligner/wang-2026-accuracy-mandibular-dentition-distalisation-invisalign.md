## Three-line Summary

Pilot pre/post study (prospective vs retrospective enrolment not stated), 12 Class III adults, digital models integrated with CBCT — voxel-based registration on stable cranial base structures for maxillary tooth/mandible-position assessment, and symphysis/mandibular-body registration for mandibular tooth movement — comparing the ClinCheck virtual plan against the actual outcome.

Median accuracy of mandibular first-molar cusp distal movement was 26.72-33.87% with Class III elastics (actual movement 0.62 +/- 0.82 to 0.80 +/- 0.89 mm) versus 39.28-44.66% with miniscrew anchorage (actual movement 2.02 +/- 1.71 to 2.29 +/- 1.53 mm); maxillary incisors proclined significantly more under elastic traction, and only the elastics group showed significant positional change of the condyle head.

Invisalign mandibular dentition distalisation falls well short of the ClinCheck-planned movement, especially without skeletal (miniscrew) anchorage; the abstract does not give per-subgroup sample sizes, p-values, or any detail beyond what is stated here.

## Summary

This pilot study (Wang, et al., Orthod Craniofac Res 2026, PMID 41999141, PMCID PMC13485232) examined 12 Class III adults undergoing Invisalign treatment aimed at mandibular dentition distalisation. Unlike prior wiki studies of aligner distalization, which measured maxillary molars via digital-model/iTero surface-scan registration, this study integrated digital models with CBCT and used voxel-based registration on stable hard-tissue structures (cranial base for maxillary/mandibular-position assessment; symphysis and mandibular body for mandibular tooth movement) — a more rigorous 3D reference frame. The ClinCheck virtual plan was superimposed on the T1 digital model to define the "predicted" movement against which T2 (posttreatment) outcomes were compared. The headline finding is a pronounced accuracy gap by anchorage type: median accuracy of mandibular first-molar cusp distal movement was only 26.72-33.87% with Class III elastics alone, rising to 39.28-44.66% with miniscrew (TAD) anchorage — corresponding to actual movement of roughly 0.6-0.8 mm (elastics) versus 2.0-2.3 mm (miniscrews). Class III elastics also produced a skeletal side effect largely avoided by miniscrews: significantly more maxillary incisor proclination and a significant change in condylar position, whereas miniscrew-anchored cases showed no significant change in mandibular anatomical landmarks. The authors conclude that Invisalign may not reach expected efficacy for mandibular dentition distalisation, especially when skeletal anchorage is absent — a clinically blunt caveat for a movement that is comparatively under-studied relative to maxillary distalization.

## Key Contributions

- First wiki entry quantifying Invisalign accuracy specifically for **mandibular** (not maxillary) dentition distalisation.
- First to do so specifically in **Class III** malocclusion, using CBCT voxel-based hard-tissue superimposition rather than surface-scan/palatal-rugae registration.
- Directly contrasts Class III elastics vs miniscrew anchorage for the same movement, putting a roughly 13-15 percentage-point number on the anchorage-type accuracy gap.
- Identifies a skeletal (condylar-position) side effect specific to elastics-only anchorage that is absent with miniscrews.

## Methodology

Pilot pre/post (T1/T2) study of 12 Class III adults. Digital models were integrated with maxillofacial CBCT; T1 and T2 CBCT images were superimposed via voxel-based registration on stable cranial base structures to assess 3D movement of maxillary teeth and positional change of the mandible, while mandibular tooth movement was assessed via CBCT superimposition registered on the symphysis and mandibular body. The ClinCheck virtual planning model was superimposed based on its position relative to the T1 digital model, giving the predicted-vs-achieved comparison. Prospective vs retrospective enrolment is not stated in the retrieved abstract; `evidence_level: retrospective` is assigned here as the conservative rung (not a verified design), following the same convention already used for [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/saif-2022-efficiency-evaluation-maxillary-molar-distalization]] and [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/liu-2025-maxillary-molar-distalization-invisalign-adult]], whose abstracts have the identical gap.
