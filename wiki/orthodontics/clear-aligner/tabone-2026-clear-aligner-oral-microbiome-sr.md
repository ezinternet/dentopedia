## One-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA, PROSPERO 628072) of 12 observational studies (4 hours–12 months follow-up) found that clear aligners (CA) generally promote more stable oral microbiota and better periodontal parameters than fixed appliances (FA), though early (4–24h) CA use induces transient alpha-diversity decreases and increased acidity, and one included study found Invisalign did not outperform FA in microbiome composition/function.

## 한줄요약

12편의 관찰연구(4시간~12개월 추적)를 정성종합한 체계적 문헌고찰(PRISMA, PROSPERO 628072)로, 투명교정장치(clear aligner, CA)가 고정장치(fixed appliance, FA)보다 대체로 더 안정적인 구강 미생물총과 우수한 치주 지표를 보였으나, 초기(4~24시간) 착용 시 일시적 alpha diversity 감소와 산성화가 관찰되었고, 포함된 연구 중 하나(Wang et al.)는 Invisalign이 미생물총 구성·기능 면에서 FA보다 우월하지 않다는 상반된 결론을 보고했다.

## Summary

This PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered (628072) systematic review is the first to unify **oral microbiome composition data** (16S rRNA sequencing, qPCR, CRT bacteria system) with **clinical periodontal/caries parameters** (PI, GI, BOP, PPD) across the full timeline of clear aligner (CA) treatment — from 4 hours to 12 months. From 34 records (16 PubMed + 18 other databases via OvidSP) screened down to 12 included observational studies (cross-sectional and longitudinal; no RCTs), the review addresses two questions: (Q1) how does CA use associate with specific shifts in oral microbial composition, and (Q2) how does CA use affect oral health parameters that themselves shape the microbiome.

The overall pattern across studies: CA use causes a **transient early dysbiosis** in the first 4–24 hours (decreased alpha diversity, modest beta-diversity increase, increased Firmicutes and some anaerobic/periodontal-pathogen taxa, increasingly acidic aligner-liquid pH after 12h of continuous wear). Over the medium term (1–6 months) and long term (12 months), CA is associated with **more stable microbial communities and better periodontal/caries outcomes than FA** — lower plaque index (PI), lower gingival index (GI), lower risk of high cariogenic-bacteria (S. mutans/Lactobacilli) levels, and stable probing parameters, contrasted against FA/multi-bracket (MB) patients who showed progressive PI/GI/PD deterioration and enrichment of disease-associated genera over time.

However, the review explicitly flags a **dissenting result**: one included study (Wang et al., functional/inferred metagenome analysis) found that both FA and Invisalign caused oral microbiome dysbiosis, that Invisalign was not superior to FA in microbiome composition or function, and that the Invisalign group showed higher predisposition to periodontal-disease-associated metabolic pathway enrichment — directly contradicting the "CA is protective" narrative that dominates the other 11 studies.

Methodological quality is uniformly weak: all included studies are non-randomized observational designs (the Jadad RCT scale was inapplicable), most lack control groups, several have inadequate sample-size justification or sex imbalance, and no meta-analysis was possible due to heterogeneous methodologies (different sequencing regions, bacterial panels, and follow-up windows).

## Key Contributions

- Bridges the gap between clinical periodontal-outcome literature (e.g., PI/GI/BOP comparisons) and the underlying microbial-composition mechanism, by explicitly correlating alpha/beta diversity and taxon-level shifts with clinical parameters across the same cohorts.
- Time-stratifies the evidence into short-term (4–24h), medium-term (up to 6 months), and long-term (12 months) windows, revealing a consistent "early transient dysbiosis → later relative stabilization" trajectory under CA.
- Surfaces a genuine internal contradiction in the evidence base (Wang et al.'s functional-metagenome finding that Invisalign does not outperform FA), rather than smoothing it into the dominant favorable narrative — useful for calibrating confidence.
- Applies AXIS risk-of-bias appraisal (Jadad was not applicable, since no RCTs were found) to all 12 included cross-sectional/longitudinal studies, documenting sample-size, missing-data, and conflict-of-interest concerns study-by-study.

## Methodology

- **Design**: PRISMA systematic review without meta-analysis; PROSPERO 628072.
- **PICO**: In CA patients, how does CA use affect oropharyngeal microbiota (salivary/lingual/gingival/pharyngeal) composition, clinical parameters (PI, GI, BOP, PPD), and functional diversity (alpha/beta diversity, metabolic pathways), vs baseline or FA?
- **Search**: Medline, Cochrane, Embase, Scopus (via PubMed + OvidSP), no date limit through December 2024; ("Bacteria" OR "Microbio*") AND ("Orthodontic clear aligners" OR "invisalign").
- **Screening**: 34 → 29 (after dedup) → 12 included, by 2 independent reviewers with a third-reviewer tiebreak.
- **Bias tools**: Jadad scale considered but inapplicable (no RCTs); AXIS (Appraisal Tool for Cross-Sectional Studies) applied to all 12 studies.
- **Analytic techniques in included studies**: qPCR (1), CRT bacteria system/Ivoclar Vivadent (2), 16S rRNA sequencing — V3–V4 region (8), V1–V3/HOMING protocol (1).

## Results

**Short-term (4–24h):** CA use produces measurable, rapid microbial shifts — decreased alpha diversity, modestly increased beta diversity, increased Firmicutes abundance, and an increasingly acidic aligner-liquid environment after 12h of continuous wear (demineralization-favoring).

**Medium-term (1–6 months):** CA consistently outperformed FA/MB on clinical hygiene metrics: only ~8–10% of CA users reached high-risk S. mutans/Lactobacilli levels at 6 months vs ~40% of MB users (odds ratio ~5.05 for S. mutans, ~4.33 for Lactobacilli, MB vs CA/removable positioner); CA users maintained PI near 0 throughout, while MB users showed significant PI increases; alpha diversity trends were mixed (higher in FA in one study, no significant change pre/post CA in another) but beta diversity and taxon-level composition consistently diverged between appliance types, with disease-associated genera enriched in FA.

**Long-term (12 months):** CA groups showed more stable microbiota and periodontal parameters (stable PI/GI) versus FA groups, which showed progressive PI/GI/PD deterioration and enrichment of disease-associated genera. White-spot-lesion (WSL) development under CA was associated with lower alpha diversity and a distinct taxon profile (14 taxa enriched in WSL group, 58 taxa enriched in non-WSL controls), linked to salivary microbiome-metabolome correlations.

**Contradicting study:** Wang et al.'s functional/inferred-metagenome analysis found both FA and Invisalign caused dysbiosis, with Invisalign showing higher predisposition to periodontal-disease-associated metabolic pathways (energy, amino acid, carbohydrate, terpenoid metabolism) versus FA (membrane transport, nucleotide metabolism) — concluding Invisalign did not outperform FA microbiologically, contradicting the review's otherwise-dominant conclusion.

**Risk of bias:** all 12 studies had clear objectives and appropriate designs, but most lacked control groups, several had inadequate sample-size justification, inconsistent missing-data handling, and one (Zheng et al.) had a flagged conflict-of-interest concern.
