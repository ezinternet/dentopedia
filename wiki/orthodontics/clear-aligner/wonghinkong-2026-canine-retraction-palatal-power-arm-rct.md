## Three-line Summary

Split-mouth RCT, 18 adults (21 randomized) undergoing maxillary first-premolar-extraction treatment with in-house clear aligners (IHCA), comparing a palatal power arm (PA) side vs. control (C) side for accuracy of final canine retraction using equivalence testing (TOST, Holm–Bonferroni correction).

All three canine linear movements (distalization, extrusion-intrusion, buccolingual) were equivalent between PA and control, but canine rotation (mean diff 5.22°, 90% CI 1.48–8.95°) and distal crown tipping (mean diff 1.67°, 90% CI −0.44–3.78°) exceeded the ±1.5° equivalence margin — the PA side showed less tipping/rotation than control — while anchorage-tooth angular outcomes were mostly inconclusive and both sides showed signs of anchorage loss.

Clinical implication: a palatal power arm may improve angular (tipping/rotation) control of the canine during aligner-driven extraction space closure without changing linear-distalization accuracy, but the trial is underpowered (68.8% post-hoc power for the primary outcome), does not demonstrate anchorage preservation, and the findings are IHCA-specific — caution generalizing to commercial aligner systems.

## Summary

This split-mouth RCT (18 completers of 21 randomized, single center, Mahidol University) is the first to directly compare **power-arm-assisted vs. non-power-arm canine retraction accuracy** in maxillary first-premolar-extraction cases treated with in-house clear aligners (IHCA). Each patient contributed one power-arm (PA) side and one control (C) side, with identical aligner staging/attachment protocol otherwise — isolating the power arm + power-chain (80–100 g) as the sole variable. Accuracy was defined via GOM Inspect Suite deviation analysis (virtual/predicted vs. actual digital models) across 3 linear and 3 angular canine and anchorage-tooth movements, evaluated with equivalence testing (TOST, ±0.5 mm/±1.5° margins, Holm–Bonferroni-corrected for multiplicity).

The key finding: **linear canine movements were equivalent between PA and control, but angular movements (rotation, distal tipping) were not** — with the PA side tracking closer to the predicted plan (less deviation) than control. This supports the biomechanical rationale (power arm raises the force-application point relative to the tooth's center of resistance, increasing the moment-to-force ratio and reducing uncontrolled tipping) but the effect was only demonstrated for canine angular control, not for linear distalization accuracy or anchorage preservation — anchorage-tooth angular outcomes were mostly statistically inconclusive, and both sides showed anchorage-loss signatures (mesial tipping, relative intrusion of posterior teeth), meaning the PA did not clearly protect anchorage.

## Key Contributions

- First RCT-level (split-mouth, prospective) comparison of power-arm vs. no-power-arm canine retraction accuracy in clear aligner extraction cases — the wiki's existing extraction-biomechanics evidence ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/zhuo-2026-the-roller-coaster-effect]], [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/tang-2025-evaluating-the-effectiveness-of-clear]]) is retrospective-cohort tier; this raises the evidence tier for a specific countermeasure design.
- Demonstrates a clinically actionable dissociation: **linear accuracy ≠ angular accuracy** for the same movement (canine distalization) — a power arm can help control rotation/tipping even where raw distalization distance is already well-tracked without it.
- Uses a rigorous equivalence-testing (TOST + Holm–Bonferroni) framework rather than simple significance testing, distinguishing "not different" from "clinically equivalent" — methodologically stronger than typical predicted-vs-achieved percentage-accuracy reporting in prior Invisalign literature.
- Multi-stage IHCA protocol with interim rescanning/replanning (vs. one-stage commercial-aligner digital setup) is proposed as a mechanism for reducing cumulative tracking error during extraction space closure — a design variable orthogonal to the power-arm question itself.

## Methodology

Prospective single-center split-mouth RCT, 1:1 allocation (sequence-based online randomization), CONSORT-compliant, no blinding possible (power arm visibility). Adults >18y (mean 23.46±4.62y) requiring maxillary first-premolar extraction (mild crowding, mild canine asymmetry), enrolled 2020–2023; n=21 randomized, n=18 analyzed (3 dropouts, 85.7% retention). Both sides received identical IHCA staging (canine moved 3 mm/stage), attachment design (three-sided prism attachments on canine/2nd premolar/1st molar/lateral incisor), and aligner material (PET-G 0.5→0.75 mm sequential thermoformed sheets). PA side: prefabricated palatal power arm bonded to canine (12 mm) + ipsilateral 1st molar (8 mm), connected by superelastic power chain (80–100 g, replaced q6–8wk). Control side: aligner alone.

Outcome measurement: iTero scans pretreatment and post-final-canine-aligner; virtual (predicted) vs. actual (achieved) digital models superimposed via GOM Inspect Suite (palatal-rugae/vault reference, Geometric Element + local best-fit). Primary outcome: canine distalization accuracy. Secondary: canine/2nd-premolar/1st-molar mesio-distal, bucco-lingual, extrusion-intrusion (linear); tip, rotation, torque (angular). Statistics: TOST equivalence testing (90% CI, ±0.5 mm linear / ±1.5° angular pre-specified margins) with Holm–Bonferroni step-down correction (m=3 per tooth-domain family) for multiplicity; sample size powered for 0.31 mm mean paired difference (14 pairs minimum, 21 recruited for attrition buffer).

## Results

| Movement (canine) | 90% CI vs. equivalence margin | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Distalization (primary outcome) | within ±0.5 mm | Equivalent |
| Extrusion-intrusion | within ±0.5 mm | Equivalent |
| Buccalization | within ±0.5 mm | Equivalent |
| Distal crown tipping | −0.44 to 3.78° (exceeds ±1.5°) | **Not equivalent** |
| Torque | crosses bound, mean diff 0.43° within margin | Inconclusive |
| Rotation | 1.48 to 8.95° (mean diff 5.22°) | **Not equivalent** |

- Canine rotation deviation from plan: PA side −0.89±7.50° vs. control −6.10±6.15° (mean difference 5.22±9.11°, significant) — control-side canines rotated substantially more off-plan than PA-side canines.
- 2nd premolar (anchorage): extrusion and buccalization equivalent (linear); most angular outcomes and mesio-distal linear inconclusive.
- 1st molar (anchorage): mesialization and extrusion equivalent (linear); buccalization and all angular outcomes inconclusive.
- Both sides showed anchorage-loss signatures (mesial crown tipping, relative intrusion of posterior anchor teeth) — evidence is insufficient to conclude the PA preserves anchorage better than control.
- Post-hoc power for primary outcome equivalence test: 68.8% (study likely underpowered, especially for higher-variance angular outcomes).
- No harms observed. Measurement precision (Dahlberg's error) <0.5 mm/0.5° for all variables.
