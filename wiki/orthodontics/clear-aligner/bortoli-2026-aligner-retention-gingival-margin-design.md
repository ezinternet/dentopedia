## One-line Summary

In-vitro pull-off test on 3D-printed models (n=3/group): scalloped-extended and straight aligner trimlines retain significantly more than scalloped trimlines, and retention increases sharply with the severity of simulated marginal tissue recession (Class IV ~3× the control's retention force).

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.4041/kjod25.269](https://doi.org/10.4041/kjod25.269)), this in-vitro study is the first to test clear aligner gingival margin (trimline) retention on models simulating varying degrees of periodontal marginal tissue recession, rather than only healthy dental arches. Using 3D-printed mandibular models across five conditions (control plus Classes I–IV of combined gingival recession and interdental bone/soft-tissue loss) and three trimline designs (scalloped, scalloped-extended, straight), the authors measured peak dislodgement force via pull-off testing.

Both factors independently and significantly affected retention (two-way ANOVA, P<0.001 for each), with no significant interaction — meaning the ranking of trimline designs by retention held consistently across all recession severities, while recession itself multiplied the absolute retention force. Scalloped-extended (52.3N) and straight (47.9N) margins retained substantially more than scalloped margins (33.1N). Retention rose sharply only at advanced recession stages: Class IV (82.2N, the most severe, with interdental bone loss) and Class III (54.4N) were significantly higher than Class I, Class II, or the unaltered control (25.6–33.2N), which did not differ from each other.

Clinically, this suggests patients with advanced periodontal recession and interdental bone loss may experience meaningfully harder aligner insertion/removal — and thus more discomfort — especially when combined with retentive (scalloped-extended or straight) trimline designs. The authors note no validated clinical threshold yet exists linking measured retention force to patient-reported discomfort, so this remains a mechanistic rather than a clinically calibrated finding.

## Key Contributions

- First retention study to model periodontal marginal tissue recession (not just healthy arches) when comparing aligner trimline designs.
- Confirms, on a new (recession) substrate, the previously established healthy-arch finding that scalloped-extended/straight margins out-retain scalloped margins.
- Shows recession severity is an independent, large-magnitude driver of retention force — Class IV recession produced roughly 3× the retention force of the control across designs.
- Identifies a retention "step change" between minor (Class I–II, recession only) and advanced (Class III–IV, recession + interdental bone loss) periodontal breakdown — minor recession alone does not meaningfully alter retention.

## Methodology

3D-printed mandibular models (Form 2 SLA printer) simulating a control and four escalating classes of marginal tissue recession (Class I: 1.3mm recession; Class II: 2.5mm recession; Class III: 3.0mm recession + 1.4mm interdental bone/soft-tissue loss; Class IV: 3.0mm recession + 2.5mm interdental bone/soft-tissue loss), 9 models per class. Three trimline designs (scalloped, scalloped-extended [+2mm past zenith], straight [at zenith]) were fabricated per model in 0.6mm PETG (n=3 per design × recession-class cell). Pull-off testing (EMIC DL-2000, 10mm/min crosshead speed, buccal hook at mandibular first-molar level, 10 repeated removals averaged per aligner) measured peak dislodgement force (N). Two-way ANOVA (design × recession class) with Tukey post-hoc, α=0.05.

## Results

- Trimline design (P<0.001): scalloped-extended 52.3N and straight 47.9N (not significantly different from each other) > scalloped 33.1N.
- Recession class (P<0.001): Class IV 82.2N > Class III 54.4N > Class II 33.2N ≈ Class I 25.6N ≈ control 26.8N (latter three not significantly different).
- No significant design × recession interaction (P=0.105) — the retention hierarchy by trimline design is preserved at every recession severity.
- Study is retention-only (no tooth-movement setup); results do not speak to force delivery or orthodontic efficacy.
