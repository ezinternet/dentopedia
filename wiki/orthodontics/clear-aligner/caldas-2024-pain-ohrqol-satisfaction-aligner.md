## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study of 94 Brazilian adults (ClearCorrect clear aligners) measuring OHIP-14, VAS pain, and treatment satisfaction simultaneously. Moderate pain was the only significant predictor of higher OHIP-14 impairment (p=0.036); mean OHIP-14 was 3.36/56. High treatment satisfaction was maintained regardless of pain level.

## 세줄요약

ClearCorrect 착용 브라질 성인 94명 단면연구: OHIP-14 평균 3.36(56점 만점)으로 QoL 영향 미미. 중간 통증→OHIP-14 유의 상승(p=0.036), 경도 통증은 유의차 없음. 통증 강도에 상관없이 치료만족도 높음.

## Background

Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) during clear aligner treatment are increasingly recognized as key treatment metrics alongside clinical efficacy. While pain and discomfort are expected during orthodontic treatment, their actual impact on oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) and patient satisfaction in aligner users remained poorly characterized in the Brazilian adult population prior to this study.

## Methods

Cross-sectional observational study; n=94 adults wearing ClearCorrect aligners at a Brazilian university orthodontic clinic. Instruments: OHIP-14 (Brazilian Portuguese validated version; 0–56 scale), VAS pain (categorized mild/moderate/severe), and global treatment satisfaction questionnaire. Statistics: Kruskal-Wallis with post-hoc for OHIP-14 by pain group; Fisher's exact test for satisfaction categories.

## Results

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| OHIP-14 mean | 3.36/56 (low impact) |
| Moderate pain → OHIP-14 | Significantly higher (p=0.036) |
| Mild pain → OHIP-14 | Non-significant |
| Most affected OHIP-14 domain | Functional limitation |
| Treatment satisfaction | High regardless of pain level |

The functional limitation domain of OHIP-14 was the most affected subscale. Social well-being and confidence domains showed minimal impairment. No patient group — regardless of pain severity — showed low treatment satisfaction.

## Clinical Takeaway

Moderate pain during aligner treatment translates into measurable OHRQoL impairment (OHIP-14 increase). Pre-emptive pain management at each aligner change (e.g., timed ibuprofen at tray insertion) is supported by this evidence. The persistence of high satisfaction despite pain indicates strong patient acceptance of the treatment modality — a finding clinicians can communicate at treatment planning.
