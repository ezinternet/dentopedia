## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Chinese-language, 华西口腔医学杂志/West China J Stomatol 2026;44(1):17–24) synthesizing biomechanical mechanisms and clinical strategies for four complex clear aligner therapy (CAT) tooth-movement designs — molar distalization, arch expansion, vertical tooth movement, and extraction-space closure.

Overall CAT expression rate averages only ~50%, with molar distalization performing best (58–75%), vertical tooth movement worst (13–52.9%), and arch expansion / extraction cases showing large tipping-dominant deviations that require mini-screw anchorage, overcorrection, and torque compensation to approach bodily movement.

Clinical implication: treatment planning for these four movement types should build in movement-specific overcorrection, attachment design, and mini-screw-assisted anchorage from the outset; evidence is narrative (no formal quality appraisal or pooled statistics), so expression-rate figures should be treated as directional, not precise, benchmarks.

## Summary

This narrative review from the Fourth Military Medical University's Department of Orthodontics (Xi'an, China) synthesizes the biomechanical literature on clear aligner therapy (CAT) for four treatment scenarios considered biomechanically demanding: molar distalization, arch expansion, vertical tooth movement, and extraction-space closure. The authors frame CAT's core limitation as a persistent gap between planned (software-designed) tooth movement and achieved movement — the "expression rate" (实现率/predictability) — which averages only ~50% across CAT movements generally, and varies substantially by movement type and tooth position. The review also names and explains three CAT-specific biomechanical phenomena: the Watermelon Seed Effect (uneven force distribution from asymmetric crown coverage), the Pendulum Effect (anterior lingual tipping/extrusion reacting to posterior retraction), and the Roller Coaster Effect (mid-arch open bite from premature anterior/posterior contact during extraction closure). For each of the four movement types, the paper pairs a "biomechanics and risk factors" analysis with a "strategy optimization" section detailing overcorrection angles, attachment placement, mini-screw anchorage siting, and torque compensation protocols drawn from the primary literature (largely retrospective studies, finite-element analysis, and in-vitro simulation). The paper closes by identifying AI-assisted individualized planning and materials/3D-printing integration as the two priority directions for future CAT development.

## Key Contributions

- Organizes fragmented single-technique CAT biomechanics literature into a unified four-movement-type clinical decision-support framework (molar distalization, arch expansion, vertical movement, extraction cases).
- Provides comparative expression-rate benchmarks across movement types, clarifying that molar distalization is the most biomechanically reliable complex CAT movement while vertical movement (intrusion especially) is the least reliable.
- Names and mechanistically explains the Watermelon Seed Effect, Pendulum Effect, and Roller Coaster Effect as distinct, CAT-specific (vs. fixed-appliance) biomechanical phenomena.
- Compiles concrete, quantitative clinical strategy parameters (e.g., 8°–10° molar anchorage-preparation tip, 1.5× expansion overcorrection, 1.2° crown-lingual torque compensation trade-offs) rather than only qualitative recommendations.
- Proposes AI-assisted individualized treatment planning and multidisciplinary materials/3D-printing integration as concrete future directions to close the design-vs-actual movement gap.

## Methodology

Narrative review (not a formally reported systematic review — no explicit database search strategy, PRISMA flow, inclusion/exclusion criteria, or risk-of-bias appraisal are given in the text, despite "systematic review" phrasing in the abstract). Content is organized into four movement-type sections, each split into "biomechanics and risk factors" and "strategy optimization" subsections, followed by a concluding outlook section. Draws on retrospective/prospective clinical studies, finite element analysis (FEA), in-vitro simulator studies, and case reports (~90 references cited).

## Results

**Molar distalization** — most reliable complex movement; expression rates 58–75% (mandibular first molar lowest at 58%, second molars 74–75%); largely bodily movement (2.25 mm distalization without significant axial change); third molar presence reduces efficiency 17%; palatal mini-screws outperform buccal for torque control.

**Arch expansion** — high crown-level efficiency but low bodily-movement expression; canine/premolar region ~50–70% vs. first molar region only ~29%; CT-verified overall tooth movement only 36.35% despite 70% crown-level expansion (mostly tipping); 1.5× overcorrection raises expression to 75–122% but crown-lingual torque compensation to control buccal tipping cuts those gains substantially.

**Vertical tooth movement** — least reliable; lower incisor intrusion 34%, upper central incisor intrusion only 13%; molar extrusion 35–40%; deep-bite curve-of-Spee leveling 52.9%; open-bite molar intrusion averages just 1 mm; combined buccal+palatal mini-screws can achieve 1–2 mm absolute molar intrusion but raise palatal root-resorption risk.

**Extraction cases** — maxillary arch loses more anchorage than mandibular (incisor torque/canine inclination loss up to 10°; molar mesial movement >2 mm, 1.24–2.28× mandibular); each 1 mm increase in overbite adds ~1.5° mesial molar tip; anchorage-preparation strategy (8–10° molar, 15–20° canine pre-programmed distal tip) and mini-screws (labial incisor mini-screw + palatal cross-bite vertical elastics; labial mini-screws achieving 69.8%/53.3% expression for incisor tipping/distal movement in Class II div 2) are the principal countermeasures to the Pendulum and Roller Coaster effects.
