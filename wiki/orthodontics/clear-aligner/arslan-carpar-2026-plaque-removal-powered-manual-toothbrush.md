## Three-line Summary

This single-blind RCT compared manual vs powered toothbrush plaque removal around clear aligner attachments in 14 adults (244 teeth). Both groups showed significant plaque reduction from baseline (p<0.05), but no significant differences between toothbrush types were found across any region or surface (all p>0.05). Distal and gingival surfaces had higher plaque levels, suggesting plaque removal depends more on surface accessibility than toothbrush type.

## Summary

This single-blind randomized controlled trial investigated whether powered toothbrushes offer superior plaque removal compared to manual toothbrushes around clear aligner attachments. Under standardized single-brushing conditions, both toothbrush types achieved comparable plaque reduction. The study highlights that non-uniform plaque distribution around attachments — with distal and gingival surfaces being hardest to clean — may be more clinically relevant than toothbrush type selection. This suggests that brushing technique and attention to difficult-to-reach surfaces should be emphasized in patient education.

## Key Contributions

- First RCT specifically comparing toothbrush types around clear aligner attachments
- Demonstrates comparable efficacy between manual and powered brushes
- Identifies surface-specific plaque distribution patterns around attachments
- Clinical implication: technique over technology for aligner patients

## Methodology

Single-center, single-blind RCT at Mersin University. 14 adults (>6 months in clear aligner therapy) randomized to manual (n=7) or powered (n=7) toothbrush. 12h plaque accumulation standardized. Modified Plaque Index (MPI) on 4 surfaces per attachment (mesial, distal, occlusal, gingival) at baseline (T0) and after 2-min brushing (T1). Patient-level and tooth-level analyses.

## Results

- Plaque reduction T0→T1: significant in both groups (p<0.05)
- Manual vs powered: no significant difference (all p>0.05)
- Surface distribution: distal/gingival > mesial > occlusal (p<0.001)
- Attachment type: no significant influence on plaque reduction
- Clinical response rates: comparable between groups
