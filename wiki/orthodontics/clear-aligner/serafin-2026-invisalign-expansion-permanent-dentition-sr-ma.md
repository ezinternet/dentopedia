## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD420251123184; PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science; up to November 2025) pooling 23 studies (1391 patients; 21 in meta-analyses) on Invisalign transverse arch expansion predictability in permanent dentition across both maxillary and mandibular arches.

Pooled predictability: maxilla 75.5% (range by tooth 76–83%), mandible 80.6% (range 73–88%); mandibular 2nd premolars highest (88%), lower 2nd molars lowest (73%); gingival-margin predictability ~13.5 percentage points below cusp-level values, confirming buccal crown tipping as the dominant mechanism.

Multivariable regression (R²=0.524): younger age (−1.1%/year, p=0.001), SmartTrack/LD30 material (+32.7% vs EX30, p<0.001), and mandibular segments (+13.1% vs maxilla, p=0.001) are independent predictors of higher predictability; GRADE very low certainty; publication bias evident (Egger p<0.001).

SR+MA of 23 studies (1391 patients) reporting 75.5% maxillary and 80.6% mandibular Invisalign expansion predictability in permanent dentition, with younger age, SmartTrack material, and mandibular arch as independent predictors of higher accuracy (GRADE very low).

## 한줄요약

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the predictability of Invisalign-mediated transverse dental arch expansion in permanent dentition, pooling 23 studies (1391 patients) from a systematic search through November 2025. Meta-analyses were conducted separately for each tooth group in both arches using logit-transformed proportions in random-effects models. Overall maxillary predictability averaged 75.5% (premolars 81–83%, canines and molars ~76–78%), while mandibular predictability averaged 80.6% (canines and premolars 86–88%, second molars 73%). In tooth groups where both cusp-tip and gingival-margin predictability were available, gingival values were consistently ~13.5 percentage points lower (~83% of cusp-level), confirming that expansion is predominantly expressed as buccal crown tipping. Multivariable regression identified younger age, LD30/SmartTrack material, and mandibular arch location as independent predictors of higher predictability. Clinically, the findings support selective overcorrection in ClinCheck—particularly for molar regions, older adults, maxillary arch, and EX30 cases—while caution is warranted for periodontal safety in large expansions.

## Key Contributions

- Largest SR+MA on Invisalign expansion predictability to date (23 studies, 1391 patients), covering both maxillary and mandibular arches
- Quantifies gingival-margin predictability as ~83% of cusp-level values, providing the first pooled evidence that expansion is predominantly crown tipping rather than bodily movement
- Multivariable model (R²=0.524) identifies age, aligner material, and arch location as independent predictors, while change interval and tooth type are non-significant
- Meta-regression reveals recent publications trend lower, possibly from improved methodology and larger samples (β=−0.150, p=0.010)
- Documents significant publication bias (Egger and Begg p<0.001) and GRADE very low certainty, cautioning against over-interpretation of pooled estimates

## Methodology

- **Design**: SR+MA; PRISMA 2020; PROSPERO CRD420251123184
- **Search**: PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science; November 2025 cutoff; no language restriction
- **Included**: 23 studies (20 retrospective, 3 prospective); 1391 patients; ages 13–75+ years
- **Eligibility**: Permanent dentition; Invisalign expansion; planned vs achieved expansion ratio reported quantitatively
- **Excluded**: Mixed/primary dentition, syndromic patients, surgically assisted expansion, qualitative outcomes
- **Outcome**: Predictability = (achieved / planned expansion) × 100; cusp-tip (primary) and gingival-margin (subset)
- **Statistics**: Logit-transformed proportions; random-effects REML + Sidik-Jonkman; I² heterogeneity; 95% prediction intervals; meta-regressions (age, year, arch, tooth type, material, change interval); multivariable linear regression; hierarchical clustering; Bonferroni correction
- **RoB**: ROBINS-I v2: 4 Low, 15 Moderate, 4 Serious; sensitivity analyses excluding serious-RoB studies unchanged

## Results

**Maxillary arch (overall average 75.5%):**

| Tooth | Pooled predictability | 95% CI | I² |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canines (13–23) | 76% | 66–85% | ~90.9% |
| 1st premolars (14–24) | 81% | 73–87% | >84% |
| 2nd premolars (15–25) | 83% | 76–89% | >84% |
| 1st molars (16–26) | 78% | 67–87% | High |
| 2nd molars (17–27) | 78% | 22–93% | 94.7% |

**Mandibular arch (overall average 80.6%):**

| Tooth | Pooled predictability | 95% CI | I² |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canines (33–43) | 86% | 74–93% | 84.6% |
| 1st premolars (34–44) | 86% | 69–95% | >86% |
| 2nd premolars (35–45) | 88% | 75–95% | >86% |
| 1st molars (36–46) | 87% | 68–96% | High |
| 2nd molars (37–47) | 73% | 12–98% | 90.2% |

**Key regression findings:**
- Publication year: β=−0.150, p=0.010 (more recent → lower predictability, possibly improved methods)
- Age: β=−0.069, p=0.016; ~50% reduction in predictability odds per decade
- Achieved expansion (positive): β=0.534, p=0.002, R²=7.18%
- Multivariable model: age −1.1%/year (p=0.001); LD30 vs EX30 +32.7% (p<0.001); mandible vs maxilla +13.1% (p=0.001)
- Change interval, tooth type, arch (univariate): not significant

**Publication bias**: Egger p<0.001; Begg p<0.001; funnel plot asymmetric toward higher values
**95% PI**: Very wide (~4–99% across tooth groups), indicating substantial uncertainty in future studies
**GRADE**: Very low certainty for both arches (RoB + high inconsistency)

**Clinical implications**: Selective overcorrection in ClinCheck for molars, older adults, maxillary arch, EX30 material; CBCT before large expansions to assess alveolar envelope; do not use faster change intervals to improve predictability; consider torque programming and optimized attachments for molar regions.
