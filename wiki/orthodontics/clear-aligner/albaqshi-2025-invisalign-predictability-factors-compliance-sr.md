## Summary

This systematic review evaluated clinical, biomechanical, patient, and technological factors influencing Invisalign treatment success and predictability. Six studies met inclusion criteria (from databases searched to August 2025). Treatment success was found to be multifactorial: male patients and patients without prior orthodontic experience demonstrated higher compliance, while inadequate wear time was the leading cause of suboptimal outcomes. Tooth movement accuracy varied by type: bucco-lingual tipping showed highest accuracy, whereas rotation, intrusion, and expansion showed lowest precision. Importantly, the Invisalign Progress Assessment digital monitoring tool overestimated tooth movement. Compared to fixed appliances, Invisalign provided shorter treatment duration and improved comfort and esthetics, but less favorable results for certain smile parameters. Note: Cureus is an open-access journal with broader peer review criteria; findings should be interpreted with corresponding caution.

## Key Contributions

- Identifies gender and prior orthodontic experience as compliance predictors
- Establishes wear time inadequacy as primary failure driver
- Documents movement accuracy hierarchy: bucco-lingual tipping > mesiodistal tipping > rotation/intrusion/expansion
- Highlights Invisalign Progress Assessment overestimation bias
- Confirms treatment duration advantage but smile parameter limitations vs fixed

## Methodology

Design: SR. Databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar, Cochrane Library (to August 2025). Study designs: RCTs, cohort, case-control, cross-sectional. Population: Invisalign patients. 6 studies included. Outcomes: compliance, wear time, tooth movement accuracy, treatment duration, smile parameters. No meta-analysis (heterogeneity). Journal: Cureus (open-access, broader review criteria).

## Results

- Compliance: males > females; orthodontic-naive patients > experienced patients
- Primary failure cause: inadequate wear time
- Movement accuracy (most to least):
  1. Bucco-lingual tipping (highest)
  2. Mesiodistal tipping
  3. Rotation / intrusion / expansion (lowest)
- Invisalign Progress Assessment: overestimates actual tooth movement
- vs Fixed: shorter treatment duration, better comfort/esthetics; less favorable for some smile parameters
