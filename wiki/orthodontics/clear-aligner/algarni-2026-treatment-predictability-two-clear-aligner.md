## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study (n=80 recruited [40 Invisalign, 40 Eon Aligner], 4 excluded for absence of teeth; Riyadh Elm University Department of Orthodontics, Jan 2023–Jan 2025) comparing software-predicted (final treatment-plan step) versus achieved (pre-refinement, first aligner set only) tooth movement across interpremolar width, intercanine width, mesiodistal rotation of incisors, and horizontal/vertical movements of canines and premolars, measured via 3Shape Clear Aligner Studio superimposition.

Both brands showed good-to-excellent agreement for transverse width changes (interpremolar/intercanine, mean differences 0.06–0.34 mm, near Camardella's 0.4 mm clinical-relevance threshold) with no significant between-brand difference in magnitude; Eon Aligner outperformed Invisalign for mesiodistal rotation (differences 1.9–4.2°, though both brands still showed significant predicted-vs-achieved bias) and for horizontal movement (mean differences Invisalign 0.206–0.341 mm vs Eon 0.015–0.24 mm); vertical movement predictability was poor for both, with Invisalign described as "unreliable" and Eon as moderate but fluctuating (except first premolars).

Both clear aligner systems require systematic over-correction for most measured tooth movements — vertical/intrusive corrections in particular should not be planned with aligners alone — and this is the first study to directly compare Eon Aligner's clinical predictability against Invisalign.

양 브랜드 모두 횡적 폭경 변화는 양호~우수한 일치도(평균차 0.06–0.34mm, Camardella 임상적 유의성 기준 0.4mm에 근접)로 브랜드 간 크기 차이 없음; Eon Aligner가 근원심 회전(차이 1.9–4.2°, 다만 양쪽 다 유의한 예측-달성 편향 존재)과 수평이동(평균차 Invisalign 0.206–0.341mm vs Eon 0.015–0.24mm)에서 Invisalign보다 우수; 수직이동은 양쪽 모두 불량(Invisalign "신뢰불가", Eon은 제1소구치 제외 변동성 있는 보통 수준).

두 시스템 모두 측정된 대부분의 치아이동에서 체계적 과교정(over-correction)이 필요하며 — 특히 수직/압하(intrusive) 이동은 얼라이너 단독 계획에 의존해선 안 됨 — 이 연구는 Eon Aligner의 임상적 예측도를 Invisalign과 직접 비교한 최초 연구다.

## Summary

This retrospective study, conducted at Riyadh Elm University's Department of Orthodontics between January 2023 and January 2025, is the first to directly compare the clinical predictability of Eon Aligner (Eon Dental, Amman, Jordan) against Invisalign. Eighty adult cases with mild-to-moderate crowding or spacing (40 per brand, 4 excluded for absence of teeth) were assessed using three digital models per patient — initial, software-predicted, and achieved (pre-refinement, first aligner set only) — superimposed in 3Shape Clear Aligner Studio. The design deliberately isolates first-set performance before any refinement correction, the same principle used by [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/saif-2026-clear-aligner-anterior-3d-accuracy]] for Invisalign alone.

Both systems achieved good-to-excellent predicted-vs-achieved agreement for transverse arch-width changes (interpremolar and intercanine width), with differences of only 0.06–0.34 mm — within or close to the 0.4 mm clinical-relevance threshold reported by Camardella et al. — and no significant difference between brands in the magnitude of these discrepancies. Eon Aligner outperformed Invisalign on mesiodistal rotation of central and lateral incisors and on horizontal crown movement, though both brands still showed statistically significant bias between predicted and achieved values for these movements, indicating a systematic (not brand-specific) tendency toward over-prediction. Vertical movement was the weakest movement type for both systems: Invisalign was described as unreliable, while Eon Aligner showed moderate but fluctuating reliability (except for first premolars).

Clinically, the authors conclude that both aligner systems require planned over-correction for most tooth movements, that Eon Aligner shows a rotational and horizontal-movement advantage over Invisalign in this cohort, and that vertical corrections should not be attempted with aligners alone regardless of brand — auxiliaries (attachments, elastics, over-correction, or hybrid mechanics) are recommended instead.

## Key Contributions

- First direct clinical comparison of Eon Aligner against Invisalign for predicted-vs-achieved tooth movement (rather than virtual-setup planning alone, as in [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/telles-2025-clear-aligner-brand-virtual-setup-comparison]]).
- Isolates first-aligner-set (pre-refinement) accuracy, avoiding the confound of mid-treatment correction.
- Shows a movement-type-specific brand advantage (Eon better for rotation and horizontal movement) rather than a uniform brand superiority — both brands remain poor for vertical movement.
- Anchors observed discrepancies against a published clinical-relevance threshold (Camardella et al., >0.4 mm) and flags incisor-rotation discrepancies (1.9–4.2°) as potentially clinically significant for contact-point alignment and black-triangle risk.

## Methodology

Retrospective cohort, single institution, Jan 2023–Jan 2025. n=80 recruited (40 Invisalign, 40 Eon Aligner), 4 excluded for absence of teeth; adults with mild-to-moderate crowding (2–6 mm) or spacing (1–6 mm), non-extraction, minimal anterior IPR (0.2–0.3 mm/tooth). Three STL models per patient (initial, predicted, achieved-pre-refinement) superimposed in 3Shape Clear Aligner Studio using three stable reference points (second-molar landmarks). Outcomes: interpremolar width, intercanine width, mesiodistal rotation of central/lateral incisors, and horizontal/vertical movement of canines and premolars. Agreement assessed via ICC (poor <0.5, moderate 0.5–0.75, good 0.75–0.9, excellent 0.9–1.0); within-brand predicted-vs-achieved bias via Wilcoxon matched-pairs; between-brand magnitude comparison via Welch's t-test; Bonferroni correction applied when >10 comparisons; single calibrated examiner (ICC 0.766–0.892).
