## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 14 studies (510 participants) comparing predicted vs achieved tooth movements with clear aligner therapy (CAT) for space closure after premolar extraction (SCaPE), through November 2024; 4 low risk of bias, 10 high risk; GRADE certainty very low to low.

CAT significantly underperformed its own predictions: anterior retraction fell short (SPD = -0.87; p < 0.001), with excess lingual tipping (SPD = 1.09; p = 0.001) and extrusion (SPD = 0.88; p < 0.001) of anterior teeth, plus uncontrolled canine distal tipping (SPD = 1.42; p = 0.009) and first molar mesial drift (anchorage loss; SPD = 1.68; p < 0.001) beyond predictions.

Clear aligners are likely not effective or predictable for extraction space closure; fixed appliances may be superior for bodily tooth movement and anchorage control in extraction cases.

## 세줄요약

전치 후방이동 예측 미달(SPD=-0.87), 전치 설측 경사(SPD=1.09)·정출(SPD=0.88) 예측 초과, 견치 원심 경사(SPD=1.42)·제1대구치 근심 경사(고정원 소실, SPD=1.68) 예측 초과 — 모두 통계적으로 유의.

투명교정장치는 발치 공간 폐쇄에 효과적·예측 가능하지 않으며, 치체이동 및 고정원 조절에서 고정식 장치가 우월할 가능성이 있다.

## Summary

This SR+MA is the most comprehensive attempt to date to quantify the systematic gap between what clear aligner software predicts and what actually happens in extraction space closure cases. The headline finding is consistent: **CAT consistently underdelivers bodily retraction and overdelivers unintended tipping and extrusion**, in a pattern that mirrors the inherent biomechanical limitations of thermoplastic aligner force delivery.

### Key Quantified Failures

| Outcome | SPD | 95% CI | p | n studies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anterior retraction (less than predicted) | −0.87 | −1.15 to −0.60 | < 0.001 | 4 |
| Lingual tipping of anteriors (excess) | +1.09 | +0.46 to +1.73 | 0.001 | 3 |
| Extrusion of anteriors (excess) | +0.88 | +0.47 to +1.29 | < 0.001 | 4 |
| Canine distal tipping (excess) | +1.42 | +0.35 to +2.51 | 0.009 | 3 |
| First molar mesial tipping (anchorage loss) | +1.68 | +1.17 to +2.20 | < 0.001 | 3 |

### Why This Happens

Clear aligners transmit forces primarily through **tipping moments** rather than **bodily translation**. In extraction space closure:

- Anterior retraction requires sustained controlled **torque** to maintain root parallelism — aligners struggle with this without robust attachment systems
- **Anchorage control** (preventing posterior mesial drift) depends on differential force application that is difficult to achieve with uniform thermoplastic pressure
- The aligner's elastic recovery force tends to tip rather than translate teeth bodily

### Clinical Implications

- **Extraction space closure in clear aligners**: plan for overcorrection, mandatory power chain/elastic auxiliaries, and frequent refinements
- **TADs (임시 고정원, Temporary Anchorage Devices)** may be necessary to achieve adequate posterior anchorage in extraction cases
- **Patient counseling**: realistic expectations about treatment extension and mid-course corrections
- **Case selection**: fixed appliances should be the primary recommendation for patients with demanding space closure requirements and tight root-parallelism needs
- Fixed appliances appear superior for bodily movement and anchorage in extraction cases (limited direct comparison data, but mechanistically consistent)

### Evidence Quality

- **ROB**: 4/14 low risk, 10/14 high risk — majority low quality
- **GRADE**: Very low to low certainty across all outcomes
- **Primary system studied**: Invisalign® (generalizability to other brands limited)
- Meta-analysis limited to Invisalign® studies with comparable outcome reporting

### Relationship to Broader Clear Aligner Predictability Literature

This paper adds to a growing body showing that clear aligners systematically underperform for movements requiring:

1. Controlled bodily translation (torque)
2. Extrusion control
3. Differential anchorage management
