## Three-line Summary

Prospective cohort study (n=82 adult Invisalign patients) at two Vienna centers using a 32-item VAS questionnaire administered before treatment start, organized into five factor-derived categories.

Patients held near-ceiling expectations for treatment predictability (8.6/10) and outcome equivalence to fixed braces (9.5/10), while expressing near-zero willingness to compromise outcome quality for reduced time, cost, or fewer auxiliaries (Willingness-to-Compromise items all ≤2.6/10).

Clinicians should proactively address the expectation–experience gap during informed consent, especially for patients with severe malocclusion (PAR >17) who show significantly higher pain and diet-restriction concerns, since ~54.8% of CAT patients reportedly experience post-treatment dissatisfaction.

## Summary

This prospective cohort study (first part of a longitudinal series) surveyed 82 adult patients scheduled for Invisalign clear aligner therapy (CAT) at two Vienna centers (private practice and university clinic) prior to treatment initiation, using a non-validated 32-item visual analogue scale (VAS) questionnaire organized into five factor-derived categories: Limitations, Second Opinion, Efficiency and Predictability, Willingness to Compromise, and Quality of Treatment. Patients showed very high expectations for aesthetics (avoiding visible appliances was the top-rated limitation concern), treatment predictability, and long-term/lifetime durability of results, while expressing near-zero willingness to trade outcome quality for shorter treatment time or lower cost. Concerns about pain and dietary restriction during treatment were also rated highly and were stronger in patients with more severe baseline malocclusion (PAR index >17). The study also links expectation patterns to gender, age, and baseline OHRQoL (OHIP-14).

## Key Contributions

- Provides prospectively collected, structured baseline data on CAT patient expectations — filling a literature gap previously covered only by retrospective chart review, social media post analysis, or non-orthodontic-seeking laypeople samples.
- Organizes patient concerns into 5 clinically actionable categories via factor analysis: Limitations, Second Opinion, Efficiency/Predictability, Willingness to Compromise, Quality of Treatment.
- Quantifies that patients are essentially unwilling to accept a "slightly inferior" outcome in exchange for reduced treatment time, reduced cost, or fewer auxiliaries (elastics/attachments) — all four Willingness-to-Compromise items scored ≤2.6/10.
- Identifies subgroup patterns (gender, age, malocclusion severity, baseline OHRQoL) associated with differing expectation profiles, useful for tailoring informed-consent conversations.

## Methodology

Prospective cohort design; 82 adult patients (mean age 35 ± 12 years, 74.4% female) recruited September 2019–July 2022 from a private orthodontic practice and the University Dental Clinic of Vienna, scheduled for Invisalign "Comprehensive" or "Lite" package CAT (excluding prior aligner-treated patients, <18 years old, and combination fixed-appliance therapy). A 32-statement, non-validated VAS questionnaire (developed by experienced orthodontists) was administered prior to treatment start, alongside the validated German OHIP-14 (OHRQoL) and clinical PAR index (malocclusion severity). Items grouped by factor analysis into 5 categories (Cronbach's α ranging 0.38–0.78, two categories below the 0.7 acceptability threshold). Exploratory subgroup comparisons (gender, age ≤35 vs >35, PAR ≤17 vs >17, OHIP simple count <1 vs ≥1) used two-sample tests without multiple-testing correction (R 4.4.3).

## Results

- **Top-rated concerns**: avoiding visible orthodontic appliances (7.9/10), pain during treatment (7.7/10), restricted eating/drinking (7.3/10). Self-esteem impact rated lowest (2.3/10).
- **Efficiency/predictability**: prevention of future dental problems (9.1/10) and treatment predictability (8.6/10) rated highest of all 32 items alongside quality-of-treatment items.
- **Second opinion**: strong intent (7.2/10) to seek a second opinion if told CAT is not feasible for their case.
- **Willingness to compromise**: uniformly very low (2.1–2.6/10) across time, cost, elastics, and attachments — patients will not trade outcome quality.
- **Quality of treatment expectations**: near-ceiling expectation (9.5/10) that CAT will correct malocclusion at least as well as fixed braces; strong expectation of lifetime (8.8/10) or ≥5-year (8.7/10) durability.
- **Subgroup effects**: women rated predictability and time-efficiency higher and were more accepting of elastics than men; patients >35 years valued time efficiency more but wanted fewer visible attachments; patients with higher PAR index (more severe malocclusion) were significantly more worried about pain and dietary restriction; patients with worse baseline OHRQoL (OHIP ≥1) expected greater confidence gains post-treatment.
- Findings align with prior social-media-based reports that ~90% of CAT patients start with positive expectations, while ~54.8% report later dissatisfaction (aesthetics, discomfort, diet, speech) — underscoring a expectation-experience gap risk.
