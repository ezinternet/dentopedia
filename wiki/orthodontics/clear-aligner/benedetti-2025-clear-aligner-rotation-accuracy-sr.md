## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (12 studies, 1 RCT + 11 non-randomized; PRISMA; search through July 2025) of rotational accuracy in adult clear aligner patients, measured via 3D digital superimposition before refinement.

Overall rotational accuracy averaged ~65% (range 36–85%); molars were most predictable (74–85%), followed by premolars (54–83%) and incisors (35.1–90%), with canines least predictable (36–58%, ~4.5° residual error); attachment and IPR effectiveness remain inconsistent across studies.

Evidence quality is low to moderate; the canine is the single highest-risk tooth for rotational undercorrection, and reducing staging increments below 1.5°/aligner may improve outcomes.

## Summary

This systematic review evaluated the rotational accuracy of clear aligner therapy (CAT) across 12 studies in adult patients, using 3D digital model superimposition to compare predicted versus achieved tooth movement before any refinement stages. The overall finding is that rotational accuracy averages approximately 65%, with a wide range of 36–85% reflecting substantial heterogeneity across aligner systems, staging protocols, and tooth types.

Accuracy varies markedly by tooth type. Canines demonstrate the worst performance (~36–58%), with maxillary canines particularly challenging (~50%) and a typical residual error of ~4.5°. Molars are most reliable (74–85%), with first molars outperforming second molars. Premolars show intermediate accuracy (54–83%), and incisors show the widest range (35.1–90%), with maxillary central incisors being more predictable than laterals.

Attachments were used in 11 of 12 studies, yet controlled comparisons did not confirm consistent superiority of optimized over conventional designs for rotational movements. IPR showed a trend toward improvement (43% accuracy with IPR in the one study isolating it), but the effect was not statistically significant. Most studies used 2°/aligner staging; the literature suggests reducing this to <1.5° may improve accuracy.

## Key Contributions

- **Tooth-type accuracy hierarchy**: Canines < Incisors(variable) < Premolars < Molars — a clinically actionable ranking.
- **65% accuracy benchmark**: The first SR to report a consolidated average across all studies and tooth types, useful for patient counseling about expected undercorrection.
- **Attachment controversy reframed**: Most clinical practice assumes optimized attachments enhance rotation — this SR finds the evidence is insufficient to confirm this claim.
- **Staging implication**: Reducing rotation increment per aligner below 1.5° is an underexplored optimization target.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review (PRISMA 2020), no meta-analysis (heterogeneity precluded pooling)
- **N studies**: 12 (1 RCT, 11 non-randomized)
- **Population**: Adults ≥18, full permanent dentition, clear aligner monotherapy
- **Measurement**: 3D digital superimposition; pre-refinement only
- **Risk of bias**: RoB 2 (RCT: some concerns); ROBINS-I (non-randomized: no low-risk studies)
- **GRADE**: RCT → moderate; non-randomized → low

## Results

**Rotational accuracy by tooth type:**

| Tooth | Accuracy | Residual error |
|---|---|---|
| Molars (1st) | 74–85% | ~0.4–2.3° |
| Premolars | 54–83% | ~2.2–4.6° |
| Incisors (central) | up to 90% | — |
| Incisors (lateral) | 35.1–75% | — |
| Canines | 36–58% | ~4.5° |

Overall: 36–85%, mean ~65%

**Attachments**: used in 11/12 studies; effectiveness "inconsistent"; no controlled comparison confirms benefit for rotation

**IPR**: Kravitz study — 43% accuracy with IPR vs lower without (not statistically significant)

**Staging**: predominantly 2°/aligner, 14-day wear; literature suggests <1.5°/aligner may improve accuracy
