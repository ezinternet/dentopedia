## Three-line Summary

3D finite-element study (single CBCT/IOS-based adult maxillary model) comparing three attachment designs — vertical rectangular, combined semi-elliptical, opposed (buccal+palatal) semi-elliptical — for maxillary second-molar distalization, with and without simultaneous expansion, and testing the effect of a present erupted third molar.

The opposed semi-elliptical attachment gave the most parallel distal movement (least tipping/rotation, ~75% of the distalization of the other designs); a present third molar reduced maximum total distal displacement by 17% (10–22% at crown points) and increased distopalatal rotation; adding expansion to distalization roughly doubled/tripled distal-buccal displacement but with proportionally more tipping/rotation, especially for the opposed design.

Attachment geometry trades off distalization magnitude against parallel (bodily) control — a palatal attachment component reduces unwanted tipping/rotation but a fully-erupted third molar should be factored into distalization planning; findings are single-model FEA and require clinical validation.

## 세줄요약

CBCT/구강스캔 기반 단일 성인 상악 모델의 3차원 유한요소해석(Finite Element Analysis, FEA)으로 상악 제2대구치 원심이동(distalization)에서 3가지 어태치먼트(수직 사각형·복합 반타원형·협측+구개측 대합 반타원형) 디자인을 비교하고, 동시 확장(expansion) 적용 여부 및 완전맹출 제3대구치 존재 여부의 영향을 평가했다.

협측+구개측 대합 반타원형 어태치먼트가 가장 평행한 원심이동(최소 경사·회전, 타 디자인 대비 약 75% 이동량)을 보였고, 제3대구치가 있으면 최대 총 변위가 17%(치관점 기준 10–22%) 감소하며 원심구개측(DP) 회전은 증가; 확장을 동시 적용하면 원심·협측 변위가 2–3배 증가하나 특히 대합 반타원형 디자인에서 경사·회전도 비례해 증가.

어태치먼트 형태에 따라 이동량과 평행이동(치체이동) 제어 사이 trade-off가 뚜렷하며, 구개측 요소가 있는 디자인이 원치 않는 경사·회전을 줄이지만 완전맹출 제3대구치가 있으면 원심이동 계획을 더 신중히 세워야 한다(이동 지연, 어태치먼트·아라이너 응력 증가) — 단일모델 FEA 결과로 임상검증이 필요하다.

## 한줄요약

단일 성인 상악 모델 3D 유한요소해석(FEA) — 협측+구개측 대합 반타원형(opposed semi-elliptical) 어태치먼트(Attachment)가 가장 평행한 상악 제2대구치 원심이동(Distalization)을 만들고, 완전맹출 제3대구치가 있으면 원심이동이 10–22% 저해된다.

## Summary

This finite element study from Hacettepe University (Korean Journal of Orthodontics) built a single-patient 3D maxillary model (CBCT + intraoral scan) to directly compare three composite-attachment geometries — vertical rectangular, combined semi-elliptical (buccal only), and opposed semi-elliptical (buccal + palatal) — for maxillary second-molar distalization with clear aligners, both alone and combined with simultaneous transverse expansion. It also isolated the biomechanical effect of a fully-erupted third molar on second-molar distalization by comparing otherwise-identical models with and without the adjacent tooth present. The opposed (bilateral) attachment design produced the most parallel, tipping-minimized movement, at the cost of achieving only ~75% of the raw distalization magnitude seen with the other two designs; adding expansion amplified displacement 2–3 fold but also amplified unwanted tipping/rotation, especially for the opposed design, which showed the highest stress of all six models. Independently, the presence of a third molar reduced distal displacement 10–22% at crown-level landmarks (17% at maximum total displacement) and increased distopalatal rotation — implying slower, less efficient distalization and higher aligner/attachment stress when a second molar must move past an erupted third molar. As a single-model FEA study, findings describe biomechanical tendencies rather than guaranteed clinical outcomes, but they offer concrete, comparable attachment-design and third-molar-planning guidance that prior narrative/SR-level attachment literature could not quantify head-to-head.

## Key Contributions

- Direct **within-model comparison of three attachment geometries** for the same molar-distalization task — not a synthesis of heterogeneous studies, but a controlled computational comparison.
- Quantifies the **third-molar drag effect** on distalization (10–22% reduced crown displacement, 17% reduced max total displacement, increased DP rotation) — a concrete number for clinicians deciding whether to extract or accept a slower distalization timeline.
- Identifies a clear **displacement-magnitude vs. tipping-control trade-off**: the most parallel-moving attachment (opposed semi-elliptical) is also the least efficient at raw distalization.
- Extends the combined-movement literature by modeling **simultaneous distalization + expansion**, showing tipping/rotation compounds substantially versus distalization alone.

## Methodology

- **Model**: single 29-year-old adult female (Hacettepe Faculty of Dentistry archive), CBCT + iTero Element 2 IOS superimposed; mirrored half-jaw for symmetry; PDL modeled as 0.25 mm shell; 0.625 mm aligner thickness.
- **Attachments**: rectangular 3×2×1 mm (vertical); combined semi-elliptical Ø2.0×0.8 mm (buccal only); opposed semi-elliptical Ø2.0×0.8 mm (buccal 45° + palatal 135°).
- **6 models, 2 scenarios**: Scenario 1 (0.25 mm distalization only) — Model I (rectangular), II (rectangular + 3rd molar), III (combined semi-elliptical), IV (opposed semi-elliptical). Scenario 2 (+ 0.50 mm expansion) — Model V (III+expansion), VI (IV+expansion).
- **Analysis**: Ansys 19.2, transient dynamic non-linear FEA (geometric/contact/material non-linearity); frictional contact (teeth↔aligner, aligner↔attachment, 2nd↔3rd molar μ=0.2); bonded contact (teeth↔PDL, bone↔PDL, attachment↔tooth); maxilla fixed at pterygomaxillary suture.
- **Outcomes**: crown/root-apex landmark displacement and 3-plane rotation (buccal tipping, distal tipping, DB/DP rotation); aligner deformation pattern; von Mises stress on teeth/attachment.

## Results

| Comparison | Key finding |
|---|---|
| ± 3rd molar (Model I vs II) | 3rd molar present → distal displacement ↓10–22% at crown points, max total displacement ↓17%, DP rotation ↑, distal tipping ↓ |
| Attachment type, distalization only (I, III, IV) | Model IV (opposed) = most parallel movement (least tipping/rotation), ~75% of the magnitude of I/III; I & III similar to each other, III shows more DP rotation than I |
| + expansion (Model V vs VI) | Model VI (opposed) > Model V (combined) in distal/buccal displacement AND tipping/rotation; Model VI shows highest stress of all 6 models |
| Aligner deformation | I/III (no palatal component) → more/palatal-side aligner deformation; IV → distally-concentrated, most uniform under combined movement (V/VI) |

## Clinical Bottom Line

- Prefer an **opposed (buccal + palatal) semi-elliptical attachment** when parallel, tipping-controlled distalization is the priority (e.g., root-parallelism-sensitive cases); accept it will move the tooth less per stage than a rectangular or buccal-only design.
- When simultaneous **distalization + expansion** is planned, expect substantially more tipping/rotation with a bilateral attachment — consider staging distalization and expansion sequentially if bodily control is critical.
- A fully-erupted **third molar** slows and reduces second-molar distalization efficiency (~10–22%) and raises attachment/aligner stress — factor this into staging, force estimates, and third-molar extraction/management decisions before planning aligner-based distalization.
