## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (SANRA-compliant, 128 studies: 110 case reports/series + 18 observational) synthesizing all camouflage modalities for adult skeletal Class III malocclusion — extraction protocols, labial/lingual fixed appliances, self-ligating systems, clear aligners, Carrière Motion III, MEAW, and skeletal-anchorage (TAD/miniplate) distalization.

86% of the underlying evidence is level 4–5 (case report/small series); clear aligners achieve only 2–3 mm lower-arch distalization with ~74% crown-level but ~47% root-level efficiency (predominantly tipping, not bodily movement), while overall Class III camouflage relapse is ~30% and extraction-based camouflage shows superior long-term stability over non-extraction approaches.

Clinical implication: aligner-based Class III camouflage is a viable esthetic option for mild-to-moderate, low/normo-angle, high-compliance adults, but its torque/vertical/root-level movement limitations mean it should not be treated as interchangeable with fixed-appliance or TAD-assisted approaches for more severe or bodily-movement-heavy cases.

## Summary

This narrative review (SANRA-compliant, 5-database search to September 2025) synthesizes 128 studies on orthodontic camouflage for adult skeletal Class III malocclusion — the dentoalveolar-compensation alternative to orthognathic surgery for patients who are ineligible for, unwilling to undergo, or have skeletal discrepancies mild enough to avoid, surgical correction. The review covers the full landscape of camouflage modalities (extraction protocols, labial/lingual fixed appliances, self-ligating brackets, clear aligners, Carrière Motion III, MEAW, and skeletal-anchorage-assisted distalization), grouped by treatment protocol (extraction vs. non-extraction) and appliance type.

Overall evidence quality is low: 86% of the 128 included studies are case reports or small case series (OCEBM level 4–5), 13% are retrospective observational studies, and only 1% are prospective. The review proposes a 4-parameter framework for predicting camouflage candidacy — (1) skeletal discrepancy magnitude relative to dentoalveolar compensatory capacity, (2) mandibular symphysis morphology and incisor-inclination limits, (3) vertical growth pattern, (4) soft-tissue profile response — and positions appliance choice along an esthetics-vs-predictability spectrum: labial fixed appliances offer the best torque/vertical control but least esthetics; lingual appliances balance esthetics with good torque control at the cost of technical complexity; clear aligners are most esthetic but weakest in torque/vertical predictability.

For clear aligners specifically, the review reports lower-arch distalization is achievable in a 2–3 mm range, with efficiency around 74% at the crown level but only 47% at the root level for the first molar (i.e., movement is predominantly distal tipping rather than bodily translation). Aligners are positioned as most valuable for adults with high esthetic demands or periodontal compromise (better plaque control and lower decalcification/attachment-loss risk than fixed appliances), and can assist vertical control in hyperdivergent patients using Class III elastics, though their intrusive effect is limited because masticatory loading mostly occurs when the aligner is removed. A single prospective study found aligners plus Class III elastics modestly improved facial profile in mild Class III cases (increased upper-lip projection, lower-arch distalization) without altering the lower facial third.

Skeletal-anchorage systems (TADs, ramal plates) are favored when larger sagittal corrections or more bodily distalization are required — ramal plates achieve 2.0–3.6 mm of first-molar distalization with less tipping than miniscrews, though miniplates (7.3% failure) are more resistant to mechanical failure than miniscrews (16.4% failure). Overall relapse across all Class III camouflage approaches is about 30%, and extraction-based camouflage demonstrates statistically superior long-term stability compared with non-extraction protocols — a caveat the review extends explicitly to aligner-based camouflage, noting favorable short-term results but persisting uncertainty about the long-term stability of complex aligner-driven movements (distalization, torque control, incisor compensation).

## Key Contributions

- Comprehensive narrative synthesis of adult Class III camouflage across **all modalities**, filling the gap left by prior systematic reviews that predate widespread clear-aligner and skeletal-anchorage adoption.
- Quantitative benchmarks aggregated in one place: aligner lower-molar distalization efficiency (74% crown / 47% root), CM3 occlusal-plane rotation (−3.1°), miniplate vs. miniscrew failure rates (7.3% vs. 16.4%), ramal-plate distalization (2.0–3.6 mm), and overall Class III camouflage relapse rate (~30%).
- 4-parameter diagnostic framework for camouflage-candidacy prediction (skeletal discrepancy vs. compensatory capacity; symphysis morphology/incisor limits; vertical pattern; soft-tissue response).
- Explicit long-term-stability caveat for aligner-based Class III camouflage — "favorable short-term outcomes, but concerns remain regarding long-term stability" — that neither of the wiki's existing single-case Class III aligner reports states this plainly, since both report only their own follow-up window without population context.

## Methodology

Non-systematic literature search (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar; inception–19 September 2025; terms "camouflage" + "Class III malocclusion"; no language/year/status restriction). 306 duplicates removed → 315 screened → 212 full-text assessed → 128 studies included (110 case reports/series, 18 observational studies, 2 of which shared a patient sample). Quality qualitatively assessed via the OCEBM 2011 Levels of Evidence framework; no formal risk-of-bias tool or meta-analysis applied — a qualitative, SANRA-structured narrative synthesis, not a systematic review.

## Results

**Comparative table of Class III camouflage modalities (per this review):**

| Modality | Mechanism | Typical indication | Key limitation / evidence tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premolar extraction | Incisor retroclination/proclination into extraction space | Moderate crowding, proclined lower incisors | Best long-term stability (extraction > non-extraction); case-series tier |
| Single lower-incisor extraction | Reduced intercanine-width alteration | Mild crossbite, favorable Bolton ratio | Black-triangle risk, small Bolton discrepancy → ↑overjet |
| Labial fixed + Class III elastics | Spontaneous dentoalveolar compensation | Mild-moderate skeletal Class III, compliant patient | Compliance-dependent; molar extrusion/tipping side effects |
| Self-ligating | Reduced friction, ~1.5° less proclination vs. conventional | Similar to fixed appliance | Case-report tier only, no clear SR advantage |
| Lingual fixed | Superior esthetics + good torque control | High esthetic demand, post-lower-extraction torque control | Limited transverse expansion; technically demanding |
| **Clear aligners** | 1.5 mm occlusal disclusion aids crossbite; distalization via tipping | High esthetic demand; periodontally compromised adults; mild-moderate cases | **2–3 mm distalization; 74% crown / 47% root efficiency; weak long-term stability data** |
| Carrière Motion III | Posterior mandibular-segment distalization → "Class I platform" | Adults unwilling/unsuitable for surgery, moderate correction | Primarily dentoalveolar (−3.1° occlusal-plane rotation); limited facial-profile change |
| MEAW | Multiloop archwire, 5-phase occlusal reconstruction | Mild-moderate, mesially-tipped posterior teeth | High-angle cases risk clockwise occlusal-plane rotation unless combined with TADs |
| Skeletal anchorage (TAD/miniplate) | Independent anchorage for bodily distalization | Larger sagittal correction, reduced extraction need | Ramal plates: 2.0–3.6 mm distalization, 7.3% failure; miniscrews: ≤2–3 mm, 16.4% failure |

**Stability**: overall Class III camouflage relapse ~30% (overjet <1 mm or occlusal relationship re-emergence); extraction-based camouflage statistically more stable long-term than non-extraction. Diagnostic thresholds distinguishing camouflage-feasible from surgery-indicated borderline cases: ANB ≤ −4°, incisor–mandibular plane angle ≤ 83°, plus Wits appraisal and Holdaway angle.

**Aligner-specific caveat**: "Clear aligner–based camouflage may provide favorable short-term outcomes, but concerns remain regarding the long-term stability of complex movements such as distalization, torque control, and incisor compensation, given the limitations in movement predictability and retention dependence."
