## One-line Summary

SR (20 studies, SWiM): refinement after the first aligner series is a multifactorial outcome driven by case complexity, extraction, IPR, and specific movement limitations; remote monitoring reduces appointments but not refinement need.

## 한줄요약

## Summary

This systematic review systematically identified and synthesized evidence on factors driving refinement after the first series of clear aligners. Twenty studies were included using SWiM methodology due to high heterogeneity in refinement definitions. Refinement-related outcomes were heterogeneously defined across studies (refinement aligners, additional aligners, finishing phases, midcourse correction, first-refinement scans). The evidence indicates refinement burden is associated with malocclusion complexity, extraction treatment, interproximal reduction strategies, severe spacing, larger correction sequences, and movement-specific limitations. Rotations, intrusion, vertical correction, transverse expansion, and selected anterior movements showed reduced predictability. Remote monitoring reduced appointment burden but did not consistently reduce refinement need. Evidence for AI/ML-based prediction tools remains preliminary.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes refinement as a multifactorial clinical outcome rather than a routine finishing step
- Identifies 6 key drivers of refinement burden
- Documents that remote monitoring does not reduce refinement need
- Highlights preliminary status of AI/ML refinement prediction
- Calls for standardized definitions of refinement across research

## Methodology

Design: SR (PRISMA 2020 + SWiM). Databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane, Google Scholar. Population: clear aligner patients (any age). Inclusion: studies reporting refinement, additional aligners, finishing phases, midcourse correction, planned-vs-achieved discrepancies. 20 studies included. Risk of bias: design-specific tools. Analysis: structured narrative synthesis. PROSPERO CRD420261416896.

## Results

- Refinement drivers: malocclusion complexity, extraction, IPR strategies, severe spacing, larger correction sequences, movement limitations
- Low-predictability movements requiring refinement: rotations, intrusion, vertical correction, transverse expansion, selected anterior movements
- Remote monitoring: reduces appointment burden; does NOT consistently reduce refinement need
- AI/ML prediction tools: evidence preliminary
- Outcome heterogeneity: "refinement" defined differently across all 20 studies

## Three-line Summary

- Systematic review (20 studies, PRISMA 2020 + SWiM, PROSPERO CRD420261416896) identifying clinical, biomechanical, and digital factors associated with refinement after the first clear aligner series.
- Refinement burden was driven by malocclusion complexity, extraction treatment, interproximal reduction strategies, severe spacing, larger correction sequences, and specific movement limitations (rotations, intrusion, vertical correction, transverse expansion); remote monitoring reduced appointment burden but did not consistently reduce refinement need; AI/ML prediction evidence remains preliminary.
- Refinement should be anticipated proactively — especially in extraction and complex cases — rather than managed reactively; outcome definitions were heterogeneous across all 20 studies, limiting cross-study comparisons and preventing meta-analysis.

- 리파인먼트 부담 결정 요인: 증례 복잡도·발치·치간삭제 (Interproximal Reduction, IPR)·심한 공간·큰 교정 순서·특정 이동 유형 한계 (회전·압하·수직·수평 확장); 원격 모니터링은 내원 횟수 감소시키나 리파인먼트 필요성은 미감소; AI/ML 예측 도구 근거 예비적.
- 발치·복잡 증례에서는 선제적 리파인먼트 계획 필수; 20개 연구 모두 다른 결과 정의 사용 — 메타분석 불가, 교차 비교 제한.
