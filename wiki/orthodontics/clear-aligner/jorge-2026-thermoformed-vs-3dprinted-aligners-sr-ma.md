## One-line Summary

SR+MA (23 in vitro studies; 4 in meta-analysis) comparing thermoformed (TM) vs direct 3D-printed (DTP) aligners: TM has higher flexural strength/modulus/hardness; DTP has better shape recovery, greater vertical traction forces, superior geometric accuracy, and lower glass-transition temperature (Tg = 42.3 °C); thickness-variation meta-analysis non-significant (SMD 1.48; 95% CI −7.26 to 4.30; p = .62; I² = 97%).

## Summary

This SR+MA pooled 23 in vitro studies examining physical/mechanical properties and dimensional accuracy of thermoformed (TM) vs direct 3D-printed (DTP) clear aligners. TM aligners consistently showed higher initial rigidity — reflected in greater flexural strength, storage modulus stability, and surface microhardness — whereas DTP aligners exhibited higher shape-recovery potential, greater traction forces in vertical loading tests, and superior geometric accuracy under laboratory conditions. The thickness meta-analysis (4 studies) found TM aligners thin by ~39.76% after thermoforming and DTP thicken by ~11.02%, but the pooled SMD was non-significant (I² = 97%), signaling irreconcilable heterogeneity across testing protocols. DTP's lower Tg of 42.3 °C raises thermomechanical concerns for intraoral conditions. All evidence is in vitro; QUIN assessment found 18/23 studies at medium and 4/23 at high risk of bias, with no low-RoB studies, precluding definitive clinical conclusions.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the first SR+MA quantitative framework for TM vs DTP mechanical/physical property comparison in orthodontic aligners
- Identifies a fundamental mechanical trade-off: TM stiffness/hardness vs DTP shape-recovery/geometric precision
- Documents DTP geometric accuracy advantage under lab conditions — clinically relevant for torque, rotation, and precision tooth movements
- Quantifies the fabrication-induced thickness paradox (TM −39.76%, DTP +11.02%) with extreme between-study heterogeneity (I² = 97%) that precludes a clinical thickness-preference recommendation
- Highlights DTP's Tg = 42.3 °C as a potential intraoral thermomechanical vulnerability (hot beverages, febrile states)

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA)
- **Scope:** In vitro studies only; no clinical trials included
- **N studies:** 23 total; 4 contributed to meta-analysis
- **Populations:** Bench specimens of TM aligners (thermoplastic sheets: PETG, PU, multilayer variants) vs DTP resin aligners (photo-cured resin, MSLA/DLP)
- **Primary outcomes:** Flexural strength, storage modulus, surface microhardness, shape-recovery, traction forces, geometric accuracy, thickness variation, surface roughness (Sa, Sv, Sz), Tg
- **Risk of bias:** QUIN tool — 18 medium RoB, 4 high RoB; 0 low RoB
- **Meta-analysis:** SMD with 95% CI; heterogeneity via I²

## Results

**Mechanical/physical properties (qualitative synthesis):**
- TM superior: flexural strength, storage modulus (stiffness stability), surface microhardness, initial rigidity
- DTP superior: shape-recovery potential (spring-back toward programmed geometry), traction forces in vertical test configurations

**Dimensional accuracy:**
- DTP geometric accuracy better under laboratory conditions

**Thickness variation (meta-analysis, n = 4 studies):**
- TM post-fabrication: −39.76% (thinning)
- DTP post-fabrication: +11.02% (thickening)
- Pooled: SMD 1.48 (95% CI −7.26 to 4.30); p = .62; **I² = 97%** → non-significant; extreme heterogeneity; no clinical conclusion possible

**Surface roughness:**
- DTP: higher Sa (mean surface amplitude) and higher Sz (total amplitude after clinical use)
- TM: higher Sv (valley depth)

**Thermal:**
- DTP Tg = 42.3 °C (below temperature of hot beverages; risk of thermomechanical softening in vivo)

**Summary verdict:** No universally superior material; properties diverge by testing dimension; all data in vitro.
