## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review of 4 SRs with meta-analyses (2,042 patients; clear aligners n=978 vs fixed appliances n=1,064) assessing five periodontal parameters (PI, GI, BOP/SBI, PPD, gingival recession) across short-, mid-, and long-term follow-up.

Clear aligners showed statistically better plaque index (MD −0.35 to −1.10), gingival index, sulcus bleeding index, and PPD (MD −0.26 to −0.93) at short/mid-term; gingival recession was stable with aligners vs significant increase with fixed at 3 months; all long-term differences were non-significant.

Periodontal effects of both modalities are "comparable" — statistical short-term advantages of clear aligners are clinically negligible (<1 mm PPD; <1 index point); 1 of 4 included SRs was high quality, 2 were low quality.

## Summary

This umbrella review — the highest-level synthesis design — pooled findings from four systematic reviews with meta-analyses to compare the periodontal impact of clear aligners versus fixed appliances in 2,042 orthodontic patients. Across all five periodontal parameters assessed, clear aligners showed statistically better outcomes at short- and mid-term follow-up, with the clearest advantages in plaque index and sulcus bleeding. However, the authors concluded these differences are "practically negligible in a clinical setting" since all fall below thresholds of clinical relevance (PPD differences <1 mm; index differences <1 point).

Gingival recession is the most practically relevant finding: clear aligners maintained gingival stability at 3 months while fixed appliances showed a significant recession increase. Long-term recession data are absent from the literature. Long-term follow-up showed no significant differences for any parameter.

Quality of included evidence is mixed: one high-quality SR (Crego-Ruiz 2023), one moderate (Jiang 2018), and two low-quality SRs (Lu 2018, Wu 2022). High heterogeneity across SRs and overlapping primary studies limit the confidence of pooled estimates.

## Key Contributions

- **Umbrella review design**: Synthesizes 4 SRs (2,042 patients) — highest evidence synthesis level available for this comparison.
- **Time-stratified conclusion**: Early advantage of aligners for plaque/gingival indices attenuates over time and disappears at ≥12 months — critical for patient counseling (short-term benefit does not persist).
- **Gingival recession signal**: Only umbrella review to highlight stable gingival margins with aligners vs fixed at 3 months — potentially relevant for periodontally susceptible patients.
- **Clinical vs statistical gap**: Explicit analysis separating statistically significant from clinically meaningful differences.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Umbrella review (SR of SRs with MA)
- **Included SRs**: 4 systematic reviews with meta-analyses (Jiang 2018, Lu 2018, Wu 2022, Crego-Ruiz 2023)
- **N patients**: 2,042 (CA: 978; fixed: 1,064; ages 10–51 years)
- **Outcomes**: PI, GI, BOP/SBI, PPD, gingival recession
- **SR quality**: AMSTAR 2 (1 high, 1 moderate, 2 low)
- **Follow-up stratification**: short-term (2–3 months), mid-term (6–9 months), long-term (≥12 months)

## Results

| Parameter | Short-term | Mid-term | Long-term |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plaque Index | CA better (MD −0.35 to −0.69) | CA superior (MD −0.91 to −1.10) | NS |
| Gingival Index | CA better (MD −0.24 to −0.37) | CA small advantage (MD −0.14) | NS |
| SBI/BOP | CA superior (SMD −0.44 to −0.49) | CA favored (SMD −0.91) | — |
| PPD | CA better (MD −0.26 to −0.42) | CA better | CA limits worsening (MD −0.45 to −0.93) |
| Gingival Recession | CA stable; fixed: significant increase | — | No data |

**Clinical interpretation**: All differences <1 mm or <1 index point → below clinical significance thresholds.
