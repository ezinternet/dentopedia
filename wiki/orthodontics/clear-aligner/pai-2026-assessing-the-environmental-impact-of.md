## Three-line Summary

This PRISMA-ScR scoping review synthesized 13 studies (2014–2024) on the environmental impact of clear aligner therapy (CAT), identifying five themes: aligner materials/manufacturing, waste management/recycling, microplastics, carbon emissions, and sustainability strategies. Over 25 million aligners are discarded annually; PET-G, TPU, and PP polymers persist in landfills up to 100 years, release microplastics during degradation, and generate greenhouse gases across the production-to-disposal life cycle; direct 3D printing (DLP) life-cycle assessment shows lower environmental burden than conventional thermoforming. Current recycling programs cannot achieve closed-loop reprocessing; circular economy, biodegradable polymers, and digital workflows are proposed mitigation strategies, but evidence remains sparse due to absence of standardized life-cycle assessment methodology for CAT.

## 세줄요약

이 범위 검토 (Scoping Review, SR)는 2014–2024년 사이 출판된 13편을 종합해 투명교정장치 치료 (Clear Aligner Therapy, CAT)의 환경 영향을 5가지 주제(재료·제조, 폐기물·재활용, 미세플라스틱, 탄소배출, 지속가능성 전략)로 정리했다. 매년 2,500만 개 이상의 교정장치가 폐기되며, 폴리에틸렌 테레프탈레이트 글리콜 (Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol, PET-G)·열가소성 폴리우레탄 (Thermoplastic Polyurethane, TPU) 등 석유계 고분자는 매립 시 최대 100년 잔류하고 미세플라스틱 (Microplastics)·온실가스 (Greenhouse Gas, GHG)를 방출한다. 직접 3D 프린팅 (Digital Light Processing, DLP)이 열성형법보다 생애주기평가 (Life Cycle Assessment, LCA) 부담이 낮다는 초기 증거가 있으나 폐쇄루프 (closed-loop) 재활용 체계가 부재하고 고질적 증거 부족으로 지속가능한 교정 임상 전환에는 추가 연구가 필요하다.

## Summary

The growing adoption of clear aligner therapy (CAT) has created a substantial and largely uncharacterized environmental burden. This scoping review — conducted following PRISMA-ScR guidelines and the Population–Concept–Context (PCC) framework — searched five electronic databases (PubMed Central, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, EBSCOhost) for studies published between January 2014 and December 2024. Of 293 identified records, 13 studies met inclusion criteria and were synthesized using descriptive thematic analysis.

The review is the first comprehensive mapping of environmental evidence for CAT and establishes a five-theme framework: (1) aligner materials and manufacturing processes, (2) waste management and recycling practices, (3) microplastics and environmental contamination, (4) carbon emissions and life-cycle environmental impacts, and (5) emerging sustainability strategies and evidence gaps. Most included studies were narrative reviews or laboratory investigations; primary environmental assessments using standardized life-cycle assessment (LCA) methodology were notably scarce.

## Key Contributions

1. **Scale of the problem quantified**: >25 million aligners discarded annually; conventional PET trays persist in landfills up to 100 years.
2. **Material toxicology documented**: Incineration of PET-G releases benzene (carcinogen); polyurethane combustion releases tetrahydrofuran (suspected carcinogen).
3. **LCA evidence for 3D printing**: Direct 3D printing using Digital Light Processing (DLP) demonstrated lower raw material consumption, electricity use, and waste generation vs conventional thermoforming in one LCA study (Caelli et al. 2023).
4. **SDG alignment mapped**: CAT environmental challenges linked to SDGs 3 (health/well-being), 9 (industry/innovation), 12 (responsible consumption/production), 13 (climate action).
5. **Critical gap identified**: No standardized environmental reporting framework or comprehensive comparative LCA exists for CAT; existing evidence largely limited to narrative reviews.

Inclusion criteria required full-text, English-language publications evaluating environmental sustainability of CAT: materials, manufacturing, waste, recycling, carbon emissions, or life-cycle impacts. Studies focused exclusively on clinical outcomes, biomechanics, or patient satisfaction without environmental outcomes were excluded.

## Results

### Theme 1: Aligner Materials and Manufacturing (9 studies)

Conventional thermoplastic polymers — PET-G, polypropylene (PP), polycarbonate (PC), TPU, and ethylene-vinyl acetate (EVA) — are petroleum-based with limited recyclability. They dominate the market due to desirable mechanical and clinical properties but contribute to plastic waste and microplastic generation upon degradation. Bisphenol-A (BPA) from polycarbonate-based aligners may leach intraorally; EPA safe intake limit is 50 µg/kg body weight, but adverse effects reported below this threshold.

Direct 3D printing (DLP additive manufacturing) and shape-memory polymers were identified as promising alternatives. The sole LCA study (Caelli et al. 2023) found DLP reduces raw material consumption, electricity, and manufacturing waste compared to thermoforming. However, the authors and this review emphasize that additional LCA-based investigations are required before these technologies can be considered established sustainable alternatives.

### Theme 2: Waste Management and Recycling (8 studies)

Clear aligners are single-patient-use products replaced throughout treatment, generating a large and continuously growing waste stream. Key statistics:
- **>25 million** aligners discarded annually (Gandhi et al. 2023)
- PET trays persist in landfills for **up to 100 years**

The 4R principle (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle, Rethink/Recover) was consistently recommended across studies. Manufacturer-supported take-back programs were proposed but remain rare. Critical barrier: no closed-loop recycling system exists for producing new aligners from recycled material. Existing programs are limited to downcycling (e.g., floor tiles, energy recovery).

### Theme 3: Microplastics and Environmental Contamination (6 studies)

Polymer degradation of thermoplastics in clinical use, landfill, and incineration generates environmental microplastic contamination. Microplastic exposure in dental clinical settings was measurable; the most common polymer detected: **polyethylene terephthalate**. Polymer Hazard Index (PHI) for dental professionals: **minor to medium** health risk (Akhtar et al. 2024), with higher exposure in teaching hospitals vs clinical settings, and female dental professionals at greater inhalation risk. Standardized methods for quantifying microplastic release from aligners are absent.

### Theme 4: Carbon Emissions and Life-Cycle Impacts (7 studies)

Environmental footprint of CAT extends beyond visible plastic waste to include energy consumption during material production, manufacturing processes, transportation logistics, and end-of-life disposal. Incineration of PET-G produces **benzene** (recognized carcinogen) and other irritants; polyurethane incineration produces **tetrahydrofuran** (suspected carcinogen) (Peter et al. 2024). Reduction strategies proposed: digital workflows, shorter treatment protocols, optimized manufacturing. Very few studies have performed comprehensive comparative LCA across different aligner manufacturing and disposal pathways.

### Theme 5: Evidence Gaps and Sustainability Strategies

Thematic synthesis across all 13 studies identified a consistent finding: available sustainability evidence for CAT is limited, non-standardized, and largely based on narrative reviews or laboratory investigations. Proposed strategies include:
- Biodegradable polymers (Polyhydroxyalkanoates/PHA, Polylactic acid/PLA, Polyamide-12/PA-12)
- Direct 3D printing (DLP) with reduced thermoforming scrap
- Shape-memory polymers enabling fewer, more adaptive aligners
- Circular economy models with extended producer responsibility
- Digital orthodontic workflows (intraoral scanners reducing plastic impression waste)
- Patient education on responsible disposal and take-back programs

All remain at the proposal or early-evidence stage; none has been evaluated in a comprehensive prospective environmental study.
