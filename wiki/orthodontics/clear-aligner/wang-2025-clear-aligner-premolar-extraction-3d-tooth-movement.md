## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study, 47 patients (542 teeth) treated with four-premolar-extraction clear aligner therapy (CAT); pre/post-treatment CBCT superimposition measured crown AND root movement in three dimensions and compared to ClinCheck-designed movement.

Root movement was consistently underachieved versus crown movement — maximum discrepancy at U1 root (5.49±2.30 mm) in the anteroposterior direction; anterior teeth extruded despite designed intrusion, and U3 crowns achieved only 71.5% of designed distal movement; age, extraction site (U4 vs U5), anchorage method, staging pattern, and attachment design significantly influenced discrepancies.

Clinical implication: ClinCheck for premolar-extraction CAT needs systematic root-movement/torque overcorrection (incisor and canine root retraction, anterior intrusion), and choice of anchorage/attachment/staging materially changes anchorage-loss and root-control risk — 47-patient single-center retrospective, small subgroup n's limit factor analysis.

## 세줄요약

후향적 연구, 47명(542개 치아) 4소구치 발치 투명교정(CAT) 증례; 치료 전후 CBCT 중첩으로 크라운뿐 아니라 치근의 3차원 이동을 측정하여 ClinCheck 설계값과 비교.

치근 이동은 크라운 대비 일관되게 미달성 — 전후방(APD)에서 U1 치근이 최대 불일치(5.49±2.30 mm); 전치는 함입 설계에도 불구하고 오히려 정출되었고, U3 크라운은 설계된 원심이동의 71.5%만 달성; 연령, 발치부위(U4 vs U5), 고정원 방법, 단계화 방식, 부착물 설계가 불일치에 유의한 영향.

임상적 의미: 소구치 발치 투명교정의 ClinCheck 설계에는 치근이동·토크 과보정(절치·견치 치근 후방견인, 전치부 함입)이 체계적으로 필요하며, 고정원·부착물·단계화 선택이 anchorage loss와 root control 위험을 좌우함 — 단일기관 후향적 47명 연구로 일부 서브그룹 표본수가 작아 요인분석 해석에 제한.

## Summary

This retrospective CBCT study is among the first to measure **root** — not just crown — movement in three dimensions for four-premolar-extraction clear aligner therapy (CAT), addressing a well-known gap in the extraction-CAT literature: prior studies on the "roller coaster effect," curve-of-Spee undercorrection, and torque loss have relied almost entirely on crown-level or 2D metrics. In 47 patients (542 maxillary teeth), pre/post CBCT superimposed on the anterior cranial base showed that root movement systematically underachieved the ClinCheck design across nearly every tooth and axis, with the incisor and canine roots most affected. The maximum discrepancy — U1 root anteroposterior displacement, 5.49±2.30 mm short of design — quantifies the torque-loss problem that prior crown-only studies could only infer. Anterior teeth extruded despite being designed for intrusion, canines achieved barely 71.5% of designed distal crown movement, and posterior teeth mesially tipped beyond plan (anchorage loss), with roots moving less than crowns in nearly every comparison. A linear mixed-effects model identified concrete, actionable levers: extraction site (U4 vs U5), patient age (adolescent vs adult), anchorage method (Class II elastics, miniscrews, none), space-closing staging (sequential vs en-masse), and attachment design (vertical vs optimized vs double-rectangular vs horizontal/none) all significantly modulated the size and direction of these discrepancies — sometimes in a trade-off pattern (e.g., miniscrews protect crown movement but increase U1 root labial tipping; sequential retraction protects U1 torque but costs posterior anchorage).

## Key Contributions

- First comprehensive CBCT-based **root** movement quantification (not intraoral-scan/CBCT registration) in premolar-extraction CAT, avoiding segmentation-accuracy confounds of prior root-inference methods.
- Separates crown and root discrepancy in three axes (APD/LD/OGD) across six maxillary tooth types (U1, U2, U3, U5, U6, U7) — turns a previously qualitative "torque loss" narrative into tooth-specific, axis-specific numbers clinicians can use for ClinCheck overcorrection.
- Overturns the prior crown-only assumption that canines (U3) lingually incline during extraction-space closure: root-inclusive data show U3 actually moves **buccally** as a whole, with the crown outpacing the root.
- Identifies specific anchorage/staging/attachment trade-offs (miniscrew crown protection vs U1 root labial tipping; sequential-retraction torque protection vs posterior anchorage cost) — directly actionable for treatment-plan design.
- Provides quantitative support for "bite-block effect" skepticism: only U5 (not U6) intruded significantly, consistent with prior work suggesting typical CA thickness is insufficient to trigger a true bite-block effect.

## Methodology

Retrospective single-center cohort, Hospital of Stomatology, Fourth Military Medical University, Jan 2020–May 2024. 47 patients (mean age 22.2±8.0 y), all completed first CAT series (mean duration 40.3±12.7 months, 7-day aligner change, ≥22 h/day wear). Inclusion: 4 first/second premolar extractions, no other maxillary tooth loss (except third molars), skeletal Class I/II, no fixed/auxiliary appliances, no orthognathic surgery, high-quality pre/post CBCT. 542 teeth analyzed (U1 n=94, U2 n=94, U3 n=94, U5 n=81, U6 n=94, U7 n=85; U4 excluded, n=13 too small). 3 patients had skeletal anchorage (miniscrews between U5–U6); 28 patients used some auxiliary anchorage device.

Pre/post CBCT (KaKo Kerr; voxel 0.15 mm, 768×768 resolution) superimposed in Dolphin Imaging v11.95 using a coordinate system referenced to the maxillary functional occlusal plane, midpalatal suture/nasal septum, and U7 distal point, registered via anterior-cranial-base anatomical landmarks (frontomaxillary sutures, infraorbital foramina) then fine-aligned within a framed stable region. Crown reference points (incisal edge midpoint, cusp tip, buccal cusp, mesiobuccal cusp) and corresponding root-apex points were tracked; APD/LD/OGD computed from axis-coordinate differences. Sample size determined via G*Power from the smallest prior effect size (0.37, U1 OGD) → minimum n=60 achieved (81–94 actual). Paired t-test/Wilcoxon for designed-vs-achieved comparison; linear mixed-effects model (patient as random effect) for factor analysis (designed movement, age, extraction site, anchorage, staging, attachment type).

## Results

| Metric | Finding |
|---|---|
| U1 root APD discrepancy | Maximum in study: 5.49±2.30 mm short of design |
| U3 (canine) crown APD | Only 71.5% of designed distal movement achieved |
| U2 root LD discrepancy | Greatest of all measured (p<.001); U2 shows distal tipping |
| U3 overall LD | Buccal displacement (not lingual as prior crown-only studies assumed); crown > root |
| Anterior OGD | Extrusion despite designed intrusion (esp. U1) |
| U6 OGD | No significant discrepancy (p=.28) — argues against clinically meaningful bite-block effect |
| U5 OGD | Over-intrusion |
| U7 OGD | Over-extrusion |
| Posterior crown/root APD | Exceeded designed mesial movement (mesial tipping / anchorage loss) except U7 mesiobuccal root |

**Significant influencing factors** (linear mixed-effects model):
- **Age**: adolescents showed greater U1 crown APD/OGD discrepancy; for U6 root LD, direction differed by age (adults lingual, adolescents buccal, p<.001) — attributed to adolescent maxillary growth.
- **Extraction site**: U5 (second premolar) extraction associated with greater U1 root APD, U6 APD, U3 crown APD/OGD, U3 root LD discrepancies than U4 extraction — overall greater anchorage-loss risk.
- **Anchorage**: Class II elastics and miniscrews both reduced U1/U3 crown APD discrepancy (protective) but miniscrews increased U1 root APD discrepancy (uncontrolled labial root tipping), likely via altered force vector to U1.
- **Staging**: sequential retraction increased U1 crown APD discrepancy but preserved root position (better torque control); en-masse retraction reduced U6 crown LD discrepancy (better posterior anchorage) — a torque-vs-anchorage trade-off.
- **Attachments**: U3 vertical attachments worsened U1/U3 root APD discrepancy vs optimized attachments (more uncontrolled tipping); U6 double-rectangular attachments reduced U6 root APD discrepancy vs optimized; U6 no-attachment/horizontal-attachment groups had greater U6 crown LD discrepancy (higher anchorage-loss risk).

**Authors' conclusions**: root control (especially incisor and canine) is the weak point of premolar-extraction CAT and needs systematic overcorrection in ClinCheck design; U5 extraction carries higher anchorage-loss/tipping risk than U4 extraction; miniscrews and Class II elastics protect molar anchorage but miniscrews can cause U1 root labial tipping; sequential retraction reduces U1 labial inclination but at some anchorage cost; U3 vertical attachments should be avoided in favor of optimized attachments to protect root control.
