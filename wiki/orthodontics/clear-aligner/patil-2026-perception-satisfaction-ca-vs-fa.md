## Three-line Summary

Prospective 12-month cohort (n=120, 4 timepoints) comparing CA vs FA using PIDAQ, TSQ, VAS, OHIP-14, and objective oral health measures. Clear aligners showed superior aesthetic satisfaction (78.3% vs 41.7% "excellent" at 12mo), comfort, and psychosocial impact throughout treatment, but OHIP-14 converged by 12 months (p=0.156). White spot lesions were significantly less common with CA (11.7% vs 31.7%, p=0.008).

## Background

Comparative patient-reported outcome data at multiple timepoints for clear aligners vs fixed appliances in adult non-extraction orthodontic treatment lacked prospective longitudinal evidence with comprehensive multi-instrument assessment prior to this study.

## Methods

Prospective parallel-group cohort; self-selected treatment with matching (age, sex, PAR, aesthetic concern). Adults 18–45y, Class I/II non-extraction, good periodontal health. Invisalign (weekly change, 22h/day, 6-week recall) vs 0.022" MBT edgewise (4-week recall). Assessments at T0, T1 (3mo), T2 (6mo), T3 (12mo). Blind clinical examiner. SPSS 26; repeated-measures ANOVA with Bonferroni correction.

## Clinical Takeaway

For adult non-extraction patients where aesthetics and comfort are primary drivers, clear aligners offer demonstrably superior patient experience throughout treatment. The convergence of OHIP-14 by 12 months means functional quality of life equalizes at endpoint. The white spot lesion reduction (11.7% vs 31.7%) is a strong argument for CA in patients at caries risk. Compliance appears higher in this motivated university sample — real-world compliance monitoring remains essential.
