## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024546354, PRISMA 2020; 7 studies qualitative synthesis / 4 quantitative meta-analysis; n=526 patients, ages 11–67, predominantly retrospective cohorts + 1 RCT) comparing optimized vs conventional clear aligner attachments for accuracy/efficacy of tooth movement.

No significant mean-difference in accuracy between optimized and conventional attachments for canine derotation (SMD −0.31, p=0.06, I²=0%) or anterior teeth extrusion (SMD 0.14, p=0.60, I²=76%); pooled accuracy was 61.2% (conventional) vs 71.5% (optimized) for canine derotation and 57.5% (conventional) vs 62.4% (optimized) for anterior extrusion — neither attachment type achieved the ClinCheck-predicted movement.

Optimized attachments show no proven accuracy advantage over conventional attachments for the movements studied, though they may shorten treatment time and provide root control via 3D bevel design; evidence is low-to-moderate certainty (mostly retrospective cohorts, high heterogeneity for extrusion), so attachment-type selection should not be based on accuracy alone.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1016/j.jobcr.2025.07.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jobcr.2025.07.019)), this PROSPERO-registered SR+MA (CRD42024546354, PRISMA 2020) is the first systematic review to directly compare Align Technology's third-generation **optimized (SmartForce) attachments** against **conventional (rectangular/ellipsoidal) attachments** for the accuracy of tooth movement across multiple movement types. From 185 initial records, 7 studies (526 patients, ages 11–67) entered qualitative synthesis and 4 were pooled quantitatively.

For **canine derotation**, pooled accuracy was 61.2% (conventional) vs 71.5% (optimized), with no significant mean difference (SMD −0.31, p=0.06) and no heterogeneity (I²=0%); certainty of evidence was rated **low**. For **anterior teeth extrusion**, pooled accuracy was 57.5% (conventional) vs 62.4% (optimized), again with no significant mean difference (SMD 0.14, p=0.60), but **high heterogeneity** (I²=76%) and a detected publication-bias signal (driven by one outlier study); certainty was rated **high** despite the heterogeneity. Root angulation, incisor intrusion, and mesiodistal angulation/vestibulolingual inclination were each assessed by a single study and showed no clinically significant differences by attachment type. Across every movement studied, **neither attachment type reached the ClinCheck-predicted amount of movement**.

Clinically: this SR does not support choosing optimized over conventional attachments (or vice versa) purely on accuracy grounds for canine derotation or anterior extrusion — the difference is not statistically significant either way. Optimized attachments may still offer secondary advantages (shorter treatment time, 3D-bevel root control), and one included study found conventional attachments more accurate for lateral incisor extrusion specifically (larger size, sharper edges). The evidence base is dominated by retrospective cohorts (6/7 studies) with only one RCT, so the authors call for CONSORT-guided RCTs to settle the question.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA to directly quantify **optimized vs conventional attachment accuracy** head-to-head, rather than attachments-vs-no-attachment.
- Pooled accuracy percentages by attachment type for canine derotation (61.2% vs 71.5%) and anterior extrusion (57.5% vs 62.4%) using the standard Kravitz formula.
- Confirms that **neither attachment type achieves ClinCheck-predicted movement** — reframing the clinical question from "which attachment is more accurate" to "aligners under-express regardless of attachment type."
- Surfaces a movement-specific asymmetry: optimized attachments trend better for rotation control (esp. upper lateral incisor derotation via 3D bevel/root control), while conventional attachments trended better in one extrusion study (larger size, sharper edges).

## Methodology

PRISMA 2020 SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42024546354). Databases: Cochrane Library, PubMed, Web of Science, SCOPUS, Google Scholar, trial registries, plus manual/hand search; studies to January 2025, no language filter. RAYYAN-assisted deduplication; two independent reviewers for screening/extraction (κ=0.88–0.89), third-reviewer arbitration. 185 records → 141 duplicates removed → 44 screened → 31 excluded → 13 full-text reviewed → 7 studies qualitative synthesis, 4 quantitative meta-analysis. Risk of bias: Cochrane RoB-2 (1 RCT, some concerns) and JBI cohort tool (6 retrospective studies, low risk), κ=0.86. Accuracy = Kravitz formula, 100 − [(|predicted−achieved|)/|predicted|] × 100; random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird) pooling; I² heterogeneity; funnel plot for publication bias; GRADE for certainty. Population: 526 patients (177M/349F), ages 11–67; 3 studies each for extrusion and rotation, 1 each for intrusion and root angulation. Confidence `sr+ma`; PMC OA full text.

## Results

| Movement | Studies pooled | SMD | p-value | I² | GRADE certainty | Accuracy: conventional | Accuracy: optimized |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canine derotation | 2 | −0.31 | 0.06 (NS) | 0% | Low | 61.2% | 71.5% |
| Anterior extrusion | 3 | 0.14 | 0.60 (NS) | 76% (high) | High | 57.5% | 62.4% |

- No significant difference in accuracy between optimized and conventional attachments for canine derotation, anterior extrusion, or root angulation changes (all p>0.05).
- Publication bias: none detected for canine rotation; significant publication bias for anterior extrusion (one outlier study, Groody et al.).
- Incisor intrusion (1 study): no significant difference; only 33–40% overall accuracy achievable regardless of attachment. Root angulation (1 study, extraction cases): no significant difference after regression adjustment for wear protocol, age, sex, aligner number, attachment presence.
- Historical comparators cited: canine derotation accuracy 55–88.8% across prior Invisalign literature (any attachment) vs Kravitz 2008's 35.8%; extrusion overall efficacy 54.6–76% regardless of attachment, with extrusion historically the least-accurate Invisalign movement (Kravitz: 29.6%).
- Bottom line (authors' conclusions): (1) no significant accuracy differences for most movements, neither type reaches 100% of predicted movement; (2) optimized attachments show better upper lateral incisor rotation control, conventional may enhance anterior extrusion (size/edge sharpness); (3) root/MD-angulation and VL-inclination differences are not clinically significant; (4) optimized attachments are biomechanically designed for better force application but superiority remains inconclusive pending higher-quality RCTs.
