## One-line Summary

Systematic review (16 studies, 461 patients, search to April 2026) of clear aligner maxillary molar distalization: predictability 31.1–88.4%, very low GRADE certainty, no meta-analysis possible due to substantial heterogeneity.

## Summary

This PRISMA-compliant systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42024573061) synthesized evidence on the effectiveness and predictability of maxillary molar distalization using clear aligner systems. Of 1,388 initially identified records, 16 studies met inclusion criteria — 11 retrospective cohort and 5 prospective experimental designs — comprising 461 patients with a mean age below 40 years. Most studies used Invisalign; two used Anglealign and Ordoline. Predictability, defined as the ratio of achieved to planned distalization, ranged from 31.1% to 88.4%, with an average achieved movement of approximately 2 mm. Substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis. GRADE assessment rated the certainty of evidence as very low for both primary outcomes (achieved distalization in mm and predictability in %). Key factors associated with lower predictability included greater prescribed movement magnitude and inadequate anchorage. Class II elastics, vertical/rectangular buccal attachments on molars, and miniscrews (TADs) were used as adjuncts and showed variable anchorage-control benefits. Several studies associated longer aligner-change intervals (14 days) with improved predictability, though evidence is insufficient for a definitive recommendation.

## Key Contributions

- Broadest SR to date on clear aligner maxillary molar distalization (16 studies, 461 patients; bhate-2025 covered 11 studies, 278 patients)
- Formally separates *effectiveness* (mm achieved) from *predictability* (achieved/planned %) — two distinct outcomes that prior literature conflated
- Identifies timing of outcome assessment (end-of-distalization-phase vs. end-of-total-treatment) as a major confound explaining inter-study variation
- First SR in this subfield to apply GRADE — both primary outcomes rated very low certainty
- Establishes inverse relationship between prescribed distalization magnitude and predictability as a consistent finding across included studies

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review, PRISMA-compliant, PROSPERO registered (CRD42024573061)
- **Databases**: PubMed-Medline, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane; search up to April 2026, no language/year restriction
- **Inclusion**: RCTs, cohort, case-control, cross-sectional, multicenter studies; patients undergoing clear aligner therapy requiring maxillary molar distalization; comparisons of planned vs. achieved movement and across treatment-related factors
- **Exclusion**: Systematic/narrative reviews, case series/reports, in vitro studies
- **n included**: 16 studies (11 retrospective, 5 prospective); 461 patients (117 M, 295 F, 25 NR); mean age 21.6–36 years
- **Aligner systems**: Predominantly Invisalign; two studies used Anglealign, Ordoline
- **Outcome measurement**: Digital models (intraoral scans/plaster), cephalometric/CBCT, or combined; superimposition via craniofacial reference planes, palatal rugae, best-fit alignment, or mathematical models
- **Risk of bias**: NOS for retrospective studies (most 7–8/9); ROBINS-I for prospective studies (all low/moderate overall; confounding moderate in all 5 prospective studies)
- **Evidence certainty**: GRADE very low for both primary outcomes (risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision)
- **Meta-analysis**: Not performed — heterogeneity in outcome definitions, reporting units, measurement timing, and statistical completeness precluded pooling
- **Kappa**: 0.88

## Results

### Primary Outcomes

| Outcome | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Predictability (%) | 31.1 – 88.4 | 4 studies ≤40%; Simon et al. up to 88.4% |
| Achieved distalization, M6 (mm) | 0.84 – 4.86 | Average ~2 mm |
| Achieved distalization, M7 (mm) | 0.86 – 2.98 | Average ~2 mm |

### Aligner Change Interval

- 14-day interval associated with higher predictability in several studies: Castroflorio — 12% reduction in lack-of-correction vs 7-day; Al-Nadawi — significantly enhanced precision; Linjawi — less misalignment at 15 days vs 3/7/10-day groups
- Only one study directly compared 7/10/14-day intervals specifically for maxillary molar distalization — **evidence insufficient for definitive 14-day recommendation**

### Auxiliaries and Anchorage

- **Class II elastics**: Commonly used (9 studies); may limit anterior proclination and maintain aligner-tooth contact; Liu et al. — elastics vs microscrews showed no significant difference (both groups: efficiency 36.2–43.9%)
- **Vertical/rectangular buccal attachments**: Associated with reduced distal tipping; multiple studies support placement on distalized molars to counteract tipping moment
- **Miniscrews/TADs**: Provide stronger skeletal anchorage; some studies prefer over elastics for large movements, though head-to-head comparisons are limited
- No single auxiliary strategy proven superior across protocols

### Distalization Protocol

- V-shaped sequential, 50% sequential, two-thirds sequential — all used; no consensus on which protocol yields the most predictable outcomes

### First vs Second Molar

- 7 studies: no significant difference in predictability between M6 and M7
- 4 studies: slightly greater predictability for M7
- 5 studies: did not address comparison

### GRADE Certainty

Very low for both achieved distalization (mm) and predictability (%), due to non-randomized designs, moderate confounding, substantial inconsistency, heterogeneous aligner systems/staging/outcome timing, small samples, and incomplete statistical reporting.
