## Three-line Summary
Retrospective single-arm study of 12 adult patients (mean age 31.8y, range 24-46), 19 U6 and 19 U7 teeth sequentially distalized with Invisalign, tracked via iTero digital models at T0/T1/T2 using palatal-area registration/superimposition.
From T0 to T2, U6 moved distally 1.71 ± 0.89 mm and U7 2.04 ± 1.16 mm, with distal tipping of U6 -5.20 ± 2.62° and U7 -5.35 ± 4.19°, molar intrusion, U6 distopalatal rotation +2.83 ± 2.45°, and anterior anchorage loss (U1 sagittal change -0.88 ± 0.99 mm, torque +3.58 ± 4.69°) despite intermaxillary Class II elastics.
Invisalign achieves maxillary molar distalization primarily through tipping rather than bodily movement, with decreased angulation control and anterior anchorage loss, indicating a need for additional auxiliaries (e.g., TADs) beyond elastics.

## Summary

This retrospective, single-arm study of 12 adult patients (mean age 31.8y, range 24-46) tracked sequential Invisalign-driven maxillary molar distalization (U7 first, U6 second) using iTero-scanned 3D digital models superimposed on the palatal area at three timepoints: T0 (before), T1 (after U7 distalization), and T2 (after U6 distalization). Across 19 U6 and 19 U7 teeth, distalization from T0 to T2 was 1.71 ± 0.89 mm (U6) and 2.04 ± 1.16 mm (U7), accompanied by significant distal tipping (-5.20 ± 2.62° and -5.35 ± 4.19° respectively), molar intrusion, U6 distopalatal rotation, and anterior anchorage loss on the maxillary central incisor (U1 labial/sagittal movement and increased torque) despite continuous intermaxillary Class II elastic traction. The authors conclude that Invisalign achieves maxillary molar distalization primarily through a tipping mechanism rather than bodily movement, and that this is accompanied by decreased angulation control and anterior anchorage loss — meaning additional auxiliaries (e.g., temporary anchorage devices) beyond intermaxillary elastics are likely needed to protect anchorage during aligner-driven distalization.

## Key Contributions

- Uses iTero + palatal-area registration (a stable, reproducible landmark region) for serial 3D superimposition across three discrete stages, rather than 2D cephalometrics or unstandardized registration.
- Quantifies distal tipping, distopalatal rotation, and vertical (intrusion) change of both U6 and U7 alongside sagittal/torque change of U1 — a fuller multidimensional picture than most single-metric prior studies.
- Documents a reciprocal small mesial rebound of U7 (-0.21 ± 0.78 mm, NS) between T1 and T2, attributed to progressive loss of posterior anchor teeth as U6 begins moving — a mechanistic explanation for anchorage erosion during sequential distalization.
- Reports a significant Spearman correlation between the amount of U6 distalization and the absolute change in U6 angulation (r=0.723, P<0.05), and between initial U7 angulation and its angulation change (r=0.579, P<0.05).

## Methodology

Retrospective, single-arm (no control group), serial 3D digital-model study. 12 adult patients treated July 2019-July 2022 (skeletal Class I/II, mild upper-arch crowding ≤4 mm, Class I or end-to-end Class II molar relationship, ≥20h/day compliance) contributed 19 U6, 19 U7, and 19 U1 teeth. Sequential distalization protocol: U7 moved halfway before U6 began; U6 distalization design ≤3 mm; intermaxillary Class II elastics (4.5 oz) used throughout; vertical rectangular attachments on maxillary molars; mean 27 aligners/patient (16-40 range). iTero scans at T0/T1/T2 analyzed in RapidForm 2006 via a 12-point crown landmark scheme and occlusal/sagittal/coronal reference planes; intra-/inter-rater ICC 0.89-0.94. Paired t-tests (T0-T1, T1-T2, T0-T2) and Spearman correlation, significance p<0.05. Power analysis (referencing a prior similar study) indicated ≥9/10 molars needed for 95% power; the 19/19 sample meets this.

## Results

|---|---|---|---|
| U6 distalization (mm) | 0.91 ± 0.80 (P<0.05) | 0.80 ± 0.49 (P<0.05) | 1.71 ± 0.89 (P<0.05) |

|---|---|---|
