## One-line Summary

SR+MA of 5 non-randomized controlled trials (n=166) found no significant differences in any cephalometric outcome (SNA, SNB, ANB, Wits, overjet, incisor inclination, mandibular length) between clear aligners with mandibular advancement and traditional functional appliances for skeletal Class II malocclusion.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated whether clear aligners with mandibular advancement (CA-MA; Invisalign) produce different cephalometric outcomes compared to traditional functional appliances (FA; Twin Block, Herbst, van Beek) in adolescents with skeletal Class II malocclusion. After screening 1,578 articles, five non-randomized controlled trials were included (n=166; 77 CA-MA, 89 FA). A random-effects meta-analysis found no statistically significant differences across all nine cephalometric domains examined, including primary skeletal angles (SNA, SNB, ANB), Wits appraisal, mandibular plane angle, overjet, incisor inclinations, and mandibular length. High heterogeneity (I²=70–86%) characterized most outcomes, and treatment duration confounding — arising from the two-phase FA approach versus single-phase CA treatment — was identified as a major methodological limitation. The authors concluded that CA-MA may serve as a viable alternative to traditional FA for skeletal Class II correction, particularly for patients prioritizing aesthetics and comfort, while acknowledging that high-quality RCTs are needed to confirm these findings.

## Key Contributions

- Confirms equivalence across the most comprehensive cephalometric panel of any CA-MA vs FA SR+MA: 9 domains including Wits appraisal and mandibular plane angle alongside the standard SNA/SNB/ANB
- Restricts inclusion to post-2017 studies, ensuring only modern Invisalign Mandibular Advancement System (introduced 2018) technology is represented — unlike older reviews that may include early-generation CA
- Includes van Beek appliance as comparator alongside Twin Block and Herbst, broadening the FA reference pool
- Funnel plot asymmetry for ANB is not confirmed by Egger's test (p=0.39); trim-and-fill correction negligible — equivalence conclusion is robust to publication bias concern
- Highlights treatment duration as an unresolved confound: FA studies use growth observation periods not present in CA studies, making mandibular length changes difficult to attribute purely to appliance type

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis; PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD42024622755
- **Databases**: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science (English/Korean)
- **Eligibility**: Skeletal Class II; CA-MA (Invisalign) vs FA (Twin Block/Herbst/van Beek); non-randomized prospective and retrospective cohort studies; publication year ≥2017
- **Excluded**: RCTs, case reports, reviews, pre-2017 studies, non-comparative designs
- **Included studies**: n=5 non-randomized controlled trials; 166 patients (89 FA, 77 CA-MA)
- **Risk of bias**: ROBINS-I; 1 low, 4 moderate
- **Statistics**: Random-effects model; SMD (95% CI); I², τ²; Egger's test; trim-and-fill (R 4.3.3)

## Results

| Outcome | SMD (95% CI) | p | I² |
|---|---|---|---|
| SNA | 0.54 (−0.28, 1.36) | 0.194 | 80% |
| SNB | 0.29 (−0.34, 0.93) | 0.361 | 70% |
| ANB | 0.33 (−0.43, 1.09) | 0.397 | 72% |
| Overjet | 0.17 (−0.61, 0.96) | 0.666 | 78% |
| Wits appraisal | −0.03 (−0.92, 0.86) | 0.947 | 78% |
| Mandibular plane angle | −0.59 (−1.46, 0.29) | 0.191 | 76% |
| Mandibular incisor inclination | 0.09 (−0.89, 1.07) | 0.852 | 86% |
| Maxillary incisor inclination | 0.56 (−0.53, 1.66) | 0.314 | 82% |
| Mandibular length | 0.01 (−0.36, 0.37) | 0.972 | 21% |

All CIs include zero — no statistically significant differences. Mandibular length showed the lowest heterogeneity (I²=21%), suggesting this is the most reliably estimated null result. The slight negative SMD for mandibular plane angle (favoring CA with better vertical control) aligns with mechanistic expectations but does not reach significance.
