## Summary

This umbrella review applied JBI methodology and the PRISMA for Overviews checklist to synthesise SR-level evidence on clear aligner (CA) clinical outcomes in growing children. From 1,609 records, 6 systematic reviews were included. AMSTAR-2 quality appraisal rated 5 as critically low and 1 as low, with a high degree of corrected covered area indicating substantial primary-study overlap. All six reviews consistently reported dentoalveolar improvements — tooth alignment, overjet reduction, and arch development — supporting CA as a predictable tool for dentoalveolar correction in this age group. Skeletal evidence was discordant: 2 SRs reported possible mandibular changes, 3 reported minimal or no skeletal effects, and 1 found insufficient evidence. Meta-analyses for sagittal skeletal measures (ANB, SNA, SNB) showed low heterogeneity, while dental outcomes (overjet, overbite) showed variable heterogeneity. Periodontal outcomes and patient-reported outcomes were each addressed by only 2 SRs, precluding conclusions. The authors concluded that the current evidence base supports dentoalveolar correction but is insufficient to confirm meaningful skeletal modification, and that overall methodological quality is very low.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the first JBI-methodology umbrella review of CA outcomes specifically in growing/mixed-dentition children
- Confirms dentoalveolar correction (alignment, overjet, arch development) as the consistent, reproducible CA effect across all 6 SRs
- Resolves skeletal uncertainty at the SR level: majority (3/6) report minimal/no effect; minority (2/6) note possible mandibular adaptation — insufficient evidence for clinical skeletal claims
- Identifies periodontal and PRO domains as critically underreported; calls for standardised reporting in future primary CA/growing-patient trials
- Demonstrates substantial evidence redundancy via high CCA, cautioning against interpreting the number of SRs as independent replication

## Methodology

- **Design**: Umbrella review; JBI umbrella review protocol; PRISMA for Overviews
- **Search scope**: 1,609 records → 6 SRs included
- **PROSPERO**: CRD420251054252
- **Quality appraisal**: AMSTAR-2 — 5 critically low, 1 low; high CCA reported
- **Outcome domains**: dentoalveolar, skeletal, periodontal, patient-reported
- **Statistical analyses**: meta-analyses for ANB, SNA, SNB, overjet, overbite; low heterogeneity for sagittal skeletal, variable for dental outcomes
- **Population**: growing children (mixed/transitional dentition) receiving CA therapy
- **Journal**: J Indian Soc Pedod Prev Dent 2026;44(4):359–373

## Results

- **Dentoalveolar**: all 6 SRs positive — tooth alignment, overjet reduction, arch development. This is the most robust and consistent finding across all included reviews.
- **Skeletal**:
  - 2/6 SRs: possible mandibular changes (type unspecified in abstract)
  - 3/6 SRs: minimal or no skeletal effects
  - 1/6 SR: insufficient evidence
  - Meta-analytic heterogeneity for ANB, SNA, SNB: low (favourable pooling signal, but with very low underlying quality)
- **Dental MA**: overjet and overbite — variable heterogeneity
- **Periodontal**: 2 SRs reported; insufficient to draw conclusions
- **PROs**: 2 SRs reported; insufficient to draw conclusions
- **Overall AMSTAR-2**: 5/6 critically low; 1/6 low — no included SR rated moderate or high

## Three-line Summary

- Umbrella review (JBI methodology, PRISMA for Overviews, PROSPERO CRD420251054252) synthesizing 6 systematic reviews from 1,609 records on clinical outcomes of clear aligners in growing children with mixed/transitional dentition.
- All 6 SRs consistently reported dentoalveolar improvements (tooth alignment, overjet reduction, arch development); skeletal outcomes were discordant — 2 SRs noted possible mandibular changes, 3 reported minimal or no skeletal effects, 1 found insufficient evidence; periodontal and patient-reported outcomes (PROs) insufficient; AMSTAR-2 rated 5/6 SRs critically low.
- Clear aligners predictably correct dentoalveolar problems in growing patients but lack evidence for meaningful skeletal modification; the current evidence base rests on very low methodological quality across all included reviews.
