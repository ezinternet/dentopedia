## One-line Summary

Systematic review (95 studies, PROSPERO CRD420251110248) of clear aligner material ageing across chemical, physical, mechanical, and morphological domains: pooled coffee ΔE = 70.9, tea ΔE = 18.4, red wine ΔE = 11.5; force decay 40–90% within 48 h; TPU-based and directly printed aligners consistently more susceptible than PETG.

## Summary

Almotairy et al. (2026) conducted the first systematic review integrating all four property domains of clear aligner material ageing — chemical, physical, mechanical, and morphological. Searching five databases to March 2026 and applying PRISMA 2020, 95 studies were included after excluding single-brand intraoral-only reviews and studies on non-aged aligners. Risk of bias was low in 8 studies, moderate in 62, and high in 25. Chemical composition was largely stable; trace elemental release was brand-dependent and requires biocompatibility awareness. Discolouration was the most consistently demonstrated physical change, with pooled weighted mean ΔE values of 70.9 for coffee, 18.4 for tea, and 11.5 for red wine (7-day, 37 °C protocols), with Invisalign (polyurethane) exceeding the clinically perceptible threshold of ΔE ≥ 3.3 across studies. Force decay of 40–90% within 48 h was a consistent mechanical finding across materials, directly informing replacement intervals. TPU-based and directly printed aligners (DPAs) were generally more susceptible than PETG-based aligners for staining, energy absorption loss, and surface damage under cyclic loading; however, findings on hardness, roughness, and stiffness were inconsistent. The review concludes that material-specific clinical protocols, reinforced dietary and cleaning instructions, and force-decay-informed replacement intervals are warranted.

## Key Contributions

- **First multi-domain comprehensive SR of CA ageing**: 95 studies covering chemical, physical, mechanical, and morphological properties across multiple brands and material types — fills the gap left by prior single-brand (Bakdach) or partial-domain (Srinivasan) reviews
- **Quantified pooled discolouration**: Coffee ΔE = 70.9 >> tea 18.4 > red wine 11.5 (pooled weighted mean from 7 comparable studies); Invisalign/TPU consistently exceeds clinically perceptible threshold, PETG-based brands substantially more colour-stable
- **Force decay benchmark**: 40–90% of initial force lost within 48 h — material-dependent; informs aligner replacement interval decisions
- **Material vulnerability hierarchy**: TPU and DPAs > PETG for staining susceptibility, cyclic loading energy absorption loss, and surface damage; extends polymer-taxonomy rationale in Delgado 2025
- **Trace elemental / microplastic release**: All tested brands release microplastics under cyclic loading; some brands show trace elemental release (mercury in Eon); clinical significance unestablished but warrants awareness

## Methodology

**Design**: Systematic review; PRISMA 2020; PROSPERO CRD420251110248; no formal meta-analysis (substantial methodological heterogeneity); pooled weighted mean ΔE calculated from 7 comparable studies using Σ(n × ΔE) / Σn.

**Search**: PubMed, Web of Science, MEDLINE (Ovid), ProQuest, Scopus — inception to 18 March 2026; no language, date, or publication-type restrictions; backward and forward citation chaining.

**Inclusion**: In vivo studies of CA properties after intraoral use; in vitro studies of CA properties after thermocycling, cyclic loading, or liquid immersion. Outcomes: chemical (composition), physical (optical, water sorption, thermal, dimensional), mechanical (elastic modulus, strength, hardness, stiffness, force decay), morphological (roughness, cracks, scratches, porosities).

**Exclusion**: Cytotoxicity, adverse reactions, microbial accumulation; case reports; review articles.

**Study selection**: 2,471 records → 1,075 after deduplication → 940 excluded (title/abstract) → 40 excluded (full-text) → 95 included.

**Risk of bias**: QUIN (in vitro, purely), JBI (cohort in vivo), Cochrane RoB 2 (RCTs). Results: low n=8, moderate n=62, high n=25. Inter-reviewer kappa ≥ 0.81.

## Results

### Chemical Properties
- CA chemical composition largely stable across most studies; ester bond hydrolysis (PETG → carboxylic acid + alcohol) confirmed by ATR-FTIR after intraoral use
- Trace elemental release brand-dependent: mercury in Eon (EDX 17.31%; Alhendi 2022); highest nitrogen in Invisalign (46.36%)
- Hot solutions (55 °C) increased FTIR peak intensity suggesting possible hydrolysis; cold and control solutions remained within dimensional limits (<0.25 mm RMS)

### Physical Properties — Colour / Discolouration
| Beverage | Pooled weighted mean ΔE | Clinically perceptible (>3.3)? |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee | **70.9** | Yes — all PU/TPU brands |
| Tea | **18.4** | Yes — PU/TPU; borderline PETG |
| Red wine | **11.5** | Yes — PU/TPU; less severe PETG |
- Pooled from 7 studies using comparable 7-day, 37 °C immersion protocols
- Invisalign (SmartTrack TPU) consistently exceeds ΔE ≥ 3.3 threshold across studies
- PETG-based brands (Beforedent, Käse Aligner, MTM) substantially more colour-stable
- Cleaning reduces but does not eliminate staining; whitening toothpaste post-intraoral wear → ΔE 3.21 (lowest) vs Invisalign Crystals → ΔE 7.15 (one RCT)

### Mechanical Properties — Force Decay and Stress Relaxation
- **Force decay: 40–90% within 48 h** — consistent across materials; rate varies by polymer type
- Rapid initial decay phase (0–8 h) followed by plateau (consistent with viscoelastic stress relaxation)
- Stress retention at 15 days (extrapolated): F22 Evoflex (TPU) highest (39.2%); Duran (PETG) 3.9%; all showed rapid early decay
- Cyclic loading energy absorption: PETG (Essix Ace) stable ~4 Nmm; TPU (Ghost Aligner) decreased 7→2.6 Nmm; DPA (AM-PU) gradual moderate decrease
- PETG: minor surface damage under cyclic loading; TPU (Ghost): severe localized cracks (>3.5 mm after 16,750 cycles); DPA: minimal acoustic emission signals
- Microplastic release: all three brands tested released MPs; PETG >30 μm fragments; TPU/DPA <30 μm, smaller axial dimensions

### Mechanical Properties — Hardness, Stiffness, Modulus
- Findings inconsistent: both increases and decreases in hardness reported post-ageing across studies
- Hot beverage immersion had greatest impact on hardness vs cold/control
- No significant hardness differences after 2-week artificial saliva immersion for Invisalign, Eon, SureSmile, Clarity (Alhendi 2022)
- Erkodur (PETG) > Duran (PETG) in hardness; Duran > Erkodur in elastic modulus; post-thermocycling significance material-specific

### Morphological Properties
- Surface roughness increases after ageing in most studies (magnitude and significance inconsistent)
- Dimensional stability: hot solutions cause greatest change (single-layer > multilayer); cold and control within clinical limits
- Multilayer aligners generally more dimensionally stable than single-layer under hot-solution challenge
