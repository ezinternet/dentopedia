## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (PRISMA/PROSPERO, 10 studies — 4 non-RCTs + 6 before-after) comparing clear aligner treatment (CAT) vs fixed appliances with temporary anchorage devices (FATADs) for anterior open bite correction.

CAT increases overbite +2.77 mm mainly via incisor extrusion (U1 +0.87 mm, L1 +1.06 mm) with no significant molar intrusion, while FATADs gain +1.64 mm more overbite than CAT via greater molar intrusion (U6 +1.88 mm, L6 +0.45 mm).

FATADs are more efficacious for open bite overall; CAT-corrected open bites are stable during retention, but evidence certainty is low to very low with no RCTs available.

## Summary

This is the first systematic review + meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024588198) to dissect the detailed mechanism by which clear aligner treatment (CAT) closes an anterior open bite, and the first to formally compare CAT against fixed appliances with temporary anchorage devices (FATADs) on the vertical positions of incisors and molars, not just overbite as a single endpoint.

Pooling 10 studies (6 before-and-after single-arm studies, 4 non-RCTs, 3 of which directly compared CAT vs. FATADs), the authors found that CAT corrects open bite predominantly through **anterior incisor extrusion** (both U1 and L1 extrude significantly) with **no significant molar intrusion** at either arch. In contrast, FATADs achieve open-bite correction primarily through **molar intrusion** (significant U6 and L6 intrusion) while limiting incisor extrusion — and this mechanism produces significantly **greater total overbite gain** than CAT. Where retention data existed, CAT-corrected open bites were stable over 6 months to >1 year, with no significant relapse in overbite or any of the four measured tooth positions.

Certainty of evidence was rated low (before/after CAT comparisons) to very low (stability comparisons; CAT vs. FATADs comparisons), reflecting the retrospective, heterogeneous, and small-sample nature of the underlying literature — no RCTs were available for inclusion.

## Key Contributions

- First SR+MA to quantify the relative contribution of incisor extrusion vs. molar intrusion in CAT-corrected anterior open bite.
- First direct meta-analytic comparison of CAT vs. FATADs protocols on overbite and vertical tooth position (U1, L1, U6, L6).
- First pooled stability analysis of CAT open-bite correction during the retention period.
- Uses weighted mean difference (millimeters) rather than standardized mean difference for direct clinical interpretability.

## Methodology

- PRISMA/Cochrane Handbook SR+MA; PROSPERO CRD42024588198.
- Search: Medline, Scopus, Cochrane Central, Embase, VHL (to 2025-04-17) + Google Scholar grey literature + manual reference screening; Covidence for screening/extraction.
- Risk of bias: ROBINS-I (non-RCTs), NIH tool (before-and-after single-arm studies).
- Outcomes: overbite, and vertical position of U1, L1, U6, L6 from cephalograms/CBCT at pre-treatment, post-treatment, and retention (≥6 months).
- Synthesis: RevMan 5.4, weighted mean difference, random-effects model when heterogeneous (I² thresholds 25/50/75%).
- Sensitivity analyses for possible duplicate-participant studies (Suh et al.) and a manually-traced study without stated magnification calibration (Harris et al.) — results robust to both.
- GRADE certainty assessment; Egger's test/funnel plots for publication bias (no evidence found).

## Results

**Study flow**: 1,610 records → 211 duplicates removed → 1,399 screened → 26 full-text reviewed → **10 studies included** (6 before-after, 4 non-RCTs; 3 non-RCTs compared CAT vs. FATADs). All CAT studies used Invisalign.

**CAT vs. FATADs (2-study meta-analysis)**:
- Overbite: FATADs +1.64 mm greater than CAT (p<0.0001, 95% CI 0.86–2.43, I²=71%)
- U1 position: no significant difference (p=0.08, I²=72%)
- L1 position: CAT +2.35 mm greater extrusion than FATADs (p=0.04, 95% CI 0.06–4.64, I²=60%)
- U6 intrusion: FATADs +1.88 mm greater than CAT (p<0.00001, 95% CI 1.41–2.35, I²=48%)
- L6 intrusion: FATADs +0.45 mm greater than CAT (p=0.03, 95% CI 0.04–0.86, I²=0%)

**Before-and-after CAT (all 10 studies)**:
- Overbite: +2.77 mm pooled increase (p<0.00001, 95% CI 2.42–3.12, I²=83%)
- U1 extrusion: +0.87 mm (p<0.0001, 95% CI 0.47–1.27, I²=0%)
- L1 extrusion: +1.06 mm (p<0.00001, 95% CI 0.63–1.50, I²=0%)
- U6: no significant intrusion, 0.03 mm (p=0.85, I²=0%)
- L6: no significant intrusion, 0.22 mm (p=0.29, I²=0%)

**Stability (2 studies, retention 6 months–>1 year)**: no significant relapse in overbite (change essentially negligible/near-zero) or in U1/L1/U6/L6 positions (p=0.92/0.35/0.55/0.31/0.64).

**Evidence certainty (GRADE)**: low for before/after CAT outcomes; very low for stability and for CAT-vs-FATADs comparisons (confounding, measurement bias, moderate-high heterogeneity, small samples). No publication bias detected (symmetrical funnel plots, Egger's p>0.05 for all outcomes).

**Clinical bottom line (authors' conclusion)**: CAT is less efficacious than FATADs for open-bite correction overall — FATADs achieve greater molar intrusion and greater total overbite gain — but CAT produces significantly more lower-incisor extrusion than FATADs, with no difference at the upper incisor. CAT-corrected open bites appear stable during retention. For severe open bite or patients with a pronounced gummy smile (where further incisor extrusion is undesirable), FATADs are the more appropriate choice; CAT combined with TADs to gain molar-intrusion capability remains an unexplored comparison.
