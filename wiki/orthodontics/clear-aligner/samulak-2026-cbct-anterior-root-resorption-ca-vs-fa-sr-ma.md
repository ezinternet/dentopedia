## One-line Summary

SR+MA of 4 CBCT cohort studies (1,204 anterior teeth) confirms CA causes significantly less orthodontically induced inflammatory root resorption (OIIRR) than fixed appliances (WMD −0.47 mm vs −0.54 mm; p<0.01), with the greatest differential in non-extraction maxillary central incisors.

## 한줄요약

SR+MA (CBCT 코호트 4편/1,204 전치) — 투명교정(Clear Aligner)이 고정식 교정장치 대비 전치 교정유발 치근염증성 흡수(Orthodontically Induced Inflammatory Root Resorption, OIIRR) 유의하게 적음 (WMD −0.47 mm vs −0.54 mm; p<0.01); 비발치·상악 중절치(Maxillary Central Incisor)에서 차이 가장 두드러짐; CA는 OIIRR 위험 증가 환자의 선호 술식.

## Summary

This SR+MA pooled 4 high-quality CBCT cohort studies (1,204 anterior teeth) to compare orthodontically induced inflammatory root resorption (OIIRR) between clear aligner (CA) and fixed appliance (FA) therapy. Both groups showed statistically significant root resorption: CA group WMD −0.47 mm (95% CI −0.55 to −0.40) and FA group WMD −0.54 mm (95% CI −0.75 to −0.33). The between-group difference was statistically significant (p<0.01), confirming CA-associated less anterior OIIRR. The most pronounced CA–FA differential appeared in non-extraction maxillary central incisors. The absolute difference (~0.07 mm) is modest, suggesting the advantage is statistically real but unlikely to drive treatment selection in average-risk patients. The authors conclude CA may be the preferred technique for patients at elevated OIIRR risk.

## Key Contributions

- Pooled CBCT evidence from 4 studies (1,204 anterior teeth) demonstrating CA causes statistically less OIIRR than FA in the anterior dentition (p<0.01).
- Identifies **non-extraction maxillary central incisors** as the subgroup with the most pronounced CA-vs-FA OIIRR differential — the highest-risk single tooth group.
- Provides CA-specific and FA-specific WMDs (−0.47 mm and −0.54 mm) enabling direct per-group risk communication to patients.
- Supports selective use of CA for **elevated-OIIRR-risk patients** (thin/short roots, prior resorption, pipette root morphology) without over-generalizing the 0.07 mm absolute difference.
- Converges with Yıldırım 2026 (MD −0.50 mm) from an independent evidence base, strengthening the CA-vs-FA EARR signal at the SR+MA level.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis (SR+MA)
- **Studies included**: 4 high-quality cohort studies
- **Sample**: 1,204 anterior teeth; CBCT pre- and post-treatment measurements
- **Intervention**: Clear aligner therapy (CA)
- **Control**: Fixed orthodontic appliances (FA)
- **Outcome**: OIIRR measured by CBCT linear root length change (mm) from treatment start to finish
- **Statistical model**: Weighted mean difference (WMD) with 95% CI; p<0.01 significance threshold
- **Subgroup analyses**: Non-extraction vs. extraction; tooth type (maxillary central incisor emphasized)
- **Risk of bias**: 4 high-quality cohort studies per abstract; full RoB table not available (abstract-only ingest)

## Results

**Primary outcomes — CBCT linear OIIRR:**
- **CA group**: WMD −0.47 mm (95% CI −0.55 to −0.40)
- **FA group**: WMD −0.54 mm (95% CI −0.75 to −0.33)
- **Between-group difference**: statistically significant, p<0.01 — CA causes significantly less anterior OIIRR

**Subgroup:**
- Most pronounced CA-vs-FA differential: **non-extraction maxillary central incisors**
- Both extraction and non-extraction cases show the same directional finding; non-extraction amplifies the observable difference

**Absolute difference**: ~0.07 mm — statistically significant but modest in absolute clinical terms

**Clinical conclusion**: CA is preferable for elevated-OIIRR-risk patients; absolute difference is minor in average-risk patients
