## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42020190272) of 5 non-randomized studies (4 retrospective + 1 prospective, 20–38 patients each, 612 total upper-incisor movements) quantifying Invisalign predicted-vs-achieved tooth-movement accuracy specifically in the maxillary central and lateral incisors, post-2010 (SmartTrack era).

Accuracy ranged from 0% (predicted intrusion instead reversed into extrusion) to 155.7% (lingual tip overshoot); torque was consistently among the lowest-accuracy movements (49.1–51.5%), tip ranged 38.5–155.7%, rotation 41.8–66.2%, and extrusion was the standout high-accuracy movement (up to 142.4%) while horizontal translation was comparably good (67.6–78.9%).

Clinically, upper-incisor aligner efficiency is inconsistent by movement type — torque and intrusion are unreliable and often need overcorrection/refinement, while extrusion and translation are relatively predictable; attachments/Power Ridge improve but do not resolve the torque/intrusion weak points, and no RCT evidence exists on the topic.

정확도는 0%(예측된 함입이 반대로 정출됨)부터 155.7%(설측경사 과달성)까지; torque는 일관되게 가장 낮은 정확도 축(49.1–51.5%), tip 38.5–155.7%, rotation 41.8–66.2%, extrusion은 최대 142.4%로 가장 정확한 축이었고 수평이동(translation)도 67.6–78.9%로 양호.

## Summary

According to PMC ([DOI 10.4274/TurkJOrthod.2022.2021.0254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10318852/)), this systematic review isolates the accuracy literature specifically for **upper (maxillary) incisors** with Invisalign clear aligners — a narrower and more clinically actionable lens than prior SRs that reported accuracy by movement type across the whole dentition. Only studies published from 2010 onward were included, since 2009 marked Invisalign's introduction of SmartTrack material, SmartForce features (including the Power Ridge), and SmartStage technology, which changed the accuracy baseline for earlier literature.

Five non-randomized studies (4 retrospective, 1 prospective; 20–38 patients each; 612 total incisor movements analyzed) met inclusion criteria out of 291 initially identified records. Risk of bias (ROBINS-I) was low in 1 study, moderate in 3, and serious in 1 — driven mainly by missing-data/dropout reporting and, in 3 studies, insufficient accuracy metrics for cross-study comparison. No meta-analysis was possible given this heterogeneity; accuracy is reported per study as a percentage (achieved/predicted ratio) or as mean predicted-achieved differences.

The headline finding is that **accuracy varies sharply by movement type** rather than being uniformly good or bad for incisors. Torque (third-order root movement) was consistently among the lowest-accuracy categories (49.1–51.5% when reported as a clean percentage), and torque terminology itself was used inconsistently across the underlying studies — a measurement-definition problem, not just a mechanical one. Intrusion was the least reliable movement overall, in one study reversing direction entirely (0% accuracy — a predicted intrusion instead produced extrusion in both central and lateral incisors, attributed to a bite-block effect from posterior-tooth superimposition). By contrast, **extrusion was the single most accurate/overachieved movement** in the review, with two studies reporting accuracy >100% (up to 142.4%) — the achieved movement exceeded what was predicted. Horizontal movements (mesiodistal/labiolingual translation, 67.6–78.9%) and rotation (41.8–66.2%) fell in between.

## Key Contributions

- First SR to isolate Invisalign accuracy data **specifically for upper incisors** (vs. prior SRs organized by movement type across all teeth), giving tooth-group-specific clinical guidance.
- Uniformizes heterogeneous accuracy reporting (percentage ratio vs. mm/degree mean-difference vs. min/max range) into comparable accuracy percentages across the 5 studies.
- Flags that "torque" is inconsistently defined in the underlying primary-study literature (clinical torque vs. labiolingual tip conflated in some studies) — a field-wide measurement problem, not just this review's limitation.
- Overturns the common clinical assumption that vertical movement is uniformly hard for aligners: intrusion is unreliable (down to 0%), but extrusion is one of the *most* accurate movements (up to 142.4%).

## Methodology

PRISMA-guided systematic review; protocol PROSPERO CRD42020190272. Databases: PubMed, LILACS, Scopus, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, plus manual journal search; languages English/French/Spanish/Portuguese; publication window 2010–present (post-SmartTrack cutoff). 291 records → 238 after deduplication → 12 full-text reviewed → 4 eligible + 1 added via reference search = 5 included studies. Three independent reviewers for study selection; two independent reviewers applied ROBINS-I (7 bias domains) for non-randomized studies. Study profile: 4 retrospective + 1 prospective non-randomized cohort, 20–38 patients/study, 612 total upper-incisor tooth movements. Confidence `sr` (systematic review without meta-analysis — heterogeneous reporting precluded pooling).

## Results

Accuracy (achieved/predicted, CI = central incisor, LI = lateral incisor; >100% = achieved overshot predicted; 0% = movement reversed direction):

| Movement | CI accuracy | LI accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| **Torque** | 49.1–51.5% (2 studies); one study instead reports 1.75°±2.86° mean difference | 0.08°±2.93° mean difference (near-perfect in that study) |
| **Labiolingual tip** | lingual 57.4–155.7%; labial 52.8–54.2% | lingual 54.4–57.4%; labial 61.4–69.9% |
| **Mesiodistal tip** | 45.5–57.5% | 38.5–51.5% |
| **Intrusion** | 33.4–33.9%; **0% in one study** (reversed to extrusion) | 36.7–44.6%; **0% in same study** |
| **Extrusion** | up to 142.4% (mean diff −0.50±1.17 mm) | up to 1.36±0.63 mm mean difference |
| **Rotation** | 48.7–61.1% | 41.8–66.2% |
| **Translation (labial/lingual)** | 67.6% | near-perfect (0.01±0.66 mm) |
| **Translation (mesiodistal)** | 78.9% | 77.2% |

- **Overall accuracy range across all movement types and both tooth groups: 0% to 155.7%.**
- Torque and intrusion are the least reliable movements; extrusion and mesiodistal translation are the most reliable.
- Attachments were used in 3/5 studies, Power Ridge in 1/5, neither in 1/5; the review notes a general (not fully isolable) trend toward higher accuracy when auxiliary features were used, though the single highest tip-accuracy value (155.7%) occurred in a bicuspid-extraction protocol study, complicating attribution.
- No RCTs exist on this topic in the post-2010 (SmartTrack-era) literature — all 5 included studies are non-randomized.
