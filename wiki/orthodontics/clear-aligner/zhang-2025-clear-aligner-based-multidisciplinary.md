## Three-line Summary

Case report, n=1, 28-year-old adult female with skeletal Class III malocclusion (Wits −4.92 mm, Holdaway angle 11°, full Class III molar relationship with a functional/positional component), treated non-surgically with clear aligners (Invisalign) integrated with restorative dentistry and implantology.

Dentoalveolar camouflage was achieved via bite opening (mandibular incisor intrusion/retroclination + maxillary incisor proclination: U1-SN 99.17°→109.74°, L1-MP 96.37°→88.08°), Class III elastics, and molar occlusal-surface attachment bite ramps; a discolored UL1 was ultimately extracted and replaced with an implant plus anterior esthetic restoration; SNB improved 79.58°→78.49° and soft-tissue profile improved (NA-Apo −5.31°→−2.51°).

Seven-year follow-up showed stable Class I occlusion, overjet/overbite, and molar intercuspation, supporting clear aligners as a viable non-surgical camouflage option in borderline (Holdaway >10.3°, Wits >−5.8 mm) skeletal Class III adults who decline orthognathic surgery — though as a single case report, generalizability and longer-term recurrence risk remain unknown.

## Summary

This case report ([DOI 10.1016/j.ortho.2025.101110](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ortho.2025.101110)) describes a 28-year-old adult female with skeletal Class III malocclusion — a full Class III molar relationship, midface deficiency, concave profile, and a functional component (mandible retrudable from centric occlusion to edge-to-edge) — whose cephalometric values (Wits −4.92 mm, Holdaway angle 11°) fell just outside the commonly cited surgical thresholds (Wits <−5.8 mm, Holdaway <10.3°, per Eslami et al.). Because the patient declined orthognathic surgery, the clinicians pursued a multidisciplinary, non-surgical camouflage strategy: clear aligners (Invisalign, two phases totaling ~138 aligner steps) drove dentoalveolar compensation (mandibular incisor intrusion/retroclination, maxillary incisor proclination, molar-attachment bite ramps, Class III elastics), while a discolored maxillary left central incisor was managed conservatively, then extracted and replaced with an implant plus anterior esthetic restoration. Treatment achieved a stable Class I occlusion with normal overjet/overbite, improved SNB and soft-tissue profile, and — most notably — remained stable at 7-year follow-up.

## Key Contributions

- Provides rare long-term (7-year) stability data for clear-aligner-only camouflage of an adult skeletal Class III case with a functional component, a scenario more commonly managed with fixed appliances or surgery.
- Demonstrates a coordinated multidisciplinary treatment arc — orthodontic dentoalveolar compensation, conservative monitoring of a compromised incisor, extraction/implant replacement, and anterior esthetic restoration — sequenced within a single aligner-based plan.
- Illustrates practical clear-aligner Class III mechanics: molar occlusal-surface attachments used deliberately as bite ramps to open the bite and correct anterior crossbite, combined with Class III elastics to promote favorable mandibular-plane and occlusal-plane rotation.
- Adds a concrete example just outside the Eslami borderline-surgical thresholds (Holdaway 11° vs. cutoff 10.3°; Wits −4.92 mm vs. cutoff −5.8 mm) that was successfully camouflaged non-surgically, informationally reinforcing where that decision boundary can still yield stable outcomes.

## Methodology

Single case report. Diagnosis combined extraoral/smile analysis (midface deficiency, concave profile, flattened smile curve, narrow buccal corridor) with cephalometric assessment (Wits −4.92 mm, Holdaway 11°) and functional exam (mandible retrudable to edge-to-edge from centric occlusion, indicating a functional/positional component layered on the skeletal discrepancy). LL8 was extracted before aligner therapy to exploit the regional acceleratory phenomenon and speed distal molar movement. Invisalign aligners were used in two phases (preliminary: 31 maxillary + 31 mandibular; refinement: 38 maxillary + 38 mandibular), mechanically driving bite opening through mandibular incisor intrusion/retroclination and maxillary incisor proclination, assisted by molar occlusal-surface attachments (bite ramps) and Class III elastics. A discolored UL1 was observed conservatively before being extracted and replaced with an implant, with concurrent anterior esthetic restoration. Follow-up extended to 7 years.

## Results

- Occlusion: bilateral super Class I molar and Class I canine relationship achieved; upper/lower crowding resolved; normal overjet and overbite; anterior crossbite corrected.
- Dentoalveolar: U1-SN (maxillary incisor inclination) increased 99.17° → 109.74°; L1-MP (mandibular incisor inclination) decreased 96.37° → 88.08°; roots reported to remain within basal bone.
- Skeletal/soft tissue: SNB decreased 79.58° → 78.49°; NA-Apo (soft-tissue convexity) improved −5.31° → −2.51°.
- Restorative/implant: discolored UL1 extracted and successfully replaced with an implant plus anterior esthetic restoration.
- Stability: results remained stable at 7-year follow-up — the longest reported follow-up interval identified for a clear-aligner-only Class III camouflage case in this wiki.

**Clinical implication**: for adult, borderline-surgical skeletal Class III patients (especially those with a functional/positional component and who decline orthognathic surgery), a carefully planned multidisciplinary clear-aligner camouflage approach — using molar attachments as bite ramps plus Class III elastics — can achieve a stable Class I occlusion sustained over at least 7 years. As a single case report, this does not establish generalizable recurrence rates or override the recognized risks of dentoalveolar compensation (gingival recession, root resorption, relapse) documented in the broader Class III camouflage literature.
