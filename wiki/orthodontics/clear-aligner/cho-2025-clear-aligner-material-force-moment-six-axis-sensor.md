## One-line Summary

In-vitro six-axis mechanical sensor bench study (n=6/group, 6 groups) comparing three clear-aligner materials (Zendura and Trioclear thermoplastics vs Graphy TA-28 direct-3D-printed resin) at two labial-tipping magnitudes (0.3 mm, 0.6 mm) of a maxillary lateral incisor, finding no significant inter-material differences at 0.3 mm tipping but at 0.6 mm Zendura produced greater labial force than Trioclear while Graphy paradoxically produced lingual force, and Zendura/Trioclear generated greater labial-inclination moment than Graphy (all p<0.01), with Graphy showing the lowest absolute force/moment values overall.

## 한줄요약

## Summary

This in-vitro bench study used a novel six-axis mechanical sensor setup with a 3D-printed maxillary typodont (including root, 0.5 mm PDL space, proximal contacts, 37°C water-bath immersion) to directly measure the forces and moments clear aligners transmit to teeth during labial tipping of the maxillary right lateral incisor (#12). Three aligner materials — Zendura (single-layer thermoplastic), Trioclear (three-layer thermoplastic), and Graphy TA-28 (direct-3D-printed resin) — were tested at two tipping magnitudes (0.3 mm, 0.6 mm), with force/moment also recorded on the adjacent anchor tooth (#11). At the smaller (0.3 mm) tipping magnitude, materials behaved similarly for labio-lingual and mesio-distal forces. At the larger (0.6 mm) magnitude, material differences became clinically apparent: Zendura generated the greatest labial force and, together with Trioclear, a significantly stronger labial-inclination moment than Graphy. Graphy showed a paradoxical lingual force on the labially-tipped target tooth, attributed to shape-memory/thermal-softening artifacts, and generally the lowest absolute force and moment magnitudes of the three materials. The authors conclude aligner material should be selected based on the specific biomechanical demand of the case, since materials do not behave interchangeably and can generate unexpected force/moment directions.

## Key Contributions

- Provides real hardware-measured (not simulated) force/moment data directly comparable across three clinically-used aligner materials under matched, clinically-relevant conditions (root/PDL, proximal contact, 37°C water bath, tooth-axis-aligned sensor).
- Isolates the effect of tipping *magnitude* (0.3 mm vs 0.6 mm) within each material, showing that material differences only become statistically significant at the larger (0.6 mm) displacement — implying dose-dependence of material choice relevance.
- Identifies a specific, mechanistically-explained failure mode of a direct-3D-printed resin (Graphy TA-28): reverse-direction (lingual) force on a labially-programmed tooth, linked to thermal-softening/shape-memory spring-back during insertion.
- Reports reciprocal/anchor-tooth (#11) force-moment patterns alongside the target tooth (#12), showing that "expected" reactive moment direction (lingual inclination) was reproduced only by Trioclear, not by Zendura or Graphy.

## Methodology

In-vitro experimental study. A CT-derived, 3D-printed maxillary typodont (100 µm layer thickness) incorporated crown, root, and a 0.5 mm PDL space filled with a custom soft resin to emulate physiologic tooth mobility. Labial tipping of #12 was digitally programmed at 0.3 mm and 0.6 mm (Meshmixer); no attachments were used. Six groups (2 tipping magnitudes × 3 materials: Zendura, Trioclear, Graphy TA-28), n=6 aligners/group (power-analysis-derived sample size, referencing McKay et al.). A metal rod embedded in the roots of #11 and #12 connected to a six-axis mechanical sensor; forces (Fx, Fy, Fz) were computed at incisal-edge midpoints and moments (Mx, My, Mz) at the estimated center of resistance (root apex). Thermoplastic aligners were seated directly and immersed in a 37°C bath; the 3D-printed aligner was pre-softened (60°C, 5 s) before seating. Data acquisition ran 2 minutes; the mean of the final 10 seconds was analyzed. Reliability was assessed via repeated measurement (2-week interval) using the Dahlberg formula (mean error 88.79 mN for force, 0.40 N·mm for moment). Statistics: Mann-Whitney U, Kruskal-Wallis with DSCF pairwise comparison, α = 0.05.

## Results

- **0.3 mm tipping, cross-material (#12):** no significant labio-lingual/mesio-distal force differences among materials; Zendura had greatest occlusal force (p<0.001); Trioclear > Graphy for distal-in rotation moment (p<0.01); Graphy generated mesial angulation moment vs Zendura/Trioclear's distal angulation moment (p<0.01).
- **0.6 mm tipping, cross-material (#12) — primary clinically relevant contrast:** Zendura produced significantly higher labial force than Trioclear and Graphy (p<0.01); Graphy showed particularly low labial/lingual force values, i.e., a lingual-direction force despite the labially-tipped target. Zendura and Trioclear generated significantly stronger labial-inclination moment than Graphy (p<0.01); Graphy showed almost no angulation moment vs Zendura/Trioclear's distal angulation moment (p<0.001).
- **Within-material, 0.3 vs 0.6 mm:** Zendura's labial-inclination moment on #12 increased significantly with more tipping (p<0.05); Trioclear's labial force on #12 significantly decreased while occlusal force and labial-inclination moment increased (p<0.05/p<0.01); Graphy showed no significant change in labial-inclination moment but a significant occlusal-to-gingival force shift (p<0.01).
- **Anchor tooth (#11) reactive pattern:** only Trioclear produced the theoretically expected lingual-inclination reactive moment; Zendura and Graphy instead produced labial-inclination moments on #11, an unpredictable pattern.
- Graphy TA-28 consistently showed the lowest absolute force/moment magnitudes among the three materials, alongside its paradoxical reverse-direction force finding.
- Conclusion: aligner-material selection should be guided by the specific biomechanical requirement of the case, since materials are not interchangeable and can produce unexpected force/moment magnitudes and directions.
