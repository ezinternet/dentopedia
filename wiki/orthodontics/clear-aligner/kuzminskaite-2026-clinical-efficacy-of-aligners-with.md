## Three-line Summary

PRISMA SR (726 records → 9 studies, n=412 growing skeletal Class II patients; PubMed/ScienceDirect/Cochrane/Google Scholar, 2020–2025; PICO framework; ROBINS-I risk-of-bias) comparing clear aligners with mandibular advancement against conventional functional orthopedic appliances using cephalometric analysis.

Both aligner mandibular advancement and conventional functional appliances demonstrated similar skeletal and dental correction — consistent reductions in overbite and overjet, increases in SNB, and decreases in ANB and Wits appraisal — with no significant differences in these parameters.

Aligners with mandibular advancement are an effective alternative to conventional functional appliances for skeletal Class II in growing patients, with equivalent dentoalveolar and skeletal effects; methodological limitations and heterogeneity limit evidence strength — independently corroborating the larger Huang 2026 SR+MA (n=465).

## Summary

According to PubMed (PMID 42734614; no DOI returned), this PRISMA-aligned systematic review from a Lithuanian orthodontic group (Stomatologija, 2026; Vol 28(1):2–9) searched PubMed, ScienceDirect, The Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar for studies published 2020–2025 using a PICO framework. ROBINS-I assessed risk of bias.

From 726 identified records, 9 studies were included enrolling 412 growing skeletal Class II patients. Outcomes were evaluated primarily through cephalometric analysis of skeletal and dental changes. The review found that despite variability in parameters and methodology across studies, **both aligner mandibular advancement and conventional functional appliances produced similar skeletal and dental correction**: consistent reductions in overbite and overjet, increases in SNB, and decreases in ANB and Wits appraisal.

The authors conclude that aligners with mandibular advancement are "an effective option for treating Class II malocclusions in growing patients" with "similar dentoalveolar and skeletal effects" to conventional functional appliances. Methodological limitations and heterogeneity among studies restrict evidence strength.

This SR independently corroborates the larger [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/huang-2026-clear-aligner-mandibular-advancement-vs-functional-class-ii-sr-ma]] (PROSPERO-registered SR+MA, n=465, full text, meta-analysis), which quantitatively found no statistically significant difference in any sagittal skeletal parameter (SNA/SNB/ANB/Wits all NS). The present SR adds independent replication from a European research group with a 2020–2025 search window, though it lacks the quantitative synthesis that makes Huang 2026 the stronger reference.

**This paper does not supersede Huang 2026.** Huang 2026 remains the primary evidence reference (SR+MA, larger n, PROSPERO, quantitative pooling, full-text access). Use Huang 2026 for clinical decisions.

## Key Contributions

- Independent European-context SR corroborating CAMA–functional-appliance equivalence on cephalometric outcomes.
- Confirms the equivalence signal across a 2020–2025 restricted search window (post-COVID literature).
- Adds to the converging multi-SR evidence base that aligner mandibular advancement is a viable functional-appliance alternative for growing Class II patients.

## Methodology

- Design: Systematic Review (no meta-analysis)
- Search: PubMed, ScienceDirect, The Cochrane Library, Google Scholar; 2020–2025; English language only
- Framework: PICO
- Risk-of-bias: ROBINS-I
- Records: 726 identified → 9 studies included, n=412 patients
- Outcomes: cephalometric parameters — overbite, overjet, SNB, ANB, Wits appraisal

## Results

- Both CAMA and conventional functional appliances: similar skeletal and dental correction
- Consistent outcomes across included studies: overbite ↓, overjet ↓, SNB ↑, ANB ↓, Wits ↓
- No significant differences between groups reported
- Heterogeneity in parameters and methodologies across studies noted
- Evidence strength limited by methodological limitations and study heterogeneity
