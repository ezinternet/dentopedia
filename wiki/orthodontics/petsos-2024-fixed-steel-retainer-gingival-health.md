## Three-line Summary

This RCT evaluated fixed steel retainers vs removable vacuum-formed retainers on gingival health in 37 orthodontically treated patients over 12 months. REC and PPD showed no significant differences between groups; 73.3% of fixed retainer patients maintained healthy gingiva at 12 months vs 88.2% in controls. Gingival recessions were clinically non-relevant at all time points.

## Summary

This randomized clinical trial compared the periodontal effects of fixed steel retainers with removable vacuum-formed retainers in young orthodontically treated patients with intact periodontium. Over 12 months, fixed retainers showed comparable gingival health outcomes to removable retainers, with no clinically significant recessions in either group. The study provides evidence that fixed retainers, despite their potential for plaque retention, do not compromise gingival health in compliant patients with good oral hygiene.

## Key Contributions

- First RCT directly comparing fixed steel vs removable vacuum-formed retainers on gingival health
- Demonstrates fixed retainers maintain acceptable gingival health (73.3% at 12 months)
- Clinically irrelevant recessions with both retainer types
- Objective periodontal parameters (PPD, REC, BOP) at three time points

## Methodology

Randomized clinical controlled trial at Goethe University Frankfurt. 37 patients (test: n=15, control: n=17) with intact periodontium. Periodontal parameters (PPD, REC, BOP) and plaque/gingival indices assessed at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months on mandibular anterior teeth. Clinical trial registration: DRKS00016710.

## Results

- REC: no significant pairwise differences (all time points)
- PPD: no significant pairwise differences (all time points)
- Gingival health at 12mo: test 73.3%, control 88.2% (inter-group NS)
- Intra-group: control BL→6mo improvement significant (p=0.043)
- All recessions clinically non-relevant
