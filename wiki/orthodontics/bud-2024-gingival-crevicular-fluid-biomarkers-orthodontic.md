## One-line Summary

Narrative review (19 articles, Aug 2015–Aug 2024) mapping how gingival crevicular fluid (GCF) inflammatory mediators, enzymes and bone-turnover markers (PGE2, TNF-α, IL-1β, RANKL/OPG, ALP, MMP-1/2/3/8/9/13, osteocalcin, N-telopeptide) rise and fall during orthodontic tooth movement, arguing GCF sampling could non-invasively monitor OTM to shorten treatment and avoid adverse effects.

## Summary

Orthodontic force displaces teeth by remodeling the periodontium: on the pressure side the periodontal ligament (PDL) and alveolar bone are compressed and resorb, while on the tension side the PDL is stretched and bone forms. This review asks whether the molecules released during that remodeling — captured in the **gingival crevicular fluid (GCF)** seeping from the gingival blood-vessel plexus into the sulcus — can be sampled chairside to *monitor* orthodontic tooth movement (OTM) rather than relying only on clinical parameters (PPD, CAL, BOP) and radiographs.

Searching PubMed, Embase and Scopus (Aug 2015–Aug 2024) yielded 387 records, narrowed to **19 studies** in systemically healthy patients. The consistent message across them is that GCF biomarker levels *do* change with orthodontic force (rejecting the null hypothesis), and that different molecules report on different phases and sides of remodeling. The authors group them into inflammatory mediators, enzymes, and metabolic/bone-turnover products, and conclude that — if confounders (gingival inflammation, occlusal load, poor hygiene) are controlled — GCF biomarkers could serve as biological indicators of orthodontic effect. The practical bottleneck is that biomarker assay (ELISA) is laborious and expensive, motivating chairside point-of-care tests.

## Key Contributions

- A biomarker-by-biomarker map of OTM, split by **pressure vs tension** side and by **timing** (early inflammatory phase vs later bone-turnover phase).
- Positions GCF as a non-invasive, repeatable, site-specific monitoring fluid for orthodontics — a bridge from OTM *mechanism* to OTM *monitoring*.
- Appliance-specific signals: PEA vs self-ligating (TNF-α time course), lingual vs labial (higher IL/TNF cytokines lingually), Invisalign distalization (IL-1b/RANKL vs TGF-1b/OPN), Twin Block vs Clear Block/Forsus (ALP).
- A comparison of GCF harvesting methods and the case for developing in-office tests.

## Methodology

Narrative (non-systematic) review; no meta-analysis or pooled effect sizes. Electronic search across three databases with the keywords "orthodontic treatment", "gingival crevicular fluid", "biomarkers", "review"; longitudinal, case–control, retrospective, prospective studies and reviews from Aug 2015–Aug 2024; duplicates and non-eligible records removed to leave 19 articles; only systemically healthy patients included. GCF quantified via electronic transducer and biomarkers via ELISA in the source studies.

## Results

**Inflammatory mediators**
- **PGE2** — promotes bone resorption; ↑ on both pressure and tension sides. Compressed PDL cells raise activated FAK and PGE2.
- **TNF-α** — ↑ at 1 h and 24 h after force in both PEA and self-ligating (SL) appliances; by 168 h it falls in PEA but stays elevated in SL.
- **IL-1β + TNF-α** — immediate ↑ at 1 h, peak at 24 h → inflammation drives *initial* tooth movement.
- **IL-1α/β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α** — higher with fixed **lingual** than labial appliances → greater periodontal mechanical stress lingually.
- **RANKL/OPG** — the RANKL/OPG ratio is the master switch for resorption: compression zone → RANKL ↑, OPG ↓ (ratio ↑, osteoclastogenesis); tension zone → OPG ↑ (RANKL inhibited). Invisalign molar distalization (~1 N) raises IL-1b/RANKL at pressure sites and TGF-1b/OPN at tension sites.

**Enzymes**
- **ALP** — bone-*formation* marker; ↑ by day 14, greater in tension than pressure zone, higher with conventional than self-ligating brackets; Twin Block > Clear Block/Forsus at 1/3/6 months.
- **MMPs** — MMP-1/2/3/8/9/13 all ↑ on both sides. MMP-3 peaks ~24 h (p38 MAPK). **MMP-9** = sensitive periodontal-inflammation marker, detectable by 4 h and elevated throughout OTM. MMP level proposed as an **early marker of active tooth movement**.

**Metabolic / bone-turnover products**
- **Chondroitin sulfate** — ↑ across loading; no 120 g vs 180 g difference → stronger force unnecessary.
- **Pentraxin-3 (PTX-3)** — ↑ after activation, peaks 24 h, back to baseline ~2 wk (children) / ~1 wk (adults) → initial remodeling + aseptic inflammation.
- **Osteocalcin (OC)** — bone-formation/turnover marker; GCF peak day 7–14, significant rise days 14 and 21; serum OC tracks alveolar bone *density* loss.
- **N-telopeptide (NTx)** — bone-*resorption* marker; GCF NTx rises day 7→21, significant days 14/21, max day 21; reflects systemic osteoclast activity (e.g. mandibular protrusive appliances).

**GCF harvesting** — three methods: gingival lavage (cells/proteins, imprecise dilution), capillary microtubes (precise/undiluted but slow, tissue damage), and **absorbent paper strips** (most used, 30–60 s, fast, no tissue damage, discard blood/saliva-contaminated). Volume best read by electronic transducer; biomarkers assayed by **ELISA**.

**Bottom line** — GCF biomarkers change reproducibly with orthodontic force and could monitor OTM to shorten treatment and reduce adverse effects (root resorption, bone thinning), *provided* gingival inflammation, occlusal forces and hygiene are controlled. Assay cost/complexity is the barrier to routine use.
