## Three-line Summary

In-vivo pig experiment (J Biomech 2020;104:109716; University of Washington; NIHMS 1630169 = PMC version) measuring, for the first time simultaneously, maxillary molar mobility, alveolar bone bending, and PDL fluid pressure during natural mastication and masseter muscle stimulation in 12 three-month-old farm pigs.

During the power stroke the molar displaced buccally and apically 192 ± 95 μm regardless of chewing side; compressive strain was greater on the buccal alveolar plate; PDL pressure rose to 3.63 ± 0.80 kPa; masseter contraction produced the same pattern at lower values (20–302 μm, avg 113 ± 89 μm).

micro-CT PDL space widths 250–400 μm (buccal/mesial > palatal/distal) were consistently larger than the mobility range with no correlation; in-vivo mobility 63–322 μm overlaps the published pig range (0–800 μm) and exceeds rat/monkey/human molar values — occlusal function causes only partial PDL strain.

## 세줄요약

돼지 상악 구치에 초음파 크리스털(치수강·치조판), 로제트 스트레인 게이지, 골내 압력변환기를 장착해 자유 섭식과 깨물근 자극 중 동요도·치조골 휨·PDL 액압을 처음으로 동시 계측한 생체 실험 (J Biomech 2020;104:109716, NIH/NIDCR R21DE023127).

교합강타 중 구치는 씹는 쪽과 무관하게 협측·치근단으로 192±95 μm 변위; 압축 변형률은 협측판에서 큼; PDL 압력 3.63±0.80 kPa 증가; 깨물근 자극 시 같은 양상이 낮은 값(20–302 μm, 평균 113±89 μm)으로 재현.

micro-CT PDL 폭 250–400 μm(협측/근심>구개측/원심)는 동요도 범위(63–322 μm)보다 크고 상관 없음 — 생리적 동요도는 PDL 공간의 일부만 사용, 치아 동요도 판단 시 PDL 폭과 변위의 비중첩 이해 필요.

## Overview

Tooth mobility is a fundamental characteristic used clinically to judge tooth prognosis, yet the direction and magnitude of functional tooth movement were previously unknown. This study instrumented 12 three-month-old farm pigs (anesthesia: butorphanol 0.3 mg/kg + xylazine 0.3 mg/kg) and recorded, during unrestrained feeding and subsequent masseter muscle stimulation: (1) ultrasonic crystal displacement (one crystal implanted in the pulp chamber of a deciduous maxillary molar, additional crystals glued to buccal and palatal alveolar plates — the pulp-buccal distance capturing tooth displacement relative to bone); (2) rosette strain gauges on buccal/palatal alveolar ridges; (3) PDL fluid pressure via a pressure transducer inserted into palatal alveolar bone facing the PDL. PDL widths were measured on micro-CT (59 kV, 34.4 μm pixel size).

## Key Findings

- **Tooth displacement during power stroke**: molar displaced **buccally and apically 192 ± 95 μm**, independent of chewing side. Natural mastication range 63–322 μm; masseter contraction 20–302 μm (average 113 ± 89 μm).
- **Alveolar bending**: compressive bone strain greater on the **buccal** than on the **palatal** alveolar plate; alveolar bending on average ~1/3 greater than tooth displacement.
- **PDL fluid pressure**: rose during the power stroke to **3.63 ± 0.80 kPa**; similar but generally lower values with masseter contraction.
- **PDL space widths (micro-CT)**: 250–400 μm overall; **buccal and mesial widths larger than palatal and distal**; widths consistently **larger than the mobility range**, with ranges overlapping only partially and **no correlation between PDL width and mobility**.
- **Comparative mobility context**: in-vivo molar mobility 63–322 μm is within the published pig range (0–800 μm) and exceeds rat molars (100–240 μm), monkey molars (0–100 μm), and human molars (100–400 μm).

## Clinical Implications

1. Functional loading displaces the tooth buccally + apically (~190 μm) with the chewing-side independent — a physiologic baseline for interpreting pathological mobility (jepsen-2023 shows periodontitis roughly doubles stiffness loss, not displacement per se).
2. PDL width does NOT predict mobility — a wide PDL space does not imply greater functional displacement; clinical mobility judgment should not be inferred from PDL/lamina dura appearance.
3. The combination of tooth displacement + alveolar bending (~1/3 greater) means PDL strain during function is only partial — relevant to FEM models (papadopoulou-2013) that must be validated against physiological strain levels rather than full PDL compression.
