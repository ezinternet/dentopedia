## Three-line Summary

Comprehensive narrative review (Stem Cell Research & Therapy 2025, Peking University) of how periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs) regulate alveolar bone remodeling under orthodontic mechanical forces and periodontal inflammatory signals.

Compression-side PDLSCs secrete M-CSF/RANKL → osteoclastogenesis; tension-side PDLSCs respond to BMP/OIF → osteoblast formation; mechanotransduction mediated by TRPV4, Piezo1, TRPC6, integrins, and non-coding RNAs; PDLSCs immunomodulate T cells/macrophages via PGE2, exosomal miR-155-5p, and IL-10 pathways.

Identifies PDLSCs as the central orchestrators linking mechanical and inflammatory inputs to periodontal tissue homeostasis and OTM, with therapeutic implications for exosome-based periodontal regeneration.

## 세줄요약

치주인대 줄기세포 (Periodontal Ligament Stem Cells, PDLSCs)가 교정력(기계적 힘)과 치주 염증 신호 하에서 치조골 리모델링을 어떻게 조절하는지 종합 리뷰 (Stem Cell Res Ther 2025, 북경대).

압박측 PDLSCs는 M-CSF/RANKL 분비 → 파골세포 (Osteoclastogenesis) 유도; 장력측은 BMP/OIF → 조골세포 분화·골형성; 기계수용 (Mechanotransduction)은 TRPV4·Piezo1·TRPC6·인테그린·비코딩 RNA가 매개; PDLSCs는 PGE2·엑소좀 miR-155-5p·IL-10 경로로 T세포·대식세포를 면역조절.

PDLSCs를 기계적·염증 자극을 조직 리모델링으로 통합하는 핵심 조절자로 규명하며, 엑소좀 기반 치주 재생치료의 이론적 근거를 제시함.

## Summary
Narrative review from Peking University School of Stomatology (Yan Liu & Danqing He labs), published in Stem Cell Research & Therapy (2025) 16:653. Reviews four key aspects of PDLSC biology: (1) dynamic biological property changes under mechanical and inflammatory stimuli; (2) cellular interactions with immune cells; (3) ECM remodeling; (4) molecular mechanisms of mechanotransduction and inflammatory signaling pathways.

PDLSCs first isolated and characterized by Seo et al. (Lancet 2004). Neural crest-derived. Display >100 population doublings with self-renewal; higher SSEA-4 expression than BMMSCs or DPSCs; >2× proliferative capacity vs BMMSCs. Multipotent: osteogenic, adipogenic, chondrogenic, cardiomyocyte, islet-like, neuronal, cementoblast differentiation documented. OTM is the primary mechanical force model (in vivo: nickel-titanium coil springs between maxillary first molar and incisors; in vitro: Flexcell tension/compression systems, LMHF vibration).

## Key Findings

### PDLSCs Under Mechanical Forces (OTM Model)
| Cellular Event | Key Mediators | Functional Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Sterile inflammation | TNF-α, IL-1β, IFN-γ, PGE2, IL-6, MMPs | Initiates osteoclastogenesis cascade |
| M1 macrophage polarization | PDL compressive stress | Bone resorption at pressure sites |
| Osteoclastogenesis (compression) | M-CSF, RANKL-RANK-OPG axis | Bone resorption → tooth displacement |
| Bone formation (tension) | BMP, OIF, COL-I, OCN, ALP | New bone matrix synthesis |
| PDLSC proliferation | TRPV4, CD146⁺Ki67⁺ | Peaks day 3–7 post-force |
| Apoptosis | Bax↑, BCL-2↓, caspase-3 | Heavy force > light force; peak day 7 |
| Autophagy (compression) | LC3-II↑, Beclin-1↑ | Modulates M1 macrophage via AKT |
| Pyroptosis | Caspase-1 | Regulates OTM (Chen 2024) |

### Mechanotransduction Pathway Map
| Mechanosensor | Mechanism | Effect on PDLSCs |
|---|---|---|
| Cytoskeleton | F-actin/microtubule reorganization | Force propagation; cytokine release |
| TRPV4 (TRP channel) | Ion channel gating under load | Modulates stemness; bone remodeling |
| Piezo1 (Lepr⁺ PDLSCs) | Mechanosensitive ion channel | Converts force → biological response |
| TRPC6 (GPCR-associated) | Non-selective cation channel | Regulates osteogenic differentiation |
| α7 nAChR | Cyclic compression → Wnt↑ | Promotes osteoclastogenesis |
| αVβ5 integrin | Compressive force → TGF-β1↑ | Collagen synthesis |
| LRP6 integrin | Tension → F-actin dynamics↑ | Enhances osteogenic differentiation |
| miR-146a / miR-34a | Cyclic stretch → downregulated | Suppresses osteogenic potential |
| CircRNAs | Tension-responsive expression | Controls tension-induced osteogenesis |
| lncRNAs | Compression → ECM gene networks | ECM organization, collagen fibril assembly |

### PDLSCs Under Inflammatory Conditions: Immunomodulatory Effects
- **T cells**: PGE2-dependent immunosuppression; exosomal miR-155-5p → SIRT1 inhibition in CD4⁺ T cells → Th17/Treg balance; IL-6/IDO1 → Treg expansion; Jagged1-Notch2 signaling (reciprocal: Treg → PDLSC osteogenesis↑)
- **B cells**: PD-L1/PD-1 axis suppresses B cell activation; inhibits IgM/IgG/IgA; context-dependent IL-6 survival signal
- **Macrophages**: M0→M2 shift by day 21 post-transplantation; IL-10/TGF-β paracrine upregulation; LPS-pretreated PDLSC exosomes (miR-143-3p) → M1 polarization; NLRP3 inflammasome activation via EVs
- **Neutrophils**: IL-6 → neutrophil survival; P. gingivalis recognition → neutrophil recruitment via paracrine signaling

### PDLSC Biological Changes Under Inflammation
- **Proliferation**: Dose-dependent biphasic responses to TNF-α and LPS; F. nucleatum → apoptosis + ferroptosis; P. gingivalis OMVs inhibit proliferation
- **Osteogenesis**: Periodontitis-derived PDLSCs have diminished osteogenic potential; LPS → BMAL1↓ → PANoptosis → osteogenic inhibition; P. gingivalis OMVs → ER stress/PERK pathway → osteogenic inhibition
- **Rescue mechanisms**: Healthy PDLSC-derived exosomes restore osteogenesis (suppress excessive Wnt); LPS-pretreated dental follicle stem cell exosomes → antioxidant + pro-osteogenic effects

## Clinical Relevance
- PDLSCs as the central cellular mediators linking OTM, periodontal inflammation, and bone remodeling in one unified model
- TRPV4, Piezo1 mechanosensors and miRNA regulators as potential therapeutic targets for modulating bone remodeling during orthodontic treatment
- Exosome-based therapies using healthy PDLSC-derived exosomes for periodontal regeneration under inflammatory conditions
- PDLSC transplantation alleviates autoimmune symptoms (lupus-prone mouse models) via PGE2-mediated immunosuppression
- Optimal mechanical force magnitude for PDLSC function: 12% strain for healthy PDLSCs; 8% for periodontitis-affected PDLSCs (Flexcell in vitro)

## Limitations
- Narrative review; no systematic search protocol or quality appraisal of included studies
- Many cited studies use rodent (rat/mouse) OTM models — translational gap to human PDLSCs under clinical orthodontic forces
- In vitro mechanical loading systems (Flexcell, four-point bending) may not recapitulate complex 3D PDL force environment
- PDLSC isolation varies across studies (premolars aged 12–35 years; different culture conditions) — heterogeneity in findings
