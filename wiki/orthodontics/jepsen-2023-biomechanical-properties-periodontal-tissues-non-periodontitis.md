## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional clinical study (Bonn, Germany; 28 untreated periodontitis + 20 healthy patients, 68 incisors) measuring tooth mobility with a computerized intraoral loading device (ILD): displacement to max 0.2 mm orally over 0.5 s / 1 s / 10 s, extracting maximum force (Fmax, N/mm) as a tooth-mobility parameter (TM-ILD).

Periodontitis teeth were significantly more mobile: Fmax 12.3±5.2 / 12.4±4.6 / 10.1±5.1 N/mm vs healthy 16.3±6.3 / 15.4±6.6 / 16.3±6.3 (differences 3.9 [p=0.024], 3.1 [p=0.067], 2.8 [p=0.001]), with longer loading durations further lowering force in diseased teeth.

TM-ILD correlated inversely with clinical attachment loss at all durations (r = −0.47 / −0.57 / −0.47) and with BOP at 0.5 s (r = −0.52); repeat-measurement reproducibility Spearman 0.68–0.79 — time-dependent tooth mobility reflects both inflammatory status (BOP) and quantitative bone/attachment loss (CAL).

## Summary

This cross-sectional clinical study (University Hospital Bonn; COI 2023;27:797–805, published online 10 Jan 2023) compared time-dependent tooth mobility between 28 untreated periodontitis patients and 20 periodontally healthy patients (25 F / 26 M, ages 20–81 y; 68 target teeth: 28 periodontitis + 40 healthy maxillary/mandibular central or lateral incisors). Mobility was measured in vivo with the intraoral loading device (ILD) — an individualized-splint computerized force/deflection system — by displacing the tooth orally to a maximum of 0.2 mm over loading durations of 0.5 s, 1 s, and 10 s; the maximum force (Fmax, N/mm) from each force/deflection curve defined the TM-ILD value. Periodontitis teeth showed mean BOP 36.9±32.8% (vs 1.7–2.6% in health) and maximum CAL loss 4–15 mm, maximum PPD 10 mm.

Irrespective of loading time, healthy maxillary incisors showed similar Fmax (0.5 s: 16.3±6.3; 1 s: 15.4±6.6; 10 s: 16.3±6.3 N/mm), whereas periodontally compromised teeth had lower Fmax overall and a further decline with longer loading (12.3±5.2 / 12.4±4.6 / 10.1±5.1 N/mm). Between-group differences for identical durations were 3.9 (0.5 s; 95% CI 1.2–6.7; p=0.024), 3.1 (1 s; CI 1.4–6.0; p=0.067, ns), and 2.8 (10 s; CI 0.2–5.3; p=0.001) — significant at 0.5 s and 10 s. Fmax correlated inversely with clinical attachment loss at all durations (Spearman −0.47 / −0.57 / −0.47) and with BOP at 0.5 s (−0.52). Repeat-measurement reproducibility was good (Spearman 0.68–0.79). The authors conclude that time-dependent tooth displacement measured with the ILD is associated with attachment loss and bleeding on probing, making TM-ILD a candidate objective monitor of periodontal disease — reflecting qualitative (inflammation, BOP) and quantitative (CAL loss) disease parameters.

## Key Contributions

- First clinical application of the Bonn ILD (intraoral loading device — same group as [[orthodontics/keilig-2016-in-vivo-measurements-pdl]]) to distinguish periodontal health from disease via biomechanics: significantly higher mobility (lower Fmax) in untreated periodontitis teeth.
- Demonstrated that longer loading durations (10 s) further reduce Fmax in periodontitis-affected teeth only — a time-dependence signature of compromised PDL biomechanics.
- Established correlations between TM-ILD and clinical parameters: CAL (all durations, r ≈ −0.5) and BOP (0.5 s, r = −0.52) — objective mobility measurement tracks both bone/attachment loss and inflammation.
- Reproducibility data (Spearman 0.68–0.79) support ILD as a practical chairside/biomechanical monitor, potentially addressing the historical difficulty of diagnosing periodontal tissue damage (e.g., occlusal trauma) non-invasively.

## Methodology

Cross-sectional comparative clinical study; 28 untreated periodontitis + 20 healthy patients; 68 incisors (central/lateral, maxillary and mandibular). Periodontitis defined per Papapanou et al. classification with CAL loss 4–15 mm. ILD: individualized splints; displacement 0.2 mm oral direction; loading durations 0.5/1/10 s; Fmax extracted from force/deflection curves (TM-ILD, N/mm). BOP assessed dichotomously at 6 sites/tooth (positive within 15 s). Statistics: T-tests for group differences (null hypothesis: no difference in TM-ILD), Spearman rank correlations (TM-ILD vs CAL, BOP; repeated-measurement reproducibility). Published in Clinical Oral Investigations 2023;27:797–805 (DOI 10.1007/s00784-023-04859-w).

## Results

| Fmax (N/mm) | Healthy (n=40 teeth) | Periodontitis (n=28 teeth) | Difference (95% CI) | p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.5 s loading | 16.3±6.3 | 12.3±5.2 | 3.9 (1.2–6.7) | 0.024 |
| 1.0 s loading | 15.4±6.6 | 12.4±4.6 | 3.1 (1.4–6.0) | 0.067 |
| 10 s loading | 16.3±6.3 | 10.1±5.1 | 2.8 (0.2–5.3) | 0.001 |

- Healthy maxillary incisors: Fmax similar across loading durations (≈15.4–16.3 N/mm) — force-independent of time.
- Periodontitis teeth: Fmax lower at every duration and decreasing with longer loading (12.3→10.1 N/mm) — time-dependent mobility signature.
- Correlations (Spearman): attachment loss r = −0.47 (0.5 s), −0.57 (1 s), −0.47 (10 s); BOP r = −0.52 (0.5 s only, significant).
- Reproducibility: repeated-measurement Spearman 0.68–0.79 (all p<0.0001).
- Healthy maxillary vs mandibular incisors differed at 10 s (p=0.011, CI 0.7–4.5).

## Clinical Takeaways

- Tooth mobility is not uniform: it is load-rate–dependent in disease — shorter (0.5 s) and longer (10 s) loading both separated groups best; the time-dependence itself (Fmax drop at 10 s) is a disease marker.
- TM-ILD gives a chairside-quantitative mobility value that parallels CAL (≈−0.5 correlation): useful for monitoring attachment loss progression without repeated probing.
- BOP correlation at fast loading (0.5 s) links acute inflammation to biomechanical response — mobility reflects inflammatory state, not just bone loss.
- Interpretation: lower Fmax = higher mobility; periodontitis teeth are ~20–40% more mobile under the same displacement protocol.

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional design — no longitudinal validation of TM-ILD as a progression/response predictor.
- 68 teeth, two groups, single center (Bonn); incisors only — canine/premolar/molar biomechanics not covered.
- Untreated periodontitis cohort (no interventional comparison); CAL/BOP as reference standards are examiner-dependent.
- Spearman correlations moderate (−0.47 to −0.57) — TM-ILD accounts for a fraction of variability; not a standalone diagnostic.
