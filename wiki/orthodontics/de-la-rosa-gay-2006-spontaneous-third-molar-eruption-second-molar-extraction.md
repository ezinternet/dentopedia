## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study (48 patients, 128 extracted second molars, ages 11–23) using pre- and post-eruption panoramic radiographs to quantify spontaneous third-molar eruption success after orthodontic second-molar extraction; success defined as proximal contact with adjacent first molar and inter-tooth angle ≤35°; median follow-up 3–4 years.
Maxillary third molars erupted successfully in 96.2% of cases; mandibular third molars in 66.2%; mandibular failures were predominantly caused by excessive mesial tilting or absent proximal contact; both jaws showed worse outcomes at higher Nolla developmental stage and older patient age.
Maxillary second-molar extraction for orthodontic purposes reliably achieves third-molar replacement; mandibular outcomes are acceptable but less predictable, particularly when Nolla stage exceeds 8 or patients are older at extraction.

## 세줄요약

교정 목적의 제2대구치 (second molar) 발거 후 자발적인 제3대구치 (third molar) 맹출을 파노라마로 추적한 후향적 코호트 연구(환자 48명, 발거 치아 128개, 연령 11–23세); 성공 기준: 제1대구치와의 인접 접촉 형성 + 두 치아간 각도 ≤35°; 중앙 추적 기간 3–4년.
상악 제3대구치 성공 맹출률 96.2%, 하악 제3대구치 (mandibular third molar) 66.2%; 하악 실패 원인은 주로 과도한 근심 경사 또는 인접 접촉 불형성이었으며, 높은 놀라 발육 단계 (Nolla developmental stage)와 고령에서 실패율 증가.
교정 목적의 상악 제2대구치 발거는 제3대구치로의 신뢰성 있는 대체를 기대할 수 있으나, 하악은 예측성 낮음 — 특히 Nolla 8단계 초과 또는 고령 환자에서는 근심 경사 실패 위험을 고려해 발거 시기 계획이 필요하다.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort from the Gay-Escoda group at the University of Barcelona analyzed 48 orthodontic patients who had 128 second molars extracted during or before orthodontic treatment (age 11–23 at extraction). Panoramic radiographs taken before extraction and after third-molar eruption were used to classify each erupted third molar as successful (proximal contact with first molar + inter-tooth angle ≤35°) or unsuccessful. Median time to eruption was 3–4 years. Maxillary third molars achieved a 96.2% success rate, uprighting and replacing their second molars in nearly all cases; when the Nolla developmental stage exceeded 8 at extraction, proximal contact risked remaining open. Mandibular third molars succeeded in 66.2%, with failures attributed to excessive mesial tilting or failure to establish proximal contact; unsuccessful eruptions occurred predominantly in older patients with higher Nolla developmental stages. The findings support orthodontic second-molar extraction as a viable strategy when third-molar replacement is anticipated, provided patient age and developmental stage are optimized.

## Key Contributions

- Quantified eruption success rates: 96.2% maxillary, 66.2% mandibular third molars after orthodontic second-molar extraction
- Established a reproducible radiographic success criterion: proximal contact with adjacent first molar AND inter-tooth angle ≤35°
- Identified Nolla developmental stage >8 as a predictor of open proximal contact (maxillary) and eruption failure (mandibular)
- Showed older patient age at extraction independently predicts unsuccessful third-molar eruption
- Defined predominant mandibular failure mode: excessive mesial tilting and/or absent proximal contact

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Retrospective cohort |
| Sample | 48 patients; 128 extracted second molars |
| Age at extraction | 11–23 years |
| Inclusion context | Orthodontic treatment (during or before) |
| Imaging | Panoramic radiographs — pre-extraction and post-eruption |
| Success criterion | Proximal contact with adjacent first molar + inter-tooth angle ≤35° |
| Median follow-up | 3–4 years (IQR 2 years) |
| Key predictors | Nolla developmental stage; patient age at extraction |

Radiographic reproducibility was anchored to Larheim & Svanaes (1986) methodology for rotational panoramic angular/linear measurements.

## Results

### Eruption Success by Jaw

| Jaw | Successful eruption | Primary failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Maxillary third molar | **96.2%** | Open proximal contact (Nolla stage >8) |
| Mandibular third molar | **66.2%** | Excessive mesial tilting; absent proximal contact |

### Predictors of Failure (Both Jaws)

- **Nolla developmental stage**: Higher stage at extraction → greater risk of unsuccessful eruption or open contact
- **Patient age**: Older patients at extraction → higher incidence of unsuccessful eruption
- Maxillary risk: Nolla stage >8 specifically associated with open proximal contact post-eruption
- Mandibular risk: Older patients with higher Nolla stages most susceptible to mesial tilting or contact failure

### Qualitative Findings

- Most maxillary and mandibular third molars uprighted spontaneously and replaced the second molars in acceptable position
- The maxillary arch showed more reliable third-molar replacement than the mandibular arch
- Mandibular failures were mechanically explained by residual mesial inclination from the space formerly occupied by the second molar
