## Three-line Summary

Tissue Engineering Part B Reviews narrative review (Chang & Liu 2021, Texas A&M College of Dentistry) providing a hierarchical, six-level structural map of cortical bone osteons: collagen fibril → lamellar unit → osteon → cortex → organ. The osteon's Haversian canal, concentric lamellae, cement line, and lacunocanalicular network collectively govern mechanical toughness through crack deflection and fluid-shear mechanosensing. BMU-mediated secondary osteon formation (osteoclast tunneling → osteoblast lamellar deposition → mineralization) takes 4–6 months per cycle; ~2–5% cortical annual turnover in healthy adults, rising to 10–15% with aging.

## Summary

Cortical bone comprises approximately 80% of skeletal mass but is relatively understudied in the regeneration literature compared to trabecular bone. Chang and Liu (2021) provide a systematic review of the osteon — the basic structural and functional unit of cortical bone — covering its hierarchical architecture, cellular turnover mechanisms, and bioengineering regeneration strategies.

The hierarchical structure progresses from the nanoscale (type I collagen fibrils + hydroxyapatite crystals) through the lamellar unit (3–7 μm thick concentric sheets of aligned collagen) to the secondary osteon (100–300 μm diameter cylinder) containing a central Haversian canal (20–100 μm), surrounded by 10–30 lamellae and bounded by a cement line. The lacunocanalicular network (LCN), comprising osteocyte lacunae and canaliculi, serves as the bone's mechanosensing organ — fluid shear stress from loading travels through canaliculi to osteocyte cell bodies, initiating RANKL/OPG signal cascades that regulate BMU activation.

BMU (Basic Multicellular Unit) remodeling cycles drive the conversion from woven bone (primary, disorganized) to lamellar bone (secondary, organized): osteoclast cone advances 3–4 mm/day, followed by osteoblast concentric lamellar deposition; full secondary osteon formation requires 4–6 months. Cement lines (collagen-poor, highly mineralized) demarcate the periphery of each secondary osteon and act as crack-deflection barriers, contributing to cortical bone fracture toughness. Aging reduces LCN connectivity, increases osteon density, and accumulates hypermineralized interstitial lamellae — collectively degrading toughness and adaptive remodeling capacity.

## Key Contributions

- Maps six hierarchical levels of cortical bone structure with quantitative parameters (osteon diameter, lamellar thickness, LCN density)
- Identifies cement line as primary crack-deflection structure — explains why cortical bone resists crack propagation better than synthetic HA ceramics
- Establishes LCN mechanosensing as the mechanism linking mechanical loading to BMU activation (Wolff's Law cellular basis)
- Distinguishes primary osteons (modeling) from secondary osteons (remodeling) and interstitial lamellae (oldest, most mineralized remnants)
- Highlights cortical bone as a regeneration priority gap — current scaffold approaches achieve density but not organized osteon architecture

## Methodology

Narrative/systematic review. Literature synthesized across three domains: ① osteon structure and function, ② osteon modeling/remodeling (BMU cellular biology), ③ bioengineering regeneration strategies (electrospinning, 3D printing, decellularized scaffolds). Based on histological, SEM, synchrotron micro-CT, and in vitro/in vivo studies from the literature.

## Results

**Structural parameters:**
- Secondary osteon diameter: 100–300 μm; Haversian canal: 20–100 μm
- Lamellae per osteon: 10–30; lamellar thickness: 3–7 μm
- Cortical bone porosity: 5–10% (Haversian + Volkmann canals)
- Osteocyte density: ~25,000/mm³; LCN canalicular density: ~100/mm²

**Turnover:**
- BMU osteoclast advance rate: 3–4 mm/day
- BMU cycle duration: 4–6 months
- Annual cortical turnover: ~2–5% young adult; ~10–15% aged bone

**Aging effects:**
- Increased secondary osteon density (more remodeling events)
- Decreased LCN connectivity → impaired mechanosensing → reduced adaptive bone response
- Increased hypermineralized interstitial lamellae → decreased toughness
