## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science and Google Scholar searched to 30 November 2024; 1104 records screened, 19 in-vitro studies eligible; methodological quality appraised with the QUIN tool) of photobiomodulation (PBM) effects on osteogenic differentiation of oral- and adipose-derived human mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs).

PBM enhanced proliferation and osteogenic differentiation, most consistently in periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs), dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs) and adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs); wavelengths of 660–1064 nm and energy densities of approximately 2–6 J/cm² gave the most consistent statistically significant osteogenic responses, with ALP, RUNX2 and OCN upregulated and enhanced mineralization, mechanistically via Wnt/β-catenin, BMP/Smad and PI3K/Akt signalling activation.

The clinical value is a parameter starting point rather than a protocol — methodological heterogeneity, medium risk of bias in some studies from incomplete reporting, and the complete absence of translational or clinical data mean standardized protocols and further studies are needed (abstract-only ingest).

## Summary

This systematic review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pdpdt.2025.105238); PMID 41052564) asks a bench-to-clinic question about a specific tool: does photobiomodulation (PBM), a non-invasive light-based therapy, actually push oral- and adipose-derived human mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) toward osteogenesis, and under which irradiation parameters? The review pooled 19 eligible in-vitro studies out of 1104 records identified in PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science and Google Scholar (search closed 30 November 2024), appraising each with the QUIN tool for methodological quality and risk of bias. No meta-analysis was performed — heterogeneity in study designs and outcome measures precluded pooling — so the synthesis is a parameter-mapping exercise rather than an effect estimate.

The direction of the effect was consistent across the included literature: PBM enhanced both proliferation and osteogenic differentiation, with the responses most reliably reported in periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs), dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs) and adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs). On the dosing side, wavelengths associated with statistically significant osteogenic outcomes ranged from 660 to 1064 nm, and energy densities of approximately 2–6 J/cm² produced the most consistent responses; readout markers (ALP, RUNX2, OCN) were upregulated and mineralization increased, with Wnt/β-catenin, BMP/Smad and PI3K/Akt signalling implicated mechanistically.

Read it as a protocol starting point, not as clinical evidence. QUIN rated most included studies at low risk of bias, but some at medium risk because of incomplete reporting; there is no in vivo arm in the pooled set; and the authors themselves frame the conclusion as "promising adjunctive" while explicitly calling for standardized protocols plus translational and clinical studies to validate utility in regenerative dentistry and maxillofacial applications. Because only the abstract was retrievable (closed access; no OA copy via PMC, Unpaywall or OpenAlex), per-study parameter tables, effect sizes and p-values are not reproduced on this page.

## Key Contributions

- **First dedicated synthesis of PBM and osteogenic differentiation of oral- and adipose-derived human MSCs**, with irradiation parameters extracted as a standalone variable rather than background detail.
- **Pins the useful dosing zone**: wavelengths of 660–1064 nm and energy densities of approximately 2–6 J/cm² are the range associated with statistically significant osteogenic outcomes and the most consistent responses.
- **Ranks cell sources**: PDLSCs, DPSCs and ADSCs respond most consistently — directly relevant to regenerative dentistry, where oral-derived progenitors are the accessible cell source.
- **Connects the readout to mechanism**: Wnt/β-catenin, BMP/Smad and PI3K/Akt activation alongside upregulation of ALP, RUNX2 and OCN and enhanced mineralization.
- **Quality-appraised at the review level**: QUIN assessment — low risk of bias in most studies, medium risk in some due to incomplete reporting.

## Methodology

Systematic review of in-vitro studies, **no meta-analysis**. Databases: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar; search up to 30 November 2024. 1104 records identified → 19 studies met eligibility criteria. Methodological quality and risk of bias appraised with the QUIN tool (in-vitro dental research quality instrument). Extraction covered cell source, laser parameters and osteogenic outcomes. Outcomes: proliferation, osteogenic differentiation (ALP, RUNX2, OCN), mineralization, signalling-pathway activation. Evidence level `sr` (systematic review without meta-analysis); abstract-only ingest.
