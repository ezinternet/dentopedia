## Three-line Summary

Micron review (Franchi et al. 2005, University of Bologna) providing the detailed histological sequence of peri-implant osteogenesis: blood clot → afibrillar calcified layer (day 1) → woven bone (days 3–7) → reparative trabecular bone (weeks 1–2) → lamellar bone (weeks 6–12), with both contact osteogenesis (implant surface outward) and distant osteogenesis (host bone inward) occurring simultaneously. Osteoporosis prolongs the woven bone phase and impairs lamellar conversion, mechanistically explaining higher failure rates in osteoporotic patients. The final lamellar BIC persists under continuous remodeling turnover in the 1 mm peri-implant zone throughout life.

## Summary

This narrative review from the University of Bologna synthesizes the histological evidence for the sequence of bone types formed around titanium endosseous dental implants, from initial implant insertion to mature osseointegration, comparing healthy and osteoporotic healing trajectories.

The implant wound initiates a stereotyped healing response: the implant cavity acts as a bone wound, with blood clot and fibrin as the initial matrix. Within 24 hours, mesenchymal cells and pre-osteoblasts adhere to the implant surface via an afibrillar calcified layer (comparable to the lamina limitans or incremental lines in bone), which can form even in vitro on implant surfaces. This layer precedes collagen deposition and represents the earliest implant–tissue interface.

Between days 3–7, woven bone forms de novo immediately adjacent to the implant surface (contact osteogenesis) and advancing from the host bone margins (distant osteogenesis). This rapidly-deposited, disorganized-collagen woven bone evolves by weeks 1–2 into reparative trabecular bone with large, vascularized marrow spaces populated by mesenchymal cells — the biologically active scaffold for subsequent remodeling. By weeks 6–12, lamellar bone progressively replaces the trabecular matrix; mature osseointegration corresponds to lamellar bone-to-implant contact. A continuous remodeling zone persists within ~1 mm of the implant surface throughout life — transiently exposing portions of the implant during BMU cycles.

Osteoporosis (both senile and postmenopausal) impairs this trajectory: reduced osteoblast differentiation and activity prolongs the woven bone phase, delays lamellar conversion, and reduces final BIC values, mechanistically explaining the clinical observation of higher implant failure rates in osteoporotic patients.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes the five-stage bone type sequence around implants with specific timeframes (afibrillar layer → woven → trabecular → lamellar)
- Differentiates contact vs distant osteogenesis and establishes their coexistence as the mechanistic basis for BIC improvement with micro-rough surfaces
- Quantifies mature lamellar BIC as not a static endpoint — ongoing 1 mm peri-implant remodeling means the implant surface is periodically re-exposed during BMU cycles
- Identifies osteoporosis as a disease of impaired woven-to-lamellar conversion, not merely of reduced bone mass
- Afibrillar calcified layer as a previously underappreciated initial interface — formed within 1 day, before any osteoid deposition

## Methodology

Narrative review of animal (rabbit, rat, sheep) and human histological studies using SEM, backscattered electron imaging, confocal microscopy with fluorescent bone labels (calcein, alizarin), and light microscopy. Titanium implants with various micro-rough surfaces. Both healthy and osteoporotic models (ovariectomized rodents and rabbits; postmenopausal human data where available).

## Results

**Timeline (healthy bone):**
- Day 0: Blood clot, fibrin, platelet activation
- Day 1: Afibrillar calcified layer on implant surface; osteoblast precursor adhesion
- Days 1–3: Osteoid collagen fibril deposition
- Days 3–7: Woven bone (contact + distant osteogenesis)
- Weeks 1–2: Reparative trabecular bone with vascular marrow spaces
- Weeks 6–12: Progressive lamellar bone replacement
- Mature state: Lamellar BIC + ongoing peri-implant remodeling within 1 mm

**Osteoporosis vs healthy (qualitative):**
- Delayed osteoblast differentiation → later woven bone onset
- Prolonged woven bone phase → lower BIC at 6 weeks
- Reduced final lamellar BIC compared to healthy controls
- Lower trabecular bone volume at implant site → reduced primary stability at insertion
