## Three-line Summary
2015 narrative review of the structure and function of the four bone cell types and the three-phase BMU-driven remodeling cycle.
Osteocytes are the most abundant and long-lived bone cells, acting as lacunocanalicular mechanosensors and remodeling orchestrators while RANKL/RANK/OPG controls osteoclastogenesis.
Bone is also an endocrine organ — undercarboxylated osteocalcin and osteocyte FGF23 act systemically — and resorption/formation imbalance produces osteoporosis or osteopetrosis.

## Summary
This 2015 narrative review (BioMed Research International, Article ID 421746) synthesizes the structural, molecular and functional biology of bone tissue, organized around four cell types — osteoblasts, bone lining cells, osteocytes and osteoclasts — and the three-phase basic multicellular unit (BMU) remodeling cycle of resorption, reversal and formation. It traces osteoblast differentiation from mesenchymal stem cells (Runx2, Dlx5, osterix) through matrix deposition and two-phase mineralization via matrix vesicles, and describes bone lining cells as quiescent osteoblasts that produce OPG and RANKL. Osteocytes, comprising 90–95% of bone cells with a lifespan up to 25 years, are presented as the key mechanosensors and orchestrators of remodeling: their lacunocanalicular network transduces mechanical stimuli into biochemical signals (piezoelectric effect), and their apoptosis provides a chemotactic signal for osteoclast recruitment. Osteoclastogenesis is governed by the RANKL/RANK/OPG system with M-CSF, NFATc1 and cathepsin K, and coupling between resorption and formation involves semaphorins and ephrins. Finally, the review frames bone as an endocrine organ — undercarboxylated osteocalcin acts on pancreas, adipose tissue, testis and brain, and osteocyte-derived FGF23 regulates phosphate — and ties remodeling imbalance to osteoporosis and osteopetrosis.

## Key Contributions
- Canonical map of bone's four cell types and their roles in locomotion, support/protection, mineral storage and marrow harboring.
- Three-phase BMU remodeling framework: osteoclast resorption → reversal → osteoblast formation, with bone lining cells contributing to coupling.
- Osteoblast lineage and matrix biology: Runx2 master gene, Dlx5/Osx, ALP-positive preosteoblasts, type I collagen and noncollagen proteins, and vesicular/fibrillar mineralization to hydroxyapatite.
- Osteocyte biology: 90–95% of cells, up to 25-year lifespan, four maturation stages, DMP1/sclerostin markers, up to 50 processes per cell, and a lacunocanalicular surface estimated 400-fold larger than the Haversian/Volkmann systems.
- Osteocyte mechanosensing and remodeling orchestration via PGE₂, PGI₂, NO, IGF-1 (loading) and sclerostin, DKK-1, RANKL (unloading); sclerostin antagonizes Lrp5 in Wnt/β-catenin signaling.
- RANKL/RANK/OPG as the key osteoclastogenesis axis (with M-CSF/cFMS, NFATc1, DC-STAMP, TRAP, cathepsin K) and four osteoclast membrane domains.
- Endocrine functions of bone: undercarboxylated osteocalcin effects on pancreas, adipose, testis and brain; osteocyte FGF23 regulation of phosphate; the emergence of osteoimmunology.

## Methodology
Narrative (non-systematic) review of current literature on bone cell biology and the local and systemic factors influencing remodeling, illustrated by light and electron micrographs of rat alveolar bone (Figures 1–5) and a schematic of the RANKL/RANK/OPG system. No cohort, no patients and no reported n; qualitative synthesis of in vitro and in vivo findings with no quantitative endpoints or effect sizes.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Osteoblast share of resident bone cells | 4–6% |
| Osteocyte share of bone cells / lifespan | 90–95%; up to 25 years |
| Osteocyte cytoplasmic processes | Up to 50 per cell, within the lacunocanalicular system |
| Osteocyte surface area | 400-fold larger than Haversian + Volkmann systems; >100-fold larger than trabecular surface |
| Matrix vesicle diameter | 30–200 nm |
| Osteocyte maturation stages | Osteoid-osteocyte, preosteocyte, young osteocyte, mature osteocyte |
| Mature osteocyte marker shift | Down: OCN, BSP II, collagen I, ALP; up: DMP1, sclerostin |
| Mechanical loading response | Anabolic factors PGE₂, PGI₂, NO, IGF-1 |
| Mechanical unloading response | Sclerostin and DKK-1 (osteoblast inhibitors) plus local osteoclastogenesis factors |
| Remodeling imbalance | Excess resorption → osteoporosis; excess formation → osteopetrosis |
| Site difference in osteoclastogenesis | Long-bone marrow osteoclasts form faster than jaw osteoclasts |
| Endocrine actions | Undercarboxylated osteocalcin: insulin secretion/sensitivity, β-cell proliferation, adiponectin, testosterone, hippocampal monoamines/GABA; FGF23 lowers circulating phosphate |
