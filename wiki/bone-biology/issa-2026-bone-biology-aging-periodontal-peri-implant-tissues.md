## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Periodontology 2000, Univ. of Pittsburgh) that organizes age-related bone biology as a framework linking periodontal disease, impaired healing, and peri-implant bone in older adults (PubMed search, no meta-analysis).

Aging degrades bone through matrix change (AGE crosslinks up to 10× higher, ~80% strength loss age 20→80), inflammaging + cellular senescence/SASP (3× more senescent cells in old mice; SA-β-gal+ periodontal ligament cells drive horizontal bone loss), immunosenescence (blunted neutrophil phagocytosis/NETs, M1-shifted macrophages, Th1/Th17 expansion), osteocyte TGF-β dysregulation, and osteoprogenitor (SSC/MSC) exhaustion with an osteogenesis→adipogenesis shift (↑RANKL, ↓OPG); parabiosis/chimera experiments show the aging immune milieu itself impairs bone repair.

Increased age alone is NOT a contraindication to implants or regeneration, but osteoporosis effects on peri-implant bone are unresolved and the aging effect on alveolar-bone healing/osseointegration specifically is largely unstudied — the translational rejuvenation targets (senolytics/metformin/rapamycin/PTH/melatonin/aspirin) remain preclinical.

노화는 기질(AGE 가교 최대 10배↑, 20→80세 강도 약 80%↓), inflammaging + 세포노화/SASP(노령 마우스 senescent 세포 3배, SA-β-gal+ 치주인대세포가 수평 골흡수 유발), 면역노화(중성구 탐식·NET 저하, M1 대식세포, Th1/Th17 확장), 골세포 TGF-β 신호 교란, 골전구세포(SSC/MSC) 고갈로 골형성→지방생성 편향(RANKL↑, OPG↓)을 통해 골을 악화. parabiosis/키메라 실험으로 노령 면역환경 자체가 골재생을 저해함을 확인.

## Summary

This Periodontology 2000 review takes the **hallmarks-of-aging** paradigm and applies it to **alveolar and craniofacial bone**, arguing that the same age-related biology that raises fracture risk in the orthopedic skeleton also (1) heightens **periodontal disease (PD)** susceptibility, (2) impairs **bone healing and regeneration**, and (3) may compromise **peri-implant bone** — all in the exact population that most often receives implants. PD is reframed as an age-related chronic inflammatory condition (47% of US adults; 68% of those ≥65).

The clinical framing is deliberately balanced: across the cited cohorts (Table 1), several found **no significant difference in implant survival by age**, while others reported an **age-related rise in late failure** tied to impaired remodeling. Osteoporosis-vs-implant evidence (Table 2) is likewise **mixed** — some studies show no effect, others show increased crestal bone loss/failure. The authors' conclusion: **age alone is not a contraindication**, but case selection and management matter, and the peri-implant-specific aging biology is largely unstudied.

## Methodology

Non-systematic narrative review; PubMed search on age-related pathological processes in bone relevant to peri-implant and periodontal disease. Evidence is heavily rodent-mechanistic (aged-mouse periodontitis, ovariectomized rats, fracture-healing **chimera** and **parabiosis** models) supplemented by human observational studies for the implant/osteoporosis tables. No risk-of-bias appraisal and no pooled estimates.

## Results

**1. Bone matrix & mineral.** Bone strength drops up to **~80% from age 20 to 80**. Age raises **non-enzymatic collagen crosslinking via advanced glycation end-products (AGEs)** — content **up to 10× higher** in older bone; AGEs crosslink mid-fibril, restrict deformation, reduce toughness and impair loading capacity (worse in diabetes; relevant to occlusal/implant load). Hydroxyapatite carbonate-for-phosphate substitution and crystallinity changes predict lower stiffness. **Osteoporosis**: alveolar BMD is not DXA-measurable (heterogeneous tissue), so surrogates (mandibular cortical width, densitometry) that correlate with axial BMD are used; systemic BMD associates with alveolar crest height and CAL, but the **2017 World Workshop** held osteoporosis associated with alveolar bone loss yet **not with other PD parameters**.

**2. Osteoimmunology / inflammaging.** **Inflammaging** = chronic low-grade inflammation (↑IL-6, CRP, IFN-γ, TNF-α). **Cellular senescence + SASP** (cytokines, prostaglandins, ROS) mirror those mediators; apoptosis-resistant senescent cells accumulate (**~3× more in old mice**, p16^Ink4a-marked), and clearing p16+ cells extends healthy lifespan in mice. **SA-β-gal+ senescent periodontal ligament cells accumulate in aged periodontium and associate with horizontal bone resorption.** **Immunosenescence**: neutrophils lose phagocytosis and NET formation; macrophages skew **M1** with a distinct age transcriptional signature during healing; thymic involution reduces naïve T cells; CD4+ bias **Th1>Th2** and **Th17 expansion** promotes osteoclast activity. Older adults mount a **more severe gingival inflammatory response at equal plaque**. Crucially, **chimera** (young marrow→old mouse) and **parabiosis** (shared young circulation) both **rescue fracture healing in old mice via β-catenin modulation**, proving the aging immune milieu itself is detrimental to repair — but the peri-implant equivalent is unstudied.

**3. Aging bone cells.** Old vs young bone shows **↓RUNX2/OPN/OC**, **↑osteoclastogenesis (↑RANKL, ↓OPG)**, and ~10× higher p16^Ink4a/p21^Cip1 across osteoblasts/osteoclasts/osteocytes. **Osteocyte aging disrupts resorption–formation coupling through TGF-β–dependent signaling**: ↓perilacunar/canalicular remodeling genes (Mmp13, Acp5, Ctsk), ↓toughness, ↑brittleness; an osteocyte-specific TGF-β receptor knockout reproduces the aged phenotype in young mice.

**4. Osteoprogenitor (SSC/MSC) aging — the review's emphasis.** Aged osteoprogenitors show ↑ROS, mitochondrial degradation, DNA damage, telomere shortening, and senescence (↑p16^INK4a, SA-β-gal, ↑SASP), producing a **decline in osteogenic differentiation (↓RUNX2, ALP, BSP, OPN, OC) and a shift toward adipogenesis (↑aP2/FABP4, ↑PPAR-γ)** — i.e., marrow fat accumulating at the expense of bone. Young serum/marrow can rejuvenate aged MSCs; the aged marrow environment biases even young MSCs toward adipogenesis.

**5. Translational targets (preclinical, Table 3).** Environmental (young serum; hypoxia/HIF-1→RUNX2/BMP-9/Wnt), hormonal (17β-estradiol reverses SSC impairment; PTH/PTHG2 → osteoblast over adipogenic differentiation via cAMP-PKA; melatonin → p38-MAPK/ERK/Wnt, ↑bone-to-implant contact in animals, ↓crestal bone loss in humans in one meta-analysis), natural compounds (celastrol, resveratrol → ↓marrow adipocytes, ↓peri-implant bone loss in diabetic rats), drugs (metformin → AMPK/autophagy, ↑osseointegration in OVX rats; aspirin → ↑RUNX2/ALP, ↑periodontal regeneration; rapamycin → autophagy, reverses PD-induced alveolar bone loss in aged mice; simvastatin → ↓adipogenesis, ↑osseointegration interface), BDNF, H₂S (Ca²⁺-GSK3β/β-catenin), autophagy (Optn), epigenetic (KDM4B, HDAC6), and anti-miR-155-5p — nearly all in vitro or animal, with no established human efficacy for aged alveolar bone or osseointegration.

**Bottom line.** Aging is a multi-level (matrix → immune → cellular → stem-cell) degradation of alveolar bone that plausibly links PD susceptibility, delayed healing, and peri-implant risk; but it is **not a contraindication**, and the specific peri-implant/osseointegration aging biology remains an open translational frontier.
