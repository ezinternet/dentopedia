## Summary
This Japanese animal study used spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHR, with sympathicotonia) undergoing orthodontic tooth movement to compare β1-, β2-, and non-selective β-adrenergic receptor blockers on alveolar bone and RANKL/sclerostin expression. All three β-AR blockers consistently inhibited tooth movement, increased maxillary alveolar bone volume, reduced osteoclast activity on the compression side, and decreased RANKL and sclerostin (SOST) expression in osteocytes. The dental-context demonstration of sympathetic → RANKL → bone resorption regulation provides mechanistic grounding for why beta-blockers might benefit peri-implant bone in hypertensive patients with elevated sympathetic tone.

## Key Contributions
- First direct comparison of selective (β1, β2) vs non-selective β-AR blockers on alveolar bone in dental context
- All three blockers equally protective on alveolar bone → not β-receptor subtype specific
- RANKL and sclerostin simultaneously downregulated → dual bone-protective signal (inhibit resorption + preserve formation)
- SHR model relevance: hypertensive patients with sympathicotonia may especially benefit from β-blockers for bone
- Mechanistic dental-context link in Huang 2024 βAR → RANKL pathway

## Methodology
SHR rat model (spontaneously hypertensive, sympathicotonia). n=6/group: control, atenolol 0.1 mg/kg/day, butoxamine 1 mg/kg/day, propranolol 1 mg/kg/day. Orthodontic force via closed-coil spring. Outcomes: tooth movement distance, alveolar bone volume (histology), osteoclast activity, RANKL and SOST immunohistochemistry (osteocytes, compression side).

## Results
All β-AR blockers vs SHR control: ↓ tooth movement, ↑ alveolar bone volume, ↓ osteoclast activity, ↓ RANKL+ osteocytes, ↓ SOST+ osteocytes (all on compression side). No significant between-drug differences reported.
