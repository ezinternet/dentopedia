## Three-line Summary
Prospective comparative evaluation (BMC Oral Health 2026; 38 standardized zygomatic implantology questions developed by 4 OMS expert surgeons; 5 calibrated independent raters; ICC 0.86–0.91 inter-rater reliability) assessing ChatGPT-4o, Claude 3.5, and Gemini 1.5 Pro using DISCERN, GQS, and a 5-point Accuracy Rubric.

No clinically meaningful performance difference among the three models (maximum score gap ≤0.5 points on 5-point scales); Gemini 1.5 Pro scored marginally higher; all models produced readable, clinically relevant content but showed persistent variability in depth and specificity of clinical guidance.

No current LLM is ready for unsupervised specialist clinical decision support; continuous expert validation, version transparency, and domain-specific benchmarking are prerequisites for integration into dental education or clinical practice.

## One-line Summary
Prospective rater-blinded evaluation (38 expert questions, 5 raters) found no clinically meaningful performance difference among ChatGPT-4o, Claude 3.5, and Gemini 1.5 Pro on zygomatic implantology Q&A (max difference ≤0.5 points on 5-point scales).

## Summary
This study prospectively evaluated three leading large language models (LLM)—ChatGPT-4o, Claude 3.5, and Gemini 1.5 Pro—on their ability to answer 38 standardized zygomatic implantology questions developed by four expert oral and maxillofacial surgeons. Five calibrated raters assessed responses using DISCERN, GQS, and a 5-point Accuracy Rubric. Inter-rater reliability was excellent (ICC 0.86–0.91). Gemini 1.5 Pro scored marginally higher on response quality and accuracy, while Claude 3.5 and ChatGPT-4o performed comparably. However, all absolute score differences were ≤0.5 points, indicating no clinically meaningful superiority. All models produced readable, clinically relevant content but showed persistent variability in the depth and specificity of clinical guidance. The authors conclude that continuous validation, version transparency, and expert supervision remain essential before integrating LLMs into dental education or clinical decision-making.

## Key Contributions
- Demonstrates that current top-tier LLMs have similar, moderate—but not expert-level—competency in specialist implantology
- Excellent ICC (0.86–0.91) validates the rater panel methodology for future AI dental benchmarking
- Highlights that absolute score differences among models are clinically negligible despite statistical comparisons
- Establishes precedent for domain-specific expert-question benchmarking in dentistry (vs. generic medical Q&A)

## Methodology
Prospective, rater-blinded comparative evaluation. 38 standardized questions developed by 4 OMS surgeons expert in zygomatic implantology; questions aligned with 2023 ITI Consensus Workshop recommendations. Each LLM response rated independently by 5 calibrated clinicians using DISCERN, GQS, and Accuracy Rubric. Statistics: Kruskal-Wallis + Bonferroni post hoc; Spearman correlation; ICC(2,1) for inter-rater reliability.

## Results
- Gemini 1.5 Pro: marginally highest mean scores (quality and accuracy)
- Claude 3.5 ≈ ChatGPT-4o
- Maximum inter-model difference: ≤0.5 points on 5-point scales
- ICC = 0.86–0.91 (p<0.001) — excellent inter-rater reliability
- All models: readable and clinically relevant output; all showed depth/specificity variability
- Clinical conclusion: No model ready for unsupervised clinical decision support
