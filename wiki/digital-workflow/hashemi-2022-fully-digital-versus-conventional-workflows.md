## Three-line Summary

Prospective crossover clinical trial (n=10 patients, 20 three-unit ISFDPs) at Tehran University compared fully digital workflow (3Shape TRIOS 3 IoS + CAD/CAM zirconia) vs. conventional workflow (PVS open-tray + CoCr cast frameworks) for posterior mandibular implant-supported reconstructions.
Impression accuracy, framework passivity and adaptation, occlusal contact quality, and aesthetics (patient VAS 8.6 vs. 8.4; FDI criteria) showed no significant difference between workflows; total and laboratory fabrication time was significantly shorter with the digital workflow (p<0.001).
Within study limitations, fully digital workflow is clinically equivalent to conventional for posterior three-unit ISFDPs while offering a significant laboratory time advantage.

## Summary

This prospective crossover clinical trial enrolled 10 patients requiring three-unit implant-supported fixed dental prostheses (ISFDPs) in the posterior mandible. Each patient received both a digitally fabricated (IoS + CAD/CAM monolithic zirconia) and conventionally fabricated (PVS impression + CoCr cast framework with porcelain veneers) prosthesis, with impression sequence randomized via envelope technique. A 2 µm-accuracy industrial scanner (Atos Core 5 Mp, GOM) digitized conventional casts and reference models; STL files were superimposed using GOM Inspect best-fit algorithm to compare impression accuracy. Framework passivity was quantified by the flag technique (screw resistance test angle) and the one-screw Sheffield test. Occlusal contact quality was assessed by scanning elastomeric bite records and measuring the 10 smallest contact thicknesses in functional cusp regions. Aesthetics were rated by patients (VAS) and clinicians (FDI criteria).

The null hypothesis was accepted for most outcomes: impression accuracy (linear displacement p=0.506, inter-implant distance p=0.858, rotational displacement p=0.759), framework passivity (anterior p=0.280, posterior p=0.739), fitness (p=0.747), occlusal contact thickness (0.49 vs. 0.60 µm, p=0.487), and all aesthetic parameters (p>0.05). The null hypothesis was partially rejected for fabrication time: total fabrication time (p<0.001) and laboratory time (p<0.001) were significantly shorter with the digital workflow. Clinical chair time (p=0.444) and occlusal adjustment time (p=0.143) did not differ.

## Key Contributions

- Prospective crossover design controls inter-patient variability — each patient served as their own control across both workflows
- Demonstrates clinical equivalence of digital and conventional three-unit ISFDP workflows across accuracy, passivity, occlusion, and aesthetics
- Quantifies significant laboratory time reduction with digital workflow (p<0.001)
- IoS (3Shape TRIOS 3) produced comparable impression accuracy to gold-standard splinted open-tray PVS technique measured against a 2 µm-accuracy reference scanner
- 8/10 digital frameworks met acceptable fitness criteria vs. 10/10 conventional (NS difference) — small signal worth monitoring in future larger trials
- Patient and clinician aesthetics ratings equivalent despite material difference (zirconia vs. CoCr + porcelain)
- Crossover design enabled framework selection based on superior passivity/adaptation for final delivery

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective crossover clinical controlled trial (CONSORT-compliant)
- **n**: 10 patients (7 females, mean age 47.1±11 years); 20 ISFDPs total
- **Setting**: Tehran University of Medical Sciences (Oct 2019–Feb 2021)
- **Digital workflow**: 3Shape TRIOS 3 IoS; Arum scan body (10 Ncm); CAD/CAM monolithic zirconia (Katana translucent, Kuraray); 5-axis mill (Amann Girrbach); Ti-base abutment
- **Conventional workflow**: PVS open-tray (Panasil, Kettenbach); square-shaped impression copings; type IV stone (Herostonel); CoCr frameworks (Cara CoCr-SLM, Kulzer) cast from wax; leucite-reinforced feldspathic porcelain veneering (Vintage PRO, Shofu)
- **Reference model**: Splinted resin impression copings + elastomeric mono-phase impression; verification jig; industrial scan at 2 µm (Atos Core 5 Mp)
- **Accuracy**: STL superimposition best-fit (GOM Inspect v7.5); Δr = √(Δx²+Δy²+Δz²)
- **Passivity**: Flag technique for screw resistance angle (AutoCAD) + one-screw test + periapical radiograph
- **Occlusion**: Futar D elastomeric bite record → industrial scan → 10 smallest contact thickness ≥1 mm
- **Aesthetics**: Patient VAS; clinician FDI criteria (shade, translucency, lustre, anatomical form)
- **Statistics**: Student t-test, Mann–Whitney U, Fisher's exact test, GEE model; α=0.05 (SPSS v24)
- **Power**: Based on Amin et al.; minimum n=8/group; 20% dropout → 10 enrolled
