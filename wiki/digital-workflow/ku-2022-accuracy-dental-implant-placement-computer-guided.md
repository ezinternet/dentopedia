## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort using CBCT superimposition (BMC Oral Health 2022;22:8, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital; 34 patients, 89 implants, mean 17.3 months) to measure how far actual implant positions departed from the virtual plan.

Guided implants deviated 2.69 ± 1.00 mm at the entrance point and 5.52 ± 2.80° in angulation, versus 14.53 ± 6.64 mm and 11.75 ± 4.39° in the 38 implants (42.7%) where the guide was abandoned; within the guided group, flapless was more accurate than flapped (2.22 vs 3.18 mm, P < 0.001; 4.27° vs 6.82°, P = 0.001).

The headline number is not 2.69 mm but 42.7% — in this cohort the printed guide was unusable in nearly half the implants, mostly for insufficient primary stability, so guided-accuracy figures describe a selected subset rather than routine practice.

## Summary

The paper does two useful things that guided-surgery accuracy studies usually avoid: it measures deviation against a postoperative CBCT in a routine clinical cohort rather than a phantom, and it keeps the cases where the guide failed in the analysis instead of excluding them. In the 51 implants actually placed through the guide, mean entrance deviation was 2.69 mm and angular deviation 5.52°; the 38 implants that converted to flap or freehand drifted 14.53 mm and 11.75° from plan, a magnitude that makes "guided" meaningless for those sites. Within the guided subset flapless placement was more accurate and had markedly better primary stability (ISQ 72.7 vs 55.8), but that comparison follows clinical necessity — the flapped subset was younger and had worse bone, so approach and case difficulty are entangled. The practical message is that static guidance should be read as conditional on guide seating and bone engagement; the authors' own drop-out breakdown (19 insufficient stability, 9 anatomic risk, 4 dehiscence, 6 poor fit) is the most transferable part of the paper.

## Key Contributions

- Supplies a real-world guided-accuracy figure (2.69 mm / 5.52°) with postoperative CBCT superimposition rather than phantom or model data.
- Reports the guide-abandonment denominator (38 of 89 implants, 42.7%) that accuracy meta-analyses typically omit, together with the reasons for conversion.
- Shows guided flapless placement achieving both better accuracy and higher primary stability than guided flapped placement in the same cohort.
- Documents a case-mix shift: patients whose guides failed were older (65.8 vs 61.9 years), consistent with poorer bone quality driving both instability and guide failure.
- Confirms short-term safety of even deviated placements — 100% survival with MBL within 0.5 mm at 17.3 months.

## Methodology

**Design.** Retrospective cohort, single centre, all patients planned with CBCT-based computer-guided surgery.

**Groups.** Group 1 flapped guided (n = 25 implants); Group 2 flapless guided (n = 26); drop-out group (n = 38) — planned for the guide but converted to flap/freehand intraoperatively.

**Accuracy measurement.** Postoperative CBCT superimposed on the planned implant position; deviation distance at the entrance point (mm) and angular deviation of the implant axis (°).

**Other outcomes.** Primary stability (ISQ); demographics; short-term survival and marginal bone change.

**Analysis.** Independent t-tests for deviations and ISQ; chi-square for demographics.

## Results

| Group | Entrance deviation (mm) | Angular deviation (°) | Primary ISQ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drop-out (n=38) | 14.53 ± 6.64 | 11.75 ± 4.39 | 66.49 ± 15.18 |
| Guided all (n=51) | 2.69 ± 1.00 | 5.52 ± 2.80 | 64.43 ± 18.26 |
| Flapped (n=25) | 3.18 ± 0.89 | 6.82 ± 2.71 | 55.84 ± 20.47 |
| Flapless (n=26) | 2.22 ± 0.88 | 4.27 ± 2.30 | 72.69 ± 10.97 |

- Flapped vs flapless: distance P < 0.001 (abstract) / P = 0.001 (table), angulation P = 0.001, ISQ P = 0.001.
- Drop-out vs guided: distance and angulation P < 0.001; ISQ P = 0.578 (no difference).

**Cohort.** 89 implants in 34 patients (19 male / 15 female), mean age 62.1 years, all Dentium fixtures; mean follow-up 17.3 months; no failures; marginal bone loss within 0.5 mm.

**Why the guide was abandoned (n=38).** Insufficient primary stability 19; risk of anatomic damage (inferior alveolar canal proximity) 9; bone dehiscence during drilling 4; poor stent fitness 6.

**Demographics.** Drop-out 65.8 ± 9.3 years vs guided 61.9 ± 10.8 (P = 0.076). Within the guided group: flapped 59.7 ± 11.9 vs flapless 63.9 ± 9.4 (P < 0.001) — a confound for the accuracy comparison.

## Limitations

Retrospective, single centre, 34 patients. The flapped/flapless split is by clinical necessity, not randomization, and the two subgroups differ in age and primary stability — so "flapless is more accurate" is not a clean causal claim. The paper's group labeling contradicts itself between abstract and tables, which will trip readers who skim. Deviation measurement blinding is not described. The 42.7% abandonment means the reported accuracy applies to a selected half of the planned cases, and the drop-out deviations are reported descriptively rather than analyzed for predictors. Follow-up addresses survival, not the prosthetic consequences of a 2-3 mm positional error.
