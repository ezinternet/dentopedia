## Three-line Summary
In vitro study (n=45 typodont maxillary incisors, Primescan): post space RMS increases with length — 6 mm: 58 µm, 8 mm: 81 µm, 10 mm: 101 µm. Coronal and middle thirds maintain equivalent accuracy across all lengths; apical third accuracy declines significantly at 10 mm (163 µm vs 96 µm at 6 mm). Post lengths of 6 and 8 mm yield clinically acceptable IOS accuracy comparable to conventional PVS impressions.

## Summary
Almalki et al. prepared 45 typodont maxillary central incisors with standard endodontic access and obturation simulations, then created post spaces at three depths (6, 8, and 10 mm; n=15 per group). Each specimen was scanned directly with Primescan and also impressed conventionally with PVS (light/heavy body), with the PVS impression scanned using an inEos X5a laboratory scanner as reference. RMS deviations were calculated at three cross-sectional levels (coronal, middle, apical thirds) using Geomagic Control X. The coronal and middle thirds showed no significant differences across all post lengths (p>0.5), confirming that the coronal portion of the post channel is reliably scanned at all tested depths. The apical third showed progressive accuracy decline: 10 mm subgroup (163 µm) was significantly worse than 6 mm (96 µm) and 8 mm (131 µm) subgroups (p<0.05). Overall, 6 and 8 mm IOS accuracy was clinically comparable to the conventional PVS reference, while 10 mm showed a clinically meaningful accuracy decrease particularly at the apex.

## Key Contributions
- Clinical threshold established: 6 and 8 mm post spaces → Primescan acceptable alternative to PVS; 10 mm → accuracy declines, conventional preferred
- Thirds-level analysis reveals the apical zone as the accuracy-limiting region — consistent with active-triangulation geometry entering a narrow deep channel
- Confirms depth-dependent accuracy pattern seen in wildhagen-2026 (µ-CT study) using a different methodology (typodont + PVS reference)
- Provides specific RMS benchmarks (58/81/101 µm) for clinical decision thresholds
- Coronal and middle thirds reliably accurate regardless of post length — scanning up to these levels is predictable

## Methodology
In vitro comparative study. N=45 typodont (Frasaco) maxillary central incisors. Standard endodontic access and post space preparation (6, 8, 10 mm; ISO-standardized burs). IOS: CEREC Primescan. Reference: PVS conventional impression (light/heavy body) scanned with inEos X5a lab scanner. Analysis: Geomagic Control X — RMS at coronal, middle, apical thirds. Statistics: One-way ANOVA + Bonferroni post-hoc; α=0.05.

## Results
**Overall RMS by post length:**
- 6 mm: **58 µm** (n=15)
- 8 mm: **81 µm** (n=15)
- 10 mm: **101 µm** (n=15)

**By anatomical third:**
- Coronal and middle: NS across all subgroups (p>0.5)
- Apical — 10 mm: **163 µm** (significantly worse vs 6 mm [96 µm] and 8 mm [131 µm], p<0.05)

**Clinical interpretation:**
- 6 mm and 8 mm: IOS accuracy comparable to PVS → acceptable chairside digital alternative
- 10 mm: accuracy decreases, conventional impression preferred for the apical portion
