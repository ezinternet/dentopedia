## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (n=239) at Peking University First Hospital comparing digital workflow (DWG, intraoral scan + auxiliary occlusal device + CAD/CAM, n=101) vs. conventional workflow (CWG, polyether impression + stone cast, n=138) for posterior monolithic zirconia implant-supported crowns.
DWG significantly outperformed CWG on all occlusal accuracy measures: lower max/mean occlusal deviation, adjustment area, occlusion time ratio (all P<0.001), lower implant crown ROF (P=0.004), fewer crowns needing adjustment (P=0.012), and shorter laboratory time (P<0.001).
Digital workflow incorporating intraoral scanning with an auxiliary occlusal device yields superior occlusal accuracy, more physiologic force distribution, and reduced chairside burden for posterior implant restorations.

## Summary

This retrospective study from Peking University First Hospital enrolled 239 patients receiving single posterior monolithic zirconia implant-supported crowns between July 2020 and July 2025. Patients were divided into a digital workflow group (DWG, n=101) using intraoral scanning with a custom PMMA auxiliary occlusal device (2.0 mm wafer, maximal intercuspal position) and CAD/CAM fabrication, versus a conventional workflow group (CWG, n=138) using polyether impressions and stone casts. Occlusal outcomes were assessed using the T-Scan III system at crown insertion and 6-month follow-up. The DWG demonstrated statistically significant improvements in all measured occlusal parameters: lower maximum and mean occlusal deviation, smaller occlusal adjustment area, lower occlusion time ratio, and reduced relative occlusal force on the implant crown with correspondingly higher ROF on adjacent natural teeth—a more physiologically appropriate loading pattern. Additionally, fewer crowns in the DWG required any chairside occlusal adjustment, and total laboratory time was significantly shorter. The auxiliary occlusal device during intraoral scanning is identified as a critical element enabling accurate interocclusal registration in the digital workflow.

## Key Contributions

- Large clinical cohort (n=239) demonstrating digital workflow superiority in occlusal accuracy over conventional workflow
- Quantifies force distribution benefit: DWG distributes force more physiologically (less on implant crown, more on adjacent natural teeth)
- Auxiliary occlusal device (PMMA wafer) during intraoral scanning validated as effective tool for accurate occlusal registration without stone casts
- Significant time efficiency gain: shorter total laboratory time and impression/scan time with digital workflow
- 6-month follow-up confirms sustained occlusal stability in both groups

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort, single center
- **n**: 239 (DWG n=101, CWG n=138)
- **Setting**: Peking University First Hospital, Beijing, China (July 2020–July 2025)
- **DWG**: Medit i500 intraoral scanner, 2.0 mm PMMA auxiliary occlusal wafer (maximal intercuspal position), Medit Link CAD, CAD/CAM milling (Katana zirconia, Kuraray Noritake), RelyX Unicem cement
- **CWG**: Polyether impression (Impregum Penta), Type IV stone cast, semi-adjustable articulator, same zirconia/cement
- **Occlusal analysis**: T-Scan III system — ROF, occlusion time ratio, occlusal deviation mapping
- **Follow-up**: Baseline (crown insertion) + 6 months

## Results

| Outcome | DWG vs. CWG | P |
|---|---|---|
| Max occlusal deviation | DWG lower | <0.001 |
| Mean occlusal deviation | DWG lower | <0.001 |
| Occlusal adjustment area | DWG lower | <0.001 |
| Occlusion time ratio | DWG lower | <0.001 |
| ROF — implant crown | DWG lower | 0.004 |
| ROF — adjacent natural tooth | DWG higher | 0.002 |
| Total laboratory time | DWG shorter | <0.001 |
| Impression/scan time | DWG shorter | <0.001 |
| Crowns requiring adjustment | DWG fewer | 0.012 |

6-month follow-up: occlusal stability maintained in both groups; DWG retained all measured advantages.
