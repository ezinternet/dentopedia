## Three-line Summary
In vitro reference aid–based study (phantom head, human-tooth mandibular model, 12 repetitions × 5 settings) comparing five intraoral scanners (CS3600, Primescan, Trios 4, Medit i500, Emerald S) and conventional alginate impression (CAI) for full-arch transfer accuracy, with and without fixed orthodontic appliances (FOAs: metal/ceramic brackets, ± wire).
All IOSs showed lower deviations than CAI in every bracket setting (IOS range 28±23 to 141±140 µm vs. CAI 103±103 to 212±204 µm), except natural teeth (group A) where CAI was most accurate (21±20 µm); Medit i500 had the largest deviations among IOSs but still beat CAI; Primescan was fastest overall (92–115 s) while CAI+plaster-casting took longest (224–238 s); all group/technique comparisons p<0.001.
Clinically, IOS full-arch scanning is recommended over alginate for patients with fixed orthodontic appliances on both accuracy and time-efficiency grounds, but the in vitro design (no saliva, single trained operator, occlusal-surface-only reference) means clinical re-verification is still needed.

## 세줄요약
기준체(reference aid) 기반 in vitro 연구(팬텀헤드, 실치아 하악 모델, 5개 세팅 × 12회 반복): 5종 구강스캐너(Intraoral Scanner, IOS)(CS3600, Primescan, Trios 4, Medit i500, Emerald S)와 알지네이트 인상(Conventional Alginate Impression, CAI)의 전악(full-arch) 전이정확도(transfer accuracy)를 고정성 교정장치(Fixed Orthodontic Appliance, FOA: 금속/세라믹 브라켓, 와이어 유무) 유무별로 비교.
브라켓이 있는 모든 세팅에서 구강스캐너가 CAI보다 편차가 작았음(구강스캐너 28±23~141±140 µm vs CAI 103±103~212±204 µm), 단 자연치(A군)에서는 CAI가 가장 정확(21±20 µm); Medit i500이 구강스캐너 중 편차가 가장 컸지만 CAI보다는 우수; Primescan이 전체 중 가장 빠름(92–115초), CAI+석고모형 제작이 가장 오래 걸림(224–238초); 모든 군/기법 간 비교 p<0.001.
임상적으로 고정성 교정장치 환자에서는 정확도·시간효율 양쪽 모두 알지네이트보다 구강스캐너가 권장되나, in vitro 설계(타액 없음, 단일 숙련 술자, 교합면 기준체만 사용)라 임상 재검증이 필요.

## Summary
This in vitro study used a reference aid–based method (4 high-precision bearing spheres bonded to a human-tooth mandibular phantom model, measured independently by coordinate measurement machine) to assess the transfer accuracy of full-arch impressions taken by five intraoral scanners (IOSs: CS3600, Primescan, Trios 4, Medit i500, Emerald S) and conventional alginate impression (CAI), across five settings: natural teeth, and metal or ceramic brackets with or without archwire. Unlike prior studies that relied on best-fit superimposition (which masks true error by averaging it across the arch), this study compared each dataset directly against an independently measured ground-truth reference per ISO 5725-1. All five IOSs outperformed CAI in every bracket-present setting, though CAI was most accurate on natural teeth alone. Medit i500 consistently showed the largest deviations among the IOSs. Clinically, this supports preferring intraoral scanning over conventional alginate impressions for full-arch records in patients undergoing fixed orthodontic treatment, both for accuracy and chair time, while flagging the need for clinical (not just phantom-head) confirmation.

## Key Contributions
- First reference aid–based (non-best-fit) assessment of full-arch transfer accuracy specifically in the presence of fixed orthodontic appliances, following ISO 5725-1 trueness/precision methodology.
- All 5 tested IOSs beat CAI in every FOA setting (metal/ceramic brackets, ± wire); CAI was only superior on natural teeth without appliances (21±20 µm).
- Bracket material and wire presence both modulate accuracy: brackets+wire (groups C, E) generally showed higher deviations than brackets alone (groups B, D) for most IOSs and for CAI; metallic brackets generally showed larger deviations than ceramic.
- IOS scanning was also faster than alginate+casting in every setting; several IOSs (CS3600, Primescan, Trios 4, Medit i500) needed up to 16% less time scanning brackets+wire than brackets alone, while CAI accuracy degraded sharply once wire was present.

## Methodology
In vitro, phantom-head comparative study. A Frasaco ANA-4 mandibular model was fitted with real extracted human teeth and modified into 5 groups (A: natural teeth; B: metal brackets, no wire; C: metal brackets + wire; D: ceramic brackets, no wire; E: ceramic brackets + wire). Four precision-ground steel spheres were bonded to the occlusal surfaces as a reference aid; their true inter-sphere distances were established via 10 independent CMM measurements. Each group was captured 12 times by each of 5 IOSs (CS3600, Primescan, Trios 4, Medit i500, Emerald S) and once per repetition by CAI (alginate, cast in dental stone and measured by CMM) — 300 IOS datasets + 60 plaster casts total. Deviations of the 6 inter-sphere linear distances from the reference were analyzed in GOM Inspect; trueness = mean deviation, precision = SD, per ISO 5725-1. Statistics: SPSS v26, GENLINMIXED (2-/3-way ANOVA for heterogeneous variances), one-way ANOVA per group/distance, Kruskal–Wallis due to outliers, α=0.05. Single operator performed all impressions/measurements (no inter-rater reliability assessed).
