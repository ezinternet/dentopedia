## Three-line Summary
In vitro study (9 crown preparation designs, Primescan vs. TRIOS 5): preparation geometry was the dominant factor for IOS trueness (p < 0.001) while scanner type alone had no significant main effect (p = 0.056). A significant scanner × preparation interaction indicates scanner-specific performance varies by design. RMS range 24.7–62.3 µm; anterior veneer with palatal extension was the most demanding scenario for Primescan.

## Summary
Sanioğlu et al. prepared 9 single-tooth designs (anterior veneers, partial occlusal veneers, posterior onlay variants, full crowns) plus an unprepared control on a maxillary phantom model, then scanned each with Primescan and TRIOS 5 (5 repetitions each). Trueness was computed as RMS deviation against an ATOS Q industrial scanner reference. Two-way ANOVA showed preparation design as the significant main effect (F(9,80) = 9.26, p < 0.001); scanner type was not significant as a main effect (p = 0.056) but showed a significant interaction with design (F(9,80) = 4.84, p < 0.001). The best trueness combination was VE3 (partial occlusal veneer) + TRIOS 5 (24.7 µm); the worst was VE2 (anterior veneer buccal + palatal) + Primescan (62.3 µm). The interaction effect is interpreted in light of acquisition technology differences: Primescan's active triangulation struggles more with palatal concavities than TRIOS 5's confocal technology.

## Key Contributions
- Preparation geometry is a stronger predictor of IOS trueness than scanner brand — a key clinical insight for selecting IOS by indication
- Significant scanner × preparation interaction: TRIOS 5 is advantageous for anterior palatal preparations; Primescan equivalent or superior in simpler geometries
- Overall RMS range (24.7–62.3 µm) well within clinical acceptability; both scanners clinically acceptable across all tested designs
- Mechanistic explanation: active triangulation (Primescan) vs. confocal (TRIOS 5) difference may account for the interaction
- Provides design-specific guidance: anterior veneer cases with palatal reduction require extra attention when using Primescan

## Methodology
In vitro comparative study. Maxillary phantom model (Frasaco typodont). Nine preparation designs: VE1 (anterior buccal veneer), VE2 (anterior buccal + palatal veneer), VE3 (partial occlusal veneer), plus posterior onlay variants and full crown designs. Control: unprepared tooth. IOS: Primescan (Dentsply Sirona) and TRIOS 5 (3Shape); 5 scans per design per scanner. Reference: ATOS Q industrial photogrammetric scanner. Trueness: RMS deviation after best-fit STL superimposition. Precision: IQR of repeated RMS values (descriptive). Statistics: two-way ANOVA (design × scanner); α=0.05.

## Results
**RMS range (µm):** 24.7 (VE3 + TRIOS 5) to 62.3 (VE2 + Primescan).
**Preparation design effect:** F(9,80)=9.26, **p < 0.001** (significant dominant factor).
**Scanner type main effect:** p=0.056 (not significant).
**Scanner × design interaction:** F(9,80)=4.84, **p < 0.001** (significant — TRIOS 5 advantage in palatal/complex geometries).
Simpler, optically accessible preparations → lower RMS for both scanners. Complex multi-surface anterior designs → worse accuracy, especially with Primescan.
