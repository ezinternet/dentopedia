## Three-line Summary
Systematic review (PRISMA 2020, OSF-registered; Medline/PubMed, EBSCO, Web of Science, Scopus searched November 19, 2024; 10 cross-sectional studies, 2016–2024, n=5–50 each) comparing intraoral scanners (IOS) against conventional impressions on accuracy, chairside time, and patient comfort in adolescent/adult (≥18y) populations with full or near-full dentition.

No meta-analysis was possible (outcome heterogeneity); accuracy across the 10 studies remained mixed (several favored conventional impressions for full-arch/posterior/long-span measurements, one favored IOS, several found both comparable), while the 2 studies measuring chairside time and the 2 measuring patient comfort (VAS) both found significantly shorter time and higher comfort for IOS.

Clinical implication: findings apply mainly to young, fully dentate, healthy adults — QUADAS-2 showed unclear/high risk of bias in patient selection across most included studies, so the review stops short of a firm accuracy verdict and calls for standardized protocols and more diverse populations.

## Summary
This systematic review (PRISMA 2020, protocol pre-registered on OSF) searched Medline/PubMed, EBSCO, Web of Science, and Scopus on November 19, 2024, screening 269 records down to 10 included cross-sectional studies (published 2016–2024) comparing intraoral scanners (IOS) against conventional impressions in adolescent/adult (≥18y) populations with complete or near-complete dentition. Because accuracy, chairside time, and comfort were measured with heterogeneous protocols across studies, a meta-analysis was not attempted and findings were synthesized qualitatively. Accuracy results across the 10 studies were mixed — several found conventional impressions more accurate for full-arch, posterior, or long-span measurements, one found IOS more accurate overall, and several found both techniques clinically comparable. Only 2 of the 10 studies actually measured chairside time and patient comfort, but both found IOS significantly faster and more comfortable. Risk-of-bias appraisal via QUADAS-2 showed unclear or high risk in patient selection across most studies (small, young, healthy, fully dentate samples; non-randomized impression order in all but one study), so the authors stop short of a firm accuracy recommendation and call for standardized protocols and more diverse populations, including partially/completely edentulous and craniofacially atypical patients.

## Key Contributions
- Applies PRISMA 2020 and QUADAS-2 risk-of-bias appraisal (with documented inter-rater Cohen's kappa per domain) to the accuracy/chairside-time/comfort literature — a higher methodological bar than an earlier narrative critical review on closely related questions.
- Explicitly documents why pooling was not attempted: accuracy was measured via linear/angular distances, shell-to-shell deviation, colorimetric mapping, and qualitative assessment across studies — methods judged not directly comparable.
- Distinguishes isolated "chairside" time from total procedure time: in the one study that separated them (Sfondrini et al.), the IOS chairside-only advantage (5 min 49 s vs 7 min 32 s) was far smaller than the total-time advantage including lab processing (5 min 49 s vs 22 min 6 s) — most of the apparent IOS time savings comes from eliminating downstream gypsum-model processing, not from a much faster in-chair procedure.
- Flags a comfort-assessment-timing confound: VAS administered immediately after each procedure (Sfondrini et al.) vs. only after both procedures were complete (Janosi et al.) may inflate IOS's apparent comfort advantage through recall bias, recency effects, and social-desirability bias.
- Notes that findings are applicable mainly to young, fully dentate, systemically healthy adults — the predominant profile of the 10 included studies' samples.

## Methodology
Systematic review, PRISMA 2020, OSF-registered protocol. Databases: Medline/PubMed, EBSCO (D&OSS), Web of Science, Scopus; searched November 19, 2024; English/Spanish, no publication-date restriction. 269 records → 153 duplicates removed → 87 excluded at screening → 27 assessed for eligibility → 17 further excluded → **10 studies included** (all cross-sectional observational designs; sample sizes 5–50; mean age 26.4y where reported, range 18–36). Risk-of-bias tool: **QUADAS-2** (patient selection, index test, reference standard, flow and timing), applied by two calibrated reviewers, repeated after 4 months for intra-rater reliability (Cohen's kappa: patient selection 0.88, index test 1.00, reference standard 0.89, flow/timing 1.00, general risk 0.63). **No meta-analysis** — heterogeneity of accuracy-measurement methods, chairside-time definitions, and comfort-assessment protocols precluded quantitative pooling; findings synthesized qualitatively.
