## Three-line Summary

Interrupted time series (ITS) analysis of 48 months (24 pre-/24 post-scanner) of Invisalign receipt data from 1,871 GP and orthodontic practices worldwide, examining the impact of iTero intraoral scanner acquisition on Invisalign treatment starts; a subset of 319 North American low-volume GP practices (≤5 receipts in the prior 12 months) was analyzed separately.

Scanner introduction produced a significant abrupt increase in Invisalign receipts for the full sample (b = 0.49; p < 0.001), from a predicted 2.38 to an observed 2.88 in month 25 (+20.71% vs. counterfactual), and a significant abrupt increase for the low-volume North American subset (b = 0.28; p < 0.001) that persisted 24 months post-introduction; projected 12-month gains were +5.92 receipts (full sample) and +3.41 receipts (low-volume subset).

The outcome is Align's own Invisalign receipt data with no non-Invisalign comparator and no cost-side accounting (scanner capital + per-scan cost not netted against gains), and an ITS built on manufacturer-supplied receipt data cannot separate a scanner effect from concurrent marketing or organic practice growth — the finding should be read as association, not an isolated causal scanner effect, and not as net economic benefit.

## Summary

> abstract-only — publisher landing/abstract page, full text not retrieved

This study, published in a J Clin Dent supplement, uses interrupted time series (ITS) analysis on Align Technology's own Invisalign receipt data to estimate the practice-economics impact of acquiring an iTero intraoral scanner. Across 1,871 GP and orthodontic practices worldwide (48 months, 24 pre-/24 post-acquisition), scanner introduction was associated with a significant, abrupt increase in Invisalign receipts (+20.71% vs. the no-scanner counterfactual at month 25), and the effect held in a low-volume North American GP subset (n=319) for at least 24 months. Projected across 12 months, the authors estimate +5.92 additional receipts for the full sample and +3.41 for the low-volume subset. The design and data source impose a hard ceiling on interpretation: there is no non-Invisalign comparator, no cost-side accounting, and no way to separate the scanner's own effect from concurrent marketing or practice growth in manufacturer-supplied receipt data — this is association evidence for a capital-equipment purchase decision, not a validated ROI or causal claim.

## Key Contributions

- Quantitative ITS estimate, at scale (1,871 practices, 48 months), linking a specific capital-equipment acquisition (iTero scanner) to a measurable increase in Invisalign practice receipts.
- Separate low-volume North American GP subgroup analysis (n=319, ≤5 prior-year receipts) showing the effect persisted at least 24 months — the segment most relevant to a GP weighing whether an IOS purchase is worth it specifically for Invisalign starts.
- Converts a raw effect size into a practice-facing number: a projected +5.92 (full sample) or +3.41 (low-volume subset) additional Invisalign receipts in the first 12 months post-acquisition.

## Methodology

Interrupted time series (ITS) analysis on Align Technology's Invisalign gross-receipt records for 1,871 GP and orthodontic practices worldwide, spanning 24 months before and 24 months after iTero scanner introduction (48 months total). A subgroup analysis restricted to 319 North American GP practices with low baseline volume (≤5 Invisalign receipts in the 12 months prior to scanner acquisition) tested whether the effect held and persisted in that segment specifically. The analysis modeled both an abrupt level change at the scanner-introduction month (month 25) and a longer-term slope-change in the monthly receipt trend.

**Critical appraisal (drives the `retrospective` evidence_level and the caution above):** the outcome is Invisalign gross receipts drawn from Align Technology's own data on Align's own scanner (iTero), published in a J Clin Dent supplement. There is no non-Invisalign comparator, no cost side (scanner capital cost and per-scan cost are never netted against the receipt gain), and an ITS design on manufacturer receipt data cannot separate the scanner's own effect from concurrent marketing pushes or organic practice growth that often accompany a new-technology purchase. Read this as association evidence for a purchase decision, not as a validated net-economic-benefit or causal estimate.
