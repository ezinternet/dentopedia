## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional questionnaire study (Dicle University, Türkiye, n=255 fourth- and fifth-year dental students) presenting a standardised symptomatic-irreversible-pulpitis vignette and using multivariable logistic regression to identify independent predictors of self-reported willingness to use full pulpotomy as a definitive treatment.

Overall 55.7% reported willingness to adopt full pulpotomy definitively (vs. only 45.1% who actually chose a pulpotomy-based approach in the vignette itself); the adjusted model found previously *performing* a pulpotomy (OR 2.51) and a mandatory-rubber-dam attitude (OR 1.78) independently raised willingness, while reporting an implementation barrier (OR 0.29) and uncertainty about barriers (OR 0.06) independently lowered it — year of study and hemostasis/bleeding-control confidence were **not** independently significant in the Results section, despite the Abstract's conflicting claim to the contrary.

This is undergraduate stated intent on a single-centre hypothetical vignette, not delivered treatment, and the model explains only 24.0% of outcome variance — real predictors, but a weak model; the paper says nothing about whether full pulpotomy itself works, which is a separate question this page does not answer either.

횡단면 설문 연구(터키 Dicle 대학, 4·5학년 치과대학생 n=255) — 표준화된 증상성 비가역성 치수염(Symptomatic Irreversible Pulpitis) 비네트(vignette)를 제시하고, 완전 치수절단술(Full Pulpotomy)을 최종치료로 사용할 의향의 독립 예측인자를 다변량 로지스틱회귀로 분석.

전체 55.7%가 완전 치수절단술을 최종치료로 쓸 의향을 밝혔지만(실제 비네트에서 치수절단 기반 치료를 선택한 비율은 45.1%뿐), 보정모델에서 과거 치수절단술 시행 경험(승산비(Odds Ratio, OR) 2.51)과 러버댐(Rubber Dam) 필수 사용 태도(OR 1.78)가 의향을 독립적으로 높였고, 시행 장벽 인지(OR 0.29)와 장벽에 대한 불확실성(OR 0.06)이 독립적으로 낮췄다 — 학년과 지혈 조절 자신감은 본문 Results에서 유의하지 않았는데, 초록(Abstract)은 이와 상충하는 주장을 담고 있다.

이는 단일기관 학부생의 가상 비네트에서의 stated intent(의향 진술)이지 실제 시행된 치료가 아니며, 모델은 결과 분산의 24.0%만 설명한다 — 예측인자 자체는 실재하지만 모델은 약하며, 완전 치수절단술이 실제로 효과가 있는지는 이 논문이 다루지 않는 별개의 질문이고 이 페이지 역시 그 답은 주지 않는다.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this cross-sectional questionnaire study ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare14101342); PMID 42194434; PMCID PMC13206138) surveyed 255 fourth- and fifth-year dental students at Dicle University (Diyarbakır, Türkiye) about diagnosis and management of symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, using a standardised vignette (22-year-old female, tooth 36, deep caries with pulp exposure). Its central question was not what students *knew*, but what independently predicted whether a student would report willingness to use full pulpotomy — a biologically conservative, pulp-preserving alternative to root canal treatment — as a **definitive** treatment.

Diagnostically, students leaned on spontaneous pain (69.4%), prolonged heat sensitivity (50.6%), percussion sensitivity (46.7%), and radiographic findings (43.9%). Faced with the vignette itself, 45.1% chose a pulpotomy-based approach, 28.6% chose single-visit pulpectomy with obturation, and 24.7% chose pulpectomy with a calcium hydroxide dressing. MTA was the preferred capping material (57.3%, vs. Biodentine 12.9% and calcium hydroxide 8.2%). But asked directly whether they would use full pulpotomy as a *definitive* treatment, 55.7% said yes — ten points higher than the share who actually picked it when forced to choose. The paper does not cross-tabulate these two items, so it is not possible to say from the text whether this reflects genuine "willing in principle, chose otherwise here" behaviour or simply two loosely related survey questions; it is flagged here rather than resolved, because the gap itself is informative about how fragile "willingness" is as a construct.

The multivariable model is the paper's substantive contribution. After adjustment, two variables independently raised willingness: having *previously performed* a pulpotomy (OR 2.51, 95% CI 1.18–5.33, p=0.016) and holding a mandatory-rubber-dam-use attitude (OR 1.78, 95% CI 1.01–3.14, p=0.046). Two variables independently lowered it: reporting an implementation barrier (OR 0.29, 95% CI 0.15–0.58, p=0.0005) and being uncertain about implementation barriers (OR 0.06, 95% CI 0.02–0.25, p<0.001, though this subgroup was small, n=22). Critically, year of study, merely *observing* (not performing) a pulpotomy, and the perceived hemostasis/bleeding-control requirement were **not** independently significant (all p>0.05) — this is stated explicitly in Results §3.5. The Abstract, however, claims "clinical year, previous exposure to pulpotomy cases, and confidence in bleeding control were independently associated with willingness," which directly contradicts the Results text. This source treats the Results section as authoritative and flags the Abstract wording as an unresolved internal inconsistency in the source article (see [[practice-management/cangul-2026-full-pulpotomy-adoption-determinants-trainee-decision-making]] for the full deviation note) — a caution against citing this paper's abstract alone.

**Why this matters beyond the paper itself**: [[overviews/treatment-planning-decision-variability-overview]] documents that generalists choose extraction roughly three times more often than endodontists and speculates this "may reflect a lack of awareness or confidence in conservative treatment options." This paper is the first evidence this wiki holds that actually tests a mechanism, and it points away from awareness and toward *hands-on procedural experience and confidence* — specifically, having *done* the procedure, not merely knowing about it or being further along in training. If that generalizes, continuing-education strategies built around informing clinicians about conservative options (webinars, guideline updates, literature reviews) are targeting the wrong lever; what the data here associate with adoption is supervised, hands-on exposure. That reframing is speculative — this is one cross-sectional, single-centre, undergraduate-population study, and the model explains only 24.0% of variance (Nagelkerke R²) with 66.3% classification accuracy — but it is a concrete, testable hypothesis the wiki did not previously have.

Whether full pulpotomy is itself an effective treatment for symptomatic irreversible pulpitis in mature permanent teeth is a separate question this paper does not address at all; see [[endodontics/vpt/mtalsi-2026-pulpotomy-symptomatic-irreversible-pulpitis-mature-umbrella]] and [[endodontics/vpt/taha-2017-mta-pulpotomy-mature-permanent-carious]] for that evidence.

## Key Contributions

- **First determinant-level (not just descriptive) evidence in this wiki on pulpotomy adoption.** Multivariable logistic regression (n=255, 142 events, adequate EPV) isolates independent predictors rather than reporting raw preference frequencies alone.
- **Procedural experience, not academic seniority, predicts willingness.** Previously *performing* a pulpotomy: OR 2.51 (95% CI 1.18–5.33, p=0.016). Year of study alone dropped out of the adjusted model — a class-level trend (48.7% vs. 57.2%) did not reach significance (p=0.153).
- **A protocol-adherence attitude tracks a different protocol-driven decision**: viewing rubber dam use as mandatory independently predicted willingness to adopt full pulpotomy (OR 1.78, 95% CI 1.01–3.14, p=0.046) — a distinct procedure, but the same underlying orientation toward structured, evidence-based practice.
- **Barriers and uncertainty dominate the negative side**, with uncertainty about barriers carrying by far the largest effect size of any predictor in the model (OR 0.06) — though from a small subgroup (n=22) the authors themselves ask readers to interpret cautiously.
- **Surfaces the willingness/behaviour gap as a measurable phenomenon**: 55.7% general willingness vs. 45.1% actual vignette choice, and flags its own Abstract/Results inconsistency on which variables were significant — both are treated here as substantive findings worth stating plainly, not smoothed over.

## Methodology

Cross-sectional, single-centre, questionnaire-based study (Ethics Committee approval, Dicle University Faculty of Dentistry, No. 2025-64) of fourth- and fifth-year dental students who had completed at least one clinical rotation in endodontics and restorative dentistry. Distributed to 300 eligible students via Google Forms; 255 valid responses (85.0% response rate), 117 (45.9%) fourth-year and 138 (54.1%) fifth-year. The 19-item questionnaire was piloted (n=15, excluded from analysis) and reviewed by two endodontists and one restorative dentistry specialist for face validity.

All participants were presented an identical vignette (22-year-old female, nocturnal spontaneous pain, tooth 36, radiographic deep caries, pulp exposure during caries removal) and asked to (1) select diagnostic criteria used, (2) choose a treatment approach from four options, (3) report preferred capping material, and (4) state whether they would use full pulpotomy as a *definitive* treatment. Independent variables (year of study, prior pulpotomy experience — dummy-coded never/observed/performed, rubber dam attitude, hemostasis requirement, perceived barriers — dummy-coded against "no barrier," uncertainty about barriers) were selected for the multivariable model via univariable screening (p<0.10) plus clinical relevance. Multicollinearity checked via VIF/tolerance (none problematic). Model fit assessed via likelihood-ratio test, Hosmer–Lemeshow, Nagelkerke R², and classification accuracy. Sample size confirmed adequate via G*Power (medium effect size w=0.30, α=0.05, power=0.80) and the events-per-variable principle (142 events, ratio >10).
