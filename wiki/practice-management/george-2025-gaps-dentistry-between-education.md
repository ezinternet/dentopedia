## Three-line Summary

Qualitative exploratory study (semi-structured interviews, n=10 new BDS graduates in a 1-year general-dentistry training programme at CMC Vellore, India, Feb–Jul 2023) probing perceived theory–practice gaps in knowledge, skills, and practice.
All 10/10 participants felt unprepared for independent unsupervised practice after graduation; thematic analysis (content validity 0.90) produced six themes — gap existence, preparedness impact, curriculum integration, preceptor role, direct supervision, patient safety.
Fixes proposed are systemic, not individual: competency-based integrated curricula, structured mentorship/preceptorship, direct supervision of early cases, and feedback-driven assessment.

## Summary

Dentistry is a skill-based discipline where Indian regulations permit graduates to open independent practice immediately after the Bachelor of Dental Surgery (BDS). Yet this qualitative study from Christian Medical College, Vellore finds that every one of ten new graduates entering a 1-year Certificate in General Dentistry (CGD) programme reported being unprepared for unsupervised practice. Interviews structured around three domains — knowledge (theoretical understanding), skills (procedural competence), and practice (real-world application) — surfaced a consistent theory–practice gap: education was exam-oriented and theory-heavy with minimal hands-on or patient work, leaving deficits in clinical reasoning, management of medically compromised patients, medical emergencies, jurisprudence, and practice management.

Six themes emerged: (1) the gap exists; (2) it degrades practice preparedness; (3) curriculum integration (case-based/patient-based learning) is needed to close it; (4) preceptors/role models bridge classroom idealism and workplace reality; (5) direct supervision of early clinical cases builds confidence and improves quality/safety; (6) unbridged gaps threaten patient safety — one participant described being helpless when a medically compromised patient developed an emergency outside the teaching hospital's umbrella.

The authors position this as the first clear description of the theory–practice gap in dentistry, long recognized in nursing (since 1943), medicine, and paramedics. Recommendations target educators, regulators, and policymakers: integrate theory with planned clinical application, adopt competence-based outcome assessment, train faculty as preceptors, expand supervised clinical exposure with reasonable ratios, add reflection-based assessments, strengthen institutional feedback systems, and introduce bridging programmes.

## Key Contributions

- First qualitative characterization of the theory–practice gap in undergraduate dentistry, from graduates' own accounts.
- A domain-structured gap map (knowledge / skills / practice) plus an education-gaps vs practice-gaps vs recommendations checklist (Table 2).
- Explicit linkage of educational gaps to **patient safety**, elevating a pedagogical issue to a safety issue.
- Cross-national benchmark figures showing under-preparedness is global, not India-specific (85–93% want established practice/mentorship in Australia; 40% unprepared in Saudi Arabia; 65% low confidence in India).

## Methodology

- Design: qualitative exploratory study; Feb–Jul 2023; Dental Unit 1, CMC Vellore; ethics CMCH 15195/2023.
- Participants: n=10 CGD trainees (2022–23 batch), purposive + convenience sampling; ages 23–30; 6 F/4 M; 8 with no prior clinical experience.
- Instrument: expert-reviewed semi-structured interview guide covering knowledge, psychomotor/skills, patient care, and practice readiness; scale/item-level content validity averaged 0.90.
- Analysis: manual thematic analysis by principal researcher using grounded and emergent theory (codes → themes/subthemes); interviews recorded, transcribed, field-noted.

## Results

- 10/10 participants acknowledged the theory–practice gap and reported feeling unprepared for independent practice immediately after graduation; all desired additional supervised training first.
- Education gaps: overwhelming theory content for examinations, minimal hands-on/patient work, weak clinical reasoning and decision-making training, little dental content on medically compromised patients, medico-legal blind spots; named holes — jurisprudence, medical emergencies, practice management.
- Practice gaps: skill exposure, communication/soft skills, consultation, treatment planning, procedural competence, quota-based system effects, absent clinical mentorship.
- Preparedness mechanisms that worked: preceptors/role models, one-to-one direct supervision of initial cases, formative + summative evaluation beyond written theory, feedback-driven learning with self-assessment checklists.
- Safety consequence: inability to translate theory into action during complications/emergencies endangers patients once the teaching-hospital safety net is removed.
