## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (BMJ Open Quality 2024) defining dental-specific patient safety terms (dental adverse event, dental near miss, dental safety culture, dental sentinel event) and identifying 7 barriers to dentistry's delayed patient safety recognition: historical technical-proficiency focus, lower perceived risk, lack of standardised reporting, professional autonomy, ambulatory/dispersed practices, profit concerns, absence of safety culture.

Seven-component institutional framework for building safety culture: (1) strategic priority, (2) care-delivery integration, (3) annual training, (4) patient safety plan, (5) incident management system, (6) blame-free culture, (7) performance indicators; plus ethical framework and patient representatives in decision-making.

Dentistry must be proactive given pharmaceutical use, aggressive surgical techniques (implantology), ionising radiation/laser use, and infection-control risks; safety culture must be built from dental school education through to clinical career.

## Summary

Padmanabhan et al. (2024) provide a comprehensive narrative review of patient safety in dentistry, with three main contributions. First, they supply **dental-specific definitions** that distinguish dentistry from general healthcare: dental adverse event, dental medical error, dental near miss, dental safety culture, and dental sentinel event — establishing a common vocabulary the field lacks. Second, they articulate **7 reasons for dentistry's delayed recognition** of patient safety: historical focus on technical proficiency, perception of lower procedure risk versus medicine, absence of standardised reporting systems, professional autonomy, ambulatory/dispersed practice nature, private-profit concerns, and no explicit safety culture. Third, they propose a **7-component institutional framework** (strategic priority, care-delivery integration, annual training, patient safety plan, incident management system, blame-free culture, performance indicators) applicable to dental practices, dental schools, and hospitals. The review also highlights the role of dental schools in instilling safety culture from the beginning of dental education, and the value of patient representatives on decision-making committees.

## Key Contributions

- Dental-specific definitions for 5 key patient safety concepts
- 7-barrier explanation for dentistry's delayed patient safety recognition
- 7-component institutional safety framework applicable across practice types
- Patient representative involvement in safety governance
- Ethical framework as foundation for safety culture
- Annual training and continuous improvement cycles as core components

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative literature review (no systematic search protocol)
- **Sources**: Research articles, guidelines, reports on patient safety in dentistry
- **Scope**: Dental practices, dental schools, dental hospitals internationally

## Results

- Patient safety gaining recognition in dentistry but remains challenged by lack of standardised reporting and procedural complexity
- Dental education now incorporating patient safety training; guidelines covering infection control, medication safety, anaesthesia management, patient communication
- Blame-free culture identified as essential prerequisite for effective incident reporting
