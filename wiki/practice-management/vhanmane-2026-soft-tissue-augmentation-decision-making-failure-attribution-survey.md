## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional questionnaire survey (Bharati Vidyapeeth Dental College and Hospital, India, January–March 2025; n=150: 65 periodontists, 45 oral surgeons, 40 prosthodontists; 63.3% male, most with 11–15 years' implantology experience and 11–20 implants placed per month) on clinical decision thresholds, technique preference, failure attribution, and outcome calibration for peri-implant soft tissue augmentation (STA).

Most clinicians across all three specialties intervened at 1–2 mm keratinized tissue width (no significant interspecialty difference) and preferred connective tissue grafting (CTG) over xenogeneic collagen matrix or free gingival graft in a thin-mucosa scenario (p=0.027); most estimated STA failure at 5–10% and favored shared 50/50 responsibility for unexplained failure; CTG confidence was higher among males (3.43±0.79 vs 3.13±0.90, p=0.041) and biomaterial confidence varied significantly by specialty (periodontist 2.80±0.94 vs oral surgeon 2.53±0.97, p=0.004); patient dissatisfaction despite clinical success was the single most commonly reported complication (41.3%), ahead of graft necrosis (36.7%) and infection (28.0%).

This is a self-reported, convenience-sampled, single-country survey — every figure is perceived failure rate, self-rated confidence, or stated preference, not measured performance; its sharpest finding is structural rather than clinical — moderate outcomes were accepted as personally successful by 65–71.1% of respondents but judged publishable by only 40–48.9%, meaning the literature clinicians read to calibrate their own expectations is itself skewed toward excellent-outcome cases.

## 세줄요약

인도 횡단면 설문조사(Bharati Vidyapeeth Dental College and Hospital, 2025년 1–3월; n=150: 치주과전문의(Periodontist) 65명·구강외과의(Oral Surgeon) 45명·보철전문의(Prosthodontist) 40명; 남성 63.3%, 대다수 11–15년 임플란트 경력·월 11–20개 임플란트 식립) — 임플란트 주위 연조직증대술(Soft Tissue Augmentation, STA)의 임상적 개입 기준·술식선호·실패귀인·결과보정에 관한 설문.

세 전문분과 모두 각화조직폭(Keratinized Tissue Width, KTW) 1–2mm에서 증대술을 시행한다는 응답이 다수였고(전문분과 간 유의차 없음), 얇은 점막 시나리오에서는 결합조직이식(Connective Tissue Graft, CTG)을 이종콜라겐기질(Xenogeneic Collagen Matrix)이나 유리치은이식(Free Gingival Graft, FGG)보다 유의하게 선호(p=0.027); 대다수가 STA 실패율을 5–10%로 추정했고 원인불명 실패 시 술자·환자 50/50 공동책임을 선호; CTG 자신감은 남성이 유의하게 더 높았고(3.43±0.79 vs 3.13±0.90, p=0.041), 생체재료 자신감은 전문분과별로 유의한 차이(치주과 2.80±0.94 vs 구강외과 2.53±0.97, p=0.004); 임상적으로 성공했음에도 환자 불만족(41.3%)이 이식편괴사(36.7%)·감염(28.0%)보다 더 흔한 합병증으로 보고됨.

자가보고·편의표본·단일국가 설문이므로 여기 실린 모든 수치는 실제 시행결과가 아니라 지각된 실패율·자가평정 자신감·희망 선호일 뿐이다; 가장 날카로운 소견은 임상적이라기보다 구조적이다 — 중등도(moderate) 결과는 응답자의 65–71.1%가 "개인적으로는 성공"으로 받아들였지만 "출판 가능"으로 판단한 비율은 40–48.9%에 그쳤다. 즉 술자가 자신의 기대치를 보정하는 데 쓰는 그 문헌 자체가 이미 우수한 결과 쪽으로 편향돼 있다는 뜻이다.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this cross-sectional survey ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.109202); PMID 42326151; PMCID PMC13278387) asks whether the treatment-decision variability this wiki has already documented for endodontic extraction-vs-retention decisions ([[practice-management/dufey-portilla-2026-treatment-decision-making-apical-periodontitis-clinician-groups]]) also appears in a different specialty and a different decision — implant soft-tissue augmentation. It does, but the more useful finding here is not the variance itself; it is what the survey reveals about the evidence base clinicians use to calibrate their expectations.

150 dental professionals actively placing or restoring implants (65 periodontists, 45 oral surgeons, 40 prosthodontists; 11–15 years' experience typical; 11–20 implants/month typical) answered a validated five-domain questionnaire (Cronbach's α = 0.82) on when they intervene, which technique they choose, who they blame when augmentation fails, how they judge success, and how confident they feel doing it. On the threshold question there was real agreement: a plurality across all three specialties intervenes at 1–2 mm keratinized tissue width, with no statistically significant interspecialty split (χ²=7.82, p=0.253). Technique preference converged too — connective tissue grafting (CTG) was the majority choice in a thin/translucent mucosa scenario across all groups, though the margin by which it won varied significantly by specialty (p=0.027).

The most interesting finding in the paper is not a p-value from the threshold or technique tables — it is the gap between two acceptance criteria applied to the *same* hypothetical case. Scenario 2, a moderate outcome (≈2 mm keratinized tissue, ≈1.5 mm thickness), was accepted as a personal, daily-practice success by 65.0–71.1% of respondents across specialties, but judged acceptable for peer-reviewed publication by only 40.0–48.9%. Excellent outcomes (Scenario 3) were near-universally accepted on both counts (93–98%); poor outcomes (Scenario 1) were rejected on both (2.5–15.6%). Only the moderate case shows a wide clinically-accepted/publication-accepted split. That split is a statement about the literature itself: if a large share of clinically acceptable, real-world outcomes never becomes a publication because reviewers and authors alike treat "moderate" as unpublishable, then the published evidence base on soft-tissue augmentation is systematically enriched for excellent outcomes — and any clinician reading that literature to set expectations for their own next case is reading a censored sample, not a representative one. The paper's own words for the underlying pattern: "excellent outcomes are widely accepted as successful, whereas moderate outcomes are less frequently considered publishable."

A second finding worth foregrounding for the same reason: despite clinically successful outcomes, patient dissatisfaction was the single most commonly reported complication (41.3%) — more common than graft necrosis (36.7%) or infection (28.0%). Clinician-defined success (the tissue measurements meeting a threshold) and patient-defined success (satisfaction) are shown here to diverge, in a study that — like its endodontic counterpart in this wiki — never once measured what the patient wanted, expected, or actually experienced.

## Key Contributions

- **First wiki evidence that clinician-dependent decision variability extends outside endodontics.** The overview this paper extends, [[overviews/treatment-planning-decision-variability-overview]], names as an open gap whether the same phenomenon appears in "restorability judgements, periodontal prognosis, or implant-versus-bridge planning." This survey is the first evidence in either direction for implant soft-tissue augmentation specifically — decision thresholds converge across specialties, but technique choice, confidence, and training pathway do not.
- **Quantifies a clinically-acceptable-vs-publishable outcome gap on the same hypothetical case.** Scenario 2 (moderate outcome): 65.0–71.1% personal success vs. 40.0–48.9% publishable success across specialties — the widest split of the three scenarios, and the paper's most consequential finding for how the field's own evidence base gets built.
- **Separates where specialty predicts practice from where it doesn't.** No significant interspecialty difference in intervention thresholds (KTW p=0.253, thickness p=0.737) or smoker-technique modification (p=0.530); significant differences in thin-mucosa technique choice (p=0.027), xenogeneic biomaterial confidence (p=0.004, periodontist > oral surgeon on post hoc), and training source (p=0.003).
- **Surfaces a sex-linked confidence gap that should not be read as a competence claim**: CTG confidence 3.43±0.79 (male) vs. 3.13±0.90 (female), p=0.041 — a self-rated Likert difference, not a measured-outcomes difference.
- **Names clinician-patient outcome divergence directly**: patient dissatisfaction despite clinical success was the most common reported complication (41.3%), against a study design that collected no patient-reported data at all.

## Methodology

Cross-sectional, questionnaire-based, single-center study conducted in the Department of Periodontology at Bharati Vidyapeeth (Deemed to be University) Dental College and Hospital, Sangli/Pune, India, January–March 2025. Ethics approval BVDU/DCH//09/2024-25 (Declaration of Helsinki; informed consent; anonymized responses).

Non-probability convenience sample recruited through professional networks, institutional contacts, and digital platforms — 180 clinicians invited, 150 completed (83.3% response rate), exceeding the pragmatically set minimum of 120 (no formal power calculation was feasible; the study is explicitly exploratory). Eligible participants: periodontists, oral surgeons, and prosthodontists actively placing or restoring implants with ≥1 year of clinical experience; postgraduate residents were not recruited as a separate subgroup.

The structured, self-administered questionnaire covered five domains — demographics/practice characteristics; clinical decision thresholds; failure attribution and economic/medicolegal considerations; subjective vs. objective outcome evaluation via three hypothetical clinical scenarios; and confidence, training background, and complications — using multiple-choice, scenario-based, and 4-point Likert items. Content validity was reviewed by a 5-expert panel and piloted on 15 clinicians; internal consistency of Likert items was Cronbach's α = 0.82. The three outcome scenarios were verbally described, not standardized photographs: Scenario 1 (poor) = <1 mm keratinized tissue, thin mucosa, graft contraction; Scenario 2 (moderate) = ≈2 mm keratinized tissue, ≈1.5 mm thickness; Scenario 3 (excellent) = >3 mm keratinized tissue, >2 mm thickness.

Statistics: IBM SPSS v20; Shapiro-Wilk normality testing; Pearson's chi-square or Fisher's exact test for categorical associations; Mann-Whitney U (two groups) or Kruskal-Wallis H with Bonferroni-corrected post hoc Dunn's test (more than two groups) for ordinal Likert data. Significance set at p<0.05. The authors flag a ceiling effect on Scenario 3 comparisons (near-universal agreement leaves little room to detect a group difference) and caution that multiple exploratory comparisons make borderline-significant results fragile.
