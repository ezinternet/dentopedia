## Three-line Summary

Prospective cohort (n=85 patients, 131 flapless ZirTi implants), University of Bologna; pre-extractive endodontic diagnosis determined placement timing — acute abscess → Early (8-12 weeks), chronic lesion → Immediate, healthy → Delayed; 2-year follow-up.

100% survival; MBL at 24 months: Early 0.48 ± 0.72 mm (best) < Immediate 0.78 ± 0.70 mm < Delayed 1.02 ± 1.01 mm; Early vs Delayed p=0.0001.

Acute periapical abscess = contraindication to immediate placement; Early placement (8-12 weeks post-abscess) preserves the most marginal bone; delayed placement carries highest bone resorption risk.

## Summary

This prospective cohort study ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/ID.0000000000000666)) from the University of Bologna Endodontic Department enrolled 85 patients (131 flapless ZirTi implants) and used the pre-extractive endodontic diagnosis as the sole criterion for placement timing. Acute periapical abscess (tumor, rubor, dolor, calor + fistula + pus) was classified as a contraindication to immediate placement — these patients received Early placement at 8-12 weeks. Chronic periapical lesions with intact cortical bone received Immediate placement. Fully healed sites received Delayed placement (10-12 months). Survival was 100% in all groups. At 24 months, the Early group showed the least MBL (0.48 ± 0.72 mm), significantly less than Delayed (1.02 ± 1.01 mm; p=0.0001). The Immediate group was intermediate (0.78 ± 0.70 mm; NS vs Delayed, p=0.088). The authors conclude that early placement (not immediate) is optimal when acute infection was present, and that delayed placement carries the highest bone loss risk due to drilling-induced necrosis of mature cortical bone.

## Key Contributions

- Established the clinical decision algorithm: acute infection signs → wait 8-12 weeks → Early; chronic lesion + intact cortex → Immediate
- Demonstrated Early placement (ITI Type 2) has significantly less MBL than Delayed (−0.400 mm, p=0.0001)
- Challenged the common assumption that waiting longer is safer: Delayed group showed most bone loss
- Provides mechanistic explanation: drilling through mature cortical bone causes necrosis + osteoclast activation → more resorption
- All flapless transmucosal, eliminating re-entry surgery as confound; ZirTi surface; platform-switching

## Methodology

- **Design**: Non-randomized prospective cohort (STROBE-compliant)
- **n**: 85 patients, 131 implants; 3 groups by pre-extractive diagnosis
  - Immediate (Type 1, n=29): chronic periapical lesion, no acute signs, intact cortex
  - Early (Type 2, n=29): acute abscess resolved → 8-12 weeks
  - Delayed (Type 4, n=73): healed site → 10-12 months
- **Implants**: ZirTi blasted surface; tulip platform-switching; flapless transmucosal
- **Gap fill**: porcine corticocancellous bone in immediate group
- **Loading**: 3 months (provisional), then definitive ceramic crown
- **MBL**: periapical radiographs T1/T3/T6/T12/T24; single-blind calibrated measurement
- **Statistics**: multilevel linear regression; Friedman/Wilcoxon/Kruskal-Wallis; Bonferroni

**Location**: Maxilla 0.20 mm less MBL than mandible (p=0.001).

**Survival**: 100% all groups; 1 dropout (early group, 6 months); zero infections/osteitis/loosening.

Clinical decision protocol (Bologna):
- **Immediate**: chronic lesion, no fistula/pus/pain, bone cortex intact
- **Early**: fistula + pus + pain + swelling (acute) → extract → wait 8-12 weeks
- **Delayed**: healthy bone → wait 10-12 months
