## Three-line Summary

Conceptual development study with three-round Delphi consensus and exploratory retrospective review of 64 immediately placed molar implants (38 maxillary, 26 mandibular), proposing a six-category anatomy-based case-selection classification.

Type A (complete septal containment) was most predictable — insertion torque (IT) >30 N/cm and survival >90% — while Type P (palatal root socket) showed implant stability quotient (ISQ) ≥60 and early marginal bone loss (MBL) 0.11–0.19 mm at 6–24 months; cohort means were IT 35 ± 12 N/cm, ISQ 72 ± 8 at placement rising to 78 ± 6 at 4 weeks, and ΔMBL −0.4 ± 0.3 mm at 12 months.

Extends the Smith–Tarnow septal system with root-socket anchorage and a treatment hierarchy, but is explicitly decision support rather than outcome validation — prospective multicenter validation is required before predictive use.

## Summary

This 2026 Journal of Dental Sciences paper proposes a **six-category classification** for immediate implant placement (IIP) in molar extraction sites, designed to overcome the septum-dependence of the Smith–Tarnow A–C system. It combines three septal types (A complete containment, B partial support, C peripheral cortical engagement) with three root-socket anchorage types (D distal, M mesial, P palatal). The system was built by structured literature review, CBCT anatomical analysis, retrospective case-pattern review, and a three-round Delphi consensus (6 specialists), then applied to 64 consecutive molar IIP cases for an exploratory applicability check.

The authors are explicit that this is a **conceptual development / exploratory applicability study**, not an outcome-validation trial: it is meant as a decision-support adjunct to comprehensive anatomical, prosthetic, and patient-specific assessment.

> [!note] Evidence scope
> Consensus-derived classification with an exploratory retrospective cohort (n=64). Stability/MBL numbers are descriptive cohort values, not comparative inter-type outcomes. Do not cite as predictive validity.

## Key Contributions

- Six categories integrating septal bone (A–C) and root-socket anchorage (D/M/P) into one anatomy-driven framework.
- Standardized diagnostic parameters per type (≥3 mm septal support for A; IT ~30 N/cm / ISQ ≥60 stability target; ≥2 mm sinus/nerve clearance; prosthetic emergence feasibility; MBL <0.5 mm/12–24 mo predictability benchmark).
- Explicit **treatment hierarchy**: A first → P (maxilla) / D,M (favorable root sockets) → B,C or delayed (higher risk).
- Type-specific **contraindication thresholds** (P: palatal bone <4 mm or sinus contact <1 mm; D: emergence angle >25° or buccal defect >4 mm; C: buccolingual width <6 mm).
- Type P framed as a **sinus-augmentation-sparing**, minimally invasive option in selected maxillary molars.

## Methodology

- Development: PubMed/MEDLINE 2000–2023 targeted review; CBCT morphometry (Blue Sky Plan); three-round Delphi (6 specialists, ≥80% agreement threshold).
- Reliability: CBCT intraobserver ICC 0.92 (95% CI 0.85–0.97); Delphi κ 0.78 → 0.92 pre/post clarification; final 64-case assignment 100% agreement.
- Exploratory cohort: 64 consecutive single-molar IIP cases (2018–2023), ≥12 mo follow-up, flapless 59/64 (92%), Astra Tech 37 / Osstem 27, platform 5.0–7.0 mm (mean 5.4 ± 0.4 mm).
- Stability: IT by wrench (N/cm); RFA/Osstell ISQ at placement and 4 weeks; MBL from platform to first bone contact via ImageJ.

## Results

- Type distribution (n=64): A 12 (19%), B 15 (23%), C 14 (22%), D 10 (16%), M 8 (12%), P 5 (8%); A/P maxilla-predominant, B/C/D/M mandible-predominant.
- Mean IT 35 ± 12 N/cm; ISQ 72 ± 8 at placement, 78 ± 6 at 4 weeks; ΔMBL −0.4 ± 0.3 mm at 12 months.
- Type A: IT >30 N/cm, survival >90%; Type P: ISQ ≥60, early MBL 0.11–0.19 mm at 6–24 mo, mean palatal inclination 9.6°.
- No major complications (early failure, sinus perforation, MBL >2 mm at 12 mo) in any type; exploratory class–stability association r = 0.67, P < 0.01 (descriptive only).
- Type C required ~6–7 mm wide implants and favorable "hourglass" morphology; mandibular second molars cited at ~9 mm buccolingual width.

## Limitations

- Conceptual/developmental, not powered for inter-type comparison, survival superiority, or predictive accuracy.
- Retrospective single-center evidence base, short-to-mid-term outcomes, technique/implant-system heterogeneity.
- Operator-dependent CBCT interpretation; misclassification in borderline/mixed morphology.
- Macrodesign, loading strategy, bruxism, periodontal history, occlusal pattern, bone quality, and soft-tissue conditions not yet integrated.
