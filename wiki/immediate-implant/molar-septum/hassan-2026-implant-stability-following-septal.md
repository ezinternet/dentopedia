## Three-line Summary

Pilot feasibility RCT (20 patients, 1:1, single center, Alexandria University) comparing piezoelectric implant site preparation (PISP, Intralift Kit) vs osseodensification (OD, Densah burs) for interradicular-septum expansion in immediate mandibular molar implant placement.

Primary and secondary implant stability quotients (ISQ) were comparable (primary 66.75±2.86 vs 69.25±2.86, p=0.082; secondary at 3 months 76.75±2.05 vs 78.50±2.50, p=0.124); PISP produced higher insertion torque (40.33±3.24 vs 35.00±3.53 Ncm, p=0.004) and lower VAS pain (p<0.05) at the cost of longer osteotomy preparation time (9.00±0.94 vs 6.00±0.82 min, p<0.001).

Both techniques achieved clinically acceptable stability with no intraoperative or postoperative complications — PISP is clinically feasible as an alternative to OD for molar septal expansion, though pilot design and differing implant diameters (3.7 vs 4.2 mm) limit the strength of inference.

## Summary

This single-center pilot feasibility RCT randomized 20 patients (1:1) to piezoelectric implant site preparation (PISP, Intralift Kit) or osseodensification (OD, Densah burs) for septal expansion and immediate placement in mandibular molar sockets. Primary and secondary stability as measured by resonance frequency analysis (ISQ) were statistically comparable between groups (primary 66.75±2.86 vs 69.25±2.86, p=0.082; secondary at 3 months 76.75±2.05 vs 78.50±2.50, p=0.124), with significant within-group gains in both. PISP yielded significantly higher insertion torque (40.33 vs 35.00 Ncm, p=0.004) and lower first-week VAS pain (p<0.05) but took significantly longer osteotomy preparation (9.00 vs 6.00 min, p<0.001). All feasibility criteria were met with no dropouts, infections, or failures, supporting PISP as a clinically feasible alternative to OD for anatomically complex immediate molar sites; results are preliminary pending larger trials.

## Key Contributions

- First study applying the Intralift Kit for controlled septal expansion in mandibular molars (authors' claim); first pilot RCT directly comparing PISP vs OD in immediate molar sockets.
- Comparable primary and secondary stability (ISQ p>0.05) between PISP and OD, both rising significantly to 3 months (p<0.001 within group).
- PISP: higher insertion torque (+5.33 Ncm, p=0.004) and lower VAS pain (p<0.05) but longer osteotomy time (+3 min, p<0.001) than OD.
- All implants exceeded the accepted insertion-torque threshold (>30–35 Ncm) regarded as sufficient for predictable primary stability and immediate placement; no intra- or postoperative complications.

## Methodology

Parallel-group pilot feasibility RCT (Nov 2023–Aug 2024), 20 patients (18–40 y), 1:1 computer-generated blocked randomization with sealed opaque envelopes; surgeon unblinded, outcome assessors blinded. Inclusion: septum width ≥2.5 mm, residual bone height ≥5 mm, intact buccal/lingual walls (CBCT), ASA I–II. PISP used Intralift diamond tips TKW1–TKW4 (1.35→2.80 mm) under continuous irrigation; OD used Densah burs VT1525→VT3238 (2.0→3.5 mm), counterclockwise, 800–1500 rpm. Primary outcome: ISQ (Osstell) immediately and at 3 months (mean of bucco-lingual and mesio-distal). Secondary: insertion torque, osteotomy preparation time, first-week VAS pain. Statistics: Shapiro–Wilk/Q–Q, independent/paired t-tests, Mann–Whitney U and Friedman for VAS (α=0.05). Socket gaps grafted with cancellous allograft (MinerOss) and sealed with customized healing abutments.

## Results

|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline bone density (HU-eq) | 523.00±35.92 | 515.00±33.75 | 0.614 |

| Secondary ISQ (3 mo) | 78.50±2.50 | 76.75±2.05 | 0.124 |
| % ISQ increase | 13.40±1.17 | 15.13±4.81 | 0.310 |
| Insertion torque (Ncm) | 35.00±3.53 | 40.33±3.24 | 0.004* |
| Osteotomy time (min) | 6.00±0.82 | 9.00±0.94 | <0.001* |
| VAS pain (week 1) | higher | lower | <0.05* |

Within-group primary→secondary ISQ improved significantly in both groups (p<0.001). No dropouts, no serious adverse events, no early implant failure/infection/wound dehiscence/neurosensory disturbance.
