## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis addressing two focused questions on immediate versus delayed dental implant placement (search through November 2019; MEDLINE, EMBASE, CENTRAL, OpenGray + hand search, 6457 records → 138 full-text): Q1 pooled 6 head-to-head RCTs comparing immediate vs delayed placement (495 patients, 480 implants); Q2 pooled 53 studies (22 RCTs, 11 CCTs, 20 case series) of immediate placement alone.

Q1 meta-analyses found no significant difference in implant survival (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.95–1.01; I²=0%), but immediate placement showed significantly better marginal bone levels and Pink Esthetic Score (PES) at 1-year post-loading; Q2 found immediate implants had a 97.0% pooled 1-year survival (95% CI 95–99%) and high PES scores (10.36–11.25 at 5–10 years); all five meta-regression covariates (flap, restoration timing, grafting, position, follow-up) were non-significant.

Despite similar survival, immediate implants carried threefold early (mainly surgical) and twofold delayed (mainly aesthetic) complication rates versus delayed placement — 11% of immediate implants experienced complications, which would have counted as failures under strict success criteria; success criteria reporting needs to be more consistent and complication incidence/type further explored.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (search through November 2019; MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane CENTRAL, OpenGray + hand search of 11 journals; 6457 records → 138 full-text) tackled the immediate-vs-delayed implant placement timing question with an unusually clean design split. Q1 restricted eligibility to RCTs with a delayed-placement control arm (6 papers, 495 patients, 480 implants — 243 immediate, 237 delayed), yielding a true head-to-head comparative effect size. Q2 broadened eligibility to any immediate-implant arm — RCT arms, CCTs, and prospective case series (53 papers total: 22 RCTs, 11 CCTs, 20 case series) — to estimate the absolute effect size of immediate placement across eight outcome domains: survival, success, radiographic marginal bone levels, buccal wall height/thickness, peri-implant mucosal margin position, esthetics, and patient-reported outcomes.

The headline finding is a dissociation between survival and other outcomes: Q1's RCT-only pooling found no significant survival difference between immediate and delayed placement (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.95–1.01; 5-study MA, I²=0%), but immediate placement was significantly favored for both marginal bone levels and PES at 1-year post-loading (each 2-study MA). Q2's broader pooling of immediate-implant arms alone found a 97.0% 1-year survival rate (95% CI 95–99%) — sustained at 98.0%/97.0%/97.0% at 3/5/8–10 years — and PES scores of 10.36–11.25 at 5–10 years, consistent with immediate placement performing well in absolute terms, though this arm is not directly compared against delayed placement. Meta-regression found none of the five clinical covariates (flap design p=.382, immediate non-occluding restoration p=.688, bone grafting p=.591, anterior/posterior position p=.804, follow-up duration p=.713) significantly affected survival.

Complication reporting is the paper's most clinically actionable — and most under-emphasized — finding: immediate implants showed roughly threefold early complications (24 vs 7; mainly post-surgical) and twofold delayed complications (28 vs 12; mainly aesthetic) relative to delayed placement despite equivalent survival. Pooled across 2426 immediate implants (2077 patients), 11% experienced complications (6% early, 5% late) — under strict success criteria these would have been counted as failures. This decouples "implant survives" from "the course to that survival was uneventful," a distinction easily lost when survival is used as the sole headline metric. Risk of bias was generally moderate-poor: only 2/6 Q1 studies (Felice 2015, Tonetti 2017) and 6/53 Q2 studies rated low risk; allocation concealment and blinding were the most common bias sources.

## Key Contributions

- Separates the immediate-vs-delayed question into a narrow RCT-only comparative analysis (Q1) and a broad pooled single-arm analysis (Q2), rather than blending heterogeneous study designs into one pooled estimate — this design choice is itself a useful evidence-appraisal teaching point (see Related Papers below).
- Meta-analyzes across eight distinct outcome domains (survival, success, MBL, buccal wall height/thickness, mucosal margin position, esthetics, PROMs) rather than survival alone, and adds meta-regression calibration across five clinical covariates — all non-significant for survival.
- Explicitly quantifies the complication-rate cost of immediate placement (3x early, 2x delayed; 11% of immediate implants overall, with surgical and aesthetic complications dominant) even where survival is statistically equivalent — the first SR to report this dimension for immediate placement.

## Methodology

- **Search**: MEDLINE (Ovid, 1950–present), EMBASE Classic+EMBASE (Ovid, 1947–present), Cochrane CENTRAL, OpenGray + hand/cross-reference search of 11 journals (2001–Nov 2019), no language restriction. PRISMA (Moher 2010), PROSPERO CRD42020186059.
- **Screening**: 6457 records → 138 full-text; duplicate review with kappa 0.762 (Q1) / 0.782 (Q2); third-reviewer arbitration.
- **Q1 eligibility**: RCTs with delayed implant placement (types 2–4, ≥6 weeks post-extraction) as control — 6 papers, 495 patients, 480 implants.
- **Q2 eligibility**: immediate-implant arms of RCTs, CCTs, and prospective case series — 53 papers (22 RCT arms, 11 CCT arms, 20 case series).
- **Inclusion thresholds**: ≥10 patients/group (RCTs), ≥20 patients (single arms/case series), ≥12 months post-loading; retrospective, medically compromised, full-occluding immediate-loading, and adjacent-implant studies excluded.
- **Risk of bias**: modified Cochrane tool (Higgins & Green 2011), 7 domains, 2 independent assessors.
- **Statistics**: random-effects meta-analysis throughout; RR with 95% CI (dichotomous, Q1); proportion estimates with 95% CI (Q2); meta-regression on flap, GBR/grafting, restoration timing, membrane presence, position. Stata 13.
- **Outcomes**: survival rate, success rate, radiographic marginal bone levels, buccal wall height/thickness, peri-implant mucosal margin position (recession), esthetic outcomes (PES/WES), patient-reported outcomes.

## Results

|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (comparative vs delayed) | 6 RCTs, 480 implants | RR 0.98 (95% CI 0.95–1.01), I²=0%, no significant difference; trend favoring delayed | MBL and PES significantly favor immediate at 1-year post-loading (2-study MAs); complications 24E+28L vs 7E+12L |
| Q2 (pooled single-arm immediate) | 53 studies (22 RCT + 11 CCT + 20 case series) | 1-yr 97.0% (95–99%); 3-yr 98.0%; 5-yr 97.0%; 8–10-yr 97.0% | PES 10.36–11.25 (5–10 yr); MBL +0.08 to −0.88 mm at 1 yr; 11% complication rate |

- **Meta-regression (Q2)**: flap design p=.382, immediate non-occluding restoration p=.688, bone graft p=.591, implant position p=.804, follow-up duration p=.713 — none significantly influenced survival.
- **Complications (Q2)**: 2426 immediate implants / 2077 patients; 11% experienced complications — 6% early (surgical 2.8%, biological 2.3%) and 5% late (biological 2.3%, aesthetic 2.0%). Raw counts: 147 early (126 post-surgical dominant) + 119 late (58 biological, 49 aesthetic).
- **Success (Q1+Q2)**: 84.6–100% (Q1 immediate) reported, but criteria non-uniform across all three reporting Q1 studies; 16 Q2 studies reported success (66.7–100%) — no success meta-analysis possible in either question.
- **Marginal bone (Q2)**: 1-yr range +0.08 mm gain to −0.88 mm loss; 2-yr +0.1 to −1.0 mm; 3-yr +0.17 to −1.1 mm; 5-yr +0.2 to −1.08 mm. Gingival margin 0.23 mm gain to 0.71 mm loss at 1–5 yr; papilla recession <0.5 mm at ≥5 yr.
- **Risk of bias**: Q1 — 2 low, 2 unclear, 2 high (Block 2009, Tsirlis 2005). Q2 — 6 low, 12 unclear, 27/53 high in ≥1 domain; allocation concealment and blinding most common sources.
- **PROMs (Q2)**: only 11/53 studies reported PROMs; only 4 used a validated tool (OHIP-14).
