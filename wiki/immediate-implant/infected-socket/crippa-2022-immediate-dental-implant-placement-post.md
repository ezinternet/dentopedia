## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (2014-2019, 98 patients, 149 Straumann implants: 90 immediate implants in infected sockets decontaminated with Er,Cr:YSGG laser vs 59 conventional implants in healed edentulous sites), mean follow-up 1.7 vs 1.5 years.

One failure in the laser group (1%) and none in controls; marginal bone level (MBL) changed +0.1 mm (test) vs -0.1 mm (control), a 0.2 mm difference that was not significant (P = 0.058).

Laser-treated infected sockets did not show a higher failure risk in the short term, but the comparison is non-randomized, the control is a delayed healed site, and antibiotics/chlorhexidine were co-interventions, so the laser effect itself cannot be isolated.

## Summary

Crippa et al. (Odontology 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10266-022-00734-4), PubMed 36074306) compared immediate implants placed into post-extraction infected sockets that were debrided and decontaminated only with an Er,Cr:YSGG laser against conventional implants placed in healed edentulous sites at least 3 months after extraction. Across 149 implants in 98 patients, there was one failure (test group, 1%) and no failures in controls. MBL change favored the laser group by 0.2 mm (+0.1 vs -0.1 mm), not statistically significant (P = 0.058). The paper does not define infection by acute/chronic category or purulence; it records "abscess or fistula" and "lesion" as baseline covariates, with lesions more frequent in the test group. Because antibiotics and chlorhexidine were also used and the design was retrospective and non-randomized, the authors state that a causal laser effect cannot be established.

## Key Contributions

- Controlled (non-randomized) comparison of laser-decontaminated infected-socket immediate implants versus healed-site implants; authors describe it as the only such study for the Er,Cr:YSGG 2780 nm laser.
- Fully specified laser parameters for reproducibility.
- Practical candidate-selection rules (healthy, preferably non-smoking, no untreated periodontal disease; atraumatic extraction; antibiotic and chlorhexidine; biomaterials for defects).

## Methodology

- Retrospective series 2014-2019 (University of Valencia / Istituto Stomatologico Italiano), STROBE; no sample size calculation; minimum 1-year follow-up.
- Exclusions: significant systemic disease, radiotherapy, steroids, immunocompromise, bruxism, smoking >15/day, alcohol/drug use, poor compliance.
- Test: atraumatic extraction, laser flap and debridement (MC-3 and MZ-8 tips, 20/80 and 40/60 air/water), then decontamination in hard-tissue mode 2.0 W, 20% air/80% water, MZ-6 9 mm tip non-contact 1-2 mm from bone, 60-90 s per socket; Straumann implant same visit; collagen plus absorbable membrane, often synthetic bone; antibiotic from the night before; 0.2% chlorhexidine gel for 2 weeks; immediate loading or loading at 3 months.
- Control: similar implant protocol in sites healed at least 3 months, no laser.
- Outcome: MBL on periapical radiographs (ImageJ, blinded, ICC 0.97), mixed model with patient random effect and baseline MBL covariate.
- Tables and figures were not available in the retrieved full text.

## Results

| Item | Laser test group | Control (healed site) |
|---|---|---|
| Implants | 90 (60%) | 59 (40%) |
| Mean follow-up | 1.7 +/- 0.6 y | 1.5 +/- 0.5 y (P = 0.082) |
| Failures | 1 (1%) | 0 |
| Complications | none besides the failure | 1 mucositis (2%) |
| MBL change vs baseline | +0.1 mm | -0.1 mm |
| Between-group MBL difference | 0.2 mm, P = 0.058 (NS) | |

Overall: 98 patients, mean age 58.0 +/- 14.6 y, 22 smokers. Baseline imbalances: more lesions, longer implants, more membrane and synthetic bone in the test group. Extraction reasons (test): caries 36%, endodontic 11%, fracture 48%, periodontal 6%.
