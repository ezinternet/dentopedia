## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (single private clinic, Rio de Janeiro, 2006–2018; single surgeon): 423 immediate implants in 186 patients, 215 placed into sockets of teeth with chronic apical periodontitis (CAP) and 208 into sockets without it; mean follow-up 39.4 months.

Overall implant survival 91% (385/423): 88.8% (191/215) in CAP sockets vs 93.3% (194/208) in non-CAP sockets, a difference that was not significant (bivariate p=0.111; GEE adjusted OR 2.36, 95% CI 0.70–7.97, p=0.167), whereas smoking >20 cigarettes/day (adjusted OR 7.66, 95% CI 1.56–37.49, p=0.012) and short implants of 6.0–8.5 mm (adjusted OR 14.06, 95% CI 2.67–73.95, p=0.002) independently predicted failure.

Chronic apical periodontitis was not a significant failure predictor here, but the CIs are wide (only 5 of 26 short implants failed; 3 of 11 heavy smokers), acute or purulent infection was not analyzed, and non-random retrospective records with no lesion-size data limit inference — prefer longer implants and address heavy smoking before immediate placement in infected sites.

## Summary

This single-clinic, single-surgeon retrospective cohort (Rio de Janeiro, 2006–2018) examined which subject- and implant-level factors predict failure of immediate implants, using sockets of teeth with radiographic chronic apical periodontitis (CAP) as the "infected" group. Of 423 implants in 186 patients, 215 were in CAP sockets. Sockets were curetted of granulation tissue and irrigated with saline; all patients received postoperative antibiotics. Survival was 91% overall, 88.8% in CAP sockets and 93.3% in non-CAP sockets; the difference was not significant in bivariate (p=0.111) or GEE-adjusted analysis (OR 2.36, 95% CI 0.70–7.97, p=0.167). Heavy smoking (>20 cigarettes/day) and short implants (6.0–8.5 mm) were the two significant independent predictors.

**What the paper says about infection type:** only chronic apical periodontitis, defined radiographically (periapical, panoramic or CBCT radiolucency at the root apex), was studied. The paper does not analyze acute infection, purulence, fistula or periodontal-origin infection as separate categories, so it offers no acute-versus-chronic comparison.

**Reading against the rest of the wiki [미검증 — Claude 해석]:** the non-significant CAP effect is compatible with the held reports that chronic periapical lesions are not an absolute contraindication when the socket is debrided, but the 95% CI (0.70–7.97) is too wide to exclude a clinically important increase in failure, so it neither confirms nor refutes the higher-risk estimate in [[immediate-implant/infected-socket/de-oliveira-neto-2019-immediate-dental-implants-placed-into]]. Its main added value is the modifier signal: the two predictors were short length and heavy smoking, not infection status.

## Key Contributions

- GEE-adjusted failure predictors in a 423-implant cohort with a 215-implant chronic apical periodontitis subgroup.
- Adjusted OR 7.66 (95% CI 1.56–37.49) for >20 cigarettes/day and 14.06 (2.67–73.95) for short implants, both independent of CAP status.
- Long-term follow-up up to and beyond 84 months (300 implants reviewed at 84 months).

## Methodology

- Retrospective cohort from records and imaging (periapical, panoramic, CBCT) at one private clinic; one specialist surgeon; single or multiple immediate implants; minimum 12 months follow-up after loading; smokers and monitored systemic disease allowed; previously healed sites and prior implant failures excluded.
- Groups: teeth/roots with chronic apical periodontitis (radiolucency at the apex; kappa 0.810 on 50 random images between operator and an endodontist) vs without.
- Protocol: atraumatic extraction, thorough curettage of granulation tissue, saline irrigation; postoperative antibiotics in all patients, preoperative antibiotics only when needed; immediate or delayed loading.
- Analysis: chi-square/Fisher/Mann-Whitney bivariate tests; variables with p<0.20 entered a forward stepwise GEE logistic model accounting for multiple implants per patient; required sample 158 sites (G*Power, w=0.30, power 90%).

## Results

| Factor | Failure / survival | Test |
|---|---|---|
| CAP sockets vs non-CAP | 24/215 (11.2%) vs 14/208 (6.7%) | bivariate p=0.111; GEE OR 2.36 (0.70–7.97), p=0.167 |
| >20 cigarettes/day vs non-smoker | 3/11 (27.3%) vs 9/131 (6.9%) | GEE OR 7.66 (1.56–37.49), p=0.012 |
| <=20 cigarettes/day vs non-smoker | 2/21 (9.5%) vs 9/131 (6.9%) | GEE OR 1.31 (0.26–6.58), p=0.745 |
| Short (6.0–8.5 mm) vs long (9.0–18.0 mm) | 5/26 (19.2%) vs 33/397 (8.3%) | bivariate p=0.059; GEE OR 14.06 (2.67–73.95), p=0.002 |

Overall survival 91% (385/423) by implant and 91.4% by patient; 93.4% at 12 months. Not associated with failure: age (p=0.812), sex (p=0.317), systemic condition (p=0.410), diameter (p=0.295), arch region (p=0.498), insertion torque (p=0.596), prosthesis type (p=0.656), immediate loading (9/82 failed, p=0.482). Peri-implantitis affected 71 implants (10 failed, 14.1%); the 102 mucositis cases all survived (peri-implant status and prosthetic adaptation were excluded from GEE for multicollinearity). Limitations: incomplete records, no data on grafting, lesion size, plaque or periodontal history; few events for the short-implant and heavy-smoker estimates.
