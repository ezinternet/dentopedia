## Three-line Summary

Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR, JBI methodology; 72 records screened, 24 clinical studies 2015–2025 included) mapping evidence on immediate implant placement (IIP) in infected extraction sockets.

Most included studies were case reports/series or non-comparative observational designs; atraumatic extraction, meticulous debridement, and primary stability were the consistently reported procedural keys, with favorable survival/MBL/esthetics reported overall.

Evidence is feasible-suggestive but heterogeneous and largely non-comparative — no standardized clinical protocol for infected-socket IIP can currently be recommended; well-designed comparative studies are still needed.

## Summary

This scoping review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.4317/medoral.28256)), published in the same journal as [[immediate-implant/infected-socket/munoz-camara-2020-immediate-implants-acute-periapical-infected]], maps — rather than pools — the clinical evidence on immediate implant placement (IIP) into infected extraction sockets. Following JBI scoping-review methodology and PRISMA-ScR reporting, the authors searched six databases through 2026-03-17, screened 72 de-duplicated records, and included 24 clinical studies published 2015–2025 (case reports, case series, retrospective and prospective cohorts, one split-mouth study, one RCT). Most addressed endodontic/periapical infected sockets; periodontal and mixed-etiology infected sockets were comparatively under-studied. Across the included literature, atraumatic extraction, meticulous socket debridement, and achievement of adequate primary stability were the consistently reported procedural keys, alongside a wide range of adjuncts (systemic antibiotics, chlorhexidine decontamination, bone grafts, barrier membranes, PRF/platelet concentrates, laser decontamination, vestibular socket techniques, immediate provisionalization). While outcomes were generally favorable, the review's central finding is methodological: the evidence base is heterogeneous, largely non-comparative, and lacks a standardized infection classification or decontamination protocol — so no single protocol can currently be recommended.

## Key Contributions

- First scoping (mapping) review of infected-socket IIP literature, complementing effect-pooling SR+MA work such as [[immediate-implant/infected-socket/pranckeviciene-2024-immediate-implant-periapical-pathology-sr-ma]]
- Catalogues the full adjunctive-intervention landscape (antibiotics, CHX, grafts, membranes, PRF/CGF, laser, vestibular socket technique, immediate provisionalization) rather than isolating one variable
- Surfaces a study-design skew (case reports/series, non-comparative) that quantitative SR+MA papers restricting to controlled studies (e.g., Pranckeviciene's 6-study final set) do not make visible
- Explicitly separates endodontic/periapical from periodontal/mixed infected-socket etiologies as under-studied subgroups

## Methodology

- **Design**: Scoping review, JBI methodology, PRISMA-ScR reporting
- **Databases**: Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, PubMed/MEDLINE, LILACS (BVS), SciELO, ClinicalTrials.gov; search date to 2026-03-17
- **Screening**: 72 de-duplicated records → independent dual-reviewer screening → 24 included clinical studies (2015–2025)
- **Included designs**: case reports, case series, retrospective cohorts, prospective cohorts, 1 split-mouth study, 1 RCT
- No meta-analysis or formal risk-of-bias grading performed (by scoping-review design)

## Results

- Consistently reported procedural keys: atraumatic extraction, meticulous socket debridement, adequate primary stability
- Adjuncts documented (descriptive, not pooled): systemic antibiotics, CHX decontamination, bone grafts, barrier membranes, PRF/platelet concentrates, laser therapy, vestibular socket technique, immediate provisionalization
- Generally favorable implant survival, marginal bone stability, and esthetic outcomes reported across studies — but not quantitatively synthesized
- No standardized infection-severity classification or decontamination protocol identified across the 24 studies
