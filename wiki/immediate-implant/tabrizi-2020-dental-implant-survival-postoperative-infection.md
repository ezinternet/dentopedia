## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional cohort (n=980 implant patients, Tehran, Iran): 34 (3.46%) developed acute postoperative infection — unique focus on survival AFTER the infection occurs, not pre-existing socket pathology.

Failure rate 70.6% (24/34); survival predictors: no smoking, delayed placement, no bone graft, early infection diagnosis; without post-op antibiotics, HR for failure ×1.1 from day 4; with antibiotics, critical threshold day 6 (smokers) / day 9 (non-smokers).

Acute postoperative infection is a high-risk event (70.6% failure); antibiotic timing, fresh-socket avoidance in high-risk patients, and smoking cessation are key preventive levers.

## Summary

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed, this cross-sectional cohort ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12663-020-01460-6)) examined 980 implant patients in Tehran and identified 34 (3.46%) who developed acute postoperative infection. This is a distinct clinical scenario from pre-existing socket infection: the implant is placed in what appears to be a healthy site, but infection develops in the early postoperative period. Of the 34 infected cases, only 10 (29.4%) survived — a 70.6% failure rate. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed time-dependent failure thresholds: without postoperative antibiotics, failure risk increased by a hazard ratio of 1.1 from day 4 post-surgery. With antibiotics, smokers tolerate until day 6 and non-smokers until day 9 before failure risk significantly increases. Smoking, fresh-socket placement, and bone graft use were the three significant failure predictors (all p<0.05).

## Key Contributions

- Quantified failure rate (70.6%) specifically for acute postoperative infection — far higher than baseline implant failure
- Established antibiotic-sensitive failure timing thresholds (day 4 without / day 6–9 with antibiotics)
- Identified fresh socket as independent risk factor for infection-related failure
- Supports postoperative antibiotic prescription for fresh-socket/smoking/bone graft cases

## Methodology

- **Design**: Cross-sectional cohort
- **Population**: 980 implant surgery patients, single center, Tehran, Iran
- **Outcome**: failed vs survived among 34 patients who developed acute postoperative infection
- **Variables**: placement site, age/gender, bone augmentation, postoperative antibiotic therapy, smoking, infection diagnosis timing
- **Statistics**: Kaplan-Meier survival analysis; hazard ratio calculation

## Results

| Factor | Survived (n=10) | Failed (n=24) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smokers | Fewer | More | <0.05 |
| Fresh socket | Fewer | More | <0.05 |
| Bone graft | Fewer | More | <0.05 |
| Mean infection diagnosis day | Later | Earlier | <0.05 |

**Kaplan-Meier failure thresholds:**

| Antibiotic status | Smoking | Critical day |
|---|---|---|
| No post-op antibiotic | — | Day 4 (HR +1.1×) |
| Post-op antibiotic | Smoker | Day 6 |
| Post-op antibiotic | Non-smoker | Day 9 |
