## Three-line Summary

Retrospective case series, 107 REPLICATE (NDI Berlin) root-analogue implants (RAIs) placed in one German private practice (Sept 2016–Aug 2019), 31 RAIs in 28 patients available for full clinical/radiographic/esthetic follow-up.

Survival 94.4% (mean follow-up 18.9 ± 2.4 months); mean marginal bone loss (MBL) 1.20 ± 0.73 mm at follow-up with significantly slower MBL progression after loading (p = 0.013); combined 4-domain success only 64.5%, held back by implant-level (80.8%) and prosthetic/marginal-fit (83.3%) shortfalls despite strong soft-tissue (96.8%) and patient-satisfaction (90.0%) results.

Clinical implication: immediate RAI placement did not outperform literature benchmarks for conventional screw-shaped immediate implants on MBL, and showed weaker marginal fit at ~15% higher cost — the authors conclude RAIs are not yet justified for routine use, though esthetics/soft-tissue stability were favorable (this is the largest single-center RAI series and the primary source of the hybrid-implant MBL benchmark cited by later systematic reviews).

## Summary

This retrospective case series is the largest single-center clinical dataset on the REPLICATE Immediate Tooth Replacement System (NDI Berlin), a customized root-analogue implant (RAI) that is subtractively milled from pre-extraction CBCT/intraoral-scan data to replicate the exact morphology of the tooth being removed, then hammered into the fresh socket immediately after extraction. Of 107 RAIs placed by a single surgeon over three years, only 31 (in 28 patients) received full protocol follow-up (independent examiner, blinded radiographic measurement) — the remainder's survival was confirmed by phone or referring-dentist records.

Applying a comprehensive 4-domain success framework (implant level, peri-implant soft tissue, prosthetic level, patient satisfaction), the study found a striking gap between survival (94.4%) and true combined success (64.5%): RAIs were reliably retained and esthetically/subjectively satisfying, but underperformed on radiographic bone stability and prosthetic marginal fit compared to what is expected of conventional screw-shaped immediate implants. Notably, the manufacturer (NDI Berlin) ceased business operations during the study period, which the authors cite alongside the modest net benefit as evidence the RAI concept had not yet proven itself.

## Key Contributions

- Largest reported single-center RAI series (107 placed, 31 followed with full protocol), with bias-reducing role separation (independent follow-up examiner, independent radiographic measurer, distinct from the treating surgeon).
- Demonstrates the survival-vs-success gap explicitly: 94.4% survival but only 64.5% combined 4-domain success — a distinction frequently blurred in smaller RAI case reports.
- Shows MBL progression significantly slows after prosthetic loading vs. the healing phase (p=0.013), paralleling known screw-implant bone-remodeling biology.
- Tests 12 candidate MBL predictors (gender, age, arch, region, material, implant/root/abutment length, surface area, bone quality, surgical difficulty, root number) — none reach significance, a useful null result for risk stratification given the small sample.
- Provides the primary-study source for the hybrid titanium-zirconia RAI MBL benchmark (1.20 ± 0.73 mm) subsequently cited in [[immediate-implant/alqutaibi-2026-root-analog-dental-implants]]'s pooled systematic review table.

## Methodology

Single-center, single-surgeon retrospective case series (Berlin, Germany; Charité ethics approval EA4/140/18). 107 REPLICATE RAIs (one-piece hybrid titanium/zirconia-abutment or one-piece all-zirconia, macro/micro-retentive surface) placed Sept 2016–Aug 2019 in carefully selected non-smoking patients with intact bone and non-inflamed periodontium confirmed on CBCT. Atraumatic extraction (Benex system where feasible) + Try-In fit check + PRGF wetting + mallet seating + adjacent-tooth temporary protective cover for load protection; final restoration after 3–6 month healing check.

Outcome assessment used a 4-domain success framework (per a 2012 SR methodology): (1) implant level — radiographic MBL at surgery/loading/follow-up via ImageJ, Albrektsson-style criteria; (2) peri-implant soft tissue — mPI, mBI (Mombelli), keratinized gingiva width ≥1.5 mm; (3) prosthetic level — PES/WES (threshold ≥6) and modified USPHS 7-criterion grading; (4) patient satisfaction — VAS ≥80% for esthetics and chewing. All 4 domains had to be individually successful for overall case success. Statistics via SciPy: paired t-tests, Welch's t-tests for subgroups, Pearson correlations, α=0.05.

## Results

- **Survival**: 94.4% (101/107 RAIs in situ; mean observation 18.9 ± 2.4 months). 6 failures: 4 failed osseointegration during healing, 2 lost after prosthetic delivery.
- **Cohort followed in detail**: 31 RAIs (28 patients, 11 M/17 F, mean age 55.3 ± 14.2 y); 25 hybrid + 6 all-zirconia; 29 single crowns + 1 three-unit FDP; mean healing-to-loading 6.6 ± 2.5 months.
- **Marginal bone loss (MBL)**: 0.82 ± 0.54 mm surgery→loading; 1.20 ± 0.73 mm surgery→follow-up (mean 12.1 ± 6.9 months post-loading); 0.40 ± 0.41 mm loading→follow-up — significantly slower progression after loading (p=0.013). No tested variable (12 candidates) significantly predicted MBL.
- **Implant-level success**: 80.8% (21/26 radiographically evaluable RAIs).
- **Soft-tissue success**: 96.8% (30/31); mPI 0.6±0.5, mBI 0.6±0.7, keratinized gingiva 3.9±1.7 mm.
- **Prosthetic-level success**: 83.3% (25/30); PES/WES 15.35 ± 2.33 (PES 7.45±1.50, WES 7.90±1.74); marginal integrity was the weakest USPHS domain (soundable/explorer-penetrable gaps common, though none required replacement).
- **Patient satisfaction**: 90.0% (27/30); esthetics VAS 91.6±17.5%, chewing VAS 89.1±18.9%.
- **Combined overall success (all 4 domains)**: 64.5% (20/31).
- **Cost**: ~15% higher than conventional screw-shaped implants (including bone/soft-tissue augmentation cost of the comparator).
