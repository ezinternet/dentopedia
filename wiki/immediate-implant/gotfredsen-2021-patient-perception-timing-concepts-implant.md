## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 35–40 randomized/prospective trials evaluating patient perception (PROMs: discomfort, satisfaction, esthetics) of immediate vs early vs conventional/delayed implant placement and loading.

No strong evidence that placement or loading timing alone influences patient discomfort, functional/esthetic satisfaction, or overall satisfaction with implant treatment; some weak evidence for transient patient preference for immediate loading in edentulous full-arch cases at short follow-up.

Timing protocols are not driven by patient preference alone; clinical outcomes (bone loss, esthetics) remain primary decision drivers; long-term PROM data remain sparse.

## 세줄요약

즉시·조기·종래 임플란트 식립 및 부하 타이밍에 대한 환자 인식(환자보고결과 Patient-Reported Outcome Measure, PROM: 불편감, 만족도, 심미도)을 평가한 35~40건의 무작위/전향적 시험의 체계적 문헌고찰(Systematic Review, SR).

식립 또는 부하 타이밍 단독으로는 환자의 불편감, 기능/심미 만족도, 전체 임플란트 치료 만족도에 강한 근거가 없음; 무치악 완전틀니(Full-Arch Edentulous) 환자군에서만 즉시부하(Immediate Loading, IL)에 대한 일시적 환자 선호도의 약한 근거가 있음(단기 추적).

타이밍 프로토콜은 환자 선호도만으로 결정되지 않음; 임상적 결과(골소실, 심미도)가 주요 결정 요인으로 남음; 장기 PROM 자료는 여전히 부족함.

## Summary

This systematic review (PRISMA-guided; literature search to 30 June 2021) synthesizes patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) — discomfort, functional/esthetic satisfaction, and overall treatment satisfaction — across 40 articles representing 35 unique cohorts comparing immediate, early, and conventional/delayed implant placement and loading protocols. Two independent reviewers screened 1,439 initially identified records via Rayyan, evaluated 76 in full text, and assessed risk of bias with Cochrane RoB2.0 (RCTs) and the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (prospective cohorts); PROM-instrument heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis, so synthesis remained qualitative. For single-tooth and short-span reconstructions, no strong evidence emerged that placement or loading timing alone affects patient discomfort or satisfaction. For full-arch edentulous reconstructions, some evidence suggests immediate loading yields higher short-term (≤1 year) patient satisfaction than early or conventional loading, though this advantage becomes unclear by 12 months. The authors conclude that timing-protocol selection should not be driven by patient preference alone, since clinical outcomes remain the primary determinant and long-term PROM data are still sparse.

## Key Contributions

- Systematic mapping of PROMs used across the implant-timing literature, identifying discomfort, functional/esthetic satisfaction, and overall treatment satisfaction as the primary recurring endpoints.
- Quantitative/qualitative synthesis showing no strong evidence that immediate vs delayed placement or loading affects patient discomfort or satisfaction in single-tooth or short-span reconstructions.
- Isolates a full-arch edentulous-specific signal: transient patient preference for immediate loading over early/delayed protocols at ≤1-year follow-up, weakening by 12 months.
- Quality critique: >50% of included trials show "some concerns" on risk of bias, and PROM instrument heterogeneity (VAS, NRS, validated vs non-validated questionnaires) limits cross-study comparability.
- Clinical framing: reframes timing-protocol choice as a trade-off between patient experience and clinician-measured outcomes rather than a patient-preference-driven decision.

## Methodology

Systematic review (PRISMA) of RCTs and prospective controlled cohort studies comparing immediate/early vs conventional/delayed implant placement or loading, with PROMs as a primary or secondary endpoint. Search across PubMed, Cochrane, and Scopus to 30 June 2021; 1,439 records screened, 76 full-text reviewed, 40 articles (35 unique cohorts) included. Risk of bias: Cochrane RoB2.0 (RCTs), Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (prospective cohorts). Outcomes: patient-reported discomfort, satisfaction with function, satisfaction with esthetics, overall satisfaction with implant treatment. Analysis: qualitative synthesis only — quantitative meta-analysis was not feasible given PROM-instrument heterogeneity.

## Results

| Population | Outcome | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Single-tooth / short-span | Discomfort | No strong evidence immediate placement reduces discomfort vs delayed |
| Single-tooth / short-span | Function/esthetic satisfaction | No strong evidence immediate/early loading improves satisfaction vs conventional |
| Single-tooth / short-span | Overall satisfaction | No consistent difference across timing protocols |
| Full-arch edentulous | Short-term (≤1y) satisfaction | Some evidence favoring immediate loading over early/delayed loading |
| Full-arch edentulous | ≥12-month satisfaction | Difference between protocols unclear |
| All included studies | Risk of bias | >50% rated "some concerns" (randomization concealment, selective reporting, performance/detection bias) |

Pommer's SR+MA cross-tabulates placement×loading timing against clinician-measured long-term (≥3y) survival and marginal bone remodeling in maxillary single-tooth implants, finding no significant differences across any combination tested. This review covers the patient-reported side of the same timing debate (discomfort, satisfaction, esthetics) across a broader population (single-tooth through full-arch edentulous). The two are complementary, not superseding — clinician-measured and patient-reported axes of the same ITI timing question, and neither's conclusion overturns the other's.
- [[immediate-implant/loading-protocol/huynh-ba-2018-immediate-loading-vs-early-conventional]] — narrower predecessor PROM systematic review (partially edentulous, single-tooth, Type 1/immediately-placed implants only), reaching a consistent bottom line: patient satisfaction is uniformly high regardless of loading protocol, with little difference between immediate and early/conventional loading. This review reinforces that finding while broadening scope to include placement timing and full-arch edentulous populations, where a modest, short-term immediate-loading preference emerges that Huynh-Ba's narrower single-tooth population could not detect.
- [[immediate-implant/loading-protocol/pannuti-2026-loading-timing-edentulous-maxilla-pro]] — later, narrower SR cataloging which PROM/CROM instruments have actually been used in edentulous-maxilla loading-timing trials. It extends this review's full-arch edentulous finding (transient immediate-loading preference at short follow-up) by showing that instrument non-standardization (VAS dominates 80% of studies, no cross-study consensus on clinician-reported measures) is a likely reason that preference signal is weak and fades past 12 months.

## Glossary

- **PROM (Patient-Reported Outcome Measure)**: Validated or non-validated questionnaire/scale capturing patient perception of treatment burden (discomfort), functional satisfaction, esthetic satisfaction, or overall satisfaction.
- **Immediate loading**: Prosthetic restoration (crown, bridge, or denture) placed on the implant at the time of surgery or within 24 hours.
- **Early loading**: Prosthetic restoration placed within 3–6 months after implant placement.
- **Conventional (delayed) loading**: Prosthetic restoration placed after 3–6 months of osseointegration (traditional 4–6 month wait).
- **Immediate placement (Type 1)**: Implant inserted into a fresh extraction socket immediately after tooth removal.
