## Three-line Summary

Prospective single-arm clinical trial, n=30 patients receiving custom-made two-piece ZrO2 (ZV3) implants replacing a single missing maxillary premolar, followed up to 5 years (median 63.0 months) at University Medical Center Groningen.

Implant survival at 5 years was 75.8% (95% CI 60.0-91.0%) and success 71.0% (95% CI 54.0-88.0%); 9/30 implants were lost — 4 early osseointegration failures and 5 horizontal mechanical fractures — with no significant survival difference by implant manipulation (81.3% vs 68.6%) or occlusal scheme (canine guidance 80.0% vs group function 73.8%).

Survival was substantially lower than the literature benchmark for zirconia implants (~95.6% at 1 year), and the authors explicitly concluded ZV3 implant placement as applied "cannot be recommended for clinical practice," attributing the high fracture rate to off-axis (palatal) placement and lateral occlusal overload rather than to zirconia as a material per se.

## Summary

This is the longest-running prospective clinical trial of custom-made, two-piece, metal-free zirconia (ZrO2, ZV3 system) dental implants identified in the wiki's root-analog/custom-implant evidence base, following 30 patients with single-tooth maxillary premolar replacements out to 5 years (median 63.0 months, range up to 77 months). Implant survival fell from 83.3% at 1 year to 75.8% (95% CI 60.0-91.0%) at 5 years, with success dropping to 71.0% (95% CI 54.0-88.0%) — both notably below the ~95.6% 1-year benchmark reported elsewhere in the zirconia-implant literature. Nine of 30 implants were lost: 4 early osseointegration failures and 5 horizontal mechanical fractures, an unusually high fracture rate the authors could not fully explain even after direct comparison with a related same-institution cohort (Becker/Brunello, standard ZV3 implants) that saw zero implant fractures over up to 9 years. Neither intraoral buccal manipulation of the implant nor occlusal scheme (canine guidance vs group function) reached statistical significance as risk factors, though the trends (manipulated implants surviving *better*, not worse) argue against manipulation-induced fracture as a primary driver. Despite the poor survival/success figures, patient-reported satisfaction was high and significantly exceeded pre-treatment expectations across multiple domains, and the surviving implant-supported crowns performed well with no failures or interventions required. The authors' bottom line is unambiguous: this specific custom ZrO2 implant protocol, as applied, "cannot be recommended for clinical practice," and they call for a larger RCT and further mechanistic research into ZrO2 implant fracture before broader adoption.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the SR-flagged **lowest-bound zirconia-RAI survival figure (75.8% at 5 years)** — the cautionary counterweight to more favorable short-term zirconia survival data cited elsewhere.
- Directly tests two candidate risk factors (intraoral manipulation, occlusal scheme) for zirconia implant fracture/failure via retrospective stratified Kaplan-Meier analysis; neither reaches significance, and manipulation trends protective rather than harmful — challenging the common assumption that post-sintering intraoral adjustment predictably weakens ZrO2 implants.
- Documents an unusually concentrated mechanical (horizontal) fracture pattern (5/30, ~17%) occurring largely after prosthetic loading, contrasted against a same-institution cohort with the standard (non-custom) ZV3 design that saw zero implant fractures — implicating implant design/placement axis over material class.
- Shows a dissociation between poor objective survival/success metrics and strongly positive patient-reported outcomes, illustrating that PROMs alone are insufficient to judge whether an implant system should be recommended.

## Methodology

Prospective single-arm clinical trial, 31 patients enrolled / 30 treated (single missing maxillary premolar, indication for implant, bone height >=10 mm below the sinus, no augmentation needed). Custom-made two-piece ZrO2 implants (ZV3, Zircon Vision GmbH) individually milled per CBCT + intraoral scan data, placed in native bone (no augmentation) by experienced operators, restored ~15 weeks later with a lithium disilicate crown on the original ZV3 fiberglass post. Follow-up at baseline (T1, 2 weeks post-crown), 1 year (T12), and ~5 years (T60). Outcomes: Kaplan-Meier implant survival/success (Albrektsson-type criteria) with 95% CI, stratified log-rank comparisons by manipulation status and occlusal scheme, radiographic MBL change, periodontal clinical parameters, FDI crown criteria, and a 16-item VAS PROM questionnaire compared via Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.

## Results

- Implant survival: 83.3% (1y) -> 75.8% (95% CI 60.0-91.0%) at 5y -> 59.6% at study end (77mo). 9/30 implants lost (4 early osseointegration failure, 5 horizontal mechanical fracture).
- No significant survival difference by manipulation (81.3% manipulated vs 68.6% not manipulated, p=0.590) or occlusal scheme (80.0% canine guidance vs 73.8% group function, p=0.745).
- Implant success at 5y: 71.0% (95% CI 54.0-88.0%).
- MBL stable and low: -0.26 mm (1y), -0.25 mm (5y), no significant change over time, no annual loss >0.2 mm detected after year 1.
- Crown performance (n=14 at T60): no failures/interventions; aesthetic scores declined but stayed clinically acceptable; 30% lost a strong approximal contact point (lower than 43%/58% comparators).
- PROMs: satisfaction significantly exceeded pre-treatment expectations across self-confidence, loading confidence, chewing ability, implant visibility, and pain during function (all p<=0.035).
- Authors' conclusion: survival "lower than expected and clinically not acceptable"; this ZV3 protocol "cannot be recommended for clinical practice."
