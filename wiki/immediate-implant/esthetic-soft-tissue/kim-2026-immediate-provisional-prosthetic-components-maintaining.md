## Three-line Summary

Two case reports (n=2 patients) of immediate post-extraction provisional prosthetic components used to support and transfer anterior soft-tissue contours: one immediate implant with a chairside customized healing abutment, one tooth-supported ovate-pontic fixed dental prosthesis (FDP).
Case 1 (59-year-old man) placed a 4.1 × 10-mm implant ~4 mm apical to the gingival margin with xenograft and left the customized abutment unadjusted for 3 months; Case 2 (66-year-old man) delivered a PMMA ovate pontic extending ~3 mm into the socket with 0.25 g Bio-Oss + 13 × 25-mm Bio-Gide, modified at 4 weeks; both showed relatively maintained labial contours on 1-month Exocad superimposition.
Contour assessment was visual rather than quantitative, follow-up was ~1 month, and both cases were grafted — so maintenance cannot be attributed solely to the provisional components; longer-term follow-up is needed.

## Summary

This 2026 case report (Journal of implantology and applied sciences 30(3):195-204) from Kyung Hee University describes two maxillary anterior cases in which an immediate post-extraction provisional prosthetic component was used as a scaffold to support the soft tissues before collapse and to transfer the established contour to the definitive prosthesis. Case 1 combined immediate implant placement in the maxillary left central incisor with a chairside flowable-resin customized healing abutment (concave subcritical contour, slightly under-contoured critical contour) left undisturbed for 3 months; emergence-profile development was deferred to the screw-retained provisional phase, and a definitive screw-retained zirconia prosthesis followed. Case 2 delivered a digitally milled PMMA ovate-pontic FDP (~3 mm into the socket) immediately after extraction plus ridge preservation, modified at 4 weeks to improve gingival symmetry and papilla, then restored with a zirconia FDP and crown. In both cases, Exocad superimposition of pre-extraction and 1-month post-delivery scans suggested relatively maintained labial soft-tissue contours. Because only two cases with different prosthetic designs were included, assessment was visual rather than quantitative, follow-up was ~1 month, and both sites were grafted, the contour maintenance cannot be attributed solely to the provisional components.

## Key Contributions

- Shows one common scaffold principle applied across two prosthetic designs: an immediately placed provisional component supports the extraction-site soft tissue before collapse, and the developed contour is digitally transferred to the definitive prosthesis.
- Case 1: a passive, concave subcritical contour and slightly under-contoured critical contour on a customized healing abutment avoid compression during 3-month osseointegration; the emergence profile is developed later in the provisional phase via a customized impression coping and resin addition.
- Case 2: an immediately delivered ovate pontic into a grafted socket, with 4-week resin modification to correct gingival asymmetry and insufficient papilla, before definitive contour transfer.
- Digital transfer by superimposing scans of the provisional in place, the provisional itself, and the scan body / abutment teeth; pre- vs post-delivery scan superimposition on stable references (nonmobile teeth, adjacent hard tissue) for contour assessment.

## Methodology

- **Design**: two case reports (n = 2 patients, 2 sites), no comparator; conducted at Kyung Hee University College of Dentistry, Seoul, Korea.
- **Case 1**: 59-year-old man, fractured maxillary left central incisor with a root-extending crack; CBCT-confirmed suitability, surgical guide placing a 4.1 × 10-mm BlueDiamond implant (MegaGen) ~4 mm apical to the gingival margin; Bio-Oss xenograft to the labial gap; chairside flowable-resin customized healing abutment unadjusted for 3 months; screw-retained provisional then screw-retained zirconia implant FDP.
- **Case 2**: 66-year-old man, buccal fistula/periapical lesion at the maxillary left lateral incisor and deep palatal root caries of the maxillary right central incisor; pre-extraction preparation and scan; milled PMMA ovate-pontic FDP (~3 mm into socket) delivered immediately after extraction and ridge preservation (0.25 g Bio-Oss + 13 × 25-mm Bio-Gide); 4-week modification; definitive zirconia FDP + crown.
- **Outcomes**: visual comparison of labial soft-tissue contour by Exocad (Exocad GmbH) superimposition of pre-extraction and 1-month post-definitive-delivery intraoral scans; no validated quantitative measure.

## Results

| Outcome | Case 1 (implant-supported) | Case 2 (tooth-supported pontic) |
|---|---|---|
| Patient / site | 59-year-old man, maxillary left central incisor | 66-year-old man, maxillary right central incisor |
| Implant / grafting | 4.1 × 10-mm BlueDiamond implant, guided, ~4 mm apical to gingival margin; Bio-Oss xenograft in labial gap | No implant; 0.25 g Bio-Oss + 13 × 25-mm Bio-Gide ridge preservation |
| Provisional component | Chairside flowable-resin customized healing abutment (concave subcritical, slightly under-contoured critical), unadjusted 3 months; screw-retained provisional with resin added to critical contour | Milled PMMA FDP with ovate pontic ~3 mm into socket, immediate delivery; at 4 weeks subgingival resin added and pontic shifted mesially |
| Definitive prosthesis | Screw-retained zirconia implant-supported FDP | Zirconia FDP + single crown |
| Labial contour at 1 month (Exocad superimposition) | Relatively well maintained | Relatively well maintained around pontic site |

- Case 1 peri-implant soft tissue was clinically stable at 3 months; Case 2 showed gingival asymmetry and insufficient papilla at 4 weeks, improved after provisional modification.
- No quantitative contour values, effect sizes, or p-values were reported.
