## Three-line Summary

Cosyn group SR+MA of 6 RCTs (n=222) comparing immediate implant placement (IIP) versus early implant placement (EIP) for single-tooth replacement in the esthetic area (search to February 2023); primary outcome was vertical midfacial soft tissue change; published in Clinical Oral Implants Research, April 2024.

No statistically significant difference between IIP and EIP in vertical midfacial soft tissue change (MD 0.31 mm, p=0.260; I²=83%), Pink Esthetic Score (SMD 0.92, p=0.120; I²=89%), implant survival (RR 0.98, p=0.480; I²=0%), or marginal bone level change (MD 0.03 mm, p=0.700; I²=0%); 4/6 RCTs had high risk of bias.

In low-risk patients with an intact buccal bone wall, IIP and EIP yield equivalent esthetic and clinical outcomes — certainty rated **low** due to high/unclear risk of bias in all included RCTs and systematic underreporting of patient-reported outcomes.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis from the Cosyn group at Ghent University — one of the leading implant esthetics research groups in Europe — directly compares immediate implant placement (IIP) with early implant placement (EIP) for single-tooth replacement in the aesthetic area. From 1185 screened records, 6 RCTs were included, reporting on 222 patients who received 222 single implants (IIP: 112, EIP: 110) in the anterior maxilla or mandible, with follow-up of 8–24 months (mean patient age 35.6–52.6 years).

The primary outcome — vertical midfacial soft tissue change — showed no significant difference between IIP and EIP (MD 0.31 mm, 95% CI [-0.23; 0.86], p=0.260), with substantial heterogeneity (I²=83%), and three of the four meta-analyzed studies restricted to cases with an intact buccal bone wall. Pink Esthetic Score, implant survival, and marginal bone level change were also non-significantly different between protocols. Implant survival in particular was highly consistent across studies (I²=0%), confirming that the timing protocol does not affect implant loss rates.

The authors explicitly rate the strength of their conclusion as **low certainty** — driven by unclear or high risk of bias in all 6 included RCTs and by the finding that state-of-the-art augmentation protocols (connective tissue grafts, simultaneous contour augmentation) were applied in only a minority of studies. They call for future RCTs that include patient-reported outcomes (discomfort, chair time, satisfaction), which have been systematically underreported in this literature.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the most rigorous (highest-impact journal, strongest research group) direct RCT-based comparison of IIP versus EIP in the esthetic zone as of mid-2024.
- Explicitly rates certainty of evidence as **low** — a frank acknowledgment that "no significant difference" should not be interpreted as established equivalence.
- Separates hard-tissue consistency (I²=0% for survival and MBL) from soft-tissue heterogeneity (I²=83–89% for midfacial soft tissue and PES), clarifying where the clinical uncertainty lies.
- Identifies systematic evidence gaps in PROs and contemporary augmentation protocols, giving a roadmap for next-generation RCTs.
- Largely confirms the null-difference finding for hard tissue outcomes from Asghar 2023, but diverges on PES (Ickroth: non-significant vs Asghar: EIP significantly better) — a clinically important discrepancy warranting attention in future RCTs.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review + meta-analysis; RCTs only.
- **Databases**: PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane; plus manual search.
- **Search date**: up to February 2023.
- **Population**: Patients requiring single-tooth implant in the esthetic area (anterior maxilla or mandible).
- **Comparison**: IIP (Type 1 ITI) vs EIP (Type 2 ITI).
- **Primary outcome**: Vertical midfacial soft tissue change.
- **Secondary outcomes**: Horizontal midfacial soft tissue change, vertical papillary change, PES, implant survival, buccal bone thickness, marginal bone level change, patient discomfort, chair time, patient satisfaction.
- **Risk of bias**: 2 RCTs "some concerns"; 4 RCTs "high risk."
