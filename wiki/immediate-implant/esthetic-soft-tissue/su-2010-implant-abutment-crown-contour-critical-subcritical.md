## Three-line Summary

Narrative conceptual paper (IJPRD 2010) redefining "contour" for implant restorations by naming two zones of the abutment/crown: the critical contour and the subcritical contour.

States that overcontouring generally moves the gingival margin apically and undercontouring the opposite, and that altering either zone can change gingival margin level, papilla height, gingival architecture, labial alveolar profile, and gingival color.

The origin of the emergence-profile zone vocabulary; expert opinion without quantitative data.

## Summary

Su and colleagues observed that "overcontoured / flat / undercontoured" had been used loosely for implant crowns, borrowed from tooth-borne restorations. They split the transmucosal part of the restoration into a **critical contour** (just below the gingival margin) and a **subcritical contour** (deeper, toward the platform), and argued that changes in either zone reshape the peri-implant soft tissue. The abstract states the working rule plainly: overcontouring generally pushes the gingival margin apically; undercontouring lets it move the other way. Because the gingival margin's most apical point is the zenith, this is the conceptual hinge between emergence-profile design and zenith position.

## Key Contributions

- Two-zone (critical / subcritical) model.
- Overcontour → apical margin; undercontour → opposite (stated as a general rule).
- Soft-tissue targets: margin level, papilla height, architecture, labial alveolar profile, color.
- Transition needs "running room" between platform and margin.
