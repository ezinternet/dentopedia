## Three-line Summary

PRISMA-registered systematic review of 16 publications (11 independent RCT/CCT investigations) comparing soft tissue augmentation (STA) — primarily connective tissue graft (CTG) and acellular dermal matrix (ADM) — during immediate implant placement (IIP) versus IIP without STA; search through March 2024, ≥12-month follow-up required.

Meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (149 implants, 12 months) demonstrated that CTG significantly reduced mid-facial recession (WMD 0.38 mm, 95% CI 0.15–0.61); however, no significant difference was found for overall Pink Esthetic Score (PES) or patient-reported esthetic outcomes in any included study.

CTG during IIP is justified for recession prevention (0.38 mm gain) but does not improve composite PES or patient satisfaction; immediate anatomical provisionalization — not CTG — appears to be the principal driver of esthetic success, and CTG's buccal-profile benefit may be obscured by composite PES scoring.

## Summary
This PRISMA-compliant systematic review (Periodontology 2000, 2025) evaluated the impact of soft tissue augmentation (STA) during immediate implant placement (IIP) on clinician-assessed esthetic outcomes and patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs). Sixteen publications representing 11 independent RCT/CCT investigations (search through March 2024) were included. Meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (149 implants, 12-month follow-up) demonstrated that connective tissue graft (CTG) during IIP significantly reduces mid-facial soft tissue recession (WMD 0.38 mm, 95% CI 0.15–0.61). However, no significant difference was found between STA and control groups for overall Pink Esthetic Score (PES) or patient-reported esthetic satisfaction at any follow-up point. High overall esthetic satisfaction was reported in both grafted and non-grafted groups, suggesting that favorable site selection and immediate provisioning with anatomically shaped crowns are the primary drivers of esthetic success.

## Key Contributions
- First SR to include PROMs as a primary outcome alongside clinician-assessed esthetics for STA during IIP
- CTG significantly reduces mid-facial soft tissue recession (WMD 0.38 mm) — aligns with prior meta-analyses (0.41–0.59 mm range)
- STA does not significantly improve overall PES or patient-reported esthetic satisfaction
- Immediate anatomical provisionalization — not CTG — appears to be the principal driver of positive esthetic outcomes
- The isolated benefit of CTG may lie in buccal volume/profile, but composite PES scores cannot detect this without item-level reporting

## Methodology
- Study design: Systematic review (PRISMA); PROSPERO registration
- Search: PubMed, Cochrane Library, Embase (up to March 2024) plus citation search
- Eligibility: RCT/CCT; single-tooth IIP premolar-to-premolar area; ≥12 months follow-up; ≥10 implants per arm at 12 months
- 16 publications identified (some studies reported at multiple timepoints or in multiple publications)
- Comparators: IIP without STA (PICOS 1); IIP with different STA technique (PICOS 2)
- Interventions included: subepithelial CTG (multiple techniques), acellular dermal matrix (ADM)
- Meta-analyses performed for: mid-facial recession, PES (short- and long-term), keratinized tissue gain

## Results
**Mid-facial recession** (primary esthetic outcome): CTG reduces recession WMD = **0.38 mm** (95% CI 0.15–0.61); 4 RCTs, 149 implants, 12-month follow-up — statistically significant.

**Pink Esthetic Score**: No significant difference between STA and non-STA at either short- or long-term follow-up in any included study.

**PROMs (patient-reported esthetics)**: No significant difference between grafted and non-grafted groups; high satisfaction in both.

**Keratinized tissue**: Modest increase with STA but inconsistently reported across studies.

**Post-operative morbidity**: Higher in STA groups (expected — donor-site morbidity).

**Overall esthetic levels**: Both groups achieved high absolute PES values, consistent with strict patient selection.
