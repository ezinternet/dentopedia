## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cross-sectional comparative cohort (45 anterior maxillary sockets in 44 patients, flapless immediate implant placement with palatally biased osteotomy) comparing four groups by bone graft and provisional restoration status: none (No BGPR), provisional only (PR), bone graft only (BG), and both (BGPR); soft-tissue dimensions measured on gypsum casts.

Grafted sites showed greater vertical mucosal height (2.72 vs 2.29 mm, P < .06) and greater facial thickness at the gingival third (2.90 vs 2.28 mm, P < .008), provisional sites showed greater gingival thickness (2.81 vs 2.37 mm, P < .06), and the BGPR-vs-none mean difference was largest (3.09 vs 2.03 mm) but non-significant (P < .79); net gain was ~1 mm, concentrated in the gingival and middle zones above the 2.0 mm masking threshold.

Clinical implication: gap bone grafting plus a screw-retained provisional restoration at flapless immediate placement was associated with roughly 0.5–1.0 mm more peri-implant mucosa height and thickness, supporting their combined use for esthetic sites — but retrospective design, missing baseline thickness, cast-only measurement and inconsistent significance reporting keep this at hypothesis-generating strength.

## Summary

Chu et al. (2015), in Part 2 of the Tarnow/Chu flapless immediate-placement series, asked a practical question that the bone-focused literature leaves open: when an implant is placed flapless into an anterior extraction socket with a palatally biased osteotomy, does filling the labial gap with bone graft and/or immediately restoring the patient change the *soft tissue* envelope? Forty-five sockets in 44 patients were allocated across four treatment combinations and evaluated after healing on gypsum casts, with vertical mucosal height measured from the free gingival margin to the implant shoulder and labial-palatal thickness measured at gingival, middle and incisal zones. Grafted sites had ~0.4 mm more mucosal height and ~0.6 mm more gingival-level thickness than nongrafted sites; provisional restoration added ~0.4 mm of gingival thickness. The combined BGPR group had the largest mean thickness (3.09 mm) versus 2.03 mm with neither intervention, but that contrast was not statistically significant, and the authors' own significance labels are inconsistent between abstract, text and Fig 13. The clinical message — that graft plus provisional restoration preserves a thicker, taller mucosa and pushes the gingival and middle zones over the 2.0 mm color-masking threshold — is plausible and consistent with the biologic rationale (graft containment and soft-tissue support), but the study cannot separate treatment effect from phenotype and implant-position confounding.

## Key Contributions
- Provides one of the few **4-arm clinical comparisons** isolating bone grafting versus immediate provisional restoration as separate contributors to peri-implant **soft-tissue** dimensions after flapless IIP.
- Measures mucosa in three corono-apical zones (G/M/I) on casts, showing that the graft/provisional benefit is concentrated in the **gingival and middle** zones and is absent at the incisal zone.
- Anchors the outcome to the **2.0 mm thickness threshold** for color masking and crestal bone stability rather than to radiographic bone fill.
- Documents the practical protocol: flapless extraction, palatally biased osteotomy, 3–4 mm subcrestal implant depth, 25–35 Ncm primary stability, small-particle allograft in the labial gap, screw-retained acrylic provisional in infraocclusion, ≥4–5 months before first disconnection.

## Methodology
- **Design**: Retrospective cross-sectional comparative cohort (2×2 treatment combinations); 45 sockets / 44 patients; 70% central incisors; mean age 48.5 y (22–75).
- **Groups**: No BGPR (clot, healing abutment + resin-bonded pontic); PR (provisional only); BG (graft only, healing abutment); BGPR (graft + provisional).
- **Surgery**: Flapless atraumatic extraction; palatally biased osteotomy; tapered non-platform-switched internal-connection implants 3–4 mm apical to FGM; primary stability 25–35 Ncm.
- **Gap**: blood clot versus small-particle bone allograft.
- **Timing**: No BGPR/BG first disconnection ≥4 months + PEEK abutment and soft-tissue sculpting (≥3 weeks); PR/BGPR ≥5 months.
- **Outcomes**: Vertical mucosal height (FGM → implant shoulder) and labial-palatal thickness at G/M/I midpoints on gypsum casts, spring-loaded caliper (0.1 mm), 2.5× loupes.
- **Statistics**: Regression/ANCOVA (JMP v10); α = .05; contralateral tooth used to estimate site stability.

## Results
| Comparison | Result | Reported P |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical mucosal height, grafted vs nongrafted | 2.72 vs 2.29 mm | < .06 |
| Gingival-level facial thickness, grafted vs nongrafted | 2.90 vs 2.28 mm | < .008 |
| Gingival-level facial thickness, provisional vs none | 2.81 vs 2.37 mm | < .06 |
| Gingival-level facial thickness, BGPR vs neither | 3.09 vs 2.03 mm | < .79 (NS) |
| Overall vertical mucosal height | 2.5 mm | — |
| Overall thickness G / M / I | 2.7 / 2.1 / 1.3 mm | — |
| Vertical collapse in all groups | ~0.5 mm | — |
| Incisal and middle zones | No significant association with graft and/or provisional | NS |

- Thickness was always > 2 mm at the apical extent (implant-abutment interface) and decreased toward the FGM in every group.
- Authors' conclusion: graft + provisional increased vertical and labial-palatal dimensions by 0.5–1.0 mm versus neither, most frequently in gingival and middle zones.

## Critical Appraisal
- **Reporting inconsistency (read directly from the PDF)**: the abstract assigns P < .008 to grafted-vs-nongrafted thickness (2.90 vs 2.28 mm), while the Fig 13 panel associates P = .008 with the PR-vs-no-PR pair (2.9 vs 2.8 mm), and the body text reverses the 2.28/2.90 order. The headline "net gain ~1 mm" therefore rests on mean differences whose significance labels cannot be cleanly matched to comparisons.
- **P < .06 treated as superiority** in two comparisons despite α = .05.
- **Largest effect is non-significant** (BGPR vs neither, P < .79) — with 4 small arms, the study is underpowered for the interaction the conclusion implies.
- **No baseline soft-tissue thickness**, so cast values cannot be converted into change; phenotype differences between groups are uncontrolled.
- **Cast/caliper measurement** (0.8 mm tip, 0.5 mm intervals) approximates but does not equal in vivo thickness; no CBCT, no histology, no long-term follow-up.
- **Clustering ignored** (45 sockets in 44 patients).
- Part 1 (ridge dimensional change, Tarnow 2014) is not held here, so bone-level claims from the series are out of scope for this page.
