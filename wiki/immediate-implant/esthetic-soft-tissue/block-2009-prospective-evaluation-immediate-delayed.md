## Three-line Summary
Prospective RCT, n=55 patients completing 2-year follow-up (baseline 76 recruited), comparing immediate implant placement & provisionalization vs. delayed placement (4-month healing) then immediate provisionalization; both groups showed similar crestal bone response and implant integration.
Immediate placement group preserved 1 mm more facial gingival margin position (P < .05) vs. delayed group; crestal bone levels remained stable after first 6 months in both groups.
Immediate provisionalization supports esthetic outcomes in maxillary anterior by maintaining soft tissue position, though hard tissue resorption profiles are equivalent between timing protocols.

## 세줄요약
전향적 RCT, n=55명 2년 추적 (초기 모집 76명), 즉시 임플란트 식립·즉시 가철성 임시치관 (Immediate Provisionalization) vs. 지연 식립(4개월 경과) 후 즉시 임시치관 비교; 양 군 모두 유사한 골정 반응 및 임플란트 골유착 (Osseointegration)을 보임.
즉시 식립군은 지연 식립군 대비 협측 치은변연 (Facial Gingival Margin) 위치를 1mm 더 보존 (P < .05); 변연골수준 (Crestal Bone Level, CBL)은 양 군 모두 처음 6개월 이후 안정적으로 유지.
즉시 임시치관은 상악 전치부 심미 결과를 지원하여 연조직 위치를 유지하나, 경조직 흡수 양상은 식립 시기 프로토콜 간 동등함.

## Summary
This prospective randomized controlled trial enrolled 76 patients requiring single-tooth replacement in the maxillary anterior region (central incisor to premolar) and randomized them to either immediate implant placement with immediate provisionalization at the time of extraction, or delayed placement (extraction with immediate socket grafting, 4-month healing, then implant placement with immediate provisionalization). Fifty-five patients (73%) completed standardized 2-year radiographic follow-up. Crestal bone levels and implant integration did not differ between the immediate and delayed groups (P > .05), with both arms showing an initial bone-level shift in the first 6 months that then remained stable through 2 years. The key distinguishing finding was soft-tissue, not hard-tissue: the immediate-placement group preserved the facial gingival margin roughly 1 mm more coronally than the delayed group (P < .05). As one of the earliest RCTs to isolate placement timing as a variable while holding immediate provisionalization constant across both arms, the study helped establish that the esthetic advantage of immediate placement lies in soft-tissue position rather than crestal bone response.

## Key Contributions
- One of the earliest prospective RCTs to isolate placement **timing** (immediate vs. 4-month delayed) as the study variable while holding immediate provisionalization constant in *both* arms — cleanly separating the timing effect from the provisionalization effect.
- Demonstrated **equivalent crestal bone response and osseointegration** between immediate and delayed placement — timing alone does not compromise hard-tissue outcomes when both protocols use immediate provisionalization.
- Quantified that immediate placement preserves **~1 mm more facial gingival margin position** than delayed placement (P < .05) — an early, randomized-trial-level attribution of a soft-tissue esthetic benefit specifically to placement timing (not to provisionalization, which both groups received).
- Established a standardized long-term (2-year) radiographic monitoring protocol using implant threads as an internal magnification reference and pre-extraction imaging as baseline.

## Methodology
Prospective RCT; 76 patients randomized, 55 completed 2-year follow-up (21 lost — implant loss n=5, relocation n=11, noncompliance n=3, medical problems n=1). Two parallel arms, both receiving immediate provisionalization: **Group 1 (Delayed)** — tooth extraction with immediate socket grafting, 4-month healing, then implant placement + immediate provisionalization; **Group 2 (Immediate)** — implant placement + provisionalization at the time of extraction. Sites: maxillary anterior (central incisor, lateral incisor, canine, premolar). Standardized digital radiographs obtained every 6 months from baseline to 2 years post-restoration. Primary endpoint: crestal bone level (implant and adjacent tooth). Secondary endpoints: facial gingival margin position and interdental bone/papilla measures, from standardized reference points.
