## Three-line Summary

RCT (n=40, 1 year) comparing VST — immediate implant + GBR via vestibular access — versus early implant placement + conventional flap + contour augmentation for compromised maxillary esthetic-zone sockets.

VST showed significantly less mid-facial soft tissue recession (-0.53 ± 1.17 mm) than early placement + contour augmentation (-1.87 ± 0.69 mm; p<0.001); papilla and horizontal soft tissue changes also significantly favored VST; labial bone thickness increased in both groups with no between-group difference.

VST represents an innovative approach for esthetic-zone immediate implants with better soft-tissue preservation than early placement + GBR, though limited by small n (40), single center, and 1-year follow-up only.

## Summary

This randomized controlled trial (Alexandria/Cairo, Egypt; Kiel, Germany; n=40, 1 year) is the first to directly compare Vestibular Socket Therapy (VST) against early implant placement with contour augmentation for compromised (nonrestorable, non-infected) maxillary anterior sockets. Twenty patients in each group: VST used a vestibular (labial) access incision for GBR with immediate implant placement, preserving the crestal gingival architecture; the control underwent an initial healing period followed by implant placement with GBR through a conventional access flap plus contour augmentation. All implants osseointegrated except one in the VST group (95% vs 100% survival). VST produced significantly less mid-facial soft tissue change (-0.53 ± 1.17 mm vs -1.87 ± 0.69 mm; p<0.001) and significantly smaller changes in mesial papilla, distal papilla, and horizontal soft tissue (all p<0.05). Labial bone plate thickness increased significantly within both groups over 12 months with no between-group difference. Regression analysis identified VST use and increased coronal bone thickness as significant predictors of reduced mid-facial soft tissue change.

## Key Contributions

- **First RCT** directly comparing VST (immediate implant + vestibular GBR) vs early implant placement + contour augmentation for compromised esthetic-zone sockets
- VST significantly preserved mid-facial soft tissue: -0.53 mm recession vs -1.87 mm in early placement + GBR (p<0.001; difference ~1.34 mm clinically significant in the esthetic zone)
- All papilla and horizontal soft tissue secondary outcomes favored VST (p<0.05)
- Both techniques increased labial bone thickness — neither approach caused bone loss at 1 year
- Regression identified coronal bone thickness gain and VST technique as independent predictors of soft tissue preservation
- Near-equivalent implant survival (VST 95%, control 100%) — one early VST failure does not exceed expected RCT variance at n=20

## Methodology

- **Design**: Parallel-group RCT, 1:1 allocation; n=20 VST (test), n=20 early placement + contour augmentation (control)
- **Population**: Adults with single nonrestorable maxillary anterior teeth in the esthetic zone; "compromised" sockets (not specifically infected)
- **VST protocol**: Atraumatic extraction → immediate implant placement → GBR via vestibular (labial) access incision (gingival crest anatomy preserved)
- **Control protocol**: Atraumatic extraction → initial healing period (early placement) → implant insertion + GBR via conventional access flap + contour augmentation
- **Outcomes**: Primary — mid-facial soft tissue level change; Secondary — mesial/distal papilla dimensions, horizontal soft tissue change, labial bone plate thickness (apical/middle/coronal CBCT levels)
- **Follow-up**: 12 months post-loading
- **Limitation**: Small sample (n=40), single center, 1-year only; abstract-only ingest (randomization/allocation concealment/ITT not verifiable)
