## Three-line Summary

2-year RCT (n=60: 30 immediate loading, 30 conventional/delayed loading; 72 enrolled) of immediately placed implants in extraction sockets of periodontally compromised patients, all receiving allograft bone graft and a collagen barrier membrane.

Immediate loading: 96.7% survival, mean bone gain 1.19 mm at 2 years; conventional loading: 93.3% survival, mean bone gain 1.00 mm — no significant between-group difference in bone gain or papilla index change (P >0.05).

Immediate loading of immediately-placed implants in patients with periodontal disease history achieved bone regeneration and soft-tissue esthetics equivalent to delayed loading, indicating primary closure and an osseointegration delay are not prerequisites for bone gain in this population.

## Summary

This 2-year prospective RCT (Shibly et al., 2010, *Journal of Periodontology* 81(12):1743–1751) evaluated whether immediate loading compromises bone regeneration or soft-tissue esthetics when implants are placed immediately into extraction sockets of patients with a history of periodontal disease. Of 72 patients enrolled, 60 completed treatment (30 immediate loading, 30 conventional/delayed loading), all receiving allograft bone grafting and a collagen barrier membrane at placement. Radiographic bone level and papilla index were assessed at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months. Both groups achieved high survival (96.7% vs. 93.3%) and similar bone gain (1.19 mm vs. 1.00 mm at 2 years), with no significant between-group difference in bone regeneration or papillary esthetics; most of the bone gain occurred within the first postoperative year in both arms. The findings support immediate loading as a viable option for immediately placed implants in periodontally compromised patients, without sacrificing bone regeneration or esthetic outcomes.

## Key Contributions

- Direct RCT comparison of immediate vs. conventional loading for immediately-placed single implants specifically in periodontally compromised patients — a population with higher baseline bone loss than periodontally healthy cohorts
- Longitudinal bone regeneration quantified at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months via radiographic linear measurement
- Blinded papillary index (PI) esthetic assessment at the same time points
- Evidence that primary closure and delayed loading are not prerequisites for bone gain when bone graft support is used at immediate placement
- Demonstrates immediate loading does not compromise bone regeneration outcomes vs. conventional loading in this comorbid subpopulation

## Methodology

Prospective, randomized, parallel-group RCT. 72 patients with a history of periodontal disease requiring single-tooth extraction and replacement were enrolled; 60 completed (30 immediate loading / 30 conventional loading). All patients underwent tooth extraction with immediate implant placement in the same visit, plus a periodontal flap, allograft bone placement, and a collagen barrier membrane. The immediate-loading group received a temporary crown at insertion; the conventional-loading group had primary closure without a crown and was loaded conventionally after 3 months of osseointegration. Outcomes — implant survival, radiographic bone level change, and papilla index — were evaluated at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months.

## Results

| Outcome | Immediate Loading (n=30) | Conventional Loading (n=30) | P-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implant survival (2 yr) | 96.7% (29/30) | 93.3% (28/30) | NS |
| Mean bone gain (2 yr) | 1.19 mm | 1.00 mm | >0.05 (NS) |
| Mean bone gain (baseline→1 yr) | 1.05 mm | 0.95 mm | <0.001 (within-group) |
| Bone gain 1→2 yr | 0.14 mm | 0.05 mm | Minimal change |
| Papilla index (baseline→1 yr) | Significant decrease | Significant decrease | <0.001 (within both groups) |
| Papilla index (1→2 yr) | Minimal change | Minimal change | NS |

Most bone gain occurred within the first postoperative year in both groups, with minimal additional change from 12–24 months. No implant stability quotient (ISQ) or resonance frequency analysis (RFA) data were reported, and bone change was inferred from 2D radiography only.
