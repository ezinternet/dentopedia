## Three-line Summary

Pilot prospective RCT (n=24) comparing immediate vs early loading of 6 immediately placed bone-level tapered implants for full-arch maxillary rehabilitation, with 2-year follow-up. ISQ dynamics equivalent between groups (p>0.268); MBL submillimeter in both; cumulative survival 91.80% vs 97.22% not significantly different (p=0.162); OHRQoL improved significantly in both (p<0.001). Both immediate and early loading protocols are adequate for full-arch maxillary rehabilitation with immediately placed implants.

## Background

Full-arch maxillary rehabilitation with immediately placed implants presents a clinical dilemma regarding the optimal loading protocol. Immediate loading (IL) reduces treatment time and improves patient experience but concerns persist about whether early secondary stability development may be compromised. Early loading (EL) defers prosthetic restoration until week 7, allowing for undisturbed initial osseointegration. This pilot RCT was designed to compare both protocols systematically in a prospective framework, evaluating implant stability (ISQ), marginal bone loss, survival, and patient-reported outcomes over 2 years.

## Methods

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Pilot prospective RCT |
| Sample | 24 patients with failing maxillary dentition |
| Groups | 12 immediate loading (IL), 12 early loading (EL) |
| Implants | Bone-level tapered, hydrophilic surface, 6 per arch |
| IL protocol | Temporary restorations placed at surgery (day 0) |
| EL protocol | No temporization; final PFM prosthesis at week 7 |
| Final prosthesis | Screw-retained complete PFM (both groups at week 7) |
| Stability measurement | RFA: Penguin and Ostell ISQ devices |
| Follow-up | 2 years |

Insertion torque measured at placement: IL 33.0±4.87 Ncm, EL 29.26±8.31 Ncm.

## Results

| Outcome | Immediate Loading | Early Loading | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISQ dynamics (Penguin) | — | — | 0.268 (NS) |
| ISQ dynamics (Ostell) | — | — | 0.552 (NS) |
| MBL | Submillimeter | Submillimeter | NS |
| Cumulative survival | 91.80% | 97.22% | 0.162 (NS) |
| OHIP total (time) | Significant decrease | Significant decrease | <0.001 |
| Patient satisfaction (2yr) | High | High | — |

NS = not significant. Specific ISQ values and MBL measurements not available from abstract.

## Discussion

The equivalence of ISQ stability dynamics between groups suggests that provisional temporization in the immediate loading group did not adversely affect the osseointegration trajectory. The submillimeter MBL in both groups aligns with current benchmarks for successful implant-supported restorations. The non-significant survival difference (91.80% vs 97.22%) should be interpreted cautiously given the pilot sample size (n=12 per group); the study was not powered to detect small survival differences. The consistent improvement in OHRQoL regardless of loading protocol reinforces that patient-centered outcomes are achievable with both approaches. The insertion torques achieved (33.0 Ncm IL, 29.26 Ncm EL) are within the range considered adequate for immediate loading of immediately placed implants, though slightly below the commonly cited ≥35 Ncm threshold.

## Limitations

- Pilot study (n=24) with limited statistical power — survival difference of 5.42% is clinically meaningful but statistically inconclusive
- Abstract-only: full methodology, randomization/blinding, bone augmentation details, per-implant data not available
- 2-year follow-up insufficient for long-term MBL and prosthesis durability assessment
- Single-center, maxilla only — mandibular and multi-center generalizability uncertain
- No comparison to conventional delayed loading

## Clinical Takeaway

For full-arch maxillary rehabilitation with 6 immediately placed bone-level tapered implants, both immediate and early loading protocols deliver equivalent ISQ stability trajectories, submillimeter MBL, and significant OHRQoL improvement at 2 years. This pilot RCT supports immediate loading as a viable option in this indication — provided adequate insertion torque is achieved — though larger confirmatory trials are needed.
