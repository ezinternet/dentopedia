## Three-line Summary

This SR of 10 studies (7 low, 3 medium quality) found that loaded dental implants consistently show significantly higher peri-implant nerve fibre density compared to unloaded implants or extraction sites. Sensory innervation is present in both bone-implant interface and peri-implant epithelium with a distinct pattern. Current evidence is insufficient to differentiate innervation outcomes between immediate and delayed placement or loading protocols due to high bias risk and study heterogeneity.

## Background

Natural teeth possess a periodontal ligament (PDL) richly supplied with mechanoreceptors that mediate proprioception and occlusal load sensing. When a tooth is replaced by a dental implant, the PDL is lost and the implant integrates directly with bone — raising the question of whether and how sensory feedback is restored.

골지각 (Osseoperception) refers to the ability to detect forces and tactile stimuli applied to an osseointegrated implant. The mechanism relies on mechanoreceptors and nerve fibres within the surrounding periosteum, bone, and peri-implant soft tissues rather than the absent PDL. Understanding how innervation develops around implants — and whether timing of placement or loading modulates this process — is relevant to predicting the sensory and functional quality of implant-retained prostheses.

Implant placement and loading timing protocols range from immediate (Type 1 placement / immediate loading) to delayed (conventional placement / conventional loading), but their differential effects on peri-implant neural biology had not been systematically examined before this review.

## Methods

- **Study design**: Systematic review following PRISMA-type methodology
- **PEO question**: Effect of time between tooth extraction and implant placement, or implant loading timing, on neural fibre content in peri-implant hard and soft tissues
- **Databases searched**: MEDLINE, Cochrane, EMBASE, Web of Science, LILACS, OpenGrey; plus hand searching
- **Date range**: Up to July 2013
- **Screening**: 683 titles → 10 included studies
- **Subgroups**:
  - 5 studies evaluating innervation of peri-implant epithelium (soft tissue component)
  - 5 studies evaluating sensory function in peri-implant bone (hard tissue component)
- **Quality assessment**: 3 studies rated medium quality; 7 rated low quality

## Results

### Peri-implant innervation pattern

All 10 included studies confirmed sensory innervation around osseointegrated implants — either at the bone-implant interface or in peri-implant epithelium. The innervation follows a distinct pattern distinct from that around natural teeth.

### Effect of loading on nerve fibre density

The primary quantitative finding: **loaded implants showed significantly higher nerve fibre density** compared with unloaded implants or extraction sites without implantation. This was a consistent finding across studies addressing this comparison.

This supports the hypothesis that mechanical loading is a key stimulus for neural ingrowth and maturation in peri-implant tissues — analogous to mechanotransduction pathways in bone remodelling.

### Effect of placement/loading timing

Evidence was **insufficient** to differentiate innervation patterns following:
- Immediate vs delayed implant placement
- Immediate vs early vs conventional/delayed loading protocols

The inability to distinguish between timing protocols was attributed to high risk of bias, considerable variability in study design, and lack of standardised loading protocol definitions across included studies.

### Study quality and limitations

- 7 of 10 studies: low quality
- 3 of 10 studies: medium quality
- High risk of bias across the evidence base
- Inconsistency in study design and loading protocol definitions
- No included studies were rated high quality

## Clinical Takeaway

1. **Loading promotes innervation**: Initiating occlusal load on an osseointegrated implant appears to stimulate peri-implant nerve fibre ingrowth. This may mean that immediately loaded implants, if clinically indicated, could develop osseoperceptive capacity faster.

2. **Placement timing effect undetermined**: Immediate vs delayed placement cannot be distinguished in terms of innervation outcomes based on current evidence — clinicians should not select placement timing specifically to optimise innervation.

3. **Osseoperception is real but PDL-inferior**: Implant patients have reduced tactile acuity compared to natural-tooth patients. The neural biology reviewed here underlies that functional difference — and suggests it may be partially recoverable through loading-stimulated innervation.

4. **Evidence quality is low**: All clinical decisions about placement or loading timing should rely on primary stability, bone volume, and patient-specific factors — not on this innervation SR, whose quality base is too weak for protocol recommendations.
