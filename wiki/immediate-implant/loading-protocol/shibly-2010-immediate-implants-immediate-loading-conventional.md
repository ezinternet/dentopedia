## Three-line Summary

Randomized clinical trial (n=60 patients with a history of periodontal disease) comparing immediate loading versus conventional (3-month delayed) loading of implants placed immediately into extraction sockets, followed for 1 year.

Immediate loading: 96.6% survival (29/30), bone gain 0.99 ± 0.22 mm; conventional loading: 93.3% survival (28/30), bone gain 0.75 ± 0.17 mm — neither difference statistically significant (p > 0.5) — but conventional loading showed far greater mucogingival junction (MGJ) coronal displacement (65% vs 15% of sites).

For periodontally compromised patients receiving single-tooth immediate implants, immediate provisional loading is as safe as conventional delayed loading for survival and bone stability, and better preserves mucogingival position — a soft-tissue argument for immediate loading beyond mere non-inferiority.

## Summary

This 1-year randomized controlled trial by Shibly et al. (University of Pennsylvania School of Dental Medicine) enrolled 60 patients with a history of periodontal disease who required extraction of a hopeless tooth followed by immediate implant placement. Patients were randomized to immediate loading with a provisional crown (Group A, n=30) or conventional loading with a submerged cover screw and delayed restoration at 3 months (Group B, n=30); both groups received the same surgical protocol (mucoperiosteal flap, atraumatic extraction, immediate implant placement, allograft bone, collagen barrier membrane). At 1 year, implant survival (96.6% vs 93.3%) and radiographic bone-level change (0.99 ± 0.22 mm vs 0.75 ± 0.17 mm) did not differ significantly between groups, but the conventional (submerged) loading group showed markedly more coronal displacement of the mucogingival junction (65% vs 15% of sites) — attributed to the flap/primary-closure step absent from the transmucosal immediate-loading protocol. The trial demonstrates that immediate provisional loading of immediately placed implants is a viable, non-inferior alternative to conventional delayed loading in a periodontally compromised population, with an added soft-tissue-position advantage.

## Key Contributions

- One of few RCTs directly randomizing the *loading protocol* (immediate vs. conventional) — rather than placement timing — within immediately placed implants, isolating the loading-protocol variable specifically.
- Conducted in a periodontally compromised population, a subgroup often excluded from earlier immediate-loading trials.
- Identifies mucogingival junction (MGJ) coronal displacement as a soft-tissue outcome favoring immediate (transmucosal) loading over conventional (submerged, primary-closure) loading, independent of the survival/bone equivalence.
- Provides 1-year benchmark survival and bone-level data (96.6%/93.3% survival; ~1 mm bone gain in both arms) for periodontally compromised single-tooth immediate implants.

## Methodology

Randomized controlled trial; n=60 patients with a history of periodontal disease requiring single-tooth extraction and immediate implant placement. Group A (n=30): immediate loading with a provisional crown. Group B (n=30): conventional loading — cover screw, submerged healing, restoration at 3 months. Both groups: mucoperiosteal flap, atraumatic extraction, immediate implant placement, allograft bone graft, collagen barrier membrane. Follow-up at 3, 6, and 12 months. Primary outcomes: implant survival, radiographic bone-level change. Secondary outcome: mucogingival junction (MGJ) position/displacement.

## Results

| Outcome | Immediate Loading (n=30) | Conventional Loading (n=30) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-year implant survival | 96.6% (29/30) | 93.3% (28/30) | ns |
| Bone level change (mm ± SD) | 0.99 ± 0.22 | 0.75 ± 0.17 | > 0.5 (ns) |
| MGJ coronally displaced at 1 year | 15% (4/26 sites) | 65% (17/26 sites) | significant |

Both loading protocols achieved high survival and stable/increasing radiographic bone levels with no significant between-group difference; the transmucosal immediate-loading protocol resulted in substantially less coronal MGJ displacement than the submerged conventional protocol.
