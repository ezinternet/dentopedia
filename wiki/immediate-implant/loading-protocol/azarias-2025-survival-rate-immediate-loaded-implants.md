## Three-line Summary

Literature review (15 included studies, 546 screened; PubMed/MEDLINE 2006–Feb 2024) of human clinical studies evaluating implants immediately loaded (≤48h) with a Branemark protocol (full-arch, screw-retained) prosthesis in fully edentulous patients.

All 15 included studies reported implant survival rates above 93% (range ~93.9–100%) across follow-ups of 1–10 years; marginal bone loss stayed clinically acceptable (<2 mm at 1 year) in all studies that reported it; moderate-to-high primary stability (insertion torque ≥30 N·cm, ISQ ≥60 where reported) was typical.

Immediate loading of full-arch implant-supported prostheses (including the All-on-4 concept) is supported as a viable, high-survival treatment by this body of literature, but the review itself is a narrative/literature review (defined PubMed search and inclusion criteria, no PRISMA flow, no formal risk-of-bias/quality appraisal, no meta-analysis) — treat the pooled ">93%" figure as a descriptive range across heterogeneous studies, not a meta-analytic estimate.

## Summary

This literature review searched PubMed/MEDLINE (2006–February 2024) using the MeSH terms "completely edentulous" and "immediate loading," screening 546 articles and including 15 human clinical studies that evaluated survival of implants immediately loaded (within 48 hours of placement, most same-day) with a Branemark protocol — i.e., a full-arch, screw-retained fixed prosthesis, either the traditional 5–6+ implant protocol or the reduced 4-implant "All-on-4" variant (2 axial anterior + 2 distally tilted posterior implants). All 15 included studies, spanning follow-up periods of 1 to 10 years and covering both prospective and retrospective designs, reported implant survival rates above 93%. Provisional and definitive prosthesis survival was 100% in nearly every study, even where acrylic provisional prostheses fractured (repairable). Marginal bone loss, where reported, stayed under the 2 mm/year clinically-acceptable threshold. The review concludes that moderate primary stability (insertion torque 30–44 N·cm, or ISQ ≥60) is sufficient for immediate loading of a Branemark protocol prosthesis — notably lower than the 45 N·cm high-stability bar typically recommended for immediately loading a *single* implant. This is a narrative/literature review with a defined but non-PRISMA search protocol; it does not include formal risk-of-bias appraisal or meta-analysis.

## Key Contributions

- Tabulates 15 studies (Table 1) with per-study n, implant count, arch, implant configuration, prosthesis-delivery timing, follow-up, and survival rate — a ready reference table for immediate full-arch loading outcomes.
- Confirms All-on-4 as a viable variant achieving comparable survival to the traditional 5–6+ implant Branemark protocol while avoiding grafting and reducing implant count/cost.
- Synthesizes primary-stability interpretation thresholds from the literature: insertion torque <30/30–44/≥45 N·cm = low/moderate/high stability; ISQ <60/60–64/65–69/≥70 = low/medium-low/medium-high/high stability.
- Notes that moderate stability (torque ≥30 N·cm or ISQ ≥60) appears sufficient for full-arch immediate loading, contrasting the higher (45 N·cm) bar generally recommended for single immediately-loaded implants.
- Flags a reporting gap in the literature: only 1 of 6 studies with definitive-prosthesis cantilevers explicitly verified the cantilever met the 1.5× anteroposterior-spread "clinically acceptable" rule.

## Methodology

Electronic search of PubMed/MEDLINE, 2006 to February 2024, using MeSH descriptors "completely edentulous" AND "immediate loading." Inclusion: human clinical studies in English reporting implant survival after immediate loading (≤48h) with a Branemark protocol prosthesis. 546 records screened; 15 included. Marginal bone loss data collected where available in the source articles. No PRISMA flow diagram, no dual-reviewer process, and no formal quality/risk-of-bias appraisal tool reported — a narrative review with a defined (not fully systematic) search protocol rather than a formal systematic review.

## Results

- Implant survival across all 15 included studies: >93% (range ~93.9–100%), follow-up 1–10 years, both maxilla and mandible, both All-on-4 and traditional (5–6+ implant) configurations.
- Bergkvist et al (2009): mean ISQ 50.6 at placement (below the commonly cited 60 threshold) yet 98.2% survival at 3 years — full-arch/multi-implant splinting may tolerate lower individual-implant ISQ than single-implant immediate loading.
- Mohamed et al (2022): mean ISQ >65 (adequate), 100% survival at 1 year; only study to explicitly confirm cantilever lengths met the clinically-acceptable 1.5× rule.
- Marginal bone loss: <2 mm at 1 year in all reporting studies (values ranged roughly 0.07–1.6 mm depending on study/timepoint).
- Prosthesis-level outcomes: provisional and definitive prosthesis survival was 100% in essentially every included study; provisional prosthesis fractures occurred in several studies but were always repairable.
