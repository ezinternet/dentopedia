## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (no meta-analysis; PRISMA/PROSPERO CRD420251162616) of 28 clinical studies (432 root-analog implants, RAIs) covering titanium, zirconia, and hybrid titanium-zirconia implants fabricated by CAD-CAM milling or additive manufacturing (SLM/DLMS/DLMF), placed to mimic extracted-tooth root morphology for immediate single-tooth replacement.

Most studies reported survival 71–100% and success 64.5–100%; titanium and hybrid titanium-zirconia RAIs were most predictable (survival up to 100%, MBL as low as 0.7 mm at 1 year), whereas zirconia RAIs ranged from 33.3% to 100% survival and showed higher failure/fracture risk when macroretentive design or surface treatment was inadequate.

RAIs show promising short- to medium-term esthetic and soft-tissue outcomes with reduced grafting need, but evidence is dominated by single-patient clinical reports and small case series with short follow-up (≤2 years in most), heterogeneous protocols, and only 1 RCT — well-designed multicenter RCTs with longer follow-up are needed before RAIs can be recommended over conventional immediate implants.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰(메타분석 없음; PRISMA/PROSPERO CRD420251162616)로, 발거 치아의 치근 형태를 그대로 복제해 제작한 root-analog implant(RAI, 치근형태모사 임플란트) 28개 임상연구(432개 RAI, 티타늄·지르코니아·하이브리드 티타늄-지르코니아, CAD-CAM 밀링 또는 SLM/DLMS/DLMF 적층제조)를 종합.

대부분 연구에서 생존율 71–100%, 성공률 64.5–100%를 보고했으며, 티타늄·하이브리드 RAI가 가장 예측 가능(생존율 최대 100%, 1년차 MBL 최저 0.7mm)했고, 지르코니아 RAI는 생존율 33.3–100%로 편차가 크고 macroretention·표면처리가 불충분할 때 파절·실패 위험이 높았다.

RAI는 단기-중기 심미·연조직 결과가 양호하고 골이식 필요성을 줄일 수 있지만, 근거 대부분이 단일환자 임상보고·소규모 케이스시리즈(추적기간 대부분 ≤2년, RCT 단 1편)로 이질성이 커서, 기존 즉시식립 임플란트를 대체 권고하기 전에 잘 설계된 다기관 RCT와 장기 추적이 필요하다.

## Summary

Root-analog implants (RAIs) are custom, patient-specific implants designed from CBCT/surface-scan data to replicate the exact external morphology of the tooth being extracted, aiming to close the geometric mismatch between conventional screw-type implants and the natural extraction socket. This systematic review (PROSPERO CRD420251162616) pooled 28 clinical studies (432 RAIs, 1997–2025) — predominantly single-patient clinical reports and small case series, plus 1 RCT and 1 retrospective comparative study — evaluating titanium, zirconia, and hybrid titanium-zirconia RAIs fabricated by subtractive CAD-CAM milling or additive manufacturing (SLM, DLMS, DLMF). Because of heterogeneous designs and outcome reporting, only a descriptive synthesis was possible (no meta-analysis).

Survival rates were mostly 71–100% and success rates 64.5–100%, with titanium and titanium-zirconia hybrid RAIs the most predictable (survival up to 100%, MBL as low as 0.7 mm at 1 year) and zirconia RAIs the most variable (33.3–100% survival), particularly failing when macroretentive design or surface roughening was omitted. Esthetic and soft-tissue outcomes were consistently favorable across designs. The evidence base remains immature: short follow-up, small samples, near-absence of controlled comparative trials, and reporting bias limit confidence, and the authors call explicitly for multicenter RCTs before RAIs can be positioned as a standard alternative to conventional immediate implants.

## Key Contributions

- First SR to focus specifically on clinical (not feasibility/technical) evidence for RAIs, synthesizing 28 studies/432 implants spanning three decades (1997–2025).
- Material/fabrication-stratified outcomes table: titanium-milled, zirconia-milled, titanium-3D-printed (SLM/DLMF/DLMS), and hybrid titanium-zirconia, each with survival/success/MBL ranges.
- Identifies interdental macroretention + surface roughening as a critical design factor for zirconia RAI success (0% vs 92% survival with/without macroretentions in one prospective trial).
- Historical timeline (1997 ReImplant system → 2022 10-year DLMS follow-up → 2025 5-year zirconia RCT) contextualizing how the RAI concept matured with digital fabrication.
- Benchmarks RAI survival (71.1–100%, one 33.3% outlier) against prior SRs of conventional immediate implants (83.7–100%), showing RAIs are broadly comparable for titanium/hybrid designs but more variable overall.

## Methodology

- PRISMA-guided systematic search of PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Library (September 2025, no language restriction), registered a priori on PROSPERO (CRD420251162616).
- 8821 records → 2940 duplicates removed → 5881 screened → 30 full-text assessed (+1 other-source) → 28 included, 3 excluded (non-human/non-clinical).
- Included designs: 16 clinical reports, 1 retrospective case series, 7 prospective case series, 2 prospective nonrandomized studies, 1 RCT, 1 retrospective comparative study.
- Risk of bias assessed with design-appropriate tools: RoB 2 (the RCT — moderate risk), ROBINS-I (2 nonrandomized studies — high risk), JBI checklists (clinical reports/case series — mostly full scores, some incomplete demographic reporting), Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (retrospective comparative study — low risk).
- No meta-analysis attempted; descriptive/narrative synthesis only, due to heterogeneity in designs, follow-up length, and outcome definitions.

## Results

- **432 RAIs**, ages 18–82, maxillary and mandibular sites (anterior incisors through molars), follow-up from months to 10 years (longest prospective series: 5 years).
- **Titanium–milled**: survival 70.6–100% (up to 3 y), success 96.5–100%, MBL ~1.1–1.3 mm — the established standard, predictable.
- **Zirconia–milled**: survival 33.3–100% depending on surface treatment/macroretention, success 71% (5 y)–100%, MBL 0.71–1.38 mm — excellent bone stability when successful, but severe vulnerability to early failure/fracture with minimal surface treatment.
- **Titanium–3D printed (SLM/DLMF/DLMS)**: survival 44.4–100% (up to 10 y), success 100%, lowest MBL (0.7–1.3 mm at 1 y) — outcomes comparable to or better than milled titanium when initial stability achieved.
- **Hybrid titanium-zirconia**: survival 83.3–100%, success 64.5–100%, mean MBL 1.20 ± 0.73 mm — combines titanium strength with zirconia esthetics.
- Comparative trial (Akkoyun 2022): CNC-milled titanium (70.6%) > DLMS titanium (44.4%) > zirconia (33.3%) survival — fabrication method matters as much as material.
- Esthetic/soft-tissue outcomes and patient satisfaction consistently favorable; occasional buccal recession/metal show-through with early titanium designs.
- Conclusion (authors): RAIs are a promising minimally invasive option for immediate single-tooth replacement with favorable short- to medium-term outcomes; titanium remains most reliable, zirconia needs optimized macroretentive design, and hybrid/porous-titanium designs are promising but need further validation. High-quality RCTs are needed.
