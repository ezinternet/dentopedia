## Three-line Summary

Prospective single-center RCT (n = 20 enrolled, 18 completed all phases) at Damascus University comparing porcelain-fused-to-metal (PFM) vs indirect composite resin (ICR) crowns on custom-made, thorny-macroretentive, root-analogue anatomic zirconia implants placed immediately (flapless, un-drilled) into fresh premolar extraction sockets.

18-month implant survival was 90% (2/20 early failures before loading, both before crown placement); mean marginal bone resorption (MBR) at 18 months was 0.61–0.77 mm across mesial/distal sites in both crown groups, with no statistically significant difference between PFM and ICR at either 12 or 18 months (all P > 0.05).

Restoration material (PFM vs composite) did not influence peri-implant crestal bone resorption on this root-analogue zirconia implant design — both are viable options — but the trial is small (n = 18 analyzed), non-split-mouth, single-center, and short-term (18 months), so it is hypothesis-generating rather than definitive.

## Summary

This is a prospective, single-center RCT conducted at Damascus University (2019–2020, ISRCTN88677526, CONSORT-reported) evaluating a custom-made, root-analogue, anatomic zirconia implant with novel thorn-like macro-retentions. Twenty patients requiring premolar extraction had their extracted tooth optically scanned; a one-piece Y-TZP zirconia implant replicating the root's external morphology (with 0.5 mm buccal reduction and thorn-retentions added mesially/distally) was CAD/CAM-milled, sandblasted, and sintered, then placed flapless — without socket drilling — directly into the fresh extraction socket by finger pressure and gentle malleting. After 3 months of healing, osseointegrated implants (18/20, 90%) were randomized 1:1 to receive either a PFM or an indirect composite resin (ICR) crown. Marginal bone resorption (MBR) was tracked radiographically at 12 and 18 months.

MBR ranged 0.2–1.2 mm across all implants at 18 months and did not differ significantly between PFM and ICR groups at either timepoint (all P > 0.05). No implant, peri-implant, or prosthetic complications were observed among the 18 surviving implants through 18 months. The authors conclude that PFM and ICR are both viable restoration options for this root-analogue zirconia implant design with respect to bone preservation, though they call for longer-term, larger, multicenter trials.

## Key Contributions

- Direct clinical test of restoration-material effect (metal-ceramic vs composite) on peri-implant MBR — a variable not previously isolated for root-analogue zirconia implants.
- Detailed, reproducible fabrication/placement protocol for a novel thorny-macroretentive, one-piece, root-analogue zirconia implant (optical scan → exocad design → Y-TZP CAD/CAM milling → sandblasting → sintering → flapless, drill-free socket placement).
- CONSORT-compliant, prospectively registered RCT design (rare within the root-analog-implant literature, which is dominated by case reports/series).
- 18-month survival (90%) and MBR data (0.2–1.2 mm) benchmarked against established implant-success criteria (≤1–1.5 mm bone loss in year 1) and against a comparable 5-year cohort (Bradley et al., 94.3% survival).

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective, single-center, randomized (random-number table) controlled trial; IRB-approved (Damascus University Research Ethics Committee, Reg. No. 2019-1593); ISRCTN88677526.
- **Population**: 20 patients (7 M, 13 F), age 21–56 (mean 35.2 ± 10.7), requiring premolar extraction (untreatable root caries, fracture, or perforation); Type-2/3 bone density; buccal cortical bone ≥1 mm; root length ≥10 mm. Smokers, uncontrolled systemic disease, pregnancy, and age >60 excluded.
- **Implant**: custom one-piece Y-TZP zirconia implant with fused coronal abutment, root morphology replicated from an optical scan of the extracted tooth, 0.5 mm buccal reduction, apical two-thirds mesial/distal thorn-retentions, sandblasted surface, sintered at 1500°C/8h.
- **Surgery**: flapless placement into curetted, un-drilled fresh extraction socket by finger pressure + gentle mallet; Periotest confirmed primary stability (negative PTV required); standard post-op antibiotic/analgesic/mouthwash regimen and soft diet.
- **Loading & randomization**: after 3-month healing, osseointegrated implants randomized 1:1 to PFM (cast metal + ceramic) or ICR (GRADIA PLUS composite) crowns, cemented with self-cured glass-ionomer (GC Fuji I).
- **Outcome assessment**: paralleling-technique periapical radiographs at baseline, 12, and 18 months; MBR measured mesially/distally, calibrated by implant height (Adobe Photoshop CC); independent t-tests, α = 0.05, SPSS V26.

## Results

- **Survival**: 18/20 implants (90%) survived to 18 months; both failures occurred early, before crown loading.
- **MBR at 12 months**: ICR 0.53 ± 0.21 mm (mesial) / 0.60 ± 0.14 mm (distal) vs PFM 0.67 ± 0.16 mm / 0.61 ± 0.27 mm — not significant (P = 0.91 mesial, P = 0.27 distal).
- **MBR at 18 months**: ICR 0.61 ± 0.27 mm / 0.67 ± 0.16 mm vs PFM 0.77 ± 0.29 mm / 0.77 ± 0.27 mm — not significant (P = 0.36 mesial, P = 0.25 distal). Overall MBR range 0.2–1.2 mm.
- **Complications**: none reported at implant, peri-implant, or prosthetic levels through 18 months (no pain, mobility, continuous radiolucency, or peri-implant infection).
- **Baseline equivalence**: no significant between-group differences in age, gender, implant length (mean 12.2 ± 1.3 mm), or baseline marginal bone level (9–14.4 mm) — supporting comparability despite the non-split-mouth design.
- **Bottom line**: restoration material (PFM vs composite resin) did not significantly affect peri-implant crestal bone resorption on this thorny-macroretentive root-analogue zirconia implant at 12 or 18 months; both are clinically viable choices.
