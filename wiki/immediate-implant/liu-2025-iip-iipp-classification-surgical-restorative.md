## One-line Summary
Retrospective study (n=44 patients, 53 implants, 15-year period): proposed 2-axis (surgical × restorative difficulty) classification for IIP/IIPP shows that high-difficulty cases have significantly worse survival (80–87.5%), higher MBL, and higher complication rates (33.3%) vs low-complexity cases (100% survival, 0% complications).

## 한줄요약

## Summary
This retrospective study from the University of Pennsylvania and Kaohsiung Medical University (2009–2024) proposed and validated a novel 2-axis classification for IIP and IIPP that simultaneously evaluates surgical difficulty (bone volume, socket anatomy, soft tissue condition, anatomical challenges) and restorative difficulty (occlusal loading, aesthetic demands, patient factors, prosthetic complexity) on a 3-tier (Low/Moderate/High) scale, producing a 3×3 risk matrix. Among 53 implants in 44 patients followed for a minimum of 12 months, overall survival was 96.2%. The combined High–High difficulty group showed 80% survival and 33.3% complication rate compared to 100% survival and 0% complications in Low–Low cases. Marginal bone loss and soft tissue recession both correlated significantly with surgical difficulty level.

## Key Contributions
- First classification addressing combined surgical + restorative difficulty specifically for IIP/IIPP (vs. ITI timing-based Type 1–4 which focuses on healing stage)
- 4 surgical parameters: bone volume (CBCT), socket anatomy (Elian Type I/II/III), soft tissue condition, anatomical proximity to vital structures
- 4 restorative parameters: occlusal loading (parafunctions/bruxism), aesthetic demands (smile line/visibility), patient factors (compliance/OH), prosthetic complexity
- Stratification creates actionable 3×3 risk matrix enabling pre-surgical counseling and realistic expectation setting
- Validated outcome gradient: survival 100% → 87.5% → 80%; complications 0% → 11.8% → 33.3% across Low→Moderate→High×High

## Methodology
- Design: Retrospective clinical study; single-center; multi-operator implied (university/private practice)
- n: 44 patients (23F, 21M), mean age 56.3 ± 11.8 years; 53 implants
- Site distribution: 52.8% maxillary anterior, 18.9% maxillary posterior, 15.1% mandibular anterior, 13.2% mandibular posterior
- Inclusion: single-tooth IIP or IIPP; complete records; ≥12 months follow-up
- Outcomes: survival, MBL, probing depth, bleeding on probing, keratinized tissue width, soft tissue recession, PES + White Esthetic Score (WES), patient satisfaction (0–10 VAS)
- Statistical: one-way ANOVA or chi-square across difficulty strata

## Results
**Implant survival**:
- Low×Low: 100% (8/8); all low-moderate combinations: 100%
- High×Moderate: 87.5% (7/8); High×High: 80.0% (4/5)
- Group comparison p=0.045

**Marginal bone loss (MBL) by surgical difficulty**:
- Low: 0.43 ± 0.31 mm; Moderate: 0.69 ± 0.52 mm; High: 1.12 ± 0.78 mm (p<0.001)

**Soft tissue recession**: Low 0.2±0.3, Moderate 0.5±0.7, High 1.1±1.0 mm (p<0.001)

**Esthetic scores (PES+WES)**: Low restorative difficulty 21.1±1.6; Moderate 19.7±2.1; High 17.8±2.5 (p<0.001)

**Patient satisfaction**: Low 9.3±0.8; Moderate 8.8±1.1; High 7.4±1.8

**Complication types**: soft tissue recession 7.5%, thread exposure 4.2%, provisional fracture 3.8%, mucositis 3.3%, implant failure 2.8%

Overall mean MBL: 0.74 ± 0.63 mm; probing depth 2.8 ± 0.9 mm; BoP 18.4%
