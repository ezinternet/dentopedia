## Three-line Summary

Narrative literature review (MEDLINE 1990–June 2003; 31 studies, ≥10 cases; survival/success studies ≥12 months follow-up) on immediate and delayed (early) implant placement after tooth extraction, integrating extraction-socket healing biology with clinical procedures and outcomes.

All included studies reported implant survival but not formal clinical success; peri-implant defects showed high regenerative potential, with horizontal defects ≤2 mm often healing by spontaneous bone fill around rough-surface implants, while larger/damaged defects typically required membranes and grafts; short-term survival of immediate/delayed-immediate implants was comparable to healed-ridge placement.

Clinical implication: immediate/early placement can be predictable for short-term survival, but evidence for long-term peri-implant tissue health, prosthetic stability, and esthetics was limited, and timing decisions must weigh infection/soft-tissue advantages of delayed healing against ridge resorption during the healing interval.

## Summary

Chen et al. (2004, ITI consensus-era review in *Int J Oral Maxillofac Implants* supplement) reviewed the biologic basis and clinical outcomes of immediate and delayed/early implant placement following tooth extraction. Based on a MEDLINE search (1990–June 2003) yielding 31 eligible studies (≥10 cases; survival/success studies ≥12 months), the review described extraction-socket healing and dimensional changes, then synthesized evidence on defect healing and timing-related trade-offs. Across included studies, survival outcomes were consistently reported and short-term survival for immediate/delayed-immediate placement appeared comparable to placement in healed ridges, but formal clinical success outcomes were not reported. The authors emphasized the regenerative capacity of peri-implant defects (with spontaneous fill for small horizontal gaps around rough surfaces and augmentation for larger/damaged defects), while noting unresolved gaps in long-term tissue, prosthetic, and esthetic outcomes.

## Key Contributions
- Integrated extraction-socket healing biology (histologic phases and dimensional ridge changes) with implant placement timing decisions.
- Clarified defect-healing expectations: small horizontal defects (≤2 mm) may heal spontaneously around rough-surface implants; larger/damaged defects generally require GBR/bone grafting.
- Compared immediate/early vs delayed approaches as a trade-off: delayed placement can aid infection resolution and soft-tissue management but allows ridge resorption during healing.
- Identified a key evidence limitation of the era: survival data were available, but standardized long-term "success" metrics (tissue health, prosthesis stability, esthetics) were sparse.

## Methodology
- **Type**: Narrative literature review (not a meta-analysis).
- **Search**: MEDLINE 1990–June 2003; supplemented by bibliography screening.
- **Eligibility**: RCTs/nonrandomized trials, cohort/case-control studies, and case series with ≥10 cases; survival/success reports required ≥12 months follow-up.
- **Evidence base**: 31 included articles; most short-term and not randomized by placement timing or augmentation method.
- **Endpoints synthesized**: Survival/success reporting, peri-implant defect regeneration, procedural considerations, and tissue/esthetic implications.

## Results
- **Survival**: Reported in all included studies; short-term survival for immediate and delayed-immediate placement comparable to implants in healed ridges (as synthesized).
- **Success**: No included studies reported formal clinical success outcomes.
- **Peri-implant defects**: High regenerative potential overall.
- **Defect size rule-of-thumb (review synthesis)**: Horizontal defects ≤2 mm often showed spontaneous bone fill with rough-surface implants; defects >2 mm or damaged socket walls generally required concomitant augmentation (barrier membranes/grafts).
- **Timing considerations**: Delayed placement could improve infection control and soft-tissue conditions for flap adaptation, but was accompanied by ridge resorption and potentially greater augmentation need.
