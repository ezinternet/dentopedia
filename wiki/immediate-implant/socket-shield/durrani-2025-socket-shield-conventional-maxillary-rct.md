## Three-line Summary

Parallel-arm randomized controlled trial (n=24; 12 socket-shield technique [SST] / 12 conventional immediate implant placement [IIP]) in Type I intact maxillary extraction sockets, with assessments at 8, 12, and 36 months; outcomes: marginal bone loss (MBL) on radiograph and Pink Esthetic Score (PES).

SST consistently outperformed conventional IIP at all time points: MBL at 36 months was 1.40 vs 1.70 mm (p=0.040), PES was 10.50 vs 9.36 (p=0.008); both groups achieved 100% implant survival over 36 months.

The SST advantage did not diminish over 3 years; both MBL difference (0.30 mm) and PES difference (~1.1 points) are clinically relevant, extending the longest reported SST vs conventional IIP comparison to 36 months in Type I intact sockets.

## One-line Summary
RCT (n=24, 36-month, maxillary esthetic zone, Type I sockets): socket-shield technique yielded significantly less marginal bone loss (1.40 vs 1.70 mm, p=0.040) and superior Pink Esthetic Score (10.50 vs 9.36, p=0.008) vs conventional IIP at 36 months; 100% survival in both groups.

## Summary
This randomized controlled trial from Banaras Hindu University compared socket-shield technique (SST) to conventional immediate implant placement (IIP) in 24 patients with Type 1 (intact) maxillary extraction sockets. Twelve implants per group were followed for 36 months with a prosthetic timeline of temporary prosthesis at 8 months and final crown at 12 months. SST consistently outperformed conventional IIP at all time points (8, 12, 36 months) for both marginal bone loss (MBL) and Pink Esthetic Score (PES). All 24 implants survived the 36-month observation period. The study validates that the periodontal ligament preservation inherent to the socket-shield concept translates into measurable hard and soft tissue advantages over an extended follow-up.

## Key Contributions
- Long-term (36-month) RCT comparing SST vs conventional IIP — one of the few with this duration
- SST advantage consistent across all time points, not just short-term — benefits do not diminish over 3 years
- 100% survival in both groups confirms SST safety comparable to conventional approach
- MBL difference (0.30 mm at 36mo) is clinically relevant and statistically significant
- PES advantage (~1.1 points) crosses the clinically meaningful threshold (~1 point)

## Methodology
- Study design: Parallel-arm RCT
- n: 24 patients (12 SST, 12 conventional); 24 implants; 12 females, 12 males; age 18–55, mean 35.7 ± 1.5 years
- Inclusion: Type I (intact buccal bone and soft tissue) extraction sockets, maxillary esthetic zone
- Prosthetic protocol: Temporary at T1 (8 months), final crown at T2 (12 months)
- Assessments: T1 (8 months), T2 (12 months), T3 (36 months)
- Primary outcomes: MBL (radiographic), PES
- Statistical: Two-way repeated measures ANOVA; pairwise group comparisons at each time point

## Results
| Outcome | SST | Conventional | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| MBL at 8mo (mm) | 0.66 ± 0.37 | 1.00 ± 0.42 | 0.047 |
| MBL at 12mo (mm) | 0.98 ± 0.36 | 1.36 ± 0.40 | 0.027 |
| MBL at 36mo (mm) | 1.40 ± 0.29 | 1.70 ± 0.36 | 0.040 |
| PES at 8mo | 11.04 ± 0.91 | 9.92 ± 0.99 | 0.009 |
| PES at 12mo | 10.77 ± 0.88 | 9.65 ± 0.93 | 0.006 |
| PES at 36mo | 10.50 ± 0.90 | 9.36 ± 0.98 | 0.008 |
| Implant survival | 100% | 100% | — |

Two-way ANOVA: treatment × time interaction NS; treatment alone and time alone each significant.
