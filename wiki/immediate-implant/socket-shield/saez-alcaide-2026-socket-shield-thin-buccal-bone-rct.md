## Three-line Summary

RCT (Med Oral 2026): 40 patients, thin buccal bone phenotype (BBT <1.5 mm); socket shield technique (SST) vs immediate implant placement (IIP) alone; alveolar ridge dimensions assessed by CBCT superimposition at 1 year.

SST produced significantly less horizontal ridge reduction, buccal vertical bone loss, and overall volumetric bone loss vs IIP at all evaluated levels (all p<0.001); marginal bone stability significantly better with SST from prosthetic loading onward.

SST is a predictable strategy for preserving peri-implant hard tissues in anatomically compromised sites with thin buccal bone.

## Summary

Sáez-Alcaide et al. (Complutense University of Madrid, Med Oral 2026) conducted the first RCT specifically targeting the thin buccal bone phenotype (BBT <1.5 mm) — a subgroup at particularly high risk for post-extraction ridge resorption — to compare socket shield technique (SST) vs conventional immediate implant placement (IIP). Forty patients were randomized 1:1 and assessed at baseline, implant placement, prosthetic loading, and 1-year follow-up using CBCT superimposition for volumetric analysis and standardized periapical radiographs for MBL. SST consistently outperformed IIP across all dimensional outcomes: horizontal and buccal vertical ridge dimensions were significantly better preserved (p<0.001), volumetric bone loss was significantly less in the IIP group (p<0.001), and MBL progression was significantly greater in IIP from prosthetic loading onward (p<0.001). These results specifically address a gap in the existing SST literature, which previously had not stratified outcomes by buccal bone phenotype.

## Key Contributions

- First RCT specifically designed for thin buccal bone phenotype (BBT <1.5 mm) comparing SST vs IIP
- CBCT superimposition provides volumetric dimensional analysis — more comprehensive than linear measurements
- Demonstrates MBL superiority of SST beginning at prosthetic loading (not just at 1 year)
- Strengthens evidence for SST as a site-specific intervention for anatomically high-risk extraction sockets
- Fills key stratification gap in existing SST systematic reviews

## Methodology

Parallel-arm RCT; 40 patients, 1 tooth per patient; single-rooted extraction sites with BBT <1.5 mm confirmed by CBCT. SST group: socket shield preparation + immediate implant. IIP group: immediate implant alone. Assessments: CBCT superimposition (baseline and 1 year); standardized periapical radiographs (implant placement, prosthetic loading, 1 year). Outcomes: horizontal ridge change (mm), buccal vertical bone loss (mm), volumetric changes (mm³), MBL (mm). Statistics: ANCOVA adjusting for baseline values.
