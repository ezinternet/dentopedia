## Three-line Summary

Proof-of-principle animal experiment (1 beagle dog, mandibular premolars; 4 implants, 4-month healing) introducing the socket-shield technique (SST): hemisect tooth, retain buccal distal root fragment ~1 mm coronal to buccal bone plate, apply EMD, and place implant lingual to the fragment for immediate implant placement.

All four implants osseointegrated without histologic inflammatory reaction; the retained fragment showed no resorption, preserved physiologic periodontal ligament attachment to buccal bone, and exhibited newly formed cementum on treated surfaces and at implant-contact areas.

Clinical implication: SST suggested a biologic approach to preserve buccal bone during immediate implantation, but evidence at this stage was limited to a single short-term preclinical model and required further histologic and long-term clinical validation.

## Summary

Hürzeler et al. (2010) reported the first proof-of-principle of the socket-shield technique (SST), a partial root-retention approach intended to preserve the buccal periodontal attachment and buccal bone during immediate implant placement. In one beagle dog, mandibular premolars were hemisected and the buccal distal root fragment was retained ~1 mm coronal to the buccal bone plate; after enamel matrix derivative application, implants were placed lingual to the fragment (with or without contact) and healed for 4 months. Histology and BSEM showed osseointegration of all four implants without inflammation, absence of root-fragment resorption, preserved buccal periodontal ligament, and new cementum formation on treated dentin and implant-contact interfaces. The study established the biologic plausibility of SST but explicitly called for further histologic and long-term follow-up before routine clinical recommendation.

## Key Contributions
- First histologic proof-of-principle of **socket-shield technique (SST)** for buccal tissue preservation in immediate implant placement.
- Demonstrated osseointegration adjacent to a retained buccal root fragment without inflammatory complications in a canine model.
- Showed preservation of buccal periodontal ligament attachment and cementum formation on treated/implant-contact surfaces.

## Methodology
- **Design**: Preclinical proof-of-principle (animal).
- **Model**: 1 beagle dog; mandibular 3rd/4th premolars hemisected.
- **Technique**: Buccal fragment of distal root retained ~1 mm coronal to buccal bone plate; EMD applied; titanium implant placed lingual to fragment (with/without contact); healing abutment connected.
- **Outcomes/timepoint**: 4 months; histologic evaluation and backscatter scanning electron microscopy (BSEM).

## Results
- **Implants**: 4/4 osseointegrated without histologic inflammatory reaction.
- **Root fragment**: No resorption observed.
- **Buccal interface**: Physiologic periodontal ligament maintained between fragment and buccal bone plate.
- **Lingual/contact interfaces**: Newly formed cementum on treated dentin and on implant surface where contact occurred (as reported in abstract/supporting figures).
