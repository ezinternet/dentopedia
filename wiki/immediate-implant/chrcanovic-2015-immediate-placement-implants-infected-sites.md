## Summary

This systematic review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/cid.12098)) searched PubMed through March 2013 and reviewed 28 studies (human and animal) examining immediate implant placement into sites classified with periapical, endodontic, perioendodontic, or periodontal infection. The conclusion was that high survival rates are achievable in infected sites provided appropriate clinical procedures are followed: meticulous socket cleaning, curettage/debridement, and 0.12% chlorhexidine rinse. A key finding was the absence of RCTs — all evidence came from non-randomized cohort studies and animal experiments. The paper highlighted the heterogeneity of infection classification across studies as a major limitation. This SR is cited by both Prati 2017 and the subsequent meta-analyses (Saijeva 2020, Pranckeviciene 2024) as the foundational reference, now superseded by more recent systematic work.

## Key Contributions

- Established the three-element minimum decontamination protocol: cleaning + curettage + CHX 0.12%
- First comprehensive SR to include both animal and human evidence for infected-site IIP
- Identified the classification heterogeneity problem (periodontal vs endodontic vs combined) that persists in subsequent SRs
- Documented the evidence gap (0 RCTs at 2013) that spurred subsequent RCT-based meta-analyses

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review (no meta-analysis)
- **Search**: PubMed, March 2013; 706 references → 28 studies
- **Inclusion**: Human and animal studies; implant into periapical/endodontic/periodontal infected sites
- **Exclusion**: Reviews, case reports

## Results

- High survival across studies with decontamination protocol
- Minimum required: (1) cleaning, (2) curettage/debridement, (3) CHX 0.12% rinse
- 0 RCTs available at search date
- Heterogeneous infection classification limits synthesis
