## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42016043309; 30 eligible studies, search through November 2016) of 3,049 implants in 1,435 patients (mean age 46.68 years, minimum 6-month follow-up), comparing immediate implant placement in fresh extraction sockets versus delayed implant placement in healed sockets.

Delayed implants showed significantly higher survival than immediate implants (98.38% vs 95.21%, p=.001); no significant difference was found for marginal bone loss (p=.32), implant stability quotient (p=.44), or pocket probing depth (p=.94).

Immediate placement in fresh sockets should be approached with caution given the significantly lower survival rate versus delayed placement in healed sockets — an important caution against over-generalizing later "immediate is equivalent" claims that are based on smaller or RCT-restricted samples.

## Summary

This 2017 systematic review and meta-analysis by Mello et al. compared implant survival and peri-implant tissue outcomes between immediate placement in fresh extraction sockets and delayed placement in healed sockets. Registered at PROSPERO (CRD42016043309), the review searched PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Library through November 2016 and pooled 30 eligible studies totaling 3,049 implants in 1,435 patients (mean age 46.68 years), with a minimum 6-month follow-up requirement. The headline finding is a statistically significant survival advantage for delayed implants (98.38%) over immediate implants (95.21%, p=.001), while marginal bone loss, implant stability quotient (ISQ), and pocket probing depth showed no significant between-group difference. The authors conclude that immediate implants placed in fresh sockets warrant cautious case selection given the lower pooled survival rate.

Only the PubMed structured abstract was retrievable for this source (no PMC full text or PDF available), so study-level details — design mix of the 30 included studies, heterogeneity/I² statistics, risk-of-bias assessment, and subgroup breakdowns (e.g., by arch, socket wall integrity, grafting, loading protocol) — are not captured here. The pooled survival-rate difference and p-values above are taken directly from the abstract.

## Key Contributions

- Pools one of the larger contemporaneous datasets on immediate-vs-delayed implant timing: 30 studies, 3,049 implants, 1,435 patients.
- Reports a statistically significant survival disadvantage for immediate implants (95.21%) versus delayed implants (98.38%), p=.001 — a stronger and more definitive result than several earlier, smaller reviews on the same question.
- Shows that the survival gap is not mirrored in marginal bone loss, ISQ, or pocket probing depth, isolating implant loss/failure (not peri-implant tissue health) as the outcome driving the difference.
- Frames immediate placement in fresh sockets as requiring cautious patient/site selection rather than being presumptively equivalent to delayed placement.

## Methodology

- PROSPERO registration: CRD42016043309.
- Two reviewers searched independently across PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Library, using different search term sets, for articles published through November 2016.
- 30 eligible studies included; pooled analysis of 3,049 implants across 1,435 patients (mean age 46.68 years); minimum 6-month follow-up required for study inclusion.
- Outcomes analyzed: implant survival rate, marginal bone loss, implant stability quotient (ISQ), and pocket probing depth.
- **Retrieval limitation**: only the structured PubMed abstract was available (no PMC full text/PDF), so the statistical model (fixed vs random effects), risk-of-bias tool, and individual study design breakdown could not be extracted for this page.

Across 3,049 pooled implants in 1,435 patients (mean age 46.68 years, ≥6-month follow-up), survival rate was the only outcome of the four analyzed to reach statistical significance, consistently favoring delayed implant placement into healed sockets over immediate placement into fresh extraction sockets.
