## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional CBCT morphometric study (n = 250 subjects aged 17–66, all maxillary front teeth present, iCAT unit, Clin Oral Implants Res 2011) measuring anterior maxillary facial bone-wall thickness at 1, 3 and 5 mm apical to the facial bone crest, plus the CEJ-to-crest distance.

The CEJ-to-facial-crest distance ranged **1.6–3 mm**, and the facial bone wall was **≤1 mm thick at most measured locations in every tooth site examined**, with **close to 50% of sites at ≤0.5 mm**.

Because such a thin wall may undergo marked dimensional diminution after extraction, facial bone thickness must be assessed before tooth removal and before planning anterior maxillary rehabilitation — but the record is abstract-only: no per-tooth breakdown, no dispersion or p-values, no implant outcome.

## Summary

This 250-subject CBCT survey (17–66 years, all maxillary front teeth present, iCAT unit) is the largest anterior-maxilla facial bone-wall dataset held in this folder, and the one the folder's other papers lean on without quoting it. It reports two morphometric quantities: the distance from the cemento-enamel junction to the facial bone crest, which varied between 1.6 and 3 mm, and the thickness of the facial bone wall measured at three depths — 1, 3 and 5 mm apical to the crest. The wall was ≤1 mm thick at most measured locations in **all** tooth sites examined, and close to half of all sites were ≤0.5 mm, so the thin-wall finding is arch-wide rather than confined to a few positions, and the ≤1 mm figure is a floor on the problem rather than a ceiling. The authors' clinical conclusion is a planning rule rather than a measurement: because a wall this thin may undergo marked dimensional diminution after extraction, its thickness has to be assessed before the tooth is removed and before rehabilitation of the anterior maxilla is planned. What this paper does not do is test any minimum thickness against an outcome — it establishes how thin the wall usually is, not which sites survive immediate placement intact — so it functions as the preoperative gate that
[[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2013-ridge-alterations-post-extraction-esthetic]]
measures the consequences of, not as evidence that augmentation works.

## Key Contributions

- **Prevalence, not just a mean.** The claim is that ≤1 mm holds "in most locations in **all** tooth sites examined" and that close to 50% of sites are ≤0.5 mm — a case-selection statement about *how often* an anterior site fails a thickness threshold, which an average alone cannot express. Half the sites having effectively no preservable facial plate is a stronger planning fact than "the mean is about 1 mm".
- **Largest n in this folder for the question.** 250 subjects in one dataset, against 93 extraction sites in [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/huynh-ba-2010-analysis-socket-bone-wall]] and 39 patients in [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2013-ridge-alterations-post-extraction-esthetic]].
- **Three fixed apical levels by design (1, 3, 5 mm below the crest)**, so the depth-gradient of thinning was part of the protocol — though the abstract reports the conclusion only in aggregate, and per-level values cannot be recovered from this record.
- **A second measurement unique in this folder: the CEJ-to-crest distance (1.6–3 mm).** None of the buccal-plate-thickness pages here report the supracrestal tissue-attachment dimension alongside wall thickness.
- **Explicitly converts anatomy into a pre-extraction planning rule** — the direct precedent for using preoperative CBCT morphometry as immediate-implant case selection, which is this folder's entire scope.

## Methodology

Cross-sectional CBCT morphometric survey — single timepoint, no intervention, no comparator, no
follow-up, no outcome. **n = 250 subjects**, aged **17–66 years**, all with **all maxillary front
teeth present**; written informed consent obtained. Country, centers and ethics approval are **not
stated in the abstract**. Whether 250 subjects corresponds to 250 measured sites or more is also
unstated — the abstract notes the scan "included all tooth and edentulous sites in the dentition".
Imaging on the **iCAT** cone-beam CT unit, images acquired with iCAT software and processed on a
computer; **voxel size is not reported**, which matters because sub-millimetre walls sit near the
resolution limit of many CBCT protocols. Two measurements: (i) distance between the CEJ and the
facial bone crest, and (ii) facial bone wall thickness at **1, 3 and 5 mm apical to the facial bone
crest**. Outcomes are purely morphometric — **no implant outcome of any kind is reported**. The
abstract contains **no statistical test, p-value, mean or standard deviation**; results are given
as ranges and proportions only.

## Results

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance CEJ → facial bone crest | **1.6–3 mm** |
| Facial bone wall — most locations, all tooth sites | **≤1 mm** |
| Sites with facial bone wall ≤0.5 mm | **close to 50%** |
| Measurement levels | 1, 3 and 5 mm apical to the facial bone crest |
| n | 250 subjects (17–66 y, all maxillary front teeth present) |

- **Arch-wide, not site-specific**: ≤1 mm holds at most measured locations in *every* tooth site examined — no anterior position escapes it.
- **Half the sites are thinner than 0.5 mm**, so the ≤1 mm figure understates the prevalence of walls with nothing left to preserve.
- **Depth dependence is not reportable**: results are given pooled across the 1/3/5 mm levels, so whether the wall thins further apically **cannot be stated from this abstract**.
- **No threshold is tested.** 1 mm and 0.5 mm are descriptive cut-points in the results; the paper sets no minimum thickness against any outcome, so it cannot by itself justify an augmentation decision.

## Limitations

- **Abstract-only record** — full text not retrieved; per-tooth/per-level values, dispersion, statistics, voxel size and reliability are unrecoverable.
- **Anatomy only, no outcome** — the paper predicts dimensional loss after extraction but measures none of it; the causal step from "thin wall" to "resorption" is imported from prior literature.
- **No tested decision threshold** — shows how thin walls usually are, not which thickness is safe.
- **Sub-millimetre measurement against finite CBCT resolution** — half the sites measure ≤0.5 mm while the voxel size is unreported, so the precision of the very values driving the conclusion cannot be appraised.
- **Population and selection undescribed** — no country, center, sex distribution, or reason for tooth loss; facial wall thickness varies by population and by extraction indication.
- **Sites vs subjects ambiguous** — the scan covered all tooth and edentulous sites, so the denominator behind the proportions is unclear.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Preoperative CBCT is the gate, and the gate is usually failed.** With most anterior sites at ≤1 mm and half at ≤0.5 mm, an intact facial contour at immediate placement is the exception, and it must be planned for rather than discovered intraoperatively.
- **Read the finding as prevalence, not severity.** The clinical implication is a *planning* rule (assess before extraction), not a *technique* conclusion — the paper supplies no outcome against which to compare grafted vs ungrafted sites.
- **Pairs directly with the downstream damage reports.** The 1–2 mm inflection in [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/yang-2019-labial-bone-thickness-esthetics-iipp]] and the 7.5 mm vertical loss in thin-wall sites in [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/chappuis-2013-ridge-alterations-post-extraction-esthetic]] describe what these ubiquitous thin walls cost; this page quantifies how many sites start in that state.
