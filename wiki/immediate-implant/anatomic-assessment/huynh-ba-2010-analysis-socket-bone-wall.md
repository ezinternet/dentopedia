## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional intraoperative morphometric study (n = 93 extraction sites, maxillary esthetic zone — canine-to-canine and premolars; recorded within an ongoing prospective randomized-controlled multicenter trial, Clin Oral Implants Res 2010) measuring buccal and palatal socket bone-wall width at extraction.

Mean buccal wall 1 mm vs palatal 1.2 mm (P<0.05); anterior (canine-to-canine) buccal mean 0.8 mm vs posterior/premolar 1.1 mm (P<0.05), with 87% of anterior buccal walls ≤1 mm and only 3% 2 mm wide, against 59% and 9% respectively in premolar sites.

If the ≥2 mm minimal-buccal-width criterion is valid, only a limited number of anterior maxillary sites meet it, so augmentation is needed in most cases — but the record is abstract-only: no instrument, no measurement level, no standard deviations, no implant outcome.

## Summary

Huynh-Ba and colleagues measured the buccal and palatal bony walls at 93 maxillary extraction sites in the esthetic zone, as a morphometric sub-study of an ongoing prospective randomized-controlled multicenter trial on immediate implant placement — the intraoperative counterpart to the CBCT bone-thickness datasets already held in this folder. The buccal wall was thinner than the palatal wall at the same sites (1 vs 1.2 mm, P<0.05), and thinner anteriorly than in premolar sites (0.8 vs 1.1 mm, P<0.05), so risk is position-dependent rather than uniform across the arch. The clinically load-bearing finding is the prevalence distribution rather than the mean: 87% of anterior buccal walls were ≤1 mm and only 3% were 2 mm wide, against 59% and 9% in premolar sites. Judged against the ≥2 mm minimal-buccal-width criterion the authors import from prior work, only a limited minority of anterior maxillary sites qualify, which is why they conclude that augmentation procedures are needed in most clinical situations. That conclusion is explicitly conditional — the 2 mm threshold is an assumption this study tests the anatomy against, not something it establishes — and because the ingested record is abstract-only, neither the measurement method nor any implant outcome can be reported from this page.

## Key Contributions

- **Intraoperative socket-wall morphometry in actual IIP candidates** — the buccal and palatal walls were measured at extraction in the very sites considered for immediate placement, rather than inferred from pre-operative scans of an intact dentition. This is the design gap this folder had before: every other page here is CBCT-based.
- **Prevalence, not just a mean** — 87% of anterior buccal walls ≤1 mm, 3% 2 mm wide (premolars: 59% / 9%). A case-selection statement about *how often* a site fails a thickness threshold, which a mean alone cannot give.
- **Buccal < palatal at the same sites** (1 vs 1.2 mm) — identifies the facial wall as the deficient side in the maxillary esthetic zone.
- **Anterior-vs-premolar gradient** (0.8 vs 1.1 mm, P<0.05) — the esthetic zone is the high-risk end of a single maxillary gradient, not a uniformly thin region.
- **Conditional framing of the threshold** — the authors apply the ≥2 mm criterion as an imported assumption ("*if* … *is valid*"), keeping the anatomy claim separate from the threshold claim.

## Methodology

Cross-sectional intraoperative morphometric measurement, nested inside "an ongoing prospective randomized-controlled multicenter clinical study on immediate implant placement" — **no randomized comparison and no control arm are reported in this paper**. Setting: extraction sites in the maxillary esthetic zone (anterior teeth and premolars), analysed as *anterior (canine to canine)* and *posterior (premolar)* subgroups. n = **93 extraction sites**; the abstract does not state whether that is 93 sites in 93 patients, nor the subgroup n's. Measurement: width of the buccal and palatal bony walls recorded at extraction — the instrument, the reference level (crest, mid-root, or a fixed distance apical to the crest) and the number of apicocoronal levels are **not stated in the abstract**. Outcomes: buccal/palatal wall width, mean plus the distribution of buccal width against 1 mm and 2 mm cut-points; **no implant outcome** is reported. Statistics: the abstract attaches (P<0.05) to the buccal-vs-palatal and the anterior-vs-posterior mean differences; tests, exact p-values and dispersion measures are not given.

- **Buccal is the deficient wall**: thinner than the palatal wall at the same sites (1 vs 1.2 mm).
- **Anterior is the deficient region**: buccal mean 0.8 mm anteriorly vs 1.1 mm in premolars.
- **10% of anterior buccal walls are unaccounted for** in the abstract — the 87% (≤1 mm) and 3% (2 mm wide) figures leave the 1–2 mm band and anything >2 mm unitemised, so "how many anterior sites clear 2 mm" cannot be stated exactly.
- **Background premise accepted, not tested**: immediate placement fails to prevent post-extraction socket dimensional change, and 1–2 mm of buccal bone has been *suggested* as necessary to maintain a stable vertical alveolar crest dimension.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Case selection before Type 1 placement.** In the anterior maxilla the buccal wall is ≤1 mm in the large majority of sites, so immediate placement without a plan for the facial contour is the exception rather than the rule.
- **Posterior maxilla is not automatically safe.** Premolar sites were also majority-thin (59% ≤1 mm), just less severely so — the anterior/premolar difference is in degree, not in kind.
- **Read the conclusion conditionally.** The paper shows how few anterior sites *would* satisfy a 2 mm wall; it does not establish 2 mm as the correct threshold, and it reports no outcome with which to argue that augmentation improves results.
