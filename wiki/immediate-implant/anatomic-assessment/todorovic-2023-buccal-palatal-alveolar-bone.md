## Three-line Summary

Cadaveric ex-vivo micro-CT morphometric study (Clin Implant Dent Relat Res 2023;25(2):261-270; PMID 36596566) — 72 dry human skulls scanned at the South African Nuclear Energy Corporation, axial planes over every alveolus/tooth from canine to canine, buccal and palatal bone dimensions measured at crestal, 3 mm, 6 mm and 9 mm levels.

Buccal bone rarely exceeded 0.5 mm and consisted of bundle bone only for all investigated teeth at all levels; up to a third of teeth showed buccal fenestrations; palatal bone was thicker than buccal, rising from <1 mm at crestal level to 3.77 mm (centrals), 4.56 mm (laterals) and 5.43 mm (canines) at the 9 mm level.

Immediate implant placement in the anterior maxilla therefore carries a real anatomic risk — the facial wall is essentially all bundle bone and fenestrated in up to a third of teeth — so per-site planning and an individualised approach are required; the limitation is that this is bone-only cadaveric morphometry, with no soft tissue, no clinical outcome, and no voxel size, skull demographics or statistics in the abstract.

## 세줄요약

시체(ex-vivo) 미세전산단층촬영(micro-CT) 형태계측 연구 — 남아프리카 원자력발전공사(South African Nuclear Energy Corporation)에서 건조 인체 두개골 72개를 스캔하고, 상악 견치~견치 구역 각 치조·치아마다 축면(axial plane)을 생성해 협측·구개측 골 두께를 치아경사부(crestal)·3 mm·6 mm·9 mm 수준에서 측정함.

협측골(buccal bone)은 모든 치아의 모든 수준에서 0.5 mm를 거의 넘지 않고 전부 다발뼈(bundle bone)만이었으며, 최대 1/3의 치아에서 협측 개구부(fenestration)가 관찰됨. 구개측(palatal) 골은 협측보다 두꺼워 치아경사부 <1 mm에서 시작해 9 mm 수준에서 중앙치 3.77 mm, 측치 4.56 mm, 견치 5.43 mm에 이름.

임상적 함의: 협측골이 사실상 다발뼈뿐이고 최대 1/3에서 개구부를 보이므로 상악 전치부 즉시식립(immediate implant placement)은 해부학적 위험이 크며 부위별 철저한 계획과 개별적 접근이 요구됨. 한계는 생존 환자 코호트가 아닌 골-only cadaveric 자료이고, 초록 기반이라 연조직·임상결과·복셀 크기·두개골 인구 특성 및 통계가 보고되지 않았음.

## Summary

This ex-vivo micro-CT study measured buccal and palatal alveolar bone dimensions across the maxillary anterior segment (canine to canine) in 72 dry human skulls, at four apicocoronal levels from the crestal level down to 9 mm — the authors' stated rationale being that clinical CBCT studies of the same region have produced varying results and a higher-resolution tool was needed. The buccal wall almost never exceeded 0.5 mm and was composed of bundle bone only, for every investigated tooth at every level, and up to a third of teeth showed buccal fenestrations; palatal bone was consistently thicker, increasing from <1 mm at the crestal level to 3.77, 4.56 and 5.43 mm at the 9 mm level for centrals, laterals and canines respectively. Because the buccal plate in the esthetic zone is essentially tooth-dependent bone with fenestrations, immediate implant placement there carries an anatomic risk that the authors frame as requiring thorough, per-site, individualised planning to secure long-term esthetic and functional stability. Read as a `refines` rather than a `contradicts` contribution: the direction of the finding agrees with the CBCT literature we hold, but the magnitude is systematically thinner than clinical CBCT can resolve, because a higher-resolution modality measures a thinner wall and because a dry skull has no soft-tissue envelope to blur the boundary.

## Key Contributions
- **Micro-CT resolution instead of clinical CBCT**: the explicit methodological contribution — higher spatial resolution, no beam-hardening or partial-volume error, which is precisely the error source that makes thin buccal plates hard to quantify on CBCT.
- **Buccal bone rarely exceeded 0.5 mm, bundle bone only** — for all investigated teeth at all measured levels, i.e. the facial wall in the anterior maxilla is tooth-dependent bone throughout the 0–9 mm corridor that matters for implant placement, with no apical thickening into a stabilisable facial plateau.
- **Buccal fenestrations in up to a third of teeth** — a site-level risk datum; the buccal wall is not merely thin but intermittently absent over the root.
- **Palatal reference values by tooth type at 9 mm** (central 3.77 mm, lateral 4.56 mm, canine 5.43 mm) — quantifying the side that actually has bone to anchor an immediately-placed implant, and showing the palatal wall grows apico-coronally while the buccal wall does not.
- **Implication for the ~2 mm facial-wall premise**: if the buccal wall rarely exceeds 0.5 mm at any level from 0 to 9 mm, then a protective 2 mm facial wall is essentially never present in this zone — which sharpens, rather than overturns, the standing recommendation to treat thin facial walls as a high-risk feature for immediate placement.

## Methodology
Cadaveric ex-vivo morphometric study. **72 dry human skulls** were scanned with micro-CT at the South African Nuclear Energy Corporation; specialised (unnamed) software handled 3-D rendering, segmentation and visualisation of the reconstructed volume data. Axial planes were created over each alveolus/tooth from canine to canine, and buccal and palatal bone dimensions were measured at the **crestal, 3 mm, 6 mm and 9 mm** levels. Outcomes were buccal thickness, palatal thickness, buccal fenestration presence, and bone composition (bundle bone) per level and tooth type. Not reported in the abstract: voxel size and scan settings, number of teeth measured, software identity, whether quoted values are means or medians, inter-/intra-rater reliability, and all statistical tests. Skull demographics (age, sex, ancestry, anterior dentate status) are not stated; the scanning country is a facility location, not a statement of sample provenance.

## Results

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---|
| Buccal bone thickness (all teeth, all levels) | Rarely exceeded **0.5 mm** |
| Buccal bone composition | **Bundle bone only** |
| Buccal fenestrations | **Up to 1/3 of teeth** |
| Palatal vs buccal thickness | Palatal thicker |
| Palatal at crestal level | **<1 mm** |
| Palatal at 9 mm — central incisor | **3.77 mm** |
| Palatal at 9 mm — lateral incisor | **4.56 mm** |
| Palatal at 9 mm — canine | **5.43 mm** |

- Buccal wall: **no apico-coronal gradient** — ≤0.5 mm at crestal, 3 mm, 6 mm and 9 mm alike.
- Palatal wall: **increases apico-coronally**, from <1 mm at the crest to 3.77 mm (centrals) / 4.56 mm (laterals) / 5.43 mm (canines) at 9 mm — canine palatal bone is the thickest, central incisor palatal bone the thinnest of the three.
- The **3 mm and 6 mm palatal values are not given** in the abstract; only the crestal (<1 mm) and 9 mm endpoints.
- Authors' conclusion as stated: immediate implants in the anterior maxilla have anatomical risks; buccal alveolar bone is very thin with fenestrations at certain positions; thorough planning and an individualised approach are needed to avoid complications and achieve stable long-term esthetic and functional results.

## Limitations
- **Cadaveric, bone-only** — no mucosa, periosteum or connective tissue, so the measured buccal thickness is *not* the same quantity as the clinical buccal plate-plus-soft-tissue envelope measured in living-patient CBCT series. This is the main reason these numbers should not be pooled directly with CBCT-derived values.
- **Resolution asymmetry cuts both ways** — a higher-resolution modality resolves thinner bone, so "rarely >0.5 mm" is partly a measurement-capability statement, not solely a biological one; conversely, clinical CBCT voxel size inflates apparent thin-wall thickness by partial volume. Neither paper's raw figure can be read without its voxel size.
- **Unknown specimen provenance** — age, sex and ancestry of the 72 skulls are unreported, and buccal plate thickness is age-dependent; transferability to a clinical population is asserted, not demonstrated.
- **Morphometry, no outcome** — no implant was placed; provides anatomic risk quantification only (no survival, marginal bone loss or esthetic score).
- **Single time point** — no post-extraction measurement, so it cannot speak to resorption dynamics.
- **Abstract-only record** — measurement uncertainty, segmentation protocol and all supporting statistics are unavailable.
