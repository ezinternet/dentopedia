## One-line Summary
Retrospective cohort (n=32 posterior immediate implants, 2-year): reactive soft tissue primary closure resulted in KM reduction of 0.56 mm in group A (bone defect only) vs KM gain of 0.67 mm in group B (bone + soft tissue defect; p=0.009), with both groups maintaining bone/soft tissue levels at 2 years.

## Summary
This 2-year retrospective study from the same Sichuan University group extends the reactive soft tissue primary closure technique (Liu 2019) to a more challenging population: sockets presenting with both buccal bone dehiscence and mucogingival recession (Group B, n=15), compared to sockets with intact soft tissue but buccal bone dehiscence only (Group A, n=17). All 32 posterior immediate implants utilized reactive soft tissue from the socket for primary closure. At 6 months, buccal bone dimensional changes were comparable between groups. A statistically significant difference in keratinized mucosa (KM) change was found: Group A lost 0.56 mm KM while Group B gained 0.67 mm (p=0.009). At 2-year follow-up, bone and soft tissue levels were well maintained in both groups. The findings support the technique's applicability to complex dual-defect posterior sites.

## Key Contributions
- Extends reactive tissue closure concept to dual-defect (bone + soft tissue) posterior sockets (Group B)
- KM gain (+0.67 mm) in Group B: the pre-existing soft tissue deficiency drives net KM augmentation via reactive tissue closure
- Buccal bone dimensional changes comparable between the two socket types at 6 months
- 2-year maintenance of hard and soft tissue levels in both groups
- Validates reactive tissue closure across a broader clinical spectrum than Liu 2019

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort. n=32 posterior immediate implants. Group A (n=17): intact buccal soft tissue + buccal bone dehiscence. Group B (n=15): both buccal bone dehiscence + mucogingival recession. Both groups: reactive socket soft tissue used as primary wound closure. CBCT at 6 months for buccal bone. Clinical assessment at 2 years for KM and bone/soft tissue levels.

## Results
- Buccal bone at 6 months: comparable between Group A and B
- KM at 6 months: Group A −0.56 mm, Group B +0.67 mm (p=0.009)
- 2-year bone and soft tissue levels: well maintained in both groups
- No reported implant failures
