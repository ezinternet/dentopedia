## Three-line Summary

Moy et al. (2016) systematically reviewed 36 studies (1985–2014) evaluating single implants in posterior quadrants (maxilla and mandible). Immediate placement yielded 96.9% survival and 0.85 mm bone loss; delayed placement yielded 96.8% survival and 0.55 mm bone loss. No significant difference in survival rates was observed between placement timing protocols; success rates were slightly higher for delayed loading.

## 세줄요약

Moy 등(2016)은 구치부 단일 임플란트를 다룬 36편(1985–2014)을 체계적으로 고찰했다. 즉시 식립 생존율 96.9%·골소실 0.85 mm, 지연 식립 생존율 96.8%·골소실 0.55 mm였다. 생존율은 두 군 간 유의한 차이가 없었으며, 성공률은 지연 로딩 프로토콜에서 소폭 높았다.

## Methods

- **Search**: MEDLINE/PubMed, Ovid MEDLINE, Cochrane CENTRAL; publications 1985–2014
- **Screening**: 141 articles reviewed; 36 met inclusion criteria
- **Scope**: Single missing teeth in posterior quadrants (maxilla and/or mandible); molar and premolar regions
- **Outcomes**: Survival rate, success rate, mean marginal bone loss

## Results

### Standalone group outcomes

| Group | Survival Rate | Success Rate | Mean Bone Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Immediate placement | 96.9% | 100% | 0.85 mm |
| Delayed placement | 96.8% | 94.1% | 0.55 mm |

### Head-to-head comparison (immediate vs delayed)

| Outcome | Immediate | Delayed |
|---|---|---|
| Survival rate | 96.8% | 96.3% |
| Success rate | 85.8% | 93.3% |
| Mean bone loss | 0.57 ± 0.57 mm | 0.55 ± 0.37 mm |

No statistically significant difference in survival rates between immediately and delayed placed implants in posterior regions.

## Clinical Takeaway

Single implants in posterior quadrants (molar and premolar regions) are a viable and predictable treatment option. Clinicians may choose either immediate or delayed placement without a meaningful survival penalty. When success rate (a stricter criterion including absence of complications) is prioritized, delayed loading protocols show a modest advantage. The slightly higher bone loss with immediate placement (0.85 mm vs 0.55 mm standalone; comparable in head-to-head) warrants consideration in bone-sensitive cases.
