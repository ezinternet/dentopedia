## Three-line Summary

Retrospective comparative study (n=40; 20 root-analogue implant [RAI] restorations vs 20 forced orthodontic extrusion [FOE]-preserved natural-root restorations, split premolar/anterior, Berlin) scored with the Functional Implant Prosthodontic Score (FIPS) at mean follow-up 18.4 months (RAI) vs 43.9 months (FOE).

Both groups had 100% survival, but FOE scored significantly better than RAI on marginal bone loss (p<0.01), interproximal papillae/contacts (p<0.05, one rater), and mucosa quality (p<0.02, one rater); composite FIPS was higher for FOE (~9.0/10) than RAI (~7.5/10) per the body text.

Clinically, FOE (root preservation via forced extrusion) may be more bone/soft-tissue-preserving than immediate RAI placement and could serve as a socket-preservation bridge to eventual conventional implant placement — but small sample, unequal follow-up length between groups, and single-operator bias limit strength of conclusion.

## Summary

This retrospective, single-center study (Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin) is the first to directly compare two divergent strategies for restoring a deeply destroyed, otherwise-extractable tooth: (1) extraction followed by immediate placement of a custom root-analogue implant (RAI, REPLICATE™ System — milled titanium/zirconia root with a zirconia abutment), versus (2) tooth preservation via forced orthodontic extrusion (FOE) to re-establish biologic width and a ferrule before conventional crown restoration. Forty cases (20 per treatment, split evenly between premolars and anterior teeth) were scored with the Functional Implant Prosthodontic Score (FIPS — interproximal, occlusion, design, mucosa, bone; 0–10) by two independent raters, with Krippendorff's alpha used to check inter-rater reliability and Mann–Whitney U tests to compare groups.

Both groups achieved 100% survival over their observation periods (RAI 18.4±5.7 months; FOE 43.9±16.4 months — note the substantially longer FOE follow-up). FOE restorations scored significantly better than RAI restorations on marginal bone loss (p<0.01, consistent across both raters), and — for one of the two raters only — on interproximal papillae/contacts (p<0.05) and mucosa quality/quantity (p<0.02). Occlusion scored perfectly (2/2) in every case and could not be statistically compared. The paper's own working hypothesis (that both concepts would perform comparably) was explicitly rejected regarding marginal bone.

**A data-quality note for readers**: the published abstract and the Results/Discussion body text assign the composite FIPS means (9.2/8.8±1.1/1.2 vs 7.4/7.7±1.3/1.5) to *opposite* groups. This wiki page follows the body-text attribution (FOE = 9.2/8.8, the higher/better score; RAI = 7.4/7.7) because it is the only version internally consistent with the paper's own narrative that FOE significantly outperforms RAI on bone, interproximal, and mucosa sub-scores.

## Key Contributions

- First direct head-to-head comparison of RAI-supported restorations vs. FOE-preserved natural-root restorations — two fundamentally different answers (implant-replace vs. extrude-and-restore) to the same clinical problem.
- Adapts FIPS (originally an implant-restoration score) to a non-implant natural-tooth restoration by substituting a segmental-radiographic bone-loss method for FOE cases.
- Granular FOE protocol data: mean extrusion 3.50±0.87 mm over 17.88±10.98 days, retention ≥8 weeks (mean 130.60±89.12 days), fiber-post extrusion anchor.
- Granular RAI fabrication data for the REPLICATE™ System: CBCT/STL-superimposed CAD/CAM milling, Y-TZP all-ceramic (n=4) or titanium-root/Y-TZP-abutment hybrid (n=16) construction, PRGF-wetted placement, Temporary Protective Cover load protection.
- Explicitly names orthodontic relapse (15% of FOE teeth) and ceramic chipping (1 case per group) as the observed complication profile.

## Methodology

- Retrospective, single-center, n=40 (20 RAI + 20 FOE), each split 10 premolar/10 anterior.
- RAI inclusion: non-smoker, no bone-metabolism-affecting medication, non-inflamed tissues, intact bone compartment.
- FOE inclusion: probing depth ≤2 mm, biologic-width violation or missing ferrule, crown-to-root ratio ≤1, mobility ≤1, single-tooth restoration planned; molars, suspected ankylosis, and vertical root fractures excluded.
- Outcome: FIPS score (5 domains × 0–2, max 10), scored independently by two raters from photos, floss-checked contacts, shimstock-checked occlusion, and superimposed pre-/post radiographs; Krippendorff's alpha for reliability, Mann–Whitney U for between-group comparison (α=0.05). Post-hoc power 97% (G*Power, n=20/group, effect size 1.17).

## Results

- Survival 100% in both groups.
- FOE significantly better than RAI on bone loss (p<0.01, both raters), interproximal (p<0.05, 1 rater), mucosa (p<0.02, 1 rater); occlusion tied (2/2 all cases).
- Composite FIPS (body-text attribution): FOE ≈9.2/8.8±1.1/1.2 vs RAI ≈7.4/7.7±1.3/1.5.
- Complications: 1 ceramic chip per group; orthodontic relapse in 3/20 FOE teeth (15%).
- Follow-up asymmetry (RAI 18.4 vs FOE 43.9 months) is flagged by the authors as a limitation that could bias the bone-loss comparison against RAI (shorter observation could under- or over-estimate true long-term RAI bone remodeling relative to the longer-observed FOE group).
