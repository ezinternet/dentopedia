## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of 25 studies (clinical, in vitro, FEA, and narrative reviews; Jan 2007–Aug 2025) evaluating wide-thread implant macrogeometry effects on primary stability, marginal bone loss, and survival specifically under immediate implant placement.

Wider/more aggressive threads and tapered bodies consistently improved insertion torque and ISQ in low-density (D3-D4) bone and post-extraction sockets; clinical survival for immediately placed wide-thread/tapered implants ranged ~93.9–98.6%, comparable to delayed placement, with MBL generally within acceptable limits when adequate stability and loading protocol were achieved.

Wide-thread macrogeometry is a biomechanically sound strategy to enhance primary stability under immediate placement, but small sample sizes, short follow-up, and non-standardized reporting across the 25 studies (no meta-analysis performed) mean multicenter RCTs with long-term follow-up are still needed.

넓거나 공격적인 나사산과 테이퍼형 바디는 저밀도 골(D3-D4)·발치 즉시식립 소켓에서 식립 토크와 ISQ를 일관되게 향상시켰고, 즉시식립 wide-thread/테이퍼 임플란트의 임상 생존율은 ~93.9–98.6%로 지연식립과 대등했으며 적절한 안정성·로딩 프로토콜 하에서 MBL도 허용 범위 내였다.

## Summary

This systematic review synthesizes 25 studies (clinical, in vitro, finite element analysis, and narrative reviews; searched Jan 2007–Aug 2025) examining whether wide-thread implant macrogeometry improves outcomes specifically under immediate implant placement (IIP) protocols. The review integrates three evidence tiers into one narrative synthesis rather than a pooled meta-analysis, given heterogeneous outcome reporting. Wider and more aggressive thread designs, along with tapered implant bodies, consistently improved insertion torque and implant stability quotient (ISQ), particularly in low-density (D3-D4) bone and post-extraction sockets — the exact biomechanical scenario immediate placement creates. Clinical survival rates for immediately placed wide-thread/tapered implants ranged from roughly 93.9% (long-term, 5–14y follow-up) to 98.6% (large-scale retrospective comparator including delayed implants), broadly comparable to delayed placement. Marginal bone loss remained within clinically acceptable limits when adequate primary stability and appropriate loading protocols were achieved. The author concludes wide-thread macrogeometry is a biomechanically sound strategy for improving predictability of immediate protocols but calls for multicenter RCTs with long-term follow-up before drawing definitive clinical conclusions.

## Key Contributions

- First review to specifically isolate wide-thread macrogeometry (as opposed to implant design broadly) as the variable of interest under immediate placement protocols.
- Cross-tier synthesis (clinical + in vitro/FEA + narrative review) into a single structured comparison table of insertion torque and ISQ by thread design.
- Contextualizes wide-thread IIP survival against a large-scale (50,333-implant) immediate-vs-delayed comparator and a dedicated IIP-vs-delayed SR+MA.

## Methodology

- **Design**: systematic review (no meta-analysis), single reviewer, no prospective protocol registration (acknowledged limitation).
- **Search**: PubMed (MEDLINE) as primary database, Jan 2007–Aug 2025, supplemented by manual reference-list screening and forward/backward citation tracking.
- **Inclusion**: clinical studies, in vitro experiments, FEA studies, and reviews assessing implant macrogeometry (especially wide-thread configurations) on insertion torque, ISQ, MBL, or survival in immediate placement.
- **Exclusion**: studies without biomechanical relevance, non-English, case reports, studies lacking quantitative stability/bone data.
- **Yield**: 36 records identified → 25 included.

## Results

- **Insertion torque**: wide-thread SL implants 48.42Ncm vs standard-thread SY implants 43.42Ncm (n=38 implants, 12mo, not statistically significant); tapered vs cylindrical in mandible 46.67±6.85Ncm vs 35.77±6.72Ncm (p=0.01).
- **ISQ**: deeper-thread implants 70.13±1.12 vs shallower-thread 65.58±1.37 (in vitro); tapered vs cylindrical mandible 71.67±5.16 vs 57.15±4.83 (p=0.01), maxilla 67.2±4.42 vs 49.17±15.30 (p=0.01).
- **Survival (wide-thread/tapered IIP cohorts)**: 96% (3–5y, n=25 implants); 93.88% cumulative (mean follow-up 7.43y, n=49 implants); one small deep-thread cohort showed 73.3% at 1 year driven by early failures, an outlier flagged by the reviewer.
- **Survival (IIP vs delayed, large comparators)**: 50,333-implant retrospective — delayed 98.6% vs immediate 98.4%, not significant; 10-study SR+MA — immediate 97.4% (332/341) vs delayed 97.5% (350/359), not significant.
- **MBL**: platform-switching implants showed bone gain (0.19±0.53mm) vs platform-matching bone loss (0.04±0.58mm) at 60 months (MD=0.23mm, p=0.025); a tapered self-tapping IIP cohort showed mesial bone gain from 3 months onward, stable to 5 years.
- Insertion torque ≥30Ncm alone did not guarantee survival, but success rates exceeded 95% when this threshold was achieved (per one included SR).
