## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional survey by the Chongqing Stomatological Association (February–March 2021) of 3,020 Chinese dental medical staff (1,855 dentists, 1,165 dental nurses) across 180 institutions in 11 provinces, using the Chinese GHQ-12 (psychological distress), the Chinese Maslach Burnout Inventory (CMBI), and job-stress/satisfaction and career-choice-regret measures.

Dentists reported higher psychological distress than dental nurses (25.7% vs. 20.8%, p=0.003) and higher burnout prevalence (28.9% vs. 23.6%); high job stress was the single strongest independent risk factor for both roles (dentists OR 3.017; nurses OR 3.37), but dental nurses carried a broader risk-factor profile — age ≥35 (OR 2.069), working >55 h/week (OR 2.433), low job satisfaction (OR 1.742), and poor medical environment (OR 2.2) — none of which were significant for dentists.

Cross-sectional, self-report design precludes causal inference and the sample is limited to 11 of China's provinces; the authors conclude that dentist- and nurse-specific (not uniform) intervention strategies are needed.

## Summary

This cross-sectional survey of 3,020 Chinese dental medical staff (1,855 dentists, 1,165 dental nurses) across 11 provinces is one of the few studies to directly compare psychological distress and burnout between dentists and dental nurses within the same sample and instrument set. Using the Chinese GHQ-12 and the Chinese Maslach Burnout Inventory (CMBI) alongside job-stress/satisfaction and career-choice-regret measures, it found dentists had higher prevalence of both psychological distress (25.7% vs. 20.8%) and burnout (28.9% vs. 23.6%) than dental nurses. High job stress was the dominant independent risk factor for psychological distress in both roles, but dental nurses showed a distinctly broader risk-factor profile — age, long working hours, low job satisfaction, and poor medical environment all mattered for nurses but not for dentists. The authors attribute dentists' higher distress partly to greater education, longer hours, teaching load, and seniority/income relative to nurses, while nurses' added risk factors are linked to a subordinate collaborative relationship with dentists and, for older nurses, mismatched career-development expectations. The cross-sectional, self-report design cannot establish causality, and the sample is limited to 11 of China's provinces.

## Key Contributions

- One of few studies to directly compare psychological distress between dentists and dental nurses within one sample/instrument set, rather than studying either role in isolation.
- Large sample (n=3,020) spanning 11 Chinese provinces and 180 institutions — far larger than the entire DCP evidence base [[professional-wellbeing/kaki-2022-health-wellbeing-clinical-dental-professionals]] found for the UK (12 studies total).
- Fits separate multivariable risk-factor models by role, showing dental nurses carry a broader/more distinct risk-factor profile (age, long hours, job satisfaction, medical environment) than dentists, despite dentists having the higher overall prevalence of both distress and burnout.
- Models burnout as a risk factor for psychological distress in each role (dentists OR 2.171; nurses OR 1.772), rather than reporting burnout prevalence only as an isolated endpoint.

## Methodology

Cross-sectional, self-administered questionnaire survey conducted by the Chongqing Stomatological Association, February–March 2021, across 180 institutions in 11 Chinese provinces (n=3,020 valid responses: 1,855 dentists, 1,165 dental nurses). The 5-part questionnaire comprised: demographics; the Chinese GHQ-12 (psychological distress, cutoff ≥4); the Chinese Maslach Burnout Inventory (CMBI, 22 items across three domains); the Consultants' Mental Health Questionnaire (job stress/satisfaction); and career-choice-regret items. Descriptive prevalence and multivariable (odds-ratio) analyses were run separately by role. The exact composite cutoff defining "burnout" prevalence in the CMBI is not detailed in the retained source text and would need the full PMC article to confirm.

## Related Papers

- [[professional-wellbeing/kaki-2022-health-wellbeing-clinical-dental-professionals]] — Kaki's UK SR of 12 DCP studies explicitly asked "what's the burnout prevalence in hygienists vs. dentists?" as an open question, since no such direct comparison existed. This paper extends that evidence base with actual dentist-vs-role comparative numbers, though the compared role (Chinese hospital dental nurses/chairside assistants) and health system differ from Kaki's UK hygienist/therapist population — a partial, not exact, answer to the gap.
- [[professional-wellbeing/moro-2022-burnout-syndrome-among-dentists-systematic]] — pools dentist-only burnout at ~13% overall / 28% emotional-exhaustion-alone (mostly Europe/Americas, SR+MA). This paper's dentist burnout prevalence (28.9%, CMBI) sits numerically closer to the 28% EE-alone figure, but the CMBI single-prevalence definition differs from Moro's three-subscale Maslach breakdown and China was not represented in the pooled SR — read as descriptively adjacent, not a reinforcement of Moro's estimate.
- [[professional-wellbeing/long-2023-prevalence-professional-burnout-dentists-systematic]] — same caveat: Long's pooled ~13% dentist-only burnout (15 studies, n=6,038, Europe-heavy) uses a different instrument/composite than this paper's CMBI prevalence, so the two numbers should not be treated as directly comparable.
- [[professional-wellbeing/negucioiu-2024-prevalence-management-burnout-dental-covid]] — this paper's data collection (Feb–Mar 2021) falls within the pandemic window Negucioiu's SR covers; consistent with Negucioiu's finding that pandemic-era prevalence did not depart sharply from pre-pandemic baselines, though this paper does not itself analyze a pre/during/post-COVID contrast.

## Open Questions

- Whether the CMBI's "burnout" composite (28.9%/23.6%) is defined comparably to the Maslach three-subscale approach used in Moro 2022/Long 2023 is unresolved from the retained text — would need the full PMC article's Methods to confirm.
- Whether Chinese "dental nurses" map cleanly onto UK-style hygienists/therapists (Kaki 2022's DCP population) or are closer to a chairside-assistant role is unresolved — the roles' scope-of-practice differences could materially affect any burnout comparison.
- No OR was reported for "lower income" in the available text despite it being named a shared risk factor — a gap for future re-extraction from the primary source if the magnitude becomes clinically relevant.
