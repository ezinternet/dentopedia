## Three-line Summary

Narrative review covering burnout definition, six etiological theories, organizational/individual antecedents, consequences, prevention strategies, and 10+ validated measurement instruments across all occupational groups.
Maslach's 3-dimension model (EE → DP → reduced PA) remains dominant; WHO/ICD-11 (2019) classifies burnout as occupational phenomenon; prevalence estimates legitimately range 3.2%–91.4% depending on instrument and cutoff applied.
No clinically validated diagnostic threshold exists for any instrument; burnout is primarily organizational, but most interventions incorrectly target individuals.

## Summary

This 2022 UNED (Spain) narrative review provides the most comprehensive publicly available synthesis of burnout theory and measurement. It traces the construct from Freudenberger's (1974) clinical observations through Maslach & Jackson's (1981/1986) operationalization of the three-dimension model — emotional exhaustion (EE), depersonalization/cynicism (DP), reduced professional efficacy (PA) — now recognized by WHO in ICD-11 as an occupational phenomenon. Six complementary etiological theories are reviewed (social cognitive, social exchange, organizational, JD-R, structural, emotional contagion), alongside organizational antecedents (work overload, emotional labor, role conflict, inadequate supervision) and individual moderating factors (neuroticism, locus of control, coping style). Crucially, the paper explains why burnout prevalence estimates range from 3.2% to 91.4%: instruments differ in dimensions, scoring, and — critically — none has a clinically validated diagnostic cutoff. Most interventions wrongly target individuals despite burnout being primarily organizational in origin.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes construct history: Freudenberger (1974) → Maslach (1976) → 3-dimension model (1981/1986) → ICD-11 occupational phenomenon (2019)
- Explains instrument-driven prevalence heterogeneity: no validated cutoff → estimates are researcher choices, not clinical thresholds
- Reviews 10+ instruments with dimensional structure, cultural validation status, and occupation-specific adaptations
- Synthesizes 6 etiological theories as complementary, not competing
- Documents the primary/secondary/tertiary prevention framework and the individual-vs-organizational intervention mismatch

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative review + Special Issue editorial
- **Scope**: All occupational groups (not healthcare/dentistry-specific)
- **Note**: Also serves as introduction to IJERPH Special Issue summarizing 21 contributing papers

## Results

### Burnout Definition (Maslach & Jackson 1986)

| Dimension | Korean | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Emotional Exhaustion (EE) | 정서적 소진 | Depletion of emotional resources; the core/first dimension |
| Depersonalization/Cynicism (DP) | 비인격화/냉소 | Distancing from work/clients as EE coping mechanism |
| Reduced Professional Efficacy (PA) | 직업효능 저하 | Feelings of incompetence; may be antecedent OR consequence |

WHO/ICD-11 (2019): burnout is an occupational phenomenon — NOT a disease or mental disorder.

### Prevalence Heterogeneity

US surgery residents: **3.2%–91.4%** across definitions. Source: different instruments, different dimensions, no validated cutoff in any instrument.

### Burnout Subtypes (Montero-Marín)

| Subtype | 한국어 | Core feature |
|---|---|---|
| Frenetic | 과열형 | Work overload; high dedication; personal sacrifice |
| Under-challenged | 저자극형 | Monotony; boredom; cynicism; escapist coping |
| Worn-out | 탈진형 | Hopelessness; lack of control/recognition; passive coping |

### Key Measurement Instruments

| Instrument | Items | Dimensions | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| MBI-HSS | 22 | EE, DP, PA | Human services (original) |
| MBI-GS | 16 | Exhaustion, Cynicism, Efficacy | All occupations (most used) |
| CESQT/SBI | 20 | Enthusiasm, Psychic burnout, Indolence, Guilt | Spanish Burnout Inventory |
| CBI | – | Personal, Work-related, Client-related | Context-free |
| BAT | 33 | 4 core + 2 secondary | Most comprehensive |
| BCSQ-12 | 12 | Overload, Lack of development, Neglect | Subtype-based |

**Critical gap**: None of the above have clinically validated diagnostic cutoffs → prevalence figures across studies cannot be directly compared.

### Antecedents (Summary)

**Organizational triggers**: work overload, emotional labor (suppress/display emotions), lack of autonomy, role ambiguity/conflict, inadequate supervision, lack of social support, poor working hours  
**Individual moderators (risk)**: neuroticism ↑, external locus of control, Type A behavior, high expectations  
**Individual moderators (protective)**: extraversion ↑, agreeableness ↑, conscientiousness ↑, active problem-focused coping
