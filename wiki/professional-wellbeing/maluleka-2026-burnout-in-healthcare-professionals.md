## Three-line Summary

Narrative review for practising healthcare professionals covering WHO/ICD-11 burnout definition, global epidemiology (~50% prevalence), contributing factors, MBI-HSS clinical assessment cutoffs, and primary/secondary/tertiary prevention plus the ABC approach.
Nearly half of healthcare providers globally experience burnout; MBI-HSS high-burnout thresholds are EE ≥27 / DP ≥10 / PA ≤33; outpatient providers show more EE than inpatient; no definitive gender difference in large systematic reviews.
Burnout is reversible when recognized early; primary (organizational) prevention is underused; burnout and depression overlap but are distinct — burnout is work-specific.

## Summary

This 2026 South African Family Practice review provides a clinician-facing synthesis of burnout in healthcare professionals. It explicitly anchors the WHO/ICD-11 definition — "syndrome resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed" (not a medical condition) — and confirms the 3-dimension construct (EE, DP, reduced PA) in a clinical context. Global evidence indicates nearly half of practising clinicians experience burnout; medical students exceed 50%. The review states MBI-HSS high-burnout clinical cutoffs (EE ≥27, DP ≥10, PA ≤33) — values rarely specified in dental burnout literature. It carefully distinguishes burnout from depression (burnout is work-specific; depression is not), presents a primary/secondary/tertiary prevention framework, and introduces the ABC approach (Applying self-awareness, Balancing domains, Cultivating habits). South African-specific contributors — fiscal austerity, workplace violence, xenophobia — illustrate how structural/systemic factors amplify burnout beyond individual coping.

## Key Contributions

- Explicitly states WHO/ICD-11 classification: occupational phenomenon, NOT a medical condition
- Provides MBI-HSS high-burnout clinical cutoffs (EE ≥27 / DP ≥10 / PA ≤33) — clarifies interpretation of subscale data in Jin-2014 and similar studies
- Draws a principled distinction between burnout and depression for screening/treatment purposes
- Introduces ABC prevention framework as complement to primary/secondary/tertiary model
- Documents South African (LMIC) systemic burnout contributors absent from Western literature

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative review
- **Scope**: Healthcare professionals globally; South African context
- **Not systematic** — no registered protocol or PRISMA

## Results

### ICD-11 Definition

WHO ICD-11: burnout = **"syndrome conceptualised as resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed."** NOT a medical condition.

| Dimension | Korean | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Emotional Exhaustion (EE) | 정서적 소진 | Feelings of energy depletion |
| Depersonalisation (DP) | 비인격화 | Mental distance, negativity, cynicism toward work |
| Reduced Professional Accomplishment (PA) | 직업효능 저하 | Diminished sense of personal achievement |

### Global Prevalence

| Population | Burnout prevalence |
|---|---|
| Healthcare providers (global) | ~50% (nearly half) |
| Medical students | >50% during academic journey |
| Highest-risk specialties | Urology, EM, anaesthesiology, surgical subspecialties, radiology, internal medicine |
| Gender | No definitive difference in large SRs |
| Outpatient vs inpatient | More EE in outpatient; DP and reduced PA similar |

### MBI-HSS High-Burnout Cutoffs

| Subscale | Items | Range | High-burnout threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| EE | 9 | 0–54 | **≥ 27** |
| DP | 5 | 0–30 | **≥ 10** |
| PA | 8 | 0–48 | **≤ 33** |

*Implication*: Jin-2014 (this wiki) reports EE 41.2% using these thresholds — which counts high-EE subscale scores, not full three-dimension burnout syndrome.

### Burnout vs. Depression

Overlap: fatigue, low energy, negativity, functional impairment.  
Key distinction: **burnout is work-specific** (tied to occupational factors); depression can exist without workplace stress.  
Burnout may predispose to depression; the two can coexist in high-stress environments.  
→ Screening and treatment must address both without conflating them.

### Prevention

| Level | Aim | Healthcare examples |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Prevent onset | Flexible rosters, EHR optimisation, peer recognition programmes |
| Secondary | Detect/intervene early | Quarterly MBI surveys, rapid referral to employee assistance |
| Tertiary | Treat/rehabilitate | Occupational health-led reintegration, therapy groups |

**ABC Approach**:
- **A (Applying)**: self-awareness — recognise personal limits, stress signals; stay empathetic
- **B (Balancing)**: equilibrium across family, social, spiritual, leisure
- **C (Cultivating)**: healthy routines, consistency, lifestyle choices for long-term performance

### Individual Strategies

Mindfulness, meditation, exercise, gratitude journaling, self-care, stress-management programmes  
Social connection with peers/family; reconnect with professional purpose; mentorship  
Learn to set limits; regular short breaks > infrequent extended leave; periodic digital disconnection

### Organizational Strategies

Value alignment, fair compensation, EHR streamlining, delegating non-clinical tasks, flexible scheduling (part-time, remote)

### Prognosis

**Favorable** when recognized early + personal + systemic management applied.  
If left unaddressed → depression, substance misuse, suicide.
