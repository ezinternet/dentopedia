## One-line Summary

In-vitro study (10 instrument groups × n=30, Macor substrate, electric handpiece vs air-turbine) showing that with the electric handpiece, a carbide bur outperformed all tested diamond burs in cutting efficiency, while overall the electric handpiece yielded higher advancement and lower pulp-chamber temperature than the air-turbine.

## Summary

Ercoli et al. (2009) is Part II of a series benchmarking ten rotary cutting instrument groups (nine diamond, one carbide) across two handpiece types. Part I (not held) used an air-turbine; Part II used the KaVo Intramatic Lux K200 electric handpiece on Macor glass-ceramic blocks under standardised conditions (n=30 per group, 300 total cuts, 4 cuts per block). Two-way ANOVA confirmed a statistically significant handpiece × instrument interaction, meaning the optimal bur choice differs by handpiece. The key reversals: with the electric handpiece, the tested carbide bur (Great White Ultra) showed the **highest rate of advancement (0.17 mm/s)** and **lowest applied load (108.35 g)** of all ten groups — outperforming every diamond bur tested. Combined across all instruments, the electric handpiece produced **higher cutting efficiency and lower pulp-chamber temperature** than the air-turbine (26.68°C vs 28.37°C; 0.12 vs 0.11 mm/s). The authors attribute electric handpiece superiority to its greater and more constant torque. All burs showed temperature reduction in the simulated pulp chamber compared to baseline when used with the electric handpiece.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes a **statistically significant handpiece × bur interaction**: the bur that performs best depends on which handpiece is used — a finding that undermines any single bur-type recommendation made without specifying handpiece type.
- With electric handpiece: **carbide bur outperforms all nine diamond burs** on cutting efficiency (highest advancement, lowest load on Macor substrate) — a direct counter-example to the general "diamond always wins" assumption.
- **Electric handpiece is superior to air-turbine overall**: higher cutting efficiency, lower pulp-chamber temperature, higher load tolerance — across all instrument groups combined.
- SEM documentation at three timepoints (pre-use, post-use uncleaned, post-ultrasonic clean) provides morphological basis for wear assessment and sterilization workflow.
- Provides the only held bench data quantifying electric vs air-turbine cutting performance head-to-head.

## Methodology

- **Design**: In-vitro bench study (Part II of series; Part I = air-turbine, not held)
- **Substrate**: Macor blocks (machinable glass-ceramic tooth analog); 75 blocks × 4 cuts = 300 cuts total
- **Handpiece**: Electric (KaVo Intramatic Lux K200); protocol identical to Part I for comparability
- **Instruments (10 groups, n=30 each)**:
  - 9 diamond: 7 multi-use, 2 disposable (brands not extractable from abstract)
  - 1 carbide: Great White Ultra (SS White Dental)
- **Outcomes**: Rate of advancement (mm/s), applied load (g), pulp-chamber temperature (°C vs baseline)
- **Morphology**: SEM × 3 per group
- **Statistics**: 2-way ANOVA (group × handpiece); 1-way ANOVA + Tukey's HSD (α=.05)

## Results

| Metric | Electric (all groups) | Air-turbine (all groups) |
|---|---|---|
| Mean temperature (°C) | 26.68 | 28.37 |
| Rate of advancement (mm/s) | 0.12 | 0.11 |
| Applied load (g) | 124.53 | 121.7 |

- **Carbide (Great White Ultra) with electric**: advancement 0.17 mm/s (highest), load 108.35 g (lowest) — most efficient of all 10 groups
- **Pulp chamber temperature**: all burs reduced temperature vs baseline with electric handpiece
- **Interaction**: handpiece × instrument group interaction statistically significant (2-way ANOVA)
- *Note*: Per-diamond-group breakdown, exact p-values, and effect sizes not available from abstract

## Clinical Implications

> **The bur–handpiece pairing determines outcome — not bur type alone.**

1. **Electric handpiece + carbide bur**: highest cutting efficiency on Macor (glass-ceramic analog) — consider carbide for dentin/enamel preparations with electric micromotor
2. **Electric handpiece overall**: cooler (lower pulp-chamber temperature) and more efficient than air-turbine — supports preference for electric when thermal safety matters
3. **Zirconia exception** (Peters 2019): diamond always outperforms carbide on sintered zirconia regardless of handpiece — substrate overrides the handpiece interaction for this ultra-hard material
4. **Substrate matters**: Macor ≠ natural tooth; clinical extrapolation requires caution (enamel is harder, dentin is viscoelastic)
