## Three-line Summary

Controlled bench (dental mannikin) study at the University of Glasgow Dental Clinical Research Facility, real-time particulate sensors (2× Plantower PMS5003, 0.3–10 μm), comparing 6 intra-oral suction devices (dynamic vs static) against no suction during ultrasonic scaling and high-speed handpiece use, n=5 replicates per device.

Without suction, the high-speed handpiece generated 3.4× more particles/min than the ultrasonic scaler (single-sensor setup); every suction device significantly reduced particle counts vs no suction (p<0.001) for both procedures, but only dynamic (instrument-following) high-volume devices — standard HVE, Purevac® (±lightweight hose) — brought counts to near/below background, while static/hands-free devices (DryShield®, ReLeaf™) and standard low-volume suction showed periodic particle "spikes."

Suction-device *type* (dynamic vs static), not just presence/absence of suction, is the operationally controllable determinant of aerosol escape; the study is manikin-based (no saliva/breathing/coughing) with a single operator for the ultrasonic arm, so inter-operator and patient-factor variability remain untested.

## 세줄요약

University of Glasgow Dental Clinical Research Facility의 마네킹 벤치 연구 — 실시간 파티클센서(Plantower PMS5003 2대, 0.3–10 μm)로 초음파 스케일링과 하이스피드 핸드피스 사용 시 구강내 흡인기구 6종(능동추종형 vs 고정형)과 무흡인을 비교(기구당 n=5 반복).

흡인이 없을 때 하이스피드 핸드피스가 초음파 스케일러보다 분당 입자수 3.4배 많았고(단일센서), 모든 흡인기구가 무흡인 대비 유의하게 입자수를 감소시켰으나(p<0.001) 표준 고용량흡인기(HVE)·Purevac® 같은 **능동추종형(dynamic)** 고용량 흡인만 배경수준 근처까지 저감시켰고, DryShield®·ReLeaf™ 같은 **고정형(static/hands-free)** 및 표준 저용량흡인(LVE)은 간헐적 입자 "스파이크"를 보였다.

흡인 유무보다 흡인기구의 **유형**(능동추종 vs 고정형)이 실질적으로 통제 가능한 에어로졸 노출 결정요인이라는 결론 — 마네킹 기반 연구로 타액·호흡·기침 등 환자 요인과 술자간 변이는 미검증(초음파 시술은 단일 술자로만 진행).

## Summary

This bench study used a dental mannikin head in a deliberately worst-case ventilation room (zero air changes/hour, no mechanical ventilation) to measure real-time aerosol escape — via two laser-scattering particulate sensors positioned near the oral cavity — during ultrasonic scaling and high-speed handpiece use, each paired against a panel of commonly used intra-oral suction devices. Without any suction, the high-speed handpiece generated 3.4 times more particles/min than the ultrasonic scaler. Every suction device tested significantly reduced particle counts relative to no suction for both procedures, but the study's central finding is that suction device *type* mattered more than simple presence: "dynamic" devices that are actively repositioned to follow the instrument (standard high-volume evacuator (HVE), Purevac® with or without a lightweight hose) reduced particle counts to near or below background levels, whereas "static" hands-free devices fixed in one position (DryShield®, Ivory® ReLeaf™) and standard low-volume suction left periodic particle "spikes," concentrated at the labial/incisal regions. The clinical implication is that when selecting intra-oral suction for aerosol mitigation during hygienist scaling or restorative handpiece work, an actively-positioned high-volume device outperforms a hands-free/static or low-volume alternative — though the manikin-based, single-operator design leaves patient-derived factors and inter-operator variability unexamined.

## Key Contributions

- First study (per the authors) to compare a broad panel of commonly used intra-oral suction devices in a real-time, continuous-monitoring format across two distinct AGPs (ultrasonic scaling and high-speed handpiece use) within the same protocol.
- Introduces an explicit **dynamic** (instrument-following: standard HVE, Purevac® ± lightweight hose) vs **static** (hands-free, fixed position: DryShield®, Ivory® ReLeaf™, standard LVE) suction-device taxonomy.
- Quantifies that high-speed handpiece use generates 3.4× more particles/min than ultrasonic scaling in the absence of suction (single-sensor setup).
- Shows all tested suction devices significantly reduce particle counts vs no suction, but only dynamic high-volume devices reduce counts to near/below background for *both* AGPs — static/low-volume devices leave periodic escape "spikes" despite a significant average reduction.

## Methodology

In-vitro/bench design: dental mannikin head on a dental unit, University of Glasgow Dental Clinical Research Facility, zero air changes/hour, no mechanical ventilation. Two Plantower PMS5003 particulate sensors (0.3–10 μm, laser scattering) in "single sensor" (10 cm above central incisors + funnel, half-mouth 4-min scale) and "dual sensor" (nose + chin, full-mouth 8-min scale) arrangements. Ultrasonic scaling via Cavitron Touch® system (0.89% saline, 18.0 mL/min, 100% power), solo operator, standard supragingival pattern. High-speed handpiece via Midwest Stylus® Plus with diamond bur (0.89% saline, 68.0 mL/min), operator+assistant, 3 depth cuts × 3 surfaces on the upper-right central incisor (90 s) mimicking an anterior crown prep. Suction devices tested: ultrasonic arm — standard HVE, Purevac® (+H)/(−H) [dynamic], DryShield®/ReLeaf™/standard LVE [static] (6 devices + no-suction control); handpiece arm — standard HVE, Purevac® (±H), DryShield® (3 devices + control). Outcome = "normalised particle count" (background-subtracted AUC), n=5 replicates/device, one-way ANOVA + Tukey post-hoc (α=0.05).

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Baseline aerosol, no suction | High-speed handpiece generated 3.4× more particles/min than ultrasonic scaler (single-sensor setup); comparable in dual-sensor setup |
| Any suction vs no suction, ultrasonic scaling | Significant reduction with every device tested, both sensor setups (p<0.001) |
| Any suction vs no suction, high-speed handpiece | Significant reduction with every device tested, both sensor setups (p<0.001) |
| Dynamic devices (standard HVE, Purevac® ±H) | Reduced particle counts to near/below background for both ultrasonic scaling and handpiece use |
| Static/hands-free devices (DryShield®, ReLeaf™) and standard LVE | Remained consistently above background with periodic particle "spikes," concentrated at labial/incisal regions |
| DryShield® vs dynamic devices, high-speed handpiece arm | DryShield® allowed significantly more particle escape than all dynamic devices (p<0.001), both sensor setups |
| Dynamic devices vs standard LVE/ReLeaf™, ultrasonic scaling (dual-sensor) | All dynamic devices significantly outperformed standard LVE and ReLeaf™ |
