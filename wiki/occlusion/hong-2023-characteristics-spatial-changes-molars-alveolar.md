## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cross-sectional CBCT study (42 CBCT scans with missing mandibular first molars, mean 62.6 months; 42 matched controls; West China Hospital of Stomatology) measuring 3D spatial changes in teeth and alveolar bone after mandibular first molar loss.

Maxillary first molars extruded 1.37 ± 0.83 mm (mesial cusp) and 0.85 ± 0.70 mm (distal cusp); mandibular second molars tipped mesially (57.47° vs 82.75°, P<0.001) and lingually; buccal bone defect at CEJ averaged 3.15 mm; all changes worsened with duration of tooth loss (R = −0.334 to −0.726).

3D molar protraction simulation showed available mesialization distance < required at all cross-section levels, establishing buccal CEJ bone as the primary limiting factor and mandating bone augmentation + lingual root torque for molar protraction success.

상악 제1대구치 정출 (Supraeruption): 근심교두 1.37 ± 0.83 mm, 원심교두 0.85 ± 0.70 mm (근심>원심, P<0.01); 하악 제2대구치 근심 경사 (Mesial Tipping): 57.47° (대조군 82.75°, P<0.001), 설측 경사 (Lingual Tipping): 71.75° (P<0.05); 법랑-백악질 경계부 (CEJ) 협측 치조골 결손 3.15 ± 0.87 mm; 결손 기간 길수록 모든 변화 악화(R = −0.334 ~ −0.726).

## Summary

This retrospective cross-sectional CBCT study from West China Hospital of Stomatology quantified 3D spatial changes in teeth and alveolar bone among adult patients (18–40 years) with missing mandibular first molars (mean duration 62.6 ± 13.3 months). Compared to matched controls, mandibular second molars showed significant mesial tipping (57.47° vs 82.75°, P<0.001) and lingual tipping (71.75° vs 73.23°, P<0.05), while opposing maxillary first molars extruded asymmetrically — mesial cusp 1.37 ± 0.83 mm, distal cusp 0.85 ± 0.70 mm (P<0.01 between cusps). Vertical alveolar bone height decreased ~1.4 mm, and horizontal bone loss was greatest on the buccal side at the CEJ. All three secondary changes correlated with duration of tooth loss (R = −0.334 to −0.726). A 3D simulation of mandibular second molar protraction revealed that available mesialization distance was less than required at all cross-section levels, with the buccal bone defect at CEJ (3.15 ± 0.87 mm) as the primary constraint.

## Key Contributions

- First CBCT-based 3D quantification of asymmetric maxillary first molar supraeruption: mesial cusp (1.37 mm) > distal cusp (0.85 mm), both measured over ~5 years of tooth loss
- Demonstrates progressive worsening of all secondary changes with duration: mesio-distal tipping (R = −0.726), bucco-lingual tipping (R = −0.528), maxillary extrusion (R = −0.334)
- 3D simulation shows buccal bone defect at CEJ as the primary structural barrier to molar protraction — exceeds available bone width at all levels
- Provides clinical framework for pre-treatment CBCT assessment before molar protraction planning

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective cross-sectional CBCT morphometric study
- **n**: 42 missing mandibular first molar CBCT scans (36 patients, 28.7 ± 6.4 years) vs 42 controls (36 patients, 26.1 ± 5.8 years)
- **Inclusion**: Age 18–40; all remaining posterior teeth present; no orthodontic/orthognathic history; no generalized periodontitis
- **Duration**: 62.6 ± 13.3 months mean post-extraction
- **Reference**: Mandibular posterior tooth plane, Invivo Dental v5.1.6
- **Measurements**: Mesio-distal and bucco-lingual angulation of mandibular second molar; mesial/distal cusp extrusion of maxillary first molar; vertical alveolar bone height (buccal/middle/lingual); horizontal bone width (buccal/lingual at CEJ/mid-root/apex); 3D molar protraction simulation
- **Statistics**: Student t-test, ANOVA, Pearson correlation; α = 0.05
