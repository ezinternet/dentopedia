## Three-line Summary

Case-control study using travelling microscope measurements on study casts to compare vertical lower second molar positions between subjects who had upper second permanent molars extracted orthodontically and age- and sex-matched controls.
The distal aspect of the lower second permanent molar over-erupted significantly in the extraction group (P<0.001 right; P<0.05 left), while mesial over-eruption was moderated by the distal position of the upper first permanent molar (P<0.01).
Upper third molar eruption did not prevent lower second molar over-eruption, and most erupted third molars occupied unfavorable contact positions, challenging the assumption that third molar eruption compensates for extracted upper second molars.

## Summary

This case-control study investigated whether orthodontic extraction of upper second permanent molars causes over-eruption of the now-antagonist-free lower second permanent molars. Subjects previously treated with upper second molar extractions (experimental group) were compared to age- and sex-matched controls using travelling microscope measurements on study casts to objectively quantify vertical molar positions. Results showed statistically significant distal over-eruption of the lower second molar in the extraction group on both sides, with the mesial aspect modulated by the position of the remaining upper first permanent molar. Crucially, the eruption of upper third molars — often cited as a potential compensation for extracted second molars — neither prevented lower second molar over-eruption nor reliably restored favorable occlusal contact, with most third molars erupting into suboptimal positions relative to the first molar. These findings provide early quantitative evidence that upper second molar extraction for orthodontic purposes carries a clinically meaningful risk of antagonist-loss overeruption in the opposing arch.

## Key Contributions

- Quantifies over-eruption of the lower second permanent molar following orthodontic upper second molar extraction using a validated objective measurement method (travelling microscope on study casts)
- Demonstrates side-specific significance: right (P<0.001) stronger than left (P<0.05) — suggests possible clinical asymmetry or sample-size effect
- Identifies upper first permanent molar's distal position as a moderating variable for mesial (but not distal) lower second molar over-eruption
- Definitively shows upper third molar eruption does NOT compensate for upper second molar extraction in terms of preventing lower second molar overeruption
- Establishes a case-control template that later studies (Craddock 2004, Christou 2007) built upon with larger samples and more sophisticated measurement tools

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Study design | Case-control |
| Experimental group | Subjects with prior orthodontic upper second permanent molar extraction |
| Control group | Age- and sex-matched subjects without extraction |
| Measurement tool | Travelling microscope (validated: Bhatia & Harrison 1987) |
| Measurement substrate | Upper and lower study model casts (impressions taken at study) |
| Outcomes | Vertical position of lower 2nd molar (mesial and distal aspects); influence of upper 1st molar distal position; effect of upper 3rd molar eruption status |
| Statistics | Dahlberg 1940 methods; P-values for group differences |
| Sample size | Not determinable from abstract (full text behind paywall) |
| Follow-up | At least 3 years post-extraction for majority of subjects (based on 3rd molar timing data) |

## Results

### Primary Outcome: Lower Second Molar Vertical Position

| Site | Comparison | P-value |
|---|---|---|
| Distal aspect — right | Experimental > Control (over-erupted) | < 0.001 |
| Distal aspect — left | Experimental > Control (over-erupted) | < 0.05 |
| Mesial aspect | Moderated by upper 1st molar distal position | < 0.01 |

### Third Molar Eruption Findings

| Finding | Result |
|---|---|
| Upper 3rd molar effect on lower 2nd molar overeruption | No significant effect |
| 3rd molar eruption timing (majority) | ≥ 3 years after upper 2nd molar extraction |
| 3rd molar occlusal position (majority) | Unfavorable contact with upper first permanent molar |

### Interpretation

The distal aspect of the lower second molar over-erupts more consistently than the mesial aspect, consistent with the lever-arm effect of the molar: the distal cusp loses antagonist contact entirely, while the mesial cusp may retain partial contact with the distal of the upper first permanent molar (whose distal position moderates mesial over-eruption, P<0.01). Third molar eruption, frequently invoked as a justification for second molar extraction, failed to prevent over-eruption or establish favorable contacts.
