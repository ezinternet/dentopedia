## Three-line Summary

Systematic review of measurement properties (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251165166; PubMed, Cochrane Library, EBSCOhost + Google Scholar, 1 Jan 2000–30 Sep 2025; last search 20 Oct 2025), 40 included studies in adults ≥18 y, appraised with the COSMIN risk-of-bias checklist and Terwee criteria.

Mixing ability tests using colour-changeable or two-coloured chewing gum showed the most consistent evidence — reliability and validity rated sufficient (+) with levels of evidence from limited to strong (e.g. Kamiyama ICC 0.86–0.94, r = 0.979 vs colorimeter; Schimmel ViewGum ICC 0.95–0.97, r = −0.97 vs visual scale) — whereas comminution tests were heterogeneous (sieving/optical scanning unknown–strong, fuchsine beads reliability unknown, gummy jelly limited–strong) and measurement error stayed unknown-to-limited in every family because MIC/SDC were rarely reported.

No standardized, clinically validated masticatory performance test exists; the authors recommend gum-based mixing ability tests as the most clinically reliable current option and keep comminution tests for research until test food, particle analysis and error thresholds are standardized.

## 세줄요약

측정 특성 (Measurement Properties) 체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review, SR; PROSPERO CRD420251165166; PubMed·Cochrane Library·EBSCOhost + Google Scholar, 2000-01-01–2025-09-30; 최종 검색 2025-10-20), 성인(≥18세) 40편을 COSMIN 위험도 편향 (Risk of Bias, RoB) 도구와 Terwee 기준으로 평가.

색 변화 껌·이색 껌 기반 혼합 능력 (Mixing Ability) 검사가 가장 일관된 근거를 보였고(신뢰도·타당도 sufficient (+), 근거수준 limited–strong; 예: Kamiyama ICC 0.86–0.94, 색도계 대비 r = 0.979; Schimmel ViewGum ICC 0.95–0.97, 시각 척도 대비 r = −0.97), 분쇄 (Comminution) 검사는 이질적이었으며(체가름·광학 스캔 unknown–strong, 후크신 비드 신뢰도 unknown, 구미 젤리 limited–strong), 측정 오차 (Measurement Error)는 모든 군에서 최소 중요 변화 (Minimal Important Change, MIC)·최소 검출 가능 변화 (Smallest Detectable Change, SDC) 보고 부족으로 unknown–limited에 머물렀다.

표준화되어 임상적으로 검증된 저작 능력 (Masticatory Performance, MP) 검사는 존재하지 않는다; 저자들은 껌 기반 혼합 능력 검사를 현재 가장 신뢰할 만한 임상 옵션으로 권고하고, 분쇄 검사는 시험식품·입자 분석·오차 임계값 표준화 전까지 연구용으로 남긴다.

## Summary

This PROSPERO-registered systematic review asks a narrower question than the existing method inventories: not *which masticatory performance tests exist*, but *how well each one measures*. Forty studies (2000–2025) in adults ≥18 y were appraised in two layers — the COSMIN risk-of-bias checklist for methodological quality, restricted to the four domains that matter for performance-based instruments (reliability, validity, measurement error, responsiveness, "worst score counts"), then Terwee's +/?/− quality criteria and strong/moderate/limited/conflicting/unknown levels of evidence. The methods cluster into three families: mixing ability (colour-changeable gum 8 studies, two-coloured gum 12, two-coloured wax 4), comminution (sieving/optical scanning 9, fuchsine beads 2, gummy jelly 7) and "other" (odour sensor, Eichner index). Gum-based mixing ability tests come out best — sufficient reliability and validity with evidence reaching *strong* for colorimeter and visual-scale variants — while comminution tests keep strong theoretical validity but lose reproducibility to heterogeneity in test food and particle analysis. The single most consistent finding is a reporting failure rather than a performance failure: MIC and SDC are almost never stated, so measurement error is graded limited/unknown nearly everywhere and longitudinal or interventional change cannot be interpreted. The authors' conclusion is blunt: no standardized, clinically validated MP test exists.

## Key Contributions

- **Appraises measurement properties, not method popularity.** Previous reviews either described colour-changeable gum without a validated appraisal framework (Tarkowska et al.) or applied one before smartphone colour analysis and automated particle analysis entered the field (Elgestad Stjernfeldt et al.); this review re-runs the appraisal through September 2025 on contemporary digital instruments.
- **A three-family taxonomy with sub-families** — mixing ability (colour-changeable gum / two-coloured gum / two-coloured wax), comminution (sieving-optical scanning / fuchsine beads / gummy jelly), other (odour sensor, occlusal-support index) — that maps methods onto the construct they actually measure.
- **A criterion-validity map per instrument**: each method is tabulated against the reference it was validated against (colorimeter a* value, glucose extraction, β-carotene automation, sieving X₅₀, photographic surface area, visual 5-point scales), so "validated" stops being an unqualified claim.
- **Family-specific levels of evidence** instead of one global verdict — the review's answer to "which test should I use" is explicitly conditional on the family and, within a family, on the analysis variant (colorimeter vs visual scale, digital VoH vs subjective SA1–SA5).
- **Names measurement error as the field's systemic gap**: MIC/SDC reporting, not instrument accuracy, is what blocks clinical adoption and comparison of intervention effects.

## Methodology

- **Design / registration**: systematic review of measurement properties, PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO **CRD420251165166**; no meta-analysis.
- **Search**: MEDLINE via PubMed, Cochrane Library, EBSCOhost (Dentistry and Oral Sciences Source), Google Scholar for grey/forward searching, plus manual reference-list checking. Window 1 Jan 2000 – 30 Sep 2025; **last search 20 Oct 2025**.
- **Eligibility**: full-length English articles evaluating measurement properties of *objective* MP methods in adults ≥18 y, no age-subgroup restriction. Excluded: animal/in-vitro; MP measured only by interview or self-report questionnaire; craniofacial-anomaly, neuromuscular or other non-masticatory populations; MP used only as a clinical outcome without instrument appraisal; reviews, protocols, abstracts without data, editorials, letters, case reports/series ≤10 participants.
- **Selection**: two independent reviewers, title/abstract then full text; disagreements by discussion, then a third or fourth reviewer adjudicated. PRISMA 2020 flow diagram.
- **Appraisal layer 1 — COSMIN RoB**: domains restricted to reliability, validity, measurement error, responsiveness; 5–18 items per domain rated very good / adequate / doubtful / inadequate; **"worst score counts"** sets the domain score.
- **Appraisal layer 2 — Terwee criteria**: each property rated +/?/− from the reported statistic. Thresholds actually used: reliability + if ICC/weighted kappa ≥0.70 or Pearson r ≥0.80; measurement error + if MIC > SDC or MIC outside the limits of agreement; criterion validity + if the gold standard is convincing *and* r ≥0.70; construct validity + if r ≥0.50 with a same-construct instrument or ≥75% of hypotheses confirmed.
- **Evidence synthesis**: strong (consistent, multiple good-quality studies or ≥1 excellent), moderate (consistent, multiple fair-quality or ≥1 good), limited (one fair-quality study), conflicting, unknown (only poor-quality studies). Same-method studies synthesised by quality, number and consistency.

## Results

### The taxonomy: what the three families actually measure

| Family | Sub-family | n studies | Test material / analysis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mixing ability | Colour-changeable chewing gum | 8 | Gum colour progression read by colorimeter (a* in CIELAB), 10-grade visual colour scale, or smartphone image analysis |
| Mixing ability | Two-coloured chewing gum | 12 | Degree of colour mixing by digital analysis (ViewGum variance of hue, ImageJ geometric dispersion, MATLAB hue histograms, Diffpix) or 5-point visual/subjective scales (SA1–SA5), colour-mixing index |
| Mixing ability | Two-coloured wax | 4 | Wax cube colour mixing / mixing-ability index (MAI), visual 5-grade scale vs digital mixing index |
| Comminution | Sieving / optical scanning | 9 | Optosil / Optosil Comfort silicone cubes, natural foods (carrot, peanut, beef, rice, sausage, vegetables), alginate; single or multi-sieve X₅₀, optical scanning (ImageJ), digital image analysis of particle area |
| Comminution | Fuchsine beads | 2 | Dye-containing capsule; fuchsine release quantified by UV–Vis spectrophotometry |
| Comminution | Gummy jelly | 7 | Glucose elution (mg/dL), 10-stage visual scoring, photographic increase in surface area (ISA, smartphone) |
| Other | Odour sensor; Eichner index | 2 | Portable odour sensor (OMX-SR) on odour-compound gum; occlusal support classified by Eichner index |

Counts sum to 44 for 40 included studies because three studies (Imamura 11, Carneiro 13, Sanchez-Ayala 40) evaluate more than one material and appear in several rows — the review does not flag this overlap explicitly.

### What each instrument was validated against (criterion validity)

- **Colour-changeable gum** → colorimeter a* values, glucose extraction, digital colour analysis, food-fragmentation tests, and known functional gradients (masticable food categories, dentate vs edentulous, chewing-stroke counts).
- **Two-coloured gum** → colorimeters, scanner-based VoH, glucose-based tests, comminution of alternative test foods; and internally, digital vs visual scoring (ViewGum VoH vs SA1–SA5), which is treated as agreement evidence rather than true criterion validity.
- **Two-coloured wax** → visual 5-grade scale vs digital mixing index (r = 0.95, P < .001); sieving (rₛ = 0.56–0.72); peanut comminution (r = −0.56 to −0.70, P < .001) — but **not** gummy-jelly glucose (ns), which the authors read as wax MAI reflecting brittle-food comminution rather than glucose-elution ability.
- **Sieving / optical scanning** → optical scanning against conventional sieving (Eberhard: x₅₀ r = 0.919–1.0); digital image area against sieve-measured weight (Sugimoto: r = 0.83); Optosil as the artificial reference food.
- **Fuchsine beads** → median particle size (X₅₀) from Optosil silicone-cube comminution; against that reference the bead test ran −31.8% lower (P < .001), i.e. it is criterion-validated but not interchangeable.
- **Gummy jelly** → fully automated β-carotene extraction, photographic ISA, two-coloured gum tests, glucose sensors, colour-change gum (R² = 0.965); ISA cut-off 1687 mm² was calibrated to the 100 mg/dL glucose threshold.
- **Other** → odour intensity against gummy-jelly glucose (r = 0.68); Eichner index against occlusal force and MP decline across occlusal-support groups.

### Measurement error: the real bottleneck

Across all three families the limiting grade is measurement error, and its cause is reporting, not instrument noise. Colour-changeable gum: "measurement error was rated limited because most studies lacked the smallest detectable change (SDC) or minimal important change (MIC) reports." Two-coloured gum: error examined in only a subset, "frequently lacked complete reporting of key indices such as SDC or MIC", synthesis "inconsistent". Two-coloured wax: "none of the studies reported the MIC, SDC, or comparable indices required by COSMIN". Sieving/optical scanning: two studies assessed error, level of evidence unknown. Fuchsine beads: one study, rated unknown and "insufficient". Practical consequence stated by the authors: without MIC/SDC, "interpretation of longitudinal changes and intervention effects" is limited — the exact use case (monitoring, prosthesis trials) the tests are deployed for.

### Responsiveness: named as a domain, never answered

Responsiveness is one of the four COSMIN domains the review set out to assess and is named in the methods and in the evidence table's column set. But no per-family result section and no conclusion statement reports a responsiveness finding — the synthesis prose for every family covers reliability, validity and measurement error only. Read as a gap rather than a null result: the domain the review declared in scope is absent from what it grades, which matters because the whole clinical case for these tests (monitoring, prosthesis trials) is a claim about responsiveness.

### Authors' verdict and the gaps

- **Prefer gum-based mixing ability tests.** "From a clinical perspective, gum-based mixing ability tests currently demonstrate the strongest evidence for reliability and validity and represent the most supported tools for assessing MP." Simplicity and ease of standardization make them suitable for clinical and epidemiological use; smartphone colour analysis, visual colour scales and colorimetry extend feasibility where equipment or examiner experience is limited.
- **Comminution tests are methodologically strong but not yet standardizable.** "Strong theoretical validity but … less consistent methodological quality"; variability in test foods, chewing protocols and analytical thresholds "hinders their immediate clinical standardization" — better suited to research or structured screening than routine clinical assessment.
- **Wax and "other" methods are not ready.** Two-coloured wax shows less consistent evidence (material properties, scoring protocols, limited methodological reporting); odour-based and occlusal-index approaches "lack sufficient evidence across key measurement properties" to stand alone.
- **Age, dentition and test-food mechanics modulate the measurement itself.** Lower MP in older adults and with reduced posterior support, plus greater variability in tests requiring brittle-food fragmentation; elastic materials (gum) reproduce better than viscoelastic (gummy jelly) or brittle foods because mixing outcomes are less sensitive to occlusal force and stroke pattern.
- **What the field needs**: standardized test food and particle-analysis protocols, explicit MIC/SDC reporting, and rigorous validation of digital/automated methods against established references. Bottom line: "no standardized clinical test with established diagnostic accuracy is currently available."
