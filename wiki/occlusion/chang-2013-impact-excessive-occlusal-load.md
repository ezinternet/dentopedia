## Three-line Summary

Literature review of English-language Medline and Wiley Online Library papers (January 1991–December 2011) reporting peri-implant bone conditions under excessive occlusal loading with a controlled oral hygiene regime.

Evidence on the peri-implant bone response to overload was limited and of poor quality, with conflicting animal studies and an unknown biologically-acceptable load limit, but consistently higher peri-implant bone remodeling activity around implants subjected to high loading forces.

Whether overload causes marginal bone loss or total loss of osseointegration in already osseointegrated implants therefore remains unproven — occlusal force management rests on biomechanical rationale, not an evidence-based threshold (abstract-only source; full text not retrieved).

문헌고찰 (literature review) — Medline·Wiley Online Library 영문 문헌(1991–2011) 중 구강위생 조절 하에 과도한 교합부하 (excessive occlusal load)를 받은 임플란트 주위 골 조건을 보고한 실험·고찰 연구를 검토.

## Summary
This 2013 literature review (Journal of Investigative and Clinical Dentistry) examined English-language Medline and Wiley Online Library papers from January 1991 to December 2011 on how peri-implant bone responds when an already osseointegrated dental implant is subjected to excessive occlusal force under a controlled oral hygiene regime. It concludes that knowledge is limited and the level of evidence is poor: animal experimental studies give conflicting results, so it remains unclear whether occlusal overload causes marginal bone loss or total loss of osseointegration, and the biologically-acceptable load limit is unknown. One finding is consistent across the included evidence — peri-implant bone shows higher remodeling activity around implants subjected to high loading forces. Because no evidence-based overload threshold exists, the clinical implication is that occlusal force management in implant prosthodontics rests on biomechanical rationale rather than quantified limits. The source PDF is a PubMed abstract printout, so methodology detail and per-study results could not be retrieved.

## Key Contributions
- First review dedicated to the peri-implant bone response of *successfully osseointegrated* implants under excessive occlusal load (search window 1991–2011).
- Concludes the evidence base is limited and poor quality, with conflicting animal experimental results on overload-induced marginal bone loss.
- Identifies the unknown biologically-acceptable occlusal load limit as the central knowledge gap blocking causal inference and clinical thresholds.
- Reports consistently elevated peri-implant bone remodeling activity around implants under high loading forces — a subclinical response distinguishable from overt bone loss.

## Methodology
Literature (narrative) review. Databases: Medline and Wiley Online Library; English-language papers, January 1991 – December 2011. Inclusion: experimental or review papers reporting peri-implant bone conditions of dental implants submitted to excessive occlusal loading with a controlled oral hygiene regime. Outcomes: peri-implant bone response — marginal bone loss, total loss of osseointegration, bone remodeling activity.
