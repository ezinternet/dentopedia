## Three-line Summary

Narrative literature review assessing when a missing second molar warrants implant restoration, synthesizing evidence on masticatory efficiency, super-eruption, and occlusal interferences.

First-molar occlusion provides ~90% masticatory efficiency; ~20% of unopposed posterior teeth extrude ≥2 mm, yet the degree of over-eruption is NOT strongly correlated with occlusal interference incidence.

Replacement decision is patient-preference–driven: no perceived deficit → monitoring is the default; perceived masticatory deficit or esthetic gap concern → implant restoration appropriate.

## 세줄요약

제2대구치 (Second Molar) 결손 후 임플란트 (Implant) 수복 여부를 평가한 서술적 문헌고찰 — 저작효율 (Masticatory Efficiency)·과맹출·교합간섭 (Occlusal Interference) 데이터 종합.

제1대구치 교합만으로 저작효율 약 90% 달성; 대합치 없는 구치의 약 20%가 ≥2 mm 정출 (Supraeruption)하나, 정출 정도와 교합간섭 발생은 강한 상관이 없음.

수복 여부는 환자 선호 (Patient Preference)에 따름 — 저작 불편감 없으면 모니터링이 기본값, 불편감/심미 불만 있으면 임플란트 지지 수복 적절.

## Summary

Greenstein et al. (2018) reviewed the dental literature to resolve the controversy over whether a missing second molar should routinely be replaced with an implant-supported restoration. The review evaluated masticatory efficiency data, super-eruption prevalence and magnitude, and the relationship between over-eruption and occlusal interference. Key quantitative anchors: first-molar occlusion alone achieves approximately 90% of normal masticatory efficiency, making second-molar replacement a quality-of-life enhancement rather than a functional necessity. Super-eruption of the unopposed antagonist is frequent, but only approximately 20% of teeth extrude ≥2 mm, and the degree of extrusion is not strongly predictive of clinically significant occlusal interference. The authors concluded that the patient's own perception of chewing deficiency or esthetic dissatisfaction should be the primary driver of the replacement decision; if neither is present, monitoring with preventive management of supraeruption is appropriate. A caveat: unchecked super-eruption can complicate future restorative planning.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes ~90% masticatory efficiency with first-molar contact only — the quantitative anchor justifying observation over replacement when the patient is asymptomatic.
- Reframes the clinical decision around patient-perceived deficit rather than anatomic tooth loss alone.
- Synthesizes the paradox that super-eruption is common but occlusal interference is not a predictable downstream consequence, refuting reflexive replacement.
- Acknowledges the restorative complexity of unchecked supraeruption, maintaining preventive monitoring as the minimum standard of care.

## Methodology

Narrative literature review of studies addressing: chewing efficiency with first-molar occlusion, tooth super-eruption / extrusion / over-eruption rates after second-molar loss, and occlusal interference incidence. Published as a CE article with quiz in *Compend Contin Educ Dent* 2018. Specific databases and study count not reported in abstract.

## Results

| Outcome | Finding |
|---|---|
| Masticatory efficiency, first-molar occlusion only | ~90% |
| Super-eruption of unopposed posterior teeth | Occurs frequently (majority) |
| Proportion extruding ≥2 mm | ~20% |
| Over-eruption degree → occlusal interference | NOT strongly correlated |
| Replacement decision arbiter | Patient preference |
| When to replace | Perceived chewing deficiency or esthetic gap concern |
| When NOT to replace | No perceived deficit (monitoring appropriate) |
