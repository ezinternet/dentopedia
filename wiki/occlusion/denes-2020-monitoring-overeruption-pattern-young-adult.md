## Three-line Summary

Longitudinal in-vivo micro-CT study (56 Wistar rats, 4-week-old vs 26-week-old, 12-week follow-up) measuring overeruption rate of mandibular first molars after maxillary antagonist extraction.

Young unopposed molars: 172 ± 67 µm/day eruption rate, most marked in first 3 weeks; adult overloaded molars did not erupt; clinical crown height in young unopposed was 4.5-fold higher than controls.

Confirms the age effect (young >> adult) with a longitudinal design: the first 3 weeks post-extraction are the critical window for rapid overeruption in growing animals.

## Summary

Denes et al. (2020) conducted a 12-week longitudinal in-vivo micro-CT study in Wistar rats to quantify the overeruption rate and alveolar bone response after antagonist loss, comparing young (4-week-old) versus adult (26-week-old) animals. Young unopposed mandibular first molars showed the highest eruption rate of 172 ± 67 µm/day, with overeruption most pronounced during the first 3 weeks post-extraction and then decelerating toward control rates (~12.9 µm/day). By contrast, overloaded contralateral adult molars showed no eruption during the experimental period. Alveolar bone height increased alongside tooth eruption in young animals but at a slower rate, resulting in a net increase in clinical crown height 4.5-fold greater than controls. These findings independently corroborate Fujita 2009's finding that young rats overerupt approximately twice as much as adults, and add longitudinal rate data that identify the critical post-extraction window.

## Key Contributions

- Quantifies daily overeruption rate in young unopposed rat molars at 172 ± 67 µm/day — highest measured rate in this model.
- Establishes the first 3 weeks post-extraction as the critical eruption window; thereafter, rate returns to near-control levels.
- Shows alveolar bone adapts upward but lags, resulting in net 4.5-fold clinical crown height increase in young vs control group.
- Confirms adult contralateral (overloaded) molars do not erupt — complementary to the young-rat eruption finding.
- Corroborates Fujita 2009 age effect with a more controlled, longitudinal, in-vivo protocol.

## Methodology

Longitudinal in-vivo micro-CT study. 56 male Wistar rats split by age: 28 young (4-week-old), 28 adult (26-week-old). Within each group: 16 experimental (maxillary right molar extraction) + 12 controls. Outcome: eruption rate (µm/day) and alveolar bone height of mandibular first molars, measured from stable reference at mandibular canal. Scans at regular intervals over 12 weeks.

## Results

| Outcome | Finding |
|---|---|
| Young unopposed eruption rate (peak) | 172 ± 67 µm/day |
| Rate after first 3 weeks (returned to control level) | 12.9 ± 6.8 µm/day |
| Adult overloaded molar eruption | None (0 µm/day) |
| Clinical crown height, young unopposed vs control | 4.5-fold higher |
| Critical window | First 3 weeks post-extraction |
