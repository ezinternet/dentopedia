## Three-line Summary

COSMIN-based systematic review (5 databases to March 2021; 23 studies, 19 PROMs, 16,886 adults) appraising every patient-reported outcome measure (PROM) of masticatory function in adults and grading each psychometric property with modified GRADE.

No PROM reached high-level evidence across all properties; chewing-function questionnaire (CFQ)-Croatian/Albanian was the only COSMIN category A ("recommended") instrument, while FIQ-Japanese, New-FIQ-Japanese, PDC-Tanzania, CFQ-Chinese and SMDOA reached category B1 (potentially recommendable) and Subset-OHIP plus QMFQ-Persian fell to category C (not recommended).

Measurement error was evaluated in zero of 23 studies, responsiveness in three, and content validity never exceeded a low evidence level — so any longitudinal or treatment-change claim built on these questionnaires rests on thin measurement evidence.

## 세줄요약

COSMIN 기반 체계적 문헌고찰(5개 데이터베이스, 2021년 3월까지; 23편 연구·19개 환자보고결과측정도구(PROM)·성인 16,886명)로 저작기능(masticatory function) PROM 전체를 평가하고 각 심리측정적 특성을 수정 GRADE로 등급화했다.

어떤 PROM도 모든 특성에서 높은 수준의 근거를 확보하지 못했다; 저작기능설문지(Chewing Function Questionnaire, CFQ)-크로아티아/알바니아판만 COSMIN 범주 A(사용 권고)였고, 식이섭취설문지(Food Intake Questionnaire, FIQ)-일본어판·신FIQ-일본어판·저작곤란지각(Perceived Difficulty of Chewing, PDC)-탄자니아판·CFQ-중국어판·노인저작장애선별(Screening for Masticatory Disorders in Older Adults, SMDOA)이 범주 B1(권고 가능성), 구강건강영향프로파일(Oral Health Impact Profile, OHIP) 부분척도와 저작기능질문지(Quality of Masticatory Function Questionnaire, QMFQ)-페르시아판이 범주 C(권고 안 함)였다.

측정오차(measurement error)를 평가한 연구는 23편 중 0편, 반응성(responsiveness)은 3편뿐이었고 내용타당도(content validity)는 어떤 도구에서도 높은 근거수준에 도달하지 못해, 이 설문들로 종단·치료변화를 주장하는 근거는 얇다.

## Summary

Self-reported chewing ability is the patient-side readout of masticatory function, and it does not simply mirror objective performance — some studies find correlation with comminution/mixing-ability tests, others find disagreement. This review asks the prior question: are the questionnaires themselves any good? Under the COSMIN guideline, 23 studies covering 19 PROMs and 16,886 adults were appraised on two separate axes — the risk of bias of each validation study, and the quality and certainty of each psychometric property. The verdict is that the field is instrument-rich and evidence-poor: content validity (which the authors call the most important validity type) was assessed for only five instruments and never reached a high level, measurement error was never assessed at all, responsiveness was tested in three studies, and cross-cultural validity consisted of forward–backward translation in two. Only CFQ-Croatian/Albanian satisfies COSMIN category A, and it is recommended for general prosthodontic patients; five further instruments have the profile of category B1, i.e. worth using with the caveat that their quality is not yet fully established.

## Key Contributions

- **First COSMIN-based appraisal of masticatory-function PROMs.** The authors state that no previous systematic review had reported the methodological quality and psychometric properties of these instruments.
- **A four-way content taxonomy** that maps the field as it actually is: food-item questionnaires (13 PROMs), chewing-problem questionnaires (3), hybrids (2), and a single global question (1).
- **Two-axis appraisal instead of a single score.** Study risk of bias (COSMIN checklist, worst-score-counts) is kept separate from property quality (+/−/?), and both feed the modified-GRADE level of evidence — which is why e.g. CFQ-Chinese can hold *high* evidence for internal consistency yet only *very low* evidence for content validity.
- **A usable recommendation tier list** (A/B1/B2/C) plus population-matched advice: CFQ-Croatian/Albanian for prosthodontic patients, FIQ-Japanese and New-FIQ-Japanese for complete denture wearers, SMDOA/CFQ-Chinese/PDC-Tanzania for community screening.
- **An explicit gap map for future validation work**: measurement error, content validity, responsiveness, cross-cultural invariance — each with a named statistical method the authors consider appropriate.

## Methodology

- **Design**: PROSPERO-registered (CRD42020171591) systematic review, PRISMA 2020; no meta-analysis reported — results are synthesized qualitatively and graded per property (heterogeneous instruments make pooling inapplicable).
- **Search**: Medline (PubMed), Embase (Ovid), Web of Science Core Collection, CINAHL Plus (EBSCOhost), APA PsycINFO (ProQuest) from inception to March 2021; three-part string (function terms × questionnaire/subjective terms × validation/psychometric terms); plus reference-list hand-search and Google Scholar.
- **Eligibility**: studies developing or validating a *subjective* measure of masticatory function in adults, any design, English full text. Excluded: objective-only assessment, PROMs used merely as an outcome measure, case studies, expert opinion, animal studies, reviews.
- **Screening/extraction**: two independent reviewers, third reviewer for disagreements; EndNote 9.3.1 for deduplication.
- **Appraisal**: COSMIN risk-of-bias checklist across 10 aspects (development, content validity, structural validity, internal consistency, cross-cultural validity, reliability, measurement error, criterion validity, hypotheses testing, responsiveness), each rated very good/adequate/doubtful/inadequate with "worst score counts"; property results rated +/−/?; level of evidence graded high/moderate/low/very low using modified GRADE over risk of bias, inconsistency, imprecision and indirectness (publication bias excluded per COSMIN).
- **Recommendation**: COSMIN category A (sufficient content validity + at least low-quality evidence of sufficient internal consistency) = recommended; C (high-quality evidence for an insufficient property) = not recommended; B otherwise, with B1 = construct-validity hypotheses testing sufficient (potential to recommend) and B2 = further research needed.

Total 23 studies / 19 PROMs / 16,886 participants; 20 cross-sectional + 3 cohort; 8 studies from Japan, 4 from China, 2 from Canada, one each from Spain, Iran, Brazil, Croatia, Kosovo, Korea, Sudan, Tanzania and Sweden; sample sizes 20–2,244. Most PROMs are unidimensional; QMFQ-Persian has five domains, and Table 1 labels SMDOA both "unidimensional" and "two factors".

### Psychometric property results

- Internal consistency: 14 studies, **9 indeterminate** — they failed the "at least low evidence for sufficient structural validity" precondition.
- Test–retest: 8 studies, 7 sufficient. Structural validity: 8 studies, 7 sufficient. Content validity: 6 studies, 4 sufficient. Criterion validity: 3 studies, 2 sufficient.
- Hypotheses testing for construct validity: 12 of 21 studies sufficient.
- Responsiveness: 3 of 3 sufficient (standardized effect size larger than expected and in the hypothesized direction).
- Cross-cultural translation: 2 of 2 indeterminate — translation performed, invariance not statistically tested.

### Evidence synthesis — instruments that clear "low" evidence somewhere

| PROM | Sufficient properties at moderate/high evidence |
|---|---|
| **CFQ-Croatian / CFQ-Albanian** | internal consistency, test–retest, structural validity, hypotheses testing, responsiveness (moderate or high) |
| **CFQ-Chinese** | internal consistency (**high**); test–retest, structural validity, hypotheses testing (moderate) |
| **New-FIQ-Japanese** | structural validity, criterion validity, hypotheses testing (moderate or high) |
| **FIQ-Japanese (1994, 1998)** | hypotheses testing (moderate) |
| **PDC-Tanzania** | hypotheses testing (moderate); internal consistency low/indeterminate |
| **SMDOA** | structural validity, hypotheses testing (**high**); content validity low |

### COSMIN recommendation tiers and who should use what

- **Category A (recommended)**: CFQ-Croatian/Albanian only — recommended for assessing masticatory function in **general prosthodontic patients**.
- **Category B1 (potential to recommend)**: FIQ-Japanese, New-FIQ-Japanese, PDC-Tanzania, CFQ-Chinese, SMDOA; FIAQ-key food and SMF are "may be B1" but need more research. Population match: FIQ-Japanese and New-FIQ-Japanese for **complete denture wearers**; SMDOA, CFQ-Chinese and PDC-Tanzania for **community-dwelling adults in epidemiological screening**.
- **Category B2 (further research needed)**: ICA-1990, PDC-Sudan, IED, CFQ-Japanese, FIAQ, MACE, MPI.
- **Category C (not recommended)**: Subset-OHIP and QMFQ-Persian — high-quality evidence of an insufficient property.
- **Unclassifiable**: FIQ-Chinese — convergent validity insufficient, discriminative validity sufficient.

No instrument is a gold standard. Feasibility and interpretability were not formally appraised; the authors flag them as additional selection criteria.

## Clinical Reading

- **Picking an instrument.** For a prosthodontic caseload, CFQ (Croatian or Albanian version) is the only evidence-based default; the Chinese-language food-item CFQ and the Japanese FIQs are defensible within their own populations and languages, not as translations to other languages.
- **Cross-language transfer is the weak link.** Both cross-cultural studies were rated doubtful and their property results indeterminate — the review gives no basis for assuming a translated instrument retains the properties of its original. Using a validated foreign-language CFQ in a new language is an unvalidated act, not a supported shortcut.
- **Longitudinal use is weakest of all.** Only three studies measured change (all after prosthodontic treatment), and no study quantified measurement error, so a small pre/post score difference cannot currently be distinguished from instrument noise. Effect sizes from these instruments should be read as directional, not as calibrated change.
- **The objective/subjective distinction is a real one.** The review's framing — patient perspective captures adaptational and psychological factors that comminution tests miss, and the literature disagrees on how closely the two track — argues against substituting one for the other when judging whether a patient's masticatory function has improved.
- **Instrument mismatch is a live publication bias risk.** Results from a B2/C instrument carry the same rhetorical weight as category-A evidence when reported as "chewing function improved", so the instrument name matters for interpreting any masticatory-outcome study, including the SDA and RPD literature the wiki already holds.

## Limitations

- **English-language restriction** (author-stated): non-English PROMs, possibly well validated in their own languages, were excluded by design — this biases the inventory toward instruments validated in English-language journals.
- **Reporting-quality confound** (author-stated): rigorous development/validation that was under-reported gets downgraded, so some "doubtful" ratings may reflect reporting rather than conduct.
- **Zero measurement-error studies** (author-stated): the authors treat this as the field's central methodological gap.
- **Responsiveness and content validity rest on very few studies** — three and six respectively — so the per-instrument evidence levels are unstable and re-gradable by a single new study.
- **No meta-analysis** is possible here by construction (heterogeneous instruments, constructs and populations); conclusions are qualitative grades, not pooled estimates.
- **Internal inconsistencies in the source.** The paper states item counts ranged from seven to 35, which contradicts Table 1 (SMF is a single global question; FIAQ-key food has 5 items). Table 1 lists 24 instrument rows for the 19 stated PROMs, and Table 4 merges four pairs (FIQ-Japanese, FIQ-Chinese, ICA, SMF) without stating the mapping — merging ICA-1990 with ICA-2020 is questionable given their different item structures. The quality narrative also says "hypothesis testing for structural validity" where the results narrative says "construct validity".
