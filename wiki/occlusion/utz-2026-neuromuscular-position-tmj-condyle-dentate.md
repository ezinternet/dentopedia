## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional method-comparison study (n=81 fully dentate volunteers, healthy oral function; 4 independent operators; fifth/final part of a programmatic series) recorded the neuromuscular mandibular position with a central bearing point system and rapid closing movements, then compared it on a custom electronic measuring articulator against centric relation (CR, defined by central-bearing registration at the Gothic arch tip) and maximal intercuspal position (MI).

Reproducibility of the neuromuscular registration averaged 0.52 ± 0.16 mm (range 0.04–2.53 mm); the condylar spatial distance from MI averaged 0.88 ± 0.30 mm (range 0.12–5.98 mm) and from CR averaged 0.83 ± 0.27 mm (range 0.10–7.89 mm).

The neuromuscular position is neither MI nor CR — a third, distinct position — and the authors conclude registration of the neuromuscular adduction field should not be recommended for prosthodontic restorations in dentate or edentulous patients.

신경근위치는 MI도 CR도 아닌 제3의 위치이며, 저자들은 유치악·무치악 환자의 보철 수복에 신경근 흡착역(Adduction Field) 등록을 권장할 수 없다고 결론지었다.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this cross-sectional study (Int J Prosthodont 2026;39(1):18-28; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.11607/ijp.9236); PMID 39993166) is the fifth and final part of a programmatic investigation of temporomandibular joint condylar positions. Where the prior parts of the series (not held in this wiki) presumably characterized CR and MI, this paper adds a third candidate reference: the **neuromuscular position**, recorded with a central bearing point system and rapid closing movements by four independent operators in 81 fully dentate subjects with healthy oral function. Each subject's most frequent adduction point was determined and the recording repeated twice. Maxillary casts were mounted via individual facebow transfer; mandibular casts were then mounted using a central bearing point registration at the tip of the Gothic arch tracing — the study's own operational definition of CR. A custom-made electronic measuring articulator then quantified the spatial distance between the condyles across CR, MI, and the neuromuscular position.

The design is notably strong for this literature: 81 subjects, four independent operators, and double registration per subject, situating the result as the culmination of a multi-part series rather than a one-off finding. The headline numbers look small — MI↔neuromuscular 0.88 ± 0.30 mm, CR↔neuromuscular 0.83 ± 0.27 mm — but the ranges tell a different story: 0.12–5.98 mm and 0.10–7.89 mm respectively, with the neuromuscular registration's own reproducibility ranging up to 2.53 mm. A registration method whose own reproducibility can miss by 2.5 mm cannot itself define a reliable restorative position, and individual subjects reached displacement magnitudes several times the mean. It is the dispersion, not the average, that carries the authors' categorical conclusion: **registration of the neuromuscular adduction field should not be recommended for prosthodontic restorations in dentate or edentulous patients.**

Two caveats belong alongside that conclusion. First, the CR standard against which the neuromuscular position is judged is itself a specific methodological choice — the Gothic arch tracing apex, via central-bearing registration — not a neutral ground truth; a reader who rejects gothic-arch-defined CR would need to reinterpret the CR-vs-neuromuscular comparison accordingly. Second, the enrolled sample is exclusively fully dentate with healthy oral function (n=81, apparently one research group/series); the conclusion's extension to **edentulous** patients is not tested by this study and should be read as extrapolation, not direct evidence.

## Key Contributions

- **A hard negative for neuromuscular/myocentric registration**: the wiki previously held no paper directly testing this school of jaw-position registration against CR and MI in the same well-controlled design.
- **Neuromuscular position is a third, distinct position** — measurably different from both MI (0.88 ± 0.30 mm) and CR (0.83 ± 0.27 mm), not an approximation of either.
- **Foregrounds dispersion over central tendency**: small means (<1 mm) coexist with ranges up to 5.98 mm and 7.89 mm — the paper's negative conclusion is carried by the tails.
- **Names its own CR reference frame explicitly**: CR = central-bearing registration at the Gothic arch tip, a methodological choice rather than a neutral fact.
- **Categorical clinical recommendation** against using neuromuscular adduction-field registration for prosthodontic restorations — stated for dentate patients (tested) and extended to edentulous patients (not tested, extrapolation).

## Methodology

- **Design**: cross-sectional method-comparison study; fifth and final part of a comprehensive investigation of TMJ condylar positions.
- **Sample**: 81 fully dentate subjects with healthy oral function.
- **Neuromuscular position recording**: central bearing point system + rapid closing movements, performed independently by 4 operators; the most frequent adduction point was determined; recordings repeated twice per subject.
- **CR position**: maxillary casts mounted via individual facebow transfer; mandibular casts mounted using a central bearing point registration at the tip of the Gothic arch tracing — this operational definition of CR is a substantive methodological choice, not a neutral reference.
- **MI position**: the subject's own maximal intercuspal position.
- **Measurement instrument**: custom-made electronic measuring articulator, determining the spatial distance between the condyles across CR, MI, and the neuromuscular position.
- **Statistics**: means ± SD and ranges reported for reproducibility and for each pairwise condylar distance (right/left sides averaged); no inferential test reported in the abstract.

**On Fukushima 2016 — why no `contradicts`/`refines` edge was forced**: Fukushima argues that when condylar seating is mechanically unstable, clinicians should abandon CR in favor of a muscle-guided target — specifically, a stable intercuspal position (ICP) at which the patient's own **habitual closing movements (HCM)** terminate without premature contact, verified chairside and continually re-inspected. That target is procedurally the patient's *own natural bite*, checked for soundness — not a distinct position derived from an external registration device. Utz's "neuromuscular position," by contrast, is recorded with a **central bearing point system and rapid closing movements** performed by an operator — an instrument-derived adduction point, methodologically closer to commercial neuromuscular-dentistry registration than to Fukushima's chairside HCM-termination check. Utz shows this instrument-derived position diverges from MI by ~0.88 mm on average (up to 5.98 mm); Fukushima's target, being defined as (a variant of) the patient's own stable ICP rather than an independently registered point, is not the same construct being measured. The two papers are therefore best read as **compatible**: Utz's finding does not undermine Fukushima's proposal, because Fukushima never proposes substituting an instrument-registered "neuromuscular position" for the patient's own verified habitual bite.
