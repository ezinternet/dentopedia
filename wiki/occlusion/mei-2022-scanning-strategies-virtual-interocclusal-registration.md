## Three-line Summary

In-vitro accuracy study (five pairs of reference cubes embedded in a volunteer's digitized dentitions, printed as resin casts, articulated at maximal intercuspal position, MIP) that used an industrial computed tomography (CT) system — not carbon paper or a second intraoral scanner — as the reference virtual interocclusal record (VIR), then compared nine intraoral-scanner (IOS) scanning strategies against it via six reference-point deviations on the XY-plane.

Accuracy was regional, not global: right-posterior-only scanning (RP) gave the smallest deviation on the right side, bilateral-posterior scanning (BP) gave the smallest deviation on the left side and across the full arches, and left-posterior scanning (LP) gave the (weakly, non-significantly) smallest anterior deviation; wider spans and full-arch scans (CA1/CA2) produced larger deviations than confined unilateral scans.

The practical rule: where the buccal-bite scan is placed determines where the resulting virtual articulation is accurate, so BP is the safest general-purpose default for full-arch work, while single-side posterior work (e.g. one implant crown) is best served by scanning only that side; because the reference was an independent industrial CT rather than an imperfect proxy, this in-vitro result carries more methodological weight than its design tier alone would suggest.

## 세줄요약

가상 교합기록(Virtual Interocclusal Record, VIR) 정확도 실험실 연구 — 지원자 치열의 디지털 모형에 기준 큐브 5쌍을 삽입해 레진 모형으로 출력하고, 최대교두감합위(Maximal Intercuspal Position, MIP)로 교합기에 부착한 뒤, 산업용 컴퓨터단층촬영(Computed Tomography, CT) 장비로 스캔한 결과를 기준 VIR로 삼았다 — 카본지나 두 번째 구강스캐너가 아니라 독립적 산업용 CT를 기준으로 삼은 점이 특징. 이후 9가지 구강스캐너(Intraoral Scanner, IOS) 스캔 전략을 이 기준과 비교했다(상악 기준 큐브의 기준점 6개, XY평면까지의 편차로 측정).

정확도는 부위별로 달랐다 — 우측 구치부는 우측 구치부만 스캔(RP)이 편차 최소, 좌측 구치부와 전체 치열은 양측 구치부 스캔(BP)이 편차 최소, 전치부는 좌측 구치부 스캔(LP)이 (통계적으로는 CA2 대비만 유의한 약한 차이로) 편차 최소였다. 스캔 범위가 넓을수록, 특히 전체 치열 스캔(CA1/CA2)일수록 편차가 컸다.

실무 규칙: 협측 교합 스캔을 어디에 위치시키느냐가 가상 교합의 정확도가 어디서 나오는지를 결정한다 — 전체 치열 작업의 기본값으로는 BP(양측 구치부)가 가장 안전하고, 단측 구치부 작업(예: 임플란트 단일 크라운)은 해당 측만 스캔하는 것이 낫다. 카본지·교합지 같은 불완전한 대리 기준이 아니라 독립적 산업용 CT를 기준으로 삼았기 때문에, 이 실험실 연구는 연구설계 등급(in-vitro) 이상의 방법론적 무게를 가진다.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this Journal of Advanced Prosthodontics study ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.4047/jap.2022.14.6.369); PMID 36685791; PMCID PMC9832144) from West China Hospital of Stomatology asked whether the *scanning strategy* used to capture a virtual interocclusal record (VIR) — not just the scanner itself — determines its accuracy. The authors built a physical ground truth: five pairs of reference cubes were embedded in a volunteer's digitized upper and lower dentitions, printed as acrylic-resin casts, and articulated at maximal intercuspal position (MIP) in a mechanical articulator locked against mandibular deviation. Crucially, the reference VIR was obtained by scanning that fully articulated cast with an **industrial CT system** (Zeiss Metrotom 1500 G3) — an independent metrology-grade ground truth, not carbon paper, articulating paper, or a second intraoral-scanner (IOS) pass. That is a stronger reference-standard design than most agreement studies in this wiki, where accuracy is validated against an imperfect clinical proxy instead (e.g. IOS vs carbon paper).

Against that CT reference, the resin casts were then scanned with an IOS (TRIOS 3) using 9 designed scanning strategies (right posterior RP, left posterior LP, bilateral posterior BP, right quadrant RQ, left quadrant LQ, bilateral quadrant BQ, complete arch direction 1 CA1, complete arch direction 2 CA2, anterior ANT), 10 repetitions each. Accuracy turned out to be regional, not global: RP was most accurate for the right posterior region, BP was most accurate for the left posterior region and for the full arch overall, and LP nominally had the smallest anterior deviation — though the anterior result was statistically weak, significant only against CA2 and not distinguishable from the other seven groups. Full-arch scans (CA1, CA2) were consistently among the worst performers for every region, which the authors attribute to error accumulating during image stitching as the scanned span widens; the two full-arch scanning *directions* (CA1 vs CA2) did not differ from each other, so direction itself is not the variable — span is.

The discussion offers an explicit mechanism for the right/left asymmetry: citing prior work (Edher), the authors describe a "tilting effect toward the site of the VIR of a single section" — i.e., when only one region is scanned, error rotates or levers away from that region rather than distributing evenly, so accuracy is best exactly where the scan was concentrated and worse elsewhere. This explains why RP is best on the right and LP is best on the left; no equivalent mechanistic explanation is offered for why LP happened to have the lowest (barely significant) anterior deviation, and given how weak that particular result was statistically, it should be read as unexplained rather than forced into the same "tilting" story. The paper's own conclusion translates this into practice: for single-crown or implant restoration focused on one posterior quadrant, use BP, BQ, RP, or RQ on that side; for anterior-focused work, avoid full-arch (CA1/CA2) scanning; and when multiple teeth are missing across quadrants (e.g. bilateral posterior edentulism), virtual interocclusal registration is not recommended at all with this protocol. Note that this last point is a caution drawn from citing another group's edentulism findings and from this study's own missing-region comparisons (ANT group performing worse than RP/BP) — the study itself used a fully dentate cast throughout, so its specific strategy rankings should not be extrapolated to edentulous jaws.

Limitations are real: this is an in-vitro study on printed resin casts from a single 20-year-old volunteer, using one IOS model; the reference cubes are precision-manufactured landmarks, not teeth, and the excellent CT reference cannot substitute for the soft tissue, saliva, mandibular flexure, and muscle-driven jaw closure of a live patient. Deviations were reported to fall within the previously published 60–200 µm trueness/precision range for complete-arch digital scans, but the exact per-strategy numeric values were not recoverable from the returned full text — only the rank-order and significance pattern among the 9 groups.

## Key Contributions

- **Independent ground truth**: industrial CT reference standard, not an imperfect clinical proxy — the first paper in this wiki's VIR/occlusion cluster to compare against true metrology rather than another fallible method.
- **Nine scanning strategies formally compared** (RP, LP, BP, RQ, LQ, BQ, CA1, CA2, ANT) with 10 repetitions each (90 total samples).
- **Region-specific accuracy law with a stated mechanism**: accuracy is best near the scanned span and degrades away from it (a "tilting effect"), explicitly demonstrated for right- vs left-posterior asymmetry.
- **Actionable default rule**: BP (bilateral posterior) for general full-arch work; scan only the ipsilateral side for single-quadrant restorative work (RP≈RQ, LP≈LQ — the shorter quadrant scan loses no accuracy and saves time).
- **Full-arch scans are the weakest strategy**, regardless of direction (CA1 ≈ CA2), attributed to stitching-error accumulation over a wider span.

## Methodology

- **Reference cast**: 1 volunteer (male, 20 y, full dentition, stable MIP, normal overjet/overbite), digitized (TRIOS 3), 5 pairs of reference cubes added digitally (Geomagic 15.0), 3D-printed as acrylic-resin master casts (DLP printer).
- **Articulation**: resin casts articulated at MIP in a mechanical articulator (Artex CPR), locked in hinge movement to prevent mandibular deviation.
- **Reference VIR**: the articulated resin casts scanned by an industrial CT system (Zeiss Metrotom 1500 G3), reported comparable in accuracy to a coordinate measuring machine.
- **Investigated VIRs**: 9 designed IOS scanning strategies, each scanned once per repetition (10 reps/group = 90 STL samples), reference cubes excluded from the VIR scan itself to avoid landmark-bias confounding.
- **Outcome**: 6 reference points on the 5 maxillary cubes, distance to the XY-plane (built from the 5 mandibular cubes) measured in GOM Inspect 2020; deviation from the CT reference (δ1–δ6) grouped into δR (right posterior), δA (anterior), δL (left posterior), δD (full arch).
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk → Kruskal-Wallis (all 4 outcomes non-normal) with Bonferroni-corrected post hoc pairwise comparisons, α=.05.
- Note: exact numeric δ values (µm) per strategy were reported in a table/figure not returned by the PMC full-text extraction; only the significance/rank structure below is confirmed from the article body.

## Results

| Outcome (region) | Smallest-deviation group | Not significantly different from | Significantly worse than smallest group |
|---|---|---|---|
| δR — right posterior | **RP** | RQ, BP, BQ | LP, LQ, ANT, CA1, CA2 |
| δL — left posterior | **BP** | LP, LQ, BQ | RP, RQ, ANT, CA1, CA2 |
| δA — anterior | **LP** (weak) | all except CA2 | CA2 only |
| δD — full arches | **BP** | RP, LP, RQ, LQ, BQ, ANT | CA1, CA2 |

- Right-sided scans (RP, RQ) had δR significantly < δL; left-sided scan (LP) had δL significantly < δR (mirrored "tilting" pattern); LQ showed no significant asymmetry.
- CA1 and CA2 (opposite full-arch scan directions) never differed from each other — direction is not the driver of error, span is.
- The anterior/δA result is the weakest finding: LP beat only CA2, and was statistically indistinguishable from the remaining seven groups.
