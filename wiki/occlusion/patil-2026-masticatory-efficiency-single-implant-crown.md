## Three-line Summary

Prospective, non-randomized parallel-group observational study (consecutive allocation — **not** an RCT), n=60 (30 single-piece implant crowns vs 30 three-unit metal-ceramic FPDs, one posterior tooth, single centre in India, 2021–2023), measuring masticatory performance (MP%, almond comminution then four-sieve dry sieving) at 3, 6 and 12 months post-loading.

Implant crowns comminuted significantly better at every timepoint — 28.4 ± 4.2 vs 22.6 ± 3.8% at 3 months, 32.1 ± 3.7 vs 25.4 ± 3.9% at 6 months, 35.7 ± 3.9 vs 28.3 ± 4.1% at 12 months (all p = 0.001, t = 5.60/6.82/7.16) — and both groups improved over time (ANOVA F = 25.71 implants, 15.73 FPDs, p = 0.001; every Bonferroni pairwise contrast p = 0.001).

Directionally consistent with implant functional advantage for single posterior replacement, but with consecutive (non-randomized) allocation, no bite-force or survival data reported, and a 12-month horizon, the ~6 MP%-point gap is hypothesis-generating rather than a settled causal effect.

## 세줄요약

비무작위 전향적 평행군 관찰연구 (연속 배정 — 무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) 아님), n=60 (단일체 임플란트 크라운 30 vs 3-unit 금속-도재 고정성 국소의치 (Fixed Partial Denture, FPD) 30; 단일 후방치; 인도 단일기관; 2021–2023), 저작 수행력 (Masticatory Performance, MP%)을 아몬드 분쇄 후 4단 체가름으로 부하 후 3·6·12개월에 측정.

임플란트 크라운군이 모든 시점에서 유의하게 높았다 — 3개월 28.4 ± 4.2 vs 22.6 ± 3.8%, 6개월 32.1 ± 3.7 vs 25.4 ± 3.9%, 12개월 35.7 ± 3.9 vs 28.3 ± 4.1% (모두 p = 0.001, t = 5.60/6.82/7.16); 양군 모두 시간에 따라 개선 (반복측정 분산분석 (Repeated-measures ANOVA) F = 25.71 vs 15.73, p = 0.001; Bonferroni 사후검정 모든 쌍 p = 0.001).

## Summary

This single-centre Indian study asked a narrow, practical question: for one missing posterior tooth, does a single-piece implant-supported crown chew better than a conventional three-unit metal-ceramic FPD? Sixty partially edentulous adults (25–60 y) were allocated **consecutively** — not randomly — into two equal arms: an Equinox Myriad one-piece titanium implant (3.75–4.2 mm × 10–12 mm) restored with a cemented monolithic zirconia crown after three months of osseointegration, or a three-unit metal-ceramic FPD on the two adjacent teeth. Masticatory performance was measured with a natural test food: five almonds chewed for 20 strokes, expectorated with a 50 mL rinse, washed through a sieve, dried at 130 °C for 40 min, fractionated on a 4.0/2.8/2.0/1.0 mm sieve stack, and expressed as the Kapur–Soman MP% (mass passing 2.8 mm relative to total dry mass).

Implant crowns were ahead at all three post-loading timepoints, and the absolute difference grew from 5.8 to 7.4 MP%-points, while both groups improved monotonically. The authors read this as faster neuromuscular/occlusal adaptation on the implant side — mechanistically, rigid bone anchorage without periodontal ligament (PDL) proprioceptive inhibition — and conclude that single-piece implant crowns offer superior masticatory efficiency for single posterior tooth replacement, while explicitly calling for randomized multicentre trials before definitive recommendations.

Three things temper the result. The allocation was consecutive, so treatment assignment was systematic and unblinded to operators (only the outcome examiner was blinded); standard clinical outcomes (implant survival, marginal bone, prosthetic complications, abutment tooth outcomes) and the promised bite-force data are absent; and the report contains internal inconsistencies (one-piece implants described as placed via a "two-stage" protocol; a "no baseline assessment" limitation alongside a baseline MP% in Table 1). The Discussion itself cites Kumar et al. and Altayyar et al. as finding **no** significant MP difference between single-tooth implant and FPD — i.e. the literature is split, and this paper sits on the "implant superior" side of a genuine disagreement.

## Key Contributions

- A **three-point functional trajectory** (3/6/12 months) for single-piece implant crowns vs three-unit FPDs, measured with natural-food comminution rather than a single endpoint.
- Quantified intergroup gap of **5.6–7.4 MP%-points** (≈25–26% relative, as the authors state) plus intragroup time effects (F 25.71 vs 15.73).
- Explicitly frames the **PDL-bypass mechanism** as the explanation for higher sustainable force and better comminution on the implant side — a testable mechanistic claim, not just an outcome report.
- Documents that both reconstructions keep improving for at least 12 months, i.e. **functional adaptation is not complete at delivery** for either modality.

## Methodology

- **Design**: prospective, parallel-group **observational** study with consecutive allocation (no randomization sequence, no allocation concealment, no ITT). Participants and operators unblinded; one calibrated examiner blinded for all functional measurements (ICC reported qualitatively, no value).
- **Setting/period**: Department of Prosthodontics, RKDF Dental College and Research Centre, India; March 2021 – December 2023.
- **n**: 60 (30/30), all completed. Sample size from G*Power 3.1.9.2 (effect size 0.64 borrowed from Kumar et al.; 80% power; α = 0.05).
- **Inclusion/exclusion**: age 25–60, single premolar or first molar to replace, ≥10 mm height / ≥6 mm width on CBCT, healthy FPD abutments, no bruxism; excluded uncontrolled systemic disease (HbA1c >7%), >10 cigarettes/day, head-and-neck radiotherapy history, TMD.
- **Interventions**: one-piece titanium implant (Equinox Myriad, 3.75–4.2 mm × 10–12 mm) + cemented monolithic zirconia crown after 3-month osseointegration, versus three-unit metal-ceramic FPD (Wiron 99 / VMK Master, glass-ionomer cement).
- **Outcome**: MP% by almond comminution — 5 almonds, 20 mandibular strokes, 50 mL rinse, sieve wash with 500 mL water, 130 °C/40 min drying, 4-sieve fractionation (4.0/2.8/2.0/1.0 mm) on a gypsum vibrator for 60 s, fraction weights on a precision balance, Kapur–Soman formula.
- **Timepoints**: 3, 6 and 12 months post-loading.
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk; independent-samples t-tests per timepoint; repeated-measures ANOVA + Bonferroni pairwise tests within group; χ²/t for baseline; SPSS 23.0; α = 0.05.

## Results

Baseline (Table 1) was comparable: age 44.7 ± 8.3 vs 45.1 ± 7.9 y (p = 0.82), sex p = 0.79, premolar/molar distribution p = 0.77, edentulism duration 6.8 ± 2.4 vs 6.5 ± 2.1 months (p = 0.63), baseline MP% 21.8 ± 4.1 vs 21.3 ± 3.8 (p = 0.64).

**Intergroup MP% (independent-samples t-test, Table 2)**

| Timepoint | Single-piece implant (n=30) | Conventional FPD (n=30) | t | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 months | 28.4 ± 4.2 | 22.6 ± 3.8 | 5.60 | 0.001 |
| 6 months | 32.1 ± 3.7 | 25.4 ± 3.9 | 6.82 | 0.001 |
| 12 months | 35.7 ± 3.9 | 28.3 ± 4.1 | 7.16 | 0.001 |

**Intragroup change (repeated-measures ANOVA, Table 3)**

| Group | 3 mo | 6 mo | 12 mo | F | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single-piece implants | 28.4 ± 4.2 | 32.1 ± 3.7 | 35.7 ± 3.9 | 25.71 | 0.001 |
| Conventional FPDs | 22.6 ± 3.8 | 25.4 ± 3.9 | 28.3 ± 4.1 | 15.73 | 0.001 |

**Pairwise Bonferroni (Table 4; negative t = improvement)**

| Group | 3→6 mo | 3→12 mo | 6→12 mo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implants | t = −4.92, p = 0.001 | t = −5.40, p = 0.001 | t = −8.16, p = 0.001 |
| FPDs | t = −3.88, p = 0.001 | t = −3.74, p = 0.001 | t = −6.28, p = 0.001 |

- All 60 patients completed follow-up; no dropouts or complications are reported.
- Not reported despite being named as objectives: maximum bite force, implant survival/failure, marginal bone level, peri-implant or abutment-tooth health, prosthetic complications, patient-reported outcomes.
- The authors' own derived figure: 25–26% higher MP% in the implant group "across the follow-ups".
- The FPD cohort's 12-month mean (28.3%) is essentially level with the implant cohort's 3-month mean (28.4%) — 12 months of FPD adaptation produced what the implant side reached in 3.
