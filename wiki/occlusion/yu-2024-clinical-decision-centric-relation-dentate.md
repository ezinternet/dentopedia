## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Yu et al., Hua Xi Kou Qiang Yi Xue Za Zhi / West China Journal of Stomatology 2024;42(5):558-565, single group at West China Hospital of Stomatology, Sichuan University) that asks whether centric relation (CR) — a mandibular position developed for edentulous complete-denture prosthodontics — remains rational once extended to dentate jaws, framing the debate as three explicit clinical questions: should CR anchor temporomandibular disorder (TMD) diagnosis/treatment, should it be the build position for occlusal reconstruction, and should it be the orthodontic target position?

The authors state that CR's clinical use is objectively widespread despite these doubts, and that they "preliminarily put forward" a decision tree restricting CR's recommended use to clinical situations they judge to have "little controversy" — the wiki's first attempt at an algorithmic decision rule for when CR should and should not be used, rather than a blanket for-or-against position.

The decision tree's actual branch conditions and the authors' situation-by-situation answers to the three questions — the paper's central contribution — are NOT captured in this page. Evidence status is a single group's preliminary ("初步提出") narrative-review proposal, not a validated guideline or consensus statement.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this narrative review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.7518/hxkq.2024.2024147); PMID 39304499; PMCID PMC11493863) traces centric relation (CR) from its origin as a condyle-referenced mandibular reference position for edentulous complete-denture prosthodontics, through its gradual extension into dentate-jaw clinical diagnosis and treatment. The authors frame the controversy around that extension as three explicit clinical questions: (1) should CR be the core of TMD diagnosis and treatment, (2) should CR be the jaw position used to build occlusion in occlusal reconstruction, and (3) should CR serve as the target jaw position in orthodontic treatment? These three questions map directly onto the three poles the wiki's existing overview already documents — TMD-oriented condylar monitoring, full-mouth-rehabilitation build position, and habitual-MIP acceptance in healthy dentate patients (see [[overviews/cr-co-micp-reference-position-debate]]).

Despite the doubts raised by each question, the authors note that "varieties of CR clinical applications are objective" — i.e., CR is in fact used clinically across many situations regardless of the theoretical debate. Working from existing evidence, they state they "summarize the applicable conditions of several main clinical situations with little controversy" and, on that basis, "preliminarily put forward the decision tree for the clinical application of CR, which can be used as a reference in clinical practice." This is explicitly positioned as a first, unvalidated pass at turning the field's scattered CR-use debate into a situational algorithm — the authors' own hedge language ("preliminarily," 初步) signals expert-opinion status, not a validated or consensus decision aid.

**What could not be retrieved**: `get_full_text_article` on PMC11493863 returned an empty full-text body on two independent calls (2026-07-17). Only the structured English abstract was obtainable via `get_article_metadata`. As a result, this page captures the paper's *framing* (the three questions, the existence of a proposed decision tree, and its preliminary status) but not the decision tree's actual nodes, branch conditions, or the authors' specific answer to each of the three questions. This is a substantive limitation for a paper whose stated central contribution is the algorithm itself.

## Key Contributions

- **Structures the CR-in-dentate-jaws controversy as three explicit, separable clinical questions** (TMD core position? occlusal-reconstruction build position? orthodontic target position?) rather than treating "should CR be used" as one monolithic question — this decomposition itself is a contribution, independent of the tree's specific content.
- **Proposes the wiki's first algorithmic CR decision tree**, restricting recommended CR use to clinical situations the authors judge have "little controversy," rather than arguing for wholesale adoption or abandonment of the term (as the Zonnenberg/Türp/Greene vs. Goldstein poles do).
- **Explicitly preliminary status**: the authors label the tree "preliminarily put forward" (初步提出) — this is a single group's synthesis into an algorithm, not a validated guideline. An algorithm's tree/flowchart format tends to read as more authoritative than a narrative position paper; this status label must be preserved whenever the paper is cited.
- **Contrast with evidence-graded consensus**: unlike [[occlusion/kattadiyil-2021-relationship-centric-occlusion-maximal-intercuspal]] (a formal Best Evidence Consensus Statement screening 313 SR/RCT/MA sources), this is a narrative review with no stated systematic search strategy visible in the retrievable abstract — a materially different evidence class despite superficially similar "decision guidance" framing.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative literature review / single-group expert synthesis (State Key Laboratory of Oral Diseases, Dept. of Prosthodontics, West China Hospital of Stomatology, Sichuan University, Chengdu, China).
- **Not a systematic review**: no PRISMA flow, no stated inclusion/exclusion criteria, or search strategy is visible in the retrievable abstract.
- **Scope**: CR's historical origin in edentulous complete-denture prosthodontics, its extension into dentate-jaw practice, and derivation of applicable-condition criteria for a proposed decision tree covering TMD, occlusal reconstruction, and orthodontic treatment scenarios.
- Full methodological detail — how the "little controversy" situations were selected, what evidence underpins each branch, and how the tree was validated (if at all) — resides in the article body, which was not retrievable through the sanctioned PubMed MCP tool (see retrieval limitation above). No independent translation of Chinese source text was performed; nothing beyond the official English abstract was used.

## Results

Only abstract-level claims could be retrieved; the decision tree's actual content is not available in this ingest:

- CR originated in edentulous complete-denture prosthodontics and was subsequently extended to dentate-jaw clinical diagnosis and treatment.
- The rationality of that extension is doubted along three specific, named axes: (1) TMD diagnosis/treatment core position, (2) occlusal-reconstruction build position, (3) orthodontic target position.
- Despite these doubts, CR's clinical use is described as "objectively" widespread in practice.
- The authors state they identified "several main clinical situations with little controversy," derived applicable conditions for CR use in each, and assembled these into a preliminary decision tree intended as a clinical-practice reference.
- **Not retrievable**: which specific clinical situations qualify as "little controversy," what the applicable conditions/criteria are for each branch, and the authors' explicit answer to each of the three framing questions. Readers needing the operational decision tree should treat this page as an abstract-level pointer only, and consult the primary source (DOI above) directly.
