## Three-line Summary
Single case report (n=1): a mandibular implant that lost osseointegration through aseptic loosening in a 79-year-old female breast-cancer patient was treated solely by occlusal correction.
Re-osseointegration was confirmed clinically and radiographically after 8 months and remained stable over a 21-month follow-up.
Suggests reducing occlusal forces may permit re-osseointegration, but n=1 with concurrent antineoplastic chemotherapy as an uncontrolled confounder and no evidence-based therapy concept for aseptic loosening yet.

## Summary
This Swiss Dental Journal case report (German original, English abstract) describes a 79-year-old female patient with mamma carcinoma whose mandibular dental implant lost osseointegration because of aseptic loosening — a poorly understood cause of sudden implant failure for which no evidence-based therapy concept currently exists. The only treatment performed was a correction of the occlusion. After eight months, re-osseointegration was observed both clinically and radiographically, and the result remained stable for the 21-month follow-up period. The authors propose that reduction of occlusal forces after correction of the occlusion may have facilitated the re-osseointegration, while explicitly cautioning that the concurrent antineoplastic chemotherapy for breast carcinoma or other unknown influences cannot be ruled out. Evidence level is a single case (n=1), and the source is abstract-only — the paper cannot establish efficacy and functions only as an existence proof that re-osseointegration after aseptic loosening is possible.

## Key Contributions
- Existence proof (n=1): osseointegration lost through aseptic loosening can be re-established after occlusal correction alone, with no surgical or graft intervention.
- Names the clinical gap: aseptic implant loosening is poorly understood and has **no evidence-based therapy concept**.
- Keeps causal attribution deliberately weak: occlusal force reduction "may have" facilitated re-osseointegration; chemotherapy and unknown influences cannot be excluded.

## Methodology
Single case report (n=1) — 79-year-old female with mamma carcinoma, mandibular implant with loss of osseointegration caused by aseptic loosening. Sole intervention: occlusal correction. Outcomes: clinical and radiographic assessment of re-osseointegration at 8 months, with stability followed for 21 months. No databases, no control group, no quantified occlusal-force measurement reported in the abstract.
