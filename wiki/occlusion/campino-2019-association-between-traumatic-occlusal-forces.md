## Three-line Summary

Systematic review with narrative synthesis (no meta-analysis; J Int Acad Periodontol 2019) of traumatic occlusal forces and periodontitis: 30 articles retrieved, 14 analyzed (2 RCTs, 1 cohort, 4 retrospective, 7 cross-sectional).

Cross-sectional studies linked occlusal discrepancies to probing depth and clinical attachment level, but the effect magnitude was negligible; intervention studies showed minimal effect of occlusal adjustment on PD and CAL in periodontitis patients.

Evidence that traumatic occlusion is associated with periodontitis, or that occlusal adjustment meaningfully improves periodontal status, is limited (abstract-only).

## Summary

Campiño and colleagues systematically reviewed human clinical and observational studies on traumatic occlusal forces and periodontitis, asking whether occlusal discrepancies affect periodontal parameters and whether occlusal interventions help periodontitis patients. Of 30 retrieved articles, 14 were analyzed (2 RCTs, 1 cohort, 4 retrospective, 7 cross-sectional) and synthesized narratively. Cross-sectional data showed a significant but negligible-magnitude association between occlusal discrepancies and PD/CAL, and occlusal adjustment had minimal effect on PD and CAL. The authors conclude evidence for the association, and for routine occlusal adjustment to improve periodontal status, is limited.

## Key Contributions

- Separates the association question (cross-sectional) from the intervention question (occlusal adjustment)
- Finds statistical association but negligible effect size
- Finds minimal PD/CAL benefit from occlusal adjustment in periodontitis

## Methodology

Systematic review of clinical and observational studies, presented narratively; 30 articles retrieved after title/abstract review, 14 full texts analyzed. Outcomes: periodontal parameters (PD, CAL).
