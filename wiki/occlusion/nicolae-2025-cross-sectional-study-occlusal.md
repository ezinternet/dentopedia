## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study (n=50) using OccluSense digital occlusal analysis comparing 493 deflective-contact teeth with 473 adjacent and 457 homologous controls; deflective-contact teeth carried significantly higher maximum occlusal loading (mean 208 vs 72–97) and exhibited more advanced periodontal damage across all measured parameters.

Centric-relation premature contacts were more strongly linked to periodontal deterioration than protrusive/lateral interferences despite similar force magnitudes, suggesting that the direction and timing of mandibular deviation — not load magnitude alone — modulates periodontal impact.

Study provides digitally quantified evidence for the occlusal-overload/periodontal-damage association but cannot establish causation; convenience sample (n=50) and cross-sectional design limit generalizability.

## 세줄요약

50명 환자에서 OccluSense 디지털 교합분석으로 편위성 교합접촉치 493개를 인접치·대합치 대조군과 비교; 편위접촉치는 교합부하(mean 208 vs 72–97)가 유의하게 높고 탐침 깊이·치은 퇴축·골소실·치주인대 (Periodontal Ligament, PDL) 폭경 확장 등 치주 손상이 더 심했다.

정중위 (Centric Relation, CR) 조기접촉은 전방이동·측방이동 간섭보다 교합력이 유사함에도 치주 손상과 더 강하게 연관되어, 힘 크기보다 접촉 유형·하악 편위 방향이 독립적 인자임을 시사한다.

## Background

Periodontal disease is one of the most prevalent oral conditions worldwide, affecting approximately 50% of adults and worsening with age. Occlusal trauma — excessive forces on teeth from precipitating factors (magnitude, direction, duration, frequency of force) and predisposing factors (root morphology, alveolar anatomy, plaque, inflammation, bruxism, TMD, tooth migration) — has long been hypothesized to influence periodontal health. The periodontal tissue response to traumatic forces involves three stages: active destruction, adaptation, and remodeling.

Digital occlusal analysis methods (OccluSense, T-Scan) offer objective, quantifiable force data superior to articulating paper, reducing subjectivity. Prior research on occlusal trauma and periodontitis shows mixed results — some studies demonstrate more severe damage in occlusal trauma cases, others do not — prompting this study's controlled design.

## Study Design

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| Design | Cross-sectional, convenience sample |
| N patients | 50 |
| N teeth (deflective) | 493 |
| N teeth (adjacent control) | 473 |
| N teeth (homologous control) | 457 |
| Occlusal measurement device | OccluSense (Dr. Jean Bausch GmbH); 256 pressure levels; 100 Hz; 10 s recording; 60 µm sensor thickness |
| Radiographic evaluation | Panoramic (within 6 months) |
| Statistical tests | Kruskal–Wallis, Friedman, Cochran's Q, chi-squared |
| Ethics | Carol Davila University PO-35-F-03, March 2025 |

**Inclusion**: Adults ≥18 y; primary and/or secondary occlusion disorders on digital exam; ≥10 pairs of occlusal units; panoramic within 6 months.

**Exclusion**: Occlusal adjustments or periodontal treatment within 12 months; systemic disease known to affect periodontium (diabetes, hypertension, blood disorders, kidney disease, radiotherapy/chemotherapy); pregnancy; non-cooperative.

## Key Definitions

- **Deflective occlusal contacts (편위성 교합접촉)**: Nonfunctional occlusal contacts causing deviation in normal mandibular closure path
- **Premature contacts (조기접촉)**: Deflective contacts during centric path closure, just before reaching maximal intercuspal position (MIP)
- **Occlusal interferences (교합 간섭)**: Nonfunctional contacts during left/right lateral or protrusive mandibular movements

## Results

### Occlusal Loading

| Group | Mean maximum occlusal load |
|---|---|
| Teeth with deflective contacts | **208** |
| Adjacent control teeth | 72 |
| Homologous control teeth | 97 |

All between-group differences significant at p < 0.05.

### Periodontal Parameters

Teeth with deflective occlusal contacts showed statistically more advanced periodontal damage vs both control groups:

- **Probing depth**: Greater in deflective-contact teeth
- **Gingival recession**: More extensive
- **Alveolar bone loss**: Greater (radiographic assessment)
- **PDL space widening**: Wider on panoramic

### Contact Type Subanalysis

| Contact type | Occlusal load | Periodontal damage |
|---|---|---|
| Centric relation premature contacts | Similar to interferences | **Greater** periodontal deterioration |
| Protrusive interferences | Similar | Less deterioration |
| Lateral movement interferences | Similar | Less deterioration |

Key finding: centric relation contacts drove more periodontal damage **despite similar force magnitudes**, implicating contact direction/timing as an independent variable.

## Clinical Implications

1. **Digital occlusal analysis (OccluSense)** can detect and quantify deflective contacts that are clinically associated with elevated occlusal load and worsened periodontal parameters — supporting its use in comprehensive periodontal assessment
2. **Centric relation premature contacts** may be clinically more important to identify and address than lateral/protrusive interferences from a periodontal standpoint
3. Results are **associative only** — the study cannot prove occlusal trauma causes periodontal damage; this aligns with the established position that occlusion is a modifier/co-factor, not an initiator of periodontitis

## Limitations

- Small convenience sample (n=50), single center, Bucharest Romania; generalizability uncertain
- Cross-sectional design: association not causation
- OccluSense values expressed as % of session maximum — not absolute force units (N or MPa)
- Plaque levels and gingivitis burden not reported as covariates
- Periodontal treatment history controlled only at 12-month exclusion window
