## Three-line Summary

Clinical cross-sectional study (n=30 dentate subjects, 20–45 y) comparing four interocclusal registration materials — alu wax, hard wax, polyvinylsiloxane (PVS, Jetbite), and bis-acrylic resin (Luxabite) — for vertical accuracy in recording centric relation (CR), against a direct-closure maximum intercuspation (MIP) baseline, on a Hanau Wide Vue articulator with Lucia jig stabilization.

On the recovered left-side data (Table 1), mean vertical readings were MIP 48.05±9.01, ALU 54.28±8.98, HARD 58.48±9.42, JET 50.24±8.95, LUX 50.11±8.94 (unit not stated anywhere in the paper); the omnibus left-side intergroup comparison reached p<0.05, but **the only material that differed significantly from MIP pairwise was alu wax** — hard wax, Jetbite, and Luxabite did not, despite hard wax carrying the numerically largest computed discrepancy (+10.43). Right-side numeric data were not recoverable from the extracted full text — only the qualitative statement survives (lowest MIP, highest hard wax).

The rank order (waxes worst, PVS and bis-acrylic resin best) is therefore a **numerical trend that the paper's own significance testing does not establish**: with n=30 and SD≈9 against differences of 2–10, the study is underpowered for the pairwise contrasts, and the largest mean difference failing significance while a smaller one reaches it is itself a sign the data are noisy. Read the direction, not the ranking — and note the magnitudes are clinically implausible on their face (a ~10-unit vertical difference from a bite record would be enormous if the unstated unit is mm), which is a further reason to treat this paper as directional only. Its more durable framing is conceptual: closing onto any record material and then releasing the bite introduces vertical error at all, not merely different amounts by material.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this clinical cross-sectional study ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/jpbs.jpbs_1125_24); PMID 39926723; PMCID PMC11805238) — published as a 3-page article in a supplement issue of the Journal of Pharmacy & Bioallied Sciences (2024;16(Suppl 4):S3824–S3826), a format that carries weaker peer-review context than a full-length parent-journal article — asked a genuinely useful chairside question the wiki previously had no page on: which interocclusal registration material distorts the vertical bite relationship least when a patient closes onto it in centric relation (CR)?

Thirty dentate volunteers (20–45 y) each provided four CR records — alu wax, hard wax, polyvinylsiloxane (PVS, brand Jetbite), and bis-acrylic resin (brand Luxabite) — all stabilized with a Lucia jig, mounted on a Hanau Wide Vue articulator, and compared against a no-material direct-closure maximum intercuspation (MIP) baseline using a digital vernier caliper. The abstract itself reports almost no numbers, only adjectives ("lowest mean was MIP, highest was hard wax" on the right; "highest was alu wax" on the left; "Luxabite and Jetbite exhibited the highest mean accuracy scores"). The PMC full text does contain one real numeric table (Table 1, left side only): MIP 48.05±9.01, ALU 54.28±8.98, HARD 58.48±9.42, JET 50.24±8.95, LUX 50.11±8.94 — units not stated in the text but presumed mm given the caliper instrument. Subtracting the MIP baseline gives a computed vertical discrepancy of +10.43 for hard wax, +6.23 for alu wax, +2.19 for Jetbite, and +2.06 for Luxabite — waxes distort roughly 3–5× more than the elastomeric/resin materials on this side. **The right-side table was not recoverable from the PMC extraction used for this ingest** — only the qualitative statement survives, so treat the right-side claim as directionally consistent but numerically unverified.

The clinically load-bearing point is broader than "pick Jetbite or Luxabite": the paper frames vertical error as partly *inherent to the act of registering* — closing onto any interposed material and then releasing the bite measurably shifts the reading away from true MIP, even for the best-performing materials (neither Jetbite nor Luxabite was statistically different from MIP, but neither was they identical to it either). Waxes (hard wax worst, alu wax second) distort the most, consistent with their higher viscosity limiting fine surface-detail capture; PVS and bis-acrylic resin distort the least, consistent with their addition-polymerization/auto-mix curing producing minimal dimensional change.

## Key Contributions

- **Fills a materials-level gap**: prior wiki pages on occlusal recording ([[occlusion/didier-2026-occlusal-contacts-ios-vs-carbon-paper-concordance]], [[occlusion/rovira-lastra-2026-articulating-paper-accuracy-occlusal-points]]) evaluate *contact-marking* methods; this is the first page on interocclusal *record materials themselves*.
- **Class-level ranking with real numbers (left side)**: wax (hard > alu) worst; PVS (Jetbite) and bis-acrylic resin (Luxabite) best — reconciled from brand names to material class, since brand alone is not portable to another market's product line.
- **Reframes the clinical question**: not just "which material is most accurate" but "how much vertical error does the act of registering itself introduce, on top of material choice" — even the best materials showed nonzero, if non-significant, deviation from MIP.
- **Honest evidence-weight flag**: a 3-page supplement article with an essentially numberless abstract — the useful content required mining the PMC full text, and even then only half the numeric data (left side) survived.

## Methodology

- **Design**: clinical, within-subject comparative cross-sectional study; 4 materials × 30 subjects, each subject providing all four CR records.
- **Sample**: 30 dentate volunteers (dental students/staff), 20–45 y, no gender restriction — population is dentate and relatively young; **do not extrapolate to edentulous jaw-relation records** (see [[complete-denture/de-sousa-ervolino-2023-centric-relation-recording-techniques-edentulous]] for that population instead).
- **Materials, by class (brand in parentheses)**:
  - Wax — Alu wax (double thickness)
  - Wax — Hard wax
  - PVS / addition silicone — Jetbite® (premixed auto-mix cartridge)
  - Bis-acrylic resin — Luxabite® (4:1 dual-cartridge auto-mix)
- **Procedure**: CR record with Lucia jig anterior stop for all four materials; casts mounted with maximum intercuspation on a Hanau Wide Vue articulator; vertical distance between two predefined points measured with a digital vernier caliper; discrepancy computed per side (left/right) between each material's reading and the direct-closure MIP reading.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 22.0; α=0.05; Shapiro–Wilk normality; mean±SD; Mann–Whitney U and one-way ANOVA with Fisher's LSD post-hoc for intergroup comparisons.

## Results

| Group (left side, Table 1) | Mean | SD | Discrepancy vs. MIP (computed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| MIP (direct closure, no material) | 48.05 | 9.01 | — baseline |
| ALU wax | 54.28 | 8.98 | +6.23 |
| HARD wax | 58.48 | 9.42 | +10.43 |
| JET (Jetbite, PVS) | 50.24 | 8.95 | +2.19 |
| LUX (Luxabite, bis-acrylic) | 50.11 | 8.94 | +2.06 |

- Unit not stated in the retrieved full text (instrument: digital vernier caliper); the "discrepancy vs. MIP" column is computed for this page (material mean − MIP mean), not printed directly in the source.
- Left side: alu wax differed significantly from MIP (p<0.05); hard wax, Jetbite, and Luxabite did **not** differ significantly from MIP individually, despite hard wax having the numerically largest computed discrepancy — small sample (n=30) and SD ≈9 relative to the differences likely limit power.
- MIP itself did not differ significantly between right and left sides (p=0.84), supporting that the baseline was consistent within subjects.
- **Right side**: numeric table not recovered from this extraction. Text states only: lowest mean in MIP, highest in hard wax, with a statistically significant one-way ANOVA intergroup result — no SD or discrepancy values available.
- **Bottom line stated by the authors**: waxes showed the most, and bis-acrylic resin (Luxabite) and addition silicone/PVS (Jetbite) showed the least, vertical dimensional distortion from centric-relation registration.
