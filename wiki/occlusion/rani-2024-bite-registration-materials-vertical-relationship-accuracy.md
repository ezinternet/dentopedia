## Three-line Summary

In-vitro bench study (n=10 interocclusal records per material, 40 total; ideal maxillary/mandibular casts with mock preparations on teeth 46/47/48, semi-adjustable articulator, Medit T500 lab scanner) comparing four bite registration materials — polyether (Ramitec), two polyvinyl siloxane/addition silicone products (CADbite, Jet Bite), and wax (Aluwax) — for their capability to reproduce the vertical relationship after demounting and remounting the cast using the record.

All four materials produced a small but statistically significant deviation from the control (no-material, direct-contact) reading (Ramitec 0.136, CADbite 0.152, Jet Bite 0.165, Aluwax 0.939 — unit not stated in the source; all p≤0.001 vs. control); the three elastomeric/polyether materials did not differ significantly from each other (pairwise p=0.90–0.99), while Aluwax deviated far more than all three (mean difference ~0.77–0.80 vs. each, p<0.001 for every Aluwax comparison).

This is a 3-page journal-supplement in-vitro study with mock preparations only (no patient, no muscle/closure-force variability, no soft tissue), and the paper's own conclusion overstates a "best" material despite a nonsignificant difference among the top three — clinically, wax should be avoided for accurate vertical-relationship transfer, but the choice among the elastomeric/polyether options is not evidence-differentiated by this study.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/jpbs.jpbs_990_23); PMID 38595573; PMCID PMC11000910), this 3-page supplement study (J Pharm Bioallied Sci 2024;16(Suppl 1):S745-S747) asked a chairside-relevant question: how accurately do four interocclusal bite-registration materials reproduce the vertical relationship once a cast is demounted and remounted using the record? This is a bench (in-vitro) study, not a clinical trial — ideal maxillary and mandibular casts, with mock tooth preparations on teeth 46, 47, and 48, were mounted on a semi-adjustable articulator; a baseline (control) scan was taken with the casts in direct contact, then ten interocclusal records were made with each of four materials, the mandibular cast was demounted and remounted using each record, and the resulting position was rescanned and compared to control. Ten records per material (40 total) — n refers to records, not patients.

The four materials, by class (confirmed from the paper's Methods, not assumed from brand names alone): Group A Ramitec — polyether (3M ESPE); Group B CADbite — polyvinyl siloxane / addition silicone (Ivoclar Vivadent); Group C Jet Bite — polyvinyl siloxane / addition silicone (Coltene Whaledent); Group D Aluwax — wax (Dental Products Co., USA). All four produced a small deviation from the control (no-material) reading that reached statistical significance individually (Ramitec 0.136, CADbite 0.152, Jet Bite 0.165, Aluwax 0.939). **The paper does not state the unit of measurement** for these values; given the scanner-based vertical-dimension context they are plausibly millimeters, but this is not confirmed in the retrieved text and should be treated as unverified. Overall ANOVA across the five groups (control + 4 materials) was highly significant (F(4,45)=261.498, p<0.001). Tukey HSD pairwise comparisons showed the three elastomeric/polyether materials did not differ significantly from one another (Ramitec vs. CADbite p=0.990; Ramitec vs. Jet Bite p=0.905; CADbite vs. Jet Bite p=0.994), while Aluwax differed significantly from every other material and from control (mean differences ~0.77–0.94, all p<0.001).

The paper's own Discussion is internally inconsistent: it states "when the materials were compared within the group, the mean value was significant (0.005), but when the materials were compared between the groups, it showed an insignificant value (1.407)." These numbers are actually the ANOVA mean-square values from Table 1 (within-groups MS=0.005, between-groups MS=1.407), not p-values — and the ratio of those two mean squares is exactly what produces the highly significant F=261.498 result reported in the same table. Calling the between-groups result "insignificant" directly contradicts the paper's own Table 1. This reads as confused or mistyped wording rather than a real second analysis, and is flagged here rather than silently corrected, per this wiki's source-fidelity rule. The paper's Conclusions section states all four materials are "suitable for clinical use," that elastomeric materials were "superior," and that polyether "was found to be the best" — but this ranking rhetoric outruns the paper's own statistics: Ramitec, CADbite, and Jet Bite were **not** significantly different from each other (p=0.90–0.99), so "best" is not supported by the reported p-values. What the data do support is a class-level distinction — elastomer/polyether vs. wax — not a within-elastomer ranking.

## Key Contributions

- **First wiki page on bite-registration/interocclusal-record MATERIALS** — an everyday chairside question (which material to use for a bite registration) the wiki did not previously have a dedicated page on.
- **Quantifies deviation-from-control by material** with ANOVA + Tukey HSD: wax (Aluwax) deviates far more from the control reading than polyether or polyvinyl-siloxane/addition-silicone materials (mean difference ~0.77–0.94 vs. the smaller ~0.14–0.17 range for the elastomeric/polyether group; unit unstated in source).
- **Class-level, not brand-level, evidence**: within the elastomer/polyether group (Ramitec, CADbite, Jet Bite), no material was significantly more accurate than another (pairwise p=0.90–0.99) — the paper's "polyether was best" conclusion is not supported by its own pairwise statistics.
- **Confirms material-class identity for each brand from the Methods** (not inferred from brand name alone): Ramitec = polyether; CADbite and Jet Bite = polyvinyl siloxane / addition silicone; Aluwax = wax.

## Methodology

- **Design**: in-vitro (bench) comparative study; not a clinical trial. Byline institution: Dept. of Prosthodontics and Crown and Bridge, Dental Institute, RIMS, Ranchi, Jharkhand; the Methods text itself states the research was conducted at Hazaribag College of Dental Sciences and Hospital, Hazaribag, Jharkhand — the two are not reconciled in the retrieved text.
- **Sample**: ideal maxillary and mandibular casts, mock tooth preparation on teeth 46, 47, 48, mounted on a semi-adjustable articulator in maximum intercuspation. 4 groups × 10 interocclusal records = 40 records total. n = interocclusal records, **not patients** — there is no patient, muscle activity, closure-force variability, or soft tissue in this design.
- **Materials tested**: Group A Ramitec (polyether, 3M ESPE); Group B CADbite (polyvinyl siloxane, Ivoclar Vivadent); Group C Jet Bite (polyvinyl siloxane, Coltene Whaledent); Group D Aluwax (wax, Dental Products Co.).
- **Procedure**: baseline (control) scan of the casts in direct occlusal contact using a Medit T500 Dental Lab Scanner → interocclusal record made, with 50 lb of load applied to standardize seating pressure → mandibular cast demounted → remounted using the record → rescanned ("final reading") → deviation from control computed. Repeated 10× per material.
- **Statistics**: one-way ANOVA (5 groups: control + 4 materials) + Tukey HSD post-hoc pairwise comparisons, α=0.05.

## Results

| Comparison | Mean difference | 95% CI | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control vs. Ramitec (polyether) | −0.136 | −0.230 to −0.043 | 0.001 |
| Control vs. CADbite (PVS) | −0.152 | −0.245 to −0.059 | <0.001 |
| Control vs. Jet Bite (PVS) | −0.165 | −0.258 to −0.072 | <0.001 |
| Control vs. Aluwax (wax) | −0.939 | −1.032 to −0.846 | <0.001 |
| Ramitec vs. CADbite | −0.015 | −0.109 to 0.078 | 0.990 (ns) |
| Ramitec vs. Jet Bite | −0.029 | −0.122 to 0.065 | 0.905 (ns) |
| CADbite vs. Jet Bite | −0.013 | −0.107 to 0.080 | 0.994 (ns) |
| Ramitec vs. Aluwax | −0.802 | −0.896 to −0.709 | <0.001 |
| CADbite vs. Aluwax | −0.787 | −0.880 to −0.694 | <0.001 |
| Jet Bite vs. Aluwax | −0.774 | −0.867 to −0.681 | <0.001 |

Overall ANOVA (5 groups): F(4,45) = 261.498, p < 0.001. **Unit of measurement is not stated in the source text** (plausibly mm given the scanner/vertical-dimension context, but unconfirmed — treat as unverified).

- **Bottom line**: wax (Aluwax) is clearly and consistently less accurate at reproducing the vertical relationship than the three elastomeric/polyether materials (large, significant differences in every comparison). Among the elastomeric/polyether group, no material significantly outperformed another — the paper's claim that "polyether was found to be the best" is not supported by its own pairwise comparisons (p=0.90–0.99).
- All four materials produced a small but statistically detectable deviation from the true (control) vertical position, consistent with the general principle that no interocclusal material perfectly reproduces the unrecorded contact.
