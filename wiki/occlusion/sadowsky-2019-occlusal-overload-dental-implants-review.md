## Three-line Summary
Non-systematic narrative review of preclinical (monkey, dog) and clinical literature asking whether occlusal overload causes peri-implant bone loss and implant failure.

Animal studies conflict (Isidor: 6/8 overloaded monkey implants lost osseointegration; Heitz-Mayfield dogs: no marginal bone loss, BIC 72.6% vs 73.9%), while clinical data link bruxism to failure (OR 2.71; 5-year survival down to 72%) yet show high crown-to-implant ratios (2–3:1), cantilevers, and non-splinted designs perform as well as conventional alternatives.

Avoiding these designs based on the natural-tooth model is not evidence-based; preclinical research must establish patient-specific load thresholds (>4000 microstrain fatigue failure vs 1000–3000 microstrain anabolic window).

## Summary
Sadowsky's single-author narrative review (Int J Implant Dent 2019) surveys the preclinical and clinical literature on whether occlusal overload causes peri-implant bone loss. The animal evidence is conflicting: Isidor's monkey experiments showed loss of osseointegration under overload (6/8 implants), but the later dog studies (Heitz-Mayfield, Kozlovsky) found no marginal bone loss from supra-occluding crowns under plaque control, with bone-to-implant contact statistically unchanged or increased. Clinically, bruxism is the best-supported overload risk factor (OR 2.71 for failure; 5-year survival 72% in one cohort; mean bruxing bite force 827 ± 620 N), yet prosthetic designs long avoided for fear of overload — crown-to-implant ratios of 2–3:1, cantilevers (except arms ≥8 mm), and non-splinted restorations — show no bone-level disadvantage in long-term studies. The review's thesis is that tooth-model-derived prosthetic dogma, not evidence, has driven unnecessarily invasive planning, and that preclinical/FEA research must define patient-specific microstrain thresholds (>4000 μstrain fatigue failure; 1000–3000 μstrain anabolic window per Frost's mechanostat).

## Key Contributions
- Demonstrates the animal-model evidence on overload is conflicting (monkey studies positive, later dog studies negative) and that the biological fatigue threshold for peri-implant bone remains undefined.
- Quantifies the bruxism clinical link: 20–35.9% of patients generate microfracture-level forces; OR 2.71 for implant failure; 5-year retrospective survival 72%.
- Argues evidence does not support blanket avoidance of high crown-to-implant ratios (2–3:1), cantilevers, or non-splinted implant restorations — these biases are tooth-model dogma, not evidence.
- Places overload in Frost's mechanostat framework: >4000 microstrain fatigue-failure threshold vs 1000–3000 microstrain anabolic window, modulated by bone density (Lekholm & Zarb types 1–4).
- Identifies a quantitative overload anchor in Duyck's model (73.5 Ncm bending moment, 2520 cycles at 1 Hz → crater-like defects) that may explain why cantilever arms ≥8 mm correlate with marginal bone loss.

## Methodology
Non-systematic narrative review (author-stated limitation) of preclinical overload experiments (macaque and dog models) and clinical studies on bruxism, crown-to-implant ratio, cantilevers, and splinting (~63 references). No meta-analysis or formal search protocol. Controlled human overload trials are noted as ethically infeasible (Helsinki accords), so all causal inference rests on animal models and heterogeneous clinical cohorts.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Isidor monkey overload: osseointegration loss | 6/8 implants after 18 months |
| Miyata monkey superstructure-gap threshold | Bone resorption at ≥180 µm, 4 weeks |
| Heitz-Mayfield dog split-mouth BIC | 73.9% (overloaded) vs 72.6% (control), n.s.; no MBL |
| Kozlovsky dog supra-occlusion | No MBL; BIC increased without inflammation |
| Bruxism prevalence generating microfracture-level force | 20–35.9% |
| Implant failure OR, bruxers vs non-bruxers | 2.71 (98 bruxers + matched) |
| Bruxism 5-year implant survival (Chitumalla) | 90% (1y) → 85% (3y) → 72% (5y) |
| Mean bruxer bite force | 827 ± 620 N (Nishigawa ~790 N, 7.1 s contacts) |
| Implant tactile sensitivity vs teeth | ~8-fold lower |
| Fatigue-failure vs anabolic microstrain window | >4000 vs 1,000–3,000 μstrain |
| Successful crown-to-implant ratios | 2–3:1 (anabolic, not catabolic, bone response) |
| Cantilever arm length linked to MBL/peri-implantitis | ≥8 mm |
| Splinted vs non-splinted (Vigolo 10y split-mouth, n=44) | No crestal bone loss difference |
| Splinted vs single crowns (Naert, 644 implants, 16y) | 95.8% survival; no hazard-rate difference |
| Peri-implantitis burden among implant patients | Up to 20% |
