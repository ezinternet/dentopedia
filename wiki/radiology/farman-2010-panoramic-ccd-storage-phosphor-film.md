## Three-line Summary

Comparative evaluation of image-layer characteristics, dose requirements, and workflow convenience for four panoramic receptor types — conventional film, DenOptix storage phosphor, and two CCD sensors (Kodak OP100D and Trophy DigiPan) — during the film-to-digital transition.

OP100D CCD produced instant images with a wide focal trough (positioning-error tolerant) but at higher dose than film; DigiPan CCD achieved significant dose reduction with instant images but with a narrower focal trough prone to positioning errors; DenOptix storage phosphor gave high resolution but no dose saving and was slower.

No single receptor excelled on all parameters; receptor selection requires balancing focal-trough tolerance, dose, resolution, and workflow speed depending on the clinical setting and operator skill.

## 세줄요약

필름에서 디지털로의 전환기에 파노라마 수용체 4종 — 기존 필름, DenOptix 저장형광판(Storage Phosphor), Kodak OP100D CCD, Trophy DigiPan CCD — 의 초점층(Image Layer) 특성·선량·워크플로 편의성을 비교한 연구.

OP100D CCD: 즉각 영상·넓은 초점골(Focal Trough)(위치오류 허용) but 필름 대비 선량 높음; DigiPan CCD: 유의한 선량절감·즉각 영상 but 좁은 초점골(위치오류 취약); DenOptix 저장형광판: 고해상도 but 선량절감 없음·처리 느림.

단일 최우수 수용체 없음; 임상 환경·술자 숙련도에 따라 초점골 허용범위·선량·해상도·속도 균형을 고려해 수용체 선택 필요.

## Summary

Establishes the receptor-level trade-off between focal-trough width (positioning tolerance), dose, and workflow speed during the film-to-digital transition.

## Key Contributions

- OP100D CCD: instant image, wide focal trough (positioning-error tolerant) but higher dose than film.
- DigiPan CCD: significant dose reduction + instant image, but narrower trough (positioning-error prone).
- DenOptix storage phosphor: high resolution, reasonable trough width, but slower and no dose saving vs film.

## Methodology

Comparative evaluation of image-layer characteristics, dose requirements, and convenience across 4 receptors (film, storage phosphor, two CCD sensors) for panoramic radiography.

## Results

Establishes the receptor-level trade-off between focal-trough width (positioning tolerance), dose, and workflow speed during the film-to-digital transition.
