## Three-line Summary

In 20 bruxism subjects, T2-weighted IDEAL MRI maximum signal intensity in the periodontal ligament (PDL) space correlated significantly with a 7-item clinical occlusal trauma score (Spearman ρ = 0.529 all teeth; ρ = 0.517 anterior; ρ = 0.396 molars; p < 0.001). Signal intensity increased monotonically from score 0 (40.0 ± 5.9) to score 6 (73.4 ± 1.5), suggesting PDL edema as the imaging substrate. The study proposes IDEAL MRI as the first objective, non-ionizing, quantitative method for diagnosing occlusal trauma and detecting pre-clinical (early-stage) PDL edema.

## 세줄요약

이갈이 피험자 20명에서 T2 강조 IDEAL MRI 로 치주인대강(Periodontal Ligament, PDL) 최대 신호강도를 측정한 결과, 7항목 교합외상(Occlusal Trauma) 임상 점수와 유의한 양의 상관(ρ = 0.529, p < 0.001)을 보였다. 신호강도는 점수 0(40.0)에서 점수 6(73.4)까지 단조 증가하며 PDL 부종(edema)을 반영하였다. 임상 소견이 뚜렷하지 않은 초기 교합외상도 MRI 로 조기 탐지할 수 있음을 시사한 첫 번째 정량적 연구이다.

## Background

Occlusal trauma is defined as injury to the attachment apparatus from excessive occlusal forces (AAP Glossary 2001). Definitive histologic diagnosis requires biopsy; clinicians instead rely on surrogate markers: tooth mobility, fremitus, PDL space widening on radiograph, lamina dura changes, wear facets, and occlusal force measurements. These assessments are inherently qualitative and cannot quantify severity or detect sub-clinical states.

T2-weighted MRI detects tissue water (edema); it has been applied to osteomyelitis and TMJ effusion in the maxillofacial region. Edema of the PDL space during occlusal overload was reported histologically (Svanberg & Lindhe 1974) but never previously quantified using MRI signal intensity.

## Study Design

Cross-sectional observational; n = 20 (9M/11F; mean age 35.9 ± 14.0 yr; range 22–65 yr). Inclusion: subjective bruxism symptoms; PD < 4 mm with no BOP at any site; ≥ 20 teeth; normal occlusal relationship; no dental irregularities. MRI contraindications excluded. Ethics: Matsumoto Dental University No. 0138; Declaration of Helsinki.

## Clinical Occlusal Trauma Scoring

Seven binary items (each = 1 point): subjective bruxism symptoms; tooth mobility; fremitus; excessive occlusal contact area (Dental Prescale/Occluser); excessive occlusal force; PDL space widening on intraoral radiograph; lamina dura thickening. Total = 0–7 (higher = more severe).

## MRI Protocol

- Scanner: Signa HDx 1.5T (GE Healthcare)
- Coils: two 3-inch 2-channel surface coils, bilateral cheek placement
- Sequence: **T2-weighted IDEAL (Iterative Decomposition of water and fat with Echo Asymmetry and Least-squares estimation)** — water-selective imaging
- Parameters: TR 2,500 ms; TE 107 ms; matrix 256×256; FOV 24 cm; section thickness 5.0 mm; gap 1.0 mm; 4 min 33 s total
- Evaluation plane: parallel to occlusal plane at 12 mm root-ward from occlusal contact point
- Analysis: Photoshop CS3; PDL-equivalent pixel extraction at full root circumference; maximum grayscale value = maximum signal intensity

## Key Results

| Clinical Occlusal Trauma Score | Mean Max Signal Intensity (± SD) |
|---|---|
| 0 | 40.0 ± 5.9 |
| 1 | 46.7 ± 8.8 |
| 2 | 49.6 ± 9.9 |
| 3 | 53.3 ± 11.9 |
| 4 | 57.6 ± 12.0 |
| 5 | 65.7 ± 15.8 |
| 6 | 73.4 ± 1.5 |

**Spearman ρ (p < 0.001 for all):**
- All teeth: **ρ = 0.529**
- Anterior teeth: **ρ = 0.517**
- Molar teeth: **ρ = 0.396**

**MRI signal by tooth type:** Molars (53.0 ± 11.9) > anteriors (45.1 ± 9.7), consistent with higher habitual occlusal pressure on posterior teeth.

**Distribution of clinical scores:** All 20 subjects had ≥ 1 manifestation. Molars reached score 6; anteriors reached score 4. Molars showed more concentrated score 2–3 range.

## Interpretation

The monotonic increase in PDL signal intensity with clinical score confirms that T2-weighted IDEAL MRI detects PDL edema proportional to occlusal load severity. The moderate correlation for molars (ρ = 0.396) vs anteriors (ρ = 0.517) is attributed to higher variance at extreme molar scores, not absence of correlation.

**Critical finding — early detection:** Individual teeth whose signal intensity exceeded mean + 1 SD within their clinical score class showed elevated MRI signal despite minimal clinical manifestations. This is interpreted as pre-clinical PDL edema, suggesting that MRI can detect occlusal trauma **before** clinical signs become apparent — a capability not possible with conventional radiography, tooth mobility assessment, or articulating paper.

## Clinical Implications

1. **Quantification replaces qualitative assessment:** MRI signal intensity provides a continuous numeric measure of PDL edema, enabling comparison over time and thresholding for treatment decisions.
2. **No ionizing radiation:** Unlike intraoral radiographs and CBCT, MRI imposes no X-ray dose — relevant for longitudinal monitoring.
3. **Early intervention opportunity:** Teeth with MRI signal above reference thresholds but without clinical signs could justify prophylactic occlusal adjustment — a major shift from current principle (avoid removal of cooperative contacts without clear indication).
4. **Integration with occlusal analysis:** Should be validated alongside digital occlusal force systems (T-Scan, Dental Prescale) to correlate signal intensity with quantified force magnitude.

## Limitations

- Small sample (n = 20); too few teeth at score 5–7 for robust statistical sub-analysis
- Bruxism-only subjects → asymptomatic occlusal trauma not captured; clinical transferability uncertain
- Plaque status not assessed (plaque modifies PDL biology)
- Wide age range without age-stratified analysis
- MRI contraindicated in patients with: dental implants, orthodontic appliances, vascular stents, artificial joints, claustrophobia, pregnancy
- Signal-to-noise ratio not formally calculated
- Correlation with digital occlusal force device not performed

## Future Directions

Authors plan: larger sample with age stratification; longitudinal tracking of pre-clinical signal cases; SNR analysis; correlation with digital occlusal force measurement; cases with higher-severity trauma (score 6–7) including root fracture.
