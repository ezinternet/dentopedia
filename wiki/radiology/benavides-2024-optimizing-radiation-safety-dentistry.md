## Three-line Summary

ADA expert panel systematic review of 95 publications (2010–2022) synthesized evidence-based recommendations on dental radiation safety, imaging optimization, and regulatory compliance for all dental imaging modalities including CBCT.
Priority recommendations: digital sensors over film, rectangular collimation (>40% dose reduction), minimum-necessary FOV for CBCT, ALARA/ALADA principles; CBCT delivers 10–15× the dose of conventional radiography.
Clinicians must adhere to applicable federal/state regulations; CBCT should never replace lower-dose modalities when adequate diagnostic information can otherwise be obtained.

## 세줄요약

ADA 전문가 패널이 95편(2010–2022) 체계적 문헌고찰로 치과 방사선 안전·영상 최적화·규정 준수 권고안을 제시한 2024년 JADA 가이드라인.
최우선 권고: 디지털 센서, 직사각형 콜리메이션 (rectangular collimation) (40% 이상 선량 감소), CBCT 최소 시야각 (Field of View, FOV), 합리적 최저선량 원칙 (As Low As Reasonably Achievable, ALARA)/진단허용 최저선량 원칙 (As Low As Diagnostically Acceptable, ALADA) 준수; 콘빔 전산화단층촬영 (Cone-beam Computed Tomography, CBCT)은 기존 방사선의 10–15배 선량.
CBCT는 저선량 방법으로 동등 진단 불가능할 때만 처방; 모든 치과의사는 연방·주 규정(OSHA/FDA) 및 방사선방호 기준 준수 의무가 있다.

## Summary

This 2024 ADA evidence-based clinical guideline synthesizes 95 systematic reviews, organizational guidelines, and regulatory documents to provide updated recommendations on radiation safety in dentistry. The article updates the 2012 ADA/FDA publication on dental radiographic examinations, with a primary focus on digital imaging modalities. An expert panel including oral and maxillofacial radiologists, general/pediatric/public health dentists, a health physicist, and a patient representative developed priority recommendations to minimize ionizing radiation exposure while maintaining diagnostic quality. Key dose data show that a full-mouth series with rectangular collimation delivers 34.9 μSv (adult), while CBCT can deliver up to 68–1,073 μSv (large FOV, adult) — up to 10–15× conventional radiography. Rectangular collimation reduces dose by more than 40% consistently. CBCT should never be used routinely; the smallest FOV necessary should always be selected. CBCT dosimetry performance must be evaluated by a qualified expert at least every 2 years. Children require heightened consideration due to higher organ radiosensitivity and longer cumulative exposure lifespan.

## Key Contributions

- Comprehensive effective dose reference table for all dental modalities (intraoral, panoramic, cephalometric, CBCT) with background radiation equivalents
- Six priority recommendations explicitly designated for practice-level implementation
- Distinguishes regulatory requirements (OSHA, FDA, state programs) from professional guidance (ADA, NCRP, ICRP)
- FDA-cleared handheld device guidance: backscatter shield use, secure storage, staff training
- F-speed film = 60% dose reduction vs D-speed; rectangular collimation = >40% dose reduction
- CBCT QA/QC protocol: annual dosimetry evaluation preferred (mandatory ≥every 2 years)
- Pediatric-specific guidance: size/age/eruption stage must guide both prescription and dose optimization settings

### Priority Recommendations (Box 1)

1. **1.0.1** Adhere to all applicable federal, state, and local regulations
2. **3.1.1.0** Use digital sensors instead of film for intraoral imaging
3. **3.1.2** Use rectangular collimation whenever possible (>40% dose reduction)
4. **3.2.1** Never use CBCT routinely when lower-dose modality is adequate
5. **3.2.2** Use smallest necessary FOV for CBCT
6. **4.1.2** CBCT dosimetry/performance evaluated by qualified expert at least every 2 years

### Regulatory Framework

- **OSHA**: Occupational (staff) exposure; workplace environment standards
- **FDA**: Device performance requirements; handheld device clearance
- **State programs**: Installation/use of imaging equipment; training requirements
- **NCRP Report 177**: Dose estimates; D-speed film "shall not be used"
- **ICRP 103**: Effective dose methodology used in all dose comparisons
