## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + proportional meta-analysis of 40 CT/CBCT studies (17,714 patients; 31,973 hemi-mandibles) quantifying the prevalence, laterality, sex distribution, and dimensions of bifid mandibular canal (BMC).

Pooled patient-level BMC prevalence was 20.7% (95% CI 15.9–26%) and hemi-mandible-level prevalence 14.3% (95% CI 10.7–18.3%); bilateral symmetry occurred in 22.8% of BMC-positive patients; mean accessory-canal length was 12.14 mm and mean diameter 1.54 mm; prevalence was significantly higher in males and on the right side.

Clinically, roughly 1 in 5 CT-imaged patients and 1 in 7 hemi-mandibles harbor a BMC — relevant to inferior alveolar nerve block failure and implant/surgical injury risk — but very high heterogeneity (I² ≈ 99%), moderate overall risk of bias (JBI 54.69%), and detected funnel-plot asymmetry (publication bias) in the prevalence estimates temper precision.

통합 환자수준 BMC 유병률은 20.7%(95% CI 15.9–26%), 반측하악골수준 유병률은 14.3%(95% CI 10.7–18.3%)였고, BMC 양성 환자 중 22.8%가 양측성이었으며, 평균 부속관 길이는 12.14mm, 평균 직경은 1.54mm였다. 남성과 우측에서 유병률이 유의하게 높았다.

## Summary

This paper is a proportional (prevalence) meta-analysis synthesizing 40 CT/CBCT studies (17,714 patients; 31,973 hemi-mandibles; ages 6–103) to answer: what is the prevalence of bifid mandibular canal (BMC) among patients undergoing CT examination? BMC is an anatomic variant where the mandibular canal branches into two or more accessory canals — clinically relevant because it is linked to failed inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) block, and to neurovascular injury risk during third-molar extraction, endodontic treatment, implant placement, and osteotomy. The authors pooled patient-level and hemi-mandible-level prevalence separately (a distinction most prior narrative reviews blurred), quantified bilateral symmetry and accessory-canal dimensions, and ran subgroup analyses by sex, laterality, continent, and the three competing BMC classification systems (Naitoh's, Norje's, Langlais's). They also assessed methodological quality (JBI tool) and publication bias (Doi plot/LFK index, trim-and-fill).

## Key Contributions

- Separately pooled **patient-level prevalence** (20.7%, 95% CI 15.9–26%) and **hemi-mandible-level prevalence** (14.3%, 95% CI 10.7–18.3%) of BMC — a unit-of-analysis distinction prior reviews (e.g., Castro et al.) did not consistently make.
- Pooled **bilateral symmetry** (22.8% of BMC-positive patients) and pooled **dimensions** of the accessory canal: mean length 12.14 mm (95% CI 10.08–14.21 mm), mean diameter 1.54 mm (95% CI 1.27–1.82 mm).
- Demonstrated **classification-system-dependent prevalence**: Naitoh's classification (which uniquely includes a coronal-plane buccolingual canal subtype) yielded significantly higher prevalence than Norje's or Langlais's (hemi-mandible-level: 16.9% vs 11.3% vs 1.6%, p<0.00001) — showing that much of the literature's wide prevalence range is a measurement-definition artifact, not just population variation.
- Identified a **positive association between CBCT voxel size and hemi-mandible-level prevalence** (counter-intuitive, since finer voxels should improve detection) — flagged as an unexplained methodological finding for future work.
- Formal risk-of-bias (JBI 54.69%, moderate) and publication-bias (major funnel asymmetry in both prevalence outcomes) appraisal, largely absent from earlier BMC reviews.

## Methodology

Systematic search of PubMed, Google Scholar, ResearchGate, ProQuest, Scopus, and LILACS (inception–April 2022, no language/date restriction), combining BMC-terminology and CT/CBCT-terminology search strings ("1 AND 2"). Forty studies met eligibility for both JBI quality appraisal and quantitative synthesis. Extracted variables: patient/hemi-mandible counts (with/without BMC), bilateral symmetry, mean length/diameter of accessory canals (with SD), sex, geography, classification system, CBCT voxel size/FOV/mA/kVp, and reliability-testing status. Proportions meta-analyzed in MetaXL (random-effects); continuous outcomes (length, diameter) in Meta-Essentials via generic inverse-variance random-effects. Heterogeneity quantified by I²; subgroup analyses by sex, laterality, continent, and classification system; sensitivity analysis restricted to studies with documented inter/intra-examiner reliability testing; moderator (meta-regression) analysis of CBCT voxel size vs. prevalence. Publication bias assessed via funnel plot, Doi plot, and LFK index (with trim-and-fill for the diameter outcome).

## Results

- **Patient-level BMC prevalence**: 20.7% (95% CI 15.9–26%; range 1–67%; I²=99%), 36 studies, n=17,239.
- **Hemi-mandible-level BMC prevalence**: 14.3% (95% CI 10.7–18.3%; range 1–46%; I²=99%), 38 studies, n=31,603.
- **Bilateral symmetry**: 22.8% (95% CI 16.3–30%) of BMC-positive patients, 29 studies, n=2,416.
- **Dimensions**: mean length 12.14 mm (95% CI 10.08–14.21 mm; range across studies 7.1–16.9 mm); mean diameter 1.54 mm (95% CI 1.27–1.82 mm; range 0.9–2.28 mm).
- **Sex**: male 22.6% vs female 18.9% patient-level prevalence (chi-square p=0.000074) — BMC more common in males.
- **Laterality**: right 14% vs left 12.6% hemi-mandible-level prevalence (p=0.023) — slight right-side predominance.
- **Continent** (patient-level, high→low): Europe 26.5% > Asia 18.8% > America 13.9% (no African/Australian patient-level data). (Hemi-mandible-level): Africa 32.8% (only 2 Egyptian studies, low generalizability) > Europe 17.2% > Asia 13.3% > America 7.8%.
- **Classification system**: Naitoh's classification yielded significantly higher prevalence than Norje's/Langlais's at both patient level (23.9% vs 17.7% vs 2.9%) and hemi-mandible level (16.9% vs 11.3% vs 1.6%), p<0.00001, attributed to Naitoh's inclusion of the coronal-section buccolingual-canal subtype.
- **Naitoh subtype breakdown** (hemi-mandible level): retromolar canal 6.2% > forward canal 4.7% > dental canal 2.8% > inferior bifid canal 2.2% > buccolingual canal 0.8%; 8.5% of accessory canals rejoined the main canal (confluent type).
- **Sensitivity analysis**: excluding studies without reliability testing modestly increased both prevalence estimates (patient-level 21.8%, hemi-mandible-level 15.3%), suggesting the pooled overall estimate may be a slight underestimate.
- **Moderator analysis**: no significant effect of CBCT voxel size on patient-level prevalence, but a significant positive association with hemi-mandible-level prevalence (unexplained).
- **Quality/bias**: overall mean JBI score 54.69% (moderate risk of bias); major funnel-plot asymmetry (publication bias) for both prevalence outcomes (LFK 2.66, 2.78); no asymmetry for bilateral symmetry.

**Clinical implication / limitation**: Roughly 1 in 5 CT-imaged patients and 1 in 7 hemi-mandibles carry a BMC, reinforcing the need for pre-surgical 3D imaging review before mandibular anesthesia, third-molar extraction, implant placement, or osteotomy — particularly in males and on the right side. However, extremely high heterogeneity (I²≈98–99%), moderate risk of bias, and detected publication bias mean the pooled point estimates should be read as a wide plausible range rather than a precise population parameter; this SR+MA is CT/CBCT-only and does not itself resolve how these figures compare against MRI (the soft-tissue reference standard).

## Related Papers

- [[wiki/radiology/wamasing-2018-imaging-bifid-mandibular-canal]] — single-center, MRI-based descriptive study (n=154 patients, BMC 6.4% of hemi-mandibles) using direct neurovascular-bundle visualization. This SR+MA's CT/CBCT-pooled prevalence (14.3% hemi-mandible-level) is notably higher than wamasing-2018's MRI-detected figure — consistent with wamasing's own finding that MRI-confirmed BMCs were largely invisible on CT/panoramic imaging, i.e., the two papers use different reference-standard modalities and are complementary rather than directly comparable; extends wamasing-2018's single-center MRI finding with a much larger, multi-continent CT/CBCT evidence base and formal quality/bias appraisal.
