## Three-line Summary

Retrospective audit of 1,782 digital panoramic radiographs (Strato 2000D; Indian single dental college, Jan 2007–Mar 2010), grading 9 positioning-error categories and overall diagnostic quality (excellent / diagnostically acceptable / unacceptable).

89% of images carried ≥1 positioning error and only 11.0% were error-free; the most common error was failure to position the tongue against the palate (55.7%) and the least common was patient movement during exposure (1.6%); 24.9% were diagnostically unacceptable (exceeding the ≤10% benchmark) and 64.1% acceptable.

Positioning errors are operator-side and largely preventable through careful patient positioning and surgeon–patient communication; each repeat carries a cancer risk estimated at 0.21–1.9 cases per million examinations.

## Summary

This retrospective audit examined 1,782 digital panoramic radiographs (Strato 2000D, adults ≥18 y, single Indian dental college, Jan 2007–Mar 2010) for nine positioning-error categories and for overall diagnostic quality. Positioning errors were the rule: 89% of images had ≥1 positioning error, only 11.0% were error-free, and 24.9% were diagnostically unacceptable — well above the ≤10% guideline benchmark and far from the ≥70% "excellent" target. Failure to position the tongue against the palate was the most common single error (55.7%), and patient movement the least common (1.6%). Because the images came from a digital machine, processing artifacts did not contribute — the shortfall is attributable to patient positioning and operator–patient communication, and every non-diagnostic image that is repeated carries an estimated cancer risk of 0.21–1.9 cases per million examinations. The unacceptable rate sits between two landmark audits (Brezden & Brooks 18.2%; Rushton et al. 33%), differences the authors attribute to sampling frame and technician training.

## Key Contributions

- **Prevalence anchor (older, single-center)**: 89% of panoramics carried ≥1 positioning error; 24.9% diagnostically unacceptable — a replicated finding alongside the newer Riyadh audit.
- **Separated forward vs backward positioning**: forward 18.3%, backward 30.0% — where Rushton et al. had combined them (58.8%).
- **Error ranking table**: a usable 9-item checklist mapping each positioning error to its radiographic manifestation (tongue 55.7% > slumped 35.0% > backward 30.0% > forward 18.3% > chin high 17.9% > head turned 17.4% > chin low 16.2% > head tilted 12.7% > movement 1.6%).
- **Radiation-protection framing**: repetition of panoramic radiography carries a cancer risk of 0.21 or 1.9 cases per million examinations.

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective audit of archived radiographs (inactive patient files).
- **n**: 1,782 panoramics (Jan 2007–Mar 2010); adults ≥18 y; pediatric and edentulous patients excluded; first image only when multiple taken; repeated images excluded.
- **Equipment**: Strato 2000D (Villa Sistemi Medicali), 50–80 kVp, 4–10 mA, 15 s, magnification 1.23; 2 radiographers (>3 y experience); digital images viewed on a Dell 15-inch 1280×800 monitor in subdued lighting.
- **Assessment**: one OMF radiology professor (>12 y) scored 9 positioning errors and 3-point quality (excellent/acceptable/unacceptable); 25% random subsample (446 images) re-rated after 3 months; SPSS 15.0; kappa + % agreement (Altman grading).

## Results

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Error-free ("excellent") | 196/1,782 = **11.0%** |
| ≥1 positioning error | 1,586/1,782 = **89.0%** |
| Most common error | Tongue not against palate — 993 (**55.7%**) |
| Least common error | Patient movement during exposure — 28 (**1.6%**) |
| Slumped position | 624 (**35.0%**) |
| Patient positioned backward | 534 (**30.0%**) |
| Patient positioned forward | 326 (**18.3%**) |
| Chin tipped high / head turned / chin low / head tilted | 17.9% / 17.4% / 16.2% / 12.7% |
| Diagnostically acceptable | 1,142 (**64.1%**) |
| **Diagnostically unacceptable** | 444 (**24.9%**) — benchmark ≤10%, not met |
| Intra-examiner reliability (positioning) | kappa 0.77, 92% agreement |
| Intra-examiner reliability (quality) | kappa 0.63, 83% agreement |
| Cancer risk from repeat panoramic | 0.21–1.9 cases per million examinations |

**Contextual anchors from the paper**: unacceptable rate 24.9% sits between Brezden & Brooks (18.2%) and Rushton et al. (33%). Rushton's tongue-error rate exceeded this study's 55.7%, and its combined forward+backward errors (58.8%) exceeded this study's 48.3% split.
