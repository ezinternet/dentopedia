## Three-line Summary

Teaching-oriented narrative review (2015) of basic dentomaxillofacial imaging: panoramic radiography (PTG) technique/anatomy/errors and cone beam CT (CBCT) technology, dose, artifacts, and specialty indications.

Panoramic + intraoral radiographs remain the baseline 2D modalities; CBCT adds accurate 3D hard-tissue imaging between them and MSCT in dose (dentoalveolar CBCT 11–674 µSv median 61; craniofacial 30–1,073 µSv median 87; MSCT 280–1,410 µSv).

CBCT's disadvantages are poor soft-tissue contrast and artifacts; use only in justified demanding cases with the smallest field of view, per ALARA.

## 세줄요약

구강악안면방사선 교육 리뷰(2015) — 파노라마(PTG) 촬영법·정상 해부·positioning 오류와 CBCT(콘빔CT, Cone Beam Computed Tomography) 기술·선량·인공물·전문과목별 적응증을 통합 고찰.

파노라마+구내방사선이 기본 2D 기법이고, CBCT는 MSCT(다중슬라이스CT)보다 낮은 유효선량으로 정확한 3D 경조직 영상을 제공(치조 CBCT 11–674 µSv 중앙값 61, 두개안면 30–1,073 µSv 중앙값 87 vs MSCT 280–1,410 µSv).

CBCT는 연조직 대조도 저하·인공물이 단점 — ALARA 원칙하에 2D로 답이 안 되는 정당화된 경우에만, 과제에 맞는 최소 FOV(Field of View, 시야)로 사용해야 한다.

## Summary

This 2015 teaching-oriented narrative review establishes panoramic and intraoral radiographs as the basic 2D imaging methods in dentomaxillofacial radiology and positions cone beam CT as the tool for more demanding cases requiring accurate 3D hard-tissue imaging. It details panoramic tomography's narrow-beam image formation, normal anatomy (36 labeled structures, FDI numbering, ghost shadows) and common positioning errors, then covers CBCT devices, acquisition, radiation dose versus MSCT, artifacts, and the Sedentexct (EC 2012 RP 172) specialty-specific indications. Its clinical bottom line — 2D first, CBCT justified only when 2D cannot answer the question, smallest FOV per ALARA — remains current and is reinforced by the newer CBCT-only reviews.

## Key Contributions
- Panoramic tomography primer: ~8° upward narrow-beam rotational technique, horseshoe image layer (IL)/central plane (CP), and the positioning error catalog (posteroanterior, horizontal, vertical, ghost-shadow, air-shadow) with image consequences.
- Systematic PTG normal-anatomy interpretation framework including ghost-shadow recognition and FDI two-digit tooth numbering.
- Consolidated effective-dose ladder for conventional dental radiography, CBCT (dentoalveolar vs craniofacial, medians) and MSCT.
- Specialty-by-specialty CBCT indications per Sedentexct evidence-based guidelines: implants, third molars/tooth localisation, endodontics, facial fractures, TMJ, cleft palate (SABG), orthodontics, sinus and airway.

## Methodology
Narrative review with clinical case examples; anchored on the Sedentexct/European Commission Radiation Protection No. 172 (2012) evidence-based CBCT guidelines and EADMFR consensus. Primary data cited include Kaeppler et al. 2014 (fracture decision impact, 9.5%) and Mota de Almeida et al. 2014 (endodontic decision efficacy). n/a (review).

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Intraoral dose | <1.5 µSv |
| Panoramic dose | 2.7–24.3 µSv |
| Cephalometric dose | <6 µSv |
| Dentoalveolar CBCT (FOV height <10 cm) | 11–674 µSv, median 61 |
| Craniofacial CBCT (FOV height >10 cm) | 30–1,073 µSv, median 87 |
| MSCT maxillo-mandibular | 280–1,410 µSv |
| DRL available | 250 mGy·cm² (upper first molar implant, adults) |
| CBCT acquisition / voxel | 6–20 s, 180–360° rotation; isotropic voxels down to 0.076 mm |
| Implant rule (EAO/ICOI) | no extra imaging if clinical + 2D suffice; CBCT for measurements, ridge topography, vital structures, surgical guides |
| Mandibular fracture | CBCT changed treatment plan in 9.5% (Kaeppler 2014) |
| TMJ | accurate for osseous abnormalities, but no routine use recommended |
| Orthodontics | no high-quality evidence of benefit; large FOV not routine |
| Endodontics | significant impact on therapeutic decisions (Mota de Almeida 2014, per Sedentexct) |
