## Three-line Summary

Retrospective analysis of 4051 cone beam CT (CBCT) scans (2306 females, 1745 males, ages 7-88) from the University of Michigan School of Dentistry, evaluating the prevalence, location, and anatomical course of accessory canals (ACs) in the maxilla and mandible.

Accessory canals were identified in 1737 scans overall (42.9%; 1004 females, 733 males) — 532 in the maxilla (13.1%) and 1205 in the mandible (29.8%) — revealing a network of accessory canals connecting the inner and outer cortical plates of the jaws.

## Summary

This retrospective study analyzed 4200 successive CBCT scans at the University of Michigan School of Dentistry (Ann Arbor, MI), across a wide age range (7-88 years) and both genders, to determine the prevalence, location, and anatomical course of accessory canals (ACs) — also referred to in the literature as nutrient canals — throughout the jaws. After applying exclusion criteria (severe ridge resorption, pre-existing implants, prior craniofacial malformation/syndrome history, prior trauma/surgery, inadequate image quality, and repeat scans from the same patient), 4051 scans qualified for analysis (2306 females, 1745 males). Accessory canals were found in 1737 scans (42.9%), split between 532 maxillary cases (13.1%) and 1205 mandibular cases (29.8%) — the mandible showing more than double the prevalence of the maxilla. The authors describe a network of accessory canals that brings the inner and outer cortical plates of the jaws into communication, and conclude that clinicians should carefully assess for these canals prior to surgical intervention to reduce the risk of vascular/neurovascular complications.

## Key Contributions

- Largest sample size in the wiki's accessory/nutrient canal literature (n=4051 qualifying CBCT scans out of 4200 screened), by roughly an order of magnitude versus the mandibular-anterior IOPAR series ([[radiology/kaur-2019-evaluation-mandibular-anterior-nutrient]] n=600, [[radiology/hasan-2022-prevalence-nutrient-canals-mandibular]] n=200).
- First CBCT-based (3D, whole-jaw) prevalence figure in the wiki for accessory canals spanning both the maxilla and the mandible, rather than a single 2D-radiograph region (mandibular anterior only) or a single high-resolution modality applied to a small cohort ([[radiology/wamasing-2018-imaging-bifid-mandibular-canal]], n=154).
- Reports a clear anatomic gradient: mandible (29.8%) more than double the maxilla (13.1%) in accessory-canal prevalence.
- Frames the clinical takeaway explicitly around presurgical risk assessment — the identified network connects inner/outer cortical plates, which is directly relevant to implant osteotomy and other surgical planning.

## Methodology

Retrospective analysis of 4200 successive CBCT scans, University of Michigan School of Dentistry, Ann Arbor, MI, patients aged 7-88 years, both genders. Exclusion criteria applied: severe ridge resorption, pre-existing implants, previously reported history of craniofacial malformations/syndromes, previous history of trauma or surgery, inadequate image quality, and subsequent (repeat) scans from the same individual. 4051 scans (2306 females, 1745 males) qualified for inclusion. Scans were reviewed for the presence, anatomical location (maxilla vs mandible), and course of accessory canals. Further protocol detail (CBCT unit/settings, voxel resolution, number/training of readers, interobserver reliability, statistical tests) is not available from the abstract (full text not retrieved).

## Results

- Overall accessory canal (AC) prevalence: 1737/4051 scans (42.9%) — 1004 females, 733 males.
- Maxillary AC: 532 scans (13.1% of total) — 296 females, 236 males.
- Mandibular AC: 1205 scans (29.8% of total) — 708 females, 497 males.
- Mandible prevalence (29.8%) more than double maxilla prevalence (13.1%).
- A network of accessory canals connecting the inner and outer cortical plates of the jaws was identified, supporting the abstract's clinical recommendation to assess for accessory canals before surgical intervention.
- No p-values or inferential statistics are reported in the available abstract — figures are descriptive prevalence only.
