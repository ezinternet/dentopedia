## Three-line Summary

Prospective quality-assessment study of 127 single-tooth radiographs taken with a handheld Nomad Pro 2 in German nursing-home domiciliary dental care, graded against German Federal Dental Association criteria by 3 dentists of varying experience.

80% showed no quality defects; periapical bone was diagnostic in 86%; field of view was adequate in 84%; unexpected clinically important secondary findings appeared in 61% of images.

Handheld mobile radiography achieves image quality comparable to conventional wall-mounted units, supporting its use for domiciliary dental care and the legal justification of surgical procedures in nursing-home patients.

## Summary

This prospective study evaluated the image quality and clinical benefit of a handheld portable dental X-ray device (Nomad Pro2) used in domiciliary/nursing-home dental care for patients who cannot attend a conventional dental practice. 127 single-tooth radiographs, all taken by one experienced dentist, were graded independently by 3 dentists of differing experience levels against the German Federal Dental Association's standard quality-criteria catalogue. Overall, 80% of radiographs showed no quality defects, periapical bone structure was diagnostic in 86%, and field of view was adequate for the justifying indication in 84% — results comparable to, and on several metrics better than, previously published benchmarks for conventional wall-mounted units. Motion artefacts were rare (4%) despite the anticipated difficulty of imaging limited-compliance nursing-home patients. Critically, 61% of radiographs revealed unexpected clinically important secondary findings (e.g., apical osteolysis, root remnants) — findings that would otherwise go undetected in a population that cannot access conventional radiography, and that carry legal significance since surgical procedures performed without radiographic justification are legally contestable in Germany. The authors conclude handheld mobile radiography meets diagnostic quality requirements and supports domiciliary dental care.

## Key Contributions

- First quality-criteria-based (not dose-based) evaluation of a handheld device specifically in the domiciliary/nursing-home context that motivates portable radiography's existence.
- Demonstrates diagnostic image quality comparable to, and on some metrics exceeding, conventional wall-mounted units and published stationary/inpatient benchmarks.
- Quantifies clinical yield: 61% secondary/incidental-finding rate shows portable radiography closes a diagnostic gap for immobile patients, with direct legal/ethical implications for justifying surgical procedures.
- Multi-rater design (oral & maxillofacial surgeon, oral surgeon, second-year trainee) controls for evaluator-experience bias.

## Methodology

- **Device**: Nomad Pro2 (Dexis/KaVo), 60 kV / 2.5 mA, meeting German minimum specifications (≥60 kV, ≥5 line pairs/mm resolution, ≥200 mm focus-to-tube-end distance); shorter exposure time than stationary units (0.12 s anterior / 0.16 s molar vs. 0.25 s / 0.32 s).
- **Sample**: 127 single-tooth radiographs from routine domiciliary dental care in a retirement/nursing-home setting; indications were root remnants (surgical), caries, periodontitis, and implantology.
- **Consent**: written informed consent from patient or legal guardian/next of kin; images anonymised.
- **Raters**: 3 dentists of varying specialisation/experience, assessing independently against the German Federal Dental Association quality-criteria catalogue (beam centring, PSP defects, foreign-body/overlap, contrast/sharpness; osseous structures: alveolar border, interradicular septum, PDL, periapical bone).
- **Comparator**: descriptive comparison to a published 2012 wall-mounted-device study and other stationary/inpatient benchmarks (not a within-patient head-to-head).
- **Ethics**: Ethics Committee of University Ulm (no. 149/09), per Declaration of Helsinki; no external funding, no conflicts of interest declared.

## Results

- **Overall quality**: 101/127 (80%) no qualitative limitations.
- **Periapical bone structure**: diagnostic in 109/127 (86%); unassessable in <4%.
- **Field of view adequate for justifying indication**: 107/127 (84%); inadequate in 20/127 (16%).
- **Secondary/incidental findings**: 78/127 (61%), e.g. apical osteolysis, root remnants.
- **Specific defects (of all 127 images)**: overexposure 9% (n=11), superimposition 3% (n=4), PSP not aligned occlusally 43% (n=54), PSP quality issues 47% (n=60), beam center not centred 28% (n=36), motion artefacts 4% (n=5), foreign-body superimposition 0%, angulation too steep 1% (n=1).
- **Vs. stationary benchmarks**: 83% (anterior/premolar/molar combined) showed no diagnostically relevant defects; German quality criteria met in >96% (vs. 83% reported in non-mobile environments); field-of-view adequacy 84% (vs. 86% non-mobile); overall qualitative defect rate 16% (vs. 17% stationary-condition rate reported elsewhere).
- **Motion artefacts**: only 4%, despite anticipated compliance difficulty; no evidence handheld use increases motion artefacts vs. wall-mounted units — shorter exposure time may even lower the risk.
- Bottom line: handheld mobile radiography achieved image quality comparable to conventional wall-mounted intraoral units, supporting its clinical adequacy for domiciliary dental care and legal justification of surgical procedures in nursing-home patients.

## Glossary

- **Nomad Pro2**: CE-marked handheld, battery-powered intraoral dental X-ray unit (Dexis/KaVo) with internal backscatter radiation shield.
- **PSP (Phosphor Storage Plate)**: reusable digital detector plate; misalignment/wear is a common defect source.
- **Domiciliary dental care**: on-site dental treatment for patients unable to attend a conventional practice (e.g., nursing-home residents).
- **Justifying indication**: the clinical reason a radiograph must adequately address (field of view/content) to be considered diagnostically adequate.
