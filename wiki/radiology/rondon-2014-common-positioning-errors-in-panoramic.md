## Three-line Summary
Narrative literature review (PubMed search, 2014) cataloguing the most common patient-positioning errors in panoramic radiography with each error's image consequence and correction.
Patient positioning is the most frequent panoramic error class; re-examination causes cited are head turned right/left (24.84%), head in front of the focal trough (21.15%), head tilted forward (21.21%), and head behind the focal trough (20.30%); correct setup requires the occlusal plane at -20° to -30° from horizontal and the tongue against the palate.
Lets operators recognize and correct each misposition (flattened/reversed occlusal curve, tooth overlap, condyle signs, ghost images) to avoid repeat exposures and unnecessary radiation.

## Summary
A narrative review of the literature (PubMed-only search) cataloguing the most common patient-positioning errors in panoramic radiography and their radiographic consequences, so that operators can recognize and correct them and avoid repeat exposures. The review states that patient positioning is the single most frequent class of panoramic error and collects re-examination proportions for head mispositioning (21.15% in front of the focal trough, 24.84% turned right/left, 21.21% tilted forward, 20.30% behind). It explains the geometric basis — a limited-dimension focal layer with 10–60-fold more sensitive screen-film — by which minor positioning deviations produce unequal vertical/horizontal magnification, tooth overlap, blurring, and ghost images. Each error type (head antero-posterior, tilt/turn, chin height, tongue, movement) is tied to a specific image signature and to corrective technique. Clinical implication: reproducible panoramic quality depends on operator training and disciplined patient positioning, and recognizing these errors reduces unnecessary radiation.

## Key Contributions
- Systematic (by-axis) classification of panoramic positioning errors: focal-trough antero-posterior, lateral tilt/turn, chin elevation/depression, tongue position, posture/movement — each with a distinct image consequence.
- Reference frequency data for re-examination causes due to head misposition (24.84% turned; 21.15% anterior; 21.21% forward tilt; 20.30% posterior).
- Chairside recognition checklist (occlusal-plane curvature, hyoid overlap, condyle displacement, palatoglossal air space, ghost images) to distinguish and correct errors.

## Methodology
Narrative review identified via a PubMed database search for "radiographic positioning errors" and "panoramic radiographs"; it included reviews and case reports. No systematic protocol, no explicit inclusion criteria, and no quantitative synthesis were reported; error-frequency figures are cited second-hand from empirical studies (e.g., Dhillon et al 2012, Kaviani et al 2008). Outcomes are the classification of positioning errors, their image signatures, and corrective techniques.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Most common panoramic error classes (decreasing) | positioning → exposure factors → artifact appearance → technical performance |
| Re-exam cause: head turned right/left | 24.84% |
| Re-exam cause: head in front of focal trough | 21.15% |
| Re-exam cause: head tilted forward | 21.21% |
| Re-exam cause: head behind focal trough | 20.30% |
| Correct occlusal-plane angulation | -20° to -30° from horizontal |
| Panoramic screen-film sensitivity | 10–60-fold more sensitive than fluoroscopic X-ray film |
| Head in front of focus → | anterior arches blurred, shortened, narrowed; premolar + ramus overlap |
| Head behind focus → | anterior teeth blurred, horizontally expanded; condyles at lateral edges |
| Head tilted back → | occlusal plane flat/reversed; upper incisor apices out of focus; condyles out (increased intercondylar distance) |
| Head tilted forward → | occlusal plane excessively curved; lower incisor apices out of focus; hyoid overlap; narrower intercondylar distance |
| Lateral tilt | asymmetric structures; slope-side reduced; proximal overlap |
| Head turn | teeth extended one side/shortened other; unequal rami and condyles |
| Chin elevated | flat/inverted occlusal plane; palate shadow over maxillary roots; wider mandible |
| Chin depressed | tooth overlap; symphysis may vanish; condyles out of upper edge; shorter mandible |
| Tongue/lips error | radiolucent band at upper-tooth apices; palatoglossal air space obscures maxillary apices |
| Movement / posture | ghost image — blurred zones and step defects at inferior mandibular border |
