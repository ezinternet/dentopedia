## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional audit of 2,629 digital panoramic radiographs (Orthophos XG; Riyadh, Aug 2020–Jun 2021), grading 10 positioning error categories and overall diagnostic quality in all-age patients.

Only 32.8% of images were error-free; 77.2% had ≥1 positioning error; ghost/reflected images (error 10) were the least frequent error (~2.2%) but significantly concentrated in 5–12-year-old children (P=0.001), and 15% of images were diagnostically unacceptable — exceeding the ≤10% benchmark.

The large shortfall in image quality (15% unacceptable vs target ≤10%; only 32.1% excellent vs target ≥70%) reflects a training and positioning problem, not hardware; ghost images in children are most often caused by thyroid collars or lead aprons acting as ghost-image sources.

## 세줄요약

사우디 리야드 단일기관에서 2020년 8월–2021년 6월 촬영된 디지털 파노라마(OPG, Orthopantomogram) 2,629장에 대해 10가지 자세 오류(Positioning Error) 및 전반적 진단 화질을 평가한 단면 감사 연구.

무오류 영상은 32.8%뿐이고 77.2%에 자세 오류가 1개 이상 있었으며, 유령상(Ghost Image, 오류 10)은 전체 중 가장 드물지만(~2.2%) 5–12세 소아에서 유의하게 집중(P=0.001)됐고, 진단 불가 영상이 15%로 권고 기준(≤10%)을 초과.

화질 불량은 하드웨어가 아닌 자세 교육 문제이며, 소아 유령상은 주로 갑상선 보호대나 방호복이 방사선원과 회전 중심 사이에 위치해 발생한다.

## Summary

This large single-institution audit graded 2,629 orthopantomograms for ten categories of positioning error and for overall diagnostic quality. Positioning errors were the rule rather than the exception: only 32.8% of images were error-free, and 15% were diagnostically unacceptable — exceeding the National Radiological Protection Board benchmark that unacceptable images stay below 10% and that excellent images exceed 70%. For the wiki's ghost-image thread, the most useful contribution is a **prevalence** number: reflected **ghost images (error 10) were the least common single error (~2.2%)**, but they were significantly age-dependent, concentrated in 5–12-year-old children (P=0.001) — the paper illustrates a **thyroid collar appearing as a pediatric ghost image**. The stated mechanism matches the wiki's other ghost pages: a ghost image forms when a radiopaque object (jewelry, hair clip, lead apron, thyroid collar, or a dense bony structure) lies **between the X-ray source and the machine's center of rotation**, projecting a magnified, blurred, contralateral artifact that can be mistaken for a radio-opaque disease entity. Interexaminer agreement was near-perfect (kappa = 0.998).

## Key Contributions

- **Prevalence anchor for ghost images**: ~2.2% of clinical panoramics — rare, but real — complementing the mechanism/case-report literature which never quantified frequency.
- **Age dependence**: ghost images (error 10) significantly more frequent in 5–12-year-old children (P=0.001), plausibly from thyroid-collar/lead-apron and accessory positioning.
- **Overall quality shortfall**: 15% diagnostically unacceptable (target ≤10%), 32.1% excellent (target ≥70%) — a training-and-positioning problem, not a hardware one (digital imaging removes processing errors).
- **Error taxonomy**: a usable 10-error checklist (Table 1) mapping each error to its identifying radiographic feature and its positioning cause.

## Methodology

- **Design**: cross-sectional radiographic quality audit; 2,629 OPGs selected from 3,900 new patients (Aug 2020 – Jun 2021), all ages.
- **Device**: Sirona Orthophos XG digital panoramic; two technicians (matched 8-yr experience).
- **Assessment**: two radiology specialists (>15 yr) scored 10 error types and a 3-point NRPB-consistent quality scale (Excellent / Diagnostically acceptable / Unacceptable); interobserver reliability re-tested after 1 month.
- **Exclusions**: developmental abnormality, maxillofacial fracture, orthognathic surgery, tumors/cysts; first image only when repeats existed.
- **Stats**: SPSS v25.0, frequency test, kappa statistic.

## Results

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Error-free (excellent) | 32.8% (863/2,629) |
| ≥1 positioning error | 77.2% (1,766) |
| Most common error | Error 8 — tongue not against palate (avg 17.9%) |
| **Least common error** | **Error 10 — ghost/reflected images (~2.2%)** |
| Diagnostically unacceptable | 15.08% (benchmark ≤10%) |
| Excellent (quality grade) | 32.1% (target ≥70%) |
| Interexaminer agreement | kappa = 0.998 |

**Age-significant errors**: Error 3 (head tilted down) P=0.024; Error 8 (tongue) P=0.027; **Error 10 (ghost) P=0.001** — ghost cases ~48% in 5–12-year-olds. Errors 1–9 predominate in 19–59-year-olds. No gender difference.

**Ghost-image formation (as stated)**: a radiopaque object between the X-ray source and the center of rotation, dense enough to attenuate the beam, projects a magnified reflected image to the contralateral side. Typical culprits: jewelry, hair clips, lead apron, thyroid collar, dense bony structures. Unilateral jewelry can be misread as a radio-opaque disease entity (Figure 1: thyroid-collar ghost in a child).
