## Three-line Summary
Systematic review (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42024588224) of 29 in vivo studies (982 trauma patients, 0.5–83 y, published 1991–2023) evaluating MRI for dental, oral, and maxillofacial trauma: 12 orbital, 10 condylar/subcondylar/TMJ, 5 mandibular, 1 temporal bone, 1 dental trauma; 59% prospective, 79% comparative; field strengths 0.23–3 T, acquisition 6–53 min.
MRI matches CT sensitivity for orbital floor fractures and detects inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) lesions in >90% of cases (confirmed intraoperatively), assesses TMJ disc status in ~90% of cases, and CT-like MRI classifies mandibular fractures with inter/intra-reader agreement κ 0.92–0.98.
MRI is a promising radiation-free adjunct (ALADAIP) for younger, radio-sensitive patients but remains complementary — not a standalone replacement for CT/CBCT in acute trauma — due to inferior depiction of fine bone detail such as microfractures.

## Summary
This systematic review (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42024588224) evaluated in vivo MRI for dental, oral, and maxillofacial trauma across 29 studies and 982 patients published between 1991 and 2023. Twelve studies concerned orbital trauma, ten condylar/subcondylar or TMJ trauma, five mandibular fractures, and one each temporal bone and dental trauma, at field strengths of 0.23–3 T with acquisition times of 6–53 min. MRI matched CT sensitivity for orbital floor fracture detection and identified traumatic inferior alveolar nerve lesions in over 90% of cases, with disc status assessable in most condylar cases and excellent agreement (κ 0.92–0.98) for CT-like MRI classification of mandibular fractures. The authors position modern CT-like (UTE/ZTE) and Black Bone MRI as valuable radiation-free alternatives for young, radio-sensitive patients requiring repeated imaging, but conclude that MRI currently complements rather than replaces CT or CBCT in acute trauma because fine osseous detail remains better depicted by CT.

## Key Contributions
- First consolidated synthesis of radiation-free MRI (CT-like UTE/ZTE and Black Bone sequences) across the full spectrum of dentomaxillofacial trauma, with protocol-oriented conclusions per trauma subsite.
- Quantifies maturity of the existing in vivo evidence (risk of bias low in 22/29 studies by adapted STROBE; QUADAS-2 applicability assessed).
- Frames trauma imaging around the ALADAIP principle: MRI-first or MRI-adjunct strategies to cut avoidable radiation in children and young adults.

## Methodology
Systematic review without meta-analysis (PRISMA), registered PROSPERO CRD42024588224. Sources included MEDLINE, EMBASE, BIOSIS, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, LILACS, and BBO Dentistry, searched up to November 2024, restricted to English/German and in vivo studies, clinical trials, observational studies, and case series (≥5 cases). 1160 records → 986 after dedup → 943 excluded at title/abstract → 14 excluded at full-text → 29 included. Quality was scored with an adapted 18-criteria STROBE checklist (low ≥15, moderate 11–14, high ≤10) plus QUADAS-2.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Studies / patients | 29 / 982 (prospective 59%, retrospective 41%, comparative 79%) |
| Trauma subsites | orbital 12 · condylar/subcondylar/TMJ 10 · mandibular 5 · temporal bone 1 · dental 1 |
| MRI parameters | field strength 0.23/0.5/1.5/3 T; acquisition 6–53 min; post-traumatic, postoperative, or both |
| Risk of bias (adapted STROBE) | low 22, moderate 6, high 1 (Kleinheinz 2000, 8/18 = 44%) |
| Orbital floor fracture sensitivity | MRI equivalent to CT (Freund 2002); poorer for small/associated fractures |
| Retained orbital foreign body | detected preoperatively in only ~50% of cases, CT and MRI alike (Nasr 1999) |
| MRI 3D-printed orbital models | as accurate as CT for blowout margins; less accurate for zygomaticomaxillary; no plate-bending difference (Cooper 2020) |
| TMJ disc assessment | feasible in ~90% of cases (Zheng 2016); >70% of fractured-side discs orthotopic, excursion limitation in 30% of treated joints (Neff 2000) |
| Inferior alveolar nerve lesions | detected in >90% of mandibular-fracture cases, confirmed intraoperatively (Kress 2003); higher SNR/NMCNR/nerve diameter with impairment (Burian 2020) |
| CT-like MRI mandibular fracture classification | inter/intra-reader agreement κ 0.92–0.98; no significant difference vs CT in fracture-dislocation assessment (Feuerriegel 2023b) |
| Dental trauma MRI (3 T, 20-ch coil) | pulp distinguished from surrounding structures; reperfusion/vitality evaluated, potentially avoiding unnecessary root canal treatment (Assaf 2015) |
