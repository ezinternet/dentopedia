## Three-line Summary
Cross-sectional observational feasibility study (n = 24 patients / 48 TMJs) comparing dental-dedicated MRI (ddMRI, 0.55 T) with CBCT, independently assessed by three observers for hard- and soft-tissue TMJ conditions (STARD 2015).
Mean inter-observer κ was 0.36 (ddMRI) vs 0.50 (CBCT); the most prevalent finding was anterior disc displacement (58.3%); no significant CBCT-vs-ddMRI differences for hard-tissue conditions (McNemar p > 0.05), though CBCT detected bone changes more often (2.1–41.7%) than ddMRI (0–31.3%).
ddMRI is feasible and radiation-free, depicting bone plus disc/joint fluid at point-of-care, but larger clinically correlated studies are needed before it guides TMJ diagnostics.

## Summary
This cross-sectional feasibility study evaluated dental-dedicated MRI (ddMRI) for TMJ assessment, comparing its depiction of bony changes with CBCT in 24 patients (48 TMJs) referred for TMJ imaging. Three observers independently scored hard- and soft-tissue conditions, and disc position was the most prevalent finding (anterior disc displacement 58.3%). Inter-observer agreement was modest (mean κ 0.36 ddMRI vs 0.50 CBCT), and CBCT trended toward higher bone-change prevalence (up to 41.7% flattening) yet no CBCT-vs-ddMRI difference reached significance (McNemar p > 0.05). ddMRI is a feasible, radiation-free modality that images bone and soft tissue at point-of-care, positioned as an adjunct rather than replacement pending larger validation.

## Key Contributions
- First feasibility evaluation of dental-dedicated MRI (0.55 T, 7-channel dental coil) for TMJ hard- and soft-tissue diagnostics.
- Direct CBCT comparison showing no statistically significant differences for bony TMJ conditions (p > 0.05), despite lower detection prevalence on ddMRI.
- Radiation-free single-modality depiction of bone, articular disc, and joint fluid at point-of-care.
- Reproducibility benchmarks: inter-observer mean κ 0.36 (ddMRI) / 0.50 (CBCT), raw agreement 81.3% / 85.9%.

## Methodology
Cross-sectional observational feasibility study (STARD 2015; ethics #1-10-72-101-22), 24 patients / 48 TMJs, 3 observers (>5 years TMJ imaging experience, 1 year ddMRI). CBCT: X1 unit (90 kVp, 10 mAs, 15 × 5 cm FOV, 0.20 mm voxel). ddMRI: Siemens MAGNETOM Free.Max 0.55 T, 7-channel dental receive coil; PD/T1/T2 (0.2 × 0.2 × 2.5 mm), PD-FS (0.4 × 0.4 × 2.5 mm), T2-STIR (0.4 × 0.4 × 2.0 mm), oblique sagittal/coronal. Five-point scoring (0 = not evaluable) dichotomized to normal/diseased; disc position scored in sagittal + coronal; joint fluid dichotomous. Statistics: Cohen's Kappa + raw agreement; McNemar test (two-sided p halved) for CBCT-vs-ddMRI comparisons, α = 0.05; sample-size plan 45 TMJs (80% power, 10% non-inferiority limit).

## Results
| Outcome | ddMRI | CBCT |
|---|---|---|
| Inter-observer agreement (mean κ, n=48) | 0.36 (−0.021–0.733) | 0.50 (0.143–1) |
| Inter-observer raw agreement | 81.3% (39.6–100) | 85.9% (60.4–100) |
| Intra-observer κ (n=10) | 0.444–1 (70–100%) | 0.545–1 (80–100%) |
| Anterior disc displacement (sagittal / coronal) | 58.3% / 18.8% | — (soft tissue, ddMRI only) |
| Condylar flattening | 31.3% | 41.7% (p=0.332) |
| Condylar cortical erosion | 8.3% | 10.4% (p=1.000) |
| Condylar subcortical sclerosis | 10.4% | 12.5% (p=1.000) |
| Condylar osteophyte | 22.9% | 27.1% (p=0.625) |
| Condylar subcortical cyst | 0% | 2.1% (p=1.000) |
| Temporal flattening / cortical erosion | 4.2% / 0% | 12.5% / 6.3% (p=0.125 / 0.250) |
| Temporal subcortical sclerosis | 12.5% | 6.3% (p=0.625) |
| Loose body | 0% | 0% |

No CBCT-vs-ddMRI comparison for hard-tissue conditions reached significance (all p > 0.05).
