## Three-line Summary

Retrospective review of 263 panoramic exposures (Dentsply Sirona Orthophos SL 2D/3D) made by 52 third-year (D3) predoctoral students across 4 formative assessments + 1 final summative assessment (East Carolina University, 2023–2024), grading 13 technical error categories.

Most common errors: chin up 41.02%, head rotation 24.84%, tongue placement 17.19%, chin down 16.36%, head tilt 9.91%, foreign objects 9.14%; error probabilities showed no statistically significant change across assessments, and only 4/263 images were nondiagnostic.

Students reached diagnostic competence (all passed the summative assessment first attempt), but Frankfort-plane, midsagittal-plane, and spinal-slumping errors trended upward at the SA — marking them as priority teaching targets and showing error count alone does not predict diagnostic adequacy.

## Summary

This retrospective study graded 263 panoramic radiographs produced by a single class of 52 third-year (D3) students at East Carolina University across four formative assessments (assistance allowed) and one independent summative assessment, using a 13-category technical error taxonomy scored by two calibrated radiology technologists (kappa 0.95). Chin-up positioning dominated (41.02% of exposures) followed by head rotation (24.84%), tongue placement (17.19%), and chin-down (16.36%); the same top-10 error ranking held whether errors were counted by frequency or by occurrence probability. Only 4/263 exposures (1.5%) were nondiagnostic — three in FA2 and one in FA3 — and every student passed the SA on the first attempt, all SA images being diagnostic. No error probability changed significantly across assessments, yet Frankfort-plane, midsagittal-plane, and spinal-slumping errors increased in the SA, indicating that effort must shift to the positioning criteria most dependent on accurate patient setup rather than to exposure handling, which digital latitude largely neutralizes.

## Key Contributions

- **Longitudinal learning-curve data for predoctoral panoramic acquisition** — the first analysis spanning 4 formative + 1 summative assessment, vs prior single-time-point studies
- **Error ranking (student, supervised setting)**: chin up 41.02% > head rotation 24.84% > tongue 17.19% > chin down 16.36% > head tilt 9.91% > foreign objects 9.14% (per-exposure probability)
- **Error quantity ≠ diagnostic adequacy**: over 80% of exposures carried 1–4 errors yet only 1.5% were nondiagnostic — severity drives retakes, not count
- **Persistent training targets isolated**: Frankfort, midsagittal, and spinal errors persisted and rose at the independent SA while other errors (exposure, focal trough) improved or stayed flat — these require targeted instructional reinforcement

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective review of radiographs from scheduled D3 clinical assessments (IRB-exempt, UMCIRB 24-000936)
- **n**: 52 D3 students; 263 exposures (FA1 51, FA2 55, FA3 53, FA4 52, SA 52 — one missing FA1 assignment, 4 remakes)
- **Device**: Dentsply Sirona Orthophos SL 2D/3D, 90 kV, 160 mA, ~14 s (settings adjustable for body habitus)
- **Assessments**: 4 FAs (licensed dental assistant/technologist assistance allowed) + 1 SA (independent); students attended didactic sessions and practiced positioning without radiation before the FAs
- **Errors (13)**: Frankfort plane (chin up/down), midsagittal plane (rotation/tilt), focal trough (forward/backward), tongue placement, foreign object, spinal slumping, patient motion, over/underexposure, lead apron artifact
- **Raters**: 2 technologists (>20 yr each, calibrated biannually); Cohen's kappa 0.95 (10 random images)
- **Stats**: Clopper–Pearson 95% CI; SCORE method for across-assessment change; SAS 9.4M8; p<0.05 significant

- **Errors per exposure**: 0–4; one error 37.93%, two errors 36.12% (most students at 1–2); SA had fewer error-free students and more with 3–4
- **Between-assessment change**: no statistically significant differences (p≥0.05); rising SA trend in Frankfort, midsagittal, and spinal errors — chin down climbed 7.84%→25.00% (FA1→SA)
- **Diagnostic quality**: 4/263 (1.5%) nondiagnostic — FA2 ×3 (2 chin-up anterior distortion, 1 head rotation ramus cutoff), FA3 ×1 (tongue-induced palatoglossal airspace over maxillary apices); 0 at SA; 52/52 students passed SA first attempt
- **Inter-rater reliability**: Cohen's kappa 0.95
