## Three-line Summary

Instrument-comparison study: 10 evaluators (5 oral and maxillofacial radiologists [OMFR], 5 general dentists [G]) scored 285 clinical panoramic radiographs twice, 7 days apart, with both the KAOMFR professional (P chart) and simple (S chart) clinical image-quality evaluation charts, comparing reliability, item correlation, scores, time, and preference.

The S chart gave similar evaluation scores at markedly shorter evaluation time (OMFR: 96.67 s P vs 62.93 s S, diff 33.74 s, p<0.001; G: 71.13 s vs 45.28 s, diff 25.85 s, p<0.001), produced lower item correlations than the P chart overall (P 0.373–0.884 vs S 0.231–0.801), and 80% of evaluators (all general dentists) preferred it.

Inter-rater reliability was excellent (single-measures ICC 0.929 OMFR / 0.870 G), but patient movement was the least reliable item in both charts (r=0.373 P, r=0.231 S); the S chart needs revision of its artifacts, coverage area, and patient movement items.

## Summary

This cross-sectional instrument-comparison study is the first validation of the KAOMFR Simple (S) chart, developed for routine quality control of panoramic radiography by non-radiologists in dental clinics, against the established professional (P) chart. Ten evaluators (5 OMFR, 5 general dentists) independently scored 285 clinical panoramic radiographs from multiple imaging systems with both charts in a balanced order (P→S vs S→P) and repeated after 7 days. Reliability was acceptable to excellent (inter-rater ICC 0.929/0.870 by group; intra-rater ≥0.722/≥0.694), and the S chart cut evaluation time by roughly one third (33.74 s OMFR, 25.85 s G; both p<0.001) while producing similar scores and a 0.231–0.801 correlation range against the P chart's 0.373–0.884. Because the S chart is substantially faster and was preferred by 80% of raters (100% of general dentists), it is a viable everyday QC tool for general practices — but its artifacts, coverage area, and patient movement items had the weakest agreement and require revision before the chart is relied on for those criteria.

## Key Contributions

- **First evaluation of the S chart in real use**: similar scores at ~1/3 shorter evaluation time (OMFR 33.74 s, G 25.85 s; p<0.001) — validated fast screening tool for non-specialists
- **Chart vs chart, group vs group correlation map**: total-score Pearson r 0.884 (P) / 0.801 (S); OMFR raters more consistent (0.452–0.871) than general dentists (0.405–0.663)
- **Reliability benchmarks**: inter-rater single measures 0.929 (OMFR) / 0.870 (G), intra-rater ≥0.722 / ≥0.694 — quality control is feasible across raters
- **Weak-item isolation**: patient movement (r=0.373 P, 0.231 S), artifacts (0.428–0.495), and S-chart coverage area (0.434) are the items needing upgrade — directly actionable revision list for KAOMFR
- **Preference data**: 80% preferred S (all 5 general dentists; 3 of 5 OMFR), reasons = ease of item selection · time saving (7 each), clarity · simplicity (4 each)

## Methodology

- **Design**: cross-sectional instrument-reliability comparison on archived clinical panoramic radiographs (IRB-approved re-use)
- **Evaluators**: OMFR group n=5 (20 invited, 4–30 yrs experience, 5 consented), G group n=5 general dentists (1–3 yrs, online-recruited volunteers)
- **Images**: n=285 panoramic radiographs, randomly mixed, acquired across multiple imaging systems (stated generalizability advantage)
- **Instruments (both =100 points)**: P chart — identification 12, artifacts 4, coverage area 6, patient positioning 18 + patient movement 4, density/contrast/sharpness 46, overall quality grade 10. S chart — identification 12, coverage area 6, artifacts 4, patient movement 4, head positioning error 18, density and contrast 46, overall quality grade 10. S-chart weights were assigned post hoc by the authors (the chart had none)
- **Procedure**: balanced crossover (A subgroups P→S, B subgroups S→P) to cancel learning bias; ≤50 images/evaluator/day; full repeat after 7 days
- **Outcomes**: inter-/intra-rater reliability, item-wise Pearson correlations, evaluation time, scores, preference questionnaire
- **Statistics**: Pearson correlation, paired t-test, SPSS 26.0; p<0.05
