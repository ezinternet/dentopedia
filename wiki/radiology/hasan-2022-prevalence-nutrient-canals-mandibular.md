## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study (n=200 of 800 screened, India, 10 months) comparing nutrient canal (NC) prevalence on mandibular anterior IOPARs across four groups: healthy controls, diabetes mellitus (DM), hypertension (HTN), and chronic periodontitis.

NC prevalence was significantly higher in disease groups than controls (DM 84% vs control 20%, p=0.000004; HTN 66%, p=0.000003; periodontitis 52%, p=0.00085), with DM showing the highest prevalence overall (inter-group p=0.0028); NC presence correlated with disease duration in DM (p=0.047) and HTN (p=0.012) but not with age or gender in any group.

Elevated NC frequency on routine mandibular anterior IOPARs may serve as an ancillary screening clue for underlying undiagnosed DM/HTN/periodontitis, but the single-center design and reliance on chi-square comparisons without confounder adjustment limit generalizability.

## Summary

This cross-sectional study evaluated 200 subjects (of 800 screened at a single Indian dental faculty department) to compare the prevalence of **nutrient canals (NCs)** — radiolucent neurovascular passages visible on mandibular anterior intraoral periapical radiographs (IOPARs) — across four groups: healthy controls, diabetes mellitus (DM), hypertension (HTN), and chronic periodontitis (50 per group). All radiographs were taken with the bisecting angle technique as part of routine care, and NC presence/number/location was assessed with a magnifying glass.

NC prevalence was markedly and significantly elevated in every disease group relative to controls: DM 84% vs 20% (p=0.000004), HTN 66% vs 20% (p=0.000003), periodontitis 52% vs 20% (p=0.00085). A direct inter-group comparison showed DM had the highest NC prevalence, followed by HTN then periodontitis (p=0.0028). Disease duration — but not patient age or gender — was significantly associated with NC presence in the DM (p=0.047) and HTN (p=0.012) cohorts. Most NCs (72.4-74.2%) were located beyond the root apex rather than interproximally. The authors propose vascular-collateralization mechanisms (insulin-deficiency-driven endothelial mitogenesis and atherosclerosis in DM; arteriolar wall hypertrophy and arteriosclerosis in HTN) to explain the increased NC formation, though these mechanisms are cited from prior physiology literature rather than directly tested.

## Key Contributions

- First multi-disease comparative NC dataset in the wiki: directly contrasts DM, HTN, and chronic periodontitis against a shared healthy control group within one study design, rather than relying on separate single-disease studies.
- Establishes a prevalence gradient (DM 84% > HTN 66% > periodontitis 52% > control 20%) with statistically significant inter-group differentiation (p=0.0028).
- Identifies disease duration as the significant patient-level correlate of NC presence in DM/HTN — age and gender were not significant in any group, refining which clinical variable is actually diagnostically informative.
- Confirms a periapical (beyond-root-apex) anatomic predominance for NC location (72.4-74.2%), consistent with the recognized vascular supply pattern to the mandibular anterior region.

## Methodology

- Prospective cross-sectional study, single center (Faculty of Dentistry, Jamia Millia Islamia, India), June 2019-April 2020.
- 800 subjects screened → 200 enrolled (97 male, 103 female), ages 20-70, presenting with anterior mandibular pain, deep proximal caries, or attrition.
- Four groups of 50: healthy controls, DM (>1yr history), HTN (>1yr history), chronic periodontitis (clinical criteria: bleeding on probing, pocket depth ≥4mm, plaque/calculus, tooth mobility); each subdivided by missing anterior teeth and interdental bone loss level.
- IOPAR of the 43-33 region via bisecting angle technique (70 kVp, 10 mA, 0.6 sec, size-2 E-speed film, manual processing); NC assessed visually with a magnifying glass.
- Chi-square test, SPSS 20.0, p<0.05 threshold; no confounder-adjusted multivariable modeling.

## Results

- NC prevalence: DM 84%, HTN 66%, periodontitis 52%, controls 20%.
- All disease-vs-control comparisons highly significant (DM p=0.000004; HTN p=0.000003; periodontitis p=0.00085).
- Inter-group comparison (DM vs HTN vs periodontitis) significant, p=0.0028, DM highest.
- No significant gender association in any group (p=0.775 control, 0.237 DM, 0.195 HTN, 0.768 periodontitis).
- No significant age association in any group (p=0.509 control, 0.536 DM, 0.482 HTN, 0.090 periodontitis).
- Disease duration significantly associated with NC in DM (p=0.047) and HTN (p=0.012).
- 72.4-74.2% of NCs located beyond the root apex (periapical) rather than interproximal.
- All 8 DM patients missing lower anterior teeth showed NC presence; DM and periodontitis groups showed highest NC counts with more advanced (apical 1/3) bone loss, while HTN group peaked at moderate (middle 1/3) bone loss.
