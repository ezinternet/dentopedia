## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (literature search 1931-2023, PubMed + Web of Science, ~50 full-text articles included) synthesizing anatomical variants of the mandibular canal (MC) and inferior alveolar neurovascular bundle (IANVB).

Proposes MC variants be classified into four categories — bifid MC, trifid MC, intramandibular accessory canals, and dental canals — while extramandibular buccal/lingual "nutrient function" canals (independent of the IAN, prevalence up to ~95% per Aps study) should be treated as a distinct, non-connected category requiring separate clinical consideration.

Clinical implication: CBCT (not panoramic radiography) is essential for pre-surgical/implant planning in the mandible to detect these variants and avoid hemorrhage, anesthesia failure (IANB), or nerve injury; key limitation is that this is a narrative (not systematic) review from a lower-tier journal with no formal quality appraisal of included studies.

## 세줄요약

문헌 고찰(1931-2023년, PubMed·Web of Science, 약 50편 전문 포함)을 통해 하악관(Mandibular Canal, MC) 및 하치조 신경혈관다발(Inferior Alveolar Neurovascular Bundle, IANVB)의 해부학적 변이를 종합한 서술적 review.

MC 변이를 이분지 하악관(Bifid Mandibular Canal, BMC), 삼분지 하악관(Trifid Mandibular Canal, TMC), 하악골내 부속관(intramandibular accessory canal), 치성관(dental canal) 4가지로 분류하고, 하악골외 협측/설측 "영양공급 기능(nutrient function)" 관(하치조신경과 독립적, Aps 연구 기준 유병률 최대 ~95%)은 별도 범주로 명확히 구분해야 한다고 제안.

임상적 의미: 파노라마(OPG)가 아닌 CBCT(콘빔 전산화단층촬영, Cone Beam Computed Tomography)가 하악골 수술·임플란트 사전계획에서 이러한 변이를 발견해 출혈·하치조신경차단(Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block, IANB) 마취 실패·신경 손상을 예방하는 데 필수적이며, 핵심 한계는 이 논문이 formal 체계적 문헌고찰(systematic review)이 아닌 서술적 review이고 낮은 임팩트 저널(In Vivo)에서 발표되어 포함 연구에 대한 공식적 질 평가가 없다는 점.

## Summary

This 2025 narrative review by Menditti et al. attempts to bring order to a fragmented literature on mandibular canal (MC) anatomical variants. Drawing on a search spanning nearly a century of publications (1931-2023) across PubMed and Web of Science, the authors trace MC anatomy from embryology (three separate canal precursors — incisal, deciduous molar, permanent molar — that coalesce during ossification) through gross anatomy (posterior/middle/anterior segments, average 65-70 mm length, 2.5→2 mm diameter taper) to modern radiological classification.

The paper's central contribution is a proposed simplification: MC variants should be grouped as **bifid MC**, **trifid MC**, **intramandibular accessory canals**, and **dental canals**, while **extramandibular buccal/lingual canals** (nutrient-function canals independent of the inferior alveolar nerve, IAN) should be recognized as a separate, non-connected category. The authors argue that existing classifications (Naitoh 2009, Langlais) create confusion by lumping the retromolar canal (RMC) into "bifid" statistics, which artificially inflates reported bifid mandibular canal (BMC) prevalence — some RMC-inclusive studies report 40.4-70% bifidity, which the authors instead interpret as a normal anatomical variant rather than a true duplication.

Clinically, the review reinforces that panoramic radiography (OPG) is only a first-line screening tool (with known "ghost image" and distortion artifacts) and that CBCT is the necessary second-level exam for accurate pre-surgical evaluation — relevant to implant placement, third molar extraction, mandibular osteotomy, and IANB anesthesia planning.

## Key Contributions

- Integrated review connecting embryological origin, gross anatomy, and radiological classification of the MC and its variants.
- Proposed 4-category classification (bifid MC, trifid MC, intramandibular accessory canals, dental canals) plus a separately-flagged extramandibular nutrient-canal category.
- Critical re-analysis of why RMC-inclusive classifications overestimate BMC prevalence.
- Reinforces CBCT as the necessary imaging standard over OPG for mandibular surgical/implant planning.

## Methodology

Narrative literature review, not a formal systematic review. PubMed and Web of Science searched 1931-2023 with keywords covering mandibular canal, retromolar canal, anatomic mandibular canal variations, bifid/trifid mandibular canal, and accessory bucco-lingual branches. Abstracts screened for relevance; ~50 full-text articles included after eligibility screening, plus 17 manually added articles (including etiopathogenesis papers) outside the database search. No PRISMA flow diagram, no dual-reviewer screening, and no risk-of-bias/quality appraisal tool reported — a methodological limitation given the journal's lower tier and the narrative (not systematic) nature of the synthesis.

## Results

- MC divided into posterior (retromolar triangle/RMT), middle (contains mental foramen, MF), and anterior (contains incisal canal) segments; average length 65-70 mm; diameter tapers ~2.5 mm (third molar) to ~2 mm (premolar).
- BMC prevalence reported ranges wildly by modality: 0.08-1.2% (some OPG studies) to 38% (range 10-44%, other OPG studies) to >65% on CBCT.
- RMC bifidity alone reported at 40.4-70% in some series when classified under Naitoh's Type I — authors argue this inflates true BMC estimates and should instead be read as a normal IAN variant.
- Extramandibular buccal/lingual nutrient canals: ~95% prevalence in the cited Aps study, with no patients showing zero foramina/accessory canals and no significant sex-based difference in number or diameter.
- Naitoh's (2009) CBCT-based classification detailed: Type 1 = RMC, Type 2 = subdental plexuses, Type 3 = forward canals in the mandibular body, Type 4 = buccolingual canals (~2% prevalence).
- No significant geographic, gender, or age-based differences reported for MC/BMC diameter.
