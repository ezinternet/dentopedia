## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional comparative radiographic study (n=600; 200 hypertension, 200 type 2 diabetes mellitus, 200 healthy controls, age 21-60) evaluating nutrient canals (NCs) on mandibular anterior intraoral periapical radiographs (#33-#43) using the paralleling technique.

NC presence was highest in the diabetes group (93.5%), followed by hypertension (88.5%), and lowest in healthy controls (44.5%); total NC count was also highest in the hypertension group (394, 65.5%) vs diabetes (356, 59.3%) vs controls (138, 23%), with both disease groups significantly higher than controls (P<0.05) but no significant difference between the two disease groups.

NCs were predominantly interradicular rather than periapical/beyond-root in all groups, and no significant correlation was found between NC presence and disease duration/severity or patient gender — supporting NC count as an adjunct (not standalone) radiographic marker prompting further systemic workup.

NC는 모든 군에서 치근단 주변보다 치근 사이(interradicular)에 더 많이 위치했고, NC 존재와 질환 이환기간·중증도·성별 간 유의한 상관관계는 없었다 — NC 개수를 전신질환 추가 검사를 유도하는 보조적 방사선학적 지표로 활용 가능하다.

## Summary

This cross-sectional study evaluated mandibular anterior nutrient canals (NCs) on intraoral periapical radiographs (IOPARs, #33-#43, paralleling technique) in 600 patients divided equally into hypertension (HT), type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM), and healthy control groups. NCs are radiolucent channels carrying vessels/nerves through alveolar bone, and prior literature links their increased presence to vascular changes seen in HT (arteriolar dilatation, hypertrophy/hyperplasia, arteriosclerosis with collateral opening) and DM (insulin-deficiency-driven endothelial mitogenesis and collateral vessel formation). Two blinded oral & maxillofacial radiologists scored NC presence, count, and location, with strong interobserver agreement (kappa up to 0.87). NC presence was highest in the DM group (93.5%), followed by HT (88.5%), and lowest in controls (44.5%) — both disease groups significantly exceeded controls, but did not significantly differ from each other. Interestingly, while DM had the higher per-patient presence rate, the HT group had the higher total NC count (394 vs 356), suggesting a difference in NC multiplicity rather than mere presence between the two conditions. NCs clustered interradicularly rather than beyond the root apex in all groups, consistent with the horizontal trabecular pattern of the anterior mandible. No significant correlation was found between NC presence and disease duration, severity, or patient gender.

## Key Contributions

- Head-to-head three-group comparison (HT vs DM vs healthy) in one design, isolating disease-specific NC signal via strict exclusion of comorbid/confounding conditions (periapical pathology, edentulism, severe bone loss, pregnancy, dual HT+DM).
- Distinguishes NC *presence* (per-patient, DM > HT > control) from NC *count* (per-group total, HT > DM > control) — a nuance not always separated in prior single-group studies.
- Reinforces the mechanistic rationale (vascular collateralization in both HT and DM) with location data showing predominance of interradicular NCs consistent with anterior mandibular trabecular architecture.
- Negative finding: no correlation between NC presence and disease duration/severity, tempering claims that NC count could serve as a severity/staging marker (it functions only as a presence/adjunct-diagnostic marker).

## Methodology

Cross-sectional comparative study, Surendera Dental College and Hospital, Rajasthan, India (SDCRI/IEC/2015/002). n=600 (21-60 years): Group I hypertension (n=200, BP >120/80 mmHg via auscultatory method, minimum 3-year history), Group II type 2 DM (n=200, RBS >=200 mg/dL or FBS >=126 mg/dL, minimum 3-year history), Group III healthy controls (n=200). Exclusions: comorbid HT+DM, other systemic disease, severe bone loss/crowding of lower anteriors, edentulism, periapical pathology, pregnancy, poor-quality films. IOPARs of #33-#43 taken via paralleling technique (XCP holder, Care Stream 2100CS). Two independent, blinded oral & maxillofacial radiologists scored NC presence/absence, total count, and location (interradicular vs beyond root apex). Statistics: SPSS 20.0, Chi-square for intergroup comparison, Cohen's kappa for interobserver reliability, Pearson correlation for duration/severity association, significance P<0.05.

## Results

- Interobserver agreement: kappa 0.635-0.87 across measures (P<0.01), indicating negligible bias.
- NC presence: DM 93.5% (187/200) > HT 88.5% (177/200) > control 44.5% (89/200); HT group 1.9x controls, DM group 2.1x controls.
- Total NC count (of 888 total): HT 394 (65.5%) > DM 356 (59.3%) > control 138 (23%).
- Intergroup comparison: both disease groups significantly exceeded controls (P<0.05); HT vs DM not significantly different (P>0.05).
- Location: NCs predominantly interradicular in all three groups (~93-95% of NCs between roots vs beyond apex); no significant intergroup difference in location distribution (P>0.005).
- No significant gender difference in NC presence in any group (P>0.005); males numerically higher overall (40.3% vs 35.2%).
- No significant correlation between NC presence and HT duration (r=0.024, P=0.74) or DM duration (r=0.05, P=0.48).
