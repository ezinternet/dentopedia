## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cone beam CT (CBCT) study of 252 patients (301 mandible sides, imaged October 2004–September 2005) evaluating the incidence and configuration of the bifid mandibular canal (BMC) using the Nortjé et al. classification.

BMC was present in 47/301 sides (15.6%) — Type I in 2, Type II in 40, Type III in 0, Type IV in 5 — with the accessory canal ≥50% of the main canal's diameter in 23 cases and <50% in 24 cases.

CBCT detected BMC at a markedly higher rate than prior panoramic-radiograph studies, supporting CBCT as a suitable modality for detailed pre-surgical evaluation of BMC in the posterior mandible; the source is abstract-only, limiting verification of methodology detail.

## Summary

This retrospective study evaluated the incidence and configuration of the bifid mandibular canal (BMC) — an anatomic variant in which the inferior alveolar neurovascular bundle's bony canal splits into two or more branches — using cone beam CT (CBCT). The abstract frames this as the first systematic sectional-imaging (as opposed to 2D panoramic) evaluation of BMC. Among 252 patients (301 evaluable mandible sides) imaged between October 2004 and September 2005, 47 sides (15.6%) demonstrated a BMC, classified per the Nortjé et al. four-type scheme, with accessory-canal diameter further graded relative to the main canal. The reported 15.6% incidence markedly exceeds earlier panoramic-radiograph-based incidence figures, which the authors attribute to CBCT's superior sectional resolution for detecting this variant — directly relevant to surgical planning (implant placement, third-molar removal, and other posterior-mandible procedures) where an undetected accessory canal risks incomplete anesthesia or neurovascular injury.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes a CBCT-era incidence baseline for BMC (15.6%), substantially higher than prior panoramic-radiograph estimates, framed by the authors as the first systematic sectional-imaging evaluation of this variant.
- Applies the Nortjé et al. four-type (I–IV) classification to CBCT data and adds a secondary accessory-canal diameter grading (≥50% vs <50% of the main canal), giving a two-axis description of BMC morphology.
- Demonstrates the practical case-mix: of 47 bifid cases, the great majority (40/47) were Type II, with Type III essentially absent (0 cases) in this cohort.

## Methodology

Retrospective review of CBCT scans acquired for 252 patients (301 mandible sides evaluable) between October 2004 and September 2005. Each CBCT dataset was assessed for presence of a BMC. Where present, the bifurcation pattern was classified into one of the four Nortjé et al. types (I–IV), and the accessory canal's diameter was categorized as either ≥50% or <50% of the main mandibular canal's diameter. Beyond these points, no further methodological detail (scanner model, number/blinding of readers, statistical analysis) is available — this page is built from the published abstract only, as PMC returned an empty full-text body for this article (a known quirk affecting some DMFR-journal articles in PMC's API).

## Results

- Overall BMC incidence: 47/301 mandible sides (15.6%).
- Type distribution among BMC cases: Type I — 2; Type II — 40; Type III — 0; Type IV — 5.
- Accessory canal diameter: ≥50% of main canal — 23 cases; <50% of main canal — 24 cases.
- CBCT-detected incidence (15.6%) was markedly higher than incidence figures previously reported from panoramic radiography, supporting CBCT as a suitable modality for detailed BMC evaluation.

**Limitations**: abstract-only source — full text unavailable (PMC returned an empty body for this DMFR article); no access to scanner parameters, reader reliability, or statistical methodology beyond what the abstract states; single-institution retrospective series with no stated generalizability discussion; no within-study comparison arm against panoramic radiography on the same patients (the "markedly higher" comparison is against separate prior literature, not a paired analysis).
