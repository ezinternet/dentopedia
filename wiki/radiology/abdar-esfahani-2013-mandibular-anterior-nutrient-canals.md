## Three-line Summary

Case-control study (n=64: 32 hypertensive patients, 32 normotensive controls, Isfahan, Iran) examining periapical radiographs of the mandibular anterior cuspid–central incisor region for nutrient canal (NC) presence.

Nutrient canal incidence was 37.5% in hypertensive patients vs 53.1% in normotensive subjects — not statistically significant (P = 0.209); no association with HTN duration (P = 0.292) or control status (P = 0.144); NC presence correlated with older age overall (47.1 vs 42.6 years, P = 0.002).

Contradicts the "NC as a hypertension diagnostic clue" claim reported elsewhere in the literature; this paper's null result aligns instead with Yilmaz et al. and Patni et al., indicating the NC–hypertension association is not a settled clinical marker.

## Summary

This blinded case-control study from Isfahan, Iran investigated whether the radiographic presence of mandibular anterior nutrient canals (NC) — vertically-oriented radiolucent canal-like structures thought to house blood vessels/nerves in the cuspid–central incisor region — can serve as a diagnostic clue for hypertension (HTN). Nutrient canals had previously been proposed as a radiographic marker for several systemic conditions (periodontal disease, hypertension, diabetes, tuberculosis, rickets, calcium deficiency, disuse atrophy, coarctation of the aorta), but their clinical normalcy and disease association remained controversial.

Thirty-two cardiologist-confirmed hypertensive patients and 32 normotensive dental-school patients (ages 30–55) were compared using standardized periapical radiographs, with radiologists blinded to each subject's hypertensive status. The incidence of NC was numerically **lower** in hypertensive patients (37.5%) than in normotensive controls (53.1%), and this difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.209). No association was found between NC presence and hypertension duration, hypertension control status, or sex. The one significant association found was with **age**: participants with NC present were on average 5 years older than those without (47.1 vs 42.6 years, P = 0.002), an effect that was statistically significant within the normotensive subgroup specifically (P < 0.001).

## Key Contributions

- A well-designed, blinded, adequately-powered (a priori sample-size calculation) case-control study directly testing the NC–hypertension diagnostic-clue hypothesis, adding a rigorous negative result to a mixed literature.
- Rules out hypertension duration and control status as confounds — NC presence tracked with neither.
- Identifies participant age, not hypertensive status, as the variable independently associated with NC presence, reframing NC more plausibly as an age-related vascular/anatomic finding than a hypertension biomarker.
- Explicitly situates its null finding against both camps in the literature: aligns with Yilmaz et al. and Patni et al. (no NC–HTN association) and contradicts Mani et al. and the broader positive-association literature (higher NC prevalence in hypertensive patients).

## Methodology

- **Design**: Blinded case-control study. Hypertensive patients recruited from a cardiovascular research center; normotensive controls recruited from a dental school population. Radiologists were blinded to hypertensive/normotensive status when scoring radiographs.
- **Sample**: 64 participants aged 30–55 (32 hypertensive, cardiologist-confirmed; 32 normotensive, simple randomization). Excluded diabetes, periodontal disease, tuberculosis, rickets, and pregnancy to reduce confounding.
- **Imaging**: Periapical radiographs (Kodak E-speed film, long-cone bisecting-angle technique, 70 kVp/10 mA) of the mandibular anterior cuspid–central incisor region, the site where nutrient canals are most commonly observed.
- **Blood pressure**: Standardized mercury sphygmomanometer measurement on the right arm after 15 minutes rest, avoiding recent caffeine/smoking/exercise/full bladder. HTN defined as ≥140/90 mmHg.
- **Statistics**: Chi-square and Fisher's exact tests (SPSS v21); significance at P < 0.05; sample size pre-calculated (d=0.3, α=0.05, β=0.1).

## Results

| Finding | Hypertensive | Normotensive | P value |
|---|---|---|---|
| NC present | 37.5% (12/32) | 53.1% (17/32) | 0.209 (NS) |
| NC by sex | M 39.1% vs F 33.3% (within HTN) | M/F comparable (P=0.804) | 0.761 (NS) |
| NC vs HTN duration | — | — | 0.292 (NS) |
| NC vs HTN control status | — | — | 0.144 (NS) |
| Mean age, NC present vs absent (whole cohort) | 47.1 ± 3.2 vs 42.6 ± 5.1 y | | 0.002 (significant) |
| Mean age, NC present vs absent (normotensive only) | | 45.9 vs 39.2 y | <0.001 (significant) |

Baseline metabolic parameters (BMI, fasting blood sugar, total cholesterol, triglycerides, HDL, LDL) were statistically comparable between the hypertensive and normotensive groups, arguing against these as confounders of the null NC–HTN result.

**Clinical implication**: On this evidence, mandibular anterior nutrient canal appearance on periapical radiography should not be relied upon as a screening clue for hypertension. If nutrient canals prompt any clinical follow-up, age-related vascular changes are a more evidence-consistent explanation than systemic hypertension. Practitioners should not over-interpret an incidental NC finding as a hypertension "red flag" based on this literature.

**Key limitation**: Small sample (n=64) for a case-control design; 2D periapical film cannot definitively distinguish true vascular canals from artifact; single-center Iranian cohort restricted to ages 30–55.
