## Three-line Summary

Retrospective CT/CBCT study (n=123 patients, 246 mandibular canals) evaluating prevalence and anatomical classification of bifid mandibular canals (BMCs) and trifid mandibular canals (TMCs), with detailed sub-classification and measurement of retromolar canals.

53.6% of patients (66/123) had at least one BMC or TMC; site-level prevalence was 36.2% for BMC and 4.5% for TMC, with forward canal (type III, 12.6%) and retromolar canal (type I, 10.2%) the most common accessory-canal subtypes; retromolar canals were significantly wider (1.44±0.65 mm) than other accessory canal types.

Accurate CT/CBCT evaluation of accessory mandibular canals — especially the retromolar canal, whose branch point, width, and course were characterized in detail — is recommended before posterior mandibular surgery to reduce neurovascular complication risk; limitation: single-center retrospective sample of pre-existing scans referred mostly for third-molar/implant planning, not a screened healthy population.

## 세줄요약

후향적 CT/CBCT 연구(환자 123명, 246개 하악관)로 이분 하악관(Bifid Mandibular Canal, BMC)과 삼분 하악관(Trifid Mandibular Canal, TMC)의 발생률 및 해부학적 분류를 평가하고, 특히 후구치관(retromolar canal)의 세부 아형과 계측치를 제시했다.

전체 환자의 53.6%(66/123)가 BMC 또는 TMC를 1개 이상 보유했으며, 부위(반측하악골) 단위 발생률은 BMC 36.2%, TMC 4.5%였고, 전방관(forward canal, type III, 12.6%)과 후구치관(type I, 10.2%)이 가장 흔한 아형이었으며, 후구치관의 평균 폭(1.44±0.65mm)이 다른 아형보다 유의하게 넓었다.

후구치관의 분지점·폭·주행을 포함한 정밀 CT/CBCT 평가는 하악 후방부 수술 시 신경혈관 손상 위험을 줄이기 위해 권고되나, 본 연구는 제3대구치 발치·임플란트 계획을 위해 이미 촬영된 스캔을 활용한 단일기관 후향적 표본이라 건강인 무작위 표본을 대표하지 않는다는 한계가 있다.

## Summary

This retrospective study analysed CT/CBCT scans from 123 patients (246 mandibular canals) collected at a single institution (University of Naples "Federico II", May 2018–January 2020) to determine the prevalence and anatomical characteristics of bifid mandibular canals (BMC) and trifid mandibular canals (TMC) — accessory branches of the mandibular canal that can carry neurovascular structures and are relevant to surgical complication risk (paresthesia, bleeding, traumatic neuroma) during third-molar extraction, implant placement, and posterior ridge augmentation. BMCs were classified using the Naitoh et al CBCT-based system (retromolar/type I, dental/type II, forward/type III, buccolingual/type IV), and retromolar canals — the subtype most associated with surgical complications — received an additional sub-classification (vertical/curved/horizontal, per von Arx et al) and detailed positional/width measurements. A high combined prevalence of 53.6% (66/123 patients) was found, with the forward canal (12.6%) and retromolar canal (10.2%) being the most frequent site-level variants. This complements the wiki's existing MRI-based bifid canal page ([[wiki/radiology/wamasing-2018-imaging-bifid-mandibular-canal]]) by providing CT/CBCT-derived prevalence and formal TMC/retromolar-subtype classification data.

## Key Contributions

- Combined BMC+TMC patient-level prevalence of 53.6% via CT/CBCT — near the upper range of prior literature (15.6–66.5% cited for BMC alone).
- Applies both the Naitoh (BMC type I–IV) and von Arx (retromolar canal vertical/curved/horizontal) classification systems in the same cohort, with retromolar canal course, branch point, and width fully characterized.
- Reports a rare unclassifiable variant, the temporal crest canal (TCC), in one hemimandible.
- No significant sex or side differences found in prevalence or subtype distribution — accessory canal risk should be assessed per-patient/per-side, not assumed from demographic factors.

## Methodology

Retrospective review of 123 patients' CT/CBCT scans (246 mandibular canals; 64 female/59 male, mean age 31.4±11.62y) meeting inclusion criteria (≥18y, full mandibular arch FOV, complete canal path visible) after excluding scans with incomplete canal visualization or posterior osteolytic/osteosclerotic lesions. Two calibrated examiners evaluated multiplanar reconstruction (MPR) images (RadiAnt DICOM Viewer) plus 3D volumetric reconstructions (SimPlant Pro 18); interexaminer agreement assessed by kappa (κ≥0.90 accepted). Canals were classified as normal/bifid/trifid, BMCs subtyped per Naitoh et al, and retromolar canals additionally subtyped per von Arx et al with width and positional measurements (distance to second molar, height, width, branch-point distance from mandibular foramen, parent-canal width at branch point). Chi-squared/Fisher's exact tests compared prevalence by gender/side/type; ANOVA compared width across subtypes (p<0.05 significant).

## Results

- 53.6% of patients (66/123) had ≥1 BMC or TMC (46.8% females vs 61% males, NS, p=0.148); 51.5% of affected patients were bilateral.
- Site-level (n=246): BMC 36.2%, TMC 4.5%. Patient-level: BMC 50.4%, TMC 6.5%.
- BMC subtypes (site-level): forward canal (type III) 12.6%, retromolar canal (type I) 10.2%, dental canal (type II) 6%, buccolingual canal (type IV) 3.7%; 3.7% unclassifiable (including one temporal crest canal, TCC).
- Retromolar canal (n=25 from 19/123 patients): mean width 1.44±0.65 mm, significantly wider than forward/anterior canals (1.08±0.26 mm, p=0.043); 60% vertical course, 40% curved, 0% horizontal.
- Retromolar canal positional data: 15.7±3.5 mm to 2nd molar, 12.9±2.7 mm height, 1.03±0.28 mm width, 12.18±5.32 mm from mandibular foramen to branch point, 3.12±0.67 mm parent-canal width at branch point — closely matching prior von Arx et al measurements.
- No significant gender (p=0.145) or side (p=0.742) differences in accessory canal subtype distribution.
- Limitation: single-center retrospective sample of scans referred mainly for third-molar/implant planning (not a screened random population); modest sample size (123 patients); no new scans obtained to limit radiation exposure.
