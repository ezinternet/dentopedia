## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional radiographic comparison of 2989 patients (1440 known hypertensives vs 1549 nonhypertensive controls), using intraoral periapical radiographs (IOPAR) of the lower anterior region to score presence/number of nutrient canals (NC) against periodontal bone-loss status, hypertension severity, and duration.

NC incidence was significantly higher in hypertensive patients (55.2%) than controls (36.2%, p=0.001, ~2.17x); incidence rose with age up to 60 years and with increasing alveolar bone loss (22.1% no bone loss → 75.8% with >2/3 root bone loss), and was higher in edentulous vs dentulous patients (55.5% vs 44.1%), but showed no correlation with hypertension severity and an unexpected drop in patients with hypertension >10 years (17.6%).

Clinically, NC presence on a routine periapical radiograph is not diagnostic of hypertension by itself but should raise suspicion and prompt blood-pressure screening, especially given how many hypertensive patients are undiagnosed at dental visits; key limitations are the purely descriptive/observational design (no adjustment for confounders such as age distribution imbalance between groups) and reliance on visual interpretation via magnifying lens rather than digital/CBCT quantification.

## 세줄요약

2989명(고혈압군 1440명 vs 비고혈압 대조군 1549명)을 대상으로 하악 전치부 치근단방사선사진(IOPAR)에서 영양관(Nutrient Canal, NC) 유무를 치주 골소실 상태·고혈압 중증도·이환기간과 함께 평가한 단면연구다.

NC 발생률은 고혈압군(55.2%)이 대조군(36.2%)보다 유의하게 높았고(p=0.001, 약 2.17배), 60세까지는 연령이 증가할수록, 그리고 치조골 소실이 심할수록(골소실 없음 22.1% → 2/3 이상 소실 75.8%) NC 발생률이 증가했으며 무치악 환자가 유치악 환자보다 높았으나(55.5% vs 44.1%), 고혈압 중증도와는 상관관계가 없었고 이환기간 10년 초과 군에서는 오히려 발생률이 급감했다(17.6%).

임상적으로 치근단방사선사진에서 NC 소견은 그 자체로 고혈압을 진단하지 못하지만 미진단 고혈압 환자가 많은 치과 내원 상황에서 혈압 재검사를 유도하는 보조 단서로 활용할 수 있으며, 순수 관찰연구 설계(군간 연령분포 불균형 등 교란변수 미보정)와 확대경을 이용한 육안 판독(디지털/CBCT 정량화 미적용)이 핵심 한계다.

## Summary

This cross-sectional radiographic study compared the incidence of mandibular nutrient canals (NC) — radiolucent channels in alveolar bone carrying vessels/nerves, first described by Hirschfeld in 1923 — between 1440 known hypertensive patients and 1549 nonhypertensive controls, using lower-anterior intraoral periapical radiographs (IOPAR). NC incidence was significantly higher in hypertensive patients (55.2%) than controls (36.2%, p=0.001), roughly 2.17x. The study also examined NC incidence across age groups, periodontal bone-loss severity, hypertension severity/duration, and dentulous vs edentulous status, finding NC rose with age (up to 60 years), with alveolar bone loss, and in edentulous patients, but was unrelated to hypertension severity and paradoxically decreased in patients hypertensive for >10 years.

## Key Contributions

- Large single-institution cross-sectional cohort (n=2989) directly testing the NC–hypertension association with a proper nonhypertensive control group.
- Multi-axis stratification of NC incidence: hypertension status, age decade, periodontal bone-loss extent, hypertension severity (BHS bands), hypertension duration, and dentulous/edentulous status within the same cohort.
- Proposes a mechanistic explanation (arteriolar dilatation, vessel wall hypertrophy/hyperplasia, arteriosclerotic luminal narrowing promoting collateral vessel formation) for increased NC incidence in hypertension, and a separate mechanism (terminal-stage vessel calcification) for the NC decline seen after >10 years of hypertension.

## Methodology

Cross-sectional, case-control-style comparison at a single institution (Department of Oral Medicine and Radiology), with IRB clearance. 2989 patients aged 10–70y; excluded diabetes, tuberculosis, coarctation of aorta, rickets, other bone disorders, smokers, and periapical lesions. Study group (n=1440, known hypertensive) subdivided into no-bone-loss (n=574), bone-loss (n=655), and edentulous (n=211) subgroups; control group (n=1549, nonhypertensive) subdivided analogously (n=813 / 632 / 104). IOPAR of the lower anterior region via bisecting-angle technique (70 kVp, 10 mAs; 0.6s dentulous / 0.5s edentulous exposure), processed on an automatic film processor and read with a magnifying lens. Bone loss graded as none / up to 1/3 / up to 2/3 / >2/3 root length. Hypertension severity graded by British Hypertension Society bands (mild/moderate/severe) via auscultatory BP measurement. Chi-square test for group comparison; binomial probability distribution for subgroup significance; 95% CI reported.

## Results

- Overall NC incidence: 45.3% (1354/2989).
- Hypertensive (study) group vs control: 55.2% vs 36.2% (p=0.001, ~2.17x higher).
- Age 41–50y: 63.9% (study) vs 39.9% (control); age 51–60y: 63.7% vs 52.4% — largest gap in the 41–60y band; incidence declined again above 60y and was low below 30y.
- Bone loss gradient: 22.1% NC with no bone loss → 44.2% (up to 1/3 loss) → 68.9% (up to 2/3 loss) → 75.8% (>2/3 loss).
- Within bone-loss status, study group consistently exceeded control: with bone loss 75.4% vs 59.8%; without bone loss 31.4% vs 15.6%.
- Hypertension severity showed no meaningful gradient: mild 55.7%, moderate 57.9%, severe 58.3% NC incidence.
- Hypertension duration: 1–3y 61.6%, 4–6y ~59% (332/564), 7–9y 62.5%, but >10y only 17.6% — a sharp, unexpected decline.
- Edentulous vs dentulous overall: 55.5% vs 44.1%. Edentulous study vs control: 56.8% vs 52.8% — not statistically significant.
