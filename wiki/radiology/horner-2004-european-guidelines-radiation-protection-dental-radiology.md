## Three-line Summary

European Commission Radiation Protection 136, produced by University of Manchester, implements Directives 96/29/Euratom and 97/43/Euratom as a practical guide covering dose benchmarks, age-stratified risk, and evidence-graded referral criteria for all dental radiographic modalities in EU primary care.
Effective dose benchmarks: intraoral 1–8.3 µSv, panoramic 3.85–30 µSv, cephalometric 2–3 µSv, mandibular CT 364–1,202 µSv; risk multipliers: ×3 for under-10s, ×0.3 for 50–80-year-olds.
Provides a three-tier framework (ED legally required; A–C evidence-graded; NR national) across justification, equipment optimization, quality assurance, and staff protection — the legal baseline for European dental radiation protection practice.

## Summary

European Commission Radiation Protection 136 is the foundational EU guideline on dental radiation protection, produced by the Victoria University of Manchester under contract and implementing the two core Euratom Directives: Directive 96/29/Euratom (basic safety standards for workers and public) and Directive 97/43/Euratom (Medical Exposures Directive). Published in 2004, it updates Radiation Protection 81 (1995) and provides a structured, evidence-graded approach covering six major areas: an introduction to ALARA principles, effective dose and risk data, referral criteria for all dental radiographic scenarios, equipment optimization factors, quality assurance standards, and staff protection. Dental radiology accounts for approximately one-third of all radiological examinations in the EU, making it a high-priority area for protection optimization. Notably, CBCT had not yet entered widespread clinical use at the time of publication, representing a significant gap addressed by subsequent European guidelines.

## Key Contributions

- **Dose benchmarks with EU survey data**: Intraoral 1–8.3 µSv, panoramic 3.85–30 µSv, cephalometric 2–3 µSv, mandibular CT 364–1,202 µSv (Table 2.3)
- **Age-stratified risk multipliers**: Under-10 = ×3; 10–20 = ×2; 20–30 = ×1.5; 30–50 = ×0.5; 50–80 = ×0.3; 80+ = negligible (Table 2.2)
- **Three-tier grading**: ED (Euratom-legally binding), A (SR/RCT), B (cohort/case-control), C (case series/expert opinion), NR (national recommendations)
- **Comprehensive referral criteria**: Caries (children/adults), orthodontics, periodontics, endodontics, new adult patients, edentulous patients, implantology, oral surgery, pregnancy, patient consent
- **Diagnostic Reference Levels (DRLs)**: Intraoral, panoramic, cephalometric — with multi-country EU survey data
- **Equipment optimization**: Filtration, rectangular collimation, film speed (E/F), digital receptor QA, leaded apron limitations
- **Quality standards tables**: For bitewing, periapical, panoramic, and cephalometric radiography (Tables 5.3–5.6)
- **Staff protection**: Distance rules (2 m effective), dose limits per Directive 96/29, controlled area designation, pregnant staff guidance

## Results

### Effective Dose and Risk (Tables 2.3, 2.2)

| Modality | Effective Dose | Per-million stochastic cancer risk |
|---|---|---|
| Intraoral (bitewing/periapical) | 1–8.3 µSv | 0.02–0.6 per million |
| Panoramic | 3.85–30 µSv | 0.21–1.9 per million |
| Lateral cephalometric | 2–3 µSv | 0.1–0.2 per million |
| Mandibular CT | 364–1,202 µSv | 18–72 per million |

| Age at exposure | Risk multiplier vs baseline |
|---|---|
| <10 years | ×3 |
| 10–20 years | ×2 |
| 20–30 years | ×1.5 |
| 30–50 years | ×0.5 |
| 50–80 years | ×0.3 |
| 80+ years | Negligible |

### Referral Criteria Highlights (Chapter 3)

- **Children caries**: Bitewing radiographs at 6-month intervals (high caries risk) or ≥12 months (low risk); individualized, not routine
- **Adult new patients**: Individualized assessment — not routine full-mouth series for all
- **Implantology**: Pre-operative cross-sectional (CT) required for bone volume/density; panoramic as baseline; intraoral periapical during/post surgery
- **Endodontics**: Pre-op + working length + pre-condensation + post-op + review radiographs per root canal
- **Pregnancy**: Radiation protection possible with appropriate technique; not contraindicated if clinically indicated
- **Edentulous patients**: Assessment individualized; full-mouth series generally not required

### Equipment Optimization (Chapter 4)

- Minimum 2.5 mm Al filtration at 70 kV; higher kV reduces dose and improves contrast
- Rectangular collimation for intraoral: substantially reduces dose vs round collimator
- E-speed or faster film; D-speed "should not be used"; digital receptors (CCD/PSP) typically dose-equivalent or lower
- Leaded aprons: limited benefit for head-and-neck exposure; thyroid collar limited additional value for intraoral
- Digital equipment requires dedicated QA protocol; image quality targets distinct from film

### Staff Protection (Chapter 6, per Directive 96/29/Euratom)

- Occupational dose limit: 20 mSv/year (averaged 5 years); max 50 mSv in single year
- Public dose limit: 1 mSv/year
- Practical ALARA: 2 m distance from tube + behind primary beam = effective protection in most dental setups
- Controlled area designation required around X-ray units; written procedures mandatory
- Pregnant staff: must be notified of dose limits; equivalent dose to foetus <1 mSv for remainder of pregnancy
