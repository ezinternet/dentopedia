## Three-line Summary
Mixed-methods explanatory sequential study of UK hospital staff responses to online criticism (Care Opinion platform), quantitatively coding defensive tactics then qualitatively explaining the organisational tensions that produce them.

Six reliably-coded defensive tactics were identified — redirecting to other channels, evading issues, psychologising concerns, invalidating concerns as incomplete, closing the feedback episode, and individualising with bespoke workarounds — all linked to lower-quality, less learning-oriented responses.

Defensiveness is a structural product of contradictory demands (be transparent and open vs protect organisational reputation under workload pressure) rather than individual failing; resolving the 'bind' requires addressing contradictory organisational expectations.

## Summary
Gillespie and Reader studied how UK hospital staff respond to online criticism and found six reliably-coded defensive tactics -- from redirecting and evading to psychologising and quietly closing the episode. Crucially, they show defensiveness is not mainly a personal flaw but a response to a structural 'bind': handlers are simultaneously asked to be transparent and to protect the organisation's reputation under workload pressure. Defensive responses were linked to weaker engagement and less organisational learning, implying that fixing complaint responses requires resolving the contradictory demands placed on responders.

## Key Contributions
- Reframes defensiveness as a *structural* product of contradictory demands, not individual failing.
- Names six defensive tactics: redirecting to other channels, evading issues, psychologising concerns, invalidating concerns as incomplete, closing the feedback episode, individualising with bespoke workarounds.
- Links defensive tactics to lower-quality, less learning-oriented responses.
- Identifies the 'bind': handlers told to be open yet protect the organisation.

## Methodology
Mixed-methods explanatory sequential design. UK hospital staff responses to online criticism (Care Opinion). Quantitative reliability coding of tactics followed by qualitative explanation of the organisational tensions producing them.

## Results
Six defensive tactics were reliably identified. They were generally associated with lower-quality engagement (less issue resolution, less learning). Defensiveness arose where handlers faced contradictory demands -- being responsive/transparent while also protecting organisational reputation and managing workload.
