## Three-line Summary

Comprehensive narrative review (27 studies, 1997–2025, from PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Scielo, and Web of Science; no PRISMA/systematic protocol) synthesizing oral health conditions and stage-specific dental management strategies for Alzheimer's disease (AD) patients, with emphasis on caregiver integration.

AD patients commonly present periodontal disease, root caries, xerostomia/hyposalivation, and traumatic (denture-related) mucosal lesions; evidence does not support strong bidirectional causality between AD and oral disease, pointing instead to shared antecedent factors (biological aging, chronic inflammation) producing a "syndemic" pattern of parallel decline.

Effective care requires stage-adapted strategies (conventional preventive/restorative care in early AD → minimally invasive/ART in moderate AD → palliative infection/comfort control in advanced AD) plus structured caregiver behavioral training (hand-over-hand technique, short positive commands, music-based distraction); as a narrative (non-systematic) review, no quantitative pooling or risk-of-bias/GRADE assessment was performed.

## 세줄요약

포괄적 내러티브 리뷰(narrative review) (27편, 1997–2025년, PubMed/MEDLINE·Scopus·Scielo·Web of Science; PRISMA 체계적 프로토콜 아님) — 알츠하이머병(Alzheimer's Disease, AD) 환자의 구강건강 상태 및 병기별(stage-specific) 치과관리 전략을 종합, 보호자(caregiver) 통합을 강조.

AD 환자는 치주질환(periodontal disease)·치근우식(root caries)·구강건조증/침분비저하(xerostomia/hyposalivation)·의치 관련 외상성 점막병변을 흔히 보임; AD와 구강질환 간 강한 양방향 인과관계는 근거 부족하며, 오히려 생물학적 노화·만성 염증 등 공통 선행요인에 의한 "신드믹(syndemic)" 동시 악화 패턴으로 설명됨.

효과적 관리는 병기별 맞춤 전략 필요(경도: 통상적 예방·수복 치료 → 중등도: 최소침습/ART → 중증: 완화적 감염관리·안위 중심) + 구조화된 보호자 행동훈련(핸드오버핸드 기법, 짧은 긍정적 구두지시, 음악 기반 주의전환); 내러티브(비체계적) 리뷰이므로 정량적 메타분석이나 비뚤림위험(risk-of-bias)/GRADE 평가는 수행되지 않음.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1111/scd.70179](https://doi.org/10.1111/scd.70179)), this comprehensive narrative review synthesized 27 studies (1997–2025; PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Scielo, Web of Science) on oral health in Alzheimer's disease (AD) patients into three domains: clinical manifestations, stage-adapted dental management, and the caregiver's role. AD patients commonly present periodontal disease, root caries, xerostomia/hyposalivation, and denture-related traumatic mucosal lesions. Rather than a bidirectional causal relationship, the authors propose a "syndemic" model — AD and oral disease share antecedent exposures (biological aging, chronic systemic inflammation, social determinants) and reinforce each other through a self-perpetuating cycle: cognitive/functional decline impairs oral self-care and medication-induced xerostomia accelerates dental breakdown, which in turn feeds back into pain, malnutrition, and systemic inflammatory burden that worsen frailty.

Clinically, the review's core contribution is a stage-specific management framework: early-stage AD patients tolerate conventional preventive/restorative dental care with caregiver-assisted hygiene education; moderate-stage patients need minimally invasive techniques (ART), short caregiver-attended morning appointments, and intensive hygiene training; advanced-stage patients should receive only emergency/palliative treatment focused on comfort and infection control (e.g., SDF for non-restorative caries arrest, denture removal if aspiration risk). A parallel caregiver-training toolkit (hand-over-hand technique, short positive-affect verbal commands, chaining/priming, music-based distraction, structured de-escalation) is presented as a practical way to work around apraxia and language decline while leveraging preserved procedural and emotional memory.

## Key Contributions

- Frames AD–oral disease co-occurrence as a "syndemic" (shared antecedent factors: aging, chronic inflammation, social determinants) rather than a bidirectional causal relationship — periodontal-pathogen CNS translocation and neuroinflammation remain mechanistic hypotheses, not established causal pathways.
- Provides an explicit stage-by-stage dental management table (early / moderate / advanced AD) mapping behavioral profile → appointment structure → treatment objective → recommended clinical strategy.
- Documents the mechanistic basis of AD-associated xerostomia: autonomic salivary-gland dysfunction present even in early AD independent of medication, compounded by acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine) routinely co-prescribed with anticholinergic/xerostomic antidepressants and antipsychotics — extending the general geriatric polypharmacy-xerostomia mechanism (see [[geriatric-dentistry/halasabalu-kalgeri-2026-polypharmacy-oral-health-elderly]]) into the AD-specific clinical picture.
- Details 8 concrete, evidence-supported caregiver behavioral techniques (positioning, short positive communication, hand-over-hand, chaining/priming, music-based distraction, de-escalation, adaptive tools, denture care protocols).
- Identifies root caries as a disease-specific marker rather than an incidental finding in AD — driven by AD-specific oral dysbiosis (increased acidogenic/aciduric taxa) plus reduced salivary buffering capacity.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Comprehensive narrative review (Green et al. framework) — not a PRISMA systematic review; no registered protocol (no PROSPERO), no formal risk-of-bias tool, no GRADE certainty rating, no quantitative meta-analysis.
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Scielo, Web of Science (searched November 2025); MeSH + free-text terms, Boolean operators/truncation; complementary "caregiver*" search arm; no language/year restriction.
- **Eligibility**: studies on oral health conditions, dental management, or caregiver roles in AD patients; excluded other-dementia-only, purely pharmacological/neurological/lab studies, and non-clinical editorials/opinion pieces.
- **Screening**: two independent reviewers, disagreements resolved by a third reviewer; blinded to author/journal/COI; reference lists hand-searched.
- **Synthesis**: qualitative, organized into 3 thematic domains (clinical manifestations; adapted management; caregiver role) — no quantitative pooling.

## Stage-Specific Management Framework

**Search yield**: 640 records → 203 duplicates removed → 12 met eligibility + 15 via manual/reference search → **27 studies** (1997–2025): observational (n=7), literature reviews (n=7), systematic/scoping reviews (n=6), non-randomized/pilot studies (n=4), RCTs (n=2), experimental study (n=1).

**Clinical manifestations**:
- *Periodontitis*: chronic inflammatory burden; declining manual dexterity/immunosenescence → higher plaque/gingival bleeding vs cognitively healthy older adults; causal link to AD not established.
- *Root caries*: a distinct "third peak" of cariogenic activity on exposed root surfaces; significantly higher prevalence/decayed-surface counts vs cognitively intact controls; driven by AD-specific dysbiosis + reduced salivary buffering (critical pH for dentin ~6.2–6.7 vs enamel ~5.5).
- *Xerostomia/hyposalivation*: autonomic dysfunction (present even early, independent of medication) + polypharmacy (acetylcholinesterase inhibitors combined with anticholinergic co-medications) → reduced buffering → accelerated caries/erosion.
- *Traumatic mucosal lesions*: denture stomatitis >2x more frequent than in cognitively healthy older adults; driven by ill-fitting dentures (weight loss, muscle atrophy), continuous overnight wear, poor hygiene; hyaluronic acid gel (0.2–0.3%) + adjustment accelerates healing.

**Stage-specific dental management (Table 1)**:

| Stage | Appointment | Treatment objective | Recommended strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early | Up to 50 min, patient-centered | Restore function, eliminate infectious foci | Conventional dental treatment; patient + caregiver hygiene education |
| Moderate | ~30 min, morning, mandatory caregiver presence | Maintain oral health, prevent pain | ART (atraumatic restorative treatment), fluoride-releasing materials, possible conscious sedation, intensive caregiver training |
| Advanced | Very brief, comfort-focused | Palliation, comfort, infection control | Emergency treatment only; assisted/palliative hygiene; denture removal if aspiration risk; SDF (silver diamine fluoride) for non-restorative caries arrest |

**Caregiver behavioral toolkit**: positioning/head stabilization (11–12 o'clock); short single-step positive commands ("Open," "Good," "All done"); hand-over-hand technique (strongest evidence, exploits preserved procedural memory in apraxia); chaining/priming (sensory cues before motor task); music-based distraction (preserved emotional/auditory memory, reduces agitation); rescuing/de-escalation (interrupt before verbal refusal → physical aggression); adaptive tools (electric toothbrush, high-fluoride toothpaste, mouth props); denture care (daily extraoral cleaning + mandatory overnight removal).

## Limitations

- Narrative (Green et al. framework), not systematic — no registered protocol, no formal risk-of-bias/AMSTAR-2/Cochrane RoB assessment of the 27 included studies, no GRADE certainty-of-evidence rating, and no quantitative meta-analytic pooling of prevalence/association estimates.
- Highly heterogeneous evidence base (case reports/pilot studies through RCTs and reviews; only 2/27 RCTs); mechanistic claims (periodontal-pathogen CNS translocation, neuroinflammation) rest largely on experimental rather than clinical-epidemiological evidence.
- Causality between periodontal/oral infection and AD progression remains unestablished; authors explicitly favor a shared-antecedent "syndemic" model over bidirectional causation and call for further longitudinal studies.
