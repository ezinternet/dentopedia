## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD420251003698; MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane; final search 17 April 2025, updated 22 December 2025) of 23 studies (21 cross-sectional, 2 cohort; predominantly low risk of bias on the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale) on multimorbidity, polypharmacy and oral conditions in adults aged 75 years or older.

Pooled analysis of two studies showed polypharmacy (more than 5 medications) was significantly associated with dry mouth (OR = 2.05, 95% CI 1.31-3.22, I2 = 0%), and polypharmacy was consistently associated with poorer Oral Health Assessment Tool (OHAT) scores; multimorbidity meta-analyses were non-significant and heterogeneous (edentulism OR = 1.91, 95% CI 0.24-15.13, I2 = 86%; dry mouth OR = 1.87, 95% CI 0.50-7.00, I2 = 85%).

Dry mouth should be assessed before treatment planning and after new medications, but the polypharmacy-dry mouth pooled estimate rests on only two self-report studies, and evidence for teeth number, edentulism and oral function is inconsistent; no study evaluated management targeting multimorbidity or polypharmacy.

## 세줄요약

75세 이상 노인에서 다중이환 (Multimorbidity)과 다약제복용 (Polypharmacy)이 구강 상태와 맺는 연관성을 본 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis, SR+MA); 23편 (횡단 21편, 코호트 2편)을 포함했고 뉴캐슬-오타와 척도 (Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, NOS) 상 대부분 낮은 편향 위험.

2편을 합친 분석에서 다약제복용 (5종 초과)은 구강건조 (Dry mouth)와 유의한 연관 (OR = 2.05, 95% CI 1.31-3.22, I2 = 0%)을 보였고 구강건강평가도구 (Oral Health Assessment Tool, OHAT) 점수 악화와도 일관되게 연관; 다중이환 메타분석은 비유의적이고 이질성이 컸음 (무치악 OR = 1.91, 95% CI 0.24-15.13, I2 = 86%; 구강건조 OR = 1.87, 95% CI 0.50-7.00, I2 = 85%).

치료계획 전과 새 약물 시작 후 구강건조를 평가할 만하나, 합산 추정치는 자가보고 연구 2편에 불과하고 잔존치 수·무치악·구강기능 근거는 일관되지 않으며 다중이환·다약제복용 관리 중재를 평가한 연구는 없었음.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (Gerodontology 2026; retrieved via PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/ger.70065)) examined whether multimorbidity and polypharmacy are associated with oral conditions in adults aged 75 years or older. Twenty-three studies were included after screening 3433 records. A pooled analysis of two studies found polypharmacy significantly associated with dry mouth (OR = 2.05, 95% CI 1.31-3.22, I2 = 0%), and polypharmacy was consistently associated with poorer OHAT scores. Multimorbidity or comorbidity showed inconsistent associations, and both exposures showed inconsistent associations with number of teeth, edentulism and oral function. The authors conclude polypharmacy may affect dry mouth and hyposalivation independent of multimorbidity, but other oral outcomes remain unclear.

## Key Contributions

- First pooled estimate in this wiki for polypharmacy vs dry mouth in older adults: OR = 2.05 (1.31-3.22), I2 = 0%.
- Separates the two exposures: polypharmacy tracks dry mouth and OHAT; multimorbidity (CCI, FCI, disease counts) does not consistently.
- Longitudinal studies (3 years, 6 months) found no association with extractions, restorations, DMF-T or OHAT.
- Identifies an evidence gap: no study evaluated management of multimorbidity or polypharmacy for oral outcomes.

## Methodology

- PRISMA-reported; PROSPERO CRD420251003698; MEDLINE (PubMed), Embase, Cochrane Library; searched 17 April 2025, updated 22 December 2025.
- Population: mean age >= 75 years, >= 10 participants per group. 3433 records, 202 full texts, 23 included (21 cross-sectional and 2 cohort per abstract; section 3.2 says 20 cross-sectional, 2 prospective cohort, 1 retrospective).
- Risk of bias by Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (18 of 21 cross-sectional low risk; both cohorts moderate). Meta-analysis in RevMan 5.4.1 with ORs and I2.

## Results

| Comparison | Studies | Pooled OR (95% CI) | I2 | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Polypharmacy (more than 5 medications) vs dry mouth | 2 (Smidt 2011; Viljakainen 2016) | 2.05 (1.31-3.22) | 0% | Significant; both self-reported dry mouth |
| Multimorbidity vs edentulism | 2 (Islas-Granillo 2019; Katsoulis 2012) | 1.91 (0.24-15.13) | 86% | Non-significant, inconclusive |
| Multimorbidity vs dry mouth | 2 (Islas-Granillo 2019; Smidt 2011) | 1.87 (0.50-7.00) | 85% | Non-significant, inconclusive |

Narrative findings:
- Dry mouth and saliva: number of medications associated with dry mouth (Smidt, Viljakainen; particularly 10 or more medications) and with stimulated/unstimulated saliva volume or flow (Ichikawa 2011; Syrjala 2011); only Tada 2025 found no medication-count difference. Loop diuretics, proton pump inhibitors and cardiac agents were more common in those with dry mouth.
- OHAT: polypharmacy (6 or more medications) associated with poorer OHAT (Nakamura 2021; Rosenberg 2021); CCI or FCI not associated (Tanaka 2024; Ni Chroinin 2016 after pH adjustment; Tuuliainen 2020).
- Teeth and edentulism: inconsistent. Edentulism 55.3% with vs 32.7% without multimorbidity (Islas-Granillo); Wu 2025 linked multimorbidity to fewer than 20 teeth; Katsoulis, Anliker, Saarela and Tan found no association. For polypharmacy, Anliker and Tamaki found none; Janssens found more medications associated with fewer teeth.
- Oral function: no multimorbidity evidence; polypharmacy inconsistent (Tamaki 2024 and Hirata 2025 no association with oral frailty; Weyant 2004 and Morita 2021 more than 8 medications associated with dysfunction).
- Other: more than 3 diseases and 6 medications associated with oral candidiasis (Pedersen 2015); opioid use associated with more extractions (Tan 2020).
- Longitudinal: Tan 2020 (3 years, n = 30,995) and Klotz 2020 (6 months, n = 114) found no association with extractions, restorations, DMF-T or OHAT.

Clinical implications stated by the authors: assess dry mouth before treatment planning and after new medications; coordinate with the physician to reduce medication burden; medication review and deprescribing may help prevent dry mouth (RCTs are lacking); antiseptic mouthwash beyond 2 weeks may worsen xerostomia under polypharmacy. Limitations: mostly cross-sectional; about half institutionalised or hospitalised; heterogeneous exposure definitions; only two studies per pooled analysis.
