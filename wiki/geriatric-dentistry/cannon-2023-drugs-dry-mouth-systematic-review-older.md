## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (MEDLINE, EMBASE, PsycINFO, CINAHL; PROSPERO CRD42021288945) of 9 studies (8 cross-sectional, 1 longitudinal; 37,459 participants aged 65+) on health conditions of dry mouth with polypharmacy.

Natural teeth fell from 16 (SD 9) to 12 (SD 8) as medicines rose from 0 to 5; only 2 studies addressed psychological outcomes, with depression prevalence up to 64% in older adults with dry mouth.

Narrative synthesis only (heterogeneous outcomes, mostly cross-sectional designs); supports prevention through medication-regimen optimisation rather than treating dry mouth after it develops.

## Summary

Cannon et al. (Drugs & Aging 2023; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40266-023-01017-5), via PubMed) review what is known about physical and psychological consequences of dry mouth in older adults with polypharmacy. About 60% of older adults are reported to complain of dry mouth. Physical conditions centre on dental health (tooth loss) and functional impairment (swallowing difficulty); psychological evidence is sparse.

## Key Contributions

- Frames dry mouth in polypharmacy as a preventable, modifiable-risk problem rather than only a treatment target.
- Separates physical from psychological conditions and flags the psychological evidence gap (n = 2 studies).

## Methodology

Four databases searched; PROSPERO CRD42021288945; JBI critical appraisal; 6852 citations screened, 9 studies included (published 2005-2019, mean age range 68.5-85.0 years); narrative synthesis because of outcome heterogeneity.

## Results

- Medicines 0 to 5: remaining natural teeth 16 (SD 9) to 12 (SD 8).
- Medicines 1 to 3 or more: dental complications 1 (SD 2) to 2 (SD 2).
- Depression prevalence as high as 64% among older adults with dry mouth; six further psychological conditions reported (self-consciousness, feeling tense, difficulty relaxing, irritability, difficulty completing tasks, feeling less satisfied in life).
- Abstract-level only; study-level details not retrieved.
