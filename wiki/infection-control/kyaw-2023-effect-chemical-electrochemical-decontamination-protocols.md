## One-line Summary

RCT on 90 implants + 90 healing abutments (80 patient-retrieved): repeated NaOCl-only cleaning progressively enlarged micro-gap/microleakage and shrank the implant–HA contact surface, while combined chemical + electrochemical decontamination removed debris up to triple use without surface alteration (near the new-HA control) — Ti HA reuse is acceptable multiple times only with the combined protocol.

## Summary

Kyaw et al. (2023) is a randomized controlled trial — the highest study design in this wiki's "healing abutment (HA) reuse" batch — testing whether reused titanium HAs can be decontaminated **without damaging the implant–abutment interface**. Where the in vitro batch papers argue over how much residual protein/biofilm survives cleaning ([[infection-control/burioni-2024-could-dental-material-reuse-play]], [[infection-control/cetinsoy-2026-ultrasonication-assisted-sequential-chemical-removal]], [[infection-control/naghsh-2024-comparison-four-decontamination-procedures-reusing]]) and whether the peri-implant biologic response tolerates reuse ([[infection-control/abreu-2024-assessment-detoxification-strategies-used-dental]]), Kyaw adds a distinct **device-integrity axis**: contact surface area, micro-gap volume, and microleakage measured across escalating reuse frequency.

The key finding is that the *cleaning method itself* alters device integrity. Repeated **NaOCl-only** cleaning (ultrasonic 5.25% NaOCl + autoclave) progressively enlarged the micro-gap and microleakage and shrank the implant–HA contact surface, worst at >triple use. Adding an **electrochemical** step kept the interface near the new-HA control even up to triple use, with residual debris optimally removed and no surface alteration. Kyaw therefore stakes out the **pro-reuse-with-rigorous-protocol** pole: Ti HAs may be reused multiple times *provided* a combined chemical + electrochemical protocol is used — a conclusion in direct tension with abreu-2024's biologic "do not reuse," and one that refines the anchor SR [[infection-control/bidra-2019-should-healing-abutments-cover-screws]] by making reuse-adequacy conditional on the specific decontamination protocol rather than a blanket yes/no.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces a **device-integrity axis** (implant–HA contact surface area, micro-gap volume, microleakage, surface topography) into the HA-reuse debate, complementing the batch's cleaning-efficacy and biologic-response axes.
- Shows the **cleaning method is not integrity-neutral**: repeated NaOCl-only cleaning changed the implant–HA contact surface area and worsened micro-gap/microleakage with each reuse cycle.
- Demonstrates that a **combined chemical + electrochemical** protocol removes residual biological debris up to triple use **without altering surface properties**, staying near the new-HA control.
- Conditional pro-reuse conclusion: Ti HAs can be reused multiple times **only if** the combined chemical + electrochemical protocol is used — refines the yes/no framing of the anchor SR.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Randomized controlled trial on retrieved + new titanium HAs.
- **Specimens**: 90 bone-level titanium implants + 90 bone-level titanium HAs; **80 contaminated HAs collected from human participants** (patient-retrieved).
- **Cleaning groups**:
  - **Group I** — ultrasonication with 5.25% NaOCl (15 min) + steam autoclave.
  - **Group II** — ultrasonication with 5.25% NaOCl (15 min) + **electrochemical cleaning** + steam autoclave.
  - **Group III** — control: 10 new, unused HAs.
- **Reuse tiers** (cleaning after each insertion): (a) single-use + 1 cycle; (b) double-use + 2 cycles; (c) triple-use + 3 cycles; (d) >triple-use + >3 cycles (labeled Ia–Id, IIa–IId).
- **Outcomes**: contact surface area + micro-gap volume by **micro-CT**; microleakage by **2% methylene blue** dye penetration; surface morphology by **SEM**; elemental composition by **EDX**.

## Results

- **Group Id** (NaOCl only, >triple use) had the **smallest implant–HA contact surface area**.
- **Micro-gap volume and microleakage differed significantly (p < 0.001)**, in descending order:
  **Id > Ic > Ib > IId > Ia, IIa, and III.**
  Repeated NaOCl-only reuse progressively worsened the interface (Id worst), whereas the combined chemical + electrochemical groups stayed near the new-HA control (III).
- **Groups IIa, IIb, IIc** (combined protocol, up to triple use): residual biological debris **optimally removed without altering surface properties**.
- **Conclusion**: combined chemical + electrochemical decontamination is more effective than NaOCl alone — especially across multiple consecutive uses — decreasing micro-gap volume and microleakage without surface alteration; NaOCl-only cleaning changed the implant–HA contact surface area. Ti HA reuse can be considered multiple times only under the combined protocol.
- Per-group numerical values (micro-gap volumes, contact-area measurements) not reported — **abstract-only**.
