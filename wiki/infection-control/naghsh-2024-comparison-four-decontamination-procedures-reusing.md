## Three-line Summary

In vitro comparison (50 used HAs, DIO UFII, each used once 4–6 weeks in 50 patients; 5 groups × 10) testing four adjunct decontamination methods layered onto ultrasonic + autoclave baseline; residual protein visualized with Phloxine B staining.

On the body/shank surface, glycine air polishing (1.7±1.1%) and 3% NaOCl (2.4±1.1%) left significantly less contamination than control (6.1%), H2O2 (4.6%), or CHX (5.4%) (p<0.001); on the occlusal surface, no adjunct made a difference (p=0.26) due to deep-recess inaccessibility.

No group fully eliminated contamination, confirming the "no 100% virgin surface" conclusion; CHX and H2O2 provided no benefit over the control baseline.

## Summary

Healing abutments (HAs) are manufacturer-labeled single-use, yet clinicians commonly reuse them to cut cost and material waste. The persistent concern is cross-contamination: inadequate cleaning reduces fibroblast/epithelial adhesion, compromises the peri-implant biological barrier, and can drive tissue infection and implant failure. This in vitro study asked which adjunct decontamination method, layered onto a standard ultrasonic + autoclave baseline, best reduces residual protein contamination on genuinely used HAs.

Fifty used HAs (DIO UFII, Ø4.5 × 3 mm, each used once for 4–6 weeks in 50 patients) were divided into 5 groups of 10. Every group received ultrasonic cleaning (60°C, 10 min) then autoclaving (121°C); four groups added an adjunct — 3% sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl, 20 min), 12% chlorhexidine (CHX, 5 min), glycine-powder air polishing (Perio-mate/NSK), or 3% hydrogen peroxide (H2O2, 1 min) — while the control had no adjunct. Residual protein was visualized with Phloxine B and quantified as % stained surface area (stereomicroscope ×15, 5 images per HA).

The result reinforces the anchor systematic-review finding that **no protocol restores a virgin (contaminant-free) surface** — contamination was reduced but never eliminated in any group. It also ranks the adjuncts: on the body/shank surface, **glycine air polishing and 3% NaOCl** were significantly better than control, whereas **12% CHX and 3% H2O2 were no better than control**. On the occlusal (top) surface, whose deep recesses resist access, **no adjunct helped** (p=0.26).

## Key Contributions

- Head-to-head comparison of 4 adjunct decontamination methods on a common ultrasonic + autoclave baseline, using genuinely used clinical HAs.
- Empirically confirms the "no 100% virgin surface" SR conclusion: contamination reduced but **never fully eliminated** in any of 5 groups.
- Identifies **3% NaOCl and glycine air polishing** as the two effective adjuncts for body-surface decontamination; **12% CHX and 3% H2O2 provided no benefit** over the control.
- Shows the effect is **surface-region-specific**: the occlusal surface's deep recesses defeated every method.

## Methodology

- **Design**: In vitro comparative study; one-way ANOVA (α=0.05, power 0.80).
- **Specimens**: 50 used HAs (DIO UFII, Ø4.5 × 3 mm), each used once 4–6 weeks in 50 patients; 5 groups × 10.
- **Common baseline (all groups)**: ultrasonic (Eurosonic 4D, 60°C, 10 min) → autoclave (Melag, 121°C).
- **Adjuncts**: G1 control (baseline only); G2 3% NaOCl (20 min); G3 12% CHX (5 min); G4 glycine air polishing (Perio-mate/NSK, 15 s, 5 mm, 45–60°); G5 3% H2O2 (1 min). Chemical groups rinsed in saline then re-autoclaved.
- **Outcome**: residual protein by Phloxine B staining (% stained area); stereomicroscope ×15; 5 images/HA (4 body/shank + 1 occlusal).

## Results

**Occlusal (top) surface — % contaminated area (mean ± SD):** Control 5.5±2.8, NaOCl 4.9±2.5, CHX 5.3±2.5, air polishing 3.1±1.8, H2O2 4.8±3.1 → **no significant difference (p=0.26)** (HA geometry limits access to deep occlusal recesses).

**Body / shank surface — % contaminated area (mean ± SD):**

| Group | Contamination (%) | vs control |
|---|---|---|
| Air polishing (glycine) | **1.7 ± 1.1** | significantly lower |
| NaOCl (3%) | **2.4 ± 1.1** | significantly lower |
| H2O2 (3%) | 4.6 ± 0.7 | not significant |
| CHX (12%) | 5.4 ± 2.4 | not significant |
| Control | 6.1 ± 2.3 | — |

→ **Significant group difference (p<0.001)**: air polishing and NaOCl each significantly lower than control / H2O2 / CHX; no difference between air polishing and NaOCl; no difference among control / H2O2 / CHX.

- **Bottom line**: contamination **not completely eliminated in any group**. NaOCl + glycine air polishing (with ultrasonic + autoclave) are the most effective body-surface adjuncts; the occlusal surface benefited from none.
- **Caveat**: glycine air polishing may alter surface roughness/wettability/energy — the body-surface benefit must be weighed against possible topographic change on a surface intended for repeated reuse.
