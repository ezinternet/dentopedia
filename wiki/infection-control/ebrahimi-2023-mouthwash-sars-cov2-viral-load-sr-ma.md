## Three-line Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the effects of mouthwashes on SARS-CoV-2 viral load reduction in the oral cavity, including 9 clinical trials (low risk of bias). Meta-analysis showed that 1% povidone-iodine mouthwash was effective (MD = 3.61, 95% CI: 1.03 to 6.19), while cetylpyridinium chloride (MD = 0.61, 95% CI: -1.03 to 2.25) and chlorhexidine gluconate (MD = -0.04, 95% CI: -1.20 to 1.12) were not effective against SARS-CoV-2.

PVP-I mouthwash may be recommended for reducing SARS-CoV-2 viral load in the oral cavity before and during dental procedures.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the effectiveness of mouthwashes in reducing SARS-CoV-2 viral load in the oral cavity. Nine clinical trials with low risk of bias were included. Meta-analysis showed that 1% povidone-iodine (PVP-I) mouthwash significantly increased cycle threshold (Ct) values (MD = 3.61, 95% CI: 1.03 to 6.19), indicating viral load reduction. The effect of PVP-I was sustained from 5 minutes to 6 hours after rinsing. Cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC, MD = 0.61, 95% CI: -1.03 to 2.25, p = 0.47) and chlorhexidine gluconate (CHX, MD = -0.04, 95% CI: -1.20 to 1.12, p = 0.95) showed no significant effect. The authors concluded that PVP-I mouthwash may be recommended to reduce SARS-CoV-2 viral load in dental settings, while evidence for CPC and CHX is insufficient.

## Key Contributions

- PVP-I mouthwash significantly reduces SARS-CoV-2 viral load (MD = 3.61 Ct increase)
- PVP-I effect sustained from 5 minutes to 6 hours post-rinsing
- CPC mouthwash not effective against SARS-CoV-2 (MD = 0.61, p = 0.47)
- CHX mouthwash not effective against SARS-CoV-2 (MD = -0.04, p = 0.95)
- PVP-I recommended for pre-procedural use during COVID-19 pandemic
- All included studies had low risk of bias

## Methodology

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed PRISMA 2020 guidelines with a registered protocol (PROSPERO CRD42021274832). A comprehensive search was conducted in PubMed, EMBASE, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Central, and grey literature up to July 20, 2022. Eligible studies were RCTs, non-RCTs, and quasi-experimental studies evaluating mouthwashes in COVID-19 patients with Ct value or viral load outcomes. Nine studies were included. Risk of bias was assessed using the modified Downs and Black checklist; all studies had low risk of bias (scores ≥5). Meta-analysis used random-effects models with subgroup analysis by mouthwash type and time points. GRADE indicated moderate certainty (downgraded for heterogeneity).
