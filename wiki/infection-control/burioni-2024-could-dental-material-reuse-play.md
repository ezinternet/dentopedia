## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (IRCCS San Raffaele, Milan; TG=40 patient-used+sterilized vs CG=35 new Winsix titanium healing abutments) using Micro BCA protein assay and turbidity-based sterility testing to decouple sterilization efficacy from surface cleanliness.

The decontamination + autoclave protocol achieved 100% sterility (no bacterial growth in either group), but reused HAs carried significantly more residual surface protein than new ones (18.76 vs 9.35 µg/mL; mean difference 9.41, 95% CI 4.28–14.54; t(30)=4.52, p<0.001; ~12 µg/screw); the clinical significance of this residual-protein difference cannot be determined a priori.

Clinical implication: "sterile" is not the same as "clean" for reused HAs — the protocol reliably kills bacteria but does not strip the residual denatured protein/organic film; this paper quantifies the gap the anchor SR (Bidra 2019) identified and provides the cleanliness-side mechanistic substrate for Abreu 2024's inflammatory-response finding.

## Summary

Burioni et al. tested whether reprocessing (decontamination + steam sterilization) returns patient-used titanium healing abutments (HAs) to a state equivalent to new ones, using two independent endpoints: microbiological sterility and residual surface protein. Forty reused+sterilized HAs (test group, TG) were compared with 35 new HAs (control group, CG).

The central finding decouples **sterility from cleanliness**. Every sample — reused and new — was culture-negative after the protocol, so the reprocessing was fully effective against viable bacteria. But a Micro BCA protein assay (OD 562 nm) showed reused HAs carried significantly more residual surface protein than new ones. Sterilization killed the organisms but did not strip the adherent organic film (denatured proteins/peptides from blood, saliva, plaque biofilm, and gingival epithelium) off the titanium.

This is the quantitative in vitro counterpart to the anchor SR [[infection-control/bidra-2019-should-healing-abutments-cover-screws]]: routine reprocessing does not reliably restore a virgin surface. Residual denatured protein is a theoretical immune/inflammatory stimulus (supported by Sennerby's in vivo rat data showing increased macrophage activation and fibrous encapsulation around used Ti), but Burioni explicitly state the **clinical significance of the residual-protein difference cannot be determined a priori**. The study neither endorses nor forbids reuse — it establishes that "sterile" is not the same as "clean" and that the clinical threshold for the observed protein burden is unknown.

## Key Contributions

1. **Sterility ≠ cleanliness, demonstrated on patient-used screws.** 100% culture-negative yet significantly more residual protein on reused HAs — the two endpoints move independently.
2. **Quantified the residual-protein gap** with effect size and confidence interval on real clinical specimens (see Results).
3. **Reinforces the anchor SR's caution** against indiscriminate reuse without overstating harm — the difference exists; its clinical meaning is undetermined.
4. **Frames the reuse debate within sustainability** (raw-material/water/energy/cost, WHO 2030), while showing the microbiological data don't yet license routine reuse.

## Methodology

- **Specimens**: Winsix titanium HA screws (Biosafin, Cefla, Imola, Italy). TG = 40 abutments previously used in patients (one HA/patient, retrieved at implant reopening: 4–5 mo maxillary, 3 mo mandibular healing). CG = 35 new abutments.
- **Reprocessing**: decontamination + steam (autoclave) sterilization.
- **Sterility assay**: laminar-flow extraction → Tryptone Soya Broth → 24 h at 37 °C → turbidity = growth.
- **Surface-protein assay**: Micro BCA, spectrophotometry OD 562 nm; 500 µL eluate per screw.
- **Statistics**: SPSS v20; Shapiro-Wilk; one-way ANOVA + independent t-test; Tukey adjustment; α = 0.05. Reported per STROBE. Setting: IRCCS San Raffaele Hospital, Milan (Sept 2022–Oct 2023).

## Results

- **Sterility**: no turbidity in any sample (TG or CG) — reused HAs were culture-negative; the protocol eliminated all viable bacteria.
- **Residual surface protein**:
  - Reused (TG): **18.76 µg/mL** (SD 3.62)
  - New (CG): **9.35 µg/mL** (SD 2.15)
  - **Mean difference 9.41 µg/mL (95% CI 4.28–14.54)**; independent t-test **t(30)=4.52, p<0.001**
  - One-way ANOVA **F(2,45)=7.34, p<0.01**
- **Per-screw load**: TG eluate ≈ 23.88 µg/mL protein → with 500 µL solution, **~12 µg protein per screw**.
- **Clinical interpretation**: authors state the significance of the new-vs-reused difference **cannot be determined a priori** — residual denatured protein is a plausible immune trigger, but the clinically relevant threshold is unknown.
