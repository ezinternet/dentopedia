## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (5 databases, 812 screened → 8 included; January 2000–December 2020) of resterilization protocols for titanium healing abutments (HAs) intended for reuse; steam autoclave was the most widely used method across included studies.

No protocol achieved a 100% virgin (contaminant-free) surface — every evaluated method left residual microbial or organic traces, establishing the fundamental limitation of HA reuse.

Thorough surface decontamination followed by resterilization is recommended before reuse, with the reuse decision also weighing patient cost and preference; per-protocol efficiency figures were not reported in the abstract.

## Summary

Although healing abutments (HAs) are designated for single use by most implant manufacturers, clinicians commonly reuse them, and adequate references describing detailed sterilization protocols for reuse are lacking. This systematic review compiled and organized the most common HA sterilization techniques and evaluated their efficiency in eliminating traces of microorganisms.

An electronic search of five databases (MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, Web of Science, Google Scholar) covering January 2000 to December 2020 identified 812 articles, of which 8 met inclusion criteria (minimum 10 HAs, 5 per group; English). Steam autoclave was the most widely used form of resterilization. The pivotal finding is that **not a single protocol was able to achieve a 100% virgin surface** — every evaluated method left residual traces.

The review concludes that while reuse of HAs is a cost-effective measure, thorough surface decontamination followed by resterilization is highly recommended before reuse, with the reuse decision also weighing cost and patient preference. This is an abstract-only ingest (no PMC; paywalled), so per-protocol efficiency figures and the risk-of-bias ratings of the 8 studies are not available.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the systematic-review anchor for the HA reuse controversy: **no resterilization protocol restores a virgin surface**, so "reuse" is never contaminant-free.
- Identifies **steam autoclave** as the dominant HA resterilization modality in the literature.
- Positions reuse as a cost-effectiveness decision that must be gated by thorough decontamination + resterilization, not assumed safe on the basis of resterilization alone.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review with risk-of-bias assessment.
- **Databases**: MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, Web of Science, Google Scholar.
- **Window**: January 2000 – December 2020.
- **Inclusion**: ≥10 HAs (≥5 per group); English language.
- **Yield**: 812 identified → 8 included.
- Quantitative pooling / per-study synthesis: abstract-only (not retrievable from the abstract).

## Results

- **812 → 8** studies included.
- **Steam autoclave** = most widely used resterilization form.
- **No protocol achieved a 100% virgin surface** — residual microbial/contaminant traces persisted after every evaluated protocol.
- Conclusion: reuse is cost-effective, but **thorough decontamination + resterilization is highly recommended before reuse**; decision should also consider cost and patient preference.
- Per-protocol efficiency %, residual quantities, and risk-of-bias ratings: abstract-only (not reported in the abstract).
