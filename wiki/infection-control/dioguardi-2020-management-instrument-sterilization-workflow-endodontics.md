## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA, 130 eligible studies from PubMed/Scopus, 40-year search window) evaluating sterilization/disinfection methods for endodontic instruments and root-filling materials.

Meta-analysis found autoclaving superior to glutaraldehyde (I²=0%, fixed-effects) and glutaraldehyde superior to glass-bead sterilization (I²=73%, random-effects); autoclaving at minimum 120°C for ≥30 min is confirmed as the most effective method, with NiTi instrument reuse limited to ≤5 autoclave cycles and non-autoclavable gutta-percha/resin cones requiring chemical disinfection (NaOCl 2–5.25% or chlorhexidine 2%).

Clinically, a structured presterilization ultrasonic/washer-disinfector cleansing step is essential before final sterilization regardless of method, and glutaraldehyde is discouraged for routine outpatient dental use due to its 8–12h exposure requirement and toxicity.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰+메타분석(PRISMA, PubMed/Scopus 130편, 40년 검색창)으로 근관치료 기구·근관충전재의 멸균/소독 방법을 평가.

메타분석 결과 오토클레이브가 글루타르알데히드보다 우수(I²=0%, 고정효과모형), 글루타르알데히드가 유리구슬 멸균보다 우수(I²=73%, 랜덤효과모형); 최소 120°C·30분 이상 오토클레이브가 최적 방법으로 확인되었고, NiTi 파일 재사용은 오토클레이브 5회 이하로 제한, 열처리 불가능한 거타퍼차/레진 콘은 화학적 소독(NaOCl 2–5.25% 또는 클로르헥시딘 2%) 필요.

임상적으로는 최종 멸균 방법과 무관하게 초음파세척기/세척소독기를 이용한 사전세척 단계가 필수이며, 글루타르알데히드는 8–12시간 노출·독성 문제로 외래 진료 일상 사용에는 권장되지 않음.

## Summary

This PRISMA-guided systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizes 40 years of literature on sterilization and disinfection procedures for endodontic instrumentation, addressing which methods guarantee maximum sterility while remaining clinically practical. The authors screened 845 PubMed/Scopus records down to 130 eligible studies and ran two quantitative meta-analyses: autoclave vs. glutaraldehyde, and glutaraldehyde vs. glass-bead sterilization. Both favored the higher-tier method — autoclaving over glutaraldehyde, and glutaraldehyde over glass-bead — with autoclaving confirmed as the single most effective and clinically appropriate sterilization method for endodontic (and by extension general dental) reusable instruments.

The paper proposes a structured, phase-based reprocessing protocol: (1) presterilization cleansing/disinfection (ultrasonic bath or washer-disinfector outperforms manual scrubbing), (2) chemical disinfection of non-autoclavable single-use materials (gutta-percha and resin cones cannot tolerate autoclave heat), and (3) final sterilization proper, with autoclaving at ≥120°C for ≥30 min as the benchmark. It also quantifies mechanical wear on reused files under repeated autoclave cycles and proposes a concrete reuse-cycle ceiling for NiTi instruments.

## Key Contributions

1. First meta-analytic (not just narrative) comparison of endodontic instrument sterilization methods, directly quantifying the superiority of autoclaving.
2. Concrete, phase-structured protocol distinguishing reusable metal instruments from non-autoclavable single-use root-filling materials.
3. Evidence-based reuse-cycle limit for NiTi rotary/reciprocating instruments (≤5 autoclave cycles) based on cyclic-fatigue and torsional-resistance data.
4. Explicit chemical-disinfection regimen for gutta-percha/resin cones (NaOCl, chlorhexidine, peracetic acid) with time/concentration parameters.
5. Documents that burs arrive nonsterile from manufacturers and require autoclaving before first clinical use — not merely decontamination.

## Methodology

PRISMA-guided systematic review of PubMed and Scopus literature (22 Jan – 4 Feb 2019 search/update window), restricted to English-language works from the last 40 years. Six keyword searches ("endodontic sterilization," "endodontic autoclave," "decontamination dental burs," "sterilization dental burs," "gutta-percha cones/points sterilization") yielded 845 records → 761 after year-of-publication filtering → 130 eligible after full screening and deduplication. Two independent reviewers screened studies (κ = 0.675) with third/fourth reviewer arbitration for disagreements; Newcastle-Ottawa scale assessed risk of bias in observational studies; Review Manager 5.3 performed the meta-analyses (fixed-effects for low-heterogeneity comparisons, random-effects for high-heterogeneity comparisons).

## Results

- **Autoclave vs. glutaraldehyde** (Kumar et al., Raju et al., Venkatasubramanian et al., Hurtt & Rossman): I²=0% (low heterogeneity), fixed-effects model, result favors autoclaving.
- **Glutaraldehyde vs. glass-bead** (Hurtt & Rossman, Raju et al., Venkatasubramanian et al.): I²=73% (high heterogeneity), random-effects model, result favors glutaraldehyde.
- Ultrasonic-bath cleaning of non-containerized instruments achieved ~5% higher measured cleanliness than manual or containerized cleaning — presterilization cleansing should preferentially use ultrasonic/washer-disinfector methods, not manual scrubbing.
- **Final sterilization benchmark**: autoclaving at minimum 120°C for ≥30 min is most efficient for total destruction of bacteria, viruses, and spores. UV light (240–280nm) fails on hollow/shadowed instrument surfaces; glass-bead sterilization (45s at 240°C) is ineffective; CO2 laser sterilization performs comparably to autoclaving and better than chemical methods but is rarely used in clinical practice.
- **Prions**: only partially inactivated by standard sterilization/disinfection. WHO-suggested prion-inactivation protocols (NaOCl 20,000ppm for 1h; 1M NaOH heating for 1h; or vacuum autoclaving at 121°C for 30–90 min with NaOH) are impractical for routine dental clinics due to corrosivity, mechanical damage, and time cost.
- **Mechanical effects of repeated autoclaving on files**: conflicting evidence — NiTi files may show improved fatigue resistance after autoclaving (a thermal-treatment side effect), while steel files show reduced torsional resistance and cutting capacity with repeated cycles. Cutting-capacity reduction reported at 20% after 7 cycles in one study and 1–12% after 5–10 cycles in another. **Recommendation: limit NiTi instrument reuse to ≤5 autoclave cycles.**
- **Non-autoclavable materials protocol**: gutta-percha and resin cones cannot tolerate autoclave heat. Disinfect with NaOCl 2–5.25% (5–10 min) or chlorhexidine 2% (1–2 min) as primary methods; peracetic acid 1–2% (2.5–5 min) is a valid alternative. Glutaraldehyde requires 8–12h exposure and is highly toxic — not recommended for outpatient dental use. Rinse with distilled water or alcohol afterward to remove chlorine crystal residue.
- **Burs**: supplied nonsterile by manufacturers; autoclaving is the most effective sterilization system; ultrasonic baths/washer-disinfectors alone cannot remove all detritus lodged between blades.
