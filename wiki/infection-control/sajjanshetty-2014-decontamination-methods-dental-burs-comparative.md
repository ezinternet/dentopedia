## Three-line Summary

In-vitro comparative study (n=96 round diamond burs, 6 groups) assessing 5 decontamination methods (manual scrubbing, hot air oven, glass bead sterilizer, ultrasonic cleaner, autoclave) used routinely in dental clinics.

Autoclave achieved the greatest colony-forming-unit (CFU) reduction for Streptococcus mutans (80%) and Lactobacilli (76%); glass bead sterilizer achieved the greatest reduction for Candida albicans (74%).

No method achieved complete/absolute decontamination of the burs — autoclave was the relatively best method overall, but organism-specific residual contamination persisted across all groups.

## Summary

This abstract-only in-vitro study addresses a narrow but clinically important question: how effective are the decontamination methods routinely used on dental burs in real clinics — manual scrubbing, hot air oven, glass bead sterilizer, ultrasonic cleaning, and autoclave — at removing organic/microbial contamination from a complex-geometry rotary instrument? Ninety-six round diamond burs were contaminated via clinical use (access cavity preparation), cultured for three organisms relevant to the oral cavity (Streptococcus mutans, Lactobacilli, Candida albicans), then decontaminated by one of the five methods and re-cultured. The key finding is that autoclave is the best-performing method overall (highest CFU reduction for two of the three organisms), but even autoclave — and every other method tested — fell short of absolute/complete decontamination.

## Key Contributions

1. Directly compares five decontamination methods head-to-head on the same instrument type (diamond burs) using the same baseline-contamination protocol, rather than testing each method in isolation.
2. Reports organism-specific efficacy rather than a single pooled "decontamination rate," showing that the best method differs by organism (autoclave for bacteria, glass bead sterilizer for the fungal organism tested).
3. Empirically demonstrates that terminal sterilization (autoclave) does not guarantee complete decontamination of a bur without adequate presterilization cleaning — a finding with direct implications for cross-contamination risk in endodontic/restorative practice.

## Methodology

96 round diamond burs, divided into 6 groups (5 decontamination-method groups + baseline/control). Burs were used clinically for access cavity preparation to generate standardized contamination, then baseline bacteriological cultures were taken. Each group's burs were then subjected to one of: manual scrubbing, hot air oven, glass bead sterilizer, ultrasonic cleaner, or autoclave, followed by post-decontamination culture. Outcome measure: % reduction in mean colony-forming units (CFU)/mL for each of three organisms (S. mutans, Lactobacilli, C. albicans), comparing post-decontamination to baseline. ## Results

| Organism | Best-performing method | % CFU reduction |
|---|---|---|
| Streptococcus mutans | Autoclave | 80% |
| Lactobacilli | Autoclave | 76% |
| Candida albicans | Glass bead sterilizer | 74% |

- Autoclave produced the greatest overall reduction across the bacterial organisms tested and was judged the relatively best method among all five.
- Glass bead sterilizer outperformed autoclave specifically for the fungal organism (C. albicans).
- **No method tested achieved complete/absolute decontamination** — residual CFUs remained detectable in every group, including autoclave, indicating that the tested reprocessing protocols (as applied) did not reliably eliminate all organic/microbial burden from the bur surface and flutes.
