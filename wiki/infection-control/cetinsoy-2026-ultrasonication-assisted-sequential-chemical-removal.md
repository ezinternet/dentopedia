## One-line Summary

In vitro study on 95 retrieved titanium healing abutments (2 brands): after enzymatic-detergent precleaning, ultrasonication in 1% sodium hypochlorite + autoclave removed 99.7% of mature biofilm (vs 54.4% for autoclave-only control), with SEM/EDX surface characteristics resembling unused abutments.

## Summary

Reuse of titanium healing abutments (HAs) is widespread in practice, but the infection-control literature is split on whether any chairside protocol can restore a clinically clean surface. A prominent systematic review (Eswaramurthy 2021) concluded that **no protocol reproduces a 100% virgin (unused) surface**, and several in vitro studies report **persistent residual protein** (Burioni 2024) or **persistent inflammatory response** after conventional detoxification (Abreu 2024), arguing against reuse.

This in vitro study — the newest in the set (2026, abstract-only; full text not yet on PMC) — refines the "cannot fully clean" narrative from the **pro-decontamination / biofilm-removal** pole. Using **95 clinically retrieved HAs** of two distinct brands/designs bearing **mature** (4–6 week intraoral) biofilm, it holds autoclaving constant and varies only the ultrasonic debridement medium. **Autoclave alone is the weakest option** (54.4% debridement), while **ultrasonication (US) in 1% sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl)** removes **99.7%** of biofilm and leaves a surface whose SEM morphology and EDX elemental composition **resemble unused abutments**. The authors propose a concrete, low-cost **three-step protocol** — enzymatic-detergent (ED) soak → US in 1% NaOCl → autoclave — as an efficient fallback for **at least one reuse cycle** where placing a new HA is not feasible.

The result is a surface-and-debridement claim, not a biological-safety claim: it does not measure peri-implant tissue response and therefore sits alongside, not on top of, the inflammation-based caution from Abreu 2024.

## Key Contributions

- Compares 5 protocols on **clinically retrieved, mature-biofilm** HAs (not lab-inoculated surfaces).
- Shows **autoclave-only** is the poorest debridement approach (54.4%), isolating the debridement agent's contribution.
- Establishes **1% NaOCl + US** as the standout (99.7% removal) with **no detectable surface alteration** (SEM/EDX ≈ unused).
- Result is **independent of HA macrogeometry** (two brands: 99.6% and 99.9%).
- Proposes a defined, cost-effective **ED soak → US + 1% NaOCl → autoclave** protocol, explicitly bounded to **≥ 1 reuse cycle** and to cases where a new HA is not feasible.

## Methodology

- **Design**: in vitro comparison of 5 decontamination protocols on 95 retrieved titanium HAs (2 brands/designs), retrieved after 4–6 weeks intraorally.
- **Precleaning**: all HAs immersed in enzymatic detergent (ED) before allocation.
- **Groups**: (1) autoclave only [control]; (2) US in 40°C tap water + autoclave; (3) US in 40°C **1% NaOCl** + autoclave; (4) US in 80°C **citric + maleic + lactic acid** + autoclave; (5) US in 40°C **ED** + autoclave.
- **Outcome**: staining + before/after photography (lateral + occlusal); residual stained area quantified as contamination proxy.
- **Surface analysis**: SEM (morphology) + EDX/EDS (elemental composition) vs unused HAs.
- **Limitation**: abstract-only retrieval — US parameters, staining/quantification detail, and full statistics not recoverable. In vitro; no peri-implant biological outcome measured.

## Results

- **Autoclave-only control**: lowest debridement, mean **54.4%**, highest residual contamination.
- **1% NaOCl + US**: mean **99.7% biofilm removal** (Brand1 99.6%, Brand2 99.9%), superior to all other groups (p < 0.05).
- **SEM/EDX**: NaOCl-decontaminated surface morphology and elemental composition **similar to unused HAs** — no detectable surface degradation.
- Effect **holds across both brand macrogeometries**.
- **Bottom line**: where a new HA is not feasible, **ED soak → US in 1% NaOCl → autoclave** is an efficient, cost-effective option for **at least one reuse cycle**.
