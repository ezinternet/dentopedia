## Three-line Summary

This Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis included 40 randomized controlled trials (5675 participants) evaluating oral hygiene care interventions for preventing ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) in critically ill patients receiving mechanical ventilation for at least 48 hours in intensive care units. Searches were conducted up to February 2020 across multiple databases including CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, LILACS, and trial registries.

Moderate-certainty evidence from 13 studies (1206 participants) showed that chlorhexidine (CHX) mouthrinse or gel probably reduces VAP incidence (RR 0.67, 95% CI 0.47 to 0.97; I²=66%; NNTB 12, 95% CI 7 to 128) compared to placebo/usual care, with no difference in mortality (RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.84 to 1.23; I²=0%). Low-certainty evidence from 5 studies (910 participants) suggested toothbrushing may reduce VAP incidence (RR 0.61, 95% CI 0.41 to 0.91; I²=40%), with no difference in mortality (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.67 to 1.05; I²=0%).

CHX mouthwash/gel probably prevents VAP in critically ill patients, though no mortality benefit was demonstrated. Toothbrushing may also reduce VAP, but evidence is of lower certainty. Adverse effects were poorly reported across studies, limiting safety conclusions. Future research should use standardized VAP definitions and report adverse events systematically.

## Summary

This Cochrane systematic review evaluated the effectiveness of oral hygiene care (OHC) interventions for preventing ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) in critically ill patients receiving mechanical ventilation for at least 48 hours in intensive care units. The review included 40 randomized controlled trials with 5675 participants, with searches updated to February 2020. Two primary comparisons were emphasized: chlorhexidine (CHX) mouthrinse or gel versus placebo/usual care, and toothbrushing versus no toothbrushing. For CHX, moderate-certainty evidence from 13 studies showed a 33% relative reduction in VAP incidence (RR 0.67, 95% CI 0.47 to 0.97; NNTB 12), with no difference in mortality. For toothbrushing, low-certainty evidence from 5 studies suggested a 39% relative reduction in VAP incidence (RR 0.61, 95% CI 0.41 to 0.91), with no mortality benefit. Duration of ICU stay may be reduced by toothbrushing (very low certainty). Most studies did not adequately report adverse effects, limiting safety assessments. The review concluded that CHX-based OHC probably prevents VAP, but the balance between benefits and potential harms remains uncertain; toothbrushing may also be beneficial but evidence is less certain. Future high-quality trials with standardized diagnostic criteria and comprehensive safety reporting are needed.

## Key Contributions

- Moderate-certainty evidence: Chlorhexidine reduces VAP incidence by 33% (RR 0.67, 95% CI 0.47 to 0.97; NNTB 12) in critically ill patients.
- Moderate-certainty evidence: No mortality benefit from chlorhexidine (RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.84 to 1.23).
- Low-certainty evidence: Toothbrushing may reduce VAP incidence by 39% (RR 0.61, 95% CI 0.41 to 0.91).
- Low-certainty evidence: No mortality benefit from toothbrushing (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.67 to 1.05).
- Very low-certainty evidence: Toothbrushing may reduce ICU stay duration (MD -1.89 days, 95% CI -3.52 to -0.27).
- Adverse effects poorly reported; only mild reversible oral mucosal irritation documented in one CHX study.
- Updated review including 40 trials (5675 participants); 9 new studies; 7 previously included studies excluded; new comparison (CHX vs other oral agents) added.

## Methodology

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed Cochrane methodology. The Information Specialist searched Cochrane Oral Health's Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, LILACS, VIP Database, and trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP) up to February 2020 without language restrictions. Eligible studies were RCTs evaluating any OHC intervention (mouthrinse, gel, swab, toothbrush, or combination) in critically ill patients receiving mechanical ventilation for ≥48 hours without baseline respiratory infection. Two review authors independently screened search results, extracted data, and assessed risk of bias using the Cochrane domain-based tool. Primary outcomes were VAP incidence (defined as pneumonia developing after ≥48 hours of mechanical ventilation) and mortality (ICU or 30-day all-cause). Meta-analyses used random-effects models when ≥4 trials were combined, reporting risk ratios for dichotomous outcomes and mean differences for continuous outcomes. Heterogeneity was assessed with Chi² and I² statistics. Certainty of evidence was rated using GRADE. Subgroup analyses included toothbrushing versus no toothbrushing and chlorhexidine concentration. Overall, 40 RCTs (5675 participants) were included; 29 (73%) were at high risk of bias in at least one domain; only 2 studies were at low risk of bias overall.

**Adverse effects:** One study (Tantipong 2008) reported mild reversible irritation of oral mucosa: 10/102 (10%) in CHX group vs 1/105 (1%) in control (RR 10.29, 95% CI 1.34 to 78.97). Most studies did not report adverse effects. Four studies explicitly stated no adverse effects; two additional studies reported no significant difference in adverse events.

**Evidence certainty (GRADE):** CHX vs control: VAP (moderate), mortality (moderate), duration of ventilation (very low), ICU stay (low), adverse effects (very low). Toothbrushing vs no: VAP (low), mortality (low), duration of ventilation (low), ICU stay (very low), adverse effects (very low).
