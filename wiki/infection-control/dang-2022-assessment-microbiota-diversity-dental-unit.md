## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study of 60 dental chair units (350 culture samples plus 16S rDNA sequencing of air/water syringe water) quantifying dental unit waterline (DUWL) contamination.

Median 166 CFU/mL (range 0–3,816,000); only 42.6% of samples met the 100 CFU/mL threshold; Proteobacteria dominated (>85%), and 7 potential human pathogens were detected including Pseudomonas (31%) and Acinetobacter (7.6%).

Air/water (three-way) syringe output water is heavily and variably contaminated; flushing alone did not lower counts because suck-back reintroduces oral fluids, so validated disinfection and functioning anti-retraction valves are needed.

## Summary

Dang et al. (PeerJ 2022) measured the microbial burden of dental unit waterlines (DUWLs) at a Peking University dental hospital, sampling the three output points a patient is exposed to — high-speed handpieces, **air/water (three-way) syringes**, and mouth-rinse outlets — across 60 dental chair units in periodontics, endodontics, and prosthodontics. Beyond culture, they applied 16S rDNA high-throughput sequencing specifically to **250-mL air/water syringe water samples**, giving a culture-independent picture of what actually flows out of the syringe.

The contamination was substantial and highly variable: median 166 CFU/mL but a range spanning 0 to 3,816,000 CFU/mL, with only 42.6% of samples below the 100 CFU/mL standard. Crucially, counts **did not differ before vs after daily practice** (192 vs 160 CFU/mL, P=0.104): morning flushing does not solve the problem because **suck-back** (retraction of saliva/blood on instrument release) continually reseeds the line, and biofilm in the narrow tubing (0.5–2 mm lumen, 6:1 surface-to-volume ratio, stagnation) sheds bacteria into the output. The paper directly implicates **anti-retraction valve failure** (a cited 51.72% failure rate after months of use) and recommends retraction volume stay ≤40 μL.

## Key Contributions

1. **Three-way syringe water profiled by sequencing.** The 16S analysis targeted air/water syringe water (60 × 250 mL), not just handpiece water, showing Proteobacteria dominance (>85%) and 7 potential human-pathogenic genera >1%: Pseudomonas (31%), Acinetobacter (7.6%, up to 21% in prosthodontics), Ochrobactrum, Sphingomonas, Methylobacterium, Rhizobium, Brevundimonas.
2. **Contamination is severe and standard-failing.** Only 42.6% met 100 CFU/mL; peak 3.8 million CFU/mL.
3. **Flushing is not enough.** No before/after difference confirms suck-back and biofilm shedding, not just overnight stagnation, drive contamination.
4. **Specialty-dependent community structure.** Periodontics differed from endodontics/prosthodontics (PCoA/ADONIS), attributed to suck-back of oral microorganisms and hydrodynamic-stress differences.

## Methodology

- 60 DCUs (19 perio / 21 endo / 20 prostho); municipal feed water (China standard <100 CFU/mL).
- 350 × 5-mL culture samples (BHI, 48 h, 37 °C, 5% CO₂); threshold 100 CFU/mL.
- 60 × 250-mL **air/water syringe** samples → 0.2-μm filtration → DNA; 17 sequenced (16S V3–V4, Illumina MiSeq PE250, 97% OTUs).
- Kruskal–Wallis (specialties), Wilcoxon signed-rank (before/after), PCoA/ADONIS, LEfSe.

## Clinical Takeaways

- Treat the **three-way syringe** as a genuine microbial output, not a "clean air/water" source; its water carries the same DUWL biofilm burden as the handpiece.
- **Morning flush alone is insufficient** — suck-back reseeds the line during the day. Pair flushing with a **validated chemical disinfection protocol** and a **working anti-retraction valve** (retraction ≤40 μL).
- Immunocompromised/medically compromised patients face the highest opportunistic-infection risk (Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, Legionella detected in the periodontics line).
