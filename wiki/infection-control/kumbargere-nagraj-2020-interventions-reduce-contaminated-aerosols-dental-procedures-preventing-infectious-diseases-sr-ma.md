## Three-line Summary

This Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis included 16 studies (425 participants) to assess the effectiveness of methods to reduce contaminated aerosols during dental procedures for preventing infectious diseases. The review found no studies that evaluated disease transmission via aerosols and all evidence was based on surrogate outcomes (bacterial colony-forming units, CFU).

For high-volume evacuation (HVE), a meta-analysis of three split-mouth RCTs (122 participants) showed a reduction in bacterial contamination at less than one foot from the patient's mouth (MD -47.41 CFU, 95% CI -92.76 to -2.06, I²=95%) but not at longer distances. Rubber dam use showed a reduction at one meter (MD -16.20 CFU, 95% CI -19.36 to -13.04, one RCT, 10 participants) and two meters (MD -11.70, 95% CI -15.82 to -7.58) from the mouth. Antimicrobial coolants (chlorhexidine, povidone iodine, cinnamon extract) reduced CFU compared to water.

The overall certainty of evidence was very low for all interventions due to risk of bias, inconsistency, and imprecision. No studies measured infection transmission or viral contamination. The authors concluded that while interventions may reduce bacterial contamination, reliable conclusions cannot be drawn. High-quality RCTs measuring direct outcomes (infection rates, viable particles) are needed.

## 세줄요약

이 코크란 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석은 치과 시술 중 오염된 에어로졸을 줄여 감염성 질환을 예방하는 방법의 효과를 평가하기 위해 16개 연구(참가자 425명)를 분석했다. 본 고찰에서는 에어로졸을 통한 질병 전파를 평가한 연구는 없었으며, 모든 근거는 대리 결과물(세균 집락형성단위, CFU)에 기반했다.

고용량 흡입기(HVE)의 경우, 3개 분할구강 무작위배정 임상시험(참가자 122명)의 메타분석에서 환자 구강에서 1피트(약 30cm) 미만 거리에서 세균 오염 감소를 보였으나(MD -47.41 CFU, 95% CI -92.76 to -2.06, I²=95%), 더 먼 거리에서는 효과가 없었다. 러버댐 사용은 구강에서 1미터(MD -16.20 CFU, 95% CI -19.36 to -13.04, RCT 1개, 참가자 10명) 및 2미터(MD -11.70, 95% CI -15.82 to -7.58) 거리에서 감소를 보였다. 항균 쿨런트(클로르헥시딘, 포비돈-아이오딘, 계피 추출물)는 물과 비교하여 CFU를 감소시켰다.

모든 중재에 대한 근거의 확실성은 비뚤림 위험, 불일치 및 부정확성으로 인해 매우 낮았다. 감염 전파나 바이러스 오염을 측정한 연구는 없었다. 저자들은 중재가 세균 오염을 감소시킬 수 있지만 신뢰할 수 있는 결론을 도출할 수 없다고 결론지었다. 직접적인 결과(감염률, 생존 가능한 입자)를 측정하는 고품질의 무작위배정 임상시험이 필요하다.

## Summary

This Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis assessed the effectiveness of interventions to reduce contaminated aerosols produced during dental procedures for preventing infectious diseases, motivated by concerns during the COVID-19 pandemic. The review included 16 studies (11 RCTs, 5 CCTs) with 425 participants. No studies evaluated the primary outcome of infection incidence in dental staff or patients; all evidence was based on the surrogate outcome of bacterial colony-forming units (CFU). Meta-analysis of three split-mouth RCTs (n=122) found high-volume evacuation (HVE) may reduce bacterial contamination at less than one foot (∼30 cm) from the patient's mouth (MD -47.41 CFU, 95% CI -92.76 to -2.06, I²=95%), but not at longer distances. Rubber dam use showed reductions at one meter (MD -16.20 CFU, 95% CI -19.36 to -13.04) and two meters (MD -11.70, 95% CI -15.82 to -7.58). Antimicrobial coolants (chlorhexidine, povidone iodine, cinnamon extract) reduced CFU compared to water. However, the certainty of evidence was uniformly very low due to risk of bias, inconsistency, and imprecision. The review found no evidence on viral contamination. The authors conclude that while there may be benefits, reliable conclusions cannot be drawn, and high-quality RCTs measuring direct outcomes (infection rates, viable particles) are urgently needed.

## Key Contributions

- First Cochrane systematic review evaluating aerosol-reduction interventions for preventing infectious diseases in dentistry.
- Comprehensive search identified only surrogate outcome (bacterial CFU) studies; no studies measured infection transmission.
- Meta-analysis shows potential CFU reduction with HVE (MD -47.41 CFU, 95% CI -92.76 to -2.06, I²=95%).
- Rubber dam shows reduction in CFU at 1m and 2m distances.
- Antimicrobial coolants (chlorhexidine, povidone iodine, cinnamon) show CFU reduction compared to water.
- All evidence rated VERY LOW certainty; no conclusions on superiority of any intervention.

## Methodology

This Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis (rapid review) searched Cochrane Oral Health's Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE (1946), Embase (1980), WHO COVID-19 database, ClinicalTrials.gov, and Cochrane COVID-19 Study Register up to September 17, 2020, with no language restrictions. Eligible studies were RCTs and CCTs on aerosol-generating procedures (AGPs) performed by dental healthcare providers, evaluating methods to reduce contaminated aerosols (excluding preprocedural mouthrinses). Two review authors screened studies, extracted data, assessed risk of bias (Cochrane Risk of Bias tool), and judged certainty of evidence using GRADE. Random-effects meta-analysis was used to combine data, with mean differences (MD) and 95% CIs for continuous outcomes, and generic inverse variance for split-mouth studies. Heterogeneity was assessed using I². Sixteen studies (11 RCTs, 5 CCTs) with 425 participants were included, but most comparisons were based on single trials.
