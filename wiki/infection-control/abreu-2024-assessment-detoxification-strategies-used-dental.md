## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (50 used healing abutments, 5 enzymatic-cleaner decontamination strategies A–E, n=10 each; 10 new sterile HAs as controls) comparing macroscopic/proteomic cleanliness (Micro BCA residual protein + Phloxine B debris staining) against biologic inertness (human primary macrophage 9-cytokine multiplex, up to 5 days).

Groups D/E (prophy jet + enzymatic steps) removed the most debris and protein, Group A the least; however, all cleaned groups — including the cleanest — still induced high inflammatory cytokine secretion vs new controls for up to 5 days, demonstrating that cleanliness does not predict biologic inertness.

Clinical implication: the strongest explicit anti-reuse conclusion in the batch — clinicians should NOT reuse healing abutments even after enzymatic decontamination and sterilization; this "sterile ≠ inert" decoupling extends the anchor SR's caution (Bidra 2019) into a biologic-response endpoint.

## Summary

This in vitro study is the **biologic-response pole** of the healing-abutment (HA) reuse debate. Where most of the reuse literature argues on whether a used HA can be made *clean* (residual debris/protein removal), Abreu 2024 asks the downstream question: even the best-cleaned used HA — does it still provoke host inflammation? Fifty HAs recovered after 2–4 weeks of intraoral use were split into five enzymatic-cleaner decontamination strategies, with ten new sterile HAs as controls. Cleanliness was measured (Micro BCA residual protein + Phloxine B debris staining), and biologic inertness was tested by exposing human primary macrophages to the abutments and profiling nine cytokines/chemokines over up to five days.

The key finding is a **decoupling of cleanliness from biologic inertness**. The strategies that removed the most debris and protein (Groups D and E, both using a prophy jet) were not the strategies that avoided inflammation — because *every* cleaned group, including the cleanest, still triggered high inflammatory cytokine secretion relative to new controls. Enzymatic-cleaner decontamination therefore failed to re-establish an inert surface. The authors give the strongest explicit anti-reuse recommendation in this batch: clinicians should not reuse HAs even after attempts to decontaminate and sterilize. Ingested abstract-only (no PMC; paywalled), so per-group numeric values are not reported here and are not invented.

## Key Contributions

- Supplies the **biologic-response endpoint** missing from the surface-cleanliness literature: tests whether a decontaminated used HA still induces host inflammation, not just whether it looks clean.
- Documents a **decoupling** between macroscopic/proteomic cleanliness and biologic inertness — Groups D/E were cleanest yet still inflammatory, so residual-protein reduction did not predict an inert surface.
- Delivers the **strongest explicit anti-reuse conclusion** in the reuse-controversy set: do not reuse HAs even after decontamination + sterilization.

## Methodology

- **Specimens**: 50 used healing abutments (uHAs), collected after 2–4 weeks intraoral use, randomized into 5 groups (A–E; n=10 each). Control = 10 new sterile HAs.
- **Decontamination strategies** (all enzymatic-cleaner-based):
  - A: enzymatic cleaner foam + autoclave
  - B: ultrasonic bath with enzymatic cleaner + autoclave
  - C: prophy jet + enzymatic cleaner foam + autoclave
  - D: prophy jet + ultrasonic bath with enzymatic cleaner + autoclave
  - E: prophy jet + autoclave
- **Cleanliness**: Micro BCA protein assay (residual protein) + Phloxine B staining with macroscopic debris examination.
- **Biologic response**: human primary macrophages exposed to the HAs; supernatant 9-cytokine/chemokine profile via multiplex bead assay, tracked up to 5 days.
- **Confidence**: `in-vitro`. Abstract-only ingestion — exact residual-protein values and individual cytokine magnitudes unavailable.

## Results

- All test groups differed in visual decontamination vs new controls (none matched a virgin surface).
- **Groups D and E** = most effective debris removal + lowest residual protein; **Group A** = least effective.
- **However**: vs controls, **ALL** test groups showed high inflammatory cytokine secretion for up to 5 days.
- **Conclusion**: enzymatic-cleaner decontamination failed to re-establish an inert HA surface or prevent an in vitro inflammatory response → clinicians should NOT reuse HAs even after decontamination/sterilization.
