## Three-line Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the efficacy of preprocedural mouthrinses in reducing the number of microorganisms in aerosol generated during dental procedures, including 13 RCTs (397 participants). Meta-analysis showed preprocedural mouthrinses significantly reduced colony-forming units by 64.8% (95% CI: 50.4% to 79.3%, I² = 37%, moderate-quality evidence). Chlorhexidine (78.9% reduction), cetylpyridinium chloride (61.2% reduction), and essential oils (61.3% reduction) were all significantly effective.

There is moderate evidence that preprocedural mouthrinses significantly reduce microorganisms in dental aerosol, potentially reducing cross-contamination risk in dental offices.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the efficacy of preprocedural mouthrinses in reducing microorganisms in dental procedure-generated aerosol. Thirteen randomized controlled trials (397 participants) were included. Meta-analysis demonstrated that preprocedural mouthrinses significantly reduced colony-forming units (CFU) by 64.8% compared to control (95% CI: 50.4% to 79.3%, moderate-quality evidence). Subgroup analyses showed chlorhexidine reduced CFUs by 78.9% (I² = 0%), cetylpyridinium chloride by 61.2% (I² = 0%), and essential oils by 61.3% (I² = 76%). Herbal mouthrinse showed no significant reduction (35.9%, p = 0.08). The authors concluded that there is moderate evidence that preprocedural mouthrinses significantly reduce microorganisms in dental aerosol, which may help reduce cross-contamination risk in dental offices. However, all included studies had high or unclear risk of bias, and no studies were rated low risk.

## Key Contributions

- Preprocedural mouthrinses reduce aerosol CFUs by 64.8% overall
- Chlorhexidine is most effective (78.9% reduction, I² = 0%)
- CPC and essential oils also significantly effective (61.2% and 61.3% reduction)
- Herbal mouthrinse not significantly effective (p = 0.08)
- Moderate-quality evidence supports preprocedural rinsing for infection control
- No study had low risk of bias; allocation concealment uniformly absent

## Methodology

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed PRISMA guidelines with a registered protocol (PROSPERO CRD42018090207). A structured search was conducted in MEDLINE (PubMed), Embase, Google Scholar, and LILACS through May 31, 2019. Eligible studies were RCTs comparing preprocedural mouthrinse versus placebo, water, or no rinse in patients undergoing aerosol-generating dental procedures. Thirteen RCTs (397 participants) were included, with 12 in the meta-analysis. Risk of bias was assessed using the Cochrane RoB tool; no study had low risk (6 high risk, 7 unclear risk). Meta-analysis used random-effects models with generic inverse variance. GRADE was used for evidence quality rating.
