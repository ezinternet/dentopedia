## Three-line Summary

In-vitro decontamination assay study on 30 clinically used dental burs plus experimentally contaminated unused burs (Streptococcus sanguis NCTC 7863 alone, or combined with blood/saliva/blood+saliva), comparing six pre-sterilisation cleaning techniques.

Microbial counts on used burs ranged 0–6.92×10^4 CFU/mL with multiple pathogenic species identified; autoclaving alone was not sufficient to sterilise dental burs, manual scrubbing in air was significantly less effective than manual scrubbing under water (p<0.001), and washer-disinfectors were the most effective pre-sterilisation cleaning method.

Clinical implication: pre-sterilisation cleaning (ideally washer-disinfector, or enzymic soak immediately after use) is a mandatory prerequisite step before autoclaving dental burs — autoclave sterilization cannot be relied upon alone; abstract-only record (no PMC full text retrieved).

## Summary

This foundational 2004 British Dental Journal study developed a novel assay system to compare the efficacy of pre-sterilisation cleaning techniques for dental burs, and characterised the microbial contamination present on clinically used burs. Thirty burs removed from clinical use after cavity preparation were microbiologically analysed, alongside unused burs experimentally contaminated with *Streptococcus sanguis* NCTC 7863 alone or in combination with blood, saliva, or blood+saliva to simulate real-world clinical soiling. Six pre-sterilisation cleaning techniques were then tested for their ability to reduce this contamination. The key finding — that autoclaving alone was not sufficient to sterilise dental burs — establishes an important scope limitation for autoclave sterilization: the autoclave process itself does not remove organic debris or biofilm, and cannot be relied upon to compensate for inadequate pre-cleaning. Washer-disinfectors emerged as the most effective pre-sterilisation cleaning method, with enzymic agents recommended as a suitable immediate post-use soak. This record is abstract-only (no PMC full text or PDF retrieved); detailed statistical results per technique beyond those stated in the abstract are not available in this wiki entry.

## Key Contributions

- Novel assay system for quantitatively comparing pre-sterilisation cleaning technique efficacy on dental burs.
- Microbiological characterisation (bacterial and fungal species) of clinically used dental burs.
- Experimental demonstration that autoclaving alone does not sterilise contaminated dental burs.
- Comparative ranking of six pre-sterilisation cleaning techniques by log reduction in contamination achieved.
- Clinical recommendation: washer-disinfectors as method of choice for pre-sterilisation cleaning; enzymic agents suitable for immediate post-use soak.

## Methodology

- **Clinically contaminated arm**: 30 dental burs contaminated during clinical cavity preparation were collected and analysed for microbiological total viable counts and identification of bacterial/fungal species present.
- **Experimentally contaminated arm**: Unused dental burs were inoculated with a culture of *Streptococcus sanguis* NCTC 7863, either alone or combined with blood, saliva, or a mixture of blood and saliva, to simulate a range of clinically realistic contamination scenarios.
- **Intervention comparison**: Contaminated burs were subjected to six pre-sterilisation cleaning techniques, including (among others) manual scrubbing in air, manual scrubbing under water, enzymic agents, and washer-disinfectors.
- **Outcome measure**: Log reduction in microbial contamination achieved by each cleaning method.

## Results

- Microbial count from used dental burs ranged from 0 to 6.92 × 10^4 CFU/mL, with many potentially pathogenic bacterial and fungal species identified.
- **Autoclaving alone was not sufficient to sterilise dental burs** — the central finding of the study.
- Manual scrubbing in air was significantly less effective than manual scrubbing under water (p<0.001).
- Washer-disinfectors were the most effective method of pre-sterilisation cleaning for dental burs.
- Enzymic agents were suitable for soaking contaminated dental burs immediately after use, and are recommended as an interim measure before more thorough cleaning.
- Overall recommendation: washer-disinfectors as the method of choice for pre-sterilisation cleaning of contaminated dental burs.
