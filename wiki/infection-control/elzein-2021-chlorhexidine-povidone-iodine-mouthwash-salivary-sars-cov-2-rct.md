## Three-line Summary

This was a parallel-group, blinded, randomized, placebo-controlled trial evaluating the efficacy of 0.2% chlorhexidine and 1% povidone-iodine mouthwashes against salivary SARS-CoV-2 in 61 COVID-19 positive patients (mean age 45.3 ± 16.7 years) conducted between June and September 2020 at Rafik Hariri University Hospital, Lebanon. Saliva was collected before and 5 minutes after a 30-second mouthwash/gargle, and viral load was measured via rRT-PCR by assessing change in cycle threshold (delta Ct).

Both 0.2% chlorhexidine and 1% povidone-iodine significantly reduced salivary SARS-CoV-2 viral load compared to distilled water (P = .0024 and P = .012, respectively). Mean Ct increases were 5.69 for chlorhexidine and 4.45 for povidone-iodine (both P < .0001 for paired before-after comparisons), with no significant difference between the two active solutions (P = .24).

Preprocedural rinsing with 0.2% chlorhexidine or 1% povidone-iodine appears effective as a preventive strategy to reduce SARS-CoV-2 transmission during dental procedures, though further studies are needed to determine the duration of their antiviral effect.

## 세줄요약

본 연구는 COVID-19 양성 환자 61명(평균 연령 45.3 ± 16.7세)을 대상으로 0.2% 클로르헥시딘과 1% 포비돈-아이오딘 구강 세정액의 타액 내 SARS-CoV-2 불활성화 효과를 평가한 평행군, 이중맹검, 무작위배정, 위약대조 임상시험으로, 2020년 6월부터 9월까지 레바논 라피크 하리리 대학병원에서 수행되었다. 시술 전후(세정 30초, 5분 후) 타액을 채취하여 rRT-PCR로 주기 역치(CT) 변화를 측정하였다.

0.2% 클로르헥시딘과 1% 포비돈-아이오딘 모두 증류수 대비 타액 내 SARS-CoV-2 바이러스 농도를 유의하게 감소시켰으며(P = .0024 및 P = .012), 평균 Ct 증가값은 각각 5.69와 4.45로 두 군 간 유의한 차이는 없었다(P = .24).

치과 시술 전 0.2% 클로르헥시딘 또는 1% 포비돈-아이오딘 구강 세정은 COVID-19 전파 위험을 줄이는 예방 전략으로 효과적이나, 항바이러스 효과의 지속 시간에 대한 추가 연구가 필요하다.

## Summary

This randomized controlled trial assessed whether preprocedural mouthwashes with 0.2% chlorhexidine or 1% povidone-iodine could reduce salivary SARS-CoV-2 viral load, aiming to mitigate aerosol transmission risk in dental settings. Sixty-one laboratory-confirmed COVID-19 patients were randomized to rinse for 30 seconds with distilled water (control), 0.2% chlorhexidine, or 1% povidone-iodine. Saliva was collected before and 5 minutes after rinsing, and viral load was measured by rRT-PCR using normalized Ct values (RNase P internal control). Both active mouthwashes significantly increased Ct values (decreased viral load) compared to distilled water, with no significant difference between the two. Mean Ct increases were 5.69 for chlorhexidine and 4.45 for povidone-iodine. The authors concluded that both solutions are effective preprocedural mouthwashes against salivary SARS-CoV-2 and recommended their use as a preventive strategy in dental practice, while calling for further research on the duration of effectiveness.

## Key Contributions

- First large-scale in vivo RCT demonstrating efficacy of 0.2% chlorhexidine and 1% povidone-iodine mouthwashes against salivary SARS-CoV-2.
- Both active solutions significantly reduced viral load compared to distilled water placebo (P = .0024 and P = .012, respectively).
- Mean Ct increases of 5.69 (chlorhexidine) and 4.45 (povidone-iodine) within 5 minutes post-rinse (both P < .0001).
- No significant difference in efficacy between the two active mouthwashes (P = .24).
- Confirmed consistent detection of SARS-CoV-2 in saliva (61/66 patients), supporting its utility for diagnostic and research purposes.

## Methodology

This was a parallel-group, blinded, randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial conducted at Rafik Hariri University Hospital, Beirut, between June and September 2020. Participants were laboratory-confirmed COVID-19 positive patients (via nasopharyngeal swab), with diagnosis-to-inclusion interval of 0–2 days. Exclusion criteria included intubation/mechanical ventilation or declined consent. Simple randomization using Excel with SNOSE allocation concealment assigned patients to distilled water (n=9), 0.2% chlorhexidine (n=25), or 1% povidone-iodine (n=27). The intervention supervisor, laboratory technicians, and outcome assessor were blinded; group codes were revealed only at study end. Patients self-collected saliva before and 5 minutes after a 30-second mouthwash/gargle. Viral RNA was extracted and rRT-PCR was performed targeting RdRP, N, and E genes, with RNase P as endogenous internal control for normalization. The primary outcome was delta Ct (post-wash normalized Ct minus pre-wash normalized Ct). Statistical analyses used Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn's post-hoc for between-group comparisons and paired t-test for within-group comparisons, with significance at P < .05.

| Pre-post Ct change (Control) | Pre: 28.9 ± 5.5 → Post: NR (Δ = 0.519 ± 0.519) | NR | .566 | NR |

No significant difference was found in RNase P Ct values between pre- and post-wash samples (P = .332), confirming that differences in SARS-CoV-2 Ct were not due to variation in specimen cellularity. No adverse events were reported in any group. Ct values are inversely related to viral load, thus increases in Ct indicate reduction in viral RNA copies.
