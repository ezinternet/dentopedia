## One-line Summary

Systematic review (6 observational studies, >300 used healing abutments): routine mechanical/chemical/steam decontamination often fails to fully remove contaminants, yet no clinical study and no adverse consequence has been reported — so indiscriminate reuse of healing abutments and cover screws should be avoided until stronger evidence exists.

## Summary

This anchor systematic review (Bidra et al., J Prosthodont 2020; e-pub 2019) asked two questions: can used titanium healing abutments (HAs) and cover screws be effectively resterilized for reuse, and have any adverse consequences of reuse to implants or patients been reported? From 657 titles, 6 studies met the objectives — all observational, describing a combined sample of more than 300 used HAs retrieved from patients. Three studies found that routine mechanical/chemical/steam methods did not completely remove contaminants, two found adequate decontamination only when routine methods were supplemented with an additional regimen, and one found routine methods sufficient. No true clinical studies were identified, and no study reported any adverse consequence (infection, bone loss, mechanical complication, implant failure, or patient harm) from reuse. The authors conclude that the biological/mechanical significance of residual contamination is undetermined and that indiscriminate reuse should be avoided until more evidence is established.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic review to formally frame the HA/cover-screw reuse question and its safety evidence base.
- Documents that the entire evidence base is observational — no true clinical study exists — so the clinical significance of residual contamination is undetermined.
- Shows routine decontamination is often incomplete (3 of 6 studies) but sometimes adequate, especially when supplemented with an additional regimen (2 of 6).
- Notes the absence of any reported adverse consequence, while cautioning this is not equivalent to demonstrated safety.
- Delivers the practical recommendation: avoid *indiscriminate* reuse until supporting evidence is established.

## Methodology

Electronic search of the English-language literature (PubMed + Cochrane) by multiple independent investigators, applying predetermined inclusion/exclusion criteria. Primary objective: whether HAs and cover screws can be resterilized effectively for reuse. Secondary objective: whether adverse biological/mechanical consequences to implants or harm to patients from reuse have been reported. Screening reduced 657 initial titles to 6 included observational studies. (Abstract-only — full text not retrieved; detailed risk-of-bias methodology unavailable.)

## Results

- 6 included studies, all observational, describing >300 used healing abutments from patients.
- 3 studies: routine methods (mechanical, chemical, steam) did NOT completely remove contaminants.
- 2 studies: routine methods plus an additional regimen achieved adequate decontamination.
- 1 study: routine methods alone were sufficient.
- No true clinical studies identified.
- No adverse consequences (infection, bone loss, mechanical complication, implant failure, patient harm) reported from reuse.
- Conclusion: limited evidence that routine methods may not fully decontaminate; clinical implication undetermined; avoid indiscriminate reuse until further evidence.
