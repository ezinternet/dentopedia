## Three-line Summary

Six-year cross-sectional survey (2012–2017) of 318 dental chair units across 64 hospitals in Tianjin, China, sampling handpiece and air/water (three-way) syringe output water against the national standard.

Only 56.14% of samples complied and 78.93% of units used no disinfection (peak 1.8×10⁵ CFU/mL); the three key factors governing contamination were disinfection frequency, feed-water source, and a valid anti-retraction valve.

Air/water syringe output-water contamination is controllable — periodic disinfection, cleaner feed water, and functioning anti-retraction valves are the actionable levers.

## Summary

Ji et al. (Int Dent J 2019, e-pub 2018) conducted a large 6-year survey of dental unit waterline (DUWL) contamination across 318 dental chair units in 64 Tianjin hospitals, sampling both handpiece and **air/water (three-way) syringe** output water. They built a custom sampling connector and, importantly, a **retraction-measurement device** to quantify each unit's suck-back — the negative-pressure backflow of oral fluids into the line when the instrument is released.

The results were poor: only **56.14%** of samples met the national DUWL output-water standard, and **78.93% of units performed no disinfection at all**, with peak counts reaching 1.8×10⁵ CFU/mL and isolates including *Bacillus cereus*, *Burkholderia cepacia*, and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. The value of the paper is its distillation to **three controllable factors**: (1) disinfecting the DUWL daily or weekly, (2) using hospital-purified or purchased purified/bottled feed water rather than raw municipal water, and (3) maintaining a **valid anti-retraction valve** to stop suck-back. These are the practical levers behind the syringe-water contamination that other studies merely quantify.

## Key Contributions

1. **Actionable control model.** Reduces DUWL/three-way syringe contamination to three levers: disinfection frequency, feed-water source, anti-retraction valve validity.
2. **Retraction quantified.** Purpose-built device measured suck-back at the chair-unit level, linking valve failure to contamination.
3. **Scale.** 318 units, 64 hospitals, 6 years — a real-world compliance baseline (only 56% pass; 79% never disinfect).

## Methodology

- 318 DCUs randomly selected across 64 Tianjin hospitals, 2012–2017.
- Handpiece and **air/water syringe** output water tested vs China's national standard.
- Custom sampling connector; retraction (suck-back) volume measured with a bespoke device.

## Clinical Takeaways

- The three-way syringe line is only as clean as the **feed water + disinfection routine + anti-retraction valve** behind it — all three matter.
- **Anti-retraction valves degrade**; verify function periodically (cf. the ~52% failure rate noted by [[infection-control/dang-2022-assessment-microbiota-diversity-dental-unit]]).
- Prefer purified/bottled feed water over raw municipal supply where DUWL contamination is a concern.
