## Three-line Summary

In-vitro/bench engineering evaluation testing a reusable digital parametric sterilization indicator (data logger, DS1922F) across three iterative prototype batches (150 samples each) subjected to repeated autoclave cycles (300, 300, and 600 cycles respectively), with Kaplan-Meier survival analysis of device failure.

The final (Batch 3) prototype design was robust against 600 autoclave cycles, with mean lifetime survival of 498 cycles (95% CI 480–516) — several multiples of the typically prescribed replacement/re-verification interval.

Digital data loggers provide continuous, immediate time/temperature trajectory data throughout each sterilization cycle, complementing chemical indicators (single-point verification only) and biological indicators (days-to-a-week for confirmatory results), particularly useful where sterilization-monitoring-service infrastructure is limited.

## Summary

This bench engineering study evaluates a reusable, multi-cycle digital parametric sterilization indicator (data logger, model DS1922F) intended for use in dental autoclaves. Unlike single-use chemical indicators (which confirm exposure to specific process conditions but not necessarily whether the full validated parameter range was achieved throughout a cycle) or biological indicators (which require days of incubation before results are available), a digital data logger continuously records temperature and time throughout each autoclave cycle and can be reused across multiple cycles. The authors developed and tested the DS1922F prototype through three iterative batches, using failure data from each batch to redesign and improve the device before the next round of testing. **Note: this page is built from a PubMed abstract only (`full_text: false`); full-text detail on autoclave models, cycle parameters, and specific failure mechanisms was not retrievable.**

## Key Contributions

- Introduces and validates a reusable digital data-logger approach to sterilization monitoring as a complement to conventional chemical and biological indicators.
- Establishes an iterative prototype-development methodology (failure analysis → redesign → re-test) for a reusable sterilization-monitoring device.
- Provides a quantitative device-lifetime benchmark (mean survival 498 cycles, 95% CI 480–516) under an autoclave-cycle longevity challenge, useful for setting practical replacement/re-verification intervals.

## Methodology

- Three sequential batches of the DS1922F prototype (150 samples per batch) were autoclave-cycle challenged:
  - **Batch 1**: 150 samples, 300 cycles — failures analyzed to inform redesign.
  - **Batch 2**: 150 samples of the redesigned prototype, 300 cycles.
  - **Batch 3 (final design)**: 150 samples, 600 cycles — a longevity stress test of the finalized design.
- Kaplan-Meier survival analysis (α = 0.05) was applied to device lifetime data in all three batches.
- Failed samples were qualitatively studied at each stage to identify failure variables and guide iterative design changes.

## Results

- Iterative redesign across Batches 1–2 progressively reduced failure rates and informed the final (Batch 3) design.
- The final prototype survived the full 600-cycle challenge, with mean lifetime survival of 498 cycles (95% CI 480–516).
- A mean lifetime >450 cycles was reported as several multiples of the number of cycles typically prescribed before replacement or re-verification of comparable monitoring devices — supporting practical feasibility of a reusable digital indicator for routine sterilizer monitoring.
