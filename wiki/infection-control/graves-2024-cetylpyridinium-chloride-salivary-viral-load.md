## Three-line Summary

This study comprises two double-blind randomized controlled trials (RCTs) investigating the efficacy of commercially available mouthrinses in reducing salivary SARS-CoV-2 viral load in COVID-19-positive patients. In the first trial (n=128, final n=101), six rinses were tested against a saline placebo, and in the second (n=230, final n=146), ethanol (EtOH), cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC), and sterile water were compared.

Rinsing with the CPC-containing product resulted in a significant reduction in salivary SARS-CoV-2 viral load at 30 minutes post-rinse (P = .015), whereas no other rinse showed a significant effect at that time point. At 60 minutes post-rinse, no group exhibited a significant reduction relative to baseline, indicating a rebound in viral load.

The findings suggest that preprocedural oral rinsing with a CPC-based mouthwash is a feasible, inexpensive, and well-tolerated method to transiently reduce salivary viral load, potentially mitigating the spread of SARS-CoV-2 and other enveloped viruses in clinical settings.

## Summary

This study employed two double-blind, randomized controlled trials to evaluate the in vivo efficacy of commercially available mouthrinses in reducing salivary SARS-CoV-2 viral load. The first trial compared six different rinses (EtOH, CPC, H2O2, PI, CHX, and saline placebo), while the second focused on the two most promising candidates from the first trial (EtOH and CPC) against a sterile water placebo. Participants with confirmed COVID-19 provided saliva samples at baseline, and again at 30 and 60 minutes after a 60-second rinse. The primary outcome was the change in viral load, measured by qPCR. The results showed that only the CPC-containing mouthrinse significantly reduced viral load at 30 minutes post-rinse (P = .015), an effect that was also significant compared to the placebo (P = .017). However, this effect was transient, as viral load returned to baseline levels by 60 minutes. The authors concluded that a CPC mouthrinse is a feasible, inexpensive, and acceptable intervention to temporarily reduce salivary viral load, offering a potential strategy to mitigate the spread of SARS-CoV-2 and other enveloped viruses in clinical settings.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrated significant efficacy of a CPC mouthrinse in reducing salivary SARS-CoV-2 viral load in a large, controlled human trial.
- Showed the effect is transient, lasting for at least 30 minutes but not 60 minutes post-rinse.
- Confirmed the lack of efficacy for other commonly used mouthrinses (EtOH, H2O2, PI, CHX) in reducing salivary viral load.
- Assessed patient acceptance, indicating high willingness to use such rinses in healthcare settings.
- Provided a practical, antigen-agnostic intervention that could be relevant across different viral variants and for other enveloped viruses.

## Methodology

Two prospective, double-blind, parallel-group, placebo-controlled randomized controlled trials were conducted. In the first trial, 128 participants were randomized to one of six groups (EtOH, CPC, H2O2, PI, CHX, or saline) and the second trial randomized 230 participants to one of three groups (EtOH, CPC, or sterile water). Participants were adults (18-65 years) with a confirmed positive SARS-CoV-2 test within 10 days of symptom onset. After providing a baseline unstimulated saliva sample, they rinsed with their assigned product for 60 seconds. Further saliva samples were collected at 30 and 60 minutes post-rinse. Viral load (N2 copy number) was measured using qPCR. An adjusted linear mixed-effects model was used for statistical analysis.

## Results

| Outcome | Effect | 95% CI | p | I² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPC vs. Baseline (30 min) | Viral load reduction | NR | .015 | NR |
| CPC vs. Placebo (30 min) | Viral load reduction | NR | .017 | NR |
| EtOH vs. Placebo (30 min) | No significant reduction | NR | NR | NR |
| H2O2 vs. Placebo (30 min) | No significant reduction | NR | NR | NR |
| PI vs. Placebo (30 min) | No significant reduction | NR | NR | NR |

No other rinse significantly affected viral load at 30 min. By 60 min, the effect of CPC was lost, indicating a rebound.
