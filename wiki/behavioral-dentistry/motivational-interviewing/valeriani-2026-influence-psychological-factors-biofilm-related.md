## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD420251119213; NHLBI risk-of-bias tool; MEDLINE, PsycINFO, Web of Science through May 2026) of prospective longitudinal studies in adults linking psychological exposures to later biofilm-related outcomes (periodontal parameters, caries, tooth loss, oral-hygiene behaviours); 10 studies of fair-to-good quality, no meta-analysis reported.

Cognitive constructs gave mixed results: self-efficacy predicted later self-reported oral hygiene behaviours and, after motivation and OHI, clinically assessed plaque control, but inconsistently; perceived treatment benefits (especially with perceived susceptibility) and Theory of Reasoned Action variables were associated with later gingival or plaque outcomes; depression and stress showed sparse signals of poorer self-care and periodontal outcomes; sense of coherence (one study) showed no association.

Psychological factors may inform risk assessment and personalised preventive care, but the evidence is limited and heterogeneous, so no construct can be treated as an established predictor.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰(Systematic Review, PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD420251119213; NHLBI 편향위험 도구; MEDLINE·PsycINFO·Web of Science 2026년 5월까지): 성인에서 심리적 요인이 이후 치면세균막(biofilm) 관련 결과(치주 지표·치아우식·치아상실·구강위생 행동)에 미치는 영향을 본 전향적 종단연구 10편(질 fair~good), 메타분석은 보고되지 않음.

인지 요인 결과는 혼재: 자기효능감(self-efficacy)은 이후 자가보고 구강위생 행동과, 동기부여·구강위생교육(Oral Hygiene Instruction, OHI) 후에는 임상적 치태 조절과 연관되었으나 연구·결과 간 일관되지 않았고, 치료 이익 인식(특히 민감성 인식 병행)과 합리적 행동이론(Theory of Reasoned Action) 변수는 이후 치은·치태 결과와 연관; 우울·스트레스는 자료가 적으나 불량한 자가관리·치주 결과와 연관 시사; 일관성 감각(sense of coherence)은 1편에서 무연관.

심리적 요인은 위험 평가와 맞춤 예방에 참고될 수 있으나 근거가 제한적이고 이질적이어서 확립된 예측인자로 볼 수 없다.

## Summary

This systematic review (PRISMA, PROSPERO-registered) asked whether psychological factors are associated with subsequent biofilm-related oral outcomes in adults. Searching MEDLINE, PsycINFO and Web of Science through May 2026, it included 10 prospective studies of fair-to-good quality. Self-efficacy predicted later self-reported hygiene behaviour and, after motivation and OHI, clinically assessed plaque control, though not consistently; perceived treatment benefits and Theory of Reasoned Action variables were associated with later gingival or plaque outcomes. Depression and stress data were sparse but pointed to poorer self-care and periodontal outcomes, and sense of coherence (one study) showed no association. The authors call the evidence limited and heterogeneous; only the abstract was available.

## Key Contributions
- Maps prospective evidence for a broad set of psychological exposures (depression, anxiety, stress, self-efficacy, health beliefs, personality, sense of coherence) against biofilm-related oral outcomes.
- Separates cognitive constructs (self-efficacy, perceived benefits/susceptibility, Theory of Reasoned Action) from psychopathology and personality evidence.
- Highlights that self-efficacy's association with clinically assessed plaque control is conditional on motivation and OHI.

## Methodology
- Systematic review following PRISMA; protocol PROSPERO CRD420251119213; risk of bias with the NHLBI tool.
- Databases: MEDLINE, PsycINFO, Web of Science, searched through May 2026.
- Eligibility: prospective longitudinal studies in adults measuring psychological exposures before oral outcomes; outcomes = periodontal parameters, dental caries, tooth loss, oral-hygiene behaviours.
- Included: 10 prospective studies of fair-to-good quality. Study-level n, effect sizes and follow-up lengths: not reported in the abstract.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Studies included | 10 prospective studies (fair-to-good quality) |
| Self-efficacy | Associated with later hygiene behaviour; plaque control after motivation + OHI; inconsistent |
| Perceived benefits/susceptibility, TRA | Associated with later gingival or plaque outcomes |
| Depression / stress | Sparse; poorer self-care and periodontal outcomes suggested |
| Sense of coherence | 1 study, no association |

Limitations: Only 10 studies; evidence described by the authors as limited and heterogeneous. Abstract-only: no pooled estimates, per-study effect sizes or risk-of-bias distribution beyond 'fair-to-good'. Associations are prospective but not shown to be causal; no meta-analysis reported. Psychopathology and personality findings rest on very few studies; sense of coherence on one.
