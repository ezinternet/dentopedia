## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional SERVQUAL survey (n=416: 286 preanesthetic oral examination [PAOE] vs 130 control; Cronbach's α=0.861) at an academic medical center in Taiwan: PAOE patients scored lower on reliability (β=-0.074, P=0.003), responsiveness (β=-0.148, P=0.006), and empathy (β=-0.140, P=0.011).

Reliability (β=0.655, P<0.001) and responsiveness (β=0.147, P=0.008) directly drove satisfaction; absolute satisfaction stayed high in both groups (PAOE 6.17±1.208 vs control 6.39±0.792 on a 7-point scale), and 83.9% of PAOE patients agreed the exam was necessary.

An interdisciplinary dental-injury prevention program modestly lowers perceived service quality but remains a win-win for patient safety and malpractice-claim reduction; waiting-time management and patient-centered education are the levers.

## Summary

Kaohsiung Medical University Hospital extended its two-phase quality improvement program (which cut perioperative dental injury from 0.108% to 0.051%, then to 0.009%) into **phase III — preanesthetic oral examination (PAOE)** in November 2018: every elective general-anesthesia patient is referred to family dentistry, where an intern examines, a resident double-checks, and a visiting staff triple-checks; mobile teeth receive wire fixation or extraction, and mobility grades are uploaded to the hospital information system. Injury incidence under PAOE was 0.0144% (Nov 2018–Jul 2020) vs 0.0255% before.

This study asked what patients think of the program. A modified SERVQUAL questionnaire (22 items, five dimensions — tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy; perceptions only, seven-point Likert) was administered to 286 PAOE referrals and 130 voluntary dental patients (Aug 2020–Apr 2021). Path analysis showed PAOE lowered reliability, responsiveness, and empathy perceptions; reliability and responsiveness were the only direct predictors of satisfaction. The authors attribute the responsiveness/empathy dip to low health literacy among older surgical patients, unfamiliarity with the new program, and dialect barriers in southern Taiwan — while noting scores remained high overall, so the service-quality cost of the safety program is limited. They recommend reasonable waiting times, patient-centered education about perioperative dental-injury risk, and interprofessional education.

## Key Contributions

- First patient-experience evaluation of phase III PAOE (prior phases judged by injury incidence alone)
- Quantifies the "service-quality price" of a mandatory safety step: lower reliability/responsiveness/empathy vs routine dental visitors
- Identifies reliability (timeliness/accuracy → waiting time) and responsiveness (communication) as the satisfaction-driving dimensions — actionable targets
- Clinical yield data: tooth mobility 13.3%, fixation 8.4% among 286 referred GA patients; top referral sources otolaryngology (29.7%) and OB/GYN (15.0%)
- Frames dentist–anesthesiologist interdisciplinary cooperation as reducing both injuries and malpractice claims

## Methodology

- **Design**: cross-sectional convenience-sample survey; Aug 2020–Apr 2021; IRB KMUHIRB-E(II)-20200166
- **Groups**: PAOE referrals (n=286, 68.8%) vs voluntary family-dentistry attendees (n=130, 31.2%); 416 valid questionnaires
- **Instrument**: demographics + perspectives (satisfaction, recommendation, necessity; 7-point Likert) + adapted SERVQUAL perceptions (9 reverse-scored items; dimension score = item mean); Cronbach's α=0.861
- **Analysis**: χ², independent t-test (SPSS 20); path analysis (AMOS 26) with fit cutoffs CFI/TLI ≥0.95, SRMR <0.08, RMSEA <0.06; medical-university students excluded from path model

## Results

- Satisfaction 6.24±1.093 overall (PAOE 6.17±1.208 vs control 6.39±0.792); "agree" 82.9% vs 97.7% (P<0.001)
- Recommendation 6.16±1.266 overall; agree 80.4% vs 94.6% (P<0.001); PAOE necessity agreed by 83.9%
- Dimension means (total): tangibles 6.32, reliability 6.39, responsiveness 5.85 (lowest), assurance 6.34, empathy 6.15
- PAOE vs control totals: tangibles NS (P=0.056); reliability 31.47±4.42 vs 32.92±3.15, responsiveness 22.45±7.00 vs 25.48±4.48, empathy 29.81±7.56 vs 32.82±4.61 (all P<0.001); assurance 25.05±4.31 vs 26.05±2.57 (P=0.004)
- Path model (fit: χ²=19.765, df=15, P=0.181, CFI=0.996, TLI=0.989, SRMR=0.038, RMSEA=0.031): PAOE→reliability β=-0.074, →responsiveness β=-0.148, →empathy β=-0.140; reliability→satisfaction β=0.655, responsiveness→satisfaction β=0.147; older age→lower responsiveness (β=-0.122), higher education→higher responsiveness (β=0.155)
