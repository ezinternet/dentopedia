## 한줄요약
RCT(n=76): 부분의치 선택 시 PDA 사용군 결정갈등 점수 8.0 vs 비사용군 20.6(p<0.001); PDA군 71.1%가 고정성 선택.

## Three-line Summary
RCT (n=76) randomized prosthodontic patients to aided (PDA: evidence-based RPD vs FPD comparison tool) vs unaided (usual consultation) groups for partial denture selection. Aided group had significantly lower Decisional Conflict Scale (DCS-16) scores: mean 8.0±6.4 vs 20.6±15.9. PDA also shifted treatment preference: 71.1% of aided group chose fixed partial denture versus 65.8% of unaided group opting for removable.

## 세줄요약
RCT(n=76): 가철성 vs 고정성 부분의치 선택 시 PDA 사용군 DCS 8.0 vs 미사용군 20.6(p<0.001). PDA군에서 고정성 선택률 71.1%(미사용군 34.2%). 연령이 낮을수록 PDA가 있어도 결정 확신이 낮음.

## Summary
Iyer et al. (2025) conducted an RCT at a Saudi dental college to test whether an evidence-based patient decision aid (PDA) reduces decisional conflict when selecting between removable (RPD) and fixed (FPD) partial dentures. Arabic DCS-16 was translated, validated (Cronbach's α=0.84), and administered to 76 patients (38 aided, 38 unaided). The PDA was co-developed with a board-certified prosthodontist and presented treatment options with structured evidence on advantages, disadvantages, and cost implications. Aided group DCS was 8.0±6.4 vs unaided 20.6±15.9 — a clinically meaningful difference indicating that unaided patients experienced moderate-to-high conflict while aided patients experienced minimal conflict. A notable secondary finding: aided patients preferentially chose FPD (71.1%), while unaided patients mostly chose RPD (65.8%), suggesting that better-informed patients select more durable, invasive options when presented with balanced evidence. Age was the significant moderator: younger patients (20–40) remained less decisive even with aid.

## Key Contributions
- Only RCT in dentistry (2024–2025 data) measuring PDA impact on decisional conflict using validated DCS-16 in Arabic
- Decisional conflict reduction: aided DCS 8.0 vs unaided 20.6 — unaided patients approached threshold of "high conflict" (>37.5 indicates problematic conflict)
- PDA shifted treatment choice from RPD to FPD: informing patients of evidence changes preference toward better-evidence option
- Validated Arabic DCS-16 for first time in dental context (Cronbach's α=0.84; all 16 items retained)
- Age as moderator: younger patients need additional support even with PDA

## Methodology
RCT. Single-center. n=76 (38 aided, 38 unaided). Randomization: concealed envelopes. Setting: outpatient dental college, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia (May–November 2024). Aided: PDA administered by senior dental student clinicians during consultation. Unaided: standard clinical consultation. Instrument: DCS-16 (5-point Likert, 0=no conflict, 100=maximum conflict); 5 subscales: Informed, Values Clarity, Support, Uncertainty, Effective Decision. Blinded questionnaire administrator. Statistics: independent t-test, Chi-square, multinomial logistic regression.

## Results
DCS overall: aided 8.0±6.4 vs unaided 20.6±15.9 (p<0.001). FPD-specific DCS: aided 6.3±4.4 vs unaided 27.2±13.3 (p<0.001). Treatment choice: aided 71.1% FPD / 28.9% RPD; unaided 34.2% FPD / 65.8% RPD. Age: younger patients (20–40) in aided group significantly less likely to feel sure about their decision vs older (41–60) (OR −17.8, CI 1.47–2.28, p=0.00). Cronbach's α=0.84 (all subscales reliable; removing any single item did not improve reliability).
