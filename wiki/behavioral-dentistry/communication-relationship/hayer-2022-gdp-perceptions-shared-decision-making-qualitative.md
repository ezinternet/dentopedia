## 한줄요약
질적 연구(n=9 영국 일반 치과의사): SDM이 informed consent와 혼동되고 있음; 장벽 3개 — 소통 습관, 소송 두려움, NHS 시간 제약.

## Three-line Summary
Qualitative semi-structured interviews with 9 UK GDPs revealed that SDM is systematically conflated with informed consent in daily practice. Three barrier themes emerged: (1) communication defaulting to information-provision over collaborative discussion, (2) regulatory/litigation fear driving defensive practice, (3) NHS time pressure structurally incompatible with SDM conversations. Standardized decision-making aids were identified as the most practical intervention to bridge the gap.

## Summary
Hayer & Wassif (2022) conducted qualitative semi-structured interviews with 9 GDPs across the UK to understand how SDM is perceived and applied in dental practice. Despite all participants expressing that the patient should make the final decision, their described consultation behaviors corresponded to informed consent (information provision + patient choice) rather than true SDM (collaborative exploration of patient values, preferences, and life context). Three overarching themes explained this gap: (1) communication defaulting to information-listing without eliciting patient preferences; (2) regulatory body confusion (NHS England, GDC, Dental Protection gave inconsistent messages) plus fear of litigation shifting communication toward defensive documentation; (3) NHS system time and fee structure making thorough SDM conversations economically unsustainable. More experienced GDPs in long-tenured practices described more organic SDM, attributable to established patient rapport — suggesting that SDM improves with continuity of care. The study explicitly linked SDM implementation to reduced litigation risk (some evidence) while noting most GDPs were unaware of this relationship.

## Key Contributions
- Empirically documented that UK GDPs confuse SDM with informed consent in clinical practice
- Identified that "giving all options and risks" is perceived as practicing SDM by most GDPs — a systematic misunderstanding
- Showed litigation fear paradoxically reduces SDM quality (defensive information-dumping rather than collaborative discussion)
- Demonstrated that continuity of care (rapport over time) is the main enabler of organic SDM in current practice
- Connected SDM to lower litigation rates — a practical argument for its implementation in defensive-medicine cultures

## Methodology
Qualitative interpretive paradigm; purposive sampling via private dental Facebook forum. n=9 GDPs (6M, 3F; experience 3–16 years; 8 mixed-practice NHS+private, 1 fully private). Semi-structured Skype interviews, 25–45 min (avg 30 min); ~4h total data. Transcribed within 48h. Thematic analysis. Data saturation reached at n=9.

## Results
Theme 1 (Communication): GDPs varied in receptiveness assessment; most gave information lists vs. tailoring to patient values. "Small talk" enabled rapport but not always leveraged for SDM. Theme 2 (Regulatory perception): All participants cited fear of being blamed/sued; provision of all options/risks perceived as medico-legal requirement rather than SDM enabler; conflicting guidelines from NHS England, GDC, Dental Protection. Theme 3 (Dental care structure): All cited time constraints under NHS as barrier; NHS seen as structurally incompatible with SDM; private context allowed more thorough discussions.
