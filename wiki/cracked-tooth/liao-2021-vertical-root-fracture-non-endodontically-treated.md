## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (MEDLINE/PubMed/Google Scholar, English-language literature 1978–2021, 106 articles included, no formal risk-of-bias appraisal) comparing vertical root fracture (VRF) in non-endodontically treated teeth (VRFNETT) with endodontically treated teeth (VRFETT) across prevalence, demography, diagnosis, etiology, clinical/radiographic features and treatment.

VRFETT prevalence is reported as 2–25%; most VRFs occur after age 40 (86.79% in one series), VRFNETT patients are older (mean 69 vs 56 years in one series) and more often male and Chinese; deep narrow probing is present in 64–93% of VRFs, a coronally positioned sinus tract in 35–42%, and 13–14% show no radiographic lesion; surgical repair series are small case reports (e.g., 4-META/MMA-TBB replantation survival 88.5% at 12 months falling to 59.3% at 60 months).

Diagnosis needs a combination of probing, bite test, multi-angle radiographs, selective CBCT (limited by intracanal metal and radiopaque fillings) and, when doubt remains, exploratory surgery; evidence is descriptive and extraction remains the default, so long-term prognosis studies of repair techniques are still needed.

## Summary

This narrative review (Liao et al., National Taiwan University and Chang Gung groups, J Pers Med 2021) compares vertical root fracture in endodontically treated teeth (VRFETT) with VRF in non-endodontically treated teeth (VRFNETT), which it argues is an under-diagnosed entity whose signs mimic endodontic or periodontal disease. VRFETT clusters in maxillary premolars and mesial roots of mandibular molars, whereas VRFNETT is reported mainly in older, male, Chinese patients with severely attrited first molars and has been linked to betel-quid and hard-food chewing. Diagnosis rests on a combination of a deep narrow isolated pocket, a coronally positioned sinus tract, bite pain, multi-angle radiographs and selective CBCT, with exploratory surgery as the visual gold standard. Reported repair techniques (bonded fragment re-cementation, intentional replantation, calcium hydroxide, bioceramic sealing) are single cases or small series with limited long-term survival, so extraction remains the usual outcome.

## Key Contributions

- Side-by-side VRFETT vs VRFNETT comparison (Table 3) covering prevalence, sex, age, tooth/root distribution, etiology, clinical and radiographic features.
- Compiled frequency tables of clinical signs (9 primary series, 30–227 teeth) and radiographic features, plus a diagnostic flowchart ending in exploratory surgery.
- Tabulated published repair attempts (Tables 4–6) as a catalog of materials and approaches.
- Raises genetic/developmental susceptibility (multiple VRFs in the same patient) and chewing habits as hypotheses for VRFNETT predominance in Chinese populations.

## Methodology

- Narrative review; English-language articles from 1978 to 2021 searched in MEDLINE, PubMed and Google Scholar.
- Included systematic reviews, retrospective cohorts, demographic research, clinical studies, case reports and case series; excluded in vitro, ex vivo and animal studies. Two researchers screened independently; 106 articles included.
- No PRISMA flow, no risk-of-bias tool, no pooled analysis. Illustrative NTUH cases were used for figures only (ethics approval noted).

## Results

**Epidemiology and demography**

| Item | VRFETT | VRFNETT |
|---|---|---|
| Prevalence | 2–25% of endodontically treated teeth (3.69–25% in several studies) | Not reported; 40% of 315 VRFs and 80% of 51 VRFs in two Chinese series were non-endodontically treated |
| Sex | No clear preference | More males |
| Age | Mostly over 40 (86.79% in one series) | Older than VRFETT (mean 69 vs 56 years in one series) |
| Typical teeth | Maxillary premolars, mandibular molars (mesial roots) | First molars; severe attrition, minimal restorations |

**Diagnostic signs (ranges across published series)**

- Deep narrow periodontal pocket: 64–93% of VRFs in the commonly cited series (Table 1 spans 40–93%).
- Coronally positioned sinus tract: 35–42% in two series; up to 60% in a later series; a gutta-percha point traced along the PDL strongly suggests VRF.
- Periradicular halo radiolucency: 37–75% in the series reporting it; 13–14% of VRFs show no detectable radiolucency.
- Other radiographic signs: fragment displacement, canal radiolucent line, space between canal wall and post/filling, PDL widening, angular, step-like or V-shaped defects, resorption along the fracture line, canal-space widening, failure after prior healing.
- CBCT: useful in VRFNETT; in filled canals metal posts and radiopaque materials create artifacts (one in vivo study judged high-resolution CBCT non-diagnostic) and no consensus on accuracy exists. Exploratory surgery (sharp explorer, methylene blue, microscope) is the gold standard.

**Etiology / predisposing factors:** over-preparation during instrumentation, excessive obturation or post force (stress studies suggest condensation force alone is rarely the direct cause), loss of internal tooth structure, oval/flattened roots with thin proximal dentin, age-related dentin sclerosis, implant-protective occlusion loading adjacent natural teeth, heavy chewing habits.

**Treatment series (Tables 4–6):**

| Approach | Reported outcome |
|---|---|
| 4-META/MMA-TBB bonding, intraoral vs extraoral replantation (Sugaya, 23 teeth) | 9/11 vs 9/12 good prognosis at 22–33 months |
| 4-META/MMA-TBB + replantation (Hayashi 2002, n=20) | Survival 83.3% at 12 months, 36.3% at 24 months |
| 4-META/MMA-TBB + replantation (Hayashi 2004, n=26) | Survival 88.5% / 69.2% / 59.3% at 12 / 36 / 60 months |
| Silver glass-ionomer + GTR (Selden, 6 teeth) | All 6 failed (5 within 2–11 months, 1 at 1 year) |
| MTA retrograde fill + collagen membrane (Floratos, 4 teeth) | Asymptomatic with PDL re-formation at 8–24 months |
| Calcium hydroxide, CO2 laser, fragment removal, hemisection, extraction | Single cases or small series; long-term outcome questionable |

Prevention advice: conservative canal enlargement and flare, minimal and passive force for fillings, posts and restorations, fiber posts (dentin-like modulus) and night guards for bruxism or clenching.
