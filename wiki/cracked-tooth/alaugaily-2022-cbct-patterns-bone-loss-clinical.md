## Three-line Summary

Retrospective records-based comparison of 95 patients (95 teeth) with a visually confirmed longitudinal fracture (cracked tooth only or VRF); 54 teeth had both periapical (PA) radiographs and CBCT plus probing depth and clinical images, read by 2 independent examiners for bone-defect pattern (none, angular, J-shaped, combined).

CBCT had 4.4 times the odds of detecting bony defects suggestive of longitudinal fracture compared with PA radiographs. VRF teeth were associated with indirect restorations, deep probing (>6 mm), absent cortical plate and J-shaped defects (P < .05); cracked teeth with direct restorations, shallow probing (<6 mm), intact cortical plate and angular defects (P < .001); radicular extension of the fracture line correlated with deep probing and J-shaped defects (P < .05).

CBCT bone-loss pattern plus probing depth can likely differentiate cracked teeth from VRF before intervention (angular defect suggests a crack; J-shaped defect, probing >6 mm and cortical-plate loss suggest VRF); limitations are the retrospective design and the abstract-only record (per-group n and effect sizes not available).

## Summary

Alaugaily and Azim (J Endod 2022) retrospectively compared clinical and radiographic characteristics of cracked teeth and teeth with vertical root fracture (VRF) in 95 patients with a longitudinal fracture confirmed by clinical visualization of the fracture line; 54 teeth had complete PA, CBCT and clinical data. Two independent examiners classified bone loss as no defect, angular, J-shaped or combined. CBCT detected bony defects suggestive of longitudinal fracture with 4.4 times the odds of PA radiographs. VRF teeth were associated with indirect restorations, probing >6 mm, absent cortical plate and J-shaped defects, whereas cracked teeth were associated with direct restorations, probing <6 mm, an intact cortical plate and angular defects. The authors conclude that CBCT bone-loss patterns can likely differentiate cracked teeth from VRF, and that an angular defect may suggest a crack before intervention. ## Key Contributions

- Describes CBCT bone-defect patterns (angular vs J-shaped) that track with cracked tooth vs VRF.
- Quantifies CBCT's advantage over PA radiographs for detecting bony defects of longitudinal fracture (4.4x odds).
- Pairs the imaging pattern with clinical predictors: restoration type (direct vs indirect), probing depth (<6 mm vs >6 mm), cortical plate status.
- Shows radicular extension of the fracture line correlates with deep probing and J-shaped defects.

## Methodology

Retrospective review of patient records: 95 patients (95 teeth) with a longitudinal fracture (cracked tooth only or VRF) confirmed by clinical visualization of the fracture line; 54 teeth with PA, CBCT, probing depths and clinical images. Two independent examiners evaluated PA and CBCT bone-loss patterns (no defect, angular, J-shaped, combined). Cohen kappa (between examiners; PA vs CBCT), Pearson chi-square, Fisher exact test and Bonferroni-adjusted post hoc tests (P < .05) were used. Per-group counts and kappa values are not given in the abstract.

## Results
