## Summary

Hernando-Dumaraog et al. conducted a systematic review of 12 studies evaluating diagnostic accuracy of methods for detecting dental cracks. Among individual technologies, optical coherence tomography (OCT) demonstrated the highest diagnostic accuracy. Single-method approaches were consistently inferior to multimodal strategies combining visual inspection, bite testing, transillumination, dye penetration, and imaging modalities. A critical finding was the absence of standardized diagnostic protocols across the included studies, hampering cross-study comparison. The authors recommend development of standardized multimodal protocols incorporating OCT for future clinical guidelines on dental crack diagnosis.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic review (12 studies) aggregating evidence on dental crack diagnostic accuracy across methods
- OCT identified as highest-accuracy single method — establishes OCT as priority technology for crack detection
- Confirms multimodal superiority over single-method diagnosis at SR evidence level
- Identifies standardization gap as major barrier to advancing the field
- Bridges in-vitro accuracy data (Kindaro 2025) with SR-level evidence synthesis

## Methodology

Systematic review design (12 studies included). Diagnostic accuracy outcomes extracted across methods: OCT, clinical visual examination, bite test, transillumination, dye penetration (methylene blue/crystal violet), CBCT, and potentially confocal microscopy. Reference standards varied across studies. Specific meta-analytic pooling details not available from abstract. Published in Dentistry Journal (MDPI open-access), 2026.

## Results

- OCT: highest diagnostic accuracy among single methods
- Multimodal (≥2 methods combined): superior to any single method

- Standardized protocols: absent across included studies — identified as primary clinical gap
