## Three-line Summary

Abstract-only SR+MA of endodontically treated cracked teeth (15 studies; 9 in time-anchored quantitative synthesis; all at serious risk of bias, very low certainty).

Pooled 4–5-year survival was 82.2% (95% CI 67.7–91.1%; I² 89.5%; 7 cohorts); two cohorts reported 10-year survival of 54% and 66%, and 55% at 15 years in the Singapore cohort; deep probing (aHR 2.48 for 4–5 mm, 3.19 for ≥6 mm) was the persistent adverse marker.

Prognosis should be time-dependent and individualized: survival is often acceptable at 4–5 years but declines thereafter, and the pooled estimate is highly heterogeneous.

## Summary

> [!note] Abstract-only page
> No PMCID exists; abstract-only — full text not retrieved. Content is grounded in the PubMed abstract only ([DOI 10.1016/j.joen.2026.10.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joen.2026.10.002)).

According to PubMed, this J Endod SR+MA re-anchors the prognosis of endodontically treated cracked teeth in time. Earlier syntheses emphasized 1–5-year cohorts; this review pools only 4–5-year proportions (logit scale, REML random effects, modified Knapp-Hartung) and treats 10- and 15-year data narratively. Survival at 4–5 years is 82.2% but with a very wide interval (67.7–91.1%) and I² of 89.5%, so setting-specific variation dominates. Beyond that horizon survival falls: 54% and 66% at 10 years in two independent cohorts, and 55% at 15 years in the Singapore cohort.

Deep crack-associated probing was the consistent adverse marker; in the longest cohort adjusted extraction hazards were 2.48 (4–5 mm) and 3.19 (≥6 mm). Radicular extension, periapical disease, multiple cracks, older age and absence of definitive cuspal coverage were additional risk indicators. The authors state probing depth is not newly identified; the contribution is quantifying its persistence. All included studies were at serious risk of bias and certainty was very low.

## Key Contributions

- Time-anchored pooled 4–5-year survival (82.2%) plus narrative 10/15-year horizons.
- Integrates probing depth, radicular extension, periapical disease, multiple cracks, age and cuspal coverage into one prognostic picture.
- Frames prognosis as time-dependent and individualized rather than a single survival figure.

## Methodology

PubMed/Europe PMC, Embase, Web of Science through 2026-10-03; longitudinal cohorts of endodontically treated permanent cracked teeth with ≥1 year follow-up; primary outcome tooth survival; duplicate screening and data verification; ROBINS-E-informed risk of bias. 15 studies included, 9 in quantitative synthesis (7 cohorts for the pooled 4–5-year estimate).

## Results

- Pooled 4–5-yr survival: 82.2% (95% CI 67.7–91.1%; I² 89.5%; 7 cohorts).
- 10-yr survival: 54% and 66% (two independent cohorts); 15-yr: 55% (Singapore cohort).
- Longest cohort: aHR for extraction 2.48 (probing 4–5 mm), 3.19 (≥6 mm).
- Other risk indicators: radicular extension, periapical disease, multiple cracks, older age, no definitive cuspal coverage.
- Risk of bias: serious in all studies; certainty very low.
