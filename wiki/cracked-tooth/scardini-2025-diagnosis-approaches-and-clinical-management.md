## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional online questionnaire survey (7 questions, content-validated by 8 experts, Brazil): 517 dentists (72.3% endodontists, 17.41% general practitioners, 10.25% other specialists) reported how they diagnose and manage suspected vertical root fracture (VRF).

The most-cited clinical sign was a narrow, deep periodontal pocket (71.8%), followed by pain on vertical percussion (54.7%) and mobility (42.7%); a J-/halo-shaped radiolucency was the leading radiographic sign (59.3%); 85.7% (443/517) request CBCT, 23.6% combine four exams, and 91.9% review both CBCT images and report; after CBCT, extraction was the most cited management (59.6%; 308 respondents) versus surgical exposure (17.2-17.4%; about 90), with endodontists more likely than other groups to consider surgical exploration or endodontic (re)treatment before extraction.

Self-reported practice from a convenience online sample (no outcome data, no ground-truth diagnosis): it shows that VRF work-up varies with professional training and that extraction is the default, but it provides no evidence on diagnostic accuracy or treatment success.

## Summary

Scardini et al. (J Clin Exp Dent 2025) surveyed 517 Brazilian dentists, mostly endodontists, on how they work up and manage suspected vertical root fracture (VRF). Respondents relied on a cluster of findings, led by a narrow deep periodontal pocket (71.8%) and a J-/halo-shaped radiolucency (59.3%), and 85.7% requested CBCT; nearly all (91.9%) reviewed both images and report. After CBCT, extraction was the most cited management (59.6%), with surgical exposure second (17.2%). Professional qualification was associated with the number of signs and exams reported and with management, endodontists being more diagnostic-intensive and more likely to consider alternatives to immediate extraction. As a self-reported convenience sample without outcomes, it describes practice, not accuracy or prognosis.

## Key Contributions

- Quantifies real-world VRF work-up: three signs and four auxiliary exams are the modal patterns; CBCT is requested by 85.7%.
- Shows training-dependent variation: median exams 4 (endodontists) vs 3 (general practitioners) vs 2 (other specialists), p<0.001; management differs by qualification (p<0.05).
- Documents extraction as the default after a CBCT-supported VRF assessment (59.6%), with surgical exposure the main alternative (17.2%).

## Methodology

Cross-sectional anonymous online questionnaire (Google Forms; e-mail and social media); 7 questions, content validity by 8 experts; n=517; Fisher's exact and Kruskal-Wallis with DSCF post hoc, Jamovi 2.5.4, p<0.05. Students and non-practising dentists excluded.
