## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of 64 studies (35 observational, 17 case series, 6 in vitro, 4 interventional, 2 SR) from multi-database search up to February 2022 on permanent cracked tooth treatment outcomes.
Cracked teeth with NP/RP show 80–93% pulp survival with direct/indirect composite restorations; monitoring is viable for asymptomatic teeth (80% stable at 3 years); endodontic outcomes comparable to non-cracked teeth (84–88% SR-level survival) when paired with cuspal-coverage restorations.
No consensus on crack line management or timing; treatment should be individualized with full-crown protection strongly favored post-RCT and direct restorations acceptable for vital cracked teeth with cuspal coverage.

## 세줄요약

2022년 2월까지 발표된 64편(관찰연구 35, 증례 17, 실험 6, 중재 4, 체계적 문헌고찰 2)을 포함한 균열치 (Cracked Tooth) 치료 결과에 관한 포괄적 서술 문헌고찰.
정상 치수 (Normal Pulp, NP) 또는 가역적 치수염 (Reversible Pulpitis, RP) 단계의 균열치는 직·간접 복합레진 수복 시 치수 생존율 80–93%; 무증상 균열치는 3년간 80% 안정적 경과(모니터링 가능); 근관치료 (Root Canal Treatment, RCT) 후 완전 크라운 장착 시 체계적 문헌고찰 수준 생존율 84–88%(비균열치와 동등).
균열선 처리 및 치료 시점에 관한 합의 없음; 치료는 증례별 개별화, RCT 후 반드시 크라운 보호 강력 권고, 활성치수 균열치는 교두 피개 (Cuspal Coverage) 직접수복 허용.

## Summary

This comprehensive narrative review synthesizes 64 studies on the treatment of permanent cracked teeth, covering monitoring, restorative approaches, endodontic management, and prognostic factors. A key finding is that monitoring without intervention is a valid option for asymptomatic cracked teeth: 80% of 2,858 cracked teeth initially recommended for monitoring remained on a monitoring plan at 3 years, with symptom decrease twice as common as symptom increase. For vital cracked teeth (NP/RP), pulp survival rates of 80–93% are achievable with direct or indirect restorations, and no statistically significant difference was found between restorations with or without cuspal coverage at 7 years, though higher failure rates without cuspal coverage were noted. Endodontically treated cracked teeth show survival comparable to non-cracked root-filled teeth when paired with full-crown restorations, with systematic review-level data reporting 84–88% survival rates. The review identifies key negative prognostic factors — deep periodontal pockets (≥5 mm), periapical lesions, distal marginal crack extension, and terminal abutment position — that should guide treatment planning.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that monitoring is a first-line option for asymptomatic cracked teeth: 80% stable at 3 years; symptoms decrease in 23% and increase in only 10% over 1 year without treatment
- Distinguishes single-stage vs. multiple-stage restorative approaches with evidence tables (Tables 1–3)
- Documents that full-crown restoration post-RCT dramatically improves survival: 97% vs 68% without crown at 4.5 years
- Identifies Iowa Index (periradicular diagnosis + distal marginal crack + probing ≥5 mm) as a clinical decision tool
- Demonstrates endodontic outcomes for cracked teeth comparable to non-cracked teeth when appropriately restored

## Methodology

- Design: Comprehensive narrative review (PRISMA flow documented)
- Databases: MEDLINE (Ovid), Embase (Ovid), Scopus, Web of Science + grey literature + hand-searching
- Search dates: up to February 22, 2022 (supplementary searches September 2021, February 2022)
- Inclusion: Clinical follow-up studies, lab studies, SR/MAs on permanent cracked teeth; English only
- Study selection: Two independent reviewers (Kakka, Gavriil); consensus for discrepancies
- Flow: 1318 initial hits → 679 screened → 85 full texts → 64 included
- Article types: 35 observational, 17 case series/reports, 6 in vitro, 4 interventional, 2 SR

## Results

### Monitoring vs. Restoring

- Ferracane et al. (n=2,858): 64% monitored; 80% remain on monitoring recommendation at 3 years; tooth survival >98%
- Hilton et al. (n=1,850): symptom decrease 23%, increase 10% at 1 year without treatment
- Female gender, molar, distal/buccal crack, and parafunction associated with symptom decrease; mesial crack with increase

### Vital Cracked Teeth (NP/RP)

| Study | Restoration | Pulp survival | Tooth survival |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opdam et al. (n=41, 7yr) | Direct composite ± cuspal coverage | 93% | — |
| Abbott & Leow (n=100, 5yr) | GI → Crown/onlay | 80% | 100% |
| Banerji et al. (n=151) | DCS → definitive | 93% | — |
| Signore et al. (n=43, 6yr) | Indirect composite onlays | 93% | — |
| Wu et al. (n=199, 3yr) | Band → Crown | 71% overall; 81% with crown | — |

No significant difference between with/without cuspal coverage at 7 years (Opdam), but higher failure rate without cuspal coverage.

### Endodontically Treated Cracked Teeth (CT-RCT)

| Study/SR | n | Follow-up | Survival | Success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leong SR | — | 5yr est. | 84% | — |
| Olivieri SR | — | 1yr est. | 88% | 82% |
| Nguyen Thi & Jansson | 200 | 4.5yr | 68% overall; **97% with crown** | — |
| Davis & Shariff | 65 | 2.8yr | 96.6% | 90.6% |

Full crown after RCT: 11-fold lower extraction risk vs no crown (Nguyen Thi & Jansson).

### Crack Line Management

- Complete removal: reduces biofilm burden; risk of pulp exposure (2/100 in Abbott & Leow)
- Partial removal: preferred to avoid iatrogenic damage; trace to fine craze or composite liner
- FEA: crack removal + composite core + crown > crown alone for fracture resistance
- Leaving cracks in situ + crown: 21% required RCT within 6 months (Krell & Rivera)

### Key Prognostic Factors (Negative)

- Periapical lesion: 27.5% lower success (Chen et al.); 11% lower 1-year success (Krell & Caplan)
- Deep periodontal pockets ≥5 mm: Iowa Index component
- Distal marginal crack: Iowa Index component; higher 1-year failure (Krell & Caplan)
- Terminal abutment: 96% higher tooth-loss risk (Ng et al.); not significant in SR (Leong)
- No cuspal coverage post-RCT: 11-fold higher extraction risk
