## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-centre diagnostic/descriptive study (Nippon Dental University, Niigata; CBCT 2017-2019): 50 patients with extraction-confirmed root fractures in endodontically treated teeth, of which 36 were vertical root fractures (VRF; 72%) analysed for cause, core construction, tooth type, fracture direction, and detectability on intraoral radiography vs CBCT (two radiologists, consensus read).

All 36 VRFs occurred in non-traumatized teeth; VRF counts did not differ by core type (metal / composite resin / no core, p = 0.558) but differed by tooth type (premolars most, p = 0.005) and direction (bucco-lingual about twice mesio-distal, p = 0.046; in premolars 76.2% vs 23.8%, p = 0.016). CBCT detected 91.7% of bucco-lingual and 100% of mesio-distal VRFs, whereas intraoral radiography detected 25% of bucco-lingual and none of the mesio-distal ones (both p < 0.001); two bucco-lingual VRFs were missed on CBCT because of metal artifacts.

The authors conclude CBCT is an adequate method for diagnosing VRF in root-filled teeth, but the design (only extraction-confirmed fractures, no fracture-free comparison teeth, n = 36, single centre, consensus read at the time of imaging) yields sensitivity only and cannot give specificity or true risk estimates for tooth type or core type.

## 세줄요약

후향적 단일기관 진단·기술 연구 (일본치과대학 니가타; CBCT 2017-2019) — 발거 후 파절이 확인된 근관치료치 환자 50명 중 수직치근파절 (Vertical Root Fracture, VRF) 36건(72%)을 대상으로 원인, 코어 (Core) 종류, 치아 종류, 파절 방향, 구내 방사선사진 (Intraoral radiography) 대 콘빔 CT (Cone-Beam CT, CBCT) 검출률을 분석 (방사선과 의사 2인 합의 판독).

VRF 36건 모두 외상 없는 치아였고, 코어 종류(금속·복합레진·코어 없음) 간 차이는 없었으나 (p = 0.558) 치아 종류(소구치 최다, p = 0.005)와 파절 방향(협설 파절이 근원심 파절의 약 2배, p = 0.046; 소구치에서 76.2% 대 23.8%, p = 0.016)에서 차이가 있었음. CBCT 검출률은 협설 파절 91.7%, 근원심 파절 100%, 구내 방사선사진은 협설 25%, 근원심 0% (모두 p < 0.001); 협설 파절 2건은 금속 아티팩트 (Artifact) 때문에 CBCT에서 놓침.

저자는 CBCT가 근관치료치 VRF 진단에 적절하다고 결론짓지만, 발거로 확인된 파절만 포함하고 파절 없는 비교 치아가 없으며 n = 36의 단일기관·촬영 당시 합의 판독 설계라 민감도만 산출되고 특이도나 치아·코어 종류별 실제 위험 추정은 불가능함.

## Summary

Mizuhashi et al. (J Imaging 2022) reviewed CBCT images of 50 patients whose root fractures in endodontically treated teeth were confirmed after extraction, and analysed the 36 vertical root fractures (VRF). VRF occurred only in non-traumatized teeth, was most frequent in premolars and was about twice as often bucco-lingual as mesio-distal, with no difference among metal-core, composite-resin-core and no-core teeth. Intraoral radiography detected about a quarter of bucco-lingual VRFs and no mesio-distal VRFs, while CBCT detected 91.7% and 100% respectively, with the only two CBCT misses attributed to metal artifacts. Because the sample contains only confirmed fractures read by consensus at the time of imaging, the paper supports "CBCT sees more VRF than intraoral radiography" but not specificity, risk ranking of tooth or core types, or equivalence of core materials.

## Key Contributions

- In vivo, extraction-confirmed series on VRF in root-filled teeth: premolar predominance and a bucco-lingual bias (premolars 76.2% bucco-lingual), with an anatomical explanation (thin mesio-distal dimension, oval canals prepared round, remaining dentin thinned).
- Direction-specific detection: intraoral radiography sees a fraction of bucco-lingual VRFs (those whose plane is parallel to the beam) and none of the mesio-distal VRFs; CBCT sees nearly all.
- Practical advice: align the beam parallel to the suspected bucco-lingual plane when using intraoral radiography; remove metal before CBCT when feasible, since metal artifacts caused the only CBCT misses.
- No difference in VRF counts across core constructions (metal, composite resin, none) among fractured teeth, though without an at-risk denominator.

## Methodology

Retrospective review (Jan 2017 - Aug 2019) of 50 patients (18 male, 32 female; mean age 58.7 +/- 16.0 y) with root fractures in endodontically treated teeth, all confirmed after extraction; 36 were VRF. All had intraoral radiography (70 kV, 7 mA) before CBCT (Fine Cube, standard mode 90 kV, 4 mA, FOV 81 x 81 mm, slice thickness 0.144 mm). Two radiologists read images independently with consensus for disagreements; detectability was decided at the time of imaging. Chi-square tests for characteristics; cross-tabulation with Yates correction and residual analysis for detection ability.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Vertical vs horizontal fracture (n = 50) | 72% vs 28%; chi2(1) = 9.68, p = 0.002 |
| Cause | All 36 VRF in non-traumatized teeth |
| Core construction | No difference (metal / composite resin / none), p = 0.558 |
| Tooth type | Premolars most; p = 0.005 |
| Direction | Bucco-lingual about 2x mesio-distal; p = 0.046 (upper p = 0.346, lower p = 0.059, n.s.) |
| Direction in premolars | 76.2% bucco-lingual vs 23.8% mesio-distal; p = 0.016 (anterior, molar n.s.) |
| CBCT detection | Bucco-lingual 91.7%, mesio-distal 100% |
| Intraoral radiography detection | Bucco-lingual 25%, mesio-distal 0%; both vs CBCT p < 0.001 (V = 0.68 and 1.00) |
| CBCT misses | 2 bucco-lingual VRFs, metal artifact |

Limits to carry with these numbers: no fracture-free comparison teeth (sensitivity only), consensus reading at the time of imaging, n = 36, single centre; tooth-type and core-type comparisons use only the distribution among fractured teeth, so they are not risk estimates. Table-level counts were not available in the PMC text.
