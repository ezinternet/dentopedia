## Three-line Summary

Study evaluating AI language model performance in cracked tooth syndrome (CTS) diagnosis across 50 standardized clinical scenarios versus established diagnostic criteria.

AI demonstrated variable performance; typical/classical CTS presentations were handled better than complex or atypical cases, establishing AI as a viable decision-support tool but not a standalone diagnostic replacement.

The first systematic AI benchmarking study in CTS diagnosis provides a reproducible evaluation framework and identifies the gap between AI pattern recognition and expert clinical judgment in non-typical scenarios.

## Summary

Eraslan Akyüz et al. evaluated the diagnostic capability of artificial intelligence (AI) language models for cracked tooth syndrome (CTS) by presenting 50 standardized clinical scenarios and comparing AI outputs to established diagnostic criteria. AI performance was variable across scenarios, with better accuracy on typical presentations and reduced agreement in complex or atypical cases. The study positions AI as a potential decision-support tool for CTS diagnosis — capable of pattern recognition in classic cases — rather than a standalone diagnostic system. This represents the first systematic benchmarking of AI for CTS-specific diagnosis, providing a reproducible evaluation framework for future studies as AI capabilities evolve. Published in Odontology (Springer), 2026.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic AI benchmarking study specifically for CTS diagnosis using 50 standardized scenarios
- Characterizes AI diagnostic capability profile: stronger on classic CTS, weaker on atypical/complex presentations
- Establishes reproducible 50-scenario evaluation framework for future AI diagnostic tool comparisons
- Frames AI appropriately as decision-support, not diagnostic replacement — important for clinical adoption framing
- Connects emerging AI diagnostic technology to existing multimodal diagnostic evidence base (Hernando 2026 SR)

## Methodology

Experimental performance evaluation study. 50 standardized CTS clinical scenarios presented to AI language models. Reference standard: established CTS diagnostic criteria (AAE/ESE guidelines). AI outputs compared to reference standard for each scenario. Outcome: diagnostic agreement per scenario, overall accuracy profile. Odontology (Springer), 2026. ## Results

- AI performance: variable across 50 scenarios
- Classic/typical CTS presentations: better AI diagnostic accuracy
- Complex/atypical CTS presentations: reduced accuracy vs expert clinical judgment
- Conclusion: AI = supplementary decision-support tool at current capability level
