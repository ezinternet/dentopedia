## Three-line Summary

Harvard narrative review synthesizing crack development mechanisms and framing crack management within a preventive endodontic paradigm to reduce tooth loss attributable to dental cracks.

Early protective restoration (before pulpal symptoms), modification of modifiable risk factors (bruxism, parafunction), and timely endodontic treatment are presented as the three pillars of preventive management to interrupt the crack → pulpitis → necrosis → tooth loss cascade.

Delayed intervention is emphasized as a key driver of tooth loss, reinforcing existing SR+MA data showing that cuspal coverage delay multiplies tooth loss risk by an order of magnitude.

## Summary

Pryles et al. from Harvard School of Dental Medicine present a narrative review reframing dental crack management through the lens of preventive endodontics. The paper synthesizes crack development mechanisms — including occlusal fatigue, parafunctional loading, iatrogenic factors, and restorative stress — and argues that the clinical goal should be interrupting the crack progression cascade (craze line → cracked tooth → incomplete/complete fracture) before irreversible pulpal compromise occurs. The three-pillar preventive approach includes: (1) early protective restoration before symptom onset, (2) modification of modifiable risk factors such as bruxism and parafunction, and (3) timely endodontic intervention when pulpal involvement is detected. The authors emphasize that delayed intervention significantly worsens outcomes, consistent with existing SR+MA data showing an approximately 11-fold increase in tooth loss when cuspal coverage is withheld after endodontic treatment.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces "preventive endodontics" as a conceptual framework for dental crack management — novel paradigm shift from reactive to proactive care
- Synthesizes crack development cascade with emphasis on clinically modifiable intervention points
- Positions bruxism/parafunction management as underutilized preventive lever in cracked tooth care
- Reinforces timing-of-intervention as the dominant predictor of outcomes — aligns with Zhang 2024 SR+MA
- Harvard institutional authority strengthens clinical practice change argument for pre-symptomatic cuspal coverage

## Methodology

Narrative review; Harvard School of Dental Medicine. Scope: crack development mechanisms, risk factors, prevention strategies, timing of endodontic intervention. Literature not systematically searched or PRISMA reported — review reflects expert synthesis and clinical perspective from a leading endodontic academic center. Published in Journal of Endodontics, 2025.

## Results

- Delayed protective restoration → significantly worse tooth loss outcomes (quantitative data consistent with prior SR+MA)
- Pre-symptomatic cuspal coverage → reduces crack cascade progression risk
- Bruxism/parafunction identified as key modifiable risk factors — overlooked in most crack management protocols
- Preventive endodontics framework: early detection + conservative restoration + risk factor management = tooth retention strategy
