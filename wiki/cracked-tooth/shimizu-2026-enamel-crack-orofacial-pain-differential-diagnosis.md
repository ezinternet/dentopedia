## Three-line Summary

Prospective 3-year follow-up study investigating enamel crack-associated orofacial pain (구강안면통증, OFP) patterns and their differential diagnosis from TMD and neuropathic pain using multimodal clinical criteria.

Enamel cracks produce characteristic, distinguishable orofacial pain patterns; differential diagnosis from non-crack OFP conditions (TMD, neuropathic pain, dentinal hypersensitivity) requires integration of multiple clinical criteria over a longitudinal observation period.

The 3-year longitudinal data provide the first prospective evidence for crack-specific OFP progression patterns and support routine enamel crack evaluation within orofacial pain workup protocols.

## Summary

Shimizu et al. conducted a prospective clinical study with 3-year follow-up to investigate the relationship between enamel cracks and orofacial pain (OFP), with a specific focus on differential diagnosis. The study identifies characteristic OFP patterns associated with enamel cracks and distinguishes them from other common OFP conditions including temporomandibular disorder (TMD), neuropathic pain, and dentinal hypersensitivity. The 3-year longitudinal design is particularly valuable, as it documents crack-related pain progression over time — data not available from cross-sectional studies. Differential diagnosis required the integration of multiple clinical criteria, confirming that no single test is sufficient for distinguishing crack-related OFP. The study supports incorporation of systematic enamel crack assessment into standard orofacial pain evaluation protocols. Published in Healthcare (MDPI, Basel), 2026.

## Key Contributions

- 3-year longitudinal OFP data for enamel crack patients — fills critical temporal gap in crack-pain literature
- Establishes crack-specific OFP patterns distinguishable from TMD and neuropathic pain
- Provides differential diagnosis framework integrating multiple clinical criteria
- Supports enamel crack evaluation as a standard component of OFP workup
- Bridges crack science (Raj 2025, Hernando 2026) with orofacial pain medicine (TMD, neuropathic pain literature)

## Methodology

Prospective clinical study with 3-year follow-up. Patients with orofacial pain and/or enamel cracks. Comparisons: crack-related OFP vs TMD, neuropathic pain, dentinal hypersensitivity. Clinical criteria applied longitudinally over 3 years to track pain patterns and diagnostic accuracy. Specific cohort size, inclusion criteria, and statistical methods not available from abstract. Healthcare (MDPI open-access), 2026.

## Results

- Enamel cracks: associated with characteristic orofacial pain patterns, distinguishable from TMD/neuropathic pain
- Differential diagnosis requires multimodal criteria — single test insufficient
- 3-year progression patterns documented for crack-related OFP (vs non-crack OFP)
- Specific diagnostic accuracy metrics not available from abstract
