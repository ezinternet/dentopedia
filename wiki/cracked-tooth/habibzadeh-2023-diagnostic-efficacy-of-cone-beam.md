## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (5 databases 2000–2022; 1493 records identified, 20 studies included — 17 no-post experiments and 8 post experiments, mostly in-vitro, only 2 in-vivo and 1 clinical study) of CBCT diagnostic performance for vertical root fracture (VRF) in endodontically treated teeth.

Unweighted mean ± SD across studies without a post: sensitivity 71.50 ± 22.19% (range 32–100), specificity 75.64 ± 19.41% (36.7–100), accuracy 78.47 ± 17.19%; with a post: sensitivity 72.76 ± 18.73%, specificity 75.44 ± 18.26%, accuracy 74.02 ± 10.64%. Sensitivity was 58.5% in-vivo and 66.4% ex-vivo (specificity 46.8% ex-vivo) versus 75.1% in-vitro, and 100% sensitivity occurred only in-vitro.

The authors could not confirm optimal CBCT efficacy because of low sensitivity, strong heterogeneity (no pooled meta-analysis, no formal risk-of-bias tool reported) and scarce clinical data, and advise combining radiographic and clinical examination.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 (Systematic Review) — 5개 데이터베이스(2000–2022)에서 1493편을 확인해 20편 포함(무포스트 17개·포스트 8개 실험; 대부분 체외 연구, 생체 내 2편·임상 1편), 근관치료치 수직치근파절 (Vertical Root Fracture, VRF)에 대한 콘빔 CT (Cone-Beam Computed Tomography, CBCT) 진단 성능을 평가.

포스트 없는 경우 연구 간 단순 평균 ± SD: 민감도 71.50 ± 22.19% (32–100), 특이도 75.64 ± 19.41% (36.7–100), 정확도 78.47 ± 17.19%; 포스트 있는 경우 민감도 72.76 ± 18.73%, 특이도 75.44 ± 18.26%, 정확도 74.02 ± 10.64%. 민감도는 생체 내 (in-vivo) 58.5%·발거치 (ex-vivo) 66.4% (특이도 46.8%)로 체외 (in-vitro) 75.1%보다 낮았고, 민감도 100%는 체외 연구에서만 나옴.

낮은 민감도·연구 간 이질성·임상 근거 부족으로 CBCT의 최적 효능을 확증하지 못하며(메타분석 통합·편향위험 평가 없음), VRF는 방사선 소견과 임상 검사를 병용해 진단해야 함.

## Summary

This systematic review (Habibzadeh et al., BMC Medical Imaging 2023) asks whether CBCT is accurate enough to detect vertical root fractures in endodontically treated teeth. Searching five databases for 2000–2022 yielded 20 diagnostic studies, 17 experiments in root-filled teeth without a post and 8 with a post (several studies contribute to both), with no formal risk-of-bias appraisal and no pooling. Unweighted averages of study-level estimates cluster around 71–73% sensitivity and 75–76% specificity regardless of post presence, but the spread is wide (sensitivity 30–100%) and performance drops in the more clinically realistic designs (in-vivo sensitivity 58.5%, ex-vivo specificity 46.8%, a retrospective clinical-image study with cast metal posts 46.6% sensitivity). The authors conclude that CBCT efficacy for VRF remains unproven and that diagnosis should combine clinical and radiographic examination. Beam-hardening artifacts from gutta-percha, metal posts and bioceramic fillers, and the high resolution required to resolve narrow fracture lines, are the proposed reasons.

## Key Contributions

- Per-study extraction of CBCT sensitivity, specificity and accuracy for VRF in root-filled teeth, split by presence of an intracanal post and by study design (in-vitro, ex-vivo, in-vivo, clinical, cross-sectional, retrospective).
- Shows that the high accuracy figures come from in-vitro studies with induced, wide fractures; 100% sensitivity appears only in-vitro.
- Collates artifact mechanisms and acquisition parameters (voxel size, FOV, kVp, mA, post alloy atomic number, filler radiopacifiers) relevant to VRF detection.

## Methodology

- Systematic review with descriptive synthesis (reporting follows "a modification" of the Cochrane Handbook; PRISMA flowchart; PICO table).
- Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, Embase (2000–2022) plus Google Scholar and grey literature; English full text only.
- 1493 records, 93 after screening and de-duplication, 73 excluded on eligibility, 20 included; two screeners with a third author arbitrating.
- Outcomes: sensitivity, specificity, accuracy (AUC also extracted). "Overall" values are unweighted means and SDs of study-level numbers, not a meta-analytic pooled estimate.

## Results

Root-filled teeth, no intracanal post (17 experiments), mean (SD) %:

| Study design | Sensitivity | Specificity | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-vitro | 75.11 (23.53) | 81.93 (16.90) | 88.98 (8.60) |
| In-vivo (2 studies) | 58.50 (36.06) | 79.00 (2.83) | 81.00 |
| Ex-vivo (2 studies) | 66.37 (12.04) | 46.80 (17.49) | 54.87 (15.90) |
| Clinical (Edlund 2011, n = 32) | 88.0 | 75.0 | 84.0 |
| Cross-sectional (Wanderley 2021) | 53.1 | 80.5 | 67.7 |
| **Overall** | **71.50 (22.19)** | **75.64 (19.41)** | **78.47 (17.19)** |

Root-filled teeth with intracanal post (8 studies):

| Study design | Sensitivity | Specificity | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-vitro | 74.09 (18.46) | 78.57 (17.81) | 76.04 (10.07) |
| Ex-vivo (Oliveira 2021, metallic post) | 83 | 53 | 68 |
| Retrospective (Wanderley 2021, cast metal post) | 46.6 | 60.4 | 57.8 |
| **Overall** | **72.76 (18.73)** | **75.44 (18.26)** | **74.02 (10.64)** |

- Ranges: sensitivity 32–100%, specificity 36.7–100% (no post); sensitivity 30–92%, specificity 45–100% (post).
- Voxel size: 0.125 mm voxels gave sensitivity 100% / accuracy 99% versus 78% / 79% at 0.25 mm in one included study (Junqueira 2013).
- Fiber vs metal posts: the authors argue fiberglass posts produce fewer artifacts, but one included study found lower sensitivity with fiberglass (30–50%) than with metal (70–80%), consistent with a less conspicuous fracture line; two in-vitro studies reported 100% specificity with metal posts, which the authors ascribe to wide induced fractures.
- Inter-observer agreement spanned 32–99.4%; observer calibration was reported as performed in 9 of 20 studies.
- The authors cite the wider literature as discordant: a meta-analysis (Talwar et al.) with sensitivity 0.752 / specificity 0.652, an SR (Chang et al.) with 84–100% / 64–100% in clinical studies, and an SR (Corbella et al.) finding no evidence of added benefit of CBCT over other modalities.
