## Three-line Summary

International cross-sectional survey (J Dent 2026): 427 dentists across Greece, UK, and Europe; electronic questionnaire assessed diagnostic methods, risk factor beliefs, and treatment decisions for symptomatic cracked teeth across four clinical scenarios.

Periodontal burden was the single strongest extraction trigger (multivariate p<0.001); CBCT use was not independently associated with changes in diagnosis or treatment planning; significant management heterogeneity persisted across all clinical scenarios.

Cracked tooth management is inconsistent globally, driven primarily by periodontal status — not technology (CBCT) — underscoring the need for evidence-based international guidelines.

## Summary

Gioti et al. (J Dent 2026) surveyed 427 dentists across Greece, UK, and other European countries on their diagnostic and treatment approaches to symptomatic cracked teeth. The questionnaire, validated by a 6-expert panel, included demographic data, diagnostic practices, risk factor beliefs, and four clinical scenarios. History taking and magnifying devices were equally the most common diagnostic tools (both 79.5%). Nearly all respondents linked cracked teeth to bruxism (95.2%) and metal restorations (92.4%). In multivariate analysis, periodontal burden independently and most strongly predicted extraction decisions (p<0.001), while CBCT use showed no independent association with changes to diagnosis or treatment planning. Management variability was highest in cases of uncertain prognosis, confirming the absence of consensus protocols.

## Key Contributions

- Periodontal status (probing depth, bone loss) is the dominant and independent driver of extraction decisions — quantifies what clinicians already sense
- CBCT use does not independently change clinical decisions — questions routine CBCT in cracked tooth workup beyond ruling out VRF
- History taking + magnifying devices + bite test = core diagnostic triad in real-world practice
- 1–5 cracked tooth cases per month is the majority experience — high prevalence in daily practice
- Management heterogeneity in uncertain-prognosis scenarios reveals a gap that evidence-based guidelines must fill

## Methodology

Cross-sectional electronic questionnaire study; international sample (Greece, UK, Europe); n=427 completed responses; content validity by 6 experts; test-retest reliability (n=20); 4 clinical scenarios; descriptive + multivariate statistics; p<0.05.
