## Three-line Summary

Retrospective records review of one university endodontics program (UEP; 21,156 patients, 2011-Mar 2022) and one private endodontics practice (PP; 7,209 treatment letters, 2006-Mar 2022) estimating vertical root fracture (VRF) prevalence and its change before vs during COVID-19.

Overall VRF prevalence was 1.80% (UEP), 2.62% (PP) and 2.01% combined; the combined total rose from 1.72% pre-COVID-19 to 3.82% during COVID-19 (p < 0.0001), but the rise in confirmed-positive cases was smaller (combined 0.65% to 1.01%, p = 0.0052; PP alone not significant, p = 0.07) than the rise in merely suspected cases (combined 0.99% to 2.31%).

VRF is uncommon at patient level and not an "epidemic", yet suspected cases far outnumber visually confirmed ones, so visual confirmation should precede an extraction recommendation; the keyword-search retrospective design, shorter COVID window and no causal data limit the COVID interpretation.

## 세줄요약

후향적 기록 조사 — 대학 근관치료 프로그램 (University Endodontics Program, UEP; 환자 21,156명, 2011~2022.3)과 개인 근관치료 의원 (Private Practice, PP; 진료 서신 7,209건, 2006~2022.3)에서 수직치근파절 (Vertical Root Fracture, VRF) 유병률과 COVID-19 전후 변화를 평가.

전체 VRF 유병률은 UEP 1.80%, PP 2.62%, 합산 2.01%였고, 합산 유병률은 COVID-19 이전 1.72%에서 COVID-19 기간 3.82%로 증가 (p < 0.0001); 다만 확진 (confirmed positive) 증가는 합산 0.65%→1.01% (p = 0.0052, PP 단독은 비유의 p = 0.07)로 의심 (suspected) 증가 (합산 0.99%→2.31%)보다 작았음.

VRF는 환자 단위로는 흔하지 않아 "유행 (epidemic)"이라 할 수 없으나 의심 사례가 확진보다 훨씬 많으므로 발치 권고 전 육안 확인이 필요; 키워드 검색 기반 후향 설계, 짧은 COVID 구간, 인과 자료 부재로 COVID 해석에는 한계.

## Summary

This retrospective study (University of Southern California and King Abdulaziz University groups; Int J Dent 2023) searched electronic notes of a university advanced-endodontics department (2011 to March 2022) and treatment letters of one private endodontics office (2006 to March 2022) for vertical root fracture. VRF prevalence per patient was 1.80% in the university program, 2.62% in private practice and 2.01% combined, which the authors take as evidence against the claim that VRF is an epidemic. Comparing pre-COVID-19 with COVID-19 years, the combined total prevalence rose from 1.72% to 3.82% (p < 0.0001), but most of that rise came from suspected cases (combined 0.99% to 2.31%), whereas confirmed-positive cases rose from 0.65% to 1.01% (p = 0.0052; significant in the university group, not in private practice). Because suspected cases outnumber confirmed ones and crown-originating cracks and split teeth were often mislabeled as VRF, the authors stress visual confirmation before recommending extraction.

## Key Contributions

- Patient-level VRF prevalence from an academic and a private endodontic setting (1.80% vs 2.62%; combined 2.01%).
- Pre-COVID-19 vs COVID-19 comparison showing a rise in recorded VRF, driven mainly by suspected cases.
- Distinguishes suspected from confirmed (radiographic, visual with methylene blue after gingival reflection, or surgical) VRF; fracture seen only after extraction was not accepted as confirmation.
- Notes misuse of the VRF label for crown-originating cracks and split teeth.

## Methodology

- Retrospective review: Axium electronic notes (UEP advanced endodontics, Jan 2011-Mar 2022; 21,156 patients) and treatment letters (PP, 2006-Mar 2022; 7,209 letters, 1,018 in the COVID-19 period).
- Keyword search ("vertical root fracture", "VRF", "fracture", "longitudinal ...", "vertical", etc.), duplicates removed (same patient and tooth).
- Cases classified as suspected, confirmed positive (radiographic [PP only], visual, surgical) or confirmed negative; one-tailed z-test, 95% confidence. COVID-19 period = Jan 2020-Mar 2022.

## Results

| Outcome | UEP | PP | Combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall prevalence | 1.80% | 2.62% | 2.01% |
| Total, pre-COVID-19 to COVID-19 | - | - | 1.72% to 3.82% (p < 0.0001) |
| Suspected, pre-COVID-19 to COVID-19 | 0.78% to 1.91% (p < 0.0001) | 1.62% to 3.44% (p < 0.0001) | 0.99% to 2.31% (significant) |
| Confirmed positive, pre-COVID-19 to COVID-19 | 0.65% to 0.99% (p = 0.0202) | 0.66% to 1.08% (p = 0.0708, ns) | 0.65% to 1.01% (p = 0.0052) |

- Abstract and Results disagree in places (abstract: "increasing from 2.1% to 3.82%", Results: 1.72% to 3.82% with a 2.1 percentage-point increase; PP confirmed p = 0.0721 in the abstract vs 0.0708 in the text; UEP notes reviewed 60,733 vs 60,773); the text version is used here.
- The authors speculate that pandemic stress, masticatory load and deferred dental care may explain the rise; no causal data were collected. Higher PP prevalence is attributed to more retreatment cases and longer data collection.
- Limitations: keyword retrieval of free-text notes, multi-tooth notes, undefined fate of suspected cases, no UEP radiographic confirmation, few CBCT scans, and no minimally invasive access or preparation in the treated cohort.
