## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (PubMed search to March 2022, English full texts; Int Endod J "Present status and future directions" series) of the prevalence, aetiology, pathogenesis, diagnosis, management and prevention of vertical root fracture (VRF) in root filled teeth.

In the cited retrospective series 86-97% of extracted VRF teeth were root filled, and reported VRF prevalence in root filled teeth is 4-32%; CBCT cannot reliably detect the fracture line itself but does reveal the periradicular bone-loss pattern, and extraction remains the most predictable treatment (root resection in selected multi-rooted teeth).

Prevention (preserving residual tooth structure, prompt cuspal coverage within about 4 months, passive posts, occlusal/parafunction control) is the main lever, but the review itself notes high-level evidence is lacking and most data are retrospective or ex vivo.

## Summary

This narrative review (King's College London; PubMed search to March 2022) is the long-form account of vertical root fracture (VRF) in root filled teeth. Most extracted VRF teeth in the cited series were root filled (86-97%), VRF prevalence in root filled teeth is reported at 4-32%, and the disease behaves as a slow fatigue process (crack initiation, propagation, separation) driven by lost tooth structure, dentine ageing, root anatomy (isthmus, flattened roots) and occlusal load. Diagnosis rests on magnification, probing for isolated narrow pockets, sinus tracts and the periradicular bone-loss pattern; CBCT shows that pattern but not the fracture line itself. Extraction is the most predictable treatment, so prevention (conservative procedures, early cuspal coverage, passive posts, occlusal control) is emphasized, with the authors openly stating that high-level evidence is lacking.

## Key Contributions

- Working classification: incomplete VRF (one side of root) vs complete VRF (proximal to opposite side); split tooth = visible separation.
- Predisposing vs contributory risk factors (Table 1) and a three-stage pathogenesis model (initiation, propagation, separation) with fracture toughness as probably the dominant factor.
- Critical appraisal of the microcrack literature: sectioning studies are invalid; micro-CT/cadaver data show canal preparation does not create new microcracks.
- Positions CBCT as a tool for secondary periradicular bone-loss patterns, not for detecting the fracture line; artefacts from gutta-percha and metal posts raise false positives (fibre posts interfere less).
- Prevention framework and research agenda (RCTs of cuspal coverage; outcome studies of VRF repair).

## Methodology

Narrative review, single database (PubMed), English full texts, cut-off March 2022, plus manual reference search; no formal appraisal or pooling. Evidence cited is predominantly retrospective cohort/epidemiological, ex vivo/micro-CT, and case reports.

## Results

| Domain | Key finding (as cited by the review) |
|---|---|
| Extracted VRF teeth that were root filled | 93.6% (Yoshino 2015), 97% (Sugaya 2015, n=304), 86% (Liao 2017) |
| VRF prevalence in root filled teeth | 4-32% (heterogeneous diagnosis) |
| Residual structure | <30% coronal tooth structure: higher 1-year retreatment failure (Al-Nuaimi 2017, n=137) |
| Pre-existing cracks | 7.1% of 633 non-endodontically treated extracted teeth (PradeepKumar 2017); cracked-tooth 5-year survival after RCT 84.1% (Leong 2020 SR) |
| Microcracks from canal preparation | None in 178 cadaver teeth / 65,530 micro-CT images (De-Deus 2019) |
| Occlusion | Terminal teeth without proximal contacts: 58% of extractions due to fracture vs 38% with contacts (Ng 2011) |
| Cuspal coverage | 10-year survival 91.3% crowned vs 76% not (Boren 2015); crown >4 months after RCT ~3x extraction risk (Pratt 2016) |
| Presentation | Typically 2-5 y after RCT; sinus tract 18.2-67%; isolated deep narrow pocket pathognomonic if on both sides of root |
| CBCT | Cannot reliably detect VRF within root; more sensitive for periradicular bone loss (Byakova 2019) |
| Treatment | Extraction most predictable; root resection in selected multi-rooted teeth; repair only case-series level |
| Barrier + coverage in cracked teeth | 96.6% survival at 4 years (Davis & Shariff 2019, prospective) |

Limitations: selection/operator bias in retrospective survival data, extraction-time misclassification of VRF as periodontal disease, no RCTs on cuspal coverage and VRF-specific failure.
