## Three-line Summary

Retrospective 5-year study of 321 posterior cracked teeth treated with cuspal coverage direct resin composite (single operator, no indirect restorations) achieved 94.1% tooth survival.
Periodontal lesion from crack propagation was the dominant independent risk factor for tooth loss (OR=10.52, 95% CI 4.1–27.1), far outweighing endodontic treatment (OR=2.67); fracture type and vitality were non-significant.
Results challenge the assumption that indirect restorations are necessary for cracked teeth, and highlight crack-induced periodontal involvement as the critical prognostic determinant.

## Summary

This retrospective clinical study evaluated 321 posterior cracked teeth (321 patients; mean age 53.9 years) treated exclusively with cuspal coverage direct resin composite restorations over a mean follow-up of 63 months (minimum 5 years). Two direct techniques were employed: full cusp coverage and partial cusp coverage with box-shaped reinforcement of opposing walls. No indirect restorations were included. Ethics approval from a university committee was obtained. Stepwise logistic regression identified independent predictors of tooth loss.

At 5-year follow-up, 302/321 teeth survived (94.1%). During follow-up, 126 teeth (39.2%) required root canal treatment and 41 (12.8%) developed periodontal lesions from crack deepening. The two restoration designs showed similar clinical performance.

The dominant risk factor for tooth loss was development of a periodontal lesion (OR=10.52, 95% CI 4.1–27.1, p<0.0001), reflecting crack propagation into the periodontium. Endodontic treatment need was a secondary independent risk factor (OR=2.67, 95% CI 1.04–6.89, p=0.04). Fracture type, pulp calcification, tooth vitality, and apical periodontitis were not statistically significant (all p>0.05).

## Key Contributions

- Provides 5-year survival data (94.1%) for direct resin composite cuspal coverage in cracked teeth, supporting direct restoration as a viable alternative to indirect approaches.
- Establishes periodontal lesion (OR=10.52) as the critical prognostic determinant — more influential than endodontic status or fracture morphology.
- Demonstrates equivalence between full and partial cuspal coverage direct designs, offering flexibility in cavity planning.
- Supports the concept that direct resin composite can suppress crack propagation and symptom remission when fracture has not yet extended to the periodontium.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective, single-operator, private-practice clinical study
- **Recruitment**: January 1991 – December 2018
- **Inclusion**: Posterior cracked teeth; ≥5-year follow-up; no pre-existing endodontic treatment
- **Teeth**: 79 maxillary premolars, 21 mandibular premolars, 78 maxillary molars, 143 mandibular molars
- **Interventions**: Full cusp coverage direct resin composite; partial cusp coverage with box reinforcement
- **Primary outcome**: Tooth survival at 5 years
- **Statistics**: Stepwise logistic regression for tooth loss predictors
