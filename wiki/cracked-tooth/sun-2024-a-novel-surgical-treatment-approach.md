## Three-line Summary

Single case report (BMC Oral Health 2024): a 34-year-old woman with a complete buccal-palatal vertical root fracture (VRF) of a root-filled, crowned maxillary right first premolar (#14) with a sinus tract, an 8-10 mm narrow palatal pocket and periapical plus palatal bone destruction on CBCT.

The tooth was extracted, the stained fracture was widened (~1 mm wide, 1.5-2 mm deep) with two trapezoidal retention forms, filled with adhesive resin, covered with iRoot BP Plus bioceramic cement (dual layer), and replanted with a 4-week fiber-band splint within about 15 min extraoral; at 24 months the tooth was asymptomatic with ~3 mm probing depth, normal mobility and radiographic/CBCT bone regeneration.

One tooth with one 24-month observation cannot show efficacy or compare against other VRF repairs; the authors themselves say clinical trials are needed, and the first-layer material (called adhesive resin but listed under a brand name) is described inconsistently in the text.

## Summary

Sun and colleagues (Wuhan University) report one maxillary right first premolar with a complete buccal-palatal vertical root fracture that was repaired extraorally with a "dual-layered" technique and replanted. The fracture line was widened, two trapezoidal retention forms were cut, the space was filled with adhesive resin, and a bioceramic (iRoot BP Plus) cement layer was placed over the resin and in a retrograde apical cavity before replantation with a 4-week fiber-band splint, all within about 15 minutes. At 24 months the tooth was asymptomatic with about 3 mm probing depth and normal mobility, and radiograph and CBCT showed bone regeneration. This is a single case without a comparator, so it is hypothesis-generating only; the authors themselves call for clinical trials.

## Key Contributions

- Describes a resin + bioceramic dual-layer fracture repair with mechanical retention forms for a posterior (premolar) VRF, argued to combine the fixation strength of resin with the periodontal bioactivity of calcium silicate cement.
- Notes that previously reported repairs were mostly in anterior teeth and positions this case as a posterior-tooth example.
- Provides a step-by-step extraoral protocol (methylene-blue staining, microscope, 1 mm x 1.5-2 mm fracture widening, retention forms, retrograde apical cavity, <= 15 min extraoral time).

## Methodology

Case report, n = 1 (34-year-old woman, tooth #14 root-treated and crowned 2 years earlier, sinus tract, 8-10 mm palatal pocket, apical and palatal bone destruction on CBCT, diagnosis VRF). Treatment: extraction, repair, replantation, 4-week elastic fiber-band fixation. Follow-up: clinical and radiographic/CBCT review at 24 months.

## Results

| Outcome | Result at 24 months |
|---|---|
| Symptoms | asymptomatic, normal function |
| Gingiva | normal mucosa |
| Probing depth | about 3 mm (pre-operative palatal pocket 8-10 mm) |
| Mobility | normal |
| Radiograph/CBCT | significant periodontal bone regeneration, periapical defect healing (qualitative; no measurements) |

Caveats flagged from the text: the first-layer material is called a self-etching light-curing adhesive resin but the listed product is Ketac Molar Easymix (3M ESPE), which the paper does not reconcile; no intermediate follow-up, root-resorption or ankylosis assessment is reported.

## Clinical Insights

- Held evidence places this at the lowest rung: [[resin-bonding/corbella-2025-surgical-techniques-vertical-root-fractures-sr]] found only low-quality case-series evidence for surgical VRF repair and concluded no technique is a standard; a single 24-month case does not change that bottom line.
- What the case adds is a design rationale (separate the "fixation" and "bioactivity" jobs between two materials) and a posterior-tooth example with a short extraoral time (about 15 min). The material identity of the first layer should be confirmed before copying the protocol.
- Extraction remains the default for VRF per the held reviews; this report is relevant only as an option to discuss for a motivated patient with a restorable premolar and little periodontal destruction, with long-term uncertainty disclosed.
