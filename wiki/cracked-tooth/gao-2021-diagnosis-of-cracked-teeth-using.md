## Three-line Summary

Narrative literature review plus clinical case experience (Nanjing Stomatological Hospital radiology department) on CBCT diagnosis of the five American Association of Endodontists (AAE) cracked-tooth types, of which vertical root fracture (VRF) is one; no n or quantitative pooling is reported in the abstract.

Because cracks and fractures vary and overlap, some narrow VRF root fractures cannot be seen on CBCT while some wide crown cracks can; diagnosis is influenced by CBCT device-related, patient-related and evaluator-related factors, and the review also contrasts root fractures with lateral canals and with external root resorption on CBCT images.

CBCT is useful but not definitive for crack/fracture diagnosis; strategies targeting device settings, patient factors and evaluator training can improve accuracy, but the abstract gives no sensitivity/specificity figures (abstract-only record).

## Summary

Gao, Cao and Lin (Dentomaxillofac Radiol 2021) review how CBCT contributes to diagnosing the five AAE cracked-tooth types, with typical CBCT cases from their own radiology practice. They stress that cracks and fractures vary and overlap, so narrow root fractures in VRF may be invisible on CBCT while some wide crown cracks are visible. The review groups the factors that affect CBCT diagnosis into device-related, patient-related and evaluator-related factors and discusses strategies to raise accuracy in practice. It closes by comparing CBCT images of root fractures with lateral canals and external root resorption, the main look-alikes. This page is built from the abstract only; the full text was not retrieved, so no quantitative accuracy data are recorded.

## Key Contributions

- Frames CBCT value by AAE crack type rather than treating "cracked tooth" as one entity.
- Names three modifiers of CBCT diagnostic accuracy: device, patient, evaluator.
- Adds a CBCT differential: root fracture vs lateral canal vs external root resorption.
