## Three-line Summary

A retrospective cohort of 204 endodontically treated cracked teeth followed for 10–15 years found overall survival rates of 66% at 10 years and 55% at 15 years. Preoperative probing depth (PD) was the dominant prognostic factor: teeth with PD <4 mm achieved 76%/64% survival at 10/15 years, while PD ≥6 mm carried a 3.19× adjusted hazard of extraction. Success rates (asymptomatic + complete radiographic healing) declined steeply from 64% to 37% between years 10 and 15.

## Summary

This retrospective study investigated long-term survival and success of 204 endodontically treated cracked teeth in 196 patients over a 10–15 year period. Two endpoints were tracked: survival (tooth still present) and success (asymptomatic with complete radiographic healing). Cox proportional hazards regression identified preoperative probing depth as the primary statistically significant predictor of tooth loss. Multivariable analysis revealed that PD 4–5 mm and PD ≥6 mm carried adjusted hazard ratios of 2.48 and 3.19 respectively for extraction. Older patient age was a modest additional independent risk factor (aHR 1.03 per year). The authors concluded that endodontic treatment of cracked teeth with favorable preoperative PDs is a viable long-term treatment option comparable to non-cracked teeth.

## Key Contributions

1. Longest follow-up cohort (15 years) for endodontically treated cracked teeth in the current literature.
2. Quantifies PD-stratified survival thresholds enabling evidence-based triage: PD <4 mm is a favorable prognostic threshold.
3. Distinguishes survival (tooth present) from success (asymptomatic + radiographic healing) — success at 15 years is markedly lower (37%) than survival (55%).
4. Provides adjusted hazard ratios for PD categories and patient age as independent survival predictors.
5. Supports recommending endodontic treatment for cracked teeth when preoperative PD is favorable.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort with 10–15 year clinical and radiographic review
- **Sample**: 196 patients, 204 endodontically treated cracked teeth
- **Outcome 1 — Survival**: tooth present at review
- **Outcome 2 — Success**: asymptomatic with complete radiographic healing
- **Statistical analysis**: Cox proportional hazards regression; multivariable analysis with adjusted hazard ratios
- **Key predictor**: Preoperative probing depth categorized as <4 mm, 4–5 mm, ≥6 mm

| 15 years | 55% | 48%–63% |

### Overall Success
| Timepoint | Success Rate |
|---|---|
| 10 years | 64% |

### Age as Risk Factor
- Older age: aHR = 1.03 per year (95% CI: 1.00–1.05, P = .026) — modest independent risk factor for extraction

### Clinical Conclusion
Endodontic treatment of cracked teeth with preoperative PD <4 mm yields 15-year survival comparable to non-cracked teeth and is a recommended long-term treatment option.
