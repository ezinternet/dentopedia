## Three-line Summary

Ex vivo diagnostic-accuracy study (University of Minnesota): 115 analysed extracted, root-canal-treated human tooth roots (62 with mechanically induced vertical root fracture, VRF; 53 controls) imaged with MRI (4.0 T, custom intraoral coil), CBCT (small FOV, 0.08 mm voxel) and microCT as the reference standard, read by 3 board-certified endodontists.

MRI vs CBCT: sensitivity 0.66 (95% CI 0.53-0.78) vs 0.58 (0.45-0.70; p = 0.27), specificity 0.72 (0.58-0.83) vs 0.87 (0.75-0.95; p = 0.12), AUC 0.74 vs 0.75 (p = 0.88); intra-rater kappa 0.29-0.48 (MRI) and 0.30-0.44 (CBCT), inter-rater kappa 0.37 (MRI) and 0.49 (CBCT).

No significant difference between the modalities, so the hypothesis that MRI would outperform CBCT was not supported; MRI looks comparable to CBCT for small VRF but the study is an extracted-root laboratory model with fair rater agreement, wide confidence intervals and a sample sized for precision, not for a between-modality difference.

## Summary

This ex vivo diagnostic-accuracy study (Groenke et al., J Endod 2023) compared MRI with CBCT for detecting small vertical root fractures in extracted, root-canal-treated human roots, using microCT as the reference standard. Of 120 prepared roots, 115 were analysed (62 with mechanically induced VRF, 53 controls); three board-certified endodontists rated axial MRI and CBCT stacks as VRF yes/no with a 4-point confidence score. MRI and CBCT did not differ significantly in sensitivity (0.66 vs 0.58), specificity (0.72 vs 0.87) or AUC (0.74 vs 0.75), and rater agreement was only fair to moderate for both. The authors conclude that MRI, although early in development, performs in the range of the imaging currently used clinically for small VRF; because the sample was sized for precision rather than for a between-modality difference, and the setting is an extracted-root phantom, this is a feasibility signal, not clinical evidence.

## Key Contributions

- First direct MRI-versus-CBCT comparison for VRF in the wiki, with microCT (not stain plus visual inspection) as the reference.
- Shows CBCT sensitivity of only 0.58 (95% CI 0.45-0.70) for small induced VRF in root-filled roots, i.e. CBCT missed roughly 4 in 10 fractures even in a controlled ex vivo setting.
- Confidence data: "very confident" VRF calls were correct 100% (CBCT) and 80% (MRI) of the time, but raters never gave a consensus "very confident no VRF" for either modality, so ruling VRF out remained uncertain.
- Names the MRI barrier as the voxel-size versus signal-to-noise trade-off (grainy images, probable false positives).

## Methodology

- Design: paired ex vivo diagnostic-accuracy study; sample sized for a narrow CI on MRI sensitivity (60 VRF + 40 controls planned, 20 spare), not powered for MRI-vs-CBCT difference.
- Samples: 120 decoronated, tip-amputated, root-canal-treated human roots; OCT screening excluded fractures induced during treatment; 62 VRF (55 single-canal, 7 two-canal) and 53 controls (44 single-canal, 9 two-canal) analysed.
- Imaging: 4.0 T whole-body MRI with custom intraoral coil (focused with ImageJ), Carestream CS 9000 small-FOV CBCT (70 kV, 8 mA, voxel 0.08 mm), microCT Nikon XT H 225; head-mimicking agar/plastic phantom.
- Raters: 3 board-certified endodontists; calibration to 80% agreement; axial scroll only; consensus = at least 2 of 3 raters.
- Statistics: McNemar (sensitivity/specificity), DeLong (AUC), Cohen's kappa (intra-rater, 30% re-read), Fleiss' kappa (inter-rater).

## Results

| Outcome | MRI | CBCT |
|---|---|---|
| Sensitivity (95% CI) | 0.66 (0.53-0.78) | 0.58 (0.45-0.70); p = 0.27 for difference |
| Specificity (95% CI) | 0.72 (0.58-0.83) | 0.87 (0.75-0.95); p = 0.12 for difference |
| AUC (95% CI) | 0.74 (0.65-0.83) | 0.75 (0.66-0.84); p = 0.88 |
| Intra-rater kappa | 0.29-0.48 | 0.30-0.44 |
| Inter-rater kappa | 0.37 (fair) | 0.49 (moderate) |
| "Very confident" VRF calls correct | 80% | 100% |

The group's earlier study (Schuurmans et al., stain plus visual inspection as reference; not held in the wiki) reported MRI 0.59/0.83 and CBCT 0.59/0.90 (sensitivity/specificity); the authors attribute the present MRI shift (higher sensitivity, lower specificity) to noise in the highest-resolution images being read as tiny fractures.

## Clinical Insights

- Reasoning from held evidence: in a controlled lab setting with microCT as truth, CBCT detected only about 58% of small induced VRFs, in line with the pooled estimate for incomplete fractures in [[cracked-tooth/de-2025-exploring-diagnostic-reliability-of-cbct]] (sensitivity 0.522), so a negative CBCT cannot rule out VRF and clinical signs still have to drive the decision.
- MRI offers radiation-free fracture imaging without the metal/gutta-percha beam-hardening artifact that limits CBCT, but this paper provides no clinical data: it is a research scanner (4.0 T, custom coil) on extracted roots, so it should not change current imaging choices.
