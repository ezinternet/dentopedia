## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (n=258 denosumab-treated osteoporosis patients; 91 patients / 185 dentoalveolar interventions, Rabin Medical Center, Israel) quantifying MRONJ risk by months of delay between the last 60 mg denosumab injection and surgery.

MRONJ occurred in 5.43% (14/258) of patients (7.57% of interventions); intervention delay was independently protective (GEE OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.16–0.77, p=.0089), mean delay was 2.03 mo in MRONJ vs 5.74 mo in non-MRONJ interventions, and zero MRONJ occurred when surgery was ≥4 mo post-injection.

Cumulative antiresorptive duration independently raised risk (OR 1.33, p=.0101) while injection count did not; authors recommend avoiding elective bone-related surgery within the first 4 mo post-denosumab (optimal 4–5 mo), balancing MRONJ avoidance against rebound vertebral fracture risk better than the ECTS 5–6 mo window.

## Summary

Retrospective single-center cohort of 258 osteoporosis patients on denosumab 60 mg q6mo at Rabin Medical Center, Israel, analyzing 185 dentoalveolar interventions in 91 patients to test whether timing relative to the last injection affects MRONJ risk. MRONJ developed in 14 patients (5.43%; 7.57% of interventions), all with prior prolonged bisphosphonate exposure. After multivariable adjustment via generalized estimating equations, longer intervention delay was independently protective (OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.16–0.77, p=.0089), as was shorter cumulative antiresorptive duration (OR 1.33 per year, p=.0101); the number of denosumab injections predicted nothing. No MRONJ occurred when surgery was performed ≥4 months after the last dose, leading the authors to advise against elective bone-related surgery in the first 4 months and to propose an optimal 4–5 month window — deliberately earlier than the ECTS 5–6 month recommendation to limit exposure to rebound-associated vertebral fractures.

## Key Contributions

- Continuous timing variable ("dentoalveolar intervention delay") shown to be an independent protective factor: OR 0.26 univariate (p=.0008), OR 0.35 after GEE adjustment (p=.0089) — a 65% odds reduction
- Empirical zero-event threshold: no MRONJ when surgery ≥4 mo after last denosumab injection → proposed optimal window 4–5 mo
- Timing > cumulative exposure for denosumab: injection count not significant at either analysis level, consistent with denosumab's reversible, non-matrix-bound pharmacology (terminal half-life ~25–30 d; CTX recovers from ~4 mo)
- Sequential therapy risk: 100% of MRONJ cases had prior prolonged BP exposure (mean ARD duration >9 yr), explaining the elevated 5.43% rate vs typical cited figures
- Practical schedule pricing in the rebound dilemma: 4–5 mo surgery keeps the next dose near month 7–7.5 vs month 8–9 under ECTS timing once 6–8 wk mucosal healing is added

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort, tertiary OMFS center (Rabin Medical Center—Beilinson Hospital, Israel)
- **Levels**: Patient level (n=258, logistic regression) + intervention level (91 patients, 185 procedures, generalized estimating equations)
- **Cohort**: Mean age 71.35 ± 8.26 yr; 91.47% female; mean 6.33 ± 5.05 injections; ARD duration 9.16 ± 5.46 yr; HT 44.57%, hyperlipidemia 35.66%, DM 20.54%
- **Exposure**: Months from last denosumab dose to dentoalveolar surgery (extractions, bone grafts, implant-related procedures)
- **Statistics**: Univariable p<.1 screen → multicollinearity check → multivariable model; p<.05 significance

All 14 MRONJ patients had prior prolonged bisphosphonate exposure (100%) — sequential antiresorptive therapy framed as the dominant susceptibility background.
