## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42022327645; PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane, Embase, 2013 to June 2023): 765 records screened, 10 observational studies (6 case-control, 3 cohort, 1 retrospective) with 272 MRONJ cases, mean age about 66 years, asking whether medication-induced salivary changes relate to MRONJ.

Reduced salivary flow was significantly associated with MRONJ in 3 of the 4 studies that measured flow (Liao p=0.017, Kuo p=0.0024, Stockmann p=0.039; Margaix-Muñoz resting and stimulated saliva p>0.05), and single studies reported higher salivary IL-6, IL-1 family cytokines, MMP9, hypotaurine and RBMS3 in MRONJ; meta-analysis was not possible because of heterogeneity.

Evidence is hypothesis-generating only: small heterogeneous case-control and cohort studies, no standardized saliva sampling, chemotherapy imbalance between groups, and no validated salivary biomarker; the authors recommend saliva sampling before and after MRONJ onset.

## Summary

This PRISMA-guided systematic review (Front Med 2023; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2023.1164051), text retrieved from PubMed/PMC) asked whether medications that alter saliva contribute to medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw. Ten observational studies (272 MRONJ cases) were included after screening 765 records; no meta-analysis was possible. Decreased salivary flow, often in the setting of Sjögren's syndrome, polypharmacy or diabetes, and altered salivary proteins (IL-6, IL-1α/β, IL-1RA, MIP-1β, TNF-α, MMP9, hypotaurine, RBMS3) were associated with MRONJ, but each biomarker rests on one or two small studies and the direction of causality is unknown. The authors themselves advise cautious interpretation.

## Key Contributions

- Frames saliva as a candidate pathogenic and diagnostic axis for MRONJ, distinct from the local-infection, surgical-trauma and systemic-comorbidity risk factors usually discussed.
- Catalogues the salivary changes reported in MRONJ: flow reduction (Liao p=0.017, Kuo p=0.0024, Stockmann p=0.039), cytokines, MMP9, hypotaurine, RBMS3.
- Identifies the evidence gap: no prospective before-and-after saliva sampling and no validated salivary biomarker for early MRONJ detection.

## Methodology

PROSPERO CRD42022327645; PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane, Embase (January 2013 to June 2023) plus grey literature; cancer and osteoporosis patients on saliva-altering drugs, outcome MRONJ; patients with prior radiotherapy, jaw metastasis or head and neck cancer surgery excluded. Two independent reviewers, third expert for disagreements. Risk of bias with the modified Newcastle-Ottawa Scale. Qualitative synthesis only.

## Results

- 765 records to 10 studies: case-control 6, cohort 3, retrospective 1; 272 MRONJ cases (35% women, 32% men, 32% gender not reported), mean age 65.6 years (33-81).
- Primary disease: breast cancer 57, multiple myeloma 55, prostate cancer 19, renal cancer 8, other cancer 58, osteoporosis 19. Comorbidity: hypertension 107, diabetes 30. Medication counts in Table 1: bisphosphonate 530, denosumab 4, corticosteroids 11, chemotherapy 11 (counts exceed case number in the paper's table).
- Salivary flow: decreased in Liao (p=0.017), Kuo (p=0.0024), Stockmann (p=0.039); not significant in Margaix-Muñoz (resting and stimulated whole saliva, p>0.05).
- Salivary proteins and other markers: Badros MIP-1β up (p=0.01), IL-6 up (p=0.02), TNF-α up (p=0.09); Bagan IL-6 up (p<0.01) and IL-1α, IL-1β, IL-1RA up (p<0.05); Lorenzo-Pouso MMP9 up (p<0.05); Yatsuoka hypotaurine up (p=0.017); Nicoletti RBMS3 associated.
- Discussion-level claims (not pooled results): polypharmacy of five or more drugs had the highest xerostomia and MRONJ prevalence (71%); Bagan et al. reported an odds ratio of 1.01 for MRONJ with high salivary IL-6; on drug holidays the authors note conflicting studies and no detailed guidelines.
- Quality: modified NOS, overall good (78%) and fair (22%); the text also mentions one poor-rated study, an inconsistency within the paper.
- Limitations: few, small, heterogeneous studies; nonstandard saliva collection; more MRONJ than non-MRONJ patients on chemotherapy at sampling; conclusion to be interpreted with caution.
