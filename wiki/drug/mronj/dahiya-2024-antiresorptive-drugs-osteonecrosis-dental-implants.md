## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (10 systematic reviews, 7 without and 3 with meta-analysis, 2014-2024, PROSPERO CRD42024583017) synthesizing evidence on antiresorptive drug (ARD: bisphosphonates, denosumab) therapy and MRONJ in dental implant patients.

Across the 10 reviews, total failed implants = 524 with MRONJ accounting for 2-17% of failures; IV bisphosphonate for ≥45-60 months carried the highest risk, while 3 meta-analyses on implant failure/loss found no statistically significant pooled effect (heterogeneity I² up to 96.87%).

AMSTAR 2 appraisal rated only 20% of included reviews as moderate quality (60% low, 20% critically low, none high) — the evidence base supports individualized risk assessment (route, duration, comorbidities) rather than a single pooled quantitative risk estimate.

## Summary

This umbrella review (Dahiya et al. 2024, Cureus) is the first overview of systematic reviews specifically addressing whether antiresorptive drug (ARD) therapy — bisphosphonates or denosumab — significantly manifests MRONJ in patients receiving dental implants. Following PRISMA methodology and registered on PROSPERO (CRD42024583017), the authors searched four databases (Google Scholar, Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed/MEDLINE) from January 2014 to July 2024, screening 950 records down to 10 eligible systematic reviews (7 without meta-analysis, 3 with meta-analysis) spanning 171 primary studies in total. All 10 reviews were appraised with AMSTAR 2. Across the included reviews, 524 implants failed overall, with MRONJ responsible for 2-17% of failures depending on the review. IV-route bisphosphonate use, particularly for ≥45-60 months, was consistently the dominant risk driver, while oral-route ARD and drug holidays (cessation ≥12-132 months before implant placement) were associated with lower MRONJ incidence (4-14%). Only 2 of 10 reviews reached AMSTAR 2 moderate quality; none reached high quality, underscoring that the pooled meta-analytic estimates (heterogeneity I² up to 96.87%) should not be over-interpreted, and that individualized clinical risk assessment remains the primary decision tool.

## Key Contributions

- First umbrella review synthesizing SR/MA-level evidence specifically on ARD therapy and MRONJ in dental implant patients (PROSPERO CRD42024583017).
- Quantifies cross-review MRONJ burden: 524 total failed implants across 10 reviews, MRONJ accounting for 2-17% of failures.
- Cross-review AMSTAR 2 quality map: only 20% moderate quality, 60% low, 20% critically low, 0% high — a methodological-quality layer not available from any single included SR.
- Confirms IV bisphosphonate route and long treatment duration (≥45-60 months) as the dominant MRONJ risk factor, and drug holiday (ARD cessation ≥12-132 months pre-op) as risk-reducing.
- Identifies posterior mandible as the predominant MRONJ site and comorbidities (smoking, diabetes, hypertension, corticosteroid therapy, anti-cancer therapy, ill-fitting dentures, peri-implantitis) as trigger factors across the pooled evidence.
- Reports that meta-analytic pooling of "implant failure/loss" across 3 reviews was statistically uninterpretable due to high heterogeneity (I² up to 96.87%), cautioning against citing a single pooled risk ratio for this question.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Umbrella review (overview of systematic reviews), PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42024583017).
- **Focused question / PICOS**: P = dental implant patients; I = ARD history before/during/after implant placement; C = no ARD history; O = MRONJ; S = SR with or without meta-analysis.
- **Search**: Google Scholar, Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed/MEDLINE; MeSH-based Boolean strings; January 2014-May 2024 (English, human).
- **Screening**: Two independent reviewers (title/abstract, then full text), third reviewer for conflicts, fourth reviewer for further disputes.
- **Eligibility**: SR/MA with MRONJ as an explicit outcome in implant patients with ARD history; excluded non-English, in vitro/animal, primary research, narrative/scoping reviews, expert opinion. 9 near-miss studies explicitly excluded with stated reasons (e.g., no MRONJ outcome, peri-implantitis-induced MRONJ only, oral-route-only comparison).
- **Quality appraisal**: AMSTAR 2 (16 items), 3 independent examiners + 4th for disagreement, reviews graded high/moderate/low/critically low.
- **Yield**: 950 records → 10 included SR/MA (7 without MA, 3 with MA).

## Results

### Cross-review burden
- Total failed implants across 10 reviews: **524**; MRONJ share of failures: **2-17%** (review-dependent).
- Implants analyzed per review: 38.5-3583 (mean 1830); primary studies per review: 7-36.
- 6 reviews assessed ARD taken before implant placement; 1 assessed ARD taken after placement; none assessed ARD taken during placement.

### Meta-analytic pooling (3 of 10 reviews performed MA)
| Review | Event | p-value | RR | 95% CI | Heterogeneity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stavropoulos 2018 | Implant loss | <0.01 | 2.7 | 0.00-0.007 | I²=36.9% |
| Ata-Ali 2016 | Implant failure rate | 0.156 | 2.18 | 0.01-51.21 | OR=1.43 |
| Chappuis 2018 | Implant failure rate | <0.01 | 2.02 | 2.67-6.39 | I²=96.87% |

All three were judged statistically insignificant/uninterpretable given heterogeneity — no reliable pooled effect size for "implant failure due to ARD" currently exists.

### AMSTAR 2 quality distribution (10 reviews)
- Moderate: 20% (Ata-Ali 2016, Chappuis 2018)
- Low: 60% (Sher 2021, Rebelo 2023, Gelazius 2018, Anitua 2024, Papadakis 2021, de-Freitas 2016)
- Critically low: 20% (Stavropoulos 2018, Li and Leung 2024)
- High: 0%
- Score range 4-11.5/16 (mean ≈ 6).

### MRONJ incidence by review (% of failed implants)
Sher 2021 = 3%; Stavropoulos 2018 = 2%; Rebelo 2023 = 4%; Li and Leung 2024 = 17%; Papadakis 2021 = 0.4%; Anitua 2024 = 4%; Gelazius 2018 = 4%; de-Freitas 2016 = 8%; Ata-Ali 2016 = 0.5%; Chappuis 2018 = 4%.

### Risk modifiers
- **Route**: IV bisphosphonate >> oral/SC for MRONJ risk (e.g., 100% implant loss with IV cancer-dose ARD in de-Freitas 2016; 100% failure with 72-month IV bisphosphonate in Gelazius 2018; 100% survival with 20.5-month oral bisphosphonate in Papadakis 2021).
- **Duration/timing**: ARD cessation ≥12-132 months before implant placement associated with lower MRONJ (4-14%) in 6 reviews; ARD initiated 8-72 months after implant placement associated with 100% implant success in 2 reviews.
- **Indication**: cancer patients more MRONJ-prone than benign-disease (osteoporosis) patients.
- **Site**: posterior mandible most common, followed by posterior maxilla.
- **Triggers/comorbidities**: hypertension, ischemic heart disease, anemia, dementia, renal failure, diabetes, smoking, chemotherapy, corticosteroid use, ill-fitting dentures, peri-implantitis.
- **Mitigation**: antibiotic prophylaxis (one meta-analysis cited 66.9% reduction in implant failure with antibiotics), strict oral hygiene/chlorhexidine, drug holiday.

## Clinical Implications

- No single reliable pooled risk ratio exists for "ARD → implant failure via MRONJ" — the 3 available meta-analyses are heterogeneous and statistically uninterpretable; clinicians should not cite a specific pooled RR/OR from this literature.
- IV-route ARD, especially for oncologic indications and ≥45-60 month duration, remains the highest-risk scenario for implant-associated MRONJ; oral-route ARD for osteoporosis carries substantially lower risk.
- A drug holiday (ARD cessation ≥12 months pre-op where feasible) and antibiotic prophylaxis are supported mitigation strategies across the pooled evidence, though the underlying SR quality is mostly low-to-critically-low (AMSTAR 2).
- Peri-implantitis and ill-fitting removable prostheses are practical, modifiable trigger factors that should prompt closer MRONJ surveillance in ARD-treated implant patients.
- Because only 2/10 underlying reviews reached AMSTAR 2 moderate quality, this umbrella review should be read as a risk-factor/quality map rather than a definitive quantitative verdict — individualized risk assessment (route, duration, comorbidities) remains primary.
