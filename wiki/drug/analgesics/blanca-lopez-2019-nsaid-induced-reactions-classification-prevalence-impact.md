## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (J Asthma Allergy 2019;12:217-233) from the Spanish RIRAAF/Málaga group synthesizing the classification, prevalence, impact, and management of hypersensitivity drug reactions to NSAIDs (HDR-NSAID).

NSAIDs are now the leading cause of hypersensitivity drug reactions (past beta-lactams); the ENDA/EAACI scheme divides reactions into cross-intolerant (CI: NERD, NECD, NIUA — COX-1-mediated, multi-drug) and selective responders (SR: IgE-mediated SNIUAA, T-cell SNIDR — single drug/group, ASA-tolerant), CI:SR ≈ 3:1; in a >4,000-case series 47.2% were confirmed multiple-NSAID hypersensitivity vs 18% antibiotics, and NERD prevalence is ~1.9% general population (GA2LEN) rising to ~9% in confirmed asthma cohorts.

There is no validated in vitro/in vivo test for CI (aspirin drug provocation is the only diagnostic option), and management is incomplete without a proven safe alternative — up to ~19% of cross-reactive patients react to paracetamol and ~10% to selective COX-2 inhibitors, so neither can be assumed safe.

## Summary

This narrative review from the group that established NSAID-induced urticaria/angioedema (NIUA) as the most prevalent NSAID-hypersensitivity entity lays out the modern mental model for reading a reported "painkiller allergy." The central fork is **cross-intolerant (CI)** vs **selective responder (SR)**:

- **CI** reactions are non-allergic, driven by cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1) inhibition and PGE2 depletion with shunting to the lipoxygenase pathway. The patient reacts to several chemically unrelated **strong COX-1 inhibitors**. Three entities: **NERD** (respiratory — rhinosinusitis, nasal polyposis, asthma; the classic "aspirin triad"), **NECD** (cutaneous — NSAID aggravation of underlying chronic spontaneous urticaria), and **NIUA/NIUAA** (urticaria/angioedema ± anaphylaxis in otherwise healthy people).
- **SR** reactions are immunologically specific: the patient reacts to **one drug or one chemical group** and tolerates aspirin (ASA) and other NSAIDs. **SNIUAA** is immediate/IgE-mediated; **SNIDR** is T-cell mediated and delayed (24–48 h), spanning non-immediate urticaria and fixed drug eruption through to SJS-TEN, AGEP and DRESS.

In the authors' and other populations the **CI:SR ratio is about 3:1**, and children are more often CI. Culprit drugs have shifted: **ibuprofen is the "universal culprit"** while aspirin's role has declined with its falling cardiovascular use; selective COX-2 inhibitors are an increasingly reported cause.

**Epidemiology**: NSAIDs have overtaken beta-lactam antibiotics as the single most common cause of hypersensitivity drug reactions. In a consecutive >4,000-case, 6-year, DPT-confirmed drug-hypersensitivity series, 47.2% were multiple-NSAID hypersensitivity vs 18% antibiotics. NERD population prevalence is ~1.9% (GA2LEN, >60,000 subjects, 15 countries; range 0.9–4.9%), rising to ~9% in asthma cohorts confirmed by oral provocation.

**Diagnosis**: there is no validated in vitro or in vivo test for CI. **Drug provocation testing (DPT) with aspirin is the only diagnostic route** unless there are repeated, consistent episodes with two or more chemically unrelated NSAIDs. Skin/patch testing is useful mainly for pyrazolones (metamizole; sensitivity ≤50%, ≤60% combined with basophil activation) and for delayed T-cell reactions.

**Management**: a diagnosis is not complete until a **safe alternative is documented**. The safety of **paracetamol** and of **COX-2 inhibitors must not be taken for granted** — paracetamol >500 mg can worsen respiratory symptoms and up to ~19% of cross-reactive patients react to it; ~10% react to selective COX-2 inhibitors. For SR patients (e.g. selective ibuprofen or naproxen reactors), other NSAIDs including ASA are usually tolerated. **ASA desensitization** is an option for NERD. The EAACI **"drug allergy passport"** (written, native language + English) is recommended.

**Dental relevance**: this is the framework for interpreting a patient-reported analgesic allergy before choosing a postoperative regimen — CI implies avoiding all strong COX-1 inhibitors and actively testing/choosing an alternative, whereas SR usually leaves the rest of the NSAID class (and ASA) available. It also flags that acetaminophen/COX-2, the reflex fallbacks, are usually but not automatically safe.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates the ENDA/EAACI 5-group classification with the **CI vs SR** split as the primary clinical decision point, and defines each entity (NERD, NECD, NIUA/NIUAA, SNIUAA, SNIDR + a new Type IVe subgroup).
- States and quantifies the epidemiological shift: **NSAIDs > antibiotics as the #1 cause of hypersensitivity drug reactions** (47.2% vs 18% in a >4,000-case DPT-confirmed series).
- Identifies **NIUA as the most prevalent** NSAID-hypersensitivity entity (the group's own contribution to the field).
- Names **ibuprofen as the universal culprit** and documents aspirin's declining role.
- Establishes that **no validated CI test exists** — DPT with ASA is the reference standard.
- Gives the practical alternative-drug risk figures (paracetamol ~19%, selective COX-2 ~10%, piroxicam ~20%) and the caution against assuming paracetamol/COX-2 safety.
- Endorses the EAACI **drug allergy passport** for cross-country portability.

## Methodology

Narrative review / expert perspective. No systematic search, no PRISMA flow, no risk-of-bias or GRADE appraisal. Built on ENDA/EAACI position papers, the RIRAAF group's own primary studies, and collaborators' cohorts. Prevalence figures are cited from named primary studies (GA2LEN multinational survey; a >4,000-case single-series; a systematic review of NERD) rather than pooled by the authors. Source ingested as PMC JATS full text; tables and figures were not captured in extraction, so entity tables and the NSAID chemical-class list are summarized from running text.
