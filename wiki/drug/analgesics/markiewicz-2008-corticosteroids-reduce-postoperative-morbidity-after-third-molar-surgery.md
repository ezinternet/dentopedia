## Three-line Summary
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 12 RCTs testing perioperative corticosteroids (CS, yes/no) on edema, trismus, and pain after third molar (M3) removal, assessed at early (1–3 days) and late (>3 days) postoperative periods.

CS significantly reduced edema (early SMD 1.4, 95% CI 0.6–2.2, P<.001; late SMD 1.1, 95% CI 0.1–2.0, P=.03) and trismus (early WMD 4.1 mm, 95% CI 2.8–5.5 mm, P<.001; late WMD 2.7 mm, 95% CI 0.8–4.6 mm, P=.005), but pain was not significantly different between groups at either timepoint (early WMD 0.4 VAS, P=.07; late WMD 0.5 VAS, P=.4).

Perioperative CS gives a mild-to-moderate reduction in edema and better range of motion after M3 surgery, but the pooled evidence does not support a significant pain benefit — this abstract-only source limits verification of heterogeneity/study-level detail.

## Summary
This 2008 systematic review and meta-analysis (Markiewicz, Brady, Ding & Dodson, J Oral Maxillofac Surg) pooled 12 RCTs to test whether perioperative corticosteroid (CS) administration reduces edema, trismus, and pain after third molar (M3) removal, evaluated separately at early (1–3 days) and late (>3 days) postoperative periods using random-effects models and metaregression. CS produced statistically significant reductions in edema at both timepoints and significant improvement in trismus (mouth opening) at both timepoints, but average pain scores did not differ significantly between CS and control groups at either timepoint. The authors conclude that perioperative CS gives a mild-to-moderate reduction in edema and improved range of motion after M3 removal — notably stopping short of claiming a pain benefit. This is one of the earliest and most-cited quantitative syntheses in this space, though the copy held here is an abstract-only publisher landing page (no PMC full text exists for this article), so per-study detail and heterogeneity statistics are not verifiable from this source.

## Key Contributions
- Demonstrates that CS effect on third-molar postoperative morbidity is outcome-specific, not uniform: strong, significant effect on edema and trismus; no significant average effect on pain.
- Separates early (1–3d) vs late (>3d) postoperative windows for all three outcomes — showing the CS edema/trismus benefit persists (not just an early transient effect) into the late window.
- Uses random-effects pooling + metaregression appropriate to expected between-trial heterogeneity (different CS drugs, doses, routes, timing across the 12 trials).
- One of the highest evidence-tier (SR+MA), most-cited papers underpinning the widespread clinical practice of perioperative corticosteroid use before third-molar surgery.

## Methodology
Systematic review + meta-analysis. Predictor variable: perioperative CS exposure (yes/no). Outcomes: edema (pooled as SMD), trismus/mouth-opening and pain (pooled as WMD, pain via VAS) — each at early (1–3d) and late (>3d) postoperative periods. Differences between CS and control groups assessed via random-effects models and metaregressions. 12 trials met inclusion criteria; databases searched, search dates, and specific trial identities are not stated in the retrieved abstract (full text not available — see caveat above).
