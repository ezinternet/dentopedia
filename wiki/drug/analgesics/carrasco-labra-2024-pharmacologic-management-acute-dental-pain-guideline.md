## Three-line Summary

ADA clinical practice guideline (JADA 2024;155(2):102-117) covering two scenarios — postoperative pain after simple and surgical tooth extraction(s), and temporary management of toothache when definitive dental treatment is not immediately available — in adolescents (12–<17 y), adults (17–<65 y), and older adults (≥65 y).

Nonopioid first-line everywhere: NSAID alone (ibuprofen 400 mg or naproxen sodium 440 mg) or + acetaminophen 500 mg; opioid-containing steps (APAP 325 mg + APAP-opioid combination, e.g., hydrocodone 5–7.5 mg or oxycodone 5 mg; lowest dose, fewest tablets, rarely >3 days) only if inadequate or NSAID-contraindicated; opioids recommended AGAINST after simple extraction; corticosteroids recommended AGAINST as analgesic add-on.

All recommendations conditional at low/very low certainty — the clinical bottom line rests on net benefit balance (NSAID efficacy plus serious opioid-specific harms), so pair with shared decision making, minimum effective dosage, and no just-in-case opioid prescriptions.

## 세줄요약

ADA 임상진료지침 (JADA 2024;155(2):102-117): 단순·외과적 발치 후 통증과, 확정치과치료가 즉시 불가할 때의 치통 잠정관리 두 시나리오를 소아청소년(12–<17세)·성인(17–<65세)·고령자(≥65세) 대상으로 다룸.

전 시나리오 비오피이드(NSAID±아세트아미노펜) 1차요법: NSAID 단독(이부프로펜 400 mg 또는 나프록센나트륨 440 mg) 또는 +아세트아미노펜 500 mg; 오피이드 포함 단계(APAP 325 mg+APAP-오피이드 복합제, 예: 하이드로코돈 5–7.5 mg·옥시코돈 5 mg, 최저용량·최소 정제·3일 이내)는 무효 시 또는 NSAID 금기 시에만; 단순 발치 후 오피이드는 반대 권고, 코르티코스테로이드 진통 보조 추가도 반대 권고.

모든 권고가 조건부·확실성 낮음~매우 낮음 — 결론의 무게중심은 NSAID 유효성+오피이드 고유 중대 위해의 순이익에 있으므로, 공유 의사결정·최소유효용량 원칙·just-in-case 오피이드 처방 금지를 함께 적용해야 한다.

## Summary

An ADA Science and Research Institute / University of Pittsburgh / University of Pennsylvania panel — funded by an FDA grant under Congressional mandate after the 2020 National Academies report — formulated evidence-based recommendations for acute dental pain in adolescents (12–<17 years), adults (17–<65 years), and older adults (≥65 years), across two scenarios: **(A)** postoperative pain after simple and surgical tooth extraction(s) including impacted mandibular third molars, and **(B)** temporary management of toothache (symptomatic reversible or symptomatic irreversible pulpitis ± symptomatic apical periodontitis, or pulp necrosis with symptomatic apical periodontitis, or acute apical abscess) when definitive dental treatment (pulpectomy, nonsurgical root canal treatment, incision & drainage of abscess, extraction) is not immediately available.

The central conclusion: **nonopioid medications are first-line therapy** in both scenarios. NSAIDs target the inflammatory source of dental pain whereas opioids do not; NSAIDs alone or with acetaminophen provide superior pain relief with a more favorable safety profile than opioids. Opioids are reserved for inadequate first-line response or NSAID contraindication, at the lowest effective dose, fewest tablets, shortest duration rarely exceeding 3 days. The panel recommends against routine delayed ("just-in-case") opioid prescriptions and urges extreme caution prescribing opioids to adolescents and young adults, who face increased risk of subsequent misuse and substance use disorder even after a single prescription.

## Key Contributions

- First systematically developed ADA CPG for pharmacologic acute dental pain management, built on 4 commissioned systematic reviews (opioid/nonopioid analgesics, local anesthetics, corticosteroids, topical anesthetics) with GRADE certainty assessment and the GRADE Evidence-to-Decision framework
- Complete stepwise, opioid-sparing analgesic ladder for both scenarios (initiate → add → replace → contraindicated path) with explicit numeric guardrails
- Recommends AGAINST opioids entirely after simple extraction (conditional, low certainty)
- Recommends AGAINST adding oral/submucosal/intramuscular corticosteroids to standard analgesic therapy for post-extraction pain (conditional, very low certainty)
- Legitimizes supplemental long-acting local anesthesia at discharge and short-term topical benzocaine as adjuncts
- 7 good practice statements operationalizing safe prescribing (PDMP review, storage/disposal counseling, informed consent, naloxone discussion)

## Methodology

- Panel of 14 members (clinicians, researchers, epidemiologists, pharmacologists, public health dentists, patient representative) + methodological team of 16; remote meetings December 2020–December 2021; consensus-first decision making, simple-majority vote only when consensus failed
- Evidence base: 4 systematic reviews/meta-analyses; MEDLINE via PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Central, gray literature through September 30, 2021; RCTs prioritized for beneficial outcomes, observational data admitted for long-term opioid harms; Cochrane RoB 2.0 / ROBINS-I; GRADE summary-of-findings tables
- Where direct evidence for temporary toothache management was lacking (none found for analgesics or local anesthetics), recommendations were informed by **indirect evidence** from RCTs on post-extraction pain — stated explicitly rather than hidden
- Patient values from a primary values-and-preferences study: most people place high value on avoiding serious opioid harms even with severe pain; a smaller group accepts increased risk to manage severe pain; patients value agency and personalized pain-management discussion
- Guidance types: (1) graded recommendations, (2) good practice statements backed by extensive indirect evidence, (3) panel remarks; stakeholder engagement (31 organizations, twice) plus public comment period

## Results

### Scenario A — Postoperative pain after simple and surgical tooth extraction(s) (Box 1)

| # | Recommendation | Strength, certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Rec 1 | Surgical extraction(s): postprocedural **nonopioid analgesics first-line instead of opioids** | Conditional, low |
| 1.1 | Initiate NSAID alone (eg, ibuprofen 400 mg or naproxen sodium 440 mg) OR NSAID + acetaminophen (eg, 500 mg) | Conditional, low |
| 1.2 | If NSAID-alone inadequate: ADD 1 tablet acetaminophen 325 mg + 1 tablet acetaminophen-opioid combination (325 mg APAP + eg, hydrocodone 5–7.5 mg or oxycodone 5 mg) — lowest effective dose, fewest tablets, shortest duration rarely exceeding 3 days | Conditional, low |
| 1.3 | If NSAID + acetaminophen (500 mg) inadequate: REPLACE initial prescription with NSAID (ibuprofen 400 mg or naproxen sodium 440 mg) + APAP 325 mg + APAP-opioid combination, same dose/duration limits | Conditional, low |
| 1.4 | NSAIDs contraindicated: acetaminophen alone at full therapeutic dose (eg, 1,000 mg) OR APAP 325 mg + APAP-opioid combination scheme | Conditional, low |
| 1.5 | Suggests **AGAINST adding oral, submucosal, or intramuscular corticosteroids** to standard analgesic therapy | Conditional, very low |
| Rec 2 | Simple extraction(s): nonopioid analgesics ONLY; recommends **AGAINST opioid use** | Conditional, low |
| 2.1 | Simple extraction first line: same options as 1.1 | Conditional, low |
| 2.2 | NSAIDs contraindicated (simple extraction): acetaminophen alone at full therapeutic dose (eg, 1,000 mg) | Conditional, low |
| Rec 3 | Simple + surgical extraction(s): postprocedural supplemental long-acting LA before discharge — 0.5% bupivacaine + 1:200,000 epinephrine by block or infiltration injection OR 4% articaine + 1:100,000/1:200,000 epinephrine by infiltration injection, instead of 2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine or 3% mepivacaine | Conditional, low |

### Scenario B — Temporary management of toothache before definitive dental treatment (Box 2)

| # | Recommendation | Strength, certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Rec 1 | Short-acting LA in-visit for immediate relief (2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine or 4% articaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine) | Conditional, very low |
| Rec 2 | Postvisit **nonopioid analgesics first-line instead of opioids** | Conditional, low |
| 2.1 | Initiate NSAID alone (ibuprofen 400 mg or naproxen sodium 440 mg) OR + acetaminophen (eg, 500 mg) | Conditional, low |
| 2.2 | NSAID-alone inadequate → ADD APAP 325 mg + APAP-opioid combination (hydrocodone 5–7.5 mg or oxycodone 5 mg), lowest dose/fewest tablets/rarely >3 days | Conditional, low |
| 2.3 | NSAID + APAP inadequate → REPLACE initial prescription with NSAID + APAP 325 mg + APAP-opioid combination | Conditional, low |
| 2.4 | NSAIDs contraindicated → acetaminophen alone (1,000 mg) OR APAP 325 mg + APAP-opioid combination scheme | Conditional, low |
| Rec 3 | Extended temporary management: supplemental 0.5% bupivacaine + 1:200,000 epinephrine by block or infiltration OR 4% articaine + 1:100,000/1:200,000 epinephrine infiltration | Conditional, very low |
| Rec 4 | Short-term temporary management: topical benzocaine 10% or 20% vs no topical benzocaine | Conditional, low |

### Good practice statements (shared core of both boxes)

- Counsel patients to expect some pain; analgesics should make pain manageable; discuss past experience, preferences, and values before prescribing
- Thoroughly review medical and social history (including illicit/recreational drug use), medications, supplements to avoid overdose and drug-drug interactions
- Minimum effective dosage principle; avoid routine delayed (just-in-case) opioid prescriptions for breakthrough pain
- If opioids prescribed after NSAID ± acetaminophen failure, counsel on appropriate storage and disposal
- Review state's prescription drug monitoring program (PDMP) for coprescribed controlled substances; patients already on chronic opioids → prioritize nonopioid analgesics
- Special care with substance use disorder patients, including communication with their other health care providers

### Numeric guardrails and safety footnotes

- Maximum daily doses: ibuprofen 2,400 mg · naproxen sodium 1,100 mg · acetaminophen 4,000 mg (cumulative APAP ≤4 g/day; warn that combination pills contain both drugs)
- Opioid-containing options NOT offered to patients taking gabapentinoids or CNS-active medications (benzodiazepines, antidepressants, anticonvulsants, narcotics) or already taking opioids
- When opioids prescribed: informed consent (patient or parent/guardian) covering physiological dependence, substance misuse risk, respiratory depression, driving/operating-machinery effects — particularly critical in adolescents and young adults (extreme caution; misuse/SUD risk even after a single prescription; caregiver consent advised at high misuse risk)
- Benzocaine evidence detail: 10% (2 RCTs) or 20% (3 RCTs) increases the proportion with substantial pain reduction across ≥2 consecutive time points (20–30 minutes) vs placebo vehicle (low certainty); 20% vs 10% probably negligible additional benefit (moderate certainty); apply directly to affected tooth and surrounding gingiva

### General remarks

- FDA 2020 Drug Safety Communication: discuss naloxone availability with all patients prescribed opioids; consider prescribing for increased-overdose-risk patients (benzodiazepines/CNS depressants, OUD history, prior overdose, household members at accidental-ingestion risk)
- FDA 2018 communications: tramadol contraindicated <18 years after tonsillectomy/adenoidectomy; codeine/tramadol warning for ages 12–18 with obesity, obstructive sleep apnea, or severe lung disease; breastfeeding not recommended with codeine/tramadol (infant serious adverse reactions incl. death)
- Monitor total local anesthetic dose carefully, especially when LA is given at visit start as a post-discharge pain strategy
- Discharge-time LA block/infiltration is complementary — it does not replace analgesic pain management
- Out of scope: IV corticosteroids; corticosteroids for inflammatory complications (trismus, facial swelling, infection)
- Breakthrough pain persisting day 2–3 after extraction(s): return to clinic to rule out other causes (eg, alveolar osteitis, sharp alveolar ridge) before any new prescription, especially an opioid
