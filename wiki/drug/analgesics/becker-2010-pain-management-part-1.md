## Three-line Summary

- Narrative continuing-education review (single author, Anesthesia Progress 57(2):67-79, 2010; no systematic search) of nonopioid and opioid analgesic pharmacology for acute and postoperative dental pain.
- Claims NSAIDs are generally superior to conventional-dose opioids for dental pain; analgesic ceilings at ibuprofen 400 mg and aspirin/acetaminophen 1000 mg; equianalgesic oral doses codeine 200 mg = hydrocodone 30 mg = oxycodone 20 mg = morphine 30 mg PO (vs 10 mg IM); oxycodone 5 mg alone no better than placebo after third molar surgery while ibuprofen 400 mg clearly relieved pain.
- Stepped clinical doctrine: around-the-clock ibuprofen 400-800 mg and/or acetaminophen 500-1000 mg as Step 1, opioid titrated as Step 2 addition only for breakthrough pain; limitation — narrative expert synthesis from 2010 predating guideline-era network meta-analyses.

## 세줄요약

- 단일 저자의 서술형 문헌고찰(narrative review, Anesthesia Progress 57권 2호, 2010, 체계적 검색 없음)로 급성·술후 치과 통증의 진통제 약리학을 비오피이드(nonopioid)·오피이드(opioid) 계열별로 종합했다.

- 임상 적용: 시계 기반 정기투여 이부프로펜 400-800 mg 및/또는 아세트아미노펜 500-1000 mg을 1단계로 하고, 돌발통에만 오피이드를 2단계 추가; 한계 — 2010년 전문가 서술 종합으로 계통검색·메타분석 이전의 근거 수준.

## Summary

Becker's Part 1 review organizes dental analgesia around one mechanism-based distinction: opioids activate mu/kappa receptors with unlimited dose-dependent efficacy, while nonopioids interrupt prostaglandin synthesis and hit an analgesic ceiling. Because dental pain is musculoskeletal and usually inflammatory, the review argues NSAIDs are the most rational first-line agents — often superior to conventional opioid doses — with acetaminophen as the alternative when NSAIDs are contraindicated, and the combination more effective than either alone. Opioid pharmacology is then worked through receptor by receptor (mu: analgesia, respiratory depression, dependence, unlimited response; kappa: ceiling-limited efficacy, dysphoria), through dependence/tolerance/addiction distinctions, and agent by agent (codeine's CYP2D6 prodrug behavior, hydrocodone vs oxycodone metabolism, meperidine's normeperidine hazard, propoxyphene, pentazocine, tramadol). The practical output is a stepped algorithm: optimize around-the-clock nonopioids first, add a titrated opioid only for breakthrough pain, avoid fixed opioid-acetaminophen combinations when acetaminophen is already prescribed (4 g/day cap), and never add opioids for patients on physician-managed chronic opioid therapy.

## Key Contributions

- Mechanism-first classification: both classes act centrally and peripherally; the distinguishing feature is receptor activation vs prostaglandin synthesis interruption.
- Analgesic ceiling quantification: ibuprofen 400 mg; aspirin and acetaminophen 1000 mg; lower NSAID doses confer analgesia while higher ranges add anti-inflammatory benefit — relevant because most dental pain has an inflammatory component.
- Equianalgesic dosing table defending oxycodone 20 mg PO (not the traditional 30 mg) as equipotent with morphine 10 mg IM, with trial-level support extrapolated from codeine-comparison studies.
- CYP2D6 cascade: codeine ~10% demethylated to morphine (entire analgesic effect); 5-10% poor metabolizers and 1-7% ultra-rapid metabolizers among Caucasians; hydrocodone effect shared between parent and hydromorphone; oxycodone acts almost entirely as parent drug — preferable under CYP2D6 inhibition (SSRIs such as fluoxetine and paroxetine).
- Safety pharmacology for prescribing: GI toxicity (dyspepsia does not correlate with mucosal injury), irreversible antiplatelet effect unique to aspirin (platelet lifespan 10-14 days), bleeding-risk multiples with clopidogrel (twofold-threefold) and warfarin (fourfold-fivefold), renal contraindications, leukotriene-shunt pseudoallergy, aspirin-ibuprofen competition with the 1-2 hour separation workaround.
- Preemptive analgesia rationale: inhibit prostaglandin formation before local anesthesia wanes to prevent central wind-up.
- Stepped regimen table (Table 4, adapted from Becker and Phero 2005) stratified by anticipated pain intensity.

## Results

- Class claim: studies over three decades repeatedly found NSAIDs generally superior to opioids at conventional doses for musculoskeletal (including dental) pain.
- Figure 4 benchmark (third molar impaction patients): oxycodone 5 mg alone no more effective than placebo; ibuprofen 400 mg provided substantially greater relief; adding oxycodone 5 mg to ibuprofen improved relief further — cited as evidence of opioid-nonopioid synergy rather than opioid monotherapy value.
- Ceilings and doses: ibuprofen 400-800 mg tid/qid (ceiling 400 mg); naproxen sodium 550 mg bid; celecoxib 200 mg qd/bid; acetaminophen 500-1000 mg tid/qid (ceiling 1000 mg); diclofenac potassium 50-100 mg STAT then 50 mg tid; diflunisal 1000 mg STAT then 500 mg q8-12h.
- Equianalgesic anchors: morphine 10 mg IM = 75-100 mg meperidine = 120 mg codeine parenterally; oral equivalents morphine 30 mg, codeine 180-200 mg (practically limited to ~90 mg to limit nausea/constipation), hydrocodone 30 mg, oxycodone 20 mg, propoxyphene 130-200 mg.
- Toxicity thresholds: acetaminophen hepatotoxicity above 200-250 mg/kg per 24 h (max 4 g/day healthy; 2 g/day chronic alcoholism); normeperidine half-life 15-20 h vs meperidine 3 h with ~25% oral bioavailability making oral meperidine a poor choice; norpropoxyphene half-life 30 h.
- Regimen logic: around-the-clock nonopioids reduce required opioid "sometimes to only a fraction" of maximum listed doses; routine first-line prescription of opioid combination products called irrational; pentazocine/tramadol positioned for patients with abuse history but not for currently dependent patients.
