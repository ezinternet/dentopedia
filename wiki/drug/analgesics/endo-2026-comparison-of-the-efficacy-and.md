> [!note] Non-dental evidence
> Older adults (65 or older) with hip/knee osteoarthritis, 8 weeks of continuous dosing. Extrapolation to short-course dental or postoperative analgesia is limited.

## Three-line Summary

Multicenter, double-blind, parallel-group RCT (RETHINK, Japan) in 400 adults aged 65 or older with hip/knee osteoarthritis pain, comparing acetaminophen 1800 mg/day (n=191) with NSAIDs (loxoprofen 180 mg/day, n=98, or celecoxib 200 mg/day, n=99) for 8 weeks.

Worst-pain (BPI item 3) change was -1.79 with acetaminophen versus -1.94 with NSAIDs (difference 0.14, 95% CI -0.33 to 0.61), so non-inferiority (margin 0.6) was not demonstrated by a hair; gastrointestinal disorders occurred in 17.1% vs 25.9%, and GI disorders led to discontinuation in 0.5% (acetaminophen) vs 8.2% (loxoprofen) and 6.1% (celecoxib).

Non-dental, older-adult, 8-week chronic-use population: the GI-tolerability signal (and the early GSRS rise with loxoprofen at week 2) is informative for NSAID-intolerant patients, but extrapolation to short-course dental analgesia is limited.

## 세줄요약

일본 다기관 이중맹검 평행군 무작위대조시험 (RETHINK): 65세 이상 고관절·슬관절 골관절염 환자 400명에서 아세트아미노펜 (acetaminophen) 1800 mg/일 (n=191)과 NSAID (록소프로펜 (loxoprofen) 180 mg/일 n=98, 셀레콕시브 (celecoxib) 200 mg/일 n=99)를 8주간 비교.

최악 통증 (BPI 3번 문항) 변화는 아세트아미노펜 -1.79 대 NSAID -1.94 (차이 0.14, 95% CI -0.33~0.61)로 비열등성 (margin 0.6)은 근소하게 입증되지 못함; 위장관 장애는 17.1% 대 25.9%, 위장관 장애로 인한 중단은 아세트아미노펜 0.5% 대 록소프로펜 8.2%, 셀레콕시브 6.1%.

비치과 고령자 8주 만성 투여 연구이므로 단기 치과 진통 처방으로의 외삽은 제한적이나, NSAID 위장관 불내성 환자에서 아세트아미노펜 전환의 근거 신호로 참고할 수 있음.

## Summary
The RETHINK trial randomized 400 Japanese adults aged 65 or older with hip/knee osteoarthritis pain to acetaminophen 1800 mg/day or NSAIDs (loxoprofen 180 mg/day or celecoxib 200 mg/day) for 8 weeks. Pain relief was similar (difference 0.14, 95% CI -0.33 to 0.61) but non-inferiority within a 0.6 margin was not shown. Gastrointestinal disorders were more frequent with NSAIDs (25.9% vs 17.1%) and were the leading cause of NSAID discontinuation (loxoprofen 8.2%, celecoxib 6.1%, acetaminophen 0.5%). Clinically, acetaminophen is a reasonable switch when NSAID GI intolerance occurs, with the caveats of population and duration.

## Key Contributions
- Per-NSAID GI discontinuation: loxoprofen 8/98 (8.2%) vs celecoxib 6/99 (6.1%) vs acetaminophen 1/193 (0.5%).
- Loxoprofen showed the most pronounced early GSRS rise (week 2) and a larger systolic BP rise than celecoxib or acetaminophen.
- Both NSAIDs lowered eGFR (pooled -1.71 vs +1.60 for acetaminophen); acetaminophen showed more ALT elevations above normal (15.2% vs 4.2%).

## Methodology
Multicenter double-blind parallel-group RCT, 5 Japanese sites, 2021-2023. FAS n=400 (acetaminophen 191, loxoprofen 98, celecoxib 99). Primary: BPI item 3 change at week 8, non-inferiority margin 0.6. Secondary: GSRS, SF-8, eGFR, BP, liver enzymes, adverse events. No PPI/H2RA prophylaxis or rescue analgesics allowed. Industry-sponsored.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| BPI item 3 change, week 8 | Acetaminophen -1.79 vs NSAIDs -1.94 (diff 0.14, 95% CI -0.33 to 0.61; non-inferiority not shown) |
| Per-NSAID vs acetaminophen (PPS) | Loxoprofen 0.33 (-0.24 to 0.91); celecoxib -0.06 (-0.65 to 0.52) |
| GI disorders | 33 (17.1%) acetaminophen vs 51 (25.9%) NSAIDs |
| Discontinuation, any adverse event | 3.1% acetaminophen; 10.2% loxoprofen; 8.1% celecoxib |
| Discontinuation, GI disorders | 0.5% acetaminophen; 8.2% loxoprofen; 6.1% celecoxib |
| eGFR change, week 8 | +1.60 acetaminophen vs -1.71 NSAIDs |
| ALT above normal, week 8 | 15.2% acetaminophen vs 4.2% NSAIDs |

Limitations: no placebo, GI assessed by questionnaire not endoscopy, per-NSAID analyses exploratory.
