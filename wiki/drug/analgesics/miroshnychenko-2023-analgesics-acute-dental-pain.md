## Three-line Summary

- Systematic review and meta-analysis of 6 RCTs (45–201 participants each; mean age 5.5–9.3 years) of oral analgesics after dental extraction in children ≤ 12 years — the evidence base for the 2022 ADA pediatric acute dental pain guideline.
- Ibuprofen and acetaminophen both beat placebo but differ only trivially from each other; the ibuprofen (5 mg/kg) + acetaminophen (15 mg/kg) combination reduced pain intensity importantly more than acetaminophen alone (MD −0.75 points, moderate certainty); acetaminophen-codeine gains were trivial and no trial addressed irreversible pulpitis.
- Supports ibuprofen ± acetaminophen as first-line in children, but all adverse-effect conclusions rest on very low certainty evidence — pediatric safety data are the urgent gap.

## 세줄요약

- 소아(≤ 12세) 발치 후 급성 치통의 경구 진통제에 관한 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis, SRMA): 무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) 6편(연구당 45–201명, 평균 연령 5.5–9.3세), 2022년 ADA(미국치과협회) 소아 급성 치통 임상지침의 근거.
- 이부프로펜과 아세트아미노펜은 위약보다 낫지만 서로 간 차이는 사소했고, 이부프로펜(5 mg/kg)+아세트아미노펜(15 mg/kg) 병합은 아세트아미노펜 단독보다 통증 강도를 중요하게 더 줄였으며(평균차이 (Mean Difference, MD) −0.75점, moderate certainty), 아세트아미노펜+코데인 이득은 사소했고 가역성 치수염 (irreversible pulpitis) 시험은 전무.
- 소아에서 이부프로펜 ± 아세트아미노펜 1차 선택을 뒷받침하나, 부작용 결론은 모두 매우 낮은 확신도 (very low certainty)라 소아 안전성 자료가 시급한 한계.

## Summary

First GRADE-rated SR and meta-analysis of analgesics for acute dental pain in children ≤ 12 years, conducted by the McMaster/Penn/Pittsburgh group to inform the 2022 ADA clinical practice guideline produced in partnership with the FDA. From 4,716 screened records, 6 RCTs were included — all addressing pain after simple extraction (half also surgical extraction); none addressed irreversible pulpitis. Comparators covered ibuprofen, acetaminophen, their combination, acetaminophen-codeine, and placebo across pain intensity, pain relief, global efficacy, rescue analgesia use, and adverse effects.

Bottom line: NSAID monotherapy and acetaminophen both outperform placebo with only trivial differences between them; adding acetaminophen to ibuprofen yields an important additional reduction in pain intensity; acetaminophen-codeine adds nothing clinically meaningful over single agents despite FDA restriction of codeine/tramadol in this age group since April 2017. Adverse-effect evidence is very low certainty across every comparison, so benefit-harm balancing in children rests almost entirely on indirect safety data.

## Key Contributions

- First SR + meta-analysis of pediatric acute dental pain pharmacotherapy with formal GRADE certainty assessment.
- Quantifies that combination ibuprofen + acetaminophen is the most effective available option for post-extraction pain in children ≤ 12 years (moderate certainty).
- Establishes that comparative differences among single-agent options (ibuprofen vs acetaminophen) are trivial at 4 hours — supporting choice on safety/practicality grounds.
- Explicitly maps the evidence void: zero trials for irreversible pulpitis analgesia in children, near-zero usable adverse-effect data.

## Methodology

- PRISMA 2020-compliant random-effects meta-analysis; searches of MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Central, and US Clinical Trials registry through November 2020.
- Eligibility: RCTs of any non-intravenous analgesic vs another analgesic or placebo, patients ≤ 12 years, simple/surgical extraction or irreversible pulpitis, English peer-reviewed.
- Duplicate independent screening and extraction in Covidence with third-reviewer adjudication; modified Cochrane RoB 2.0 per outcome; GRADE per comparison-outcome with minimally contextualized null thresholds (10% of baseline risk / scale range); absolute effects expressed as natural frequencies per 100 patients.

## Results

- Pain intensity at 4 h (1–4 scale): ibuprofen vs acetaminophen MD 0.27 points (95% CI −0.13 to 0.68, low certainty); ibuprofen vs placebo MD −0.19 (95% CI −0.58 to 0.21, low); acetaminophen vs placebo MD −0.13 (95% CI −0.52 to 0.26, low) — trivial.
- Combination (acetaminophen 15 mg/kg + ibuprofen 5 mg/kg): important reduction in pain intensity 15 min after general anesthesia recovery vs acetaminophen alone (MD −0.75 points; 95% CI −1.22 to 0.27; moderate certainty); trivial vs ibuprofen alone (MD −0.01; 95% CI −0.53 to 0.51; moderate certainty).
- Pain relief at 4 h (1 complete–5 none): acetaminophen worse than ibuprofen (MD 0.2, moderate certainty) and better than placebo (MD −0.30, moderate certainty), both trivial; ibuprofen importantly better than placebo (MD −0.49, low certainty).
- Global efficacy at 4 h (1 excellent–5 discontinued): ibuprofen importantly better than placebo (MD −0.97, moderate certainty); acetaminophen worse than ibuprofen (MD 0.53, moderate certainty).
- Rescue analgesia: acetaminophen may increase need at 4 h vs ibuprofen (RR 3.45; 95% CI 0.80–14.92, low certainty); ibuprofen reduces need vs placebo (RR 0.39; 95% CI 0.06–2.71, low certainty).
- Acetaminophen (240 mg) + codeine (24 mg): trivial gains over ibuprofen (MD −0.29), acetaminophen (MD −0.09), and placebo (MD −0.16), all low certainty.
- Adverse effects: very low certainty in all six comparisons — no conclusions possible.
- Dominant RoB problems: missing outcome data and selective reporting; GRADE downgrades driven by risk of bias plus imprecision (CIs crossing benefit and harm thresholds).
