## Three-line Summary
Multi-database, distributed-network retrospective cohort study (common data model) of new users of diclofenac, loxoprofen, mefenamic acid, or celecoxib in Australia, Hong Kong, Japan, Korea, and Taiwan (9,879 Japan; 70,492 Taiwan; 263,741 Korea; 246 Hong Kong; 44,013 Australia).
Versus diclofenac, loxoprofen had a lower gastrointestinal (GI) hospitalization risk in Korea (HR 0.37, 95% CI 0.25-0.54) but not in Japan (HR 1.65, 95% CI 0.47-5.78); mefenamic acid was lower in Taiwan (HR 0.45, 95% CI 0.26-0.78) and Korea (HR 0.11, 95% CI 0.05-0.27) but not Hong Kong (HR 2.16, 95% CI 0.28-16.87).
Loxoprofen looks no worse than diclofenac for serious GI events in Korean data, but the effect was not reproduced in Japan (wide CI) and the abstract reports hospitalization only, not mild GI symptoms such as nausea or vomiting; abstract-only, so confounding handling cannot be appraised.

## Summary
This retrospective cohort study used a distributed network with a common data model across Australia, Hong Kong, Japan, Korea, and Taiwan to compare the risk of hospitalization for gastrointestinal events in new users of diclofenac, loxoprofen, mefenamic acid, or celecoxib. Follow-up ended at first GI hospitalization, switch or discontinuation, disenrollment, or end of database coverage, and Cox proportional hazards models estimated risk. Incidence of GI hospitalization varied widely by country. Against diclofenac, loxoprofen was associated with lower risk in Korea only, and mefenamic acid in Taiwan and Korea. Only the abstract was available.

## Key Contributions
- Rare multi-country data on loxoprofen and mefenamic acid, NSAIDs widely used in Asia-Pacific but little studied for safety.
- Shows country-level heterogeneity: the loxoprofen advantage over diclofenac appeared in Korea but not Japan.

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort, distributed network with common data model; new users of diclofenac, loxoprofen, mefenamic acid, celecoxib; Cox proportional hazards; outcome = hospitalization for GI events. abstract-only — full text not retrieved.
