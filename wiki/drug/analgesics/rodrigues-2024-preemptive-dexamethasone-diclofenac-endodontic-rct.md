## Three-line Summary

Preemptive oral dexamethasone (4 mg) or diclofenac potassium (50 mg) administered 60 minutes before IANB with 4% articaine significantly increased anesthetic success in mandibular molars with irreversible symptomatic pulpitis: 39.3%, 21.4%, and 3.6% for dexamethasone, diclofenac, and placebo respectively (p < .001).

Both active drugs reduced postoperative pain at 24–72 hours; dexamethasone was additionally superior to placebo at 6 hours, while diclofenac showed intermediate behavior at 6–12 hours.

No significant difference in anesthetic success was found between the two drugs, making either a viable preemptive adjunct, though dexamethasone offers earlier postoperative pain control.

## Summary

The "hot tooth" — mandibular molar with irreversible symptomatic pulpitis — represents a notoriously difficult anesthetic challenge. Baseline IANB success in this scenario is very low due to peripheral sensitization and altered sodium channel expression in inflamed pulp tissue. This double-blind RCT (n=84, 28/group) evaluated whether preemptive oral dexamethasone (4 mg) or diclofenac potassium (50 mg), given 60 minutes before IANB, could improve anesthetic success and reduce postoperative pain.

The primary finding confirms both drugs significantly improve IANB success over placebo (dexamethasone 39.3%, diclofenac 21.4%, placebo 3.6%; p < .001), with no significant difference between active drug groups. For postoperative pain, dexamethasone's anti-inflammatory mechanism conferred early benefit at 6 hours; both drugs were comparable and superior to placebo from 24 to 72 hours.

Clinically, even with premedication, anesthetic success in hot teeth remains well below 50%, indicating supplemental techniques (intraligamentary, intrapulpal, or buccal infiltration with articaine) are still often necessary.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes RCT-level evidence that preemptive corticosteroid (dexamethasone) or NSAID (diclofenac potassium) meaningfully increases IANB success in symptomatic irreversible pulpitis.
- Quantifies the time-resolved postoperative pain benefit at 6, 12, 24, 48, and 72 hours for each intervention class.
- Demonstrates dexamethasone's advantage in early postoperative pain control (6 h), relevant for patient counseling and postoperative protocol design.
- Confirms the pragmatic equivalence of the two drug classes for the primary outcome (IANB success), allowing clinical selection based on patient contraindications.

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Randomized double-blind placebo-controlled RCT |
| Sample | 84 patients (28 per group) |
| Diagnosis | Mandibular molar, irreversible symptomatic pulpitis |
| Groups | Dexamethasone 4 mg / Diclofenac potassium 50 mg / Placebo (all oral, 60 min pre-op) |
| Anesthetic | IANB, 4% articaine + 1:200,000 epinephrine |
| Success measure | Cold thermal test + mNRS at 15 min post-injection |
| Postoperative monitoring | mNRS at 6, 12, 24, 48, 72 h |
| Treatment | Single-session endodontic treatment + provisional restoration |
