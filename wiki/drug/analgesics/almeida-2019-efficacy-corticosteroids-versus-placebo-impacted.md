## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis of placebo-controlled RCTs (PubMed/Scopus/Cochrane, PRISMA-structured) on corticosteroids for pain, oedema, and trismus after impacted third molar surgery — 1223 studies screened, 17 included qualitatively (730 patients, 15–45 y), 10 pooled quantitatively via Review Manager.

Corticosteroids significantly reduced pain (mean difference −17.38, 95% CI −24.81 to −9.95, P=0.002) and trismus/improved mouth opening (mean difference 6.10, 95% CI 3.42 to 8.77, P<0.00001) versus placebo; route of administration did not affect outcomes except the submucosal route, and preoperative timing was superior to postoperative specifically for trismus control.

This is placebo-controlled, pooled-level confirmation of corticosteroid efficacy for third-molar postoperative sequelae — but only the abstract was retrievable, so no pooled oedema/swelling effect size or heterogeneity (I²) statistic can be reported from this ingest.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (Almeida et al., IJOMS 2019) pooled placebo-controlled RCTs testing corticosteroids against the three classic sequelae of impacted third molar surgery — pain, oedema, and trismus. Searching PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane Library under PRISMA structure, the authors screened 1223 studies down to 17 for qualitative synthesis (730 patients, aged 15–45) and pooled 10 of those in a Review Manager meta-analysis. Corticosteroids significantly outperformed placebo on pooled pain (mean difference −17.38, 95% CI −24.81 to −9.95, P=0.002) and trismus/mouth-opening (mean difference 6.10, 95% CI 3.42 to 8.77, P<0.00001). Route of administration made no difference except for the submucosal route, and preoperative timing was specifically superior to postoperative timing for trismus control. As the placebo-controlled pooled-evidence anchor for this cluster, it independently confirms — at meta-analytic scale — the same direction of effect that single trials like [[drug/analgesics/tamgadge-2025-preoperative-dexamethasone-third-molar-pain-swelling-trismus]] demonstrate for one specific drug (dexamethasone). Only the abstract/landing page was retrievable for this ingest (no PMC full text exists for this PMID), so the pooled oedema/swelling estimate and heterogeneity statistics are not reported here.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the placebo-controlled, pooled (meta-analytic) effect sizes for corticosteroid efficacy on pain and trismus after third molar surgery — the strongest evidence tier in this wiki's corticosteroid/third-molar cluster.
- Isolates administration **timing** as a driver of the trismus benefit specifically: preoperative dosing beat postoperative dosing, while route of administration (aside from submucosal) did not matter.
- PRISMA-structured, multi-database (PubMed/Scopus/Cochrane) search feeding a 17-study qualitative pool and a 10-study quantitative pool (730 patients total).

## Methodology

Systematic review + meta-analysis. Databases: PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, PRISMA-structured. Search yielded 1223 records; 17 randomized, placebo-controlled trials met inclusion criteria for qualitative synthesis (730 patients, 15–45 years); 10 of those were quantitatively pooled using Review Manager (RevMan). Outcomes assessed: postoperative pain, oedema/swelling, and trismus, with planned subgroup analyses by corticosteroid drug type, administration route, and administration timing (preoperative vs postoperative).

## Results

| Outcome | Pooled effect (corticosteroid vs placebo) | 95% CI | P value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain | Mean difference −17.38 | −24.81 to −9.95 | 0.002 |
| Trismus (mouth opening) | Mean difference 6.10 | 3.42 to 8.77 | <0.00001 |
| Oedema/swelling | Not stated in retrievable abstract | — | — |

- Route of administration: no significant effect on any outcome except the submucosal route (direction/magnitude not given in the abstract).
- Timing: preoperative corticosteroid administration was superior to postoperative administration specifically for trismus control.
- Per-outcome study counts, forest plots, heterogeneity (I²), and risk-of-bias grading were not present in the retrievable text (abstract-only source — see sources page Limitations).
