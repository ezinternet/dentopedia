## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD42016051448; 1999-2018 search across Ovid/PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science; 875 titles → 79 full text → 13 included: 2 in vitro on titanium, 2 RCTs + 1 retrospective clinical, 7 animal; no meta-analysis due to heterogeneity) of NSAID effects on osseointegration.

Osseointegration is NOT negatively affected by NSAIDs in human clinical studies (two ibuprofen 600 mg 4×/day for 7 days RCTs showed no marginal-bone difference at 3 and 6 months), contrasting with in vitro and animal data where long-term selective COX-2 inhibition reduced bone ingrowth, BIC, and density while short-term (2 weeks) did not.

Evidence is insufficient to conclude a relationship between NSAID use and early implant failure; most studies are at high risk of bias and no human clinical study has tested a selective COX-2 inhibitor — a key gap.

## Summary

Luo et al. (2018) is a PRISMA systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42016051448) asking whether NSAIDs impair the osteogenic activity underlying osseointegration. Its defining contribution is a direct juxtaposition of evidence tiers that point in opposite directions: human clinical studies show no negative effect on osseointegration, whereas in vitro and animal experiments show impairment. The authors conclude there is insufficient evidence to establish a relationship between NSAID use and early implant failure. Rather than a blanket "NSAIDs harm bone" verdict, the review reframes the risk along three modifiable axes — dose, duration, and COX-2 selectivity — with harm concentrated in long-term, selective COX-2 exposure that has been tested only in animals, never in a human clinical trial.

## Key Contributions
- Juxtaposes human clinical vs experimental (in vitro + animal) evidence and finds they disagree in direction.
- Central message: osseointegration does not appear negatively affected by NSAIDs in human clinical studies; insufficient evidence to link NSAID use to early implant failure.
- Reframes the question along a dose / duration / COX-2-selectivity axis.
- Names the key gap: no human clinical study has tested a selective COX-2 inhibitor on osseointegration.

## Methodology
Systematic review following PRISMA, protocol registered in PROSPERO (CRD42016051448). Four databases (Ovid, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science) searched over 1999-2018 (1999 = introduction of selective COX-2 inhibitors). Screening: 875 titles → 79 full-text → 13 included studies (2 in vitro on titanium, 2 clinical trials + 1 retrospective clinical, 7 animal). No meta-analysis was performed because of heterogeneity in model, agent, dose, duration, and outcomes.

## Results
- **Human clinical = no effect.** Two RCTs (Alissa; Sakka) of ibuprofen 600 mg 4×/day for 7 days found no significant difference in marginal bone level at 3 and 6 months.
- **Human retrospective = association.** Winnett: perioperative NSAID cohort (n=60) had 119 failed implants vs non-NSAID cohort (n=44) with 78 failures, associating NSAID exposure with failure.
- **Animal = duration/selectivity dependent.** Long-term selective COX-2 inhibition (meloxicam 3 mg/kg/day 60 d; rofecoxib 12.5 mg/kg/day continuous 6 wk) significantly reduced bone ingrowth, BIC, and density → failed osseointegration; short-term (2 wk) rofecoxib/meloxicam did NOT. COX-2-knockout mice (Chikazu): minimal BIC. Diclofenac 5 d delayed peri-implant healing (Pablos) but not in Cai/Salduz — animal data inconsistent.
- **In vitro (titanium) = dose-dependent inhibition.** Boyan: NSAIDs reduced osteoblast PGE2 on rough Ti (PGE2 favorable for proliferation/differentiation); Arpornmaeklong: indomethacin + celecoxib inhibited rat calvarial osteoblast growth dose-dependently.
- **High risk of bias** across most included studies; prior SRs (Gomes = impaired; Kalyvas = short-term no impact) both agree prolonged use impairs.
