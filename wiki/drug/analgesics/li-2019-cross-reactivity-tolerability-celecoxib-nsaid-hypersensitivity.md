## Three-line Summary

Systematic MEDLINE search (research letter) pooling 62 articles / 3218 single- or double-blind placebo-controlled oral challenges with selective COX-2 inhibitors (mostly celecoxib and rofecoxib) in adults with any of the 5 NSAID-hypersensitivity phenotypes.

Overall challenge-reaction rate was 3.29% (106/3218) — mostly urticaria/angioedema/pruritus, with no severe reaction requiring emergency care or epinephrine; in the respiratory-reactor subgroup (AERD/AIA/isolated respiratory) only 1 of 753 challenges reacted (0.13%), whereas meloxicam cross-reacted in 4.72% of 847 challenges.

Celecoxib can be given without a supervised challenge to low-risk isolated-respiratory-reactors with well-controlled asthma; a urticaria/angioedema/anaphylaxis history warrants supervised dosing or a 2-step graded oral challenge, allergy evaluation is advised before meloxicam, and first-dose caution is prudent in severe or poorly-controlled asthma.

## 세줄요약

체계적 MEDLINE 검색(연구 서한, Research Letter): 5가지 비스테로이드성 항염증제 과민반응(NSAID Hypersensitivity) 표현형 중 어느 하나라도 가진 성인에서 선택적 사이클로옥시게나제-2 억제제(Selective COX-2 Inhibitor, 주로 셀레콕십·로페콕십)로 시행된 단일/이중맹검 위약대조 경구 유발검사 3,218건(논문 62편)을 통합.

전체 유발검사 반응률은 3.29%(106/3,218)로 대부분 두드러기·혈관부종·소양증이었고 응급진료나 에피네프린(epinephrine)이 필요한 중증 반응은 없었음; 호흡기 반응 아형(아스피린 악화 호흡기질환(AERD)·아스피린 유발 천식(AIA)·단독 호흡기 반응)에서는 753건 중 1건만 반응(0.13%), 반면 멜록시캄(meloxicam)은 847건 중 4.72%에서 교차반응.

잘 조절되는 천식을 동반한 저위험 단독 호흡기 반응자에게는 셀레콕십을 감독 없이 투여 가능; 두드러기·혈관부종·아나필락시스(anaphylaxis) 병력이 있으면 감독하 투여 또는 2단계 점증 경구 유발검사가 필요하고, 멜록시캄 사용 전에는 알레르기 평가를 권고하며, 중증·조절불량 천식에서는 첫 투여 시 주의.

## Summary

A recurring dental analgesic problem is the patient who reports an "aspirin allergy" or "NSAID allergy": fewer than 20% of such electronic-health-record labels actually reflect hypersensitivity, and most genuine NSAID hypersensitivity is a pharmacologic (COX-1 inhibition) rather than immunologic reaction — so a selective COX-2 inhibitor should be tolerated. This research letter tests that expectation quantitatively. The authors ran an updated systematic MEDLINE search (prespecified strategy; blinded placebo-controlled studies before November 2018; terms celecoxib/rofecoxib/etoricoxib/valdecoxib/parecoxib AND hypersensitivity/intolerance; retrospective studies and case reports excluded; manual reference-list searches added) and pooled the oral drug-challenge outcomes.

Across 62 articles and **3218** single- or double-blind placebo-controlled challenges in patients with **any** NSAID-hypersensitivity phenotype, there were **106** reactions — a **3.29%** rate. Reactions were predominantly cutaneous (urticaria, angioedema, pruritus); bronchospasm, headache, cough and one laryngeal edema also occurred, but **no** study reported a severe reaction needing emergency care or epinephrine. In the subgroup with a history of **respiratory** reactions to NSAIDs (AERD, AIA, or isolated respiratory reactions) — previously an evidence gap outside stable mild–moderate AERD — only **1 of 753** challenges reacted (**0.13%**; transient urticaria to 5 mg rofecoxib, higher doses then tolerated). By contrast, **meloxicam**, a preferential (not fully selective) COX-2 inhibitor, cross-reacted in **4.72%** of 847 challenges.

The authors reconcile these reassuring aggregates with a handful of published case reports of celecoxib-triggered bronchospasm (FEV1 falls of 21–64%, one requiring epinephrine) — all in **poorly-controlled or brittle** asthma, and in one case abolished after asthma control improved. They propose an evidence-based **risk-stratification tool for non-allergy providers**: celecoxib without further testing for low-risk isolated-respiratory reactors with well-controlled asthma; supervised administration or a formal 2-step graded oral challenge (one-tenth to one-quarter dose, then full dose, 1 h observation each) for a urticaria/angioedema/anaphylaxis history; allergy evaluation before meloxicam; and delaying the first celecoxib dose in severe or poorly-controlled asthma.

## Key Contributions

- **Pooled COX-2 inhibitor challenge-reaction rate across all NSAID-hypersensitivity phenotypes: 3.29%** (106/3218, 62 articles), with **zero** severe reactions requiring emergency care or epinephrine.
- **Respiratory-reactor subgroup rate: 0.13%** (1/753) — fills the prior evidence gap for AERD / AIA / isolated respiratory reactors beyond stable mild–moderate AERD.
- **Meloxicam cross-reacts more: 4.72%** (847 challenges) — so meloxicam is not a safe default substitute and warrants allergy evaluation first.
- A **risk-stratification algorithm for non-allergists** keyed to the allergy history (isolated-respiratory vs urticaria/angioedema/anaphylaxis), specifying when no testing, supervised dosing, or a graded challenge is appropriate.
- Practical caveat from case reports: **delay first celecoxib administration in severe or poorly-controlled asthma**; celecoxib bronchospasm risk tracks asthma control, not the coxib itself.

## Methodology

Updated systematic electronic MEDLINE search with a prespecified strategy, capturing all blinded, placebo-controlled studies published before November 2018. Search terms: (celecoxib OR rofecoxib OR etoricoxib OR valdecoxib OR parecoxib) AND (hypersensitivity OR intolerance); no language restriction predefined. Retrospective articles and case reports were excluded from the pooled challenge analysis; reference lists of included trials were hand-searched. Outcomes pooled were single- or double-blind placebo-controlled oral drug-provocation challenges, with reaction rate = reactions ÷ challenges, computed for the whole cohort, the respiratory-reaction subgroup, and meloxicam separately. Celecoxib and rofecoxib were the two most-studied agents. Detailed study-level tables are in the online repository (the article is a "To the Editor" research letter, so the main text carries abbreviated methods and no formal risk-of-bias or heterogeneity assessment). Recommendations mirror standard practice at the Brigham and Women's Hospital AERD Center (>900 AERD patients).

## Results

| Population | Challenges | Reactions | Reaction rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any NSAID-hypersensitivity phenotype (selective COX-2 inhibitors) | 3218 | 106 | **3.29%** |
| Respiratory reactors (AERD / AIA / isolated respiratory) | 753 | 1 | **0.13%** |
| Meloxicam (any NSAID hypersensitivity) | 847 | 40 | **4.72%** |

- **Reaction phenotype (overall)**: mostly urticaria and/or angioedema and pruritus; also bronchospasm, headache, cough, and 1 laryngeal edema. No severe reaction requiring emergency medical care or epinephrine in any pooled study.
- **The one respiratory-subgroup reaction**: AIA confirmed by aspirin challenge; transient urticaria at a 5 mg rofecoxib dose, then tolerated higher doses. Aspirin challenge raised urinary LTE4 within 6 h in these AIA subjects; rofecoxib and placebo did not.
- **Celecoxib case reports (poorly-controlled / brittle asthma, outside the pooled set)**: FEV1 −21% (30 y, corticosteroid-dependent asthma + AERD); FEV1 −64% requiring epinephrine (25 y, brittle asthma + AIA); FEV1 ~−40% at 15 mg (poorly-controlled AERD); FEV1 2.84 → 0.81 L at 50 mg, but a repeat challenge to 200 mg was entirely negative once asthma control improved.
- **Risk-stratification tool**:
  - Low-risk **isolated respiratory** reaction history + well-controlled asthma → celecoxib **without** supervised challenge.
  - **Urticaria / angioedema / anaphylaxis** history → supervised administration, or allergy evaluation + formal **2-step graded oral challenge** (one-tenth to one-quarter dose, then full dose; 1 h observation after each).
  - **Before meloxicam** → allergy evaluation.
  - **Severe / poorly-controlled asthma** → consider delaying the first celecoxib dose.
- Celecoxib is the only selective COX-2 inhibitor marketed in the United States.

## Dental Relevance

- When a dental patient reports an aspirin/NSAID reaction and needs post-operative analgesia, **celecoxib is a well-supported alternative** — overall cross-reaction risk ~3%, cutaneous and non-severe, and ~0.13% if the reported reaction was purely respiratory.
- **Clarify the reported reaction first.** An isolated respiratory history (wheeze, rhinorrhea, "asthma got worse") in a patient with well-controlled asthma is the low-risk group; a history of hives, facial swelling, or anaphylaxis is the group needing a supervised first dose or a referral for graded challenge.
- **Do not swap in meloxicam as an easy substitute** — its cross-reactivity (4.72%) is higher than celecoxib's, and it should be preceded by allergy evaluation in these patients.
- **Poorly-controlled or brittle asthma**: defer the first celecoxib dose (or give it where rescue is available) rather than sending the patient home with a first-ever dose.
- Acetaminophen (paracetamol) remains the other non-cross-reacting option for mild pain; for moderate post-extraction pain where an anti-inflammatory is wanted, celecoxib fills the gap left by avoiding ibuprofen.
