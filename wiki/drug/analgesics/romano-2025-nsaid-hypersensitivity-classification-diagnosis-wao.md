## Three-line Summary

World Allergy Organization consensus Statement (2025) that updates the classification and routine diagnosis of hypersensitivity reactions to non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs, NSAIDs).

It sorts reactions into cross-reactive COX-1–driven phenotypes (N-ERD, NECD, NIUAA — the new umbrella term replacing NIUA — plus mixed variants), drug-specific immunologic phenotypes (SNIUAA IgE-mediated, SNIDHR T-cell), and a newly added NSAID-as-food-allergy-cofactor category (NEFA/NIFA); oral aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid, ASA) challenge / drug provocation testing is the diagnostic gold standard, with skin testing useful only for pyrazolones and delayed reactions.

For a patient labelled "NSAID/aspirin allergic," the safe analgesic path is phenotype-dependent: most cross-reactive patients tolerate a selective COX-2 inhibitor (celecoxib, etoricoxib; ~3.3% challenge positivity, 0.13% in respiratory reactors) or low-dose paracetamol, while true single-NSAID IgE allergy means avoiding only that drug and its chemical group.

## 세줄요약

세계알레르기기구 (World Allergy Organization, WAO) 합의 성명서 (2025): 비스테로이드성 소염진통제 (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs, NSAIDs) 과민반응의 분류와 일상 진단 갱신.

반응을 COX-1 억제 기반 교차반응형 표현형 (N-ERD, NECD, NIUAA — 기존 NIUA를 대체하는 새 포괄 용어 — 및 혼합형), 약물특이 면역학적 표현형 (SNIUAA IgE 매개, SNIDHR T세포 매개), 새로 추가된 NSAID-음식알레르기 공동인자 범주 (NEFA/NIFA)로 분류; 경구 아스피린 (Acetylsalicylic Acid, ASA) 유발검사·약물유발검사 (Drug Provocation Test)가 진단 표준이고, 피부검사는 피린계 (pyrazolone)와 지연형 반응에서만 유용.

"NSAID/아스피린 알레르기"로 표시된 환자의 안전한 진통제 경로는 표현형에 따라 다름: 대부분의 교차반응형 환자는 선택적 COX-2 억제제 (셀레콕시브, 에토리콕시브; 유발검사 양성률 ~3.3%, 호흡기 반응군 0.13%)나 저용량 파라세타몰을 내약하며, 진성 단일-NSAID IgE 알레르기는 해당 약물과 같은 화학군만 회피하면 됨.

## Summary

This World Allergy Organization (WAO) Task Force Statement is the current authoritative framework for classifying and diagnosing NSAID hypersensitivity. It moves past the older timing-only split (immediate vs delayed) to a scheme that pairs timing with mechanism, and it adds two things the previous European and North American classifications lacked: a single umbrella term (**NIUAA**) for cross-reactive urticaria/angioedema ± second-organ reactions, and recognition of **NSAIDs as a cofactor or aggravator of food allergy** (NEFA/NIFA).

The clinically decisive division is **cross-reactive vs drug-specific**:

- **Cross-reactive phenotypes** — N-ERD (= AERD), NECD, NIUAA, and their mixed variants — are caused by **COX-1 inhibition**, not by IgE. Blocking COX-1 shunts arachidonic acid to 5-lipoxygenase, producing a cysteinyl-leukotriene surge and removing the PGE2/EP2 brake on mast cells. These patients react to *any* sufficiently strong COX-1 inhibitor, which is why cross-reactivity is the defining feature, and why selective COX-2 inhibitors are usually safe.
- **Drug-specific phenotypes** — SNIUAA (immediate, IgE-mediated, best proven for pyrazolones) and SNIDHR (delayed, T-cell mediated: fixed drug eruption, maculopapular exanthem, photoallergic contact dermatitis, rarely SJS/TEN, DRESS, AGEP) — are true immunologic reactions to one drug or one chemical group, with other NSAIDs tolerated.

**Diagnosis** centres on the **oral ASA challenge / drug provocation test** as gold standard. Skin tests help only for pyrazolone SNIUAA and for delayed reactions (patch tests + delayed-reading intradermal tests). The basophil activation test is not recommended for cross-reactive phenotypes.

**For dentistry**, the practical message: a patient carrying an unverified "NSAID/aspirin allergy" label should be phenotyped by an allergist. For the large cross-reactive majority, a selective COX-2 inhibitor or low-dose acetaminophen (avoiding ≥1 g doses) is the safe analgesic — ideally challenge-verified before prescribing for NECD or NIUAA. Only confirmed single-NSAID (SNIUAA/SNIDHR) allergy requires avoiding that specific drug plus its chemical group, leaving chemically unrelated NSAIDs available.

## Key Contributions

- **NIUAA replaces NIUA** as an umbrella term: reactions to ≥2 chemically unrelated NSAIDs with urticaria/angioedema and/or a second organ system, **no underlying chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU)**. Absorbs the former "blended reactions."
- **Mixed N-ERD** (N-ERD + cutaneous/extra-respiratory signs, ~20% of N-ERD) and **mixed NECD** (NECD + respiratory symptoms) formalized; may be termed anaphylactic reactions.
- **NEFA/NIFA newly included**: NSAID as aggravating factor (NEFA — worsens an otherwise mild food reaction) or obligate cofactor (NIFA — food tolerated alone, systemic reaction only with NSAID co-exposure within ~4 h). ~16–18% of immediate NSAID reactions in two European prospective cohorts; Pru p 3 (peach lipid transfer protein) and ω-5 gliadin the usual allergens.
- Consolidated **diagnostic algorithm**: oral ASA challenge / drug provocation test as gold standard; skin testing restricted to pyrazolones (SNIUAA) and delayed reactions.
- Pooled **alternative-agent challenge data**: coxibs safest, meloxicam intermediate, high-dose paracetamol risky in cross-reactive patients.

## Methodology

- WAO-commissioned expert Task Force; per-phenotype topic teams did literature research and drafting; Steering Committee and Review Panel revised.
- **Narrative synthesis + expert consensus**, not a systematic review. Recommendations graded implicitly ("we suggest," "we recommend," "according to most of the authors").
- `evidence_level: consensus` — position paper; individual claims rest on heterogeneous primary studies (prospective cohorts, challenge series, one 2013 meta-analysis of coxib respiratory safety, a 62-study systematic search of coxib challenges).
- WAO funded open-access publication only; authors report no competing interests.

## Results

### Phenotype scheme

| Phenotype | Timing | Culprit drugs | Underlying disease | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **N-ERD** (= AERD) | 30 min–3 h | ≥2 COX-1 inhibitors | asthma + CRS with nasal polyps | COX-1 inhibition → CysLT surge (non-IgE) |
| **Mixed N-ERD** | immediate | COX-1 inhibitors | N-ERD + cutaneous signs | as N-ERD |
| **NECD** | minutes–hours | ≥2 chemically unrelated COX-1 inhibitors | **CSU** | COX-1 inhibition; intensity tracks CSU activity |
| **Mixed NECD** | minutes–hours | COX-1 inhibitors | NECD + respiratory symptoms | as NECD |
| **NIUAA** (replaces NIUA) | within hours | ≥2 chemically unrelated NSAIDs | **no CSU** (often atopic/dust-mite) | COX-1 inhibition (non-IgE) |
| **SNIUAA** | <1 h (up to 6 h) | single NSAID / same chemical group | no CSU | **IgE-mediated** (proven for pyrazolones); COX-1-potency independent |
| **SNIDHR** | >24 h | single NSAID / same chemical group | — | **T-cell mediated** (FDE, MDE, PACD; rarely SJS/TEN, DRESS, AGEP) |
| **NEFA / NIFA** (new) | immediate | NSAID + food within ~4 h | food sensitization (Pru p 3, ω-5 gliadin) | NSAID ↑ gut allergen absorption + mast-cell effect |

### Epidemiology
- N-ERD: 0.1–0.6% population; 7.2% of adult asthmatics, up to 14.9% in severe asthma.
- **NIUAA is the most common phenotype at all ages** — >40% of adult NSAID hypersensitivity, 45–60% in children.
- NECD: up to 30% of CSU patients flare on chemically unrelated NSAIDs.
- SNIUAA: 15.9–47.2% of immediate NSAID hypersensitivity in adult series (pyrazolones, ibuprofen, ketoprofen).
- SNIDHR: rarest type diagnosed by allergy testing.

### Diagnosis
- **Oral ASA challenge / drug provocation test = gold standard.** N-ERD sensitivity ~80–90%; tolerating graded ASA to 325 mg generally excludes COX-1 hypersensitivity. Inhaled lysine-ASA/ketorolac challenge: sensitivity 77–90%, safer.
- β-blockers held before challenge; FEV1 must be ≥70% predicted; not during active CSU (NECD).
- NIUAA can be diagnosed without challenge if recent (<5 y), well-documented reactions to ≥2 chemically unrelated NSAIDs and no CSU — but ASA challenge is positive in only ~63% of 2-NSAID reactors vs ~92% of ≥3-NSAID reactors (overdiagnosis risk).
- **Skin tests**: useful only for pyrazolones (metamizole BAT sensitivity 42–65%, specificity 83–100%) and delayed reactions (patch tests + delayed-reading intradermal tests — high specificity, low sensitivity; dIDT with metamizole > patch test). Not recommended for common NSAIDs. BAT not recommended for cross-reactive phenotypes.

### Alternative analgesic agents (dental-relevant)
| Agent | Challenge positivity in NSAID-hypersensitive patients | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Selective COX-2 inhibitors (coxibs), overall** | **3.3%** (106 / 3218 blinded placebo-controlled challenges) | 93% of reactions urticaria/angioedema only |
| Celecoxib | 3.5% (40 / 1140) | |
| Etoricoxib | 5.4% (32 / 595) | more COX-2 selective than celecoxib; a coxib reactor often tolerates another |
| Parecoxib | 0% (0 / 116) | |
| **Coxibs in respiratory (N-ERD) reactors only** | **0.13%** (1 / 753) | strongest safety signal |
| **Meloxicam** (preferential COX-2) | **4.7%** (of 847 challenges); up to 14–16% in dedicated N-ERD cohorts | higher than coxibs |
| Nimesulide / meloxicam (retrospective series) | ~8–10% | |
| **Paracetamol (acetaminophen), high dose ≥1 g** | **~20% of cross-reactive patients** (22.4%, 11/49 N-ERD; ~9% NIUAA) | low doses generally tolerated — dose matters |

- Reactions to alternative-agent challenges were **milder than the index reaction** and easily controlled.
- **A verifying drug challenge is still recommended before prescribing an alternative for NECD or NIUAA**; consider it for N-ERD with prior anaphylaxis. Protocol: 1/10–1/4 therapeutic dose → remainder, 1–2 h observation after each step.
- **SNIUAA / SNIDHR**: avoid the culprit drug **and its chemical group** (cross-reactivity within propionic acids, acetic acids, pyrazolones, oxicams); chemically unrelated NSAIDs remain usable. Ketoprofen photoallergy → also avoid benzophenone drugs and chemical sunscreens.
- **NEFA/NIFA**: avoid NSAIDs (incl. antiplatelet-dose ASA) for 4 h before/after meals containing the culprit food — this alone lets patients keep using NSAIDs.

### Clinical bottom line for dentistry
A patient labelled "NSAID/aspirin allergic" needs phenotyping. For the cross-reactive majority (N-ERD / NECD / NIUAA), low-dose acetaminophen or a selective COX-2 agent (ideally challenge-verified) is the safe analgesic path. True single-NSAID IgE allergy (SNIUAA) or delayed T-cell reaction (SNIDHR) means avoiding that drug plus its chemical group only — other NSAIDs are fine.
