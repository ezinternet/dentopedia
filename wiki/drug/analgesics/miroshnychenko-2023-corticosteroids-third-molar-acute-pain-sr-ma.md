## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis of 40 placebo-controlled RCTs (Epistemonikos/L·OVE search of MEDLINE, Embase, CENTRAL, ClinicalTrials.gov from inception to April 2023; Cochrane RoB 2; GRADE) on oral, submucosal, or intramuscular corticosteroids for acute pain after surgical extraction of mandibular third molars — the commissioned evidence review for the ADA 2024 acute dental pain CPG.

Corticosteroids reduced pain intensity by 8.79 points on a 0–100 VAS at 6 hours (95% CI 14.8 to 2.77 lower; 7 RCTs, n=396; low certainty) and 8.89 points at 24 hours (95% CI 10.71 to 7.06 lower; 23 RCTs, n=1,555; very low certainty) — both below the 10-point triviality threshold (10% of scale range) the authors pre-specified; no difference in postoperative infection (RD 0%, 95% CI −1% to 1%; 21 RCTs, n=1,630; low) or alveolar osteitis (RD 0%, 95% CI −3% to 4%; 8 RCTs, n=410; very low).

The finding is not "corticosteroids do nothing" but "the analgesic benefit falls below the smallest difference patients would notice" — and because no included study measured total pain relief, global efficacy rating, or mood alteration, the patient-centred outcomes that could justify the addition were never tested; this is the reasoning behind ADA 2024 Recommendation 1.5 against corticosteroids as an analgesic add-on.

## 세줄요약

40편 위약대조 무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT)의 체계적 문헌고찰·메타분석 (Epistemonikos/L·OVE 검색, MEDLINE·Embase·CENTRAL·ClinicalTrials.gov, 개시~2023년 4월; 코크란 비뚤림위험도구 2판 (Risk of Bias 2, RoB 2); 근거수준평가체계 (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation, GRADE)): 하악 제3대구치 외과적 발치 후 급성통증에 대한 경구·점막하·근육내 코르티코스테로이드 (Corticosteroids).

통증강도는 시각아날로그척도 (Visual Analog Scale, VAS) 0–100에서 6시간 8.79점 감소 (95% 신뢰구간 14.8~2.77 감소; RCT 7편, n=396; 낮은 확실성), 24시간 8.89점 감소 (95% 신뢰구간 10.71~7.06 감소; RCT 23편, n=1,555; 매우 낮은 확실성) — 둘 다 저자가 사전 규정한 미미함(trivial) 문턱 10점(척도 범위의 10%)에 못 미친다. 술후 감염(위험차 0%, 95% 신뢰구간 −1%~1%; RCT 21편, n=1,630)·건성발치와 (Alveolar Osteitis)(위험차 0%, 95% 신뢰구간 −3%~4%; RCT 8편, n=410) 모두 차이 없음.

결론은 "효과 없다"가 아니라 **"진통 효과가 환자가 체감하는 최소 차이에 못 미친다"** 이며, 총진통량 (Total Pain Relief)·전반효능평가 (Global Efficacy Rating)·기분변화를 측정한 연구가 단 한 편도 없어 추가 투여를 정당화할 환자중심 결과가 애초에 검증된 적이 없다 — 이것이 미국치과의사협회 (American Dental Association, ADA) 2024 지침 권고 1.5가 진통 목적 코르티코스테로이드 추가를 조건부 반대하는 근거다.

## Summary

This is one of four systematic reviews commissioned by the ADA Science and Research Institute to build the 2024 evidence-based clinical practice guideline on pharmacologic management of acute dental pain. It asks a deliberately narrow question: does adding a corticosteroid — orally, submucosally, or intramuscularly — reduce *pain* after surgical removal of mandibular third molars? Forty placebo-controlled RCTs qualified, spanning dexamethasone 4–10 mg, methylprednisolone 40 mg, and prednisolone 10–30 mg across parallel-group and split-mouth designs from more than ten countries.

The pooled estimates are directionally favourable and statistically significant, yet the authors classify the benefit as *trivial*. That verdict follows from a methodological choice made before analysis: using GRADE's minimally contextualized approach, they set the threshold for an important effect at 10% of the measurement scale — 10 points on a 0–100 VAS. Observed reductions of 8.79 points at 6 hours and 8.89 points at 24 hours land just underneath it. The distinction matters clinically, because the same numbers reported without a threshold read as "corticosteroids significantly reduce pain."

Safety findings are neutral rather than reassuring: postoperative infection and alveolar osteitis show risk differences of exactly 0%, and the only signal of possible benefit — fewer GI adverse effects — rests on 3 RCTs and 120 participants with a confidence interval running from 54% fewer to 16% more. Certainty is low or very low for every outcome the review could estimate.

The most consequential result is an absence. Not one of the 40 trials measured total pain relief, global efficacy rating, or mood alteration. The outcomes that would capture whether patients actually *feel* better, or worse, on a corticosteroid were never collected — so the review reports an evidence vacuum, not a null finding, on precisely the question a clinician deciding whether to add dexamethasone would want answered.

This review is also **not the last word, and the wiki holds the disagreement.** The larger network meta-analysis by Canellas 2022 (61 RCTs, n=3,561) finds dexamethasone 8 mg submucosal reducing pain by 30.95 VAS units on day 1 — far above this review's threshold. The divergence tracks three design differences: measurement window (6–24 h here vs days 1–2 there), pooling granularity (all agents/doses/routes collapsed here vs agent-and-route-resolved there), and the threshold-versus-significance judgment. The clinical question should be read as contested.

Scope discipline is essential when reading this page. Studies reporting only inflammatory-complication outcomes — trismus, facial swelling, infection as a primary endpoint — were excluded by design, and intravenous administration was excluded entirely. This review is evidence about **pain**, and must not be repurposed as evidence against corticosteroids for swelling or trismus control.

## Key Contributions

- First SR on this question to apply **GRADE certainty assessment with a pre-specified triviality threshold**; the authors note that almost all prior SRs predate 2018, none used GRADE, and they used suboptimal methods to synthesize pain outcomes
- Shifts the corticosteroid question from *statistical significance* to **minimal important difference** — the pooled effect is real but sub-threshold
- Documents that **no included RCT measured total pain relief, global efficacy rating, or mood alteration**, exposing a vacuum in patient-important outcomes
- Establishes the evidence base for ADA 2024 Recommendation 1.5 (conditional, very low certainty) against corticosteroids as an analgesic add-on
- Preserves the pain/inflammation boundary by excluding trismus- and swelling-only studies, so it does not overreach into the indication corticosteroids are actually used for
- Finds no clinically significant difference between oral, submucosal, and intramuscular routes

## Methodology

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | SR + random-effects meta-analysis, PRISMA-compliant |
| Search | Epistemonikos (meta-search: Cochrane CDSR, PubMed, MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, PsycINFO, LILACS) then L·OVE platform; inception → April 2023 |
| Selection | 240 records screened → 103 full texts → **40 RCTs** (64 references). Full-text exclusions: wrong outcomes 52, wrong intervention 8, wrong population 3 |
| Population | Adolescents/adults/older adults, *surgical* third-molar extraction (flap + osteotomy) |
| Intervention | Corticosteroids oral / submucosal / intramuscular, any dose, vs placebo. **IV excluded** |
| Outcomes | Pain intensity 6 h and 24 h; total pain relief 6 h; global efficacy rating 6 h; adverse effects (infection, alveolar osteitis, mood alteration, GI) |
| Excluded | Studies reporting only inflammatory complications (trismus, facial swelling, infection) |
| Risk of bias | Cochrane RoB 2 |
| Certainty | GRADE, minimally contextualized, null-effect threshold; **trivial = 10% of scale range** (10 points on VAS 0–100) or 10% of baseline risk |

## Results

| Outcome | Studies | n | Effect vs placebo | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pain intensity at 6 h (VAS 0–100) | 7 RCTs | 396 | **MD 8.79 lower** (95% CI 14.8 to 2.77 lower) | Low |
| Pain intensity at 24 h (VAS 0–100) | 23 RCTs | 1,555 | **MD 8.89 lower** (95% CI 10.71 to 7.06 lower) | Very low |
| Postoperative infection | 21 RCTs | 1,630 | RD 0% (95% CI −1% to 1%) | Low |
| Alveolar osteitis | 8 RCTs | 410 | RD 0% (95% CI −3% to 4%) | Very low |
| GI adverse effects (nausea/vomiting) | 3 RCTs | 120 | RD −19% (95% CI −54% to +16%) | Very low |
| Total pain relief at 6 h | 0 | — | **Not measured** | — |
| Global efficacy rating at 6 h | 0 | — | **Not measured** | — |
| Mood alteration | 0 | — | **Not measured** | — |

**Reading the pain rows.** Both confidence intervals exclude zero, so each estimate is statistically significant. Both point estimates sit below 10 points, so each is classified as trivial. A reader who takes only the significance and drops the threshold will reach the opposite clinical conclusion from the one the guideline panel reached.

**Route.** Subgroup analyses across oral, submucosal, and intramuscular administration found that "any minor quantitative differences observed across analyses were not clinically significant." A statistically significant route-dependent difference appeared for GI adverse effects, but on very-low-certainty evidence with a CI spanning benefit and harm.

- [[drug/analgesics/carrasco-labra-2024-pharmacologic-management-acute-dental-pain-guideline]] — the ADA 2024 CPG this review was commissioned to inform. Recommendation 1.5 (suggests AGAINST oral/submucosal/IM corticosteroids as an analgesic add-on; conditional, very low certainty) rests directly on these estimates.
- [[drug/analgesics/almeida-2019-efficacy-corticosteroids-versus-placebo-impacted]] — earlier SR+MA (17 studies, 730 patients) reporting a *significant* pain reduction (MD −17.38, 95% CI −24.81 to −9.95). Superseded on the pain axis by this review: 40 vs 17 RCTs, GRADE certainty assessment, full text vs abstract-only, and explicitly designed to correct the pre-2018 SRs' synthesis methods.
- [[drug/analgesics/markiewicz-2008-corticosteroids-reduce-postoperative-morbidity-after-third-molar-surgery]] — SR+MA (12 RCTs) that likewise found **no significant pain benefit** (early WMD 0.4 VAS, P=.07) while confirming edema and trismus benefit. Converges with this review on the pain axis and illustrates that the deflationary pain finding is not new — it was simply never carried into guidance until GRADE was applied.
- [[drug/analgesics/tamgadge-2025-preoperative-dexamethasone-third-molar-pain-swelling-trismus]] — later split-mouth RCT (n=60) reporting significantly lower VAS pain at day 2 and day 7 with preoperative IM dexamethasone 4 mg. A standing disagreement rather than a resolved one: a single centre RCT against a 40-trial pooled estimate, at later timepoints (day 2/7) than this review's 6 h and 24 h windows.
- [[drug/analgesics/canellas-2022-corticosteroids-third-molar-nma]] — **head-on conflict, and the most important one on this page.** This frequentist NMA (61 RCTs, n=3,561) reports dexamethasone 8 mg submucosal reducing pain by 30.95 VAS units on day 1 and 15.25 on day 2 — an effect three times this review's triviality threshold, from a *larger* evidence base. Three structural differences plausibly explain the divergence, and a clinician should decide which applies to their case:
  1. **Timepoint.** This review measured 6 h and 24 h; Canellas measured days 1–2 and [[drug/analgesics/tamgadge-2025-preoperative-dexamethasone-third-molar-pain-swelling-trismus]] days 2 and 7. Corticosteroid anti-inflammatory action peaks after the first 24 hours, so the two syntheses may be sampling different parts of the same curve.
  2. **Pooling granularity.** This review collapses every agent, dose, and route into one binary corticosteroid-vs-placebo contrast. Canellas separates five agents by dose and route and finds the benefit concentrated in one regimen — dexamethasone 8 mg submucosal. Binary pooling dilutes a regimen-specific effect toward the null.
  3. **Threshold vs significance.** This review's verdict depends on a pre-specified 10-point minimal important difference; Canellas reports statistical significance without one.

  None of the three is resolved by existing evidence. Treat the corticosteroid analgesia question as **contested**, not closed — the ADA recommendation is conditional and rated very low certainty for exactly this reason.
