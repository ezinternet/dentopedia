## Three-line Summary

Submucosal injection of dexamethasone (덱사메타손) or long-acting betamethasone (베타메타손) significantly reduced postoperative pain compared to placebo in 197 patients undergoing single-visit root canal treatment (근관치료, RCT) for irreversible pulpitis (비가역적 치수염, IP).
Dexamethasone was superior in the first 24 hours; betamethasone showed equal efficacy at 48 hours and extended relief through 72 hours and 7 days.
Both corticosteroids improved patient quality of life (삶의 질, QOL); mandibular pain exceeded maxillary pain in all groups; pain peaked at 12 hours across all three groups.

## Summary

This double-blind RCT (n=197 completers from 242 enrolled) investigated the effect of submucosal corticosteroid infiltration on postoperative pain and QOL following single-visit endodontic treatment of irreversible pulpitis. Patients were randomized to submucosal injection of long-acting betamethasone, dexamethasone, or placebo, and reported pain and QOL at baseline and six postoperative intervals over 7 days.

Both corticosteroids significantly outperformed placebo at every time point. The pattern of relative efficacy was time-dependent: dexamethasone provided superior pain relief at 6, 12, and 24 hours, while both agents were equivalent at 48 hours, and betamethasone's long-acting formulation sustained greater relief at 72 hours and through 7 days. Pain peaked at 12 hours in all groups. Mandibular teeth consistently generated higher pain scores than maxillary teeth. Sex had no significant influence on pain intensity within the corticosteroid groups. QOL was measurably higher in both treatment groups versus placebo across the observation period.

## Key Contributions

- Head-to-head RCT establishing time-dependent differential efficacy between dexamethasone and long-acting betamethasone for endodontic postoperative pain
- Demonstrates that choice of corticosteroid should reflect the clinical priority: dexamethasone for early (≤24h) pain suppression; long-acting betamethasone for sustained (≥72h) multi-day relief
- Adds QOL measurement as a patient-centered outcome alongside pain VAS, strengthening clinical relevance
- Confirms mandibular teeth produce higher postoperative pain than maxillary — actionable for preoperative patient counseling

## Methodology

- **Design**: Double-blind randomized clinical trial
- **Population**: 242 healthy adults with irreversible pulpitis; 197 completed 7-day follow-up (67 placebo, 66 betamethasone, 64 dexamethasone)
- **Intervention**: Submucosal injection of long-acting betamethasone or dexamethasone at treatment completion
- **Control**: Submucosal placebo injection
- **Outcome**: Self-reported pain severity and QOL questionnaire at 6h, 12h, 24h, 48h, 72h, 7d
- **Statistics**: Friedman test, Kruskal-Wallis test; P ≤ .05
- **Attrition**: 18.6% (45/242 lost to follow-up)

## Results

| Time Point | Dexamethasone | Betamethasone | Placebo |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6h | Best | Better than placebo | Worst |
| 12h | Best (peak pain all groups) | Better than placebo | Worst |
| 24h | Best | Better than placebo | Worst |
| 48h | Equal to betamethasone | Equal to dexamethasone | Worst |
| 72h | Better than placebo | Best | Worst |
| 7d | Better than placebo | Best | Worst |

- Both corticosteroids improved QOL vs placebo throughout follow-up
- Mandibular pain > maxillary pain (all groups)
- No sex difference in pain within corticosteroid groups
- All groups experienced 12-hour pain peak (flare-up pattern)
