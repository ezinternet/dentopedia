## Three-line Summary

Structured review (Cochrane/PubMed/Medline, Jan 1982–Dec 2012; 360 references screened, 31 included: 2 randomized double-blind placebo-controlled clinical trials, 20 in vivo animal osseointegration studies, 9 in vitro osteoprogenitor-cell studies) of how NSAIDs affect osseointegration and dental implant survival.

Clinical trials showed COX-1 inhibitors did not impair osseointegration; animal studies showed any drug capable of inhibiting COX-2 may impair it; in vitro studies identified COX-2 inhibitors as the most potent depressors of osseointegration at the cellular level.

Caution is warranted when selecting COX-2-active NSAIDs in the postoperative period around implant placement.

## 세줄요약

NSAID가 골유착 (Osseointegration)과 임플란트 실패에 미치는 영향을 다룬 구조화 리뷰 (Cochrane/PubMed/Medline, 1982.1–2012.12; 360편 스크리닝 후 31편 포함: 무작위 이중맹검 위약대조 임상시험 2편, 동물 골유착 연구 20편, 시험관 골전구세포 (Osteoprogenitor cell) 연구 9편).

임상시험에서 사이클로옥시게나제-1 (Cyclooxygenase-1, COX-1) 억제제는 골유착을 저해하지 않았으나, 동물 연구에서는 COX-2를 억제할 수 있는 약물이 골유착을 저해할 수 있었고, 시험관 연구에서 COX-2 억제제가 세포 수준에서 가장 강력한 골유착 억제 (depressor)로 나타났다.

임플란트 식립 후 술후 기간에 COX-2 활성 NSAID 선택에는 주의가 필요하다.

## Summary

This review asks whether NSAIDs impair osseointegration, whether they cause dental implant failures, and whether chronic NSAID users can safely receive implants. Drawing on a bibliographic electronic search of the Cochrane Library, PubMed, and Medline for articles published January 1982–December 2012, it screened 360 references and included 31 that met design-based criteria (randomized double-blind placebo-controlled clinical studies, in vivo animal osseointegration studies, and in vitro osteoprogenitor-cell studies). The organizing insight is that the relevant variable is COX isoform selectivity, not NSAIDs as a class: across the clinical, animal, and cellular tiers, COX-1 inhibition appears compatible with osseointegration while COX-2 inhibition is the concern. The practical message is caution when selecting COX-2-active NSAIDs during the postoperative period.

## Key Contributions

- COX-1 inhibitors did not impair osseointegration in the included clinical trials — reframing the NSAID question toward isoform selectivity rather than the drug class as a whole.
- COX-2 is the culprit: any drug capable of inhibiting COX-2 may impair osseointegration (animal evidence).
- In vitro, COX-2 inhibitors are the most potent depressors of osseointegration at the cellular level — a mechanistic anchor for the postoperative caution.

## Methodology

Bibliographic electronic search (Cochrane Library, PubMed, Medline) of articles published January 1982–December 2012, using the keywords nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, dental implants, bone healing, and osteoprogenitor cells. Inclusion was restricted to three study designs. Of 360 references identified, 31 met inclusion: 2 clinical trials, 20 animal studies, and 9 osteoprogenitor-cell studies.

## Results

- Clinical trials (n=2): COX-1 inhibitors did NOT impair osseointegration.
- Animal studies (n=20): any drug capable of inhibiting COX-2 may impair the osseointegration process.
- In vitro osteoprogenitor-cell studies (n=9): COX-2 inhibitors are the most potent depressors of osseointegration at the cellular level.
- Recommendation: caution when selecting COX-2 NSAIDs during the postoperative period.
