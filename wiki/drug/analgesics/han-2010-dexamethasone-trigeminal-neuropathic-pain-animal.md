## Three-line Summary

Rat model of mal-positioned dental implant directly injuring the inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) to test dexamethasone's analgesic effect on trigeminal neuropathic pain.

Daily dexamethasone (25 or 50 mg/kg i.p.) produced prolonged anti-allodynic effects when given early, but failed to reduce mechanical allodynia once already established; bilateral (mirror-image) hypersensitivity was also observed.

Early corticosteroid intervention is critical after IAN injury — once neuropathic sensitization is established, dexamethasone provides no benefit.

## 세줄요약

악위치 임플란트로 하치조신경(Inferior Alveolar Nerve, IAN)을 손상시킨 쥐 모델에서 덱사메타손(dexamethasone)의 진통 효과를 평가한 동물 연구.

덱사메타손 25·50 mg/kg 조기 복강 투여 시 지속적 항이질통(anti-allodynic) 효과 확인; 이미 확립된 기계적 이질통(mechanical allodynia)에는 효과 없음; 양측성 과민(bilateral hypersensitivity) 관찰로 중추 감작(central sensitization) 시사.

신경 손상 후 코르티코스테로이드(corticosteroid) 조기 투여가 필수적이며, 신경병증 감작 확립 이후에는 효과 없음.

## Summary

This animal study created a clinically relevant model of implant-induced IAN injury by extracting the left mandibular 2nd molar in rats and replacing it with a miniature dental implant positioned to injure the IAN. Nociceptive behavior was tracked post-surgery.

Key findings:
- Mal-positioned implants caused significant bilateral decrease in air-puff thresholds (mechanical allodynia), as well as mechanical hyperalgesia and cold/thermal hypersensitivity — notably present on **both** the ipsilateral and contralateral sides, indicating central sensitization.
- **Early** daily dexamethasone (25 or 50 mg/kg i.p.) produced prolonged anti-allodynic effects — preventing the development of established neuropathic pain.
- **Delayed** dexamethasone (given after allodynia was already established) failed to reduce mechanical allodynia.

The study emphasizes a clinically critical window: corticosteroids must be given **before** central sensitization is established to be effective.

## Key Contributions

1. First animal model directly simulating implant-induced IAN neuropathic pain (not a generic nerve ligation model)
2. Demonstrated mirror-image (bilateral/contralateral) pain — evidence that IAN injury drives central sensitization, not merely peripheral nociception
3. Established a timing threshold: prevention (early dexamethasone) vs. no effect (delayed dexamethasone) on established allodynia
4. Dose-response: both 25 and 50 mg/kg effective in the early treatment window

## Methodology

- **Model**: Rat — left mandibular 2nd molar extraction + miniature dental implant (mal-positioned to injure IAN)
- **Measures**: Air-puff threshold, mechanical hyperalgesia, cold and thermal hypersensitivity (ipsilateral + contralateral)
- **Drug**: Dexamethasone 25 mg/kg or 50 mg/kg i.p. daily
- **Groups**: Early treatment (pre-allodynia establishment) vs. delayed treatment (post-allodynia establishment)

## Results

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Mal-positioned implant | Bilateral allodynia + hyperalgesia + cold/thermal hypersensitivity |
| Early DEX 25 mg/kg | Prolonged anti-allodynic effect (prevented/delayed neuropathic pain) |
| Early DEX 50 mg/kg | Prolonged anti-allodynic effect |
| Delayed DEX (either dose) | No reduction in established mechanical allodynia |

**Bilateral nature of pain**: contralateral hypersensitivity suggests spinal glial activation and central sensitization as in other trigeminal neuropathy models (CCI-ION model).
