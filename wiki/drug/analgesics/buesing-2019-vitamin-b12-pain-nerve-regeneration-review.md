## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (PubMed) of animal mechanism studies and clinical trials using pharmacological doses (>100 µg) of vitamin B12 (cyanocobalamin, methylcobalamin, hydroxycobalamin) for pain treatment.

Animal studies confirm nerve regeneration, COX enzyme inhibition, TRPV1 modulation, and synergy with NSAIDs/opioids; RCTs show significant efficacy for low back pain (IM cyanocobalamin 1000 µg/day × 2 wk → pain −87%) and neuralgia (hydroxycobalamin → VAS −60%, herpetic neuralgia IM injection → ≥50% reduction in 60% of subjects).

Optimal dosing regimen remains undefined and large placebo-controlled trials are lacking, but the favorable safety profile supports consideration as adjunct therapy for neuropathic and musculoskeletal pain.

## Summary

Vitamin B12 (first isolated 1948) has been studied for pain relief since the 1950s but interest waned with pharmaceutical dominance; renewed focus driven by the opioid epidemic. This narrative review synthesizes animal mechanism studies and pharmacological-dose clinical trials across multiple pain conditions.

**Key mechanisms (animal data):**
- **Nerve regeneration**: B12 promotes axonal growth and Schwann cell differentiation in nerve crush models; upregulates BDNF; increases nerve conduction velocity
- **COX inhibition**: B12 reduces formalin-induced orofacial pain 2nd phase (COX-mediated) similarly to NSAIDs; both central and peripheral COX effects demonstrated
- **TRPV1 modulation**: Methylcobalamin reduces heat hyperalgesia via decreased TRPV1 ion influx
- **Neurotransmitter potentiation**: B1+B6+B12 combination enhances noradrenaline/5-HT inhibitory pain response; B vitamins lower homocysteine → restore neurotransmitter synthesis
- **Opioid synergy**: B12 + morphine significantly reduces morphine tolerance and dependence

**Clinical evidence summary:**

| Condition | Best result | Regimen |
|---|---|---|
| Chronic low back pain | Pain −87%, disability −82% | IM cyanocobalamin 1000 µg/day × 2 wk (RCT) |
| Compressive neuralgia | VAS −60% | Oral hydroxycobalamin 2 mg 3×/day (comparative RCT) |
| Herpetic neuralgia | ≥50% reduction in 60% of subjects | IM methylcobalamin 500 µg/day (RCT) |
| Diabetic neuropathy | Pain −70%; improved nerve conduction | Oral methylcobalamin 1500 µg/day × 3 mo (DBPC RCT) |
| Aphthous stomatitis | Pain-free 74% vs 32% placebo | Sublingual cyanocobalamin 1000 µg/day × 6 mo (RCT) |
| Chemotherapy neuralgia | Pain −23% | IM methylcobalamin 500 µg q.o.d. × 10 doses (comparative) |

**Dental relevance**: Aphthous stomatitis RCT (sublingual B12) directly relevant to oral medicine. The nerve-regeneration mechanism is applicable to iatrogenic inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) paresthesia after third molar extractions or implant surgery, though no dedicated IAN RCT exists in this review.

## Key Contributions

- Comprehensive synthesis of B12's multi-mechanism pain action (nerve regeneration + COX + TRPV1 + neurotransmitter + opioid synergy)
- Identifies pharmacological dosing threshold: >100 µg/dose required for clinical effect (dietary supplementation insufficient)
- Establishes B12 as non-opioid pain adjunct candidate with favorable safety profile
- Notes synergy of oral B-complex with diclofenac for low back pain — reduced diclofenac dose achievable

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative review; PubMed search with 4 search strings
- **Inclusion**: Primary animal mechanism studies; clinical trials using pharmacological B12 doses (>100 µg/dose)
- **No systematic protocol, PRISMA, or risk-of-bias assessment**
- **Forms reviewed**: Cyanocobalamin, methylcobalamin, hydroxycobalamin; oral, IM, sublingual, local injection

## Results

**Optimal regimen**: Not yet established. IM routes generally outperform oral in neuralgia. Oral methylcobalamin 500–1500 µg/day effective for diabetic neuropathy. IM cyanocobalamin 1000 µg/day × 2 weeks for low back pain. Sublingual 1000 µg/day × 6 months for aphthous stomatitis.

**Safety**: Vitamin B12 has an extremely low adverse effect profile. Even high-dose administration has no significant toxicity — excess B12 is renally cleared. No serious adverse events reported in any included trial.

**Limitations**: No IAN-specific dental nerve injury trial; most trials are small; many lack placebo group; study methodology heterogeneous; no standardized outcome measures across studies.
