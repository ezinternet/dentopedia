## Three-line Summary

This narrative review synthesized literature from the past 10 years (up to May 2024) on intravenous (IV) NSAIDs for perioperative pain management in adults and children, focusing on ibuprofen, ketorolac, ketoprofen, naproxen, paracetamol, and acetylsalicylic acid, with emphasis on European approvals and practice.

IV NSAIDs reduce postoperative opioid consumption by approximately 20–60%, with IV ibuprofen demonstrating a favorable profile for opioid-sparing and pain relief; head-to-head trials remain limited but available data suggest IV ibuprofen had comparable or superior efficacy to IV paracetamol and ketorolac in several surgical settings.

The review supports IV NSAIDs as a cornerstone of multimodal analgesia; however, the lack of robust head-to-head comparative RCTs and limited pediatric data (particularly for infants under 6 years) represent significant evidence gaps that future research should address.

## Summary

This narrative review provides an updated evaluation of intravenous (IV) nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for perioperative pain management in adults and children, with a focus on European-approved formulations including ibuprofen, ketorolac, ketoprofen, naproxen, paracetamol, and acetylsalicylic acid. The authors searched MEDLINE for publications from the past 10 years (up to May 2024) and synthesized data from clinical studies, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses. Key findings indicate that IV NSAIDs reduce postoperative opioid consumption by approximately 20–60%, with IV ibuprofen demonstrating a favorable efficacy and safety profile. IV ibuprofen showed comparable or superior pain relief compared to IV paracetamol in several surgical settings, with lower nausea rates and similar adverse event frequencies. In pediatrics, IV ibuprofen is approved in France for children >6 years or >20 kg, though off-label use occurs in younger children. The review highlights significant evidence gaps, including a lack of head-to-head comparative RCTs and limited pediatric data for infants. Future research priorities include direct comparisons of IV NSAIDs, standardized outcome measures, and longer observation periods. The authors conclude that IV NSAIDs remain a cornerstone of multimodal perioperative analgesia, particularly when the oral route is unavailable.

## Key Contributions

- IV NSAIDs reduce postoperative opioid consumption by 20–60% based on meta-analyses.
- IV ibuprofen shows a favorable profile for opioid-sparing and pain relief versus other NSAIDs.
- IV ibuprofen and IV paracetamol have similar safety profiles (13.7% vs 14.5% adverse events).
- IV ibuprofen is the preferred IV NSAID for pediatric patients in Europe (approved >6 years).
- Pre-emptive IV ibuprofen reduces 24-hour opioid consumption by up to 45%.
- Fixed-dose combination of IV ibuprofen + paracetamol provides superior analgesia to either alone.
- Continuous infusion may offer additional opioid-sparing benefits over bolus administration.

## Methodology

This is a narrative review, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. The authors searched MEDLINE via PubMed using logical keyword combinations covering IV NSAIDs, pain, surgery, and perioperative care, focusing on publications from the 10 years prior to May 2024. French-language publications in the investigators' personal collections were also considered. Titles and abstracts were screened by one investigator (A.M.-S.), and full-text versions of relevant publications were assessed by all investigators. Case reports, conference abstracts, oral abstracts, in vitro studies, and publications without available full-text were excluded. The review is centered on European (particularly French) clinical practice and regulatory approvals. No formal quality assessment, data extraction protocol, or meta-analytic synthesis was performed, consistent with the narrative review design.
