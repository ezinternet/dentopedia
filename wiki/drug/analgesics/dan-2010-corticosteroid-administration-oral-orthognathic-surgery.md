## Three-line Summary
Systematic review + meta-analysis pooling clinical trials on corticosteroid (CS) administration for edema, pain, and infection risk in oral (dental extraction) surgery, plus orthognathic surgery outcomes (edema, neuroregeneration) and a narrative review of other side effects.

CS significantly reduced oral-surgery edema and pain (both P<.0001) with no significant increase in infection risk (RR 1.0041, P=.89); orthognathic-surgery edema responded to IV methylprednisolone ≥85mg, with a suggested but statistically untested neuroregeneration benefit.

CS use in oral surgery carries only a minimal chronic adrenal-suppression risk, while orthognathic surgery carries elevated risks of avascular osteonecrosis, steroid-induced psychosis, and adrenal suppression — but this page is built from the structured abstract only, so pooled effect sizes, CIs, and heterogeneity statistics are not available.

## Summary
This 2010 systematic review and meta-analysis (Dan, Thygesen & Pinholt, J Oral Maxillofac Surg 68(9):2207-2220) pooled clinical trials evaluating corticosteroid (CS) administration in oral surgery (dental extraction) and orthognathic surgery. In oral surgery, CS produced statistically significant reductions in both edema and pain (P<.0001 for each), with no significant increase in infection risk (relative risk 1.0041, P=.89) — addressing the standard clinical objection to perioperative steroid use with a pooled null result. Local methylprednisolone injection ≥25mg was expected to produce significant edema reduction. In orthognathic surgery, intravenous methylprednisolone ≥85mg appeared sufficient to significantly reduce edema, and several trials suggested a neuroregeneration effect, though the authors explicitly note no statistical analysis could be performed on that signal. Side-effect profiles diverged sharply by surgical context: oral surgery carried only a minimal risk of chronic adrenal suppression, while orthognathic surgery carried elevated risks of avascular osteonecrosis, steroid-induced psychosis, and adrenal suppression, with no reports of decreased wound healing in either setting. This wiki page is built entirely from the paper's structured abstract (the full text sits behind a publisher paywall), so pooled effect-size point estimates, confidence intervals, heterogeneity statistics, and the included-study count are not captured here — see Limitations.

## Key Contributions
- Meta-analytically confirms (P<.0001) that CS reduces both edema and pain after oral surgery, upgrading the evidence tier above any single trial.
- Quantifies the infection-risk counterargument directly: RR 1.0041, P=.89 — a pooled, non-significant result rather than one underpowered trial's null finding.
- Reports rough dose thresholds from the pooled literature: local methylprednisolone ≥25mg (oral surgery edema) and IV methylprednisolone ≥85mg (orthognathic surgery edema).
- Extends the evidence base into orthognathic surgery, with a distinct (more severe) side-effect signature — avascular osteonecrosis and steroid-induced psychosis — not seen in the oral-surgery arm, plus a preliminary neuroregeneration signal.

## Methodology
Systematic review of the literature with meta-analysis (SR+MA) for the edema/pain/infection outcomes in oral and orthognathic surgery; the broader side-effect profile (adrenal suppression, avascular osteonecrosis, psychosis, wound healing) was assessed through a non-pooled narrative ("simple") review. Primary predictor variable: CS administration (presence/dose). Outcome variables: edema, pain, infection. Databases searched and total n (studies/patients) are not stated in the retrieved abstract — those details are in the paywalled full text.
