## Three-line Summary

This umbrella review included 16 systematic reviews (all based on randomized controlled trials) to evaluate celecoxib safety in osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, and ankylosing spondylitis, searching MEDLINE, Cochrane Central, and Scopus through November 2024. Celecoxib demonstrated superior gastrointestinal safety compared with non-selective NSAIDs, with a pooled risk ratio for gastroduodenal ulcers of 0.22 (95% CI 0.15–0.32, I² = 0%) from one high-quality meta-analysis; cardiovascular outcomes were generally similar to non-selective NSAIDs, although one meta-analysis showed reduced cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.57–0.99, p = 0.04) with celecoxib, and renal adverse events appeared lower with celecoxib than placebo (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.66–0.94, p < 0.05).

Despite consistent gastrointestinal safety advantages, most included reviews (14/16) were rated critically low quality by AMSTAR-2, and evidence certainty was downgraded to moderate for gastroduodenal ulcers and low for all other outcomes due to risk of bias, imprecision, and inconsistency. The authors recommend individualized risk-benefit assessment and future integration of both randomized and observational data to better inform long-term safety in high-risk populations.

## Summary

This umbrella review systematically synthesized evidence from 16 systematic reviews and meta-analyses (all based on randomized controlled trials) evaluating the safety of celecoxib in adults with osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, or ankylosing spondylitis. A comprehensive literature search of MEDLINE, Cochrane Central, and Scopus was conducted through November 2024, following PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Methodological quality was assessed using AMSTAR-2, and evidence certainty was graded using GRADE. Celecoxib was consistently associated with a lower risk of gastroduodenal ulcers compared with non-selective NSAIDs (pooled RR 0.22, 95% CI 0.15–0.32, I² = 0%; moderate certainty evidence), and some studies reported fewer gastrointestinal complaints and serious events. Cardiovascular safety outcomes were generally similar to non-selective NSAIDs, though one meta-analysis showed lower cardiovascular mortality with celecoxib (RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.57–0.99). Compared with placebo or non-selective NSAIDs, celecoxib did not increase renal dysfunction risk and may be associated with fewer renal adverse events. All-cause mortality evidence was limited and inconsistent. However, most included reviews (14/16) were rated as critically low quality by AMSTAR-2, and GRADE certainty was low for most outcomes. The authors conclude that while celecoxib offers superior gastrointestinal safety, evidence for other outcomes remains limited and of low certainty, emphasizing the need for individualized risk-benefit assessment and future integration of randomized and observational data.

## Key Contributions

- Celecoxib demonstrated superior gastrointestinal safety, with 78% lower risk of gastroduodenal ulcers compared with non-selective NSAIDs (RR 0.22, 95% CI 0.15–0.32, I² = 0%)
- Cardiovascular outcomes were generally comparable between celecoxib and non-selective NSAIDs, but one meta-analysis showed reduced cardiovascular mortality with celecoxib (RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.57–0.99, p = 0.04)
- Renal composite events (dysfunction, edema, hypertension) were lower with celecoxib than placebo (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.66–0.94)
- PRECISION trial findings suggest celecoxib may have superior composite major toxicity profile compared with ibuprofen and naproxen
- Most included systematic reviews (14/16) were critically low methodological quality, limiting confidence in findings
- Evidence certainty (GRADE) was moderate only for gastroduodenal ulcers; all other outcomes were low certainty

## Methodology

This umbrella review followed PRISMA 2020 guidelines and the Joanna Briggs Institute manual for umbrella reviews, with a protocol registered on Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/jn52b/). A comprehensive literature search of MEDLINE (Ovid), Cochrane Central Systematic Reviews (Ovid), and Scopus was conducted in November 2024, supplemented by manual bibliography searches and expert consultation. Two independent reviewers screened titles/abstracts and full texts for eligibility. Included studies were systematic reviews (with or without meta-analysis) reporting celecoxib safety in adults with osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, or ankylosing spondylitis, with any dose or treatment duration, published in peer-reviewed journals. Of 2,294 identified records, 16 systematic reviews met inclusion criteria (all based on RCTs), published between 2001 and 2021. Quality appraisal used AMSTAR-2 (two reviewers), with studies categorized as high, moderate, low, or critically low quality; only 2 studies were rated high quality, 14 were critically low. Data synthesis used narrative synthesis with GRADE adapted for umbrella reviews to assess evidence certainty for each outcome. Comparators included placebo (13 studies), non-selective NSAIDs (13 studies), and NSAIDs plus proton pump inhibitors (2 studies).

For gastrointestinal complaints, results were mixed: some meta-analyses showed celecoxib caused fewer abdominal and GI complaints than non-selective NSAIDs; others showed no difference or higher risk than placebo. GI bleeding/perforation/obstruction findings were also inconsistent. All-cause mortality was reduced with celecoxib vs non-selective NSAIDs in one meta-analysis but not supported by another.
