## Three-line Summary

Animal study (32 male Wistar rats, machined-surface Ti implant in tibia): four groups received 42 days of pre-surgical daily gavage of saline vs low-dose aspirin (6.75 mg/kg ≈ human 75-80 mg), then were assessed at 7 or 28 days post-implant by histomorphometry (BIC, BABT).

Aspirin significantly reduced bone-to-implant contact at 7 days (62.8% vs 82.7%, p<0.05) but the difference vanished by 28 days (91.9% vs 89.9%, NS); bone area between threads followed the same transient pattern.

Low-dose aspirin causes only a discrete, self-limiting inhibition of the early phase of osseointegration and does not contraindicate implants; caution is warranted mainly for aggressive early loading.

## 세줄요약

동물 연구 (수컷 Wistar 랫 32마리, 경골에 기계가공 표면 티타늄 임플란트): 4개 군에 이식 42일 전부터 생리식염수 vs 저용량 아스피린 (Low-dose Aspirin, 6.75 mg/kg ≈ 인체 75-80 mg)을 매일 경구 투여하고 이식 후 7일·28일에 조직형태계측 (Histomorphometry)으로 골-임플란트 접촉률 (Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC)과 나사산 사이 골면적 (Bone Area Between Threads, BABT)을 측정했다.

아스피린은 7일째 BIC를 유의하게 낮췄으나 (62.8% vs 82.7%, p<0.05) 28일째에는 차이가 사라졌고 (91.9% vs 89.9%, 유의차 없음), BABT도 동일한 일시적 패턴을 보였다.

저용량 아스피린은 골유착 (Osseointegration) 초기 단계만 경미하게·일시적으로 억제할 뿐 임플란트 금기가 아니며, 주의는 주로 공격적인 조기 하중 (Early Loading)에 국한된다.

## Summary

Chronic low-dose aspirin — the cardioprotective dose taken by a growing share of implant candidates — was tested for its effect on osseointegration in a controlled rat model. Because COX-derived prostaglandins participate in early bone healing, the concern is that antiplatelet-dose aspirin might impair bone forming around a fresh implant. The study answers with a nuanced result: there IS an early deficit, but it is discrete and fully self-corrects. At one week the aspirin group had markedly lower bone-to-implant contact, but by four weeks it was indistinguishable from controls. The practical message is that low-dose aspirin is not a reason to withhold implants; it is a reason to be cautious about aggressive loading in the very first phase of healing.

## Key Contributions

- Isolates the specifically cardioprotective LOW dose (≈75-80 mg human equivalent), distinct from analgesic/anti-inflammatory aspirin doses studied previously.
- Chronic-exposure design (42-day pre-surgical dosing) mimics a patient already on long-term aspirin, not an acute peri-operative dose.
- Time-resolved histomorphometry (7 vs 28 days) demonstrates that the effect is a transient early-phase deficit, not permanent impairment.

## Methodology

- 32 male Wistar rats, 4 groups of 8 (CG-7, AG-7, CG-28, AG-28); ARRIVE guidelines.
- Aspirin 6.75 mg/kg/day by oral gavage (from human 75-80 mg via FDA body-surface-area conversion) vs saline; dosing began 42 days before surgery.
- Machined commercially pure titanium implant, 1.5 mm × 8.0 mm, placed in the tibia.
- Endpoints by undecalcified histomorphometry: bone-to-implant contact (BIC) and bone area between threads (BABT), at 7 and 28 days.

## Results

| Parameter | Timepoint | Aspirin | Control | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BIC | 7 days | AG-7 62.8% ± 17.1 | CG-7 82.7% ± 15.2 | significantly reduced (p < 0.05) |
| BIC | 28 days | AG-28 91.9% ± 5.4 | CG-28 89.9% ± 9.7 | no difference (recovered) |
| BABT | 7 days | AG-7 70.9% ± 15.2 | higher | trend lower |
| BABT | 28 days | AG-28 95.4% ± 3.7 | comparable | recovered |

- Both BIC and BABT increased from 7 to 28 days within every group (normal maturation).
- The inhibition was confined to the early (7-day) stage; by 28 days no effect was detectable — a transient, self-limiting early-phase deficit.
- Proposed mechanism: COX inhibition lowers PGE2 (early vasodilation/angiogenesis), with reduced BMP-2, CBFA-1/Runx2 and VEGF signaling; aspirin also suppresses osteoclast formation via RANKL, so the four-week remodeling balance allows full catch-up.
