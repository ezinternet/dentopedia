## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of ibuprofen pharmacology and clinical use in dentistry. Ibuprofen is a nonselective COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor with analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and antipyretic effects — the most widely studied NSAID for postoperative dental pain. Covers mechanism, dosing, safety, drug interactions, and dental-specific applications (extraction, implant, periodontal, orthodontic, bleaching).

## Summary

This narrative review by Pozzi and Gallelli provides a comprehensive overview of ibuprofen's role in dental pain management. The authors describe ibuprofen's mechanism as a nonselective cyclooxygenase (COX) inhibitor that blocks both COX-1 (constitutive, GI/renal/platelet) and COX-2 (inducible, inflammatory) enzymes, reducing prostaglandin synthesis at the site of tissue injury. Beyond classical COX inhibition, ibuprofen also inhibits fatty acid amide hydrolase (FAAH), increasing endocannabinoid (anandamide) levels and producing synergistic analgesia via CB1/CB2 receptors. The review surveys clinical evidence across dental disciplines: third molar extraction pain (ibuprofen 400 mg superior to acetaminophen 1000 mg), implant surgery postoperative pain, periodontal scaling and root planing pain, orthodontic separator discomfort, and bleaching-induced tooth sensitivity. Dosing ranges from 200-400 mg OTC for mild-moderate pain to 600-800 mg prescription for severe pain, with a maximum of 3200 mg/day. The safety profile is favorable compared to COX-2 selective inhibitors (lower cardiovascular risk) and aspirin (lower GI toxicity), though drug interactions with aspirin, ACE inhibitors, lithium, methotrexate, and warfarin require caution.

## Key Contributions

- Comprehensive pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profile of ibuprofen in dental context
- Evidence review across five dental disciplines: oral surgery, implantology, periodontics, orthodontics, cosmetic dentistry
- Preemptive analgesia concept applied to dentistry — preoperative dosing reduces postoperative pain and rescue medication
- Safety comparison: ibuprofen vs COX-2 inhibitors (cardiovascular), ibuprofen vs aspirin (GI), ibuprofen vs opioids (addiction/sedation)
- Drug interaction table for dental prescriptions (anticoagulants, antihypertensives, lithium, methotrexate)

## Methodology

- Design: Narrative review
- Scope: English and Italian literature on ibuprofen pharmacology and dental applications
- No systematic search strategy reported
- No meta-analysis; qualitative synthesis of published RCTs and reviews

## Results

- **Pharmacokinetics**: T½ = 2.1 h; Tmax < 15 min (granules), ~2 h (tablets); 50-60% R(-) → S(+) enantiomer conversion
- **Postoperative pain (third molar)**: Ibuprofen 400 mg > acetaminophen 1000 mg; onset 20 min for soluble formulation
- **Preemptive analgesia**: Pre-op ibuprofen 400-600 mg reduces postoperative pain scores and analgesic consumption
- **Implant surgery**: NSAIDs reduce post-op edema and pain; ibuprofen 1200 mg/day for 3 days significantly suppresses edema at 48 h
- **Periodontal**: Ibuprofen arginine 600 mg reduces SRP pain vs placebo
- **Orthodontic**: Pre-op ibuprofen reduces separator placement pain
- **Bleaching**: Pre-operative ibuprofen reduces bleaching-induced sensitivity
- **Safety**: GI tolerability better than aspirin; cardiovascular risk lower than celecoxib/rofecoxib

## Limitations

- Narrative review — no systematic methodology, potential selection bias
- Published 2011; does not include recent evidence on newer formulations or comparative effectiveness
- Primarily Western (European/US) literature; limited pharmacogenomic data
