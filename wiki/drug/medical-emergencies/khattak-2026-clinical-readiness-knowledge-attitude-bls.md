## Three-line Summary

A cross-sectional survey of 400 Saudi dentists found only 26% had good BLS/CPR knowledge, 7.5% had an AED available, and 44.5% had an emergency protocol in their clinic. Recent workshop attendance (aOR 4.91) and prior emergency experience (aOR 12.97) were the dominant predictors of good knowledge; the same workshop predictor was even stronger for CPR confidence (aOR 6.93). Findings support mandatory refresher training and clinic-level AED provision to bridge the attitude-behavior gap in dental emergency preparedness.

## Summary

This PeerJ cross-sectional study surveyed 400 licensed dentists across Saudi Arabia (Sep–Oct 2024) using a validated 27-item instrument assessing BLS/CPR knowledge (9 MCQs), attitudes, and clinical readiness/confidence. Only 26% achieved good knowledge scores and a mere 7.5% of clinics had an AED on-site. Attitudes were strongly positive — 90.5% felt morally obligated to respond to emergencies — highlighting a significant attitude-behavior gap. Binary logistic regression identified BLS/CPR workshop attendance within the last 5 years (aOR 4.91) and prior emergency encounter (aOR 12.97) as the two dominant modifiable predictors of good knowledge, while demographic variables (age, gender, designation, years of experience) were non-significant. For CPR confidence, workshop attendance again dominated (aOR 6.93), with female gender associated with higher confidence (inverse of male aOR 0.59). The authors recommend mandatory periodic refresher training, curricular reinforcement in dental schools, and enforceable clinic-level AED availability standards.

## Key Contributions

1. Establishes baseline BLS/CPR preparedness data for Saudi dentists with multivariate predictor analysis (n=400, 2024).
2. Documents AED availability at only 7.5% of dental clinics — the most critical equipment gap.
3. Confirms training recency, not seniority or specialty, as the decisive predictor of both knowledge and confidence.
4. Adds a Middle East/Gulf region data point to the growing international literature on dental emergency preparedness.
5. Quantifies attitude-behavior gap: 74% support mandatory BLS for licensure yet <45% have clinic emergency protocols.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Cross-sectional; structured self-administered online questionnaire
- **Period**: September–October 2024
- **Sampling**: Convenience (professional associations, social media, direct clinic contact); n=400 (minimum 385 by power calculation)
- **Instrument**: 27 items; 4 sections — sociodemographics, knowledge (9 MCQ), attitudes (4 dichotomous), clinical readiness/confidence (8 items); validated; Cronbach's α: knowledge 0.78, attitude 0.81, readiness 0.76
- **Knowledge scoring**: Low 1–3, Moderate 4–6, Good 7–9 (out of 9)
- **Analysis**: SPSS v26; descriptive + chi-square/t-test/ANOVA; binary logistic regression (2 models)
