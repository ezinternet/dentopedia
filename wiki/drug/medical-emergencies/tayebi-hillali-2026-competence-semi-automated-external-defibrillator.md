## Three-line Summary

Final-year dental students at the University of Santiago de Compostela showed critically deficient AED competence under conventional voice instructions (Phase 1, n=42): 8/10 checklist items were completed by <10% of students, and median time to shock delivery was 143 s — well above the accepted 90 s threshold. Modified 80-second instructions (Phase 2, n=39) yielded significant improvement in 7/10 items and reduced shock time to 116 s (−27 s, p<0.001), but post-shock CPR resumption remained poor in both phases (9.5% vs 7.6%, p=1.000). The authors conclude that optimized voice instructions improve task performance but cannot substitute for structured AED training integrated into the dental curriculum.

## 세줄요약

스페인 산티아고 데 콤포스텔라 대학교 치과 최종학년을 대상으로 한 시뮬레이션 기반 평가 (Simulation-Based Assessment)에서 기존 음성안내 조건(Phase 1, n=42)의 반자동 외부제세동기 (Semi-Automated External Defibrillator, AED) 실기 역량은 심각하게 부족했다: 10개 항목 중 8개에서 이행률 <10%, 제세동 (Defibrillation) 전달 중앙값 143초(허용 기준 90초 초과). 수정 음성안내(80초, Phase 2, n=39)는 7개 항목에서 유의한 개선과 쇼크 시간 116초(-27초, p<0.001)를 달성했으나, 쇼크 후 심폐소생술 (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation, CPR) 재개는 양 단계 모두 극히 저조(9.5% vs 7.6%)했다. 저자들은 최적화된 음성안내가 수행 능력을 향상시키나 정규 교육과정 내 구조화된 AED 훈련을 대체할 수 없다고 결론지었다.

## Summary

Simulation-based two-cohort study at the University of Santiago de Compostela (Spain) assessing practical AED competence in final-year dental students. Phase 1 (n=42, 2023–24) used conventional Philips HeartStartFRx voice instructions; Phase 2 (n=39, 2024–25) used modified 80-second instructions developed from Phase 1 error analysis.

**Context**: Europe experiences 350,000 out-of-hospital cardiac arrests annually with survival <10%. Dental practice incidence is ~0.003 cardiac arrest events per dentist per year — rare but catastrophic without prompt defibrillation. ILCOR accepts ≤90 s (ideally <60 s) as threshold for first shock delivery.

**Phase 1 baseline**: median time-to-shock 143 s (95% CI 129–156 s); battery check 0%; power on 4.8%; electrode placement 7.1%. Only electrode connection to monitor (80.9%) and shock delivery (59.5%) exceeded minimal thresholds. >85% of students had no university-led BLS/AED training during their degree.

**Phase 2 outcome**: Modified instructions reduced shock delivery to 116 s (−27 s, p<0.001) and significantly improved 7/10 items. However, post-shock CPR resumption (9.5% → 7.6%, p=1.000) and 2-minute reevaluation (9.5% → 0%, p=0.117) showed no improvement — persisting gaps that require active training.

## Key Contributions

1. Objective OSCE measurement of AED performance (vs survey-based self-report) quantifying actual skill gaps in graduating dental students
2. First controlled evidence that optimizing AED voice instruction duration (22 s → 80 s) significantly shortens time-to-defibrillation in dental students
3. Identification of an irreducible post-shock CPR resumption deficit unresponsive to instruction optimization alone
4. Evidence base for mandatory AED curriculum integration: prior self-directed training (14.2%) did not confer competence

## Methodology

**Design**: Prospective simulation-based assessment; OSCE format; two sequential cohorts (not RCT).

**Equipment**: Philips HeartStartFRx training AED; Laerdal resuscitation manikin with wet T-shirt and metallic necklace (realistic impedance simulation); scissors, absorbent paper provided.

**Assessment**: 10-item checklist across 3 domains:

| Domain | Items |
|---|---|
| Preparation | Turn on AED; Check battery; Follow verbal instructions; Connect electrodes to monitor |
| Electrode placement & shock delivery | Place electrodes on bare dry chest; Remove rescuers during analysis; Ensure no contact before shock; Deliver shock |
| Post-shock actions | Immediately resume CPR; Reevaluate patient at 2 minutes |

**Competence criterion**: 100% correct completion of all critical steps.

**Statistics**: Chi-square / Fisher's exact for proportions; Mann-Whitney U for non-parametric time data.

## Results

| Checklist Item | Phase 1 (n=42) | Phase 2 (n=39) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power On | 4.7% | 100% | <0.001 |
| Check Battery | 0% | 61.5% | <0.001 |
| Follow Instructions | 4.7% | 97.4% | <0.001 |
| Electrode Connection to Monitor | 80.9% | 100% | 0.006 |
| Electrode Placement on Patient | 7.1% | 87.1% | <0.001 |
| Remove Rescuers During Analysis | 14.2% | 87.1% | <0.001 |
| No Contact Before Shock | 7.1% | 46.1% | <0.001 |
| Deliver Shock | 59.5% | 76.9% | 0.150 |
| **Resume CPR (post-shock)** | **9.5%** | **7.6%** | **1.000** |
| Reevaluation at 2 min | 9.5% | 0% | 0.117 |
| **Median time to shock (s)** | **143 (95%CI 129–156)** | **116 (95%CI 107–124)** | **<0.001** |

**Prior BLS/AED training**: Phase 1 14.2% (6/42); Phase 2 7.6% (3/39). Prior training did not correlate with competence.

**Sex**: No significant differences in overall performance between male and female students in either phase.
