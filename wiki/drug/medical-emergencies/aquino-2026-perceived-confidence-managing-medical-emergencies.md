## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study (n=100 dental students, Brazil) evaluated the relationship between technical knowledge, practical training, and emotional intelligence (EI) with self-reported confidence in managing medical emergencies.

82% of students did not feel prepared; 91% could not administer injectable medications; students who reported satisfactory technical and practical training showed significantly higher confidence (p < 0.01); EI significantly predicted diagnostic preparedness (p = 0.04).

Dental emergency preparedness is multidimensional: technical training and EI are both independent predictors of confidence — purely cognitive curricula are insufficient; emotionally intelligent students are better equipped to translate knowledge into confident action.

## 세줄요약

단면연구 (n=100, 브라질 치과대학생) — 기술 지식·실기 훈련·감성지능 (Emotional Intelligence, EI)과 응급 처치 자기 확신도 (Perceived Confidence) 관계 분석.

82%가 응급 처치 미준비 느낌; 91%가 주사용 약물 투여 불가; 충분한 기술+실기 훈련이 있는 학생은 자신감 유의하게 높음 (p<0.01); EI는 응급 진단 준비도를 유의하게 예측 (p=0.04).

치과 응급 준비도는 다차원적 — 기술 훈련과 감성지능 양쪽이 독립 예측 변수; 인지적 교육만으로는 임상 응급 대응 확신도 형성에 불충분.

## Study Design

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Observational cross-sectional analytical study |
| n | 100 undergraduate dental students in clinical courses |
| Setting | Universidade Estadual da Paraíba (UEPB), Brazil |
| Instruments | Sociodemographic questionnaire + emergency preparation instrument + TEIQue-SF |
| EI measurement | TEIQue-SF (Trait Emotional Intelligence Questionnaire–Short Form; validated for Brazil) |
| Analysis | Fisher's exact test, Student's t-test; SPSS v.25.0 |

## Key Results

| Outcome | Finding |
|---|---|
| Not prepared to manage emergencies | 82% |
| Unable to diagnose emergency complications | 61% |
| Cannot administer injectable medications | 91% |
| Mean EI score (TEIQue-SF) | 5.06 ± 0.49 |
| Satisfactory training → confidence | p < 0.01 (significant) |
| EI → diagnostic preparedness | p = 0.04 (significant) |

## Emotional Intelligence and Emergency Confidence — Mechanism

- Students who felt able to diagnose emergencies had significantly higher EI scores
- EI regulates emotional responses in high-stress clinical situations
- High EI likely reduces panic and enables structured emergency protocols even when knowledge is incomplete
- Practical training without EI component may leave students cognitively prepared but emotionally unprepared

## Limitations

- Single institution; n=100; limited generalizability
- Cross-sectional — cannot determine causality
- Self-reported confidence only; no objective skill or behavioral assessment
- TEIQue-SF is a general trait EI measure, not dental-emergency-specific
- EI and confidence correlation may reflect a common underlying factor (academic engagement)
