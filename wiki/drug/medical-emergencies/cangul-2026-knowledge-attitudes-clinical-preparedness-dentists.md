## Three-line Summary

Nationwide cross-sectional survey (n=300 Turkish practicing dentists; 66.6% response rate) using 16-scenario knowledge MCQ + Likert preparedness scale found median knowledge score 11/16 with correct performance on common emergencies (syncope, bleeding) but critical gaps in anaphylaxis, hypertensive crisis, and epileptic seizures.

Only 40% of dentists felt sufficiently competent to manage medical emergencies; avoidance of high-risk patients was common among the low-preparedness group; higher knowledge scores and emergency equipment availability were significantly associated with greater clinical confidence.

Theoretical knowledge of common dental emergencies is adequate, but competence in managing rare high-acuity events requires targeted hands-on simulation training and clinic-level emergency resource investment.

## 세줄요약

전국 단면연구 (n=300, 터키 개원 치과의사, 응답률 66.6%) — 16문항 지식 점수 중앙값 11점, 실신·구강내 출혈 등 흔한 응급은 높은 정답률, 아나필락시스 (Anaphylaxis)·고혈압 위기 (Hypertensive Crisis)·뇌전증 발작 (Epileptic Seizure)에서 결정적 격차.

응급 처치 역량을 느끼는 치과의사 40%에 불과; 저준비도 집단에서 고위험 환자 기피 (Avoidance Behavior) 흔함; 지식 점수 높고 응급 장비 구비 시 자기 역량감 유의하게 증가.

이론적 지식은 흔한 응급에서 충분하나, 고위험·저빈도 응급 관리에는 체계적 실습 시뮬레이션 훈련과 클리닉 수준의 응급 자원 투자가 필요.

## Study Design

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Nationwide cross-sectional survey |
| n | 300 (invited 450; 66.6% response rate) |
| Population | Practicing dentists, Turkey |
| Knowledge instrument | 16 scenario-based single-best-answer MCQs covering medical emergencies |
| Preparedness instrument | Likert-scale questionnaire — attitudes and clinical preparedness |
| Analysis | Chi-square tests with effect size (Cramér's V) |
| Categorization | Knowledge level groups; preparedness subgroups |

## Key Results

| Domain | Finding |
|---|---|
| Overall knowledge | Median 11/16 (IQR 9–13) |
| High correct responses | Syncope, intraoral bleeding |
| Low correct responses | Anaphylaxis, hypertensive crisis, epileptic seizures |
| Self-perceived competence | 40% feel sufficiently competent |
| Avoidance of high-risk patients | Common in low-preparedness group |
| Equipment + high knowledge → confidence | Significantly associated (chi-square, p < 0.05) |

## Preparedness Gap Analysis

| Emergency Type | Correct Rate | Clinical Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Vasovagal syncope | High | Low-acuity |
| Intraoral hemorrhage | High | Manageable |
| Anaphylaxis | **Low** | **Life-threatening** |
| Hypertensive crisis | **Low** | **High-acuity** |
| Epileptic seizures | **Low** | **Medium-high acuity** |

The competence-knowledge dissociation: dentists who know emergency theory still feel unprepared — suggesting simulation-based practical exposure is the missing link.

## Clinical Implications

1. **Equipment investment**: Clinics with emergency drugs + AED → dentists feel more competent; structural readiness supports clinical confidence
2. **Curriculum gap**: Anaphylaxis, hypertensive emergencies, and seizures need dedicated simulation modules beyond didactic teaching
3. **Avoidance problem**: Low preparedness drives patient avoidance — a patient safety issue; not just a training deficit but a care access problem
4. **Continuing education priority**: Rare high-acuity emergencies must be the focus of CPD (continuing professional development)

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional; no causal inference
- Turkey only; training patterns differ internationally
- Self-reported preparedness — social desirability bias possible
- Knowledge MCQ scenario-based but not validated against objective skill observation
