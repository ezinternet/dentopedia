## Three-line Summary

Two-group comparative study (Cureus, n=40) placing 3D Bond biphasic calcium sulfate (BCS) cement plus a single suture and compression into extraction sockets of 20 patients on **uninterrupted** vitamin K antagonist (VKA) therapy (24 extractions, mean INR 2.54), against a **historical 2005–2006 control group** of 20 patients whose anticoagulation was **discontinued** with low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) bridging and who received standard collagen sponge compression (24 extractions, mean INR 1.25).

No bleeding in 19/20 (95%) of the cement arm versus 16/20 (80%) of the collagen arm; the single cement-arm bleed was grade I (one day of tranexamic acid rinse) while all four control-arm bleeds were grade II (resuturing, dressing change, three days of tranexamic acid), with mean visual analog scale (VAS) pain 34 mm versus 46 mm.

The 95%-versus-80% gap **cannot be attributed to the cement**: the arms differ simultaneously in hemostatic material, anticoagulant status, treatment era, antibiotic protocol, and suture type, and the difference was statistically non-significant (p = 0.342) — this page records a feasibility observation about a candidate material, not evidence that BCS outperforms collagen.

## Summary

This Cureus study asks whether 3D Bond (Augma Biomaterials, Katzir, Israel), a biphasic calcium sulfate (BCS) regenerative bone cement normally used for socket preservation, can also serve as a local hemostatic agent in patients undergoing oral surgery on chronic vitamin K antagonist (VKA) therapy. Twenty consecutive patients (mean age 65.5 y; acenocoumarol 9, warfarin 11; indications including atrial fibrillation, mechanical valves, CABG, and pulmonary embolism) underwent 24 atraumatic extractions **without interrupting anticoagulation**, at a mean international normalized ratio (INR) of 2.54 (range 1.80–3.34). Sockets were curetted, packed and compacted with 0.5–1.0 cm³ of BCS cement, covered with a single compressed collagen sponge, and closed with single polydioxanone sutures. The comparison arm is a **historical cohort treated in 2005–2006**: 20 patients whose VKA was discontinued and bridged with enoxaparin 40–80 mg/day until INR ≤ 1.5, receiving standard collagen dressing (Cutanplast Dental) and polyglycolic acid sutures, plus universal amoxicillin/clavulanate prophylaxis. Bleeding was scored on a 3-point clinician-judged scale at 60 minutes and days 2, 3, 5, 7, and 10.

The reported outcome — 95% bleeding-free in the cement arm versus 80% in the collagen arm — is presented by the authors as evidence for BCS hemostatic efficacy, but the study design does not support that inference. **The two arms differ in at least five variables at once**, and the arm that supposedly performed better is also the arm that remained fully anticoagulated. The paper's own statistics confirm the caution: the group-by-bleeding comparison was non-significant (Fisher's exact, p = 0.342). What this study establishes is narrower and still worth holding: a BCS-cement protocol was **feasible** in 20 uninterrupted-VKA patients, including mechanical-valve cases at therapeutic INR, with one grade I bleed managed by an outpatient tranexamic acid rinse. The proposed mechanism — in-situ hardening occluding cancellous-bone microvasculature, plus calcium ions acting as a coagulation cofactor — is stated by the authors as a thesis awaiting in vitro validation, not a demonstrated finding. The concurrent socket-preservation claim is likewise unsupported here: no radiographic or histomorphometric analysis was performed.

## Key Contributions

- Places biphasic calcium sulfate (BCS) cement on the map as a candidate **dual-purpose** socket material — hemostatic plug and socket-preservation graft — extending the local hemostatic agent menu beyond collagen, oxidized cellulose, gelatin sponge, and fibrin adhesives.
- Documents feasibility of a BCS protocol in 20 patients extracted on therapeutic uninterrupted VKA (mean INR 2.54), including mechanical aortic and mitral valve patients, with a single grade I bleed.
- Articulates a two-part mechanistic hypothesis: mechanical sealing of socket cancellous-bone microvessels by the in-situ hardening cement, plus calcium ion release supporting prothrombin-to-thrombin conversion. Both remain untested here.
- Reports secondary signals — lower VAS pain (mean 34 vs 46 mm) and no impaired wound healing in the cement arm — that are hypothesis-generating only, being subject to the same confounding and reported without dispersion statistics or significance tests.

## Methodology

- **Design**: two-group comparative clinical study; prospective study arm vs **retrospective historical control arm (2005–2006)**. Not randomized, not blinded, no power calculation, no sample-size justification.
- **Study arm (n = 20; 9 F / 11 M; 24 extractions)**: VKA **continued**. Acenocoumarol 4.9 ± 1.76 mg/day (n=9), warfarin 6.8 ± 2.52 mg/day (n=11). Mean age 65.5 ± 12.65 y; INR 2.54 ± 0.43.
- **Control arm (n = 20; 11 F / 9 M; 24 extractions)**: VKA **discontinued**, bridged with enoxaparin 40–80 mg/day (mean 62.00 ± 15.76) until INR ≤ 1.5. Mean age 64.25 ± 9.11 y; INR 1.25 ± 0.17.
- **Shared surgical protocol**: 4% articaine local anesthesia; atraumatic removal of erupted teeth, impacted teeth, and roots; one or two teeth per session; socket curettage of inflamed tissue.
- **Study-arm socket**: 3D Bond BCS cement compacted with dry sterile gauze and crestal compression (median 1.0 cm³, range 0.5–1.0), single compressed collagen sponge cover, single **polydioxanone** 4-0/5-0 resorbable monofilament sutures; graft exposure permitted. Antibiotics withheld except for mechanical-valve and impacted-tooth patients.
- **Control-arm socket**: collagen dressing only (Cutanplast Dental; median 4 sponges/patient), **polyglycolic acid** sutures, and **universal** amoxicillin/clavulanate two-shot prophylaxis (2.0 g pre-op, 1.0 g post-op).
- **Bleeding scale (clinician-judged, unblinded)**: 0 = none; I = seepage needing tranexamic acid mouthwash drops for 3–5 days; II = bleeding needing larger tranexamic acid volume, local re-intervention, ± systemic vitamin K 5–10 mg.
- **Assessment**: 60 minutes and days 2, 3, 5, 7, 10. Pain by 100 mm VAS; healing on a 2-point visual scale. Sutures removed at 10 days.
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk; Student's t-test (Cochran-Cox when variances unequal); Mann-Whitney U; chi-squared with Fisher's adjustment; alpha = 0.05. One- or two-tailed regions chosen per hypothesis without per-test specification.

## Results

- **Bleeding**: study arm 19/20 (95%) bleeding-free vs control arm 16/20 (80%). **The between-group difference was not statistically significant (Fisher's exact, p = 0.342)** — the study's own Table 12. Overall cohort bleeding 5/40 (12.5%).
- **Interpretation constraint — the confound.** The 95%-vs-80% contrast is **not attributable to the cement**. The arms differ simultaneously in (1) hemostatic material (BCS vs collagen sponge), (2) anticoagulant status (continued at INR 2.54 vs discontinued+LMWH-bridged at INR 1.25), (3) treatment era (contemporary vs 2005–2006), (4) antibiotic protocol (withheld in most study patients vs universal in controls), and (5) suture material (polydioxanone vs polyglycolic acid). Critically, the arm reported as bleeding **less** is the arm that stayed **fully anticoagulated** — the higher-risk hemostatic condition — which makes the comparison uninterpretable rather than conservative. This finding must never be phrased as "3D Bond reduced bleeding versus collagen."
- **Bleeding severity**: the one study-arm event (male, tooth 37, INR 2.69, day 3) was grade I, controlled by intraoral tranexamic acid three times daily for one day. All four control-arm events were grade II on day 3, requiring resuturing, collagen dressing change, and three days of tranexamic acid.
- **Coagulation parameters differed by design**: INR 2.54 ± 0.43 vs 1.25 ± 0.17 (p < 0.001, eta² = 0.86); PT median 34.15 s vs 99.26 s (p < 0.001, r = 0.87); APTT 54.08 ± 9.79 vs 38.64 ± 5.13 s (p < 0.001, eta² = 0.58).
- **Baseline comparability**: no significant differences in age (p = 0.722), weight (p = 0.518), sex (p = 0.527), cardiac conditions (all p > 0.25), or anticoagulant dose (p = 0.787). However, per-tooth type distribution **was** imbalanced (chi² = 8.13, p = 0.043, Cramér's V = 0.41; incisors 29% vs 4%, bicuspids 17% vs 46%) and was left unadjusted.
- **Sex**: all 5 bleeds occurred in males (Fisher's exact p = 0.047; OR 14.55, 95% CI 0.75–283.39). The confidence interval crosses 1, so the odds ratio carries no usable information.
- **Not associated with bleeding**: age (p = 0.447), weight (p = 0.066), cardiological treatment (p = 0.443), anticoagulant type (p = 0.658) or dose (p = 0.499), intervention type (p = 0.469), jaw (p = 0.813), number of teeth (p > 0.999), tooth type (p = 0.358). Within the control arm, bleeding was unrelated to INR, PT, or APTT class; bleeders trended toward higher enoxaparin doses (80.0 vs 60.0 mg/day, p = 0.080).
- **Pain (VAS)**: 20–45 mm (mean 34) study vs 35–65 mm (mean 46) control — ranges and means only, no dispersion, no significance test, and equally confounded.
- **Wound healing**: impaired at day 7 in the four control-arm bleeders (20%), managed with chlorhexidine and Alveogyl dressing; none reported in the study arm. This outcome is fully collinear with the bleeding events.
- **What can be taken from this paper**: a BCS-cement socket protocol was feasible and uneventful in 20 patients extracted at therapeutic INR without interrupting VKA. That is a feasibility observation about a candidate material, and the appropriate weight to give it. Comparative efficacy against collagen remains untested; the authors call for prospective randomized multicenter trials with standardized bleeding assessment.
- **Additional quality caveats**: Cureus is a low-barrier author-pays journal; single-brand product-specific evaluation with a co-author in private practice; no funding or conflict-of-interest statement in the retrieved text; the socket-preservation claim in the abstract is unsupported by any radiographic or histomorphometric analysis.
