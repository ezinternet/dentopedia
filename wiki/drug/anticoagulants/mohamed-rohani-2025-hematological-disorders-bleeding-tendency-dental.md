## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Universiti Malaya, Spec Care Dentist 2025) of dental management for patients with common hematological disorders — anemia, hemophilia, von Willebrand disease, leukemia, and multiple myeloma — and their associated bleeding tendency.

Covers oral manifestations (delayed wound healing, mucosal ulcers, increased bleeding risk), diagnostic criteria for each disorder, the implications of concurrent anticoagulant and antiplatelet therapy on dental procedures, and the dentist–hematologist collaborative pathway.

Orientation and scoping material for risk assessment, not citable effect evidence: the review is qualitative and reports no incidence rates, effect sizes, or numeric hemostatic thresholds.

## Summary

This narrative review addresses a population the dental bleeding literature usually treats only as an aside: patients whose bleeding tendency is *intrinsic to a hematological disease* rather than induced by a prescribed antithrombotic. It covers five common conditions — anemia, hemophilia, von Willebrand disease, leukemia, and multiple myeloma — and organizes them around a shared clinical problem, bleeding risk during dental procedures, rather than around disease taxonomy. For each, the review describes oral manifestations (delayed wound healing, mucosal ulcers, increased bleeding risk) and diagnostic criteria, so that the dentist can recognize the disorder and speak to the hematologist in shared terms. The review then layers on the drug-induced dimension, outlining how concurrent anticoagulant and antiplatelet therapy compounds procedural risk and demands explicit risk assessment and management strategies. Its central practical claim is that safe care in this population is interdisciplinary by necessity: the dentist–hematologist collaboration is presented as a required pathway, not an optional consult. Because the ingest is abstract-only and the article is a narrative review, this page functions as orientation and scoping material — it maps the terrain and names the decision points, but supplies no incidence rates, factor-level or platelet-count thresholds, or effect sizes that could be cited as evidence.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates five distinct hematological conditions into a single dental-management frame organized around bleeding tendency rather than around the individual disease.
- Bridges disease-intrinsic and drug-induced coagulopathy, treating concurrent anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy as a compounding overlay on hematological disease rather than as a separate problem.
- Frames the dentist–hematologist collaboration as a required pathway, with diagnostic criteria serving as the shared language between the two disciplines.
- Fills a structural gap for this wiki: the bleeding-management literature held here is almost entirely about antithrombotic drugs, with intrinsic coagulopathies largely uncovered.

## Methodology

- **Design**: narrative review — no stated search strategy, inclusion criteria, quality appraisal, or quantitative synthesis.
- **Scope**: anemia, hemophilia, von Willebrand disease, leukemia, multiple myeloma, plus an anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy overlay.
- **Structure per abstract**: oral manifestations → diagnostic criteria → procedural risk assessment → management strategies → interdisciplinary collaboration.
- **Retrieval limitation**: no PMCID exists for this record; only the PubMed abstract was obtained. Per-disorder protocols and any numeric parameters remain in the unretrieved full text.

## Results

- **No quantitative results are reported at abstract level** — no incidence rates, bleeding percentages, factor-level or platelet-count thresholds, and no effect sizes.
- Hematological conditions produce oral manifestations including delayed wound healing, mucosal ulcers, and increased bleeding risk (qualitative).
- Anticoagulant and antiplatelet therapies carry implications for dental procedures requiring careful risk assessment and management strategies (qualitative).
- Safe and effective care depends on the dental professional understanding the underlying disorder and on structured collaboration with hematology (qualitative).
- **Interpretive caution**: as a narrative review ingested abstract-only, this page is the weakest evidence tier the wiki holds on bleeding management. Use it to frame a case and identify what to look up — not as the source of a number.
