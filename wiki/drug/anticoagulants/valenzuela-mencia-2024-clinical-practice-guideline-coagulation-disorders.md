## Three-line Summary

Clinical practice guideline (Spanish Society of Oral Surgery, SECIB; Med Oral Patol Oral Cir Bucal 2024) answering 15 PICO questions on extractions and implants in patients with coumarin, heparin, DOAC or hereditary coagulation disorders, using literature from December 2011 to December 2021.

Two PICO questions received a grade C recommendation and the rest grade D (mostly expert opinion, limited evidence without control groups); the main recommendations are to continue the oral anticoagulant for simple extractions and implants with local hemostatic measures, with post-extraction bleeding risk appearing similar to healthy patients at INR up to 2.2.

Evidence is weak throughout (few acenocoumarol and DOAC studies, mostly retrospective); INR above 3 or multiple extractions and hereditary coagulopathies need physician or haematologist input, and the authors call for controlled trials with representative samples.

## Summary

SECIB convened an expert panel to produce a clinical practice guideline on oral surgery in patients with coagulation disorders, following the Spanish NHS CPG methodological manual (GuiaSalud). Evidence was searched in MEDLINE and EMBASE (December 2011 to December 2021) and appraised with SIGN checklists (OSTEBA for case series, AGREE for guidelines). Recommendations are graded A-D; only C and D grades were assigned. The guideline covers VKAs (acenocoumarol, warfarin), heparin, DOACs and hereditary coagulopathies, each for extractions and implants, plus local and pharmacological hemostatic measures.

## Key Contributions

- 15 PICO-based recommendations; grade C for PICO 1 and PICO 8, grade D for the rest.
- Anchors a practical INR statement for coumarin patients: bleeding risk similar to healthy individuals when INR does not exceed 2.2; INR >3 or several extractions lacks evidence, so contact the physician (Grade C).
- Favours continuing anticoagulation with local hemostasis over interruption or heparin bridging.
- Flags acenocoumarol (most used in Spain) and DOAC evidence gaps.

## Methodology

- Sources: MEDLINE (PubMed) and EMBASE, December 2011 to December 2021; English, French, Spanish.
- Appraisal: SIGN (RCT, SR/MA, cohort, case-control), OSTEBA (case series), AGREE (guidelines); grading A-D via SIGN / Oxford CEBM.
- Panel: oral surgeons, haematologists, oral medicine; methodology expert and health librarian support.

## Results

- **Coumarin, extraction**: predictable when INR controlled, single tooth, local hemostasis; risk similar to healthy at INR up to 2.2; INR >3 or multiple extractions, contact physician (Grade C).
- **Heparin, extraction**: bridging appears to raise bleeding versus continuing the anticoagulant; local measures should suffice (Grade D).
- **DOAC, extraction**: increased bleeding versus healthy individuals (Grade D); evidence limited, heterogeneous, mostly retrospective.
- **Implants**: continuing coumarin or heparin in simple implant surgery does not significantly increase haemorrhage; suspension or heparin substitution not justified; DOAC patients can be treated safely, intraoperative bleeding possibly slightly greater, postoperative similar (all Grade D).
- **Local measures**: do not stop anticoagulant in simple extractions; no measure proven superior; use sutures, collagen or cellulose sponges, gauze soaked in tranexamic acid (Grade C); local measures advised in older patients (Grade C).
- **DOAC timing**: extract at maximum plasma decrease and delay the dose at least 4 hours after extraction (expert opinion, Grade D).
- **Pharmacological adjuncts**: not needed for coumarin or heparin patients when local measures are used (Grade D).
- **Hereditary coagulopathies**: insufficient evidence; minimum factor level before extraction in severe cases; implants may be placed; consider GA/sedation, avoid trunk anaesthesia, 3D CT; work with haematology (Grade D).

## Limitations

Lack of control groups, small samples, retrospective DOAC evidence, few acenocoumarol studies, and predominantly expert-opinion recommendations. The text lists questions 1-12, 14, 15 (no item 13). The PubMed title refers to coagulation disorders; the retrieved text has no separate antiplatelet section, although each question asks about concomitant antiplatelets.
