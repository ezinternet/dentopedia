## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42024562289; no meta-analysis) of 11 clinical studies (7 RCTs, 1 non-randomized controlled study, 3 uncontrolled series; 864 patients, at least 1148 teeth) on autologous platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) as a local hemostatic after dental extraction in patients on anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs.

All studies reported only mild-to-moderate bleeding, mostly controlled by local compression; PRF outperformed stitches alone, dry gauze and cellulose plugs, showed no significant bleeding difference versus chitosan, tranexamic acid or gelatin sponge, and achieved hemostasis more slowly than chitosan (2-3 min in both chitosan-comparison RCTs that reported it).

PRF is a feasible fully autologous option, but heterogeneous PRF protocols, few controlled trials, no analysis of extraction extent, and only one small DOAC RCT (Kyyak 2023) mean its hemostatic effect, especially under direct oral anticoagulants, cannot yet be quantified.

## Summary

The review asks whether PRF, a fully autologous fibrin clot prepared without added anticoagulant, works as a hemostatic plug after dental extraction in patients on anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy, a setting where guidelines favor continuing the drug and controlling the socket locally. Four databases (PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, SCOPUS) were searched to 1 June 2024; 1782 records became 789 after de-duplication, 13 reached full text and 11 were included (inter-reviewer agreement kappa 0.822). Risk of bias (RoB 2) was low in 5 studies and moderate in 6. Heterogeneity prevented any statistical pooling, so the synthesis is narrative (hence `evidence_level: sr`, not `sr+ma`).

Every included study reported sufficient hemostasis; the bleeding events that occurred were mild to moderate and mostly resolved with local compression. The comparative picture is: PRF better than stitches alone, dry gauze and cellulose plugs; no significant difference in postoperative bleeding episodes versus a chitosan dressing, tranexamic acid or a gelatin sponge; and slower hemostasis than chitosan. The authors conclude PRF can support hemostasis and may also help wound healing and pain, but that controlled RCTs are lacking.

## Key Contributions

- Updates two earlier reviews the authors cite (Filho 2021, 3 studies; Campana, 6 studies mixing PRF and PRP) with an 11-study PRF-only review restricted to anticoagulated/antiplatelet patients.
- Sorts the PRF evidence by comparator: stitches/gauze/cellulose (PRF better), chitosan, tranexamic acid and gelatin (no significant bleeding difference; chitosan faster).
- Separates the evidence by drug class: 4 antiplatelet studies, 4 vitamin K antagonist (VKA), 3 direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC), and one with no anticoagulant information.
- Flags that no included study analyzed extraction number, type, osteotomy or tooth position against bleeding.

## Methodology

- Design: systematic review per PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD42024562289; no meta-analysis because of heterogeneity.
- Population: patients on anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs undergoing dental extraction. Intervention: autologous PRF (PRP and PRGF excluded). Comparators: other hemostatic agents or control sites. Outcomes: hemostasis, bleeding, postoperative pain. German/English, human clinical studies.
- Screening by two independent reviewers with a third adjudicating; risk of bias by RoB 2 (applied to all 11, although 4 were not randomized trials).

## Results

| Comparator | Studies (from text) | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Dry gauze | Harfoush 2016 (non-randomized, mean INR 2.4) | PRF better; more bleeding over 20 min without PRF |
| Stitches only / cellulose plug | Giudice 2019, Brancaccio 2021 (split-mouth RCTs; L-PRF and A-PRF+) | PRF better; L-PRF vs A-PRF+ similar bleeding, better healing with L-PRF in Brancaccio |
| Chitosan dressing | Eldibany 2014, Sarkar 2019, Rajendra 2021 (RCTs) | No significant bleeding difference; hemostasis faster with chitosan (Sarkar, Rajendra; both stabilized in 2-3 min); chitosan arm had more alveolitis, delayed healing, pain (Eldibany); PRF had better healing and less pain (Sarkar) |
| Tranexamic acid | Munawar 2020 (RCT, n=84) | No significant difference in Results section (see inconsistency note) |
| Gelatin sponge | Kyyak 2023 (double-blind split-mouth RCT, n=21 DOAC) | No significant difference |
| None | Sammartino 2011 (n=50, warfarin), Mourao 2018 (n=25), Berton 2022 (n=112, mean age 77.1) | Only mild bleeding; Sammartino had two episodes at INR 3.7 (mean 3.16), resolved by compression |

- Totals: 864 patients, at least 1148 teeth; 20-300 patients per study.
- VKA studies: mean INR 2.28 (Eldibany), 2.4 (Harfoush), 3.16 (Sammartino); the authors treat INR as the key bleeding parameter and cite Febbo (INR 3 or higher, higher hemorrhage risk).
- DOAC studies: de Almeida 2018 no bleeding; Kyyak and Berton mild oozing only.
- Only one study was blinded; six studies did not classify the PRF type; some protocols used prolonged centrifugation for VKA or antiplatelet patients.

## Limitations

- Narrative synthesis only; studies differ in PRF protocol (L-PRF, A-PRF/A-PRF+, unspecified), comparator, drug class and outcome.
- Only three studies compared PRF with stitches/compression controls, one with tranexamic acid, one with gelatin; three had no control group.
- DOAC conclusions rest on a single small RCT (n=21); the authors state the effect in DOAC patients is difficult to anticipate.
- Extraction extent (number, osteotomy, anterior vs posterior) was not analyzed in any study.
- Internal inconsistencies in the source: the Results say no significant PRF vs tranexamic acid difference whereas the Discussion lists the tranexamic acid group among controls with significantly more bleeding; the abstract gives four antiplatelet and seven anticoagulant studies while the full text counts 4 + 4 VKA + 3 DOAC plus one study with no drug information; "789 studies identified" in the abstract is the post-deduplication count of 1782 records. Flagged, not resolved.

## Clinical Implication

For a patient who keeps an anticoagulant or antiplatelet drug through extraction, PRF is a workable autologous plug when the clinic already prepares it, with bleeding controllable by compression in these studies. The held evidence does not show it outperforming chitosan, tranexamic acid or gelatin sponge, and chitosan achieved hemostasis faster. It should not be presented as the evidence-preferred first-line local hemostatic, particularly under DOAC, and INR level (3 or higher in VKA patients) remains the main bleeding-risk cue discussed by the authors.
