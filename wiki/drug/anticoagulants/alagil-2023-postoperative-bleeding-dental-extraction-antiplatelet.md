## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PubMed and Google Scholar; 35 papers in the SR, 23 in the meta-analysis) of bleeding after minor dental extraction in patients on different antiplatelet therapy (APT) regimens, with healthy, interrupted-APT, and no-control subgroups.

Aspirin vs healthy controls showed no significant immediate or delayed bleeding risk (RR 1.26, P = .5; RR 2.17, P = .09), whereas dual APT vs healthy controls had RR 10.3 (immediate, P < .0001) and RR 7.72 (delayed, P = .001); continued vs interrupted dual APT was RR 2.13 for immediate bleeding (P = .07, not significant).

Extraction is safe on aspirin monotherapy, but dual APT patients should be regarded as at risk for immediate and continued bleeding; abstract-only retrieval, so study-level detail and bias assessment are not available.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1016/j.oooo.2023.10.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oooo.2023.10.006)), this SR+MA (AlAgil et al., Oral Surg Oral Med Oral Pathol Oral Radiol 2023;137(3):224-242) estimates the bleeding risk after minor extraction across antiplatelet regimens. Thirty-five papers entered the SR and 23 the meta-analysis, stratified by control type (none, healthy, interrupted APT) and by immediate vs delayed bleeding.

The regimen gradient is the main finding: aspirin alone is not distinguishable from healthy controls, a single non-aspirin agent raises immediate bleeding (RR 3.72), and dual APT raises both immediate (RR 10.3) and delayed (RR 7.72) bleeding. Continuing dual APT carried a higher immediate-bleeding risk than interruption (RR 2.13) that did not reach significance (P = .07).

## Key Contributions

- Dental-specific, regimen-stratified RR estimates that complement the general-surgery estimates of Shah 2022.
- Distinguishes immediate from delayed bleeding.
- Quantifies how large the DAPT signal is relative to healthy controls, which the umbrella review described only qualitatively.

## Methodology

SR+MA; PubMed and Google Scholar; 35 papers in the SR, 23 with extractable data for meta-analysis; subgroups by control type; immediate and delayed bleeding analyzed separately. (Risk-of-bias method, heterogeneity, and confidence intervals not available from the abstract.)

## Results

| Comparison | Outcome | RR | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aspirin vs healthy | Immediate | 1.26 | .5 |
| Aspirin vs healthy | Delayed | 2.17 | .09 |
| Single non-aspirin APT vs healthy | Immediate | 3.72 | .0009 |
| Dual APT vs healthy | Immediate | 10.3 | <.0001 |
| Dual APT vs healthy | Delayed | 7.72 | .001 |
| Dual APT continued vs interrupted | Immediate | 2.13 | .07 |

## Limitations

- Abstract-only: no CIs, I², risk-of-bias ratings, bleeding definitions, or local hemostatic protocols.
- Two databases searched only.
- Borderline continued-vs-interrupted DAPT result (P = .07); thromboembolic cost of interruption not assessed in the abstract.
- Delayed-bleeding aspirin estimate (RR 2.17, P = .09) is imprecise.
