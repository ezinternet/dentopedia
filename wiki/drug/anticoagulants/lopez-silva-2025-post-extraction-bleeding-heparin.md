## Three-line Summary

Prospective observational study at two Australian hospital-based dental clinics; 95 heparinized patients received 185 dental extractions, with bleeding tracked over the 48-hour post-procedure window.

Six patients (6.3%) had minor bleeding within 48 hours, all controlled by gauze pressure alone; unfractionated heparin (vs low-molecular-weight heparin) and a shorter interval since the last heparin dose were associated with higher bleeding rates (p ≤ 0.01).

Heparin therapy need not be interrupted or dose-adjusted for dental extraction when careful local hemostatic measures are used — though unfractionated heparin and extraction timed close to the last dose warrant extra hemostatic attention.

## 세줄요약

호주 병원 기반 치과 2곳의 전향적 관찰연구 — 헤파린 (Heparin) 투여 환자 95명이 발치 185건을 받았고, 시술 후 48시간 동안 출혈을 추적했다.

48시간 내 6명 (6.3%)에서 경미한 출혈이 발생했고 전원 거즈 압박만으로 지혈됨; 미분획헤파린 (Unfractionated Heparin, UFH)이 저분자량헤파린 (Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin, LMWH)보다, 그리고 마지막 헤파린 투여와 가까운 시점의 발치일수록 출혈률이 높았다 (p ≤ 0.01).

국소 지혈을 충실히 하면 헤파린 중단이나 용량 변경 없이 발치가 가능하다 — 다만 미분획헤파린 사용 중이거나 마지막 투여 직후 발치하는 경우엔 지혈에 더 신경 써야 한다.

## Summary

Management protocols for dental extraction in patients on warfarin, direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs), and antiplatelet/antithrombotic drugs are well established, but guidance for patients on **injectable anticoagulants — heparins** is limited. The authors argue this gap leaves dentists and physicians uncertain about the safety of extraction in heparinized patients and delays timely care. To supply direct evidence, they ran a prospective observational study at two Australian hospital-based dental clinics, following 95 heparinized patients through 185 dental extractions and recording bleeding events in the 48 hours after each procedure. Minor bleeding occurred in six patients (6.3%), every episode controlled by direct gauze pressure at the site; no severe or escalation-requiring hemorrhage was reported. Within the heparinized cohort, two factors tracked with higher bleeding: treatment with unfractionated heparin rather than a low-molecular-weight heparin, and extraction performed closer in time to the last heparin dose (both p ≤ 0.01). The authors conclude that extractions can be performed safely in patients on injectable anticoagulants provided careful local hemostatic measures are used, and that interrupting or modifying heparin dosing to accommodate dental extraction is unnecessary.

## Key Contributions

- Fills an explicitly named guideline gap: the drug class (injectable heparins) for which extraction protocols were absent, in contrast to warfarin, DOACs, and antiplatelets.
- First prospective incidence figure held in this wiki for post-extraction bleeding under heparin therapy: 6.3% of patients, all minor.
- Risk stratification *within* heparin therapy — heparin type (UFH > LMWH) and dose-to-extraction interval — rather than a single undifferentiated "on heparin" risk statement.
- Supports a no-interruption position for injectable anticoagulants, aligning heparin with the continuation consensus already established for oral agents.

## Methodology

- **Design**: prospective, observational (no interruption/comparator arm).
- **Setting**: two Australian hospital-based dental clinics.
- **Population**: 95 patients on heparin therapy; 185 teeth extracted.
- **Outcome window**: 48 hours post-procedure.
- **Outcomes**: frequency and severity of bleeding events; management required.
- **Analysis**: bivariate analysis of bleeding rate vs heparin type and vs interval since last heparin dose.
- **Not available (abstract-only ingest)**: inclusion/exclusion criteria, the specific local hemostatic protocol used, bleeding-severity grading scale, per-subgroup denominators.

## Results

- **Cohort**: 95 heparinized patients, 185 extractions.
- **Bleeding rate**: 6/95 patients = **6.3%** within 48 hours.
- **Severity**: all events minor; every episode managed by direct gauze pressure alone.
- **Heparin type**: unfractionated heparin > low-molecular-weight heparin for bleeding rate, **p ≤ 0.01**.
- **Timing**: extraction closer to the last heparin dose → higher bleeding rate, **p ≤ 0.01**.
- **Conclusion**: local hemostatic measures suffice; interruption or dose change of heparin is unnecessary for extraction.
- **Limitations**: observational without a non-heparinized or interrupted comparator; only 6 events underpin the bivariate signals; hospital-based Australian case-mix; 48-hour window may miss later delayed bleeding; abstract-only ingest limits verification of protocol detail.
