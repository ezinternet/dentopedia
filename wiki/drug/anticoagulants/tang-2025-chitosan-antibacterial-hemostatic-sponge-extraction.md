## Three-line Summary

Anticoagulated rat tooth-extraction model plus in vitro characterisation of a quaternized carboxymethyl chitosan/polydopamine composite hemostatic sponge (QCD) — not a study of human anticoagulated patients, despite the title's wording.

In rats, QCD reported blood loss 0.011 ± 0.001 g and hemostasis time 59.667 ± 4.163 s versus a commercial gelatin sponge at 0.019 ± 0.001 g and 87.000 ± 4.082 s; the quaternary ammonium modification conferred antibacterial activity in vitro, and the hemostatic advantage was attributed to rapid blood absorption with enhanced blood-cell and platelet adhesion.

There is no human data in this paper: the clinical claim carried by its title and closing sentence is an extrapolation from a rodent model and benchtop assays, with no comparison against tranexamic acid or oxidized cellulose.

## 세줄요약

항응고 처치된 흰쥐(rat) 발치 모델과 in vitro 실험으로 평가한 4급 암모늄화 카르복시메틸 키토산/폴리도파민 복합 지혈 스폰지(Quaternized Carboxymethyl Chitosan/Polydopamine, QCD) 연구 — 제목과 달리 항응고 환자를 대상으로 한 임상연구가 아니다.

쥐 모델에서 QCD는 출혈량 0.011 ± 0.001 g, 지혈시간 59.667 ± 4.163초로 상용 젤라틴 스폰지(0.019 ± 0.001 g, 87.000 ± 4.082초)보다 우수했고, 4급 암모늄 변형으로 in vitro 항균능을 보였으며, 저자들은 빠른 혈액흡수와 혈구·혈소판 부착 증가를 기전으로 제시한다.

사람 데이터는 전혀 없다 — 제목과 결론 문장의 임상적 함의는 설치류 모델과 벤치탑 실험에서의 외삽이며, 트라넥삼산(Tranexamic Acid, TXA)이나 산화셀룰로스(Oxidized Cellulose)와의 비교도 없다.

## Summary

This is a **preclinical biomaterials study**, not a clinical one. The authors developed a quaternized carboxymethyl chitosan/polydopamine composite sponge (QCD) and evaluated it in vitro and in an anticoagulated **rat** tooth-extraction model against a commercial gelatin sponge.

The title — and the paper's own closing sentence, that QCD "may serve as a practical and promising approach for managing post-extraction hemorrhage in anticoagulated patients" — reads as if the work concerns anticoagulated patients. It does not. The MeSH indexing confirms the in vivo subjects were male Sprague-Dawley rats, and no human arm exists anywhere in the study. The clinical framing is the authors' aspiration for the material, not a finding; this page should never be cited as human evidence for chitosan sponges in anticoagulated extraction.

What the study does establish, at preclinical level, is a rationale for the material design. Chitosan's hemostatic action is largely charge-mediated and cascade-independent, which is the theoretical reason a chitosan dressing might help when anticoagulants have impaired the cascade. On top of that base, quaternary ammonium groups add antibacterial activity aimed at the polymicrobial oral environment and post-extraction infection risk — a property plain gelatin sponges lack — while polydopamine contributes wet adhesion and mechanical integrity. In the rat model QCD stopped bleeding faster and with less blood loss than gelatin sponge, which the authors attribute to rapid blood absorption and enhanced blood-cell/platelet adhesion driving clot formation.

The practical reading for a clinician: this is an interesting materials direction that sits *beneath* the human evidence base, not alongside it. The human-level question — whether chitosan dressings outperform standard local hemostatics in antithrombotic patients — is addressed by [[drug/anticoagulants/guardieiro-2023-chitosan-cellulose-hemostasis-dapt-rct]], not here.

## Key Contributions

- A single composite material (QCD) intended to unite mechanical robustness, biocompatibility, hemostatic performance, and intrinsic antibacterial activity — the last being what distinguishes it from a conventional gelatin sponge.
- Quaternary ammonium modification of carboxymethyl chitosan as the antibacterial mechanism, explicitly motivated by the oral environment and post-extraction infection risk.
- A head-to-head preclinical comparison performed **under anticoagulation** rather than in healthy animals, so the impaired-hemostasis condition of interest is at least modelled.
- A proposed mechanism for the hemostatic advantage: rapid blood absorption plus enhanced adhesion of blood cells and platelets promoting clot formation.

## Methodology

*(Abstract-level detail only — full text was not retrieved. Sample sizes, anticoagulant agent/dose/route, randomisation, blinding, and statistical methods are not recoverable.)*

- **Test material**: quaternized carboxymethyl chitosan / polydopamine composite sponge (QCD).
- **Comparator**: commercial gelatin sponge ("Gel group").
- **In vitro**: hemostatic performance assays; antibacterial testing (MeSH includes Microbial Sensitivity Tests).
- **In vivo**: tooth extraction in **anticoagulated rats** (MeSH: Rats, Sprague-Dawley; Male).
- **Outcomes**: blood loss (g), hemostasis time (s).
- **Mechanistic assessment**: blood absorption; blood-cell and platelet adhesion to the sponge surface.

## Results

All values are **rat-model values**.

| Outcome (anticoagulated rat extraction) | QCD | Commercial gelatin sponge |
|---|---|---|
| Blood loss | 0.011 ± 0.001 g | 0.019 ± 0.001 g |
| Hemostasis time | 59.667 ± 4.163 s | 87.000 ± 4.082 s |

- QCD was reported as significantly better on both outcomes; the test statistic and p-value are not given in the abstract.
- **In vitro**, QCD showed markedly enhanced hemostatic and antibacterial performance versus the Gel group, the antibacterial effect being credited to the quaternary ammonium modification.
- **Reported precision is the authors' own.** Three decimals on a ~60-second hemostasis time and on a ~10-milligram blood loss imply a resolution the method is unlikely to possess; the figures read as means over very small n. Take the direction of the difference as the finding, not the digits.
- **Absolute differences are small**: roughly 8 mg of blood and ~27 seconds in a rat socket. Whether that scales to a human socket is untested.

### Limitations

- **Rat model** — rodent socket size, blood volume, coagulation physiology, and oral flora differ substantially from human; no clinical efficacy is established.
- **Small effect sizes in absolute terms** (~8 mg blood loss, ~27 s hemostasis time), reported with implausibly tight precision.
- **No human data**, despite the title's and conclusion's framing around anticoagulated patients.
- **No comparison against tranexamic acid or oxidized cellulose** — the two agents that dominate the real-world local-hemostasis decision; the sole comparator is a gelatin sponge.
- **Antibacterial claim tested in vitro only** — no in vivo infection endpoint or biofilm model, so the anti-infective benefit is inferred rather than demonstrated.
- **Abstract-only ingest** — sample size, anticoagulant protocol, randomisation/blinding, statistics, biocompatibility and degradation data are unavailable.
