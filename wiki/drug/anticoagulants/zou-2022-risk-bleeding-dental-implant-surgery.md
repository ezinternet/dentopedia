## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 7 studies (PubMed, Embase, CENTRAL) comparing bleeding after dental implant placement with continued oral anticoagulant (OAC) or antiplatelet (AP) therapy vs controls, or OAC vs AP.

Pooled OAC vs control: no significant difference in bleeding (RR 1.81, 95% CI 0.70-4.63; I2 = 14%); VKA subgroup borderline (RR 3.42, 95% CI 1.00-11.67; P = .05), DOAC subgroup not elevated (RR 1.67, 95% CI 0.49-5.70).

Continuing OAC for implant surgery does not significantly increase bleeding when local haemostatic measures are used; the VKA signal and the OAC-vs-AP comparison rest on very few studies (abstract-only — full text not retrieved).

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (Zou and Hua, Acta Odontol Scand 2022) pooled 7 studies on bleeding after dental implant placement in patients continuing OAC or AP therapy. Continued OAC therapy did not significantly increase bleeding versus controls (RR 1.81, 95% CI 0.70-4.63). By OAC class, VKAs showed a borderline tendency toward more bleeding (RR 3.42, 95% CI 1.00-11.67; P = .05) while DOACs did not (RR 1.67, 95% CI 0.49-5.70). The authors conclude that OAC continuation is acceptable when local haemostatic measures are used, and that VKAs may carry a higher bleeding tendency than DOACs by indirect comparison. Based on the PubMed abstract only.

## Key Contributions

- Pooled, implant-specific estimate for continued OAC therapy (the earlier implant-focused review was narrative, pre-DOAC).
- VKA vs DOAC subgroup contrast; limited OAC vs AP comparison.

## Methodology

PubMed, Embase and CENTRAL; studies comparing bleeding after implant placement between OAC/AP and controls or OAC vs AP; 7 studies included. Full methods (risk-of-bias tool, bleeding definitions) not available from the abstract.

## Results

| Comparison | RR (95% CI) | I2 | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continued OAC vs control | 1.81 (0.70-4.63) | 14% | .22 |
| VKA subgroup | 3.42 (1.00-11.67) | 23% | .05 |
| DOAC subgroup | 1.67 (0.49-5.70) | 0% | .41 |
| OAC vs AP (limited data) | 0.08 (0.01-0.76) | 0% | .03 |

Caveat: the abstract states OAC carries higher bleeding than AP but reports RR 0.08 without defining the reference arm; direction cannot be verified without the full text. Note that the DOAC point estimate (1.67) is similar to the overall estimate, and its CI is wide — "not elevated" reflects imprecision, not proven equivalence.
