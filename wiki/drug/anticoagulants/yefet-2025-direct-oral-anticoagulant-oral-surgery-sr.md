## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (no meta-analysis stated in the abstract): 628 records screened, 17 studies on DOAC-treated patients (dabigatran, rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban) undergoing oral surgery.

Minor to moderate bleeding was relatively common, severe bleeding requiring hospitalization was rare, and most events were managed with standard local hemostatic measures.

Continuing DOAC was deemed safe with local management, and scheduling when DOAC levels are lowest is advised; only the abstract was available, so no pooled estimates are known.

## Summary

Source: PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10006-025-01348-0). This systematic review evaluates bleeding risk and management strategies in patients on DOACs undergoing oral surgery. Only DOAC-only populations were included; studies with other anticoagulants or combined therapy were excluded.

## Key Contributions

- DOAC-specific (not mixed-anticoagulant) synthesis for oral surgery: extractions, implants, soft-tissue procedures.
- Practical advice: schedule procedures when DOAC levels are lowest; continue DOAC with local hemostasis.

## Methodology

Systematic review; 628 articles identified, 17 included. Databases, risk-of-bias assessment, and protocol registration are not stated in the abstract.

## Results

Abstract-level only: minor to moderate bleeding relatively common; severe bleeding requiring hospitalization rare; most events controlled with standard local hemostatic measures; DOAC continuation deemed safe. No quantitative rates are reported in the abstract.
