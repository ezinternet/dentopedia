## Three-line Summary

Practice guideline (American College of Chest Physicians, Chest 2022; 43 PICO questions; GRADE) on perioperative management of patients on long-term vitamin K antagonist (VKA), heparin bridging, direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) or antiplatelet therapy who need an elective surgery/procedure.

The multidisciplinary panel issued 44 recommendations, of which two are strong: against heparin bridging in atrial fibrillation, and continuation of VKA for pacemaker/ICD implantation; separate recommendations cover minor procedures including dental.

The abstract states that substantial new evidence has emerged since the 2012 guideline, but uncertainty remains for the majority of perioperative questions; dental-specific recommendations are not detailed in the abstract (abstract-only ingest).

## Summary

Source: PubMed abstract (PMID 35964704), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2022.07.025). The ACCP guideline addresses 43 PICO questions on long-term oral anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy in patients needing an elective surgery/procedure, in four categories: VKA, heparin bridging, DOAC, antiplatelet. Recommendations are strong or conditional, based on high to very low certainty of evidence (GRADE). The abstract notes separate recommendations for minor procedures including dental, but does not state their content.

## Key Contributions

- 44 recommendations from a multidisciplinary panel; two strong: (1) against heparin bridging in atrial fibrillation; (2) continue VKA in pacemaker/ICD implantation.
- Separate minor-procedure recommendations: dental, dermatologic, ophthalmologic, pacemaker/ICD, GI (endoscopic).
- Update of the 2012 iteration, citing substantial new evidence on VKA/bridging, DOAC and antiplatelet management.

## Methodology

PICO-based guideline (43 questions), multidisciplinary panel, GRADE methodology. Further methods not retrieved.

## Results

Two strong recommendations as above; the remaining recommendations are conditional or their grades are not given in the abstract. The abstract concludes that uncertainty remains as to best practices for the majority of perioperative management questions. Dental-procedure recommendations: content not available from the abstract.
