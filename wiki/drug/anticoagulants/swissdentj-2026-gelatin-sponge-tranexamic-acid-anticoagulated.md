## Three-line Summary

Scoping review (Arksey & O'Malley framework, PRISMA-ScR reporting) of 16 studies on anticoagulated patients undergoing tooth extraction with combined gelatin sponge and tranexamic acid (TXA) local hemostasis; PubMed, Scopus, and ScienceDirect searched through April 2026.

Included studies were predominantly observational with few randomized trials; vitamin K antagonists (VKA) dominated while direct oral anticoagulants (DOAC) were underrepresented, and gelatin sponge + TXA combinations were classifiable only as simultaneous, sequential, or unspecified, with substantial heterogeneity in TXA formulation, protocol, and outcome definitions.

Combined gelatin sponge + TXA appears promising for achieving hemostasis without interrupting anticoagulant therapy, but the evidence base is too heterogeneous and too low in study design level to support a standardized protocol — this is a mapped research gap, not a pooled effect estimate.

## Summary

Managing post-extraction bleeding in anticoagulated patients requires balancing hemorrhagic against thromboembolic risk, and the prevailing strategy is to keep the anticoagulant running while controlling the socket locally. Absorbable gelatin sponge and tranexamic acid (TXA) are the two most widely used local hemostatic agents for this, and they are frequently used together — yet, as this Swiss Dental Journal scoping review documents, their **combined** use has been inconsistently reported and never standardized. Following the Arksey and O'Malley framework and reported per PRISMA-ScR, the authors searched PubMed, Scopus, and ScienceDirect through April 2026 and charted 16 studies of anticoagulated patients undergoing tooth extraction with local hemostatic intervention, synthesizing them descriptively.

The map that results is mostly a map of gaps. The included literature is predominantly observational with only limited randomized trials. Vitamin K antagonists (VKA) are the most represented anticoagulant class while direct oral anticoagulants (DOAC) are underrepresented — meaning the combination is least evidenced for the drug class most patients now take. TXA appears in multiple formulations, and the way it is paired with the gelatin sponge could only be classified as simultaneous, sequential, or unspecified, the last category itself indicating how often sequencing goes unreported. Reported outcomes cluster around bleeding incidence, severity, and need for additional intervention, but with substantial heterogeneity in both protocols and outcome definitions.

The authors' conclusion is deliberately bounded: the combination "appears promising" for achieving hemostasis without interrupting anticoagulant therapy, but protocol variability, inconsistent outcome reporting, and the scarcity of higher-level evidence prevent evidence-based clinical guidance. The paper's contribution is therefore to define the research agenda — harmonized protocols and stronger comparative studies — rather than to establish an effect size.

## Key Contributions

- First evidence map dedicated specifically to the **combination** of absorbable gelatin sponge and TXA, rather than to either agent in isolation, in anticoagulated extraction patients.
- Proposes an explicit taxonomy of combination modality — **simultaneous, sequential, or unspecified** — making visible that much of the literature does not report how the two agents are sequenced.
- Quantifies the anticoagulant-class imbalance: VKA-dominant, DOAC-underrepresented, flagging a mismatch between the evidence base and current prescribing.
- Names the specific barriers to guideline-level recommendation: heterogeneous TXA formulations, non-harmonized protocols, and inconsistent bleeding-outcome definitions.

## Methodology

- **Design**: scoping review per the **Arksey and O'Malley** framework; reported per **PRISMA-ScR**.
- **Databases**: PubMed, Scopus, ScienceDirect; searched through **April 2026**.
- **Included**: **16 studies** of anticoagulated patients undergoing tooth extraction with local hemostatic interventions.
- **Synthesis**: descriptive only — **no meta-analysis, no pooled estimate, no certainty grading reported in the abstract** (hence `evidence_level: sr`, not `sr+ma`).
- **Charted dimensions**: study design, anticoagulant class, TXA formulation, mode of combination with gelatin sponge, and outcome definitions.
