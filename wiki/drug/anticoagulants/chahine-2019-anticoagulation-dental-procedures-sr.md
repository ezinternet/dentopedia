## Three-line Summary
Systematic review of 10 clinical trials (5 RCT, 5 CCT, n=1,331) comparing continued, interrupted, and heparin-bridged anticoagulation across multiple common dental procedures including extraction, implant surgery, and periodontal surgery.
Continuing VKA or NOAC did not increase post-operative bleeding in most trials, whereas heparin bridging was consistently associated with higher bleeding incidence; no thromboembolic events were reported in any study.
The authors recommend maintaining oral anticoagulation for the vast majority of dental procedures combined with local hemostatic agents; evidence is limited to PubMed (1996–2016) with no meta-analysis possible due to heterogeneity.

## Summary
This systematic review searched PubMed (1996–2016) and included 10 clinical trials (5 RCT, 5 CCT, n=1,331) comparing continued, interrupted, or heparin-bridged anticoagulation in patients undergoing dental procedures. Procedures covered included extraction, implant surgery, excision of cysts, biopsy, periodontal surgery, and apicectomy. Across most trials, continuing anticoagulation (VKA or NOAC) did not increase post-operative bleeding. Heparin bridging was associated with higher bleeding incidence. No thromboembolic events were reported after interruption, though follow-up was too short to reliably assess this. The authors recommend maintaining oral anticoagulation for the vast majority of dental procedures combined with local hemostatic agents.

## Key Contributions
- Procedure-specific recommendations for VKA and NOAC covering 8 categories of dental procedures
- Evidence that heparin bridging is NOT safer — it increases bleeding without clear thromboprophylactic benefit
- NOAC (dabigatran, rivaroxaban) data: equivalent safety to VKA for dental procedures
- Identifies critical evidence gap: NOAC-specific high-quality RCT data still limited (2019)
- Aligns with and updates Madrid 2009 with NOAC coverage

## Methodology
Systematic review, PubMed only (no EMBASE/Cochrane — limitation), 1996–2016. Included: 10 trials (5 RCT, 5 CCT), n=1,331. Compared anticoagulation management strategies for dental procedures. Data extracted: patient numbers, anticoagulant type/dose, INR, procedure, local hemostats, thromboembolic outcome, bleeding characteristics. No meta-analysis possible due to heterogeneity.

## Results
- Continuing anticoagulation: no significant increase in bleeding in most trials
- Heparin bridging: consistently associated with higher bleeding
- Thromboembolic events after interruption: none reported (follow-up too short/inconsistent)
- INR-therapeutic VKA patients: safe to continue for most minor procedures
- NOAC: 4-year cross-sectional data showing no significant bleeding when continuing
- Local hemostatic agents (tranexamic acid, gelatin sponge, oxidized cellulose): effective in all groups
