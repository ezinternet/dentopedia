## Three-line Summary

PRISMA-ScR scoping review (search October 2024, six databases, 1,127 records screened) of topical tranexamic acid (TXA) powder prepared from crushed tablets — not commercial mouthwash — applied at the surgical site during minor oral surgery.

Ten studies were included (3 retrospective, 2 clinical trials, 2 prospective cohorts, 1 case-control, 1 case report, 1 letter to the editor); TXA doses ranged 250–500 mg per application (six studies used 250-mg tablets, three used 500-mg), most commonly crushed and mixed with saline into a paste used to soak a resorbable sponge placed in the alveolar socket, and all ten studies reported successful bleeding control in patients on anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy or with hemophilia, Glanzmann thrombasthenia, or liver disease.

The clinically actionable protocol — a crushed 250-mg TXA tablet in saline soaking a resorbable sponge intraoperatively, plus one tablet in 100 mL cold saline as a rinse four times daily for seven days — is cheap and available where 5% commercial mouthwash is not, but TXA was almost always combined with sutures, sponges, and compression, so its isolated effect remains unquantified.

## Summary

Tranexamic acid (TXA) is well established in dentistry as a ready-to-use 5% mouthwash for postoperative bleeding control in anticoagulated patients. This scoping review from Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais deliberately steps below that formulation and asks a narrower question: what evidence exists for **TXA powder made by crushing oral tablets** and applying it directly at the surgical site? Ready-to-use mouthwash, injectable, and systemic TXA were all exclusion criteria, as were major (cardiovascular, orthopedic) surgeries.

Searching six databases in October 2024 with no language, date, or geographic restriction, the authors screened 1,127 records and included 10 studies published 1998–2022. Every included population had a coagulation disorder: antiplatelet therapy, vitamin K antagonists (VKA), direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs), hemophilia, Glanzmann thrombasthenia, or liver disease. Eight studies concerned dental extraction exclusively; one added periodontal therapy and one added oral biopsy.

The dominant technique is a crushed 250-mg tablet mixed with saline into a paste used to soak a resorbable sponge that fills the alveolar socket, followed by a homemade rinse (one tablet in 100 mL cold saline, four times daily for seven days). Variants include Gelfoam impregnated with TXA powder, paste placed directly in the alveolus without a carrier, and — the simplest, developed for hemophilia in a resource-limited setting — crushed 500-mg tablets on wet cotton wool with 30 minutes of biting pressure.

All ten studies reported successful bleeding control, and none required interruption of antithrombotic therapy. The review's own central caveat is that TXA was almost always bundled with sutures, sponges, and mechanical compression, so its independent contribution cannot be isolated. Being a scoping review, it maps protocols rather than pooling outcomes: there is no effect estimate, no comparative bleeding incidence, and no meta-analytic weight behind the "promising" verdict.

The practical value is access. Tablets are far cheaper than intravenous preparations and are stocked where a commercial 4.8–5% TXA mouthwash is not, and the pharmacokinetic argument supports the route — plasma TXA after topical application is less than one tenth of intravenous levels, and a 1-g oral dose leaves no detectable TXA in saliva while a 10 mL rinse of 5% solution inhibits salivary fibrinolysis for hours.

## Key Contributions

- First synthesis focused specifically on locally applied TXA powder from crushed tablets, with commercial mouthwash explicitly excluded — a formulation-level rather than drug-level review.
- Consolidates ten heterogeneous reports into one comparable protocol table covering dose, vehicle, carrier, and timing, making the technique reproducible chairside.
- Establishes powder TXA as a cost-and-access answer for settings without commercial 4.8–5% mouthwash, including a 30-minute cotton-wool-and-bite protocol usable without any sponge or suture.
- Collects the pharmacokinetic rationale for the topical route: <1/10 of intravenous plasma levels; no detectable salivary TXA after 1-g oral dosing versus hours of fibrinolytic inhibition after a 5% rinse; tablet-derived rinse solutions stable up to 31 days at 5°C or 23°C.

## Methodology

- **Design**: scoping review reported per PRISMA-ScR; protocol pre-registered on the Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/n5mbp). No meta-analysis.
- **Search**: October 2024, four independent calibrated reviewers; MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, Embase, BVSalud, Web of Science, Periódicos CAPES, plus reference-list screening; no language, date, or geographic limits. Rayyan for screening, EndNote for deduplication.
- **Inclusion**: clinical studies, case reports, and letters to the editor in oral surgery patients reporting topical use of crushed TXA tablets.
- **Exclusion**: reviews, conference abstracts, editorials, in vitro and animal studies; ready-to-use mouthwash, injectable, and systemic TXA; major surgery.
- **Selection**: two independent blinded reviewers, third reviewer arbitrating disagreements.
- **Risk of bias**: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for non-randomized designs (7–9 stars low, 4–6 moderate, <4 high); JBI Critical Appraisal Checklists for trials and case reports (≥70% low, 40–69% moderate, <40% high).

## Results

- **Study flow**: 1,127 records (Scopus 496, Web of Science 245, BVSalud 205, PubMed 143, Periódicos CAPES 25, Embase 13) → 22 duplicates removed → 1,105 screened → 1,057 excluded → 48 eligible → 46 full texts assessed → 36 excluded → **10 included**.
- **Designs**: 3 retrospective, 2 clinical trials, 2 prospective cohorts, 1 case-control, 1 case report, 1 letter to the editor. Publication span 1998–2022; 4 of 10 from a single Brazilian group (Rocha et al.).
- **Dose**: 250-mg tablets in six studies, 500-mg tablets in three, unspecified in one — reported range **250–500 mg per application**.
- **Primary application technique** (most common): crushed **250-mg** tablet + saline paste used to **soak a resorbable sponge filling the alveolar socket** (Rocha 2017/2019/2020, Souza 2022).
- **Variants**: Gelfoam impregnated with TXA powder, socket repacked (Lu 2018); paste directly into the alveolus without a sponge (Buhatem Medeiros 2017); gauze soaked in 500 mg TXA in saline for pressure plus a socket sponge (Blinder 1999); crushed **500-mg** tablets on wet cotton wool with the patient **biting for 30 minutes** (Coetzee 2007, hemophilia n=30); powdered 500-mg tablet paste delivered on a soft plate/splint (Bhavyaa 2021, Glanzmann thrombasthenia).
- **Postoperative rinse regimens described**: one tablet in **100 mL cold saline, 4×/day × 7 days** (3 studies); 500 mg for 2 minutes, 4×/day × 4 days (Blinder 1999); 3 minutes every 6 hours × 7 days (Bernardoni-Socorro 1998); 500 mg in 10 mL water (5%), 6×/day × 2 days preoperatively (Bhavyaa 2021).
- **Populations**: all had coagulopathy — antiplatelet, VKA, or DOAC therapy; plus hemophilia (n=30), Glanzmann thrombasthenia (n=1, age 7), liver disease (n=47).
- **Procedures**: 8/10 extraction only; 1 also periodontal therapy (scaling/curettage); 1 also oral biopsy.
- **Outcome**: **all 10 studies reported successful bleeding control**, with no interruption of anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy required. Souza 2022 also reported fewer hospitalizations and transfusions in liver-disease patients. Blinder 1999 flagged severe periodontitis as an added bleeding risk.
- **Limitations**: TXA was co-administered with sponges, sutures, and compression in nearly every study, so its isolated effect is unquantified; no pooled estimate or comparative bleeding incidence is reported; the evidence base includes a case report and a letter to the editor with only 2 clinical trials; one study omitted concentration and vehicle volumes vary (10 mL vs 100 mL, saline vs water). Authors call for studies of the isolated efficacy of powder TXA.
