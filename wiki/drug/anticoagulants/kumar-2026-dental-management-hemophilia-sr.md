## One-line Summary

SR of 23 studies on dental management in congenital hemophilia: TXA/EACA antifibrinolytics significantly reduce factor infusion requirements; standard target is 30–50% factor activity; infiltration anesthesia safe without factor, IAN block requires factor correction.

## 한줄요약

## Summary

This PRISMA-compliant systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42025635460) included 23 studies published from inception to March 2025 covering dental management in congenital hemophilia A and B. Most studies targeted 30–50% factor activity correction pre-procedure per WFH guidelines. Tranexamic acid (TXA, 트라넥삼산) antifibrinolytic therapy — administered orally (1g TID × 5–10 days), intravenously (20 mg/kg), or as mouthwash/compress (5%) — consistently reduced factor concentrate requirements across studies. Infiltration anesthesia with adrenaline is safe without factor correction; inferior alveolar nerve block requires factor pre-coverage due to deep tissue bleeding risk. Evidence quality was low-to-moderate due to retrospective designs and heterogeneous reporting; no meta-analysis was possible.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes TXA as an effective factor-sparing antifibrinolytic in hemophilia dental procedures across 50+ years of literature
- Documents TXA dosing range: oral 1g TID, IV 20 mg/kg, local 5% mouthwash/compress — all formats effective
- Confirms IAN block as the key anesthesia risk point requiring mandatory factor correction; infiltration is safe
- In mild hemophilia A, oral TXA alone (without factor) sufficient for some dental procedures (Raso 2022, Emicizumab cases)
- Mouth splints significantly reduce post-extraction socket bleeding by stabilizing the hematoma

## Methodology

**Design**: Systematic review, PRISMA; databases: PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, CINAHL, EBSCO Dental, ProQuest; search to March 2025. **Included**: 23 studies — RCTs (n=3), cohort, case-control, case reports; hemophilia A/B (congenital, all severity); acquired hemophilia and inhibitor-positive excluded. **Outcomes**: Factor dosing, TXA/EACA use, anesthesia protocols, local hemostatic agents, complications. **Quality**: JBI appraisal; low-to-moderate; heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis.

## Results

### Factor correction targets
- **30–50% factor activity** is the standard WFH-guideline target for dental procedures
- Dosing formula: FVIII dose (IU) = weight (kg) × desired rise (%) / 2; FIX = weight × desired rise × 1
- High-risk/severe hemophilia: 50–80% correction used for complex extractions and nerve blocks

### TXA protocols across studies
| Protocol | Details | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Oral 1g TID × 5 days | Starting 2h pre-op; reduced transfusion requirements vs placebo | Forbes 1972 (RCT) |
| IV 20 mg/kg → oral TID × 7 days | Pre-op IV + oral + TXA-soaked gauze 30–60 min | Zanon 2000 |
| 5% compress × 10 min q1h/q2h/q3h | Day 1/2/3 intermittent compression; 24h hospital stay | Frachon 2005 |
| 5% mouthwash + 1g TID oral | Socket flood with 5% TXA + Surgicel; severe hemophilia B | Hewson 2011 |
| Oral 1g TID × 10 days | Routine post-op in oral surgery protocol | Fribourg 2024 |
| Mouthwash alone (no factor) | For dental scaling in hemophilia — non-inferior to factor | Lee 2005 (RCT) |

### Anesthesia safety
- **Infiltration (±adrenaline)**: Safe without factor correction across all included studies
- **IAN block**: Requires factor correction (deep intramuscular; life-threatening hematoma risk)
- **General anesthesia**: Safe with appropriate factor coverage

### Emicizumab era
- Aruche 2022: Severe hemophilia A on emicizumab prophylaxis → extraction with TXA + local measures only, no additional factor → successful
