## Three-line Summary

Prospective single-blind case-control study (Bursa Uludağ University, Turkey, Jul 2022–Jul 2024; 599 screened, 430 enrolled) comparing 211 patients on uninterrupted DOACs (apixaban 69, dabigatran 29, edoxaban 55, rivaroxaban 58) with 219 non-anticoagulated healthy controls after single-tooth extraction, with the operating surgeon blinded to group at both surgery and day-7 assessment.

Bleeding grades were significantly worse in DOAC patients (grade 0 in 47.9% vs 73.1% of controls; grade 2–2 4.3% vs 0%; grade 3 8.1% vs 0%; χ²=48.40, p<0.001), with a clear per-agent gradient (rivaroxaban grade 3 13.8%, edoxaban 7.3%, apixaban 7.2%, dabigatran 0%; χ²=47.2, p<0.001), while pain was identical (VAS 4.01±2.01 vs 3.95±2.04, p=0.848) and only bone exposure differed among healing outcomes (4.7% vs 1.4%, p=0.039).

Uninterrupted DOAC extraction remains clinically manageable but is not bleeding-neutral — risk is agent-specific and warrants individualized planning rather than a uniform protocol; note the deliberately minimal hemostasis protocol (no sutures, no routine hemostatic agents) inflates absolute bleeding rates relative to normal practice.

## Summary

Most of the DOAC extraction literature answers the question "should the drug be interrupted?" by comparing continued against discontinued patients — a design that cannot say how much absolute bleeding burden a DOAC patient carries relative to a healthy one. This Turkish prospective case-control study asks the second question directly, comparing 211 patients extracted while continuing apixaban, dabigatran, edoxaban, or rivaroxaban against 219 healthy, non-anticoagulated controls, with the same experienced oral surgeon performing every extraction and assessing every day-7 outcome while blinded to group allocation.

The answer is a qualified one. DOAC patients bled significantly more: only 47.9% reached grade 0 (no bleeding) versus 73.1% of controls, and the two most severe grades — delayed oozing (2–2) and hemorrhage requiring treatment beyond compression (grade 3) — occurred in 4.3% and 8.1% of cases but in **zero** controls (χ²=48.40, p<0.001). Yet no patient required anything beyond a compression brace or electrocoagulation, and the author's conclusion is that extraction under uninterrupted DOAC therapy is clinically safe.

Two findings sharpen this. First, bleeding severity is not a class property: rivaroxaban users had 13.8% grade 3 bleeding while dabigatran users had none at all, reproducing the rivaroxaban-worst ordering reported by Inokoshi, Bensi, and Berton. Second, bleeding dissociates from the patient's experience — VAS pain was statistically identical between groups (4.01 vs 3.95, p=0.848) and across all four agents (p=0.689), and soft-tissue healing (erythema/edema, suppuration) was likewise unaffected. Only bone exposure was more frequent under DOACs (4.7% vs 1.4%, p=0.039), and notably in an ordering *inverse* to the bleeding gradient (dabigatran 13.8%, edoxaban 9.1%, rivaroxaban 0%), which the author attributes speculatively to clot stability and delayed epithelialization rather than to blood loss.

The single most important qualifier for reading these numbers into practice: local hemostatic agents were deliberately withheld and no sutures were placed, expressly so that the natural course of hemostasis under anticoagulation could be observed. The reported rates therefore describe a floor-of-intervention scenario, not routine care with sutures and packing.

## Key Contributions

- First prospective DOAC-extraction study combining a **non-anticoagulated healthy control arm** with **blinding of both the operating surgeon and the day-7 outcome assessor**, separating anticoagulation status from operator expectation.
- Establishes the **absolute** bleeding burden of DOAC patients versus healthy patients, complementing the continue-vs-discontinue literature that can only report relative risk within anticoagulated populations.
- Grades bleeding on a five-level ordinal scale that **separates same-day prolonged hemostasis (grade 2−1) from delayed next-day oozing (grade 2−2)**, distinguishing immediate from delayed events that binary designs collapse.
- Measures **pain (VAS) and soft-tissue healing** alongside bleeding in the same cohort, demonstrating that these outcomes dissociate — bleeding differs, pain and mucosal healing do not.
- Reports a **within-cohort per-agent gradient across all four DOACs**, supporting agent-specific rather than class-level preoperative risk assessment.

## Methodology

- **Design**: prospective observational case-control, single-blind (blinded operator and assessor), STROBE-compliant; Bursa Uludağ University, July 2022 – July 2024. Ethics approval 13/06/2022, decision 12/26.
- **Flow**: 599 screened → 430 enrolled (211 cases, 219 controls).
- **Cases**: adults ≥18 on apixaban, dabigatran, edoxaban, or rivaroxaban, needing single-tooth extraction under local anesthesia. **Controls**: healthy adults on no anticoagulant/antiplatelet within 4 weeks.
- **Exclusions (both arms)**: concomitant anticoagulant or antiplatelet agents, thromboembolic event within 3 months, uncontrolled systemic disease, active local infection, pregnancy/breastfeeding, head-and-neck radiotherapy or bisphosphonate history, and **any case requiring flap elevation or osteotomy**.
- **Surgery**: one surgeon with ≥8 years' experience, blinded to group; 0.12% chlorhexidine rinse; 2% lidocaine with 1:80,000 epinephrine; atraumatic extraction with periotomes and elevators; no flaps, no osteotomy, **no sutures**.
- **Hemostasis (deliberately minimal)**: gauze pressure 30 min, repeat cycle if needed, ≥30 min observation. **Oxidized cellulose and gelatin sponge intentionally withheld** to observe natural hemostasis. DOACs continued uninterrupted. Analgesia: paracetamol 500–1000 mg PRN, NSAIDs excluded.
- **Bleeding scale (day 7)**: grade 0 none; 1 excess clot, no treatment; 2−1 hemostasis needing >30 min compression; 2−2 oozing on or after the next day, controlled by simple compression; 3 hemorrhage needing treatment beyond compression.
- **Pain**: 10 cm VAS, patient-marked **average** pain over the first postoperative week, read in mm at day 7.
- **Healing**: binary present/absent for erythema (within 5 mm of socket margin), edema, suppuration, bone exposure.
- **Power**: a priori G*Power — w=0.2, α=0.05 → power >0.95 (chi-square) at n=350; Cohen's d=0.4 → power ≈0.95 (t-test).
- **Statistics**: SPSS 22.0; chi-square for bleeding and complications, Mann-Whitney U and Kruskal-Wallis H for pain, Welch t for age; α=0.05.

## Results

**Baseline — matched except age**

| Variable | Case (DOAC, n=211) | Control (n=219) | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Male | 99 (46.9%) | 113 (51.6%) | χ²=1.004, p=0.316 |
| Prior extraction history | 126 (59.7%) | 135 (61.6%) | χ²=0.167, p=0.683 |
| Comorbidity present | 38 (18.0%) | 43 (19.6%) | χ²=0.203, p=0.652 |
| Age (mean ± SD, y) | 55.6 ± 10.5 | 51.7 ± 9.1 | Welch t=4.14, **p<0.0001** |

**Primary outcome — bleeding grade (χ²=48.40, p<0.001)**

| Bleeding grade | Case (n=211) | Control (n=219) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (none) | 101 (47.9%) | 160 (73.1%) |
| 1 (excess clot, no treatment) | 63 (29.9%) | 53 (24.2%) |
| 2−1 (>30 min compression) | 21 (10.0%) | 6 (2.7%) |
| 2−2 (delayed oozing) | 9 (4.3%) | 0 (0.0%) |
| 3 (beyond compression) | 17 (8.1%) | 0 (0.0%) |

**Per-agent gradient (χ²=47.2, p<0.001)**

| Subgroup | n | Grade 0 | Grade 2−1 | Grade 2−2 | Grade 3 | Grade ≥2−1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rivaroxaban | 58 | 37.9% | 6.9% | 6.9% | **13.8%** | 27.6% |
| Apixaban | 69 | 50.7% | 15.9% | 4.3% | 7.2% | 27.4% |
| Edoxaban | 55 | 47.3% | 7.3% | 3.6% | 7.3% | 18.2% |
| Dabigatran | 29 | 62.1% | 6.9% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 6.9% |
| Control | 219 | 73.1% | 2.7% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 2.7% |

**Secondary outcomes**

| Outcome | Case | Control | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain, VAS (mean ± SD) | 4.01 ± 2.01 | 3.95 ± 2.04 | z=0.191, **p=0.848 (NS)** |
| Pain across 5 subgroups | 3.90–4.22 | 3.95 | H=2.2, **p=0.689 (NS)** |
| Erythema/edema | 156 (73.9%) | 161 (73.5%) | χ²=0.008, **p=0.930 (NS)** |
| Suppuration | 8 (3.8%) | 6 (2.7%) | χ²=0.361, **p=0.548 (NS)** |
| Bone exposure | 10 (4.7%) | 3 (1.4%) | χ²=4.266, **p=0.039 (sig.)** |

Bone exposure by agent (χ²=18.42, p<0.001): dabigatran 13.8%, edoxaban 9.1%, apixaban 1.4%, rivaroxaban 0.0%, control 1.4% — an ordering **inverse** to the bleeding gradient.

**Limitations**: hemostasis protocol deliberately omitted sutures and local hemostatic agents, so absolute bleeding rates overstate routine practice; renal/hepatic function not stratified; DOAC arm significantly older with no adjustment; 7-day follow-up may miss late complications; tooth type/location not standardized and only simple single-tooth extractions included; single center and single operator; dabigatran subgroup small (n=29); patients unblinded, so self-reported pain and bleeding history remain open to reporting bias.
