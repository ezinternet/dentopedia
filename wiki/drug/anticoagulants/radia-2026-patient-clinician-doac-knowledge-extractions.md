## Three-line Summary

Three-month prospective service evaluation at Liverpool University Dental Hospital: 26 patients on DOAC or antiplatelet therapy assessed, 24 of whom underwent extractions (most three or fewer routine extractions).

Patient-knowledge gaps dominated — 6 of 26 patients had received no initial information about their medication, only 11 of 26 were aware of its bleeding risks, and 3 patients altered their own regimen against clinician advice.

Bleeding outcomes were unremarkable at this scale (4 patients with minor day-one bleeding, all self-managed; none beyond day one), so the usable signal is the education and documentation gap, not any bleeding rate.

## Summary

Almost all of the wiki's direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) evidence answers a clinician-facing question: continue or interrupt, and which local haemostatic measure. This British Dental Journal service evaluation asks the adjacent question that those designs cannot reach — what does the patient actually know about the drug they are taking into the extraction chair?

Over three months at Liverpool University Dental Hospital, 26 patients on anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy were assessed; 24 proceeded to extraction, most needing three or fewer routine extractions. Alongside the treatment plan and the clinician's recorded advice, patients completed a questionnaire on their understanding of their own medication and were telephoned on day 1 and day 5 for complications.

The education findings are the point. Six of the 26 patients had received no initial information about their medication at all, and only 11 of the 26 were aware that it carried a bleeding risk — meaning most of this cohort arrived without the single fact that governs their peri-operative risk. Three patients went further and altered their regimen against clinician advice: a self-directed change in anticoagulation is a thrombotic and haemorrhagic hazard in both directions, and it is invisible to any study that measures only what the protocol prescribed.

Bleeding outcomes were benign — 4 patients had minor day-one bleeding, all self-managed, and none reported bleeding after day one — but at n=24 these counts carry no rate information and are recorded here only to show that the education gap did not, in this small group, translate into a bleeding signal. The authors' conclusion is about process: multimodal information delivery, guideline adherence, and robust documentation.

## Key Contributions

- Supplies the **patient-knowledge dimension** absent from the wiki's clinician-facing DOAC pages — what patients understood, not what the protocol specified.
- Documents **3 patients who self-altered their anticoagulant or antiplatelet regimen against advice**, an event class that continuation-vs-interruption studies structurally cannot observe.
- Reframes the improvement target from drug-interruption protocol refinement to **patient education and documentation**.
- Provides a UK teaching-hospital snapshot of real-world guideline adherence, against which systematic-review recommendations can be audited.

## Methodology

- **Design**: three-month prospective service evaluation (NHS audit family) — no control group, no randomisation, no power calculation.
- **Setting**: Liverpool University Dental Hospital, United Kingdom.
- **Participants**: 26 patients on anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy; 24 underwent extraction; most had three or fewer routine extractions.
- **Data collected**: demographics, DOAC/antiplatelet agent, treatment plan, and the clinician's recorded advice on peri-operative medication management.
- **Patient measure**: self-completed questionnaire on medication understanding — whether information had been given, and whether bleeding risk was known.
- **Follow-up**: telephone contact at day 1 and day 5 to capture post-operative complications.
- **Haemostasis**: local haemostatic measures, reported by the authors as effective; specific agents not enumerated in the abstract.

## Results

Reported as raw counts. At this n, converting to percentages would imply a precision the design does not support.

- **Extractions performed**: 24 of 26 patients; most required three or fewer routine extractions.
- **No initial information about their medication**: 6 patients.
- **Aware of the medication's bleeding risk**: only 11 patients.
- **Altered their regimen against clinician advice**: 3 patients — the principal safety finding.
- **Minor day-one bleeding**: 4 patients, all self-managed.
- **Bleeding beyond day one**: none.
- **Clinical takeaway**: this paper supports statements about *patient education and consent process*, not about bleeding incidence. For bleeding rates, cite the cohorts and systematic reviews below instead.

### Limitations

Single UK dental-hospital service evaluation; n=26 (24 extracted); no control group; patient understanding is self-reported by questionnaire and unvalidated against the actual consultation; and the study was **not designed or powered to estimate bleeding incidence**. DOAC and antiplatelet patients are pooled, so drug-class-specific inference is unavailable.
