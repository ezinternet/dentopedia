## Three-line Summary

Bench fabrication study (Naresuan University, Thailand) of a ready-to-use freeze-dried tranexamic acid (TXA)-impregnated cotton gauze at 2.5% and 5% (w/v), characterised by FESEM/FTIR, tensile testing, UV-vis drug release, MTT cytotoxicity on 3T3 fibroblasts, and in vitro blood clotting time (BCT) / plasma recalcification time (PRT) on citrated blood from 6 healthy volunteers.

Both TXA gauzes shortened BCT and PRT significantly vs plain gauze (p<0.05) with 2.5% statistically indistinguishable from 5%, released ~87% of loaded TXA within the first minute (burst complete by 3 min), preserved 3T3 fibroblast viability (75.2%/83.7% at 24 h vs QuikClot 48.4%), and retained hemostatic bioactivity unchanged across 180 days at both 30 °C/65% RH (ICH Zone IV) and 4 °C.

This is preclinical bench evidence only — no patient was treated, no anticoagulated subject was studied, and the authors explicitly name "the absence of in vivo validation" as a limitation; the gauze is a manufacturable prototype whose clinical efficacy in anticoagulated patients remains entirely untested.

## 세줄요약

태국 나레수안대 벤치 연구 — 동결건조로 트라넥삼산 (Tranexamic Acid, TXA)을 코팅한 즉시사용 (ready-to-use) 면 거즈 2.5%·5% (w/v)를 제작해 FESEM/FTIR 특성분석, 인장시험, UV-vis 약물방출, 3T3 섬유아세포 MTT 세포독성, 그리고 건강 지원자 6명의 구연산 처리 혈액을 쓴 시험관 내 혈액응고시간 (Blood Clotting Time, BCT)·혈장재석회화시간 (Plasma Recalcification Time, PRT)으로 평가.

두 TXA 거즈 모두 순수 거즈 대비 BCT·PRT를 유의하게 단축 (p<0.05)했고 2.5%와 5% 사이엔 차이가 없었으며, 담지 TXA의 약 87%가 1분 내 방출 (버스트는 3분에 종료), 3T3 세포생존율 유지 (24시간 75.2%/83.7% vs QuikClot 48.4%), 30 °C/65% RH (ICH Zone IV)와 4 °C 양 조건 180일간 지혈 활성 변화 없음.

**이건 전임상 벤치 근거일 뿐이다** — 치료받은 환자도, 연구된 항응고 환자도 없고 저자들 스스로 "생체 내 (in vivo) 검증 부재"를 한계로 명시한다; 제조 가능한 시제품일 뿐 항응고 환자에서의 임상 유효성은 전혀 검증되지 않았다.

## Summary

Topical tranexamic acid (TXA) is well established in oral surgery, but every current delivery route is prepared at the point of use: a mouthwash, an irrigant, or a standard gauze soaked chair-side in 5% TXA solution. Each carries dosage variability, added chair time, bulk and transport burden, and short shelf life — and the worst case, patients dissolving TXA tablets at home, is the least controlled of all. This study asks whether the drug can instead be fixed onto the gauze at the factory.

The answer at the bench is yes. Freeze-drying deposits TXA onto ordinary 100% cotton medical gauze with ~100% loading efficiency, verified structurally (FESEM crystal deposits on fibers) and chemically (FTIR peaks at 1532, 1380, 1008 cm⁻¹ preserved). The resulting dressing shortens in vitro clotting times, keeps fibroblasts alive far better than the only commercial prefabricated hemostat (QuikClot), forms visibly denser fibrin networks, and survives six months at Zone IV heat and humidity without losing bioactivity. Two findings matter most practically: 2.5% (w/v) performs identically to 5%, so the lower dose suffices; and release is a burst — ~87% in the first minute — which maps well onto the 30–60 minutes of gauze-biting pressure that follows an extraction.

The scope limit is absolute and must not be blurred. Blood came from six healthy volunteers who were specifically screened to *exclude* anyone on anticoagulants, antiplatelets, NSAIDs, or SSRIs, and who had no coagulation disorder. No patient was treated. There was no animal experiment either — the Animals/Mice MeSH terms come from the 3T3 mouse fibroblast cell line in the MTT assay, and the Discussion lists rat tail amputation and porcine extraction models as *future* work that must precede any clinical trial. The abstract's closing phrase — a promising material "especially for patients on anticoagulant therapy" — is therefore a stated aspiration about where the material is headed, not a result this study produced. What the wiki gains here is a delivery-format engineering result, not clinical efficacy evidence.

## Key Contributions

- Converts ordinary medical cotton gauze into a prefabricated, dose-standardised TXA dressing by a single freeze-drying step, directly attacking the dosage-variability and preparation-burden problem that the clinical TXA literature repeatedly names.
- Establishes bench dose equivalence: 2.5% (w/v) matches 5% on hemostatic performance, physicochemical structure, and biocompatibility — halving drug load without measurable cost.
- Supplies 180-day real-time (not accelerated) stability under ICH Zone IV conditions, the shelf-life and transportability argument that liquid TXA formats structurally cannot make.
- Benchmarks against QuikClot combat gauze, the only commercially available prefabricated hemostatic gauze, showing far better fibroblast biocompatibility despite slower clotting.
- Positions low-cost familiar cotton gauze against "high-tech" TXA scaffolds and aerogels whose adoption is limited by production cost and application complexity.

## Methodology

- **Design**: in vitro / bench materials-science study — fabrication, characterisation, in vitro coagulation assays, cytotoxicity, and real-time stability. **No in vivo animal experiment; no clinical arm.** Ethics: Naresuan University REC 059/2025, written informed consent (Helsinki).
- **Fabrication**: 2.5 mL of 25 or 50 mg/mL TXA solution onto a 5 × 5 cm² 100% cotton gauze → 5 °C for 1 h → −80 °C overnight → freeze-dried at −50 °C for 48 h. Theoretical load 62.5 mg (2.5%) and 125 mg (5%) per 25 cm².
- **Characterisation**: FESEM at 10 kV for fiber-surface morphology and fibrin architecture; ATR/FTIR 4000–400 cm⁻¹ for TXA chemical integrity; Instron tensile testing at 2 mm/min per ASTM D882-12, dry and wet, n=3.
- **Blood source**: 6 healthy volunteers aged 18–65 (n=6 biological donors). Exclusions: anticoagulants, antiplatelets, NSAIDs or SSRIs within 10 days; pregnancy/breastfeeding; hereditary or acquired coagulation disorders — i.e. the anticoagulated population the material targets was deliberately absent.
- **Drug release**: full-size gauze in 30 mL PBS pH 7.4 at 37 ± 0.5 °C, sampled at 1, 3, 5, 10, 15, 30, 60 min; UV-vis at 220 nm; standard curve Y = 0.1676X + 0.0361, R² = 0.9993.
- **Cytotoxicity**: MTT on 3T3 mouse fibroblasts, indirect extract contact (0.2 g/mL, 24 h), read at 24 h and 72 h.
- **BCT**: 100 µL PBS aliquot from 5-min gauze immersion + 250 µL of 3.2% citrated whole blood, vortex, 3 min at 37 °C, recalcified with 25 µL of 0.25 M CaCl₂, tilted every 15 s.
- **PRT**: 250 µL platelet-poor plasma + 100 µL aliquot, recalcified identically, inspected every 15 s for silky fibrin thread.
- **Stability**: packed in opaque zip-lock bags; 30 °C/65% RH (ICH Zone IV long-term) and 4 °C/65% RH; sampled at 0, 15, 30, 90, 180 days with BCT as the bioactivity readout.
- **Comparator**: QuikClot combat gauze (Teleflex) as positive control — a functional benchmark only, since its kaolin/intrinsic-pathway mechanism differs from TXA's antifibrinolysis.
- **Statistics**: one-way ANOVA with Tukey post-hoc, n=6 donors, p<0.05.

## Results

- **Dose equivalence — the headline**: BCT and PRT were both significantly shorter for TXA gauze than plain gauze (p<0.05), and **2.5% vs 5% showed no significant difference** on either assay, nor on biocompatibility or physicochemical structure. The lower dose therefore suffices, maximising cost-effectiveness and minimising drug exposure.
- **Burst release**: **~87% of the loaded TXA released within the first minute**, the remaining ~13% over minutes 2–3 — burst complete by 3 min. Cumulative release reached 100% at 60 min equilibrium, implying ~100% loading efficiency and confirming actual doses of 62.5 mg and 125 mg per gauze. This matches the 30–60 min of post-extraction gauze-biting pressure.
- **Stability over 180 days**: BCT ranged **6.25–8.75 min across all groups, timepoints, and both storage conditions, with no significant difference from day-0 freshly prepared gauze** — at both 30 °C/65% RH (ICH Zone IV) and 4 °C/65% RH. Hemostatic bioactivity survives six months anywhere in the 4–30 °C range.
- **Cytotoxicity (3T3, % viability)** — 24 h: plain 100, 2.5% TXA 75.23, 5% TXA 83.68, QuikClot 48.43. 72 h: plain 90.49, 2.5% TXA 72.82, 5% TXA 79.42, QuikClot 45.73. **Only QuikClot was significantly toxic.**
- **QuikClot comparison**: significantly shorter BCT and PRT than all other groups (p<0.05), consistent with kaolin-driven intrinsic-pathway activation — but at roughly half the fibroblast viability, and with a loose fibrin network resembling plain gauze.
- **Fibrin architecture**: both TXA gauzes produced visibly denser fibrin networks with barely visible inter-fibril gaps and embedded red blood cells and platelets, versus loose wide-gapped networks on plain gauze *and* QuikClot — the structural correlate of antifibrinolytic clot stabilisation.
- **Coating verified**: FESEM showed TXA crystals densifying on fibers with concentration; FTIR retained TXA peaks at 1532 cm⁻¹ (amide N-H), 1380 cm⁻¹ (carboxylic), 1008 cm⁻¹ (C-N), confirming lyophilisation did not alter the drug's functional structure.
- **Mechanical**: dry Young's modulus fell from 2.71 (plain) to 2.00 (2.5%) and 2.10 MPa (5%) — significant vs plain but concentration-independent; wet 3.22 / 3.29 / 3.59 MPa. Judged adequate against commercial hemostatic dressings at 0.24–0.95 MPa dry.
- **Fluid absorption**: plain gauze ~750% of own weight; TXA gauzes significantly lower and decreasing with concentration, but the 500–800% range remains within the 100–900% ideal band for medical gauze.
- **Limitations**: no in vivo validation (authors' own first-listed limitation, with rat tail amputation, rodent intraoral, and porcine extraction models planned before any clinical trial); no clinical arm and no anticoagulated subject; single non-oral cell line (3T3, not human gingival fibroblasts or oral keratinocytes); sterilisation method unresolved (gamma/EtO/autoclave may degrade fiber or drug); burst-only kinetics with no sustained phase toward the theoretically ideal ~3 h fibrinolysis protection; 180-day laboratory-scale data insufficient for registration; no head-to-head against chair-side freshly impregnated TXA gauze, the actual incumbent practice; n=6 donors.
