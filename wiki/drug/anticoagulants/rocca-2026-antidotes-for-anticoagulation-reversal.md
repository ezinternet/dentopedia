## Three-line Summary

NEJM narrative review (2026) of pharmacologic reversal strategies for anticoagulant-associated major bleeding, organized by anticoagulant and antidote pharmacology, regulatory indication and evidence quality.
Protamine neutralizes unfractionated heparin but no specific antidote exists for LMWH or fondaparinux; 4F-PCC effectively reverses VKAs; idarucizumab reverses dabigatran (delayed rebound possible); andexanet alfa targets factor Xa inhibitors but efficacy-safety, rebound, perioperative use and cost concerns drive off-label 4F-PCC use.
For dentistry, it confirms the antidote map while placing every agent in the major-bleeding setting — dosing, lab monitoring, thrombotic risk and perioperative guidance remain unsettled.

## Summary

This NEJM review argues that reversal choice should follow the pharmacology of the specific anticoagulant and antidote, its regulatory indication, the quality of evidence, patient factors and local availability. Its class map is the most compact the wiki holds: protamine for unfractionated heparin, nothing specific for LMWH or fondaparinux, 4F-PCC for VKAs, idarucizumab for dabigatran, and andexanet alfa for factor Xa inhibitors — with 4F-PCC increasingly used off-label for the latter. The abstract lists the field's open problems: dosing, validated laboratory monitoring, thrombotic-risk mitigation and perioperative guidelines.

## Key Contributions

- Complete antidote map across anticoagulant classes, current to 2026.
- **LMWH and fondaparinux have no specific antidote** — protamine is the UFH antidote.
- Dabigatran can rebound after idarucizumab.
- Andexanet uncertainties are pushing practice toward off-label 4F-PCC for factor Xa inhibitors.

## Results

| Anticoagulant | Antidote |
|---|---|
| Unfractionated heparin | Protamine sulfate |
| LMWH (e.g. enoxaparin), fondaparinux | No specific antidote |
| VKA (warfarin) | 4F-PCC |
| Dabigatran | Idarucizumab (watch for rebound) |
| Factor Xa inhibitors (apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban) | Andexanet alfa; off-label 4F-PCC |

**Dental reading.** The seminar slide paired enoxaparin with protamine. This review calls protamine the antidote for unfractionated heparin and says LMWH has no *specific* antidote. The older reviews held in the wiki still list protamine for LMWH, with dosing — 1 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin within 8 h, 0.5 mg/mg after 8 h — but only ~60% ([[drug/anticoagulants/yee-2019-emergency-reversal-of-anticoagulation]]) or ~50% ([[drug/anticoagulants/aldhaeefi-2023-practical-guide-for-anticoagulant-and]]) of the anti-Xa effect is reversed. The two positions agree once read together: protamine is a partial, non-specific option for enoxaparin, not a true antidote. For dental extraction on heparin, the held cohort controlled all bleeding with gauze pressure ([[drug/anticoagulants/lopez-silva-2025-post-extraction-bleeding-heparin]]).
