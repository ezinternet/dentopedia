## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 19 studies (18 cohorts + 1 RCT, n=2,878; searched to August 2015) on 4-factor prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC) for vitamin K antagonist (VKA)-associated bleeding, mostly intracranial haemorrhage.
INR 1 h after PCC was 1.4–1.9 vs 2.2–12 after fresh frozen plasma (FFP); mortality OR 0.64 (0.27–1.5) vs FFP and 0.41 (0.13–1.3) vs no treatment (both NS); thromboembolic events mean 2.5% with PCC vs 6.4% with FFP.
4F-PCC reverses VKA rapidly and with low thrombotic risk, but the evidence is observational (one RCT) and drawn from life-threatening medical bleeding — not dental bleeding, which held cohorts control locally.

## Summary

This PRISMA systematic review restricted itself to 4-factor PCC in patients bleeding on VKAs, excluding 3-factor and activated PCC and emergent-procedure reversal. Across 19 studies and 2,878 patients, PCC brought the INR to 1.4–1.9 within an hour, whereas FFP left it at 2.2–12, and PCC shortened time to correction against both FFP and no treatment. Pooled mortality favoured PCC numerically but not significantly (OR 0.64 vs FFP; 0.41 vs no treatment), and thromboembolic complications averaged 2.5%. The introduction anchors the role of vitamin K: intravenous vitamin K begins lowering the INR within 4 h and normalizes it after 12–16 h, oral vitamin K within about 24 h — so vitamin K is the definitive but slow reversal, and PCC or FFP supply immediate factor replacement.

## Key Contributions

- 4F-PCC normalizes INR within 1 h (1.4–1.9); FFP rarely achieves full normalization.
- Mortality benefit over FFP is **not significant** in a 4F-only analysis (OR 0.64, 0.27–1.5).
- Thromboembolic risk with PCC is low (mean 2.5%; 0–18%).
- Vitamin K timing: IV — INR falls from 4 h, normal at 12–16 h; oral — up to 24 h. Most included cohorts co-administered vitamin K with PCC.

## Methodology

- PubMed, EMBASE, CENTRAL to August 2015; two independent reviewers; Mantel–Haenszel random-effects pooling.
- 13 prospective cohorts, 5 retrospective cohorts, 1 RCT (Beriplex vs FFP, n=202); intracranial haemorrhage in 15 studies; mean age 67–78.
- PCC dose 5.3–80 IU/kg, typically 25–50 IU/kg; fixed, INR-based, weight-based or combined dosing.
- Quality: 6 good, 9 moderate, 4 poor.

**Dental reading.** These are patients with intracranial and major GI bleeding. In the wiki's dental cohorts on warfarin, post-extraction bleeding was controlled locally without any reversal ([[drug/anticoagulants/lu-2018-warfarin-antiplatelet-dental-extraction-management]] — 9.1% at INR <4.0).
