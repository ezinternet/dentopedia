## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered; PubMed/Ovid/Cochrane/Embase) of 4 systematic reviews assessing bleeding-related complications during/after dental extractions in patients on dual anti-platelet therapy (DAPT).

DAPT increases bleeding-related complication risk after dental extractions, but the difference may not be clinically significant since local haemostatic measures adequately controlled bleeding in the underlying SRs.

It may not be necessary to interrupt DAPT for dental extractions — local haemostasis is generally sufficient, while the thromboembolic complications of discontinuing DAPT may be more severe or fatal; abstract-only (no PMC full text).

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1016/j.jormas.2022.06.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jormas.2022.06.004)), this umbrella review appraises existing systematic reviews on the risk of bleeding-related complications during and after dental extractions for patients on dual anti-platelet therapy (DAPT) — a regimen prescribed after cerebrovascular or cardiovascular ischemic events. Conducted per PRISMA guidelines and registered on PROSPERO, the review searched PubMed, Ovid, Cochrane, and Embase, identifying 4 systematic reviews that met inclusion criteria.

The synthesis shows DAPT does increase the risk of bleeding-related complications after dental extractions, but this difference may not be clinically significant, because local haemostatic measures were adequate to control both primary and secondary bleeding across the included reviews.

Clinically: it may not be necessary to interrupt DAPT for dental extractions. Local haemostatic agents are generally sufficient to manage bleeding, whereas the complications of discontinuing DAPT (thromboembolic/cardiovascular events) may be more severe or even fatal — this umbrella review adds SR-of-SRs weight to the wiki's existing continue-therapy consensus.

## Key Contributions

- Synthesizes 4 existing **systematic reviews** on DAPT and dental-extraction bleeding — one evidence tier above a single SR/MA.
- PRISMA-guided and **PROSPERO-registered**, strengthening methodological rigor.
- Reinforces the continue-DAPT recommendation with explicit risk-tradeoff framing: manageable bleeding vs potentially fatal thromboembolic risk from discontinuation.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Umbrella review of systematic reviews.
- **Registration/reporting:** PROSPERO-registered, PRISMA-guided.
- **Databases:** PubMed, Ovid, Cochrane, Embase.
- **Included reviews:** 4 systematic reviews meeting inclusion criteria (identities and individual AMSTAR/GRADE ratings not available from abstract alone).

## Results

- DAPT patients show **increased bleeding-related complication risk** after dental extractions across the 4 included SRs.
- The increase **may not be clinically significant** — local haemostatic measures adequately controlled primary and secondary bleeding in the underlying reviews.
- **Authors' conclusion:** it may not be necessary to interrupt antiplatelet therapy for dental extractions; local haemostatic agents may suffice, while DAPT-discontinuation complications may be more severe/fatal.
