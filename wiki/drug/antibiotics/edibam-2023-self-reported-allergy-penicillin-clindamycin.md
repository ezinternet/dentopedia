## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD42022343716; PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science) of 4 retrospective cohort studies covering 10,853 implants — 1,210 placed under clindamycin for self-reported penicillin allergy vs 9,643 under amoxicillin.

Clindamycin-substituted patients were over three times more likely to undergo implant failure (OR 3.30, 95% CI 2.58–4.22, p < .00001), with cumulative failure 11.0% (95% CI 3.5–22.0%) vs 3.8% (95% CI 1.2–7.7%); comparative heterogeneity I² = 0%, but trial sequential analysis required 17,421 patients vs the 10,853 pooled.

Evidence is limited to retrospective studies with unverified (self-reported) allergy, so penicillin allergy, clindamycin, or their combination cannot be separated — the paper therefore proposes a penicillin-allergy delabeling protocol (low-risk: single 250 mg oral amoxicillin challenge + 1 h observation).

체계적 문헌고찰·메타분석(PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD42022343716; PubMed·Scopus·Web of Science) — 후향 코호트(Retrospective cohort) 4편, 임플란트 10,853개(자가보고 페니실린 알레르기로 클린다마이신 1,210개 vs 아목시실린 9,643개).

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis tested whether dental implant failure is higher in patients who receive clindamycin as a substitute for amoxicillin because of a self-reported penicillin allergy. From 572 records (PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science, searched June–September 2022) four retrospective cohort studies covering 10,853 implants were pooled: 1,210 implants in self-reported penicillin-allergic patients given clindamycin (114 failed) vs 9,643 in non-allergic patients given amoxicillin (252 failed). Fixed-effects pooling gave OR 3.30 (95% CI 2.58–4.22, p < .00001, I² = 0%); proportional pooling gave 11.0% (95% CI 3.5–22.0%) vs 3.8% (95% CI 1.2–7.7%) cumulative failure. Because every included study used unverified self-reported allergy and retrospective design, the authors cannot attribute the excess failure to the allergy, the drug, or both, and note the sample falls short of the trial-sequential required information size (17,421). The paper closes by proposing a risk-stratified penicillin-allergy delabeling protocol for implant patients — most (low-risk) patients need only a single 250 mg oral amoxicillin challenge with 1 h observation, higher-risk patients skin testing first, and SCAR patients (SJS/TEN, DRESS) no challenge at all.

## Key Contributions
- First meta-analysis demonstrating a pooled 3.3× higher odds of implant failure in clindamycin-substituted, self-reported penicillin-allergic patients (OR 3.30, 95% CI 2.58–4.22, p < .00001).
- Pooled failure proportions across 10,853 implants: 11.0% (clindamycin) vs 3.8% (amoxicillin) — 114/1,210 vs 252/9,643.
- Supplies a practical, risk-stratified penicillin-allergy delabeling protocol tailored to dental implant patients (oral challenge ± skin testing; SCAR exclusion; 1-1-1 criterion).
- TSA quantifies remaining uncertainty (RIS 17,421 vs 10,853 pooled) and shows significance already crossed after the second study — a concrete target for future prospective trials.

## Methodology
PRISMA 2020 systematic review registered on PROSPERO (CRD42022343716). Three databases (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science) plus reference hand-search; searches run 22 June and 19 September 2022. Inclusion: English-language retrospective cohort/case-control/observational studies in healthy adults >18 needing extraction then implant treatment, with a test group self-reporting penicillin allergy; exclusion covered case reports/series, reviews, radiotherapy patients, uncontrolled systemic/bone disease, animal/cell studies, and multi-antibiotic cases. 572 records → 24 full texts → 4 studies (Wagenberg & Froum 2006; French et al. 2016; Salomó-Coll et al. 2018; Zahra et al. 2022), all retrospective cohorts at low risk of bias. Comparative OR pooled with a fixed-effects model; single-arm proportions pooled with Freeman–Tukey transformation in a random-effects model (MedCalc 20.218); heterogeneity (I²), Egger's test, fail-safe N, leave-one-out sensitivity, meta-regression, and TSA performed.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Studies included | 4 retrospective cohorts (2006–2022; USA ×2, Canada, Spain) |
| Implants pooled | 10,853 (clindamycin 1,210 / amoxicillin 9,643) |
| Failures | 114/1,210 (clindamycin) vs 252/9,643 (amoxicillin) |
| Comparative odds ratio | OR 3.30 (95% CI 2.58–4.22), p < .00001, I² = 0% |
| Pooled failure — clindamycin | 11.0% (95% CI 3.5–22.0%), I² = 96.2% (Discussion cites 10.5%, 4.6–18.5%) |

| Per-study failure (test vs control) | Wagenberg 2006: 8.52% vs 2.95% (16-y); French 2016: 2.10% vs 0.80% (10-y); Salomó-Coll 2018: 18.86% vs 5.17% (1-y); Zahra 2022: 19.90% vs 8.40% (1-y) |
