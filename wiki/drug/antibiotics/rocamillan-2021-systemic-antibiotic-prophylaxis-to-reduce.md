## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42021248348; PubMed/MEDLINE + Scielo + Cochrane CENTRAL to 9 Apr 2021) of 11 RCTs — 2144 patients, 4037 implants, ≥3-month follow-up — on whether systemic antibiotic prophylaxis prevents early implant failure and which regimen is best.

Prophylaxis significantly reduced early implant failure (RR 0.30, 95% CI 0.19–0.47, p<0.00001; I²=0%; 1.55% vs 4.61% failure), a single preoperative dose was sufficient (RR 0.34, 95% CI 0.21–0.53), and preoperative-only vs pre+postoperative showed no difference (RR 0.57, NS) — so postoperative continuation is not justified.

The large RR comes with an evidence-quality ceiling: only 4 of 11 RCTs were low risk of bias (the rest "some concerns", mostly deviations from intended interventions) and the dose comparison rested on just two estimable studies — which is why the later placebo-only, low-RoB reviews reach the opposite (null) conclusion in healthy patients.

## Summary

Roca-Millan et al. (Antibiotics 2021) conducted a PRISMA/PROSPERO-registered SR+MA of 11 RCTs (2144 patients, 4037 implants, mean follow-up 4.5 months) testing whether systemic antibiotic prophylaxis prevents early implant failure and which regimen is most appropriate. Pooled across the 8 trials with a no-antibiotic or placebo comparator, prophylaxis was strongly protective: RR 0.30 (95% CI 0.19–0.47, p<0.00001) with no statistical heterogeneity (I²=0%), corresponding to a failure rate of 1.55% in antibiotic arms versus 4.61% in control arms. On the regimen question the review found no significant difference between a preoperative-only and a pre- plus postoperative course (RR 0.57, 95% CI 0.21–1.55, NS), and a single preoperative dose compared with no antibiotic was sufficient on its own (RR 0.34, 95% CI 0.21–0.53). Only one of the eight comparator trials individually reached significance. The authors conclude that a single preoperative antibiotic dose is indicated in healthy patients, that postoperative continuation is unnecessary, and that the effect would be even less disputable in at-risk patients — while flagging the small sample, an unclear/some-concerns risk of bias in 7 of 11 RCTs (mainly blinding and deviations from intended interventions), and the complete absence of dose-comparison RCTs as the study's limitations.

## Key Contributions

- Supplies the **largest pooled effect estimate** in the implant-prophylaxis literature (RR 0.30) with **I² = 0%**, and the raw failure proportions (1.55% vs 4.61%) that anchor absolute-effect reasoning.
- Separates the regimen question from the yes/no question: **preoperative-only ≈ pre+postoperative** (RR 0.57, NS) → postoperative continuation adds nothing.
- Isolates the **single preoperative dose** as sufficient against no antibiotic (RR 0.34).
- Documents the **evidence-quality ceiling** honestly: 4/11 low risk of bias, 7 with "some concerns"; the highest-risk RoB 2 domain was deviations from intended interventions.
- Proposes a **metronidazole research direction** grounded in resistance-gene ecology (metronidazole-resistance genes rarer in peri-implant tissue than beta-lactam resistance genes) given the Gram-negative anaerobic component of implant-failure and peri-implantitis flora.
- Notes **local antibacterial delivery systems** as a route to maintain minimum inhibitory concentration through the osseointegration window.

## Methodology

- **Design**: PRISMA SR+MA; PROSPERO CRD42021248348; 11 RCTs published 2008–2019.
- **PICO**: edentulous / partially edentulous implant patients; any systemic antibiotic any dose/timing vs another regimen, placebo, or no antibiotic; outcome = early implant failure (loss before prosthetic loading).
- **Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Scielo, Cochrane Central Trials Database; English/Spanish; Jan 2000 – Apr 2021; search run 9 Apr 2021; plus hand search. Search terms: (antibiotics OR anti-bacterial agents OR amoxicillin) AND (dental implant OR implantology OR implant placement OR implant failure).
- **Inclusion/Exclusion**: RCTs, ≥3 months follow-up, regimen clearly described; excluded local (non-systemic) application and patients needing prophylaxis for medical reasons.
- **n**: 2144 patients / 4037 implants; 5 single-center (327 patients / 624 implants) + 6 multicenter (1817 patients / 3413 implants); 4 university-based, 7 private practice.
- **Comparators**: 5 RCT vs placebo; 3 RCT vs no antibiotic; 3 RCT regimen-vs-regimen only.
- **Regimens**: amoxicillin in all studies (clindamycin 600 mg for penicillin allergy in one). Standard single dose 2 g amoxicillin 1 h preop (1 g in one study, 3 g in another). Postoperative-only = 7 days; pre+postoperative = 7 days in two studies, 2–3 days in three.
- **Statistics**: RevMan 5.4; risk ratio + 95% CI; fixed-effect model (all I² < 40%; random-effects was the pre-specified rule at I² ≥ 40%); p<0.05; Chi²/I² for heterogeneity; **unit of analysis = implant**.
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB 2 (5 domains), Robvis plots.

## Results

### Pooled meta-analyses (fixed-effect)

| Comparison | Studies | RR (95% CI) | p | Heterogeneity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Antibiotic prophylaxis (any regimen) vs none/placebo | 8 | 0.30 (0.19–0.47) | <0.00001 | I²=0%, p=0.54 |
| Preoperative-only vs pre+postoperative | 4 | 0.57 (0.21–1.55) | 0.27 (NS) | I²=0%, p=0.37 |
| Single preoperative dose vs no antibiotic | 6 | 0.34 (0.21–0.53) | <0.00001 | I²=0%, p=0.61 |

> [!warning] Read the regimen row with care
> In 2 of the 4 studies in the preoperative-vs-pre+postoperative comparison there were **zero implant failures in either arm**, so the effect was not estimable there — the pooled RR 0.57 effectively rests on only two studies [17,19]. A non-significant result driven by two estimable trials is weak evidence of *equivalence*, not proof of it.

### Absolute failure rates

| Arm | Early implant failure |
|---|---|
| Antibiotic groups | 1.55% |
| No antibiotic / placebo groups | 4.61% |

### Individual-trial results and risk of bias

- 4/11 RCTs low overall risk of bias; 7/11 "some concerns".
- Highest-risk RoB 2 domain: **deviations from intended interventions**. Lowest-risk domains: missing outcome data and outcome measurement.
- Of the 8 trials with a no-antibiotic/placebo comparator, **only one** individually reached statistical significance (preoperative amoxicillin 2 g *or* clindamycin 600 mg vs no antibiotic); the rest showed fewer failures in the antibiotic arm without significance.
- No RCT compared different single preoperative amoxicillin doses.
