## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42023427802; PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science to May 18, 2023) of 7 double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs, 1,846 participants, testing whether preoperative antibiotic prophylaxis prevents early osseointegration complications and implant failure within 6 months in immunocompetent patients.

Six trials used amoxicillin and one clindamycin 600 mg; NO trial showed a statistically significant difference in implant failure or postoperative complications (all p > 0.05; closest: Nolan et al. 0/27 vs 5/28 failures, p = 0.0515; sole significant outcome anywhere was less pain at day 7 in Nolan, p = 0.016); RoB 2: four studies low risk, three some concerns.

Findings support NON-recommendation of routine antibiotic prophylaxis before implant surgery in healthy patients — concordant with AFSSAPS 2011, EAO 2015 and ITI positions and with antimicrobial-resistance stewardship; limited by narrative-only synthesis (no pooling), single-reviewer risk-of-bias assessment, and a three-database search without grey literature.

## Summary

This Bordeaux/Rome systematic review asks whether preoperative antibiotic prophylaxis, compared with placebo, prevents early osseointegration complications and failure within 6 months after implant surgery in immunocompetent patients. It deliberately restricts inclusion to double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs — the design filter that explains why prior reviews diverged — and synthesizes 7 trials (1,846 participants) narratively without meta-analysis. Six trials tested amoxicillin (four × 2 g one hour preoperatively; one × 3 g; one four-arm trial comparing preoperative vs immediate-postoperative vs preoperative-plus-short-course dosing) and one tested clindamycin 600 mg for penicillin-allergic contexts. Two trials included simultaneous bone grafting. No trial found a statistically significant difference in implant failure or postoperative complications (all p > 0.05). The only statistically significant finding across the entire review was lower postoperative pain at day 7 with antibiotics in Nolan et al. (p = 0.016). The authors conclude that routine prophylaxis is not justified in healthy patients given antimicrobial resistance (4.95 million deaths annually associated with resistant bacteria, 1.27 million directly attributable), aligning with AFSSAPS 2011, EAO 2015 and ITI recommendations. A secondary observation: all implant losses in Nolan occurred during multiple-implant surgeries and in procedures exceeding 1 h, hinting that surgical burden — not prophylaxis — drives early failure risk.

## Key Contributions

- Applies the strictest design filter in this question family (double-blind + placebo-controlled + immunocompetent + ≤6-month follow-up) and still finds zero statistically significant trials.
- Extends the null verdict beyond failure to secondary outcomes rarely synthesized together: surgical complications, patient-reported adverse events, and VAS pain.
- Quantifies the stewardship stakes: routine prescribing drives antimicrobial resistance, cost, and adverse effects without measurable benefit in healthy patients.
- Documents guideline convergence (AFSSAPS 2011, EAO 2015, ITI): all discourage or restrict prophylaxis in systemically healthy implant patients.

## Methodology

- PRISMA-reported SR; PROSPERO CRD42023427802; no meta-analysis performed (tabular/narrative synthesis only).
- PICOS: edentulous-area patients without immunodepression or high-risk heart disease; any preoperative antibiotic any dose/duration; comparator placebo; primary outcome early implant failure within 6 months; secondary surgical complications, adverse events, pain; double-blind RCTs ≤6-month follow-up in English/French.
- Search: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science with MeSH + free terms, completed May 18, 2023; no handsearching/grey literature; Zotero deduplication; Rayyan double-blind two-reviewer selection (JD, SC); independent dual data extraction.
- PRISMA flow: 1,852 records → 1,047 deduplicated → 20 screened in → 13 excluded at full text → 7 included.
- Risk of bias: RoB 2 by a single reviewer; no study excluded on RoB.

## Results

Individual-trial implant failure (test group vs control, verbatim from Table III):

- Esposito et al. 2008: 2/158 vs 8/158, p value = 0,104
- Anitua et al. 2009 (single implants): 2/52 vs 2/53, p value = 0,362
- Esposito et al. 2010: 5/252 vs 12/254, p value = 0,09
- Tan et al. 2014 (4 arms): 0/81, 0/82, 0/86 vs 1/80, p value = 0,47
- Nolan et al. 2014: 0/27 vs 5/28, p value = 0,0515
- Momand et al. 2022: 6/238 vs 7/235, p value = 0,75
- Santamaría Arrieta et al. 2022 (clindamycin 600 mg): 2/31 vs 0/31, p value = 0,246

Aggregate:

- Implant failure: no statistically significant difference in any trial (all p > 0.05).
- Postoperative complications: no statistically significant difference in any trial (all p > 0.05).
- Pain (VAS, 3 trials): only Nolan reported significant reduction with prophylaxis (day 7, p = 0.016).
- Risk-of-bias profile: 4 low risk / 3 some concerns; D3 (missing outcome data) and D5 (selective reporting) highest-risk domains; D1 (randomization) and D4 (measurement) low risk in ALL studies — credited to the double-blind restriction.
- Failure-context signals: all Nolan failures followed multiple-implant surgeries and procedures > 1 h; Anitua (single implants only) found no association between procedure time and failure.
