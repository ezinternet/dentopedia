## Three-line Summary

Cochrane systematic review + meta-analysis (search to 17 June 2013) of 6 RCTs, 1162 participants, comparing prophylactic antibiotics vs placebo/no antibiotics at dental implant placement.

Antibiotics (all amoxicillin regimens; mostly a single 2g or 3g preoperative oral dose) significantly reduced implant failures (RR 0.33, 95% CI 0.16–0.67, P=0.002, I²=0%; NNTB=25); prosthesis failure was borderline significant (RR 0.44, 95% CI 0.19–1.00); postoperative infections and adverse events showed no significant difference.

Evidence graded moderate quality; no trials compared different antibiotics or dosages, and the one trial evaluating different prophylaxis durations was high risk of bias with zero events in any arm — postoperative antibiotic benefit and optimal regimen remain unknown.

## Summary

This Cochrane review is the classic evidence base behind the widely taught "give 2g amoxicillin one hour before implant placement" protocol. Pooling 6 RCTs (1162 participants), prophylactic amoxicillin (mostly a single preoperative 2–3g oral dose) significantly reduced implant failure (RR 0.33) with an NNT of 25. Prosthesis-failure benefit was borderline significant, and no difference was found for postoperative infections or adverse events. Overall evidence was rated moderate quality by GRADE. No trial compared different antibiotics or dosages, and postoperative antibiotic benefit remains unresolved. A newer, methodologically stricter re-analysis restricted to placebo-controlled/double-blind/low-RoB trials (momand-2024) found the benefit does not survive tightened RCT-inclusion criteria — see supersession banner above.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes the trial-level pooled estimate (RR 0.33, NNTB 25) that underlies the standard preoperative-amoxicillin-2g implant prophylaxis protocol taught worldwide.
- GRADE summary-of-findings table converts the RR into an absolute-risk framing (6% failure without antibiotics → ~2% with) that is directly clinically actionable.
- Explicitly identifies the open questions (postoperative antibiotic value, comparative dosing/agent choice) that motivated subsequent SRs.

## Methodology

Cochrane SR+MA; searches of Cochrane Oral Health Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, EMBASE to 17 June 2013; RCTs with ≥3 months follow-up comparing prophylactic antibiotics vs no antibiotics/placebo in adults undergoing dental implant placement, excluding medically compromised populations (cardiac risk, immune compromise, diabetes, prior head/neck radiotherapy, penicillin allergy, active local infection, IV bisphosphonates, pregnancy, long-term NSAID use, bleeding disorders). Random-effects Mantel-Haenszel risk ratios (95% CI) for dichotomous outcomes; fixed-effect model for adverse events. Screening, risk-of-bias assessment and data extraction done in duplicate.

## Results

[확인] Implant failure: RR 0.33 (95% CI 0.16–0.67), 6 RCTs/1162 participants, I²=0%, NNTB=25 (95% CI 14–100) at a 6% no-antibiotic baseline failure rate.

[근거중간] Prosthesis failure: RR 0.44 (95% CI 0.19–1.00), 5 RCTs/1107 participants — borderline significant (P=0.05).

[근거없음/NS] Postoperative infections: RR 0.69 (95% CI 0.36–1.35). Adverse events: RR 1.00 (95% CI 0.06–15.85; only 2 minor events total).

No conclusive data on optimal duration (the one 4-arm duration-comparison trial was high risk of bias with zero events per 25-participant arm) or on comparative antibiotic agents/doses (none evaluated). Overall quality of evidence: moderate (GRADE).
