## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort of dental records (179 subjects, 374 implants) comparing patients reporting penicillin allergy (exposed) with unexposed patients at a 1:2 ratio, for surgical complications, early implant failure and preloading crestal bone loss (PLCBL).

Early failure occurred in 6.1% and surgical complications in 8.0% of implants (PLCBL 32.5%); crude prevalence of both was higher with reported allergy, but regression showed no independent association of allergy with any outcome (p > 0.05), while azithromycin and other alternatives had higher odds of surgical complications versus penicillin in exploratory models (p < 0.05).

Only the abstract was retrieved, so group sizes, effect sizes and the early-failure window are unverified; the authors call for larger studies.

## Summary

Retrospective cohort of 179 subjects and 374 implants examining self-reported penicillin allergy and alternative antibiotic regimens against postsurgical complications, early implant failure and PLCBL, using bivariate analyses and mixed-effects regression. Early failure 6.1%, surgical complications 8.0%, PLCBL 32.5% of implants. Reported allergy was associated with higher crude prevalence of complications and failure (p < 0.05) but not independently in regression (p > 0.05). Azithromycin raised crude complications; azithromycin or clindamycin had higher crude early failure than penicillin (p < 0.05). In exploratory multivariable models azithromycin and other alternatives had higher odds of surgical complications (p < 0.05); antibiotic type was not associated with PLCBL or early failure (p > 0.05).

## Key Contributions

- Separates the allergy label from the antibiotic actually prescribed as exposures.
- Adds a third outcome (PLCBL) to the usual failure endpoint.
- Suggests the crude allergy-failure link may be confounded, since it disappears in adjusted models.

## Methodology

Retrospective cohort from dental records; patients with a reported penicillin allergy matched by selection 1:2 to those without; patient- and implant-level factors recorded; mixed-effects models. Setting, early-failure time window and covariates are not stated in the abstract.

## Results

[확인] 179 subjects, 374 implants; early failure 6.1%, surgical complications 8.0%, PLCBL 32.5%. Allergy not independently associated with complications, PLCBL or early failure (p > 0.05). Azithromycin and other alternatives: higher odds of surgical complications vs penicillin in exploratory models (p < 0.05); no antibiotic-type association with PLCBL or early failure (p > 0.05). No effect sizes or CIs in the abstract.

## Limitations

- Abstract-only: effect sizes, CIs, group sizes, early-failure definition and adjustment set not verifiable.
- Retrospective, record-based; allergy self-reported.
- Few events; multivariable antibiotic-type models were exploratory.
- Allergy status and antibiotic choice are entangled.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)

A patient-reported penicillin allergy label alone should not be read as a proven cause of early implant failure on this evidence; the signal that azithromycin and other alternatives go with more early surgical complications is exploratory and calls for caution rather than a rule. Clarifying the allergy history (delabeling pathway) so penicillin can be used where appropriate is consistent with the held allergy literature.
