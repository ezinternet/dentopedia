## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (systematic review of reviews) of 28 review articles (>457 primary studies) screened from 1156 records, synthesizing post-WHO-2018 evidence on discontinuing postoperative antibiotic prophylaxis (PAP) after surgical incision closure across general surgical settings.

Pooled meta-analysis of 9 reviews (80,483 patients) found no significant benefit to continuing PAP beyond 24 h post-surgery versus immediate discontinuation (RR 1.07, 95% CI 0.97–1.17, I²=25%, p=0.22); AMSTAR-2 rated review quality as critically low (16/28) to low (12/28), none high/moderate.

Supports WHO/CDC recommendation to stop PAP at incision closure; recommends de-implementation strategies (guideline refinement, multidisciplinary collaboration, resource availability, provider/patient education) to close the persistent practice-evidence gap, especially in LMICs.

## Summary

This umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42024566124; PRISMA-P/PRIOR-guided) synthesizes evidence on discontinuing postoperative antibiotic prophylaxis (PAP) following the WHO 2018 recommendation to stop antibiotic prophylaxis immediately after surgical incision closure, for both clean and clean-contaminated surgeries. From 1156 screened articles, 28 review articles (encompassing >457 primary studies) were included: 14 directly compared PAP discontinuation vs. continuation on surgical site infection (SSI) incidence, 13 synthesized best-practice strategies, and 1 covered both. Pooling 9 meta-analyses (80,483 patients) produced a fixed-effects RR of 1.07 (95% CI 0.97–1.17, I²=25%, p=0.22) — continuing PAP beyond 24 h post-surgery showed no statistically significant benefit over immediate discontinuation. Methodological quality of the included reviews (AMSTAR-2) was uniformly poor: none rated high or moderate, 16/28 critically low, 12/28 low, mainly due to missing justification of excluded studies and lack of protocol pre-registration. The review's narrative synthesis proposes five de-implementation strategy themes for closing the persistent evidence-practice gap, most acute in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs): guideline refinement, multidisciplinary collaboration, resource availability, provider education/antibiotic stewardship, and patient education/shared decision-making.

## Key Contributions

1. First umbrella review to synthesize PAP-discontinuation evidence specifically framed around the WHO 2018 guideline update
2. Cross-review pooled RR (1.07, 95% CI 0.97–1.17) directly quantifying the lack of added SSI-prevention benefit from extending PAP beyond 24 h
3. Systematic AMSTAR-2 appraisal exposing uniformly low/critically-low methodological quality across the 28 included reviews — a caution on the underlying evidence base's rigor, even while the pooled direction is consistent
4. Five-theme narrative framework for de-implementing unnecessary PAP: guideline refinement, multidisciplinary collaboration, resource availability, provider education, patient education
5. Documents specific exceptions where continuation was favored (cardiac/heart surgery — Chesdachai 2022 RR 1.38; non-best-practice-adherent cohorts in de Jonge 2020), underscoring that de-implementation must be context- and patient-specific, not blanket

## Methodology

- **Design**: Umbrella review (systematic review of reviews) with pooled meta-analysis of review-level effect estimates
- **Registration/reporting**: PROSPERO CRD42024566124; PRISMA-P protocol; PRIOR reporting
- **Search**: PubMed/MEDLINE + Scopus, inception–5 July 2024, English, human studies, PRESS-guided MeSH strategy
- **PICO**: humans undergoing surgery with SSI-prevention antibiotic prophylaxis; intervention = immediate PAP discontinuation at incision closure or ≤24 h postoperative antibiotics; comparator = PAP continuation >24 h; outcome = SSI incidence (meta-analyses) and de-implementation strategies (narrative synthesis); reviews published after 2018 only
- **Screening/extraction**: Covidence, two independent reviewers, third-reviewer arbitration
- **Quality appraisal**: AMSTAR-2 (16 domains, 7 critical), independent dual-review, arbitrated
- **Statistics**: Fixed-effects (Mantel-Haenszel) RR, 95% CI, I² heterogeneity, subgroup by surgical site, R v4.2.3; funnel plot for publication bias

## Results

- 1156 screened → **28 reviews included** (>457 primary studies)
- **9 meta-analyses pooled, n=80,483**: RR **1.07 (95% CI 0.97–1.17, I²=25%, p=0.22)** — no significant SSI-risk difference between ≤24 h discontinuation and >24 h continuation
- Exceptions favoring continuation: Chesdachai 2022 (cardiac/heart surgery) RR 1.38 (95% CI 1.09–1.74); de Jonge 2020 sensitivity analysis including non-best-practice-adherent studies, RR 1.12 (95% CI 1.03–1.21, p=0.30); de Jonge 2020 subgroup — best-practice-adherent RR 1.04 (95% CI 0.85–1.27, ns) vs. non-adherent RR 0.79 (95% CI 0.67–0.94, favoring continuation)
- **AMSTAR-2 quality**: 0/28 high or moderate; 16/28 critically low; 12/28 low. Main deficiency: no justified list of excluded full-text studies (critical domain 7); 14/28 unregistered protocol; only 1/28 reported study funding sources
- **De-implementation strategy themes**: (1) reassess/refine guidelines per surgical setting, (2) multidisciplinary collaboration (ID specialists, pharmacists, infection-control nurses), (3) resource availability (equipment, antimicrobials, protocols), (4) provider education/antibiotic stewardship, (5) patient education/shared decision-making
