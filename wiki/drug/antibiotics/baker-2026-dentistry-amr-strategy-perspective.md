## Three-line Summary

mBio perspective (2026): dentists account for ~10% of global antibiotic prescriptions and hold a large patient-contact footprint, placing dentistry in a strategically unique position in the global antimicrobial resistance (AMR) response.

This position is systematically underleveraged — dentistry is excluded from most national AMR action plans (NAPs), underrepresented in AMR research funding, and AMR stewardship is insufficiently emphasized in dental curricula.

Four-pillar response proposed: unified international prescribing guidelines, integration into national AMR action plans, targeted clinician education, and oral resistome surveillance — transforming dentistry from a "blind spot" to a "fulcrum" in global AMR response.

## Summary

Baker et al. (mBio 2026) argue that dentistry occupies a structurally important yet underexploited position in global AMR response. With dentists prescribing approximately 10% of antibiotics globally and maintaining high patient contact frequency, the dental sector could significantly influence AMR trajectories. However, dentistry is systematically excluded from national AMR action plans and from mainstream AMR research and funding. Stewardship receives insufficient attention in dental curricula and competency standards. The authors propose four integrated actions: (1) implementation of unified, evidence-based prescribing guidelines; (2) formal integration of dentistry into national and international AMR action plans; (3) targeted AMR education for dental clinicians; (4) establishment of oral resistome surveillance programs. The paper positions these reforms within the One Health framework.

## Key Contributions

- Quantifies dentistry's systemic AMR lever: ~10% of global antibiotic prescriptions
- Names the structural failure: dentistry absent from most national AMR Action Plans (NAPs)
- Oral resistome surveillance: identified as a critical unmapped gap in AMR data
- One Health framing: dental AMR is not siloed — it intersects with human, animal and environmental health
- Policy prescription is specific: four pillars with actionable targets, not vague calls to action

## Methodology

mBio Perspective (invited format); narrative synthesis; no formal systematic search or inclusion criteria; draws on global prescribing data, AMR policy analysis, dental education literature, and oral microbiome research.

## Results

Key data points cited:
- Dentists: ~10% of global antibiotic prescriptions
- Most national AMR NAPs: dentistry not included or marginally integrated
- Dental education: AMR stewardship is insufficiently emphasized in curricula and competency standards
- Oral resistome: largely uncharacterized — surveillance infrastructure absent
