## Three-line Summary

Cochrane systematic review (3rd update, CD003811.pub3) of 23 RCTs (3,206 participants; 21/23 in impacted third-molar surgery) evaluating perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis vs. placebo in otherwise healthy patients.

Antibiotics significantly reduced post-extraction infection (RR 0.34, NNT 19) and dry socket (RR 0.66, NNT 46) at low certainty; no significant effect on pain, fever, or adverse events was found, with timing of administration making no clear difference.

Given low certainty evidence and high NNTs, routine prophylaxis in healthy patients is not recommended; individual clinical risk stratification is required, and immunocompromised patients need specialist input as this review provides no data for them.

## Summary
This Cochrane systematic review (third update, 2021) synthesizes randomized evidence on whether perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis prevents complications after tooth extraction in otherwise healthy patients. The review evaluates eight outcomes at day 6–7 post-extraction: infectious complications, pain (dichotomous and VAS), fever, swelling, trismus, dry socket, and adverse events. Meta-analyses (Analyses 1.1–1.8) assess each. The review provides the highest level of evidence informing antibiotic stewardship in oral surgery: while antibiotics reduce several post-extraction complications, the absolute benefit must be weighed against antibiotic-related adverse events and the broader public health imperative of minimizing antibiotic resistance.

## Key Contributions
- Highest-quality evidence (Cochrane) on antibiotic prophylaxis for tooth extraction complications
- Covers all key post-extraction outcomes: infection, pain, dry socket, fever, swelling, trismus, adverse events
- Informs international clinical guidelines on when antibiotic prophylaxis is and is not justified
- Quantifies benefit (RR reduction for each outcome) vs. harm (adverse event NNH) to enable shared decision-making
- Especially relevant for third-molar (wisdom tooth) extractions where dry socket risk is highest

## Methodology
- **Type**: Cochrane SR, 3rd update of CD003811
- **Databases**: Cochrane Oral Health Group Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, EMBASE; no language restriction
- **Included studies**: RCTs comparing antibiotic vs. placebo/no treatment perioperatively for tooth extraction
- **Population**: Generally healthy (immunocompetent) adults
- **Antibiotic**: Any type, dose, timing
- **Primary outcome**: Postsurgical infectious complications at day 6–7
- **Secondary outcomes**: Pain (dichotomous; VAS), fever, swelling, trismus, dry socket, adverse events (all at day 6–7)

## Results
23 trials randomised ~3,206 participants (2,583 analysed); 21/23 enrolled **healthy patients having impacted third-molar surgery**. Risk of bias: 16 high, 4 unclear, 3 low.

| Outcome | Effect (RR / MD) | n / studies | Certainty | NNT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Postsurgical infection (day 6–7) | RR 0.34 (0.19–0.64) | 1,728 / 12 | Low | **19** (15–34) |
| Dry socket | RR 0.66 (0.45–0.97) | 1,882 / 13 | Low | **46** (29–62) |
| Pain — dichotomous | RR 0.59 (0.31–1.12), NS | 675 / 3 | Very low | — |
| Pain — VAS (MD) | −0.26 (−0.59–0.07), NS | 422 / 4 | Very low | — |
| Fever | RR 0.66 (0.24–1.79), NS | 475 / 4 | Very low | — |
| Adverse effects (mild, transient) | RR 1.46 (0.81–2.64), NS | 1,277 / 8 | Very low | — |

- Timing of administration (preoperative / postoperative / both) showed **no clear difference**.
- Benefit demonstrated almost exclusively in healthy third-molar surgery; **no data** in immunocompromised or systemically-ill patients, or routine carious/periodontal extractions.

Key clinical balance: antibiotics reduce post-extraction infection (NNT 19) and dry socket (NNT 46) at **low certainty**, with no clear effect on pain/fever and only mild, transient adverse effects — but absolute benefit is modest. Given antimicrobial resistance, the panel advises against routine prophylaxis in healthy patients; prescribe individually by clinical condition and infection risk. Immunocompromised patients need an individualised approach with their medical specialist.
