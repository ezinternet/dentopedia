## Three-line Summary

In silico genomic analysis (J Antimicrob Chemother 2026): 258 Streptococcus IE isolates (1935–2021, 12 countries); 79.46% classified as oral streptococci; ResFinder used to predict antibiotic resistance gene carriage relevant to dental prophylaxis.

None of the oral streptococcal isolates carried amoxicillin resistance genes — primary IE prophylaxis protocol genomically validated; however, azithromycin resistance predicted in 24.88% and doxycycline in 16.59% of oral streptococci — both used as penicillin-allergy alternatives.

The first-line IE prophylaxis regimen (amoxicillin) remains genomically sound, but penicillin-allergy alternative regimens carry clinically significant predicted resistance rates, warranting reassessment of alternative antibiotic selection protocols.

## Summary

Ceccon Chianca et al. (J Antimicrob Chemother 2026) performed an in silico analysis of antibiotic resistance genes in 258 Streptococcus IE isolates retrieved from NCBI BioSample/RefSeq databases. Of these, 79.46% were oral streptococci. ResFinder software was used to predict resistance phenotypes against 91 drugs in 22 classes, with specific focus on antibiotics recommended for IE prophylaxis before dental procedures. No oral streptococcal isolates carried genes conferring amoxicillin resistance — supporting the safety of the first-line prophylactic regimen. However, 24.88% of oral streptococcal assemblies showed predicted azithromycin resistance, and 16.59% showed doxycycline resistance. Both agents are recommended alternatives for penicillin-allergic patients. The Tn6331 transposon, found in 7 isolates, has potential for multi-class resistance transfer.

## Key Contributions

- First study systematically evaluating antibiotic resistance gene carriage in oral streptococcal IE isolates against all recommended prophylaxis agents
- Amoxicillin: 0% resistance — primary IE prophylaxis protocol remains genomically justified
- Azithromycin resistance: 24.88% — one in four isolates potentially resistant to penicillin-allergy alternative
- Doxycycline resistance: 16.59% — second-line alternative also at significant predicted risk
- Tn6331 transposon: mobile element conferring multi-class resistance (macrolides, lincosamides, tetracyclines) — resistance transfer potential

## Methodology

In silico genomic analysis; BioSample + RefSeq (NCBI); n=258 Streptococcus IE assemblies; date range 1935–2021; 12 countries; 79.46% oral streptococci; ResFinder for resistance prediction; 91 drugs, 22 classes analyzed.
