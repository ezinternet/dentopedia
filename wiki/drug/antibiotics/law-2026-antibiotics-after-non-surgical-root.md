## Three-line Summary

Prospective observational study across 153 US dentists (104 general dentists, 49 endodontists) enrolled 1,723 patients undergoing non-surgical root canal treatment to quantify post-treatment antibiotic prescribing patterns and associated dentist, patient, and clinical predictors.

Antibiotics were prescribed to 322 patients (19%); apical periodontitis (OR=1.56, p=.004) and tenderness to palpation (OR=1.40, p=.046) increased prescribing likelihood, while younger dentist age (<45 yr, OR=0.47), non-Hispanic White ethnicity (OR=0.40), and large/academic practice setting (OR=0.25) were independently associated with lower prescribing.

Nearly 1 in 5 patients received antibiotics post-RCT despite guidelines limiting systemic antibiotics to systemic signs or medical risk factors; sociodemographic disparities in prescribing — minority and lower-education patients received antibiotics more frequently — identify equity gaps for targeted stewardship intervention.

## 세줄요약

전향적 관찰연구: 미국 국립 치과 진료 기반 연구네트워크 (Practice-Based Research Network, PBRN) 소속 153명의 치과의사(일반의 104명, 근관치료 전문의 49명)가 비수술적 근관치료 (Root Canal Treatment, RCT)를 받는 1,723명 환자의 항생제 처방 패턴 및 관련 예측 인자 수집.

근관치료 후 322명(19%)에게 항생제 투여 — 근첨 주위염 (Apical Periodontitis, AP, OR=1.56, p=.004) 또는 치근단 촉진 통증 (Tenderness to Palpation, TTP, OR=1.40, p=.046)이 처방을 증가시켰고, 45세 미만 치과의사(OR=0.47, p=.02)·비히스패닉 백인(OR=0.40, p=.01)·대규모/학술기관(OR=0.25, p=.007) 소속일수록 처방 감소.

진료지침이 전신 증상 또는 의학적 위험 인자가 있는 경우에만 전신 항생제 사용을 권장함에도 환자 5명 중 1명이 항생제를 복용하였으며, 소수 인종·저학력 환자의 항생제 과처방은 형평성 격차를 드러내어 표적화된 항생제 적절성 (Antibiotic Stewardship) 중재의 필요성을 시사.

## Summary

This prospective practice-based research network study enrolled 1,723 patients across 153 US dental providers to measure the rate and predictors of systemic antibiotic prescribing following non-surgical root canal treatment. Overall, 19% of patients received antibiotics after treatment — a rate that substantially exceeds the narrow guideline indications (systemic signs such as fever, cellulitis, or lymphadenopathy, or documented medical risk factors). Generalized estimating equations identified clinical factors (apical periodontitis, tenderness to palpation), dentist-level factors (older age, Hispanic/other ethnicity, solo/small practice), and patient-level factors (lower education, minority racial-ethnic background) as independent predictors of higher antibiotic receipt. The study provides the most comprehensive US real-world benchmark to date for post-RCT prescribing and quantifies persistent guideline-practice discordance, while also revealing equity gaps where more vulnerable patient populations receive more antibiotics — not because of greater clinical need, but likely due to practitioner prescribing habits and communication dynamics.

## Key Contributions

- **Real-world prescribing rate benchmark**: 19% of post-RCT patients received systemic antibiotics across diverse US practices, providing a PBRN-derived baseline for future stewardship intervention trials.
- **Dentist-level predictors identified**: Younger age, non-Hispanic White ethnicity, and affiliation with large group or academic practices independently associated with lower antibiotic prescribing — implying training environment and organizational culture modulate stewardship behavior.
- **Equity gap documented**: Minority and lower-education patients were significantly more likely to receive antibiotics even in the secondary analysis restricted to vital pulp and no swelling — a clinical scenario where antibiotics are not guideline-indicated.
- **Guideline-practice gap quantified**: Persistent deviation from AAE and ESE recommendations documented across all practice types despite decades of antibiotic-stewardship messaging in dental education.
- **GEE methodology for clustered dental data**: Demonstrates appropriate statistical handling of within-dentist patient clustering for practice-based research designs.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective observational study; National Dental Practice-Based Research Network (PREDICT project), multi-institutional.
- **Setting**: US general dental and endodontic practices (solo, small group, large group, academic); 153 providers.
- **Participants**: 1,723 patients undergoing non-surgical RCT; 104 general dentists (GDs), 49 endodontists.
- **Primary outcome**: Systemic antibiotic prescribed after non-surgical RCT (yes/no), recorded by practitioner.
- **Secondary analysis**: Restricted to vital pulp + no swelling (scenario where guidelines contraindicate antibiotics).
- **Statistical method**: Generalized estimating equations (GEE) to account for clustering within dentists; adjusted for dentist, patient, and clinical characteristics.
- **Data period**: 6-month consecutive enrollment window.
- **Limitations**: Systemic signs (fever, lymphadenopathy, cellulitis) not collected; self-reported prescribing without pharmacy verification; no postoperative clinical outcome data.

## Results

| Outcome / Predictor | Result | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| Overall antibiotic prescribing rate | 322 / 1,723 (19%) | — |
| Apical periodontitis (present vs absent) | OR = 1.56 | p = .004 |
| Tenderness to palpation (present vs absent) | OR = 1.40 | p = .046 |
| Dentist age < 45 yr (vs ≥ 45) | OR = 0.47 | p = .02 |
| Non-Hispanic White ethnicity (vs Hispanic/other) | OR = 0.40 | p = .01 |
| Large group or academic practice (vs solo/small) | OR = 0.25 | p = .007 |
| ≥ High school education (patient, vs < high school) | Significantly lower | p < .05 |
| Racial-ethnic minority background (patient) | Significantly higher | p < .05 |

**Secondary analysis** (vital pulp + no swelling subgroup): sociodemographic prescribing patterns persisted, confirming that equity disparities are not driven by clinical severity differences.
