## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review synthesizing 75 systematic reviews (725 RCTs, 119 outcomes, 163,832 participants) across 78 clinical scenarios (37 surgery types, 11 nonsurgical invasive procedures, 29 medical prophylaxis conditions) evaluating antibiotic prophylaxis (AP) efficacy and safety.

Of 119 outcomes, 67 (56.3%) showed statistically significant benefit (34 convincing/highly suggestive evidence); AP had little/no benefit in Mohs surgery, simple hand surgery, herniorrhaphy, hepatectomy, thyroid surgery, rhinoplasty, stented distal hypospadias repair, midurethral sling placement, endoscopic sinus surgery, and TURBT, and AP significantly increased antibiotic resistance/adverse events in cystoscopy and afebrile neutropenia post-chemotherapy.

AP efficacy appeared largely independent of surgical wound cleanliness or baseline infection risk, but low-to-very-low certainty evidence (70% of outcomes) and scarce safety reporting (only 21/78 scenarios) limit external validity, so clinicians must weigh case-by-case rather than apply AP routinely.

## Summary

This umbrella review is the broadest evidence map to date of antibiotic prophylaxis (AP) efficacy and safety, pooling 75 systematic reviews (725 RCTs, 163,832 participants, 119 outcomes) across 78 clinical scenarios spanning surgery (37 types), nonsurgical invasive procedures (11 types), and medical (non-procedural) prophylaxis (29 conditions). Overall, AP showed statistically significant benefit in 56.3% of outcomes, but only 30% of the underlying evidence reached moderate-to-high GRADE certainty. AP benefit did not track cleanly with wound cleanliness class or disease baseline risk — some clean-surgery scenarios (total joint arthroplasty, breast cancer surgery) had convincing benefit while some contaminated-adjacent scenarios (endoscopic sinus surgery) did not. Safety data were sparse (21/78 scenarios) but showed significantly increased antibiotic resistance and adverse events in cystoscopy and afebrile neutropenia post-chemotherapy. The authors conclude AP generally improves prognosis but its safety profile and the low certainty of much of the underlying evidence demand individualized, not routine, decision-making.

## Key Contributions

- Single evidence map spanning both surgical and medical AP scenarios (rare — most prior reviews cover one domain), enabling cross-domain comparison of AP effect size, NNT, and certainty.
- Reanalysis of pooled data after removing NRSIs flipped 2 of 28 reanalyzed outcomes from significant to nonsignificant, illustrating how study-design mixing can inflate apparent AP benefit.
- Explicit five-tier evidence grading (convincing/highly suggestive/suggestive/weak/nonsignificant) combining effect size and GRADE certainty, giving a reusable evidence-hierarchy lens for judging AP claims in any specialty, including dentistry.
- First cross-scenario synthesis of AP-associated antibiotic resistance and adverse events, flagging cystoscopy and afebrile neutropenia (post-chemo) as scenarios where AP harms outweigh its benefit signal.

## Methodology

Umbrella review (PRISMA 2020) of meta-analyses of AP vs placebo/no-treatment RCTs (NRSI-only meta-analyses excluded), searched across PubMed/EMBASE/Web of Science/Cochrane through 30 Dec 2022. Reanalysis used random-effects DerSimonian-Laird pooling; certainty assessed via GRADE, methodological quality via AMSTAR2. Outcomes classified into 6 categories (mortality, overall infection, SSI/wound infection, localized infection, bloodstream infection, symptoms/complications); scenarios split into surgical (stratified by NAS wound-cleanliness class: clean/clean-contaminated/contaminated/dirty) and medical (nonemergency/emergency). NNT calculated for all significant outcomes; publication bias assessed via Begg's/Egger's/Harbord's tests with trim-and-fill sensitivity analysis where indicated.

## Results

- **Overall**: 75 SRs, 725 RCTs, 119 outcomes, 163,832 participants; 39% of SRs high AMSTAR2 quality, 20% low; 30% of outcomes high-to-moderate GRADE certainty, 70% low-to-very-low. 67/119 (56.3%) outcomes statistically significant, 34 convincing/highly suggestive.
- **Surgical (37 types, 55 outcomes)**: AP benefit in most surgery types. Little/no benefit: Mohs surgery, simple hand surgery, herniorrhaphy, hepatectomy, thyroid surgery, rhinoplasty, stented distal hypospadias repair, midurethral sling placement, endoscopic sinus surgery, TURBT (all low/very-low certainty). Convincing benefit with NNT: total joint arthroplasty (NNT=24), breast cancer surgery (NNT=29), cesarean section (NNT=18), laparoscopic cholecystectomy (NNT=62), abdominal hysterectomy (NNT=31).
- **Nonsurgical invasive procedures (11 types, 16 outcomes)**: possible UTI reduction with cystoscopy, urinary catheterization, urodynamic studies, but only suggestive/weak low-to-very-low certainty evidence — guideline discouragement of routine use is consistent with this low certainty.
- **Medical prophylaxis (29 scenarios, 47 outcomes)**: 22/47 significant, 10 convincing/highly suggestive, e.g. tick bite (NNT=69), spontaneous bacterial peritonitis in cirrhosis-ascites (NNT=9), neonatal infection with maternal GBS colonization (NNT=14), COPD quality of life (high certainty). Mortality benefit in cirrhotic GI bleeding and chemo-associated afebrile neutropenia; no mortality benefit in acute necrotizing pancreatitis, post-cardiac-arrest, or ICU stay.
- **Adverse events (21/78 scenarios with data)**: antibiotic resistance significantly increased with cystoscopy (RR 1.73) and afebrile neutropenia post-chemo (RR 1.47); other adverse events significantly increased with HSCT (RR 3.32) and afebrile neutropenia post-chemo (RR 1.58).
- **Bottom line**: AP efficacy is largely independent of wound cleanliness/baseline infection risk, but low evidence certainty (70% of outcomes) and sparse safety data (21/78 scenarios) constrain generalizability — decisions should be individualized rather than routine, especially where certainty is low.
