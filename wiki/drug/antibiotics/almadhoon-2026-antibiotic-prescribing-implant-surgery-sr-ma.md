## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (Jpn Dent Sci Rev 2026): 39 studies, 7266 dentists, 137,207 patients; pooled prevalence of routine antibiotic prescribing in dental implant surgery 61.3% (95%CI 53.4–68.8%; I²=96.8%); amoxicillin most prescribed (50.9%).

Guideline concordance was critically low at only 1–28% across studies, with a declining temporal trend; combined pre- and post-operative regimens dominated (52.6%) despite post-operative courses showing no benefit over a single pre-operative dose.

Antibiotic over-prescribing in implant surgery is global, severe, and worsening — procedure-specific international guidelines and targeted stewardship are urgently needed, though GRADE certainty is very low.

## Summary

Almadhoon et al. (Jpn Dent Sci Rev 2026) conducted the first global SR+MA examining antibiotic prescribing in dental implant surgery. Searching MEDLINE, Scopus and Web of Science (2010–2025), 39 studies including 7266 dentists and 137,207 patients were included. Pooled prescribing prevalence was 61.3% with extreme heterogeneity (range 24.9–88.8%). Combined pre- and post-operative regimens predominated, with post-op courses typically lasting 5–7 days — despite evidence that a single pre-operative dose is sufficient for uncomplicated cases. Guideline concordance was alarmingly low (1–28%) and declining. Amoxicillin was the most common choice. Prescribing scaled with complexity, reaching 97.3% in compromised patients. GRADE certainty was very low throughout.

## Key Contributions

- Pooled prescribing prevalence 61.3% — majority of implant procedures receive antibiotics routinely
- Post-operative 5–7-day courses are predominant despite no demonstrated superiority over pre-op single dose
- Guideline concordance: only 1–28%, and the trend is declining — stewardship efforts have not translated
- Amoxicillin (50.9%) dominates — but prescribing choice is not the core problem; over-prescribing is
- Compromised patients: 97.3% prescribing — appropriate escalation, but routine-case over-prescribing unacceptable
- Calls for procedure-specific international guidelines — no currently unified standard exists

## Methodology

SR+MA; MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science; January 2010 – October 2025; n=39 studies (dentist surveys + patient records + registers); pooled prevalence via Freeman-Tukey + random effects; I²=96.8%; JBI risk-of-bias; GRADE certainty (overall very low).
