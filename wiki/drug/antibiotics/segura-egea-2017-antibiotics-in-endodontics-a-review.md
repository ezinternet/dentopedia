## Three-line Summary

Narrative review synthesizing 60+ studies and multiregional European prescribing surveys on antibiotic indications in endodontic patient management. Antibiotics are ineffective for symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, pulp necrosis, and localized acute apical abscess; drainage/debridement is the sole primary intervention. Amoxicillin is first-line for odontogenic infections with systemic spread; clindamycin for penicillin-allergic patients; 40–86% of European dentists were inappropriately prescribing at the time of review.

## Summary

This international multiauthor narrative review, produced by a European Endodontology Society working group, synthesizes evidence from over 60 published studies and prescribing surveys across seven European countries to define when antibiotics are and are not indicated in endodontic practice. The central finding is that antibiotics confer no benefit for symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, pulp necrosis, acute apical periodontitis, or localized acute apical abscess — conditions in which mechanical drainage and endodontic debridement are the definitive treatments. Systemic antibiotic use is justified only when infection extends beyond the periodontium with systemic signs (fever ≥38°C, trismus, lymphadenopathy, progressive cellulitis) or in medically compromised patients with impaired immunity. Amoxicillin (or amoxicillin-clavulanic acid) is the empirical drug of choice; clindamycin is recommended for confirmed penicillin allergy. Survey data from Spain, Turkey, Belgium, Lithuania, and Croatia revealed that 40–86% of dentists were prescribing antibiotics in contraindicated situations, underscoring a major stewardship gap.

## Key Contributions

- **Clear NO-antibiotic indications**: symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, pulp necrosis, acute apical periodontitis (no swelling), chronic apical abscess, and localized acute apical abscess — all managed by drainage/debridement alone without antibiotic adjunct
- **YES-antibiotic indications defined**: systemic spread (fever ≥38°C, trismus, lymphadenopathy, widespread swelling), medically compromised hosts (prosthetic valves, immunosuppression, uncontrolled diabetes), and progressive/persistent infections refractory to drainage
- **Drug-of-choice hierarchy**: amoxicillin > amoxicillin-clavulanic acid (± metronidazole for anaerobes) > clindamycin (penicillin allergy); Penicillin VK acceptable but inferior absorption
- **European prescribing crisis documented**: cross-national surveys show 40–86% inappropriate antibiotic use in contraindicated endodontic conditions; response rates 30–45%
- **Rising antibiotic resistance** in oral Porphyromonas spp., Prevotella spp., α-hemolytic Streptococci, and macrolide targets — broad-spectrum empirical use accelerates resistance
- **No evidence for intracanal topical antibiotics**: current data do not support routine intracanal antibiotic use during root canal therapy

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative review with systematic collation of European antibiotic prescribing surveys
- **Scope**: Literature from 1970s–2016; emphasis on European prescribing patterns and randomized/controlled data on symptom resolution
- **Surveys collated**: 10 surveys across UK, Turkey, Sweden, Belgium, Lithuania, Spain, Croatia (Table 1 — first-choice agent, second-choice, allergic alternative, duration)
- **Clinical decision framework**: indications matrix (Table 2: YES/NO for systemic antibiotics by pulpal/periapical condition; Table 3: traumatic dental injuries)
- **Key referenced Cochrane SRs**: Agnihotry et al. 2016 (irreversible pulpitis), Cope et al. 2014 (symptomatic apical periodontitis/AAA)

## Results

### Antibiotic Indication Matrix (Table 2)

| Condition | Systemic Signs | Antibiotics? |
|---|---|---|
| Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis | None | **NO** |
| Pulp necrosis | None | **NO** |
| Acute apical periodontitis | Pain on biting; widened PDL space | **NO** |
| Chronic apical abscess | Sinus tract; periapical radiolucency | **NO** |
| Acute apical abscess (localized) | Fluctuant swelling, no fever | **NO** |
| Acute apical abscess (medically compromised) | Localized swelling; impaired immunity | **YES** |
| Acute apical abscess (systemic involvement) | Fever ≥38°C; trismus; lymphadenopathy | **YES** |
| Progressive infections | Cellulitis; rapid onset <24 h | **YES** |
| Persistent infections | Osteomyelitis; chronic exudation | **YES** |

### Key Drug Selection Principles

- Odontogenic infections are polymicrobial (gram-positive, gram-negative, facultative + strict anaerobes in >90% of cases)
- Amoxicillin preferred over Penicillin VK: better oral absorption, lower side-effect profile
- Metronidazole added when anaerobic coverage is needed (combination with amoxicillin)
- Clindamycin covers gram-positive and anaerobes; suitable alternative for penicillin-allergic patients
- Empirical prescribing without culture/susceptibility data is universal in dental practice — reinforces stewardship importance
