## Three-line Summary

2021 AHA Scientific Statement (Circulation 2021) reviewing 14 years of post-2007 guideline data on viridans group streptococcal (VGS) infective endocarditis (IE) prophylaxis after invasive dental procedures.

2007 IE prophylaxis guidelines reaffirmed unchanged: prophylaxis recommended only for 4 highest-risk cardiac conditions (prosthetic valve, prior IE, certain congenital heart disease, cardiac transplant with valvulopathy); VGS-IE frequency and mortality not increased since 2007.

Amoxicillin 2 g single dose (30–60 min pre-procedure) remains the universal first choice; **clindamycin was removed** from all regimens (single-dose C. difficile risk) and penicillin-allergic patients now get cephalexin / azithromycin / doxycycline; good oral health maintenance is emphasized as more protective than prophylaxis for low/moderate-risk patients.

## 세줄요약

2021 AHA 과학성명(Circulation 2021): 2007년 가이드라인 이후 14년간 침습적 치과시술 후 연쇄상구균(Viridans Group Streptococcus, VGS) 감염성 심내막염(Infective Endocarditis, IE) 예방 데이터 검토. **2021년판은 클린다마이신(Clindamycin)을 전 처방에서 삭제** — 단회 투여도 클로스트리디오이데스 디피실(C. difficile) 감염(사망 포함) 유발.

2007년 IE 예방 가이드라인 변경 없이 유지: 4개 최고위험 심장 상태(인공판막, 과거 IE, 특정 선천성 심장병, 이식 후 판막병)에만 항생제 예방 권고; 2007년 이후 VGS-IE 빈도·사망률 증가 없음 확인.

Amoxicillin 2g 단일 투여(시술 30–60분 전)가 표준 1차 선택; 저/중간 위험 환자에서 예방적 항생제보다 구강 건강 유지가 더 보호적임을 강조.

## Summary
2021 AHA Scientific Statement reviewing and **reaffirming the 2007 IE prophylaxis guidelines unchanged.** After 14 years of follow-up data, there is no evidence that relaxing prophylaxis increased VGS-IE frequency or mortality. Antibiotic prophylaxis (AP) recommended only for 4 highest-risk cardiac categories.

## The 4 Highest-Risk Conditions — AP Required

| Category | Specifics |
|---|---|
| **Prosthetic cardiac valve** | Including transcatheter-implanted prostheses |
| **Prior infective endocarditis** | Any history of IE |
| **Congenital heart disease (CHD)** | Unrepaired cyanotic CHD; repaired with residual defect; repaired within 6 months |
| **Cardiac transplant** | Recipients with valvulopathy |

> All other cardiac conditions (MVP, RHD, bicuspid aortic valve, hypertrophic CM, repaired CHD with no residual defect) → **NO AP required**

## Antibiotic Protocol (2021 update — Table 5)

| Situation | Drug | Dose (adult / child) | Route | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | **Amoxicillin** | 2g / 50mg/kg | PO | 30–60 min before |
| Unable to take oral | Ampicillin **OR** cefazolin/ceftriaxone | 2g / 50mg/kg | IM/IV | 30 min before |
| Allergic to penicillin/ampicillin — oral | **Cephalexin** 2g **OR** azithromycin/clarithromycin 500mg **OR** doxycycline 100mg | (child: cephalexin 50mg/kg / azithro–clarithro 15mg/kg / doxy <45kg 2.2mg/kg, >45kg 100mg) | PO | 30–60 min before |
| Allergic to penicillin/ampicillin + unable to take oral | **Cefazolin or ceftriaxone** | 1g / 50mg/kg | IM/IV | 30 min before |

> [!warning] Clindamycin removed in 2021
> The 2021 statement **explicitly dropped clindamycin** from every prophylaxis regimen: *"Clindamycin is no longer recommended for antibiotic prophylaxis for a dental procedure."* Rationale: a single prophylactic dose can cause *Clostridioides difficile* infection including death ([[drug/antibiotics/thornhill-2019-adverse-reactions-oral-antibiotics-dentists]], UK data), and clindamycin causes more frequent/severe reactions than other AP options. Cephalexin (or another 1st/2nd-gen cephalosporin) is now first-line for penicillin-allergic patients — cephalosporins are contraindicated only with a history of anaphylaxis/angioedema/urticaria to penicillin or ampicillin.

## Dental Procedures Requiring AP (if high-risk cardiac condition)
- Manipulation of gingival tissue
- Periapical region of teeth
- Perforation of oral mucosa
- NOT required for: routine anesthesia injections through non-infected tissue, dental X-rays, orthodontic bracket placement, shedding of primary teeth

## Key Evidence Review (2007→2021)
- VGS-IE incidence: **not increased** since 2007 guideline change
- VGS-IE morbidity/mortality: **not increased**
- General compliance with 2007 guidelines: good, but variable
- Conclusion: 2007 recommendations remain valid

## Clinical Takeaway
- **Know the 4 criteria** — do not prescribe AP for any other cardiac condition
- Amoxicillin 2g is the universal first choice (single dose)
- **Penicillin-allergic → cephalexin 2g (first choice) / azithromycin–clarithromycin 500mg / doxycycline 100mg. Clindamycin is no longer an option.**
- **Good oral health maintenance** emphasized as more protective than AP for low/moderate-risk patients
