## Three-line Summary
Narrative review (2000–2019 literature) of antibiotic prescribing in pediatric dentistry, examining indications, dosages, duration, and guideline adherence among dentists, dental students, and non-dentist providers.
Global antibiotic consumption rose 65% from 2000–2015 (21.1→34.8 billion DDDs); dentists account for 7–11% of all prescriptions; ~1/3 of outpatient antibiotic prescriptions are unnecessary, with low guideline adherence documented globally.
Key gap: insufficient knowledge of appropriate indications drives overuse; early-life antibiotic exposure in children causes dysbiosis, obesity, Candida superinfection, enamel defects, and gastrointestinal disturbances.

## Summary
This narrative review synthesizes literature from 2000–2019 on antibiotic prescribing practices in pediatric dentistry, drawing on MEDLINE, EBSCO host, and Google Scholar sources. Global antibiotic consumption increased 65% between 2000 and 2015, with dentists responsible for 7–11% of all prescriptions; approximately one-third of outpatient prescriptions are unnecessary, reflecting low adherence to professional guidelines. The review defines appropriate indications — systemic infections with diffuse swelling and pyrexia, acute facial cellulitis, contaminated wounds — and contrasts them with inappropriate scenarios such as irreversible pulpitis, necrotic pulps with localized abscess, and localized dentoalveolar abscess without systemic signs, where drainage and debridement suffice. Multidrug-resistant bacteria have been found in children as young as 4 years, and early-life antibiotic exposure is linked to intestinal dysbiosis, obesity risk, Candida superinfection, developmental enamel defects from amoxicillin, and gastrointestinal disturbances. The authors advocate heightened clinician education and adherence to the "4 D's" framework (right Drug, Dose, De-escalation, Duration) to curb overuse and resistance.

## Key Contributions
- Documented that dentists contribute 7–11% of all antibiotic prescriptions globally, with ~1/3 of outpatient prescriptions unnecessary and guideline adherence low among dental students, dentists, and pediatric dentists.
- Identified key drivers of inappropriate prescribing: insufficient clinical knowledge of indications, practitioner scarcity in rural settings (1:150,000 dentist-to-patient ratio in rural India vs WHO ideal 1:7,500), and non-dentist providers defaulting to antibiotics.
- Quantified antimicrobial resistance epidemiology in children: multidrug-resistant bacteria present in children as young as 4 years; MRSA (Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus) cited as exemplar of extensive resistance.
- Linked early-life antibiotic exposure to adverse pediatric outcomes: dysbiosis, obesity, Candida superinfection, developmental enamel defects (amoxicillin linked to permanent first molar and maxillary central incisor defects), and increased diarrhea with TID vs BID regimens.
- Projected that if 2000–2015 growth continues, global antibiotic consumption will reach 128 billion DDDs by 2030 (+202%), underscoring urgency of stewardship.

## Methodology
- **Design**: Narrative literature review (no systematic risk-of-bias assessment)
- **Databases**: MEDLINE/PubMed, EBSCO host, Google Scholar; supplemented by manual textbook review
- **Search terms**: MeSH — "Antibacterial agents", "Antibacterial agents AND Pediatric Dentistry"
- **Time period**: 2000–2019
- **Synthesis approach**: Opinion-based narrative synthesis combining epidemiological statistics, clinical guideline recommendations, and expert commentary; no meta-analysis performed

### Appropriate vs Inappropriate Indications

| Indication | Antibiotic appropriate? | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Irreversible pulpitis, no systemic signs | No | No blood circulation to canal; antibiotics cannot reach infection site |
| Necrotic pulp, localized apical abscess | No | Drainage/debridement required; antibiotics ineffective |
| Localized dentoalveolar abscess, no pyrexia | No | Edema → anti-inflammatories; purulence → drainage |
| Diffuse soft-tissue swelling + pyrexia within 24 h | Yes | Systemic response indicates spreading infection |
| Acute facial cellulitis / spreading apical abscess | Yes | Interstitial/tissue space involvement |
| Contaminated wounds with extrinsic debris/foreign bodies | Yes | Extrinsic bacterial load |
| Open fractures and joint injuries | Yes | Bone/joint infection risk |

### Adverse Effects of Inappropriate Pediatric Antibiotic Exposure

| Effect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Intestinal dysbiosis | Altered microbiota → long-term metabolic effects |
| Obesity | Early-life microbiota disruption linked to adiposity |
| Candida superinfection | Secondary fungal overgrowth |
| Developmental enamel defects | Amoxicillin exposure linked to permanent first molars and maxillary central incisors |
| GI disturbances | Diarrhea more frequent with TID than BID regimens |
| Allergy/asthma | Increased risk in antibiotic-treated children |
| Type 2 diabetes risk | Sugar-containing liquid antibiotic formulations |

### The "4 D's" of Antimicrobial Therapy (Joseph & Rodvold)
1. Right **Drug** — appropriate spectrum selection
2. Right **Dose** — weight-based pediatric dosing
3. **De-escalation** — narrow to pathogen-directed therapy once culture results available
4. Right **Duration** — shortest effective course
