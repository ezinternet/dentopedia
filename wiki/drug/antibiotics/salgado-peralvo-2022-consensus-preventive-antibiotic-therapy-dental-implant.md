## Three-line Summary

Consensus report / clinical practice guideline from the Spanish Society of Implants (SEI): 12-member expert panel + 3 external reviewers, PRISMA-structured, SIGN level-of-evidence + GRADE recommendation grading, MEDLINE Jan 2020–Feb 2021, across 6 PICO questions (routine placement, immediate implants, sinus lifts, GBR, prosthetic phase, penicillin-allergic patients).

Routine placement: 2–3 g amoxicillin 1 h preoperatively (GRADE A), no postoperative/perioperative extension, not prescribing also acceptable in simple cases (GRADE B); prosthetic phase needs nothing (GRADE D); PAT prevents one early implant failure per 24–50 healthy patients (OR 0.08–0.45) but does not reduce postoperative infection (NNT 143).

Penicillin-allergic patients: avoid clindamycin (up to 6× higher implant failure/infection); azithromycin 500 mg 1 h preoperatively for placement (GRADE C); azithromycin OR clarithromycin OR metronidazole multi-day regimens for immediate implants (GRADE D); ciprofloxacin 500 mg/12 h × 9 days for sinus lifts (GRADE D) — evidence for everything except routine placement is LoE 2+ to 4.

## Summary

This is a summary/dissemination of the first published clinical practice guideline on preventive antibiotic therapy (PAT) in dental-implant procedures, developed by the Spanish Society of Implants (SEI) and endorsed by several international societies and the General Council of Dentists of Spain. A 12-member expert panel with 3 external reviewers graded the literature with SIGN checklists (levels of evidence 1++ to 4) and issued GRADE recommendations (A–D) for six clinical scenarios. The strongest evidence base — routine implant placement in healthy patients — supports a single **preoperative dose of 2–3 g amoxicillin 1 h before surgery (GRADE A)**, with no benefit and added harm from postoperative or perioperative extension, and explicitly permits *not* prescribing in simple cases (GRADE B). PAT reduces early implant failure (OR 0.08–0.45; number needed to treat 24–50) but not postoperative infection (NNT 143). Evidence for immediate implants, sinus lifts, guided bone regeneration and the penicillin-allergic population is much weaker (LoE 2+ to 4), and several regimens are extrapolated from endodontic, periodontal or sinusitis data. The document's central purpose is antimicrobial stewardship: it recommends **no** antibiotics for the prosthetic phase and repeatedly cautions against habitual over-prescription. Its most practice-changing content is the **penicillin-allergy** section: it recommends against reflex clindamycin substitution (observational data show markedly higher implant failure and infection) and instead favours azithromycin for placement, with procedure-specific alternatives for immediate implants and sinus lifts.

## Key Contributions

- First procedure-stratified guideline for implant PAT: separate graded recommendations for routine placement, immediate implants, sinus lifts, GBR, and the prosthetic phase.
- Explicit **penicillin-allergic** drug/dose/posology recommendations that differ by procedure, not a single blanket alternative.
- Formal recommendation **against clindamycin** as the default penicillin-allergy substitute in implant surgery.
- Recommends **no antibiotic** for second-stage surgery, impressions and prosthesis delivery (GRADE D).
- Quantifies the stewardship trade-off: 1 early failure prevented per 24–50 healthy patients treated; no reduction in postoperative infection (NNT 143); PAT patients carry ~1.8% higher adverse-reaction risk.

## Methodology

Consensus report structured per PRISMA; expert panel of 12 dentists/stomatologists plus 3 independent external reviewers. Two reviewers appraised each study with SIGN critical-appraisal templates (third reviewer resolved disagreements); evidence ranked 1++ / 1+ / 1- / 2++ / 2+ / 2- / 3 / 4. Recommendations graded with GRADE: **A** (≥1 SR/MA/CT rated 1++, or a consistent body of 1+), **B** (2++ directly applicable, or extrapolated from 1++/1+), **C** (2+ directly applicable, or extrapolated from 2++), **D** (level 3–4, or extrapolated from 2+). Search: MEDLINE via PubMed, elaborated January 2020–February 2021, with a separate individualized strategy and filter set per PICO question and hand-searching of reference lists. Where implant-specific literature was absent, the panel descended the evidence pyramid or extrapolated from tooth/periodontal/endodontic studies.

## Results

### Recommendations by procedure

| PICO | Scenario | First-line (non-allergic) | GRADE | Penicillin-allergic | GRADE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Placement, no anatomical constraints, healthy | Amoxicillin 2–3 g PO 1 h preop; **no** postop/periop; not prescribing also acceptable | A / B | Azithromycin 500 mg 1 h preop (see PICO 6; avoid clindamycin) | C |
| 2 | Immediate implants | Loading 2–3 g amoxicillin 1 h preop (B) + 500 mg/8 h × 5–7 postop days (D) | D | Azithromycin 500 mg 1 h preop → 250 mg/24 h × 5–7 d; **or** clarithromycin 500 mg 1 h preop → 250 mg/12 h × 5–7 d; **or** metronidazole 1 g 1 h preop → 500 mg/6 h × 5–7 d | D |
| 3 | Sinus lift, 1- or 2-stage | No perforation: amoxicillin 2–3 g 1 h preop (B). Assume perforation: amoxicillin/clavulanate 875/125 mg/12 h from 1 d preop, then /8 h × 7 d (D) | B / D | Ciprofloxacin 500 mg/12 h × 9 days (macrolides not justified — sinus flora macrolide-resistant) | D |
| 4 | GBR / bone augmentation, 1- or 2-stage | Single 2–3 g amoxicillin 1 h preop; preoperative beats perioperative for infection | A / C | (No procedure-specific regimen; PICO 6 azithromycin applies) | C |
| 5 | Prosthetic phase (2nd stage, impressions, prosthesis) | **No PAT** | D | — | — |
| 6 | Non-amoxicillin / penicillin-allergic, placement ± GBR / sinus / immediate | — | — | Azithromycin 500 mg 1 h before surgery; **avoid clindamycin** | C |

### Key numbers

| Outcome / metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Early implant failure reduction with PAT | OR 0.08–0.45; absolute 1.8–4% (LoE 1++) |
| NNT — early implant failure | 24–50 |
| NNT — postoperative infection | 143 (100 preop amoxicillin, 143 postop) |
| Most effective single protocol (Romandini NMA) | 3 g amoxicillin 1 h preop, OR 0.41 |
| Immediate vs delayed implant failure (Cosyn) | 5.1% vs 1.1% |
| Schneiderian membrane perforation rate | 18.3–23.5%; perforation → graft-failure OR 16.82; sinusitis 11.3% vs 1.4% |
| Clindamycin vs amoxicillin — implant failure (allergic vs non-allergic) | RR 3.34–3.84; HR 2.16; OR 3.10; patient-level 18.9% vs 5.2% |
| Clindamycin vs amoxicillin — postoperative infection | up to 6× higher; sinus graft failure 6% vs 0% |
| Adverse-reaction risk, PAT vs none | ~1.8% higher |

- Penicillin allergy in the PICO 6 studies was allergy-tested in only 1 of 5 — most "allergic" patients were self-reported, which likely inflates the measured penalty of the label.
- Overall conclusion: PAT indicated for all implant *surgical* procedures except the prosthetic phase; type and dose adapted to procedure; PAT does not replace aseptic/sterile technique.
