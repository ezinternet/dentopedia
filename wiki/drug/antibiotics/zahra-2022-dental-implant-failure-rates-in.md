## Three-line Summary

Retrospective chart review at New York University College of Dentistry of patients with at least 1 year of follow-up after receiving one or more dental implants, testing whether self-reported penicillin allergy and the antibiotic given are linked to implant failure.

Overall implant failure was 12.9%; failure was 8.4% in non-allergic patients taking amoxicillin versus 17.1% with reported allergy (adjusted OR 2.22, 95% CI 1.44-3.44), and 19.9% in allergy-reporting patients taking clindamycin (adjusted OR 2.9, 95% CI 1.77-4.47 versus amoxicillin).

The authors report a significant increase in early implant failure in allergy-reporting patients; evidence is retrospective and abstract-only, so sample size, early-failure definition and early-failure rate could not be verified.

## Summary
Retrospective chart review at the New York University College of Dentistry (eligibility: one or more implants placed at the College and at least 1 year of follow-up). Overall implant failure was 12.9%. Failure was 8.4% in patients with no reported penicillin allergy who took amoxicillin and 17.1% in allergy-reporting patients (adjusted OR 2.22, 95% CI 1.44-3.44). Allergy-reporting patients who took clindamycin failed more often than those taking amoxicillin (19.9%; adjusted OR 2.9, 95% CI 1.77-4.47) or any non-amoxicillin antibiotic (20.9%; adjusted OR 2.77, 95% CI 1.77-4.32).

## Key Contributions
- Separates the effect of reported allergy from the effect of the antibiotic actually administered.
- Reports that amoxicillin users had lower failure than users of other antibiotics.
- Reports a significant increase in early implant failure among allergy-reporting patients (no figure given in the abstract).

## Methodology
[확인] Retrospective chart review, single institution (NYU College of Dentistry, USA); participants eligible if they received one or more implants and had at least 1 year of follow-up. Abstract-only: patient and implant counts, covariates in the adjusted model, and the definition and time window of early failure are not stated.

## Results
[확인] Overall failure 12.9%; no-allergy plus amoxicillin 8.4%; allergy-reporting 17.1% (adjusted OR 2.22, 95% CI 1.44-3.44); allergy plus clindamycin 19.9% (adjusted OR 2.9, 95% CI 1.77-4.47 versus amoxicillin); allergy plus any non-amoxicillin antibiotic 20.9% (adjusted OR 2.77, 95% CI 1.77-4.32). Lower failure with amoxicillin than other antibiotics; early failure significantly increased in allergy-reporting patients (no numbers in the abstract).

## Limitations
- Retrospective chart review; confounding by indication is possible because the antibiotic given depends on the reported allergy.
- Abstract-only: n, covariates, early-failure definition and early-failure rate are not verifiable.
- The 12.9% overall failure is high relative to typical survival cohorts and mixes early and late failure; the abstract does not say how the 1-year-minimum follow-up was handled.
- Single academic dental school; generalizability is uncertain.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
A self-reported penicillin allergy should prompt verification (delabeling) rather than automatic substitution with clindamycin, because in this cohort the clindamycin group had the highest failure; the abstract cannot show causation, so treat it as a risk signal.
