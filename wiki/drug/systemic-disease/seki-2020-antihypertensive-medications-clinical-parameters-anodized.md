## One-line Summary
Retrospective cohort (77 anodized implants/35 patients, 7-year follow-up): antihypertensive medication users showed significantly higher peri-implantitis rates (26.5% vs 4.7%, OR 7.38) despite zero implant loss.

## 한줄요약

## Summary
This Japanese retrospective cohort (Nihon University, 2003–2018) examined peri-implant tissue health in 35 patients (77 TiUnite anodized implants) over a mean follow-up of 7 years. Despite zero implant loss in both groups, antihypertensive medication users had significantly higher peri-implantitis rates (26.5% vs 4.7%, OR 7.38) and greater probing pocket depth. Multivariate logistic regression identified antihypertensive medication as the only significant independent predictor of peri-implantitis (OR 12.90). This stands in counterpoint to studies showing improved implant survival with antihypertensives: it distinguishes implant survival from implant success.

## Key Contributions
- Demonstrates that antihypertensive medications may worsen peri-implant tissue health despite preserving implant survival
- First report linking antihypertensive use to higher peri-implantitis prevalence in anodized implants
- Multivariate analysis: AHT medication is independent predictor (OR 12.90) beyond age, sex, site
- CCB-induced gingival hyperplasia mechanism proposed for peri-implant tissue changes
- "Survival ≠ Success" concept: AHT patients had 100% implant retention but significantly worse tissue parameters

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort, single periodontist, Nihon University. 35 patients, 77 TiUnite Nobel Biocare implants, mean follow-up 84.8 months. Groups: AH (13 patients, 34 implants), H (22 patients, 43 implants). Medications: CCB, ARB, thiazide (mono or combination). Outcomes: PPD, BoP, mPI, marginal bone loss. Peri-implantitis defined as PPD ≥6 mm + suppuration/BoP + radiographic bone resorption ≥25% implant length. Exclusion: smokers, periodontitis history, bone grafting, systemic diseases other than HTN, age <50.

## Results
Peri-implantitis (implant level): AH 26.5% (9/34) vs H 4.7% (2/43), OR 7.38 (95% CI 1.474–36.954, significant). Multivariate OR for AHT: 12.90 (only significant predictor). PPD: AH 3.8 ± 1.3 mm vs H 3.0 ± 0.7 mm (significant). Marginal bone loss: AH 0.66 mm vs H 0.34 mm (not significant). BoP and mPI: higher in AH, not significant. Implant loss: 0 in both groups.
