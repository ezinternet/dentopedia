## Three-line Summary

Abstract-only clinical review (Dent Clin North Am 2023) on preventing and managing vaso-occlusive sickle cell crisis (VOC) triggered in the dental setting. Dental anxiety is identified as a physiological VOC trigger (sympathetic activation → increased O₂ demand → HbS polymerization), and the review provides pre-procedure evaluation, short-procedure planning, and emergency response protocol. No RCT evidence; evidence level is expert opinion and observational data.

## Summary
This clinical review from the University of Pennsylvania Oral Medicine department (Dent Clin North Am 2023) addresses the scenario of a sickle cell disease (SCD) patient undergoing dental extraction who develops a vaso-occlusive crisis (VOC) in the dental chair. SCD (autosomal recessive hemoglobinopathy) results from a point mutation in the β-globin chain (valine substitutes glutamic acid), producing hemoglobin S (HbS) which polymerizes under deoxygenation, forming the sickle shape that occludes capillaries causing ischemia and pain. The review emphasizes that dental anxiety itself is a physiological trigger — sympathetic activation increases oxygen consumption, accelerating HbS polymerization. Core recommendations: (1) Exclude active infection, neurological deficits, and multi-organ involvement before proceeding; (2) Plan short, uncomplicated procedures; (3) Optimize anxiety management; (4) Maintain oxygen saturation monitoring; (5) Activate emergency response immediately if VOC begins. Hematologist consultation is advised for presurgical hemoglobin optimization and prophylactic antibiotics decisions.

## Key Contributions
- Explains the VOC trigger chain specifically in the dental context: anxiety → hyperventilation → deoxygenation → HbS polymerization → capillary occlusion → crisis
- Provides pre-procedure evaluation checklist: infection clearance, neurological status, organ involvement screening
- Identifies dental anxiety management as a clinical safety intervention, not just comfort measure
- Notes vasoconstrictor risk: local anesthetic with epinephrine may contribute to localized tissue hypoxia in already-compromised vasculature

## Methodology
Clinical narrative review / case-based vignette format (Dent Clin North Am 2023, 4 pages). No RCT, no meta-analysis, no PMID-based systematic search described. Evidence level: expert opinion + cited observational studies.

## Results
- SCD prevalence ~10% in endemic populations (central India rural hospital cohort: 5.7% hospitalization rate, Kamble 2000)
- VOC triggers in dental setting: deoxygenation, dehydration, anxiety, infection, temperature change, prolonged procedures
- Crisis signs: sudden severe pain (typically limbs, back, abdomen), pallor, jaundice, oxygen desaturation
- Emergency response: supplemental O2, IV hydration, analgesics, emergency services activation
- No specific hemoglobin threshold or transfusion protocol specified within the abstract
