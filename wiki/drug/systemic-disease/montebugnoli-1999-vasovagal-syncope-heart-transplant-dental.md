## Three-line Summary

Case report of 3 vasovagal syncope episodes in 3 heart transplant patients during periodontal surgery (Bologna, Italy; abstract-only), used as a natural experiment because the transplanted donor heart is said to be completely deprived of vagal and sympathetic innervation.
Systolic blood pressure fell from 137 ± 5 mmHg to 76 ± 3.6 mmHg in each episode, but heart rate showed no relevant change (96.7 ± 4.5 → 102.6 ± 7.6 beats/min) instead of the bradycardia expected in normal subjects.
The authors conclude the heart plays only a marginal role in vasovagal syncope and that the reaction can occur without bradycardia — an inference that constrains, but with 3 single-centre episodes and abstract-only data does not settle, the mechanism debate.

## Summary

Montebugnoli and Montanari (Oral Surg Oral Med Oral Pathol Oral Radiol Endod 1999;87(6):666-9) report 3 episodes of vasovagal syncope in 3 heart transplant recipients undergoing periodontal surgery. The paper is set up as a test of mechanism: the pathogenesis of vasovagal syncope during emotional stress is contested, and one prominent account places the trigger in cardiac C-fiber mechanoreceptors connected through cardiac vagal afferents to the medullary cardiovascular centre. Because the donor heart is transplanted completely deprived of vagal and sympathetic innervation, it has been argued that transplant patients cannot mount a vasovagal reaction at all. The three observed episodes contradict that expectation. In each, systolic blood pressure fell dramatically (137 ± 5 mmHg → 76 ± 3.6 mmHg) while heart rate did not change relevantly (96.7 ± 4.5 → 102.6 ± 7.6 beats/min) — a vasodepressor collapse without the bradycardia that normally defines the reflex. The authors read this dissociation as evidence that the heart plays only a marginal role in the pathogenesis of vasovagal syncope, and that a vasovagal reaction can develop in the absence of bradycardia. Only the abstract was retrieved, so the report cannot be checked for monitoring method, denervation status, immunosuppression, or what was actually done during the episodes.

## Key Contributions

- Provides a clinical natural experiment on the efferent limb of the vasovagal reflex: a denervated donor heart cannot generate vagal bradycardia, yet syncope still occurred.
- Quantifies the dissociation — large systolic pressure fall (137 ± 5 → 76 ± 3.6 mmHg) with essentially unchanged heart rate (96.7 ± 4.5 → 102.6 ± 7.6 beats/min).
- Directly challenges the claim that heart transplant patients cannot show a vasovagal reaction.
- Argues that the heart's role in vasovagal syncope is marginal, shifting explanatory weight toward the vasodepressor (vascular) side rather than cardiac vagal outflow.
- States the clinical corollary explicitly: vasovagal syncope can occur without bradycardia, so bradycardia cannot be used as a necessary diagnostic feature.
- Extends the debate to dentistry by locating the episodes in periodontal surgery, where emotional stress and pain are the usual triggers.

## Methodology

Case report/series of 3 episodes in 3 heart transplant patients during periodontal surgery, from the Department of Emergency and Operative Dentistry, Clinica Odontoiatrica, Bologna, Italy. Blood pressure and heart rate were recorded during the episodes; the abstract does not state the monitoring device, sampling interval, syncope definition, patient ages, transplant vintage, immunosuppressive regimen, denervation assessment, or the intraoperative management given. There is no control group; the comparison is with the expected pattern in innervated normal subjects (vasodepressor response accompanied by bradycardia). Outcomes are the occurrence of syncope and the concurrent systolic blood pressure and heart rate values.

## Evidence Appraisal

| Domain | Assessment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Design | Very low | Uncontrolled case report; 3 episodes, 1 centre, no denominator (how many transplant patients were operated on without syncope is unknown), so no incidence can be derived |
| Retrieval | Incomplete | Abstract-only — full text not retrieved; methods, monitoring protocol, per-patient values and management are unreadable, so the haemodynamic numbers cannot be checked against the recording conditions |
| Denervation premise | Asserted, not measured | The argument depends on the donor heart being "completely deprived" of innervation; the abstract gives no denervation assessment, and transplant denervation is incomplete/heterogeneous in practice, so the natural experiment is imperfect |
| Mechanism inference | Constrained, not settled | Absent bradycardia is exactly what an absent efferent vagal limb predicts, so the report is strong evidence against bradycardia being *necessary*; it does not identify the afferent trigger or prove the heart irrelevant, since a vasodepressor response can coexist with residual or non-cardiac reflex pathways |
| Clinical use | Hypothesis-generating | Does not change acute management; it removes bradycardia as a required diagnostic criterion and warns that transplant patients are not exempt from vasovagal syncope |

**Bottom line:** 3 episodes from one centre, read from an abstract, cannot settle a mechanism debate. The defensible claim is narrow — in these patients a vasodepressor vasovagal collapse occurred without bradycardia, which constrains any model that treats cardiac vagal efferent outflow as the necessary final pathway. It does not establish where the reflex originates, and it does not license the stronger claim that the heart is irrelevant in innervated patients.
