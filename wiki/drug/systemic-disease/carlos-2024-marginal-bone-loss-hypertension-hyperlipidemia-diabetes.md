## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional record-based study (UNLV dental clinics, 2012–2022; 1,310 records screened → 57 patients/165 implants; predominantly elderly ≥65yr = 79%; IRB exempt) evaluating MBL correlation with hypertension (HTN), hyperlipidemia (HL), and diabetes mellitus (DM).

MBL prevalence was highest in HTN (78.95%, p<0.001) and HL (73.68%, p<0.001) patients, lowest in DM (40.35%, NS); combined HTN+HL had highest MBL incidence (29.82%, p=0.036); antihypertensive and statin medications themselves showed MBL association (both p<0.001).

The MBL-medication association likely reflects polypharmacy confounding in multimorbid elderly patients rather than direct drug-mediated bone loss — this is a descriptive cross-sectional study with a very small sample (n=57) and cannot establish causation.

## Study Design

- **Design**: Cross-sectional, retrospective record-based
- **Site**: UNLV School of Dental Medicine, Las Vegas (dental school clinic)
- **Period**: 2012–2022
- **Ethics**: IRB exempt (UNLV #UNLV-2022-256), HIPAA compliant
- **Screened**: 1,310 records → **57 patients / 165 implants** met inclusion
- **Software**: AxiUM dental practice management + MiPACS imaging
- **Radiographs**: Periapical only (no CBCT), long-cone technique, 70kV, 0.16s
- **MBL measurement**: Implant-abutment junction to coronal bone crest (mesial + distal); intra-examiner kappa=0.85

### Patient Demographics

| Variable | Value |
|---|---|
| Age ≥65 yr | 79% |
| Age 55–64 yr | 18% |
| Female | 52.63% |
| Smoking | 12.28% |
| Alcohol use | 31.58% |

| ≥4 medications | 67% |

## Results

### MBL by Systemic Disease

| Systemic disease | % with MBL | Chi-square | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertension (HTN) | **78.95%** | 19.105 | **<0.001** |
| Hyperlipidemia (HL) | **73.68%** | 12.789 | **<0.001** |
| Diabetes mellitus (DM) | 40.35% | 2.123 | 0.145 (NS) |

### MBL by Comorbidity Combination

| Combination | % with MBL | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| HTN + HL | 29.82% | **0.036** |
| HTN + DM | — | NS |
| HL + DM | — | NS |
| HTN + HL + DM | — | NS |

More systemic diseases → higher MBL burden (chi-square trend significant).

### MBL by Medication

| Medication | n | Chi-square | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| HTN medications | 41 | 30.422 | **<0.001** |
| Statin medications | 40 | 34.381 | **<0.001** |
| Diabetic medications | 24 | — | NS |

**Important**: ≥5 medications group had highest MBL burden.

## Critical Appraisal

### Why the Medication-MBL Association Should NOT Be Interpreted as Drug-Induced Bone Loss

The study finds antihypertensive and statin medications associated with MBL — this appears to contradict [[chidiac-2026-antihypertensive-antihyperlipidemic-antidepressant-implant-survival]] and [[masri-2024-early-implant-failure-antihypertensive-medications]]. The reconciliation:

1. **Polypharmacy confounding**: 67% of patients on ≥4 medications; 45.6% with ≥4 systemic diseases. Medications correlate with disease burden, not drug effects.
2. **Selection bias**: patients on AHT medications are, by definition, hypertensive — and HTN independently associated with MBL. The medication is a marker for disease, not a cause of MBL.
3. **Cross-sectional design**: cannot establish temporality (which came first — medication or MBL?). May reflect reverse causation (sicker patients accumulate more MBL and more medications over time).
4. **Very small sample** (n=57): all associations are susceptible to chance; wide confidence intervals not reported.
5. **Authors' own conclusion**: "further investigation is required to correlate the presence of MBL around dental implants with antihypertensive and statin medication use."

### Design Weaknesses

- **n=57 from 1,310**: only 4.4% met inclusion — extreme selection rate may introduce spectrum bias
- **No quantitative MBL measurements**: MBL assessed categorically (presence/absence) not in mm
- **Single examiner for measurements**: reduces inter-examiner error but cannot blind to patient diagnosis
- **Record-based**: relies on documentation accuracy in a dental school setting
- **No oral hygiene data**: cannot exclude plaque-related MBL as confounder

---

## Clinical Takeaway

This paper provides descriptive epidemiological data from a highly multimorbid elderly population. The main finding is that **HTN and hyperlipidemia are associated with higher MBL prevalence in a dental school elderly cohort** — but the study cannot determine whether this is caused by the diseases, the medications, or by confounders (polypharmacy, age, oral hygiene). It should be read alongside larger cohort studies (Chidiac 2026, Masri 2024) which show antihypertensives are protective for implant osseointegration.
