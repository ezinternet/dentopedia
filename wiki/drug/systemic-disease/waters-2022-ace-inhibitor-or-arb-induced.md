## Three-line Summary

Two dental patients on ACE inhibitor or ARB antihypertensives took their medication within 24 hours of general anesthesia for oral surgery, then developed persistent profound intraoperative hypotension refractory to conventional vasopressors. Both cases were successfully rescued with vasopressin administration. The mechanism is depletion of the renin-angiotensin system by ACE-I/ARB, rendering sympathomimetics ineffective, while vasopressin acts via V1 receptors independently of that pathway.

## 세줄요약

ACE-I/ARB가 레닌-안지오텐신 시스템을 고갈시켜 교감신경흥분제가 무력해지는 기전이며, 바소프레신은 V1 수용체를 통해 이 경로를 우회한다.

## Background

Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACE-I) and angiotensin receptor blockers (ARB) are first-line antihypertensive agents widely prescribed to dental patients. A known perioperative concern is that their continued use blunts the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) response to anesthesia-induced vasodilation, predisposing to refractory hypotension that fails to respond to typical vasopressor agents.

## Case Summary

**Setting**: Ambulatory dental surgery clinic; intubated general anesthesia for oral surgery.

**Patient profile (both cases)**:
- Diagnosis: hypertension managed with ACE-I (Case 1) or ARB (Case 2)
- Medication timing: drug taken within 24 hours prior to surgery

**Intraoperative event**:
- Persistent profound hypotension developed
- Refractory to conventional vasopressor treatment (traditional sympathomimetics)

**Resolution**:
- Vasopressin administration successfully managed hypotension in both cases

## Mechanism

ACE inhibitors and ARBs chronically suppress the renin-angiotensin system. Under general anesthesia, when systemic vascular resistance drops, the normal compensatory RAAS response is blunted. Conventional vasopressors (e.g., ephedrine — works via norepinephrine release/reuptake; phenylephrine — alpha-1 agonist dependent on intact adrenergic response) fail because the downstream pathway is depleted.

Vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone, ADH) acts via V1 vascular receptors — a pathway entirely separate from the RAAS and adrenergic system — thereby bypassing the ACE-I/ARB-induced deficit and restoring vascular tone.

## Clinical Takeaways

1. **Preoperative medication review**: Ask specifically about ACE-I and ARB use and timing before dental GA.
2. **Consider withholding**: Guidelines suggest holding ACE-I/ARB 24 hours before major procedures; these cases support applying this to dental GA as well.
3. **Emergency preparedness**: Vasopressin should be available in dental GA settings as a rescue agent for refractory hypotension in patients on ACE-I or ARB.
4. **Algorithm**: If hypotension is refractory to ephedrine/phenylephrine in an ACE-I/ARB patient → escalate to vasopressin before further standard measures.

## Limitations

- Case-report level evidence (n=2); no control group or systematic dosing protocol reported
- Abstract-only: specific doses, timing of vasopressin, and patient details not available from retrieved text
- Generalizability limited to ambulatory dental GA settings
