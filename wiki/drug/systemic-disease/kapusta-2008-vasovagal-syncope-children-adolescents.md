## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (MEDLINE search; Gen Dent 2008;56(4):340-5) aimed at dentists, defining the pathophysiology, incidence, treatment options, and dental implications of vasovagal syncope in children and adolescents.

The abstract reports no numbers at all: it asserts only that vasovagal syncope is not uncommon in this age group but not widely known among the dental community, and that information on its incidence in the paediatric dental setting is scarce.

The clinical implication is procedural, not quantitative — remove factors that predispose a patient to a vasovagal reaction, and keep the dental team familiar with the emergency protocol for a vasovagal episode.

## Summary

A dental-audience narrative review (General Dentistry 2008) whose argument is stated in its abstract in three moves. First, vasovagal syncope "is not an uncommon event in children and adolescents; however, this fact is not widely known among the dental community" — the review's premise is a recognition gap, not a new observation. Second, the author concedes the evidential floor: "there is little information about the incidence of vasovagal syncope occurring among children and adolescents in the dental setting." Third, the recommendation that follows is operational — dentists should eliminate factors that predispose a patient to a vasovagal reaction, and the dental team should be familiar with the emergency protocols for a vasovagal syncope episode.

The method described is a MEDLINE search using "vasovagal syncope", "syncope in children", "syncope treatments", and "syncope and dentistry", restricted to English-language peer-reviewed articles plus recent medical and dental textbooks related to syncope; the aim was to define pathophysiology, incidence, treatment options, and dental implications.

Read honestly, the paper is a 2008 problem statement for dentistry rather than an evidence source: it names paediatric VVS as under-recognised and under-counted, and it supplies no incidence figure, no treatment dose, and no protocol step in the abstract. Its lasting use in this wiki is that admission — the dental-setting incidence gap is explicitly stated by the review itself.

## Key Contributions

- Frames paediatric and adolescent vasovagal syncope as an under-recognised entity in dentistry, addressing a general-dentistry readership rather than a cardiology one.
- States outright that incidence data for vasovagal syncope in children and adolescents in the dental setting are scarce — an explicit evidence-gap statement, and the review's most citable sentence.
- Converts the topic into two chairside obligations: remove predisposing factors, and be competent in the emergency protocol for a vasovagal episode.
- Assembles pathophysiology, incidence, treatment options, and dental implications under four MEDLINE search themes — a topic map for 2008 readers.

## Methodology

Narrative (non-systematic) review. Single database: MEDLINE. Search terms: "vasovagal syncope", "syncope in children", "syncope treatments", "syncope and dentistry". Inclusion: English-language, peer-reviewed journal articles, plus the most recent medical and dental textbooks as they related to syncope. No search window, inclusion/exclusion criteria, study count, risk-of-bias assessment, or evidence grading is reported in the abstract. Outcomes are descriptive: pathophysiology, incidence, treatment options, dental implications.
