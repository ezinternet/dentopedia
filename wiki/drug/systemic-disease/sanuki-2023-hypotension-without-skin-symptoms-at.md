## Summary

This two-page Anesthesia Progress contribution (2023;70(2):91-92) by four Japanese dental anesthesiologists is built around a single question posed in its title: when a patient becomes hypotensive during local anesthesia in dental treatment but shows no skin symptoms, is the event anaphylaxis or a vasovagal reaction? The framing matters because the usual shortcut to "anaphylaxis" leans on cutaneous signs — urticaria, flushing, angioedema — and their absence leaves the clinician choosing between two very different interventions (epinephrine versus positional and vagal management) on hemodynamics alone.
