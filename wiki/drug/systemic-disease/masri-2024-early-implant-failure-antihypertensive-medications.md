## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (Rabin Medical Center, Israel; 792 patients/2,971 implants, 2013–2018) comparing early implant failure (EIF ≤12 months from loading) in normotensive (NT), hypertensive-medicated (HTN+med), and hypertensive-unmedicated (HTN-med) patients.

HTN+med group (n=185/784 implants) had significantly lower EIF than NT (2.29% vs 4.33% implant-level, p=.01; 9.70% vs 14.50% patient-level, p=.047); HTN-med group (n=14/64 implants) had the worst outcomes (EIF 6.25% implant-level, 28.60% patient-level), though the tiny unmedicated HTN group limits conclusions.

The dual mechanism — antihypertensives eliminate HTN-induced calcium dysregulation AND add direct osteoanabolic effects (RAAS inhibition, beta-blockade, thiazide-driven osteoblast stimulation) — explains why HTN patients on medication outperform normotensive controls.

후향코호트(라빈메디컬센터, 이스라엘; 792환자/2,971임플란트, 2013–2018) — 정상혈압(NT)·항고혈압약 복용고혈압(HTN+med)·항고혈압약 미복용고혈압(HTN-med) 세 군의 조기임플란트실패(EIF, 하중 후 ≤12개월) 비교.

고혈압 자체는 임플란트에 불리하지만 항고혈압약 복용 시 오히려 정상혈압 대조군보다 더 낮은 실패율 — 약물의 뼈 대사 촉진(RAAS 억제→RANKL 감소, 티아지드→조골세포 자극)이 기전.

## Study Design and Population

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort
- **Site**: Rabin Medical Center, Campus Beilinson, Israel (tertiary referral — high ASA 2/3 prevalence)
- **Period**: 2013–2018 (dental records)
- **Ethics**: 0674-19 RMC
- **Total**: **792 patients / 2,971 implants**

### Groups

| Group | Patients | Implants | EIF rate (implant) | EIF rate (patient) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normotensive (NT) | 593 | 2,123 | 4.33% (92) | 14.50% (86/593) |
| HTN + medications | 185 | 784 | **2.29%** (18) | **9.70%** (18/185) |
| HTN − medications | 14 | 64 | 6.25% (4) | 28.60% (4/14) |

**Note on HTN-med group (n=14)**: too small for definitive conclusions; results directionally consistent with the hypothesis but require validation with larger samples.

### Antihypertensive Medications (HTN+med group)

- Beta-blockers (BB): 61% of HTN+med patients — **most common**
- ACE inhibitors: 41%
- ARBs: 21%
- Calcium channel blockers: 19%
- Loop diuretics (Fusid): 10%
- Thiazide diuretics: 5%

**All grouped together** — no drug-specific EIF analysis performed.

### Inclusion/Exclusion

- **Included**: ≥12 months follow-up post-loading, specialist oral surgeon, ≥18 years, sufficient records
- **Excluded**: head/neck cancer, immune deficiency, immunosuppressants, heavy smokers (≥10 cig/10yr), untreated periodontal disease, malignant hypertension, ASA 4

## Results

### EIF — Implant Level

| Comparison | EIF rate | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| Overall cohort | 3.84% (114/2,971) | — |
| HTN+med vs NT | 2.29% vs 4.33% | **0.01** |
| HTN-med vs others | 6.25% | **<0.001** |

### EIF — Patient Level

| Group | EIF rate | p-value vs NT |
|---|---|---|
| NT | 14.50% (86/593) | — |
| HTN+med | 9.70% (18/185) | **0.047** |
| HTN-med | 28.60% (4/14) | **0.041** |

### Multivariate Logistic Regression

Controlling for age, gender, and implant count per patient:
- HTN+med vs NT: **OR = 0.618** (p=.059, marginal)
- The marginal p-value is consistent with the small absolute failure numbers; directional effect is robust

## Mechanistic Analysis

### Three-Part Argument

**Part 1 — HTN harms bone**

Hypertension → impaired calcium metabolism → increased calcium losses → secondary hyperparathyroidism → calcium mobilization from bone → decreased BMD and alveolar bone quality. Simultaneously, angiotensin II upregulates RANKL on osteoblasts → osteoclast activation.

**Part 2 — Antihypertensives reverse HTN damage**

- ARBs block angiotensin-II → RANKL reduction → osteoclast suppression
- Thiazides → intestinal calcium uptake ↑ + PTH ↓ + osteoblast differentiation ↑
- Beta-blockers → sympathetic input on osteoclasts ↓ → propranolol shown to enhance osseointegration in rat tibiae

**Part 3 — Antihypertensives add independent osteoanabolic effects**

RAAS inhibitors reduce periodontal inflammation and increase alveolar bone volume in preclinical studies. This may go beyond simply reversing HTN damage.

**Net result**: the dual mechanism (eliminate HTN-induced harm + add osteoanabolic effect) explains why HTN+med performs BETTER than normotensive controls.

---

## Limitations

1. **Retrospective design** — no randomization; compliance with medication unverified objectively
2. **HTN-med group very small** (n=14) — 28.6% EIF in this group drives the narrative but is statistically fragile
3. **All AHT drugs grouped** — no drug-specific analysis (BB vs ACEi vs ARB vs thiazide vs CCB)
4. **No blood pressure classification** — severity/control level not documented
5. **No soft tissue wound healing assessment** — only osseointegration (EIF) measured
6. **Referral center bias** — high ASA 2/3 prevalence may not represent general practice

---

## Clinical Takeaways

- **Hypertensive patients on their medications are good implant candidates** — EIF rates even better than normotensive controls
- **Critical question**: is the patient taking their antihypertensive medications? Compliance matters; unmedicated HTN is the highest-risk state (28.6% patient-level EIF)
- **Pre-operative question to ask**: "Are you taking your blood pressure medications regularly?" — this is a stronger predictor than the diagnosis alone
- Consistent with [[chidiac-2026-antihypertensive-antihyperlipidemic-antidepressant-implant-survival]] (HR 0.23 for AHT) and [[hamade-2024-hypertension-dental-implants-sr-ma]] (OR not significant overall — medication status not stratified in most studies is the likely confounder)
