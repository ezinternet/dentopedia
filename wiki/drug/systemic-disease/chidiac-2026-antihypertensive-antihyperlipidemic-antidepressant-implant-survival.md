## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort (STROBE; 655 patients/2,004 implants from 2,815/5,853 screened; Saint Joseph University Beirut, 2012–2022; 10-yr Kaplan-Meier) comparing mono-medicated antihypertensive, antihyperlipidemic, and mixed antidepressant groups against a non-medicated control (512 patients/1,581 implants).

Antihypertensive therapy: implant failure 1.29% vs control 5.12% — hazard ratio 0.23 (95% CI 0.07–0.74, p=0.013); antihyperlipidemic: 1.01%, HR 0.16 (p=0.048); mixed antidepressants: 12.09%, HR 2.17 (95% CI 1.17–4.04, p=0.014) — all log-rank tests p<0.05.

Antihypertensive and antihyperlipidemic monotherapy confer a significant protective effect on long-term implant survival, while mixed antidepressant (SSRI-containing) regimens significantly increase failure risk.

## Study Design and Population

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort, STROBE guidelines, Declaration of Helsinki compliant
- **Site**: Saint Joseph University of Beirut, Lebanon
- **Period**: January 2012 – December 2022 (10-year follow-up)
- **Ethics**: CER-2024-245
- **Screened**: 2,815 patients / 5,853 implants → **655 patients / 2,004 implants** met inclusion criteria

### Groups

| Group | Patients | Implants | Drug class |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control (non-medicated) | 512 | 1,581 | None |
| Antihypertensive (AHT) | 74 | 233 | Thiazides, beta-blockers, CCBs, ACEIs, ARBs |
| Antihyperlipidemic | 40 | 99 | Statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) |
| Mixed antidepressant | 29 | 91 | SSRIs and mixed antidepressants |

**Key inclusion criterion**: mono-medicated only (one drug class, no combination medications). This design isolates individual drug effects but may over-select healthier patients.

**Key exclusions**: ASA III/IV, diabetes, cancer, corticosteroids, antiepileptics, smokers, alcoholics, periodontal disease (BOP>10%), implant diameter <3.5mm, non-conical implants.

### Implant Failure Definition

Per Pisa Consensus Conference (ICOI): pain on function, mobility, radiographic bone loss ≥50% of implant length, uncontrolled exudate, or absence from oral cavity.

## Results

### Kaplan-Meier Survival

Over 10 years, cumulative survival was **higher** for AHT and antihyperlipidemic groups vs control, and **lower** for mixed antidepressants vs control. All differences statistically significant by log-rank test.

| Comparison | Chi-square | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| AHT vs Control | 8.406 | **0.004** |
| Antihyperlipidemic vs Control | 3.966 | **0.046** |
| Mixed antidepressant vs Control | 8.467 | **0.004** |

### Failure Rates and Cox Regression

| Group | Failure n/N | Failure % | HR (95% CI) | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control | 81/1,581 | 5.12% | Reference | — |
| AHT | 3/233 | **1.29%** | **0.23 (0.07–0.74)** | **0.013** |
| Antihyperlipidemic | 1/99 | **1.01%** | **0.16 (0.02–1.15)** | **0.048** |
| Mixed antidepressant | 11/91 | **12.09%** | **2.17 (1.17–4.04)** | **0.014** |

### Logistic Regression (Fixed-Effects)

OR antihypertensive = 0.248; OR antihyperlipidemic = 0.194; OR mixed antidepressant = 2.54.

### Confounders

Omnibus test: oral hygiene and detrimental habits did NOT show statistically significant effect on implant survival within study groups — confounding was tested and rejected.

## Mechanistic Framework

### Antihypertensives → Bone Protection

- **Beta-blockers**: sympathetic nervous system inhibition → reduced osteoclast differentiation and activity (propranolol enhances implant osseointegration in rat tibiae)
- **ACEIs/ARBs**: block angiotensin II → prevent RANKL upregulation on osteoblasts → reduced osteoclast activation
- **Thiazide diuretics**: enhance calcium uptake in intestines, suppress PTH, stimulate osteoblast differentiation and bone mineral formation
- **CCBs**: improve microvascular perfusion → better healing environment

### Statins (Antihyperlipidemics) → Osteoanabolic Effects

- Inhibit HMG-CoA reductase (cholesterol synthesis)
- **Pleiotropic bone effects**: enhance osteoblast activity, inhibit osteoclastic resorption, promote angiogenesis, improve bone-to-implant contact (BIC)
- Experimental and clinical studies confirm osseointegration benefit

### SSRIs (Mixed Antidepressants) → Bone Harm

- Serotonin receptors present on osteoblasts
- Reuptake inhibition → increased osteoclast differentiation, reduced osteoblast proliferation
- Decreased bone mineral density (BMD), increased fracture risk
- Impaired peri-implant bone healing during early osseointegration phase

## Limitations and Appraisal

1. **Retrospective design** — selection bias, recall bias for self-reported medications
2. **Group imbalance** — mixed antidepressant group very small (n=29); wide CI for antihyperlipidemic HR (0.02–1.15)
3. **Mono-medication exclusion** — strict inclusion may not represent real-world polypharmacy
4. **No drug-specific breakdown within AHT class** — all AHT grouped together
5. **Cureus journal** — open-access, peer-reviewed but lower impact factor than specialty journals
6. **Lebanese single-center** — may not generalize globally

**Evidence grade**: moderate — large n (655 patients), 10-yr KM, rigorous exclusion of confounders, but retrospective and single-center.

---

## Clinical Takeaways

- **Antihypertensive use is NOT a contraindication to implant therapy** — it is associated with improved outcomes
- **Statin use similarly protective** — supports implant placement in dyslipidemic patients on statins
- **SSRI/mixed antidepressant use warrants heightened monitoring** — 12% failure rate vs 5% control
- **Pre-treatment counseling**: document medication class, not just presence of hypertension; compliance with AHT medications predicts better outcomes (see [[masri-2024-early-implant-failure-antihypertensive-medications]])
- Consistent with [[hamade-2024-hypertension-dental-implants-sr-ma]] which found no significant overall failure increase from hypertension — the medication effect may be the driving variable
