## Three-line Summary

Prospective single-surgeon cohort in 387 adults (mean 56 ± 17 y; 183 women) injected with 2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine subcutaneously, without sedation, for minor skin, eyelid and hand procedures in Newfoundland (July 2019–November 2020) — **not a dental cohort**.

15/387 (3.9%) reported reactions: an "adrenaline rush" in 8 (2.2%) and a vasovagal response in 7 (1.8%); adrenaline rush was likelier at ages 18–50 (6/112, 5.4% vs 2/256, 0.8%; P = 0.006), with head/neck or upper-extremity injection (P = 0.048) and with onset >10 min after injection (P = 0.034), while dose (P = 0.433) and sex (P = 0.933) were unrelated.

Because every patient received epinephrine there is no epinephrine-free comparison, so the usable clinical content is the differential label at the moment of injection — sympathetic (palpitations/tremor/flushing → reassure) versus vasovagal (bradycardia/hypotension/pallor → head-down) — and the fact that mislabelling as allergy starts a false allergy record.

## 세줄요약

단일 술자 전향적 코호트, 성인 387명(평균 56 ± 17세, 여 183명) — 2% 리도카인 + 1:100,000 에피네프린(epinephrine)을 진정 없이 피하 주입해 피부·안검·수부 소수술을 시행했다(뉴펀들랜드, 2019-07~2020-11). **치과 코호트가 아니다.**

387명 중 15명(3.9%)이 반응을 보고 — "아드레날린 러시"(adrenaline rush) 8명(2.2%), 미주신경성 반응(vasovagal response) 7명(1.8%). 아드레날린 러시는 18–50세(6/112, 5.4% vs 2/256, 0.8%; P = 0.006), 두경부·상지 주입(P = 0.048), 주입 후 10분 초과 발현(P = 0.034)에서 많았고 용량(P = 0.433)·성별(P = 0.933)과는 무관했다.

전원이 에피네프린을 받아 **에피네프린 무첨가군과의 비교는 불가** — 이 논문의 쓸모는 주입 순간의 감별(교감신경성 = 안심 / 미주신경성 = 두위 하강)과, 알레르기로 오분류하면 허위 알레르기 기록이 생긴다는 점이다.

## Summary

This is a prospective single-surgeon cohort of 387 consecutive adults injected with 2% lidocaine containing 1:100,000 epinephrine for minor procedures (skin lesion excision, eyelid surgery, trigger finger and other small hand cases), all subcutaneous, all at room temperature, none sedated. Every patient was asked to describe any unusual symptom after injection and its duration, and the authors then sorted the reports into two labelled groups: an "adrenaline rush" (nervousness, anxiety, tremor, shaky feeling, flushing, diaphoresis, light-headedness, tingling, "heart racing") and a vasovagal response (nausea, feeling unwell, light-headedness, circumoral pallor, "may faint"). Fifteen of 387 patients (3.9%) reported any reaction: 8 (2.2%) were classed as adrenaline rush and 7 (1.8%) as vasovagal. Adrenaline rush was associated with younger age (18–50 y, 6/112 = 5.4% vs 2/256 = 0.8%; P = 0.006), with head/neck or upper-extremity injection rather than trunk/lower extremity (P = 0.048), and with onset more than 10 minutes after injection (P = 0.034); it was unrelated to sex or to volume injected. The population is non-dental (hand and plastic surgery), and because no patient received plain lidocaine the paper cannot say how much of the reaction is attributable to epinephrine — its clinical use is the bedside distinction between a sympathetic and a vasovagal reaction, and the argument that telling patients in advance prevents a false "local anaesthetic allergy" label.

## Key Contributions

- Puts **two incidence figures side by side from one cohort**: adrenaline rush 2.2% (8/387) and vasovagal response 1.8% (7/387), against 3.9% (15/387) reporting any reaction at all.
- Documents the **mislabelling pathway**: adrenaline-rush symptoms are commonly interpreted by patients as allergy, and the authors supply a pre-injection explanation script ("like you have had too much coffee … you are not allergic … it will go away in 30–60 minutes").
- Identifies **conditional risk markers for the sympathetic reaction**: age 18–50 (P = 0.006), head/neck or upper extremity site (P = 0.048), and onset >10 min (P = 0.034) — the latter consistent with slow extravascular→lymphatic epinephrine entry rather than an immediate intravascular spike.
- Restates a **vasovagal rescue sequence** usable at the chair: supine injection, then hip/knee flexion, pillow moved from head to feet, and Trendelenburg positioning.

## Methodology

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Prospective cohort (single surgeon, consecutive patients), no control arm |
| Setting | Plastic/hand surgery clinic, Newfoundland, Canada; July 2019–November 2020 |
| n | 387 (Cochran formula target 385; 95% CI, 5% margin, SD 0.5) |
| Age / sex | 56 ± 17 y (range 18–94); 183 women (48.5%), 194 men (51.5%) |
| Drug | 2% lidocaine + **1:100,000 epinephrine**, single regimen, room temperature |
| Route / volume | All subcutaneous; mostly 5–20 mL, 8 patients >20 mL |
| Sedation | None |
| Exclusions | <18 y; prior adverse reaction to local anaesthetic |
| Outcome | Patient-reported unusual symptoms after injection (type, duration); chi-squared associations with sex, age, site, volume, injection-to-procedure time |

Reported symptom sets (author-assigned groups):

- **Adrenaline rush** — nervousness, anxiety, tremor, shaky feeling, flushing, diaphoresis, light-headedness, tingling, "heart racing" (sympathetic).
- **Vasovagal** — nausea, feeling unwell, light-headedness, circumoral pallor, "may faint" (bradycardia / hypotension / pallor).

## Clinical Reading for Dentistry

> [!warning] This is **not** a dental cohort. Hand and plastic surgery, subcutaneous injection, 2% lidocaine with 1:100,000 epinephrine, volumes of 5–20 mL — several-fold a typical dental cartridge (1.8 mL). Use the differential logic, not the absolute percentages, at the dental chair.

- The two reactions point to **opposite first actions**. Suspected adrenaline effect: stop, reassure, observe — it self-limits in 30–60 minutes and needs no treatment. Suspected vasovagal: **do not leave the patient upright** — supine/Trendelenburg, legs up, monitor pulse (bradycardia confirms), and only then consider atropine if the pulse stays low with symptoms.
- The discriminator that survives at the chair is **pulse and skin**: tachycardia with tremor/flushing/diaphoresis suggests a catecholamine effect; bradycardia with circumoral or glabellar pallor, yawning, nausea and a sense of impending faint suggests vasovagal.
- Light-headedness appears in **both** symptom lists in this paper — it is not a discriminator, and the authors' grouping was a judgement, not an objective test.
- Neither reaction is an allergy. Labelling a tremor/flush episode as "lidocaine allergy" creates a durable false record that follows the patient into future care, so the explanation should be given **before** injection, not after.
