## One-line Summary
Landmark retrospective cohort (1499 implants/728 patients, McGill): antihypertensive medication users had dramatically lower implant failure (0.6% vs 4.1%; adjusted HR 0.12, 95% CI 0.03–0.49).

## Summary
Wu 2016 is the landmark study that first established a link between systemic medication (antihypertensives) and improved dental implant survival. In a McGill University retrospective cohort of 728 patients and 1499 implants, antihypertensive drug users experienced only 0.6% implant failure versus 4.1% in non-users — a hazard ratio of 0.12 (88% risk reduction). Drug composition: 54% RAS inhibitors, 18.9% beta-blockers, 5.4% thiazides. The dominant RAS inhibitor share (later noted by Saravi 2021) means the overall protective effect may be primarily RAS inhibitor-driven, but no subgroup analysis was performed. All subsequent studies on this topic have built upon or compared with this finding.

## Key Contributions
- First study showing systemic medication associated with higher dental implant survival
- HR 0.12 — 88% failure risk reduction for antihypertensive users
- Multilevel mixed effects survival analysis adjusting for confounders
- Drug composition analysis (54% RAS inhibitors) suggests RAS inhibitor-dominant effect, but unconfirmed
- Foundational reference for Masri 2024, Saravi 2021, Chidiac 2026, and Hamade 2024 SR+MA

## Methodology
Retrospective cohort, McGill University Faculty of Dentistry. 728 patients, 1499 implants (AHT: 142 patients/327 implants; Non-users: 586/1172). Multilevel mixed effects parametric survival analysis adjusted for confounders. Drug types: RAS inhibitors (54%), beta-blockers (18.9%), thiazides (5.4%), other (21.6%).

## Results
Implant failure: AHT 0.6% vs Non-users 4.1%. Adjusted HR 0.12 (95% CI 0.03–0.49) — highly significant. No subgroup analysis by drug class.
