Clinical practice guideline (ACC/AHA/HRS consensus document, Circulation 2017;136(5):e60–e122) for the evaluation and management of patients with syncope, retrieved here at bibliographic-record level only.

## Summary

This is the 2017 ACC/AHA/HRS guideline on syncope, a multi-society clinical practice guideline authored by a 16-member writing committee and published in Circulation (2017;136(5):e60–e122, epub 2017-03-09; erratum 2017;136(16):e271–e272). Of that document, only the PubMed bibliographic record was retrieved — the full text is paywalled, there is no PMCID or open-access version, and the stored artifact contains no structured abstract, let alone recommendation tables. Consequently this page documents the guideline's *shape* and refuses to document its contents: the document frames syncope evaluation around the reflex-versus-cardiac distinction and around short-term versus long-term risk stratification, which is exactly the cardiology-side assumption that procedural and dental vasovagal literature imports when it calls a vasovagal episode benign. Anyone needing a specific Class I/IIa/III statement or a diagnostic pathway from this guideline must open the full text; nothing here can stand in for it. The page's deliberate value is that the gap is visible rather than filled with plausible-sounding recalled recommendations.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishes the differential axis the dental literature assumes**: reflex (neurally mediated) syncope versus cardiac syncope. A chairside vasovagal episode is benign *conditional on* that exclusion having been considered, not by default.
- **Introduces time-horizon risk stratification**: short-term risk (events around the presentation) versus long-term risk (events over follow-up). The horizon, not the severity of the index event, determines the work-up.
- **Multi-society authority**: ACC + AHA + HRS authorship makes it the reference standard for syncope in North America, which is why secondary literature cites it instead of re-arguing the cardiology.
- **A boundary for this wiki**: this page adds the "is it cardiac?" question to the dental emergency cluster, and marks its own ceiling — the guideline's graded recommendations are *not* part of what the wiki currently holds.
