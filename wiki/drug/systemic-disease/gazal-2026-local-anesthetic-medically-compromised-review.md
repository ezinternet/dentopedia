## Three-line Summary

Comprehensive narrative review (35 studies, 2000–2025; Saudi J Anaesth 2026) on selecting the safest local anesthetic for medically compromised dental patients across nine systemic disease categories, introducing a unified condition-specific clinical decision table — the first such integrated reference in the literature.

Key recommendations: lidocaine for pregnancy (FDA Category B) and diabetes; mepivacaine for cardiovascular disease/hyperthyroidism (no vasoconstrictor); articaine for hepatic/renal impairment (85–90% plasma esterase metabolism, reduced systemic burden); prilocaine with felypressin contraindicated in pregnancy (uterotonic, fetal methemoglobinemia risk); bupivacaine avoided in elderly and cardiac patients (high cardiotoxicity, prolonged half-life); chloroprocaine (ester) for confirmed amide allergy.

Evidence is heterogeneous (8 observational, 1 RCT, 18 reviews, 8 case reports/guidelines); no large RCTs in multi-comorbid populations — emerging approaches include buffered LA, low-epinephrine articaine (1:400,000), and ultrasound-guided injection for anticoagulated patients.

## Clinical Decision Table

| Medical condition | Recommended LA | Key rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular disease** | Plain mepivacaine; lidocaine ≤2 carpules; prilocaine + felypressin | Avoid vasoconstrictors or limit epi ≤0.04 mg; minimize adrenergic stress |
| **Diabetes mellitus** | Lidocaine 2% with 1:100,000 epi (max 2 carpules) | Stress minimization; monitor blood glucose |
| **Hyperthyroid/thyroid disease** | Plain mepivacaine; plain prilocaine | Avoid epi — risk of thyrotoxic crisis |
| **Renal impairment** | Articaine; reduced-dose lidocaine | Plasma esterase metabolism; avoid accumulation |
| **Hepatic impairment** | Articaine (85–90% plasma); reduced-dose lidocaine | Minimal hepatic dependence |
| **Pregnancy** | Lidocaine (FDA Category B) | Avoid prilocaine/felypressin — uterotonic + fetal methemoglobinemia |
| **Bleeding disorders** | Plain lidocaine or articaine (infiltration only) | Avoid deep nerve blocks (hematoma risk) |
| **Elderly** | Articaine; reduced-dose lidocaine | Reduced hepatic/renal function; avoid bupivacaine |
| **Amide LA allergy** | Chloroprocaine (ester) | Rapid hydrolysis by plasma cholinesterase |
| **Asthma/COPD** | Plain lidocaine; plain mepivacaine | Avoid metabisulfite in vasoconstrictors; prilocaine caution in hypoxic states |

## Agent Pharmacology Summary

**Lidocaine**: Most widely used amide; rapid onset, intermediate duration; well-established safety in pregnancy (FDA Category B) and diabetes; requires caution with epinephrine (≤0.04 mg total) in cardiovascular patients.

**Mepivacaine**: Low vasodilatory activity; suitable without vasoconstrictor; preferred for cardiovascular/thyroid patients to avoid adrenergic stress.

**Articaine**: 85–90% metabolized by plasma esterases (not exclusively hepatic); high lipid solubility; rapid onset; preferred for hepatic/renal impairment and elderly. Available as 4% with 1:400,000 epi for reduced vasoconstrictor load.

**Prilocaine**: Dual hepatic/pulmonary metabolism; methemoglobinemia risk at high doses; contraindicated with felypressin in pregnancy (uterotonic + fetal methemoglobin formation). Caution in hypoxic/pulmonary-compromised patients.

**Bupivacaine**: Long duration; high cardiotoxicity (low CC/CNS ratio); restricted to post-op pain management; contraindicated in cardiac/elderly patients.

**Chloroprocaine** (ester): Ultra-short half-life via plasma cholinesterase hydrolysis; renewed clinical interest 2023–2024; safe alternative in amide allergy; ideal for pediatric/hepatic/renal-impaired patients.

## Clinical Insights

This review fills a practical gap: most LA safety literature addresses individual conditions in isolation. The unified decision table enables practitioners to navigate common clinical scenarios without consulting multiple sources.

**Key clinical decision points not always explicit in other sources:**
- Epinephrine limit in cardiovascular disease: ≤0.04 mg total (= ~2 standard dental carpules of 1:100,000 epi)
- Prilocaine/felypressin contraindication in pregnancy is dual: (1) uterotonic oxytocin-like effect of felypressin, (2) prilocaine metabolite o-toluidine causes fetal methemoglobin formation
- Chloroprocaine re-emergence: not just for allergy; fast hydrolysis makes it safer in compromised organ function, and recent formulations improve injection comfort
- Articaine's plasma metabolism advantage: relevant when hepatic CYP450 is impaired (liver disease, elderly with polypharmacy)
