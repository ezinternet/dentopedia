## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42024583905, PRISMA 2020; 6 studies qualitative synthesis, 4 studies meta-analyzed) evaluating the effect of RAAS-inhibitor (ACEI/ARB) and statin medication on periodontal status in patients at cardiovascular risk (hypertension or hypercholesterolemia).

RAAS-inhibitor effects on periodontal status in hypertensive patients were inconsistent across the 2 available studies (effect undetermined); statin therapy in hypercholesterolemic patients significantly reduced bleeding-on-probing (MD -13.4%, p=0.007) and probing depth (MD -0.38mm, p<0.00001) versus comparators, though clinical significance of the probing-depth change is uncertain.

Clinically: ACEI/ARB use should not be assumed periodontally neutral (external evidence cites 1.6–4.8× periodontitis risk with ACEI/ARB), while statins used for hypercholesterolemia appear to confer a modest, possibly non-clinically-significant, periodontal benefit — evidence base for both remains small and non-RCT.

## 세줄요약

심혈관질환 위험군(고혈압·고콜레스테롤혈증) 환자의 RAAS 억제제(ACEI/ARB)·스타틴 복용이 치주 상태에 미치는 영향을 평가한 SR+MA(정성분석 6편, 메타분석 4편, PROSPERO CRD42024583905).

RAAS 억제제는 고혈압 환자의 치주 상태에 비일관적 영향(2편만 존재, 효과 판정 불가)을 보였고, 스타틴은 고콜레스테롤혈증 환자의 출혈지수(BOP, -13.4%, p=0.007)와 치주낭깊이(PD, -0.38mm, p<0.00001)를 유의하게 개선했으나 PD 변화의 임상적 유의성은 불확실.

임상적으로 ACEI/ARB 복용을 치주적으로 중립적이라고 가정해서는 안 되며(외부 근거상 치주염 위험 1.6~4.8배), 스타틴은 경미한 치주 이득을 줄 수 있으나 근거 기반이 작고 RCT가 없어 신중한 해석이 필요.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD42024583905) synthesized evidence on how two major classes of cardiovascular medication — RAAS inhibitors (ACEI/ARB, used for hypertension) and statins (used for hypercholesterolemia) — affect periodontal status. Searching PubMed, ProQuest, EBSCO, and SpringerLink yielded 6,669 records, narrowed to 6 studies for qualitative synthesis and 4 for meta-analysis (ROBINS-I risk of bias: 4 low, 2 moderate). For RAAS inhibitors, only 2 qualifying studies existed and gave contradictory results (one found significantly worse periodontal parameters with ACEI use, though of doubtful clinical magnitude; the other found no significant difference except a small, clinically-insignificant gingival index difference between ARB and CCB groups), so no pooled effect could be determined. For statins, meta-analysis across 4 studies found a statistically significant reduction in bleeding-on-probing index and probing depth in statin users versus diet/normolipidemic comparators, but no significant effect on clinical attachment loss, and the authors themselves note the probing-depth effect's clinical significance is uncertain given the absence of an established MCID for this outcome.

## Key Contributions

- Directly quantifies the periodontal effect of RAAS-inhibitor and statin medication used for their primary cardiovascular indications (not as periodontal-therapy adjuncts), filling a gap left by adjunct-therapy-focused reviews like Muniz et al.
- Surfaces a coherent mechanistic narrative for ACEI/ARB potential harm: kinin-pathway pro-inflammatory activity and Th1 polarization via IL-12 (ACEI), and hyposalivation (ARB) — both plausible contributors to periodontal susceptibility.
- Reinforces the statin anti-inflammatory/antibacterial/bone-promoting mechanistic literature (IL-1β/IL-6/TNF-α suppression, BMP-2/VEGF-mediated osteoblast differentiation, RANK/RANKL/OPG modulation) with a systemic (not local/adjunct) statin-exposure dataset.
- Explicitly cautions against over-interpreting statistically significant but small-magnitude periodontal-index changes as clinically meaningful.

## Methodology

PRISMA 2020-compliant SR+MA of non-RCT clinical studies (case-control, cross-sectional, retrospective cohort). PICO: hypertension or hypercholesterolemia patients (P); systemic ACEI, ARB, or statin (I); other medicated/non-medicated comparator (C); periodontal indices (BOP, PI, GI), CAL, and probing depth as primary outcomes, periodontal-disease diagnosis as secondary (O). Four databases searched (PubMed, ProQuest, EBSCO, SpringerLink); Rayyan-based dual-blinded screening; ROBINS-I risk-of-bias assessment; RevMan 5.4 random-effects mean-difference meta-analysis with 95% CI.

## Results

**RAAS inhibitors (2 studies, not pooled):**
- Rodrigues et al.: ACEI group had significantly worse BOP, PI, CAL, PD than combination-antihypertensive control, but PD (0.47mm) and CAL (0.26mm) differences were not clinically significant.
- Ustaoğlu et al.: no significant difference between ACEI, ARB, CCB except GI (ARB 1.38 vs CCB 1.78 — 0.4-point difference, not clinically significant).
- External corroborating evidence cited in Discussion (not part of this review's own pooled analysis): ACEI associated with periodontitis risk ratios of 1.64× (Wang et al.) to 4.847× (Chatzopoulos et al.); ARB associated with 3.32× risk (Pająk-Łysek et al.).

**Statins (4 studies, meta-analyzed):**
- BOP index: MD -13.4% (95% CI -23.08 to -3.72, p=0.007) — significant.
- Probing depth: MD -0.38mm (95% CI -0.53 to -0.23, p<0.00001) — statistically significant, clinical significance uncertain.
- CAL: MD -0.16mm (95% CI -0.40 to -0.07, p=0.17) — not significant.
- PI/GI: not pooled due to heterogeneous index scales across studies.
