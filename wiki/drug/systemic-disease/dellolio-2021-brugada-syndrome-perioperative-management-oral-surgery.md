## Three-line Summary

Abstract-only case series (n=3 male patients with Brugada syndrome, single Italian oral surgery unit, 2020) reporting a multidisciplinary individual risk-assessment pathway built before oral surgical treatment.

Each patient was stratified by cardiologic hazard ratio, event-free survival and 5-year average risk of ventricular dysrhythmia and sudden cardiac death, plus ASA physical status, procedure complexity/duration and Modified Dental Anxiety Scale score; two received intravenous diazepam conscious sedation and all three were treated under lidocaine 2% with epinephrine 1:100,000 with no perioperative electrocardiographic changes.

The transferable content is the referral and risk-stratification step — pro-dysrhythmic exposures (local anaesthetics, anxiety, postoperative pain) are identified and modified before the chair — while the abstract reports no numerical risk values, no diazepam dose, no follow-up duration and no comparative outcome, so this cannot be read as a management trial.

## 세줄요약

초록만 확보된 증례군(case series, n=3) — 2020년 단일 이탈리아 구강악안면외과에서 브루가다 증후군 (Brugada Syndrome, BrS) 남성 3명에게 구강외과 치료를 시행하며, 치료 전 다학제 개별 위험평가 경로를 보고했다.

## Summary

This is a three-patient case series from the Complex Unit of Odontostomatology, University of Bari Aldo Moro (Italy), describing how three men with Brugada syndrome (BrS) were taken through oral surgical treatment in 2020. The authors' framing is that BrS carries a risk of major dysrhythmias that is *increased further* by pro-dysrhythmic exposures intrinsic to oral surgery — local anaesthetics, anxiety and postoperative pain — and that this risk is manageable through updated multidisciplinary management rather than avoidance. Before treatment, each patient went through a six-axis individual risk assessment: cardiologic hazard-ratio stratification, event-free-survival stratification, the 5-year average risk of ventricular dysrhythmia and sudden cardiac death described for Brugada patients, ASA physical status class, expected complexity and duration of the procedure, and anxiety measured with the Modified Dental Anxiety Scale (MDAS).

The anaesthetic plan then followed that assessment rather than a fixed protocol. One patient, susceptible to vasovagal syncope and needing a tooth extraction with concomitant cystectomy — a longer procedure — received conscious sedation with intravenous diazepam; a second, needing only a routine extraction (brief) but with a moderate dental anxiety score, received the same sedation; the third received local anaesthesia alone because his anxiety was low, his susceptibility to vasovagal syncope was low, and the extraction was routine. All three were treated with external biphasic defibrillator pads in place, continuous 12-lead electrocardiogram and a peripheral venous line, under local anaesthesia with lidocaine 2% plus epinephrine 1:100,000, with acetaminophen suggested for postoperative analgesia. No electrocardiographic changes occurred in the perioperative period.

The clinical value is therefore in the *front end* of the pathway — the decision to send the patient for cardiologic risk stratification and to let that stratification, plus anxiety and procedure length, set the anaesthetic plan. What the abstract does not provide is equally important: it reports no hazard ratios, no event-free-survival figures, no 5-year risk values, no diazepam dose, no sedation depth, no follow-up duration, and no outcome beyond the absence of perioperative ECG changes. With three patients and no comparator, this is a description of how one unit reasoned, not evidence that the reasoning works.

## Key Contributions

- **Names the dental-specific pro-dysrhythmic exposures.** Local anaesthetics, anxiety and postoperative pain are presented as factors that *add* to Brugada risk — a reframing of the dental appointment as an arrhythmic exposure rather than a neutral event.
- **A six-axis risk assessment that can be reproduced as a checklist**: cardiologic hazard ratio, event-free survival, 5-year ventricular-dysrhythmia/SCD risk, ASA class, expected procedure complexity and duration, MDAS anxiety score. The innovation is combining a cardiology axis with a dental-behaviour axis (MDAS) and a procedural axis (duration) in one decision.
- **Anxiety and procedure length as the levers that changed the anaesthetic**: sedation went to the vasovagal-susceptible patient facing the longer procedure and to the anxious patient facing a short one — i.e. the plan was individualized on two different grounds, not "all cardiac patients get sedation."
- **A concrete monitored-setting template**: external biphasic defibrillator pads, continuous 12-lead ECG, peripheral venous access — resuscitation capability established *before* the stimulus begins.
- **A drug-selection direction (not a validated protocol)**: IV diazepam for sedation, lidocaine 2% with epinephrine 1:100,000 for local anaesthesia, acetaminophen for postoperative analgesia.
- **An explicit statement that no perioperative ECG change occurred** — the only outcome the abstract reports.

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective descriptive case series (n=3). No control group, no randomization, no pre-specified outcome measures.
- **Setting**: Complex Unit of Odontostomatology, Department of Interdisciplinary Medicine, University of Bari Aldo Moro, Italy; year 2020. One co-author is from the same university's Unit of Anesthesiology.
- **Patients**: three men with Brugada syndrome requiring oral surgical treatment. Ages, comorbidities, Brugada ECG type (spontaneous vs drug-induced), genetic status, ICD status and medication history are **not reported in the abstract**.
- **Risk assessment (as listed in the abstract)**: cardiologic hazard-ratio stratification; event-free-survival stratification; 5-year average risk of ventricular dysrhythmias and sudden cardiac death for Brugada patients; ASA physical status risk class; expected complexity and duration of the procedure; anxiety score by MDAS.
- **Intervention**: IV diazepam conscious sedation (2 patients) or local anaesthesia alone (1 patient); all received lidocaine 2% with epinephrine 1:100,000.
- **Monitoring/preparedness**: external biphasic defibrillator pads positioned, continuous 12-lead ECG, peripheral venous access.
- **Postoperative analgesia**: acetaminophen suggested.
- **Outcomes reported in the abstract**: absence of perioperative electrocardiographic changes; completion of treatment. No numerical outcomes.

**Reading the table.** Every row marked "not reported" is genuinely absent from the abstract, not merely absent from the summary above — the page deliberately leaves those cells empty rather than inferring plausible values. In particular, the abstract's outcome statement is a *negative* monitoring finding ("no electrocardiographic changes"), which is not the same as a complication-free follow-up: no follow-up window is defined, so late arrhythmic events cannot be excluded from what is printed.

**Why "three patients" matters for interpretation.** A case series of three cannot estimate an event rate: with n=3 the confidence interval around "zero arrhythmic events" spans most of the plausible range, and patient selection (all three were assessed as fit enough to be treated in this unit at all) guarantees a favourable-looking denominator. The paper is therefore a description of a decision process, not a test of it.
