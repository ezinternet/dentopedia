## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (abstract-only retrieval) from the University of Zagreb on medical emergencies in paediatric dentistry, with no study population, no n, and no reported frequencies. Causes are classified as toxic/allergic drug reactions (local anaesthetics chiefly toxic; antibiotics, usually penicillin, chiefly allergic), stress-mediated vasovagal syncope, underlying disease (acute asthma attack, diabetic ketoacidosis, hypoglycaemia, seizures), and accidents (foreign-body aspiration with airway obstruction); untreated events can progress to cardiac and respiratory arrest requiring CPR. Its clinical value is the aetiological checklist plus the rule that every procedure and dosage be scaled to the child's age — it cannot be cited for frequency, and VVS-specific numbers must come from another source.

## Summary

This Croatian narrative review addresses life-threatening medical emergencies arising during paediatric dental treatment. It organises causes into four families: reactions to medication, which may be allergic or toxic (toxic reactions to local anaesthetics are the most common drug reaction, while allergies follow mainly antibiotic use, usually penicillin); the stress response, of which vasovagal syncope is the typical manifestation; underlying disease-specific pathology such as acute asthma attack, diabetic ketoacidosis, hypoglycaemia, or seizures; and accidents, exemplified by foreign-body aspiration obstructing the respiratory tract. The authors note that guidelines exist for all of these conditions and must be known by the treating dentist, because unrecognised complications or omitted measures can progress to cardiac and respiratory arrest requiring cardiopulmonary resuscitation. The paper closes on the paediatric-specific principle that all procedures and drug dosages must be adapted to the child's age. Critically for evidence use, the retrieved text is abstract-only and contains no numerical data whatsoever.

## Key Contributions

- Provides an aetiological taxonomy of paediatric dental emergencies: drug (toxic vs allergic), stress, systemic disease, accident.
- Distinguishes drug-reaction mechanisms by drug class — local anaesthetics predominantly toxic, antibiotics (penicillin) predominantly allergic.
- Positions vasovagal syncope as the canonical stress-mediated emergency of the dental setting.
- Enumerates the disease-specific emergencies to be recognised in children: acute asthma attack, diabetic ketoacidosis, hypoglycaemia, seizures.
- States the arrest pathway (cardiac/respiratory arrest → CPR) and the age-scaling rule for procedures and dosages.
