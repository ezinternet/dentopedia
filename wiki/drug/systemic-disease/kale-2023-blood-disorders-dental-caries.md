## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Cureus 2023) synthesizing oral manifestations and dental caries risk mechanisms across 10 hematological disorders including β-thalassemia, sickle cell disease (SCD), iron deficiency anemia (IDA), hemophilia, Plummer-Vinson syndrome (PVS), and leukemia. Salivary hypofunction (reduced flow, impaired buffering) is identified as the shared cariogenic pathway; IDA-driven early childhood caries (ECC) and post-radiation leukemia (DMFT 7.13 vs controls 1.8) represent the highest documented caries burdens. Clinical oral hygiene guidelines are provided per disorder.

## Summary
This narrative review from Sharad Pawar Dental College (Cureus 2023) synthesizes oral and dental consequences of 10 hematological disorders: β-thalassemia (BTM), sickle cell disease (SCD), iron deficiency anemia (IDA), leukemia, hemophilia, Plummer-Vinson syndrome (PVS), erythroblastosis fetalis, Fanconi anemia, cyclic neutropenia, and acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL). The unifying mechanism is that these disorders impair salivary function (reduced flow, altered pH, decreased buffering), compromise immune defense against cariogenic bacteria, alter bone structure (especially BTM), and create medication-related cariogenic exposures. The review recommends individualized oral hygiene protocols including fluoride toothpaste, soft toothbrushes, sugar restriction, artificial saliva, and adjusted scaling frequency. It emphasizes dentist–hematologist collaboration, especially for procedures requiring coagulation support (e.g., hemophilia IAN blocks) or pre-chemotherapy extractions (Fanconi anemia, ALL).

## Key Contributions
- Maps caries risk mechanism for each of 10 blood disorders, enabling disorder-specific preventive protocols
- Highlights salivary hypofunction as the shared pathway: reduced flow → reduced buffering → pH drop below 5.5 → enamel demineralization
- Identifies IDA as a driver of early childhood caries (ECC) in children under 5 via impaired salivary gland function
- Notes BTM-specific maxillofacial deformity (marrow hyperplasia → maxillary protrusion, malocclusion) that complicates dental rehabilitation
- Flags hemophilia-specific risk: IAN block requires factor replacement; post-extraction hemorrhage frequency correlates with severity classification

## Methodology
Narrative review; PubMed electronic search (no PRISMA, no PROSPERO registration reported). Covers published case-control studies, observational studies, and case reports on each disorder. No meta-analysis or pooled effect sizes.

## Results
- BTM: DMFT higher than controls (Al-Wahadni 2002 n=61+63; Arora 2014; Dhote 2015); salivary hypofunction + oral burning + atrophic glossitis prominent
- SCD: decreased salivary flow, enamel hypomineralization, delayed eruption, mental nerve neuropathy; low-income SCD patients had more decayed, fewer filled lesions (Laurence 2002 pilot)
- IDA: ECC in <5 years via reduced salivary pH and buffering; early first molar loss → nutritional compromise cascade
- Leukemia: post-CNS radiation DMFT 7.13 vs chemotherapy-only 3.4 vs controls 1.8 (Pajari); 2.7 new carious lesions/year during ALL treatment
- Fanconi anemia: 35% caries prevalence in children aged 2–18 (Tekcicek 2007)
- Hemophilia: higher caries and poorer oral hygiene due to avoidance of brushing and dental care to prevent bleeding episodes
