## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 23 placebo-controlled RCTs (4217 adults, mean age 40.3 y; depression n=13, pain n=9, dyspepsia n=1) comparing oral amitriptyline vs placebo for adverse drug reactions (ADRs).

Anticholinergic ADRs were far more frequent with amitriptyline (OR 7.41, 95% CI 4.54-12.12; RD 0.35; NNH 2.89); dry mouth was the most frequently reported, with OR 11.10 (6.46-19.06) for the aggregated dry-mouth category and 11.60 (6.42-20.98) for individual dry mouth (15 studies), while non-anticholinergic ADRs did not differ from placebo (OR 1.18, 0.74-1.89).

Clinically, dry mouth is a highly probable, non-dose-dependent effect of amitriptyline, but 17/23 trials were high risk of bias with heterogeneity of 83% and mean age ~40, so estimates may understate risk in older adults.

## 세줄요약

플라시보 대조 무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) 23편(성인 4217명, 평균 40.3세; 우울증 13편·통증 9편·소화불량 1편)을 대상으로 아미트립틸린 (Amitriptyline)과 위약의 약물이상반응 (Adverse Drug Reaction, ADR)을 비교한 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis, SR+MA).

항콜린성 ADR이 아미트립틸린군에서 크게 많았고 (OR 7.41, 95% CI 4.54-12.12; 위험차 0.35; 유해수 (Number Needed to Harm, NNH) 2.89), 구강건조 (dry mouth)가 가장 흔히 보고된 항목으로 통합 구강건조 범주 OR 11.10 (6.46-19.06), 개별 구강건조 OR 11.60 (6.42-20.98; 15편)였으며 비항콜린성 ADR은 위약과 차이가 없었다 (OR 1.18, 0.74-1.89).

임상적으로 구강건조는 용량과 무관하게 나타날 가능성이 높은 아미트립틸린 효과이나, 23편 중 17편이 높은 비뚤림 위험 (Risk of Bias)이고 이질성 83%이며 평균 연령이 약 40세여서 고령자 위험은 과소평가일 수 있다.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0284168](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0284168)), this PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO-registered SR+MA pooled 23 double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs (published 1970-2018, at least 100 participants each) of oral amitriptyline across indications, to quantify adverse drug reactions independent of the indication. Anticholinergic ADRs occurred much more often with amitriptyline than placebo (OR 7.41; RD 0.35; NNH 2.89), general ADRs were also increased (OR 3.74; NNH 6.23), and non-anticholinergic ADRs were not (OR 1.18). Dry mouth was the strongest single-symptom signal (individual OR 11.60, 95% CI 6.42-20.98, 15 studies) and was the most commonly reported anticholinergic ADR. The paper found no dose dependence, but its own discussion attributes this partly to poor reporting of the dose at ADR onset.

Dental relevance is indirect: the paper does not address oral health, salivary flow, caries or any dental outcome. It documents how likely and how large the dry-mouth effect of a tricyclic antidepressant is in placebo-controlled trials, which is the drug-side input to the xerostomia mechanisms and deprescribing-first management described in [[geriatric-dentistry/halasabalu-kalgeri-2026-polypharmacy-oral-health-elderly]].

## Key Contributions

- First multiple-indication SR of amitriptyline ADRs vs placebo, restricted to RCTs to avoid disease-symptom and nocebo bias.
- Splits ADRs into anticholinergic, non-anticholinergic and general categories with OR, RD and NNH.
- Identifies dry mouth as the largest and most frequently reported anticholinergic reaction.
- Shows the anticholinergic signal is robust to risk-of-bias and outcome-type sensitivity analyses.

## Methodology

- Searches: MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO, PsycLIT, Psyndex, CENTRAL (inception to Sept 2022), grey literature, FDA/EMA and trial registries; 1,898 records, 471 full texts, 23 included.
- Eligibility: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs, oral amitriptyline, any indication/dose, at least 100 adults, group-wise ADR data.
- Population: 4217 patients (67% female), mean age 40.34 y; depressive disorders (n=13), pain (n=9), functional dyspepsia (n=1); doses 5-300 mg/day, flexible in 20/23; median titration period 8 weeks.
- Analysis: RoB 2; random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird) ORs; RD and NNH for primary outcomes; meta-regression on dose; subgroup and sensitivity analyses; analysis only with at least 5 studies.

## Results

| Outcome | Studies | OR (95% CI) | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACH-ADRs (primary) | 20 (n=3,510) | 7.41 (4.54-12.12) | RD 0.35 (0.26-0.46); NNH 2.89; I2 83% |
| NACH-ADRs | 14 (n=2,442) | 1.18 (0.74-1.89) | NNH 34.27; RD 0.03 (-0.02 to 0.07) |
| G-ADRs | 21 (n=3,437) | 3.74 (2.69-5.20) | NNH 6.23; RD 0.16 (0.10-0.22) |
| Dry mouth-related (aggregated) | 15 (1294 vs 1247) | 11.10 (6.46-19.06) | I2 69% |
| Dry mouth (individual) | 15 (1100 vs 1050) | 11.60 (6.42-20.98) | I2 70% |
| Somnolence | 8 | 5.06 (4.01-6.39) | I2 0% |
| Constipation | 13 | 3.06 (2.16-4.34) | I2 14% |
| Discontinued due to ADRs | 13 | 3.57 (2.26-5.65) | I2 13% |
| Headache | 9 | 0.73 (0.55-0.97) | favors amitriptyline |

- Risk of bias: 17 high, 4 medium, 2 low; 15 of the 17 high-RoB studies had attrition of at least 20%.
- Dose was not a predictor of anticholinergic ADR frequency (meta-regression p = .40).
- Funnel plot asymmetry was significant only for NACH-ADRs (p = .01).
- The authors cite Leucht et al. (depression) reporting dry mouth OR 13.50, versus 11.60 here.
- Limitations: mean age ~40 (older adults, who the authors say are more sensitive, were not studied); 269 small studies (<100 participants) excluded; falls and cognitive decline not reported; salivary flow not measured; no dry-mouth-specific NNH reported.
