## Three-line Summary

Statins exert pleiotropic effects on oral health — anabolic bone metabolism via BMP-2 upregulation and osteoclast inhibition, anti-inflammatory/MMP-inhibiting, and antimicrobial properties — spanning periodontitis, implant osseointegration, pulp cells, orthodontic relapse, wound healing, and oral oncology. Local adjunctive statin use significantly improves periodontal clinical parameters (CAL, PPD, bone fill) in 6 SR/MAs; ~95% of animal implant studies confirm enhanced BIC; pulp cell studies support dentin regeneration potential. Most evidence is preclinical; human studies are few and heterogeneous; optimal dose, route, and statin type require large-scale RCTs.

## Summary

Tahamtan et al. (2020) conducted a PRISMA-guided narrative review of 68 preclinical and clinical studies (1990–2019) evaluating statins across seven dental domains. Statins are HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors primarily used to lower LDL cholesterol and reduce cardiovascular risk. Their pleiotropic biological properties — BMP-2-driven osteoblast stimulation, osteoclast suppression via OPG/RANKL modulation, MMP inhibition, anti-inflammatory cytokine reduction, antimicrobial and antifungal activity, and VEGF-stimulated angiogenesis — collectively make them relevant to multiple aspects of oral and dental health.

The review documents consistent beneficial effects across periodontal therapy (local > systemic delivery), alveolar bone preservation (mostly animal data), implant osseointegration (95% positive animal studies, no human RCTs), dental pulp regeneration (DPSC mineralization, pulp capping potential), reduction of orthodontic relapse (via OPG upregulation), wound/bone healing acceleration, radioprotection of salivary glands, and anti-tumor activity against HNSCC and SACC. Key limitations include reliance on animal models at supratherapeutic doses, absence of standardized local delivery protocols, and scarcity of long-term human RCT data.

## Key Contributions

1. First comprehensive review synthesizing statin effects across all major dental domains in a single paper
2. Pooled SR/MA evidence confirms SRP + local statin significantly improves CAL, PPD, bone fill vs SRP alone; rosuvastatin most efficacious locally
3. ~95% of animal implant studies demonstrate enhanced osseointegration (BIC, new bone formation) with local or systemic statins
4. Statins stimulate DPSC mineralization and dentin regeneration; cohort study shows 93% vs 70% healing rate of apical periodontitis (statin users vs non-users, 2–5 yr)
5. OPG upregulation + RANKL suppression = statin mechanism for reduced orthodontic relapse; confirmed in RCT (simvastatin gel, space re-opening, p<0.001)
6. Simvastatin radioprotective in salivary gland irradiation model (restored amylase activity, reduced MDA)
7. Atorvastatin/simvastatin anti-tumor effects on HNSCC and SACC via ERK1/2 + STAT3 inhibition, RhoC membrane fraction reduction

## Methodology

- **Study type:** Narrative review, PRISMA-compliant
- **Search period:** January 1990 – March 2019
- **Databases:** Web of Science, Medline, SCOPUS, Google Scholar
- **Inclusion:** Animal and human studies (in vitro molecular studies excluded)
- **Sample:** 69 screened → 68 full-text → 48 included (20 excluded as in vitro only)
- **Outcome threshold:** Only results with p < 0.05 reported
- **Drug delivery strategies and concentrations:** Catalogued per study in Table 2

## Results

### Chronic Periodontitis

Six SR/MAs (Bertl 2017 ×2, Akram 2017, Estanislau 2015, Meza-Mauricio 2018, Muniz 2018) confirm:
- SRP + local statin adjunct significantly improves CAL, PPD, bone fill, sulcus bleeding index at 6–9 months
- Local delivery superior to systemic for clinical periodontal outcomes
- Rosuvastatin most efficacious local agent; atorvastatin significant by meta-regression
- Clinical dose: 1.2 µg/site with biodegradable carrier (PLGA or methylcellulose)

### Alveolar Bone Preservation

- Animal studies: local statin in PLGA or carbonate apatite → significantly greater ridge height, BMD, trabecular thickness post-extraction
- Two clinical RCTs showed non-significant trends; PLGA/HA/simvastatin had fewer complications than PLGA/HA alone but failed to form bone at 3 months
- Periodontitis models: atorvastatin 27 mg/kg prevents ABL, cemental resorption; simvastatin reverses ligature-induced ABL by 46% (micro-CT)
- Dose note: systemic anti-resorptive in rodents requires ≥10× the human cholesterol-lowering dose

### Implant Osseointegration

- ~95% of animal studies: local/systemic statins enhance BIC, bone density, new bone formation around titanium
- Fluvastatin 75 µg topical: significantly higher BIC and MBV vs control at 2–4 weeks; positive correlation MBV ↔ push-in strength
- One study: implant surface coating with statin impaired osseointegration
- No human RCT data; translation barriers — supratherapeutic animal doses, peritoneal vs oral route, tibia ≠ alveolar bone, first-pass effect reduces oral bioavailability to 10–20%

### Dental Pulp Cells

- PLGA-lovastatin nanoparticles: comparable to MTA at 4 weeks (tubular reparative dentin, complete dentinal bridge in rat)
- Simvastatin 1 mmol/L: suppresses DPSC proliferation; ↑ ALP activity + mineral nodule formation + dentin regeneration post-pulpotomy
- Cohort (n=60): 93% healing of apical periodontitis (statin ≥2 yr) vs 70% non-statin at 2–5 years
- Pulp calcification: retrospective study shows ↓ vertical height ratio of pulp chamber in statin users (↑ odontoblastic activity)
- Direct pulp capping: simvastatin 1–10 µM inferior to calcium hydroxide but showed hard tissue formation without inflammation

### Orthodontic Tooth Movement / Relapse

- Atorvastatin: significantly reduces OTM and osteoclast counts in rats; no effect on femoral endochondral ossification
- Simvastatin RCT (n=16): significant reduction in space re-opening (p<0.001); GI reduction greater in statin group
- Mechanism: OPG ↑, RANKL ↓ → inhibited osteoclastogenesis → reduced relapse
- Inconsistent results: two studies found no significant relapse reduction; effects likely dose- and model-dependent

### Wound and Bone Healing

- Rosuvastatin local: significantly more new bone at 2 weeks post-mandibular fracture (R-14 vs C-14)
- Simvastatin/chitosan gel 10 mg/mL: significant wound-healing score reduction at days 3 and 7 post-FGG; VAS pain significant on days 1, 3, 5, 7
- Mechanism: statin inhibits leukocyte adhesion/extravasation → reduces T-cell co-stimulation → facilitates early wound repair; enhances macrophage infiltration → VEGF, re-epithelialization

### Salivary Gland

- Simvastatin i.p.: radioprotective — remits saliva secretion reduction, restores amylase activity in irradiated murine model
- Mechanism: scavenges MDA (malondialdehyde), remits collagen deposition, delays TGF-β1 elevation induced by radiation

### Anti-tumor Effects (HNSCC / Salivary Carcinoma)

- Simvastatin dose-dependently inhibits SACC-LM proliferation; miR-21 inhibitor reduces statin resistance
- Atorvastatin: ↓ RhoC membrane fraction, ↓ ERK1/2 + STAT3 phosphorylation, ↓ HNSCC migration/invasion/proliferation
- Lovastatin + gefitinib: >90% SCC9 cell death (vs monotherapy); simvastatin + cetuximab + radiation: ↓ tumor growth in xenograft
- Statin monotherapy: >50% tumor cell burden reduction possible
- Mevalonate pathway products (cholesterol, isoprenoids) required for malignant cell viability; statin interruption → cell cycle arrest

### Adverse Oral Effects

- Small study: dry mouth, itchiness, bitterness, cough during statin therapy; marked improvement after temporary discontinuation
- Evidence base insufficient; requires adequately powered prospective trials
