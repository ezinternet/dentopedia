## Three-line Summary

Single case report (n=1, 33-year-old female, no medical history) of recurrent vasovagal syncope during a surgically complicated mandibular third molar extraction in a patient who had already fainted during a previous simple extraction.

Two episodes in one session: the first without loss of consciousness (malaise, pallor, nausea) and the second with transient loss of consciousness about 10 min after the procedure was resumed; a later appointment finished the extraction without syncope after prodrome-triggered interruption and supine positioning.

The authors propose a three-phase escalation model and flag hypersalivation as a parasympathetic prodromal marker, but no blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation, or local anesthetic dose was recorded — so the model is conceptual, not demonstrated.

## Summary

A single-author case report describes a 33-year-old woman with no relevant medical history who presented for emergency extraction of a right mandibular third molar with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, and who had fainted during her only previous extraction (a technically simple maxillary third molar). Anticipatory anxiety was high because she had been told the current extraction would be more complex. Profound anesthesia was difficult to obtain, requiring multiple local anesthetic administrations, hypersalivation was pronounced, and the crown fractured intraoperatively leaving two of three roots, prolonging the session. A first vasovagal episode without loss of consciousness (malaise, pallor, nausea) resolved rapidly after interruption, instrument removal and supine positioning with lower-limb elevation; about ten minutes after resuming, a second episode produced transient loss of consciousness and the procedure was definitively stopped. At a subsequent appointment the same prodrome (dizziness, hypersalivation) recurred during completion, was recognized immediately, and the extraction was finished without syncope. The authors read the sequence as evidence that apparent recovery does not equal autonomic stability — citing literature that parasympathetic dominance can persist 15–30 min — and therefore advise against resuming in the same session once prodromal signs (especially hypersalivation) persist. The paper is explicit that no blood pressure, heart rate, or oxygen saturation was recorded and presents its three-phase model as a conceptual application of known physiology rather than a measured demonstration.

## Key Contributions

- Documents **recurrence with escalating severity inside a single appointment**: episode 1 without loss of consciousness → episode 2 with transient loss of consciousness, separated by only ~10 minutes of resumed treatment.
- Formalizes the clinical rule that **apparent recovery ≠ autonomic stability** (claimed window 15–30 min from continuous-monitoring literature), turning "how long do I wait?" into an explicit decision point.
- Promotes **hypersalivation** from a background finding to a *parasympathetic prodromal marker* — its persistence after episode 1 is framed as the warning that recurrence is imminent.
- Argues that **prior vasovagal history during a technically easy procedure** is itself a high-susceptibility signal, warranting enhanced prophylaxis from the first appointment rather than after the first faint.
- Provides a practical three-phase escalation model (trigger accumulation → incomplete autonomic reset → premature re-exposure) usable as a chairside teaching scaffold for anxious extraction patients.

## Methodology

- **Design**: case report (clinical observational case), single patient, single operator, narrative chronology; no protocolized measurement schedule, no comparator.
- **Patient**: 33-year-old female, no relevant medical history; symptomatic irreversible pulpitis of the right mandibular third molar.
- **Vasovagal history**: one prior episode during a previous, technically uncomplicated maxillary third molar extraction, attributed to anxiety at a first-ever extraction.
- **Procedure**: surgical extraction — multiple local anesthetic administrations (agent, concentration, and total dose **not reported**), crown fracture with two of three roots retained, prolonged operative time.
- **Outcomes reported**: presence/absence of loss of consciousness, prodromal signs, sequence and nature of interventions, and completion of treatment at a follow-up appointment. Hemodynamic parameters were **not** recorded.
- **Analysis**: qualitative mapping of the observed sequence onto established autonomic reflex physiology; the proposed three-phase model is described by the authors as conceptual, not a physiological demonstration.

| Throughout procedure | Pronounced **hypersalivation** (parasympathetic predominance) | — |
| Intraoperative complication | **Crown fracture**; 2 of 3 roots retained → longer operative time, higher stress | Continued root retrieval |
| **Episode 1** | **No LOC** — malaise, pallor, nausea | Immediate interruption; all instruments removed from oral cavity; reassurance; **supine + lower-limb elevation** |
| Recovery 1 | Rapid, "apparently complete" clinical recovery | — |
| **Episode 2** | **~10 min after resuming** — **transient loss of consciousness** | Definitive cessation; emergency management protocols; new appointment scheduled |
| Follow-up visit | Anxiety about the previous experience; **prodrome recurred** (dizziness, hypersalivation) during completion | Immediate interruption; supine; extended observation + supportive management |
| Outcome | Extraction **completed without syncope**; monitored to full stabilization before discharge; advised medical review if recurrence | No drugs/doses reported |
| Recorded vitals | **None** — BP, HR, and SpO2 not systematically recorded during either episode (stated in Limitations) | — |

Management recommendations the authors draw from the case (not interventions actually given):

| Phase | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Acute episode | Stop at first prodrome; supine with legs elevated; clear the airway/mouth; monitor pulse, BP, SpO2 until stable; reassure; observe **≥15–20 min** before vertical positioning |
| Resume or defer | Resume only if recovery is complete **and** little work remains, with continuous monitoring; defer if substantial work remains; **never resume while prodrome persists** |
| Next appointment | Anxiety assessment ± oral benzodiazepine premedication; morning slot; hydration/food; semi-reclined start; profound anesthesia before invasive steps; intermittent breaks; gradual return upright |
| Referral | Recurrent or refractory cases → medical evaluation; midodrine/fludrocortisone or cardioneuroablation only by specialists |
