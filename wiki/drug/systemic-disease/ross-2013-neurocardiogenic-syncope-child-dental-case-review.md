## Three-line Summary

Pediatric case review (Temple University 2013; Pediatr Dent 35(1):71-3) documenting a 7-year-old child who experienced loss of consciousness due to neurocardiogenic syncope (NCS) during dental treatment, with description of clinical manifestations and successful recovery with supportive management.

Neurocardiogenic syncope — a synonym for vasovagal syncope (VVS) — frequently occurs in children; this case illustrates that dental appointments are a known triggering context, and that the clinical presentation and immediate management in a child are consistent with the adult VVS model.

Abstract-only; no quantitative data — a brief teaching case for pediatric dental practitioners.

## Key Clinical Points

**Diagnosis**: Neurocardiogenic syncope (NCS) = vasovagal syncope — the two terms are interchangeable; NCS is preferred in cardiology/pediatrics literature.

**Frequency in children**: NCS is described as "frequently" occurring in children — consistent with Kapusta 2008 ([[kapusta-2008-vasovagal-syncope-children-adolescents]]) which characterizes orthostatic VVS in a pediatric population.

**Dental treatment as trigger**: The case documents loss of consciousness occurring *during* dental treatment, adding dental-chair context to the pediatric VVS trigger map.

**Management**: Remedial supportive management successful — specific details not available in abstract.

## Clinical Insights

This case is the most direct evidence in the current wiki that **children can experience VVS/NCS during routine dental treatment**. Most of the wiki's VVS literature concerns adults; Kapusta 2008 covers pediatric VVS in a general (non-dental) context, and this paper fills the gap by placing a child in a dental chair.

For pediatric dental practitioners: NCS should be on the differential for any child who becomes pale, clammy, or unresponsive during an appointment. The management principles (Trendelenburg, stimulus removal, supportive care) are the same as for adults, though lower body weight changes dosing calculations if pharmacological intervention is ever required.
