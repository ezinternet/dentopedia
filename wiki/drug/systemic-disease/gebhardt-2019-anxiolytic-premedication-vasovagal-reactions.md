## Three-line Summary

Retrospective single-centre chart analysis of 2747 outpatients (2008–2017) receiving low-dose intrathecal anaesthesia (IA), comparing vasovagal reactions and time to home readiness in patients with versus without anxiolytic premedication (1–2 mg midazolam IV, n=1291).

Vasovagal incidents during application of IA occurred in 314 patients — 15.0% (217) without premedication vs 7.5% (97) with premedication, P<0.0001 — and premedication did not prolong readiness for discharge (mepivacaine P=0.5886; chloroprocaine P=0.1555; prilocaine actually earlier, P=0.0002).

Premedication roughly halves vasovagal reactions without delaying discharge in intrathecal anaesthesia, but this is a non-randomised, non-dental, abstract-only cohort — transferring it to the dental chair is extrapolation.

> [!warning] Scope — this is not dental evidence
> The cohort is **outpatient intrathecal (spinal) anaesthesia**, where the dural puncture and sympathetic block are themselves strong vagal triggers. It is cited in the dental literature as background for "premedicate the anxious patient", but nothing in this paper measured a dental procedure. Use it as a pharmacological *hypothesis*, not as chairside evidence.

## Summary

This retrospective analysis of anaesthesia records from a single German university centre pooled all outpatients who underwent surgery under low-dose intrathecal anaesthesia (IA) between January 2008 and June 2017 (n=2747). Patients were categorised by whether they had received anxiolytic premedication — 1291 received 1–2 mg midazolam intravenously — and the outcomes were (a) vasovagal reactions documented during application of IA, defined as a decrease in blood pressure and/or heart rate with a need for cardiovascular activating medication, and (b) time from intrathecal injection until readiness for discharge home. Vasovagal incidents occurred in 314 patients: 217 of the no-premedication patients (15.0%) versus 97 of the premedicated patients (7.5%), P<0.0001. Time to readiness for discharge was not prolonged (mepivacaine P=0.5886; chloroprocaine P=0.1555) and was significantly shorter in the prilocaine subgroup (P=0.0002). The authors conclude that anxiolytic premedication reduces vasovagal reactions during IA without delaying discharge — a finding that directly rebuts the day-surgery fear that premedication slows turnover. For dentistry the value is indirect: it supplies the only large incidence pair (15.0% → 7.5%) behind the common recommendation to premedicate anxious patients, but the population, the trigger and the drug protocol are all non-dental.

## Key Contributions

- Provides a hard incidence pair for premedication prophylaxis of vasovagal reactions — **15.0% → 7.5%, P<0.0001** — in a 2747-patient cohort, the kind of number narrative reviews usually only assert.
- Separates the *efficacy* question from the *throughput* question: premedication did not delay home readiness, removing the main operational objection to using it in day surgery.
- Shows the discharge-time effect may be local-anaesthetic dependent (prilocaine subgroup earlier, P=0.0002) rather than a uniform "premedication speeds recovery" claim.

## Methodology

- **Design**: retrospective chart review (non-randomised exposure — premedication given at clinical discretion).
- **Setting**: outpatient surgery under low-dose intrathecal anaesthesia, University Medical Centre Mannheim, Germany.
- **Period**: January 2008 – June 2017.
- **n**: 2747 records; 1291 premedicated (1–2 mg midazolam IV).
- **Primary outcomes**: incidence of vasovagal reaction during IA application (BP and/or HR decrease with need for cardiovascular activating medication); time from intrathecal injection of local anaesthetic to readiness for discharge.
- **Subgroups**: mepivacaine, chloroprocaine, prilocaine.
- **Evidence level note**: `retrospective` — the abstract explicitly states "The aim of this retrospective analysis was…", so the parent-supplied default was confirmed, not adjusted.

## Dental applicability — what can and cannot be carried over

| Element | This study | Dental chair |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Dural puncture + sympathetic block during IA | Psychogenic / trigeminocardiac / pain-driven |
| Drug | Midazolam 1–2 mg IV | Rarely IV midazolam in a general dental practice; N₂O-O₂ or oral benzodiazepine is the realistic analogue |
| Population | Outpatient surgery, all ages/comorbidities of a surgical list | Generally healthier, ambulatory, self-transported |
| Setting for discharge | Anaesthesia recovery unit with formal criteria | Walk-out from the operatory |
| Endpoint | Vasovagal reaction *during needle/procedure* | Syncope in the chair, usually at injection or on sitting up |

Directionally consistent with the review literature ([[drug/systemic-disease/malave-2022-vasovagal-reactions-during-interventional-pain]] recommends anticholinergic/fluid prophylaxis while noting premedication evidence is procedure-specific), but the size of effect should not be quoted for dentistry.
