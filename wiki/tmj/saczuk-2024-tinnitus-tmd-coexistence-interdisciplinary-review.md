## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (SANRA-compliant, 77 publications spanning 2009–2024, screened from 30,621 initial hits) synthesizing the anatomical, physiological, and clinical evidence linking somatosensory tinnitus (ST) and temporomandibular disorders (TMD).

Tinnitus prevalence in TMD patients ranges 2–59% and is ~8x more common than in the general population; among tinnitus patients, TMJ disorders occur at 19% frequency overall, rising to 36% in high-severity tinnitus; the review proposes a concrete multidisciplinary diagnostic algorithm and reports 43–86% tinnitus improvement after TMD therapy.

Clinicians should consider TMD/stomatognathic dysfunction as an occult cause when otolaryngological/audiological workup for tinnitus is unremarkable, and route patients through coordinated otolaryngologist–audiologist–neurologist–psychiatrist–dentist–physiotherapist care — though the authors stress that causality between TMD and tinnitus (vs. shared confounders like stress) remains unproven.

## Summary

This 2024 narrative review addresses a long-standing but under-recognized clinical link: the coexistence of tinnitus and temporomandibular disorders (TMD). Tinnitus is traditionally managed by otolaryngology, and its multidimensional etiology (subjective vs. objective, tonal vs. noise-like, pulsing vs. non-pulsing) makes uniformly effective therapy elusive. A subtype — somatosensory (somatic) tinnitus (ST) — arises when input from the temporomandibular or upper-cervical-spine region can trigger or modulate tinnitus, based on anatomical/functional connections between the dorsal cochlear nucleus (DCN) and somatosensory brainstem nuclei. Following SANRA methodology, the authors screened 30,621 initially retrieved articles down to 77 qualifying publications (2009–2024, Polish and English, opinion and research papers) covering ST/TMD etiology, epidemiology, diagnostic criteria, and treatment outcomes. The review's central clinical contribution is a proposed multidisciplinary diagnostic algorithm for tinnitus patients in whom standard otological workup is unrevealing, positioning the dentist and physiotherapist as necessary partners alongside otolaryngology, audiology, neurology, and psychiatry.

## Key Contributions

- **The multidisciplinary diagnostic algorithm** (the paper's most clinically actionable output): when a patient presents with audiovestibular symptoms (ear pain, tinnitus, dizziness, sudden hearing impairment) and standard otolaryngological examination plus imaging fails to identify a cause, evaluation should extend to the stomatognathic system/TMJ (dentist), cervical spine and musculoskeletal structures (physiotherapist), and psychological/psychiatric status (given stress's shared etiological role in both TMD and tinnitus). Presence of ST-suggestive features — tinnitus modulation by voluntary head/jaw/neck/eye movement or myofascial trigger-point pressure, concurrent cervical/head/shoulder pain, trauma-preceded onset, bilaterally symmetric audiometry with unilateral/asymmetric tinnitus, coexisting TMD, and/or bruxism/clenching/dental disease — routes the patient into coordinated interdisciplinary care (otolaryngologist, audiologist, neurologist, psychiatrist, dentist, physiotherapist). Discordant laterality (unilateral TMD and unilateral tinnitus on **opposite** sides) argues against a TMD-tinnitus link in that individual, whereas ipsilateral concordance supports it.
- **Six-theory mechanistic framework** unifying why TMD/masticatory-system dysfunction can produce or modulate tinnitus: (1) joint phylogenetic/ontogenetic development — medial pterygoid, tensor veli palatini, and tensor tympani muscles share first-pharyngeal-arch origin; (2) topographical proximity — discomalleolar ligament and condylar pressure on the auriculotemporal nerve, or posterior condylar displacement injuring the chorda tympani and immobilizing the stapes; (3) joint sensory innervation — trigeminal (V), facial (VII), glossopharyngeal (IX), vagus (X), C2–C3, and sympathetic carotid-plexus fibers link SS and ear regions; (4) joint motor innervation — the trigeminal mandibular branch co-innervates the masseter and tensor tympani muscles; (5) neuromodulation/cross-modal plasticity — the dorsal cochlear nucleus acts as a multi-sensory (auditory + somatosensory) integration site, so abnormal TMJ/cervical input raises DCN spontaneous firing and disturbs neuronal synchrony; (6) stress — a shared predisposing/aggravating factor for both TMD and tinnitus via muscle hyperexcitability and neurotransmitter imbalance.
- **Prevalence and outcome benchmarks** consolidating a fragmented literature into single reference figures clinicians can cite (see Results).
- **TMD subclassification applied to the tinnitus workup**: TMJ disorder (TMJD, acoustic/mobility/pain/degenerative signs), myofascial pain disorder (MPD), and cervical spine dysfunction (CSD) — mapped onto DC/TMD's pain-related vs. intra-articular vs. complex-symptom categories.

## Methodology

- Narrative review complying with SANRA (Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles).
- Databases: PubMed, Google Scholar, ClinicalKey; keyword combinations (tinnitus AND TMD/stress/etiology/diagnosis; TMD AND diagnosis/stress/etiology; somatosensory tinnitus AND TMD/etiology/diagnosis; temporomandibular dysfunction; stress; bruxism), extended via reference-list snowballing and selected books.
- Screening funnel: 30,621 initially retrieved → 2,356 after de-duplication → 106 assessed for eligibility → 50 excluded (not relevant) → **77 publications** included (PRISMA flow diagram in source article), years 2009–2024, in Polish and English, mixing opinion and research papers.

## Results

- **Tinnitus prevalence (general population)**: 5.1–42.7%, most commonly cited as 10–15%; ~1–2% experience significant lifestyle/emotional detriment.
- **Tinnitus prevalence in TMD patients**: 2–59%; reported ~8x more common than in the general population.
- **TMJ-disorder frequency in tinnitus patients**: 19% overall; 36% in patients with high tinnitus severity.
- **Concomitant symptom rates** (TMD + oto-vestibular disorder patients): ear pain ~50%, tinnitus 45.5% (one study) vs. 52.1% (Porto de Toledo et al.); masticatory muscle pain most common comorbid TMD finding among tinnitus patients, acoustic TMJ symptoms/muscle fatigue in ~33%.
- **Laterality**: ipsilateral unilateral-TMD/unilateral-otologic-symptom localization significantly more common than contralateral; tinnitus patients 3x more likely to report myofascial pain than non-tinnitus patients; tinnitus-laterality-to-body-pain-side correlation ≈54%.
- **Treatment outcomes**: multimodal conservative therapy (TMJ/cervical/masticatory exercises, manual therapy, physical therapy, acrylic splint, patient education) is most effective; cervico-mandibular manual therapy improved physical (mandibular ROM), psychological (depressive symptoms), and tinnitus/TMD-impairment measures, with greater masticatory-muscle pressure-pain-threshold gains than comparator; **tinnitus resolution or improvement in 43–86% of cases after TMD therapy**.
- **Diagnostic algorithm steps** (see Key Contributions for full description): otolaryngology/audiology + imaging first → if unrevealing, add stomatognathic (dentist), musculoskeletal/cervical (physiotherapist), psychological (psychiatrist) evaluation → ST-suggestive criteria present → interdisciplinary team management; discordant TMD/tinnitus laterality argues against a link.
- **Open question**: the authors explicitly conclude it remains unclear whether TMD and tinnitus coexist independently, are causally related, or share a common upstream driver (e.g., stress) — this is stated as the key unresolved question for future research.
