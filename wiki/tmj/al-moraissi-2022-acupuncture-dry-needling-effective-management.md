## Three-line Summary

Network meta-analysis of RCTs (searched to September 2019) comparing acupuncture, dry needling, and active or inactive placebo for masticatory muscle pain (TMD-M); outcomes were pain intensity, pressure pain threshold (PPT), and maximum mouth opening (MMO).

Neither acupuncture nor dry needling differed from active or inactive placebo for pain intensity or PPT (p > .05); dry needling increased MMO versus placebo (very low-quality evidence).

The authors conclude that the placebo effect accounts for most of the treatment effect in TMD-M rather than a specific therapeutic effect of needling; numbers of trials and pooled effect sizes are not available from the abstract.

## 세줄요약

무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT)의 네트워크 메타분석 (Network Meta-Analysis, NMA, 2019년 9월까지 검색): 저작근 통증 (masticatory muscle pain, TMD-M)에서 침술 (acupuncture)·건침 (dry needling)을 능동·비활성 위약과 비교; 결과변수는 통증 강도, 압통역치 (Pressure Pain Threshold, PPT), 최대 개구량 (Maximum Mouth Opening, MMO).

침술·건침 모두 통증 강도와 PPT에서 능동·비활성 위약과 차이 없음 (p > .05); 건침만 위약 대비 MMO 증가 (근거 질 매우 낮음).

저자들은 치료효과의 대부분이 침 자체의 특이 효과가 아닌 위약 효과 (placebo effect)로 설명된다고 결론; 포함 시험 수와 합성 효과크기는 초록에서 확인 불가.

## Summary

Source: PubMed, PMID 36314254 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/joor.13382)); J Oral Rehabil 2022;50(1):87-97. This frequentist network meta-analysis (NMA) of randomised trials compared acupuncture, dry needling, and inactive or active placebo in patients with masticatory muscle pain (TMD-M). Both the NMA and direct pairwise meta-analysis found no difference between either needling therapy and placebo for pain intensity or pressure pain threshold (p > .05). Dry needling produced a significant increase in maximum mouth opening versus placebo, rated very low-quality evidence. The authors infer that the placebo effect accounts for most of the treatment effect in TMD-M. This record is abstract-level; trial counts, sample sizes, and pooled mean differences were not retrieved.

## Key Contributions

- Placebo-anchored NMA of acupuncture and dry needling for masticatory muscle pain, using both active and inactive placebo comparators.
- No pain-intensity or PPT advantage of needling over placebo.
- Short-term MMO gain with dry needling versus placebo (very low-quality evidence).

## Methodology

- RCTs published until September 2019 comparing dry needling, acupuncture, and inactive or active placebo in TMD-M.
- Outcomes: post-treatment pain intensity, PPT, MMO.
- Risk of bias rated with the Cochrane tool; mean differences pooled in a frequentist NMA (STATA), plus direct pairwise meta-analysis.

## Results

| Outcome | Acupuncture / dry needling vs placebo (active or inactive) |
|---|---|
| Pain intensity | No difference (p > .05) |
| Pressure pain threshold | No difference (p > .05) |
| Maximum mouth opening | Dry needling significantly higher than placebo (very low-quality evidence) |

Numeric estimates are not reported in the abstract. Clinical reading: the pain benefit previously attributed to needling for masticatory muscle pain may be largely non-specific.
