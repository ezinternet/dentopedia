## Three-line Summary

A 72-year-old woman with 3 years of bilateral preauricular pain experienced near-complete left hearing loss exclusively during mandibular occlusion, confirmed on examination by dynamic external auditory canal (EAC) narrowing with jaw closure. MRI revealed a left posterior TMJ ganglion cyst (~7 × 10 mm) adherent to retrodiscal tissues; histopathology after preauricular excision confirmed fibrous wall without synovial lining, distinguishing it from a synovial cyst. At 2-year follow-up, hearing normalized and preauricular pain resolved with no recurrence, supporting open surgical excision as definitive treatment.

## Summary

Akbar et al. (2026) describe a case that illustrates how a posterior TMJ mass can manifest purely as an otologic complaint, with the diagnostic clue hidden in the mandibular position-dependence of symptoms. The report is notable for three contributions: (1) a detailed mechanical explanation of EAC obstruction via retrodiscal cyst displacement during jaw closure; (2) histopathologic confirmation of the ganglion cyst subtype in a location where synovial cysts are also common; and (3) a literature synthesis on TMJ periarticular cysts with otologic manifestations, including the caveat that immunohistochemistry-based series suggest synovial cysts may be more prevalent than ganglion cysts in this joint.

## Key Contributions

1. **Position-dependent otologic mechanism**: Closed-mouth posterior condylar translation creates retrodiscal tissue tension that anteriorly displaces the cyst against the EAC wall — a functionally novel pathomechanism.
2. **Dynamic EAC examination**: Bedside observation of EAC caliber change with mandibular position is the key diagnostic maneuver; not described in prior case series.
3. **Histopathologic subtyping**: Ganglion cyst confirmed by absence of synovial lining (fibrous wall only, gelatinous fluid); critical distinction from synovial cyst requires histopathology — MRI insufficient.
4. **Surgical outcome data**: Complete excision via preauricular approach with facial nerve monitoring; 2-year recurrence-free, full hearing recovery.
5. **Literature context**: Largest pathologic review suggests periarticular TMJ cysts may more frequently be synovial when IHC is applied — clinicians should not assume ganglion cyst subtype preoperatively.

## Case Description

| 2026 (presentation) | Near-complete left hearing loss on mandibular occlusion, reversing on opening; dynamic EAC narrowing confirmed |

### Patient Details

- **Age/sex**: 72-year-old woman
- **PMH**: Osteoarthritis, fibromyalgia, depression
- **Surgical history**: Rhytidectomy 2008 (provided surgical access); Mohs surgery left conchal region 2016 (altered local anatomy)
- **Max opening**: 52 mm with mild discomfort; no clicking or crepitus; cranial nerves V and VII intact

### Pathomechanism of EAC Obstruction

The posterior TMJ is anatomically contiguous with the anterior EAC wall. When a cystic mass arises in the retrodiscal/posterior compartment, mandibular closure drives the condyle posteriorly via the bilaminar zone, tensioning retrodiscal attachments. This posterior displacement translates the cyst anteriorly against the EAC wall, producing functional narrowing — and hearing loss — that resolves immediately on mouth opening as the condyle moves anteriorly.

## Discussion Highlights

**Ganglion vs. synovial cyst**: The distinction is clinically relevant because synovial cysts may communicate with the joint space, influencing surgical approach. Histopathologically, ganglion cysts have a fibrous wall without synovial lining; synovial cysts are lined by synovial cells. MRI cannot reliably distinguish the two. The largest pathologic review of TMJ periarticular cysts reports that synovial cysts may predominate when IHC is used — the published literature likely underreports synovial cysts due to incomplete histopathologic workup.

**Pathogenesis**: Prevailing theory is myxoid degeneration of periarticular collagenous connective tissue driven by chronic mechanical stress, repetitive microtrauma, or degenerative joint change. Concurrent anterior disc displacement with reduction (ADDwR) in this case supports a degenerative context.

**Differential diagnosis for posterior TMJ mass with otologic symptoms**:
- TMJ: synovial cyst, ganglion cyst, inflammatory pseudocyst, synovial chondromatosis, neoplasm (rare)
- Parotid: parotid tail mass, Warthin tumor
- EAC: exostoses, osteoma, cholesteatoma, keratosis obturans

**Treatment**: Open excision via preauricular approach is standard. Complete excision is associated with favorable outcomes; incomplete excision or persistent joint pathology increases recurrence risk. Arthroscopic approaches have been described but open excision offers better access for posterior adherent masses.

## Surgical Technique

- **Approach**: Preauricular incision through prior rhytidectomy scar (minimized additional morbidity)
- **Safety**: Continuous facial nerve (CN VII) monitoring throughout
- **Dissection**: Cyst adherent to posterior articular disc and retrodiscal tissues; required en-bloc excision of cyst with small disc portion
- **EAC**: Anterior EAC wall preserved; no cartilage resection required
- **Closure**: Standard layered closure; no drain reported
