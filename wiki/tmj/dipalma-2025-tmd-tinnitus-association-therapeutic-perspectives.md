## Three-line Summary

PRISMA-compliant, PROSPERO-registered (CRD 641352) systematic review synthesizing a decade (2014–2024) of PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science literature (976 records screened → 12 studies included) on the association between temporomandibular disorders (TMDs) and subjective tinnitus in adults.

A consistent, moderate-to-strong association was found (e.g., painful TMD vs. tinnitus OR = 7.3, 95% CI 3.50–15.39; TMD present in 97.8% of one somatosensory-tinnitus cohort), attributed to somatosensory/neuroplastic cross-modal mechanisms at the dorsal cochlear nucleus, and TMD-directed therapy (manual therapy, education, stabilization splints) reduced tinnitus-related distress in several interventional studies.

Evidence remains inconsistent and methodologically limited (small samples, weak/absent control groups, cross-sectional designs, diagnostic heterogeneity), so the review recommends standardized DC/TMD-based criteria and larger controlled trials before firm causal or treatment-protocol conclusions can be drawn.

## Summary

This systematic review addresses a long-standing clinical observation — that patients with temporomandibular disorders (TMDs) frequently report tinnitus — by systematically consolidating the past decade of clinical/observational literature under a PRISMA-compliant, PROSPERO-registered protocol (CRD 641352). Of 976 records identified across PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science (Oct 2014–Oct 2024), 12 studies (prevalence/correlation studies plus interventional trials) were included after title/abstract and full-text screening, with risk of bias assessed via the Cochrane RoB tool (RCTs) and ROBINS-I (non-randomized studies). The review frames the TMD-tinnitus link through a somatosensory-tinnitus mechanistic model — cross-modal convergence of auditory and somatosensory afferents at the dorsal cochlear nucleus — and reviews both association evidence and therapeutic (manual therapy, splint, education) outcomes, concluding that the relationship is real but the evidence base remains heterogeneous and underpowered.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates 12 studies (7 prevalence/correlation studies, 5 interventional/therapeutic studies) into the first PRISMA-compliant systematic synthesis of the TMD-tinnitus literature covering the most recent decade.
- Articulates and applies the somatosensory-tinnitus mechanistic framework (dorsal cochlear nucleus cross-modal convergence) as the unifying explanation across the reviewed studies.
- Applies formal risk-of-bias appraisal (Cochrane RoB / ROBINS-I across 7 domains) to a literature base that has historically been synthesized only narratively.
- Surveys both diagnostic/associative evidence (odds ratios, prevalence rates, anatomical correlates like petrotympanic fissure position) and therapeutic evidence (manual therapy, education, splints), giving a combined "is it associated" + "can treating TMD help" picture.
- Makes explicit, actionable methodological recommendations (standardized DC/TMD criteria, objective audiometry, larger RCTs with control arms) for the field going forward.

## Methodology

- PRISMA-compliant systematic review; PROSPERO-registered (CRD 641352).
- Databases: PubMed (n=675), Scopus (n=251), Web of Science (n=50) — 976 records total; search terms "tinnitus" AND "TMJ".
- Timeframe: October 2014 – October 2024 (most recent decade of literature).
- Inclusion: human studies, open access, English language, adult populations.
- Exclusion: case reports, animal studies, in vitro studies, reviews, off-topic articles, pediatric trials; criteria structured under PICO.
- Screening: 976 → 211 duplicates removed → 765 title/abstract screened → 642 excluded → full-text retrieval attempted (all retrieved) → 111 excluded at full-text stage → **12 studies included** for qualitative synthesis. (The source article's reported intermediate counts contain an internal inconsistency in the PRISMA flow numbers; the final included count of 12 is unambiguous and consistent throughout Results/Discussion.)
- Quality appraisal: Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool (RCTs) and ROBINS-I (non-randomized studies) across 7 bias domains — most studies "low risk" or "some concerns," with higher risk concentrated in confounding (D1) and selection bias (D3); outcome measurement (D6) generally well-handled.

## Results

**Association / mechanistic evidence (7 studies):**
- Fernandes et al. (2014, n=261 women): painful TMD strongly associated with tinnitus (OR = 7.3, 95% CI 3.50–15.39, p<0.001); sleep bruxism (SB) more weakly associated (OR = 1.9, p=0.0163); painful TMD without SB still strongly associated (OR = 6.7); painful TMD + SB → more severe tinnitus (OR = 7.0).
- Maciejewska-Szaniec et al. (2017, n=246 TMD patients): 36.18% had otologic symptoms; tinnitus in 14.63% overall; sound hypersensitivity in 10.57%.
- Peleg et al. (2022, n=51 tinnitus patients): bruxism in 66.7%, TMD in 27.5%, 92.8% of TMD patients also had bruxism; tinnitus+TMD+bruxism associated with highest anxiety; tinnitus severity correlated with depression.
- Manfredini et al. (n=250 TMD patients): 30.4% reported tinnitus, higher with masseter myalgia and TMJ osteoarthritis.
- Didier et al. (n=97 somatosensory-tinnitus / 50 TMD patients): TMD diagnosed in 97.8% of tinnitus patients studied.
- Ralli et al. (n=226 chronic tinnitus patients): somatic modulation history associated with TMD presence, but no direct TMD–tinnitus etiologic correlation found.
- Kijak et al. (n=331, mostly women): petrotympanic fissure position and mandibular condyle dislocation significantly correlated with tinnitus; posterior/intracranial condyle positions more common in tinnitus patients — proposed anatomical predisposing factor.

**Mechanism:** Somatosensory tinnitus (modulated by neck/head/jaw movement or muscle contraction, present in ~two-thirds of subjective-tinnitus patients) is explained by cross-modal interaction between auditory and somatosensory pathways converging at the dorsal cochlear nucleus, giving an anatomical/functional basis for TMD-related tinnitus modulation.

**Therapeutic evidence (5 studies):**
- Mahmoudian et al. (2023, n=6): after TMD therapy, SMTDI scores fell from 34.50±1.3 to 24.37±8.6 (p=0.0001); VAS tinnitus intensity/annoyance/awareness significantly improved (p<0.05); audiometric thresholds unchanged; bruxism resolved in 3/6.
- Plaza-Manzano et al. (2020, n=61, secondary RCT analysis): higher baseline tinnitus severity and lower baseline temporalis pressure pain thresholds were the strongest predictors of treatment response (explaining 10.5–45% of outcome variance) after exercise/education ± cervico-mandibular manual therapy.
- De la Serna et al. (multicenter RCT): exercise+education+manual therapy (EEMT) outperformed exercise+education alone on pain, tinnitus handicap, TMD disability, quality of life, depression, pain sensitivity, and mandibular range of motion.
- Michiels et al. (RCT, immediate- vs. delayed-start): conservative TMD treatment (jaw-use education, relaxation, stabilization splints) produced significant, durable reduction in tinnitus-related distress.
- Arruda de Souza Alcarás et al. (2023, n=19): mean THI score 37.8 (moderate QoL impact); tinnitus duration/frequency correlated with greater impact.

**Bottom line:** consistent moderate-to-strong TMD–tinnitus association across heterogeneous study designs, with converging mechanistic (somatosensory/neuroplastic) and therapeutic (TMD treatment improves tinnitus outcomes) evidence, but methodological limitations prevent the review from establishing definitive causality or a standardized treatment protocol.
