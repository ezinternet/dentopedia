## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies (MEDLINE and Scopus, 2004–2024) estimating global prevalence of orofacial pain by ICOP-type origin.

Pooled prevalence: myofascial pain 20.60% (95% CI 9.71–38.50; 5 studies), myofascial plus TMJ pain 11.95% (8.85–15.96; 31 studies, 522,056 observations), TMJ pain alone 9.51% (6.01–14.73; 13 studies), dentoalveolar 27.46% (22.83–32.63; 66 studies), cranial nerve 7.98% (3.28–18.18; 2 studies).

Substantial methodological heterogeneity and inability to apply ICOP directly to included studies mean estimates need cautious interpretation.

## 세줄요약

관찰연구 대상 SR+MA (MEDLINE, Scopus, 2004–2024)로 구안면통증 국제분류 (International Classification of Orofacial Pain, ICOP) 기준 기원별 세계 유병률을 추정.

통합 유병률: 근막 구안면통증 20.60%, 근막통+측두하악관절 (Temporomandibular Joint, TMJ) 통증 11.95% (31편, 522,056건), TMJ 통증 단독 9.51%, 치조·관련구조 27.46%, 뇌신경 7.98%.

연구 간 방법론적 이질성이 크고 ICOP을 직접 적용하지 못해 해석에 주의 필요; 근막통 (5편)·뇌신경 (2편) 추정치는 근거 편수가 적어 신뢰구간이 넓음.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.17219/dmp/214271)), this meta-analysis (Dent Med Probl 2026) pooled observational studies published 2004–2024 to estimate the global prevalence of orofacial pain categories following the 2020 International Classification of Orofacial Pain (ICOP). The authors report that the available studies did not permit direct application of ICOP categories, so pooled groupings are approximate. Estimated prevalence was about 21% for myofascial orofacial pain, 12% for myofascial pain combined with TMJ pain, 10% for TMJ pain alone, 27% for dentoalveolar-origin pain and 8% for cranial-nerve pain. Only the abstract was available; methods detail (risk-of-bias tool, pooling model) is not retrieved.

## Key Contributions

- Pooled global prevalence across five orofacial pain categories in one analysis.
- Documents that ICOP cannot yet be applied directly to the epidemiologic literature; calls for standardized criteria and reporting guidelines.
- Relative to [[tmj/valesan-2021-tmd-prevalence-sr-ma]] (TMD signs/symptoms, painful TMD ~11–13%, disc displacement), this paper separates myofascial from TMJ pain and adds dentoalveolar and cranial-nerve categories; it is not restricted to RDC/TMD or DC/TMD-based population studies.

## Methodology

Systematic search of MEDLINE and Scopus; observational studies 2004–2024; pooled prevalence with 95% CI. Further methods not retrieved (abstract-only).
