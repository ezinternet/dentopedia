## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (JARO 2023) on somatosensory tinnitus (ST) — tinnitus influenced by somatosensory input from the cervical spine and/or temporomandibular (TMD) region — summarizing recent diagnostic and treatment developments.

A 4-criterion clinical decision tree diagnoses ST with 82.2% accuracy (82.5% sensitivity, 79% specificity); musculoskeletal physical therapy directed at the cervical spine, temporomandibular area, or both (counseling + exercise + manual techniques) reduces tinnitus severity/loudness in most patients, with rare total remission.

## Summary

This narrative review updates the diagnosis and treatment of somatosensory tinnitus (ST), a tinnitus subtype in which changes in somatosensory input from the head-neck area (cervical spine and/or temporomandibular region) influence the patient's tinnitus. Because ST commonly co-occurs with other influencing factors, isolating a clear somatosensory contribution in an individual patient is challenging. The review highlights a validated 4-criterion clinical decision tree that diagnoses ST with 82.2% accuracy, 82.5% sensitivity, and 79% specificity. Once diagnosed, patients are treated with musculoskeletal physical therapy directed at cervical spine dysfunction, temporomandibular disorders, or both, combining counseling, exercises, and manual techniques to restore normal cervical spine and temporomandibular function. Other proposed techniques exist but require confirmation in larger RCTs. Outcomes are generally a decrease in tinnitus severity or loudness, with total remission achieved only rarely.

**Limitation**: only the structured abstract was retrievable (PMC full-text body returned empty); the underlying primary studies behind the decision tree and the treatment evidence base are not detailed here.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates a 4-criterion decision tree for diagnosing somatosensory tinnitus (ST), with reported accuracy 82.2%, sensitivity 82.5%, specificity 79%.
- Frames first-line ST treatment as musculoskeletal physical therapy (counseling + exercise + manual technique) directed at the cervical spine and/or temporomandibular area.
- Flags that alternative treatment techniques need further validation in larger randomized controlled trials before adoption.

## Methodology

## Results

- Diagnostic decision tree (4 clinical criteria): **82.2% accuracy**, **82.5% sensitivity**, **79% specificity** for identifying somatosensory tinnitus.
- Musculoskeletal physical therapy (cervical spine-directed, temporomandibular-directed, or combined): most patients show decreased tinnitus severity/loudness; total remission occurs in rare cases.
- Techniques beyond musculoskeletal physical therapy are suggested in the literature but remain under-validated, pending larger RCTs.
