## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of somatosensory (somatic) tinnitus — tinnitus perceptibly modulated by temporomandibular joint (TMJ), head/neck, or eye-movement maneuvers via auditory–somatosensory convergence in the cochlear nucleus, driven by neuroplastic reorganization after deafferentation.

TMJ maneuvers (clenching, maximal opening, jaw protrusion) modulate tinnitus loudness in up to 94% of a large clinical cohort, TMJ disorders raise tinnitus risk/prevalence 2- to 8-fold across epidemiological studies, and treatment of the underlying TMJ disorder resolves or improves tinnitus in an average of 69% of patients pooled across 16 studies (1964–2014).

Somatic screening (history + a standardized somatic maneuver battery) is essential to select tinnitus patients — particularly those with normal hearing — who are likely to benefit from multidisciplinary somatic/dental management rather than otologic treatment alone.

## 세줄요약

체성감각 이명 (Somatosensory Tinnitus, ST) — 턱관절(Temporomandibular Joint, TMJ)·두경부·안구운동 조작으로 변조되는 이명 — 을 다룬 서술적 리뷰로, 청각-체성감각 신호가 와우핵(Cochlear Nucleus, CN)에서 수렴하고 구심로 차단 후 신경가소성 (Neuroplasticity)에 의해 재조직화되는 것을 기전으로 제시한다.

TMJ 조작(악물기·최대개구·하악 전방이동)은 대규모 임상 코호트에서 최대 94%까지 이명 크기를 변조시켰고, TMJ 장애는 역학연구에서 이명 위험/유병률을 2~8배 높였으며, TMJ 장애 치료 후 16개 연구(1964~2014) 종합 평균 69%에서 이명이 호전 또는 소실되었다.

체성 선별검사(병력 확인 + 표준화된 체성 조작 검사)는 특히 정상 청력을 가진 이명 환자 중 이비인후과적 단독 치료보다 다학제적 체성/치과적 접근이 필요한 환자를 가려내는 데 필수적이다.

## Summary

This narrative review synthesizes anatomical, physiological, and clinical evidence on somatosensory tinnitus — a subtype of tinnitus that can be evoked or modulated by inputs from the somatosensory, somatomotor, and visual-motor systems (TMJ, head/neck, eyes, limbs) rather than by the auditory system alone. The pathophysiologic model centers on convergence of trigeminal/dorsal-column somatosensory afferents and auditory afferents at the dorsal and ventral cochlear nucleus (DCN/VCN). After cochlear damage/deafferentation, animal studies show upregulated glutamatergic (VGLUT2+) somatosensory input and increased spontaneous firing rates in DCN/VCN neurons — a neuroplastic shift proposed as the origin of somatically modulated tinnitus in humans.

Clinically, somatic modulation of tinnitus loudness/pitch has been reported in roughly one-third to nearly nine-tenths of tested patients across studies, with the TMJ being the single most frequent modulating region, followed by head/neck maneuvers, then eye movements (notably gaze-evoked tinnitus after acoustic neuroma surgery). TMJ disorders are strongly and consistently associated with tinnitus across multiple epidemiological cohorts, and TMJ-directed treatment (splints, physiotherapy, cervical manipulation) improves or resolves tinnitus in a majority of patients across pooled historical studies. The authors argue for routine somatic screening in tinnitus patients, particularly those with normal hearing, to identify candidates for multidisciplinary somatic management.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates the neuroanatomical/electrophysiological rationale (auditory–somatosensory convergence at the CN) for why non-auditory somatic maneuvers can modulate tinnitus.
- Reviews and compares prevalence rates of somatic tinnitus modulation across at least 9 independent clinical cohorts.
- Establishes TMJ maneuvers as the single most reliably tinnitus-modulating somatic region (94.31% loudness increase in the authors' 310-patient cohort).
- Summarizes multiple epidemiological studies quantifying the TMJ-disorder–tinnitus association (risk ratios 2.4–8×, prevalence ~30% in TMD patients).
- Pools historical treatment-outcome data (16 studies, 1964–2014) showing an average 69% improvement/resolution of tinnitus after TMJ disorder treatment.
- Proposes a practical somatic-screening framework (history + 19-maneuver examination battery) for identifying tinnitus patients suited to multidisciplinary somatic management.

## Methodology

Narrative (non-systematic) review — no PRISMA methodology, formal search strategy, or risk-of-bias/quality appraisal is reported. Evidence synthesized includes:

- Animal electrophysiology (rodent DCN/VCN neuron recordings after trigeminal ganglion stimulation and cochlear deafferentation).
- Human tractography/fMRI evidence of auditory–somatosensory cortical connections (A1–SII).
- Multiple independent clinical cohorts testing tinnitus modulation with standardized somatic maneuvers: TMJ maneuvers (clenching teeth, maximal mouth opening, jaw protrusion, pressure on TMJ), head/neck maneuvers (pressure on occiput/temple/mandible, head rotation, muscle contraction), and eye-gaze maneuvers.
- The authors' own prior 310-patient cohort study (normal hearing, no psychiatric comorbidity, positive TMJ/head-neck history and/or positive tinnitus modulation), which used a 19-maneuver somatic examination protocol to systematically test TMJ and head/neck modulation.
- Epidemiological cross-sectional and prospective cohort studies linking TMJ disorder diagnosis to subsequent tinnitus incidence/prevalence.
- Treatment-outcome literature (stabilization splints, physiotherapy, cognitive therapy, cervical manipulation, myofascial trigger-point injection, laser therapy, TENS, magnet therapy) assessing tinnitus change after TMJ-directed therapy.

## Results

**Somatic modulation prevalence** (test-dependent, varies widely): reported figures across cited studies include 33% (Rubinstein et al.), 57.1–85% (various), 65.3% (Sanchez et al., n=121), 68% with head/neck maneuvers rising to 80% with TMJ maneuvers added (Levine, n=70), and self-reported prevalence of 16% in a large UK cohort (Ward et al.), where somatic tinnitus was predicted by age, pulsatility, loudness, and TMJ complaints.

**TMJ as the dominant modulating region**: in the authors' 310-patient cohort, TMJ maneuvers (clenching, maximal opening, protrusion) increased tinnitus loudness in 94.31% of cases (only 5.69% decreased); head/neck maneuvers showed a more mixed pattern (59.1% increase, 40.9% decrease). Eye-gaze modulation was common specifically after acoustic neuroma surgery (up to 99% loudness increase with lateral gaze in that subgroup), but rarer in the general tinnitus population.

**TMJ disorder–tinnitus epidemiological association**: Bernhardt et al. found a risk ratio of 2.4 (95% CI 1.6–3.7) for developing tinnitus over 5 years in patients with TMJ palpation pain; Buergers et al. found tinnitus incidence 36.6% vs 4.4% (>8-fold) in TMD vs non-TMD patients; Lee et al. found a 3.22-fold higher 3-year tinnitus risk; Manfredini et al. reported ~30% tinnitus prevalence in TMD patients; Vielsmeier et al. (n=1204) found concurrent TMJ+tinnitus patients (22%) were significantly younger and more often female.

**Treatment outcomes**: Tullberg and Ernberg found 43% of TMJ-treated patients had decreased tinnitus at 2 years vs 12% of controls; Buergers et al. found 44% improvement with combined occlusal stabilization + physiotherapy. Across 16 pooled studies spanning 1964–2014, an average 69% of patients reported improvement or complete resolution of tinnitus after TMJ disorder treatment, while 32% reported no change.

**Within-patient correlation**: the authors' cohort found a significant within-subject association between a positive somatic (TMJ/head-neck) history and positive somatic modulation in the same anatomical region — 76.2% concordance for TMJ in unilateral tinnitus and 56.0% for TMJ+head/neck in bilateral tinnitus (p<0.001), supporting a causal/mechanistic (not incidental) link.
