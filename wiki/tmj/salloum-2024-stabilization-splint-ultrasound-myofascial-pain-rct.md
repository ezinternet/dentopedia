## Three-line Summary

Single-blind 4-arm RCT (n=80) at Damascus University comparing ultrasound therapy, stabilization splint (SS), TheraBite passive motion device, and masticatory muscle exercises for myofascial pain syndrome (MPS) over 4 weeks with 5-month follow-up.

Ultrasound therapy and SS produced the greatest VAS pain reduction at week 4 (VAS ≈1.1 each) and were significantly superior to masticatory muscle exercises (VAS ≈4.7; p=0.013); mandibular mobility improved equivalently across all groups with no significant between-group differences by 5-month follow-up.

For MPS, ultrasound therapy and SS are the most effective short-term pain-relief modalities; masticatory exercises alone provide slower pain relief but achieve comparable functional recovery long-term.

## Summary

This single-blind, 4-arm randomized controlled trial enrolled 80 patients with myofascial pain syndrome (MPS, a TMD subtype) at Damascus University (April–October 2023) and compared four active treatments over 4 weeks:

- **Ultrasound therapy** (n=20): 3 sessions/week × 4 weeks
- **Stabilization splint / SS** (n=20): Full-coverage maxillary acrylic splint, 8 h/night × 4 weeks
- **TheraBite device** (n=20): Passive jaw motion, 10 bites × 10 s hold daily × 4 weeks
- **Masticatory muscle exercises** (n=20): Resistance exercises for vertical, lateral, protrusive jaw movement, 2×/day × 4 weeks

Primary outcomes — VAS pain (0–10), maximum interincisal opening (MIO, mm), right lateral movement (RLM, mm), and left lateral movement (LLM, mm) — were measured at baseline, weeks 1, 2, 4, and at 2 and 5 months post-treatment.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates head-to-head superiority of ultrasound therapy = SS > exercises for MPS pain at 4 weeks
- TheraBite shows intermediate pain relief (VAS ~2.5 at week 4), significantly better than exercises for lateral mandibular movement but not for pain vs. exercises at week 4
- All modalities converge to equivalent mandibular mobility by 5-month follow-up — exercises "catch up" functionally despite being slower for pain relief
- Supports continued use of SS as a gold-standard conservative comparator, matching ultrasound in pain outcomes

## Methodology

- **Design**: Single-blind (assessor-blinded), parallel-group, active-controlled RCT
- **n**: 80 patients (20/arm); mean age 30.1 years; 60% female
- **Randomization**: Simple 1:1:1:1, online software; ISRCTN20833186
- **Analysis**: Kruskal–Wallis with post-hoc pairwise comparisons (non-normal distribution confirmed by Kolmogorov–Smirnov)
- **Sample size**: Powered for small effect size (f=0.37), 80% power, α=0.05

## Results

**VAS pain (mean ± SD)**:

| Timepoint | Ultrasound | SS | TheraBite | Exercises | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 8.7 ± 0.9 | 8.0 ± 0.7 | 8.0 ± 1.1 | 8.4 ± 0.7 | 0.206 |
| Week 1 | 6.1 ± 0.9 | 6.3 ± 0.8 | 6.8 ± 1.1 | 7.4 ± 1.2 | 0.059 |
| Week 2 | 2.6 ± 1.1 | 2.7 ± 1.6 | 3.5 ± 1.4 | 5.5 ± 1.9 | **0.002*** |
| Week 4 | 1.1 ± 1.3 | 1.1 ± 1.0 | 2.5 ± 1.9 | 4.7 ± 1.9 | **0.006*** |
| Month 2 | 0.6 ± 1.1 | 1.1 ± 0.9 | 1.9 ± 2.0 | 2.9 ± 2.5 | 0.085 |
| Month 5 | 0.9 ± 1.4 | 0.9 ± 0.9 | 1.5 ± 2.3 | 2.5 ± 2.5 | 0.383 |

**Key pairwise contrasts (week 4)**:
- Ultrasound vs. Exercises: p=0.012 (US superior)
- SS vs. Exercises: p=0.013 (SS superior)
- Ultrasound vs. TheraBite: p=0.012 (US superior)
- SS vs. TheraBite: p=0.013 (SS superior)
- Ultrasound vs. SS: NS (equivalent)
- TheraBite vs. Exercises: NS for pain at week 4

**Mandibular mobility**: MIO showed no significant between-group differences at any timepoint. LLM differed at weeks 2 and 4 (exercises inferior to US/SS); by month 5, all groups equivalent.
