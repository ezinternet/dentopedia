## Three-line Summary

Systematic review with meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD420251120631; 13 RCTs/experimental studies, 10 magnesium) of electrolyte supplementation for muscle cramps and myalgia in non-TMD populations, with a prespecified, hypothesis-generating extrapolation to TMD myalgia; no trial in TMD patients was found.

Oral magnesium reduced cramp frequency in pregnancy (4 trials, N about 364; pooled RR 1.35, 95% CI 1.05-1.74, P=.02) but not in adult nocturnal/persistent leg cramps (4 trials, N about 396; MD -0.42 cramps/week, 95% CI -1.15 to 0.31, P=.26); calcium and potassium had no eligible trials, sodium-based drinks helped only in exercise and cirrhosis contexts.

The adult null result is a negative signal for systemic magnesium in TMD myalgia; the authors favor testing targeted local magnesium in TMD trials, but all TMD relevance is indirect extrapolation and not clinical evidence.

## 세줄요약

근육 경련·근육통에 대한 전해질 보충 효과를 본 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis, SR+MA; 무작위대조시험 13편, 마그네슘 10편)으로, 측두하악장애 (Temporomandibular Disorder, TMD) 근육통으로의 외삽은 가설 생성 수준이며 TMD 환자 대상 시험은 없었다.

경구 마그네슘은 임신 중 경련 빈도를 줄였으나 (4편, 약 364명; 통합 위험비 1.35, 95% CI 1.05-1.74, P=.02), 성인 야간·지속성 다리 경련에서는 효과가 없었다 (4편, 약 396명; 평균차 -0.42회/주, 95% CI -1.15~0.31, P=.26); 칼슘·칼륨은 적격 시험이 없었고 나트륨 음료는 운동·간경변 상황에서만 효과가 있었다.

성인 경련의 무효 결과는 TMD 근육통에 대한 전신 마그네슘 보충에 불리한 신호이며, 저자들은 국소 마그네슘 투여 시험을 제안하나 TMD 관련성은 모두 간접적 외삽일 뿐 임상 근거가 아니다.

## Summary

This SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD420251120631) asks whether electrolyte supplementation reduces muscle cramps and myalgia and whether that could inform TMD myalgia management. It includes 13 trials (10 magnesium, 3 sodium or multi-electrolyte beverage) and finds no calcium or potassium monotherapy trial. Oral magnesium helped in pregnancy-associated cramps but did not help in adult nocturnal or persistent leg cramps, which the authors regard as the population closer to TMD patients. The TMD conclusion is therefore a hypothesis: local rather than systemic magnesium is proposed for future trials.

## Key Contributions

- No RCT of electrolytes in TMD myofascial pain exists (as of the August 2025 search).
- Pooled null effect of oral magnesium in adult cramps (MD -0.42/week, 95% CI -1.15 to 0.31) is a negative translational signal for systemic supplementation in TMD.
- Sodium-containing drinks show context-specific effects (heat exercise, cirrhosis), judged not relevant to chronic orofacial pain; calcium and potassium unsupported.

## Methodology

PRISMA; PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, CENTRAL (1995 to 15 Aug 2025); randomized or quasi-randomized human trials, English; RoB 2; random-effects meta-analysis when at least 2 comparable studies, otherwise narrative. 75 records, 34 full texts, 13 included (n 9 to 184). TMD extrapolation prespecified as hypothesis-generating.

## Results

| Context | Studies | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Pregnancy cramps, oral Mg | 4 RCTs, N about 364 | Pooled RR about 1.35 (95% CI 1.05-1.74, P=.02) on cramp frequency; 3 of 4 trials positive, 1 null |
| Adult nocturnal/persistent cramps, oral Mg | 4 RCTs, nearly 400 (N about 396) | MD -0.42 episodes/week (95% CI -1.15 to 0.31, P=.26) |
| Older adults, IV MgSO4 | 1 RCT | No effect on rest cramps |
| Perioperative, high-dose IV MgSO4 | 1 trial | Less suxamethonium fasciculation and postoperative myalgia |
| Sodium/multi-electrolyte drinks | 3 studies | Lower experimentally induced cramp susceptibility; less cramp severity in cirrhosis (pickle juice) |
| Calcium, potassium | 0 | No eligible trials |

Risk of bias was low to moderate overall; older community trials and small crossover studies had some concerns. The direction convention of the pooled RR 1.35 is not stated in the abstract or text (a ratio above 1 would normally mean more events), so the magnitude should be read cautiously.

**Relation to masticatory-muscle pain care.** The authors cite a recent RCT of local magnesium sulphate injection into the masseter that reduced pain and improved function in TMD myalgia (not part of this review's pooled data). Apart from that, the evidence is extrapolated from non-TMD cramps and does not support adding systemic magnesium to TMD management. Compared with held pages on manual therapy, botulinum toxin, and general myogenous-TMD care, this paper adds only a mechanistic and negative-signal perspective, not a usable treatment estimate.

**Limitations.** Heterogeneous populations, magnesium salts, and durations; small samples; English-only; inferential extrapolation.
