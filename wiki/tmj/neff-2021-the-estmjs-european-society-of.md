## Three-line Summary

International consensus recommendation by the European Society of TMJ Surgeons (ESTMJS): 20/44 members participated in blinded preliminary Delphi grading of 30 draft recommendations (from the German S3 guideline, AWMF 007-063); 22/44 voted at the final consensus meeting (Marburg, May 2019); literature updated to February 2021 (230 total papers); preliminary and final SoC differed significantly (p=0.0005), but all **24 final recommendations were adopted at strong consensus (>95%)**.

Recommendations span uniform nomenclature (three axes: self-reducibility, time course, duration), diagnosis (history/palpation sufficient for non-traumatic cases; X-ray optional), and a treatment ladder — Hippocratic manual reduction first (thumbs on oblique line); if fails, add muscle relaxants/sedation/GA; for recurrent/habitual: non-surgical first (ABI best evidence LoE Ib; BTX off-label but endorsed as potential indication), then minimally invasive, then surgical (eminectomy / restrictive / capsulorrhaphy); only 1 GoR A = "non-surgical must precede invasive intervention"; wrist pivot method explicitly excluded despite LoE Ib evidence, due to ESTMJS inexperience.

Key divergence from the German S3 guideline: the ESTMJS declined the unilateral-first sequence and wrist pivot method recommendations, and added BTX as a potential off-label indication — reflecting how expert consensus can validly deviate from evidence rankings when collective clinical experience differs.

## Summary

Neff et al. (2021) present the first international evidence- and consensus-based recommendations for the management of condylar (temporomandibular joint) dislocation, produced by the ESTMJS using a modified Delphi procedure anchored in 230 papers from systematic literature searches spanning 2014–2021. The 24 final recommendations — all adopted at strong consensus (>95%) by 22 participating European TMJ surgeons — codify nomenclature, diagnostic approach, and a stepwise treatment algorithm. For acute non-traumatic dislocation: attempt Hippocratic manual reduction without medication first (thumbs on oblique line, not on molars), escalate to muscle relaxants, sedation, or general anaesthesia if needed. The wrist pivot method, which holds LoE Ib–IV evidence in the systematic literature, was excluded from the recommendations because fewer than 2/20 ESTMJS members in preliminary voting would support it — an explicit acknowledgment that collective clinical practice can depart from evidence rankings. For recurrent/habitual dislocation: non-surgical modalities first (ABI holds the best evidence at LoE Ib), BTX injection included as a potential off-label indication (unlike the German guideline which did not include it), then escalation to minimally invasive and surgical approaches. The sole GoR A recommendation in the entire document is that non-surgical methods must fail before invasive intervention. The document diverged significantly from the German S3 guideline predecessor in preliminary voting (p=0.0005) but was reconciled through moderated discussion, highlighting that national guideline homogeneity does not translate automatically to European-level consensus.

## Key Contributions

- **First international evidence + consensus-based recommendations** for TMJ dislocation: combines Delphi methodology with systematic literature (230 papers) to produce a globally applicable framework.
- **Uniform three-axis nomenclature** endorsed by ESTMJS: fixed/non-fixed · one-time/recurrent/habitual · acute/chronic (>4 weeks)/longstanding — addressing documented global terminology chaos.
- **Explicit exclusion of wrist pivot method despite LoE Ib evidence**: only 2/20 ESTMJS members would adopt it, demonstrating the interaction between evidence grading and collective clinical familiarity.
- **BTX included as off-label potential indication**: the German precursor guideline omitted BTX; the ESTMJS added it under GoR B, opening the door for future formal indication recognition.
- **"Oblique line" thumb placement** formalized: places thumbs on the mandibular oblique line rather than on the molars or under bite blocks — avoids bite injury during Hippocratic reduction.
- **Transparent reporting of GoR distribution**: only 1 GoR A ("shall"), 15 GoR B ("should"), 8 GoR 0 ("may") — unusually conservative GoR distribution for a guideline document, reflecting honest appraisal of low aggregate evidence quality.

## Methodology

- **Study type**: modified Delphi consensus procedure, AWMF S3 protocol
- **Draft base**: 30 recommendations from German S3 guideline (AWMF 007-063, 2016; same first author A.N.), translated to English
- **Evidence grading**: Oxford CEBM LoE I–V (with ± quality modifiers for n>100 or n<10)
- **GoR**: A = "shall" (highest LoE or extremely high consensus); B = "should" (LoE II or lower); 0 = "may" (LoE III–V, open decision)
- **Preliminary vote**: 20/44 ESTMJS members (12 countries), blinded grading; independent monitor
- **Final vote**: 22/44 members at GA Marburg May 2019; moderated live discussion and voting
- **SoC thresholds**: strong consensus >95%; consensus >75–95%; majority >50–75%; no consensus ≤50%
- **Literature**: initial search 2014–2016 (PubMed/Cochrane/Embase/ZB Med) = 104 papers; 2019 update +34; 2020–Feb 2021 update +92 → 230 total
- **Statistical test**: Wilcoxon signed-rank for GoR/SoC comparisons

## Results

| Phase | n members | Papers | Recommendations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preliminary vote | 20/44 (12 countries) | 138 | 30 (from German S3) |
| Final vote | 22/44 | 230 | 24 (8 discarded, 2 added) |

GoR distribution of final 24 recommendations: **GoR A: 1 (4%)** · GoR B: 15 (63%) · GoR 0: 8 (33%)

SoC outcome: all 24 final recommendations at **strong consensus (>95%)**; preliminary vs. final SoC difference p=0.0005.

Recommendation trajectory:
- Initial "consensus" (>75–95%): 57% unchanged, 14% modified to strong consensus, 29% discarded
- "Approved by majority" (>50–75%): 46% unchanged, 38% modified (wording/GoR), 15% discarded
- "No consensus" (≤50%): 1/3 survived with change, 2/3 discarded
