## Three-line Summary

This scoping review (PRISMA-ScR, OSF-registered protocol) maps the evidence on ultrasonography (US) for evaluating temporomandibular joint (TMJ) involvement in rheumatoid arthritis (RA): of 88 records screened, only 5 studies (154 RA patients total, mostly women with moderate-to-high disease activity) met inclusion criteria.

US consistently identified joint effusion, synovial thickening, and disc alterations across the included studies; only one diagnostic-accuracy study (Guiducci et al. 2025, n=50) reported high performance — 92.4% sensitivity and 90.3% specificity for joint effusion versus MRI — while the same single study found markedly weaker performance for disc displacement (72%) and condylar surface irregularities (53%), and associations between US findings and clinical indices (DAS28, HAQ) or pain were observed only inconsistently across studies.

As a scoping review, this work maps rather than ranks or pools evidence (no meta-analysis performed); the authors' own verdict is that US is a practical, non-invasive, affordable adjunct/initial examination that should complement rather than replace MRI, particularly for structural assessment — with methodological heterogeneity, small samples, and marked operator dependence flagged as the main limitations of the underlying evidence.

스코핑 리뷰라는 설계 특성상 이 논문은 근거를 서열화·통합(메타분석)하지 않고 지도화만 한다. 저자들의 결론은 US가 실용적이고 비침습적이며 저렴한 보조/초기 검사로서 특히 구조적 평가에서 MRI를 대체하기보다 보완해야 한다는 것이며, 방법론적 이질성·소규모 표본·뚜렷한 시술자 의존성(operator dependence)을 근거 기반의 주된 한계로 지적했다.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this scoping review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11282-026-00918-6); PMID 41917480; PMCID PMC13291074) maps the existing evidence on ultrasonography (US) for evaluating temporomandibular joint (TMJ) alterations in patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA). Following PRISMA-ScR methodology with a protocol registered on OSF, the authors searched PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, and gray literature through October 2025, screening 88 records down to 5 included studies covering 154 RA patients (2003–2025, Italy, Egypt, Turkey). Across the included studies, US consistently identified joint effusion, synovial thickening, and disc alterations. Only one diagnostic-accuracy study — Guiducci et al. (2025), n=50 — directly compared US against MRI and reported 92.4% sensitivity and 90.3% specificity for joint effusion, but the same study found markedly lower performance for disc displacement (72%) and condylar surface irregularities (53%), showing that US diagnostic capacity varies sharply by anatomical target rather than being a single fixed accuracy figure. Associations between US findings and clinical indices (DAS28, HAQ) or pain were reported inconsistently across studies. As a scoping review, no meta-analysis or formal risk-of-bias/GRADE assessment was performed — the paper maps and describes the evidence rather than ranking or pooling it. The authors' conclusion is that US is a practical, non-invasive, affordable adjunct/initial imaging examination for detecting inflammatory TMJ changes in RA, but it should complement rather than replace MRI, particularly for structural assessment, given methodological heterogeneity, small sample sizes, and substantial operator dependence across the underlying studies.

## Key Contributions

- First dedicated evidence map on US specifically for TMJ evaluation in RA (as distinct from TMJ osteoarthritis broadly or juvenile idiopathic arthritis), using a COCOPOP search strategy and PRISMA-ScR methodology, protocol pre-registered on OSF.
- Quantifies how thin the evidence base is for TMJ involvement in a common systemic disease: 88 records → only 5 studies → 154 patients total — the sparseness is itself a finding, not just a caveat, given RA's reported 40–55% TMJ involvement prevalence.
- Isolates the widely quotable 92.4%/90.3% sensitivity/specificity figure for joint effusion to a single diagnostic-accuracy study (n=50) against MRI, and shows the same study's accuracy drops sharply for disc displacement (72%) and condylar surface irregularities (53%) — diagnostic performance depends on which anatomical target is being assessed, not a single "US accuracy in RA" number.
- Foregrounds operator dependence and the complete absence of a standardized TMJ-specific US protocol (transducer frequency, probe positioning, patient position, static vs. dynamic acquisition, examiner background) as a primary limiter of reproducibility across studies.
- Flags that associations between US findings and systemic clinical/serological indices (DAS28, HAQ, pain) were inconsistent — imaging findings do not reliably track composite disease activity or how the patient feels, since DAS28 itself does not include TMJ assessment.
- Frames US explicitly as a complementary, non-invasive, radiation-free adjunct/initial examination rather than a substitute for MRI, especially for structural (disc, bone) assessment.

## Methodology

PRISMA-ScR (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews) methodology; protocol registered on the Open Science Framework (10.17605/OSF.IO/UQWSH). Search strategy used the COCOPOP framework (Condition = TMJ alterations; Context = assessed via US imaging; Population = individuals diagnosed with RA). Electronic searches were conducted in October 2025 across PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, and Embase without language or date restrictions, supplemented by gray literature (OpenGrey, Google Scholar — first 200 results screened) and reference-list checking, with monthly automated alerts activated post-search. Eligible study designs were observational (cross-sectional, cohort, case-control) and clinical trials evaluating US-assessed TMJ changes in RA patients of any age, sex, disease activity, or ethnicity; case reports/series, conference abstracts, letters, reviews, animal/in vitro studies, and studies with other TMJ-affecting autoimmune/musculoskeletal disease were excluded. Two reviewers independently screened titles/abstracts and full texts (EndNote deduplication, Rayyan screening), resolving disagreements by discussion or a third reviewer; non-English/Portuguese/Spanish articles were machine-translated, and corresponding authors were contacted up to three times for inaccessible full texts or incomplete data. Findings were synthesized descriptively in tabular form — **no meta-analysis, pooled effect size, or formal risk-of-bias/GRADE assessment was performed**, which the authors state is by design for a scoping review rather than an oversight.

## Results

Study selection: 88 records retrieved → 40 screened after deduplication → 7 selected for full-text review → 5 studies included (1 inaccessible despite repeated author contact, 1 lacked absolute RA-group data, 6 gray-literature hits were duplicates of already-retrieved records).

| Study (year, country) | Design | n (RA) | Mean age (RA) | Key US finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Melchiorre et al. 2003, Italy | Cross-sectional | 22 | UD | Disc alterations 31.8%; condylar changes 72.7%; effusion 54.5%; US detected more changes than MRI overall but with lower specificity (75.0%), especially for condylar changes |
| El-Melegy et al. 2017, Egypt | Cross-sectional | 20 | 47.3 ± 10.0 | Erosion 57.5%; effusion 62.5%; disc displacement 52.5%; comparable to but slightly below MRI detection (77.5% vs. 82.5%); effusion/disc displacement significantly associated with DAS28 and mHAQ |

| Becenen Durmuş et al. 2025, Turkey | Cross-sectional (vs. 51 controls) | 51 | 53.0 ± 10.4 | Greater disc thickness than controls (p<0.001); proposed 1.55 mm cut-off for dysfunction; effusion more frequent than controls (significant on one side, p=0.041) |
| Guiducci et al. 2025, Italy | Prospective diagnostic accuracy (vs. MRI) | 50 | UD | **Only diagnostic-accuracy study**: effusion 92.4% sensitivity / 90.3% specificity; disc displacement 72%; condylar surface irregularities 53%; also disc displacement 76%, erosion 28%, effusion 88%, condylar alterations 52% detected overall |

*[미검증-source flag]*: the source article's body text states "In total, 152 individuals with RA were evaluated," while the abstract states "One hundred and fifty-four patients," and the per-study n values above sum to 154. This 152-vs-154 inconsistency is internal to the source article and is reproduced/flagged here rather than silently corrected (see `papers/tenorio-2026-ultrasonography-tmj-rheumatoid-arthritis-scoping-review.txt`).

The 92.4% sensitivity / 90.3% specificity figure for joint effusion — the number most likely to be remembered and quoted — comes from a **single study, for a single target (joint effusion), against MRI**. It is not a pooled estimate, this scoping review performed no meta-analysis, and it must not be cited as "ultrasound's accuracy for RA TMJ involvement" in general. Diagnostic performance in that same study fell sharply for disc position (72%) and bone/condylar surface (53%), and Melchiorre (2003) separately found lower specificity (75.0%) for condylar changes — the consistent pattern across studies is that US is comparatively strong for detecting joint effusion/synovial change and comparatively weak for structural (disc, bone) assessment relative to MRI. Associations between US findings and DAS28/HAQ/pain were significant in some analyses (El-Melegy 2017) but inconsistent overall, and US-detected structural changes were sometimes found in clinically asymptomatic patients. **Operator dependence** is highlighted by the authors as the limitation most likely to determine transferability to a given practice: transducer frequency (7–16 MHz across studies), probe position, patient position, static vs. dynamic acquisition, and examiner background (radiologist, rheumatologist, sonographer) varied across every included study, with no standardized TMJ-specific US protocol in use. Main limitations overall: methodological heterogeneity, small sample sizes (11–51 per study), only one study with a healthy control group, and no formal risk-of-bias/GRADE rating (by design for a scoping review).
