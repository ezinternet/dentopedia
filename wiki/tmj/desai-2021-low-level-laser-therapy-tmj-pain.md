## Three-line Summary

Double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT (n=60 TMD patients, randomized 30/30) comparing 20 sessions of low-level laser therapy (LLLT, applied in closed-mouth and maximum-mouth-opening positions) versus sham laser over 8 weeks; abstract-only ingest.

Both groups improved in pain, maximum mouth opening, clicking, and mandibular deviation, but the LLLT group showed a statistically better treatment outcome than the placebo group.

LLLT is a viable non-surgical adjunct for TMD symptom management with a favorable safety profile that can be combined with other conservative modalities; exact effect sizes and laser parameters are not recoverable because only the abstract was retrievable (PMC full-text body was empty for this record).

## Summary

This double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT from the Indian Army Dental Corps directly tests whether **low-level laser therapy (LLLT)** outperforms a true sham comparator for temporomandibular disorder (TMD) symptoms — a more rigorous isolation of LLLT's specific effect than multi-modality comparison trials that bundle LLLT alongside splints, exercise, or manual therapy.

Sixty TMD patients were randomized 1:1 into a placebo group (sham laser) and an LLLT group. Both groups received 20 treatment sessions over 8 weeks, with the laser (active or sham) applied in both the closed-mouth position and at maximum mouth opening. Four outcome domains were tracked: pain, maximum mouth opening (MMO), joint clicking, and mandibular deviation.

Both groups improved across all four domains — a reminder that conservative/placebo effects in TMD trials are real and substantial — but the active LLLT group showed a **better treatment outcome** than placebo. The authors conclude LLLT is a useful non-surgical adjunct that can be combined with other conservative TMD modalities.

## Key Contributions

- Isolates LLLT against a **true placebo (sham laser) arm** in a double-blind design, giving a cleaner efficacy signal than multi-arm comparative trials that test LLLT alongside several other modalities simultaneously
- Applies a relatively intensive dosing protocol: **20 sessions over 8 weeks**, delivered in both closed-mouth and maximum-mouth-opening positions
- Tracks four clinically relevant TMD domains together (pain, MMO, clicking, deviation) rather than a single outcome measure
- Reinforces that LLLT's benefit is incremental **on top of** a substantial placebo/conservative-care response, not a replacement for it

## Methodology

- **Design**: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- **Sample**: n=60 patients diagnosed with TMD; randomized into Group I (placebo laser, n=30) and Group II (active LLLT, n=30)
- **Intervention**: 20 sessions of LLLT (or sham) over 8 weeks; applied in both closed-mouth position and maximum mouth opening position
- **Outcomes assessed**: pain, maximum mouth opening, clicking, mandibular deviation
- Statistical analysis performed on collected outcome data (specific test statistics not recoverable from the abstract)

## Results

- Both the placebo and LLLT groups showed improvement in pain reduction, maximum mouth opening, and reduction in deviation and clicking
- The **LLLT group showed a better treatment outcome** than the placebo group across the assessed domains
- Conclusion: conservative measures alone improve TMD symptoms, but LLLT adds a further, statistically better benefit versus sham — supporting its use as a non-surgical adjunct, potentially in combination with other conservative modalities
- Precise effect sizes, p-values, and laser parameters (wavelength, power density, energy per session) are not available from the abstract-only retrieval
