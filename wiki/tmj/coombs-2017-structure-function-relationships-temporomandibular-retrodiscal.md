## Three-line Summary

Ex-vivo tensile biomechanics study of 20 porcine retrodiscal-tissue (RDT) specimens (incremental stress-relaxation, 5–50% strain) with second-harmonic-generation (SHG) microscopy of collagen and elastin. Young's modulus stayed <1 MPa from 5–20% strain and reached a maximum of only 2.9 MPa at 50% strain, with region- and strain-dependence (higher temporally and mediolaterally, p < 0.001); collagen was most organized near the disc boundary and progressively disorganized posteriorly. The very low tensile moduli indicate the RDT is not stiff enough to resist pathologic anterior disc displacement, challenging the classical "posterior brake" model of disc stability. *(abstract-only — full text not retrieved.)*

## Summary

*abstract-only — publisher/PMC full text not retrieved; content below is built from the structured abstract.*

The retrodiscal tissue has long been described in textbooks as a structure that helps hold the articular disc in place and resists its anterior displacement. This study set out to test that mechanically. Using incremental stress-relaxation testing of porcine retrodiscal tissue in two regions (temporal, condylar) and two directions (anteroposterior, mediolateral), paired with second-harmonic-generation microscopy of the collagen and elastin networks, the authors measured how stiff the tissue actually is and how its microstructure varies by location.

The retrodiscal tissue proved to be very compliant — a Young's modulus below 1 MPa across the physiological strain range, stiffening only at extreme (50%) strain to about 2.9 MPa. Stiffness was modestly higher in the temporal region and the mediolateral direction, tracking a collagen network that is well organized adjacent to the disc and increasingly disorganized posteriorly, with elastin concentrated at the disc boundary and tissue mid-body. The authors conclude that the retrodiscal tissue is too soft to serve as a mechanical restraint against disc displacement, and that its functional role (elastic recoil, sensory function, vascular/synovial support) needs rethinking.

## Key Contributions

1. First region- and direction-resolved tensile map of the retrodiscal tissue.
2. Empirical challenge to the "posterior brake" model — the tissue is not stiff enough to restrain disc displacement.
3. Structure–function link via SHG microscopy: collagen organization highest at the disc boundary, decreasing posteriorly; elastin at boundary and mid-body.
4. Quantified strain-stiffening: <1 MPa physiologically, ~2.9 MPa only at 50% strain.

## Methodology

- 20 porcine retrodiscal-tissue specimens.
- Incremental stress-relaxation: strain 5%→50%, ramp 2%/s, 2.5-min relaxation per step.
- Comparisons: temporal vs condylar region; anteroposterior vs mediolateral direction.
- SHG microscopy for collagen; elastin imaging.
- Outcomes: Young's modulus, instantaneous/relaxed moduli, regional fiber organization; significance by region and strain (p < 0.001).
