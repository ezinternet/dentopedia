## Three-line Summary

Retrospective study (Seoul National University Bundang Hospital, n=19 TMD patients, Jan 2022–Dec 2023) with chronic TMJ pain refractory to conventional treatment (splint, botulinum injection, intra-articular dexamethasone/hyaluronic acid, arthrocentesis), treated with hypertonic (10%) dextrose prolotherapy injected at retrodiscal attachment, anterior disc attachment, lateral capsule, masseter origin, and stylomandibular ligament, up to 3 sessions 2–3 weeks apart.

Mean NRS pain dropped from 5.7 at baseline to 1.7 after completing all sessions (13/19 completed 3 sessions); mean maximum mouth opening increased from 34.5 mm to 38.8 mm; outcomes were positive regardless of whether the disorder originated from soft-tissue (muscle/capsule/disc, group A) or bone-related (osteoarthritis, group B) causes; no serious complications, only transient injection-site pain and brief facial nerve weakness.

Small, uncontrolled, single-surgeon retrospective series (no comparator arm, subjective NRS outcome) — supports dextrose prolotherapy as a low-cost, low-risk option for refractory chronic TMD, but efficacy needs confirmation in a controlled prospective design.

## 세줄요약

후향적 연구 (서울대학교병원 분당서울대병원, n=19명 TMD 환자, 2022년 1월–2023년 12월): 스플린트·보툴리눔 주사·관절강 내 덱사메타손/히알루론산 주사·관절세정술(arthrocentesis) 등 기존 보존적 치료에 반응하지 않는 만성 통증 환자에게 고장성(10%) 덱스트로스 (dextrose)를 후원판부착부, 전방 원판 부착부, 외측 관절낭, 교근 기시부, 경상하악인대 부위에 최대 3회(2–3주 간격) 주입.

NRS 통증 점수 기저 5.7 → 전 세션 완료 후 1.7로 감소(19명 중 13명 3세션 완료); 최대 개구량 34.5mm → 38.8mm 증가; 연조직 원인군(A군)과 골성/골관절염군(B군) 모두 유사한 개선; 중대 합병증 없음, 경미한 주사부위 통증과 일과성 안면신경 약화만 관찰.

단일기관·대조군 없는 소규모(n=19) 후향적 연구로 주관적 NRS에 의존 — 난치성 만성 TMD에 대한 저비용·저위험 대안으로서의 근거를 제공하지만, 대조군을 갖춘 전향적 연구로 확증이 필요함.

## Summary

This retrospective study from Seoul National University Bundang Hospital evaluates TMJ (Temporomandibular Joint, 하악관절) prolotherapy(증식치료) using hypertonic dextrose in 19 patients with chronic TMD pain that had failed conventional conservative and interventional treatments (occlusal splint, botulinum toxin, intra-articular corticosteroid/hyaluronic acid injection, arthrocentesis). A 10% dextrose solution (mixed from 20% dextrose, 4% lidocaine, and saline in a 2:1:1 ratio) was injected at up to five anatomical points around the TMJ — retrodiscal attachment tissue, anterior disc attachment tissue, superior and inferior lateral capsule, masseter muscle origin, and stylomandibular ligament insertion — over a maximum of 3 sessions spaced 2–3 weeks apart. Both pain (NRS) and maximum mouth opening (MMO) improved progressively across sessions, with benefit seen in patients regardless of whether their TMD originated from soft-tissue (muscle/capsule/disc) or bone-related (osteoarthritic) causes. The complication profile was minor and self-limiting.

## Key Contributions

- Provides primary clinical (retrospective) evidence for dextrose prolotherapy as a treatment option in chronic TMD refractory to standard conservative and minimally invasive care
- Concrete, reproducible 5-point injection protocol with a specified 10% dextrose solution recipe and needle gauge
- Introduces a practical patient-stratification scheme: group A (soft-tissue origin, Japanese TMJ Society types I–III) receives injections targeting retrodiscal/disc-attachment/capsule tissue and masseter origin; group B (bone-related, type IV osteoarthritis) additionally targets the stylomandibular ligament
- Shows a session-dependent, incremental improvement pattern (NRS 5.7 → 4.0 → 2.5 → 1.7 across up to 3 sessions)
- Reports a favorable, low-complication safety profile suitable for outpatient chairside injection by a trained oral-maxillofacial surgeon

## Methodology

- **Design**: Retrospective chart review, single center, single expert operator (IRB-approved, SNUBH)
- **Period**: January 2022 – December 2023
- **Sample**: n=19 (6 male, 13 female; mean age 43, range 18–74); 11/19 had bruxism/clenching
- **Inclusion**: TMD pain unresponsive to conventional treatment; treated by the same expert operator; voluntary NRS respondents
- **Exclusion**: diabetes mellitus, TMJ tumor/severe ankylosis, prior TMJ open surgery, systemic NSAID/steroid use, need for psychiatric treatment
- **Diagnostic framework**: Japanese Society for the Temporomandibular Joint (2001) — type I muscle disorder, II capsule-ligament disorder, III disc disorder, IV osteoarthritis/degenerative disease (group B), V psychiatric (excluded); types I–III grouped as group A
- **Injection protocol**: auriculotemporal nerve block (2% lidocaine) + topical EMLA; 10% dextrose solution, 1 cc per point, 26-gauge 22-mm needle; up to 3 sessions at 2–3 week intervals; concurrent TMD interventions discontinued
- **Outcomes**: 0–10 NRS (pain, opening limitation, hypermobility, joint sounds), MMO, subjective satisfaction, assessed 2–3 weeks post-session; Mann-Whitney U test for group A vs B comparison

## Results

- NRS: 5.7 (baseline, n=19) → 4.0 (after session 1, n=19) → 2.5 (after session 2, n=15) → 1.7 (after session 3, n=13)
- Session completion: 68.4% (13/19) completed all 3 sessions; 10.5% stopped after 2 (already improved); 21.1% stopped after 1 (already improved)
- MMO: transient dip from 34.5 mm to 33.5 mm after session 1, then increase to 38.8 mm by final follow-up
- Group A (n=10, soft-tissue) and Group B (n=9, osteoarthritis) both showed improved pain and MMO — outcome independent of underlying TMD origin
- No serious adverse events; minor transient injection-site pain and brief (2–3 hour) ipsilateral facial nerve weakness only
- Overall patient satisfaction was positive
