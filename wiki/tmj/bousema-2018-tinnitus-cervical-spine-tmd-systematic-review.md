## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis of 24 studies (22 independent, screened from 2,139 initial records; Medline/Embase/Pedro, 1966–Sep 2017) on the association between subjective tinnitus and cervical spine disorders (CSD) or temporomandibular disorders (TMD).

Tinnitus patients had higher odds of CSD (OR 2.6, 95% CI 1.1–6.4), and a bidirectional association with TMD was found, with odds ratios ranging from 2.3 (95% CI 1.5–3.6) for arthrogenous TMD to 6.7 (95% CI 2.4–18.8) for unspecified TMD (myogenous TMD OR 4.1, 95% CI 2.1–8.1; combined CSD+TMD OR 5.5, 95% CI 3.4–9.0).

Overall level of evidence was graded low (no high-quality studies; only 2/22 blinded, 3/22 randomized) and publication bias likely inflated several pooled estimates (trim-and-fill reduced unspecified-TMD OR from 6.7 to 4.5), so the authors describe the evidence as "weak" despite statistical significance.

## Summary

This is a systematic review and meta-analysis investigating whether subjective tinnitus is associated with cervical spine disorders (CSD) and/or temporomandibular disorders (TMD). The authors searched Medline, Embase, and Pedro (1966–September 2017) and identified 2,139 records, narrowing to 22 independent studies (24 articles, 2 excluded as duplicate datasets) after full-text screening (interobserver kappa 0.70). Studies were grouped into five association categories — tinnitus-CSD, tinnitus-(CSD+TMD combined), tinnitus-TMD (unspecified), tinnitus-arthrogenous TMD (TMDa), and tinnitus-myogenous TMD (TMDm) — and pooled via random-effects meta-analysis (Comprehensive Meta-Analysis V3). All associations were statistically significant, but methodological quality was uniformly modest-to-poor (9-item checklist, scores 1–7/9), and funnel-plot analysis suggested publication bias inflated several pooled odds ratios. The review concludes there is a bidirectional tinnitus-TMD association and a (only) unidirectional tinnitus-CSD association, both rated as low-quality/weak evidence per GRADE.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic quantitative synthesis of the tinnitus-CSD/TMD literature, expressing associations as pooled odds ratios rather than narrative summary.
- Demonstrates the tinnitus-TMD relationship is bidirectional (elevated in both directions), while tinnitus-CSD is only shown unidirectionally (no study tested tinnitus prevalence in CSD patients vs. controls).
- Subtype stratification (TMDa vs. TMDm vs. unspecified TMD vs. combined CSD+TMD) gives clinicians differentiated risk estimates rather than one blended figure.
- Publication-bias-adjusted (trim-and-fill) estimates alongside raw pooled ORs, giving a more conservative "corrected" reading of the effect sizes.
- Proposes a brainstem-level mechanistic model (trigeminal spinal nucleus–cochlear nucleus convergence) consistent with the observed ipsilateral tendency of the association.

## Methodology

- **Databases/period**: Medline, Embase, Pedro; 1966–September 2017.
- **Screening**: title/abstract by first author; full-text independently by two reviewers (kappa 0.70, 86% agreement).
- **Inclusion**: tinnitus and CSD/TMD as primary or secondary outcome, with a control group comparison.
- **Exclusion**: reviews, letters, animal studies, n<10, trauma-caused disorders.
- **Quality tool**: 9-item modified checklist covering research question clarity, population definition, control comparability, case/control criteria validity, patient-control differentiation, random selection, matching, exposure-measure validity, and assessor blinding — scored [+]/[–]/[?] and reported per-item (not summed), since item severity is not uniform.
- **Statistics**: Comprehensive Meta-Analysis V3, random-effects model, odds ratio as summary statistic; funnel plots + nonparametric trim-and-fill for publication-bias correction.

## Results

- **Yield**: 2,139 records (PubMed 1,069; Embase 1,053; Pedro 17) → 1,581 after dedup → 64 full-texts → 24 inclusions → 22 independent studies (20 cross-sectional, 2 longitudinal/baseline).
- **Category counts**: tinnitus-CSD (5 studies), tinnitus-CSD+TMD combined (3), tinnitus-TMD unspecified (6), tinnitus-TMDa (11), tinnitus-TMDm (7).
- **Pooled odds ratios (raw / study-range)**:
  - Tinnitus-CSD: **OR 2.6** (95% CI [1.1, 6.4]); individual studies 1.2–10.9.
  - Tinnitus-CSD+TMD combined: **OR 5.5** (95% CI [3.4, 9.0]); range 4.9–7.0; 67–83% tinnitus prevalence in these specialized-hospital cohorts.
  - Tinnitus-TMD (unspecified): **OR 6.7** (95% CI [2.4, 18.8]); range 1.7–44.3; 37–88% of TMD-clinic patients reported tinnitus.
  - Tinnitus-TMDa (arthrogenous): **OR 2.3** (95% CI [1.5, 3.6]); range 1.2–9.9; 20–59% (specialized clinics) / 21–48% (other settings) tinnitus prevalence.
  - Tinnitus-TMDm (myogenous): **OR 4.1** (95% CI [2.1, 8.1]); range 1.3–10.0.
- **Laterality**: 3 studies; 2 found predominantly ipsilateral tinnitus-symptom co-occurrence (contralateral TMJ asymptomatic in 94% in one study); the third found 56.5% laterality concordance (p<.001) between tinnitus side and the side with the most myofascial trigger points.
- **Publication bias**: funnel-plot asymmetry suggested bias for the CSD+TMD, TMD (unspecified), and TMDm associations. After trim-and-fill correction: CSD+TMD OR 5.5→4.9; TMD (unspecified) OR 6.7→4.5; TMDm OR 4.1→3.3.
- **Quality**: criteria met ranged 1–7/9 (kappa 0.33, 64% agreement). Blinded assessors in only 2/22 studies; random selection in 3/22; age/gender matching in 5/22; same-population recruitment in 16/22; validated instruments (RDC/TMD) in only 8/22. Tinnitus was assessed by a single non-validated question in most studies, with **no severity data** collected anywhere.
- **GRADE**: overall evidence level rated **low** for both the tinnitus-CSD and tinnitus-TMD associations; authors explicitly caution the CSD/TMD-combined, unspecified-TMD, and TMDm associations "may be overestimated due to publication bias."
