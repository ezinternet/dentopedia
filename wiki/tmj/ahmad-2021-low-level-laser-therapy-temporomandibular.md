## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (J Med Life 2021) of 37 RCTs (2000-June 2020; PubMed and Science Direct only) testing low-level laser therapy (LLLT) in patients with temporomandibular disorders.

18 studies found LLLT efficacious for TMD pain, 12 found it equal to placebo or comparator, and 4 reported mixed effects; no pooled estimate was computed, and laser parameters varied widely (632.8-1064 nm; 1.5-112.5 J/cm2; 4-20 sessions).

The authors call LLLT a promising non-invasive option, but the evidence is heterogeneous and only two databases were searched, so they themselves state there is still no conclusive validation for or against LLLT.

## Summary

This systematic review (source: PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.25122/jml-2020-0169)) asks whether low-level laser therapy (LLLT) is efficacious in TMD, with pain intensity (VAS) as the primary outcome and mouth opening, excursions, pressure pain threshold, EMG, joint noises, masticatory efficiency and psychological aspects as secondary outcomes. It includes 37 RCTs published 2000 to June 2020, identified in only two databases, and synthesizes them narratively without pooling.

Trial verdicts were split: 18 studies showed LLLT efficacious for pain, 12 showed similar efficacy to placebo, controls, or other interventions, and 4 showed varied effects. Secondary outcomes responded variably. The authors conclude that LLLT appears efficient in diminishing TMD pain, is non-invasive and reversible with few adverse effects, yet also concede that no conclusive validation exists for or against it, and attribute contradictory trial results mainly to heterogeneity in dose, power, wavelength, session number and frequency.

## Key Contributions

- Tabulates laser type, wavelength, dose, power, session schedule, application site and result for each of 37 RCTs.
- Counts trial verdicts (18 efficacious / 12 equal to placebo or comparator / 4 varied) rather than pooling effect sizes.
- Highlights parameter heterogeneity (dose 1.5-112.5 J/cm2; power 1.76-500 mW; 4-20 sessions) as the probable driver of inconsistent findings.
- Notes seven active-comparator RCTs (botulinum toxin A, TENS, ibuprofen, needling, occlusal splints, physiotherapy/drug protocol).

## Methodology

- Search Jan 2000 - June 2020 in PubMed and Science Direct, plus hand-searching; English only.
- Inclusion: human RCTs in TMD with at least 10 subjects. Exclusion: non-randomized or crossover designs, animal studies, systemic disease or non-TMD pain.
- Risk of bias visualized with Robvis; no meta-analysis, no certainty grading, no registration reported.

## Results

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Included RCTs | 37 |
| Risk of bias | 33 (89.18%) low risk or some concerns; 4 high risk |
| Pain verdicts | 18 LLLT efficacious; 12 similar to placebo/control/other; 4 varied |
| Wavelength / dose / power | 632.8-1064 nm; 1.5-112.5 J/cm2; 1.76-500 mW |
| Sessions | 4-20 total; most commonly 8 (8 studies), 12 (7), 10 (6); usually 2 per week |
| Application site | TMJ and/or muscles in 18 RCTs; TMJ only 9; muscles only 8 |
| Active comparators | Botulinum toxin A, TENS, ibuprofen, needling, occlusal splints, physiotherapy/drug protocol (7 studies) |

Selected comparisons as reported: LLLT reduced pain faster than botulinum toxin A with no significant mouth-opening difference (Carli 2016); LLLT equaled occlusal splints (Oz 2010); PBM plus manual therapy was no better than either alone (Brochado 2018); laser acupuncture was suggested as an alternative needing less chair time (Madani 2020). Two studies reported improved psychological and emotional aspects.

Caveats: the review asserts no publication bias from a risk-of-bias plot, which does not assess publication bias; only two databases were searched; the authors themselves call for a further SR with meta-analysis.

## Clinical and Comparative Notes

- **What this adds over [[tmj/desai-2021-low-level-laser-therapy-tmj-pain]]:** Desai is one positive sham-controlled RCT (n=60, abstract-only); this review shows that across 37 RCTs the sham-controlled picture is mixed (roughly 18 positive vs 12 null for pain), so a single positive trial should not be read as settled.
- **Versus [[tmj/candido-do-prado-2025-differential-effectiveness-photobiomodulation-muscular-articular]]:** that later SR+MA pooled 18 RCTs and reported positive pooled pain and function effects; this earlier review is qualitative only and cannot give effect sizes or certainty. For quantitative estimates prefer the SR+MA (itself abstract-only here).
- No supersession marked: the older review's bottom line (LLLT probably helps pain, evidence heterogeneous) is not overturned by later work, merely quantified.
