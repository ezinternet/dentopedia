## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (no meta-analysis reported in the abstract) of studies on stabilization splints for muscular-origin TMD versus other treatments; six databases plus gray literature searched; 10 studies included.

Splints (n = 160) were reported as effective as other treatments (n = 209) for pressure pain threshold, chewing pain, mouth opening, spontaneous pain and palpation pain; 5 studies low and 5 some-concerns risk of bias; certainty very low for all outcomes.

A positive splint effect on muscular TMD could be neither confirmed nor refuted; splints are not shown to be superior to alternatives.

## Summary

This systematic review (Cranio 2022; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/08869634.2022.2047510); source: PubMed, PMID 35311479) asked whether stabilization splints improve signs and symptoms of muscular-origin TMD compared with other treatments. Ten articles were included after searching six databases and gray literature. Splints (n = 160) were reported to be as effective as other treatments (n = 209) across pressure pain threshold, pain during chewing, mouth opening, spontaneous pain intensity and pain on palpation. Risk of bias was low in five and "some concerns" in five studies, and GRADE certainty was very low for every outcome. The authors conclude a positive splint effect could neither be confirmed nor refuted.

Only the abstract was retrieved; no effect sizes are available.

## Key Contributions

- Isolates the muscular (myogenous/myofascial) TMD subtype for the splint question.
- Benchmarks splints against other active treatments: splints are not inferior, but certainty is very low.
- Adds a subtype-specific, GRADE-rated statement that complements all-TMD splint reviews.

## Methodology

Six electronic databases and gray literature; Cochrane Collaboration risk-of-bias tool; GRADE. Ten studies included. Further methodological detail not available from the abstract.

## Results

- Splints (n = 160) vs other treatments (n = 209): equivalent effectiveness reported for pressure pain threshold, chewing pain, mouth opening, spontaneous pain, palpation pain.
- Risk of bias: 5 low, 5 some concerns.
- Certainty of evidence: very low for all outcomes.
