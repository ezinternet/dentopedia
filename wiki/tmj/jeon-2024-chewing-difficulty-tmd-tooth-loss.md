## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional comparative study, n=392 patients aged ≥50 with chewing difficulty, split into a prosthetic/tooth-loss group (n=193) and a TMD group (n=199) at a Korean university hospital dental clinic.

The prosthetic group had significantly higher chewing discomfort (NRS) and more missing posterior teeth/worse Eichner class; the TMD group had significantly higher Stress Response Inventory (SRI) scores (tension, anger, depression, fatigue, frustration, total) and a higher rate of clinically significant insomnia (13.6% vs 9.9%, ISI ≥15).

Tooth loss (occlusal support) drives chewing discomfort severity more than TMD pain does, but TMD patients need concurrent stress/sleep assessment and a multidisciplinary approach, especially women (77.4% of the TMD group).

## Summary

This cross-sectional study from Pusan National University Hospital (Korea) compared 392 patients aged ≥50 who presented with chewing (masticatory) difficulty, splitting them into two etiologically distinct groups: a Prosthetic group (n=193, seeking prosthodontic treatment for tooth loss, free of TMD signs/symptoms) and a TMD group (n=199, referred for temporomandibular joint/muscle pain, diagnosed per RDC/TMD axis I). Using three self-report instruments — Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) for chewing discomfort, Stress Response Inventory (SRI) for psychological stress, and Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) for sleep quality — the study found a clear divergence: the Prosthetic group's chewing discomfort was driven by loss of occlusal support (mean 6.5±5.2 missing posterior teeth vs 1.5±3.1 in the TMD group; worse Eichner class), while the TMD group's burden was psychological/sleep-related (significantly higher SRI subscales and clinical insomnia rate). Women dominated the TMD group (77.4%) and showed the strongest sex-related stress/insomnia differences. The authors conclude that treatment direction should follow etiology — prosthodontic occlusal restoration for tooth-loss-driven discomfort, and stress/sleep screening plus multidisciplinary management for TMD-driven discomfort, particularly in older women.

## Key Contributions

- First head-to-head comparison of TMD-caused vs tooth-loss-caused chewing difficulty in the same patient population and time period, rather than studying each cause in isolation.
- Shows posterior occlusal support loss (tooth count, Eichner class) is the dominant driver of chewing-discomfort severity (NRS), exceeding the contribution of TMD pain itself.
- Shows TMD (not tooth loss) is the dominant driver of psychological stress (SRI) and clinical insomnia (ISI ≥15) among older chewing-difficulty patients.
- Identifies sex as an effect modifier: TMD group is 77.4% female; interaction analysis shows female prosthetic patients are more susceptible to severe chewing discomfort than TMD patients.
- Translates findings into an etiology-driven triage principle for clinicians managing older adults presenting with chewing complaints.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Cross-sectional, two-group comparison, single center (PNUH Dental Clinic Center, Korea), enrolled June 2018–September 2020.
- **Groups**: Prosthetic group (n=193, tooth-loss driven, no TMD signs) vs TMD group (n=199, diagnosed via RDC/TMD axis I).
- **Exclusions**: facial/jaw fracture or orthognathic surgery history, psychiatric conditions, neurologic disease, other systemic muscular disorders (fibromyalgia, RA).
- **Instruments**: NRS (0–10, chewing discomfort, severe = ≥7); Eichner's index (occlusal support Class A/B/B1–4/C) plus missing posterior tooth count; SRI (39 items, 7 subscales — tension, aggression, somatization, anger, depression, fatigue, frustration — total 0–156, Korean-validated); ISI (0–28, clinically significant insomnia ≥15, Korean-validated).
- **Statistics**: Kolmogorov–Smirnov normality, t-test/Wilcoxon for continuous variables, chi-square/Fisher exact for categorical, logistic regression (adjusted for age, sex, Eichner's class) plus interaction-effect testing; significance p<0.05.

## Results

| Measure | Prosthetic group (n=193) | TMD group (n=199) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age | Higher | Lower | significant |
| NRS (chewing discomfort) | Higher; severe (≥7) in 36.3% | Lower; severe (≥7) in 12.1% | <0.001 |
| Missing posterior teeth (mean) | 6.5 ± 5.2 | 1.5 ± 3.1 | <0.001 |
| Eichner's Class A | 31.6% | 85.9% | <0.001 |
| Eichner's Class C | 21.2% | 2.0% | <0.001 |
| Female proportion | 60.6% | 77.4% | <0.001 |
| ISI average | Lower | Higher | <0.001 |
| Clinically significant insomnia (ISI≥15) | 9.9% | 13.6% | 0.015 |
| SRI (tension, anger, depression, fatigue, frustration, total) | Lower | Higher | significant (all listed subscales) |

- Sex subgroup, TMD group: women scored higher than men on tension, somatization, fatigue, frustration, total SRI; no sex difference in severe chewing discomfort or clinical insomnia.
- Sex subgroup, Prosthetic group: women had higher clinical insomnia prevalence (13.7% vs 3.9%) and higher frustration scores (p=0.044); no sex difference in severe chewing discomfort.
- Logistic regression: Prosthetic group more susceptible to severe chewing discomfort; TMD group more susceptible to clinically significant insomnia. Interaction effect: sex significantly affects severe chewing discomfort (p=0.041) — female prosthetic patients most susceptible.
- Clinical takeaway (authors): restore occlusion via prosthodontic treatment for tooth-loss-driven masticatory discomfort; assess stress response and sleep quality and consider multidisciplinary management for TMD patients with chewing discomfort, especially women.
