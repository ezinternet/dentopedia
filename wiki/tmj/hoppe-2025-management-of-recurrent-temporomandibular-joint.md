## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251139493), searching BASE, PubMed and Scopus on 21 September 2025 with PICOS eligibility: 268 records identified (102 BASE / 96 PubMed / 70 Scopus) → 168 after deduplication → 34 full texts → 24 excluded and 1 unobtainable → **9 studies included (5 case reports, 3 case series, 1 case–control; 10 paediatric patients tabulated, 2 excluded from outcome measures)**. Oxford CEBM levels: only 4 publications above level 5 (one level 3, three level 4).

Conservative and minimally invasive methods dominate the paediatric literature — botulinum toxin type A (BoNT-A) injection into the lateral pterygoid muscle is the most frequently reported minimally invasive approach and autologous blood injection nearly as frequent — while **invasive treatment appeared in only 2 of the 9 studies** (French 2017 eminectomy, Gadre 2010 Dautrey's procedure), both used as last resort and both recurrence-free at their follow-ups (6 and 36/28 months). Dislocation frequency before treatment reached >40 episodes in 2 weeks (Stark) and >20 in 2 months (Sicard); after treatment the reported series were 0 episodes at 33–36 months (Coser, Yoshida, Gadre), 2 episodes in 6 months (Sicard), and recurrence requiring repeat injections at 4 and a further 4 months (Stark).

Clinical implication: conservative and minimally invasive methods are the preferred first-line in children given low risk and better psychological tolerance, and interdisciplinary input (psychology, neurology) recurs in the successfully resolved cases — but heterogeneity across design, intervention and outcome measures makes effectiveness comparisons impossible, only 1 of the 5 pre-specified outcome measures (dislocation frequency) was reported consistently, and no long-term evidence supports choosing surgery, which is why the authors explicitly refuse to call age alone a contraindication to operative treatment.

## 세줄요약

메타분석 없는 체계적 리뷰 (systematic review without meta-analysis, SR) — PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251139493, 2025년 9월 21일 BASE·PubMed·Scopus 검색, PICOS 기준으로 **포함 9편**(사례보고 5·케이스시리즈 3·케이스-컨트롤 1), 표에 정리된 소아 환자 10명 중 2명은 결과 지표에서 제외. 근거 수준은 Oxford CEBM 기준 5단계 초과가 **4편뿐**(3단계 1편, 4단계 3편)이고, 수술법은 **2편에서만** 보고되었다.

소아 재발성 악관절 탈구 (recurrent TMJ dislocation) 에서는 보존적·미세침습법이 압도적으로 많이 보고되었고, 그중 **미세침습법의 최다 보고는 외항교근 (lateral pterygoid muscle) 에 대한 보툴리눔 독소 A형 (botulinum toxin type A, BoNT-A) 주사**, 자가혈액 주사 (autologous blood injection) 가 거의 같은 빈도로 뒤따랐다. 수술은 9편 중 2편(French 2017 결절절제술 (eminectomy), Gadre 2010 도트리 수술 (Dautrey's procedure) )에서만, 두 편 모두 마지막 수단으로 선택되어 각각 6개월·36/28개월 추적에서 재발이 없었다. 치료 전 탈구 빈도는 2주간 40회 초과(Stark), 2개월간 20회 초과(Sicard)였고, 치료 후에는 33~36개월 재발 0회(Coser·Yoshida·Gadre), 6개월간 2회(Sicard), 주사 반복이 필요한 재발(4개월 후 재발, 재주사 후 다시 4개월 후 재발; Stark)이 보고되었다.

임상적 함의: 위험이 낮고 심리적 수용성이 좋은 보존·미세침습법이 소아의 1차 선택이며, 성공적으로 해소된 사례들에서 **학제 간(multidisciplinary) 접근 — 심리·신경과 협업 — 이 반복적으로 등장**한다. 그러나 연구설계·중재·결과지표의 이질성 때문에 방법 간 우열 비교가 불가능하고, 사전 지정한 5개 지표 중 **탈구 빈도 하나만 일관되게 보고**되었으며 수술 선택을 뒷받침할 장기 근거가 없다 — 그래서 저자들은 "소아라는 이유만으로 수술을 금기라고 할 근거는 없다"고 명시한다. 이 논문은 급성 복원 자체가 아니라 그 다음 단계인 **재발 방지**를 다루지만, 소아 급성 복원 시 아산화질소·프로포폴 진정 (nitrous oxide / propofol sedation) 하에서 자연 복원 (spontaneous reduction) 이 보고된 사례(프로포폴 진정 중 자발적 복원, Sicard)가 있어 환자 주도 급성 복원 맥락과 연결된다.

## Summary

This PRISMA-registered systematic review from the Cracow/Warsaw group (Polish National Medical Institute) asked which treatments are actually used for **recurrent** temporomandibular joint dislocation in patients under 18, and how effective they are — the second step after acute reduction. From 268 records in BASE, PubMed and Scopus, 9 studies survived screening: 5 case reports, 3 case series and 1 case–control study, covering 10 tabulated paediatric patients (10 months to 17 years), graded on the Oxford CEBM scale with only 4 publications above level 5. Conservative and minimally invasive methods dominate, with botulinum toxin type A injected into the lateral pterygoid the most commonly reported minimally invasive option and autologous blood injection close behind; invasive treatment appears in just 2 of 9 studies — one eminectomy and one Dautrey's procedure — both deployed as last resort after conservative and injection therapy had failed, and both recurrence-free at follow-up (6 months; 36 and 28 months). Outcomes were reported inconsistently: pre-treatment dislocation frequency reached >40 episodes in 2 weeks in one child and >20 in 2 months in a 26-month-old, while post-treatment series reached 33–36 months without recurrence in three patients but relapsed at 4 and again 4 months after repeat injections in another; only dislocation frequency was reported consistently across studies, and the pre-specified mandibular-opening-path measure was measured by none. The authors conclude that conservative and minimally invasive approaches should be first line in children on risk and psychological-tolerance grounds and that multidisciplinary involvement (psychology, neurology) recurs in the resolved cases, but they explicitly decline to draw comparative conclusions and state the current literature does **not** support treating age alone as a contraindication to surgery.

## Key Contributions

- **First PRISMA-registered count of the paediatric recurrent-dislocation literature**: 9 usable studies in 25 years (2000–2025), explicitly described by the authors as "an unsatisfactory level of scientific attention."
- **BoNT-A into the lateral pterygoid named as the paediatric first-choice injection**, with autologous blood injection a close second — the muscle-level rationale is the same one that BoNT literature and lateral-pterygoid ultrasound work rest on.
- **Bounds the surgical question instead of assuming it**: invasive treatment in 2/9 studies, both last-resort, both recurrence-free — and the authors state age alone cannot be treated as a contraindication to operative treatment in children, which is the opposite of the intuition that children are too young for eminectomy.
- **Documents the paediatric sedation/anaesthesia layer around reduction**: nitrous oxide before repositioning or BoNT injection (Mohan, Sicard, Stark), light IV sedation (French), propofol (Sicard), local anaesthetic before arthrocentesis or reduction (Coser, Stark) — including one spontaneous reduction under propofol sedation alone.
- **Sets an outcome-measure standard**: dislocation frequency, acoustic symptoms, mandibular opening path, pain intensity and range of jaw abduction should all be reported before and after treatment; opening path was in fact measured by none of the 9 studies and had to be dropped from the analysis.
- **Flags an appraisal-instrument artefact**: Cohen's κ ≈ 0.10 despite ~78% reviewer response overlap, because both reviewers over-selected "Yes"; PABAK 0.56 and AC1 Gweta 0.71 recover moderate-to-high concordance.
- **Reports no ankylosis after autologous blood injection** on clinical and radiographic follow-up, relevant to the child-specific fear of joint fusion.

## Methodology

- **Design**: systematic review, PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420251139493 — tabulation and narrative synthesis, **no meta-analysis**.
- **Search**: BASE, PubMed, Scopus on 21 September 2025 → 102 + 96 + 70 = **268 records** → 168 after deduplication → 34 full texts → 24 excluded (Table A2) + 1 unobtainable → **9 included**.
- **Eligibility (PICOS)**: patients <18 with recurrent TMJ dislocation; any treatment; any or no comparator; ≥1 of five pre/post measures (dislocation frequency, acoustic symptoms, mandibular opening path, pain intensity, range of abduction); 2000–2025 journal articles. Excluded cadaver/animal studies, unclear diagnosis or undefined treatment, dislocation managed as part of broader therapy, preprints, conference papers, pre-2000.
- **Designs**: 5 case reports, 3 case series, 1 case–control; Oxford CEBM 2011 levels — one level 3, three level 4, five level 5. No controlled comparison of any two methods exists.
- **Screening**: Rayyan, two independent reviewers (K.C., A.H.) at title/abstract and full-text stages, third author (M.C.) as adjudicator.
- **Risk of bias**: Joanna Briggs Institute checklists per design, two reviewers (A.H., M.C.), agreement via % agreement, Cohen's κ, PABAK, AC1 Gweta.

### Context cited

- TMD prevalence up to 31% adults / 11% children; projected 44% globally and 37% in Europe by 2050.
- Most TMJ dislocations are themselves recurrent — 62.5% (Papoutsis 2018) and 61.9% (Tarhio 2023).
- Paediatric joint hypermobility affects 8–39% of school-aged children, benign form peaking at ages 3–10.
- Predisposing factors: Ehlers-Danlos and other connective-tissue disorders, neuromuscular disease, seizure/involuntary-movement disorders, chronic corticosteroids, and — in children — trauma.
- No radiographic or functional ankylosis after autologous blood injection.
