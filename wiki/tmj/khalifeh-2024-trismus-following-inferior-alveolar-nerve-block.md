## Three-line Summary

A 59-year-old woman developed severe trismus (18 mm opening) within 12 hours of two IANB injections and recovered fully (42 mm) by day 50 through a structured program of jaw exercises, NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, and corticosteroid injections. The case documents six mechanisms by which IANB can cause trismus — medial pterygoid penetration, hematoma, infection, myotoxicity, barbed needle injury, and volume-induced pressure — and shows that escalating physical therapy after day 12 was the primary driver of recovery. Early, sustained jaw exercise is the cornerstone of management; delayed treatment risks scar tissue formation and chronic restriction.

## Summary

Khalifeh et al. (2024) report a 59-year-old female who developed right-sided facial oedema and trismus (interincisal opening 18 mm) 12 hours after two IANB injections (~3.4 cc of 2% lidocaine with 1:100,000 epinephrine) for restorative treatment of tooth 47. Lateral movements were asymmetrically restricted (9 mm right, 3 mm left). Palpation revealed pain at the TMJ and medial pterygoid, consistent with muscle-origin trismus rather than joint pathology.

Management was staged over 50 days. Early conservative measures (warm application, jaw exercises, oral NSAIDs, muscle relaxant, venotonic) resolved oedema but produced no improvement in opening; intramuscular NSAID plus corticosteroid injections followed (with an antibiotic added at day 10 for suspected infection), and opening actually worsened to 13 mm — attributed to forced exercises antagonizing healing muscle. Increasing the frequency of physical exercises from day 12 onward generated steady gains: 19 mm by day 14, 28 mm by day 30, and 42 mm (full recovery) by day 50.

## Key Contributions

- Granular day-by-day management protocol over a 50-day arc — uncommonly detailed for IANB complication literature
- Six-mechanism taxonomy for IANB-induced trismus with distinct pathophysiological rationale for each
- Illustrates paradox: forced early jaw exercise worsened opening (day 8–12), while graduated escalation drove recovery (day 12–50)
- Contextualizes dexamethasone evidence: effective for post-extraction oedema, mixed evidence for trismus specifically; submucosal route cited as superior to IM (Kaewkumnert et al.)
- Highlights that chronic trismus = scar tissue → irreversible; early management window is critical

## Methodology

- **Design**: Single case report
- **Patient**: 59-year-old female, unremarkable medical history
- **Index procedure**: IANB × 2, ~3.4 cc Persocaine® (2% lidocaine + 1:100,000 epinephrine), tooth 47 restorative treatment
- **Outcome measures**: Interincisal opening (mm), lateral movements, oedema status, palpation pain

### Six Mechanisms of IANB-Related Trismus

1. **Medial pterygoid penetration** — accidental intramuscular needle placement → reflex spasm
2. **Intramuscular hematoma** — inferior alveolar vessel injury → pterygomandibular space hematoma → mechanical restriction
3. **Needle-track infection** — bacterial seeding along needle path; delayed onset 2–3 days post-injection
4. **Local anesthetic myotoxicity** — direct cytotoxic effect of anesthetic agent on muscle fibers
5. **Barbed needle tip** — repeated insertion/withdrawal tears muscle fibers mechanically
6. **Large volume injection** — excess solution in confined pterygomandibular space → pressure-induced tissue damage

### Prognosis Framework

- **Acute iatrogenic trismus**: typically resolves in 2–6 weeks (range 4–20 weeks) with appropriate management
- **Risk of chronicity**: inadequate early management → scar tissue → progressive fibrotic restriction → requires aggressive physiotherapy, pharmacotherapy, or surgical intervention
