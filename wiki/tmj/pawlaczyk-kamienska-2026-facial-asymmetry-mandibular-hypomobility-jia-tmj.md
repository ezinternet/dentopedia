## Three-line Summary

Case-based narrative review (CABARET-format, single author) synthesizing 30 studies on TMJ involvement in juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA), anchored on an illustrative case: a 9-year-old girl with 5-year polyarticular JIA and no reported TMJ symptoms, found on examination to have facial asymmetry, mandibular deviation, and restricted mouth opening, with CBCT showing unilateral condylar flattening and a shortened mandibular ramus.

Reported TMJ involvement prevalence in JIA ranges 17%–over 90% depending on diagnostic method; clinical symptoms alone are unreliable (some cited studies found no MMO difference between JIA patients with vs. without TMJ arthritis), while facial asymmetry (23–41%) and mandibular deviation (up to 62%) are reported more consistently as objective correlates of radiographic damage; the case's own maximal mouth opening (MMO) was 30 mm (normal ≥35 mm under age 10), deviation 5 mm to the right (pathological ≥2 mm), and CBCT scored 3/4 on Billiau's grading scale for the affected condyle vs. 0 for the unaffected side.

Clinically, the paper argues facial asymmetry and mandibular hypomobility should be treated as primary screening red flags for TMJ involvement in any child with JIA regardless of symptoms, since MRI (the gold standard for active synovitis) is often inaccessible; but as a single-case narrative review with no risk-of-bias assessment, it supports awareness of signs, not diagnostic accuracy — that question belongs to the companion paper [[tmj/chana-2026-ultrasound-tmj-juvenile-idiopathic-arthritis-sr]].

## 세줄요약

CABARET 형식의 케이스 기반 서술적 문헌고찰(단독 저자, 30편 인용)로, 다낭관절형 소아 특발성 관절염(Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis, JIA) 5년 병력의 9세 여아 사례를 중심으로 종합 — 측두하악관절(Temporomandibular Joint, TMJ) 관련 증상은 없었으나 진찰상 안면비대칭·하악 편위·개구 제한이 있었고, CBCT에서 편측 과두 편평화와 하악지 단축이 확인됨. JIA의 TMJ 침범은 무증상인 경우가 많다는 것이 이 논문의 핵심 논지다.

문헌상 JIA의 TMJ 침범 유병률은 진단기준·영상기법·이환기간에 따라 17%~90% 이상으로 다양하게 보고되며, 임상 증상만으로는 진단 신뢰도가 낮음(일부 인용 연구는 TMJ 관절염 유무에 따른 최대개구량(Maximal Mouth Opening, MMO) 차이가 없다고 보고); 반면 안면비대칭(23~41%)과 하악 편위(최대 62%)는 방사선학적 손상과 비교적 일관되게 연관되는 객관적 소견으로 보고됨. 본 사례에서는 MMO 30mm(10세 미만 정상 ≥35mm), 우측 편위 5mm(병적 기준 ≥2mm), 측방운동 좌측 2mm/우측 7mm(정상 ≥6mm), 전방운동 1mm(정상 ≥4mm)가 측정됐고, CBCT상 이환측 과두는 Billiau 등급 3(편평화+피질하골 결손), 비이환측은 0(정상)이었으며 이환측 하악지 수직고경 감소가 확인됨.

MRI(활동성 활막염 진단의 표준)에 대한 접근성이 제한적인 현실에서, 이 논문은 증상 유무와 무관하게 안면비대칭과 하악 운동제한을 JIA 환아의 1차 선별 적신호로 취급해야 한다고 주장한다. 다만 단일 사례 기반 서술적 문헌고찰(비뚤림위험평가 없음)이므로 유병률·진단정확도 주장의 근거로는 부적합하고 '징후 인지'의 근거로만 활용해야 하며, 진단정확도(초음파 vs MRI/CBCT) 질문은 같은 배치의 동반논문 [[tmj/chana-2026-ultrasound-tmj-juvenile-idiopathic-arthritis-sr]]이 담당한다.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this case-based narrative review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.5114/reum/214399); PMID 42306289; PMCID PMC13267334) examines temporomandibular joint (TMJ) involvement in juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA), a manifestation the authors frame as "often underrecognized" because of a frequent discrepancy between clinical symptoms and radiologic findings. Written per the CABARET (CAse-BAsed REview sTandards) format by a single author from the Department of Dental Traumatology, Chair of Pediatric Dentistry, Poznan University of Medical Sciences, the paper combines a synthesis of 30 prior studies with one illustrative index case: a 9-year-old girl with a 5-year history of polyarticular JIA who had no reported TMJ symptoms — no pain, no history of trauma or masticatory dysfunction — yet on structured clinical examination showed facial asymmetry, a 5 mm mandibular deviation on opening, a reduced maximal mouth opening (MMO) of 30 mm, and markedly restricted lateral excursion and protrusive movement. Cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) confirmed unilateral condylar flattening and subcortical bone defects (Billiau grading scale score 3, vs. 0 on the unaffected side) and a shortened mandibular ramus on the affected side, consistent with impaired unilateral condylar growth. The paper's central clinical argument is that because the mandibular condyle is a growth centre adjacent to the synovial membrane, chronic inflammation there can silently disrupt craniofacial development — producing asymmetry, retrognathia, or anterior open bite — well before pain or joint sounds appear, and that a dentist performing routine morphofunctional screening (facial-symmetry photography, MMO/deviation/excursion/protrusion measurement) is often better positioned than a rheumatologist to catch it. Because this is a single-case narrative review with no formal risk-of-bias assessment, it should be read as evidence for *awareness of clinical signs*, not as evidence for prevalence or diagnostic accuracy of any specific sign or imaging modality.

## Key Contributions

- First wiki page addressing TMJ involvement in juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA) — closes a category gap across the wiki's TMJ pages, none of which previously addressed JIA.
- Frames TMJ arthritis in JIA as frequently "silent": structural/radiographic damage (condylar flattening, ramus shortening) can be present without pain, crepitus, or subjective complaint, and symptom presence/absence correlates poorly with radiographic severity across the cited literature.
- Positions facial asymmetry and mandibular deviation/hypomobility — not pain — as the practical, low-cost screening red flags a general or pediatric dentist can pick up at a routine visit, ahead of specialist imaging referral.
- Documents a concrete diagnostic example: an asymptomatic 9-year-old with unilateral CBCT damage (Billiau grade 3 vs. 0) detected purely through morphofunctional examination (facial photograph landmarking + MMO/deviation/lateral-excursion/protrusion measurement with a ruler and pencil-line technique).
- Explicitly names the mandibular condyle as a growth centre: unilateral inflammation there produces asymmetric ramus growth (measured condylion-to-gonion), tying TMJ screening to craniofacial developmental outcomes, not just joint pain management.
- Cites the standardized Clinical Orofacial Examination (COFE) protocol (Stoustrup et al.) as a structured tool for incorporating this screening into routine rheumatology follow-up.

## Methodology

Case-based narrative review developed per the CABARET framework — literature synthesis interpreted through a single representative clinical case, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. Literature search across PubMed (MEDLINE), Scopus, and Web of Science for studies published up to June 2025, using combinations of "juvenile idiopathic arthritis," "temporomandibular joint," "TMJ," "arthritis," "facial asymmetry," "mandibular mobility," and "CBCT." Inclusion: original studies in JIA patients with clinical and/or radiological TMJ assessment, population under 20 years, English full text. Exclusion: reviews/meta-analyses/editorials/conference abstracts, adult-only (≥20y) populations, studies unrelated to TMJ arthritis in JIA. Selection was performed by the sole author (non-independent, no second reviewer); 30 studies were included in the narrative synthesis; reference-list snowballing yielded no additional eligible articles. No formal risk-of-bias assessment was conducted, consistent with the narrative (not systematic) character of the review.

For the index case: a 9-year-old girl with 5-year polyarticular JIA history was examined despite reporting no TMJ symptoms. Informed consent was obtained from the legal guardian; the study protocol was approved by the Ethical Committee of Poznan University of Medical Sciences (No. 1255/18), per the Declaration of Helsinki. Examination protocol: en-face photograph with anatomical landmarks (trichion, ophryon, pronasale, subnasale, gnathion) for facial-symmetry assessment; palpation of masticatory muscles and TMJ for tenderness and joint sounds; MMO measured twice with a millimeter ruler between upper/lower central incisor incisal edges; mandibular deviation and lateral excursions measured via pencil-line midline tracking from centric occlusion; protrusive movement measured as anterior mandibular translation from centric occlusion; CBCT performed for bony morphometric analysis, graded per Billiau's TMJ radiographic grading scale. No MRI was performed on this patient.

## Results

**Literature-reported prevalence and clinical-sign rates (as cited by this review, not this paper's own primary data):**

| Source (as cited) | Population/measure | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| — (general) | TMJ involvement in JIA | Prevalence 17% to >90%, depending on diagnostic criteria/imaging/duration/population |
| Savioli et al. | Helkimo Index | TMJ dysfunction in 94% of JIA patients; ~half grade II/III (moderate–severe); only 30% grade I (mild) |
| Mohamed et al. | Symptom/sign survey | TMJ-related symptoms in 35%; pain 17.5%; crepitus 7.5%; clinical signs 62%; reduced MMO most common sign (25%) |
| Leksell et al. | Symptom survey | Pain at rest 22%; pain during chewing 71%; pain during mouth opening 68%; reduced MMO in 32% |
| Pawlaczyk-Kamieńska et al. (prior study, cited) | Unilateral TMJ arthritis cohort | Reduced MMO in 58%; facial asymmetry in 23%; mandibular deviation >2 mm in 46.15% (mostly toward the more-affected joint) |
| Stoll et al.; Koss et al. | MMO comparison | No significant MMO difference between JIA patients with vs. without TMJ arthritis (limited diagnostic specificity of MMO alone) |
| Collin et al. | CBCT | TMJ deformities in 39% of JIA patients; significantly associated with reduced MMO |
| Keller et al. | Facial asymmetry/deviation | Facial asymmetry in 37–41% of JIA patients (vs. healthy controls); mandibular deviation during opening in up to 62% (vs. 16% in controls) |
| Müller et al.; Weiss et al. | MRI | TMJ involvement detection rate up to 80% |
| Jank et al. | Conventional ultrasound | Disc displacement visualized in 55.5% of examined TMJs |
| Assaf et al.; Tonni et al. | High-resolution ultrasound (HR-US) | Enhanced visualization of synovial width/condylar irregularities; correlated with MRI inflammation findings, but SRs flag limited sensitivity for early synovitis and heterogeneous protocols |

Proposed clinical marker (cited): MMO <40 mm as a sensitive indicator of structural TMJ involvement. Cited correlation pattern: mandibular/chin deviation direction corresponds to the side with more radiographic damage; earlier disease onset associates with greater restriction of lateral movement toward the less-affected side; no association with disease duration itself; MMO positively correlated with mandibular protrusion. No direct association was found (cited prior study) between severity of condylar radiographic damage and presence/severity of clinical symptoms — some patients with major structural deformity were asymptomatic, others with mild structural change were symptomatic.

**Index case (this paper's own data — 9-year-old girl, 5-year polyarticular JIA, no reported TMJ symptoms):**

- MMO: 30 mm (reduced; normal ≥35 mm for children <10 years)
- Mandibular deviation on opening: 5 mm to the right (pathological threshold ≥2 mm)
- Lateral excursion: 2 mm left, 7 mm right (normal ≥6 mm) — restricted left-side mobility
- Protrusive movement: 1 mm (normal ≥4 mm) — markedly restricted
- Palpation: no tenderness, no joint sounds (no clicking/crepitus)
- CBCT: right condyle — flattening + subcortical bone defects, Billiau's TMJ radiographic grading scale score 3; left condyle — preserved morphology and cortical integrity, score 0; right mandibular ramus — reduced vertical height (condylion-to-gonion) vs. contralateral side, consistent with impaired unilateral growth
- Diagnosis: unilateral TMJ arthritis, "silent" morphofunctional presentation
- No MRI was performed, so active synovitis could not be confirmed or excluded — only chronic structural damage was documented via CBCT.

**Limitations:** single-case narrative review — cannot support prevalence, sensitivity/specificity, or generalizability claims; single non-independent reviewer selecting the cited literature with no formal risk-of-bias assessment; no MRI on the index case; no long-term follow-up of the case; findings may not generalize across JIA subtypes (this is a polyarticular case) or disease stages.
