## Three-line Summary
The paper describes an educational innovation: construction of an inexpensive skull-based biomechanical model to teach/practice mandible reduction techniques for temporomandibular joint dislocation.
The model uses a plastic skull, elastic string, resistance bands, and simple hardware to simulate the joint and muscle resistance; the total parts cost reported is $67 (USD) with a linked video demonstration.
The clinical implication is that hands-on practice with a trainer can improve understanding and execution of techniques (including wrist pivot and extraoral approaches) that are difficult to learn from static images, though the model itself has no patient data or outcome validation.

## Summary
Tummings et al. (2023) present an inexpensive biomechanical model to facilitate teaching and learning of newer mandible reduction techniques for acute temporomandibular joint dislocation. The model is constructed from a plastic skull, elastic string, resistance bands, and hardware, with reported parts cost of $67 USD. It allows practice of conventional/sequential reduction, the wrist pivot method, and the extraoral technique, with demonstration videos provided. The letter argues that hands-on practice improves understanding of hand positioning and force application for techniques difficult to learn from static images; however, it provides no patient outcome data and no controlled educational validation.

## Key Contributions
- Describes a low-cost, reproducible bench model for mandible reduction training.
- Explicitly supports practice of wrist pivot and extraoral methods in addition to conventional/sequential approaches.
- Provides construct details (drill locations, elastic string through condyle/articular fossa, resistance band placement) and links to demonstration video.

## Methodology
Educational/technical letter describing an inert physical bench model (no living subjects). Materials: plastic skull, elastic string, resistance bands, toggle stoppers, drywall screws, nut/fender washer. Techniques demonstrated: conventional, sequential, wrist pivot, extraoral.

## Results
- Total parts cost: $67 USD.
- Model enabled practice of sequential reduction and visualization of force direction in the extraoral technique and condyle disengagement in the wrist pivot method.
- Feasibility demonstrated in an authors' practice session.
