## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO-registered) of 8 studies (2 RCTs, 1 prospective cohort, 2 retrospective/matched cohorts, 2 cross-sectional, 1 SR; n=42 to >34,000) examining dental extraction (predominantly third molar) versus non-extraction/pre-extraction controls on TMJ pain/TMD outcomes.

Pooled meta-analysis of 6 studies showed extraction was associated with a significant reduction in TMJ pain/symptoms (RR/OR=0.49, 95% CI 0.31-0.66, p<0.00001, I2=34%), i.e., extraction patients were ~51% less likely to have TMJ pain than non-extraction controls, though certainty of evidence was low-to-moderate (GRADE).

Evidence is dominated by third molar extraction (not orthodontic premolar extraction, for which data were minimal/absent); therapeutic extraction (e.g., supraerupted/interfering molars in TMD patients) improved symptoms, while prophylactic third molar removal caused common but usually transient short-term postoperative morbidity (trismus 92%, myofascial pain 69% at 1 week in one RCT).

## 세줄요약

PRISMA 2020 기반 체계적 문헌고찰+메타분석(PROSPERO 등록)으로, 발치(주로 제3대구치)와 TMJ 통증/TMD 증상의 관계를 다룬 8편(RCT 2, 전향적 코호트 1, 후향적/매칭 코호트 2, 단면연구 2, SR 1편; n=42~34,000명 이상)을 분석했다.

6편의 정량 메타분석 결과 발치군이 비발치군 대비 TMJ 통증/증상 위험이 유의하게 낮았다(pooled RR/OR=0.49, 95% CI 0.31-0.66, p<0.00001, I2=34%) — 발치군이 약 51% 덜 통증을 경험, 다만 GRADE 근거수준은 low-to-moderate.

근거 대부분이 교정 소구치 발치가 아닌 제3대구치 발치에 편중되어 있으며, 치료적 발치(TMD 환자의 과맹출/간섭 대구치 제거)는 증상을 개선시킨 반면 예방적 제3대구치 발치는 흔하지만 대개 일시적인 단기 술후 이환(1주차 개구제한 92%, 근막통 69%, 한 RCT 기준)을 동반했다.

## Summary

This PRISMA 2020-compliant, PROSPERO-registered systematic review + meta-analysis asks a long-debated question in TMD management: does dental extraction help or harm TMJ pain? The authors carefully separate two distinct clinical narratives that are often conflated — the historical orthodontic debate over **premolar extraction** and TMD, versus the empirically richer literature on **third molar extraction** and TMJ outcomes. They find that essentially all usable comparative evidence (8 studies meeting inclusion criteria, 6 pooled quantitatively) concerns third molar extraction; orthodontic premolar extraction has no direct RCT evidence in this review.

The headline quantitative result is a pooled RR/OR of 0.49 (95% CI 0.31-0.66, p<0.00001, I2=34%) — extraction patients were about 51% less likely to report TMJ pain/symptoms than non-extraction controls. This runs counter to several earlier reviews (e.g., Damasceno et al., pooled OR 1.81-2.15) that concluded extraction increases TMD risk. The reversal is driven largely by inclusion of therapeutic-extraction data (Gururaj et al.: 96% pain reduction after removing supraerupted/distoverted molars in TMD patients), which the authors argue represents a fundamentally different clinical scenario from routine/prophylactic third molar removal.

## Key Contributions

- Explicit methodological distinction between orthodontic premolar extraction (evidence-poor) and third molar extraction (evidence-rich) in the TMJ-pain literature — a categorization not always made clear in prior reviews.
- Quantitative synthesis reversing the direction of several earlier SRs: pooled RR/OR=0.49 favors extraction reducing TMJ pain, driven by therapeutic-extraction contexts.
- Separates prophylactic (routine/asymptomatic) from therapeutic (established-TMD, interfering-tooth) extraction indications, showing opposite short-term risk profiles.
- Full RoB toolkit applied per design (RoB 2.0 / ROBINS-I / NOS) plus GRADE certainty rating.

## Methodology

PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420250655883. Databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane Library, Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar, ClinicalTrials.gov, CTRI-India, searched to August 2025. Inclusion: human studies comparing extraction (third molar or other) to non-extraction/pre-extraction controls with TMJ pain/TMD outcome measures; exclusion: TMD surgical-management studies (arthrocentesis/arthroscopy/joint replacement), case reports/series. Two independent reviewers, two-stage screening, third-reviewer arbitration. RoB 2.0 (RCTs) / ROBINS-I (non-randomized) / NOS (observational); GRADE overall certainty. Random-effects meta-analysis (RevMan Web) for dichotomous outcomes as RR with 95% CI, with effect-direction standardization so RR/OR<1 = favors extraction (less pain); heterogeneity via chi-square/I2; sensitivity analysis excluding high-RoB studies; publication-bias assessment planned only if ≥10 pooled studies (not reached — 6 studies pooled, so not performed).

## Results

- 8 studies included (2 RCT, 1 prospective cohort, 2 retrospective/matched cohort, 2 cross-sectional, 1 SR); n=42 to >34,000; follow-up baseline-only to 6 years.
- 6/8 primarily third molar extraction; 1 therapeutic extraction of supraerupted maxillary third molars in TMD patients; no RCT specifically on orthodontic premolar extraction.
- **Pooled (6 studies): RR/OR=0.49 (95% CI 0.31-0.66), Z=5.49, p<0.00001, I2=34%, tau2=0.01.** Top weight: Huang and Rue (29.5%), Gururaj et al. (27.6%), Barbosa et al. (25.4%).
- Gururaj et al.: 96% pain reduction after therapeutic extraction (p<0.05); reduced clicking, headache, neck pain.
- Munawar et al.: short-term worsening — myofascial pain 69% and trismus 92% at 1 week post third-molar surgery, some persisting to 6 months (p<0.005); low meta-analytic weight (<5%) but highest point estimate risk (RR=5.00).
- Huang et al.: RR 1.4 (95% CI 0.9-2.2, NS). Huang and Rue: adjusted RR 1.6 (95% CI 1.3-2.0, significant; ~23% of TMD attributable to extraction in that cohort).
- Barbosa et al. (n=1,381, RDC/TMD criteria): OR 1.30-2.15 across subgroups (p=0.041-0.006) — history of third molar removal associated with higher odds of TMD symptoms.
- Uppgaard et al. (RCT, mandibular support device vs manual): no functional difference by support method; full impaction predicted higher pain (OR 3.44, p=0.004); low RoB, >95% retention.
- Prior SR by Damasceno et al. (7 studies): OR 1.81-2.15, RR ~2.1 for TMD after extraction — direction opposite to this review's pooled result.
- GRADE certainty: low-to-moderate for most outcomes.
