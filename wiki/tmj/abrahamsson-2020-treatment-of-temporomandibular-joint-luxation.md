## Three-line Summary

Systematic review without meta-analysis, restricted to randomized controlled trials, searching PubMed, Cochrane Library and Web of Science from database inception to 26 March 2018: 113 unique abstracts → 9 full texts → 8 RCTs with 338 patients (3 studies / 185 patients on acute manual reduction; 5 studies / 153 patients on autologous blood or dextrose prolotherapy for recurrent luxation); **zero RCTs on any surgical technique**.

Acute first-try reduction success was 87% conventional intraoral vs 67% extraoral vs 97% wrist pivot (n=90, p<0.01 vs extraoral) and 86.2% vs 55.2% (p=0.009) in a second trial; a supine technique reduced operating time from 269±70 s to 173±17 s and pain (VAS) from 8.1±0.7 to 6.3±0.8. For recurrent luxation, autologous blood injection into the superior joint space **plus** pericapsular tissue with 4 weeks of intermaxillary fixation gave the largest interincisal reduction (11 mm vs 9 mm IMF vs 8 mm injection alone, p<0.0001) and **0/16 recurrences**, versus 8/16 with injection alone.

Best-supported regimen for recurrence is autologous blood injection plus intermaxillary fixation, and manual reduction is first-line for acute episodes — but the whole surgical literature (eminectomy and variants) rests on case reports, 6 of 8 trials are at moderate risk of bias, no follow-up exceeds 12 months against a 36-month recurrence-free benchmark, and dextrose prolotherapy showed no consistent superiority over placebo.

## Summary

Abrahamsson, Eriksson, Abrahamsson and Häggman-Henrikson (Malmö/Halmstad/Umeå, Sweden) systematically reviewed randomized controlled trials on the treatment of temporomandibular joint (TMJ) luxation, searching PubMed, the Cochrane Library and Web of Science from each database's inception to an update on 26 March 2018. Of 113 unique articles, 9 were read in full and 8 RCTs (338 patients) met the criteria — three studies (185 patients) addressing acute manual reduction and five studies (153 patients) addressing minimally invasive injection for recurrent luxation, with **no trial at all evaluating a surgical technique**. For acute episodes, first-try reduction success ranged from 55–67% for the extraoral approach to 86–87% for the conventional intraoral method and 97% for the wrist pivot method, and a supine positioning technique shortened reduction time and reduced pain, though all three studies measured only acute, short-term outcomes. For recurrent luxation, the best-performing regimen was autologous blood injection into the superior joint space *and* pericapsular tissue combined with 4 weeks of intermaxillary fixation (11 mm interincisal reduction, 0/16 recurrences at 1 year), while injection alone had the highest recurrence rate (8/16); dextrose prolotherapy was not superior to placebo in any of the three trials that included a placebo arm. The authors conclude that autologous blood injection plus intermaxillary fixation is the treatment for recurrent luxation with the best current scientific support, that manual repositioning is first choice for acute luxation, and that the evidence base for eminectomy and other surgical procedures remains limited to case reports and non-randomised cohorts — none of the included studies evaluated quality of life, six of eight were at moderate risk of bias, and no follow-up exceeded 12 months against a proposed 36-month recurrence-free benchmark.

## Key Contributions

- **The only evidence-tiering of the TMJ-luxation treatment literature.** Across roughly 50 years of publications only 8 RCTs (338 patients) survive basic methodological criteria, and none concerns surgery — quantifying, for the first time, how thin the evidence is for a condition that is described as one of the most severe in dentistry.
- **Establishes the acute-reduction comparator set**: conventional intraoral 86–87%, extraoral 55–67%, wrist pivot 97%, plus a supine variant that halves operating time and lowers pain — the reference values against which any new acute technique must be judged.
- **Names the single best-supported preventive regimen**: autologous blood injection into the superior joint space *plus* pericapsular tissue, combined with 4 weeks of intermaxillary fixation (0/16 recurrences, 11 mm opening gain) — the highest success rate in the review.
- **Refutes the prolotherapy-for-luxation claim on controlled evidence**: across three placebo-armed trials (Mustafa 2018, Cömert Kiliç 2016, Refai 2011) dextrose was not consistently superior to placebo for mouth opening, pain or luxation frequency, and no concentration outperformed another.
- **Quantifies repeat-injection escalation**: 6/16 recurrences after the first injection, 2/16 after the second, 0/16 after the third (Hegab 2013) — repeated autologous blood injection may be a viable strategy before surgery.
- **Documents the surgical fall-out pathway that has never been followed up**: in Daif 2010, 6/15 superior-joint-space-only and 3/15 superior-space-plus-pericapsular patients recurred and proceeded to surgery after refusing further injections, and no surgical outcomes were reported.
- **Explains the evidence gap mechanistically**: rarity of recurrent luxation prevents recruitment, and sham-surgical controls are ethically questionable — the same dilemma the knee arthroscopy placebo RCT illustrates.

## Methodology

- **Design**: systematic literature review of **randomized controlled trials only**; no meta-analysis and no pooled effect estimates.
- **Databases**: PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science — inception to 31 October 2015, updated 26 March 2018. PubMed funnel: TMJ/MeSH terms 26,047 → luxation/subluxation/dislocation/open lock/hypermobility/JHS 73,406 → combined 3,203 → RCT filter 718,112 → **93 hits**. Reference lists hand-searched; grey literature excluded; authors not contacted for missing data.
- **Eligibility**: clinical RCTs in English, Swedish or German on patients diagnosed with TMJ luxation. Excluded: non-RCT designs, dual publication, TMJ fracture studies, and studies with **fewer than 10 patients**.
- **Selection**: two reviewers (HA, BHH) independently screened titles/abstracts and resolved disagreement by discussion → 113 unique → 9 full texts → 1 excluded (Sato et al.) → **8 included**.
- **Extraction**: HA extracted, BHH reviewed — setting, eligibility criteria, n, age/sex, dropouts, treatment method, follow-up, results, authors' conclusions.
- **Quality appraisal**: SBU (Swedish Agency for Health Technology Assessment) RCT tool, modified by merging both Cochrane Risk of Bias domains and adding a conflict-of-interest item under "other risks of bias" → **6 moderate / 2 low** risk of bias.
- **Outcomes**: first-try successful reduction, reduction time, pain (VAS), maximal mouth opening / interincisal distance, recurrence frequency, radiographic bony change. Quality of life was **not** measured by any included study.

### Cross-cutting findings

- **Pain**: no significant between-group differences at follow-up in the three studies that measured it.
- **Recurrence is injection-site dependent**: injecting pericapsular tissue in addition to the superior joint space roughly halves recurrence (60% → 20% in Daif 2010), and only that group retained the condyle within the fossa boundary on open-mouth radiographs.
- **Repeat injection escalates**: 6/16 → 2/16 → 0/16 recurrences after first, second and third autologous blood injections (Hegab 2013), with only 12 months of follow-up.
- **Epidemiology quoted**: two emergency departments with 100,000 combined annual visits averaged **5.3 luxation cases per year over 7 years**; TMJ luxation is **3% of all reported dislocated joints in the body**; bilateral, straight-open presentation is most common.
- **Unevidenced options named**: jaw exercises (moderate effect claimed, weak evidence) and botulinum toxin into masseter and pterygoid muscles (no RCTs, no guideline consensus).
