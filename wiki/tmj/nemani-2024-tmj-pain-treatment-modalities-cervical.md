## Three-line Summary

RCT, n=48 (12/group), chronic cervical + TMJ pain patients at SRM Dental College randomized to control, soft splint, TENS, or low-level laser (LLL), with occlusal equilibration in all groups except control, followed 12 months.

TMJ pain VAS at final follow-up was significantly lower with TENS (1.05±1.39) and LLL (0.87±0.89) vs. control (3.97±0.98) and soft splint (4.14±1.21) (Tukey HSD p<0.001 for those pairs); TENS vs. LLL and control vs. splint were not significantly different; Pearson correlation between TMJ and cervical pain reduction was weak (r range -0.452 to 0.336).

TENS and LLLT are equally effective and superior to soft splints for reducing TMJ pain over 1 year, but no meaningful correlation was found between TMJ pain relief and cervical pain relief — treating the jaw joint does not reliably resolve neck pain.

## 세줄요약

RCT, n=48 (군당 12명), SRM 치과대학에서 만성 경부통(chronic cervical pain) 및 TMJ 통증 동반 환자를 대상으로 대조군·soft splint·TENS·저출력레이저(LLL) 4군에 무작위 배정(대조군 제외 전 군 교합조정 병행), 12개월 추적.

최종 추적 시 TMJ 통증 VAS는 TENS(1.05±1.39)와 LLL(0.87±0.89)이 대조군(3.97±0.98)·splint(4.14±1.21) 대비 유의하게 낮음(Tukey HSD p<0.001); TENS vs LLL, 대조군 vs splint는 유의차 없음; TMJ-경부통 상관관계는 미약(Pearson r -0.452~0.336).

TENS와 LLLT는 동등하게 효과적이며 soft splint보다 TMJ 통증 감소에 우월하지만, TMJ 통증 개선이 경부통 개선과 유의한 상관을 보이지 않아 턱관절 치료만으로 경부통 해소를 기대하기 어려움.

## Summary

This double-blind, parallel-group RCT enrolled 48 patients with both chronic cervical pain (already under physiotherapy) and RDC/TMD-diagnosed TMJ pain at SRM Dental College, Ramapuram (screened from a 200-patient reference population). Patients were randomized 1:1:1:1 into four groups (n=12 each):

- **Control**: cervical physiotherapy alone, monthly for 1 year
- **Soft splint**: occlusal equilibration + 2 mm vacuum-formed thermoplastic soft splint, worn ~8 h/night
- **TENS**: occlusal equilibration + transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (30 min/session, up to 80 Hz, 25 mA)
- **LLL (low-level laser)**: occlusal equilibration + 660 nm GaAlAs diode laser, 1 min/point at 3 TMJ-region sites

TMJ pain and cervical pain were tracked with VAS monthly over 12 months, with thermographic imaging (Fluke thermal camera) as an adjunct objective marker pre/post treatment. The study also tested whether TMJ pain relief correlates with cervical pain relief.

## Key Contributions

- One of the few TMD conservative-therapy RCTs with a full 12-month follow-up (vs. the more common 4-week to 5-month windows, e.g. [[wiki/tmj/salloum-2024-stabilization-splint-ultrasound-myofascial-pain-rct]])
- Head-to-head confirms TENS and LLLT are statistically equivalent and both superior to soft splints and physiotherapy-only control for TMJ pain reduction
- Directly and quantitatively tests (rather than assumes) the cervical spine–TMD comorbidity link via paired VAS + Pearson correlation, and finds it weak/inconsistent (r range -0.452 to 0.336 across groups)
- Adds thermographic imaging as a semi-objective adjunct to subjective VAS scoring for TMD pain assessment

## Methodology

- **Design**: Double-blind (participant + assessor), parallel-group, prospective longitudinal RCT; IRB SRMDC/IRB/2020/MDS/No. 203; CTRI/2021/05/033809
- **n**: 48 (12/group), from 200 screened via RDC/TMD Criterion 3 (pain) + Criterion 15g (occlusal interference); inflated for 20% expected dropout
- **Inclusion**: age 18-60, RDC/TMD-diagnosed TMD, both genders, concurrent chronic cervical pain under physiotherapy
- **Exclusion**: head/face trauma, dental pain resolved by tooth treatment alone, systemic inflammatory bone disease
- **Randomization**: simple randomization, sealed opaque envelopes, allocation concealed
- **Occlusal equilibration**: articulating-paper-guided enameloplasty via Lucia jig + semi-adjustable articulator, applied to all groups except control
- **Outcomes**: VAS (0-10) for TMJ pain and cervical pain (McCaffery & Beebe numeric scale), monthly to 12 months; thermography pre/post
- **Statistics**: repeated-measures ANOVA (within-group trend), one-way ANOVA (between-group), Tukey HSD post hoc, paired t-test (cervical pain before/after), Pearson correlation (TMJ vs. cervical pain)

## Results

**TMJ pain VAS, baseline → 12-month final follow-up (within-group, repeated-measures ANOVA)**:
| Group | Baseline | Final | F | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control | 7.27±1.29 | 3.97±0.98 | 50.900 | 0.001 |
| Soft splint | 7.53±0.70 | 4.14±1.21 | 53.801 | 0.001 |
| TENS | 7.76±0.80 | 1.05±1.39 | 115.554 | 0.001 |
| LLL | 7.61±0.61 | 0.87±0.89 | 423.146 | 0.001 |

**Between-group at final follow-up** (one-way ANOVA F=29.798, p=0.001); Tukey HSD: TENS and LLL each significantly better than control and soft splint (all p=0.001, mean differences 2.92-3.27); TENS vs. LLL not significant; control vs. soft splint not significant.

**Cervical pain**: significant within-group improvement in all four groups (paired t-test, all p=0.001, mean reduction 0.92-1.67 points), but NO significant between-group difference at final follow-up (one-way ANOVA F=2.025, p=0.124) — cervical pain improved similarly regardless of which TMJ intervention was used.

**TMJ-cervical pain correlation** (Pearson, post-treatment): Control r=-0.132, Soft splint r=-0.02, TENS r=0.336, LLL r=-0.452 — weak and directionally inconsistent, supporting the paper's conclusion of no meaningful correlation between TMJ and cervical pain.

**Thermography**: qualitative reduction in "hot" (painful) color signature most visible in TENS and LLL groups post-treatment.
