## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PRISMA-DTA) of 8 diagnostic-accuracy studies (2007–2025; 312 JIA patients, 6 countries) evaluating ultrasound (US) against MRI as the reference standard for temporomandibular joint (TMJ) arthritis in children with Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA); no study used CT or CBCT as reference.

B-mode US sensitivity ranged 21%–85% and specificity 36.4%–89% depending on which TMJ alteration was targeted (condylar changes, joint effusion, synovial thickness, lateral periarticular space); GRADE certainty was LOW for sensitivity and MODERATE for specificity, none of the 8 studies fulfilled all QUADAS-2 criteria, and no meta-analysis was possible due to methodological heterogeneity.

The sensitivity floor is the clinically load-bearing number here — a negative US does not rule out TMJ involvement in JIA, so the authors' own framing (US as a triage/adjunct tool alongside clinical exam, not an MRI replacement) is the correct read; US remains attractive in this population because it needs no ionizing radiation, no sedation, is chairside, and is repeatable in children, where MRI access and sedation are real barriers.

## 세줄요약

JIA(소아 특발성 관절염) 환아의 측두하악관절 (Temporomandibular Joint, TMJ) 관절염을 초음파 (Ultrasound, US)로 평가한 진단정확도 체계적 문헌고찰(PRISMA-DTA), 2007~2025년 8편·312명·6개국, 기준표준은 8편 전부 MRI(CT·CBCT를 기준표준으로 쓴 연구는 없음).

B-mode 초음파 민감도(Sensitivity)는 21~85%, 특이도(Specificity)는 36.4~89%로 평가 대상(과두 변화, 관절 삼출, 활막 비후, 외측 관절주위공간/LPAS)에 따라 편차가 크며, GRADE 확실성 등급은 민감도 LOW·특이도 MODERATE, 8편 모두 QUADAS-2 전 항목을 충족하지 못했고 방법론적 이질성으로 메타분석은 불가능했음.

가장 중요한 임상적 함의는 민감도 하한선 — 초음파 음성 소견이 TMJ 침범을 배제하지 못한다는 것이며, 저자들도 초음파를 MRI 대체가 아닌 임상진찰과 병행하는 선별·보조(triage) 도구로 결론짓는다; 그럼에도 소아 MRI는 진정(sedation)이 필요하고 비용·접근성 문제가 있는 반면 초음파는 무방사선·체어사이드·반복시행이 가능해 이 연령군에서 매력적이다.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this systematic review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/ijod/2825133); PMID 41568132; PMCID PMC12815698) followed the PRISMA-DTA checklist to assess the diagnostic capability of ultrasound (US) for TMJ arthritis in children (under 18) with Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA), against MRI, CT, or CBCT as reference standard. Eight diagnostic-accuracy studies (2007–2025, 312 JIA patients across Germany, Norway, Switzerland, Italy ×3, USA, and Brazil) met inclusion criteria out of 1198 initially identified records. All eight studies used MRI as the reference standard — none used CT or CBCT. B-mode US (7 of 8 studies, 12–18 MHz) showed sensitivity ranging from 21% to 85% and specificity from 36.4% to 89%, with performance depending heavily on which TMJ alteration was being assessed (condylar changes/erosions, joint effusion, synovial thickness/inflammation, or lateral periarticular space width). One study (Zwir et al.) tested power Doppler US specifically for synovial inflammation and found it non-useful (sensitivity 0%, specificity 36.4%). GRADE certainty of evidence was graded LOW for sensitivity and MODERATE for specificity. QUADAS-2 risk-of-bias assessment found all 8 studies methodologically acceptable with low applicability concerns, but none fulfilled every QUADAS-2 criterion. A planned quantitative meta-analysis was abandoned due to high methodological heterogeneity (differing TMJ parameters assessed, differing US frequencies), so the review reports accuracy ranges rather than pooled estimates. The authors' conclusion is measured: MRI remains the reference-standard imaging modality, and US "can provide valuable supplementary information to assist in diagnosis alongside clinical examination and guide the need for advanced diagnostic assessment" — i.e., a triage/adjunct role, not a diagnostic replacement. US's appeal in this population rests on practical factors independent of raw accuracy: it involves no ionizing radiation, requires no sedation (a real barrier for MRI in young children), is low-cost, portable, and can be repeated chairside for monitoring.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic review to isolate US diagnostic accuracy for TMJ arthritis specifically in the pediatric JIA population (as opposed to adult TMD or rheumatoid-arthritis populations), synthesizing 8 studies (312 patients, 2007–2025) with a uniform MRI reference standard.
- Reports honest accuracy **ranges**, not a pooled estimate: B-mode US sensitivity 21%–85%, specificity 36.4%–89%, varying by which TMJ alteration is the target — explicitly states meta-analysis was infeasible due to heterogeneity in protocols, transducer frequency, and outcome definitions.
- Assigns formal GRADE certainty: **LOW for sensitivity, MODERATE for specificity** — the low-certainty sensitivity rating is the review's central caution, since it means US cannot be trusted to exclude TMJ arthritis on a negative finding.
- Applies QUADAS-2 to all 8 studies: none fulfilled every criterion, though all showed low applicability concerns — a "methodologically acceptable but imperfect" evidence base, not a strong one.
- Separately evaluates power Doppler US (1 study) and finds it non-useful for synovial inflammation (SN 0%, SP 36.4%), distinct from — and worse than — B-mode US performance.
- Frames US's clinical role explicitly as adjunct/triage alongside clinical examination to guide the need for MRI, not as an MRI replacement, while naming the practical case for US in children: no ionizing radiation, no sedation, chairside, low-cost, repeatable.
- Flags that no included study used CBCT as reference standard, despite CBCT's superior resolution of cortical bone changes (erosion, flattening) relevant to JIA-related condylar damage — an explicit gap for future research.

## Methodology

PRISMA-DTA-compliant systematic review (OSF-registered). Searched Medline, Embase, Cochrane, Web of Science, and SCOPUS, plus partial gray literature (first 100 Google Scholar results by relevance); initial search June 26, 2024, updated July 24, 2025; no language or time restriction. Eligible studies: diagnostic studies (RCTs, cohort, observational) evaluating 2D or 3D US in children (<18y) with JIA, with TMJ arthritis status confirmed against MRI, CT, or CBCT as reference standard, and reporting sensitivity/specificity or an ROC curve. Excluded: reviews, letters, case reports/series, opinions, book chapters, conference abstracts, studies lacking a reference-standard comparison or SN/SP data, and studies of non-TMJ joints. Two-phase independent screening (Covidence) by two reviewers with consensus resolution; data extraction by one author, cross-checked by a second. Risk of bias and applicability assessed with QUADAS-2 by two independent raters, disagreements resolved by a third reviewer (an oral and maxillofacial radiologist with 8 years of US experience). Certainty of evidence graded via GRADEpro (GRADE Summary of Findings). A planned quantitative meta-analysis of diagnostic test accuracy was abandoned due to high methodological heterogeneity across studies (different TMJ parameters assessed, different US frequencies) — the review is narrative/qualitative, reporting ranges rather than pooled effect sizes. From 1198 identified records, 515 duplicates were removed, 683 titles/abstracts were screened, 673 were excluded, 11 underwent full-text review, 3 were excluded, and 8 studies were included.

**B-mode US overall (7 studies, excludes Zwir's power Doppler)**: sensitivity 21%–85%, specificity 36.4%–89% depending on target. The lowest individual sensitivity value locatable in the retrieved full text is Müller's 23% (joint effusion/active arthritis); the abstract's stated 21% floor could not be traced to a specific individual-study figure in the retrieved body text — it may reside in Supporting Information Appendix S4 (GRADE certainty tables), which was not returned by full-text retrieval. **GRADE certainty**: LOW for sensitivity, MODERATE for specificity (based on 6 of 8 studies with true-negative/false-positive data; Assaf and Farronato were excluded from this specific assessment for lacking non-JIA comparators).

**QUADAS-2**: none of the 8 studies fulfilled all criteria; all showed low applicability concerns in patient selection, index test, and reference standard domains. No meta-analysis was performed — figures above are ranges across heterogeneous studies, not pooled estimates.
