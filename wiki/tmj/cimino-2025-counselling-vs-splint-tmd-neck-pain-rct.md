## Three-line Summary

Parallel-arm RCT (n=51 completers, Turin, Italy) in DC/TMD-diagnosed patients with coexisting neck pain (NRS >3) comparing 3-month counselling alone vs counselling plus Michigan-type hard occlusal splint.

Both groups significantly reduced neck pain VAS (C: −7.0 mm, p=0.006; OS: −8.0 mm, p=0.02) and NDI (C: −3.3, p=0.007; OS: −4.2, p=0.001); no between-group difference for any cervical outcome (all p>0.57; Cohen's d<0.5).

Adding an occlusal splint to counselling provides no additional cervical benefit at 3 months; conservative self-management counselling alone is sufficient for neck pain comorbidity in TMD.

## Summary

This RCT from the University of Turin (Italy) enrolled adult patients with DC/TMD pain-related diagnosis and coexisting neck pain (NRS >3). Participants were randomised to counselling alone (Group C, n=27 completers) or counselling plus a Michigan-type hard occlusal splint worn nightly (Group OS, n=24 completers). The study's primary outcome was the Neck Disability Index (NDI); secondary outcomes included neck pain VAS, TMD pain VAS, and maximum mouth opening (MMO).

At 3 months, both groups showed statistically significant improvements in neck pain VAS and NDI, with no clinically or statistically meaningful between-group differences. Effect sizes were small (Cohen's d<0.5) for all cervical comparisons. A baseline imbalance in TMD pain (counselling group scored higher) limits the within-study comparison of TMD pain outcomes between groups. The study was somewhat underpowered (52 completers vs 64 targeted), creating some risk of type II error.

The key clinical message is that counselling — a low-cost, risk-free self-management education approach — is as effective as splint therapy for improving neck pain and disability in TMD patients. This supports international consensus favouring conservative, self-management-first approaches for TMD.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that adding an occlusal splint (교합안정장치) to counselling does not improve cervical outcomes beyond counselling alone in TMD patients with neck pain
- First RCT specifically powered to detect between-group cervical differences in this population
- Both modalities produce clinically real (if modest) improvement in neck pain VAS (~7–8 mm) and NDI (~3–4 points) over 3 months
- Supports conservative self-management counselling as first-line treatment for TMD comorbid with neck pain
- Highlights need for adherence monitoring in splint trials — compliance was not formally measured

## Methodology

- **Design**: Parallel-arm RCT, per-protocol analysis
- **Setting**: CIR Dental School TMD Clinic, University of Turin (March–December 2019)
- **n**: 67 randomised → 52 completers (27 C, 24 OS); 8 dropouts per group
- **Diagnosis**: DC/TMD (pain-related: myalgia, arthralgia, or headache attributed to TMD)
- **Inclusion**: age 18–60; TMD pain NRS >3; neck pain NRS >3
- **Exclusion**: prior TMD/cervical therapy within 3 months; NSAIDs/TCA in past month; neurological disease; facial trauma/whiplash/TMJ surgery history
- **Randomisation**: Block randomisation by independent statistician; concealed allocation; statistician blinded at analysis
- **Counselling**: Self-care education (jaw muscle awareness, parafunctional habit control, soft diet, mandibular rest position, home exercises)
- **Occlusal splint**: Full-coverage upper-arch Michigan-type hard resin stabilisation splint worn nightly; no formal adherence monitoring
- **Outcomes**: NDI (primary); neck pain VAS, TMD pain VAS, MMO (secondary)
- **Statistics**: Mann-Whitney / t-test; Shapiro-Wilk normality; 95% CI; Cohen's d; R software

## Results

| Outcome | Group C (n=27) | Group OS (n=24) | Between-group p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neck pain VAS Δ | −7.0 mm (p=0.006) | −8.0 mm (p=0.02) | p=0.69 |
| NDI Δ | −3.3 (p=0.007) | −4.2 (p=0.001) | p=0.57 |
| MMO Δ | +2.6 mm (p=0.03) | NS | p=0.64 |

- Effect sizes: Cohen's d<0.5 for all between-group comparisons (small, not clinically meaningful)
- TMD diagnosis: myalgia 100% (C) vs 96% (OS); arthralgia 44% (C) vs 38% (OS) — no significant group difference
- Baseline TMD pain higher in counselling group (p<0.05) — limits direct TMD pain comparison between groups
- Sample demographics: predominantly female (~80%), mean age ~36 years in both groups
