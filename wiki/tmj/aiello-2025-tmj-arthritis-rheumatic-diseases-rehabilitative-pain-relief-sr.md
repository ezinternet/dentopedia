## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42024517524) evaluating rehabilitative/conservative pain-relief approaches for TMJ arthritis specifically in patients with rheumatic diseases (rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, Sjögren's syndrome, fibromyalgia, common variable immunodeficiency, chronic polyarthritis), searching PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science through February 25, 2024.

The yield was brutal: of 479 records, only 5 studies (1996–2021, n=11–91, four rheumatoid-arthritis-predominant plus one systemic-lupus-erythematosus cohort plus one mixed-diagnosis cohort) met inclusion criteria; interventions were four heterogeneous approaches — intra-articular corticosteroid injection (± lidocaine, ± irrigation) in three studies, dextrose subcutaneous perineural injection in one, and low-level laser therapy (LLLT) in one — assessed with the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Checklist, with GRADE quality ranging from low to high across studies.

The authors' own conclusion is the honest one: these approaches "might be effective" for pain relief, but the heterogeneity of the approaches and the low quality of the included studies "do not allow to draw certain conclusions" — this is a scoping-level evidence gap, not an endorsement of any specific technique, and the review does not address the safety of local joint injection in patients concurrently on systemic immunosuppressive therapy.

## 세줄요약

류마티스 질환(류마티스 관절염(Rheumatoid Arthritis, RA), 전신홍반루푸스(Systemic Lupus Erythematosus, SLE), 강직성척추염, 건선성 관절염, 쇼그렌증후군, 섬유근육통, 흔한변이형면역결핍증(Common Variable Immunodeficiency, CVID), 만성다발관절염 등)을 가진 환자의 측두하악관절(Temporomandibular Joint, TMJ) 관절염에 대한 재활적·보존적 통증완화 접근법의 효과를 평가한 체계적 문헌고찰(Systematic Review, SR)(PROSPERO 등록번호 CRD42024517524).

성과는 냉정하게 빈약하다 — PubMed·Scopus·Web of Science 479건 검색 중 최종 5편(1996~2021년, 대상자 11~91명; RA 중심 4편 + SLE 1편 + 혼합진단 1편)만 포함됐고, 4가지 개입(관절강내 코르티코스테로이드 주사(±리도카인, ±관절세척) 3편, 덱스트로스 피하 신경주위 주사 1편, 저출력레이저치료(Low-Level Laser Therapy, LLLT) 1편)이 시도됐으며, JBI(Joanna Briggs Institute) 비평평가체크리스트로 평가한 GRADE 근거 질은 낮음~높음까지 연구마다 혼재했다.

저자들 스스로의 결론이 핵심이다 — 이들 접근법은 "효과가 있을 수 있다(might be effective)"이지만 "개입의 이질성과 포함 연구의 낮은 질 때문에 확정적 결론을 내릴 수 없다"; 이는 특정 술기에 대한 지지가 아니라 근거 공백을 보여주는 scoping 수준의 결과이며, 면역억제 전신치료를 받는 환자에게 국소 관절 주사를 놓는 것의 안전성·상호작용은 이 리뷰가 다루지 않는다.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this systematic review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-024-08196-1); PMID 39966784; PMCID PMC11834569) is — per the authors' own claim — the first to specifically evaluate conservative/rehabilitative approaches for pain relief in rheumatic-disease patients with TMJ arthritis, as distinct from prior reviews on TMD prevalence in rheumatic disease generally, or on TMJ injection/arthrocentesis in the general (non-rheumatic) TMD population. The review was registered on PROSPERO (CRD42024517524) and followed PRISMA/Cochrane Handbook methodology, searching PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science through February 25, 2024, using a PICOS framework restricted to studies reporting TMJ pain intensity (VAS/NRS) in patients with a diagnosed rheumatic disease affecting the TMJ.

The screening funnel was steep: 479 records → 115 duplicates removed → 364 title/abstract screened → 19 full-text screened → 5 studies included. Those five studies (1996–2021, conducted in Sweden, Turkey, Egypt, and the USA; n=11–91) covered four rheumatic diagnoses/diagnosis-clusters and four distinct interventions — intra-articular corticosteroid injection with or without lidocaine or joint irrigation (3 studies), dextrose subcutaneous perineural injection (1 study), and low-level laser therapy (1 study, the only RCT). Quality, assessed with the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist and GRADE, ranged from low (case series) to high (the single RCT), and only 2 of 5 studies used a control group. No meta-analysis was possible given the heterogeneity of conditions, interventions, and outcome measures — this is a narrative synthesis.

The headline finding is not efficacy — it is the near-absence of dedicated evidence. The authors state plainly that these approaches "might be effective" for pain relief in this population, but that "the heterogeneity of the rehabilitative approaches performed, and the low quality of the included studies do not allow to draw certain conclusions regarding the efficacy of these approaches." This page should not be read as endorsing intra-articular steroid injection, dextrose perineural injection, or LLLT over one another — five low-to-moderate-quality studies spread across four interventions and multiple distinct rheumatic diagnoses cannot rank anything. What the review reliably establishes is (a) how thin the evidence base is, and (b) which interventions have actually been tried. It also does not address how local injection interacts with patients' ongoing systemic therapy (NSAIDs, glucocorticoids, methotrexate, leflunomide, hydroxychloroquine, or biologics such as etanercept, all present across the included cohorts per Table 2) — a real open question for a dentist deciding whether to inject a TMJ in a patient on systemic immunosuppression, which this review leaves to the treating rheumatologist's judgment rather than addressing directly.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic review, per the authors, focused specifically on conservative/rehabilitative pain-relief approaches for TMJ arthritis in rheumatic-disease patients (PROSPERO CRD42024517524).
- Documents that the entire dedicated evidence base is 5 studies (1996–2021, n=11–91), covering only four interventions: intra-articular corticosteroid injection (± lidocaine, ± irrigation; 3/5 studies), dextrose subcutaneous perineural injection (1/5 — Elshoura et al. 2020), and LLLT (1/5 — Benli et al. 2021, the only RCT).
- Applies design-specific JBI Critical Appraisal Checklists (RCT / quasi-experimental / case series) plus GRADE rating; only the RCT (Benli et al.) reached "high" quality (11/13), the case series (Trieger et al.) was "low" (6/10), and the remaining three were "moderate" (7–8/10).
- States explicitly that intervention/condition/outcome heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis, making the review's core contribution a map of the evidence gap rather than an efficacy ranking.
- Cites a related SR (Hysa et al., 56 papers) placing TMJ-involvement prevalence in rheumatic disease at 8–70% depending on outcome measure, typically emerging within 5 years of RA onset — epidemiological context this review does not itself re-derive but relies on to motivate the treatment question.

## Methodology

PICOS: participants = patients with a diagnosed rheumatic disease involving the TMJ; intervention = rehabilitative approaches for pain reduction; comparison = placebo, sham, or alternative interventions; outcome = TMJ pain intensity (VAS or NRS); study designs = case series, cohort, cross-sectional, RCT. Search covered PubMed (189 records), Scopus (266), and Web of Science (24) from inception to February 25, 2024, plus manual reference-list search of prior related reviews, per PRISMA and Cochrane Handbook guidance. Exclusions: pediatric/growth-age patients, prior TMJ trauma, congenital/neoplastic TMJ conditions, cross-over designs, non-English-language studies, unretrievable full text (posters/abstracts), and animal studies. Two independent reviewers extracted data and assessed risk of bias (JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist, design-specific variants) with a third reviewer arbitrating disagreements; GRADE was used to rate evidence quality per study. No meta-analysis was performed given intervention, condition, and outcome heterogeneity — narrative synthesis only.

**Discrepancy in disease composition (reproduced, not smoothed over)**: the abstract states "Four studies assessed patients affected by rheumatoid arthritis, **one systemic sclerodermia**, and one included patients affected by ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, Sjögren's syndrome, fibromyalgia, common variable immunodeficiency, and chronic polyarthritis." The Results body text and Table 2 both instead identify the fifth study (Benli et al.) as **systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)** — a different disease from systemic sclerosis (SSc) — so the abstract appears to contradict the paper's own body/table. Separately, the stated composition (4 RA + 1 SLE/SSc + 1 mixed = 6) does not sum to the 5 included studies; cross-checking Table 2 shows this is because Alstergren et al.'s single cohort is counted in both the "RA" bucket (9 of its 22 patients had RA) and the "mixed" bucket (the same cohort also included ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, Sjögren's, CVID, and unspecified polyarthritis/fibromyalgia patients) — one study double-counted across two labels, not a sixth study.

Interventions overall: intra-articular corticosteroid injection (3/5 studies), dextrose subcutaneous perineural injection (1/5), LLLT (1/5). Only 2/5 studies used a control/comparison group. All cohorts remained on systemic therapy for their rheumatic disease throughout (NSAIDs, glucocorticoids, methotrexate, leflunomide, hydroxychloroquine, etanercept per Table 2), but the review reports these drugs without analyzing any interaction with the local rehabilitative intervention — TMJ arthritis in rheumatic disease is managed primarily by the rheumatologist's systemic therapy, and this review covers only the local adjunct layer, leaving the interaction between the two unaddressed.
