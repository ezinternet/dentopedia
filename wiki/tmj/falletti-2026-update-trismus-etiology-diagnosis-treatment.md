## Three-line Summary

Narrative review proposing a novel 4-quadrant etiological classification (neurological vs. non-neurological × structural vs. non-structural) for trismus, paired with a red-flag-first stepwise diagnostic algorithm. Trismus is defined as interincisal distance < 35 mm (normal 40–60 mm); etiology ranges from CNS/PNS lesions, tetanus, and oromandibular dystonia to TMJ disorders, orofacial infections, post-radiation fibrosis, and metabolic disturbances. Management is cause-directed: NSAIDs/muscle relaxants for acute uncomplicated cases, BoNT-A or baclofen for neurological/spastic variants, physical therapy for all, and coronoidectomy for refractory extra-articular restriction.

## Summary

Trismus — colloquially "lockjaw" — is restricted mouth opening arising from masticatory muscle dysfunction. Falletti et al. (2026) present a narrative review synthesising the full etiological spectrum and proposing a 4-quadrant classification matrix and a clinical decision algorithm.

**Definition and normal range**: Interincisal distance < 35 mm defines trismus; physiological range is 40–60 mm. Severity is typically graded by degree of restriction below this threshold.

**Etiological breadth**: Causes span neurology (CNS structural lesions such as TBI/stroke/tumours; CNS non-structural such as Parkinson's, oromandibular dystonia, drug-induced; PNS lesions; tetanus via tetanospasmin-mediated inhibitory interneuron blockade), oral/maxillofacial (TMJ internal derangement, ankylosis, orofacial infections, post-surgical oedema, radiation fibrosis, trauma, burns), systemic (RA, scleroderma, hypocalcaemia, hypomagnesaemia, malignant hyperthermia), and congenital conditions.

**Red-flag emergencies** requiring immediate workup: airway compromise, deep neck space infections, tetanus, CNS infections, intracranial lesions.

**Diagnosis** is clinical-first (history + interincisal measurement + cranial nerve exam) followed by targeted imaging (MRI/CT), neurophysiology (nerve conduction, trigeminal reflex, EMG), and functional tools (jaw tracking, FEES for aspiration risk).

**Treatment** is cause-directed; physical therapy (passive stretching, jaw mobilisation) is universal and constitutes the largest RCT evidence base, particularly in head-and-neck cancer (HNC) patients. BoNT-A provides significant functional improvement in neurological and non-structural cases (case series evidence). Baclofen addresses spastic neurological trismus. Coronoidectomy offers immediate large gains in mouth opening in extra-articular cases but demands aggressive postoperative PT to prevent relapse. Most cases are self-limiting within 2 weeks; post-radiation fibrosis is the most protracted and treatment-resistant subtype.

## Key Contributions

- First 4-quadrant etiological taxonomy for trismus (neurological/non-neurological × structural/non-structural)
- Red-flag-prioritised stepwise diagnostic algorithm applicable across clinical specialties
- Treatment hierarchy mapping each etiological quadrant to specific interventions
- Evidence appraisal of BoNT-A in neurological and non-structural trismus
- Coronoidectomy indications and postoperative PT requirements formalised

## Methodology

- Study type: Narrative review (multi-disciplinary authorship: neurology + oral/maxillofacial surgery, Sapienza University Rome)
- No PROSPERO registration; no PRISMA flow
- Treatment evidence quality: RCT-level only for tetanus immunoglobulin and rehabilitation (HNC); all other interventions based on case series or expert opinion
- No quantitative synthesis

## Results

### Etiological Classification Matrix

| | Structural | Non-structural |
|---|---|---|
| **Neurological — CNS** | TBI/DAI, brain tumours, stroke, MS, CNS infections | Parkinson's/PSP/CBD, status epilepticus, oromandibular dystonia, drug-induced (phenothiazines, metoclopramide, fluoxetine, tricyclics), Wilson's disease, uremic encephalopathy, hemimasticatory spasm, functional disorder, catatonia |
| **Neurological — PNS** | Trigeminal nerve lesions, tumours of masticator space | Tetanus (tetanospasmin blocks GABA/glycine → sustained jaw-closer spasm), heavy metal poisoning (Pb/Hg) |
| **Non-neurological** | TMJ disorders (internal derangement, OA, ankylosis), oral/oropharyngeal/masticator tumours, trauma, orofacial infections (pericoronitis, periapical abscess, deep fascial space, peritonsillar abscess, osteomyelitis), mandibular/maxillary fractures, post-surgical oedema/haematoma, radiation-induced fibrosis, burns, autoimmune (RA, scleroderma, temporal arteritis), congenital (trismus pseudo-camptodactyly, arthrogryposis) | Hypocalcaemia, hypomagnesaemia, malignant hyperthermia (succinylcholine/halothane → masseter spasm), drug-induced (etomidate), muscle contractures, myofascial pain syndrome |

### Stepwise Diagnostic Algorithm

1. Measure interincisal distance → confirm < 35 mm
2. Clinical history: onset, duration, progression, trauma, infections, dental procedures, neurological symptoms, systemic diseases, medications, vaccination (tetanus)
3. Physical exam: TMJ function and ROM, orofacial soft tissue, dental/pharyngeal/cervical assessment, cranial nerve exam
4. **Red-flag exclusion** (any positive → emergency pathway):
   - Airway compromise
   - Deep neck space infection
   - Tetanus
   - CNS infection
   - Intracranial lesion
5. Instrumental workup (targeted by suspected pathway):
   - MRI/CT — intracranial lesions
   - Nerve conduction + trigeminal reflex — peripheral nerve lesions
   - EMG — muscle activity characterisation
   - EEG — status epilepticus
   - Ultrasound (USS) — masseter thickness
   - FEES — aspiration risk assessment
   - Quantitative jaw tracking — objective ROM monitoring
6. Branch to neurological vs. non-neurological management pathway

### Treatment Hierarchy

| Cause type | First intervention | Adjuncts / escalation |
|---|---|---|
| Acute uncomplicated | NSAIDs + muscle relaxants | Physical therapy |
| Drug-induced | Discontinue offending agent | Supportive |
| Metabolic (↓Ca²⁺, ↓Mg²⁺) | Electrolyte correction | — |
| Tetanus | Tetanus immunoglobulin + supportive care | ICU/ventilation if severe |
| Orofacial infection | Antibiotics + surgical drainage | PT after resolution |
| Neurological / non-structural | BoNT-A (inhibits ACh at NMJ) | Physical therapy |
| Spastic neurological (stroke, DAI, MS, CP) | Baclofen (GABA-B agonist) oral/intrathecal | BoNT-A combination |
| All cases | Physical therapy: passive stretching + jaw mobilisation (multiple daily sessions) | — |
| Extra-articular / post-radiation | Coronoidectomy (coronoid process resection → immediate MIO gain) | Aggressive postop PT to prevent relapse |
| Muscular contracture | Myotomy (less predictable, may impair masticatory efficiency) | PT |
| Advanced fibrosis / post-oncologic | Free vascularised flap reconstruction | Multidisciplinary |

### Prognosis

- Self-limiting within ~2 weeks in most cases
- Post-radiation fibrosis: protracted, treatment-resistant subtype
- Tetanus: prognosis determined by infection severity and treatment timeliness
- BoNT-A: effective in severe fibrosis/contracture cases where conservative measures fail
