## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (181 patients, 86% female) testing low-level GaAlAs laser (780-940 nm) against sham, placebo or drug comparators for myofascial pain-type TMD, searched to April 2020.

Pooled VAS pain difference at end of treatment was borderline (WMD = -0.76, 95% CI -1.51 to 0.01, P = .046) and gone at 3-4 weeks (WMD = 1.24, 95% CI -0.04 to 2.51); maximum mouth opening did not change (WMD = -0.03 mm, 95% CI -4.13 to 4.06; 3-4 weeks WMD = 1.22, 95% CI -2.94 to 5.39).

The abstract concludes evidence is insufficient to show efficacy of GaAlAs laser for TMD pain or mouth opening; very high heterogeneity (I2 about 89%), small trials and allocation-concealment problems limit any firm recommendation.

## 세줄요약

8편의 무작위대조시험 (Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT; 181명, 여성 86%)을 대상으로 근막통형 측두하악장애 (Temporomandibular Disorders, TMD)에서 저출력 갈륨알루미늄비소 (Gallium Aluminium Arsenide, GaAlAs) 레이저 (780-940 nm)를 위약·가짜·약물 대조와 비교한 체계적 문헌고찰 및 메타분석 (2020년 4월까지 검색).

치료 종료 시 시각통증척도 (Visual Analogue Scale, VAS) 통합 차이는 경계 수준 (가중평균차 = -0.76, 95% CI -1.51 ~ 0.01, P = .046)이었고 3-4주 후에는 사라졌으며 (1.24, 95% CI -0.04 ~ 2.51), 최대개구량 (Maximum Mouth Opening, MMO)은 변화 없었음 (-0.03 mm, 95% CI -4.13 ~ 4.06; 3-4주 1.22, 95% CI -2.94 ~ 5.39).

초록은 GaAlAs 레이저의 TMD 통증·개구 개선 효과를 뒷받침할 근거가 불충분하다고 결론; 매우 높은 이질성 (I2 약 89%), 소규모 시험, 배정은폐 결함 때문에 강한 권고는 불가.

## Summary

According to PubMed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000028015)), this meta-analysis pooled 8 RCTs (181 patients) of low-level GaAlAs laser for myofascial-pain TMD (RDC/TMD axis 1), compared with sham/placebo (6 trials) or piroxicam/naproxen (2 trials). Pain on the VAS was lower with laser at the end of treatment by a borderline margin (P = .046) but the difference was not present 3-4 weeks later, and mouth opening did not change. Heterogeneity was very high (I2 = 89%) and results depended on removing the most discrepant trials. The abstract concludes evidence is insufficient to show efficacy.

## Key Contributions

- Narrows the laser evidence to one laser class and one TMD phenotype.
- Shows the pain effect is short-lived (not sustained at 3-4 weeks) and mouth opening is unaffected.
- Flags laser dose heterogeneity (2.5 to 112.5 J/cm2; 780 to 940 nm) and weak allocation concealment (2 of 8 trials).

## Methodology

- Search to April 5, 2020 in five databases; English RCTs only.
- Cochrane ROB v5.1.0; RevMan 5.3 and Stata 15.1; fixed-effects if I2 < 50%, else random-effects; sensitivity analysis; funnel plot.
- Outcomes: VAS pain (primary), MMO (secondary).
- No GRADE rating reported.

## Results

| Outcome | Time point | Estimate | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| VAS pain | End of treatment | WMD = -0.76 (95% CI -1.51 to 0.01) | .046 |
| VAS pain | 3-4 weeks | WMD = 1.24 (95% CI -0.04 to 2.51) | .057 |
| MMO | End of treatment | WMD = -0.03 (95% CI -4.13 to 4.06) | .987 |
| MMO | 3-4 weeks | WMD = 1.22 (95% CI -2.94 to 5.39) | .565 |

- All 8 trials in the pain analysis: I2 = 89%; Khalighi 2016 and Cetiner 2006 were the main sources and the significant result came from the remaining 6 trials in a fixed-effects model. The 3-4 week analysis used 4 trials; MMO used 2 trials per time point.
- Descriptively, Ferreira 2013 reported faster and greater pain reduction with laser; Cetiner 2006 found no between-group difference at 1 month; de Godoy 2014 found no MMO change; Khalighi 2016 found MMO improved.
- Drug comparators disagreed: laser better than drug at follow-up (Khalighi 2016) versus similar at end of treatment with drug more stable afterward (De Carli).
- Risk of bias: 5/8 adequate sequence generation, 2/8 allocation concealment, 7/8 low-risk blinding.
- Inconsistencies in the source: the abstract CI reaches 0.01 while P = .046; the abstract says three reviewers but the Methods describe two plus an arbiter; the Conclusions text says "superior... moderate evidence" while the abstract says "insufficient evidence".

## What this paper adds

Compared with [[tmj/desai-2021-low-level-laser-therapy-tmj-pain]] (a single 60-patient RCT favoring laser, abstract-level), this review pools several GaAlAs trials and finds the benefit small, transient and absent for opening. Compared with [[tmj/candido-do-prado-2025-differential-effectiveness-photobiomodulation-muscular-articular]] (18 RCTs, n = 1038, SMD -0.84 for pain, all wavelengths, abstract-level), this review is smaller, confined to GaAlAs and myofascial pain, and much less favorable; the two used different effect metrics, so the magnitudes are not directly comparable. Together they indicate laser benefit is sensitive to wavelength, dose and trial selection. [[tmj/alowaimer-2024-noninvasive-therapies-tmj-sr]] likewise rates LLLT alone as inconsistent.
