## Three-line Summary

Systematic review published as an AWMF/DGMKG clinical practice guideline (Marburg/Rostock/Hamburg, Dtsch Arztebl Int 2018;115:59–64): PubMed/Cochrane/Embase/ZB Med searched from May 2014 with two updates (Aug 2015, Nov 2016), 24 650 hits screened down to 136 included studies, evidence graded on the Oxford CEBM LoE I–V scheme with GoR A/B/0 and structured interdisciplinary consensus; German TMJ-dislocation incidence estimated at ≥25/100 000/year (3% of all documented dislocations).

Acute non-traumatic dislocation is reduced manually without delay, with the Hippocratic and wrist-pivot techniques judged equivalent and extraoral reduction succeeding in 96.7% of unilateral vs only 54.5% of bilateral cases; manual reduction usually fails beyond 3–4 weeks of persistence (→ surgical reduction), ~30% of presented dislocations are persistent, recurrence follows the first episode in 22%, and recurrent cases respond to autologous blood injection (~80% symptom-free at 12 months) or prolotherapy (91% recurrence-free at 6 months).

Clinically this fixes a defensible chairside default — sitting position, head stabilized with a headrest, bite blocks and gloves, fingers lateral to (never on) the molar occlusal surfaces, and a medication-free first attempt before muscle relaxants/analgesics, analgosedation or general anaesthesia — but the authors concede that no more than a few RCTs exist, so the high-grade recommendations still cannot be issued and the recurrence-prevention data rest on case series of ≤30 patients.

급성 비외상성 악관절 탈구는 즉시 수동복원 (manual reduction)하며, 히포크라테스법 (Hippocratic method)과 손목축법 (wrist pivot method)은 동등하고, 구외복원 (extraoral reduction) 성공률은 편측 96.7% 대 양측 54.5% — 3–4주 이상 지속되면 수동복원은 대개 실패하여 외과적 복원 (open/surgical reduction)을 고려하고, 진료된 탈구의 약 30%가 만성 지속이며 초회 후 재발이 22%, 재발성에는 자가혈액 주입 (autologous blood injection, 12개월 무증상 약 80%)과 프로로테라피 (prolotherapy, 6개월 재발 없음 91%)가 효과적.

실무적으로 앉은 자세·머리 받침 고정·교합 블록(bite block)·장갑 사용·지미 교합면 위가 아니라 치아 아치 바깥쪽에 손가락 배치·무약물 우선 시도 후 근이완제/진통제·진정/마취 단계적 시도라는 방어 가능한 기본값을 확정해 주나, 저자들은 무작위 대조시험이 몇 편에 불과해 높은 등급의 근거 권고는 여전히 불가능하고 재발 예방 근거는 30명 이하의 사례군에 의존한다고 명시한다.

## Summary

This German clinical practice guideline, published in Dtsch Arztebl Int 2018 and also registered with AWMF (online 30 June 2016), systematically searched PubMed, Cochrane, Embase and ZB Med from May 2014 — 24 650 citations screened to 136 included studies — and graded every recommendation with Oxford CEBM levels of evidence and grades of recommendation, ratified by a structured consensus of the DGMKG TMJ Surgery Guideline Group plus an interdisciplinary Delphi panel of four professional societies. Its clinical payload is the complete management pathway for non-fractured TMJ dislocation, which accounts for 3% of all documented dislocations and affects at least 25 per 100 000 people per year in Germany: clinical diagnosis alone is acceptable for a first-time non-traumatic episode, every acute dislocation is manually reduced immediately, and the Hippocratic and wrist-pivot techniques are equivalent while the extraoral route reaches 96.7% success in unilateral but only 54.5% in bilateral cases. The guidance then escalates along a natural-history timeline — roughly 30% of presented dislocations persist, manual reduction usually fails past 3–4 weeks and surgery takes over, and 22% of patients recur — with recurrence prevention by botulinum toxin (off-label, no recurrence over ≥5 months in series of ≤21 patients), autologous blood injection (~80% symptom-free at 12 months) and prolotherapy (91% recurrence-free at 6 months), reserving eminectomy and other open procedures for failure of these measures. The authors are explicit that the underlying literature is small, heterogeneous and short-follow-up, so high-grade recommendations cannot yet be issued; what the document delivers instead is a well-graded, internationally convergent standard of care rather than an evidence ceiling it cannot claim.

## Key Contributions

- The only **guideline-level treatment pathway** in the vault for acute TMJ dislocation itself, spanning diagnosis → manual reduction → persistent-dislocation surgery → recurrence prevention, each step carrying a GoR and LoE.
- Establishes **technique equivalence** — the modified Hippocratic method and the wrist-pivot method are at least equal on success rate, reduction-related pain, and reduction time — so neither is the default on the basis of superiority.
- Quantifies the **extraoral route's asymmetry**: 96.7% success for unilateral vs 54.5% for bilateral dislocation, restricting it to raised bite or infection risk (dementia, hepatitis C, known IV drug abuse) rather than routine use.
- Fixes the **3–4 week surgical crossover point**, at which manual reduction attempts are usually no longer successful and surgical reduction (redressive, eminectomy, condylectomy, osteotomy, endoprosthesis) should be considered.
- Frames **recurrence as a guideline target** with its own therapeutic tier: 22% recur, so injections and then open surgery are pre-specified rather than improvised — and petitions for recurrent dislocation to be recognized as a **new, currently off-label botulinum toxin indication** (lateral pterygoid).
- Adds a **preventive pre-procedure rule** (GoR B; LoE IV–V+): ask about previous dislocations and risk factors before any general-anaesthesia procedure, and check functional jaw mobility before and after intubation — with iatrogenic triggers explicitly including intubation, endoscopy, and prolonged dental treatment such as tooth extraction.

## Methodology

Systematic review **without meta-analysis**, issued as a clinical practice guideline. PubMed, Cochrane, Embase and ZB Med were searched from May 2014 (English/German, from 2000, term "temporomandibular joint dislocation"), with reference-list screening and two updates (August 2015, November 2016): 24 650 hits → duplicates removed → 1 296 screened by headings → 150 by abstract → 104, reaching **136 included articles** summarized in tables. Evidence was graded on the Oxford Centre for Evidence-based Medicine scheme (LoE I–V with `+`/`−` quality suffixes for good quality or n > 100 / poor quality or n < 10, and `a`/`b` for review or single study; GoR A "shall" / B "should" / 0 "may"; consensus strength >95% strong). The draft went to the DGMKG Guideline Group TMJ Surgery for structured consensus (adopted unanimously) and then an interdisciplinary Delphi with the DGMKG, DGPro, DGFDT and ZVK societies; AWMF published it online 30 June 2016, and two subsequently available studies did not trigger revision. Outcomes are recommendation statements rather than trial endpoints — diagnostic workup, reduction success, pain, reduction time, recurrence, and complications.

## Results

### Epidemiology and natural history

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Share of all documented dislocations | 3% |
| German annual incidence | ≥25 / 100 000 population (2017 DGMKG e-mail survey; "as common as giant-cell arteritis") |
| Typical age band | 25–45 years, with a second incidence peak in older, multimorbid patients |
| Persistent among physician-presented cases | ~30% |
| Recurrence after first dislocation | 22% ("about 20%" in Key Messages) |

### Acute reduction

| Step | Recommendation | GoR / LoE |
|---|---|---|
| First-line | Manual reduction of every non-traumatic dislocation, without delay — earlier reduction, greater success chance | GoR B; LoE IIIa |
| Technique (a) | **Modified Hippocratic**: one side at a time; thumb lateral to the dental arch (oblique line); remaining fingers on the external inferior mandible; press **caudally first, then dorsally**; two steps | most commonly used |
| Technique (b) | **Wrist pivot**: both sides at once; thumbs on the inferior external chin pressing cranially; index/middle fingers on mandibular teeth or oblique line pressing caudally, wrists rotating ulnarward — **at least equal** to Hippocratic on success rate, pain, and reduction time | — |
| Technique (c) | **Extraoral**: one side at a time; thumb on coronoid process with fingers on mastoid process as abutment; then fingers around the mandibular angle with thumbs on the zygomatic arch, pressing ventrally to increase ipsilateral dislocation — **96.7% success unilateral vs 54.5% bilateral**; more painful and time-consuming | GoR 0; LoE I |
| Escalation | Attempt **without any medication** first; on failure, retry under muscle relaxants and/or analgesics, or analgosedation or general anaesthesia | GoR 0; LoE IIIa–V+ |
| Chairside setup | Patient **sitting**, head stabilized on a headrest; **bite blocks and gloves** against bite injury and associated infection | GoR B; LoE IV–V+ |
| Finger placement | Fingers **not on, but lateral to** the occlusal surfaces of the mandibular molars | GoR B; LoE V; expert consensus |

The extraoral route is recommended **only** for raised bite or infection risk (dementia, hepatitis C, known IV drug abuse); otherwise the bilateral success rate does not justify it. Physician experience with the technique is stated to be the key determinant of success.

### Diagnosis

| Element | Recommendation | GoR / LoE |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical diagnosis | History + physical examination alone suffice for a first-time, non-traumatic dislocation — cardinal signs are impaired occlusion/inability to close the jaws with pain, and an empty TMJ socket on exam | GoR 0; LoE IIIa–V+ |
| Imaging | OPG, CBCT/DVT, MRI where symptoms allow other differential diagnoses — to exclude facial fracture and plan treatment | GoR B; LoE I–V+ |
| Peri-procedural screening | Ask about prior dislocations and risk factors before general-anaesthesia procedures; check functional jaw mobility before and after intubation | GoR B; LoE IV–V+ |

Dislocation is easily missed in sedated, post-traumatic, demented and persistently dislocated patients; milder symptoms plus reduced ability to communicate make delayed diagnosis common in multimorbid older patients.

### Persistent and recurrent dislocation

| Situation | Recommendation | GoR / LoE |
|---|---|---|
| Persistence > 3–4 weeks | Manual reduction usually no longer successful → **surgical reduction** (redressive, eminectomy, condylectomy, osteotomy, endoprosthesis) | GoR B; LoE I–V+ |
| Refractory, with e.g. anterior open bite | Individualized functional TMJ surgery → reconstructive and orthognathic surgery | GoR 0; LoE IIIa–V |
| Recurrence after failure of conservative/minimally invasive care | **Open surgery**: eminectomy (best documented), blocking or sling procedures, capsular ligament complex surgery | GoR 0; LoE IIIa–IV+ |

| Recurrence-prevention method | Evidence base | Reported outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Botulinum toxin, lateral pterygoid | case series up to 21 patients | no further dislocations over **≥5 months**; **off-label** (GoR A; LoE V; expert consensus) — authors petition for the new indication |
| Autologous blood injection | case series up to 30 patients | **~80% symptom-free at 12 months**, no aggravation reported; proposed fibrosis **not** confirmed on MRI |
| Prolotherapy (sclerotherapy) | one case series, 30 patients | **91% no recurrence over 6 months** |

Minimally invasive injections are indicated primarily in patients with reduced compliance or increased surgical risk; long-term outcomes are often unsatisfactory and invasive management is eventually required.
