## One-line Summary
Prospective split-mouth RCT (n=26, 108 extractions) demonstrating L-PRF socket filling reduces postextraction pain (VAS 3.2 vs 4.1) and improves soft tissue healing at 7 days compared to natural clot healing.

## Summary
This split-mouth prospective RCT (University of Naples Federico II, 2012–2013) evaluated L-PRF socket filling in simple postextraction sockets. Twenty-six patients (108 extractions total) received L-PRF on one side and natural clot healing on the contralateral side, allocated by coin toss. Pain was assessed by VAS at 24, 48, 72, and 96 hours; soft tissue healing by the modified Healing Index at 3, 7, 14, and 21 days. L-PRF significantly reduced postoperative pain (mean VAS 3.2±0.3 vs 4.1±0.1) and improved the modified Healing Index at 7 days (4.8±0.6 vs 5.1±0.9). The authors propose L-PRF as a practical adjunct in daily private practice given its low cost and simple preparation relative to PRP.

## Key Contributions
- L-PRF reduces postextraction pain (VAS ~0.9 points lower than natural healing over 24–96h)
- Modest improvement in 7-day soft tissue healing index (mHI 4.8 vs 5.1)
- L-PRF releases PDGF-AB, TGF-β, VEGF for >7 days (vs PRP 3 days) — sustained growth factor delivery
- Demonstrates feasibility in private practice setting (no anticoagulants, low cost, 12-min centrifugation)
- No dry socket in L-PRF group (explicit comparison not quantified for control)

## Methodology
- Design: prospective single-blind randomized split-mouth RCT
- n=26 patients (9M/17F); mean age 53±4 years; nonsmokers or light smokers (<5/day)
- 108 total extractions; 2–4 paired bilateral extractions per patient
- Anesthesia: 2% mepivacaine (no epinephrine to avoid blood supply restriction)
- No full-thickness flap elevation; alveolar walls preserved; 3/0 Vicryl suture
- VAS pain at 24, 48, 72, 96h; mHI at 3, 7, 14, 21 days

## Results
- VAS pain: 3.2±0.3 (L-PRF) vs 4.1±0.1 (control) — L-PRF superior
- mHI at 7 days: 4.8±0.6 (L-PRF) vs 5.1±0.9 (control) — L-PRF superior
- Reduced early inflammatory adverse effects in L-PRF group
- Clinically modest but statistically significant differences
