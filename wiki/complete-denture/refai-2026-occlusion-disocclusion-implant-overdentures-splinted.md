## Three-line Summary

Parallel three-arm RCT, n=36 completely edentulous adults (12 per group): conventional complete denture (CD) control, two-implant mandibular overdenture with ball attachments (IB, unsplinted), and with intraoral welded titanium bar (IW, splinted); T-Scan occlusion time (OT) and right/left disocclusion times (DR/DL) measured at implant placement (T1) and 3 months (T2).

At T1 both implant groups had significantly shorter OT/DR/DL than CD (CD OT 0.52±0.07 s vs IB 0.35±0.06 s vs IW 0.29±0.02 s, p<0.001) with no significant IB-vs-IW difference (OT Q=3.29, p=0.06); CD improved significantly over 3 months (settling/adaptation) while both implant groups remained stable.

Splinting two interforaminal implants gave no functional-occlusion advantage over ball attachments, so attachment choice can rest on simplicity/maintenance/cost; limited by 3-month follow-up, insertion-torque-only stability (no ISQ), and absent patient-reported outcomes.

## Summary

This three-arm randomized controlled trial (n=36, 12 per group) asked whether **splinting** two immediately loaded interforaminal implants in a mandibular overdenture improves functional occlusion, using the T-Scan system to measure occlusion time (OT) and disocclusion time (DT). It compared an intraoral welded titanium bar (IW, splinted) against ball attachments (IB, unsplinted), with a conventional complete denture (CD) as tissue-supported control. All patients wore a maxillary complete denture and were restored with a standardized lingualized balanced occlusion.

Both implant-retained groups produced markedly shorter (more efficient) OT and DT than the complete denture, reflecting the rigid implant foundation versus resilient, settling mucosa. Crucially, **the splinted welded bar and the unsplinted ball attachments were statistically indistinguishable** on every occlusal parameter at both timepoints. Over three months only the CD group changed significantly (improving as the base settled and tissues adapted), while both implant groups stayed stable from the outset.

The clinical message: for two-implant mandibular overdentures under immediate loading, rigid bar splinting confers no measurable functional-occlusion benefit over simpler ball attachments, so attachment selection can be driven by maintenance, technique sensitivity, and cost rather than occlusal performance.

## Key Contributions

- First T-Scan (OT/DT) evaluation of **immediately loaded** two-implant mandibular overdentures comparing splinted (intraoral welded bar) vs unsplinted (ball) attachments.
- Quantifies the large functional-occlusion gap between rigid implant support and a tissue-supported complete denture.
- Provides RCT-level evidence that splinting two interforaminal implants offers no occlusal advantage over ball attachments.
- Demonstrates chairside intraoral welding (Mondani technique) within an immediate-loading overdenture protocol.

## Methodology

- **Design**: parallel three-arm RCT (CONSORT 2010), 1:1:1 computer-generated randomization via opaque sealed envelopes; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06376019.
- **Groups (n=12 each)**: CD (conventional mandibular complete denture); IB (two-implant overdenture, ball abutments 3.5 mm × 11.5 mm); IW (two-implant overdenture, 2 mm rounded titanium bar, intraoral welded — JD Weld, 1 pulse, 0.37% power, water-cooled). All had maxillary complete dentures; occlusion standardized to lingualized balanced occlusion.
- **Implants**: IS-III ACTIVE (Neobiotech) 4.0 × 10 mm, mandibular interforaminal region, inter-implant distance ~20 ± 2 mm, angulation <10°, open-flap after CBCT (800–1200 HU, D1–D2), insertion torque 35–40 Ncm for immediate loading; resilient soft liner used during immediate-loading phase.
- **Outcomes**: T-Scan III (Tekscan, ~100 μm sensor), OT (first contact → maximum intercuspation) and DR/DL (excursion onset → complete posterior separation), upright standardized head position, center-of-force correction to 50%±2% per side, 4 repetitions, single blinded operator. Measured at T1 (placement day) and T2 (3 months).
- **Statistics**: Mixed ANOVA (Greenhouse-Geisser) for time × treatment; paired t-test within groups; post hoc Tukey with Bonferroni correction; p<0.05.
- **Blinding**: only the T-Scan assessor was blinded (prosthetic designs visibly differ).

## Results

- **Mixed ANOVA**: significant main effects of group and time plus significant group × time interaction for OT, DR, DL (all p<0.001); large group partial eta² (OT 0.791, DR 0.896, DL 0.826).
- **Between groups (Tukey)**: CD significantly different from both IB and IW (p<0.001) at T1 and T2; **IB vs IW never significant** (OT Q=3.29 p=0.06; DR Q=2.8 p=0.13; DL Q=3.05 p=0.09).

| Parameter | Time | CD (s) | IB (s) | IW (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OT | T1 | 0.52±0.07 | 0.35±0.06 | 0.29±0.02 |
| OT | T2 | 0.47±0.07 | 0.33±0.03 | 0.28±0.02 |
| DR | T1 | 0.57±0.04 | 0.40±0.04 | 0.36±0.03 |
| DR | T2 | 0.51±0.05 | 0.38±0.05 | 0.33±0.04 |
| DL | T1 | 0.58±0.04 | 0.42±0.01 | 0.40±0.02 |
| DL | T2 | 0.52±0.04 | 0.41±0.03 | 0.37±0.04 |

- **Within groups over time (paired t-test)**: significant reduction only in CD (OT p=0.04, DR p=0.005, DL p=0.0008) as the base settled and tissues adapted; IB and IW showed no significant change (already short from rigid support).
- **Interpretation**: force distribution in two-implant overdentures is largely governed by supporting bone and denture base, limiting the added effect of splinting; the small, flexible, rounded titanium bar likely further neutralized any splinting advantage.
