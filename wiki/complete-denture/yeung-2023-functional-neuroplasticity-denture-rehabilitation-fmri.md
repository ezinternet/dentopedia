## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (PICO + 3-database search + risk-of-bias; meta-analysis not feasible) of nine fMRI studies, n≈4–20 each, eight in complete-edentulous and one in partial-edentulous adults, on how complete dentures, implant-supported overdentures, and implant-supported fixed dentures reshape task-evoked brain activation.

During jaw-clenching, implant-supported fixed dentures showed the highest activation in primary sensorimotor cortex (precentral/postcentral gyri) vs overdentures and complete dentures; new complete dentures caused a transient activation rise returning to baseline after a ~3-month habituation; gum-chewing was mixed — overdentures sometimes produced LOWER activation than complete dentures (attributed to more stable, regular chewing).

Restoring occlusion does remap sensorimotor cortical activation, but this is functional neuroplasticity (a BOLD-signal change), NOT proven cognitive benefit — samples are tiny, paradigms heterogeneous, several studies used subjective signal grading, and no meta-analysis was possible.

## 세줄요약

이악물기 (Jaw-clenching) 시 임플란트지지 고정성의치가 일차감각운동피질(중심전회 PreCG·중심후회 PostCG)에서 가장 높은 활성과 연관; 새 총의치는 일시적 활성 증가 후 약 3개월 적응기 (habituation)를 거쳐 기저선 복귀; 껌씹기 (Gum-chewing)는 혼재 — 피개의치가 총의치보다 오히려 낮은 활성(더 안정적·규칙적 저작으로 해석).

교합 회복이 감각운동피질 활성을 재구성하는 것은 맞지만 이는 기능적 신경가소성 (functional neuroplasticity, BOLD 신호 변화)일 뿐 인지기능 향상이 입증된 것은 아님 — 표본이 매우 작고 패러다임이 이질적이며 일부는 주관적 신호등급 사용, 메타분석 불가.

## Summary

Tooth loss is linked to altered brain structure (gray-matter atrophy in hippocampus/motor cortex) and to steeper cognitive decline, raising the clinically important question this review addresses: **does prosthetic rehabilitation of edentulous jaws restore task-evoked brain activation?** The authors systematically searched Web of Science, Scopus, and PubMed (2023-02-01), screened 31→16→9 records, and appraised the nine included fMRI studies with RoB 2 (one randomized crossover) and RoBANS (eight non-randomized). Heterogeneity and missing brain coordinates (6/9 studies reported region names only) made a coordinate-based meta-analysis impossible, so the synthesis is narrative.

The three rehabilitation options compared are **complete dentures**, **implant-supported overdentures** (usually a maxillary complete denture + a 2-implant mandibular overdenture), and **implant-supported fixed dentures**. Across jaw-clenching studies, fixed dentures evoked the greatest activation in the primary motor (precentral gyrus, PreCG) and primary somatosensory (postcentral gyrus, PostCG) cortices, framed as restoration of peripheral sensory feedback. Longitudinal data show new complete dentures transiently raise PreCG/PostCG activation that normalizes after ~3 months of habituation. Gum-chewing data run partly the other way: a more stable, kinematically regular prosthesis (implant overdenture) can produce LOWER cortical activation than a mobile complete denture — so "more activation" is not a clean quality marker, and direction is task-dependent.

**Interpretive caution is the take-home.** These are BOLD-signal (functional-neuroplasticity) changes, not demonstrated cognitive improvement. The cognitive/MMSE and neuropsychology signals reported by two studies are preliminary and confounded (practice effects, no denture-removal control, unadjusted multiple comparisons, n=4–20). This page is the wiki's single node for the nine primary fMRI studies (Luraschi, Tan, Verma, Bhattacharjee, Yan, Kimoto, Padmanabhan, Nakasato, Shoi), which are not separately ingested.

## Key Contributions

1. First systematic collation of fMRI evidence on denture rehabilitation, framing prosthetics as a driver of measurable functional neuroplasticity.
2. Clenching hierarchy: implant-supported **fixed** dentures > implant overdentures > complete dentures in evoked primary sensorimotor (PreCG/PostCG) activation.
3. Habituation trajectory: new complete dentures transiently increase PreCG/PostCG activation (peak ~1 wk), returning to baseline by ~3 months, tracking behavioral chewing-efficiency and bite-force adaptation.
4. Task-dependent contradiction: gum-chewing can show overdentures with LOWER activation than complete dentures — opposite direction to the clenching hierarchy — cautioning against reading "more activation = better."
5. Explicit critical appraisal: subjective signal grading, unblinded assessment, unadjusted multiplicity, and 6/9 studies lacking brain coordinates → meta-analysis not feasible.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review, narrative synthesis (PubMed type: Review). PICO; search string `((denture* OR overdenture*) AND (fMRI OR "functional magnetic resonance" OR "functional MRI"))` across 3 databases on 2023-02-01; eligibility flow 31 records → 15 duplicates removed → 16 assessed → 9 included (excluded: non-English 3, no tooth replacement 3, no comparison 1); citation chasing for completeness.
- **Risk of bias**: RoB 2 (Shoi, randomized crossover) + RoBANS (8 non-randomized). All nine had at least "some concerns"; four had high risk (unblinded subjects or incomplete outcome data).
- **Included designs/tasks**: 7 within-group (longitudinal) + 2 between-group; only 3 had a pre-intervention baseline. Tasks: jaw-clenching (most), gum-chewing (next), plus single-study teeth-tapping, lip-pursing, memory recall, continuous performance, go/no-go.
- **Why no meta-analysis**: neuroimaging meta-analysis needs whole-brain coordinates (and ideally ≥17–20 studies); only 3/9 reported coordinates.

## Results

**Jaw-clenching (5 studies):**
- Luraschi (n=11, old→new complete dentures, T0–T3): bilateral PreCG+PostCG activation; clenching activation rose significantly at T2 (1 wk) vs T0, returned to baseline at T3 (3 mo). Chewing efficiency and max bite force improved over the same window.
- Tan (n=4): complete denture → implant overdenture caused an initial DECREASE in sensorimotor activation at 1 wk, recovering by 6 wk (opposite initial direction to Luraschi).
- Verma (n=12, subjective grading): implant mandibular overdenture (3 mo) → stronger signals in PreCG, PostCG, hippocampus, insula, frontal/temporal lobe vs complete denture.
- Bhattacharjee (n=18, 3 groups): fixed dentures → more PreCG/PostCG, angular gyrus, temporal-lobe activation; but NO difference in tactile sensibility or oral stereognosis.
- Yan (n=20, cross-sectional): higher proportion of fixed-denture patients activated PreCG/PostCG during clenching (socioeconomic confounding unadjusted).

**Gum-chewing (4 studies):**
- Kimoto (n=4): implant overdenture → reduced bilateral prefrontal activation vs complete denture (more stable, regular chewing).
- Padmanabhan (n=10): overdenture → reduced prefrontal vs edentulous; vs complete denture higher insula but LOWER hippocampus. Memory recall: overdenture → higher hippocampus+prefrontal. MMSE overdenture 25.5 > complete denture 24.0 > edentulous 20.5 (median) — but at Bonferroni p<0.017 the CD-vs-overdenture difference is non-significant and re-test confounds remain.
- Nakasato (n=14): new > old complete dentures → higher PreCG, prefrontal, putamen, cerebellum, inferior parietal; masseter EMG higher with new dentures and correlated with fMRI (hippocampus/parahippocampal/PostCG); neuropsych improved — but no statistical link tested between cognition and mastication.
- Shoi (n=11, only partial-edentulous study): full-arch RPD > shortened-arch RPD → higher bilateral medial/middle frontal gyri, with better food comminuting/mixing.

**Bottom line**: clenching → fixed > overdenture > complete denture (framed as restored feedback); chewing → direction can reverse (stabler prosthesis, less activation). All samples ≤20. Restored occlusion remaps sensorimotor cortex, but cognitive benefit is unproven.
