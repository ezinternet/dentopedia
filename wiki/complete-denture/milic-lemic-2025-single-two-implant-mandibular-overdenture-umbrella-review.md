## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PRISMA + PROSPERO CRD42024599304) of 7 systematic reviews (from 243 screened) comparing single-implant (SIMO) vs two-implant (TIMO) mandibular overdentures on implant failure, peri-implant marginal bone loss (MBL), prosthetic complications/maintenance, patient satisfaction, and OHRQoL, each appraised with AMSTAR 2.

The 7 SRs disagree directionally: one favours SIMO over TIMO for both implant failure and MBL, another finds TIMO has significantly more implant failures (1Y, 5Y), more 1Y prosthetic failures, and more 5Y O-ring replacements than SIMO; AMSTAR 2 quality across the 7 SRs was 2 critically low, 2 low, 3 moderate — none high.

SIMO is a reasonable alternative to conventional TIMO for edentulous mandible rehabilitation, but the underlying SR evidence base is of variable and mostly low-to-moderate methodological quality, so guideline-level conclusions should await more robust patient-level evidence.

SIMO는 기존 표준 TIMO와 대등한 하악 무치악 재활 대안이 될 수 있으나, 근거로 쓰인 SR들의 방법론적 질이 대체로 낮아 가이드라인 수준의 결론을 내리기 전 더 견고한 환자 수준 근거가 필요함(핵심 한계).

## Summary

This umbrella review by Milić Lemić et al. (2025) synthesizes 7 systematic reviews comparing single-implant (SIMO) versus two-implant (TIMO) mandibular overdentures across implant failure, peri-implant marginal bone loss, prosthetic complications and maintenance, patient satisfaction, and oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL). Following PRISMA methodology and registered on PROSPERO (CRD42024599304), the authors screened 243 papers and selected 7 SRs, appraising each with AMSTAR 2. The included SRs disagreed directionally on the primary clinical outcomes: one SR favoured SIMO for both implant failure and marginal bone loss, while another reported TIMO had significantly more implant failures at 1 and 5 years, more prosthetic failures at 1 year, and more O-ring replacements at 5 years compared to SIMO. Quality appraisal revealed that none of the 7 SRs reached AMSTAR 2 "high" quality (2 critically low, 2 low, 3 moderate), indicating the evidence base underpinning the SIMO-vs-TIMO decision is methodologically inconsistent. The authors conclude SIMO corresponds with the conventional TIMO approach as a potentially effective treatment for edentulous mandible rehabilitation, but call for additional robust patient-level studies to refine clinical guidelines. This ingest is abstract-only — the retrieved PDF is a paywalled Wiley abstract landing page (no body text/tables), so no per-SR breakdown or AMSTAR 2 item-level scores could be extracted.

## Key Contributions

- Adds an "evidence about the evidence" layer (AMSTAR 2 appraisal of 7 SRs) on top of the primary SIMO-vs-TIMO SR/MA literature, rather than another direct meta-analysis of primary studies.
- Documents directional disagreement between included SRs on implant failure and MBL — one favours SIMO, another favours TIMO at specific timepoints — making clear the primary evidence is not uniform.
- Reports no included SR reached AMSTAR 2 "high" quality (2 critically low, 2 low, 3 moderate), a quality-of-evidence caveat that applies across the SIMO-vs-TIMO literature generally.

## Methodology

PRISMA-reported, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42024599304) umbrella review. Literature search without language restriction across Web of Science, ProQuest, SCIELO, KCI, Grants Index, Scopus, PubMed/MEDLINE, and Cochrane Library. 243 papers screened at title/abstract level → 7 SRs included, each critically appraised with AMSTAR 2. Outcomes: implant failure, peri-implant MBL, overall prosthetic complications and maintenance, patient satisfaction, OHRQoL.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Implant failure / MBL | 1 SR favours SIMO over TIMO on both; another SR reports TIMO significantly worse (more failures at 1Y and 5Y) |
| Prosthetic failure (1Y) | Significantly more with TIMO in one SR |
| O-ring replacement (5Y) | Significantly more with TIMO in one SR |
| AMSTAR 2 quality (7 SRs) | 2 critically low, 2 low, 3 moderate, 0 high |
| Overall conclusion | SIMO corresponds with conventional TIMO as a potentially effective edentulous-mandible rehabilitation |
