## Three-line Summary

Umbrella systematic review (JBI methodology) of 8 systematic reviews (6 SR-only, 1 SR+MA, 1 MA) evaluating oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) and patient satisfaction in edentulous patients treated with conventional complete dentures (CCDs) vs implant-retained overdentures (IRODs).

IRODs showed superiority over CCDs on both OHRQoL and satisfaction outcomes across the included reviews (evidence level 1+ to 1- per SIGN); the advantage was most pronounced when patients specifically wanted implant treatment or could not adapt to CCDs.

Financial factors and individual adaptive capability moderate patient tolerance of both prosthesis types; this is an abstract-only ingest (no PMC/PDF full text available, Wiley paywalled) so per-review breakdown and quality-appraisal detail could not be extracted.

## Summary

Sharka et al. (2019) conducted an umbrella systematic review, following JBI methodology, to appraise existing systematic reviews (with or without meta-analysis) comparing conventional complete dentures (CCDs) and implant-retained overdentures (IRODs) on two patient-centered outcomes: oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) and satisfaction, among edentulous patients. Searching Medline, Google Scholar, Cochrane Library, and PROSPERO, the authors identified 8 systematic reviews for synthesis (6 SR without meta-analysis, 1 SR+MA, 1 MA), graded using SIGN levels of evidence (ranging 1+ to 1-). The synthesis found that IRODs are superior to CCDs on both OHRQoL and satisfaction, but this advantage is more pronounced in patients who specifically demand implant treatment or who cannot adapt to CCDs — introducing patient preference and adaptive capability as effect modifiers rather than treating IROD superiority as universal. Financial factors were also identified as affecting patient tolerance of either treatment modality. This ingest is abstract-only (no PMC record; Wiley paywalled full text), so per-review AMSTAR/SIGN item-level detail and any pooled quantitative estimates are not available.

## Key Contributions

- Umbrella-review synthesis (review-of-reviews) rather than primary-study synthesis, appraising 8 systematic reviews at once using SIGN evidence-level grading.
- Confirms directional superiority of IROD over CCD for OHRQoL/satisfaction, consistent with later, larger syntheses already in the wiki.
- Introduces patient-centered effect modifiers (treatment demand, adaptive capability, financial factors) rather than presenting IROD superiority as unconditional — a nuance relevant to shared decision-making with edentulous patients.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Umbrella systematic review (JBI methodology).
- **Sources searched**: Medline, Google Scholar, Cochrane Library; PROSPERO checked for ongoing/recent reviews.
- **Population**: Edentulous patients treated with CCDs and/or IRODs.
- **Outcomes required for inclusion**: OHRQoL and/or patient satisfaction.
- **Quality appraisal**: SIGN levels of evidence (1+ to 1-) applied to the 8 included reviews.
- No further methodological detail (search dates, AMSTAR scoring, PRISMA flow) is recoverable from the abstract-only record.

## Results

- 8 systematic reviews synthesized: 6 SR-only, 1 SR+MA, 1 MA.
- SIGN evidence level of included reviews: 1+ to 1- (moderate-to-lower quality range, no reviews at the highest 1++ tier reported).
- Direction of effect: IRODs outperform CCDs on OHRQoL and satisfaction.
- Effect is amplified when patients want implant treatment or cannot adapt to CCDs.
- Financial constraints and individual adaptive capability moderate tolerance of both prosthesis types.
- No pooled numeric effect sizes (e.g., OHIP score differences, SMD/MD) are available from the abstract.
