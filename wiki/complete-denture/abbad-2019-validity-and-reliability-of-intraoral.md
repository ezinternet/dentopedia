## Three-line Summary

In-vivo method-comparison study (n=24 completely edentulous patients) comparing a MATLAB-linked intraoral digital gothic-arch tracer with a conventional intraoral tracer, and comparing upright vs supine recording of horizontal jaw relation (centric relation).

Supine recording was significantly more reproducible than upright (mean 3.80 upright vs 5.12 supine; median 4 vs 5; SD 0.81650 vs 1.33292; P=0.0001; Fischer's exact P=0.002), while upright–supine agreement was very weak and non-significant (Pearson r=−0.130, P=0.535); the digital tracer was reported as more valid than the conventional tracer.

Record horizontal jaw relation supine for higher reproducibility and prefer the digital tracer for visualization/data storage; transfer of the record to a virtual articulator is difficult, and the paper contains internal reporting inconsistencies (n=24 enrolled vs n=25 in the correlation table; r reported as r²).

## 세줄요약

생체 내 방법 비교 연구 (완전무치악 환자 n=24명) — MATLAB 연동 구내 디지털 고딕아치 트레이서와 기존 구내 트레이서를 비교하고, 직립·앙와위 체위에서 수평 악간관계(중심관계, Centric Relation, CR) 기록을 평가.

앙와위 기록이 직립보다 유의하게 재현성 높음 (평균 직립 3.80 vs 앙와위 5.12; 중앙값 4 vs 5; SD 0.81650 vs 1.33292; P=0.0001; Fischer 정확검정 P=0.002), 직립–앙와위 일치도는 매우 약하고 유의하지 않음 (Pearson r=−0.130, P=0.535); 디지털 트레이서가 기존 트레이서보다 유효하다고 보고.

임상 시사점: 수평 악간관계는 앙와위에서 기록하고 시각화·데이터 저장 측면에서 디지털 트레이서를 선호; 가상 관절기(Virtual Articulator)로 기록 이전이 어렵고, 논문에 내부 보고 불일치(등록 n=24 vs 상관표 n=25; r을 r²로 표기) 존재.

## Summary

Recording and transferring the horizontal jaw relation — **centric relation (CR)** — from the edentulous patient to the articulator is essential for restoring speech, function, facial appearance, and comfort. The conventional intraoral (needlepoint/gothic-arch) tracer is accurate but its readings cannot be visualized or stored. This in-vivo study (n=24 edentulous patients) introduced a **custom intraoral digital gothic-arch tracer coupled to MATLAB**, compared its validity against the conventional intraoral tracer, and tested whether **body posture (upright vs supine)** changes the reproducibility of the recorded horizontal mandibular position.

The authors report that the **digital tracing technique is more valid** than the conventional intraoral tracer, mainly because the tracing is visualized on screen and stored, and that **reproducibility is significantly higher in the supine position** than upright (mean 3.80 vs 5.12, P=0.0001). Agreement between the two postures was very weak and non-significant (Pearson r=−0.130, P=0.535), indicating the two postures do not yield interchangeable recordings. The explanation offered (citing prior conventional-method work) is reduced lateral pterygoid muscle activity in the supine position.

## Key Contributions

- First reported use of **MATLAB software to record horizontal mandibular position** with an intraoral digital tracer (authors' claim, Maharashtra).
- On-screen visualization and digital storage of the gothic-arch tracing, overcoming the conventional tracer's inability to visualize/store readings.
- Direct **validity comparison of intraoral digital vs conventional tracer** within the same edentulous patients.
- Adds the **body-posture dimension** (upright vs supine) to digital CR-recording reproducibility, reporting supine superiority (P=0.0001).

## Methodology

- **Design**: in-vivo method-comparison study, n=24 completely edentulous patients with well-formed ridges and inter-ridge distance ≥22 mm; exclusions: macroglossia, limited mouth opening, TMJ signs/symptoms, poor neuromuscular control, extraction socket. Ethics approval and consent obtained.
- **Prosthetic set-up**: conventional impressions → stone master cast → heat-cure acrylic bases (Acrayln-H) → working cast (Kalrock) → occlusal rims; facebow (Hanau Springbow); semi-adjustable articulator (Hanau Wide Vue II); tentative CR at established vertical dimension.
- **Conventional tracer**: custom CNC central bearing screw/stylus (DX200) + central bearing plate with graph paper on the mandibular rim, attached with modeling wax.
- **Digital tracer**: resistance intraoral sensor pad (2.5 cm × 3.5 cm, 400–500 mg) → extra-oral digitizer/A-D converter → laptop with MATLAB via MAX 232C; stylus contact yields X/Y coordinates; arrowhead apex defines CR.
- **Recording**: light closure to stylus contact → most retruded closure → guided left/right lateral → retruded → forward/backward → return to origin; repeated upright then supine, for both digital and conventional tracers.
- **Statistics**: Student's paired t-test, Fischer's exact test, Pearson's correlation.

## Results

Reproducibility of intraoral digital tracing by posture (Table 1):

| Measure | Upright | Supine |
|---|---|---|
| Mean | 3.8000 | 5.1200 |
| Median (range) | 4 (0–5) | 5 (3–7) |
| SD | 0.81650 | 1.33292 |

Statistical tests:

| Test | Comparison | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Student's paired t-test | Supine vs upright readings | P = 0.0001 (supine significantly higher) |
| Fischer's exact test | Association of supine with upright | P = 0.002 (significant) |
| Pearson's correlation | Upright ↔ supine agreement | r = −0.130 (abstract: "r² = −0.130"), P = 0.535 — very weak, downhill, not significant |

- Graph 1 / narrative: mean reproducibility upright **3.8**, supine **5.12** — supine yields maximum reproducibility.
- **Bottom line**: intraoral digital tracing is more valid than the conventional intraoral tracer (visualization/accuracy), and supine recording is more reproducible/accurate than upright.
- **Reporting caveats**: abstract labels the correlation as "r²=−0.130" while Table 2 calls it Pearson r=−0.130; correlation table reports n=25 per variable although n=24 were enrolled; Table 1 caption lists columns as "supine and upright" while the Discussion maps 3.8 to upright and 5.12 to supine (values presented using the Discussion mapping).
