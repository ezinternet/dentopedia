## Three-line Summary

Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR; Arksey and O'Malley + Joanna Briggs Institute protocol; OSF-registered osf.io/rf4xm) that maps methods for recording the maxillomandibular relationship (MMR) in CAD-CAM/computer-engineered complete denture (CECD) digital workflows, searching MEDLINE (PubMed), Scopus, and Science Direct.

Of 4779 records screened, only 10 studies were included; the review identified 4 commercially available CAD-CAM denture systems (only the Baltic System and WholeYouNexteeth are individually named in the abstract) and 3 innovative, non-commercial methods that abbreviate treatment to 2-4 visits, with the Baltic System and all 3 innovative techniques requiring a trial denture step.

Three techniques — 2 of the innovative methods plus WholeYouNexteeth — were rated lower difficulty regardless of operator skill, but this is a descriptive landscape map, not an accuracy comparison, and the authors caution that all techniques remain dependent on the learning curve and the patient's clinical condition.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this Clinical Oral Investigations (2024;28(6):320) scoping review ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00784-024-05706-2); PMID 38750145) by Ribeiro and colleagues (Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte and University of Pernambuco, Brazil; University of Zurich, Switzerland) sets out to identify the different methods used to record the maxillomandibular relationship (MMR) for CAD-CAM/computer-engineered complete dentures (CECDs). Recording MMR is one of the most critical steps in complete denture fabrication regardless of workflow, and the digital-CD market has produced multiple proprietary and experimental approaches to it; this review's purpose is to inventory those approaches, not to determine which is most accurate.

The review followed PRISMA-ScR reporting guidelines and the Arksey and O'Malley / Joanna Briggs Institute scoping-review methodology, with methods pre-registered on the Open Science Framework (osf.io/rf4xm). Two investigators independently searched MEDLINE (PubMed), Scopus, and Science Direct against the focus question "What are the different techniques for recording the maxillomandibular relationship in the digital workflow used in CECDs?" Of 4779 records identified, only 10 studies met inclusion criteria (clinical studies and reviews assessing MMR-recording techniques within a digital CECD-manufacturing workflow). A descriptive analysis then characterized each included study by design, manufacturing system, clinical steps, MMR-determination tools, and procedural difficulty level.

The review identified 4 commercially available CAD-CAM denture systems and 3 innovative (non-commercial) methods; the abstract individually names only two entities — the **Baltic System** among the commercial systems, and **WholeYouNexteeth** among techniques rated lower difficulty. The remaining 2 commercial systems and 1 of the 3 innovative methods are not recoverable from the available text and should not be guessed at. A trial denture step is inherent to the Baltic System and to all 3 innovative techniques, and these approaches are positioned as abbreviating treatment to 2-4 appointments. Three techniques overall — 2 innovative methods plus WholeYouNexteeth — showed lower difficulty levels "regardless of the professional skills" of the operator, a descriptive appraisal by the reviewers rather than a validated or patient-centered outcome measure.

Because this is a **scoping review**, it maps what techniques exist and how demanding they appear to be — it does not rank, pool, or statistically compare their accuracy. A reader should not cite this page for a comparative-accuracy claim; that evidence, if it exists, would have to come from the 10 primary studies themselves or from a subsequent systematic review/meta-analysis. The more striking finding is arguably methodological rather than clinical: only 10 of 4779 screened records (about 0.2%) qualified for inclusion, a sparse evidence base for a commercially active segment of digital prosthodontics whose systems are marketed well ahead of the indexed clinical literature that would validate them. The authors' own bottom line is a caution, not an endorsement: the techniques "are effective, however, rely on the learning curve and the patient's clinical condition" — method selection is not yet reducible to a simple ranking.

## Key Contributions

- First scoping review to specifically map **methods for recording MMR** within CAD-CAM/digital complete-denture (CECD) workflows, rather than the digital CD workflow as a whole (contrast with the broader clinical/laboratory-procedure scope of [[complete-denture/thu-2024-digital-complete-denture-clinical-laboratory]]).
- Catalogs two parallel tracks of MMR-recording innovation: **4 commercially available systems** (Baltic System and WholeYouNexteeth individually named) and **3 innovative, non-commercial methods**.
- Surfaces that a trial-denture step is **inherent** to the Baltic System and to all 3 innovative techniques, and that this class of techniques is associated with abbreviating treatment to **2-4 visits**.
- Reports a descriptive difficulty appraisal — 3 techniques (2 innovative + WholeYouNexteeth) rated lower difficulty independent of operator skill — useful for adoption planning but explicitly not a validated performance metric.
- The screening yield (10/4779, ~0.2%) is itself a finding: digital-CD MMR recording is thinly evidenced in indexed clinical literature relative to how aggressively the underlying systems are marketed.
- Methodologically rigorous for a scoping review: PRISMA-ScR compliant, Arksey and O'Malley + JBI protocol, pre-registered on OSF.

## Methodology

- **Design**: PRISMA-ScR–compliant scoping review; Arksey and O'Malley framework + Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) scoping-review protocol; pre-registered on the Open Science Framework (osf.io/rf4xm).
- **Focus question**: "What are the different techniques for recording the maxillomandibular relationship in the digital workflow used in CECDs?"
- **Search**: two investigators, independent search of MEDLINE (PubMed), Scopus, and Science Direct; 4779 records identified.
- **Inclusion criteria**: clinical studies and reviews assessing techniques for recording MMR using a digital workflow for manufacturing CAD-CAM (computer-engineered) complete dentures.
- **Data analysis**: descriptive, characterizing each of the 10 included studies by study design, manufacturing system, clinical steps, tools used to determine MMR, and difficulty level of the procedure — no meta-analysis or pooled accuracy comparison was performed (consistent with the scoping-review design).
