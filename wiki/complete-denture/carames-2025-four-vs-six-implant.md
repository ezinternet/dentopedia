## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cohort study of 943 patients receiving 5989 immediately loaded implants (2431 quadrants) supporting four- or six-implant full-arch fixed complete dentures (IFCDs), with mean follow-up 5.0 ± 3.2 years (range 0–17), using a standardized anatomy-driven clinical decision support system (CDSS).

Overall 2-year and 5-year cumulative survival rates were comparable between four and six IFCDs (2-year: 98.6% vs 98.8%, p=0.362; 5-year: 98.4% vs 98.7%, p=0.343); multilevel Cox models found jaw type (maxilla HR 1.815, p=0.037) and age (HR 1.026/year, p=0.030) — not implant number or alveolar atrophy classification — as significant predictors of implant loss.

Under a systematically applied patient-centered CDSS, four and six IFCDs achieve comparably high mid-to-long-term implant survival regardless of jaw or statistical model, though prosthetic/technical complications were not evaluated (beyond scope).

## Summary

This large single-center retrospective study (943 patients, 5989 implants, up to 17-year follow-up) directly compared implant survival between four- and six-implant immediately loaded full-arch fixed complete dentures (IFCDs), addressing a long-debated question in full-arch implant prosthodontics: does adding two more implants per arch materially change survival? Unlike much prior literature where implant number was chosen empirically, this cohort was treated under a standardized, anatomy-driven clinical decision support system (CDSS) — the Carames Classification (CC I–V, based on alveolar ridge height/width at defined positions) — that systematically mapped atrophy severity and risk factors (smoking, bruxism, systemic conditions) to a four- or six-implant restorative strategy. This design reduces (but does not eliminate) confounding-by-indication that plagues most retrospective implant-number comparisons.

The headline finding is a null result: overall 2-year and 5-year Kaplan–Meier cumulative survival rates were statistically indistinguishable between four and six IFCDs (98.6% vs 98.8% at 2 years; 98.4% vs 98.7% at 5 years), and this held at both maxillary and mandibular levels, although the maxilla showed the largest (still non-significant) gap early on (2-year CSR 97.7% vs 98.6%, p=0.084). Using causal DAG-derived Canonical Adjustment Sets to build multilevel Cox proportional hazard models — a more principled confounder-selection method than typical stepwise regression — the study found that jaw type (maxilla > mandible risk, HR 1.815) and patient age (HR 1.026/year) were the only significant predictors of implant loss at the overall level; implant number and the CC atrophy classification itself were not significant predictors once adjusted.

## Key Contributions

- Largest direct four-vs-six IFCD comparison to date (943 patients, 5989 implants), extending prior small-cohort or overdenture-focused comparisons to fixed, screw-retained full-arch prostheses with up to 17-year follow-up.
- Demonstrates that within a standardized CDSS-guided treatment pathway, implant number (4 vs 6) is not an independent risk factor for implant loss after adjustment — jaw type and age dominate.
- Applies causal DAG/Canonical Adjustment Set methodology to confounder selection, a methodologically stronger approach than typical univariate/stepwise adjustment common in the implant-survival literature.
- Documents qualitative differences in failure timing/clustering between four- and six-implant restorations (non-significant but clinically notable) that warrant further investigation with larger event counts.

## Methodology

Retrospective cohort of patients treated at a single private clinic (Lisbon, Portugal) from November 2004 to November 2021, all following a previously published standardized anatomy- and patient-centered CDSS. The CDSS classifies maxillary/mandibular alveolar atrophy (Carames Classification, CC I–V, least to most severe) from vertical/horizontal ridge dimensions at three predefined positions per quadrant, then maps CC class plus risk factors (systemic disease, smoking, bruxism) to a structured four- or six-implant restorative option (Option A/B), determining implant number, position, angulation, and need for grafting. All restorations were screw-retained, immediately loaded IFCDs (acrylic provisional, definitive prosthesis — porcelain-veneered/monolithic zirconia, ceramic, or acrylic-metal hybrid — at 6 months), placed by one experienced surgeon plus the clinic's prosthodontists, with standardized CBCT protocol and a fixed recall schedule (2-weekly for 12 weeks, then 4-monthly). Statistical analysis used Kaplan–Meier survival curves with Sidak-adjusted log-rank tests, plus single-predictor and multilevel Cox proportional hazard models (patient modeled as random effect) with confounders selected via causal DAG-derived Canonical Adjustment Sets, run separately for the overall cohort and the maxillary and mandibular subcohorts.

## Results

- Overall implant loss: 1.61% (four IFCD, 42/2608) vs 1.21% (six IFCD, 41/3381), p=0.240 — not significant, and consistent at both maxillary (2.4% vs 1.4%, p=0.130) and mandibular (1.4% vs 0.6%, p=0.109) levels.
- Kaplan–Meier CSRs: overall 2-year 98.6% vs 98.8% (p=0.362), 5-year 98.4% vs 98.7% (p=0.343); log-rank p-values non-significant at overall (0.249), maxillary (0.105), and mandibular (0.111) levels.
- Overall multilevel Cox model: restoration type (4 vs 6 IFCD) HR 1.678 (CI 0.908–3.102, p=0.098, NS); jaw type (maxilla vs mandible) HR 1.815 (CI 1.037–3.175, p=0.037, significant); age HR 1.026 per year (CI 1.003–1.050, p=0.030, significant). CC atrophy classification, implant position, bone regeneration, implant length, gender, bruxism, smoking intensity, and comorbidity count were all non-significant.
- Jaw-level Cox models: maxilla — 4 vs 6 IFCD HR 1.294 (NS, p=0.710), age HR 1.034/year significant (p=0.038); mandible — 4 vs 6 IFCD HR 1.905 (NS, p=0.240), bone regeneration and cigarette consumption trended toward but did not reach significance.
- Failure-pattern differences (non-significant but descriptively notable): mandibular four-implant restorations that failed did so later on average (476.3 ± 713.6 days) with lower clustering (26%) than six-implant restorations (111.8 ± 44.8 days, 67% clustered).
- Patient-level implant loss occurred in 7.2% (68/943) overall, with no significant between-group differences by jaw.
