## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 20 prospective studies / RCTs (913 patients — 542 fixed, 371 removable; 4414 standard implants; median 5-yr, up to 10-yr follow-up) pooling prosthetic and technical outcomes of full-arch implant-assisted restorations in the edentulous maxilla, framed by the ID-COSM lifespan domains.

Catastrophic failure was uncommon — overall prosthesis loss 4.6% (1.9% at 3–6 yr) and clustering-adjusted implant loss ~5.5% — but complication-free prosthesis survival was only 61.9% and patient-level complication-free implant survival 46.5%; fixed and removable were similar over comparable periods, yet the dominant technical complication differed (fixed: acrylic veneer fracture ~41%; overdenture: denture-base/tooth repair & reline ~91%).

Both modalities are predictable for survival but carry a high, ongoing maintenance burden, so patient counselling and a structured maintenance program matter more than the fixed-vs-removable choice itself (evidence certainty low to very low, high heterogeneity).

## Summary

This SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD420251001443; PRISMA 2020) asks what happens **prosthetically** — not just to implants — when the edentulous maxilla is restored with a full-arch implant-assisted prosthesis followed for ≥3 years. Twenty studies (13 RCTs, 4 cohort, 3 controlled clinical trials; 913 patients, 4414 standard implants) were pooled by random-effects meta-analysis of proportions and mapped to the 2023 **ID-COSM lifespan domain**. The clinical message is a **"low loss ≠ low burden" dissociation**: outright prosthesis loss (4.6% overall, 1.9% at 3–6 yr) and clustering-adjusted implant loss (~5.5%) are both uncommon, but **complication-free prosthesis survival is only 61.9%** and **patient-level complication-free implant survival only 46.5%** — meaning maintenance events requiring intervention are frequent even when the restoration "survives."

Fixed one-piece full-arch and removable overdenture restorations performed **similarly** on the headline outcomes over comparable follow-up (prosthesis loss 2.1% vs 1.8%; complication-free prosthesis survival 62.2% vs 60.2%; implant loss 5.5% vs 5.6%; MBL 0.78 mm vs 0.78 mm). What differs is the **kind** of maintenance: fixed prostheses are dominated by **veneering acrylic fracture (~41%)** (PFM porcelain fracture is rare at 2.8%), whereas overdentures are dominated by **denture-base/tooth repairs and relines (~91%)** plus periodic occlusal readjustment and occasional remakes/bar fractures. This complication-subtype-by-type breakdown is the piece the wiki's edentulous-maxilla evidence was missing, and it is what actually drives chair time and cost. Evidence certainty is **low** (prosthesis loss, complication-free prosthesis survival, technical complications) to **very low** (complication-free implant survival, implant loss), downgraded chiefly for high heterogeneity.

## Key Contributions

- Separates **survival from complication-free survival**, quantifying the gap: loss <6%, but ~38% of prostheses and ~53% of patients experience ≥1 implant/prosthesis complication requiring intervention.
- Gives **complication-subtype rates stratified by restoration type**, the maintenance-cost axis:
  - **Fixed one-piece**: acrylic veneer fracture **41.4%** (dominant), PFM porcelain fracture 2.8%, screw 7.0%, abutment 2.1%, framework fracture 0.7% (Ti cast) → 3.8% (laser-welded Ti).
  - **Overdenture**: denture base/tooth repair or reline **91.2%**, remakes 21.3%, occlusal readjustment 11.9%, bar fracture 10%, screw/abutment 1.8–3.6%.
- Shows **fixed ≈ removable** on prosthesis loss, complication-free prosthesis survival, implant loss, and MBL over comparable observation — moving the decision toward patient factors and maintenance planning.
- Confirms **MBL < 0.8 mm** at all analyzable timepoints for both types (below the peri-implantitis threshold), with ≥10-yr overdenture MBL stable at 0.64 mm.
- Documents the **patient-level vs implant-level gap** in complication-free implant survival (46.5% vs 72.2%): one complicated implant flags the whole patient.

## Methodology

- **Included**: RCTs + prospective clinical studies, ≥5 patients, ≥3-yr follow-up, fully edentulous maxilla, any fixed or removable full-arch implant-assisted reconstruction. Retrospective/case series/in vitro/animal/reviews excluded.
- **20 studies / 913 patients**: 542 fixed, 371 removable; median follow-up 5 yr (longest 10 yr, Slot 2022/2023); mean age 61.6 ± 3.6 yr. 8 overdenture studies, 13 fixed full-arch (Ferrigno 2002 both arms).
- **Implants**: 4414 standard endosseous; 4–8 per arch. Four-implant fixed cases used the **All-on-4 concept with tilted distal implants**. Systems: Nobel Biocare/Straumann/Astra Tech most common.
- **Loading**: conventional 13, immediate 5, early 3. Overdentures always conventional. Materials: fixed mostly **PMMA-on-metal** (few PFM; one PMMA-on-PEEK); overdenture retention **bar 336** > locator 42 > telescopic 10, all cast-metal frameworks.
- **Analysis**: random-effects meta-analysis of single proportions (Freeman–Tukey double arcsine); within-patient clustering via ICC = 0.07 (sensitivity 0.05–0.30); heterogeneity τ² (DerSimonian-Laird) + I²; Egger's test for prosthesis loss; RoB2 / ROBINS-I V2; adapted GRADE.

- **Dominant technical complications**: fixed → veneering **acrylic fracture 41.4%** (PFM porcelain only 2.8%); overdenture → **denture-base/tooth repair or reline 91.2%**, remakes 21.3%, occlusal readjustment 11.9%, bar fracture 10%.
- **Loading (prosthesis loss)**: immediate 3.0% (0.0–12.1), conventional-fixed 1.7% (0.4–4.1), conventional-overdenture 2.5% (0.3–6.7); Egger's z=0.91, p=0.361 (no significant small-study effect). Immediate-vs-conventional CIs wide/overlapping — no proven superiority.
- **≥10-yr overdenture MBL** stable at 0.64 mm (0.43–0.85). **PROMs** (9 studies): QoL consistently improved but too heterogeneous to pool.
- **Implant number**: evidence supports ≥4 implants for OD (All-on-4) or fixed one-piece, and 6 for a fixed one-piece without tilted implants; implant loss not correlated with prosthesis type.

## Clinical Bottom Line

For the edentulous maxilla, the fixed-vs-removable decision does **not** hinge on survival — both are predictable (loss <6%, MBL <0.8 mm). It hinges on the **maintenance profile the patient can accept**: a fixed prosthesis will most often need **acrylic veneer repairs** (favor higher-durability veneering / consider monolithic materials), while an overdenture will most often need **denture-base/tooth relines and occlusal adjustment**. Counsel that roughly one-third of prostheses and half of patients will need a complication-related intervention over 3–6 years, and build a structured recall/maintenance program in from the start. ≥10-yr fixed data are sparse (only 2 long-term studies, both overdentures), so treat long-term fixed projections cautiously.
