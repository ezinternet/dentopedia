## Three-line Summary

Controlled instrumented study in five healthy subjects comparing three intra-oral tracing-plate inclinations (horizontal 0 degrees, fully inclined along the condylar-point-to-stylus line, and half-inclined) during Gothic arch and tapping movements (n=30), with incisal and condylar points captured by a 6-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) jaw tracking system.

Mean tapping-point position differed significantly among the appliances (repeated-measures one-way ANOVA): the half-inclined plate recorded the 30 tapping points in a convergent area nearer to the intercuspal position (IP), and tap contact points lay anterior to the Gothic arch apex in every appliance.

Plate inclination materially shifts the condylar position registered by a tapping registration, but the abstract reports no numeric effect sizes, p-values or millimetre offsets and n=5, so the direction of the optimum is supported while its magnitude is not.

## 세줄요약

건강한 성인 5명(n=5)을 대상으로 구내 추적판(Intra-oral Tracing Plate) 경사도 3종 — 수평(0°), 완전경사(과상돌기점-스타일러스 연결선), 반경사 — 을 비교한 계측 연구; 고딕아치(Gothic Arch) 및 탭핑운동(Tapping Movement, n=30) 중 절치점(Incisal Point)과 과상돌기점(Condylar Point)을 6자유도(6 Degrees of Freedom, 6-DOF) 하악운동 추적시스템으로 기록.

반복측정 일원분산분석(Repeated-measures One-way ANOVA)에서 30개 탭핑점의 평균 위치는 기기 간 유의하게 달랐다; 반경사(Half-inclined) 기기가 탭핑점을 최대교두접촉위(Intercuspal Position, IP)에 가장 가까운 수렴 영역에 기록했고, 모든 기기에서 탭핑 접촉점은 고딕아치 정점보다 전방이었다.

추적판 경사도는 탭핑 기록으로 등록되는 과상돌기 위치를 실질적으로 바꾸지만, 초록에 수치적 효과크기·p값·mm 오차가 없고 표본이 5명뿐이어서 최적 경사의 방향만 지지되고 크기는 확인되지 않는다.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this 2004 Journal of Oral Rehabilitation study from Niigata University ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2842.2004.01190.x); PMID 15189311; no PMCID) asks which inclination of an intra-oral tracing plate best registers the condylar position during a tapping movement. Rather than comparing different jaw-relation recording modalities, it keeps the device concept constant and manipulates one geometric setting: the plate was horizontal (0 degrees to the occlusal plane), inclined along the line drawn from the condylar point to the stylus position at the occlusal plane, or set to half of that inclined angle. Five healthy subjects performed Gothic arch and tapping movements (n=30) at a 30 mm interincisal distance with the head Camper plane horizontal, while a 6-DOF jaw movement tracking system recorded both the incisal and the condylar point. Comparing the Gothic arch apex location and the distribution and mean position of the 30 tapping points relative to intercuspal position (IP) across the three appliances, the authors found a significant difference in mean tapping-point position; the half-inclined appliance concentrated taps nearer to IP, and in all appliances the tap contact points fell anterior to the Gothic arch apex. The practical reading is that the device's plate geometry is not neutral — it moves the condylar position that a tapping registration yields — but the retrieved abstract carries no numbers for how far.

## Key Contributions

- Treats **tracing-plate inclination** as an independent device variable, comparing three inclinations inside one intra-oral tracing device instead of contrasting record techniques.
- Uses 6-DOF jaw tracking to observe the **condylar point as well as the incisal point**, connecting a plate setting to the registered condylar position rather than to the incisal tracing alone.
- Shows the **half-inclined plate** produces the most convergent tapping-point area nearest the intercuspal position (IP).
- Demonstrates that **tap contact points lie anterior to the Gothic arch apex for every inclination**, qualifying the assumption that the apex and the tapping-registered position coincide.
- Holds posture and opening constant (30 mm interincisal distance, Camper plane horizontal, 30 taps), making the inclination comparison a controlled one.

## Methodology

- **Design**: instrumented comparative experiment, three appliances differing only in tracing-plate inclination, tested in the same subjects.
- **Subjects**: five healthy subjects.
- **Inclinations**: (1) 0 degrees to the occlusal plane — horizontal; (2) the angle formed by a line from the condylar point to the stylus position at the occlusal plane — inclined; (3) half of the inclined angle — half-inclined.
- **Task**: Gothic arch tracing plus tapping movements (n=30) at a 30 mm interincisal distance, head Camper plane horizontal.
- **Tracking**: 6-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) jaw movement tracking system; incisal and condylar points recorded.
- **Outcomes**: Gothic arch apex location; distribution and mean position of the 30 tapping points from intercuspal position (IP), at incisal and condylar points, across appliances.
- **Analysis**: repeated-measures one-way ANOVA.

## Results

- Mean position of the tapping points was **significantly different among the three appliances** (repeated-measures one-way ANOVA).
- The **half-inclined appliance** recorded tapping points in a convergent area **nearer to intercuspal position (IP)** than the horizontal or fully inclined appliances.
- In **all appliances**, the contact points of the tapping movement were **anterior to the Gothic arch apex**.
- No numeric effect sizes, p-values, angular offsets or condylar-coordinate values are present in the retrieved abstract; the findings above are directional only.
