## One-line Summary
RCT + FEA (n=45, 90 mini-implants, 24 months): bar-splinted mini-implants had less marginal bone loss (0.92 mm) and significantly lower rate of vertical bone resorption than unsplinted ball-type (1.43 mm); FEA confirmed 52% bone stress reduction with splinting.

## Summary
This study combined finite element analysis (FEA) and a clinical RCT to evaluate whether splinting two mini-implants with a bar superstructure reduces marginal bone loss compared to unsplinted ball-type mini-implants for mandibular overdentures. The FEA showed bone stress around unsplinted mini-implants (-118 MPa) was twice that around splinted implants (-56.8 MPa). In the RCT (n=45 edentulous patients, 90 mini-implants, anterior mandible, flapless, immediate load), the bar group had lower 24-month MBL (0.92 mm) than the ball group (1.43 mm). Vertical bone resorption was significantly more prevalent in the unsplinted group.

## Key Contributions
- Provides biomechanical (FEA) rationale for clinical finding: splinting reduces bone stress by ~52%
- First RCT with matched FEA for mini-implant splinting vs. non-splinting in overdentures
- Vertical bone resorption morphology (angular/dehiscence-type) significantly more common without splinting
- Demonstrates clinical translation of biomechanical principle to bone maintenance outcome

## Methodology
Parallel-arm RCT + FEA. n=45 edentulous patients (public health center, Chile). Group-ball (n=22, 44 mini-implants, ball-type attachment, unsplinted) vs. Group-bar (n=23, 46 mini-implants, prefabricated bar, splinted). Flapless placement, anterior mandible, immediate loading. Standardized retroalveolar radiographs at 5, 10, 15, 24 months. FEA: mathematical stress modeling of splinted vs. unsplinted mini-implant configurations.

## Results
- FEA: unsplinted -118 MPa vs. splinted -56.8 MPa (52% stress reduction)
- Clinical MBL at 24 months: ball 1.43 ± 1.26 mm vs. bar 0.92 ± 0.75 mm (trend; p not specified as significant for continuous measure)
- Vertical bone resorption: significantly more prevalent in ball group (chi-square, p<0.05)
- All implants immediately loaded; no survival data reported in abstract
