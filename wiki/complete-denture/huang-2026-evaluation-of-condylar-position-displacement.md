## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and single-arm random-effects meta-analysis (12 cross-sectional studies, 1994–2024; 1,693 records screened) compared condylar displacement from maximum intercuspation position (MIP) across five centric relation (CR) recording techniques: chin-point guidance (CPG), bimanual manipulation (BM), power-centric registration (PCR), Gothic arch tracer (GA), and active movement (AM).

Pooled results: CPG shifted only the Y-axis (SMD = 1.17, 95% CI 0.19–2.16, P = 0.02, I² = 98%) and PCR shifted all three axes (X SMD = 0.35, P = 0.0001; Y SMD = 0.76, P < 0.00001; Z SMD = 0.66, P < 0.00001), whereas BM, GA, and AM showed no significant displacement on any axis — with I² mostly above 90%.

All techniques showed a certain degree of reproducibility within a narrow range; the authors conclude CR is a reproducible condylar region rather than a single technique-specific point, so the clinical equivalence or superiority of any single method cannot be definitively determined.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰·단일군 메타분석(1994~2024년 횡단면 연구 12편, 1693편 스크리닝)이 최대교두감합위(Maximum Intercuspation Position, MIP)를 공통 기준으로 턱끝유도(Chin-Point Guidance, CPG)·양수조작(Bimanual Manipulation, BM)·로스 파워 중심위 기록(Roth's Power-Centric bite registration, PCR)·고딕아치 추적자(Gothic Arch tracer, GA)·능동운동(Active Movement, AM) 5가지 중심위(Centric Relation, CR) 기록법의 과두 변위를 비교했다.

풀링 결과 턱끝유도는 Y축만 유의하게 이동(표준화평균차(Standardized Mean Difference, SMD) = 1.17, 95% 신뢰구간(Confidence Interval, CI) 0.19~2.16, P = 0.02, I² = 98%), 파워 중심위는 세 축 모두 유의한 변위(X SMD 0.35, P = 0.0001; Y 0.76, P < 0.00001; Z 0.66, P < 0.00001)를 보였고, 양수조작·고딕아치·능동운동은 어느 축에서도 유의차가 없었다(대부분 I² > 90%).

## Summary

For full-mouth rehabilitation when the existing occlusal area no longer provides guidance, the clinician must reproduce a stable mandibular reference. Two candidates compete: the **maximum intercuspation position (MIP)** — tooth-determined habitual occlusion, the most reproducible practical closure — and **centric relation (CR)** — the condyle-to-glenoid-fossa relationship independent of tooth contact. This 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis does not ask "which technique is closest to an anatomic CR?" (the paper states there is no universal agreement on an ideal CR position); it asks **how far each CR recording technique places the condyle from MIP**, using MIP as a common, patient-specific zero point. A three-dimensional coordinate system is calibrated at MIP (X sagittal, +anterior; Y vertical, +superior; Z transverse), and the CR–MIP distance on each axis is pooled with a DerSimonian–Laird random-effects model.

**Bottom line**: only **power-centric registration (PCR)** displaced the condyle significantly on all three axes, and **chin-point guidance (CPG)** on the vertical axis alone; **bimanual manipulation (BM)**, the **Gothic arch tracer (GA)**, and **active movement (AM)** were not significantly different from MIP on any axis. Because heterogeneity was moderate to very high (I² up to 99%), the authors do not declare a winner: CR is framed as a **reproducible region rather than a single point**, all methods showed a certain degree of reproducibility, and technique choice can follow patient habit, intraoral conditions, and operator proficiency. In asymptomatic patients, either CR or MIP can serve as the functional treatment benchmark.

## Key Contributions

- **MIP-anchored quantitative comparison** of five CR techniques (CPG, BM, PCR, GA, AM) — a single-arm meta-analysis of 12 studies (1994–2024) with condylar offsets expressed as standardized mean differences (SMD) with 95% CI on X, Y, and Z, rather than a within-method reproducibility narrative.
- **Technique-specific displacement signature**: PCR significant on all axes; CPG significant only vertically (Y); BM, GA, and AM non-significant on all axes — with the Y axis identified as the direction most prone to variation.
- **Independent agreement with [[complete-denture/jasz-2024-centric-relation-methods-digital-mandibular-motion]]** that Dawson's bimanual manipulation yields condylar position comparable to MIP; Jász et al. is cited directly (ref. 16), including the observation that the gothic-arch vertex lay anterior to MIP.
- **CR-as-region conclusion** — pooled CR–MIP distances were "within the same order of magnitude," supporting a reproducible *region* of condylar position and the clinical statement that "different CR techniques can achieve consistent results."
- **Practical cautions** carried into the discussion: light 1–2 pound (4.4–8.8 N) force for CPG to avoid a forced posterior seat; neuromuscular deprogramming with cotton rolls or an anterior jig for 5–10 (or 15) minutes; avoid eccentric Gothic-arch recording at extreme jaw positions (>6 mm from center); operator skill and patient cooperation govern accuracy.

## Methodology

- **Design / reporting**: PRISMA-compliant systematic review + single-arm meta-analysis; PICO defined with MIP condylar location as the comparison benchmark; ROBINS-I for risk of bias; Review Manager 5.4; DerSimonian–Laird random-effects pooling; I² heterogeneity.
- **Search**: PubMed, EMBASE, Google Scholar, Cochrane Library (plus PubMed "related articles"), English only, no year restriction, through January 2026; terms included CR technique, MIP, chin-point guidance, bimanual manipulation, gothic arch, CR registration.
- **Eligibility**: complete/near-complete dentition, ≥5 subjects, quantitative condylar data permitting SMD, English original research; **excluded** edentulous/removable-denture patients, CR–CR-only comparisons, studies without MIP as reference, reviews/case reports/duplicates, CBCT/MRI/T-scan-only analyses, and percentage-only conclusions.
- **Screening flow (as stated)**: 1,693 studies → 97 full texts → 45 CR/MIP trials → **12 included**; exclusions included 11 without MIP reference, 7 edentulous, 3 TMD, 2 CBCT, 2 MRI, 1 T-scan, 2 CR-material accuracy, 3 percentage-only, 2 questionnaires.
- **Data handling**: two independent reviewers (L.S.H., Y.C.H.), third reviewer (I.T.W.) for discrepancies; condylar positions via CPI recording method in MIP-calibrated 3D coordinates; five technique subgroups analyzed on three axes.
- **Risk of bias**: the artifact states three trials had moderate and ten had low risk of bias (an internal count discrepancy for 12 included articles — flagged, not corrected).

Verbatim abstract Results: *"This study included 12 articles published between 1994 and 2024. Five different types of CR recording techniques were analyzed. Most techniques showed slight differences in CR and MIP with moderate to high heterogeneity. The chin-point guidance technique demonstrated a significant difference in the Y-axis (SMD = 1.17, P = 0.02), but no statistically significant differences were observed in the X and Z axes between CR and MIP. Roth's power-centric relation recording method was the only technique to show significant differences relative to MIP in all three axes. Dawson's bimanual method and Gothic arch tracer method for CR recording did not reveal any significant differences in condylar position on any axis."*

Verbatim abstract Conclusion: *"While the various methods for obtaining CR records in this study have their own advantages and disadvantages, and the CR positions obtained differed among the techniques, they all demonstrated a certain degree of reproducibility."*

**Clinical interpretation given by the authors**: most CR methods differ only slightly from MIP and, in the absence of TMJ pain or other symptoms, either CR or MIP can be used as a functional treatment benchmark; the Y axis is the most variable; operator skill, equipment (jigs, leaf gauges, wax), and patient status (TMJ, muscle tone) still matter; and because of high heterogeneity, "the clinical equivalence or superiority of any single method cannot be definitively determined."
