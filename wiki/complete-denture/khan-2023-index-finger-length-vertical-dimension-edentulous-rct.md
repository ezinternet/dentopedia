## Three-line Summary

Randomized controlled trial (n=80 randomized, 71 analyzed; Dow University of Health Sciences, Pakistan) correlating right index-finger (2D) length with the Sn-Me (subnasion-to-menton) facial distance used as occlusal vertical dimension (OVD), then fabricating complete dentures on the correlated OVD for an experimental group versus a conventional-method control group.

Finger and facial measurements were significantly greater in males than females; strong, positive, statistically significant Spearman correlations were found in both sexes (the only coefficient recoverable from the retrieved full text is female r=0.966, p<0.001); at 1-week follow-up, 97.0% of the experimental (index-finger) group reported satisfaction on an 8-item Visual Analog Scale (VAS) versus 84.2% of the control group, though no variable reached statistical significance for association with satisfaction.

The authors themselves conclude index-finger length is an "adjunct method" for OVD determination, not a standalone standard — a framing consistent with the wiki's governing anthropometric systematic review and meta-analysis (SR+MA); the study's 1-week satisfaction endpoint and single-population (Pakistani) sample meaningfully limit how far the correlation and satisfaction findings generalize.

## Summary

According to PubMed, this randomized controlled trial ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-33722-x); PMID 37150797; PMCID PMC10164741; trial registration NCT05153213) published in *Scientific Reports* (2023;13:7414) tests whether right **index-finger (2D) length** correlates with **occlusal vertical dimension (OVD)** — measured as the Sn-Me (subnasion-to-menton) facial distance — in **completely edentulous** patients, and whether fabricating complete dentures on that correlated OVD produces satisfactory outcomes. Anthropometric OVD-proxy methods exist precisely because edentulous patients usually lack pre-extraction records; this trial is notable for going beyond a cross-sectional correlation study to an actual RCT in which dentures were fabricated on the anthropometrically-derived OVD and patient satisfaction subsequently measured with a Visual Analog Scale (VAS).

The trial found statistically significant positive correlations between Sn-Me and 2D length in both sexes, with finger and facial measurements significantly larger in males — consistent with androgen-driven anthropometric dimorphism reported elsewhere in the literature. At 1-week follow-up, 97.0% of the experimental (index-finger-OVD) group reported satisfaction versus 84.2% of the control group, though the difference was not tied to any statistically significant covariate. The authors' own conclusion is measured: index-finger length is proposed as an **adjunct** method for OVD determination in low-resource settings, not a replacement for clinical judgment or a validated standalone standard.

## Key Contributions

- Extends the index-finger(2D)-to-OVD anthropometric correlation, previously established mainly in dentate populations, to **completely edentulous** patients via a properly randomized, CONSORT-following clinical trial rather than a cross-sectional correlation study alone.
- Rare pairing of an anthropometric correlation with a **downstream clinical outcome**: dentures were actually fabricated on the correlated OVD, and patient satisfaction (VAS) was measured after delivery — most anthropometric-OVD literature stops at the correlation coefficient.
- Reports sex-stratified anthropometric data (finger and facial length both significantly greater in males), reinforcing sex-specific anthropometric variation already flagged by the wiki's governing SR+MA.
- Uses an inexpensive, non-invasive, radiation-free measuring instrument (digital vernier caliper) requiring no specialized equipment, explicitly framed by the authors as suited to low-resource clinical settings.

## Methodology

- **Design**: parallel-group randomized controlled trial (CONSORT), single center — Department of Prosthodontics, Dow University of Health Sciences, Karachi, Pakistan. IRB approval IRB-1189/DUHS/Approval/2019/32; trial registration NCT05153213 (registered 2021-12-10).
- **Sample**: 250 patients screened by the principal researcher → 170 excluded → 80 randomized (coin toss by the principal investigator; patients blinded to group assignment) into experimental (anthropometric/index-finger OVD) and control (conventional OVD) arms, 40 per arm. 71 completed the trial after 9 lost to follow-up (38 control, 33 experimental). Mean age of the analyzed sample was 58.2 ± 7.4 years (range 45-77); 25 male (35.2%), 46 female (64.8%).
- **Eligibility**: inclusion — completely edentulous, both sexes, aged 30-80 years, males without a beard (for chin-landmark access), willing to consent. Exclusion — partial dentition; maxillofacial/myofascial disorders or orthognathic surgery history; finger deformity/disfigurement; head/neck neurological problems; bony defects or visible sharp spicules; nose/chin deformity; TMJ disorders; bulky chin area.
- **Measurement**: right index-finger (2D) length via digital vernier caliper (fingertip to the metacarpophalangeal crease, hand supine on a flat surface, nails trimmed); Sn-Me facial distance via Willis gauge, with the posterior occlusal plane oriented to the ala-tragus (Camper) line using a Fox plane. For the experimental group, the OVD used for denture fabrication was set from the correlated index-finger-length value; both groups otherwise followed standard denture fabrication protocol, with patients involved in tooth selection and positioning.
- **Outcome instrument**: 8-item Visual Analog Scale (VAS) questionnaire (each item 0-10, total range 0-80, cutoff 40) administered at 1-week post-insertion follow-up, covering: regularity of denture wear, comfort, pain during mastication, ease of swallowing, gagging, facial appearance, functional satisfaction, and perceived need for replacement.
- **Statistics**: SPSS v16.0; Shapiro-Wilk test for normality; Mann-Whitney U test for sex differences in Sn-Me distance and 2D length; Spearman's rho computed separately by sex for the Sn-Me vs. 2D correlation; Fisher's exact test for association between categorical variables and satisfaction level; significance threshold p ≤ 0.05.

## Interpretation — what this trial does and does not establish

- **A group-level correlation is not individual-patient validation.** A statistically significant Spearman correlation — even the strong female r=0.966 — describes how Sn-Me and 2D length co-vary across the sampled population; it does not by itself establish that predicting an individual patient's OVD from their finger length lands within a clinically acceptable error margin. The paper reports **correlation only**; it does not report an agreement or limits-of-agreement analysis (e.g., Bland-Altman) against directly-measured OVD, which would be the stronger form of validation evidence.
- **97.0% satisfaction at 1 week is a weak endpoint for OVD accuracy.** One week is far too short a window for OVD-related complications (angular cheilitis, muscle fatigue, TMJ symptoms, accelerated ridge resorption) to manifest, and near-ceiling satisfaction scores are common in new denture wearers regardless of OVD accuracy. The authors themselves flag the 1-week window as a limitation. This satisfaction figure should not be read as evidence that the anthropometrically-derived OVD was clinically correct — only that patients were not, on this early and coarse measure, dissatisfied.
- **Population/generalizability**: the trial is single-center and drawn from a Pakistani population; anthropometric finger-to-facial ratios are known to vary by ethnicity (the authors themselves note this when discussing why comparator studies' finger-length/OVD means differ from theirs).
- **Position relative to the wiki's governing evidence**: [[complete-denture/goyal-2026-anthropometric-methods-vertical-dimension-occlusion-sr-ma]] pooled 13 cross-sectional studies (3,275 subjects, 11 anthropometric methods) and found thumb length the strongest overall predictor (r≈0.63) with very high heterogeneity (I²≈99%) and high risk of bias, concluding anthropometric methods are **adjuncts only**. This RCT is a primary study whose topic sits inside that SR's scope; its own conclusion — index-finger length as an "adjunct method," not a standalone standard — is consistent with, not a challenge to, the SR's verdict. Nothing in this trial's design (single-center, non-blinded coin-toss randomization, short follow-up, no Bland-Altman agreement analysis) licenses treating the index-finger method as validated for individual-patient OVD determination.
