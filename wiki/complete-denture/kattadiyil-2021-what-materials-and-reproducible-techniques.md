## Three-line Summary

Best Evidence Consensus Statement (American College of Prosthodontists, J Prosthodont 2021;30(S1):34-42) on centric-relation (CR) recording techniques and materials in dentate and partially dentate populations.

From >3500 initial hits, restriction to systematic reviews, randomized controlled studies, meta-analyses and clinical trials selected 291 articles for further analysis; no pooled effect estimates are given in the abstract.

Chin-point guidance, bimanual manipulation, power centric, Gothic arch tracing, leaf gauge and anterior deprogramming can be comparably precise and clinically accurate; operator experience is critical, polyvinyl siloxane/polyether perform best, and virtual capture needs further study.

## Summary

This Best Evidence Consensus Statement (American College of Prosthodontists) asks two focus questions: which techniques are used to record centric relation (CR) and how reproducible they are in dentate/partially dentate populations, and what effect different recording materials have on CR reproducibility. A keyword search (CR, interocclusal records, CR accuracy, CR reproducibility, CR technique) returned more than 3500 articles; restricting to systematic reviews, randomized controlled studies, meta-analyses and clinical trials left 291 articles selected for further analysis.

The consensus conclusion is deliberately broad: chin point guidance, bimanual manipulation, power centric, Gothic arch tracing, leaf gauge and anterior deprogramming devices "can all be comparable in precision and clinical accuracy in regards to clinical relevance." Rather than declaring one technique superior, the statement makes **practitioner experience and familiarity with a particular technique** the critical determinant of accuracy. On materials, polyvinyl siloxane and polyether "consistently performed better in the broad range of studies," and virtual capture of CR is described as a potentially comparable recording medium that still requires further clinical study.

**Scope caveat**: the stated population is dentate and partially dentate — edentulous patients are outside the focus questions — so the statement is a technique/material framework rather than an edentulous-specific accuracy dataset. Because only the PubMed abstract was retrieved (no PMCID), the appraisal method and consensus process cannot be verified here, and no numeric precision estimates are available to quote.

## Key Contributions

- Elevates operator experience/familiarity over any single technique as the critical factor for CR-recording accuracy.
- Places six distinct technique families (guided, graphic, deprogramming) on a common "comparable precision/clinical accuracy" footing.
- Adds the recording-material axis: polyvinyl siloxane and polyether as the consistently better-performing materials.
- Flags virtual (digital) capture of CR as comparable but not yet clinically validated.

## Methodology

- Consensus statement derived from a structured keyword literature search, not a new clinical experiment.
- Search terms: CR, interocclusal records, CR accuracy, CR reproducibility, CR technique.
- Search limited to Systematic Reviews, Randomized Controlled Studies, Meta-analyses and Clinical Trials.
- No detail on consensus voting, reviewer count, or quality-appraisal instruments is available in the abstract (see abstract-only caveat above).

## Results

- Initial search: **>3500 articles**.
- After study-type restriction: **291 articles** selected for further analysis.
- **Techniques**: chin point guidance, bimanual manipulation, power centric, Gothic arch tracing, leaf gauge and anterior deprogramming devices can all be comparable in precision and clinical accuracy.
- **Operator**: practitioner experience and familiarity with the technique is critical for accuracy.
- **Materials**: polyvinyl siloxane and polyether consistently performed better across the broad range of studies.
- **Digital**: virtual capture of CR could serve as a comparable recording medium but requires further clinical study.
- No quantitative effect sizes or precision magnitudes are reported in the abstract.
