## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study (81 stomatognathically healthy, fully dentate subjects; 4 licensed clinicians; 6 centric-relation (CR) record types) compared the condylar positions produced by different CR registration methods.

Median spatial distance between the central bearing point (CBP) registration on the tip of the Gothic arch and all other CR records was 0.73 ± 0.24 mm (range 0–3.19 mm); median spatial values among the different CR records were 0.48–0.79 mm, almost all registration positions were slightly but significantly different, yet reproducibilities and SDs were similar.

No single CR record is universally "best" — unrefined wax-wafer registration with bimanual manipulation performed best for precision and time requirements, whereas CBP registration was best with regard to condylar position.

## Summary

This study asks the pragmatic question its title poses — is there a single "best" centric-relation (CR) record? Four licensed clinicians repeatedly recorded CR condylar positions in 81 stomatognathically healthy, fully dentate subjects using six different CR record types. Rather than judging each method against an external anatomic truth, the design mounts the mandibular cast with a central bearing point (CBP) registration on the tip of the Gothic arch and then measures how far the condylar positions from the other records sit from it and from one another. The result is that the methods cluster within a sub-millimetre median band but almost all sit slightly significantly apart — and reproducibility is comparable across them — so the answer is not one winner but a criterion-dependent choice: a wax wafer handled with bimanual manipulation is best for precision and chair time, while CBP registration best captures condylar position. ## Key Contributions

- **Direct answer to "is there a best CR record?"** — six CR record types compared within the same cohort on both the position they register and their reproducibility, rather than a single-method accuracy claim.
- **CBP as an explicit condylar-position reference** — mandibular casts mounted via a central bearing point registration on the tip of the Gothic arch, giving a defined reference for the inter-method distance calculations.
- **Separation of reproducibility from position** — similar reproducibilities and SDs alongside slightly significantly different positions means "best" splits by criterion (precision/time versus condylar position) instead of collapsing to one rank.

## Methodology

- **Design**: cross-sectional, repeated measures; 81 stomatognathically healthy, fully dentate subjects; 4 licensed clinicians.
- **CR records**: 6 different types, each registered three times; mean of the three registrations per method used for comparative calculations.
- **Mounting**: maxillary casts in articulators after individual facebow transfer; mandibular casts mounted with a CBP registration on the tip of the Gothic arch.
- **Outcome**: spatial distances (mm) between condylar positions across CR records; reproducibility and SD.
- **Evidence limit**: PubMed abstract only (no PMCID, full text not retrieved), so statistical tests and detailed methodology are unavailable. [확인]

## Results

- **CBP versus all other CR records**: median spatial distance of condylar positions **0.73 ± 0.24 mm** (range **0 to 3.19 mm**). [확인]
- **Between different CR records**: median spatial values **0.48 to 0.79 mm**. [확인]
- **Statistical separation**: positions of almost all registration types were **slightly significantly different** from each other. [확인]
- **Reproducibility**: reproducibilities and SDs between registrations were **similar**. [확인]
- **Practical winners**: **unrefined wax-wafer with bimanual manipulation** best for precision and time; **CBP registration** best for condylar position. [확인]
