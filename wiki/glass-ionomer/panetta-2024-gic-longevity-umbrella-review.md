## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42022320602) of 13 systematic reviews (J Funct Biomater 2024; MedLine/PubMed, WoS, Scopus, 132 initial references → 13 included) evaluating GIC (including RMGIC and compomer) longevity in primary and permanent teeth across 6-month to 6-year follow-ups.

None of the 13 SRs satisfied all AMSTAR-2 criteria and none had an a priori design; ROBIS rated 7/13 SRs at low risk of bias and 1/13 at very high risk; the pooled GRADE assessment was Class II recommendation / Level B evidence, reflecting heterogeneous and methodologically limited underlying evidence.

GIC longevity evidence is insufficient to draw definitive conclusions — performance varies by subtype (silver-reinforced GIC underperforms; compomer and RMGIC often outperform conventional GIC) and by cavity class (Class II fails more than Class I) — so future higher-quality RCTs with standardized reporting are needed before strong clinical recommendations can be made.

## 세줄요약

우산 리뷰 (PROSPERO CRD42022320602; J Funct Biomater 2024; MedLine/PubMed·WoS·Scopus 검색 132편 → 최종 13편, 추적기간 6개월–6년): 유치·영구치에서 GIC·RMGIC·컴포머의 임상 수명을 평가했다.

포함된 SR 중 AMSTAR-2 전 기준을 충족한 연구는 없었고, 사전 설계(a priori) SR도 없었으며, ROBIS 기준 13편 중 7편이 저비뚤림·1편이 고비뚤림 위험이었다. 종합 GRADE 평가는 권고등급 Class II·근거수준 Level B였다.

GIC 수명 근거는 방법론적 한계로 확정적 결론을 내리기 어렵다 — 아형별 차이가 존재하며(은강화 GIC는 열등, 컴포머·RMGIC는 대체로 전통 GIC보다 우수), Class II 수복이 Class I보다 실패율이 높다 — 표준화된 보고 기준을 갖춘 고질적 RCT가 더 필요하다.

## Summary
This umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42022320602, reported per PRIO) searched MedLine/PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus on 28 April 2022 (no language/date restriction) for systematic reviews of clinical trials evaluating GIC longevity/clinical performance in primary and/or permanent teeth (excluding studies on root caries, non-carious cervical lesions, or enamel alterations). The 132 initial references (37 PubMed, 51 Scopus, 44 WoS) were reduced to 13 included SRs after duplicate removal (41), title/abstract screening (67 excluded), and full-text assessment (24 screened, 11 further excluded). Follow-up across the 13 SRs ranged 6 months to 6 years. GIC types covered conventional GIC, RMGIC, compomers, low-/high-viscosity GIC, silver- and zirconia-reinforced GIC, several reviews comparing GIC to amalgam and composite, and both conventional and ART (atraumatic restorative treatment) placement technique.

AMSTAR-2 showed none of the 13 SRs met all criteria and none had an a priori design (4 SRs lacked a quality-analysis tool; 2 SRs used no risk-of-bias tool; 2 SRs — Amorim et al. and Mickenautsch et al. — disclosed a conflict of interest tied to GIC-technique teaching). ROBIS rated 7/13 SRs at low risk of bias (2 very low + 5 low), 1/13 (Ruengrungsom et al.) at high risk, and 1/13 at very high risk (negative on all domains). The GRADE-based pooled assessment across the evidence body was degree of recommendation Class II (conflicting evidence) and level of evidence Level B (derived from less-robust meta-analyses and single RCTs).

## Key Contributions
- Authors state this is the first umbrella review addressing GIC exclusively across both primary and permanent dentitions.
- Highest-tier (umbrella) synthesis of GIC longevity across dentitions, GIC subtypes, and placement technique (conventional vs. ART).
- Explicitly quantifies the weak methodological quality of the underlying SR pool with two independent tools (AMSTAR-2, ROBIS) plus a pooled GRADE class-of-recommendation/level-of-evidence rating.

## Methodology
Umbrella review. Databases: MedLine/PubMed, WoS, Scopus (searched 28 April 2022) plus reference-list hand-search; PICO(S) — P: primary/permanent teeth with restorations; I: GIC restorations; C: comparison across GIC types, composite, amalgam; O: restoration longevity; S: systematic review of clinical trials. Two independent reviewers for screening/extraction/quality appraisal, a third resolving conflicts. 13 SRs included (from 24 full-text-screened, from 132 initial references). Follow-up 6 mo–6 yr. Quality: AMSTAR-2; bias: ROBIS; pooled evidence grading: GRADE (Class/Level).

## Results
GIC longevity evidence is heterogeneous and limited by low underlying-review quality. Of the 13 SRs, only 6 stated a PICO question and 7 reported protocol registration; 2 did not report a search strategy.

By subtype (from the included SRs' findings):
- **Conventional HVGIC** (permanent teeth): high survival 2–6 years (surface texture, marginal discoloration/adaptation, anatomic form). ART techniques: 29.6–100% survival for single-occlusal restorations (4 mo–6 yr), 30.6–100% for multi-surface (6 mo–2 yr); ART annual failure rates higher in primary than permanent dentition; main failure modes were marginal defects, restoration loss, excessive wear, and retention loss.
- **HVGIC with resin coating**: comparable to conventional GIC/composite up to 5 years in Class I/II (fracture toughness, retention, abrasion resistance); one SR found it inferior to composite.
- **RMGIC**: good option for Class I/II; outperformed HVGIC and composite in some SRs (survival, fluoride release, biologic considerations), but no significant difference vs. conventional GIC/composite/compomer/amalgam in others. One SR found RMGIC and HVGIC had less favorable compressive strength/microleakage than zirconia-reinforced GIC.
- **Silver-reinforced GIC**: lower survival, higher secondary-caries recurrence.
- **Compomer**: generally outperformed conventional GIC (and sometimes RMGIC) on median survival time, surface texture, marginal discoloration/adaptation, decay, and fatigue/fracture resistance.
- **Class II restorations showed higher failure rates than Class I** across dentitions regardless of material or technique; cavity type and operator experience strongly influenced survival.

7/13 SRs low risk of bias (ROBIS); no SR satisfied all AMSTAR-2 criteria; none a priori designed; pooled GRADE = Class II recommendation / Level B evidence.
