## One-line Summary

In-vitro RCT-equivalent study (Lim 2022, J Mech Behav Biomed Mater, n=16/group) showing glycerin immersion during post-polymerization yields significantly higher DC and surface hardness than vacuum or air in 3D printed denture base resin, with GL+80°C producing the best mechanical properties.

## Summary

Lim et al. (Yonsei, 2022) evaluated whether post-polymerization oxygen shielding with glycerin immersion (GL) or vacuum (VA) improves material properties of 3D printed denture base resin compared to air curing (CON), with and without 80°C heating. Using FTIR, microhardness, flexural testing, water sorption/solubility, DMA, and SEM (n=16/group), the GL group showed the highest degree of conversion (DC) and hardness at both RT and 80°C. SEM confirmed more robust inter-layer interfaces in GL specimens. The VA group outperformed CON for flexural modulus at 80°C but showed lower storage modulus than GL. Glass transition temperature was similar across all groups (DMA), indicating oxygen shielding improves surface completion without altering fundamental network architecture. One caution: GL at room temperature showed elevated water solubility, suggesting heat elevation is preferable when using glycerin shielding.

## Key Contributions

- First direct comparison of glycerin immersion vs vacuum vs air as post-polymerization oxygen shielding strategies for a dental photopolymer
- Demonstrated GL produces highest DC regardless of temperature — glycerin is more effective than vacuum at eliminating surface oxygen inhibition
- Identified GL+80°C as optimal combination for maximizing flexural modulus and minimizing water solubility
- SEM confirmation that glycerin shielding produces denser, more continuous polymer structure at inter-layer interfaces
- Showed Tg is unaffected by oxygen shielding method — shielding improves surface completion, not bulk crosslink architecture

## Methodology

- **Design**: In-vitro, 3-group × 2-temperature factorial
- **Material**: 3D printed photosensitive denture base resin (photopolymer)
- **Groups**: GL (glycerin immersion), VA (5×10 Torr vacuum), CON (air; control)
- **Temperature sub-groups**: RT vs 80°C during post-polymerization
- **n**: 16 specimens/group/temperature
- **Outcomes**: DC (FTIR), microhardness (Vickers), flexural strength + modulus (3-point), water sorption/solubility (ISO), DMA (storage/loss modulus, Tg), SEM

## Results

| Metric | GL | VA | CON |
|---|---|---|---|
| DC (FTIR) | **Highest** | Middle | Lowest |
| Microhardness | **Highest** | Middle | Lowest |
| Flexural modulus (80°C) | **Highest** | Lowest | Middle |
| Water solubility (RT) | ⚠️ Elevated | Normal | Normal |
| Water solubility (80°C) | Normal | Normal | Normal |
| Glass transition Tg (DMA) | ~ similar | ~ similar | ~ similar |
| SEM inter-layer interface | **Robust/dense** | Moderate | Visible boundaries |

- GL and VA both reduce OIL vs CON; GL > VA for DC and hardness at both temperatures
- Effect magnitudes larger at 80°C than RT across all groups
