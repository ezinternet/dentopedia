## Three-line Summary

In vitro study (Sci Rep 2026) on micro-shear bond strength (µSBS) of TheraCem dual-cure self-adhesive resin cement to 3D-printed hybrid ceramic (Saremc Print Crowntec) across five surface treatment groups: untreated, NTAP alone, SB50, SB110, and the combination SP50 (50 µm sandblasting + NTAP).

The combined SP50 treatment achieved the highest µSBS at 2.70 ± 0.49 MPa, followed by SP110 (2.37), NTAP alone (2.35), SB110 (2.18), and SB50 (2.12 MPa); all absolute bond values were relatively low across all groups.

3D-printed hybrid ceramics are a challenging adhesion substrate; the combination of 50 µm sandblasting + NTAP plasma provides synergistic mechanical + chemical activation, though current bond values may be insufficient for high-stress clinical applications.

## Summary
In vitro study on micro-shear bond strength of TheraCem (dual-cure self-adhesive resin cement) to 3D-printed hybrid ceramic (Saremc Print Crowntec) after non-thermal atmospheric plasma (NTAP) ± sandblasting surface treatment. All bond strength values relatively low (2.12–2.70 MPa), suggesting 3D-printed hybrid ceramics present challenges for resin adhesion compared to milled materials.

## Key Contributions
- NTAP plasma: alternative to sandblasting for 3D-printed hybrid ceramics
- Combination SP50 (50µm SB + NTAP): highest µSBS — synergistic mechanical + chemical activation
- Low absolute bond values: 3D-printed polymers/hybrids bond differently from ceramics
- Clinical implication: surface treatment protocol for 3D-printed ceramics still evolving; current values may be insufficient for high-stress applications
