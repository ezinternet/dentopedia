## Three-line Summary

In vitro bench study (BMC Oral Health 2026; Chulalongkorn University group, Bangkok; 100 CAD/CAM ceramic specimens 10 × 10 × 3 mm³, 2 materials × 5 surface treatments, n = 10 per group) testing three chairside polishing systems (Komet ZR kit, OptraGloss, EVE Diapro/Diacera) after fine-grain diamond grinding of lithium disilicate (IPS e.max CAD) and ultra-translucent zirconia (KATANA UTML), with a 48-h *Streptococcus mutans* biofilm assay.

Polishing always improved roughness but never reached the glazed baseline (zirconia Ra glaze 0.02, grind 0.97, EVE 0.68, Komet 0.39, OptraGloss 0.40 µm; lithium disilicate Ra glaze 0.03, grind 1.29, EVE 0.86, Komet 0.51, OptraGloss 0.44 µm; P < 0.001), the three kits differed in roughness but not in bacterial adhesion (lithium disilicate P = 1.000; zirconia P ≥ 0.903), and polishing lowered biofilm only on zirconia (2.59% ground → 0.33–0.74%), not on lithium disilicate (8.51% ground → 6.38–8.11%).

Clinically, chairside polishing is a defensible endpoint after adjusting zirconia (Komet or OptraGloss bring Ra to 0.39–0.40 µm, under the 0.5 µm tactile threshold, and cut biofilm ~3.5–8×), whereas adjusted lithium disilicate gains no biofilm benefit and should be re-glazed when feasible — a single-species, 48-h static in vitro conclusion with no strength or aging data.

## 세줄요약

인비트로 실험실 연구 (BMC Oral Health 2026; 방콕 출라롱콘 대학 그룹; CAD/CAM 세라믹 시편 100개 10 × 10 × 3 mm³, 재료 2종 × 표면처리 5군, 군당 n = 10) — 리튬 디실리케이트 (Lithium Disilicate, LDS; IPS e.max CAD)와 초투명 지르코니아 (Ultra-Translucent Zirconia; KATANA UTML)를 미세 다이아몬드 버로 연삭한 뒤 세 가지 체어사이드 연마 시스템 (Komet ZR kit·OptraGloss·EVE Diapro/Diacera)으로 마무리하고, 표면거칠기 (surface roughness, Ra·Rz)와 48시간 스트렙토코커스 뮤탄스 (*Streptococcus mutans*) 생물막을 평가.

연마는 항상 조도를 개선했지만 유약 기준선에는 도달하지 못했고 (지르코니아 Ra: 유약 0.02, 연삭 0.97, EVE 0.68, Komet 0.39, OptraGloss 0.40 µm; LDS Ra: 유약 0.03, 연삭 1.29, EVE 0.86, Komet 0.51, OptraGloss 0.44 µm; P < 0.001), 세 키트는 조도에서는 유의차가 있었으나 세균 부착에서는 차이가 없었으며 (LDS P = 1.000; 지르코니아 P ≥ 0.903), 생물막 감소는 지르코니아에서만 나타났다 (연삭 2.59% → 0.33–0.74%; LDS는 연삭 8.51% → 6.38–8.11%).

임상적으로 조정된 지르코니아는 체어사이드 연마로 마무리해도 되지만 (Komet·OptraGloss가 Ra 0.39–0.40 µm로 촉각 인지 한계 0.5 µm 이하, 생물막 약 3.5–8배 감소), 조정된 LDS는 생물막 이득이 없어 재유약이 권장된다 — 단일 균종·48시간 정적 인비트로 모델이며 강도·노화 데이터는 없음.

## Summary

A two-material, five-arm bench study on the chairside finishing step that follows intraoral adjustment of CAD/CAM ceramic restorations. One hundred specimens of lithium disilicate glass-ceramic and ultra-translucent zirconia were left glazed, ground with a fine-grain diamond bur until a marker line disappeared (simulating occlusal/interdental adjustment), or ground and then polished for 60 s at 10,000 rpm under ~2 N with one of three two-step diamond/polyurethane kits (Komet ZR kit, OptraGloss, EVE Diapro for lithium disilicate / EVE Diacera for zirconia). Roughness was measured by contact profilometry (Ra, Rz), topography by SEM, the abrasive tips themselves by SEM-EDS, and bacterial retention by 48-h *S. mutans* biofilm area coverage under CLSM after LIVE/DEAD staining.

Every kit significantly reduced roughness after grinding, but none reproduced the glaze: glaze stayed at 0.02–0.03 µm Ra while polished surfaces settled at 0.39–0.86 µm, i.e. 13–40× rougher than glaze and above Bollen's 0.2 µm plaque threshold. Which kit won depended on the material — OptraGloss was numerically best on lithium disilicate (0.44 µm) and Komet on zirconia (0.39 µm), with the two statistically tied on both. EVE was consistently the roughest, and its high-gloss polisher carried the largest diamond particles (~8.69 µm Diapro / 4.58 µm Diacera vs 4.38 µm Komet, 4.22 µm OptraGloss).

The biological outcome split by material rather than by kit: on zirconia, polishing cut biofilm coverage from 2.59% (ground) to 0.33–0.74% with no difference between kits and OptraGloss statistically indistinguishable from glaze; on lithium disilicate, polished surfaces stayed statistically identical to the merely ground surface (6.38–8.11% vs 8.51%), seven-fold above zirconia at the same treatment level. The authors read this as material surface-energy/hydrophilicity dominance over roughness, and recommend re-glazing for adjusted lithium disilicate while accepting chairside polishing for adjusted zirconia.

## Key Contributions

- **Head-to-head of three chairside polishing protocols on two ceramic classes**, framed as material-specific (EVE Diapro/Diacera) versus universal (OptraGloss, Komet) — a comparison the material-specific-kit marketing implies but rarely tests directly.
- **Quantifies the residual roughness gap left by adjustment**: even the best kit ends 13–20× above glaze (0.39–0.40 µm vs 0.02 µm on zirconia) and above the 0.2 µm plaque threshold, against a 0.5 µm patient-detectability ceiling.
- **Material-dependent ranking**: OptraGloss best on lithium disilicate (0.44 µm, tied with Komet at 0.51), Komet best on zirconia (0.39 µm, tied with OptraGloss at 0.40); EVE worst on both (0.86 / 0.68 µm).
- **Pairs roughness with a microbiological endpoint** on the same specimens: zirconia polishing reduces *S. mutans* coverage 3.5–8×, lithium disilicate polishing reduces it not at all.
- **Shows Ra is an insufficient predictor of adhesion**: Komet and OptraGloss gave essentially identical zirconia Ra (0.39 vs 0.40 µm) with different biofilm (0.74% vs 0.33%) and visibly different SEM scratch patterns.
- **Characterizes the abrasives** by SEM/EDS: diamond in a C/O polyurethane matrix, EVE with the largest particles, OptraGloss stage 2 additionally containing Al and Si (likely Al₂O₃/SiC), which the authors offer as the explanation for its finer finish.
- **Explicit material-specific clinical protocol**: re-glaze lithium disilicate when feasible; chairside polish is acceptable for zirconia because of its intrinsically lower bacterial retention.

## Methodology

- **Materials**: IPS e.max CAD lithium disilicate glass-ceramic (57–80% SiO₂, 11–19% Li₂O) and KATANA Zirconia UTML ultra-translucent zirconia (87–92% ZrO₂ + HfO₂, 8–11% Y₂O₃).
- **Specimens**: 100 blocks 10 × 10 × 3 mm³, low-speed saw under water cooling, planarized with 300/400/600/1200-grit SiC paper, ultrasonically cleaned in 70% ethanol; lithium disilicate crystallized (Programat P300), zirconia sintered (inFire HTC speed).
- **Groups (n = 10/material)**: glazed control; grinding only; grind + Komet ZR Polishing Kit Set 4622; grind + OptraGloss; grind + EVE (Diapro for lithium disilicate, Diacera for zirconia). Glaze: AKZENT Plus GLAZE LT fired per manufacturer.
- **Adjustment simulation**: fine-grain diamond bur ZR 8881 (Komet) in a high-speed handpiece with water cooling until a permanent marker line was fully removed; bur changed every 5 specimens.
- **Polishing**: slow-speed handpiece, 10,000 rpm for all kits, ~2 N finger pressure, linear motion, 60 s, one trained operator.
- **Roughness**: contact profilometer Form Talysurf Series 2, cutoff 0.8 mm, 1 mN, 0.5 mm/s, 5 mm evaluation length, 3 traces per specimen (mean analysed).
- **Topography/chemistry**: SEM at 100×; SEM-EDS of the polisher tips at 3,000×, 15 kV.
- **Biofilm (n = 5/group)**: *S. mutans* ATCC 25175, standardized to 3 × 10⁸ cells/mL (1.0 McFarland), specimens autoclaved and pre-coated with artificial saliva 2 h, 48-h biofilm at 37 °C/5% CO₂, LIVE/DEAD BacLight staining, CLSM 63× (488 nm SYTO 9 / 543 nm PI), maximum-intensity z-projection, 5 fields per specimen (25 per group), area fraction in ImageJ.
- **Statistics**: Shapiro–Wilk; Welch's ANOVA + Games–Howell for Ra/Rz; Kruskal–Wallis + Bonferroni for biofilm area; α = 0.05. Sample size a priori by G*Power (f = 0.40, power 0.80) → <10 per group needed.

## Results

**Roughness, mean ± SD (µm), n = 10 per group**

| Material | Glaze | Grind | EVE | Komet | OptraGloss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lithium disilicate Ra | 0.03 ± 0.00 | 1.29 ± 0.07 | 0.86 ± 0.09 | 0.51 ± 0.10 | 0.44 ± 0.09 |
| Lithium disilicate Rz | 0.11 ± 0.03 | 8.45 ± 0.50 | 5.66 ± 0.62 | 3.33 ± 0.64 | 3.02 ± 0.77 |
| Zirconia Ra | 0.02 ± 0.00 | 0.97 ± 0.22 | 0.68 ± 0.22 | 0.39 ± 0.06 | 0.40 ± 0.12 |
| Zirconia Rz | 0.09 ± 0.00 | 6.41 ± 1.35 | 4.50 ± 1.43 | 2.34 ± 0.27 | 2.60 ± 0.57 |

- All treatment effects significant (P < 0.001 for the between-group comparisons). Glaze is significantly smoother than everything; every polished surface is significantly smoother than ground; **no polished surface reaches glaze**.
- Komet and OptraGloss are statistically tied on both materials (different letters only versus EVE and glaze); EVE is significantly rougher than both on lithium disilicate and tied with grind on zirconia Ra.
- Against clinical thresholds: only OptraGloss clears 0.5 µm on both materials; Komet clears zirconia (0.39) and sits exactly at the limit for lithium disilicate (0.51); EVE fails both (0.86 / 0.68); nothing except glaze clears 0.2 µm.
- SEM: diamond grinding leaves deep grooves and scratches; all polished surfaces are smoother but retain scratches; OptraGloss surfaces appear the most uniform.
- Abrasive particle size (high-gloss polisher): EVE Diapro ~8.69 µm > EVE Diacera ~4.58 µm > Komet ~4.38 µm > OptraGloss ~4.22 µm; EDS shows C/O matrices with Si/Fe/Ti/Al/Cr/Mn/Ni traces, OptraGloss stage 2 additionally Al + Si.

**Biofilm area coverage, median % (IQR), n = 5 per group**

| Material | Glaze | Grind | EVE | Komet | OptraGloss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lithium disilicate | 0.14 (0.06–0.34) b | 8.51 (5.83–13.35) a | 7.30 (3.00–13.98) a | 6.38 (3.20–12.53) a | 8.11 (4.56–8.03) a |
| Zirconia | 0.15 (0.05–0.38) c | 2.59 (2.07–3.30) a | 0.60 (0.31–0.95) b | 0.74 (0.33–1.64) b | 0.33 (0.20–0.69) bc |

- **Lithium disilicate**: glaze far below all others; the three polished groups are not different from grinding only (P = 1.000) → polishing changed roughness but not bacterial retention.
- **Zirconia**: grinding worst; all three kits reduce coverage versus ground; no difference between kits (P ≥ 0.903); OptraGloss is statistically indistinguishable from glaze (0.33% vs 0.15%) though 2.2× higher in absolute terms.
- **Cross-material**: lithium disilicate retains 3–25× more *S. mutans* than zirconia at every treatment level, attributed to surface free energy/hydrophilicity rather than roughness.
