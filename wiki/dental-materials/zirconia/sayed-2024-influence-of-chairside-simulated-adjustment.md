## Three-line Summary

Two preshaded multilayered monolithic zirconia ceramics — Nacera Pearl Multi-Shade (PSZ/3Y-TZP) and Ceramill Zolid FX Multilayer (FSZ) — showed significant baseline color differences despite identical Vita shade designations, explained by differing Y₂O₃ content and manufacturer shade categorization methods. Chairside diamond-bur adjustment followed by 14-day chlorhexidine gluconate (ChG) mouthwash immersion produced clinically perceptible color changes (ΔE = 1.0–3.3) and contrast ratio increases (CR Y ≥ 0.07, indicating translucency reduction) in both ceramics regardless of adjustment kit. Glazed MLZ specimens were the most resistant to ChG-induced color and translucency changes, confirming that all clinical adjustments should be completed before final glazing.

## Summary

This in vitro study investigated the effect of simulated chairside adjustment and chlorhexidine gluconate (ChG) mouthwash on the color stability and translucency of two preshaded multilayered monolithic zirconia (MLZ) ceramics: Nacera Pearl Multi-Shade (NPM; PSZ, Y₂O₃ 4–6 mol%) and Ceramill Zolid FX Multilayer (CZM; FSZ, Y₂O₃ 8.5–9.5 mol%). Disc-shaped specimens (1.2 mm thick, 14 mm diameter; n = 72 per ceramic) were CAD/CAM milled, sintered, glazed, then subjected to diamond-bur grinding followed by finishing and polishing with either the manufacturer-recommended adjustment kit or a third-party (swapped) kit. After adjustment, specimens were immersed in artificial saliva or 2% ChG mouthwash (10 min × 2/day × 14 days). Color (CIELAB ΔE) and translucency (contrast ratio CR = Yb/Yw) were measured with a portable colorimeter, and changes assessed against clinical thresholds (CAT/CPT: ΔE ≥ 3.3 acceptable; ΔE 1.0–3.3 perceptible; CR Y ≥ 0.07 translucency perceptible).

Key results: (1) NPM and CZM differed significantly in baseline color despite identical Vita shade designation; (2) NPM was sensitive to third-party adjustment kits (color increase ΔE 2.74→2.90 in ChG); CZM was compatible with both kits; (3) ChG produced clinically perceptible but acceptable color changes (ΔE = 1.0–3.3) and translucency reduction in all adjusted specimens; (4) glazed specimens showed minimal or no color/translucency changes in ChG.

**Clinical implication**: When ChG mouthwash must be prescribed for MLZ restoration patients, complete all chairside adjustments before final glazing to protect the restoration's optical properties.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes that preshaded MLZ ceramics with the same nominal Vita shade may differ substantially in CIELAB baseline color due to differences in yttria content (PSZ vs FSZ) and manufacturer shade-categorization schemes
- Demonstrates differential sensitivity of PSZ (NPM) vs FSZ (CZM) to third-party adjustment kits — NPM showed significant color increase; CZM remained compatible
- Provides first evidence that ChG mouthwash causes clinically perceptible (but clinically acceptable) color and translucency changes in preshaded multilayered MLZ after chairside adjustment
- Glazing as protective barrier: confirms glazed surfaces resist ChG-induced optical degradation, reinforcing the "adjust before glazing" protocol

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Study type | In vitro comparative experiment |
| Ceramics | NPM (PSZ, 3Y-TZP, Y₂O₃ 4–6%); CZM (FSZ, Y₂O₃ 8.5–9.5%) |
| Specimens | Disc 1.2 ± 0.2 mm × 14 mm; n = 72 per ceramic; ISO 6872-2015 |
| Surface groups | Glazed (control); Adjusted Kit 1 (manufacturer-rec.); Adjusted Kit 2 (third-party) |
| Immersion media | Artificial saliva; 2% ChG mouthwash (10 min × 2/day × 14 days) |
| Total groups | 12 (6 per ceramic) × n = 12 per group |
| Color measurement | Portable CIELAB colorimeter; ΔE via √[(ΔL)² + (Δa)² + (Δb)²] |
| Translucency | TP (white/black backing); CR = Yb/Yw; threshold Y ≥ 0.07 |
| Statistics | One-way ANOVA + post hoc LSD; Shapiro-Wilk; p < 0.05 |

## Results

**Baseline color (before adjustment/immersion):**
- NPM ≠ CZM in color despite same Vita A1+A2 shade (p < 0.05 by ANOVA)
- No significant baseline TP difference between ceramics

**Adjustment kit effect:**
- NPM + third-party kit: color increase (ΔE saliva: 0.33→0.43; ΔE ChG: 2.74→2.90); CR values exceeded threshold (Y ≥ 0.07)
- CZM: compatible with both kits in saliva (Gp CDS ≈ Gp CSS, no significant color difference)
- NPM more manufacturer-kit-dependent than CZM

**Chlorhexidine immersion effect:**
- All ChG-immersed groups: clinically perceptible color change (ΔE = 1.0–3.3) — perceivable by skilled operators, clinically acceptable
- All ChG-immersed groups: CR > threshold (Y ≥ 0.07) → clinically detectable translucency reduction
- TP differences between ceramics in all ChG groups statistically significant except glazed condition
- Saliva-immersed groups: ΔE ≤ 1.00 (not clinically appreciable) across all conditions

**Glazing effect:**
- Glazed + ChG: least/no color change; no significant TP change between the two ceramics
- Glazed + saliva: no clinically appreciable color changes (ΔE ≤ 1.00)
- Glazing provides surface smoothness that resists chemical staining by ChG
