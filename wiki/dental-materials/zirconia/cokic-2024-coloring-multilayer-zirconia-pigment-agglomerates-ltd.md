## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study (3 multilayer 5Y-PSZ discs — Lava Esthetic A2 [Zr-AGG_A2] and Lava Esthetic Bleach [Zr-AGG_BL], both 3M Oral Care, vs Katana STML A2 [Zr-NoAGG], Kuraray Noritake; pigment distribution by light/fluorescence microscopy, electron probe microanalysis and nano-SEM; phases by XRF/XRD; pigment aging by micro-Raman).

Pigments in both Lava Esthetic grades formed Er-, Hf- and Al-containing agglomerates of small grains (0.57 µm A2, 0.38 µm Bleach vs 0.92 µm surrounding) with lower Y₂O₃; Zr-AGG_A2 had the lowest aging resistance, with transformation occurring only inside the agglomerates; Katana STML had a typical 5Y-PSZ microstructure, higher translucency (TP₀₀) and higher flexural strength but lower mechanical reliability; hardness and toughness did not differ.

The way a multilayer zirconia is coloured can create local low-yttria spots that degrade first — bulk yttria and cubic content (all grades 42–55 wt% cubic) do not guarantee aging resistance; in-vitro, abstract-only, aging conditions not stated.

## Summary

Čokić et al. (2024, Journal of Dental Research; KU Leuven) examined whether the pigments used to colour monolithic multilayer 5Y-PSZ change its optical, mechanical and aging behaviour. Two pigmented Lava Esthetic grades (A2 and Bleach) were compared with Katana STML A2, which has no pigment agglomerates. In both Lava Esthetic grades the pigment additions formed large, yellow- and blue-fluorescent agglomerates containing Er, Hf and Al, made of grains much smaller than the surrounding matrix and with lower Y₂O₃ content. Aging-induced transformation in Zr-AGG_A2 was confined to these agglomerates, and that grade had the lowest aging resistance. Bulk composition was similar across grades (tetragonal phase with 4.2–5.7 mol% Y₂O₃; 42–55 wt% cubic ZrO₂). The pigment-free Katana STML had higher translucency and flexural strength but lower mechanical reliability; hardness and fracture toughness did not differ. The authors attribute the lower optical and mechanical properties and reduced aging resistance of the Lava Esthetic grades to the rare-earth-oxide agglomerates added as pigments.

## Key Contributions

1. **Pigment agglomerates as local weak spots**: Er/Hf/Al-containing agglomerates with small grains and lower Y₂O₃ in pigmented multilayer 5Y-PSZ
2. **Localized aging**: in Zr-AGG_A2, transformation degradation occurred exclusively within the agglomerates — lowest aging resistance of the three
3. **Bulk composition is not enough**: all three grades had high-yttria tetragonal phase and 42–55 wt% cubic phase, yet aging behaviour differed
4. **Optical/mechanical trade-off**: pigment-free Katana STML — higher TP₀₀ and flexural strength, lower reliability; no difference in hardness or toughness

## Methodology

- Materials: Lava Esthetic A2 (Zr-AGG_A2), Lava Esthetic Bleach (Zr-AGG_BL) — 3M Oral Care; Katana STML A2 (Zr-NoAGG) — Kuraray Noritake; all multilayer 5Y-PSZ discs
- Pigment composition/distribution: light and fluorescence microscopy, electron probe microanalysis (EPMA), nano-scanning electron microscopy
- Chemical and phase composition, aging behaviour: X-ray fluorescence (XRF), X-ray diffraction (XRD)
- Pigment aging sensitivity: micro-Raman spectroscopy
- Properties: translucency parameter (TP₀₀), fracture toughness, Vickers hardness, biaxial strength, hydrothermal stability
- Source artifact: PubMed abstract only (no full text); hydrothermal aging conditions not stated in abstract

## Results

| Measure | Zr-AGG_A2 (Lava Esthetic A2) | Zr-AGG_BL (Lava Esthetic Bleach) | Zr-NoAGG (Katana STML A2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pigment agglomerates | Yes — Er/Hf/Al, small grains 0.57 µm | Yes — Er/Hf/Al, small grains 0.38 µm | None; typical 5Y-PSZ microstructure |
| Surrounding grain size | 0.92 µm | 0.92 µm | — |
| Aging resistance | Lowest; transformation only within agglomerates | Not separately reported | Not separately reported |
| Translucency (TP₀₀) | Lower | Lower | Significantly higher |
| Flexural strength | Lower | Lower | Significantly higher |
| Mechanical reliability | Higher | Higher | Lower |
| Hardness / toughness | No significant difference | No significant difference | No significant difference |

- All grades: tetragonal ZrO₂ with 4.2–5.7 mol% Y₂O₃; 42–55 wt% cubic ZrO₂
