## Three-line Summary
In vitro bench study (Applied Bionics and Biomechanics 2022, n = 48 ST zirconia specimens 10×10×2 mm sintered 7 h at 1500 °C; 8 groups of n = 6) testing whether two three-step intraoral polishing systems (Youdent vs Toboom) at 20/40/60 s per step can restore surface roughness after 20 s of diamond-bur occlusal adjustment, with profilometry, SEM and XRD.

Ground-but-unpolished zirconia had the highest roughness; 20 s of polishing was significantly rougher than 40 s or 60 s, the two systems were equivalent at 20 s but Toboom was significantly smoother at 40 s and 60 s (best = Toboom 60 s), and even 60 s stayed rougher than the glazed surface, while XRD peaks after all polishing protocols matched the untreated baseline — no monoclinic phase transformation.

Clinically, polishing reaches an acceptable surface within 20 s and 60 s per step is the effective ceiling, and because polishing heat did not alter the crystal structure, the concern after chairside adjustment is residual stress and surface roughness rather than t→m transformation — but flat specimens and no strength testing limit extrapolation.

## 세줄요약
시험관내 (In vitro) 벤치 연구 (Applied Bionics and Biomechanics 2022, n = 48, ST 지르코니아 10 × 10 × 2 mm, 1500 °C 7 h 소결, 8군 × n = 6): 3단계 구강내 연마 시스템 2종 (Youdent vs Toboom) × 단계당 20 · 40 · 60 s를 다이아몬드 버 20 s 조정 후 적용하고 표면조도계 · SEM · XRD로 평가.

연삭 후 미연마군이 표면거칠기 (surface roughness, Ra) 최고; 20 s 연마는 40 s · 60 s보다 유의하게 거칠고, 20 s에서는 두 시스템 간 차이 없으나 40 s · 60 s에서는 Toboom이 유의하게 매끄러움 (최적 = Toboom 60 s); 60 s도 유약면보다는 거칠었으며 XRD 피크는 모든 군에서 미처리 기준과 일치 — 단사정상 (monoclinic phase, m-ZrO₂) 상전이 (phase transformation) 없음.

임상적으로 연마 20 s에 이미 허용 조도 도달, 단계당 60 s가 실효 상한이며, 연마열이 결정구조를 바꾸지 않으므로 조정 후 문제는 상전이보다 표면거칠기와 잔류응력 — 단, 평면 시편·강도시험 부재로 외삽에 한계.

## Summary

Chen and Zeng asked a chairside question that every occlusal adjustment raises: after a diamond bur opens the surface, how long and with which polishing system must you polish to get back to an acceptable surface — and does the friction heat of polishing push tetragonal zirconia into the monoclinic phase? Forty-eight ST zirconia specimens (UPzir Solid Zirconia, 10×10×2 mm, 7 h at 1500 °C) were divided into a glazed/ground control group (GL), a ground-unpolished group (GR, diamond bur 20 s under water cooling), and six polishing groups crossing two three-step intraoral systems (Youdent, Toboom) with 20/40/60 s per step (n = 6 each). Roughness was measured with a stylus profilometer, topography with SEM at ×1000, and crystal structure with XRD against an untreated reference specimen.

Grinding alone produced the roughest surfaces, and none of the polishing protocols brought the surface below the glazed condition, but all polished groups landed inside the clinically acceptable roughness range. Polishing time showed diminishing returns — 20 s was significantly rougher than 40 s or 60 s, and 40 s vs 60 s was not consistently different — while the system brand mattered only beyond 20 s, where Toboom was significantly smoother than Youdent. XRD was the decisive negative finding: peak positions and distribution after grinding and after every polishing duration matched the baseline pattern, so neither adjustment nor up to 60 s of polishing caused a measurable t→m transformation; the authors attribute any reported peak shifts in the literature to residual stress rather than structural change.

## Key Contributions

- Separates the two variables a clinician controls — **polishing duration** (20/40/60 s per step) and **polishing system** (Youdent vs Toboom) — in a realistic three-step post-adjustment sequence.
- Establishes a **time ceiling**: 20 s is already clinically acceptable, 40 s and 60 s add little, and 60 s is the best achievable with the tested systems (still rougher than glazed).
- Establishes a **brand/duration interaction**: systems are equivalent at 20 s but diverge at 40 s and 60 s, with Toboom significantly smoother and SEM showing shallower scratches.
- Provides a **paired roughness + XRD** dataset on the same specimens, showing that polishing heat up to 60 s does not produce monoclinic transformation.
- Reframes the post-adjustment risk: what polishing changes is surface stress and topography, not crystal phase — so phase-transformation fears are not the reason to limit polishing time.

## Methodology

- **Specimens**: CAD/CAM-designed ST zirconia disc (3Shape), cut on a Cradle machine, 48 specimens of 10×10×2 mm, sintered 7 h at 1500 °C (upfire C1, Upcera); one random specimen XRD-scanned as untreated reference.
- **Groups (n = 6)**: GL = finished/glazed control surface (polishing + grinding + glazing); GR = diamond bur 20 s, water-cooled, parallel to the surface, no polishing; then Youdent (Y20/Y40/Y60) and Toboom (T20/T40/T60) three-step polishing at 20/40/60 s per step, pressure-free, direction parallel to the grinding grooves, followed by 30 s ultrasonic cleaning in distilled water.
- **Roughness**: DEKTAK-XT stylus profilometer (Bruker), GB/T 1031-2009 reference, scan perpendicular to the grinding direction, 3 measurements averaged per specimen.
- **Topography**: one random specimen per group, Pt-coated, field-emission SEM (SIGMA 500, ZEISS) at ×1000.
- **Crystal structure**: one random specimen per group, XRD (Ultima TV, Rigaku), patterns compared with the untreated reference.
- **Statistics**: SPSS 24.0, mean ± SD, one-way ANOVA + LSD, independent-samples t-test, chi-square; P < 0.05.

## Results

- **GR highest roughness** of all groups; glazed surface (GL) smoothest; all polished groups within the clinically acceptable range.
- **Duration**: 20 s significantly rougher than 40 s and 60 s within the same system; 40 s vs 60 s not significantly different within a system.
- **System at matched duration**: Y20 ≈ T20 (no significant difference); Y40 and Y60 significantly rougher than T40 and T60 → Toboom superior from 40 s onward.
- **Best protocol**: Toboom 60 s per step, still rougher than the glazed surface.
- **SEM**: GL smooth and regular without obvious defects; GR showed wide, deep scratches and dense pits; scratching decreased progressively with polishing time; Youdent surfaces remained slightly rougher than Toboom at equal duration.
- **XRD**: peak distribution after grinding and after all polishing protocols matched the untreated baseline → no t→m phase transformation detected.
- **Context values cited (not measured here)**: plaque retention drops when Ra < 0.2 μm (Bollen); enamel Ra ≈ 0.45–0.65 μm sets the clinical acceptance band; typical clinical polishing pressure ≈ 2 N; t→m transformation involves a 3–4 % volume increase.
