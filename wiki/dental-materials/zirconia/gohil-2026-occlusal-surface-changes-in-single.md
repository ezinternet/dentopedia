## Three-line Summary

Retrospective observational study (Cureus 2026; n=120: 64 single-ISZRs, 56 three-ISZRs; posterior sites; 12-month) using 3D digital scan superimposition (Geomagic Control X) to quantify occlusal wear and antagonist enamel wear in implant-supported zirconia restorations (임플란트 지지 지르코니아 보철물, ISZRs).

Single-ISZRs showed significantly less occlusal wear at 12 months (14.6 ± 3.4 vs 19.8 ± 4.1 μm; p<0.001; d=-1.39) and less antagonist enamel wear (15.2 ± 3.8 vs 19.7 ± 4.5 μm; p<0.001); overall complication rate was also lower (9.4% vs 25.0%; p=0.022).

Restoration configuration significantly influences wear trajectory and complication burden; multi-unit ISZRs warrant precise occlusal adjustment, adequate surface polishing, and regular maintenance.

## Summary

This retrospective cohort study analyzed 120 digital records from K. M. Shah Dental College, India, to compare occlusal surface changes between single-unit and three-unit implant-supported zirconia restorations (ISZRs) over 12 months. All restorations were Nobel Procera full-contour monolithic zirconia on NobelActive implants; digital impressions were taken with the 3Shape TRIOS 3 intraoral scanner at delivery, 6 months, and 12 months and superimposed in Geomagic Control X to measure vertical occlusal surface height loss.

Baseline characteristics were comparable between groups (age, sex, smoking status, arch location, region). Both groups showed progressive wear over time, but three-ISZRs exhibited significantly greater occlusal wear at 6 months (11.2 vs 8.4 μm) and 12 months (19.8 vs 14.6 μm). Antagonist enamel wear followed the same pattern. Individual complication types (chipping, fracture, debonding) did not differ significantly, but the cumulative complication rate was significantly higher for three-ISZRs (25.0% vs 9.4%).

The authors attribute the greater wear in three-unit prostheses to differences in occlusal force distribution across the larger restorative surface and to complex connector-geometry stress concentrations — a hypothesis supported by finite-element analysis literature they cite. The study concludes that findings should inform post-delivery maintenance rather than influence the initial indication for each restoration type, as the two designs address different clinical needs.

## Key Contributions
- Clinical (in-vivo) reference wear values for single-unit and three-unit ISZRs at 6 and 12 months using 3D superimposition
- Demonstrates that restoration type (not material) is a significant modifier of wear rate; large effect sizes (d=-1.39 at 12 months)
- Documents higher overall complication burden for three-unit ISZRs despite comparable individual failure modes
- Methodology: excellent inter-examiner reliability (ICC 0.92) for digital superimposition-based wear measurement

## Methodology

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Retrospective observational cohort |
| n | 120 (64 single-ISZR, 56 three-ISZR) |
| Implant | NobelActive (Nobel Biocare); posterior premolar/molar |
| Restoration | Nobel Procera full-contour monolithic zirconia, CAD/CAM (3Shape Dental System 2019) |

| Exclusions | Bruxism/parafunction; excessive adjustment; incomplete records; uncontrolled systemic disease |

## Results

**Occlusal surface wear (μm, mean ± SD):**

| Time | Single-ISZR | Three-ISZR | p | Cohen's d |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 2.1 ± 0.8 | 2.3 ± 0.9 | 0.204 | -0.24 |
| 6 months | 8.4 ± 2.1 | 11.2 ± 2.8 | <0.001 | -1.14 |
| 12 months | 14.6 ± 3.4 | 19.8 ± 4.1 | <0.001 | -1.39 |
| Δ 0→12 mo | 12.5 ± 3.2 | 17.5 ± 3.8 | <0.001 | -1.43 |

**Secondary outcomes:**
- Antagonist enamel wear at 12 months: 15.2 ± 3.8 (single) vs 19.7 ± 4.5 μm (three); p<0.001; d=-1.09
- Occlusal roughness (rough/degradation): 18.8% vs 32.1%; p=0.091 (NS)
- Need for occlusal adjustment: consistently higher in three-ISZR at all time points (NS individually)
- Overall complications: 9.4% vs 25.0%; p=0.022; φ=0.21
