## Three-line Summary

Polishing Y-TZP crowns (3M Lava Esthetic) with Meisinger coarse or fine polishers at 20,000 RPM consistently exceeded the 5.5°C pulpal damage threshold (≥8°C rise within 2 min) in a novel water-circulation in vitro model simulating intrapulpal blood flow. At 10,000 RPM, heat generation was significantly lower (p < .0001) and remained below threshold for most groups. Clinicians should avoid 20,000 RPM intraoral polishing of zirconia and prefer low-speed (10,000 RPM) or intermittent protocols.

## 세줄요약

Y-TZP 크라운 (3M Lava Esthetic)을 20,000 RPM으로 연마 시 치수 손상 역치(5.5°C 상승)를 초과하는 발열(2분 내 ≥8°C 상승)이 조대 및 미세 연마 모두에서 발생했다. 10,000 RPM에서는 대부분 역치 이하로 유의하게 낮았으며(p < .0001), 치수 혈류를 모의한 순환수 모델을 최초 적용한 연구다. 구내 연마 시 고속(20,000 RPM)은 피하고 저속·간헐 연마를 권장한다.

## Summary

This in vitro study measured intrapulpal temperature changes during intraoral polishing of full-contour 이트리아 안정화 정방정계 지르코니아 다결정 (Yttria-stabilized Tetragonal Zirconia Polycrystal, Y-TZP) crowns. Using a novel water-circulation system to simulate 37°C intrapulpal temperature and blood flow, the authors tested Meisinger coarse and fine diamond polishers (Ivoclar Vivadent 2-step system) at 10,000, 15,000, and 20,000 RPM. The key finding is that the manufacturer's maximum recommended speed (20,000 RPM) generates heat above the 5.5°C pulpal damage threshold for both polisher types, while 10,000 RPM stays safely below threshold for most configurations. 주사전자현미경 (Scanning Electron Microscopy, SEM) confirmed that coarse polishers better removed grinding-induced surface defects.

## Key Contributions

1. **Novel simulation model**: Water-circulation system (37°C) with submersible pump to mimic intrapulpal blood flow — more valid than static water or room-temperature designs used in prior literature.
2. **Speed-dependent danger threshold**: 20,000 RPM consistently exceeded the 5.5°C pulp damage threshold (Zach & Cohen 1965) within 60 s regardless of polisher type. 10,000 RPM was significantly safer (p < .0001).
3. **Polisher efficacy (SEM)**: Coarse polishers (abrasive ~20–50 μm) more effectively smoothed grinding marks; fine polishers (~5–10 μm) less effective even at higher speeds. Higher speed caused greater polisher tip wear.
4. **Crown thickness**: At 20,000 RPM, there was no significant difference in heat generation between thicker (1.0–1.5 mm) and thinner (<1.0 mm) Y-TZP crowns.

## Methodology

- **Material**: 24 CAD/CAM-milled Y-TZP crowns (3M™ Lava™ Esthetic; ZrO₂ + 5 mol% Y₂O₃; 800 MPa flexural strength); sintered + glazed
- **Tooth models**: Two plastic mandibular first molars — Tooth 1 (1.0–1.5 mm occlusal reduction) and Tooth 2 (<1.0 mm reduction)
- **Pre-polishing**: Diamond bur grinding at 30,000 RPM × 30 s, no coolant (simulates occlusal adjustment)
- **Polishing**: 6 groups × 10 cusps each = n = 120 total; 2 min per sample; no coolant
- **Groups**: Coarse at 10k/15k/20k RPM; Fine at 10k/15k/20k RPM
- **Temperature recording**: K-type thermocouple on crown intaglio surface; EasyLog USB data logger
- **Statistics**: Kruskal-Wallis test; p < .05 significant

## Results

| Group | Tooth 1 ΔT (2 min) | Tooth 2 ΔT (2 min) | Above 5.5°C threshold? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coarse 10,000 RPM | 3.50°C | 5.10°C | No / borderline |
| Coarse 15,000 RPM | 6.90°C | 4.50°C | Yes / No |
| **Coarse 20,000 RPM** | **8.40°C** | **8.00°C** | **Yes / Yes** |
| Fine 10,000 RPM | 5.71°C | 5.35°C | Yes / borderline |
| Fine 15,000 RPM | 6.35°C | 4.40°C | Yes / No |
| **Fine 20,000 RPM** | **8.45°C** | **8.25°C** | **Yes / Yes** |

- 10,000 RPM vs 20,000 RPM: p < .0001 for all comparisons
- 20,000 RPM: no significant difference between the two crown thicknesses (p > .99)
- Manufacturer-recommended 60 s: all 20k RPM groups still exceeded threshold (ΔT ≥ 6°C)
- Fine polishing at 10k RPM produced a continuous temperature rise over 2 min (slowest heat climb but eventual accumulation)
