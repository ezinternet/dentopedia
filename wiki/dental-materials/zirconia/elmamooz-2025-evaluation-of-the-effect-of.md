## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study of 56 Ceramill ZI monolithic zirconia discs divided into 7 groups testing chairside grinding, polishing, or reglazing across one vs. two surface areas, followed by ISO 13356 autoclave hydrothermal aging (134 °C, 2 bar, 5 h), with biaxial flexural strength (BFS) tested by the piston-on-three-ball method per ISO 6872:2024.
Polishing after grinding achieved the highest BFS (G6: 999.2 ± 81.1 MPa vs. control 688.8 MPa); reglazing after grinding produced the lowest (G4: 528.9 ± 17.5 MPa); both surface treatment (p < 0.0001) and adjustment extent (p < 0.0001) significantly affected BFS, while their interaction was not significant (p = 0.114).
Rubber polishing should follow chairside grinding of monolithic zirconia; reglazing post-adjustment is contraindicated; wider adjustment area (43% surface) consistently yields higher BFS than smaller area (21.5%), though all groups exceeded 500 MPa, maintaining clinical mechanical adequacy.

## Summary

This in-vitro study was the first to specifically evaluate how the *extent* of chairside adjustment area (one vs. two zones, corresponding to 21.5% and 43% of specimen surface) affects the biaxial flexural strength of monolithic zirconia after hydrothermal aging. Fifty-six standardized Ceramill ZI discs were fabricated per ISO 6872:2024, glazed, assigned to seven treatment groups, and subjected to autoclave aging (134 °C, 2 bar, 5 h, equivalent to approximately 15–20 years in vivo per ISO 13356:2015). BFS was measured using the piston-on-three-ball method. Polishing with the EVE Diacera two-step diamond rubber kit after grinding significantly enhanced BFS above all other groups, while reglazing after grinding reduced BFS below the unground control. The extent of adjustment independently and significantly influenced BFS — wider adjusted areas yielded higher strength regardless of treatment modality, likely due to greater extent of transformation toughening-related compressive stress generation. All groups maintained BFS above 500 MPa, confirming sufficient mechanical integrity for clinical use even after the most adverse treatment.

## Key Contributions

- Novel isolation of adjustment *extent* (surface area) as a significant independent variable on zirconia BFS after aging
- Grinding alone increased BFS above control (781.8 vs. 688.8 MPa), attributed to t→m transformation toughening compressive stress
- Polishing (EVE Diacera two-step rubber-diamond kit) produced the highest BFS among all treatments (up to 999.2 MPa)
- Reglazing post-grinding consistently reduced BFS below control due to crack formation from repeated firing and reverse phase transformation
- Wider adjustment area (two zones) produced significantly higher BFS than one zone (828.7 vs. 736.3 MPa overall)
- Interaction between surface treatment and extent of adjustment was not significant (p = 0.114)

## Methodology

- **Design**: In-vitro, two-way ANOVA, 7 groups × n = 8
- **Material**: Ceramill ZI (Amann Girrbach), Y-TZP, sintered 1450 °C/2 h, then glazed
- **Specimen**: Ø15 × 1.2 ± 0.2 mm disk
- **Groups**: G1 control; G2 grind 1-area; G3 grind+polish 1-area; G4 grind+reglaze 1-area; G5 grind 2-areas; G6 grind+polish 2-areas; G7 grind+reglaze 2-areas
- **Aging**: ISO 13356:2015, 134 °C, 2 bar, 5 h autoclave (≈15–20 yr in vivo)
- **BFS test**: piston-on-three-ball (ISO 6872), 1 mm/min, treated surface under tension
- **Statistics**: two-way ANOVA + Tukey HSD, α = 0.05
