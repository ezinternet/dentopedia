## Three-line Summary

In-vitro interface-fracture study (J Adhes Dent 2022; Lava Frame Y-TZP bars, 6 groups × 45 specimens; bilayer four-point bending n=13/group) testing 0.3 mm diamond grinding at 20,000 rpm without irrigation, followed by regenerative heat treatment at 900 °C/60 min or 1000 °C/30 min.

Grinding raised monoclinic content from 1.2 to 2.6 vol% and Ra from 0.17 to 1.16 µm (p<0.005), and both heat treatments reset monoclinic to 0.0 vol% without changing Ra (p=0.22); the zirconia/porcelain strain energy release rate stayed statistically equal in all six groups (C 6.8 / C900 6.6 / C1000 7.6 vs G 8.3 / G900 8.0 / G1000 5.7 J/m², p>0.05).

Regenerative heat treatment is therefore compatible with *veneered* zirconia — it reverses t→m at 900 °C or 1000 °C without sacrificing veneer adhesion — but it does not restore surface smoothness (~1.0 µm Ra persists after grinding) and the absolute interface toughness (~6–8 J/m²) is 5–10× below metal-ceramic.

## 세줄요약

체외 계면파괴 연구(J Adhes Dent 2022; Lava Frame Y-TZP 막대, 6군 × 45개; 이층 4점 굽힘 군당 n=13): 0.3 mm 다이아몬드 연삭(20,000 rpm, 무수냉) 후 900 °C/60분 또는 1000 °C/30분 재생 열처리.

연삭은 단사정 상 (monoclinic phase)을 1.2 → 2.6 vol%, 표면거칠기 (surface roughness, Ra)를 0.17 → 1.16 µm로 올렸고(p<0.005), 두 열처리 모두 단사정 상을 0.0 vol%로 되돌렸으나 Ra는 그대로였다(p=0.22). 지르코니아/도재 계면의 변형에너지 방출률 (strain energy release rate, G)은 6군 모두 통계적 차이 없음(C 6.8 / C900 6.6 / C1000 7.6 vs G 8.3 / G900 8.0 / G1000 5.7 J/m², p>0.05).

따라서 재생 열처리는 베니어 지르코니아에서도 상전이 (phase transformation)를 되돌리면서 베니어 접착을 해치지 않는 안전한 추가 공정이나, 연삭 후 거칠기(~1.0 µm)는 회복되지 않고 계면 인성 절대값(~6–8 J/m²)이 금속-도재의 5–10분의 1에 불과하다.

## Summary

Candido et al. tested whether the heat treatment used to reverse grinding-induced tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation damages the bond between a Y-TZP framework and its veneering porcelain. Bar-shaped Lava Frame Y-TZP specimens were either left as sintered or ground 0.3 mm with a medium-grit diamond stone at ~20,000 rpm without water, then heat treated at 900 °C/60 min or 1000 °C/30 min. Grinding produced the expected monoclinic increase and a roughly sevenfold rise in arithmetic mean roughness; both heat treatments eliminated the monoclinic phase entirely while leaving roughness statistically unchanged. Veneered bilayer specimens were then tested in four-point bending and the interfacial strain energy release rate was computed with the Charalambides approach: all six groups landed between 5.7 and 8.3 J/m² with no significant effect of grinding or heat treatment. The practical reading is that regenerative heat treatment is safe for the porcelain interface, but grinding damage to surface finish is not repaired by firing, and the zirconia/porcelain interface remains intrinsically much weaker than metal-ceramic regardless of the adjustment protocol.

## Key Contributions

- **Interface-level safety of regenerative HT**: neither 900 °C/60 min nor 1000 °C/30 min degraded the zirconia/porcelain energy release rate — the first direct answer to "can I fire a veneered zirconia crown after occlusal adjustment without risking the veneer bond".
- **Complete t→m reversal at both protocols**: monoclinic fraction 2.6 vol% (ground) → 0.0 vol%, with tetragonal fraction back to 85.8–88.5 vol% and the grinding-induced cubic/rhombohedral fraction (31.3 → 14.2/11.5 vol%) largely normalized.
- **Phase restoration ≠ surface restoration**: Ra stayed at 1.01–1.07 µm after HT vs 1.16 µm as-ground (p=0.22); SEM showed grooves, debris and microcracks persisting with only slight edge rounding.
- **Negative adhesion finding**: grinding did not improve interface toughness (p=0.52) despite the transformation-toughening literature, and HT did not either (p=0.29) — the "grind to improve bond" premise is not supported at the interface.
- **Quantified the zirconia/porcelain weakness**: ~6–8 J/m² vs cited ~39.4 J/m² (Ni-Cr) and ~80 J/m² (gold-treated Ti) metal-ceramic values — a mechanistic basis for veneered-zirconia chipping/delamination.
- **Mixed fracture mode documented**: residual liner porcelain on the zirconia surface in every group, i.e. cohesive-in-liner plus adhesive-to-zirconia failure rather than pure interfacial separation.

## Methodology

- **Design**: in vitro, six parallel groups, two-way ANOVA (grinding × heat treatment).
- **Material**: partially sintered Y-TZP (Lava Frame, 3M Oral Care) cut to 25×5×1.9 mm (ground) or 25×5×1.5 mm (control); sintered in a Lava 200 furnace → 20×4×1.5 mm (G) / 20×4×1.2 mm (C), allowing 20% shrinkage plus 0.3 mm planned loss.
- **Groups (n=45)**: C, C900, C1000, G, G900, G1000.
- **Grinding**: automated device; medium-grit cylindrical diamond (MasterCeram 133-104-SDN) in a prosthetic micromotor, ~20,000 rpm, **dry**, 0.3 mm depth.
- **Heat treatment**: isothermal in a preheated ceramic oven (AluminiPress) at 900 °C/60 min or 1000 °C/30 min, bench cooling.
- **Characterization**: XRD (Bruker D8 Advance, Cu Kα, 20–80°, 0.02° step, 3 s) with Rietveld refinement (n=2); Ra by contact profilometer (Mitutoyo SJ 400, 2.5 mm length, 3 readings/specimen, n=10, measured perpendicular to grinding direction); optical profilometry 500×500 µm (n=2); SEM topography, crack path and fracture surface, including backscattered phase contrast (n=2).
- **Veneer**: IPS e.max Ceram nano-fluorapatite porcelain — liner (~0.1 mm), wash, two dentin layers, glaze, each fired per manufacturer schedule in a Programat P310; final bilayer 20.0×4.0×2.4 mm, zirconia:porcelain = 1:1.9.
- **Notch**: 0.7 mm deep in the veneer, centred and perpendicular to the long axis, verified under optical microscope.
- **Interface test**: four-point bending (EMIC DL2000, 5 kN load cell, 0.1 mm/min, n=13) to steady-state crack propagation; G from the Charalambides equation with E=200 GPa/ν=0.32 (zirconia), E=70 GPa/ν=0.27 (porcelain).
- **Statistics**: Shapiro-Wilk then two-way ANOVA + Tukey (α=0.05) for Ra, P and G (BioEstat 5.0).

## Results

| Group | Monoclinic (vol%) | Ra (µm) | Steady-state load P (N) | G (J/m²) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C | 1.2 | 0.17 ± 0.04 | 51.4 ± 10.9 | 6.8 ± 2.1 |
| C900 | 0.0 | 0.18 ± 0.03 | 48.6 ± 8.7 | 6.6 ± 1.4 |
| C1000 | 0.0 | 0.18 ± 0.03 | 52.2 ± 7.5 | 7.6 ± 1.3 |
| G | 2.6 | 1.16 ± 0.24 | 55.8 ± 3.8 | 8.3 ± 2.0 |
| G900 | 0.0 | 1.07 ± 0.08 | 55.0 ± 7.3 | 8.0 ± 2.7 |
| G1000 | 0.0 | 1.01 ± 0.11 | 46.0 ± 8.5 | 5.7 ± 1.7 |

- **Phase state**: grinding increased monoclinic (1.2 → 2.6 vol%) and cubic/rhombohedral (12.9 → 31.3 vol%) fractions while dropping tetragonal to 66.1 vol%; both heat treatments extinguished monoclinic and restored tetragonal to 82.3–88.5 vol%.
- **Roughness**: grinding effect significant (p<0.005); heat-treatment effect not (p=0.22); interaction not significant (p=0.15). Ground groups stayed ~6× rougher than controls regardless of firing.
- **Topography**: controls showed regular grains with preserved boundaries; ground surfaces had longitudinal grooves, deformation, scale-like debris and superficial cracks; HT only rounded chip edges, visible mainly after 1000 °C/30 min.
- **Interface toughness**: interaction significant (G p=0.005; P p=0.02) but no main effect of grinding (G p=0.52; P p=0.42) or heat treatment (G p=0.29; P p=0.15). All G values 5.7–8.3 J/m².
- **Fracture surface**: porcelain (mostly the liner) remained attached to zirconia in every group with similar backscattered contrast → mixed cohesive/adhesive failure; crack propagated along the interface with focal zirconia detachment.
- **Context numbers from the discussion**: metal-ceramic interfaces cited at ~39.4 J/m² (Ni-Cr) and ~80 J/m² (Ti with gold-based treatment), supporting a higher delamination risk for veneered Y-TZP.
