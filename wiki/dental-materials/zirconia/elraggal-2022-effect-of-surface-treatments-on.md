## Three-line Summary

LT zirconia (3Y-TZP, Cercon/Lava, ~1247–1271 MPa) has significantly higher biaxial flexural strength than HT zirconia (4Y-PSZ, Katana/BruxZir, ~813–834 MPa), but HT zirconia demonstrates higher fracture toughness and superior fatigue resistance. Low-pressure APA (50 µm alumina, 0.5 bar) increased flexural strength in all groups except BruxZir, while surface grinding consistently and significantly decreased both flexural strength and fracture toughness across all zirconia types. Cyclic fatigue (10⁶ cycles) further reduced residual strength and Weibull reliability for all materials, with LT zirconia losing proportionally more strength (17–32%) than HT zirconia (11–14%).

## 세줄요약

## Summary

This in vitro study compared the effects of three surface treatments — as-sintered control, low-pressure airborne particle abrasion (APA), and surface grinding — on biaxial flexural strength (이축 굴곡강도, BFS), fracture toughness (파절인성, KIC), and fatigue resistance (피로저항성) of four clinically available zirconia types: two low translucency (LT) 3Y-TZP (Cercon, Lava) and two high translucency (HT) 4Y-PSZ (Katana, BruxZir).

A total of 480 discs were prepared and tested under ISO 6872:2015 piston-on-3-balls setup. Half received cyclic fatigue (10⁶ cycles, 37°C distilled water) prior to residual strength measurement. Surface roughness, grain size, and fracture surfaces were characterized by profilometry and SEM.

Key finding: LT zirconia has higher strength but lower fracture toughness and worse fatigue resistance compared to HT zirconia. Low-pressure APA is beneficial (or neutral), while grinding is consistently harmful across all zirconia types. The clinical implication is that intraoral adjustment by grinding should be avoided or followed by polishing, while low-pressure sandblasting prior to cementation is acceptable and may enhance strength in most zirconia types.

## Key Contributions

1. Demonstrated that HT zirconia, despite lower flexural strength, has higher fracture toughness than LT zirconia — attributed to larger grain size creating longer crack propagation paths.
2. Showed low-pressure APA (0.5 bar, 50 µm alumina) is safe or beneficial for flexural strength, distinguishing it from high-pressure APA that may cause surface damage.
3. Grinding (400 grit SiC) consistently decreased both initial strength and fracture toughness across all four tested zirconia types — directly relevant to intraoral occlusal adjustment.
4. Cyclic fatigue preferentially degrades LT zirconia: 25–32% reduction in ground LT vs 13–14% in ground HT after 10⁶ cycles.
5. Weibull modulus data (range 6.7–14.8) shows HT zirconia is less reliable (lower m) despite higher toughness — important for predicting clinical longevity.

## Methodology

- **Study design**: In vitro, 480 discs, 4 zirconia × 3 surface treatments × 2 fatigue conditions (n=20/subgroup)
- **Materials**: Cercon base (LT 3Y-TZP), Lava Frame (LT 3Y-TZP), Katana HT (HT 4Y-PSZ), BruxZir (HT 4Y-PSZ)
- **Specimen dimensions**: 12 mm diameter × 1.2 mm thickness (after sintering shrinkage ~20%)
- **Surface treatments**: As-sintered control / APA 50 µm at 0.5 bar / Grinding 400 grit SiC under 300 g with water cooling
- **Testing**: Piston-on-3-balls BFS (ISO 6872:2015); ACTA fatigue tester 10⁶ cycles at 40% mean initial BFS in 37°C water
- **Fracture toughness**: KIC = Y·Fs·√cr, where cr measured from SEM images of fractured surfaces
- **Weibull analysis**: Quinn and Quinn method, 95% CI, Weibull++ v21

## Results

### Flexural Strength by Group (Initial, MPa)

| Material | As-sintered | APA | Grinding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cercon (LT) | 1247.5 ± 25.8 | 1451.5 ± 34.9 | 909.7 ± 22.9 |
| Lava (LT) | 1271 ± 14.1 | 1442.4 ± 24.3 | 973.4 ± 23.9 |
| Katana (HT) | 812.8 ± 27.9 | 860.3 ± 18.2 | 692.3 ± 16.8 |
| BruxZir (HT) | 833.5 ± 13.2 | 858.3 ± 17.1 | 686.5 ± 13.2 |

- LT significantly higher than HT across all conditions (p < 0.001)
- APA significantly increased BFS vs control except BruxZir (p < 0.001)
- Grinding significantly decreased BFS in all groups (p < 0.001)

### Fatigue Degradation (% reduction from initial)
- LT as-sintered: ~17%; LT APA: 13–16%; LT ground: 25–32%
- HT as-sintered: 12–14%; HT APA: ~11%; HT ground: 13–14%

### Fracture Toughness
- HT > LT overall (larger grain size → longer crack path → higher KIC)
- APA increased KIC in all types except BruxZir
- Grinding significantly decreased KIC in all types (p < 0.001)

### Surface Roughness
- APA: no significant change vs as-sintered for any group
- Grinding: significantly increased Ra, Rv, Rp (p < 0.001) with deep grooves acting as stress concentrators
