## Three-line Summary

In a 25-disc in vitro study, Ceramill Zolid monolithic zirconia specimens were polished with eZr or OptraFine kits after chairside grinding and compared to glazed controls; OptraFine achieved the lowest Ra (0.47 μm) vs eZr (0.49 μm) vs glazed control (0.53 μm). No statistically significant difference existed between the two polishing kits by one-way ANOVA. Both kits produced Ra values below glazed-only control and dramatically below the unpolished roughened baseline (1.24 μm), supporting chairside polishing as an effective alternative to reglazing.

## Summary

This Indian in vitro study (Gaonkar et al., 2020) evaluated two commercially available chairside polishing kits — eZr (Garrison Dental Solution) and OptraFine (Ivoclar Vivadent) — for reducing surface roughness of monolithic zirconia after simulated chairside occlusal adjustment. Twenty-five Ceramill Zolid HT discs were fabricated via CAD/CAM and divided into five groups (n=5): negative control (roughened only), positive control (glazed without roughening), eZr polishing, OptraFine polishing, and roughened-then-glazed. Surface roughness (Ra) was measured with a contact profilometer and qualitative surface morphology with SEM.

OptraFine achieved the numerically lowest Ra (0.47 μm), slightly below eZr (0.49 μm), glazed control (0.53 μm), and roughened-then-glazed (0.59 μm). The unpolished roughened group had a mean Ra of 1.24 μm — roughly 2.5× higher than any finishing treatment. One-way ANOVA found no statistically significant difference between the three finishing groups (Groups 3, 4, 5). SEM showed OptraFine produced more uniform, regularly polished surfaces with fewer striations compared to eZr.

The authors conclude that OptraFine can serve as an alternative to reglazing after chairside adjustment of monolithic zirconia, with performance comparable to or better than glazing in terms of surface smoothness.

## Key Contributions

1. **Kit-level Ra benchmark**: OptraFine (0.47 μm) ≈ eZr (0.49 μm); both below glazed control (0.53 μm)
2. **Statistical equivalence**: no significant difference between the two polishing systems (ANOVA)
3. **SEM morphology advantage**: OptraFine surfaces smoother and more regular under SEM
4. **Polishing vs glazing**: both polishing kits outperformed roughened-then-glazed (0.59 μm) and unpolished baseline (1.24 μm)

## Methodology

- **Material**: Ceramill Zolid HT (Amann Girrbach), A2 shade, 10 mm × 2 mm discs, CAD/CAM milled (n=25)
- **Roughening**: red band diamond bur, air rotor, 15 s constant pressure
- **eZr protocol**: coarse (blue) → medium (green) → fine (orange) grits at 7,000–12,000 rpm, 40 s each
- **OptraFine protocol**: light blue silicone points → dark blue silicone points → nylon brush + diamond polishing paste at 15,000 rpm, 40 s each
- **Ra measurement**: Surftest SJ 210 (Mitutoyo)
- **SEM**: Zeiss Evo 18 Special Edition (qualitative topography, n=1 per group)
- **Statistics**: one-way ANOVA + post hoc test

## Results

| Group | Treatment | Mean Ra (μm) |
|---|---|---|
| Group 1 (negative control) | Roughened, unpolished | 1.24 |
| Group 2 (positive control) | Glazed (no roughening) | 0.53 |
| Group 3 | Roughened + eZr | 0.49 |
| Group 4 | Roughened + OptraFine | 0.47 |
| Group 5 | Roughened + glazed | 0.59 |

- No statistically significant inter-group difference among Groups 3, 4, 5
- OptraFine SEM: smooth, regular surface; eZr SEM: regular with more striations
