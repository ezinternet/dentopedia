## Three-line Summary

In vitro multifactorial study (BMC Oral Health 2026; n = 120 specimens × 1 mm discs; LDC = IPS e.max CAD HT A2, ZLC = Celtra Duo HT A2, TrZ = Cercon HT A2; n = 20/subgroup) comparing glazing vs polishing after standardized grinding (20 s, red-band diamond bur, 20,000 rpm) and 10,000 coffee thermocycles (5–55 °C), measuring surface roughness (Ra, Rz), color stability (ΔE₀₀ by CIEDE2000), and translucency parameter (TP).

After grinding, ZLC showed the highest Ra (2.59 μm) > LDC (2.00 μm) > TrZ (1.08 μm), reflecting material hardness differences; polishing produced significantly lower Ra in TrZ (best for zirconia) while glazing was equivalent or better in ZLC/LDC; thermocycling increased Ra in all glazed groups (not in TrZ-P or ZLC-P); ZLC-P had the highest ΔE₀₀ (median 1.84, perceptible but clinically acceptable < 2.23); thermocycling decreased TP in all groups except LDC-P.

Glazing is recommended for glass-ceramics (LDC, ZLC) for color stability and roughness; mechanical polishing is superior for translucent zirconia; all groups remained within clinical acceptability thresholds for both color change and translucency loss.

## 세줄요약

시험관내 (in vitro) 다요인 연구 (BMC Oral Health 2026; n = 120, 1 mm 디스크; LDC = IPS e.max CAD HT A2, ZLC = Celtra Duo HT A2, TrZ = Cercon HT A2; 군당 n = 20): 표준화 연삭 (chairside adjustment 모사, 20 s 적색밴드 버 20,000 rpm) 후 유약 처리 (glazing) vs 기계적 연마 (mechanical polishing), 커피 열순환 (thermocycling) 10,000회(5–55 °C) 적용; 표면거칠기 (Ra, Rz), 색안정성 (ΔE₀₀, CIEDE2000), 반투명도 파라미터 (TP) 측정.

연삭 후 Ra: ZLC 2.59 μm > LDC 2.00 μm > TrZ 1.08 μm (경도 차이 반영: TrZ ~1485 HV vs ZLC ~700 HV); TrZ에서만 연마가 유약보다 Ra 유의미하게 낮음; 열순환 후 모든 유약군 Ra 증가, TrZ-P·ZLC-P는 불변; ZLC-P ΔE₀₀ 중앙값 1.84 (지각가능역치 1.25 초과, 허용역치 2.23 미만); LDC-P 제외 전 군 열순환 후 TP 유의미하게 감소.

글라스 세라믹 (LDC, ZLC)은 유약 처리가, 반투명 지르코니아 (TrZ)는 기계적 연마가 색안정성·표면거칠기에서 우수; 전체 군의 색변화·반투명도 변화는 임상적 허용 범위 내 유지.

## Summary

### Study Design

**Purpose**: Compare glazing vs polishing effects on surface quality and optical properties of three CAD/CAM ceramics after chairside grinding simulation and coffee thermocycling.

**Materials**:
| Material | Brand | Hardness |
|---|---|---|
| Translucent zirconia (TrZ) | Cercon HT A2 (DeguDent) | ~1485 HV |
| Lithium disilicate ceramic (LDC) | IPS e.max CAD HT A2 (Ivoclar Vivadent) | ~580 HV |
| Zirconia-reinforced lithium silicate ceramic (ZLC) | Celtra Duo HT A2 (DeguDent) | ~700 HV |

**Finishing protocols**:
- Glazing: material-specific glaze pastes fired per manufacturer protocol
- Polishing: TrZ → EVE Diacera (2-step); LDC → Optrafine (3-step); ZLC → StarTec (2-step)

**Aging**: 10,000 thermocycles in coffee solution (Nescafe Classic 1 g/100 mL), 5–55 °C, 10 s dwell, 5 s transfer

### Key Results

**Surface roughness after grinding (Ra, μm)**:
- ZLC: 2.59 ± 0.29 | LDC: 2.00 ± 0.30 | TrZ: 1.08 ± 0.17 (all pairs p < 0.001)

**Post-finishing Ra summary**:
- TrZ-P Ra significantly lower than TrZ-G (polishing superior for zirconia; p < 0.05)
- ZLC-G and LDC-G had lower Ra than polished counterparts (not significant)
- After thermocycling: Ra increased in all glazed groups; TrZ-P and ZLC-P remained stable

**Color stability (ΔE₀₀) after thermocycling**:
| Group | ΔE₀₀ Median | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| LDC-G | 0.23 | Imperceptible |
| TrZ-P | 0.25 | Imperceptible |
| TrZ-G | 0.67 | Imperceptible |
| LDC-P | 0.78 | Imperceptible |
| ZLC-G | 0.79 | Imperceptible |
| ZLC-P | **1.84** | Perceptible (> 1.25) but clinically acceptable (< 2.23) |

**Translucency parameter (TP)**:
- Pre-thermocycling: LDC highest TP; TrZ lowest TP (consistent with material optical behavior)
- LDC-G ΔTP = 2.22 (exceeded perceptibility threshold 1.33; within acceptability limit 4.43)
- All groups decreased in TP after thermocycling; LDC-P decrease not statistically significant

### Mechanistic Interpretations

1. **Why TrZ responds better to polishing than glazing**: Zirconia contains < 1% SiO₂, so the glaze does not flow as effectively to fill irregularities; polishing mechanically removes surface scratches more reliably.
2. **Why glazed surfaces roughen after thermocycling**: Thermal stress from cycling induces microcrack formation in glaze layer; acidic coffee promotes silica dissolution and alkali ion leaching, compromising glaze integrity.
3. **Why ZLC-P has highest ΔE₀₀**: Polishing leaves microcracks that are not sealed; exposed microstructure allows coffee pigment penetration. Glazing thermally seals such cracks via glassy matrix flow (Riquieri mechanism).
4. **TP decrease in zirconia after aging**: Tetragonal-to-monoclinic (t→m) phase transformation increases light scattering at grain boundaries — consistent with both TrZ groups showing significant TP loss.

## Key Contributions

1. Demonstrates material-specific finishing recommendation: glazing for LDC/ZLC, polishing for TrZ
2. Shows polished zirconia surfaces are more stable after thermocycling than glazed zirconia
3. Quantifies that all ΔE₀₀ values remain clinically acceptable despite coffee thermocycling
4. Confirms that thermocycling-induced TP loss stays within clinical acceptability (ΔTP < 4.43) for all tested groups
5. Provides Ra reference values after standardized grinding for three common CAD/CAM materials

## Methodology

**Specimen preparation**: 120 discs, 1.0 ± 0.1 mm; LDC crystallized at Programat P500; TrZ sintered at 1500 °C × 5.5 h; all surfaces standardized with progressive abrasive papers (800/1000/1200 grit).

**Grinding standardization**: Single operator, KaVo EWL 4990 handpiece, red-band diamond bur at 20,000 rpm, 20 s unidirectional; new bur every 10 specimens.

**Measurement equipment**: Profilometer Perthometer M2 (Mahr, 2 μm stylus, 0.8 mm cutoff, 5.6 mm length, 3 measurements/specimen); Colorimeter Konica Minolta CR-321 (3 readings/background × 3 backgrounds); SEM qualitative morphology.

**Statistics**: Shapiro–Wilk normality; one-way ANOVA (post-grinding Ra); repeated-measures ANOVA (Ra, TP pre/post); Kruskal–Wallis + Dunnett's (ΔE₀₀); paired t-test (TP change); Duncan post-hoc throughout; α = 0.05.
