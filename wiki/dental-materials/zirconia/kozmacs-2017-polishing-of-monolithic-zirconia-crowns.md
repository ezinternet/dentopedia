## Three-line Summary

A pilot in-vitro study (n=48 CAD/CAM Y-TZP crowns, 24 participants across 4 groups) found no statistically significant difference in surface roughness reduction (Ra) between dental students, junior dentists, senior dentists, and dental technicians using a 2-step commercial zirconia polishing kit.
Technicians showed the highest median ΔRa (25.4 nm) in the unrestricted trial; experienced dentists led in the 15-second trial (41.35 nm); dental students showed the lowest improvement in both conditions.
Polishing duration was not correlated with outcome; the authors conclude chairside polishing is feasible and reliable across all practitioner experience levels when protocol is followed.

## Summary

This pilot study from University Witten/Herdecke (2017) investigated whether chairside polishing results of monolithic zirconia (지르코니아, ZrO₂) crowns vary across dental practitioner groups. Forty-eight CAD/CAM-fabricated full-contour Y-TZP (이트리아 안정화 정방정계 지르코니아 다결정, Y-TZP) zirconia crowns were standardized (occlusal adjustment by one operator), then distributed to 24 participants representing four groups: dental clinical students, dentists with <2 years experience, dentists with >2 years experience, and dental technicians (n=6 each).

Each participant polished two crowns using a two-step Komet Dental polishing kit (blue then white polisher, 6000 rpm) under two trial conditions: T1 (no time limitation) and T2 (15 seconds per step). Surface roughness (표면 거칠기, Ra) was measured before and after polishing using Alicona optical profilometry by two blinded investigators.

The primary finding was that no statistically significant between-group differences in Ra reduction were detected. While technicians achieved the numerically greatest median improvement in T1 (25.4 nm) and experienced dentists in T2 (41.35 nm), these differences did not reach significance. All groups reduced Ra in every trial, confirming the polishing kit's basic efficacy. Polishing duration showed no significant correlation with result.

## Key Contributions

- **Experience independence**: Chairside zirconia polishing with a two-step commercial kit does not require specialist-level skill; students can achieve adequate results when the protocol is followed.
- **Duration flexibility**: The 15-second-per-step restriction did not impair outcomes, suggesting that the standard clinical polishing time is sufficient and that extended polishing does not add benefit.
- **Measurement rigor**: Use of optical profilometry (Alicona) with dual blinded investigators and ISO 11562-compliant methodology provides a reliable benchmark for Ra in zirconia polishing studies.
- **Clinical relevance**: After occlusal adjustment of monolithic zirconia restorations, chairside re-polishing replaces the need for laboratory re-glazing.

## Methodology

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Specimens | 48 CAD/CAM Y-TZP crowns (3M Lava Plus High Translucency, 3% Y) |
| Occlusal adjustment | 5 strokes, red ring diamond bur, 20,000 rpm |
| Polishing kit | Komet 2-step: blue (94018C) → white (94018F), 6000 rpm |
| Trial T1 | Unrestricted time |
| Trial T2 | 15 seconds per step |
| Measurement | Alicona IFS G3 optical profilometry; Ra (mean roughness average) |
| Resolution | Vertical 450 nm; Lc = 25 µm (ISO 11562) |
| Measurements per crown | 10 linear (100 µm) + 10 area (50×50 µm²) |
| Investigators | 2, blinded and independent |
| Statistics | Mann–Whitney U; Wilcoxon signed-rank; Spearman's rho; p < 0.05 |

## Results

- No statistically significant between-group differences in Ra reduction (all comparisons, Mann–Whitney p ≥ 0.05)
- No significant correlation between polishing time and Ra outcome (Spearman's rho)
- Greatest median ΔRa: technicians in T1 = **25.4 nm** (IQR 10.15–35.26 nm); experienced dentists in T2 = **41.35 nm** (IQR 7.77–54.11 nm)
- Lowest median ΔRa: dental students in T1 = **6.72 nm** (IQR 4.7–17.9 nm)
- Both investigators showed consistent trends
- Surface roughness decreased in all groups across both trials
