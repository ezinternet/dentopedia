## Three-line Summary

Grinding and polishing of 5Y-PSZ with either chairside protocol achieved final surface roughness below the 0.20 µm clinical threshold after high-gloss finishing. Protocol II (Jota superfine diamond bur at high speed with coolant, then sequential diamond-impregnated silicone burs) produced a significantly smoother surface than Protocol I (EVE DIASYNT stone bur at low speed without coolant) in Lava Esthetic. Biaxial flexural strength was significantly reduced only in Lava Esthetic with Protocol I (508 → 424 MPa); no clinically significant monoclinic phase transformation occurred with either protocol.

## 세줄요약

5Y-PSZ 고투명 지르코니아(Lava Esthetic, Cercon xt) 두 제품에 의자 옆 연삭·연마 프로토콜 두 가지를 비교한 결과, 최종 고광택 연마 후 모든 군의 표면조도(Ra)가 임상 허용치(0.20 µm) 이하로 감소하였다. Lava Esthetic에서 프로토콜 II(Jota 초미세 다이아몬드 버, 고속+수냉)가 프로토콜 I(EVE DIASYNT 석재 버, 저속·무냉각)보다 최종 표면조도가 유의하게 낮았다(0.14 vs 0.18 µm). 굴곡강도는 Lava Esthetic+프로토콜 I 군에서만 유의하게 감소(508→424 MPa)하였으며, 단사정상 증가에 의한 상전환은 어느 군에서도 나타나지 않았다.

## Summary

This in vitro study evaluated the effect of two chairside grinding and polishing protocols on two commercial 5Y-PSZ (high-translucency) zirconia products: Lava Esthetic (3M ESPE) and Cercon xt (DeguDent). The rationale is that after cementation, occlusal adjustment (grinding/polishing) is clinically unavoidable, yet little is known about how different protocols affect 5Y-PSZ specifically.

Sixty-six disk specimens (15 mm × 1 mm, ISO 6872:2015) were fabricated by CAD/CAM. Each specimen had a simulated high-contact protrusion (3 mm × 0.5 mm) mimicking an occlusal high-spot. A custom polishing machine standardized direction, speed, and applied force (0.98 N grinding, 2 N polishing). Surface roughness was measured at four sequential steps (Ra0–Ra3). After high-gloss polishing, biaxial flexural strength, SEM, and XRD were performed.

Both protocols reduced surface roughness into clinically acceptable range (< 0.20 µm) after high-gloss polishing. Protocol II produced smoother surfaces in Lava Esthetic at every step. Flexural strength reduction was material-specific: Lava Esthetic (no HfO₂ additive) showed significant reduction with Protocol I; Cercon xt (contains HfO₂, Al₂O₃, SiO₂) was unaffected by either protocol. XRD revealed cubic→tetragonal phase shift after polishing in both products, but monoclinic phase (indicator of classical transformation toughening) remained stable across all groups.

## Key Contributions

1. **Protocol II (Jota system) preferred for Lava Esthetic**: lower grinding-induced roughness and preserved flexural strength vs Protocol I (EVE stone bur)
2. **Material composition modifies polishing outcomes**: HfO₂ content in Cercon xt correlates with higher resistance to roughness and strength reduction
3. **5Y-PSZ does not exhibit monoclinic transformation toughening**: grinding/polishing induces cubic→tetragonal reversal but not tetragonal→monoclinic, confirming mechanistic difference from 3Y-TZP
4. **Sequential polishing to high-gloss is mandatory**: Ra remains elevated after grinding alone and after first polishing step; final high-gloss step is clinically essential
5. **Custom machine-controlled polishing**: standardized methodology advancing reproducibility over hand-polishing studies

## Methodology

- **Materials**: Lava Esthetic (5Y-PSZ, 3M ESPE) and Cercon xt (5Y-PSZ + HfO₂ + Al₂O₃ + SiO₂, DeguDent)
- **Groups** (n=11 each): LC/CC (unpolished controls), LE/CE (Protocol I: EVE), LJ/CJ (Protocol II: Jota)
- **Protocol I**: EVE DIASYNT PLUS HP stone bur (150–200 µm, 10,000 rpm, no coolant) → EVE DIACERA RA medium (15–20 µm) → EVE DIACERA RA fine (4–8 µm)
- **Protocol II**: Jota Z850.FG superfine diamond bur (38–45 µm, 160,000 rpm, with coolant) → Jota ZIR Gloss medium (15–20 µm) → Jota ZIR Gloss fine (4–8 µm)
- **Surface roughness**: Talyscan 150 contact profilometer (Ra, µm), 2×2 mm², 5 parallel lines
- **Biaxial flexural strength**: Instron, piston-on-3-balls, 0.5 mm/min, ISO 6872:2015 (MPa)
- **Phase analysis**: XRD Bruker D8, Rietveld refinement (TOPAS V3.0); SEM at 3,000× and 10,000×
- **Statistics**: One-way ANOVA + Bonferroni; repeated-measures ANOVA for within-group roughness progression

## Results

### Surface Roughness (Ra, µm)

| Group | Baseline (Ra0) | After Grinding (Ra1) | After Polish (Ra2) | High-Gloss (Ra3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LE (Lava + EVE) | 0.12 | **1.62** | 0.21 | 0.18 |
| LJ (Lava + Jota) | 0.12 | 0.74 | 0.13 | **0.14** |
| CE (Cercon + EVE) | 0.12 | 0.38 | 0.21 | 0.18 |
| CJ (Cercon + Jota) | 0.12 | 0.38 | 0.18 | **0.13** |

- LJ had significantly lower Ra1–Ra3 than LE (p < 0.05) for Lava Esthetic
- CJ Ra3 was the only group not significantly different from baseline (p > 0.05)
- Clinical threshold ≤ 0.20 µm achieved by all groups after high-gloss step

### Biaxial Flexural Strength (MPa)

| Group | Mean ± SD | vs Control |
|---|---|---|
| LC (Lava control) | 508.16 ± 34.01 | — |
| **LE (Lava + EVE)** | **423.77 ± 77.96** | **p < 0.05** |
| LJ (Lava + Jota) | 486.69 ± 70.58 | ns |
| CC (Cercon control) | 532.37 ± 15.73 | — |
| CE (Cercon + EVE) | 513.82 ± 21.14 | ns |
| CJ (Cercon + Jota) | 516.40 ± 37.18 | ns |

### XRD Phase Composition (%)

| Group | Tetragonal | Monoclinic | Cubic |
|---|---|---|---|
| LC | 69.82 | 0.75 | 29.43 |
| LE | 92.74 | 1.13 | 6.13 |
| LJ | 89.79 | 1.00 | 9.21 |
| CC | 64.92 | 0.73 | 34.35 |
| CE | 91.32 | 1.08 | 7.60 |
| CJ | 93.31 | 0.78 | 5.91 |

- Grinding/polishing shifted cubic → tetragonal phase in both materials
- Monoclinic phase remained stable (< 1.2%) in all groups → no classical phase transformation toughening
