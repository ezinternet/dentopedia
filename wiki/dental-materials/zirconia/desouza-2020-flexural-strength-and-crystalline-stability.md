## Three-line Summary

In-vitro bench study (90 Ø12 × 1 mm discs of alumina-free 3Y-TZP monolithic translucent zirconia; 3 surface treatments — as-sintered, diamond-bur grind, grind + rubber polish — × 3 aging conditions, n = 10 per cell): roughness, biaxial flexural strength and monoclinic fraction were measured at baseline, after autoclave aging (134 °C / 2.2 kgf·cm⁻² / 5 h) and after thermocycling (200,000 cycles, 5/55 °C).

Grinding raised roughness but also raised biaxial flexural strength (Grind 1078 ± 235 MPa, Grind + Polish 1129 ± 139 MPa vs As Sintered 775 ± 190 MPa, surface-treatment p < 0.001); both aging methods then reduced strength substantially and equivalently (Baseline 1149 ± 232 MPa → Autoclave 939 ± 211 → Thermocycling 916 ± 230, aging p < 0.001; interaction p = 0.239), while polishing reduced roughness without restoring as-sintered smoothness.

Adjustment-induced transformation toughening therefore buys strength only in the un-aged state — clinical grinding should still use fine-grain burs followed by polishing, because LTD erases the toughening gain and no surface treatment protected the ceramic in this model.

연삭은 거칠기와 함께 이축 굴곡강도도 높였고 (연삭 1078 ± 235 MPa, 연삭+폴리싱 1129 ± 139 MPa vs 소결 직후 775 ± 190 MPa, 표면처리 p < 0.001), 두 에이징 방법 모두 강도를 유의하게 떨어뜨렸다 (무에이징 1149 ± 232 MPa → 오토클레이브 939 ± 211 → 열사이클링 916 ± 230, 에이징 p < 0.001; 상호작용 p = 0.239). 폴리싱은 거칠기를 낮췄으나 소결 직후의 매끄러움은 회복하지 못했다.

따라서 교합조정으로 얻는 변태강화 (transformation toughening)는 에이징 전 상태에서만 유효하다 — 임상적으로는 세립 버 사용과 폴리싱이 여전히 필수이며, 저온열화 (low-temperature degradation, LTD)는 이득을 상쇄하고 어떤 표면처리도 이를 막지 못했다.

## Summary

De Souza et al. (2020) tested whether clinically realistic adjustment of a monolithic translucent zirconia is a net harm or a net benefit. Ninety discs of an alumina-free 3Y-TZP ("Vipi Block Zirconn Translucent") were divided into as-sintered, diamond-bur-ground, and ground-then-polished groups, then aged by autoclave (134 °C, 2.2 kgf/cm², 5 h, ≈ 21 years at 37 °C) or thermocycling (200,000 cycles, 5/55 °C, ≈ 20 years in service) or left unaged.

The central result is a two-term balance. Grinding introduces surface defects (roughness up) and simultaneously triggers tetragonal→monoclinic transformation with 3–5% grain volume expansion, generating compressive stress that resists crack propagation. At baseline the compressive term dominates: ground specimens were significantly *stronger* than the untouched as-sintered surface (1078 and 1129 MPa vs 775 MPa) even though they were rougher — a strong positive roughness–strength correlation confined to the baseline groups. Polishing reduced defect size and depth without fully erasing the medium-grain bur pattern, and without changing strength relative to grinding alone.

Aging flips the balance. Both aging methods produced monoclinic transformation and a significant, near-identical strength loss (Baseline 1149 MPa → Autoclave 939 → Thermocycling 916), with no significant surface-treatment × aging interaction, meaning the relative ranking held but the absolute toughening benefit was consumed. XRD also showed broadening of the tetragonal 30° 2θ peak in surface-treated groups, indicating lattice plastic deformation. Because XRD penetration is shallow, the authors concede that the subsurface transformed layer responsible for the strength drop was not measured.

Practical reading: adjustment is not free, but it is not catastrophic either — the danger is a rough ground or intaglio surface left unpolished (biofilm, antagonist wear, margin fracture, fatigue crack growth), and the long-term strength penalty arrives with LTD regardless. The authors recommend fine/extra-fine diamond burs, mandatory polishing, and prefer zirconias with greater hydrothermal resistance for long-term performance.

## Key Contributions

1. **Quantifies both sides of chairside adjustment in one design** — defect introduction (Ra ↑) and transformation toughening (Vm ↑ → compressive stress → strength ↑) measured on the same specimens, resolving why ground zirconia can be rougher *and* stronger.
2. **Establishes the magnitude of the adjustment-induced strength gain**: +303 MPa (Grind) and +354 MPa (Grind + Polish) over as-sintered pooled means (p < 0.001).
3. **Shows polishing does not restore the as-sintered surface**: medium-grain diamond defects survived the standard rubber-polish sequence; polishing is strength-neutral, not strength-restoring.
4. **Equates autoclave and thermocycling as LTD inducers for strength** — 200,000 thermocycles cost as much strength as 5 h at 134 °C, extending thermocycling's role from bonding studies to structural aging.
5. **Reports that roughness alone fails to predict strength** (Spearman non-significant in every condition), cautioning against Ra-only acceptance criteria for adjusted surfaces.
6. **Demonstrates no protective effect of surface treatment against LTD** — interaction non-significant, so the aging penalty applies to polished, ground and untouched surfaces alike.

## Methodology

- **Specimens**: 90 discs cut from pre-sintered 3Y-TZP blocks, finished with 1200-grit SiC, ultrasonically cleaned in isopropyl alcohol, sintered 1450 °C / 2 h at 8 °C/min; final Ø12 × 1 mm (± 0.05 mm).
- **Surface treatment (n = 30/group)**: As Sintered (no treatment); Grind (diamond bur, low-speed handpiece 8,000–10,000 rpm, oscillating movements, digital pressure until a marker line disappeared); Grind + Polish (diamond bur followed by a rubber-point polishing sequence). One calibrated operator; burs replaced every 5 specimens.
- **Aging (n = 10/cell)**: Baseline; Autoclave 134 °C, 2.2 kgf/cm², 5 h; Thermocycling 200,000 × 15 s at 5 °C / 55 °C. Specimens cleaned in an ultrasonic isopropyl bath for 10 min afterwards.
- **Roughness**: contact profilometer, 5 µm diamond tip, 2.5 mm traverse at 0.5 mm/s, 0.25 mm cut-off, three readings 45° apart, mean used as Ra.
- **Biaxial flexural strength**: piston-on-three-ball, treated surface under tension, immersed in artificial saliva at 37 °C, 1.7 mm flat piston, 1 mm/min to catastrophic fracture, ISO 6872 equations 1–3.
- **Phase analysis**: XRD CuKα, 2θ 25–35°, 0.03° step, 1 s/step; Vm from monoclinic (111)m / (111)m and tetragonal (101)t intensities.
- **Topography**: FE-SEM at 500×, 1000×, 5000× after Au/Pd coating.
- **Statistics**: two-way ANOVA (surface treatment × aging) + Tukey, α = 0.05; Spearman correlation between Ra and strength.

## Results

Biaxial flexural strength — pooled means (SD) in MPa; different letters = significant difference:

| Factor | Level | Strength (MPa) | Group |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surface treatment | As Sintered | 775 (190) | B |
| Surface treatment | Grind | 1078 (235) | A |
| Surface treatment | Grind + Polish | 1129 (139) | A |
| Aging | Baseline | 1149 (232) | A |
| Aging | Autoclave | 939 (211) | B |
| Aging | Thermocycling | 916 (230) | B |

- **ANOVA**: surface treatment p < 0.001; aging p < 0.001; surface × aging interaction p = 0.239 (n.s.) — the aging penalty was proportionally similar across surface conditions.
- **Roughness**: surface treatment p < 0.001, aging p = 0.025, interaction p < 0.001. Ranking Grind > Grind + Polish > As Sintered; autoclave aging increased Ra above baseline; aging otherwise had little effect. Per-group Ra values are reported only in Fig. 1 / Table 2 (not in the text layer).
- **Grind vs Grind + Polish**: statistically tied for strength ("A" both) — polishing neither strengthened nor weakened the ground surface.
- **Correlation (Ra vs strength)**: weak positive overall, strong positive within baseline-only groups, non-significant in all conditions (p > 0.05) → confounding by phase transformation.
- **XRD**: monoclinic peak at 28° 2θ present in all ground/polished groups (mechanical stress) and after both aging methods (LTD); tetragonal peak at 30° 2θ broadened in surface-treated groups (lattice plastic deformation). Vm increased after aging in As Sintered, while surface-treated groups were already high at baseline and remained similarly high. Numeric Vm values are in Table 2 only (image).
- **SEM**: grinding produced directional defects with lateral projections; rubber polishing visibly reduced defect length and depth but left a rougher surface than as-sintered; neither aging method produced visible topographical change.
