## Three-line Summary

In-vitro microtensile bond strength study (360 bars, 3 surface treatments × Silano-Pen × 3 self-adhesive resin cements × thermal aging) testing how airborne particle abrasion (APA), Piranha acid, and hot acid etching influence bonding to translucent zirconia after 10,000 thermocycles.

Hot acid etching produced the highest mean bond strength and a homogeneous granular/porous SEM texture; Piranha acid yielded the lowest; Panavia SA significantly outperformed TheraCem and Panavia F2.0; hot acid + Silano-Pen combination achieved the best overall bonding.

Pre-treatment method and cement choice both significantly affect zirconia bonding durability; the cement × Silano-Pen interaction was non-significant (p = .067).

## Summary

This in-vitro study evaluated how three surface treatment protocols affect the microtensile bond strength (μTBS) durability of self-adhesive resin cements to translucent zirconia. Thirty-six zirconia blocks were cut, sintered, and divided into three groups: (1) airborne particle abrasion (APA), (2) Piranha acid etching, and (3) hot acid etching. Each group received Silano-Pen treatment, then bonding with one of three cements — Panavia SA, TheraCem, or Panavia F2.0. A total of 360 μTBS bars were fabricated; half underwent 10,000 thermal aging cycles.

Hot acid etching produced the highest bond strength values, attributable to SEM-confirmed formation of a homogeneous granular texture with wide distribution of porous networks that enhances micro-mechanical retention. Piranha acid — despite its strong chemical oxidising potential — produced the lowest μTBS. Among the cements, Panavia SA (MDP-containing, self-adhesive) was significantly superior to both TheraCem and Panavia F2.0. The interaction between cement type and Silano-Pen treatment was not statistically significant (p = .067), suggesting cement selection drives outcomes more than silane status alone.

## Key Contributions

- Hot acid etching creates wider porous network (SEM confirmed) → highest μTBS among three surface treatments
- Piranha acid — despite strong hydroxylation chemistry — yields the lowest bond strength in this study
- Panavia SA (MDP monomer) significantly superior to TheraCem and Panavia F2.0 for translucent zirconia
- Silano-Pen + hot acid yields best combined bonding; silane effect alone is surface-treatment-dependent
- 10,000 thermocycles: all groups degraded; relative ranking maintained — pre-treatment choice is durable discriminator
- Cement × Silano-Pen interaction non-significant (p = .067) — cement chemistry is the dominant variable
