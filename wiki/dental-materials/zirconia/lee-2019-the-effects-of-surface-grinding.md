## Three-line Summary

Surface grinding of 3Y-TZP zirconia with high-speed coarse-grit diamond burs (150 µm) without polishing produces the highest tetragonal-to-monoclinic (t→m) phase transformation and lowest flexural strength among four treatment protocols. Post-grinding polishing with an NTI ceramic polisher significantly reduces monoclinic phase fraction by removing the shallow surface-transformed layer, particularly after coarse-bur use. All four groups retained flexural strength >500 MPa (ISO 6872 threshold), but polishing is clinically necessary after coarse-grit occlusal adjustment to preserve mechanical integrity.

## 세줄요약

고속 조립(150 µm) 다이아몬드 버 연삭 후 연마를 생략하면 4개 처치군 중 단사정(monoclinic) 상변환이 가장 크고 굴곡강도가 가장 낮다. NTI 세라믹 폴리셔로 연마하면 표층에 집중된 단사정상이 제거되어 조립 버군에서 상변환이 유의하게 감소한다. 전 군에서 500 MPa 초과로 임상 허용 기준(ISO 6872)을 충족하나, 조립 버 교합조정 후 반드시 연마가 필요하다.

## Summary

Lee et al. (2019) conducted a four-group in vitro study comparing the effects of high-speed diamond bur grinding and polishing on 3Y-TZP zirconia (Prettau, Zirkonzahn). Forty disc specimens (∅15 mm × 1.25 mm, n=10/group) with a central cylindrical protrusion simulating a high-occlusion contact point were fabricated by CAD/CAM.

Groups were:
- **Control**: coarse bur (150 µm) only
- **Group 1**: coarse bur + polishing
- **Group 2**: fine bur (50 µm) + polishing
- **Group 3**: fine bur only

Surface roughness (Ra), XRD-based monoclinic phase fraction (Xm), and biaxial flexural strength were measured. Surface roughness ordered Control > Group 3 > Group 1 > Group 2. Monoclinic transformation ordered Control > Group 3 ≥ Group 1 ≥ Group 2; Control showed significantly higher Xm than Groups 1 and 2 (P<.001). Flexural strength ordered Group 2 ≥ Group 1 ≥ Group 3 ≥ Control; unpolished coarse-bur control was significantly weaker than Groups 1 and 2.

All groups exceeded 500 MPa, meeting ISO 6872 requirements for posterior dental ceramics. The key mechanism: coarse grinding generates a shallow monoclinic-enriched surface layer; polishing physically removes this layer, reducing Xm and restoring flexural strength.

## Key Contributions

1. **Polishing is mandatory after coarse grinding**: Coarse bur + no polish = most t→m transformation and lowest strength. Polishing after coarse grinding significantly recovers strength.
2. **Fine bur reduces transformation inherently**: Fine-grit (50 µm) grinding creates less phase transformation; polishing adds no statistically significant benefit in fine-bur conditions.
3. **All groups are clinically acceptable**: >500 MPa threshold met across all protocols — but coarse-without-polish is significantly weaker.
4. **Mechanism confirmed**: Monoclinic phase accumulates in the superficial grinding layer; polishing ablates it.
5. **XRD identifies monoclinic (not rhombohedral)**: 2θ = 28.10° confirms t→m, not t→rhombohedral transformation.

## Methodology

**Design**: In vitro, 4-group, n=10/group

**Material**: 3Y-TZP (Prettau, Zirkonzahn); milled by CAD/CAM into ∅15 × 1.25 mm discs with 1 mm central protrusion

**Grinding**: High-speed handpiece, 300,000 rpm, water-cooled; Komet Zr 6881 (coarse, 150 µm) or Komet Zr 8881 (fine, 50 µm)

**Polishing**: NTI ceramic polisher, low-speed handpiece, 20,000 rpm, 10 s, dry

**Measurements**:
- Ra: 3D-OM (Nikon LV150L)
- Phase transformation Xm: XRD (PANalytical X'pert), Garvie-Nicholson method
- Flexural strength: Biaxial, ISO 6872, Shimadzu DSC-500, 0.5 mm/min

**Statistics**: One-way ANOVA + Games-Howell post-hoc (P < .05)

## Results

| Group | Bur | Polish | Ra (relative) | Xm (relative) | Strength (relative) |
|-------|-----|--------|--------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Control | Coarse (150 µm) | No | Highest | Highest* | Lowest* |
| Group 3 | Fine (50 µm) | No | 2nd | 2nd | 3rd |
| Group 1 | Coarse (150 µm) | Yes | 3rd | 3rd | 2nd |
| Group 2 | Fine (50 µm) | Yes | Lowest | Lowest | Highest |

*Control vs. Groups 1 and 2: P < .001 for both Xm and flexural strength

- **All groups: flexural strength >500 MPa** (ISO 6872 compliant)
- Polishing eliminated surface grinding scratches and reduced Ra in both bur-type conditions
- Fracture origin: polished groups — surface-initiated, inward; unpolished groups — ground-surface defect initiated
