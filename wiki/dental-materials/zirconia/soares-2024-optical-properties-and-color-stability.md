## Three-line Summary

This in vitro study compared four surface-treatment conditions (control/as-sintered, grinding only, grinding+polishing, grinding+glaze) on 3Y-TZP zirconia discs (Zenostar T) after 18-day red wine immersion. Grinding alone produced clinically unacceptable color change (ΔE00 > 1.8) at both 9 and 18 days, while glaze application yielded the most color-stable surface; polishing reduced color change relative to grinding-only but performed worse than glaze. Translucency (TP00) and opalescence (OP) were statistically unaffected by surface treatment or immersion across all groups.

## Summary

Dental zirconia (3Y-TZP) restorations often require intraoral adjustment with diamond burs (grinding), which introduces surface roughness and micro-defects that can increase pigment accumulation. This study quantified the impact of post-grinding finishing choices — polishing or glaze — on color stability (ΔE00 by CIEDE2000), translucency parameter (TP00), and opalescence (OP) of Zenostar T discs after simulated dietary staining (red wine, 18 days × 30 min/day at 37°C).

The key finding is a clear hierarchy: glaze > polishing > grinding-only for color stability. Only glaze brought color change below the perceptibility threshold (ΔE00 < 0.8) at 9 days. Grinding-only was the only clinically unacceptable condition at both time points (ΔE00 > 1.8). Importantly, translucency and opalescence were resilient across all conditions, suggesting that surface finishing primarily affects extrinsic pigmentation uptake without altering the material's intrinsic light-transmission properties.

## Key Contributions

- **Grinding alone = clinically unacceptable color change**: Gr group ΔE00 = 2.65 (9d), 2.94 (18d) — above Paravina 2015 unacceptability threshold (1.8).
- **Glaze is the preferred post-grinding treatment**: Gr+Gl ΔE00 = 0.65 at 9d (sub-perceptible), 1.66 at 18d (acceptable). SEM confirmed smoother surface than polished specimens.
- **Polishing is an acceptable but inferior alternative**: Gr+Pol ΔE00 = 1.29 (9d) and 1.65 (18d) — perceptible but acceptable; Ra remained at 1.34 μm vs. 0.48 μm for glaze group.
- **Surface roughness moderately predicts staining**: Spearman r = 0.42, p = 0.02.
- **Translucency and opalescence not compromised**: TP00 and OP statistically stable across all groups and all time points.

## Methodology

- **Specimens**: Zenostar T (3Y-TZP, Ivoclar AG), Ø12 mm × 0.8 mm discs, sintered 1450°C/2h; n=8/group.
- **Groups**: Ctrl (as-sintered), Gr (diamond bur #4219, 90–120 μm grit), Gr+Pol (OptraFine kit — two diamond rubbers + diamond paste), Gr+Gl (Ivocolor glaze, fired at 710°C).
- **Staining**: 18-day red wine immersion (30 min/day, 37°C), brushed/rinsed/stored in distilled water between cycles.
- **Measurements**: SP60 spectrophotometer (X-Rite), CIE L*a*b* on gray/black/white backgrounds; CIEDE2000 formula for ΔE00; TP00 from black vs white; OP from a*/b* coordinate difference.
- **Roughness**: Mitutoyo SJ-410 profilometer, Ra and Rz per ISO 4287:1997.
- **Statistics**: Repeated-measures two-way ANOVA/Tukey (ΔE00, TP00, OP); Kruskal-Wallis (Ra, Rz); Spearman correlation.

## Results

| Group | ΔE00 9d | ΔE00 18d | Ra μm (median) | Rz μm (median) | Clinical status (18d) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ctrl | 1.19 | 1.34 | 0.17 | 1.30 | Perceptible, acceptable |
| Gr | 2.65 | 2.94 | 2.36 | 13.20 | **Unacceptable** |
| Gr+Pol | 1.29 | 1.65 | 1.34 | 8.33 | Perceptible, acceptable |
| Gr+Gl | 0.65 | 1.66 | 0.48 | 2.54 | Perceptible, acceptable |

- Gr group: highest Ra/Rz and greatest ΔE00 at all time points; only clinically unacceptable group.
- Gr+Gl: lowest ΔE00 at 9 days (below perceptibility limit of 0.8), second-lowest at 18 days.
- Roughness–color correlation: r = 0.42, p = 0.02 (moderate, significant).
- TP00 and OP: p = 0.180 and p = 0.259 respectively — no significant group or time effects.
- All groups' ΔTP00 values within clinically acceptable range (< 2.62 threshold).
