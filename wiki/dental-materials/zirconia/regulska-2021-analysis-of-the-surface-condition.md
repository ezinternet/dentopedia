## Three-line Summary

Mechanical surface treatment (grinding and polishing) of 3Y-TZP zirconia induces tetragonal-to-monoclinic phase transformation detectable by XRD, with polishing producing less monoclinic phase than grinding. Neither grit size (120 vs. 500) nor wet/dry cooling significantly affected the degree of transformation, but grazing incidence XRD revealed a subsurface monoclinic concentration gradient. Surface free energy (total 37–43 mJ/m²) showed no significant inter-group differences, with the apolar component dominating, suggesting that bonding of veneering ceramics relies primarily on apolar adhesion during firing.

## 세줄요약

3Y-TZP 지르코니아의 기계적 표면 처리(연삭·연마)는 X선 회절법 (X-ray Diffraction, XRD)으로 확인되는 정방정→단사정 상전이 (Tetragonal-to-Monoclinic Phase Transformation)를 유발하며, 연마군에서 연삭군보다 단사정상 함량이 낮다. 입자 크기(120 vs. 500) 및 습식/건식 냉각 여부는 전이 정도에 유의미한 차이를 주지 않았으나, 경사입사 X선 회절법 (Grazing Incidence XRD)으로 표면에서 심부로 갈수록 단사정상 농도 구배가 존재함을 확인했다. 표면 자유 에너지 (Surface Free Energy, SFE) 총값(37–43 mJ/m²)은 군 간 유의차 없었으며, 비극성 성분이 극성 성분보다 높아 소성 과정에서 세라믹과의 결합이 비극성 접착에 의존함을 시사한다.

## Summary

This in vitro study examined how different mechanical processing regimens affect the surface condition and crystallographic structure of 3Y-TZP zirconia (Ceramill Zi, Amann Girrbach). Six groups were compared: coarse/fine grinding under wet/dry conditions, polishing, and an untreated milled reference. Both Bragg-Brentano and grazing incidence XRD were used to characterize t→m phase transformation. The key finding is that polishing induces the least monoclinic phase among treated groups, and the monoclinic phase is concentrated in near-surface layers (gradient toward core). Surface free energy was dominated by the apolar component (~27–36 mJ/m²) across all groups, implying that ceramic-zirconia bonding during firing is governed by apolar adhesion rather than polar wettability.

## Key Contributions

1. Polishing produces less t→m transformation than grinding; grit size and wet/dry cooling do not significantly differ
2. Grazing incidence XRD reveals a subsurface monoclinic concentration gradient confined to near-surface layers
3. SFE data (polar 8–11 mJ/m², apolar 27–36 mJ/m², total 37–43 mJ/m²) — no significant inter-group differences
4. Apolar component dominance explains why high-temperature ceramic firing achieves better bonding than room-temperature adhesive bonding

## Methodology

- 15 cylindrical 3Y-TZP specimens (Ceramill Zi, sintered 1450°C/2 h), n=3 per group
- Groups: coarse dry (120), coarse wet (120), fine dry (500), fine wet (500), polishing, milled reference
- XRD: Bragg-Brentano (θ-θ) + grazing incidence (5° incident angle); Rietveld quantitative analysis
- SFE: Owens-Wendt model (water + diiodomethane contact angles)
- SEM-EDS (JEOL JSM-6610LV, 20 kV)

## Results

| Group | Monoclinic % (B-B) | Monoclinic % (GI) |
|---|---|---|
| Milled reference | ~0% | ~0% |
| Coarse/fine grinding (all) | moderate | higher (surface gradient) |
| Polishing | lowest among treated | lower than grinding |

- GI consistently higher than B-B in all treated groups → monoclinic concentrated at surface
- SFE: polar 8–11 mJ/m², apolar 27–36 mJ/m², total 37–43 mJ/m²; no group differences
- Coarser grits → deeper surface fissures in SEM; wet cooling → less surface damage vs dry
