## Three-line Summary

This in vitro study compared diamond grinder cutting efficiency on sintered 3Y-TZP zirconia and resin-based composite CAD/CAM blanks under a constant 160 g load, measuring weight removal over serial cuts (n = 10 per material-grinder combination).
Significant inter-bur differences in initial weight removal existed for both materials; zirconia required five times longer grinding time to achieve weight removal comparable to composite.
SEM confirmed diamond grain rounding and particle loss as the dominant wear mechanism; a special zirconia-specific bur (Z-CUT) maintained removal stability over four cuts despite lower absolute removal per cut.

## 세줄요약

소결 3Y-TZP 지르코니아 (Yttrium-stabilized Tetragonal Zirconia Polycrystal, 3Y-TZP)와 레진강화복합재 (Resin-Based Composite, RBC) CAD/CAM 블록에서 160 g 일정 하중 조건으로 다이아몬드 버 (Diamond Grinder)의 절삭 효율을 시험관 내 비교했다 (n = 10).
버 간 초기 삭제량 차이가 두 재료 모두에서 유의했으며, 동일한 삭제량을 얻으려면 지르코니아에서 복합레진보다 5배 긴 연마 시간이 필요했다.
주사전자현미경 (Scanning Electron Microscopy, SEM)으로 다이아몬드 입자 마모 및 탈락이 절삭 저하의 주원인임을 확인했고, 지르코니아 전용 Z-CUT 버는 낮지만 안정적인 삭제량을 4회까지 유지했다.

## Summary

This in vitro study by Rosentritt et al. (2024) systematically evaluated diamond grinder cutting efficiency on two fundamentally different CAD/CAM dental materials — sintered 3Y-TZP zirconia and resin-based composite — using weight removal as the primary metric. A custom handpiece holder applied a constant 160 g load, with protocols differing by material hardness: 8 cuts × 40 s for composite and 4 cuts × 5 min for zirconia. Both material groups demonstrated significant inter-bur differences in initial cutting capacity, disproving the study hypothesis of equivalent removal across grinder types. For composite, significant performance decline appeared as early as the third cut, with all burs showing some reduction by the seventh cut. For zirconia, conventional burs degraded significantly by the fourth cut (one showing complete diamond loss), whereas the Z-Cut special bur maintained its lower removal level throughout. The critical practical finding is that zirconia demands five times more grinding time to achieve equivalent weight loss compared to composite, underscoring the material's extreme abrasion resistance and the importance of bur selection and timely replacement.

## Key Contributions

- Quantified the 5× grinding time requirement for zirconia vs composite to achieve equivalent weight removal under standardized conditions
- Demonstrated significant inter-bur variation in initial removal: composite group p = 0.009; zirconia group p < 0.001
- Established that bur diameter (1.4 vs 1.6 mm) within the same conventional bur type does not significantly affect composite removal (p = 0.069)
- Positive Pearson correlation (r = 0.673, p < 0.001) between cut number and weight removal in zirconia group, suggesting progressive surface conditioning
- SEM evidence of diamond grain rounding, matrix erosion, and complete particle loss as failure modes in conventional burs on zirconia
- Special Z-CUT bur (harder, geometrically defined diamond, nickel bond) showed no SEM-detectable wear and maintained removal level — suggesting superior durability despite lower initial cutting depth

## Methodology

**Materials**: 3Y-TZP zirconia blocks (Cercon base 30 colored; Dentsply) sintered at 1350 °C, cut to 12 × 14 × 18 mm cubes; resin-based composite (Grandio blocs 14L A3.5 HT; VOCO) used as-delivered.

**Grinder systems**: NTI-Kahla and Komet Dental burs at 1.4 mm and 1.6 mm diameters; conventional vs special-coating (ABACUS, TURBO) for composite; conventional vs special zirconia (Z-CUT) for zirconia.

**Protocol**: Custom handpiece holder, 160 g constant load; composite 8 × 40 s cuts; zirconia 4 × 5 min cuts; n = 10 per material-bur combination.

**Measurements**: Weight before and after each cut (precision balance); SEM of bur surfaces and material surfaces pre/post.

**Statistics**: One-way ANOVA, Bonferroni, Pearson (α = 0.05).

## Results

### Composite (8 cuts × 40 s each)

| Bur | First cut removal | Significant decline vs baseline |
|-----|-----------------|--------------------------------|
| CS16 ABACUS | 0.25 ± 0.05 g (highest) | Cut 3 |
| CB16 (conventional) | — | Cut 6 (lowest final: 0.09 ± 0.05 g) |
| CA16 (conventional) | — | Cut 7 |
| CS14/CA14/CB14 | — | Cut 3–4 |

- Inter-bur significant at baseline (p = 0.009) and final cut (p = 0.049)
- No correlation between cut number and removal (Pearson −0.27, p = 0.333)
- Diameter (1.4 vs 1.6 mm) not significant (p = 0.069)

### Zirconia (4 cuts × 5 min each)

| Bur | First cut removal | Performance trajectory |
|-----|-----------------|----------------------|
| ZA16 (conventional) | 0.21 ± 0.05 g | No significant decline (p ≥ 0.056) |
| ZB16 (conventional) | — | Significant decline cut 4 (p = 0.006); complete diamond loss on SEM |
| ZS16 Z-CUT | ~0.10 g | No significant decline (p = 1.000); no SEM wear traces |
| ZK16 (Komet) | lower | Decline observed |

- Inter-bur significant at baseline (p < 0.001) and final cut (p < 0.001)
- Positive correlation cuts × removal (Pearson 0.673, p < 0.001)
- Weight removal range: 0.07–0.21 g
- Zirconia grinding time 5× longer for equivalent removal vs composite
