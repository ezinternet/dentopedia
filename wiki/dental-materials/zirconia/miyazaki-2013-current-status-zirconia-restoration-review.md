## Three-line Summary
Narrative review (J Prosthodont Res 2013, five Japanese university collaborators) synthesizing material science (Y-TZP vs Ce-TZP/Al₂O₃), CAD/CAM workflow, veneer bonding failure mechanisms, zirconia–resin bonding, surface finish effects on antagonist enamel wear, and clinical performance of zirconia FDPs.

Veneering porcelain chipping was identified as the main clinical complication with no clear chemical bond mechanism at the zirconia–porcelain interface; zirconia–resin bonding requires MDP primer plus sandblasting, and bond strength degrades with aging; polished surfaces caused less enamel wear than glazed.

Monolithic zirconia is introduced as a chipping-free alternative to veneered designs, and marginal adaptation of zirconia FDPs was described as clinically acceptable — foundational review for understanding first-generation zirconia clinical translation.

## Summary
Comprehensive narrative review of zirconia restorations from five Japanese universities. Covers material science (Y-TZP vs Ce-TZP), CAD/CAM workflow, veneer bonding failure mechanisms, resin cement bonding (MDP critical), surface finish and antagonist enamel wear, and clinical performance of zirconia FDPs.

## Key Contributions
- Y-TZP: best mechanical properties; Ce-TZP/Al2O3: highest fracture toughness
- Main complication: veneering porcelain chipping — no clear chemical bond mechanism identified
- Zirconia-resin bond: MDP primer + sandblasting required; aging degrades bond
- Surface finish: polished < glazed for antagonist enamel wear
- Monolithic zirconia: eliminates chipping risk; introduced as alternative to veneered
- Marginal adaptation: clinically acceptable for FDPs
