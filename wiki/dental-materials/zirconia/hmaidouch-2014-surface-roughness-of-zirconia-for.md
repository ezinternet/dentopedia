## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study (Int J Oral Sci 2014; 30 bar-shaped 3% Y-TZP specimens, n=15 per group — glazed full-contour vs feldspathic-veneered+glazed) measuring maximum roughness depth (Rmax) by profilometer after sequential diamond grinding (20/10/7.5 µm, 15 s, 2 N, water coolant) and 3-step polishing.

Rmax rose significantly after coarse grinding in both groups; fine grinding restored full-contour zirconia (FZ) to glazed level (P=0.19) but not veneered zirconia (VFZ) (P=0.009), and fine-polished FZ reached 2.41±0.51 µm versus 4.31±1.93 µm glazed (P=0.49) while polished VFZ remained significantly rougher than its glazed state.

For full-contour zirconia the NTI 3-step grinding sequence alone reaches glazed-level smoothness and polishing lowers roughness below glaze, whereas veneered zirconia retains deep grinding grooves and condensation voids that polishing can only round off, not remove.

## Summary

This in-vitro bench study from Frankfurt (Int J Oral Sci, 2014) systematically quantified Rmax across every step of a clinically simulated grinding and polishing sequence for two zirconia types: glazed full-contour Y-TZP (FZ) and glazed feldspathic-veneered Y-TZP (VFZ). Using a purpose-built apparatus enforcing constant 2 N force and 15 s dwell per step, the study eliminated operator variability while isolating material-dependence. The key finding is that FZ and VFZ diverge at fine grinding: FZ returns to glazed-level roughness with the NTI fine-grind bur alone and achieves sub-glaze smoothness after polishing, whereas VFZ retains significantly higher Rmax than its glazed baseline even after full polishing due to deep grooves and condensation voids in the veneering ceramic. A notable incidental finding is that the as-glazed FZ surface (4.31±1.93 µm) is *rougher* than the fine-polished FZ surface (2.41±0.51 µm), attributed to bubbles trapped in the glaze layer.

## Key Contributions

- First study to report Rmax across the **complete step-by-step clinical adjustment sequence** (seven measurement points per specimen: glazed → coarse grind → medium grind → fine grind → coarse polish → medium polish → fine polish) rather than a single endpoint.
- **Constant-force apparatus** (2 N, 15 s/step): removes the most common confound in bench polishing studies.
- Shows **glazed FZ is not the smoothest FZ surface**: glaze Rmax 4.31±1.93 µm > fine-polished FZ Rmax 2.41±0.51 µm (small bubbles in the glaze layer).
- **Material-dependence of polishability**: VFZ cannot recover glazed smoothness with the tested polishing kit; FZ can.
- Raises a clinically testable shortcut: after the 3-step NTI grind, FZ roughness was statistically equivalent to coarse-polished FZ — suggesting the grind alone may suffice, pending further investigation.

## Methodology

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | In-vitro profilometry + SEM |
| Material | Zirluna ACF 3% Y-TZP (Amberger Central Fraescenter) |
| Groups | Group 1 FZ n=15 (glazed, no veneer); Group 2 VFZ n=15 (feldspathic veneer VM9 + glaze) |
| Specimen | 30 mm × 6 mm × 2 mm bars, sintered 1530 °C 2 h |
| Outcome | Rmax (Perthometer SP5), 5 readings/specimen/step (525/group) |
| Grinding | NTI K881 diamonds 20/10/7.5 µm; turbine 200,000 rpm; 2 N; 15 s; water-cooled |
| Polishing | NTI HP 802104 kit; 15,000/10,000/5,000 rpm; 2 N; 15 s each |
| Statistics | One-way ANOVA + Tukey HSD, α=0.05 (OriginLab v7.5) |
| Imaging | SEM FEI Quanta 600 FEG ×500 |

## Results

| Step | FZ Rmax | vs glazed FZ | VFZ Rmax | vs glazed VFZ |

| Glazed | 4.31±1.93 µm | — | (higher than FZ, P<0.05) | — |
| Coarse grind | Significantly ↑ | — | Significantly ↑ | — |
| Fine grind | No sig. diff. (P=0.19) | recovered | Significantly rougher (P=0.009) | not recovered |

- Coarse polishing added no benefit over fine grinding in either group (FZ P=0.54; VFZ P=0.99).
- SEM: FZ grooves progressively resolved through polishing; VFZ showed persistent ridges, grooves, and rounded pits from condensation voids.
