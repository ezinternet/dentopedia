## Three-line Summary
Umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42023430885, J Indian Prosthodont Soc 2024) synthesizing 4 systematic reviews (2013–2022, from 86 records screened across Cochrane Central/EBSCOhost/PubMed/Google Scholar, Jan 2013–Jan 2023) on antagonist enamel wear opposing full-coverage zirconia crowns versus other ceramics and natural enamel.

Polished monolithic zirconia produced antagonist enamel wear ≤ natural enamel over time (León Velastegui et al.: linear wear 0.2574 μm for zirconia vs 8.9149 μm feldspathic vs 0.0189 μm lithium disilicate) and was less damaging than glazed zirconia, metal-ceramics, and feldspathic porcelain; AMSTAR-2 rated 2 of the 4 SRs high quality and 2 critically low, and meta-analysis was not possible due to study heterogeneity.

The clinical guideline from the highest level of evidence available is to polish monolithic zirconia rather than glaze it when enamel preservation of opposing teeth is the priority; the evidence base remains a qualitative (non-pooled) synthesis limited to English-language SRs.

## Summary
Umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42023430885; PRISMA 2020) of 4 systematic reviews (2013–2022) providing the highest level of synthesis on antagonist enamel wear opposing zirconia crowns. PRISMA flow: 86 records → 81 after dedup → 8 after title screening → 4 included after abstract screening (3 excluded, 1 unretrievable). AMSTAR-2 rated 2 SRs high quality (Solá-Ruíz 2020, León Velastegui 2022) and 2 critically low (Hmaidouch & Weigl 2013, Gou et al. 2018). Concludes polished monolithic zirconia is the most enamel-friendly of the tested ceramic materials.

## Methodology
- PROSPERO-registered (CRD42023430885), PRISMA 2020-compliant umbrella review; PICOTS: full-coverage crown recipients, zirconia vs. other ceramics (feldspathic, leucite-reinforced glass, lithium disilicate, glass-infiltrated alumina, PFM) and natural enamel, outcome = antagonist enamel wear, 6–24 month follow-up, umbrella-review design
- Search: Cochrane Central, EBSCOhost, PubMed (incl. MEDLINE), Google Scholar; Jan 1 2013–Jan 1 2023; English-language only; two independent reviewers with a third for disagreements; Cohen's kappa 0.63 (title), 0.68 (abstract), 0.75 (full-text) — moderate inter-reviewer agreement at each stage
- Quality appraisal: AMSTAR-2 (16-item checklist; 7 critical domains: items 2, 4, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15)
- No meta-analysis performed — heterogeneous study designs, wear-measurement techniques, and outcome variables across the 4 included SRs

## Results
**PRISMA flow**: 86 records → 5 duplicates removed → 81 → title screening excluded 73 → 8 remained → abstract screening excluded 4 (1 unretrievable despite contacting authors + 3 excluded) → **4 SRs included**.

**4 included SRs** (all evaluated posterior full-coverage crowns; wear quantified in μm/mm via 3D scan/image-superimposition methods):

| SR | Design | Studies (n) | Follow-up | Comparison | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hmaidouch & Weigl 2013 | SR | 5 | 6mo–3yr | metal-ceramic (feldspathic) & lithium disilicate vs. enamel | unglazed/unpolished ceramic → higher enamel wear rate than glazed/polished |
| Gou et al. 2018 | SR | 5 | 12–24mo | monolithic zirconia vs. enamel | zirconia wear ≈ or > natural teeth, but < metal-ceramic; polished < glazed |
| Solá-Ruíz et al. 2020 | SR+MA | 4 | 12–24mo | monolithic zirconia crowns vs. enamel | zirconia crowns cause greater antagonist wear over time; max enamel wear 6.13 μm/month, max crown wear 3.40 μm/month |
| León Velastegui et al. 2022 | SR+MA | 12 | 3–24mo | lithium disilicate, feldspathic, zirconia, ZLS, monolithic zirconia vs. enamel | feldspathic (8.9149 μm) > zirconia (0.2574 μm) > lithium disilicate (0.0189 μm) linear wear; zirconia wear decreases over time to ≤ natural wear |

**AMSTAR-2 overall confidence**: high — Solá-Ruíz 2020, León Velastegui 2022; critically low — Hmaidouch & Weigl 2013, Gou et al. 2018 (both lacked a priori protocol, RoB assessment of included studies, and heterogeneity discussion).

**3 excluded studies** (Supplementary Table 1, did not meet clinical 6–24mo follow-up SR criteria): Passos et al. 2013 ("wear behavior of zirconia opposing enamel: a systematic review"); Aljomard et al. (in vitro SR+MA, "Enamel wear against monolithic zirconia restorations" — same paper as [[dental-materials/zirconia/aljomard-2022-enamel-wear-monolithic-zirconia-sr-ma]]); Ghaffari et al. 2022 ("Natural teeth wear opposite to glazed and polished ceramic crowns: a systematic review").

**Mechanistic detail**: enamel loss tracks ceramic microstructure/surface roughness rather than hardness (Hmaidouch & Weigl); polished zirconia < glazed because thin glaze layers degrade within the first months post-cementation even without occlusal adjustment, exposing a rougher substrate (Gou et al.); molars wear more than premolars (greater occlusal force); cumulative wear rises over follow-up but the wear *rate* falls over ~2 years as "break-in wear" from initial occlusal loading settles (León Velastegui). Reference natural physiological wear ≈29 μm/yr (molars), ≈15 μm/yr (incisors); background literature (Lambrechts, cited in Introduction) puts average natural opposing-enamel wear at 20–40 μm/year.

## Key Contributions
- Polished monolithic Zr ≤ natural enamel wear over time
- Polished < glazed for antagonist enamel wear
- Polished Zr < metal ceramics, feldspathic porcelains, lithium disilicate (linear wear 0.2574 vs 8.9149 vs 0.0189 μm, León Velastegui 2022)
- AMSTAR-2: 2/4 included SRs high quality, 2/4 critically low
- Meta-analysis not possible due to heterogeneous study designs and outcome variables
- Clinical guideline: polish monolithic zirconia; do not routinely glaze if enamel preservation is priority
