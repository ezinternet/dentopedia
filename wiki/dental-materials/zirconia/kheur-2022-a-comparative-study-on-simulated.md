## Three-line Summary

Two-phase in vitro study (Materials 2022, n = 140 sintered Y-TZP specimens 15 × 3 × 3 mm, 3M ESPE Lava, 1500 °C 2 h): Phase 1 compared diamond burs, zirconia-specific diamond points, and tungsten carbide burs for grinding to 1.5 mm and 1.0 mm residual thickness across Ra, Vickers hardness (VH), flexural strength, SEM, and XRD; Phase 2 tested three polishing protocols — diamond paste, Shofu ceramic polishing kit, novel low-temperature-resintered zirconia slurry — on diamond-bur-ground specimens.

Tungsten carbide burs, despite producing the smoothest surface (Ra ≈ 0.40 µm, comparable to control 0.27 µm), reduced flexural strength catastrophically — from 978 MPa to 496 MPa at 1.5 mm and 196 MPa at 1.0 mm — with fine grinding-direction cracks visible on SEM; diamond burs roughened the surface (Ra ≈ 0.81 µm) but did not significantly reduce flexural strength at 1.5 mm; no T→M phase transformation was detected by XRD in any group.

After diamond-bur grinding, diamond polishing paste (Signum HP diamond + Z-Shine wheel) restored Ra to 0.24 µm (better than the 0.27 µm control); the ceramic polishing kit was acceptable (Ra 0.32 µm); the novel zirconia slurry failed completely (Ra 2.81 µm); all polishing groups at 1.5 mm residual thickness showed only insignificant flexural-strength reduction.

## Summary

Kheur et al. set out to answer two sequential clinical questions that arise every time a clinician adjusts a monolithic zirconia restoration: which abrasive causes the least damage during grinding, and which polishing protocol best repairs that damage? Their two-phase in vitro design is the most systematic framework for this problem in the literature at the time, because it uses the same outcome battery — Ra, Vickers hardness (VH), flexural strength, SEM, XRD — across both phases and explicitly feeds Phase 1's winner into Phase 2.

**Phase 1** tested three commercially available burs (diamond, zirconia-specific diamond points, tungsten carbide) plus a control on 3M ESPE Lava sintered Y-TZP bars ground to 1.5 mm and 1.0 mm residual thickness. The key finding is a dissociation between surface roughness and structural integrity: tungsten carbide burs produced surfaces only slightly rougher than the unground control (Ra 0.40 vs 0.27 µm, not significant), yet reduced flexural strength by nearly 50% at 1.5 mm (496 MPa vs 978 MPa, p = 0.000) and by 80% at 1.0 mm. SEM confirmed fine cracks propagating along the grinding direction — the smooth surface conceals a cracked substrate. Diamond burs, by contrast, roughened surfaces significantly (Ra 0.81 µm) but preserved flexural strength at 1.5 mm (901 MPa, not significant vs control). XRD across all groups showed no T→M transformation.

**Phase 2** polished diamond-bur-ground specimens with three protocols. Diamond polishing paste (Signum HP diamond + Z-Shine rubber wheel) was the best result — Ra 0.24 µm, actually lower than the unground control. The Shofu ceramic polishing kit left Ra at 0.32 µm, statistically equivalent to the control. The novel indigenously developed zirconia slurry (presintered zirconia particles in glazing liquid, 450 °C resintering) failed: Ra 2.81 µm, far worse than grinding alone. Importantly, all polishing groups at 1.5 mm residual thickness showed flexural-strength reductions that were not statistically significant (Group D 896 MPa, P 882 MPa, S 832 MPa vs control 978 MPa). At 1.0 mm, all groups fell to ~365–398 MPa (significant).

The clinical takeaway is explicit: avoid chairside grinding when possible; when necessary, use diamond burs (not tungsten carbide), maintain at least 1.5 mm residual thickness, and follow with diamond polishing paste or a ceramic polishing kit. The novel slurry approach is not viable.

## Key Contributions

- **Contraindication for tungsten carbide burs**: the first study to show that the apparent advantage of tungsten carbide (smooth surface) is misleading — flexural strength is halved even at conservative 1.5 mm residual thickness, with SEM-confirmed crack nucleation.
- **Critical residual thickness = 1.5 mm**: at this thickness diamond-bur grinding preserves flexural strength; below 1.0 mm all protocols fail.
- **Diamond polishing paste outperforms the unground control** in Ra: uniquely demonstrates that polishing can produce a smoother surface than the as-sintered zirconia baseline.
- **Two-phase matched design**: directly links Phase 1 bur selection to Phase 2 polishing optimization on the same material, specimen geometry, and outcome battery.
- **Novel zirconia slurry closed**: tests and definitively rules out low-temperature-resintered zirconia slurry as a repair strategy.

## Methodology

- **Specimens**: n = 140 presintered 3M ESPE Lava Y-TZP blanks, milled, sintered 1500 °C 2 h; 15 × 3 × 3 mm bars; ultrasonically cleaned in ethyl alcohol.
- **Phase 1 groups** (n = 20 each, residual thicknesses 1.5 mm and 1.0 mm): Group I control; II diamond bur (medium grit 100–125 µm); III zirconia-specific diamond points; IV tungsten carbide finishing bur (8-bladed toothed geometry).
- **Phase 2 groups** (n = 20 each, same two thicknesses): Group D diamond paste (Signum HP diamond + Z-Shine wheel, 2–4 µm, 30 s, 10,000–12,000 rpm); Group P Shofu Porcelain Laminate kit (30 s, same rpm, color-sequence protocol); Group S zirconia slurry (zirconia particles:glazing liquid 1:1 by mass, 450 °C 7 min resintering).
- **Outcomes**: Ra by contact profilometer (Perthometer SP6 / MarSurf XR20, velocity 0.1 mm/s, Lc 0.25 mm, Lm 2.4 mm, 3×); VH by Vickers tester (Struers, 9.807 N, 15 s, 3×); flexural strength by ISO 6872 (Instron 5566, 0.5 mm/min, circular fixture, 2 mm tungsten piston); SEM (Zeiss MERLIN FE-SEM); XRD (Bruker D8 Advance, CuKα 1.54 Å, 2θ 20°–80°, Karakoca–Yilmaz monoclinic quantification).
- **Statistics**: ANOVA + Bonferroni post-hoc, α = 0.05; Anderson–Darling normality test.

## Results

**Phase 1 — Grinding agent** (1.5 mm residual thickness):

| Bur | Ra (µm) | Ra vs control | Flexural strength (MPa) | FS vs control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control | 0.27 ± 0.03 | — | 978 ± 24.81 | — |
| Diamond | 0.81 ± 0.26 | p = 0.000 ↑ | 901.6 ± 28.07 | p > 0.05 NS |
| Zirconia diamond | 0.85 ± 0.35 | p = 0.000 ↑ | 840.3 ± 35.44 | p > 0.05 NS |
| Tungsten carbide | 0.40 ± 0.32 | p = 0.824 NS | 496.2 ± 23.45 | p = 0.000 ↓ |

At 1.0 mm residual thickness: diamond bur FS 441.95 MPa, zirconia diamond 375.14 MPa, tungsten carbide 196.42 MPa — all significantly below control (all p = 0.000).

**Phase 2 — Polishing protocol** (1.5 mm residual thickness, all specimens ground with diamond bur):

| Protocol | Ra (µm) | vs control | Flexural strength (MPa) | vs control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control | 0.27 ± 0.03 | — | 978 ± 24.81 | — |
| Diamond paste | 0.24 ± 0.05 | p = 0.000 ↓ (better) | 896.7 ± 48.56 | p > 0.05 NS |
| Ceramic polishing kit | 0.32 ± 0.25 | p > 0.05 NS | 882.7 ± 47.11 | p > 0.05 NS |
| Zirconia slurry | 2.81 ± 0.23 | p < 0.05 ↑ (failed) | 832.97 ± 43.23 | p > 0.05 NS |

At 1.0 mm: all polishing groups showed significant FS reduction (p = 0.000; range 365–398 MPa).

XRD across all Phase 1 and Phase 2 groups: tetragonal phase confirmed, no T→M transformation detected, asymmetric peak broadening attributed to lattice distortion (residual stress), not phase change.

Hardness: no statistically significant differences between any groups in either phase (all p > 0.05).
