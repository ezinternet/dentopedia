## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study (n=64 bar specimens, Y-TZP, 8 surface-treatment × aging groups), combined LTD (134°C/40h steam + pH cycling + toothbrushing): grinding and polishing significantly increased flexural strength vs standard polish or glaze.

Aging did NOT significantly reduce flexural strength in any group (P=0.109–0.970); however, monoclinic content rose sharply in all aged groups (Gr-Ag: 51.2%, Gl-Ag: 41.8%, Po-Ag: 38.8%, Sp-Ag: 15.4%).

The paradox — high monoclinic content yet unchanged strength — reflects transformation toughening: surface compressive stress from t→m offset crack propagation; glazing neither increases strength nor protects against LTD.

Y-TZP 64개 막대 시편, 8군(표면처리×에이징), 복합 LTD(134°C/40h 증기+pH사이클+칫솔질): 연삭·폴리싱이 표준연마·글레이징보다 굴곡강도 유의하게 높음.

에이징 후 굴곡강도 유의한 감소 없음(P=0.109–0.970); Gr-Ag 단사정 51.2%로 급증.

강도 유지 이유 = 변태강화: t→m 체적팽창이 표면 압축응력 → 균열 억제; 글레이징은 강도 개선·LTD 방어 모두 무효.

## Summary

Sarabi et al. (2024) investigated the interaction between surface treatment (grinding, glazing, polishing) and combined aging (steam LTD + pH cycling + toothbrushing) on Y-TZP zirconia. The study found that grinding and polishing — via transformation toughening — both increase baseline flexural strength, while glazing restores surface smoothness but does not restore or enhance strength. Critically, despite substantial t→m phase transformation after combined aging (up to 51.2% monoclinic in the grinding+aging group), flexural strength was not significantly reduced in any group. This counter-intuitive result is explained by transformation toughening: the t→m surface transformation creates compressive stress that resists crack propagation.

## Key Contributions

1. **Grinding and polishing increase FS** via compressive stress induction (transformation toughening): Gr=900 MPa, Po=934 MPa vs Sp=730 MPa, Gl=753 MPa
2. **Aging does not reduce FS** despite high monoclinic content — challenges the assumption that LTD always weakens restorations in the clinical time frame
3. **Glazing does not protect against LTD** — Gl-Ag showed 41.8% monoclinic, comparable to Gr-Ag (51.2%)
4. **Multi-modal aging protocol** (steam + pH + toothbrushing) is more clinically representative than single-stressor approaches

## Methodology

- 64 bar-shaped specimens (20×4×2mm), Y-TZP (Zirconzahn), sintered at 1450°C
- Surface groups: Sp (standard polish), Gr (diamond rotary grinding), Gl (grinding + overglaze 820°C), Po (grinding + 2-step intraoral polish)
- Aging: (I) 134°C/2bar steam 40h; (II) pH cycling 15× (demineralizing pH4.3 / remineralizing pH7); (III) 10,000 toothbrushing cycles
- XRD monoclinic quantification; 3-point flexural test (ISO 6872); Two-way ANOVA + Tukey; Pearson correlation

## Results

| Group | FS (MPa) | Monoclinic Before | Monoclinic After |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sp | 729.7 ± 38 | 0% | 15.4% |
| Gr | 900.4 ± 71.8 | 12.6% | 51.2% |
| Gl | 752.6 ± 32.3 | 0% | 41.8% |
| Po | 933.7 ± 35.4 | 10.2% | 38.8% |

- Gr/Po >> Sp/Gl in FS (P<0.0001); aging effect non-significant (P=0.109–0.970)
- Pearson correlation: FS increased with monoclinic% in Gr/Po (transformation toughening active)
