## Three-line Summary
In vitro study (J Adv Prosthodont 2018; 100 monolithic zirconia disks, n=20/group) simulating chairside occlusal adjustment with a diamond bur (20 s, water-cooled) and comparing two zirconia polishing kits against one porcelain polishing kit by profilometric roughness (Ra), SEM, and XRD phase analysis.

Grinding raised Ra to 1.77 µm; the zirconia kits reduced it to 0.28 µm (Meisinger) and 0.23 µm (EVE Diacera) versus 0.78 µm for the porcelain kit (all polishing groups smoother than grinding, p<.05; the two zirconia kits not different, p>.05), and XRD detected no tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation in any group.

Clinically, adjusted zirconia should be finished with a zirconia-specific polishing kit rather than a porcelain kit, and short (~3 min) water-cooled polishing appears phase-safe — but flexural strength and antagonist wear were not measured.

## Summary

Turkish in vitro study asking whether the polishing step after simulated occlusal adjustment can restore the surface and whether it risks phase transformation in monolithic zirconia. A diamond bur roughened the surface to Ra 1.77 µm; zirconia-specific polishing systems brought it down to 0.23–0.28 µm, while a porcelain polishing system plateaued at 0.78 µm. XRD showed no monoclinic phase increase after either grinding or polishing, and the authors attribute that to water cooling plus a deliberately short total polishing time of about 3 minutes.

The paper's argument is material matching: polishing kits are formulated against a target material's hardness, so porcelain abrasives (softer than zirconia) cannot efficiently cut a zirconia surface. It is a roughness-and-phase study only — no strength, no wear, no aging.

## Key Contributions

- Benchmarks the roughness penalty of a realistic chairside adjustment: a single diamond bur for 20 s under coolant produces Ra 1.77 µm on monolithic zirconia.
- Same-operator, same-protocol comparison of Meisinger zirconia kit, EVE Diacera zirconia kit, and EVE Diapol porcelain kit (n=20 each, 100 specimens total).
- Zirconia kits (0.28 / 0.23 µm) significantly outperformed the porcelain kit (0.78 µm); the two zirconia kits were equivalent.
- Polishing-time framing: total polishing was capped at ~3 min specifically to stay below the heating threshold that prior work associated with t→m transformation.
- Negative phase-transformation finding for both grinding and polishing under water cooling — a counterpoint to reports of monoclinic increase with prolonged polishing.
- Supplies the roughness data that the antagonist-wear literature converts into wear predictions.

## Methodology

- **Specimens**: 100 disks (10 mm × 3 mm) milled from presintered Katana Zirconia HT (Kuraray-Noritake) on a Yenadent DC40, sintered 1500 °C/7 h, standardized with a silicon carbide rotary abrasive plus 500- and 1500-grit papers.
- **Groups (n=20)**: C control; G ground only; M Meisinger zirconia kit; E EVE Diacera zirconia kit; P EVE Diapol porcelain kit.
- **Adjustment**: diamond 6830RL (Komet-Brasseler) in a Ti-Max X600L (NSK) high-speed handpiece, 2 × 10 s under water coolant, single examiner.
- **Polishing**: each of 3 instruments per kit, 30 s one direction + 30 s at 90°, at 80% of manufacturer maximum RPM, low-speed handpiece; ~3 min total.
- **Measurement**: Ra by Perthometer M2 profilometer (3 readings/specimen, mean); SEM at ×1000 (one specimen per group); XRD (CuKα, 20–40° 2θ, 0.01° steps) for monoclinic peak-intensity ratio.
- **Statistics**: power analysis → n=20/group; Shapiro-Wilk; one-way ANOVA + Tukey at P<.05.

## Results

| Group | Treatment | Ra (µm) |
|---|---|---|
| G | Ground only | 1.77 |
| P | Ground + porcelain polishing kit | 0.78 |
| M | Ground + Meisinger zirconia kit | 0.28 |
| E | Ground + EVE Diacera zirconia kit | 0.23 |

- All polishing groups were significantly smoother than the ground group (P<.05); the polishing system had a significant overall effect on Ra.
- Zirconia kits beat the porcelain kit (P<.05); M vs E not significant (P>.05), though SEM suggested M had the cleaner surface.
- SEM: grinding left parallel deep scratches along the bur path; polishing progressively flattened them, but deep grooves persisted in all groups and striations remained in P.
- XRD: identical patterns across groups — no t→m transformation from grinding or polishing.
- Threshold reading: against the cited Ra = 0.2 µm bacterial-retention threshold, P (0.78 µm) is clearly above it, while M (0.28 µm) and E (0.23 µm) are described as clinically acceptable although both means still exceed 0.2 µm numerically.
