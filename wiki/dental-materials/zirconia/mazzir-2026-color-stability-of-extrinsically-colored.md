## Three-line Summary

This narrative review synthesized 9 in vitro studies evaluating color stability of extrinsically stained monolithic zirconia under simulated aging. Mechanical surface reduction (>100 μm occlusal adjustment) was the most critical factor, causing ΔE00 to exceed the clinical acceptability threshold (>1.8), while chemical and thermal aging produced generally acceptable color changes. Glazing consistently outperformed polishing as a surface finish for preserving extrinsic stain integrity over time.

## 세줄요약

외재성 착색 (Extrinsic Staining) 단일체 지르코니아 (Monolithic Zirconia)의 색 안정성을 평가한 체외 연구 9편을 종합한 서술적 고찰 (Narrative Review). 교합면 조정 (Occlusal Adjustment) 100 μm 초과 시 색차 (ΔE00) >1.8 (임상 허용 한계 초과)로, 기계적 마모가 가장 중요한 색 불안정 인자로 확인됨. 유약 처리 (Glazing)가 연마 (Polishing)에 비해 외재성 착색의 장기 색 안정성 보존에 일관되게 우수하였음.

## Summary

Extrinsically stained monolithic zirconia (외재성 착색 단일체 지르코니아) is widely used for esthetic characterization of ceramic restorations. This narrative review addressed whether the superficial stain layer is durable enough under clinical oral aging conditions. Nine in vitro studies from 2016–2026 were included, evaluating four aging categories: mechanical, chemical, thermal, and hydrothermal.

**Main conclusion**: Extrinsic stains on monolithic zirconia are clinically stable provided the glaze layer is preserved and surface adjustments are minimized. When the stain layer is mechanically disrupted — particularly by occlusal adjustments exceeding 100 μm — color change exceeds clinical acceptability. The longevity of these restorations is more dependent on clinical handling than on oral environment.

**Clinical significance**: Glazing is the most important modifiable variable. Polished surfaces perform significantly worse under both chemical and mechanical challenges. Precolored (intrinsically pigmented) zirconia offers superior stability when extensive post-processing is anticipated.

## Key Contributions

1. **Aging factor hierarchy**: Mechanical aging > chemical aging > thermal aging for color stability impact
2. **100 μm threshold**: 교합면 조정 (Occlusal Adjustment) >100 μm → ΔE00 exceeds 50:50% acceptability threshold (>1.8) in a linear manner
3. **Glazing is protective**: Glazed surfaces outperform polished surfaces consistently across aging types
4. **Translucency is a separate endpoint**: Thermal aging affects 상대 투명도 파라미터 (Relative Translucency Parameter, RTP) more than color; ΔE and ΔE00 are not interchangeable metrics
5. **Composition effect**: 6Y-PSZ less stable than 3Y-TZP under combined mechanical and thermal aging
6. **Clinical guideline**: Prefer precolored zirconia when mechanical adjustments or long-term durability are priorities

## Methodology

- **Design**: Narrative review; structured electronic search (PubMed, ScienceDirect, Wiley Online Library)
- **Search period**: 2016–February 2026
- **Initial records**: 1,354; **Final included**: 9 in vitro studies
- **Reviewers**: 2 independent reviewers + third for disagreement resolution
- **Aging protocols**: Thermal (5 studies), mechanical (3), chemical (3), hydrothermal (1)
- **Outcome metrics**: ΔE (CIELAB), ΔE00 (CIEDE2000), RTP, Ra
- **Thresholds used**: ΔE ≤ 3.3–3.7 (perceptibility); ΔE00 ≤ 1.8 (50:50% acceptability)

## Results

### Mechanical Aging — Dominant Factor

- Herpel et al. (2021): 교합면 조정 (Occlusal Adjustment) >100 μm → ΔE00 >1.8 (threshold exceeded); relationship is linear with depth of removal
- Spyropoulou et al. (2016): 500,000 cycles cyclic loading affected L*, a*, b* but ΔE <3.7 (clinically acceptable)
- Mechanism: extrinsic pigments are confined to a thin superficial layer; grinding/polishing removes them, exposing the zirconia substrate
- Abounassif et al. (2026): 6Y-PSZ less stable than 3Y-TZP under thermocycling + toothbrushing; glazing > polishing

### Chemical Aging — Moderate, Generally Acceptable

- Coffee/tea immersion: moderate color change, clinically acceptable in most studies when glaze intact
- Kanat-Ertürk (2020): Glazed specimens maintained ΔE within acceptable limits; polished did not
- Externally stained > preshaded for discoloration after coffee thermocycling, but both acceptable
- Zirconia more resistant to staining agents than hybrid ceramics or feldspathic ceramics
- Alcoholic beverages (red wine, whiskey) also increased surface roughness — may facilitate stain retention

### Thermal Aging — Limited Color Effect, Notable Translucency Effect

- Thermocycling generally had minimal impact on ΔE/ΔE00
- Subaşı et al. (2018), Alp et al. (2018): Coffee thermocycling significantly affected RTP without necessarily producing clinically perceptible color change
- Externally shaded + thinner specimens more susceptible to RTP reduction
- Combined thermal + mechanical/chemical aging showed synergistic color changes

### Hydrothermal Aging — Pronounced Effect with Fluorescent Additives

- Rafael et al. (2018): Autoclave significantly increased discoloration, especially with fluorescent characterization liquids
- Fluorescent surface additives particularly susceptible to degradation under heat + moisture

### Clinical Recommendations from Review

- Minimize occlusal adjustment (<100 μm) on stained zirconia
- Use glazing to protect extrinsic stains and reduce surface roughness
- Prefer preshaded (precolored) zirconia when mechanical adjustments are anticipated
- Counsel patients with high coffee, tea, red wine consumption about moderate color change risk
