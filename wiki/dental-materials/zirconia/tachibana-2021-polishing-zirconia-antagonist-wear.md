## Three-line Summary
In vitro study (Dent Mater J 2021; Kyushu University) compared polished vs ground Y-TZP under three contact conditions (contact-slide, back-and-forth slide, repetitive contact) to determine the relative impact of surface finish versus contact type on antagonist enamel wear.

Polished Y-TZP consistently produced the lowest antagonist enamel wear across all three contact conditions; polishing was the dominant factor over contact condition type, and ground Y-TZP generated higher enamel wear regardless of contact configuration.

Polishing after any chairside grinding or occlusal adjustment of zirconia is clinically mandatory; intraoral polishing kits are essential tools for every zirconia restoration delivery appointment.

## Summary
Kyushu University study evaluating antagonist enamel wear of polished vs ground Y-TZP under three contact conditions (contact-slide, back-and-forth slide, repetitive contact). Confirms polishing after grinding is mandatory; intraoral polishing kits are essential clinical tools.

## Key Contributions
- 3 contact conditions tested to simulate varying intraoral situations
- Polished Y-TZP: consistently lowest antagonist enamel wear across ALL contact conditions
- Ground Y-TZP: higher roughness → higher antagonist wear regardless of contact type
- Polishing is the dominant factor over contact condition
- Clinical mandate: always polish zirconia after any grinding/occlusal adjustment
