## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study (5 commercial yttria-partially stabilized zirconias (Y-PSZ) — KATANA HT, KATANA STML, KATANA UTML, Zpex 4, Zpex Smile; speed sintering ~90 min vs conventional ~7 h, plus super-speed 18 min for KATANA STML; discs 14.5 mm × 1.2 mm; LTD at 134 °C, 2–3 bar autoclave).

Translucency and biaxial flexural strength were not significantly affected by the sintering program (p > 0.05); conventionally sintered Y-PSZ had larger average grain size and fewer fine grains; speed-sintered Y-PSZ had lower cubic ZrO₂ content except KATANA HT and STML; Weibull modulus was highest for conventional KATANA STML and lowest for speed-sintered Zpex 4; some speed- and conventionally sintered groups showed similar monoclinic fractions after LTD.

Authors conclude speed sintering is acceptable for Y-PSZ — but LTD similarity is reported for "some" groups only and reliability (Weibull) varied by product; abstract-only, numeric values not reported.

## Summary

Liu et al. (2022, Dental Materials; Tokyo Medical and Dental University) compared speed (~90 min) and conventional (~7 h) sintering across five commercial highly translucent Y-PSZ products, with an additional 18-min super-speed arm for KATANA STML. Outcomes covered translucency (colorimeter), biaxial flexural strength with Weibull analysis, microstructure (SEM), crystallography (XRD before/after hydrothermal aging) and low-temperature degradation (134 °C, 2–3 bar). Neither translucency nor flexural strength differed significantly between sintering programs, and fractography was comparable. Speed sintering did change the microstructure — smaller grains and, for most products, less cubic phase — and mechanical reliability differed by product (Zpex 4 speed-sintered lowest Weibull modulus). LTD behaviour was similar between programs for some, not all, groups. The authors conclude speed sintering is acceptable for Y-PSZ; the abstract does not report how large the cubic-phase or LTD differences were.

## Key Contributions

1. **Multi-product comparison**: 5 commercial Y-PSZ across 3 KATANA translucency grades and 2 Zpex grades
2. **Translucency and strength preserved**: no significant difference between speed and conventional sintering (p > 0.05)
3. **Microstructure shifts with speed sintering**: smaller average grain size, larger fine-grain fraction, lower c-ZrO₂ (except KATANA HT/STML)
4. **Reliability is product-dependent**: Weibull modulus highest for conventional KATANA STML, lowest for speed-sintered Zpex 4
5. **LTD only partly equivalent**: some speed- and conventionally sintered groups had similar monoclinic fractions

## Methodology

- Materials: KATANA HT, KATANA STML, KATANA UTML; Zpex 4, Zpex Smile
- Sintering: speed (~90 min) vs conventional (~7 h); KATANA STML also super-speed (18 min)
- Specimens: discs 14.5 mm diameter × 1.2 mm thickness after sintering
- Tests: translucency (colorimeter); biaxial flexural strength (universal testing machine) + Weibull analysis; fractography; SEM microstructure; XRD crystallography before and after hydrothermal aging
- LTD: 134 °C, 2–3 bar water vapour, autoclave (duration not given in abstract)
- Source artifact: PubMed structured abstract only (no full text)

## Results

| Outcome | Speed vs conventional |
|---|---|
| Translucency | No significant difference (p > 0.05) |
| Biaxial flexural strength | No significant difference (p > 0.05) |
| Weibull modulus | Highest: conventional KATANA STML; lowest: speed-sintered Zpex 4 |
| Grain size | Conventional: larger average grain, smaller fine-grain fraction |
| Cubic ZrO₂ | Lower after speed sintering, except KATANA HT and KATANA STML |
| Fractography | Comparable |
| LTD (monoclinic fraction) | Similar for some speed- and conventionally sintered groups |

- Authors' significance statement: speed-sintering programs are acceptable for Y-PSZ zirconia
