## Summary
Bollen et al. (1997) review compares surface roughness of oral hard materials and treatment effects relative to bacterial plaque retention. They propose a threshold R_a ≈ 0.2 µm below which further reduction in bacterial accumulation is not expected; above this threshold, plaque accumulation increases with surface roughness, raising caries/periodontal inflammation risk. Different materials and treatment modalities produce wide range of roughness and require material-specific approaches. Due to abstract-only availability, precise numeric values from full text could not be extracted.

## Key Contributions
- Establishes/advocates R_a ≈ 0.2 µm as threshold for bacterial plaque retention.
- Surveys initial roughness across materials (teeth, abutments, gold, amalgam, acrylic resin, resin composite, glass ionomer/compomer, ceramics) and effects of polishing/scaling/brushing/condensing/glazing/finishing.
- Emphasizes material-specific treatment to obtain/maintain smooth surfaces.

## Methodology
Narrative review with Medline search; inclusion based on stated threshold values or comparable standardized surface conditions.
