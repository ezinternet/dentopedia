## Three-line Summary

Narrative/comprehensive literature review (Materials 2025, 58 articles from 2008–2025 spanning narrative/systematic reviews and in vitro studies) giving a clinician-facing framework for all-ceramic material selection and cementation in CAD/CAM digital dentistry.

Ceramics divide by composition into silica/glass-matrix (feldspathic, leucite-reinforced, lithium disilicate — HF-etchable, silane-coupled) vs oxide-based (alumina, zirconia — air-abrasion + MDP); material choice matches mechanical demand to site (translucent glass-ceramics anteriorly, high-strength zirconia/ZLS posteriorly/load-bearing), and etchability dictates the surface-treatment + resin-cement protocol; the field has shifted from layered to monolithic restorations to eliminate veneer chipping.

This is decision-support synthesis, not new primary data — evidence is heterogeneous (in vitro + reviews, no meta-analysis) and outcomes remain operator- and manufacturer-instruction dependent.

## Summary

Cuzic et al. (2025) is a narrative/comprehensive review that reframes contemporary all-ceramic dentistry as a two-step clinical decision: **which ceramic** and **how to cement it**. Reviewing 58 articles (2008–2025), the authors argue that classifying dental ceramics by **chemical composition** — silica/glass-matrix (feldspathic, leucite-reinforced, lithium disilicate) vs oxide-based (alumina, zirconia) — is the most clinically actionable scheme because composition determines both the material's mechanical/optical envelope and, critically, its **etchability**, which in turn dictates the surface-treatment and luting protocol. The paper documents the field's defining recent trend: a **shift from multilayered/veneered to monolithic restorations** to avoid low-strength veneer-layer fracture, enabled by CAD/CAM and by high-translucency zirconia formulations. It then maps each material class to site-specific indications and details the adhesive/cementation decision (glass-ionomer vs resin cement; light-/self-/dual-cured resin cements; HF + silane for glass-matrix vs air-abrasion + MDP for zirconia). As a narrative synthesis with no meta-analysis and heterogeneous (in vitro + review) evidence, it functions as chairside decision-support rather than a survival dataset, and it stresses that outcomes remain dependent on operator skill and manufacturer-specific instructions.

## Key Contributions

- **Composition-first classification** (silica/glass-matrix vs oxide-based) elevated as the most clinically useful scheme over indication/microstructure/translucency-based schemes.
- Explicit linkage of **etchability → surface treatment → luting-agent choice**: glass-matrix ceramics are HF-etchable + silane-coupled; oxide ceramics (zirconia) need air-abrasion + MDP primers.
- Documents the **layered → monolithic** transition as the field's central recent trend, with its trade-offs (antagonist wear, aesthetic matching).
- Site-specific material map from anterior aesthetics (feldspathic/leucite) through lithium disilicate and ZLS to posterior/load-bearing zirconia, plus hybrid ceramics for elasticity.

## Methodology

- Databases: Google Scholar, PubMed/Medline, Web of Science; years **2008–2025**; English only.
- Keywords: "all-ceramic restorations", "modern dentistry", "computer-aided design", "dental ceramics", "adhesion", "resin cements".
- Included narrative/comprehensive/systematic reviews + in vitro studies; excluded non-English and case reports.
- **58 articles** selected + supplementary references. Narrative appraisal — **no meta-analysis / no pooled statistics**.

## Results

| Ceramic class | Composition | Best-fit indication | Surface treatment for cementation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feldspathic | Silica/glass-matrix | Anterior veneers, inlays, onlays (high translucency, low strength) | HF etch + silane |
| Leucite-reinforced | Silica/glass-matrix | Anterior aesthetics (veneers/inlays) | HF etch + silane |
| Lithium disilicate | Glass-ceramic | Anterior + posterior crowns, short-span bridges | HF etch + silane |
| Zirconia-reinforced lithium silicate (ZLS) | Glass-ceramic | Wide range incl. implant-supported crowns | HF etch + silane |
| Zirconia | Oxide-based | Posterior multi-unit bridges, implant abutments (high strength/toughness) | Air-abrasion + MDP primer (not HF-etchable) |
| Hybrid ceramic | Resin-matrix/polymer-infiltrated | Various incl. implant crowns (elasticity, moderate strength, less abrasive) | Per manufacturer (composite-like) |

- **Cements**: glass-ionomer (fluoride release → prevents secondary caries) vs resin cement (higher bond strength, better shade-matching). Resin-cement polymerisation mode (light/self/dual-cured) chosen by ceramic translucency/thickness, light-curing access, and working-time needs.
- **Surface-treatment caution**: excessive mechanical roughening can initiate cracks → ceramic fracture. Silanization couples ceramic silica to resin acrylic group. Chlorhexidine post-etch preserves collagen/inhibits MMP in etch-and-rinse dentin adhesives (avoid with self-etch/self-adhesive cements).
- **Bottom line**: anterior → translucent glass-ceramics (feldspathic, lithium disilicate); posterior/load-bearing → zirconia / ZLS; long-term success hinges on correct resin-cement selection and surface protocol.
