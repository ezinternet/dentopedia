## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 범위: **한국 소재 기관(대학병원·치과대학병원)과 한국 건강보험(NHIS) 자료로 만든 연구 29편**만 모았다. 외국 연구, 다국가 연구, 국제 체계적 문헌고찰은 뺐다. 기관 코호트 논문 대부분은 환자 국적을 따로 적지 않았고 "한국 기관"이라는 사실로 한국 환자로 본다. 건강보험 3편은 한국 가입자 전체가 대상이라 한국 환자 근거로 가장 직접적이다.
> - **수명(생존율)**: 한국 기관 코호트의 장기 누적생존율 (Cumulative Survival Rate, CSR)은 대체로 **95–98%**: 연세대 Straumann 10년 98.23%, 서울대 마이크로스레드 >10년 97.9%, 서울대 SLA ≥5년 96.8%, TST ≥5년 98.1%, 단국대 장애 환자 최대 10년 98.3%, 서울대+부산대 5년 98.8%. 예외는 RBM 표면 임플란트 92.5% (10–15년, 고정체 파절 다수)와 연세대 평균 12.3년 코호트 95.2% (환자 단위 88.4%).
> - **환자 단위는 임플란트 단위보다 낮다**: 연세대 12.3년 코호트 95.2% vs 88.4%, 연세대 Straumann 10년 98.23% vs 95.70%. 환자에게 말할 때는 어느 단위인지 밝혀야 한다.
> - **보철 전 조기실패율은 정의·임플란트에 따라 0.8–15%**: 표준 직경 대형 코호트 0.8–3.4% (연세대 Straumann 13/1,692=0.77%, 서울대+부산대 32/3,555=0.90%, 단국대 장애 환자 5/465=1.1%, 단국대 Luna SLA 35/1,031=3.4%), 협폭 임플란트는 높다 (경희대 NDI 12/274=4.4%, 서울대 3.0 mm 6/64=9.4%, 모두 보철 전 0–4개월). 즉시식립 소규모 연구는 3/49=6.1%, 3/54=5.6%, 3/20=15%.
> - **한국 건강보험 자료는 조기실패를 못 본다**: 3편 모두 청구 기반(재식립·제거 청구)이고 보철 전 실패를 구분하지 않거나 제외한다. 노인 2,354,706개의 후기 제거는 0.53% (평균 2.84년).
> - **전국 자료의 위험인자**: 현재 흡연 보정 HR 1.59 (과거 흡연은 비유의, 10개비/일·10년·10갑년 초과에서 용량-반응, 여성 3.39 vs 남성 1.48). RBM 표면은 SA 대비 제거 HR 1.841, HA 1.424. 병원급 치과가 개인의원보다 실패 청구 위험이 낮다 (IPTW HR 0.35) — 증례 선택 차이를 배제할 수 없다.
> - **기관 코호트의 위험인자**: 수련의 식립 aHR 2.86, 하악 aHR 2.31 (단국대, 보철 전 조기실패), 고령 HR 1.17 (3.0 mm), 치주염 관련 치아 상실 aHR 3.57과 정기 유지관리(SPT) 불량 aHR 2.35 (서울대+부산대), 2차 수술 HR 4.769 (마이크로스레드, 이벤트 10개). 이벤트 수가 6–48개라 추정이 불안정하다.
> - **실패 후 재식립은 불리하다**: 강릉원주대 1년 생존 88.1%, 서울대 5년 생존 치주염 병력 90.2% vs 97.0% (aHR 3.417).
> - **후기 실패는 기계적**: 고정체·나사 파절이 RBM 코호트 17개 실패 중 11개, 저작근 부피 1,000 mm³당 OR 1.24 (이화여대, 초록만). 조기실패(골유착 실패)와 기전이 다르다.
> - **한국 자료로 임플란트 "중앙 수명"을 낸 연구는 없다**: 가장 가까운 것은 16기관 다기관 연구의 **보철물** 중앙 생존 16년이고 임플란트 생존율이 아니다.
> - **공백**: 한국 전향 다기관 조기실패 연구, 보철 전 실패를 분리한 건강보험 분석, 시스템·술자 보정이 된 전국 자료가 위키에 없다.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 29 studies (2011–2026) done in Korean institutions or on Korean National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) data: 3 nationwide NHIS claims cohorts, one 16-institution prosthesis-lifespan study and about 25 single- or two-centre cohorts and trials; patient nationality is rarely stated and is implied by the Korean setting.

Long-term implant-level cumulative survival clustered at 95–98% (92.5% for a fracture-prone RBM design; patient-level 88.4–95.7%), while pre-prosthetic early failure ranged from 0.8% in large standard-diameter cohorts to 4.4–9.4% in narrow-diameter implants, with claims-based NHIS studies unable to separate early failure.

Risk signals (smoking HR 1.59, trainee operator aHR 2.86, periodontitis-related tooth loss aHR 3.57, RBM surface HR 1.841) come mostly from small-event single-centre data or claims without clinical covariates, so they are directional; no Korean study reports a median implant lifespan.

## 세줄요약

한국 기관 또는 한국 건강보험(NHIS) 자료로 만든 29편(2011–2026) 종합: 건강보험 전국 청구자료 3편, 16기관 보철 수명 연구 1편, 나머지는 단일·2기관 코호트와 시험이며 환자 국적은 거의 명시되지 않아 한국 기관이라는 점으로 한국 환자로 본다.

장기 임플란트 단위 누적생존율은 95–98%에 몰려 있고(고정체 파절이 많은 RBM 설계 92.5%, 환자 단위 88.4–95.7%), 보철 전 조기실패는 표준 직경 대형 코호트 0.8%에서 협폭 임플란트 4.4–9.4%까지 벌어지며 건강보험 청구 연구는 조기실패를 분리하지 못한다.

위험 신호(흡연 HR 1.59, 수련의 식립 aHR 2.86, 치주염 관련 치아 상실 aHR 3.57, RBM 표면 HR 1.841)는 이벤트 수가 적은 단일기관 자료이거나 임상 공변량이 없는 청구 자료에서 나와 방향성이고, 임플란트 중앙 수명을 낸 한국 연구는 없다.

## Summary

This page answers one question using only Korean-setting evidence held in the wiki: what survival and early-failure experience should be expected for implants placed in Korea, and which factors are linked to loss? It narrows and updates [[overviews/korean-university-hospital-implant-survival-cohorts-overview]] (which has been trimmed to Korean-setting papers) and complements [[overviews/early-implant-failure-surveillance-update-2022-2026-overview]], which mixes international data.

Inclusion rule: the study was conducted at a Korean institution or on Korean NHIS data. International systematic reviews, multinational cohorts and non-Korean cohorts are excluded. Because institution cohort texts seldom report ethnicity, Korean patients are inferred from the setting and each source page says so.

## Key Contributions

### 1. Longevity: implant-level cumulative survival

| Cohort (setting) | Follow-up | Survival |

| [[implants/survival/kim-2018-retrospective-radiographic-observational-study-of]] (Yonsei, Straumann tissue-level, 1,692 implants; abstract only) | 10 y | 98.23% implant / 95.70% patient |
| [[implants/survival/nam-2023-the-cumulative-survival-rate-of]] (Seoul National University Dental Hospital, micro-thread, 490) | >10 y | 97.9% (10 failures) |
| [[implants/survival/ryoo-2023-a-retrospective-study-of-the]] (SNUDH, RBM, 247) | 10–15 y | 92.5% (17 late failures: 10 fixture fractures, 6 peri-implantitis, 1 screw fracture) |
| [[implants/survival/song-2024-long-term-clinical-radiographic-outcomes]] (Yonsei, 872 implants) | mean 12.3 y | 95.2% implant / 88.4% patient; 78.4% of failures late |
| [[implants/survival/yum-2023-the-cumulative-survival-rate-of]] (SNUDH, SLA, 782) | ≥5 y after prosthesis | 96.8% (25 failures; 17 peri-implantitis) |
| [[implants/survival/kim-2025-clinical-evaluation-of-tapered-straight]] (SNUDH, TST SLA, 316; early failures excluded) | ≥5 y | 98.1% (6 late failures) |
| [[implants/survival/lee-2026-supportive-periodontal-therapy-improves-implant]] (SNUDH + Pusan National, 3,555) | 5 y | 98.8% implant / 99.0% patient |
| [[implants/survival/kim-2026-long-term-survival-rates-of]] (Dankook, patients with disabilities, 465) | up to 10 y | 98.3%; 99.7% functional survival of restored implants |
| [[implants/survival/kim-2025-survival-rate-of-dental-implants]] (Wonkwang, anterior mandible, 604) | mean 44.4 mo | 97.68% (14 failures) |
| [[implants/survival/jang-2011-a-retrospective-study-on-related]] (Seoul Veterans Hospital, 6,385) | mean 45.7 mo | 96.33% as reported (crude 98.3%; internal inconsistency noted on the page) |
| [[implants/short-narrow/kim-2024-long-term-clinical-study-of-fixed]] (SNU Bundang, one-piece narrow, 40) | mean 10.5 y | 95.1% implant / 96.0% patient |

Patient-level survival is lower than implant-level survival wherever both are given (88.4% vs 95.2%; 95.70% vs 98.23%).

**Nationwide NHIS data.** [[implants/survival/lee-2024-risk-of-late-implant-loss]] followed 2,354,706 implants in people aged 65 or older (mean 2.84 years): late removal was 0.53%, and relative to sandblasted, large-grit, acid-etched (SA) surfaces the adjusted hazard ratio for removal was 1.841 for resorbable blast media (RBM) and 1.424 for hydroxyapatite (HA); abutment type was not associated. This direction agrees with the single-centre RBM cohort above, but claims data lack bone, smoking, periodontitis and brand information.

**Prosthesis lifespan, not implant lifespan.** [[implants/survival/kim-2024-prediction-of-lifespan-and-assessing]] studied 841 implant prostheses in 485 patients at 16 institutions and estimated a median prosthesis survival of 16 years; the outcome was prosthesis failure, not implant loss. No Korean study in the wiki reports a median implant lifespan.

Definitions differ (before prosthesis; first months; first radiograph after prosthesis), so these are descriptive. Two patterns are consistent: large cohorts of standard-diameter implants sit at about 0.8–3.4%, while narrow-diameter and small immediate-placement or immediate-loading series reach 4–15%.

**What nationwide data can and cannot say.** The three NHIS papers define failure by re-implantation or removal claims after a completed procedure. [[implants/survival/lee-2024-risk-of-late-implant-loss]] excludes failure before restoration, and [[implants/survival/kim-2026-association-between-smoking-and-the]] and [[implants/survival/kim-2025-association-between-the-type-of]] report no early/late split. They therefore describe overall or late loss and say nothing about pre-prosthetic failure.

### 3. Risk factors (Korean data only)

- **Smoking:** NHIS-HEALS cohort of 23,573 adults: 605 claim-based failures; current smokers adjusted HR 1.59 (95% CI 1.20–2.09), ex-smokers 1.16 (not significant), dose-response above 10 cigarettes per day, 10 years or 10 pack-years; current smokers 3.39 in women vs 1.48 in men. In narrow-diameter implants, smoking OR 4.758 and osteoporosis OR 3.420. The SNUDH SLA cohort found smoking not significant (HR 2.050, p = 0.333) with a very small smoker group.
- **Operator and site (early failure):** trainee surgeon aHR 2.86 (1.37–5.94) and mandible aHR 2.31 (1.12–4.76); the mandible effect appeared only after adjustment.
- **Periodontitis and maintenance:** periodontitis-related tooth loss aHR 3.57, poor supportive periodontal therapy (SPT) compliance aHR 2.35, and no-GBR aHR 0.42 for removal; regular SPT offsets the periodontitis penalty (98.8% vs 96.3%, p = 0.004).
- **Age:** HR 1.17 per year in the 3.0 mm series (only 6 events); lowest survival in patients older than 79 in the Seoul Veterans cohort (66.67%).
- **Care setting:** hospital-based clinics had lower failure claims than private practices (IPTW HR 0.35) and group practices (0.34); the top 10% and 5% volume private practices had higher failure (HR 1.23, 1.38). Residual confounding by case mix is likely; this is not evidence that hospital care itself prevents failure.
- **Surface and system:** RBM HR 1.841 vs SA in NHIS data; fixture fracture dominated RBM cohort failures; double-threaded tapered vs dual-thread HR 2.507 in the SLA cohort.
- **Surgery staging and GBR:** two-stage surgery HR 4.769 (10 events); GBR trend HR 8.12 (p = 0.07, 3.0 mm series).
- **Osteoporosis:** OR 3.420 in narrow-diameter implants, but a one-year prospective series of postmenopausal women stratified by bone mineral density had no failures ([[implants/survival/cho-2025-clinical-outcomes-and-bone-marker]]; survival from the abstract); the follow-up is too short and the group too small to reconcile the two.
- **Sinus-related:** all three failures in the immediate-placement series were sinus-related, and [[sinus-lift/lateral/nguyen-2022-analysis-of-acute-sinusitis-related]] describes three early failures within about one month of sinus lifting with allograft and acute sinusitis (retrieved-implant analysis, hypothesis-generating).

### 4. Failure after replacement and late mechanical failure

- [[implants/survival/kim-2024-risk-factors-for-the-failure]]: of 259 failed implants, 80 re-implanted implants had a 1-year survival of 88.1%; implant surface and reimplantation timing were risk factors, smoking HR 5.066 was not significant (abstract only).
- [[implants/survival/lee-2023-risk-factors-impacting-the-survival]]: 464 replaced implants in 370 patients; 5-year survival 90.2% at sites with a periodontitis history vs 97.0% without; periodontitis history aHR 3.417 and GBR at the first placement aHR 2.152 (abstract only).
- [[implants/survival/choi-2026-masseter-muscle-volume-and-cross]]: 23 late mechanical failures vs 92 controls; odds of failure 1.24 per 1,000 mm³ of masseter volume and AUC 0.846 (exploratory; abstract only).

### 5. Stability measurement before loading

[[implants/isq/hong-2025-comparative-analysis-of-novel-damping]] (Ilsan Hospital) compared two damping-capacity devices before loading in 37 patients and 58 implants and found strong agreement with the reference device for one (r = -0.870) and moderate for the other (r = -0.473). No implant failure was analysed, so this is device agreement, not early-failure prediction.

## Methodology

Papers are the Korean-setting pages in the wiki as of 2026-11-08, gathered by PubMed surveillance on survival, failure and early failure with Korean affiliation, then summarised as source and wiki pages. This page reads those pages only. Counts and percentages in the tables are quoted from each page; early-failure percentages were computed here as count over the stated denominator and are rounded. No pooling was attempted because follow-up, failure definition and unit (implant, patient, prosthesis, claim) differ. Pages built from abstracts are marked.

## Results

1. Korean institution cohorts report long-term implant-level survival of about 95–98% with patient-level values lower.
2. The 92.5% RBM result reflects fixture fracture, and NHIS data point the same way for the RBM surface (HR 1.841).
3. Pre-prosthetic early failure is about 0.8–3.4% in large standard-diameter cohorts and higher in narrow-diameter and small immediate series.
4. Nationwide claims data cannot separate early failure and lack clinical covariates, so their effect sizes (smoking 1.59, care setting 0.35) are population-level associations.
5. Trainee placement, periodontitis-related tooth loss, poor maintenance and prior failure are the factors that recur in institution cohorts.
6. Reported ORs and HRs above 4 rest on 6–48 events and should be treated as hypotheses.

**Clinical reasoning drawn from the held pages (not new facts).** When counselling Korean patients, quote 95–98% long-term implant survival, say whether the figure is per implant or per patient, and add that the early-failure risk is higher for narrow-diameter or immediate protocols. Use maintenance (SPT) and periodontitis control as the modifiable levers, and warn smokers with a dose-response framing. Do not read hospital-versus-private differences as proof that either setting causes failure. Final judgement rests with the treating clinician.

**Gaps.** No Korean prospective multicentre study of early failure; no NHIS analysis that separates pre-prosthetic failure; no Korean data on anticoagulant or PPI effects; no study reports a median implant lifespan; and most cohorts give no ethnicity variable.
