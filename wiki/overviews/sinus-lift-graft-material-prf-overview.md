## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 범위: 상악동 거상 내부 이식재 선택(DBBM·BCP·dpDTM), PRF 보조, 이식 용량 계획(모체 클러스터 5)을 분리한 하위 종합. 절 번호는 모체 기준을 유지한다. [확인]
> - L-PRF + DBBM(Almutairi 2025, RCT 5편, n=140): 신생골 +7.07%, 잔존 이식재 −7.93%(잔존 이질성 0%). 그러나 부하 시 ISQ 차이는 없었다(p=0.56). [확인]
> - A-PRF + DBBM 분할구강 RCT(Motimath 2026, n=30, 60 상동, 9개월): 신생골 42.8% vs 31.2%, 잔존 이식재 28.4% vs 41.6%(p=0.001). 임플란트 생존은 두 군 모두 100%였다. [확인]
> - BCP vs DBBM(Alkandari 2025, RCT 10편, n=328): BCP가 신생골 +3.48%, 잔존 이식재 −8.41% 우위였다. 임플란트 생존 자료는 없다. 조직계측 근거다. [확인]
> - dpDTM(사람 치아 유래 탈단백 탈회 기질): 단일 기관 RCT(Paetnukroh 2024, n=24, 6개월, 동시 식립)에서 체적 감소는 차이가 없었고(p=0.690), ISQ는 약 70로 비슷했으며, 실패는 0이었다. [확인]
> - dpDTM의 유일한 차이는 근심 이식재 높이 감소(−1.08 vs −0.58 mm, p=0.04)였다. 차이가 0.5 mm이고 군당 12상동이라 검출력이 부족하므로, 이는 가설이지 DBBM 선호의 근거가 아니다. [미검증]
> - 재료 간 장기 골 안정성 자료는 모든 재료에서 부족하다(Del Fabbro 2025, SR 49 RCT, 생존 84.6–100%, 재료 간 차이 없음). 재료는 생존 기대보다 생물학적 위험, 공급원, 비용으로 고르라는 결론이다. [확인]
> - 이식 용량(Kim 2007, n=19, CT 체적): 무치악 상동은 5·10·15 mm 거상에서 더 많은 이식재가 필요했다(15 mm: 4.85 vs 3.80 cm³, 약 36% 차이). 2007년 소규모 연구지만 참조표로 쓸 가치가 있다. [확인]
> - **단일 재료 NMA 재료 간 순위** (Somngam 2025, NMA 11 RCT, 283 환자, 362 상악동; 복합체 제외): BCP 대비 신생골 — 자가골 +12.33%, 동종골 +5.14%, 이종골 −4.14%. 누적순위곡선하면적 (Surface Under the Cumulative Ranking, SUCRA) 순위: 자가골 > 동종골 > BCP > TCP > 이종골. 이종골 잔존 이식재 BCP 대비 +6.21%. 생존 데이터는 거의 없음. [확인]
> - **베이지안 NMA: 복합체 포함 시 자가골이 최고가 아님** (Trimmel 2021, Bayesian NMA 34 RCT, 842 상악동, 28 하위군): 378개 쌍 비교 중 유의는 2건뿐; SUCRA — 이종골+골수농축액 (Bone Marrow Concentrate, BMC) 81%, 이종골+혈소판풍부혈장 (Platelet-Rich Plasma, PRP) 77%, 자가골 단독 12위(57%). 자가골이 gold standard라는 단순화는 이 근거 틀에서 지지받지 않음. 확실성 낮음~매우 낮음. [확인]
> - **동종골 블록이 자가골·DBB 동등** (Pogacian-Maier 2024, SR+MA 7 관찰연구, 238 환자): 동결건조피질골 (Freeze-Dried Bone Allograft, FDBA) vs 탈단백우골 (Deproteinized Bovine Bone, DBB) 골부피 WMD −0.17(p=0.53, I²=81%). FDBA vs 자가골 생존 RR 1.00. [확인]
> - **CO3Ap 상악동 데이터** (탄산칼슘아파타이트 (Carbonate Apatite, CO3Ap); Nunn 2024, SR): 인체 상악동 신생골 33.8%(7개월), 3년 임플란트 생존 100%(n=13), 잔존 이식재 14.2% vs DBBM 24.2%(소규모). RCT 없음 — 관찰 참고치. [확인]
> - **Trimmel vs Somngam 자가골 순위 불일치 이유**: Trimmel은 복합체(이종골+BMC/PRP) 포함, 5–8개월 치유창, Bayesian; Somngam은 단일 재료만 포함, 동질 설계. 범위 차이가 불일치의 주 원인. [미검증]
> - **RCT 4편 추가(2026-10-28)**: 돼지 이종골은 DBBM과 같았다(광물화 조직 32.51 vs 29.24%, p=.570; Galindo-Moreno 2022, 10명 분할구강). DBBM 입자 크기(소 0.25–1 mm vs 대 1–2 mm)는 신생골·4년 높이 유지·3년 생존(100%)에 차이가 없었고 골-이식재 접촉만 소입자가 높았다(27.9 vs 19.9%; Krennmair 2024). [확인]
> - **넓은 상악동(잔존골 <3 mm)에서는 자가골 혼합이 가장 좋았다**: 이종골+자가골 70:30의 신생골 면적이 이종골+L-PRF의 약 2.7배, 이종골 단독의 약 5.6배였다(Harlos 2022, 36명). L-PRF는 단독보다 낫지만 결합조직이 많았다. 즉 Almutairi의 L-PRF 이득은 큰 결손에서 자가골을 대신하지 못한다. [확인]
> - **골유도성 인산칼슘(BCPN, 바늘형 미세표면)**: 양 모델에서 입자형보다 신생골이 많았고, 사람 RCT 10명에서 자가골과 안정성·높이·신생골이 비슷했다(van 2023, 초록만). 표면 형상이 재료 등급만큼 중요하다는 신호다. [확인]
> - 종합 한 줄: 기본 선택은 DBBM ± PRF이고, BCP는 신생골 최대화, 동종골은 공급원 대안, dpDTM은 자가 공급원이라는 근거로 고르는 대안이다. 임플란트 결과에서 어떤 단일 재료도 우위가 입증되지 않았으며, 복합체(이종골+BMC/PRP)가 NMA 조직계측 순위에서 자가골 단독을 앞선다. [미검증]

## Three-line Summary

This sub-overview holds the graft-material cluster of the parent lateral-window sinus-lift synthesis: DBBM with PRF, BCP, dpDTM, graft-volume planning, and (updated 2026-10-21) cross-class network meta-analyses (NMAs).
PRF added to DBBM consistently raises new bone without changing ISQ; BCP outperforms DBBM histomorphometrically; dpDTM rests on one small RCT; among single materials NMA ranks autograft first, but composites (xenograft+BMC/PRP) rank above autograft alone when included.
The defensible default remains DBBM ± PRF; BCP and allograft are evidence-supported alternatives; no single material has demonstrated implant-outcome superiority.

## 세줄요약

이 하위 종합은 모체 측방창 상악동 거상 종합의 이식재 절을 담는다: PRF를 더한 DBBM, BCP, dpDTM, 이식 용량 계획, 그리고 재료 간 네트워크 메타분석 (Network Meta-Analysis, NMA) 결과(2026-10-21 추가).
DBBM에 PRF를 더하면 신생골이 일관되게 늘고 잔존 이식재가 줄지만 부하 시 임플란트 안정성 지수 (Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ)는 바뀌지 않는다. 단일 재료 NMA에서 자가골이 1위이나 복합체(이종골+골수농축액 (Bone Marrow Concentrate, BMC)/PRP)를 포함하면 자가골 단독이 12위로 내려간다. 동종골은 이종골·자가골과 동등한 생존율을 보인다.
기본 선택은 DBBM ± PRF이며, BCP와 동종골은 근거가 있는 대안이고, dpDTM은 자가 공급원이 목적인 대안이다. 임플란트 결과 우위가 입증된 재료는 없다.

## Cluster 5 — Graft material selection and PRF adjunct (이식재 선택·PRF 보조)

This cluster addresses what goes *inside* the augmented sinus: graft material choice across three biological families (bovine xenograft DBBM, synthetic ceramic BCP, and human tooth-derived dpDTM) and the biological adjunct value of PRF (platelet-rich fibrin).

### PRF adjunct to DBBM: histomorphometric benefit is real

Two studies converge on a consistent direction of effect for PRF combined with DBBM:

- **Almutairi 2025 SR+MA (L-PRF+DBBM, 5 RCTs, n=140).** The highest-evidence synthesis specifically for L-PRF adjunction in sinus augmentation. L-PRF+DBBM produced significantly more new bone (MD **+7.07%**, 95% CI 2.20–11.93, p<0.001, I²=37%) and significantly less residual graft (MD **−7.93%**, 95% CI −11.20 to −4.66, p<0.001, I²=0%). ISQ at loading was not significantly different (MD −2.25, p=0.56). The zero heterogeneity for the residual graft outcome is exceptional — DBBM's slow incorporation is reliably mitigated by L-PRF across all 5 included RCTs.
- **Motimath 2026 split-mouth RCT (A-PRF+DBBM, n=30, 60 sinuses, 9 months).** A-PRF prepared at the low-speed centrifugation protocol (2700 rpm/12 min) combined with i-PRF yielded **42.8% vs 31.2%** new bone (p=0.001) and **28.4% vs 41.6%** residual graft (p=0.001) vs DBBM alone. Bone density (HU), graft height stability, and postoperative morbidity all favored A-PRF. Implant survival was 100% in both groups.

The magnitude of new bone benefit is consistent across PRF generations (L-PRF MD +7.07% by Almutairi 2025; A-PRF absolute difference +11.6% by Motimath 2026), and the residual graft reduction is equally consistent (L-PRF −7.93%; A-PRF −13.2%). Despite histomorphometric superiority, **neither study found ISQ benefit at implant loading** — functional osseointegration as measured by ISQ does not appear to accelerate even when the graft bed histology improves.

### BCP vs DBBM: synthetic bone can match or exceed xenograft in new bone formation

- **Alkandari 2025 SR+MA (BCP vs DBBM, 10 RCTs, n=328, 389 implants).** BCP (typically 60% HA + 40% β-TCP) showed significantly greater new bone formation than DBBM (MD **+3.48%**, 95% CI 0.89–5.97, p=0.01, I²=44%), significantly less residual graft (MD **−8.41%**, 95% CI −13.12 to −3.72, p<0.001, I²=86%), and higher soft tissue fraction (MD +6.01%, p<0.001, I²=72%). High heterogeneity for residual graft and soft tissue reflects differing BCP formulations (HA:TCP ratios) across included RCTs. Critically, **no implant survival data** are reported — this SR+MA addresses histomorphometry only.

The BCP advantage over DBBM in new bone formation is mechanistically explained by the TCP component's ion-release osteostimulatory effect vs DBBM's near-permanent osteoconductive scaffold. DBBM's near-permanence was historically valued for volume stability; BCP resorbs more completely (lower residual graft), creating space that new bone actively occupies.

### dpDTM vs DBBM: a third material family — human tooth-derived matrix

The BCP-vs-DBBM axis above compares a synthetic ceramic against a bovine xenograft. A third biological family sits outside both: **deproteinized human demineralized tooth matrix (dpDTM)**, derived from extracted human teeth rather than from cattle or from a reactor. Dentin matrix is developmentally and compositionally closer to bone than either comparator — a collagen-I-rich mineralized matrix carrying endogenous non-collagenous proteins and growth factors that a deproteinized bovine mineral has had removed and a synthetic ceramic never had. Because the donor tooth is typically the patient's own, dpDTM also sidesteps the xenogenic-source objection (religious, dietary, or prion-risk concerns) that DBBM raises for some patients, without opening an iliac or intraoral autograft donor site. This is a genuinely new material axis in this cluster, not a restatement of BCP vs DBBM.

- **Paetnukroh 2024 RCT (dpDTM vs DBBM, parallel-group, n=24, 6 months).** Block-randomized single-center trial (Prince of Songkla University, Thailand), 12 sinuses per arm, lateral-window augmentation with **simultaneous** implant placement. Outcomes were 3D CBCT volumetric subtraction and resonance frequency analysis (ISQ) at 6 months. Total volumetric graft reduction did not differ (dpDTM 120.33 mm³ vs DBBM 108.51 mm³, **p=0.690**), and ISQ rose comparably in both arms to **~70** at 6 months (NS). There were **no implant failures in either group**. The one significant difference was mesial graft height reduction: **−1.08 mm (dpDTM) vs −0.58 mm (DBBM), p=0.04**; mid and distal positions showed no difference (−0.59 to −0.93 vs −0.55 to −0.82, all p>0.05).

**How much weight the mesial finding carries.** The honest reading sits between "significant, therefore real" and "small, therefore ignorable." Against dismissing it: the point estimates lean the same direction on *every* dimension measured — dpDTM lost more total volume (120.33 vs 108.51 mm³) and more height at mid and distal as well — so the mesial result is not a lone outlier pointing against an otherwise uniform pattern. Against over-reading it: the absolute difference is **0.5 mm**, at the margin of what CBCT height measurement resolves reliably; the trial has 12 sinuses per arm, which is underpowered to separate a half-millimetre effect from noise; and with four height/volume comparisons reported, one crossing p=0.05 while the others do not is an unremarkable multiplicity pattern. This is the same discipline applied to Alkandari 2025's residual-graft and soft-tissue results above, where I²=86% and 72% mean the pooled point estimate conceals formulation-driven variation rather than settling the question. Practical reading: treat mesial height as a hypothesis for a larger trial to test, not as a reason to prefer DBBM at the mesial wall.

**What this does and does not settle about the material-choice literature.** Del Fabbro 2025 (SR, 49 RCTs, 1,722 lateral augmentations) found implant survival of 84.6–100% with **no significant difference** among DBBM, autogenous bone ± DBBM, and synthetic HA/β-TCP, and concluded that graft choice should rest on biological risk profile, source, and cost rather than on survival expectations — while flagging that **high-quality long-term bone-stability data remain insufficient across all material classes**. Paetnukroh 2024 is a first installment in the right measurement modality for that gap: objective 3D volumetric subtraction rather than the linear height or histomorphometric proxies most of this cluster relies on. But it is explicitly a **6-month** study, so it does not close a gap defined as *long-term* stability — it supplies short-term volumetric evidence where previously there was none for this material, and leaves the long-horizon question exactly where Del Fabbro 2025 left it. Read alongside Del Fabbro's "choose on source and cost, not survival" conclusion, dpDTM's appeal is precisely a source argument: equivalent short-term behaviour from a graft the patient supplies themselves.

**Evidence-strength caveat — this is the weakest entry in the cluster.** The other Cluster 5 entries are SR+MAs pooling 5 and 10 RCTs (Almutairi 2025; Alkandari 2025) or a 30-patient split-mouth design with within-patient control (Motimath 2026). Paetnukroh 2024 is a **single, small (n=24), single-center, parallel-group RCT with 6-month follow-up and no histologic evaluation** — so there is no new-bone-percentage or residual-graft figure comparable to the histomorphometric numbers that anchor the rest of this cluster, and volumetric stability at 6 months cannot speak to the multi-year horizons implicit elsewhere on this page. Parallel-group allocation also leaves it more exposed to inter-patient anatomical variation than Motimath's split-mouth design. dpDTM is therefore a **promising signal warranting replication, not a practice-changing result**, and it should not yet be presented to patients as evidentially equivalent to DBBM, whose sinus-augmentation track record spans decades and thousands of implants.

### Graft volume planning — how much material is needed, not just which material

All four papers above answer "which graft material" — Kim 2007 (Korean case-control, CT volumetry, n=19 patients/20 sinuses) answers a prerequisite practical question the cluster otherwise skips: **how much** graft to prepare or harvest. Using automated CT volume analysis at three clinically standard augmentation heights (5, 10, 15 mm from sinus floor), edentulous sinuses required significantly more graft volume than dentulous sinuses at every height — 0.56 vs 0.41 cm³ (5 mm, p=0.027), 2.35 vs 1.76 cm³ (10 mm, p=0.018), 4.85 vs 3.80 cm³ (15 mm, p=0.044), a ~36% difference at 15 mm. The likely mechanism is post-extraction sinus pneumatization enlarging the edentulous sinus, consistent with (though not directly tested against) Schriber 2019's pneumatization data in Cluster 2. Kim 2007 also tabulates graft-material resorption rates from the contemporary literature (iliac autograft ~50%, mandibular autograft less, DBBM xenograft ~20%) to convert a target final volume into an as-placed harvest/order quantity.

**Where this fits.** This is planning-stage arithmetic, not a material-superiority claim — it answers "how much of whatever material I choose do I need at this patient's edentulous/dentulous status and target height," a question every material-selection decision above (DBBM, BCP, dpDTM, ± PRF) still has to answer downstream. The 2007 vintage and single small Korean cohort (n=19) limit generalizability, and the linear cross-sectional-surface assumption is a stated methodological simplification — but no other paper in this wiki's sinus-lift literature supplies a comparable graft-quantity reference table, so it remains the practical anchor for this specific question until superseded.

### Cross-class network meta-analyses: how materials rank across the full substitution space (2026-10-21)

The four papers above (Almutairi 2025, Motimath 2026, Alkandari 2025, Paetnukroh 2024) all compare one or two specific graft options against DBBM. Three newly ingested NMAs attempt a broader simultaneous ranking across the full substitution space:

**Somngam 2025 NMA (single materials only, composites excluded, 11 RCTs, 283 patients, 362 sinuses).** Using BCP as the common reference, the mean new bone differences were: autograft +12.33% (95% CI 5.36–19.30), allograft +5.14% (0.17–10.11), xenograft −4.14% (−8.24 to −0.04). SUCRA ranking: autograft (1st) > allograft (2nd) > BCP (3rd) > TCP (4th) > xenograft (5th). Residual graft vs BCP: xenograft +6.21% higher (more residual). Importantly, **implant survival data were nearly absent** (most studies did not report it), so the ranking is histomorphometric only. Somngam 2025's eligibility excluded composite (multi-material) interventions, which is appropriate for isolating material-class effects but means the top ranking of autograft applies to autograft used alone, without a carrier or adjunct. See [[sinus-lift/lateral/somngam-2025-new-bone-formation-biphasic-calcium]].

**Trimmel 2021 Bayesian NMA (composites included, 34 RCTs, 842 sinuses, 28 subgroups ranked by NB% at 5–8 months).** Only 2 of 378 pairwise comparisons reached significance (bovine xenograft+BMC vs allograft; bovine+BMC vs biodegradable membrane). SUCRA top 5: bovine+BMC (81%), bovine+PRP (77%), bioactive glass+autograft 1:1 (70%), nanocrystalline HA in silica gel (70%), bioactive glass (70%). Autologous bone alone ranked **12th (SUCRA 57%)** — not the top material at 5–8 months. Trimmel 2021 directly contradicts the assumption that autograft is the histomorphometric gold standard in sinus grafting: when composites are eligible, the best single-material composite (xenograft+BMC) outperforms autograft alone significantly, and autograft falls mid-table. Certainty: low to very low. See [[sinus-lift/lateral/trimmel-2021-relative-performance-various-biomaterials]].

**Reconciling the Somngam vs Trimmel discrepancy.** Somngam shows autograft first; Trimmel shows autograft 12th. The discrepancy is largely explained by eligibility: Somngam excluded all composites, so autograft competes only against other single materials. Trimmel included composites with adjuvants (BMC, PRP, bioactive glass mixtures), so autograft alone competes against multi-ingredient combinations. The two conclusions are compatible: *autograft is the best single material, but composite adjuvant strategies can outperform it.* Additional explanatory factors: different healing-window definitions (Somngam's studies are not restricted to 5–8 months; Trimmel's are), and different reference anchors. The low certainty of both NMAs means neither should be used to conclusively rank materials for individual clinical decisions.

**Pogacian-Maier 2024 SR+MA (FDBA allograft in lateral sinus, 7 observational studies, 238 patients).** FDBA vs deproteinized bovine bone (DBB): bone volume WMD −0.17 (p=0.53, I²=81%) — no significant volume difference, high heterogeneity. FDBA vs autograft: implant survival RR 1.00. This SR+MA closes the allograft gap in the evidence cluster: allograft in sinus augmentation achieves comparable histomorphometric graft volume and equivalent implant survival to autograft and to xenograft, at no donor-site cost. The I²=81% on volume means the specific allograft preparation and patient selection drive outcomes more than the generic class comparison. See [[sinus-lift/lateral/pogacian-maier-2024-allograft-bone-lateral-sinus-sr]].

**CO3Ap sinus data (Nunn 2024 SR).** The small human sinus dataset from the Nunn 2024 carbonate apatite SR (n=13–27, observational) shows: sinus height loss 16.4–27.8%; new bone 33.8% at 7 months; 3-year implant survival 100%; resorption 14.2% vs DBBM 24.2%. These are favourable but from non-randomised studies with small n — categorize as hypothesis-generating for future RCTs, not practice-changing. CO3Ap's fast-resorption profile may reduce the residual-graft burden that DBBM accumulates.

**Note on thomas-2026 internal inconsistency.** The thomas-2026 SR summary line states "autograft > xenograft ≥ allograft > alloplast," but its NBF table (Schmitt data) shows: autograft 41.7%, allograft 35.4%, alloplast/BCP 30.3%, xenograft 24.9% — placing xenograft last, not second. This inconsistency within the thomas-2026 page means the summary-line ranking should not be used directly; check the source table data instead.

### Four more lateral-window RCTs (2026-10-28)

What they change:
- **Xenograft source and particle size are interchangeable.** Porcine behaves like bovine (Galindo-Moreno), and small and large DBBM give the same bone and the same 4-year height (Krennmair). Pick on availability, patient preference (for example, avoiding bovine material) and handling.
- **Defect size changes the PRF answer.** In wide sinuses with under 3 mm of residual bone, Harlos 2022 found autograft mixing far ahead of L-PRF. The L-PRF benefit pooled by Almutairi 2025 (+7.07% new bone) comes mostly from ordinary defects. Where the defect is very large, a small autograft fraction is the better-supported addition. This also matches the Trimmel 2021 finding that composites rank high.
- **Surface design matters within synthetics.** van 2023 shows that two BCPs with the same chemistry differ in bone formation because of submicron surface shape. Pooling "BCP" as one material (Alkandari 2025, Somngam 2025) hides this.

### Synthesis [미검증]

PRF (both L-PRF and A-PRF) reliably improves histomorphometric new bone formation when added to DBBM, with consistent effect across SR+MA and split-mouth RCT levels of evidence. The benefit is biologically real but does not translate to ISQ advantage at the time of loading. BCP outperforms DBBM in new bone formation and resorbability, but the clinical bottom line on implant survival requires a separate evidence base. For clinicians choosing between graft materials: BCP is a credible synthetic alternative to DBBM when maximizing histomorphometric new bone is the goal; PRF adjunction is a low-cost enhancement that consistently boosts new bone formation regardless of which xenograft base is used.

The three material families in this cluster (DBBM, BCP, dpDTM) are evidenced at very different depths, and that asymmetry should drive how confidently each is offered. DBBM is the reference material throughout — every comparison here is *against* it. BCP's advantage is histomorphometric and pooled across 10 RCTs, but silent on survival. dpDTM rests on one small 6-month RCT showing equivalence in volumetric stability and ISQ with zero failures — enough to call it viable and worth watching, not enough to call it established. Allograft (FDBA), supported by Pogacian-Maier 2024, now joins the cluster as a fourth viable family: equivalent bone volume to xenograft and equivalent implant survival to autograft, without donor-site cost.

The cross-class NMAs add two important dimensions absent from the pairwise cluster: (1) across single materials, autograft consistently tops histomorphometric rankings (Somngam 2025), but composites (xenograft+BMC/PRP) outperform autograft alone when both compete in the same network (Trimmel 2021); (2) the certainty is uniformly low to very low for all NMA comparisons, so the rankings inform clinical reasoning about relative biological potential but do not mandate material choice. The cluster's real weakness remains the near-total absence of long-term bone-stability and implant-survival data stratified by graft material (Del Fabbro 2025). The defensible default remains DBBM ± PRF; BCP, allograft, and dpDTM are principled alternatives chosen for specific reasons — maximal new-bone histomorphometry (BCP), xenogenic-source avoidance without donor site (allograft or dpDTM) — rather than for demonstrated superiority in implant outcomes.
