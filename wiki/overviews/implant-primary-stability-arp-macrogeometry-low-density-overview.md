## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 이 페이지는 도전적 골환경(저밀도 골, 치조제보존술(Alveolar Ridge Preservation, ARP) 이식 부위)에서 임플란트 1차 안정성(primary stability)을 결정하는 두 축 — 식립 위치(ARP 경계 이내 vs 경계 초과)와 매크로지오메트리(macro-geometry, 나사산 설계) — 를 종합한다.
> - **ARP 경계 초과 이점은 단기적**: 원래 골에 걸치면 삽입 토크(Insertion Torque, IT)가 2배 높으나(17 vs 33 Ncm, p=0.018), 10주 임플란트 안정성 지수(Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ)는 차이 없음(76 vs 80, NS) — 이식 골이 충분히 성숙해 2차 안정성을 보상한다 (Ko 2024, n=30).
> - **ARP 부위 전체가 연약한 골**: Ko 2024에서 양 군 모두 골질 3형(75%) 또는 4형(25%)이었음 — ARP 이식 부위는 본질적으로 저밀도 골환경이고, 이는 두 축이 임상적으로 교차하는 지점이다.
> - **매크로지오메트리가 ISQ 결정**: in vitro에서 나사산 형태만으로 D3/D4 골에서 IT·ISQ 순위가 갈렸으며, 토크 최고(Zimmer TSV triple-thread) ≠ ISQ 최고(Dentium Superline deep double-thread) — 단일 지표로 임플란트를 선택하면 오판 위험 (Ayub 2025).
> - **강한 골밀도-안정성 상관**: 매크로지오메트리에 관계없이 모든 설계에서 골밀도와 IT·ISQ 간 상관계수 r=0.83–0.97 — 골밀도 자체가 지배 변수이며, 매크로지오메트리는 그 위에 추가 이득을 제공한다.
> - **임상 통합**: ARP 이식 부위에서 낮은 초기 토크는 즉시 부하 실패를 의미하지 않는다. ISQ ≥70은 골 경계 내 식립에서도 달성 가능하며, 저밀도 골에 적합한 매크로지오메트리(deeper thread, multi-thread)가 ISQ를 추가로 보강할 수 있다.

## Three-line Summary

Two papers address primary stability in challenging low-density sites: a retrospective case series (n=30 ARP sites) comparing implant placement within vs. beyond the graft boundary, and an in vitro study (n=36, polyurethane blocks) isolating macro-geometry effects in D3/D4 bone.

ARP-site implants extending beyond the graft boundary achieved nearly double the insertion torque (33.33 vs. 17.08 Ncm, p=0.018) but equivalent 10-week ISQ (80.66 vs. 76.44, NS); across macro-geometries, triple-thread designs led in insertion torque while deep double-thread designs led in ISQ, and bone density correlated strongly with all stability metrics (r=0.83–0.97).

In challenging low-density sites — including ARP grafted bone — adequate secondary stability (ISQ ≥70) is achievable regardless of initial torque; however, implant macro-geometry selection matters when optimizing ISQ in soft bone, and a single stability metric is insufficient to rank implant designs.

## Summary

This synthesis integrates two papers that approach the same clinical problem — how to achieve adequate primary stability in challenging, low-density bone environments — from complementary angles.

**Ko et al. (2024)** examined whether implant placement extending up to 3 mm beyond an ARP graft boundary provides superior outcomes compared to remaining entirely within the graft. In this retrospective case series (30 sites, 28 patients; Seoul), ARP was performed with DBBM-C (Bio-Oss Collagen) and porcine collagen membrane (Bio-Gide) following periodontally compromised extractions, with implant placement after 16–24 weeks of healing. The key finding: native bone engagement approximately doubled insertion torque (IT), but this initial torque advantage had fully resolved by 10 weeks, with ISQ values indistinguishable between groups — both exceeding the ISQ ≥70 loading threshold. Critically, all sites in both groups were type 3 (75%) or type 4 (25%) bone quality, confirming that ARP-grafted sites are inherently low-density environments.

**Ayub et al. (2025)** tested three commercial tapered implants (Straumann BLT, Zimmer TSV, Dentium Superline) in standardized polyurethane blocks simulating D3 and D4 bone, isolating macro-geometry as the sole variable. The study demonstrated that thread design determines both insertion torque and ISQ — but via different geometric mechanisms. The triple-thread, small-pitch Zimmer TSV achieved the highest IT and removal torque (RT), while the deep double-thread Dentium Superline achieved the highest ISQ; these rankings were consistent across both bone densities. Bone density itself was the dominant determinant of all stability metrics (r=0.83–0.97), with macro-geometry providing an incremental advantage atop the density effect.

The two papers are clinically complementary: Ko 2024 confirms that the ARP grafted site is a low-density environment where even a low-torque implant placement achieves adequate secondary stability by 10 weeks, while Ayub 2025 shows that within that environment, macro-geometry choices can meaningfully influence ISQ — with the important caveat that torque and ISQ reward different design features and should not be conflated.

## Axis 1 — ARP Site Placement and the Torque-to-ISQ Transition

The Ko 2024 study reveals a fundamental biological principle: **primary stability metrics (insertion torque) and secondary stability metrics (10-week ISQ) are dissociable in ARP-grafted sites**.

Implants extending into pristine native bone beyond the ARP boundary engage denser cortical support, generating significantly higher insertion torque. However, the DBBM-C graft material undergoes angiogenesis, osteoclast-mediated remodeling, and new bone deposition during the 10-week healing period. By the time ISQ is measured, the grafted bone has matured sufficiently to provide equivalent stability to native bone — the initial mechanical advantage disappears.

This has a direct clinical implication: **a low insertion torque at an ARP site should not trigger alarm or alter the protocol**. The 29/30 implant survival rate across both groups, and the fact that both groups exceeded ISQ ≥70, support proceeding with the standard 10-week ISQ-guided loading protocol regardless of initial torque — provided ISQ confirms adequate secondary stability at loading time.

The single failure in the study (within-ARP group; IT 10 Ncm, ISQ 72.5 at 10 weeks) occurred at 2 years after loading — beyond the measurement window — suggesting that even the lowest-torque implant in the series had achieved satisfactory stability at loading, and that 2-year failure reflects factors beyond initial placement mechanics.

| Parameter | Within-ARP (n=12) | Beyond-ARP (n=18) | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insertion torque (Ncm) | 17.08 ± 11.17 | 33.33 ± 13.39 | p=0.018 |
| ISQ at 10 weeks | 76.44 ± 6.62 | 80.66 ± 6.27 | NS |
| Torque–ISQ correlation | r=0.415, p=0.023 | | moderate |
| Bone quality type 3/4 | 75% / 25% | 72.2% / 27.8% | similar |
| Survival (through prosthesis) | 11/12 | 18/18 | 96.7% overall |

The moderate torque–ISQ correlation (r=0.415, R²=0.17) means initial torque explains only ~17% of variance in 10-week ISQ — a weak predictive signal. ISQ at loading time, not placement torque, should govern loading decisions.

This moderate coupling is not unique to ARP-grafted sites. Bavetta et al. (2019), studying implants placed into fresh extraction sockets — another bone-quality-compromised site type — found that torque and ISQ decouple in a similar way: fresh-socket implants started at significantly lower placement-day ISQ than implants in healed bone (57.55 vs 72.86, p<0.001), yet all 41 implants survived when loading was governed by an insertion-torque criterion (ITV ≥35 Ncm) rather than an ISQ threshold, with fresh-socket ISQ only converging toward healed-bone levels by 4 months. That torque-based loading succeeds even while ISQ sits well below conventional thresholds is an independent illustration of the same pattern Ko 2024 quantified numerically — across two very different compromised-bone contexts (ARP graft, fresh socket), insertion torque and ISQ track related but distinct dimensions of stability, and neither should be read as a proxy for the other.

The ISQ ≥70 loading threshold applied throughout this synthesis (both Ko 2024 groups exceeded it) sits above the more widely used ISQ ≥65 threshold reported by Andersson et al. (2019) in a large 5-year retrospective series of 745 Neoss implants in 334 patients. That study found unadjusted odds of failure rising sharply below ISQ 70 at placement (OR 3.9) and below ISQ 75 at loading (OR 17.9), while too few failures occurred below ISQ 65 for the comparison to reach significance — suggesting ISQ ≥65 functions as a failure-risk floor rather than a target threshold. Read against this wider evidence base, Ko 2024's requirement of ISQ ≥70 in ARP-grafted, inherently low-density sites looks conservative rather than idiosyncratic: it sits comfortably above Andersson's failure-risk inflection points, providing a margin of safety appropriate to grafted bone that is, by definition, less predictable than the native-bone-dominant population underlying the Andersson 2019 cohort.

## Axis 2 — Macro-geometry in Low-Density Bone: Metric-Dependent Rankings

The Ayub 2025 study isolates macro-geometry by holding all other variables constant: tapered body form, 10 mm length, ~4.0–4.1 mm diameter, and identical standardized drilling. The three systems differ only in thread geometry:

| Feature | Straumann BLT | Zimmer TSV | Dentium Superline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thread count | Single | Triple | Double |
| Thread shape | Reverse-buttress | V-shape | Reverse-buttress |
| Pitch (mm) | 0.8 | 0.6 | 1.8 |
| Thread depth (mm) | 0.30 | 0.36 | 0.40 |
| Self-cutting | Yes | No | Half-apical blade |

The outcome rankings across both bone densities:

**D3 bone (20 PCF / 0.35 g/cm³):**
| Metric | Straumann BLT | Zimmer TSV | Dentium Superline |
|---|---|---|---|
| IT (Ncm) | 26.50 | **30.21** | 22.90 |
| RT (Ncm) | 21.43 | **23.25** | 14.23 |
| ISQ | 61.00 | 60.88 | **63.29** |

**D4 bone (15 PCF / 0.24 g/cm³):**
| Metric | Straumann BLT | Zimmer TSV | Dentium Superline |
|---|---|---|---|
| IT (Ncm) | 10.93 | **18.07** | 10.37 |
| RT (Ncm) | 8.86 | **14.48** | 6.34 |
| ISQ | 57.33 | 55.70 | **58.46** |

The ranking inversion between IT/RT and ISQ is mechanistically interpretable:
- **High insertion torque**: requires large contact area per unit advance → smaller pitch (more threads per mm), larger surface area → Zimmer TSV (triple thread, 0.6 mm pitch).
- **High ISQ**: requires stiffness at the interface, meaning deep thread purchase with the surrounding bone → deeper threads (0.40 mm) that interlock with bone trabecular structure → Dentium Superline.

The half-apical self-cutting blade on the Dentium Superline likely removes bone at the apex during insertion, reducing compressive engagement and lowering RT despite a deep thread profile.

Note: ISQ values in this in vitro study (55–63) are lower than clinical ISQ values typically reported (70–80+) because polyurethane blocks lack cortical bone and vary from natural bone's elastic modulus. The relative rankings are valid, but absolute values should not be imported into clinical decision thresholds.

## Evidence Limitations

**Ko 2024** is retrospective, single-center, small (n=30), with a 16-patient beyond-ARP group and 12-patient within-ARP group. Only one implant system (Astra Osseospeed and Dentium Superline) was used, and the study lacks a no-ARP control arm. The 10-week ISQ measurement window captures secondary stability at a single point; longer-term marginal bone level (MBL) and prosthetic outcomes beyond 2 years are not reported. The beyond-ARP group's higher torque may partly reflect the clinician's selection of more favorable sites for larger implants.

**Ayub 2025** uses polyurethane synthetic bone blocks — an accepted but imperfect surrogate. Blocks lack cortical bone, periosteum, angiogenesis, and bone remodeling, so results represent purely mechanical engagement without biologic osseointegration. The study is also limited to three specific commercial systems; findings about pitch and thread depth should be interpreted as geometric principles, not brand endorsements.

Together, the two papers converge on a clinically useful finding that is mechanistically plausible — the torque/ISQ dissociation and the macro-geometry metric-dependency — but both require prospective clinical validation before informing firm protocol changes.

Beyond the two levers covered above — ARP placement position and implant macro-geometry — a third lever deserves note: surgical technique. Rittipakorn et al. (2025), in a paired-site cadaveric tibia study of low-density (D3/D4) bone, compared a novel clockwise osseodensification protocol (CW-OD) against standard drilling and found CW-OD trending toward higher ISQ (67.5 vs 62.9, p=0.077) and insertion torque (34.0 vs 29.5 Ncm, p=0.052), with tighter variability, no low-torque outliers, and — notably — a significant IT–ISQ correlation only in the OD group (ρ=0.577 vs ρ=0.208 with standard drilling). This raises the possibility that osteotomy technique itself modulates how tightly torque and ISQ track each other in soft bone, offering a third, complementary axis alongside ARP placement position and macro-geometry selection. Like Ko 2024 and Ayub 2025, the Rittipakorn 2025 findings are small-sample and non-significant on their primary endpoints (cadaveric, no healing) and require in vivo confirmation before informing protocol changes.
