## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 핵심 명제: 전환율 (Degree of Conversion, DC)을 낮추는 원인은 재료 화학이 아니라 대부분 **광조사 기하학** — 큐어링 팁-표면 거리, 노출모드(exposure mode), 그리고 간접수복물의 광감쇠(light attenuation)다. 4편(직접 레진 2 + 간접수복 luting cement 2)을 하나의 "빛 전달 사슬"로 통합.
> - **거리 축 1 (Lehmann 2026)**: 복합레진(RBC) 3종 × 노출모드 3종 × 거리 3단계(0/2/4mm), 라만분광법. 상면 DC는 재료·거리가 유의(p<0.001, p=0.049), 하면 DC는 재료·노출모드가 유의(p<0.001, p=0.032). 벌크필(bulk-fill) 하면 DC <45%로 임상 권장 최소치 ~55% 미달.
> - **거리 축 2 (Hashemian 2025)**: 나노복합레진(Filtek Z350 XT)을 0/2/4/6/8mm에서 광중합, DC%(ATR-FTIR)와 치은섬유아세포 생존율(MTT) 동시 측정. 거리 증가 시 DC%·세포생존율 모두 유의 감소(44.57%→32.99%), **6mm 이상에서 ISO 70% 생체적합성 임계값 미만**. DC%-생존율 양의 상관(상면 R=0.597, 하면 R=0.490) — 광중합 부족→저DC→미반응 단량체 용출→세포독성의 기전 사슬을 정량화.
> - **거리 임상 함의**: 두 논문 모두 팁-표면 거리 증가가 DC 저하의 1차 변수. 근원심 인접면·깊은 와동에서 팁이 표면에 닿지 않으면 기계적 물성뿐 아니라 **생물학적 안전성**까지 저하 — "닿을 수 있으면 반드시 닿게" 원칙.
> - **간접수복 광감쇠 (Yalçinkaya 2026)**: hybrid ceramic(Cerasmart) 1.5/2.0/2.5mm 하에서 dual-cure resin cement·flowable composite·bulk-fill composite의 DC 비교(FTIR, n=7/군). Material×두께 상호작용 유의(p<0.001) — "최고 시멘트"가 두께에 따라 뒤바뀜: flowable은 ≤2.0mm 최고이나 2.5mm 최저로 급락(11.69%), **dual-cure resin cement는 전 두께 안정적이며 2.5mm에서 유일하게 최고(30.88%)**.
> - **화학 축 (Aguirre-Gil 2026)**: dual-cure 시멘트 2종(NX3 Nexus vs Variolink N) 물리화학 비교 — Variolink N 방사선불투과도(radiopacity) 유의 우위(2.00 vs 1.59mmAl, ytterbium 함유), NX3 Nexus pH 유의 우위(5.84 vs 5.32, 더 중성). 용해도(solubility)는 차이 없음, 둘 다 ISO 4049 기준 충족.
> - **왜 dual-cure가 두꺼운 간접수복에 안전판인가**: 화학적(자가) 중합 성분이 광 도달 부족을 보완 — Yalçinkaya의 "dual-cure 두께 안정성"이 그 실증. 단, Aguirre-Gil은 DC·기계적 물성을 측정하지 않아 dual-cure의 자가중합 성분이 "안전판"으로 작동하려면 **충분한 대기시간(자가중합 kinetics)**이 전제됨을 명심.
> - **임상 결정 체크리스트**: ① 큐어링 팁은 물리적으로 닿을 수 있는 한 최대한 근접 (거리 증가는 DC·세포생존율 동시 저하); ② 벌크필은 하면(바닥면) DC 리스크 인지 — 깊은 와동엔 sculptable capping layer 또는 dual-cure 대체 고려; ③ hybrid ceramic/지르코니아 등 간접수복 2.5mm 이상 두께엔 dual-cure resin cement 우선 선택(광 의존 단독 composite luting 지양); ④ 방사선 검출성이 중요한 부위(변연 잔사·임플란트 보철)엔 ytterbium계 고radiopacity 시멘트(Variolink N) 우선.
> - **확신도**: 4편 모두 in-vitro(전임상) — 임상 RCT 검증 없음. n이 작아(그룹당 3~7) 예비적(preliminary) 근거로 읽어야 함. Hashemian은 윤리승인서 소재불명을 저자 스스로 명시 — 근거 중량은 다소 낮게 잡을 것.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 4 in-vitro studies (2025–2026) on light-curing adequacy: curing-tip distance is the primary driver of degree of conversion (DC) loss in direct composites — at ≥6 mm, DC falls below ISO 70% cytocompatibility threshold for gingival fibroblast viability (Hashemian 2025); exposure mode determines bottom-surface DC, where bulk-fill drops below 45% even under standard mode (Lehmann 2026).

For indirect restorations, hybrid ceramic (CAD/CAM) thickness is the attenuating variable: dual-cure resin cement is the only material maintaining adequate DC at 2.5 mm (30.88%); flowable composite collapses to 11.69% at 2.5 mm; dual-cure cement selection also involves a pH vs radiopacity trade-off (NX3 Nexus pH 5.84 and less radiopaque vs Variolink N pH 5.32 and 2.00 mmAl radiopacity, Aguirre-Gil 2026).

Clinical rule: DC failure is a light-delivery-geometry problem, not a material-chemistry problem — minimize curing-tip distance in every increment, prefer dual-cure resin cement under thick indirect restorations (≥2.5 mm), and choose cement based on radiopacity needs; all four studies are in vitro (n=3–7/group, preliminary evidence only).

## 세줄요약

4편의 in-vitro 연구(2025–2026) 종합: 직접 복합레진에서 전환율(Degree of Conversion, DC) 저하의 1차 변수는 큐어링 팁-표면 거리 — 6mm 이상에서 ISO 70% 세포생존율 임계값 미달(Hashemian 2025); 노출 모드(exposure mode)는 하면 DC를 결정해 벌크필(bulk-fill)이 표준 모드에서도 45% 미만으로 떨어짐(Lehmann 2026).

간접수복에서는 하이브리드 세라믹(hybrid ceramic, CAD/CAM) 두께가 광감쇠(light attenuation) 변수: 2.5mm 두께에서 이중중합 레진 시멘트(dual-cure resin cement)만 적절한 DC 유지(30.88%); 플로어블 복합레진은 11.69%로 급락; 이중중합 시멘트 선택에는 pH(NX3 Nexus 5.84, 더 중성) vs 방사선불투과도(Variolink N 2.00mmAl, ytterbium, 유의 우위) 상충관계(Aguirre-Gil 2026).

임상 원칙: DC 저하는 재료 화학 문제가 아니라 광전달 기하학 문제 — 팁 거리 최소화·두꺼운 간접수복(≥2.5mm) 시 이중중합 시멘트 우선·방사선 검출 중요 부위엔 ytterbium계 고radiopacity 선택; 4편 모두 소규모 in-vitro(그룹당 n=3~7)이므로 예비근거로 해석.

## Summary

This overview synthesizes four in-vitro papers around a single physical chain: **light delivery → degree of conversion (DC) → downstream outcome** (mechanical adequacy, cytotoxicity, or luting-cement performance under indirect restorations). Two papers ([[resin/lehmann-2026-distance-exposure-mode-degree-conversion-rbc]], [[resin/hashemian-2025-tip-distance-conversion-cytotoxicity]]) isolate the curing-light-device side of the chain — tip-to-surface distance and LED exposure mode — in direct resin-based composites (RBCs). Two more ([[resin/yalcinkaya-2026-degree-conversion-luting-hybrid-ceramic]], [[resin/aguirre-gil-2026-ph-solubility-radiopacity-dual-cure]]) move the same physics into the indirect-restoration context, where a CAD/CAM substrate itself becomes the attenuating medium between the light source and the luting cement, and where dual-cure chemistry's self-cure reserve becomes clinically decisive.

The unifying thesis: **DC is not primarily a material problem — it is a light-delivery-geometry problem**, and where geometry cannot guarantee adequate irradiance (deep proximal boxes, thick CAD/CAM restorations), the material's *chemical* backup (dual-cure) becomes the safety margin. This is a genuinely different angle from the polymerization-shrinkage/monomer-chemistry discussion in [[overviews/direct-resin-restoration-adhesion-placement-overview]] §2.3 — that section is about stress reduction via resin-matrix modification; this page is about whether the resin polymerizes adequately at all, given real-world light-tip geometry.

## Part 1 — Curing-Light Distance and Exposure Mode in Direct Composites

### 1.1 Distance and exposure mode both matter, but at different depths

[[resin/lehmann-2026-distance-exposure-mode-degree-conversion-rbc]] ran a 27-group factorial design (3 RBCs × 3 exposure modes × 3 distances, n=3/group, 81 specimens) and measured DC by Raman spectroscopy at top and bottom surfaces separately. The key finding is a **depth-dependent dissociation** not previously characterized together:

| Surface | Significant factors | Non-significant |
|---|---|---|
| Top | material (p<0.001), **distance** (p=0.049) | exposure mode (p=0.391) |
| Bottom | material (p<0.001), **exposure mode** (p=0.032) | distance (p=0.068) |

- Flowable composite (G-aenial Universal Flo): best overall — top 74%, bottom >60%.
- Bulk-fill composite (Filtek One Bulk Fill): worst — bottom DC **<45%**, below the ~55% clinically suggested minimum for load-bearing occlusal restorations.
- Soft-start mode was the best top-surface performer (69%) and nearly tied standard mode at the bottom (56% vs 57%); pulse mode gave the lowest overall DC but may trade off against reduced pulp-overheating risk in deep, vital-pulp cavities.
- Increasing distance 0→4 mm still permitted adequate top-surface DC (~70% at 2 mm, ~60% at 4 mm) — the top surface is comparatively forgiving; the **bottom surface (deepest part of the increment) is where exposure-mode choice, not distance, becomes the binding constraint**.

### 1.2 Distance degrades DC *and* crosses a cytotoxicity threshold

[[resin/hashemian-2025-tip-distance-conversion-cytotoxicity]] took the distance question further by adding a biological safety endpoint. A single nanocomposite (Filtek Z350 XT) was cured at five tip distances (0/2/4/6/8 mm, n=4/group) and assessed for both DC% (ATR-FTIR) and human gingival fibroblast viability (MTT, ISO 10993).

- DC% fell from 44.57% (0 mm) to 32.99% (8 mm), with the decline reaching significance mainly at the bottom surface (G0 vs G6, G0 vs G8, p<0.05).
- Cell viability declined significantly with distance (all pairwise comparisons except G0 vs G2, p<0.001) and **crossed below the ISO 70% biocompatibility threshold at ≥6 mm** (65.5% at 6 mm, 58% at 8 mm).
- DC% and cell viability were positively correlated at both surfaces (top R=0.597, bottom R=0.490, both p<0.05) — supporting the causal chain **insufficient irradiance → incomplete polymerization → residual monomer release → cytotoxicity**.
- Mechanistically, at 8 mm measured irradiance fell to ~313 mW/cm², near the ~300 mW/cm² floor cited in the literature as the minimum for adequate cure — driven by the curing unit's 15.8° emission angle and inverse-square intensity falloff.

[확인 for the DC-viability correlation direction; 미검증 for the exact 70%-crossing distance, since this is a single composite/single LCU combination, n=4/group, and the authors themselves flag an undocumented ethics-approval record for the cell-culture work as a self-disclosed limitation].

### 1.3 Reading the two direct-composite papers together

Both studies agree that **distance is a real, modifiable, clinically controllable variable** — not a minor technical nuance. Lehmann 2026 shows distance is significant specifically at the surface closest to the light source (top); Hashemian 2025 shows the cumulative effect of distance (measured 0–8 mm, a wider range than Lehmann's 0–4 mm) degrades DC enough to become a biocompatibility problem, not just a mechanical-property problem. Read together:

- **0–4 mm** (Lehmann's range): DC stays in a clinically workable zone at the top surface regardless of exposure mode; the bottom surface is the vulnerable zone, and exposure-mode choice (soft-start ≈ standard > pulse) matters more there than distance itself.
- **≥6 mm** (Hashemian's extended range): a genuinely different regime — cell viability crosses below the ISO safety threshold. This is the clinically relevant scenario for deep proximal boxes or Class II cavities where the curing tip cannot physically reach the cavity floor.
- Neither paper measured mechanical properties (microhardness, flexural strength) alongside DC — both flag this as a limitation and a direction for future work, so the DC→mechanical-adequacy link is inferred from prior literature (~55% clinical minimum), not directly tested here.

[미검증] The practical reading: a curing tip that cannot get within a few millimeters of the composite surface is not just a "slightly weaker cure" — past ~6 mm it becomes a biological safety issue, per this evidence. In deep proximal boxes, a wedge/matrix system and cavity access design that lets the tip approach the cavity floor is not a cosmetic technique preference; it is directly load-bearing on both mechanical DC-adequacy and cytotoxicity risk.

## Part 2 — Indirect Restorations Compound the Same Attenuation Problem

### 2.1 Hybrid ceramic thickness reproduces the distance problem for luting cement

[[resin/yalcinkaya-2026-degree-conversion-luting-hybrid-ceramic]] moves the same physics into the indirect-restoration context: instead of curing-tip distance, the attenuating medium is the CAD/CAM restoration itself. Three light-curable luting materials from one manufacturer line (dual-cure resin cement Estecem II Plus, flowable composite Estelite Universal Flow, bulk-fill composite Estelite Bulk-Fill Flow) were polymerized beneath hybrid ceramic (Cerasmart) substrates of 1.5/2.0/2.5 mm thickness (n=7/group, 63 specimens, FTIR).

The critical finding is a **significant material × thickness interaction (p<0.001)** — there is no single best luting material; the ranking flips with thickness:

| Thickness | Best DC | Worst DC | Notable |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 mm | Flowable composite (36.57±1.8%) | Bulk-fill (23.34±3.6%) | Flowable sig. > both others |
| 2.0 mm | Flowable composite (30.18±1.9%), ≈ dual-cure | Bulk-fill (22.69±2.9%) | Flowable ≈ dual-cure, NS |
| 2.5 mm | **Dual-cure cement (30.88±2.5%)** | **Flowable composite (11.69±2.2%)** | Flowable DC collapses |

Dual-cure cement and bulk-fill composite each showed **no significant DC difference across thicknesses** (thickness-stable), while flowable composite's DC was highly thickness-dependent — winning thin, failing thick. The optical-property data explain the mechanism: translucency (RTP_ab) fell from 11.21 (1.5 mm) to 6.06 (2.5 mm) and contrast ratio rose from 0.63 to 0.81 — the ceramic becomes progressively more light-attenuating with thickness, and only the cement with a chemical-cure reserve tolerates that.

[확인, large effect size f=2.75, power ≈1.000 — but single ceramic/shade (Cerasmart A2 HT), single LCU protocol, DC at 24 h only, no aging or bond-strength correlation].

### 2.2 Physicochemical trade-offs among dual-cure cements themselves

[[resin/aguirre-gil-2026-ph-solubility-radiopacity-dual-cure]] complements the DC picture with a property axis Yalçinkaya did not measure: pH, solubility, and radiopacity, comparing two commonly used dual-cure resin cements (NX3 Nexus vs Variolink N, n=15/cement).

- **Radiopacity**: Variolink N significantly higher (2.00 vs 1.59 mmAl, p=0.026), attributed to its ytterbium trifluoride filler (Yb, Z=70) vs NX3 Nexus's barium-glass fillers (Ba, Z=56) — higher atomic number attenuates X-rays more.
- **pH**: NX3 Nexus significantly higher/more neutral (5.84 vs 5.32, p=0.008); both remain above the pH=3 threshold associated with pulpal inflammation/postoperative sensitivity.
- **Solubility**: no significant difference (p=0.841); both cements within ISO 4049:2019 thresholds.

This paper did **not** measure DC or mechanical properties — it is a pure physicochemical characterization, not a polymerization-adequacy study. Its relevance to Part 1's "dual-cure as a safety margin under thick restorations" thesis is indirect: it establishes that *which* dual-cure cement is chosen still carries clinically meaningful trade-offs (radiographic detectability of marginal excess vs pulpal-pH safety margin) independent of the thickness-related DC advantage Yalçinkaya demonstrated. [확인 — properties measured are chemistry-adjacent to DC, not DC itself].

### 2.3 Why dual-cure is the thickness safety valve — and its limits

Put together, Yalçinkaya 2026 and Aguirre-Gil 2026 support a two-part clinical inference:

1. **Dual-cure resin cement's chemical (self-)cure component compensates for attenuated light beneath thick indirect restorations** — this is directly demonstrated by its thickness-stable DC (Yalçinkaya), in contrast to flowable/bulk-fill composites that depend entirely on light penetration and fail once the restoration exceeds ~2.0 mm.
2. **This safety valve is not free of trade-offs at the chemistry level** — pH and radiopacity differ meaningfully between dual-cure products (Aguirre-Gil), so "use dual-cure cement" is necessary but not sufficient; product-level selection still matters for pulpal-safety margin and radiographic marginal-excess detection.

[미검증] Neither paper tested whether the self-cure reaction *actually completes* under clinically realistic working times — dual-cure's chemical-cure kinetics typically need minutes, not seconds, to reach useful conversion, and neither study reports a delayed (non-immediate) DC measurement that would confirm the chemical-cure component compensates in practice rather than only in principle. This is a genuine evidence gap (see below), not a settled point.

## Part 3 — Integrated Clinical Decision Checklist

### 3.1 Direct composite restorations

1. **Minimize curing-tip-to-surface distance** whenever cavity access allows — every mm of avoidable distance protects both DC and (per Hashemian 2025) biocompatibility. Treat distance as a first-order variable, not a minor technique nuance.
2. **Deep proximal boxes / Class II cavities where the tip cannot reach the floor** (≥6 mm equivalent): recognize this is a biological-safety scenario, not just a mechanical one — consider a sectional matrix/wedge system optimized for tip access, or a flowable liner placed and cured incrementally to shorten the effective light path at each step.
3. **Bulk-fill composite**: bottom-surface DC is the vulnerable zone (both papers). Respect manufacturer-specified maximum increment depth; do not assume "bulk-fill" means "distance-tolerant" — Lehmann 2026 found it had the *lowest* DC of the three materials tested, not the most distance-robust.
4. **Exposure mode**: standard and soft-start modes are essentially interchangeable at the bottom surface (57% vs 56%); soft-start is marginally better at the top. Pulse mode gives the lowest overall DC — reserve for scenarios specifically prioritizing reduced pulp-overheating risk (deep cavity, vital pulp) over maximal conversion, and cure longer/closer to compensate if used.
5. **Flowable composite**: best DC performer in direct-composite testing (Lehmann 2026) — a reasonable choice as a base/liner layer in deep cavities precisely because it tolerates the polymerization-adequacy question better than bulk-fill.

### 3.2 Indirect restorations (hybrid ceramic, CAD/CAM luting)

1. **Restoration thickness ≤2.0 mm**: flowable (or bulk-fill) light-cure composite luting is adequate and may give the highest DC.
2. **Restoration thickness approaching or exceeding 2.5 mm**: prefer **dual-cure resin cement** — it is the only luting material tested whose DC does not collapse with thickness, and it is the top DC performer specifically at 2.5 mm.
3. **Do not assume a single "best" luting material** — the material × thickness interaction is statistically significant and the ranking inverts; luting-material choice should be thickness-informed, not a fixed default.
4. **Among dual-cure cement options**, weigh radiopacity vs pH: choose a higher-radiopacity (ytterbium-containing) cement when radiographic detectability of marginal excess or defect matters (subgingival margins, implant-supported restorations); either cement tested remains within safe pH/solubility bounds otherwise.
5. **Allow adequate working/setting time** for the self-cure component of dual-cure cement to progress before assuming full conversion under thick restorations — this compensatory mechanism is chemically plausible and consistent with the thickness-stability data, but was not directly time-course–verified in either paper (see Evidence Gaps).

## Evidence Gaps (open questions in our wiki)

- **No delayed/time-course DC measurement for dual-cure cement under thick restorations** — Yalçinkaya 2026 measured DC at a single (24 h) timepoint; whether the self-cure component's compensatory effect is already captured at 24 h or would improve further is unaddressed.
- **No mechanical-property correlation with DC** in either direct-composite paper (Lehmann 2026, Hashemian 2025) — the ~55% "clinically suggested minimum" DC threshold is drawn from prior literature, not directly validated against microhardness/flexural strength in these specific datasets.
- **No cytotoxicity data for luting cements under indirect restorations** — Hashemian 2025's DC-cytotoxicity link is established only for a direct-composite/tip-distance scenario; whether under-cured luting cement beneath a thick ceramic restoration produces a comparable cytotoxic signal is untested in this wiki.
- **Single LCU, single composite/cement brand per study** — none of the four papers cross-validates findings across multiple curing-light models or product lines; generalizability beyond the tested combinations is unverified.
- **Clinical (in-vivo/RCT) confirmation absent** — all four papers are in-vitro; the distance/thickness thresholds identified (6 mm cytotoxicity crossing, 2.5 mm dual-cure advantage) are laboratory findings pending clinical validation.

When a clinical decision falls into one of these gaps, the wiki cannot answer — say so and bring the PDF.
