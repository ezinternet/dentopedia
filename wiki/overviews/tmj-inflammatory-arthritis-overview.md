> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **다루는 것**: 자가면역·염증성 관절염이 턱관절(Temporomandibular Joint, TMJ)을 침범한 경우 — 소아특발성관절염 (Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis, JIA)·류마티스관절염 (Rheumatoid Arthritis, RA)·건선관절염 (Psoriatic Arthritis, PsA). 위키의 TMJ 오버뷰 8개는 근막통·스플린트·관절강세척·퇴행성 골관절염 (Osteoarthritis, OA) 축을 덮지만 **염증성 축은 없었다** — 기전·진단·치료가 모두 다른 별개 질환군이다.
> - **핵심 테제 — 임상의가 평소 이어 쓰는 세 고리가 여기서는 각각 끊어져 있다**: ①증상→질환 ②영상→질환 ③전신 활성도→관절, 그리고 ④치료반응→구조. 넷 다 어긋나므로 **증상으로 선별할 수도, 초음파로 확진할 수도, 혈액지표로 추적할 수도, 영상으로 치료성공을 확인할 수도 없다.**
> - **고리 ① 증상이 질환을 드러내지 않는다**: JIA의 TMJ 침범 유병률은 진단법에 따라 **17%~90% 이상**으로 요동하고, 최대개구량 (Maximum Mouth Opening, MMO)은 TMJ 관절염이 **있는 JIA와 없는 JIA를 구분하지 못한다**(Stoll·Koss). 과두 방사선 손상의 심도와 증상 유무·강도 사이에 직접 연관이 없어 — **심한 변형인데 무증상, 경미한 변화인데 유증상**이 공존한다. 지표 증례: 9세 여아, TMJ 증상 전무, CBCT Billiau 3/4 손상.
> - **고리 ② 영상이 안정적으로 잡아내지 못한다 — 그리고 불안정한 방식이 질환마다 뒤집힌다**: JIA에서 초음파(US)는 과두 변화에 강하고 삼출에 약한데(과두 85%/80%, 삼출 **민감도 23%**/89%), RA에서는 정반대로 삼출에 강하고 구조에 약하다(삼출 **92.4%**/90.3%, 디스크 72%, 과두 표면 53%). **US로 무엇을 볼 수 있는가에 안정된 답이 없다.**
> - **⚠️ 0이 두 번 나온다**: Weiss에서 US는 급성 관절염을 **0%** 검출(MRI는 같은 환자군의 75%에서 검출, κ=0.00), Zwir의 파워도플러 활막 염증 **민감도 0%**(PPV 0%). 즉 **음성 초음파는 아무것도 배제하지 못한다** — 애초에 무증상이 흔한 질환에서 특히 위험한 성질.
> - **고리 ③ 전신 활성도가 관절을 대변하지 않는다**: US 소견과 DAS28·HAQ·통증의 연관은 연구에 따라 있다 없다 하고(El-Melegy는 유의, 전체적으로 비일관), Kroese는 치료반응이 ESR·CRP·세로토닌·IL-1β **어느 것과도 상관없음**을 보고. 무증상자에서 US 구조변화가 발견되기도 한다.
> - **고리 ④ 치료가 들어도 구조는 그대로**: Elshoura에서 덱스트로스 신경주위 주사 후 통증은 유의 개선(안정시 5→3, 최대개구시 7→5, 둘 다 p<0.001)했으나 **삼출은 57.1%→42.8%(p=0.57), 개구량·초음파 소견 불변**. 증상 호전을 구조 호전으로 읽으면 안 된다.
> - **그럼 무엇으로 선별하나 — 형태 신호가 유일하게 남는다**: 안면비대칭(23~41%)·개구 시 하악 편위(최대 62%, >2mm가 46.15%)·MMO<40mm가 제안된 표지이며, **편위 방향은 손상이 더 심한 쪽과 일치**한다. 소아 MRI는 진정이 필요해 접근성이 떨어지므로 US는 **대체가 아니라 triage**.
> - **치료 근거는 사실상 비어 있다**: 479편→**5편**(RA 4·SLE 1·혼합 1이 실은 코호트 중복 계상), 개입은 관절강내 스테로이드(3/5)·덱스트로스 주사(1)·저출력레이저 (Low-Level Laser Therapy, LLLT)(1), **대조군을 둔 연구는 2/5뿐, RCT는 1편**. 스테로이드 효과는 **T2(6.3주)에 소실**(Kroese). 전신치료(메토트렉세이트·생물학제제) 위에 얹는 **국소 보조층**일 뿐인데, 그 상호작용을 분석한 연구는 없다.
> - **수술 종착점 — 숫자는 크지만 전부 대조군이 없다**: 전치환술 16편/760명 중 퇴행성·염증성 579명(OA>골관절증>JIA>RA), 개구 24.32±5.8 → **40.74±3.1mm**, 통증 소실, 기계적 실패 4.07%. 단 **GRADE 16편 전부 low**, 무작위·대조·맹검 0. **⚠️ 그리고 619명 중 210명(33.92%)이 사전 치료 없이 곧장 인공관절로 갔다** — "언제 수술할지 정할 근거가 없다"는 저자 결론과 겹쳐 읽어야 할 수치.
> - **기전은 질환마다 다르고, 그게 영상 표적과 연결돼야 하는데 아무도 검증하지 않았다**: PsA는 부착부염(enthesitis)·IL-17/22/23 주도로 **변연부 미란 + 증식성 골변화**가 공존, RA는 활막 주도, OA는 기계적 부하. Bernardi는 감별에 **미란 위치 규칙**(OA는 중심부만, PsA는 중심·변연 모두)을 제시한다. JIA/RA에서 US 최적 표적이 뒤집힌 것도 이 기전 차이로 설명될 수 있으나 — **질환별 US 표적 성능을 비교한 연구는 없다**(가설).
> - **원장 실무 요지**: ①JIA 아동은 증상이 없어도 얼굴 대칭·개구로 편위·MMO를 본다(과두는 성장중심이라 증상이 날 땐 이미 늦다) ②음성 US로 안심시키지 말 것 ③건선·RA 환자의 새 TMJ 증상은 스플린트가 아니라 **류마티스내과 협진 대화** ④국소 주사는 수주 단위 완화이지 질환조절이 아님 ⑤PsA 항TNF는 **턱뼈 골괴사** 보고가 있어 침습 처치 전 확인.

## Three-line Summary

Six-paper synthesis on autoimmune/inflammatory arthritis of the temporomandibular joint (JIA, RA, PsA) — an axis none of the wiki's eight existing TMD/TMJ overviews covers, because its biology, diagnostics, and treatment ladder differ from mechanical TMD and from degenerative osteoarthritis.

The unifying finding is a set of broken links: symptoms do not reveal the disease (JIA TMJ involvement is frequently silent; MMO does not discriminate JIA patients with versus without TMJ arthritis; radiographic damage severity and symptom severity are unassociated), ultrasound does not reliably find it and fails inconsistently — its best and worst targets invert between JIA (condylar 85% sens, effusion 23%) and RA (effusion 92.4%, condylar surface 53%), with two independent zero-sensitivity results (Weiss κ=0.00 for acute arthritis, Zwir 0% for power-Doppler synovitis) — systemic disease-activity indices track joint findings only inconsistently, and symptomatic improvement after local injection leaves structural findings unchanged (Elshoura: VAS p<0.001, effusion 57.1%→42.8% p=0.57).

What survives clinically is morphological screening (facial asymmetry 23–41%, mandibular deviation up to 62%, MMO <40 mm) plus MRI as reference standard where accessible; treatment evidence is nearly empty (479 records → 5 heterogeneous studies, 2 with any control group, 1 RCT, steroid benefit lost by ~6 weeks), and total joint replacement reports large uncontrolled gains (MMO 24.3→40.7 mm) with GRADE low across all 16 studies, no timing evidence, and 33.92% of patients reaching a prosthesis without documented prior treatment.

## 세줄요약

턱관절의 자가면역·염증성 관절염(JIA·RA·PsA) 6편 종합 — 위키의 기존 TMD/TMJ 오버뷰 8개가 덮지 않는 축이다(기전·진단·치료 사다리가 기계적 TMD·퇴행성 OA와 다르다).

관통하는 소견은 **끊어진 고리들**이다: 증상이 질환을 드러내지 않고(JIA 침범은 흔히 무증상, MMO는 TMJ 관절염 유무를 구분 못 함, 방사선 손상 심도와 증상이 무관), 초음파는 안정적으로 잡지 못하며 **실패 방식이 질환마다 뒤집힌다**(JIA는 과두 85%·삼출 23%, RA는 삼출 92.4%·과두 53%; 독립적인 민감도 0% 결과가 둘 — Weiss κ=0.00, Zwir 0%), 전신 활성도 지표는 관절 소견과 비일관하게만 연관되고, 국소 주사로 통증이 좋아져도 구조 소견은 그대로다(Elshoura: 통증 p<0.001인데 삼출 57.1%→42.8% p=0.57).

임상에 남는 건 형태 선별(안면비대칭 23~41%, 하악 편위 최대 62%, MMO<40mm)과 접근 가능할 때의 MRI다. 치료 근거는 거의 비어 있고(479편→5편, 대조군 2편, RCT 1편, 스테로이드 효과는 ~6주에 소실), 전치환술은 큰 전후 개선(개구 24.3→40.7mm)을 보고하나 **16편 전부 GRADE low·대조군 없음·수술 시점 근거 없음**이며 **33.92%가 사전 치료 기록 없이 인공관절에 도달**했다.

## Thesis

A clinician reasoning about a joint normally chains four things: the patient's symptoms tell you to look, imaging tells you what is there, systemic markers tell you how active the disease is, and follow-up imaging tells you whether treatment worked. **In inflammatory arthritis of the TMJ, all four links are broken — and they break in different directions, which is why this disease is missed.**

**Link 1 — symptoms do not reveal the disease.** Reported TMJ involvement in JIA ranges from 17% to over 90% depending purely on how you look for it. Maximum mouth opening, the obvious bedside measure, **does not discriminate** JIA patients with TMJ arthritis from those without (Stoll, Koss, as cited in [[tmj/pawlaczyk-kamienska-2026-facial-asymmetry-mandibular-hypomobility-jia-tmj]]). Most decisively, that review reports no direct association between the severity of condylar radiographic damage and the presence or severity of symptoms: some children with major structural deformity are asymptomatic, others with mild change are symptomatic. Its index case makes the point concretely — a 9-year-old with five years of polyarticular JIA and **no reported TMJ symptoms**, who on examination had facial asymmetry, 5 mm mandibular deviation, MMO 30 mm (normal ≥35 under age 10), and CBCT scoring **3 of 4 on Billiau's scale** for the affected condyle against 0 for the other side. The condyle is a growth centre; by the time it hurts, growth has already been altered.

**Link 2 — imaging does not reliably find it, and the unreliability is not stable.** Ultrasound is the modality under active study because MRI in children requires sedation and is often inaccessible. But its performance is target-dependent in a way that has no settled answer. In JIA ([[tmj/chana-2026-ultrasound-tmj-juvenile-idiopathic-arthritis-sr]], 8 studies, 312 patients, MRI reference): condylar/composite targets reach 85% sensitivity (Farronato) while joint effusion falls to **23%** (Müller, whose authors explicitly advise against US for early screening). In RA ([[tmj/tenorio-2026-ultrasonography-tmj-rheumatoid-arthritis-scoping-review]], 5 studies, 154 patients): the single diagnostic-accuracy study reaches **92.4% sensitivity for effusion** but drops to 72% for disc displacement and **53% for condylar surface**. **The best and worst targets invert between the two diseases.** Whether that reflects real biology — a child's cartilaginous, small joint versus an adult's — or simply reflects different studies, machines (6.7–18 MHz), and operators, no study has tested. It is a hypothesis, and it is the reason no single "ultrasound sensitivity for TMJ arthritis" figure should ever be quoted.

Two results deserve to be read as hard limits rather than low numbers. Weiss found US agreement with MRI of **κ=0.00 for acute arthritis** — MRI found active disease in 75% of patients, US in **0%**. Zwir's power-Doppler assessment of synovial inflammation returned **0% sensitivity with 0% PPV** against MRI-positive inflammation in 64.7% of joints. Combined with GRADE certainty rated **low for sensitivity** across the JIA review, the operational conclusion is blunt: **a negative ultrasound excludes nothing.** In a disease that is frequently silent, a falsely reassuring scan is the specific harm to guard against.

**Link 3 — systemic disease activity does not track the joint.** Associations between US findings and DAS28, HAQ, or pain were significant in some analyses (El-Melegy 2017) and absent in others; Tenório reports them as inconsistent overall, and notes US-detected structural change in clinically asymptomatic patients. On the treatment side, Kroese found response to intra-articular steroid **uncorrelated with ESR, CRP, serotonin, or IL-1β**. The TMJ is not a reliable readout of the systemic disease, and the systemic disease is not a reliable readout of the TMJ.

**Link 4 — and treatment response does not track structure.** Elshoura's perineural dextrose series improved pain convincingly (VAS at rest median 5→3, at maximum opening 7→5, both p<0.001) while **joint effusion moved 57.1%→42.8% (p=0.57) and mouth opening and ultrasound findings were unchanged**. Symptomatic success and structural success are separate outcomes here, and the literature routinely reports the first.

**What follows for practice is not paralysis but a change of instrument.** Since symptoms fail as a trigger and ultrasound fails as a rule-out, the screening burden falls on **objective morphological signs**, which the JIA literature reports far more consistently: facial asymmetry in 23–41%, mandibular deviation on opening in up to 62% (>2 mm in 46.15% in the authors' own prior cohort, deviating toward the more damaged side), reduced MMO in 32–58%, and a proposed marker of **MMO < 40 mm**. These are cheap, need no machine, and are exactly what a dentist is already positioned to observe in a child seen for anything else.

**The treatment layer is close to empty, and its papers know it.** [[tmj/aiello-2025-tmj-arthritis-rheumatic-diseases-rehabilitative-pain-relief-sr]] screened 479 records and included **5** (1996–2021, n=11–91): intra-articular corticosteroid in three, perineural dextrose in one, LLLT in one. Only two had any control group; only one was an RCT — and that RCT studied systemic lupus, not RA. Effects, where present, are short: Kroese's steroid injections improved pain from T0 to T1 (median VAS 3→2, p<0.01) with **benefit lost by T2 (~6.3 weeks)**. Every cohort remained on systemic therapy (methotrexate, leflunomide, hydroxychloroquine, etanercept) throughout, and **no study analysed the interaction between that systemic therapy and the local intervention** — which matters, because the local layer is an adjunct to rheumatological disease control, not a substitute for it. The authors' verdict — heterogeneity and low quality preclude firm conclusions — is the honest one and should not be softened into an endorsement of steroid injection or LLLT.

**At the far end sits total joint replacement, with impressive numbers and no comparator.** [[tmj/ravelo-2025-tmj-replacement-degenerative-disease-sr]] pooled 16 studies and 760 patients (1,231 prostheses), of whom **579 (76.18%) had degenerative or inflammatory disease** — osteoarthritis most frequent, then osteoarthrosis, **JIA, and RA**, which is why this surgical endpoint belongs to the inflammatory chain and not only to the degenerative one. Maximum mouth opening rose from 24.32 ± 5.8 mm to **40.74 ± 3.1 mm**, all participants reported reduced or absent pain and progressed to a solid diet, and prosthesis replacement for system failure was **4.07%**. But ROBINS-I rated 3 of 16 studies at moderate and 2 at high risk of bias, **GRADE rated all 16 low certainty**, and not one had a control arm — so the ~16 mm gain cannot be attributed to the prosthesis with the confidence the number invites.

Two further details in that review deserve to be uncomfortable. The authors state plainly that they **could not identify the best timing** for replacement — not by time since diagnosis, not by time since starting non-surgical treatment, not by number of previous surgeries. And of 619 patients whose prior treatment was reported, **210 (33.92%) had no prior treatment at all** before receiving a prosthesis. A field that cannot say when to operate is nonetheless operating on a third of its patients without documented conservative care first. Whether those were appropriate primary indications (end-stage destruction, ankylosis) or premature escalation is not recoverable from the data — and that irrecoverability is itself the finding.

**Finally, the three diseases are not one disease, and the difference should drive diagnosis.** [[tmj/bernardi-2024-psoriatic-arthritis-tmj-pathogenesis-gender-differences]] describes PsA as enthesitis- and cytokine-driven (IL-17, IL-22, IL-23), producing the unusual combination of **erosive and proliferative bone change simultaneously** — IL-22 upregulating osteogenic factors (Wnt-3a, BMP4) alongside osteoclastic erosion. RA is synovium-driven; osteoarthritis is mechanical. The review offers a usable differentiator: **erosion location — central only in OA, central or marginal in PsA** — plus disease-course speed (RA faster). If the mechanisms genuinely differ this way, then the optimal imaging target should differ by disease too, which is precisely the pattern the JIA/RA ultrasound inversion hints at. Nobody has tested it. Note also a treatment-relevant hazard the same review flags: **anti-TNF therapy carries reported osteonecrosis of the jaw** — a specific concern before any invasive TMJ procedure in a PsA patient.

## Clinical Decision Points

1. **In any child with JIA, screen the face and the opening path — not the symptom history.** Symptoms and even MMO alone are unreliable; facial asymmetry, mandibular deviation on opening (note the direction — it points to the more damaged side), and MMO < 40 mm are the signs the literature reports consistently. This is the one place a general dentist has a decisive advantage: the rheumatologist is examining knees and hands.
2. **Never let a negative ultrasound close the question.** US sensitivity for active/acute inflammation reached 0% in two independent studies. Use it as triage — it can raise suspicion, it cannot lower it. **MRI remains the reference standard** for active synovitis; if it is unavailable or requires sedation the child cannot have, the correct fallback is clinical follow-up and referral, not a reassuring scan.
3. **Ask what target you are imaging before you trust a number.** Ultrasound performs differently for effusion, synovial change, disc position, and condylar bone — and the ranking of those targets is not the same in children and adults. A quoted sensitivity without its target, its reference standard, and its operator is uninterpretable.
4. **Do not read symptomatic improvement as structural improvement.** Pain fell significantly after perineural dextrose while effusion, mouth opening, and ultrasound findings did not move. If the goal is protecting the joint (especially a growing condyle), pain scores are not the endpoint that tells you whether you achieved it.
5. **Position local treatment as an adjunct, on a short clock.** Intra-articular steroid, perineural dextrose, and LLLT all have some supportive signal and none has durable evidence; the best-documented steroid effect was gone by about six weeks. Systemic control by the rheumatologist is the foundation; the interaction between the two layers has never been studied, which is itself a reason for coordination rather than unilateral local treatment.
6. **A new TMJ complaint in a patient with psoriasis, RA, or a child with JIA is a referral conversation, not a splint.** And before any invasive TMJ procedure in a PsA patient on anti-TNF therapy, note the reported association with **osteonecrosis of the jaw**.
7. **Discuss total joint replacement with its evidence tier attached.** Roughly 16 mm of opening and near-universal pain relief are real reported gains — from uncontrolled before-after series rated low certainty throughout. There is **no evidence on when to operate**, and a third of patients in the pooled cohort reached a prosthesis with no documented prior treatment. That is an argument for a documented conservative trial and multidisciplinary review before escalation, not against surgery in end-stage disease.

## Gaps & Future Research

- **No study compares ultrasound target performance across diseases.** The JIA/RA inversion (condylar-strong in children, effusion-strong in adults) is the most interesting observation in this synthesis and is entirely unexamined — it may be biology (joint size, cartilaginous condyle, disease mechanism) or it may be machines and operators. A single study imaging both populations with one protocol and one reader would settle it.
- **No standardized TMJ ultrasound protocol exists.** Transducer frequency spanned 6.7–18 MHz, with probe position, patient position, static-versus-dynamic acquisition, and examiner background all varying across every included study. Until that is fixed, pooled accuracy estimates are not meaningful and the modality cannot be evaluated as a modality.
- **Treatment evidence is a near-vacuum**: 5 studies across six-plus diseases and four interventions, two with controls, one RCT (in the wrong disease). A single adequately powered RCT of intra-articular corticosteroid in RA-TMJ with both pain and imaging endpoints would more than double the usable evidence in this area.
- **The local/systemic interaction is unstudied.** Every patient in the treatment review was on systemic therapy, and no analysis asked whether local injection adds anything on top of adequate systemic control, or whether it merely tracks it. That is the actual clinical question and nobody has posed it.
- **No evidence on when to replace the joint** — not by disease duration, prior conservative treatment, or prior surgeries. Coupled with 33.92% receiving a prosthesis without documented prior treatment, the escalation threshold in this field is undefined and unaudited.
- **PsA-TMJ has no epidemiology and no sex-stratified data.** The prevalence of TMJ involvement in psoriatic arthritis is, in the reviewing authors' own words, hard to find; the sex-difference claims in that literature are extrapolated from general PsA patterns and general TMD hormone biology, with no PsA-TMJ-specific dataset anywhere.
- **Nobody has linked TMJ screening to outcomes.** The case for examining asymptomatic JIA children rests on the plausible mechanism that early detection protects condylar growth. No study has shown that a screening programme changes mandibular growth, function, or the eventual need for surgery — the trial that would justify the recommendation this page makes.
