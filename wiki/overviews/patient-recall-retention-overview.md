## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 명제**: 구환 리콜은 단일 문제가 아니라 **3층 시스템**이다 — ① 누구를 언제 부를지(위험도 기반 주기) ② 어떻게 실제로 오게 할지(미내원 방어) ③ 왜 다시 오는지(동기·관계). 위키가 보유한 근거의 강도는 **② > ① > ③** 순으로 급격히 떨어지며, 대부분의 개원가는 근거가 가장 약한 ③에 노력을 쏟고 근거가 가장 강한 ②를 자동화하지 않는다.
> - **①의 핵심 반전**: "6개월 정기 스케일링"은 저위험 건강 성인에서 **치주 근거가 없다** — 치은염 표준화평균차 (Standardized Mean Difference, SMD) −0.01/−0.04, **고신뢰도로 차이 없음**; 6개월 vs 12개월도 SMD 0.03으로 무차이 (Lamont 2018 Cochrane, RCT 2편·n=1,711). 치석만 소폭 감소(SMD −0.32).
> - **모순이 아니라 인구가 다르다**: 같은 위키가 3~6개월을 지지하는 근거도 갖고 있다 — 단 그건 **치료 완료 치주염 환자**다 (Farooqi 2015, 코호트 8편·**RCT 0편**; 12개월에 근접하면 치아상실 유의 증가). Lamont(저위험 건강인) ↔ Farooqi(치료된 치주염)는 **다른 질문에 답한 것**이며, 이 구분이 이 페이지의 하중 지지 축이다.
> - **순응의 값어치**: 지지치주치료 (Supportive Periodontal Therapy, SPT) 순응 환자는 5~15년간 평균 임상부착수준 (Clinical Attachment Level, CAL) 손실 ≤0.5 mm로 안정(14연구 중 10; Jung 2019, 964명). 진행 예측인자는 구강위생 불량·흡연·불순응 — 전부 수정 가능 인자.
> - **주기는 계산하는 것**: 잔존 탐침깊이 (Probing Depth, PD) 분포 + 탐침시출혈 (Bleeding on Probing, BoP) % + 흡연 + 당뇨로 **3~12개월** 개별 산출 (Ramseier 2024, 883명·11,842회 방문). 임플란트 고위험군은 흔히 3~4개월 (Mojaver 2025), 고정성 full-arch는 6개월 전문 구강위생 + **연 1회 이상 보철 제거** (Lanzetti 2024, 11편 중 저비뚤림 0편).
> - **자가관리 미숙 구간은 1개월**: 주 1회 전문가 치면세정술 (Professional Mechanical Tooth Cleaning, PMTC) 4회 후 치면세균막관리점수 38.98→57.57·치은염지수 1.55→0.85(p<0.001)였으나 **3개월에 재악화**(46.33/1.15); 환자 54.5%도 1개월을 선택 (Cho 2018, n=33, 부산).
> - **②는 근거가 가장 단단하다**: 리마인더 자체가 효과 — 단문메시지 (Short Message Service, SMS) vs 무(無) = 정시 내원 **79.2% vs 35.5%** (Prasad 2012). 그러나 **채널이 효과를 가른다** — 음성 전화가 SMS보다 우월(노쇼 8.2% vs 17.7%, 보정 교차비 (Odds Ratio, OR) 2.12; Nelson 2011 RCT, **단 소아 세팅**). 환자 선호도 전화 48.3% > 예약카드 25.9% > SMS 23.8% > 이메일 2% (Bhatia 2018).
> - **누구에게 전화할지는 감이 아니라 변수**: 예약~진료 간격(리드타임)이 단일 최강 예측인자(제거 시 곡선하면적 (Area Under the Curve, AUC) 7.5~7.9% 하락), 과거 미내원 이력이 2위이며 **평균이 아닌 이진 시퀀스(1,0,0,1,0)로 인코딩**할 때 AUC 2~5% 개선 (Alabdulkarim 2022, 196,018건·AUC 0.718).
> - **비용은 주동인이 아니다**: 보험 85% 보유에도 소아 미내원 44%(Khries 2024), 상-중류층이 오히려 최고 노쇼율 58.8%(Bhatia) — 할인·가격 인하로 리콜을 푸는 접근은 근거가 약하다. 진짜 지렛대는 슬롯 설계(고위험은 오버부킹 대신 **슬롯 단축**, 저녁·워크인 개설; Alkhurayji 2024)와 **직원 의사소통 오류**(행정 장벽 1위 10.3%, Khries).
> - **③은 정직하게 비어 있다**: 통증 시에만 오는 환자를 정기 내원으로 전환시키는 중재는 8개 DB를 뒤져 **단 3편**, 전부 재현 불가 수준 보고 (Currie 2021) — 검증된 레시피가 없다는 것이 현재의 답이다. 동기면담 (Motivational Interviewing, MI)은 일반 의료에서 OR 1.55(1.40–1.71)로 짧은 상담에서도 작동하나(Lundahl 2013, 48 RCT), 성인 치주 결과는 BoP −6.41%p만 유의해 결론 보류 (Cartes-Velasquez 2024). 행동중재에서 가장 유망한 묶음은 **목표설정+계획+자기점검+피드백** (Vilar Doceda 2023, 21편).
> - **순응은 결과이자 원인**: SPT 방문의 50% 이상을 예정보다 **일찍** 온 환자는 20년간 치아 0.60개 상실 vs 늦게 온 환자 1.45개(p<0.0001; Ramseier 2024) — 리콜 순응 자체가 측정 가능한 예후 인자다.
> - **원격 리콜 기대치**: 원격치과 (Teledentistry)는 구강건강 결과 신호가 나쁘지 않으나 체계적 고찰 10편 **전부 AMSTAR-2 low/critically low**, 경제성 원논문 5편뿐, 비용편익·비용효용 분석 전무 — **"비용 절감"은 findings가 아니라 assumption** (Scheerman 2024). 보조 채널로 시험은 가능하되 도입 근거로 쓰면 안 된다.
> - **임상 takeaway**: 구환을 Tier로 층화(3~4개월 / 6개월 / 12개월 + 한시적 1개월) → 리드타임·미내원 이력으로 고위험 예약을 플래그 → 전 구환 SMS 기본선 + 고위험만 사람 전화 → 고위험 슬롯 단축 → 리콜 방문에서 실제로 측정(탐침·%BoP·방사선). ③에는 기대치를 낮추고 ①②를 먼저 자동화하라.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 19 papers spanning periodontal/peri-implant maintenance, appointment attendance, and behavior change, reframing "patient recall" as a three-layer operational system: (1) who to recall and when, (2) how to make the booked visit actually happen, and (3) why the patient returns at all.

Evidence strength falls sharply across the layers: layer 2 rests on two attendance RCTs and a 196,018-appointment machine-learning model (SMS vs none 79.2% vs 35.5%, Prasad 2012; voice beat SMS with adjusted OR 2.12, Nelson 2011; lead time is the single strongest no-show predictor, Alabdulkarim 2022), layer 1 on cohort-level maintenance evidence with an apparent conflict that resolves by population (routine scale-and-polish shows high-certainty *no* benefit in low-risk healthy adults — gingivitis SMD −0.01, Lamont 2018 — while 3–6 month recall favors tooth retention in *treated periodontitis*, Farooqi 2015), and layer 3 is nearly empty (only 3 studies exist on converting pain-only attenders, Currie 2021).

The defensible protocol is therefore: stratify recall intervals by risk (3–12 months computed from residual PD, %BoP, smoking, diabetes) rather than defaulting to 6 months, flag high-risk appointments by lead time and prior-attendance sequence, deliver a scalable SMS baseline with personal calls reserved for flagged patients, shorten (never blindly overbook) high-risk slots — and set low expectations for motivational interventions until better evidence exists.

## 세줄요약

19편 종합(치주·임플란트주위 유지관리 + 예약 내원 + 행동변화) — "구환 리콜"을 3층 운영 시스템으로 재정의: ① 누구를 언제 부를지 ② 예약된 방문이 실제로 일어나게 하는 법 ③ 애초에 왜 다시 오는지.

층별 근거 강도가 급격히 다르다: ②는 내원 RCT 2편 + 196,018건 머신러닝 모델(SMS vs 무 79.2% vs 35.5%, Prasad 2012; 음성>SMS 보정 OR 2.12, Nelson 2011; 리드타임이 최강 예측인자, Alabdulkarim 2022)로 가장 단단하고, ①은 코호트 수준이며 표면적 충돌이 **인구 차이로 해소**되고(저위험 건강인에서 루틴 스케일링·폴리싱은 고신뢰도 무효 — 치은염 SMD −0.01, Lamont 2018 / 치료된 치주염에서는 3~6개월이 치아보존에 유리, Farooqi 2015), ③은 사실상 비어 있다(통증-only 환자 전환 연구 단 3편, Currie 2021).

따라서 방어 가능한 프로토콜: 6개월 일괄 대신 위험도 기반 3~12개월 산출(잔존 PD·%BoP·흡연·당뇨), 리드타임과 내원 이력 시퀀스로 고위험 예약 플래그, SMS를 확장 가능한 기본선으로 두고 플래그된 환자에만 사람 전화, 고위험 슬롯은 오버부킹 대신 단축 — 그리고 동기부여 중재에는 근거가 나올 때까지 기대치를 낮춘다.

## Summary

"구환을 어떻게 챙기는가"는 개원가에서 하나의 질문처럼 다뤄지지만, 위키가 보유한 근거는 그것이 **세 개의 다른 질문**임을 보여준다. 각 층은 다른 문헌, 다른 연구설계, 다른 확실성 수준을 갖는다.

**The unifying thesis: the three layers of recall are inversely related in effort and evidence.** Clinics typically invest the most energy in layer 3 (persuasion, relationship, marketing), where the evidence base is nearly empty, and the least in layer 2 (reminder channel, lead-time flagging, slot design), where two RCTs and a 196,018-appointment model give directly actionable numbers. Layer 1 sits in between — well-studied but routinely misapplied, because the widely-practiced "6-month cleaning" is imported from a population it was never tested in.

### Layer 1 — Who to recall, and when

**The single most consequential correction this synthesis makes: a fixed 6-month cleaning interval for low-risk healthy adults is not supported by periodontal evidence.** Lamont 2018 is a Cochrane review with *high-certainty* evidence of little or no difference in gingivitis, probing depth, or oral-health-related quality of life between 6-monthly, 12-monthly, and no scheduled scale-and-polish over 2–3 years. The 6- vs 12-month head-to-head is equally null (gingivitis SMD 0.03, I²=0%). Only calculus falls, and its clinical importance is unclear.

Two boundary conditions keep this from being over-read:

1. **Lamont did not measure caries, restoration failure, oral-cancer screening yield, or tooth loss.** The correct conclusion is *"periodontal health cannot justify the 6-month interval in low-risk adults"* — not *"low-risk adults need no recall."* If the clinic recalls Tier-C patients, the stated purpose has to change accordingly.
2. **The population is regularly-attending adults without severe periodontitis.** That is exactly the population the positive evidence does *not* cover.

**Where shorter intervals are supported:**

- **Treated periodontitis.** Farooqi 2015 found 3–6 month intervals favored tooth retention in regular compliers, with significantly more tooth loss as intervals approached 12 months. Critically, this rests on **8 cohort studies and zero RCTs** — the 3–6 month standard is practice-based consensus, not trial evidence, and Farooqi's own conclusion is that risk-stratified rather than uniform intervals are needed.
- **What compliance actually buys.** Jung 2019 (964 patients, 5–15 years) shows treated periodontitis is clinically stable under SPT: 10 of 14 investigations reported mean CAL loss ≤0.5 mm, with 3–20% of *sites* losing ≥2 mm. The residual progression tracks poor oral hygiene, smoking, and poor compliance — all modifiable, which is what makes the recall visit worth designing around.
- **Implants.** Risk-based supportive peri-implant therapy outperforms both fixed-interval and no maintenance (PD −1.0–1.5 mm, BoP −10–25 pp, 40–70% lower peri-implantitis incidence/implant loss in adherent patients; Mojaver 2025), with high-risk patients commonly landing at 3–4 months. Fixed full-arch prostheses — the hardest reconstruction to keep clean — take 6-monthly professional oral hygiene plus prosthesis removal at least annually (Lanzetti 2024, an explicitly low-certainty default: none of its 11 studies was at low risk of bias).
- **Patients who cannot yet clean their own mouth.** Cho 2018 supplies the short end: after four weekly PMTC visits, plaque and gingival scores improved sharply (p<0.001) but **had already decayed by the 3-month visit**, and 54.5% of participants themselves chose a 1-month interval. This is a *skill-acquisition* interval, not a lifelong one — the patient graduates to a longer interval once self-care holds.

**Stop hand-picking intervals.** Ramseier 2024 computes a 3–12 month interval from residual PPD distribution, %BoP, furcation, smoking, and diabetes (derived from 11,842 SPT visits across 883 patients). Replacing clinician intuition with this algorithm is what makes stratification reproducible across a team rather than dependent on who saw the patient.

### Layer 2 — Making the booked visit actually happen

This is where the evidence is strongest and the operational leverage is highest. The full factor analysis lives in [[overviews/dental-appointment-no-show-overview]]; what matters for *recall* specifically:

**Reminders work, and the channel changes the size of the effect.** SMS against no reminder raised on-time attendance to 79.2% vs 35.5% (Prasad 2012) — *any* reminder beats none. Head-to-head, voice reminders beat SMS (no-show 8.2% vs 17.7%, adjusted OR 2.12; Nelson 2011), converging with patients' stated preference for a dentist's call (48.3%) over SMS (23.8%) (Bhatia 2018). Two caveats keep this directional rather than settled: Nelson is a **pediatric** setting, and the two trials use different comparators in single centers. The practical reading is a two-tier design — SMS as the scalable baseline for the whole recall list, a human call reserved for flagged patients — rather than a claim that calls are universally superior.

**Flag appointments by behavior, not demographics.** Alabdulkarim 2022 (196,018 appointments, 42.7% no-show) found **lead time** — the booking-to-visit interval — to be the single strongest predictor (removing it costs 7.5–7.9% AUC), with prior no-show history second. The methodological contribution transfers directly to a clinic EMR: store attendance history as a **binary sequence** (1=attended, 0=missed; e.g. 1,0,0,1,0) rather than a collapsed count or average, which improved logistic-regression AUC by 2–5%. Recall lists are *by construction* long-lead-time bookings, which is precisely why they are structurally no-show-prone.

Secondary flags: new/initial-visit origin, morning (<10 AM) slots, and the descriptive profile of single, female, ≤35-year-old patients (Alkhurayji 2024 — note these did not reach statistical significance against scheduling dimensions), plus patients who track appointments **by memory alone** (Bhatia, p=0.024) — the one high-risk marker any external reminder directly displaces.

**Cost is not the lever.** Pediatric non-attendance ran 44% despite 85% insurance coverage (Khries 2024), and the upper-middle SES group had the *highest* no-show rate (58.8%, Bhatia). Discount-driven recall campaigns are therefore poorly supported. The better structural levers are slot design — shorten flagged high-risk slots rather than blindly overbooking, because dental slots are long (36–49 min vs ~17 min in primary care) and overbooking disrupts the following patient — plus evening and walk-in capacity (Alkhurayji), and fixing **staff miscommunication**, the single largest administrative barrier at 10.3% (Khries).

**Recall the family, not the individual.** A parent's own avoidance of preventive care was the strongest independent predictor of an adolescent's non-attendance (mother AOR 4.19, father AOR 3.41; Samohyl 2021), and skipping pediatric care co-occurred with skipping dental care (AOR 5.14). Non-attendance behaves as a household health-behavior trait, which argues for grouping recall lists by family rather than by patient record.

### Layer 3 — Why the patient returns at all

**The honest finding here is an absence.** Currie 2021 searched eight databases for interventions converting problem-orientated (pain-only) attenders to regular attendance and found **three studies** — a dental anxiety clinic, a free "dental update" advertising campaign, and Scotland's free check-up policy — all reported too poorly to replicate. No single intervention can be recommended. The paper's value is as a gap map plus four design targets: dentist communication, the dentist–patient relationship, raising awareness of unrecognised (silent) disease, and cost/access.

**Motivational interviewing is real but bounded.** In general medical care MI works, including as brief consultation (OR 1.55, 95% CI 1.40–1.71; Lundahl 2013, 48 RCTs, n=9,618). In adult dentistry the picture is unresolved: across 7 adult RCTs — all periodontal — only BoP improved significantly (−6.41 pp), with plaque and gingival index non-significant (Cartes-Velasquez 2024). That is *inconclusive*, not *negative*; reading it as "MI doesn't work in adults" is the standing misinterpretation flagged in [[overviews/behavioral-dentistry-decision-ladder]].

**If something is to be built here, build the technique bundle rather than the conversation.** Across 21 studies spanning six psychological models, the most promising cluster was social-cognitive interventions combining **goal-setting, planning, self-monitoring, and feedback**, with CBT+MI as a candidate for reducing plaque and bleeding (Vilar Doceda 2023). Heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis, so these remain directional signals.

**Adherence is itself a measurable prognostic variable.** Patients attending more than half of their SPT visits *earlier* than scheduled lost 0.60 teeth over 20 years, against 1.45 for later attenders (p<0.0001; Ramseier 2024). Recall compliance is not merely an administrative metric — it belongs in the risk assessment.

### Layer 4 — What the recall visit must actually contain

A recall system with an empty visit does not survive. The measurement core that triggers and justifies the next interval (Ramseier 2024, and see [[overviews/supportive-peri-implant-therapy-maintenance-overview]]):

- **Probing + %BoP + radiographs** are the validated, decision-driving measures.
- **Implant probing at ≤0.15 N** with non-metallic probes (vs 0.25 N at teeth); raising force from 0.15 to 0.25 N inflates BoP by **+13.7%** at implants against only +6.6% at teeth — over-forceful probing manufactures false positives that corrupt the very numbers the interval algorithm consumes.
- **Smoking-adjusted %BoP thresholds**: stability at <20–23% for non-/former smokers but **<16% for smokers**, whose gingival bleeding is suppressed.
- **Peri-implant stability criteria**: PD <5 mm, no BoP/suppuration, <2 mm bone loss from baseline (initial remodeling up to 1.5 mm may be physiological).
- When mechanical debridement fails to resolve inflammation, **antimicrobial photodynamic therapy (aPDT)** is the only adjunct with repeated independent evidence of added benefit (~0.6–0.8 mm additional PD reduction); lasers and growth-factor gels show no consistent gain (Mojaver 2025).
- Motivational aids used at the visit (phase-contrast microscopy, feeding back index scores) showed trends but **no statistical significance** (Cho 2018, p>0.05) — use them, do not oversell them.

### Reconciling the apparent conflict

The wiki holds two findings that look contradictory and are not:

| | Lamont 2018 | Farooqi 2015 / Jung 2019 / Cho 2018 |
|---|---|---|
| Population | Regularly-attending healthy adults, **no severe periodontitis** | **Treated periodontitis** patients / patients without established self-care skill |
| Intervention | Routine scale & polish (prophylaxis; no root planing, no chemotherapeutics) | Supportive periodontal therapy / structured PMTC + instruction |
| Outcome measured | Gingivitis, PD, calculus, OHRQoL over 2–3 y | Tooth retention, CAL over 5–15 y / plaque & gingival index over 6 mo |
| Result | High-certainty **no benefit**; interval irrelevant | Shorter interval **favors retention**; 12 mo worsens; skill decays by 3 mo |
| Evidence grade | sr+ma, 2 RCTs, high certainty | sr of **0 RCTs**; sr of cohorts; single small prospective |

They answer different questions on different populations with different endpoints. The synthesis is not "one of these is wrong" but **"the interval is a function of risk, and applying either study's number outside its population is the actual error."** Note the asymmetry in evidence grade: the *negative* finding (low-risk adults) is the better-evidenced one, while the *positive* finding (treated periodontitis) — the one clinics act on daily — rests on zero randomized trials.

### Remote recall — set the ceiling honestly

Teledentistry's oral-health signals are comparable or better than in-person comparators in the two reviews reporting them, but all 10 systematic reviews rated low or critically low on AMSTAR-2, and across all of them only **five distinct primary studies** carried any economic evaluation — none a cost-benefit, cost-utility, or incremental analysis (Scheerman 2024). Cost-saving is an assumption in this literature, not a finding. Remote check-ins may be piloted as a supplementary recall channel; they must not be justified to a practice on cost grounds. (Note also that Scheerman explicitly *excluded* unidirectional automated reminder texts from the teledentistry definition — SMS recall reminders are evaluated by the layer-2 trials above, not by this literature.)

## Clinical Decision — the operational protocol

Apply in order; each rung addresses a different layer.

1. **Re-tier the existing patient list by risk, not by calendar.**
   - **3–4 months**: treated periodontitis with smoking or diabetes; high-risk implant patients; unstable %BoP.
   - **6 months**: stable treated periodontitis; fixed full-arch prostheses (plus prosthesis removal ≥1×/year).
   - **12 months**: low-risk healthy dentition — and change the stated purpose of the visit, since periodontal benefit is not what justifies it.
   - **1 month, time-limited**: patients without established self-care skill, graduating out once plaque control holds.
   - Compute rather than guess, using residual PPD + %BoP + smoking + diabetes (Ramseier algorithm).
2. **Instrument the appointment record.** Store attendance as a binary sequence and surface lead time. These are the two strongest no-show predictors and both are already in the appointment system.
3. **Two-tier the reminders.** Automated SMS as the baseline for the whole recall list (79.2% vs 35.5% against nothing); a human call for flagged high-risk appointments (voice OR 2.12 vs SMS, pediatric evidence). Establish the recall mechanism **at the first visit**, since new-patient visits are over-represented among misses.
4. **Design the slots.** Shorten flagged high-risk appointments instead of overbooking; add evening and walk-in capacity; treat morning (<10 AM) slots as elevated risk.
5. **Fix the administrative layer.** A written, consistently-communicated cancellation/no-show policy addresses the single largest administrative barrier (staff miscommunication, 10.3%). Group recall by family unit.
6. **Make the visit measure something.** Probing + %BoP + radiographs, correct probing force at implants, smoking-adjusted thresholds, aPDT when inflammation persists after debridement.
7. **Set low expectations for persuasion.** Use brief MI and the goal-setting/planning/self-monitoring/feedback bundle, but treat any pain-only-to-regular conversion program as a locally-designed, locally-evaluated experiment — the published field is empty.

## Gaps & Future Research

- **No RCT exists on periodontal maintenance recall interval** (Farooqi screened 1,095 abstracts and found zero). The interval every practice runs on is consensus, not trial evidence.
- **No trial covers the recall interval for low-risk adults on non-periodontal endpoints** — caries, restoration failure, mucosal screening. This is exactly the gap that would justify or retire the 12-month check-up, and Lamont explicitly did not measure it.
- **Reminder-channel evidence is thin and partly pediatric.** Nelson (voice > SMS) is a pediatric dental setting; Prasad (SMS > none) uses a different comparator in a single Indian OPD. No trial tests app or push reminders, and no dental meta-analysis or cost-effectiveness comparison of call vs SMS vs app exists.
- **The ML no-show model is retrospective Saudi data.** Prospective deployment, fairness across demographic groups, and transferability to Korean clinic appointment systems are untested.
- **No Korean private-practice recall operations data** — re-attendance rates, recall-program return on investment, membership/subscription models, or message-copy A/B results. These numbers cannot be supplied from what the wiki holds; per Rule #4 they need either ingested papers or locally-collected clinic data.
- **Layer 3 is the largest gap in the whole page.** Currie's 3 studies are all poorly reported; adult dental MI is inconclusive across 7 periodontal RCTs; and no study has tested the goal-setting/planning/self-monitoring/feedback bundle *as a recall-attendance intervention* rather than an oral-hygiene one.
- **Full-arch maintenance cadence rests on zero low-RoB studies** (Lanzetti) — the 6-month/annual-removal default is pragmatic, not proven.
