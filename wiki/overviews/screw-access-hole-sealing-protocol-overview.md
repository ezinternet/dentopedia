> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 명제**: 나사유지 (screw-retained) 보철을 택한 뒤 남는 실무 문제는 스크류 접근홀 (Screw Access Hole, SAH)을 **무엇으로 막고(plug) · 벽을 어떻게 처리하고 · 레진 콤포지트 (resin composite)를 얼마나 두껍게 채우느냐**의 3변수다. 이 페이지는 세 변수를 각각 따로 검증한 단일 연구 3편을 하나의 체어사이드 프로토콜로 조립한다.
> - **⚠️ 회색지대 (gray zone)**: 세 변수를 **한꺼번에 묶어 검증한 연구는 없다.** 아래 프로토콜은 단일변수 연구 3편의 조합이지 통합 프로토콜의 직접 증명이 아니다.
> - **왜 중요한가**: 나사유지 크라운은 스크류 풀림 (screw loosening)이 **18% vs 4%**(p=0.032, Khurshid 2025, n=100, 12개월)로 시멘트유지보다 많아 SAH를 **반복해서 열고 닫게 되며**, Khurshid는 "접근홀 관리"를 나사유지의 유지관리 부담으로 명시한다. SAH 봉쇄는 일회성이 아니라 리콜마다 반복되는 술식이다.
> - **(1) 충전재 (plugging material) — Singla 2025 (RCT, 35명/45개)**: 건조 코튼펠렛 (cotton pellet) 대비 폴리테트라플루오로에틸렌 (Polytetrafluoroethylene, PTFE) 테이프와 1% 클로르헥시딘 (Chlorhexidine, CHX) 적신 코튼 (Co1%CHX) 모두 세균오염 유의하게 감소 — 혐기성 양성률 **코튼 95% → PTFE 50%**(p=0.001), **코튼 70% → Co1%CHX 20%**(p=0.001). 혐기성균은 Co1%CHX가 PTFE보다 우수(**20% vs 50%, p=0.04**), 호기성은 차이 NS(15% vs 40%, p=0.07). **건조 코튼 단독은 쓰지 말 것.**
> - **단, 임상 결과로는 아직 연결 안 됨**: 치은열구액 (Peri-implant Sulcular Fluid, PISF)의 인터루킨-6 (Interleukin-6, IL-6)는 3개월간 어느 군에서도 유의한 상승 없음 — 미생물학적 이득이 염증지표 차이로 측정되진 않았다(추적 짧음·균종 동정 없음).
> - **(2) 벽 표면처리 — Pereira 2016 (in vitro)**: 산화알루미늄 에어어브레이전 (Al₂O₃ air-abrasion) → **10% 불화수소산 (Hydrofluoric Acid, HF)** → 실란 (silane) → 본딩 → 코튼펠렛 → P-60 콤포지트로 SAH를 봉쇄해도 파절저항 (fracture resistance)이 미봉쇄군과 **차이 없음** — 봉쇄 술식이 크라운을 약화시키지 않는다. 다만 시편이 **금속-도재 (metal-ceramic)** 였으므로 HF+실란은 **도재·리튬 디실리케이트 (Lithium Disilicate, LDS) 같은 실리카계 세라믹**에만 해당한다.
> - **지르코니아 (zirconia) 벽이면 HF 대신**: 지르코니아는 HF로 에칭되지 않으므로 **에어어브레이전 + 10-MDP 프라이머**가 표준(Li 2024 NMA, Nasri 2025 umbrella — 위키 내 기존 근거). Pereira 프로토콜을 지르코니아에 그대로 옮기면 안 된다.
> - **(3) 콤포지트 두께 — Packaeser 2025 (in vitro)**: PTFE 테이프 위 콤포지트 **≥1.5mm** 군이 피로강도 (Fatigue Strength)에서 유의하게 높음(**1426N·1307N vs 1093–1127N**, p≤0.05); 단회하중 (monotonic)에선 차이 없음 — **피로에서만 드러나는 효과**. 그래서 정적하중만 본 Pereira의 "차이 없음"은 피로 영향까지 배제하지 못한다.
> - **⚠️ 범위 경고**: Packaeser의 SAH는 **시멘트유지 LDS 크라운 아래 지르코니아 어버트먼트의 나사 접근홀**이지, 나사유지 크라운 자체의 교합면 SAH가 아니다. **1.5mm 기준을 전형적 나사유지 크라운 SAH에 적용하는 것은 외삽**이다(해당 모델에서 1.5mm = 3mm 높이 어버트먼트의 "깊이 절반 이상").
> - **임상 종합 프로토콜**: ① 0.2% CHX로 SAH 소독 → ② 나사머리 위에 PTFE 테이프 또는 Co1%CHX 충전 (건조 코튼 X, 나사산에 PTFE 감지 말 것) → ③ 벽 처리: 도재·LDS는 HF+실란+본딩, 지르코니아는 에어어브레이전+MDP → ④ 콤포지트는 가능하면 **≥1.5–2mm** 확보(Singla도 2mm 사용).
> - **근거등급 (전체) — 약~중등도 (Weak-to-Moderate)**: 충전재 = 중등도(단일 RCT, 소규모·단기), 벽처리·두께 = 약(초록만 확보된 in vitro 단일연구). 강한 권고 수준 아님.

## Three-line Summary

Once screw retention is chosen, the screw access hole (SAH) must be re-plugged and re-sealed at every re-opening — screw-retained crowns loosen more often than cemented ones (18% vs 4%, Khurshid 2025) — and three single-variable studies inform how: PTFE tape or 1%-chlorhexidine-soaked cotton instead of dry cotton as the plug (Singla 2025 RCT: anaerobic-positive plugs cotton 95%→PTFE 50%, cotton 70%→Co1%CHX 20%; Co1%CHX beat PTFE for anaerobes, 20% vs 50%, p=0.04).

Bonding the composite to a silica-ceramic SAH wall with air-abrasion → 10% HF → silane → bond does not weaken the restoration (Pereira 2016, metal-ceramic, static load; zirconia walls instead need air-abrasion + 10-MDP), and a composite layer ≥1.5 mm over PTFE raises cyclic-fatigue failure load (1426/1307 N vs 1093–1127 N; Packaeser 2025) — an effect invisible under monotonic loading.

No study tested the combined protocol, Packaeser's SAH is an abutment-screw channel beneath a cement-retained crown (so the 1.5 mm threshold is extrapolated to screw-retained-crown SAHs), the IL-6 benefit of better plugs was not demonstrated at 3 months, and two of three studies are abstract-only in vitro — overall evidence is Weak-to-Moderate.

## 세줄요약

나사유지를 택한 뒤엔 SAH를 열 때마다 다시 막아야 하며(나사유지 스크류 풀림 18% vs 4%, Khurshid 2025), 충전재는 건조 코튼 대신 PTFE 테이프 또는 1% CHX 적신 코튼이 세균오염을 유의하게 줄인다(Singla 2025 RCT: 혐기성 양성률 코튼 95%→PTFE 50%, 코튼 70%→Co1%CHX 20%; 혐기성에선 Co1%CHX가 PTFE보다 우수 20% vs 50%, p=0.04).

실리카계 세라믹 SAH 벽을 에어어브레이전→10% HF→실란→본딩으로 처리해 콤포지트로 봉쇄해도 파절저항은 떨어지지 않으며(Pereira 2016, 금속-도재, 정적하중; 지르코니아 벽은 에어어브레이전+10-MDP로 대체), PTFE 위 콤포지트를 ≥1.5mm 채우면 피로 파괴하중이 유의하게 높아진다(1426·1307N vs 1093–1127N, Packaeser 2025) — 단회하중에선 안 보이는 효과.

통합 프로토콜을 검증한 연구는 없고, Packaeser의 SAH는 시멘트유지 크라운 아래 어버트먼트 나사홀이라 1.5mm 기준은 나사유지 크라운 SAH로의 외삽이며, 좋은 충전재의 IL-6 이득은 3개월 내 확인되지 않았다 — 전체 근거는 약~중등도.

## Summary

The parent decision — screw-retained vs cement-retained vs SCRP — is covered in [[overviews/implant-prosthesis-retention-screw-vs-cement-overview]]. This page starts *after* that decision has landed on a screw-based mode, and asks the operational question every screw-retained restoration raises: **how should the screw access hole (SAH) be closed so that the seal neither fails mechanically nor becomes a bacterial reservoir?**

**Why it matters.** Screw retention is favored for retrievability and for avoiding subgingival cement; the systematic review of Hamed et al. (2020, 12 studies) found no survival difference between modes but more *biological* complications with cement and more *mechanical* complications (screw loosening/fracture) with screws, with a trend toward lower marginal bone loss for screw retention. Khurshid et al. (2025, prospective, n=100, 12 months) quantified the mechanical cost — **screw loosening 18% vs 4% (p=0.032)** — and explicitly listed *access-hole management* as part of the screw-retained maintenance burden. The practical corollary is that the SAH is not sealed once: every retorque means removing the composite, retrieving the plug, and re-closing. A sealing protocol therefore needs to be (a) retrievable, (b) mechanically durable under chewing, and (c) microbiologically clean. Singla et al. frame the biological risk directly: wear of the sealing composite lets saliva and flora into the SAH, which can act as a reservoir leaking toward the implant–abutment junction.

**Variable 1 — the plug under the composite (Singla 2025, RCT).** In 35 patients / 45 single-tooth screw-retained implants, all SAHs were first disinfected with 0.2% CHX, plugged with dry autoclaved cotton and sealed with **2 mm of composite**; at 1 month the cotton was retrieved and cultured, and sites were randomized to **PTFE tape** (quilled into tight rolls) or **cotton soaked in 1% CHX (Co1%CHX)**, re-sealed, and re-cultured a month later. Both alternatives were far cleaner than cotton: aerobic-positive plugs 90%→40% (PTFE, p=0.01) and 65%→15% (Co1%CHX, p=0.001); anaerobic-positive 95%→50% (PTFE, p=0.001) and 70%→20% (Co1%CHX, p=0.001). Head-to-head, Co1%CHX beat PTFE for anaerobes (20% vs 50%, p=0.04) but not significantly for aerobes (15% vs 40%, p=0.07). Two cautions temper this. First, the cotton-vs-alternative contrasts are *sequential within the same sites* (cotton always came first, in the first month after loading), so they are before/after comparisons rather than parallel randomized arms; only PTFE-vs-Co1%CHX was randomized. Second, **IL-6 in peri-implant sulcular fluid did not rise significantly over 3 months in either group** (PTFE 256→261 pg/ml, p=0.79; Co1%CHX 227→244 pg/ml, p=0.17), no species were identified, and follow-up ended ~5 months after restoration. The microbiological signal is strong; its clinical translation is not yet shown. The authors' pragmatic claim — fewer complaints of halitosis/cacogeusia — is a plausible rationale, not a measured outcome.

**Variable 2 — the wall treatment (Pereira 2016, in vitro, abstract-only).** Thirty UCLA-abutment metal-ceramic mandibular molar crowns were split into screw-retained with sealed SAH, screw-retained unsealed, and cement-retained. The sealing protocol was **Al₂O₃ air-abrasion → 10% HF → silane → bonding agent → cotton pellet → P-60 composite**. Sealing did not change fracture resistance versus leaving the SAH open, and the cement-retained group had the highest mean fracture resistance of the three. The clinically useful reading is narrow: an adhesively bonded SAH restoration is *mechanically neutral* for the crown. Two limits matter. (i) The substrate was **feldspathic porcelain on metal**, a silica-based ceramic — HF + silane is the right chemistry there and for lithium disilicate (the wiki's silica-ceramic bonding synthesis, [[overviews/ceramic-bonding-hf-silane-contamination-overview]], supports HF → dedicated silane → adhesive layer). But **zirconia is not etched by HF**; for zirconia SAH walls the held evidence points to air-particle abrasion + 10-MDP primer/cement instead ([[resin-bonding/li-2024-zirconia-surface-treatments-monomers-nma]], [[dental-materials/zirconia/nasri-2025-surface-conditioning-zirconia-bonding-umbrella]]). (ii) Loading was **static to fracture** — and Variable 3 shows that static testing can miss exactly the effect a sealing layer has.

**Variable 3 — composite thickness (Packaeser 2025, in vitro, abstract-only).** One hundred lithium disilicate discs were luted (Multilink N) onto zirconia abutments whose screw channel was filled with PTFE tape alone or PTFE + 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 or 2.0 mm of bulk-fill composite (Tetric N-Ceram Bulk Fill). Monotonic fracture load did not differ, but under stepwise **cyclic fatigue** the ≥1.5 mm groups failed at significantly higher loads (1426 N and 1307 N) than PTFE-only, 0.5 mm and 1.0 mm (1093–1127 N; p≤0.05). The authors translate this as "fill more than half of the SAH depth with composite." Two things follow. First, it reconciles with Pereira: a sealing layer can be neutral under a single static load yet matter under fatigue, so "sealing doesn't weaken the crown" and "sealing thickness changes fatigue survival" are compatible statements. Second — the key **scope caveat** — Packaeser's SAH is the *abutment-screw channel beneath a cement-retained crown*: the composite supports the ceramic crown from below, over an unsupported void. That is mechanically different from the occlusal SAH of a screw-retained crown, where the composite itself forms part of the occlusal surface. Applying "≥1.5 mm" to classic screw-retained crown SAHs is therefore an **extrapolation**, not a direct finding; the more transferable principle may be the ratio ("more than half the channel depth composite, not PTFE"), since in the tested model 1.5 mm was half of a 3 mm abutment. Reassuringly, Singla's clinical protocol independently used 2 mm of composite, so ≥1.5–2 mm is at least consistent with clinical practice in the held literature.

**A PTFE distinction to keep straight.** PTFE *as an SAH plug* (above the screw head) is a different use from PTFE *wrapped on screw threads*. The preload synthesis ([[overviews/abutment-screw-preload-joint-stability-overview]]) records that thread-applied PTFE acts as a lubricant and lowers preload (Coelho 2024/2025). Nothing in the three sealing studies suggests PTFE in the threads; the plug goes in *after* torquing, over the head.

**The gray zone.** No held study tested the assembled intervention — CHX-disinfected SAH + PTFE/Co1%CHX plug + substrate-appropriate wall conditioning + ≥1.5 mm composite — as one protocol, let alone against a clinical endpoint such as seal loss, screw loosening, or peri-implant disease. Each step rests on one study, two of which are abstract-only in vitro work. The protocol below is a reasoned assembly of single-variable evidence and should be read that way.

### Chairside SAH sealing protocol (synthesized)

1. **Confirm the parent decision and torque first.** This protocol applies once a screw-retained (or SCRP) restoration has been chosen ([[overviews/implant-prosthesis-retention-screw-vs-cement-overview]]) and the screw is torqued to specification. Do not place PTFE on the screw threads (preload loss; see preload overview).
2. **Disinfect the SAH.** Irrigate/swab the channel with 0.2% CHX, as in Singla's protocol at every closure, maintaining isolation.
3. **Plug over the screw head with PTFE tape or cotton soaked in 1% CHX — not dry cotton** (Singla 2025). Choose Co1%CHX when anaerobic control is the priority (20% vs 50% anaerobic-positive vs PTFE); choose PTFE when a clean, inert, easily retrieved plug is preferred and anaerobic difference is acceptable. Keep the plug compact so it leaves room for composite (step 5).
4. **Condition the SAH walls by substrate.**
   - *Feldspathic porcelain / lithium disilicate (silica-based):* air-abrasion → HF etch (Pereira used 10%) → dedicated silane → bonding agent (Pereira 2016; ceramic-bonding overview). Intraoral HF carries a toxicity risk (flagged in the ceramic-bonding overview) — isolate carefully.
   - *Zirconia:* HF is ineffective — use air-particle abrasion + 10-MDP-containing primer/adhesive (Li 2024 NMA; Nasri 2025 umbrella).
5. **Fill with resin composite, targeting ≥1.5–2 mm of composite (more than half the channel depth) where anatomy allows** (Packaeser 2025, with the scope caveat above; Singla used 2 mm). If the channel is shallow, reduce the plug volume rather than the composite thickness.
6. **Re-seal the same way at every re-opening** — expect re-entry for retorque in a meaningful minority of screw-retained crowns (~18% loosening at 12 months, Khurshid 2025) and recheck seal integrity at recall, since composite wear is the stated route of SAH contamination (Singla).
