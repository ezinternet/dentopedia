## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 핵심 명제: 투명교정(Clear Aligner Therapy, CAT)은 **경도~중등도 비발치 부정교합**에서 고정성장치(Fixed Appliance, FA)와 **교합결과가 동등**하고 치주·편의성에서 우위지만, **복잡 이동(torque·rotation·extrusion·bodily movement)·중증/골격 증례·발치 공간폐쇄**에서는 예측성이 낮거나 열등하다. 보조장치(attachment·TADs·IPR·trimline 설계)가 적응증 범위를 넓힌다. 임상 위키 보유 58편을 11축으로 합성.
> - **효율(교합결과)**: RCT 21편 메타분석(Baneshi 2024, n=970) — 단순 비발치서 ABO/LII/PAR 교합결과 CAT=FA **차이 없음**. 우산고찰(Yassir 2022, 18 SR)도 경중등도엔 효과적이나 **중증·특정 이동엔 열등** 결론.
> - **특정 이동 한계**: 성인 전치 이동(Ubuzima 2025, 12편) — 수직이동 비일관, **FA가 회전·토크 조절 우수**. 발치/복잡(Jaber 2023, 6편) — 결과는 동등하나 FA가 협설경사·교합접촉을 **단기간**에 달성, CAT는 예측-실제 괴리 큼. 치료기간(Alhamwi 2024)은 경중등도서 CAT≈FA. 상악 전치 정확도 SR(Gonçalves 2023, 5편·612 movements)은 **토크가 가장 낮은 정확도**(49.1–51.5%)·압하는 방향 역전까지 발생, 신출(extrusion)·수평이동만 상대적으로 예측가능 — Ubuzima의 "FA가 회전·토크 우수" 결론을 정량 재확인.
> - **발치 3차원 이동 정확도**: 상악 4소구치 발치 CAT 후향연구(Wang 2025, 47명·542치아, CBCT 중첩) — **치근이동이 치관이동보다 항상 과소달성**, 절치 치근 최대 오차 5.49 mm, 전치는 계획된 압하 대신 오히려 정출; ClinCheck 설계 시 치근/토크 과보정 필요성을 정량 제시 — Tang 2025의 CoS leveling failure와 함께 발치 CAT의 "이중 생체역학 페널티"를 구성. 구개측 파워암(palatal power arm) split-mouth RCT(Wonghinkong 2026, 18명) — 견치 원심이동량 자체는 동일하나 **파워암이 견치 회전·원심경사를 유의하게 감소**(선형이동 정확도는 무관, 각이동 조절에만 기여).
> - **착용 프로토콜(Wear Protocol)**: 단축 착용(7일/10일) SR(Monisha 2024, 6편, n=449) — 전치부 이동은 14일과 차이 없으나 **후방 압하·원심경사·협측토크는 14일이 더 정확**(GRADE 매우 낮음); 획일적 단축보다 단순=단축·복잡=14일의 하이브리드 프로토콜 권장, 통증은 착용기간 무관.
> - **발치 후방개교합 — "Roller Coaster Effect"**: 발치 65명 후향연구(Zhuo 2026) — 경도 총생군에서 제1대구치 기능교두거리(FCD)가 가장 크게 증가(+1.64 mm), 작은 ANB·큰 상악전치 순측경사가 위험인자, **경도 총생이 오히려 더 위험**(직관과 반대). 내러티브 리뷰(Lei 2026)는 이 현상을 포함해 CAT 특이 생체역학 3대 현상(Watermelon Seed·Pendulum·Roller Coaster Effect)을 명명하고 기전을 설명.
> - **Class II 전략 — 원심이동(distalization)·고정원(anchorage)**: 상악 대구치 ~2 mm 가능(Bhate 2025), 단 **전치 anchorage loss 가능성 높음**; TADs+IPR 병용 시 예측성·안정성 향상(Inchingolo 2023). TAD/미니스크류 병용 SR(Marinelli 2025, PRISMA 14편·대부분 case report)도 distalization·intrusion·extrusion·수직조절을 향상시키나 **대조비교 부재로 근거 low**. 원심이동 후 상악 전치부 개방형 치은유두(Open Gingival Embrasure, OGE, black triangle) 발생률 17.3%, 나이·ANB·U1-NA로 술전 예측 가능(AUC 0.839, Shi 2026 후향연구 n=75).
> - **Class II 전략 — 하악전방유도(Mandibular Advancement, MA)**: MA aligner vs 기능성장치 골격효과 동등(Yu 2023 n=283 / Huang 2026 n=465), CAT가 **하악전치 순측경사를 더 억제**하나 하악지 성장(Co-Go)은 다소 적음 — 골격보다 치성/postural 경향.
> - **Class III 교정**: case-report(Zhang 2025 성인 n=1 7년 안정, Ye 2025 성장기 scissor bite n=1)에서 **비교연구 단계로 진입**. 내러티브 리뷰(Rutili 2026, 128편·86%가 level 4-5)는 CAT의 하악 원심이동을 2-3mm·**치관 수준 효율 ~74% vs 치근 수준 ~47%**(주로 tipping)로 정량화, 전체 camouflage 재발률 ~30%. 후향비교(Panda 2025, n=50, 25 CA vs 25 braces) — CAT가 치료기간 단축(18 vs 22개월)·순응도 우수하나 **골격(ANB) 개선은 braces가 우수**(1.5° vs 0.8°). Surgery-first 전향비교(Cunha 2025, n=34, CA20 vs FA14) — 술식(CA/FA) 간 **OHRQoL 궤적 차이 없음**. 성장기 pseudo-Class III 전치부 반대교합 RCT(Gok 2025, n=30, CA vs Z-spring) — Z-spring이 더 빠르나(48 vs 96일) CAT가 arch depth 증가를 동반 — Ye 2025 case report의 growing-patient 축에 첫 RCT급 근거 추가.
> - **생체역학(설계)**: composite attachment이 전치 토크·회전·근원심이동·후방 anchorage를 대체로 개선(Nucera 2022); trimline 설계(straight/extended margin)가 힘 전달·유지력 좌우(Nakornnoi 2024). FEA 29편 SR(Cao 2025) — CA 부작용은 **치관-치근 불균등 응력**에서 기인, attachment·power ridge·divot·elastics·막두께가 응력을 재분배해 **tipping→translation 전환**. → CAT 효율은 "장치 설계 의존적". Lei 2026(내러티브 리뷰)은 CAT 전체 실현율 평균 **~50%**(원심이동 최고 58-75%, 수직이동 최저 13-52.9%)를 4대 이동유형별로 정리; attachment **재료과학** SR(Frias 2026, 15편)은 나노하이브리드/고점도 레진이 기계적 저항·마모저항 우수, 유동성 레진은 취급성 우수하나 표면조도·변색(커피·레드와인) 취약이라는 재료 trade-off 제시. 단일모델 3D FEA(Kuguoglu 2024)는 어태치먼트 형태를 직접 비교해 **협측+구개측 대합 반타원형**이 가장 평행한 상악 제2대구치 원심이동(타 디자인 대비 이동량 ~75%)을 만든다는 것과, **완전맹출 제3대구치**가 원심이동을 10–22% 저해·응력을 증가시킨다는 정량치를 제시 — Class II distalization(axis 2) 계획 시 사전 고려 요인. 회전·경사·토크에 대한 어태치먼트 크기·형상 FEA(Pede 2024, 15개 모델) — **작을수록 이동량 증가**(역비례), 사각-베벨형이 회전에, 삼각-베벨형이 경사에, 최소형 power ridge가 토크에 가장 유리 — Kuguoglu/Cao의 형상-의존성 결론을 개별 이동 유형별로 세분화. 압출 벤치 실측(Baik 2024, DIC+force/moment transducer) — **어태치먼트 유무가 결정적**: 어태치먼트 없이는 8개 디자인 전부가 계획과 반대인 압하력을 발생시켜, "형태 최적화"보다 "어태치먼트 존재 자체"가 선행 조건임을 실측으로 증명. 최적화(optimized) vs 재래식(conventional) attachment 직접비교 SR+MA(Muthuswamy Pandian 2025, 7편·n=526) — 견치 재회전·전치 신출 모두 **유의한 정확도 차이 없음**(SMD −0.31/0.14) — "3D bevel 최적화"가 치료기간엔 이점 있어도 정확도 우위는 아직 미입증, attachment 선택을 정확도만으로 결정하지 말 것.
> - **상악확장**: 성장기서 CAT 확장은 가능하나 conventional expander 대비 유의 적음(Fonseca-Planells 2026), 확장은 주로 **치조성(dentoalveolar)**, 골격엔 conventional 우위. 성인 예측성 후향코호트(de la Rosa-Gay 2025, 98명·multilevel GLMM) — Invisalign 확장 **오차 0.92 mm·과소확장 72.2%**, 상악·구치·crossbite·대량 계획확장일수록 악화. 전향적 다기관 비열등성 코호트(Kim 2026, n=48, 성장기) — 구치간 확장은 SME 대비 **비열등**이나 **CAT군만 유지기 구치 협측경사 재발**(ΔMI −7.1°) — Fonseca-Planells SR 결과를 정교화(경증례는 "따라잡되" tipping은 여전히 더 큼). **혼합치열** 소아 전향연구(Bruni 2024, n=40, 평균 8.7세) — 확장 정확도가 전방(유견치 87.7%)→후방(영구대구치 67.9%)으로 감소, 계획 1mm당 임상 0.6mm만 발현(전체 80.2%) — 대구치 확장은 대부분 협측 치관경사이므로 과교정(5mm 목표 시 ClinCheck 약 7mm) 필요.
> - **안전성(치근흡수·치조골)**: CBCT 9편 메타(Butsabul 2024) — **최소** 외흡수(절치 치근길이 −0.56 mm), **상악 중절치 최대**. CBCT 94명 후향연구(Luo 2025)는 발치 CAT의 상악 중절치 주위 **구개측 골결손이 유의**(순측골은 유지)하며, 이동 양상(후방이동·압하·치근원심이동·설측경사)별로 위험 부위·유형이 다름을 규명 — 단순 후방이동량이 아니라 이동 벡터 전체를 계획해야 함.
> - **재료·화학 안전성**: 열가소성수지(PETG/hTPU/multilayer/blend)+신소재(SMP·3D프린트 레진)의 광학·기계·열·생체적합성 특성이 재료선택을 좌우(Delgado 2025); 화학물질 용출 SR(Ferreira 2025, 7편) — CA **대체로 안전**하나 BPA 등 용출 잔여우려(독성한계 미만), thermoforming 후 세포독성↑, Biolon 최고(64.6%).
> - **부위별 회전 정확도(신규 축)**: 회전 전용 SR(Benedetti 2025, 12편·1 RCT+11 비무작위, 리파인먼트 *전* 3D 중첩) — 전체 정확도 **평균 ~65%(36–85%)**, 부위별로 **대구치 74–85%(최고) > 소구치 54–83% > 절치 35.1–90% > 견치 36–58%(최저, 잔여오차 ~4.5°)**. 견치가 최악인 것은 직관과 반대이나 근원이 원형에 가까워 aligner가 짝힘(couple)을 걸 지지점이 없다는 점에서 Cao 2025 FEA의 치관-치근 응력 불균등과 같은 한계의 부위별 표현. **12편 중 11편이 attachment를 썼는데도 회전 이득은 연구간 불일치**(축 4의 Muthuswamy Pandian 2025 null과 수렴), IPR도 미입증(Kravitz 43%, NS). 실행 가능한 레버는 **staging 하나** — 대부분 2°/aligner를 썼고 **<1.5°/aligner로 낮추기** 권장. 근거 낮음–보통(비무작위 11편 중 저위험 0편) → 65%는 **환자 설명·계획용 벤치마크지 가이드라인 수치가 아니다**.
> - **치주·구강위생**: CAT가 치태·치은·출혈지수 유리(Baneshi)하나 SR+MA(Crego-Ruiz 2023, I²=99%)·GRADE SR(Alhuwaizi 2026, very low~low)은 **근거 불충분·compliance 의존**으로 신중. **최고 합성수준인 umbrella review(Di Spirito 2023, SR 4편·2,042명: 투명교정 978 vs 고정식 1,064)가 시간축을 붙여 결론을 못박는다** — 단·중기엔 PI·GI·SBI·PPD 모두 CAT 통계적 우위(지수 MD −0.14~−1.10; PPD −0.26~−0.93 mm), 치은퇴축은 3개월 시점 CAT 안정 vs 고정식 유의 증가, **장기엔 전부 NS**. 저자 결론은 두 방법이 **"비교 가능(comparable)"** — 모든 단기 우위가 **<1 mm 또는 <1점이라 "임상적으로는 미미"**하기 때문. 즉 쟁점은 *효과의 유무*가 아니라 **크기**이며, **CAT의 치주 우위는 지속적 치주 이득이 아니라 단기 위생접근성 효과**로 읽는 것이 compliance 의존 소견과 정합. 동의서엔 "잇몸에 더 좋다"가 아니라 이 강도로 적을 것. 보조로 프로바이오틱이 aligner biofilm을 단기 감소(Thakur 2026, 예비적). 타액 염증지표 종적연구(Hassan 2024, 성인 40명, FA 20 vs CA 20) — 골개조 사이토카인 TNF-α가 양 군 모두 24시간 최고치 후 감소하나 **전 시점(24h·7d·21d)에서 FA가 CA보다 유의하게 높음** — CAT 치주 우위에 대한 세포/생화학적 기전 근거 보강.
> - **저작근·TMJ·이갈이(bruxism)**: 저작근 활성/불편은 착용 초기 일시 변화 후 정상화(Almalki 2024), sEMG pooled 차이는 무의(Lekavičiūtė 2024); 턱관절(TMJ) 형태변화는 성인 Class I/II 비발치서 미미(Montasser 2026, serious RoB). CA–bruxism 첫 SR(Porporatti 2026, 11편·n=818) — **대체로 중립**(tonic 수축 흔히 감소·phasic 비일관·SB index 불변, protective/harmful 단정 불가). → 대체로 중립, 단 근거 약.
> - **가속(adjunct)**: 진동·저출력레이저(LLLT) 등 물리적 보조중재는 CAT 결과를 **일관되게 개선하지 못함**(Sirri 2026, GRADE low~very low).
> - **확신도 종합**: 효율 동등성·치근흡수 = 근거 중상(SR+MA); 치주·저작/TMJ·가속 = 근거 약(high RoB, low certainty); Class III·복잡 이동 열등·설계 의존성 = 일관된 신호이며, Class III는 이제 case-report에 더해 후향/전향 비교연구와 narrative review(128편)가 추가돼 근거층이 두꺼워졌으나 여전히 RCT+MA는 부재.
> - **환자 보고 결과(PRO/OHRQoL/순응도 — 신규 축, 6편)**: 내러티브 리뷰(Chehab 2026, 140편) — CA가 65–75%의 비교연구에서 구강건강관련 삶의 질 (Oral Health-Related Quality of Life, OHRQoL) 우위; 3단계 궤적(초기적응→안정화→완료); 통증 24–48h 피크→4–7일 소실; OHRQoL 저하 8–12%로 미미. 범위검토(Chong 2025, 37편) — 완전 순응(≥22h/일) **36%**에 불과, 순응도와 만족도는 **독립**. 전향 12개월(Patil 2026, n=120) — CA 심미·편안함 전 기간 우위; 구강건강영향지수 (Oral Health Impact Profile, OHIP)-14 12개월 수렴; 백색반점 CA 11.7% vs FA 31.7%(p=0.008). 복잡 케이스(Hamid 2026, n=60) — OHRQoL 1개월 CA 우위→치료 완료 시 수렴. 브라질 단면(Caldas 2024, n=94) — 중간 통증→OHIP-14 유의 상승; 만족도는 통증과 무관. 포르투갈 일상생활영향지수 (Daily Impacts on Life, DIDL)(Oliveira 2026, n=112) — FA 초기 불만족 35.7% vs CA 후기 0%; 적응이 그룹 간 균등화. **임상 시사**: CA의 OHRQoL 우위는 초기 편의 이점이고 치료 완료 시 수렴; 순응도 36%는 실제 착용시간을 과신하지 말 것.
> - **전치부 3D 정확도·구치 근원심·발치 공간폐쇄 신규 데이터**: 후향연구(Saif 2026, n=40, 480치아, Invisalign, 비발치, 초기 세트만) — 전체 평균 50.3%(상악 47.6%, 하악 53%); 압하 23.1% 최악; 상악 원심 경사 64.85% 최고. 구치 근원심 이동 SR(Garcia-Marin 2025, PROSPERO 등록, 6편) — 보조장치 없이 예측성 낮음. 발치 공간폐쇄 SR+MA(Vicioni-Marques 2025, 14편, n=510, GRADE 매우 낮음~낮음) — 전반적 과소달성 확인. 세 편 합산: 전치 압하·구치 근원심 이동·발치 공간폐쇄 모두 ClinCheck 과대예측; 재치료 루틴화·과교정 설계 필수.
> - **수술 후 투명교정(Alghafli 2026, SR, 10편, n=258)**: 술 후 CA 골격·치아 안정성 FA 동등(단기); OHIP-14(p=0.004)·구강악안면 삶의 질 설문 (Orthognathic Quality of Life Questionnaire, OQLQ)-22(p=0.002)·치주건강 CA 유의 우위; 수술-우선+CA 18.05 vs 22.83개월 단축(p<0.05). Cunha 2025와 합산: 수술 후 CA는 안정성·QoL·치료기간 모두 FA 대비 비열등 이상.
> - **브랜드·재료 신규 데이터**: 색안정성(Kobkiatkawin 2025, 4브랜드 704샘플 — Invisalign·ClearCorrect·Beforedent·Käse; 커피·차·콜라 6/12h) — 브랜드별 침착 패턴·표면 변화 상이. 바이오필름(Pasaoglu-Bozkurt 2024, 6브랜드 — S. mutans+L. acidophilus 0–240h) — 브랜드 × 시간 교호작용 유의; 우식 고위험 환자에서 브랜드 선택이 바이오필름 결과에 영향. 열성형 물성(Ryu 2018, 4소재) — 열성형 후 전 소재 유의한 물성 변화; eCligner만 수분용해도 감소·경도·투명도 유지·굴곡 탄성계수 증가. 가상치료계획 (Virtual Treatment Setup, VTS) 브랜드 비교(Telles 2025, 파일럿, 4브랜드, 동일 환자 10명) — 동일 처방에서 브랜드별 단계수·어태치먼트 계획 상이. **임상 시사**: 브랜드 선택이 색안정성·바이오필름·제작물성·VTS 모두에 영향 — 단순 가격·가용성 외 기준이 필요.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 73 wiki papers on clear aligner therapy (CAT) across 12 axes — efficacy (incl. wear protocol, vertical/open-bite mechanism, product-line predictability), movement-specific limits (incl. extraction-driven "Roller Coaster Effect" and curve-of-Spee leveling failure, anterior 3D accuracy, molar mesiodistal SR, extraction-space-closure SR+MA), Class II strategies, Class III camouflage/growth-modification (incl. post-surgical CAT SR), biomechanics/design (incl. attachment material science, bench force/moment, brand VTS comparison), maxillary expansion (incl. 2-year mixed-dentition stability), root resorption & alveolar bone (incl. extraction-protocol-specific risk), periodontal/hygiene (incl. oral microbiome mechanism and brand-level biofilm comparison), masticatory-muscle/TMJ/bruxism, acceleration adjuncts, patient expectations/informed consent, and patient-reported outcomes/QoL/compliance (new axis 12).

In mild-to-moderate non-extraction cases CAT equals fixed appliances (FA) for occlusal outcome (Baneshi 2024 RCT-MA, 21 RCTs, n=970) with a periodontal/comfort edge, but is inferior or unpredictable for torque, rotation, extrusion, extraction, bodily movement, and severe/skeletal cases; auxiliaries (composite attachments, TADs, IPR, extended trimline) extend the treatment envelope; root resorption is minimal (CBCT MA: incisor MD −0.56 mm), masticatory/TMJ/bruxism effects are broadly neutral (low certainty), and chemical leaching stays below toxic thresholds.

Aligners are the evidence-based choice for mild-to-moderate, non-extraction, compliance-amenable cases, but add attachments/TADs/IPR for complex movements and defer to fixed appliances for severe skeletal, heavy-torque, or bodily-movement cases; acceleration devices are not cost-justified.

## 세줄요약

투명교정(Clear Aligner Therapy, CAT) 위키 73편을 효율(착용 프로토콜·개방교합 기전·제품라인별 예측성 포함)·이동특이 한계(발치 Roller Coaster Effect·스피 곡선 성형 실패·전치 3D 정확도·구치 근원심 SR·발치 공간폐쇄 SR+MA 포함)·Class II 전략·Class III camouflage/성장기 증례(수술 후 CAT SR 포함)·생체역학/설계(attachment 재료과학·실측 force/moment·브랜드 VTS 비교 포함)·확장(혼합치열 2년 안정성 포함)·치근흡수/치조골(발치 프로토콜별 위험 포함)·치주(구강미생물총 기전·브랜드별 바이오필름 포함)·저작/턱관절/이갈이·가속보조·환자 기대/동의·환자 보고 결과/QoL/순응도 12축으로 종합.

경중등도 비발치에서 고정장치(Fixed Appliance, FA)와 교합결과 동등(Baneshi 2024 RCT 메타, 21편, n=970) + 치주·편의 우위; 토크·회전·정출·발치·체이동·중증 골격 증례엔 열등·비예측적; 보조장치(composite attachment·TADs·치간삭제(IPR)·trimline 연장)로 적응증 확장; 치근흡수 최소(절치 −0.56 mm), 저작/턱관절/이갈이 영향 대체로 중립(근거 약), 화학용출 독성한계 미만; Class III 교정은 아직 case-report 수준.

경중등도·비발치·컴플라이언스 가능 환자에게 투명교정이 근거기반 선택; 복잡 이동은 attachment·TADs·IPR 보강, 중증 골격·과중 토크·체이동은 고정장치로 전환; 가속 장치는 비용 대비 근거 없음.

## Synthesis

According to the 58 clear-aligner papers held in this wiki (`orthodontics/clear-aligner`), the clinical bottom line is an **indication gradient**: clear aligner therapy (CAT) performs at parity with fixed appliances (FA) at the simple end of the malocclusion spectrum and degrades toward the complex/skeletal end, where movement *type*, *appliance design*, and *wear compliance* — not the aligner concept itself — govern success. The eleven axes below organize the evidence from efficacy through safety to ancillary effects and patient expectations.

### 1. Efficacy vs fixed appliances (the core equivalence + its boundary)

The quantitative anchor is [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/baneshi-2024-clear-aligners-malocclusion-sr-ma]] — an RCT-only SR+MA (21 RCTs, n=970) finding **no significant CAT-vs-FA difference in occlusal outcome** (ABO-OGS, Little Irregularity Index, PAR) for simple non-extraction malocclusions, with CAT favored on plaque/gingival/bleeding indices and 6-month quality of life. The umbrella review [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/yassir-2022-cat-vs-fat-overview-systematic-reviews]] (18 SRs) states the same direction narratively while drawing the boundary: CAT is **effective for mild-to-moderate malocclusion but inferior for severe cases and specific movements (torque, extrusion, rotation)**, with greater relapse. Treatment duration in mild-to-moderate crowding is comparable ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/alhamwi-2024-clear-aligner-vs-fixed-treatment-duration-crowding-sr]], GRADE low). The boundary is sharpened by two movement-specific reviews: adult anterior tooth movement ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/ubuzima-2025-clear-aligner-fixed-anterior-teeth-movement-adults-sr]], 12 studies) found vertical movement inconsistent and **FA superior for rotation and torque control**; complex/extraction cases ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/jaber-2023-clear-aligners-complex-extraction-vs-fixed-sr]], 6 trials, n=283) reached equivalent ABO-OGS/PAR endpoints but FA achieved better buccolingual inclination and occlusal contacts in less time, with notable predicted-vs-achieved discrepancies for CAT in extraction cases. A movement-type-specific SR focused purely on maxillary incisors ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/goncalves-2023-invisalign-upper-incisor-accuracy-sr]], 5 studies, 612 tooth movements, post-2010 SmartTrack era) quantifies exactly which movements drive that FA advantage: **torque was consistently the least accurate movement** (49.1–51.5%), intrusion sometimes reversed into extrusion, while extrusion (up to 142.4%) and horizontal translation (67.6–78.9%) were comparatively reliable — attachments/Power Ridge improve but do not resolve the torque/intrusion weak points, and no RCT evidence exists.

A retrospective study provides a complementary real-world benchmark for those accuracy figures: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/saif-2026-clear-aligner-anterior-3d-accuracy]] (40 adults, 480 anterior teeth, Invisalign non-extraction, first aligner set without refinement; 10-day change, mean 27 aligners) measured predicted-vs-achieved accuracy across all four movement types simultaneously in the anterior arch, finding an **overall mean accuracy of 50.3%** (upper 47.6%, lower 53.0%). By movement type the ranking is: intrusion 23.1% (worst — upper incisors), followed by mesiodistal rotation (63.32% upper mesial) and mesiodistal tipping (64.85% upper distal), with buccolingual tipping most reliable (lingual 75.52% lower); all four showed significant predicted-vs-achieved discrepancies (p<0.05 for all). The 50.3% overall figure aligns tightly with Lei 2026's ~50% expression-rate synthesis across movement types and isolates intrusion as the floor — confirming ClinCheck's systematic overestimation of achievable anterior tooth movement across all planes. The molar analogue of these anterior accuracy data is addressed by a dedicated SR on **mesiodistal molar movement** ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/garcia-marin-2025-clear-aligner-molar-mesiodistal-sr]], PROSPERO CRD42022357639; 919 screened → 6 included; ROBINS-I; GRADE; search through September 2024): without auxiliaries, predictability of upper and lower molar mesiodistal movement via CA is limited and highly variable. Read alongside saif-2026 (anterior) and the Gonçalves SR (maxillary incisors), the full-arch accuracy hierarchy becomes: buccolingual tipping (anterior) most reliable → mesiodistal rotation/tipping → intrusion (least reliable) → molar mesiodistal movement requiring auxiliary support for clinically useful predictability. A critical baseline consideration for upper second molar accuracy targets: a 3D digital cast study ([[orthodontics/goracci-2021-maxillary-second-molar-angulation-normal-occlusion]], n=41 untreated adolescents with normal occlusion) found that fully-erupted maxillary second molars in natural occlusion show **significantly more distal crown tip** (−18.9° vs Andrews' +0.4°, P<0.0001) and **more lingual inclination** (−10.6° vs −8.0°, P=0.0118) than Andrews' historical values — the reference encoded in most preadjusted bracket and aligner prescription tables. If ClinCheck's U7 tip target derives from Andrews' nearly zero mesial tip, the planned-vs-achieved discrepancy for second molar tipping in distalization cases may partly reflect a prescription target that is nearly 19° off in direction, not only aligner force delivery failure.

Rotation — the movement Ubuzima flagged narratively and Gonçalves did not isolate — now has its own tooth-type breakdown. A dedicated SR ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/benedetti-2025-clear-aligner-rotation-accuracy-sr]], 12 studies: 1 RCT + 11 non-randomized, adults, 3D digital superimposition of predicted-vs-achieved measured **before refinement**) puts overall rotational accuracy at **~65% (range 36–85%)** and, more usefully, stratifies it by tooth: **molars are the most predictable (74–85%)**, then premolars (54–83%) and incisors (35.1–90%), with **canines the problem tooth (36–58%, ~4.5° residual error)**. That ranking is worth reading against intuition — the canine's near-circular root cross-section gives an aligner little purchase to deliver a couple, which is the same crown-versus-root force-transfer limit Cao 2025's FEA synthesis describes mechanistically in axis 4, expressed as a per-tooth number. Two negative findings matter as much as the ranking: **11 of the 12 studies used attachments, yet the evidence for their rotational benefit remains inconsistent** — converging directly with Muthuswamy Pandian 2025's optimized-vs-conventional derotation null in axis 4 — and IPR's contribution is unproven (the single study isolating it, Kravitz, found 43% accuracy with IPR, not statistically significant). The one actionable lever the review does identify is **staging**: most included studies used 2° per aligner, and reducing to <1.5° per aligner is suggested by the literature to improve accuracy. Certainty is low-to-moderate (the single RCT rated moderate; **no non-randomized study rated low risk of bias**), so ~65% is a planning and patient-counseling benchmark — and a concrete one for axis 11's expectation-calibration problem — rather than a validated guideline figure.

Efficacy also depends on a factor orthogonal to malocclusion type: **wear protocol (activation schedule)**. A dedicated SR ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/monisha-2024-efficacy-of-clear-aligner]], 6 studies, n=449, GRADE very low) found expedited 7-/10-day tray-wear schedules perform comparably to the conventional 14-day schedule for anterior tooth movement, but the single low-RoB RCT directly testing it found 14-day wear significantly more accurate for **posterior intrusion, distal tipping, and buccal torque** — the same movement categories flagged as CAT-weak in axis 1 above — with no pain penalty for shortened wear. The authors propose a hybrid protocol (short wear for simple movements, 14-day for complex ones) rather than a uniform expedited schedule.

Vertical correction — specifically anterior open bite — shows a distinctive aligner mechanism: an SR+MA ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/charoenrat-2025-clear-aligner-anterior-open-bite-molar-intrusion-sr-ma]], 10 studies, no RCTs) found CAT closes open bite predominantly via **incisor extrusion** (U1 +0.87 mm, L1 +1.06 mm) with **no significant molar intrusion**, whereas fixed appliances with temporary anchorage devices (FATADs) achieve significantly greater total overbite gain (+1.64 mm more) primarily through **molar intrusion** (U6 +1.88 mm) — the same anchorage/torque-transmission constraint seen in axis 2's molar-distalization anchorage loss recurring in the vertical dimension; CAT-corrected open bites were stable in retention (6 months–>1 year), though certainty is low-to-very-low with no RCTs available. Predictability itself varies by product line: a retrospective analysis of the reduced-movement **Invisalign Lite** variant ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/meade-2026-invisalign-lite-efficacy-retrospective]], n=122, abstract-only) found overjet and mandibular incisor inclination tracked plan well, but overbite correction accuracy worsened as initial overbite increased (proportional bias) and maxillary incisor inclination under-achieved plan by −2.905° — a maxilla-mandible torque-accuracy asymmetry consistent with axis 4's design-dependence thesis.

Extraction-based CAT carries a specific, now-named posterior complication: the **"Roller Coaster Effect"** (mid-arch posterior open bite from premature anterior/posterior contact during space closure). A retrospective cohort ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/zhuo-2026-the-roller-coaster-effect]], 65 patients post four-premolar extraction) operationalized it via posterior functional cusp distance (FCD): the first molar showed the largest increase (mesiobuccal +1.64±1.39 mm, p<0.001), with **mild-crowding cases paradoxically at higher risk** than moderate-to-severe crowding, and a smaller ANB angle plus greater upper incisor proclination as independent risk factors. The narrative review [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/lei-2026-advancements-in-biomechanics-and]] supplies the mechanistic name and explanation (posterior anchorage/torque loss during space closure), alongside two sibling CAT-specific phenomena — the **Watermelon Seed Effect** (uneven force from asymmetric crown coverage) and the **Pendulum Effect** (anterior lingual tipping/extrusion reacting to posterior retraction) — and reports an overall CAT movement "expression rate" averaging only ~50%, ranging from 58–75% for molar distalization (most reliable) down to 13–52.9% for vertical movement (least reliable, upper incisor intrusion worst). A related extraction-specific biomechanical penalty affects the mandibular curve of Spee (CoS): a retrospective comparison ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/tang-2025-evaluating-the-effectiveness-of-clear]], n=60, 30 extraction/30 non-extraction) found extraction patients undercorrected CoS 1.64× more than predicted by ClinCheck software (prediction gap −1.38 vs −0.84 mm, p<0.001), attributed to reduced aligner stiffness/anchorage over the longer inter-support span after first-premolar extraction and current attachment designs (G5/G6) not being optimized for combined sagittal-vertical control — reinforcing that extraction cases carry compounding biomechanical penalties (Roller Coaster Effect + CoS leveling failure) beyond simple space closure. A companion 3D CBCT-superimposition study on the maxillary arch ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/wang-2025-clear-aligner-premolar-extraction-3d-tooth-movement]], 47 patients, 542 teeth, four-premolar extraction) extends this from crown-level to **root-level** discrepancy: root movement was consistently underachieved versus crown movement (maximum gap at the U1 root, 5.49±2.30 mm anteroposterior), anterior teeth extruded despite a designed intrusion, and U3 crowns reached only 71.5% of designed distal movement — meaning ClinCheck planning for extraction CAT needs systematic root/torque overcorrection, not just crown-level target adjustment. A split-mouth RCT testing one candidate mitigation ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/wonghinkong-2026-canine-retraction-palatal-power-arm-rct]], 18 adults, in-house aligners) found a **palatal power arm** during premolar-extraction space closure did not change linear canine-distalization accuracy but significantly reduced canine rotation (mean diff 5.22°) and distal crown tipping — an angular-control auxiliary, not an anchorage fix (both sides still showed anchorage loss), and IHCA-specific pending replication in commercial systems.

The question of whether aligners can reliably close **extraction space** itself — at the whole-case level — now has an SR+MA: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/vicioni-marques-2025-space-closure-extraction-clear-aligner-sr]] (14 studies, 510 patients; search through November 2024; 4 low-bias, 10 high-bias; GRADE very low to low) meta-analyzed predicted-vs-actual tooth movement for extraction-space closure cases. Consistent with Wang 2025 (crown vs root underachievement) and Tang 2025 (CoS leveling failure), the pooled finding confirms significant predicted-vs-achieved discrepancies for the extraction arch as a whole. The GRADE very-low certainty means these results are directionally concordant but insufficiently precise for a specific numeric target; the clinical message is that extraction CAT should plan systematic overcorrection across all movement types, and that the three individually documented biomechanical penalties (Roller Coaster Effect, CoS leveling failure, root-movement underachievement) are now joined by an SR+MA-level confirmation of global space-closure underprediction.

A further extraction-specific angular risk — independent of the Roller Coaster Effect and CoS leveling failure — is **maxillary lateral incisor mesiodistal tipping** during space closure. A retrospective CBCT study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/yan-2025-mesiodistal-angulation-maxillary-lateral-incisor-clear-aligner]], 27 patients, 54 lateral incisors, four-premolar extraction) identified significant distal inclination at the lateral incisor (p<0.001) and isolated three independently modifiable design factors: **attachment presence** reduced distal tipping (88.4% vs 86.9% accuracy, p<0.001); **canine-first retraction** sequence significantly preserved angulation vs en masse (p<0.001); **horizontal movement distance** was dose-dependent (>4 mm: p=0.008; >7 mm: p=0.01); crown-to-root ratio had no effect. The lateral incisor is biomechanically vulnerable in extraction CAT — small blunt crown limits aligner seating, simultaneous mesiodistal and labiopalatal movement demands compound, and AlBalaa reported actual CAT precision for lateral incisor movement at only 51.19%. ABO OGS requires apex deviation ≤1 mm post-treatment, making angulation control clinically consequential. Practical design response: specify **attachment + canine-first retraction** as default choices for any extraction case with lateral incisor involvement.

### 2. Class II correction — two aligner strategies

**Molar distalization**: achievable with aligners (up to ~2 mm, [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/bhate-2025-cat-maxillary-molar-distalization-class-ii-sr]], 11 Invisalign studies, achieved/predicted 36–78%) but **anterior anchorage loss is highly probable**; predictability and stability improve markedly with skeletal anchorage (TADs) and IPR — a hybrid approach is best, especially with large overjet ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/inchingolo-2023-clear-aligner-distalization-predictability-sr]], 19 studies). A biomechanical constraint often overlooked at the treatment-planning stage: a fully-erupted **third molar** reduces second-molar distalization efficiency by 10–22% and raises aligner/attachment stress (FEA evidence, axis 4) — worth screening for before staging a distalization case. A dedicated SR of temporary anchorage devices in CAT ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/marinelli-2025-temporary-anchorage-devices-clear-aligner-sr]], PRISMA, 14 studies, mostly case reports; ROBINS-I 1 low/9 some-concerns/4 high) confirms that adding TADs/miniscrews improves control of distalization, intrusion, extrusion and vertical movements, but the evidence is **low-certainty** with no controlled comparisons — the hybrid concept is sound, the quantitative backing is not yet. A direct clinical consequence of this anchorage-loss risk is now quantified: a retrospective study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/shi-2026-pre-treatment-associated-factors]], n=75) found **17.3% incidence of open gingival embrasure (OGE, "black triangle")** of the maxillary central incisors after sequential molar distalization, with age, anteroposterior overlap, ANB angle, and U1-NA angle as independent pre-treatment predictors (AUC 0.839) — a candidate chairside risk-screening tool, pending external validation. **Mandibular advancement (MA)**: two SR+MAs converge — [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/yu-2023-mandibular-advancement-aligner-vs-functional-class-ii-sr-ma]] (9 controlled trials, n=283) and the larger/newer [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/huang-2026-clear-aligner-mandibular-advancement-vs-functional-class-ii-sr-ma]] (9 studies, n=465) both find MA aligners give skeletal Class II correction **comparable to traditional functional appliances** (Twin Block/Herbst; SNA/SNB/ANB n.s.) while **better controlling lower-incisor proclination** (Huang IMPA MD −0.90°; Yu 1.94° less) — i.e. superior sagittal dental control but a more dental/postural than skeletal-growth effect (Yu: ramus growth Co-Go 1.10 mm less).

### 3. Class III correction — camouflage & growth-modification (evidence now maturing beyond case reports)

Unlike Class II, where SR+MA-level evidence exists (axis 2), the wiki's Class III aligner evidence started from two single case reports bracketing the age spectrum and has since gained a narrative-review umbrella plus its first controlled comparative studies. In an **adult**, [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/zhang-2025-clear-aligner-based-multidisciplinary]] (n=1, 28-year-old, skeletal Class III just outside surgical thresholds — Wits −4.92 mm, Holdaway 11° vs. cutoffs of −5.8 mm/10.3°) achieved non-surgical camouflage via aligner-driven bite opening (mandibular incisor intrusion/retroclination + maxillary proclination), Class III elastics, and molar-attachment bite ramps, combined with extraction/implant replacement of a compromised incisor; the result was **stable at 7-year follow-up** — the longest reported CAT-only Class III camouflage interval identified in this wiki. In a **growing patient**, [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/ye-2025-combined-use-of-miniscrews]] (n=1, 11-year-old, unilateral posterior scissor bite + skeletal Class III) combined facemask maxillary protraction with clear aligners (attachments used as the anchorage interface for facemask elastics) and posterior miniscrew anchorage/intrusion, correcting the scissor bite and occlusal cant over 17 months — illustrating CAT's auxiliary role (anchorage interface, occlusal-plane correction) alongside orthopedic appliances, rather than as the primary corrective mechanism, complementing the dental/postural (not skeletal) profile seen for Class II MA aligners above.

A SANRA-compliant narrative review spanning all adult Class III camouflage modalities ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/rutili-2026-class-iii-camouflage-modalities-narrative-review]], 128 studies: 110 case reports/series + 18 observational) now contextualizes those two case reports within the field as a whole: **86% of all Class III camouflage evidence — across every modality, not just aligners — is level 4–5** (case report/small series), so the wiki's case-report-heavy aligner evidence is not an aligner-specific evidence gap but the field's baseline. Within that landscape, CAT-specific lower-arch distalization achieves only 2–3 mm with ~74% crown-level but just ~47% root-level efficiency (predominantly tipping, not bodily movement — the same crown-vs-root discrepancy pattern seen in axis 1's extraction data), and overall Class III camouflage relapse runs ~30% with extraction-based protocols showing superior long-term stability over non-extraction. The review's verdict: aligner-based Class III camouflage suits mild-to-moderate, low/normo-angle, high-compliance adults, but its torque/vertical/root-level limitations mean it is not interchangeable with fixed-appliance or TAD-assisted approaches for more severe or bodily-movement-heavy cases — directly echoing axis 1's torque/rotation findings (Ubuzima, Gonçalves) in a Class III-specific context.

The field's first head-to-head comparative data have also arrived. A retrospective study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/panda-2025-clear-aligner-braces-class-iii-comparison]], n=50, 25 CAT vs 25 fixed braces, mild-to-moderate Angle Class III) found CAT achieved shorter treatment duration (18±3 vs 22±4 months) and higher compliance (92% vs 75%) with comparable overjet correction, but **fixed braces achieved significantly greater skeletal (ANB) improvement** (1.5±0.5° vs 0.8±0.3°) and needed less refinement — a direct quantitative echo of Rutili's root/skeletal-control limitation. A prospective controlled study in the surgical end of the spectrum ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/cunha-2025-class-iii-surgery-first-aligner-fixed-ohrqol]], n=34, CA 20 vs FA 14, Surgery-First Approach bimaxillary orthognathic surgery) found **no significant between-group difference in OHRQoL trajectory** (OQLQ, OHIP-14, SF-36) through 6 months post-surgery — suggesting that once skeletal correction is handled surgically, appliance choice (aligner vs fixed) for the surgery-first orthodontic phase does not differentially affect patient-reported quality of life, though small unequal groups and non-randomized allocation limit strength.

The growing-patient side gains its first RCT-level counterpart to Ye 2025's case report, though for a narrower **isolated dental (pseudo-Class III)** presentation rather than true skeletal Class III: an RCT in mixed dentition ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/gok-2025-clear-aligner-z-spring-anterior-crossbite-mixed-dentition]], n=30, 7–12y, Angle Class I molar relationship + isolated anterior crossbite) compared clear aligners against a Z-spring appliance. Both corrected the crossbite in all patients with comparable OHRQoL, but the Z-spring was markedly faster (48.4±27 vs 96.3±22.7 days) while CAT alone produced significant arch-depth increases — consistent with a tipping-dominant (Z-spring) vs. tipping+alignment+bodily-movement (CAT) mechanism difference; this is abstract-only (methodological detail unconfirmed) and notably a dental pseudo-Class III rather than the skeletal cases the rest of this axis addresses. Taken together, the four new studies do not overturn the case-report-era conclusion that CAT is feasible for mild-to-moderate Class III camouflage while ceding skeletal correction to fixed appliances/surgery — they substantiate it with the field's first comparative numbers, while confirming (via Rutili's 86% figure) that Class III camouflage evidence overall, aligner or otherwise, remains structurally thin.

Post-orthognathic surgery — the furthest end of the skeletal-intervention spectrum — now has its own dedicated SR for the aligner phase: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/alghafli-2026-postsurgical-aligners-sr]] (10 studies including 2 RCTs, n=258). Post-surgical CAT showed **skeletal and dental stability comparable to fixed appliances** at short-term follow-up; the two RCTs found CA significantly better on OHIP-14 (p=0.004) and OQLQ-22 (p=0.002) as well as periodontal health markers; a Surgery-First protocol with CA achieved 18.05 vs 22.83 months total treatment time (p<0.05). This complements Cunha 2025 (which found no OHRQoL trajectory difference for Class III surgery-first CA vs FA at the 6-month mark) by extending the post-surgical evidence base to orthognathic patients generally, adding stability data, and providing time-to-completion figures. The combined reading: **post-surgical CA is a viable alternative to fixed appliances** on stability, OHRQoL, and treatment-duration grounds, with the caveat that the non-RCT majority of alghafli's evidence base and short follow-up window mean this is an encouraging but not yet SR+MA-level certainty finding.

### 4. Biomechanics — the success is in the design

Aligner efficacy is **appliance-design-dependent**. Composite attachments mostly increase effectiveness — improving anterior root torque, rotation, mesiodistal movement and posterior anchorage — though evidence is weaker for intrusion and lacking for extrusion/posterior expansion ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/nucera-2022-composite-attachments-clear-aligners-sr]], 5 trials). Trimline (gingival margin) design drives force delivery and retention: straight/extended margins generate higher forces/moments and greater displacement than scalloped/short designs, with the effect varying by movement type ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/nakornnoi-2024-aligner-trimline-biomechanics-tooth-movement-sr]], 12 studies). A finite-element-analysis SR ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/cao-2025-clear-aligner-biomechanics-finite-element-analysis-sr]], 29 FEA studies) supplies the mechanistic substrate: CA side effects arise from **uneven crown-vs-root stress distribution**, and auxiliaries (attachments, power ridges, divots, elastics, membrane thickness) work by *rebalancing* that stress to convert uncontrolled tipping into translation. These explain *why* the movement-specific limitations in axis 1 exist and *how* they are partly mitigated. Trimline retention also depends on periodontal anatomy, not just design: an in-vitro study on 3D-printed models simulating marginal tissue recession ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/bortoli-2026-aligner-retention-gingival-margin-design]]) found scalloped-extended/straight margins retain more than scalloped margins regardless of recession severity, but retention itself climbs sharply with advanced recession + interdental bone loss (Class IV ≈3× the healthy-control force) — a potential insertion/removal-discomfort factor in periodontally compromised patients that the original trimline SR (healthy arches only) could not capture. Extrusion specifically depends on a design *precondition*, not just a design *variant*: a bench study combining digital image correlation with a force/moment transducer ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/baik-2024-3d-printed-aligner-premolar-extrusion-force-moment]], 8 design combinations, n=15/group) found that **without an attachment, all eight margin designs paradoxically generated an intrusive force** instead of the planned premolar extrusion — extended margins and reduced buccolingual margin width delivered adequate extrusion force only when an attachment was present, and a thickened margin underperformed even then. This reframes axis 4's design-dependence thesis: attachment presence is the gate, margin geometry is the fine-tuning.

Two further papers add depth to this design-dependence thesis. The narrative review [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/lei-2026-advancements-in-biomechanics-and]] (introduced in axis 1 for its extraction/Roller Coaster discussion) organizes movement-specific overcorrection, attachment placement, and mini-screw anchorage strategies across all four biomechanically demanding movement types (distalization, expansion, vertical, extraction) — the practical, strategy-level counterpart to Cao 2025's mechanistic FEA catalog. And the attachment itself is not just a shape but a **material**: a material-science SR ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/frias-2026-material-properties-of-composite]], 15 studies) found all tested composite resins fall within clinically acceptable shear bond strength (~6–14 MPa), but with a clear trade-off — nanohybrid/high-viscosity resins (e.g., Filtek P60, Tetric N-Ceram) give superior mechanical/wear resistance and dimensional stability (favoring high-load posterior attachments), while flowable resins adapt more easily to attachment molds but show greater surface roughness and staining susceptibility (favoring esthetic anterior zones with high-filler flowables). This extends axis 3's biomechanical-design argument down to the resin-selection level. Direct hardware confirmation of this material-dependence comes from a six-axis mechanical-sensor bench study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/cho-2025-clear-aligner-material-force-moment-six-axis-sensor]], in-vitro, 3D-printed typodont with PDL simulation) comparing two thermoplastics (Zendura, Trioclear) against a direct-3D-printed resin (Graphy TA-28): at larger (0.6 mm) tipping activations, Zendura generated significantly greater labial force and, with Trioclear, greater labial-inclination moment than Graphy, while Graphy paradoxically produced a reverse-direction (lingual) force on the labially-programmed tooth — attributed to thermal-softening/shape-memory spring-back during insertion — and only Trioclear reproduced the theoretically expected lingual reactive moment on the anchor tooth. Material differences only became significant at the larger activation magnitude, meaning material choice matters more as case complexity/movement magnitude increases.

Attachment *geometry*, not just material, is a controllable design lever for molar distalization: a single-model 3D FEA study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/kuguoglu-2024-clear-aligner-attachment-third-molar-distalization-fea]]) directly compared vertical-rectangular, buccal-only combined semi-elliptical, and bilateral (buccal+palatal) opposed semi-elliptical attachments for maxillary second-molar distalization, with and without simultaneous expansion. The opposed (bilateral) design produced the most parallel, tipping-minimized movement but only ~75% of the raw distalization magnitude of the other two designs — the same displacement-vs-control trade-off Cao 2025's FEA synthesis frames mechanistically — and adding expansion to distalization amplified both displacement and unwanted tipping/rotation 2–3 fold, especially for the bilateral design, which also showed the highest stress of all modeled scenarios. The same study isolated a separate, clinically actionable variable: a fully-erupted **third molar** reduced second-molar distal displacement by 10–22% (17% at maximum total displacement) and increased both distopalatal rotation and aligner/attachment stress — a quantified biomechanical argument for considering third-molar status before staging a distalization case (cf. axis 2). A companion single-canine FEA ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/pede-2024-clear-aligner-attachment-rotation-tipping-torquing-fea]], 15 attachment models, 5 shapes × 3 sizes, ANSYS) extends Kuguoglu's shape-dependence finding to rotation/tipping/torque specifically: **smaller attachments of the same shape consistently produced greater displacement than larger ones** (inverse size–displacement relationship), rectangular-beveled shapes favored rotation, triangular-beveled favored tipping, and the smallest rectangular power ridge (0.5×0.5×5 mm) maximized torque expression — displacement peaked incisally and was lowest at the root in every configuration, giving clinicians two independent design levers (shape and size) rather than one.

Whether these attachment refinements translate into measured clinical accuracy gains is a separate, still-open question. A direct optimized-vs-conventional-attachment comparison ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/muthuswamy-pandian-2025-optimized-conventional-attachment-accuracy-sr-ma]], SR+MA, 7 studies qualitative/4 meta-analyzed, n=526) found **no significant accuracy difference** between "optimized" (3D-bevel) and conventional attachments for canine derotation (SMD −0.31, p=0.06) or anterior extrusion (SMD 0.14, p=0.60, I²=76%) — pooled accuracy for both attachment types fell well short of the ClinCheck-predicted movement (61.2–71.5% derotation, 57.5–62.4% extrusion). Read together with the FEA evidence above, the picture is: attachment shape/size *can* be engineered to bias force delivery (bench-proven), but current commercial "optimized" attachments have not yet demonstrated that engineering translates into superior *clinical* accuracy — so attachment-type selection should not be based on accuracy claims alone, though optimized designs may still offer other benefits (shorter treatment time, root control via bevel design).

### 5. Maxillary expansion

In growing patients, CAT can expand the maxilla but **significantly less than conventional expanders** ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/fonseca-planells-2026-clear-aligner-maxillary-expansion-growing]], 15 studies/7 pooled: intermolar width −1.77 mm, palatal volume −460.6 mm³, arch perimeter −1.75 mm favoring conventional), and the expansion is **mainly dentoalveolar** (tipping of anterior/deciduous teeth) rather than skeletal — a clear limitation for true transverse skeletal deficiency. A prospective multicenter cohort with a predefined non-inferiority design ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/kim-2026-efficacy-and-stability-of]], n=48 growing patients, CA vs. slow maxillary expander/SME) **refines** this SR+MA picture: CA was **not inferior** to SME on intermolar expansion and showed *greater* canine cusp-tip expansion, but the CA group alone showed significant buccal molar tipping relapse during retention (ΔMI −7.1±9.8°) — i.e., for milder, growing-patient cases CA can "catch up" on total expansion magnitude while still moving via more tipping/less bodily translation than SME, a mechanistic nuance rather than a contradiction of the SR+MA's dentoalveolar-vs-skeletal thesis. In adults the limitation is one of *predictability*: a retrospective cohort ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/de-la-rosa-gay-2025-expansion-predictability-clear-aligner]], 98 patients, multilevel GLMM) found a mean Invisalign expansion error of **0.92 mm with 72.2% of sites under-expanded**, worsening in the maxilla, on posterior teeth, in crossbite cases and as the planned expansion magnitude increases — so planned expansion should be over-corrected and concentrated anteriorly.

A younger, more homogeneous slice of the growing-patient population — **mixed dentition** specifically, rather than "growing patients" broadly — is quantified by a prospective cohort ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/bruni-2024-clear-aligner-maxillary-expansion-mixed-dentition]], n=40, mean age 8.7y, Invisalign First, transverse discrepancy ≤5 mm). It refines Kim 2026's non-inferiority finding with region-stratified accuracy: expansion accuracy declined anterior→posterior (deciduous canine 87.7% cusp-level → permanent first molar 67.9%), and regression showed only ~0.6 mm of clinical expression per 1 mm planned (overall 80.2% of target) — with cusp-level accuracy consistently exceeding gingival-level accuracy, fingerprinting **buccal crown tipping** as the dominant mechanism at the molar, not bodily translation. Practical corollary: plan roughly 7 mm of ClinCheck expansion to net 5 mm clinically at the permanent molar in mixed-dentition patients, and reserve rapid maxillary expansion (RME) for cases requiring a true sutural/orthopedic effect.

The **post-treatment stability** of Invisalign First expansion in mixed dentition is addressed by the first prospective follow-up with a 2-year horizon and no retainer: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/gazzani-2026-mixed-dentition-2year-stability]] (n=20, Invisalign First, Herbst-absence design — tracked without retainer for 2 years post-treatment). Lateral expansion, derotation, and alignment achieved during active treatment were largely stable across the follow-up window; the only statistically significant regression was mandibular first-molar torque (mean −1.1°), while Little's Irregularity Index held at 0.4 mm from post-treatment through 2 years. This is the first prospective retention-phase dataset for Invisalign First, and it is cautiously optimistic — noting that the mandibular molar torque regression mirrors exactly the movement category Bruni 2024 identified as least accurately expressed at the permanent first molar (67.9%), suggesting a buccal-crown-tipping mechanism that is both the hardest to achieve and the first to partially rebound. Practical corollary for both expansion and retention planning: any residual over-tipping in the active phase will likely recover; any under-tipping will likely relapse.

### 6. Safety — root resorption & alveolar bone

Reassuring on root length: CBCT meta-analysis ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/butsabul-2024-clear-aligner-root-resorption-cbct-sr-ma]], 9 studies) shows **minimal** external apical root resorption after CAT (overall incisor root length MD −0.56 mm; upper-incisor volume −13.34 mm³), with the **maxillary central incisor** most affected — consistent with the umbrella finding of lower root-resorption risk than FA. A companion CBCT study looking at bone rather than root ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/luo-2025-factors-of-alveolar-bone-changes]], n=94, extraction cases) found a **labial-palatal asymmetry**: significant palatal bone defect around the maxillary central incisor while labial bone was largely maintained, with the specific risk pattern depending on the 3D tooth-movement vector — retraction, incisor intrusion (worst, defects both labial and palatal height), root distal movement, and lingual inclination each produced a distinct bone-change signature. The clinical message is the same as axis 3/4's design-dependence thesis applied to bone safety: treatment planning should anticipate the predicted movement vector, not just the net retraction distance, to flag alveolar bone risk before finalizing a case.

Extraction protocol itself is an independent bone-risk variable, not just tooth-movement vector: a large CBCT cohort ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/zhang-2025-clear-aligner-extraction-protocol-alveolar-bone-cbct]], n=281 — 186 non-extraction, 59 two-premolar-extraction [TPE], 36 one-lower-incisor-extraction [OLIE]) found alveolar bone loss in all groups including non-extraction, but TPE carried a distinct lingual-bone-dehiscence/torque-loss signature (lingual AC-CEJ >2 mm in maxillary incisors/canines, p<0.01) while OLIE showed the highest interproximal contact-to-alveolar-crest distances, crossing open-gingival-embrasure risk thresholds (≥6 mm → 44% OGE); canine root resorption was greater in both extraction protocols than non-extraction (p<0.001), and mandibular anterior teeth lost more bone than maxillary anterior teeth across all protocols — extraction-protocol choice (TPE vs OLIE) should itself be part of tissue-risk counseling, not just the decision to extract.

### 7. Periodontal & oral hygiene — real but compliance-dependent

CAT's periodontal advantage is consistently *signalled* but **not firmly proven**. Baneshi's RCT-only MA gives favorable pooled effect sizes (plaque/gingival/bleeding), yet the dedicated periodontal SR+MA ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/crego-ruiz-2023-periodontal-gingival-recession-aligner-vs-fixed-sr-ma]], 12 studies; only plaque mid-term MD −0.99 and PPD long-term −0.93 mm significant, both I²=99%) and the GRADE SR ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/alhuwaizi-2026-clear-aligner-fixed-oral-hygiene-periodontal-grade-sr]], 6 RCTs, certainty very-low to low) conclude the **advantage is highly compliance-dependent** and evidence is insufficient for a firm claim.

The highest synthesis level available on this axis reaches the same verdict by a different route, and adds the time dimension the others lack. An umbrella review ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/di-spirito-2023-clear-aligner-periodontal-umbrella-review]], a systematic review of 4 SRs-with-meta-analysis, 2,042 patients: 978 aligner vs 1,064 fixed) pools at the SR layer rather than the trial layer and stratifies by follow-up. Clear aligners were **statistically better on plaque, gingival, bleeding and probing-depth indices at short- and mid-term** (index MD −0.14 to −1.10; PPD MD −0.26 to −0.93 mm); **gingival recession stayed stable with aligners while fixed appliances showed a significant increase at 3 months**; and **all long-term differences were non-significant**. The authors' own conclusion is the one to carry chairside: periodontal effects are **"comparable"** between the two modalities, because every short-term advantage is smaller than 1 mm or 1 index point — statistically significant but, in their words, practically negligible in a clinical setting. This is the sharpest statement in the wiki of this axis's real problem, which is not *whether* the effect exists but whether its **size** is clinically meaningful; it converges with Crego-Ruiz and Alhuwaizi from above rather than overturning them, and it inherits their limits (its four inputs are 1 high / 1 moderate / 2 low AMSTAR 2 quality). Read with the fade-over-time pattern, the practical synthesis is that **CAT's periodontal edge is a short-term hygiene-access effect rather than a durable periodontal benefit** — precisely what a compliance-dependent mechanism predicts, and a claim worth stating at that strength in consent (axis 11) rather than the stronger "aligners are better for your gums."

As an adjunct, consumer probiotics reduced aligner biofilm (CFU + EPS) short-term ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/thakur-2026-probiotics-clear-aligner-biofilm-rct]], crossover RCT n=20), but this is preliminary (label-only verification, no mechanical-cleaning comparator). The mechanism behind CAT's plaque/gingival advantage is now partly characterized at the microbiome level: the first SR unifying microbiome composition with clinical periodontal parameters across the CAT timeline ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/tabone-2026-clear-aligner-oral-microbiome-sr]], 12 observational studies, no RCTs) found a transient early dysbiosis (4–24h: decreased alpha diversity, increased Firmicutes, increasingly acidic aligner-liquid pH) followed by medium-to-long-term stabilization and a consistent CAT advantage over fixed appliances on cariogenic-bacteria risk (~8–10% high-risk S. mutans/Lactobacilli vs ~40% in brackets) and periodontal stability — but one dissenting functional-metagenome study found Invisalign was **not** superior to fixed appliances in microbiome composition/function, a genuine internal contradiction the review does not smooth over and a reason to keep the "CAT periodontal advantage" claim compliance- and evidence-quality-qualified.

A direct six-brand biofilm comparison adds a commercial-stratification layer to Tabone 2026's organism-level narrative: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/pasaoglu-bozkurt-2024-clear-aligner-biofilm-brand-comparison]] (Invisalign, Clarity, ClearCorrect, Smartee, Orthero, Graphy; S. mutans + L. acidophilus; 0–240h staged culture; two-way repeated-measures ANOVA + Bonferroni T2 post hoc) found significant time × brand interaction effects — biofilm accumulation kinetics differ between brands, not only over time. This is an in-vitro design (not oral ecology), but it extends the compliance-dependent, caries-risk framework of axis 7 by showing that **material surface chemistry is itself a biofilm-accumulation determinant** — a potential argument for brand selection based on biofilm-resistance in high-caries-risk patients, pending in-vivo replication. Read with kobkiatkawin-2025's color-stability data (axis 10), the combined signal is that CA brand and material properties co-determine both esthetic durability and microbiological behavior — two compliance-relevant dimensions that current clinical evidence frameworks have not yet systematically accounted for.

A biomarker-level mechanistic complement to these microbiome and clinical-index findings comes from a longitudinal salivary study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/hassan-2024-salivary-tnf-alpha-clear-aligner-fixed-appliance]], n=40, 20 FA + 20 CA, ELISA at baseline/24h/7d/21d): tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) — an early cytokine mediator of osteoclastic bone resorption — rose and peaked at 24 h in both appliance groups, but FA levels were significantly higher than CA at every post-placement time point (24h, 7d, 21d; all p<0.05), with CA returning near baseline by day 21 while FA did not. This offers a plausible cellular mechanism (continuous FA force vs. intermittent CA force) for the periodontal/comfort edge repeatedly signalled but not firmly proven in the clinical-index literature above — though as a single small non-randomized study without correlation to pain or long-term periodontal outcomes, it supports rather than replaces that evidence.

### 8. Masticatory muscles, TMJ & bruxism — broadly neutral, low certainty

Masticatory effects appear **transient and self-limiting**: muscle activity/discomfort change in the first days then normalize, with a noted bite-force reduction ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/almalki-2024-clear-aligner-masticatory-stomatognathic-sr]], 6 studies), and surface-EMG pooling shows **no significant net change** in masseter/temporalis activity during CAT ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/lekaviciute-2024-clear-aligner-masticatory-muscle-emg-sr-ma]], 6 studies). TMJ morphology (condylar position, joint spaces) is largely unchanged in adult Class I/II non-extraction cases, with forward condylar repositioning only in Class II div 2 ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/montasser-2026-tmj-changes-clear-aligner-scoping-review]], 4 retrospective CBCT studies, serious RoB) — exploratory, not confirmatory. The first SR on the CAT–bruxism interaction ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/porporatti-2026-clear-aligners-bruxism-systematic-review]], 11 studies, n=818, no meta-analysis) is likewise reassuring-but-uncertain: the effect is **predominantly neutral** — tonic muscle contractions are often reduced, phasic activity is inconsistent and the sleep-bruxism index is unchanged — so aligners can be classified as neither protective nor harmful for bruxers on current evidence.

### 9. Accelerated orthodontics — adjuncts do not help

Physical adjunctive interventions (vibration devices, low-level laser therapy) marketed to accelerate movement / improve tracking / reduce pain do **not** produce consistent benefit in CAT — no improvement in Little Irregularity Index, only transient day-1–3 pain reduction, vibration helping tracking only under a 7-day change cadence, GRADE low to very low ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/sirri-2026-physical-adjunctive-interventions-clear-aligner-rct-sr]], 7 RCTs, n=266). The change cadence matters more than the device.

### 10. Material science & chemical safety — reassuring, selection matters

The aligner material is itself a clinical variable. A material-science review ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/delgado-2025-thermoplastics-clear-aligners-review]]) maps the thermoplastics in use — PETG, thermoplastic polyurethane (hTPU), multilayer laminates and polymer blends, plus emerging shape-memory polymers and 3D-printable resins — by their optical, mechanical, stress-relaxation, thermal and biocompatibility properties, the determinants of force retention (the engineering basis for axis 3). On safety, a dedicated SR ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/ferreira-2025-clear-aligners-toxic-chemicals-systematic-review]], 7 in-vitro/in-vivo studies) finds clear aligners are **generally safe**: chemical leaching (including bisphenol-A) stays **below toxic thresholds**, though a residual concern remains — thermoforming raises cytotoxicity and one material (Biolon) was most cytotoxic (64.6%). Net: material choice influences both performance and biocompatibility, but no current evidence contraindicates CAT on toxicity grounds.

Three further material/brand studies extend this axis in clinically specific directions. **Color stability** — the patient-facing esthetic dimension of CA material — is characterized in-vitro by [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/kobkiatkawin-2025-clear-aligner-color-stability-brand]] (4 brands: Invisalign, ClearCorrect, Beforedent, Käse Aligner; 704 samples; coffee, tea, cola, artificial saliva; 6h and 12h immersion; FTIR polymer analysis + 3D laser surface microscopy): brand-specific staining patterns and surface-texture changes were observed, extending the Delgado 2025 material-property map and the Ferreira 2025 safety profile to the esthetic-durability dimension. This is compliance-relevant because patients who observe aligner discoloration may reduce wear time — a behavioral loop the axis 1 wear-protocol SR did not model but axis 12 (below) identifies as a compliance-satisfaction mediator. **Thermoforming process** behavior is examined by [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/ryu-2018-ecligner-thermoforming-toa]] (4 thermoplastics: Duran, eCligner, Essix A+, Essix ACE; multiple mechanical and optical properties pre- and post-thermoforming): thermoforming significantly altered properties in all four materials, rejecting the null hypothesis; eCligner showed a unique post-thermoforming profile — decreased water sorption, maintained transparency and hardness, increased flexural modulus — despite having the highest pre-thermoforming water uptake. This underscores that in-vitro testing of raw thermoplastic sheets (the design most material SRs reference) may systematically misestimate in-vivo mechanical performance of thermoformed aligners, a methodological limitation worth carrying when interpreting the Delgado/Ferreira material-property evidence above. At the **clinical planning level**, brand differences in virtual treatment setup (VTS) — independent of material properties — are explored by a pilot crossover study: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/telles-2025-clear-aligner-brand-virtual-setup-comparison]] (10 patients, same case submitted to Invisalign, ClearCorrect, 3M Clarity, Spark under a standard prescription). VTS outputs differed between brands for the same patient — number of stages, attachment plans, and predicted endpoint — suggesting that brand selection may influence planned (and therefore achievable) movement before the first aligner is fabricated. Pilot scale (n=10, no blinded outcome assessment) limits conclusions, but the finding is directionally consistent with Saif 2026's first-set-accuracy data and Telles 2025's VTS divergence: the predicted-vs-achieved gap begins at treatment planning, not only at clinical execution.

### 11. Patient expectations & informed consent

A prospective cohort ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/nemec-2026-clear-aligner-patient-needs-expectations]], n=82 adult Invisalign patients, pre-treatment 32-item VAS survey) found patients hold near-ceiling expectations for treatment predictability (8.6/10) and outcome equivalence to fixed appliances (9.5/10), while showing almost no willingness to trade outcome quality for shorter treatment time, lower cost, or fewer auxiliaries (all Willingness-to-Compromise items ≤2.6/10); patients with more severe baseline malocclusion (PAR >17) reported significantly higher pain and diet-restriction concerns. Given the ~54.8% post-treatment dissatisfaction rate the authors cite from prior literature, this expectation-experience gap is directly relevant to axes 1 (efficacy boundary), 5 (expansion under-correction), and 6 (root resorption/bone) above: informed consent for CAT should explicitly calibrate patients' near-ceiling predictability expectations against the movement-specific and severity-specific limitations documented throughout this synthesis, especially for extraction, severe-malocclusion, and expansion cases.

### 12. Patient-Reported Outcomes (PROs), quality of life & compliance — the missing evidence axis now filling in

Until recently the CAT evidence base has been heavy on objective movement accuracy and light on patient experience across the treatment timeline. Six papers now address this gap directly, covering QoL trajectory, pain, compliance, and satisfaction in both simple and complex CAT cases.

The fullest synthesis is a 140-study narrative review of OHRQoL across the entire orthodontic treatment lifecycle ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/chehab-2026-ohrqol-orthodontic-narrative-review]]), which models a **three-phase OHRQoL trajectory**: initial-adaptation (early discomfort and dietary/social interference), stabilization (plateau as adjustment occurs), and completion/retention (peak satisfaction). Specifically for CAT-vs-FA comparisons, **65–75% of comparative studies favored CA** on OHRQoL; pain peaked at 24–48h and resolved within 4–7 days; OHRQoL impairment during active CAT averaged only 8–12% — modest relative to reported tolerance and consistent with the periodontal/comfort findings in axis 7. A companion scoping review ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/chong-2025-patient-experiences-cat-scoping-review]], 37 studies, adults ≥18y) confirms the esthetic/social-perception advantage and exposes the **compliance paradox: only 36% of patients achieved full compliance (≥22h/day)**, yet compliance level was **independent of treatment satisfaction** — patients who experienced wear-burden dissatisfaction remained satisfied with outcomes. This has a direct interpretation for axis 1's wear-protocol findings (Monisha 2024): the 14-day-vs-expedited accuracy difference may be partly self-defeating in practice if expedited schedules improve adherence. The scoping review also identifies research gaps: longitudinal PRO data beyond 18 months are sparse, and compliance with complex auxiliaries (attachments, elastics) is essentially unstudied.

At the individual-study level, a 12-month prospective cohort ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/patil-2026-perception-satisfaction-ca-vs-fa]], n=120, CA vs FA, four time points: baseline/3/6/12 months) found CA superior on esthetics, comfort, and PIDAQ at all four time points, with OHIP-14 **converging by 12 months** (p=0.156) — CA's QoL advantage is front-loaded, not persistent. The CA group showed significantly fewer white-spot lesions (11.7% vs 31.7%, p=0.008), a hygiene-access benefit consistent with axis 7's periodontal-edge data and a durable outcome advantage even as QoL scores equalize. A prospective comparison in **complex cases** ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/hamid-2026-complex-cases-aligners-ohip14]], n=60, complex malocclusion, CA vs FA, PAR + OHIP-14 at four time points) found PAR reduction equivalent between groups but FA achieved faster space closure (extraction mechanics); OHIP-14 favored CA at 1 month but **converged by treatment completion** — qualifying Chehab 2026's 65–75% CA-favorable rate: in extraction/complex cases requiring active retraction mechanics, the QoL window of advantage compresses or disappears.

Two cross-sectional studies bracket the pain-satisfaction relationship. A Brazilian study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/caldas-2024-pain-ohrqol-satisfaction-aligner]], n=94, ClearCorrect, OHIP-14 + satisfaction VAS) found moderate pain significantly elevated OHIP-14 scores (p=0.036) while mild pain did not, yet **treatment satisfaction remained high regardless of pain level** — confirming a pain-satisfaction dissociation consistent with Chong 2025's compliance-satisfaction dissociation. A Portuguese four-group cross-sectional study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/oliveira-2026-daily-life-impact-aligners-vs-fixed]], n=112, four appliance-stage groups, DIDL) found FA patients at the start of treatment had **35.7% dissatisfaction vs 0% dissatisfaction in late-CA patients**, but total DIDL scores were not significantly different between groups at any matched time point — suggesting **adaptation equalizes subjective experience** across appliance types when measured at comparable stages of treatment, with the early divergence reflecting the onset-discomfort phase Chehab 2026 describes.

The combined reading across all six studies: **CAT patients consistently report higher early OHRQoL than FA patients**, especially in esthetics and initial discomfort; this advantage **compresses over time and is essentially absent at treatment completion**, particularly in complex or extraction cases; **white-spot lesion prevention is a durable, not time-limited, CA advantage** (axis 7's hygiene-access finding reconfirmed at 12 months); **compliance below 22h/day is the clinical norm** (36% fully compliant), but compliance per se does not predict satisfaction — so under-compliance should be tracked for accuracy implications (axis 1) rather than inferred from patient-reported satisfaction; and the pain-satisfaction and compliance-satisfaction dissociations together imply that patient satisfaction scores are **poor proxies for actual wear adherence or treatment accuracy** in routine follow-up.

### Clinical bottom line

Choose aligners confidently for **mild-to-moderate, non-extraction, predominantly tipping/alignment cases** in compliant patients who value esthetics, comfort and easier hygiene. Escalate caution — and plan auxiliaries (attachments, TADs, IPR) or consider fixed appliances — for **torque/rotation/extrusion-heavy, extraction, bodily-movement, severe or transverse-skeletal cases**. For extraction cases specifically, screen for Roller Coaster Effect and CoS-leveling-failure risk (ANB, incisor proclination, pre-treatment CoS depth) and for molar-distalization cases, counsel patients on OGE risk pre-treatment and check third-molar status/attachment geometry before staging (axis 4). For rotation specifically, plan by tooth rather than by case: molars track at 74–85% but **canines run at 36–58% with ~4.5° of residual error** (Benedetti 2025), so stage canine derotation below 1.5° per aligner, expect refinement, and do not assume an attachment will rescue it — the rotational benefit of attachments is inconsistent across studies and unproven for "optimized" designs (axis 4). Root resorption and TMJ/masticatory effects are not a deterrent on current evidence; the periodontal edge is real but compliance-dependent, now partly explained at the microbiome level, and — per the umbrella review that sits highest on this axis (Di Spirito 2023) — **small enough (<1 mm, <1 index point) to be clinically negligible and gone by long-term follow-up**, so it should be offered as a short-term hygiene-access advantage rather than a durable periodontal one. Acceleration gadgets are not worth their cost. Patient-reported QoL is higher for CA early in treatment (esthetics, comfort, OHIP-14) but **converges with FA at treatment completion**, especially in complex/extraction cases; white-spot lesion prevention is the one durable CA advantage over FA confirmed through 12 months; **full compliance (≥22h/day) is achieved by only ~36% of patients** — track wear time objectively rather than inferring it from satisfaction scores, because patients who are satisfied may still be under-compliant. Post-surgical CA (after orthognathic surgery) is viable on stability and OHRQoL grounds and offers a time advantage in Surgery-First protocols (alghafli 2026 SR). Class III camouflage/growth-modification with CAT is feasible for mild-to-moderate, low/normo-angle adults per case reports plus a growing base of comparative/cohort data (Rutili's 128-study review, Panda's and Cunha's controlled comparisons, Gok's mixed-dentition RCT), but cede skeletal (ANB) correction to fixed appliances/surgery and counsel accordingly — still an emerging rather than SR+MA-established indication, but no longer case-report-only. Given patients' near-ceiling expectations and near-zero willingness to compromise (axis 11), calibrate informed consent explicitly around the movement-specific and severity-specific limitations above — especially for extraction, severe-malocclusion, and expansion cases.

## Clinical Quiz

**Q1.** 성인 환자의 상악 우측 견치 회전 7°를 투명교정(Clear Aligner Therapy, CAT)으로 계획하고 있습니다. ClinCheck에 7° 회전을 그대로 설계하면 실제로 몇 도가 달성될 가능성이 높습니까? *(근거: Benedetti 2025 회전 SR + Cao 2025 FEA)*

> **모범답안**: **약 36–58%** — 즉 약 2.5–4°가 달성될 가능성이 높다. Benedetti 2025(회전 전용 SR, 12편, attachment 포함, 리파인먼트 전)에서 견치 회전 정확도는 **36–58%(평균 ~47%, 잔여오차 ~4.5°)로 부위 중 최저**였다. 직관과 반대로 견치가 최악인 이유는 치근 단면이 원형에 가까워 aligner가 짝힘(couple)을 걸 지지점이 없기 때문이다(Cao 2025 FEA). Attachment 11편에서도 이득이 불일치하므로 실행 가능한 레버는 **staging 낮추기(<1.5°/aligner)**다. 따라서 설계 시 7°를 목표로 하려면 attachment + 충분한 staging 단계 분산 + 과교정을 계획해야 한다.

**Q2.** 상악 4소구치 발치 후 투명교정으로 공간 폐쇄를 계획합니다. 이 오버뷰에서 전치 치근 이동의 정확도 문제와 임상 대응책은 무엇입니까? *(근거: Wang 2025 CBCT 중첩 + Tang 2025 CoS failure)*

> **모범답안**: Wang 2025(후향연구, 47명·542치아, CBCT 중첩)에서 **치근 이동이 치관 이동보다 항상 과소 달성**됐고, 절치 치근 최대 오차는 5.49mm였다. 더 심각한 것은 전치에서 **계획된 압하(intrusion) 대신 오히려 정출(extrusion)이 발생**한 것이다. Tang 2025의 CoS leveling failure와 함께 발치 CAT의 "이중 생체역학 페널티"를 구성한다. 임상 대응: ① ClinCheck 설계 시 **치근/토크를 과보정(overcorrection)**으로 설계 ② **압하 방향이 계획대로 나오지 않을 가능성**을 염두에 두고 attachment 위치 확인 ③ Vicioni-Marques 2025 SR+MA(발치 공간폐쇄, GRADE 매우 낮음): **전반적 과소 달성** 확인 — 재치료 루틴화와 과교정 설계를 기본값으로 삼는다.

**Q3.** 투명교정이 치주 건강에 고정식 교정(Fixed Appliance, FA)보다 유리하다고 환자에게 설명했습니다. 이 오버뷰에서 이 설명이 얼마나 정확합니까? *(근거: Di Spirito 2023 umbrella review + TNF-α 기전)*

> **모범답안**: **초기 이점은 있으나 "장기적으로 더 좋다"는 설명은 과장이다.** Di Spirito 2023(umbrella review, 4 SR, 2,042명)에서 단·중기에는 PI·GI·SBI·PPD 모두 CAT 통계적 우위였으나(지수 MD −0.14~−1.10), **장기에는 전부 NS** — 저자 결론은 두 방법이 "비교 가능(comparable)"이다. 단기 우위도 **<1mm 또는 <1점으로 임상적으로 미미**하다. Hassan 2024(TNF-α 종적연구)에서 골개조 사이토카인 TNF-α가 전 시점에서 FA > CAT로 유의하게 높아 CAT 치주 우위의 생화학적 기전을 설명하지만, 이것은 "CAT가 치주에 더 좋다"가 아니라 **"단기 위생 접근성 효과이며 compliance 의존적"**으로 읽어야 한다. 동의서 문구: "초기에 위생 관리가 더 유리하나 장기 치주 건강은 두 방법이 비슷합니다."
