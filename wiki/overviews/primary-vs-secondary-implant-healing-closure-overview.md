## Three-line Summary

Chairside question "primary vs secondary healing for implant suturing — which is better?" is answered here by cross-reading the implant open-healing literature separately from the third-molar suture cluster, because the two answer different questions and the second does not transfer.

**Across immediate implant placement (IIP), alveolar ridge preservation (ARP), and membrane-based GBR, the evidence does not support forced tension-free primary closure as a superior default.** One RCT (n=46/48) shows open healing significantly better on the composite PES at 12 months (MD 1.49, 95% CI 0.62–2.36, p=0.0014); one SR+MA (12 studies, 431 patients/438 implants) shows open healing *equivalent* to primary closure on midfacial mucosal level (SMD −0.26, NS) with a mesial-papilla advantage (SMD 0.76, p=0.01) and equivalent MBL, keratinized mucosa width, and survival (92–100%).

The price of primary closure is quantified: in ARP, flap elevation costs **3.21 mm of keratinized gingiva width** (WMD −3.21, 95% CI −4.10 to −2.33, p<0.00001) while bone width and height outcomes are indistinguishable — and in titanium-mesh GBR, periosteal releasing incision (PRI) plus closure produced dehiscence in 41.7–50% versus 8.3% for open non-PRI healing in dogs. What survives as the defensible claim is narrower than "open is better": **the cost-benefit of forcing closure is unfavorable wherever a tension-free seal is achievable without sacrificing soft tissue, and the literature provides no evidence-based ceiling on primary closure where it closes passively.**

## 세줄요약

"임플란트 봉합에 1차(일차) 힐링이 낫나, 2차(개방) 힐링이 낫나"라는 chairside 질문을, 매복 사랑니 봉합 군과 분리해 임플란트 개방치유 문헌만으로 답한다 — 둘은 다른 질문에 답하고 둘째는 임플란트로 전이되지 않는다.

**즉시식립(IIP)·치조제 보존술(ARP)·막 기반 GBR 전체를 관통해, 억지로 만들어낸 무장력 일차봉합이 우위라는 근거는 없다.** RCT 1편(n=46명/48개)에서 개방치유가 12개월 분홍 심미 지수(PES)에서 유의하게 우수(MD 1.49, 95% CI 0.62~2.36, p=0.0014), SR+MA 1편(12편, 431명/438개)에서는 정중협측 점막레벨이 동등(SMD −0.26, NS)하면서 근심 치유두에서 개방 우위(SMD 0.76, p=0.01), 변연골소실·각화점막폭·생존(92~100%)도 동등.

일차봉합의 대가는 정량화된다: ARP에서 판막거상은 골 폭·높이에는 차이가 없으면서(NS) 각화치은폭을 3.21 mm 더 날린다(WMD −3.21, p<0.00001), 티타늄 messages GBR에서는 골막이완절개+봉합이 열개 41.7~50%인 반면 무이완 개방은 8.3%. 방어 가능한 결론은 "개방이 더 낫다"가 아니라 **"무장력 실링이 가능한 자리에서 닫도록 강제하는 것의 비용대비가 불리하며, 수동적으로 닫히는 자리에서 일차봉합의 천장이 없다는 것"**이다.

## Thesis

The primary-vs-secondary closure question is usually framed as a search for the biologically "ideal" wound — primary closure being presumed ideal because a sealed wound is what flap surgery is *for*. The evidence held here does not support that framing, and it does not support the opposite dogma either. It supports something narrower and more useful: **primary closure is not intrinsically protective, and the maneuvers required to manufacture it carry measurable soft-tissue cost.**

Three findings carry that claim.

**First, the hard-tissue outcomes are indistinguishable, so the closure question has to be answered on soft tissue.** In ARP, a meta-analysis isolating flap management as the independent variable found bone width and height preservation did not differ between flapped and flapless approaches — the only outcome that moved was keratinized gingiva width, where flap elevation cost 3.21 mm more (p<0.00001) (Lee 2018). Whatever primary closure buys at the bone level, it is not buying it here. What it costs is measurable and clinically consequential, because keratinized mucosa around a future implant is the tissue reserve that later soft-tissue augmentation depends on.

**Second, at the esthetic-zone immediate implant the composite esthetic outcome actually favors open healing.** Bouffleur 2026 (RCT, n=46 patients/48 implants, Heidelberg) randomized immediate placement to open (n=25) versus closed/submerged healing (n=23) with PES as the primary endpoint at 12 months: open healing scored higher by MD 1.49 (95% CI 0.62–2.36, p=0.0014). Notably the paper's own framing concedes the biological counter-argument — that thin biotype or high volumetric-resorption risk may justify closed healing on grounds PES does not capture.

**Third, where closure is forced, the failure mode moves rather than disappears.** Kim 2026 (RCT, n=40) tested whether adding a tension-relieving horizontal mattress suture reduces wound dehiscence after horizontal GBR; it did not (60% vs 55%, all p>0.05), and the absolute dehiscence rate was high in both arms. In titanium-mesh GBR, Wang 2025 (beagle) found PRI plus closure produced dehiscence in 41.67–50% while open healing *without* PRI produced 8.3% and *gained* 1.77 mm of keratinized gingiva against a 4.37 mm loss in the PRI arms. The tension-relieving incision that exists to enable primary closure is, in that model, itself the dominant exposure risk.

The synthesis, then: **case selection sets the ceiling, and the closure variable mostly redistributes soft tissue rather than improving hard tissue.** The literature's consistent boundary conditions are anatomy-driven — intact buccal wall, adequate keratinized tissue, a crestal wound width within a workable range — not closure-driven.

## Results

### 축 1 — 즉시식립(IIP): 복수 지표에서는 개방이 불리하지 않다

The two highest-grade implant studies split the question across two different outcome axes, and that split is the most important thing this synthesis can say.

| Study | Design | n | Outcome | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/zadikian-2026-open-healing-immediate-implant-mucosa]] | SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42023439073) | 12 studies, 431 pts / 438 implants | **MML (primary)** | SMD −0.26 (95% CI −0.71 to 0.18, p=0.25; 3 RCTs), I²=6% — **no difference** |
| " | " | " | Mesial papilla | SMD 0.76 (95% CI 0.18–1.34), **p=0.01 — favors open** |
| " | " | " | Distal papilla | SMD 0.40 (95% CI −0.16 to 0.96), p=0.16, NS |
| " | " | " | MBL | SMD 0.14 (95% CI −0.32 to 0.59), p=0.55, NS (3 RCTs, I²=10%) |
| " | " | " | Survival | 92–100% over 4–58 months, no group difference |
| [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/bouffleur-2026-open-vs-closed-healing-immediate-implant-rct]] | RCT | 46 pts / 48 implants | **PES (primary, 12 mo)** | Open higher by **MD 1.49 (95% CI 0.62–2.36), p=0.0014** |

Read together: **the SR+MA's designated primary outcome (midfacial mucosal level) is null, while the RCT's designated primary outcome (a 7-parameter composite) favors open.** This is not a contradiction to be resolved — it is a measurement-sensitivity finding of the same species documented elsewhere in this literature. A linear point metric on the midfacial margin is less sensitive to the volumetric and papillary changes that define whether an esthetic-zone implant site looks acceptable; the composite score catches them. The practically important consequence is that **an equivalence demonstrated on linear metrics is not evidence that the two approaches are esthetically equivalent** (cf. the same pattern in the immediate-provisionalization literature, where a linear null became a volumetric difference of 11.9% vs 17.4% contour loss, p=0.04).

Two further caveats on Zadikian 2026 constrain how far the equivalence generalizes, and both are stated by the authors themselves: 92% of the pooled implants were anterior, all were flapless with intact buccal walls, and more than half were followed only through osseointegration (≤6 months) — mean follow-up 10.6 months, with only Cosyn 2011 reaching 3 years. Immediate *functional* loading (<48 h in occlusion) was excluded by design, so the result describes soft-tissue management, not mechanical loading. Dissent within the pool is worth noting too: Perez 2020 found *less* recession with a standard abutment (+0.1 ± 0.5 mm) than with a customized one (−0.2 ± 0.4 mm).

The SR+MA's own framing is the most defensible sentence available on this axis: **omitting primary closure does not destabilize the peri-implant mucosa in selected anterior, intact-buccal-wall cases** — with "selected" doing real work in that sentence.

### 축 2 — 동시 GBR: 초기 창상폭이 임계점이다

When membrane coverage is mandatory (simultaneous GBR at IIP), the open-vs-closed question becomes a measurable threshold rather than a preference. Zhou 2024 (retrospective two-cohort, single center Shanghai, 118 patients: 58 complete closure vs 60 open healing, all Bio-Gide) is the only study in the held set that reports the threshold numerically.

| Initial crestal wound width | Open-healing outcome |
|---|---|
| **≤6 mm** | Fully healed soft tissue by W16 (wound width 5.8 ± 1.3 → **0.1 ± 0.4 mm**); vertical and horizontal bone change **equivalent to complete closure** |
| **≥7 mm** | Cover-screw exposure at W16 in **5/16** patients — *final restorations unaffected* |

Complete-closure arms showed no dehiscence W0→W16, and CBCT bone change did not differ between groups. Histology in 16 open-healing patients showed keratinized mucosa and new bone over the implant.

The clinical translation is unusually crisp: **below ~6 mm of crestal exposure, flap release and connective-tissue grafting are an avoidable morbidity cost.** Above it, the open route still completed — the exposure was managed without compromising the restoration — but the safety margin narrows. Note the design caveat: these were sequentially-treated cohorts (pre-2020 protocol vs post-2020 protocol), so protocol and calendar time are perfectly confounded, and the direction of any secular trend in technique cannot be separated from the closure strategy itself.

Supporting this from a different material: Deng 2024 (retrospective, 40 posterior immediate implants) compared two *open-wound sealing* strategies — autologous reactive soft tissue vs absorbable collagen sponge — and found them equivalent on total soft-tissue healing score, gingival recession, horizontal bone width, and MBL at ~6 months, with only divergent sub-scores (ACS better on tissue colour, p=0.016; RST better on fibrous repair, p=0.043). Its contribution is legitimacy rather than ranking: **if a flap-to-flap primary seal is not feasible over a fresh posterior socket, an open wound with a deliberate seal is a reasonable alternative to the traditional ideal.**

### 축 3 — 일차봉합의 대가: 각화치은 −3.21 mm

This is the quantified price tag, and it is the axis on which the closure decision genuinely moves an outcome.

Lee 2018 (SR+MA, IJOMI; 11 studies — 9 RCTs + 2 CCTs) isolated flap management in ARP and pooled three outcomes. Bone width change: no significant difference. Bone height change: no significant difference. **Keratinized gingiva width change: WMD −3.21 mm (95% CI −4.10 to −2.33, p<0.00001; I²≈51%) favoring flapless.**

The implication is that the closure variable is worth choosing on keratinized tissue, because on bone it is worth nothing measurable. This is corroborated in the IIP literature at the flap-vs-flapless level: Pitman 2023 (SR+MA, RCT-only, Cosyn group) found flapless favored horizontal buccal hard- and soft-tissue volume preservation, with clinical and esthetic differences small and largely operator-dependent, and concluded that flap elevation plus GBR remains indicated when buccal defects are present — i.e. the indication for a flap is *defect-driven*, not *closure-driven*.

The same mechanism shows up in the third-molar/GBR suture cluster from the opposite direction, and the wiki's own [[suture-wound-closure/suture-wound-closure]] category records it as an explicit scope item ("primary vs secondary closure"): Kim 2026 (RCT, n=40) added a tension-relieving horizontal mattress suture to interrupted sutures in horizontal GBR and found no significant difference in dehiscence width (2.92 vs 2.17 mm), length (5.32 vs 4.37 mm), or incision-ratio (0.42 vs 0.34) at 2 weeks — with high absolute dehiscence in both arms (60% vs 55%), attributable to an elderly Veterans cohort (mean age ~72), a non-fixed acellular dermal matrix membrane, and a sensitive measurement context. **If you are going to close, the pattern of suturing is not the lever; tension-free apposition is.**

### 축 4 — 개방치유 프로토콜 자체의 임상 성립

A recommendation for open healing is only as good as the evidence that open healing works at all. That evidence is not thin.

| Study | Design | n | Long-term result |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/ionescu-2022-open-healing-flapless-ridge-preservation]] | Prospective case series | 104 pts, 135 teeth, **5-yr** | Survival **98.5%**, success **94.8%** (26 immediate, 109 delayed); CBCT 6-mo confirmed 3D ridge preservation in width and height; uneventful healing despite membrane exposure |
| [[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/friedmann-2026-arp-crosslinked-xenograft-open-healing-case-series]] | Prospective case series | 49 pts, 62 sites | All 62 healed without complication; soft-tissue closure by secondary intention at 3–4 weeks; **72% of implant-designated sites needed no additional augmentation**; 1-yr survival **98.2%**; late (>6 mo) reentry biopsy showed complete xenograft remodeling |
| [[bone-regeneration/ridge-preservation/kim-2024-collagenated-xenograft-arp-rct]] | Single-blind 3-arm RCT (Seoul National Univ.) | 50 completed, 180 d | DBBM-C groups maintained ridge volume at 3 mm below crest over days 84–180 (**+0.72%**) while spontaneous-healing controls lost volume (**−2.10%**, p=0.026); all groups ISQ ≥70 |

Two qualifications belong with these numbers. Ionescu's 94.8% *success* against 98.5% *survival* is a 3.7-point gap — a real complication burden sits behind that headline, and the "uneventful healing" phrasing should not be read as zero morbidity. And Friedmann 2026 is an uncontrolled case series with an explicit inclusion threshold (≥50% residual buccal bone); its comparative claim is unavailable by design. Its distinctive contribution is material rather than technical — a sugar-crosslinked collagen/hydroxyapatite sponge that **completely** remodeled on late reentry, which contrasts with the 20.49% pooled residual xenograft content reported by Benekou 2025 (SR+MA, 22 RCTs, n=816).

That contrast is worth stating plainly, because it qualifies the open-healing ladder in [[open-healing-arp-technique-variables-overview]]: **the scaffold you leave open is not a neutral variable.** Leaving a slowly-resorbing xenograft exposed without primary closure risks locking socket space — Benekou's histology puts xenograft + membrane at −22.47% new bone versus spontaneous healing (p=0.004), with PRF/PRP at +16.28% (p<0.0001). A material designed to fully resorb removes that concern; a slow-resorbing one does not.

The animal evidence runs the same direction. Wang 2025 (beagle, randomized block, 4 protocols, ~12 sites/group) tested whether a flap can heal over a clean titanium-membrane surface:

| Group | Protocol | Dehiscence | KG width change |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Bio-Oss + Ti-mem + Bio-Gide + PRI, closed | 50% | — |
| B | A + Bio-Oss over mesh, PRI, closed | 41.67% | −4.37 ± 0.45 mm |
| **C** | B but **no PRI, open healing** | **8.3% (1 site)** | **+1.77 ± 0.37 mm** |
| D | Ti-mem + Bio-Gide, no PRI, closed | 8.3% | — |

Group C's gingiva was also histologically superior. This challenges a near-universal habit — cutting the periosteum to obtain tension-free primary closure — on the grounds that PRI narrows keratinized gingiva, shallows the vestibule, interrupts periosteal blood supply, and that Ti-mesh exposure is reported up to ~51% in the literature anyway. It is short-term animal data and needs human confirmation.

### 축 5 — 이환율 축: 임플란트로 전이되지 않는다

The `suture-wound-closure/` category contains a third-molar cluster that looks directly on-point and is **not** an implant study. It is reported here explicitly as a boundary rather than borrowed as support.

- [[suture-wound-closure/pachipulusu-2018-primary-secondary-closure-third-molar]] (RCT, n=60, 30/arm): secondary closure gave significantly less pain and swelling and greater mouth opening (p<0.05), with no difference in periodontal healing at 6 months (alveolar osteitis in 1 secondary-closure patient, 3.3%). The frontmatter records a `contradicts` edge to Takadoum.
- [[suture-wound-closure/takadoum-2022-sutureless-socket-technique-third-molars]] (multicentric open RCT, 3 French hospitals, CONSORT-compliant, n=94 analyzed): **no difference in Day-3 pain (p=0.904) or any secondary outcome** — swelling, trismus, painkillers, healing, complications, quality of life; operating time shorter without sutures. Smoking independently raised complication risk 3.65-fold (p=0.0244).

The wiki's own reading of this cluster — recorded in [[suture-wound-closure/suture-wound-closure]] and its ladder [[suture-wound-closure/suture-wound-closure-decision-ladder]] — is that the correct interpretation is **"omitting sutures does no harm," not "sutureless is superior."** The larger adequately-powered trial sets the equivalence baseline against which the single-center positives should be read.

Two things follow for implant practice. First, **no implant study in this set demonstrates a morbidity reduction from leaving the wound open** — the implant literature measures esthetic and dimensional outcomes, not pain and swelling. Any chairside claim that open healing is more comfortable in implant surgery is currently unsupported here. Second, the transferable item is a risk modifier, not a technique: **smoking raises complication risk 3.65-fold regardless of closure strategy**, which makes smoking cessation a higher-yield intervention than the closure choice itself. That same cluster also flags an important population boundary — the sutureless RCTs *excluded* antiplatelet/anticoagulant users and coagulopathy patients, so "omitting sutures is harmless" must not be extrapolated to them, where suturing is hemostasis in its own right.

A genuinely implant-specific soft-tissue result does exist, and it favors the suture-free direction on a different outcome: Im 2026 (RCT, n=30, posterior maxilla and mandible) compared suture-free closure using a healing abutment attachment against conventional suturing and found significantly less keratinized tissue width loss (−0.46 ± 0.71 vs −1.23 ± 0.95 mm at implant level, p<0.05), greater supracrestal tissue height gain (0.95 ± 0.47 vs 0.59 ± 0.67 mm, p<0.05), and 4.5-fold shorter flap stabilization time (43.73 vs 198.27 s, p<0.001). Small, single technique, but the keratinized-tissue direction matches Lee 2018's SR+MA.

## Decision ladder

The question has one upstream variable: **does the wound close passively, or does closing it require creating tension?** Everything else branches off that.

| Decision | Recommendation | Evidence / trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Esthetic-zone IIP, intact buccal wall, medium/thick biotype** | **Open healing / flapless favored** | Bouffleur 2026 PES MD 1.49 (p=0.0014); Zadikian 2026 MML NS, mesial papilla favors open. Caveat: requires a transmucosal seal (provisional or customized healing abutment) |
| **Simultaneous GBR, initial crestal wound ≤6 mm** | **Open OK — skip flap release and CTG** | Zhou 2024: full closure by W16 (5.8 → 0.1 mm), bone change equivalent to complete closure |
| **Simultaneous GBR, initial wound ≥7 mm** | Complete closure worth considering | Zhou 2024: cover-screw exposure 5/16 at ≥7 mm (final restorations unaffected) |
| **Thin buccal wall / dehiscence** | **Do not hold it open — flap + GBR, or stage to ARP + delayed placement** | Pitman 2023: flap + GBR indicated when buccal defects present; case selection sets the esthetic ceiling |
| **ARP, thick buccal bone** | Flapless / open healing | Lee 2018: bone equivalent, KGW preserved; Khehra 2025 (n=20) BG-alone ≈ BG + collagen membrane in thick buccal bone |
| **ARP, material choice under an open seal** | Prefer a fully-resorbing scaffold; counsel on residual xenograft burden | Benekou 2025: xenograft + membrane −22.47% new bone, 20.49% residual content; Friedmann 2026 SCLC/HA fully remodeled |
| **Posterior extraction socket** | Open/flapless acceptable, but counsel that horizontal loss is unavoidable | Arroteia 2025: horizontal resorption significant in all four arms |
| **Titanium-mesh vertical GBR** | Reconsider routine PRI + closure | Wang 2025 (animal): open non-PRI dehiscence 8.3% vs 41.67–50%; KG +1.77 vs −4.37 mm |
| **Smoker** | Smoking cessation outranks the closure choice | Takadoum 2022: 3.65× complication risk independent of closure (third-molar data, but a risk modifier not a technique) |
| **Anticoagulated / coagulopathy** | Do not extrapolate "sutureless is safe" here | Takadoum 2022 and Sen 2024 excluded these patients; suturing is a hemostatic maneuver in its own right |
| **If you are closing anyway** | Optimize for tension-free apposition, not suture pattern | Kim 2026: mattress supplementation did not reduce dehiscence (60% vs 55%) |

**The one-line rule:** *close it if it closes without tension; if closing it requires a releasing incision, the cost of that incision is at least as large as the benefit of the closure.* And where a deliberate open seal is the plan, choose a scaffold and a transmucosal seal deliberately — the open label itself is not a free pass on material selection.

## Grey zones and reverse evidence

**The evidence grade is 🟡 Moderate, not 🟢, and the split is structural.** The SR+MA's designated primary outcome is null (MML SMD −0.26, p=0.25); the RCT's designated primary outcome favors open (PES MD 1.49, p=0.0014). One RCT at n=48, single-center, 12-month, with no long-term esthetic follow-up, cannot outrank a 12-study meta-analysis — but neither is outranked, because they measure different things. **The honest synthesis is "no evidence-based ceiling on primary closure where it closes passively, and an unfavorable cost-benefit where it must be forced,"** not "open healing is better."

**Design weaknesses concentrate in the direct comparisons.** Zhou 2024 is a retrospective two-cohort design with protocol perfectly confounded with calendar time; Deng 2024 is retrospective with n=40 and its full text is paywalled beyond the abstract; the Ionescu and Friedmann series have no control arms. Bouffleur 2026 is the only randomized implant evidence, and the [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/bouffleur-2026-open-vs-closed-healing-immediate-implant-rct]] wiki page has its Results table still carrying a `[확인]` placeholder for implant survival and volumetric change.

**A sign-convention error is present in the wiki record.** The Bouffleur page reports the PES difference as both "MD 1.49 (95% CI 0.62–2.36)" and "mean difference −1.49 favoring open (95% CI −2.36 to −0.62)". The PDF abstract reads *"The open healing group showed significantly higher PES outcomes compared to the closed healing group (mean difference: −1.49; 95% CI: [−2.36, −0.62]; p = 0.0014)"* — the negative sign reflects a closed-minus-open coding. **The direction is unambiguous (open higher); the magnitude is 1.49 PES points.** Anyone quoting the sign should normalize it.

**An open wound's counter-argument is conceded by the open-healing proponents themselves.** Bouffleur 2026's own summary states that thin biotype or high volumetric-resorption risk may still justify closed healing on biological grounds PES does not capture. Zadikian 2026 excludes immediate *functional* loading by design, so it speaks only to soft-tissue management. Neither result licenses open healing in a thin-walled, high-resorption site.

**Three gaps in the held evidence, named rather than papered over:**

1. **No implant RCT demonstrates that open healing reduces infection or major complications.** The implant literature measures esthetic and dimensional outcomes. The morbidity evidence lives entirely in the third-molar cluster, where the largest trial found equivalence (Takadoum 2022) and the smaller ones found advantage (Pachipulusu 2018) — a genuine unresolved conflict, flagged by a `contradicts` edge between those two pages.
2. **No data beyond 12 months on esthetic outcome after open healing.** The specific worry a clinician has about leaving a buccal dehiscence open — whether the mucosal margin ultimately reconstitutes or leaves a residual concavity — is not addressed by any study in this set.
3. **No stratified analysis by Seibert socket class.** A socket with buccal tissue deficiency (class III) is biologically a different problem from an intact socket, and no study here separates them. Combined with the exclusion of coagulopathy patients from the sutureless trials, these are the two populations where "close it" is most likely to remain the default for reasons this evidence base cannot address.

**Finally, the site sets the ceiling, not the closure technique.** The consensus running through [[immediate-implant-conditions]], [[immediate-implant-esthetic-soft-tissue-overview]], and [[open-healing-arp-technique-variables-overview]] is the same: bundle-bone loss is unavoidable regardless of placement timing or closure strategy; thin buccal walls lose a median 7.5 mm vertically at 8 weeks versus 1.1 mm for thick walls; and the controllable levers are palatal positioning, gap grafting, soft-tissue grafting where the phenotype is thin, and immediate provisionalization when stability permits. Closure strategy is a real but secondary decision — the one that governs **keratinized tissue**, not the one that governs survival.
