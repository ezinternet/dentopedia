## Three-line Summary

Three oral surgery complication domains synthesized — CHX for alveolar osteitis (SR+MA, 23 RCTs, n=2,824), saline irrigation during oral cancer surgery (prospective cohort, n=104), and orthognathic surgery nerve injury treatment (retrospective cohort, n=287).

CHX reduced alveolar osteitis by 47% (RR=0.53, NNT=8); saline irrigation cut tumor-cell wound contamination from 55% to 7.6%; LLLT produced 75% subjective nerve-recovery improvement vs 33% without treatment.

Each axis confirms that low-cost adjuncts (CHX gel, isotonic saline wash, laser therapy) substantially reduce morbidity — all lack head-to-head comparison with alternatives and would benefit from prospective standardization.

## 세줄요약

3개 구강외과 합병증 주제 종합: ① CHX·건조치조(SR+MA, 23 RCT, n=2,824), ② 구강암 수술 식염수 세정(전향적 코호트, n=104), ③ 악교정 수술 신경손상 치료(후향적 코호트, n=287).

CHX는 건조치조와(Alveolar Osteitis, AO) 발생 47% 감소 (RR=0.53, NNT=8); 생리식염수 세정은 종양세포 오염률 55%→7.6%로 감소; 저출력레이저치료(LLLT)는 주관적 신경 회복률 75%(무치료 33%) 달성.

세 축 모두 저비용 보조요법이 상당한 합병증 감소를 보이나, 표준화된 비교 대조군이 부족하고 전향적 연구가 필요하다는 한계를 공유.

## Summary

This synthesis covers three clinically distinct but thematically unified problems in oral surgery: prevention of alveolar osteitis after third molar extraction using chlorhexidine; reduction of wound contamination during oral cavity cancer resection using saline irrigation; and management of trigeminal nerve injury after orthognathic surgery using pharmacologic and physical modalities. All three axes share a common finding — a simple, low-cost adjunct markedly reduces the complication burden — and all three would benefit from higher-quality prospective data.

The existing `oral-surgery-decision-ladder` overview covers 22 papers on third molar IAN risk, post-extraction antibiotic prophylaxis, PRF and LLLT after extraction, hemostasis protocols, and deep neck infection. The present synthesis does NOT overlap: it specifically addresses CHX for dry socket prevention (not covered in the decision ladder, which references AO management rather than CHX prevention evidence), saline irrigation in oral cancer surgery (absent from the decision ladder), and orthognathic nerve injury treatment (distinct from third-molar IAN injury covered in the decision ladder).

## Axis 1 — Chlorhexidine for Alveolar Osteitis Prevention

**Source**: [[oral-surgery/rodriguez-sanchez-2017-chlorhexidine-alveolar-osteitis-third]] — Rodríguez Sánchez et al. 2017, SR+MA, JOMS

Alveolar osteitis (dry socket) occurs in approximately 20–30% of mandibular third molar extractions and is characterized by breakdown of the blood clot, severe pain, and delayed healing. Chlorhexidine in various forms has been proposed as a prophylactic adjunct.

### Evidence Summary

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Studies included | 23 RCTs (18 trials, 23 publications) |
| Total extraction sites | 2,824 (1,458 CHX / 1,366 control) |
| Overall RR for AO | 0.53 (95% CI 0.45–0.62) |
| NNT | 8 (CI 7–11) |
| Heterogeneity I² | 9.3% (low) |
| Adverse effects vs placebo | No significant difference |

### Formulation Comparison

| Formulation | RR | 95% CI | Clinical note |
|---|---|---|---|
| CHX rinse (mouthwash) | 0.58 | 0.47–0.71 | Convenient, widely available |
| CHX gel (local application) | 0.47 | 0.37–0.60 | Slightly superior; topical retention advantage |

### Key Clinical Points

- **No threshold effect by concentration or timing**: any standard CHX formulation applied peri-operatively reduces AO risk. Clinician choice of rinse vs gel can be guided by patient preference and availability, with gel preferred when AO risk is high.
- **Effect is consistent**: I²=9.3% confirms the finding replicates across diverse surgical settings and patient populations.
- **NNT=8** means 8 patients treated with CHX prevent one case of AO — a clinically meaningful benefit given AO's significant pain burden and the need for repeated appointments.
- **Safety**: CHX causes no more adverse events than placebo in this context. Staining and taste effects (common at higher doses for gingivitis indications) were not a significant study endpoint here.

### Decision Branch

```
Surgical third molar extraction planned
    │
    ├── High AO risk (mandibular, impacted, smoker, OCP use, previous AO)
    │       → CHX gel preferred (RR 0.47); consider perioperative rinse also
    │
    └── Standard/low AO risk
            → CHX rinse sufficient (RR 0.58); flexible scheduling
```

## Axis 2 — Saline Irrigation to Reduce Wound Contamination in Oral Cancer Surgery

**Source**: [[oral-surgery/shandilya-2025-saline-irrigation-oral-cancer-surgery]] — Shandilya et al. 2025, prospective cohort, Asian Pacific Journal of Cancer Prevention

After tumour resection and neck dissection for oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma, residual exfoliated epithelial and abnormal cells in the wound pose a theoretical risk of implantation metastasis. The standard practice of glove/instrument change and wound irrigation after extirpation has lacked rigorous cytological evidence.

### Contamination Reduction Data

| Measure | Pre-wash (cell block) | Post-wash (smear) |
|---|---|---|
| Epithelial/abnormal cells | 24% (25/104) | 0% |
| Cellular debris | 21% (22/104) | 7.6% (8/104) |
| **Combined positivity rate** | **55% (57/104)** | **7.6% (8/104)** |
| Absolute reduction | — | **47.4 percentage points** |
| Statistical significance | — | chi²=29.8, **p<0.001** |

### Clinico-Pathological Predictors of Lavage Positivity

| Variable | Significance |
|---|---|
| Perineural invasion (PNI) | **p=0.037** (significant) |
| T-stage | NS |
| N-stage | NS |
| Tumour site | NS |
| Grade, LVI, WPOI, margin, DOI | All NS |

### Key Clinical Points

- **Mechanism is purely mechanical**: isotonic normal saline has no cytotoxic effect — the reduction is achieved through physical dilution and removal of exfoliated cells, confirming that 500–1,500 mL saline is sufficient without adding antiseptics or chemotherapy agents.
- **PNI patients warrant more thorough irrigation**: the only predictor of post-wash residual contamination, implying more aggressive local spread. Although no volume threshold by PNI status was tested, clinicians should be attentive to thoroughness of irrigation in PNI-positive cases.
- **Comparison with published head/neck series**: contamination percentages in other series ranged 7.5–26% malignant and 13–19% suspicious — the present pre-wash 55% combined positivity is consistent with that range, and post-wash 7.6% matches lower-bound benchmarks (chi²=3.35, p=0.764 for equivalence with existing literature).
- **Study limitation**: single Indian tertiary center; no immunohistochemical staining (cell block cytology ~85–90% accuracy); no follow-up data on implantation metastasis rates as an endpoint.

### Practical Protocol

```
Oral cavity SCC resection + neck dissection complete
    │
    ├── Change gloves and instruments (break scrub)
    │
    └── Irrigate wound with 500–1,500 mL normal saline
            │
            ├── Standard case: 500–700 mL adequate
            └── PNI-positive case: ensure thorough lavage across entire cavity
```

## Axis 3 — Treatment of Orthognathic Surgery-Related Trigeminal Nerve Injury

**Source**: [[oral-surgery/yaniv-klein-2026-orthognathic-surgery-nerve-injuries-treatment]] — Yaniv-Klein et al. 2026, retrospective cohort, JOFPH

Orthognathic surgery involves osteotomies in close proximity to the inferior alveolar nerve (IAN/CN V3) and, to a lesser extent, the infraorbital nerve (CN V2). Neurosensory deficit is a recognized complication but incidence in large cohorts and comparative treatment data are sparse.

### Incidence and Distribution

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Overall neurosensory deficit incidence | **5.9%** (17/287, 95% CI 3.7–9.3%) |
| Mandibular (CN V3) distribution | **88.2%** (15/17) |
| Maxillary (CN V2) distribution | 11.8% (2/17) |
| Procedures with higher risk | BSSO, BSSO+LeFort-I combinations |
| Median operative time, affected vs cohort | 230 min vs 198 min |

### Treatment Response Comparison

| Treatment | n | Subjective improvement | Full restoration |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLLT (970 nm diode, 6–8 sessions) | 8 | **75%** (6/8) | 0% |
| No treatment | 9 | 33% (3/9) | 0% |
| Preoperative dexamethasone (prophylactic) | 14/17 | — | 0% |
| Vitamin B complex (adjunct, 1 case) | 1 | — | 0% |

### LLLT Parameters Used

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Wavelength | 970 nm (diode) |
| Power | 2 W continuous mode |
| Duration per point | 10 seconds |
| Frequency | 20 Hz |
| Energy density | ~10–20 J/cm² per point |
| Session schedule | 6–8 sessions over 3–4 weeks |

### Key Clinical Points

- **Incidence 5.9% is clinically significant**: pre-surgical informed consent must explicitly include trigeminal neurosensory deficit as a known risk. Mandibular procedures (BSSO, IVRO) carry higher risk than LeFort-I alone.
- **LLLT is the most promising available modality**: 75% subjective improvement vs 33% without treatment is a clinically meaningful difference, though the retrospective design, small n (17 affected), and absence of randomization prevent causal inference. LLLT should be offered early post-operatively where available.
- **No modality achieves complete sensory restoration**: patients should be counseled that improvement, not cure, is the realistic goal. Spontaneous partial recovery also occurs (33% no-treatment arm).
- **Operative time as surrogate**: longer operative time (230 vs 198 min median) in affected patients suggests neural traction during prolonged retraction may be a contributing mechanism — a modifiable risk factor worth attention.
- **No standardized protocol exists**: this is an explicit finding of the study and mirrors the gap documented in [[oral-surgery/lee-2015-inferior-alveolar-nerve-injury-management]] for third-molar IAN injury. Prospective RCTs are needed.
- **Early intervention principle**: while not formally tested in this cohort, the broader neuroregenerative literature supports earlier onset of physical/laser therapy to leverage the window of maximal plasticity.

### Decision Branch

```
Post-orthognathic neurosensory deficit detected
    │
    ├── Immediate: reassess surgical site; rule out haematoma/oedema compression
    │       → if compressive cause suspected: imaging ± early decompression
    │
    └── No compressive cause (typical neuropraxia/axonotmesis)
            │
            ├── Perioperative dexamethasone (if not already given: prophylactic dose 20 mg)
            │
            ├── Initiate LLLT early (970 nm, 6–8 sessions over 3–4 wk) — best available evidence
            │
            ├── Consider vitamin B complex (B1/B2/B3/B6/B12 oral × 30 d) — low risk, adjunctive
            │
            └── Monitor at 3, 6, 12 months with standardized sensory testing
```

## Cross-Axis Themes

| Theme | Axis 1 (CHX) | Axis 2 (Saline) | Axis 3 (LLLT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence level | SR+MA (strongest) | Prospective cohort | Retrospective cohort |
| Cost of intervention | Very low | Negligible | Moderate (device) |
| Mechanism | Antimicrobial + anti-inflammatory | Mechanical cell removal | Photobiomodulation |
| Outcome achieved | 47% AO risk reduction | 87% contamination reduction | 75% symptomatic improvement |
| Standardized protocol? | Yes (any CHX formulation) | Yes (500–1,500 mL NS) | No — parameters vary |
| Need for further RCT? | Low (SR+MA already strong) | Moderate (single center, no endpoint metastasis) | High (no RCT data yet) |

All three interventions share the property of being **additive to standard care** — none replaces the primary surgical technique, each layers on top of it to reduce a known complication class.
