## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 17편(동물·전향·RCT·SR·SR+MA·in-vitro) 종합. 탈단백 우골 광물질(Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral, DBBM)은 탁월한 골전도 공간유지 재료이나 흡수가 느려 신생골 형성이 열등하다 — 부피 안정성과 신생골 형성의 trade-off가 핵심.
> - **세포 수준 기전 근거(1999)**: 무기질 우골(Anorganic Bovine Bone, ABB, DBBM의 원형)이 조골세포 부착·증식을 지지한다는 최초 정량 시험관 증거(Stephan 1999) + PDGF-BB가 ABB 흡착 후에도 생물학적 활성을 유지해 조골세포 증식을 유의 촉진(IGF-I는 무효, Jiang 1999) — Axis 1의 "골전도 지지체" 명제의 세포생물학적 토대.
> - **같은 "이종골" 라벨도 조성 따라 치유 속도 다르다**: 콜라겐 함량이 다른 두 우골 이종골(Bio-Oss Collagen ~10% 콜라겐 vs Extra Graft XG13 ~25% 콜라겐)을 쥐 두개관 결손에서 직접 비교 시 14·28일 신생골·BV/TV 모두 XG13이 유의 우세(Sousa 2026) — 초기 염증·혈관신생은 동등한데 후기 결과가 갈림. Li 2023(Axis 8, 입자크기)은 "같은 재료 내 변수는 무해"를 보였지만, Sousa 2026은 "다른 제품 간 조성 차이는 유해할 수 있다"는 반대 방향 신호 — 제품명이 아니라 조성표를 확인해야 하는 이유.
> - 역설적 소견: DBBM 단독은 초기 신생골을 오히려 억제할 수 있다 — 쥐 두개골 4주 모델에서 DBBM 단독이 무이식(공백) 대조군보다 신생골 적음(p=0.025, Fujioka-Kobayashi 2022).
> - 이상성 인산칼슘(Biphasic Calcium Phosphate, BCP)이 DBBM보다 신생골↑·잔존입자↓ — 토끼 6주서 신생골 +70%·잔존 −60%(Chakar 2014); 상악동 RCT SR+MA서 신생골 +3.48%·잔존 −8.41%(Alkandari 2025).
> - 최고 근거 보조제는 백혈구-혈소판풍부피브린(L-PRF): DBBM에 추가 시 신생골 +7.07%·잔존 −7.93%(잔존 I²=0%, 기전적 일관성, Almutairi 2025 SR+MA 5 RCT).
> - **혈소판 농축물은 계열 효과가 아니라 아형 특이적**: 더 큰 SR+MA(Zhang 2025, 16편 n=372)서 PRP·PRF·PRGF 통합 시 신생골 +5.92%(p=0.002)·잔존 −1.93(p<0.001)이나, 잔존 graft 감소는 PRF·PRGF에서만 유의하고 **PRP는 유의하지 않음** — "혈소판 농축물이 좋다"를 "PRF/PRGF가 좋다"로 정밀화.
> - 진보된 PRF(A-PRF)는 단일 RCT서 더 큰 raw 효과(신생골 42.8 vs 31.2%, Motimath 2026) — 저속 원심분리(2700 rpm) 가설. 단 직접 비교 RCT 없음.
> - PRF 효과는 histomorphometry에 국한: L-PRF도 PRP·PRF·PRGF 전체도 임플란트 안정성(ISQ/RFA)·생존율에는 유의 효과 없음(Almutairi 2025, Zhang 2025) — 조직 소견 개선이 곧 조기 loading 근거는 아님.
> - **입자 크기는 재료 선택 기준이 아니다**: 상악동거상술 DBBM 대입자(1–2mm) vs 소입자(0.25–1mm) SR+MA(Li 2023, 4 RCT n=94)서 결합조직·신생골·잔존이식재 모두 유의차 없음 — 입자 크기는 술식 편의(수술시간·void space) 변수일 뿐 조직학적 결과를 좌우하지 않음.
> - 콜라겐 변형(C-DBBM, 95% DBBM + 5% bovine collagen) ARP에서 식립 시 골유도재생술(GBR) 0/10 필요(Van Orten 2024); porcine-free(할랄) 요구에도 부합.
> - **이종골 vs 동종골 등가**: 단일 파일럿 RCT(Rao 2026, ACBP vs DBBM 동등)를 넘어, 12편 SR(Abushama 2025, n=395, 즉시·지연식립·ARP·상악동 전반)이 변연골·생존·조직학 모두 유의차 없음을 확인 — 재료 선택은 효능이 아니라 비용·가용성·환자 수용성 문제.
> - **장기 안전성은 별개 endpoint**: 임플란트 생존 성공이 장기 비흡수·이물반응을 배제하지 못한다 — SR(Inchingolo 2025, 11편)이 GRADE moderate(생존) vs LOW(장기 합병증)로 갈리며, 우골 이종골 입자가 2–13년 뒤 이동·만성염증·구강상악동 누공·낭종을 일으킨 증례를 종합. 자가골이 여전히 gold standard.
> - **돼지골(PBX) ≈ 소골(BBX)**: SR+MA(Wang 2025, 10편 452 sites, MSFA+ARP) — 신생골 WMD=1.5 (p=0.321, NS), 잔존이식재·결합조직·용적감소 모두 NS → PBX가 BBX의 임상적 대안; 소 유래 재료에 종교·문화적 제약이 있는 진료 환경에서 의미.
 - **한국산 DBBM-C(OCS-B Collagen®) vs Bio-Oss Collagen® 첫 직접 비교 전임상**: 비글견(n=13) 하악 표준화 결손 모델(10×3×4 mm)에서 두 콜라겐화 이종골 이식재 (Collagenated Xenograft)를 micro-CT·조직형태계측·공명주파수분석(RFA) 8주·16주·임플란트 식립 후 24주 비교 — 골부피(BV)·BV/TV·골-임플란트 접촉률(BIC)·임플란트 안정성 지수(ISQ) 전 지표 군간 유의차 없음; OCS-B.C의 Bio-Oss.C 대비 비열등성(Non-Inferiority) 확인. 콜라겐 가교화 차이(OCS-B.C = gelation 가교, Bio-Oss.C = 비가교)는 결과에 영향 없음(Lee 2025, beagle, Scientific Reports).
> - **콜라겐의 골재생 역할 기전(Li 2025 narrative-review)**: 제1형 콜라겐(Col-I)은 수산화인회석(Hydroxyapatite, HA) 결정 핵형성 템플릿; 공급원(소·돼지·해양·재조합/GEPP) 및 가공 형태(스캐폴드·멤브레인·하이드로젤·스폰지) 체계화; rhBMP-2/흡수성 콜라겐 스폰지(ACS)가 FDA 승인 핵심 사례; 장벽 — 기계적 강도 부족·혈관화 미흡·면역반응.
> - 가장 임상적으로 강한 음성 신호: 동시 교정이동(OTM) 부위에 DBBM/DBB 단독 금기 — 비흡수성 입자가 치근흡수를 공백 대조군의 1.87배로 유발(Jiang 2020); BMP2 기능화 CaP(BioCaP)가 대안.
> - 임상 결정: 상악동거상술 routine 1차는 DBBM(장기 임플란트 생존 데이터 우위), PRF/PRGF 추가는 비용 대비 합리적(PRP는 근거 약함), BCP는 신생골 속도가 우선일 때, 다년 비흡수가 특히 우려되는 환자에서는 동종골/빠른 turnover 재료로 소폭 기울 여지. BCP는 단일 재료가 아니라 이질성 높음(I²=86%) 주의.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 19 papers (animal models, in-vitro studies, prospective series, RCTs, SRs, SR+MAs) on DBBM and bone substitute adjuncts: DBBM's slow resorption preserves volume but can paradoxically suppress early new bone formation (DBBM alone < empty control at 4 weeks, p=0.025, Fujioka-Kobayashi 2022); BCP (60% HA/40% β-TCP) consistently outperforms DBBM on new bone in rabbit models (+70%, Chakar 2014) and in clinical sinus SR+MA (+3.48% new bone, −8.41% residual graft, Alkandari 2025); DBBM particle size (large 1–2 mm vs small 0.25–1 mm) does NOT affect histomorphometry in sinus elevation (all outcomes NS, Li 2023 SR+MA), so it is not a material-selection lever.

Platelet-concentrate adjuncts are subtype-specific, not a class effect: L-PRF+DBBM yields +7.07% new bone and −7.93% residual graft (I²=0% for residual graft, Almutairi 2025 SR+MA, 5 RCTs), and the broader PRP/PRF/PRGF SR+MA (Zhang 2025, 16 studies, n=372) confirms +5.92% new bone / −1.93 residual graft overall but shows the residual-graft reduction is driven by PRF/PRGF and NOT replicated by PRP; neither L-PRF nor any platelet concentrate improves implant stability (ISQ/RFA) or survival; DBBM is contraindicated as a solo graft in sites undergoing simultaneous orthodontic tooth movement (root resorption 1.87× control, Jiang 2020).

Clinical bottom line: routine sinus augmentation should use DBBM as first line (best long-term implant survival data), with PRF/PRGF addition as a reasonable low-cost adjunct (PRP evidence is weaker); BCP is preferred when faster new bone formation matters; xenograft and allograft are broadly interchangeable across indications (Abushama 2025 SR, n=395; Rao 2026 pilot RCT) with material choice driven by cost/availability/patient acceptance; but implant survival is a different endpoint from long-term safety — bovine xenograft's limited resorbability carries real multi-year non-resorption/foreign-body/migration risk (GRADE low, Inchingolo 2025 SR), so autogenous bone remains the gold standard and faster-turnover options may be preferred where indefinite xenograft persistence is a specific concern.

## 세줄요약

19편(동물·in-vitro·전향·RCT·SR·SR+MA) 종합: DBBM의 느린 흡수는 부피를 보존하지만 초기 신생골 형성을 역설적으로 억제할 수 있음(DBBM 단독 < 무이식 대조군 4주, p=0.025, Fujioka-Kobayashi 2022); BCP는 토끼 모델서 신생골 +70%(Chakar 2014), 임상 상악동 SR+MA서 +3.48% 신생골·−8.41% 잔존(Alkandari 2025)으로 일관되게 DBBM 초과; DBBM 입자 크기(대 1–2mm vs 소 0.25–1mm)는 상악동거상 조직학 결과에 유의 영향 없음(모두 NS, Li 2023) — 재료 선택 기준 아님.

혈소판 농축물 보조제는 계열 효과가 아니라 아형 특이적: L-PRF+DBBM +7.07% 신생골·−7.93% 잔존(잔존 I²=0%, Almutairi 2025), 더 넓은 PRP·PRF·PRGF SR+MA(Zhang 2025, 16편 n=372)는 전체 +5.92% 신생골·−1.93 잔존이나 잔존 감소는 PRF·PRGF에서만 유의하고 PRP는 아님; L-PRF도 전체 혈소판 농축물도 임플란트 안정성(ISQ/RFA)·생존율에는 무효; 동시 교정이동 부위에 DBBM 단독 금기(치근흡수 1.87배, Jiang 2020).

임상 결론: 상악동거상 루틴 1차는 DBBM(장기 임플란트 생존 데이터 우위), PRF/PRGF 추가는 비용 대비 합리적(PRP 근거 약함), BCP는 신생골 속도 우선 시; 이종골·동종골은 적응증 전반에서 상호교환 가능(Abushama 2025 SR n=395, Rao 2026 파일럿 RCT)해 재료 선택은 비용·가용성·환자 수용성 문제; 단 임플란트 생존과 장기 안전성은 별개 endpoint — 우골 이종골의 낮은 흡수율은 다년 비흡수·이물반응·입자 이동의 실제 위험(GRADE low, Inchingolo 2025)이라 자가골이 gold standard, 다년 비흡수가 우려되면 빠른 turnover 재료로 소폭 기울 여지.

## Summary

DBBM (Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral, 탈단백 우골 광물질) has been the clinical gold standard for bone augmentation procedures for decades, valued for its predictable space maintenance, osteoconductive architecture, and extreme volume stability. However, a consistent body of preclinical and clinical evidence now characterizes a core biological trade-off: DBBM's very slow resorption — the property that confers volume stability — also limits the rate and total volume of new bone formation at early time points. The 14 papers synthesized here define this trade-off with quantitative precision, and map five intervention/decision vectors that address it: (1) blending DBBM with resorbable synthetics (BCP/β-TCP), (2) modifying the collagen carrier or block form, (3) adding platelet-derived growth-factor concentrates (PRF/PRGF/PRP), and (4) functionalizing calcium phosphate with recombinant BMP2, plus (5) substituting an allograft. Two further dimensions are non-levers or cross-cutting concerns rather than trade-off fixes: DBBM particle size (large vs small granule) does not change histomorphometric outcome, and long-term safety (non-resorption, foreign-body/migration complications) is a distinct endpoint from the implant-survival and histomorphometry endpoints that dominate the efficacy literature.

### Core Propositions

1. **DBBM alone may paradoxically suppress early bone formation.** In a 4-week rat calvarial critical-size defect model, DBBM alone produced significantly less new bone than the empty (no-graft) control (p=0.025). All synthetic-additive combinations rescued new bone to natural healing levels (Fujioka-Kobayashi 2022). This counterintuitive finding reframes DBBM's role: it is a volumetric scaffold, not an osteogenic material, and it may occupy space at the expense of ingrowth at short time windows.

2. **BCP outperforms DBBM on new bone formation and resorption across models.** In a 6-week rabbit femoral critical-size defect, BCP (60% HA / 40% β-TCP) produced 70% more new bone and had 60% less residual material than DBBM (both p<0.001; Chakar 2014). In the clinical sinus augmentation context, the RCT SR+MA (10 RCTs, 328 patients) confirmed BCP yields +3.48% more new bone (p=0.01) and −8.41% less residual graft (p<0.001) vs DBBM (Alkandari 2025). The advantage is consistent across species and procedures.

3. **L-PRF addition to DBBM is the highest-evidence adjunct for sinus augmentation histomorphometry.** The SR+MA of 5 RCTs (n=140) showed L-PRF+DBBM vs DBBM alone: new bone +7.07% (95% CI 2.20–11.93; p<0.001; I²=37%) and residual graft −7.93% (95% CI −11.20 to −4.66; p<0.001; I²=0%). Zero heterogeneity for residual graft is exceptional and signals a mechanistically consistent PRF effect on DBBM incorporation (Almutairi 2025).

4. **A-PRF shows a larger raw effect in a single RCT (Motimath 2026).** Split-mouth RCT (n=30, 60 sinuses): A-PRF+DBBM at 9 months yielded 42.8% new bone vs 31.2% for DBBM alone (p=0.001; +11.6 percentage points) and 28.4% vs 41.6% residual graft (p=0.001; −13.2 percentage points). The A-PRF low-speed protocol (2700 rpm/12 min) is hypothesized to preserve more viable platelets and growth factors than classical L-PRF centrifugation.

5. **PRF benefit is PRF-subtype-specific and procedure-specific.** The 2023 SR (7 RCTs; Idiri 2023) — which pooled all PRF subtypes without meta-analysis — found no significant histomorphometric benefit of PRF+DBBM in sinus lift. The subsequent Almutairi 2025 SR+MA, restricted to L-PRF only with quantitative pooling, demonstrated significant benefit. The discrepancy resolves methodologically: PRF subtypes (L-PRF vs A-PRF vs solid PRF) and centrifugation protocols are not interchangeable in the sinus environment.

6. **Platelet lysate (APL) provides no measurable benefit to either DBBM or BCP.** Despite confirmed in vitro mitogenic activity (100–200% cell proliferation enhancement), APL addition to DBBM or BCP in a 6-week rabbit model yielded no significant difference in new bone or residual material (Chakar 2014). This negative finding emphasizes that growth factor release alone, without the fibrin scaffold architecture of modern PRF, does not drive in vivo bone formation.

7. **Collagen modification of DBBM expands clinical utility without sacrificing scaffold function.** The species-specific collagen-enriched DBBM block (C-DBBM: 95% DBBM + 5% bovine collagen) in ARP achieved 0/10 patients requiring GBR at implant placement and histological evidence of appositional membranous osteogenesis across multiple concurrent stages (van Orten 2024). The integrated collagen may address the DBBM-alone bone formation gap by providing cellular attachment sites and growth factor binding capacity. The all-bovine material chain also addresses porcine-free religious/ethical requirements.

8. **L-serine incorporation into collagen scaffolds provides tunable non-crosslinked membrane persistence.** Subcutaneous mouse model: 40 wt% L-serine collagen showed 2.70 mm² residual area vs 0.37 mm² unmodified collagen (7× more persistent, p<0.05), while Bio-Gide remained most stable at 5.64 mm². Structural mechanism: β-sheet enrichment and crystalline domain formation via hydrogen bonding without cytotoxic chemical crosslinkers (Kim 2025). This creates a design pathway for DBBM-carrying membrane scaffolds with programmable resorption timing.

9. **BMP2-functionalized calcium phosphate (BioCaP) outperforms DBBM in the challenging context of simultaneous orthodontic tooth movement.** Beagle dog study (n=9, 8 weeks): BioCaP produced 1.61× more new bone vs empty control and 1.25× more than DBB; bone mineral density 1.92× higher vs control and 1.36× higher vs DBB; root resorption 1.39× less than DBB. The BioCaP BMP2 dose (140 μg at 1 μg/mL co-precipitation) is 10-fold below conventional rhBMP2, reducing ectopic bone and seroma risk. DBB's slow-resorbing rigid particles are mechanically incompatible with OTM, causing 1.87× more root resorption than the empty control (Jiang 2020). This is the most clinically actionable negative signal for DBBM — it is contraindicated as a solo graft in sites requiring simultaneous tooth movement.

10. **A partially demineralized allograft plug matches DBBM on dimension and vital bone but turns over faster.** Pilot RCT (n=25; ACBP 12 vs DBBM 13; sinus floor elevation + socket preservation, collagen membrane both arms): at 6 months a partially demineralized allogeneic cancellous bone plug (ACBP) was statistically equivalent to DBBM on CBCT dimensional stability (sinus height gain 7.82 vs 8.21 mm, P=0.852; socket ridge change NS) and on newly formed vital bone (38.84% vs 32.18%, P=0.105), but left significantly less residual graft (11.18% vs 19.80%, P=0.006) (Rao 2026). The authors read the lower residual graft as faster graft turnover, not greater osteogenesis — and warn that external contour stability can mask material-specific intermediate remodeling. This separates two endpoints often conflated: dimensional/contour stability (comparable across materials) vs internal tissue composition (residual graft differs, vital bone does not). The study is underpowered, pools two indications, and has only 6-month follow-up.

11. **DBBM particle size does not change histomorphometric outcome — it is a non-lever for material selection.** SR+MA of 4 RCTs (n=94; 44 large-particle vs 50 small-particle implant sites; PROSPERO CRD42022379384) directly compared large-granule (1–2 mm) vs small-granule (0.25–1 mm) Bio-Oss in maxillary sinus floor elevation. None of the three histomorphometric endpoints differed significantly: connective tissue (WMD −5.76%, p=0.117), newly formed bone (WMD +5.15%, p=0.132), residual xenograft (WMD +1.89%, p=0.353) (Li 2023). Particle size therefore should be chosen on procedural-convenience grounds (large granules shorten surgical time and reduce graft volume needed but increase void space; small granules allow finer fill control), not on any expectation of a histological outcome difference. Evidence is limited (4 RCTs, high heterogeneity for CT and NFB) and individual studies disagreed in direction, but the pooled signal is clear non-difference.

12. **Platelet-concentrate benefit is enhancer-subtype-specific, not a class effect — and PRP does not replicate the PRF/PRGF residual-graft effect.** The broader SR+MA (16 studies, 372 patients, 455 procedures) of PRP/PRF/PRGF as adjuncts to DBBM in two-stage sinus floor elevation found overall +5.92% new bone (p=0.002) and −1.93 residual graft volume (p<0.001), but the residual-graft reduction was significant only in the PRF (MD −4.32) and PRGF (MD −1.89) subgroups and NOT in the PRP subgroup, and the standalone new-bone benefit reached significance only for PRF (MD 7.54) (Zhang 2025). This directly extends the L-PRF-only Almutairi 2025 finding to the whole platelet-concentrate family while refining "platelet concentrate helps" into "PRF/PRGF helps; PRP does not reliably." Neither Zhang 2025 nor Almutairi 2025 found any effect on implant stability (RFA/ISQ) or survival (RR 1.03, p=0.252), reinforcing that the benefit is histomorphometric, not implant-level.

13. **Xenograft and allograft are broadly interchangeable across pre-implant indications.** A PROSPERO-registered SR of 12 comparative studies (n=395; 6 immediate implant, 2 delayed implant, 3 ARP, 1 sinus augmentation) comparing xenograft (mostly Bio-Oss) vs allograft (FFB/DFDBA) found no consistent significant difference on marginal bone level, implant survival/stability, gingival/plaque indices, or dimensional change (Abushama 2025). Histomorphometry was the least consistent domain — one study favored allograft (new bone 65% vs 45%, p<0.05), another the reverse — precluding meta-analysis. This lifts the single-pilot-RCT Rao 2026 equivalence signal to SR level across a broad indication range: material choice can be driven by non-efficacy factors (cost, availability, patient acceptance of animal-derived tissue). The synthesis is narrative-only with moderate-to-serious risk of bias, so "interchangeable" is supported but not quantitatively confirmed.

14. **Implant-survival success does not preclude long-term non-resorption/foreign-body complications — long-term safety is a separate endpoint.** A PRISMA/PROSPERO SR (11 of 217 screened studies; sinus elevation, ARP, ridge augmentation) graded the certainty of bovine-xenograft evidence as GRADE moderate for short/mid-term implant survival and graft integration but GRADE low for long-term complications (Inchingolo 2025). The two long-term complication case series it synthesizes (Rodriguez 2019, Nowzari 2022) documented particle migration, chronic inflammation, oroantral communication, and cyst formation emerging 2–13 years after apparently successful grafting; Schmitt 2013 found autogenous bone superior in osteoinductive potential to Bio-Oss. This is the same endpoint-disaggregation lens used in Axis 7 (residual-graft vs vital-bone): a functioning implant on stable contour does not certify that the underlying xenograft has resorbed or integrated without late foreign-body reaction. Autogenous bone remains the gold standard where feasible; xenograft use warrants transparent risk communication and informed consent, and the true complication incidence is likely underreported (the review names the "Proteus Phenomenon" of selective positive publication).

## Results

### Axis 1 — DBBM Core Properties: Slow Resorption, Volume Stability, Osteoconductive Scaffold

DBBM's defining characteristic is near-permanent residual material persistence. At 6 weeks in rabbit femoral defects, DBBM remained largely non-resorbed while BCP (β-TCP component) actively degraded (Chakar 2014). At 4 weeks in rat calvarial defects, DBBM showed no significant material density change versus the synthetic additives that resorbed measurably (Fujioka-Kobayashi 2022). In the clinical sinus augmentation context, residual graft % for DBBM alone ranged 38–44% at 6–9 month biopsies (Almutairi 2025; Motimath 2026), confirming the same kinetics in humans.

The scaffold function is genuine: new bone forms around DBBM granules in all models — the issue is rate and total volume relative to resorbable materials. DBBM's value is therefore primarily volumetric/mechanical (space maintenance, prevention of membrane collapse), not biologically stimulatory. This dichotomy is clinically important: choose DBBM for sites where long-term volume stability is the dominant need; choose resorbable alternatives or DBBM hybrids when early bone ingrowth is the priority.

### Axis 2 — DBBM + Synthetic Additive Hybrids (BCP / β-TCP)

**Fujioka-Kobayashi 2022** (rat calvarial, 4 weeks, n=30 defects, 5 groups): α-TCP + DBBM (1:1) and BBCP (3% or 6% HA / 97% or 94% α-TCP) + DBBM (1:1) all restored new bone to empty-defect levels. DBBM alone was significantly inferior to all combinations and to the empty defect (p=0.025). The synthetic components resorbed (mineral density decrease) while maintaining volume — preventing premature scaffold collapse. HA coating degree (3% vs 6%) had no significant effect at 4 weeks.

**Chakar 2014** (rabbit femoral, 6 weeks, n=12): BCP (60% HA / 40% β-TCP) vs DBBM — new bone +70% (p<0.001), residual material −60% (p<0.001). BCP's active resorption through the β-TCP component is the mechanism: as TCP dissolves, Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ ions are released into the local environment, creating a mineral-saturated milieu that drives osteoblast activity and matrix mineralization.

**Alkandari 2025** (10 RCTs, 328 patients, sinus elevation): BCP vs DBBM histomorphometry — new bone MD +3.48% (95% CI 0.89–5.97; p=0.01; I²=44%), residual graft MD −8.41% (95% CI −13.12 to −3.72; p<0.001; I²=86%), soft tissue MD +6.01% (95% CI 2.39–9.63; p<0.001; I²=72%). The higher soft tissue fraction with BCP reflects active graft resorption creating space occupied transiently by soft tissue before bone fills in. High I² for residual graft (86%) reflects BCP formulation heterogeneity across the 10 included RCTs — BCP is not a single material.

**Synthesis**: The hybrid graft strategy (DBBM for space maintenance + resorbable synthetic for bone formation drive) addresses the core DBBM trade-off without abandoning its volume stability advantage. Clinically applicable ratio: 1:1 DBBM:synthetic showed benefit in both rat (Fujioka-Kobayashi 2022) and clinical (Alkandari 2025 pooled) contexts.

### Axis 3 — Collagen-Modified DBBM

**Van Orten 2024** (prospective case series, n=10, ARP, 130–319 days): Species-specific C-DBBM block (95% DBBM + 5% bovine collagen, same species as DBBM) with bovine cross-linked membrane using open-healing technique. Histological results: lattice-pattern cancellous bone trabeculae by appositional membranous osteogenesis surrounding residual C-DBBM granules. GBR requirement at implant placement: 0/10 patients (0%). No complications over 2.5–4.5 year follow-up. The collagen component likely provides integrin-binding RGD sites for osteoprogenitor cell attachment — addressing the cell-recruitment gap of mineral-only DBBM. The species-specific (all-bovine) chain is the first in literature to report histological confirmation.

Methodological note: the 0% GBR rate vs Fischer et al.'s SR+MA 6–19% augmentation-free rate gap reflects design differences — private practice case selection, longer healing periods (>6 months vs 3–4 months in RCTs), and block vs particulate form. No control group prevents causal attribution.

**Li 2025** (narrative review, J Orthop Transl 2025): provides the biomaterials-science foundation for all collagen-modified bone substitute strategies in this overview. Type I collagen (Col-I) forms the structural ECM of bone by templating hydroxyapatite (HA) crystal nucleation within mineralized fibrils; its triple-helix Gly-X-Y repeat structure confers tensile strength. The review maps ~29 collagen types, four source categories (bovine, porcine, marine/fish, recombinant/GEPP), and all major processed forms — scaffolds, sponges, membranes, hydrogels, microspheres — together with their composite partners: natural polymers (alginate, chitosan/HA, hyaluronan, silk fibroin), synthetic polymers (PCL, PLGA, PVA), bioceramics (HA, β-TCP), and growth-factor-functionalized variants. The single FDA-approved composite in bone regeneration is rhBMP-2/absorbable collagen sponge (ACS; InFUSE®), directly relevant to the Axis 6 BioCaP growth-factor strategy. Key barriers to clinical translation remain: mechanical weakness of pure collagen, insufficient vascularization, immune/batch-variability risk, and very few approved composites. Future direction: genetically engineered protein polymers (GEPP) enabling sequence-level BMP-2 binding at ~10× dose reduction vs natural rhBMP-2. The marine/fish source category is the mechanistic backdrop for the carp-collagen membrane in Bujda 2026 (not in this overview but referenced in the source paper). This review contextualizes why collagen carrier form — not just presence — drives the Van Orten 2024 C-DBBM (integrated bovine collagen, 5%) result, and why Kim 2025's L-serine scaffold modification works at the molecular level (β-sheet enrichment, hydrogen-bond stabilization).

**Kim 2025** (mouse subcutaneous model + in vitro): L-serine incorporation into bovine collagen scaffolds at 40 wt% achieved 7× greater persistence than unmodified collagen (2.70 ± 1.45 vs 0.37 ± 0.22 mm², p<0.05). Bio-Gide® remained most persistent (5.64 ± 2.76 mm²). The stabilization mechanism is non-crosslinking: L-serine's hydroxyl group forms hydrogen bonds with collagen backbone amide/carbonyl groups and increases crystallinity (2θ=22–23° XRD peak), while low concentrations (10–30 wt%) preserve the native triple helix. No cytotoxicity is expected (no aldehyde or NHS-ester chemistry). Osteogenic performance in defect models: not yet tested — this is a degradation-tuning proof-of-concept.

**Lee 2025** (beagle controlled study, Scientific Reports): First head-to-head in vivo comparison of OCS-B Collagen® (NIBEC, Jincheon, Korea; 90% DBBM + 10% gelation-crosslinked porcine collagen) vs Bio-Oss® Collagen (Geistlich; 90% DBBM + 10% non-crosslinked porcine collagen). Thirteen male beagles, standardized 10×3×4 mm mandibular box defects (three groups: OCS-B.C / Bio-Oss.C / no graft), all covered with Bio-Gide® membrane. Assessment at 8 and 16 weeks post-graft and 24 weeks post-implant placement. Key findings: both collagenated grafts produced significantly greater BV and BV/TV than no-graft control at 8 and 16 weeks (p<0.0001). No significant difference between OCS-B.C and Bio-Oss.C on any endpoint — BMD, BV/TV, BIC, ISQ — at any timepoint. The collagen crosslinking difference (gelation/crosslinked in OCS-B.C vs non-crosslinked in Bio-Oss.C) did not translate into any detectable histomorphometric or implant-stability difference. ALP activity was similar between graft groups at 3 days in vitro.

**Synthesis**: Collagen modification occupies the middle ground between mineral-only DBBM and complex growth factor delivery. Integrated collagen (Van Orten 2024) appears to enable cellular colonization without requiring synthetic additives; amino acid-modified scaffolds (Kim 2025) offer programmable resorption timing for barrier membrane applications. Head-to-head comparison of two collagenated DBBM products (Lee 2025) shows that collagen crosslinking strategy (gelation vs non-crosslinked) does not alter GBR or osseointegration outcomes — suggesting the 10% collagen component's osteoconductive and handling benefits are preserved regardless of crosslinking method. Both Van Orten 2024 (bovine collagen) and Lee 2025 (porcine collagen, gelation-crosslinked) eliminate the need for additional porcine components when paired with DBBM, relevant where porcine-free requirements apply to the membrane carrier but not to the graft itself.

### Axis 4 — Platelet-Derived Adjuncts: L-PRF, A-PRF, All-PRF, and the PRP-vs-PRF/PRGF Distinction

| Paper | Design | Enhancer Type | New bone effect | Residual graft effect | Implant stability/survival |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Idiri 2023 | SR (7 RCTs, no pooling) | Mixed (all PRF) | No significant difference | No significant difference (1 GBR study: significant reduction) | — |
| Almutairi 2025 | SR+MA (5 RCTs, pooled) | L-PRF only | +7.07% MD (p<0.001, I²=37%) | −7.93% MD (p<0.001, I²=0%) | ISQ NS (MD −2.25, p=0.56) |
| Zhang 2025 | SR+MA (16 studies, n=372, pooled) | PRP + PRF + PRGF (subgrouped) | +5.92% MD overall (p=0.002); significant only in PRF subgroup (MD 7.54) | −1.93 MD overall (p<0.001); significant in PRF (−4.32) and PRGF (−1.89); **PRP NS** | RFA NS; survival RR 1.03 (p=0.252) |
| Motimath 2026 | Split-mouth RCT (n=30) | A-PRF (2700 rpm) | +11.6% (42.8 vs 31.2%, p=0.001) | −13.2% (28.4 vs 41.6%, p=0.001) | — |

The hierarchical interpretation: Idiri 2023's null result was a PRF-type pooling artifact and a meta-analysis power limitation (7 RCTs, no pooling). When restricted to L-PRF and quantitatively pooled, Almutairi 2025 demonstrates significance with acceptable heterogeneity (I²=37% for new bone; 0% for residual graft). The zero I² for residual graft is the most reliable single-study signal in this axis — it means L-PRF consistently reduces DBBM persistence across all 5 included RCTs regardless of protocol variation.

**Zhang 2025 broadens and refines this.** Its larger SR+MA (16 studies, 372 patients) pools not just L-PRF but the whole platelet-concentrate family (PRP, PRF, PRGF) and stratifies by enhancer type. The overall effect direction matches Almutairi (new bone +5.92%, residual graft −1.93, both significant), but the subgroup analysis delivers the key refinement: the residual-graft reduction is significant only for PRF (MD −4.32) and PRGF (MD −1.89), and is **NOT** significant for PRP; the standalone new-bone benefit reaches significance only for PRF. In other words, "platelet concentrate helps DBBM" is too coarse — the effect is a PRF/PRGF effect, and PRP (a first-generation liquid concentrate with rapid growth-factor washout and no dense fibrin matrix) does not reliably replicate it. This mirrors Chakar 2014's APL (liquid platelet lysate) finding within the same overview: the fibrin architecture, not growth-factor content alone, drives the in vivo graft-incorporation effect. Zhang 2025 also independently confirms the histomorphometry-vs-implant-level dissociation — no platelet enhancer of any type improved implant stability (RFA) or survival (RR 1.03, p=0.252).

Almutairi 2025 likewise showed ISQ at loading was not significantly different (MD −2.25; p=0.56) despite better histomorphometry. This dissociation between tissue-level (histomorphometry) and implant-level (ISQ/osseointegration) benefit has clinical implications across both SR+MAs: better histomorphometric appearance does not necessarily justify earlier loading decisions on ISQ grounds alone.

A-PRF vs L-PRF: Motimath 2026 is the only head-to-head proxy available (no direct comparison RCT). A-PRF's lower centrifugation speed (2700 rpm vs L-PRF's 2700–3000 rpm in Choukroun protocols, and 3200–3300 rpm in Dohan) is hypothesized to retain more viable cells and platelets within the fibrin matrix, potentially explaining its numerically larger raw effect (+11.6% vs +7.07%). This remains a hypothesis without a direct comparison RCT.

Chakar 2014's APL (liquid platelet lysate, no fibrin scaffold) added no benefit — underscoring that the fibrin architecture of PRF membranes, not growth factor content alone, is critical for the in vivo effect.

### Axis 5 — BCP vs DBBM in Sinus Elevation (Alkandari 2025)

The Alkandari 2025 SR+MA provides the first RCT-level synthesis dedicated to this head-to-head comparison in sinus augmentation. Across 10 RCTs (328 patients, 389 implants):

- New bone formation: BCP +3.48% (p=0.01, I²=44%) — significant but moderate heterogeneity
- Residual graft: BCP −8.41% (p<0.001, I²=86%) — highly significant but very high heterogeneity; reflects BCP formulation diversity (60:40 vs 70:30 HA:TCP ratios, particle sizes)
- Soft tissue fraction: BCP +6.01% (p<0.001, I²=72%) — reflects turnover space before bone fills in

Critically, no implant survival data were available in this SR+MA. DBBM's long-term implant survival track record (10+ year data from Mordenfeld series) is more established than BCP's. Until survival-level equivalence is demonstrated in sinus augmentation, BCP's histomorphometric superiority should not be interpreted as clinical superiority for the final outcome of functioning implants.

The high I² for BCP outcomes (44–86%) is a material-heterogeneity problem: BCP is not one material. Clinicians should note that the pooled "BCP advantage" likely underestimates the effect of the best-performing BCP formulations and overestimates it for others.

### Axis 6 — BMP2-Functionalized Calcium Phosphate (BioCaP)

Jiang 2020 is the only paper in this set addressing growth factor functionalization. The BioCaP approach — BMP2 co-precipitated into a biomimetic CaP coating at 1 μg/mL (total 140 μg per graft) — achieves sustained release at 10× lower dose than conventional rhBMP2 delivery systems, reducing ectopic bone and seroma risks.

In the unique clinical scenario of alveolar defect grafting during concurrent orthodontic tooth movement (OTM), BioCaP achieved:
- New bone: 1.61× empty control, 1.25× DBB
- Bone mineral density: 1.92× empty control, 1.36× DBB
- Root resorption: 1.39× less than DBB
- OTM rate: no significant difference vs control
- GCF IL-1β: significantly lower (anti-inflammatory)
- DBB root resorption: 1.87× the empty control — a significant safety concern unique to the OTM context

The DBB root resorption signal is the most clinically actionable finding: rigid, non-resorbing DBB particles under orthodontic compressive forces create localized stress concentration at the root-PDL-graft interface, driving external root resorption. Fast-resorbing BioCaP particles are progressively replaced by bone, eliminating this stress concentrator.

This axis currently has one animal study (n=9 beagles, 3 groups); human data do not yet exist for BioCaP.

### Axis 7 — Allograft Alternative to DBBM and the Residual-Graft-Turnover Endpoint (Rao 2026)

Rao 2026 is the first direct head-to-head clinical comparison of a commercial partially demineralized allograft plug (ACBP; 9×9×12 mm partially demineralized allogeneic cancellous bone, Hubei Osteolink) against the xenograft benchmark DBBM (Bio-Oss) in membrane-based GBR. The single-center pilot RCT (n=25; ACBP 12, DBBM 13) pooled two indications under one protocol — posterior maxillary lateral-window sinus floor elevation and mandibular posterior socket preservation — both with absorbable collagen membrane, single surgeon, tension-free closure. Outcomes at 6 months (implant placement) combined CBCT dimensional analysis with histomorphometry of a 3-mm trephine core.

| Outcome | ACBP | DBBM | p | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vital bone (%) | 38.84 ± 8.37 | 32.18 ± 11.04 | 0.105 (NS) | Equivalent new bone |
| **Residual graft (%, primary)** | 11.18 ± 5.48 | 19.80 ± 8.23 | **0.006** | ACBP lower → faster turnover |
| Sinus height gain (mm) | 7.82 ± 1.52 | 8.21 ± 4.30 | 0.852 (ANCOVA 0.801) | Equivalent (baseline subantral height imbalanced) |
| Socket ridge height change (mm) | −0.30 | +0.51 | 0.079 (NS) | Equivalent |
| Socket ridge width change (8/10/12 mm) | — | — | 0.437 / 0.957 / 0.945 | All NS |

**The conceptual contribution is endpoint disaggregation.** ACBP's lower residual graft is not a marker of more bone — vital bone was statistically equivalent — but of faster graft material turnover. Rao 2026 thereby reinforces a theme running through this overview: residual-graft % and vital-bone % are distinct endpoints, and external contour/dimensional stability (comparable across ACBP and DBBM here) can mask material-specific remodeling. This is the same logic that resolves the BCP and L-PRF axes, where the consistent signal is reduced *residual graft* (faster incorporation) rather than uniformly more new bone. The allograft plug joins BCP (Axis 2/5) and L-PRF (Axis 4) as a third lever that shifts DBBM's slow-resorption trade-off toward faster turnover — here by substituting a partially demineralized allogeneic scaffold rather than blending or supplementing the xenograft.

Caveats limiting evidence weight: pilot scale (n=25, no a priori power calculation, not prospectively registered), two pooled indications under one analysis, surgeon unblinded, and 6-month follow-up only. ACBP also carries the allograft-specific considerations (donor sourcing, immunogenicity, patient acceptance) that DBBM's xenograft chain does not.

### Axis 8 — DBBM Particle Size Does Not Affect Histomorphometric Outcome (Li 2023)

All seven axes above concern material *type* (DBBM vs BCP vs collagen-DBBM vs allograft vs BMP2-CaP) or adjuncts to DBBM. Li 2023 addresses an orthogonal dimension entirely internal to DBBM itself: does the **granule/particle size** of the same xenograft change the result? Its SR+MA (4 RCTs, n=94; 44 large-particle vs 50 small-particle implant sites; PROSPERO CRD42022379384) is the first and only synthesis to isolate this variable in maxillary sinus floor elevation.

| Outcome | Model | WMD (large vs small) | 95% CI | p | I² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| % Connective tissue | Random | −5.76 | −12.96 to 1.45 | 0.117 (NS) | 65.7% |
| % Newly formed bone | Random | +5.15 | −1.56 to 11.86 | 0.132 (NS) | 75.3% |
| % Residual xenograft | Fixed | +1.89 | −2.10 to 5.88 | 0.353 (NS) | 0.0% |

None of the three histomorphometric endpoints differed significantly between large-granule (1–2 mm) and small-granule (0.25–1 mm) Bio-Oss. **Practical implication: particle size is not a basis for material-selection decisions.** It should be chosen on procedural-convenience grounds — large granules can shorten surgical time and reduce the graft volume needed but leave larger void spaces (a theoretical infection/space-fill concern); small granules allow finer control of fill volume but require more material and patient burden. This is the particle-size analogue of the "material type matters less than expected" theme: within DBBM, granule size is a non-critical lever, echoing the same conclusion reached in the ARP context (Yu 2026, referenced by Li 2023). Evidence caveats: only 4 RCTs, substantial heterogeneity for connective tissue (I²=65.7%) and new bone (I²=75.3%) driven by biopsy-site and timing differences, and individual studies disagreed in direction (Testori and Kamolratanakul numerically favored large particle for new bone) — the pooled non-difference partly masks that variability. Notably, Zhang 2025 (Axis 4) independently found particle-size subgroups (0.25–1 mm vs 1–2 mm) did not modify implant stability or survival, converging on the same non-lever conclusion from the implant-outcome side.

### Axis 9 — Xenograft-vs-Allograft Equivalence and Long-Term Safety as a Distinct Endpoint (Abushama 2025, Inchingolo 2025)

This axis adds two SRs that reframe DBBM against two references the earlier axes did not fully engage: allograft as a broad interchangeable class (extending the single Rao 2026 pilot RCT of Axis 7), and autograft as the true gold standard against which xenograft's *long-term* safety — not just its efficacy — must be judged.

**Abushama 2025 — xenograft ≈ allograft across indications.** A PROSPERO-registered SR of 12 comparative studies (n=395; 6 immediate implant, 2 delayed implant, 3 ARP, 1 sinus augmentation) directly compared xenograft (predominantly Bio-Oss, 58.1% of grafts) vs allograft (FFB/DFDBA, 41.9%). No consistent statistically significant difference emerged on marginal bone level (xenograft −1.85 vs allograft −1.75 mm, P=0.791), implant survival (one study: BBM 100% vs FFB 97.8%, P=0.352), implant stability, gingival/plaque indices, or ridge dimensional change. Histomorphometry was the least consistent domain — one study favored allograft (new bone 65% vs 45%, P<0.05; residual particles 27% vs 41%), another the reverse (new bone 25.6% allograft vs 35.3% xenograft) — and this heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis. This lifts the Rao 2026 single-pilot-RCT equivalence signal to SR level across a much broader indication range, supporting the practical conclusion that xenograft and allograft are interchangeable GBR biomaterials and material choice can be driven by non-efficacy factors (cost, availability, patient acceptance of animal- vs human-derived tissue). Weight caveat: narrative synthesis only, 12 studies, moderate-to-serious risk of bias (confounding dominant in the non-randomized subset), no GRADE — "interchangeable" is supported but not quantitatively confirmed.

**Wang 2025 — PBX = BBX across MSFA and ARP.** SR+MA (PROSPERO CRD42024628683; 10 studies; 452 sites: BBX 239 = MSFA 51 + ARP 188; PBX 213 = MSFA 51 + ARP 162). Primary outcome — newly formed bone (NFB) across 6 studies, 202 sites: WMD=1.5 (95% CI −1.46–4.46; p=0.321; I²=44.6%) — not significant. All secondary outcomes (residual bone graft, connective tissue, vertical height reduction, horizontal width reduction, volume reduction) also non-significant. This is the first SR+MA to compare porcine bone xenograft (PBX) against bovine bone xenograft (BBX) specifically in MSFA and ARP (controlled, single-defect augmentation environments). The equivalence finding joins Axis 9's broader xenograft-vs-allograft equivalence (Abushama 2025) and adds a clinically important sub-dimension: in practice settings where bovine-derived materials face cultural, religious, or disease-concern restrictions (halal, kosher, CJD-risk perception), PBX is now SR+MA–supported as an equivalent substitute specifically within the two most common contained augmentation procedures. Evidence gap: no long-term implant survival data yet for direct PBX vs BBX comparison in sinus or ridge preservation contexts.

**Inchingolo 2025 — implant survival ≠ long-term safety.** The overview so far has framed DBBM almost entirely through histomorphometry (new bone / residual graft %) and implant survival endpoints. Inchingolo 2025 (PRISMA/PROSPERO SR, 11 of 217 screened studies, spanning sinus elevation, ARP, and horizontal/vertical ridge augmentation) introduces a genuinely new endpoint: **long-term complication risk.** Its two-axis appraisal splits sharply — GRADE **moderate** certainty for short/mid-term implant survival and graft integration, but GRADE **low** for long-term complications (particle migration, chronic inflammation, sinus pathology), which rest mainly on case series (Rodriguez 2019, Nowzari 2022) documenting migration, chronic inflammation, oroantral communication, and cyst formation emerging **2–13 years** post-grafting. ROBINS-I rated most of the 11 studies at moderate risk of bias, with only Mordenfeld 2017 low-risk across all seven domains; Schmitt 2013 found autogenous bone superior in osteoinductive potential to Bio-Oss.

The important nuance, and the reason this is a distinct axis rather than a footnote to survival data: **a successful, surviving implant on a dimensionally stable ridge does not certify that the underlying xenograft has resorbed or biologically integrated without late foreign-body reaction.** These are different endpoints measured at different times — exactly the endpoint-disaggregation lens Axis 7 applies to residual-graft vs vital-bone (dimensional/contour stability can mask internal remodeling differences). Here the disaggregation runs on the time axis: DBBM's defining slow resorption (Axis 1), an asset for volume stability, is simultaneously the mechanistic root of its multi-year non-resorption/foreign-body/migration risk. Autogenous bone remains the gold standard where feasible; porcine xenografts and PRF derivatives are flagged as promising better-resorbing alternatives pending long-term RCTs. Because complication incidence is likely underreported (the review names the "Proteus Phenomenon" of selective positive publication) and rests on GRADE-low evidence, this is a calibrated caution — not a contraindication — that warrants transparent risk communication and informed consent, and that can tip material choice toward faster-turnover options in patients for whom indefinite xenograft persistence is a specific concern.

### Axis 10 — Cellular Mechanistic Basis and Product-Specific Healing Variability (Stephan 1999, Jiang 1999, Sousa 2026)

The nine axes above operate at the tissue/histomorphometric level (new bone %, residual graft %, implant survival). Three papers add a cellular mechanistic foundation and a cautionary product-specificity finding.

**Cellular basis for osteoconduction (Stephan 1999, in vitro)**: this is the earliest and most basic evidence in this overview — a direct test of whether anorganic bovine bone (ABB, the sintered deproteinized bovine bone mineral commercially known as DBBM/Bio-Oss) actually supports osteoblast attachment and proliferation at the cellular level. Primary neonatal rat calvarial osteoblasts attached to ABB in a time-dependent manner (significantly more cells at 180 min vs 15/90 min, p<0.05) and proliferated in a dose-dependent manner (100 mg ABB > 10 mg, p<0.05), with SEM confirming cells transitioning from round to flattened, spread morphology with cytoplasmic processes extending into ABB surface pores. This is the cellular-level "why" underlying Axis 1's characterization of DBBM as a genuine osteoconductive scaffold, not merely an inert filler.

**Growth-factor carrier capacity (Jiang 1999, in vitro, direct companion to Stephan 1999)**: the same group tested whether ABB could additionally serve as a growth-factor delivery vehicle. Both PDGF-BB and IGF-I adsorbed to ABB in a concentration-dependent manner, with IGF-I binding ~10-fold more tightly (only ~10% released by day 10, vs ~50% for PDGF-BB) — yet PDGF-BB-loaded ABB significantly enhanced osteoblast proliferation (p<0.05) while IGF-I-loaded ABB did not, despite its higher affinity. Binding affinity and biological effect dissociate: the growth factor that stays bound longest is not the one that drives proliferation. This early (1999) proof-of-concept anticipated the modern growth-factor-functionalized graft strategies now seen at the clinical level in Axis 6 (Jiang 2020's BMP2-functionalized BioCaP) and the collagen-composite delivery systems reviewed in Axis 3 (Li 2025).

**Same graft category, different healing dynamics (Sousa 2026, animal, 54 Wistar rats)**: this is the axis's cautionary counterpoint. Two commercially distinct collagen-containing bovine xenografts — Bio-Oss Collagen® (~90% HA/~10% collagen) and Extra Graft XG13® (~75% HA/~25% collagen) — were compared head-to-head in 5-mm rat calvarial critical-size defects at 7/14/28 days. Early inflammatory and angiogenic responses were equivalent between the two materials, but XG13® showed significantly greater new bone formation at 14 and 28 days (histomorphometry, p<0.05), higher BV/TV on micro-CT (14d p<0.0001; 28d p<0.05), lower porosity, and more mature collagen by 28 days (p<0.01). Both products fall under the identical clinical category — "collagenated bovine xenograft" — yet diverge statistically on multiple healing parameters. This is the mirror image of Axis 8's Li 2023 finding: where Li 2023 showed a within-material variable (particle size) does *not* change outcome, Sousa 2026 shows a between-product variable (collagen fraction, and whatever unmeasured manufacturing differences accompany it) *can* change outcome even within the same nominal category. Practical implication: clinicians should not treat "collagenated bovine xenograft" as a single interchangeable material class based on category label alone — as with the xenograft-vs-allograft equivalence caveat in Axis 9, the product-level details matter more than the category-level ones. Caveat: micro-CT cannot distinguish new bone from residual graft in this model, and the 28-day endpoint does not capture long-term remodeling, so this is preclinical signal, not a clinical product ranking.

## Clinical Decision Threads [미검증]

**Thread 1 — Routine extraction socket ARP: choosing between DBBM and C-DBBM**

Standard particulate DBBM provides volume stability but limited early bone formation (Fujioka-Kobayashi 2022). The collagen-enriched block form (C-DBBM, Van Orten 2024) appears to enable more complete bone colonization — possibly by providing cell-adhesion substrate — with 0% GBR requirement at implant placement in 10 ARP cases over 2.5–4.5 years. For patients with porcine-free requirements (halal practice), C-DBBM + bovine xCM is now the only species-consistent evidence-based ARP protocol. For standard patients, C-DBBM block + open-healing membrane is a reasonable protocol if the operator is comfortable without primary closure; particulate DBBM + primary closure membrane remains the RCT-validated standard.

**Thread 2 — Lateral sinus augmentation: DBBM alone vs DBBM + which platelet concentrate**

The default choice (DBBM alone) is well-supported by long-term implant survival data. Adding a platelet concentrate improves histomorphometry — but the choice of concentrate matters, and this is the key 2025 update. L-PRF is supported by the tightest synthesis: SR+MA of 5 RCTs shows +7.07% new bone and −7.93% residual graft, with zero I² on the residual graft finding (Almutairi 2025). The broader PRP/PRF/PRGF SR+MA (16 studies, n=372) then shows the benefit is enhancer-subtype-specific: PRF and PRGF both significantly reduce residual graft (MD −4.32 and −1.89), but **PRP does not** (PRP subgroup non-significant), and the standalone new-bone benefit reaches significance only for PRF (Zhang 2025). Practical guidance: prefer PRF (or PRGF) over PRP — the first-generation liquid PRP washes out its growth factors too fast and lacks the dense fibrin matrix that appears to drive graft incorporation (echoing Chakar 2014's null APL result). Across both SR+MAs the effect does not translate to better ISQ/RFA at loading or to higher implant survival — meaning it improves tissue quality at biopsy without proven functional acceleration of osseointegration, so it should not by itself justify an earlier loading decision. PRF cost is minimal (patient's own blood); the benefit/cost ratio favors routine PRF/PRGF addition, but PRP is not a substitute.

**Thread 3 — Lateral sinus augmentation: DBBM vs BCP**

BCP yields more new bone and less residual graft than DBBM in sinus augmentation RCTs (Alkandari 2025). However: (a) no implant survival SR+MA exists yet for BCP-augmented sinuses, (b) BCP heterogeneity is high (I²=86% for residual graft), and (c) DBBM has decades of implant survival data. The recommendation is: DBBM remains the preferred default for routine sinus augmentation. BCP or DBBM+BCP hybrids are appropriate in cases where maximum new bone formation speed is the priority (medically limited healing time, need for earlier loading) and the operator can select a well-characterized BCP formulation. The hybrid approach (DBBM+BCP 1:1) informed by Fujioka-Kobayashi 2022 and the Alkandari 2025 data may represent the optimal balance.

**Thread 4 — BMP2 use in grafted sites with simultaneous orthodontics**

Pure DBBM/DBB should not be used as the solo graft in alveolar defects receiving concurrent orthodontic tooth movement — root resorption risk is 1.87× the ungrafted control (Jiang 2020). BioCaP (BMP2-functionalized CaP) appears to be the only graft tested in this specific scenario with favorable root resorption profile. Until human RCT data exist, clinical use of BMP2-functionalized CaP in OTM sites should be considered experimental. Standard protocol: avoid non-resorbable graft particles in any site subject to orthodontic force.

**Thread 5 — Allograft vs DBBM for routine GBR (sinus/socket)**

When an allograft option is acceptable to the patient and practice, allograft is a reasonable DBBM alternative — and the evidence for interchangeability is now broader than the single pilot RCT. Rao 2026 shows a partially demineralized allogeneic cancellous bone plug (ACBP) equivalent to DBBM on 6-month dimensional stability and vital bone with faster graft turnover (lower residual graft), and Abushama 2025's SR (12 studies, n=395) extends the equivalence across immediate/delayed implant, ARP, and sinus augmentation with no consistent difference on marginal bone, survival, or dimensional outcomes. So xenograft vs allograft is largely a non-efficacy decision (cost, availability, patient acceptance of animal- vs human-derived tissue). One consideration can tip the balance: Inchingolo 2025 documents that DBBM's slow resorption carries real (GRADE-low) multi-year non-resorption, particle-migration, and foreign-body-reaction risk surfacing 2–13 years out. Where indefinite xenograft persistence is a specific concern — younger patients with long horizons, sites near the sinus/nasal floor where late particle migration matters, patients wanting complete replacement by host bone — the faster turnover of allograft (or another resorbable option) is a modest point in its favor. Do not overstate this: the complication evidence is GRADE-low and DBBM retains the deeper long-term implant-survival track record; allograft's turnover advantage is not yet shown to improve any implant-level outcome, and Rao 2026 is a pilot with pooled indications. Treat the allograft equivalence as evidence-supported-but-not-yet-confirmed-superior, with the long-term-safety nuance as a tie-breaker rather than a primary driver. Where feasible, autogenous bone remains the biological gold standard for both efficacy and long-term safety (Inchingolo 2025, Schmitt 2013).

**Thread 6 — Interpreting PRF generation heterogeneity in the literature**

Clinicians reading PRF studies should discriminate: (a) solid PRF vs liquid PRF (i-PRF) — different growth factor concentrations; (b) L-PRF centrifugation protocol (Dohan high-speed vs Choukroun protocols) — different fibrin architecture; (c) A-PRF low-speed (2700 rpm) — different platelet/cell distribution; (d) PRF mixed into graft vs used as membrane layer; (e) crucially, generation/class — first-generation PRP behaves differently from second-generation PRF/PRGF (Zhang 2025: PRP does not replicate PRF/PRGF's residual-graft reduction). Idiri 2023's null result and Almutairi 2025's / Zhang 2025's positive results for the same general procedure (sinus lift + DBBM + platelet concentrate) are both methodologically valid and non-contradictory: they measure different subsets of practice. Future meta-analyses should stratify by concentrate generation and centrifugation protocol.

**Thread 7 — Does DBBM particle size matter when selecting a product? No.**

When choosing between large-granule (1–2 mm) and small-granule (0.25–1 mm) Bio-Oss for sinus floor elevation, histological outcome should not drive the decision: the SR+MA found no significant difference in connective tissue, new bone, or residual xenograft between the two (Li 2023), and Zhang 2025's implant-outcome subgroups similarly found particle size did not modify stability or survival. Choose particle size on procedural-convenience grounds instead: large granules pack faster and use less material (shorter surgical time) but leave larger void spaces; small granules give finer control of fill volume and adaptation to irregular defects at the cost of more material and handling. There is no evidence-based reason to stock or select a specific particle size expecting a better histological or implant result. (Evidence is limited to 4 RCTs with high heterogeneity, so this is a "no material difference demonstrated" conclusion, not a proof of exact equivalence.)

**Thread 8 — Long-term safety and informed consent for xenograft grafting**

Because bovine xenograft does not meaningfully resorb (Axis 1) and can trigger late complications years out (Inchingolo 2025: migration, chronic inflammation, oroantral communication, cyst formation at 2–13 years; GRADE-low but real), long-term safety belongs in the consent conversation and the material-choice logic — not just the short-term survival statistics. Practical stance: (a) for routine sinus/ridge augmentation with implant-survival as the goal, DBBM remains well-supported and the complication risk is low-incidence, so this does not change first-line use; (b) but disclose to the patient that the graft material is expected to persist long-term and that rare late complications are possible, and document this — the review explicitly calls for transparent risk communication and informed consent; (c) where a specific patient factor makes indefinite persistence undesirable (long life horizon, sinus/nasal-floor proximity with migration concern, patient preference for complete host-bone replacement or against animal-derived tissue), weigh a faster-turnover option — allograft (Abushama 2025, Rao 2026: interchangeable efficacy), porcine xenograft, or autogenous bone (the osteoinductive and long-term-safety gold standard, Schmitt 2013). Keep this calibrated: the complication evidence is GRADE-low and likely to look worse than reality only because efficacy is over-published (Proteus Phenomenon) — it is a reason for disclosure and individualized tie-breaking, not a reason to abandon DBBM.

**Q1.** 상악동거상술에 DBBM 단독을 사용하는 것이 왜 신생골 측면에서 최선이 아닐 수 있습니까? 이 오버뷰의 역설적 소견을 설명하세요. *(근거: Fujioka-Kobayashi 2022 + BCP 비교)*

> **모범답안**: 이 오버뷰의 핵심 역설: **DBBM 단독이 초기 신생골 형성을 무이식(공백) 대조군보다 억제할 수 있다**(쥐 두개관 4주: DBBM 단독 < 무이식 p=0.025, Fujioka-Kobayashi 2022). DBBM의 느린 흡수는 부피 안정성을 주지만, 그 공간을 장기간 차지해 신생골 침입을 방해할 수 있다. 이에 비해 BCP(이상성 인산칼슘, 60% HA/40% β-TCP)는 상악동거상 임상 SR+MA에서 DBBM 대비 신생골 +3.48%, 잔존이식재 −8.41%로 일관되게 우세했다(Alkandari 2025 SR+MA). 즉 DBBM은 "용적 지지체"이지 "골형성 촉진제"가 아니며, 신생골 속도가 우선인 경우 BCP가 더 적합하다.

**Q2.** 상악동거상술에서 PRF 계열 보조제를 DBBM에 추가할 때 L-PRF와 PRP 중 어느 것이 잔존이식재 감소에 더 근거가 있습니까? *(근거: Almutairi 2025 + Zhang 2025)*

> **모범답안**: **L-PRF가 더 근거가 강하다.** Almutairi 2025 SR+MA(5 RCT)에서 L-PRF+DBBM은 잔존이식재를 −7.93%로 감소시켰고, 이 결과의 **I²=0%** — 연구 간 이질성이 없는 매우 강한 일관성이다. 반면 더 넓은 PRP·PRF·PRGF 통합 분석(Zhang 2025, 16편)에서 잔존이식재 감소는 **PRF·PRGF에서만 유의**하고 **PRP는 유의하지 않았다**. 결론: 보조제는 계열 효과(혈소판 농축물 전체)가 아니라 아형 특이적 — PRP보다 PRF/PRGF를 선택하고, 임플란트 안정성(ISQ/RFA)·생존율에는 어느 혈소판 농축물도 효과가 없다는 점을 함께 기억한다.

**Q3.** 상악동거상술에 사용할 DBBM 입자 크기를 선택해야 합니다. 대입자(1–2mm)와 소입자(0.25–1mm) 중 어느 것이 조직학적으로 우수합니까? *(근거: Li 2023 SR+MA)*

> **모범답안**: **차이 없다.** Li 2023 SR+MA(4 RCT, n=94, 상악동거상)에서 DBBM 대입자(1–2mm) vs 소입자(0.25–1mm)의 결합조직·신생골·잔존이식재 비율 **모두 유의차 없음**. 입자 크기는 재료 선택 기준이 아니라 **술식 편의 변수** — 수술 조작성·void space 충전 용이성을 고려해 선택하면 된다. 재료 선택 시 실질적으로 더 중요한 변수는 보조제(L-PRF 추가 여부), BCP vs DBBM 선택, 콜라겐 함량 등 조성이며 Sousa 2026이 보여주듯 같은 "이종골"이라도 콜라겐 함량(10% vs 25%)에 따라 초기 치유 결과가 갈린다.
