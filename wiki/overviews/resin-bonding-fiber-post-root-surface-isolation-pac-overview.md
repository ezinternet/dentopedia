> - **핵심 명제**: 기존 접착 개요(CHX·10-MDP·수분·가수분해)가 다루지 않는 4가지 **도전적 임상 환경**에 대한 결합 근거 — 섬유 포스트(fiber post, FP) 표면 전처리, 고령 환자 근면 우식(root caries) 수복, 치경부 비우식성 병소(Noncarious Cervical Lesion, NCCL) 격리법, 프로안토시아니딘(proanthocyanidin, PAC) 프라이머의 층별 내구성 기전.
> - **FP 전처리 — 레이저 = H2O2**: Nd:YVO4 레이저(1064 nm)로 GFP(Glass Fiber Post) 표면처리 시 Ra 1.32 µm로 24% 과산화수소(1.28 µm)와 통계적 동등; 두 처리 모두 무처리(0.43 µm) 대비 결합강도 유의 우위. 화학제 없는 대안으로 임상 활용 가능 (El-Azzouni 2026, in-vitro).
> - **시멘트-치근부 방향 상호작용**: 전통형 이중경화 레진시멘트(Conventional Dual-Cure Resin Cement, CDCRC)는 치경부→치근단 방향으로 결합강도 감소(최대 11.47 MPa 치경부), 자가접착형(Self-Adhesive Dual-Cure Resin Cement, SADCRC)은 반대로 치근단 방향으로 증가(최대 11.31 MPa 치근단) — 치근 위치별 시멘트 선택 근거.
> - **4-META/MMA-TBB + 유기복합 충진재**: 수분 조절 어려운 노인 근면 우식 수복에 Super-Bond 베이스에 유기복합 충진재(Organic Composite filler, OC) 3 wt% 첨가 시 열순환(TC) 후 전단접착강도(Shear Bond Strength, SBS) 유의 상승 (OC_multi ↑, p=0.0376); 반면 45S5 생활성유리(Bioactive Glass, BG) 3 wt%는 경화 지연·강도 저하·수산화인회석(Hydroxyapatite, HA) 형성 실패 (Kikuta 2025, in-vitro).
> - **NCCL 격리법 동등**: 1단계 자가산부식 접착제 사용 NCCL 수복에서 러버댐(Rubber Dam, RD)과 코튼롤/견인사(Cotton Roll/Retraction Cord, CR/RC)는 18개월 유지율(73% vs 73%), 술의 시간(20.8 vs 21.2분), 환자 선호도(54% vs 46%) 모두 차이 없음 — CR/RC 격리 임상적으로 유효 (Loguercio 2015, split-mouth RCT).
> - **PAC 프라이머 층별 기전**: AB형 PAC 중성 프라이머는 1년 후 혼성층(Hybrid Layer, HL)·하부 상아질(Underlying Dentin, UD) 복합탄성계수(Complex Modulus, E*) 향상 및 미세투과도(Micropermeability) 감소를 동시 달성; 삼량체(DP3, PM-AB) > 사량체(DP4) — 교원섬유 간 공간(interfibrillar space) 적합도 우위 (Neves 2026, in-vitro).
> - **PAC vs CHX 위치 분리**: CHX는 MMP 억제(기존 durability 개요 항목), PAC은 콜라겐 가교(collagen cross-linking)를 통한 기질 강화 — 상보 기전으로 병용 가능성; 현재 PAC는 임상 RCT 미흡, 내구성 근거는 in-vitro 1년.
> - **임상 한 줄**: FP 시멘테이션은 레이저 전처리·치근 위치 맞춤 시멘트; 노인 근면 우식엔 OC 충진 Super-Bond; NCCL 수복에 CR/RC는 러버댐 대등; PAC 프라이머는 HL+UD 구조 강화로 미래 durability 프로토콜 후보.

## Three-line Summary

Four in-vitro/RCT studies address adhesive bonding in challenging contexts not covered by existing durability and strategy overviews: glass fiber post (GFP) surface conditioning, 4-META/MMA-TBB resin modification for sclerotic root dentin, isolation equivalence for subgingival NCCL, and AB-type proanthocyanidin (PAC) collagen cross-linking.

Key numbers: laser = H2O2 conditioning (Ra 1.32 vs 1.28 µm; bond strength statistically equal at all canal thirds); organic composite filler raised post-thermocycling SBS while bioactive glass failed to form hydroxyapatite at 3 wt%; rubber dam vs cotton roll/retraction cord showed 73% vs 73% retention at 18 months (P>0.05); trimeric PAC (PM-AB) maintained highest hybrid-layer and underlying-dentin E* and lowest micropermeability at 1 year.

These results collectively redirect clinicians toward context-specific preparation protocols — post-surface chemistry, filler class, isolation pragmatism, and collagen biomodulation — as the levers that determine bond performance in difficult environments.

## 세줄요약

글래스 파이버 포스트(GFP) 표면 전처리, 4-META/MMA-TBB 충진재 개질, 치경부 비우식성 병소(NCCL) 격리법, AB형 프로안토시아니딘(PAC) 콜라겐 가교 — 기존 내구성·전략 개요가 다루지 않는 4개의 도전적 접착 환경에 대한 in-vitro/RCT 근거를 통합.

레이저 = H2O2 표면처리(Ra 동등, 모든 치근 3등분 결합강도 동등); 유기복합 충진재만 열순환 후 전단접착강도 상승(BG는 HA 형성 실패); 러버댐 vs 코튼롤/견인사 18개월 유지율 73% vs 73%(P>0.05); 삼량체 PAC(PM-AB)가 1년간 혼성층·하부상아질 E*최고·미세투과도 최저 유지.

이들 결과는 도전적 환경에서의 접착을 표준 술식 최적화(용매·건조·CHX)만으로는 해결할 수 없음을 보여주며, 포스트 표면 화학, 충진재 선택, 격리 실용주의, 콜라겐 생체조절이라는 맥락별 전처리 전략이 결합 성능을 결정한다는 근거를 제시한다.

## Summary

The existing resin-bonding overviews — [[overviews/resin-dentin-bond-durability-degradation-overview]] (covering water-tree, MMP/cathepsin, CHX, 10-MDP nanolayering, ethanol-wet bonding) and [[overviews/adhesive-bonding-strategies-overview]] (covering universal vs SE vs E&R, saliva contamination, silane timing, 3D-printed crown bonding) — address the mainstream durability and system-selection landscape. This page fills four gaps in that landscape: (1) what to do to the post surface before cementation; (2) which filler to add to 4-META/MMA-TBB when restoring root caries in moisture-compromised older adults; (3) whether isolation method choice matters for NCCL adhesive restorations; (4) how AB-type proanthocyanidin primers stabilize the resin-dentin interface layer by layer.

Each gap is answered by one focused study. The unifying thread is that each addresses a context where standard adhesive protocols — universal adhesive, phosphoric acid etch, rubber dam, chlorhexidine primer — either cannot be applied as written or are less decisive than a preparation-step that precedes them: conditioning the post surface with laser, modifying the resin matrix with organic filler, accepting retraction cord as equivalent to rubber dam, or cross-linking dentin collagen with a neutral PAC primer before any adhesive is placed.

## Axis 1: Glass Fiber Post Surface Conditioning — Laser vs H2O2

Source: [[resin-bonding/el-azzouni-2026-laser-hydrogen-peroxide-fiber-post-dual-cure]]

**Clinical question**: Is Nd:YVO4 laser irradiation a viable replacement for 24% hydrogen peroxide as a GFP surface conditioner?

**Answer**: Yes — laser and H2O2 conditioning produce statistically equivalent surface roughness and push-out bond strength at all three canal thirds, for both cement classes. Both outperform the unconditioned control.

### Push-out bond strength (MPa) after 10,000 thermocycles

| Conditioning | Cement | Cervical | Middle | Apical |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| None (control) | CDCRC | 6.21±0.91 | 5.09±0.13 | 3.78±0.21 |
| None (control) | SADCRC | 5.12±0.20 | 5.86±0.11 | 6.75±0.21 |
| H2O2-silane | CDCRC | 11.47±0.52 | 9.67±0.43 | 7.02±0.54 |
| H2O2-silane | SADCRC | 9.41±0.27 | 10.80±0.19 | 11.31±0.21 |
| Laser-silane | CDCRC | 11.26±1.17 | 9.52±0.54 | 7.32±0.43 |
| Laser-silane | SADCRC | 9.56±0.17 | 10.76±0.23 | 11.23±0.17 |

CDCRC = conventional dual-cure resin cement (All Bond 2 + Duo-Link); SADCRC = self-adhesive dual-cure resin cement (RelyX Unicem). Laser vs H2O2 not significant at any third (p>0.05).

**Surface roughness**: control Ra 0.43±0.11 µm < H2O2 1.28±0.18 ≈ laser 1.32±0.22 µm (laser vs H2O2 p>0.05; both vs control p<0.05).

### Cement choice by canal region — opposite gradients

| Cement class | Cervical | Apical | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| CDCRC | Strongest (11.47 MPa) | Weakest (7.02 MPa) | Phosphoric-acid etch efficiency drops apically (fewer/narrower tubules, limited light) |
| SADCRC | Weakest cervically | Strongest (11.31 MPa) | Moisture-tolerant chemistry; less dependent on tubule geometry |

**Clinical decision rule**: When conditioning is used, CDCRC is preferred in the cervical third; SADCRC is preferred in the middle and apical thirds. Either cement substantially exceeds the ~5 MPa masticatory-force threshold across all thirds when conditioning is applied.

**Advantage of laser**: avoids oxidative tissue exposure from hydrogen peroxide; no chemical agent in the canal. Both require a silane application step after conditioning.

## Axis 2: 4-META/MMA-TBB Resin for Root Caries — Filler Class

Source: [[resin-bonding/kikuta-2025-fillers-4-meta-mma-tbb-resin-root-caries]]

**Clinical question**: Which filler class best supports 4-META/MMA-TBB (Super-Bond) chemistry as a restorative material for root caries in moisture-compromised older patients?

**Broader evidence context**: At the network-meta-analysis level, Schmidt et al. (2024) pooled 9 RCTs (1,263 root caries lesions, 473 patients) comparing adhesive-class × restorative-material strategies and found no statistically robust restorative hierarchy for root caries — 24-month point estimates favored 2–3-step etch-and-rinse composite (OR 2.65) and RMGIC (OR 2.05) over GIC, but CINeMA confidence was low and the differences were not significant after adjustment. This NMA is the broader restorative/adhesive evidence base for root caries generally, into which Kikuta (2025)'s filler-class finding fits as material-science depth for the specific 4-META/MMA-TBB (Super-Bond) arm.

**Answer**: Organic composite filler (OC) at 3 wt%. Bioactive glass (BG) and aluminosilicate glass (AS) are detrimental at this load.

### Filler comparison at 3 wt%

| Property | OC (organic composite) | BG (45S5 bioactive glass) | AS (aluminosilicate glass) | BSP (commercial control) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Curing time | Significantly shorter | ≈ BSP (prolonged vs OC) | Significantly shorter | Reference |
| Flexural strength | OC_multi: not significantly < BSP | Significantly lower | Significantly lower | Reference |
| SBS 24 h (root dentin) | No difference vs BSP | No difference | No difference | Reference |
| SBS after 10,000 TC | **Significantly increased** (↑) | Significantly decreased (↓) | No change | Significantly decreased (↓) |
| Hydroxyapatite (SBF 4/8 wk) | Not tested | **Not detected** (GIXRD) | Not tested | Minimal |

BSP = Bondfill SB Plus (Sun Medical); TC = thermocycling; SBS = shear bond strength; SBF = simulated body fluid.

**Why BG fails**: 3 wt% load is insufficient for hydroxyapatite formation (literature requires 30–50 wt%); 45S5 glass likely raises local pH, suppressing the TBB radical initiation and prolonging cure; the resulting weaker polymer matrix degrades faster under thermocycling.

**Why OC succeeds**: reduced polymerization shrinkage stress and better interfacial stress distribution during thermocycling, attributable to the organic filler's modulus-matching and particle morphology.

**Geriatric relevance**: 4-META/MMA-TBB is chemically cured (no light access needed), moisture-tolerant (TBB initiator functions in wet fields), and bonds to root dentin without separate etching — properties that address the specific constraints of restoring root caries in elderly patients with limited mouth opening, gingival recession, and subgingival margins.

## Axis 3: Isolation for NCCL Adhesive Restorations — Rubber Dam vs Cotton Roll/Retraction Cord

Source: [[resin-bonding/loguercio-2015-isolation-method-operative-field-gingival.md]]

**Clinical question**: Does isolation method (rubber dam vs cotton roll/retraction cord) affect retention, gingival damage, patient preference, or chairside time for adhesive NCCL restorations?

**Broader decision context**: Peumans et al. (2020)'s narrative review frames NCCL management as a broader when/why/how decision — an etiologic checklist to decide whether to monitor or intervene, adhesive restoration positioned as the last-resort intervention, and annual maintenance (margin repolishing) to prolong restoration life. Within that framework, Loguercio (2015)'s isolation-equivalence finding answers one specific "how" question: which isolation method to use once an adhesive restoration has already been indicated.

**Answer**: No. The two methods are equivalent across all outcomes at 18 months.

### Outcomes at 18 months (split-mouth RCT, n=30, 60 NCCLs)

| Outcome | Rubber dam | Cotton roll/retraction cord | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retention | 73% | 73% | NS |
| Patient preference | 54% | 46% | 0.86 |
| Chairside time (min) | 20.8±5.2 | 21.2±5.2 | 0.77 |
| Gingival laceration | 63% | 37% | 0.64 |
| 1-week bleeding | 60% | 47% | 0.57 |
| 1-week sensitivity | 43% | 27% | 0.52 |

All gingival parameters were transient/reversible at 1 week.

**Contextual note on the 73% retention rate**: this reflects the one-step self-etch adhesive used (GO!, SDI), not the isolation method. The study does not address whether better adhesives would show group differences. The null result for isolation method holds within the adhesive system studied.

**Scope of validity**: results apply to experienced operators performing NCCL restorations with subgingival margins. Cotton roll/retraction cord cannot replace rubber dam for restorations requiring a dry field during composite layering in non-cervical lesions.

**Decision rule**: For NCCL (Class V) adhesive restorations at or below the gingival margin, cotton roll plus retraction cord is a valid, clinically equivalent alternative to rubber dam when placed by a trained, experienced operator.

## Axis 4: AB-Type Proanthocyanidin Primers — Layer-Specific Durability

Source: [[resin-bonding/neves-2026-ab-type-proanthocyanidin-primers-nano-dma]]

**Clinical question**: How do AB-type proanthocyanidin neutral primers stabilize the resin-dentin interface, and which structural class is most effective?

**Answer**: All PAC primers raise the complex modulus (E*) of the hybrid layer and underlying dentin and reduce micropermeability at 1 year. Trimeric (DP3, PM-AB) outperforms tetrameric (DP4) due to better fit in interfibrillar spaces. Underlying mineralized dentin is also reinforced — not only the demineralized hybrid zone.

### Layer-by-layer outcomes at 1 year (nano-DMA + rhodamine micro-permeability)

| Interface layer | Unprimed control | Any AB-type PAC | Best PAC (PM-AB, trimeric DP3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hybrid layer E* | Reference | Significantly higher | Highest; stable across 6 mo → 1 yr |
| Underlying dentin E* | Reference | Significantly higher | Highest |
| Adhesive layer E* | Reference | No difference at 6 mo | CV-ABB & PM-AB higher at 1 yr |
| Hybrid layer permeability | Reference | Significantly lower (all PAC, p<0.001) | Lowest |
| Underlying dentin permeability | **Increased at 1 year** | Stable (no increase) | Stable |

**Structure–activity relationship**: DP3 > DP4 (trimeric > tetrameric); among trimerics, PM-AB (Pinus massoniana A-linkage) > CV-AB (Cinnamomum verum). A-type linkage preferentially reinforces the hybrid layer; B-type the adhesive-layer interface at long term.

**Mechanism summary**: PAC cross-links free amine/hydroxyl groups on collagen fibrils (both demineralized matrix and mineralized dentin), reducing enzymatic degradation access points and displacing unbound water from the interfibrillar space — dual effect of E* increase and permeability decrease. Damping capacity (tan δ) is preserved within the native dentin range (0.02–0.3), indicating the collagen remains viscoelastic rather than brittled.

**Positioning relative to CHX**: CHX inhibits MMP activity (covered in [[overviews/resin-dentin-bond-durability-degradation-overview]]); PAC cross-links the collagen substrate directly. The two mechanisms are complementary and target different failure pathways. PAC primers currently lack clinical RCT evidence; the 1-year in-vitro data are mechanistically compelling but cannot be translated to a clinical recommendation without bridging RCT data.

**Where this sits in the broader crosslinker/MMP-inhibitor literature**: At the systematic-review level, Zhou et al. (2025) pooled 14 in vitro studies (11/14 low risk of bias) and found that collagen crosslinkers as a class — not only PAC but also EDTA, phytic acid, rosmarinic acid, and sodium ascorbate — broadly restore resin-dentin bond strength on NaOCl-treated dentin, with the magnitude of benefit modulated by NaOCl concentration and exposure time; Neves (2026)'s nano-DMA data supply the layer-specific hybrid-layer/underlying-dentin mechanism (E* gain, permeability drop) that plausibly underlies this bulk-strength signal for one specific crosslinker family (AB-type PAC). On the complementary MMP-inhibition side, Kiuru et al. (2021) pooled a 21-article chlorhexidine (CHX) subset from a 43-study SR+MA and found no effect on immediate bond strength (p=0.308) but significant preservation at 6, 12, and 24 months (all p<0.001) — the SR+MA-level evidence for the CHX pathway that this axis contrasts against PAC's direct collagen cross-linking mechanism.

**PAC application protocol in this study**: glycolic acid etch (35%, rinse) → 6.5% PAC w/v in HEPES buffer (pH ~7, 1 min) → rinse out → model methacrylate adhesive. The neutral pH (not acidic like most current conditioners) is intentional to protect collagen from low-pH damage during the cross-linking step.

## Cross-Axis Synthesis

These four papers share a structural argument: standard adhesive protocols — phosphoric acid etch, rubber dam, conventional cement, no pretreatment beyond universal adhesive — are not universally optimal once the clinical context departs from sound coronal enamel-dentin under dry conditions.

| Context | Standard risk | Evidence-based modification | Evidence level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glass fiber post cementation | Smooth epoxy surface → adhesive failure | H2O2 or laser conditioning + silane before cementation | In vitro (El-Azzouni 2026) |
| Root caries in elderly (moist field) | Poor bond, hydrolytic degradation in wet root dentin | OC-modified 4-META/MMA-TBB (not BG-modified) | In vitro (Kikuta 2025) |
| Subgingival NCCL restoration | Assumption: rubber dam mandatory for acceptable retention | CR + retraction cord equally valid in experienced hands | Split-mouth RCT 18 mo (Loguercio 2015) |
| Hybrid-layer durability at any site | Exposed demineralized collagen degrades enzymatically over time | AB-type PAC rinse primer before adhesive (trimeric DP3 preferred) | In vitro 1 year (Neves 2026) |

None of these four modifications replaces good adhesive technique. They are add-on steps that address specific vulnerabilities in specific contexts: the post surface being inert to adhesive chemistry without pretreatment; root dentin being sclerotic, wet, and inaccessible; subgingival margins being the reason rubber dam was assumed mandatory; and demineralized collagen being the structural weak point that both CHX (MMP inhibition) and PAC (cross-linking) target from different directions.
