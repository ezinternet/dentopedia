> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **결론 (thesis)**: 치과 방사선과 임신은 "얼마나 위험한가"가 아니라 **"몇 자릿수 떨어져 있는가"**의 문제다. 태아 조직반응 (tissue reaction, 결정적 영향) 역치는 최저 50–100 mGy인데, 차폐 촬영실 직원의 연간 피폭은 0.13–0.18 mSv, 환자 구내촬영 1회 태아 선량은 약 6–7 µGy다 — 각각 수백 배, 약 1만 배 이상 차이.
> - **태아 역치**: 임신 전 기간 50 mGy 미만에서는 관찰된 영향 없음. 50–100 mGy는 17주 이전 "잠재적 영향"뿐, 100 mGy 초과부터 27주 이전 임상적으로 검출. 기형 약 200 mGy, 중증 지적장애 60–310 mGy (8–15주). 확률적 소아암 위험은 10 mGy당 평생 약 0.4% (역치 없음 가정).
> - **직원 선량의 최대 결정인자는 차폐 촬영실**: 한국 치과의사 실측 연 0.13 (여)–0.18 (남) mSv vs 차폐 없는 개방형 진료실 조무사 연 5.3 mSv (~30배). 납 앞치마 (lead apron)가 아니라 구조 차폐 (structural shielding)가 핵심.
> - **포터블 (handheld) PA**: 산란선 차폐판 (backscatter shield) 뒤·초점 수직면 뒤에서는 산란선 미검출 (Rottke), 다만 잡는 자세가 술자 선량을 유의하게 바꾸고 (Makdissi) EADMFR은 "예외적 사용"으로 분류 (Berkhout) — 사람에 따라 변하는 유일한 노출원이라 임신 기간 배정 조정의 합리적 근거.
> - **법적 한도 — 세 개의 서로 다른 양(quantity)**: EU 태아 등가선량 1 mSv (남은 임신기간), 한국 원안위 고시 하복부 표면 등가선량 2 mSv (임신 확인~출산, 원자력안전법 종사자 대상), 한국 진단용 방사선 규칙은 **임신 조항 없음** (일반 한도 연 50 mSv). NCRP는 태아 5 mSv.
> - **한국 치과에 직접 적용되는 것**: 진단용 방사선 규칙 (선량계 착용, TLD 3개월 판독, 건강진단 2년) + 근로기준법 §74⑤ (본인 요구 시 쉬운 업무 전환 **의무**). 원안위 2 mSv는 원내 목표로 차용하는 것이 합리적 (적용 여부는 해석).
> - **임신 ≠ 자동 배제**: EURATOM·스페인 합의문 모두 업무 조건 평가를 요구하지 자동 제외를 요구하지 않는다. 임신 중 근무한 중재시술 의사의 보고 선량은 모두 ≤1 mSv. 결정은 정보를 받은 본인이 — 원하면 빼 주고(한국은 법적 의무), 원치 않는데 일괄 배제하지도 않는다.
> - **환자 측도 같은 결론**: 디지털 구내 1회 태아 약 6–7 µGy, 팬텀 전 모달리티 0.009–6.9 µGy, 교익 몬테카를로 최고 27 nGy. 적응증이 있으면 임신부 치과촬영을 미루지 않으며, 태아·갑상선 차폐는 권고되지 않는다 (AAOMR/ADA).
> - **긴장점**: 한국 직원 한도 (50 mSv)는 EU (20 mSv)보다 높고, 법령마다 측정량이 달라 (태아선량·하복부 표면·유효선량) 같은 숫자로 직접 비교하면 안 된다. 선량계 착용 위치 (목깃 vs 복부)도 해석을 바꾼다.
> - **임상 takeaway (원장용)**: ① 차폐실 파노·CBCT는 평소대로 ② 포터블 PA는 가능하면 다른 직원 배정 ③ 원내 목표 "임신 확인~출산 하복부 2 mSv 미만"을 선량계로 추적 ④ 본인 요구 시 촬영 제외는 법적 의무 ⑤ 걱정되는 노출은 선량계 기록을 들고 산과 상담.
> - **공백**: 임신 치과 직원 대상 선량 측정 연구 없음, 핸드헬드의 복부(허리) 선량 수치 미보유, 자녀 결과 추적 연구 거의 없음, 태아 역치 자료는 원폭·사고·동물 기반 범위값.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 17 held documents (5 fetal-dose/threshold papers, 7 occupational-dose and protection papers, 2 patient-shielding guidelines, 3 Korean legal texts) on pregnancy and dental radiation, covering both pregnant staff and pregnant patients.

Fetal tissue-reaction thresholds begin at 50–100 mGy, whereas annual dose to staff in shielded Korean dental settings is 0.13–0.18 mSv and fetal dose from one intraoral radiograph is ~6–7 µGy — separations of several hundred-fold and >10,000-fold; the legal limits (EU fetus 1 mSv, Korean abdomen surface 2 mSv) sit in between.

For a Korean dental clinic the operative levers are structural shielding, dosimetry, reassignment of handheld exposures, and the Labor Standards Act duty to convert a pregnant worker's tasks on request; pregnancy is not grounds for automatic exclusion, nor for withholding indicated dental radiographs from pregnant patients.

## 세줄요약

임신과 치과 방사선에 관한 위키 보유 문서 17건(태아 선량·역치 5편, 직업피폭·방호 7편, 환자 차폐 지침 2편, 국내 법령 3건)을 임신 직원과 임신 환자 양쪽에서 종합.

태아 조직반응 역치는 50–100 mGy부터인데, 한국 차폐 치과 환경의 직원 연간 선량은 0.13–0.18 mSv, 구내촬영 1회 태아 선량은 약 6–7 µGy로 각각 수백 배, 1만 배 이상 떨어져 있고, 법적 한도(EU 태아 1 mSv, 한국 하복부 2 mSv)는 그 사이에 놓인다.

한국 치과에서 실제 작동하는 장치는 구조 차폐·선량계·포터블 촬영 재배정, 그리고 근로기준법상 본인 요구 시 업무 전환 의무이며, 임신은 직원의 자동 배제 사유도, 임신 환자의 적응증 있는 촬영을 보류할 이유도 아니다.

## Summary

Pregnancy questions in dental radiology come from two directions — the pregnant staff member who takes radiographs and the pregnant patient who needs them — and both are routinely answered by intuition rather than numbers. The wiki now holds enough to answer them quantitatively along a single dose ladder: biological thresholds at the top (tens to hundreds of mGy), legal limits in the middle (1–2 mSv for pregnancy, 20–50 mSv/year for workers), and real dental exposures at the bottom (nGy–µGy fetal doses for patients; sub-mSv annual doses for shielded staff).

The thesis is that the decisive variables are not pregnancy per se but **geometry and shielding** (staff behind a structural barrier vs. in an open clinic; fetus outside vs. inside the primary beam) and **operator-dependent exposures** (handheld units). Where those are controlled, every held source — EU guideline, Spanish consensus, dental systematic review, phantom and Monte Carlo studies — converges on "continue, monitor, don't exclude, don't withhold". The Korean legal layer adds one directly enforceable employer duty (job conversion on request) and one borrowed numeric target (2 mSv lower-abdomen surface), but no dental-specific pregnancy limit.

## Axis 1 — Fetal Effect Thresholds (what dose actually harms?)

Two effect classes (Mainprize 2023, adopting ICRP 103 terminology):

- **Tissue reactions** (formerly deterministic) — threshold effects; ICRP defines the threshold as the dose affecting >1% of those exposed.
- **Stochastic effects** — childhood cancer, modelled linear no-threshold.

| Gestational stage (post-conception) | Effect | Threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 weeks | All-or-none (embryo death or no effect) | 50–100 mGy | [[radiology/mainprize-2023-ionizing-radiation-pregnancy-fetal-effects]], [[radiology/velazquez-martin-2025-pregnant-staff-occupational-radiation-consensus]] |
| Organogenesis (2–8 weeks) | Malformations (skeleton, eyes, genitals) | ~200 mGy | same |
| Organogenesis | Growth retardation | 200–250 mGy | [[radiology/velazquez-martin-2025-pregnant-staff-occupational-radiation-consensus]] |
| 8–15 weeks | Severe intellectual disability (high risk) | 60–310 mGy | same |
| 8–15 weeks | IQ loss | ~20 points per 100 mGy | same |
| 8–15 weeks | Microcephaly | 200 mGy | same |
| 16–25 weeks | Severe intellectual disability (low risk) | 250–280 mGy | same |
| Any stage | No observable effects | **<50 mGy** | [[radiology/mainprize-2023-ionizing-radiation-pregnancy-fetal-effects]] (ACR-SPR) |
| >27 weeks | No relevant tissue reactions in diagnostic range | — | same |
| Stochastic | Lifetime cancer risk | ~0.4% per 10 mGy; childhood cancer death 1/1,700 at 10 mGy | same |

The ACR-SPR rule summarised by Mainprize: 50–100 mGy gives only "potential effects" before 17 weeks; above 100 mGy and before 27 weeks effects become clinically detectable. Gamba 2024 cites slightly different round numbers (embryonic death ~100 mGy; intellectual disability/microcephaly 300 mGy at 8–25 weeks) — the ranges overlap and the conclusion is the same. The counselling statement from Mainprize is that at diagnostic doses there is >99% likelihood the conceptus is unaffected.

## Axis 2 — Fetal Dose from Dental Imaging (the pregnant patient)

| Exposure | Fetal dose | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Bitewing, 25-week ICRP phantom, digital sensor 0.05 s | ≤0.0054 µGy (5 nGy, brain) | [[radiology/andersson-2026-bitewing-fetal-dose-monte-carlo]] |
| Bitewing, phosphor plate 0.25 s | ≤0.027 µGy (27 nGy, brain) | same |
| All dental modalities (intraoral, pano, ceph, CBCT), phantom upper bound, unshielded | 0.009–6.9 µGy | [[radiology/kelaranta-2016-dental-xray-fetal-breast-dose-lead-shield]] |
| Same, with lead shields | 0.005–2.1 µGy | same |
| Single intraoral digital examination | ~6–7 µGy | [[radiology/gamba-2024-dental-imaging-pregnancy-fetal-dose-sr]] |
| Background radiation over a pregnancy | ~0.9 mGy | same |

Even the CBCT upper bound (6.9 µGy) is <1% of 1 mSv and roughly 1/14,000 of the 100 mGy embryonic-death figure. Maternal tissue absorbs scatter so efficiently that the 25-week fetus receives ~0.0015 of the mother's dose (Andersson). Lead aprons reduce fetal dose 39–97% (Gamba) but the absolute benefit is negligible; AAOMR/ADA recommend discontinuing fetal, gonadal and thyroid shielding ([[radiology/benavides-2023-patient-shielding-dentomaxillofacial-radiography]]). The one contrary signal — Hujoel's low-birth-weight association — was judged high risk of bias in exposure measurement and was not reproduced by Mortazavi (Gamba). **Conclusion: indicated dental radiographs should not be withheld or postponed because of pregnancy.**

## Axis 3 — Occupational Dose to Dental Staff (the pregnant worker)

| Setting | Dose | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Panoramic scatter at 1 m, per exposure | ≤0.10 µSv; 500 exposures/y ≤0.04 mSv | [[radiology/gijbels-2005-dosimetry-digital-panoramic-occupational-exposure]] |
| Korean dentists, national dose registry (shielded rooms) | 0.13 (female) – 0.18 (male) mSv/y | [[radiology/kim-2016-occupational-radiation-procedures-doses-korean-dentists]] |
| Spanish medical radiodiagnostic workers | mean 0.6 mSv/y; 96% of monitored workers <1 mSv | [[radiology/velazquez-martin-2025-pregnant-staff-occupational-radiation-consensus]] |
| Pregnant interventionalists (cath lab), published series | all ≤1 mSv fetal/abdominal; most at background | same |
| Open clinics without structural shielding (Iraq), assistants | 5.28 mSv/y | [[radiology/kabier-2025-xray-radiation-exposure-open-dental-clinics-tld]] |
| Handheld unit, operator behind focal-spot plane | scatter not detected; control area max 42 cm | [[radiology/rottke-2018-operator-safety-during-the-acquisition]] |
| Handheld unit, worst hold position | peak 0.031 mGy (left palm); position significantly changes dose | [[radiology/makdissi-2016-effects-device-position-operators-radiation]] |

Two conclusions follow. First, **structural shielding dominates**: the ~30-fold gap between Korean shielded practice and open clinics is larger than anything personal protection achieves. Second, **the handheld unit is the one exposure that depends on the person**: it is safe when used per manufacturer instructions with the backscatter shield (Rottke; ADA [[radiology/benavides-2024-optimizing-radiation-safety-dentistry]]), but dose rises with poor positioning (Makdissi), and EADMFR classes handheld use as exceptional, justification-only ([[radiology/berkhout-2015-justification-and-good-practice-using]]). That variability — not a measured excess — is the rationale for reassigning handheld exposures during pregnancy.

Even if a whole year of the Korean shielded-room dose (0.18 mSv) reached the fetus unattenuated — a deliberately pessimistic assumption, since badges read surface dose and the body attenuates — it would be about 1/280 of the 50 mGy lowest threshold and below both the EU 1 mSv and the Korean 2 mSv pregnancy limits (interpretation combining Kim 2016, Mainprize 2023 and the limits below; 1 mGy ≈ 1 mSv for diagnostic X-rays).

## Axis 4 — Dose Limits and the Korean Legal Layer

| Instrument | Pregnancy provision | Quantity | Applies to Korean dental staff? |
|---|---|---|---|
| EU RP136 / Directive 96/29 ([[radiology/horner-2004-european-guidelines-radiation-protection-dental-radiology]]) | Fetus <1 mSv for remainder of pregnancy; occupational 20 mSv/y | Fetal equivalent dose | No (reference standard) |
| EURATOM 2013/59 / ICRP (via Velázquez Martín 2025) | Fetus treated as public, ≤1 mSv; pregnancy ≠ exclusion | Fetal dose | No (reference) |
| NCRP (via Velázquez Martín 2025) | Fetus 5 mSv | Fetal dose | No (reference) |
| Korean MOHW diagnostic radiation rule ([[radiology/korea-mohw-2026-diagnostic-radiation-safety-rule]]) | **None**; general 50 mSv/y, 100 mSv/5 y | Effective dose | **Yes, directly** |
| Korean NSSC notice §4 ([[radiology/korea-nssc-2026-radiation-protection-pregnancy-dose-limit]]) | 2 mSv from confirmation to delivery | Lower-abdomen surface equivalent dose | Nuclear Safety Act workers; for dental staff a borrowed internal target (interpretation) |
| Korean Labor Standards Act §65/§74 + Annex 4 ([[practice-management/korea-moel-2026-labor-standards-act-pregnant-worker-protection]]) | No radiation work exceeding Nuclear Safety Act limits; easier work on request; no overtime; 2 h/day reduction <12 wk or >32 wk | — | **Yes, directly** |

The limits are **not interchangeable numbers**: fetal dose (EU/ICRP), abdomen-surface equivalent dose (Korean NSSC) and whole-body effective dose (Korean MOHW) are different physical quantities measured at different places. A collar-level badge reading is not a fetal dose. The Korean worker limit (50 mSv/y) is also numerically higher than the EU 20 mSv/y.

## Clinical Decision Ladder — Pregnant Staff Member in a Korean Dental Clinic

1. **Notification** — when the staff member reports the pregnancy, explain the evidence: thresholds ≥50 mGy vs. shielded-room doses of ~0.1–0.2 mSv/year (Axis 1, 3). The NSSC limit is triggered by notification; adopt the same logic internally.
2. **Shielded panoramic/CBCT room** — continue normal duties; always expose from behind the barrier, door closed, never hold the patient (Axis 3, Kabier/Kim).
3. **Handheld PA** — reassign to another staff member where possible; if unavoidable, backscatter shield fitted, body behind the focal-spot plane, manufacturer hold position, dosimeter worn (Rottke, Makdissi, Berkhout).
4. **Dosimetry** — badge worn as legally required, TLD read every 3 months (Korean MOHW rule); track against an internal target of <2 mSv lower-abdomen from confirmation to delivery (borrowed NSSC value). Consider abdominal placement for the pregnancy period (Velázquez Martín recommends a monthly abdominal dosimeter).
5. **Choice** — if she asks to stop radiography, convert her tasks: this is a legal duty (Labor Standards Act §74⑤). If she wishes to continue, do not exclude her by default (EURATOM/Spanish consensus logic).
6. **Past exposure worries** — compare her dosimeter record with the thresholds; refer to her obstetrician with the record.

## Clinical Decision Ladder — Pregnant Patient

1. Radiograph only with a clinical indication (justification applies to everyone).
2. If indicated, do not postpone because of pregnancy; fetal doses are nGy–µGy (Axis 2).
3. Use rectangular collimation and fast receptors; fetal/thyroid shielding is not recommended (Benavides 2023), though local rules may differ.
4. Counsel with numbers: >99% likelihood the fetus is unaffected even at full diagnostic doses; a dental radiograph is a tiny fraction of the ~0.9 mGy natural background over a pregnancy.

## Contradictions and Tensions

- **Lead apron for staff vs. patient** — for patients the guidance is to stop shielding (Benavides 2023, Kelaranta, Andersson); for interventional staff Velázquez Martín recommends 0.25 mm vest+skirt. Not a contradiction: staff face continuous scatter in cath labs, patients receive single exposures to distant anatomy. In shielded dental rooms the barrier, not an apron, protects staff.
- **Threshold numbers differ slightly between sources** (Mainprize 50–100 mGy / 200 mGy vs. Gamba ~100 mGy / 300 mGy) — ranges from different compilations; conclusions are identical.
- **Korean vs. EU worker limits** (50 vs. 20 mSv/y) and **three different pregnancy quantities** — see Axis 4.
- **Low birth weight signal** (Hujoel) vs. null finding (Mortazavi) — Gamba rates the former high risk of bias.

## Open Questions / Evidence Gaps

1. No held study measures dose to pregnant **dental** staff; the pregnant-worker data are from cath labs (higher scatter), so they bound dental exposure from above.
2. Handheld operator dose at waist/abdomen level is not available in the held pages (Makdissi reports the peak at the palm).
3. Offspring outcome data for occupationally exposed mothers are sparse (Velázquez Martín).
4. Fetal thresholds derive largely from atomic-bomb, accident and animal data and are ranges, not point values.
5. Whether the NSSC 2 mSv limit legally binds Medical-Service-Act diagnostic staff is not stated in either text — confirm with the local public health centre.
6. Kelaranta 2016 is held as abstract only (per-modality values unavailable).
