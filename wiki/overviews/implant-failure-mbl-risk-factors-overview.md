> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **범위**: 이 오버뷰는 *골유착 이후(after osseointegration)* — 이미 하중을 받는 정착 임플란트의 실패·변연골소실(Marginal Bone Loss, MBL) 위험인자를 다룬다. 로딩 전 실패는 [[overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]] 참고 (상호보완 관계).
> - **큰 그림(giok-2026, 25편 메타분석·35개 연관성 우산리뷰)**: "확증(convincing)" 등급은 단 하나도 없다. 흡연만 관찰근거 최고등급(highly suggestive)이고, 산화(anodized) 표면은 RCT·관찰 근거 모두에서 일관 지지되는 유일한 요인.
> - **숙주 요인**: 흡연(치주염·남성과 함께 ting-2017의 3대 유의 요인), 이갈이/브럭시즘(barboza-2026 OR 4.68로 서술적 주장을 SR+MA급으로 격상), 방사선치료(pacheco-2025: 조사골 생존율 81.52% vs 94.64%, 조사 상악 RR 3.16 더 나쁨).
> - **경사(tilted) vs 수직(axial) 임플란트**: 실패위험은 동일(RR=1.02)하나 MBL 격차는 **시간의존적** — 단기 무의미, 3년(+0.08mm)·장기(+0.18mm) 유의(malak-2024). ting-2017(2017년, 추적기간 짧은 SR 종합)이 "무영향"으로 결론낸 것과 표면적으로 충돌하나, 실은 추적기간 차이로 설명됨.
> - **외과 술식 다수는 MBL에 무영향**(ting-2017, 각 4~5편 SR 종합): flapless vs flapped, 1-stage vs 2-stage(short implant 제외), 삽입토크, 연조직이식. 반면 **발치와 보존술은 MBL 감소**, **골증대 후 표준길이 임플란트는 오히려 짧은 임플란트 대체보다 MBL 더 큼** — short implant 선택을 뒷받침하는 간접 근거.
> - **Short implant(≤6mm)**: 10년 장기추적 SR+MA(lin-2026)는 long implant 대비 생존율 통계적으로 유의하게 낮으나(RR 0.92~0.94) 89%↑로 임상수용 가능; MBL·합병증은 동일. 단일기관 6.5년 코호트(kim-2026)는 short vs standard MBL 완전 동등(0.05 vs 0.12mm) — **임플란트 길이 자체보다 삽입깊이(insertion depth, r=0.372)와 나사유지(screw-retention, 생존율 격차 최대 16%p)가 실질 변수**.
> - **로딩 프로토콜**: 즉시(Immediate Loading, IL) vs 비즉시(Nonimmediate Loading, NIL) 단일임플란트 — 생존율·MBL 유의차 없음(rismanchian-2025)이나, 이는 giok-2026의 "즉시 로딩=suggestive 위험인자" 결론과 결이 다름(범위 차이: 로딩 *타이밍* 단독 vs 즉시 *식립+로딩* 결합 시나리오) — 초기안정성 확보가 핵심 전제조건이라는 점은 두 근거 모두 일치.
> - **해부학적 회피 옵션(관골 임플란트, Zygomatic Implant, ZI)**: 중증 위축 상악에서 생존율 90~100%로 높으나 부비동염이 흔함(2.4~27.2%); ZAGA 술식이 OST보다 부비동염 낮고(3.7% vs 27.2%) 즉시부하 훨씬 많이 씀(89.6% vs 22.3%)(dambrosio-2026). 의료법적 사전동의·술자숙련도 강조.
> - **1차 근거층 추가 (2026-07-17, 10 → 23편)**: 이 페이지의 축 10편 중 **7편이 우산리뷰**라 등급은 있고 효과크기가 없었다. 그 아래 층을 실으니 네 곳이 바뀐다 — ① **흡연 수치의 범위 교정**: 가장 선명한 pooled OR 2.59(fan-2024, 32편·59,246 임플란트)는 **조기실패**용이라 이 페이지 것이 아니다. 후기 창의 실측치는 moy-2005 RR 1.56과 **gjelvold-2021의 5년 MBL — 흡연자 −1.90 vs 비흡연자 −0.40 mm (p=0.021)**이며, 이 RCT에서 **유의한 MBL 예측인자는 로딩 프로토콜이 아니라 흡연 하나뿐**이었다(이 페이지 명제의 가장 깨끗한 진술). ② **흡연은 이분법이 아니라 용량반응**(naseri-2020, 개비/일) → 완전금연 실패해도 감량이 의미 있다. ③ **경사 임플란트 시간의존성이 같은 연구진 데이터로 재현**: del-fabbro 2014(≥3년 P=.30, NS) → 2022(3–18년 P<.0001, 축방향이 MBL 적음). 단 표본이 4배로 늘어 "시간" vs "검정력"이 이 쌍만으론 분리 안 되며, malak의 층화(NS → +0.08 → +0.18mm)가 그 매듭을 푼다. ④ **"충분한 초기 안정성"에 드디어 수치**: benic-2014이 RCT 동등성이 성립하는 봉투를 명시 — **삽입토크 ≥20–45 Ncm 또는 ISQ ≥60–65, 동시 골증대 없음**. giok의 "즉시로딩=suggestive"는 이 봉투 밖 관찰데이터를 포함하므로 tension의 상당부분이 해소된다.
> - **충돌 1건 표면화 (평균내지 않음)**: short implant 생존율 — arbildo-vega-2025(**SR 60편 우산리뷰**)는 표준 대비 생존·실패·보철합병증 **전부 NS**이고 **MBL은 오히려 short가 적음**(high confidence)인데, lin-2026은 생존율 유의하게 낮다(RR 0.92–0.94). 범위를 읽으면 충돌이 아니다 — arbildo-vega는 넓은 short 대역·전체 추적기간, lin은 **엄격 ≤6mm·≥10년**. 즉 결손은 **가장 짧은 길이 × 가장 긴 지평**에서만 나타난다(경사 임플란트와 같은 정의·시간 의존 구조). 인용 시: "short는 표준만큼 생존한다"는 넓은 대역엔 방어 가능, **≤6mm 10년엔 불가**.
> - **신규 축 2개 (우산리뷰층엔 없던 것)**: ① **악궁** — 상악이 나쁘다는 신호가 4개 독립 설계에서 수렴(moy 상악 RR 1.79·구치부 상악 8.9–9.7% vs 전치부 하악 2.89%; del-fabbro-2014 상악 생존 97.4% vs 하악 99.6%; barausse 4mm 상악 p=0.02; pacheco 조사 상악 최악). 어느 것도 악궁을 주가설로 시험하진 않았다. ② **유지관리** — barausse-2024에서 **위생 내원 빈도가 실패를 유의하게 낮춤(p<0.001)**, 그것도 4mm·중증위축 구치부라는 최악 조건 8년 추적에서. 우산리뷰 10편 중 이걸 수치화한 건 하나도 없다. **식립 후에 바꿀 수 있는 유일한 변수**다.
> - **금기 아닌 것들 (moy-2005)**: 고혈압·심장질환·스테로이드는 **전부 유의하지 않았다**. 반면 당뇨 RR 2.75·HRT 2.55·60–79세 2.24는 실재. giok의 "확증 0건"과 합쳐 자세는 하나 — **절대금기는 없다**.
> - **임상 결론**: 후기 실패·MBL은 *숙주(흡연·이갈이·방사선·당뇨)×해부(경사·조사골·**상악**)×술식선택(로딩 타이밍·골증대 대신 short implant)×보철설계(나사유지·삽입깊이)×**유지관리(위생 내원 빈도)***의 다인성 문제. 단일 "convincing" 근거가 없다는 겸손함을 유지하되, 흡연 **감량**상담·이갈이 스크리닝·정밀 삽입깊이 관리·나사유지 지양·**리콜 예약**이 실행 가능한 레버.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 23 papers on late/established implant failure and marginal bone loss (MBL) — i.e., after successful osseointegration, distinct from early (pre-loading) failure covered in [[overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]] — built on ten umbrella/synthesis anchors plus the primary SR+MA and cohort layer beneath them, since umbrella reviews grade evidence without reporting effect sizes.

The broadest umbrella review (giok-2026, 25 meta-analyses/35 associations) finds zero associations reach "convincing" evidence; smoking is the only "highly suggestive" observational risk factor, joined by bruxism (barboza-2026, OR 4.68), radiotherapy (pacheco-2025, 81.52% vs 94.64% survival in irradiated vs non-irradiated bone), and periodontitis/male sex (ting-2017); implant angulation (tilted vs axial) shows a time-dependent MBL penalty (malak-2024: NS short-term, +0.08mm at 3y, +0.18mm long-term) despite equivalent failure risk (RR=1.02).

Short implants (≤6mm) show statistically lower but clinically acceptable 10-year survival vs long implants (lin-2026: 89.7–92.8% LTFU-adjusted) with equivalent MBL/complications, and a single-center 6.5-year cohort (kim-2026) found implant length itself uncorrelated with MBL — insertion depth and screw- vs cement-retention design were the operative variables; loading-protocol choice (immediate vs delayed, rismanchian-2025) and anatomic-avoidance strategies (zygomatic implants, dambrosio-2026) round out a multifactorial risk picture where host optimization (smoking cessation, bruxism screening) and precise prosthetic/surgical execution matter more than any single "hard" contraindication.

The primary layer folded in on 2026-07-17 supplies magnitudes the umbrellas omit and changes four readings: smoking's crisp pooled OR of 2.59 (fan-2024) is for **early** failure, so the late-window anchors are moy-2005's RR 1.56 and gjelvold-2021's 5-year MBL of **−1.90 mm in smokers vs −0.40 mm in non-smokers (p=0.021, the only significant predictor in that RCT)**, while naseri-2020 makes the exposure **dose-dependent** rather than binary; del-fabbro's own 2014 null (P=.30 at ≥3y) flipping to 2022 significance (P<.0001, 3–18y) reproduces malak's time-dependence within one research group; benic-2014 supplies the **numeric primary-stability envelope** (IT ≥20–45 Ncm or ISQ ≥60–65, no simultaneous augmentation) that the umbrella layer's "adequate stability" leaves undefined; and arbildo-vega-2025 (umbrella of 60 SRs: short-vs-standard survival **NS**, MBL **lower**) contradicts lin-2026's survival deficit until scope is read — the deficit is specific to ≤6 mm at ≥10 years. Two axes appear that the umbrella layer never isolated: **arch** (maxilla worse across moy, del-fabbro-2014, barausse, pacheco) and **maintenance** (barausse: hygiene-visit frequency reduced failure, p<0.001) — the latter being the only modifiable variable here that acts after placement.

## 세줄요약

후기(정착 후) 임플란트 실패·변연골소실(MBL) 관련 논문 **23편** 종합 — 로딩 전 조기실패는 [[overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]]와 상호보완. 우산리뷰 10편을 축으로 삼되, **우산리뷰는 등급만 매기고 효과크기를 안 주므로** 그 아래 1차 SR+MA·코호트 층을 함께 싣는다.

가장 광범위한 우산리뷰(giok-2026, 메타분석 25편/35개 연관성)는 "확증" 등급 연관성이 전무함을 보이고, 흡연이 유일한 관찰근거 최고등급이며, 이갈이(barboza-2026, OR 4.68)·방사선치료(pacheco-2025, 조사골 81.52% vs 비조사골 94.64% 생존율)·치주염/남성(ting-2017)이 뒤를 따름; 경사(tilted) vs 수직(axial) 임플란트는 실패위험은 동일(RR=1.02)하나 MBL 격차가 시간의존적(malak-2024: 단기 무의미, 3년 +0.08mm, 장기 +0.18mm).

Short implant(≤6mm)는 long implant 대비 통계적으로 낮지만 임상수용 가능한 10년 생존율(lin-2026: LTFU보정 89.7~92.8%)에 MBL·합병증은 동등하고, 단일기관 6.5년 코호트(kim-2026)는 임플란트 길이 자체가 MBL과 무관함을 발견 — 삽입깊이·나사/시멘트유지 설계가 실질 변수; 로딩 프로토콜 선택(즉시 vs 지연, rismanchian-2025)과 해부학적 회피 전략(관골 임플란트, dambrosio-2026)이 다인성 위험 그림을 완성하며, 단일 "강력" 금기보다 숙주 최적화(금연·이갈이 스크리닝)와 정밀한 보철·외과 실행이 관건.

## Thesis

**Clinical question:** Once an implant has successfully osseointegrated, what predicts its long-term failure or progressive marginal bone loss (MBL), and how should that risk be managed?

This is the mirror question to early implant failure ([[overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]]): here the concern is not failure-to-integrate but **breakdown after successful loading** — driven by host biology, mechanical/occlusal overload, anatomic placement choices, and prosthetic design, rather than surgical technique alone. The broadest current umbrella review ([[implants/giok-2026-factors-implant-failure-umbrella-review]], 25 meta-analyses/35 associations) offers a crucial epistemic anchor: **no risk factor in the implant-failure literature has reached "convincing" evidence** — only smoking clears "highly suggestive," with nine others (periodontal compromise, PPI therapy, bone quality, immediate loading, etc.) at "suggestive." This caution should temper how confidently any single factor below is treated as a hard contraindication.

**A structural note about this page's own evidence, before the factors.** Seven of the ten syntheses anchoring the sections below are *umbrella reviews* (giok, ting, barboza, malak, pacheco, dambrosio, rismanchian). That buys breadth and evidence grading at a real cost: umbrella reviews report **gradings, not effect sizes**, and they inherit their source SRs' quality (pacheco's 11/11 AMSTAR-2 "critically low" is representative, not an outlier). Read alone, this page could tell you smoking is "highly suggestive" without telling you *how much* risk that is. The primary SR+MA and cohort layer beneath the umbrellas — folded into the sections below on 2026-07-17 — supplies the missing magnitudes, and in three places it does more than that: it **reproduces the umbrellas' reconciliations from independent data** (Del Fabbro's own 2014→2022 flip on tilted implants), **supplies a decision threshold the umbrella framing lacks** (Benic's primary-stability envelope), and **surfaces a conflict the umbrella layer smooths over** (Arbildo-Vega vs Lin on short-implant survival). Where an umbrella grading and a pooled estimate disagree below, both are shown rather than averaged.

Host factors converge across independent lines of evidence: smoking is corroborated three ways (giok-2026's graded umbrella; [[implants/ting-2017-surgical-patient-factors-affecting-marginal]]'s 4/5-SR MBL concordance; [[implants/kate-2016-implant-failure-a-dentists-nightmare]]'s older secondary-citation figures), periodontitis and male sex add two more consistent patient-factor signals (Ting), and bruxism — long a narrative-level claim ([[implants/song-2021-implant-complications-bruxism-patients]]) — is now SR+MA-level evidence via [[implants/barboza-2026-bruxism-implant-failure-umbrella-review]] (OR 4.68). Radiotherapy is the most quantitatively severe host-modifying factor: [[implants/pacheco-2025-dental-implant-survival-irradiated]] pools 73,674 implants to show irradiated-bone survival dropping to 81.52% (vs 94.64% non-irradiated), worse still in irradiated maxilla or grafted bone — though all 11 pooled reviews were AMSTAR-2 critically low, so the direction is trustworthy but the magnitude is not precise.

**How much risk is "highly suggestive" smoking, actually — and is the number even about *this* page's question?** The pooled layer answers with a caveat that matters more than the number. [[implants/fan-2024-smoking-early-implant-failure-sr-ma]] is the largest quantitative anchor held (SR+MA, 32 observational studies, 59,246 implants): smoking carries an implant-level **OR 2.59 (95% CI 2.08–3.23)**, concordant at patient level. But its endpoint is **early** failure — which belongs to the sibling page ([[overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]]), not this one. Stating that honestly: **the crisp pooled smoking effect the wiki holds is for pre-loading failure, and no held paper pools smoking against late failure specifically.** What we do hold for the late window is narrower but on-target: [[implants/moy-2005-dental-implant-failure-rates-risk]] (4,680 implants, single operator, 21 years) puts smoking at **RR 1.56** across its whole horizon, and — most usefully — [[immediate-implant/gjelvold-2021-immediate-delayed-loading-single-tooth-5year-rct]] measured **5-year MBL** directly and found **smokers lost −1.90 mm vs non-smokers −0.40 mm (p=0.021)**. Gjelvold is small (n=50) and was designed to test loading protocol, not smoking; that it found **smoking to be the *only* significant MBL predictor while loading protocol was not** makes it an unusually clean statement of this page's whole thesis — host biology outranks the technique choice the trial was actually about.

[[implants/naseri-2020-smoking-levels-implant-failure-sr-ma]] adds the axis that changes the counselling script: it restricted inclusion to the 23 studies reporting **cigarettes per day** and found failure risk rises **dose-dependently**, with heavy smokers significantly worse than non-smokers. Smoking is therefore not a binary contraindication to be checked but a **graded, partially modifiable exposure** — a patient who halves their consumption is plausibly moving down a real gradient, which is a very different conversation from "quit or accept the risk."

**Moy 2005 is the page's only single-operator long-horizon cohort, and it carries three things the umbrella layer does not.** *First*, it independently corroborates radiotherapy at a similar magnitude to Pacheco's pooled survival gap — **head-and-neck radiation RR 2.73** — from wholly different data (4,680 implants, one operator, 21 years), which matters because Pacheco's 11 source SRs were all AMSTAR-2 critically low. *Second*, it names host factors this page otherwise omits entirely: **diabetes RR 2.75** (its largest), **post-menopausal HRT RR 2.55**, and **age 60–79 RR 2.24**. *Third* — and most useful for consent conversations — it names what is **not** a risk factor: **hypertension, cardiac disease, and steroid therapy were all non-significant.** Moy's own conclusion is the same epistemic posture Giok reaches twenty years later by a different route: **no absolute contraindication exists** — diabetes calls for glycemic control and monitoring, irradiated patients for HBO consideration, and cardiovascular comorbidity for neither exclusion nor alarm. Single-operator data limits generalizability (technique is held constant, which is both its strength and its ceiling), and the 2005 vintage predates modern roughened surfaces.

**Arch and site are a risk axis this page previously lacked, and four independent lines converge on it.** Moy: **maxillary location RR 1.79**, with posterior maxilla failing at **8.9–9.7% vs 2.89% in anterior mandible**. [[implants/del-fabbro-2014-marginal-bone-axial-tilted-sr-ma]]: maxillary survival **97.4% vs mandibular 99.6%** (significant). [[implants/barausse-2024-4mm-short-implants-posterior-atrophic-8year]]: in 4-mm short implants, **maxillary placement significantly raised failure risk (p=0.02)**. Pacheco: irradiated **maxilla** worse than irradiated mandible. Four designs — cohort, SR+MA, retrospective, umbrella — spanning 2005–2025, all pointing the same way. None of the four was designed to test arch as its primary question, so this is convergent observational signal rather than a tested hypothesis; but for a page whose thesis is multifactorial risk, "which jaw" belongs alongside "which patient" and "which technique."

Surgical/anatomic choices show a more nuanced picture than older literature suggested. [[implants/malak-2024-implant-failure-marginal-bone-loss]] resolves an apparent tension with [[implants/ting-2017-surgical-patient-factors-affecting-marginal]] (which found no significant tilted-vs-axial MBL effect across 4 shorter-follow-up SRs): the tilted-implant MBL penalty is **time-dependent**, absent short-term but significant and widening by 3 years (+0.08mm) and long-term (+0.18mm) — Ting's earlier null result reflected shorter-follow-up source studies, not a true absence of effect. Failure risk itself remains equivalent between tilted and axial (RR=1.02), so tilting to avoid anatomic structures is not contraindicated, but warrants longer-term crestal-bone monitoring.

**The time-dependence claim does not rest on Malak's reconciliation alone — the same research group reproduced the flip in its own data.** [[implants/del-fabbro-2014-marginal-bone-axial-tilted-sr-ma]] (19 studies, 670 patients, 1,494 axial + 1,338 tilted, ≥1 year) found crestal bone loss **not significantly different** at 1 year (axial 0.43–1.13 vs tilted 0.34–1.14 mm, P=.09) or at ≥3 years (P=.30) — the foundational SR+MA that established tilted implants as predictable. Eight years later [[implants/del-fabbro-2022-full-arch-tilted-axial-implants-sr-ma]] (24 studies, 2,637 patients, 5,594 tilted + 5,611 axial, **3–18 year** follow-up) reports MBL **significantly lower around axial implants (P<.0001)**. Same investigators, same question, opposite verdict — and in the direction Malak's time-stratified pooling predicts. This is stronger corroboration than the Ting-vs-Malak reconciliation the paragraph above offers, because it removes between-group methodology as the explanation. **One honest confound:** the 2022 update brought both longer follow-up *and* roughly four times the sample, so "the effect needed time to appear" and "the 2014 analysis was underpowered" are not separable from this pair alone. Malak breaks the tie — its stratification shows the effect *growing* within a single analysis (NS → +0.08 mm at 3 y → +0.18 mm long-term), which power alone would not produce. The practical reading is unchanged and now better supported: **tilt freely for anatomy, but the crestal-bone cost is a late cost, so the monitoring interval — not the placement decision — is what tilting should change.** Several other surgical variables Ting examined (flapless vs flapped, 1- vs 2-stage, insertion torque, soft-tissue grafting) showed **no proven MBL effect** — useful for de-prioritizing intuitively-appealing-but-unproven technique debates. The flapless case is worth opening up, because "no MBL effect" is only half of it and the other half is a small lesson in this page's own thesis. [[implants/chrcanovic-2014-flapless-vs-conventional-implant-surgery-ma]] (23 studies: 10 RCT, 7 CCT, 6 retrospective) confirms Ting on bone — **MBL MD −0.07 mm, P=0.16, NS** — and on infection (RR 0.96, P=0.96). But on *failure* it found flapless carrying a **75% higher relative risk (RR 1.75, 95% CI 1.07–2.86, P=0.03)** — which **disappeared when the analysis was restricted to the 5 low-risk-of-bias studies**, revealing the signal as an artefact of the 16 high-RoB studies (funnel plots showed no publication bias). So flapless is not a *bone-loss* question at all, and its *failure* signal is a bias signal. This is Giok's "nothing is convincing" operating at the level of a single technique: the same dataset yields a significant harm or a null depending only on how strictly you filter methodology. One directly actionable Ting finding: **ridge augmentation to fit a standard-length implant produced more MBL than simply placing a shorter implant instead** — a load-bearing argument for the short-implant papers below.

Short implants (≤6mm) get their most rigorous long-term evaluation yet in this batch. [[implants/lin-2026-ten-year-outcomes-short-dental]] (first ≥10-year meta-analysis for the strict ≤6mm definition) finds statistically lower survival vs long implants (RR 0.920 patient-level/0.943 implant-level) but a still-acceptable >89% 10-year survival after LTFU correction, with equivalent MBL (0.277mm) and complications — and identifies **screw-retention as a major, actionable survival deficit** (83.6–80.7% vs 97.0–96.8% for cement-retention). A complementary single-center 6.5-year cohort ([[implants/kim-2026-long-term-outcomes-short-standard-implants]]) found MBL statistically indistinguishable between 6mm short and 8.5mm standard implants (0.05 vs 0.12mm), with **insertion depth — not implant length or crown-implant ratio — the only variable significantly correlated with MBL** (r=0.372). Read together: the "short implant" literature's real lesson may be less about length per se and more about **prosthetic retention design and precise subcrestal positioning**.

**But Lin's survival deficit is contested by a larger synthesis, and the disagreement is informative rather than embarrassing.** [[implants/arbildo-vega-2025-short-vs-standard-implants-edentulous-umbrella-review]] (umbrella of **60 systematic reviews**, 790 records screened, PROSPERO CRD42020218497) finds **survival, failure, and prosthetic complications all non-significant** between short and standard implants — and reports short implants showing **less MBL and fewer biological complications, at high-confidence grading**. That is the opposite sign to Lin on survival, from far more source reviews. The two do not actually collide once scope is read: Arbildo-Vega pools the **broad short-implant band across all follow-up horizons**, while Lin is deliberately the strict **≤6 mm definition at ≥10 years**. The deficit appears only at the shortest length *and* the longest horizon — which is the same **definition-and-time-dependent** structure this page already documented for tilted implants, arriving independently in a different literature. Practical consequence: quoting "short implants survive as well as standard" is defensible for the general short band, and *not* defensible for ≤6 mm at ten years; and in both readings **short implants win on MBL**, which is the endpoint this page is about.

The primary layer beneath both fills in what length alone cannot explain. [[implants/barausse-2024-4mm-short-implants-posterior-atrophic-8year]] takes the extreme case — **4 mm** implants, 212 patients / 496 implants, mean **8.02 years** in severely atrophic posterior jaws — and reports **95.36% cumulative survival** (95% CI 93.12–97.04) with **MBL of 0.47 mm at 1 year progressing only to 0.59 mm at 10 years, with no significant increase after year 3**. That plateau is a genuine contrast worth holding next to the tilted-implant finding above: **tilted MBL grows with time; short-implant MBL flattens after year 3.** "Late MBL" is not one behaviour — it depends on what is driving it. Barausse also surfaces the single most actionable variable in this entire page: **higher hygiene-visit frequency significantly reduced failure risk (p<0.001)** — a modifiable, prosthesis-independent protective factor that none of the ten umbrella reviews above put a number on. (Its maxillary-risk finding, p=0.02, feeds the arch axis established earlier.) [[implants/emfietzoglou-2024-short-implants-6mm-survival-posterior-edentulism-sr]] frames the same ≤6 mm question as an RCT-only SR positioning short implants as a **regeneration-sparing** alternative in resorbed posterior sites — the same conclusion Ting reaches from the augmentation side — though its pooled figures sit in full text this wiki does not hold. And the historical baseline [[implants/lee-2009-marginal-bone-single-short-implant]] (n=40, machined-surface Brånemark, 1 year) reported short-vs-standard MBL non-significant **and no effect of crown-to-implant ratio** (neither C:I ≤1 nor >1 differed) — an early null on the mechanical intuition that short implants must fail from leverage, later corroborated by Kim's finding that insertion depth, not C:I, tracks MBL. Machined surfaces and one year of follow-up make Lee a reference point, not evidence for current practice.

Loading-protocol timing ([[implants/rismanchian-2025-immediate-nonimmediate-loading-umbrella-review]]) and anatomic-avoidance strategy (zygomatic implants, [[implants/dambrosio-2026-clinical-risk-medico-legal-implications]]) round out the picture. Immediate vs nonimmediate loading of single implants shows no significant survival or MBL difference given adequate primary stability — though this narrative-umbrella conclusion sits in some tension with giok-2026's formally graded finding that immediate loading reaches "suggestive" risk-factor status; the difference is likely one of scope (single-implant loading *timing* alone vs. immediate placement-and-loading combined scenarios in giok-2026's pooled observational data) rather than a true contradiction.

**"Given adequate primary stability" is the load-bearing clause, and the umbrella layer never quantifies it. Benic does.** [[implants/benic-2014-loading-protocols-single-implant-crowns-sr-ma]] (SR+MA, 11 RCTs) found immediate and conventional loading of single-implant crowns equivalent on survival (1-year OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.32–1.76) and MBL (SMD −0.05 mm, 95% CI −0.41 to +0.31) at 1, 2, 3 and 5 years, with no papilla-level difference — but states the **envelope inside which that equivalence was demonstrated: insertion torque ≥20–45 Ncm or ISQ ≥60–65, and no simultaneous bone augmentation.** Outside that envelope the evidence does not support immediate loading. That converts decision point 7 below from a disposition ("reserve for confirmed stability") into a **checkable threshold** — and it also dissolves much of the Rismanchian-vs-Giok tension, since Giok's pooled observational data is not restricted to this envelope while the RCTs are. [[immediate-implant/gjelvold-2021-immediate-delayed-loading-single-tooth-5year-rct]] then tests the envelope prospectively at its stated boundary (n=50 healed maxillary single-tooth sites, IL eligibility **≥30 Ncm**, 5 years): survival 100% vs 95.8%, **MBL −0.50 vs −0.54 mm (p=0.782)**, PES/WES equivalent, and the 1-year papilla advantage for delayed loading **resolved by 5 years**. Equivalence therefore holds out to five years *at the threshold Benic specifies* — and, as noted in the host-factor section, the only variable that did move MBL in that trial was **smoking**. The consistent message across the loading literature is that timing is not the risk; the stability gate and the patient are. For patients requiring implants in severely atrophic maxillae, zygomatic implants achieve high survival (90–100%) at the cost of common (if often under-reported) complications — chiefly sinusitis — with the ZAGA/anatomy-guided technique outperforming the original surgical technique on both sinusitis risk (3.7% vs 27.2%) and immediate-loading feasibility (89.6% vs 22.3%); Dambrosio's explicit medico-legal framing (informed consent, surgeon experience, "no viable alternative" case selection) is a reminder that high survival numbers alone do not discharge the duty to disclose realistic complication rates.

**Zygomatic implants may partly escape the irradiation penalty documented above — a signal worth holding at exactly its measured strength, no more.** [[implants/scocca-2026-zygomatic-implants-head-neck-cancer-sr-ma]] (SR+MA, 17 studies, 294 head-and-neck oncology patients, 679 zygomatic implants) pools **97% survival (95% CI 94–99, I²=42.4%)** in precisely the population Pacheco identifies as worst-off, and finds **radiotherapy raising failure odds only non-significantly (OR 3.42, 95% CI 0.97–12.06, p=0.0546)**. Set against Pacheco's conventional-implant irradiated survival of 81.52%, the contrast invites a mechanistically plausible reading — zygomatic bone is denser, better vascularized, and often outside the primary radiation field — but the honest statement is narrower: **p=0.0546 with a CI running from 0.97 to 12.06 is an underpowered near-miss, not a null.** The point estimate (3.42) is entirely compatible with real harm; what the data rule out is precision, not risk. Two further findings are usable: failure was **2% when ZIs were placed at the primary resection vs 9% at secondary surgery** (p=0.4073, NS — directionally favouring primary placement but not established), and patient-reported outcomes (UW-QOL, EORTC QLQ-H&N35) showed satisfactory function **even in irradiated patients** — which is the outcome the medico-legal framing above is ultimately about. Scocca's own caution matches Dambrosio's: high survival does not eliminate a substantial prosthodontic and maintenance burden.

## Clinical Decision Points

1. **Treat no single factor as "convincing."** giok-2026's own grading shows zero implant-failure risk factors reach the top evidence tier — communicate probabilistic, not deterministic, risk to patients.
2. **Smoking cessation and bruxism screening are the two highest-yield host-factor interventions — and smoking counselling should be graded, not binary.** Naseri-2020 shows failure risk rising **dose-dependently with cigarettes/day**, so reduction is worth pursuing even when cessation fails. Quote magnitudes at their correct scope: **OR 2.59 is for *early* failure** (Fan-2024), while for this page's late window the on-target figures are Moy's RR 1.56 and Gjelvold's 5-year MBL of **−1.90 mm in smokers vs −0.40 mm in non-smokers (p=0.021)** — the latter being the single cleanest late-MBL smoking number the wiki holds. Bruxism carries SR+MA-level support (OR 4.68). Both are modifiable and belong in planning, not just at the early-failure stage.
2-bis. **Book the hygiene recalls — it is the most actionable finding on this page.** Barausse-2024 found **higher hygiene-visit frequency significantly reduced failure risk (p<0.001)** over 8 years in the hardest case available (4-mm implants in severely atrophic posterior jaws). None of the ten umbrella reviews quantifies maintenance, which is a gap in the literature rather than evidence of unimportance. Unlike arch, length, or tilt, recall frequency is modifiable after the implant is in the mouth.
2-ter. **Do not import contraindications the data do not support.** Moy-2005 found **hypertension, cardiac disease, and steroid therapy all non-significant** for failure, while naming diabetes (RR 2.75), HRT (2.55), and age 60–79 (2.24) as real. Combined with Giok's zero "convincing" associations, the posture is: no absolute contraindication — control diabetes, plan around radiation, and stop treating cardiovascular comorbidity as a barrier.
3. **Radiotherapy history changes the survival math, not the indication.** 81.52% vs 94.64% (pacheco-2025) still supports offering implants to irradiated patients, but requires multidisciplinary planning, closer follow-up, and explicit informed consent about the lower (though still majority-favorable) survival — especially in irradiated maxilla or grafted sites.
4. **Tilted-implant MBL risk is real but delayed — plan monitoring, not avoidance.** Failure risk is equivalent to axial (RR=1.02); the MBL penalty only emerges by 3 years and grows — schedule longer-interval crestal-bone radiographs for tilted-implant patients rather than treating tilting itself as contraindicated.
5. **Favor a short implant over augmentation-to-fit-a-standard-length implant when anatomy is limited — but quote its survival at the right scope.** Ting-2017's finding that ridge augmentation carries a higher MBL penalty than simply using a shorter implant, combined with lin-2026's >89% 10-year survival, kim-2026's MBL equivalence, arbildo-vega-2025's 60-SR umbrella (survival **NS** vs standard, with **less** MBL), and emfietzoglou-2024's regeneration-sparing framing, supports short implants as a first-line (not last-resort) option in reduced-height posterior ridges. **The one caveat that must travel with the claim:** "survives as well as standard" holds for the broad short band but **not for ≤6 mm at ten years**, where Lin does find a real deficit (RR 0.92–0.94). Short-implant MBL, unlike tilted-implant MBL, **plateaus after year 3** (Barausse: 0.47 → 0.59 mm out to 10 y), and crown-to-implant ratio is not the driver it is intuited to be (Lee-2009; Kim-2026).
5-bis. **Treat the maxilla as its own risk stratum.** Four independent designs converge: Moy (maxilla RR 1.79; posterior maxilla 8.9–9.7% vs anterior mandible 2.89%), Del Fabbro-2014 (maxillary survival 97.4% vs 99.6%), Barausse (maxillary 4-mm implants p=0.02), and Pacheco (irradiated maxilla worst). None tested arch as a primary hypothesis, so this is convergent observational signal — but it should shift consent language and recall intervals in the posterior maxilla, not just implant selection.
6. **Within short implants, prioritize cement retention and precise subcrestal positioning over length itself.** Lin-2026's screw- vs cement-retention survival gap (up to 16 percentage points) and Kim-2026's insertion-depth correlation (r=0.372, the only significant MBL predictor) are more actionable levers than the length decision alone.
7. **Immediate loading is a stability-gated choice — and the gate now has numbers.** Rismanchian-2025 shows IL≈NIL survival/MBL when primary stability is adequate, but giok-2026's independent observational grading still flags immediate loading as "suggestive." Benic-2014 resolves most of that tension by naming the envelope inside which the RCT equivalence was actually demonstrated: **insertion torque ≥20–45 Ncm or ISQ ≥60–65, and no simultaneous bone augmentation** — outside it, the evidence does not support IL. Gjelvold-2021 confirms the envelope prospectively at **≥30 Ncm out to 5 years** (MBL −0.50 vs −0.54 mm, p=0.782). Giok's pooled observational data is not restricted to that envelope, which is the likeliest reason it still sees risk. Measure the gate; do not eyeball it. (Note the ceiling on the same instrument, though: high torque is not itself a stability guarantee — see [[overviews/high-insertion-torque-primary-stability-crestal-bone-overview]].)
8. **Zygomatic implants: disclose complications, not just survival.** High ZI survival (90–100%, and 97% pooled in head-and-neck oncology per scocca-2026) should not be quoted without the accompanying sinusitis rate (2.4–27.2%, technique-dependent) — ZAGA technique lowers this risk and enables more immediate loading than the original surgical technique, and case selection/consent should follow the explicit medico-legal framework Dambrosio proposes. In irradiated patients specifically, ZIs look *less* penalized than conventional implants (Scocca: RT **OR 3.42 but p=0.0546**, CI 0.97–12.06) — read that as **underpowered, not reassuring**: the point estimate is compatible with substantial harm, so it may inform offering the option, never the consent discussion's risk figure.

## Gaps & Future Research

- **Full-text access remains a systemic gap**: giok-2026, malak-2024, barboza-2026, ting-2017 (partially), and kim/lin/rismanchian's peer umbrella reviews were largely ingested from paywalled abstract-only landing pages — individual OR/CI forest-plot detail and complete association tables are not verifiable in this wiki without institutional access.
- **A data-integrity caveat carries over from the same ingest batch**: [[digital-workflow/tomar-2025-comparing-clinical-outcomes-guided-freehand]] and [[digital-workflow/mehta-2025-accuracy-assessment-robot-assisted-dental]] (surgical-accuracy umbrella reviews, out of scope for MBL/failure but methodologically adjacent) report a numerically identical coronal-deviation statistic despite being different reviews — a general reminder to cross-check pooled statistics across concurrently-published umbrella reviews from the same journal before treating them as independently corroborating.
- **AMSTAR-2 quality across this evidence base skews low**: pacheco-2025's 11/11 "critically low" rating is not an outlier — most umbrella reviews synthesized here inherit substantial methodological limitation from their source SRs, reinforcing giok-2026's "nothing is convincing" framing as a structural feature of this literature, not a one-off caveat.
- **Rismanchian-2025 vs giok-2026 tension on immediate loading is now largely explained but not formally resolved** — Benic-2014's stated envelope (IT ≥20–45 Ncm or ISQ ≥60–65, no simultaneous augmentation) identifies the likely scope difference: the RCT literature demonstrates equivalence *inside* that envelope while Giok's pooled observational data is not restricted to it. A head-to-head using a common "immediate" definition and a stability floor would still settle it.
- **No held paper pools smoking against *late* failure specifically.** Fan-2024's OR 2.59 is an early-failure endpoint; Moy's RR 1.56 spans the whole horizon in one operator's hands; Gjelvold's smoker MBL gap (−1.90 vs −0.40 mm) is a 5-year RCT of n=50 that was not designed to test smoking. A late-failure smoking meta-analysis is the single most obvious missing piece for this page's best-supported risk factor.
- **The short-implant survival conflict needs a scope-matched comparison, not a tiebreaker.** Arbildo-Vega (60 SRs, survival NS) and Lin (≤6 mm at ≥10 y, RR 0.92–0.94) are read here as differing by length definition and horizon; nothing held tests that reading directly. An analysis stratifying survival by exact length band × follow-up decade would confirm or kill it.
- **Arch is convergent signal, not a tested hypothesis.** Four designs put the maxilla at higher risk, but none isolated arch with site, bone quality, and loading controlled — so it belongs in consent and recall planning, not yet in a risk model.
- **Maintenance frequency has one number and it comes from one team.** Barausse's p<0.001 hygiene-visit effect is retrospective, single-surgical-team, and confounded by patient adherence (patients who attend recalls differ systematically from those who do not). For the most actionable lever on the page, that is thin — a prospective recall-interval trial would be disproportionately valuable.
- **Short-implant "insertion depth" mechanism needs prospective confirmation** — kim-2026's r=0.372 correlation is retrospective/single-center; a multi-center prospective study isolating insertion depth from implant length and retention type would strengthen the reframing proposed here.
