## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 범위: 점막염 관리(1차 예방), 비수술 보조 요법 순위, 수술 표면 오염 제거, 임플란트플라스티와 프로토콜 선택, 재건 수술(모체의 다섯 절)을 분리한 하위 종합. [확인]
> - 점막염 관리가 곧 임플란트주위염의 1차 예방이다(Jepsen 2015). 전문가 기계적 치면 제거(PMPR)가 표준이고, 보조제(CHX, 국소 항생제, NaOCl, 프로바이오틱스)는 일관된 추가 이득이 없다(Mauriello 2026, RCT 9편, n=414). Brunello 2026(21편, n=1,068)에서도 기계적 제거만으로 BoP가 줄었고 점막염 해소율은 9–100%였다. [확인]
> - 비수술 보조 요법 순위(Bai 2024 네트워크 MA, RCT 33편): 단일 보조제가 우세하지 않다. 전신 항생제 + 기계적 제거(SA+MD)가 임플란트주위염의 CAL(SUCRA 87.4%)과 MBL(99.9%)에서 최상위이지만, EFP S3 지침(Herrera 2023)은 일상적 전신 항생제에 반대한다. 프로바이오틱스 + MD는 점막염 PPD에서 1위(100%)지만 통계적으로 유의하지 않아, 네트워크 상위가 입증된 임상 효과는 아니다. [확인]
> - 수술 표면 오염 제거(Baima 2022 SR+MA, RCT 16편): 기계적, 화학적, 물리적 프로토콜 중 우월한 것이 없다. 항균 광역동 요법은 확실성이 낮고 단기 이득이 작으며 기계적 제거를 대체하지 못한다(Jervoe-Storm 2024 Cochrane). [확인]
> - 티타늄 브러시 vs 임플란트플라스티(Park 2025 RCT, n=30, 12개월): PPD 감소는 동등했고(−3.6 vs −3.3 mm), 12개월 MBL은 브러시 0.0 vs 임플란트플라스티 +0.7 mm(p=0.03), 시간은 3분 1초 vs 5분 27초(p=0.006)였으며, 임플란트 상실은 0 vs 2였다. 인접 임플란트의 임플란트주위염이 pocket 폐쇄 실패를 예측했다(p=0.033). [확인]
> - 완전 vs 부분 임플란트플라스티(Monje 2026, quasi-RCT, 40개): 완전 길이(FLIP)가 MBL 이득이 더 컸다(2.42 vs 1.62 mm, p=0.009). 점막 퇴축도 더 컸다(1.84 vs 1.33 mm, p=0.006). 흡연이 독립적 실패 예측이었고, 저자가 사용한 버의 로열티를 받는 이해충돌이 있어 추정 자료로 본다. [확인]
> - 실제 진료(Zhou 2026, 후향 406개): 1년 성공률은 OFD 62.5%, 임플란트플라스티 58.3%, 재건 55.4%, 복합 48.3%, 전체 54.7%로 군 간 유의하지 않았다(검정력 27%). 집도의 경험과 수술 전 항생제(OR 3.54)가 프로토콜 선택보다 예측력이 컸다. 다만 항생제 쪽은 단일 후향 코호트 결과이고, 같은 항생제 변수가 임플란트 상실도 예측해(OR 3.13) 적응증 교란 (Confounding by Indication) 가능성이 있다. Ramanauskaite 2026 리뷰는 전신 항생제의 이득을 미결로 본다. 5년에는 성공률이 약 27–42%로 떨어진다. [확인]
> - 재건(Ramanauskaite 2023 NMA): 격납형 골내 결손에서 비재건보다 결손 충전과 골 수준 이득이 있다. Hakkers 2026(단일맹검 RCT, 52명/63개): 재건이 MBL(β −1.65 mm)과 협측 퇴축에서 우월했지만 PPD, BoP, 해소율은 같았다(43.8% vs 44.4%). 방사선 골 충전과 임상 염증 해소가 분리되는 역설이며, 재건군에서 통증·구강건조·금속 맛·두통이 더 많았다. 3벽 결손은 T3–T6에, 4벽 결손은 T12에만 이득이 보였다. [확인]
> - 서술 고찰(Su 2026): 이종골이 자가골보다 선호되는 경향이다. 생분해성 Mg 막, 맞춤 3D 티타늄 메시, 기계학습 위험 예측이 후보로 거론되지만 근거는 서술 수준이다. [확인]
> - 종합 한 줄: 어떤 접근이든 1년 해소율이 약 44–55%에서 정체하는 것은 프로토콜 실패보다 면역병리적 저항과 일치한다. 프로토콜 라벨보다 집도의 숙련이 더 중요해 보이며, 전신 항생제는 2025–2026년 RCT와 메타분석을 더해도 단기·출혈 지표 수준의 이득에 그친다(아래 신규 항목). [미검증]
> - **신규 (2026-10) — 항생제·장기 내구성**: 전신 항생제 병용은 1–2년만 유효 (OR 2.33), ≥3년 무이득, 표면 개질 임플란트 한정 (Antonoglou 2025). 외과 치료는 ≥5년에서 해소 58.6%·재치료 27.2% (Monje 2025), 재건술 7년 성공률 23–31% (Isler 2025) — 비완치, 지지치료 필수.
> - **신규 (2026-10 배치 A) — 항생제 메타분석 간 모순 (Liu 2026 vs Bai 2024)**: Liu 2026 체계적 문헌고찰·메타분석 (Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis, SR+MA; RCT 22편, 1,028명)은 보조 항생제의 골 수준 (Bone Level, BL) 이득이 없다고 보고했다 (가중평균차 (Weighted Mean Difference, WMD) 0.03 mm, P=0.39). Bai 2024 네트워크 메타분석 (Network Meta-Analysis, NMA)은 전신 항생제 + 기계적 제거 (SA+MD)를 변연골 소실 (Marginal Bone Loss, MBL) 1위로 꼽았다 (표준화평균차 (Standardized Mean Difference, SMD) 3.92, SUCRA 99.9%). 갈리는 이유로 보이는 것: 효과 척도(mm 대 단위 없는 SMD), 대조군(Liu는 MD 단독과 프로바이오틱스·광역학 요법·클로르헥시딘 같은 활성 대조를 섞음), 경로(Liu는 국소 + 전신 통합), 술식(Bai는 비외과만, Liu는 외과/비외과 미구분), Bai의 MBL 네트워크는 RCT 6편에 전역 비일관성 유의(p=0), Liu는 I² 91–95%와 GRADE 판정 문제. 승자는 정하지 않는다. 다만 mm 단위 근거(Liu 0.03 mm, Antonoglou 0.37 mm, Hakkers 항생제 RCT −0.02 mm)로는 임상적으로 큰 골 이득이 보이지 않는다. [확인]
> - **신규 — 수술 후 보조 전신 항생제 (Hakkers 2026 RCT vs Antonoglou 2025 SR+MA)**: 아목시실린·메트로니다졸 (Amoxicillin/Metronidazole) 1주를 접근 판막 수술에 더한 RCT(n=59, 12개월, 0–2벽 비포위형 결손)에서 탐침 시 출혈 (Bleeding on Probing, BoP)만 T9·T12에 낮았다(−10.57%p, −14.47%p). 탐침 깊이 (Probing Pocket Depth, PPD), 화농 (Suppuration on Probing, SoP), MBL, 복합 해소율(39.6% vs 23.8%, 비유의)은 차이가 없었다. Antonoglou의 단기 성공 OR 2.33은 RCT만 보면 OR 2.29(95% CI 0.69–7.64, 비유의)이고, ≥3년 이득이 없으며, 표면 개질 임플란트에 국한된다. 두 근거가 겹치는 지점은 "PPD는 그대로이고 출혈 지표만 단기적으로 낮아진다"이다. [확인]
> - **신규 — Zhou 2026·Riben 2026과의 조정**: Zhou 2026 코호트의 OR 4.49는 같은 항생제 변수가 임플란트 상실도 예측해(OR 3.13) 적응증 교란 (Confounding by Indication) 가능성이 크고, 무작위 배정한 Hakkers RCT에서는 성공률 차이가 유의하지 않았다. Riben 2026(n=27, 탐색적)은 항생제 대 무항생제가 아니라 두 처방(아목시실린 + 메트로니다졸 vs 페니실린 V + 메트로니다졸)을 비교했다. 타액 미생물총 (Salivary Microbiome) 종 풍부도가 8일째 줄었다가 6개월에 회복했고, 처방 간 다양성 차이와 12개월 성공률 차이(83% vs 69%, p=0.645)는 없었다. **임상 결론: 일상적 보조 전신 항생제는 지지되지 않는다. 이득이 있다면 단기·출혈 지표 수준이고 표면 의존적이며, 미생물총 교란·설사(Antonoglou 21.2% vs 0%)·항생제 관리 (Antibiotic Stewardship) 비용이 따른다.** [확인]
> - **신규 — 비외과 직접 비교 RCT (둘 다 초록만 확인)**: Clementini 2026(파일럿 3군 RCT, 임플란트 84개, 6개월)은 티타늄 큐렛에 에리스리톨 에어폴리싱 (Erythritol Air-Polishing)이나 Er:YAG 레이저를 더해도 군간 차이가 없었고, 치료 종결점 도달률은 12–18%였으며, 에어폴리싱군에서 기종 (Emphysema) 1례가 있었다. Moreira 2025(RCT, 12개월, 양군 모두 전신 메트로니다졸 병용)는 초음파 기구 (Ultrasonic Instrumentation)가 PPD·BoP 감소에 유리해 보였으나 초록에 표본 수와 효과 크기가 없다. [확인]
> - **신규 — 점막염 (Peri-implant Mucositis, PM)**: de Tapia 2026 RCT(48명/118개, 3개월)에서 구강위생교육 (Oral Hygiene Instructions, OHI) 단독 해소율은 환자 39.1%/임플란트 36.4%, OHI + 기계적 기구치료는 56%/67.3%였고, 순응도가 가장 강한 예측인자였다(OR 11.4). Aksu 2026 준무작위 시험(43명/55개, 각화조직 폭 (Keratinized Tissue Width, KTW) <2 mm, 초록만 확인)에서 유리구개이식 (Free Palatal Graft, FPG)을 더하면 6개월 BoP 범위가 줄고(MD −1.06) 성공 OR 8.44였으며, PPD는 차이가 없었다. [확인]
> - **신규 — 재건·연조직 보조**: Holtzman 2026 RCT(n=32, 12개월)에서 골유도재생술 (Guided Bone Regeneration, GBR)에 결합조직이식 (Connective Tissue Graft, CTG)을 더하면 임상부착수준 (Clinical Attachment Level, CAL) 획득(3.21 vs 1.65 mm)과 각화점막 (Keratinized Mucosa, KM) 폭 증가(2.25 vs 0.26 mm)는 컸지만 질환 해소율은 같았다(62.5% vs 56.3%, p=0.719). Puisys 2026 RCT(n=31, 초록만 확인)에서 자가 상악결절골 재건에 전해질 세정 (Electrolytic Cleaning, EC)을 더해도 임상 이득은 없고 방사선 근심 충전만 컸다(4.6 vs 2.5 mm). Hakkers 재건 RCT·Soldini 2025와 같은 패턴이다: 구조·방사선·연조직 이득은 임상 질환 해소와 같지 않다. [확인]
> - **맥락 — Zuercher 2026 (5년 RCT, 22명 완료)**: 임플란트주위염 치료가 아니라 *식립 시* 협측 열개 (Dehiscence) GBR 연구다. 탈단백 우골 무기질 (Deproteinized Bovine Bone Mineral, DBBM) 블록이 입자형보다 협측 골 두께를 더 유지했지만(1.36 vs 0.07 mm), MBL·BoP·생존은 같았고 양군 모두 임플란트주위염이 없었다. 이식재 형태는 윤곽을 좌우하지만, 이 시험에서 윤곽 차이가 질환 차이로 이어지지는 않았다. [확인]

## Three-line Summary

This sub-overview holds the mucositis-prevention, non-surgical adjunct, surgical decontamination, implantoplasty, reconstruction, and systemic-antibiotic/long-term-durability sections of the parent peri-implantitis synthesis.
Mechanical debridement is the backbone for mucositis (hygiene instruction alone resolved 36–39% of cases in one RCT and professional instrumentation added more) and chemical or device adjuncts add no consistent benefit, no surgical decontamination protocol is superior, a titanium brush is non-inferior to implantoplasty, and in practice one-year success is about 55% whatever the protocol.
Reconstruction and soft-tissue adjuncts improve radiographic bone fill, recession, attachment or keratinized mucosa but not clinical disease resolution, and surgeon experience predicts success better than protocol choice; adjunctive systemic antibiotics gave only a bleeding-on-probing benefit in a surgical RCT and a short-term, surface-dependent success gain in pooled data, two meta-analyses conflict on the bone outcome, and routine use is not supported.

## 세줄요약

이 하위 종합은 모체 임플란트주위염 종합의 점막염 예방, 비수술 보조 요법, 수술 표면 오염 제거, 임플란트플라스티, 재건, 전신 항생제·장기 내구성 절을 담는다.
점막염에서는 기계적 제거가 중심이고(RCT 1편에서 구강위생교육만으로 36–39%가 해소됐고 전문가 기구치료가 더 보탰다) 약제·기구 보조제는 일관된 이득이 없으며, 우월한 수술 오염 제거 프로토콜은 없고, 티타늄 브러시는 임플란트플라스티에 뒤지지 않으며, 실제 진료의 1년 성공률은 프로토콜과 무관하게 약 55%다.
재건과 연조직 보조술은 방사선 골 충전·퇴축·부착·각화점막은 개선하지만 임상 해소율은 개선하지 않고, 집도의 경험이 프로토콜 선택보다 성공을 더 잘 예측한다. 보조 전신 항생제는 수술 RCT에서 탐침 시 출혈 이득만, 통합 자료에서 단기·표면 의존적 성공 이득만 보였고, 두 메타분석은 골 지표에서 서로 모순되며, 일상적 사용은 지지되지 않는다.

## Primary Prevention: Mucositis Management

The single most important strategic message: **managing peri-implant mucositis IS the primary prevention of peri-implantitis** (Jepsen 2015).

Key principles:
- Bleeding on probing (BoP) is the primary clinical sign differentiating healthy vs inflamed peri-implant tissue
- Absence of regular supportive therapy (SPT) increases progression risk
- Mucositis is reversible; peri-implantitis is not

**Mauriello 2026 (narrative review, 9 RCTs, n=414):**
- Professional mechanical plaque removal (PMPR) is the gold standard for mucositis management
- Adjunctive agents (CHX, local antibiotics, NaOCl, probiotics, bioactive agents): within-group improvement but NO consistent statistically significant added benefit over PMPR alone
- Clinical bottom line: establish PMPR protocol first; adjunctives do not substitute for mechanical removal [확인]

**Brunello 2026 (systematic review, 21 studies — 19 RCTs + 2 NRCTs, n=1068):** reinforces Mauriello with systematic evidence — all non-surgical protocols *including mechanical debridement alone* reduced BoP; mucositis resolution ranged 9%–100% across studies; adjuncts gave only minor 6-month gains; only one-third of studies had low risk of bias and heterogeneity precluded a network meta-analysis. Same conclusion: mechanical debridement is the backbone, adjuncts add little. [근거중간]

> **How to remove the biofilm** (powder/instrument choice, erythritol vs glycine, surface-safety, and the air-polishing emphysema caveat) is detailed in [[overviews/professional-biofilm-management-gbt-air-polishing-overview]]. For *early peri-implantitis*, Eraydın-Tüfek 2026 (5-arm RCT, 80 implants) found glycine powder air abrasion numerically best but with no statistically significant superiority over mechanical instrumentation.

**de Tapia 2026 (RCT, 48 analysed patients/118 implants, 3 months) — what hygiene instruction alone achieves** ([[implants/peri-implantitis/de-2026-impact-of-oral-hygiene-instructions]]):
- Oral hygiene instruction (OHI) alone resolved mucositis (mBI ≤0.16) in 39.1% of patients / 36.4% of implants, vs 56% / 67.3% with OHI plus professional mechanical instrumentation (implant-level OR 0.28 for OHI alone, p=0.007).
- Both arms reduced mBI (p<0.001), and the 3-month mBI did not differ significantly between arms (p=0.408).
- Compliance was the strongest predictor (OR 11.4; success 63.6% compliant vs 13.3% non-compliant); history of periodontitis gave a higher final mBI, posterior location trended toward lower resolution (p=0.056), and every implant whose prosthesis was modified for hygiene access resolved.
- Read with Mauriello and Brunello: professional instrumentation adds resolution on top of instruction, and compliance and cleanability are levers of their own. [rct, 3-month follow-up, not powered for superiority, self-reported compliance]

**Aksu 2026 (quasi-randomized trial, 43 patients/55 implants with keratinized tissue width <2 mm; abstract-only — full text not retrieved)** ([[implants/peri-implantitis/aksu-2026-soft-tissue-phenotype-modification-as]]):
- A free palatal graft placed one month after OHI and non-surgical therapy lowered 6-month BoP extent (0.75 vs 1.83 sites; MD −1.06, p=0.001) and raised treatment success (OR 8.44), with keratinized tissue width +2.36 mm and thickness +0.97 mm; PPD and soft-tissue dehiscence did not differ.
- The authors' mediation analysis attributed the bleeding benefit largely to factors other than improved plaque control.
- This is a surgical phenotype adjunct, unlike the chemical adjuncts above that add nothing consistent. Quasi-random allocation and an abstract-only reading keep it hypothesis-level; see [[overviews/keratinized-mucosa-peri-implant-health-overview]]. [quasi-rct, abstract-only]

## Non-surgical Adjunctive Therapy: Ranking Across Modalities

**Bai 2024 (network meta-analysis, 33 RCTs) — the first head-to-head SUCRA ranking of 7 non-surgical adjuncts + mechanical debridement (MD):**

The seven adjuncts (each combined with MD, vs MD alone): laser, photobiomodulation (PBMT), photodynamic therapy (PDT), systemic antibiotics (SA), probiotics, local antimicrobials (LA), air-powder polishing (APP). The core message matches the mucositis evidence above — **no single adjunct dominates; the best choice depends on which outcome matters and on disease stage (peri-implantitis PI vs mucositis PM)**:

| Disease | Outcome | Top-ranked adjunct (SUCRA) | Significant vs MD alone? |
|---|---|---|---|
| PI | PPD reduction | PBMT+MD (75.3%) | PBMT arm underpowered; SA+MD & LA+MD sig. |
| PI | CAL gain | SA+MD (87.4%) | Yes (SMD 2.20) |
| PI | MBL | SA+MD (99.9%) | Yes (SMD 3.92) |
| PM | PPD reduction | Probiotics+MD (100%) | **No** — top-ranked but NS |
| PM | BoP reduction | SA+MD (88.1%) | Yes |
| PM | PLI reduction | Probiotics+MD (83.2%) | No pairwise sig. |

- **Two guideline-relevant tensions.** (1) **SA+MD is the strongest performer for hard-tissue/attachment outcomes (CAL, MBL) in PI and for BoP in PM — yet the EFP S3 guideline (Herrera 2023) recommends *against* routine systemic antibiotics** for peri-implant disease (antimicrobial-stewardship/patient risk). Bai's authors explicitly flag this and advise prioritizing other adjuncts, especially for PM and mild PI. (2) **Probiotics+MD ranks #1 by SUCRA for PM soft-tissue outcomes but does not reach statistical significance for PPD** — a top rank on a network graph is not the same as a proven clinical effect.
- **Convergence with the mucositis evidence:** the "best" PM adjunct being top-ranked-but-non-significant is the same signal Mauriello 2026 and Brunello 2026 report — mechanical debridement is the backbone and adjuncts add little *consistent, significant* benefit. Bai adds the quantitative ranking layer without overturning that bottom line. [sr+ma / network MA, 33 RCTs — 10 low-RoB, low publication bias]
- **Contradicted on the bone outcome (2026).** Liu 2026 (SR+MA, 22 RCTs) found no bone-level benefit of adjunctive antibiotics (WMD 0.03 mm, P=0.39), against Bai's SA+MD top rank for MBL. The likely reasons and what remains undetermined are set out under "Liu 2026 vs Bai 2024" in the antibiotic section below.

**Direct-comparison RCTs since Bai (both abstract-only — full text not retrieved):**
- **Clementini 2026** (pilot single-masked 3-arm RCT, 47 patients/84 implants, 6 months; [[implants/peri-implantitis/clementini-2026-evaluation-of-the-adjunctive-use]]): titanium curettes alone vs plus erythritol air-polishing vs plus Er:YAG laser. PPD fell 1–1.4 mm at 1 month in all arms with slight relapse at 6 months; BoP fell 24–36% with no intergroup differences; only 12–18% of implants reached the therapeutic endpoint. Vestibular keratinized mucosa ≥2 mm and Er:YAG use were associated with success in an exploratory multilevel model. One surgical emphysema occurred with erythritol air-polishing.
- **Moreira 2025** (RCT, 12 months; [[implants/peri-implantitis/moreira-2026-air-polishing-versus-ultrasonic-instrumentation]]): erythritol air-polishing vs ultrasonic instrumentation, both with systemic metronidazole. The abstract reports that ultrasonic instrumentation reduced probing depth and BoP throughout 12 months but gives no sample size or effect sizes. Because both arms received metronidazole, it does not test the antibiotic.
- Together with Eraydın-Tüfek 2026, the direct trials match the network picture: device adjuncts do not clearly separate from mechanical instrumentation, and non-surgical endpoint achievement in established peri-implantitis is low.

## Surgical Treatment: Implantoplasty & Protocol Selection

Three 2025–2026 studies directly address which surgical *technique* to use and whether protocol choice matters — a gap that older decontamination-focused SR+MAs could not fill.

### Implantoplasty vs Titanium Brush — Park 2025 (RCT, n=30, Yonsei)

First head-to-head RCT comparing rotating titanium brush vs implantoplasty for peri-implantitis surgical decontamination (resective setting; osseous recontouring for crater defects; 12-month follow-up).

| Outcome | Titanium Brush | Implantoplasty | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| PPD reduction (mm) | −3.6 ± 1.5 | −3.3 ± 1.2 | NS — non-inferior |
| MBL change 12 mo (mm) | **0.0 ± 0.6** | **+0.7 ± 1.2** | **0.03** |
| Implant loss | 0 | 2 | — |
| Decontamination time | 3 min 1 s | 5 min 27 s | **0.006** |
| Disease resolution (composite) | 40.0% | 26.7% | 0.70 (NS) |

**Key message**: Titanium brush is non-inferior to implantoplasty for PPD reduction while preserving marginal bone (0 vs +0.7 mm) and halving surface decontamination time. Implantoplasty's irreversible thread grinding carries a MBL cost in the resective setting that brushes do not.

Pocket-closure failure predictors: higher baseline PPD (p=0.044) and **adjacent implant with peri-implantitis** (p=0.033 — novel finding; possible cross-contamination or extended surgical time). [rct, n=15/arm, single operator, underpowered for composite primary outcome]

### Full vs Partial Implantoplasty — Monje 2026 (quasi-RCT, n=40 implants, Spain)

First controlled comparison of implantoplasty *extent* in combined (resective + reconstructive) peri-implantitis surgery: FLIP (full-length: supracrestal + intrabony) vs PLIP (partial: supracrestal only; intrabony treated with NiTi brush + H₂O₂ + tetracycline).

| Outcome | PLIP | FLIP | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| PPD reduction (mm) | 3.53 ± 2.02 | 3.83 ± 1.45 | 0.42 (NS) |
| mSBI reduction | −1.73 | −2.19 | **0.003** |
| MBL gain (mm) | 1.62 ± 1.27 | **2.42 ± 1.20** | **0.009** (adjusted) |
| Mucosal recession (mm) | +0.71 → 1.33 | +1.73 → 1.84 | **0.006** (FLIP worse) |
| Disease resolution | 64.6% | 90.4% | — |
| Dehiscence | 4.8% | 21.1% | — |

**Adjusted disease resolution**: OR=14, p=0.13 (NS) — FLIP trend is strong but the study is underpowered for this endpoint. Smoking independently predicts failure (OR=0.01; p=0.013) regardless of implantoplasty extent.

**Key message**: When combined surgery is chosen, extending implantoplasty into the intrabony compartment (FLIP) yields better MBL gain and mSBI reduction, but at the cost of ~50% more mucosal recession. The esthetic zone trade-off is clinically meaningful. **COI note**: primary author (Monje) owns royalties on the burs used and receives SigmaGraft funding — treat as estimation data pending a powered confirmatory RCT. [quasi-rct, n=40, 1yr, single-center]

### OFD vs Implantoplasty vs Reconstructive vs Combined — Zhou 2026 (retrospective cohort, n=406 implants, Frankfurt)

Largest single-centre retrospective comparing all four surgical protocol categories, with standardised titanium brush decontamination across all groups, four trained surgeons, mean follow-up 30.80 months.

**1-year treatment success (max PD ≤5 mm + BOP ≤1 site + no suppuration):**

| Protocol | n | Success |
|---|---|---|
| OFD | 37 | 62.5% |
| Implantoplasty | 39 | 58.3% |
| Reconstructive | 241 | 55.4% |
| Combined | 89 | 48.3% |
| **Overall** | **406** | **54.7%** |

Group differences: **not significant** (FDR-adjusted). **Post-hoc power: 27%** — cannot interpret as equivalence.

**Multivariable predictors — treatment success:**

| Predictor | OR | p |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-operative antibiotics | 3.54 | 0.04 |
| Pre- + post-operative antibiotics | 4.49 | 0.02 |
| Surgeon experience | 0.12 | 0.003 |

**Key message**: Protocol choice (OFD vs Implantoplasty vs Reconstructive vs Combined) was not a significant predictor of 1-year success in real-world practice. **Surgeon experience and systemic antibiotics were stronger predictors than protocol selection** in this cohort. The surgeon-experience finding is echoed by the Ramanauskaite 2026 narrative review ([[implants/peri-implantitis/ramanauskaite-2026-factors-influencing-effectiveness-surgical-therapy]]), which lists surgeon experience among factors associated with effectiveness. The antibiotic signal is weaker than it looks: it comes from a single retrospective cohort, the same pre- + post-operative antibiotic variable also predicted implant loss (OR 3.13, p=0.02), which the authors attribute to antibiotics being prescribed for more complex or suppurative cases (confounding by indication), and Ramanauskaite 2026 classes the benefit of systemic antibiotics in surgical therapy as undetermined. Treat it as a hypothesis, not as evidence that adjuvant antibiotics matter more than the protocol label; a randomized test of adjunctive amoxicillin + metronidazole after access-flap surgery (Hakkers 2026, below) found no significant success difference. Implant loss was numerically highest in the Combined group (16.9%), consistent with this being applied to the most complex defects. Long-term success rates decline substantially: ~55% at 1 year → ~27–42% at 5 years across outcome criteria. [retrospective, 27% power, observational confounding]

### Synthesis Across Three Studies

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Titanium brush vs implantoplasty (resective)? | Ti brush non-inferior for PPD, superior MBL preservation, faster — favour brush (Park 2025 RCT) |
| Implantoplasty extent in combined surgery? | FLIP better MBL gain; weigh against recession in esthetic zones; avoid in aesthetically sensitive sites (Monje 2026) |
| Does protocol selection predict real-world success? | No — surgeon experience dominated; systemic antibiotics also predicted success, but in a single retrospective cohort with likely confounding by indication (antibiotics also predicted implant loss), and Ramanauskaite 2026 calls the antibiotic benefit undetermined, and a 2026 RCT found BoP-only benefit (see antibiotic section); 54.7% success regardless of protocol (Zhou 2026) |
| Why do all approaches plateau at 44–55% at 1 yr? | Consistent with immunopathological resistance (M1 macrophage bias, Treg dysfunction, titanium abiotic driver) rather than protocol failure alone |

## Reconstructive Surgery: GBR for Intrabony Defects

**Ramanauskaite 2023 (NMA):**
- GBR reconstructive therapy for contained peri-implantitis intrabony defects shows favorable defect fill and bone level gains vs non-reconstructive
- Indication: contained (circumferential) intrabony component
- Quantitative SUCRA rankings: full-text extraction recommended [미검증]

Reconstruction goals: defect fill, re-osseointegration, soft-tissue recession limitation.

**Hakkers 2026 (single-blind RCT, n=52 patients/63 implants, 1 year — defect-morphology subgroup data):**

Single-blind RCT (Groningen, Netherlands) comparing reconstructive surgery (autogenous bone + Bio-Oss xenograft 1:1 + Bio-Gide membrane) vs open-flap debridement (OFD) in 3- and 4-wall peri-implantitis defects that failed prior non-surgical treatment.

Key findings:
- **MBL (radiographic bone level)**: Reconstruction significantly superior at all timepoints — β = −1.65 mm at 12 months (95% CI −2.54 to −0.77, p<0.001); benefit increases progressively T3→T12
- **Mid-buccal recession**: Reconstruction significantly less recession — β = −1.68 mm at 12 months (p<0.001); clinically meaningful soft-tissue preservation
- **PPD, BoP, SoP, disease resolution**: NO significant differences between groups at any timepoint
- **Disease resolution rate at 12 months**: Test 43.8% vs Control 44.4% — virtually identical
- **Radiographic-clinical paradox**: Sustained radiographic bone fill without improved clinical inflammatory resolution — challenges use of radiographic endpoints alone as success criteria; xenograft radiopacity may confound MBL readings
- **Adverse events**: Reconstruction group had significantly more pain, mouth dryness, metal taste, and headache (all p<0.05) — should factor into patient counseling given equivalent disease resolution

**Defect morphology subgroup (exploratory, post hoc):**

| Defect type | Reconstruction advantage | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 3-walled defects | MBL (p=0.03 at T3, p=0.01 at T6); disease resolution at T6: 58.3% vs 16.7% (p=0.045) | Early benefit (T3–T6) |
| 4-walled defects | MBL at T12 only: test 2.5 vs control 3.0 mm (p=0.04) | Late benefit (T12 only) |

**Clinical implication**: 3-wall defects respond earlier and more robustly to reconstruction than 4-wall defects. The key decision driver is NOT defect morphology alone (3 vs 4 wall) but rather that **radiographic gain does not equate to clinical disease resolution** in either defect type. Reconstruction offers a soft-tissue and radiographic advantage; the patient must also accept a higher adverse event burden for no improvement in composite disease resolution. [rct, n=52, 1yr — methodologically rigorous]

**Su 2026 (narrative review) — mechanistic and material context for GBR:**
- **Graft type and inflammation — conflicting evidence.** Meta-analytic evidence cited by Su favors **xenogenic bone substitutes over autogenous bone or non-GBR treatment** for inflammation reduction and radiographic bone gain. The graft-type and radiographic part agrees with the Ramanauskaite 2026 narrative review ([[implants/peri-implantitis/ramanauskaite-2026-factors-influencing-effectiveness-surgical-therapy]]), which reports that slowly resorbing substitutes may outperform autogenous bone, and with Ramanauskaite 2023's favorable defect-fill and bone-level verdict above. The inflammation part conflicts with the other held pages: Ramanauskaite 2026 finds no clear benefit of reconstructive adjuncts in resolving clinical inflammation; Hakkers 2026 (autogenous + xenograft + membrane vs OFD) found no between-group difference in BoP, SoP, PPD or disease resolution; and Soldini 2025 found equal disease resolution (about 44% in both arms) and no difference in clinical variables despite about three times more bone fill. Read Su's inflammation-reduction claim as unconfirmed: the radiographic benefit of reconstruction is consistent, the inflammatory benefit is not.
- Adds mechanistic nuance often missing from RCT-level reporting: peri-implant biofilm on titanium is compositionally and antibiotic-resistance-wise **distinct from periodontal biofilm**, and **titanium corrosion/ion release** compounds local inflammation and bone loss — relevant background for why Hakkers' reconstructed sites still showed persistent inflammatory signs (BoP/SoP) despite bone fill.
- **CBCT** is framed as the diagnostic gold standard for 3D defect morphology (superior to periapical radiography for dehiscence/infrabony lesions) — a methodological note relevant to how defect morphology (3-wall vs 4-wall, per Hakkers) should be characterized pre-surgically.
- Extends the materials landscape beyond collagen membrane + xenograft (the Hakkers protocol): **biodegradable magnesium (Mg) membranes** offer mechanical stability with resorption timed to bone healing, and **customized 3D-printed titanium mesh** improves precision for complex ridge augmentation but carries mesh-exposure risk — candidate next-generation alternatives to the standard collagen-membrane GBR protocol.
- Echoes Hakkers' adverse-event finding: short-term postoperative pain increase with resorbable GBR membranes, reinforcing the need for patient counseling.
- Flags **machine learning** risk-prediction models (demographic + clinical + surgical variables) as an emerging direction for personalized peri-implantitis/implant-failure risk stratification. [narrative-review — non-systematic, synthesizes existing meta-analyses/RCTs/case series]

**Holtzman 2026 (dual-center RCT, n=32, 12 months, no dropouts) — connective tissue graft as a GBR adjunct** ([[implants/peri-implantitis/holtzman-2026-effect-of-connective-tissue-graft]]):
- GBR (collagenated DBBM block + resorbable membrane, coronally advanced flap, perioperative amoxicillin-clavulanate in both arms) with vs without a subepithelial connective tissue graft (CTG), stratified by baseline keratinized mucosa (<2 vs ≥2 mm).
- CTG increased CAL gain (3.21 vs 1.65 mm, p=0.022; primary outcome) and keratinized mucosa width gain (2.25 vs 0.26 mm, p=0.010); PPD reduction favoured CTG but was not significant (3.25 vs 1.97 mm, p=0.052).
- Disease resolution did not differ (EFP criteria 62.5% vs 56.3%, p=0.719; Derks criteria 62.5% vs 50.0%), and the effect did not differ by keratinized-mucosa stratum. Between-group bone level was not significantly different at either time point.
- Resolution here (50–62.5%) is higher than Hakkers or Soldini (about 44%); the authors attribute this to milder baseline disease (mean PPD 6.3/5.6 mm vs 8.6 mm in Derks 2022). [rct, n=32, 1yr, no histology, powered for CAL]

**Puisys 2026 (RCT, n=31, 12 months; abstract-only — full text not retrieved) — autogenous reconstruction ± electrolytic cleaning** ([[implants/peri-implantitis/puisys-2026-peri-implant-reconstruction-with-autogenous]]):
- Autogenous tuberosity bone graft + mechanical debridement, with or without electrolytic cleaning (EC). Both arms improved PPD, GI and BOP with no between-group differences, and suppuration resolved in both.
- Radiographic mesial defect fill was greater with EC (4.6 vs 2.5 mm, p=0.002), but intraoperative distal fill at 6 months did not differ (p=0.069). No histology, so re-osseointegration is unconfirmed.
- This reinforces the clinical null result for EC in [[implants/peri-implantitis/regidor-2026-electrolytic-decontamination-peri-implantitis-rct]] and adds an autogenous-graft protocol to a xenograft-dominated literature. [rct, abstract-only]

**Cross-trial pattern — structural or radiographic gain is not disease resolution:**

| Trial | What improved | Disease resolution / clinical inflammation |
|---|---|---|
| [[implants/peri-implantitis/hakkers-2026-reconstructive-peri-implantitis-3wall-4wall-rct]] | MBL β −1.65 mm; mid-buccal recession β −1.68 mm (reconstruction vs OFD) | 43.8% vs 44.4%; PPD, BoP, SoP not different |
| [[implants/peri-implantitis/soldini-2025-reconstructive-therapy-peri-implantitis-rct]] | About three times more bone fill | About 44% in both arms; clinical variables not different |
| [[implants/peri-implantitis/holtzman-2026-effect-of-connective-tissue-graft]] | CAL gain 3.21 vs 1.65 mm; KM width gain 2.25 vs 0.26 mm (CTG vs none) | 62.5% vs 56.3% (p=0.719) |
| [[implants/peri-implantitis/puisys-2026-peri-implant-reconstruction-with-autogenous]] | Radiographic mesial fill 4.6 vs 2.5 mm (EC vs none; abstract-only) | PPD, GI, BOP not different |
| [[implants/peri-implantitis/hakkers-2026-adjunctive-systemic-amoxicillin-and-metronidazole]] | BoP −10.57 / −14.47 percentage points at T9 / T12 (antibiotic vs none) | 39.6% vs 23.8% (not significant); PPD, SoP, MBL not different |

Five randomized comparisons of different adjuncts (graft, soft-tissue graft, decontamination device, antibiotic) show the same dissociation: the adjunct moves the outcome it acts on (bone fill, attachment, mucosa, bleeding) but not the composite resolution endpoint at 12 months. All five are small, with one-year follow-up and binary endpoints they were not powered for, so "no difference" is not equivalence. The pattern fits the immunopathology account of resistant lesions in [[overviews/peri-implantitis-etiology-prevalence-risk-factors-overview]] but does not prove it. [synthesis of held RCTs]

**Context, not peri-implantitis treatment — Zuercher 2026 (5-year RCT, 22 completers)** ([[bone-regeneration/zuercher-2026-block-versus-particulate-deproteinized-bovine]]):
- Simultaneous GBR for buccal dehiscence defects (≥3 mm) *at implant placement*, comparing an individually shaped DBBM block vs particulate DBBM under a pinned collagen membrane.
- At 5 years the block preserved more buccal hard tissue at the implant shoulder (1.36 vs 0.07 mm; adjusted difference −1.2 mm, p<0.001); the particulate arm lost most of its thickness in the first 6 months. Marginal bone level, BoP, plaque and survival were comparable, mucositis was 40.9% vs 38.1%, and no peri-implantitis or implant loss occurred in either arm. Two block cases had late graft separation.
- What it adds here: graft form governs long-term buccal contour in non-contained dehiscence defects, but in this trial that contour difference did not translate into a difference in peri-implant disease. It is a preventive and placement-stage result, not evidence for choosing graft form when treating peri-implantitis defects. See [[overviews/gbr-simultaneous-implant-placement-contour-dehiscence-overview]]. [rct, n=22 of 34 planned, exploratory]

## Systemic Antibiotics & Long-Term Durability of Surgical Outcomes

This section addresses two questions the 1-year protocol trials above cannot: does an adjunct antibiotic help, and do surgical gains last. Three 2025 syntheses opened it; the 2026-10 surveillance batch added an antibiotic meta-analysis (Liu 2026), a surgical antibiotic RCT (Hakkers 2026) and a salivary-microbiome study (Riben 2026).

**Adjunct systemic antibiotics — Antonoglou 2025 (SR+MA, 7 studies: 3 RCTs + 4 non-RCTs, 595 patients/1388 implants):**
- Short-term (1–2 years) treatment success favoured antibiotics (OR 2.33, 95% CI 1.29–4.21), with less BoP (OR 0.49) and SoP (OR 0.33) and +0.37 mm bone gain.
- No long-term (≥3 years) benefit (OR 1.84, p=0.43); the benefit was confined to **modified-surface implants** (OR 4.10 vs 0.79 for turned surfaces).
- This partly supports the Zhou 2026 antibiotic signal above, but only as a short-term, surface-dependent effect — consistent with the "undetermined" reading in Ramanauskaite 2026 rather than a routine-use recommendation. [sr+ma, only 3 RCTs]

### Pooled estimate vs a dedicated surgical RCT — Antonoglou 2025 and Hakkers 2026 (with Zhou 2026 and Riben 2026)

**Reconciliation:**
- **Where Antonoglou and Hakkers agree.** Neither finds a PPD effect (Antonoglou MD −0.15 mm, residual PPD ≥5 mm unchanged; Hakkers −0.14 mm), and both find lower bleeding (Antonoglou BoP OR 0.49; Hakkers BoP at T9/T12). Hakkers' resolution difference (39.6% vs 23.8%) points the same way as the pooled success OR but is not significant in a trial underpowered for a binary endpoint.
- **Where they diverge.** Antonoglou pools a bone gain (0.37 mm) and a suppuration reduction (SoP OR 0.33, GRADE high); Hakkers finds neither (MBL −0.02 mm; mean SoP −2.71%, p=0.26; a post hoc SoP difference at T6 only). The pooled success benefit rests mainly on non-randomized studies: the RCT-only subgroup was not significant at 1–2 years and pointed the other way, without significance, at ≥3 years (OR 0.69, 0.32–1.47). The surface interaction (modified OR 4.10 vs turned 0.79) cannot be checked against Hakkers from the wiki page.
- **Zhou 2026.** Its OR 4.49 is the largest antibiotic signal held, and it is the least causal: the same variable predicted implant loss (OR 3.13), which the authors attribute to antibiotics being given to harder cases. When allocation was randomized (Hakkers), the success difference was not significant.
- **Riben 2026.** It does not test antibiotic vs no antibiotic. It shows the ecological cost of a 7-day course (salivary richness loss at Day 8, recovered by 6 months) and gives no community-level or clinical basis to prefer the narrow-spectrum penicillin V partner over amoxicillin (success 83% vs 69%, p=0.645, n=25). It cannot speak to efficacy against placebo.

### Do the meta-analyses agree on bone? Liu 2026 vs Bai 2024 (contradiction)

[[implants/peri-implantitis/liu-2026-efficacy-of-adjunctive-antibiotics-compared]] (SR+MA, 22 RCTs, 1028 patients, search to January 2026) found no bone-level benefit of adjunctive antibiotics (WMD 0.03 mm, 95% CI −0.04 to 0.11, P=0.39, 7 trials, I²=0%), alongside small soft-tissue gains (PPD −0.69 mm, CAL −0.55 mm, BOP −12.45 percentage points; I² 91–95%) and similar PPD gains by route (local −0.68 vs systemic −0.70 mm). [[implants/peri-implantitis/bai-2024-seven-nonsurgical-methods-peri-implantitis-network]] (network MA, 33 RCTs, search to July 2023) ranked SA+MD first for peri-implantitis MBL (SUCRA 99.9%, SMD 3.92 vs MD alone) and CAL (SUCRA 87.4%, SMD 2.20). The Liu page types this as `contradicts` on the bone outcome specifically. Why the two may diverge, from what the two pages record:

| Factor | Liu 2026 | Bai 2024 | Effect on the comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| Effect metric | WMD in mm | SMD (unitless; ≥0.8 counts as large) plus SUCRA rank | 3.92 is a standardized effect, not 3.92 mm; SUCRA 99.9% is a rank probability, not a size. The two numbers cannot be compared directly |
| Antibiotic route | Local and systemic pooled (bone-level route subgroup not reported on the page) | Systemic antibiotics only (6 SA+MD studies) | A systemic-specific bone effect could be diluted in Liu's pool; the page cannot show it either way |
| Comparator | MD alone pooled with active non-antibiotic adjuncts (probiotics, PDT, chlorhexidine, EMD, iodine glycerin); the announced sensitivity analysis excluding active controls has no reported result | MD alone (plus indirect comparisons through the network) | Active controls shrink between-arm differences in Liu |
| Treatment setting | Surgical and non-surgical not stratified | Non-surgical only (surgical approaches excluded) | The populations differ |
| Evidence behind the bone result | 7 trials, I²=0% | 6 RCTs across 3 treatments, with significant global inconsistency (p=0); few CAL/MBL studies flagged by the authors | Bai's MBL ranking rests on a small, internally inconsistent network |
| Other appraisal problems | I² 91–95% for PPD, CAL and BOP; CAL not significant under Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman (P=0.0675); GRADE for PPD rated high despite I²=91% | Follow-up pooled across 3–12 months; authors call definitive conclusions premature | Both carry substantial uncertainty |

**What can and cannot be said.** Neither page lets one result override the other: they answer different questions (systemic vs any route, non-surgical vs mixed, MD alone vs mixed controls) on different scales. What the mm-scale evidence does show is that wherever a bone effect has been measured in millimetres it is small or absent: Liu 0.03 mm (pooled), Antonoglou 0.37 mm (surgical, GRADE low), Hakkers −0.02 mm (surgical RCT, not significant). Bai's top rank for MBL should therefore not be read as a large bone benefit. Still undetermined: whether systemic antibiotics specifically, added to non-surgical debridement and compared with debridement alone, change bone level — no held paper isolates that comparison with a consistent network and a mm-scale estimate. Liu is newer and larger but does not supersede Bai, because it does not test the same comparison. [Grusovin 2022, [[implants/peri-implantitis/grusovin-2022-antibiotics-non-surgical-peri-implantitis-ma]], reported larger PPD gains with systemic than local antibiotics in non-surgical therapy; Liu's route comparison did not reproduce that.]

### Clinical bottom line on adjunctive antibiotics

- **Routine adjunctive systemic antibiotics are not supported** for either non-surgical or surgical peri-implantitis therapy. This is consistent with the EFP S3 guideline ([[implants/peri-implantitis/herrera-2023-prevention-treatment-peri-implant-efp]]) and with the conclusions of Liu, Hakkers and Antonoglou.
- **Any benefit is short-term and mostly on bleeding:** BoP at 9–12 months in the surgical RCT, short-term success in pooled data that relies mainly on non-randomized studies, and none at ≥3 years. PPD and bone level do not improve in the RCT, and pooled bone effects in mm are small or nil.
- **Any benefit is surface-dependent** in the only held analysis that stratifies by surface (modified surfaces, Antonoglou). Antonoglou's authors suggest case-by-case short-term use for modified-surface implants; Liu's authors suggest reserving antibiotics for acute episodes or well-defined cases (for example deep pockets with suppuration, immunocompromised patients). These are author suggestions, not tested selection rules; Hakkers notes the EFP PPD ≥7 mm or suppuration criterion was written for non-surgical therapy.
- **Costs are documented:** diarrhoea 21.2% vs 0% in the one trial with harms data (Antonoglou), a transient salivary microbiome disturbance with either penicillin partner (Riben), no resistance monitoring in any of Liu's 22 trials, and stewardship concerns raised by Liu, Hakkers, Antonoglou and Riben.
- **Local antibiotics** are a separate question: Lu 2026 (2% minocycline, 12 weeks; [[implants/peri-implantitis/lu-2026-minocycline-local-delivery-peri-implantitis-multicenter-rct]]) and Liu's local-route subgroup show soft-tissue gains (PPD/BoP, plaque score), and neither supplies bone-level evidence for the local route. [synthesis — clinical reasoning from held papers]

**Durability — Monje 2025 (SR, 17 studies ≥5 years, 839 patients/1326 implants, mean 75.4 months, under supportive care ~3×/year):**
- Mean disease resolution 58.6% (range 30.4–97.7%), arrest of progressive bone loss 69.6%, retreatment need 27.2%, implant survival 88.6%.
- Residual pockets ≥6 mm carried ~8× higher recurrence odds (OR 7.3–9.2); advanced baseline bone loss, keratinized mucosa <2 mm and modified surfaces also predicted recurrence.
- Surgery is effective but **not curative** in the long term. [sr, qualitative synthesis — high heterogeneity]

**Reconstructive durability — Isler 2025 (7-year follow-up of an RCT, 34 of 57 patients completed):**
- Xenograft + concentrated growth factor (CGF) vs collagen membrane: treatment success fell from 46.2%/50.0% at 1 year to 23.1%/30.8% at 7 years; survival 81.3% vs 88.8%.
- CGF gave no long-term advantage over collagen membrane; baseline suppuration on probing was the dominant failure predictor (OR 15.45, very wide CI 1.42–168.5). [rct, 41% attrition]

**Synthesis:** the 44–55% 1-year plateau (Hakkers, Zhou) is not a ceiling reached before decline stops — gains relapse over 5–7 years even under regular supportive care. Residual pocket depth, baseline suppuration and keratinized mucosa width are the modifiable or screenable predictors; post-surgical supportive care (see [[overviews/supportive-peri-implant-therapy-maintenance-overview]]) is necessary, not sufficient.
