## Three-line Summary

Oral mucosal epithelial turnover varies markedly by site: junctional epithelium renews in 1–6 days, buccal mucosa in 5–6 days, gingival oral epithelium in 10–12 days, and the palate in ~24 days — all faster than skin (~28 days). Non-keratinized sites cycle faster than keratinized ones because the cornification sequence is absent, and the junctional epithelium is exceptional due to constant microbial challenge and loose cell junctions. Clinically, these rates predict wound-healing timelines, device tolerance, and cytological sampling yield.

## 1. Background — Why Does Turnover Rate Matter?

Oral mucosal epithelium is a stratified squamous epithelium that acts as a mechanical, immunological, and chemical barrier. Its renewal rate — the time from mitosis in the basal layer to surface desquamation — determines:

- **Wound healing speed** after surgery, trauma, or ulceration
- **Device tolerance** (aligners, dentures, orthodontic appliances)
- **Drug permeation kinetics** (transmucosal drug delivery)
- **Diagnostic sensitivity** of exfoliative cytology
- **Periodontal susceptibility** — JE renewal is a first-line defense

The basal cell (Stratum Basale) is the stem-cell-containing layer; daughter cells differentiate and migrate superficially through the spinous (Stratum Spinosum) and granular (Stratum Granulosum, keratinized sites only) layers until they reach the surface and are shed.

## 2. Site-by-Site Turnover Times [미검증]

| Site | Epithelium Type | Turnover Time | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junctional epithelium (JE) | Non-keratinized | **1–6 days** | Fastest; bacterial challenge; loose junctions |
| Buccal mucosa (협점막) | Non-keratinized | **5–6 days** | Best cytology site; flexible; high cell yield |
| Floor of mouth (구강저) | Non-keratinized | ~14 days | Thin, high permeability |
| Gingival oral epithelium (치은) | Keratinized / parakeratinized | **10–12 days** | Cornification slows migration |
| Hard palate (경구개) | Keratinized | **~24 days** | Slowest; thick keratin layer |
| Soft palate (연구개) | Non-keratinized | ~14–16 days | Intermediate |
| **Skin epidermis (reference)** | Keratinized | **~28 days** | Standard comparator |

### 2.1 The Basal-to-Surface Pathway

```
Stratum Basale (mitosis → daughter cell)
    ↓
Stratum Spinosum (differentiation; desmosomes form)
    ↓ [keratinized sites only]
Stratum Granulosum (keratohyalin granules; lamellar bodies)
    ↓
Stratum Corneum (anucleate; shed as squames) ← KERATINIZED
    OR
Superficial non-keratinized layer (nucleated cells shed) ← NON-KERATINIZED
    ↓
Desquamation (탈락)
```

In **non-keratinized** epithelium (buccal mucosa, floor of mouth, soft palate, JE), cells retain their nuclei to the surface and are shed as nucleated squames. The absence of a stratum corneum shortens the overall transit time.

In **keratinized / parakeratinized** epithelium (attached gingiva, hard palate), cornification adds dwell time in the upper layers — hence the slower turnover at gingiva (10–12 d) and palate (~24 d).

## 3. Junctional Epithelium — A Special Case [미검증]

The junctional epithelium (JE) is the fastest-renewing oral epithelium (1–6 days) and is structurally unique:

| Property | JE | Oral Sulcular Epithelium |
|---|---|---|
| Turnover | 1–6 days | ~6–12 days |
| Keratinization | None | None |
| Cell junctions | Loose (wide intercellular spaces) | Tighter |
| GCF passage | Yes — intercellular route | Minimal |
| Basal lamina | Dual (external tooth + internal connective tissue) | Single |
| β4-integrin | Strongly expressed | Moderate |

**Why so fast?**

1. Constant microbial challenge from the subgingival biofilm — rapid cell replacement is a defense mechanism.
2. Loose cell junctions allow GCF (Gingival Crevicular Fluid, 치은열구액) to flow through, mechanically detaching cells faster.
3. The reduced adhesion forces relative to oral epithelium mean cells transit the layer quickly despite strong basal-lamina attachment (β4-integrin).

After periodontal surgery, JE begins re-formation within **days** and is histologically recognizable within ~1 week; full structural maturation takes 4–6 weeks.

## 4. Keratinized vs Non-Keratinized — Comparative Biology [미검증]

| Feature | Keratinized (치은, 경구개) | Non-Keratinized (협점막, 구강저) |
|---|---|---|
| Turnover | Slower (10–24 d) | Faster (5–14 d) |
| Barrier function | ↑ (keratin layer) | ↓ (no keratin) |
| Drug permeability | Lower | Higher |
| Mechanical resistance | ↑ | ↓ |
| Cytology yield | Lower surface cell shedding | Higher (best: buccal) |
| Response to trauma | Slower initial re-epithelialization | Faster |

This divergence explains why the buccal mucosa is the **preferred site for transmucosal drug delivery** (faster absorption due to high permeability and no keratin barrier) and for **oral cytology brushings** (abundant, freshly shed nucleated cells).

## 5. Clinical Applications

### 5.1 Wound Healing & Ulceration [미검증]

- **Simple aphthous ulcers / traumatic ulcers** on buccal mucosa: re-epithelialization theoretically completes within 7–14 days, consistent with the observed clinical course of minor aphthae (typically heal in 7–10 days without scarring).
- **Post-surgical healing** (e.g., frenectomy, biopsy site): buccal and floor-of-mouth wounds close faster than palatal donor sites — aligns with the faster non-keratinized turnover.
- **Palatal donor sites** after connective tissue graft: slower re-epithelialization (~3–4 weeks for full surface coverage) matches the ~24-day palatal turnover.

### 5.2 Periodontal Surgery [미검증]

- JE reformation begins within **2–3 days** post-surgery.
- Long junctional epithelium (LJE) — the biologic reattachment mechanism in flap surgery without bone-graft — is histologically complete in **3–4 weeks**.
- The speed of JE reformation is why regenerative procedures must use membranes or biologics to exclude JE from the defect space.

### 5.3 Clear Aligners & Orthodontic Appliances [미검증]

- Initial mucosal irritation from a new aligner tray: buccal mucosa renews in ~5–6 days → **most patients adapt within 1 week**, consistent with reported adaptation literature.
- Repeated trauma (poor-fitting appliance) overrides the renewal capacity → ulceration persists.

### 5.4 Exfoliative Cytology [미검증]

- The buccal mucosa is the optimal sampling site because:
  - High turnover → abundant surface cells available for brushing
  - Non-keratinized → cells are nucleated and analyzable
  - Accessible and low trauma
- The hard palate yields fewer diagnostic cells due to its thick keratin layer and slower turnover.

### 5.5 Transmucosal Drug Delivery [미검증]

Turnover rate indirectly affects drug delivery:
- Fast-turning non-keratinized sites (buccal, sublingual) have high permeability → preferred for sublingual/buccal tablets, films
- The palate — thick keratin, slow turnover — is a poor delivery site
- **Sublingual** (구강저) area is fastest-clearing but also fastest-absorbing

## 6. Comparison: Oral Mucosa vs Skin vs GI Epithelium [미검증]

| Tissue | Turnover |
|---|---|
| Junctional epithelium | 1–6 days |
| Buccal mucosa | 5–6 days |
| Small intestinal villus epithelium | ~3–5 days |
| Gingival oral epithelium | 10–12 days |
| Hard palate | ~24 days |
| Skin epidermis (forearm) | ~28 days |
| Esophageal epithelium | ~2–3 days |

The GI epithelium turns over even faster (intestinal villi: 3–5 days; esophagus: 2–3 days), which puts oral mucosa in an intermediate position — faster than skin but generally slower than gut.
