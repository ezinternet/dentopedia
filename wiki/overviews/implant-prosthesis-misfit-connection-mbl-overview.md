> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **결론(bottom line)**: 수동적합 (passive fit)은 오래된 도그마지만, 부적합 (misfit)이 임플란트주위 변연골소실 (marginal bone loss, MBL)을 직접 일으킨다는 근거는 생각보다 약하고 미묘하다 — 부적합의 1차 임상결과는 **골 소실이 아니라 나사 합병증(screw-related complication)**이다.
> - **Katsoulis 2017 체계적 문헌고찰 (systematic review, SR)** (생체내 10편 = 인체 5 + 동물 5): 부적합 → 나사 풀림·파절 위험↑은 확인됐으나, **골 소실 연관성은 확인 불가**, 허용 가능 부적합 임계값 (tolerable misfit threshold)도 미정. 정의·측정법이 연구마다 제각각인 게 핵심 문제.
> - **측정법(assessment)은 비표준화**: 선형 왜곡 (linear distortion), 수직 갭 (vertical gap), 변형률 게이지 (strain gauge), 나사 회전력 (screw torque) — 4가지가 혼재해 메타분석 (meta-analysis) 자체가 불가능했다.
> - **Jokstad 2014** (n=30 무치악, 평균 19년, 3D 스캐너): 임상 평균 부적합 **150 μm**인데도 임플란트 생존율 96.7% — 장기적으로 상당한 부적합을 **생물학적으로 견뎠다(long-term tolerance)**. 골 소실과의 상관은 약함(R²=0.04, P=.29).
> - **Jokstad의 진짜 신호**: 나사 합병증을 겪은 보철군의 부적합이 유의하게 더 컸다(169 vs 134 μm, P=.005) — 즉 부적합의 위험은 **역학적(나사)** 경로로 발현.
> - **Couso-Queiruga 2025** (n=505 임플란트, 10.6년): 부적합 ↔ 골 소실을 **최초로 정량화**. 갭 임계값 **≥0.1 mm**, 용량-반응 = 갭 0.1 mm당 변연골소실 (distance to bone, DIB) +0.08 mm.
> - **단, Couso-Queiruga의 반전**: 다변량 모델에서 갭 크기(GapDIM)는 유의성을 잃고(P=.28), **흡연(+0.36 mm)·치주염 병력(+0.16 mm)**이 독립적으로 더 강한 예측인자. → 부적합은 순수 역학보다 **생물학적 경로(갭 → biofilm 저류 → 염증 → 골 소실)**로 작용할 가능성.
> - **연결부 설계(connection)는 부적합과 독립적인 별개 변수**: Kim 2022(n=374, 동일 설계·연결부만 상이)에서 외부연결 (external hex)이 1년 변연골소실↑(1.23 vs 0.72 mm, P<.001)이나 5년엔 수렴, 생존율은 차이 없음(93.3 vs 90.7%). 초기 골 적응 후 수렴하는 패턴.
> - **임상 보호인자**: Kim 2022에서 나사-시멘트 혼합유지 보철 (Screw-and-Cement-Retained Prosthesis, SCRP)이 변연골소실 감소와 독립 연관(P=.027) — 잉여 시멘트 완전 제거 가능 때문. 위험인자는 중증 흡연·긴 임플란트·브릿지.
> - **종합 임상 프레임워크**: ① 가능한 최소 부적합을 목표로 하되 ㎛ 단위 완벽주의에 집착할 필요는 없다(150 μm도 19년 생존). ② ≥0.1 mm 가시적 변연 갭은 재제작·수정 대상. ③ 골 소실의 진짜 동인은 흡연·치주염 같은 **환자 위험인자**이므로 부적합 통제와 함께 이 인자들을 우선 관리. ④ 연결부는 내부 원추형 (internal conical)이 초기 변연골소실에 유리하나 장기 생존은 동등 — 부적합 관리가 연결부 선택보다 비중이 크지 않다.
> - **[2026-07-09 추가] Raabe 2026 (25년 조직수준 임플란트, n=233)**: 보철유형(단일치관/연결치관/브릿지)·유지방식·변연부적합·발출부 형태(emergence geometry) **전부** 25년 변연골 변화(ΔCBL)에 유의한 영향 없음(p≥0.17) — 절치부 위치·탐침깊이·화농만 유의(p<0.001). Kim 2022의 "5년 내 수렴" 패턴을 **25년까지 확장·재확인**하는 최장기 근거 — 보철 미시역학보다 환자·부위 생물학이 지배적이라는 이 overview의 핵심 명제를 강화.
> - **[2026-07-09 추가] Gehrke 2019 (in-vitro, CAD/CAM 어버트먼트)**: 기존에 "SCRP가 잉여 시멘트 제거 가능해 MBL 보호적"이라던 서술(Kim 2022)에 대한 **기전적 정량 근거**를 최초로 제공 — 어버트먼트 변연이 점막하로 깊어질수록 미검출 잔류 시멘트가 증가하며, 근원심·설측부는 **1.5mm 이하 점막하 깊이**를 넘지 않아야 한다는 실행 가능한 수치 임계값 제시.
> - **[2026-07-09 추가] Wach 2026 (5년, n=155)**: 크라운 발출각(emergence angle, 32°±10°)은 단일·연결치관에서 MBL과 유의한 연관 없음, 브릿지에서만 약한 연관(p=0.042) — Raabe 2026의 "발출부 형태 무관" 소견과 일관되며, 보철 형태 변수 전반이 MBL의 약한 예측인자임을 재확인.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 8 papers (Katsoulis 2017 SR, Jokstad 2014 retrospective, Couso-Queiruga 2025 retrospective, Kim 2022 retrospective 5-year, Raabe 2026 retrospective 25-year, Gehrke 2019 in-vitro, Wach 2026 retrospective 5-year, Song 2024 retrospective 10-year): implant-prosthesis misfit reliably raises screw-loosening/fracture risk (concern band >134 µm; Katsoulis 2017), yet 150 µm mean misfit survived ~19 years at 96.7% implant survival (Jokstad 2014) and bone-loss correlation was weak (R²=0.04, p=.29) — misfit's primary clinical consequence is mechanical, not biological.

A ≥0.1 mm radiographic gap is the quantifiable bone-loss threshold (+0.08 mm bone loss per 0.1 mm gap, Couso-Queiruga 2025), but gap dimension loses significance in multivariate models once smoking (+0.36 mm) and periodontitis history (+0.16 mm) are entered — the bone-loss pathway is biological (biofilm reservoir) rather than direct biomechanical overload, a conclusion now extended to 25 years (Raabe 2026: prosthesis type, retention, misfit, AND emergence geometry all non-significant for ΔCBL; only incisor location/probing depth/suppuration mattered).

Abutment-connection type independently affects early MBL (external hex 1.23 vs internal conical 0.72 mm at 1 year, p<0.001) but converges by 5 years with equivalent survival (Kim 2022); SCRP retention independently protects against MBL, now mechanistically explained by Gehrke 2019's in-vitro finding that undetected residual cement rises with subgingival margin depth (keep margins ≤1.5 mm submucosal); patient biology (smoking, periodontitis) and gross-fit control matter more than the last micron of passive fit or the exact prosthetic geometry (crown emergence angle: Wach 2026).

## Summary

Passive fit — seating a fixed implant prosthesis without inducing static strain in the framework, components, or peri-implant bone — has been a prosthodontic dogma since the Brånemark era. The implicit assumption was that any marginal misfit transmits load to bone and provokes peri-implant bone loss. The four papers synthesized here, read together, substantially **nuance** that assumption: misfit's most reliable consequence is **mechanical (screw complications)**, its biological (bone-loss) effect is small, threshold-dependent, and likely **biofilm-mediated rather than purely biomechanical**, and connection design is a **separate, independent** contributor to early marginal bone loss.

### The misfit-and-bone-loss question is genuinely nuanced

**Katsoulis 2017** (SR + Foundation for Oral Rehabilitation 2016 consensus; 2,626 records → 10 in vivo studies, 5 human + 5 animal) is the definitional anchor. Its three findings frame the whole topic: (1) misfit of screw-retained implant-supported fixed dentures **does** raise screw-loosening/fracture risk; (2) a **biological sequela — bone loss — could not be confirmed**; (3) a maximum tolerable misfit threshold **could not be defined**. Crucially, Katsoulis 2017 attributes the inability to answer the bone-loss question partly to **methodological heterogeneity**: misfit was assessed four incompatible ways across studies — linear distortion, vertical gap, strain gauges, and screw torque — making meta-analysis impossible. The clinical bottom line was conservative: aim for the least misfit achievable, but gaps and static strains do not appear to harm initial osseointegration or long-term peri-implant bone stability; they raise screw complications.

**Jokstad 2014** (retrospective, n=30 edentulous mandibles, average 19 years, range 12–32) supplies the longest-follow-up clinical misfit data and the strongest evidence for **long-term tolerance**. Using a then-novel 3D method (intraoral scan of implant positions + desktop scan of the detached FDP intaglio, matched in metrological software), it quantified a mean clinical misfit of **150 μm** (range 95–232). Despite that, implant survival was **96.7%** over ~19 years and the correlation between misfit and marginal bone loss was **weak and non-significant (R²=0.04, P=.29)**. The signal that *was* significant matched Katsoulis 2017: prostheses with screw complications had significantly higher misfit than those without (**169 vs 134 μm, P=.005**), giving an empirical screw-complication concern threshold around **>134 μm**. So Jokstad 2014 reinforces that misfit's consequence is mechanical, not biological — and that substantial misfit is survivable long-term.

**Couso-Queiruga 2025** (retrospective, n=301 patients / 505 Straumann tissue-level implants, 10.6 years, University of Bern) is the paper that finally **quantifies** the bone-loss association Katsoulis 2017 could not confirm — and in doing so reveals why it had been elusive. Radiographic gap analysis showed a monotonic, statistically significant univariate relationship: distance-to-bone (DIB) of **3.22 mm (no gap) → 3.36 mm (gap >0–<0.1 mm) → 3.45 mm (gap ≥0.1 mm)**, with **≥0.1 mm** as the threshold and a **dose-response of +0.08 mm bone loss per 0.1 mm gap** (P=.03). But the decisive twist is in the multivariate model: **gap dimension (GapDIM) lost significance (P=.28)**, while **smoking (+0.36 mm, P=.002)** and **history of periodontitis (+0.16 mm, P=.001)** remained strong independent predictors. The authors interpret this as misfit acting through a **biological pathway** — the gap is a biofilm reservoir that drives inflammation and bone loss — rather than direct biomechanical overload. (One scope caveat: tissue-level implants place the gap supracrestally, so bone-level implants, with the gap nearer the crest, may show a larger effect.)

So the misfit→bone-loss story across these three: **associated and dose-dependent in isolation, but small in magnitude, biologically (not mechanically) mediated, and subordinate to patient risk factors.** This is exactly the "nuanced, less catastrophic than dogma assumed" picture — passive fit matters, but perfect ㎛-level fit is neither achievable nor the dominant determinant of peri-implant bone health.

**Raabe 2026** (single-center observational, n=147 patients / 233 tissue-level implants, University of Bern) pushes this exact question out to the longest horizon yet tested — **25 years**. Patients restored with single crowns, splinted crowns, or bridges were re-examined at both 10 and 25 years. The result is a clean null across every prosthetic-design variable studied: prosthesis type (p=0.37 for ΔCBL, p≥0.95 for peri-implantitis prevalence at either timepoint), prosthetic retention, **marginal misfit**, and transmucosal **emergence geometry** were all non-significant for 25-year crestal bone change. What *was* significant — incisor-region location, probing depth, and suppuration on probing (all p<0.001) — are patient/site biology variables, not prosthetic ones. This is the strongest available confirmation that the Couso-Queiruga 2025 finding (gap loses significance once smoking/periodontitis are modeled) is not a multivariate artifact of a shorter follow-up: even out to a quarter-century, misfit and prosthetic geometry do not reliably outcompete biology as bone-loss drivers. **Wach 2026** (retrospective, n=155 patients, MIS implants, 5-year) independently reinforces the emergence-geometry null: crown emergence angle (mean 32°±10°) was not significantly associated with MBL for single or splinted crowns, and only weakly so for bridges (p=0.042) — consistent with Raabe 2026's finding that emergence geometry does not survive as an independent long-term predictor.

### Connection design is a separate, independent lever

**Kim 2022** (retrospective 5-year cohort, n=374 implants / 175 patients, Gangneung-Wonju National University, Korea) isolates a different variable: implant–abutment **connection type**. Its design strength is a near-matched pair — Sola (external hex, butt joint) vs Luna (internal 11° conical hex), Shinhung — identical in design and SLA surface, differing **only** in connection geometry, minimizing confounding. Findings: (1) **no survival difference** (external 93.3% vs internal 90.7%, P=.353); (2) **external connections lost significantly more bone at 1 year** (1.23 vs 0.72 mm, P<.001), but this **converged by 5 years** (1.20 vs 1.00 mm, P=.137) — an early-adaptation pattern consistent with microgap/microleakage being a transient early-loading phenomenon at the external butt joint. The internal conical connection's tighter seal reduces microgap and early crestal remodeling, but does not buy a long-term survival advantage. Kim 2022 also surfaces a clinical lever orthogonal to misfit: **SCRP (screw-and-cement-retained prosthesis) was independently associated with less MBL (P=.027)** — attributed to extraoral cementation allowing complete excess-cement removal — while **heavy smoking (P=.001), longer implants (P=.018), and bridge prostheses (P=.004)** raised MBL. The smoking finding independently echoes Couso-Queiruga 2025. **Gehrke 2019** (in-vitro, 20 CAD/CAM titanium molar abutments) supplies the missing mechanistic quantification behind the SCRP/excess-cement claim: undetected residual cement area increased as the crown-abutment margin was placed progressively more submucosally (0/1/2/3 mm tested), with no clean depth cutoff below which residue disappeared — the practical recommendation is to keep CAD/CAM abutment margins as coronal as possible and **not exceed 1.5 mm subgingival depth** in proximal/oral surfaces when esthetics forces a submucosal margin. This gives the overview's "excess-cement control matters" claim an actual number, not just a plausibility argument.

### Clinical decision framework

1. **Aim for the least misfit achievable, but do not chase perfection.** Passive fit remains the goal, yet 150 μm misfit survived ~19 years (Jokstad 2014) and no consensus maximum exists (Katsoulis 2017). Micron-level obsession is not evidence-supported; **gross misfit avoidance** is.
2. **Use ≥0.1 mm visible radiographic gap as an actionable trigger.** A gap at or above this threshold is associated with measurable bone loss (Couso-Queiruga 2025) and warrants framework correction or remake rather than acceptance.
3. **Treat misfit's primary risk as mechanical (screw complications).** Misfit >134 μm clusters with screw loosening/fracture (Jokstad 2014; Katsoulis 2017) — manage with verified seating, correct preload/torque, and screw maintenance recall.
4. **Recognize the bone-loss pathway is biological, so control biology first.** Because gap dimension loses independent significance once smoking and periodontitis are accounted for (Couso-Queiruga 2025; smoking reconfirmed in Kim 2022), **smoking cessation and periodontal control outrank ㎛-level fit refinement** for protecting bone.
5. **Connection choice: prefer internal conical for early crestal preservation, but expect long-term parity.** External-hex butt joints show more 1-year MBL that converges by 5 years with equal survival (Kim 2022), and the "converges over time" pattern now holds out to 25 years for prosthesis type, retention, misfit, and emergence geometry alike (Raabe 2026). Connection type is a real but limited and self-limiting variable — not a substitute for misfit and patient-factor control.
6. **Favor cement-removal-friendly retention (SCRP) where indicated, and keep CAD/CAM margins shallow.** SCRP was independently protective against MBL (Kim 2022), and Gehrke 2019 supplies the concrete mechanism and threshold: undetected residual cement rises with subgingival margin depth, so keep CAD/CAM abutment margins as coronal as esthetics allow, and do not exceed **1.5 mm submucosal depth** in proximal/oral surfaces.
7. **Do not over-invest in crown contour/emergence-angle optimization as an MBL lever.** Crown emergence angle showed no reliable independent association with MBL for single or splinted crowns (Wach 2026) and was non-significant even at 25 years across prosthesis types (Raabe 2026) — unlike misfit and cement control, this is not a well-supported place to spend clinical effort chasing bone protection.

**Net thesis:** passive fit is a durable principle, not an absolute. The dominant, manageable consequence of misfit is screw complication; its bone effect is real but small, threshold-gated (≥0.1 mm gap), and mediated through biofilm/inflammation, which is why **patient risk factors (smoking, periodontitis) and excess-cement control matter more than the last micron of fit** — a conclusion now confirmed out to 25 years, across prosthesis type, retention, misfit, and emergence geometry alike (Raabe 2026). Connection design contributes independently to early bone remodeling but converges over time. Cement-margin depth (Gehrke 2019) is a controllable, quantified lever within the biofilm pathway; crown emergence angle (Wach 2026) is not. The clinician's leverage is greatest on biology, gross fit, and cement control — not on perfectionist passivity or prosthetic-contour micromanagement.
