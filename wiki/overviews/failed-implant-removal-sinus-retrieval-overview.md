> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **주제 2갈래**: (1) 실패 임플란트를 **어떻게 뽑나** (explantation 기법 선택), (2) 상악동에 **빠진** 임플란트를 어떻게 **원인 관리·제거**하나. 5편을 하나로 묶은 이유 — 두 갈래가 "제거(retrieval)"라는 공통 실행 문제를 공유.
> - **일반 explantation 기법 서열** (Shenoy scoping review 388편, Solderer narrative review): 역토크 래칫 기법 (Counter-Torque Ratchet Technique, CTRT)이 가장 덜 침습적 → **1차 선택**; 잔존 골유착 **4 mm**가 CTRT-단독 임계값, 제거토크 **>200 Ncm**면 trephine 병용. Trephine bur가 실전에서 가장 흔하지만 골을 희생.
> - **압도적 원인**: 두 근거 모두 **주위염(peri-implantitis)**이 후기 실패·제거의 최다 원인(Solderer 81.9~82.9%, Shenoy ~150편).
> - **지르코니아**는 파괴인성이 티타늄의 1/4 이하(4–18 vs 77 MPa·m^0.5) → CTRT 사실상 **금기**, trephine/piezo/laser로 대체.
> - **재식립 생존율**: 71–100% (2차 시도), 큰 직경·거친 표면 임플란트가 유리.
> - **상악동 변위**는 별도 임상 시나리오 — 원인은 **잔존골 부족·과드릴링(각 ~28%)·폐쇄형 sinus lift(~15%)**가 식립 중 변위의 다수(Mahmood Hashemi 39례: 22/39 식립 중), 술후는 골유착 실패(힐링어버트먼트·보철 단계).
> - **변위 시점이 예후를 가른다**: 기능부하 *후* 변위는 수술 중 변위보다 상악동 opacification·osteitis가 유의하게 심함(Lee 2024, opacification 3.6 vs 1.7 p=0.019; osteitis 40% vs 0%) — timing이 유일한 독립 예측인자.
> - **제거 접근**: Caldwell-Luc(전통, 국소마취 가능, 술자 다수 선호) → 내시경(FESS, 전신마취 필요) → 측방 골창(현재 선호, 최소침습). An 2017은 **확장 골창**으로 제거+골이식+재식립을 1회 동시 시행하는 술식을 제안(2–3단계 대비 재활 단축).
> - **예방이 곧 치료**: 잔존골 **<5 mm**는 식립 전 sinus lift 필수 — 두 시리즈(Mahmood Hashemi, Lee) 모두 site preparation 누락을 반복 확인.
> - **무증상이어도 제거**: 이물반응성 만성 부비동염 위험 때문에 증상 없어도 상악동 변위 임플란트는 제거가 원칙(일부 예외적 무증상 장기 존치 보고 있음).
> - **임상 결론**: (1) 일반 explantation은 CTRT 우선, 골 보존을 위해 침습도 낮은 순으로 escalate; (2) 상악동 변위는 즉시 제거 + 술전 골평가/sinus lift로 예방; (3) 재식립은 대체로 예후가 좋으나 부위·재료 선택이 성패를 가름.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 5 papers spanning general implant explantation technique selection (Shenoy 2025 scoping review, 388 studies; Solderer 2019 narrative review, 34 studies) and the maxillary-sinus-displacement retrieval scenario (An 2017 technique note; Lee 2024 retrospective cohort n=13/14; Mahmood Hashemi 2024 case series n=39, 25 years).

For general failed-implant removal, the counter-torque ratchet technique (CTRT) is least invasive and the recommended first choice (4 mm remaining osseointegration = CTRT-alone threshold; removal torque >200 Ncm → add trephine), while zirconia's low fracture toughness (4–18 vs titanium's 77 MPa·m^0.5) makes CTRT risky for that material; peri-implantitis drives 82–83% of late failures/removals, and reinsertion in previously failed sites survives 71–100%.

For sinus-displaced implants, timing of displacement is the dominant prognostic variable (post-loading displacement causes significantly worse sinus opacification and osteitis than perioperative displacement, p=0.019/0.013) while inadequate bone height and over-drilling account for most intraoperative displacements (22/39 cases, Mahmood Hashemi) — immediate removal via Caldwell-Luc, FESS, or an extended lateral bony window (allowing simultaneous grafting and reimplantation) is the shared management principle, and residual bone <5 mm should be sinus-lifted before placement to prevent it in the first place.

## Thesis

**Clinical question:** When a dental implant fails or is displaced, how should it be removed, and — for the specific and increasingly common scenario of maxillary sinus displacement — how should the complication be managed and prevented?

The literature splits cleanly into two connected problems. The **first** is technique selection for explantation in general: across 388 studies ([[implants/shenoy-2025-dental-implant-failure-retrieval-techniques-scoping]]) and a focused 34-study Q&A review ([[implants/solderer-2019-removal-failed-dental-implants-revisited]]), the same five techniques recur — counter-torque ratchet (CTRT), trephine burs, piezosurgery, laser, and electrosurgery-induced thermoexplantation — ranked consistently by invasiveness, with **CTRT as the least traumatic first choice** and trephine burs as the common but bone-sacrificing fallback. Peri-implantitis is overwhelmingly the reason removal becomes necessary (~82%), and reinsertion after removal is broadly successful (71–100%) provided the replacement implant is appropriately sized. Zirconia implants complicate this picture: their low fracture toughness makes CTRT more likely to shatter the fixture than unscrew it.

The **second** problem is a specific, increasingly reported retrieval scenario: implants displaced into the maxillary sinus. Three papers — a Korean technique note proposing single-stage extended-window retrieval ([[implants/an-2017-treatment-dental-implant-displacement-maxillary-sinus]]), a Seoul National University cohort quantifying the sinus damage this causes ([[implants/lee-2024-characteristics-impacts-implant-displacement-maxillary-sinus]]), and a 25-year, 39-patient Iranian case series mapping *when and why* displacement happens ([[implants/mahmood-hashemi-2024-causes-implant-migration-maxillary-sinus-case-series]]) — together establish that (1) displacement is usually a site-preparation failure (inadequate residual bone, over-drilling, crestal sinus lift gone wrong), (2) **timing of displacement, not just its occurrence, determines prognosis** (post-loading displacement causes measurably worse sinus pathology than intraoperative displacement), and (3) removal via Caldwell-Luc, FESS, or a lateral bony window is uniformly effective and should not be delayed. The unifying clinical message across both problems: **prefer the least-destructive retrieval method the situation allows, and treat prevention (site-appropriate planning, sinus-lift thresholds, primary-stability protocols) as the more powerful lever than any removal technique.**

## Clinical Decision Points

1. **Default to CTRT for general explantation.** It is the least traumatic technique and preserves the most bone for reimplantation (Solderer). Escalate to trephine/piezo/laser only when removal torque exceeds ~200 Ncm, the implant/prosthetic component is fractured, or CTRT access is unavailable.
2. **Treat zirconia differently.** Its fracture toughness is roughly 1/5–1/20 that of titanium; CTRT is likely to snap the fixture rather than unscrew it. Bone-resection methods (trephine, piezo, laser, extraction set) apply more comparably across materials.
3. **Manage peri-implantitis proactively — it is the dominant reason removal becomes necessary at all** (~82% of late failures/removals, both Shenoy and Solderer). Removal is the last-resort step after non-surgical/surgical peri-implantitis therapy fails (see [[implants/peri-implantitis]]).
4. **For maxillary sinus displacement, remove promptly regardless of symptoms.** Foreign-body chronic sinusitis risk persists even in asymptomatic cases (An 2017); delay allows osteitis to develop, especially in post-loading displacements (Lee 2024).
5. **Choose the retrieval access by what the sinus needs, not by habit.** Caldwell-Luc remains reliable under local anesthesia with low complication rates in large series (Mahmood Hashemi, 39 patients, 0 complications 2–10 y); FESS/endoscopic access is less invasive but needs general anesthesia and more equipment; a single-stage extended lateral window (An 2017) can combine removal, grafting, and reimplantation to shorten rehabilitation when the case allows it.
6. **Prevent displacement at the planning stage.** Residual bone height <5 mm is the recurring red flag across both sinus-displacement papers — perform a sinus lift before placement rather than relying on over-drilling or an unsupported closed sinus lift.
7. **Set expectations for reinsertion.** Whether after general explantation (71–100% survival, Solderer) or sinus retrieval, replacement implants should favor larger diameter/rough surface for primary stability — but counsel patients that displacement itself often causes reluctance to re-treat (31/39 in Mahmood Hashemi).

## Gaps & Future Research

- No randomized comparisons exist between explantation techniques (CTRT vs trephine vs piezo vs laser) — all evidence is observational/case-based (Shenoy, Solderer both note this).
- Zirconia-specific removal data remain scarce; electrosurgical thermoexplantation is anecdotal (single case reports) in both general and sinus-displacement literature.
- No study has directly compared Caldwell-Luc vs FESS vs lateral-window outcomes in a controlled design for sinus-displaced implants; current preference is driven by case-series consensus, not head-to-head trials.
- Histopathological characterization of sinus osteitis after implant displacement is missing (Lee 2024 explicitly flags this) — needed to clarify whether osteitis changes long-term prognosis after removal.
- Standardized definitions of "explantation success" and "implant failure" are still inconsistent across the literature, limiting pooling (echoed across all five source papers).
