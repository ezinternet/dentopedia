## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 7편 종합. 질문은 "어떤 이식재를 쓸까"가 아니라 "**골을 어디서 채취할까**" — 기존 오버뷰들이 다루지 않은 공여부(Donor Site) 선택 축.
> - **1차 위계는 명확하다: 구강내(Intraoral) > 구외(Extraoral)**. 구강내 자가골(IOG)이 장골능(ICG) 대비 전 추적시점(6~120개월) 생존율 우위, 24개월에서 격차 최대(98.2% vs 85.9%, p<0.001, McKenna 2022 SR+MA 23편). Donkiewicz 2021(SR 100편)도 자가골 블록(AUBB) 하위군에서 구강내>구외 유의(p=0.0003) 재확인.
> - **자가골 vs 동종골은 전체적으로 동등**(96.23% vs 97.66%, p=0.54 NS, Donkiewicz 2021) — 공여부 회피가 우선이면 동종골 블록(ALBB)이 실행 가능한 대안. 단 ALBB도 수직증대·신선동결처리 시 생존율 하락.
> - **장골능(ICG)은 "열등"이 아니라 "최후 수단"**: Sethi 2020(173명·190그래프트·869임플란트·최장 23년)는 95%±2.7% 장기 생존을 보고 — 대형 결손에서 구강내로 불충분할 때 여전히 유효한 옵션. 공여부 합병증도 혈청종(seroma)뿐, 보행장애·감각이상 보고 없음(McKenna 2022의 "합병증 흔함" 서술과 대비되는 저이환 코호트).
> - **신흥 프론티어 = 채취가 아니라 재활용**: 상악동 거상 측방골창(lateral bony window)은 원래 버려지거나 재위치되는 조직인데, 이를 인접 광범위 발치와 결손의 자가골 블록으로 전용하면 **추가 공여부 자체가 사라진다**(Park 2022, 4증례, 6개월 재진입 100% 완전 충전).
> - **16년 전 이식된 상악동도 재채취 가능**: DBBM으로 거상한 상악동을 16년 후 재개봉해 골코어 채취 시 96.4%가 신생 생골(잔존 DBBM 3.6%) — 장기간 이종골이 숙주골로 거의 완전 치환된다는 직접 조직학적 증거이자, 재수술 부위 자체를 자가골 공여부로 쓸 수 있다는 증명(Park 2022, n=1).
> - **후구치 공여부는 12개월에 89.2% 자발 치유**(6개월 64.5%→12개월 89.2%, 밀도 102.5→453.9 HU, Daoud 2024 전향 n=20) — 공여부 추가이식 불필요를 지지하며 동일 부위 순차 재채취 가능성을 시사.
> - **상악결절(Maxillary Tuberosity)은 "삼중이식편"으로 경조직+연조직을 한 번에**: 결합조직+피질해면골블록+잔여 해면골을 단일 절개로 채취 — 공여부 재건 불필요(비하중 부위, 이차치유), 신경혈관 위험 낮음, 6년 추적 무합병증(Zufia 2022, n=4). 단 채취량이 2~10mm로 매우 가변적이라 술전 CBCT 필수.
> - **공여부 이환율 서열**: 장골능(통증·보행장애·감각이상) > 하악 이부(하악전치 감각이상) > 후구치·상악결절(합병증 사실상 0). 이환율이 위계를 뒤집는 실제 임상 변수 — 생존율 차이보다 환자가 체감하는 부담 차이가 더 크다.
> - **임상 결정 순서**: ① 인접 수술(상악동거상 등)로 부산물 골이 생기는가 → 무료 재활용 우선 ② 소~중형 결손 + 경연조직 동시 필요 → 상악결절 삼중이식편 ③ 표준 중형 결손 → 후구치/이부 등 구강내 표준 부위(재생 가능, 12개월 89% 자발치유) ④ 구강내로 불충분한 대형 결손만 → 장골능(장기 생존 검증됨, 이환율 감수).
> - 근거 수준 편차 큼 — McKenna·Donkiewicz는 SR/SR+MA(고근거), Sethi는 대형 후향(양호), 나머지 4편(Daoud·Zufia·Park×2)은 소규모 전향/증례 수준 — 신흥 재활용 개념은 개념증명 단계로 읽을 것.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 7 papers on autogenous bone graft **donor site** selection — a question distinct from graft *material* choice: intraoral sources consistently outperform extraoral (iliac crest) on implant survival at every follow-up point (98.2% vs 85.9% at 24 months, p<0.001, McKenna 2022 SR+MA) and carry far lower donor-site morbidity, while autogenous and allogeneic blocks are broadly equivalent overall (96.23% vs 97.66%, NS, Donkiewicz 2021 SR).

Iliac crest remains a viable last-resort option for large defects intraoral sites cannot supply — Sethi 2020's 25-year, 869-implant cohort reports 95%±2.7% long-term survival with only seroma as donor-site morbidity — but the emerging frontier is not choosing among conventional harvest sites at all: repurposing surgical byproducts (a sinus lateral bony window, a 16-year-old previously-grafted sinus core) as autogenous donor material eliminates the second surgical site entirely, and the retromolar donor site itself heals 89.2% spontaneously by 12 months, supporting sequential re-harvest.

Evidence weight is uneven — the site-hierarchy conclusions rest on strong SR/SR+MA evidence (McKenna, Donkiewicz) and a large retrospective cohort (Sethi), while the repurposing concept rests on small prospective/case-report data (Daoud, Zufia, Park×2) that should be read as proof-of-concept, not yet practice-changing volume.

## 세줄요약

자가골이식 **공여부** 선택(재료 선택과는 다른 축) 7편 종합 — 구강내 공여부가 전 추적시점에서 장골능(구외)보다 임플란트 생존율 우수(24개월 98.2% vs 85.9%, p<0.001, McKenna 2022 SR+MA), 자가골 vs 동종골 블록은 전체적으로 동등(96.23% vs 97.66% NS, Donkiewicz 2021 SR).

장골능(ICG)은 구강내로 부족한 대형 결손의 최후수단으로 여전히 유효(Sethi 2020, 25년·869임플란트, 생존 95%±2.7%, 합병증은 혈청종뿐)하나, 진짜 신흥 프론티어는 재래식 공여부 사이의 선택이 아니라 **수술 부산물의 재활용** — 상악동 측방골창·16년 전 이식 상악동 골코어를 재채취해 추가 공여부를 아예 없애는 것이며, 후구치 공여부 자체도 12개월에 89.2% 자발 치유돼 순차 재채취 가능성을 시사한다.

근거 무게는 불균등 — 공여부 위계 결론은 SR/SR+MA·대형 후향 코호트(McKenna·Donkiewicz·Sethi)에 기반한 견고한 근거이나, 재활용 개념(Daoud·Zufia·Park×2)은 소규모 전향·증례 수준으로 개념증명 단계로 읽어야 한다.

## Thesis

Most bone-regeneration synthesis pages in this wiki ask "which graft *material*" — DBBM vs BCP vs allograft. This overview asks a prior, distinct question: when the graft is autogenous, **where does the surgeon cut it from?** The seven papers held here answer that question at two very different levels of maturity.

At the level of settled evidence, the answer is a clean hierarchy: intraoral donor sites beat extraoral (iliac crest) on both implant survival and patient morbidity, and this hierarchy is now supported by SR+MA-level data (McKenna 2022) rather than tradition alone. Within the extraoral option, long-term outcome data (Sethi 2020) shows iliac crest is not obsolete — it remains a durable option when intraoral volume is insufficient — but its adoption should be reserved, not routine, given the well-documented (if in Sethi's cohort, mild) donor-site burden.

At the level of active innovation, four small studies converge on a theme none of the larger reviews anticipated: **the best donor site may be material that would otherwise be discarded or left to heal on its own.** A sinus lateral bony window, normally repositioned or discarded at the end of a sinus lift, can instead graft an adjacent post-extraction defect (Park 2022a). A sinus grafted with xenograft 16 years earlier can be re-opened and its now-96%-vital-bone core harvested as autograft (Park 2022b). A retromolar donor site heals to 89.2% of its own volume within a year, raising the possibility of sequential re-harvest from the same site (Daoud 2024). And the maxillary tuberosity, an underused corner of intraoral anatomy, yields a "triple graft" — bone block, particulate bone, and connective tissue from one incision (Zufia 2022). None of this displaces the intraoral-over-extraoral hierarchy; it extends it, by shrinking the very idea of a dedicated "second surgical site."

## Results

### Axis 1 — The Donor Site Hierarchy: Intraoral > Extraoral (McKenna 2022, Donkiewicz 2021, Sethi 2020)

**McKenna 2022** (SR+MA, 23 studies, 14 meta-analyzed, PROSPERO-registered) is the highest-evidence direct comparison: implant survival for intraoral grafts (IOG) vs iliac crest grafts (ICG) at five timepoints.

| Timepoint | ICG | IOG | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 mo | 95.8% | 98.4% | <0.001 |
| 12 mo | 97.0% | 98.4% | <0.001 |
| 24 mo | 85.9% | 98.2% | <0.001 |
| 60 mo | 90.0% | 91.5% | <0.001 |
| 120 mo | 88.8% | 95.2% | <0.001 |

The gap is largest at 24 months (12.3 percentage points) and never reverses. Donor-site morbidity — pain, gait disturbance, sensory disturbance — was consistently higher for ICG; mental-region intraoral harvest carries its own risk of anterior mandibular paresthesia, but this is a lesser burden than iliac harvest's systemic effects (gait, ambulation).

**Donkiewicz 2021** (SR, 100 studies, 10,295 implants) reframes the comparison around block-graft type rather than site alone, and the **overall** result is graft-type equivalence, not site superiority: autogenous bone block (AUBB) 96.23±5.27% vs allogeneic bone block (ALBB) 97.66±2.68%, p=0.54 (NS). But its AUBB subgroup analysis independently reconfirms McKenna's site hierarchy from a different 100-study pool: intraoral donor sites significantly outperform extraoral within the AUBB arm (p=0.0003), and total edentulism (93.92%) underperforms partial edentulism (98.08%, p=0.0002) — likely reflecting reduced residual bone stock and vascularity in the fully edentulous jaw. Immediate implant placement into a fresh AUBB graft underperforms early/delayed placement (91.56% vs 97.60%, p=0.018) — a timing lever independent of donor site.

**Sethi 2020** (retrospective, 173 patients, 190 ICG onlay grafts, 869 implants, up to 23-year follow-up, mean 109 months) supplies the long-term denominator for the ICG arm that McKenna's SR+MA pooled from shorter multi-study data: Kaplan-Meier survival 95%±2.7%, remaining above 92.3% out to 200 months. This is a **low-morbidity** ICG cohort — the only donor-site complication was seroma (11/173 patients); no gait disturbance or sensory loss was reported, in contrast to McKenna's pooled complication profile. The two papers should be read together, not as contradictory: McKenna's pooled ICG morbidity draws on heterogeneous multi-center data across decades and techniques, while Sethi's single-center, single-surgical-team cohort demonstrates that with a stable technique and long institutional experience, ICG morbidity can be minimized even if it is not eliminated as a *possibility*.

**Synthesis of Axis 1**: the intraoral > extraoral survival hierarchy is robust across two independent SR/SR+MA analyses (McKenna 2022, Donkiewicz 2021's AUBB subgroup) and is not an artifact of small-study bias. Iliac crest is not "bad bone" — Sethi's data shows it performs durably over decades — but it carries a morbidity profile (even at its best, seroma; at its more typical, pain/gait/sensory disturbance per McKenna's pool) that intraoral harvest simply does not. The clinical decision is therefore volume-driven: use intraoral sources whenever they can supply sufficient graft volume, and reserve iliac crest for defects that genuinely exceed intraoral capacity.

### Axis 2 — Donor Site Healing and Re-Harvest Feasibility (Daoud 2024)

A question the survival-hierarchy literature does not address: what happens to the intraoral donor site itself afterward? Daoud 2024 (prospective cohort, n=20, mandibular retromolar/external oblique ridge harvest, 3D CBCT with Boolean-subtraction volumetry) tracked spontaneous healing of the donor defect with no additional grafting material placed.

| Time Point | Volumetric Healing | Bone Density (HU) |
|---|---|---|
| Intraoperative (baseline) | — | 690.3 ± 81 |
| 6 months | 64.5 ± 4.24% | 102.5 ± 27.8 |
| 12 months | 89.2 ± 2.6% | 453.9 ± 91.4 |

No sex, age, block size, or jaw-side effect on healing rate was found — meaning patient selection for retromolar harvest need not be narrowed by these factors. The density trajectory (102.5 HU at 6 months, immature/woven-bone range → 453.9 HU at 12 months, approaching cancellous/trabecular density but still short of the 690.3 HU cortical baseline) indicates the donor site is well on its way to full remodeling by 12 months but not yet complete. This directly supports the standard practice of closing the donor site without grafting material, and opens — without yet proving — the possibility of returning to the same retromolar site for a second harvest once healing matures.

### Axis 3 — Repurposing Surgical Byproducts as Donor Material (Park 2022a, Park 2022b)

Two case reports from the same group describe a genuinely novel donor-site category: bone that already exists as a byproduct of another procedure, rather than bone that must be freshly cut from an untouched site.

**Previously-grafted sinus as donor (Park 2022, n=1)**: bone cores were harvested from a maxillary sinus that had been augmented with DBBM 16 years earlier. Micro-CT and histology of one core showed **96.4% newly-formed vital bone and only 3.6% residual DBBM** by volume — direct histological confirmation that xenograft in a mature, long-integrated sinus graft is overwhelmingly replaced by host bone over the long term. The second core, crushed to 1–3 mm fragments, was used for GBR around a peri-implant defect and covered with a resorbable collagen membrane; crestal bone levels were stable at 2-year follow-up. The clinical implication is narrow but real: a patient with a mature, long-standing sinus graft who needs a small subsequent GBR procedure has a nearly-autogenous donor source sitting in their own sinus, requiring no new donor incision.

**Lateral sinus bony window as donor (Park 2022, n=4)**: during standard lateral-window maxillary sinus augmentation, the cortical bone window is normally discarded or repositioned. This case series instead adapted the window — alone or combined with particulate DBBM — as a block autograft for an adjacent wide post-extraction defect requiring GBR, applicable whenever both procedures are needed in the same surgical field. All 4 cases achieved complete bone filling at 6-month re-entry with successful implant placement, stable at 12–24 months. This is not a technique that creates a new donor site; it converts material that would otherwise be surgical waste into graft, at zero incremental donor-site morbidity.

**Synthesis of Axis 3**: both papers share a structural logic distinct from Axis 1's site-comparison framing — they do not ask "which site is best" but "is there already bone available from a procedure I am doing anyway." Evidence weight is minimal (n=1 and n=4, case-report/case-series level, single group), so these are proof-of-concept demonstrations rather than validated protocols. But the concept — treating surgical byproducts and long-integrated grafts as harvestable material — is a genuinely distinct axis from the traditional donor-site hierarchy, and merits monitoring as larger series emerge.

### Axis 4 — Maxillary Tuberosity: A Niche Dual-Tissue Donor Site (Zufia 2022)

The maxillary tuberosity (MT) is a comparatively underused intraoral site relative to the chin and retromolar/ramus. Zufia 2022 (case series, n=4) describes its distinguishing feature: the **"triple graft" technique**, in which a single incision distal to the maxillary second molar yields connective tissue (palatal aspect), a corticocancellous bone block, and residual cancellous particulate — enabling simultaneous hard- and soft-tissue augmentation from one donor site in one surgical session (suited to Immediate Dentoalveolar Restoration protocols).

Advantages specific to this site: no donor-site restoration is required (non-load-bearing, heals by secondary intention), lower neurovascular risk (distal to the inferior alveolar nerve), and — in patients with recent third-molar extraction — post-extraction remodeling can increase the available volume. The binding limitation is anatomical variability: harvestable cancellous bone ranges from 2 mm to >10 mm, so preoperative CBCT assessment is mandatory before committing to this donor site, and it is only suitable for small-to-moderate localized defects, not large reconstructions. All 4 cases (immediate implant into type II/III sockets, horizontal/vertical, and circumferential defects) showed stable outcomes up to 6 years with zero donor-site complications (no oroantral communication, hematoma, or nerve injury).

## Clinical Decision Threads [미검증]

**Thread 1 — Default to intraoral, reserve iliac crest for volume the mouth cannot supply.** The survival and morbidity data both point the same direction: whenever a defect can be reconstructed with intraoral bone (chin, retromolar/ramus, tuberosity, or now, repurposed sinus-derived material), it should be preferred over iliac crest. Iliac crest is not contraindicated — Sethi 2020's 25-year data confirms durable long-term survival — but it should be a volume-driven fallback, not a default, given McKenna's pooled morbidity data (pain, gait disturbance, sensory disturbance) even though these were minimal in Sethi's own single-center cohort.

**Thread 2 — Check for "free" donor material before opening a new site.** Before planning a dedicated donor-site incision, ask: is there a concurrent or prior procedure that already produced usable bone? A simultaneous lateral sinus lift generates a bony window that can graft an adjacent defect at zero added morbidity (Park 2022a). A patient with a mature (multi-year) sinus graft undergoing a subsequent GBR procedure may be able to donate from that same sinus (Park 2022b). This is currently case-report-level evidence and should be treated as an emerging option to consider case-by-case, not a validated first-line protocol.

**Thread 3 — Retromolar donor sites can likely be re-harvested, but wait for maturation.** Daoud 2024's 89.2% volumetric healing at 12 months (density still short of native cortical bone) suggests a second harvest from the same retromolar site is biologically plausible after roughly a year, though this has not been directly tested — no study in this set re-harvested the same site.

**Thread 4 — Maxillary tuberosity for small defects needing both hard and soft tissue.** When a defect is small-to-moderate and the case also needs soft-tissue augmentation (e.g., thin biotype, keratinized tissue deficit at IDR), the tuberosity triple-graft technique (Zufia 2022) can supply both from one incision — but confirm adequate tuberosity volume by CBCT first, since it is not usable for large reconstructions.

**Thread 5 — Donor-site morbidity, not survival, is often the deciding factor for the patient.** Across this evidence set, implant survival differences between donor sites are real but modest in absolute terms (a few percentage points at most timepoints, larger at 24 months); the morbidity gap (systemic gait/sensory effects for iliac crest vs near-zero morbidity for tuberosity/retromolar/repurposed-byproduct sources) is often the more clinically meaningful axis for informed consent and patient preference.
