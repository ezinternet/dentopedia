> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 명제**: 임시 크라운(Provisional Crown) 재료 선택(Bis-acryl vs PMMA)과 소아 유치관(Pediatric Primary Crown) 재료 선택은 각각 별개의 근거 축으로 관리한다. 두 축 모두 재료별 강·약점 프로파일이 단일 지표(경도·유지율)로 환원되지 않는다.
> - **Axis 1 — 임시 크라운 경도(Surface Hardness)**: Bis-acryl 3종 모두 PMMA 자가중합(Pyrax SC-10 16.65 VHN)보다 유의하게 높음(p<0.01). 순위: Protemp 4 (20.54 VHN) > Integrity (19.80) > Vericom Care C&B (18.55) > PMMA. 장기·고응력 임시수복에는 Bis-acryl 우선.
> - **Axis 2 — 변연 적합 + 색안정성(Marginal Adaptation & Color Stability)**: 이중중합(Dual-cure) Luxatemp Solar가 변연오차 최대(319 µm). Protemp Garant만 임상적으로 인지되는 착색(ΔE=4.33). SNAP(폴리에틸메타크릴레이트, Polyethyl Methacrylate, PEMA) 아크릴은 변연 적합·색안정성 모두 Bis-acryl과 동등 — 단기·조정 필요 상황에서 경제적 선택.
> - **통합 임시 크라운 결정 규칙**: 장기(≥4주) 또는 고응력 부위 → Bis-acryl 중 Protemp 4 또는 Integrity(경도 최고, 색안정성 양호). 단기(1–2주), 조정 필요, 비용 제약 → SNAP/PMMA(변연·색 동등, 조정 용이). Luxatemp Solar는 이중중합 사용 시 변연 최대 오차 주의.
> - **Axis 3 — 소아 유치관 5년 유지율 순위(SR+MA, 13 RCTs)**: SSC 97.88% > 지르코니아(Zirconia) 97.09% > 복합레진(Composite Resin) 92.18% > RMGIC 90.30% > 컴포머(Compomer) 88.90% > 스트립관(Strip Crown) 83.48%.
> - **SSC**: 유일하게 RCT에서 유지 관련 합병증(탈회·파절·탈락 등) 0건. 심하게 파괴된 유치의 표준치료로 재확인.
> - **지르코니아 특이 신호**: 유지율은 SSC에 근접하나 치은염증(Gingival Inflammation) 21.8% — 다른 재료 대비 최고. 출판 비뚤림(Funnel Plot, Egger test) 유의(p<.001) → 과추정 가능성 주의.
> - **기존 오버뷰와의 차별점**: `caries-biomimetic-remineralization-orthodontic-pediatric-crown-overview`가 **전치부 PZC vs RCSC(2종 비교)**를 다루는 반면, 본 오버뷰는 **6종 전장 크라운(후치 포함) 유지율 전체 순위** + **임시 크라운 재료 3축 비교**를 제공하여 보완적 관계.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of three papers on material selection for provisional and pediatric primary crowns: in-vitro hardness study (sokhal-2025, n=160), in-vitro marginal-fit/color study (givens-2007, n=40), and SR+MA of pediatric primary-tooth crown failures (lampl-2025, 13 RCTs/5 clinical studies, 3839 crowns).

For provisional crowns, all bis-acryl composites outperform PMMA on hardness (Protemp 4 20.54 VHN > PMMA 16.65 VHN, p<0.01), but PMMA acrylic (SNAP) matches auto-cure bis-acryl on marginal fit and color stability; dual-cure Luxatemp Solar has the worst marginal gap (319 µm) and Protemp Garant the only noticeable staining (ΔE=4.33); for pediatric primary crowns the 5-year retention ladder is SSC 97.88% > zirconia 97.09% > composite 92.18% > RMGIC 90.30% > compomer 88.90% > strip crowns 83.48%.

Clinical rule: use bis-acryl (Protemp 4 or Integrity) for long-term/high-stress provisionals and PMMA for short-term adjustable cases; for primary teeth, SSC remains the gold standard for broken-down teeth, zirconia is a close second for esthetics but demands gingival monitoring.

## 세줄요약

임시 크라운 경도(sokhal-2025, n=160 in-vitro)·변연/색 안정성(givens-2007, n=40 in-vitro)·소아 유치관 실패 원인 SR+MA(lampl-2025, 13 RCT/5임상연구, 3,839개)를 통합한 합성 페이지.

임시 크라운: Bis-acryl이 PMMA 대비 경도 우위(Protemp 4 20.54 > PMMA 16.65 VHN, p<0.01)이나 SNAP(PEMA) 아크릴은 변연적합·색안정성에서 Bis-acryl과 동등; 이중중합(Dual-cure) Luxatemp Solar는 변연오차 최대(319 µm), Protemp Garant만 임상 인지 착색(ΔE=4.33). 소아 유치관 5년 유지율: SSC 97.88% > 지르코니아 97.09% > 복합레진 92.18% > RMGIC 90.30% > 컴포머 88.90% > 스트립관 83.48%.

임상 결론: 장기 임시수복 → Bis-acryl(Protemp 4·Integrity), 단기·조정 → PMMA/SNAP; 유치 전장 수복 → SSC가 1차 표준, 심미 요구 시 지르코니아(단 치은염증 21.8% 모니터링 필수).

## Summary

This overview integrates evidence from three papers across two distinct clinical contexts — provisional crown materials for adult restorative dentistry and full-coverage crown selection for pediatric primary teeth — neither of which is addressed by the existing prosthetic-materials decision ladder (which covers single implant crowns, screw-vs-cement retention, overdenture attachments, and abutment design for adult implant prosthetics) or the caries-biomimetic overview (which covers only prefabricated zirconia crowns vs composite strip crowns specifically for anterior primary teeth in a 2-material comparison).

**Provisional crown axis**: Two complementary in-vitro studies (Sokhal 2025 for hardness; Givens 2007 for marginal fit and color stability) build a complete three-dimensional material profile. Neither paper alone is sufficient for clinical decision-making: knowing a material is hard does not predict its color stability or how accurately it seats on the preparation, and knowing it fits well does not predict how it will wear over a multi-week temporization period. The combined read is that bis-acryl composites are superior on hardness and therefore preferred for long-term or mechanically demanding provisionals, but traditional PMMA acrylic (SNAP) is not inferior on fit or color, making it rational for short-term or adjustment-heavy situations. Within the bis-acryl class, specific weaknesses must be memorized by product: Protemp Garant stains noticeably within one week of tea immersion (ΔE=4.33), and dual-cure Luxatemp Solar seats with the largest marginal gap (319 µm).

**Pediatric primary crown axis**: Lampl 2025 is the highest-quality evidence for this question (SR+MA, PROSPERO-registered, RoB2-assessed, Poisson regression on cumulative crown-time). It quantifies a six-material ladder and, crucially, shows that retention rate alone is not the right summary statistic — materials separate on their complication *profile* (retentive vs biological vs technical), which changes the clinical choice depending on the patient's risk context.

## Axis 1 — Provisional Crown Surface Hardness

*Source: sokhal-2025 (Cureus 2025, in-vitro, n=160 specimens, ADA spec. no. 27 dies, Vickers hardness)*

| Rank | Material | Class | Hardness (VHN) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3M Protemp 4 | Bis-acryl | **20.54 ± 0.69** | Highest hardness; all pairwise vs others p<0.01 |
| 2 | Dentsply Integrity | Bis-acryl | 19.80 ± 0.47 | — |
| 3 | Vericom Care C&B | Dual-cure bis-acryl | 18.55 ± 1.36 | Widest SD; dual-cure |
| 4 | Pyrax SC-10 | PMMA (self-cure) | **16.65 ± 0.66** | Lowest; largest pairwise gap (vs Protemp 4 MD=3.89) |

All pairwise comparisons significant (Tukey HSD, p≤0.001). Cross-linked polymer matrix + inorganic filler explains bis-acryl advantage. Clinical translation: harder material resists surface wear and degradation over time, but is also more difficult to adjust and polish chairside.

**Decision rule — hardness axis:**
- Long-term provisional (≥4 weeks) or posterior high-stress area → prefer bis-acryl, best choice **Protemp 4**
- Short-term provisional (1–2 weeks) or high adjustment likelihood → PMMA acceptable despite lower hardness
- Cost-sensitive setting without extended temporization → PMMA (Pyrax SC-10 class)

## Axis 2 — Provisional Crown Marginal Adaptation and Color Stability

*Source: givens-2007 (J Prosthodont 2007, in-vitro, n=10/group, marginal discrepancy at 4 reference points; ΔE after 1-week cyclic tea immersion)*

| Material | Class | Marginal discrepancy | Color change (1-wk tea) |
|---|---|---|---|
| SNAP (Parkell) | PEMA acrylic | Low (∼ auto-cure bis-acryls) | No clinically detectable change |
| Protemp Garant (3M) | Auto-cure bis-acryl | Low | **ΔE = 4.33 (noticeable, p<0.05)** |
| Integrity (Dentsply) | Auto-cure bis-acryl | Low | No clinically detectable change |
| Luxatemp Solar (DMG) | Dual-cure bis-acryl | **319 µm (worst, p<0.05)** | No clinically detectable change |

Key take-homes:
1. The **dual-cure** curing mechanism correlates with worst marginal fit — the extended working time and different polymerization kinetics of dual-cure materials may allow more stress-induced distortion before set.
2. SNAP (PEMA acrylic, the "old technology") was **not inferior** to auto-cure bis-acryls on either dimension tested — the assumption that bis-acryl universally dominates is wrong for these properties.
3. Only one product-specific staining risk: **Protemp Garant** (the same brand that ranks second on hardness). A clinician who picks Protemp Garant for hardness should counsel on potential short-term staining in tea/coffee consumers.

## Axis 3 — Pediatric Primary-Tooth Crown 5-Year Retention Ladder

*Source: lampl-2025 (SR+MA; 13 RCTs n=454 children/1172 crowns + 5 clinical studies n=810/2667 crowns; Poisson regression on cumulative crown-exposure time; PROSPERO-registered; RoB2 assessed)*

### Retention Ranking

| Rank | Material | 5-year retention | Retentive complications (RCT) | Key biological signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **SSC** (Stainless Steel Crown) | **97.88%** | **Zero** (decementation, fracture, chipping: all 0) | Gingival inflammation 3.3% (clinical studies) |
| 2 | **Zirconia** | **97.09%** | 5 decementation + 1 complete + 1 partial loss | **Gingival inflammation 21.8%** (clinical studies); Egger p<.001 |
| 3 | **Composite resin** | 92.18% | 12 chipping, 3 fracture, 3 poor anatomic form | Gingival inflammation 24.24% (RCTs); shade mismatch 29.2%, staining 15.6% |
| 4 | **RMGIC** | 90.30% | — | — |
| 5 | **Compomer** | 88.90% | 8 fractures; marginal adaptation failure 29% | Secondary caries 8.3% |
| 6 | **Strip crowns** | **83.48%** | 7 complete + 19 partial material loss (highest) | — |

Overall unstratified: 92.20% (95% CI 92.14–92.26%).

### Complication Profile Summary

The retention ladder should be read alongside the **complication type** each material produces, because the clinical implication differs:

- **SSC**: best retention + fewest complications; opposing-tooth wear 13.6% (technical issue, not failure); remains gold standard for badly broken-down primary molars.
- **Zirconia**: nearly matches SSC retention, but gingival inflammation is a biological safety signal (21.8% vs SSC's 3.3%). Publication bias is significant for zirconia (Egger p<.001), meaning its retention rate may be over-estimated. Best suited for esthetic zones with informed consent for gingival monitoring. *See also*: `caries-biomimetic-remineralization-orthodontic-pediatric-crown-overview` for the anterior-specific PZC vs RCSC comparison (RR=0.15 for crown dislodgement favoring PZC).
- **Composite resin**: moderate retention; widest spread of esthetic failures (29.2% shade mismatch, 15.6% staining). Choose when SSC/zirconia are not available but expect composite-specific monitoring.
- **RMGIC**: slightly below composite; fluoride release is a biological advantage not captured in retention rate alone — relevant for high-caries-risk patients.
- **Compomer**: near RMGIC retention, but 29% marginal adaptation failure is a concern for longevity. Compomer had secondary caries 8.3%.
- **Strip crowns**: lowest retention (83.48%) and highest material-loss burden. Best reserved for anterior teeth with limited structural destruction where RCSC esthetics are essential and retention risk is discussed with parents.

### Decision Tree — Primary Tooth Crown Selection

```
Is the tooth badly broken down (>2 surfaces involved)?
  YES → SSC (gold standard, zero retentive complications)
  NO (moderate breakdown, esthetic zone)
    └─ Anterior primary tooth?
         YES → Is antagonist wear a concern (bruxism/parafunctional)?
                YES → RCSC/composite strip crown (softer)
                NO  → Prefabricated Zirconia Crown (PZC) — superior caries/retention
                       → Monitor gingival inflammation at every recall
         NO  (posterior, esthetic demand)
              └─ Zirconia (2nd choice, esthetic + near-SSC retention, gingival watch)
                 or RMGIC (high caries risk, fluoride release benefit)
```

## Contrasts and Gaps

| Comparison | Finding | Evidence level |
|---|---|---|
| Bis-acryl vs PMMA hardness | Bis-acryl > PMMA, all p<0.01 | in-vitro (Sokhal 2025) |
| PMMA vs bis-acryl marginal fit | Equivalent (SNAP = auto-cure bis-acryl) | in-vitro (Givens 2007) |
| PMMA color stability | No detectable ΔE; competitive with most bis-acryls | in-vitro (Givens 2007) |
| Dual-cure vs auto-cure marginal fit | Dual-cure (Luxatemp Solar) worst, 319 µm | in-vitro (Givens 2007) |
| SSC vs all other pediatric crowns | SSC = highest retention + zero retentive complications | SR+MA (Lampl 2025) |
| Zirconia gingival inflammation | 21.8% — highest among all pediatric crown materials (clinical studies) | SR+MA (Lampl 2025) |
| Zirconia publication bias | Egger p<.001 — potential over-estimation of retention | SR+MA (Lampl 2025) |

**Gaps not covered by these 3 papers:**
- Long-term clinical hardness vs wear data for provisional crowns under masticatory force (both Sokhal and Givens are in-vitro lab conditions only)
- Direct head-to-head of Protemp 4 (Sokhal 2025) against Protemp Garant (Givens 2007) — different products, different papers, no shared study
- Pediatric crown retention beyond 5 years (Lampl 2025 models 5-year endpoint; exfoliation confounds longer follow-up)
- Cost-effectiveness analysis across pediatric crown materials
