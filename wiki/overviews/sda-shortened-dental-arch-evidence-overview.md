>
> - **15년 RCT (Walter 2021):** 보철 성공률 14.2 vs 14.3년(중앙값) — 어느 비교도 유의차 없음; SDA는 타협이 아니라 *근거 기반 계획*.
> - **OHRQoL 일관성 (Fueki 2017 SR+MA·McKenna 2018 RCT·Nakai 2022 RCT):** 3개 독립 연구에서 SDA ≥ RPD — OHRQoL 비열등 반복 확인.
> - **수요 기울기 (Nassani 2013):** 대구치만 결손 → 3%만 보철 원함; 제1·2대구치 결손 → 58%; 소구치 결손 → 93%. 대구치 간극은 *술자의 문제*이지 환자의 문제가 아닌 경우가 많다.
> - **저작 기능 (Liang 2015 SR):** (E)SDA 환자 저작 수행력 28–39% 저하; RPD 약 50% 보상; 더 많은 인공치 = 더 나은 기능(용량-반응).
> - **선호 저작측 (PCS) 분기 (Koyama 2026 RCT):** 선호 저작측 (Preferred Chewing Side, PCS) 대구치 결손만 저작 효율 (Masticatory Efficiency, ME)·주관적 저작능력 (Subjective Masticatory Ability, SMA) 유의 저하; 비선호측은 NS → 측면 비대칭이 보철 결정의 축.
> - **임플란트 확장 (Gao 2025):** M1만 임플란트 = SDA 개념을 임플란트로 구현; 동등 생존율, 비용 1.7배↓, 치주염 0% vs 16.2%; SDA 철학은 고정성 임플란트에도 적용 가능.
> - **Witter 3단계 임계값:** 경도(대구치 결손) = 연장 근거 없음; 중등도 = 예외적으로; 극단 단축 = 중등도 수준까지만. 완전치열 회복 목표 설정은 증거 없다.
> - **노인 특화 (McKenna 2020):** SDA는 유지 부담을 줄여 aging population에서 기능 지향 치료(Functionally Orientated Tooth Replacement, FOTR)의 핵심 요소.
> - **SR 종합 (McLister 2018):** 10편(RCT 3편) 체계 검토 — 어떤 전략도 SDA 유지 대비 우월하지 않음; 개인화된 의사결정 지지.
> - **치주 (Kalsi 2021):** 10년 CAL SDA 0.79 mm 우위(NS 임상적); 치아 추가 소실 SDA군 > RPD군이라는 근거 없음.

## Three-line Summary

**Thesis**: The shortened dental arch (SDA) — bilateral canine and premolar support intact, dentition ending at the first molar — is a 15-year RCT-validated treatment strategy that is non-inferior to full-arch prosthetic restoration across OHRQoL, tooth survival, and periodontal health outcomes.

**Indexed papers**: 11 papers across SDA concept, RCTs, SR+MAs, masticatory function, periodontics, and implant extension.

**Clinical bottom line**: SDA legitimacy is established; the decision axis has shifted from "should we restore?" to "what does this specific patient need?" — shaped by demand gradient, preferred chewing side asymmetry, arch extent, and prosthetic modality.

**임상 요점**: "보철해야 하는가?"가 아니라 "이 환자에게 무엇이 필요한가?" — 수요 기울기·선호 저작측·치열 범위·보철 방식에 따라 개인화 결정.

## Thesis

The SDA concept posits that a functional dentition can end bilaterally at the first molar (or even premolar) without prosthetic extension, provided that bilateral canine and premolar support is intact. First articulated by Witter and colleagues and carried forward by the Radboud/Nijmegen and later European multicentre research programmes, the SDA concept has now accumulated 15-year randomised trial evidence demonstrating non-inferiority to prosthetically restored arches across survival, OHRQoL, and periodontal outcomes. A 2018 systematic review (McLister) confirms: "There is insufficient evidence to recommend one tooth replacement strategy over another." This is not a conclusion of uncertainty — it is a statement that SDA maintenance is a clinically valid, evidence-grounded option. The burden of proof has shifted: the question is no longer whether SDA is acceptable but which patients benefit from extending it.

## Clinical Decision Framework

### Axis 1 — Does this patient qualify for SDA?

**Witter criteria (Nassani 2013)**:

```
Bilateral canine + premolar support preserved?
  └── YES: SDA is evidence-based — no default extension
  └── NO (premolars missing):
        → Assess arch extent:
              Slight SDA (1st molar missing)  → No extension indicated
              Moderate SDA (M1+M2 missing)   → Extension exceptional only
              Extreme SDA (≤2 occluding pairs) → Extend to moderate level (not full arch)
```

**Age context**: SDA is particularly well-supported in patients ≥35 years with stable periodontium (Walter 2021 eligibility). In older adults (>65), the functionally-oriented approach actively reduces maintenance burden (McKenna 2020).

---

### Axis 2 — Does the patient actually want restoration?

**Demand gradient (Nassani 2013)**:

| Missing teeth | % who seek restoration |
|---|---|
| Second molar only | ~3% |
| First + second molar | ~58% |
| Premolar(s) included | ~93% |

**Clinical implication**: For second-molar-only defects, the default should be observation and counselling, not treatment. Most of the perceived need is clinician-driven, not patient-driven.

---

### Axis 3 — Is masticatory function a clinical concern?

**Quantified deficit (Liang 2015 SR)**:
- SDA/ESDA patients: 28–39% lower masticatory performance vs complete dentition
- RPD compensation: ~50% recovery (dose-effect — more artificial teeth → better)
- Residual deficit persists even with RPD (mixing ability 28–83% lower on RPD side)

**Side-specificity (Koyama 2026 RCT)**:
- Preferred chewing side (PCS) molar loss → significant ME and SMA decrease (p<0.05)
- Non-PCS molar loss → no significant masticatory effect
- Protective factor: MOA located at 2nd premolar (smaller function loss)

**Decision rule**: Prosthetic intervention for masticatory function is most defensible when molar loss occurs on the preferred chewing side. Non-PCS molar loss may not require intervention on functional grounds alone.

---

### Axis 4 — What prosthetic modality if treatment is chosen?

**OHRQoL hierarchy (Nakai 2022 RCT)**:
```
Non-metal clasp denture (NMCD) > SDA > Conventional RPD
```
Conventional RPD ranked *lowest* in within-patient OHRQoL comparison — the "standard" option is not the preferred option.

**Implant option (Gao 2025)**:
- If implants are chosen: M1-only restoration (SDA concept) = equivalent survival (HR 1.66, p=0.567)
- M1-only advantage: 1.7x lower cost, 0% vs 16.2% peri-implantitis at 3yr, protective against MBL
- **M2 implant adds risk without adding benefit** for most patients

**Summary table**:

| Modality | OHRQoL | Survival | Cost | Periodontal risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SDA (no prosthesis) | ≥ RPD (multiple RCTs) | 14.2 yr (15yr RCT) | Lowest | Best (Kalsi 2021) |
| Conventional RPD | Worst in 3-way comparison | 14.3 yr | Low | OR 1.99 abutment (Drummond 2024) |
| NMCD / adhesive FDP | Best / ≥ RPD | — | Moderate | — |
| M1-only implant | NS vs M1+M2 (PROMs) | HR 1.66 NS | 1.7x < M1+M2 | 0% PI (Gao 2025) |
| M1+M2 implant | NS vs M1-only | — | Highest | 16.2% PI at 3yr |

## Key Controversies and Evidence Gaps

1. **Long-term masticatory adaptation**: All masticatory function studies are short-term; whether patients adapt functionally to SDA over years is unknown. Walter 2021 shows no survival difference at 15yr but functional outcomes were not the primary endpoint.

2. **PCS molar loss threshold in real patients**: Koyama 2026 used occlusal splints on dentate volunteers — real SDA patients may have adapted differently. The clinical threshold for PCS-driven intervention needs prospective validation.

3. **M1-only implant — longer follow-up**: Gao 2025 is retrospective with 3-year PI data. The peri-implantitis advantage may shrink with longer follow-up as cumulative exposure increases. A prospective RCT with ≥5yr is needed.

4. **ESDA boundary**: The line between SDA (3–5 pairs) and ESDA (0–2 pairs) drives different treatment thresholds, but most RCT evidence comes from moderate SDA. Extreme SDA data are sparse.

5. **Korean population data**: All major trials are European (German multicentre, UK/Ireland McKenna, Dutch/Danish cohorts) and Japanese (Nakai, Koyama). Korean-specific demand gradient, dietary patterns (harder food texture), and cost/access data are unavailable in this corpus.

6. **Implant vs RPD long-term**: McLister 2018 found no poolable evidence. The Gao 2025 retrospective adds implant-specific data, but an RCT comparing RPD vs M1-only implant in SDA patients at ≥5yr is the most direct remaining gap.
