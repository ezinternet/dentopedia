## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **근단 개통성 (Apical Patency, AP)**: 근관 형성 중 #10 K-file을 근단 수축(apical constriction) 부위에 유지해 근단 폐쇄를 방지하는 임상 술식.
> - **핵심 구분 — Ishizaki 2026**: "해부학적 개통성(Anatomical Patency)"(원래 형태 존중) vs "시술적 개통성(Operative Patency)"(근단 너머 강압적 삽입) — 전자를 우선할 것.
> - **치료 결과에 미치는 효과(Kuzhanchinathan 2024 SR, 5편 4370근관)**: AP 유지 시 장기 치유율 **2배** 증가; 5편 중 3편에서 유의한 양의 효과 확인. 근거 수준은 제한적(RCT 1편만).
> - **술후 통증과의 관계**: "개통성이 통증을 악화시킨다"는 전통적 우려는 최신 메타분석에서 지지되지 않음 — 오히려 통증 완화 가능성이 시사되나 개별 연구 간 상충.
> - **CBCT의 역할**: 술전 CBCT가 MB2 근관 등 복잡 해부학 확인에 필수 → AP 달성 가능성 예측.
> - **기구 선택**: NiTi + 왕복형 모터 키네마틱스가 활주로 형성 예측가능성 향상.
> - **임상 bottom line**: AP는 장기 치유율 개선과 연관되므로 유지가 권장되나, 달성 실패가 항상 나쁜 결과를 의미하지는 않음 — 근거 기반의 노력 vs 강압 사이에서 "해부학적 개통성"을 목표로.

## Three-line Summary

Two dedicated studies synthesize the clinical evidence for apical patency (AP): Kuzhanchinathan 2024 (SR, 5 clinical studies, 4370 roots; PROSPERO CRD42022374966) found that maintaining AP was associated with a 2-fold increase in healing at long-term follow-up, and Ishizaki 2026 (comprehensive review) introduced the Anatomical vs Operative Patency conceptual distinction while synthesizing patency's relationship to postoperative pain and procedural anatomy.

Recent meta-analyses cited by Ishizaki 2026 suggest AP may *alleviate* rather than exacerbate postoperative pain — contrary to decades of clinical anxiety — but the evidence across individual studies remains conflicting; the evidence base for AP and healing is thin (only 1 RCT among 5 studies) and heterogeneous, precluding meta-analysis.

Clinical guidance: prioritize Anatomical Patency (respecting original canal morphology rather than forcing instruments beyond the apex); use preoperative CBCT to identify complex anatomy; and NiTi files with reciprocating kinematics for predictable glide path establishment — failure to achieve patency does not mandate a negative outcome, but maintaining it is associated with improved long-term healing.

## 세줄요약

두 전용 연구가 근단 개통성 (Apical Patency, AP) 임상 근거를 종합한다: Kuzhanchinathan 2024 SR(5편 임상연구, 4370근관; PROSPERO CRD42022374966)에서 AP 유지가 장기 치유율 **2배** 증가와 연관됐고, Ishizaki 2026 종합 리뷰는 "해부학적 개통성 vs 시술적 개통성" 개념 구분을 제시하며 AP와 술후 통증·해부학과의 관계를 종합했다.

Ishizaki 2026이 인용한 최신 메타분석들은 AP가 술후 통증을 악화보다 오히려 완화할 수 있다고 시사하지만 개별 연구 간 상충이 지속되며, 치유 근거 기반도 취약하다(RCT 1편·전향 임상연구 4편, 이질적 설계 → 메타분석 불가).

임상 지침: 원래 근관 형태를 존중하는 "해부학적 개통성"을 목표로, 술전 CBCT로 복잡 해부학(예: MB2 근관)을 확인하고, NiTi + 왕복형 키네마틱스로 활주로 형성; 개통성 미달이 항상 나쁜 결과를 의미하지 않으나 유지 시 장기 치유 개선과 연관됨.

## Thesis

**AP is clinically worthwhile to pursue, but not by force.** The distinction Ishizaki 2026 introduces — Anatomical Patency (maintaining patency within the canal's natural morphology) vs Operative Patency (forcing instruments beyond the apex) — is clinically important. The limited but consistent evidence (Kuzhanchinathan 2024: 3/5 studies positive, 2-fold healing improvement at long follow-up) supports making a genuine effort to maintain AP. However, the evidence does NOT support aggressive instrumentation beyond the apical constriction to achieve patency at any cost — that is the "Operative" form, which risks iatrogenic foramen transportation and periapical inflammation without proven additional benefit.

The classical fear that AP worsens postoperative pain has become less defensible: the most recent meta-analytic evidence Ishizaki 2026 synthesizes points in the opposite direction — pain is not worsened, and may be slightly reduced, by maintaining patency. The mechanism is plausible: patency prevents packed debris accumulation at the apical foramen, reducing the inflammatory burden. However, the evidence here is not settled and practitioners should not overinterpret it as "AP actively reduces pain."

## Conceptual Framework

### Anatomical Patency vs Operative Patency (Ishizaki 2026)

| Concept | Definition | Evidence status | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anatomical Patency** | Maintaining a small file (#10 K-file) at or near the apical constriction, respecting original canal morphology | Supported by healing evidence | **Prioritize** |
| **Operative Patency** | Forceful negotiation of instruments *beyond* the apical foramen | No evidence of benefit; risk of foramen transportation | **Discourage** |

### Patency and Healing (Kuzhanchinathan 2024)

| Study metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Total roots analyzed | 4370 (5 studies) |
| Studies showing positive influence of AP | 3 of 5 (60%) |
| Estimated healing improvement | ~2-fold at long-term follow-up |
| Design quality | 1 RCT (Arslan et al.), 4 prospective clinical; all low RoB |
| Follow-up range | 1–4 years |
| Meta-analysis possible? | No — heterogeneous designs |

### Patency and Postoperative Pain

Traditionally, AP was associated with increased pain because of the perceived risk of apical extrusion. Ishizaki 2026 reviews recent meta-analytic data suggesting the opposite:
- AP may **alleviate** postoperative pain (not worsen it)
- Mechanism hypothesis: apical patency prevents debris accumulation at the foramen → reduces inflammatory load
- **Caveat**: individual study results remain conflicting; this should not be interpreted as strong evidence for AP as a pain-reduction strategy

### Anatomic Enablers of Patency

- **Preoperative CBCT**: essential for identifying anatomy that would impede patency (MB2 canals, severe curvatures, C-shaped canals, calcifications)
- **NiTi files with reciprocating kinematics**: enhance predictability of glide path establishment before patency confirmation
- **Anatomical complexity as a patency barrier**: when anatomy prevents AP despite correct technique, failure to achieve patency is not a clinical failure per se — forcing it becomes Operative Patency and is contraindicated

## Clinical Decision Points

**When and how to pursue AP:**

1. **Confirm working length (WL) first** — AP is distinct from WL determination but complementary: WL sets the stop point; AP is the active maintenance of canal access to that point throughout instrumentation.
2. **Establish a glide path before AP confirmation** — rotary/reciprocating systems require a pre-established glide path; AP #10 K-file should move freely after glide path.
3. **Anatomical Patency is the goal** — verify #10 K-file passes to (not through) the apical constriction with minimal resistance. If resistance is met:
   - Check for ledges, calcifications, canal curvature
   - Use CBCT if complex anatomy is suspected
   - Do NOT force past resistance → shifts from Anatomical to Operative Patency
4. **Document AP attempts** — if AP cannot be achieved, note it; outcome monitoring becomes more important in these cases.
5. **Pain counseling** — AP is not a pain-increasing procedure based on current evidence; inform patients accordingly if concern arises.

## Gaps & Future Research

- **Only 1 RCT in the healing evidence base** (Arslan et al.) — powered prospective RCTs with standardized AP protocols are the priority
- **AP and pain: the evidence reversal needs replication** — most meta-analyses cited by Ishizaki 2026 are not specifically designed as AP-pain RCTs; the finding should be confirmed in dedicated trials
- **Anatomical vs Operative Patency distinction is conceptual, not validated** — prospective studies comparing outcomes of morphology-respecting vs aggressive patency are needed
- **Long-term follow-up** — 1–4 year data is the current ceiling; 5–10 year data would clarify whether the 2-fold healing advantage persists or converges
