> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 질문**: 임플란트 골절제(osteotomy) 드릴링 발열을 실제로 좌우하는 변수는 무엇이며, 무엇을 통제하면 가장 효율적이고, 그 통제가 골유착(osseointegration)이라는 생물학적 결과를 실제로 바꾸는가.
> - **임계값 앵커**: 골괴사(thermal osteonecrosis) 역치는 47°C/1분 — 정형외과 교차검증에서도 ~50°C로 재확인됨(Timon). 이 선을 넘기지 않는 것이 모든 프로토콜의 목표.
> - **1순위 지렛대 = 관주 온도(irrigation temperature)**: 여러 요인을 한 연구에서 동시에 비교했을 때 **관주액 온도만 유의**했고 실온·드릴직경·깊이는 모두 무의미(Kapse). 냉각 saline(5–10°C)이 상온보다 일관되게 우수(Sener, Raj, Markovic). 발열은 얕은 피질골에서 가장 크므로 관주는 그곳에 집중(Sener: 3mm 50.9°C vs 12mm 38.1°C, 무관주).
> - **관주 유량은 임계점(plateau)이 있다**: 전산유체(CFD)상 ~60 mL/min 이후로는 추가 이득 없음(Hu). 한 in vitro는 유량 증가보다 **온도**가 지배적이라고 봄(Aquilanti) — 즉 "더 많이"보다 "더 차갑게"가 핵심.
> - **1순위 확증 — 온도계가 아니라 조직에서도 (Kosior 2025 조직학)**: Tier 1 근거가 전부 *온도* 계측이라 조직 손상까지 한 걸음 남아 있었는데, 돼지 늑골 54개 골삭제(드릴 3종 × 속도 3 × 냉각 3, 형광 조직학 1–16점)에서 **4°C saline이 3개 시스템 전부에서 최고 골상 품질**(최고 15.3/16), **무냉각은 전부에서 최저이며 속도가 오를수록 악화**(1500 rpm 무냉각 5.5/16). 단 최적 속도는 시스템별로 달랐다(Hiossen 1200–1500, Paltop·AnyRidge 800 rpm) → **냉각은 시스템을 건너 이전되지만 rpm 권고는 안 된다.**
> - **2순위 = 드릴 예리함/마모(drill wear)**: 마모는 재질·설계와 무관한 독립적 발열 인자 — TiN 코팅 소실과 함께 온도 progressive 상승(Sorgato), 50회 사용에 걸쳐 모든 군에서 상승(Bernabeu-Mira), 마모된 드릴은 어떤 설계든 역치 초과(Chauhan). **제조사 교체주기(대략 25–30회) 준수**가 재질 선택보다 우선. 설계는 twisted < straight로 발열 낮음(Bernabeu-Mira), 제조사별 미세형상도 측정상 유의하게 다름(Marenzi).
> - **"마모 > 재질"은 이 페이지의 추론이 아니라 전용 SR의 결론(Chakraborty 2024)**: 재질을 독립 인자로 시험한 SR(11편)이 세라믹 vs 금속을 **결론 불가**로 판정 — 방법론 이질성·기하학적 교락(재질만 바꾸고 기하를 고정한 연구가 드묾)·후원 편향 때문. **연구 전반에서 일관된 신호는 재질이 아니라 마모가 온도를 예측한다는 것 하나**뿐이며, 저자들 스스로 "재질 선택이 아니라 교체 프로토콜로 관심을 돌리라"고 권고. 예외 1건: 지르코니아 vs 스테인리스 SR+MA(Bento 2023, 10편)에서 **지르코니아가 유의하게 저발열** — 다만 전부 in vitro이고 병목은 발열이 아니라 **파괴인성·마모 거동 미지**다. 마모가 2순위 인자인데 "차갑게 깎지만 빨리 무뎌지는" 재질이면 정작 중요한 축에서 진다.
> - **상황 증폭인자 — 가이드수술(guided surgery)**: 금속 슬리브가 외부 관주를 차단해 **피질골 입구부에서만** 발열↑(Markovic: 입구 p<0.001, 중간·바닥 NS; 가이드온도↔골온도 rho=0.868). SR도 가이드>비가이드 발열 확인, 1500–2000 rpm에서 역치 돌파 사례(Saxena) → full-guided라면 냉각(~10°C) saline + 800–1200 rpm + peck drilling.
> - **통념 반박 — 순차 드릴링(sequential drilling)은 자동 안전장치가 아니다**: 무관주 조건에서 pilot drill이 >100°C, 열손상대가 측방 10mm까지, 후속 확대드릴은 열을 제거가 아니라 추가(Rugova). 단 이는 관주 없는 합성골 극단 조건.
> - **통념 반박 2 — peck(간헐) 드릴링은 일반 지렛대가 아니라 조건부다 (본 페이지 종전 권고 정정)**: 직접 시험한 두 편이 나란히 반대한다. Gehrke 2020(**in vivo** 토끼 n=48, 기하 × 기법)에서 간헐 이동은 **원통형 드릴에서만** ΔT를 2.6°C 낮췄고(6.91→4.30°C, p=0.001, −37%) **원추형에선 무효**(p=0.977, Δ−0.008°C) — 원추형은 기법과 무관하게 모든 지표에서 우수. Di Fiore 2018(in vitro 소 늑골 n=80, 기법 × 관주온도 2×2)에서는 **관주 온도를 맞추면 연속 vs 간헐 차이 없음**(p=0.45)이고 냉각 saline(6.0°C)만 결정적(p=0.0001). → **peck은 이미 열을 못 빼는 기하를 보상하는 수단이지 독립 인자가 아니다.** 원추형이거나 관주가 제대로 차가우면 peck이 할 일이 남지 않는다 — Tier 1 우위 모델이 예측하는 그대로.
> - **술식을 바꿔도 프로토콜에서 면제되지 않는다 (교차검증)**: ① 피에조 — 정전 리뷰(Stübinger 2015)가 "열손상 회피"를 장점으로 열거하나 계측이 반박: Aquilanti에서 피에조 ΔT 53–65°C vs 회전식 <2°C, Jain 2024 SR(9편)은 이를 **통증 vs 발열 trade-off**로 정식화(술후 통증은 유의 감소, 골내 온도는 오히려 상승) → 피에조는 해부학적 섬세함(슈나이더막·하치조신경)의 적응증이지 열 안전 업그레이드가 아니다. ② 골밀도화 — **절삭하지 않는 기구인데도 Tier 2를 그대로 따른다**: Soldatos 2024(사람 사체 경골 360 골삭제)에서 CCW 3.0/4.0 버를 **23회 이상 재사용** + 800/1200 rpm이면 47°C 초과(chatter 동반), **1000 rpm이 양 모드 최저**. 즉 누적 사용이 독립 발열 인자이고 해법은 선택이 아니라 교체 — 다른 기구 계열에서의 Tier 2 재현이다. OD 자체는 뜨겁지 않다(Tao 2025 OD ≤ conventional; de Lima 2026 최고 ~28–31°C·군간 NS p=0.087) — **무딘 버 + 잘못된 rpm이 뜨겁다.** ③ 5개 술식 조직학 SR(Kosior 2025, 338→29편): 회전식 드릴링이 미세균열·골세포 손상·열손상·%BIC 전 축에서 최하위이나 **저자 결론은 정반대 — 이 페널티는 대부분 프로토콜 매개이며 속도·냉각·하중을 최적화하면 드릴링이 대안들과 동등**해진다. → **프로토콜이 기구를 지배한다.**
> - **아마 신경 안 써도 되는 것들**: 수술실 실온 25 vs 30°C(Kapse NS), 드릴 직경·깊이(테스트 범위 내 Kapse NS), feed rate/이송속도(Hu NS), **드릴 재질(Chakraborty 결론 불가 — 마모가 지배)** — 발열 관리의 우선순위에서 하위.
> - **속도·압력은 단조롭지 않다**: 고속이라 무조건 더 뜨거운 게 아니라 압력·냉각과 상호작용 — 2500 rpm + 높은 압력 + 냉각 saline이 오히려 최저 발열(Raj). 발열은 다인자 상호작용(Chauhan)이라 단일변수 통제로는 불충분.
> - **생물학적 폐곡선(does it matter?) = YES**: in vivo 미니피그에서 최고온도 ~11°C 감소(46.5→35.4°C)가 **2주 BIC를 31.3→44.9%(+13.6%p, p<0.0001)** 로 끌어올림(Heuzeroth). 발열 통제는 단순 안전마진이 아니라 골유착 정량 결정인자.
> - **측정·해석 주의**: thermocouple·적외선(IR)·광섬유 측정법은 상호 호환 안 됨 → 문헌 이질성의 숨은 원천(Jung). 다수 in vitro가 관주 없이 측정(Rugova·Sorgato·Bernabeu) → 절대온도는 임상보다 과대. 상대비교로만 읽을 것.
> - **임상 결론(우선순위)**: ① 냉각 saline을 기본으로 얕은 피질골에 집중, ② 드릴 교체주기 엄수(**OD 버는 ~23회·1000 rpm** — Soldatos), ③ 가이드·치밀골·깊은 구치부에서는 냉각+저rpm 강화, ④ **peck은 원통형 드릴일 때만**(원추형·냉각 충분 시 무효), ⑤ 실온·직경·feed rate·**재질** 최적화는 후순위, ⑥ 기구를 바꾸기 전에 프로토콜을 고칠 것(피에조는 통증↓/발열↑ trade-off). 이 순서를 지키면 발열이 골유착 결과로 이어진다.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 28 papers on implant osteotomy drilling heat, reorganized from "list of factors" into an evidence-weighted ranking of *what to actually control*: the 47°C/1-min necrosis threshold (Timon 2019 cross-specialty; ~50°C orthopedic), a multifactorial framing (Chauhan 2018 SR 34 studies; Jung 2021 internal-vs-external taxonomy), and the biological endpoint (Heuzeroth 2021 in vivo minipig, n=36; Kosior 2025 histologic bone-bed scoring).

Across studies that pitted determinants against each other, **irrigation temperature is the single most reproducible lever** — the only significant factor when four were tested head-to-head (Kapse 2022), with chilled 5–10°C saline consistently outperforming room temperature (Sener 2009, Raj 2021, Markovic 2016, Woods 2022 in vivo) and irrigation *volume* plateauing near 60 mL/min (Hu 2019 CFD; Aquilanti 2023 found temperature dominant over volume); **drill wear is the second-tier independent driver** (Sorgato 2025, Bernabeu-Mira 2020, Marenzi 2018 — replace on schedule, design twisted<straight), while **guided-surgery sleeves add cortical-entrance heat by blocking irrigation** (Markovic entrance-only rho=0.868; Saxena 2024 SR), **sequential drilling is not automatically protective** (Rugova 2024, no-irrigation extreme), and operatory room temperature, drill diameter, drilling depth, feed rate, and drill *material* are low-priority (Kapse NS; Hu feed-rate NS; Chakraborty 2024 SR inconclusive on material with wear the only consistent signal — zirconia the lone pooled exception, Bento 2023, still in vitro).

Three findings added 2026-07-17 sharpen rather than confirm the ranking: **peck drilling is conditional, not general** — it helps cylindrical drills (−37%, Gehrke 2020 in vivo) but does nothing for conical ones (p=0.977) and nothing at matched irrigation temperature (Di Fiore 2018, p=0.45), so this page's own earlier "peck as escalation" guidance is corrected; **switching instrument does not exempt you from the protocol** — piezo trades lower postoperative pain for *higher* intraosseous heat (Jain 2024 SR; contradicting Stübinger 2015's thermal-safety claim), while osseodensification reproduces Tier 2 in a non-cutting instrument (Soldatos 2024: Densah burs past ~23 uses at 800/1200 rpm breach 47°C; 1000 rpm minimizes ΔT) even though OD itself runs cool (Tao 2025; de Lima 2026 ~28–31°C, p=0.087 NS); and across all five preparation families, rotary drilling's histologic penalty is **protocol-mediated — optimized speed/cooling/load lets drilling match laser/piezo/osteotome/OD** (Kosior 2025 SR, 29 studies), which is this overview's thesis argued from outside it. The loop closes biologically twice over: an ~11°C peak-temperature reduction raised 2-week BIC from 31.3% to 44.9% (Heuzeroth), and 4°C irrigation produced the best histologic bone-bed quality in every drill system tested while no cooling produced the worst (Kosior 2025, 15.3 vs 5.5 / 16) — heat control is a quantitative osseointegration determinant, not merely a safety margin.

Most in-vitro absolute temperatures overstate clinical risk because they omit irrigation and vital-bone perfusion, and thermocouple/IR/fiber-optic methods are non-interchangeable (Jung) — so this overview reads the literature for *relative* effect direction and determinant ranking, not for transferable absolute numbers.

## 세줄요약

임플란트 골절제 드릴링 발열 28편을 "요인 나열"이 아니라 **무엇을 실제로 통제할지의 근거가중 순위**로 재구성: 47°C/1분 괴사 역치(Timon 2019 교차검증; 정형외과 ~50°C), 다인자 프레이밍(Chauhan 2018 SR 34편; Jung 2021 내부·외부 인자 분류), 생물학적 endpoint(Heuzeroth 2021 in vivo 미니피그 n=36; Kosior 2025 조직학적 골상 점수).

여러 결정인자를 맞대결시킨 연구에서 **관주 온도가 가장 재현성 높은 지렛대** — 4개 요인 동시비교에서 유일하게 유의(Kapse 2022), 냉각 5–10°C saline이 상온보다 일관되게 우수(Sener 2009, Raj 2021, Markovic 2016, Woods 2022 in vivo), 관주 유량은 ~60 mL/min에서 임계점(Hu 2019 CFD; Aquilanti 2023은 유량보다 온도 지배); **드릴 마모가 2순위 독립 인자**(Sorgato 2025, Bernabeu-Mira 2020, Marenzi 2018 — 교체주기 준수, 설계 twisted<straight), **가이드 슬리브는 외부관주 차단으로 피질골 입구 발열 증가**(Markovic 입구부 rho=0.868; Saxena 2024 SR), **순차드릴링은 자동 안전장치 아님**(Rugova 2024, 무관주 극단), 실온·드릴직경·깊이·feed rate·**재질**은 하위 우선순위(Kapse NS; Hu feed-rate NS; Chakraborty 2024 SR 재질 결론불가·마모만 일관 — 지르코니아만 pooled 예외 Bento 2023, 여전히 in vitro).

2026-07-17 추가 3건은 순위를 확인하기보다 **날카롭게** 만든다: **peck 드릴링은 조건부** — 원통형에만 유효(−37%, Gehrke 2020 in vivo)하고 원추형엔 무효(p=0.977)이며 관주 온도를 맞추면 아예 차이 없음(Di Fiore 2018, p=0.45) → 본 페이지의 종전 "peck 강화" 권고를 정정; **기구를 바꿔도 프로토콜에서 면제되지 않음** — 피에조는 통증↓/골내 발열↑ trade-off(Jain 2024 SR, Stübinger 2015의 열안전 주장 반박), 골밀도화는 **절삭하지 않는 기구인데도 Tier 2를 재현**(Soldatos 2024: Densah 버 23회 초과 + 800/1200 rpm이면 47°C 돌파, 1000 rpm 최저)하되 OD 자체는 저온(Tao 2025; de Lima 2026 ~28–31°C, p=0.087 NS); 5개 술식 조직학 SR(Kosior 2025, 29편)에서 회전식의 조직학적 페널티는 **프로토콜 매개 — 속도·냉각·하중 최적화 시 드릴링이 레이저·피에조·osteotome·OD와 동등**. 생물학적 폐곡선은 이중으로 닫힌다: 최고온도 ~11°C 감소가 2주 BIC를 31.3→44.9%로 상승(Heuzeroth), 4°C 관주가 시험한 모든 드릴 시스템에서 최고 조직학적 골상 품질·무냉각이 최저(Kosior 15.3 vs 5.5/16) — 발열 통제는 단순 안전마진이 아닌 골유착 정량 결정인자.

다수 in vitro 절대온도는 관주·활성골 혈류를 뺐기에 임상 위험을 과대평가하며 thermocouple/IR/광섬유 측정법은 상호 호환 안 됨(Jung) — 본 overview는 절대수치가 아니라 **상대적 효과 방향과 결정인자 순위**로 문헌을 읽는다.

## Thesis

**Clinical question:** During implant osteotomy, which of the many cited heat-generation factors actually move the needle, in what priority order, and does controlling them change the biological outcome rather than merely staying under a threshold?

The drilling-heat literature is usually presented as a flat catalogue — drill geometry, material, wear, speed, load, depth, diameter, irrigation type/volume/temperature, bone density, guided vs freehand, sequential vs single-drill, room temperature. Chauhan's 34-study SR ([[implants/chauhan-2018-biomechanical-factors-heat-generation-osteotomy]]) and Jung's internal-vs-external taxonomy ([[implants/jung-2021-heat-development-medical-drilling-influencing]]) both conclude "many interacting factors, none dominant" — true, but not actionable at chairside. This overview re-reads the same evidence as a **ranked decision list**, anchored to the 47°C/1-minute necrosis threshold that Timon shows holds across specialties ([[implants/timon-2019-thermal-osteonecrosis-bone-drilling-orthopedic]], orthopedic ~50°C).

**Tier 1 — irrigation temperature is the dominant, most reproducible lever.** The strongest single piece of ranking evidence is Kapse ([[implants/kapse-2022-thermal-effect-operatory-room-temperature-surgical]]): when operatory room temperature, drill diameter, drilling depth, and irrigant temperature were tested in the *same* crossed design, **only irrigant temperature was significant** (5°C saline ~5°C cooler than 25°C, p=0.003). This is reinforced by Sener's depth-stratified data ([[implants/sener-2009-effects-irrigation-temperature-heat-control]]) — without irrigation, superficial bone reaches 50.9°C at 3 mm vs 38.1°C at 12 mm, so most heat is generated cortically and that is where cooling matters most; either 25°C or 10°C irrigation drops shallow readings below body temperature, with colder consistently better. Raj's factorial ([[implants/raj-2021-analysis-factors-determining-thermal-changes]]) and Markovic ([[implants/markovic-2016-effect-surgical-drill-guide-irrigans]]) independently confirm chilled saline as the technique-independent mitigator, and Woods provides the *in vivo* histologic confirmation ([[implants/woods-2022-does-irrigating-while-drilling-decrease]]): saline irrigation reduced both temperature rise and acute osteonecrosis in a canine model. On *volume*, Hu's CFD model ([[implants/hu-2019-evaluation-fluid-distribution-implant-site]]) shows fluid delivery increases up to ~60 mL/min then plateaus, while Aquilanti ([[implants/aquilanti-2023-heat-generation-initial-osteotomy]]) found irrigant *temperature* significant but volume increases not — converging on "colder beats copious once a working flow is reached."

Every Tier-1 study above measures **temperature**, which leaves the ranking one inferential step short of tissue damage. Kosior closes that step ([[implants/kosior-2025-comparative-analysis-histological-characteristics]], 54 implant-bed preparations in swine ribs, 3 drill systems × 3 speeds × 3 cooling conditions, fluorescence histology scored 1–16): **4°C saline produced the highest bone-bed quality in every one of the three systems** (best combinations 15.3, 15.2, 13.7 / 16), while **no cooling produced the worst score in every system and degraded further as speed rose** (worst: 1500 rpm without cooling = 5.5 / 16). So irrigation temperature is not merely the most reproducible lever on the thermometer — it is the dominant variable on a *histologic* readout of the bone bed, which is the thing the thermometer was standing in for. One nuance qualifies the "just go colder" reading: each drill system had its own optimal speed (Hiossen best at 1200–1500 rpm; Paltop and AnyRidge at 800 rpm), so **speed is system-specific in a way irrigation temperature is not** — cooling transfers across systems, an rpm recommendation does not. Swine rib, in vitro, no operator-technique control, so the specific speed/system pairings need human validation before being applied as settings.

**Tier 2 — drill wear (and, secondarily, design) is an independent driver you control by replacement, not selection.** Sorgato isolates wear as the variable ([[implants/sorgato-2025-drill-bit-wear-temperature]]): peak intraosseous temperature rises progressively as the TiN coating is lost, and heat rise begins *before* the manufacturer's ~25–30-use limit. Bernabeu-Mira ([[implants/bernabeu-mira-2020-bone-heating-drilling-implant]]) shows temperature climbing across all 50 uses regardless of group, with drill *design* (twisted < straight) outweighing material (stainless ≈ DLC), and Marenzi ([[implants/marenzi-2018-micromorphological-structure-implant-drills]]) demonstrates that manufacturer micromorphology is statistically separable — changing drill brand should be assumed to change heat behavior. Chauhan's blunt summary applies: worn drills of any design exceed the necrosis threshold, so drill-replacement discipline outranks material choice.

That "wear over material" ordering is not an inference this page is making on its own — it is the explicit conclusion of the one SR that set out to test material as an independent factor. Chakraborty ([[implants/chakraborty-2024-implant-drill-materials-heat-osteotomy]], SR, 11 in vitro studies) found the ceramic-vs-metal question **inconclusive** — defeated by heterogeneous methodology, geometric confounding (material is rarely varied with geometry held constant), and sponsorship bias — and reported that **the only signal consistent across the included studies was that drill wear, not material, predicts temperature rise**, with worn drills of any material capable of breaching 47°C/1 min. Its own recommendation is to redirect clinical attention from material selection to replacement protocols, which is Tier 2 stated independently.

One material pair is the exception, and it is worth keeping separate rather than averaging away. Bento ([[implants/bento-2023-steel-versus-zirconia-drills-heat]], SR+MA, 10 in vitro studies, inverse-variance pooling) found **zirconia drills produced significantly lower bone-temperature variation than stainless steel**. This *qualifies* rather than contradicts Chakraborty — "material is inconclusive in general" and "this specific pair separates when pooled" are compatible claims, and the two reviews should be read as a pair. But the pooled signal is entirely in vitro, and the translation blocker is not thermal: **zirconia's fracture toughness and wear behaviour are the unaddressed questions**, and since wear is the Tier-2 driver, a material that cuts cooler while dulling faster (or fracturing) could lose on the very axis that matters most. Material selection therefore stays below replacement discipline in the ranking — with zirconia flagged as the one place a future clinical trial could change that.

**Tier 3 — situational amplifiers.** Guided surgery: Markovic localizes the guide penalty to the *cortical entrance only* (entrance p<0.001; middle/bottom NS), driven by sleeve obstruction of irrigation plus conductive heating from the metal sleeve itself (guide-temp ↔ bone-temp Spearman rho=0.868); Saxena's SR ([[implants/saxena-2024-guided-implant-drilling-bone-temperature]]) confirms guided > non-guided across 11 studies with threshold breaches at 1500–2000 rpm — hence chilled ~10°C saline, 800–1200 rpm, and peck drilling for full-guided cases. Sequential drilling: Rugova ([[implants/rugova-2024-thermal-evaluation-bone-drilling-sequential]]) overturns the assumption that graduated drills protect bone — the pilot drill peaked >100°C and each enlarging drill *added* heat — but this is the extreme no-irrigation synthetic-bone condition, so the real message is that irrigation failure (e.g. sleeve blockage) plus worn drills removes the sequential-drilling safety margin. Speed/pressure: Raj shows these are non-monotonic — the lowest heat occurred at the *highest* speed (2500 rpm) paired with higher pressure and chilled saline, so the three interact rather than acting independently.

**Peck drilling is conditional, not a general escalation — a correction to this page's own earlier guidance.** Intermittent ("peck") drilling is recommended above and in most protocol lists as if it were an independent thermal lever. Two studies that tested it directly say otherwise, and they disagree with each other in an informative way. Gehrke ([[implants/gehrke-2020-technique-drill-design-osteotomy]], **in vivo** rabbit, n=48, cylindrical vs conical drill × continuous vs intermittent) found intermittent movement reduced temperature by 2.6°C **for cylindrical drills only** (6.91 → 4.30°C, p=0.001, −37%) and did **nothing for conical drills** (p=0.977, Δ −0.008°C) — with conical drills outperforming cylindrical on every metric regardless of technique. Di Fiore ([[implants/di-fiore-2018-continuous-intermittent-drilling-temperature]], in vitro bovine rib, n=80, 2×2 crossing technique with irrigation temperature) found **no significant continuous-vs-intermittent difference at matched irrigation temperature** (p=0.45), while chilled 6.0°C saline was decisive regardless of technique (p=0.0001) and all groups stayed under 47°C.

Read together: **peck drilling is a compensator for drill geometry that is already shedding heat poorly, not a lever in its own right.** Where the geometry is conical, or where the irrigant is properly chilled, the technique has nothing left to contribute — which is exactly what a Tier-1-dominant model predicts. Di Fiore's design is the cleaner test of the ranking (it crosses technique *against* irrigation temperature and finds irrigation wins); Gehrke's is the more clinically weighty design (in vivo, with 30-day healing) and finds the geometry interaction. Neither supports "always peck." The honest guidance: peck when using cylindrical drills, and treat it as a fallback when irrigation is compromised (Rugova's scenario) — not as a routine addition on top of adequate cooling.

**Tier 4 — probably not worth optimizing.** Operatory room temperature (25 vs 30°C, Kapse NS), drill diameter and drilling depth within tested ranges (Kapse NS), and feed rate (Hu NS) are low-yield relative to irrigation and wear.

**Cross-technique check — do the same rules govern piezo and osseodensification, or does switching technique buy you out of them?** This matters because the commonest response to a thermal problem is to change instrument rather than protocol. The held evidence says the switch does not exempt you, and in one direction it costs you.

*Piezosurgery inverts its own marketing.* The canonical review ([[implants/stubinger-2015-piezosurgery-implant-dentistry]]) lists avoidance of thermal damage among piezo's advantages alongside selective mineralized-tissue cutting and soft-tissue/membrane/nerve preservation. That specific claim does not survive the measurements: Aquilanti recorded piezo ΔT of **53–65°C against rotary's <2°C** in the same experiment, and Jain ([[implants/jain-2024-heat-generation-pain-piezosurgery-drilling]], SR, 9 studies from 2,279 hits, RoB 2.0/ROBINS-I/QUIN-stratified) frames the result as an explicit **pain-versus-heat trade-off** — piezo significantly reduces postoperative pain (VAS) while generating *higher* intraosseous temperature. This is a genuine contradiction inside the wiki's own holdings, and it resolves by indication rather than by verdict: piezo earns its place where anatomy is delicate (Schneiderian membrane, inferior alveolar nerve, ridge splitting), not as a thermal safety upgrade for routine osteotomy — and when it is used, the Tier-1 rules tighten rather than relax (chilled saline, controlled load), with dense bone and high thermal load favouring rotary.

*Osseodensification obeys Tier 2 in a different instrument class — which is the strongest available evidence that the ranking is about physics, not about drills.* OD ([[implants/huwais-2017-novel-osseous-densification-osteotomy-primary-stability]], the originating in-vitro paper) burnishes rather than cuts, so it has no reason a priori to follow a cutting-drill heat model. It follows it anyway. Soldatos ([[implants/soldatos-2024-temperature-changes-osseodensification-cadaver-tibiae-cw-ccw]], human cadaver tibiae, 360 osteotomies, bur diameter × CW/CCW × rpm × **number of prior uses**) found that in densifying (CCW) mode, **3.0/4.0 burs after ≥23 prior uses at 800 or 1200 rpm drove ΔT above 47°C with audible chatter**, while **1000 rpm minimized ΔT in both rotation modes**. That is Tier 2 restated in an instrument that does not cut: *accumulated use is an independent heat driver, and the fix is replacement, not selection.* Two bench studies keep OD's baseline profile modest — Tao ([[implants/versah-protocols/tao-2025-optimizing-osseodensification-drilling-implant]], CNC, Type IV foam) reports lower heat than conventional drilling and recommends 1500 rpm **with irrigation** for Type IV, and de Lima ([[implants/versah-protocols/de-lima-2026-osseodensification-vs-conventional-drilling-exvivo]], bovine rib, n=16) found peak temperatures of ~28–31°C with **no significant difference across Versah, an alternative kit, and conventional drilling** (p=0.087) — so OD is not intrinsically hot; a *worn OD bur at the wrong rpm* is. Both papers independently converge on irrigation as non-optional, matching Tier 1.

*And switching technique does not rescue a bad protocol.* The broadest histologic synthesis ([[implants/kosior-2025-histological-histomorphometric-implant-bed-preparation]], PRISMA SR, 338 screened → 29 included, comparing rotary drilling / laser / piezo / osteotome / osseodensification on %BIC, microcracks, osteocyte damage, thermal injury) reports rotary drilling as the worst performer on every histologic axis — most microcracks, most osteocyte damage, most thermal injury, lowest %BIC — with OD highest on %BIC, especially in D3/D4. Taken alone that reads as an argument to abandon drilling. The review's own conclusion is the opposite, and it is this page's thesis stated from the outside: **the drilling penalty is largely protocol-mediated — with optimal rotation speed, cooling, and load, drilling matches the alternatives, so careful technique rather than method-switching is the primary mitigation.** Heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis, so this is a directional finding, not a pooled effect. The cross-technique verdict: **protocol dominates instrument.**

**Does any of this change biology, or is it just staying under a line?** Heuzeroth answers directly ([[implants/heuzeroth-2021-thermal-exposure-osteotomy-osseointegration]]): in an in vivo minipig model, an optimized drill sequence that lowered peak intraosseous temperature from 46.5°C to 35.4°C (~11°C) raised **2-week BIC from 31.3% to 44.9% (+13.6 percentage points, p<0.0001)**, with the control CI crossing the 47°C threshold. Heat control is therefore a *quantitative osseointegration determinant*, not merely a necrosis-avoidance margin — although the industry affiliation on that paper warrants caution.

**Reading caveat (Rule #4 boundary).** Several in-vitro studies deliberately omitted irrigation (Rugova, Sorgato, Bernabeu-Mira) and used synthetic or non-vital animal bone with no perfusion sink, so their absolute temperatures overstate clinical risk; Jung further shows thermocouple, IR-thermography, and fiber-optic methods are not interchangeable, a hidden source of between-study heterogeneity. This overview is therefore built on the *direction and relative ranking* of effects that reproduce across models, not on any single absolute temperature.

## Clinical Bottom Line — Priority-Ordered Protocol

1. **Chilled saline first.** Use cooled (~5–10°C) irrigant as the default; it is the single most reproducible lever and technique-independent. Direct it at the cortical entrance, where most heat is generated, and maintain it *between* drilling steps, not only during active cutting.
2. **Respect drill-replacement schedules.** Drill wear is an independent heat driver that begins before the stated use limit; replacement discipline outranks drill material or brand selection. Prefer twisted over straight geometry where offered.
3. **Escalate cooling for high-load situations** — full-guided surgery (sleeve blocks irrigation → cortical-entrance heat), dense/D1 bone, and deep posterior osteotomies: chilled ~10°C saline + 800–1200 rpm. Do not assume sequential drilling is self-protective if irrigation is compromised.
4. **Peck only when it can do something.** Intermittent drilling is *conditional*, not a routine addition: it helps **cylindrical** drills (−37%, Gehrke in vivo) and does nothing for **conical** ones (p=0.977), and it shows no benefit at all once irrigation temperature is matched (Di Fiore, p=0.45). Use it for cylindrical geometry, and as a fallback when irrigation is compromised — not as a substitute for chilled saline.
5. **Changing instrument does not buy you out of the protocol.** Piezo trades lower postoperative pain for *higher* intraosseous heat (Jain), so reach for it on anatomic grounds (membrane, IAN), not thermal ones. Osseodensification obeys the same wear rule in a non-cutting instrument — **replace Densah burs at ~23 uses and run ~1000 rpm** (Soldatos), or a worn bur will breach 47°C. Across all five preparation families, optimized speed/cooling/load lets plain drilling match the alternatives (Kosior SR) — fix the protocol before switching the method.
6. **Don't over-invest in low-yield variables** — operatory room temperature (25 vs 30°C), drill diameter/depth within normal ranges, and feed rate are not meaningful heat levers relative to irrigation and wear. Drill *material* belongs here too (Chakraborty: inconclusive; wear dominates) — with zirconia the single pooled exception (Bento), still unproven clinically.
7. **Remember the payoff is biological, not just a threshold** — heat reduction demonstrably raises early BIC (Heuzeroth) and 4°C irrigation demonstrably raises histologic bone-bed quality (Kosior), so treat thermal management as part of the osseointegration protocol, not an optional safety courtesy.
