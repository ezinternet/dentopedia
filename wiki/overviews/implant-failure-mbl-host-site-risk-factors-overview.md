> - 범위: 모체 Thesis의 숙주·부위 위험인자 단락(흡연, 방사선, 전신 인자, 악궁)을 분리한 하위 종합. [확인]
> - 수렴: 흡연은 세 경로로 확인된다(Giok 2026의 등급화 umbrella, Ting 2017의 SR 5편 중 4편 MBL 일치, Kate 2016의 오래된 2차 인용 수치). 치주염과 남성은 Ting에서 일관되고, 이갈이는 이제 SR+MA 수준이다(Barboza 2026, OR 4.68). [확인]
> - 방사선은 수치상 가장 심한 숙주 요인이다. Pacheco 2025(73,674 임플란트)에서 조사된 뼈의 생존은 81.52%로 비조사 94.64%보다 낮았고, 조사된 상악이나 이식골에서 더 나빴다. 11개 리뷰 모두 AMSTAR-2 critically low라 방향은 믿을 수 있지만 크기는 정확하지 않다. [확인]
> - 흡연의 크기와 한계: Fan 2024(관찰연구 32편, 59,246 임플란트)의 OR 2.59(95% CI 2.08–3.23)는 조기 실패 종결점이라 이 페이지의 후기 실패 질문에 직접 해당하지 않는다. 후기 실패만 따로 풀링한 보유 논문은 없다. [확인]
> - 후기 구간의 흡연: Moy 2005(4,680 임플란트, 단일 술자, 21년)는 RR 1.56이다. Gjelvold 2021(n=50)은 5년 MBL이 흡연자 −1.90 mm, 비흡연자 −0.40 mm였다(p=0.021). 이 시험에서 MBL을 움직인 유일한 변수는 흡연이고 부하 프로토콜은 아니었다. [확인]
> - Kumar 기준선과 비교: 비흡연자의 5년 −0.40 mm는 풀링된 1년 값 0.56 mm보다 작고, 흡연자의 −1.90 mm는 정상 1년 손실의 약 3.4배다. 흡연자는 5년에 정상 첫 3년치보다 더 잃었고 비흡연자는 1년치보다 적게 잃었다. [확인]
> - 용량 의존: 하루 흡연량을 보고한 23편(Naseri 2020)에서 중증 흡연자가 비흡연자보다 유의하게 나빴다. 흡연은 이분법적 금기가 아니라 단계적이고 부분적으로 교정 가능한 노출이다. [확인]
> - Moy 2005의 전신 인자: 두경부 방사선 RR 2.73(Pacheco와 독립적으로 일치), 당뇨 RR 2.75, 폐경 후 호르몬 요법 RR 2.55, 연령 60–79 RR 2.24였다. 고혈압, 심장질환, 스테로이드 치료는 비유의였다. 절대 금기는 없다는 입장이며(Giok과 같음), 단일 술자 자료이고 2005년 이전의 표면 기술이라 일반화에 한계가 있다. [확인]
> - 악궁과 부위: Moy의 상악 RR 1.79(후방 상악 실패 8.9–9.7% vs 전방 하악 2.89%), Del Fabbro 2014의 상악 97.4% vs 하악 99.6%, Barausse 2024의 4 mm 임플란트 상악 p=0.02, Pacheco의 조사된 상악이 하악보다 나쁨이 모두 같은 방향이다. 어느 연구도 악궁을 주 질문으로 설계하지 않았다. [확인]
> - 종합 한 줄: 후기 실패는 환자·부위·기술의 다요인 위험이고, 숙주 생물학이 시험이 실제로 검증하려던 기술 선택보다 크게 작용한다. [미검증]

## Three-line Summary

This sub-overview holds the host and site risk-factor passages of the parent late-failure and marginal-bone-loss synthesis: smoking, radiotherapy, systemic factors, and arch.
Smoking, bruxism, radiotherapy, diabetes, and maxillary location are the converging signals, but the best pooled smoking estimate concerns early failure, and the late-window evidence rests on a single-operator cohort and a small 5-year trial.
Host biology outranks the technique choice a trial was designed to test, no absolute contraindication exists, and smoking behaves as a graded, partly modifiable exposure.

## 세줄요약

이 하위 종합은 모체 후기 실패·변연골 소실 종합의 숙주·부위 위험인자 단락을 담는다: 흡연, 방사선, 전신 인자, 악궁.
흡연, 이갈이, 방사선, 당뇨, 상악 위치가 수렴하는 신호이지만 가장 좋은 풀링 흡연 추정치는 조기 실패에 대한 것이고, 후기 구간의 근거는 단일 술자 코호트와 작은 5년 시험에 의존한다.
숙주 생물학은 시험이 검증하려던 기술 선택보다 크게 작용하고, 절대 금기는 없으며, 흡연은 단계적이고 부분적으로 교정 가능한 노출로 작동한다.

## Synthesis

Host factors converge across independent lines of evidence: smoking is corroborated three ways (giok-2026's graded umbrella; [[implants/mbl/ting-2017-surgical-patient-factors-affecting-marginal]]'s 4/5-SR MBL concordance; [[implants/survival/kate-2016-implant-failure-a-dentists-nightmare]]'s older secondary-citation figures), periodontitis and male sex add two more consistent patient-factor signals (Ting), and bruxism — long a narrative-level claim ([[implants/survival/song-2021-implant-complications-bruxism-patients]]) — is now SR+MA-level evidence via [[implants/survival/barboza-2026-bruxism-implant-failure-umbrella-review]] (OR 4.68). Radiotherapy is the most quantitatively severe host-modifying factor: [[implants/survival/pacheco-2025-dental-implant-survival-irradiated]] pools 73,674 implants to show irradiated-bone survival dropping to 81.52% (vs 94.64% non-irradiated), worse still in irradiated maxilla or grafted bone — though all 11 pooled reviews were AMSTAR-2 critically low, so the direction is trustworthy but the magnitude is not precise.

**How much risk is "highly suggestive" smoking, actually — and is the number even about *this* page's question?** The pooled layer answers with a caveat that matters more than the number. [[implants/survival/fan-2024-smoking-early-implant-failure-sr-ma]] is the largest quantitative anchor held (SR+MA, 32 observational studies, 59,246 implants): smoking carries an implant-level **OR 2.59 (95% CI 2.08–3.23)**, concordant at patient level. But its endpoint is **early** failure — which belongs to the sibling page ([[overviews/early-implant-failure-risk-prevention-overview]]), not this one. Stating that honestly: **the crisp pooled smoking effect the wiki holds is for pre-loading failure, and no held paper pools smoking against late failure specifically.** What we do hold for the late window is narrower but on-target: [[implants/survival/moy-2005-dental-implant-failure-rates-risk]] (4,680 implants, single operator, 21 years) puts smoking at **RR 1.56** across its whole horizon, and — most usefully — [[immediate-implant/loading-protocol/gjelvold-2021-immediate-delayed-loading-single-tooth-5year-rct]] measured **5-year MBL** directly and found **smokers lost −1.90 mm vs non-smokers −0.40 mm (p=0.021)**. Gjelvold is small (n=50) and was designed to test loading protocol, not smoking; that it found **smoking to be the *only* significant MBL predictor while loading protocol was not** makes it an unusually clean statement of this page's whole thesis — host biology outranks the technique choice the trial was actually about. **The gap is also wider than the bare numbers suggest, once Kumar's baseline is applied:** −0.40 mm in non-smokers is *five years* of loss coming in **below** the pooled *one-year* figure of 0.56 mm, while the smokers' −1.90 mm is roughly **3.4× a normal year-one loss** accumulated over a period in which the non-smokers barely moved. "Smokers lost 1.90 mm" is a number; "smokers lost more in five years than three ordinary first years combined, while comparable non-smokers lost less than one" is the same number made legible.

[[implants/survival/naseri-2020-smoking-levels-implant-failure-sr-ma]] adds the axis that changes the counselling script: it restricted inclusion to the 23 studies reporting **cigarettes per day** and found failure risk rises **dose-dependently**, with heavy smokers significantly worse than non-smokers. Smoking is therefore not a binary contraindication to be checked but a **graded, partially modifiable exposure** — a patient who halves their consumption is plausibly moving down a real gradient, which is a very different conversation from "quit or accept the risk."

**Moy 2005 is the page's only single-operator long-horizon cohort, and it carries three things the umbrella layer does not.** *First*, it independently corroborates radiotherapy at a similar magnitude to Pacheco's pooled survival gap — **head-and-neck radiation RR 2.73** — from wholly different data (4,680 implants, one operator, 21 years), which matters because Pacheco's 11 source SRs were all AMSTAR-2 critically low. *Second*, it names host factors this page otherwise omits entirely: **diabetes RR 2.75** (its largest), **post-menopausal HRT RR 2.55**, and **age 60–79 RR 2.24**. *Third* — and most useful for consent conversations — it names what is **not** a risk factor: **hypertension, cardiac disease, and steroid therapy were all non-significant.** Moy's own conclusion is the same epistemic posture Giok reaches twenty years later by a different route: **no absolute contraindication exists** — diabetes calls for glycemic control and monitoring, irradiated patients for HBO consideration, and cardiovascular comorbidity for neither exclusion nor alarm. Single-operator data limits generalizability (technique is held constant, which is both its strength and its ceiling), and the 2005 vintage predates modern roughened surfaces.

**Arch and site are a risk axis this page previously lacked, and four independent lines converge on it.** Moy: **maxillary location RR 1.79**, with posterior maxilla failing at **8.9–9.7% vs 2.89% in anterior mandible**. [[implants/mbl/del-fabbro-2014-marginal-bone-axial-tilted-sr-ma]]: maxillary survival **97.4% vs mandibular 99.6%** (significant). [[implants/short-narrow/barausse-2024-4mm-short-implants-posterior-atrophic-8year]]: in 4-mm short implants, **maxillary placement significantly raised failure risk (p=0.02)**. Pacheco: irradiated **maxilla** worse than irradiated mandible. Four designs — cohort, SR+MA, retrospective, umbrella — spanning 2005–2025, all pointing the same way. None of the four was designed to test arch as its primary question, so this is convergent observational signal rather than a tested hypothesis; but for a page whose thesis is multifactorial risk, "which jaw" belongs alongside "which patient" and "which technique."
